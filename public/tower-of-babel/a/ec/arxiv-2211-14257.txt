Colding-Minicozzi Entropies in Cartan-Hadamard Manifolds
Jacob Bernstein, Arunima Bhattacharya

Abstract
. We introduce a family of functionals on submanifolds of Cartan-Hadamard manifolds that generalize the Colding-Minicozzi entropy of submanifolds of Euclidean space. We show that these functionals are monotone under mean curvature flow under natural conditions. As a consequence, we obtain sharp lower bounds on these entropies for certain closed hypersurfaces and observe a novel rigidity phenomenon.



1.
Introduction

In
[
13
]
, Colding and Minicozzi introduced the following functional on the space of
n
-dimensional submanifolds
\Sigma\subset\mathbb{R}^{n+k}
,

\lambda[\Sigma]=\sup_{\mathbf{x}_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{n+k},\tau>0}(4\pi\tau)^{-\frac{n}{2}}\int_{\Sigma}{e^{-\frac{|\mathbf{x}(x)-\mathbf{x}_{0}|^{2}}{4\tau}}}dVol_{\Sigma}(x).

Here,
(t,x)\mapsto(4\pi t)^{-\frac{n}{2}}{e^{-\frac{|\mathbf{x}(x)|^{2}}{4t}}}
is the heat kernel of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. In the definition of
\lambda[\Sigma]
, this kernel is extended to all of
\mathbb{R}^{n+k}
in the obvious way.
Colding and Minicozzi called this quantity the
entropy
of
\Sigma
and observed that, by Huisken’s monotonicity formula
[
20
]
, it is monotone along reasonable mean curvature flows. That is, if
t\in[0,T)\mapsto\Sigma_{t}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n+k}
is a family of complete
n
-dimensional submanifolds of polynomial volume growth that satisfy

\frac{d\mathbf{x}}{dt}=\mathbf{H}_{\Sigma_{t}},

then
\lambda[\Sigma_{t}]
is monotone non-increasing in
t
. Here
\mathbf{H}_{\Sigma_{t}}=\Delta_{\Sigma_{t}}\mathbf{x}=-H_{\Sigma_{t}}\mathbf{n}_{\Sigma_{t}}
is the mean curvature vector of
\Sigma_{t}
and a submanifold,
\Sigma
, has polynomial volume growth if
Vol_{\mathbb{R}}(\Sigma\cap B_{R})\leq N(1+R)^{N}
for some
N>0
. This notion of entropy has been extensively studied in recent years. See, for instance,
[
10
,
6
,
41
,
12
,
7
,
9
,
8
,
33
,
25
]
.

In
[
3
]
, an analogous quantity was introduced by the first author for submanifolds of hyperbolic space,
\mathbb{H}^{n+k}
. In particular, it was shown that there is a notion of entropy that is monotone non-increasing along mean curvature flows that have exponential volume growth, i.e.,

Vol_{\mathbb{H}}(\Sigma\cap\mathcal{B}^{\mathbb{H}}_{R}(x_{0}))\leq Ne^{NR}

for some
N>0
. See
[
27
,
40
,
32
,
31
]
for other extensions of the concepts to curved ambient manifolds. For applications of the Colding-Minicozzi entropy in hyperbolic space, we refer to
[
4
]
and
[
38
]
.

In this note, we generalize and unify both quantities by introducing a one-parameter family of entropies defined on submanifolds of any Cartan-Hadamard manifold. Indeed, suppose that
(M,g)
is a Cartan-Hadamard manifold, i.e., a complete simply connected Riemannian manifold with non-positive sectional curvature. For any
n\geq 1
and
\kappa\geq 0
, let

(1.1)

K_{n,\kappa}(t,r)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{cc}\kappa^{n}K_{n}(\kappa^{2}t,\kappa r)&\kappa,t>0,r\geq 0\\
(4\pi t)^{-n/2}e^{-\frac{r^{2}}{4t}}&\kappa=0,t>0,r\geq 0\end{array}\right.

where
K_{n}
is the function introduced in
[
14
]
and used in
[
3
]
– see Section
3
. In particular, this function makes

H_{n,\kappa}(t,x;t_{0},x_{0})=K_{n,\kappa}(t-t_{0},\mathrm{dist}_{g}(x,x_{0}))

the heat kernel on
(M,g)
with singularity at
x_{0}
and time
t_{0}
precisely when
(M,g)
is the space form of constant curvature
-\kappa^{2}
.

Following
[
13
]
and
[
3
]
, let

\Phi_{n,\kappa}^{t_{0},x_{0}}(t,x)=K_{n,\kappa}(t_{0}-t,\mathrm{dist}_{g}(x,x_{0}))

and for
\Sigma\subset M
, an
n
-dimensional submanifold, define
the Colding-Minicozzi
\kappa
-entropy of
\Sigma
in
(M,g)
to be

\lambda_{g}^{\kappa}[\Sigma]=\sup_{x_{0}\in M,\tau>0}\int_{\Sigma}\Phi_{n,\kappa}^{0,x_{0}}(-\tau,x)dVol_{\Sigma}(x)=\sup_{x_{0}\in M,\tau>0}\int_{\Sigma}\Phi_{n,\kappa}^{\tau,x_{0}}(0,x)dVol_{\Sigma}(x).

When
\kappa=0
and
(M,g)=(\mathbb{R}^{n+k},g_{\mathbb{R}})
is Euclidean space, this is just the usual Colding-Minicozzi entropy,
\lambda[\Sigma]
, of
\Sigma
. When
\kappa=1
and
(M,g)=(\mathbb{H}^{n+k},g_{\mathbb{H}})
is hyperbolic space, this is the entropy in hyperbolic space,
\lambda_{\mathbb{H}}[\Sigma]
, introduced in
[
3
]
.

The main result of this paper is a monotonicity property of an appropriate range of entropies along well-behaved mean curvature flows when the ambient Cartan-Hadamard manifold has sectional curvature bounded above by
-\kappa^{2}_{0}
.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
(M,g)
be an
(n+k)
-dimensional Cartan-Hadamard manifold with
\sec_{g}\leq-{\kappa}^{2}_{0}
. If
0\leq\kappa\leq\kappa_{0}
and
t\in[t_{1},t_{2}]\mapsto\Sigma_{t}\subset M
is a locally smooth mean curvature flow of
n
-dimensional complete submanifolds of exponential volume growth and
Ric_{g}\geq-\Lambda^{2}g
on
\Sigma_{t}
, for
t\in[t_{1},t_{2}]
and a uniform
\Lambda\geq 0
, then

\lambda_{g}^{\kappa}[\Sigma_{t_{1}}]\geq\lambda_{g}^{\kappa}[\Sigma_{t_{2}}].

In particular, in any Cartan-Hadamard manifold,
t\mapsto\lambda^{0}_{g}[\Sigma_{t}]
is monotone non-increasing along a classical mean curvature flow of closed hypersurfaces.

As observed in
[
3
]
, this monotonicity property allows one to generalize the results of
[
10
,
6
,
41
,
7
,
9
,
8
,
33
]
on properties of the Colding-Minicozzi entropy of closed hypersurfaces in Euclidean space to the curved setting. The extension to spaces of variable curvature also leads to an interesting rigidity phenomenon that is reminiscent of the classical Cartan-Hadamard conjecture on the sharp isoperimetric inequality in Cartan-Hadamard manifolds.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
(M,g)
be an
(n+1)
-dimensional Cartan-Hadamard manifold. If
\Omega\subset M
is a compact domain with
\partial\Omega
smooth and mean convex, then

\lambda_{g}^{0}[\partial\Omega]\geq\lambda[\mathbb{S}^{n}].

Equality holds if and only if, for some
R>0
,
(\Omega,g|_{\Omega})
is isometric to
(\bar{B}_{R},g_{\mathbb{R}}|_{\bar{B}_{R}})
, the standard Euclidean metric restricted to the closed Euclidean ball in
\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
of radius
R
.
When
n=1
or
2
, the same result is true without the hypothesis that
\partial\Omega
is mean convex.

Remark 1.3
.

When the ambient manifold is flat and
n=1
or
2
this is the main result of
[
17
]
or
[
7
]
. When
n\geq 3
this is a special, and easier, case of
[
6
]
and
[
41
]
. Without the hypothesis of mean convexity, the arguments of
[
6
]
and
[
41
]
require a suitable weak formulation of mean curvature flow in order to flow past singularities. A subtlety that complicates things in the curved setting is that without a uniform lower bound on the Ricci curvature certain pathological behavior may arise – see
[
22
,
23
]
and Remark
5.5
. This seems to be only a technical issue, but for the sake of simplicity, we impose hypotheses that allow us to remain in the setting of classical mean curvature flows.

As a final remark, we mention that in
[
3
]
the first author showed that if
\Sigma
is an
n
-dimensional submanifold of
\mathbb{H}^{n+k}
that is regular up to the ideal boundary, then there is an inequality between
\partial_{\infty}\Sigma
, the conformal volume of the ideal boundary of
\Sigma
, which is a subset of the ideal boundary of
\mathbb{S}^{n+k-1}=\partial_{\infty}\mathbb{H}^{n+k}
the ideal boundary of hyperbolic space, and
\lambda_{\mathbb{H}}[\Sigma]
, the Colding-Minicozzi entropy of
\Sigma
in hyperbolic space. In forthcoming work
[
5
]
, we will use the monotonicity properties of the present paper to study an analogous relationship for certain minimal submanifolds of complex hyperbolic space.

2.
Notation and background

Unless stated otherwise, throughout this paper we suppose that
(M,g)
is a Cartan-Hadamard manifold, i.e., a complete simply connected manifold with non-positive sectional curvature. We denote by
(\mathbb{R}^{l},g_{\mathbb{R}})
the Euclidean space and by
(\mathbb{H}^{l}(\kappa),g_{\mathbb{H}(\kappa)})
the space form of constant curvature
-\kappa^{2}
. When
\kappa=1
, i.e., for hyperbolic space we suppress
\kappa
from the notation. These are all Cartan-Hadamard manifolds.

We will repeatedly use that there is a unique minimizing geodesic connecting any two points in a Cartan-Hadamard manifold and that the distance to any
x_{0}\in M
is a smooth function on
M\setminus\left\{x_{0}\right\}
and the square of this function is smooth on
M
. For any fixed point,
x_{0}\in M
,
\rho:M\to\mathbb{R}
denotes the distance function with respect to
g
between the points
x
and
x_{0}

\rho(x)=\rho(x;x_{0})=\mathrm{dist}_{g}(x,x_{0}).

When
x_{0}
is clear from the context we write
\rho(x)
. For a
C^{1}
function
F:M\to\mathbb{R}
we write

\partial_{\rho}F=g(\nabla_{g}\rho,\nabla_{g}F)=d\rho(\nabla_{g}F)=dF(\nabla_{g}\rho)

which is continuous on
M\setminus\left\{x_{0}\right\}
. If
\nabla_{g}F|_{x=x_{0}}=0
, then this extends continuously to
M
. When
F
is
C^{2}
we also write

\partial^{2}_{\rho}F=\nabla_{g}^{2}F(\nabla_{g}\rho,\nabla_{g}\rho).

By
\mathcal{B}^{g}_{R}(x)
, we denote a ball of radius
R
around
x
with respect to the metric
g
in the manifold
M
. By
B_{R}(x)
, we denote a ball of radius
R
around
x
in the Euclidean space. A submanifold
\Sigma\subset M
has exponential volume growth if there is a constant
N>0
so, for some
x_{0}\in M
,

Vol_{g}(\mathcal{B}_{R}^{g}(x_{0})\cap\Sigma)\leq Ne^{NR}.

Given vectors
\mathbf{v},\mathbf{w}\in T_{x}M
we will sometimes write

\mathbf{v}\cdot\mathbf{w}=g_{x}(\mathbf{v},\mathbf{w})

and

|\mathbf{v}|^{2}=\mathbf{v}\cdot\mathbf{v}=g_{x}(\mathbf{v},\mathbf{v}).

When
\mathbf{v}
and
\mathbf{w}
are linearly independent we denote the plane they span by

\mathbf{v}\wedge\mathbf{w}=\mathrm{span}\left\{\mathbf{v},\mathbf{w}\right\}\in G_{2}(T_{x}M).

We will suppress the point
x
in the notation when it is clear from the context.

2.1.
Mean curvature flows

Let
(M,g)
be a general
(n+k)
-dimensional Riemannian manifold and
I\subset\mathbb{R}
an interval with non-empty interior. Consider a family
t\in I\mapsto\Sigma_{t}\subset M
of smooth properly embedded
n
-dimensional submanifolds.
We say this family is a
(classical) mean curvature flow (in
M
)
if there is a fixed
n
-dimensional manifold
\Sigma
and a smooth family of maps

F:I\times\Sigma\to M

satisfying,
F(t,\cdot):\Sigma\to\Sigma_{t}\subset M
is a proper embedding for each
t\in I
and

(2.1)

\left(\frac{\partial F}{\partial t}(t,x)\right)^{\perp}=\mathbf{H}_{\Sigma_{t}}(F(t,x)).

Here
\left(\frac{\partial F}{\partial t}(t,x)\right)^{\perp}\in T_{F(t,x)}M
is the normal component of
\frac{\partial F}{\partial t}(t,x)
. Observe that if
\Sigma
is closed and
I
is compact, then

F(I\times\Sigma)=\bigcup_{t\in I}\Sigma_{t}

is a compact subset of
M
. We remark that (
2.1
) is invariant under (time varying) reparameterizations. In many cases of interest, one can apply a reparameterization to obtain a solution to

\frac{\partial F}{\partial t}(t,x)=\mathbf{H}_{\Sigma_{t}}(F(t,x)).

That is, the embeddings move normally to the hypersurfaces with velocity given by the mean curvature vector.

We say the family
t\in I\mapsto\Sigma_{t}\subset M
is a
locally smooth mean curvature flow
if, for every
t_{0}\in I
and
x_{0}\in M
, there is an
\epsilon>0
so if
I^{\prime}=I\cap\left\{t:|t-t_{0}|<\epsilon^{2}\right\}
, then
t\in I^{\prime}\mapsto\mathcal{B}_{\epsilon}^{g}(x_{0})\cap\Sigma_{t}
is a, possibly empty, classical mean curvature flow in
\mathcal{B}_{\epsilon}^{g}(x_{0})
.
Every classical mean curvature flow is automatically a locally smooth flow, but locally smooth flows need not be classical – for instance the flow of Remark
5.5
.

3.
Fundamental solutions of the heat equation and the heat kernel

We recall some basic properties of the fundamental solutions of the heat equation on a Riemannian manifold. Our source is
[
15
]
and
[
14
]
– we refer the reader also to
[
26
]
. Note we follow the convention of
[
14
]
and put the time variable first.

3.1.
Background and terminology

Let
(M,g)
be a connected Riemannian manifold, not necessarily complete. Following Dodziuk
[
15
]
, a continuous function
p:(0,\infty)\times M\times M\to\mathbb{R}
is a
fundamental solution of the heat equation
if, for every bounded continuous function
u_{0}
on
M
, the function

u(t,x)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{cc}\int_{M}p(t,x,y)u_{0}(y)dVol_{g}(y)&t>0\\
u_{0}(x)&t=0\end{array}\right.

is a solution of the Cauchy problem with initial data
u_{0}
. That is,
u\in C^{0}([0,\infty)\times M)\cap C^{1}((0,\infty)\times M)
and for all
t>0
,
u\in C^{2}(M)
and

\left\{\begin{array}[]{cc}\frac{du}{dt}-\Delta_{g}u=0&\mbox{on }(0,\infty)\times M\\
u(0,x)=u_{0}(x)&\mbox{on }M.\end{array}\right.

By
[
15
, Theorem 2.2]
, if
(M,g)
is complete and has a uniform lower bound on its Ricci curvature, then
p
is unique, but this need not be the case in general. However, as observed in
[
15
]
one may always choose a unique fundamental solution by selecting the one that is minimal. This selection,
p_{M}
, which we call the
minimal fundamental solution of the heat equation
is characterized by

p_{M}(t,x,y)=\sup_{D\in\mathcal{D}(M)}p_{D}(t,x,y)

where
\mathcal{D}(M)
is the collection of precompact connected open sets
D\subset M
with
\partial D
smooth and
p_{D}
is the, necessarily unique, fundamental solution of the heat equation in
D
with Dirichlet boundary conditions. We refer to
[
15
, Lemma 3.2 and Theorem 3.6]
for a list of properties of
p_{D}
and
p_{M}
.

The minimal fundamental solution satisfies, for
t>0
,

\int_{M}p_{M}(t,x,y)dVol_{g}(y)\leq 1.

In general, one cannot expect equality even when
M
is complete.
However, when
(M,g)
is complete and has a uniform lower bound on the Ricci curvature Dodziuk shows in
[
15
, Theorem 4.2]
that, in addition to
p_{M}
being the unique fundamental solution to the heat equation, one also has conservation of total heat for all
t>0

\int_{M}p_{M}(t,x,y)dVol_{g}(y)=1,x\in M.

In this case, we refer to
p_{M}
as the
heat kernel
of
(M,g)
– see
[
29
,
2
]
for other conditions that ensure this property holds.

3.2.
Properties of the heat kernel of
\mathbb{H}^{n}

We specialize now to hyperbolic space,
\mathbb{H}^{n}
– the space form of constant sectional curvature
-1
. As
(\mathbb{H}^{n},g_{\mathbb{H}})
is complete and has constant Ricci curvature there is a unique heat kernel
p_{\mathbb{H}^{n}}
in the sense above. Following the notation of
[
3
]
and
[
14
]
, let us write

H_{n}(t,x;t_{0},x_{0})=p_{\mathbb{H}^{n}}(t-t_{0},x,x_{0})

which we think of as a function

H_{n}(\cdot,\cdot;t_{0},x_{0}):(t_{0},\infty)\times\mathbb{H}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}

and call
the heat kernel on
\mathbb{H}^{n}
with singularity at
x=x_{0}
and
t=t_{0}
.
Formally, this is a solution to

\left\{\begin{array}[]{cc}\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial t}-\Delta_{\mathbb{H}}\right)H_{n}=0&t>t_{0}\\
\lim_{t\downarrow t_{0}}H_{n}=\delta_{x_{0}}\end{array}\right.

where
\delta_{x_{0}}
is the Dirac delta at
x_{0}
.

The symmetries of
\mathbb{H}^{n}
and uniqueness of the heat kernel ensure that if
H_{n}(t,x;t_{0},x_{0})
is the heat kernel on
\mathbb{H}^{n}
with singularity at
x=x_{0}
and
t=t_{0}
, then, there is a smooth positive function
K_{n}:(0,\infty)\times[0,\infty)\to\mathbb{R}
such that

H_{n}(t,x;t_{0},x_{0})=K_{n}(t-t_{0},\mathrm{dist}_{\mathbb{H}}(x,x_{0})).

For instance, as
\mathbb{H}^{1}
is just the Euclidean line,

K_{1}(t,r)=(4\pi t)^{-1/2}e^{-\frac{r^{2}}{4t}}.

Likewise,

K_{3}(t,r)=(4\pi t)^{-3/2}\frac{r}{\sinh(r)}e^{-t-\frac{r^{2}}{4t}}.

Note that for
t>t_{0}
one has

\nabla_{g}H_{n}(t,x;t_{0},x_{0})|_{x=x_{0}}=0.

Properties of
K_{n}
were extensively studied by Davies and Mandouvalos in
[
14
]
and we will make heavy use of their results, which, for the convenience of readers, we summarize below. We first recall two recurrence relations for
K_{n}
from
[
14
]
.
The first is attributed to Millison:

(3.1)

K_{n+2}(t,r)=-\frac{e^{-nt}}{2\pi\sinh(r)}\partial_{r}K_{n}(t,r).

The second is:

(3.2)

K_{n,0}(t,r)=\int_{r}^{\infty}\frac{e^{\frac{1}{4}\left(2n-1\right)t}K_{n+1}(t,s)\sinh(s)}{\left(\cosh(s)-\cosh(r)\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}}ds.

Using these relations, Davies and Mandouvalos obtained the following uniform estimate on
K_{n+1}

[
14
, Thereom 3.1]
:

(3.3)

K_{n+1}(t,r)\leq C_{n}t^{-\frac{1}{2}(n+1)}e^{-\frac{1}{4}n^{2}t-\frac{r^{2}}{4t}-\frac{1}{2}nr}(1+r+t)^{\frac{1}{2}n-1}(1+r)

and a corresponding lower bound

(3.4)

C_{n}^{-1}t^{-\frac{1}{2}(n+1)}e^{-\frac{1}{4}n^{2}t-\frac{r^{2}}{4t}-\frac{1}{2}nr}(1+r+t)^{\frac{1}{2}n-1}(1+r)\leq K_{n+1}(t,r)

where
C_{n}>1
depends only on
n
.
In particular, for any fixed
x_{0}\in\mathbb{H}^{n+1}
and
R>0
, one has for
t\geq 1

(3.5)

\sup_{x\in\mathcal{B}_{R}^{\mathbb{H}}(x_{0})}H_{n+1}(t,x;0,x_{0})\leq C_{n,R}^{\prime}t^{-\frac{3}{2}}e^{-\frac{1}{4}n^{2}t}.

Next, we recall the following super-convexity estimate of
K_{n}
shown for
n\leq 4
in
[
3
]
and for all
n
in
[
39
]
. This is equivalent to a super-convexity property of
H_{n}

Proposition 3.1
.

For all
n\geq 1
,

(3.6)

\partial^{2}_{r}\log K_{n}-\coth(r)\partial_{r}\log K_{n}\geq 0.

Moreover, this inequality is strict for
r>0
.

4.
A characterization of constant curvature Cartan-Hadamard manifolds

We use the functions
K_{n,\kappa}
of (
1.1
), which are rescalings of the functions
K_{n}
introduced in Section
3.2
, in order to characterize constant curvature Cartan-Hadamard manifolds in terms of the minimal fundamental solution of the heat equation. Observe no
a priori
assumptions are made to ensure that this is the unique fundamental solution – i.e., the heat kernel in our terminology.

Theorem 4.1
.

Fix
\kappa\geq 0
and let
(M,g)
be an
n
-dimensional Cartan-Hadamard manifold with
n\geq 2
and
\mathrm{sec}_{g}\leq-\kappa^{2}
and denote by
p_{M}
the minimal fundamental solution of the heat equation on
M
. For all
t>0
, one has

0<p_{M}(t,x,y)\leq K_{n,\kappa}(t,d_{g}(x,y)).

This inequality is strict unless
(M,g)
has constant sectional curvature
-\kappa^{2}
, in which case
p_{M}(t,x,y)=K_{n,\kappa}(t,d_{g}(x,y))
is the heat kernel of
(M,g)
.

Before proving this result we will need to first prove some auxiliary results.
In what follows, it is convenient to introduce the following notation:

\mathrm{ct}_{\kappa}(r)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{cc}\kappa\coth(\kappa r)&\kappa,r>0\\
\frac{1}{r}&\kappa=0,r>0.\end{array}\right.

We next recall the following sharp statement of the Hessian comparison theorem:

Proposition 4.2
.

Let
(M,g)
be a Cartan-Hadamard manifold that satisfies
\mathrm{sec}_{g}\leq-\kappa^{2}\leq 0
.
Fixing
x_{0}\in M
, for any
x\neq x_{0}
and
v\in T_{x}M
with
v\neq 0
one has:

(\nabla^{2}_{g}\rho)_{x}(v,v)\geq\mathrm{ct}_{\kappa}(\rho(x))(g_{x}(v,v)-(d\rho(v))^{2})

and this inequality is strict unless
v
is parallel to
\nabla_{g}\rho(x)
or

\mathrm{sec}_{g}(\gamma^{\prime}(t)\wedge V(t))=-\kappa^{2}

for all
t\in[0,\rho(x)]
,
where
\gamma:[0,\rho(x)]\to M
is the unique unit speed geodesic connecting
x_{0}
to
x
and
V:[0,\rho(x)]\to TM
is the parallel transport of
v
along
\gamma
.

A consequence of this is a reverse form of Laplacian comparison

(\Delta_{g}\rho)(x)\geq(n-1)\mathrm{ct}_{\kappa}(\rho(x)),

which is a strict inequality unless,

\mathrm{sec}_{g}(\gamma^{\prime}(t)\wedge W)=-\kappa^{2}

for all
t\in[0,\rho(x)]
and all
W\in T_{\gamma(t)}M
that are orthogonal to
\gamma^{\prime}(t)
.

Proof.

As
(M,g)
is a Cartan-Hadamard manifold there is a unique length minimizing geodesic connecting
x_{0}
to
x
and so the inequality is the statement of the Hessian comparison theorem – e.g.,
[
30
, Theorem 1.1]
. The strictness of the inequality follows from the proof – namely because equality forces
V
to be proportional to a Jacobi field.
∎

The definition of
K_{n,\kappa}
in (
1.1
) and properties of
K_{n}
ensure that for all
t>0
,

\partial_{t}K_{n,\kappa}(t,r)=\partial_{r}^{2}K_{n,\kappa}(t,r)+(n-1)\mathrm{ct}_{\kappa}(r)\partial_{r}K_{n,\kappa}(t,r).

Observe that for
r,t>0
, the Millison identity (
3.1
) and the fact that
K_{n,\kappa}
is positive for all
t>0
imply

\partial_{r}K_{n,\kappa}(t,r)=-2\pi e^{n\kappa^{2}t}\kappa^{-1}\sinh(\kappa r)K_{n+2,\kappa}(t,r)<0,

and so, for
\kappa,r,t>0
,

\partial_{r}K_{n,\kappa}(t,r)<0.

When
\kappa=0
and
r,t>0
, one may directly compute that

\partial_{r}K_{n,0}(t,r)=-\frac{r}{2t}K_{n,0}(t,r)=-2\pi\rho K_{n+2,0}(t,r)<0.

The
K_{n,\kappa}
can be used to give the heat kernel on the Cartan-Hadamard space forms of constant curvature
-\kappa^{2}
. More generally, they give supersolutions of the heat equation on Cartan-Hadamard manifolds of curvature bounded above by
-\kappa^{2}
.

Lemma 4.3
.

Fix
\kappa\geq 0
and let
(M,g)
be an
n
-dimensional Cartan-Hadamard manifold with
n\geq 2
and
\mathrm{sec}_{g}\leq-\kappa^{2}
and fix a point
x_{0}\in M
. For all
t>0
, the function

H_{n,\kappa}(t,x)=K_{n,\kappa}(t,\rho(x))=K_{n,\kappa}(t,\mathrm{dist}_{g}(x,x_{0}))

is a smooth super solution of the heat equation; i.e.,

\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial t}-\Delta_{g}\right)H_{n,\kappa}\geq 0.

Moreover, this inequality is strict at some point
x\in M
and time
t>0
unless
(M,g)
has constant curvature
-\kappa^{2}
.

Proof.

One computes at
(t,x)
that

\displaystyle\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial t}-\Delta_{g}\right)H_{n,\kappa}

\displaystyle=\left(\partial_{t}K_{n,\kappa}(t,r)-\partial_{r}^{2}K_{n,\kappa}(t,r)\right)|_{r=\rho}-\partial_{r}K_{n,\kappa}(t,r)|_{r=\rho}\Delta_{g}\rho

\displaystyle=(n-1)\mathrm{ct}_{\kappa}(\rho)\partial_{r}K_{n,\kappa}(t,\rho)-\partial_{r}K_{n,\kappa}(t,\rho)\Delta_{g}\rho.

Hence, as
-\partial_{r}K_{n,\kappa}\geq 0
, Proposition
4.2
implies

\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial t}-\Delta_{g}\right)H_{n,\kappa}\geq 0

and the inequality is strict at
x\neq x_{0}
unless

\sec_{g}(\gamma^{\prime}(t)\wedge W)=-\kappa^{2}

where
\gamma:[0,L]\to M
is the unit speed geodesic connecting
x_{0}
to
x
and
W\in T_{\gamma(t)}M
is orthogonal to
\gamma^{\prime}
.

That is, the inequality is strict somewhere unless
\sec_{g}(\nabla_{g}\rho\wedge W)=-\kappa^{2}
for all
x\neq x_{0}
and
W\in T_{x}M
orthogonal to
\nabla_{g}\rho(x)
. In this case, one has, by Proposition
4.2
, that, for all
x\neq x_{0}
,

(\nabla_{g}^{2}\rho)_{x}=\mathrm{ct}_{\kappa}(\rho(x))(g_{x}-d\rho^{2}_{x}).

Let

(4.1)

f_{\kappa}(r)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{cc}\frac{1}{\kappa^{2}}\left(\cosh(\kappa r)-1\right)&\kappa>0\\
\frac{1}{2}r^{2}&\kappa=0.\end{array}\right.

Using the chain rule, one concludes that on
M\setminus\left\{x_{0}\right\}

(\nabla^{2}_{g}f_{\kappa}(\rho))_{x}=(1+\kappa^{2}f_{\kappa}(\rho(x)))g_{x}.

In addition, as
f_{\kappa}(\rho)
is readily seen to be smooth at
x_{0}
, the equality extends to all of
M
. This conformal property of the Hessian means that the metric
g
locally has a warped product structure – see
[
28
, Theorem 4.3.3]
where the result is attributed to Brinkmann
[
11
]
. Moreover, the specific form of the conformal term
[
28
, Corollary 4.3.4]
shows that the metric has constant sectional curvature
-\kappa^{2}
in a neighborhood of
x_{0}
. It is not hard to modify this argument to see that
(M,g)
globally has constant curvature
-\kappa^{2}
.
∎

We need the following short time estimate for
H_{n,\kappa}
near the singularity. We state this result quite generally as it will be used elsewhere.

Lemma 4.4
.

For
k\geq 0
, let
(M,g)
be an
(n+k)
-dimensional Riemannian manifold (not necessarily Cartan-Hadamard) and let
\Sigma\subset M
be a smooth properly embedded
n
-dimensional submanifold (i.e., an open subset of
M
when
k=0
). Fix a point
x_{0}\in M
and an
R>0
so
U=\mathcal{B}_{R}^{g}(x_{0})
is geodesically convex and precompact in
M
. Suppose that

i:(U,g|_{U})\to(\mathbb{R}^{d},g_{\mathbb{R}})

is an isometric embedding. For
x\in U\cap\Sigma
define

H(t,x)=K_{n,\kappa}(t,\rho(x))=K_{n,\kappa}(t,\mathrm{dist}_{g}(x,x_{0}))\mbox{ and }G(t,x)=K_{n,0}(t,|i(x)-i(x_{0})|).

There is a constant
C(U,i,n,\kappa)>0
, independent of
\Sigma
, so for
0<t\leq 1
and
f\in L^{\infty}(U\cap\Sigma)
,

\displaystyle\left|\int_{U\cap\Sigma}f(x)\left(H(t,x)-G(t,x)\right)dVol_{\Sigma}(x)\right|\leq Ct^{1/3}\Theta(t)\|f\|_{L^{\infty}(U\cap\Sigma)}

where

\Theta(t)=Vol_{g}(U\cap\Sigma)+\int_{U\cap\Sigma}G(t,x)\leq C^{\prime}(\Sigma,n)<\infty.

Proof.

We first claim that there exists
C_{1}=C_{1}(n,\kappa,R)
so, for
0<t\leq 1
and
0\leq r\leq R
,

|K_{n,\kappa}(t,r)-K_{n,0}(t,r)|\leq C_{1}(t+r^{2})K_{n,0}(t,r).

This follows from
[
14
, Equations (3.2) and (3.3)]
for odd
n
. The expansion for even
n
follows from (
3.2
) above. Hence, for
0<t<\min\left\{1,R^{3}\right\}
and
0\leq r<t^{1/3}
we have, up to increasing
C_{1}
,

|K_{n,\kappa}(t,r)-K_{n,0}(t,r)|\leq C_{1}t^{2/3}K_{n,0}(t,r).

The geometry of
i(U)
ensures that there exists
\beta=\beta(i,R)>1
so

0\leq\rho(x)-|i(x)-i(x_{0})|=d_{g}(x,x_{0})-|i(x)-i(x_{0})|\leq\beta|i(x)-i(x_{0})|^{3}.

Hence, for
x\in\mathcal{B}^{g}_{t^{1/3}}(x_{0})
, and
0<t\leq 1

\displaystyle|K_{n,0}(t,\rho(x))-K_{n,0}(t,|i(x)-i(x_{0})|)|

\displaystyle\leq\left(e^{\frac{1}{2}\beta t^{1/3}}-1\right)K_{n,0}(t,|i(x)-i(x_{0})|)

\displaystyle\leq C_{2}t^{1/3}K_{n,0}(t,|i(x)-i(x_{0})|)

where
C_{2}
depends only on
\beta
. That is, for
x\in\mathcal{B}^{g}_{t^{1/3}}(x_{0})
, the two inequalities imply

|H(t,x)-G(t,x)|\leq C_{1}t^{2/3}K_{n,0}(t,\rho(x))+C_{2}t^{1/3}G(t,x).

This in turn yields for
x\in\mathcal{B}^{g}_{t^{1/3}}(x_{0})
,

|H(t,x)-G(t,x)|\leq(C_{1}t^{2/3}+C_{2}t^{1/3}+C_{1}C_{2}t)G(t,x)\leq C_{2}^{\prime}t^{1/3}G(t,x).

In the above, we used
t\leq 1
. Hence,

\displaystyle\Big|\int_{\mathcal{B}^{g}_{t^{1/3}}(x_{0})\cap\Sigma}

\displaystyle f(x)\left(H(t,x)-G(t,x)\right)dVol_{\Sigma}(x)\Big|

\displaystyle\leq C_{2}^{\prime}t^{1/3}\|f\|_{L^{\infty}(U\cap\Sigma)}\int_{\mathcal{B}^{g}_{t^{1/3}}(x_{0})\cap\Sigma}G(t,x)dVol_{\Sigma}(x)

\displaystyle\leq C_{2}^{\prime}t^{1/3}\Theta(t)\|f\|_{L^{\infty}(U\cap\Sigma)}.

Next, we observe that by (
3.3
) for
0\leq r\leq R
there is a constant,
C_{3}=C_{3}(n,\kappa,R)
so

K_{n,\kappa}(t,r)\leq C_{3}K_{n,0}(t,r).

Moreover, because
|i(x)-i(x_{0})|\leq\mathrm{dist}_{g}(x,x_{0})
and
\partial_{r}K_{n,0}\leq 0
,

K_{n,0}(t,\mathrm{dist}_{g}(x,x_{0}))\leq G(t,x).

Hence, on
U
we have

|H(t,x)-G(t,x)|\leq H(t,x)+G(t,x)\leq(1+C_{3})G(t,x),

which implies there is a
C_{4}
so

\displaystyle\int_{\Sigma\cap(\mathcal{B}_{R}^{g}(x_{0})\setminus\mathcal{B}^{g}_{t^{1/3}}(x_{0}))}|H(t,x)-G(t,x)|

\displaystyle\leq C_{4}t^{-\frac{n}{2}}e^{-\frac{1}{t^{1/3}}}Vol_{g}(\mathcal{B}_{R}^{g}(x_{0})\cap\Sigma)

\displaystyle\leq C_{4}^{\prime}t^{1/3}\Theta(t).

Combining the estimates on
\mathcal{B}^{g}_{t^{1/3}}(x_{0})
and on
\mathcal{B}_{R}^{g}(x_{0})\setminus\mathcal{B}^{g}_{t^{1/3}}(x_{0})
we obtain the first bound for an appropriate choice of
C
.

Finally, that
\Theta(t)
is bounded independent of
t
is a straightforward consequence of
i(\Sigma)
being a properly embedded submanifold – see
[
37
, Theorem 9.1]
.
∎

We will also need the fact that
K_{n,\kappa}
can be used at small times to give an approximation of the identity.

Proposition 4.5
.

For
k\geq 0
, let
(M,g)
be an
(n+k)
-dimensional complete Riemannian manifold (not necessarily Cartan-Hadamard) and let
\Sigma\subset M
be a smooth properly embedded
n
-dimensional submanifold. If
f\in C^{0}_{c}(\Sigma)
is a compactly supported function and
x_{0}\in\Sigma
, then

\lim_{t\to 0^{+}}\int_{\Sigma}f(x)H(t,x)dVol_{\Sigma}(x)=f(x_{0})

where

H(t,x)=K_{n,\kappa}(t,\mathrm{dist}_{g}(x,x_{0})).

If, in addition,
\Sigma
is complete, properly embedded, and has exponential volume growth, then the same holds when
f\in C^{0}(\Sigma)\cap L^{\infty}(\Sigma)
.

Proof.

Pick
r>0
sufficiently small so
U=\mathcal{B}^{g}_{r}(x_{0})
is geodesically convex and precompact. Let
i:(U,g)\to(\mathbb{R}^{d},g_{\mathbb{R}})
be some choice of isometric embedding. It follows from (
3.3
) that
H(t,x)\to 0
uniformly as
t\to 0
in
M\setminus\mathcal{B}_{r/2}^{g}(x_{0})
. Let
\psi
be a smooth bump function supported in
\mathcal{B}_{r/2}^{g}(x_{0})
with
\psi=1
in a neighborhood of
x_{0}
. Since
f
is compactly supported, one has

\lim_{t\to 0^{+}}\int_{\Sigma}H(t,x)f(x)dVol_{\Sigma}(x)=\lim_{t\to 0^{+}}\int_{\Sigma}H(t,x)\psi(x)f(x)dVol_{\Sigma}(x)

provided both limits exist. As such we may assume
f
has compact support in
U
.
By Lemma
4.4
this reduces to a question on submanifolds of Euclidean space where the existence and value of the above limit can be readily established.

It remains to treat the case where
f
is bounded and
\Sigma
has exponential volume growth. In this case, the exponential volume growth and (
3.3
) imply

\lim_{t\to 0^{+}}\int_{\Sigma}H(t,x)(1-\psi(x))f(x)dVol_{\Sigma}(x)=0

and the result follows from the compactly supported case.
∎

We are now ready to prove Theorem
4.1
.

Proof of Theorem
4.1
.

Set
P_{\kappa}(t,x,y)=K_{n,\kappa}(t,d_{g}(x,y))
. Proposition
4.5
implies that, for any
\phi\in C^{0}_{c}(M)
, one has

\phi(x)=\lim_{\tau\to 0^{+}}\int_{M}P_{\kappa}(\tau,x,y)\phi(y)dVol_{g}(y).

Fix a domain
D\in\mathcal{D}(M)
and let
p_{D}(t,x,y)
be the Dirichlet heat kernel extended by
0
outside
D\times D
. The definition of heat kernel likewise ensures that for
x\in D
and
\psi\in C^{0}(M)\cap L^{\infty}(M)
, one has

\psi(x)=\lim_{\tau\to 0^{+}}\int_{M}p_{D}(\tau,x,y)\psi(y)dVol_{g}(y).

Hence, for any
\tau>0
and
x,y\in D
,

p_{D}(\tau,x,y)-P_{\kappa}(\tau,x,y)=\lim_{\epsilon\to 0^{+}}\int_{M}\int_{\epsilon}^{\tau-\epsilon}\frac{d}{ds}\left(p_{D}(s,x,z)P_{\kappa}(\tau-s,z,y)ds\right)dVol_{g}(z).

Hence, by Fubini’s theorem and the fact that
p_{D}
is a fundamental solution in
D
one has, for
x,y\in D
and
\tau>0
, that

\displaystyle p_{D}(\tau,x,y)-P_{\kappa}(\tau,x,y)

\displaystyle=\lim_{\epsilon\to 0^{+}}\int_{\epsilon}^{\tau-\epsilon}\int_{M}\bigg[P_{\kappa}(\tau-s,z,y)\partial_{t}p_{D}(s,x,z)-p_{D}(s,x,z)\partial_{t}P_{\kappa}(\tau-s,z,y)\bigg]dVol_{g}(z)ds

\displaystyle=\lim_{\epsilon\to 0^{+}}\int_{\epsilon}^{\tau-\epsilon}\int_{\bar{D}}\bigg[P_{\kappa}(\tau-s,z,y)\Delta_{g}^{z}p_{D}(s,x,z)-p_{D}(x,z,s)\partial_{t}P_{\kappa}(\tau-s,z,y)\bigg]dVol_{g}(z)ds.

Integrating by parts and using that
p_{D}(\tau,x,y)\equiv 0
for
y\in\partial D
while
\partial_{\nu}p_{D}(\tau,x,y)\leq 0
for
y\in\partial D
and
\nu
the outward normal to
D
yields, for
\tau>0
,

\displaystyle p_{D}(\tau,x,y)-P_{\kappa}(\tau,x,y)

\displaystyle\leq\lim_{\epsilon\to 0^{+}}\int_{\epsilon}^{\tau-\epsilon}\int_{M}-(\partial_{t}-\Delta_{g}^{z})P_{\kappa}(\tau-s,z,y)dVol_{g}(z)

\displaystyle\leq 0

where the second inequality follows from Lemma
4.3
. As
P_{\kappa}(\tau,x,y)>0
for
\tau>0
, it follows that

p_{D}(\tau,x,y)\leq P_{\kappa}(\tau,x,y)

for all
x,y\in M
and
\tau>0
.

As
D\in\mathcal{D}(M)
was arbitrary, we conclude that,

p_{M}(t,x,y)\leq P_{\kappa}(t,x,y)

for all
t=\tau>0
and
x,y\in M
.

For fixed
y_{0}\in M
, set

u(t,x)=P_{\kappa}(t,x,y_{0})-p_{M}(t,x,y_{0})\geq 0.

By Lemma
4.3
,
u
is a supersolution of the heat equation, i.e.,

(\partial_{t}-\Delta_{g})u\geq 0.

Moreover, the inequality is strict at some point unless
(M,g)
has constant sectional curvature
-\kappa^{2}
.
In particular, as
M
is connected, by the strong maximum principle, either
u(t,x)>0
for all
x\in M
and
t>0
or
u(t,x)\equiv 0
. In the latter case,
u=P_{\kappa}
is a solution of the heat equation and so
(M,g)
has constant sectional curvature
-\kappa^{2}
. If this occurs, then the Ricci curvature is bounded from below and there is a unique fundamental solution of the heat equation. Moreover,
p_{M}
and
P_{\kappa}
are both fundamental solutions and so
p_{M}=P_{\kappa}
is the heat kernel of
(M,g)
.
∎

Corollary 4.6
.

For
n\geq 2
,
K_{n,\kappa_{0}}(t,r)<K_{n,\kappa}(t,r)
for
0\leq\kappa<\kappa_{0}
and all

.

Remark 4.7
.

It is immediate from the definition that
K_{1,\kappa}=K_{1,0}
for all
\kappa\geq 0
.

Proof.

Observe that the space form
(\mathbb{H}^{n}(\kappa_{0}),g_{\mathbb{H}(\kappa_{0})})
with constant curvature
-\kappa^{2}_{0}
is a Cartan-Hadamard manifold with sectional curvature bounded above by
-{\kappa}^{2}
. Moreover, as
((\mathbb{H}^{n}(\kappa_{0}),g_{\mathbb{H}(\kappa_{0})})
is complete and has constant Ricci curvature, the space admits a unique heat kernel. In particular, for all
t>0
and
x,y\in M

p_{\mathbb{H}^{n}(\kappa_{0})}(t,x,y)=K_{n,\kappa_{0}}(t,d_{\mathbb{H}(\kappa_{0})}(x,y)).

Hence, Theorem
4.1
implies that, for
t>0
and all
x,y\in\mathbb{H}^{n}(\kappa_{0})
,

K_{n,{\kappa}_{0}}(t,d_{\mathbb{H}(\kappa_{0})}(x,y))=p_{\mathbb{H}^{n}(\kappa_{0})}(t,x,y)<K_{n,{\kappa}}(t,d_{\mathbb{H}(\kappa_{0})}(x,y)).

Clearly,
d_{\mathbb{H}(\kappa_{0})}(x,y)
achieves every value in

and the result follows.
∎

5.
Huisken Monotonicity in Cartan-Hadamard manifolds

Let
(M,g)
be an
(n+k)
-dimensional Cartan-Hadamard manifold with
\mathrm{sec}_{g}\leq-\kappa^{2}\leq 0
. We establish a form of Huisken monotonicity for well-behaved mean curvature flows in
M
. To that end, set

\Phi_{n,\kappa}^{t_{0},x_{0}}(t,x)=K_{n,\kappa}(t_{0}-t,\mathrm{dist}_{g}(x,x_{0}))=K_{n,\kappa}(t_{0}-t,\rho(x)).

When
k=0
, this is the backwards heat kernel on
(\mathbb{H}^{n}(\kappa),g_{\mathbb{H}(\kappa)})
, the space form of constant curvature
-\kappa^{2}
.
Observe that, for
x\neq x_{0}
,

\displaystyle\partial_{\rho}\Phi_{n,\kappa}^{t_{0},x_{0}}(t,x)=\partial_{r}K_{n,\kappa}(t_{0}-t,\rho(x))=\partial_{\rho}K_{n,\kappa}\leq 0,

where the second equality is a slight abuse of notation. Likewise,

\displaystyle\partial_{\rho}^{2}\Phi_{n,\kappa}^{t_{0},x_{0}}(t,x)=\partial_{r}^{2}K_{n,\kappa}(t_{0}-t,\rho(x))=\partial_{\rho}^{2}K_{n,\kappa}.

Computing as in Lemma
4.3
and using the above notion we obtain

\displaystyle\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial t}+\Delta_{g}\right)\Phi_{n,\kappa}^{t_{0},x_{0}}

\displaystyle=-\partial_{t}K_{n,\kappa}+\partial_{\rho}^{2}K_{n,\kappa}+\partial_{\rho}K_{n,\kappa}\Delta_{g}\rho

\displaystyle=-(n-1)\mathrm{ct}_{\kappa}(\rho)\partial_{\rho}K_{n,\kappa}+\partial_{\rho}K_{n,\kappa}\Delta_{g}\rho.

We may rewrite the second equality as

(5.1)

\displaystyle\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial t}+\Delta_{g}\right)\Phi_{n,\kappa}^{t_{0},x_{0}}=\left(\Delta_{g}\rho-(n-1)\mathrm{ct}_{\kappa}(\rho)\right)\partial_{\rho}\Phi_{n,\kappa}^{t_{0},x_{0}}.

While we do not use it in what follows, we remark that the estimates of Proposition
4.2
imply that

\displaystyle\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial t}+\Delta_{g}\right)\Phi_{n,\kappa}^{t_{0},x_{0}}\leq kct_{\kappa}(\rho)\partial_{\rho}\Phi_{n,\kappa}^{t_{0},x_{0}}\leq 0.

Moreover, the first inequality is strict at some point unless
(M,g)
is
(\mathbb{H}^{n+k}(\kappa),g_{\mathbb{H}(\kappa)})
and the
second inequality is strict unless
k=0
.

We compute the change of the integral of the kernel
\Phi_{n,\kappa}^{t_{0},x_{0}}
along a well-behaved mean curvature flow when paired with a compactly supported test function.

Proposition 5.1
.

Suppose
t\in[T_{1},T_{2})\mapsto\Sigma_{t}
is a locally smooth mean curvature flow in
M
of smooth properly embedded
n
-dimensional submanifolds. For any
t_{0}\in(T_{1},T_{2}]
,
x_{0}\in M
,
t\in[T_{1},t_{0})
and time varying test function
\psi\in C^{\infty}_{c}([T_{1},T_{2}]\times M)
one has

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\int_{\Sigma_{t}}

\displaystyle\psi\Phi_{n,\kappa}^{t_{0},x_{0}}dVol_{\Sigma_{t}}=\int_{\Sigma_{t}}\left(\frac{d}{dt}\psi-\Delta_{\Sigma_{t}}\psi\right)\Phi_{n,\kappa}^{t_{0},x_{0}}dVol_{\Sigma_{t}}

\displaystyle-\int_{\Sigma_{t}}\psi\left(\left|\frac{\nabla^{\perp}_{\Sigma_{t}}\Phi_{n,\kappa}^{t_{0},x_{0}}}{\Phi_{n,\kappa}^{t_{0},x_{0}}}-\mathbf{H}_{\Sigma_{t}}\right|^{2}+Q^{t_{0},x_{0}}_{n,\kappa}(t,x,N_{x}\Sigma_{t})\right)\Phi_{n,\kappa}^{t_{0},x_{0}}dVol_{\Sigma_{t}}.

Here,

Q^{t_{0},x_{0}}_{n,\kappa}(t,x,E)=\sum_{i=1}^{k}\nabla^{2}_{g}\log\Phi_{n,\kappa}^{t_{0},x_{0}}(E_{i},E_{i})+\left((n-1)\mathrm{ct}_{\kappa}(\rho)-\Delta_{g}\rho\right)\partial_{\rho}\log\Phi_{n,\kappa}^{t_{0},x_{0}}

where
\rho(x)=\mathrm{dist}_{g}(x,x_{0})
,
E
is a
k
-dimensional subspace of
T_{x}M
and
\left\{E_{1},\ldots,E_{k}\right\}
is an orthonormal basis of
E
.

Proof.

Recall, that for any
f\in C^{1}([T_{1},T_{2})\times M)
and
x\in\Sigma_{t}
we define

\frac{d}{dt}f(t,x)=\frac{\partial}{\partial t}f(t,x)+\mathbf{H}_{\Sigma_{t}}\cdot\nabla_{g}f(t,x).

As in
[
16
, Chapter 3]
, first variation formula yields,

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\int_{\Sigma_{t}}

\displaystyle\psi\Phi_{n,\kappa}^{t_{0},x_{0}}dVol_{\Sigma_{t}}=\int_{\Sigma_{t}}\bigg[\frac{d}{dt}\bigg(\psi\Phi_{n,\kappa}^{t_{0},x_{0}}\bigg)-\psi\Phi_{n,\kappa}^{t_{0},x_{0}}|\mathbf{H}_{\Sigma_{t}}|^{2}\bigg]dVol_{\Sigma_{t}}

\displaystyle=\int_{\Sigma_{t}}\bigg[\Phi_{n,\kappa}^{t_{0},x_{0}}\frac{d}{dt}\psi+\psi\frac{d}{dt}\Phi_{n,\kappa}^{t_{0},x_{0}}-\psi|\mathbf{H}_{\Sigma_{t}}|^{2}\Phi_{n,\kappa}^{t_{0},x_{0}}\bigg]dVol_{\Sigma_{t}}

\displaystyle=\int_{\Sigma_{t}}\bigg[\Phi_{n,\kappa}^{t_{0},x_{0}}\bigg(\frac{d}{dt}-\Delta_{{\Sigma_{t}}}\bigg)\psi+\psi\bigg(\frac{d}{dt}+\Delta_{{\Sigma_{t}}}\bigg)\Phi_{n,\kappa}^{t_{0},x_{0}}-\psi|\mathbf{H}_{\Sigma_{t}}|^{2}\Phi_{n,\kappa}^{t_{0},x_{0}}\bigg]dVol_{\Sigma_{t}}

where the last equality follows from integrating by parts and we used throughout that
\Sigma_{t}
is properly embedded and
\psi(t,\cdot)
has compact support.

Standard geometric computations imply that on
\Sigma_{t}
,

\Delta_{g}\Phi_{n,\kappa}^{t_{0},x_{0}}=\Delta_{{\Sigma_{t}}}\Phi_{n,\kappa}^{t_{0},x_{0}}-\mathbf{H}_{\Sigma_{t}}\cdot\nabla_{g}\Phi_{n,\kappa}^{t_{0},x_{0}}+\sum_{i=1}^{k}\nabla^{2}_{g}\Phi_{n,\kappa}^{t_{0},x_{0}}(E_{i},E_{i})

where
\left\{E_{1},\ldots,E_{k}\right\}
is an orthonormal basis of
N_{x}\Sigma_{t}\subset T_{x}M
.
Hence,

\displaystyle\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial t}+\Delta_{\Sigma_{t}}\right)\Phi_{n,\kappa}^{t_{0},x_{0}}

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial t}+\Delta_{g}\right)\Phi_{n,\kappa}^{t_{0},x_{0}}+\mathbf{H}_{\Sigma_{t}}\cdot\nabla_{g}\Phi_{n,\kappa}^{t_{0},x_{0}}-\sum_{i=1}^{k}\nabla^{2}_{g}\Phi_{n,\kappa}^{t_{0},x_{0}}(E_{i},E_{i}).

It follows from (
5.1
) that

(5.2)

\displaystyle\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial t}+\Delta_{\Sigma_{t}}\right)

\displaystyle\Phi_{n,\kappa}^{t_{0},x_{0}}=\left(\Delta_{g}\rho-(n-1)\mathrm{ct}_{\kappa}(\rho)\right)\partial_{\rho}\Phi_{n,\kappa}^{t_{0},x_{0}}

\displaystyle+\mathbf{H}_{\Sigma_{t}}\cdot\nabla_{{g}}\Phi_{n,\kappa}^{t_{0},x_{0}}-\sum_{i=1}^{k}\nabla^{2}_{g}\Phi_{n,\kappa}^{t_{0},x_{0}}(E_{i},E_{i})

\displaystyle=\mathbf{H}_{\Sigma_{t}}\cdot\nabla_{g}\Phi_{n,\kappa}^{t_{0},x_{0}}-Q_{n,\kappa}^{t_{0},x_{0}}(t,x,N_{x}\Sigma_{t})\Phi_{n,\kappa}^{t_{0},x_{0}}-\frac{|\nabla_{\Sigma_{t}}^{\perp}\Phi_{n,\kappa}^{t_{0},x_{0}}|^{2}}{\Phi_{n,\kappa}^{t_{0},x_{0}}},

where the second equality uses

\nabla_{\Sigma_{t}}^{2}\log\Phi_{n,\kappa}^{t_{0},x_{0}}=\frac{\nabla_{\Sigma_{t}}^{2}\Phi_{n,\kappa}^{t_{0},x_{0}}}{\Phi_{n,\kappa}^{t_{0},x_{0}}}-\frac{d\Phi_{n,\kappa}^{t_{0},x_{0}}\otimes d\Phi_{n,\kappa}^{t_{0},x_{0}}}{(\Phi_{n,\kappa}^{t_{0},x_{0}})^{2}}.

Hence, by putting everything together, one has

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\int_{\Sigma_{t}}

\displaystyle\psi\Phi_{n,\kappa}^{t_{0},x_{0}}dVol_{\Sigma_{t}}=\int_{\Sigma_{t}}\bigg[\frac{d}{dt}\psi-\Delta_{\Sigma_{t}}\psi\bigg]\Phi_{n,\kappa}^{t_{0},x_{0}}dVol_{\Sigma_{t}}

\displaystyle+\int_{\Sigma_{t}}\psi\bigg[\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial t}+\Delta_{\Sigma_{t}}\right)\Phi_{n,\kappa}^{t_{0},x_{0}}+\nabla_{g}\Phi_{n,\kappa}^{t_{0},x_{0}}\cdot\mathbf{H}_{\Sigma_{t}}-|\mathbf{H}_{\Sigma_{t}}|^{2}\Phi_{n,\kappa}^{t_{0},x_{0}}\bigg]dVol_{\Sigma_{t}}

\displaystyle=\int_{\Sigma_{t}}\bigg[\frac{d}{dt}\psi-\Delta_{\Sigma_{t}}\psi\bigg]\Phi_{n,\kappa}^{t_{0},x_{0}}dVol_{\Sigma_{t}}

\displaystyle-\int_{\Sigma_{t}}\psi\left(\left|\frac{\nabla_{\Sigma_{t}}^{\perp}\Phi_{n,\kappa}^{t_{0},x_{0}}}{\Phi_{n,\kappa}^{t_{0},x_{0}}}-\mathbf{H}_{\Sigma_{t}}\right|^{2}+Q_{n,\kappa}^{t_{0},x_{0}}(t,x,N_{x}\Sigma_{t})\right)\Phi_{n,\kappa}^{t_{0},x_{0}}dVol_{\Sigma_{t}}.

This completes the proof.
∎

We use the super-convexity estimate of Proposition

and the Hessian comparison of Proposition

to see that

has a sign.

Lemma 5.2
.

Fix
x_{0}\in M
and
t_{0}\in\mathbb{R}
and choose

,
t<t_{0}
and a
k

. The inequality

Q_{n,\kappa}^{t_{0},x_{0}}(t,x,E)\geq 0

holds, where

Q_{n,\kappa}^{t_{0},x_{0}}(t,x,E)=\sum_{i=1}^{k}\nabla^{2}_{g}\log\Phi_{n,\kappa}^{t_{0},x_{0}}(E_{i},E_{i})+\left((n-1)\mathrm{ct}_{\kappa}(\rho)-\Delta_{g}\rho\right)\partial_{\rho}\log\Phi_{n,\kappa}^{t_{0},x_{0}},

\rho=\mathrm{dist}_{g}(\cdot,x_{0})
and
E_{1},\ldots,E_{n+k}
is an orthonormal basis of
T_{x}M
with
E=\mathrm{span}\left\{E_{1}\ldots,E_{k}\right\}
. Moreover, the inequality is strict unless both:

(1)

\sec_{g}(E_{i}(t)\wedge\gamma^{\prime}(t))=-\kappa^{2}
, where
\gamma:[0,\rho(x)]\to M
is the unique minimizing geodesic connecting
x_{0}
and
x
,
E_{i}(t)
is the parallel transport of
E_{i}
along
\gamma
and
k+1\leq i\leq n+k
is such that
\gamma^{\prime}(\rho(x))=\nabla_{g}\rho(x)
is not parallel to
E_{i}
;

(2)

Either
\kappa=0
or
g(E_{i},\nabla_{g}\rho(x))=0
, for all
1\leq i\leq k
.

Proof.

We first compute that, for
\kappa>0
,

\displaystyle\partial_{r}^{2}\log K_{n,\kappa}-\mathrm{ct}_{\kappa}(r)\partial_{r}\log K_{n,\kappa}

\displaystyle=\kappa^{2+n}\left(\partial^{2}_{r}\log K_{n}(t,\kappa r)-\coth(\kappa r)\partial_{r}\log K_{n}(t,\kappa r)\right).

Hence, by Proposition
3.1
, one has, for
\kappa>0
,

\partial_{r}^{2}\log K_{n,\kappa}(t,r)-\mathrm{ct}_{\kappa}(r)\partial_{r}\log K_{n,\kappa}(t,r)\geq 0

with strict inequality when

\kappa=0
and

one directly computes,

\partial_{r}^{2}\log K_{n,0}-\mathrm{ct}_{0}(r)\partial_{r}\log K_{n,0}=\partial_{r}^{2}(-\log r)-\frac{1}{r}\partial_{r}(-\log r)=0.

As such, for all
\kappa\geq 0
,

\partial_{r}^{2}\log K_{n,\kappa}-\mathrm{ct}_{\kappa}(r)\partial_{r}\log K_{n,\kappa}\geq 0

with equality only when

or
\kappa=0
.

Abusing notation as above, we readily compute,

\displaystyle\nabla^{2}_{g}\log\Phi_{n,\kappa}^{t_{0},x_{0}}

\displaystyle=(\partial^{2}_{\rho}\log K_{n,\kappa})d\rho^{2}+(\partial_{\rho}\log K_{n,\kappa})\nabla_{g}^{2}\rho.

Hence, continuing to abuse notation,

\displaystyle Q_{n,\kappa}^{t_{0},x_{0}}(t,x,E)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{k}(\partial^{2}_{\rho}\log K_{n,\kappa})(d\rho(E_{i}))^{2}+(n-1)(\partial_{\rho}\log K_{n,\kappa})\mathrm{ct}_{\kappa}(\rho)

\displaystyle-(\partial_{\rho}\log K_{n,\kappa})\sum_{i=k+1}^{n+k}(\nabla^{2}_{g}\rho)(E_{i},E_{i})

\displaystyle\geq\sum_{i=1}^{k}(\partial^{2}_{\rho}\log K_{n,\kappa})(d\rho(E_{i}))^{2}+(n-1)(\partial_{\rho}\log K_{n,\kappa})\mathrm{ct}_{\kappa}(\rho)

\displaystyle-(\partial_{\rho}\log K_{n,\kappa})\sum_{i=k+1}^{n+k}\mathrm{ct}_{\kappa}(\rho)(g(E_{i},E_{i})-(d\rho(E_{i}))^{2})

where the inequality used the fact that
-\partial_{\rho}\log K_{n,\kappa}\geq 0
and Proposition
4.2
. Moreover, as
-\partial_{\rho}\log K_{n,\kappa}>0
for
x\neq x_{0}
, Proposition
4.2
further implies that this inequality is strict unless case (
1
) holds.
As

1=|\nabla_{g}\rho|^{2}=\sum_{i=1}^{n+k}(d\rho(E_{i}))^{2},

we obtain

\displaystyle Q_{n,\kappa}^{t_{0},x_{0}}(t,x,E)

\displaystyle\geq(\partial^{2}_{\rho}\log K_{n,\kappa})\sum_{i=1}^{k}(d\rho(E_{i}))^{2}-\mathrm{ct}_{\kappa}(\rho)\partial_{\rho}\log K_{n,\kappa}

\displaystyle+\mathrm{ct}_{\kappa}(\rho)\partial_{\rho}\log K_{n,\kappa}\sum_{i=k+1}^{n+k}(d\rho(E_{i}))^{2}

\displaystyle=(\partial^{2}_{\rho}\log K_{n,\kappa}-\mathrm{ct}_{\kappa}(\rho)\partial_{\rho}\log K_{n,\kappa})\sum_{i=1}^{k}(d\rho(E_{i}))^{2}\geq 0.

Here the final inequality follows from the convexity property of
K_{n,\kappa}
. One has equality in the final inequality only when
\kappa=0
or when
\kappa>0
and

\sum_{i=1}^{k}(d\rho(E_{i}))^{2}=0.

That is, only when
\kappa=0
or when
g(E_{i},\nabla_{g}\rho)=0
for all

.
∎

We will need the following characterization of geodesic cones in Cartan-Hadamard manifolds.

Lemma 5.3
.

Let
\Sigma\subset M
be a complete and proper
n
-dimensional submanifold and
x_{0}\in M
. If
\rho=\mathrm{dist}_{g}(\cdot,x_{0})
and
|\nabla_{\Sigma}^{\perp}\rho|=0
on
\Sigma\setminus\left\{x_{0}\right\}
, then
\Sigma
is a geodesic cone over
x_{0}
.

Proof.

First observe that,
\Sigma
is connected and
x_{0}\in\Sigma
. Indeed, if
\Sigma
was not connected or
x_{0}\not\in\Sigma
, then there would be a component
\Sigma^{\prime}
that does not contain
x_{0}
. As
\Sigma
is proper, this implies the existence of a point
x^{\prime}\in\Sigma^{\prime}
at which
\rho
achieves its (non-zero) minimum. As this is a regular point of
\rho
, one must have
\nabla_{\Sigma}\rho=0
at this point and so
|\nabla^{\perp}_{\Sigma}\rho|(x^{\prime})=1
, a contradiction.

Let
f_{\kappa}(r)
be given by (
4.1
). We have

\nabla^{2}_{g}f_{\kappa}(\rho)=f^{\prime}_{\kappa}(\rho)\nabla^{2}_{g}\rho+(1+\kappa^{2}f_{\kappa}(\rho))d\rho^{2}.

Using
f_{\kappa}^{\prime}\geq 0
and the lower bound on
\nabla_{g}^{2}\rho
given by Proposition
4.2
yields

\nabla^{2}_{g}f_{\kappa}(\rho)\geq(1+\kappa^{2}f_{\kappa}(\rho))g.

Hence, the hypothesis that
\nabla_{\Sigma}^{\perp}\rho=0
ensures
\nabla_{\Sigma}^{\perp}f_{\kappa}(\rho)=0
and

\nabla_{\Sigma}^{2}f_{\kappa}(\rho)\geq(1+\kappa^{2}f_{\kappa}(\rho))g_{\Sigma}.

As
\Sigma
is connected, complete and contains
x_{0}
, for each
x\in\Sigma\setminus\left\{x_{0}\right\}
, there is a
\gamma:[0,L]\to\Sigma
which is a minimizing geodesic in
\Sigma
parameterized by arclength that connects
x_{0}
to
x
. That is,
\gamma
satisfies
\gamma(0)=x_{0}
and
\gamma^{\prime}(s)
is of unit length.
One computes,

\frac{d^{2}}{ds^{2}}f_{\kappa}(\rho(\gamma(s)))=\nabla^{2}_{\Sigma}f_{\kappa}(\rho)(\gamma^{\prime}(s),\gamma^{\prime}(s))\geq 1+\kappa^{2}f_{\kappa}(\rho(\gamma(s))).

Set

g(s)=f_{\kappa}(\rho(\gamma(s)))

so we have
g(0)=f_{\kappa}(0)=0
and
g^{\prime}(0)=f_{\kappa}^{\prime}(0)=0
and

g^{\prime\prime}(s)\geq 1+\kappa^{2}g(s).

As
f_{\kappa}^{\prime\prime}=1+\kappa^{2}f_{\kappa}
, standard ODE comparison implies that

f_{\kappa}(\rho(\gamma(s)))=g(s)\geq f_{\kappa}(s).

As
f_{\kappa}
is strictly monotone on the positive reals it follows that,
\rho(\gamma(s))\geq s
. This means

(5.3)

\mathrm{dist}_{g}(x,x_{0})=\rho(x)=\rho(\gamma(L))\geq L=\mathrm{dist}_{\Sigma}(x,x_{0}).

This is only possible if
\gamma
is also a minimizing geodesic in
(M,g)
and we have equality throughout (
5.3
). In particular,
\gamma
is the unique unit speed geodesic connecting
x_{0}
to
x
and lies entirely within
\Sigma
. As
x
is arbitrary,
\Sigma
must be a cone over
x_{0}
.
∎

Theorem 5.4
.

Suppose
t\in[T_{1},T_{2})\mapsto\Sigma_{t}
is a locally smooth mean curvature flow in
M
of properly embedded
n
-dimensional submanifolds with exponential volume growth. For any
t_{0}\in(T_{1},T_{2}]
,
x_{0}\in M
, and
T_{1}\leq t_{1}\leq t_{2}<t_{0}
if
Ric_{g}\geq-\Lambda^{2}g
on
\Sigma_{t}
, for all
t\in[t_{1},t_{2}]
and a uniform
\Lambda\geq 0
, then

\displaystyle\int_{\Sigma_{t_{2}}}\Phi_{n,\kappa}^{t_{0},x_{0}}(t,x)dVol_{\Sigma_{t_{2}}}(x)

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\Sigma_{t_{1}}}\Phi_{n,\kappa}^{t_{0},x_{0}}(t,x)dVol_{\Sigma_{t_{1}}}(x).

When
\kappa>0
, the inequality is strict unless
\Sigma_{t}
is a geodesic cone over
x_{0}
that is a totally geodesic submanifold isometric to
\mathbb{H}^{n}({\kappa})
.

Remark 5.5
.

If all
\Sigma_{t}
remain in a fixed compact subset of
M
, then there automatically exists such a constant
\Lambda
. For instance, this is automatic if one has a classical mean curvature flow of a closed hypersurface on a compact time interval.

In general, without a lower bound on the Ricci curvature, the result does not hold. For instance, consider the metric in polar coordinates on
M=\mathbb{R}^{2}

g=dr^{2}+r^{2}e^{\frac{1}{2}r^{4}}d\theta^{2}.

Computing the Gauss curvature one has

K(r,\theta)=-(5r^{2}+r^{6})\leq 0.

Clearly, all geodesics rays from
0
are of infinite length and so the metric is complete by the Hopf-Rinow theorem. Hence,

(\mathbb{R}^{2},g)
is a Cartan-Hadamard manifold.
Moreover, the curves
\sigma_{R}=\partial\mathcal{B}_{R}^{g}(0)
for
R>0
are of constant geodesic curvature (with respect to the outward normal) and have curvature

H_{\sigma_{R}}=\frac{1}{R}+R^{3}.

It follows that if,

t\mapsto R(t)=\sqrt{\tan(-2t)}

for
t\in\left(-\frac{\pi}{4},0\right)
, then

t\mapsto\partial\mathcal{B}_{R(t)}^{g}(0)

is a classical mean curvature flow.
However, as
\lim_{t\to-\frac{\pi}{4}^{+}}R(t)=\infty
, we can create a locally smooth mean curvature flow that is not a classical flow by extending the time of the flow to
(-\infty,0)
by setting

t\mapsto\Sigma_{t}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{cc}\emptyset&t\leq-\frac{\pi}{4}\\
\partial\mathcal{B}_{R(t)}^{g}(0)&t\in\left(-\frac{\pi}{4},0\right).\end{array}\right.

This is an example of a flow “blossoming from infinity” mentioned in
[
23
]
and is also one for which the monotonicity formula does not hold across
t=-\frac{\pi}{4}
.

Proof.

To verify the inequality we use Proposition
5.1
and Lemma
5.2
together with appropriately chosen test functions
\psi_{R}
.
First pick a smooth cutoff
\chi:(-\infty,\infty)\to[0,1]

so
\chi(s)=1
for
s\leq 1
,
\chi(s)=0
for
s\geq 2
,
\chi^{\prime}\leq 0
, and

|\chi^{\prime}|+|\chi^{\prime\prime}|\leq 100.

For
R\geq 1
, let
\chi_{R}(s)=\chi(R^{-1}s)
. It follows that
\chi_{R}
is supported in
(-\infty,2R]
,
\chi_{R}^{\prime}\leq 0
,
\chi_{R}^{\prime}
and
\chi_{R}^{\prime\prime}
are supported in
[R,2R]
, and

R|\chi_{R}^{\prime}|+R^{2}|\chi^{\prime\prime}_{R}|\leq 100\chi_{2R}.

Fix a point
x_{0}\in M
and, for
R\geq 1
, let

\psi_{R}(x)=\chi_{R}(\rho(x))=\chi_{R}(\mathrm{dist}_{g}(x,x_{0})).

Observe that this is compactly supported in
\bar{\mathcal{B}}_{2R}^{g}(x_{0})
.
We compute that

\Delta_{\Sigma_{t}}\psi_{R}=\Delta_{g}\psi_{R}+\mathbf{H}_{\Sigma_{t}}\cdot\nabla_{g}\psi_{R}-\sum_{i=1}^{k}\nabla^{2}_{g}\psi_{R}(E_{i},E_{i}),

where
\left\{E_{1},\ldots,E_{k}\right\}
is an orthonormal basis of
N_{x}\Sigma_{t}\subset T_{x}M
.
Hence, on
\Sigma_{t}

\left(\frac{d}{dt}-\Delta_{\Sigma_{t}}\right)\psi_{R}=-\Delta_{g}\psi_{R}+\sum_{i=1}^{k}\nabla^{2}_{g}\psi_{R}(E_{i},E_{i}).

One computes that

\nabla_{g}^{2}\psi_{R}=\chi^{\prime\prime}_{R}d\rho\otimes d\rho+\chi^{\prime}_{R}\nabla^{2}_{g}\rho.

By the Laplace comparison theorem (e.g.,
[
30
]
), the lower bound on Ricci curvature, the Hessian comparison theorem, the fact that
g
is Cartan-Hadamard, and
\chi^{\prime}\leq 0
, we conclude that on
\Sigma_{t}
, for
R\geq 1
,

\displaystyle\left(\frac{d}{dt}-\Delta_{\Sigma_{t}}\right)\psi_{R}

\displaystyle\leq-\chi^{\prime\prime}_{R}|\nabla_{\Sigma_{t}}\rho|^{2}-(n-1)\chi^{\prime}_{R}\mathrm{ct}_{\Lambda}(\rho)-\chi^{\prime}_{R}\mathrm{ct}_{0}(\rho)(k-|\nabla^{\perp}_{\Sigma_{t}}\rho|^{2})

\displaystyle\leq 100\left(R^{-2}+(n-1)R^{-1}\Lambda+kR^{-2}\right)\chi_{2R}

\displaystyle\leq CR^{-1}\psi_{2R}

where the second inequality used that
\chi^{\prime}_{R}
and
\chi^{\prime\prime}_{R}
are supported on
[R,2R]
and
C=(n,k,\Lambda)
.

For each
R\geq 1
, Proposition
5.1
implies

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\int_{\Sigma_{t}}

\displaystyle\psi_{R}\Phi_{n,\kappa}^{t_{0},x_{0}}dVol_{\Sigma_{t}}=\int_{\Sigma_{t}}\left(\left(\frac{d}{dt}-\Delta_{\Sigma_{t}}\right)\psi_{R}\right)\Phi_{n,\kappa}^{t_{0},x_{0}}dVol_{\Sigma_{t}}

\displaystyle-\int_{\Sigma_{t}}\psi_{R}\left(\left|\frac{\nabla^{\perp}_{\Sigma_{t}}\Phi_{n,\kappa}^{t_{0},x_{0}}}{\Phi_{n,\kappa}^{t_{0},x_{0}}}-\mathbf{H}_{\Sigma_{t}}\right|^{2}+Q^{t_{0},x_{0}}_{n,\kappa}\right)\Phi_{n,\kappa}^{t_{0},x_{0}}dVol_{\Sigma_{t}}.

Integrating and using the above estimate with the fact that
\psi_{R}
is non-negative yields

\displaystyle\int_{\Sigma_{t_{2}}}

\displaystyle\psi_{R}\Phi_{n,\kappa}^{t_{0},x_{0}}dVol_{\Sigma_{t_{2}}}+\int_{t_{1}}^{t_{2}}\int_{\Sigma_{t}}\psi_{R}\left(\left|\frac{\nabla^{\perp}_{\Sigma_{t}}\Phi_{n,\kappa}^{t_{0},x_{0}}}{\Phi_{n,\kappa}^{t_{0},x_{0}}}-\mathbf{H}_{\Sigma_{t}}\right|^{2}+Q^{t_{0},x_{0}}_{n,\kappa}\right)\Phi_{n,\kappa}^{t_{0},x_{0}}dVol_{\Sigma_{t}}dt

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\Sigma_{t_{1}}}\psi_{R}\Phi_{n,\kappa}^{t_{0},x_{0}}dVol_{\Sigma_{t_{1}}}+CR^{-1}\int_{t_{1}}^{t_{2}}\int_{\Sigma_{t}}\psi_{2R}\Phi_{n,\kappa}^{t_{0},x_{0}}dVol_{\Sigma_{t}}dt.

Applying the monotone convergence theorem and using the exponential volume growth of
\Sigma_{t}
, along with the fact that
\psi_{R}
is bounded, for any
t\in[t_{1},t_{2}]
one has

\lim_{R\to\infty}\int_{\Sigma_{t}}\psi_{R}\Phi_{n,\kappa}^{t_{0},x_{0}}dVol_{\Sigma_{t}}=\lim_{R\to\infty}\int_{\Sigma_{t}}\psi_{2R}\Phi_{n,\kappa}^{t_{0},x_{0}}dVol_{\Sigma_{t}}=\int_{\Sigma_{t}}\Phi_{n,\kappa}^{t_{0},x_{0}}dVol_{\Sigma_{t}}<\infty.

Hence, letting
R\to\infty
and appealing to Lemma
5.2
and the monotone convergence theorem, one concludes

(5.4)

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle\leq\int_{t_{1}}^{t_{2}}\int_{\Sigma_{t}}\left(\left|\frac{\nabla^{\perp}_{\Sigma_{t}}\Phi_{n,\kappa}^{t_{0},x_{0}}}{\Phi_{n,\kappa}^{t_{0},x_{0}}}-\mathbf{H}_{\Sigma_{t}}\right|^{2}+Q^{t_{0},x_{0}}_{n,\kappa}\right)\Phi_{n,\kappa}^{t_{0},x_{0}}dVol_{\Sigma_{t}}dt

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\Sigma_{t_{1}}}\Phi_{n,\kappa}^{t_{0},x_{0}}dVol_{\Sigma_{t_{1}}}-\int_{\Sigma_{t_{2}}}\Phi_{n,\kappa}^{t_{0},x_{0}}dVol_{\Sigma_{t_{2}}},

which immediately proves the main inequality.

It remains only to characterize the case of equality.
To that end, first observe that
Q_{n,\kappa}^{t_{0},x_{0}}(t,x,N_{x}\Sigma_{t})
is continuous in
x\neq x_{0}
and so if
Q_{n,\kappa}^{t_{0},x_{0}}(t^{\prime},x^{\prime},N_{x^{\prime}}\Sigma_{t})>0
at a point
x^{\prime}\in\Sigma_{t^{\prime}}\setminus\left\{x_{0}\right\}
for
t^{\prime}\in(t_{1},t_{2})
, then
it is positive in a neighborhood of
x^{\prime}
in
\Sigma_{t}
. In fact, it is positive in a spacetime neighborhood of
(t^{\prime},x^{\prime})
and so

\displaystyle 0<\int_{t_{1}}^{t_{2}}\int_{\Sigma_{t}}\left(\left|\frac{\nabla^{\perp}_{\Sigma_{t}}\Phi_{n,\kappa}^{t_{0},x_{0}}}{\Phi_{n,\kappa}^{t_{0},x_{0}}}-\mathbf{H}_{\Sigma_{t}}\right|^{2}+Q^{t_{0},x_{0}}_{n,\kappa}\right)\Phi_{n,\kappa}^{t_{0},x_{0}}dVol_{\Sigma_{t}}dt.

Thus, equality can occur only when
Q_{n,\kappa}^{t_{0},x_{0}}(t,x,N_{x}\Sigma_{t})=0
for all
x\in\Sigma_{t}\setminus\left\{x_{0}\right\}
and
t\in(t_{1},t_{2})
. As
\kappa>0
, Lemma
5.2
implies that at all such
x
,

g(\nabla_{g}\rho,E_{i})=0

where
E_{i}\in N_{x}\Sigma_{t}
. That is,

|\nabla_{g}^{\perp}\rho|=0

on
\Sigma_{t}\setminus\left\{x_{0}\right\}
. By Lemma
5.3
,
\Sigma_{t}
is a cone over
x_{0}
that is totally geodesic. As
\Sigma_{t}
is assumed to be smooth at
x_{0}
it is actually a totally geodesic submanifold. Hence,
\Sigma_{t}
is also minimal and so the flow is static. For notational simplicity let us set
\Sigma^{\prime}=\Sigma_{t}
and observe we can think of
\Sigma^{\prime}_{s}=\Sigma
,
s\in\mathbb{R}
as a static solution to mean curvature flow which agrees with
\Sigma_{t}
for
t\in(t_{1},t_{2})
. The minimality of
\Sigma^{\prime}
along with the fact that
\nabla_{g}^{\perp}\rho=0
on
\Sigma^{\prime}
and the computation (

) imply that

\left(\frac{d}{dt}+\Delta_{\Sigma^{\prime}}\right)\Phi_{n,\kappa}^{t_{0},x_{0}}=0.

This means
H_{n,\kappa}(t,x)=\Phi_{n,\kappa}^{t_{0},x_{0}}(-t,x)
solves the linear heat equation exactly on
\Sigma^{\prime}
. Again using the fact that
\Sigma^{\prime}
is totally geodesic the Gauss equations imply that

\sec_{\Sigma^{\prime}}(P)=\sec_{g}(P)\leq-\kappa^{2}

for any two-plane
P\subset T_{x}\Sigma^{\prime}\subset T_{x}M
.
Hence, by Lemma
4.3
, as
H_{n,\kappa}(t,x)
is an exact solution to the heat equation on
\Sigma^{\prime}
it must be the case that
\Sigma^{\prime}
has constant curvature
-\kappa^{2}
. That is,
\Sigma_{t}=\Sigma^{\prime}
is a totally geodesic smooth cone that is isometric to
\mathbb{H}^{n}(\kappa)
, the space-form of constant curvature
-\kappa^{2}
.
∎

As a consequence of this monotonicity, we may use the kernels
\Phi_{n,\kappa}^{t_{0},x_{0}}
to define Gaussian density for mean curvature flows in Cartan-Hadamard manifolds as well as conclude the existence of tangent flows modeled on (singular) self-shrinkers in Euclidean space. Before stating the next corollary, for the convenience of the readers, we first recall the definition of Gaussian density given by White
[

]
. For

, let

be defined by

\displaystyle G(t,x)=K_{n,0}(-t,|{x}|)=\frac{1}{(-4\pi t)^{\frac{m}{2}}}e^{-\frac{|x|^{2}}{-4t}}\text{ and }\hat{G}(t,x)=\chi(|x|)G(t,x),

where
\chi
is smooth and compactly supported in
[0,1)
with
\chi=1
on
[0,\frac{1}{2}]
and
\chi^{\prime}\leq 0
.

Definition 5.6
.

[
37
, Definition 6.3]

Given a locally smooth mean curvature flow of properly embedded
n
-dimensional submanifolds

\mathcal{M}=t\in[T_{1},T_{2})\to\Sigma_{t}\subset M

and a point
x_{0}\in M
and a time

, the
Gaussian density
of
\mathcal{M}
at
(t_{0},x_{0})
is defined to be

\Theta(\mathcal{M};(t_{0},x_{0}))=\lim_{t\to t_{0}^{-}}\int_{i(\Sigma_{t})}\hat{G}(t,x)dVol_{i(\Sigma_{t})}

where
i:M\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
is an isometric embedding of
M
. This value is well defined and independent of the choice of
\chi
and

.

Corollary 5.7
.

Suppose
\mathcal{M}=t\in[T_{1},T_{2})\mapsto\Sigma_{t}
is a locally smooth mean curvature flow in
M
of
n
-dimensional submanifolds with exponential volume growth and
Ric_{g}\geq-\Lambda^{2}g
on
\Sigma_{t}
, for
t\in[t_{1},t_{2}]
and a uniform
\Lambda\geq 0
. For any
x_{0}\in M
and
t_{0}\in(T_{1},T_{2}]
, there is a well defined and finite limit

\Theta_{\kappa}(t_{0},x_{0})=\lim_{t\to t_{0}^{-}}\int_{\Sigma_{t}}\Phi_{n,\kappa}^{t_{0},x_{0}}(t,x)dVol_{\Sigma_{t}}(x)\leq\int_{\Sigma_{T_{1}}}\Phi_{n,\kappa}^{t_{0},x_{0}}(t,x)dVol_{\Sigma_{T_{1}}}(x).

Moreover, this quantity agrees with the Gaussian density of the flow at
(t_{0},x_{0})
. As a consequence, for any sequence of times
t_{i}\to t_{0}^{-}
, up to passing to a subsequence, there is a self-shrinking integer rectifiable
n
-varifold
V
in
\mathbb{R}^{n+k}
so

\Theta_{\kappa}(t_{0},x_{0})=\lambda[V]=\int\Phi_{n,0}^{0,0}(-1,x)d\mu_{V}(x)=\Theta(\mathcal{M},(t_{0},x_{0}))

and
\Sigma_{t_{i}}
converges to
V
in an appropriate sense, i.e., after natural identifications, rescalings of
\Sigma_{t_{i}}
converge in the sense of varifolds to
V
.

Proof.

The existence and finiteness of the limit is an immediate consequence of Theorem
5.4
. As the flow has exponential volume growth, it follows from (
3.3
) that for any
R>0
one has

\Theta_{\kappa}(t_{0},x_{0})=\lim_{t\to t_{0}^{-}}\int_{\Sigma_{t}\cap\mathcal{B}_{R}^{g}(x_{0})}\Phi_{n,\kappa}^{t_{0},x_{0}}(t,x)dVol_{\Sigma_{t}}(x).

It then follows from Lemma
4.4
that
\Theta_{\kappa}(t_{0},x_{0})
agrees with the Gaussian density – here the uniform bound on
\Theta(t)
on
\Sigma_{t}
is a consequence of a straightforward modification of
[
16
, Proposition 3.9]
and
[
37
, Theorem 6.1]
. The remaining claims then follow from
[
37
, Section 6 and 11]
and
[
25
, Theorem 10.2]
.

∎

6.
Colding-Minicozzi
\kappa
-Entropy

Fix an
(n+k)
-dimensional Cartan-Hadamard manifold
(M,g)
. Let
\Sigma\subset M
be a submanifold of dimension
n
. Recall that, for any
\kappa\geq 0
, the Colding-Minicozzi
\kappa
-entropy of
\Sigma
in
(M,g)
is defined to be

\lambda_{g}^{\kappa}[\Sigma]=\sup_{x_{0}\in M,\tau>0}\int_{\Sigma}\Phi_{n,\kappa}^{0,x_{0}}(-\tau,x)dVol_{\Sigma}=\sup_{x_{0}\in M,\tau>0}\int_{\Sigma}\Phi_{n,\kappa}^{\tau,x_{0}}(0,x)dVol_{\Sigma}.

It follows from Theorem
5.4
that the
\kappa
-entropy is monotone along well-behaved mean curvature flows in a Cartan-Hadamard manifold with sectional curvature bounded from above by
-\kappa^{2}
. However, the quantity makes sense independent of its relationship with mean curvature flow and we first record some elementary properties of
\kappa
-entropies that hold on any Cartan-Hadamard manifold.

6.1.
Elementary Properties of
\kappa
-Entropies

In what follows we suppose
(M,g)
is an
(n+k)
-dimensional Cartan-Hadamard manifold.

Proposition 6.1
.

If
\Sigma\subset M
is an
n
-dimensional submanifold and
\lambda_{g}^{\kappa}[\Sigma]<\infty
for some
\kappa
, then
\Sigma
has exponential volume growth. If
\lambda_{g}^{0}[\Sigma]<\infty
, then
\Sigma
has polynomial volume growth.

In fact, for
n\geq 2,\kappa>0
and
R\geq\kappa^{-1}
we have the growth estimate:

Vol_{g}(\mathcal{B}_{R}^{g}(x_{0})\cap\Sigma)\leq C_{n,\kappa}\lambda_{g}^{\kappa}[\Sigma]\sqrt{R}e^{\kappa(n-1)R}

and, when
\kappa=0
or
n=1
or
R\leq\kappa^{-1}
,

Vol_{g}(\mathcal{B}_{R}^{g}(x_{0})\cap\Sigma)\leq C_{n,\kappa}\lambda_{g}^{\kappa}[\Sigma]R^{n}.

Proof.

We first treat the case
\kappa=0.
In this case, it is immediate from the definition that for
0\leq r\leq R
and
t=R^{2}
one has

c_{n}R^{-n}\leq K_{n,0}(R^{2},R)\leq K_{n,0}(R^{2},r).

Here
1>c_{n}>0
.
Hence, on
\mathcal{B}^{g}_{R}(x_{0})
one has
\Phi_{n,0}^{0,x_{0}}(-R^{2},x)\geq c_{n}R^{-n}
.
It then follows that

\displaystyle c_{n}R^{-n}Vol_{g}(\mathcal{B}_{R}^{g}(x_{0})\cap\Sigma)

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\mathcal{B}_{R}^{g}(x_{0})\cap\Sigma}\Phi_{n,0}^{0,x_{0}}(-R^{2},x)dVol_{g}(x)

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\Sigma}\Phi_{n,0}^{0,x_{0}}(-R^{2},x)dVol_{g}(x)\leq\lambda_{g}^{0}[\Sigma].

The claim follows by taking
C_{n,0}=c_{n}^{-1}
. We note that
K_{1,\kappa}=K_{1,0}
and so the same result holds when
n=1
.

We now suppose
\kappa>0
and
n\geq 2
.
As
\partial_{r}K_{n+1}(t,r)\leq 0
, one has for
0\leq r\leq R
and
t>0

K_{n,0}(t,R)\leq K_{n,0}(t,r).

It follows from (
3.4
) that there is a
c^{\prime}=c^{\prime}(n)>0
such that, when
0\leq R\leq 1
and
t=R^{2}\leq 1
,

\displaystyle c^{\prime}R^{-n-1}\leq K_{n+1}(R^{2},R)\leq K_{n+1}(R^{2},r).

Hence, for
\kappa>0
and
0\leq r\leq R\leq\kappa^{-1}
, one has

\displaystyle c^{\prime}R^{-n-1}

\displaystyle=c^{\prime}\kappa^{n+1}(\kappa R)^{-n-1}\leq\kappa^{n+1}K_{n+1}(\kappa^{2}R^{2},\kappa R)=K_{n+1,\kappa}(R^{2},R)

\displaystyle\leq K_{n+1,\kappa}(R^{2},r).

Arguing as above gives the claimed bounds when
R\leq\kappa^{-1}
.

Finally, suppose
R\geq 1
. For
0\leq r\leq R
and
t=\frac{1}{n}R
and some
c^{\prime\prime}_{n}>0
, one has

c^{\prime\prime}R^{-\frac{1}{2}}e^{-nR}\leq K_{n+1}\left(\frac{1}{n}R,R\right)\leq K_{n+1}\left(\frac{1}{n}R,r\right).

Hence, for
\kappa>0
,
R\geq\kappa^{-1}
and any
0\leq r\leq R
,

\displaystyle c^{\prime\prime}\kappa^{n+\frac{1}{2}}R^{-\frac{1}{2}}e^{-\kappa nR}

\displaystyle\leq\kappa^{n+1}K_{n+1}\left(\frac{\kappa}{n}R,\kappa R\right)

\displaystyle=K_{n+1,\kappa}\left(\frac{1}{\kappa n}R,R\right)\leq K_{n+1,\kappa}\left(\frac{1}{\kappa n}R,r\right).

Arguing as before, we conclude that

c^{\prime\prime}\kappa^{n-\frac{1}{2}}R^{-\frac{1}{2}}e^{-\kappa(n-1)R}Vol_{g}(\mathcal{B}_{R}^{g}(x_{0})\cap\Sigma)\leq\lambda_{g}^{\kappa}[\Sigma]

and so the desired estimate holds with
C_{n,\kappa}=\frac{1}{c^{\prime\prime}}\kappa^{-n+\frac{1}{2}}
.
∎

Lemma 6.2
.

If
\Sigma\subset M
is an
n
-dimensional properly embedded smooth submanifold such that
\lambda_{g}^{\kappa}[\Sigma]<\infty
for some
\kappa\geq 0
and
x_{0}\in M
is fixed, then

\lim_{\tau\to 0}\int_{\Sigma}\Phi_{n,\kappa}^{0,x_{0}}(-\tau,x)dVol_{\Sigma}(x)\leq 1

with equality if and only if
x_{0}\in\Sigma
.

Proof.

By Proposition
6.1
,
\Sigma
has exponential volume growth. Hence, by Proposition
4.5
and (
3.3
) we have

\lim_{\tau\to 0^{+}}\int_{\Sigma}\Phi_{n,\kappa}^{0,x_{0}}(-\tau,x)dVol_{\Sigma}(x)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{cc}1&x_{0}\in\Sigma\\
0&x_{0}\not\in\Sigma.\end{array}\right.

∎

Proposition 6.3
.

If
\Sigma,\Sigma^{\prime}\subset M
are
n
-dimensional submanifolds with
\Sigma\subset\Sigma^{\prime}
and
0\leq\kappa\leq\kappa_{0}
, then

1\leq\lambda_{g}^{{\kappa}_{0}}[\Sigma]\leq\lambda_{g}^{\kappa}[\Sigma]\leq\lambda_{g}^{\kappa}[\Sigma^{\prime}].

Proof.

By Corollary
4.6
and the Remark following it, one has, for
t<0
,

\Phi_{n,{\kappa}_{0}}^{t_{0},x_{0}}(t,x)\leq\Phi_{n,\kappa}^{t_{0},x_{0}}(t,x).

Hence,

\int_{\Sigma}\Phi_{n,{\kappa}_{0}}^{t_{0},x_{0}}(t,x)dVol_{\Sigma}\leq\int_{\Sigma}\Phi_{n,\kappa}^{t_{0},x_{0}}(t,x)dVol_{\Sigma}\leq\int_{\Sigma^{\prime}}\Phi_{n,\kappa}^{t_{0},x_{0}}(t,x)dVol_{\Sigma^{\prime}}

and the second and third inequalities follow by taking suprema. The first inequality is an immediate consequence of Lemma
6.2
.
∎

Remark 6.4
.

It is possible that
\lambda_{g}^{{\kappa}_{0}}[\Sigma]<\infty
and
\lambda_{g}^{{\kappa}}[\Sigma]=\infty
for some
0\leq\kappa<{\kappa}_{0}.

Proposition 6.5
.

If
\Sigma\subset M
is an
n
-dimensional totally geodesic submanifold on which the induced metric is of constant curvature
\kappa
, then

\lambda_{g}^{\kappa}[\Sigma]=1.

Proof.

By Lemma
6.2
,
\lambda_{g}^{\kappa}[\Sigma]\geq 1
so we only have to prove the reverse inequality. To that end, Millison’s identity (
3.1
) and the positivity of
K_{n,\kappa}
imply that
\Phi_{n,\kappa}^{0,x_{0}}
is radially decreasing.
Given
x_{0}\in\Sigma\subset M
one has by the hypotheses on
\Sigma
that
\Phi_{n,\kappa}^{0,x_{0}}(t,x)
restricts to the backwards heat kernel and so

\int_{\Sigma}\Phi_{n,\kappa}^{0,x_{0}}(t,x)dVol_{\Sigma}(x)=1.

While for
x_{0}\not\in\Sigma
, let
x_{0}^{\prime}\in\Sigma
be the nearest point in
\Sigma
to
x_{0}
. For
x\in\Sigma
, it follows from the law of cosines and the triangle comparison property of Cartan-Hadamard manifolds that
\mathrm{dist}_{g}(x,x_{0}^{\prime})\leq\mathrm{dist}_{g}(x,x_{0})
. Hence, for all
x\in\Sigma
,

\Phi_{n,\kappa}^{0,x_{0}}(t,x)=K_{n,\kappa}(t,\mathrm{dist}_{g}(x,x_{0}))\leq K_{n,\kappa}(t,\mathrm{dist}_{g}(x,x_{0}^{\prime}))=\Phi_{n,\kappa}^{0,x_{0}^{\prime}}(t,x)

and the claim follows.
∎

Finally, we collect observations about the
\kappa
-entropy of closed submanifolds.

Proposition 6.6
.

If
\Sigma\subset M
is an
n
-dimensional closed submanifold and
\kappa\geq 0
, then for any
\kappa
such that
\lambda_{g}^{\kappa}[\Sigma]>1
, there is a pair
(t_{0},x_{0})\in(0,\infty)\times M
, possibly depending on
\kappa
, so

\lambda_{g}^{\kappa}[\Sigma]=\int_{\Sigma}\Phi_{n,\kappa}^{0,x_{0}}(-t_{0},x)dVol_{\Sigma}(x)=\int_{\Sigma}\Phi_{n,\kappa}^{t_{0},x_{0}}(0,x)dVol_{\Sigma}(x)<\infty.

For such a
\Sigma
and
\kappa
, if
n\geq 2
and
\kappa^{\prime}>\kappa
, then

\lambda_{g}^{\kappa^{\prime}}[\Sigma]>\lambda_{g}^{\kappa}[\Sigma]>1.

For such a
\Sigma
and
\kappa
, if
\Sigma^{\prime}\subset M
is an
n
-dimensional submanifold with
\Sigma\subset\Sigma^{\prime}
and
\Sigma^{\prime}\setminus\Sigma\neq\emptyset
, then

\lambda_{g}^{\kappa}[\Sigma^{\prime}]>\lambda_{g}^{\kappa}[\Sigma]>1.

Proof.

Fix a point
x_{1}\in M
and
\kappa\geq 0
. As
\Sigma
is closed, there is an
R_{1}
so
\Sigma\subset\mathcal{B}_{R_{1}}^{g}(x_{1})
.
By the decay estimates on
K_{n}
of (
3.3
), for any
\epsilon>0
there is an
R_{\epsilon,\kappa}>R_{0}
, such that if
x_{0}\in M\setminus\mathcal{B}_{R_{\epsilon,\kappa}}^{g}(x_{1})
, then, for all
\tau>0
, one has

\int_{\Sigma}\Phi_{n,\kappa}^{0,x_{0}}(-\tau,x)dVol_{\Sigma}(x)<\epsilon.

Similarly, by (
3.5
) for
x_{0}\in\mathcal{B}_{R_{1}}^{g}(x_{1})
one has

\lim_{\tau\to\infty}\int_{\Sigma}\Phi_{n,\kappa}^{0,x_{0}}(-\tau,x)dVol_{\Sigma}(x)=0.

As
\Sigma
is smooth and automatically has exponential volume growth, by Lemma
6.2
, for any fixed
x_{0}
, one has

\lim_{\tau\to 0}\int_{\Sigma}\Phi_{n,\kappa}^{0,x_{0}}(-\tau,x)dVol_{\Sigma}(x)\leq 1.

Hence, if
\lambda_{g}^{\kappa}[\Sigma]>1
there must be a
\tau_{1}\in(0,1)
and an
R_{2}>R_{1}
such that

\lambda_{\kappa}^{g}[\Sigma]=\sup_{x_{0}\in\bar{\mathcal{B}}_{R_{2}}^{g}(x_{1}),\tau\in[\tau_{1},\tau_{1}^{-1}]}\int_{\Sigma}\Phi_{n,\kappa}^{0,x_{0}}(-\tau,x)dVol_{\Sigma}(x).

This is the supremum of a continuous function over a compact set, so it is achieved and has a finite value. This proves the first claim.

For the second claim observe that, as
n\geq 2
, we have, by Corollary
4.6
, that

\Phi_{n,{\kappa}_{0}}^{t_{0},x_{0}}(t,x)<\Phi_{n,\kappa}^{t_{0},x_{0}}(t,x).

As we may apply the first claim to
\Sigma
and
\kappa
, there is a
(t_{0},x_{0})
so

\lambda_{g}^{\kappa}[\Sigma]=\int_{\Sigma}\Phi_{n,{\kappa}}^{t_{0},x_{0}}(0,x)dVol_{\Sigma}(x)<\int_{\Sigma}\Phi_{n,{\kappa}^{\prime}}^{t_{0},x_{0}}(0,x)dVol_{\Sigma}(x)\leq\lambda_{g}^{\kappa^{\prime}}[\Sigma].

As
\Sigma\subset\Sigma^{\prime}
,
\Sigma\setminus\Sigma^{\prime}\neq\emptyset
and
\Phi_{n,\kappa}^{t_{0},x_{0}}(0,\cdot)
is everywhere positive.

\lambda_{g}^{\kappa}[\Sigma]=\int_{\Sigma}\Phi_{n,\kappa}^{t_{0}0,x_{0}}(0,x)dVol_{\Sigma}<\int_{\Sigma^{\prime}}\Phi_{n,\kappa}^{t_{0},x_{0}}(0,x)dVol_{\Sigma^{\prime}}\leq\lambda_{g}^{\kappa}[\Sigma^{\prime}].

Again the
(t_{0},x_{0})
exist based on the hypotheses on
\Sigma
and
\kappa
.
∎

6.2.
Monotonicity of
\kappa
-entropies

We next use the monotonicity formula to show Theorem
1.1
, i.e., that the
\kappa
-entropy is monotone along a well-behaved mean curvature flow inside a Cartan-Hadamard manifold with sectional curvature bounded from above by
-\kappa^{2}_{0}
.

Proof of Theorem
1.1
.

Given
\epsilon>0
, the definition of
\lambda_{g}^{\kappa}
ensures there is a
\tau>0
and
x_{0}\in M
so

\lambda_{g}^{\kappa}[\Sigma_{t_{2}}]-\epsilon\leq\int_{\Sigma_{t_{2}}}\Phi_{n,\kappa}^{0,x_{0}}(-\tau,x)d\mathrm{Vol}_{\Sigma_{t_{2}}}=\int_{\Sigma_{t_{2}}}\Phi_{n,\kappa}^{\tau+t_{2},x_{0}}(t_{2},x)d\mathrm{Vol}_{\Sigma_{t_{2}}}.

The second equality follows from the definition of the
\Phi_{n,\kappa}^{t_{0},x_{0}}
.
The curvature hypotheses and volume bound imply Theorem
5.4
holds and so

\displaystyle\lambda_{g}^{\kappa}[\Sigma_{t_{2}}]-\epsilon

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\Sigma_{t_{2}}}\Phi_{n,\kappa}^{\tau+t_{2},x_{0}}(t_{2},x)d\mathrm{Vol}_{\Sigma_{t_{2}}}\leq\int_{\Sigma_{t_{1}}}\Phi_{n,\kappa}^{\tau+t_{2},x_{0}}(t_{1},x)d\mathrm{Vol}_{\Sigma_{t_{1}}}

\displaystyle=\int_{\Sigma_{t_{1}}}\Phi_{n,\kappa}^{0,x_{0}}(-\tau+t_{1}-t_{2},x)d\mathrm{Vol}_{\Sigma_{t_{1}}}\leq\lambda_{g}^{\kappa}[\Sigma_{t_{1}}].

The first conclusion follows as
\epsilon>0
was arbitrary.

To verify the last claim, observe that when the flow is a classical mean curvature flow of a closed manifold,
F:[t_{1},t_{2}]\times\Sigma\to M
, then
F([t_{1},t_{2}]\times\Sigma)
is a compact subset of
M
and so there is automatically a uniform lower bound,
\Lambda
, on
Ric_{g}
along the flow. In addition, in a Cartan-Hadamard manifold, the sectional curvatures are, by definition, non-positive and so one may always take
\kappa=\kappa_{0}=0
.

∎

Because the result becomes technically simpler for classical flows of closed submanifolds it yields information about the
\kappa
-entropy of closed hypersurfaces.

Proposition 6.7
.

Suppose
(M,g)
is an
(n+1)
-dimensional Cartan-Hadamard manifold with
\sec_{g}\leq-{\kappa}^{2}_{0}
,
0\leq\kappa\leq\kappa_{0}
and
\Sigma\subset M
is a closed hypersurface. There is a constant
\epsilon(n)>0
so

\lambda_{g}^{\kappa}[\Sigma]\geq 1+\epsilon(n)>1.

Moreover if either
\Sigma
is mean convex or
n=1
or
2
, then,

\lambda_{g}^{\kappa}[\Sigma]\geq\lambda[\mathbb{S}^{n}]>1.

When
\kappa>0
, the inequality is strict.
Furthermore, if
\Sigma
is connected but not diffeomorphic to
\mathbb{S}^{n}
, then

\lambda_{g}^{\kappa}[\Sigma]>\lambda[\mathbb{S}^{n-1}]>\lambda[\mathbb{S}^{n}].

Proof.

Fix a point
x_{1}\in M
and choose
R_{1}\geq 2
large enough so
\Sigma\subset\mathcal{B}^{g}_{\frac{1}{2}R_{1}}(x_{1})
. As
(M,g)
is a Cartan-Hadamard manifold it follows from the mean curvature comparison theorem that
\partial\mathcal{B}^{g}_{R_{1}}(x_{1})
is smooth and strictly convex – see Proposition
4.2
or
[
24
, Section 4.8]
. In particular, one has short time existence of the classical mean curvature flow,
t\in[0,T)\mapsto\Sigma_{t}
of
\Sigma_{0}=\Sigma
and this flow remains inside of
\mathcal{B}^{g}_{R_{1}}(x_{1})
. In fact, we may take
T\in(0,\infty]
to be the maximal smooth time of existence of the classical flow. As there are no closed minimal surfaces in a Cartan-Hadamard manifold,
T<\infty
and the flow must also form a singularity in finite time. If
\Sigma_{0}
is mean convex, then it follows from standard computations on the evolution of mean curvature and the parabolic maximum principle that
\Sigma_{t}
remains mean convex. Note that there is a uniform lower bound on
Ric_{g}
in
\mathcal{B}^{g}_{R_{1}}(x_{1})
and so Theorem
5.4
applies to the flow automatically.

At the first singular time,
T>0
, there is at least one singular point
x_{0}\in\mathcal{B}_{R_{1}}^{g}(x_{1})
. One may take a parabolic blow-up around
(x_{0},T)
, and it follows from Theorem
5.4
and White’s version of Brakke regularity
[
36
]

that, up to passing to a subsequence, the flow converges to a non-flat self-shrinker in
\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
. As this shrinker is the limit of classical flows,
[
36
]
applies and we obtain an
\epsilon=\epsilon(n)>0
so

\lambda_{g}^{\kappa}[\Sigma]\geq\Theta_{\kappa}(T,x_{0})\geq 1+\epsilon(n)>1.

This proves the first claim.

When the flow is mean convex it follows from
[
18
]
– see also
[
21
,
34
,
35
]
– that the singularity must be a non-flat generalized cylinder and hence has entropy at least
\lambda[\mathbb{S}^{n}]
. Likewise, when
n=1
the classification of embedded shrinkers in
[
1
]
and when
n=2
the classification of low entropy shrinkers in
[
7
]
imply the entropy is at most
\lambda[\mathbb{S}^{n}]
– here we use that an immersed shrinker has entropy at least
2
. The strictness of the inequality when
\kappa>0
is an immediate consequence of the strictness of the monotonicity in Theorem
5.4
when
\kappa>0
and the fact that the flow consists of closed hypersurfaces – which cannot be geodesic cones.

To conclude the proof observe that in either case above if
\lambda_{g}^{\kappa}[\Sigma]\leq\lambda[\mathbb{S}^{n-1}]
, then the shrinker obtained must be the round sphere
\sqrt{2n}\mathbb{S}^{n}
and the convergence of the rescaled flow must be locally smooth and converge to the round sphere. As
\Sigma
is connected this means
\Sigma
is diffeomorphic to the sphere.

∎

7.
Rigidity of some
\kappa
-entropy minimizers

Observe that for
\kappa_{0}\geq\kappa\geq 0
the lower bound on entropy for closed hypersurfaces in an
(n+1)
-dimensional Cartan-Hadamard manifold,
(M,g)
, with curvature bounded from above by
-\kappa^{2}_{0}
is sharp in the sense that, for any
x\in M
,

\lim_{R\to 0}\lambda_{g}^{\kappa}[\partial\mathcal{B}_{R}^{g}(x)]=\lambda[\mathbb{S}^{n}].

However, for
\kappa>0
, by Proposition
6.7
, equality is never achieved. Indeed,

\lambda_{g}^{\kappa}[\Sigma]>\lambda[\mathbb{S}^{n}]

where
\Sigma
is a hypersurface for which Proposition
6.7
applies. When
\kappa=0
it is possible for the lower bound to be achieved, but this implies rigidity of both the hypersurface
\Sigma
and the metric
g
in the region
\Sigma
encloses.

Theorem 7.1
.

Suppose
(M,g)
is an
(n+1)
-dimensional Cartan-Hadamard manifold and that
\Omega\subset M
is a compact domain with
\Sigma=\partial\Omega
smooth and mean convex. If

\lambda_{g}^{0}[\partial\Omega]=\lambda[\mathbb{S}^{n}],

then there is an
R>0
so
(\Omega,g|_{\Omega})
is isometric to
(\bar{B}_{R},g_{\mathbb{R}}|_{\bar{B}_{R}})
, the standard Euclidean metric restricted to the closed Euclidean ball in
\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
of radius
R
.
When
n=1
or
2
, the same result is true without the hypothesis that
\partial\Omega
is mean convex.

To prove this result we first use a result of Huisken
[
19
]
to verify it for sufficiently convex sets.

Proposition 7.2
.

Suppose
(M,g)
is an
(n+1)
-dimensional Cartan-Hadamard manifold. Fix a compact set
V\subset M
and let

\gamma=n\max_{x\in V,P\in G_{2}(T_{x}M)}|\mathrm{sec}_{g}(P)|+n^{2}\max_{x\in V}|\nabla_{g}\mathrm{Riem}_{g}|.

If
\Omega\subset V
is a compact, geodesically convex domain with
\partial\Omega
smooth, connected, and with all principal curvatures with respect to the inward normal strictly larger than
\gamma
,
then there is a point
x_{0}\in\mathrm{int}(\Omega)
, a time
T\in(0,\infty)
, and a unique classical mean curvature flow
t\in[0,T)\mapsto\Sigma_{t}
that disappears at
x_{0}
at time
T
satisfying
\Sigma_{0}=\partial\Omega
, and for all
t\in[0,T)
,
\Sigma_{t}=\partial\Omega_{t}
where
\Omega_{t}
are compact and geodesically convex.

If, in addition,
\Omega
satisfies

\lambda_{g}^{0}[\partial\Omega]=\lambda[\mathbb{S}^{n}],

then there is an
R>0
so
(\Omega,g|_{\Omega})
is isometric to
(\bar{B}_{R},g_{\mathbb{R}}|_{\bar{B}_{R}})
the standard Euclidean metric restricted to a closed Euclidean ball in
\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
of radius
R
.

Proof.

We first observe that as
(M,g)
is a Cartan-Hadamard manifold, for any fixed
x_{1}\in M
and any
r>0
,
\bar{\mathcal{B}}^{g}_{r}(x_{1})
, the closed ball around
x_{1}
in
M
with respect to the metric
g
of radius
r
is geodesically convex and has
\partial\bar{\mathcal{B}}^{g}_{r}(x_{1})
smooth with positive second fundamental form – see
[
24
, Section 4.8]
.

Fix a point
x_{1}\in M
and choose
R_{1}>0
large enough such that
V\subset\mathcal{B}^{g}_{R_{1}}(x_{1})
.
We immediately observe that the choice of
\gamma
implies that the mean curvature of
\partial\Omega
with respect to the inward normal satisfies
H_{\partial\Omega}>n\gamma\geq 0
.
The choice of
\gamma
and the fact that
(M,g)
is a Cartan-Hadamard manifold ensures that the second fundamental form of
\partial\Omega
satisfies the hypotheses of
[
19
, Theorem 1.1]
. As
\partial\Omega\subset\bar{\mathcal{B}}^{g}_{R_{1}}(x_{1})
and
\partial\bar{\mathcal{B}}^{g}_{R_{1}}(x_{1})
has positive mean curvature with respect to the inward normal, we may apply
[
19
, Corollary 1.4]
to
\Sigma_{0}=\partial\Omega
in order to obtain a unique classical mean curvature flow
t\in[0,T)\mapsto\Sigma_{t}
.
The result of
[
19
]
ensures that each
\Sigma_{t}
has strictly positive principle curvatures with respect to the inward normal and that it becomes singular at time
T
and disappears in a round point at
x_{0}
. Clearly, the flow moves strictly inward and so
x_{0}\in\mathrm{int}(\Omega)
. This proves the first claim.

To conclude the proof, observe that as the mean curvature flow disappears in a round point at
x_{0}
, Corollary
5.7
ensures

\lim_{t\to T}\int_{\Sigma_{t}}\Phi_{n,0}^{T,x_{0}}(t,y)dVol_{\Sigma_{t}}(y)=\lambda[\mathbb{S}^{n}].

The entropy assumption and Theorem
5.4
imply that for all
t\in[0,T)
one has

(7.1)

\int_{\Sigma_{t}}\Phi_{n,0}^{T,x_{0}}(t,y)dVol_{\Sigma_{t}}(y)=\lambda[\mathbb{S}^{n}]=\lambda_{g}^{0}[\Sigma_{t}].

Now choose compact sets
\Omega_{t}
so
\Omega_{t}\subset\Omega
and
\partial\Omega_{t}=\Sigma_{t}
. One verifies that, for
0\leq t_{0}<t_{1}<T
,

x_{0}\in\Omega_{t_{1}}\subset\mathrm{int}(\Omega_{t_{0}})\subset\Omega.

Moreover, as
(M,g)
is a Cartan-Hadamard manifold and
\Sigma_{t}=\partial\Omega_{t}
has positive second fundamental form with respect to the inward normal,
\Omega_{t}
is geodesically convex.
In particular, for each
t\in(0,T)
and each
y\in\partial\Omega_{t}
, the unique unit speed geodesic connecting
x_{0}
to
y
,
\gamma_{y}:[0,L]\to M
satisfies
\gamma_{y}(s)\in\Omega_{t}
for all

and
\gamma_{y}^{\prime}(L)\in T_{y}M
is transverse to

. It follows from Proposition
5.1

\sec_{g}(P)=0

where
P
is any plane in

containing
\gamma^{\prime}_{y}(s)
. In particular, if
\rho

, then one has

for all

and

orthogonal to

. Arguing as Lemma

this means the sectional curvature of

In particular, there is an isometric immersion
F:(\Omega,g|_{\Omega})\to(\mathbb{R}^{n+1},g_{\mathbb{R}})
. This map is actually an embedding as

Proof of Theorem
7.1
.

We first suppose that
\partial\Omega
is connected.
As
\Omega
is compact, for any fixed
x_{1}
, there is an
R_{1}>0
sufficiently large so
\Omega\subset\mathcal{B}_{\frac{1}{2}R_{1}}^{g}(x_{1})
. As
(M,g)
is a Cartan-Hadamard manifold one has that
\partial\mathcal{B}_{R_{1}}^{g}(x_{1})
is smooth and strictly mean convex. In particular, the classical mean curvature flow,
t\in[0,T)\mapsto\Sigma_{t}
of
\Sigma_{0}=\partial\Omega
exists uniquely and remains inside of
\mathcal{B}_{R_{1}}^{g}(x_{1})
. As there are no closed minimal surfaces in a Cartan-Hadamard manifold the flow must also form a singularity in finite time – we take
T<\infty
to be the maximal smooth time of existence. As shown in the proof of Proposition
6.7
the hypotheses on
\Omega
ensure there is an
x_{0}\in{\mathcal{B}}^{g}_{R_{1}}(x_{1})
so the flow forms a spherical singularity at time
T
and at the point
x_{0}
.

With
V=\bar{\mathcal{B}}^{g}_{R_{1}}(x_{1})
, pick
\gamma>0
as in Proposition
7.2
. We may choose compact sets
\Omega_{t}
so
\partial\Omega_{t}=\Sigma_{t}
and
\Omega_{t}\subset V
. As
x_{0}\in{\mathcal{B}}^{g}_{R_{1}}(x_{1})
and the flow forms a spherical singularity at
(T,x_{0})
, there is an
\epsilon>0
small that ensures
\Sigma_{T-\epsilon}
has principal curvatures greater than
\gamma
with respect to the inward normal to
\Omega_{T-\epsilon}
. As
\lambda_{g}^{0}[\Sigma_{T-\epsilon}]=\lambda[\mathbb{S}^{n}]
it follows from Proposition
7.2
that

(\Omega_{T-\epsilon},g|_{\Omega_{T-\epsilon}})
is isometric to
(\bar{B}_{R(T-\epsilon)},g_{\mathbb{R}}|_{\bar{B}_{R(T-\epsilon)}})
. Moreover, for all
t\in[T_{\epsilon},T)
, it is true that
(\Omega_{t},g|_{\Omega_{t}})
is isometric to
(\bar{B}_{R(t)},g_{\mathbb{R}}|_{\bar{B}_{R(t)}})
where one easily checks that
R(t)=\sqrt{2n(T-t)}
. Define a set of good times by

\mathcal{G}=\left\{t\in[0,T):(\Omega_{t},g|_{\Omega_{t}})\mbox{ isometric to }(\bar{B}_{R(t)},g_{\mathbb{R}}|_{B_{R(t)}})\right\}.

Clearly,
[T-\epsilon,T)\subset\mathcal{G}
and
\mathcal{G}
is connected. Set
t_{0}=\inf\mathcal{G}
. If
t_{0}=0
, then we have verified the result when
\partial\Omega
is connected. If
t_{0}>0
then, we can set

\gamma_{0}=(2n(T-t_{0}))^{-1/2}>0

which is the value of the principle curvatures of
\Sigma_{t_{0}}
.
As
\Omega_{t_{0}}
is isometric to flat space, for
t^{\prime}<t_{0}
sufficiently close to
t_{0}
we can ensure that the constant
\gamma
given by Proposition
7.2
for the region
\Omega_{t^{\prime}}
is less than
\frac{1}{2}\gamma_{0}
.
Hence, Proposition
7.2
also applies to
\Omega_{t^{\prime}}
which immediately implies that
t^{\prime}\in\mathcal{G}
, a contradiction.

Finally, we suppose
\partial\Omega
has more than one component. Let
\Sigma^{\prime}
be a choice of component and suppose
\Omega^{\prime}
is the compact region bounded by
\Sigma^{\prime}
(such a region exists by Alexander duality). As
\Sigma^{\prime}
is compact and, by Proposition
6.7
satisfies
\lambda[\Sigma^{\prime}]>1
, it follows from Proposition
6.6
that
\lambda[\Sigma^{\prime}]<\lambda[\Sigma_{0}]=\lambda[\mathbb{S}^{n}]
, but applying the previous argument to
\Omega^{\prime}
shows that
\lambda[\Sigma^{\prime}]=\lambda[\mathbb{S}^{n}]
which is a contradiction and so
\partial\Omega
has only one component.
∎

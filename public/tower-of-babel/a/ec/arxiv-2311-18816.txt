Family of Approximations for Dirichlet L L -functions
Mohammed Alzergani

Abstract
We introduce an infinite family of approximations for a Dirichlet L L -function L ⁡ ( s , χ ) L(s,\chi) arising from truncated Euler products. These approximations are entire functions and satisfy the same functional equation as L ⁡ ( s , χ ) L(s,\chi) . We provide numerical evidence of the accuracy of estimating values of L ⁡ ( s , χ ) L(s,\chi) in the critical strip using these approximations. We then provide a precise expression for the error of the approximations and show that the error has exponential decay in largest prime considered in the truncated Euler product.

2
A Family of Approximations for Dirichlet L-functions

Throughout the paper we will let
\chi
be a fixed primitive Dirichlet character of conductor
q
. Consider

g(s,\chi)=\left(\frac{q}{\pi}\right)^{\frac{s+\kappa(\chi)}{2}}\Gamma\left(\frac{s+\kappa(\chi)}{2}\right)

with

\kappa(\chi)=\begin{cases}0&\chi(-1)=1\\
1&\chi(-1)=-1.\end{cases}

We define a truncated Euler product
L_{u}(s,\chi)
of
L(s,\chi)
as

L_{u}(s,\chi)=\prod_{\text{prime }p\leq u}\frac{1}{1-\frac{\chi(p)}{p^{s}}},

where the product is over primes less than or equal to
u
. We also introduce the completed analog
\xi_{u}(s,\chi)
of
L_{u}(s,\chi)
as

\xi_{u}(s,\chi)=g(s,\chi)L_{u}(s,\chi).

For future convenience, we introduce the modified truncated Euler product
L_{u}^{\setminus\{p_{j}\}}(s,\chi)
with a single prime
p_{j}\leq u
removed

\displaystyle L_{u}^{\setminus\{p_{j}\}}(s,\chi)=\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}p\leq u\\
p\neq p_{j}\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{1-\frac{\chi(p)}{p^{s}}},

as well as its completed analog

\xi_{u}^{\setminus\{p_{j}\}}=g(s,\chi)L_{u}^{\setminus\{p_{j}\}}(s,\chi).

Our goal is to introduce a family of approximations
\xi_{u}^{\approx}(s,\chi)
of
\xi(s,\chi)
arising from the truncated Euler product
\xi_{u}(s,\chi)
introduced above such that each
\xi_{u}^{\approx}(s,\chi)
is an entire function and satisfies the same functional equation

\xi_{u}^{\approx}(s,\chi)=\epsilon(\chi)\xi_{u}^{\approx}(1-s,\overline{\chi})

as
\xi(s,\chi)
. In order to do this, we need to first find the principal part
\xi_{u}^{pp}(s,\chi)
of
\xi_{u}(s,\chi)
.

Lemma 1
.

Given
\xi_{u}(s,\chi)
as above, its principal part is given by

\displaystyle\xi^{pp}_{u}(s,\chi)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}j=1\\
p_{j}\nmid q\end{subarray}}^{m}\ \sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}h\in\mathbb{Z}\\
h\neq\frac{-n_{j}}{\varphi(q)}\end{subarray}}\frac{\xi_{u}^{\setminus\{p_{j}\}}\left(\frac{2\pi i}{\log(p_{j})}\left(\frac{n_{j}}{\varphi(q)}+h\right),\chi\right)}{(s-\frac{2\pi i}{\log(p_{j})}(\frac{n_{j}}{\varphi(q)}+h))\log(p_{j})}

\displaystyle+\sum_{h\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}}\frac{2(-1)^{h}\pi^{h}L_{u}(-2h-\kappa(\chi),\chi)}{q^{h}\Gamma(h+1)(s+2h+\kappa(\chi))}

\displaystyle+\text{principal part from s=0}.

Proof.

With the possible exception of the pole at
s=0
, every pole of
\xi_{u}(s,\chi)
is a simple one, and so the principal part is the sum

\sum_{s_{0}}\frac{Res(s_{0})}{s-s_{0}}

over all the poles
s_{0}
of
\xi_{u}(s,\chi)
plus a term from the pole at
s=0
.

The function
\Gamma(n)
has simple poles at each
n\in\mathbb{Z}_{\leq 0}
with residue

\frac{(-1)^{-n}}{\Gamma(-n+1)}.

Therefore when
s=-\kappa-2h
with
h\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
, the residue of
\xi_{u}(s,\chi)
is

\frac{2(-1)^{h}}{\Gamma(h+1)}\left(\frac{q}{\pi}\right)^{-h}L_{u}(-2h-\kappa(\chi),\chi).

L_{u}(s,\chi)
has simple poles when a term in the Euler product has denominator 0. This occurs when, for the
j
-th prime
p_{j}<u
,

\frac{\chi(p_{j})}{{p_{j}}^{s}}=1.

Note that there is no pole contributed by the j-th term whenever
p_{j}|q
.
Since
\chi(p_{j})
is a
\varphi(q)
-th root of unity, there is an integer
n_{j}
between 0 and
\varphi(q)
that satisfies

\chi(p_{j})=\exp{\left(2\pi i\left(\frac{n_{j}}{\varphi(q)}+h\right)\right)}

for every
h\in\mathbb{Z}
. Hence the poles
s
of
L_{u}(s,\chi)
are at points

s=\frac{2\pi i}{\log(p_{j})}\left(\frac{n_{j}}{\varphi(q)}+h\right).

The residue of
\xi_{u}
at these poles is therefore

\frac{\xi_{u}^{\setminus\{p_{j}\}}\left(\frac{2\pi i}{\log(p_{j})}\left(\frac{n_{j}}{\varphi(q)}+h\right),\chi\right)}{\log(p_{j})}.

∎

To complete our approximation, we get the holomorphic part of the Laurent expansion

\displaystyle\xi^{reg}_{u}(s,\chi)

\displaystyle=\xi_{u}(s,\chi)-\xi^{pp}_{u}(s,\chi),

and take into account the functional equation to give us our approximation
\xi_{u}^{\approx}
defined by

\xi^{\approx}_{u}(s,\chi)=\xi^{reg}_{u}(s,\chi)+\epsilon(\chi)\xi^{reg}_{u}(1-s,\overline{\chi})

where

\epsilon(\chi)=\frac{\tau(\chi)}{i^{\kappa}\sqrt{q}}=\frac{\sum_{n=1}^{q}\chi(n)e^{2\pi in/q}}{i^{\kappa}\sqrt{q}}.

Lemma 2
.

\xi_{u}^{\approx}(s,\chi)
satisfies the same functional equation of
\xi_{u}(s,\chi)
.

Proof.

Note that for primitive character modulo
q
,

\displaystyle\epsilon(\chi)\epsilon(\overline{\chi})

\displaystyle=\frac{\tau(\chi)\overline{\tau(\chi)}\chi(-1)}{i^{\kappa(\chi)}i^{\kappa(\overline{\chi})}\sqrt{q}\sqrt{q}}

\displaystyle=\frac{\lvert\tau(\chi)\rvert^{2}\chi(-1)}{i^{2\kappa(\chi)}q}=\frac{\chi(-1)}{(-1)^{\kappa(\chi)}}

\displaystyle=1.

Therefore
\xi_{u}^{\approx}
indeed satisfies the functional equation

\displaystyle\epsilon(\chi)\xi_{u}^{\approx}(1-s,\overline{\chi})

\displaystyle=\epsilon(\chi)\xi^{reg}_{u}(1-s,\overline{\chi})+\epsilon(\chi)\epsilon(\overline{\chi})\xi^{reg}_{u}(s,\chi)

\displaystyle=\epsilon(\chi)\xi^{reg}_{u}(1-s,\overline{\chi})+\xi^{reg}_{u}(s,\chi)

\displaystyle=\xi_{u}^{\approx}(s,\chi).

∎

We finish by defining our approximation for
L(s,\chi)
:

L_{u}^{\approx}(s,\chi)=\frac{\xi_{u}^{\approx}(s,\chi)}{g(s,\chi)}.

Fix a complex number
s_{0}
throughout the section. We will prove an integral formula for the error of the approximation

\xi(s_{0},\chi)-\xi_{u}^{\approx}(s_{0},\chi).

More specifically, we will show the following exact expression:

Theorem 2
.

Fix
\sigma>1
. We have

\displaystyle\xi(s_{0},\chi)-\xi^{\approx}_{u}(s_{0},\chi)=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{Re(s)=\sigma}g(s,\chi)\left(\frac{L(s,\chi)-L_{u}(s,\chi)}{s-s_{0}}+\epsilon(\chi)\frac{L(s,\overline{\chi})-L_{u}(s,\overline{\chi})}{s-(1-s_{0})}\right)ds.

Proof.

Choose a simple closed curve
\mathcal{C}
encompassing points
s_{0}
and
1-s_{0}
that doesn’t pass through any poles of
\xi_{u}(s,\chi)
on the real or imaginary axis. Then we define the following:

\mathcal{I}(u,s_{0},\mathcal{C}):=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{\mathcal{C}}\frac{\xi^{pp}_{u}(s,\chi)}{s-s_{0}}+\frac{\xi^{pp}_{u}(s,\overline{\chi})}{s-(1-s_{0})}ds.

Next we construct a sequence of rectangular closed curves
\mathcal{C}_{l}
for
l\in\mathbb{Z_{+}}
each encompassing
\mathcal{C}
while satisfying (
6
) and (
7
) and
\mathcal{C}_{l}\subseteq\mathcal{C}_{l+1}
and
\bigcup\text{Int}(\mathcal{C}_{l})=\mathbb{C}
.

For a fixed
l\in\mathbb{Z_{+}}
, by Cauchy’s Residue formula, if we let
\rho
denote the poles on the real and complex axes lying in between
\mathcal{C}
and
\mathcal{C}_{l}
and Res(
\rho
) denote the residue of

\frac{\xi^{pp}_{u}(s,\chi)}{s-s_{0}}+\frac{\xi^{pp}_{u}(s,\overline{\chi})}{s-(1-s_{0})}

at each

3.4
Bounding the error

Fix a complex number
s_{0}
throughout the section. In this section, we use the explicit formula for the error to bound the error term.

From Mathematica, we can expand the Gamma factor in
J_{1}(n)
:

\displaystyle\Gamma\left(\frac{\kappa(\chi)+s_{0}}{2},\frac{n^{2}\pi}{q}\right)

\displaystyle=\exp\left(\frac{-\pi n^{2}}{q}+O\left[\frac{1}{n}\right]^{2}\right)

(17)

\displaystyle\times n^{\kappa(\chi)+s_{0}-2}\left(\left(\frac{\pi}{q}\right)^{\frac{\kappa(\chi)+s_{0}}{2}-1}+O\left[\frac{1}{n}\right]^{2}\right).

(18)

It is apparent that
17
decays exponentially while
18
grows in a polynomial rate, therefore the Gamma factor decays exponentially.
Similarly for the Gamma term in
J_{2}(n)
,

\displaystyle\Gamma\left(\frac{\kappa(\chi)+1-s_{0}}{2},\frac{n^{2}\pi}{q}\right)

\displaystyle=\exp\left(\frac{-\pi n^{2}}{q}+O\left[\frac{1}{n}\right]^{2}\right)

\displaystyle\times n^{\kappa(\chi)-1-s_{0}}\left(\left(\frac{\pi}{q}\right)^{\frac{\kappa(\chi)+1-s_{0}}{2}-1}+O\left[\frac{1}{n}\right]^{2}\right).

Summing on
A_{u}
is less than summing over all integers greater than
u
, so we can obtain an upper bound by summing

|\xi(s_{0},\chi)-\xi^{\approx}_{u}(s_{0},\chi)|\leq\sum_{n=u}^{\infty}|J_{1}(n)|+|J_{2}(n)|.

This sum itself is bounded by integrating

|\xi(s_{0},\chi)-\xi^{\approx}_{u}(s_{0},\chi)|\leq\int_{u}^{\infty}|J_{1}(x)|dx+\int_{u}^{\infty}|J_{2}(x)|dx.

Using the main terms, from our previous expansions of the Gamma factors, along with the fact that
|\chi(n)|\leq 1
always and
|\epsilon(\chi)|=1
for primitive characters,

\displaystyle|\xi(s_{0},\chi)-\xi^{\approx}_{u}(s_{0},\chi)|

\displaystyle\ll\int_{u}^{\infty}x^{\kappa(\chi)}\left(\frac{q}{\pi}\right)^{\frac{\kappa(\chi)+s_{0}}{2}}x^{-\kappa(\chi)-s_{0}}\exp\left(\frac{-\pi x^{2}}{q}\right)x^{\kappa(\chi)+s_{0}-2}\left(\frac{\pi}{q}\right)^{\frac{\kappa(\chi)+s_{0}}{2}-1}dx

\displaystyle+\int_{u}^{\infty}x^{\kappa(\chi)}\left(\frac{q}{\pi}\right)^{\frac{\kappa(\chi)+1-s_{0}}{2}}x^{-\kappa(\chi)-1+s_{0}}\exp\left(\frac{-\pi x^{2}}{q}\right)x^{\kappa(\chi)-1-s_{0}}\left(\frac{\pi}{q}\right)^{\frac{\kappa(\chi)+1-s_{0}}{2}-1}dx.

This simplifies to

|\xi(s_{0},\chi)-\xi^{\approx}_{u}(s_{0},\chi)|\ll\int_{u}^{\infty}2\left(\frac{q}{\pi}\right)x^{\kappa(\chi)-2}\exp\left(\frac{-\pi x^{2}}{q}\right)dx.

Computing this integral, we find that the error term is bounded by

|\xi(s_{0},\chi)-\xi^{\approx}_{u}(s_{0},\chi)|\ll\begin{cases}\left(\frac{q}{\pi}\right)\Gamma\left(0,\frac{\pi u^{2}}{q}\right)&\text{if }\kappa(\chi)=1\\
\sqrt{\frac{q}{\pi}}\Gamma\left(-\frac{1}{2},\frac{\pi u^{2}}{q}\right)&\text{if }\kappa(\chi)=0.\end{cases}

This concludes the proof of Corollary
1
.

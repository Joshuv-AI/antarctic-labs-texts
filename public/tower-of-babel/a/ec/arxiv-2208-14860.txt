Chi-boundedness of graphs containing no cycles with k k chords
Joonkyung Lee, Shoham Letzter, Alexey Pokrovskiy

Abstract
We prove that the family of graphs containing no cycle with exactly k k -chords is χ \chi -bounded, for k k large enough or of form ℓ ⁡ ( ℓ − 2 ) \ell(\ell-2) with ℓ ≥ 3 \ell\geq 3 an integer. This verifies (up to a finite number of values k k ) a conjecture of Aboulker and Bousquet (2015).

Introduction

The

of a graph
G

\omega(G)

of

It is easy to see that

[
12

that provides a family of triangle-free graphs whose chromatic number is unbounded.

In 1987, Gyárfás
[
8

For a graph

The conjecture is known for some special cases, including trees of radius

Proof overview

The first step in our proof (accomplished in
Section

4
) proves the following: given positive integers
\ell
and

is a graph with large enough chromatic number, then it contains either an induced copy of

or a cycle with exactly

Our proof of this result partly follows the proof of a related result due to Bousquet and Thomassé
[
3
]

which asserts that, given

and

, every graph with large enough chromatic number contains a triangle, an induced

, or a
k
-wheel.
Recall that every
(k+2)
-wheel (defined before

1
) contains a cycle with exactly
k
chords (see
Figure

1
). Bousquet–Thomassé’s result implies a weaker version of our first step, where the graph

is assumed to be triangle-free. To avoid the triangle-free assumption, we leverage the fact that cycles with a prescribed number of chords are easier to find than wheels. This immediately implies our second main result,
1.2
.

The next step connects to a classical result in number theory, which may be of independent interests.
Recall that Lagrange’s four square theorem asserts that every natural number can be expressed as the sum of four integer squares.
In
Section

5
, we prove two variants of this result. The first (
5.1
) shows that every large enough integer can be expressed as the sum of exactly 20 integer squares that are all larger than a given constant. The second result, given in
5.2
, is a similar statement, but here we aim to express a large number

as the sum of integers of the form
a(a+1)
, for large

. To achieve this we require that

be divisible by
4
and we express it as a sum of 80 numbers of the aforementioned form.

In
Section

6
, we prove an approximate version (
6.1
) of our main result where instead of finding a cycle with exactly

chords, we find a cycle whose number of chords is in

. To do this, we first find, for some large

, many induced copies of

with no edges between them, by applying the first paragraph above several times. To find a cycle with the required number of chords, we take paths of appropriate lengths in the copies of

, and join these by

(unimodal paths are a commonly used object in the study of

-boundedness; we will explain what they are in the next section). A key point here is that the unimodal connections contribute

chords (see

and

). To get the right number of chords, we apply one of the two number-theoretic lemmas from the previous paragraph, depending on a certain parity condition. The case that requires the use of the second lemma above is the reason why this approach yields only an approximate result.

In
Section

7
we prove the exact version of our main result,

. To achieve this we proceed similarly to the paragraph above, but analyse much more carefully the interaction between the various copies of

Preliminaries

Recall that a

in a cycle

is an edge joining two non-consecutive vertices in
C
.
Analogously, a
chord
in a path
P
is an edge that joins two non-consecutive vertices in
P
.
It will be convenient to note that, given a graph
G
and a cycle

is
e(G[V(C)])-|C|
.

We now introduce the notions of

and

, which are commonly used in the study of
\chi
-boundedness. The motivation behind these notions is the following observation. For a graph
G
, a vertex

and an integer

, denote by

the set of vertices in

at distance exactly

from

. For brevity, given a set of vertices

to denote

.

Observation 3.1
.

Let
u
be a vertex in a graph
G
. Then there is an integer
i\geq 1
such that
\chi(N_{i}(u))\geq\chi(G)/2
.

Proof.

Let
U_{0}=\bigcup_{i\geq 0}N_{2i}(u)
and let
U_{1}=\bigcup_{i\geq 0}N_{2i+1}(u)
.
Since there are no edges between distinct
N_{2i}(u)

\chi(U_{0})=\max_{i\geq 0}\chi(N_{2i}(u))
.
In particular, there exists
i_{0}
such that
\chi(U_{0})=\chi(N_{2i_{0}}(u))
. By the same argument, there exists
i_{1}
such that
\chi(U_{1})=\chi(N_{2i_{1}+1}(u))
. Since
\{U_{0},U_{1}\}
is a partition of

, we have
\chi(G)\leq\chi(U_{0})+\chi(U_{1})=\chi(N_{2i_{0}}(u))+\chi(N_{2i_{1}+1}(u))
. Therefore,
\max\{\chi(N_{2i_{0}}(u),\chi(N_{2i_{1}+1}(u))\}\geq\chi(G)/2
, as claimed.
∎

Trivially, every vertex in
N_{i}(u)
has a ‘backward’ neighbour in
N_{i-1}(u)
.
Let
N_{\leq i}(u):=\bigcup_{0\leq j\leq i}N_{i}(u)
.
By chasing the backward neighbours of two vertices

and

in
N_{i}(u)
repeatedly until they first intersect or have an edge between, we obtain a path between
x
and
y
in
N_{\leq i}(u)
that is induced except the edge

, if it is an edge in

.
If this path has length

, from

.

Together with these paths,

can be reformulated in a more systematic way.
Namely, every graph

has a subgraph

with the following properties:

•

\chi(G_{1})\geq\chi(G)/2

•

has a neighbour in

•

Such a subgraph

One can repeat this process to obtain a sequence

The

We will often say that

Once a

.
This is an induced path except for the edges between the ends in

.
Moreover, the vertices in

.
The following observation is immediate from the definitions, yet crucial for our arguments.

Observation 3.2

Let

As a final piece of notation, given an index

5
Number theory lemmas

In this section we prove two results which are variants Lagrange’s four-square theorem, which asserts that every positive integer can be written as the sum of at most four integer squares.

Lemma 5.1
.

For every
c
, every large enough
k
can be written as a sum of exactly
20
squares larger than
c^{2}
.

Proof.

Given a non-negative integer
x
, let
f(x)=x^{2}+2500c^{2}+(4c+1)^{2}
. We first claim that for every integer
x\geq 0
, the number
f(x)
can be written as the sum of exactly five squares larger than
c^{2}
. If
x\geq c
, this follows by writing

f(x)=x^{2}+(30c)^{2}+(24c)^{2}+(32c)^{2}+(4c+1)^{2}.

If
x\leq c
and
x
is even, we have

f(x)=2\left(\frac{50c+x}{2}\right)^{2}+2\left(\frac{50c-x}{2}\right)^{2}+(4c+1)^{2},

which readily implies that
f(x)
is the sum of five squares larger than
c^{2}
.
Finally, if
x\leq c
and
x
is odd, we use the following equality to reach the same conclusion.

x^{2}+(4c+1)^{2}=2\left(\frac{4c+1+x}{2}\right)^{2}+2\left(\frac{4c+1-x}{2}\right)^{2}.

Let
\ell:=k-4\left(2500c^{2}+(4c+1)^{2}\right)
and suppose that
k
is large enough so that
\ell\geq 0
.
By the four squares theorem, there exist non-negative integers
x_{1},\ldots,x_{4}
such that
\ell=x_{1}^{2}+x_{2}^{2}+x_{3}^{2}+x_{4}^{2}
. Equivalently,

k=\sum_{i=1}^{4}\left(x_{i}^{2}+2500c^{2}+(4c+1)^{2}\right)=\sum_{i=1}^{4}f(x_{i}).

By using the fact that each

is the sum of five squares larger than

, we conclude that
k
is the sum of
20
squares larger than
c^{2}
, as required.
∎

Lemma 5.2

7.1
Overview of the proof

The basic idea for the proof of

To fix this, when cleaning up the

We then turn to the proof of

7.2

8
Conclusion

We showed that the family of graphs with no cycle with exactly
k
chords is
\chi
-bounded, for every integer
k
which is either sufficiently large or of form
k=\ell(\ell-2)
, where
\ell\geq 3
is an integer. This was already known to hold for
k\in\{1,2,3\}
(see
[
18
,
1
]
).
An obvious follow-up problem, which is a conjecture due to Aboulker and Bousquet
[
1
]
, would be to extend this to all
k\geq 1
.

Conjecture 8.1

(Aboulder–Bousquet
[
1
]
)
.

For
every

k\geq 1
there is a function
f_{k}
such that, if
G
is a graph with no cycle with exactly
k
chords, then
\chi(G)\leq f_{k}(\omega(G))
.

It would also be interesting to get a better understanding of whether graphs with small clique number and large chromatic number contain cycles with
k
chords that have some sort of further structure. Whereas our proof essentially just produces a cycle with
k
chords, there are conjectures that it should be possible to find more. A
k
-fan is defined as a path
P
with one additional vertex
v
added that has exactly
k
neighbours on
P
. It is easy to see that
k
-fans contain a cycle with
k-2
chords. Thus the following would be a strengthening of the results in this paper and of Conjecture
8.1
.

Conjecture 8.2

(Davies
[
5
]
)
.

For
every

k\geq 1
there is a function
f_{k}
such that, if
G
is a graph with
k
-fan, then
\chi(G)\leq f_{k}(\omega(G))
.

Currently, this conjecture is only known to hold for
k=1
(where is it equivalent to the “
k=1
” case of Conjecture
8.1
).

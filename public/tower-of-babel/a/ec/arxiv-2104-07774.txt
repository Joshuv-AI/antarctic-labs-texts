Algebroids, AKSZ Constructions and Doubled Geometry
Vincenzo Emilio Marotta, Richard J. Szabo

Abstract
. We give a self-contained survey of some approaches aimed at a global description of the geometry underlying double field theory. After reviewing the geometry of Courant algebroids and their incarnations in the AKSZ construction, we develop the theory of metric algebroids including their graded geometry. We use metric algebroids to give a global description of doubled geometry, incorporating the section constraint, as well as an AKSZ-type construction of topological doubled sigma-models. When these notions are combined with ingredients of para-Hermitian geometry, we demonstrate how they reproduce kinematical features of double field theory from a global perspective, including solutions of the section constraint for Riemannian foliated doubled manifolds, as well as a natural notion of generalized T-duality for polarized doubled manifolds. We describe the L ∞ L_{\infty} -algebras of symmetries of a doubled geometry, and briefly discuss other proposals for global doubled geometry in the literature.

1.
Introduction

This contribution is a relatively self-contained survey of some mathematical approaches to a rigorous global formulation of the geometry underlying double field theory, that we will colloquially call ‘doubled geometry’, following standard terminology from string theory (more precise definitions will be given in Section
5
). Double field theory is an extension of supergravity in which stringy T-duality becomes a manifest symmetry. The basic example of a doubled geometry in this context comes from considering toroidal compactifications of string theory, which we shall now briefly review.

1.1.
T-Duality and Doubled Geometry

Let
V
be a
d
-dimensional real vector space, and let
\Lambda
be a lattice of
V
. The symmetry group of string theory with target space the
d
-dimensional affine torus
{\mathcal{Q}}=V/\Lambda
is isomorphic to the integer split orthogonal group
{\sf O}(d,d;\mathbb{Z})
; it preserves a flat split signature metric
\eta
induced by the canonical pairing between the lattice
\Lambda\subset V
and its dual lattice
\Lambda^{*}\subset V^{*}
. This contains the geometric subgroup
{\sf GL}(d,\mathbb{Z})\subset{\sf O}(d,d;\mathbb{Z})
generated by large diffeomorphisms of the torus
{\mathcal{Q}}
, while the rest of the group is generated by T-dualities combined with integer shifts of the Kalb-Ramond
B
-field which are not geometric symmetries of
{\mathcal{Q}}
. However, T-duality does act geometrically on the doubled torus
M:=(V\oplus V^{*})/(\Lambda\oplus\Lambda^{*})\simeq{\mathcal{Q}}\times\tilde{\mathcal{Q}}
, where
\tilde{\mathcal{Q}}=V^{*}/\Lambda^{*}
is the dual torus:
{\sf O}(d,d;\mathbb{Z})
is a subgroup of the group of large diffeomorphisms
{\sf GL}(2d,\mathbb{Z})
of
M
. In this sense string theory “sees” a doubled geometry.

Let
{\mathtt{q}}:M\to{\mathcal{Q}}
and
\tilde{\mathtt{q}}:M\to\tilde{\mathcal{Q}}
be the canonical projections. The doubled torus
M
has a canonical symplectic form
\omega
when viewed as the dual torus bundle
{\mathtt{q}}:M\to{\mathcal{Q}}
, and a pair of involutive Lagrangian distributions
L_{+}=\mathsf{Ker}(\mathrm{d}\tilde{\mathtt{q}})
and
L_{-}=\mathsf{Ker}(\mathrm{d}{\mathtt{q}})
(i.e. real polarizations of
(M,\omega)
) such that
TM\simeq L_{+}\oplus L_{-}
. As we will discuss in Section
4
, this is a simple example of a ‘para-Kähler manifold’. Then there is a pair of Lagrangian fibrations

This is an example of a ‘para-Hermitian fibration’ (see Section

1.2.
Supergravity and Courant Algebroids

Supergravity is the low-energy approximation to string theory. It has long been appreciated that the geometry underlying type II supergravity is generalized geometry on Courant algebroids
[
8
,
9
]
: the complete bosonic field content (in the NS–NS sector) can be encoded in a generalized metric on an exact Courant algebroid
[
10
,
11
]
. Exact Courant algebroids over a manifold
{\mathcal{Q}}
have underlying vector bundle
E\simeq T{\mathcal{Q}}\oplus T^{*}{\mathcal{Q}}
and are classified by the class of the
3
-form
H
-flux in
{\sf H}^{3}({\mathcal{Q}},\mathbb{R})

[
12
, Letter 1]
(see Sections
2
and
3
). In this sense generalized geometry “doubles” the tangent bundle
T{\mathcal{Q}}
, which captures diffeomorphisms and
B
-field gauge transformations as transition functions, and hence are manifest symmetries of supergravity.

However, factorized T-dualities relate supergravity in different duality frames. This is reflected mathematically in the feature that topological T-duality between principal torus bundles can be implemented, using the correspondence (
1.1
), as an isomorphism between exact Courant algebroids
[
5
]
, but not generally as a symmetry of a single exact Courant algebroid. Hence supergravity is not manifestly T-duality invariant.

1.3.
Double Field Theory and Para-Hermitian Geometry

In double field theory, one instead “doubles” the underlying
d
-dimensional manifold
{\mathcal{Q}}
to a manifold
M
of dimension
2d
, and considers geometry on the tangent bundle
TM
(see Section
6
). What this doubling means exactly will be defined precisely in this paper, but the rough idea is as follows. Double field theory is a constrained theory, whose constraint follows from the level matching condition in string theory. At present this constrained theory is not very well understood, but its reduction under a stronger constraint, called the ‘section constraint’, has been extensively studied. Solving the section constraint amounts to selecting a ‘polarization’ which reduces the geometry on
TM
to generalized geometry on an exact Courant algebroid. What the doubled geometry of
M
accomplishes is that its group of (large) diffeomorphisms contains the T-duality group in
d
-dimensions, and in this way T-duality becomes a manifest symmetry of the unconstrained double field theory. In the example of the doubled tori or doubled torus bundles
M\to W
from Section
1.1
, double field theory on
M
can be reduced in this way to string theory on a torus or a T-fold
[
1
]
.

Such a duality covariantization of supergravity, with manifest
{\sf O}(d,d)
symmetry, was suggested some time ago by Siegel
[
13
,
14
]
. A theory with manifest
{\sf O}(d,d;\mathbb{Z})
symmetry was later shown to arise naturally as a consequence of string field theory on a
d
-dimensional torus by Hull and Zwiebach
[
15
]
. One of the goals of the programme that we outline in this contribution is to write double field theory on more general doubled manifolds
M
, and to understand the meaning of the doubling for general string target spaces
{\mathcal{Q}}
. This can be achieved by using the symmetries and geometry of double field theory to define a particular type of metric algebroid
[
16
]
, which we describe in Section
5
and call a ‘DFT algebroid’ following the terminology of
[
17
]
, and encoding the bosonic fields in a generalized metric on a DFT algebroid and their dynamics by the vanishing of a suitable Ricci tensor
[
18
,
19
]
.

In this contribution we aim to describe the geometric origin of the ingredients of double field theory and its section constraint, as well as its precise geometric relation with generalized geometry, in the language of algebroids, which allows us to import techniques and ideas known from the more thoroughly studied Courant algebroids. We will discuss other approaches to global double field theory, and compare them to our perspectives, at appropriate places throughout the paper, together with many more references to the pertinent literature. We focus only on the kinematical aspects of the theory in the present paper.

As alluded to in Section
1.1
, a prominent ingredient in our treatment of doubled geometry is the notion of a para-Hermitian structure, which we discuss in Section
4
, and in particular the formulation of double field theory on almost para-Hermitian manifolds, which we discuss in Section
6
. Para-Hermitian geometry can be roughly thought of as a “real version” of complex Hermitian geometry. It has proven to be a suitable framework for addressing global issues of doubled geometry, while providing a simple and elegant description of generalized flux compactifications and non-geometric backgrounds in string theory. The relevance of para-Hermitian structures in doubled geometry was originally noticed by Hull
[
1
]
(who called them ‘pseudo-Hermitian structures’), and was later put forward in a rigorous framework by Vaisman
[
16
]
. Interest in the formalism was rekindled by Freidel, Leigh and Svoboda
[
20
]
which led to some flurry of activity in the literature, see e.g.
[
21
,
22
,
23
,
24
]
.

From this modern perspective, para-Hermitian geometry involves developing the interplay between the well-studied geometry on exact Courant algebroids and the less understood geometry on the tangent bundle of an almost para-Hermitian manifold, equipped with the structure of a DFT algebroid. The most prominent examples of almost para-Hermitian manifolds in the literature are total spaces of fibre bundles, such as the cotangent bundle
T^{*}{\mathcal{Q}}
and the tangent bundle
T{\mathcal{Q}}
of a manifold
{\mathcal{Q}}
, group manifolds of doubled Lie groups and Drinfel’d doubles, and the quotients of all these by discrete group actions, which includes the basic doubled torus and doubled twisted torus examples discussed in Section
1.1
.

We mention that para-Hermitian geometry also has a brief history of other applications to physics. Para-Kähler structures appear in the special geometry of
\mathcal{N}=2
vector multiplets in Euclidean spacetimes
[
25
,
26
]
. In
[
22
]
it was shown that para-Hermitian geometry offers an alternative geometrical formulation of both Lagrangian and non-Lagrangian dynamical systems which is more natural than the commonly employed Finsler geometry. Generalized para-Kähler structures and Born structures also appear respectively in target space geometries for doubled sigma-models with
{\mathcal{N}}=(2,2)
twisted supersymmetry and
{\mathcal{N}}=(1,1)
supersymmetry in
[
27
,
28
,
29
]
.

1.4.
Graded Geometry and AKSZ Theory

In our development of geometry on certain classes of algebroids, we shall consider their incarnations in graded geometry which leads to generalizations of the AKSZ construction of topological field theories. AKSZ sigma-models capture the topological sectors of physical string theory sigma-models for target spaces with background NS–NS fields, such as the
B
-field or the
H
-flux. They are based on the structure maps of algebroids and allow for a quantization of the underlying algebroid through the BV formalism; this is explained in Section
2
. They also allow for a better systematic description of the symmetries of algebroids, through their reformulations in terms of dg-manifolds and
L_{\infty}
-algebras.

In Section
2
we discuss this in some detail for the case of Courant algebroids; in the case of exact Courant algebroids, the corresponding AKSZ sigma-models describe the coupling of closed strings to (geometric and non-geometric) tri-fluxes. In Section
5
we discuss an extension of the AKSZ theory that writes down a topological doubled sigma-model, which unifies geometric and non-geometric fluxes with manifest T-duality invariance
[
17
]
.

Along the way, we present a new version of the correspondence between metric algebroids and graded geometry in Section
3
(see Theorem
3.53
), which is entirely geometric and avoids any explicit coordinate description. It uses more recent developments on the geometrization of degree
2
manifolds based on double vector bundles and VB-algebroids. This lends a more detailed understanding of the gauge symmetries underlying metric algeboids, and their counterparts in double field theory, as well as a clearer connection with other approaches to double field theory based on graded geometry
[
30
,
31
]
. In particular, it provides a more concise picture of the various weakenings of the axioms of a Courant algebroid described in
[
17
]
and their role in the geometry of double field theory.

1.5.
Outline of the Paper

The organization of the remainder of this paper is as follows. In Section
2
we introduce general notions of algebroids, culminating in Lie algebroids and Courant algebroids. We also develop their formulations as symplectic Lie
n
-algebroids in graded geometry and the corresponding AKSZ sigma-models (for
n=0,1,2
), together with their gauge symmetries which can be formulated in terms of flat
L_{\infty}
-algebras. In Section
3
we discuss the weakening of the notion of Courant algebroid to that of a metric algebroid, and give a new geometric formulation of a metric algebroid as a symplectic
2
-algebroid in graded geometry. In Section
4
we discuss basic aspects of para-Hermitian geometry, and in particular we introduce the canonical metric algebroid which plays a central role in the applications to double field theory. In Section
5
we give a rigorous account of doubled geometry, introducing the notion of DFT algebroid. This has a broader notion of gauge symmetry that can be formulated in terms of curved
L_{\infty}
-algebras, and we demonstrate how the AKSZ construction can be extended to define a topological sigma-model for a doubled geometry. Finally, in Section
6
we describe how everything fits together to give a rigorous formulation of some of the main ideas of double field theory, and in particular how to solve the section constraint in a completely geometric and coordinate-independent manner. We give a detailed account of how DFT algebroids reduce to Courant algebroids in different polarizations of a foliated doubled manifold, how a conventional string background, including the NS–NS fields, is recovered in the language of Riemannian foliations, and how T-duality is manifested in this framework.

2.
Leibniz-Loday Algebroids and AKSZ Sigma-Models

In this section we will review some well-known material concerning algebroids, graded geometry and the AKSZ construction. The main intent is to develop a fairly self-contained bottoms-up approach to the notion of a Courant algebroid as well as its features and applications in some detail, because later on we will be interested in suitable weakenings of this notion, and we will attempt analogous constructions in those instances. We omit several noteworthy properties and examples of Courant algebroids in this section for brevity, as they will follow as special cases of our more general considerations in Sections
3
and
4
.

2.1.
Algebroids and Leibniz-Loday Algebroids

In this paper we use a very broad notion of an ‘algebroid’ which is adapted to all applications that we shall consider.

Definition 2.1
.

An
algebroid
over a manifold
{\mathcal{Q}}
is a vector bundle
E\to{\mathcal{Q}}
equipped with an
\mathbb{R}
-bilinear bracket
[\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,]_{E}:\mathsf{\Gamma}(E)\times\mathsf{\Gamma}(E)\to\mathsf{\Gamma}(E)
on its sections, and a bundle morphism
\rho:E\to T{\mathcal{Q}}
covering the identity such that the anchored derivation property

(2.2)

\displaystyle[e,f\,e^{\prime}]_{E}=f\,[e,e^{\prime}]_{E}+\big(\rho(e)\cdot f\big)\,e^{\prime}

holds for all
e,e^{\prime}\in\mathsf{\Gamma}(E)
and
f\in C^{\infty}({\mathcal{Q}})
. The map
\rho
to the tangent bundle of
{\mathcal{Q}}
is called the
anchor map
.

An
algebroid morphism
from an algebroid
(E,[\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,]_{E},\rho)
to an algebroid
(E^{\prime},[\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,]_{E^{\prime}},\rho^{\prime})
over the same manifold is a bundle morphism
\psi:E\to E^{\prime}
covering the identity such that
\rho^{\prime}\circ\psi=\rho
and
\psi\circ[\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,]_{E}=[\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,]_{E^{\prime}}\circ(\psi\times\psi)
.

Note that here the bracket
[\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,]_{E}
need not be skew-symmetric and it need not obey the Jacobi identity. Moreover, at this primitive level the only role of the anchor map
\rho
is to implement the anchored derivation property (
2.2
) whose meaning is that, for each section
e
of
E
,
[e,\,\cdot\,]_{E}
is a first-order differential operator on
\mathsf{\Gamma}(E)
whose symbol is the vector field
\rho(e)
on
{\mathcal{Q}}
. Indeed, when
{\mathcal{Q}}
is a point, then an algebroid is simply a vector space with a binary operation.

This level of generality is needed for our considerations of doubled geometry later on. As we shall discuss throughout this paper, they have natural descriptions via the language of graded geometry in terms of vector fields and local coordinates. For the different flavours of AKSZ constructions as we use them in this paper, we will need a further algebraic condition on the bracket operation in Definition
2.1
.

Definition 2.3
.

A
Leibniz-Loday algebroid
over a manifold
{\mathcal{Q}}
is an algebroid
(E,[\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,]_{E},\rho)
whose bracket satisfies the Leibniz identity

(2.4)

\displaystyle[e,[e_{1},e_{2}]_{E}]_{E}=[[e,e_{1}]_{E},e_{2}]_{E}+[e_{1},[e,e_{2}]_{E}]_{E}\ ,

for all
e,e_{1},e_{2}\in\mathsf{\Gamma}(E)
.

When
{\mathcal{Q}}
is a point, then a Leibniz-Loday algebroid is a vector space endowed with the structure of a Leibniz-Loday algebra
[
32
]
. Generally, the anchored derivation property (
2.2
) and the Leibniz identity (
2.4
) together imply that the anchor map
\rho:E\to T{\mathcal{Q}}
of a Leibniz-Loday algebroid becomes a homomorphism of Leibniz-Loday algebras:

(2.5)

\displaystyle\rho([e_{1},e_{2}]_{E})=[\rho(e_{1}),\rho(e_{2})]_{T{\mathcal{Q}}}\ ,

where
[\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,]_{T{\mathcal{Q}}}
is the usual Lie bracket of vector fields on
T{\mathcal{Q}}
.

2.2.
AKSZ Constructions

Fix an integer
n\geq 0
. We use a somewhat simplified form of the AKSZ construction
[
33
]
as a geometric tool for building BV action functionals
[
34
]
for topological sigma-models of maps from an oriented compact
n{+}1
-dimensional manifold
\Sigma_{n+1}
(the ‘source’) to a symplectic Lie
n
-algebroid
E
over a manifold
{\mathcal{Q}}
(the ‘target’). The AKSZ sigma-models that arise in this way are Chern-Simons theories. In this sense they uniquely encode (up to isomorphism) the algebroid
E
and provide a means for quantization of
E
. A review is found in
[
35
]
.

We recall some definitions. A symplectic Lie
n
-algebroid is most concisely and naturally described in the language of graded geometry as a differential graded symplectic manifold
[
36
]
. Recall that a
graded manifold

{\mathcal{M}}=({\mathcal{Q}},{\mathcal{A}})
is a ringed space together with the structure sheaf
{\mathcal{A}}
of a graded commutative algebra over an ordinary manifold
{\mathcal{Q}}
. It can be modelled locally using even and odd coordinates in fixed degrees, and treated concretely in the language of formal differential geometry by identifying smooth functions on
{\mathcal{M}}
with formal power series in globally defined coordinates
w^{\alpha}
. We write
{\mathcal{A}}^{k}
for the subsheaf of
{\mathcal{A}}
consisting of functions of degree
k
.

Definition 2.6
.

A
differential graded manifold
(
dg-manifold
for short) is a
\mathbb{Z}
-graded manifold
{\mathcal{M}}=({\mathcal{Q}},{\mathcal{A}})
equipped with a degree
1
vector field
Q
which is integrable, that is,
[Q,Q]=2\,Q^{2}=0
. The vector field
Q
is a
homological vector field
.

When
{\mathcal{M}}
is
\mathbb{N}
-graded, so that
{\mathcal{A}}^{0}=C^{\infty}({\mathcal{Q}})
, the
\mathbb{N}
-grading can be conveniently described by means of the Euler vector field
\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}
: in coordinates
w^{\alpha}
with degrees
|w^{\alpha}|\geq 0
,
\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}=\sum_{\alpha}\,|w^{\alpha}|\,w^{\alpha}\,\frac{\partial}{\partial w^{\alpha}}
. A tensor field
T
on
{\mathcal{M}}
is said to be homogeneous of degree
n
if
\pounds_{\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}}T=n\,T
, where
\pounds_{\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}}
denotes the Lie derivative along
\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}
.
The following construction, due to Roytenberg
[
37
]
, will be used extensively in this section, as well as in Section
3
.

Theorem 2.7
.

Let
{\mathcal{M}}=({\mathcal{Q}},{\mathcal{A}})
be an
\mathbb{N}
-graded manifold equipped with a symplectic structure
\omega
of degree
|\omega|=n>0
, and associated graded Poisson bracket
\{\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,\}
of degree
-n
. Then there is a one-to-one correspondence between integrable functions on
{\mathcal{M}}
of degree
n+1
and homological symplectic vector fields.

Proof.

Since

\pounds_{\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}}\omega=n\,\omega\ ,

the Cartan homotopy formula
\pounds_{\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}}=\iota_{\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}}\circ\mathrm{d}+\mathrm{d}\circ\iota_{\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}}
implies

\omega=\tfrac{1}{n}\,\mathrm{d}\iota_{\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}}\omega\ ,

where
\iota_{\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}}
denotes contraction with the Euler vector field
\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}
.

Let
Q\in\mathfrak{X}_{1}({\mathcal{M}})
be a symplectic vector field of degree
1
. Then
[\boldsymbol{\varepsilon},Q]=Q
and
\pounds_{Q}\omega=\mathrm{d}\iota_{Q}\omega=0,

which gives

\iota_{Q}\omega=\iota_{[\boldsymbol{\varepsilon},Q]}\omega=[\pounds_{\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}},\iota_{Q}]_{\circ}(\omega)=\pounds_{\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}}\iota_{Q}\omega-\iota_{Q}(n\,\omega)\ ,

where we used the Cartan structure equations in the second equality. This yields

\iota_{Q}\omega=\tfrac{1}{n+1}\,\mathrm{d}\iota_{\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}}\iota_{Q}\omega\ .

Hence
Q
is a Hamiltonian vector field with Hamiltonian
\gamma=\frac{1}{n+1}\,\iota_{\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}}\iota_{Q}\omega\in{\mathcal{A}}^{n+1}.

The graded Jacobi identity for the Poisson bracket implies

\{\gamma,\{\gamma,f\}\}=\{\{\gamma,\gamma\},f\}-\{\gamma,\{\gamma,f\}\}

for all
f\in C^{\infty}({\mathcal{Q}}).
Thus

Q^{2}=X_{\frac{1}{2}\,\{\gamma,\gamma\}}\ ,

where
X_{\frac{1}{2}\,\{\gamma,\gamma\}}
is the Hamiltonian vector field corresponding to
\frac{1}{2}\,\{\gamma,\gamma\}\in{\mathcal{A}}^{n+2}.

Hence
Q^{2}=0
if and only if
\{\gamma,\gamma\}=0
because
\{\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,\}
is non-degenerate.

Conversely, given any integrable function
\gamma\in{\mathcal{A}}^{n+1},
we use a derived bracket to set
Q\coloneqq\{\gamma,\,\cdot\,\}.
These two constructions are inverse to each other.
∎

Remark 2.8
.

The proof of Theorem
2.7
shows that every symplectic vector field of degree
1
on an
\mathbb{N}
-graded symplectic manifold
({\mathcal{M}},\omega)
with
|\omega|=n>0
is given by a Hamiltonian of degree
n+1.

For our AKSZ constructions we assume that
{\mathcal{M}}
is
n
-graded, that is, its coordinates are concentrated in degrees
0,1,\dots,n
. In this case we also call
{\mathcal{M}}
a degree
n
manifold.

Definition 2.9
.

A
symplectic Lie
n
-algebroid
is a degree
n
dg-manifold
({\mathcal{M}},Q)
with a symplectic structure
\omega
of degree
n
for which
Q
is a symplectic vector field.

Remark 2.10
.

Generally, dg-manifolds are sometimes also refered to as ‘
Q
-manifolds’. Other terminology for symplectic Lie
n
-algebroids appearing in the literature are ‘symplectic N
Q
-manifolds of degree
n
’ or ‘QP
n
-manifolds’.

Symplectic Lie
n
-algebroids
({\mathcal{M}},Q,\omega)
arise from
n
-graded vector bundles over the degree
0
body
{\mathcal{Q}}
of
{\mathcal{M}}
, and are generally characterized by the following result, due to Kotov and Strobl
[
38
]
.

Theorem 2.11
.

Let
({\mathcal{M}},Q,\omega)
be a symplectic Lie
n
-algebroid of degree
n>1
. Then functions of degree
n-1
on
{\mathcal{M}}=({\mathcal{Q}},{\mathcal{A}})
can be identified with sections of a vector bundle
E\to{\mathcal{Q}}
equipped with the structure of a Leibniz-Loday algebroid.

Proof.

Let
f\in{\mathcal{A}}^{0}
be a function of degree
0
on
{\mathcal{Q}}
, and let
e,e^{\prime}\in{\mathcal{A}}^{n-1}
be functions of degree
n-1
on
{\mathcal{M}}
, identified as functions on
E[n-1]
for a vector bundle
E\to{\mathcal{Q}}
. Define a bracket and anchor map on sections
\mathsf{\Gamma}(E)
by the derived brackets

\displaystyle[e,e^{\prime}]_{E}:=-\{\{e,\gamma\},e^{\prime}\}\qquad\mbox{and}\qquad\rho(e)\cdot f:=(-1)^{n}\,\{\{e,\gamma\},f\}\ .

Then the anchored derivation property (
2.2
) follows from the derivation rule for the Poisson bracket
\{\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,\}
induced by the symplectic structure
\omega
, while the Maurer-Cartan equation
\{\gamma,\gamma\}=0
implies the Leibniz identity (
2.4
) for the bracket
[\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,]_{E}
, and hence that the anchor
\rho
is a bracket homomorphism.
∎

With this data, the AKSZ construction proceeds as follows. Let
T[1]\Sigma_{n+1}
be the tangent bundle of the source manifold
\Sigma_{n+1}
with degree of its fibres shifted by
1
, which is isomorphic to the exterior algebra of differential forms on
\Sigma_{n+1}
; under this identification it has a canonical homological vector field induced by the de Rham differential
\mathrm{d}
. Let
\boldsymbol{{\mathcal{M}}}={\sf Map}(T[1]\Sigma_{n+1},{\mathcal{M}})
be the mapping space of degree
0
smooth maps
\hat{X}:T[1]\Sigma_{n+1}\to{\mathcal{M}}
which intertwine the homological vector fields, that is,
\hat{X}_{*}(\mathrm{d})=Q
. Given an
n
-form
\alpha\in\Omega^{n}({\mathcal{M}})
, we can lift it to an
n
-form
\boldsymbol{\alpha}\in\Omega^{n}(\boldsymbol{{\mathcal{M}}})
by trangression to the mapping space as

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{\alpha}=\int_{T[1]\Sigma_{n+1}}\,\mu\ {\rm ev}^{*}(\alpha)\ ,

where
\mu
is the natural volume measure on
T[1]\Sigma_{n+1}
and
{\rm ev}:T[1]\Sigma_{n+1}\times\boldsymbol{{\mathcal{M}}}\to{\mathcal{M}}
is the evaluation map.
Choose a local
1
-form
\vartheta
on
{\mathcal{M}}
such that
\omega=\mathrm{d}\vartheta
. In this paper we will only explicitly write the ‘classical’ or ‘bosonic’ part of the action functional underlying the AKSZ sigma-model. It is constructed by: (i) transgressing the form
-\iota_{\mathrm{d}}\vartheta+\gamma
to the mapping space
\boldsymbol{{\mathcal{M}}}
; (ii) integrating over the odd coordinates of
T[1]\Sigma_{n+1}
; and (iii) restricting to degree
0
fields. The
1
-form
\vartheta
defines the ‘kinetic term’ and the Hamiltonian
\gamma
defines the ‘interaction term’ of the AKSZ field theory.

Remark 2.12
.

The AKSZ construction is a geometric realization of the BV formalism
[
34
]
for topological field theories with generalized gauge symmetries. The full BV master action functional is obtained by allowing the fields
\hat{X}:T[1]\Sigma_{n+1}\to{\mathcal{M}}
to be maps of arbitrary
\mathbb{Z}
-degree, which yields all auxiliary fields and anti-fields of the BV formalism. Then the graded Poisson bracket
\{\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,\}
associated to the symplectic structure
\omega
implements the BV antibracket, while the Maurer-Cartan equation
\{\gamma,\gamma\}=0
implements the classical master equation which guarantees gauge invariance of the BV action functional, as well as closure of the gauge algebra.

Remark 2.13
.

Beyond Theorem
2.11
, the further algebraic conditions and structures on symplectic Lie
n
-algebroids, as classical geometric objects, are not known generally and must be unravelled on a case by case basis. We consider below the first three degrees
n=0,1,2
in some detail. For
n=3
the algebroids were characterized in
[
39
,
40
]
and their AKSZ sigma-models applied to
{\sf SL}(5,\mathbb{R})
exceptional field theory in
[
41
,
42
]
. AKSZ constructions for higher-dimensional exceptional field theory are considered in
[
43
]
.

2.3.
Topological Quantum Mechanics

The simplest instance of the AKSZ construction is when the target is a symplectic dg-manifold of degree
0
. In this case
Q=0
, and thus a symplectic Lie
0
-algebroid is just a symplectic manifold
({\mathcal{Q}},\omega)

[
36
,
37
]
; the degree
1
Hamiltonian
\gamma
is then locally constant on
{\mathcal{Q}}
. The corresponding AKSZ sigma-model is the one-dimensional Chern-Simons theory whose Chern-Simons form is a local symplectic potential
\vartheta
for the symplectic structure:
\omega=\mathrm{d}\vartheta
. In local Darboux coordinates, where
\omega=\mathrm{d}p_{i}\wedge\mathrm{d}q^{i}
, we take
\vartheta=p_{i}\,\mathrm{d}q^{i}
; here and in the following we use the Einstein summation convention over repeated upper and lower indices.

For a cotangent bundle
{\mathcal{Q}}=T^{*}W
, with
\omega
the canonical symplectic structure and
\vartheta
the Liouville
1
-form, if
\Sigma_{1}
is an oriented compact
1
-manifold, then the AKSZ construction produces a one-dimensional topological sigma-model
of smooth maps
X:\Sigma_{1}\to{\mathcal{Q}}
with action functional

\displaystyle S(X)=\int_{\Sigma_{1}}\,X^{*}\vartheta\ .

BV quantization of this action functional defines a topological quantum mechanics, which quantizes the symplectic manifold
({\mathcal{Q}},\omega)
. When
\Sigma_{1}
is an interval this computes the
\widehat{A}
-genus of
W

[
44
,
45
]
.

2.4.
Lie Algebroids and Poisson Sigma-Models

Let us turn to the lowest non-trivial rung
n=1
on the AKSZ ladder, firstly by adding a further algebraic condition on the bracket operation in Definition
2.3

[
46
]
.

Definition 2.14
.

A
Lie algebroid

(E,[\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,]_{E},\rho)
over a manifold
{\mathcal{Q}}
is a Leibniz-Loday algebroid whose bracket is skew-symmetric:

\displaystyle[e_{1},e_{2}]_{E}=-[e_{2},e_{1}]_{E}\ ,

for all
e_{1},e_{2}\in\mathsf{\Gamma}(E)
.

It follows that the bracket operation of a Lie algebroid defines a Lie bracket on the sections of the vector bundle
E\to{\mathcal{Q}}
. When
{\mathcal{Q}}
is a point, a Lie algebroid is simply a Lie algebra. At the opposite extreme, the tangent bundle
T{\mathcal{Q}}
of any manifold
{\mathcal{Q}}
is always a Lie algebroid with the Lie bracket of vector fields
[\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,]_{T{\mathcal{Q}}}
and the identity anchor map
\mathds{1}_{T{\mathcal{Q}}}
.

Lie algebroids are canonically associated to dg-manifolds of degree
1
, by the following construction due originally to Vaintrob
[
47
]
.

Proposition 2.15
.

There is a one-to-one correspondence between Lie algebroids and dg-manifolds of degree
1
.

Proof.

Given a Lie algebroid
(E,[\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,]_{E},\rho)
over a manifold
{\mathcal{Q}}
, its corresponding degree 1 manifold is
{\mathcal{M}}=E[1].
Since
C^{\infty}(E[1])=\mathsf{\Gamma}(\text{\Large$\wedge$}^{\bullet}E^{*}),
the homological vector field
Q=\mathrm{d}_{E}
of degree 1 is the Lie algebroid differential defined by

\displaystyle\mathrm{d}_{E}\,\varepsilon(e_{1},\dots,e_{k+1})

\displaystyle\coloneqq\sum_{i=1}^{k+1}\,\rho(e_{i})\cdot\varepsilon(e_{1},\dots,\widehat{e_{i}},\dots,e_{k+1})

\displaystyle\quad\ +\sum_{i<j}\,(-1)^{i+j}\,\varepsilon([e_{i},e_{j}]_{E},e_{1},\dots,\widehat{e_{i}},\dots,\widehat{e_{j}},\dots,e_{k+1})\ ,

for all
\varepsilon\in\mathsf{\Gamma}(\text{\Large$\wedge$}^{k}E^{*})
and
e_{1},\dots,e_{k+1}\in\mathsf{\Gamma}(E)
, where the hat denotes omission of the corresponding entry. This is a derivation of degree 1 that squares to zero.

Conversely, given a degree 1 manifold
{\mathcal{M}}=({\mathcal{Q}},{\mathcal{A}})
endowed with a homological vector field
Q,
its corresponding Lie algebroid is constructed as follows. Since the categories of vector bundles and degree 1 manifolds are equivalent,
{\mathcal{M}}\simeq E[1]
for some vector bundle
E\rightarrow{\mathcal{Q}}.
Then the Lie algebroid structure on
E
is given by the
C^{\infty}({\mathcal{Q}})
-linear anchor map

\rho(e)\cdot f\coloneqq\langle Q\cdot f,e\rangle\ ,

for all
f\in C^{\infty}({\mathcal{Q}})
and
e\in\mathsf{\Gamma}(E),
where
\langle\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,\rangle
is the canonical dual pairing between
{\mathcal{A}}^{1}\simeq\mathsf{\Gamma}(E^{*})
and
\mathsf{\Gamma}(E)
. The Lie bracket is given by

\langle[e_{1},e_{2}]_{E},\varepsilon\rangle\coloneqq\rho(e_{1})\cdot\langle e_{2},\varepsilon\rangle-\rho(e_{2})\cdot\langle e_{1},\varepsilon\rangle-\langle Q\cdot\varepsilon,e_{1}\wedge e_{2}\rangle\ ,

for all
e_{1},e_{2}\in\mathsf{\Gamma}(E)
and
\varepsilon\in\mathsf{\Gamma}(E^{*}).
This bracket is skew-symmetric and satisfies the anchored derivation property (
2.2
). The Leibniz identity is equivalent to the condition that
Q
is homological:
Q^{2}=0.

∎

Remark 2.16
.

When endowed with the action of the homological vector field
Q
, the space of smooth functions
C^{\infty}(E[1])
becomes a cochain complex which computes the cohomology of the Lie algebroid
(E,[\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,]_{E},\rho)
, that is, its Chevalley-Eilenberg algebra
\big(\mathsf{\Gamma}(\text{\Large$\wedge$}^{\bullet}E^{*}),\mathrm{d}_{E}\big)
.

We can now extend Proposition
2.15
to the case of symplectic dg-manifolds of degree
1
to infer that a symplectic Lie algebroid is the same thing as a Poisson manifold
[
37
]
.

Theorem 2.17
.

There is a one-to-one correspondence between symplectic Lie algebroids and Poisson manifolds.

Proof.

Any symplectic
1
-graded manifold
({\mathcal{M}},\omega)
is canonically isomorphic to the shifted cotangent bundle
{\mathcal{M}}=T^{*}[1]{\mathcal{Q}}
over some manifold
{\mathcal{Q}}
, with the canonical symplectic
2
-form

\omega=\mathrm{d}\xi_{i}\wedge\mathrm{d}x^{i}
,
where
x^{i}
are local coordinates on
{\mathcal{Q}}
and
\xi_{i}
are odd coordinates on
T^{*}[1]{\mathcal{Q}}
corresponding to the holonomic vector fields
\frac{\partial}{\partial x^{i}}
on
{\mathcal{Q}}
.
The corresponding graded Poisson bracket
\{\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,\}
can be identified with the Schouten-Nijenhuis bracket
[\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,]_{\textrm{\tiny S}}
of multivector fields on
{\mathcal{Q}}
. The most general degree
2
function
\gamma
on
T^{*}[1]{\mathcal{Q}}
is of the form

(2.18)

\displaystyle\gamma=\tfrac{1}{2}\,\pi^{ij}(x)\,\xi_{i}\,\xi_{j}\ ,

where
\pi=\pi^{ij}\,\frac{\partial}{\partial x^{i}}\otimes\frac{\partial}{\partial x^{j}}
is any
(0,2)
-tensor on
{\mathcal{Q}}
. Then invariance of
\omega
under the associated vector field
Q=\{\gamma,\,\cdot\,\}
on
T^{*}[1]{\mathcal{Q}}

implies
\pi\in\mathsf{\Gamma}(\text{\Large$\wedge$}^{2}T{\mathcal{Q}})
, and the Maurer-Cartan equation
\{\gamma,\gamma\}=0
implies that
\pi
is a Poisson bivector on
{\mathcal{Q}}
, that is,
[\pi,\pi]_{\textrm{\tiny S}}=0
.

Conversely, if
({\mathcal{Q}},\pi)
is a Poisson manifold, then the Poisson bracket
\{f,g\}_{\pi}=\pi(\mathrm{d}f,\mathrm{d}g)
defines a Lie algebra structure on the space of smooth functions
C^{\infty}({\mathcal{Q}})
and is reproduced as a derived bracket with the Hamiltonian (
2.18
) through

\displaystyle\{f,g\}_{\pi}=-\{\{f,\gamma\},g\}\ .

The homological vector field
Q
is the dg-structure of Proposition
2.15
corresponding to the cotangent Lie algebroid
(T^{*}{\mathcal{Q}},[\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,]_{T^{*}{\mathcal{Q}}},\pi^{\sharp})
: its anchor map is the natural bundle morphism
\pi^{\sharp}:T^{*}{\mathcal{Q}}\to T{\mathcal{Q}}
induced by the bivector
\pi
,
\pi^{\sharp}\alpha:=\iota_{\alpha}\pi
, and the Lie bracket is the Koszul bracket on
1
-forms which is defined by

\displaystyle[\alpha,\beta]_{T^{*}{\mathcal{Q}}}=\pounds_{\pi^{\sharp}\alpha}\beta-\pounds_{\pi^{\sharp}\beta}\alpha-\mathrm{d}\iota_{\alpha}\iota_{\beta}\pi

for
\alpha,\beta\in\Omega^{1}({\mathcal{Q}})
. The differential
Q=\{\gamma,\,\cdot\,\}
sends a function
f\in C^{\infty}({\mathcal{Q}})
to
\pi^{\sharp}\mathrm{d}f
.
∎

We now choose the Liouville
1
-form
\vartheta=\xi_{i}\,\mathrm{d}x^{i}
on
T^{*}[1]{\mathcal{Q}}
and apply the AKSZ construction with the Hamiltonian (
2.18
). The AKSZ action functional is defined on the space of vector bundle morphisms
\hat{X}:T\Sigma_{2}\to T^{*}{\mathcal{Q}}
from the tangent bundle of an oriented compact
2
-manifold
\Sigma_{2}
; such a map is given by its base map
X:\Sigma_{2}\to{\mathcal{Q}}
and a section
A\in\mathsf{\Gamma}(T^{*}\Sigma_{2}\otimes X^{*}T^{*}{\mathcal{Q}})
. Then the action functional reads

\displaystyle S(X,A)=\int_{\Sigma_{2}}\,\langle A,\mathrm{d}X\rangle+\frac{1}{2}\,\langle A,(\pi^{\sharp}\circ X)A\rangle\ ,

where we view the fields as
1
-forms
A\in\Omega^{1}(\Sigma_{2},X^{*}T^{*}{\mathcal{Q}})
and
\mathrm{d}X\in\Omega^{1}(\Sigma_{2},X^{*}T{\mathcal{Q}})
, and
\langle\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,\rangle
denotes the natural pairing defined by pairing the dual values in the pullback bundles
X^{*}T^{*}{\mathcal{Q}}
and
X^{*}T{\mathcal{Q}}
together with the exterior product of differential forms. This is the action functional of the
Poisson sigma-model

[
48
,
49
]
, which is the most general two-dimensional topological field theory that can be obtained through the AKSZ construction
[
50
]
. When
\Sigma_{2}
is a disk, the BV quantization of this sigma-model gives a string theory derivation of Kontesevich’s deformation quantization of the Poisson manifold
({\mathcal{Q}},\pi)

[
51
,
52
]
.

2.5.
Courant Algebroids and Courant Sigma-Models

We move to the next rung at
n=2
. This time, instead of further constraining the bracket operation in Definition
2.3
, we complement it with further algebraic structure generalizing the notion of a quadratic Lie alegbra
[
53
,
54
,
55
]
.

Definition 2.19
.

A
Courant algebroid
on a manifold
{\mathcal{Q}}
is a Leibniz-Loday algebroid
(E,[\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,]_{{\tt D}},\rho)
with a symmetric non-degenerate bilinear form
\langle\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,\rangle_{E}
on the fibres of
E
which is preserved by the bracket operation:

(2.20)

\displaystyle\rho(e)\cdot\langle e_{1},e_{2}\rangle_{E}

\displaystyle=\langle[e,e_{1}]_{{\tt D}},e_{2}\rangle_{E}+\langle e_{1},[e,e_{2}]_{{\tt D}}\rangle_{E}\ ,

(2.21)

\displaystyle\langle[e,e]_{{\tt D}},e_{1}\rangle_{E}

\displaystyle=\tfrac{1}{2}\,\rho(e_{1})\cdot\langle e,e\rangle_{E}\ ,

for all
e,e_{1},e_{2}\in\mathsf{\Gamma}(E)
. The bracket of sections
[\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,]_{{\tt D}}
is called a
Dorfman bracket
.

Remark 2.22
.

The anchor map
\rho
and the pairing
\langle\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,\rangle_{E}
from Definition
2.19
induce a map
\rho^{*}:T^{*}{\mathcal{Q}}\to E
given by

\displaystyle\langle\rho^{*}(\alpha),e\rangle_{E}=\langle\rho^{\rm t}(\alpha),e\rangle\ ,

for all
\alpha\in\Omega^{1}({\mathcal{Q}})
and for all
e\in\mathsf{\Gamma}(E)
, where
\rho^{\rm t}:T^{*}{\mathcal{Q}}\to E^{*}
is the transpose of
\rho
; as before, the bilinear form
\langle\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,\rangle
(without subscript) is the canonical dual pairing between the bundle
E
and its dual
E^{*}
. The map
\rho^{*}
induces a map
{\mathcal{D}}:C^{\infty}({\mathcal{Q}})\to\mathsf{\Gamma}(E)
defined by
{\mathcal{D}}f=\rho^{*}\mathrm{d}f
, for all
f\in C^{\infty}({\mathcal{Q}})
, which obeys a derivation-like rule and is the natural generalization of the exterior derivative in the algebroid
E
. This allows us to recast the condition (
2.21
) as

(2.23)

\displaystyle[e,e]_{{\tt D}}=\tfrac{1}{2}\,{\mathcal{D}}\langle e,e\rangle_{E}\ ,

for all
e\in\mathsf{\Gamma}(E)
. In particular, the symmetric part of the Dorfman bracket
[\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,]_{{\tt D}}
is given by

(2.24)

\displaystyle[e_{1},e_{2}]_{{\tt D}}+[e_{2},e_{1}]_{{\tt D}}={\mathcal{D}}\langle e_{1},e_{2}\rangle_{E}\ ,

for all
e_{1},e_{2}\in\mathsf{\Gamma}(E)
.

Remark 2.25
.

The anchored derivation property (
2.2
) can be removed from the list of defining properties of a Courant algebroid, as it now follows from the condition (
2.20
). Then the Leibniz identity (
2.4
) together with (
2.20
) and (
2.21
) (or (
2.23
)) are a minimal set of three axioms needed to specify a Courant algebroid
[
55
]
. For later reference, we also note that (
2.23
) together with the bracket homomorphism property (
2.5
) imply

(2.26)

\displaystyle\langle{\mathcal{D}}f,{\mathcal{D}}g\rangle_{E}=0

for all functions
f,g\in C^{\infty}({\mathcal{Q}})
, or equivalently that the anchor map
\rho
vanishes identically on the image of the generalized exterior derivative
{\mathcal{D}}
.

Example 2.27
.

The most common example is the standard Courant algebroid, which features prominently in generalized geometry. It is an extension of the tangent Lie algebroid
(T{\mathcal{Q}},[\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,]_{T{\mathcal{Q}}},\mathds{1}_{T{\mathcal{Q}}})
by cotangent vectors and is based on the generalized tangent bundle

\displaystyle E=\mathbb{T}{\mathcal{Q}}=T{\mathcal{Q}}\oplus T^{*}{\mathcal{Q}}

over a manifold
{\mathcal{Q}}
, with the three natural operations

\displaystyle\langle X+\alpha,Y+\beta\rangle_{\mathbb{T}{\mathcal{Q}}}

\displaystyle=\iota_{X}\beta+\iota_{Y}\alpha\ ,

\displaystyle\rho(X+\alpha)

\displaystyle=X\ ,

\displaystyle[X+\alpha,Y+\beta]_{\tt D}

\displaystyle=[X,Y]_{T{\mathcal{Q}}}+\pounds_{X}\beta-\iota_{Y}\,\mathrm{d}\alpha\ ,

where the sections of
E=\mathbb{T}{\mathcal{Q}}
are comprised of vector fields
X,Y\in\mathsf{\Gamma}(T{\mathcal{Q}})
and
1
-forms
\alpha,\beta\in\mathsf{\Gamma}(T^{*}{\mathcal{Q}})
. In this example,
{\mathcal{D}}=\mathrm{d}
.

It is a celebrated result, due to Roytenberg
[
56
,
37
]
, and independently Ševera
[
12
]
, that a symplectic Lie
2
-algebroid is the same thing as a Courant algebroid.

Theorem 2.28
.

There is a one-to-one correspondence between symplectic Lie
2
-algebroids and Courant algebroids.

Proof.

Let
({\mathcal{M}},Q,\omega)
be a symplectic dg-manifold of degree
2
. Choose local Darboux coordinates
(x^{i},\zeta^{a},\xi_{i})
with degrees
(0,1,2)
in which the graded symplectic structure is given by

(2.29)

\displaystyle\omega=\mathrm{d}\xi_{i}\wedge\mathrm{d}x^{i}+\tfrac{1}{2}\,\eta_{ab}\,\mathrm{d}\zeta^{a}\wedge\mathrm{d}\zeta^{b}\ ,

where
\eta_{ab}
is a constant metric on the degree
1
subspace of
{\mathcal{M}}
. The most general degree
3
function
\gamma
on
{\mathcal{M}}
has the form

(2.30)

\displaystyle\gamma=\rho_{a}^{i}(x)\,\xi_{i}\,\zeta^{a}-\tfrac{1}{3!}\,T_{abc}(x)\,\zeta^{a}\,\zeta^{b}\,\zeta^{c}\ ,

where
\rho_{a}^{i}
and
T_{abc}
are degree
0
functions on the body
{\mathcal{Q}}
of
{\mathcal{M}}
. We use Theorem
2.11
to construct a Leibniz-Loday algebroid
(E,[\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,]_{\tt D},\rho)
, with operations defined on degree
1
functions
e
which are identified as local sections of a vector bundle
E
over
{\mathcal{Q}}
. Then the Poisson bracket

\displaystyle\langle e_{1},e_{2}\rangle_{E}=\{e_{1},e_{2}\}

defines a fibrewise symmetric pairing
\langle\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,\rangle_{E}
on
E
with local coordinate expression
\eta_{ab}
. It satisfies the Courant algebroid axioms (
2.20
) and (
2.21
) as a consequence of the derivation rule and the Jacobi identity for the Poisson bracket
\{\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,\}
induced by the symplectic structure
\omega
.

Conversely, given a Courant algebroid
(E,[\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,]_{\tt D},\langle\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,\rangle_{E},\rho)
on
{\mathcal{Q}}
, we define a symplectic dg-manifold
({\mathcal{M}},Q,\omega)
of degree
2
by the symplectic submanifold of
T^{*}[2]E[1]
corresponding to the isometric embedding
E\hookrightarrow E\oplus E^{*}
with respect to the Courant algebroid pairing and the canonical dual pairing. Then
x^{i}
are local coordinates on
{\mathcal{Q}}
,
\xi_{i}
are local fibre coordinates of the shifted cotangent bundle
T^{*}[2]{\mathcal{Q}}
corresponding to the holonomic vector fields
\frac{\partial}{\partial x^{i}}
, and
\zeta^{a}
are local fibre coordinates of the shifted vector bundle
E[1]
corresponding to a choice of basis
e^{a}
of sections of the dual bundle
E^{*}
. With
e_{a}
the basis of sections of
E
dual to
e^{a}
, the structure functions in (
2.29
) and (
2.30
) are given by

\displaystyle\langle e_{a},e_{b}\rangle_{E}=\eta_{ab}\ ,\quad\rho(e_{a})=\rho_{a}^{i}\,\frac{\partial}{\partial x^{i}}\qquad\mbox{and}\qquad\langle[e_{a},e_{b}]_{\tt D},e_{c}\rangle_{E}=T_{abc}\ ,

and the Courant algebroid axioms imply the Maurer-Cartan equation
\{\gamma,\gamma\}=0
. The differential
Q=\{\gamma,\,\cdot\,\}
sends a function
f\in C^{\infty}({\mathcal{Q}})
to
\rho^{\rm t}\mathrm{d}f
.
∎

We now choose the Liouville
1
-form

(2.31)

\displaystyle\vartheta=\xi_{i}\,\mathrm{d}x^{i}+\tfrac{1}{2}\,\zeta^{a}\,\eta_{ab}\,\mathrm{d}\zeta^{b}

on
{\mathcal{M}}
and apply the AKSZ construction with the Hamiltonian (
2.30
). Let
\Sigma_{3}
be an oriented compact
3
-manifold, and choose a grading-preserving connection to fix an isomorphism
{\mathcal{M}}\simeq E[1]\oplus T^{*}[2]{\mathcal{Q}}
. The AKSZ action functional is defined on the space of degree
0
maps
\hat{X}:T[1]\Sigma_{3}\to{\mathcal{M}}
, which are given by a smooth map
X:\Sigma_{3}\to{\mathcal{Q}}
, a section
A\in\mathsf{\Gamma}(T^{*}\Sigma_{3}\otimes X^{*}E)
, and a section
F\in\mathsf{\Gamma}(\text{\Large$\wedge$}^{2}T^{*}\Sigma_{3}\otimes X^{*}T^{*}{\mathcal{Q}})
. The action functional then reads

(2.32)

\displaystyle S(X,A,F)=\int_{\Sigma_{3}}\,\langle F,\mathrm{d}X\rangle+\frac{1}{2}\,\langle A,\mathrm{d}A\rangle_{E}-\langle F,(\rho\circ X)A\rangle+\frac{1}{3!}\,\langle A,[A,A]_{\tt D}\rangle_{E}\ ,

where we view the fields as differential forms
A\in\Omega^{1}(\Sigma_{3},X^{*}E)
,
\mathrm{d}X\in\Omega^{1}(\Sigma_{3},X^{*}T{\mathcal{Q}})
and
F\in\Omega^{2}(\Sigma_{3},X^{*}T^{*}{\mathcal{Q}})
, and the pairings are taken in the pullback bundles together with the exterior products of differential forms. This is the action functional of the
Courant sigma-model

[
57
,
58
,
59
,
60
]
, which is a canonical three-dimensional topological field theory associated to any Courant algebroid; its BV quantization thus gives a quantization of Courant algebroids, though as yet this has not been fully achieved. This AKSZ sigma-model is a vast generalization of three-dimensional Chern-Simons gauge theory: For the special case when
{\mathcal{Q}}
is a point, a Courant algebroid is just a quadratic Lie algebra, and (
2.32
) is the classical Chern-Simons functional on the
3
-manifold
\Sigma_{3}
.

Remark 2.33
.

Gauge invariance of the action functional (
2.32
) under local BRST transformations is equivalent to the axioms and properties of a Courant algebroid structure on the vector bundle
E\to{\mathcal{Q}}
. In particular, when
E=\mathbb{T}{\mathcal{Q}}=T{\mathcal{Q}}\oplus T^{*}{\mathcal{Q}}
is the generalized tangent bundle (with general anchor map
\rho:\mathbb{T}{\mathcal{Q}}\to T{\mathcal{Q}}
and Dorfman bracket), the tensor

\displaystyle T(e_{1},e_{2},e_{3}):=\langle[e_{1},e_{2}]_{\tt D},e_{3}\rangle_{\mathbb{T}{\mathcal{Q}}}\ ,

for
e_{1},e_{2},e_{3}\in\mathsf{\Gamma}(\mathbb{T}{\mathcal{Q}})
, encodes the
fluxes
of supergravity and the axioms give their Bianchi identities.

2.6.
Gauge Algebras and Courant Brackets

The infinitesimal symmetries of symplectic Lie
n
-algebroids are linked to gauge symmetries of the corresponding AKSZ field theories. Here we focus on those which are given by inner derivations, in a suitable sense, which we shall generally call ‘gauge transformations’.

For the first two rungs of the AKSZ ladder these symmetries are essentially the same and are easy to describe. For
n=0
, an infinitesimal symmetry of a symplectic manifold
({\mathcal{Q}},\omega)
corresponds to a symplectic vector field
X\in\mathsf{\Gamma}(T{\mathcal{Q}})
, that is,
\pounds_{X}\omega=0
; they close a Lie algebra under the Lie bracket of vector fields by virtue of the Cartan structure equation

\displaystyle[\pounds_{X},\pounds_{Y}]_{\circ}=\pounds_{[X,Y]_{T{\mathcal{Q}}}}

for the Lie derivatives along
X,Y\in\mathsf{\Gamma}(T{\mathcal{Q}})
. The Hamiltonian vector fields
X_{f}
by definition satisfy
\iota_{X_{f}}\omega=\mathrm{d}f
, for functions
f\in C^{\infty}({\mathcal{Q}})
, and form a natural subalgebra which is isomorphic to the Lie algebra of smooth functions on
{\mathcal{Q}}
with the corresponding Poisson bracket; these are called (infinitesimal) canonical transformations and we think of them as infinitesimal ‘gauge symmetries’, generated by the action of functions on
{\mathcal{Q}}
through the Poisson bracket.
For
n=1
, an infinitesimal symmetry of a Poisson manifold
({\mathcal{Q}},\pi)
similarly corresponds to a Poisson vector field
X\in\mathsf{\Gamma}(T{\mathcal{Q}})
, that is,
\pounds_{X}\pi=0
. Again the Hamiltonian vector fields
X_{f}:=\pi^{\sharp}\mathrm{d}f
form a Lie algebra isomorphic to the Poisson algebra:
[X_{f},X_{g}]_{T{\mathcal{Q}}}=X_{\{f,g\}_{\pi}}
for
f,g\in C^{\infty}({\mathcal{Q}})
.

For
n=2
, the situation is more involved. Let
(E,[\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,]_{\tt D},\langle\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,\rangle_{E},\rho)
be a Courant algebroid on a manifold
{\mathcal{Q}}
. By definition, the adjoint action of
\mathsf{\Gamma}(E)
on itself by the Dorfman bracket

\displaystyle\pounds_{e}^{\tt D}:=[e,\,\cdot\,]_{\tt D}\ ,

for
e\in\mathsf{\Gamma}(E)
,
is a first order differential operator whose symbol is the vector field
\rho(e)
on
{\mathcal{Q}}
. It acts as an inner derivation of
(E,[\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,]_{\tt D},\langle\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,\rangle_{E})
, that is, it is an infinitesimal symmetry of the Courant algebroid. The operator
\pounds_{e}^{\tt D}:\mathsf{\Gamma}(E)\to\mathsf{\Gamma}(E)
is called a
generalized Lie derivative
on the Courant algebroid; the reason for the terminology is best motivated by recalling the standard Courant algebroid of Example
2.27
where it coincides with the generalized Lie derivative of generalized geometry.

The collection of generalized Lie derivatives for all sections of
E
is a vector space which we will think of as the (infinitesimal) ‘gauge transformations’ of the Courant algebroid. They should close a Lie algebra with respect to the commutator bracket on endomorphisms of
\mathsf{\Gamma}(E)
; we refer to this Lie algebra as the
gauge algebra
of the Courant algebroid. From the Leibniz identity (
2.4
) we directly obtain

(2.34)

\displaystyle\pounds^{\tt D}_{e_{1}}\circ\pounds^{\tt D}_{e_{2}}=\pounds^{\tt D}_{[e_{1},e_{2}]_{\tt D}}+\pounds^{\tt D}_{e_{2}}\circ\pounds^{\tt D}_{e_{1}}\ ,

for
e_{1},e_{2}\in\mathsf{\Gamma}(E)
, which shows that the gauge closure can be expressed in terms of the Dorfman bracket on sections of
E
. However, the Dorfman bracket is not skew-symmetric so it is not the natural bracket operation to use for this algebra. To write a manifestly skew-symmetric closure relation, we subtract from (
2.34
) the corresponding identity with
e_{1}
and
e_{2}
interchanged, and after rearrangement we obtain the gauge algebra

(2.35)

\displaystyle\big[\pounds_{e_{1}}^{\tt D},\pounds_{e_{2}}^{\tt D}\big]_{\circ}=\pounds^{\tt D}_{[e_{1},e_{2}]_{\tt C}}\ ,

where

\displaystyle[e_{1},e_{2}]_{\tt C}:=\tfrac{1}{2}\,\big([e_{1},e_{2}]_{\tt D}-[e_{2},e_{1}]_{\tt D}\big)

is the skew-symmetrization of the Dorfman bracket. The skew-symmetric bracket on sections
[\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,]_{\tt C}
is called a
Courant bracket
.

In contrast to the Dorfman bracket, the Courant bracket is neither an algebroid bracket nor a Lie bracket, as it violates both the anchored derivation property (
2.2
) and the Jacobi identity (which is equivalent to the Leibniz identity (
2.4
) for a skew-symmetric bracket). Nevertheless, it can be used to completely characterize the Courant algebroid axioms
[
56
]
.

Proposition 2.36
.

Let
(E,[\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,]_{\tt D},\langle\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,\rangle_{E},\rho)
be a Courant algebroid over a manifold
{\mathcal{Q}}
. Then the compatibility conditions on the Dorfman bracket can be equivalently expressed in terms of the Courant bracket as

\displaystyle[e_{1},f\,e_{2}]_{\tt C}

\displaystyle=f\,[e_{1},e_{2}]_{\tt C}+\big(\rho(e_{1})\cdot f\big)\,e_{2}-\tfrac{1}{2}\,\langle e_{1},e_{2}\rangle_{E}\,{\mathcal{D}}f\ ,

\displaystyle{\sf Jac}_{\tt C}(e_{1},e_{2},e_{3})

\displaystyle={\mathcal{D}}\,{\sf Nij}_{\tt C}(e_{1},e_{2},e_{3})\ ,

\displaystyle\rho(e_{1})\cdot\langle e_{2},e_{3}\rangle_{E}

\displaystyle=\big\langle[e_{1},e_{2}]_{{\tt C}}+\tfrac{1}{2}\,{\mathcal{D}}\langle e_{1},e_{2}\rangle_{E},e_{3}\big\rangle_{E}+\big\langle e_{2},[e_{1},e_{3}]_{{\tt C}}+\tfrac{1}{2}\,{\mathcal{D}}\langle e_{1},e_{3}\rangle_{E}\big\rangle_{E}\ ,

for all
e_{1},e_{2},e_{3}\in\mathsf{\Gamma}(E)
and
f\in C^{\infty}({\mathcal{Q}})
, where

\displaystyle{\sf Jac}_{\tt C}(e_{1},e_{2},e_{3}):=[[e_{1},e_{2}]_{\tt C},e_{3}]_{\tt C}+[[e_{3},e_{1}]_{\tt C},e_{2}]_{\tt C}+[[e_{2},e_{3}]_{\tt C},e_{1}]_{\tt C}

is the Jacobiator of the Courant bracket, and

\displaystyle{\sf Nij}_{\tt C}(e_{1},e_{2},e_{3}):=\tfrac{1}{3!}\,\big(\langle[e_{1},e_{2}]_{\tt C},e_{3}\rangle_{E}+\langle[e_{3},e_{1}]_{\tt C},e_{2}\rangle_{E}+\langle[e_{2},e_{3}]_{\tt C},e_{1}\rangle_{E}\big)

is the Nijenhuis tensor of the Courant bracket.

Proof.

The three conditions on the Courant bracket easily follow from using (
2.24
) to express its deviation from the Dorfman bracket as

(2.37)

\displaystyle[e_{1},e_{2}]_{\tt D}=[e_{1},e_{2}]_{\tt C}+\tfrac{1}{2}\,{\mathcal{D}}\langle e_{1},e_{2}\rangle_{E}\ ,

and substituting this into the anchored derivation property (
2.2
), the Leibniz identity (
2.4
), and the metric compatibility condition (
2.20
), respectively.
∎

2.7.
Flat
L_{\infty}
-Algebras

The formulation of the gauge algebra (
2.35
) in terms of the Courant bracket still leaves open one puzzle: the violation of the Jacobi identity by the Courant bracket from Proposition
2.36
appears to be in contradiction with the vanishing Jacobiator
{\sf Jac}_{\circ}=0
of the commutator bracket. This in fact poses no problem as one can explicitly check
[{\mathcal{D}}\,{\sf Nij}_{\tt C}(e_{1},e_{2},e_{3}),e]_{\tt D}=0
for all sections
e,e_{1},e_{2},e_{3}\in\mathsf{\Gamma}(E)
. However, the violation of the Jacobi identity itself in Proposition
2.36
, which is controlled by the generalized exterior derivative of the Nijenhuis tensor of the Courant bracket, suggests a more natural formulation of the gauge algebra and its closure in the language of strong homotopy Lie algebras, or
L_{\infty}
-algebras, which are homotopy coherent weakenings of the axioms of a Lie algebra. They generally underlie the gauge structure and dynamics of classical perturbative field theories. This formulation is dual to the BV formalism and is naturally tailored to control field theories with open gauge algebras and reducible symmetries, like the Courant sigma-model, as well as violations of the Jacobi identities.

We begin by recalling the definitions
[
61
]
.

Definition 2.38
.

Let
R
be a commutative ring. An
L_{\infty}
-algebra
over
R
is a free graded
R
-module
L=\bigoplus_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\,L_{k}
with a degree
1
derivation

\displaystyle Q:\text{\Large$\odot$}^{\bullet}L^{*}[1]\longrightarrow\text{\Large$\odot$}^{\bullet}L^{*}[1]

which is a differential,
Q^{2}=0
, making the symmetric algebra
\text{\Large$\odot$}^{\bullet}L^{*}[1]
into a commutative dg-algebra over
R
, called the
Chevalley-Eilenberg algebra
of the
L_{\infty}
-algebra
L
.

An
L_{\infty}
-morphism
from an
L_{\infty}
-algebra
(L,Q)
to an
L_{\infty}
-algebra
(L^{\prime},Q^{\prime})
is an algebra homomorphism
\Psi:\text{\Large$\odot$}^{\bullet}L^{*}[1]\longrightarrow\text{\Large$\odot$}^{\bullet}L^{\prime*}[1]
of degree
0
which intertwines the derivations:
\Psi\circ Q=Q^{\prime}\circ\Psi
.

By virtue of the Leibniz rule, the derivation is determined entirely by its action on
L^{*}[1]
, hence we may view it as an
R
-linear map
Q:L^{*}[1]\to\text{\Large$\odot$}^{\bullet}L^{*}[1]
. Let
Q_{m}:L^{*}[1]\to\text{\Large$\odot$}^{m}L^{*}[1]
be the homogeneous components of
Q
, for
m=0,1,2,\dots
. Let
s:L^{*}\to L^{*}[1]
be the suspension map of degree
1
; this is the tautological isomorphism which identifies
v\in L_{k}^{*}
with
v\in L^{*}[1]_{k-1}:=L^{*}_{k}
. Taking the dual gives a sequence of maps
Q_{m}^{*}:(\text{\Large$\odot$}^{m}L^{*}[1])^{*}\to(L^{*}[1])^{*}
which, after composing with the suspension, we can consider as maps

\displaystyle\ell_{m}:=s^{-1}\circ Q_{m}^{*}\circ s^{\otimes m}:\text{\Large$\wedge$}^{m}L\longrightarrow L

for
m=0,1,2,\dots
, which are called the
m
-brackets
of the
L_{\infty}
-algebra
L
; they are multilinear graded skew-symmetric maps of degree
2-m
which satisfy a sequence of higher homotopy Jacobi identities among them, encoded in the condition
Q^{2}=0
.

A
flat

L_{\infty}
-algebra is an
L_{\infty}
-algebra with
\ell_{0}=0
. In this case
\ell_{1}
is a differential and
\ell_{2}
is a cochain map obeying the Jacobi identity up to exact terms; hence the cohomology of the cochain complex
(L,\ell_{1})
of a flat
L_{\infty}
-algebra is a graded Lie algebra. A
curved

L_{\infty}
-algebra is an
L_{\infty}
-algebra with
\ell_{0}\neq 0
. Differential graded Lie algebras can be regarded as
L_{\infty}
-algebras with differential
\ell_{1}
, bracket
\ell_{2}
and
\ell_{m}=0
for all
m>2
.

On general grounds, any dg-manifold
({\mathcal{M}},Q)
is naturally described as a (local)
L_{\infty}
-algebra which completely captures its algebraic structure: in this case
L
is the graded vector space of polynomial functions on
{\mathcal{M}}

[
62
]
. In these applications, we take
R=\mathbb{R}
and work in an appropriate category of topological vector spaces with the natural morphisms, tensor products, and so on, though we do not indicate this explicitly in the notation. In particular,
L^{*}:={\sf Hom}_{\mathbb{R}}(L,\mathbb{R})
means the continuous dual to
L
, and
\text{\Large$\odot$}^{\bullet}L^{*}
means the completed symmetric algebra defined using the continuous product and the completed projective tensor product.

Here we are interested in symplectic Lie
n
-algebroids
({\mathcal{M}},Q,\omega)
, which have associated flat
n
-term
L_{\infty}
-algebras, comprising functions on
{\mathcal{M}}
of degrees
0,1,\dots,n-1
, whose
m
-brackets
\ell_{m}
can be computed from derived brackets with the Hamiltonian
\gamma
of the symplectic dg-structure
[
63
]
. For
n=0
all brackets are identically zero, giving the trivial
L_{\infty}
-algebra on a symplectic manifold
({\mathcal{Q}},\omega)
, while for
n=1
we obtain only one non-zero bracket
\ell_{2}=\{\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,\}_{\pi}
on
L=L_{0}=C^{\infty}({\mathcal{Q}})
, which recovers the Lie algebra of Poisson brackets on a Poisson manifold
({\mathcal{Q}},\pi)
. For
n=2
, we recover the
L_{\infty}
-algebra of a Courant algebroid, originally due to Roytenberg and Weinstein
[
64
]
.

Theorem 2.39
.

Let
(E,[\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot]_{\tt D},\langle\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,\rangle_{E},\rho)
be a Courant algebroid on a manifold
{\mathcal{Q}}
. Then there is a flat
2
-term
L_{\infty}
-algebra on
L=L_{-1}\oplus L_{0}
with

\displaystyle L_{-1}=C^{\infty}({\mathcal{Q}})\qquad\mbox{and}\qquad L_{0}=\mathsf{\Gamma}(E)\ ,

whose non-zero brackets are given by

\displaystyle\ell_{1}(f)

\displaystyle={\mathcal{D}}f\ ,

\displaystyle\ell_{2}(e_{1},e_{2})=[e_{1},e_{2}]_{\tt C}\quad

\displaystyle\ \,,\,\ \quad\ell_{2}(e_{1},f)=\tfrac{1}{2}\,\langle e_{1},{\mathcal{D}}f\rangle_{E}\ ,

\displaystyle\ell_{3}(e_{1},e_{2},e_{3})

\displaystyle=-{\sf Nij}_{\tt C}(e_{1},e_{2},e_{3})\ ,

for all
f\in C^{\infty}({\mathcal{Q}})
and
e_{1},e_{2},e_{3}\in\mathsf{\Gamma}(E)
.

Remark 2.40
.

In the context of gauge algebras, the brackets from
L_{-1}
in Theorem
2.39
represent non-trivial “higher” gauge symmetries, that is, gauge symmetries among the gauge transformations in
L_{0}
themselves; in other words, the gauge symmetries of a Courant algebroid are
reducible
. This happens as well in the BV formulation of the Courant sigma-model, which has an open gauge algebra of reducible symmetries. The corresponding dual
L_{\infty}
-algebra formalism involves infinitely many brackets. An explicit
L_{\infty}
-morphism from the Courant algebroid
L_{\infty}
-algebra of Theorem
2.39
to the gauge
L_{\infty}
-algebra of the Courant sigma-model is constructed by Grewcoe and Jonke in
[
65
]
; this requires extending the
2
-term cochain complex of Theorem
2.39
by the degree
1
subspace
L_{1}=\mathsf{\Gamma}(T{\mathcal{Q}})
and the anchor map
\ell_{1}|_{L_{0}}=\rho
in order to accomodate the field-dependent gauge algebra of the Courant sigma-model.

3.
Metric Algebroids

In this section we will introduce and study a weakening of the notion of Courant algebroid from Section
2.5
, which is the natural generalization for the algebroids underlying doubled geometry that we consider later on
[
16
]
.

3.1.
Metric Algebroids and Pre-Courant Algebroids

Definition 3.1
.

A
metric algebroid
over a manifold
{\mathcal{Q}}
is an algebroid
(E,\llbracket\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,\rrbracket_{\tt D},\rho)
with a fibrewise non-degenerate pairing
\langle\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,\rangle_{E}\in\mathsf{\Gamma}(\text{\Large$\odot$}^{2}E^{*})
which is preserved by the bracket operation:

(3.2)

\displaystyle\rho(e)\cdot\langle e_{1},e_{2}\rangle_{E}

\displaystyle=\langle\llbracket e,e_{1}\rrbracket_{\tt D},e_{2}\rangle_{E}+\langle e_{1},\llbracket e,e_{2}\rrbracket_{\tt D}\rangle_{E}\ ,

(3.3)

\displaystyle\langle\llbracket e,e\rrbracket_{\tt D},e_{1}\rangle_{E}

\displaystyle=\tfrac{1}{2}\,\rho(e_{1})\cdot\langle e,e\rangle_{E}\ ,

for all
e,e_{1},e_{2}\in\mathsf{\Gamma}(E)
. The bracket of sections
\llbracket\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,\rrbracket_{\tt D}
is called a
D-bracket
.

A
metric algebroid morphism
from a metric algebroid
(E,\llbracket\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,\rrbracket_{\tt D},\langle\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,\rangle_{E},\rho)
to a metric algebroid
(E^{\prime},\llbracket\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,\rrbracket^{\prime}_{\tt D},\langle\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,\rangle_{E^{\prime}},\rho^{\prime})
over the same manifold is an algebroid morphism
\psi
which is an isometry, that is,
\langle\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,\rangle_{E^{\prime}}\circ(\psi\times\psi)=\langle\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,\rangle_{E}
.

A metric algebroid
(E,\llbracket\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,\rrbracket_{\tt D},\langle\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,\rangle_{E},\rho)
is called
regular
if its anchor map
\rho:E\to T{\mathcal{Q}}
has constant rank, and
transitive
if
\rho
is surjective. A
split metric algebroid
is a metric algebroid whose underlying vector bundle
E\to{\mathcal{Q}}
is the Whitney sum
E=A\oplus A^{*}
of a vector bundle
A\to{\mathcal{Q}}
and its dual
A^{*}\to{\mathcal{Q}}
.

Example 3.4
.

A Courant algebroid is precisely a metric algebroid which is also a Leibniz-Loday algebroid.

Remark 3.5
.

As can be anticipated from Example
3.4
, metric algebroids share some features in common with Courant algebroids. In particular, the anchored derivation property (
2.2
) again follows from the axiom (
3.2
), and the discussion of Remark
2.22
applies verbatum to a metric algebroid to show that the symmetric part of the D-bracket
\llbracket\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,\rrbracket_{\tt D}
can be written in terms of the generalized exterior derivative
{\mathcal{D}}:C^{\infty}({\mathcal{Q}})\to\mathsf{\Gamma}(E)
and the metric
\langle\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,\rangle_{E}
analogously to (
2.24
):

(3.6)

\displaystyle\llbracket e_{1},e_{2}\rrbracket_{\tt D}+\llbracket e_{2},e_{1}\rrbracket_{\tt D}={\mathcal{D}}\langle e_{1},e_{2}\rangle_{E}\ ,

for all
e_{1},e_{2}\in\mathsf{\Gamma}(E)
. For later use, we also note that (
3.6
) together with the anchored derivation property (
2.2
) imply the left derivation property

(3.7)

\displaystyle\llbracket f\,e_{1},e_{2}\rrbracket_{\tt D}=f\,\llbracket e_{1},e_{2}\rrbracket_{\tt D}-\big(\rho(e_{2})\cdot f\big)\,e_{1}+{\mathcal{D}}f\,\braket{e_{1},e_{2}}_{E}\ ,

for all
f\in C^{\infty}({\mathcal{Q}})
and
e_{1},e_{2}\in\mathsf{\Gamma}(E)
.

Despite the similarities noted in Remark
3.5
, the failure of the Leibniz identity (
2.4
) for a generic metric algebroid means that its anchor map
\rho
is not a bracket morphism in general. On the other hand, one can impose the homomorphism property (
2.5
) independently, and arrive at an important class of (non-Courant) metric algebroids which resemble Courant algebroids in the closest possible way
[
66
,
67
,
68
]
.

Definition 3.8
.

A metric algebroid
(E,\llbracket\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,\rrbracket_{\tt D},\langle\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,\rangle_{E},\rho)
over a manifold
{\mathcal{Q}}
is a
pre-Courant algebroid
if its anchor map
\rho:E\to T{\mathcal{Q}}
is a bracket morphism:

\rho(\llbracket e_{1},e_{2}\rrbracket_{\tt D})=[\rho(e_{1}),\rho(e_{2})]_{T{\mathcal{Q}}}\ ,

for all
e_{1},e_{2}\in\mathsf{\Gamma}(E)
.

Example 3.9
.

Let
(E,\llbracket\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,\rrbracket_{\tt D},\langle\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,\rangle_{E},\rho)
be a regular pre-Courant algebroid over a manifold
{\mathcal{Q}}
. Let
\rho^{*}:T^{*}{\mathcal{Q}}\to E
be the map defined by
\langle\rho^{*}(\alpha),e\rangle_{E}=\langle\rho^{\rm t}(\alpha),e\rangle
for
\alpha\in\Omega^{1}({\mathcal{Q}})
and
e\in\mathsf{\Gamma}(E)
. Then
\mathsf{Im}(\rho^{*})
is a coisotropic subbundle of
E
and
\mathsf{\Gamma}(\mathsf{Im}(\rho^{*}))
is an abelian ideal of
(\mathsf{\Gamma}(E),\llbracket\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,\rrbracket_{\tt D}).
It follows that
\rho\circ\rho^{*}=0,
and there is a chain complex of bundle morphisms

(3.10)

T^{*}{\mathcal{Q}}\xlongrightarrow{\rho^{*}}E\xlongrightarrow{\rho}T{\mathcal{Q}}\ .

The pre-Courant algebroid is said to be
exact
if (
3.10
) is a short exact sequence.

An isotropic splitting
s:T{\mathcal{Q}}\rightarrow E
of an exact pre-Courant algebroid defines an isomorphism
E\simeq\mathbb{T}{\mathcal{Q}}=T{\mathcal{Q}}\oplus T^{*}{\mathcal{Q}}
to the generalized tangent bundle of
{\mathcal{Q}}
, viewed as a split metric algebroid, as well as a
3
-form
H\in\Omega^{3}({\mathcal{Q}})
by

\displaystyle H(X,Y,Z)=\big\langle\llbracket s(X),s(Y)\rrbracket_{\tt D},s(Z)\big\rangle_{E}\ ,

for
X,Y,Z\in\mathsf{\Gamma}(T{\mathcal{Q}})
. The D-bracket
\llbracket\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,\rrbracket_{\tt D}
maps to the bracket on the splitting
E\simeq\mathbb{T}{\mathcal{Q}}
given by

\llbracket X+\alpha,Y+\beta\rrbracket_{{\tt D}}=[X,Y]_{T{\mathcal{Q}}}+\pounds_{X}\beta-\iota_{Y}\,\mathrm{d}\alpha+\iota_{X}\iota_{Y}H\ ,

for all
X,Y\in\mathsf{\Gamma}(T{\mathcal{Q}})
and
\alpha,\beta\in\mathsf{\Gamma}(T^{*}{\mathcal{Q}})
. This is the Dorfman bracket of the standard Courant algebroid from Example
2.27
, now ‘twisted’ by the
3
-form
H
. However, since
\llbracket\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,\rrbracket_{\tt D}
violates the Leibniz identity (
2.4
), the
3
-form
H
is not closed and so does not represent any class in
{\sf H}^{3}({\mathcal{Q}},\mathbb{R}).
In other words, there is no extension of the Ševera classification of exact Courant algebroids
[
12
,
9
]
to exact pre-Courant algebroids.

Example 3.11
.

Let
({\mathcal{Q}},\eta)
be a pseudo-Riemannian manifold, and let
\nabla^{\tt LC}
denote the Levi-Civita connection of
\eta
. Define a bracket operation
\llbracket\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,\rrbracket^{\eta}_{\tt D}:\mathsf{\Gamma}(T{\mathcal{Q}})\times\mathsf{\Gamma}(T{\mathcal{Q}})\to\mathsf{\Gamma}(T{\mathcal{Q}})
by

\displaystyle\eta(\llbracket X,Y\rrbracket^{\eta}_{\tt D},Z)=\eta(\nabla^{\tt LC}_{X}Y-\nabla_{Y}^{\tt LC}X,Z)+\eta(\nabla_{Z}^{\tt LC}X,Y)

for vector fields
X,Y,Z\in\mathsf{\Gamma}(T{\mathcal{Q}})
. Then
(T{\mathcal{Q}},\llbracket\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,\rrbracket^{\eta}_{\tt D},\eta,\mathds{1}_{T{\mathcal{Q}}})
is a metric algebroid
[
3
]
which is not a pre-Courant algebroid. This D-bracket can be twisted by any
3
-form
H\in\Omega^{3}({\mathcal{Q}})
, similarly to Example
3.9
.

Definition 3.12
.

An
almost D-structure
on a metric algebroid
(E,\llbracket\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,\rrbracket_{\tt D},\langle\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,\rangle_{E},\rho)
is an isotropic vector subbundle
L\subset E.
It is a
D-structure
if
L
is also involutive with respect to the D-bracket
\llbracket\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,\rrbracket_{\tt D},
that is,

\llbracket\mathsf{\Gamma}(L),\mathsf{\Gamma}(L)\rrbracket_{\tt D}\subseteq\mathsf{\Gamma}(L).

Example 3.13
.

Let
(E,\llbracket\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,\rrbracket_{\tt D},\langle\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,\rangle_{E},\rho)
be a split exact pre-Courant algebroid over a manifold
{\mathcal{Q}}
. Then
T^{*}{\mathcal{Q}}\subset E
is a D-structure.

Given that Courant algebroids correspond to symplectic Lie
2
-algebroids (cf. Theorem
2.28
), and in view of the possibility of developing an AKSZ-type sigma-model formulation for quantization of a metric algebroid, it is natural to wonder what objects metric algebroids correspond to in graded geometry. In the remainder of this section we develop this correspondence in some detail. Whereas our main result (Theorem
3.53
) should not be surprising to experts, here we follow a more contemporary approach to the geometrization of degree
2
manifolds, see e.g.
[
69
,
70
]
, and hence offer a new geometric perspective on the correspondence.

3.2.
Involutive Double Vector Bundles

In the proof of Proposition
2.15
we saw that degree
1
manifolds correspond geometrically to vector bundles: if
{\mathcal{M}}=({\mathcal{Q}},{\mathcal{A}})
is a
1
-graded manifold, then
{\mathcal{M}}\simeq E[1]
for a vector bundle
E\to{\mathcal{Q}}
. Let us now recall some well-known facts about the geometrization of degree
2
manifolds. In particular, we discuss the implications of Batchelor’s Theorem for degree 2 manifolds, see
[
70
]
.

Theorem 3.14
.

Any degree
n
manifold is (non-canonically) isomorphic to a split degree
n
manifold.

Let us spell this out explicitly for a degree 2 manifold
{\mathcal{M}}=({\mathcal{Q}},{\mathcal{A}})
over a manifold
{\mathcal{Q}},
where
{\mathcal{A}}
is its sheaf of functions. The subsheaves
{\mathcal{A}}^{1}
and
{\mathcal{A}}^{2}
of
{\mathcal{A}}
, consisting of functions of degree
1
and
2
, respectively, are locally free finitely-generated
C^{\infty}({\mathcal{Q}})
-modules. Hence there exist vector bundles
E\to{\mathcal{Q}}
and
\bar{F}\to{\mathcal{Q}}
such that
{\mathcal{A}}^{1}\simeq\mathsf{\Gamma}(E^{*})
and
{\mathcal{A}}^{2}\simeq\mathsf{\Gamma}(\bar{F}^{*}).
Abusing notation slightly, the subalgebra of
{\mathcal{A}}
generated by
C^{\infty}({\mathcal{Q}})\oplus\mathsf{\Gamma}(E^{*})
is isomorphic to
\mathsf{\Gamma}(\text{\Large$\wedge$}^{\bullet}E^{*}).
Thus

\mathsf{\Gamma}(\text{\Large$\wedge$}^{\bullet}E^{*})\cap\mathsf{\Gamma}(\bar{F}^{*})\simeq\mathsf{\Gamma}(\text{\Large$\wedge$}^{2}E^{*})

is a proper
C^{\infty}({\mathcal{Q}})
-submodule of
\mathsf{\Gamma}(\bar{F}^{*}).
Since, as a sheaf of functions,
\mathsf{\Gamma}(\bar{F}^{*})/\mathsf{\Gamma}(\text{\Large$\wedge$}^{2}E^{*})
is again a locally free finitely-generated
C^{\infty}({\mathcal{Q}})
-module, there is a vector bundle
F
over
{\mathcal{Q}}
such that

\mathsf{\Gamma}(\bar{F}^{*})\,\big/\,\mathsf{\Gamma}(\text{\Large$\wedge$}^{2}E^{*})\simeq\mathsf{\Gamma}(F^{*})\ .

This gives a short exact sequence of
C^{\infty}({\mathcal{Q}})
-modules

0\longrightarrow\mathsf{\Gamma}(\text{\Large$\wedge$}^{2}E^{*})\longrightarrow\mathsf{\Gamma}(\bar{F}^{*})\longrightarrow\mathsf{\Gamma}(F^{*})\longrightarrow 0\ ,

which yields a short exact sequence of the underlying vector bundles over
{\mathcal{Q}}
:

(3.15)

0\longrightarrow\text{\Large$\wedge$}^{2}E^{*}\xlongrightarrow{i}\bar{F}^{*}\xlongrightarrow{p}F^{*}\longrightarrow 0\ .

A choice of splitting of either of these sequences gives an isomorphism
{\mathcal{M}}\simeq E[1]\oplus F[2].

Remark 3.16
.

This construction also yields a one-to-one correspondence (up to isomorphisms) between degree 2 manifolds and pairs of vector bundles
(E,\bar{F})
with a surjective vector bundle morphism
\bar{p}\colon\bar{F}\rightarrow\text{\Large$\wedge$}^{2}E.

In other words, degree 2 manifolds are in one-to-one correspondence with
involutive sequences
(see
[
69
]
), i.e. short exact sequences of the form

(3.17)

0\longrightarrow F\xlongrightarrow{}\bar{F}\xlongrightarrow{\bar{p}}\text{\Large$\wedge$}^{2}E\longrightarrow 0\ ,

where
F=\mathsf{Ker}(\bar{p}).
This in turn aids in understanding the correspondence between degree 2 manifolds and involutive double vector bundles, and hence the correspondence with metric double vector bundles
[
70
,
69
]
. For background and details on double vector bundles that we use in the following, see
[
46
]
.

3.3.
Poisson Structures and VB-Algebroids

To give further structure to the involutive sequences corresponding to degree
2
manifolds, we shall now require that our graded manifolds are endowed with a Poisson structure.

Definition 3.30
.

A
degree

n

Poisson manifold
is a degree
n
manifold
{\mathcal{M}}=({\mathcal{Q}},{\mathcal{A}})
together with a degree
-n
Poisson structure, i.e. an
\mathbb{R}
-bilinear map
\{\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,\}\colon{\mathcal{A}}\times{\mathcal{A}}\rightarrow{\mathcal{A}}
satisfying

(3.31)

\displaystyle|\{f,g\}|

\displaystyle=|f|+|g|-n\ ,

(3.32)

\displaystyle\{f,g\}

\displaystyle=(-1)^{(|f|+n)\,(|g|+n)}\,\{g,f\}\ ,

(3.33)

\displaystyle\{f,g\,h\}

\displaystyle=\{f,g\}\,h+(-1)^{(|f|+n)\,|g|}\,g\,\{f,h\}\ ,

(3.34)

\displaystyle\{f,\{g,h\}\}

\displaystyle=\{\{f,g\},h\}+(-1)^{(|f|+n)\,(|g|+n)}\,\{g,\{f,h\}\}\ ,

for all homogeneous functions
f,\,g,\,h\in{\mathcal{A}}
of degree
|f|
,
|g|
and
|h|
, respectively.

Let
\mathrm{\mathsf{CDO}}(E)\rightarrow{\mathcal{Q}}
denote the Lie algebroid of covariant differential operators on a vector bundle
E\to{\mathcal{Q}},
i.e. its sections are differential operators acting on
\mathsf{\Gamma}(E).

Theorem 3.35
.

There is a one-to-one correspondence between degree
-2
Poisson brackets on a degree 2 manifold
{\mathcal{M}}=({\mathcal{Q}},{\mathcal{A}})
and the following structures on its involutive sequence of vector bundles (
3.15
):

(1)

A symmetric bilinear pairing
\braket{\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,}_{E^{*}}
on
E^{*};

(2)

A Lie algebroid
(\bar{F}^{*},[\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,]_{\bar{F}^{*}},\bar{\mathtt{a}});
and

(3)

A Lie algebroid action
\Psi\colon\bar{F}^{*}\rightarrow\mathrm{\mathsf{CDO}}(E^{*})
preserving
\braket{\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,}_{E^{*}}
such that

(3.36)

[\zeta,\varepsilon_{1}\wedge\varepsilon_{2}]_{\bar{F}^{*}}=\big(\Psi(\zeta)\cdot\varepsilon_{1}\big)\wedge\varepsilon_{2}+\varepsilon_{1}\wedge\big(\Psi(\zeta)\cdot\varepsilon_{2}\big)\ ,

and

(3.37)

\Psi(\varepsilon_{1}\wedge\varepsilon_{2})\cdot\varepsilon=\braket{\varepsilon_{2},\varepsilon}_{E^{*}}\,\varepsilon_{1}-\braket{\varepsilon_{1},\varepsilon}_{E^{*}}\,\varepsilon_{2}\ ,

for all
\varepsilon,\varepsilon_{1},\varepsilon_{2}\in\mathsf{\Gamma}(E^{*})
and
\zeta\,\in\mathsf{\Gamma}(\bar{F}^{*}).

Proof.

Let
\{\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,\}
be a degree
-2
Poisson bracket on
{\mathcal{M}}
, and define

\braket{\varepsilon_{1},\varepsilon_{2}}_{E^{*}}\coloneqq\{\varepsilon_{1},\varepsilon_{2}\}\ ,

for all
\varepsilon_{1},\varepsilon_{2}\in\mathsf{\Gamma}(E^{*}).
Then
\braket{\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,}_{E^{*}}
is symmetric and bilinear by the graded skew-symmetry (
3.32
) and the graded derivation rule (
3.33
). This gives (1).

For any degree 2 function
\zeta\in\mathsf{\Gamma}(\bar{F}^{*})
, the graded derivation rule (
3.33
) implies that
\{\zeta,\,\cdot\,\}\in\mathsf{Der}(C^{\infty}({\mathcal{Q}}))
is a derivation of the algebra of functions
C^{\infty}({\mathcal{Q}})
, and we can define a vector bundle morphism
\bar{{\mathtt{a}}}\colon\bar{F}^{*}\rightarrow T{\mathcal{Q}}
in terms of the corresponding morphism of
C^{\infty}({\mathcal{Q}})
-modules
\bar{{\mathtt{a}}}\colon\mathsf{\Gamma}(\bar{F}^{*})\rightarrow\mathsf{\Gamma}(T{\mathcal{Q}})
by

\bar{{\mathtt{a}}}(\zeta)\cdot f\coloneqq\{\zeta,f\}\ ,

where
\bar{{\mathtt{a}}}(g\,\zeta)\cdot f=g\,\bar{{\mathtt{a}}}(\zeta)\cdot f
for all
f,g\in C^{\infty}({\mathcal{Q}})
also because of (
3.33
). The vector space
\mathsf{\Gamma}(\bar{F}^{*})
can be endowed with the bracket

[\zeta_{1},\zeta_{2}]_{\bar{F}^{*}}\coloneqq\{\zeta_{1},\zeta_{2}\}

for any degree 2 functions
\zeta_{1},\zeta_{2}\in\mathsf{\Gamma}(\bar{F}^{*}),
because
\{{\mathcal{A}}^{2},{\mathcal{A}}^{2}\}\subseteq{\mathcal{A}}^{2}.
This is a skew-symmetric bracket because of (
3.32
) and it satisfies the (ungraded) Jacobi identity because the Poisson bracket does on degree 2 functions, i.e.
[\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,]_{\bar{F}^{*}}
is a Lie bracket. Lastly,
\bar{\mathtt{a}}\colon\mathsf{\Gamma}(\bar{F}^{*})\rightarrow\mathsf{\Gamma}(T{\mathcal{Q}})
is a bracket homomorphism:

\displaystyle\bar{{\mathtt{a}}}([\zeta_{1},\zeta_{2}]_{\bar{F}^{*}})\cdot f=\{\{\zeta_{1},\zeta_{2}\},f\}=\{\zeta_{1},\{\zeta_{2},f\}\}-\{\zeta_{2},\{\zeta_{1},f\}\}=[\bar{{\mathtt{a}}}(\zeta_{1}),\bar{{\mathtt{a}}}(\zeta_{2})]_{T{\mathcal{Q}}}\cdot f\ ,

for all
\zeta_{1},\zeta_{2}\in\mathsf{\Gamma}(\bar{F}^{*})
and
f\in C^{\infty}({\mathcal{Q}})
, where in the second equality we used the graded Jacobi identity (
3.34
). Thus
(\bar{F}^{*},[\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,]_{\bar{F}^{*}},\bar{{\mathtt{a}}})
is a Lie algebroid over
{\mathcal{Q}}
. This gives (2).

Define the vector bundle morphism
\Psi\colon\bar{F}^{*}\rightarrow\mathrm{\mathsf{CDO}}(E^{*})
by

\Psi(\zeta)\cdot\varepsilon\coloneqq\{\zeta,\varepsilon\}

for all
\zeta\in\mathsf{\Gamma}(\bar{F}^{*})
and
\varepsilon\in\mathsf{\Gamma}(E^{*}),
where we used
\{{\mathcal{A}}^{2},{\mathcal{A}}^{1}\}\subseteq{\mathcal{A}}^{1}.
The graded derivation rule (
3.33
) implies that
\Psi
takes values in
\mathrm{\mathsf{CDO}}(E^{*})
with symbols given by
\bar{\mathtt{a}}
:

\Psi(\zeta)\cdot(f\,\varepsilon)=\{\zeta,f\,\varepsilon\}=f\,\{\zeta,\varepsilon\}+\{\zeta,f\}\,\varepsilon=f\,\big(\Psi(\zeta)\cdot\varepsilon\big)+\big(\bar{{\mathtt{a}}}(\zeta)\cdot f\big)\,\varepsilon\ .

Furthermore,
\Psi
is a Lie algebroid morphism:

\displaystyle\Psi([\zeta_{1},\zeta_{2}]_{\bar{F}^{*}})\cdot\varepsilon=\Psi(\zeta_{1})\cdot\big(\Psi(\zeta_{2})\cdot\varepsilon\big)-\Psi(\zeta_{2})\cdot\big(\Psi(\zeta_{1})\cdot\varepsilon\big)=[\Psi(\zeta_{1}),\Psi(\zeta_{2})]_{\circ}\cdot\varepsilon\ ,

as a result of the graded Jacobi identity (
3.34
). The graded Jacobi identity also
gives

\bar{\mathtt{a}}(\zeta)\cdot\braket{\varepsilon_{1},\varepsilon_{2}}_{E^{*}}=\braket{\Psi(\zeta)\cdot\varepsilon_{1},\varepsilon_{2}}_{E^{*}}+\braket{\varepsilon_{1},\Psi(\zeta)\cdot\varepsilon_{2}}_{E^{*}}\ ,

and so the Lie algebroid action
\Psi
preserves the symmetric bilinear pairing
\braket{\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,}_{E^{*}}.
Lastly, the equations (
3.36
) and (
3.37
) are immediate consequences of the graded derivation rule (
3.33
). This gives (3).

Conversely, given (1)–(3), we define the Poisson brackets
\{{\mathcal{A}}^{2},{\mathcal{A}}^{2}\}
and
\{{\mathcal{A}}^{2},{\mathcal{A}}^{0}\}
from the Lie algebroid on
\bar{F}^{*}
,
\{{\mathcal{A}}^{2},{\mathcal{A}}^{1}\}
from the
\braket{\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,}_{E^{*}}
-preserving Lie algebroid action of
\bar{F}^{*}
on
E^{*},

\{{\mathcal{A}}^{1},{\mathcal{A}}^{1}\}
from the symmetric bilinear form
\braket{\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,}_{E^{*}}
on
E^{*}
, and we set
\{{\mathcal{A}}^{1},{\mathcal{A}}^{0}\}=0.
These brackets are then extended to arbitrary functions by the graded derivation rule.
∎

Corollary 3.38
.

Under the correspondence of Theorem
3.35
, the involutive sequence

0\longrightarrow\text{\Large$\wedge$}^{2}E^{*}\xlongrightarrow{i}\bar{F}^{*}\xlongrightarrow{p}F^{*}\longrightarrow 0

is a short exact sequence of Lie algebroids.

Proof.

The equations (
3.36
) and (
3.37
) give

[\varepsilon_{1}\wedge\varepsilon_{2},\varepsilon_{3}\wedge\varepsilon_{4}]_{\bar{F}^{*}}=\braket{\varepsilon_{2},\varepsilon_{3}}_{E^{*}}\,\varepsilon_{1}\wedge\varepsilon_{2}-\braket{\varepsilon_{1},\varepsilon_{3}}_{E^{*}}\,\varepsilon_{2}\wedge\varepsilon_{4}+\braket{\varepsilon_{2},\varepsilon_{4}}_{E^{*}}\,\varepsilon_{3}\wedge\varepsilon_{1}-\braket{\varepsilon_{1},\varepsilon_{4}}_{E^{*}}\,\varepsilon_{3}\wedge\varepsilon_{2}\ ,

hence
\text{\Large$\wedge$}^{2}E^{*}
is a Lie subalgebroid of
\bar{F}^{*}.
Thus
\text{\Large$\wedge$}^{2}E^{*}
is a Lie algebroid ideal of
\bar{F}^{*}
because of the involutivity of
\text{\Large$\wedge$}^{2}E^{*}
and (
3.36
). The restriction of the anchor map
\bar{\mathtt{a}}
to
\text{\Large$\wedge$}^{2}E^{*}
vanishes:

\bar{\mathtt{a}}(\varepsilon_{1}\wedge\varepsilon_{2})\cdot f=\{\varepsilon_{1}\wedge\varepsilon_{2},f\}=\{f,\varepsilon_{1}\}\wedge\varepsilon_{2}+\varepsilon_{1}\wedge\{f,\varepsilon_{2}\}=0

because
\{f,\varepsilon_{1}\}=\{f,\varepsilon_{2}\}=0
for degree reasons. Hence
F^{*}
can be endowed with a Lie algebroid given by the bracket

[p(\zeta_{1}),p(\zeta_{2})]_{F^{*}}:=p([\zeta_{1},\zeta_{2}]_{\bar{F}^{*}})

and anchor
{\mathtt{a}}\colon F^{*}\rightarrow T{\mathcal{Q}}
through which
\bar{\mathtt{a}}:\bar{F}^{*}\to T{\mathcal{Q}}
factors:

\bar{{\mathtt{a}}}={\mathtt{a}}\circ p\ .

Therefore (
3.15
) is a short exact sequence of Lie algebroids.
∎

Proposition 3.43
.

Let

for all

and
\varepsilon\in\mathsf{\Gamma}(E^{*}).

Proof.

Choosing a splitting of the short exact sequence (

), we can write

\gamma=\varepsilon_{0}\otimes\zeta+\varepsilon_{1}\wedge\varepsilon_{2}\wedge\varepsilon_{3}\ ,

where

and

.
Then

\{\gamma,f\}=\{\varepsilon_{0}\otimes\zeta,f\}=\varepsilon_{0}\,\{\zeta,f\}=\varepsilon_{0}\,\braket{\zeta,\mathrm{d}f}=\boldsymbol{\gamma}_{1}(\mathrm{d}f)\ ,

where we identify
\mathrm{d}f
with its corresponding section of
F
, and here
\braket{\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,}
is the duality pairing between
F
and
F^{*}.
The last equality follows from (

The second equality is obtained from calculating

\displaystyle\{\gamma,\varepsilon\}

\displaystyle=\braket{\varepsilon_{0},\varepsilon}_{E^{*}}\,\zeta-\varepsilon_{0}\,\wedge\{\zeta,\varepsilon\}+\braket{\varepsilon,\varepsilon_{1}}_{E^{*}}\,\varepsilon_{2}\wedge\varepsilon_{3}-\braket{\varepsilon,\varepsilon_{2}}_{E^{*}}\,\varepsilon_{1}\wedge\varepsilon_{3}+\braket{\varepsilon,\varepsilon_{3}}_{E^{*}}\,\varepsilon_{1}\wedge\varepsilon_{2}\ .

where here

is the duality pairing between

for the component of

in

3.4.
Symplectic 2-Algebroids and Symplectic Almost Lie 2-Algebroids

In order to establish a correspondence with pseudo-Euclidean vector bundles, we require our graded manifold to be endowed with a symplectic structure.

Definition 3.46
.

A

is a degree
n

{\mathcal{M}}=({\mathcal{Q}},{\mathcal{A}})
with degree

containing

We are now ready to discuss the correspondence between symplectic degree 2 manifolds and pseudo-Euclidean vector bundles together with their gauge symmetries, through a result due to Roytenberg

Proposition 3.47
.

There is a one-to-one correspondence between symplectic degree
2
manifolds
({\mathcal{M}},\{\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,\})
and pseudo-Euclidean vector bundles
(E^{*},\braket{\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,}_{E^{*}}).
The associated Lie algebroid
(F^{*},[\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,]_{F^{*}},{\mathtt{a}})
is isomorphic to the tangent Lie algebroid
(T{\mathcal{Q}},[\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,]_{T{\mathcal{Q}}},\mathds{1}_{T{\mathcal{Q}}}),
and its involutive sequence of Lie algebroids

is isomorphic (as Lie algebroids) to the Atiyah sequence of
(E^{*},\braket{\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,}_{E^{*}})
:

0\longrightarrow\mathfrak{so}(E^{*})\xlongrightarrow{}\mathsf{At}(E^{*},\braket{\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,}_{E^{*}})\longrightarrow T{\mathcal{Q}}\longrightarrow 0\ .

Proof.

Let
(x^{i},\varepsilon^{a},\zeta^{\mu})
be local coordinates on
{\mathcal{M}}=({\mathcal{Q}},{\mathcal{A}})
of degrees
(0,1,2)
. Since the Poisson bracket
\{\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,\}
is symplectic, it follows that

(3.48)

\det\big(\{\zeta^{\mu},x^{i}\}(x)\big)\neq 0\qquad\mbox{and}\qquad\det\big(\{\varepsilon^{a},\varepsilon^{b}\}(x)\big)\neq 0\ ,

for all
x\in{\mathcal{Q}}.
The second condition in (
3.48
) implies that the symmetric bilinear pairing
\braket{\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,}_{E^{*}}
on

constructed in Theorem
3.35
is non-degenerate, so it endows

with the structure of a pseudo-Euclidean vector bundle over
{\mathcal{Q}}
. The first condition in (
3.48
) shows that the anchor map
{\mathtt{a}}\colon F^{*}\rightarrow T{\mathcal{Q}}
defined in Corollary
3.38
is an isomorphism. From Theorem
3.35
and Corollary
3.38
there is a morphism of Lie algebroid sequences given by

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\text{\Large$\wedge$}^{2}E^{*}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\bar{F}^{*}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces F^{*}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathfrak{so}(E^{*})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathsf{At}(E^{*},\braket{\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,}_{E^{*}})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces T{\mathcal{Q}}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces i}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces p}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Psi}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\mathtt{a}}}

From the non-degeneracy of
\braket{\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,}_{E^{*}}
it follows that
\text{\Large$\wedge$}^{2}E^{*}\simeq\mathfrak{so}(E^{*}).
Since this is a commutative diagram and

is an isomorphism, it follows that

is an isomorphism as well.
∎

Theorem 3.53
.

There is a one-to-one correspondence between symplectic
2
-algebroids and metric algebroids.

Proof.

Let
({\mathcal{M}},\{\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,\},\gamma)
be a symplectic
2
-algebroid, and consider its involutive sequence of Lie algebroids. By Proposition
3.47
, the vector bundle

is endowed with a fibrewise pseudo-Euclidean metric
\braket{\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,}_{E^{*}}
.
Define

\rho(\varepsilon)\cdot f\coloneqq-\{\{\gamma,\varepsilon\},f\}\ ,

for all
\varepsilon\in\mathsf{\Gamma}(E^{*})
and
f\in C^{\infty}({\mathcal{Q}}).
By the derivation property of the Poisson bracket, this defines a map

\rho\colon\mathsf{\Gamma}(E^{*})\longrightarrow{\mathsf{Der}}\big(C^{\infty}({\mathcal{Q}})\big)\ ,

which is a morphism of
C^{\infty}({\mathcal{Q}})
-modules and thus induces a vector bundle morphism

\rho\colon E^{*}\longrightarrow T{\mathcal{Q}}\ .

The D-bracket on

is given by

\llbracket\varepsilon_{1},\varepsilon_{2}\rrbracket_{\tt D}\coloneqq-\{\{\gamma,\varepsilon_{1}\},\varepsilon_{2}\}

for all
\varepsilon_{1},\varepsilon_{2}\in\mathsf{\Gamma}(E^{*}).

The compatibility conditions (
3.2
) and (
3.3
) of Definition
3.1
follow straightforwardly from the graded Jacobi identity for the graded Poisson bracket.
As a further check, the anchored derivation property (
2.2

follows from the graded derivation property of the Poisson bracket. Thus
(E^{*},\llbracket\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,\rrbracket_{\tt D},\langle\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,\rangle_{E^{*}},\rho)

For the converse statement we have to work a bit harder. Let
(E^{*},\llbracket\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,\rrbracket_{\tt D},\braket{\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,}_{E^{*}},\rho)
be a metric algebroid over
{\mathcal{Q}}
. By Proposition
3.47
, the underlying pseudo-Euclidean vector bundle corresponds to a symplectic degree
2

({\mathcal{M}},\{\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,\})
. Define the pair of vector bundle morphisms
(\boldsymbol{\gamma}_{1},\boldsymbol{\gamma}_{2})
by the dual pairings

(3.54)

\displaystyle\braket{\boldsymbol{\gamma}_{1}(\mathrm{d}f),\mathrm{d}_{D}\varepsilon}

\displaystyle\coloneqq\rho(\varepsilon)\cdot f\ ,

(3.55)

\displaystyle\braket{\boldsymbol{\gamma}_{2}(\mathrm{d}_{D}\varepsilon),\mathrm{d}f}

\displaystyle\coloneqq\rho(\varepsilon)\cdot f\ ,

(3.56)

\displaystyle\braket{\boldsymbol{\gamma}_{2}(\mathrm{d}_{D}\varepsilon_{1}),\mathrm{d}_{D}\varepsilon_{2}}

\displaystyle\coloneqq\llbracket\varepsilon_{1},\varepsilon_{2}\rrbracket_{{\tt D}}\ .

We shall prove that the pair

satisfies the properties (1)–(3) of Theorem
3.23
, and hence characterizes a degree 3 function
\gamma
on
{\mathcal{M}}
. Property (1) of Theorem

follows immediately from the definitions (

) and (
3.55

For property (2), we compute

\displaystyle\braket{(\varepsilon_{1}\otimes\mathrm{d}f)^{*}\circ\boldsymbol{\gamma}_{1}^{*}-\boldsymbol{\gamma}_{1}\circ(\varepsilon_{1}\otimes\mathrm{d}f),\mathrm{d}_{D}\varepsilon_{2}}

\displaystyle=-\braket{\boldsymbol{\gamma}_{1}(\mathrm{d}f),\mathrm{d}_{D}\varepsilon_{2}}\,\varepsilon_{1}+\boldsymbol{\gamma}_{1}(\mathrm{d}f)\,\braket{\varepsilon_{1},\varepsilon_{2}}_{E^{*}}

where we use the usual definition of the generalized exterior derivative

On the other hand, from the Leibniz rule for the Lie algebroid differential

and the left derivation property (

\displaystyle\braket{\boldsymbol{\gamma}_{2}(\varepsilon_{1}\otimes\mathrm{d}f),\mathrm{d}_{D}\varepsilon_{2}}

Finally, for property (3) of Theorem

\braket{\boldsymbol{\gamma}_{2}(\mathrm{d}_{D}\varepsilon_{1}),\mathrm{d}_{D}\varepsilon_{2}}+\braket{\boldsymbol{\gamma}_{2}(\mathrm{d}_{D}\varepsilon_{2}),\mathrm{d}_{D}\varepsilon_{1}}=\llbracket\varepsilon_{1},\varepsilon_{2}\rrbracket_{\tt D}+\llbracket\varepsilon_{2},\varepsilon_{1}\rrbracket_{\tt D}={\mathcal{D}}\braket{\varepsilon_{1},\varepsilon_{2}}_{E^{*}}\ .

Remark 3.58
.

Introduce the map

The natural home for metric algebroids, and in particular split exact pre-Courant algebroids, is provided by para-Hermitian geometry. This provides a precise mathematical framework for a global notion of ‘doubled geometry’ in string theory, as originally suggested by Vaisman

4.1.
Para-Hermitian Vector Bundles

We start with an overview of the main ideas and their relation to some of the concepts introduced in Sections

Definition 4.1
.

Let
E\rightarrow{\mathcal{Q}}
be a vector bundle of even rank
2d
over a manifold
{\mathcal{Q}}
. A

on

is a vector bundle automorphism

covering the identity such that

,
K\neq\pm\,\mathds{1}_{E}
, and the
\pm\,1
-eigenbundles of

have equal rank

. The pair
(E,K)
is a
para-complex vector bundle
.

If in addition

admits a fibrewise metric
\braket{\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,}_{E}\in\mathsf{\Gamma}(\text{\Large$\odot$}^{2}E^{*})
of split signature
(d,d)
such that

\braket{K(e_{1}),K(e_{2})}_{E}=-\braket{e_{1},e_{2}}_{E}\ ,

for all
e_{1},e_{2}\in\mathsf{\Gamma}(E)
, then the pair
(K,\braket{\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,}_{E})
is
a
para-Hermitian structure
on
E
and the triple
(E,K,\braket{\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,}_{E})
is a
para-Hermitian vector bundle
.

A
para-Hermitian bundle morphism
from a para-Hermitian vector bundle
(E,K,\braket{\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,}_{E})
to a para-Hermitian vector bundle
(E^{\prime},K^{\prime},\braket{\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,}_{E^{\prime}})
over the same manifold is an isometry
\psi:(E,\braket{\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,}_{E})\to(E^{\prime},\braket{\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,}_{E^{\prime}})
covering the identity which intertwines the para-complex structures:
\psi\circ K=K^{\prime}\circ\psi
.

The
\pm\,1
-eigenbundles
L_{\pm}
of

split the vector bundle
E
into a Whitney sum

E=L_{+}\oplus L_{-}\ ,

such that
L_{\pm}
are maximally
isotropic with respect to the fibrewise metric
\braket{\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,}_{E}.

.

Let

be a vector bundle of rank

\braket{\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,}_{E}

.

The compatibility condition between

for all

. The eigenbundles

are also maximally isotropic with respect to

and

K
are compatible in the sense of Definition
4.1
,
and the subbundles
A
and
A^{*}
are maximally isotropic with respect to
\braket{\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,}_{A\oplus A^{*}}.
Thus we obtain a fundamental
2
-form

\omega(a+a^{\textrm{\tiny$\vee$}},b+b^{\textrm{\tiny$\vee$}})=\langle\,a,b^{\textrm{\tiny$\vee$}}\rangle-\langle b,a^{\textrm{\tiny$\vee$}}\rangle\ ,

which is the additional natural non-degenerate pairing that can be defined in this case.

This construction applies to any split metric algebroid
(E,\llbracket\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,\rrbracket_{\tt D},\braket{\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,}_{E},\rho)
. A special instance is the generalized tangent bundle
E=\mathbb{T}{\mathcal{Q}}
of Example
2.27
, for which
A=T{\mathcal{Q}}
.

Example 4.5
.

Let
(E,\llbracket\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,\rrbracket_{\tt D},\braket{\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,}_{E},\rho)
be an exact pre-Courant algebroid on
{\mathcal{Q}}
specified by the short exact sequence (
3.10
) from Example
3.9
,
with fibrewise metric
\braket{\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,}_{E}
and anchor map

\rho:E\rightarrow T{\mathcal{Q}}.

From the definition of
\rho^{*}
and exactness of the
sequence (
3.10
), it follows that the subbundle

\mathsf{Im}(\rho^{*})\subset E,
which is isomorphic to
T^{*}{\mathcal{Q}},
is maximally isotropic with respect to
\braket{\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,}_{E}.

A choice of
isotropic splitting
s:T{\mathcal{Q}}\to E
of (
3.10
) gives a Whitney sum decomposition

E=\mathsf{Im}(s)\oplus\mathsf{Im}(\rho^{*})\ ,

with an associated para-complex structure defined by

K_{s}\big(s(X)+\rho^{*}(\alpha)\big)=s(X)-\rho^{*}(\alpha)\ ,

for all
X\in\mathsf{\Gamma}(T{\mathcal{Q}})
and
\alpha\in\mathsf{\Gamma}(T^{*}{\mathcal{Q}}).
The para-complex structure
K_{s}
is
compatible with the metric
\braket{\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,}_{E},
and in this way
E
is endowed with a
para-Hermitian structure. This para-Hermitian structure is isomorphic to the para-Hermitian structure on the
generalized tangent bundle
\mathbb{T}{\mathcal{Q}}
from
Example
4.4
with
A=T{\mathcal{Q}}
.

4.2.
Generalized Metrics and Born Geometry

We shall now introduce a notion of generalized metric associated to a para-Hermitian structure on a vector bundle.

Definition 4.6
.

Let
E\rightarrow{\mathcal{Q}}
be a vector bundle endowed with a fibrewise pseudo-Euclidean
metric
\braket{\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,}_{E}
. A
generalized
(
Euclidean
)
metric
on
E
is an automorphism
I\in{\sf Aut}(E)
such that

I^{2}=\mathds{1}_{E}
,
I\neq\pm\,\mathds{1}_{E}
, and

{\mathcal{H}}(e_{1},e_{2}):=\braket{I(e_{1}),e_{2}}_{E}\ ,

for all
e_{1},e_{2}\in\mathsf{\Gamma}(E)
, defines a fibrewise Euclidean metric
{\mathcal{H}}
on
E
.

A generalized metric determines a decomposition

E=V_{+}\oplus V_{-}

into the
\pm\,1
-eigenbundles of
I
, such that the subbundle
V_{+}\subset E
is maximally positive-definite with respect to the metric
\braket{\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,}_{E}
and
V_{-}
is the orthogonal complement of
V_{+}
with
respect to
\braket{\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,}_{E}.
Any generalized metric induces an isomorphism
{\mathcal{H}}^{\flat}\in{\sf Hom}(E,E^{*})
which satisfies the compatibility condition

\braket{{\mathcal{H}}^{\flat}(e_{1}),{\mathcal{H}}^{\flat}(e_{2})}_{E}^{-1}=\braket{e_{1},e_{2}}_{E}

with the fibrewise metric
\braket{\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,}_{E}
.

This definition takes the following concrete form, proven in
[
76
]
, when the metric
\braket{\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,}_{E}
is part of a para-Hermitian structure.

Proposition 4.7
.

Let
(E,K,\braket{\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,}_{E})
be a para-Hermitian vector bundle over a manifold
{\mathcal{Q}}
. A generalized metric
I\in{\sf Aut}(E)
defines a unique pair
(g_{+},b_{+})
,
where
g_{+}\in\mathsf{\Gamma}(\text{\Large$\odot$}^{2}L_{+}^{*})
is a fibrewise Euclidean metric on the

+1
-eigenbundle
L_{+}\subset E
and
b_{+}\in\mathsf{\Gamma}(\text{\Large$\wedge$}^{2}L_{+}^{*})
is a
2
-form on
L_{+}
.
Conversely, any such pair
(g_{+},b_{+})
uniquely defines a generalized metric on
(E,K,\braket{\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,}_{E})
.

Since the eigenbundles
L_{\pm}
are both maximally isotropic with respect to
\braket{\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,}_{E}
, and
V_{+}
is maximally positive-definite, it follows that
L_{\pm}\cap V_{+}=0
and
L_{\pm}\cap V_{-}=0
.
The pair
(g_{+},b_{+})
induces a fibrewise metric
g_{-}
on
L_{-}
by

\displaystyle g_{-}(e_{-},e_{-}^{\prime})=g_{+}^{-1}\big(\braket{e_{-},\,\cdot\,}_{E},\braket{e_{-}^{\prime},\,\cdot\,}_{E}\big)

and a skew-symmetric vector bundle map
B_{+}\in{\sf Hom}(L_{+},L_{-})
by

\displaystyle\braket{B_{+}(e_{+}),e_{+}^{\prime}}_{E}=b_{+}(e_{+},e_{+}^{\prime})=-\braket{e_{+},B_{+}(e_{+}^{\prime})}_{E}\ ,

for all
e_{\pm},e_{\pm}^{\prime}\in\mathsf{\Gamma}(L_{\pm})
. In the splitting
E=L_{+}\oplus L_{-}
associated with the para-complex structure
K
, the Euclidean metric
{\mathcal{H}}
then assumes the matrix form

\displaystyle{\mathcal{H}}=\bigg(\begin{matrix}g_{+}+B_{+}^{\rm t}\,g_{-}\,B_{+}&-B_{+}^{\rm t}\,g_{-}\\
-g_{-}\,B_{+}&g_{-}\end{matrix}\bigg)\ ,

where
B_{+}^{\rm t}:L_{-}^{*}\to L_{+}^{*}
is the transpose map.

Example 4.8
.

Let
E=\mathbb{T}{\mathcal{Q}}=T{\mathcal{Q}}\oplus T^{*}{\mathcal{Q}}
be the generalized tangent bundle over a manifold
{\mathcal{Q}}
. A generalized metric
I\in{\sf Aut}(\mathbb{T}M)
is equivalent to
a Riemannian metric
g
and a
2
-form
b
on
{\mathcal{Q}}
. This is a special case of the notion of generalized metric in generalized geometry
[
78
,
9
]
, and
{\mathcal{H}}
assumes the standard form

\displaystyle{\mathcal{H}}=\bigg(\begin{matrix}g+b\,g^{-1}\,b&-b\,g^{-1}\\
-g^{-1}\,b&g^{-1}\end{matrix}\bigg)

with respect to the splitting
\mathbb{T}{\mathcal{Q}}=T{\mathcal{Q}}\oplus T^{*}{\mathcal{Q}}
.

Definition 4.9
.

A
compatible generalized metric
on a para-Hermitian bundle
(E,K,\braket{\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,}_{E})
is a generalized metric
\mathcal{H}_{0}
on
E
which is compatible with the fundamental
2
-form
\omega
:

\omega^{-1}\big({\mathcal{H}}_{0}^{\flat}(e_{1}),{\mathcal{H}}_{0}^{\flat}(e_{2})\big)=-\omega(e_{1},e_{2})\ ,

for all
e_{1},e_{2}\in\mathsf{\Gamma}(E)
.
The triple
(K,\braket{\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,}_{E},\mathcal{H}_{0})
is a
Born geometry
on

E
and the quadruple
(E,K,\braket{\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,}_{E},{\mathcal{H}}_{0})
is a
Born vector bundle
.

A Born geometry is a particular type of generalized metric which can be concretely characterized as follows
[
76
]
.

Proposition 4.10
.

A Born geometry on a para-Hermitian vector bundle
(E,K,\braket{\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,}_{E})
is a generalized metric
{\mathcal{H}}_{0}
specified solely by a fibrewise metric
g_{+}
on the eigenbundle
L_{+}.

In other words, the compatible Euclidean metric
{\mathcal{H}}_{0}
can be regarded
as a choice of a metric on the subbundle
L_{+}
in the splitting associated with
K
, where in matrix notation it reads

(4.11)

\mathcal{H}_{0}=\bigg(\begin{matrix}g_{+}&0\\
0&g_{-}\end{matrix}\bigg)\ .

4.3.
B
-Transformations

To classify the distinct splittings of an exact pre-Courant algebroid, as well as to relate generic generalized metrics to the compatible generalized metrics of a Born geometry, we introduce the notion of a
B
-transformation for a
para-Hermitian vector bundle
(E,K,\braket{\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,}_{E})
. Let us fix the splitting
E=L_{+}\oplus L_{-}
induced by the para-complex structure
K.
Then any section
e\in\mathsf{\Gamma}(E)
decomposes as
e=e_{+}+e_{-}
with
e_{\pm}\in\mathsf{\Gamma}(L_{\pm})
, and
K\in{\sf Aut}(E)
can be written as
K=\mathds{1}_{L_{+}}-\mathds{1}_{L_{-}}.

Definition 4.12
.

Let
(E,K,\braket{\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,}_{E})
be a para-Hermitian vector bundle on a manifold
{\mathcal{Q}}
. A

B_{+}
-
transformation
is an isometry
e^{B_{+}}:E\rightarrow E
of
\braket{\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,}_{E}
covering the identity which is given in matrix notation by

\displaystyle e^{B_{+}}=\bigg(\begin{matrix}\mathds{1}_{L_{+}}&0\\
B_{+}&\mathds{1}_{L_{-}}\end{matrix}\bigg):E\longrightarrow E

in the chosen splitting induced by
K
, where
B_{+}:L_{+}\rightarrow L_{-}
is a skew-symmetric map:

\braket{B_{+}(e_{1}),e_{2}}_{E}=-\braket{e_{1},B_{+}(e_{2})}_{E}\ ,

for all
e_{1},e_{2}\in\mathsf{\Gamma}(E)
.

A
B_{+}
-transformation induces another
para-complex structure from the para-Hermitian vector bundle

(E,K,\braket{\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,}_{E})
given by the pullback
K_{B_{+}}=K-2\,B_{+}
, which can be cast in the form

K_{B_{+}}=e^{-B_{+}}\circ K\circ e^{B_{+}}=\begin{pmatrix}\mathds{1}_{L_{+}}&0\\
-2\,B_{+}&-\mathds{1}_{L_{-}}\end{pmatrix}\ .

Then
K_{B_{+}}^{2}=\mathds{1}_{E},
since
B_{+}(K(e))=-K(B_{+}(e))
and

B_{+}(B_{+}(e))=0,
for all
e\in\mathsf{\Gamma}(E),
and
K_{B_{+}}
satisfies the compatibility condition
\braket{K_{B_{+}}(e_{1}),K_{B_{+}}(e_{2})}_{E}=-\braket{e_{1},e_{2}}_{E}
with
\braket{\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,}_{E}
because of the skew-symmetry property of
B_{+}.

Thus
(K_{B_{+}},\braket{\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,}_{E})
is a para-Hermitian structure on
E
. Only the
-1
-eigenbundle of the original splitting
E=L_{+}\oplus L_{-}
is preserved by a

B_{+}
-transformation, while the
+1
-eigenbundle changes.

To understand how the fundamental
2
-form
\omega
changes under a
B_{+}
-transformation, we note that the endomorphism
B_{+}
defines a
2
-form
b_{+}\in\mathsf{\Gamma}(\text{\Large$\wedge$}^{2}L_{+}^{*})
by

b_{+}(e_{1},e_{2})=\braket{B_{+}(e_{1}),e_{2}}_{E}\ ,

for all
e_{1},e_{2}\in\mathsf{\Gamma}(E).
The fundamental 2-form
\omega_{B_{+}}
of
(K_{B_{+}},\braket{\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,}_{E})
is obtained by computing
\omega_{B_{+}}(e_{1},e_{2})=\braket{K_{B_{+}}(e_{1}),e_{2}}_{E},
which gives

\displaystyle\omega_{B_{+}}=\omega-2\,b_{+}\ .

Thus a
B_{+}
-transformation does not generally preserve the closure or non-closure of the fundamental
2
-form.

Remark 4.13
.

A completely analogous discussion applies to a
B_{-}
-transformation
, defined by a skew-symmetric map

B_{-}:L_{-}\rightarrow L_{+}
similarly to Definition
4.12
, by interchanging the roles of the eigenbundles
L_{+}
and
L_{-}
.

Remark 4.14
.

If
(E,\braket{\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,}_{E},L)
is an even rank vector bundle
endowed with a split signature metric and a choice of maximally
isotropic subbundle, see Remark
4.2
, then the
maximally isotropic splittings of the short exact sequence
(
4.3
) are mapped into each other via
B_{+}
-transformations which preserve
L
.

Example 4.15
.

Recall from Example
4.5
that every splitting of an exact pre-Courant algebroid
(E,\llbracket\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,\rrbracket_{\tt D},\braket{\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,}_{E},\rho)
is associated with a para-Hermitian structure on
E
. By Remark
4.14
, any two splittings of an exact pre-Courant algebroid are related by a
B_{+}
-transformation. Each distinct isotropic
splitting of (
3.10
) is associated with a different
3
-form

H\in\Omega^{3}({\mathcal{Q}})
. A
B_{+}
-transformation of an exact pre-Courant algebroid is generated by a
2
-form
b\in\Omega^{2}({\mathcal{Q}})
, which preserves the D-bracket
\llbracket\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,\rrbracket_{\tt D}
if
b
is a closed
2
-form. When
b
is not closed the corresponding D-bracket maps to the Dorfman bracket twisted by
H+\mathrm{d}b
.

Let us finally discuss the
B_{+}
-transformation of a
compatible generalized metric of a Born geometry. A compatible
generalized metric
{\mathcal{H}}_{0}
of a para-Hermitian structure
(K,\braket{\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,}_{E})
on
E
transforms under a
B_{+}
-transformation to the compatible
generalized metric
{\mathcal{H}}_{B_{+}}
of the pullback
para-Hermitian structure
(K_{B_{+}},\braket{\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,}_{E})
on
E
given by

\displaystyle{\mathcal{H}}_{B_{+}}^{\flat}=\big(e^{-B_{+}}\big)^{\rm t}\circ{\mathcal{H}}_{0}^{\flat}\circ e^{-B_{+}}\ .

Recalling that

{\mathcal{H}}_{0}
takes the diagonal form (
4.11
), we then have
[
76
]

Proposition 4.16
.

A generalized metric
I\in{\sf Aut}(E)
on a para-Hermitian
vector bundle
(E,K,\braket{\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,}_{E})
corresponds to a choice of a Born geometry

(K,\braket{\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,}_{E},{\mathcal{H}}_{0})
and a
B_{+}
-transformation.

4.4.
Almost Para-Hermitian Manifolds

The special case where
E=TM
is the tangent bundle of a manifold
M
in Definition
4.1
is particularly important because it
allows one to formulate conditions for the integrability of the
eigenbundles
L_{\pm}
, and hence on the possibility that
M
is a foliated
manifold.

Definition 4.17
.

An
almost para-Hermitian manifold

(M,K,\eta)
is a manifold
M
of even dimension
2d
with a para-Hermitian structure
(K,\eta)
on its tangent bundle
TM
.

The para-complex structure
K\in{\sf Aut}(TM)
is equivalent to the splitting of the tangent bundle

\displaystyle TM=L_{+}\oplus L_{-}

of the manifold
M
into the Whitney sum of two distributions
L_{\pm}
of the same constant rank
d
, identified as the
\pm\,1
-eigenbundles of
K
. If the eigenbundles
L_{\pm}
are both integrable, that is,
[\mathsf{\Gamma}(L_{\pm}),\mathsf{\Gamma}(L_{\pm})]_{TM}\subseteq\mathsf{\Gamma}(L_{\pm})
, then
(M,K,\eta)
is a
para-Hermitian manifold
; in this instance, by Frobenius’ Theorem,
M
admits two regular foliations
{\mathcal{F}}_{\pm}
, such that
L_{\pm}=T{\mathcal{F}}_{\pm}
. However, the integrability conditions for
L_{+}
and
L_{-}
are independent of each other
[
74
,
3
]
: one of them may be integrable while the other may not. This is the situation that most commonly occurs in examples, and in this case
M
admits only one foliation.

The fundamental
2
-form
\omega
of an almost para-Hermitian manifold
(M,K,\eta)
defines an almost symplectic structure on
M
; the
3
-form
\mathrm{d}\omega
describes the ‘generalized fluxes’ of double field theory on
M

[
76
]
. If
\omega
is symplectic, that is,
\mathrm{d}\omega=0
, then
(M,K,\eta)
is an
almost para-Kähler manifold
. In this case, since the subbundles
L_{\pm}
are maximally isotropic with respect to
\omega
, they are Lagrangian subbundles of the tangent bundle
TM
; if one of them is integrable, then
M
admits a Lagrangian foliation with respect to the symplectic structure
\omega
.

Example 4.18
.

Let
M
be the total space of the cotangent bundle
\pi:T^{*}{\mathcal{Q}}\to{\mathcal{Q}}
of a
d
-dimensional manifold
{\mathcal{Q}}
, endowed with the canonical symplectic
2
-form
\omega_{0}
. Then almost para-Kähler structures on
M
correspond to isotropic splittings of the short exact sequence of vector bundles

\displaystyle 0\longrightarrow\mathsf{Ker}(\pi_{*})\xrightarrow{\ i\ }TM\xrightarrow{\pi_{*}}\pi^{*}(T{\mathcal{Q}})\longrightarrow 0

with respect to
\omega_{0}
, where
i
is the inclusion of the vertical vector subbundle
\mathsf{Ker}(\pi_{*})
into
TM
. Such a splitting
s:\pi^{*}(T{\mathcal{Q}})\to TM
defines an Ehresmann connection on
M
with

\displaystyle TM=\mathsf{Im}(s)\oplus\mathsf{Ker}(\pi_{*})

the Whitney sum decomposition of the tangent bundle
TM
into the
\pm\,1
-eigenbundles of an almost para-complex structure
K_{s}\in{\sf Aut}(TM)
which is compatible with
\omega_{0}
. The leaves of the canonical foliation
{\mathcal{F}}
of the cotangent bundle
M=T^{*}{\mathcal{Q}}
are the fibres
{\mathcal{F}}_{q}=\pi^{-1}(q)
over
q\in{\mathcal{Q}}
, which are diffeomorphic to
\mathbb{R}^{d}
. There is a vector bundle isomorphism
\mathsf{Ker}(\pi_{*})\simeq T{\mathcal{F}}
, and the quotient by the action of the foliation is
M/{\mathcal{F}}\simeq{\mathcal{Q}}
.

If the base
{\mathcal{Q}}
is a Riemannian manifold with metric
g
, then the horizontal lift of
g
, that is, the pullback
g_{+}=\pi^{*}g
, gives a fibrewise Euclidean metric on
\mathsf{Im}(s)
. This defines a Born geometry with compatible generalized metric
{\mathcal{H}}_{0}
on
(M,K_{s},\omega_{0})
given by (
4.11
). Since any manifold
{\mathcal{Q}}
admits a Riemannian metric, one can always define a Born geometry on
M
of this type. Similarly, given any
2
-form
b\in\Omega^{2}({\mathcal{Q}})
, its horizontal lift
b_{+}=\pi^{*}b
defines a
B_{+}
-transformation of the almost para-Kähler manifold
(M,K_{s},\omega_{0})
.

4.5.
The Canonical Metric Algebroid

On any almost para-Hermitian manifold
(M,K,\eta)
, one can define the canonical D-bracket which makes the tangent bundle
TM
into a metric algebroid over
M
on which both eigenbundles
L_{\pm}
of
K
are D-structures
[
74
,
21
,
75
,
3
]
. For this, we first need the following preliminary notion.

Definition 4.19
.

Let
(M,K,\eta)
be an almost para-Hermitian manifold. Let
\nabla^{\tt LC}
be the Levi-Civita connection of
\eta
, and let
P_{\pm}:TM\to TM
be the compositions of the inclusion maps
L_{\pm}\hookrightarrow TM
with the projections
TM\to L_{\pm}
to the
\pm\,1
-eigenbundles of
K
. The
canonical connection
on
(M,K,\eta)
is

\displaystyle\nabla^{\tt can}=P_{+}\circ\nabla^{\tt LC}\circ P_{+}+P_{-}\circ\nabla^{\tt LC}\circ P_{-}\ .

The canonical connection is a
para-Hermitian connection
:
\nabla^{\tt can}K=\nabla^{\tt can}\eta=0
; in particular, it preserves the eigenbundles
L_{\pm}
of
K
. It coincides with the Levi-Civita connection,
\nabla^{\tt can}=\nabla^{\tt LC}
, if and only if
(M,K,\eta)
is an almost para-Kähler manifold
[
21
]
. By a construction similar to Example
3.11
, we then arrive at one of our central concepts.

Definition 4.20
.

Let
(M,K,\eta)
be an almost para-Hermitian manifold, and let
\nabla^{\tt can}
be its canonical connection. The
canonical D-bracket

\llbracket\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,\rrbracket_{\tt D}^{K}
is defined by

\displaystyle\eta\big(\llbracket X,Y\rrbracket_{\tt D}^{K},Z\big)=\eta(\nabla_{X}^{\tt can}Y-\nabla_{Y}^{\tt can}X,Z)+\eta(\nabla_{Z}^{\tt can}X,Y)\ ,

for vector fields
X,Y,Z\in\mathsf{\Gamma}(TM)
. The metric algebroid
(TM,\llbracket\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,\rrbracket_{\tt D}^{K},\eta,\mathds{1}_{TM})
is the
canonical metric algebroid
over
(M,K,\eta)
.

The canonical D-bracket is compatible with the almost para-complex structure
K
, that is, both of its eigenbundles
L_{\pm}
are D-structures on the metric algebroid
(TM,\llbracket\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,\rrbracket_{\tt D}^{K},\eta,\mathds{1}_{TM})
:

\displaystyle\llbracket\mathsf{\Gamma}(L_{\pm}),\mathsf{\Gamma}(L_{\pm})\rrbracket_{\tt D}^{K}\ \subseteq\ \mathsf{\Gamma}(L_{\pm})\ .

It is ‘canonical’ because it is the projection of the Lie bracket of vector fields
[
74
]
:

\displaystyle\llbracket P_{\pm}(X),P_{\pm}(Y)\rrbracket_{\tt D}^{K}=P_{\pm}\big([P_{\pm}(X),P_{\pm}(Y)]_{TM}\big)\ ,

for all
X,Y\in\mathsf{\Gamma}(TM)
. Given
(M,K,\eta)
, the bracket
\llbracket\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,\rrbracket_{\tt D}^{K}
is the unique D-bracket on
(TM,\eta,\mathds{1}_{TM})
which is compatible with
K
and related to the Lie bracket
[\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,]_{TM}
in this way
[
3
]
. In the canonical metric algebroid, the generalized exterior derivative is given by
{\mathcal{D}}^{\tt can}=\eta^{-1}{}^{\sharp}\circ\mathrm{d}
.

Remark 4.21
.

Any D-bracket on
(TM,K,\eta)
can be obtained by choosing a para-complex connection corresponding to a splitting of the short exact Atiyah sequence from Proposition
3.47
:

(4.22)

0\longrightarrow{\mathfrak{so}}(TM)\longrightarrow\mathsf{At}(TM,\eta)\longrightarrow TM\longrightarrow 0\ ,

where
\mathsf{At}(TM,\eta)\subset J^{1}(TM)
is a vector subbundle of the first order jet bundle of the tangent bundle
TM
. In other words, the canonical connection on
(M,K,\eta)
is just a change of splitting of the short exact sequence (
4.22
) when the splitting associated with the Levi-Civita connection
\nabla^{\tt LC}
is chosen, leading to the canonical D-bracket. Any other change of splitting which yields a para-complex connection induces a D-bracket on
TM.
By using the metric
\eta
to identify
\mathfrak{so}(TM)\simeq\text{\Large$\wedge$}^{2}TM
, the different splittings of (
4.22
) correspond to bundle morphisms in
{\sf Hom}(\text{\Large$\wedge$}^{2}TM,TM)
, and hence the different D-brackets can be shown to correspond to
3
-forms
T\in\Omega^{3}(M)
, similarly to
[
16
, Proposition 2.3]
.

Remark 4.23
.

Similarly to the D-bracket of a split exact pre-Courant algebroid (cf. Examples
3.9
and
4.15
), the canonical D-bracket can be twisted by any closed
3
-form
H\in\Omega^{3}(M)
. In particular, a
B_{+}
-transformation maps the canonical D-bracket
\llbracket\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,\rrbracket_{\tt D}^{K}
of
(M,K,\eta)
to the canonical D-bracket of
(M,K_{B_{+}},\eta)
, which is equal to
\llbracket\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,\rrbracket_{\tt D}^{K}
twisted by the
3
-form
\mathrm{d}b

[
21
,
3
]
:

\displaystyle\eta\big(\llbracket X,Y\rrbracket_{\tt D}^{K_{B_{+}}},Z\big)=\eta\big(\llbracket X,Y\rrbracket_{\tt D}^{K},Z\big)-\mathrm{d}b(X,Y,Z)\ ,

for all
X,Y,Z\in\mathsf{\Gamma}(TM)
.

Under the correspondence of Theorem
3.53
, the canonical metric algebroid on
(M,K,\eta)
can be identified with a symplectic
2
-algebroid
(T[1]M\oplus T^{*}[2]M,\{\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,\},\gamma)
, where the Poisson bracket
\{\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,\}
coincides with the metric
\eta
on degree
1
functions
\mathsf{\Gamma}(TM)
. In this language a
B_{+}
-transformation is a particular example of a canonical transformation of the symplectic
2
-algebroid, which twists the Hamiltonian
\gamma
(see e.g.
[
31
,
79
]
).

Remark 4.24
.

The canonical D-bracket also gives a notion of relative weak integrability of almost para-Hermitian structures
[
21
]
. If
(K,\eta)
and
(K^{\prime},\eta)
are almost para-Hermitian structures on the same even-dimensional manifold
M
, with respective eigenbundles
L_{\pm}
and
L_{\pm}^{\prime}
, then
K^{\prime}
is said to be
D-integrable
with respect to
K
if
\llbracket\mathsf{\Gamma}(L_{\pm}^{\prime}),\mathsf{\Gamma}(L_{\pm}^{\prime})]_{\tt D}^{K}\subseteq\mathsf{\Gamma}(L_{\pm}^{\prime})
. The lack of D-integrability is then measured by the
fluxes

\displaystyle T(X,Y,Z):=\eta\big(\llbracket X,Y\rrbracket_{\tt D}^{K}-\llbracket X,Y\rrbracket_{\tt D}^{K^{\prime}},Z\big)\ ,

for
X,Y,Z\in\mathsf{\Gamma}(TM)
. The
3
-forms
T\in\Omega^{3}(M)
reproduce the standard generalized fluxes of double field theory
[
22
]
.

5.
The Metric Algebroids of Doubled Geometry

In this section we will make precise some notions of doubled geometry, and in particular its algebroid structures, which are relevant to a global description of double field theory.

5.1.
Metric Algebroids from the Large Courant Algebroid

Let
(M,K,\eta)
be an almost para-Hermitian manifold of dimension
2d
. We have seen that there are two natural metric algebroids that can be defined over
M
in this case. Firstly, there is the canonical metric algebroid
(TM,\llbracket\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,\rrbracket_{\tt D}^{K},\eta,\mathds{1}_{TM})
defined on the tangent bundle
TM
of rank
2d
in Definition
4.20
. Secondly, there is the standard Courant algebroid
(\mathbb{T}M,[\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,]_{{\tt D}},\langle\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,\rangle_{\mathbb{T}M},\rho)
defined on the generalized tangent bundle
\mathbb{T}M=TM\oplus T^{*}M
of rank
4d
in Example
2.27
; we call this latter metric algebroid the
large Courant algebroid
on
M
to distinguish it from other smaller rank Courant algebroids that will appear on
M
momentarily. Both the canonical D-bracket on
TM
and the Dorfman bracket on
\mathbb{T}M
can be twisted by a closed
3
-form
H\in\Omega^{3}(M)
, and both classes of metric algebroids are classified by
B_{+}
-transformations which shift the twisting
3
-form to
H+\mathrm{d}b
for
2
-forms
b\in\Omega^{2}(M)
(cf. Remark
4.23
and Example
4.15
). We shall now discuss the precise relation between these two metric algebroids, following
[
17
,
29
]
.

For this, let us start from a more general setting. Let
(M,\eta)
be any pseudo-Riemannian manifold. On the para-Hermitian vector bundle
\mathbb{T}M\to M
(cf. Example
4.4
) we can define a generalized split signature metric by the involution

\displaystyle{\mathcal{I}}_{0}=\bigg(\begin{matrix}0&\eta^{-1}{}^{\sharp}\\
\eta^{\flat}&0\end{matrix}\bigg)

with respect to the splitting
\mathbb{T}M=TM\oplus T^{*}M
,
and its
B_{+}
-transformations
{\mathcal{I}}_{B_{+}}
given by
{\mathcal{I}}_{B_{+}}=e^{B_{+}}\circ{\mathcal{I}}_{0}\circ e^{-B_{+}}
. This defines another para-complex structure on
\mathbb{T}M
which splits the generalized tangent bundle into its
\pm\,1
-eigenbundles
C_{\pm}
of equal rank:

\displaystyle\mathbb{T}M=C_{+}\oplus C_{-}\qquad\mbox{with}\quad C_{\pm}=\mathsf{Graph}(b\pm\eta)\ ,

where
\mathsf{Graph}(b\pm\eta)=\{X+b^{\flat}(X)\pm\eta^{\flat}(X)\,|\,X\in\mathsf{\Gamma}(TM)\}\subset\mathbb{T}M
. We denote by

\displaystyle{\mathtt{p}}_{\pm}:\mathbb{T}M\longrightarrow C_{\pm}

the projections to the subbundles
C_{\pm}
of
\mathbb{T}M
, and by
{\mathtt{i}}_{\pm}:C_{\pm}\hookrightarrow\mathbb{T}M
the inclusion maps.

By this process of doubling, splitting and projecting via the generalized tangent bundle
\mathbb{T}M
, we get a pair of metric algebroids on any pseudo-Riemannian manifold.

Proposition 5.1
.

Let
(\mathbb{T}M,[\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,]_{{\tt D}},\langle\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,\rangle_{\mathbb{T}M},\rho)
be the
H
-twisted standard Courant algebroid over a pseudo-Riemannian manifold
(M,\eta)
, and let
C_{\pm}\subset\mathbb{T}M
be the eigenbundles associated to the generalized metric
{\mathcal{I}}_{B_{+}}
. Define structure maps on sections of the vector bundles
C_{\pm}\to M
by

\displaystyle\llbracket\xi_{\pm},\xi_{\pm}^{\prime}\rrbracket_{\tt D}^{\pm}

\displaystyle:={\mathtt{p}}_{\pm}\big([{\mathtt{i}}_{\pm}(\xi_{\pm}),{\mathtt{i}}_{\pm}(\xi_{\pm}^{\prime})]_{{\tt D}}\big)\ ,

\displaystyle\langle\xi_{\pm},\xi_{\pm}^{\prime}\rangle_{C_{\pm}}

\displaystyle:=\pm\,\tfrac{1}{2}\,\langle{\mathtt{i}}_{\pm}(\xi_{\pm}),{\mathtt{i}}_{\pm}(\xi_{\pm}^{\prime})\rangle_{\mathbb{T}M}\ ,

\displaystyle\rho_{\pm}

\displaystyle:=\rho\circ{\mathtt{i}}_{\pm}\ ,

for
\xi_{\pm},\xi_{\pm}^{\prime}\in\mathsf{\Gamma}(C_{\pm})
. Then
(C_{\pm},\llbracket\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,\rrbracket_{\tt D}^{\pm},\langle\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,\rangle_{C_{\pm}},\rho_{\pm})
are metric algebroids over
M
.

The construction of Example
3.11
then yields

Proposition 5.2
.

Let
(M,\eta)
be a pseudo-Riemannian manifold. Then the metric algebroids
(C_{\pm},\llbracket\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,\rrbracket_{\tt D}^{\pm},\langle\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,\rangle_{C_{\pm}},\rho_{\pm})
of Proposition
5.1
are isomorphic to the metric algebroid
(TM,\llbracket\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,\rrbracket_{\tt D}^{\eta},\eta,\mathds{1}_{TM})
with D-bracket twisted by the
3
-forms
\pm\,(H+\mathrm{d}b)
.

Proof.

The anchor maps
\rho_{\pm}:C_{\pm}\to TM
defined in Proposition
5.1
are bundle isomorphisms: the inverse maps
\rho_{\pm}^{-1}:TM\to C_{\pm}
send
X\in\mathsf{\Gamma}(TM)
to
X+(b^{\flat}\pm\eta^{\flat})X
. One then checks explicitly

\displaystyle\eta(X,Y)=\big\langle\rho_{\pm}^{-1}(X),\rho_{\pm}^{-1}(Y)\big\rangle_{C_{\pm}}

along with

\displaystyle\big\langle\llbracket\rho_{\pm}^{-1}(X),\rho_{\pm}^{-1}(Y)\rrbracket_{\tt D}^{\pm},\rho_{\pm}^{-1}(Z)\big\rangle_{C_{\pm}}=\eta\big(\llbracket X,Y\rrbracket_{\tt D}^{\eta},Z\big)\pm\tfrac{1}{2}\,(H+\mathrm{d}b)(X,Y,Z)\ ,

for all
X,Y,Z\in\mathsf{\Gamma}(TM)
,
and that the anchor maps of the two metric algebroids are compatible, which is satisfied trivially as
\rho_{\pm}\circ\rho_{\pm}^{-1}=\mathds{1}_{TM}
.
∎

Let us finally specialize Proposition
5.2
, with
b=0
and
H=0
, to the case that the metric
\eta
is part of an almost para-Kähler structure
(K,\eta)
. We then immediately arrive at

Proposition 5.3
.

Let
(\mathbb{T}M,[\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,]_{{\tt D}},\langle\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,\rangle_{\mathbb{T}M},\rho)
be the large Courant algebroid over an almost para-Kähler manifold
(M,K,\eta)
. Then the anchor map
\rho_{+}:C_{+}\to TM
defines a metric algebroid isomorphism from the metric algebroid
(C_{+},\llbracket\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,\rrbracket_{\tt D}^{+},\langle\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,\rangle_{C_{+}},\rho_{+})
with
b=0
to the canonical metric algebroid
\big(TM,\llbracket\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,\rrbracket_{\tt D}^{K^{\prime}},\eta,\mathds{1}_{TM}\big)
corresponding to the para-complex structure
K^{\prime}=\rho_{+}\circ(K-K^{\rm t})\circ\rho_{+}^{-1}
.

Remark 5.4
.

The construction of Proposition
5.3
was originally given for flat para-Kähler manifolds in
[
17
]
(see also
[
80
]
), using the local model of the cotangent bundle
M=T^{*}{\mathcal{Q}}
with the canonical symplectic structure
\omega_{0}
(cf. Example
4.18
), and in this way recovering the well known local expression for the canonical D-bracket
\llbracket\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,\rrbracket_{\tt D}^{+}
with
b=0
and
H=0
. It was extended to any generalized para-Kähler manifold in
[
29
]
(see also
[
3
]
), using Proposition
5.2
with
b\neq 0
and
H\neq 0
, where the D-brackets
\llbracket\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,\rrbracket_{\tt D}^{\pm}
coincide with the canonical D-brackets of the two para-Hermitian structures
K_{\pm}
associated to the generalized para-Kähler structure; the relative flux for this pair (cf. Remark
4.24
) is precisely the twisting
3
-form of the underlying large Courant algebroid:
T=H+\mathrm{d}b
.

5.2.
Doubled Manifolds and DFT Algebroids

The constructions of Section
5.1
motivate a generalization to a special class of metric algebroids where the deviation from a Courant algebroid is done in a controlled way. As we discuss below, this is the essence of the ‘section constraint’ in double field theory (DFT for short). For this, we consider a special class of manifolds on which the geometry of double field theory, or
doubled geometry
, is based. We denote by
{\sf O}(d,d)
the split orthogonal group, whose maximal compact subgroup is
{\sf O}(d)\times{\sf O}(d)
.

Definition 5.5
.

A
doubled manifold
is a manifold
M
of even dimension
2d
endowed with an
{\sf O}(d,d)
-structure, or equivalently a pseudo-Riemannian metric
\eta
of split signature
(d,d)
.

Example 5.6
.

Any almost para-Hermitian manifold
(M,K,\eta)
is a doubled manifold, with a reduction of the
{\sf O}(d,d)
-structure to an
{\sf O}(d){\times}{\sf O}(d)
-structure. The introduction of a compatible generalized metric
{\mathcal{H}}_{0}
further reduces this to an
{\sf O}(d)
-structure.

Definition 5.7
.

A
DFT algebroid
over a doubled manifold
(M,\eta)
is a metric algebroid
(C,\llbracket\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,\rrbracket_{\tt D},\langle\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,\rangle_{C},\rho)
whose anchor map defines an isometric isomorphism
\rho:(C,\langle\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,\rangle_{C})\to(TM,\eta)
of pseudo-Euclidean vector bundles over
M
such that
\rho\circ\rho^{*}=\eta^{-1}{}^{\sharp}
.

Sections of the vector bundle
C\to M
are called
doubled vectors
or
DFT vectors
. All constructions of Section
5.1
(in the split signature case) clearly fit into this general definition. On the other hand, Courant algebroids and pre-Courant algebroids over doubled manifolds are
not
DFT algebroids, because their anchor maps have non-trivial kernels (cf. Remark
2.25
). The following are noteworthy particular cases of DFT algebroids that will appear later on.

Example 5.8
.

The metric algebroid
(C_{+},\llbracket\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,\rrbracket_{\tt D}^{+},\langle\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,\rangle_{C_{+}},\rho_{+})
over any almost para-Kähler manifold
(M,K,\eta)
, constructed in Proposition
5.3
, is a DFT algebroid.

Example 5.9
.

The canonical metric algebroid
\big(TM,\llbracket\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,\rrbracket_{\tt D}^{K},\eta,\mathds{1}_{TM}\big)
over any almost para-Hermitian manifold
(M,K,\eta)
, constructed in Section
4.5
, is a DFT algebroid.

From Definition
5.7
we can describe how the key Courant algebroid properties (
2.4
), (
2.5
) and (
2.26
) are explicitly violated in a DFT algebroid in terms of the underlying geometry of the doubled manifold. Recalling Remark
3.58
, we can write the map (
3.59
) in terms of (
3.60
) as

\displaystyle{\sf Leib}_{\tt D}(c_{1},c_{2},c_{3})

\displaystyle\hskip 28.45274pt=\rho^{-1}\big([\rho(c_{1}),{\sf hom}_{\rho}(c_{2},c_{3})]_{TM}-[{\sf hom}_{\rho}(c_{1},c_{2}),\rho(c_{3})]_{TM}-[\rho(c_{2}),{\sf hom}_{\rho}(c_{1},c_{3})]_{TM}

\displaystyle\hskip 85.35826pt+{\sf hom}_{\rho}(c_{1},\llbracket c_{2},c_{3}\rrbracket_{\tt D})-{\sf hom}_{\rho}(\llbracket c_{1},c_{2}\rrbracket_{\tt D},c_{3})-{\sf hom}_{\rho}(c_{2},\llbracket c_{1},c_{3}\rrbracket_{\tt D})\big)\ ,

for all
c_{1},c_{2},c_{3}\in\mathsf{\Gamma}(C)
, which follows from applying the anchor map to (
3.59
) and using the Jacobi identity for the Lie bracket
[\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,]_{TM}
on
\mathsf{\Gamma}(TM)
. This shows that the failure of the anchor map from being a bracket homomorphism completely controls the violation of the Leibniz identity (
2.4
) in a DFT algebroid.

Lemma 5.10
.

Let
(C,\llbracket\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,\rrbracket_{\tt D},\langle\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,\rangle_{C},\rho)
be a DFT algebroid over a doubled manifold
(M,\eta)
. Then

\displaystyle\langle{\mathcal{D}}f,{\mathcal{D}}g\rangle_{C}

\displaystyle=\eta^{-1}(\mathrm{d}f,\mathrm{d}g)\ ,

\displaystyle{\sf hom}_{\rho}(c_{1},c_{2})\cdot f+{\sf hom}_{\rho}(c_{2},c_{1})\cdot f

\displaystyle=\eta^{-1}\big(\mathrm{d}f,\mathrm{d}\langle c_{1},c_{2}\rangle_{C}\big)\ ,

\displaystyle\big\langle{\sf Leib}_{\tt D}(c_{1},c_{2},c_{3})+{\sf Leib}_{\tt D}(c_{2},c_{1},c_{3}),c_{4}\big\rangle_{C}

\displaystyle=-\eta\big({\sf hom}_{\rho}\big(\rho^{-1}\circ\eta^{-1}{}^{\sharp}(\mathrm{d}\langle c_{1},c_{2}\rangle_{C}),c_{3}\big)\,,\,\rho(c_{4})\big)

\displaystyle\quad\,-\eta\big(\big[\eta^{-1}{}^{\sharp}(\mathrm{d}\langle c_{1},c_{2}\rangle_{C}),\rho(c_{3})\big]_{TM}\,,\,\rho(c_{4})\big)\ ,

for all
f,g\in C^{\infty}(M)
and
c_{1},c_{2},c_{3},c_{4}\in\mathsf{\Gamma}(C)
.

Proof.

The first equality follows immediately from Definition
5.7
, which implies that the generalized exterior derivative in a DFT algebroid is given by

(5.11)

\displaystyle{\mathcal{D}}=\rho^{-1}\circ\eta^{-1}{}^{\sharp}\circ\mathrm{d}\ ,

and using the isometry property

(5.12)

\displaystyle\eta\big(\rho(c_{1}),\rho(c_{2})\big)=\langle c_{1},c_{2}\rangle_{C}\ .

For the second equality, we note that, as in any metric algebroid, the properties (
3.2
) and (
3.6
) imply

\displaystyle{\sf hom}_{\rho}(c_{1},c_{2})\cdot f=\langle\llbracket{\mathcal{D}}f,c_{1}\rrbracket_{\tt D},c_{2}\rangle_{C}\ .

The symmetric part of
{\sf hom}_{\rho}(c_{1},c_{2})
may then be written as

\displaystyle\big({\sf hom}_{\rho}(c_{1},c_{2})+{\sf hom}_{\rho}(c_{2},c_{1})\big)\cdot f=(\rho\circ{\mathcal{D}}f)\cdot\langle c_{1},c_{2}\rangle_{C}=\eta^{-1}{}^{\sharp}(\mathrm{d}f)\cdot\langle c_{1},c_{2}\rangle_{C}\ ,

where in the first step we used (
3.2
) again and the second step follows from (
5.11
).

For the third equality, we use (
3.6
) and (
5.11
) to write

\displaystyle{\sf Leib}_{\tt D}(c_{1},c_{2},c_{3})+{\sf Leib}_{\tt D}(c_{2},c_{1},c_{3})=-\llbracket{\mathcal{D}}\langle c_{1},c_{2}\rangle_{C},c_{3}\rrbracket_{\tt D}=-\llbracket\rho^{-1}\circ\eta^{-1}{}^{\sharp}(\mathrm{d}\langle c_{1},c_{2}\rangle_{C}),c_{3}\rrbracket_{\tt D}\ ,

and the result then follows from the definition (
3.60
) and the isometry property (
5.12
).
∎

Remark 5.13
.

The notion of DFT algebroid was introduced in
[
17
]
, for the special case of a flat para-Kähler manifold in a local formulation based on a cotangent bundle
M=T^{*}{\mathcal{Q}}
, and with the skew-symmetrization of the D-bracket (see also
[
80
]
). In that case, one can write local expressions for the Jacobiator and the map
{\sf hom}_{\rho}
entirely in terms of the tangent bundle metric
\eta
and the Schouten-Nijenhuis bracket of multivector fields on
M
(see also
[
81
]
). In the double field theory literature, restricting to functions on
M
and sections of
C
(equivalently vector fields on
M
) for which the right-hand sides of the identities in Lemma
5.10
vanish is called imposing the ‘(strong) section constraint’; in other words, the section constraint is requirement
\langle{\mathcal{D}}f,{\mathcal{D}}g\rangle_{C}=\eta^{-1}(\mathrm{d}f,\mathrm{d}g)=0
for all functions
f,g\in C^{\infty}(M)
. Then via a suitable reduction or quotient, a DFT algebroid becomes a Courant algebroid on
M
.
Notice, however, that the well-known reduction procedure for Courant algebroids given in
[
82
]
cannot be directly extended to metric algebroids, because it relies crucially on the closure of the Leibniz identity for the algebroid bracket. Hence the precise geometric interpretation of the section constraint remains an important open problem.
We shall discuss this point further in Section
6
within the setting of a DFT algebroid over a foliated almost para-Hermitian manifold
(M,K,\eta)
, where we shall see that it is possible to make such a reduction in a suitable sense. This will clarify the sense in which a DFT algebroid lies “in between” two Courant algebroids, and how doubled geometry is reconciled with generalized geometry.

However, even in this case one needs to exercise caution with these vanishing statements, because in a metric algebroid the maps
{\sf Leib}_{\tt D}
and
{\sf hom}_{\rho}
do not define tensors (cf. Remark
3.58
). Whereas in a Courant algebroid the condition
{\sf Leib}_{\tt D}(e_{1},e_{2},e_{3})=0
would yield the Bianchi identities for fluxes in supergravity, as discussed in Section
2.5
, in a general metric algebroid this condition depends on the choice of a local frame. Instead, it is proposed by
[
72
]
to replace this condition in a DFT algebroid with the tensorial ‘pre-Bianchi identity’

\displaystyle\big\langle{\sf Leib}_{\tt D}(c_{1},c_{2},c_{3}),c_{4}\big\rangle_{C}

\displaystyle=\eta\big({\sf hom}_{\rho}(c_{1},c_{3}),{\sf hom}_{\rho}(c_{2},c_{4})\big)-\eta\big({\sf hom}_{\rho}(c_{1},c_{2}),{\sf hom}_{\rho}(c_{3},c_{4})\big)

\displaystyle\quad\,-\eta\big({\sf hom}_{\rho}(c_{1},c_{4}),{\sf hom}_{\rho}(c_{2},c_{3})\big)\ ,

for all
c_{1},c_{2},c_{3},c_{4}\in\mathsf{\Gamma}(C)
. This defines a special class of DFT algebroids, including the standard ones of local double field theory that we will discuss in Section
6.1
.

5.3.
C-Brackets and Curved
L_{\infty}
-Algebras

Let
(C,\llbracket\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,\rrbracket_{\tt D},\langle\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,\rangle_{C},\rho)
be a DFT algebroid over a doubled manifold
(M,\eta)
. The analogue of the generalized Lie derivative from Section
2.6
,

\displaystyle{\boldsymbol{\pounds}}_{c}^{\tt D}:=\llbracket c,\,\cdot\,\rrbracket_{\tt D}\ ,

for
c\in\mathsf{\Gamma}(C)
, is now only an infinitesimal symmetry of the split signature pseudo-Euclidean vector bundle
(C,\langle\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,\rangle_{C})
over
M
, which is the natural notion of symmetry for a DFT algebroid. By Proposition
3.47
, these symmetries are encoded by the Atiyah algebroid

(5.14)

\displaystyle 0\longrightarrow\mathfrak{so}(C)\xlongrightarrow{}\mathsf{At}(C,\braket{\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,}_{C})\longrightarrow TM\longrightarrow 0\ ,

which contains both infinitesimal diffeomorphisms of
M
and orientation-preserving changes of orthonormal frame for
C
.

In this case, closure of the gauge algebra is obstructed by the violation of the Leibniz identity. For this, we introduce the analogue of the Courant bracket from Section
2.6
,

\displaystyle\llbracket c_{1},c_{2}\rrbracket_{\tt C}:=\tfrac{1}{2}\,\big(\llbracket c_{1},c_{2}\rrbracket_{\tt D}-\llbracket c_{2},c_{1}\rrbracket_{\tt D}\big)\ ,

which is called a
C-bracket
of sections
c_{1},c_{2}\in\mathsf{\Gamma}(C)
. By (
3.6
), it is related to the D-bracket in the same way that the Courant bracket is related to the Dorfman bracket of a Courant algebroid:

\displaystyle\llbracket c_{1},c_{2}\rrbracket_{\tt D}=\llbracket c_{1},c_{2}\rrbracket_{\tt C}+\tfrac{1}{2}\,{\mathcal{D}}\langle c_{1},c_{2}\rangle_{C}\ .

One can now equivalently characterize the compatibility conditions on the D-bracket of a DFT algebroid (and more generally any metric algebroid) in terms of the C-bracket by a statement completely analogous to Proposition
2.36
without the Jacobiator identity; the latter is related to the map
{\sf Leib}_{\tt D}
introduced in Remark
3.58
, and so the violation of the Jacobi identity for the C-bracket is controlled not only by the generalized exterior derivative of the Nijenhuis operator
{\sf Nij}_{\tt C}:\mathsf{\Gamma}(C)\times\mathsf{\Gamma}(C)\times\mathsf{\Gamma}(C)\to C^{\infty}(M)
, but also by the section constraint
\langle{\mathcal{D}}f,{\mathcal{D}}g\rangle_{C}=\eta^{-1}(\mathrm{d}f,\mathrm{d}g)=0
. It is in this alternative formulation using the C-bracket that the notion of DFT algebroid was originally introduced in
[
17
]
.

One can then write the commutator bracket of generalized Lie derivatives as

\displaystyle\big[{\boldsymbol{\pounds}}_{c_{1}}^{\tt D},{\boldsymbol{\pounds}}_{c_{2}}^{\tt D}\big]_{\circ}(c)={\boldsymbol{\pounds}}_{\llbracket c_{1},c_{2}\rrbracket_{\tt C}}^{\tt D}(c)+{\sf Jac}_{\tt C}(c_{1},c_{2},c)-{\mathcal{D}}\,{\sf Nij}_{\tt C}(c_{1},c_{2},c)\ ,

for all
c_{1},c_{2},c\in\mathsf{\Gamma}(C)
. In other words, the gauge algebra of generalized Lie derivatives only closes on the C-bracket upon imposition of the section constraint
\eta^{-1}(\mathrm{d}f,\mathrm{d}g)=0
(see Remark
5.13
). As shown by
[
81
]
, the natural extension of Theorem
2.39
to DFT algebroids involves a curving
\ell_{0}\neq 0
of the underlying
L_{\infty}
-algebra (a map of degree
2
from the ground ring
\mathbb{R}
), in order to accomodate the non-vanishing map
\rho\circ{\mathcal{D}}=\eta^{-1}{}^{\sharp}\circ\mathrm{d}\neq 0
. This then completely characterizes the DFT algebroid, similarly to the case of Courant algebroids.

Theorem 5.15
.

Let
(C,\llbracket\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,\rrbracket_{\tt D},\langle\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,\rangle_{C},\rho)
be a DFT algebroid over a flat doubled manifold
(M,\eta)
. Then there is a curved
L_{\infty}
-algebra on
L=L_{-1}\oplus L_{0}\oplus L_{2}
with

\displaystyle L_{-1}=C^{\infty}(M)\ ,\quad L_{0}=\mathsf{\Gamma}(C)\qquad\mbox{and}\qquad L_{2}={\sf Span}_{\mathbb{R}}(\eta^{-1})\ ,

whose non-zero brackets are given by

\displaystyle\ell_{0}(1)=\eta^{-1}\quad

\displaystyle\ \,,\ \,\quad\ell_{1}(f)={\mathcal{D}}f\ ,

\displaystyle\ell_{2}(c_{1},c_{2})=\llbracket c_{1},c_{2}\rrbracket_{\tt C}\quad

\displaystyle\ \,,\,\ \quad\ell_{2}(c_{1},f)=\tfrac{1}{2}\,\langle c_{1},{\mathcal{D}}f\rangle_{C}\ ,

\displaystyle\ell_{3}(c_{1},c_{2},c_{3})

\displaystyle=-{\sf Nij}_{\tt C}(c_{1},c_{2},c_{3})\ ,

\displaystyle\ell_{3}(\eta^{-1},f,g)=\tfrac{1}{2}\,\langle{\mathcal{D}}f,{\mathcal{D}}g\rangle_{C}\quad

\displaystyle\ \,,\,\ \quad\ell_{3}(\eta^{-1},c_{1},f)=\tfrac{1}{2}\,\llbracket c_{1},{\mathcal{D}}f\rrbracket_{\tt C}-\tfrac{1}{4}\,{\mathcal{D}}\langle c_{1},{\mathcal{D}}f\rangle_{C}\ ,

\displaystyle\ell_{4}(\eta^{-1},c_{1},c_{2},c_{3})

\displaystyle={\sf Jac}_{\tt C}(c_{1},c_{2},c_{3})-{\mathcal{D}}\,{\sf Nij}_{\tt C}(c_{1},c_{2},c_{3})\ ,

\displaystyle\ell_{5}(\eta^{-1},c_{1},c_{2},c_{3},c_{4})

\displaystyle=\tfrac{1}{2}\,{\sf Alt}_{4}\big\langle{\sf Jac}_{\tt C}(c_{1},c_{2},c_{3})-{\mathcal{D}}\,{\sf Nij}_{\tt C}(c_{1},c_{2},c_{3}),c_{4}\big\rangle_{C}\ ,

for all
f,g\in C^{\infty}(M)
and
c_{1},c_{2},c_{3},c_{4}\in\mathsf{\Gamma}(C)
, where
{\sf Alt}_{4}
is the alternatization map of degree
4
.

5.4.
AKSZ Construction of Doubled Sigma-Models

Using Theorem
3.53
, it is easy to characterize a DFT algebroid
(C,\llbracket\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,\rrbracket_{\tt D},\langle\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,\rangle_{C},\rho)
as a symplectic
2
-algebroid
(C[1]\oplus T^{*}[2]M,\{\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,\},\gamma)
over a doubled manifold
(M,\eta)
. For example, from Lemma
5.10
and the derived bracket constructions of Section
3.4
, it can be characterized by the relation

\displaystyle\{\{\{\gamma,\gamma\},f\},g\}+\{\{\{\gamma,\gamma\},g\},f\}

\displaystyle=2\,\{\{\gamma,f\},\{\gamma,g\}\}

\displaystyle=2\,\langle\boldsymbol{\gamma}_{1}(\mathrm{d}f),\boldsymbol{\gamma}_{1}(\mathrm{d}g)\rangle_{C}

(5.16)

\displaystyle=2\,\eta^{-1}(\mathrm{d}f,\mathrm{d}g)\ ,

for all
f,g\in C^{\infty}(M)
, where we used the graded Jacobi identity for the Poisson bracket along with
\boldsymbol{\gamma}_{1}(\mathrm{d}f)=\{\gamma,f\}={\mathcal{D}}f
. Similarly, the other two identities of Lemma
5.10
can be written in terms of derived brackets using Remark
3.58
. However, as
\{\gamma,\gamma\}\neq 0
, a DFT algebroid does not correspond to a dg-manifold, and so we cannot apply AKSZ theory directly to write down a topological sigma-model whose BV formalism can be used to quantize the DFT algebroid. Note that all ingredients of the BV formalism, including the antibracket, are present except for the classical master equation.

In analogy to the AKSZ sigma-models of Section
2.5
, a three-dimensional topological sigma-model was associated to any DFT algebroid in
[
17
]
by pulling back the fields of the Courant sigma-model corresponding to the large Courant algebroid, using the construction of Section

. The graded symplectic geometry viewpoint of this construction was originally presented in
[

]
, and applied in
[
79
]
to AKSZ theory. The idea behind the construction is simple and is based on the way in which we motivated the definition of a DFT algebroid: Start with a Courant algebroid
(E,[\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,]_{E},\langle\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,\rangle_{E},\rho)
of rank
4d
over the doubled manifold
(M,\eta)
, introduce a generalized split signature metric on
E
, and then restrict the structure maps to obtain metric algebroid structures on the corresponding eigenbundles, as in Proposition
5.1
. This can be rephrased in the language of symplectic
2
-algebroids using Theorems
2.28
and
3.53

over a flat doubled manifold
(M,\eta)
.

The symplectic Lie

-algebroid corresponding to the large Courant algebroid over

\displaystyle\omega=\mathrm{d}\xi_{I}\wedge\mathrm{d}x^{I}+\mathrm{d}\chi_{I}\wedge\mathrm{d}\zeta^{I}\ ,

while the degree

\displaystyle\gamma=\sqrt{2}\ \xi_{I}\,\zeta^{I}\ ,

which we have rescaled for convenience.

Using the spit signature metric

on the degree
0

, we restrict the tangent space coordinates to the diagonal using the coordinates

\displaystyle\tau_{\pm}^{I}=\tfrac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\,\big(\zeta^{I}\pm\eta^{IJ}\,\chi_{J}\big)\ ,

where
\eta^{-1}=\frac{1}{2}\,\eta^{IJ}\,\frac{\partial}{\partial x^{I}}\odot\frac{\partial}{\partial x^{J}}

\displaystyle\omega_{+}=\mathrm{d}\xi_{I}\wedge\mathrm{d}x^{I}+\tfrac{1}{2}\,\eta_{IJ}\,\mathrm{d}\tau_{+}^{I}\wedge\mathrm{d}\tau_{+}^{J}\qquad\mbox{and}\qquad\gamma_{+}=\xi_{I}\,\tau_{+}^{I}\ .

This Hamiltonian is not integrable,
\{\gamma_{+},\gamma_{+}\}=\xi_{I}\,\eta^{IJ}\,\xi_{J}\neq 0

Under the correspondence of Theorem

We choose the symplectic potential

\displaystyle\vartheta_{+}=\xi_{I}\,\mathrm{d}x^{I}+\tfrac{1}{2}\,\tau_{+}^{I}\,\eta_{IJ}\,\mathrm{d}\tau_{+}^{J}\ ,

which is the pullback to

We now pull back the degree

\displaystyle S_{+}(X,A_{+},F)=\int_{\Sigma_{3}}\,\Big(

\displaystyle\langle F,\mathrm{d}X\rangle+\frac{1}{2}\,\langle A_{+},\mathrm{d}A_{+}\rangle_{C_{+}}

\displaystyle\quad\,-\langle F,(\rho_{+}\circ X)A_{+}\rangle+\frac{1}{3!}\,\langle A_{+},\llbracket A_{+},A_{+}\rrbracket_{\tt D}^{+}\rangle_{C_{+}}\Big)\ ,

Remark 5.18
.

Clearly the action functional (
5.4
) can be written down for any DFT algebroid
(C,\llbracket\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,\rrbracket_{\tt D},\langle\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,\rangle_{C},\rho)
over any doubled manifold
(M,\eta)
, not just the special instance in which we have derived it, though in the general case it cannot be derived from AKSZ theory. The reducible open gauge symmetries of (

), have been studied in detail by

,

by projecting the BRST symmetry of the large Courant sigma-model. It is found that gauge invariance and closure of the gauge algebra imply the analogue of the section constraint together with the axioms for a DFT algebroid. For a flat doubled manifold
(M,\eta)
, these give the Bianchi identities for the fluxes
T\in\Omega^{3}(M)
defined by

\displaystyle T(X,Y,Z)=\eta(\llbracket X,Y\rrbracket_{\tt D},Z)

for doubled vectors
X,Y,Z\in\mathsf{\Gamma}(TM)
, which agrees with the flux formulation of double field theory
[
84
]
. In particular, the doubled sigma-model gives a unified description of geometric and non-geometric fluxes, whilst precluding as classical solutions several physically relevant string backgrounds which do not satisfy the section constraint
[
17
]
.

Remark 5.19
.

Gauge invariance of (
5.4
) can also be understood in the AKSZ formalism and the associated (local) curved

-algebra from Theorem
5.15
. By (

) a natural constraint on the symplectic

-algebroid
(C[1]\oplus T^{*}[2]M,\{\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,\},\gamma\}
corresponding to a DFT algebroid which imposes the section constraint is given by

\displaystyle\{\{\{\gamma,\gamma\},f\},g\}=0\ ,

for all degree
0

. This is a slight weakening of the defining condition of a symplectic almost Lie
2
-algebroid from Definition
3.61
, and it coincides with the coordinate-free formulation of the section constraint of double field theory in graded geometry originally presented by
[
85

(see also
[
31
]
); an alternative derived bracket formulation of a DFT algebroid is found in
[
72
]
.

On imposing the section constraint, all brackets involving
\eta^{-1}
in Theorem

vanish and the curving

may be dropped. The remaining brackets then govern the infinitesimal gauge symmetries of the DFT algebroid with the section constraint, and are formally the same as the flat

-algebra of a Courant algebroid from Theorem

, as formulated originally in the graded geometry framework by
[
30
]
as the gauge algebra underlying double field theory (see also
[
86
]
). This is in harmony with the expectation that a DFT algebroid becomes a Courant algebroid when the section constraint is imposed. We will discuss the section constraint further, as well as explicit solutions of the section constraint, in Section

below from a classical geometric perspective. The fact that a DFT algebroid can be characterized by a curved

-algebra suggests that it may be possible to formulate it as a dg-manifold

, though not necessarily one with a compatible symplectic structure; this perspective may allow for an AKSZ-type formulation of the doubled sigma-model as an unconstrained gauge theory which admits a larger set of classical solutions, as well as an extension of BV quantization.

6.
Algebroids and Double Field Theory

In this final section we apply the mathematical framework of this paper to a rigorous study of some kinematical issues in double field theory, including how it reduces to supergravity (in the NS–NS sector) and how T-duality is realized as a manifest symmetry in the doubled geometry formalism.

6.1.
Local Double Field Theory

The standard local treatment of double field theory in the string theory literature
[
13
,
14
,
15
,
87
,
18
,
19
]
is recovered in the case when
(M,K,\eta)
is a flat para-Kähler manifold, and the DFT algebroid is the corresponding canonical metric algebroid
(TM,\llbracket\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,\rrbracket_{\tt D}^{K},\eta,\mathds{1}_{TM})

[
16
]
. In this case, the doubled manifold is locally a product of two
d
-dimensional subspaces
M={\mathcal{Q}}\times\tilde{\mathcal{Q}}
. We can then write local coordinates
x=(x^{I})
,
I=1,\dots,2d
on
M
which are adapted to the foliations in the splitting
TM=L_{+}\oplus L_{-}
into integrable distributions
L_{+}=T{\mathcal{Q}}
and
L_{-}=T\tilde{\mathcal{Q}}
, i.e.
x=(x^{I})=(x^{i},\tilde{x}_{i})
,
i=1,\dots,d
. With respect to this splitting, the split signature metric has the matrix form

\displaystyle\eta=(\eta_{IJ})=\bigg(\begin{matrix}0&\mathds{1}\\
\mathds{1}&0\end{matrix}\bigg)\ ,

and we write its inverse as
\eta^{-1}=(\eta^{IJ})
(with the same matrix form). The Levi-Civita connection
\nabla^{\tt LC}
is trivial, and the local expression for the canonical D-bracket on two vector fields
X=X^{I}\,\frac{\partial}{\partial x^{I}}
and
Y=Y^{I}\,\frac{\partial}{\partial x^{I}}
is given by

\displaystyle\llbracket X,Y\rrbracket_{\tt D}^{K}=\Big(X^{I}\,\frac{\partial Y^{J}}{\partial x^{I}}-Y^{I}\,\frac{\partial X^{J}}{\partial x^{I}}+Y^{I}\,\eta_{IL}\,\frac{\partial X^{L}}{\partial x^{M}}\,\eta^{MJ}\Big)\,\frac{\partial}{\partial x^{J}}\ .

The canonical C-bracket

\displaystyle\llbracket X,Y\rrbracket_{\tt C}^{K}=\tfrac{1}{2}\,\big(\llbracket X,Y\rrbracket_{\tt D}^{K}-\llbracket Y,X\rrbracket_{\tt D}^{K}\big)

is thus the standard C-bracket of double field theory
[
87
]
.

In this sense, standard double field theory is the flat space limit of Born geometry: in the local adapted coordinates, the Born metric is given by

\displaystyle{\mathcal{H}}_{0}(g)=\bigg(\begin{matrix}g&0\\
0&g^{-1}\end{matrix}\bigg)\ ,

for a Riemannian metric
g
. Then
B_{+}
-transformations by
2
-forms
b
give the standard generalized metric

(6.1)

\displaystyle{\mathcal{H}}(g,b)=\bigg(\begin{matrix}g-b\,g^{-1}\,b&b\,g^{-1}\\
-g^{-1}\,b&g^{-1}\end{matrix}\bigg)

of double field theory
[
19
]
. The D-bracket determines the infinitesimal gauge transformations of
{\mathcal{H}}
via the generalized Lie derivative.

The local form of the section constraint reads

\displaystyle\eta({\mathcal{D}}^{\tt can}f,{\mathcal{D}}^{\tt can}g)=\eta^{-1}(\mathrm{d}f,\mathrm{d}g)=\frac{\partial f}{\partial x^{I}}\,\eta^{IJ}\,\frac{\partial g}{\partial x^{J}}=0\ .

Solutions of this constraint select
polarizations
, which are the
d
-dimensional ‘physical’ null submanifolds of the doubled manifold
(M,\eta)
; these are also called
duality frames
. Double field theory then reduces to supergravity in different duality frames, which are related to one another by T-duality transformations. For example, in the ‘supergravity frame’ the section constraint is solved by choosing para-holomorphic functions

\displaystyle\frac{\partial f}{\partial\tilde{x}_{i}}=0\ ,

after which the C-bracket reduces to the local form of the standard Courant bracket on the generalized tangent bundle
\mathbb{T}{\mathcal{Q}}
. With this solution of the section constraint, the metric (
6.1
) becomes the standard generalized metric of generalized geometry (cf. Example
4.8
).

6.2.
Global Aspects of Double Field Theory

Para-Hermitian geometry arises as a framework for doubled geometry when one analyses the implications of the section constraint

\displaystyle\eta^{-1}(\mathrm{d}f,\mathrm{d}g)=0

from a global perspective.
This can be solved by picking a maximally
\eta
-isotropic distribution
L_{-}\subset TM
of the doubled manifold
(M,\eta)
which is integrable. Then the section constraint is solved by foliated tensors; on functions this condition reads

\displaystyle\pounds_{X}f=\iota_{X}\mathrm{d}f=0

for
f\in C^{\infty}(M)
and
X\in\mathsf{\Gamma}(L_{-})
. With
L_{-}=T{\mathcal{F}}
, this polarization selects the physical spacetime as a quotient
{\mathcal{Q}}=M/{\mathcal{F}}
by the action on the leaves of the induced foliation
{\mathcal{F}}
; foliated tensors are then those fields which are compatible with the surjective submersion from
M
to the leaf space
{\mathcal{Q}}
. The fact that the physical spacetime is a quotient, rather than a subspace, of the doubled manifold
(M,\eta)
has been appreciated many times before in the double field theory literature, see e.g.
[
88
,
16
,
89
,
90
]
.

To put this into the context of the present paper, by a
polarization
of a doubled manifold
(M,\eta)
we shall mean a choice of almost para-Hermitian structure
(K,\eta)
on
M
. A central mathematical problem in understanding how the kinematics of double field theory reduces to supergravity, under imposition of the section constraint, is to understand how doubled geometry reduces to generalized geometry. In generalized geometry
[
9
,
8
]
, a generalized vector is a section
X+\alpha
of the generalized tangent bundle
\mathbb{T}{\mathcal{Q}}=T{\mathcal{Q}}\oplus T^{*}{\mathcal{Q}}
, with
X\in\mathsf{\Gamma}(T{\mathcal{Q}})
a vector field and
\alpha\in\Omega^{1}({\mathcal{Q}})
a
1
-form on a manifold
{\mathcal{Q}}
. In doubled geometry, a generalized vector is simply a vector field
X\in\mathsf{\Gamma}(TM)
on the doubled manifold
(M,\eta)
.

For this, we assume that the eigenbundle
L_{-}
of the almost
para-Hermitian manifold is involutive, i.e. it admits integral
manifolds given by the leaves of a regular foliation
{\mathcal{F}}.
Given the projection map
P_{-}:TM\to TM
of Definition
4.19
and the split signature metric
\eta
, define the
P_{-}
-projected
canonical D-bracket
\llbracket\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,\rrbracket_{-}
by the formula

\displaystyle\eta(\llbracket X,Y\rrbracket_{-},Z)=\eta(\nabla_{P_{-}(X)}^{\tt can}Y-\nabla_{P_{-}(Y)}^{\tt can}X,Z)+\eta(\nabla_{P_{-}(Z)}^{\tt can}X,Y)\ ,

where
\nabla^{\tt can}
is the canonical connection of the almost para-Hermitian manifold
(M,K,\eta)
. Then this defines a Courant algebroid structure on the tangent bundle
TM
by
[
21
, Proposition 3.13]
(see also
[
3
, Theorem 5.1.3]
). In other words, this ‘projects’ the canonical metric algebroid
(TM,\llbracket\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,\rrbracket_{\tt D}^{K},\eta,\mathds{1}_{TM})
to the Courant algebroid
(TM,\llbracket\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,\rrbracket_{-},\eta,P_{-})
on the doubled manifold
(M,\eta)
. By Definition
5.7
, this can be straightforwardly generalized to any DFT algebroid over a foliated almost para-Hermitian manifold
(M,K,\eta)

[
17
, Proposition 5.20]
.

Having established that a DFT algebroid can be projected to a Courant algebroid on
(M,\eta)
when the section constraint is imposed, let us now examine what becomes of this Courant algebroid on an explicit solution of the section constraints.
We can construct the generalized tangent bundle
\mathbb{T}{\mathcal{S}}=T{\mathcal{S}}\oplus T^{*}{\mathcal{S}}
on any leaf
{\mathcal{S}}
of the foliation
{\mathcal{F}}
.
Then there is a morphism from

\mathbb{T}{\mathcal{S}}
to
TM
covering the inclusion

{\mathcal{S}}\hookrightarrow M
, which is fibrewise bijective and is induced at the level of sections by the split signature metric
\eta
through

(6.2)

\displaystyle{\tt p}_{-}:\mathsf{\Gamma}(\mathbb{T}{\mathcal{S}})\longrightarrow\mathsf{\Gamma}(TM)\ ,\quad X+\alpha\longmapsto{\tt p}_{-}(X+\alpha)=X+\eta^{-1}{}^{\sharp}(\alpha)\ .

By
[
21
, Proposition 3.13]
, this defines a metric algebroid morphism from the standard Courant algebroid
(\mathbb{T}{\mathcal{S}},[\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,]_{\tt D},\langle\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,\rangle_{\mathbb{T}{\mathcal{S}}},\rho)
on
{\mathcal{S}}
(see Example
2.27
) to the Courant algebroid
(TM,\llbracket\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,\rrbracket_{-},\eta,P_{-})
on
M
, that is,

\displaystyle{\tt p}_{-}\circ[\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,]_{\tt D}=\llbracket\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,\rrbracket_{-}\circ({\tt p}_{-}\times{\tt p}_{-})\ ,\quad\langle\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,\rangle_{\mathbb{T}{\mathcal{S}}}=\eta\circ({\tt p}_{-}\times{\tt p}_{-})\qquad\mbox{and}\qquad\rho=P_{-}\circ{\tt p}_{-}\ .

Altogether, this relates the canonical metric algebroid
(TM,\llbracket\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,\rrbracket_{\tt D}^{K},\eta,\mathds{1}_{TM})
to the standard Courant algebroid on any leaf
{\mathcal{S}}
of the foliation
{\mathcal{F}}
. Again, this construction can be straightforwardly generalized to any DFT algebroid over a foliated almost para-Hermitian manifold
(M,K,\eta)

[
17
, Proposition 5.27]
. In this sense, doubled geometry recovers generalized geometry.

However, the relation to the generalized geometry of the physical spacetime, i.e. the standard Courant algebroid on
\mathbb{T}{\mathcal{Q}}
, is not so transparent in this framework.
Let
{\mathcal{Q}}=M/{\mathcal{F}}
be the leaf space of the foliation
{\mathcal{F}}
of
M
defined by
L_{-}=T{\mathcal{F}}
, and denote by
{\mathtt{q}}:M\to{\mathcal{Q}}
the quotient map. Given the splitting
TM=L_{+}\oplus L_{-}
induced by
K,
the vector bundle morphism
\mathrm{d}{\mathtt{q}}:TM\rightarrow T{\mathcal{Q}},
covering
{\mathtt{q}},
is fibrewise bijective if restricted to
L_{+},
i.e.
\mathrm{d}{\mathtt{q}}\rvert_{L_{+}}:L_{+}\rightarrow T{\mathcal{Q}}
is a fibrewise isomorphism. Hence the
C^{\infty}(M)
-module
\mathsf{\Gamma}(L_{+})
is isomorphic to the
C^{\infty}({\mathcal{Q}})
-module
\mathsf{\Gamma}(T{\mathcal{Q}}).
The metric
\eta
induces a vector bundle isomorphism
L_{-}\to L_{+}^{*}
defined by
X\mapsto\eta^{\flat}(X)
, because
L_{\pm}
are maximally isotropic with respect to
\eta
. Making further statements in this direction is part of the general open problem of reducing metric algebroids to Courant algebroids in a suitable sense (see Remark
5.13
).

Remark 6.3
.

This solution of the section constraint of double field theory can be interpreted in terms of global objects as follows. Any involutive distribution
L_{-}=T{\mathcal{F}}
is naturally a Lie algebroid over
M
with the restriction of the Lie bracket of vector fields
[\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,]_{L_{-}}=[\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,]_{TM}\big\rvert_{\mathsf{\Gamma}(L_{-})\times\mathsf{\Gamma}(L_{-})}
and the inclusion of the subbundle
\ell_{-}:L_{-}\hookrightarrow TM
as anchor map; this defines a Lie subalgebroid of the tangent Lie algebroid
(TM,[\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,]_{TM},\mathds{1}_{TM})
. The Lie algebroid
(L_{-},[\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,]_{L_{-}},\ell_{-})
is naturally integrated by the holonomy groupoid
{\sf Hol}({\mathcal{F}})\rightrightarrows M
of the foliation, which provides a presentation of the leaf space as the quotient
{\mathcal{Q}}=M/{\mathcal{F}}

[
91
]
. When
{\mathcal{Q}}
is a manifold, this is a Lie subgroupoid of the pair groupoid
M\times M\rightrightarrows M
which integrates the tangent Lie algebroid on
M
. In this sense, the holonomy groupoid can be viewed as a smooth replacement for the leaf space.

Remark 6.4
.

There are several global treatments of doubled geometry available in the literature which offer complementary interpretations of the section constraint of double field theory. Here we mention a few that are related to the perspectives offered in the present paper:

becomes a Courant algebroid on

]
. In other words, the canonical metric algebroid is composed of a double of Lie algebroids, analogous to the Drinfel’d double of a pair of Lie algebras (see also
[

•

A global formulation of doubled geometry based on higher geometry appears in
[

within the framework of double field theory on the total (simplicial) space of a bundle gerbe, regarded as a

-principal

[

•

A

A global object integrating a metric algebroid, called a pre-rackoid, has been suggested by

[

for the canonical metric algebroid over any para-Hermitian manifold, which reduce to a rackoid when the section constraint of double field theory is imposed; these pre-rackoids can also be implemented in the corresponding topological doubled sigma-model of Section

,

•

On any foliated flat almost para-Hermitian manifold

[

as an

-morphism from the curved

-algebra of the canonical metric algebroid over

, given by Theorem

{\mathcal{Q}}
of the foliation, given by Theorem

6.3.
Recovering the Physical Background Fields

A central problem in understanding the global formulation of the dynamics of double field theory is to investigate the quotient
{\mathcal{Q}}=M/{\mathcal{F}}
for a foliated almost para-Hermitian manifold
(M,K,\eta)
endowed with a generalized metric
{\mathcal{H}}.
We will do this by first recalling a more general result due to Kotov and Strobl
[
99
,
100
]
.

Let
(M,{\mathcal{H}})
be any Riemannian manifold. Let
(A,[\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,]_{A},{\mathtt{a}})
be a Lie algebroid over
M
endowed with a linear connection
\nabla
, and define the representation of
A
on
TM
by the flat
A
-connection

\prescript{\tau}{}{\nabla}\colon\mathsf{\Gamma}(A)\times\mathsf{\Gamma}(TM)\longrightarrow\mathsf{\Gamma}(TM)

given by

\prescript{\tau}{}{\nabla}_{a}X\coloneqq[{\mathtt{a}}(a),X]_{TM}+{\mathtt{a}}(\nabla_{X}a)\ ,

for all
a\in\mathsf{\Gamma}(A)
and
X\in\mathsf{\Gamma}(TM).

Definition 6.5
.

The triple
(A,\nabla,{\mathcal{H}})
is a
Killing Lie algebroid
if

(6.6)

\prescript{\tau}{}{\nabla}{\mathcal{H}}=0\ .

The Killing vector fields for
{\mathcal{H}}
are given by
X={\mathtt{a}}(a)
for any covariantly constant section
a\in\mathsf{\Gamma}(A).
Killing Lie algebroids are related to quotients by

Proposition 6.7
.

Let
(A,[\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,]_{A},{\mathtt{a}})
be a Lie algebroid over a Riemannian manifold
(M,{\mathcal{H}})
, endowed with a linear conection
\nabla
, whose anchor map
{\mathtt{a}}
is injective and has constant rank, so that its image
\mathsf{Im}({\mathtt{a}})
defines a regular foliation
{\mathcal{F}}
of
M.
Then
(A,\nabla,{\mathcal{H}})
is a Killing Lie algebroid if and only if there is a Riemannian submersion

{\mathtt{q}}\colon(M,{\mathcal{H}})\longrightarrow({\mathcal{Q}},g)

where
{\mathcal{Q}}=M/{\mathcal{F}}
is the leaf space of the foliation
{\mathcal{F}}
and

is a Riemannian metric on
{\mathcal{Q}}.

This result allows us to understand under which circumstances the quotient implementing the section constraint exists. For further details and proofs see
[

,

,

]
.

Remark 6.8
.

Proposition
6.7
can be interpreted globally from a Lie groupoid perspective
[
101
]
. The existence of a Riemannian submersion
{\mathtt{q}}\colon(M,{\mathcal{H}})\rightarrow({\mathcal{Q}},g)
is equivalent to the statement that the submersion groupoid
M\times_{\mathcal{Q}}M\rightrightarrows{\mathcal{Q}}
is endowed with a
0
-metric, i.e. a metric which is invariant under the canonical action of
M\times_{\mathcal{Q}}M
on its base manifold
{\mathcal{Q}}.

Conversely, for a submersion
{\mathtt{q}}\colon M\rightarrow{\mathcal{Q}}
to be Riemannian it suffices to check for the existence of a
1
-metric on
M\times_{\mathcal{Q}}M\rightrightarrows{\mathcal{Q}}
, i.e. a metric on the manifold of arrows which is transverse with respect to the source map and for which the inversion map is an isometry, because it induces a
0
-metric. It is further shown in
[
101
]
that any
0
-metric on

can always be extended to a
1
-metric.

In order to understand the condition (
6.6
) let us discuss further the case of a regularly foliated base Riemannian manifold. Choose an orthogonal splitting
s_{\perp}
of the canonical short exact sequence

(6.9)

0\longrightarrow T{\mathcal{F}}\xlongrightarrow{}TM\xlongrightarrow{}\nu({\mathcal{F}})\longrightarrow 0

where
\nu({\mathcal{F}})
is the normal bundle of the foliation. Then
TM\simeq\mathsf{Im}(s_{\perp})\oplus T{\mathcal{F}}
, and with respect to this splitting the Riemannian metric takes the form

(6.10)

{\mathcal{H}}=\bigg(\begin{matrix}g_{\perp}&0\\
0&g_{\parallel}\end{matrix}\bigg)\ ,

where
g_{\perp}
is a fibrewise metric on
\mathsf{Im}(s_{\perp})
and
g_{\parallel}
is a fibrewise metric on
T{\mathcal{F}}.
Therefore the condition (

) is equivalent to
[

,

]

(6.11)

\pounds_{X_{\parallel}}g_{\perp}=0\ ,

for all

which states a further equivalence with the requirement that

is a
bundle-like metric
on
M,
see
[

]
.
Then (

into a Riemannian foliation. Clearly, when a Riemannian submersion

Remark 6.12
.

A Riemannian foliation
(M,{\mathcal{F}},g_{\perp})
induces a
0
-metric on the holonomy groupoid
{\mathsf{Hol}}({\mathcal{F}})\rightrightarrows M
. Again, any
0
-metric on

can be extended to a

For an almost para-Hermitian manifold
(M,K,\eta)
endowed with a generalized metric
{\mathcal{H}},
characterized by the pair
(g_{+},b_{+})
according to Proposition
4.7
, we assume the eigenbundle
L_{-}
of
K
is integrable, that is,
L_{-}=T{\mathcal{F}},
where
{\mathcal{F}}
is the induced foliation. We further assume that the leaf space
{\mathcal{Q}}=M/{\mathcal{F}}
is a manifold. Then the splitting

of (

) corresponds to the para-Hermitian structure given by the

-transformation of

induced by the
2
-form

. Thus

takes the diagonal form (
6.10
) with

that is,
(TM,K_{B_{+}},\eta,{\mathcal{H}})
is a Born vector bundle on

is characterized by the corresponding Bott connection on

as discussed in
[

, whereby the Riemannian metric on

is used to construct the corresponding connection on

On the other hand, any
B_{+}
-transformation preserves the foliation
L_{-}=T{\mathcal{F}}
and induces a splitting of
TM
such that the transformed generalized metric
{\mathcal{H}}_{B_{+}}
has only a different
g_{\parallel}
component. In other words,
B_{+}
-transformations preserve the Riemannian foliation
(M,{\mathcal{F}},g_{+}).
Thus when the quotient map
{\mathtt{q}}:M\rightarrow{\mathcal{Q}}
is a Riemannian submersion, it remains the same for all the
B_{+}
-transformed generalized metrics. Similarly, any diffeomorphism
\phi\in{\mathsf{Diff}}(M)
preserving the Riemannian foliation

such that

(M,{\mathcal{H}}_{\phi})
is still mapped into

with

The
B_{+}
-transformed subbundle

is no longer isotropic with respect to the fundamental 2-form

, and one has

\omega\big(e^{B_{+}}(X_{+}),e^{B_{+}}(Y_{+})\big)=2\,b_{+}(X_{+},Y_{+})\ ,

for all

If the 2-form

In other words, the leaf space

becomes a string target space whose background fields (in the NS–NS sector) are given by the pair

Remark 6.14
.

If we relax the requirement that the leaf space

Following the standard terminology

6.4.
Generalized T-Duality

Double field theory originated as an attempt to extend supergravity, which is described by generalized geometry, into a theory which is manifestly symmetric under the fundamental T-duality symmetry of string theory, that exchanges distinct physical spacetimes and background fields: in doubled geometry T-duality is realized as suitable diffeomorphisms of a doubled manifold. Let us now discuss how this fits into the treatment of the present paper. For this, we introduce a notion of T-duality for almost para-Hermitian manifolds endowed with a generalized metric, starting from the natural notion of symmetries of para-Hermitian vector bundles.

Proposition 6.15
.

Let
\vartheta\in{\sf Aut}(E)
be an automorphism of a para-Hermitian vector bundle
(E,K,\eta)
of rank
2d
which is an isometry of the split signature metric
\eta.
Then the para-Hermitian structure
(K,\eta)
is mapped by
\vartheta
into another para-Hermitian structure
(K_{\vartheta},\eta),
where
K_{\vartheta}=\vartheta^{-1}\circ K\circ\vartheta
.

Remark 6.16
.

The automorphisms of Proposition
6.15
form a subgroup of
{\sf Aut}(E)
denoted by
{\sf O}(d,d)(E).
Any element
\vartheta\in{\sf O}(d,d)(E)
maps a generalized metric
{\mathcal{H}}
on
(E,K,\eta)
into another generalized metric
{\mathcal{H}}_{\vartheta}
on
(E,K_{\vartheta},\eta)
.

In the applications to doubled geometry, we take
E=TM,
and write
{\sf O}(d,d)(M)
for
{\sf O}(d,d)(E)
. In this case
the transformations of Proposition
6.15
have been identified as
generalized T-dualities
in
[
76
]
, which encompass many known examples, including non-abelian T-duality transformations. They naturally induce changes of polarization
(K,\eta)
for solutions of the section constraint on a doubled manifold
(M,\eta)
. The doubled geometry viewpoint allows for an interpretation of the usual notion of T-duality by establishing a correspondence between quotients of a doubled manifold with respect to different foliations.

For this, let
(M,\eta)
be a foliated doubled manifold endowed with an almost para-Hermitian structure
(K,\eta)
and a generalized metric
{\mathcal{H}}
such that
(M,{\mathcal{F}},g_{+})
is a Riemannian foliation, where
L_{-}=T{\mathcal{F}}
is the integrable
-1
-eigenbundle of
K
and
(g_{+},b_{+})
is the pair identifying
{\mathcal{H}}
in the splitting of the tangent bundle
TM
given by
K
. Then a T-duality transformation is given by an
\eta
-isometric diffeomorphism
\phi
of
M
that maps the triple
(K,\eta,{\mathcal{H}})
into
(K_{\phi},\eta,{\mathcal{H}}_{\phi})
, and the foliation
{\mathcal{F}}
into a different foliation
{\mathcal{F}}_{\phi}.
We require that
(M,{\mathcal{F}}_{\phi},{g_{+}}_{\phi})
be a Riemannian foliation, where
({g_{+}}_{\phi},{b_{+}}_{\phi})
is the pair identifying the generalized metric
{\mathcal{H}}_{\phi}
in the splitting given by
K_{\phi}
. This construction is depicted by the diagram
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(M,{\mathcal{F}},{\mathcal{H}})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(M,{\mathcal{F}}_{\phi},{\mathcal{H}}_{\phi})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces({\mathcal{Q}},g_{+})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces({\mathcal{Q}}_{\phi},{g_{+}}_{\phi})}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\phi}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\mathtt{q}}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\mathtt{q}}_{\phi}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\mathscr{T}}}

where the dashed arrow (indicatively) defines the T-duality

from the leaf space
{\mathcal{Q}}=M/{\mathcal{F}}
to the leaf space
{\mathcal{Q}}_{\phi}=M/{\mathcal{F}}_{\phi}
via this diagram.

Here we do not demand that the leaf spaces be endowed with smooth structures. For instance, this construction makes sense when the leaf spaces admit an orbifold structure, see Remark
6.14
. Thus it may happen that a T-duality transformation takes a geometric background, with smooth leaf space
{\mathcal{Q}}
, to a T-fold. It may also happen that the eigenbundle
{L_{-}}_{\phi}
of

is not integrable; this corresponds to a generalized T-duality which sends a geometric background to an essentially doubled space. These are the ways in which the prototypical non-geometric backgrounds of string theory arise (see e.g.
[

for reviews).

Notice that diffeomorphisms

(M,{\mathcal{F}},g_{+})
give trivial T-duality transformations

. We can also extend this constuction beyond diffeomorphisms of
M
to more general automorphisms
\vartheta\in{\sf O}(d,d)(M)
of the tangent bundle
TM
. In particular,
B_{+}
-tranformations preserve the foliation, i.e. the eigenbundle
L_{-},
and so give trivial T-dualities as well.

Remark 6.17
.

There is a natural equivalence relation on foliated manifolds called ‘Haudorff Morita equivalence’ that preserves regular foliations and induces Morita equivalent holonomy groupoids, see
[
109
]
. Applying this notion to the case at hand, two foliations
{\mathcal{F}}
and
{\mathcal{F}}^{\prime}
of
M
are Hausdorff Morita equivalent if there exists a manifold
P
and two surjective submersions
\pi,\pi^{\prime}:P\to M
with connected fibres such that
\pi^{-1}{\mathcal{F}}=\pi^{\prime}{}^{-1}{\mathcal{F}}^{\prime}
:
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces P}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(M,{\mathcal{F}})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(M,{\mathcal{F}}^{\prime})}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\pi}

Then the leaf spaces
{\mathcal{Q}}=M/{\mathcal{F}}
and

The construction above is equivalent to saying that a T-duality transformation is given by two Hausdorff Morita equivalent Riemannian foliations where the equivalence classes are induced by restricting to
\eta

It might be argued that diffeomorphisms which preserve a Riemannian foliation form a subclass of the class of Hausdorff Morita equivalent foliations. Thus a chain of T-duality transformations might be given by different Hausdorff Morita equivalent subclasses of Riemannian foliations inside a Hausdorff Morita equivalence class of foliations for the doubled manifold
(M,\eta).
Then different Hausdorff Morita equivalence classes correspond to different T-duality chains.

Error-Correction for Sparse Support Recovery Algorithms
Mohammad Mehrabi, Aslan Tchamkerten

Abstract
Consider the compressed sensing setup where the support 𝐬 ∗ \mathbf{s}^{*} of an m m -sparse d d -dimensional signal 𝐱 \mathbf{x} is to be recovered from n n linear measurements with a given algorithm. Suppose that the measurements are such that the algorithm does not guarantee perfect support recovery and that true features may be missed. Can they efficiently be retrieved? This paper addresses this question through a simple error-correction module referred to as LiRE. LiRE takes as input an estimate 𝐬 in \mathbf{s}_{\text{in}} of the true support 𝐬 ∗ \mathbf{s}^{*} , and outputs a refined support estimate 𝐬 out \mathbf{s}_{\text{out}} . In the noiseless measurement setup, sufficient conditions are established under which LiRE is guaranteed to recover the entire support, that is 𝐬 out ⊇ 𝐬 ∗ \mathbf{s}_{\text{out}}\supseteq\mathbf{s}^{*} . These conditions imply, for instance, that in the high-dimensional regime LiRE can correct a sublinear in m m number of errors made by Orthogonal Matching Pursuit (OMP). The computational complexity of LiRE is 𝒪 ⁡ ( m ​ n ​ d ) {\cal{O}}(mnd) . Experimental results with random Gaussian design matrices show that LiRE substantially reduces the number of measurements needed for perfect support recovery via Compressive Sampling Matching Pursuit, Basis Pursuit (BP), and OMP. Interestingly, adding LiRE to OMP yields a support recovery procedure that is more accurate and significantly faster than BP. This observation carries over in the noisy measurement setup where the combination of LiRE and OMP is faster and more accurate than LASSO. Finally, as a standalone support recovery algorithm with a random initialization, experiments show that LiRE’s reconstruction performance lies between OMP and BP. These results suggest that LiRE may be used generically, on top of any suboptimal baseline support recovery algorithm, to improve support recovery or to operate with a smaller number of measurements, at the cost of a relatively small computational overhead. Alternatively, LiRE may be used as a standalone support recovery algorithm that is competitive with respect to OMP.

Related works

The problem of solving the under-determined system of equations given by (
1

[

Over the past fifteen years, a significant amount of work has gone into the design of ever more efficient reconstruction algorithms, and conditions on

m

. Recall that in high dimensions (say,

), the computational bottleneck for computing

lies in finding
\mathbf{s}^{*}

\mathbf{s}^{*}

is obtained by minimizing

There are two main categories of signal reconstruction algorithms. In the first category, algorithms attempt to solve a convex relaxation of the original non-convex optimization problem, similarly to BP. In the second category, a support estimate is first constructed, typically in an iterative and greedy manner, then the signal is estimated. Prominent algorithms here include OMP and CoSaMP.
It is generally accepted that algorithms based on convex relaxations achieve better sparsity-undersampling performance than greedy algorithms at the cost of an increase in computational complexity—see, for instance,
[
26
]
for complexity/performance comparison of BP and OMP. Beyond greedy algorithms and in the quest of ever faster reconstruction algorithms, a line of works takes advantage of distributed and parallel computation to further reduce computational time (see,
e.g.
,
[
27

28
]
).

Although many recovery algorithms
have been shown to perform well in certain settings, the tradeoff between reconstruction performance
and computational complexity remains elusive in general. The present work is a further exploration of this tradeoff by providing a means to increase reconstruction performance at the cost of a relatively small computational overhead.

II
List Regression Error-Correction (LiRE)

Consider the noiseless linear model

\displaystyle{\mathbf{y}}={\mathbf{\Phi}}\mathbf{x}^{*}

(2)

with
\mathbf{x}^{*}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
{\mathbf{y}}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
n\leq d
, and where the design matrix
{\mathbf{\Phi}}\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times d}
is assumed to have unit
l_{2}
columns without loss of generality. Given
{\mathbf{\Phi}}
,
{\mathbf{y}}
, and knowing that

|\mathbf{s}^{*}|\overset{\text{def}}{=}|\text{supp}(\mathbf{x}^{*})|\leq m\leq n,

we want to find
\mathbf{s}
such that
\mathbf{s}^{*}\subseteq\mathbf{s}
and
|\mathbf{s}|=m
.

Given an initial estimate
\mathbf{s}_{\text{in}}
of
\mathbf{s}^{*}
that potentially misses true features, LiRE attempts to produce a second estimate
\mathbf{s}_{\text{out}}
that contains all true features, that is
\mathbf{s}_{\text{out}}\supseteq\mathbf{s}^{*}
. At the heart of LiRE is a leave-one-out procedure for error-correction purpose which checks, for each feature of the initial support estimate, whether it can be replaced by a better one. The pseudo-code of LiRE is given below and is followed by comments in light of existing support recovery algorithms:

Feature

\ell

The support expansion through the list is reminiscent of the signal proxy formation in several greedy algorithms, including OMP, gOMP
[
31
]
, and CoSaMP. This step, however, serves here the purpose of error-correction as it allows to test whether a particular feature should be replaced or not. Intuitively, a wrong feature is less likely to be corrected if the list size is small. But a correct feature is also more likely to get replaced by a wrong feature if the list is large. Accordingly, the theoretical guarantees for successful error-correction provided below (Section
II-B
) tie the number of errors and the list size in an attempt to strike a balance between these two types of error.

For each of its
m

•

Two least square problems, cost
{\mathcal{O}}(m^{2}n)

{\mathcal{O}}(mn)
using approximation methods (see Section

•

•

Two sortings of

Unfortunately, such conditions are hardly available. And even when they are, such as for OMP, non-trivial upper bounds on the number of errors remain elusive.
3
3

3

Note that here we are interested in conditions on the design matrix under which a suboptimal algorithm produces at most a certain number of errors, in the worst-case over
\mathbf{x}^{*}
. In fact, several works investigate the fundamental limitations of non-zero error support recovery, or recovery with distortion, in probabilistic setups (see,
e.g.
,
[
24
,
25
]
).

Hence, to provide a practical assessment of the performance of LiRE without bounds on the number of errors we resorted to numerical simulations which are presented in the next section.

Lemma 1

(Lemmas
1
and
2
in
[
12
]
)
.

Let
\bm{l},\mathbf{s}
be disjoint support vectors and suppose
\delta_{|\bm{l}|+|\mathbf{s}|}<1
.
Then,
for all
\bf{a}\in\mathbb{R}^{|\bm{l}|}
and
\bf{b}\in\mathbb{R}^{|\mathbf{s}|}
we have

\displaystyle|\bf{a}^{\mathsf{T}}{\mathbf{\Phi}}_{\bm{l}}^{\mathsf{T}}{\mathbf{\Phi}}_{\mathbf{s}}\bf{b}|\leq\delta_{|\bm{l}|+|\mathbf{s}|}||\bf{a}||_{2}||\bf{b}||_{2}\,,

\displaystyle||{\mathbf{\Phi}}_{\bm{l}}^{\mathsf{T}}{\mathbf{\Phi}}_{\mathbf{s}}\bf{b}||_{2}\leq\delta_{|\bm{l}|+|\mathbf{s}|}||\bf{b}||_{2}\,.

Lemma 2
.

Let
\mathbf{s},\mathbf{q}
be support vectors that are disjoint from support vector
\bm{l}
. If
\max\{\delta_{|\mathbf{s}|+|\bm{l}|},\delta_{|\mathbf{q}|+|\bm{l}|}\}<1
, then for any
{\bf{a}}\in\mathbb{R}^{|\mathbf{s}|}
we have

\displaystyle||P\{{\mathbf{\Phi}}_{\bm{l}}\}{\mathbf{\Phi}}_{\mathbf{s}}{\bf{a}}||_{2}\leq\frac{\delta_{|\mathbf{s}|+|\bm{l}|}}{\sqrt{1-\delta_{|\bm{l}|}}}||{\bf{a}}||_{2}\,,

\displaystyle||{\mathbf{\Phi}}_{\mathbf{q}}^{\mathsf{T}}P\{{\mathbf{\Phi}}_{\bm{l}}\}{\mathbf{\Phi}}_{\mathbf{s}}{\bf{a}}||_{2}\leq\frac{\delta_{|\mathbf{s}|+|\bm{l}|}\delta_{|\mathbf{q}|+|\bm{l}|}}{{1-\delta_{|\bm{l}|}}}||\bf{a}||_{2}\,.

Proof.

We know that

||P\{{\mathbf{\Phi}}_{\bm{l}}\}{\mathbf{\Phi}}_{\mathbf{s}}{\bf{a}}||_{2}^{2}=\mathbf{u}^{\mathsf{T}}({\mathbf{\Phi}}^{\mathsf{T}}_{\bm{l}}{\mathbf{\Phi}}_{\bm{l}})^{-1}\mathbf{u}\,,

where
\mathbf{u}\overset{\text{def}}{=}{\mathbf{\Phi}}_{\bm{l}}^{\mathsf{T}}{\mathbf{\Phi}}_{\mathbf{s}}{\bf{a}}
. From the definition of the restricted isometry property we get

\mathbf{u}^{\mathsf{T}}({\mathbf{\Phi}}^{\mathsf{T}}_{\bm{l}}{\mathbf{\Phi}}_{\bm{l}})^{-1}\mathbf{u}\leq\frac{||\mathbf{u}||_{2}^{2}}{1-\delta_{|\bm{l}|}}\,.

Next, Lemma
1
gives us

||\mathbf{u}||_{2}\leq\delta_{|\bm{l}|+|\mathbf{s}|}||\mathbf{a}||_{2}\,.

This implies

||P\{{\mathbf{\Phi}}_{\bm{l}}\}{\mathbf{\Phi}}_{\mathbf{s}}a||_{2}\leq\frac{\delta_{|\bm{l}|+|\mathbf{s}|}}{\sqrt{1-\delta_{|\bm{l}|}}}||a||_{2}.

For the proof of the second inequality in Lemma
2
first assume
||\mathbf{b}||_{2}\leq 1
. Then, the Cauchy inequality yields

\mathbf{b}^{\mathsf{T}}{\mathbf{\Phi}}_{\mathbf{q}}^{\mathsf{T}}P\{{\mathbf{\Phi}}_{\bm{l}}\}{\mathbf{\Phi}}_{\mathbf{s}}\mathbf{a}\leq||P\{{\mathbf{\Phi}}_{\bm{l}}\}{\mathbf{\Phi}}_{\mathbf{q}}\mathbf{b}||_{2}||P\{{\mathbf{\Phi}}_{\bm{l}}\}{\mathbf{\Phi}}_{\mathbf{s}}\mathbf{a}||_{2}.

Next, by invoking the first inequality in Lemma
2
we obtain

\max\limits_{||\mathbf{b}||_{2}\leq 1}\mathbf{b}^{\mathsf{T}}{\mathbf{\Phi}}_{\mathbf{q}}^{\mathsf{T}}P\{{\mathbf{\Phi}}_{\bm{l}}\}{\mathbf{\Phi}}_{\mathbf{s}}\mathbf{a}\leq\frac{\delta_{|\mathbf{s}|+|\bm{l}|}\delta_{|\mathbf{q}|+|\bm{l}|}}{{1-\delta_{|\bm{l}|}}}||\mathbf{a}||_{2}\,,

which establishes the desired result.
∎

Lemma 3
.

Let
\mathbf{s}_{1},\mathbf{s}_{2}
be two disjoint support vectors with
|\mathbf{s}_{1}|=k_{1}
and
|\mathbf{s}_{2}|=k_{2}
. If
\delta_{k_{1}+k_{2}}\leq 0.5
, then
{\mathbf{\Phi}}_{\mathbf{s}_{1}}^{\mathsf{T}}(I-P\{{\mathbf{\Phi}}_{\mathbf{s}_{2}}\}){\mathbf{\Phi}}_{\mathbf{s}_{1}}
is invertible and

\displaystyle({\mathbf{\Phi}}_{\mathbf{s}_{1}}^{\mathsf{T}}(I-P\{{\mathbf{\Phi}}_{\mathbf{s}_{2}}\}){\mathbf{\Phi}}_{\mathbf{s}_{1}})^{-1}\succeq\frac{1}{1+\delta_{k_{1}}}I_{k_{1}}\,.

Proof.

Given
{\mathbf{z}}\in\mathbb{R}^{k_{1}}
we have

{\mathbf{z}}^{\mathsf{T}}({\mathbf{\Phi}}_{\mathbf{s}_{1}}^{\mathsf{T}}(I-P\{{\mathbf{\Phi}}_{\mathbf{s}_{2}}\}){\mathbf{\Phi}}_{\mathbf{s}_{1}})z=||{\mathbf{\Phi}}_{\mathbf{s}_{1}}{\mathbf{z}}||_{2}^{2}-||P\{{\mathbf{\Phi}}_{\mathbf{s}_{2}}\}{\mathbf{\Phi}}_{\mathbf{s}_{1}}{\mathbf{z}}||_{2}^{2}\,,

which is no larger than
||{\mathbf{\Phi}}_{\mathbf{s}_{1}}{\mathbf{z}}||_{2}^{2}
. Next, recall that the RIP property implies

||{\mathbf{\Phi}}_{\mathbf{s}_{1}}z||_{2}^{2}\leq(1+\delta_{k_{1}})||{\mathbf{z}}||_{2}^{2}.

(13)

Therefore,

({\mathbf{\Phi}}_{\mathbf{s}_{1}}^{\mathsf{T}}(I-P\{{\mathbf{\Phi}}_{\mathbf{s}_{2}}\}){\mathbf{\Phi}}_{\mathbf{s}_{1}})\preceq(1+\delta_{k_{1}})I_{k_{1}}.

Moreover, Lemma
2
gives us

||P\{{\mathbf{\Phi}}_{\mathbf{s}_{2}}\}{\mathbf{\Phi}}_{\mathbf{s}_{1}}{\mathbf{z}}||_{2}^{2}\leq\frac{\delta_{k}^{2}||{\mathbf{z}}||_{2}^{2}}{1-\delta_{k_{2}}}.

(14)

By combining (
13
) and (
14
) we get

\displaystyle(1-\delta_{k_{1}}-\frac{\delta_{k}^{2}}{1-\delta_{k_{2}}})I_{k_{1}}

\displaystyle\preceq({\mathbf{\Phi}}_{\mathbf{s}_{1}}^{\mathsf{T}}(I-P\{{\mathbf{\Phi}}_{\mathbf{s}_{2}}\}){\mathbf{\Phi}}_{\mathbf{s}_{1}})

\displaystyle\preceq(1+\delta_{k_{1}})I_{k_{1}}\,.

Therefore
({\mathbf{\Phi}}_{\mathbf{s}_{1}}^{\mathsf{T}}(I-P\{{\mathbf{\Phi}}_{\mathbf{s}_{2}}\}){\mathbf{\Phi}}_{\mathbf{s}_{1}})
is invertible and we have the desired property.
∎

Lemma 4
.

Let
\mathbf{s}_{1},\mathbf{s}_{2}
be two disjoint support vectors, let
\mathbf{s}=\mathbf{s}_{1}\cup\mathbf{s}_{2}
, and suppose
\delta_{|\mathbf{s}_{1}|+|\mathbf{s}_{2}|}\leq 0.5
. Then,

\displaystyle\mathbf{x}_{\mathbf{s}_{1}}=({\mathbf{\Phi}}_{\mathbf{s}_{1}}^{\mathsf{T}}(I-P\{{\mathbf{\Phi}}_{\mathbf{s}_{2}}\}){\mathbf{\Phi}}_{\mathbf{s}_{1}})^{-1}{\mathbf{\Phi}}_{\mathbf{s}_{1}}^{\mathsf{T}}{\mathbf{y}}^{\perp\mathbf{s}_{2}}.

Proof.

Since
\delta_{|\mathbf{s}_{1}|+|\mathbf{s}_{2}|}<1
we have that
{\mathbf{\Phi}}_{\mathbf{s}}
is invertible and
{\mathbf{x}}_{\mathbf{s}}=({\mathbf{\Phi}}_{\mathbf{s}}^{\mathsf{T}}{\mathbf{\Phi}}_{\mathbf{s}})^{-1}{\mathbf{\Phi}}_{\mathbf{s}}^{\mathsf{T}}{\mathbf{y}}
. Also, since
\mathbf{s}=\mathbf{s}_{1}\cup\mathbf{s}_{2}
we have

\displaystyle\begin{bmatrix}{\mathbf{x}}_{\mathbf{s}_{1}}\\
{\mathbf{x}}_{\mathbf{s}_{2}}\end{bmatrix}=\begin{bmatrix}{\mathbf{\Phi}}_{\mathbf{s}_{1}}^{\mathsf{T}}{\mathbf{\Phi}}_{\mathbf{s}_{1}}&{\mathbf{\Phi}}_{\mathbf{s}_{1}}^{\mathsf{T}}{\mathbf{\Phi}}_{\mathbf{s}_{2}}\\
{\mathbf{\Phi}}_{\mathbf{s}_{2}}^{\mathsf{T}}{\mathbf{\Phi}}_{\mathbf{s}_{1}}&{\mathbf{\Phi}}_{\mathbf{s}_{2}}^{\mathsf{T}}{\mathbf{\Phi}}_{\mathbf{s}_{2}}\\
\end{bmatrix}^{-1}\begin{bmatrix}{\mathbf{\Phi}}_{\mathbf{s}_{1}}^{\mathsf{T}}{\mathbf{y}}\\
{\mathbf{\Phi}}_{\mathbf{s}_{2}}^{\mathsf{T}}{\mathbf{y}}\\
\end{bmatrix}\,.

Let
{\mathbf{\Psi}}
denote the Schur complement of block
{\mathbf{\Phi}}_{\mathbf{s}_{2}}^{\mathsf{T}}{\mathbf{\Phi}}_{\mathbf{s}_{2}}
of the matrix
{\mathbf{\Phi}}_{\mathbf{s}}^{\mathsf{T}}{\mathbf{\Phi}}_{\mathbf{s}}
. It then follows that

{\mathbf{\Psi}}={\mathbf{\Phi}}_{\mathbf{s}_{1}}^{\mathsf{T}}{\mathbf{\Phi}}_{\mathbf{s}_{1}}-{\mathbf{\Phi}}_{\mathbf{s}_{1}}^{\mathsf{T}}{\mathbf{\Phi}}_{\mathbf{s}_{2}}({\mathbf{\Phi}}_{\mathbf{s}_{2}}^{\mathsf{T}}{\mathbf{\Phi}}_{\mathbf{s}_{2}})^{-1}{\mathbf{\Phi}}_{\mathbf{s}_{2}}^{\mathsf{T}}{\mathbf{\Phi}}_{\mathbf{s}_{1}},

i.e.
,
{\mathbf{\Psi}}={\mathbf{\Phi}}_{\mathbf{s}_{1}}^{\mathsf{T}}(I-P\{{\mathbf{\Phi}}_{\mathbf{s}_{2}}\}){\mathbf{\Phi}}_{\mathbf{s}_{1}}
. Since
\delta_{|\mathbf{s}_{1}|+|\mathbf{s}_{2}|}\leq 0.5
, we have that
{\mathbf{\Psi}}
is invertible by Lemma
3
. On the other hand, from the properties of invertible Schur complement we know that

{\mathbf{x}}_{\mathbf{s}_{1}}={\mathbf{\Psi}}^{-1}{\mathbf{\Phi}}_{\mathbf{s}_{1}}^{\mathsf{T}}{\mathbf{y}}-{\mathbf{\Psi}}^{-1}{\mathbf{\Phi}}_{\mathbf{s}_{1}}^{\mathsf{T}}{\mathbf{\Phi}}_{\mathbf{s}_{2}}({\mathbf{\Phi}}_{\mathbf{s}_{2}}^{\mathsf{T}}{\mathbf{\Phi}}_{\mathbf{s}_{2}})^{-1}{\mathbf{\Phi}}_{\mathbf{s}_{2}}^{\mathsf{T}}{\mathbf{y}},

which can be rewritten as
{\mathbf{x}}_{\mathbf{s}_{1}}={\mathbf{\Psi}}^{-1}{\mathbf{\Phi}}_{\mathbf{s}_{1}}^{\mathsf{T}}{{\mathbf{y}}}^{\perp{\mathbf{s}_{2}}}
, thereby completing the proof.
∎

Lemma 5
.

Fix a length
k
support vector
\bm{l}
, let
\ell\overset{\text{def}}{=}\max\{m+1,k\}
, and let
\mathbf{x}
be the regression of
{\mathbf{y}}
on
\bm{l}
, i.e.,
\mathbf{x}=\mathcal{E}(\bm{l})
.
If
\delta_{\ell}\leq 0.5
, then for any
1\leq j\leq k
we have

|\mathbf{x}_{\bm{l}[j]}|\leq\frac{\delta_{\ell}}{1-\delta_{\ell}-\delta_{\ell}^{2}}||\mathbf{x}^{*}_{\mathbf{s}^{*}/\bm{l}}||_{2}

whenever
\bm{l}[j]\notin\mathbf{s}^{*}
.

Proof.

Write
\bm{l}
as
\bm{l}[j]\cup\bm{l}[-j]
. Since
\delta_{k}\leq 0.5
we can apply Lemma
4
and get

|\mathbf{x}_{\bm{l}[j]}|=\frac{|{\mathbf{\Phi}}_{\bm{l}[j]}^{\mathsf{T}}{\mathbf{y}}^{\perp\bm{l}[-j]}|}{||{\mathbf{\Phi}}_{\bm{l}[j]}^{\perp\bm{l}[-j]}||_{2}^{2}}.

(15)

We proceed by upper-bounding the numerator and lower-bounding the denominator of (
15
). For the numerator we have

\displaystyle|{\mathbf{\Phi}}_{\bm{l}[j]}^{\mathsf{T}}{\mathbf{y}}^{\perp\bm{l}[-j]}|

\displaystyle=|{\mathbf{\Phi}}_{\bm{l}[j]}^{\mathsf{T}}(I-P\{{\mathbf{\Phi}}_{\bm{l}[-j]}\}){\mathbf{\Phi}}_{\mathbf{s}^{*}}\mathbf{x}^{*}|

\displaystyle=|{\mathbf{\Phi}}_{\bm{l}[j]}^{\mathsf{T}}(I-P\{{\mathbf{\Phi}}_{\bm{l}[-j]}\}){\mathbf{\Phi}}_{\mathbf{s}^{*}/\bm{l}[-j]}\mathbf{x}^{*}_{\mathbf{s}^{*}/\bm{l}[-j]}|

\displaystyle\leq|{\mathbf{\Phi}}_{\bm{l}[j]}^{\mathsf{T}}{\mathbf{\Phi}}_{\mathbf{s}^{*}/\bm{l}[-j]}\mathbf{x}^{*}_{\mathbf{s}^{*}/\bm{l}[-j]}|+|{\mathbf{\Phi}}_{\bm{l}[j]}^{\mathsf{T}}P\{{\mathbf{\Phi}}_{\bm{l}[-j]}\}{\mathbf{\Phi}}_{\mathbf{s}^{*}/\bm{l}[-j]}\mathbf{x}^{*}_{\mathbf{s}^{*}/\bm{l}[-j]}|

\displaystyle\overset{(a)}{\leq}\delta_{m+1}||\mathbf{x}^{*}_{\mathbf{s}^{*}/\bm{l}[-j]}||_{2}+||P\{{\mathbf{\Phi}}_{\bm{l}[-j]}\}{\mathbf{\Phi}}_{\bm{l}[j]}||_{2}||P\{{\mathbf{\Phi}}_{\bm{l}[-j]}\}{\mathbf{\Phi}}_{\mathbf{s}^{*}/\bm{l}[-j]}\mathbf{x}^{*}_{\mathbf{s}^{*}/\bm{l}[-j]}||_{2}

\displaystyle\overset{(b)}{\leq}\delta_{m+1}||\mathbf{x}^{*}_{\mathbf{s}^{*}/\bm{l}[-j]}||_{2}+\frac{\delta_{k}^{2}}{1-\delta_{k-1}}||\mathbf{x}^{*}_{\mathbf{s}^{*}/\bm{l}[-j]}||_{2}\,,

where
(a)
follows from Lemma
1
and where
(b)
holds by Lemma
2
and the Cauchy inequality. Therefore, from the monotonicity of the RIP constant we get

|{\mathbf{\Phi}}_{\bm{l}[j]}^{\mathsf{T}}{\mathbf{y}}^{\perp\bm{l}[-j]}|\leq\frac{\delta_{\ell}}{1-\delta_{\ell}}||\mathbf{x}^{*}_{\mathbf{s}^{*}/\bm{l}[-j]}||_{2}.

(16)

For the denominator of (
15
) we have

||{\mathbf{\Phi}}_{\bm{l}[j]}^{\perp\bm{l}[-j]}||_{2}^{2}=1-||P\{{\mathbf{\Phi}}_{\bm{l}[-j]}\}{\mathbf{\Phi}}_{\bm{l}[j]}||_{2}^{2},

hence by Lemma
2
we get
||{\mathbf{\Phi}}_{\bm{l}[j]}^{\perp\bm{l}[-j]}||_{2}^{2}\geq 1-\frac{\delta^{2}_{k}}{1-\delta_{k-1}}
. This inequality together with inequalities (
16
) and (
15
) complete the proof.
∎

Lemma 6
.

Let
\mathbf{s}
be a support vector of size
k
, and
e=|\mathbf{s}^{*}\backslash\mathbf{s}|
. Under setting (
1
), suppose
k+\ell>m
,
\ell\leq e
, and suppose the design matrix
{\mathbf{\Phi}}
satisfies
\delta_{k+\ell}\leq 0.5
. Let
\bm{l}\overset{\text{def}}{=}{\mathcal{L}}(\ell,\mathbf{s})
,

{\mathbf{x}}\overset{\text{def}}{=}\mathcal{E}(\mathbf{p})
, and
t\overset{\text{def}}{=}\max\{k+e,\ell+e\}
. Consider
j\in\bm{l}
such that
|\hat{\mathbf{x}}_{j}|=||\hat{\mathbf{x}}_{\bm{l}}||_{\infty}
. If
\mathbf{s}^{*}\not\subset\mathbf{p}
and
j\notin\mathbf{s}^{*}
, then

||\mathbf{x}^{*}_{\bm{l}\cap\mathbf{s}^{*}}||_{2}\leq\left(\frac{\delta_{t}(1-\delta^{2}_{t})\sqrt{2e}}{(1-\delta_{t}-\delta_{t}^{2})(1-2\delta_{t})}-1\right)||\mathbf{x}^{*}_{\mathbf{s}^{*}\backslash\mathbf{p}}||_{2}.

Proof.

From Lemma
5
we have

|\hat{\mathbf{x}}_{j}|\leq\frac{\delta_{k+\ell}}{1-\delta_{k+\ell}-\delta_{k+\ell}^{2}}||\mathbf{x}^{*}_{\mathbf{s}^{*}\backslash\mathbf{p}}||_{2}.

(17)

On the other hand, we have

\displaystyle||\hat{\mathbf{x}}_{\bm{l}}||_{\infty}

\displaystyle=||({\mathbf{\Phi}}_{\bm{l}}^{\mathsf{T}}(I-P\{{\mathbf{\Phi}}_{\mathbf{s}}\}){\mathbf{\Phi}}_{\bm{l}})^{-1}{\mathbf{\Phi}}_{\bm{l}}^{\mathsf{T}}{\mathbf{y}}^{\perp\mathbf{s}}||_{\infty}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{\sqrt{\ell}}||({\mathbf{\Phi}}_{\bm{l}}^{\mathsf{T}}(I-P\{{\mathbf{\Phi}}_{\mathbf{s}}\}){\mathbf{\Phi}}_{\bm{l}})^{-1}{\mathbf{\Phi}}_{\bm{l}}^{\mathsf{T}}{\mathbf{y}}^{\perp\mathbf{s}}||_{2}

\displaystyle\overset{(a)}{\geq}\frac{1}{\sqrt{\ell}(1+\delta_{\ell})}||{\mathbf{\Phi}}_{\bm{l}}^{\mathsf{T}}{\mathbf{y}}^{\perp\mathbf{s}}||_{2}

\displaystyle\overset{(b)}{\geq}\frac{1}{\sqrt{e}(1+\delta_{\ell})}||{\mathbf{\Phi}}_{\mathbf{s}^{*}\backslash\mathbf{s}}^{\mathsf{T}}{\mathbf{y}}^{\perp\mathbf{s}}||_{2}

\displaystyle\overset{(c)}{\geq}\frac{1}{\sqrt{e}(1+\delta_{\ell})}\big(||{\mathbf{\Phi}}_{\mathbf{s}^{*}\backslash\mathbf{s}}^{\mathsf{T}}{\mathbf{\Phi}}_{\mathbf{s}^{*}\backslash\mathbf{s}}\mathbf{x}^{*}_{\mathbf{s}^{*}\backslash\mathbf{s}}||_{2}-

\displaystyle||{\mathbf{\Phi}}_{\mathbf{s}^{*}\backslash\mathbf{s}}^{\mathsf{T}}P\{{\mathbf{\Phi}}_{\mathbf{s}}\}{\mathbf{\Phi}}_{\mathbf{s}^{*}\backslash\mathbf{s}}\mathbf{x}^{*}_{\mathbf{s}^{*}\backslash\mathbf{s}}||_{2}\big)

\displaystyle\overset{(d)}{\geq}\frac{1}{\sqrt{e}(1+\delta_{\ell})}\left((1-\delta_{e})||\mathbf{x}^{*}_{\mathbf{s}^{*}\backslash\mathbf{s}}||_{2}-\frac{\delta^{2}_{k+e}}{1-\delta_{k}}||\mathbf{x}^{*}_{\mathbf{s}^{*}\backslash\mathbf{s}}||_{2}\right)\,,

where
(a)
follows from Lemma
3
; where
(b)
is a direct result from the definition of
\bm{l}
along with the fact that
\mathbf{s}^{*}\backslash\mathbf{p}
is non-empty and
|\bm{l}|\leq e
; where
(c)
follows from definition of
{{\mathbf{y}}}^{\perp{\mathbf{s}}}
and the triangle inequality; and where
(d)
follows from the RIP definition together with Lemma
2
. Next, by combining the above inequality with (
17
) and using the monotonicity of the RIP constant, we deduce that if
j\notin\mathbf{s}^{*}
, then

\frac{\delta_{t}}{1-\delta_{t}-\delta_{t}^{2}}||\mathbf{x}^{*}_{\mathbf{s}^{*}\backslash\mathbf{p}}||_{2}\geq\frac{(1-2\delta_{t})}{\sqrt{e}(1-\delta_{t}^{2})}||\mathbf{x}^{*}_{\mathbf{s}^{*}\backslash\mathbf{s}}||_{2}\,.

This implies

||\mathbf{x}^{*}_{\mathbf{s}^{*}\backslash\mathbf{s}}||_{2}\leq\frac{\delta_{t}(1-\delta^{2}_{t})\sqrt{e}}{(1-\delta_{t}-\delta_{t}^{2})(1-2\delta_{t})}||\mathbf{x}^{*}_{\mathbf{s}^{*}\backslash\mathbf{p}}||_{2}.

(18)

Now, since
\mathbf{s}^{*}\backslash\mathbf{s}=(\bm{l}\cap\mathbf{s}^{*})\cup(\mathbf{s}^{*}\backslash\mathbf{p})

V
Concluding Remarks

We proposed LiRE, a low complexity error-correction module for sparse recovery algorithms, and provided sufficient conditions under which LiRE corrects all errors made by the baseline algorithm. Simulations show that LiRE may boost the performance of low complexity greedy algorithms to attain the performance of significantly more complex ones, as we saw in the comparison of LiRE
\circ
OMP vs. BP. Alternatively, LiRE may be used as a fast standalone support recovery algorithm that is competitive against OMP. Interesting venues for future research are in order:

•

Theorem
1
provides a sufficient condition for error-correction that appears conservative in light of the numerical experiments. For instance, Theorem
1
implies that if we want to correct up to
\bar{e}
errors (as opposed to correcting exactly
e
errors), then because of Condition (
3
) the list size should be equal to one. This, in turn, yields Corollary
2
which appears to be loose when the number of potential errors is large, linear in
m
—see Section
III-D
. We also observe that the list size used for the numerical experiments appears to be a robust choice across sparsity levels. Improving Theorem
1
by potentially relaxing Condition (
3
) is a natural direction for future investigation.

•

A very interesting problem is to quantify the successive refinement of the support estimate obtained by running LiRE multiple times.

•

LiRE keeps the list size constant throughout its iterations. However, as iterations proceed there are fewer and fewer errors to be corrected and since the list size impacts the likelihood of changing a feature, it might be possible to improve performance by considering an adaptive list size.

•

Theoretical guarantees in the noisy measurement setup would yield interesting and non-trivial extensions of the results presented in this paper.

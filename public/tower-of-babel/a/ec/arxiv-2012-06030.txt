A family of concordance homomorphisms from Khovanov homology
William Ballinger

Abstract
. By considering a version of Khovanov homology incorporating both the Lee and E ⁡ ( − 1 ) E(-1) differentials, we construct a 1 1 -parameter family of concordance homomorphisms similar to the Upsilon invariant from knot Floer homology. This invariant gives lower bounds on the slice genus and can be used to prove that certain infinite families of pretzel knots are linearly independent in the smooth concordance group.



1.
Introduction

The Upsilon invariant, introduced by Ozsváth, Stipsicz, and Szabó in
[
9
]
, is defined in terms of the knot Floer homology of a knot and contains a great deal of information about the smooth concordance group. The definition, especially as formulated by Livingston
[
7
]
, can be abstracted to avoid reference to any details of the construction of knot Floer homology, only relying on the fact that it associates a chain complex with two independent filtrations to any knot. This suggests an approach to creating new knot invariants by constructing a similar filtered complex in a different setting, which was used for example in
[
6
]
to define knot invariants using a second filtration on a version of the Khovanov-Rozansky
\operatorname{sl}(n)
homology. This paper will give a new way to define Upsilon-like invariants of knots, this time using the Lee and
E(-1)
differentials on Khovanov (
\operatorname{sl}(2)
) homology. Most of the results here should extend to the setting of
\operatorname{sl}(n)
homology, since analogues of the Lee and
E(-1)
differentials are also present there. Making this extension would require adapting the invariance proofs from
[
1
]
to the
\operatorname{sl}(n)
setting, so we will focus on the
\operatorname{sl}(2)
case for simplicity and ease of computation.

Theorem 1.1
.

There is a knot invariant
\Phi_{K}(\alpha)
for
K
a knot in
S^{3}
and
\alpha\in[0,2]
with the following properties:

•

For fixed
\alpha
,
\Phi_{K}(\alpha)
is a real-valued concordance homomorphism.

•

For fixed
K
,
\Phi_{K}(\alpha)
is a continuous piecewise linear function of
\alpha
.

•

|\Phi_{K}(\alpha)|\leq 2g_{4}(K)\Lambda(\alpha)
, where
\Lambda(\alpha)=1-|\alpha-1|
.

The above properties are all shared with
\Upsilon
, but notably there is no analogue of the symmetry
\Upsilon_{K}(t)=\Upsilon_{K}(2-t)
. Another property shared with
\Upsilon
is that the lines forming the graph of
\Phi_{K}
are constrained by the Khovanov homology of
K
:

Theorem 1.2
.

Suppose
L
is a line meeting the graph of
\Phi_{K}
in a segment, and let
\ell_{0}
and
\ell_{1}
be the second coordinates of the intersections of
L
with the vertical lines
\alpha=0
and
\alpha=1
, respectively. Then the reduced Khovanov homology of
K
is nonzero in homological grading
\ell_{0}/2
and delta grading
\ell_{1}
. In particular,
\ell_{0}
and
\ell_{1}
are even integers.

For some simple knots
K
, the function
\Phi_{K}
can be computed by directly examining the Khovanov homology. In particular, this can be done for the three stranded pretzel knots based on computations of their reduced Khovanov homology due to Manion
[
8
]
, leading to conclusions about their place in the smooth concordance group.

Theorem 1.3
.

For any two sequences
a_{k},b_{k}
of odd integers with
2k<a_{k}<b_{k}
, the sequence of knots
\{P(-2k,a_{k},b_{k})\}_{k=1}^{\infty}
are a basis for a
\mathbb{Z}^{\infty}
summand of the smooth concordance group.

None of the knots appearing in this theorem are algebraically slice, and it seems likely that this theorem, and even stronger statements, could be proven using other concordance invariants such as the Tristam-Levine signature. However, this would require a detailed analysis of the roots of the Alexander polynomials of these knots while, once the basic properties of the invariant are set up and the Khovanov homology of these knots is known, finding their
\Phi
invariants is a fairly simple calculation.

The invariant
\Phi_{K}
can also be calculated for small torus knots using their known Khovanov homologies (taken from
[
2
]
). Despite the complexity of these homology groups, a relatively simple pattern emerges which leads to Conjecture
6.3
on the values of
\Phi_{T_{p,q}}
for any
p,q
.

2.
A bifiltered complex from a knot

In
[
1
]
, for every knot
K
a
\mathbb{Z}
-graded,
\mathbb{Z}
-filtered chain complex over
\mathbb{Z}[x]

C(K)
was defined. In this complex, the differential lowers the grading by
3
and multiplication by
x
lowers it by
2
. The following properties of
C(K)
, proven in
[
1
]
, will be relavant here:

Proposition 2.1
.

Forgetting the filtration, the homology of
C(K)
is isomorphic to
\mathbb{Z}[x]
, and the
E_{2}
page of the spectral sequence coming from
C(K)
is the
\mathcal{F}_{3}
Khovanov homology of
K
as defined in
[
5
]
. If
K
and
K^{\prime}
are related by a cobordism of genus
g
, there is a filtered chain map
C(K)\to C(K^{\prime})
whose induced map on homology is multiplication by
(2x)^{g}
, and if
K_{-}
and
K_{+}
are related by a crossing change in which
K_{-}
has a negative crossing and
K_{+}
a positive crossing, there is a filtered chain map
C(K_{-})\to C(K_{+})
inducing an isomorphism on homology.

The above identification between the
E_{2}
page and the Khovanov homology relates the filtration on
C(K)
to the homological grading, and the grading on
C(K)
to the linear combination
q-3h
of the quantum and homological grading. We will call
q-3h
the internal grading on Khovanov homology, just as in
[
1
]
.

This paper will be concerned with the quotient of
C(K)
setting the variable
x
to
1
. To make the resulting invariants better behaved, we will also change base to
\mathbb{Q}
, although any field of characteristic not
2
would work just as well and produce a different invariant. The result is a bifiltered chain complex incorporating both the Lee (or
d_{1}
) and
d_{-1}
differentials into Khovanov homology.

Definition 2.2
.

For a knot
K
, let
\operatorname{CKh}^{\pm}(K)
be the chain complex
(C(K)\otimes\mathbb{Q})/(x-1)
, and for integers
i,j
let
\operatorname{CKh}^{\pm}(K)_{i,j}(K)
be the subgroup of
\operatorname{CKh}^{\pm}(K)
generated by the images of elements of
C(K)
in grading at least
i
and filtration level at least
j
.

Since
x
has degree
2
, in addition to the two filtrations
\operatorname{CKh}^{\pm}(K)
inherits a
\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}
grading from the grading on
C(K)
. This will not be needed for establishing the formal properties of the invariant, but can be useful in computation. This complex has the following elementary properties, immediately from Proposition
2.1
:

Proposition 2.3
.

The differential on
\operatorname{CKh}^{\pm}(K)
carries
\operatorname{CKh}^{\pm}(K)_{i,j}(K)
into
\operatorname{CKh}^{\pm}(K)_{i-3,j}(K)
, and the homology of
\operatorname{CKh}^{\pm}(K)
with respect to this differential is isomorphic to
\mathbb{Q}
. If
K
and
K^{\prime}
are related by a cobordism of genus
g
, then there is a chain map
\operatorname{CKh}^{\pm}(K)\to\operatorname{CKh}^{\pm}(K^{\prime})
that sends
\operatorname{CKh}^{\pm}_{i,j}(K)
into
\operatorname{CKh}^{\pm}_{i-2g,j}(K^{\prime})
and induces an isomorphism on homology. If
K_{-}
and
K_{+}
are related by a crossing change in which
K_{-}
has a negative crossing and
K_{+}
a positive crossing, there is a filtered chain map
\operatorname{CKh}^{\pm}(K_{-})\to\operatorname{CKh}^{\pm}(K_{+})
inducing an isomorphism on homology.

Since the differential on
\operatorname{CKh}^{\pm}(K)
lowers the first of the filtrations, this isn’t quite a filtered chain complex in the usual sense. However, most of what one might like to do to a filtered chain complex can still be done here. In particular, collapsing the two filtrations to one along a one-parameter family of directions produces a family of concordance homomorphisms
\Phi_{K}(\alpha)
for
\alpha\in[0,2]
very similar to the
\Upsilon
invariant from Heegaard Floer homology.

Theorem 2.4
.

For each
\alpha\in[0,2]
,
\Phi_{K}(\alpha)
is a real valued concordance homomorphism, and
\Phi_{K}
is a piecewise linear function of
K
. At the endpoints,
\Phi_{K}(0)=\Phi_{K}(2)=0
, and the slopes of
\Phi_{K}
at
0
and
2
are
s(K)
and
-t(K)
, respectively.

Just like
\Upsilon
, the invariant
\Phi
gives lower bounds on the slice genus of
K
.

Theorem 2.5
.

|\Phi(K,\alpha)|\leq 2g_{4}(K)\min(\alpha,2-\alpha)

Note that, although the slice genus bound from
\Upsilon
has the same shape as this one, in the case of
\Upsilon
the symmetry
\Upsilon_{K}(t)=\Upsilon_{K}(2-t)
means that all of the information is contained in the bound over the interval
[0,1]
. In the case of
\Phi
, however, it is possible that the bound on the interval
[1,2]
has additional information, although this does not occur in any known example.

3.
Preliminaries on homological algebra

Definition 3.1
.

A semibifiltered complex is a chain complex
(C,d)
over a field together with an increasing
\mathbb{Z}\oplus\mathbb{Z}
-filtration
C_{i,j}
of
C
, with no assumption on how the differential
d
interacts with the complex. A semibifiltered complex is
(m,n)
-bounded if
d(C_{i,j})\subset C_{i+m,j+n}
.

In general, we will consider filtered chain maps between semibifiltered complexes, but not require that homotopies between these maps be filtered. In particular, a homotopy equivalence of semibifiltered complexes
C,C^{\prime}
consists of filtered chain maps
C\to C^{\prime}
and
C^{\prime}\to C
, such that both composite maps are chain homotopic to the identity via not-necessarily-filtered homotopies. With this definition, the homotopy equivalence class of
\operatorname{CKh}^{\pm}(K)
as a semibifiltered complex is an invariant of the concordance class of
K
.

From an
(m,n)
-bounded semibifiltered complex
(C,d)
, one can construct associated graded complexes with respect to both filtrations, producing a
\mathbb{Z}\oplus\mathbb{Z}
-graded chain complex whose differential has degree
(m,n)
. Similarly, one can take the associated graded complex with respect to only the second filtration, producing a
\mathbb{Z}
-graded,
\mathbb{Z}
filtered chain complex whose differential has degree
n
with respect to the grading and changes the filtration by at least
m
. Call the first of these constructions the associated graded complex, and the second the vertical associated graded complex. Note that these concepts depend on the choice of
(m,n)
- If
C
is
(m,n)
-bounded, then it is also
(m^{\prime},n^{\prime})
-bounded for any
m^{\prime}\leq m
and
n^{\prime}\leq n
, but treating
C
as an
(m^{\prime},n^{\prime})
-bounded complex and taking the associated graded generally results in a complex with vanishing differential.

A straightforward homological perturbation argument proves the following:

Lemma 3.2
.

Any
(m,n)
-bounded semibifiltered complex
(C,d)
is homotopy equivalent to an
(m,n)
-bounded semibifiltered complex
(C^{\prime},d)
such that the associated graded module of
C^{\prime}
is isomorphic to the homology of the associated graded complex of
(C,d)
. Furthermore, the vertical associated graded complex of
(C^{\prime},d)
depends only on the vertical associated graded complex of
(C,d)
.

The complex
\operatorname{CKh}^{\pm}(K)
is
(-3,0)
-bounded, and the homology of its associated graded complex is precisely the underlying vector space of the rational Khovanov chain complex, as originally defined. This lemma can therefore produce a model of
\operatorname{CKh}^{\pm}(K)
with underlying vector space equal to the Khovanov chain complex. This is finite dimensional and already much more convinient for computations, but an even further simplification is possible:

Lemma 3.3
.

The complex produced from
\operatorname{CKh}^{\pm}(K)
via Lemma
3.2
is in fact
(-3,1)
-bounded, and its associated graded complex as a
(-3,1)
-bounded semibifiltered complex is precisely the Khovanov complex of
K
.

Proof.

To say that a
(-3,0)
-bounded complex is
(-3,1)
-bounded is exactly to say that the differential of it’s vertical associated graded complex vanishes. By the second part of Lemma
3.2
, this can be checked looking only at the vertical associated graded complex of
\operatorname{CKh}^{\pm}(K)
, which (directly from the definition in
[
1
]
) is precisely a direct sums of shifts of copies of
\operatorname{CKh}^{\pm}(U)
where
U
is an unlink with a varying number of components. The homology of such a complex is the reduced Lee homology of this unlink, and the homology of it’s associated graded complex is the reduced Khovanov homology of this unlink, so since these have the same rank all higher vertical differentials vanish. Finally, the differential of the associated graded complex of this
(-3,-1)
-bounded complex is precisely the differential on the
E_{1}
-page of the spectral sequence considered in
[
1
]
, where it is shown that this is the differential of the Khovanov complex.
∎

Therefore, applying Lemma
3.2
once more proves the following, which enables most of the computations to follow:

Lemma 3.4
.

\operatorname{CKh}^{\pm}(K)
is chain homotopy equivalent to a
(-3,1)
-bounded semibifiltered complex
(C,d)
such that the associated graded module of the filtration on
C
is isomorphic to the Khovanov homology
\operatorname{Kh}(K)
, with its internal and homological gradings.

Figure 1.
The allowed differentials in the model for
\operatorname{CKh}^{\pm}(K)
provided by Lemma
3.4
. The complex
\operatorname{CKh}^{\pm}(K)
has a model whose underlying vector space is the reduced Khovanov homology of
K
with rational coefficients, and the differential of an element in the grading labelled
1
in the diagram lies in the gradings marked by dots in the shaded regions. This diagram takes into account the fact that the differential must increase the homological grading
h
by at least
1
, decrease the internal grading
q-3h
by at most
3
, and change the parity of the internal grading, as well as the fact that no differential of degree
h
will be present due to the last application of Lemma
3.2
passing from the Khovanov complex to the Khovanov homology. The terms of the differential in the red region will be exactly the Lee differential of
K
, and similarly the terms in the blue region will be the
E(-1)
differential of
K
.

4.
Invariants of filtered complexes

Let
C
be a decreasing
\mathbb{R}
-filtered vector space with filtered pieces
\{C_{t}\}_{t\in\mathbb{R}}
and
d
a differential on
C
, with no assumption on how
d
interacts with the filtration. If the homology of
(C,d)
is one dimensional, define
m(C)
to be the supremum of all
t
for which there is an element
\gamma
in
C_{t}
with
d\gamma=0
and
[\gamma]
nonzero in homology.

Lemma 4.1
.

For
C
,
C^{\prime}
as above,
m(C\otimes C^{\prime})=m(C)+m(C^{\prime})
.

Proof.

For
t\in\mathbb{R}
, define
C_{>t}
to be the union of
C_{s}
for all
s>t
. Since the homology of
C
is one-dimensional, any two chains representing nonzero homology classes have homologous scalar multiples. Therefore, the quantity
m(C)
is characterized by the fact that, for all
t<m(C)
, there is
\gamma\in C_{t}
with
d\gamma=0
and
\gamma\not\in d(C)+C_{>m(C)}
. Fix
t<m(C)
and
t^{\prime}<m(C^{\prime})
, and elements
\gamma
and
\gamma^{\prime}
with this property. Then
\gamma\otimes\gamma^{\prime}\in C_{t}\otimes C_{t^{\prime}}\subset(C\otimes C^{\prime})_{t+t^{\prime}}
, and is not contained in the subspace

d(C)\otimes C^{\prime}+C_{>m(C)}\otimes C^{\prime}+C\otimes d(C^{\prime})+C\otimes C^{\prime}_{>m(C^{\prime})}

Now,
d(C\otimes C^{\prime})
is a subspace of
d(C)+d(C^{\prime})
, and
C_{>m(C)}\otimes C^{\prime}+C\otimes C^{\prime}_{>m(C^{\prime})}
is a subspace of
(C\otimes C^{\prime})_{>m(C)+m(C^{\prime})}
, so
\gamma\otimes\gamma^{\prime}
is not contained in
d(C\otimes C^{\prime})+(C\otimes C^{\prime})_{>m(C)+m(C^{\prime})}
, so taking
t
and
t^{\prime}
arbitrarily close to
m(C)
and
m(C^{\prime})
, respectively, finishes the proof.
∎

If now
(C,d)
is a semibifiltered complex with one-dimensional homology, then for any
\alpha\in[0,2]
we can collapse the filtration to one
\mathbb{R}
-filtration and apply the above construction: let

C^{\alpha}_{t}=\sum_{\alpha m+(2-\alpha)n\geq t}C_{m,n},

and let
M(C,\alpha)=m(C^{\alpha})
. Then by the additivity of
m
,
M(C\otimes C^{\prime},\alpha)=M(C,\alpha)+M(C^{\prime},\alpha)
.

The complex
\operatorname{CKh}^{\pm}(K)
is semibifiltered with one-dimensional homology, so the above definition applies:

Definition 4.2
.

For a knot
K
and
\alpha\in[0,2]
, let
\Phi_{K}(\alpha)=M(\operatorname{CKh}^{\pm}(K),\alpha)
.

Applying this construction to an appropriate version of knot Floer homology produces the Upsilon invariant. Via arguments similar to Theorem 8.1 in
[
7
]
, analogues of the integrality properties of Upsilon can be proven in this more general context.

Proposition 4.3
.

Suppose that
(C,d)
is a semibifiltered complex with
C
finite dimensional. Then the function
M(C,-)
is continuous and piecewise-linear, and it’s graph is contained in the union of the lines passing through
(0,2j)
and
(2,2i)
as
(i,j)
ranges over the support of the associated graded module to
C
.

Proof.

For fixed
\alpha
, the quantity
M(C,\alpha)
must lie in the support of the associated graded module to the collapsed filtration
C^{\alpha}
since otherwise any chain lying in filtration level
M(C,\alpha)
would in fact lie in a deeper level. As
\alpha
varies, the support of this associated module traces out the lines in the statement, so it remains only to prove that
M(C,-)
is a continuous function of
\alpha
. For a fixed chain
\gamma\in C
, let
M(\gamma,\alpha)
be the largest
t
for which
\gamma
is contained in
C^{\alpha}_{t}
. This will be the minumum of several linear functions corresponding to the support of
\gamma
, so will be continuous and piecewise linear. The quantity
M(C,\alpha)
is the maximum of
M(\gamma,\alpha)
over all
\gamma
representing a nonzero homology class. Since each
M(\gamma,\alpha)
is contained in the lines corresponding to the support of
C
, of which there are finitely many, there are only finitely many possible values for
M(\gamma,\alpha)
. Therefore,
M(C,\alpha)
is the maximum of finitely many continuous functions, so is itself continuous.
∎

Combining this with Lemma
3.4
immediately proves Theorem
1.2
.

The quantity
M(C,\alpha)
behaves in a predictable way under chain maps, which will be important to establish the basic properties of
\Phi_{K}
.

Lemma 4.4
.

If
C
and
C^{\prime}
are semibifiltered complexes and there is a filtered chain map
f:C\to C^{\prime}
inducing an isomorphism on homology, then
M(C,\alpha)\leq M(C^{\prime},\alpha)
.

Proof.

For any
t<M(C,\alpha)
, there is a chain
\gamma\in C^{\alpha}_{t}
representing a nonzero homology class. Then
f(\gamma)\in(C^{\prime})^{\alpha}_{t}
, so
t\leq M(C^{\prime},\alpha)
. By taking
t
approaching
M(C,\alpha)
, then
M(C,\alpha)\leq M(C^{\prime},\alpha)
.
∎

4.1.
Basic Properties of
\Phi_{K}(\alpha)

This section contains proofs of most of the theorems stated in the introduction, as well as a few additional facts needed for later computations.

Proposition 4.5
.

\Phi
is a concordance homomorphism.

Proof.

The complex
C(K)
from
[
1
]
satisfies
C(K_{1}\#K_{2})=C(K_{1})\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}[x]}C(K_{2})
, so
\operatorname{CKh}^{\pm}(K_{1}\#K_{2})\cong\operatorname{CKh}^{\pm}(K_{1})\otimes_{\mathbb{Q}}\operatorname{CKh}^{\pm}(K_{2})
. Additivity of
\Phi
now follows from Lemma
4.1
. To see that
\Phi
is a concordance invariant, use Lemma
4.4
and the fact that the complexes
\operatorname{CKh}^{\pm}(K)
for concordant knots admit filtered chain maps inducing isomorphisms on homology in either direction by Proposition
2.3
.
∎

Proposition 4.6
.

For any knot
K
, the values
\Phi_{K}(0)
and
\Phi_{K}(2)
are both
0
, and the slopes
\Phi_{K}^{\prime}(0)
and
\Phi_{K}^{\prime}(2)
are
s(K)
and
-t(K)
, respectively.

Proof.

In the model for
\operatorname{CKh}^{\pm}(K)
provided by Lemma
3.4
, the vertical associated graded complex of
\operatorname{CKh}^{\pm}(K)
is precisely the reduced Khovanov homology of
K
together with all differentials of the Lee spectral sequence, so the homology of this vertical associated graded complex is
\mathbb{Q}
in homological degree
0
and
0
in all other homological degrees, and with respect to the filtration by the internal degree there is a generator for the homology contained in filtration level
s(K)
but not in any deeper level of the filtration. Let
\gamma_{0}\in\operatorname{CKh}^{\pm}(K)_{s(K),0}
be any lift of a chain generating the homology of the verical associated graded complex. Since
\gamma
is a chain in the vertical associated graded,
d\gamma_{0}\in\operatorname{CKh}^{\pm}(K)_{*,2}
, and since the homology of the vertical associated graded in degree
2
vanishes, there is a
\gamma_{1}\in\operatorname{CKh}^{\pm}(K)_{*,1}
with
d\gamma_{0}+d\gamma_{1}\in\operatorname{CKh}^{\pm}(K)_{*,3}
. Continuing in this way to push remaining terms depper into the filtration, and using the fact that this model of
\operatorname{CKh}^{\pm}(K)
is finite dimensional, we produce a chain
\gamma\in\operatorname{CKh}^{\pm}(K)_{s(K),0}+\operatorname{CKh}^{\pm}(K)_{*,1}
generating the homology of
\operatorname{CKh}^{\pm}(K)
which, again using finite dimensionality, in fact lies in
\operatorname{CKh}^{\pm}(K)_{s(K),0}+\operatorname{CKh}^{\pm}(K)_{m,1}
for some
m
. Therefore,
\Phi_{K}(\alpha)\geq s(K)\alpha
for small enough
\alpha
, and the reverse inequality follows from applying the same argument to
-K
. Similarly, the horizontal associated graded complex of
\operatorname{CKh}^{\pm}(K)
(taking associated graded with respect to just the first filtration) is exactly the reduced complex
\bar{C}(K)
from
[
1
]
, since taking associated graded changes the relation
x=1
to
x=0
, so applying this same argument proves the statement about the right endpoint slope.
∎

The version of the Lee spectral sequence from the above proof is not exactly the same as the one used by Rasmussen
[
10
]
to define the
s
invariant, but is instead a reduced version of the Lee spectral sequence. The version used in
[
10
]
can be instead obtained by replacing the relation
x-1
by
x^{2}-1
and shifting all quantum gradings up by
1
(and again taking the vertical associated graded to remove
E(-1)
differentials). The unreduced complex has two dimensional homology with generators in filtration levels
s(K)-1
and
s(K)+1
, and the necessary generator of the reduced homology in filtration level
s(K)
just comes from further quotienting the unreduced complex by
x-1
to get the reduced complex, and taking the image of the generator in filtration level
s(K)-1
. This will land in filtration level
s(K)
in the reduced complex, due to the grading shift.

Proposition 4.7
.

If
K_{-}
and
K_{+}
differ by a crossing change in which
K_{-}
has a negative crossing and
K_{+}
has a positive crossing, then
\Phi_{K_{-}}(\alpha)\leq\Phi_{K_{+}}(\alpha)
for any
\alpha\in[0,2]
.

Proof.

By Proposition
2.3
, in this situation there is a filtered chain map
\operatorname{CKh}^{\pm}(K_{-})\to\operatorname{CKh}^{\pm}(K_{+})
that induces an isomorphism on homology. The inequality then follows from Lemma
4.4

∎

5.
Twisting ribbon surfaces and the slice genus bound

Arguments similar to those of the previous section suffice to prove a slice genus bound
g_{4}(K)\geq\Phi_{K}(\alpha)/\alpha
, by considering the cobordism maps on
C(K)
and the filtered maps they induce on
\operatorname{CKh}^{\pm}(K)
. This is identical to the bound stated in Theorem
2.5
when
0\leq\alpha\leq 1
, but is significantly weaker when
1\leq\alpha\leq 2
. To prove the stronger bound, more complex arguments are needed that involve modifying the knot to simplify it’s Khovanov homology and using Proposition
4.7
to control the change in
\Phi
.

Let
\Sigma\subset S^{3}
be a ribbon surface of genus
g
, with a handle decomposition consisting of
k
discs and
k+2g-1
disjoint bands. Via slides among the bands, we may assume that the ribbon graph formed by these discs and bands has the same combinatorial type as the one visible in Figure
2
. For
n\geq 0
, let
\Sigma_{n}
be the surface obtained from
\Sigma
by inserting
n
full positive twists into each band of
\Sigma
, and let
K_{n}=\partial\Sigma_{n}
.

Proposition 5.1
.

For fixed
\alpha

Corollary 5.3
.

For any
\epsilon>0
, there is an

for which, whenever

\Phi_{K_{n}}(\alpha)
is equal to
-2g\Lambda(\alpha)
.

Proof.

By the previous Lemma, there is a constant
M
for which the reduced Khovanov homologies of the knots
K_{n}
are supported in delta gradings between
-M
and
M
and, by taking
n

-2g
in any finite range of homological gradings.

By Theorem
1.2
, then, the graphs of
\Phi_{K_{n}}
for sufficiently large

are contained in the a union of lines that all either pass through
(1,-2g)
or meet the line
\alpha=1
between
-M
and

and have slope as large as desired. A sufficiently steep line meeting
\alpha=1
between
-M
and

cannot pass through

or

, so since
\Phi_{K_{n}}(0)=\Phi_{K_{n}}(2)=0

Pretzel calculations and a conjecture on torus knots

Proposition 6.1
.

If
K
is the pretzel knot
P(-2n,a,b)
with
a
and
b
odd and
0<2n<a,b
, then the only singularities of
\Phi_{K}(\alpha)
are at
\alpha=1
and
\alpha=\frac{8n}{8n+2}
. The change in the slope of
\Phi_{K}(\alpha)
at
\alpha=\frac{8n}{8n+2}
is exactly

\Delta\Phi^{\prime}_{K}\left(\frac{8n}{8n+2}\right)=-8n-2

Proof.

By Manion’s computations of the reduced Khovanov homology of pretzel knots
[
8
]
, the reduced homology of
K
is supported in delta gradings
a+b
and
a+b-2
(note that here we use the convention
\delta=q-2h
, which differs from Manion’s by a factor of
2
). In the higher delta grading the homology is supported between homological gradings
0
and
4n+1
, and in the lower delta grading the homology is supported in homological gradings at least
4n
. Furthermore, the homology group in delta grading
a+b
and homological grading
4n+1
has rank
1
, as does the homology group in delta grading
a+b-2
and homological grading
4n
. Using the model provided by Lemma
3.4
, this is enough to fully compute
\operatorname{CKh}^{\pm}(K)
. For each of the Lee and
E(-1)
differentials, there is only one possibility consistent with the facts that the overall homology is one dimensional and the surviving generator is in homological or internal grading
0
. There is no room in the reduced Khovanov homology of
K
for any differentials going deeper into both filtrations, and only one way up to isomorphism of the resulting complex to match the generators of the Lee and
E(-1)
complexes to make
d^{2}=0
, so the differential on
\operatorname{CKh}^{\pm}(K)
is just the sum of the Lee and
E(-1)
differentials. Overall, the complex
\operatorname{CKh}^{\pm}(K)
is shown in figure
4
; it is the direct sum of one main staircase complex with copies of shifts of the boxed contractible complex. The boxed complex is homotopy equivalent to
0
, so in fact
\operatorname{CKh}^{\pm}(K)
is homotopy equivalent to the main complex
C
in Figure
4
. This means that
\Phi_{K}(\alpha)=M(C,\alpha)
, which can be computed to be

\Phi_{K}(\alpha)=\begin{cases}(a+b)\alpha&0\leq\alpha\leq\frac{8n}{8n+2}\\
8n+(a+b-8n-2)\alpha&\frac{8n}{8n+2}\leq\alpha\leq 1\\
(a+b-2)(2-\alpha)&1\leq\alpha\leq 2\end{cases}

∎

Lemma 6.2
.

For any knot
K
, the quantity
\Delta\Phi^{\prime}_{K}(\alpha)(1-\alpha)
is always an even integer.

Proof.

By Theorem
1.2
, this is a difference between two delta gradings in which the reduced Khovanov homology of

is supported. The parities of the delta and quantum gradings are equal, and the reduced Khovanov homology of a knot is supported in even quantum gradings.
∎

an integer, let

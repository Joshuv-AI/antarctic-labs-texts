Introduction

The pioneer chemotaxis model was established by Keller and Segel in 1970’s
[
4
]
, to describe cells’ motion which biased by a chemical substance secreted by the cells themselves.

Recently, phenotype heterogeneity is assumed among the cells population, they are divided into two groups; one group secrets chemical signal, the other group is attracted by the signal. These abilities do not hold simultaneously, some kind of trade-off is observed between two groups. This model can be seen as an extension of the classical Keller-Segel model, with
u
and
w
presenting the density of “chemotaxis” and “secreting” groups, respectively, and
v
is the concentration of chemical substance. Under these assumptions, the following system is proposed
[
6
]
:

\left\{\begin{array}[]{lllll}&u_{t}=\Delta u-\nabla\cdot(u\nabla v)-k_{1}u+k_{2}w,&(x,t)\in\Omega\times(0,T),\\[2.0pt]
&v_{t}=\Delta v-v+w,&(x,t)\in\Omega\times(0,T),\\[2.0pt]
&w_{t}=\Delta w-w+f(u),&(x,t)\in\Omega\times(0,T),\\[2.0pt]
&\partial_{\nu}u=\partial_{\nu}v=\partial_{\nu}w=0,&(x,t)\in\par\Omega\times(0,T),\\[2.0pt]
&u(x,0)=u_{0}(x),v(x,0)=v_{0}(x),w(x,0)=w_{0}(x),&x\in\Omega,\end{array}\right.

(1.1)

where
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
(
n\geq 1
) is smooth and bounded,
T>0
,
\nu
denotes the outer normal vector on
\partial\Omega
,
k_{1},k_{2}\geq 0
, and

\displaystyle f\in C^{1}([0,\infty))\text{ and }0\leq f(s)\leq s^{\alpha}\text{ with some }{\alpha>0},

(1.2)

\displaystyle u_{0}\in C^{0}(\overline{\Omega}),v_{0}\in W^{2,\infty}(\Omega),w_{0}\in W^{2,\infty}(\Omega)\text{ are nonnegative}.

(1.3)

Compared to the classical Keller-Segel system, the indirect signal production mechanism in (
1.1
)
manifests a stronger tendency toward stabilization; it is proven in
[
2
,
3
]
that in the case
f(u)\equiv u
, the solution of (
1.1
) is bounded for
n\leq 3
, which is in contrast to the classical Keller-Segel system that there exist finite time blowup solutions in two and three dimensions
[
8
]
.
Considering
direct
nonlinear signal production model:

u_{t}=\Delta u-\nabla\cdot(u\nabla v),\quad v_{t}=\Delta v-v+f(u),

(1.4)

where

satisfies (
1.2
), the value
\alpha
determines boundedness or blow up of the solution; if

, the solution is global and bounded
[
5
]
; if

with

and the second equation is replaced by

9
]

In the present work, we study
indirect
nonlinear signal production mechanism in (

), and obtain the following:

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
n\leq 3
,
k_{1}=k_{2}=0
,
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be bounded domain with smooth boundary,

Noting that the condition on

will allow superlinear transmission in the third equation, no integrable regularity is known for the production term

Lemma 2.4
.

Let

,

\displaystyle~~\int_{0}^{t}e^{-\delta(t-s)}\left(\int_{0}^{s}e^{-(s-\sigma)}g(\sigma)d\sigma\right)^{q}ds\leq\frac{1}{1-\delta}\int_{0}^{t}e^{-\delta(t-s)}g^{q}(s)ds\text{ for all }t\in(0,T).

First we apply Hölder’s inequality to find that

\displaystyle\left(\int_{0}^{s}e^{-(s-\sigma)}g(\sigma)d\sigma\right)^{q}\leq\left(\int_{0}^{s}e^{-(s-\sigma)}g^{q}(\sigma)d\sigma\right)\left(\int_{0}^{s}e^{-(s-\sigma)}d\sigma\right)^{q(1-\frac{1}{q})}\leq\int_{0}^{s}e^{-(s-\sigma)}g^{q}(\sigma)d\sigma.

\displaystyle~~~~\int_{0}^{t}e^{-\delta(t-s)}\left(\int_{0}^{s}e^{-(s-\sigma)}g(\sigma)d\sigma\right)^{q}ds

\displaystyle\leq\int_{0}^{t}e^{-\delta(t-s)}\int_{0}^{s}e^{-(s-\sigma)}g^{q}(\sigma)d\sigma ds=\int_{0}^{t}e^{-\delta t+\sigma}g^{q}(\sigma)\int_{\sigma}^{t}e^{-(1-\delta)s}dsd\sigma\leq\frac{1}{1-\delta}\int_{0}^{t}e^{-\delta(t-\sigma)}g^{q}(\sigma)d\sigma.

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}e^{-\delta(t-s)}\|u^{\alpha}(\cdot,s)\|_{L^{1}(\Omega)}^{\frac{2}{2-\lambda}(1-b)\beta^{\prime}}ds\leq c_{2}\left(\int_{0}^{t}e^{-\delta(t-s)}\|\nabla u^{\frac{p}{2}}(\cdot,s)\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}ds\right)^{\beta^{\prime}\eta_{2}}+c_{2},

and

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}e^{-\delta(t-s)}\|u^{\alpha}(\cdot,s)\|_{L^{1}(\Omega)}^{\frac{2}{2-\lambda}}{ds}\leq c_{3}\left(\int_{0}^{t}e^{-\delta(t-s)}\|\nabla u^{\frac{p}{2}}(\cdot,s)\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}ds\right)^{\eta_{3}}+c_{3}.

Applying Lemmata
2.3
and
3.3
, we obtain
c_{4},c_{5}>0
such that

\displaystyle~~~~\int_{0}^{t}e^{-\delta(t-s)}\|\Delta v(\cdot,s)\|_{L^{\theta^{\prime}}(\Omega)}^{\frac{2}{2-\lambda}}ds

\displaystyle\leq c_{4}\int_{0}^{t}e^{-\delta(t-s)}\|w(\cdot,s)\|_{L^{\theta^{\prime}}(\Omega)}^{\frac{2}{2-\lambda}}ds+c_{4}\|v_{0}\|_{W^{2,\infty}(\Omega)}^{\frac{2}{2-\lambda}}

\displaystyle\leq c_{5}\left(\int_{0}^{t}e^{-\delta(t-s)}\|\nabla u^{\frac{p}{2}}(\cdot,s)\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}ds\right)^{\eta_{1}+\eta_{2}}+c_{5}\left(\int_{0}^{t}e^{-\delta(t-s)}\|\nabla u^{\frac{p}{2}}(\cdot,s)\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}ds\right)^{\eta_{1}}

\displaystyle\qquad+c_{5}\left(\int_{0}^{t}e^{-\delta(t-s)}\|\nabla u^{\frac{p}{2}}(\cdot,s)\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}ds\right)^{\eta_{2}}+c_{5}\left(\int_{0}^{t}e^{-\delta(t-s)}\|\nabla u^{\frac{p}{2}}(\cdot,s)\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}ds\right)^{\eta_{3}}+c_{5}.

(3.25)

Since
0<\eta_{1}+\eta_{2}<1
and
0<\eta_{i}<1
for
i=1,2,3
have been confirmed in Lemma
3.1
, we conclude the assertion by applying Young’s inequality.
∎

3.4
together with an
L^{p}

Lemma 3.5
.

Let
n\leq 3
. Assume that
1<\alpha<\min\{\frac{4}{n},1+\frac{2}{n}\}
. For any

p>\max\left\{1,\frac{\alpha(n-2)_{+}}{n},\frac{\alpha\tau(n-2)_{+}}{n},\frac{\frac{2}{n}-\frac{1}{\theta^{\prime}}}{1-b}+1-\frac{2}{n}\right\},

there is
C>0
such that

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}u^{p}(\cdot,t)\leq C\qquad\mbox{for all }t\in(0,T_{max}).

(3.26)

Proof.

We test the first equation in (
1.1
) with
pu^{p-1}
, integrate by parts,

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\int_{\Omega}u^{p}+\frac{4(p-1)}{p}\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u^{\frac{p}{2}}|^{2}=-(p-1)\int_{\Omega}u^{p}\Delta v\leq(p-1)\left(\int_{\Omega}u^{p\theta}\right)^{\frac{1}{\theta}}\|\Delta v\|_{L^{\theta^{\prime}}(\Omega)}\qquad\mbox{for all }t\in(0,T_{max}).

(3.27)

The Gagliardo-Nirenberg inequality
yields the existence of
c_{1}>0
such that

\displaystyle(p-1)\left(\int_{\Omega}u^{p\theta}\right)^{\frac{1}{\theta}}

\displaystyle=(p-1)\|u^{\frac{p}{2}}\|_{L^{2\theta}(\Omega)}^{2}\leq c_{1}\|\nabla u^{\frac{p}{2}}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2a}\|u^{\frac{p}{2}}\|_{L^{\frac{2}{p}}(\Omega)}^{2(1-a)}+c_{1}\|u^{\frac{p}{2}}\|_{L^{\frac{2}{p}}(\Omega)}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq c_{2}\|\nabla u^{\frac{p}{2}}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{\lambda}+c_{2}.

Therefore, Young’s inequality implies existence of
c_{3}>0
fulfilling

\displaystyle(p-1)\left(\int_{\Omega}u^{p\theta}\right)^{\frac{1}{\theta}}\|\Delta v\|_{L^{\theta^{\prime}}(\Omega)}\leq\frac{(p-1)}{p}\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u^{\frac{p}{2}}|^{2}+c_{3}\|\Delta v\|_{L^{\theta^{\prime}}(\Omega)}^{\frac{2}{2-\lambda}}+c_{3}.

(3.28)

Fixing
\delta\in(0,\min\{1,\frac{2}{2-\lambda},\frac{2}{2-\lambda}b\beta\})
,
we derive from the Gagliardo-Nirenberg inequality to see that

\displaystyle\delta\int_{\Omega}u^{p}\leq\frac{(p-1)}{p}\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u^{\frac{p}{2}}|^{2}+c_{4}.

(3.29)

From (
3.27
)-(
3.29
), we obtain that

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\int_{\Omega}u^{p}+\delta\int_{\Omega}u^{p}+\frac{2(p-1)}{p}\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u^{\frac{p}{2}}|^{2}\leq c_{3}\|\Delta v\|_{L^{\theta^{\prime}}(\Omega)}^{\frac{2}{2-\lambda}}+c_{4}\qquad\mbox{for all }t\in(0,T_{max}).

(3.30)

By applying variation of constants formula and Lemma
2.3
with
\varepsilon=\frac{p-1}{p}>0
, we derive that

\displaystyle~~~~\int_{\Omega}u^{p}(\cdot,t)+\frac{2(p-1)}{p}\int_{0}^{t}e^{-\delta(t-s)}\|\nabla u^{\frac{p}{2}}(\cdot,s)\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}ds

\displaystyle\leq c_{3}\int_{0}^{t}e^{-\delta(t-s)}\|\Delta v(\cdot,s)\|_{L^{\theta^{\prime}}(\Omega)}^{\frac{2}{2-\lambda}}ds+\frac{c_{4}}{\delta}+e^{-\delta t}\int_{\Omega}u_{0}^{p}\qquad\mbox{for all }t\in(0,T_{max}).

Employing Lemma
3.4
with
\varepsilon=\frac{p-1}{p}\cdot\frac{1}{c_{3}}>0
leads to the assertion.
∎

Proof of Theorem
1.1
.

According to Lemma
3.5
and (
2.5
), if
\alpha<\min\{\frac{4}{n},1+\frac{2}{n}\}
, then

\displaystyle\sup_{t\in(0,T_{max})}\|f(u(\cdot,t))\|_{L^{1}(\Omega)}<\infty.

(3.31)

This implies that
\sup_{t\in(0,T_{max})}\|u(\cdot,t)\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}<\infty
by well-established semigroup estimates. Applying Lemma
2.1
, we conclude global existence of the solution and the regularity therein.

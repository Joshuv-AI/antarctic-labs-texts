Mathematical Properties of the Zadoff–Chu Sequences
David Gregoratti, Xavier Arteaga, and Joaquim Broquetas

Abstract
. This paper is a compilation of well-known results about Zadoff–Chu sequences, including all proofs with a consistent mathematical notation, for easy reference. Moreover, for a Zadoff–Chu sequence x u ​ [ n ] x_{u}[n] of prime length N ZC N_{\text{ZC}} and root index u u , a formula is derived that allows computing the first term (frequency zero) of its discrete Fourier transform, X u ​ [ 0 ] X_{u}[0] , with constant complexity independent of the sequence length, as opposed to accumulating all its N ZC N_{\text{ZC}} terms. The formula stems from a famous result in analytic number theory and is an interesting complement to the fact that the discrete Fourier transform of a Zadoff–Chu sequence is itself a Zadoff–Chu sequence whose terms are scaled by X u ​ [ 0 ] X_{u}[0] . Finally, the paper concludes with a brief analysis of time-continuous signals derived from Zadoff–Chu sequences, especially those obtained by OFDM-modulating a Zadoff–Chu sequence.



1.
Introduction

Sequences whose periodic autocorrelation nulls out everywhere but at the
zero-lag point play a fundamental role in several applications such as
synchronization, spread spectrum signaling, random multiple access, and radar.
A notable class of sequences with this ideal autocorrelation property consists
of the Zadoff–Chu (ZC) sequences, which were introduced by Chu
[
1
]
,
building on the works of Frank and Zadoff
[
2
]
, as well as Heimiller

[
3
]
. Indeed, as outlined in the next section, ZC sequences
are endowed with a number of desirable features on top of their perfect
autocorrelation, and they are widely employed in the latest cellular
communications standards (namely, LTE and 5G NR) enabling functionalities like
synchronization, random access, channel sounding, and data spreading in control
channels. The interested reader is referred to
[
4
]
and references therein
for a more detailed account of the uses of ZC sequences in the 3GPP standards.

Since modern cellular systems rely on OFDM (Orthogonal Frequency-Division
Multiplexing) modulation, the problem of computing the Discrete Fourier Transform
(DFT) of a ZC sequence is particularly relevant.
Moreover, the 3GPP specifies ZC sequences whose length
N_{\text{ZC}}
is a prime number.
As a result, the complexity of obliviously taking a DFT is significantly
higher than the FFT algorithm, namely
O(N_{\text{ZC}}\log N_{\text{ZC}})
, see
[
5
]
.
Precomputing all the values for all possible sequences of length
N_{\text{ZC}}
is also
not an option, since it requires storing up to
N_{\text{ZC}}(N_{\text{ZC}}-1)
complex
floating-point values, with
N_{\text{ZC}}
in the order of thousands. Fortunately, it can be
shown that the DFT of a ZC sequence of prime length is itself a ZC sequence
scaled both in amplitude and time
[
6
,
7
]
, thus reducing the complexity to
simply
O(N_{\text{ZC}})
. More specifically, computing the DFT consists of two steps, both of
complexity
O(N_{\text{ZC}})
: first, compute the zero-frequency coefficient of the DFT
and, second, use this coefficient to scale the new ZC sequence.

Once a ZC sequence is OFDM-modulated, it results in a time-continuous signal that still
maintains some of the properties of the discrete sequence, although in a weaker
form. Specifically, the signal shows a low peak-to-average power ratio (as
compared to other OFDM signals) and its zero-lag autocorrelation is still
significantly higher than all other local maxima.

In this context, the purpose of this paper is three-fold. First, it aims to
compile several results regarding ZC sequences in a single, self-contained
document with uniform notation, which, we hope, will help the reader to have a
better overview of the topic.

The second objective is to show, in Section
3
, that the
zero-frequency coefficient of the DFT of a ZC sequence can be computed by means
of a
plug-and-play
formula of complexity
O(1)
, which simplifies even
further the computation and the memory requirements. Since our solution only
involves the the first of the two steps described above, the final complexity of
the DFT is still
O(N_{\text{ZC}})
, but, nonetheless, several times faster. As a side
note, it is worth remarking that real-time cellular systems (especially those
running as a software-defined radio platform) do benefit from all sorts of minor
optimizations on the long run.

The third and final objective is to provide, in Section
4
, an
initial characterization of continuous signals derived from ZC sequences, as the
ones resulting from their OFDM modulation.

2.
Definition and Correlation Properties

This section is devoted to the formal definition of the ZC sequences as well as
to an overview of their main properties. Even though these properties are well
known, we report the proofs in Appendix
A
for completeness.

In its most general form, the ZC sequence of period
N_{\text{ZC}}
and root
u
, with
relatively prime
N_{\text{ZC}},u\in\mathbb{N}
,
1\leq u\leq N_{\text{ZC}}-1
, reads

(1)

x_{u}[n]=\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{j}\pi u\frac{n(n+c+2q)}{N_{\text{ZC}}}},\qquad n\in\mathbb{Z}

where
c\equiv N_{\text{ZC}}\bmod 2
, and
q
is any integer. One can easily show (see
Claim
1
in Appendix
A
) that
x_{u}[n]

is indeed periodic of period
N_{\text{ZC}}
. It is also evident that
x_{u}[n]
is
unimodular.

As mentioned in the introduction, the most appealing property of the ZC
sequences concerns their autocorrelation, namely

R_{u,u}[\tau]=\sum_{n=0}^{N_{\text{ZC}}-1}x_{u}[n]x_{u}^{*}[n+\tau]=N_{\text{ZC}}\delta_{N_{\text{ZC}}|\tau},\qquad\tau\in\mathbb{Z}

where
∗
denotes complex conjugate and where we introduced the Kronecker delta
symbol
\delta_{N_{\text{ZC}}|\tau}=1
if
\tau\equiv 0\pmod{N_{\text{ZC}}}
and zero
otherwise (see Claim
2
in Appendix
A
).
This ideal autocorrelation property, together with unimodularity, categorizes ZC
sequences as CAZAC sequences (i.e., constant amplitude zero autocorrelation).

Although not zero, the cross-correlation between two ZC sequences with different
roots is also well behaved, namely

\lvert R_{u,v}[\tau]\rvert=\sum_{n=0}^{N_{\text{ZC}}-1}x_{u}[n]x_{v}^{*}[n+\tau]=\sqrt{N_{\text{ZC}}}

independently of
\tau
and as long as
u-v
is relatively prime to
N_{\text{ZC}}
(see
Claim
3
in Appendix
A
).

Having defined the ZC sequences and recalled their main properties, we are now
ready to move on to the study of the DFT of a ZC sequence and to the main result
of the paper. Note that, hereafter, we will always consider the special case in
which
N_{\text{ZC}}
is an odd prime number and
q=0
(indeed, it is straightforward to
show—see Claim
4
in Appendix
A
—that
q

only introduces a shift and a constant rotation of the terms of the sequence
and, thus, is irrelevant for our purposes). In other words, we will use

(2)

x_{u}[n]=\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{j}\pi u\frac{n(n+1)}{N_{\text{ZC}}}}

where
N_{\text{ZC}}
is prime and
1\leq u\leq N_{\text{ZC}}-1
(the condition of
u
and

N_{\text{ZC}}
being coprime is now implicit).

3.
The DFT of a ZC Sequence

ZC sequences of odd prime length, viz. (
2
), benefit from an additional
property: Their DFT (and inverse DFT) is itself a ZC sequence, scaled both in
amplitude and time. Namely, as proven in, e.g.,
[
6
]
(see also
Claim
5
in Appendix
A
),

(3)

X_{u}[k]=\sum_{n=0}^{N_{\text{ZC}}-1}x_{u}[n]\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{j}2\pi\frac{kn}{N_{\text{ZC}}}}=X_{u}[0]x_{u}^{*}\bigl[u^{-1}k\bigr]

where
X_{u}[\cdot]
denotes the DFT of
x_{u}[\cdot]
and
u^{-1}
is the inverse
of
u
modulo
N_{\text{ZC}}
, that is
u^{-1}u\equiv 1\pmod{N_{\text{ZC}}}
, which is well
defined since
N_{\text{ZC}}
is prime.

As mentioned in the introduction, this property makes up for the diminished performance
of DFT/FFT algorithms applied to sequences of prime length
[
5
]
, thus
facilitating the adoption of ZC sequences in modern communications systems whose
physical layer relies on OFDM modulation (e.g., 5G New Radio, see
[
8
]
).
However, one still needs to compute the zero-frequency term of the DFT by
accumulating all terms of the original ZC sequence:

(4)

X_{u}[0]=\sum_{n=0}^{N_{\text{ZC}}-1}x_{u}[n]=\sum_{n=0}^{N_{\text{ZC}}-1}\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{j}\pi u\frac{n(n+1)}{N_{\text{ZC}}}}.

The cost of computing this coefficient by taking the sum on the right-hand side of the
previous equation is
O(N_{\text{ZC}})
and, in general, it must be repeated for all

N_{\text{ZC}}-1
possible values of the root index
u
. Fortunately, the following
result, due to Gauss, provides a plug-in formula that allows us to compute the
coefficient
X_{u}[0]
with a fixed number of operations. Surprisingly, there
seems to be no mention to this result in the wireless communications or signal
processing literature.

(Generalized quadratic Gauss sum)
.

For all prime
N_{\text{ZC}}
, one has

X_{u}[0]=\ell_{2u}\eta_{N_{\text{ZC}}}\sqrt{N_{\text{ZC}}}\;\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{j}\frac{2\pi u}{N_{\text{ZC}}}\bigl(\frac{N_{\text{ZC}}+1}{2}\bigr)^{3}}

where
\ell_{n}
stands for the Legendre symbol

\ell_{n}=\begin{cases}0&\text{if $N_{\text{ZC}}$ divides $n$}\\

-1&\text{if $n\not\equiv 0$ is not a perfect square modulo $N_{\text{ZC}}$}\end{cases}

4.
Time-Continuous ZC Signals

It is sometimes convenient to think of discrete sequences as if they were the
result of sampling a continuous signal. However, most of the properties of the ZC
sequences are intrinsic to their discrete nature and only hold in a weaker sense
in the continuous case, as we are showing next.

Given the
N_{\text{ZC}}
-periodicity of the ZC sequences, it is only natural to look for
signals
x_{u}(t)
of period
T
such that
2
2

2

Discrete sequences are indexed
with brackets, continuous signals with parentheses.

x_{u}[n]=x_{u}\biggl(\frac{nT}{N_{\text{ZC}}}\biggr).

A first option would be the periodic, constant-modulus, chirp-like signal defined by

x_{u}^{(\textup{C})}(t)=\begin{cases}\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{j}\pi u\frac{N_{\text{ZC}}t(N_{\text{ZC}}t+T)}{N_{\text{ZC}}T^{2}}}&t\in[0,T)\\
x_{u}^{(\textup{C})}(t\bmod T)&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

It is straightforward to show that
\lim_{t\to T^{-}}x_{u}^{(\textup{C})}(t)=x_{u}^{(\textup{C})}(0)=1
and that
the signal is thus continuous on
\mathbb{R}
, although not differentiable at

t=mT
for all
m\in\mathbb{Z}
. However, signal
x_{u}^{(\textup{C})}(t)
is not a
good choice in most applications, since its instantaneous frequency reaches the
value

f_{\max}=u\frac{2N_{\text{ZC}}+1}{2T}

which is higher than the sampling rate
f_{0}=N_{\text{ZC}}/T
. Figure
2

shows an example chirp signal, with markers placed on the samples corresponding
to the ZC sequence.

X_{u}[n]

x_{u}[n]

Namely, we define

x_{u}^{(\textup{LP})}(t)=\frac{1}{N_{\text{ZC}}}\sum_{k=-N_{0}}^{N_{0}}X_{u}[k]\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{j}2\pi\frac{kt}{T}}

where we introduced
N_{0}=(N_{\text{ZC}}-1)/2
and where we adopted the usual circular-shift

X_{u}[-k]=X_{u}[N_{\text{ZC}}-k]
for all

5.
Summary

In this paper we have revisited some fundamental properties that make ZC
sequences very appealing for numerous applications in wireless communications.
Specifically, using a consistent mathematical notation, we have reported results
from several different authors, which show that ZC sequences are endowed with
perfect autocorrelation and very low cross-correlation
(see Section
2
).

Similarly, Section
3
summarizes results related with taking the
DFT of a ZC sequence, which turns out to be a very cheap operation in spite of
the sequence length being a prime number. The section ends by providing a very
simple expression for the zero-indexed coefficient of the DFT [or, equivalently,
for the sum of all the terms of the ZC sequence, see (
4
)]: Even
though this result follows from the well-known quadratic Gauss sum, there seems
to be no mention of it in the technical literature, at least to the best of
these authors’ knowledge.

Finally, Section
4
shows how to obtain a time-continuous
version of a ZC sequence. Although the resulting signal is still well behaved,
the price to pay is a mild crest factor and a loss of sharpness in the
correlation properties.

Proofs of Basic Results

The results of this appendix are well known in the literature (see, e.g.,

We start by showing periodicity of ZC sequences.

Claim 1
.

For all
u
, the ZC sequence
x_{u}[n]
in (
1
) is periodic of period
N_{\text{ZC}}
.

Proof.

For all
k\in\mathbb{Z}

x_{u}[n+kN_{\text{ZC}}]=\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{j}\pi u\frac{(n+kN_{\text{ZC}})(n+kN_{\text{ZC}}+c+2q)}{N_{\text{ZC}}}}\\
=\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{j}\pi u\frac{n(n+c+2q)+(2n+2q+c)kN_{\text{ZC}}+k^{2}N_{\text{ZC}}^{2}}{N_{\text{ZC}}}}\\
=\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{j}\pi u\frac{n(n+c+2q)}{N_{\text{ZC}}}-\mathrm{j}\pi uk[2n+2q+c+kN_{\text{ZC}}]}=x_{u}[n].

The last step is straightforward when
k
is even, while, when
k
is odd, it
stems from
c+kN_{\text{ZC}}
being even, since
c\equiv N_{\text{ZC}}\bmod 2
.
∎

Next, we prove two claims about autocorrelation and cross-correlation of ZC
sequences.

Claim 2
.

For all
\tau\in\mathbb{Z}
,

R_{u,u}[\tau]=\sum_{n=0}^{N_{\text{ZC}}-1}x_{u}[n]x_{u}^{*}[n+\tau]=N_{\text{ZC}}\delta_{N_{\text{ZC}}|\tau}

where
\delta_{N_{\text{ZC}}|\tau}=1
if
\tau\equiv 0\pmod{N_{\text{ZC}}}
and zero otherwise.

Proof.

By the definition of
x_{u}[n]
, one obtains

\displaystyle R_{u,u}[\tau]

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=0}^{N_{\text{ZC}}-1}\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{j}\pi u\frac{n(n+c+2q)-(n+\tau)(n+\tau+c+2q)}{N_{\text{ZC}}}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=0}^{N_{\text{ZC}}-1}\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{j}\pi u\frac{\tau(\tau+c+2q)+2n\tau}{N_{\text{ZC}}}}

\displaystyle=\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{j}\pi u\frac{\tau(\tau+c+2q)}{N_{\text{ZC}}}}\sum_{n=0}^{N_{\text{ZC}}-1}\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{j}2\pi u\frac{n\tau}{N_{\text{ZC}}}}

\displaystyle=N_{\text{ZC}}\delta_{N_{\text{ZC}}|\tau}

given the well-known result

\sum_{n=0}^{N_{\text{ZC}}-1}\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{j}2\pi u\frac{n\tau}{N_{\text{ZC}}}}=\begin{cases}N_{\text{ZC}}&\text{if $N_{\text{ZC}}$ divides $\tau$}\\
0&\text{otherwise}\end{cases}

and the fact that
\tau(\tau+c+2q)
is always even when
\tau
is a multiple of

N_{\text{ZC}}
.
∎

For all

Let

\displaystyle x_{u}[n]

\displaystyle=\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{j}\pi\frac{un(n+c+2q_{u})}{N_{\text{ZC}}}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=0}^{N_{\text{ZC}}-1}\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{j}\pi\frac{(u-v)n(n+c)+2unq_{u}-2vn(\tau+q_{v})-v\tau(\tau+c+2q_{v})}{N_{\text{ZC}}}}

\displaystyle=\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{j}\pi v\frac{\tau(\tau+c+2q_{v})}{N_{\text{ZC}}}}\sum_{n=0}^{N_{\text{ZC}}-1}\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{j}\pi\frac{(u-v)n(n+c)+2n(uq_{u}-v\tau-vq_{v})}{N_{\text{ZC}}}}

\displaystyle=\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{j}\pi v\frac{\tau(\tau+c+2q_{v})}{N_{\text{ZC}}}}\sum_{n=0}^{N_{\text{ZC}}-1}\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{j}\pi(u-v)\frac{n[n+c+2\zeta v(uq_{u}-v\tau-vq_{v})]}{N_{\text{ZC}}}}

\displaystyle=\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{j}\pi v\frac{\tau(\tau+c+2q_{v})}{N_{\text{ZC}}}}\sum_{n=0}^{N_{\text{ZC}}-1}x_{u-v}[n]

where we have introduced
\zeta=(u-v)^{-1}
modulo
N_{\text{ZC}}
(recall that, by
assumption,
u-v
and
N_{\text{ZC}}
are coprime) and where
x_{u-v}[n]
is a new ZC
sequence with root
u-v
, where
\zeta v(uq_{u}-v\tau-vq_{v})
plays the role
of the phase parameter
q
. Incidentally, note that the last step also shows
that the cross-correlation between
x_{u}[n]
and
x_{v}[n]
is nothing else but a
scaled and conjugated version of
x_{v}[n]
, that is

(6)

R_{u,v}[\tau]=x_{v}^{*}[\tau]\sum_{n=0}^{N_{\text{ZC}}-1}x_{u-v}[n].

To finish the proof, one can easily compute the (squared) modulus of the previous
expression. Indeed, after letting
q=\zeta v(uq_{u}-v\tau-vq_{v})
,

\displaystyle\lvert R_{u,v}[\tau]\rvert^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=0}^{N_{\text{ZC}}-1}\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{j}\pi(u-v)\frac{n(n+c+2q)}{N_{\text{ZC}}}}\sum_{k=0}^{N_{\text{ZC}}-1}\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{j}\pi(u-v)\frac{k(k+c+2q)}{N_{\text{ZC}}}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=0}^{N_{\text{ZC}}-1}\sum_{k=0}^{N_{\text{ZC}}-1}\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{j}\pi(u-v)\frac{n(n+c+2q)}{N_{\text{ZC}}}}\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{j}\pi(u-v)\frac{k(k+c+2q)}{N_{\text{ZC}}}}

and, since periodicity allows the change of variable
k\mapsto k+n
,

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=0}^{N_{\text{ZC}}-1}\sum_{k=0}^{N_{\text{ZC}}-1}\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{j}\pi(u-v)\frac{n(n+c+2q)}{N_{\text{ZC}}}}\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{j}\pi(u-v)\frac{(k+n)(k+n+c+2q)}{N_{\text{ZC}}}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=0}^{N_{\text{ZC}}-1}\sum_{k=0}^{N_{\text{ZC}}-1}\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{j}\pi(u-v)\frac{k(k+2n+c+2q)}{N_{\text{ZC}}}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{N_{\text{ZC}}-1}\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{j}\pi(u-v)\frac{k(k+c+2q)}{N_{\text{ZC}}}}\sum_{n=0}^{N_{\text{ZC}}-1}\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{j}2\pi(u-v)\frac{kn}{N_{\text{ZC}}}}

\displaystyle=N_{\text{ZC}}\sum_{k=0}^{N_{\text{ZC}}-1}\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{j}\pi(u-v)\frac{k(k+c+2q)}{N_{\text{ZC}}}}\delta_{k}

\displaystyle=N_{\text{ZC}}.

As a last remark, note that the combination of the last step and
(
6
) also tells us that

\biggl\lvert\sum_{n=0}^{N_{\text{ZC}}-1}x_{u}[n]\biggr\rvert=\sqrt{N_{\text{ZC}}}

for all
N_{\text{ZC}}
and
u
relatively prime. Proposition
1
in
Section
3
allows us to
be more precise when
N_{\text{ZC}}
is a prime number.
∎

We now prove a simple result that justifies focusing on the simplified ZC
sequences in (
2
), instead of the general form in (
1
).

Claim 4
.

Let

\displaystyle x_{u}[n]

\displaystyle=\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{j}\pi u\frac{n(n+c)}{N_{\text{ZC}}}}

and

\displaystyle x_{u}^{(q)}[n]

\displaystyle=\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{j}\pi u\frac{n(n+c+2q)}{N_{\text{ZC}}}}.

Then

x_{u}^{(q)}[n]=x_{u}[n+q]\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{j}\pi u\frac{q(q+c)}{N_{\text{ZC}}}}.

Proof.

With straightforward manipulations:

\displaystyle x_{u}^{(q)}[n]

\displaystyle=\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{j}\pi u\frac{n(n+c+2q)}{N_{\text{ZC}}}}

\displaystyle=\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{j}\pi u\frac{(n+q)(n+c+2q)-q(n+c+2q)}{N_{\text{ZC}}}}

\displaystyle=\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{j}\pi u\frac{(n+q)(n+c+q)+q(n+q)-q(n+c+2q)}{N_{\text{ZC}}}}

\displaystyle=\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{j}\pi u\frac{(n+q)(n+c+q)}{N_{\text{ZC}}}}\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{j}\pi u\frac{q(q+c)}{N_{\text{ZC}}}}

\displaystyle=x_{u}[n+q]\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{j}\pi u\frac{q(q+c)}{N_{\text{ZC}}}}.

∎

Finally, we show how to compute the DFT of a ZC sequence.

Claim 5

(see
[
6
]
)
.

The DFT of a ZC sequence of odd prime length
N_{\text{ZC}}
, viz. (
2
), is
itself a ZC sequence, scaled both in amplitude and time. Namely,

X_{u}[k]=\sum_{n=0}^{N_{\text{ZC}}-1}x_{u}[n]\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{j}2\pi\frac{kn}{N_{\text{ZC}}}}=X_{u}[0]x_{u}^{*}\bigl[u^{-1}k\bigr]

where
u^{-1}
is the inverse of
u
modulo
N_{\text{ZC}}
, that is
u^{-1}u\equiv 1\pmod{N_{\text{ZC}}}
, which is well defined since
N_{\text{ZC}}
is prime.

Moreover, the time-scaled ZC sequence is equivalent to a modulation of the ZC
sequence of root index
u^{-1}
:

x_{u}^{*}[u^{-1}k]=x_{u^{-1}}^{*}[k]\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{j}\pi k\frac{(u^{-1}-1)(N_{\text{ZC}}+1)}{N_{\text{ZC}}}}.

Proof.

As usual, we expand
x_{u}[n]
to obtain

\displaystyle X_{u}[k]

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=0}^{N_{\text{ZC}}-1}x_{u}[n]\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{j}2\pi\frac{kn}{N_{\text{ZC}}}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=0}^{N_{\text{ZC}}-1}\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{j}\pi u\frac{n(n+1)+2u^{-1}kn}{N_{\text{ZC}}}}

and, applying the change of variable
n\mapsto n-u^{-1}k
and
exploiting periodicity,

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=0}^{N_{\text{ZC}}-1}\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{j}\pi u\frac{(n-u^{-1}k)(n-u^{-1}k+1)+2u^{-1}k(n-u^{-1}k)}{N_{\text{ZC}}}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=0}^{N_{\text{ZC}}-1}\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{j}\pi u\frac{n^{2}+n-u^{-2}k^{2}-u^{-1}k}{N_{\text{ZC}}}}

\displaystyle=\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{j}\pi u\frac{u^{-1}k(u^{-1}k+1)}{N_{\text{ZC}}}}\sum_{n=0}^{N_{\text{ZC}}-1}\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{j}\pi u\frac{n(n+1)}{N_{\text{ZC}}}}

\displaystyle=x_{u}^{*}\bigl[u^{-1}k\bigr]X_{u}[0].

The proof of the second statement is also straightforward. Let
\alpha=\frac{N_{\text{ZC}}+1}{2}
be the inverse of 2 modulo
N_{\text{ZC}}
. Then, since
u^{-1}k(u^{-1}k+1)
is even,

x_{u}^{*}\bigl[u^{-1}k\bigr]=\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{j}\pi u\frac{u^{-1}k(u^{-1}k+1)}{N_{\text{ZC}}}}=\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{j}2\pi u\alpha\frac{u^{-1}k(u^{-1}k+1)}{N_{\text{ZC}}}}\\
=\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{j}2\pi u^{-1}\alpha\frac{k(k+u)}{N_{\text{ZC}}}}=\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{j}2\pi u^{-1}\alpha\frac{k(k+1)}{N_{\text{ZC}}}}\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{j}2\pi u^{-1}\alpha\frac{k(u-1)}{N_{\text{ZC}}}}\\
=\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{j}\pi u^{-1}\frac{k(k+1)}{N_{\text{ZC}}}}\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{j}2\pi\alpha\frac{k(u^{-1}-1)}{N_{\text{ZC}}}}=x_{u^{-1}}^{*}[k]\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{j}\pi(N_{\text{ZC}}+1)\frac{k(u^{-1}-1)}{N_{\text{ZC}}}}.

∎

Appendix B
Proof of Proposition
1

Proposition
1
follows straightforwardly from well known
number-theoretic results. We summarize here the main steps.

We start by denoting
\alpha=(N_{\text{ZC}}+1)/2
. Observing that
\alpha\equiv 2^{-1}\pmod{N_{\text{ZC}}}
and that
n(n+1)
is even, we can write

X_{u}[0]=\sum_{n=0}^{N_{\text{ZC}}-1}\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{j}\pi u\frac{n(n+1)}{N_{\text{ZC}}}}=\sum_{n=0}^{N_{\text{ZC}}-1}\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{j}2\pi u\frac{\alpha n(n+1)}{N_{\text{ZC}}}}

and, completing the square at the exponent numerator,

\displaystyle X_{u}[0]

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=0}^{N_{\text{ZC}}-1}\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{j}2\pi u\frac{\alpha(n+\alpha)^{2}-\alpha^{3}}{N_{\text{ZC}}}}

\displaystyle=\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{j}2\pi u\frac{\alpha^{3}}{N_{\text{ZC}}}}\sum_{n=0}^{N_{\text{ZC}}-1}\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{j}2\pi u\frac{\alpha(n+\alpha)^{2}}{N_{\text{ZC}}}}

\displaystyle=\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{j}2\pi u\frac{\alpha^{3}}{N_{\text{ZC}}}}\sum_{n=0}^{N_{\text{ZC}}-1}\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{j}2\pi u\frac{\alpha n^{2}}{N_{\text{ZC}}}}

where the last step is due to the fact that, modulo
N_{\text{ZC}}
,
n+\alpha
runs
over all values in
\{0,1,\dots,N_{\text{ZC}}-1\}
, same as
n
.

To conclude the proof, we only need the two following well-known results
regarding quadratic Gauss sums.

Proposition 2

(
[
10
, Chapter 6, Theorem 1]
)
.

Let
g_{N_{\text{ZC}}}=\sum_{n=0}^{N_{\text{ZC}}-1}\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{j}2\pi\frac{n^{2}}{N_{\text{ZC}}}}
denote the
quadratic Gauss sum. Then

g_{N_{\text{ZC}}}=\begin{cases}\sqrt{N_{\text{ZC}}}&\text{if }N_{\text{ZC}}\equiv 1\pmod{4}\\
\mathrm{j}\sqrt{N_{\text{ZC}}}&\text{if }N_{\text{ZC}}\equiv 3\pmod{4}\end{cases}

Proposition 3

(
[
10
, Chapter 6, Proposition 6.3.1]
)
.

For all
b\in\mathbb{Z}
that is not a multiple of
N_{\text{ZC}}
,

\sum_{n=0}^{N_{\text{ZC}}-1}\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{j}2\pi b\frac{n^{2}}{N_{\text{ZC}}}}=\ell_{b}g_{N_{\text{ZC}}}

where, we recall,
\ell_{b}
is the Legendre symbol of
b
with respect to
N_{\text{ZC}}
.

Indeed, by applying Proposition
3
with
b=-u\alpha
, we have

X_{u}[0]=\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{j}2\pi u\frac{\alpha^{3}}{N_{\text{ZC}}}}\sum_{n=0}^{N_{\text{ZC}}-1}\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{j}2\pi u\frac{\alpha n^{2}}{N_{\text{ZC}}}}=\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{j}2\pi u\frac{\alpha^{3}}{N_{\text{ZC}}}}\ell_{-u\alpha}g_{N_{\text{ZC}}}=\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{j}2\pi u\frac{\alpha^{3}}{N_{\text{ZC}}}}\ell_{-1}\ell_{2u}g_{N_{\text{ZC}}}

where we have used the fact that the Legendre symbol is multiplicative,
therefore
\ell_{-u\alpha}=\ell_{-1}\ell_{u\alpha}
, together with
\ell_{\alpha}=\ell_{2}
, since
\alpha
is the inverse of
2
. The desired result follows from
Proposition
2
, after recalling that

\ell_{-1}=\begin{cases}1&\text{if }N_{\text{ZC}}\equiv 1\pmod{4}\\
-1&\text{if }N_{\text{ZC}}\equiv 3\pmod{4}.\end{cases}

Appendix C
Proofs Regarding Time-Continuous Signals

This appendix is devoted to proving the results about time-continuous ZC
signals. Since we will exclusively focus on the low-pass option, in what follows
we adopt a simplified notation and write
x_{u}(t)
instead of

x_{u}^{(\mathrm{LP})}(t)

C.1.
Peak Amplitude of
x_{u}(t)

To study the amplitude of
x_{u}(t)
, let us first express it in terms of the
discrete sequence
x_{u}[n]
:

\displaystyle x_{u}(t)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{N_{\text{ZC}}}\sum_{k=-N_{0}}^{N_{0}}X_{u}[k]\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{j}2\pi\frac{kt}{T}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{N_{\text{ZC}}}\sum_{k=-N_{0}}^{N_{0}}\sum_{n=0}^{N_{\text{ZC}}-1}x_{u}[n]\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{j}2\pi\frac{kn}{N_{\text{ZC}}}}\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{j}2\pi\frac{kt}{T}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{N_{\text{ZC}}}\sum_{n=0}^{N_{\text{ZC}}-1}x_{u}[n]\sum_{k=-N_{0}}^{N_{0}}\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{j}2\pi k\bigl(\frac{t}{T}-\frac{n}{N_{\text{ZC}}}\bigr)}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=0}^{N_{\text{ZC}}-1}x_{u}[n]D_{N_{\text{ZC}}}\Bigl(\frac{t}{T}-\frac{n}{N_{\text{ZC}}}\Bigr)

where
D_{N_{\text{ZC}}}(\cdot)
denotes the Dirichlet kernel as defined in
(
5
). Then, by the triangle inequality,

\lvert x_{u}(t)\rvert\leq\sum_{n=0}^{N_{\text{ZC}}-1}\Bigl\lvert D_{N_{\text{ZC}}}\Bigl(\frac{t}{T}-\frac{n}{N_{\text{ZC}}}\Bigr)\Bigr\rvert.

Building upon symmetry arguments, one can show that the right-hand side of
the above inequality has period
T/N_{\text{ZC}}
and has maximum points at

T\frac{2m+1}{2N_{\text{ZC}}}
, for
m\in\mathbb{Z}
.
Therefore, we can choose
m=-1
, set
t=-T/(2N_{\text{ZC}})
on the right-hand side and
exploit the fact that
D_{N_{\text{ZC}}}(\cdot)
is an even function to write

\displaystyle\lvert x_{u}(t)\rvert

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{n=0}^{N_{\text{ZC}}-1}\Bigl\lvert D_{N_{\text{ZC}}}\Bigl(\frac{2n+1}{2N_{\text{ZC}}}\Bigr)\Bigr\rvert

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=0}^{N_{\text{ZC}}-1}\frac{\lvert\sin(\pi\frac{2n+1}{2})\rvert}{N_{\text{ZC}}\sin(\pi\frac{2n+1}{2N_{\text{ZC}}})}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=0}^{N_{\text{ZC}}-1}\frac{1}{N_{\text{ZC}}\sin(\pi\frac{2n+1}{2N_{\text{ZC}}})}.

Note that there is no need for taking the absolute value at the denominator, since the
argument of the sine function is always inside the interval
[0,\pi)
.

Now, it remains to apply the well-known inequality
\sin z\geq z(1-z/\pi)
,
which holds for
0\leq z\leq\pi
, and write

\lvert x_{u}(t)\rvert\leq\frac{4N_{\text{ZC}}}{\pi}\sum_{n=0}^{N_{\text{ZC}}-1}\frac{1}{(1+2n)(2N_{\text{ZC}}-1-2n)}.

Partial fraction decomposition and a couple of standard summation tricks allow
further simplification:

\displaystyle\lvert x_{u}(t)\rvert

\displaystyle\leq\frac{2}{\pi}\sum_{n=0}^{N_{\text{ZC}}-1}\frac{1}{1+2n}+\frac{2}{\pi}\sum_{n=0}^{N_{\text{ZC}}-1}\frac{1}{2N_{\text{ZC}}-1-2n}

\displaystyle=\frac{4}{\pi}\sum_{n=0}^{N_{\text{ZC}}-1}\frac{1}{1+2n}\qquad\qquad\text{[$n\mapsto N_{\text{ZC}}-1-n$ in the second sum]}

\displaystyle=\frac{4}{\pi}\Biggl(\sum_{n=1}^{2N_{\text{ZC}}-1}\frac{1}{n}-\sum_{n=1}^{N_{\text{ZC}}-1}\frac{1}{2n}\Biggr).

C.2.
Autocorrelation of
x_{u}(t)

The autocorrelation of
x_{u}(t)
is fairly easy to compute and only requires few
steps:

\displaystyle R_{u,u}(\tau)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{T}\int_{0}^{T}x_{u}(t)x_{u}^{*}(t+\tau)\,\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\textup{(b)}}}{{=}}\frac{1}{N_{\text{ZC}}}\sum_{k=-N_{0}}^{N_{0}}\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{j}2\pi\frac{k\tau}{T}}

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}1&\text{if }\tau=rT,r\in\mathbb{Z}\\
\displaystyle\frac{\sin\Bigl(\pi N_{\text{ZC}}\frac{\tau}{T}\Bigr)}{N_{\text{ZC}}\sin\Bigl(\pi\frac{\tau}{T}\Bigr)}&\text{otherwise}\end{cases}

where, in (a), we just expanded

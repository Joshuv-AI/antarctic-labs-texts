On uniqueness for half-wave maps in dimension d ≥ 3 d\geq 3
Eugene Eyeson, Silvino Reyes Farina, Armin Schikorra

Abstract
. Extending an argument by Shatah and Struwe we obtain uniqueness for solutions of the half-wave map equation in dimension d ≥ 3 d\geq 3 in the natural energy class.



.

1991 Mathematics Subject Classification
35L05, 35B40

1.
Introduction and main result

Half-wave maps appear in the physics literature as the continuum limit of Calagero-Moser spin systems, see
[
13
]
and references within.
They are solutions
{\bf u}:\mathbb{R}^{d}\times[0,T]\to{\mathbb{S}}^{2}\subset\mathbb{R}^{3}
to the half-wave maps equation which is given by

(1.1)

\partial_{t}{\bf u}={\bf u}\wedge(-\Delta)^{\frac{1}{2}}{\bf u}\quad\text{in $\mathbb{R}^{d}\times(0,T)$}.

Here and henceforth
\wedge
denotes the cross product in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
and
(-\Delta)^{\frac{1}{2}}=|\nabla|{}
is the half-Laplacian.
Recently, several authors, e.g.
[
10
,
11
,
3
,
1
,
9
,
20
,
14
]
, began to study mathematical properties of (
1.1
).

In
[
10
]
the structural relation of the half-wave map equation to the wave map equation

\partial_{tt}{\bf u}-\Delta{\bf u}=\left(-\partial_{t}{\bf u}\cdot\partial_{t}{\bf u}+\nabla{\bf u}\cdot\nabla{\bf u}\right){\bf u}

was discovered and exploited. Namely, by a direct computation, see
[
10
, p.663]
, a solution of (
1.1
) solves

(1.2)

\begin{split}\partial_{tt}{\bf u}-\Delta{\bf u}=&{\bf u}|\nabla{\bf u}|^{2}-{\bf u}||\nabla|{}{\bf u}|^{2}\\
&+|\nabla|{}{\bf u}\,\left(\left\langle{\bf u},|\nabla|{}{\bf u}\right\rangle\right)\\
&+{\bf u}\wedge|\nabla|{}\left({\bf u}\wedge|\nabla|{}{\bf u}\right)-{\bf u}\wedge\left({\bf u}\wedge(-\Delta){\bf u}\right).\end{split}

The authors of
[
10
]
then raised the question if one can use this route to extend methods developed for wave maps, e.g. those in the celebrated articles
[
23
,
21
,
22
]
, to half-wave maps. Following this principle, in
[
10
,
9
,
14
]
different well-posedness results for large dimensions were discovered. Observe that the energy-critical dimension for the halfwave map equation is
d=1
, as opposed to the energy-critical dimension of the wave map equation, which is
d=2
.

In this work we also follow this spirit of treating solutions to the halfwave map equation as solutions to a wave-map-type equation, but we focus on techniques developed for wave maps by Shatah and Struwe
[
19
]
. Our main result is the following uniqueness property of half-wave maps.

Theorem 1.1

(Uniqueness)
.

Let
d\geq 3
and
\alpha\in(1,d+\frac{1}{2})
. If
{\bf u},{\bf v}:\mathbb{R}^{d}\times[0,T]\to{\mathbb{S}}^{2}
are smooth solutions to the half-wave map equation with the same initial data
{\bf u}(\cdot,0)={\bf v}(\cdot,0)\in Q+C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d},\mathbb{R}^{3})
for some
Q\in{\mathbb{S}}^{2}
, and if

(1.3)

\||\nabla|^{\alpha}{\bf u}\|_{L^{2}_{t}L^{(\frac{2d}{2\alpha-1},2)}_{x}(\mathbb{R}^{d}\times(0,T))}+\||\nabla|^{\alpha}{\bf v}\|_{L^{2}_{t}L^{(\frac{2d}{2\alpha-1},2)}_{x}(\mathbb{R}^{d}\times(0,T))}<\infty

then
{\bf u}\equiv{\bf v}
.

Here
L^{(p,q)}
denotes the Lorentz space. The a priori assumptions (
1.3
) are the natural energy assumptions for initial data
{\bf u_{0}},{\bf v_{0}}\in\dot{H}^{\frac{d}{2}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, which was one of the crucial observations in
[
19
]
where Shatah and Struwe observed this for
\alpha=1
. A careful inspection of their argument actually gives the assumption (
1.3
) for small
\alpha>1
, see
Section

5
.

As in the case of Shatah-Struwe, our arguments rely mostly on geometric properties combined with fractional Leibniz rules and related commutator estimates. However, while for the wave map equation the proof of uniqueness fits on one page, our argument does not – since it relies on several further structural observations of the “tangential part” of the right-hand side of (
1.2
), which we hope are of independent interest.

Outline

In
Section

2
we introduce operators and estimates needed in the proof of
Theorem

1.1
. We believe that most, if not all, of these estimates are known at least to some experts – and they can be proven by standard techniques. In
Section

3
we discuss the main part of the proof, the decay estimates in time,
Theorem

3.1
. While we are substantially inspired by the argument by Shatah-Struwe, our estimates are more elaborate, even though they mostly rely on the fractional Leibniz rule. The decay estimates of
Theorem

3.1
combined the standard Grönwall type inequality imply
Theorem

1.1
, see
Section

4
. In
Section

5
we discuss the suitability of the assumptions (
1.3
) for
\alpha>1
,
\alpha\approx 1
.

We believe that our arguments can also be used to discuss existence for small data in the above energy class as in Shatah-Struwe, which will be the subject of a future investigations.

2.
Preliminaries: Leibniz Rule, Sobolev embedding and Gagliardo-Nirenberg

Throughout the paper we use the standard
\lesssim
,
\succsim
,
\approx
notation: we write
A\lesssim B
if there is a multiplicative constant
C>0
, which may change from line to line, such that
A\leq CB
. We write
A\approx B
if
A\lesssim B
and
B\lesssim A
.

We denote vectors in bold-face, such as
{\bf v}\in\mathbb{R}^{3}
.

The fractional Laplacian is as a multiplier operator via the Fourier transform
\mathcal{F}
for a constant
c>0
,

|\nabla|^{s}f(x)\equiv(-\Delta)^{\frac{s}{2}}f(x):=\mathcal{F}^{-1}(c|\cdot|^{s}\mathcal{F}f(\cdot))(x).

We also remark the useful potential representation for some (different) constant
c\in\mathbb{R}
and
s\in(0,1)

|\nabla|^{s}f(x)\equiv(-\Delta)^{\frac{s}{2}}f(x)=c\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\frac{f(x)-f(y)}{|x-y|^{n+s}}\,dy,

and, for
s\in(0,2)
,

|\nabla|^{s}f(x)\equiv(-\Delta)^{\frac{s}{2}}f(x)=-\frac{c}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\frac{f(x+h)+f(x-h)-2f(x)}{|h|^{d+s}}\,dh.

As for negative powers,
\mathscr{I}_{s}\equiv(-\Delta)^{-\frac{s}{2}}
denotes the Riesz potential,

\mathscr{I}_{s}f(x)\equiv(-\Delta)^{-\frac{s}{2}}f(x):=\mathcal{F}^{-1}(c|\cdot|^{-s}\mathcal{F}f(\cdot))(x).

It has the potential representation for
s\in(0,d)
,

\mathscr{I}_{s}f(x)\equiv(-\Delta)^{-\frac{s}{2}}f(x)=c\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|x-z|^{s-n}f(z)\,dz.

Some of our arguments will depend on Lorentz space estimate,
L^{p,q}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. We only recall the main properties and refer the reader to
[
4
, Section 1.4]
: For
p\in(1,\infty)
we have
L^{p,p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})=L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
,
L^{p,q_{1}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\subset L^{p,q_{2}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
whenever
q_{1}\leq q_{2}
,
q_{1},q_{2}\in[1,\infty]
.
L^{p,\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
is often referred to as the weak
L^{p}
-space.

2.1.
Embedding theorems

A casual observation we will use throughout this paper is the following comparability

Lemma 2.1
.

For any
p\in(1,\infty)
,

\|\nabla f\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\approx\||\nabla|^{1}f\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}.

More generally in the realm of Lorentz spaces, for any
q\in[1,\infty]

\|\nabla f\|_{L^{p,q}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\approx\||\nabla|^{1}f\|_{L^{p,q}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}.

Proof.

This follows since the Riesz transforms
\mathcal{R}_{i}:=\partial_{i}\mathscr{I}_{1}
are bounded operators on
L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\to L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
and
L^{p,q}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\to L^{p,q}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
for any
p\in(1,\infty)
and
q\in[1,\infty]
, combined with the following facts that can easily be checked using the Fourier transform,

\partial_{i}=c_{1}\mathcal{R}_{i}|\nabla|^{1},\quad\text{and}\quad|\nabla|^{1}=c_{2}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mathcal{R}^{i}\partial_{i}.

∎

Lemma 2.2

(Sobolev inequality)
.

Let
\alpha\in(0,d)
and
p\in(1,\frac{d}{\alpha})
then for any
f\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
,

\|f\|_{L^{\frac{dp}{d-\alpha p}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\lesssim\||\nabla|^{\alpha}f\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}.

Equivalently, in terms of the Riesz potential
\mathscr{I}_{\alpha}\equiv|\nabla|^{-\alpha}
we have

\|\mathscr{I}_{\alpha}f\|_{L^{\frac{dp}{d-\alpha p}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\lesssim\|f\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}.

In terms of Lorentz spaces we have for any
q\in[1,\infty]
,

\|f\|_{L^{\frac{dp}{d-\alpha p},q}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\lesssim\||\nabla|^{\alpha}f\|_{L^{p,q}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}.

and

\|\mathscr{I}_{\alpha}f\|_{L^{\frac{dp}{d-\alpha p,q}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\lesssim\|f\|_{L^{p,q}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}.

An important limit version is

(2.1)

\|\mathscr{I}_{\alpha}f\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\lesssim\|f\|_{L^{\frac{d}{\alpha},1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}.

All these estimates are consequence of Young’s convolution inequality in Lorentz spaces,
[
6
, Lemma 4.8]
or
[
4
, Theorem 1.4.25.]
combined with interpolation, using that
\mathscr{I}_{\alpha}f=c|\cdot|^{\alpha-d}\ast f
and that
|\cdot|^{\alpha-d}\in L^{\frac{d}{d-\alpha},\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
.

Lemma 2.3

(Gagliardo-Nirenberg inequality)
.

For
\alpha\in(0,1)
,
p\in(1,\infty)
, we have

\||\nabla|^{\beta}f\|_{L^{\frac{p}{\beta}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\lesssim\|f\|_{L^{\infty}}^{1-\beta}\,\||\nabla|f\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{\beta}.

Proof.

By
[
16
, Lemma 1, p.329]
we have

\||\nabla|^{\beta}f\|_{L^{\frac{p}{\beta}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\approx\|f\|_{\dot{F}^{\beta}_{\frac{p}{\beta},2}}\lesssim\|f\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{1-\beta}\|f\|_{W^{1,p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{\beta}.

Here
\dot{F}
denotes the homogeneous Triebel-Lizorkin space, cf.
[
17
]
.
Applying this result to
f(\lambda\cdot)
and taking
\lambda\to\infty
we conclude that

\||\nabla|^{\beta}f\|_{L^{\frac{p}{\beta}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\lesssim\|f\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{1-\beta}\|\nabla f\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{\beta}\approx\|f\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{1-\beta}\||\nabla|f\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{\beta}

∎

For convenience we record a special case of the previous inequality.

Corollary 2.4

(Gagliardo-Nirenberg-Sobolev inequality)
.

Assume

(1)

\beta\in(0,\frac{1}{2}]
and
p\in[\frac{2d}{\beta},\infty)
, or

(2)

\beta\in(\frac{1}{2},1]
and
p\in[\frac{2d}{\beta},\frac{2d}{2\beta-1}]
.

Then for
\theta=2\left(\beta-\frac{d}{p}\right)\in[\beta,1]
we have

\||\nabla|^{\beta}f\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\lesssim\|f\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{1-\theta}\,\||\nabla|f\|_{L^{2d}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{\theta}.

Proof.

First we consider the case
\beta>1/2
.

Fix
\sigma\in[0,1]
such that
\frac{1}{p}=(1-\sigma)\frac{\beta}{2d}+\sigma\frac{2\beta-1}{2d}
, i.e.
\sigma=\frac{\beta p-2d}{p(1-\beta)}
. Combining Hölder’s inequality, Sobolev inequality,
Lemma

2.2
, and Gagliardo-Nirenberg inequality,
Lemma

2.3
,

\begin{split}\||\nabla|^{\beta}f\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}=&\|||\nabla|^{\beta}f|^{1-\sigma}\,||\nabla|^{\beta}f|^{\sigma}\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\\
\lesssim&\||\nabla|^{\beta}f\|_{L^{\frac{2d}{\beta}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{1-\sigma}\ \||\nabla|^{\beta}f\|^{\sigma}_{L^{\frac{2d}{2\beta-1}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\\
\lesssim&\left(\|f\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{1-\beta}\||\nabla|f\|_{L^{2d}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{\beta}\right)^{1-\sigma}\ \||\nabla|f\|^{\sigma}_{L^{2d}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\\
=&\|f\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{(1-\beta)(1-\sigma)}\,\||\nabla|f\|_{L^{2d}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{\beta(1-\sigma)+\sigma}\\
\end{split}

Thus, for

\theta=\beta(1-\sigma)+\sigma=2\left(\beta-\frac{d}{p}\right)

we conclude the case
\beta>\frac{1}{2}
.

Assume now
\beta\in(0,\frac{1}{2}]
and
p\in[\frac{2d}{\beta},\infty)
. Pick
\gamma\in(\frac{1}{2},1]
such that
\frac{\beta}{\gamma}p\leq\frac{2d}{2\gamma-1}
. Then we have with previous estimate (with
\theta=\frac{\gamma}{\beta}2\left(\beta-\frac{d}{p}\right)
),

\begin{split}\||\nabla|^{\beta}f\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\lesssim&\|f\|_{L^{\infty}}^{1-\frac{\beta}{\gamma}}\,\left(\||\nabla|^{\gamma}f\|_{L^{\frac{\beta}{\gamma}p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\right)^{\frac{\beta}{\gamma}}\\
\lesssim&\|f\|_{L^{\infty}}^{1-\frac{\beta}{\gamma}}\,\left(\|f\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{1-\theta}\,\||\nabla|f\|_{L^{2d}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{\theta}\right)^{\frac{\beta}{\gamma}}\\
=&\|f\|_{L^{\infty}}^{1-2\left(\beta-\frac{d}{p}\right)}\,\||\nabla|f\|_{L^{2d}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{2\left(\beta-\frac{d}{p}\right)}.\end{split}

∎

2.2.
Leibniz rule commutators

In the following we discuss mostly Leibniz rule type estimates.
The Leibniz rule operator for
|\nabla|^{s}
will be denoted by

H_{|\nabla|^{s}}(f,g):=|\nabla|^{s}(fg)-f|\nabla|^{s}g-(|\nabla|^{s}f)g.

As a standing assumption, we are going to assume that all functions belong to
C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. By density arguments we can apply these inequalities to the situation in the next section. Let us stress that we make no effort to obtain the sharpest possible result with respect to
L^{p}
-spaces (in particular we generally rule out
p=1
and
p=\infty
) but instead focus on the applicability for our purposes.

By a direct computation we have the following useful formula, which has been observed by many authors.

Lemma 2.5
.

Let
s\in(0,2)
then for some
c=c(s,n)
,

H_{|\nabla|^{s}}(f,g)(x)=c\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\frac{(f(x)-f(y))(g(x)-g(y))}{|x-y|^{d+s}}\,dy

We now begin by stating several useful estimates for the Leibniz rule operator, most of them are probably known to some experts – and all of them can be proven via standard methods.

Lemma 2.6
.

For
\sigma\in(0,\alpha)
,
\alpha\in(0,1]
we have for any
p,p_{1},p_{2}\in(1,\infty)
with
\frac{1}{p}=\frac{1}{p_{1}}+\frac{1}{p_{2}}
,

(2.2)

\|H_{|\nabla|^{\alpha}}(f,g)\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\lesssim\||\nabla|^{\sigma}f\|_{L^{p_{1}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\,\||\nabla|^{\alpha-\sigma}g\|_{L^{p_{2}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}.

For a proof of (
2.2
) see e.g.
[
12
]
, or
[
7
, Theorem 3.4.1]
.

We can also estimate a differentiated version of the Leibniz rule operator.

Lemma 2.7
.

Let
\alpha\in(0,1)
and
\beta\in(0,1)
. Pick any
\gamma\in(0,1)
such that
\alpha+\beta-\gamma\in(0,1)
, and
p,p_{1},p_{2}\in(1,\infty)
such that

\frac{1}{p}=\frac{1}{p_{1}}+\frac{1}{p_{2}}.

Then

(2.3)

\||\nabla|^{\alpha}H_{|\nabla|^{\beta}}(f,g)\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\lesssim\||\nabla|^{\gamma_{1}}f\|_{L^{p_{1}}}\,\||\nabla|^{\alpha+\beta-\gamma_{1}}g\|_{L^{p_{2}}}.

Proof of (
2.3
).

This can be proven with techniques from
[
12
, Theorem 7.1.]
, see also the presentation in
[
7
, Theorem 3.4.1]
. By duality we have

\||\nabla|^{\alpha}H_{|\nabla|^{\beta}}(f,g)\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\lesssim\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}H_{|\nabla|^{\beta}}(f,g)\,|\nabla|^{\alpha}h

for some
h\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
with
\|h\|_{L^{p^{\prime}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\leq 1
. Set
\tilde{h}:=|\nabla|^{\alpha}h
and, as in
[
12
,
7
]
, let
F
,
G
,
H
be the
\beta
-harmonic extension of
f
,
g
,
\tilde{h}
, respectively. That is, cf.
[
7
, (3.1.1)]
,

\begin{cases}\operatorname{div}_{\mathbb{R}^{d+1}}(t^{1-s}\nabla_{\mathbb{R}^{d+1}}F)=0\quad&\text{for $(x,t)\in\mathbb{R}^{d+1}_{+}$}\\
\lim_{t\to 0^{+}}F(x,t)=f(x)\quad&\text{for $x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}$}\\
\lim_{t\to\infty}F(x,t)=0\quad&\text{for $x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}$}\\
\end{cases}

An integration by parts argument, see
[
7
, Proof of Theorem 3.4.1]
, implies that

\left\lvert\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}H_{|\nabla|^{\beta}}(f,g)\,h\right\rvert\lesssim\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d+1}_{+}}t^{1-\beta}\,|\nabla F|\,|\nabla G|\,|H|.

Observe that by
[
12
, (10.6)]
, we have the estimate

|H(x,t)|\leq t^{-\alpha}\mathcal{M}(\mathscr{I}_{\alpha}\tilde{h})(x)=t^{-\alpha}\mathcal{M}h(x),

where
\mathcal{M}
is the Hardy-Littlewood maximal function. That is, we have found

\begin{split}&\left\lvert\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}H_{|\nabla|^{\beta}}(f,g)\,|\nabla|^{\alpha}h\right\rvert\\
\lesssim&\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\mathcal{M}h(x)\int_{t=0}^{\infty}t^{1-\beta-\alpha}|\nabla F(x,t)|\,|\nabla G(x,t)|\,dt\,\,dx\\
\lesssim&\|\mathcal{M}h\|_{L^{p^{\prime}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\,\left\|\left(\int_{0}^{\infty}\left(t^{\frac{1}{2}-\gamma}|\nabla F(x,t)|\right)^{2}dt\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\right\|_{L^{p_{1}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\,\left\|\left(\int_{t=0}^{\infty}\left(t^{\frac{1}{2}-(\beta+\alpha-\gamma)}|\nabla G(x,t)|\right)^{2}\,dt\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\right\|_{L^{p_{2}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\\
\lesssim&\|h\|_{L^{p^{\prime}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\,[f]_{\dot{F}^{\gamma}_{p_{1},2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\,[g]_{\dot{F}^{\beta+\alpha-\gamma}_{p_{2},2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\\
\approx&\|h\|_{L^{p^{\prime}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\,\||\nabla|^{\gamma}f\|_{L^{p_{1}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\,\||\nabla|^{\beta+\alpha-\gamma}g\|_{L^{p_{2}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\end{split}

In the second to last inequality we used the boundedness of the maximal function
\mathcal{M}
on
L^{p^{\prime}}
and the identification of Triebel spaces,
[
12
, Theorem 10.8]
. In the last step we used that by Littlewood-Paley-theorem,
[
5
, Theorem 1.3.8.]
,
[f]_{\dot{F}^{\gamma}_{p_{1},2}}\approx\||\nabla|^{\gamma}f\|_{L^{p_{1}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}
. We can conclude.
∎

We will also need an estimate for a double commutator. We are not aware of this estimate in the literature, but it can be obtained with the usual paraproduct approach.

Lemma 2.8
.

Let
\alpha,\beta\in(0,1]
, and consider the double commutator

\tilde{H}_{|\nabla|^{\beta},|\nabla|^{\alpha}}(f,g):=|\nabla|^{\beta}H_{|\nabla|^{\alpha}}(f,g)-H_{|\nabla|^{\alpha}}(|\nabla|^{\beta}f,g)-H_{|\nabla|^{\alpha}}(f,|\nabla|^{\beta}g).

Then for any
\gamma\in(0,\alpha+\beta)
and any
p,p_{1},p_{2}\in(1,\infty)
with
\frac{1}{p}=\frac{1}{p_{1}}+\frac{1}{p_{2}}
we have

\|\tilde{H}_{|\nabla|^{\beta},|\nabla|^{\alpha}}(f,g)\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\lesssim\||\nabla|^{\gamma}f\|_{L^{p_{1}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\,\||\nabla|^{\alpha+\beta-\gamma}f\|_{L^{p_{2}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}.

Proof.

This can be proven in a very similar fashion to
[
2
, Section 3]
, we only sketch the main steps.

Denote by
\dot{F}^{s}_{p,q}
the homogeneous Triebel spaces, and by
\Delta_{k}
the Littlewood-Paley projection operator,
[
5
, 1.3.2]
. Then by Littlewood-Paley theorem,
[
5
, Theorem 1.3.8.]
, and duality,
for some
\psi\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
with
[\psi]_{\dot{F}^{0}_{p^{\prime},2}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\lesssim 1
,

\begin{split}\left\|\tilde{H}_{|\nabla|^{\beta},|\nabla|^{\alpha}}(f,g)\right\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\approx&\left\|\tilde{H}_{|\nabla|^{\beta},|\nabla|^{\alpha}}(f,g)\right\|_{\dot{F}^{0}_{p,2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\\
\approx&\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\tilde{H}_{|\nabla|^{\beta},|\nabla|^{\alpha}}(f,g)\,\Delta_{k}\psi.\end{split}

Here
\Delta_{k}
denotes the Littlewood-Paley projection operator onto the
2^{k}
-frequency.
With the usual paraproduct argument, denoting by
\Delta^{\ell}:=\sum_{\tilde{\ell}\leq\ell}\Delta_{\tilde{\ell}}
,

(2.4)

\begin{split}\|\tilde{H}_{|\nabla|^{\beta},|\nabla|^{\alpha}}(f,g)\|_{L^{p_{1}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\approx&\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\sum_{k\approx j}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\tilde{H}_{|\nabla|^{\beta},|\nabla|^{\alpha}}(\Delta_{j}f,\Delta^{j-4}g)\,\Delta_{k}\psi\\
&+\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\sum_{k\approx j}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\tilde{H}_{|\nabla|^{\beta},|\nabla|^{\alpha}}(\Delta^{j-4}f,\Delta_{j}g)\,\Delta_{k}\psi\\
&+\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\sum_{\ell\approx j}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\tilde{H}_{|\nabla|^{\beta},|\nabla|^{\alpha}}(\Delta_{j}f,\Delta_{\ell}g)\,\Delta^{j-10}\psi\\
&+\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\sum_{\ell\approx k\approx j}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\tilde{H}_{|\nabla|^{\beta},|\nabla|^{\alpha}}(\Delta_{j}f,\Delta_{\ell}g)\,\Delta_{k}\psi.\end{split}

Here, by a slight abuse of notation we say for indices
\ell,k
that
\ell\approx k
if
k-c\leq\ell\leq k+c
for some constant
c>0
.

The last term in (
2.4
) is the simplest to estimate, since for any
\theta_{i}\in[0,\alpha+\beta]
such that
\sum_{i=1}^{3}\theta_{i}=\alpha+\beta

\begin{split}&\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\sum_{\ell\approx k\approx j}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|\nabla|^{\theta_{1}}\Delta_{j}f\,|\nabla|^{\theta_{2}}\Delta_{\ell}g\,|\nabla|^{\theta_{3}}\Delta_{k}\psi\\
\approx&\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\sum_{\ell\approx k\approx j}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}2^{(\gamma-\theta_{1})j}|\nabla|^{\theta_{1}}\Delta_{j}f\ 2^{(\alpha+\beta-\gamma-\theta_{2})\ell}|\nabla|^{\theta_{2}}\Delta_{\ell}g\ 2^{-\theta_{3}k}|\nabla|^{\theta_{3}}\Delta_{k}\psi\\
\lesssim&\left\|\left(\sum_{j}|2^{(\gamma-\theta_{1})j}|\nabla|^{\theta_{1}}\Delta_{j}f|^{4}\right)^{\frac{1}{4}}\right\|_{L^{p_{1}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\,\left\|\left(\sum_{\ell\in\mathbb{Z}}\left(2^{(\alpha+\beta-\gamma-\theta_{2})\ell}|\nabla|^{\theta_{2}}\Delta_{\ell}g\right)^{4}\right)^{\frac{1}{4}}\right\|_{L^{p_{2}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\\
&\quad\cdot\left\|\left(\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\left(2^{-\theta_{3}k}|\nabla|^{\theta_{3}}\Delta_{k}\psi\right)^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\right\|_{L^{p^{\prime}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\\
\lesssim&[f]_{\dot{F}^{\gamma}_{p_{1},4}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\,[g]_{\dot{F}^{\alpha+\beta-\gamma}_{p_{2},4}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\,[\psi]_{\dot{F}^{0}_{p^{\prime},2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\\
\lesssim&[f]_{\dot{F}^{\gamma}_{p_{1},2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\,[g]_{\dot{F}^{\alpha+\beta-\gamma}_{p_{2},2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\,[\psi]_{\dot{F}^{0}_{p^{\prime},2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\\
\approx&\||\nabla|^{\gamma}f\|_{L^{p_{1}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\,\||\nabla|^{\alpha+\beta-\gamma}g\|_{L^{p_{3}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}.\end{split}

The other terms are very similar to each other. We only discuss the first term. By Plancherel theorem we have

\begin{split}&\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\tilde{H}_{|\nabla|^{\beta},|\nabla|^{\alpha}}(\Delta_{j}f,\Delta^{j-4}g)\,\Delta_{k}\psi\\
=&c\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}k(\xi,\eta)\,\widehat{\Delta_{j}f}(\eta)\,\widehat{\Delta^{j-4}g}(\xi-\eta)\,\widehat{\Delta_{k}\psi}(\xi)\\
\end{split}

Here
k
is the symbol of the operator
\tilde{H}_{|\nabla|^{\beta},|\nabla|^{\alpha}}
given by

\begin{split}k(\xi,\eta):=&|\xi|^{\beta}\left(|\xi|^{\alpha}-|\eta|^{\alpha}-|\xi-\eta|^{\alpha}\right)\\
&-|\eta|^{\beta}\left(|\xi|^{\alpha}-|\eta|^{\alpha}-|\xi-\eta|^{\alpha}\right)\\
&-|\xi-\eta|^{\beta}\left(|\xi|^{\alpha}-|\eta|^{\alpha}-|\xi-\eta|^{\alpha}\right)\\
=&\left(|\xi|^{\beta}-|\eta|^{\beta}-|\xi-\eta|^{\beta}\right)\,\left(|\xi|^{\alpha}-|\eta|^{\alpha}-|\xi-\eta|^{\alpha}\right)\\
\end{split}

We observe that by the support of the Littlewood-Paley projection operators
\Delta_{j}
and
\Delta^{j-4}
,
in the integral above we have
|\xi-\eta|\leq\frac{1}{2}|\eta|
. By a Taylor expansion,

\begin{split}k(\xi,\eta)=|\xi-\eta|^{\alpha+\beta}\left(1+\sum_{\ell=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{\ell!}m_{\ell}(\eta)n_{\ell}(\xi-\eta)\,|\xi-\eta|^{\ell}|\eta|^{-\ell}\right)\end{split}

where
m_{\ell}(\eta)
and
n_{\ell}(\eta)
are zero homogeneous functions. Now we observe that for any
\theta\geq 0

\begin{split}&\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\sum_{k\approx j}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|\xi-\eta|^{\alpha+\beta+\theta}\,|\eta|^{-\theta}\,\widehat{\Delta_{j}f}(\eta)\,\widehat{\Delta^{j-4}g}(\xi-\eta)\,\widehat{\Delta_{k}\psi}(\xi)\\
=&c\,\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\sum_{k\approx j}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\mathscr{I}_{\theta}\Delta_{j}f\ |\nabla|^{\alpha+\beta+\theta}\Delta^{j-4}g\,\Delta_{k}\psi\\
\lesssim&\left\|\left(\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\left(2^{j(\gamma+\theta)}\mathscr{I}_{\theta}\Delta_{j}f\right)^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\right\|_{L^{p_{1}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\ \left\|\sup_{j}2^{-j(\gamma+\theta)}|\nabla|^{\alpha+\beta+\theta}\Delta^{j-4}g\right\|_{L^{p_{2}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\,\left\|\left(\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\left(\Delta_{k}\psi\right)^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\right\|_{L^{p^{\prime}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\\
\approx&[f]_{\dot{F}^{\gamma}_{p_{1},2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\ \left\|\sup_{j}2^{-j(\gamma+\theta)}|\nabla|^{\alpha+\beta+\theta}\Delta^{j-4}g\right\|_{L^{p_{2}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\,\|\psi\|_{L^{p^{\prime}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}.\end{split}

In the last inequality we used the Littlewood-Paley theorem,
[
5
, Theorem 1.3.8.]
.
Since
\gamma+\theta>0

\begin{split}&2^{-j(\gamma+\theta)}|\nabla|^{\alpha+\beta+\theta}\Delta^{j-4}g\\
\leq&\sum_{\ell\leq j-4}2^{-\ell(\gamma+\theta)}|\nabla|^{\alpha+\beta+\theta}\Delta_{\ell}g\,2^{(\ell-j)(\gamma+\theta)}\\
\lesssim&\left(\sum_{\ell\leq j-4}\left(2^{-\ell(\gamma+\theta)}|\nabla|^{\alpha+\beta+\theta}\Delta_{\ell}g\right)^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\\
\end{split}

and thus we actually have

\begin{split}&\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\sum_{k\approx j}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|\xi-\eta|^{\alpha+\beta+\theta}\,|\eta|^{-\theta}\,\widehat{\Delta_{j}f}(\eta)\,\widehat{\Delta^{j-4}g}(\xi-\eta)\,\widehat{\Delta_{k}\psi}(\xi)\\
\lesssim&\left\|f\right\|_{\dot{F}^{\gamma}_{p_{1},2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\ \left\|g\right\|_{\dot{F}^{\alpha+\beta-\gamma}_{p_{2},2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\,\left\|\psi\right\|_{\dot{F}^{0}_{p^{\prime},2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\\
\lesssim&\||\nabla|^{\gamma}f\|_{L^{p_{1}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\,\||\nabla|^{\alpha+\beta-\gamma}g\|_{L^{p_{2}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}.\end{split}

With this method we can estimate each of the terms in (
2.4
) and obtain the claim.

∎

The next result is very similar to the estimate of
Lemma

2.7
(which indeed can be proven with the techniques of the following lemma).

Lemma 2.9
.

Assume
s\in(0,1]
and
\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2}\in(0,s)
such that
\sum_{i=1}^{2}\alpha_{i}=s
. Let
p\in(1,\infty)
. Assume for
p_{1},p_{2}\in[2,\infty)
such that

(2.5)

\frac{1}{p_{1}}+\frac{1}{p_{2}}=\frac{1}{p}.

Then we have

\begin{split}&\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\frac{\left\lvert f(x)-f(y)\right\rvert\,\left\lvert g(x)-g(y)\right\rvert}{|x-y|^{d+s}}dy\right)^{p}dx\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}\\
\lesssim&\||\nabla|^{\alpha_{1}}f\|_{L^{p_{1}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\,\||\nabla|^{\alpha_{2}}g\|_{L^{p_{2}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\,\\
\end{split}

Proof.

From Hölder’s inequality

\begin{split}&\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\frac{\left\lvert f(x)-f(y)\right\rvert\,\left\lvert g(x)-g(y)\right\rvert}{|x-y|^{d+s}}dy\right)^{p}dx\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}\\
\lesssim&\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\frac{\left\lvert f(x)-f(y)\right\rvert^{2}}{|x-y|^{d+2\alpha_{1}}}dy\right)^{p_{1}}dx\right)^{\frac{1}{p_{1}}}\ \left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\frac{\left\lvert g(x)-g(y)\right\rvert^{2}}{|x-y|^{d+2\alpha_{2}}}dy\right)^{p_{2}}dx\right)^{\frac{1}{p_{2}}}\\
\end{split}

Since
p_{i}\geq 2
we have by the results in
[
15
]
, denoting by
\dot{F}
the homogeneous Triebel-Lizorkin space, cf.
[
17
]
, and Littlewood-Paley theorem,
[
5
, Theorem 1.3.8.]
,

\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\frac{\left\lvert h(x)-h(y)\right\rvert^{2}}{|x-y|^{d+2\alpha_{i}}}dy\right)^{p_{i}}dx\right)^{\frac{1}{p_{1}}}\approx[h]_{\dot{F}^{\alpha_{i},p_{i}}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\approx\||\nabla|^{\alpha_{i}}h\|_{L^{p_{i}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}.

We can conclude.
∎

We will need Leibniz-rule estimates involving three terms, the basis of which is the following Lemma.

Lemma 2.10
.

Assume
\alpha_{i}\in(0,1)
such that
\sum_{i=1}^{3}\alpha_{i}=1
and let
p\in(1,\infty)
. Assume for
p_{i}\in(p,\infty)
such that

\frac{1}{p_{1}}+\frac{1}{p_{2}}+\frac{1}{p_{3}}=\frac{1}{p},

and

(2.6)

\frac{1}{p_{i}}-\frac{\alpha_{i}}{d}<\frac{1}{2},\quad i=1,2,3.

(Observe the previous assumptions are trivially satisfied if
p_{i}\geq 2
).

Then we have

\begin{split}&\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\frac{\left\lvert f(x)-f(y)\right\rvert\,\left\lvert g(x)-g(y)\right\rvert\left\lvert h(x)-h(y)\right\rvert}{|x-y|^{d+1}}dy\right)^{p}dx\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}\\
\lesssim&\||\nabla|^{\alpha_{1}}f\|_{L^{p_{1}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\,\||\nabla|^{\alpha_{2}}g\|_{L^{p_{2}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\,\||\nabla|^{\alpha_{3}}h\|_{L^{p_{3}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}.\end{split}

Proof.

Since

\sum_{i=1}^{3}\frac{d-\alpha_{i}p_{i}}{dp_{i}}<\frac{1}{p_{1}}+\frac{1}{p_{2}}+\frac{1}{p_{3}}=\frac{1}{p}<1

in view of (
2.6
) we can find
q_{i}\in[2,\frac{dp_{i}}{d-\alpha_{i}p_{i}})
(if
p_{i}>\frac{d}{\alpha_{i}}
we pick
q_{i}\in[2,\infty)
) such that

\frac{1}{q_{1}}+\frac{1}{q_{2}}+\frac{1}{q_{3}}=1.

Then

\begin{split}&\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\frac{\left\lvert f(x)-f(y)\right\rvert\,\left\lvert g(x)-g(y)\right\rvert\left\lvert h(x)-h(y)\right\rvert}{|x-y|^{d+1}}dy\\
\lesssim&\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\frac{\left\lvert f(x)-f(y)\right\rvert^{q_{1}}}{|x-y|^{d+\alpha_{1}q_{1}}}dy\right)^{\frac{1}{q_{1}}}\,\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\frac{\left\lvert g(x)-g(y)\right\rvert^{q_{2}}}{|x-y|^{d+\alpha_{2}q_{2}}}dy\right)^{\frac{1}{q_{2}}}\,\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\frac{\left\lvert h(x)-h(y)\right\rvert^{q_{3}}}{|x-y|^{d+\alpha_{3}q_{3}}}dy\right)^{\frac{1}{q_{3}}}\end{split}

From another application of Hölder’s inequality,

\begin{split}&\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\frac{\left\lvert f(x)-f(y)\right\rvert\,\left\lvert g(x)-g(y)\right\rvert\left\lvert h(x)-h(y)\right\rvert}{|x-y|^{d+1}}dy\right)^{p}dx\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}\\
\lesssim&[f]_{W^{\alpha_{1},p_{1}}_{q_{1}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\,[g]_{W^{\alpha_{2},p_{2}}_{q_{2}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\,[h]_{W^{\alpha_{3},p_{3}}_{q_{3}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}.\end{split}

Here the
W^{\alpha,p}_{q}
-seminorm for
\alpha\in(0,1)
and
p,q\in(1,\infty)
is defined as

[f]_{W^{\alpha,p}_{q}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}=\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\frac{|f(x)-f(y)|^{q}}{|x-y|^{d+sq}}dy\right)^{\frac{p}{q}}\,dx\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}.

Our choice for
q_{i}
ensures
p_{i}>\frac{dq_{i}}{d+\alpha_{i}q_{i}}
, so we have by the results in
[
15
]
,

[f]_{W^{\alpha_{i},p_{i}}_{q_{i}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\approx[f]_{\dot{F}^{\alpha_{i},p_{i}}_{q_{i}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})},

where
\dot{F}
denotes the homogeneous Triebel-Lizorkin space. That is, we have

\begin{split}&\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\frac{\left\lvert f(x)-f(y)\right\rvert\,\left\lvert g(x)-g(y)\right\rvert\left\lvert h(y)-h(y)\right\rvert}{|x-y|^{d+1}}dy\right)^{p}dx\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}\\
\lesssim&[f]_{\dot{F}^{\alpha_{1},p_{1}}_{q_{1}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\,[g]_{\dot{F}^{\alpha_{2},p_{2}}_{q_{2}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\,[h]_{\dot{F}^{\alpha_{3},p_{3}}_{q_{3}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}.\end{split}

Since
q_{i}\geq 2
we have, cf.
[
17
]
,

[f]_{\dot{F}^{\alpha_{i},p_{i}}_{q_{i}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\lesssim[f]_{\dot{F}^{\alpha_{i},p_{i}}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\approx\||\nabla|^{\alpha_{i}}f\|_{L^{p_{i}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}.

Thus, we have established the claim and can conclude.
∎

We now state a version similar to
Lemma

2.10
but for
\alpha_{3}<0
.

Lemma 2.11
.

Assume
\alpha_{i}\in(0,1)
,
i=1,2
such that
\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2}>1
. If
d=1
assume moreover that
\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2}-1<\frac{d}{2}
. Assume for
p_{i}\in(2,\infty)
such that
\frac{1}{p_{1}}+\frac{1}{p_{2}}+\frac{1}{p_{3}}=\frac{1}{2}
and
\frac{dp_{3}}{d-(1-\alpha_{1}-\alpha_{2})p_{3}}\in(1,\infty)
. Then we have

\begin{split}&\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\frac{\left\lvert f(x)-f(y)\right\rvert\,\left\lvert g(x)-g(y)\right\rvert\left\lvert h(y)\right\rvert}{|x-y|^{d+1}}dy\right)^{2}dx\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\\
\lesssim&\||\nabla|^{\alpha_{1}}f\|_{L^{p_{1}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\,\||\nabla|^{\alpha_{2}}g\|_{L^{p_{2}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\,\|h\|_{L^{\frac{dp_{3}}{d+(\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2}-1)p_{3}}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}.\end{split}

Proof.

As in
Lemma

2.10
, since
p_{i}>2
for
i=1,2
we can choose
q_{1}=p_{1}
and
q_{2}=p_{2}
and set

q_{3}=\frac{2p_{3}}{p_{3}+2}\in(p_{3},\infty).

Set
-\alpha_{3}:=1-\alpha_{1}-\alpha_{2}<0
. Then
\alpha_{3}\in(0,1)
. By the same argument as in the proof of
Lemma

2.10
,

\begin{split}&\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\frac{\left\lvert f(x)-f(y)\right\rvert\,\left\lvert g(x)-g(y)\right\rvert\left\lvert h(y)\right\rvert}{|x-y|^{d+1}}dy\\
\lesssim&\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\frac{\left\lvert f(x)-f(y)\right\rvert^{q_{1}}}{|x-y|^{d+\alpha_{1}q_{1}}}dy\right)^{\frac{1}{q_{1}}}\,\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\frac{\left\lvert g(x)-g(y)\right\rvert^{q_{2}}}{|x-y|^{d+\alpha_{2}q_{2}}}dy\right)^{\frac{1}{q_{2}}}\,\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\frac{\left\lvert h(y)\right\rvert^{q_{3}}}{|x-y|^{d-\alpha_{3}q_{3}}}dy\right)^{\frac{1}{q_{3}}}\\
\approx&\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\frac{\left\lvert f(x)-f(y)\right\rvert^{q_{1}}}{|x-y|^{d+\alpha_{1}q_{1}}}dy\right)^{\frac{1}{q_{1}}}\,\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\frac{\left\lvert g(x)-g(y)\right\rvert^{q_{2}}}{|x-y|^{d+\alpha_{2}q_{2}}}dy\right)^{\frac{1}{q_{2}}}\,\left(\mathscr{I}_{\alpha_{3}q_{3}}\left(\left\lvert h\right\rvert^{q_{3}}\right)(x)\right)^{\frac{1}{q_{3}}}.\end{split}

So, with the same Hölder inequality and embedding theorems as in
Lemma

2.10
,

\begin{split}&\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\frac{\left\lvert f(x)-f(y)\right\rvert\,\left\lvert g(x)-g(y)\right\rvert\left\lvert h(y)-h(y)\right\rvert}{|x-y|^{d+1}}dy\right)^{2}dx\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\\
\lesssim&\||\nabla|^{\alpha_{1}}f\|_{L^{p_{1}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\,\||\nabla|^{\alpha_{2}}g\|_{L^{p_{1}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\,\|\mathscr{I}_{\alpha_{3}q_{3}}\left(\left\lvert h\right\rvert^{q_{3}}\right)^{\frac{1}{q_{3}}}\|_{L^{p_{3}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}.\end{split}

We now observe that by Sobolev inequality,
Lemma

2.2
,

\begin{split}\|\mathscr{I}_{\alpha_{3}q_{3}}\left(\left\lvert h\right\rvert^{q_{3}}\right)^{\frac{1}{q_{3}}}\|_{L^{p_{3}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}=&\|\mathscr{I}_{\alpha_{3}q_{3}}\left(\left\lvert h\right\rvert^{q_{3}}\right)\|_{L^{\frac{p_{3}}{q_{3}}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{\frac{1}{q_{3}}}\\
\lesssim&\|\left\lvert h\right\rvert^{q_{3}}\|_{L^{\frac{dp_{3}}{q_{3}d+\alpha_{3}p_{3}q_{3}}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{\frac{1}{q_{3}}}\\
=&\|h\|_{L^{\frac{dp_{3}}{d+\alpha_{3}p_{3}}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}.\end{split}

The above is correct as long as
\frac{dp_{3}}{q_{3}d+\alpha_{3}p_{3}q_{3}}\in(1,\infty)
.
We see that if
\alpha_{3}<\frac{d}{2}
this is satisfied and can conclude.
∎

3.
Decay estimate in time

In this section we prove the main estimate for
Theorem

1.1
which is

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
u,v:[0,T]\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
be smooth solutions to the half-wave map equation (
1.1
). Set

\mathcal{E}(t):=\frac{1}{2}\left(\|D_{x}({\bf u}-{\bf v})(t)\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{2}+\|\partial_{t}({\bf u}-{\bf v})(t)\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{2}\right)

Then, for any
\alpha>1
,

\dot{\mathcal{E}}(t)\leq\Sigma(t)\mathcal{E}(t),

where for any
\alpha>1
we can estimate

(3.1)

\begin{split}\Sigma(t)\lesssim&\||\nabla|^{\alpha}{\bf u}(t)\|_{L^{\frac{2d}{2\alpha-1}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{2}+\||\nabla|^{\alpha}{\bf v}(t)\|_{L^{\frac{2d}{2\alpha-1}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{2}\\
&+\||\nabla|{}{\bf u}(t)\|_{L^{(2d,2)}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{2}+\||\nabla|{}{\bf v}(t)\|_{L^{(2d,2)}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{2}\\
\end{split}

It remains to prove
Theorem

3.1
.

3.1.
Proof of Theorem
3.1

We observe

\begin{split}\dot{\mathcal{E}}(t)=&\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\left(\|\nabla({\bf u}-{\bf v})\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{2}+\|\partial_{t}({\bf u}-{\bf v})\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{2}\right)\\
=&\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\langle\partial_{tt}{\bf w}-\Delta{\bf w},\partial_{t}{\bf w}\rangle\\
\end{split}

So what we need to do is multiply the equation for
\partial_{tt}{\bf w}-\Delta{\bf w}
with
\partial_{t}{\bf w}
. From the equation (
1.1
) for
u
and
v
, respectively, we find that

(3.2)

\begin{split}\partial_{tt}{\bf w}-\Delta{\bf w}=&{\bf u}|\nabla{\bf u}|^{2}-{\bf v}|\nabla{\bf v}|^{2}\\
&+{\bf v}||\nabla|{}{\bf v}|^{2}-{\bf u}||\nabla|{}{\bf u}|^{2}\\
&+|\nabla|{}{\bf u}\,\left(\left\langle{\bf u},|\nabla|{}{\bf u}\right\rangle\right)-|\nabla|{}{\bf v}\,\left({\bf v}\cdot|\nabla|{}{\bf v}\right)\\
&+{\bf u}\wedge[|\nabla|{},{\bf u}\wedge](|\nabla|{}{\bf u})-{\bf v}\wedge[|\nabla|{},{\bf v}\wedge](|\nabla|{}{\bf v}).\end{split}

Here we recall the commutator notation

[T,f](g)=T(fg)-fT(g).

We will prove the estimate of
Theorem

3.1
by estimating each line in (
3.2
), which will become increasingly more challenging, the last line being the most involved estimate. Having said that, the difficulties are mostly of algebraic nature, and the actual estimates rely on the fractional Leibniz rule discussed in
Section

2
.

Repeating estimates

Throughout the remainder of the section we will use
Lemma

2.1
implicitly – without further mentioning.

Moreover, observe that for
\frac{1}{2}<\alpha_{1}<\alpha_{2}<d+\frac{1}{2}
we have from Sobolev embedding,
Lemma

2.2
,

\||\nabla|^{\alpha_{1}}{\bf u}\|_{L^{\frac{2d}{2\alpha_{1}-1}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\lesssim\||\nabla|^{\alpha_{2}}{\bf u}\|_{L^{\frac{2d}{2\alpha_{2}-1}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}

In particular for any
\alpha\in[1,d+\frac{1}{2})
,

\||\nabla|{\bf u}\|_{L^{2d}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\lesssim\||\nabla|^{\alpha}{\bf u}\|_{L^{\frac{2d}{2\alpha-1}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}

This will be also used frequently and implicitly – in particular to obtain the estimate in
Theorem

3.1
from the lemmata below.

Estimating the first line of (
3.2
)

We begin with the following estimate which is proven in Shatah-Struwe
[
19
]
.

Lemma 3.2
.

For
d\geq 3
,

\begin{split}&\left\lvert\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\langle{\bf u}\,|\nabla{\bf u}|^{2}-{\bf v}\,|\nabla{\bf v}|^{2},\,\partial_{t}({\bf u}-{\bf v})\rangle\right\rvert\\
\lesssim&\left(\|\nabla({\bf u}-{\bf v})\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{2}+\|\partial_{t}({\bf u}-{\bf v})\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{2}\right)\,\left(\||\nabla|{\bf u}\|_{L^{2d}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{2}+\||\nabla|{\bf v}\|_{L^{2d}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{2}\right)\end{split}

Estimating the Second line of (
3.2
)

In a similar spirit to
Lemma

3.2
we can also obtain

Lemma 3.3
.

For
d\geq 3
we have

\begin{split}&\left\lvert\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\langle{\bf u}||\nabla|{}{\bf u}|^{2}-{\bf v}||\nabla|{}{\bf v}|^{2},\,\partial_{t}({\bf u}-{\bf v})\rangle\right\rvert\\
\lesssim&\left(\|\nabla({\bf u}-{\bf v})\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{2}+\|\partial_{t}({\bf u}-{\bf v})\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{2}\right)\,\left(\||\nabla|{\bf u}\|_{L^{2d}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{2}+\||\nabla|{\bf v}\|_{L^{2d}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{2}\right)\end{split}

Proof.

We split

(3.3)

\begin{split}&{\bf u}||\nabla|{}{\bf u}|^{2}-{\bf v}||\nabla|{}{\bf v}|^{2}\\
=&({\bf u}-{\bf v})||\nabla|{}{\bf u}|^{2}+{\bf v}\langle|\nabla|{}({\bf u}-{\bf v}),|\nabla|{}{\bf u}\rangle+{\bf v}\langle|\nabla|{}{\bf v},|\nabla|{}({\bf u}-{\bf v})\rangle\end{split}

From Hölder’s inequality and Sobolev inequality,
Lemma

2.2
,

\begin{split}&\left\lvert\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}||\nabla|{}{\bf u}|^{2}\langle{\bf u}-{\bf v},\partial_{t}({\bf u}-{\bf v})\rangle\right\rvert\\
\lesssim&\||\nabla|{}{\bf u}\|_{L^{2d}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{2}\|{\bf u}-{\bf v}\|_{L^{\frac{2d}{d-2}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\,\|\partial_{t}({\bf u}-{\bf v})\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\\
\lesssim&\||\nabla|{}{\bf u}\|_{L^{2d}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{2}\|\nabla({\bf u}-{\bf v})\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\,\|\partial_{t}({\bf u}-{\bf v})\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\\
\lesssim&\||\nabla|{}{\bf u}\|_{L^{2d}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{2}\left(\|\nabla({\bf u}-{\bf v})\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{2}+\|\partial_{t}({\bf u}-{\bf v})\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{2}\right).\end{split}

This provides the desired estimate for the first term in (
3.3
).

The second and third term in (
3.3
) are very similar, we only estimate the second one. Here we use the trick from
[
19
]
that they used to obtain
Lemma

3.2
: Since
{\bf u}\cdot\partial_{t}{\bf u}=0
and
{\bf v}\cdot\partial_{t}{\bf v}=0

(3.4)

\langle{\bf v},\partial_{t}({\bf u}-{\bf v})\rangle=-\langle{\bf u}-{\bf v},\partial_{t}{\bf u}\rangle.

Using that
u
solves the half-wave map equation (
1.1
) and
|{\bf u}|\equiv 1
we conclude

\left\lvert\langle{\bf v},\partial_{t}({\bf u}-{\bf v})\rangle\right\rvert\lesssim\left\lvert{\bf u}-{\bf v}\right\rvert\,||\nabla|{}{\bf u}|.

Thus using Hölder inequality and Sobolev inequality,
Lemma

2.2
, as before,

\begin{split}&\left\lvert\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\langle|\nabla|{}({\bf u}-{\bf v}),|\nabla|{}{\bf u}\rangle\langle{\bf v},\partial_{t}({\bf u}-{\bf v})\rangle\right\rvert\\
\leq&\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}||\nabla|{}({\bf u}-{\bf v})|\,||\nabla|{}{\bf u}|\,\left\lvert{\bf u}-{\bf v}\right\rvert\,||\nabla|{}{\bf u}|\\
\lesssim&\||\nabla|{}{\bf u}\|_{L^{2d}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{2}\,\|\nabla({\bf u}-{\bf v})\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\,\|\nabla({\bf u}-{\bf v})\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\\
\approx&\|\nabla{\bf u}\|_{L^{2d}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{2}\,\|\nabla({\bf u}-{\bf v})\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{2}.\end{split}

∎

Estimating the third line of (
3.2
)

We recall our notation for the Leibniz rule operator

H_{T}(a,b)=T(ab)-aTb-(Ta)b.

Observe that since
|{\bf u}|^{2}\equiv 1
we have

(3.5)

\left\langle{\bf u},|\nabla|{}{\bf u}\right\rangle=-\frac{1}{2}H_{|\nabla|{}}({\bf u}\cdot,{\bf u})\equiv-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i=1}^{3}\,H_{|\nabla|{}}(u^{i},u^{i})

So we consider

(3.6)

\begin{split}&|\nabla|{}{\bf u}\,\left\langle{\bf u},|\nabla|{}{\bf u}\right\rangle-|\nabla|{}{\bf v}\,\left\langle{\bf v},|\nabla|{}{\bf v}\right\rangle\\
=&-\frac{1}{2}\left(|\nabla|{}{\bf u}\,H_{|\nabla|{}}({\bf u}\cdot,{\bf u})-|\nabla|{}{\bf v}\,H_{|\nabla|{}}({\bf v}\cdot,v)\right)\\
=&-\frac{1}{2}|\nabla|{}({\bf u}-{\bf v})\,H_{|\nabla|{}}({\bf u}\cdot,{\bf u})\\
&-\frac{1}{2}|\nabla|{}{\bf v}\,H_{|\nabla|{}}(({\bf u}-{\bf v})\cdot,{\bf u})\\
&-\frac{1}{2}|\nabla|{}{\bf v}\,H_{|\nabla|{}}({\bf v}\cdot,{\bf u}-{\bf v})\end{split}

Regarding the first term in (
3.6
) we observe that this is a more complicated structure to estimate, since two terms including
{\bf u}-{\bf v}
appear to the full differential order, and we are not aware of a trick in the spirit of
[
19
]
that would change that. Instead we use the commutator structure of
H_{|\nabla|{}}(\cdot,\cdot)
.

Lemma 3.4
.

For
d\geq 2
we have

\begin{split}&\left\lvert\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\langle|\nabla|{}({\bf u}-{\bf v}),\partial_{t}({\bf u}-{\bf v})\rangle H_{|\nabla|{}}({\bf u}\cdot,{\bf u})\right\rvert\\
\lesssim&\|\nabla({\bf u}-{\bf v})\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\,\|\partial_{t}({\bf u}-{\bf v})\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\,\||\nabla|{\bf u}\|_{L^{2d,2}}^{2}.\end{split}

Proof.

By Hölder’s inequality,

\left\lvert\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\langle|\nabla|{}({\bf u}-{\bf v}),\partial_{t}({\bf u}-{\bf v})\rangle H_{|\nabla|{}}({\bf u}\cdot,{\bf u})\right\rvert\lesssim\|\nabla({\bf u}-{\bf v})\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\,\|\partial_{t}({\bf u}-{\bf v})\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\|H_{|\nabla|{}}({\bf u}\cdot,{\bf u})\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}.

By (
2.9
) we have

\|H_{|\nabla|{}}({\bf u}\cdot,{\bf u})\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\lesssim\||\nabla|{}{\bf u}\|_{L^{2d,2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{2}.

∎

Also the second and third term of (
3.6
) are relatively straight-forward to estimate using the commutator structure of
H_{|\nabla|}
.

Lemma 3.5
.

For
d\geq 2
, we have

\begin{split}&\left\lvert\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}H_{|\nabla|{}}(({\bf u}-{\bf v})\cdot,{\bf u})\,\langle|\nabla|{}{\bf v},\partial_{t}({\bf u}-{\bf v})\rangle\right\rvert\\
&+\left\lvert\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}H_{|\nabla|{}}({\bf v}\cdot,{\bf u}-{\bf v})\,\langle|\nabla|{}{\bf v},\partial_{t}({\bf u}-{\bf v})\rangle\right\rvert\\
\lesssim&\left(\|\nabla({\bf u}-{\bf v})\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{2}+\|\partial_{t}({\bf u}-{\bf v})\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{2}\right)\,\left(\|\nabla{\bf u}\|_{L^{2d}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}+\|\nabla{\bf v}\|_{L^{2d}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\right)^{2}.\end{split}

Proof.

We only consider the first term, the second follows from the same argument. By Hölder’s inequality, and (
2.7
),

\begin{split}&\left\lvert\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}H_{|\nabla|{}}(({\bf u}-{\bf v})\cdot,{\bf u})\,\langle|\nabla|{}{\bf v},\partial_{t}({\bf u}-{\bf v})\rangle\right\rvert\\
\lesssim&\|\partial_{t}({\bf u}-{\bf v})\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\||\nabla|{}{\bf v}\|_{L^{2d}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\,\|H_{|\nabla|{}}(({\bf u}-{\bf v})\cdot,{\bf u})\|_{L^{\frac{2d}{d-1}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\\
\overset{\eqref{eq:comm:4}}{\lesssim}&\|\partial_{t}({\bf u}-{\bf v})\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\||\nabla|{}{\bf v}\|_{L^{2d}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\,\|||\nabla|{}({\bf u}-{\bf v})|\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\,\||\nabla|{}{\bf u}\|_{L^{2d}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\end{split}

We can conclude since
\||\nabla|{}({\bf u}-{\bf v})\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\approx\|\nabla({\bf u}-{\bf v})\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}
.
∎

Estimating the last line of (
3.2
)

We still need to understand the estimates for

(3.7)

\begin{split}&{\bf u}\wedge[|\nabla|{},{\bf u}\wedge](|\nabla|{}{\bf u})-{\bf v}\wedge[|\nabla|{},{\bf v}\wedge](|\nabla|{}{\bf v})\\
=&({\bf u}-{\bf v})\wedge[|\nabla|{},{\bf u}\wedge](|\nabla|{}{\bf u})\\
&+{\bf v}\wedge\left([|\nabla|{},({\bf u}-{\bf v})\wedge](|\nabla|{}{\bf u})\right)\\
&+{\bf v}\wedge\left([|\nabla|{},{\bf v}\wedge](|\nabla|{}({\bf u}-{\bf v}))\right)\\
\end{split}

We observe that we can estimate the first term of (
3.7
) assuming a bound on
|\nabla|^{\alpha}{\bf u}
and
|\nabla|^{\alpha}{\bf v}
for an arbitrarily small
\alpha>1
.

Lemma 3.6
.

For
d\geq 3
, and any
\alpha\in(1,d+\frac{1}{2})
we have

\left\lvert\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\langle({\bf u}-{\bf v})\wedge[|\nabla|{},{\bf u}\wedge](|\nabla|{}{\bf u}),\partial_{t}({\bf u}-{\bf v})\rangle\right\rvert\lesssim\left(\|\nabla({\bf u}-{\bf v})\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{2}+\|\partial_{t}({\bf u}-{\bf v})\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{2}\right)\,\||\nabla|^{\alpha}{\bf u}\|_{L^{\frac{2d}{2\alpha-1}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{2}.

Proof.

As before, by Cauchy-Schwarz and Hölder’s inequality

\begin{split}&\left\lvert\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\langle({\bf u}-{\bf v})\wedge[|\nabla|{},{\bf u}\wedge](|\nabla|{}{\bf u}),\partial_{t}({\bf u}-{\bf v})\rangle\right\rvert\\
\lesssim&\|{\bf u}-{\bf v}\|_{L^{\frac{2d}{d-2}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\,\|\partial_{t}({\bf u}-{\bf v})\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\,\left\|[|\nabla|{},{\bf u}\wedge](|\nabla|{}{\bf u})\right\|_{L^{d}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\\
\lesssim&\left(\|\nabla({\bf u}-{\bf v})\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{2}+\|\partial_{t}({\bf u}-{\bf v})\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{2}\right)\,\left\|[|\nabla|{},{\bf u}\wedge](|\nabla|{}{\bf u})\right\|_{L^{d}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}.\end{split}

In the last line we used Sobolev embedding,
Lemma

2.2
. It remains to estimate the commutator term. Observe that
|\nabla|{}{\bf u}\wedge|\nabla|{}{\bf u}=0
and thus we can write

[|\nabla|{},{\bf u}\wedge](|\nabla|{}{\bf u})=H_{|\nabla|{}}({\bf u}\wedge,|\nabla|{}{\bf u}).

We apply commutator theory, more precisely (
2.2
), and find that for any
\sigma\in(1/2,1)

\begin{split}\|[|\nabla|{},{\bf u}\wedge](|\nabla|{}{\bf u})\|_{L^{d}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\lesssim&\||\nabla|^{\sigma}{\bf u}\|_{L^{\frac{2d}{2\sigma-1}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\,\||\nabla|^{2-\sigma}{\bf u}\|_{L^{\frac{2d}{2(2-\sigma)-1}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\\
\lesssim&\||\nabla|^{2-\sigma}{\bf u}\|_{L^{\frac{2d}{2(2-\sigma)-1}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{2}.\end{split}

The last line is Sobolev embedding,
Lemma

2.2
. Observe that if we set
\alpha:=2-\sigma
then
\alpha>1
and
\alpha\approx 1
if
\sigma\approx 1
, and we can conclude.
∎

Next we consider the term
{\bf v}\wedge\left([|\nabla|{},({\bf u}-{\bf v})\wedge](|\nabla|{}{\bf u})\right)
in (
3.7
).

We observe

(3.8)

\begin{split}{\bf v}\wedge\left([|\nabla|{},({\bf u}-{\bf v})\wedge](|\nabla|{}{\bf u})\right)=&{\bf v}\wedge\left(H_{|\nabla|}(({\bf u}-{\bf v})\wedge,|\nabla|{\bf u})\right)\\
&+{\bf v}\wedge\left(|\nabla|({\bf u}-{\bf v})\wedge|\nabla|{\bf u}\right)\\
\end{split}

We first establish the following estimate which estimates the first term on the right-hand side in (
3.8
)

Lemma 3.7
.

For any
\alpha\in(1,d+\frac{1}{2})
, and any
d\geq 3
,

\|{\bf v}\wedge\left(H_{|\nabla|{}}\left(({\bf u}-{\bf v})\wedge,|\nabla|{}{\bf u}\right)\right)\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\lesssim\|\nabla({\bf u}-{\bf v})\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\,\left(\||\nabla|^{\alpha}{\bf u}\|_{L^{\frac{2d}{2\alpha-1}}}^{2}+\||\nabla|^{\alpha}{\bf v}\|_{L^{\frac{2d}{2\alpha-1}}}^{2}\right)

in particular

\begin{split}&\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left\langle{\bf v}\wedge\left(H_{|\nabla|{}}\left(({\bf u}-{\bf v})\wedge,|\nabla|{}{\bf u}\right)\right),\partial_{t}({\bf u}-{\bf v})\right\rangle\\
\lesssim&\||\nabla|{}({\bf u}-{\bf v})\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\,\left(\||\nabla|^{\alpha}{\bf u}\|_{L^{\frac{2d}{2\alpha-1}}}^{2}+\||\nabla|^{\alpha}{\bf v}\|_{L^{\frac{2d}{2\alpha-1}}}^{2}\right)\,\|\partial_{t}({\bf u}-{\bf v})\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\end{split}

Proof.

We recall the formula

{\bf a}\wedge({\bf b}\wedge{\bf c})={\bf b}({\bf c}\cdot{\bf a})-{\bf c}({\bf a}\cdot{\bf b})

so

(3.9)

\begin{split}&\left({\bf v}\wedge\left(H_{|\nabla|{}}\left(({\bf u}-{\bf v})\wedge,|\nabla|{}{\bf u}\right)\right)\right)^{i}\\
=&\sum_{j=1}^{3}\left(v^{j}H_{|\nabla|{}}\left((u-v)^{i},|\nabla|{}u^{j}\right)\right)-\left(v^{j}H_{|\nabla|{}}\left((u-v)^{j},|\nabla|{}u^{i}\right)\right)\\
=&\sum_{j=1}^{3}(v-u)^{{j}}H_{|\nabla|{}}\left((u-v)^{i},|\nabla|{}u^{j}\right)\\
&-\frac{1}{2}{(u-v)^{j}H_{|\nabla|{}}\left((u-v)^{j},|\nabla|{}u^{i}\right)}\\
&+\frac{1}{2}\sum_{j=1}^{3}\left((u+v)^{j}H_{|\nabla|{}}\left((u-v)^{j},|\nabla|{}u^{i}\right)\right)\\
&+\sum_{j=1}^{3}\left(u^{{j}}H_{|\nabla|{}}\left((u-v)^{i},|\nabla|{}u^{j}\right)\right).\end{split}

Take any
\alpha\in(1,d+\frac{1}{2})
and set
\sigma:=2-\alpha
. Then
\frac{2d^{2}}{d-(1-\sigma)2d}\in(1,\infty)
, and by (
2.2
) we can estimate the first two terms of (
3.9
),

(3.10)

\begin{split}&\|\left\lvert{\bf v}-{\bf u}\right\rvert\left\lvert H_{|\nabla|{}}\left((u-v)^{i},|\nabla|{}u^{j}\right)\right\rvert\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\\
\lesssim&\|{\bf u}-{\bf v}\|_{L^{\frac{2d}{d-2}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\,\||\nabla|^{\sigma}(u-v)^{i}\|_{L^{\frac{2d^{2}}{d-(1-\sigma)2d}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}}\||\nabla|^{1+\sigma}u^{j}\|_{L^{\frac{2d}{2(2-\sigma)-1}}}\\
\lesssim&\||\nabla|{}({\bf u}-{\bf v})\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\,\left(\||\nabla|^{\alpha}{\bf u}\|_{L^{\frac{2d}{2\alpha-1}}}^{2}+\||\nabla|^{\alpha}{\bf v}\|_{L^{\frac{2d}{2\alpha-1}}}^{2}\right).\end{split}

For the third term in (
3.9
) observe that for fixed
i
, using
Lemma

2.5
,

\begin{split}&\sum_{j}\frac{1}{2}\left((u+v)^{j}H_{|\nabla|{}}\left((u-v)^{j},|\nabla|{}u^{i}\right)\right)(x)\\
=&c\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\frac{\left(|\nabla|{}u^{i}(x)-|\nabla|{}u^{i}(y)\right)\,\langle({\bf u+v})(x),\left(({\bf u}-{\bf v})(x)-({\bf u}-{\bf v})(y)\right)\rangle}{|x-y|^{d+1}}dy.\end{split}

Setting
{\bf a}:={\bf u+v}
and
{\bf b}:={\bf u}-{\bf v}
we recall that
{\bf a}(x)\cdot{\bf b}(x)=|{\bf u}(x)|^{2}-|{\bf v}(x)|^{2}=0
, and thus we can perform a discrete version of the trick in (
3.4
),

\begin{split}{\bf a}(x)\cdot({\bf b}(x)-{\bf b}(y))=&({\bf a}(x)-{\bf a}(y))\cdot({\bf b}(x)-{\bf b}(y))+{\bf a}(y)\cdot({\bf b}(x)-{\bf b}(y))\\
=&({\bf a}(x)-{\bf a}(y))\cdot({\bf b}(x)-{\bf b}(y))+{\bf a}(y)\cdot{\bf b}(x)\\
=&({\bf a}(x)-{\bf a}(y))\cdot({\bf b}(x)-{\bf b}(y))-\left({\bf a}(x)-{\bf a}(y)\right)\cdot{\bf b}(x).\end{split}

This implies that

\begin{split}&\sum_{j}\frac{1}{2}\left((u+v)^{j}H_{|\nabla|{}}\left((u-v)^{j},|\nabla|{}u^{i}\right)\right)(x)\\
=&-\sum_{j}\frac{1}{2}\left((u{-}v)^{j}H_{|\nabla|{}}\left((u{+}v)^{j},|\nabla|{}u^{i}\right)\right)(x)\\
&+c\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\frac{\left(|\nabla|{}u^{i}(x)-|\nabla|{}u^{i}(y)\right)\,\left\langle({\bf u+v})(x)-({\bf u+v})(y),{({\bf u}-{\bf v})(y)-({\bf u}-{\bf v})(y)}\right\rangle}{|x-y|^{d+1}}dy.\end{split}

For the first term, just as above for (
3.10
),

\left\|\frac{1}{2}(u-v)^{j}(x)H_{|\nabla|{}}\left((u{+}v)^{j},|\nabla|{}u^{i}\right)(x)\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\lesssim\||\nabla|{}({\bf u}-{\bf v})\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\,\left(\||\nabla|^{\alpha}{\bf u}\|_{L^{\frac{2d}{2\alpha-1}}}^{2}+\||\nabla|^{\alpha}{\bf v}\|_{L^{\frac{2d}{2\alpha-1}}}^{2}\right)

For the second term we use
Lemma

2.10
. Take
\sigma,\theta>0
such that
\sigma+\theta\in(0,1)
. Then, (if
d\geq 2
we can take
\sigma,\theta
small enough to make any of the norms below finite),

\begin{split}&\left\|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\frac{\left(|\nabla|{}u^{i}(x)-|\nabla|{}u^{i}(y)\right)\,\left\langle({\bf u}+{\bf v})(x)-({\bf u}+{\bf v})(y),({\bf u}-{\bf v})(y)-({\bf u}-{\bf v})(y)\right\rangle}{|x-y|^{d+1}}dy\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d},dx)}\\
\lesssim&\||\nabla|^{1+\sigma}u^{i}\|_{L^{\frac{2d}{2(1+\sigma)-1}}}\,\||\nabla|^{1-\theta-\sigma}({\bf u}+{\bf v})\|_{\frac{2d^{2}}{d-(\theta+\sigma)2d}}\,\||\nabla|^{\theta}({\bf u}-{\bf v})\|_{L^{\frac{d2}{d-(1-\theta)2}}}\\
\lesssim&\||\nabla|^{\alpha}u^{i}\|_{L^{\frac{2d}{2(\alpha)-1}}}\,\left(\||\nabla|{}{\bf u}\|_{L^{2d}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}+\||\nabla|{}{\bf v}\|_{L^{2d}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\right)\,\||\nabla|{}({\bf u}-{\bf v})\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}.\end{split}

This establishes the right estimate for the second term in (
3.9
).

For the last term in (
3.9
) it remains we consider, again using
Lemma

2.5
,

\begin{split}&\sum_{j=1}^{3}\left(u^{j}H_{|\nabla|{}}\left((u-v)^{i},|\nabla|{}u^{j}\right)\right)(x)\\
=&\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{\left((u-v)^{i}(x)-(u-v)^{i}(y)\right)\,\left\langle|\nabla|{}{\bf u}(x)-|\nabla|{}{\bf u}(y),{\bf u}(x)\right\rangle}{|x-y|^{d+1}}\,dy.\end{split}

Now we write

\begin{split}&\left\langle|\nabla|{}{\bf u}(x)-|\nabla|{}{\bf u}(y),{\bf u}(x)\right\rangle\\
=&\left\langle{\bf u}(x),|\nabla|{}{\bf u}(x)\right\rangle-\left\langle{\bf u}(y),|\nabla|{}{\bf u}(y)\right\rangle+\left\langle{\bf u}(y)-{\bf u}(x),|\nabla|{}{\bf u}(y)\right\rangle\\
=&\left\langle{\bf u}(x),|\nabla|{}{\bf u}(x)\right\rangle-\left\langle{\bf u}(y),|\nabla|{}{\bf u}(y),+\right\rangle\left\langle{\bf u}(y)-{\bf u}(x),|\nabla|{}{\bf u}(y)-|\nabla|{}{\bf u}(x)\right\rangle+\left\langle{\bf u}(y)-{\bf u}(x),|\nabla|{}{\bf u}(x)\right\rangle\\
=&\left\langle{\bf u}(x),|\nabla|{}{\bf u}(x)\right\rangle-\left\langle{\bf u}(y),|\nabla|{}{\bf u}(y)\right\rangle+\left\langle{\bf u}(x)-{\bf u}(y),|\nabla|{}{\bf u}(x)-|\nabla|{}{\bf u}(y)\right\rangle-\left\langle{\bf u}(x)-{\bf u}(y),|\nabla|{}{\bf u}(x)\right\rangle\\
\end{split}

Thus we have for
i=1,2,3
,

(3.11)

\begin{split}&\sum_{j=1}^{3}{u^{j}H_{|\nabla|{}}\left((u-v)^{i},|\nabla|{}u^{j}\right)}(x)\\
=&H_{|\nabla|{}}\left((u-v)^{i},\left\langle{\bf u},|\nabla|{}{\bf u}\right\rangle\right)(x)\\
&-\sum_{j=1}^{3}|\nabla|{}u^{j}(x)\,H_{|\nabla|{}}\left((u-v)^{i},u^{j}\right)(x)\\
&+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{\left((u-v)^{i}(x)-(u-v)^{i}(y)\right)\,\left\langle|\nabla|{}{\bf u}(x)-|\nabla|{}{\bf u}(y),{\bf u}(x)-{\bf u}(y)\right\rangle}{|x-y|^{d+1}}\,dy.\end{split}

We estimate the first term in (
3.11
). Applying first (
2.2
), for any small
\sigma>0
, Sobolev embedding,
Lemma

2.2

Proof.

We can write the term under consideration as a determinant using the well-known formula

{\bf a}\cdot({\bf b}\wedge{\bf c})=\det({\bf a}\,|\,{\bf b}|\,{\bf c}).

Applying this to
{\bf a}=\sum_{j=1}^{3}v^{j}H_{|\nabla|}(v^{j},|\nabla|{}({\bf u}-{\bf v}))

\begin{split}&\sum_{i,j=1}^{3}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}v^{j}H_{|\nabla|}(v^{j},(|\nabla|{}(u-v)^{i}))\left({\bf v}\wedge|\nabla|{}({\bf u-v})\right)^{i}\\

this becomes
we have

\begin{split}=&-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{k,\ell,m=1}^{3}\epsilon_{k\ell m}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\Gamma^{k}\ \left(\sum_{j=1}^{3}v^{j}|\nabla|{}\left(v^{j}\,\Gamma^{\ell}\ \ v^{m}\right)-\sum_{j=1}^{3}v^{j}|\nabla|{}\left(v^{j}\,\Gamma^{\ell}\right)\,v^{m}\right)\ \\
&+\frac{1}{2}\sum_{k,\ell,m=1}^{3}\epsilon_{k\ell m}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\Gamma^{k}\ \left(|\nabla|{}\left(\Gamma^{\ell}\,v^{m}\right)-|\nabla|{}\Gamma^{\ell}\,v^{m}\right)\\
=&-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{k,\ell,m=1}^{3}\epsilon_{k\ell m}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\Gamma^{k}\ \sum_{j=1}^{3}v^{j}\left(H_{|\nabla|}\left(v^{j}\Gamma^{\ell},v^{m}\right)+v^{j}\Gamma^{\ell}|\nabla|v^{m}\right)\\
&+\frac{1}{2}\sum_{k,\ell,m=1}^{3}\epsilon_{k\ell m}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\Gamma^{k}\ \left(H_{|\nabla|}(\Gamma^{\ell},v^{m})+\Gamma^{\ell}|\nabla|v^{m}\right).\end{split}

Since

\begin{split}&\sum_{k,\ell,m=1}^{3}\epsilon_{k\ell m}\Gamma^{k}\ \sum_{j=1}^{3}v^{j}v^{j}\Gamma^{\ell}|\nabla|v^{m}=\det\left({\bf\Gamma}\ \Big|\ \sum_{j=1}^{3}v^{j}v^{j}{\bf\Gamma}\ \Big|\ |\nabla|{\bf v}\right)=0\\
\end{split}

and

\sum_{k,\ell,m=1}^{3}\epsilon_{k\ell m}\Gamma^{k}\,\Gamma^{\ell}|\nabla|v^{m}=\det\left({\bf\Gamma}\ \Big|\ {\bf\Gamma}\ \Big|\ |\nabla|{\bf v}\right)=0

we finally have obtained

\begin{split}&\sum_{i,j=1}^{3}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}v^{j}H_{|\nabla|}(v^{j},(|\nabla|{}(u-v)^{i}))\left({\bf v}\wedge|\nabla|{}({\bf u}-{\bf v})\right)^{i}\\
=&-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{k,\ell,m=1}^{3}\epsilon_{k\ell m}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\Gamma^{k}\sum_{j}v^{j}\ H_{|\nabla|{}}(v^{j}\Gamma^{\ell},v^{m})\ \\
&+\frac{1}{2}\sum_{k,\ell,m=1}^{3}\epsilon_{k\ell m}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\Gamma^{k}\sum_{j=1}^{3}v^{j}v^{j}\ H_{|\nabla|}(\Gamma^{\ell},v^{m})\\
=&-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{k,\ell,m=1}^{3}\epsilon_{k\ell m}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\Gamma^{k}\sum_{j}v^{j}\ \left(H_{|\nabla|{}}(v^{j}\Gamma^{\ell},v^{m})-v^{j}H_{|\nabla|}(\Gamma^{\ell},v^{m})\right)\\
\end{split}

Consequently,

\begin{split}&\left\lvert\sum_{i,j=1}^{3}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}v^{j}H_{|\nabla|}(v^{j},(|\nabla|{}(u-v)^{i}))\left({\bf v}\wedge|\nabla|{}({\bf u}-{\bf v})\right)^{i}\right\rvert\\
\lesssim&\|{\bf\Gamma}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\|{\bf v}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\,\max_{j,\ell,m}\|H_{|\nabla|{}}(v^{j}\Gamma^{\ell},v^{m})-v^{j}H_{|\nabla|}(\Gamma^{\ell},v^{m})\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\\
\end{split}

From
Lemma

2.14
for any
\alpha\in(\frac{1}{2},1)
(since
d\geq 2
there is no further assumption necessary),

\begin{split}\|\left(H_{|\nabla|{}}(v^{j}\Gamma^{\ell},v^{m})-v^{j}H_{|\nabla|}(\Gamma^{\ell},v^{m})\right)\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\lesssim&\|{\bf\Gamma}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\,\||\nabla|^{\alpha}{\bf v}\|_{L^{\frac{2d}{2\alpha-1}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{2}\\
\lesssim&\|{\bf\Gamma}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\,\||\nabla|{}{\bf v}\|_{L^{2d}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{2}.\end{split}

Recalling that
{\bf\Gamma}=|\nabla|\left({\bf u}-{\bf v}\right)
we conclude.
∎

4.
Uniqueness: Proof of Theorem
1.1

Theorem

1.1
is a consequence of
Theorem

3.1

Proof of
Theorem

1.1
.

By Grönwall’s lemma, the differential inequality
\dot{\mathcal{E}}(t)\leq\Sigma(t)\mathcal{E}(t)
implies

\mathcal{E}(t_{0})\lesssim\mathcal{E}(0)\,\exp\left(\int_{0}^{t_{0}}\Sigma(t)\,dt\right).

The expression in the exponential is finite by assumption (
1.3
) combined with the estimate for
\Sigma(t)
, (
3.1
).

If
{\bf u}(0)={\bf v}(0)
then
\nabla{\bf u}(0)=\nabla{\bf v}(0)
. Moreover since
{\bf u}
and
{\bf v}
both solve the halfwave map equation (
1.1
) we have

\left(\partial_{t}{\bf u}-\partial_{t}{\bf v}\right)\Big|_{t=0}={\bf u}(0)\wedge(-\Delta)^{\frac{1}{2}}{\bf u}(0)-{\bf v}(0)\wedge(-\Delta)^{\frac{1}{2}}{\bf v}(0)=0.

Thus
\mathcal{E}(0)=0
, and we conclude that
\mathcal{E}(t_{0})=0
for all
t_{0}\in(0,T)
. Thus
{\bf u}-{\bf v}
is a constant on
\mathbb{R}^{d}\times[0,T]
– and again since
{\bf u}(0)={\bf v}(0)
we conclude
{\bf u}\equiv{\bf v}
.
∎

5.
Strichartz estimates

The assumptions of
Theorem

1.1
, (
1.3
) are natural in view of the Keel-Tao estimates
[
8
]
. The following estimate is a consequence of a careful inspection of the arguments in
[
19
, p.566]
.

Lemma 5.1
.

Let
d\geq 4
. Assume for
T>0

(5.1)

\begin{cases}(\partial_{tt}-\Delta)u=h\quad&\text{in $\mathbb{R}^{d}\times[0,T]$}\\
u=f\quad&\text{in $\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\{0\}$}\\
\partial_{t}u=g\quad&\text{in $\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\{0\}$}\\
\end{cases}

then for all
\alpha\in(\frac{1}{2},\frac{d^{2}-4d+1}{2(d-1)}]
,

\begin{split}\||\nabla|^{\alpha}u(t)\|_{L^{2}_{t}L^{(\frac{2d}{2\alpha-1},2)_{x}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\times(0,T)}\lesssim&\||\nabla|^{\frac{d}{2}}f\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}+\||\nabla|^{\frac{d}{2}-1}g\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\\
&+\||\nabla|^{\frac{d}{2}-1}h\|_{L^{1}_{t}L^{2}_{x}(\mathbb{R}^{d}\times(0,T))}.\end{split}

We sketch the argument for the convenience of the reader.

Proof.

First we assume
h\equiv 0
, i.e. consider solutions to

(5.2)

\begin{cases}(\partial_{tt}-\Delta)v=0\quad&\text{in $\mathbb{R}^{d}\times[0,T]$}\\
v=f\quad&\text{in $\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\{0\}$}\\
\partial_{t}v=g\quad&\text{in $\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\{0\}$}.\end{cases}

As in
[
19
, (5.9)]
from
[
8
, Corollary 1.3]
we have the estimate for
\gamma=\frac{d+1}{2(d-1)}

\|v\|_{L^{2}_{t}L^{\frac{2(d-1)}{d-3},2}_{x}}\lesssim\||\nabla|^{\gamma}f\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}+\||\nabla|^{\gamma-1}g\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}.

The Lorentz space estimate is is from
[
19
, p.566]
and follows from interpolation.
Observe that for any
\alpha\geq 0
the function
|\nabla|^{\alpha}v
solves

\begin{cases}(\partial_{tt}-\Delta)|\nabla|^{\alpha}v=0\quad&\text{in $\mathbb{R}^{d}\times[0,T]$}\\
|\nabla|^{\alpha}v=|\nabla|^{\alpha}f\quad&\text{in $\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\{0\}$}\\
\partial_{t}|\nabla|^{\alpha}v=|\nabla|^{\alpha}g\quad&\text{in $\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\{0\}$}\\
\end{cases}

we obtain

\||\nabla|^{\alpha}v\|_{L^{2}_{t}L^{(\frac{2(d-1)}{d-3},2)}_{x}}\lesssim\||\nabla|^{\gamma+\alpha}f\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}+\||\nabla|^{\gamma+\alpha-1}g\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}.

If we choose in the above inequality
\alpha=\frac{d^{2}-4d+1}{2(d-1)}+1
, then

\gamma+\alpha=\frac{d+1}{2(d-1)}+\frac{d^{2}-4d+1}{2(d-1)}+1=\frac{d}{2}.

and we thus have found the estimate

\||\nabla|^{1+\frac{d^{2}-4d+1}{2(d-1)}}{v}\|_{L^{2}_{t}L^{(\frac{2(d-1)}{d-3},2)}_{x}}\lesssim\||\nabla|^{\frac{d}{2}}f\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}+\||\nabla|^{\frac{d}{2}-1}g\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}.

We observe that for
d\geq 4
we have
\frac{d^{2}-4d+1}{2(d-1)}>0
. Let now
\alpha\in(\frac{1}{2},\frac{d^{2}-4d+1}{2(d-1)}]
then

\alpha-\frac{d}{\frac{2d}{2\alpha-1}}=1+\frac{d^{2}-4d+1}{2(d-1)}-\frac{d}{\frac{2(d-1)}{d-3}},

and thus by spacial Sobolev embedding,
Lemma

2.2
,

\||\nabla|^{\alpha}v(t)\|_{L^{\frac{2d}{2\alpha-1},2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\lesssim\||\nabla|^{1+\frac{d^{2}-4d+1}{2(d-1)}}v(t)\|_{L^{(\frac{2(d-1)}{d-3},2)}}.

After integrating in time we find

\||\nabla|^{\alpha}v\|_{L^{2}_{t}L^{(\frac{2d}{2\alpha-1},2)}_{x}(\mathbb{R}^{d}\times(0,T))}\lesssim\||\nabla|^{\frac{d}{2}}f\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}+\||\nabla|^{\frac{d}{2}-1}g\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}.

If
h\neq 0
we conclude by Duhamel’s principle and Minkowski’s inequality: we keep denoting by
v
the solution in (
5.2
). By Duhamel principle the solution
u
to (
5.1
) can be written as

u(x,t)=v(x,t)+\int_{0}^{t}H(x,s,t)\,ds,

where
H
solves

(5.3)

\begin{cases}(\partial_{tt}-\Delta_{x})H(\cdot,s,\cdot)=0\quad&\text{in }\mathbb{R}^{d}\times(s,T)\\
H(\cdot,s,s)=0\quad&\text{in $\mathbb{R}^{d}$}\\
\partial_{t}H(\cdot,s,s)=h(\cdot,s)\quad&\text{in $\mathbb{R}^{d}$}\end{cases}

By a slight abuse of notation we identify
H(x,s,t)=\chi_{t>s}H(x,s,t)
.
We then have by Minkowski’s inequality

\begin{split}&\left\|\int_{0}^{T}|\nabla|^{\alpha}H(x,s,t)\,ds\right\|_{L^{2}_{t}L^{(\frac{2d}{2\alpha-1},2)}_{x}(\mathbb{R}^{d}\times(0,T))}\\
\lesssim&\int_{0}^{T}\||\nabla|^{\alpha}H(x,s,t)\|_{L^{2}_{t}L^{(\frac{2d}{2\alpha-1},2)}_{x}(\mathbb{R}^{d}\times(s,T))}\,ds\end{split}

Since
H
solves the homogeneous equation (
5.3
), we can use the same estimates as we have obtained for
v
beforehand, namely we find

\begin{split}&\int_{0}^{T}\||\nabla|^{\alpha}H(x,s,t)\|_{L^{2}_{t}L^{(\frac{2d}{2\alpha-1},2)}_{x}(\mathbb{R}^{d}\times(s,T))}\,ds\\
\lesssim&\int_{0}^{T}\||\nabla|^{\frac{d}{2}-1}h(x,s)\|_{L^{2}_{x}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\,ds\\
=&\||\nabla|^{\frac{d}{2}-1}h\|_{L^{1}_{t}L^{2}_{x}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}.\end{split}

Combining these estimates with the ones for
v
we conclude.
∎

Inflationary resolution of the initial singularity
Damien A. Easson, Joseph E. Lesnefsky

Abstract
We construct geodesically complete inflationary models, addressing and overcoming existing no-go theorems related to past eternal inflation. We show that a period of accelerated expansion is indispensable for achieving nontrivial, geodesically complete (Generalized) Friedmann-Robertson-Walker spacetimes. We show within the framework of General Relativity, the persistence of eternal inflation comes at the expense of violating the null energy condition.



Introduction
–The inflationary universe paradigm is a cornerstone of modern cosmology
Guth 1981
;
Linde 1982
;
Albrecht and Steinhardt 1982
. A prevailing view asserts that inflationary scenarios cannot be past-eternal even at the classical level, a conclusion drawn independent of the energy conditions involved. Specifically, the general notion stems from the now renowned work of Borde, Guth and Vilenkin (BGV)
Borde et al. 2003
, who stated that a cosmological model which is inflating – or just expanding sufficiently fast – must be incomplete in null and timelike past directions. This belief has led to the strong statement that inflationary models necessitate physics other than inflation to describe the past boundary of the inflating region of a spacetime, and even to the perspective that, not only inflation must have had a beginning, but the universe itself must have originated from a definite beginning.

In this Letter, we challenge this perspective by explicitly constructing geodesically-complete eternal inflationary models. Furthermore, we show that all non-trivial geodesically-complete Friedmann Robertson Walker (FRW) spacetimes
necessarily
require an epoch of accelerated expansion. This finding underscores the critical role of acceleration and inflation in the formation and evolution of a geodesically-complete universe.

We show the price of this eternal inflation in a universe with non-trivial evolution in standard General Relativity (GR), is a required period in which the null energy condition is violated. Further details concerning inflationary as well as geodesically-complete bouncing and loitering models, which may require only an arbitrarily short period of accelerated expansion, are discussed in a companion paper

BGV Theorem
–As the above findings may come as a surprise to some readers given the famous no-go theorem of BGV, we now explain how the theorem is obviated.
The theorem may be quantified by Eq. 5 of
Borde et al. 2003
, which we formalize as in
Lesnefsky et al. 2023
:

Theorem 2
.

(BGV)
–Let
\left(\mathcal{M},g\right)
be a spacetime, and

\gamma\,:\,\left[\lambda_{i},\lambda_{f}\right]\rightarrow\mathcal{M}

be some causal geodesic. If the quantity

H_{avg}^{\gamma}=\frac{1}{\lambda_{f}-\lambda_{i}}\int_{\lambda_{i}}^{\lambda_{f}}H^{\gamma}\left(\zeta\right)d\zeta

(9)

is strictly positive along the image of
\gamma

Let us apply the BGV theorem to the eternal inflationary model given by Eq.
1
. Without loss of generality we take
a_{0}=1
,
t_{i}<0
, and
t_{f}=0
, and select any connected interval
\left[t_{i},0\right]
where the boundary is actually realized:

H_{avg}^{\gamma}=\frac{1}{-t_{i}}\int_{t_{i}}^{0}Hdt=\frac{1}{-t_{i}}\ln\left(\frac{1+c}{e^{\nicefrac{{t_{i}}}{{\alpha}}}+c}\right)>0

(10)

because,
1+c>e^{\nicefrac{{t_{i}}}{{\alpha}}}+c
. Thus, direct calculation of Eq.
9
, yields
H_{avg}>0
, yet despite Thm.
2
, the model is geodesically complete per Thm.
1
. What is further troubling for Thm.
2
is that Eq.
9

inherently
incomplete, as it must inevitably encounter a singularity or boundary at its endpoint, rendering it categorically incomplete yet inextendable, or it is straightforwardly extendable in a (possibly small but non-empty) open neighborhood of the endpoint by the exponential map

One may attempt to salvage Thm.
2

Borde et al. 2003

10

H_{avg}=0
, and one may then argue that the BGV theorem does not apply. This result is due to the cofinite interval suppression of
1/(-t_{i})

Lesnefsky et al. 2023

We now discuss two important Corollaries to Thm.
1

Corollary 3
.

In General Relativity, every smooth, geodesically complete, non-constant scale factor
a(t)
of a FRW spacetime must undergo accelerated expansion for at least some period of time.

This finding is not entirely surprising given the well-known fact that the NEC must be violated in order to achieve a cosmological bounce (in a flat
k=0
) FRW spacetime
Hawking and Penrose 1970
. During a bounce, the universe transitions from a contracting phase (
H<0
) to an expanding phase (
H>0
). This transition inherently requires that at the point where the contraction halts and expansion begins, the derivative of the Hubble parameter,
\dot{H}
, must be positive.
Since
\dot{H}=-4\pi G(\rho+p)
, we must have
\rho+p<0
, signalling violation of the NEC. Thus, any such spacetime which exhibits a bounce, or bounces, for any part of it’s history must violate NEC, and since SEC implies the NEC, its infringement is inevitable, thereby permitting the possibility of accelerated expansion.

Outside the context of General Relativity and for Generalized-FRW metrics, the above corollary lacks a formally established proof, and we therefore propose:

Conjecture 4
.

As a metrical theory of gravity, any geodesically complete GFRW must undergo inflation.

In support of this conjecture, for a specific class of GFRW scale factors, a proof is possible resulting in:

Corollary 5

Let

\mathbb{R}^{1}_{1}\times_{p}\Sigma

Conclusions
–Thm.
1
, derived from direct integration of the geodesic equations, is definitive in assessing the geodesic completeness of GFRW spacetimes,
opposed to integrals of
H
,
H_{avg}
, or the BGV Thm.
2

Considering the above Corollaries within the context of the incompleteness arguments presented in
Borde et al. 2003
, we arrive at a compelling contrapositive: The issue is not that inflationary spacetimes are necessarily incomplete; instead, we find that for spacetimes to be complete, they must exhibit inflationary behavior. Within classical GR, and with the caveat with respect to traditional energy conditions, accelerated expansion and inflation can play a critical role in resolving the initial singularity problem and, overlooking potentially significant quantum effects, inflation can be eternal into the past.

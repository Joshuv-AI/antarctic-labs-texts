Reconstructing almost all of a point set in ℝ d \mathbb{R}^{d} from randomly revealed pairwise distances
Douglas Barnes, Jan Petr, Julien Portier, Benedict Randall Shaw, Alan Sergeev

Abstract
Let V V be a set of n n points in ℝ d \mathbb{R}^{d} , and suppose that the distance between each pair of points is revealed independently with probability p p . We study when this information is sufficient to reconstruct large subsets of V V , up to isometry. Strong results for d = 1 d=1 have been obtained by Girão, Illingworth, Michel, Powierski, and Scott. In this paper, we investigate higher dimensions, and show that if p > n − 2 / ( d + 4 ) p>n^{-2/(d+4)} , then we can reconstruct almost all of V V up to isometry, with high probability. We do this by relating it to a polluted graph bootstrap percolation result, for which we adapt the methods of Balogh, Bollobás, and Morris.

The main idea of our proof is based on the following observation, proved in a stronger form as

Observation 1.6

Let

-independent. If all pairwise distances known except

Therefore if we knew that

-dependent, then repeatedly applying

would allow us to follow a

-percolation

, a general result about graph bootstrap percolation by Balogh, Bollobás, and Morris.
For a set of points

not necessarily in general position, we still want to apply a

-bootstrap percolation on the graph of known distances, but only reconstructing

from copies of

such that

is

-independent, so that

applies.

To account for

-dependent sets, we will work with

, defined in

In our reconstruction problem, we will take

to be exactly the

We then show that

.
Then we can deduce our main result,

, from this polluted bootstrap percolation result,

More precisely, we show in

that for every multiset of points

which contains reasonably many points of

Combining all of the above results, we obtain

(1-x)\geq e^{-2x}
. Thus for large
n
, condition (f) gives

\frac{\mathbb{E}\left(X^{\mathcal{H}}_{r}(e)\right)}{\mathbb{E}\left(X_{r}(e)\right)}=\frac{\left|T^{\mathcal{H}}\right|}{\left|T\right|}\geq\left(1-\frac{(2(d+1))^{d+1}}{n^{1-\mu}}\right)^{r}\geq e^{-2(2(d+1))^{d+1}rn^{\mu-1}}\geq e^{-2(2(d+1))^{d+1}c}=L_{d,c}^{-1/2},

where
L_{d,c}=e^{4(2(d+1))^{d+1}c}
is a constant.
Hence
\mathbb{E}\left(X^{\mathcal{H}}_{r}(e)\right)\to\infty
, and

\frac{\Var\left(X^{\mathcal{H}}_{r}(e)\right)}{\mathbb{E}\left(X^{\mathcal{H}}_{r}(e)\right)^{2}}\leq L_{d,c}\frac{\Var\left(X_{r}(e)\right)}{\mathbb{E}\left(X_{r}(e)\right)^{2}}<L_{d,c}\left(\frac{p^{\eta(d)}n}{4v(H_{r})^{2}}-1\right)^{-1}\to 0.

∎

We now proceed to the proof of
Theorem

3.1
.

Proof of
Theorem

3.1
.

Let
G^{\prime}=\langle G\rangle^{\mathcal{H}}_{K_{d+3}}
. We may assume that
\delta^{2}(1-2\delta)^{-2}<(2d)^{-1}
.
We let
p\gg p_{*}(n,d)
,
r=\omega=\left\lceil\frac{\log n}{\log\log n}\right\rceil
and apply
Lemma

3.3
. The authors of
[
1
]
have already shown that conditions (a) to (d) are satisfied, and it is clear that the additional conditions (e) and (f) are also satisfied. It follows by Chebyshev’s inequality that

\mathbb{P}\left(e\notin G^{\prime}\right)=\mathbb{P}\left(X^{\mathcal{H}}_{r}(e)=0\right)\leq\frac{\Var\left(X^{\mathcal{H}}_{r}(e)\right)}{\mathbb{E}\left(X^{\mathcal{H}}_{r}(e)\right)^{2}}<L_{d,c}\left(\frac{p^{\eta(d)}n}{4v(H_{r})^{2}}-1\right)^{-1}\to 0.

Hence, by Markov’s inequality,
G^{\prime}
has at least
n^{2}/2-\delta^{2}n^{2}/2
edges w.h.p. We now show that w.h.p.,
G^{\prime}
contains a clique on
(1-\delta)n
vertices by combining a similar claim and its proof from
[
1
]
with the classical supersaturation result in
Theorem

2.4
.

Let
D=\left\{x\in[n]:d_{G^{\prime}}(x)>(1-\delta)n\right\}
. Then

\displaystyle n^{2}-\delta^{2}n^{2}

\displaystyle\leq 2|E(G^{\prime})|

\displaystyle=\sum_{v\in D}d_{G^{\prime}}(v)+\sum_{v\in D^{c}}d_{G^{\prime}}(v)

\displaystyle\leq|D|n+(n-|D|)(1-\delta)n

\displaystyle=|D|\delta n+(1-\delta)n^{2}.

Rearranging, this gives
|D|\geq(1-\delta)n
.

We now claim that
D
is a clique for sufficiently large
n
. Suppose for contradiction that there exist
x,y\in D
such that there is no edge between
x
and
y
in
G^{\prime}
. Let
n_{*}=\left|N_{G^{\prime}}(x)\cap N_{G^{\prime}}(y)\right|\geq(1-2\delta)n
, and let
G^{*}
be the induced subgraph of
G^{\prime}
on vertex set
N_{G^{\prime}}(x)\cap N_{G^{\prime}}(y)
. For sufficiently large
n
we have

\displaystyle|E(G^{*})|

\displaystyle\geq\binom{n_{*}}{2}-\delta^{2}n^{2}/2

\displaystyle\geq\binom{n_{*}}{2}-\delta^{2}(1-2\delta)^{-2}n_{*}^{2}/2

\displaystyle\geq\left(\frac{1}{2}-\delta^{2}(1-2\delta)^{-2}\right)n_{*}^{2}.

Let
\gamma=1/2-\delta^{2}(1-2\delta)^{-2}
. By
Theorem

2.4
, the graph
G^{*}
contains
\Omega_{\gamma}(n^{d+1})
many
(d+1)
-cliques. As
|E(\mathcal{H})|\leq n^{d+\mu}
, there exists a
(d+1)
-clique in
N_{G^{\prime}}(x)\cap N_{G^{\prime}}(y)\cap E(\mathcal{H})^{c}
. This is a contradiction. Hence,
D
is a clique, which finishes the proof.
∎

4
Proof of
Theorem

1.5

We are now equipped with
Theorem

3.1
.
We aim to use it with the hyperedges corresponding to
d
-dependent tuples of size
d+1
, utilizing
1.6
.
We show in the following geometric lemma that either the number of
d
-dependent tuples of size
d+1
is small enough to apply
Theorem

3.1
, or
V
has a large intersection with a subspace of lower dimension.
This dichotomy will enable us to then deduce
Corollary

4.2
, which roughly states that there always is a large subset of
V
lying in some (possibly non-proper) subspace of
\mathbb{R}^{d}
for which we can apply
Theorem

3.1
.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
0<\mu<1
and
V
be a multiset of
n
points in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
. Then either

•

there exists a subspace
\Pi_{d^{\prime}}
of dimension
0\leq d^{\prime}\leq d-1
such that
|V\cap\Pi_{d^{\prime}}|\geq n^{\mu}
, or

•

the number of
d
-dependent tuples of
V
of size
d+1
is at most
dn^{d+\mu}
.

Proof.

Suppose that there is no subspace
\Pi_{d^{\prime}}
of dimension
0\leq d^{\prime}\leq d-1
such that
|V\cap\Pi_{d^{\prime}}|\geq n^{\mu}
. Let
I
be the set of
d
-dependent tuples of
V
of size
d+1
, and for each
0\leq i\leq d-1
, let
I_{i}
be the set of families of
V
of size
d+1
whose affine span has dimension exactly
i
. We have
I=\bigcup_{i=0}^{d-1}I_{i}
and therefore
|I|=\sum_{i=0}^{d-1}|I_{i}|
. By definition, for each element
S
of
I_{i}
, there exist
x_{1},\dots,x_{i},x_{i+1}\in S
such that the subspace
\Pi_{S}
spanned by
x_{1},\dots,x_{i},x_{i+1}
has dimension exactly
i
, and
S\setminus\{x_{1},\dots,x_{i+1}\}
lies in
\Pi_{S}
. As we assumed that no subspace
\Pi_{S}
of dimension
0\leq d^{\prime}\leq d-1
satisfies
|V\cap\Pi_{S}|\geq n^{\mu}
, it follows that once
x_{1},\dots,x_{i+1}
are fixed, there are at most
n^{(d-i)\mu}
possibilities for
S\setminus\{x_{1},\dots,x_{i+1}\}
. Therefore,
|I_{i}|\leq n^{i+1+(d-i)\mu}
, and consequently
|I|\leq\sum_{i=0}^{d-1}n^{i+1+(d-i)\mu}\leq dn^{d+\mu}
.
∎

From
Lemma

4.1
we deduce the following corollary.

Corollary 4.2
.

Let
0<\mu<1
and
V
be a multiset of
n
points in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
. Then there exists a subspace
\Pi_{d^{\prime}}
of dimension
0\leq d^{\prime}\leq d
such that
|V\cap\Pi_{d^{\prime}}|\geq n^{\mu^{d-d^{\prime}}}
and the number of
d^{\prime}
-dependent families of size
d^{\prime}+1
in
V\cap\Pi_{d^{\prime}}
is at most
d|V\cap\Pi_{d^{\prime}}|^{d^{\prime}+\mu}
.

Proof.

We prove this corollary by induction on
d
. For
d=0
, the corollary is trivially true. Suppose now
d\geq 1
. We apply
Lemma

4.1
and distinguish whether the first or second outcome of it is realised.

In the first case, there exists a subspace
\Pi_{d^{\prime}}
of dimension
0\leq d^{\prime}\leq d-1
such that
|V\cap\Pi_{d^{\prime}}|\geq n^{\mu}
. We now apply the induction hypothesis on
V^{\prime}=V\cap\Pi_{d^{\prime}}
, giving a subspace
\Pi_{d^{\prime\prime}}
of dimension
d^{\prime\prime}
such that
0\leq d^{\prime\prime}\leq d^{\prime}\leq d-1
and
|V\cap\Pi_{d^{\prime\prime}}|=|V^{\prime}\cap\Pi_{d^{\prime\prime}}|\geq|V^{\prime}|^{\mu^{d^{\prime}-d^{\prime\prime}}}\geq n^{\mu^{d-d^{\prime\prime}}}
and the number of
d^{\prime\prime}
-dependent families of
V\cap\Pi_{d^{\prime\prime}}=V^{\prime}\cap\Pi_{d^{\prime\prime}}
is at most
d|V\cap\Pi_{d^{\prime\prime}}|^{d^{\prime\prime}+\mu}
, as wanted.

In the second case, we immediately get the desired outcome for
d^{\prime}=d
and
\Pi_{d}=\mathbb{R}^{d}
.

Therefore, we are finished by induction.
∎

By the corollary above, for any set of
n
points
V
in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, there is a subspace
\Pi_{d^{\prime}}
that contains many of these points and such that there are not many
d^{\prime}
-dependent families in
V\cap\Pi_{d^{\prime}}
.
The aim of the next lemma is to give sufficient conditions to reconstruct
\mathrm{dist}(v,\Pi_{d^{\prime}})
and the projections of
v
onto
\Pi_{d^{\prime}}
for almost all of
v\in V
w.h.p., when the pairwise distances are revealed as
G(n,p)
for sufficiently large
p
.

Lemma 4.3
.

Let
d\geq 1
be an integer and
\alpha
,
\delta
,
\lambda>0
be such that
\alpha-(d+1)\delta\geq\lambda
. Let
V
be a multiset of
n
points in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
. Let
A\subset V
and
p\gg p_{*}(n,d)
. Suppose that

1.

there exists a
d^{\prime}
-dimensional subspace
\Pi_{d^{\prime}}
in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
such that
A\subset\Pi_{d^{\prime}}
and at least
\alpha\binom{|A|}{d^{\prime}+1}
of sets of
d^{\prime}+1
points of
A
are
d^{\prime}
-independent,

2.

p|A|\gg 1
, and

3.

there exists a set
A^{\prime}\subseteq A
such that
|A^{\prime}|\geq(1-\delta/2)|A|
and for every
u,v\in A^{\prime}
, we know the distance
\mathrm{dist}(u,v)
.

If the distances are revealed according to a random graph
G(n,p)
, then w.h.p. there exists a subset
V^{\prime}\subseteq V
of size
|V^{\prime}|\geq(1-\delta)|V|
such that for every
v^{\prime}\in V^{\prime}
, we can reconstruct
\mathrm{dist}(v^{\prime},\Pi_{d^{\prime}})
and the projection of
v^{\prime}
onto
\Pi_{d^{\prime}}
relative to the embedding of
A^{\prime}
.

Proof.

Let
\mathcal{I}
be the set of
d^{\prime}
-independent sets in
A^{\prime}
. Note that
|\mathcal{I}|\geq\alpha\binom{|A|}{d^{\prime}+1}-\delta/2|A|\binom{|A|}{d^{\prime}}
, and that
\binom{|A|}{d^{\prime}+1}=\binom{|A|}{d^{\prime}}\frac{|A|-d^{\prime}-1}{d^{\prime}+1}\geq\binom{|A|}{d^{\prime}}\frac{|A|}{2(d+1)}
when
n
is large enough. Therefore, we have
|\mathcal{I}|\geq\lambda\binom{|A|}{d^{\prime}+1}
for
n
large enough.

Let
B=V\setminus A
. Let
u\in B
, and for each
I\in\mathcal{I}
we define the random variable
I_{u}
to be the indicator of
u
having an edge to each element of
I
. We also define the random variable
V_{u}=\sum_{I\in\mathcal{I}}I_{u}
. Since
|A|p\to\infty
, we have

\mathbb{E}V_{u}\geq p^{d^{\prime}+1}{|A|\choose{d^{\prime}+1}}\lambda=\Omega_{d,\lambda}\left(\left(|A|p\right)^{d^{\prime}+1}\right),\mbox{ and }

\begin{split}\Var V_{u}&{}=\sum_{I,I^{\prime}\in\mathcal{I}}\left(p^{\left|I\cup I^{\prime}\right|}-p^{2\left(d^{\prime}+1\right)}\right)\\
&{}\leq\sum_{I,I^{\prime}\in A^{(d^{\prime}+1)}}\left(p^{\left|I\cup I^{\prime}\right|}-p^{2\left(d^{\prime}+1\right)}\right)\\
&=\sum_{i=0}^{d^{\prime}+1}{|A|\choose{d^{\prime}+1+i}}{{d^{\prime}+1+i}\choose{i}}{{d^{\prime}+1}\choose{i}}(p^{d^{\prime}+1+i}-p^{2(d^{\prime}+1)})\\
&<\sum_{i=0}^{d^{\prime}}{|A|\choose{d^{\prime}+1+i}}{{d^{\prime}+1+i}\choose{i}}{{d^{\prime}+1}\choose{i}}p^{d^{\prime}+1+i}\\
&{}{}=O_{d}\left(\left(|A|p\right)^{2d^{\prime}+1}\right).\end{split}

Let
B^{\prime}=\{u\in B:V_{u}>1\}
. By Chebyshev’s inequality, we have
\mathbb{P}\left(V_{u}=0\right)=O_{d,\lambda}\left(\left(|A|p\right)^{-1}\right)\to 0
, and therefore
\mathbb{E}|B^{\prime}|=(1-o(1))|B|
. Hence, w.h.p.
|B^{\prime}|\geq(1-\delta/2)|B|
. Now by
Lemma

2.2
, for every
b^{\prime}\in B^{\prime}
, there is an
I\in\mathcal{I}
such that we can reconstruct
\{b^{\prime}\}\cup I
. Since
I
is
d^{\prime}
-independent, we can extend this embedding uniquely to reconstruct
\{b^{\prime}\}\cup A^{\prime}
. Thus we can reconstruct
\mathrm{dist}(b^{\prime},\Pi_{d^{\prime}})
and the projection of
b^{\prime}
onto
\Pi_{d^{\prime}}
relative to the embedding of
A^{\prime}
. Letting
V^{\prime}=B^{\prime}\cup A^{\prime}
, we reach the desired conclusion.
∎

We now present the following technical lemma, which allows us to reduce the problem to a lower dimension.

Lemma 4.4
.

Let
d\geq 1
,
0<\varepsilon<\frac{1}{2(d+1)}
be fixed and
V=\{v_{1},\dots,v_{n}\}
be a multiset of
n
points in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
. Suppose the graph
G=(V,\mathcal{P})
of revealed pairwise distances is distributed as
G(n,p)
and that
p\gg p_{*}(n,d)
. Then, w.h.p., we can explicitly construct some constants
b_{i,j}
such that there exists a set
J\subseteq[n]
such that
|J|\geq(1-\varepsilon)n
and
\tilde{V}=\{\tilde{v}_{1},\dots,\tilde{v}_{n}\}
a multiset of
\mathbb{R}^{d^{\prime}}
for some
d^{\prime}<d
such that for every
i,j\in J
, we have
\mathrm{dist}(v_{i},v_{j})^{2}=b_{i,j}+\mathrm{dist}(\tilde{v}_{i},\tilde{v}_{j})^{2}
.

Note that while we obtain the values of the
b_{i,j}
, we do not claim to know the configuration of
\tilde{V}
. However, given
b_{i,j}
, we can compute
\mathrm{dist}(\tilde{v}_{i},\tilde{v}_{j})
from
\mathrm{dist}(v_{i},v_{j})
and vice versa. Before proving
Lemma

4.4
, we show that a simple iteration of
Lemma

4.4
enables us to deduce
Theorem

1.5
.

Proof of
Theorem

1.5
assuming
Lemma

4.4
.

We let
q(n,d)=p_{*}(n,d)
and
p\gg p_{*}(n,d)
. Note that
G
sampled according to
G(n,p)
can also be sampled as
\cup_{i=0}^{d-1}G_{i}
where the
G_{i}
are independent from each other and each
G_{i}
is sampled according to
G(n,p^{\prime})
where
1-p=(1-p^{\prime})^{d}
. By Bernoulli’s inequality,
p^{\prime}\geq p/d
, and therefore
p^{\prime}\gg p_{*}(n,d)
.

Let
\kappa>0
, and let
\varepsilon=\min(\frac{1}{2(d+1)},\kappa/d)
. We start by applying
Lemma

4.4
to
V^{(0)}=V
with the random graph
G_{0}
, and w.h.p. get explicit constants
b^{(0)}_{i,j}

and a set

such that for every

\mathrm{dist}(v^{(0)}_{i},v^{(0)}_{j})^{2}=b^{(0)}_{i,j}+\mathrm{dist}(v^{(1)}_{i},v^{(1)}_{j})^{2}
. If
d^{(1)}\neq 0
, we apply
Lemma

4.4
again to
V^{(1)}
with the random graph
G_{1}
, and get explicit constants
b^{(1)}_{i,j}
, a set
J^{(1)}\subseteq[n]
such that
|J^{(1)}|\geq(1-\varepsilon)n
, an integer

and a set

lying in
\mathbb{R}^{d^{(2)}}
such that for every

, we have
\mathrm{dist}(v^{(1)}_{i},v^{(1)}_{j})^{2}=b^{(1)}_{i,j}+\mathrm{dist}(v^{(2)}_{i},v^{(2)}_{j})^{2}

We continue in the same fashion until we have

for some integer

. As

. Let

5
Concluding remarks

For any set of
n
points
V
in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, we have obtained in
Theorem

1.5
a non-trivial bound on
p
under which, w.h.p., we can reconstruct the pairwise distances within a subset of
V
of size
n-o(n)
. However, we do not believe that our bound is tight. For instance, for
d=1
, much stronger results have been obtained in
Theorem

1.4
by Girão, Illingworth, Michel, Powierski, and Scott, suggesting that the criticial probability could be
c_{d}/n

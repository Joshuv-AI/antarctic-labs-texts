Deterministic and Stochastic Geometric Mechanics for Hall MHD
Darryl D. Holm, Ruiao Hu, Oliver D. Street

Abstract
We derive new models of stochastic Hall magnetohydrodynamics (MHD) by using a symmetry-reduced stochastic Euler-Poincaré variational principle. The new stochastic Hall MHD theory has potential applications for uncertainty quantification and data assimilation in space plasma (space weather) and solar physics. The stochastic geometric mechanics approach we take here produces coordinate-free results which may then be applied in a variety of spatial configurations.

Overview

The Hall effect in a neutral plasma is a classical problem in plasma physics
[
7
]
. The Hall effect produces an additional advective drift of the magnetic field lines
induced by the electron fluid motion. The Hall drift velocity is proportional to the current density, which generates the magnetic force, that in turn generates fluid vorticity leading to MHD turbulence. In particular, it alters
the magnetic field configuration by creating small-scale structures that seed high-wavenumber instabilities and turbulence.

Geometric mechanics models Hall MHD as a two-fluid ion-electron plasma,
[
28
,
36
]
. The nonlinear interactions of the slow-fast, large-small, resolved-unresolved decomposition of the mean and the fluctuating or turbulent components of the physical processes in MHD plasmas require a structure-preserving stochastic approach.
This type of modelling is accomplished by separating the ion-electron plasma dynamics into two maps that whose composition governs the momentum densities the ions and the electrons.

Summary

The paper will use Lie-group invariant variational principles of geometric mechanics to introduce transport noise for the purpose of deriving and investigating the effects of stochastic advection in the interaction of the two-fluid representation of Hall MHD turbulence in neutral ion-electron plasmas.

Plan

This paper takes two approaches in geometric mechanics to derive deterministic and stochastic variational formulations of the PDE system for ideal Hall MHD.
These two approaches reveal complementary features as we derive the equations for stochastic compressible Hall MHD flow and show that these stochastic equations preserve the Kelvin-Noether conservation laws for circulation and helicity. Section
2
takes the Hamilton-Pontryagin approach, which emphasises the roles of the two flow maps underlying Hall MHD. Section
3
takes the Euler-Poincaré approach which applies a more direct Lie algebraic method to derive the equations. Section
3.3
contains the derivation of stochastic Hall MHD which introduces independent transport noises into both ion and electron fluids, taking care to respect the algebraic condition of local charge neutrality. This is our main result. We derive this result using coordinate-free calculus of differential forms to ensure its applicability in any simply connected spatial configuration. Section
3.4
discusses an alternative to the SALT approach of introducing stochasticity. The alternative approach is known as Stochastic Forcing by Lie Transport (SFLT). As opposed to SALT, the SFLT approach has the feature that if preserves total integrated energy. Section
4
contains remarks, open problems and plans for future work, including further development of the SFLT approach of introducing stochasticity into fluid dynamics.

Deterministic Hamilton–Pontryagin principles for Hall MHD

2.1
Hall MHD for ideal incompressible flow

After assuming local charge neutrality, the Hall MHD equations for ideal inhomogeneous volume preserving flow
are given in vector calculus form by
[
22
]

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\partial_{t}\rho\mathbf{u}+\mathbf{u}\cdot\nabla\rho\mathbf{u}+\nabla p+\mathbf{B}\times\operatorname{curl}\mathbf{B}=0\,,\\
&\partial_{t}\rho+\mathrm{div}(\rho\mathbf{u})=0\,,\quad\mathrm{div}\mathbf{u}=0\\
&\partial_{t}\mathbf{A}+\mathbf{B}\times\mathbf{v}=0\,,\quad\mathbf{B}=\mathrm{curl}{\mathbf{A}}\,,\\
&\mathbf{v}:=\mathbf{u}-\frac{R}{a\rho}\operatorname{curl}\mathbf{B}=\mathbf{u}+\mathbf{v}_{H}\,,\quad\text{with}\quad\mathbf{v}_{H}:=-\frac{R}{a\rho}\operatorname{curl}\mathbf{B}=-\frac{R}{a\rho}{\mathbf{J}}\,.\end{split}

(2.1)

Here,
\mathbf{u}
is the divergence-free fluid flow velocity,
\rho
is the local fluid density,
p
is the fluid pressure,
{\mathbf{J}}=\mathrm{curl}{\mathbf{B}}
is the magnetic current density,
{\mathbf{A}}
is the magnetic vector potential, with magnetic field
{\mathbf{B}}=\mathrm{curl}{\mathbf{A}}
. The Hall velocity
\mathbf{v}_{H}
is proportional to the Hall parameter,
R
, determined as the ratio of the Larmor radii of the electrons and ions. The charge-to-mass ratio of electrons and ions is given by
a
(a negative number). The MHD equations are recovered for
R=0
.

Boundary conditions for (
2.1
) require vanishing of normal coordinates of
\mathbf{u}
,
\nabla p
,
{\mathbf{B}}
and
{\mathbf{J}}
on the boundary. In the radiation gauge one sets
\partial_{t}{\mathbf{A}}\times\mathbf{\hat{n}}|_{\partial D}=0
. Hence, if
{\mathbf{A}}\times\mathbf{\hat{n}}|_{\partial{\cal D}}=0
holds initially, then it will remain so. Here,
\mathbf{\hat{n}}
is the normal unit vector on the boundary
\partial{\cal D}
of the domain
{\cal D}
. See
[
28
]
for further discussion.

Coordinate-free geometric notation for Hall MHD

The coordinate-free geometric form of the equation set in (
2.1
) is

\displaystyle\begin{split}\left(\partial_{t}+\mathcal{L}_{u}\right)u^{\flat}&=-\frac{1}{\rho}\mathbf{d}p+\mathbf{d}\left(\frac{1}{2}u\intprod u^{\flat}\right)+\frac{1}{\rho}\left((v-u)\intprod\frac{a}{R}\mathbf{d}A\right)\,,\\
\left(\partial_{t}+\mathcal{L}_{u}\right)\rho&=0\,,\\
\left(\partial_{t}+\mathcal{L}_{v}\right)A&=\mathbf{d}(v\intprod A)\,,\quad\hbox{with}\quad B=\mathbf{d}A\,,\\
\mathcal{L}_{u}(d^{3}x)&=\mathbf{d}(u\intprod d^{3}x)=(\mathrm{div}\mathbf{u})d^{3}x=0\,,\\
\hbox{and}\quad\mathrm{div}\mathbf{u}&=0\,,\quad\mathrm{div}{\mathbf{B}}=0\,.\end{split}

(2.2)

Remark 2.1

(Coordinate-free geometric notation)
.

In the equation system (
2.2
), the exterior differential operator is denoted as

and the musical superscript notation

identifies the transformation of vector fields to 1-forms. The inverse transformation from 1-forms to vector fields is written in musical notation as
(\,\cdot\,)^{\sharp}:\Lambda^{1}(\mathcal{D})\rightarrow\mathfrak{X}(\mathcal{D})
. The notation

denotes the insertion of a vector field

into the 1-form

. Likewise, the notation

denotes the insertion of a vector field

into the 1-form

. In Euclidean coordinates, this particular insertion would be the dot-product. Of course, also the differential

because

In addition, the operation

is defined by

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{u}a:=u\intprod\mathbf{d}a+\mathbf{d}(u\intprod a)\,,\quad\forall a\in\Lambda^{k}(\mathcal{D})\,,

(2.3)

which is the Cartan’s form of the Lie derivative

along the vector field

on k-forms.

(Division by a density)
.

Suppose that we have a
1

. One may formally define division of

2.2
Deterministic Hamilton–Pontryagin variational principle.

In the geometric formulation of ideal compressible Hall MHD, the natural configuration space to use is the direct product space
G=\operatorname{Diff}_{1}(\mathcal{D})\otimes\operatorname{Diff}_{2}(\mathcal{D})
. Here, the first diffeomorphism group
\operatorname{Diff}_{1}(\mathcal{D})
models the fluidic part of the dynamics and the second diffeomorphism group
\operatorname{Diff}_{2}(\mathcal{D})
models the magnetic part of the motion.
For generalarity, we assume the fluidic part of the dynamics consists of two advected quantities, they are the fluid volume density
\rho\in\operatorname{Den}(\mathcal{D})
and the fluid entropy per mass
s\in\mathcal{F}(\mathcal{D})
. Similarly, the magnetic part of the dynamics also consists of an advected quantity which is the electron charge density
\widetilde{n}\in\operatorname{Den}(\mathcal{D})
. These advected quantities break the symmetry group of the dynamics from the configuration manifold
G
to isotropy subgroup
\operatorname{Diff}_{\rho_{0},s_{0}}(\mathcal{D})\otimes\operatorname{Diff}_{\widetilde{n}_{0}}(\mathcal{D})
where the notations are

\displaystyle\operatorname{Diff}_{\rho_{0},s_{0}}(\mathcal{D})=\{g\in\operatorname{Diff}_{1}(\mathcal{D})\,|\,\rho_{0}g=\rho,s_{0}g=s_{0}\}\,,\quad\text{and}\quad\operatorname{Diff}_{\widetilde{n}_{0}}(\mathcal{D})=\{g\in\operatorname{Diff}_{2}(\mathcal{D})\,|\,\widetilde{n}_{0}g=\widetilde{n}_{0}\}\,.

To use a symmetry reduced variational principle to derive the equations of motion for ideal Hall MHD, we use the Hamilton–Pontryagin principle

\displaystyle\begin{split}0=\delta S&=:\delta\int_{t_{0}}^{t_{1}}\underbrace{\left<\mu\,,\,\partial_{t}\widetilde{\phi}\cdot\widetilde{\phi}^{-1}\right>-h(\mu,\widetilde{n})}_{\hbox{Magneto-}}+\underbrace{\ell(u,\rho,s)+\left<\pi\,,\,\partial_{t}\phi\cdot\phi^{-1}-u\right>}_{\hbox{hydrodynamics}}+\underbrace{\left<\frac{a\rho}{\widetilde{n}}\mu\,,\,\partial_{t}\phi\cdot\phi^{-1}\right>}_{\hbox{minimal coupling}}\\
&\qquad\qquad+\underbrace{\left<\lambda_{\rho}\,,\,\rho_{0}\cdot\phi^{-1}-\rho_{t}\right>+\left<\lambda_{s}\,,\,s_{0}\cdot\phi^{-1}-s_{t}\right>+\left<\lambda_{\widetilde{n}}\,,\,{\widetilde{n}}_{0}\cdot\widetilde{\phi}^{-1}-{\widetilde{n}}_{t}\right>}_{\hbox{advection contraints}}\,dt\,,\end{split}

(2.7)

where all variations are arbitrary and constrained to vanish at the endpoints
t=t_{0},t_{1}
.
The various symbols appearing in the variational principle (
2.7
) are defined as follows. The fluid velocity is denoted by
u\in\mathfrak{X}_{1}(\mathcal{D})
which is naturally related to a path in the diffeomorphism group
\phi_{t}\in\operatorname{Diff}_{1}(\mathcal{D})
by
u:=\partial_{t}\phi\cdot\phi^{-1}
. There are two advected quantities associated with the fluidic part of the motion which are the volume density
\rho\in\operatorname{Den}(\mathcal{D})
and the entropy per unit mass
s\in\mathcal{F}(\mathcal{D})
.

The magnetic part of the motion is generated by the momentum one-form density,
\mu\in\mathfrak{X}_{2}^{*}(\mathcal{D})
, which is the dual variable to the vector field

v:=\partial_{t}\widetilde{\phi}\cdot\widetilde{\phi}^{-1}\in\mathfrak{X}_{2}(\mathcal{D})

where
\widetilde{\phi}\in\operatorname{Diff}_{2}(\mathcal{D})
. Physically, the momentum density
\mu
is related to the magnetic vector potential one-form
A\in\Lambda^{1}(\mathcal{D})
by the electron charge density
\widetilde{n}\in\operatorname{Den}(\mathcal{D})
by the tensor product
R\mu=A\otimes\widetilde{n}
where
R\in\mathbb{R}
is the Larmor ratio (Hall scaling parameter). Furthermore, the charge density
\widetilde{n}={\widetilde{n}}_{0}\cdot\widetilde{\phi}^{-1}
is advected by the magnetic field part of the dynamics, as

\partial_{t}\tilde{n}=-\,\mathcal{L}_{\partial_{t}\widetilde{\phi}\,\cdot\,\widetilde{\phi}^{-1}}\tilde{n}=:-\,\mathcal{L}_{v}\tilde{n}\,.

Thus, in the variational principle (
2.7
), we have the reduced Lagrangian
\ell(u,\rho,s)
together with the Hamilton–Pontryagin constraints for the variables
u,\rho
and
s
in the fluidic part of the dynamics; as well as a reduced phase space Lagrangian

\left<\mu\,,\,\partial_{t}\widetilde{\phi}\cdot\widetilde{\phi}^{-1}\right>-h(\mu,\widetilde{n})\,,

together with the push-forward constraints for
\widetilde{n}
in the magnetic part of the motion. To model the interaction between the fluidic and magnetic parts of the motion, we introduced a minimal coupling term in the form
\left<(a\rho/\widetilde{n})\mu\,,\,u\right>
where the constant
a\in\mathbb{R}
is the electron/ion charge to mass density ratio and
u=\partial_{t}\phi\cdot\phi^{-1}
.

) and the definition of magnetic potential in terms of magnetic momentum density,
\mu
, and electron charge density,
\tilde{n}
, given by

\displaystyle\mu/\tilde{n}:=A/R

(2.19)

imply Ohm’s Law

\displaystyle(\partial_{t}+\mathcal{L}_{v})\frac{\mu}{\widetilde{n}}

\displaystyle=\mathbf{d}\left(-\frac{\delta h}{\delta\widetilde{n}}+v\intprod\frac{\mu}{\widetilde{n}}-\frac{\delta h}{\delta\mu}\intprod\frac{\mu}{\widetilde{n}}\right)=\mathbf{d}\left(v\intprod\frac{\mu}{\widetilde{n}}\right)\,,

(2.20)

\displaystyle\hbox{and hence}\qquad\partial_{t}A

\displaystyle=-\mathcal{L}_{v}A+\mathbf{d}\left(v\intprod A\right)=-v\,\intprod\mathbf{d}A=-\,v\intprod B\,,

(2.21)

where the quantity
B:=\mathbf{d}A\in\Lambda^{2}(\mathcal{D})
denotes the magnetic field two-form (magnetic flux). Further more, taking the exterior derivative
\mathbf{d}
gives the advection of the
B
,

\displaystyle\partial_{t}B+\mathcal{L}_{v}B=0\,.

(2.22)

Physically, this implies that the
B
-field lines are transported by the total velocity
v
which is the sum of the fluid velocity
u
representing the ions and the Hall velocity
v_{H}
representing the electron current. A direct calculation reveals the ideal Hall MHD equations preserves the magnetic helicity integral
\int_{\mathcal{D}}A\wedge B
.

Remark 2.4

(The MHD limit)
.

In the limit that
R\to 0
, the Hall velocity
v_{H}\to 0
. Then, the dynamics of
B
is the advection by the fluid velocity
u
only

\displaystyle\partial_{t}B+\mathcal{L}_{u}B=0\,,

and the magnetic field lines become frozen into the fluid flow. Thus, when
R\to 0
, variational equations arising from the Hall MHD action principle reduce to the standard equations for compressible MHD flow. We further note that regardless of the value of
R
, the last term in (
2.15
) always represents one-half the square of the magnetic field
B:=\mathbf{d}A
as occurs in the Euler-Poincaré derivation of the standard MHD equations.

For the fluidic part of the motion, one has the fluid Lagrangian

\displaystyle\ell(u,\rho,s)=\int_{M}\frac{1}{2}\rho|u|^{2}-\rho\,e(s,\rho)\,dV\,,

where
|u|^{2}:=u\intprod u^{\flat}
, alongside its functional derivatives

\displaystyle\frac{\delta\ell}{\delta u}=\rho u^{\flat}\,,\quad\frac{\delta\ell}{\delta\rho}=\frac{1}{2}|u|^{2}-\widehat{h}(p,s)=:{\cal B}\,\quad\hbox{and}\quad\frac{\delta\ell}{\delta s}=-\rho T\,,

where the First Law of Thermodynamics for the fluid
\mathbf{d}e=-\,p\mathbf{d}\rho^{-1}+T\mathbf{d}s
implies that the enthalpy per unit mass
\widehat{h}(p,s)
satisfies

\displaystyle\mathbf{d}\widehat{h}=\rho^{-1}\mathbf{d}p+T\mathbf{d}s\quad\hbox{with}\quad\widehat{h}=e+\rho\frac{\partial e}{\partial\rho}\,,

(2.23)

where
p
denotes thermodynamic pressure and
T
denotes temperature. Substituting the functional derivatives of the fluid Lagrangian into the momentum equation (
2.10
) and using equation (
2.21
) yields the equation for the total momentum 1-form
u^{\flat}+aA
as

\displaystyle\left(\partial_{t}+\mathcal{L}_{u}\right)\left(u^{\flat}+\frac{a}{R}A\right)=\mathbf{d}\left(u\intprod\left(\frac{1}{2}u^{\flat}+\frac{a}{R}A\right)\right)-\frac{1}{\rho}\mathbf{d}p\,.

(2.24)

Notice that, from equation (
2.21
), we have, upon dropping
\flat
to simplify notation because
A
is understood to be a 1-form,

(\partial_{t}+\mathcal{L}_{u})A=\mathbf{d}(v\intprod A)+\mathcal{L}_{u-v}A=\mathbf{d}(u\intprod A)+(u-v)\intprod\mathbf{d}A\,,

and hence the the equation for the fluid momentum
u^{\flat}
is

\displaystyle\begin{split}\left(\partial_{t}+\mathcal{L}_{u}\right)u^{\flat}&=\frac{1}{2}\mathbf{d}\left(u\intprod u^{\flat}\right)-\frac{1}{\star\rho}\mathbf{d}p+(v-u)\intprod\frac{a}{R}\mathbf{d}A\\
&=\frac{1}{2}\mathbf{d}\left(u\intprod u^{\flat}\right)-\frac{1}{\star\rho}\left(\mathbf{d}p+(\bm{\delta}B)^{\sharp}\intprod B\right)\,,\end{split}

(2.25)

where in the last equality we have assumed the local charge neutrality condition. Thus, by assuming that the initial conditions
\rho_{0}
and
\widetilde{n}_{0}
satisfy
a\rho_{0}=-\widetilde{n}_{0}
, one simplifies the equation set (
2.10
) – (
2.14
) to

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\left(\partial_{t}+\mathcal{L}_{u}\right)u^{\flat}=\frac{1}{2}\mathbf{d}\left(u\intprod u^{\flat}\right)-\frac{1}{\star\rho}\left(\mathbf{d}p(e,s)+(\bm{\delta}B)^{\sharp}\intprod B\right)\,,\\
&\partial_{t}A+u\intprod B-\frac{R}{a(\star\rho)}(\bm{\delta}B)^{\sharp}\intprod B=0\,,\\
&\left(\partial_{t}+\mathcal{L}_{u}\right)\rho=0\,,\\
&\left(\partial_{t}+\mathcal{L}_{u}\right)s=0\,,\quad\text{with}\quad a\rho=-\widetilde{n}\,.\end{split}

(2.26)

Remark 2.5

(Ideal Compressible Hall MHD in 3D)
.

When working in a three dimensional domain with the Euclidean metric, we have the following definition of the Hall velocity
\mathbf{v}_{H}
expressed in terms of basis functions

\displaystyle\begin{split}v_{H}=\mathbf{v}_{H}\cdot\nabla=\frac{\delta h}{\delta\mu}=\frac{R}{\star\widetilde{n}}\left(\bm{\delta}\mathbf{d}A\right)^{\sharp}=\frac{R}{\star\widetilde{n}}\left(\star\mathbf{d}\star\mathbf{d}A\right)^{\sharp}&=\frac{R}{\star\widetilde{n}}\left(\left(\mathrm{curl}\mathrm{curl}{\mathbf{A}}\right)\cdot d\mathbf{x}\right)^{\sharp}\\
&=\frac{R}{\star\widetilde{n}}\left(\mathrm{curl}\mathrm{curl}\bm{A}\right)\cdot\nabla=\frac{R}{\star\widetilde{n}}\left(\mathrm{curl}\bm{B}\right)\cdot\nabla\,,\end{split}

where
\mathbf{A}
and
\mathbf{B}
are the basis coefficients of the magnetic potential one-form
A:=\mathbf{A}\cdot d\mathbf{x}
and the magnetic field two-form
B:=\mathbf{d}A=\mathrm{curl}\mathbf{A}\cdot\star d\mathbf{x}
respectively.
Then, by (
2.11
) we have

\displaystyle v=-\frac{a\rho}{\widetilde{n}}u+\frac{\delta h}{\delta\mu}=\left(\bm{u}-\frac{R}{a\rho}\mathrm{curl}\bm{B}\right)\cdot\nabla\,.

Following from the previous specialisation to the Euclidean 3D domain, we may write the ideal compressible Hall MHD equations in vector calculus form as

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\partial_{t}\mathbf{u}+\mathbf{u}\cdot\nabla\mathbf{u}+\frac{1}{\rho}\left(\nabla p(e,s)+\mathbf{B}\times\operatorname{curl}\mathbf{B}\right)=0\,,\\
&\partial_{t}\mathbf{A}+\mathbf{B}\times\left(\mathbf{u}-\frac{R}{a\rho}\operatorname{curl}\mathbf{B}\right)=0\,,\\
&\partial_{t}\rho+\nabla\cdot(\mathbf{u}\rho)=0\,,\\
&\partial_{t}s+\mathbf{u}\cdot\nabla s=0\,,\end{split}

(2.27)

When dissipation by viscosity and resistivity are included in these equations, they become globally well-posed
[
24
]
.

For isentropic (or isothermal) Hall MHD flow, the term
T\mathbf{d}s
vanishes in (
2.23
) and the Hall MHD equations preserve the
Hall helicity
defined as the spatial integral over the domain

\displaystyle\Lambda_{H}=\int C\wedge\mathbf{d}C=\int\mathbf{C}\cdot\mathrm{curl}\mathbf{C}\,d^{3}x\quad\hbox{with}\quad\mathbf{C}:=\mathbf{u}+\frac{a}{R}\mathbf{A}

as defined in equation (
2.5
).
The Hall helicity
\Lambda_{H}
is a Casimir functional for the Lie-Poisson bracket.
Conservation of the helicity
\Lambda_{H}
implies preservation by isentropic Hall MHD of the linkage number of the field lines of
\mathrm{curl}\mathbf{C}
. Moreover, a critical point of the sum of the Hamiltonian and the Hall helicity is an equilibrium flow.

Cases with
\mathrm{curl}\mathbf{C}=\lambda\mathbf{C}
with constant
\lambda\neq 0
can exhibit Lagrangian chaos as an analogue of the ABC flow of MHD
[
3
]
. Vanishing helicity results in a Hall Beltrami flow for which
\mathbf{C}\times\mathrm{curl}\mathbf{C}=\nabla\phi
and the evolution of
\mathbf{C}
takes place on level sets of
\phi
. For the corresponding Euler fluid case, see, e.g.,
[
35
]
.

2.4
Deterministic Hamiltonian structure

Returning to Hamilton’s Principle as applied in equation (
2.7
), we may Legendre transform the fluid part of the dynamics to obtain a Hamiltonian,
\mathfrak{h}(\pi,\rho,s)
, defined as

\ell(u,\rho,s):=\left<\pi\,,\,u\right>-\mathfrak{h}(\pi,\rho,s)\,.

(2.28)

Substituting this into equation (
2.7
), we have

\displaystyle\begin{split}0=\delta S&=:\delta\int_{t_{0}}^{t_{1}}\left<\mu\,,\,\partial_{t}\widetilde{\phi}\cdot\widetilde{\phi}^{-1}\right>-h(\mu,\widetilde{n})+\left<\pi\,,\,\partial_{t}\phi\cdot\phi^{-1}\right>-\mathfrak{h}(\pi,\rho,s)+\left<\frac{a\rho}{\widetilde{n}}\mu\,,\,\partial_{t}\phi\cdot\phi^{-1}\right>\\
&\qquad\qquad+\left<\lambda_{\rho}\,,\,\rho_{0}\cdot\phi^{-1}-\rho_{t}\right>+\left<\lambda_{s}\,,\,s_{0}\cdot\phi^{-1}-s_{t}\right>+\left<\lambda_{\widetilde{n}}\,,\,{\widetilde{n}}_{0}\cdot\widetilde{\phi}^{-1}-{\widetilde{n}}_{t}\right>\,dt\,.\end{split}

(2.29)

This form naturally lends itself to the identification of a new variable,
M:=\pi+\frac{a\rho}{\widetilde{n}}\mu
, and a Hamiltonian defined as the sum of the magnetic and fluid Hamiltonians as

\mathcal{H}(M,\rho,s,\mu,\widetilde{n}):=h(\mu,\widetilde{n})+\mathfrak{h}\left(M-\frac{a\rho}{\widetilde{n}}\mu,\rho,s\right)\,.

(2.30)

Hamilton’s Principle is therefore

\displaystyle\begin{split}0&=\delta S=:\delta\int_{t_{0}}^{t_{1}}\left<\mu\,,\,\partial_{t}\widetilde{\phi}\cdot\widetilde{\phi}^{-1}\right>+\left<M\,,\,\partial_{t}\phi\cdot\phi^{-1}\right>-\mathcal{H}(M,\rho,s,\mu,\widetilde{n})\\
&\qquad\qquad+\left<\lambda_{\rho}\,,\,\rho_{0}\cdot\phi^{-1}-\rho_{t}\right>+\left<\lambda_{s}\,,\,s_{0}\cdot\phi^{-1}-s_{t}\right>+\left<\lambda_{\widetilde{n}}\,,\,{\widetilde{n}}_{0}\cdot\widetilde{\phi}^{-1}-{\widetilde{n}}_{t}\right>\,dt\\
&=\int_{t_{0}}^{t_{1}}\left<\delta\mu\,,\,\partial_{t}\widetilde{\phi}\cdot\widetilde{\phi}^{-1}-\frac{\delta\mathcal{H}}{\delta\mu}\right>+\left<\delta M\,,\,\partial_{t}\phi\cdot\phi^{-1}-\frac{\delta\mathcal{H}}{\delta M}\right>+\left<-(\partial_{t}+\operatorname{ad}^{*}_{\partial_{t}\widetilde{\phi}\cdot\widetilde{\phi}^{-1}})\mu+\lambda_{\widetilde{n}}\diamond\widetilde{n}\,,\,\widetilde{\eta}\right>\\
&\qquad\qquad+\left<-(\partial_{t}+\operatorname{ad}^{*}_{\partial_{t}\phi\cdot\phi^{-1}})M+\lambda_{\rho}\diamond\rho+\lambda_{s}\diamond s\,,\,\eta\right>-\left<\lambda_{\rho}+\frac{\delta\mathcal{H}}{\delta\rho}\,,\,\delta\rho\right>-\left<\lambda_{s}+\frac{\delta\mathcal{H}}{\delta s}\,,\,\delta s\right>\\
&\qquad\qquad-\left<\lambda_{\widetilde{n}}+\frac{\delta\mathcal{H}}{\delta\widetilde{n}}\,,\,\delta\widetilde{n}\right>+\left<\delta\lambda_{\rho}\,,\,\rho_{0}\cdot\phi^{-1}-\rho_{t}\right>+\left<\delta\lambda_{s}\,,\,s_{0}\cdot\phi^{-1}-s_{t}\right>+\left<\delta\lambda_{\widetilde{n}}\,,\,{\widetilde{n}}_{0}\cdot\widetilde{\phi}^{-1}-{\widetilde{n}}_{t}\right>\,dt\,,\end{split}

(2.31)

The equations corresponding to this have the following Lie-Poisson structure, expressed here in matrix form

\partial_{t}\begin{pmatrix}M\\
\rho\\
s\\
\mu\\
\widetilde{n}\end{pmatrix}=-\begin{pmatrix}\operatorname{ad}^{*}_{\Box}M&\Box\diamond\rho&\Box\diamond s&0&0\\
\mathcal{L}_{\Box}\rho&0&0&0&0\\
\mathcal{L}_{\Box}s&0&0&0&0\\
0&0&0&\operatorname{ad}^{*}_{\Box}\mu&\Box\diamond\widetilde{n}\\
0&0&0&\mathcal{L}_{\Box}\widetilde{n}&0\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}\delta\mathcal{H}/\delta M\\
\delta\mathcal{H}/\delta\rho\\
\delta\mathcal{H}/\delta s\\
\delta\mathcal{H}/\delta\mu\\
\delta\mathcal{H}/\delta\widetilde{n}\end{pmatrix}\,.

(2.32)

The restult of this is that the examples considered in this paper have a Lie-Poisson structure, which we illustrate below for three dimensional compressible Hall MHD on a Euclidean domain. For this example, we will use vector calculus notation to better illustrate the problem for readers less familiar with exterior calculus. When division by a density occurs in this section, it is not necessary to take the same precaution as discussed in Remark
2.2
. Instead, we always mean division by the scalar dual to the density.

The Hamiltonian corresponding to the model of compressible MHD presented in Section
2.3
is

\displaystyle{\mathcal{H}}(M,\rho,s,\mu,\widetilde{n})=\int_{\mathcal{D}}\frac{1}{2\rho}\left|\bm{M}-\frac{a\rho}{\widetilde{n}}\bm{\mu}\right|^{2}+\rho e(\rho,s)+\frac{R^{2}}{2}\left|\operatorname{curl}\frac{\bm{\mu}}{\widetilde{n}}\right|^{2}d^{3}x\,.

(2.33)

The Poisson bracket, in vector calculus notation, is

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\begin{pmatrix}M_{i}\\
\rho\\
s\\
\mu_{i}\\
\widetilde{n}\end{pmatrix}=-\begin{pmatrix}M_{i}\partial_{j}+\partial_{i}M_{j}&\rho\partial_{i}&-s,_{j}&0&0\\
\partial_{j}\rho&0&0&0&0\\
s,_{j}&0&0&0&0\\
0&0&0&\mu_{i}\partial_{j}+\partial_{i}\mu_{j}&\widetilde{n}\partial_{i}\\
0&0&0&\partial_{j}\widetilde{n}&0\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}\delta\mathcal{H}/\delta M_{j}\\
\delta\mathcal{H}/\delta\rho\\
\delta\mathcal{H}/\delta s\\
\delta\mathcal{H}/\delta\mu_{j}\\
\delta\mathcal{H}/\delta\widetilde{n}\end{pmatrix}\,,

(2.34)

which is the Lie-Poisson bracket on the following Lie co-algebra:

\displaystyle\mathfrak{X}^{*}(\mathcal{D})\ltimes\left(\Lambda^{0}(\mathcal{D})\oplus\Lambda^{n}(\mathcal{D})\right)\oplus\left(\mathfrak{X}^{*}(\mathcal{D})\ltimes\Lambda^{n}(\mathcal{D})\right)\,.

(2.35)

Varying the Hamiltonian yields,

\displaystyle\begin{split}\delta{\mathcal{H}}&=\int_{\mathcal{D}}\delta\bm{M}\cdot\frac{1}{\rho}\left(\bm{M}-\frac{a\rho}{\widetilde{n}}\bm{\mu}\right)+\delta\bm{\mu}\cdot\left(\frac{1}{\rho}\left(\bm{M}-\frac{a\rho}{\widetilde{n}}\bm{\mu}\right)\left(-\frac{a\rho}{\widetilde{n}}\right)+\frac{1}{\widetilde{n}}\operatorname{curl}\operatorname{curl}\frac{\bm{\mu}}{\widetilde{n}}\right)\\
&\qquad+\delta\widetilde{n}\left(\frac{1}{\rho}\left(\bm{M}-\frac{a\rho}{\widetilde{n}}\bm{\mu}\right)\cdot\left(\frac{a\rho}{\widetilde{n}^{2}}\bm{\mu}\right)-\left(\operatorname{curl}\operatorname{curl}\frac{\bm{\mu}}{\widetilde{n}}\right)\cdot\frac{\bm{\mu}}{\widetilde{n}^{2}}\right)+\delta s\,\rho T\\
&\qquad+\delta\rho\left(\widehat{h}(p,s)-\frac{1}{2\rho^{2}}\Big|\bm{M}-\frac{a\rho}{\widetilde{n}}\bm{\mu}\Big|^{2}-\frac{a\bm{\mu}}{\rho\widetilde{n}}\Big(\bm{M}-\frac{a\rho}{\widetilde{n}}\bm{\mu}\Big)\right)d^{3}x\end{split}

(2.36)

and assembling the variational derivatives into the above Lie-Poisson equations (
2.34
) results in the expected equations of motion also derived in Section
2.3
. In particular, expressed
in terms of the vector fields

\displaystyle\bm{u}:=\frac{1}{\rho}\left(\bm{M}-\frac{a\rho\bm{\mu}}{\widetilde{n}}\right)\,,\quad\text{and}\quad\bm{v}:=\frac{1}{\widetilde{n}}\operatorname{curl}\operatorname{curl}\frac{\bm{\mu}}{\widetilde{n}}\,,

the following equations arise for the magnetic variables,

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\partial_{t}\mu+\operatorname{ad}^{*}_{-\frac{a\rho}{\widetilde{n}}u}\mu+\operatorname{ad}^{*}_{v}\mu+\widetilde{n}\mathbf{d}\left(\bm{u}\cdot\frac{a\rho}{\widetilde{n}}\bm{\mu}-\frac{\bm{\mu}}{\widetilde{n}^{2}}\cdot\bm{v}\right)=0\,,\\
&\partial_{t}\widetilde{n}+\mathcal{L}_{-\frac{a\rho}{\widetilde{n}}u}\widetilde{n}+\mathcal{L}_{v}\widetilde{n}=0\,.\end{split}

(2.37)

Remark 2.6

(An alternative Hamiltonian structure)
.

Making the change of variables

\displaystyle\left(M,\rho,s,\mu,\widetilde{n}\right)\longrightarrow\left(M,\rho,s,A,\widetilde{n}\right)\,,

(2.38)

where
\mu:=A\otimes\widetilde{n}\in\mathfrak{X}^{*}(\mathcal{D})
is expressed in terms of the magnetic vector potential,
A=\bm{A}\cdot d\mathbf{x}\in\Lambda^{1}(\mathcal{D})
, and the fluid charge density
\widetilde{n}d^{3}x\in\Lambda^{n}(\mathcal{D})
. The relevant Lie co-algebra becomes
\mathfrak{X}^{*}(\mathcal{D})\ltimes\left(\Lambda^{0}(\mathcal{D})\oplus\Lambda^{n}(\mathcal{D})\right)\otimes\left(\Lambda^{1}(\mathcal{{D}})\oplus\Lambda^{n}(\mathcal{D})\right)
and the Poisson bracket transforms into

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\begin{pmatrix}M_{i}\\
\rho\\

A_{i}\\
\widetilde{n}\end{pmatrix}=-\begin{pmatrix}M_{i}\partial_{j}+p_{i}M_{j}&\rho\partial_{i}&-s,_{j}&0&0\\
\partial_{j}\rho&0&0&0&0\\
s,_{j}&0&0&0&0\\
0&0&0&\widetilde{n}^{-1}\left(A_{i},_{j}-A_{j},_{i}\right)&\partial_{i}\\

\delta\widetilde{\mathcal{H}}/\delta\widetilde{n}\end{pmatrix}\,,

(2.39)

where the Hamiltonian,

, has been expressed as a Hamiltonian,
\widetilde{\mathcal{H}}
, in the new variables as

\displaystyle\widetilde{\mathcal{H}}(M,\rho,s,A,\widetilde{n})=\int_{\mathcal{D}}\frac{1}{2\rho}\left|\bm{M}-\frac{a\rho}{R}\bm{A}\right|^{2}+\rho e(\rho,s)+\frac{1}{2}\left|\operatorname{curl}\bm{A}\right|^{2}d^{3}x\,.

(2.40)

This Hamiltonian formulation of Hall MHD agrees with the existing literature on the topic
[

,

]
.

2.5
Ideal incompressible Hall MHD

The geometric structures presented thus far in this section have been tailored for compressible Hall MHD. Here, we will show that only a minimal modification is required to consider the inhomogeneous incompressible case. In this case, the collection of quantities which are advected by the velocity
u
becomes the mass density,
D\in{\rm Den}(\mathcal{D})
, and the potential temperature,
\rho\in\Lambda^{0}(\mathcal{D})
. As before, we also have the charge density,
\widetilde{n}\in{\rm Den}(\mathcal{D})
, which is advected by the velocity
v=u+v_{H}
. Incompressible Hall MHD can be derived by modifying Hamilton’s Principle as it appears in equation (
2.7
) to reflect the fact that the advected quantities are different and to alter the minimal coupling term to be
\left<\frac{D\rho a}{\widetilde{n}}\mu\,,\,\partial_{t}\phi\cdot\phi^{-1}\right>
. Note that this is simply a consequence of the fact that the volume measure has changed to be
D\rho
. Once these changes are made, the Hamiltonian structure is qualitatively unchanged. The fluid part of the Hamiltonian is now taken to correspond to inhomogeneous thermal Euler, and the total Hamiltonian for divergence free Hall MHD is now taken to be

{\mathcal{H}}_{\rm div}(M,D,\rho,\mu,\widetilde{n})=\int_{\mathcal{D}}\frac{1}{2D\rho}\left|M-\frac{D\rho a}{\widetilde{n}}\mu\right|^{2}+p(D-1)+\frac{R^{2}}{2}\left|\mathbf{d}\frac{\mu}{\widetilde{n}}\right|^{2}dV\,,

(2.41)

taken together with the Lie-Poisson structure

\partial_{t}\begin{pmatrix}M\\
D\\
\rho\\
\mu\\
\widetilde{n}\end{pmatrix}=-\begin{pmatrix}\operatorname{ad}^{*}_{\Box}M&\Box\diamond D&\Box\diamond\rho&0&0\\
\mathcal{L}_{\Box}\rho&0&0&0&0\\
\mathcal{L}_{\Box}s&0&0&0&0\\
0&0&0&\operatorname{ad}^{*}_{\Box}\mu&\Box\diamond\widetilde{n}\\
0&0&0&\mathcal{L}_{\Box}\widetilde{n}&0\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}\delta\mathcal{H}_{\rm div}/\delta M\\
\delta\mathcal{H}_{\rm div}/\delta D\\
\delta\mathcal{H}_{\rm div}/\delta\rho\\
\delta\mathcal{H}_{\rm div}/\delta\mu\\
\delta\mathcal{H}_{\rm div}/\delta\widetilde{n}\end{pmatrix}\,.

(2.42)

Computing the variational derivatives and substituting these into the Lie-Poisson equations, we get

\displaystyle(\partial_{t}+\operatorname{ad}^{*}_{u})M

\displaystyle=-D\mathbf{d}\left(p-\frac{\rho|u|^{2}}{2}-a\rho\,u\intprod\frac{\mu}{\widetilde{n}}\right)+\left(-\frac{D|u|^{2}}{2}-Da\,u\intprod\frac{\mu}{\widetilde{n}}\right)\mathbf{d}\rho\,,

(2.43)

\displaystyle\hbox{where}\quad M

\displaystyle=D\rho u^{\flat}+\frac{D\rho a}{\widetilde{n}}\mu\,,

(2.44)

\displaystyle(\partial_{t}+\operatorname{ad}^{*}_{v})\mu

\displaystyle=\widetilde{n}\mathbf{d}\left(v\intprod\frac{\mu}{\widetilde{n}}\right)\,,

(2.45)

\displaystyle\hbox{where}\quad v

\displaystyle=-\frac{D\rho a}{\widetilde{n}}u+\frac{R^{2}}{\star\widetilde{n}}\left(\bm{\delta}\mathbf{d}\frac{\mu}{\widetilde{n}}\right)^{\sharp}=-\frac{D\rho a}{\widetilde{n}}u+v_{H}\,,

(2.46)

together with the expected advection equations for
D
,
\rho
, and
\widetilde{n}
. Considering that pressure enforces
D=1
and local charge neutrality holds,
a\rho+\star\widetilde{n}=0
, the equation for
\mu/\widetilde{n}
can be written as

\displaystyle(\partial_{t}+\mathcal{L}_{u})\frac{\mu}{\widetilde{n}}

\displaystyle=-\mathcal{L}_{\frac{R^{2}}{\star\widetilde{n}}\left(\bm{\delta}\mathbf{d}\frac{\mu}{\widetilde{n}}\right)^{\sharp}}\frac{\mu}{\widetilde{n}}-\mathbf{d}\left(-\frac{R^{2}}{\star\widetilde{n}}\left(\bm{\delta}\mathbf{d}\frac{\mu}{\widetilde{n}}\right)^{\sharp}\intprod\frac{\mu}{\widetilde{n}}+\frac{D\rho a}{\widetilde{n}}u\intprod\frac{\mu}{\widetilde{n}}\right)

(2.47)

\displaystyle=\mathbf{d}\left(u\intprod\frac{\mu}{\widetilde{n}}\right)-\frac{R^{2}}{\star\widetilde{n}}\left(\bm{\delta}\mathbf{d}\frac{\mu}{\widetilde{n}}\right)^{\sharp}\intprod\mathbf{d}\frac{\mu}{\widetilde{n}}\,,

and the equation for the fluid velocity
u^{\flat}
is

\displaystyle\left(\partial_{t}+\mathcal{L}_{u}\right)u^{\flat}

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{\rho}\mathbf{d}p+\mathbf{d}\left(\frac{|u|^{2}}{2}\right)+\frac{R^{2}a}{\star\widetilde{n}}\left(\bm{\delta}\mathbf{d}\frac{\mu}{\widetilde{n}}\right)^{\sharp}\intprod\mathbf{d}\frac{\mu}{\widetilde{n}}\,,\quad\hbox{where}\quad\operatorname{div}u=0

(2.48)

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{\rho}\mathbf{d}p+\mathbf{d}\left(\frac{|u|^{2}}{2}\right)-\frac{1}{\rho}(\bm{\delta}\mathbf{d}A)^{\sharp}\intprod\mathbf{d}A\,.

Notice now that the equations derived in this section are precisely equations (
2.2
) introduced at the beginning of this paper.

3
Stochastic Hamilton-Pontryagin principles for Hall MHD

3.1
Stochastic variational principle

We will modify the above Hamilton-Pontryagin principle to include stochastic terms following Holm
[
29
]
. The variations will be constructed formally as in
[
41
]
, wherein it is demonstrated that the stochastic variational procedure is constructed such that it is rigorously defined and preserves many important properties from geometric mechanics.

The variations are constructed in the following way. Fixing arbitrary vector fields
\eta,\widetilde{\eta}\in\mathfrak{X}(\mathcal{D})
which are once differentiable in time, we construct group-valued perturbations
e_{\epsilon,t},\widetilde{e}_{\epsilon,t}\in{\rm Diff}(\mathcal{D})
as solutions to the following equations

\partial_{t}e_{\epsilon,t}=\epsilon(\partial_{t}\eta)\cdot e_{\epsilon,t}\,,\quad\hbox{and}\quad\partial_{t}\widetilde{e}_{\epsilon,t}=\epsilon(\partial_{t}\widetilde{\eta})\cdot\widetilde{e}_{\epsilon,t}\,,

(3.1)

where
(\,\cdot\,)
denotes the lifted right action of group elements on the algebra. These may be used to construct a family of variations of the curves
\phi_{t}
and
\widetilde{\phi}_{t}
as

\phi_{\epsilon,t}=e_{\epsilon,t}\cdot\phi_{t}\,,\quad\hbox{and}\quad\widetilde{\phi}_{\epsilon,t}=\widetilde{e}_{\epsilon,t}\cdot\widetilde{\phi}_{t}\,.

(3.2)

As was shown in
[
1
]
, this construction mimics the exponential map since

\frac{\partial}{\partial\epsilon}\bigg|_{\epsilon=0}e_{\epsilon,t}=\eta\,,\quad\frac{\partial}{\partial\epsilon}\bigg|_{\epsilon=0}e_{\epsilon,t}^{-1}=-\eta\,,\quad\frac{\partial}{\partial\epsilon}\bigg|_{\epsilon=0}\widetilde{e}_{\epsilon,t}=\widetilde{\eta}\,,\quad\hbox{and}\quad\frac{\partial}{\partial\epsilon}\bigg|_{\epsilon=0}\widetilde{e}_{\epsilon,t}^{-1}=-\widetilde{\eta}\,,

which implies that

\delta\phi\cdot\phi^{-1}=\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial\epsilon}\bigg|_{\epsilon=0}\phi_{\epsilon,t}\right)\cdot\phi^{-1}=\eta\,,\quad\hbox{and}\quad\delta\widetilde{\phi}\cdot\widetilde{\phi}^{-1}=\widetilde{\eta}\,.

This construction results in the following formulas for the variations

3.4
Energy preserving stochastic perturbations

When introducing stochasticity into the Hamilton–Pontryagin variational principle for the Hall MHD equations, there are no unique choices of stochastic perturbations. As demonstrated in equation (
3.5
), one particular type of stochasticity takes the form of stochastic Hamiltonians
\left<\pi\,,\,\xi_{i}\right>
and
h_{i}(\mu,\widetilde{n})
. These Hamiltonians generate stochastic transport vector fields and the stochastic perturbations are considered to be the transport noise.

Another class of stochastic perturbations derived in
[
32
]
and known as Stochastic Forcing by Lie Transport (SFLT) introduces stochastic
forces
rather than stochastic
transport
. These stochastic forces are imposed via a stochastic reduced Lagrange d’Alembert variational principle and they designed to preserve the domain integrated energy of the unperturbed dynamics.

To construct the SFLT stochasticity, we again consider the set of driving semimartingales
\{W^{i}_{t}\}_{i=1,\ldots}
and
\{B^{i}_{t}\}_{i=1,\ldots}
which are two collections of i.i.d Brownian motions. Here,
W^{i}_{t}
are the driving stochastic processes for the fluidic part of the dynamics and
B^{i}_{t}
are the driving stochastic processes for the magnetic part of the dynamics. We define the amplitudes of the stochastic perturbations using quantities taking values in the Lie co-algebra (
2.35
). Namely, we have the set of amplitudes
\{f^{M}_{i}\}_{i=1,\ldots}\in\mathfrak{X}^{*}(\mathcal{{D}})
,
\{f^{\rho}_{i}\}_{i=1,\ldots}\in\Lambda^{n}(\mathcal{{D}})
,
\{f^{s}_{i}\}_{i=1,\ldots}\in\Lambda^{0}(\mathcal{{D}})
,
\{f^{\mu}_{i}\}_{i=1,\ldots}\in\mathfrak{X}^{*}(\mathcal{{D}})
and
\{f^{\widetilde{n}}_{i}\}_{i=1,\ldots}\in\Lambda^{n}(\mathcal{{D}})
. These quantities define the stochastic perturbations to
M
,
\rho
,
s
,
\mu
and
\widetilde{n}
respectively.

2.35

4
Open problems and plans for future work

In this paper the coordinate-free Eulerian equations of Hall MHD have been derived by using two different methods SALT and SFLt that each preserve the relabelling symmetry of fluid dynamics induced in the passage from the Lagrangian, to the Eulerian representation. Taking this step enabled the SALT approach to be applied to introduce a type of stochastic transport that preserves the coadjoint Lie structure of the deterministic Hall MHD equations proved in the first step. Taking this step enabled the SFLT approach to deal with stochasti processes in Hall MHD which would preserve its total energy.

By taking Lagrangian expectations of SALT, one creates LA SALT
[
17
,
16
]
. The expected solutions of LA SALT satisfy deterministic equations, while the fluctuations satisfy linear equations. As a result, the LA SALT approach provides deterministic equations for their variances and higher moments. These deterministic equations for the statistics of the expected solutions in turn provide deterministic predictions of how the ‘climate’ is changing for the stochastic SALT equations, in Ed Lorenz’s sense that: “Climate is what you expect and Weather is what you get.”

In other future research, we plan to use LA SALT (Lagrangian-Averaged Stochastic Advection by Lie Transport)
[
16
,
17
]
to derive the expected equations and the dynamical equations for the variances of the fluctuations of the solutions away from its expected solution. After establishing the LA SALT equations for Hall MHD we plan to introduce double-bracket dissipation in order to characterise the invariant measures induced by this form of dissipation. The LA SALT approach preserves the coadjoint Lie structure of the deterministic, the stochastic (SALT) equations. Moreover, the combination of the expectation/fluctuation decomposition of LA SALT and the application of double bracket dissipation preserves the coadjoint structure of the approach to an invariant measure
[
2
,
15
]
.

Both Lagrangian averaging (LA) in SALT and Eulerian averaging (EA) in SFLT are possible. These complementary stochastic approaches – Langrangian transport and Eulerian forcing – may lead to a variety of opportunities to develop complementary
expectation dynamics
for fluids and and fluid plasmas.

We also plan to address the open problem of deriving modifications such as Levy area when introducing non-Brownian stochasticity such as coloured noise, fractional diffusion and/or geometric rough paths, following the pioneering work of
[
11
]
. This more general setting for transport noise may potentially reveal more details about the invariant measures for this class of stochastic fluid dynamics,
[
2
,
15
]
.

In particular, the following outstanding question and its counterpart remain:

1.

After the SALT Kelvin-Noether theorem for Brownian transport noise has been derived for a certain fluid model, does the addition of selective decay (e.g., double bracket dissipation) guarantee Gibbs invariant measure? If so, the solution behaviour would be determined and expectations could be taken in the context of standard statistical physics. This fundamental mathematical question is likely be the most difficult among the questions we wish to answer.

2.

If Gibbs invariant measure is not guaranteed, then under what conditions for Brownian transport noise with SALT in an Euler-Poincare fluid model does the addition of selective decay (or double bracket dissipation) lead to Gibbs invariant measure?

Kundt gravitational waves coupled with a non-Noetherian conformal scalar field
Pedro A. Sánchez

Abstract
Type N spacetimes of the Kundt class are presented as solutions to Einstein’s equations sourced by a real scalar field whose equation of motion is conformally invariant and that generalizes the standard conformal scalar field. The specific spacetimes considered model gravitational waves with uniform and totally geodesic wave fronts, propagating in a maximally symmetric background, and are characterized by the value of their constant scalar curvature and by the so-called wave profile function. All subclasses of such spacetimes are analyzed. It is shown that the scalar field solutions generically divide into two branches, of which one has a strikingly different behavior from that of the standard conformal case. The scalar field contributes to the equation satisfied by the wave profile function by adding new singular terms. Closed form and mode solutions for the wave profile function are found for different scenarios. The resulting energy-momentum tensor has a null eigenvector, but is more general than the pure radiation type usually coupled with this kind of spacetimes.



I
Introduction

Among spacetimes modeling gravitational waves, those which are Petrov type N
Petrov 1954
;
*petrov2000classification
and which belong to the Kundt class
Kundt 1961
stand out for their simplicity and for their physical and geometrical richness. Firstly, vacuum type N spacetimes exhibit appealing wave properties: deviation of their timelike geodesics has a pure transverse character (cf. §2-2.10 of
Ehlers and Kundt 1962
), their curvature tensor approximates at null infinity that of an isolated system under generic conditions
Sachs 1961
, and its curvature invariants of order zero vanish (cf. §92 of
Landau and Lifshitz 1975
). Secondly, when the principal null direction (PND) of an algebraically special spacetime has vanishing optical scalars —thus defining a Kundt class spacetime— it becomes an eigenvector of the Ricci tensor and, via Einstein’s equations, also of the energy-momentum tensor of the matter content. Electromagnetic radiation, null dust or a generic pure radiation matter (cf. §4.3 of
Hawking and Ellis 1973
or §5.2 of
Stephani et al. 2003
) are some examples of this type of energy-momentum tensor. Thus, spacetimes that are type N with their PND, say
k
, defining a Kundt class spacetime come out as reasonable simplified models of gravitational waves. In fact, they have been studied as such for over half a century.

The first to be studied
Brinkmann 1923
;
*brinkmann1925einstein
were the so-called plane-fronted waves with parallel rays, or pp waves for short, which are given locally by

ds^{2}_{pp}=Hdu^{2}-2dudv+2\left|d\zeta\right|^{2},

(1)

with
k=\partial_{v}
and
H
a real-valued function constant along each null geodesic (cf. also §2-5 of
Ehlers and Kundt 1962
and references cited therein). They are geometrically characterized by the fact that
k
is covariantly constant, justifying the term parallel rays; additionally, the surfaces at constant
u
and
v
, called wave surfaces, have vanishing Gaussian and extrinsic curvature, justifying the term plane-fronted. Furthermore, their principal null direction turns out to be a Killing vector field (KVF). Later on, Kundt
Kundt 1961
found the most general type N vacuum solutions. They describe also plane-fronted waves, but
k
is not necessarily covariantly constant, neither a Killing vector field.

Generalizing the vacuum solutions, all type N Kundt class spacetimes with a cosmological constant
\Lambda
and without additional matter are known. All of them feature uniform and totally geodesic wave surfaces, having constant Gaussian curvature, determined by
\Lambda
, and vanishing extrinsic curvature. They have the intriguing property that the third Lie derivative of the metric with respect to
k
vanishes; this might happen if the Lie derivative with respect to
k
acting twice on the metric results in zero or not. The latter case yields solutions for any
\Lambda

García Díaz and Plebański 1981
, featuring spherical, hyperbolical or plane wave fronts, while the former case admits solutions only for
\Lambda\leq 0

Ozsváth et al. 1985
, featuring only hyperbolical or plane wave fronts. The solutions not admitting spherical wave fronts contain as a particular case those for which
k
is a KVF, which for
\Lambda<0
correspond to Siklos waves
Siklos 1985
and are conformal to pp waves. Moreover, the solutions admitting spherical wave fronts are also conformal to their vacuum relatives.

All type N spacetimes of the Kundt class, exhibit a function
H^{\prime}
which is constant along each null geodesic, called wave profile function, analogous to the one appearing in (
1
). For the
\Lambda
-vacuum solutions, it determines completely the Weyl tensor and is subject to

\Box H^{\prime}=0.

(2)

Given the dependence of
H^{\prime}
and the structure of the metric, the foregoing wave equation yields an elliptic equation on the wave surfaces, reducing for pp waves to Laplace’s equation on a plane. For a non-vanishing
\Lambda
the general solution is given in terms of an arbitrary holomorphic function
F=F(u,\zeta)
which cannot be eliminated through coordinate transformations. As noted in
Trautman 1962
, the appearance of an arbitrary function enables information propagation, a characteristic feature of waves. Remarkably, these
\Lambda
-vacuum solutions have been identified as those type N spacetimes for which any symmetric conserved tensor constructed from the metric, the curvature tensor and its covariant derivatives is a multiple of the metric itself, thus called
universal
spacetimes
Hervik et al. 2014
.

In addition to the solutions considered above, there exist other Kundt class spacetimes coupled to a wide variety of fields. General type N spacetimes of the Kundt class minimally coupled with a Maxwell field and with an Abelian Higgs field were studied in
Ozsváth et al. 1985
and
Canfora et al. 2021
respectively, and spacetimes in which
k
is a KVF non-minimally coupled with a real scalar field and an electromagnetic field were found in
Ayón-Beato and Hassaine 2007
and
Gürses and Halil 1978
respectively. Broader spacetimes of the Kundt class have also been studied as solutions to alternative theories of gravity including those non-minimally coupled with an electromagnetic field, Lovelock theory and
f(R)
theories
Horndeski 1979
;
Ortaggio 2018
;
Švarc et al. 2020
;
Gürses et al. 2022
;
Baykal and Dereli 2022
. The majority of the solutions that include matter have as a common feature an energy-momentum tensor of the pure radiation type. A more comprehensive list of investigations of Kundt class spacetimes can be found in
Baykal and Dereli 2022
.

Nevertheless, despite the conformal properties of known type N Kundt solutions, there has not been so much study coupling Kundt spacetimes with matter having specific conformal properties other than the seminal works of
Ozsváth et al. 1985
and
Ayón-Beato and Hassaine 2007
. Thus, we aim to study type N spacetimes of the Kundt class as solutions to Einstein’s equations, sourced by the simplest possible matter content, namely a real scalar field, whose equation of motion is conformally invariant and for which all field equations are of second order. The most general action principle for such a field was recently found by Fernandes
Fernandes 2021
and includes as a particular case the previously known conformally invariant real scalar field (cf.
Gürsey 1963
;
Penrose 1964
;
*penrose2011republication
;
Callan Jr et al. 1970
).
Since the resulting field equations are of second order, the action belongs to Horndeski’s general class of scalar-tensor theories
Horndeski 1974
. If higher order terms are allowed in the energy-momentum tensor, the most general action principle for such a field was given in
Ayón-Beato and Hassaine 2024
.
This generalized conformal scalar field has been studied in the context of black holes, cosmology, Vaydia spacetimes and wormholes
Fernandes 2021
;
Babichev et al. 2022
for the second order case, while stealths on flat spacetime have been found for the higher order case
Ayón-Beato et al. 2025
. The present work will enhance the family of known solutions to the second order theory, to include those coupled with type N Kundt spacetimes.

This work is organized as follows. In §
II
we will describe type N spacetimes of the Kundt class, stating its defining and characteristic properties, presenting the closed form for the general class and reviewing the
\Lambda
-vacuum solutions. Most of the content of §
II
is a review, except for the general closed forms of the metric given for the general type N spacetimes of the Kundt class. Then, in §
III
we will describe the generalized conformal scalar field that we will consider. Next, in §
IV
we will present the solutions found, beginning with a pp wave spacetime and later considering more general type N Kundt spacetimes. Finally, in §
V
we will summarize and discuss the results obtained and comment on possible future work.

II
Type N Kundt spacetimes

A type N spacetime of the Kundt class is characterized by a non-vanishing Weyl tensor and by the existence of a vector field
k
satisfying

\displaystyle k_{a}k^{a}=0,

\displaystyle k_{a;b}k^{b}=0,

\displaystyle k_{a;b}k^{a;b}=0,

(3a)

\displaystyle C_{abcd}k^{d}=0,

(3b)

namely
k
is tangent to a family of null geodesics with vanishing twist, expansion and shear —the optical scalars— and is a quadruple PND. The purpose of the second equation in (
3a
) is to determine the affinely parametrized character of the geodesics, since every family of non-twisting null curves is necessarily geodesic but non-affinely parametrized. The vector field
k
is thus determined up to a scale factor constant along the null geodesics. Analogously, one can verify that the conditions (
3
) are preserved upon conformal transformations, if and only if the conformal factor
\Omega
is constant along each null geodesic

\pounds_{k}\Omega=0.

(4)

The non-twisting condition implies the existence of a family of null hypersurfaces, foliated by a family of spacelike surfaces
\Sigma
, both with normal
k
(cf. §2 of
Kundt 1961
). The former are called wave hypersurfaces, while the latter are called
wave surfaces
. Additionally, the non-expanding and non-shearing conditions imply that the induced metric on the wave surfaces is constant along each null geodesic (cf. §3 of
Kundt 1961
).

II.1
Closed form and properties

All in all, the equations in (
3a
) allow one to introduce a coordinate system
(u,v,\zeta,\bar{\zeta})
in which
k=\partial_{v}
and

ds^{2}=\frac{1}{p^{2}}\left[|q|^{2}\!\left(hdu^{2}-2dudv+4\real(wdud\zeta)\right)+2\!\left|d\zeta\right|^{2}\right],

(5)

with
p,h
real-valued functions;
q,w
complex-valued functions; and
\partial_{v}p=\partial_{v}q=0
(cp. §III of
Ozsváth et al. 1985
). The way in which (
5
) is written is such that the function
p
is a conformal factor of both the spacetime metric and the induced metric on the wave surfaces, and proves useful when dealing with the type N condition, or when studying other conformal properties for that matter. The coordinate system is adapted to the specific spacetime geometry. The coordinate
u
represents the phase of the wave and is constant on the wave hypersurfaces. On the other hand, the coordinate
v
is an affine parameter of the null geodesics and the vectors fields
\partial_{\zeta}
and
\partial_{\bar{\zeta}}
are tangent to the wave surfaces. The coordinate system is determined up to transformations of the type

\displaystyle u=u(U),

\displaystyle v=A(U)\left(V+V_{0}(U,Z,\bar{Z})\right),

\displaystyle\zeta=\zeta(U,Z),

(6)

with
\mathrm{d}u/\mathrm{d}U>0
, which is simply a composition of a monotonic change of the phase of the wave, an affine transformation of the null geodesics, and a holomorphic (thus conformal) transformation on the wave surfaces.

Additionally, equation (
3b
) guarantees the existence of a coordinate system such that

h=Kv^{2}+Lv+H,

(7)

with
K
a constant and
L,H,w
functions independent of
v
, all real-valued except for the complex-valued
w
, subject to

\displaystyle|q|^{2}\,\partial_{\bar{\zeta}}\partial_{\zeta}\ln|q|^{2}=-K,

(8a)

\displaystyle\partial_{\zeta}\left(L+\partial_{u}\ln|q|^{2}-2i|q|^{2}\,\text{Im}(\partial_{\bar{\zeta}}w)\right)+2Kw=0.

(8b)

It is noteworthy that the first equation is a Liouville’s equation, originally studied as the one satisfied by the conformal factor of two-dimensional manifolds with constant Gaussian curvature. Different representations of its general solution have been known since then
Liouville 1853
.

Let us analyze what has been obtained so far. First, from (
5
), it follows that the Gaussian curvature of the wave surfaces, calculated as half its scalar curvature, is given by

K_{\Sigma}=p^{2}\partial_{\bar{\zeta}}\partial_{\zeta}\ln p^{2}.

(9)

Second, all the metric components are independent of
v
except for
g_{uu}
which is a quadratic polynomial in
v
. Hence, one can readily see that

\pounds_{k}\pounds_{k}\pounds_{k}g=0.

(10)

This can happen for generic cases for which
\pounds_{k}\pounds_{k}g
does not vanish or in special cases where it does, which in turn might happen if
\pounds_{k}g
is different from zero or if it is equal to zero. This suggests the following useful classification of general type N spacetimes. We shall call the
quadratic
class the one for which

\displaystyle\pounds_{k}\pounds_{k}g\neq 0,

\displaystyle\partial_{v}^{2}g_{uu}\neq 0,

(11)

the
linear
class the one for which

\displaystyle\pounds_{k}\pounds_{k}g=0,

\displaystyle\partial_{v}^{2}g_{uu}=0,

(12)

and the
Killing waves
class the one for which

\pounds_{k}g=0.

(13)

As such, the quadratic and the linear classes are disjoint, while the Killing waves class is contained in the linear class (cf. figure
1
).

For either the quadratic or linear classes one can find closed form general solutions to equations (
8
). For the linear class, one can restrict the coordinate system to achieve overall

\displaystyle K=0,

\displaystyle q=1,

\displaystyle L=2\real(\partial_{z}F),

\displaystyle w=-\overline{F},

(14)

with
F=F(u,\zeta)
. On the other hand, for the quadratic class, one can similarly restrict the coordinate system, to achieve overall

\displaystyle K=1,

\displaystyle q=\frac{\zeta+\bar{\zeta}}{\sqrt{2}},

\displaystyle L=0,

\displaystyle w=\frac{\zeta+\bar{\zeta}}{2}\partial_{\zeta}^{2}\left(\frac{G-\overline{G}}{\zeta+\bar{\zeta}}\right),

(15)

with
G=G(u,\zeta)
. Thus, one sees that a general type N spacetime of the Kundt class is determined by the conformal factor
p
, the function
H
, and a holomorphic function, either

or
G
, all independent of

. The reader should note that the foregoing characterization has been made without reference to any specific matter content, neither specific field equations. This is in a similar spirit of that of
Podolskỳ and Žofka 2009
;
Coley et al. 2009a
;
Ortaggio et al. 2024
. The closed form expressions (
14
) and (
15
) do not seem to be reported in the literature.

For the rest of this work, it will be convenient to introduce a complex null tetrad
(k,l,m,\overline{m})
, namely one such that
g_{ab}=-2k_{(a}l_{b)}+2m_{(a}\overline{m}_{b)}
, given for a general type N Kundt class spacetime by

\displaystyle k=\partial_{v},

(16)

\displaystyle l=p^{2}\left\{\frac{1}{|q|^{2}}\left[\partial_{u}-\frac{1}{2}\left(h-2|qw|^{2}\right)\partial_{v}\right]-2\real\left(w\partial_{\bar{\zeta}}\right)\right\},

\displaystyle m=p\partial_{\bar{\zeta}}.

Using this tetrad, one can readily analyze further geometric properties of the spacetime under consideration. From the Ricci identities (cf., e.g., §7.2 of
Stephani et al. 2003
) and (
3

\displaystyle Ric(k,k)=0,

\displaystyle Ric(k,m)=0,

(17)

implying that

where

and

S(m,\overline{m})-\frac{R}{12}=-K\left(\frac{p}{|q|}\right)^{2}+2|\tau|^{2},

(19)

where
\tau=-k_{a;b}m^{a}l^{b}
. Moreover, given that
m
and
\overline{m}
are tangent to the wave surfaces, these are totally geodesic if

and

(cp. §4.1 of
Siklos 1985

\displaystyle\partial_{\zeta}(|q|^{2}w)=0,

\displaystyle 2\real\left(\partial_{\zeta}\left[\left(\frac{|q|}{p}\right)^{2}\overline{w}\right]\right)=\partial_{u}p^{-2}.

(20)

II.2
Spacetimes with
\Lambda
and pure radiation matter

Let us recall that a matter content of the pure radiation type (cf. §5.2 of
Stephani et al. 2003
) is defined by the following energy-momentum structure

T_{ab}=T(l,l)k_{a}k_{b}.

(21)

Then, via Einstein’s equations with a cosmological constant, spacetimes which admit that type of matter are characterized by

\displaystyle S_{ab}=S(l,l)k_{a}k_{b},

\displaystyle dR=0.

(22)

For a general type N Kundt class spacetime, the foregoing represents three additional restrictions on
S
. First, one has
S(m,\overline{m})=0
that together with
R
being constant and (
18
) imply that the Gaussian curvature of the wave surfaces is constant and given by

K_{\Sigma}=\frac{R}{12}.

(23)

Second,
S(m,m)=0
together with the specific forms of
q
serve to simplify the specific form of
p
. And third,
S(l,m)=0
determines the holomorphic function,
F
for the linear class and
G
for the quadratic class.

The linear class (
14
) only admits
R\leq 0
, which follows from (
19
), so the wave surfaces may only have non-positive Gaussian curvature. For
R=0
one obtains pp waves (
1
), while for
R<0
one obtains the hyperbolic-fronted waves

\displaystyle p=\sqrt{\frac{|R|}{24}}(\zeta+\bar{\zeta}),

\displaystyle L=i\omega(\zeta+\bar{\zeta}),

\displaystyle w=\frac{i\omega}{2}\bar{\zeta}^{2},

(24)

where
\omega
is a constant. In the vanishing
\omega
case they reduce to Siklos waves
Siklos 1985
, while in the non-vanishing
\omega
case, taking into account the residual coordinate freedom, one might take
\omega=1
without loss of generality corresponding to the solutions first found in
Ozsváth et al. 1985
. On the other hand, the quadratic class (
15
) allows any
R\in\mathbb{R}
, and hence any
K_{\Sigma}
, obtaining

\displaystyle p=1+\frac{R}{24}|\zeta|^{2},

\displaystyle w=0,

(25)

including plane, spherical, or hyperbolic wave surfaces, found in
García Díaz and Plebański 1981
;
Ozsváth et al. 1985
. Additionally, both classes feature totally geodesic wave surfaces, since they both satisfy conditions (
20
), as one can readily verify. The only undetermined function of these spacetimes, namely
H
, will be dealt with in the next section. The relations between the foregoing solutions, including how they fit into the general classes, are summarized in figure

These spacetimes have the property that when writing the metric with respect to a coordinate system in which
g_{u\zeta}
vanishes it acquires a generalized Kerr-Schild form

;
*kerr2009republication
with the background metric that of a spacetime with vanishing Weyl tensor and traceless Ricci tensor, hence locally maximally symmetric [cf. equations (4.33) and (4.36) of
Ozsváth et al. 1985
]. Moreover, conditions (
3
) and (
22
) imply that these spacetimes have constant curvature invariants of any order (cf. theorem 2.1 of
Coley et al. 2009b
), and that any symmetric tensor constructed from the metric, the curvature tensor, and its covariant derivatives is such that its trace is constant and its traceless part has a pure radiation structure, as the Ricci tensor itself, thus being
almost universal

Remarkably, there exists conformal relations among the foregoing spacetimes of the same class (cp. §II G and the appendix of
Bičák and Podolskỳ 1999
). Let

and

be the above-mentioned spacetime metrics with vanishing and non-vanishing

(26)

is satisfied between the quadratic class spacetimes with

given by (
25
) and between the Killing waves class spacetimes with

given by (
24
). The linear class with non-vanishing
\pounds_{k}g
stands out as the exception, not having a conformally related spacetime within the considered solutions.

Finally, let us show the local equivalence between the above-mentioned solutions and those found in the literature. For the linear class (
24
) with non-vanishing scalar curvature, if one makes the transformation (

allows one to choose a

vanishes and

\displaystyle p_{\text{new}}=1-\frac{|R|}{24}\left|Z\right|^{2},

\displaystyle q_{\text{new}}=1+\sqrt{\frac{|R|}{24}}e^{-i\omega U}Z,

(28)

which, up to a re-parametrization of

coincides with the expressions given by

II.3
Wave profile function

For any of the spacetimes considered in the preceding section, all the non-constant and non-vanishing curvature components are determined by the function [cp. (
16
)]

H^{\prime}=H-2|qw|^{2},

(31)

which coincides with
H
for a vanishing
w
. The only non-vanishing Weyl scalar,
\Psi_{4}=C_{abcd}\overline{m}^{a}l^{b}\overline{m}^{c}l^{d}
, is given by

\Psi_{4}=-\frac{1}{2}\frac{p^{4}}{q^{3}}\partial_{\zeta}^{2}\left(\frac{H^{\prime}}{q}\right),

(32)

while the only non-vanishing component of the traceless Ricci tensor is

S(l,l)=-\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{p}{q}\right)^{2}\Box H^{\prime},

(33)

where
\Box H^{\prime}={H^{\prime}}_{;a}^{\ \ ;a}
. Then, for a
\Lambda
-vacuum spacetime,
H^{\prime}
satisfies the wave equation (
2
) which, given that
\partial_{v}H^{\prime}=0
, yields

\real\left(\partial_{\zeta}\left[\left(\frac{q}{p}\right)^{2}\partial_{\bar{\zeta}}H^{\prime}\right]\right)=0.

(34)

The general solution of the preceding equation has a closed form given by

H^{\prime}=\frac{2p^{3}}{q}\real\left(\partial_{\zeta}\left(p^{-2}F\right)\right),

(35)

with
F=F(u,\zeta)
an arbitrary integration function
García Díaz and Plebański 1981
;
Ozsváth et al. 1985
.

III
Generalized conformal scalar field

The scalar field that we will consider is coupled to gravity by the following action

S[g,\Phi]=\int\mathrm{d}^{4}x\sqrt{|g|}\left\{\frac{R-2\Lambda}{2\kappa}-\left[\frac{R\Phi^{2}}{12}+\frac{\left(\nabla\Phi\right)^{2}}{2}+\lambda\Phi^{4}\right]-\frac{\alpha}{2}\left[\mathcal{G}\ln\Phi-\frac{4G_{ab}\Phi^{;a}\Phi^{;b}}{\Phi^{2}}-\frac{4\Box\Phi\left(\nabla\Phi\right)^{2}}{\Phi^{3}}+\frac{2\left(\nabla\Phi\right)^{4}}{\Phi^{4}}\right]\right\},

(36)

with
\mathcal{G}=R^{2}-4R_{ab}R^{ab}+R_{abcd}R^{abcd}
the Gauss-Bonnet scalar,
\left(\nabla\Phi\right)^{2}=\Phi_{;a}\Phi^{;a}
and
\Box\Phi=\Phi_{;a}^{\ \ ;a}
,
\kappa=8\pi G
and
\lambda,\alpha
coupling constants. The terms enclosed in the first squared brackets correspond to the previously known conformally invariant real scalar field
Gürsey 1963
;
Penrose 1964
including its self-interaction term (cf. §6.2 of
Callan Jr et al. 1970
), while the terms multiplied by
\alpha
are the generalization given by Fernandes
Fernandes 2021
. The action (
36
) coincides with the one given by him making
\beta_{\text{there}}=\kappa/6
,
\lambda_{\text{there}}=\kappa\lambda
, and
\alpha_{\text{there}}=\kappa\alpha
.

The equation of motion of the scalar field can be written as

e\coloneqq\Box\Phi-\frac{1}{6}R\Phi-4\lambda\Phi^{3}-\frac{\alpha}{2}\tilde{\mathcal{G}}\Phi^{3}=0,

(37)

where
\tilde{\mathcal{G}}
is the Gauss-Bonnet scalar of the auxiliary metric

\tilde{g}_{ab}=\Phi^{2}g_{ab}.

(38)

Equation (
37
) is conformally invariant, with a conformal weight
-1
for
\Phi
. This can be seen if one rewrites it in terms of
\tilde{g}
, which is conformally invariant by construction, yielding modulo an additive constant, a linear combination with constant coefficients of the scalar curvature and the Gauss-Bonnet scalar of the auxiliary metric [cf. equations (7) and (9) of
Fernandes 2021
].
Despite being (
37
) conformally invariant, its corresponding matter action is not, suggesting the name
non-Noetherian
conformal scalar field
Ayón-Beato and Hassaine 2024
. Let us remark that the contributions of
\tilde{\mathcal{G}}
to the equation of motion are of second order and quadratic in
\Phi
, as one would expect being
\tilde{\mathcal{G}}
a quadratic curvature scalar. Thus, regardless of its apparent simplicity, equation (
37
) is a fully non-linear equation, contrasting with the quasi-linear equation obtained in the vanishing
\alpha
case.

Let us write the obtained field equations as

E_{ab}\coloneqq G_{ab}+\Lambda g_{ab}-\kappa T_{ab}=0.

(39)

Since the matter action is not conformally invariant, the energy-momentum tensor has a non-vanishing trace, as opposed to the standard conformal field. In fact, being the equation of motion conformally invariant, one can show [cf. equations (6) and (18) of
Fernandes 2021
] that the variation of (
36
) with respect to the scalar field yields the identity

\text{tr}T-\frac{\alpha}{2}\mathcal{G}\equiv\Phi e.

(40)

The previous identity together with (
37
) implies, via the trace of (
39
), that the spacetime is subject to the following geometric constraint

R-4\Lambda+\frac{\kappa\alpha}{2}\mathcal{G}=0.

(41)

The expression for the energy-momentum tensor is not particularly simple. However, as it happens with the standard conformal field, it can be written more compactly in terms of

\sigma=\frac{1}{\Phi}.

(42)

Its traceless part is given by

\displaystyle T_{\langle ab\rangle}=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{3\sigma^{3}}\left[1+\alpha\sigma^{2}\left(12\Box\ln\sigma+R\right)\right]\sigma_{\langle;ab\rangle}

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{6\sigma^{2}}\left(1+12\alpha\sigma^{2}\Box\ln\sigma\right)S_{ab}

(43)

\displaystyle-\frac{4\alpha}{\sigma}\left(\frac{1}{\sigma}\sigma_{\langle;a}^{\ \ ;c}\sigma_{;b\rangle c}^{\vphantom{;c}}+S^{\vphantom{;c}}_{c\langle a}\sigma_{;b\rangle}^{\ \ ;c}+C_{acbd}\sigma^{;cd}\right),

where we have used brackets around pairs of indices to denote the symmetric traceless part, having

\displaystyle\sigma_{\langle;ab\rangle}=\sigma_{;ab}-\frac{\Box\sigma}{4}g_{ab},

(44)

\displaystyle\sigma_{\langle;a}^{\ \ ;c}\sigma_{;b\rangle c}^{\vphantom{;c}}=\sigma_{;a}^{\ \ ;c}\sigma_{;bc}^{\vphantom{;c}}-\frac{\sigma_{;cd}\sigma^{;cd}}{4}g_{ab},

\displaystyle S^{\vphantom{;c}}_{c\langle a}\sigma_{;b\rangle}^{\ \ ;c}=S^{\vphantom{;c}}_{c(a}\sigma_{;b)}^{\ \ ;c}-\frac{S_{cd}\sigma^{;cd}}{4}g_{ab}.

The full energy-momentum tensor can be obtained using (
37
), (
40
) and (
43
) on

T_{ab}=T_{\langle ab\rangle}+\frac{\text{tr}T}{4}g_{ab}.

(45)

Since both the equation of motion and the energy-momentum tensor are of second order in the metric and in the scalar field, it belongs to the known general class of scalar-tensor theories
Horndeski 1974
. The specific choice of Horndeski’s arbitrary functions which leads to the above-mentioned action were given in
Fernandes 2021
[cf. his equation (15)]. If one relaxes the condition of the energy-momentum tensor being of second order, the most general action giving rise to a conformally invariant equation of motion includes an additional term with an arbitrary function of the Weyl tensor of the auxiliary metric (
38
), as shown in
Ayón-Beato and Hassaine 2024
.

IV
Solutions

We will consider the spacetimes described in §
II.2
, namely type N spacetimes of the Kundt class with constant scalar curvature
R
and whose traceless Ricci tensor has a pure radiation structure (
22
), sourced by the generalized conformal scalar field, restricting our attention to those fields that are constant along each null geodesic

\partial_{v}\Phi=0.

(46)

This, motivated by the fact that it turns out to be sufficient for the energy-momentum tensor to satisfy conditions analogous to (
17

Given the form of the traceless Ricci tensor, the traceless energy-momentum tensor is required to have an aligned pure radiation structure

T_{\langle ab\rangle}=\left(T_{\langle cd\rangle}l^{c}l^{d}\right)k_{a}k_{b}.

(47)

This amounts to the vanishing of the three components
T_{\langle ab\rangle}m^{a}m^{b}
,
T_{\langle ab\rangle}m^{a}\overline{m}^{b}
, and
T_{\langle ab\rangle}l^{a}m^{b}
, analogous to those considered at the beginning of §
II.2
, supplemented with the additional requirement that

\partial_{v}\left(T_{\langle ab\rangle}l^{a}l^{b}\right)=0,

(48)

given that
\partial_{v}\left[S(l,l)\right]=0
, which follows from (
33
). As it is, condition (
47
) is weaker than the one satisfied by pure radiation matter (
21
), since it allows a non-vanishing trace. The foregoing, together with the equation of motion (
37
), will determine the scalar field. On the other hand, the scalar curvature
R
is subject to the geometric constraint (
41
), while the wave profile function will be determined, once (
47
) and (
48
) are solved, by the field equation

E(l,l)=0.

(49)

Before going ahead with the integration, let us analyze the particular form that the matter restriction takes when the standard conformal scalar field is considered, and its relation with that of the generalized conformal field. When
\alpha
vanishes, it follows from (
22
) and (
43
) that the matter restriction (
47
) requires the traceless part of the Hessian of
\sigma
to have also an aligned pure radiation structure

\sigma_{\langle;ab\rangle}=\left(\sigma_{\langle;cd\rangle}l^{c}l^{d}\right)k_{a}k_{b}.

(50)

Remarkably, that structure is such that each of the last three terms in (
43

) and (

), so it solves the matter restriction also for the non-vanishing
\alpha

) characterizes one of two possible branches of the matter restriction.

IV.1
Geometric constraint

Since the type of spacetimes considered have a type N Weyl tensor and a pure radiation type traceless Ricci tensor, the Gauss-Bonnet scalar gives
\mathcal{G}=R^{2}/6
, so the geometric constraint (
41

\frac{\kappa\alpha}{12}R^{2}+R-4\Lambda=0.

(51)

For generic values of the coupling constants one has two roots

R=R_{\pm}\coloneqq\frac{6}{\kappa\alpha}\left(-1\pm\sqrt{1+\frac{4\kappa\alpha\Lambda}{3}}\right),

(52)

which are real, and thus physically meaningful, only for
4\kappa\alpha\Lambda/3>-1
. Note that only the upper sign root contains the standard scalar field as a particular case, being the only one that is finite in the limit
\alpha\to 0
, for which
R=4\Lambda
. The two roots as a function of
\alpha
are depicted in figure
2
. For the special case of
\Lambda=0
one obtains

\left(1+\frac{\kappa\alpha}{12}R\right)R=0,

(53)

which in particular admits a vanishing
R
as a solution.

For those spacetimes that are defined only for specific values of the scalar curvature, not all possible roots of (

for the upper and lower sign roots in (

IV.2
PP waves solutions

To gain some insight, let us first consider the generalized conformal scalar field coupled with a pp waves spacetime. Substituting (
1
), (
16
) and (
46

48

\displaystyle T_{\langle ab\rangle}\overline{m}^{a}\overline{m}^{b}=-\frac{N\partial_{\zeta}^{2}\sigma}{3\sigma^{3}},

(54)

\displaystyle T_{\langle ab\rangle}m^{a}\overline{m}^{b}=-\frac{N\partial_{\zeta}\partial_{\bar{\zeta}}\sigma+12\alpha\sigma\mathcal{H}_{\sigma}}{6\sigma^{3}},

\displaystyle T_{\langle ab\rangle}l^{a}\overline{m}^{b}=-\frac{N\partial_{\zeta}\partial_{u}\sigma+12\alpha\sigma\mathcal{H}^{\prime}_{\sigma}}{3\sigma^{3}},

where

\displaystyle N\coloneqq 24\alpha\left|\partial_{\zeta}\sigma\right|^{2}-1,

(55)

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}_{\sigma}\coloneqq\left|\partial_{\zeta}^{2}\sigma\right|^{2}-\left(\partial_{\zeta}\partial_{\bar{\zeta}}\sigma\right)^{2},

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}^{\prime}_{\sigma}\coloneqq\left(\partial_{\zeta}^{2}\sigma\right)\partial_{\bar{\zeta}}\partial_{u}\sigma-\left(\partial_{\zeta}\partial_{u}\sigma\right)\partial_{\zeta}\partial_{\bar{\zeta}}\sigma.

Additionally, from (
37

40

\text{tr}T=\frac{\left(24\alpha\sigma\partial_{\zeta}\partial_{\bar{\zeta}}\sigma-N\right)N+96\alpha^{2}\sigma^{2}\mathcal{H}_{\sigma}+1-48\alpha\lambda}{12\alpha\sigma^{4}}.

(56)

Furthermore, one can show that

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}_{\sigma}=\frac{\left(\partial_{\bar{\zeta}}\ln\partial_{\bar{\zeta}}\sigma\right)\partial_{\zeta}N-\left(\partial_{\zeta}\ln\partial_{\bar{\zeta}}\sigma\right)\partial_{\bar{\zeta}}N}{24\alpha},

(57)

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}^{\prime}_{\sigma}=\frac{\left(\partial_{u}\ln\partial_{\bar{\zeta}}\sigma\right)\partial_{\zeta}N-\left(\partial_{\zeta}\ln\partial_{\bar{\zeta}}\sigma\right)\partial_{u}N}{24\alpha}.

Thus, the quantities
\mathcal{H}_{\sigma}
and
\mathcal{H}^{\prime}_{\sigma}
are second order expressions subsidiary to

, in the sense that they vanish if
N
does.

Consequently, the required condition of the traceless energy-momentum tensor having an aligned pure radiation structure (
47

\displaystyle N=0,

\displaystyle\alpha=\frac{1}{48\lambda},

(58)

which are sufficient conditions for the right-hand side of (
54

57

\alpha

strictly non-Noetherian
branch. The other branch may exist for vanishing
\alpha

branch; it is characterized by the traceless part of the Hessian of

having a pure radiation structure [cf. (
50

Ayón-Beato et al. 2025
where the generalized conformal scalar field was also considered but in a different context.

IV.2.1
Noetherian-like branch

The matter restriction (
47

(60)

where (

The equation of motion requires
\text{tr}T
to vanish, given (
40

\mathcal{G}=0
, which amounts to

As with the geometric constraint, one has generically two roots

|b_{0}|^{2}=d_{\mp}\coloneqq\frac{1\mp\sqrt{1-48\alpha\lambda}}{24\kappa\alpha}.

(62)

Certainly, the solution only exists when the right-hand side of (

, while a positive

|b_{0}|^{2}=\lambda/\kappa

Ayón-Beato and Hassaine 2007

as a function of

\left(12\alpha\kappa|b_{0}|^{2}-1\right)|b_{0}|^{2}=0.

Finally, it is worth noticing that for the critical value

IV.2.2
Wave profile function

Considering the Noetherian-like branch solution for the scalar field, the field equation
E(l,l)=0
yields

\partial_{\zeta}\partial_{\bar{\zeta}}H+\frac{\beta^{2}}{\zeta+\bar{\zeta}}\frac{\partial_{\zeta}+\partial_{\bar{\zeta}}}{\left(\zeta+\bar{\zeta}\right)^{2}-\beta^{2}}=0,

(64)

with

\beta^{2}=\frac{1-24\kappa\alpha b_{0}^{2}}{6b_{0}^{2}}.

(65)

Remarkably, for the critical value
\alpha=1/(48\lambda)
, which implies
b_{0}^{2}=2\lambda/\kappa
, one has
\beta=0
so one recovers Laplace’s equation, whose general solution is given by
H=F+\overline{F}
with
F=F(u,\zeta)
an arbitrary integration function. The fact that one recovers the equation for the vacuum case can be understood if one analyzes the energy-momentum tensor. In fact, this critical solution yields

T_{ab}=0,

(66)

hence being an example of a so-called stealth configuration, first exhibited in
Ayón-Beato et al. 2006
in the context of black holes.

For generic values of
\alpha
equation (
64
) is not so manageable, and a closed form solution cannot be readily found. However, it does admit a separable solution when transforming to real coordinates
\zeta=x+iy
. In fact, one can readily see that the general product-separable solution is given by

H=\left(c_{\mathcal{K}}e^{\mathcal{K}y}+d_{\mathcal{K}}e^{-\mathcal{K}y}\right)\eta,

(67)

with
\mathcal{K}=\mathcal{K}(u)
a real-valued separation function and
\eta=\eta(u,x)
satisfying

\partial_{x}^{2}\eta+\frac{\beta^{2}}{2x}\frac{\partial_{x}\eta}{x^{2}-\beta^{2}/4}+\mathcal{K}^{2}\eta=0.

(68)

The equation for
\eta
is effectively an ordinary differential equation (ODE). It has regular singular points [cf., e.g., §15.3 of
Ince 1956
] at
x=0
and at
x^{2}=\beta^{2}/4
and an irregular singular point at infinity, so it can be cast into a confluent Heun’s equation
Slavyanov 1995
by making
x\mapsto(2x/\beta)^{2}
.

IV.2.3
Strictly non-Noetherian branch

The branch that exists for a strictly non-vanishing
\alpha
[cf. (
55
) and (
58
)] involves the equation
N=0
which is a first order non-linear partial differential equation (PDE) in the variables
\zeta
and
\bar{\zeta}
. It is known that the general solution to such an equation can be constructed using as a seed a bi-parametric solution, concretely as the envelope of an arbitrary uniparametric subfamily (cf. §I-4.2 of
Courant and Hilbert 1962
). In fact, such a method has been used in other problems of gravitation and field theory when dealing with a first order non-linear PDE (cf., e.g.,
Perry and Schwarz 1997
;
Russo and Townsend 2024
;
Ayón-Beato et al. 2024
;
Ayón-Beato et al. 2025
).

Since the Noetherian-like solution is valid for any
\alpha
, it is reasonable to wonder if, when evaluated at the critical value
\alpha=1/(48\lambda)
, it solves
N=0
. In fact, this is so, as one can readily verify by substituting (
59
) and (
62
) into (
58
). This comes out as a surprise, if one recalls from the discussion around (
58
) that the form of the Noetherian-like solution is determined by the system of second order quasi-linear PDEs (
50
), in contrast with the (single) first order fully non-linear PDE
N=0
. Moving forward, since the latter equation does not involve
u
, it admits the solution given by (
59
) and (
62
) even when one allows the phase of
b_{0}
to be a function of
u
, namely

\sigma=\sqrt{2\lambda}\left[2\real\left(e^{i\Psi(u)}\zeta\right)+\Gamma(u)\right].

(69)

Hence, this solution serves as the required bi-parametric seed solution. Following §I-4.2 of
Courant and Hilbert 1962
, the general solution is given by

\sigma=\sqrt{2\lambda}\left[2\real\left(e^{i\psi}\zeta\right)+\gamma(u,\psi)\right],

(70)

with
\psi=\psi(u,\zeta,\bar{\zeta})
determined implicitly by

\partial_{\psi}\gamma=-2\text{Im}\left(e^{i\psi}\zeta\right).

(71)

The foregoing general solution is determined, for an arbitrary
\gamma
, parametrically through the function
\psi
. An explicit non-parametric representation of the general solution can be obtained by using a coordinate system which includes the function
\psi
as a coordinate. This is more simply done making
\zeta=x+iy
, in terms of which the required coordinate transformation
(x,y)\mapsto(x,\psi)
and the obtained solution are given by

\displaystyle y=\frac{\partial_{\psi}\gamma-2x\sin\psi}{2\cos\psi},

\displaystyle\sigma=\frac{\sqrt{2\lambda}}{\cos\psi}\left[2x+\gamma^{2}\partial_{\psi}\left(\frac{\sin\psi}{\gamma}\right)\right].

(72)

The cost one has to pay for having an explicit representation of the general solution for the scalar field is that the coordinate system is not adapted to the metric (
1
) anymore.

In summary, the strictly non-Noetherian branch has two different solutions: the first one given by (
69
) which includes two arbitrary functions of
u
, and the second one given by (
70
) and (
71
), or equivalently by (
72
), which exhibits an arbitrary function of two arguments
\gamma
. These two solutions contain the totality of solutions of the strictly non-Noetherian branch.

V
Discussion

Let us begin this section by recapping the results obtained. We considered type N Kundt spacetimes with a traceless Ricci tensor having a pure radiation structure and with a constant scalar curvature [cf. (
22
)], coupled with the generalized conformal real scalar field, which we considered to be constant along each null geodesic (
46
). The geometric constraint (
41
), the matter restriction equations (
47
), and the equation of motion (
37
) were solved with full generality, determining thus the value of scalar curvature and the scalar field. Two roots arose for the scalar curvature, out of which only one is finite when the coupling constant
\alpha
vanishes, and which degenerate for the critical value
\alpha=1/(48\lambda)
. The scalar field solutions split into two roots, one Noetherian-like and the other strictly non-Noetherian, defined for possibly vanishing and for strictly non-vanishing
\alpha
, respectively.
The Noetherian-like branch is characterized by the traceless part of the Hessian of the inverse of the scalar field having a pure radiation structure (
50
); this resulted in a scalar field depending only on
\zeta
and
\bar{\zeta}
for all the classes of spacetimes except for the linear class spacetime with non-vanishing
\pounds_{k}g
, which required a constant scalar field. The strictly non-Noetherian branch stood out being determined by a fully non-linear first order PDE. The totality of solutions of this branch includes a solution containing two arbitrary functions of the phase of the wave
u
for all the classes [cf. (
69
) and (
111
)], a solution containing an arbitrary function
\gamma
of two arguments for the Killing waves class (
70
), an arbitrary function
f
of one argument for the quadratic class (
112
), and a solution not containing any arbitrary element for the quadratic class (
116
). The resulting equation for the wave profile function with the Noetherian-like branch was shown to have a similar form as the one for
\Lambda
-vacuum [cf. (
98
) and (
83
) and related discussion], but including new singular terms due to the contribution of the scalar field; its general solution was found for the two critical values
\alpha=1/(48\lambda)
and
\alpha=-3/(4\kappa\Lambda)
while product-separable solutions were found otherwise, where different ODEs arose. In particular, the
x
equation of a pp waves spacetime is a confluent Heun’s equation, while the radial equation for the quadratic class spacetime can be cast into an equation with six regular singular points, out of which two coincide when the integration constant of the scalar field acquires a specific value, admitting as linearly independent solutions a linear combination of two hypergeometric functions with polynomial coefficients.

The generalized scalar field was shown in §
IV.1
to modify the value of the constant scalar curvature with respect to the
\Lambda
-vacuum case. For the root connected to the vanishing
\alpha
case, one has
\text{sgn}\left(R\right)=\text{sgn}\left(\Lambda\right)
, while for the other root opposite signs of
R
and
\Lambda
can occur. Furthermore, for vanishing
\Lambda
, there exists the possibility of a non-vanishing
R
. Thus, the scalar field enhances, reduces, or even mimics the contribution of
\Lambda
. In particular this allows the existence of Killing waves spacetimes with positive
\Lambda
, which to our knowledge had not been exhibited so far in the literature. Of course, that only makes sense when the coupling constants yield
R<0
, required for those type of spacetimes.
This mimicking feature is present only for a non-vanishing non-Noetherian coupling constant
\alpha
; it is, in fact, a generic feature of the theory when the non-Noetherian coupling constant
\alpha
is non-vanishing [cf. (
41
)], which in particular for the considered spacetimes is determined by (
51
). Similar behavior has been found with
f(R)
theories and others including couplings to
\mathcal{G}
(cf. §V of
Švarc et al. 2020
and §2 and §3 of
Baykal and Dereli 2022
). Furthermore, an analogous behavior was shown to occur for the constant
d
restricting the integration constants of the Noetherian-like scalar field solutions (cf., e.g., §
IV.4.1
): the contribution from the self-interaction constant
\lambda
to
d
is modified in the same way by the presence of a non-vanishing
\alpha
.

Another aspect exhibited of the generalized scalar field was the emergence of the strictly non-Noetherian branch. The first full characterization of this type of branch was done in
Ayón-Beato et al. 2025
, in a context different from that of gravitational waves. Since the ansatz we considered for the scalar field was slightly more general than that of the mentioned reference, the set of solutions allowed by the strictly non-Noetherian branch obtained here is larger. Surprisingly, as also happened in
Ayón-Beato et al. 2025
, the set of solutions of the two branches intersect when
\alpha=1/(48\lambda)
. This is remarkable, considering that the Noetherian-like branch was determined by second order quasi-linear equations, while the strictly non-Noetherian branch was determined by a first order non-linear PDE. We consider that the intersection of the two branches deserves further analysis.

It is worth noting that spacetimes of the linear class with non-vanishing
\pounds_{k}g
, were shown to admit a smaller family of solutions. The Noetherian-like branch only allowed a constant scalar field
\Phi
, while the strictly non-Noetherian branch only allowed a solution containing two integration functions of
u
(
69
). This lower compatibility with the conformal scalar field can be understood by recalling that, for a given scalar curvature and wave profile function, the spacetime of the mentioned type is the only one contained in its conformal class within those type N Kundt class spacetimes of §
II.2
[cf. the discussion following (
26
)].

One interesting feature, uncommon in other types of analytical models for gravitational waves, is the form of the energy-momentum tensor of the scalar field. From (
45
), (
40
) and (
47
) one obtains that the solutions presented generically satisfy

T_{ab}=T(l,l)k_{a}k_{b}+\frac{\alpha R^{2}}{48}g_{ab}.

(117)

The foregoing reduces to the pure radiation type (
21
) when
R=0
, but belongs to the more general class of energy-momentum tensors having a double null eigenvector
k
(cf. §4.3 of
Hawking and Ellis 1973
). It is characterized by the equality of the normal stresses on the wave surfaces and by an equation of state analogous to that associated with a cosmological constant (cf. §9.2.1 of
Martín-Moruno and Visser 2017
). Exceptions to
(
117
) are the linear class spacetime with non-vanishing
\pounds_{k}g
and pp waves spacetimes for a generic
\alpha
, both of which resulted in pure radiation matter (
21
). Another exception is the pp waves spacetime at the critical value
\alpha=1/(48\lambda)
, which turned out to be a stealth configuration (
66
). For (
117
), the null energy condition requires
T(l,l)>0
while the weak energy condition requires additionally
\alpha\leq 0
. Interestingly, the emergence of an energy-momentum tensor with a null eigenvector in scalar-tensor theories was foreseen in
Banerjee et al. 2023
, but one with a triple, rather than a double, null eigenvector.

Finally, let us comment on the considered ansatz for the scalar field (
46
), namely one that is constant along each null geodesic. Our main motivation was that it is a sufficient condition for the energy-momentum tensor to have
k
as an eigenvector. Additionally, this enabled a straightforward integration of the matter restriction equations (
47
). However, whether a type N Kundt spacetime can be coupled with a conformal scalar field with arbitrary dependence remains an open question. We believe this will end up being incompatible with the type N character requiring the spacetime to be conformally flat, but further investigations are required.

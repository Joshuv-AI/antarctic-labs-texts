On the Online Frank-Wolfe Algorithms for Convex and Non-convex Optimizations
Jean Lafond, Hoi-To Wai, Eric Moulines

Abstract
In this paper, the online variants of the classical Frank-Wolfe algorithm are considered. We consider minimizing the regret with a stochastic cost. The online algorithms only require simple iterative updates and a non-adaptive step size rule, in contrast to the hybrid schemes commonly considered in the literature. Several new results are derived for convex and non-convex losses. With a strongly convex stochastic cost and when the optimal solution lies in the interior of the constraint set or the constraint set is a polytope, the regret bound and anytime optimality are shown to be 𝒪 ⁡ ( log 3 ⁡ T / T ) {\cal O}(\log^{3}T/T) and 𝒪 ⁡ ( log 2 ⁡ T / T ) {\cal O}(\log^{2}T/T) , respectively, where T T is the number of rounds played. These results are based on an improved analysis on the stochastic Frank-Wolfe algorithms. Moreover, the online algorithms are shown to converge even when the loss is non-convex , i.e., the algorithms find a stationary point to the time-varying/stochastic loss at a rate of 𝒪 ⁡ ( 1 / T ) {\cal O}(\sqrt{1/T}) . Numerical experiments on realistic data sets are presented to support our theoretical claims.

Introduction

Recently, Frank-Wolfe (FW) algorithm
[
FW56
]
has become
popular for high-dimensional constrained optimization.
Compared to the projected gradient (PG) algorithm
(see
[
BT09
,
JN12a
,
JN12b
,
NJLS09
]
),
the FW algorithm (a.k.a. conditional gradient method) is appealing due to its
projection-free
nature.
The costly projection step in PG is replaced by a linear optimization in FW.
The latter admits a closed
form solution for many problems of interests in machine learning.

This work focuses on the online variants of the FW and the
FW with
away step
(AW) algorithms.
At each round, the proposed online FW/AW algorithms follow the same update equation applied in
classical FW/AW and
a step size is taken according to a non-adaptive rule.
The only modification involved is that we use an
online-computed aggregated gradient
as a
surrogate of the true gradient
of the expected loss that we attempt to minimize.
We establish fast convergence of the algorithms under various conditions.

Fast convergence for projection-free algorithms have been studied in
[
LJJ13
,
LJJ15
,
GH15a
,
GH15b
,
LZ14
,
HL16
]
.
However, many works have considered a ‘hybrid’ approach that involves solving a
regularized linear optimization during the updates
[
GH15b
,
LZ14
]
; or combining existing
algorithms with FW
[
HL16
]
.
In particular, the authors in
[
GH15b
]
showed a regret bound of

{\cal O}(\log T/T)
for their online projection-free algorithm,
where
T
is the number of iterations, under an adversarial setting. This matches the optimal bound for strongly
convex loss.
The drawback of these algorithms lies on the extra complexities (in implementation and
computation) added to the
classical FW algorithm.

Our aim is to show that simple online projection-free methods can achieve
on-the-par convergence guarantees as the sophisticated algorithms mentioned above.
In particular, we present a set of new results
for online FW/AW algorithms under the full information setting, i.e.,
complete knowledge about the loss function is retrieved at each round
[
ADX10
]
(see
section 2
).
Our online FW algorithm is similar to the online projection-free method proposed in

[
HK12
]
,
while the online AW algorithm is new.
For online FW algorithms,
[
HK12
]
has proven
a regret of
{\cal O}(\sqrt{\log^{2}T/T})
for convex and smooth stochastic costs.
We improve the regret bound to
{\cal O}(\log^{3}T/T)
under
two different sets of assumptions:
(a)
the stochastic cost is strongly convex,
the optimal solutions lie in the interior of
{\cal C}
(cf. H
1
, for online FW);

(b)

{\cal C}
is a polytope (cf. H
2
, for online AW).
An improved
anytime
optimality bound of
{\cal O}(\log^{2}T/T)

(compared to
{\cal O}(\sqrt{\log^{2}T/T})
in
[
HK12
]
) is also proven.

We use the setting introduced in

The online learner wants to minimize a loss function

f(\bm{\theta})\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=\mathbb{E}_{\omega\sim{\cal D}}[f(\bm{\theta};\omega)]

The regret of a sequence of actions

\textstyle{\cal R}_{T}\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=T^{-1}\sum_{t=1}^{T}f(\bm{\theta}_{t})-\min_{\bm{\theta}\in{\cal C}}f(\bm{\theta})\;.

(1)

Here,

Online away-step Frank-Wolfe (O-AW)
.
The online counterpart of the away step algorithm is given in
Algorithm 2
.
By construction, the iterate
\bm{\theta}_{t}
is
a convex combination of extreme points of
{\cal C}
, referred to as active atoms.
We denote by
\mathcal{A}_{t}
the set of active atoms and denote by
\alpha_{t}^{\bm{a}}

the positive weight of any active atom

\textstyle\bm{\theta}_{t}=\sum_{{\bm{a}}\in\mathcal{A}_{t}}\alpha_{t}^{{\bm{a}}}\cdot{\bm{a}}\quad\text{with}\quad\alpha_{t}^{\bm{a}}>0\;.

(4)

At each round, two types of step might be taken.
If the condition of line
5
in
Algorithm 2
is satisfied,
we call the iteration a “FW step”, otherwise
we call it an “AW step”.
When a FW step is taken,
a new atom
{\bm{a}}_{t}^{\sf FW}

\bm{\theta}_{t}
is moved towards
{\bm{a}}_{t}^{\sf FW}

and the active set

which is maximally
correlated to the negative aggregated gradient.
Note that this step is identical to a usual O-FW iteration.
When an “AW step” is taken, a currently active atom

{\bm{a}}_{t}^{\sf AW}
is selected (
3
)
and the current iterate is moved away from

{\bm{a}}_{t}^{\sf AW}
(line
8
and
15
).
The atom
{\bm{a}}_{t}^{\sf AW}
is the active atom
which is the most correlated to the current gradient approximation.
The intuition is that taking the ‘away’ step prevents the algorithm from following a ‘zig-zag’ path when
\bm{\theta}_{t}

is close to the boundary of
{\cal C}

[
Wol70
]
.

Lastly, we note that the O-AW algorithm is similar to a classical AW algorithm
[
Wol70
]
.
The exception is that a fixed step size rule is adopted due
to the online optimization setting.

Remark 1
.

As the linear optimization (
3
) enumerates over the active atoms

{\cal A}_{t}
at round
t
, the O-AW algorithm is suitable when

{\cal C}
is an atomic (or polytope) set,
otherwise
|{\cal A}_{t}|
may become too large.

Remark 2

(Linear Optimization.)
.

The run-time complexity of the O-FW and O-AW algorithms depends
on finding efficient solution to the linear optimization step.
In many cases, this is extremely efficient.
For example, when
{\cal C}
is the trace-norm ball, then the linear optimization
amounts to finding the top singular vectors of the gradient; see
[
Jag13
]
for an overview.

Remark 3

(Complexity per iteration.)
.

In addition to the linear optimization,
both O-FW/O-AW algorithms require the aggregate gradient
{\nabla}F_{t}(\bm{\theta}_{t})
to be computed
at each round, and the complexity involved grows with the round number.
In cases when the loss
f_{t}
is the negated log-likelihood of an exponential
family distribution, the gradient aggregation can be replaced by an efficient ‘on-the-fly’ update,
whose complexity is a dimension-dependent
constant over the iterations
.
As demonstrated in Section
5
and
Appendix I
, this set-up covers many problems of interest, among others the online matrix completion and online LASSO.

\begin{split}\sum_{t=T/2+1}^{T}\Big(\Delta_{t}-(F_{t}(\bm{\theta}_{t+1})-F_{t}(\bm{\theta}_{t+1}^{\star}))\Big)&=\sum_{t=T/2+1}^{T}\Big((F_{t}(\bm{\theta}_{t})-F_{t}(\bm{\theta}_{t+1}))-(F_{t}(\bm{\theta}_{t}^{\star})-F_{t}(\bm{\theta}_{t+1}^{\star}))\Big)\vskip 5.69046pt\\

Appendix H
Improved gradient error bound for online MC

Our goal is to show that with high probability,

\|{\nabla}F_{t}(\bm{\theta})-{\nabla}f(\bm{\theta})\|_{\sigma,\infty}={\cal O}(\sqrt{\log t/t}),~\forall~t~\text{sufficiently large}.

(86)

To facilitate our proof, let us state the following conditions on the observation noise statistics:

A 1
.

The noise variance is finite, that is
there exists a constant

\bar{\sigma}>0
such that for all
\vartheta\in\mathbb{R}
,

0\leq{\rm A}^{\prime\prime}(\vartheta)\leq\bar{\sigma}^{2}\;,

and the noise is sub-exponential i.e., there exist a constant
\lambda\geq 1
such
that for all
(k,l)\in[m_{1}]\times[m_{2}]
:

\int\exp\left(\lambda^{-1}\left|y-{\rm A}^{\prime}(\bar{\bm{\theta}}_{k,l})\right|\right)p_{\bar{\bm{\theta}}}(y|k,l)dy\leq\mathrm{e}\;,

(87)

where
p_{\bar{\bm{\theta}}}(\cdot)

is defined as
p_{\bar{\bm{\theta}}}(y|k,l)\mathrel{\mathop{:}}={\rm m}(y)\exp\left(y\bar{\bm{\theta}}_{k,l}-{\rm A}(\bar{\bm{\theta}}_{k,l})\right)
and

\mathrm{e}
is the natural number.

A 2
.

There exists a finite constant
\kappa>0

such that for all
\bm{\theta}\in\mathcal{C}
,
k\in[m_{1}]
,
l\in[m_{2}]

\kappa\geq\max\left(\sqrt{\sum_{l=1}^{m_{2}}{\rm A}^{\prime}(\bm{\theta}_{k,l})^{2}},\sqrt{\sum_{k=1}^{m_{1}}{\rm A}^{\prime}(\bm{\theta}_{k,l})^{2}}\right)\;.

(88)

Notice that
\kappa={\cal O}(\sqrt{\max\{m_{1},m_{2}\}})
.

We remark that A
1
and A
2
are satisfied by all the
exponential family distributions.
We also need the following proposition.

Proposition 14
.

Consider a finite sequence of independent
random matrices
(Z_{s})_{1\leq s\leq t}\in\mathbb{R}^{m_{1}\times m_{2}}

satisfying
\mathbb{E}[Z_{i}]=0
. For some
U>0
,
assume

\inf\{\lambda>0:\mathbb{E}[\exp(\|Z_{i}\|_{\sigma,\infty}/\lambda)]\leq\mathrm{e}\}\leq U\quad\forall i\in[n],

(89)

and there exists
\sigma_{Z}
s.t.

\sigma^{2}_{Z}\geq\max\left\{\left\|\frac{1}{t}\sum_{s=1}^{t}\mathbb{E}[{Z_{s}}Z_{s}^{\top}]\right\|_{\sigma,\infty},\left\|\frac{1}{t}\sum_{s=1}^{t}\mathbb{E}[Z_{s}^{\top}Z_{s}]\right\|_{\sigma,\infty}\right\}\;.

(90)

Then for any
\nu>0
,
with probability at least
1-\mathrm{e}^{-\nu}

\left\|\frac{1}{t}\sum_{i=1}^{t}Z_{i}\right\|_{\sigma,\infty}\leq c_{U}\max\left\{\sigma_{Z}\sqrt{\frac{\nu+\log(d)}{t}},U\log(\frac{U}{\sigma_{Z}})\frac{\nu+\log(d)}{t}\right\}\;,

(91)

with
c_{U}
an increasing constant with
U
.

Proof.

This result is proved in Theorem 4 in
[
Kol13
]

for symmetric matrices. Here we state a slightly different result
because
\sigma^{2}_{Z}
is an upper bound of the variance and
not the variance itself. However, it does not the alter the proof
and the result stays valid. This concentration is extended to rectangular matrices by dilation,
see Proposition 11 in
[
Klo14
]

for details.
∎

Our result is stated as follows.

Proposition 15
.

Assume A
1
, A
2

and that the sampling distribution is uniform.
Define
the approximation error

\bm{\epsilon}_{t}(\bm{\theta})\mathrel{\mathop{:}}={\nabla}F_{t}(\bm{\theta})-\nabla f(\bm{\theta})
.
With probability at least
1-\epsilon
,
for any

t\geq T_{\epsilon}\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=(\lambda/\bar{\sigma})^{2}\log^{2}(\lambda/\bar{\sigma})\log(d+2d/\epsilon)
,
and any
\bm{\theta}\in\mathcal{C}_{R}
:

\|\bm{\epsilon}_{t}(\bm{\theta})\|_{\sigma,\infty}=\mathcal{O}\left(c_{\lambda}\left(\kappa+\bar{\sigma}\right)\sqrt{\frac{\log(d(1+t^{2}/\epsilon))}{t(m_{1}\wedge m_{2})}}\right)\;,

with

\|\cdot\|_{\sigma,\infty}
the
operator norm,
c_{\lambda}
a constant which depends only on
\lambda
. The constants
\lambda
,
\bar{\sigma}
and
\kappa
are
defined in A
1
and A
2
.

Proof.

For a fixed
\bm{\theta}
, by the triangle inequality

\|\bm{\epsilon}_{t}(\bm{\theta})\|_{\sigma,\infty}\leq\|\frac{1}{t}\sum_{s=1}^{t}{Y}_{s}e_{k_{s}}e^{{}^{\prime}\top}_{l_{s}}-\mathbb{E}[{Y}_{s}e_{k_{s}}e^{{}^{\prime}\top}_{l_{s}}]\|_{\sigma,\infty}+\|\frac{1}{t}\sum_{s=1}^{t}{\rm A}^{\prime}(\bm{\theta}_{k_{s},l_{s}})e_{k_{s}}e^{{}^{\prime}\top}_{l_{s}}-\mathbb{E}[{\rm A}^{\prime}(\bm{\theta}_{k_{s},l_{s}})e_{k_{s}}e^{{}^{\prime}\top}_{l_{s}}]\|_{\sigma,\infty}

Define

Z_{s}\mathrel{\mathop{:}}={Y}_{s}e_{k_{s}}e^{{}^{\prime}\top}_{l_{s}}-\mathbb{E}[{Y}_{s}e_{k_{s}}e^{{}^{\prime}\top}_{l_{s}}]
, then

\displaystyle\|\mathbb{E}[Z_{s}Z_{s}^{\top}]\|_{\sigma,\infty}\leq\|\mathbb{E}[{Y}_{s}^{2}e_{k_{s}}e^{{}^{\prime}\top}_{l_{s}}e^{{}^{\prime}}_{l_{s}}e_{k_{s}}^{\top}]\|_{\sigma,\infty}\;,

\displaystyle=\|\frac{1}{m_{1}m_{2}}\diag\left(\left(\sum_{l=1}^{m_{2}}\mathbb{E}[{Y}_{s}^{2}|k,l]\right)_{k=1}^{m_{1}}\right)\|_{\sigma,\infty}\;,

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{m_{1}m_{2}}\max_{k\in[m_{1}]}\left(\sum_{l=1}^{m_{2}}{\rm A}^{\prime\prime}(\bar{\bm{\theta}}_{k,l})+({\rm A}^{\prime}(\bar{\bm{\theta}}_{k,l}))^{2}\right)\;,

where we used the fact that the distribution belongs
to the exponential family for the second equality.
Similarly one shows that

\|\mathbb{E}[Z_{s}^{\top}Z_{s}]\|_{\sigma,\infty}
satisfies the same upper bound.
Hence by Proposition
14
and A
1
, with probability at least
1-\mathrm{e}^{-\nu}
,
it holds

\|\frac{1}{t}\sum_{s=1}^{t}Z_{s}\|_{\sigma,\infty}\leq c_{\lambda}\sqrt{\frac{(\bar{\sigma}^{2}+\kappa^{2})(\nu+\log(d))}{t(m_{1}\wedge m_{2})}}\;,

(92)

for
t
larger than the threshold given in the proposition statement.
For the second term, define

P_{t}\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=1/t\sum_{s=1}^{t}e_{k_{s}}e_{l_{s}}^{{}^{\prime}\top}-(m_{1}m_{2})^{-1}{\bm{1}\bm{1}^{\top}}
,
we get

\begin{split}\|\frac{1}{t}\sum_{s=1}^{t}{\rm A}^{\prime}(\bm{\theta}_{k_{s},l_{s}})e_{k_{s}}e^{{}^{\prime}\top}_{l_{s}}-\mathbb{E}[{\rm A}^{\prime}(\bm{\theta}_{k_{s},l_{s}})e_{k_{s}}e^{{}^{\prime}\top}_{l_{s}}]\|_{\sigma,\infty}=\|P_{t}\odot({\rm A}^{\prime}(\bm{\theta}_{k,l}))_{k,l}\|_{\sigma,\infty}\leq\kappa\|P_{t}\|_{\sigma,\infty}\;,\end{split}

(93)

where
\odot
denotes the Hardamard product and we have used Theorem 5.5.3 in
[
HJ94
]
for the last inequality.
Define
Z^{\prime}_{s}\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=e_{k_{s}}e_{l_{s}}^{{}^{\prime}\top}-(m_{1}m_{2})^{-1}{\bm{1}\bm{1}^{\top}}
.
Since by definition,
\lambda\geq 1
, one can again apply Proposition

for

and get with probability at least

\|P_{t}\|_{\sigma,\infty}\leq c_{\lambda}\sqrt{\frac{\nu+\log(d)}{t(m_{1}\wedge m_{2})}}\;.

(94)

Hence, by a union bound argument we find that
with probability at least

(95)

Appendix I
Additional results: Online LASSO

Consider the setting where we are sequentially
given i.i.d. observations
({\bm{Y}}_{t},{\bm{A}}_{t})
such that
{\bm{Y}}_{t}\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
is
the response,
{\bm{A}}_{t}\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times n}
is the random design
and

{\bm{Y}}_{t}={\bm{A}}_{t}\bar{\bm{\theta}}+{\bm{w}}_{t}\;,

(96)

where the vector
{\bm{w}}_{t}
is i.i.d.,
[{\bm{w}}_{t}]_{i}
is independent of
[{\bm{w}}_{t}]_{j}
for
i\neq j

and
[{\bm{w}}_{t}]_{i}
is zero-mean and sub-Gaussian with parameter
\sigma_{w}
.
We suppose that the unknown parameter
\bar{\bm{\theta}}
is sparse.
Attempting to learn
\bar{\bm{\theta}}
, a natural choice for
the loss function at round
t
is the square loss, i.e.,

f_{t}(\bm{\theta})=(1/2)\|{\bm{Y}}_{t}-{\bm{A}}_{t}\bm{\theta}\|_{2}^{2}

(97)

and the stochastic cost associated is

f(\bm{\theta})\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=\frac{1}{2}\mathbb{E}_{\bar{\bm{\theta}}}[\|{\bm{Y}}_{t}-{\bm{A}}_{t}\bm{\theta}\|_{2}^{2}]
.
As
\bar{\bm{\theta}}
is sparse, the constraint set is designed to be the
\ell_{1}
ball, i.e.,

{\cal C}=\{\bm{\theta}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}~:~\|\bm{\theta}\|_{1}\leq r\}
, where
r>0
is a regularization constant.
Note that
{\cal C}
is a polytope.

The aggregated gradient can be expressed as

{\nabla}F_{t}(\bm{\theta}_{t})=t^{-1}\Big(\sum_{s=1}^{t}{\bm{A}}_{s}^{\top}{\bm{A}}_{s}\Big)\bm{\theta}_{t}-t^{-1}\Big(\sum_{s=1}^{t}{\bm{A}}_{s}^{\top}{\bm{Y}}_{s}\Big)\;.

(98)

Similar to the case of online matrix completion, the terms
\sum_{s=1}^{t}{\bm{A}}_{s}^{\top}{\bm{A}}_{s}

and
\sum_{s=1}^{t}{\bm{A}}_{s}^{\top}{\bm{Y}}_{s}
can be computed ‘on-the-fly’ as running sums.
Applying O-FW (
Algorithm 1
) or O-AW (
Algorithm 2
) with the
above aggregated gradient yields an online LASSO algorithm
with a constant complexity (dimension-dependent) per iteration.
Notice that as
{\cal C}
is an
\ell_{1}
ball constraint, the linear optimization in Line
4

of
Algorithm 1
or (
3
) in
Algorithm 2
can be evaluated simply as

{\bm{a}}_{t}=-r\cdot{\rm sign}([{\nabla}F_{t}(\bm{\theta}_{t})]_{i})\cdot{\bm{e}}_{i}
,
where
i=\arg\max_{j\in[n]}|[{\nabla}F_{t}(\bm{\theta}_{t})]_{j}|
.

Similar to the case of online MC, we derive the following
{\cal O}(\sqrt{\log t/t})
bound for the
gradient error:

Proposition 16
.

Assume that
\|{\bm{A}}_{t}^{\top}{\bm{A}}_{t}-\mathbb{E}[{\bm{A}}^{\top}{\bm{A}}]\|_{max}\leq B_{1}
and
\|{\bm{A}}_{t}\|_{max}\leq B_{2}
almost surely, with
\|\cdot\|_{max}
being the matrix max norm.
Define
c\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=\max_{\bm{\theta}\in{\cal C}}\|\bm{\theta}-\bar{\bm{\theta}}\|_{1}
.
With probability at least
1-(1+1/n)(\pi^{2}\epsilon/6)
, the following holds for all
\bm{\theta}\in{\cal C}
and all
t\geq 1
:

\|{\nabla}F_{t}(\bm{\theta})-{\nabla}f(\bm{\theta})\|_{\infty}\leq(cB_{1}+\sqrt{mB_{2}\sigma_{w}^{2}})\sqrt{\frac{2(\log(2n^{2}t^{2})-\log\epsilon)}{t}}\;,

(99)

where
\|\cdot\|_{\infty}
is the infinity norm and the dual norm of
\|\cdot\|_{1}
.

We observe that H
3
is satisfied with
\eta^{\epsilon}_{t}
asymptotically
equivalent to
4{\log(t)}
and
\alpha=0.5
.
Furthermore, the stochastic cost
f
is
L
-Lipschitz if
L{\bf I}\succeq\mathbb{E}[{\bm{A}}^{\top}{\bm{A}}]
;

\mu
-strongly convex if
\mathbb{E}[{\bm{A}}^{\top}{\bm{A}}]\succeq\mu{\bf 1}
for
some
\mu>0
;
and H
2
is satisfied as
{\cal C}
is a polytope.
The analysis from the previous section applies, i.e., O-FW/O-AW has a regret bound of
{\cal O}(\log^{2}T/T)
and an anytime bound of
{\cal O}(\log t/t)
.

Proof.

Notice that the gradient vector is given by:

{\nabla}f(\bm{\theta})=\mathbb{E}[{\bm{A}}^{\top}({\bm{A}}\bm{\theta}-{\bm{Y}})]=\mathbb{E}[{\bm{A}}^{\top}{\bm{A}}]\bm{\theta}-\mathbb{E}[{\bm{A}}^{\top}{\bm{Y}}].

(100)

We can bound the gradient estimation error as:

\begin{array}[]{l}\displaystyle\|{\nabla}F_{t}(\bm{\theta})-{\nabla}f(\bm{\theta})\|_{\infty}\leq\Big\|\frac{1}{t}\sum_{s=1}^{t}{\bm{A}}_{s}^{\top}{\bm{w}}_{s}\Big\|_{\infty}+\Big\|\frac{1}{t}\sum_{s=1}^{t}\big({\bm{A}}_{s}^{\top}{\bm{A}}_{s}-\mathbb{E}[{\bm{A}}^{\top}{\bm{A}}]\big)(\bm{\theta}-\bar{\bm{\theta}})\Big\|_{\infty}\end{array}

(101)

To bound the second term in (
101
), we define
{\bm{Z}}_{s}\mathrel{\mathop{:}}={\bm{A}}_{s}^{\top}{\bm{A}}_{s}-\mathbb{E}[{\bm{A}}^{\top}{\bm{A}}]
. Observe that

\Big\|\frac{1}{t}\sum_{s=1}^{t}{\bm{Z}}_{s}(\bm{\theta}-\bar{\bm{\theta}})\Big\|_{\infty}=\max_{i\in[n]}\Big|\frac{1}{t}\sum_{s=1}^{t}{\bm{z}}_{s,i}(\bm{\theta}-\bar{\bm{\theta}})\Big|,

(102)

where
{\bm{z}}_{s,i}
denotes the
i
th row vector in
{\bm{Z}}_{s}
. Furthermore, by the Holder’s inequality,

\big|\frac{1}{t}\sum_{s=1}^{t}{\bm{z}}_{s,i}(\bm{\theta}-\bar{\bm{\theta}})\big|\leq\|\bm{\theta}-\bar{\bm{\theta}}\|_{1}\big\|\frac{1}{t}\sum_{s=1}^{t}{\bm{z}}_{s,i}\big\|_{\infty},

(103)

Now that
{\bm{z}}_{s,i}
is a zero-mean, independent random vector with elements bounded in
[-B_{1},B_{1}]

\mathbb{P}\big(\big\|\textstyle(1/t)\sum_{s=1}^{t}{\bm{z}}_{s,i}\big\|_{\infty}\geq x,~\forall~i\big)\leq 2n^{2}\mathrm{e}^{-\frac{x^{2}t}{2B_{1}^{2}}}.

(104)

Setting
x=B_{1}\sqrt{2(\log(2n^{2}t^{2})-\log\epsilon)/t}
gives
\epsilon/t^{2}
on the right hand side. With probability at least
1-\epsilon/t^{2}
, we have

\Big\|\frac{1}{t}\sum_{s=1}^{t}{\bm{Z}}_{s}\bm{\theta}\Big\|_{\infty}\leq cB_{1}\sqrt{2(\log(2n^{2}t^{2})-\log\Delta)/t},

(105)

To bound the first term in (
101
), we find that the
i
th element of the vector
{\bm{A}}_{s}^{\top}{\bm{w}}_{s}
is zero-mean. Furthermore, it can be verified that

\mathbb{E}\big[\mathrm{e}^{\big(\lambda\sum_{j=1}^{m}A_{s,i,j}w_{s,j}\big)}\big]\leq\mathrm{e}^{\lambda^{2}\cdot m\sigma_{w}^{2}B_{2}/2},

(106)

for all
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
, where
A_{s,i,j}
is the
(i,j)
th element of
{\bm{A}}_{s}
and
w_{s,j}
is the
j
th element of
{\bm{w}}_{s}
. In other words, the
i
th element of
{\bm{A}}_{s}^{\top}{\bm{w}}_{s}
is sub-Gaussian with parameter
m\cdot\sigma_{w}^{2}B_{2}

\mathbb{P}\big(\big\|\frac{1}{t}\sum_{s=1}^{t}{\bm{A}}_{s}^{\top}{\bm{w}}_{s}\big\|_{\infty}\geq x\big)\leq 2n\mathrm{e}^{-\frac{x^{2}t}{2mB_{2}\sigma_{w}^{2}}}.

(107)

Setting
x=\sigma_{w}\sqrt{2mB_{2}(\log(2n^{2}t^{2})-\log\epsilon)/t}
yields
\epsilon/(nt^{2})
on the right hand side. Combining (
104
), (
107
) and using a union bound argument (for all

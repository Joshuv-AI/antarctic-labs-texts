Introduction

In the last years, an increasing interest has been
devoted to the study of (nontrivial bounded) solutions
of the quasilinear elliptic
p
–Laplacian type problem

\hskip-2.0pt\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}-\divg[(A_{0}(x)+A(x)|u|^{ps})|\nabla u|^{p-2}\nabla u]+s\ A(x)|u|^{ps-2}u\ |\nabla u|^{p}&\\[5.0pt]
\qquad\qquad\qquad=\ \mu|u|^{q-2}u+g(x,u)&\hbox{in $\Omega$,}\\[10.0pt]
u=0&\hbox{on $\partial\Omega$,}\end{array}\right.

(1.1)

where
\Omega
is a bounded domain in
\mathbb{R}^{N}
,
N\geq 2
,

1<p<N
,
s>1/p
,
A_{0}(x)
and
A(x)
are given functions on
\Omega
,

1\leq q\leq p^{\ast}(s+1)
with
p^{\ast}=Np/(N-p)
critical Sobolev exponent,

\mu\in\mathbb{R}
, and
g:\Omega\times\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}

is a given nonlinear term which grows with a power strictly less than
q-1
.

From a physical point of view, problem (
1.1
) is interesting for its applications.
In fact, if
\Omega=\mathbb{R}^{N}

model equations of (
1.1
) appear in Mathematical Physics
and describe several physical phenomena in the theory of superfluid film and in dissipative
quantum mechanics (for more details, see, e.g.,
[
14
]
and references therein)
and a possible approach to the study of solutions of equation (
1.1
)
on
\mathbb{R}^{N}
requires an approximation scheme related to the existence of solutions
in bounded domains (see, e.g.,
[
11
]
).

If
s=0
problem (
1.1
) has been widely investigated
by using variational methods
(see, e.g.,
[
16
]
and references therein),
but if
s>0
and coefficient
A(x)
is nontrivial,
the “natural” functional
{\cal J}
related to problem (
1.1
)
is not
C^{1}
in
W^{1,p}_{0}(\Omega)
as it
is Gâteaux differentiable only along directions of

W^{1,p}_{0}(\Omega)\cap L^{\infty}(\Omega)
.

In the past, such a problem has been overcome by introducing suitable definitions
of critical point (see, e.g.,
[
3
,
4
,
12
,
15
]
).

Here, following the ideas in
[
6
]
, we consider
the functional
{\cal J}
defined in

X=W^{1,p}_{0}(\Omega)\cap L^{\infty}(\Omega)

so that, under suitable assumptions, it is
C^{1}
.

Then, a weak solution of this problem is a function
u
belonging
to the Banach space
X
that satisfies

\begin{split}&\int_{\Omega}\left[\left(A_{0}(x)+A(x)\ |u|^{ps}\right)|\nabla u|^{p-2}\nabla u\cdot\nabla v+s\ A(x)\ |u|^{ps-2}u\ v\ |\nabla u|^{p}\right]dx\\[5.0pt]
&\qquad\qquad=\mu\int_{\Omega}|u|^{q-2}u\ v\ dx+\int_{\Omega}g(x,u)\ v\ dx\qquad\forall v\in X.\end{split}

The purpose of this paper is to study the existence of nontrivial weak solutions
of problem (
1.1
) once
q\geq 1
is given.

The case
p(s+1)<q<p^{\ast}(s+1)
was studied in Candela et al.​
[
9
]

by using a Mountain Pass argument, on the contrary
the case
1\leq q<p(s+1)
was investigated in
[
10
]

by means of a Minimum Theorem.
So, here we focus on the borderline case
q=p(s+1)
and
we note that, when
s>p/(N-p)
, it has to be

p(s+1)>p^{\ast}

and hence problem (
1.1
) may also be supercritical.

If we replace
A_{0}(x)+A(x)\ |u|^{ps}

with a generic
{\cal A}(x,u)
which has not an explicit growth depending on
u
,
the related equation becomes

-\divg[{\cal A}(x,u)|\nabla u|^{p-2}\nabla u]+\frac{1}{p}{\cal A}_{u}(x,u)\ |\nabla u|^{p}=\mu|u|^{q-2}u+g(x,u)\quad\hbox{in $\Omega$}

and the corresponding asymptotically
p
–linear case requires
q=p
.
In this setting, if symmetry occurs, the existence of multiple solutions
have been proved since the seminal papers for
p=2
and
{\cal A}(x,u)\equiv 1

(see
[
1
,
5
]
) till the most recent ones for
p>1
and

{\cal A}_{u}(x,u)\not\equiv 0
in the non resonant case (see
[
8
]
if
p>N
,

[
7
]
if
1<p\leq N
).
Here, as
{\cal A}(x,u)=A_{0}(x)+A(x)\ |u|^{ps}
,
there is a new asymptotically “linear” case
which depends not only from
p
but also from
s
,
namely
q=p(s+1)
, and as a first step in investigating the properties
of this new threshold, we look for the existence of at least one nontrivial
weak bounded solution when no symmetry occurs.

More precisely, for
q=p(s+1)
with
s>1/p
,
we have that problem (
1.1
) reduces to

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}-\divg\left[\left(A_{0}(x)+A(x)|u|^{ps}\right)|\nabla u|^{p-2}\nabla u\right]+s\ A(x)|u|^{ps-2}u\ |\nabla u|^{p}&\\[5.0pt]
\qquad\qquad\qquad=\ \mu|u|^{p(s+1)-2}u+g(x,u)&\hbox{in $\Omega$,}\\[10.0pt]
u=0&\hbox{on $\partial\Omega$.}\end{array}\right.

(1.2)

Assume that the coefficients
A_{0}
,
A:\Omega\to\mathbb{R}

and the nonlinear term
g:\Omega\times\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
are such that:

(h_{1})

A_{0}\in L^{\infty}(\Omega)
,
A\in L^{\infty}(\Omega)
, and
\alpha_{0}>0
exists so that

A_{0}(x)\geq\alpha_{0}\quad\hbox{and}\quad A(x)\geq\alpha_{0}\qquad\hbox{for a.e.\! $x\in\Omega$;}

(h_{2})

g(x,t)
is a Carathéodory function in
\Omega\times\mathbb{R}
,
i.e.,
g(\cdot,t)
is measurable
for all
t\in\mathbb{R}
and
g(x,\cdot)

is continuous for a.e.
x\in\Omega
, and
some constants
a_{1}
,
a_{2}>0
and an exponent
1\leq r<p(s+1)
exist such that

|g(x,t)|\leq a_{1}+a_{2}\ |t|^{r-1}\quad\hbox{for a.e.\! $x\in\Omega$, all $t\in\mathbb{R}$;}

(h_{3})

a parameter
\nu\in\mathbb{R}
exists such that

g(x,t)=\nu|t|^{p-2}\ t+\text{o}(|t|^{p-1})\quad\hbox{as $t\to 0$, uniformly a.e.\! in $\Omega$.}

Firstly, we consider the asymptotic eigenvalue problems

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}-\divg\left(A_{0}(x)\ |\nabla u|^{p-2}\ \nabla u\right)=\nu|u|^{p-2}u&\text{in }\Omega\\[10.0pt]
u=0&\text{on }\partial\Omega\end{array}\right.

and

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}-\divg\left(A(x)\ |u|^{ps}\ |\nabla u|^{p-2}\ \nabla u\right)+sA(x)\ |u|^{ps-2}u\ |\nabla u|^{p}=\mu|u|^{p(s+1)-2}u&\text{in }\Omega\\[10.0pt]
u=0&\text{on }\partial\Omega\end{array}\right.

Thus, we introduce the Rayleigh quotients on
X
associated with these problems, which are

R
,

R(u)=\frac{\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}A_{0}(x)|\nabla u|^{p}dx}{\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}|u|^{p}dx},\qquad S(u)=\frac{(s+1)\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}A(x)\ |u|^{ps}\ |\nabla u|^{p}dx}{\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}|u|^{p(s+1)}dx},

Set

\nu_{1}=\inf_{u\in X\setminus\left\{0\right\}}R(u),\qquad\mu_{1}=\inf_{u\in X\setminus\left\{0\right\}}S(u).

(1.3)

For any
u\in X
, we have that

|\nabla(|u|^{s}u)|^{p}\ =\ (s+1)^{p}\ |u|^{ps}\ |\nabla u|^{p}\quad\hbox{a.e.\! in $\Omega$}

(1.4)

and, hence,
|u|^{s}u\in W^{1,p}_{0}(\Omega)
.
From (
1.4
) and

(

h
1

)

it follows that

\nu_{1}\ \geq\ \alpha_{0}\lambda_{1},\qquad\mu_{1}\ \geq\ \frac{\alpha_{0}}{(s+1)^{p-1}}\ \lambda_{1}

with

\lambda_{1}\ =\ \inf_{u\in W^{1,p}_{0}(\Omega)\setminus\left\{0\right\}}\ \frac{\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u|^{p}dx}{\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}|u|^{p}dx}\ >\ 0

(1.5)

first eigenvalue of
-\Delta_{p}
in
W^{1,p}_{0}(\Omega)
.
Thus,
\nu_{1}
,
\mu_{1}>0
.

2
Abstract settings

In this section we recall from
[
9
]

the abstract tools that we need to prove our theorems.

Let
(X,\left\|\cdot\right\|_{X})
be a Banach space with dual
(X^{\prime},\left\|\cdot\right\|_{X^{\prime}})
.
Assume that
X
is continuously embedded in a Banach space
(W,\left\|\cdot\right\|_{W})
.
Let
J:{\cal D}\subset W\to\mathbb{R}
be a functional such that
X\subset{\cal D}
and
J\in C^{1}(X,\mathbb{R})
.

For
\beta\in\mathbb{R}
, let

K_{J}^{\beta}=\left\{u\in X:J(u)=\beta,\,dJ(u)=0\right\}

be the set of critical points of
J
in
X
at the level
\beta
and let

J^{\beta}=\left\{u\in X:J(u)\leq\beta\right\}

be the sublevel set of
J
with respect to
\beta
.

Definition 2.1
.

We say that
J
satisfies the weak Cerami-Palais-Smale condition in
X

at the level
\beta\in\mathbb{R}
, or the
(wCPS)_{\beta}
condition for short,
if for every
(CPS)_{\beta}
–sequence
\left(u_{n}\right)_{n}\subset X
, i.e. a sequence
such that

J(u_{n})\to\beta,\qquad\left\|dJ(u_{n})\right\|_{X^{\prime}}(1+\left\|u_{n}\right\|_{X})\to 0,

there exists
u\in K_{J}^{\beta}
such that

\left\|u_{n}-u\right\|_{W}\to 0
for a renamed subsequence.

We have the following deformation lemma (see
[
9
, Lemma 2.2]
).

Lemma 2.2
.

If
\beta\in\mathbb{R}
is such that
J
satisfies the
(wCPS)_{\beta}

condition and
K_{J}^{\beta}=\emptyset
,
then for any
\bar{\varepsilon}>0
, there exist
0<\varepsilon<\bar{\varepsilon}

and a homeomorphism
\psi:X\to X
such that

(i)

\psi(J^{\beta+\varepsilon})\subset J^{\beta-\varepsilon}
,

(ii)

\psi(u)=u
if
J(u)\leq\beta-\bar{\varepsilon}
or
J(u)\geq\beta+\bar{\varepsilon}
.

In particular, this lemma gives a minimizer when
J
is bounded from below.

Theorem 2.3
.

If

\beta:=\inf_{u\in X}\ J(u)>-\infty

(2.1)

and
J
satisfies the
(wCPS)_{\beta}
condition, then
K_{J}^{\beta}\neq\emptyset
.

Proof.

Suppose
K_{J}^{\beta}=\emptyset
. Then Lemma
2.2

gives a constant
\varepsilon>0
and a homeomorphism
\psi:X\to X

such that
\psi(J^{\beta+\varepsilon})\subset J^{\beta-\varepsilon}
.
Since
J^{\beta+\varepsilon}\neq\emptyset
by (
2.1
), then
J^{\beta-\varepsilon}\neq\emptyset
,
contradicting (
2.1
).
∎

Lemma
2.2
also implies a generalized version of the Ambrosetti-Rabinowitz
Mountain Pass Theorem (see
[
2
]
).

Theorem 2.4
.

Assume that there exist a neighborhood
U
of the origin in
X

and
u_{1}\in X\setminus\overline{U}
such that

\max\left\{J(0),J(u_{1})\right\}\ <\ \inf_{u\in\partial U}\ J(u).

Let

\Gamma\ =\ \left\{\gamma\in C([0,1],X):\ \gamma(0)=0,\,\gamma(1)=u_{1}\right\}

be the class of paths in
X
joining
0
and
u_{1}
, and set

\beta:=\inf_{\gamma\in\Gamma}\ \max_{u\in\gamma([0,1])}\ J(u)\ \geq\ \inf_{u\in\partial U}J(u).

(2.2)

If
J
satisfies the
(wCPS)_{\beta}
condition, then
K_{J}^{\beta}\neq\emptyset
.

Proof.

Suppose
K_{J}^{\beta}=\emptyset
and
let
0<\bar{\varepsilon}\leq\beta-\max\left\{J(0),J(u_{1})\right\}
.
Then, a value
0<\varepsilon<\bar{\varepsilon}

and a homeomorphism
\psi:X\to X
exist as in Lemma
2.2
.
On the other hand,
by (
2.2
),
\exists\,\gamma\in\Gamma
such that
\max J(\gamma([0,1]))\leq\beta+\varepsilon
.

Now, let
\widetilde{\gamma}=\psi\circ\gamma
.
Since
J(0),J(u_{1})\leq\beta-\bar{\varepsilon}
, we have that

\psi(0)=0
and
\psi(u_{1})=u_{1}
,
so
\widetilde{\gamma}\in\Gamma
.
However,
\max J(\widetilde{\gamma}([0,1]))\leq\beta-\varepsilon
since

\psi(J^{\beta+\varepsilon})\subset J^{\beta-\varepsilon}
, contradicting (
2.2
).
∎

From now on, let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{N}

,

.

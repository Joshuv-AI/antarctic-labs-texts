Introduction

1.1
Time operators

In this paper, we are concerned with time operators of
the one-dimensional harmonic oscillator.
Time operators are defined in this paper
as conjugate operators for self-adjoint operators.
We begin with defining time operators.
Let
[A,B]
be the commutator of linear operators
A
and
B
defined by

[A,B]=AB-BA.

Definition 1.1

Let
A
be self-adjoint and
B
symmetric on a Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}
.
If
A
and
B

satisfy
the canonical commutation relation:

\displaystyle[A,B]=-i{\mathchoice{\rm 1\mskip-4.0mul}{\rm 1\mskip-4.0mul}{\rm 1\mskip-4.5mul}{\rm 1\mskip-5.0mul}}

on a non-zero subspace
D_{A,B}\subset\mathrm{D}(AB)\cap\mathrm{D}(BA)
,
then
B
is called a time operator of
A
.

Although the term “time operator” may not be appropriate at some situations, we follow the conventions and use this terminology throughout this paper.
We show several examples of time operators.

(Position-Momentum)
Let
p=\frac{1}{i}\frac{d}{dx}
and
q=M_{x}
be the multiplication by
x
.
Both
p
and
q
are self-adjoint operators on

\displaystyle\mathrm{D}(p)=\left\{f\in{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}\ \middle|\ k\hat{f}(k)\in{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}\right\},

\displaystyle\mathrm{D}(q)=\left\{f\in{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}\ \middle|\ xf\in{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}\right\},

respectively, where
\hat{f}
denotes the
Fourier transform of
f\in{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}
,
which is given by

\hat{f}(k)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}f(x)e^{-ikx}dx
.
Hence the canonical commutation relation

\displaystyle[p,q]=-i{\mathchoice{\rm 1\mskip-4.0mul}{\rm 1\mskip-4.0mul}{\rm 1\mskip-4.5mul}{\rm 1\mskip-5.0mul}}

(1.1)

holds on
\mathrm{D}(pq)\cap\mathrm{D}(qp)
.
Thus

q
is a time operator of
p
,
and

\mathrm{D}(pq)\cap\mathrm{D}(qp)
is dense in
{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}
.

(Energy-Time: continuous case)
The operator
\frac{1}{2}p^{2}
describes the energy of the one-dimensional free quantum particle with the unit mass and the spectrum of
\frac{1}{2}p^{2}
is purely absolutely continuous, i.e.,

{\rm Spec}(\frac{1}{2}p^{2})=[0,\infty)
.
Let

\displaystyle\hat{T}_{\rm AB}=\frac{1}{2}\left(p^{-1}q+qp^{-1}\right).

(1.2)

\hat{T}_{\rm AB}
is called the Aharonov-Bohm operator
[
1
]
or the time of arrival operator.
It holds that

\displaystyle\left[\frac{1}{2}p^{2},\hat{T}_{\rm AB}\right]=-i{\mathchoice{\rm 1\mskip-4.0mul}{\rm 1\mskip-4.0mul}{\rm 1\mskip-4.5mul}{\rm 1\mskip-5.0mul}}

(1.3)

on a dense domain.
Then
\hat{T}_{\rm AB}
is a time operator of
\frac{1}{2}p^{2}
.

(Energy-Time: discrete case)
Let
\frac{1}{2}(p^{2}+q^{2})
be the one-dimensional harmonic oscillator which is the main object in this paper.
The spectrum of
\frac{1}{2}(p^{2}+q^{2})
is purely discrete, i.e.,

{\rm Spec}\left(\frac{1}{2}(p^{2}+q^{2})\right)=\{n+\frac{1}{2}\}_{n=0}^{\infty}
.
Let

\displaystyle\hat{T}=\frac{1}{2}\left(\arctan(p^{-1}q)+\arctan(qp^{-1})\right).

(1.4)

\hat{T}
is called the angle operator.
The name comes from the fact below;
in the classical phase space the angle

between
(p_{c},q_{c})
and
p_{c}
-axis is described by

.
It can be seen
that

\hat{T}
and
\frac{1}{2}(p^{2}+q^{2})

formally
satisfy

\displaystyle\left[\frac{1}{2}(p^{2}+q^{2}),\hat{T}\right]=-i{\mathchoice{\rm 1\mskip-4.0mul}{\rm 1\mskip-4.0mul}{\rm 1\mskip-4.5mul}{\rm 1\mskip-5.0mul}}

(1.5)

on some domain. Then
\hat{T}
is a time operator of
\frac{1}{2}(p^{2}+q^{2})
. However, as far as we know, there are no rigorous definitions of
both
\arctan(p^{-1}q)
and
\arctan(qp^{-1})
.

(Number-Phase)
Let
a=q+ip
and
{a^{\ast}}=q-ip
.
Then
N={a^{\ast}}a
is called the number operator.
It holds that
[a,{a^{\ast}}]={\mathchoice{\rm 1\mskip-4.0mul}{\rm 1\mskip-4.0mul}{\rm 1\mskip-4.5mul}{\rm 1\mskip-5.0mul}}
.
In physics literatures a symmetric
operator
\hat{\phi}
satisfying

\displaystyle\left[N,\hat{\phi}\right]=-i{\mathchoice{\rm 1\mskip-4.0mul}{\rm 1\mskip-4.0mul}{\rm 1\mskip-4.5mul}{\rm 1\mskip-5.0mul}}

(1.6)

is called the phase operator,
and
formally
it is described as

\displaystyle\hat{\phi}=\frac{i}{2}(\log a-\log{a^{\ast}}).

(1.7)

Thus

.
However, there are also no rigorous definitions of
both
\log a
and
\log{a^{\ast}}
.

Four canonical commutation relations (

), (

), (

but

In this paper we study (

In this paper we discuss two time operators of the one-dimensional harmonic oscillator.
One is the angle operator and the other
an operator defined by a positive operator valued measure (POVM).
The purpose of this paper is to consider properties of
both the angle operator and the operator defined by POVM, rigorously,
and to show relationships between

of Hamiltonians with purely discrete spectrum
and
time operators
of Hamiltonians with purely absolutely continuous spectrum under the firm mathematical frame.
The later can be reduced to consider a limit of the family of
angle operators
T_{\varepsilon}
defined below as
\varepsilon\to 0
.

1.2
Aharonov-Bohm operator, angle operators and POVM

Within the long-running discussion on time in quantum mechanics,
Aharonov and Bohm introduced the operator
\hat{T}_{\rm AB}

[
1
]
.
They considered the quantization of functions of the classical momentum
P
and the classical position
Q
,
which provides us with the time of arrival at a point
y
of a particle that at the instant
t=0
has position
x
and momentum
P
, i.e.,

\displaystyle\mbox{time of arrival}=\frac{x-y}{P}.

(1.8)

The classical expression (
1.8
) of the time of arrival
suggests to define the symmetric time operator of the free Hamiltonian
\frac{1}{2}p^{2}

in quantum mechanics by

\hat{T}_{\rm AB}
.
It is established that
\hat{T}_{\rm AB}
is symmetric but
no self-adjoint extensions exist, and
it satisfies

\left[\frac{1}{2}p^{2},\hat{T}_{\rm AB}\right]=-i{\mathchoice{\rm 1\mskip-4.0mul}{\rm 1\mskip-4.0mul}{\rm 1\mskip-4.5mul}{\rm 1\mskip-5.0mul}}

on a dense domain.
See e.g.,
[
29
]
and
[
4
, Theorems 4.21 and 4.22]
.

In this paper we are concerned with time operators
of the one-dimensional harmonic oscillator with parameter
\varepsilon
:

\frac{1}{2}(p^{2}+\varepsilon q^{2}),\quad 0<\varepsilon\leq 1.

Operator
\frac{1}{2}(p^{2}+\varepsilon q^{2})
is self-adjoint on
\mathrm{D}(p^{2})\cap\mathrm{D}(q^{2})
.
We shall construct
\hat{T}_{\varepsilon}
such that

\left[\frac{1}{2}(p^{2}+\varepsilon q^{2}),\hat{T}_{\varepsilon}\right]=-i{\mathchoice{\rm 1\mskip-4.0mul}{\rm 1\mskip-4.0mul}{\rm 1\mskip-4.5mul}{\rm 1\mskip-5.0mul}}.

By a heuristic argument we see in Section
2.1
,
formally
\hat{T}_{\varepsilon}
is given by

\hat{T}_{\varepsilon}=\frac{1}{2}\frac{1}{\sqrt{\varepsilon}}\left(\arctan(\sqrt{\varepsilon}p^{-1}q)+\arctan(\sqrt{\varepsilon}qp^{-1})\right).

One standard way of constructing time operators is to apply
POVMs. The details are demonstrated in Section
6
.
Let
P
be a POVM
on a measurable space
(\Omega,\mathcal{B})

associated with a self-adjoint operator
H
.
We define

T_{\rm POVM}=\int_{\Omega}tdP_{t}.

Then
T_{\rm POVM}
is a time operator of
H
.
By using the POVM associated with
\frac{1}{2}(p^{2}+q^{2})

one can also construct a time operator
T_{G}
of
\frac{1}{2}(p^{2}+q^{2})
.
See (
6.4
) and (
6.5
).

1.3
Momentum representation

In what follows we
take the momentum representation instead of the position representation.
Let
F\colon L^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{x})\to L^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{k})
be the Fourier transformation.
Then

FpF^{-1}=M_{k},\quad FqF^{-1}=+i\frac{d}{dk},

where
M_{k}
is the multiplication by
k
on
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{k})
:

\displaystyle\mathrm{D}(M_{k})=\left\{f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{k})\ \middle|\ kf(k)\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{k})\right\},

\displaystyle M_{k}f(k)=kf(k).

Since we work in
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{k})
for
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{x})

in what follows because of taking the momentum representation,
instead of notations
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{k})

and

,
we rewrite them as

and

Thus

Let us recall the Aharonov-Bohm operator in the momentum representation,
which is a time operator of
\frac{1}{2}q^{2}
.
Since

is an injective self-adjoint operator, the inverse operator
q^{-1}

exists as a self-adjoint operator.
In particular, the domain of

is dense.
Let

\displaystyle t_{0}=q^{-1}p

with the domain

\mathrm{D}(t_{0})=\{f\in\mathrm{D}(p)\mid pf\in\mathrm{D}(q^{-1})\}
.
The operator

is densely defined but not symmetric.
We also define

by

\displaystyle t_{1}=pq^{-1}

with the domain

\mathrm{D}(t_{1})=\{f\in\mathrm{D}(q)\mid qf\in\mathrm{D}(p^{-1})\}
.
As with

,

is also densely defined but not symmetric.
In the momentum representation,
the Aharonov-Bohm operator is given by

The operator
T_{\rm AB}
is a densely defined symmetric operator, and it follows that

\left[\frac{1}{2}q^{2},T_{\rm AB}\right]=-i{\mathchoice{\rm 1\mskip-4.0mul}{\rm 1\mskip-4.0mul}{\rm 1\mskip-4.5mul}{\rm 1\mskip-5.0mul}}

on a dense domain.

1.4
Heuristic derivation of angle operators

In the momentum representation,

\hat{h}_{\varepsilon}
is then transformed to

h_{\varepsilon}=\frac{1}{2}(\varepsilon p^{2}+q^{2}).

We shall construct an operator
T_{\varepsilon}
such that

[h_{\varepsilon},T_{\varepsilon}]=-i{\mathchoice{\rm 1\mskip-4.0mul}{\rm 1\mskip-4.0mul}{\rm 1\mskip-4.5mul}{\rm 1\mskip-5.0mul}}.

Heuristically we shall derive

.
We have

[h_{\varepsilon},t_{0}]=i({\mathchoice{\rm 1\mskip-4.0mul}{\rm 1\mskip-4.0mul}{\rm 1\mskip-4.5mul}{\rm 1\mskip-5.0mul}}+\varepsilon t_{0}^{2})

[h_{\varepsilon},f(t_{0})]=i({\mathchoice{\rm 1\mskip-4.0mul}{\rm 1\mskip-4.0mul}{\rm 1\mskip-4.5mul}{\rm 1\mskip-5.0mul}}+\varepsilon t_{0}^{2})f^{\prime}(t_{0})

From this we may expect that

Symmetrizing

\displaystyle T_{\varepsilon}=-\frac{1}{2\sqrt{\varepsilon}}(\arctan(\sqrt{\varepsilon}t_{0})+\arctan(\sqrt{\varepsilon}t_{1}))

.

Operator

appears in e.g.,
[
7

11

15

27

30

39
]
.
In a literature, however it is stated that

is bounded because of

for

.
It is however incorrect.
Since

is unbounded and not symmetric,
one can not apply the functional calculus for self-adjoint operators

In this paper we define them by the Taylor series:

Main results

2.1
Hierarchy of time operators

The following hierarchy of classes of time operators is introduced in
[
5
]
:

\{\mbox{Ultra-strong time}\}\subset\{\mbox{Strong time}\}\subset\{\mbox{Time}\}\subset\{\mbox{Weak time}\}\subset\{\mbox{Ultra-weak time}\}.

We explain this hierarchy below.
Let
A
and
B
be self-adjoint.
If the Weyl relation

\displaystyle e^{-isB}e^{-itA}=e^{its}e^{-itA}e^{-isB},\quad s,t\in\mathbb{R}

(2.1)

holds true,
then
[A,B]=-i{\mathchoice{\rm 1\mskip-4.0mul}{\rm 1\mskip-4.0mul}{\rm 1\mskip-4.5mul}{\rm 1\mskip-5.0mul}}
also holds true, which is deduced by taking derivatives
d/ds
and
d/dt
on both sides of (
2.1
). Then
B
is called
the ultra-strong time operator of
A
.
It is known that the position operator
q
and the momentum operator
p
satisfy the Weyl relation.
Furthermore by the von Neumann uniqueness theorem
[
41
]
,
if the Weyl relation (
2.1
) holds, and both
e^{-itA}
and
e^{-isB}
are irreducible, then

A
(resp.
B
) is unitarily equivalent to
p
(resp.
q
).

A pair
\{A,B\}
consisting of a self-adjoint operator
A
and a
symmetric operator
B

is called a weak Weyl representation
if

e^{-itA}\mathrm{D}(B)\subset\mathrm{D}(B)
for all
t\in\mathbb{R}
and

Be^{-itA}\psi=e^{-itA}(B+t)\psi,\quad t\in\mathbb{R}

(2.2)

holds true for all
\psi\in\mathrm{D}(B)
.
If
\{A,B\}
is a weak Weyl representation,
then
the canonical commutation relation
[A,B]=-i{\mathchoice{\rm 1\mskip-4.0mul}{\rm 1\mskip-4.0mul}{\rm 1\mskip-4.5mul}{\rm 1\mskip-5.0mul}}
also holds true.
Then
B
is called the strong time operator of
A
.
It is shown in
[
29
]

that
T_{\rm AB}
is a strong time operator of
\frac{1}{2}q^{2}
.
The crucial fact is that
if
\{A,B\}
is a weak Weyl representation, then
the spectrum of
A
is purely absolutely continuous.
In particular there are no eigenvalues of
A
.
We refer to see
[
29
,
2
,
4
,
31
]
for the comprehensive study
of weak Weyl representations.

One difficulty of the investigation of time operators
is to specify their domains.
Suppose that
[A,B]=-i{\mathchoice{\rm 1\mskip-4.0mul}{\rm 1\mskip-4.0mul}{\rm 1\mskip-4.5mul}{\rm 1\mskip-5.0mul}}
.
Let
Af=Ef
with
E\in\mathbb{R}
, i.e.,
f
is an eigenvector of
A
.
Then at least either
f\not\in\mathrm{D}(B)
or
Bf\not\in\mathrm{D}(A)
holds true.
To study time operators of self-adjoint operators possessing eigenvalues it is useful to introduce weak time operators and ultra-weak time operators to avoid the domain argument mentioned just above.

A symmetric operator
B
is called
the weak time operator of
a self-adjoint operator
A
if there exists a non-zero subspace
D\subset\mathrm{D}(B)\cap\mathrm{D}(A)
on which the weak canonical commutation relation holds:

(A\phi,B\psi)-(B\phi,A\psi)=-i(\phi,\psi),\quad\phi,\psi\in D.

(2.3)

We can furthermore generalize weak time operators, which is the
so-called ultra-weak time operator.
Ultra-weak time operators are the key tool in this paper.

Definition 2.1
(ultra-weak time operator
[
5
]
)

Let
H
be a self-adjoint operator on
\mathcal{H}
and

D_{1}
and
D_{2}
be non-zero subspaces of
\mathcal{H}
.
A sesqui-linear form

\mathcal{T}\colon D_{1}\times D_{2}\to\mathbb{C},\quad D_{1}\times D_{2}\ni(\phi,\psi)\mapsto\mathcal{T}[\phi,\psi]\in\mathbb{C}

with the domain
\mathrm{D}(\mathcal{T})=D_{1}\times D_{2}

is called an ultra-weak time operator of
H
if
there exist non-zero subspaces
D
and

E
of
D_{1}\cap D_{2}
such that
the following
(1)–(3)
hold:

(1)

E\subset\mathrm{D}(H)\cap D
.

(2)

\overline{\mathcal{T}[\phi,\psi]}=\mathcal{T}[\psi,\phi]
for all
\phi,\psi\in D
.

(3)

HE\subset D_{1}
and, for all
\psi,\phi\in E
,

\mathcal{T}[H\phi,\psi]-\overline{\mathcal{T}[H\psi,\phi]}=-i(\phi,\psi).

(2.4)

Here
\overline{z}
denotes the complex conjugate of
z
.
We call
E
the ultra-weak CCR-domain
and
D
the symmetric domain of
\mathcal{T}
.

2.2
New insights and main results

In this paper we are concerned with time operators of Hamiltonians with purely discrete spectrum.
We in particular focus on the one-dimensional harmonic oscillator.
As far as we know, there has been huge amount of investigations of
time operators of the one-dimensional harmonic oscillator,
there has been however few rigorous mathematical investigations.
Not even the domains of time operators have been established.
In addition, although the existence of several time operators of harmonic oscillators is known, we have never found any classification of them. Not only are there no rigorous results, but unfortunately there are also misleading results. No matter how much the algebraic relations of
p^{-1}q
and
qp^{-1}
are derived, we have the impression so that they are far from mathematics. It then appears to be an ad hoc study and
we aspire to a more systematic research. Then the new insights of this paper are to investigate (1)-(6) below:
(1) definition of
T_{\varepsilon}
,
(2) representation of
T_{\varepsilon}
,
(3) extension of
T_{\varepsilon}
,
(4) analytic continuation of representation of
T_{\varepsilon}
,
(5) limit of
T_{\varepsilon}
as
\varepsilon\to 0
,
(6) comparison of
T_{\varepsilon}
and
T_{G}
.

The problems discussed and solved in this paper are listed below:

1. Definition of
\bm{\arctan t_{\#}}
.

We give the firm mathematical definitions of both

\arctan t_{0}
and
\arctan t_{1}
,
and discuss the domains of
\arctan t_{0}
and
\arctan t_{1}
.
We also show that both are unbounded operators in Theorems
3.3
and
3.4
.

2. Representation of

.

Let

be given by
(
1.12
).
We introduce three subspaces:

\displaystyle\mathfrak{M}_{0,\varepsilon}=\textrm{LH}\left\{x^{2n}e^{-\alpha x^{2}/(2\sqrt{\varepsilon})}\ \Big|\ \alpha\in(0,1),n\in\mathbb{N}\right\},

(2.5)

\displaystyle\mathfrak{M}_{1,\varepsilon}=\textrm{LH}\left\{x^{2n+1}e^{-\alpha x^{2}/(2\sqrt{\varepsilon})}\ \Big|\ \alpha\in(0,1),n\in\mathbb{N}\right\},

(2.6)

\displaystyle\mathfrak{K}_{\varepsilon}=\textrm{LH}\left\{x^{n}e^{-x^{2}/(2\sqrt{\varepsilon})}\ \Big|\ n\in\mathbb{N}\right\}.

(2.7)

Here and in what follows

means the linear hull of
\{\ldots\}
and

.

(\alpha\in(0,1))

We describe the action

\displaystyle S_{0,\varepsilon}x^{2n}e^{-\alpha x^{2}/(2\sqrt{\varepsilon})}

\displaystyle=-\frac{i}{2\sqrt{\varepsilon}}\left(\left(x^{2}-2\sqrt{\varepsilon}\frac{d}{d\alpha}\right)^{n}\log\frac{1+\alpha}{1-\alpha}\right)e^{-\alpha x^{2}/(2\sqrt{\varepsilon})},

\displaystyle S_{1,\varepsilon}x^{2n+1}e^{-\alpha x^{2}/(2\sqrt{\varepsilon})}

\displaystyle=-\frac{i}{2\sqrt{\varepsilon}}\left(\left(x^{2}-2\sqrt{\varepsilon}\frac{d}{d\alpha}\right)^{n}\log\frac{1+\alpha}{1-\alpha}\right)xe^{-\alpha x^{2}/(2\sqrt{\varepsilon})}.

(\alpha=1)

We show in Theorem

and

Then the function

F_{0}
is analytic on
\mathbb{H}\setminus\{i\alpha\mid\alpha\in[1,\infty)\}
.
In particular

F_{0}
is the analytic continuation of
G_{0}
given by (
4.12
)
to
\mathbb{H}\setminus\{i\alpha\mid\alpha\in[1,\infty)\}
.

Proof: We see that

F_{0}
is differentiable on
z\in\mathbb{H}\setminus\{i\alpha\mid\alpha\in[1,\infty)\}
with

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dz}F_{0}(z)=\left(f,\frac{i}{2}\rho(D_{z})\frac{d}{dz}\log(-\mathcal{C})\cdot\xi_{z}\right)+\left(f,\frac{i}{2}\rho(D_{z})\log(-\mathcal{C})\cdot i\frac{x^{2}}{2}\xi_{z}\right).

Then
F
is analytic on
\mathbb{H}\setminus\{i\alpha\mid\alpha\in[1,\infty)\}

and the analytic continuation of the analytic function
G
on
D\cap\mathbb{H}
.
\Box

In a similar manner
we can see that
\rho(x^{2})x\xi_{z}\in\mathrm{D}(\tilde{S}_{1})
for
z\in\mathbb{D}\cap\mathbb{H}
,
and

\displaystyle G_{1}(z)=\left(f,\tilde{S}_{1}\rho(x^{2})x\xi_{z}\right)=\left(f,\frac{i}{2}\left(\rho(D_{z})\log(-\mathcal{C})\right)x\xi_{z}\right),\quad z\in\mathbb{D}\cap\mathbb{H}.

(4.13)

Lemma 4.9

Let
f\in{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}
. Define

F_{1}(z)=(f,\tilde{S}_{1}\rho(x^{2})x\xi_{z})
for
z\in\mathbb{H}\setminus\{i\}
.
Then

F_{1}
is analytic on
\mathbb{H}\setminus\{i\alpha\mid\alpha\in[1,\infty)\}
.
In particular

F_{1}
is the analytic continuation of
G_{1}
given by (
4.13
)
to
\mathbb{H}\setminus\{i\alpha\mid\alpha\in[1,\infty)\}
.

We define sesqui-linear forms

\tilde{\mathcal{T}}_{0}
and
\tilde{\mathcal{T}}_{1}
by

\displaystyle\tilde{\mathcal{T}}_{0}[\psi,\phi]=\frac{1}{2}\left\{\left(\tilde{S}_{0}\psi,\phi\right)+\left(\psi,\tilde{S}_{0}\phi\right)\right\},\quad\psi,\phi\in\mathfrak{N}_{0},

\displaystyle\tilde{\mathcal{T}}_{1}[\psi,\phi]=\frac{1}{2}\left\{\left(\tilde{S}_{1}\psi,\phi\right)+\left(\psi,\tilde{S}_{1}\phi\right)\right\},\quad\psi,\phi\in\mathfrak{N}_{1}.

The sesqui-linear form
\tilde{\mathcal{T}}
is defined by

\tilde{\mathcal{T}}=\tilde{\mathcal{T}}_{0}\oplus\tilde{\mathcal{T}}_{1}

with the domain
\mathfrak{N}\times\mathfrak{N}
.

Theorem 4.10

The sesqui-linear form
\tilde{\mathcal{T}}
is an ultra-weak time operator of
h

with the symmetric domain
\mathfrak{N}
and
the ultra-weak CCR-domain

\mathfrak{N}
.

Proof: The theorem follows from Lemma
4.7
.

\Box

Now we consider the matrix representation of
\tilde{\mathcal{T}}
.
We prove that
\mathcal{T}[f_{a},f_{b}]=\mathcal{T}[x^{a}\xi_{z},x^{b}\xi_{z}]
diverges at
z=i
in Theorem
3.10
.
The next theorem is however to guarantee the existence of
the analytic continuation of the map

z\mapsto\mathcal{T}[f_{a},f_{b}]
.

Theorem 4.11

Let
z\in\mathbb{H}\setminus\{i\}
,
a,b\in\mathbb{N}
,
f_{a}=x^{a}\xi_{z}
and
f_{b}=x^{b}\xi_{z}
.

(1)

The following relation holds:

\displaystyle\tilde{\mathcal{T}}[f_{a},f_{b}]=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\displaystyle-\frac{i}{4}\left(\xi_{z},\left\{\left(D_{z}^{n}x^{2m}-x^{2n}D_{z}^{m}\right)\log(-\mathcal{C})\right\}\xi_{z}\right)&a=2n\ \mbox{and}\ b=2m,\\
\displaystyle-\frac{i}{4}\left(\xi_{z},\left\{\left(D_{z}^{n}x^{2m+2}-x^{2n+2}D_{z}^{m}\right)\log(-\mathcal{C})\right\}\xi_{z}\right)&\displaystyle\!\!\!{{a=2n+1\mbox{ and}}\atop{\ b=2m+1},\phantom{and}}\\
\displaystyle 0&\text{otherwise}.\end{array}\right.

(2)

Suppose that
z=i\alpha
with
\alpha\in(0,1)
. Then

\tilde{\mathcal{T}}[f_{a},f_{b}]=\mathcal{T}[f_{a},f_{b}]
.

(3)

The map
z\mapsto\tilde{\mathcal{T}}[f_{a},f_{b}]
is analytic on

\mathbb{H}\setminus\{i\alpha\mid\alpha\in[1,\infty)\}
.
In particular,
\tilde{\mathcal{T}}[f_{a},f_{b}]
is the analytic continuation of
\mathcal{T}[f_{a},f_{b}]
.

Proof: (1) can be shown from the definition of
\tilde{\mathcal{T}}
.
(2) can be derived from
\tilde{S}_{\#}\rho(x^{2})\xi_{z}=S_{\#}\rho(x^{2})\xi_{z}
for
z\in i(0,1)
.
Finally (3) follows from Lemmas
4.8
and
4.9
.
\Box

5
Continuum limits

In this section we consider the continuum limit of

\mathcal{T}_{\varepsilon}
as
\varepsilon\to 0
.

T_{\varepsilon}
is formally written as

T_{\varepsilon}=-\frac{1}{2}\frac{1}{\sqrt{\varepsilon}}(\arctan\sqrt{\varepsilon}t_{0}+\arctan\sqrt{\varepsilon}t_{1})
.
Since

\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\frac{1}{2}\frac{1}{\sqrt{\varepsilon}}(\arctan\sqrt{\varepsilon}x+\arctan\sqrt{\varepsilon}y)=\frac{1}{2}(x+y)\quad(x,y\in\mathbb{R}),

it can be expected that

\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}T_{\varepsilon}=\frac{1}{2}(t_{0}+t_{1})
in some sense.
Since subspaces
\mathfrak{M}_{\varepsilon,\#}
depend on
\varepsilon
,
to consider the continuum limit of
\mathcal{T}_{\varepsilon}
we introduce

\mathfrak{M}_{\#}
by
\mathfrak{M}_{\#,\varepsilon}
with
\varepsilon
replaced by
1
:

\displaystyle\mathfrak{M}_{0}

\displaystyle=\textrm{LH}\left\{x^{2n}e^{-\alpha x^{2}/2}\ \Big|\ n\in\mathbb{N},\ \alpha\in(0,1)\right\},

\displaystyle\mathfrak{M}_{1}

\displaystyle=\textrm{LH}\left\{x^{2n+1}e^{-\alpha x^{2}/2}\ \Big|\ n\in\mathbb{N},\ \alpha\in(0,1)\right\}.

Let
\mathfrak{M}=\mathfrak{M}_{0}\oplus\mathfrak{M}_{1}
and
\mathfrak{D}=\mathrm{D}(S)\oplus\mathrm{D}(S_{1})
.
Then the sesqui-linear form
\mathcal{T}_{\varepsilon}
is also an ultra-weak time operator with
the symmetric domain
\mathfrak{D}
and
the ultra-weak CCR domain

\mathfrak{M}
. We also extend the Aharonov-Bohm operator
T_{\rm AB}=\frac{1}{2}(t_{0}+t_{1})

to an ultra-weak time operator.
Let

\displaystyle\mathcal{T}_{0,\mathrm{AB}}[\psi,\phi]

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{2}\left\{(\psi,t_{0}\phi)+(t_{0}\psi,\phi)\right\},\quad\psi,\phi\in\mathfrak{M}_{0},

\displaystyle\mathcal{T}_{1,\mathrm{AB}}[\psi,\phi]

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{2}\left\{(\psi,t_{1}\phi)+(t_{1}\psi,\phi)\right\},\quad\psi,\phi\in\mathfrak{M}_{1}.

Define

\mathcal{T}_{\mathrm{AB}}
by

\mathcal{T}_{\mathrm{AB}}=\mathcal{T}_{0,\mathrm{AB}}\oplus\mathcal{T}_{1,\mathrm{AB}}.

The sesqui-linear form
\mathcal{T}_{\mathrm{AB}}
is an ultra-weak time operator
of

\frac{1}{2}q^{2}
with
the symmetric domain
\mathfrak{M}
and
the ultra-weak CCR-domain
\mathfrak{M}
.

Theorem 5.1

For any
\phi,\psi\in\mathfrak{M}
,

\displaystyle\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\mathcal{T}_{\varepsilon}[\psi,\phi]=\mathcal{T}_{\mathrm{AB}}[\psi,\phi].

Proof: Let
c_{1},c_{2},d_{1},d_{2}\in\mathbb{C}
,

f_{1}=x^{2k}e^{-\alpha x^{2}/2}
,

f_{2}=x^{2l}e^{-\beta x^{2}/2}
,

g_{1}=x^{2m+1}e^{-\gamma x^{2}/2}
and

g_{2}=x^{2n+1}e^{-\delta x^{2}/2}
.
Set

\phi=c_{1}f_{1}+d_{1}g_{1}
and
\psi=c_{2}f_{2}+d_{2}g_{2}
.
It is sufficient to show that
\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\mathcal{T}_{\varepsilon}[\phi,\psi]=\mathcal{T}_{\mathrm{AB}}[\phi,\psi]
.
From Theorem
4.4
it can be seen that

\mathcal{T}_{0,\varepsilon}[f_{1},f_{2}]=\frac{i}{4\sqrt{\varepsilon}}\left(\xi_{i\alpha,1},\left\{x^{2l}D_{i\alpha,\,1}^{k}\log\frac{1+\sqrt{\varepsilon}\alpha}{1-\sqrt{\varepsilon}\alpha}-x^{2k}D_{i\beta,\,1}^{l}\log\frac{1+\sqrt{\varepsilon}\beta}{1-\sqrt{\varepsilon}\beta}\right\}\xi_{\beta i,1}\right).

Expanding
D_{i\alpha,\,1}^{k}\log\frac{1+\sqrt{\varepsilon}\alpha}{1-\sqrt{\varepsilon}\alpha}
on
x
, we have

\displaystyle x^{2l}D_{i\alpha,\,1}^{k}\log\frac{1+\sqrt{\varepsilon}\alpha}{1-\sqrt{\varepsilon}\alpha}

\displaystyle=x^{2(l+k)}\log\frac{1+\sqrt{\varepsilon}\alpha}{1-\sqrt{\varepsilon}\alpha}-k\sqrt{\varepsilon}x^{2(l+k-1)}\frac{4}{1-\varepsilon\alpha^{2}}+R_{k,l,\alpha,\varepsilon}(x),

where
R_{k,l,\alpha,\varepsilon}(x)
is a real valued function on
\mathbb{R}
such that

\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\frac{1}{\sqrt{\varepsilon}}R_{k,l,\alpha,\varepsilon}(x)=0

for all
x\in\mathbb{R}
.
Since
\frac{1}{\sqrt{\varepsilon}}\log\frac{1+\sqrt{\varepsilon}X}{1-\sqrt{\varepsilon}X}\to 2X
and
(1-\varepsilon X^{2})^{-1}\to 1
as
\varepsilon\to 0
,
we see that

\displaystyle\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\mathcal{T}_{0,\varepsilon}[f_{1},f_{2}]

\displaystyle=\frac{i}{2}\left(\xi_{i\alpha,1},x^{2(l+k-1)}\{\alpha x^{2}-2k-(\beta x^{2}-2l)\}\xi_{\beta i,1}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{i}{2}\left(\left((\alpha x^{2}-2k)x^{2(k-1)}\xi_{i\alpha,1},x^{2l}\xi_{\beta i,1}\right)-\left(x^{2k}\xi_{i\alpha,1},(\beta x^{2}-2l)x^{2(l-1)}\xi_{\beta i,1}\right)\right)

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{2}\bigl((tf_{1},f_{2})+(f_{1},tf_{2})\bigr)

\displaystyle=\mathcal{T}_{0,\mathrm{AB}}[f_{1},f_{2}].

In the same way as above, we obtain
\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\mathcal{T}_{1,\varepsilon}[g_{1},g_{2}]=\mathcal{T}_{1,\mathrm{AB}}[g_{1},g_{2}]
.
Hence, for any
\psi,\phi\in\mathfrak{M}
, we conclude that

\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\mathcal{T}_{\varepsilon}[\phi,\psi]=\mathcal{T}_{\mathrm{AB}}[\phi,\psi]
.

\Box

6
Time operators by POVM

Let
\mathcal{H}
be a Hilbert space and
(\Omega,\mathcal{B}_{\Omega})
a measurable space.
An operator-valued set function
P
on
\mathcal{B}_{\Omega}
is a POVM if and only if
for every
A\in\mathcal{B}_{\Omega}
,
P(A)
is a bounded non-negative self-adjoint operator, and

\mathcal{B}_{\Omega}\ni A\mapsto\frac{(f,P(A)f)}{\|f\|^{2}}\in[0,1]

is a probability measure for any non-zero
f\in\mathcal{H}
.
Note that
P
is not necessarily projection-valued.

In this section we compare
T=T_{\varepsilon=1}
to the time operator

T_{G}
derived through the POVM associated with
h=\frac{1}{2}(p^{2}+q^{2})
.
The normalized eigenvector of
h
associated with the eigenvalue

n+\frac{1}{2}
is denoted by
e_{n}
.
Fot
t\in\mathbb{R}
and
N\in\mathbb{N}
we define

\varphi_{N}(t)=\sum_{n=0}^{N}e^{-ith}e_{n}

and we set

P_{N}(t)=\frac{1}{2\pi}(\varphi_{N}(t),\cdot)\varphi_{N}(t).

Let
\mathcal{B}
be the Borel
\sigma
-field on
[0,2\pi]
.
The operator-valued set function
P_{N}
on

\mathcal{B}
is defined by

\mathcal{B}\ni A\mapsto(f,P_{N}(A)g)=\int_{[0,2\pi]}{\mathchoice{\rm 1\mskip-4.0mul}{\rm 1\mskip-4.0mul}{\rm 1\mskip-4.5mul}{\rm 1\mskip-5.0mul}}_{A}(t)(f,P_{N}(t)g)dt.

Let
A,A^{\prime}\in\mathcal{B}
and
A\subset A^{\prime}
. Then

0\leq(f,P_{N}(A)f)\leq(f,P_{N}(A^{\prime})f)
.

Lemma 6.1

For any
A\in\mathcal{B}
and any
f,g\in\mathcal{H}
,

\lim_{N\to\infty}(f,P_{N}(A)g)
exists.

Proof: It is immediate to see that

\displaystyle(f,P_{N}(A)g)=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{A}\sum_{n=0}^{N}(f,e_{n})(e_{n},g)dt+\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{A}\sum_{n\neq m}^{N}e^{-it(n-m)}(f,e_{n})(e_{m},g)dt.

(6.1)

Suppose that
f=g
and

A=[0,2\pi]
. Then

(f,P_{N}([0,2\pi])f)=(2\pi)^{-1}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\sum_{n=0}^{N}|(f,e_{n})|^{2}dt\leq\|f\|^{2}
.
Using the polarization identity we can also see that for any
A\in\mathcal{B}
,

\{(f,P_{N}(A)g)\}_{N}
is a Cauchy sequence in
\mathbb{C}
. Then the lemma follows.

\Box

For each
A\in\mathcal{B}
, by the Riesz representation theorem there
exists a unique bounded non-negative self-adjoint operator
P(A)
such that

\displaystyle(f,P(A)g)=\lim_{N\to\infty}(f,P_{N}(A)g).

(6.2)

Since
(f,P([0,2\pi])f)=\|f\|^{2}
,

P(\cdot)
is a POVM on
([0,2\pi],\mathcal{B})
.
Now the sesqui-linear form
\mathcal{T}_{G}[f,g]
is defined by

\displaystyle\mathcal{T}_{G}[f,g]=\lim_{N\to\infty}\int_{[0,2\pi]}t\bigl(f,P_{N}(t)g\bigr)dt

(6.3)

with the domain
{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}\times{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}
.

Lemma 6.2

The sesqui-linear form
\mathcal{T}_{G}
is bounded;

|\mathcal{T}_{G}[f,g]|\leq 2\pi\|f\|\|g\|
for
f,g\in{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}
.

Proof: By a similar computation to (
6.1
),
we obtain that

\mathcal{T}_{G}[f,g]=\pi(f,g)+i\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\left(\sum_{m\neq n}\frac{(e_{m},g)}{n-m}(f,e_{n})\right)

and

|\mathcal{T}_{G}[f,g]|\leq(2\pi)\|f\|\|g\|
.
Here we used the inequality
[
24
, Theorem 294]
:

\left|\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\left(\sum_{m\neq n}\frac{x_{n}x_{m}}{n-m}\right)\right|\leq\pi\left(\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}x_{n}^{2}\right)^{1/2}\left(\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}y_{n}^{2}\right)^{1/2}.

Then the lemma is proven.

\Box

The Riesz representation theorem yields again that
there exists a unique bounded self-adjoint operator
T_{G}

such that

\|T_{G}\|\leq 2\pi
and

\displaystyle\mathcal{T}_{G}[f,g]=(f,T_{G}g).

(6.4)

Formally writing
P(A)=\int_{A}dP
, we can express
T_{G}
as

\displaystyle T_{G}=\int_{[0,2\pi]}tdP.

(6.5)

We shall show that
T_{G}
is a time operator of
h
.

Lemma 6.3

Let
f,g\in\mathrm{D}(h)
. Then
we have

\left(f,\bigl[h,P_{N}(t)\bigr]g\right)=-\frac{1}{i}\frac{d}{dt}\left(f,P_{N}(t)g\right).

Proof: Since

he^{-ith}e_{n}=-\frac{1}{i}\frac{d}{dt}e^{-ith}e_{n}
in the strong sense,
we have

\displaystyle(f,[h,P_{N}(t)]g)

\displaystyle=\frac{-1}{2\pi}\sum_{1\leq n,m\leq N}\left\{\frac{1}{i}\frac{d}{dt}(f,e^{-ith}e_{m})\cdot(e^{-ith}e_{n},g)+(f,e^{-ith}e_{m})\cdot\frac{1}{i}\frac{d}{dt}(e^{-ith}e_{n},g)\right\}

\displaystyle=\frac{-1}{i}\frac{d}{dt}\left(f,P_{N}(t)g\right).

Then the lemma follows.

\Box

We define the unbounded operator
P_{0}
by
the strong limit:

P_{0}=\text{s-}\!\!\!\!\lim_{N\to\infty}P_{N}(0)=\text{s-}\!\!\!\!\lim_{N\to\infty}\frac{1}{2\pi}\left(\sum_{m=0}^{N}e_{m},\cdot\right)\sum_{n=0}^{N}e_{n}.

Lemma 6.4

We have

[h,T_{G}]=-i({\mathchoice{\rm 1\mskip-4.0mul}{\rm 1\mskip-4.0mul}{\rm 1\mskip-4.5mul}{\rm 1\mskip-5.0mul}}-2\pi P_{0})
on
\mathrm{D}(h)
.

Proof: Let
f,g\in\mathrm{D}(h)
.
By Lemma
6.3
we have

\displaystyle(f,[h,T_{G}]g)

\displaystyle=\lim_{N\to\infty}\int_{[0,2\pi]}t\left(f,\bigl[h,P_{N}(t)\bigr]g\right)dt=-\lim_{N\to\infty}\int_{[0,2\pi]}t\frac{1}{i}\frac{d}{dt}\left(f,P_{N}(t)g\right)dt

\displaystyle=-\lim_{N\to\infty}\left\{\frac{1}{i}\left[t(f,P_{N}(t)g)\right]_{0}^{2\pi}-\int_{[0,2\pi]}\frac{1}{i}\left(f,P_{N}(t)g\right)dt\right\}

\displaystyle=i\lim_{N\to\infty}\left(f,2\pi P_{N}(0)g\right)-i(f,g).

Then the lemma follows.

\Box

Lemma 6.5

The operator
T_{G}
is a bounded self-adjoint time operator of
h
, i.e.,
[h,T_{G}]=-i{\mathchoice{\rm 1\mskip-4.0mul}{\rm 1\mskip-4.0mul}{\rm 1\mskip-4.5mul}{\rm 1\mskip-5.0mul}}
on
\textrm{LH}\{e_{n}-e_{m}\mid n\neq m\}
.

Proof: Since
P_{0}(e_{n}-e_{m})=0
, the lemma follows from Lemma
6.4
.

\Box

Remark 6.6

By the definition of
T_{G}

we can see that

T_{G}=i\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\left(\sum_{m\neq n}\frac{(e_{m},\cdot)}{n-m}e_{n}\right)+\pi{\mathchoice{\rm 1\mskip-4.0mul}{\rm 1\mskip-4.0mul}{\rm 1\mskip-4.5mul}{\rm 1\mskip-5.0mul}}.

This can be extended to a general self-adjoint operator
H

possessing purely discrete spectrum:
{\rm Spec}(H)=\{E_{n}\}_{n=0}^{\infty}
and
Hf_{n}=E_{n}f_{n}
.
The time operators of
H
of the form

T_{H}=i\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\left(\sum_{m\neq n}\frac{(f_{m},\cdot)}{E_{n}-E_{m}}f_{n}\right)

are studied in
e.g.,
[
6
,
19
,
18
]
.

We can also define the sesqui-linear form associated with
T_{G}
by

\mathcal{T}_{G}[\phi,\psi]=\frac{1}{2}\{(\phi,T_{G}\psi)+(T_{G}\phi,\psi)\}.

The sesqui-linear form
\mathcal{T}_{G}
is also an ultra-weak time operator of
h

with the symmetric domain
{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}\times{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}
and the ultra-weak CCR domain
{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}\times{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}
.
Note that
\mathcal{T}_{G}
is a bounded sesqui-linear form.

Theorem 6.7

The sesqui-linear form
\mathcal{T}
is not equal to
\mathcal{T}_{G}
.

Proof: The sesqui-linear form
\mathcal{T}_{G}
is bounded, but
\mathcal{T}
is unbounded. Then
the theorem follows.
\Box

7
Conclusion

In this paper we discuss two time operators.
One is the angle operator of the form

T_{\varepsilon}=-\frac{1}{2}\frac{1}{\sqrt{\varepsilon}}(\arctan(\sqrt{\varepsilon}q^{-1}p)+\arctan(\sqrt{\varepsilon}pq^{-1}))

and the other is the operator of the form

T_{G}=i\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\left(\sum_{m\neq n}\frac{(e_{m},\cdot)}{n-m}e_{n}\right)+\pi{\mathchoice{\rm 1\mskip-4.0mul}{\rm 1\mskip-4.0mul}{\rm 1\mskip-4.5mul}{\rm 1\mskip-5.0mul}}.

We give the firm definition of
T_{\varepsilon}
as a sesqui-linear form
and its matrix representation.
Furthermore the analytic continuation of the matrix representation
is obtained.
We also show that
T_{\varepsilon}
converges to
the Aharonov-Bohm operator
T_{\rm AB}
as
\varepsilon\to 0
in the sense of sesqui-linear forms.
Finally it is shown that
T_{\varepsilon=1}\neq T_{G}
in the sense of sesqui-linear forms.
As far as we know these are new.

We introduced the phase operator
\hat{\phi}
in
(
1.7
) as a time operator of the number operator
N={a^{\ast}}a
.

N
and
\hat{h}=\frac{1}{2}(p^{2}+q^{2})
are simply
related as
N+\frac{1}{2}{\mathchoice{\rm 1\mskip-4.0mul}{\rm 1\mskip-4.0mul}{\rm 1\mskip-4.5mul}{\rm 1\mskip-5.0mul}}=\hat{h}
.
The rigorous definitions of both
\log a
and
\log{a^{\ast}}
are however not established.
In particular it is not obvious to define
\log{a^{\ast}}
as a densely defined operator in
{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}
.
Let
\hat{T}
be (
1.4
).
One can expect however in e.g.,
[
39
]
that

\hat{\phi}=\hat{T}+G(\hat{h})
with some function
G
.
Needless to say it is far away from mathematics.
We shall discuss this in the second paper
[
26
]
.
Furthermore
we shall also show in
[
26
]
the existence of the interpolation
connecting the angle operator
T_{\varepsilon=1}
with
T_{G}
.

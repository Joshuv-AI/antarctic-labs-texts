Stefano Ciaci

Abstract
. A Banach space is locally almost square if, for every y y in its unit sphere, there exists a sequence ( x n ) (x_{n}) in its unit sphere such that lim ‖ y ± x n ‖ = 1 \lim\|y\pm x_{n}\|=1 . A Banach space is weakly almost square if, in addition, we require the sequence ( x n ) (x_{n}) to be weakly null. It is known that these two properties are distinct, so we aim to investigate if local almost squareness implies a weaker version of the latter property by replacing the sequence with a net. In order to achieve this result, we restrict ourselves to Banach lattices and introduce a strengthening of local almost squareness by requiring that the sequence is in the positive cone of the lattice. As an application of such characterization, we prove that this positive variant of local almost squareness implies that every relatively weakly open set in the unit ball has diameter 2, that is, the Banach space has the so called diameter two property . This in particular allows us also to generate new examples of Banach spaces enjoying the diameter two property.

1.
Introduction

Let
X
be a Banach space.

(1)

X
is
locally almost square
(
\LASQ
) if, for every
y\in S_{X}
and
\varepsilon>0
, there exists
x\in S_{X}
satisfying
\|y\pm x\|\leq 1+\varepsilon
.

(2)

X
is
weakly almost square
(
\WASQ
) if, for every
y\in S_{X}
, there exists a weakly null sequence
(x_{n})
in
X
satisfying
\lim\|y\pm x_{n}\|=1
and
\lim\|x_{n}\|=1
.

(3)

X
is
almost square
(
\ASQ
) if, for every
y_{1},\ldots,y_{n}\in S_{X}
and
\varepsilon>0
, there exists
x\in S_{X}
satisfying
\|y_{i}\pm x\|\leq 1+\varepsilon
for all
1\leq i\leq n
.

These properties were defined in
[
1
]
. In the same paper the authors also proved that being
\ASQ
implies being
\WASQ
, on the other hand the fact that being
\WASQ
implies being
\LASQ
is trivial. It is known that
L_{1}[0,1]
is
\WASQ
but it is not
\ASQ
, but the fact that being
\LASQ
is not equivalent to being
\WASQ
was just proved in the recent preprint
[
8
]
. This last result is the starting point of our investigation.

In this paper we aim to prove that being
\LASQ
implies possessing a property which is very close to being
\WASQ
, namely by substituting the sequence with a net. In order to do so, we have to restrict ourselves to the class of Banach lattices and we introduce a slight strengthening of being
\LASQ
in which we additionally require the “M-orthogonal element” to be in the positive cone of the lattice. We call this property
\positiveLASQ
(see Definition
2.1
). Before explaining more in detail the content of the paper let us recall that a slice of the unit ball
B_{X}
is the intersection of
B_{X}
with some half-space and let us remind of a few more properties that a Banach space
X
can enjoy.

(1)

X
has the
local diameter two property
(
\LDtwoP
) if every slice of
B_{X}
has diameter 2.

(2)

X
has the
diameter two property
(
\DtwoP
) if every non-empty relatively weakly open set in
B_{X}
has diameter 2.

(3)

X
has the
strong diameter two property
(
\SDtwoP
) if every convex combination of slices in
B_{X}
has diameter 2.

These properties were introduced in
[
2
]
and it is known that if a Banach space is
\LASQ
, then it has the
\LDtwoP

[
9
, Proposition 2.5]
, if it is
\WASQ
, then it has the
\DtwoP

[
9
, Proposition 2.6]
and if it is
\ASQ
, then it has the
\SDtwoP

[
1
, Proposition 2.5]
.

2.
Main result

Definition 2.1
.

We say that a Banach lattice
X
is
\positiveLASQ
if, for every
y\in S_{X}
and
\varepsilon>0
, there exists
x\in S^{+}_{X}
satisfying
\|y\pm x\|\leq 1+\varepsilon
.

Notice that being
\positiveLASQ
trivially implies being
\LASQ
. However,
L_{1}[0,1]
is
\LASQ
but it cannot be
\positiveLASQ
, since it is clear that no AL-space can be
\positiveLASQ
.

Remark 2.2
.

In Definition
2.1
we can require only that
\|y+x\|\leq 1+\varepsilon
and
y\in S^{+}_{X}
. This is equivalent because, for every
y\in S_{X}
and
\varepsilon>0
, there exists
x\in S^{+}_{X}
satisfying
\||y|+x\|\leq 1+\varepsilon
, and, since
|y\pm x|\leq|y|+x
, then

\|y\pm x\|\leq\||y|+x\|\leq 1+\varepsilon.

The following proposition gives us a class of Banach lattices which are
\positiveLASQ
.

Proposition 2.3
.

Let
X
be an AM-space. If
X
is
\LASQ
, then
X
is
\positiveLASQ
.

Proof.

Let
y\in S_{X}
and
\varepsilon>0
. Since
X
is
\LASQ
, there exists
x\in S_{X}
satisfying
\|y\pm x\|\leq 1+\varepsilon
. Notice that

|y+|x||=|y+(x\vee-x)|=|(y+x)\vee(y-x)|\leq|y+x|\vee|y-x|,

therefore

\|y+|x|\|\leq\||y+x|\vee|y-x|\|=\max\{\|y+x\|,\|y-x\|\}\leq 1+\varepsilon.

∎

Let us now state and prove the main result of the paper.

Theorem 2.4
.

Let
X
be a Banach lattice. If
X
is
\positiveLASQ
, then, for every
y\in S_{X}
, there exist a weakly null net
(x_{\alpha})
in
X
satisfying

\lim\|x_{\alpha}\|=1\text{ and }\lim\|y\pm x_{\alpha}\|=1.

Proof.

Fix
y\in S_{X}
and let us temporarily fix some
m\in\mathbb{N}
. Find a sequence
(\varepsilon_{n}^{m})
of strictly positive reals such that
\prod(1+\varepsilon_{n}^{m})\leq 1+m^{-1}
. We now construct a sequence
(y_{n}^{m})\subset S^{+}_{X}
by recursion over
n\in\mathbb{N}
.

At first define
y_{0}^{m}:=|y|
. Now assume that we are given
y_{0}^{m},\ldots,y_{n}^{m}
and set

\hat{y}_{n}^{m}:=\frac{\sum_{i=0}^{n}y_{i}^{m}}{\left\|\sum_{i=0}^{n}y_{i}^{m}\right\|}\in S^{+}_{X}

and, since
X
is
\positiveLASQ
, we can find
y_{n+1}^{m}\in S^{+}_{X}
satisfying
\|\hat{y}_{n}^{m}\pm y_{n+1}^{m}\|\leq 1+\varepsilon_{n}^{m}
. This concludes the construction of the sequence
(y_{n}^{m})
.

Since
B_{X^{**}}
is weak
∗
compact, then there exists a subnet
\{y_{\alpha}^{m}:\alpha\in\mathscr{A}_{m}\}
of
(y_{n}^{m})
which is weak
∗
convergent to some point
y_{m}^{**}\in X^{**}
. Define
x_{\alpha,\beta}^{m}:=y^{m}_{\alpha}-y_{\beta}^{m}
, where
\alpha,\beta\in\mathscr{A}_{m}
and notice that

\displaystyle 1

\displaystyle=\|y_{0}^{m}\|\leq\left\|\sum_{i=0}^{n+1}y_{i}^{m}\right\|=\left\|\left\|\sum_{i=0}^{n}y_{i}^{m}\right\|\cdot\hat{y}_{n}^{m}+y_{n+1}^{m}\right\|\leq(1+\varepsilon_{n}^{m})\left\|\sum_{i=0}^{n}y_{i}^{m}\right\|\leq\ldots

\displaystyle\leq\prod_{i=1}^{n}(1+\varepsilon_{i}^{m})\leq 1+m^{-1}.

holds for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
. Moreover,

\|y\pm x_{\alpha,\beta}^{m}\|=\|y\pm(y_{n_{\alpha}}^{m}-y_{n_{\beta}}^{m})\|\leq\left\|\sum_{i=0}^{\max\{n_{\alpha},n_{\beta}\}}y_{i}^{m}\right\|\leq 1+m^{-1}

and

\|x_{\alpha,\beta}^{m}\|=\|y_{n_{\alpha}}^{m}-y_{n_{\beta}}^{m}\|\leq\left\|\sum_{i=0}^{\max\{n_{\alpha},n_{\beta}\}}y_{i}^{m}\right\|\leq 1+m^{-1}

hold for all
\alpha,\beta\in\mathscr{A}_{m}
satisfying
n_{\alpha}\not=n_{\beta}
. Moreover, it is clear that
\|y^{m}_{\alpha}\pm y^{m}_{\beta}\|\leq 1+m^{-1}
holds too, therefore

2=\|2y_{\alpha}^{m}\|\leq\|y_{\alpha}^{m}+y_{\beta}^{m}\|+\|y_{\alpha}^{m}-y_{\beta}^{m}\|\leq\|y_{\alpha}^{m}+y_{\beta}^{m}\|+1+m^{-1}.

From this, and an analogous argument, we conclude that
\|y^{m}_{\alpha}\pm y^{m}_{\beta}\|\geq 1-m^{-1}
. Similarly one can prove that
\|y\pm x^{m}_{\alpha,\beta}\|\geq 1-m^{-1}
too. To sum up, so far we proved that

(2.1)

(\forall\alpha,\beta\in\mathscr{A}:n_{\alpha}\not=n_{\beta})\hskip 2.84544pt|\|y\pm x^{m}_{\alpha,\beta}\|-1|\leq m^{-1}

and

(2.2)

(\forall\alpha,\beta\in\mathscr{A}:n_{\alpha}\not=n_{\beta})\hskip 2.84544pt|\|x_{\alpha,\beta}^{m}\|-1|\leq m^{-1}.

Now we proceed to construct the desired net. For this purpose, let us call
\mathscr{U}
the neighborhood filter around
0
in
X
endowed with the weak topology and let us choose, for every
(m,U)\in\mathbb{N}\times\mathscr{U}
, some

, where

for some
\alpha,\beta\in\mathscr{A}
satisfying

. Notice that such

and

can be found because, for all

,

, where the net

is weak

Notice that the assumption of

Question 2.5
.

Let
X
be a Banach space. If is
\LASQ
, then does it imply that, for every

, there exists a net

in

The following criterion is obtained through a few modifications in the proof of Theorem

.

Theorem 2.6
.

Let
X
be a Banach lattice such that
X^{*}
is separable. If
X
is
\positiveLASQ
, then
X
is
\WASQ
.

Proof.

Since
X^{*}
is separable, then
B_{X^{**}}
, with respect to the weak
∗
topology, is metrizable. Hence, in the proof of Theorem

, we can replace the subnet

with a subsequence

and simply define

which is clearly weakly null. Let now
(x^{*}_{n})
be a dense sequence in

. For every

there exists

satisfying
|x^{*}_{i}(x^{m}_{n})|\leq m^{-1}
for all

is weakly null and, thanks to (

) and (

\lim\|x^{m}_{n_{m}}\|=1\text{ and }\lim\|y\pm x^{m}_{n_{m}}\|=1.

∎

3.
The diameter two property

It is known that if a Banach space is
\LASQ
, then it has the
\LDtwoP
, and, if a Banach space is
\WASQ
, then it has the
\DtwoP
. Thanks to Theorem
2.4
we can extend these results.

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
X
be a Banach lattice. If
X
is
\positiveLASQ
, then
X
has the
\DtwoP
.

Proof.

Let
U
be a non-empty relatively weakly open set in
B_{X}
and
\varepsilon>0
. Notice that

U_{\delta}:=\bigcap_{i=1}^{n}\{x\in B_{X}:x^{*}_{i}(x)\geq 1-\delta\}\subset U

for some
0<\delta\leq\varepsilon
and
x^{*}_{1},\ldots,x^{*}_{n}\in S_{X^{*}}
. Since
X
is
\LASQ
, then it is infinite dimensional, hence we can find
y\in U_{\delta/2}\cap S_{X}
and construct the net
(x_{\alpha})
as in Theorem
2.4
with respect to
y
. It is now clear that we can find
\alpha
satisfying
|x^{*}_{i}(x_{\alpha})|\leq\delta/2
for all
1\leq i\leq n
and

1-\delta/2\leq\|x_{\alpha}\|,\|y\pm x_{\alpha}\|\leq 1+\delta/2.

Therefore
\|y+x_{\alpha}-(y-x_{\alpha})\|=2\|x_{\alpha}\|\geq 2-\delta\geq 2-\varepsilon
and
x^{*}_{i}(y\pm x_{\alpha})\geq 1-\delta/2-\delta/2\geq 1-\varepsilon
holds for all
1\leq i\leq n
, hence, up to a perturbation argument,
y\pm x_{\alpha}\in U
.
∎

3.1
doesn’t hold. In fact

Growth of Solutions of Complex Differential Equations in a Sector of
the Unit Disc

Abstract




Abstract.
In this paper, we deal with the growth of
solutions of homogeneous linear complex differential equation by using the
concept of lower [
p,q
]-order and lower [
p,q
]-type in a
sector of the unit disc instead of the whole unit disc, and we obtain
similar results as in the case of the unit disc.

AMS (2010)
: 34M10, 30D35.

Key words
: Complex differential equation, analytic function, [
p,q
]-order, lower [
p,q
]-order, lower [
p,q
]-type,
sector.

1
Definitions and introduction

Throughout this paper, we shall assume that readers are familiar
with the fundamental results and the standard notations of Nevanlinna’s
theory in the complex plane and in the unit disc
\Delta=\left\{z\in\mathbb{C}:\left|z\right|<1\right\},
see
\left[5,6,7,9,15,23\right]
.

Consider for
k\geq 2
the complex differential equation

f^{(k)}(z)+A_{k-1}(z)f^{(k-1)}+\cdots+A_{0}(z)f=0,

(1.1)

where coefficients
A_{j}
(
j=0,1,\dots,k-1
) are analytic functions in
the unit disc
\Delta.
It is well-known that every solution of
\left(1.1\right)
is analytic in
\Delta
, and there are exactly
k
linearly
independent solutions of equation
\left(1.1\right)
(see e.g.
\left[7\right]
). The theory of complex differential equations in the unit disc
has been developed since 1980’s, see
\left[13\right]
. In the year 2000,
Heittokangas in
\left[7\right]
firstly investigated the growth and
oscillation theory of equation
\left(1.1\right)
when the coefficients
A_{j}
(
j=0,1,\dots,k-1
) are analytic functions in the unit disc
\Delta

by introducing the definition of the function spaces. His results also gave
some important tools for further investigations on the theory of meromorphic
solutions of equations
\left(1.1\right)
. In 1994, Wu
\left[17,18\right]
used the Nevanlinna theory in an angle to study the order of growth of
solutions of the second-order linear differential equation in an angular
region. Later Xu and Yi
\left[22\right]
, Wu
\left[19\right],
Wu and
Li
\left[20\right],
Zhang
\left[24\right]
generalized some results
of
\left[17,18\right]
to the case of linear higher order differential
equations in angular domains by using the concepts of iterated
p-
order and
the spread relation. Recently, Wu in
\left[21\right]
developed a new
investigation related to linear differential equations with analytic
coefficients in a sector of the unit disc

\Omega_{\alpha,\beta}=\left\{z\in\mathbb{C}:\alpha<\arg z<\beta,\text{
}\left|z\right|<1\right\},

and obtained some results about the order of growth of solutions of the
differential equation

A_{k}(z)f^{(k)}(z)+A_{k-1}(z)f^{(k-1)}+\cdots+A_{0}(z)f=0,

(1.2)

where coefficients
A_{j}
(
j=0,1,\dots,k
) are analytic functions in the
sector
\Omega_{\alpha,\beta}.
After that, Long in
\left[11,12\right],
Zemirni and Belaidi in
\left[25\right]
obtained different results
concerning the growth of solutions of
\left(1.1\right)
and
\left(1.2\right)
by using the concepts of iterated
p
-order and [
p,q
]-order in the sector
\Omega_{\alpha,\beta}
. In this paper, we continue
to investigate this new problem and study the growth of solutions of
equation
\left(1.1\right)
when the coefficients
A_{j}
(
j=0,1,\dots,k-1
) are analytic functions of [
p,q
]-order in the sector
\Omega_{\alpha,\beta}.
Before stating our main results, we give some notations
and basic definitions of meromorphic functions in the unit disc
\Delta

and in a sector
\Omega_{\alpha,\beta}
of the unit disc. The order of a
meromorphic function
f
in
\Delta
is defined by

\rho\left(f\right)=\underset{r\rightarrow 1^{-}}{\lim\sup}\frac{\log T\left(r,f\right)}{\log\frac{1}{1-r}},

where
T(r,f)
is the Nevanlinna characteristic function of
f
. If
f
is
analytic function in
\Delta,
then

\rho_{M}\left(f\right)=\underset{r\rightarrow 1^{-}}{\lim\sup}\frac{\log\log M\left(r,f\right)}{\log\frac{1}{1-r}},

where
M(r,f)=\max\limits_{\underset{{\large z\in\Delta}}{|z|=r}}\left|f(z)\right|
is the maximum modulus function.

Remark 1.1
The following two statements hold
[15,
p.
205].

\left(\text{a}\right)
If
f
is an analytic function in
\Delta
, then

\rho\left(f\right)\leq\rho_{M}\left(f\right)\leq\rho\left(f\right)+1

\left(\text{b}\right)
There exist analytic functions
f
in
\Delta
which satisfy
\rho_{M}\left(f\right)\neq\rho\left(f\right).

For example, let
\mu>1
be a constant, and set

h\left(z\right)=\exp\left\{\left(1-z\right)^{-\mu}\right\},

where we choose the principal branch of the logarithm. Then
\rho\left(h\right)=\mu-1
and
\rho_{M}\left(h\right)=\mu
, see
[4]
.

In contrast, the possibility that occurs in
\left(\text{b}\right)
cannot occur in the whole plane
\mathbb{C},
because if
\rho\left(f\right)
and
\rho_{M}\left(f\right)
denote
the order of an entire function
f
in the plane
\mathbb{C}
(defined by the Nevanlinna characteristic and the maximum modulus,
respectively), then it is well-know that

\rho\left(f\right)=\underset{r\rightarrow+\infty}{\lim\sup}\frac{\log T\left(r,f\right)}{\log r}=\rho_{M}\left(f\right)=\underset{r\rightarrow+\infty}{\lim\sup}\frac{\log\log M\left(r,f\right)}{\log r}.

The meromorphic function
f
in the unit disc can be divided into the
following three classes:

(1) bounded type if
T\left(r,f\right)=O\left(1\right)
as
r\rightarrow 1^{-};

(2) rational or non-admissible type if
T\left(r,f\right)=O\left(\frac{1}{1-r}\right)
and
f
does not belong to (1)
;

(3) admissible in
\Delta
if

\underset{r\rightarrow 1^{-}}{\lim\sup}\frac{T\left(r,f\right)}{\log\frac{1}{1-r}}=\infty.

Definition 1.1
\left[2,3\right]
Let
p\geq q\geq 1
be
integers. Let
f
be a meromorphic function in
\Delta,
the [
p,q
]-order of
f
is defined by

\rho_{[p,q]}\left(f\right)=\underset{r\rightarrow 1^{-}}{\lim\sup}\frac{\log_{p}^{+}T\left(r,f\right)}{\log_{q}\frac{1}{1-r}},

where
\log_{1}^{+}r:=\log^{+}r=\max\left(0,\log r\right),

\log_{p+1}^{+}r:=\log^{+}\left(\log_{p}^{+}r\right),

p\in\mathbb{N}
. For
an analytic function
f
in
\Delta,
we also define

\rho_{M,[p,q]}\left(f\right)=\underset{r\rightarrow 1^{-}}{\lim\sup}\frac{\log_{p+1}^{+}M\left(r,f\right)}{\log_{q}\frac{1}{1-r}}.

It is easy to see that
0\leq\rho_{[p,q]}\left(f\right)\leq+\infty.
If
f
is non-admissible
,
then
\rho_{[p,q]}\left(f\right)=0
for any
p\geq q\geq 1.
By Definition 1.1,
\rho_{[1,1]}\left(f\right)=\rho\left(f\right)
is the order of
f
in
\Delta,

\rho_{[2,1]}\left(f\right)=\rho_{2}\left(f\right)
is the
hyper-order of
f
in
\Delta
and
\rho_{[p,1]}\left(f\right)=\rho_{p}\left(f\right)
is the
p
-iterated order of
f
in
\Delta.

Proposition 1.1

\left[2\right]
Let
p\geq q\geq 1
be
integers, and let
f
be an analytic function in
\Delta
of [
p,q
]-order. The following two statements hold :

(i)
If
p=q
, then

\rho_{[p,q]}\left(f\right)\leq\rho_{M,[p,q]}\left(f\right)\leq\rho_{[p,q]}\left(f\right)+1.

(ii)
If
p>q,
then

\rho_{[p,q]}\left(f\right)=\rho_{M,[p,q]}\left(f\right).

Proposition 1.2
\left[8\right]
Let
p\geq q\geq 1
be integers,
and let
f
be an analytic function in
\Delta
of [
p,q
]-order.
The following two statements hold :

(i)
If
p=q
, then

\mu_{[p,q]}\left(f\right)\leq\mu_{M,[p,q]}\left(f\right)\leq\mu_{[p,q]}\left(f\right)+1.

(ii)
If
p>q,
then

\mu_{[p,q]}\left(f\right)=\mu_{M,[p,q]}\left(f\right).

In what follows, we give some notations and definitions of
a meromorphic function in a sector in unit disc. Throughout this paper,
\Omega
usually denotes the sector
\Omega_{\alpha,\beta}
(
0\leq\alpha<\beta\leq 2\pi
) of the unit disc, and for any given
\varepsilon\in\left(0,\frac{\beta-\alpha}{2}\right),

\Omega_{\varepsilon}

denotes the sector

\Omega_{\alpha,\beta,\varepsilon}=\left\{z\in\mathbb{C}:\alpha+\varepsilon<\arg z<\beta-\varepsilon,\text{ }\left|z\right|<1\right\}.

In
\left[21\right]
, Wu has used the Ahlfors-Shimizu characteristic
function to measure the order of growth of a meromorphic function
f
in
\Omega.
We recall the definition of the Ahlfors-Shimizu characteristic
function, see
\left[5,6\right]
. Let
f
be a meromorphic function in
\Omega,
set

\Omega(r)=\Omega\cap\left\{z\in\mathbb{C}:0<\left|z\right|<r<1\right\}

=\left\{z\in\mathbb{C}:\alpha<\arg z<\beta,\text{ }0<\left|z\right|<r<1\right\}.

Then, the Ahlfors-Shimizu characteristic function is defined by

T_{0}\left(r,\Omega,f\right)=\int_{0}^{r}\frac{S\left(t,\Omega,f\right)}{t}dt,

where

S\left(r,\Omega,f\right)=\frac{1}{\pi}\iint\limits_{\Omega(r)}\left(\frac{\left|f^{\prime}\left(z\right)\right|}{1+\left|f\left(z\right)\right|^{2}}\right)^{2}d\sigma,\text{ }z=re^{i\theta},\text{ }d\sigma=rdrd\theta.

It follows by Hayman
\left[6\right]
, Goldberg and Ostrovskii
\left[5\right]
that

T_{0}\left(r,\mathbb{C},f\right)=T\left(r,f\right)+O\left(1\right),\text{ }0<r<1.

The meromorphic function
f
in a sector
\Omega
of the unit disc can be
divided into the following three classes:

(1) bounded type if
T_{0}\left(r,\Omega,f\right)=O\left(1\right)
as
r\rightarrow 1^{-};

(2) rational or non-admissible type if
T_{0}\left(r,\Omega,f\right)=O\left(\frac{1}{1-r}\right)
and
f
does not belong to (1);

(3) admissible in
\Omega
if

\underset{r\rightarrow 1^{-}}{\lim\sup}\frac{T_{0}\left(r,\Omega,f\right)}{\log\frac{1}{1-r}}=\infty.

Now, we introduce the concept of [
p,q
]-order and [
p,q
]-type of meromorphic functions in a sector
\Omega
.

Definition 1.2
\left[12,25\right]
Let
p\geq q\geq 1

be integers. Let
f
be a meromorphic function in
\Omega,
the [
p,q
]-order of
f
is defined by

\rho_{[p,q],\Omega}\left(f\right)=\underset{r\rightarrow 1^{-}}{\lim\sup}\frac{\log_{p}^{+}T_{0}\left(r,\Omega,f\right)}{\log_{q}\frac{1}{1-r}}.

It is clear that
0\leq\rho_{[p,q],\Omega}\left(f\right)\leq+\infty.
If
f
is non-admissible in
\Omega,
then
\rho_{[p,q],\Omega}\left(f\right)=0.
By Definition 1.2,
\rho_{[1,1],\Omega}\left(f\right)=\rho_{\Omega}\left(f\right)
is the order
of
f
in
\Omega,
see
\left[21\right]
,
\rho_{[p,1],\Omega}\left(f\right)=\rho_{p,\Omega}\left(f\right)
is the iterated
p
-order of
f
in
\Omega,
see
\left[11,24\right]
.

Definition 1.3
\left[25\right]
Let
p\geq q\geq 1
be
integers and
f
be a meromorphic function in
\Omega
with [
p,q
]-order
0<\rho_{[p,q],\Omega}\left(f\right)<+\infty.
Then, the
[
p,q
]-type of
f
is defined by

\tau_{[p,q],\Omega}\left(f\right)=\underset{r\rightarrow 1^{-}}{\lim\sup}\frac{\log_{p-1}^{+}T_{0}\left(r,\Omega,f\right)}{\left(\log_{q-1}\frac{1}{1-r}\right)^{\rho_{[p,q],\Omega}\left(f\right)}}.

Now, we introduce the concept of lower [
p,q
]-order
and lower [
p,q
]-type of a meromorphic function in a sector
\Omega
.

Definition 1.4
Let
p\geq q\geq 1
be integers. Let
f

be a meromorphic function in
\Omega,
the lower [
p,q
]-order of
f
is defined by

\mu_{[p,q],\Omega}\left(f\right)=\underset{r\rightarrow 1^{-}}{\lim\inf}\frac{\log_{p}^{+}T_{0}\left(r,\Omega,f\right)}{\log_{q}\frac{1}{1-r}}.

It is clear that
0\leq\mu_{[p,q],\Omega}\left(f\right)\leq+\infty.
If
f
is non-admissible in
\Omega,
then
\mu_{[p,q],\Omega}\left(f\right)=0.
By Definition 1.4,
\mu_{[1,1],\Omega}\left(f\right)=\mu_{\Omega}\left(f\right)
is the lower
order of
f
in
\Omega
and
\mu_{[p,1],\Omega}\left(f\right)=\mu_{p,\Omega}\left(f\right)
is the lower iterated
p
-order of
f
in

\Omega.

Definition 1.5
Let
p\geq q\geq 1
be integers and
f
be
a meromorphic function in
\Omega
with lower [
p,q
]-order
0<\mu_{[p,q],\Omega}\left(f\right)<+\infty.
Then, the lower [
p,q
]-type of
f
is defined by

\underline{\tau}_{[p,q],\Omega}\left(f\right)=\underset{r\rightarrow 1^{-}}{\lim\inf}\frac{\log_{p-1}^{+}T_{0}\left(r,\Omega,f\right)}{\left(\log_{q-1}\frac{1}{1-r}\right)^{\mu_{[p,q],\Omega}\left(f\right)}}.

2
Main results

Several authors
\left[2,3,8,10,16\right]
have
investigated the growth of solutions of the equation
\left(1.1\right)
by
using the concepts of [
p,q
]-order in the unit disc
\Delta
. In
\left[12\right]
, Long has studied the growth of solutions of the equation

\left(1.2\right)
in a sector of the unit disc with analytic coefficients
of finite [
p,q
]-order, and has obtained the following results.

Theorem A
\left[12\right]

Let
p\geq q\geq 1
be integers and
\varepsilon\in\left(0,\frac{\beta-\alpha}{2}\right)
. Let
E
be a set of complex numbers satisfying
\overline{\mathrm{dens}}\left\{\left|z\right|=r:z\in E\subset\Omega\right\}>0,
and let
A_{0}(z),A_{1}(z),\dots,A_{k}(z)
be analytic functions in
\Omega
such that for some
real constants satisfying
0\leq\gamma<\lambda
, we have

T_{0}\left(r,\Omega_{\varepsilon},A_{0}(z)\right)\geq\exp_{p}\left\{\lambda\log_{q}\left(\frac{1}{1-\left|z\right|}\right)\right\},

T_{0}\left(r,\Omega,A_{j}(z)\right)\leq\exp_{p}\left\{\gamma\log_{q}\left(\frac{1}{1-\left|z\right|}\right)\right\},\text{ }j=1,2,\dots,k

as
\left|z\right|=r\rightarrow 1^{-}
for
z\in E.
Then every nontrivial solution
f
of
\left(1.2\right)
satisfies
\rho_{[p,q],\Omega}\left(f\right)=+\infty

and

\rho_{[p+1,q],\Omega}\left(f\right)\geq\lambda.

Theorem B
\left[12\right]

Let
p\geq q\geq 1
be integers and
\varepsilon\in\left(0,\frac{\beta-\alpha}{2}\right)
. Let
A_{0}(z),A_{1}(z),\dots,A_{k}(z)
be
analytic functions in
\Omega.
If

\max_{1\leq j\leq k}\left\{\rho_{[p,q],\Omega}\left(A_{j}\right)\right\}<\rho_{[p,q],\Omega_{\varepsilon}}\left(A_{0}\right),

then every nontrivial solution of
\left(1.2\right)
satisfies

\rho_{[p+1,q],\Omega}\left(f\right)\geq\rho_{[p,q],\Omega_{\varepsilon}}\left(A_{0}\right).

Remark 2.1.
In Theorems A-B, we note that if
A_{k}(z)=1
, then all
the solutions of equation
\left(1.2\right)
are analytic functions. But
if
A_{k}(z)
is a non constant analytic function, then obviously the
solution
f
of the equation
\left(1.2\right)
can be meromorphic
function.

The

hypotheses of the Theorems A-B do not
provide that a solution is meromorphic in
\Omega
, so it is a priori
assumed that
f
is meromorphic.

Very recently, Zemirni and Belaïdi
\left[25\right]
have
continued the study of the growth of solutions of the equation
\left(1.1\right)
instead of the equation
\left(1.2\right)
in a sector of the
unit disc with analytic coefficients of finite [
p,q
]-order, and
have got the following results.

Theorem C
\left[25\right]

Let
p\geq q\geq 1
be integers and
\varepsilon\in\left(0,\frac{\beta-\alpha}{2}\right)
. Let
A_{0}(z),A_{1}(z),\newline
\dots,

A_{k-1}(z)
be analytic functions in
\Omega.
If

\max_{1\leq j\leq k-1}\left\{\rho_{[p,q],\Omega}\left(A_{j}\right)\right\}<\rho_{[p,q],\Omega_{\varepsilon}}\left(A_{0}\right),

then every nontrivial solution of
\left(1.1\right)
satisfies
\rho_{[p,q],\Omega}\left(f\right)=+\infty
and

\rho_{[p,q],\Omega_{\varepsilon}}\left(A_{0}\right)\leq\rho_{[p+1,q],\Omega}\left(f\right)\text{, }\rho_{[p+1,q],\Omega_{\varepsilon}}\left(f\right)\leq\rho_{[p,q],\Omega}\left(A_{0}\right)+1.

Furthermore, if
p>q,
then

\rho_{[p,q],\Omega_{\varepsilon}}\left(A_{0}\right)\leq\rho_{[p+1,q],\Omega}\left(f\right)\text{,\ }\rho_{[p+1,q],\Omega_{\varepsilon}}\left(f\right)\leq\rho_{[p,q],\Omega}\left(A_{0}\right).

Theorem D

\left[25\right]

Let
p\geq q\geq 1
be integers and
\varepsilon\in\left(0,\frac{\beta-\alpha}{2}\right)
. Let
A_{0}(z),A_{1}(z),\newline
\dots,

A_{k-1}(z)
be analytic functions in
\Omega.
Suppose that

\max_{1\leq j\leq k-1}\left\{\rho_{[p,q],\Omega}\left(A_{j}\right)\right\}\leq\rho_{[p,q],\Omega_{\varepsilon}}\left(A_{0}\right)=\rho\text{ }\left(0<\rho<+\infty\right)

and

\max_{1\leq j\leq k-1}\{\tau_{[p,q],\Omega}\left(A_{j}\right):\rho_{[p,q],\Omega}\left(A_{j}\right)=\rho_{[p,q],\Omega_{\varepsilon}}\left(A_{0}\right)\}

<\tau_{[p,q],\Omega_{\varepsilon}}\left(A_{0}\right)=\tau\text{
}(0<\tau<+\infty).

Then every nontrivial solution of
\left(1.1\right)
satisfies
\rho_{[p,q],\Omega}\left(f\right)=+\infty
and

\rho_{[p,q],\Omega_{\varepsilon}}\left(A_{0}\right)\leq\rho_{[p+1,q],\Omega}\left(f\right),\text{ }\rho_{[p+1,q],\Omega_{\varepsilon}}\left(f\right)\leq\rho_{[p,q],\Omega}\left(A_{0}\right)+1.

Furthermore, if
p>q,
then

\rho_{[p,q],\Omega_{\varepsilon}}\left(A_{0}\right)\leq\rho_{[p+1,q],\Omega}\left(f\right),\text{ \ }\rho_{[p+1,q],\Omega_{\varepsilon}}\left(f\right)\leq\rho_{[p,q],\Omega}\left(A_{0}\right).

Thus, the following questions arise naturally: (i) Whether
the results similar to Theorem C can be obtained in

\Omega
if
A_{0}\left(z\right)
to dominate other coefficients in the sense of lower [
p,q
]-order?

(ii) If we use the lower [
p,q
]-type of
A_{0}\left(z\right)
to dominate other coefficients, what can be said about
\mu_{[p+1,q],\Omega}\left(f\right)
similar to Theorem D? In this
paper, we give some answers to the above questions. In fact, by using the
concept of lower [
p,q
]-type, we obtain some results which indicate
growth estimate of every non-trivial analytic solution of equation
\left(1.1\right)
by the growth estimate of the coefficient
A_{0}\left(z\right).
We mainly obtain the following results.

Theorem 2.1

Let
p\geq q\geq 1
be
integers and
\varepsilon\in\left(0,\frac{\beta-\alpha}{2}\right)
. Let
A_{0}(z),A_{1}(z),\newline
\dots,

A_{k-1}(z)
be analytic functions in
\Omega.
If

\max_{1\leq j\leq k-1}\left\{\rho_{[p,q],\Omega}\left(A_{j}\right)\right\}<\mu_{[p,q],\Omega_{\varepsilon}}\left(A_{0}\right),

then every nontrivial solution of
\left(1.1\right)
satisfies
\rho_{[p,q],\Omega}\left(f\right)=\mu_{[p,q],\Omega}\left(f\right)=+\infty,

\mu_{[p,q],\Omega_{\varepsilon}}\left(A_{0}\right)\leq\mu_{[p+1,q],\Omega}\left(f\right)\leq\rho_{[p+1,q],\Omega}\left(f\right)\text{ }

and

\mu_{[p+1,q],\Omega_{\varepsilon}}\left(f\right)\leq\mu_{[p,q],\Omega}\left(A_{0}\right)+1.

Furthermore, if
p>q,
then

\mu_{[p,q],\Omega_{\varepsilon}}\left(A_{0}\right)\leq\mu_{[p+1,q],\Omega}\left(f\right)\leq\rho_{[p+1,q],\Omega}\left(f\right)

and

\text{ }\mu_{[p+1,q],\Omega_{\varepsilon}}\left(f\right)\leq\mu_{[p,q],\Omega}\left(A_{0}\right).

Remark 2.2
The Theorem 2.1 is similar to Theorem 2.2
\left(\text{i}\right)
in
\left[16\right]
in the unit disc
\Delta
.

Corollary 2.1

Let
p\geq q\geq 1
be
integers and
\varepsilon\in\left(0,\frac{\beta-\alpha}{2}\right)
. Let
A_{0}(z),A_{1}(z),\newline
\dots,

A_{k-1}(z)
be analytic functions in
\Omega.
If

\max_{1\leq j\leq k-1}\left\{\rho_{[p,q],\Omega}\left(A_{j}\right)\right\}<\mu_{[p,q],\Omega_{\varepsilon}}\left(A_{0}\right)=\rho_{[p,q],\Omega_{\varepsilon}}\left(A_{0}\right),

then every nontrivial solution of
\left(1.1\right)
satisfies
\rho_{[p,q],\Omega}\left(f\right)=\mu_{[p,q],\Omega}\left(f\right)=+\infty
and

\mu_{[p,q],\Omega_{\varepsilon}}\left(A_{0}\right)\leq\mu_{[p+1,q],\Omega}\left(f\right)\leq\rho_{[p+1,q],\Omega}\left(f\right),\text{ }

\mu_{[p+1,q],\Omega_{\varepsilon}}\left(f\right)\leq\rho_{[p+1,q],\Omega_{\varepsilon}}\left(f\right)\leq\mu_{[p,q],\Omega}\left(A_{0}\right)+1.

Furthermore, if
p>q,
then

\mu_{[p,q],\Omega_{\varepsilon}}\left(A_{0}\right)\leq\mu_{[p+1,q],\Omega}\left(f\right)\leq\rho_{[p+1,q],\Omega}\left(f\right)

and

\text{ }\mu_{[p+1,q],\Omega_{\varepsilon}}\left(f\right)\leq\rho_{[p+1,q],\Omega_{\varepsilon}}\left(f\right)\leq\mu_{[p,q],\Omega}\left(A_{0}\right).

Theorem 2.2

Let
p\geq q\geq 1
be integers and
\varepsilon\in\left(0,\frac{\beta-\alpha}{2}\right)
. Let
A_{0}(z),A_{1}(z),\newline
\dots,

A_{k-1}(z)
be analytic functions in
\Omega
such that
0<\mu=\mu_{[p,q],\Omega_{\varepsilon}}\left(A_{0}\right)\leq\rho_{[p,q],\Omega_{\varepsilon}}\left(A_{0}\right)<+\infty.
Suppose that

\max_{1\leq j\leq k-1}\left\{\rho_{[p,q],\Omega}\left(A_{j}\right)\right\}\leq\mu_{[p,q],\Omega_{\varepsilon}}\left(A_{0}\right)

\rho_{[p,q],\Omega}\left(A_{j}\right)=\mu_{[p,q],\Omega_{\varepsilon}}\left(A_{0}\right)=\mu.
So, there exists a set
I\subseteq\{1,\cdots,k-1\}
such that for
j\in I
we have
\rho_{[p,q],\Omega}\left(A_{j}\right)=\mu_{[p,q],\Omega_{\varepsilon}}\left(A_{0}\right)=\mu
and

\tau_{1}=\max_{j\in I}\{\tau_{[p,q],\Omega}\left(A_{j}\right):\rho_{[p,q],\Omega}\left(A_{j}\right)=\mu_{[p,q],\Omega_{\varepsilon}}\left(A_{0}\right)\}<\underline{\tau}_{[p,q],\Omega_{\varepsilon}}\left(A_{0}\right)=\tau<+\infty

and for
j\in\{1,\cdots,k-1\}\backslash I,
we have
b=\max_{j\in\{1,\cdots,k-1\}\backslash I}\{\rho_{[p,q],\Omega}\left(A_{j}\right)\}<\mu_{[p,q],\Omega_{\varepsilon}}\left(A_{0}\right)=\mu.
Then that for any given
\epsilon

\left(0<2\epsilon<\min\left\{\mu-b,\tau-\tau_{1}\right\}\right)
and for
r\rightarrow 1^{-},
we have for
j\in\{1,\cdots,k-1\}\backslash I

T_{0}\left(r,\Omega,A_{j}(z)\right)\leq\exp_{p}\left\{\left(b+\epsilon\right)\log_{q}\frac{1}{1-r}\right\}\leq\exp_{p}\left\{\left(\mu-\epsilon\right)\log_{q}\frac{1}{1-r}\right\}

(4.10)

and for
j\in I,
we get

T_{0}\left(r,\Omega,A_{j}(z)\right)\leq\exp_{p-1}\left\{\left(\tau_{1}+\epsilon\right)\left(\log_{q-1}\frac{1}{1-r}\right)^{\mu}\right\}.

(4.11)

By the definition of lower
\left[p,q\right]
order, we have for
r\rightarrow 1^{-}

T_{0}\left(r,\Omega_{\varepsilon},A_{0}(z)\right)\geq\exp_{p-1}\left\{\left(\tau-\epsilon\right)\left(\log_{q-1}\frac{1}{1-r}\right)^{\mu}\right\}.

(4.12)

Then, by
\left(4.1\right)
and
\left(4.12\right)
as
|u|=\varrho\rightarrow 1^{-}

T\left(\varrho,B_{0}(u)\right)=T\left(\varrho,\frac{1}{\alpha_{k}}A_{0}\left(z\left(u\right)\right)\right)

\geq\frac{b}{2}T_{0}\left(1-\frac{1-\varrho}{b},\Omega_{\varepsilon},A_{0}(z)\right)+O(1)-T\left(\varrho,\alpha_{k}\right)

\geq\frac{b}{2}\exp_{p-1}\left\{\left(\tau-\epsilon\right)\left(\log_{q-1}\frac{b}{1-\varrho}\right)^{\mu}\right\}+O(1)-T\left(\varrho,\alpha_{k}\right)

=O\left(\exp_{p-1}\left\{\left(\tau-\epsilon\right)\left(\log_{q-1}\frac{1}{1-\varrho}\right)^{\mu}\right\}\right)-T\left(\varrho,\alpha_{k}\right).

(4.13)

Also, by
\left(4.2\right),

\left(4.10\right)
and
\left(4.11\right)
for
j=1,2,\dots,k-1

T\left(\varrho,B_{j}\right)\leq\frac{16\pi}{\delta}\sum_{n=j}^{k-1}T_{0}\left(1-\frac{\delta}{8\pi}\left(1-\varrho\right),\Omega,A_{n}\left(z\right)\right)+O\left(\log\frac{1}{1-\varrho}\right)

\leq O\left(\exp_{p}\left\{\left(\mu-\epsilon\right)\log_{q}\frac{8\pi}{\delta\left(1-\varrho\right)}\right\}\right)

+O\left(\exp_{p-1}\left\{\left(\tau_{1}+\epsilon\right)\left(\log_{q-1}\frac{8\pi}{\delta\left(1-\varrho\right)}\right)^{\mu}\right\}\right)+O\left(\log\frac{1}{1-\varrho}\right)

=O\left(\exp_{p-1}\left\{\left(\tau_{1}+\epsilon\right)\left(\log_{q-1}\frac{1}{1-\varrho}\right)^{\mu}\right\}+\log\frac{1}{1-\varrho}\right).

(4.14)

It follows by
\left(4.7\right),\left(4.13\right),

\left(4.14\right)

and Lemma 3.7 that

O\left(\exp_{p-1}\left\{\left(\tau-\epsilon\right)\left(\log_{q-1}\frac{1}{1-\varrho}\right)^{\mu}\right\}\right)\leq O\left(\exp_{p-1}\left\{\left(\tau_{1}+\epsilon\right)\left(\log_{q-1}\frac{1}{1-\varrho}\right)^{\mu}\right\}\right)

+O\left(\log\frac{1}{1-\varrho}\right)+T\left(\varrho,\alpha_{k}\right)+O\left(\log^{+}T\left(\varrho,F\right)+\log\frac{1}{1-\varrho}\right)

(4.15)

holds for all
u
satisfying
|u|=\varrho\notin E
as
\varrho\rightarrow 1^{-},
where
E\subset(0,1)
is a set with
\int_{E}\frac{d\varrho}{1-\varrho}<+\infty.
By using Lemma 3.8 and
\left(4.15\right),
for all

u
satisfying
|u|=\varrho\rightarrow 1^{-},
we obtain

\exp_{p-1}\left\{\left(\tau-\epsilon\right)\left(\log_{q-1}\frac{1}{1-\varrho}\right)^{\mu}\right\}\leq O\left(\exp_{p-1}\left\{\left(\tau_{1}+\epsilon\right)\left(\log_{q-1}\frac{1}{d\left(1-\varrho\right)}\right)^{\mu}\right\}\right)

+O\left(\log\frac{1}{d\left(1-\varrho\right)}\right)+O\left(\log^{+}T\left(1-d\left(1-\varrho\right),F\right)\right).

(4.16)

Thus, from
\left(4.16\right)
we get
\rho_{[p,q]}\left(F\right)=\mu_{[p,q]}\left(F\right)=+\infty
and
\rho_{[p+1,q]}\left(F\right)\geq\mu_{[p+1,q]}\left(F\right)\geq\mu.

Then, by Remark 3.1, we get that

\rho_{[p,q],\Omega}\left(f\left(z\right)\right)=\mu_{[p,q]}\left(f\left(z\right)\right)=+\infty\text{ and }\rho_{[p+1,q],\Omega}\left(f\left(z\right)\right)\geq\mu_{[p+1,q],\Omega}\left(f\left(z\right)\right)\geq\mu.

On the other hand, by Lemma 3.6 we have
\mu_{[p+1,q],\Omega_{\varepsilon}}\left(f\right)\leq\mu_{[p,q],\Omega}\left(A_{0}\right)+1,
and if
p>q,
we have
\mu_{[p+1,q],\Omega_{\varepsilon}}\left(f\right)\leq\mu_{[p,q],\Omega}\left(A_{0}\right).

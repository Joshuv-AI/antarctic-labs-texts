A Skorohod measurable universal functional representation of solutions to semimartingale SDEs
Paweł Przybyłowicz, Verena Schwarz, Alexander Steinicke, Michaela Szölgyenyi

Abstract
In this paper we show the existence of a universal Skorohod measurable functional representation for a large class of semimartingale-driven stochastic differential equations. For this we prove that paths of the strong solutions of stochastic differential equations can be written as measurable functions of the paths of their driving processes into the space of all càdlàg functions equipped with the Borel sigma-field generated by all open sets with respect to the Skorohod metric. This result can be applied to calculate Malliavin derivatives for SDEs driven by pure-jump Lévy processes with drift. Keywords: semimartingale-driven stochastic differential equations, functional representation, Skorohod measurability, Malliavin derivative Mathematics Subject Classification (2020): 60H10, 60H07, 60G51

Introduction

In this paper we prove that the universal functional representation for solutions to general semimartingale SDEs is Skorohod measurable.
This result has various applications.
It can be used in Malliavin calculus for pure-jump Lévy processes, see Theorem
4.2
below; there our result yields a universal expression for the Malliavin derivative of an SDE solution, providing access to hedging strategies for price processes of financial assets.
Furthermore, functional representations of SDEs have applications in computational stochastics. In the jump-free case the respective classical result has been applied for example in
[
14
,
15
]
. In these articles, upper and lower bounds for approximation schemes for Brownian-motion driven SDEs are proven. Our result allows us to perform complexity analysis for jump-diffusion SDEs with irregular coefficients; a first work in this direction is
[
19

The class of SDEs we consider is rather general and therefore covers a wide range of applications.
In particular, we consider SDEs of the form

Before, one could find an expression for the solution’s derivative for particular driving processes, which may take quite different forms. Our approach yields a single, unifying expression valid for all pure-jump Lévy drivers with drift.

A seminal result concerning the functional representation of SDEs is

The proof of our result requires major changes in the technique known from

The key novel step in the first part of the proof is showing Skorohod measurability of the approximating sequence; in the second part of the proof, the key lies in proving that the limit of the sequence of stochastic processes, regarded as a sequence of random elements mapping into the space of all càdlàg functions equipped with the Skorohod topology, is indistinguishable from the solution of SDE (

Notations and Preliminaries

Let for all

{\mathbb{D}}_{n}=\{f\colon[0,T]\to{\mathbb{R}}^{n}:f\text{ is c\`{a}dl\`{a}g}\}

We equip this space with the Skorohod topology, which is the topology generated by all coordinate mappings, or equivalently, the topology generated by all pointwise evaluations (cf.

For our proof we make use of an alternative definition of the Skorohod topology based on the following metric (cf.
[
3

Define the set

\displaystyle\Lambda=\{\lambda:[0,T]\to[0,T]:

\displaystyle\lambda\text{ is strictly increasing and continuous}

\displaystyle\text{with }\lambda(0)=0\text{ and }\lambda(T)=T\}

and then we set for all

\displaystyle\|\lambda\|_{\Lambda}=\sup_{0\leq s<t\leq T}\Big|\log\Big(\frac{\lambda(t)-\lambda(s)}{t-s}\Big)\Big|.

Using this we define the Skorohod metric

for all

\displaystyle d^{S}(x,y)=\inf_{\lambda\in\Lambda}\max\Big\{\|\lambda\|_{\Lambda},\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}\|x(t)-y(\lambda(t))\|\Big\}.

Note that this metric is equivalent to the one originally used by Skorohod, see
[
8

The space

is separable and complete, see
[
3

. Moreover, the Skorohod topology is equal to the topology induced by the open subsets of

under

with this topology is a Polish space. Further, we denote by

Note that the following mappings are Borel measurable

\displaystyle{\mathbb{D}}_{n}\ni f\to f(t-),

for all

, cf.
[

]

The space

with the Skorohod topology, it becomes separable and hence a Polish space, but then it is not a topological vector space anymore. It follows from the definition by choosing

it holds that

.
Let

be the space of all linear bounded operators from

to

Representation of the SDE solution as Skorohod measurable function of the driving processes

The goal of this section is to prove that the solution of SDE (
1
) can be expressed as a Skorohod measurable function of its initial value, the process
G
, and the semimartingale
Y
. Note that in
[
10
]
it is proven that the solution can be expressed as a measurable function with respect to the Borel-
\sigma
-field generated by the topology of uniform convergence. Although the
\sigma
-field of the Skorohod topology is equal to the
\sigma
-field generated by the balls of the metric of uniform convergence, this is a strict subset of the Borel
\sigma
-field of the topology of uniform convergence; separability would guarantee equality of the two. Hence our result cannot be concluded from

\Psi:{\mathbb{D}}_{d}\times{\mathbb{D}}_{r}\times{\mathbb{D}}_{m}\to{\mathbb{D}}_{d}
such that for all

-valued càdlàg processes

, all
{\mathbb{R}}^{r}
-valued càdlàg processes
G
, and all
{\mathbb{R}}^{m}
-valued càdlàg semimartingales

\displaystyle X=\Psi(H,G,Y)

\displaystyle X_{t}=H_{t}+\int_{0}^{t}g(s,G,X)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}Y_{s},\quad t\in[0,T].

By

Definition 3.3

(cf.
[
10
, p. 368, Definition 11.4]
)

A càdlàg adapted increasing process

is said to be a dominating process for a real-valued semimartingale
\xi

, if there exists a decomposition
\xi=M+A
, with a càdlàg locally square integrable martingale

, and a càdlàg process
A
with finite variation paths such that the process

\displaystyle B_{t}=V_{t}-2\sqrt{2}([M,M]_{t}+\langle M,M\rangle_{t})^{1/2}-\sqrt{2}|A|_{t},\quad t\in[0,T+1]

is an increasing process with
B_{0}\geq 0
.

It is known that for each semimartingale

(Z_{s}^{(n)}(\omega))_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}
converges uniformly on
[0,\nu_{j}(\omega))
for all
j\in{\mathbb{N}}
.Since
{\mathbb{P}}(N)=0
and because of the definition of
\nu_{j}
we have that
Z^{(n)}
converges uniformly on
[0,T+1)
.
Therefore, the process
(\widetilde{Z}_{t})_{t\in[0,T]}
defined by

\displaystyle\widetilde{Z}_{t}(\omega)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\lim_{n\to\infty}Z^{(n)}_{t}(\omega),&\omega\in\Omega\setminus N,\\
0,&\omega\in N\\
\end{array}\right.

is well-defined and
Z^{(n)}
converges uniformly on
[0,T]
to
\widetilde{Z}
almost surely. Hence,
Z^{(n)}
also converges almost surely to
\widetilde{Z}
in the Skorohod metric, since by the definition of the Skorohod metric it follows that every convergent sequence in the
\sup
-norm is also convergent in the Skorohod metric.
Next we define a new process for all
t\in[0,T]
by

\displaystyle Z_{t}(\omega)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\lim_{n\to\infty}Z^{(n)}_{t}(\omega),&\text{if the limit exists in the Skorohod topology,}\\
0,&\text{otherwise.}\\
\end{array}\right.

We observe that
Z
and
\widetilde{Z}
differ only on the subset of a nullset and are hence indistinguishable. Hence,
Z^{(n)}=\Psi^{(n)}(H,G,Y)
converges almost surely to
Z
in the Skorohod topology. Hence,
Z=\Psi(H,G,Y)
a.s.

Furthermore, it follows from (
22
) that

\displaystyle\sup_{k\geq 1}\Big\|\sup_{0\leq s<\nu_{j}}\|Z^{(n+k)}_{s}-Z^{(n)}_{s}\|\Big\|_{2}\leq\sup_{k\geq 1}\Big\|\sum_{j=n+1}^{n+k}\sup_{0\leq s<\nu_{j}}\|Z^{(j)}_{s}-Z^{(j-1)}_{s}\|\Big\|_{2}

(23)

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{j=n+1}^{\infty}\Big\|\sup_{0\leq s<\nu_{j}}\|Z^{(j)}_{s}-Z^{(j-1)}_{s}\|\Big\|_{2}\to 0\text{ as }n\to\infty.

Extending
\widetilde{Z}
to the interval
[0,T+1]
by defining
\widetilde{Z}_{t}=\widetilde{Z}_{T}
and using (
23
) we obtain

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}{\mathbb{E}}\Big[\sup_{0\leq s<\nu_{j}}\|\widetilde{Z}_{s}-Z_{s}^{(n)}\|^{2}\Big]=0.

(24)

Further, we get by Assumption
2.1

(iv)
and the definitions of
U
and
\nu_{j}
that

\displaystyle\sup_{0\leq s<\nu_{j}}\|\widetilde{f}(s,G,\widetilde{Z})-\widetilde{f}(s,G,Z^{(n)})\|

\displaystyle\leq\sup_{0\leq s<\nu_{j}}\widetilde{C}(s,G)\|\widetilde{Z}-Z^{(n)}\|

(25)

\displaystyle\leq j\sup_{0\leq s<\nu_{j}}\|\widetilde{Z}_{s}-Z_{s}^{(n)}\|.

The second statement of Theorem
3.5
together with (
25
) and (
24
) yields

\displaystyle{\displaystyle\mathbb{E}}\Big[\sup_{0\leq s<\nu_{j}}\Big\|H_{t}+\int_{0}^{t}\widetilde{f}(s,G,\widetilde{Z})\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}Y_{s}-H_{t}-\int_{0}^{t}\widetilde{f}(s,G,Z^{(n)})\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}Y_{s}\Big\|^{2}\Big]

\displaystyle={\mathbb{E}}\Big[\sup_{0\leq s<\nu_{j}}\Big\|\int_{0}^{t}\big(\widetilde{f}(s,G,\widetilde{Z})-\widetilde{f}(s,G,Z^{(n)})\big)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}Y_{s}\Big\|^{2}\Big]

\displaystyle\leq{\mathbb{E}}\Big[\sup_{0\leq s<\nu_{j}}\sum_{i=1}^{d}m\sum_{k=1}^{m}\Big|\int_{0}^{t}\big(\widetilde{f}(s,G,\widetilde{Z})-\widetilde{f}(s,G,Z^{(n)})\big)_{ik}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}(Y_{s})_{k}\Big|^{2}\Big]

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{i=1}^{d}m\sum_{k=1}^{m}{\mathbb{E}}\Big[\sup_{0\leq s<\nu_{j}}\Big|\int_{0}^{t}\big(\widetilde{f}(s,G,\widetilde{Z})-\widetilde{f}(s,G,Z^{(n)})\big)_{ik}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}(Y_{s})_{k}\Big|^{2}\Big]

\displaystyle\leq 4dm^{2}{\mathbb{E}}\Big[\sup_{0\leq s<\nu_{j}}\|f(s,G,\widetilde{Z})-f(s,G,Z^{(n)})\|^{2}V_{\nu_{j}-}\Big]

\displaystyle\leq 4dm^{2}jj^{2}{\mathbb{E}}\Big[\sup_{0\leq s<\nu_{j}}\|\widetilde{Z}_{s}-Z_{s}^{(n)}\|^{2}\Big]\to 0\text{ as }n\to\infty.

Hence, recalling the definition of
Z^{(n)}
, we observe that
\widetilde{Z}
is a solution of SDE (
1
) on
[0,T]
. Since
Z
and
\widetilde{Z}
are indistinguishable, we get that
Z=\Psi(H,G,Y)
is a solution of SDE (
1
). This closes the proof of Theorem
3.1
.
∎

As an application of our result we illustrate how Theorem
3.1
can be used to prove in a straightforward way the existence of a regular conditional distribution. While the classical proof uses the measurability of the marginal distributions of the solution process, our proof is based on the following well-known result.

Theorem 3.6

(
[
13
, p. 185, Theorem 8.37]
)
.

Let
\mathcal{A}\subset\mathcal{F}
be a sub-
\sigma
-algebra. Let
Z
be a random variable with values in a Polish space
(E,\mathcal{E})
. Then there exists a regular conditional distribution
\kappa_{Z,\mathcal{A}}
of
Z
given
\mathcal{A}
.

Corollary 3.7
.

Let Assumption
2.1
hold. Let
t_{0}\in[0,T]
. Then the regular conditional distribution of
(X_{s})_{s\in[t_{0},T]}
given
X_{t_{0}}
exists, i.e. a stochastic kernel
\kappa_{(X_{s})_{s\in[t_{0},T]},\sigma(X_{t_{0}})}
from
(\Omega,\mathcal{F})
to
({\mathbb{D}}_{d},{\mathcal{B}}({\mathbb{D}}_{d}))
exists such that for all
A\in\mathcal{F}
and all
B\in{\mathcal{B}}({\mathbb{D}}_{d})
it holds that

\displaystyle\int_{A}\mathds{1}_{B}((X_{s})_{s\in[t_{0},T]})\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}{\mathbb{P}}=\int_{A}\kappa_{(X_{s})_{s\in[t_{0},T]},\sigma(X_{t_{0}})}(\cdot,B)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}{\mathbb{P}}.

Proof.

The measurability of the stochastic processes
H
,
G
, and
Y
implies that the mapping

\displaystyle\omega\mapsto X(\omega)=\Psi(H(\omega),G(\omega),Y(\omega))

is a measurable mapping with values in
({\mathbb{D}}_{d},\mathcal{B}({\mathbb{D}}_{d}))
as a composition of measurable mappings. Hence for
t_{0}\in[0,T]
obviously also the mapping

\displaystyle\omega\mapsto\Psi(H(\omega),G(\omega),Y(\omega))\mathds{1}_{s\in[t_{0},T]}

is measurable.
Next, we use Theorem
3.6
with
Z=\Psi(H,G,Y)\mathds{1}_{s\in[t_{0},T]}
and
\mathcal{A}=\sigma(X_{t_{0}})
to obtain that there exists a regular conditional distribution
\kappa_{\Psi(H,G,Y)\mathds{1}_{s\in[t_{0},T]},\sigma(X_{t_{0}})}
.
Hence,
{\mathbb{P}}^{(X_{s})_{s\in[t_{0},T]}|X_{t_{0}}=x}
exists and is given for all

by

\displaystyle{\mathbb{P}}^{(X_{s})_{s\in[t_{0},T]}|X_{t_{0}}=x}(B)=\kappa_{\Psi(H,G,Y)\mathds{1}_{s\in[t_{0},T]},\sigma(X_{t_{0}})}(X_{t_{0}}^{-1}(x),B).

∎

4
Application to Malliavin calculus for pure-jump Lévy processes with drift

In this section we derive a universal expression for the Malliavin derivative for solutions of SDE (
1
) in the case that
Y
is a pure-jump Lévy process with drift.

Let
\nu
be the Lévy-measure of
Y
and let
N
be its Poisson random measure, respectively
\tilde{N}
its compensated Poisson random measure. We assume that
\mathcal{F}
is the completion of
\sigma(Y)
. We can represent
Y
almost surely by

\displaystyle Y_{t}=\gamma t+\int_{]0,t]\times\{|x|>1\}}xN(ds,dx)+\int_{{]0,t]}\times\{|x|\leq 1\}}x\tilde{N}(ds,dx),\quad t\in[0,T],

see, e.g.,
[
1
]
or
[
20
]
.

Malliavin calculus can be used for determining existence of densities or existence of solutions to SDEs, backward stochastic differential equations, and stochastic partial integro-differential equations. For references in these directions, see, e.g.,
[
5
,
6
,
7
,
16
,
18
]
, of which
[
18
,
7
]
deal with the Lévy setting we consider here.

There are several ways to define a Malliavin derivative in the Lévy setting. We follow the approach used in
[
21
]
. There it is defined via chaotic decompositions of random variables. However, in
[
22
]
, they show that on a canonical probability space carrying a Lévy process, their definition equals a difference between a ’shifted’ random variable and the original one. The connection to functional representations for random variables on an arbitrary probability space carrying a Lévy process is presented in
[
23
]
. In that spirit, we define

Definition 4.1
.

Let
Z\in L^{2}(\Omega,\mathcal{F},\mathbb{P})
, with functional representation
\psi_{Z}\colon\mathbb{D}_{1}\to\mathbb{R}
measurable,
Z=\psi_{Z}(Y)
. Let for
r\in[0,T]
,
v\in\mathbb{R}
,

\displaystyle D_{r,v}Z:=\psi_{Z}(Y+v\mathds{1}_{[r,T]})-\psi_{Z}(Y),

as equivalence class in
L^{2}(\Omega\times[0,T]\times\mathbb{R},\mathcal{F}\otimes\mathcal{B}([0,T]\times\mathbb{R}),\mathbb{P}\otimes\lambda\otimes\nu)
,
whenever

\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{R}}|D_{r,v}Z|^{2}\nu(dv)dr<\infty.

(26)

The space of all
Z
satisfying (
26
) is denoted by
\mathcal{D}_{1,2}
.

Our object of interest is the solution process of the SDE

\displaystyle X_{t}=H_{t}+\int_{(0,t]}g(s,G,X)dY_{s},

of which we want to investigate the Mallivain derivative
D_{r,v}X_{t}
. By our main theorem, we see that we may express the solution
X
as
X=\Psi_{g}(H,G,Y)
. In addition we have
\mathbb{P}
-a.s. representations for the random variables
H_{t},G_{t}
for all
t\in[0,T]
through
H_{t}=\phi_{H_{t}}(Y),G_{t}=\phi_{G_{t}}(Y)
for measurable functions
\phi_{H_{t}},\phi_{G_{t}}\colon\mathbb{D}_{1}\to\mathbb{R}
, see
[
23
]
. Since
H
and
G
are càdlàg processes, they are determined by their random variables evaluated at rational time points. Hence, we get a
\mathbb{P}
-a.s. representation of the whole processes
H,G
by measurable functionals
\phi_{H},\phi_{G}\colon\mathbb{D}_{1}\to\mathbb{D}_{1}
such that
H=\phi_{H}(Y),G=\phi_{G}(Y)
, where
(h\mapsto h_{t})\circ\phi_{H}=\phi_{H_{t}},(h\mapsto h_{t})\circ\phi_{G}=\phi_{G_{t}}
for all
t\in[0,T]
. If for all such
t
the random variables
H_{t}
and
G_{t}
are in
\mathcal{D}_{1,2}
, the derivative
D_{r,v}H
can be defined by

\displaystyle D_{r,v}H=\phi_{H}(Y+v\mathds{1}_{[r,T]})-\phi_{H}(Y);

in the same way we can define it for
D_{r,v}G
.

With these representations, we can explicitly express the Malliavin derivative of
X_{t}
in dependence only of
H,G
and the functional
\Psi_{g}
.

Theorem 4.2
.

Let
X
be the solution to (
1
) where
H,G
are such that for all
t\in[0,T]
,
H_{t},G_{t}\in\mathcal{D}_{1,2}
and assume
X_{t}\in L^{2}(\Omega,\mathcal{F},\mathbb{P})
. Then for all
t\in[0,T]
,

\displaystyle D_{r,v}X_{t}=\Psi_{g}(H+D_{r,v}H,G+D_{r,v}G,Y+v\mathds{1}_{[r,T]})_{t}-\Psi_{g}(H,G,Y)_{t},

whenever it is square integrable w.r.t.
\mathbb{P}\otimes\lambda\otimes\nu.

Proof.

Since

\displaystyle H=\phi_{H}(Y),\quad G=\phi_{G}(Y),\quad X=\Psi_{g}(H,G,Y),

it follows that

\displaystyle X=\Psi_{g}(\phi_{H}(Y),\phi_{G}(Y),Y).

Hence,

\displaystyle D_{r,v}X_{t}=D_{r,v}\Psi_{g}(\phi_{H}(Y),\phi_{G}(Y),Y)_{t}

\displaystyle=\Psi_{g}(\phi_{H}(Y+v\mathds{1}_{[r,T]}),\phi_{G}(Y+v\mathds{1}_{[r,T]}),Y+v\mathds{1}_{[r,T]})_{t}-\Psi_{g}(\phi_{H}(Y),\phi_{G}(Y),Y)_{t}

\displaystyle=\Psi_{g}(H+D_{r,v}H,G+D_{r,v}G,Y+v\mathds{1}_{[r,T]})_{t}-\Psi_{g}(H,G,Y)_{t}.

∎

Finally, we give an illustrative example of the usage of our functional representation for calculating a Malliavin derivative, using Theorem
4.2
.

Example 4.3
.

We consider the SDE

X_{t}=\xi+\int_{0}^{t}g(X_{s-})\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}Y_{s},

(27)

where
\xi\in{\mathbb{R}}
,
g
is a Lipschitz continuous function, and
Y
is a pure-jump Lévy process with drift with compensator
\nu
satisfying
\int_{{\mathbb{R}}}{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}|x|^{2}}\nu(dx)<\infty
.
Then
X_{t}
is Malliavin differentiable for all
t\in[0,T]
by
[
6
, Theorem 17.4]
. Further Assumption
2.1
is satisfied and by Theorem
4.2
we know that

D_{r,v}X_{t}=\Psi_{g}(\xi,Y+v\mathds{1}_{t\in[r,T]})_{t}-\Psi_{g}(\xi,Y)_{t},

(28)

where
\Psi
does not depend on an additional process
G
since
g
does not.
By the universality of our functional representation we know

\Psi_{g}(\xi,Y+v\mathds{1}_{t\in[r,T]})=\widetilde{X}
, which satisfies the SDE

\widetilde{X}_{t}=\xi+\int_{0}^{t}g(\widetilde{X}_{s-})\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}(Y_{s}+v\mathds{1}_{s\in[r,T]}).

(29)

This implies

\displaystyle\widetilde{X}_{t}=\begin{cases}X_{t},&t\in[0,r),\\
X_{r}+g(X_{r-})v+\int_{r}^{t}g(\widetilde{X}_{s-})\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}Y_{s},&t\in[r,T].\end{cases}

(30)

Hence, it holds

D_{r,v}X_{t}=\Big(\int_{r}^{t}(g(\widetilde{X}_{s-})-g(X_{s-}))\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}Y_{s}+g(X_{r-})v\Big)\mathds{1}_{t\in[r,T]}.

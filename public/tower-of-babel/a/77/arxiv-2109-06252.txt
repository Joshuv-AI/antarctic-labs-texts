Abstract
. The category of mobi algebras has been introduced as a model to the unit interval of real numbers. The notion of mobi space over a mobi algebra has been proposed as a model for spaces with geodesic paths. In this paper we analyse the particular case of affine mobi spaces and show that there is an isomorphism of categories between R-modules and pointed affine mobi spaces over a mobi algebra R as soon as R is a unitary ring in which 2 2 is an invertible element.

1.
Introduction

This paper is the continuation of a program which aims at modelling spaces with geodesics as purely algebraic structures by considering here the particular case of affine spaces. A geodesic from a point
x
to a point
y
in a space
X
is in particular a path, that is, a curve
\gamma\colon{[0,1]\to X}
with
\gamma(0)=x
and
\gamma(1)=y
. The necessity of a suitable algebraic structure for the unit interval has lead us to the notion of mobi algebra
[
6
]
. The unit interval can be seen as a set
A
, together with three constants
0
,
\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}
,
1
and a ternary operation

p(a,b,c)=a-ba+bc

satisfying the following eight conditions:

(A1)

p(1,\text{$\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}$},0)=\text{$\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}$}

(A2)

p(0,a,1)=a

(A3)

p(a,b,a)=a

(A4)

p(a,0,b)=a

(A5)

p(b,1,a)=a

(A6)

p(a,\text{$\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}$},b)=p(a,\text{$\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}$},c)\implies b=c

(A7)

p(a,p(b_{1},b_{2},b_{3}),c)=p(p(a,b_{1},c),b_{2},p(a,b_{3},c))

(A8)

p(p(a_{1},b,c_{1}),\text{$\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}$},p(a_{2},b,c_{2}))=p(p(a_{1},\text{$\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}$},a_{2}),b,p(c_{1},\text{$\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}$},c_{2}))
.

This is precisely the definition of a mobility algebra (mobi algebra for short). That is, a mobi algebra is a system
(A,p,0,\text{$\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}$},1)
consisting of a set
A
, together with a ternary operation
p\colon{A^{3}\to A}
and three constants
0
,
\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}
and
1
that are required to satisfy the eight axioms above. We may form the category of mobi algebras by considering as morphisms the expected structure preserving maps. This means that if
(A,p,0,\text{$\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}$},1)
and
(A^{\prime},p^{\prime},0,\text{$\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}$},1)
are two mobi algebras then a morphism is a map
f\colon{A\to A^{\prime}}
such that

f(0)=0,\ f(\text{$\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}$})=\text{$\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}$},\ f(1)=1
, and
f(p(a,b,c))=p^{\prime}(f(a),f(b),f(c))
, for all
a,b,c\in A
. For example, the map

f:[0,1]\to[-1,5];\quad t\mapsto 6t-1

is a morphism from
([0,1],p,0,\frac{1}{2},1)
to
([-1,5],p^{\prime},-1,2,5)
with

p(a,b,c)=a+b(c-a)\quad\textrm{and}\quad p^{\prime}(a,b,c)=\frac{c+5a}{6}+b\,\frac{c-a}{6}.

Other examples are the inclusion of the unit interval into the real line or the projection of the mobi algebra considered in Example
6
of Section
4
into the unit interval.

If there exists an element

such that

then the structure

is a unitary ring with

(a proof can be found in

2.
Mobi algebra

In this section we briefly recall the notion of mobi algebra, introduced in
[
6
]
, and some of its basic properties. Several examples of different nature were presented in
[
6
]
. The motivating example of a mobi algebra is the unit interval with the three constants

and

In order to have an intuitive interpretation of its axioms we may consider a mobi algebra as a mobi space over itself and use the geometric intuition provided in section

Namely, that the operation

is the position of a particle moving from a point

to a point

at an instant

while following a geodesic path.

\displaystyle q(q(e,2\cdot\text{$\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}$},x),a,q(e,2\cdot\text{$\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}$},y))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle q(x,a,y).

∎

6.
The isomorphism between pointed affine mobi spaces and modules over a ring

We will now use the previous results to show that there is an isomorphism of categories between modules over a ring with
\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}
and pointed affine mobi spaces over a mobi algebra with
2
.

The category of modules over a ring (with
\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}
) is the category whose objects are pairs
(R,X_{R})
in which
R
is a unitary ring with
\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}
, the inverse of
2
, and

X_{R}=(X,+,e,\varphi)
is a module over
R
, as in the previous section. Morphisms are pairs
(f,g)
such that
f\colon{R\to R^{\prime}}
is a ring homomorphism and
g\colon{X\to X^{\prime}}
is such that

(23)

\displaystyle g(e)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle e

(24)

\displaystyle g(x+y)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle g(x)+g(y)

(25)

\displaystyle g(\varphi_{a}(x))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\varphi_{f(a)}(g(x)).

The category of pointed affine mobi spaces consists of pairs
(A,X_{A})
in which
A
is a mobi algebra with
2
, such that
p(0,\text{$\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}$},2)=1
, and
X_{A}=(X,q,e)
is an affine mobi space
(X,q)
over
A
together with an element
e\in X
considered as its origin. The morphisms in this category are pairs
(f,g)
such that
f\colon{A\to A^{\prime}}
is a morphism of mobi algebras and
g\colon{X\to X^{\prime}}
is such that

(26)

\displaystyle g(e)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle e

(27)

\displaystyle g(q(x,a,y))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle q^{\prime}(g(x),f(a),g(y)).

Note that the category of pointed affine mobi spaces can be seen as a slice category
(*\downarrow\textbf{Aff})
and it is an example of an additive category with kernels
[
3
,
4
]
.
We are now in position to prove our main result.

Theorem 6.1
.

There is an isomorphism of categories between modules over a ring with
\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}
and pointed affine mobi spaces over a mobi algebra with
2
.

Proof.

Theorem
5.1
gives us a functor from modules to pointed affine mobi spaces defined on objects. It remains to show that morphisms of modules are transformed into morphisms of pointed affine mobi spaces. Indeed, let
(f,g)
be a homomorphism of modules, then:

\displaystyle f\left(p(a,b,c)\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle f(a-b\cdot a+b\cdot c)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle f(a)-f(b)\cdot f(a)+f(b)\cdot f(c)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle p^{\prime}\left(f(a),f(b),f(c)\right)

\displaystyle g\left(q(x,a,y)\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle g\left(\varphi_{1-a}(x)+\varphi_{a}(y)\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle g\left(\varphi_{1-a}(x)\right)+g\left(\varphi_{a}(y)\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\varphi_{f(1-a)}(g(x))+\varphi_{f(a)}(g(y))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\varphi_{1-f(a)}(g(x))+\varphi_{f(a)}(g(y))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle q^{\prime}\left(g(x),f(a),g(y)\right).

Theorem
5.2
gives us a functor from pointed affine mobi spaces into modules over a ring defined on objects. It remains to show that morphisms of pointed affine mobi spaces are transformed into morphisms of modules over a ring. Let
(f,g)
be a morphism of affine mobi spaces with
2
. First, we observe that
f(2)=2
because, from the definition of
2
, we have
f\left(p(0,\text{$\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}$},2)\right)=f(1)
and consequently
p^{\prime}(0,\text{$\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}$},f(2))=1=p^{\prime}(0,\text{$\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}$},2)
which gives the result by application of Axiom
(A6)
. We then have:

\displaystyle f(a+b)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle f\left(p(0,2,p(a,\text{$\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}$},b))\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle p^{\prime}(0,2,p^{\prime}(f(a),\text{$\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}$},f(b)))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle f(a)+f(b)

\displaystyle f(a\cdot b)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle f\left(p(0,a,b)\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle p^{\prime}(0,f(a),f(b))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle f(a)\cdot f(b)

\displaystyle g\left(\varphi_{a}(x)\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle g\left(q(e,a,x)\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle q^{\prime}(e,f(a),g(x)))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\varphi^{\prime}_{f(a)}\left(g(x)\right)

\displaystyle g\left(x+y\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle g\left(q(e,2,q(x,\text{$\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}$},y))\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle q^{\prime}(e,2,q^{\prime}(g(x),\text{$\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}$},g(y)))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle g(x)+g(y).

Theorems
5.3
and
5.4
show that the two constructions are inverse to each other thus defining an isomorphism of categories.
∎

7.
Conclusion

There are several different ways to look at the notion of an affine space (see e.g.
[
1
,
2
,
10
]
). Roughly speaking it may be seen as a set of points in which every point can be chosen as the origin of a vector space. We have shown that in the case where the scalars (elements of the mobi algebra) form a field with characteristic different from
2
, our concept of affine mobi space is precisely an affine space in the usual sense. Furthermore, it is possible to consider a ring of scalars rather than a field and our results show that multiplication does not need to be commutative. In any case, the existence of
2
and
\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}
is required in order to define the module addition
x+y=q(e,2,q(x,\text{$\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}$},y))
and prove that an affine mobi space
(X,q)
admits a module structure
for every choice of an origin
e\in X
, with scalar multiplication
ax=q(e,a,x)
.

In
[
9
]
it is shown that the isomorphism between unitary rings with one-half and mobi algebras with one double is part of a more general connection between the categories of unitary rings and mobi algebras, thus suggesting that the previous result can be extended too. Another line of investigation which can be developed further is to analyse under which conditions the structure of an affine mobi space
(X,q)
, over a mobi algebra
A
, is determined by a mobi algebra homomorphism
f\colon{A\to\End(X)}
for an appropriate mobi algebra structure on the set of endomorphisms of
X
(see Proposition 2.1 in
[
7
]
).

An Evolution System for a Class of Age-Structured Diffusive Population Equations
Christoph Walker

Abstract
. Kato’s theory on the construction of strongly continuous evolution systems associated with hyperbolic equations is applied to the linear equation describing an age-structured population that is subject to time-dependent diffusion. The evolution system is used to provide conditions for the well-posedness of the corresponding quasilinear equation.

47D06, 35K90, 35M10, 92D25

1.
Introduction and Main Results

Problems of the form

\displaystyle\partial_{t}u+\partial_{a}u\,

\displaystyle=A(u,t,a)u\,,\qquad t\in(0,T]\,,\quad a\in(0,a_{m})\,,

(1.1a)

\displaystyle u(t,0)

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{a_{m}}b(a)\,u(t,a)\,\mathrm{d}a\,,\qquad t\in(0,T]\,,

(1.1b)

\displaystyle u(0,a)

\displaystyle=\phi(a)\,,\qquad a\in(0,a_{m})\,,

(1.1c)

arise as an abstract formulation of the evolution of
an age- and spatially structured population with density

u=u(t,a):[0,T]\times[0,a_{m})\rightarrow E_{0}\,.

The age of individuals is denoted by
a\in[0,a_{m})
with
maximal age
a_{m}\in(0,\infty]
. The spatial movement of individuals is described by the (usually: differential) operators

A(u,t,a):E_{1}\subset E_{0}\rightarrow E_{0}

which, in turn, may be influenced by the population itself.
We assume that these operators are generators of (analytic) semigroups on the Banach space
E_{0}
with domains
E_{1}
.
Age-dependent death processes are incorporated into the operators
A(u,t,a)
. Equation (
1.1b
) is the birth law with birth rate
b
, and
\phi
in (
1.1c
) is the initial population. We refer e.g. to
[
4
,
5
,
14
,
16
,
18
]
and the references therein for more details and concrete examples of age-structured diffusive population models.

Note that (
1.1
) has a quasilinear structure. Establishing well-posedness results for (
1.1
) naturally requires a prior investigation of the corresponding linear problem

\displaystyle\partial_{t}u+\partial_{a}u\,

\displaystyle=A(t,a)u\,,\qquad t\in(0,T]\,,\quad a\in(0,a_{m})\,,

(1.2a)

\displaystyle u(t,0)

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{a_{m}}b(a)\,u(t,a)\,\mathrm{d}a\,,\qquad t\in(0,T]\,,

(1.2b)

\displaystyle u(0,a)

\displaystyle=\phi(a)\,,\qquad a\in(0,a_{m})\,,

(1.2c)

with an operator
A=A(t,a)
depending on time
t
and possibly age
a
, but being independent of
u
. Allowing for a time dependence is essential for the study of quasilinear problems, the solvability of the latter is then a consequence. It is worth pointing out that a simultaneous dependence of the operator
A
on both variables
t
and
a
induces additional conceptual and technical difficulties making the problem more intricate as we shall see. This may be one of the reasons why there does not seem to be much literature on problem (
1.2
) (and (
1.1
)) involving a dependence on both variables
t
and
a
in the operator
A=A(t,a)
. Even for the time-independent case
A=A(a)
there is no broad literature, however, see
[
9
,
10
,
11
,
12
,
13
]
.
A time-dependence was actually included in
[
10
]
though rather in the birth process
b
than in the diffusion part
A
.
For the case of an operator
A
independent of time and age the corresponding literature list is more extensive, see for instance the references in
[
14
,
18
]
.

In this work we shall rely on the results of
[
17
]
where it was shown that the solution to (
1.2
) involving time-independent operators
A=A(a)
is given by a semigroup on the (biologically natural) phase space
\mathbb{E}_{0}:=L_{1}(J,E_{0})
(see (
1.8
) below) with a generator that is fully determinable.
Based on this result we shall prove herein that an evolution system can be associated with the time-dependent problem (
1.2
). This is done by rather direct computations verifying the conditions of Kato’s seminal theory
[
6
,
7
]
(see also
[
8
]
) on evolution equations of hyperbolic type. Moreover, we derive additional regularity properties of the evolution system and provide conditions for the existence of mild solutions to the quasilinear problem (
1.1
).

Assumptions and Notations

We shall first consider the linear problem (
1.2
) and list our assumptions for this case. Roughly speaking, we will assume that
A=A(t,a)
induces for fixed time
t
a (parabolic) evolution operator with respect to age
a
satisfying certain properties uniformly with respect to time
t
.

Set
J:=[0,a_{m}]
if
a_{m}<\infty
and
J:=[0,\infty)
if
a_{m}=\infty
. We let
E_{0}
be a real Banach space ordered by a closed convex cone
E_{0}^{+}
and
assume that
E_{1}
is a dense and continuously embedded subspace of
E_{0}
. We fix
T>0
and assume that there is
\rho>0
such that

A\in C\big([0,T],L_{\infty}\big(J,\mathcal{L}(E_{1},E_{0})\big)\big)\,,\qquad A(t)\in C^{\rho}\big(J,\mathcal{H}(E_{1},E_{0})\big)\,,\quad t\in[0,T]\,,

(1.3a)

where
\mathcal{H}(E_{1},E_{0})
is the open subset of
\mathcal{L}(E_{1},E_{0})
consisting of generators of analytic semigroups on
E_{0}
with domains
E_{1}
. It then follows from
[
1
, Corollary II.4.4.2]
that, for every
t\in[0,T]
fixed,
A(t)\in C^{\rho}\big(J,\mathcal{H}(E_{1},E_{0})\big)
generates a parabolic evolution operator

U_{A(t)}:\{(a,\sigma)\,;\,a\in J\,,\ 0\leq\sigma\leq a\}\to\mathcal{L}(E_{0})

on
E_{0}
with regularity subspace
E_{1}
in the sense of
[
1
, Section II.2]
.
We further assume that there are
M\geq 1
and
\varpi\in\mathbb{R}
such that, for fixed
t\in[0,T]
,

\|U_{A(t)}(a,\sigma)\|_{\mathcal{L}(E_{\ell})}+(a-\sigma)^{\ell}\,\|U_{A(t)}(a,\sigma)\|_{\mathcal{L}(E_{0},E_{\ell})}\leq Me^{\varpi(a-\sigma)}\,,\qquad a\in J\,,\quad 0\leq\sigma\leq a\,,

(1.3b)

for
\ell=0,1
and some
M\geq 1
(if
a_{m}<\infty
so that
J
is compact, this is automatically satisfied, see
[
1
, Lemma II.5.1.3]
), and that

\text{if $a_{m}=\infty$, then $\varpi<0$ in \eqref{EO}}\,.

(1.3c)

In order to associate with (
1.2
) an evolution system we shall further impose that the family
(U_{A(t)})_{t\in[0,T]}
is
stable
in
E_{\ell}
for
\ell\in\{0,1\}
in the sense that there are
M_{\ell}\geq 1
and
\omega_{\ell}\in\mathbb{R}
such that

\bigg\|\prod_{j=1}^{n}U_{A(t_{j})}(a_{j},\sigma_{j})\bigg\|_{\mathcal{L}(E_{\ell})}\leq M_{\ell}\exp\bigg\{\omega_{\ell}\sum_{j=1}^{n}(a_{j}-\sigma_{j})\bigg\}\,,\quad 0\leq\sigma_{j}\leq a_{j}<a_{m}\,,

(1.4)

for any finite sequence
0\leq t_{1}\leq t_{2}\leq\ldots\leq t_{n}\leq T
and
n=1,2,\ldots
.

The birth rate
b
is supposed to satisfy

b\in L_{\infty}\big(J,\mathcal{L}(E_{\ell})\big)\cap L_{1}\big(J,\mathcal{L}(E_{\ell})\big)\,,\quad\ell\in\{0,\vartheta,1\}\,,

(1.5)

where
E_{\vartheta}:=(E_{0},E_{1})_{\vartheta}
with parameter
\vartheta\in(0,1)
is a fixed interpolation space corresponding to an admissible interpolation functor
(\cdot,\cdot)_{\vartheta}
. Moreover, we assume that

b(a)U_{A(t)}(a,0)\in\mathcal{L}(E_{0})\ \text{is strongly positive for $a$ in a subset of $J$ of positive measure}\,.

(1.6)

We set

\|b\|_{\ell}:=\|b\|_{L_{\infty}(J,\mathcal{L}(E_{\ell}))}\,,\quad\ell\in\{0,\vartheta,1\}\,,

and write

A(t,a):=A(t)(a)\in\mathcal{H}(E_{1},E_{0})\,,\quad a\in J\,,\quad t\in[0,T]\,,

in the following.

Before continuing with the main results some remarks are in order concerning the imposed assumptions.

Remarks 1.1
.

(a)
That
A(t)=A(t,\cdot)
induces for fixed
t\in[0,T]
a parabolic evolution operator
U_{A(t)}
on
E_{0}
with regularity subspace
E_{1}
in the sense of
[
1
, Section II.2]
means in particular that

v(a):=U_{A(t)}(a,\sigma)v^{0}\,,\quad a\in[\sigma,a_{m})\,,

is, for given
\sigma\in[0,a_{m})
and
v^{0}\in E_{0}
, the unique solution

v\in C([\sigma,a_{m}),E_{0})\cap C^{1}((\sigma,a_{m}),E_{0})\cap C((\sigma,a_{m}),E_{1})

to the Cauchy problem

\partial_{a}v(a)=A(t,a)v(a)\,,\quad a\in(\sigma,a_{m})\,,\qquad v(\sigma)=v^{0}\,.

Moreover, given
w^{0}\in E_{0}
and
f\in\mathbb{E}_{0}=L_{1}(J,E_{0})
, the mild solution
w\in C(J,E_{0})
to

\partial_{a}w(a)=A(t,a)w(a)+f(a)\,,\quad a\in(0,a_{m})\,,\qquad w(0)=w^{0}\,,

is given by

w(a)=U_{A(t)}(a,0)w^{0}+\int_{0}^{a}U_{A(t)}(a,\sigma)\,f(\sigma)\,\mathrm{d}\sigma\,,\quad a\in J\,.

(b)
The stability assumption (
1.4
) corresponds to uniform resolvent estimates for the operator
A
(see
[
8
, Theorem 5.2.2]
,
[
1
, Section II.4.2]
) and reduces to the stability condition introduced in
[
8
, Definition 5.2.1, Theorem 5.2.2]
when
A(t)
is independent of age
a\in J
so that

U_{A(t)}(a,\sigma)=e^{(a-\sigma)A(t)}\,,\quad 0\leq\sigma\leq a\,,

is the analytic semigroup generated by
A(t)
.

(c)
For instance, assumption (
1.4
) is satisfied if
M=1
in (
1.3b
). In particular, it is satisfied if
A(t)
is independent of age and generates a contraction semigroup for every
t\in[0,T]
.

(d)
If
A\in C\big([0,T],BC^{1}\big(J,\mathcal{H}(E_{1},E_{0})\big)\big)
is such that

\|e^{sA(t,a)}\|_{\mathcal{L}(E_{0})}\leq e^{\omega s}\,,\quad s\geq 0\,,\quad(t,a)\in[0,T]\times J\,,

for some
\omega\in\mathbb{R}
, then
[
8
, Theorem 5.4.8]
(and the uniqueness of evolution operators) implies

\|U_{A(t)}(a,\sigma)\|_{\mathcal{L}(E_{0})}\leq e^{\omega(a-\sigma)}\,,\quad 0\leq\sigma\leq a<a_{m}\,,\quad t\in[0,T]\,,

and hence assumption (
1.4
) is satisfied for
\ell=0
.

(e)
Suppose that
(Q(t))_{t\in[0,T]}
is a familiy of isomorphisms from
E_{1}
to
E_{0}
such that

Q(t)A(t,\cdot)Q(t)^{-1}=A(t,\cdot)\,,\qquad\|Q(t)\|_{\mathcal{L}(E_{1},E_{0})}+\|Q(t)^{-1}\|_{\mathcal{L}(E_{0},E_{1})}\leq C

for every
t\in[0,T]
and such that
Q:[0,T]\to\mathcal{L}(E_{1},E_{0})
is of bounded variation. If (
1.4
) is satisfied for
\ell=0
, then it is satisfied also for
\ell=1
. This follows exactly as in
[
8
, Theorem 5.2.4]
. For instance, if
Q\in C^{1}([0,T],\mathrm{Isom}(E_{1},E_{0}))
, if
Q(t)
commutes with
A(t,\cdot)
, and if (
1.4
) is satisfied for
\ell=0
, then (
1.4
) is satisfied also for
\ell=1
. In particular, analogously to
[
8
, Theorem 5.4.8]
, if
A\in C^{1}([0,T],\mathcal{H}(E_{1},E_{0}))
is age-independent and (
1.4
) is satisfied for
\ell=0
, one may take
Q(t)=\lambda_{0}-A(t)
for some real
\lambda_{0}
sufficiently large (in the resolvent sets of
A(t)
) and conclude that (
1.4
) is satisfied also for
\ell=1
.

(f)
Assumption (
1.6
) is required to cite easily the results from
[
17
]
on the semigroup associated with time-independent operators
A=A(a)
, see Proposition
1.2
below.

Main Results

As mentioned previously, we first focus on the linear Cauchy problem (
1.2
). We begin by
fixing
t\in[0,T]
and considering for
v=v(s,a)
the linear problem

\displaystyle\partial_{s}v+\partial_{a}v\,

\displaystyle=A(t,a)v\,,\qquad s>0\,,\quad a\in(0,a_{m})\,,

(1.7a)

\displaystyle v(s,0)

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{a_{m}}b(a)\,v(s,a)\,\mathrm{d}a\,,\qquad s>0\,,

(1.7b)

\displaystyle v(0,a)

\displaystyle=\phi(a)\,,\qquad a\in(0,a_{m})\,.

(1.7c)

In
[
17
]
it was shown that the solution to (
1.7
) is determined by a strongly continuous semigroup
(\mathbb{S}_{t}(s))_{s\geq 0}
in
\mathbb{E}_{0}
, given by

\big[\mathbb{S}_{t}(s)\phi\big](a)\,:=\,\left\{\begin{aligned} &U_{A(t)}(a,a-s)\,\phi(a-s)\,,&&a\in J\,,\ 0\leq s<a\,,\\
&U_{A(t)}(a,0)\,B_{\phi}^{t}(s-a)\,,&&a\in J\,,\ s\geq a\,,\end{aligned}\right.

(1.8a)

for
\phi\in\mathbb{E}_{0}
, where
B_{\phi}^{t}
is the unique solution to the Volterra equation

\begin{split}B_{\phi}^{t}(s)\,&=\,\int_{0}^{s}\chi(a)\,b(a)\,U_{A(t)}(a,0)\,B_{\phi}^{t}(s-a)\,\mathrm{d}a\,\\
&\qquad+\,\int_{s}^{a_{m}}\chi(a)\,b(a)\,U_{A(t)}(a,a-s)\,\phi(a-s)\,\mathrm{d}a\,,\quad s\geq 0\,,\end{split}

(1.8b)

with
\chi
denoting the characteristic function of the interval
(0,a_{m})
. That is,
B_{\phi}^{t}
is such that

B_{\phi}^{t}(s)=\int_{0}^{a_{m}}b(a)\big[\mathbb{S}_{t}(s)\phi\big](a)\,\mathrm{d}a\,,\quad s\geq 0\,.

(1.9)

We recall the main properties of the semigroup
(\mathbb{S}_{t}(s))_{s\geq 0}
in the next proposition (for more details and further properties regarding regularity, compactness, and asynchronous exponential growth we refer to
[
17
]
).

Proposition 1.2
.

[
17
, Theorem 1.2, Theorem 1.4]

Suppose (
1.3
), (
1.5
), and (
1.6
). Let
t\in[0,T]
be fixed. There is

[\phi\mapsto B_{\phi}^{t}]\in\mathcal{L}\big(\mathbb{E}_{0},C(\mathbb{R}^{+},E_{0})\big)

(1.10)

such that
B_{\phi}^{t}=B_{\phi}^{t}(s)
is the unique solution to (
1.8b
) for
\phi\in\mathbb{E}_{0}
. Moreover:

(a)

(\mathbb{S}_{t}(s))_{s\geq 0}
defined in (
1.8a
) is a strongly continuous positive semigroup on
\mathbb{E}_{0}
with

\|\mathbb{S}_{t}(t)\|_{\mathcal{L}(\mathbb{E}_{0})}\leq M_{0}\,e^{(\varpi+\|b\|_{0}M_{0})t}\,,\quad t\geq 0\,.

(b)
Let
\mathbb{A}(t)
be the infinitesimal generator of the semigroup
(\mathbb{S}_{t}(s))_{s\geq 0}
. Then

\psi\in\mathrm{dom}(\mathbb{A}(t))
if and only if there exists
\phi\in\mathbb{E}_{0}
such that
\psi\in C(J,E_{0})\cap\mathbb{E}_{0}
is the mild solution to

\partial_{a}\psi=A(t,a)\psi+\phi(a)\,,\quad a\in J\,,\qquad\psi(0)=\int_{0}^{a_{m}}b(a)\psi(a)\,\mathrm{d}a\,.

In this case,
\mathbb{A}(t)\psi=-\phi
.

As a consequence, we can reformulate problem (
1.2
) as a linear Cauchy problem

u^{\prime}(t)=\mathbb{A}(t)u(t)\,,\quad t\in[s,T]\,,\qquad u(s)=\phi\,,

(1.11)

in the Banach space
\mathbb{E}_{0}
.
We aim at constructing an evolution system corresponding to this Cauchy problem. For this purpose we require more information on the (domain of the) generator
\mathbb{A}(t)
.

When replacing the space
\mathbb{E}_{0}=L_{1}(J,E_{0})
by
L_{p}(J,E_{0})
with
p\in(1,\infty)
and assuming the operators
A(t,\cdot)
to have maximal
L_{p}
-regularity, one can prove (see
[
15
]
for details) that the domain
\mathrm{dom}(\mathbb{A}(t))
is given by

\mathrm{dom}(\mathbb{A}(t))=\left\{\psi\in L_{p}(J,E_{1})\cap W_{p}^{1}(J,E_{0})\,;\,\psi(0)=\int_{0}^{a_{m}}b(a)\,\psi(a)\,\mathrm{d}a\right\}\,.

In particular, in this case the domain of the generator
\mathbb{A}(t)
is independent of
t\in[0,T]
which then allows one to apply directly the special case
[
8
, Theorem 5.4.8]
of Kato’s theory on hyperbolic evolution equations in order to deduce the existence of an evolution system in
L_{p}(J,E_{0})
associated with (
1.11
) (imposing time-differentiability on
A(t,\cdot)
).

However, in the biologically more relevant framework of
\mathbb{E}_{0}=L_{1}(J,E_{0})
and without the maximal regularity assumption on
A(t,\cdot)
, the domains
\mathrm{dom}(\mathbb{A}(t))
need not be independent of time and the construction of an evolution system becomes more involved.
In this regard, we shall prove that

\mathbb{Y}:=\left\{\psi\in\mathbb{E}_{1}\cap W_{1}^{1}(J,E_{0})\,;\,\psi(0)=\int_{0}^{a_{m}}b(a)\,\psi(a)\,\mathrm{d}a\right\}

with
\mathbb{E}_{1}:=L_{1}(J,E_{1})
is for every
t\in[0,T]
a core for
\mathbb{A}(t)
; that is, a dense subspace of
D(\mathbb{A}(t))
(i.e. the domain
\mathrm{dom}(\mathbb{A}(t))
endowed with its graph norm), see Lemma
2.1
below. In particular,
\mathbb{Y}
is dense in
\mathbb{E}_{0}
and

\mathbb{A}(t)\psi=-\partial_{a}\psi+A(t,\cdot)\psi\,,\quad\psi\in\mathbb{Y}\,.

We shall further prove that
\mathbb{Y}
is
\mathbb{A}(t)
-admissible
for
t\in[0,T]
, i.e. that
\mathbb{Y}
is an invariant subspace of
\mathbb{S}_{t}(s)
(see Lemma
2.2
) and the restriction
\big(\mathbb{S}_{t}(s)|_{\mathbb{Y}}\big)_{s\geq 0}
of
\big(\mathbb{S}_{t}(s)\big)_{s\geq 0}
to
\mathbb{Y}
is a strongly continuous semigroup on
\mathbb{Y}
(see Corollary
2.3
).
Moreover, relying on (
1.4
) we prove that the family
(\mathbb{A}(t))_{t\in[0,T]}
is
stable
in
\mathbb{E}_{0}
and in
\mathbb{E}_{1}
(see Proposition
3.1
). Unfortunately,
the family
(\mathbb{A}_{\mathbb{Y}}(t))_{t\in[0,T]}
of the parts of
\mathbb{A}(t)
in
\mathbb{Y}
does not seem to be stable in
\mathbb{Y}
(unless the operators
A=A(t)
are independent of
a\in J
) so that a significant condition required to apply Kato’s theory is lacking (see condition
(H_{2})
in
[
8
, Theorem 5.3.1]
). Yet we shall see that the stability in
\mathbb{E}_{1}
(and
\mathbb{E}_{0}
) is sufficient to copy Kato’s construction of evolution systems in form of the proof of
[
8
, Theorem 5.3.1]
almost verbatim. This relies on the observation that the right-hand side of the identity

\big(\mathbb{A}(t_{1})-\mathbb{A}(t_{2})\big)\psi=\big(A(t_{1},\cdot)-A(t_{2},\cdot)\big)\psi\,,\quad\psi\in\mathbb{Y}\,,\quad t_{1},t_{2}\in[0,T]\,,

is well-defined even if only
\psi\in\mathbb{E}_{1}
.
Our main result then reads:

Theorem 1.3
.

Suppose (
1.3
), (
1.4
), (
1.5
), and (
1.6
). Then, there is a unique evolution system
(\mathbb{U}_{\mathbb{A}}(t,s))_{0\leq s\leq t\leq T}
in
\mathbb{E}_{0}
associated with (
1.11
) in the sense that
\mathbb{U}_{\mathbb{A}}(t,s)\in\mathcal{L}(\mathbb{E}_{0})
satisfies

\mathbb{U}_{\mathbb{A}}(s,s)=I\,,\qquad\mathbb{U}_{\mathbb{A}}(t,s)=\mathbb{U}_{\mathbb{A}}(t,r)\mathbb{U}_{\mathbb{A}}(r,s)\,,\quad 0\leq s\leq r\leq t\leq T\,,

(1.12)

and

(t,s)\mapsto\mathbb{U}_{\mathbb{A}}(t,s)\ \text{ is strongly continuous in $\mathbb{E}_{0}$ on }\ 0\leq s\leq t\leq T\,.

(1.13)

Moreover,

\displaystyle\|\mathbb{U}_{\mathbb{A}}(t,s)\|_{\mathcal{L}(\mathbb{E}_{0})}\leq M_{0}e^{(\omega_{0}+M_{0}\|b\|_{0})(t-s)}\,,\quad 0\leq s\leq t\leq T\,,

(1.14)

\displaystyle\frac{\partial^{+}}{\partial t}\mathbb{U}_{\mathbb{A}}(t,s)\phi\big|_{t=s}=\mathbb{A}(s)\phi\,,\quad\phi\in\mathbb{Y}\,,\quad 0\leq s\leq T\,,

(1.15)

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial s}\mathbb{U}_{\mathbb{A}}(t,s)\phi=-\mathbb{U}_{\mathbb{A}}(t,s)\mathbb{A}(s)\phi\,,\quad\phi\in\mathbb{Y}\,,\quad 0\leq s\leq t\leq T\,.

(1.16)

In addition, given
\alpha\in[0,1)
denote by
E_{\alpha}:=[E_{0},E_{1}]_{\alpha}
the complex interpolation space and set
\mathbb{E}_{\alpha}:=L_{1}(J,E_{\alpha})
. Then
\mathbb{U}_{\mathbb{A}}(t,s)\in\mathcal{L}(\mathbb{E}_{\alpha})
for
0\leq s\leq t\leq T
with

\displaystyle\|\mathbb{U}_{\mathbb{A}}(t,s)\|_{\mathcal{L}(\mathbb{E}_{\alpha})}\leq M_{\alpha}e^{\eta(t-s)}\,,\quad 0\leq s\leq t\leq T\,,

(1.17)

for some
M_{\alpha}\geq 1
(depending on
M_{0},M_{1}
, and
\alpha
) and
\eta:=\max\{\omega_{0}+M_{0}\|b\|_{0}\,,\,\omega_{1}+M_{1}\|b\|_{1}\}
. Moreover,

(t,s)\mapsto\mathbb{U}_{\mathbb{A}}(t,s)\ \text{ is strongly continuous in $\mathbb{E}_{\alpha}$ on }\ 0\leq s\leq t\leq T\,.

(1.18)

The exponential bound (
1.14
) implies that the evolution system in
\mathbb{E}_{0}
is exponentially stable provided that
\omega_{0}+M_{0}\|b\|_{0}<0
. Also note that the second part of Theorem
1.3
(together with (
1.12
)) entails that
(\mathbb{U}_{\mathbb{A}}(t,s))_{0\leq s\leq t\leq T}
is an evolution system in
\mathbb{E}_{\alpha}
for every
\alpha\in[0,1)
.

In some cases one may derive additional information the evolution system:

Corollary 1.4
.

Let
a_{m}<\infty
and suppose (
1.3
), (
1.4
), (
1.5
), and (
1.6
). Let
p\in(1,\infty)
and consider
\theta\in[0,1]
. Let
(\cdot,\cdot)_{\theta}
be either the complex interpolation functor
[\cdot,\cdot]_{\theta}
or the real interpolation functor
(\cdot,\cdot)_{\theta,p}
and suppose that
E_{\theta}:=(E_{0},E_{1})_{\theta}
is reflexive.
Then there are constants
N_{0}\geq 1
and
\xi_{0}\in\mathbb{R}
(depending on
M_{\ell}
,
\omega_{\ell}
,
b
, and
\theta
) such that

\|\mathbb{U}_{\mathbb{A}}(t,s)\|_{\mathcal{L}(L_{p}(J,E_{\theta}))}\leq N_{0}e^{\xi_{0}(t-s)}\,,\quad 0\leq s\leq t\leq T\,.

Lemma 2.1
.

\mathbb{Y}
is a dense subspace of
\mathbb{E}_{0}
and of
D(\mathbb{A}(t))
for

. Moreover,

\mathbb{A}(t)\psi=-\partial_{a}\psi+A(t,\cdot)\psi\,,\quad\psi\in\mathbb{Y}\,,

and
\mathbb{A}\in C\big([0,T],\mathcal{L}(\mathbb{Y},\mathbb{E}_{0})\big)

Proof.

Fix
t\in[0,T]
. Since
\psi\in\mathbb{Y}
implies
\phi:=\partial_{a}\psi-A(t,\cdot)\psi\in\mathbb{E}_{0}
, we deduce
\mathbb{Y}\subset\mathrm{dom}(\mathbb{A}(t))
and (
2.5
) from Proposition
1.2
.
Thus, using (
2.3

\|\mathbb{A}(t)\psi\|_{\mathbb{E}_{0}}\leq\|\partial_{a}\psi\|_{\mathbb{E}_{0}}+\|A(t,\cdot)\|_{L_{\infty}(J,\mathcal{L}(E_{1},E_{0}))}\,\|\psi\|_{\mathbb{E}_{1}}\,,\quad\psi\in\mathbb{Y}\,,

(2.6)

so that
\mathbb{A}(t)\in\mathcal{L}(\mathbb{Y},\mathbb{E}_{0})
and

\|\mathbb{A}(t_{1})-\mathbb{A}(t_{2})\|_{\mathcal{L}(\mathbb{Y},\mathbb{E}_{0})}\leq\|A(t_{1},\cdot)-A(t_{2},\cdot)\|_{L_{\infty}(J,\mathcal{L}(E_{1},E_{0}))}\,,\quad t_{1},t_{2}\in[0,T]\,.

Now, (
1.3a
) entails
\mathbb{A}\in C\big([0,T],\mathcal{L}(\mathbb{Y},\mathbb{E}_{0})\big)
.
To prove that
\mathbb{Y}
is dense in
D(\mathbb{A}(t))
let
\psi\in D(\mathbb{A}(t))
and
\varepsilon>0
be arbitrary. Choose a real number
\lambda
in the resolvent set of
\mathbb{A}(t)
with
\lambda>\omega_{*}
, where
\omega_{*}:=\max\{\omega_{0},\omega_{1}\}
. Setting
\phi:=(\lambda-\mathbb{A}(t))\psi\in\mathbb{E}_{0}
there is
\phi_{\varepsilon}\in C_{c}(J,E_{\vartheta})
with

\|\phi_{\varepsilon}-\phi||_{\mathbb{E}_{0}}\leq\frac{\varepsilon}{\|(\lambda-\mathbb{A}(t))^{-1}\|_{\mathcal{L}(\mathbb{E}_{0},D(\mathbb{A}(t)))}}\,.

It then follows from Proposition
1.2
and Remark
1.1
(a) that
\psi_{\varepsilon}:=(\lambda-\mathbb{A}(t))^{-1}\phi_{\varepsilon}\in D(\mathbb{A}(t))
is given by

\psi_{\varepsilon}(a)=e^{-\lambda a}U_{A(t)}(a,0)\psi_{\varepsilon}(0)+\int_{0}^{a}e^{-\lambda(a-\sigma)}U_{A(t)}(a,\sigma)\phi_{\varepsilon}(\sigma)\,\mathrm{d}\sigma\,,\quad a\in J\,,

with

\psi_{\varepsilon}(0)=\int_{0}^{a_{m}}b(a)\,\psi_{\varepsilon}(a)\,\mathrm{d}a\,.

In particular,
\psi_{\varepsilon}(0)\in E_{\vartheta}
since, using the regularizing effects (
1.3b
) (and interpolation),

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{a_{m}}\|b(a)\,\psi_{\varepsilon}(a)\|_{E_{\vartheta}}\,\mathrm{d}a

\displaystyle\leq\|b\|_{\vartheta}\int_{0}^{a_{m}}e^{-\lambda a}\|U_{A(t)}(a,0)\|_{\mathcal{L}(E_{0},E_{\vartheta})}\,\mathrm{d}a\,\|\psi_{\varepsilon}(0)\|_{E_{0}}

\displaystyle\quad+\|b\|_{\vartheta}\int_{0}^{a_{m}}\|\phi_{\varepsilon}(\sigma)\|_{E_{\vartheta}}\int_{\sigma}^{a_{m}}e^{-\lambda(a-\sigma)}\|U_{A(t)}(a,\sigma)\|_{\mathcal{L}(E_{\vartheta})}\,\mathrm{d}a\,\mathrm{d}\sigma

\displaystyle\leq c(\vartheta)\int_{0}^{a_{m}}a^{-\vartheta}\,e^{a(\omega_{*}-\lambda)}\,\mathrm{d}a\,\|\psi_{\varepsilon}(0)\|_{E_{0}}

\displaystyle\quad+c(\vartheta)\int_{0}^{a_{m}}\|\phi_{\varepsilon}(\sigma)\|_{E_{\vartheta}}\int_{\sigma}^{a_{m}}e^{(a-\sigma)(\omega_{*}-\lambda)}\,\mathrm{d}a\,\mathrm{d}\sigma

with finite right-hand side since
\phi_{\varepsilon}\in C_{c}(J,E_{\vartheta})\subset L_{1}(J,E_{\vartheta})
and due to
\vartheta\in(0,1)
and
\lambda>\omega_{*}
. A similar computation yields then
\psi_{\varepsilon}\in\mathbb{E}_{1}
since

\displaystyle\|\psi_{\varepsilon}\|_{\mathbb{E}_{1}}

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{a_{m}}\|\psi_{\varepsilon}(a)\|_{E_{1}}\,\mathrm{d}a

\displaystyle\leq\int_{0}^{a_{m}}e^{-\lambda a}\|U_{A(t)}(a,0)\|_{\mathcal{L}(E_{\vartheta},E_{1})}\,\mathrm{d}a\,\|\psi_{\varepsilon}(0)\|_{E_{\vartheta}}

\displaystyle\quad+\int_{0}^{a_{m}}\|\phi_{\varepsilon}(\sigma)\|_{E_{\vartheta}}\int_{\sigma}^{a_{m}}e^{-\lambda(a-\sigma)}\|U_{A(t)}(a,\sigma)\|_{\mathcal{L}(E_{\vartheta},E_{1})}\,\mathrm{d}a\,\mathrm{d}\sigma

\displaystyle\leq c(\vartheta)\int_{0}^{a_{m}}a^{\vartheta-1}\,e^{a(\omega_{*}-\lambda)}\,\mathrm{d}a\,\|\psi_{\varepsilon}(0)\|_{E_{\vartheta}}

\displaystyle\quad+c(\vartheta)\int_{0}^{a_{m}}\|\phi_{\varepsilon}(\sigma)\|_{E_{\vartheta}}\int_{\sigma}^{a_{m}}(a-\sigma)^{\vartheta-1}\,e^{(a-\sigma)(\omega_{*}-\lambda)}\,\mathrm{d}a\,\mathrm{d}\sigma

with finite right-hand side since
\psi_{\varepsilon}(0)\in E_{\vartheta}
,
\phi_{\varepsilon}\in C_{c}(J,E_{\vartheta})
, and due to
\vartheta\in(0,1)
and
\lambda>\omega_{*}
. This and (
2.3
) imply that

\partial_{a}\psi_{\varepsilon}=(A(t,\cdot)-\lambda)\psi_{\varepsilon}+\phi_{\varepsilon}\in\mathbb{E}_{0}

and hence

. By construction,

\|\psi_{\varepsilon}-\psi||_{D(\mathbb{A}(t))}\leq\|(\lambda-\mathbb{A}(t))^{-1}\|_{\mathcal{L}(\mathbb{E}_{0},D(\mathbb{A}(t)))}\,\|\phi_{\varepsilon}-\phi||_{\mathbb{E}_{0}}\leq\varepsilon

which yields the density of
\mathbb{Y}
in

. Since the latter is dense in

(as the domain of the generator

), we also have that
\mathbb{Y}
is dense in
\mathbb{E}_{0}
.

\displaystyle=A(t,a)\big[\mathbb{S}_{t}(s)\psi\big](a)-\big[\mathbb{S}_{t}(s)\big(\mathbb{A}(t)\psi\big)\big](a)\,.

Gathering the two preceding identities and recalling (
2.9
) and (
2.11
) yields

\partial_{a}\big[\mathbb{S}_{t}(s)\psi\big]=A(t,\cdot)\mathbb{S}_{t}(s)\psi-\mathbb{S}_{t}(s)\big(\mathbb{A}(t)\psi\big)\in\mathbb{E}_{0}\,.

(2.14)

It now follows from (
2.14
), (
2.7
), (
1.9
), and (
1.8a
) that
\mathbb{S}_{t}(s)\psi\in\mathbb{Y}
.
Consequently,
\mathbb{Y}
is an invariant subspace of
\mathbb{S}_{t}(s)
for
s\geq 0
and (
2.14
), (
2.6
), and (
2.2
) imply that
\mathbb{S}_{t}(s)|_{\mathbb{Y}}\in\mathcal{L}(\mathbb{Y})
.
∎

We now verify that
\mathbb{Y}
is
\mathbb{A}(t)
-admissible
in the sense of
[
8
, Definition 4.5.3]
for
t\in[0,T]
; that is,
\mathbb{Y}
is an invariant subspace of
\mathbb{S}_{t}(s)
as just shown in Lemma
2.2
, and the restriction
\big(\mathbb{S}_{t}(s)|_{\mathbb{Y}}\big)_{s\geq 0}
is a strongly continuous semigroup on
\mathbb{Y}
. Regarding its generator we recall that the part
\mathbb{A}_{\mathbb{Y}}(t)
of
\mathbb{A}(t)
in
\mathbb{Y}
is defined as

\mathrm{dom}(\mathbb{A}_{\mathbb{Y}}(t)):=\big\{\phi\in\mathrm{dom}(\mathbb{A}(t))\cap\mathbb{Y}\,;\,\mathbb{A}(t)\phi\in\mathbb{Y}\big\}\,,\quad\mathbb{A}_{\mathbb{Y}}(t)\phi:=\mathbb{A}(t)\phi\,.

With these notations we have:

Corollary 2.3
.

Let
t\in[0,T]
. The restriction
\big(\mathbb{S}_{t}(s)|_{\mathbb{Y}}\big)_{s\geq 0}
is a strongly continuous semigroup on
\mathbb{Y}
with generator
\mathbb{A}_{\mathbb{Y}}(t)
. Moreover,
\big(\mathbb{S}_{t}(s)|_{\mathbb{E}_{1}}\big)_{s\geq 0}
is a strongly continuous semigroup on
\mathbb{E}_{1}
.

Proof.

(i)
It readily follows from Lemma
2.2
and Proposition
1.2
that
\big(\mathbb{S}_{t}(s)|_{\mathbb{E}_{1}}\big)_{s\geq 0}
is a semigroup on
\mathbb{E}_{1}
. For the strong continuity

\displaystyle\|\mathbb{S}_{t}(s)\psi-\psi\|_{\mathbb{E}_{1}}\to 0\ \text{ as }\ s\to 0

(2.15)

for
\psi\in\mathbb{E}_{1}
we use that

U_{A(t)}\in C\big(J_{\Delta},\mathcal{L}_{s}(E_{1})\big)\,,\qquad J_{\Delta}:=\{(a,\sigma)\in J\times J\,;\,\sigma\leq a\}\,,

according to
[
1
, Theorem II.4.4.1, Corollary II.4.4.2]
and (
1.3a
), and prove (
2.15
) analogously to the strong continuity in
\mathbb{E}_{0}
as in
[
18
, Theorem 4]
(using (
1.5
) for
\ell=1
).

(ii)
It follows from Lemma
2.2
and Proposition
1.2
that
\mathbb{S}_{t}(s)|_{\mathbb{Y}}\in\mathcal{L}(\mathbb{Y})
for
s\geq 0
so that we only have to check the strong continuity. To this end, let
\psi\in\mathbb{Y}
. Then

\displaystyle\|\mathbb{S}_{t}(s)\psi-\psi\|_{\mathbb{Y}}

\displaystyle=\|\partial_{a}\mathbb{S}_{t}(s)\psi-\partial_{a}\psi\|_{\mathbb{E}_{0}}+\|\mathbb{S}_{t}(s)\psi-\psi\|_{\mathbb{E}_{1}}\,.

(2.16)

For the first term on the right-hand side of (
2.16
) we use (
2.14
), (
2.11
), and (
2.2
) to get

\displaystyle\|\partial_{a}\mathbb{S}_{t}(s)\psi-\partial_{a}\psi\|_{\mathbb{E}_{0}}

\displaystyle\leq\|A(t,\cdot)\mathbb{S}_{t}(s)\psi-A(t,\cdot)\psi\|_{\mathbb{E}_{0}}+\|\mathbb{S}_{t}(s)(\mathbb{A}(t)\psi)-\mathbb{A}(t)\psi\|_{\mathbb{E}_{0}}

\displaystyle\leq\|A(t,\cdot)\|_{L_{\infty}(J,\mathcal{L}(E_{1},E_{0}))}\,\|\mathbb{S}_{t}(s)\psi-\psi\|_{\mathbb{E}_{1}}+\|\mathbb{S}_{t}(s)(\mathbb{A}(t)\psi)-\mathbb{A}(t)\psi\|_{\mathbb{E}_{0}}\,.

(2.17)

Since the second term on the right-hand side of (
2
) goes to zero as
s\to 0
due to the strong continuity of
(\mathbb{S}_{t}(s))_{s\geq 0}
in
\mathbb{E}_{0}
, it follows from (
2.15
), (
2.16
), and (
2
) that
\mathbb{S}_{t}(s)\psi\to\psi
in
\mathbb{Y}
as
s\to 0
.
∎

Combining Corollary
2.3
and Lemma

we deduce that
\mathbb{Y}
is dense in

.

Corollary 2.4
.

\mathbb{Y}
is dense in

.

Proof.

is a strongly continuous semigroup on

(for fixed

\phi_{\tau}:=\frac{1}{\tau}\int_{0}^{\tau}\mathbb{S}_{t}(s)\phi\,\mathrm{d}s\to\phi\ \text{ in }\ \mathbb{E}_{1}\ \text{ as }\ \tau\to 0

with

3.

A key ingredient in Kato’s construction of an evolution system is the

(see
[
8
, Definition 5.2.1]
). We prove this property in
\mathbb{E}_{0}
and in
\mathbb{E}_{1}
which turns out to be sufficient in our particular case. Actually, we shall employ the notation of stability in the equivalent formulation (see
[
8
, Theorem 5.2.2]
) of the associated family of semigroups
(\mathbb{S}_{t})_{t\in[0,T]}
as stated in the next proposition.

For the remainder of this paper we also assume (
1.4
). That is, we impose (
1.3
), (
1.4
), (
1.5
), and (
1.6
) to hold from now on.

Proposition 3.1
.

The family

(\mathbb{A}(t))_{t\in[0,T]}
is a stable family in
\mathbb{E}_{\ell}
for
\ell\in\{0,1\}
with constants
M_{\ell}
and
\omega_{\ell}+\|b\|_{\ell}M_{\ell}
; that is,

\bigg\|\prod_{j=1}^{n}\mathbb{S}_{t_{j}}(s_{j})\bigg\|_{\mathcal{L}(\mathbb{E}_{\ell})}\leq M_{\ell}\exp\bigg\{\big(\omega_{\ell}+\|b\|_{\ell}M_{\ell}\big)\sum_{j=1}^{n}s_{j}\bigg\}\,,\quad s_{j}\geq 0\,,

(3.1)

for any finite sequence
0\leq t_{1}\leq t_{2}\leq\ldots\leq t_{n}\leq T
and
n=1,2,\ldots
.

Proof.

Let
\phi\in\mathbb{E}_{\ell}
.
Observe first from (
1.8
) that, for
0\leq t_{1}\leq t_{2}\leq T
,
s_{1},s_{2}\geq 0
, and
a\in J
,

\displaystyle\big[\mathbb{S}_{t_{2}}(s_{2})

\displaystyle\mathbb{S}_{t_{1}}(s_{1})\phi\big](a)\,=\,\left\{\begin{aligned} &U_{A(t_{2})}(a,a-s_{2})\,\big[\mathbb{S}_{t_{1}}(s_{1})\phi\big](a-s_{2})\,,&&s_{2}<a\,,\\
&U_{A(t_{2})}(a,0)\,B_{\mathbb{S}_{t_{1}}(s_{1})\phi}^{t_{2}}(s_{2}-a)\,,&&a\leq s_{2}\,,\end{aligned}\right.

\displaystyle=\,\left\{\begin{aligned} &U_{A(t_{2})}(a,a-s_{2})\,U_{A(t_{1})}(a-s_{2},a-s_{2}-s_{1})\,\phi(a-s_{2}-s_{1})\,,&&s_{1}+s_{2}<a\,,\\
&U_{A(t_{2})}(a,a-s_{2})\,U_{A(t_{1})}(a-s_{2},0)\,B_{\phi}^{t_{1}}(s_{1}+s_{2}-a)\,,&&s_{2}<a\leq s_{1}+s_{2}\,,\\
&U_{A(t_{2})}(a,0)\,B_{\mathbb{S}_{t_{1}}(s_{1})\phi}^{t_{2}}(s_{2}-a)\,,&&a\leq s_{2}\,.\end{aligned}\right.

In fact, for
n\geq 2
,
0\leq t_{1}\leq t_{2}\leq\ldots\leq t_{n}\leq T
,
s_{j}\geq 0
, and
a\in J
one shows inductively the product formula

\displaystyle\bigg[\prod_{j=1}^{n}\mathbb{S}_{t_{j}}(s_{j})\phi\bigg](a)

\displaystyle=\,\left\{\begin{aligned} &\prod_{j=1}^{n}U_{A(t_{j})}\bigg(a-\sum_{l=j+1}^{n}s_{l}\,,\,a-\sum_{l=j}^{n}s_{l}\bigg)\,\phi\bigg(a-\sum_{l=1}^{n}s_{l}\bigg)\,,&&\hskip-8.53581pt\sum_{l=1}^{n}s_{l}<a\,,\\
&\prod_{j=k}^{n}U_{A(t_{j})}\bigg(a-\sum_{l=j+1}^{n}s_{l}\,,\,\bigg(a-\sum_{l=j}^{n}s_{l}\bigg)_{+}\bigg)\,B_{\prod_{i=1}^{k-1}\mathbb{S}_{t_{i}}(s_{i})\phi}^{t_{k}}\bigg(\sum_{l=k}^{n}s_{l}-a\bigg)\,,&&\hskip-8.53581pt\sum_{l=k+1}^{n}s_{l}<a\leq\sum_{l=k}^{n}s_{l}\,,\\
&&&\quad k=1,\ldots,n\,,\end{aligned}\right.

with the understanding that here and in the following

\sum_{l=n+1}^{n}s_{l}:=0\,,\qquad\prod_{i=1}^{0}\mathbb{S}_{t_{i}}(s_{i})\phi:=\phi\,.

(3.2)

We verify (
3.1
) by induction with respect to
n\geq 1
and establish simultaneously that

\left\|B_{\prod_{i=1}^{n-1}\mathbb{S}_{t_{i}}(s_{i})\phi}^{t_{n}}(s)\right\|_{E_{\ell}}\leq\|b\|_{\ell}\,M_{\ell}\exp\bigg\{\big(\omega_{\ell}+\|b\|_{\ell}M_{\ell}\big)\bigg(s+\sum_{j=1}^{n-1}s_{j}\bigg)\bigg\}\,\|\phi\|_{\mathbb{E}_{\ell}}\,,\quad s\geq 0\,.

(3.3)

To start with note from (
1.8
), (
1.4
), and Gronwall’s Inequality (see
[
17
]
or the computation below) that

\|B_{\phi}^{t_{1}}(s)\|_{E_{\ell}}\leq\|b\|_{\ell}\,M_{\ell}\,e^{(\omega_{\ell}+\|b\|_{\ell}M_{\ell})s}\,\|\phi\|_{\mathbb{E}_{\ell}}\,,\quad s\geq 0\,,

and hence

\|\mathbb{S}_{t_{1}}(s_{1})\|_{\mathcal{L}(\mathbb{E}_{\ell})}\leq M_{\ell}\,e^{(\omega_{\ell}+\|b\|_{\ell}M_{\ell})s_{1}}\,,\quad s_{1}\geq 0\,.

That is, (
3.1
) and (
3.3
) hold true for
n=1
. We now continue by induction for
n\geq 2
and suppose (
3.1
) and (
3.3
) to be true for
n-1
. To shorten notation set

z(s):=B_{\prod_{i=1}^{n-1}\mathbb{S}_{t_{i}}(s_{i})\phi}^{t_{n}}(s)\,,\quad s\geq 0\,.

Then, it follows from (
1.8b
) and the above product formula for
\prod_{i=1}^{n-1}\mathbb{S}_{t_{i}}(s_{i})\phi
that

\displaystyle z(s)=\int_{0}^{s}\chi(a)\,b(a)\,U_{A(t_{n})}(a,0)\,z(s-a)\,\mathrm{d}a

\displaystyle\qquad\quad+\int_{s}^{a_{m}}\chi(a)\,b(a)\,U_{A(t_{n})}(a,a-s)\,\bigg[\prod_{i=1}^{n-1}\mathbb{S}_{t_{i}}(s_{i})\phi\bigg](a-s)\,\mathrm{d}a

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{s}\chi(a)\,b(a)\,U_{A(t_{n})}(a,0)\,z(s-a)\,\mathrm{d}a

\displaystyle\quad+\sum_{k=1}^{n-1}\int_{s+\sum_{l=k+1}^{n-1}s_{l}}^{s+\sum_{l=k}^{n-1}s_{l}}\chi(a)\,b(a)\,U_{A(t_{n})}(a,a-s)\,\prod_{j=k}^{n-1}U_{A(t_{j})}\bigg(a-s-\sum_{l=j+1}^{n-1}s_{l}\,,\,\bigg(a-s-\sum_{l=j}^{n-1}s_{l}\bigg)_{+}\bigg)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\times B_{\prod_{i=1}^{k-1}\mathbb{S}_{t_{i}}(s_{i})\phi}^{t_{k}}\bigg(s+\sum_{l=k}^{n-1}s_{l}-a\bigg)\,\mathrm{d}a

\displaystyle\quad+\int_{s+\sum_{l=1}^{n-1}s_{l}}^{a_{m}}\chi(a)\,b(a)\,U_{A(t_{n})}(a,a-s)\,\prod_{j=1}^{n-1}U_{A(t_{j})}\bigg(a-s-\sum_{l=j+1}^{n-1}s_{l}\,,\,a-s-\sum_{l=j}^{n-1}s_{l}\bigg)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\times\phi\bigg(a-s-\sum_{l=1}^{n-1}s_{l}\bigg)\,\mathrm{d}a\,.

Taking the norm in
E_{\ell}
and using the stability assumption (
1.4
) (and recalling convention (
3.2
)) yields

\displaystyle\|z(s)\|_{E_{\ell}}

\displaystyle\leq\|b\|_{\ell}\,M_{\ell}\int_{0}^{s}e^{\omega_{\ell}a}\,\|z(s-a)\|_{E_{\ell}}\,\mathrm{d}a

\displaystyle\quad+\|b\|_{\ell}\,M_{\ell}\sum_{k=1}^{n-1}\int_{s+\sum_{l=k+1}^{n-1}s_{l}}^{s+\sum_{l=k}^{n-1}s_{l}}e^{\omega_{\ell}a}\,\bigg\|B_{\prod_{i=1}^{k-1}\mathbb{S}_{t_{i}}(s_{i})\phi}^{t_{k}}\bigg(s+\sum_{l=k}^{n-1}s_{l}-a\bigg)\bigg\|_{E_{\ell}}\,\mathrm{d}a

\displaystyle\quad+\|b\|_{\ell}\,M_{\ell}\exp\bigg\{\omega_{\ell}\bigg(s+\sum_{j=1}^{n-1}s_{j}\bigg)\bigg\}\,\int_{s+\sum_{l=1}^{n-1}s_{l}}^{a_{m}}\bigg\|\phi\bigg(a-s-\sum_{l=1}^{n-1}s_{l}\bigg)\bigg\|_{E_{\ell}}\,\mathrm{d}a\,.

We next invoke (
3.3
) for
n-1
to deduce

\displaystyle\|z(s)\|_{E_{\ell}}

\displaystyle\leq\|b\|_{\ell}\,M_{\ell}\int_{0}^{s}e^{\omega_{\ell}a}\,\|z(s-a)\|_{E_{\ell}}\,\mathrm{d}a

\displaystyle\quad+\big(\|b\|_{\ell}\,M_{\ell}\big)^{2}\sum_{k=1}^{n-1}\int_{s+\sum_{l=k+1}^{n-1}s_{l}}^{s+\sum_{l=k}^{n-1}s_{l}}e^{\omega_{\ell}a}\,\exp\bigg\{\big(\omega_{\ell}+\|b\|_{\ell}\,M_{\ell}\big)\bigg(s+\sum_{j=1}^{n-1}s_{j}-a\bigg)\bigg\}\,\mathrm{d}a\,\|\phi\|_{\mathbb{E}_{\ell}}

\displaystyle\quad+\|b\|_{\ell}\,M_{\ell}\,\exp\bigg\{\omega_{\ell}\bigg(s+\sum_{j=1}^{n-1}s_{j}\bigg)\bigg\}\,\|\phi\|_{\mathbb{E}_{\ell}}\,.

Computing further we derive

\displaystyle\|z(s)\|_{E_{\ell}}

\displaystyle\leq\|b\|_{\ell}\,M_{\ell}\int_{0}^{s}e^{\omega_{\ell}a}\,\|z(s-a)\|_{E_{\ell}}\,\mathrm{d}a

\displaystyle\quad+\big(\|b\|_{\ell}\,M_{\ell}\big)^{2}\,\exp\bigg\{\big(\omega_{\ell}+\|b\|_{\ell}M_{\ell}\big)\bigg(s+\sum_{j=1}^{n-1}s_{j}\bigg)\bigg\}\int_{s}^{s+\sum_{l=1}^{n-1}s_{l}}e^{-\|b\|_{\ell}M_{\ell}a}\,\mathrm{d}a\,\|\phi\|_{\mathbb{E}_{\ell}}

\displaystyle\quad+\|b\|_{\ell}\,M_{\ell}\,\exp\bigg\{\omega_{\ell}\bigg(s+\sum_{j=1}^{n-1}s_{j}\bigg)\bigg\}\,\|\phi\|_{\mathbb{E}_{\ell}}

\displaystyle=\|b\|_{\ell}\,M_{\ell}\int_{0}^{s}e^{\omega_{\ell}(s-a)}\,\|z(a)\|_{E_{\ell}}\,\mathrm{d}a

\displaystyle\quad-\|b\|_{\ell}\,M_{\ell}\,\exp\bigg\{\big(\omega_{\ell}+\|b\|_{\ell}M_{\ell}\big)\bigg(s+\sum_{j=1}^{n-1}s_{j}\bigg)\bigg\}\,\bigg(e^{-\|b\|_{\ell}M_{\ell}a}\bigg|_{a=s}^{a=s+\sum_{l=1}^{n-1}s_{l}}\bigg)\,\|\phi\|_{\mathbb{E}_{\ell}}

\displaystyle\quad+\|b\|_{\ell}\,M_{\ell}\,\exp\bigg\{\omega_{\ell}\bigg(s+\sum_{j=1}^{n-1}s_{j}\bigg)\bigg\}\,\|\phi\|_{\mathbb{E}_{\ell}}\,.

Therefore, simplifying the last two terms we get

\displaystyle\|z(s)\|_{E_{\ell}}

\displaystyle\leq\|b\|_{\ell}\,M_{\ell}\int_{0}^{s}e^{\omega_{\ell}(s-a)}\,\|z(a)\|_{E_{\ell}}\,\mathrm{d}a

\displaystyle\quad+\|b\|_{\ell}\,M_{\ell}\,e^{\omega_{\ell}s}\,\exp\bigg\{\big(\omega_{\ell}+\|b\|_{\ell}M_{\ell}\big)\sum_{j=1}^{n-1}s_{j}\bigg\}\,\|\phi\|_{\mathbb{E}_{\ell}}

for
s\geq 0
and thus infer from Gronwall’s Inequality that indeed

\displaystyle\left\|B_{\prod_{i=1}^{n-1}\mathbb{S}_{t_{i}}(s_{i})\phi}^{t_{n}}(s)\right\|_{E_{\ell}}

\displaystyle=\|z(s)\|_{E_{\ell}}

\displaystyle\leq\|b\|_{\ell}\,M_{\ell}\exp\bigg\{\big(\omega_{\ell}+\|b\|_{\ell}M_{\ell}\big)\bigg(s+\sum_{j=1}^{n-1}s_{j}\bigg)\bigg\}\,\|\phi\|_{\mathbb{E}_{\ell}}

for
s\geq 0
as claimed in (
3.3
). Next, we turn to (
3.1
) still assuming it to be true for
n-1
. The above product formula for
\prod_{j=1}^{n}\mathbb{S}_{t_{j}}(s_{j})\phi
yields

\displaystyle\bigg\|\prod_{j=1}^{n}

\displaystyle\mathbb{S}_{t_{j}}(s_{j})\phi\bigg\|_{\mathbb{E}_{\ell}}=\int_{0}^{a_{m}}\bigg\|\bigg[\prod_{j=1}^{n}\mathbb{S}_{t_{j}}(s_{j})\phi\bigg](a)\bigg\|_{E_{\ell}}\,\mathrm{d}a

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{k=1}^{n}\int_{\sum_{l=k+1}^{n}s_{l}}^{\sum_{l=k}^{n}s_{l}}\bigg\|\prod_{j=k}^{n}U_{A(t_{j})}\bigg(a-\sum_{l=j+1}^{n}s_{l}\,,\,\bigg(a-\sum_{l=j}^{n}s_{l}\bigg)_{+}\bigg)\bigg\|_{\mathcal{L}(E_{\ell})}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\times\bigg\|B_{\prod_{i=1}^{k-1}\mathbb{S}_{t_{i}}(s_{i})\phi}^{t_{k}}\bigg(\sum_{l=k}^{n}s_{l}-a\bigg)\bigg\|_{E_{\ell}}\,\mathrm{d}a

\displaystyle\quad+\int_{\sum_{l=1}^{n}s_{l}}^{a_{m}}\bigg\|\prod_{j=1}^{n}U_{A(t_{j})}\bigg(a-\sum_{l=j+1}^{n}s_{l}\,,\,a-\sum_{l=j}^{n}s_{l}\bigg)\bigg\|_{\mathcal{L}(E_{\ell})}\,\bigg\|\phi\bigg(a-\sum_{l=1}^{n}s_{l}\bigg)\bigg\|_{E_{\ell}}\,\mathrm{d}a

if
\sum_{l=1}^{n}s_{l}<a_{m}
(otherwise the last integral vanishes). We now first use (
1.4
) and then (
3.3
) to get

\displaystyle\bigg\|\prod_{j=1}^{n}\mathbb{S}_{t_{j}}(s_{j})\phi\bigg\|_{\mathbb{E}_{\ell}}

\displaystyle\leq M_{\ell}\,\sum_{k=1}^{n}\int_{\sum_{l=k+1}^{n}s_{l}}^{\sum_{l=k}^{n}s_{l}}e^{\omega_{\ell}a}\,\bigg\|B_{\prod_{i=1}^{k-1}\mathbb{S}_{t_{i}}(s_{i})\phi}^{t_{k}}\bigg(\sum_{l=k}^{n}s_{l}-a\bigg)\bigg\|_{E_{\ell}}\,\mathrm{d}a

\displaystyle\qquad+M_{\ell}\,\exp\bigg\{\omega_{\ell}\sum_{l=1}^{n}s_{l}\bigg\}\,\|\phi\|_{\mathbb{E}_{\ell}}

\displaystyle\leq\|b\|_{\ell}\,M_{\ell}^{2}\,\sum_{k=1}^{n}\int_{\sum_{l=k+1}^{n}s_{l}}^{\sum_{l=k}^{n}s_{l}}e^{\omega_{\ell}a}\,\exp\bigg\{\big(\omega_{\ell}+\|b\|_{\ell}M_{\ell}\big)\bigg(\sum_{i=1}^{n}s_{i}-a\bigg)\bigg\}\,\mathrm{d}a\,\|\phi\|_{\mathbb{E}_{\ell}}

\displaystyle\qquad+M_{\ell}\,\exp\bigg\{\omega_{\ell}\sum_{l=1}^{n}s_{l}\bigg\}\,\|\phi\|_{\mathbb{E}_{\ell}}

\displaystyle=\|b\|_{\ell}\,M_{\ell}^{2}\,\exp\bigg\{\big(\omega_{\ell}+\|b\|_{\ell}M_{\ell}\big)\sum_{i=1}^{n}s_{i}\bigg\}\,\int_{0}^{\sum_{l=1}^{n}s_{l}}e^{-\|b\|_{\ell}\,M_{\ell}a}\,\mathrm{d}a\,\|\phi\|_{\mathbb{E}_{\ell}}

\displaystyle\qquad+M_{\ell}\,\exp\bigg\{\omega_{\ell}\sum_{l=1}^{n}s_{l}\bigg\}\,\|\phi\|_{\mathbb{E}_{\ell}}

\displaystyle\leq M_{\ell}\,\exp\bigg\{\big(\omega_{\ell}+\|b\|_{\ell}M_{\ell}\big)\sum_{i=1}^{n}s_{i}\bigg\}\,\|\phi\|_{\mathbb{E}_{\ell}}\,.

This is (
3.1
) and the proof of Proposition
3.1
is complete.
∎

For the additional information stated in Corollary
1.4
we shall also prove a similar estimate in
L_{p}(J,E_{\ell})
.

Corollary 3.2
.

Let
\ell\in\{0,1\}
. Given
p\in(1,\infty)
there are
N\geq 1
and
\xi\in\mathbb{R}
(depending on
M_{\ell}
,
\omega_{\ell}
,
b
, and
p
) such that

\bigg\|\prod_{j=1}^{n}\mathbb{S}_{t_{j}}(s_{j})\phi\bigg\|_{L_{p}(J,E_{\ell})}\leq N\exp\bigg\{\xi\sum_{j=1}^{n}s_{j}\bigg\}\,\max\big\{\|\phi\|_{L_{p}(J,E_{\ell})},\|\phi\|_{\mathbb{E}_{\ell}}\big\}\,,\quad s_{j}\geq 0\,,

(3.4)

for every
\phi\in\mathbb{E}_{\ell}\cap L_{p}(J,E_{\ell})
and any finite sequence
0\leq t_{1}\leq t_{2}\leq\ldots\leq t_{n}\leq T
and
n=1,2,\ldots
.

Proof.

Let
\phi\in\mathbb{E}_{\ell}\cap L_{p}(J,E_{\ell})
. The product formula for
\prod_{j=1}^{n}\mathbb{S}_{t_{j}}(s_{j})\phi
entails

\displaystyle\bigg\|\prod_{j=1}^{n}

\displaystyle\mathbb{S}_{t_{j}}(s_{j})\phi\bigg\|_{L_{p}(J,E_{\ell})}^{p}=\int_{0}^{a_{m}}\bigg\|\bigg[\prod_{j=1}^{n}\mathbb{S}_{t_{j}}(s_{j})\phi\bigg](a)\bigg\|_{E_{\ell}}^{p}\,\mathrm{d}a

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{k=1}^{n}\int_{\sum_{l=k+1}^{n}s_{l}}^{\sum_{l=k}^{n}s_{l}}\bigg\|\prod_{j=k}^{n}U_{A(t_{j})}\bigg(a-\sum_{l=j+1}^{n}s_{l}\,,\,\bigg(a-\sum_{l=j}^{n}s_{l}\bigg)_{+}\bigg)\bigg\|_{\mathcal{L}(E_{\ell})}^{p}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\times\bigg\|B_{\prod_{i=1}^{k-1}\mathbb{S}_{t_{i}}(s_{i})\phi}^{t_{k}}\bigg(\sum_{l=k}^{n}s_{l}-a\bigg)\bigg\|_{E_{\ell}}^{p}\,\mathrm{d}a

\displaystyle\quad+\int_{\sum_{l=1}^{n}s_{l}}^{a_{m}}\bigg\|\prod_{j=1}^{n}U_{A(t_{j})}\bigg(a-\sum_{l=j+1}^{n}s_{l}\,,\,a-\sum_{l=j}^{n}s_{l}\bigg)\bigg\|_{\mathcal{L}(E_{\ell})}^{p}\,\bigg\|\phi\bigg(a-\sum_{l=1}^{n}s_{l}\bigg)\bigg\|_{E_{\ell}}^{p}\,\mathrm{d}a

if
\sum_{l=1}^{n}s_{l}<a_{m}
, otherwise the last integral vanishes. Using (
1.4
) and (
3.3
) we get as before

\displaystyle\bigg\|\prod_{j=1}^{n}\mathbb{S}_{t_{j}}(s_{j})\phi\bigg\|_{L_{p}(J,E_{\ell})}^{p}

\displaystyle\leq M_{\ell}^{p}\,\sum_{k=1}^{n}\int_{\sum_{l=k+1}^{n}s_{l}}^{\sum_{l=k}^{n}s_{l}}e^{p\omega_{\ell}a}\,\bigg\|B_{\prod_{i=1}^{k-1}\mathbb{S}_{t_{i}}(s_{i})\phi}^{t_{k}}\bigg(\sum_{l=k}^{n}s_{l}-a\bigg)\bigg\|_{E_{\ell}}^{p}\,\mathrm{d}a

\displaystyle\qquad+M_{\ell}^{p}\,\exp\bigg\{p\omega_{\ell}\sum_{l=1}^{n}s_{l}\bigg\}\,\|\phi\|_{L_{p}(J,E_{\ell})}^{p}

\displaystyle\leq\|b\|_{\ell}^{p}\,M_{\ell}^{2p}\,\exp\bigg\{p\big(\omega_{\ell}+\|b\|_{\ell}M_{\ell}\big)\sum_{i=1}^{n}s_{i}\bigg\}\,\int_{0}^{\sum_{l=1}^{n}s_{l}}e^{-p\|b\|_{\ell}\,M_{\ell}a}\,\mathrm{d}a\,\|\phi\|_{\mathbb{E}_{\ell}}^{p}

\displaystyle\qquad+M_{\ell}^{p}\,\exp\bigg\{p\omega_{\ell}\sum_{l=1}^{n}s_{l}\bigg\}\,\|\phi\|_{L_{p}(J,E_{\ell})}^{p}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{p}\,\|b\|_{\ell}^{p-1}\,M_{\ell}^{2p-1}\,\exp\bigg\{p\big(\omega_{\ell}+\|b\|_{\ell}M_{\ell}\big)\sum_{i=1}^{n}s_{i}\bigg\}\,\|\phi\|_{\mathbb{E}_{\ell}}^{p}

\displaystyle\qquad-\frac{1}{p}\,\|b\|_{\ell}^{p-1}\,M_{\ell}^{2p-1}\,\,\exp\bigg\{p\omega_{\ell}\sum_{l=1}^{n}s_{l}\bigg\}\,\|\phi\|_{\mathbb{E}_{\ell}}^{p}

\displaystyle\qquad+M_{\ell}^{p}\,\exp\bigg\{p\omega_{\ell}\sum_{l=1}^{n}s_{l}\bigg\}\,\|\phi\|_{L_{p}(J,E_{\ell})}^{p}\,.

This yields the claim.

Construction of the Evolution System

Given

\mathbb{A}_{n}(t):=\mathbb{A}(t_{k}^{n})\,,\quad t_{k}^{n}\leq t<t_{k+1}^{n}\,,\quad k=0,\ldots,n-1\,,\qquad\mathbb{A}_{n}(T):=\mathbb{A}(T)\,.

Recall the notation

and set analogously

A_{n}(t):=A(t_{k}^{n})\,,\quad t_{k}^{n}\leq t<t_{k+1}^{n}\,,\quad k=0,\ldots,n-1\,,\qquad A_{n}(T):=A(T)\,.

Since

2.4
), this definition yields

\|A(t)-A_{n}(t)\|_{\mathcal{L}(\mathbb{E}_{1},\mathbb{E}_{0})}\to 0\ \text{ as $n\to\infty$\,, uniformly in $t\in[0,T]$}\,.

(4.3)

We then define the family of operators
(\mathbb{U}_{n}(t,s))_{0\leq s\leq t\leq T}
in
\mathcal{L}(\mathbb{E}_{0})\cap\mathcal{L}(\mathbb{E}_{1})
by

\begin{split}\mathbb{U}_{n}(t,s):=&\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\mathbb{S}_{t_{j}^{n}}(t-s)\,,&t_{j}^{n}\leq s\leq t\leq t_{j+1}^{n}\,,\\
\mathbb{S}_{t_{k}^{n}}\big(t-t_{k}^{n}\big)\,\Big[\prod_{j=l+1}^{k-1}\mathbb{S}_{t_{j}^{n}}\left(\frac{T}{n}\right)\Big]\,\mathbb{S}_{t_{l}^{n}}\big(t_{l+1}^{n}-s\big)\,,&k>l\,,\ t_{k}^{n}\leq t\leq t_{k+1}^{n}\,,\end{array}\right.\\
&\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad t_{l}^{n}\leq s\leq t_{l+1}^{n}\,.\end{split}

Then
(\mathbb{U}_{n}(t,s))_{0\leq s\leq t\leq T}
is an evolution system satisfying

\mathbb{U}_{n}(s,s)=I\,,\qquad\mathbb{U}_{n}(t,s)=\mathbb{U}_{n}(t,r)\mathbb{U}_{n}(r,s)\,,\quad 0\leq s\leq r\leq t\leq T\,,

(4.5)

and

(t,s)\mapsto\mathbb{U}_{n}(t,s)\ \text{ is strongly continuous in $\mathbb{E}_{0}$ on }\ 0\leq s\leq t\leq T\,.

(4.6)

Moreover, it follows from Proposition
3.1
that

\displaystyle\|\mathbb{U}_{n}(t,s)\|_{\mathcal{L}(\mathbb{E}_{\ell})}\leq M_{\ell}e^{(\omega_{\ell}+M_{\ell}\|b\|_{\ell})(t-s)}\,,\quad 0\leq s\leq t\leq T\,,\quad\ell=0,1\,,

while Lemma

\displaystyle\mathbb{U}_{n}(t,s)\mathbb{Y}\subset\mathbb{Y}\,,\quad 0\leq s\leq t\leq T\,.

Since

(4.9)

\displaystyle\|\mathbb{U}_{n}(t,s)

\mathbb{U}_{\mathbb{A}}(t,s)\psi
in
\mathbb{E}_{0}
according to (
4.13
).
Since
L_{p}(J,E_{\theta})
is reflexive due to

and our assumption that

is reflexive, there is a subsequence

that converges weakly in

to

by the Theorem of Eberlein-Smulyan, and hence, invoking (

),

\|\mathbb{U}_{\mathbb{A}}(t,s)\psi\|_{L_{p}(J,E_{\theta})}\leq\liminf_{k\to\infty}\|\mathbb{U}_{n_{k}}(t,s)\psi\|_{L_{p}(J,E_{\theta})}\leq Ne^{\xi(t-s)}\,\|\psi\|_{L_{p}(J,E_{\theta})}

for
\psi\in L_{p}(J,E_{\xi})
. This proves (
1.19
). As for (
1.20
) we note that, given

there is

with

. Then

due to Lemma

1.20

.∎

Endomorphism property of vertex operator algebras over arbitrary fields
Chao Yang, Jianqi Liu

Abstract
In this paper, we study the endomorphism properties of vertex operator algebras over an arbitrary field 𝔽 \mathbb{F} , with Char ​ ( 𝔽 ) ≠ 2 \text{Char}(\mathbb{F})\neq 2 . Let V V be a strongly finitely generated vertex operator algebra over 𝔽 \mathbb{F} , and M M be an irreducible admissible V V -module. We prove that every element in End V ​ ( M ) \text{End}_{V}(M) is algebraic over 𝔽 \mathbb{F} and that End V ​ ( M ) \text{End}_{V}(M) is also finite-dimensional. As an application, we prove Schur’s lemma for strongly finitely generated vertex operator algebras over arbitrary algebraically closed fields, and we give a test for absolute irreducibility of V V -modules.

Introduction

The study of the endomorphism properties for associative algebras has a long history and rich results(see
[
D
,
DS
,
MR
,
Q
]
etc.).
If the associative algebra
A
is defined over an algebraically closed field
and
S
is a finite dimensional simple
A
-module, the famous Schur’s Lemma tells us that
\mathrm{End}_{A}(S)
is one dimensional.
However, when the base field
\mathbb{F}
is not algebraically closed, or
\text{dim}_{F}(S)
is not finite,

\mathrm{End}_{A}(S)
is not so easy to characterize. One of the most elegant results, in this case, is Quillen’s Theorem
[
Q
]
, which says that each element in
\text{End}_{A}(S)
is algebraic over
\mathbb{F}
, provided that
A
is a filtered algebra whose associated graded algebra
\text{gr}(A)
is finitely generated and commutative.

In some special cases, the endomorphism ring
\text{End}_{A}(S)
is finite-dimensional. For example, if
A=U(\mathfrak{g})
is the universal enveloping algebra of a finite dimensional lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
over a field
\mathbb{F}
with
\text{char}(\mathbb{F})=0
, it is shown in
[
D
]
that
\text{End}_{A}(S)
is finite dimensional; if
A
is a constructible algebra over an arbitrary field
\mathbb{F}
, it is shown in
[
MR
]
that
\text{End}_{A}(S)
is also finite dimensional. Moreover, if
S
is a topologically irreducible admissible Banach space representation
of a
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
-analytic group
G
over
\mathbb{F}
, where
\mathbb{F}
is a finite extension of
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
, then it is shown in
[
DS
]
that the algebra
\rm{End}_{\mathbb{F}[G]}(S)
of continuous
G
-equivariant
endomorphisms of
S
is a finite dimensional division algebra over
\mathbb{F}
. An associative algebra over
\mathbb{F}
such that
\mathrm{End}_{A}(S)
is finite-dimensional for all simple modules
S
is said to admit the (strong) endomorphism property.

In the theory of vertex operator algebras (VOAs) (
[
DLM1
,
LL
]
etc.), when the base field is
{\mathbb{C}}
, the “infinite dimensional Schur’s Lemma" due to Dixmier
[
D
]
which says that
\mathrm{End}_{A}(M)={\mathbb{C}}\text{Id}_{M}
if
M
is of countable dimension is commonly used in the proof of the semisimplicity of the Hamiltonian
L(0)
on an irreducible admissible module
M
over a vertex operator algebra
V
(cf.
[
DLM
,
DLM1
,
Z
]
.) In recent years, the vertex operator algebras defined over an arbitrary field
\mathbb{F}
has brought much attention. A series of studies on vertex operator algebras over arbitrary fields can be found in (
[
DR1
,
DR2
,
R
,
JLM
,
LM1
,
LM2
]
etc.) However, since the usual Schur’s lemma no longer holds when
\mathbb{F}
is not uncountable, a natural question is what kind of endomorphism properties are possessed by a vertex operator algebra over an arbitrary field
\mathbb{F}
. The following Theorem is our main result.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
V
be a strongly finitely generated vertex operator algebra over an arbitrary field
\mathbb{F}
,
and
M
be an irreducible admissible
V
-module. Then

(1)

Every element in
\text{End}_{V}(M)
is algebraic over
\mathbb{F}.

Moreover,
\text{End}_{V}(M)
is also finite-dimensional over
\mathbb{F}
.

(2)

(Schur’s lemma) If
\mathbb{F}
is an algebraic closed field, then
\text{End}_{V}(M)\cong\mathbb{F}
, and
there exists a
\lambda\in\mathbb{F}
such that
L(0)|_{M(n)}=\lambda+n
for all
n
.

(3)

M
is an absolutely irreducible
V
-module if and only if
\text{End}_{V}(M)\cong\mathbb{F}.

In this theorem, part (1) can be viewed as the analog of Quillen’s theorem in the context of the vertex operator algebras. Schur’s lemma and the characterization of the absolutely irreducible modules are natural corollaries of Quillen’s theorem in (1). Although we imposed the condition that
V
is strongly finitely generated in Theorem
1.1
, we note that this is not a strong condition. In fact, almost all the classic examples of vertex operator algebras over an arbitrary field, including the irrational ones, like vacuum module VOA
V_{\hat{\mathfrak{g}}}(k,0)
and the (universal) Virasoro VOA
\bar{V}(c,0)

[
FZ
]
, are strongly finitely generated
[
DR2
,
JLM
,
LM1
]
. Furthermore, for a strongly finitely generated VOA

, many examples indicate that the orbifold
V^{G}
of a reductive group
G\leq\mathrm{Aut}(V)
is also strongly finitely generated (see
[
LA
,
CL
]
.)
With Theorem
1.1
, we can prove that the conjecture proposed in
[
DR1
]
, which states that the Hamiltonian
L(0)
acts semi-simply on an irreducible admissible
M
without the uncountable condition for
\mathbb{F}
is indeed true.

To prove Theorem

, we use the (generalized) Zhu’s algebra

defined in
[

,

,

]
and transform the study of

into

-modules. We will also use the fact that there exists an epimorphism between Zhu’s

-algebra

[

,

.
More precisely, we have the following results toward the proof of Theorem

:

Theorem 1.2
.

(1)

Let

be a vertex operator algebra over a field
\mathbb{F}
, and let

Then
\text{End}_{V}(M)\cong\text{End}_{A(V)}(M(0))

(2)

Let

Then

(3)

Let

Moreover,

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle=(1\otimes v)_{\mathrm{wt}(v)+i}(k_{1}\otimes m_{1}+\cdots+k_{p}\otimes m_{p})

for the operator

on any weak

Theorem 4.2
.

(
[

,

]
)
Let

be a vertex operator algebra over a field

, and let

be an admissible

For any

(4)

.

Remark 4.3
.

In

Theorem 4.4
.

(

,

]
)
Let

, and let

.

.

Let

be a vertex operator algebra over a field

, and let

Then for any

Corollary 4.7
.

Let
V
be a vertex operator algebra over a field
\mathbb{F}
. If

is an irreducible admissible

-module,
then
\text{End}_{V}(M)\cong\text{End}_{A_{n}(V)}(M(n))
for any

Proof.

Define a linear map
\varphi:\text{End}_{V}(M)\rightarrow\text{End}_{A_{n}(V)}(M(n)),

\varphi(F):=F|_{M(n)}
,
for any

. Since

Let

, by Theorem

This shows that

∎

Next, we recall Quillen’s theorem for finite-type filtered algebras and prove some related results under field extensions.

Definition 4.8
.

An associative algebra
A
over a field
\mathbb{F}
is called filtered if
it is endowed with an increasing filtration:

0=F_{-1}A\subset F_{0}A\subset F_{1}A\subset\cdots
by
\mathbb{F}
-subspaces such that

(1)

1_{A}\in F_{0}A,

(2)

\ F_{p}A\cdot F_{q}A\subset F_{p+q}A
for all
p,q\geq 0,

(3)

A=\bigcup_{p\geq 0}F_{p}A.

Let
A
be a filtered algebra. Set
\text{gr}A=\oplus_{n=0}^{\infty}F_{n}A/F_{n-1}A=\bigoplus_{n=0}^{\infty}(\text{gr}A)_{n}
.
The multiplication in
A
gives rise to a well-defined product:

(F_{p}A/F_{p-1}A)\times(F_{q}A/F_{q-1}A)\rightarrow F_{p+q}A/F_{p+q-1}A,\ \ (\bar{a},\bar{b})\mapsto\overline{a\cdot b},

for all
a,b\in V
, making
\text{gr}A
a graded associative algebra.

Definition 4.9
.

A filtered algebra
A
over a field
\mathbb{F}
is called finite-type if
\text{gr}A
is a finitely generated commutative algebra.

Lemma 4.10
.

(Quillen’s theorem)
Let
A
be a finite-type filtered algebra over a field
\mathbb{F}
. Then
A
has the endomorphism property. i.e., if
M
is a simple
A
-module, then every element in
\text{End}_{A}(M)
is algebraic over
\mathbb{F}
.

The following Proposition, which is a stronger version of Quillen’s theorem, might be well-known, but
we cannot find any reference to this version. So we present the proof for it in the rest of this section.

Proposition 4.11
.

Let
A
be a finite-type filtered algebra over
\mathbb{F}
. Then
A
has the strong endomorphism property. i.e.,
if
M
is a simple
A
-module, then
\text{dim}_{\mathbb{F}}\text{End}_{A}(M)<\infty.

Remark 4.12
.

In the case when
\text{Char}(\mathbb{F})=0
and
A=U(\mathfrak{g})
the universal enveloping algebra of a finite dimensional Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
over
\mathbb{F}
,
Proposition
4.11
is proved in
[
D
, Proposition 2.6.9]
.
On the other hand, when
\mathbb{F}
is of arbitrary characteristic and

is a constructible algebra over
\mathbb{F}
, Proposition
4.11
is proved in
[
MR
, Theorem 9.5.5]
. Note that
U(\mathfrak{g})
is a constructible algebra when
\mathfrak{g}
is a finite-dimensional Lie algebra over
\mathbb{F}
.

In our case, we cannot assume that
\text{gr}A
is generated by
F_{1}A/F_{0}A
, since we will let

be Zhu’s algebra
A(V)
later. So

might not be a constructible algebra.
But we can still prove Proposition
4.11
using some techniques in
[

]
.

Lemma 4.13
.

(
[
MR
]
)
Let
D
be a division ring whose center is
\mathbb{K}
, and let
\mathbb{E}
be a maximal subfield of
D
.
If
\text{dim}_{\mathbb{K}}(\mathbb{E})<\infty
, then
\text{dim}_{\mathbb{E}}(D)<\infty
and
\text{dim}_{\mathbb{K}}(D)<\infty.

The proof of the following Lemma is fairly standard (see
[
MR
]
).

Lemma 4.14
.

If
A
is a filtered algebra such that
\text{gr}(A)
is left noetherian, then
A
is left noteherian.

The next Lemma shows that the finite-type property and the notherianess are preserved under field extensions.

Lemma 4.15
.

Let
A
be a finite-type filtered algebra over a field
\mathbb{F}
. Let
\mathbb{K}/\mathbb{F}
be a field extension. Then

(1)

A^{\mathbb{K}}
is also a finite-type filtered algebra.

(2)

A^{\mathbb{K}}
is noetherian. In particular,
A
is noetherian.

Proof.

Let
0=F_{-1}A\subset F_{0}A\subset F_{1}A\subset\cdots
be a filtration of
A
.
Then

0=\mathbb{K}\otimes F_{-1}A\subset\mathbb{K}\otimes F_{0}A\subset\mathbb{K}\otimes F_{1}A\subset\cdots

is a filtration of
A^{\mathbb{K}}
, here
\otimes
means
\otimes_{\mathbb{F}}
.
It is clear that
\text{gr}(A^{\mathbb{K}})\cong\mathbb{K}\otimes_{\mathbb{F}}\text{gr}(A)
.
Hence
\text{gr}(A^{\mathbb{K}})
is a finitely generated commutative algebra.
Consequently,
A^{\mathbb{K}}
is a finite-type filtered algebra and
\text{gr}A^{\mathbb{K}}
is left noetherian.
By Lemma
4.14
,
A^{\mathbb{K}}
is left noetherian.

To prove
A^{\mathbb{K}}
is right noetherian, we consider the opposite ring
(A^{\mathbb{K}})^{\text{op}}
of
A^{\mathbb{K}}
.
Note that
A^{\mathbb{K}}
is right noetherian if and only if
(A^{\mathbb{K}})^{\text{op}}
is left noetherian.
It is easy to verify that
(F_{n}A^{\mathbb{K}})_{n=0}^{\infty}
is also a filtration of
(A^{\mathbb{K}})^{\text{op}}
, where
F_{n}A^{\mathbb{K}}=K\otimes_{\mathbb{F}}F_{n}(A)
for each
n\geq 0
.
Since
\text{gr}A^{\mathbb{K}}
is commutative,

\text{gr}((A^{\mathbb{K}})^{\text{op}})\cong\text{gr}A^{\mathbb{K}}
is a finitely generated commutative algebra.
Therefore,
(A^{\mathbb{K}})^{\text{op}}
is left noetherian, and so
A^{\mathbb{K}}
is right noetherian. The proof is complete.
∎

The following lemma is the key to prove Proposition
4.11
.

Lemma 4.16
.

(
[
MR
]
)
Let
A
be a
\mathbb{F}
-algebra,
M
be a finite generated
A
-module, and
\mathbb{K}
be a subfield of
\text{End}_{A}(M)
containing
\mathbb{F}
.
If
A^{\mathbb{K}}
is left noetherian, then
\mathbb{K}
is a finitely generated field extension of
\mathbb{F}
.

We now return to the proof of Proposition
4.11
.

Proof of Proposition
4.11
.

Let
M
be a simple
A
-module and

D=\text{End}_{A}(M)
. Then
D
is a division ring. Let
\mathbb{K}
be the center of
D
, and let
\mathbb{E}
be a maximal subfield of

.

Note that
\mathbb{F}\leq\mathbb{K}\leq\mathbb{E}.
By Lemma
4.15
,
A^{\mathbb{E}}
is left noetherian.
Since

is a simple

-module, it is finitely generated. Then by Lemma
4.16
,
\mathbb{E}
is a finitely-generated field extension of
\mathbb{F}
.
Moreover, every element in

is algebraic over

by Quillen’s theorem, then

is a finitely generated algebraic extension, hence

is a finite extension.
In particular,

and
\text{dim}_{\mathbb{F}}(\mathbb{K})<\infty
.
By Lemma

, we have

. Consequently,

, and the proof is complete.
∎

5
Strong endomorphism property of
A(V)

Using the results from previous sections, we can prove that Zhu’s algebra
A(V)
of a strongly finitely generated vertex operator algebra
V
over an arbitrary field
\mathbb{F}
satisfies the strong endomorphism property.

To proof this theorem, we first review the relations between the commutative algebras
R(V)
and
\text{gr}A(V)
studied in
[
A
,
L1
,
L2
,
Liu
,
Z
]
.

Let
V
be a vertex operator algebra over a field
\mathbb{F}
.
Recall that
R(V)=V/C_{2}(V)
is a commutative Poisson algebra (cf.
[
Z
]
), with the identity element
\bar{{\bf 1}}
, under the product and Lie bracket:

\overline{u}\cdot\overline{v}=\overline{u_{-1}v},\qquad\{\bar{u},\bar{v}\}=\overline{u_{0}v},\quad\text{for}\ u,v\in V,

(5.1)

where
\overline{u}
represents the image of
u
in
R(V)
.

For any
n\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{+}
, we define

F_{n}(A(V))=A(V)_{n}=(V_{0}+\cdots+V_{n}+O(V))/O(V)\subseteq A(V)
(cf.
[
Z
]
).
It is easy to verify that
(A(V)_{n})_{n=0}^{\infty}
is a filtration of
A(V)
.
Hence
A(V)
is a filtrated algebra. Moreover, by the following property of
A(V)
:

u\ast v-v\ast u\equiv\sum_{i\geq 0}\binom{\mathrm{wt}u}{i}u_{i}v\pmod{O(V)},

we can show that
\text{gr}A(V)
is also a commutative Poisson algebra
[
A
,
Liu
]
. Define a linear map
\phi:R(V)\rightarrow\text{gr}A(V)
by letting:

\phi(\bar{u}):=u+A(V)_{n-1}\in A(V)_{n}/A(V)_{n-1},

for
n\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{+}
and
u\in V_{n}
.
Then
\phi
is a well-defined epimorphism between the commutative Poisson algebras
R(V)
and
\text{gr}A(V)
(see
[
CJL
,
Liu
]
).

The following lemma is well-known (see
[
A
,
L1
,
L2
]
). We include the proof of it for the completeness.

Lemma 5.1
.

Let
V
be a strongly finitely generated vertex operator algebra over
\mathbb{F}
. Then:

(1)

R(V)
is a finitely generated commutative algebra.
In particular,
R(V)
is noetherian.

(2)

A(V)
is a finite-type filtered algebra.

Proof.

(1) Let
U
be a finite set that strongly generates
V
. Since

a_{-n}b_{-2}c=b_{-2}a_{-n}c+\sum_{j\geq 0}\binom{-n}{j}(a_{j}b)_{-n-2-j}c\equiv b_{-2}a_{-n}c\pmod{C_{2}(V)},

for any
a,b,c\in V
and
n\geq 0
. It follows that for any spanning element
u^{1}_{-n_{1}}\dots u^{r}_{-n_{r}}{\bf 1}
of
V
, where
u^{i}\in U
and
n_{i}\geq 1
for all
i
, if there exists some
i
such that
n_{i}\geq 2
, then
u^{1}_{-n_{1}}\dots u^{r}_{-n_{r}}{\bf 1}\in C_{2}(V)
. Then by (
5.1
), we have
R(V)
is generated by
(U+C_{2}(V))/C_{2}(V)
, and so
R(V)
is noetherian by Hilbert’ basis theorem.

(2) Since
\phi
is an epimorphism between the commutative algebras
R(V)
and
\text{gr}A(V)
,
it follows that
\text{gr}A(V)
as a commutative algebra is also finitely generated, and so
A(V)
is a finite-type filtered algebra.
∎

Now we prove the main theorem of this section:

Theorem 5.2
.

If
V
is a strongly finitely generated vertex operator algebra over a field
\mathbb{F}
, and
\mathbb{K}/\mathbb{F}
is a field extension, then

A(V)^{\mathbb{K}}
has the strong endomorphism property. In particular,
A(V)
has the strong endomorphism property.

Proof.

Because
V
is strongly finitely generated,
V^{\mathbb{K}}
is also strongly finitely generated by Proposition
3.4
.
This implies that
A(V)^{\mathbb{K}}\cong A(V^{\mathbb{K}})
is a finite-type filtered algebra by Proposition
4.6
and Lemma
5.1
.
Now it follows from Proposition
4.11
that
A(V)^{\mathbb{K}}
has the strong endomorphism property.
∎

6
The endomorphism property of vertex operator algebras

In this section, we will prove the main results of the paper: Theorem
6.4
and Corollary
6.10
.

Lemma 6.1
.

Let
V
be a vertex operator algebra over a field
\mathbb{F}
. Then the following conditions are equivalent:

(1)

A(V)
has the endomorphism property.

(2)

V
has the endomorphism property.

(3)

For any
n\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{+}
,
A_{n}(V)
has the endomorphism property.

Proof.

(1)
\Rightarrow
(2). Let
M
be any irreducible admissible
V
-module over
\mathbb{F}
. Note that
M(0)
is a simple
A(V)
-module.
By Corollary
4.7
, we have
\text{End}_{V}(M)\cong\text{End}_{A(V)}(M(0))
.
Since
A(V)
has the endomorphism property, every element in
\text{End}_{V}(M)
is algebraic over
\mathbb{F}
. Thus,
V
has the endomorphism property as well.

(2)
\Rightarrow
(3).
Let
U
be a simple
A_{n}(V)
-module. By (3) of Theorem
4.4
,
L_{n}(U)
is an irreducible admissible
V
-module.
By Corollary
4.7
again, we have
\text{End}_{V}(L_{n}(U))\cong\text{End}_{A_{n}(V)}(U)
.
Hence each element in
\text{End}_{A_{n}(V)}(U)
is algebraic over
\mathbb{F}
.

(3)
\Rightarrow
(1). Let
U
be a simple
A(V)
-module.
By Theorem
4.2
, we know that
A(V)
is a quotient algebra of
A_{n}(V)
,
hence
U
is also a simple
A_{n}(V)
-module and
\text{End}_{A(V)}(U)\cong\text{End}_{A_{n}(V)}(U)
.
Hence each element in
\text{End}_{A(V)}(U)
is algebraic over
\mathbb{F}
.
∎

By adopting a similar argument, we can prove the equivalency of the strong endomorphism properties:

Lemma 6.2
.

Let
V
be a vertex operator algebra over a field
\mathbb{F}
. Then the following conditions are equivalent:

(1)

A(V)
has the strong endomorphism property.

(2)

V
has the strong endomorphism property.

(3)

For any
n\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{+}
,
A_{n}(V)
has the strong endomorphism property.

Before we prove the main theorem, we give some examples of vertex operator algebras with the strong endomorphism property.
Note that the vertex operator algebras in the following example are all strongly finitely generated, but none of them are
C_{2}
-cofinite.

Example 6.3
.

Let
\mathbb{F}
be an arbitrary field with
\text{Char}(\mathbb{F})=0
.

(1) If
V=\bar{V}_{vir}(c,0)
is the (universal) Virasoro vertex operator algebra over
\mathbb{F}
with the central charge
c
.
It is well-known that
A(V)\cong\mathbb{F}[x]
(see
[
W
]
).
Since
\mathbb{F}[x]
is a principal ideal domain, every simple
\mathbb{F}[x]
-module is finite-dimensional, then it follows that
A(V)
has the strong endomorphism property, and so
\bar{V}_{vir}(c,0)
also has the strong endomorphism property by Lemma
6.2
.

(2) If
V=M_{\mathfrak{\hat{h}}}(1,0)
is the level one Heisenberg vertex operator algebra (
[
FZ
]
) over
\mathbb{F}

associated with the
n
-dimensional vector space
\mathfrak{h}
, then
A(V)\cong\mathbb{F}[x_{1},\cdots,x_{n}]
.
Let
S
be any simple
\mathbb{F}[x_{1},\cdots,x_{n}]
-module. Then
S\cong\mathbb{F}[x_{1},\cdots,x_{n}]/J
, for some maximal ideal of
\mathbb{F}[x_{1},\cdots,x_{n}]
.
Clearly,
\mathbb{F}[x_{1},\cdots,x_{n}]/J
is a field extension of
\mathbb{F}
, and it is a finitely generated
\mathbb{F}
-algebra.
Hence
\mathbb{F}[x_{1},\cdots,x_{n}]/J
is a finte algebraic extension of
\mathbb{F}
by Hilbert’s Nullstellensatz.
Consequently, every simple
A(V)
-module is finite-dimensional.
Hence,
M_{\mathfrak{\hat{h}}}(1,0)
has the strong endomorphism property by Lemma
6.2
.

(3) Let
\mathfrak{g}
be a finite-dimensional simple Lie algebra over a field
\mathbb{F}
,
equipped with a nondegenerate symmetric invariant bilinear form
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
.
Assume that the Casimir operator
\Omega
acts on
\mathfrak{g}
as a scalar
2h\in\mathbb{F}
, with
k\neq-h
.
Let
V=V_{\mathfrak{\hat{g}}}(k,0)
be the level

vacuum module vertex operator algebra over
\mathbb{F}

associated with the finite-dimensional simple Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
(
[
FZ
,
LL
]
).
Then
A(V)\cong U(\mathfrak{g})
, the universal enveloping algebra of
\mathfrak{g}
(see
[
FZ
]
).
It is well-known that
U(\mathfrak{g})
has the strong endomorphism property (
[
D
,
MR
]
).
Hence
V_{\mathfrak{\hat{g}}}(k,0)
has the strong endomorphism property by Lemma
6.2
.

Theorem 6.4
.

Let
V
be a vertex operator algebra over a field
\mathbb{F}
.

(1)

If
\mathbb{F}
is an uncountable field, then
V
has the endomorphism property.

(2)

If
V
is
C_{2}
-cofinite, then
V
has the strong endomorphism property.

(3)

If
\mathbb{F}={\mathbb{R}}
the real number field, then
V
has the strong endomorphism property.

(4)

(Quillen’s theorem) If
V
is strongly finitely generated over
\mathbb{F}
, then
V
has the strong endomorphism property.

Proof.

(1) By Lemma
6.1
, it is enough to prove that
A(V)
has the endomorphism property.
Let
S
be a simple
A(V)
-module. Recall that
V
is of countable dimension over
\mathbb{F}
. Then
A(V)
, as a quotient space of
V
, also has countable dimension. Thus
S
has countable dimension over
\mathbb{F}
as well.

Let
\theta\in\text{End}_{A(V)}(S)
, we adopt a similar argument in
[
Q
]
to show that
\theta
is algebraic. Suppose
\theta
is not algebraic, then
S
is a nonzero vector space over
\mathbb{F}(\theta)
, and
\text{dim}_{\mathbb{F}}(S)\geq\text{dim}_{\mathbb{F}}(\mathbb{F}(\theta))\geq\text{card}\penalty\ \mathbb{F}
,
where the last inequality follows from the fact that
the elements of the form
(\theta-\lambda)^{-1}\ (\lambda\in\mathbb{F})
in
\mathbb{F}(\theta)
are linearly independent.
This contradicts the assumption that
\mathbb{F}
is uncountable. Hence
\theta
must be algebraic.

(2) If
V
is
C_{2}
-cofinite, then by the epimorphism
\phi:R(V)\rightarrow\mathrm{gr}(A(V))
in Section 5 we know that
\mathrm{gr}(A(V))
, as well as
A(V)
, are finite-dimensional.
Naturally,
A(V)
has the strong endomorphism property. Then by Lemma
6.2
,
V
also has the strong endomorphism property.

(3) By Lemma
6.2
again, it is enough to prove that
A(V)
has the strong endomorphism property.
Let
S
be any simple
A(V)
-module, and let
D=\text{End}_{A(V)}(S)
.
Since
{\mathbb{R}}
is an uncountable field, every element in
D
is algebraic over
{\mathbb{R}}
by (1).
Consequently,
D
is a division algebra over
{\mathbb{R}}
such that every element in
D
is algebraic over
{\mathbb{R}}
.
Hence
D
must be isomorphic to
{\mathbb{R}},{\mathbb{C}}
or
\mathbb{H}
, where
\mathbb{H}
is the quaternion algebra (see
[
H
]
).
This implies that
\text{dim}_{{\mathbb{R}}}(D)<\infty
, as desired.

(4) Since
V
is strongly finitely generated, it follows from Theorem
5.2
that
A(V)
has the strong endomorphism property. So does
V
by Lemma
6.2
.
∎

Remark 6.5
.

The
C_{2}
-cofinite CFT-type vertex operator algebras over the complex number field
{\mathbb{C}}
are all strongly finitely generated (see
[
L1
]
). So part (2) in Theorem
6.4
is a special case of part (4) when
\mathbb{F}={\mathbb{C}}
and
V
is of the CFT-type.

The following corollary is an immediate consequence of Lemma
6.2
and Theorem
6.4
:

Corollary 6.6
.

(1) If
V
is a vertex operator algebra over the real number field
{\mathbb{R}}
, then
A_{n}(V)
has the strong endomorphism property.

(2) If
V
is a strongly finitely generated vertex operator algebra over an arbitrary field
\mathbb{F}
, then
A_{n}(V)
has the strong endomorphism property.

Remark 6.7
.

If
V
is a strongly finitely generated vertex operator algebra over a field
\mathbb{F}
,
by Lemma
4.15
and Lemma
5.1
(2), we know that
A(V)
is stably noetherian.
Moreover, by Corollary
6.6
,
A_{n}(V)
has the strong endomorphism property for any
n\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{+}
.
A natural question is whether
A_{n}(V)
is also stably noetherian.

Proposition 6.8
.

Let
V
be a vertex operator algebra over a field
\mathbb{F}
, with the strong endomorphism property. Then

(1)

If
V
is rational, then
A_{n}(V)
is a finite dimensional semi-simple associative algebra for each
n\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{+}
.

(2)

If
V
is simple, then
V
is rational if and only if
A_{n}(V)
is finite-dimensional semi-simple for each
n\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{+}
.

Proof.

(1) If
V
is rational, then by Theorem
4.2
(4),
A_{n}(V)
is a semi-simple left
A_{n}(V)
-module for each
n\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{+}
.
Thus
A_{n}(V)
is an aritinian algebra.
Let
\{S_{1},\cdots,S_{k}\}
be the set of the isomorphism classes of simple
A_{n}(V)
-modules, then by Artin-Wedderburn theorem,

A_{n}(V)^{op}\cong\bigoplus_{i=1}^{k}\text{M}_{n_{i}}(D_{i})\ \text{for some}\ n_{i}\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{+},\ \text{where}\ D_{i}=\text{End}_{A_{n}(V)}(S_{i}),\ \text{for\ all\ }i.

Since
V
has the strong endomorphism property, we have
\text{dim}_{\mathbb{F}}(D_{i})<\infty
for all
i
.
Consequently,
A_{n}(V)
is a finite dimensional semi-simple associative algebra for each
n\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{+}
.

(2) follows immediately from (1) and Theorem
4.2
(5).
∎

Proposition 6.9
.

Let
\mathbb{F}
be an algebraically closed field, and let
V
be a vertex operator algebra over
\mathbb{F}
, with the endomorphism property. Let
M=\bigoplus_{n\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{+}}M(n)
be an irreducible admissible
V
-module. Then

(1)

\text{End}_{V}(M)\cong\mathbb{F}
. Moreover, there exists a
\lambda\in\mathbb{F}
such that
L(0)|_{M(n)}=\lambda+n
for all
n
.

(2)

If
V
is rational, then
A_{n}(V)
is a finite dimensional semi-simple associative algebra for every
n\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{+}
.

Proof.

The proof of (1) is straightforward (see also
[
LL
, Proposition 4.5.5]
). It follows from (1) that
V
has the strong endomorphism property.
Hence (2) is an immediate consequence of Proposition
6.8
.
∎

By the previous results, Theorem
6.4
(4), Proposition
6.8
, and Proposition
6.9
, we have the following:

Corollary 6.10
.

Let
V
be a strongly finitely generated vertex operator algebra over a field
\mathbb{F}
, and let
M=\bigoplus_{n\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{+}}M(n)
be an irreducible admissible
V
-module. Then we have:

(1)

(Quillen’s theorem)
\text{End}_{V}(M)<\infty
.

(2)

(Schur’s lemma) If
\mathbb{F}
is an algebraic closed field, then
\text{End}_{V}(M)\cong\mathbb{F}
.
Moreover, there exists a
\lambda\in\mathbb{F}
such that
L(0)|_{M(n)}=\lambda+n
for all
n
.

(3)

If
V
is rational, then
A_{n}(V)
is a finite-dimensional semi-simple associative algebra for any
n\geq 0
.

Remark 6.11
.

Let
V
be a vertex operator algebra over an arbitrary field
\mathbb{F}
.
It is well-known that if
\mathbb{F}
is uncountable and algebraically closed, then Schur’s Lemma in Corollary
6.10
(2) is satisfied.
In
[
DR1
]
, the authors made the conjecture that the assumption
\mathbb{F}
is uncountable in Schur’s Lemma is unnecessary.

Now by Corollary
6.10
, we can drop the uncountable assumption for
\mathbb{F}
in Schur’s Lemma for strongly finitely generated vertex operator algebra
V
.

7
Applications: Scalar Extensions

In
[
M
]
, the author studied the vertex operator algebras
V
over
{\mathbb{R}}
, and proved that
V^{\mathbb{C}}={\mathbb{C}}\otimes_{\mathbb{R}}V
is rational if
V
is rational over
{\mathbb{R}}
. As an application of Proposition
6.8
, we will prove that the inverse is also true.

Theorem 7.1
.

Let
\mathbb{K}/\mathbb{F}
be a finite separable field extension, and
V
be a simple vertex operator algebra over
\mathbb{F}
s.t.
V_{0}=\mathbb{F}{\bf 1}
.
Assume that
V
and
V^{\mathbb{K}}
both have the strong endomorphism property. Then
V
is rational if and only if
V^{\mathbb{K}}
is rational.

Proof.

The “if" part: Since
V^{\mathbb{K}}
is rational and has the strong endomorphism property, then by Proposition
6.8
,
A_{n}(V^{\mathbb{K}})\cong\mathbb{K}\otimes A_{n}(V)
is a finite-dimensional
semi-simple associative algebra for any
n\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{+}
.
Since
\text{dim}_{\mathbb{F}}(A_{n}(V))=\text{dim}_{\mathbb{K}}(\mathbb{K}\otimes A_{n}(V))
, we have
\text{dim}_{\mathbb{F}}(A_{n}(V))<\infty
.
We claim that
A_{n}(V)
is also semi-simple for each
n\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{+}
. Indeed, we just need to show that
A_{n}(V)
has no nonzero nilpotent ideal since it is already finite-dimensional.
Suppose that
A_{n}(V)
has a nonzero nilpotent ideal
I
, then
\mathbb{K}\otimes I
is a nonzero nilpotent ideal of
A_{n}(V^{\mathbb{K}})
, which contradicts the fact that
A_{n}(V^{\mathbb{K}})
is semi-simple. Hence each
A_{n}(V)
is semi-simple, and so
V
is rational by Theorem
4.2
(5).

The “only if" part: If
V
is rational, then by Proposition
6.8
again,
A_{n}(V)
is a finite-dimensional semi-simple associative algebra for each
n\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{+}
.
Since
\mathbb{K}/\mathbb{F}
is a finite separable extension, it follows from Lemma
3.3
that
A_{n}(V^{\mathbb{K}})\cong\mathbb{K}\otimes A_{n}(V)
is also finite dimensional semi-simple over
\mathbb{K}
.
Moreover,
V^{\mathbb{K}}
is a simple vertex operator algebra over
\mathbb{K}
by Corollary
3.7
. Hence
V^{\mathbb{K}}
is rational by Theorem
4.2
(5).
∎

Remark 7.2
.

When
\mathbb{K}/\mathbb{F}
is a finite separable extension, we believe that
V
has the strong endomorphism property if and only if
V^{\mathbb{K}}
has the strong endomorphism property.
But we do not know how to prove it.

Corollary 7.3
.

Let
V
be a simple vertex operator algebra over the real number field
{\mathbb{R}}
, with
V_{0}={\mathbb{R}}{\bf 1}
.
Then
V
is rational if and only if
V^{{\mathbb{C}}}
is rational.

Proof.

By Theorem
6.4
(3), any vertex operator algebra
V
over
{\mathbb{R}}
has the strong endomorphism property, and by Proposition
6.9
(1),
V^{\mathbb{C}}
also has the strong endomorphism property. Moreover, the extension
{\mathbb{C}}/{\mathbb{R}}
is clearly finite separable. Then by Theorem
7.1
, we have
V
is rational if and only if
V^{\mathbb{C}}
is rational.
∎

Corollary 7.4
.

Let
\mathbb{K}/\mathbb{F}
be a finite separable extension, and
V
be a simple strongly finitely generated vertex operator algebra over a field
\mathbb{F}
such that
V_{0}=\mathbb{F}{\bf 1}
.
Then
V
is rational if and only if
V^{\mathbb{K}}
is rational.

Proof.

Note that
V^{\mathbb{K}}
is also strongly finitely generated by Proposition
3.4
.
By Corollary
6.10
(1), both
V
and
V^{\mathbb{K}}
have the strong endomorphism property. Now the conclusion follows from Theorem
7.1
.
∎

Theorem 7.5
.

Let
V
be a strongly finitely generated vertex operator algebra over a field
\mathbb{F}
, and
M
be an irreducible admissible
V
-module.
Then the following conditions are equivalent:

(1)

M
is an absolutely irreducible
V
-module.

(2)

M(0)
is an absolutely simple
A(V)
-module.

(3)

\text{End}_{V}(M)=\text{End}_{A(V)}(M(0))=\mathbb{F}.

Proof.

By Lemma
3.6
and Corollary
4.7
,
it is enough to prove that
M(0)
is absolutely simple if and only if
\text{End}_{A(V)}(M(0))=\mathbb{F}.

The following proof is similar to
[
D
, Proposition 2.6.5]
.

Assume that
\text{End}_{A(V)}(M(0))=\mathbb{F}
.
We claim that
M(0)^{\mathbb{K}}
is a simple
A(V)^{\mathbb{K}}
-module for any field extension
\mathbb{K}/\mathbb{F}
.
Indeed, let
N
be a nonzero submodule of
M(0)^{\mathbb{K}}
.
Take a nonzero element
k_{1}\otimes m_{1}+\cdots+k_{p}\otimes m_{p}\in N\ (k_{i}\in\mathbb{K},m_{i}\in M(0),p>0)

such that
m_{1},\dots,m_{p}
are linearly independent over
\mathbb{F}=\mathrm{End}_{A(V)}(M(0))
.
Since
M(0)
is a simple
A(V)
-module,
by the Jacobson’s density theorem, there exists some
v\in A(V)
such that
[v]\cdot m_{1}=o(v)m_{1}=m_{1}
and
[v]\cdot m_{i}=o(v)m_{i}=0
for any
i\neq 1
.
Hence
o(k_{1}^{-1}\otimes v)(k_{1}\otimes m_{1}+\cdots+k_{p}\otimes m_{p})=1\otimes m_{1}\in N.

Since
M(0)
is a simple
V
-module, we have
M(0)=\{o(v)m_{1}\penalty\ |\penalty\ v\in V\}.

Therefore, we have
M(0)^{\mathbb{K}}=N
, and
M(0)^{\mathbb{K}}
is a simple
A(V)^{\mathbb{K}}
-module.

Now we assume that
M(0)
is absolutely simple. For any
f\in\text{End}_{A(V)}(M(0)),

we need to show that
f=\lambda Id_{M(0)}
for some
\lambda\in F
. Let
\overline{\mathbb{F}}
be an algebraic closure of
\mathbb{F}
.
Then
\text{End}_{A(V)^{\mathbb{K}}}(M(0)^{\mathbb{K}})=\overline{\mathbb{F}}
by Corollary
6.10
.
On the other hand,
1\otimes f\in\text{End}_{A(V)^{\mathbb{K}}}(M(0)^{\mathbb{K}}),

so
1\otimes f=\lambda\otimes Id_{M(0)}
for some
\lambda\in\overline{\mathbb{F}}.

This implies that
\lambda\in\mathbb{F}
and
f=\lambda Id_{M(0)}.
The proof is complete.
∎

Remark 7.6
.

The Theorem
7.5
is useful, especially because it provides an intrinsic test for a module to be absolutely simple, which
involves only the computation of
\text{End}_{V}(M)
or
\text{End}_{A(V)}(M(0))
, rather than the consideration of extension fields.

Corollary 7.7
.

Let
V
be a strongly finitely generated vertex operator algebra over an algebraically closed field
\mathbb{F}
, and let
M
be a simple admissible
V
-module.
Then
M
is absolutely simple.

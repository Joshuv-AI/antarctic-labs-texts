A distributive lattice of model structures relating to spectral sequences Thanks: This work was supported by the Engineering and Physical Sciences Research Council.
James A. Brotherston

Abstract
. The S S -model category structures on filtered chain complexes and bicomplexes were introduced by Cirici, Egas Santander, Livernet and Whitehouse and later generalised by this author. In this paper we show they are left proper, cellular and stable model categories. We use these properties and the Cellularization Principle of Greenlees and Shipley to show that an adjunction with right adjoint the product totalisation functor from bicomplexes to filtered chains is a Quillen equivalence. Combined with other known Quillen equivalences between filtered chains this shows these model categories all present the same homotopy category. We also construct a distributive lattice whose elements are the S S -model categories of filtered chain complexes.

Preliminaries

Throughout
R
will denote a fixed commutative unital ring.
Chain complexes are graded cohomologically and filtrations are increasing filtrations.
Bicomplexes are graded cohomologically vertically and homologically horizontally.

We recall the necessary preliminaries on filtered chain complexes, bicomplexes, the shift-décalage adjunction, spectral sequences, model categories, Bousfield localisations, the Cellularization Principle, and construction of the
S
-model structures on filtered chain complexes and bicomplexes.

Our conventions agree with those of
[
CELW20
]
minus a choice of sign on certain differentials.

2.1.
Filtered chain complexes

Definition 2.1.1
.

A

A
is a chain complex equipped with an
increasing filtration

F_{p}A

Definition 2.1.2
.

The

with objects and morphisms given as in

.

Definition 2.1.3
.

An

is said to be of pure filtration degree

.
We denote by

a graded filtered

-module given by a copy of

concentrated in pure filtration degree

and concentrated in cohomological degree

We use this notation to build further objects of

later, e.g.

will denote a direct sum of two such objects with an identity differential appearing in filtration degrees
p
and above.

We will need to have an understanding of the small and finite objects in filtered chain complexes in dealing with colimits, as in the likes of the small objects argument.
We take
[
Hov99
, Definitions 2.1.3 and 2.1.4]
as our definitions of small and finite objects respectively.
The following two lemmas are proved in a similar way to
[
Hov99
, Lemma 2.3.2]
.

Lemma 2.1.4
.

Every object of
f\mathcal{C}
is small relative to the whole category.∎

Lemma 2.1.5

A filtered chain complex

2.3.
Bicomplexes

Definition 2.3.1
.

A

is a bigraded collection of
R
-modules

for

Definition 2.3.3

\mathrm{Tot}^{\Pi}(A)^{n}\coloneqq\prod_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}A^{i,i+n}

we have

.

Similarly there is a

denoted

obtained by replacing the product

Definition 2.3.4
.

Let

. We define the

-suspension

and

\displaystyle\left(\Sigma^{r}A\right)^{p,q}

\displaystyle\coloneqq A^{p-r,q-r+1}

\displaystyle\left(\Omega^{r}A\right)^{p,q}

\displaystyle\coloneqq A^{p+r,q+r-1}

and whose differentials are

The following lemma is proved in the same way as

Every object of

2.5.
Model categories

We take as our definition of a
model category
that of
[
Hov99
, Definition 1.1.3]
, in particular it is a complete and cocomplete category with functorial factorisations, and take
[
Hov99
, Definition 2.1.17]
as our definition of a
cofibrantly generated model category
.

In a cofibrantly generated model category the
generating cofibrations
will be denoted
I
and
generating acyclic cofibrations

J
(we will frequently adorn these with subscripts to specify a particular model category).
For a class
K
of morphisms we write
K\text{-}\mathrm{Inj}
(resp.
K\text{-}\mathrm{Proj}
) for the the
K
-injectives (resp.
K
-projectives), i.e. those morphisms having the right (resp. left) lifting property with respect to
K
and set
K\text{-}\mathrm{Cof}\coloneqq(K\text{-}\mathrm{Inj})\text{-}\mathrm{Proj}
.
We write
K\text{-}\mathrm{Cell}
for the
K
-cellular morphisms
, i.e. those built as transfinite compositions of pushouts of elements of
K
, see
[
Hov99
, Definition 2.1.9]
.

We additionally say a model category is
finitely cofibrantly generated
if the domains and codomains of
I
and
J
are finite relative to the cofibrations,
[
Hov99
, §7.4]
.

We list some standard definitions of desirable properties of model categories.

Definition 2.5.1
.

A model category
\mathcal{M}
is said to be

•

left proper
if pushouts of weak equivalences along cofibrations are weak equivalences,

•

right proper
if pullbacks of weak equivalences along fibrations are weak equivalences,

•

proper
if
\mathcal{M}
is both left proper and right proper,

•

cellular
if it is a cofibrantly generated model category and

(1)

the domains and codomains of
I
are small,

(2)

the domains of
J
are small relative to
I
,

(3)

the cofibrations are
effective monomorphisms
, i.e. any cofibration
i\colon X\rightarrow Y
is the equaliser of the two inclusions

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces Y}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces Y\coprod_{X}Y\,,}

•

pointed
if the initial and terminal objects are isomorphic (in which case we denote either by
\ast
).

Given a model category
\mathcal{M}
one can form the
Reedy model structure
on the categories of simplicial objects in
\mathcal{M}
and cosimplicial objects of
\mathcal{M}
, see
[
Hov99
, Theorem 5.2.5]
.
These lead to the definitions of (left and right)
homotopy function complexes

\map_{l}(-,-)
and
\map_{r}(-,-)
which are simplicial sets, see
[
Hov99
, Corollary 5.4.4]
.
Their right derived functors are isomorphic by
[
Hov99
, Theorem 5.4.9]
so for
A

\map_{l}(A,X)\simeq\map_{r}(A,X)
and in this case we write
\map(-,-)
for either choice.
The use of homotopy function complexes in this paper will be in dealing with Bousfield localisations.

For computing the
homotopy loops functor
we make use of the following lemma whose proof can be found in
[
Hir03
, Corollary 13.3.8]
.

Lemma 2.5.2
.

Let
\mathcal{M}
be a right proper pointed model category,
f
be a fibration, and

.
Then the following pullback diagram gives a model for the homotopy pullback of

by two points

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces Z}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces Y}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\ast}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces X}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\lrcorner}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces f}

.∎

There is an analogous statement for constructing homotopy pushouts in a left proper model category.

Definition 2.5.3
.

A pointed model category
\mathcal{M}
is said to be stable if the suspension and loops functors give an equivalence of categories on the homotopy category.

2.6.
Bousfield localisations

Left (resp. right) Bousfield localisations provide a method of constructing a new model category from an existing one by expanding the class of weak equivalences and retaining the same cofibrations (resp. fibrations).
Existence of a left or right Bousfield localisation is not guaranteed, however under some reasonable conditions they can be constructed.
The definitions and results in this section can mostly be found in
[
Hir03
]
.

Definition 2.6.1
.

Let
\mathcal{M}
be a model category and
\mathcal{C}
be a subclass of its morphisms, then:

•

an object

of
\mathcal{M}
is

-local

of

•

of
\mathcal{M}
is a
\mathcal{C}
-local equivalence
if for every
\mathcal{C}
-local object
W
of
\mathcal{M}
the induced maps of homotopy function complexes,
\map(g,W)\colon\map(Y,W)\rightarrow\map(X,W)
, are weak equivalences of simplicial sets.

Definition 2.6.2
.

The
left Bousfield localisation
of a model category
\mathcal{M}
at a class of morphisms

denoted

\mathcal{M}
and

\mathcal{M}
, and

\mathcal{M}

7.
Bousfield localisations of
\left(f\mathcal{C}\right)_{S}
and
\left(b\mathcal{C}\right)_{S}

Given a morphism of spectral sequences

Thus writing

or

Given then finite non-empty sets

and

or

with

the

-weak equivalences are a subclass of the
T
-weak equivalences.
We can then ask “is the

-model structure a Bousfield localisation of the

-model structure?”.

7.1.
Non-existence of certain left Bousfield localisations

Given one of the model structures on

or

with

-weak equivalences we show there is no left Bousfield localisation with weak equivalences the
(r+1)
-weak equivalences.

Proposition 7.1.1
.

Let

be any of
\left(f\mathcal{C}\right)_{S}
or
\left(b\mathcal{C}\right)_{S}

.
There is no left Bousfield localisation

of

whose weak equivalences are the

-weak equivalences.

Proof.

Suppose such a

\mathrm{Cof}_{\mathrm{new}}=\mathrm{Cof}_{S}

and that

-weak equivalence since transfinite compositions of weak equivalences are weak equivalences,

∎

Twists of graded Poisson algebras and related properties
Xin Tang, Xingting Wang and James J. Zhang

Abstract
. We introduce a Poisson version of the graded twist of a graded associative algebra and prove that every graded Poisson structure on a connected graded polynomial ring A := 𝕜 ⁡ [ x 1 , … , x n ] A:=\Bbbk[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}] is a graded twist of a unimodular Poisson structure on A A , namely, if π \pi is a graded Poisson structure on A A , then π \pi has a decomposition π = π u ​ n ​ i ​ m + 1 ∑ i = 1 n deg ⁡ x i ​ E ∧ 𝐦 \pi~=~\pi_{unim}+\frac{1}{\sum_{i=1}^{n}\deg x_{i}}E\wedge{\mathbf{m}} where E E is the Euler derivation, π u ​ n ​ i ​ m \pi_{unim} is the unimodular graded Poisson structure on A A corresponding to π \pi , and 𝐦 {\mathbf{m}} is the modular derivation of ( A , π ) (A,\pi) . This result is a generalization of the same result in the quadratic setting. The rigidity of graded twisting, P ​ H 1 PH^{1} -minimality, and H H -ozoneness are studied. As an application, we compute the Poisson cohomologies of the quadratic Poisson structures on the polynomial ring of three variables when the potential is irreducible, but not necessarily having isolated singularities.

P\wedge Q\in{\mathfrak{X}}^{p+q}(A)
is the skew-symmetric

(p+q)
-derivation of
A
, defined by

\displaystyle(P\wedge Q)

\displaystyle[F_{1},\cdots,F_{p+q}]:=

\displaystyle\sum_{\sigma\in{\mathbb{S}}_{p,q}}sgn(\sigma)P[F_{\sigma(1)},\ldots,F_{\sigma(p)}]\,Q[F_{\sigma(p+1)},\ldots,F_{\sigma(p+q)}],

for all
F_{1},\cdots,F_{p+q}\in A
. In particular, if

P\in{\mathfrak{X}}^{1}(A)
and

Q\in{\mathfrak{X}}^{2}(A)
, then we have

(E1.5.2)

(P\wedge Q)[F_{1},F_{2},F_{3}]=P[F_{1}]Q[F_{2},F_{3}]-P[F_{2}]Q[F_{1},F_{3}]+P[F_{3}]Q[F_{1},F_{2}].

Therefore
({\mathfrak{X}}^{\ast}(A),\wedge,d)
is a dga
(differential graded algebra). For each
q\geq 0
, the

q
-th Poisson cohomology
of
A
is defined to be

(E1.5.3)

PH^{q}(A):=\frac{\ker d_{\pi}^{q}}{\text{im}d^{q-1}_{\pi}}.

It is clear from the definition that the 1st Poisson cohomology
of
A
is

(E1.5.4)

PH^{1}(A):=\frac{{\text{the set of Poisson derivations}}}{{\text{the set of Hamiltonian derivations}}}.

If
A
is a quadratic Poisson algebra
\Bbbk[x,y,z]
(with

\deg(x)=\deg(y)=\deg(z)=1
), then the complex

({\mathfrak{X}}^{\bullet}(A),d_{\pi})
is
[
Pi1
, (15)]

0\to A\to(A[1])^{\oplus 3}\to(A[2])^{\oplus 3}\to A[3]\to 0.

By the additivity of Hilbert series, we have

(E1.5.5)

\sum_{i=0}^{3}(-1)^{i}h_{PH^{i}(A)}(t)=-t^{-3}.

It is easy to check that

(a)

the lowest degree of nonzero elements in
PH^{0}(A)
is 0
and
PH^{0}(A)_{0}=\Bbbk
.

(b)

the lowest degree of nonzero elements in
PH^{1}(A)
is
\geq-1
.

(c)

the lowest degree of nonzero elements in
PH^{2}(A)
is

\geq-2
.

(d)

the lowest degree of nonzero elements in
PH^{3}(A)
is
-3

and
PH^{3}(A)_{-3}=\Bbbk
.

If
A
is further unimodular, then

(e)

the lowest degree of nonzero elements in
PH^{2}(A)
is
-2

and
PH^{2}(A)_{-2}=\Bbbk^{\oplus 3}
.

A natural operation on
{\mathfrak{X}}^{\bullet}(A)
is the
Schouten bracket

[\cdot,\cdot]_{S}:{\mathfrak{X}}^{p}(A)\times{\mathfrak{X}}^{q}(A)\to{\mathfrak{X}}^{p+q-1}(A)

for all
p,q\geq 0
. We refer to
[
LPV
, Section 3.3.2]

for the precise definition. By
[
LPV
, (4.5)]
,

d_{\pi}(\cdot)=-[\cdot,\pi]_{S}.

By
[
LPV
, Proposition 3.7]
,
({\mathfrak{X}}^{\bullet}(A),\wedge,[\cdot,\cdot]_{S})
is a Gerstenhaber algebra .

Let
A
or
(A,\pi)
be a Poisson algebra with Poisson bracket

\pi
. Let
\xi
be any nonzero scalar. We define a new Poisson

or

.
Then it is easy to see that

is indeed

, but they are closely related as follows.

.

Retain the notations as above.
Let

2.
Twists of graded Poisson algebras

Let
G
be an abelian group and
A
be a
G
-graded Poisson
algebra (namely, both the multiplication
\cdot
and the Poisson bracket
\{-,-\}
of
A
are
graded of degree 0). We use
g
for elements in
G
. If

a
is a homogeneous element in
A
, we use
|a|
to denote
its degree in
G
.

The aim of this section is to define a Poisson version of
the graded twist of graded associative algebras
[
Zh
]
.

Definition 2.1
.

Let
\delta:=\{\delta_{g}\mid g\in G\}
be a set of graded
derivations of
A
(of degree 0). We say
\delta
is a

Poisson twisting system
if it satisfies the following
conditions

(1)

For all
g,h\in G
,

(E2.1.1)

\delta_{g}\delta_{h}~=~\delta_{h}\delta_{g}.

(2)

For homogeneous elements
a,b\in A
,

(E2.1.2)

\delta_{|ab|}~=~\delta_{|a|}+\delta_{|b|}.

(3)

For homogeneous elements
a,b,c,\in A
,

(E2.1.3)

\displaystyle a[\delta_{|a|}(\{b,c\})-\{\delta_{|a|}(b),c\}-\{b,\delta_{|a|}(c)\}]-b

\displaystyle[\delta_{|b|}(\{a,c\})-\{\delta_{|b|}(a),c\}-\{a,\delta_{|b|}(c)\}]

\displaystyle+c[\delta_{|c|}(\{a,b\})-\{\delta_{|c|}(a),b\}-\{a,\delta_{|c|}(b)\}]

\displaystyle=0.

Remark 2.2
.

(1)

The definition of a Poisson twisting system is a “translation”
of the twisting system in the setting of graded associative
algebras given in
[
Zh
, Definition 2.1]
.

(2)

Let

(E2.2.1)

\displaystyle p(\{-,-\},\delta\;a,b,c)~:=~

\displaystyle a[\delta_{|a|}(\{b,c\})-\{\delta_{|a|}(b),c\}-\{b,\delta_{|a|}(c)\}]

\displaystyle-b[\delta_{|b|}(\{a,c\})-\{\delta_{|b|}(a),c\}-\{a,\delta_{|b|}(c)\}]

\displaystyle+c[\delta_{|c|}(\{a,b\})-\{\delta_{|c|}(a),b\}-\{a,\delta_{|c|}(b)\}].

Then (
E2.1.3
) is equivalent to
p(\{-,-\};\delta;a,b,c)=0
.
If each
\delta_{g}
is a Poisson derivation, it is
automatic that
p(\{-,-\},\delta,;a,b,c)=0
. The converse
is not true, see Example
2.6
.

(3)

Suppose
G={\mathbb{Z}}
and let
\phi=\delta_{1}
. By (
E2.1.2
),

\delta_{n}=n\phi
. It is clear that

p(\{-,-\},\delta\;a,b,c)=(E\wedge d_{\pi}^{1}(\phi))(a,b,c),

which implies that (
E2.1.3
) is equivalent to

E\wedge d_{\pi}^{1}(\phi)=0
.
By
[
LPV
, Sect. 4.3]
and the fact that
d_{\pi}^{1}(E)=0
,
the equation
E\wedge d_{\pi}^{1}(\phi)=0
is equivalent to

d_{\pi}^{2}(E\wedge\phi)=0
. Assume that
a,b,c
are homogeneous
of degree one. Then

(E2.2.2)

\displaystyle p(\{-,-\},\delta;a,b,c)~=~

\displaystyle|a|a[\phi(\{b,c\})-\{\phi(b),c\}-\{b,\phi(c)\}]

\displaystyle-|b|b[\phi(\{a,c\})-\{\phi(a),c\}-\{a,\phi(c)\}]

\displaystyle+|c|c[\phi(\{a,b\})-\{\phi(a),b\}-\{a,\phi(b)\}]

which agrees with
p(\{-,-\},\phi;a,b,c)
as defined in
(
E0.3.1
).

(4)

Let
G={\mathbb{Z}}
and
A
be a
{\mathbb{Z}}
-graded Poisson
algebra. A convenient Poisson twisting system is constructed as
follows. Let
\phi
be a graded Poisson derivation of
A
(namely,

d_{\pi}^{1}(\phi)=0
). For
each
n\in{\mathbb{Z}}
, let
\delta_{n}:=n\phi
and

\delta:=\{\delta_{n}\mid n\in{\mathbb{Z}}\}
. Then
(
E2.1.1
) and (
E2.1.2
) are obvious and (
E2.1.3
)
follows from the fact that
\delta_{n}
is a Poisson derivation,
see part (2) or (3).

Example 2.3
.

Let
G={\mathbb{Z}}/(n)
for some positive integer
n
. Let

A
be a
G
-graded Poisson algebra and
\delta
be a graded
Poisson derivation of
A
. Suppose
p:={\text{char}}\;\Bbbk

is positive. If
p\mid n
, let
\delta_{i}=i\delta
for all

i\in G
. Then
\{\delta_{i}\mid i\in G\}
is a Poisson twisting
system. If
p\nmid n
, then there is no nontrivial Poisson
twisting system for
A
.

Let
A
be a
G
-graded Poisson algebra and let
\delta:=\{\delta_{g}\mid g\in G\}
be a system of derivations of
A
.
We define

(E2.3.1)

\langle a,b\rangle:=\{a,b\}+a\delta_{|a|}(b)-b\delta_{|b|}(a)

for all homogeneous elements
a,b\in A
.

Theorem 2.4
.

Let
\delta:=\{\delta_{g}\mid g\in G\}
be a set of
graded derivations of
A
satisfying (
E2.1.1
)
and (
E2.1.2
). Then
(A,\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle)

is a Poisson algebra if and only if (
E2.1.3
) holds.

Proof.

If
G={\mathbb{Z}}
, there is a shorter proof using the Schouten
bracket. For a general abelian group
G
, we make the following
direct computation.

Claim 1:
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle

is skew-symmetric. This claim follows immediately from
(
E2.3.1
).

Claim 2: for every homogeneous element
a
,
\langle a,-\rangle

is a derivation.

For homogeneous elements
a,b,c
in
A
, we have

\displaystyle\langle a,bc\rangle

\displaystyle=\{a,bc\}+a\delta_{|a|}(bc)-bc\delta_{|bc|}(a)

\displaystyle=\{a,b\}c+\{a,c\}b+a(b\delta_{|a|}(c)+\delta_{|a|}(b)c)-bc\delta_{|bc|}(a)

\displaystyle\langle a,b\rangle c

\displaystyle=(\{a,b\}+a\delta_{|a|}(b)-b\delta_{|b|}(a))c

\displaystyle b\langle a,c\rangle

\displaystyle=b(\{a,c\}+a\delta_{|a|}(c)-c\delta_{|c|}(a)).

By the above and (
E2.1.2
), we obtain that

\langle a,bc\rangle=\langle a,b\rangle c+b\langle a,c\rangle.

Claim 3:
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
satisfies the Jacobi identity
if and only if (
E2.1.3
) holds. As a consequence,

(A,\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle)
is a Poisson algebra if and only
if (
E2.1.3
) holds.

For homogeneous elements
a,b,c
in
A
, we have

\displaystyle\langle a,\langle b,c\rangle\rangle

\displaystyle=\{a,\langle b,c\rangle\}+a\delta_{|a|}(\langle b,c\rangle)-\langle b,c\rangle\delta_{|bc|}(a)

\displaystyle=\{a,(\{b,c\}+b\delta_{|b|}(c)-c\delta_{|c|}(b))\}

\displaystyle+a\delta_{|a|}(\{b,c\}+b\delta_{|b|}(c)-c\delta_{|c|}(b))

\displaystyle-(\{b,c\}+b\delta_{|b|}(c)-c\delta_{|c|}(b))\delta_{|bc|}(a)

\displaystyle=\{a,\{b,c\}\}

\displaystyle+\{a,b\}\delta_{|b|}(c)+b\{a,\delta_{|b|}(c)\}

\displaystyle-\{a,c\}\delta_{|c|}(b)-c\{a,\delta_{|c|}(b)\}

\displaystyle+a\delta_{|a|}(\{b,c\})

\displaystyle+a\delta_{|a|}(b)\delta_{|b|}(c)+ab\delta_{|a|}\delta_{|b|}(c)

\displaystyle-a\delta_{|a|}(c)\delta_{|c|}(b)-ac\delta_{|a|}\delta_{|c|}(b)

\displaystyle-\{b,c\}\delta_{|bc|}(a)-b\delta_{|b|}(c)\delta_{|bc|}(a)+c\delta_{|c|}(b)\delta_{|bc|}(a)

and

\displaystyle\langle\langle a,b\rangle,c\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle c,\langle b,a\rangle\rangle

\displaystyle=\{c,\{b,a\}\}

\displaystyle+\{c,b\}\delta_{|b|}(a)+b\{c,\delta_{|b|}(a)\}

\displaystyle-\{c,a\}\delta_{|a|}(b)-a\{c,\delta_{|a|}(b)\}

\displaystyle+c\delta_{|c|}(\{b,a\})

\displaystyle+c\delta_{|c|}(b)\delta_{|b|}(a)+cb\delta_{|c|}\delta_{|b|}(a)

\displaystyle-c\delta_{|c|}(a)\delta_{|a|}(b)-ca\delta_{|c|}\delta_{|a|}(b)

\displaystyle-\{b,a\}\delta_{|ba|}(c)-b\delta_{|b|}(a)\delta_{|ba|}(c)+a\delta_{|a|}(b)\delta_{|ba|}(c)

and

\displaystyle\langle b,\langle a,c\rangle\rangle

\displaystyle=\{b,\{a,c\}\}

\displaystyle+\{b,a\}\delta_{|a|}(c)+a\{b,\delta_{|a|}(c)\}

\displaystyle-\{b,c\}\delta_{|c|}(a)-c\{b,\delta_{|c|}(a)\}

\displaystyle+b\delta_{|b|}(\{a,c\})

\displaystyle+b\delta_{|b|}(a)\delta_{|a|}(c)+ba\delta_{|b|}\delta_{|a|}(c)

\displaystyle-b\delta_{|b|}(c)\delta_{|c|}(a)-bc\delta_{|b|}\delta_{|c|}(a)

\displaystyle-\{a,c\}\delta_{|ac|}(b)-a\delta_{|a|}(c)\delta_{|ac|}(b)+c\delta_{|c|}(a)\delta_{|ac|}(b).

Using the Jacobi identity

-\{a,\{b,c\}\}+\{\{a,b\},c\}+\{b,\{a,c\}\}=0,

(
E2.1.1
), and (
E2.1.2
), we can simplify

-\langle a,\langle b,c\rangle\rangle+\langle\langle c,b\rangle,a\rangle+\langle b,\langle a,c\rangle\rangle

to

p(\{-,-\},\delta;a,b,c).

Therefore
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
satisfies the Jacobi
identity if and only if
p(\delta;a,b,c)=0
. Claim 3
follows. The consequence is clear.
∎

is a Poisson algebra and by the first

\delta
is a Poisson twisting system of

and

. So we have

. Let

.
Then

. We claim that

. Suppose
on the contrary that

. Then
Z=X^{h}
for some
Poisson twisting system
h
of

. Applying
(
E2.3.1
) to pairs of elements of the form

for all

, we see that

. But it is clear that (

)
fails for

Therefore there is no Poisson twisting system

This example suggests that there should be a more general

We conclude this section with some examples.

Example 2.11
.

Here are three examples of twists of graded Poisson algebras.

(1)

Let

be the Poisson polynomial ring
\Bbbk[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}]

\delta:=\{\delta_{(a_{1},\ldots,a_{n})}\mid(a_{1},\ldots,a_{n})\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}\}

(2)

Let
A
be the Poisson polynomial ring
\Bbbk[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}]

with trivial Poisson bracket. Consider
A
as a

{\mathbb{Z}}
-graded algebra with

Let

0&i=1.\end{cases}

Let

Since
\delta_{1}
is a graded Poisson derivation,
\delta
is a
twisting system of

. By (
E2.3.1
), the Poisson bracket
of the new Poisson algebra

is determined by

\langle x_{i},x_{j}\rangle=x_{i}\delta(x_{j})-x_{j}\delta(x_{i})=-x_{i}x_{j-1}+x_{j}x_{i-1}\quad{\text{for all $i<j$.}}

When

(3)

Suppose that
{\text{char}}\;\Bbbk=0
. Let

be the

\Bbbk[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n},y_{1},\ldots,y_{n}]

be any

\Bbbk
. We consider

as a

{\mathbb{Z}}
-graded Poisson algebra with

and

\deg(y_{i})=-1

\delta_{1}

for all

is a twisting

), the Poisson bracket of the
new Poisson algebra

\langle x_{i},x_{j}\rangle=0=\langle y_{i},y_{j}\rangle

\langle x_{i},y_{j}\rangle=\delta_{ij}+x_{i}\delta_{1}(y_{j})-y_{j}\delta_{-1}(x_{i})=\delta_{ij}+x_{i}(\sum_{s=1}^{n}m_{sj}y_{s})-y_{j}(\sum_{s=1}^{n}m_{is}x_{s})

\Bbbk

for all

using

. In other words, we can assume
that

is strictly upper triangular. Now we can assume that

is a block matrix with diagonal block equal to

\begin{pmatrix}0&1&0&\cdots&0\\
0&0&1&\cdots&0\\
\cdots&\cdots&\cdots&\cdots&\cdots\\
0&0&0&\cdots&1\\
0&0&0&\cdots&0\end{pmatrix}.

To illustrate the idea, we assume

and
M
is given as above.
(For general

, the proof is more complicated.) The Poisson

is determined by

\displaystyle\langle x_{1},x_{2}\rangle

\displaystyle=0,

\displaystyle\langle y_{1},y_{2}\rangle

\displaystyle=0,

\displaystyle\langle x_{1},y_{1}\rangle

\displaystyle=1-x_{2}y_{1}

\displaystyle\langle x_{1},y_{2}\rangle

\displaystyle=x_{1}y_{1}-x_{2}y_{2}

\displaystyle\langle x_{2},y_{1}\rangle

\displaystyle=0

\displaystyle\langle x_{2},y_{2}\rangle

\displaystyle=1+x_{2}y_{1}

Now we are ready to show that every nonzero Poisson

of

contains
1
. Let
f
be a
nonzero element in

. We assume that the
y_{2}
-degree
of

is minimal and that

. Write

where

for all
i
. If
f_{i}\neq 0

for some

. It is clear that the

-degree of

-degree of

. Similarly, by using

Write

g_{i}\in\Bbbk[x_{2}]

for all
i
. Assume that

has minimal

-degree.
If the

-degree of

, is positive, then

which has

-degree

, yielding a contradiction.
Therefore
0\neq f\in\Bbbk[x_{2}]\cap I
.
Write

where
c_{a}c_{b}\neq 0
.
Assume that
b-a\geq 0
is minimal. Next we show that

. Suppose to the contrary that
a<b
. Define

\Phi(f)=-H_{y_{2}}(f)-\deg(f)fy_{1}
. Then

\Phi(f)=\sum_{i=a}^{b}c_{i}ix_{2}^{i-1}+\sum_{i=a}^{b}c_{i}(i-b)x_{2}^{i}y_{1}.

Then

\displaystyle H_{x_{1}}(\Phi(f))+\Phi(f)x_{2}

\displaystyle=(\sum_{i=a}^{b}c_{i}(i-b)x_{2}^{i})(1-x_{2}y_{1})

\displaystyle+\sum_{i=a}^{b}c_{i}ix_{2}^{i}+\sum_{i=a}^{b}c_{i}(i-b)x_{2}^{i}x_{2}y_{1}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=a}^{b}c_{i}(i-b)x_{2}^{i}+\sum_{i=a}^{b}c_{i}ix_{2}^{i}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=a}^{b}c_{i}(2i-b)x_{2}^{i}

\displaystyle=2f^{\prime}(x_{2})x_{2}-bf\in\Bbbk[x_{2}]\cap I.

This implies that

and a possible linear combination
of

and
x_{2}f^{\prime}
gives a nonzero polynomial with smaller

,
yielding a contradiction. Therefore we can assume

.
In this case,
-H_{y_{2}}(f)=ax_{2}^{a-1}(1+x_{2}y_{1})=ax_{2}^{a-1}+afy_{1}

This implies that

as required.

4.

Lemma 4.1

\Bbbk[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}]

for every

Proof.

Since

both
\divv(\delta)
and
{\mathfrak{l}}=\divv(E)

\Bbbk

with modular derivation

Since

(respectively,

By (

\{-,-\}=\langle-,-\rangle-E\wedge\delta.

\displaystyle\delta(\{a,b\})-

\displaystyle\{\delta(a),b\}-\{a,\delta(b)\}

\displaystyle=\delta(\langle a,b\rangle)-\langle\delta(a),b\rangle-\langle a,\delta(b)\rangle

\displaystyle-\delta((E\wedge\delta)[a,b])+E\wedge\delta[\delta(a),b]+E\wedge\delta[a,\delta(b)]

\displaystyle=0-\delta(|a|a\delta(b)-|b|b\delta(a))

Lemma 4.2

Proof.

Write

\displaystyle\;[E\wedge\phi_{1},E\wedge\phi_{2}]_{S}

\displaystyle=\pm[E,E\wedge\phi_{2}]_{S}\wedge\phi_{1}\pm E\wedge[\phi_{1},E\wedge\phi_{2}]_{S}

\displaystyle=\pm([E,E]_{S}\wedge\phi_{2}\pm[E,\phi_{2}]_{S}\wedge E)\wedge\phi_{1}

\displaystyle\pm E\wedge([\phi_{1},E]_{S}\wedge\phi_{2}\pm[\phi_{1},\phi_{2}]_{S}\wedge E)

\displaystyle=0.

Let

\displaystyle=[E\wedge\phi,\pi+E\wedge\delta]_{S}

\displaystyle=[E\wedge\phi,\pi]_{S}+[E\wedge\phi,E\wedge\delta]_{S}

\displaystyle=0.

Therefore

∎

Part (1) is Lemma

.

(3) By Corollary

One of the main definitions in this paper is the following.

Definition 4.3
.

Let
A
be a
{\mathbb{Z}}
-graded Poisson algebra.

(1)

The

(or simply

)
of

is defined to be

(2)

We say

is

if
rgt(A)=0
.

(3)

We say

is
(-1)
-rigid

rgt(A)=-1
.

Note that this notion of rigidity is different from
the rigidity defined in
[

, Definition 0.1]

and other papers.

It follows from Lemma
4.2
that

(E4.3.1)

rgt(A)=rgt(A^{\delta}).

Other basic facts about
rgt(A)
are listed in the following lemma.

Lemma 4.4
.

Let
A
be a
{\mathbb{Z}}
-graded Poisson algebra with
A_{i}\neq 0

for some
i\neq 0
. In parts
(2)-(6)
, we further assume that

A
is
\Bbbk[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}]
with
\deg x_{i}>0
for all
i
.

(1)

Suppose that
rgt(A)=0
. Then every graded twist of
A
is isomorphic
to
A
.

(2)

If
A
is not unimodular, then
rgt(A)\leq-1
.

(3)

If
rgt(A)=0
, then
A
is unimodular.

(4)

If
rgt(A)\neq 0
, then
\dim_{\Bbbk}Gspd(A)\geq\dim_{\Bbbk}Gpd(A)\geq 2
.

(5)

If
rgt(A)=-1
, then
\dim_{\Bbbk}Gspd(A)=\dim_{\Bbbk}Gpd(A)=2
.

(6)

Let
\{A(a)\}_{a\in\Bbbk}
be a family of Poisson polynomial algebras such that

(i)

A(a)
is a Poisson twist of
A(a^{\prime})
for all
a,a^{\prime}\in\Bbbk
and that

(ii)
there is an
a_{0}
such that
A(a_{0})
is unimodular. If
\dim_{\Bbbk}Gpd(A(a_{0}))=2
, then
rgt(A(a))=-1
for all
a
.

(7)

If
rgt(A)=0
and
A
is a connected graded domain, then every
Poisson normal element of
A
is Poisson central.

(8)

If
\dim_{\Bbbk}Gpd(A)=1
and
\divv(E)\neq 0
, then

rgt(A)=0
.

Proof.

(1) Since
A_{i}\neq 0
for some
i
, the Euler derivation
E
is not zero.
Since
rgt(A)=0
,
Gspd(A)=\Bbbk E
. Let
B
be a graded twist of
A
. Then

B=A^{\delta}
where
\delta\in Gspd(A)
. Let
\langle-,-\rangle
be the
Poisson bracket of
B
. Since
\delta=\alpha E
for some
\alpha\in\Bbbk
,
one sees from (
E2.3.1
) that
\langle a,b\rangle=\{a,b\}
where

\{a,b\}
is the original Poisson bracket of
A
. The assertion follows.

(2) Since
A
is not unimodular, the modular derivation
{\mathbf{m}}

is not in
\Bbbk E
, as
\divv(E)=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\deg x_{i}\neq 0
and

\divv({\mathbf{m}})=0
[Lemma
0.1
]. Therefore

\dim_{\Bbbk}Gspd(A)\geq 2
. The assertion follows.

(3) This is equivalent to (2).

(4) By definition, it is clear that
\dim_{\Bbbk}Gspd(A)\geq\dim_{\Bbbk}Gpd(A)
. It remains to show
\dim_{\Bbbk}Gpd(A)\geq 2
. If
A
is unimodular, then, by Lemma
4.1
,

\dim_{\Bbbk}Gpd(A)=\dim_{\Bbbk}Gspd(A)=1-rgt(A)\geq 2.

Now we assume that
A
is not unimodular with nonzero
modular derivation
{\mathbf{m}}
. Since
\divv(E)\neq 0
and

\divv({\mathbf{m}})=0
, the
\Bbbk
-dimension of
Gpd(A)
is
at least 2 as desired.

(5) By definition,
\dim_{\Bbbk}Gspd(A)=1-rgt(A)=2
. The assertion
follows from part (4).

(6) It follows from Lemmas
4.1
and
4.2
that we have

rgt(A(a))=rgt(A(a_{0}))=1-\dim_{\Bbbk}Gspd(A(a_{0}))=1-\dim_{\Bbbk}Gpd(A(a_{0}))=-1.

The assertion follows from (4).

(7) We only need to consider a homogeneous Poisson normal
element
f
of positive degree. Note the log-Hamiltonian
derivation
LH_{f}:=f^{-1}\{f,-\}
is a Poisson derivation of
degree
0
. Suppose

is not central. Then
LH_{f}(f)\neq 0
.
Note that
LH_{f}
is clearly not the Euler derivation. Therefore

rgt(A)\leq-1
, yielding a contradiction. The assertion follows.

(8) We know
Gpd(A)
is spanned by the Euler derivation

,
which is not the modular derivation by Lemma
0.1
.
This implies that

is unimodular and the result follows by
Lemma
4.2
.
∎

will be given in the next 2 sections.

5.
Examples and comments

Note that the graded twists in the associative algebra setting
has an important property, namely, a graded algebra
R
and
its twist have isomorphic corresponding graded module categories

[
Zh
]
. So we are wondering if a similar result holds in the
Poisson setting. The following example shows that this is not the
case.

Example 5.1
.

Let
A
be the Poisson algebra
\Bbbk[x_{1},x_{2}]
with trivial
Poisson structure. Let
\delta
be the Poisson derivation of

A
determined by

\delta(x_{1})=0,\quad{\text{and}}\quad\delta(x_{2})=x_{2}.

Let
B
be the graded twist of
A
by
\delta
, namely,

B=A^{\delta}
. By definition,
B
is a Poisson algebra

\Bbbk[x_{1},x_{2}]
with Poisson bracket determined by

\{x_{1},x_{2}\}=x_{1}x_{2}.

Let
U(A)
denote the Poisson enveloping algebra of
A

[
Ba3
]
. Since
A
has the trivial Poisson structure,
U(A)

is the commutative polynomial ring
\Bbbk[x_{1},x_{2},y_{1},y_{2}]
.
Let
U(B)
be the Poisson enveloping algebra of
B
. We claim
that
U(B)
is not a graded twist of
U(A)
in the sense of

[
Zh
]
.

Suppose on the contrary that
U(B)
is a graded twist of
U(A)

in the sense of
[
Zh
]
. Then, by
[
Zh
, Theorem 1.1]
,

(E5.1.1)

{\text{GrMod-}}U(A)\cong{\text{GrMod-}}U(B).

Let
D(A)
(respectively,
D(B)
) be the degree zero part of
graded quotient ring of
U(A)
(respectively,
U(B)
). Then
it follows from (
E5.1.1
) that
D(A)\cong D(B)
. Since

U(A)
is commutative,
D(A)
is commutative. Thus
D(B)
is
commutative. Next we prove that
D(B)
is not commutative, so
we obtain a contradiction. By
[
Ba3
, Theorem 2.2]
,
U(B)

is generated by four elements
x_{1},x_{2},\delta_{1},\delta_{2}

and subject to 6 relations

\displaystyle x_{1}x_{2}

\displaystyle=x_{2}x_{1},

\displaystyle\delta_{1}x_{1}

\displaystyle=x_{1}\delta_{1},

\displaystyle\delta_{1}x_{2}

\displaystyle=x_{2}\delta_{1}+x_{1}x_{2},

\displaystyle\delta_{2}x_{1}

\displaystyle=x_{1}\delta_{2}-x_{1}x_{2},

\displaystyle\delta_{2}x_{2}

\displaystyle=x_{2}\delta_{2},

\displaystyle\delta_{2}\delta_{1}

\displaystyle=\delta_{1}\delta_{2}+x_{2}\delta_{1}+x_{1}\delta_{2}.

Let
a=x_{1}x_{2}^{-1}
and
b=\delta_{2}x_{2}^{-1}
which are
elements in
D(B)
. It follows from the six relations that

ba=ab-a.

So
D(B)
is not commutative, yielding a contradiction.
Therefore
U(B)
is not a graded twist of
U(A)
.

As a consequence, the category of graded Poisson modules over

A^{\delta}
, denoted by
{\text{GrPMod-}}A^{\delta}
is not
equivalent to the category of graded Poisson modules over

A
, denoted by
{\text{GrPMod-}}A
. That is,

{\text{GrPMod-}}A^{\delta}\not\cong{\text{GrPMod-}}A.

Remark 5.2
.

When
A
is a connected graded Poisson algebra with

A_{i}\neq 0
for some
i>0
. Then
PH^{1}(A)
is also graded.
Since
(PH^{1}(A))_{0}\cong Gpd(A)
, we have

rgt(A)\leq 1-\dim_{\Bbbk}(PH^{1}(A))_{0}
. If

\dim_{\Bbbk}(PH^{1}(A))_{0}=1
, then
rgt(A)=0
by Lemma
4.4
(8).
Therefore we can obtain information about
rgt(A)
if we know

PH^{1}(A)
.

Remark 5.3
.

Let
A
be the quadratic Poisson algebra corresponding to the

4
-dimensional Sklyanin algebra. The Poisson (co)homologies
of
A
have been computed in
[
Pe1
]
. By the Hilbert
series of Poisson homologies of
A
given in
[
Pe1
, p.1154]

and the Poincaré duality, both
PH^{0}(A)
and
PH^{1}(A)
have
Hilbert series
\frac{1}{(1-t^{2})^{2}}
. By Remark
5.2
,

rgt(A)=0
. Therefore
A
is rigid of graded twisting. By the
correspondence between quadratic Poisson algebras and the
homogeneous coordinate rings of quantum spaces, the corresponding
Sklyanin algebra is considered as rigid in some sense. Further,
since
h_{PH^{1}(A)}(t)=h_{PH^{0}(A)}(t)=h_{Z}(t)=\frac{1}{(1-t^{2})^{2}}
,

A
is
PH^{1}
-minimal in the sense of Definition
7.3
(1).

Note that the Poisson (co)homologies of the quadratic Poisson
algebra corresponding to the
3
-dimensional Sklyanin algebra were
computed in
[
Pe2
,
Pi1
,
VdB
]
, an argument similar to the above shows
that
rgt(A)=0
(namely,
A
is rigid of graded twisting) and
A
is

PH^{1}
-minimal. We will give an elementary and direct computation
of this
rgt(A)
in Example
6.6
(Case 3).

Remark 5.4
.

Let
n\geq 2
and
a\in\mathbb{C}
. Let

A(n,a)=\mathbb{C}[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}]
be the family of
Poisson polynomial algebras studied in
[
LS
]
. Then
one can check for each fixed
n\geq 2
the family

satisfies all the assumptions stated in
Lemma
4.4
(6) with

such that

. Computations are omitted. In

,

[

.

We will compute
rgt
for some classes of Poisson algebras.
Here is a warmup.

Example 5.5
.

Let
\deg x_{i}=i
for

. Let
\Omega=x^{6}+y^{3}+z^{2}+\lambda xyz
where

. For any

\Omega
is irreducible. Define a Poisson structure on

by

\{f,g\}:=\det\begin{pmatrix}\Omega_{x}&f_{x}&g_{x}\\
\Omega_{y}&f_{y}&g_{y}\\
\Omega_{z}&f_{z}&g_{z}\end{pmatrix}

for all

. It is easy to see that

(E5.5.1)

\displaystyle\{x,y\}

\displaystyle=\Omega_{z}=2z+\lambda xy,

(E5.5.2)

\displaystyle\{x,z\}

\displaystyle=-\Omega_{y}=-(3y^{2}+\lambda xz),

(E5.5.3)

\displaystyle\{y,z\}

\displaystyle=\Omega_{x}=6x^{5}+\lambda yz

In this case,
\Omega
has isolated singularity.
Let
\delta
be a graded Poisson derivation. Then

\displaystyle\delta(x)

\displaystyle=c_{1}x,

\displaystyle\delta(y)

\displaystyle=c_{2}y+c_{3}x^{2},

\displaystyle\delta(z)

\displaystyle=c_{4}z+c_{5}x^{3}+c_{6}xy.

Subtracting by

, we may assume that
c_{1}=0
. Applying
\delta

(with
c_{1}=0
) to (

), we
obtain that

c_{2}(2z+\lambda xy)=2(c_{4}z+c_{5}x^{3}+c_{6}xy)+\lambda x(c_{2}y+c_{3}x^{2}),

which implies that
c_{2}=c_{4}
,
c_{6}=0
, and
2c_{5}+\lambda c_{3}=0
.
Applying
\delta

(with
c_{1}=0
and
c_{6}=0

.

. By Lemma
4.4
(1),

does
not have any non-trivial twists.

(after matching

6.6

6.
Some computations of
rgt

In this section we compute
rgt
for all quadratic Poisson
structures on
\Bbbk[x,y,z]
. Some of the computations have
been done by other researchers in different language (for
example, some are hidden inside in Poisson cohomology
computation), but we provide all details of computations of

rgt
for completeness. The classification of all quadratic
Poisson structures on
\Bbbk[x,y,z]
were given in

[
DH
,
DML
,
LX
]
.

First we fix some notations. Let
\Bbbk
be an algebraically
closed field of characteristic zero (one might assume

\Bbbk={\mathbb{C}}
if necessary). Let

V=A_{1}=\Bbbk x+\Bbbk y+\Bbbk z
and let
\{-,-\}
be a
quadratic Poisson bracket of
A:=\Bbbk[x,y,z]=\Bbbk[V]
. Let

f
be a graded Poisson derivation of
(A,\{-,-\})
. Let

W=\{V,V\}
. It is clear that

(E6.0.1)

f(W)=f(\{V,V\})\subseteq\{f(V),V\}+\{V,f(V)\}\subseteq\{V,V\}=W.

Write

(E6.0.2)

f(x)=a_{1}x+a_{2}y+a_{3}z,f(y)=b_{1}x+b_{2}y+b_{3}z,f(z)=c_{1}x+c_{2}y+c_{3}z.

After replacing
f
by
f-a_{1}E
, we can further assume that

(E6.0.3)

a_{1}\ {\text{in \eqref{E6.0.2} is zero.}}

If
\{-,-\}
is unimodular, then it comes from a “potential”

\Omega\in A_{3}
. One can classify
\Omega
as follows: (a):

\Omega
is a product of three linear terms, (b):
\Omega
is a
product of a linear term and an irreducible polynomial of
degree
2
, and (c):
\Omega
is irreducible of degree
3
.
This classification is classical and well-known, see for
example
[
BM
,
KM
,
Ri
]
.

The following four examples deal with the first case, namely,

\Omega
is a product of three linear terms.

Define
A_{sing}
to be
A/(\Omega_{x},\Omega_{y},\Omega_{z})
.

Example 6.1
.

Let
\Omega=x^{3}
. Then

\displaystyle\{x,y\}

\displaystyle=\Omega_{z}=0,

\displaystyle\{z,x\}

\displaystyle=\Omega_{y}=0,

\displaystyle\{y,z\}

\displaystyle=\Omega_{x}=3x^{2}.

It is clear that
\Kdim(A_{sing})=2
. Let
f
be a Poisson
derivation of
A
. By (

)

2xf(x)=f(x^{2})\in f(W)\subseteq W=\Bbbk x^{2}.

Then

for some

. By (
E6.0.3
),

(

to

b_{2}+c_{3}=0
with

. One can check that every Poisson

Example 6.2
.

Let
\Omega=x^{2}y
. Then

\displaystyle\{x,y\}

\displaystyle=\Omega_{z}=0,

(E6.2.1)

\displaystyle\{z,x\}

\displaystyle=\Omega_{y}=x^{2},

\displaystyle\{y,z\}

\displaystyle=\Omega_{x}=2xy.

and

Example 6.3

\displaystyle\{x,y\}

\displaystyle=\Omega_{z}=xy,

\displaystyle\{z,x\}

\displaystyle=\Omega_{y}=xz,

\displaystyle\{x,y\}

\displaystyle=\Omega_{z}=0,

\displaystyle\{z,x\}

\displaystyle=\Omega_{y}=x^{2}+2xy,

Lemma 6.10
.

Let
A
be the quadratic Poisson algebra in Example

6.6
(2) with potential
\Omega=x^{3}+x^{2}z+y^{2}z
.
Then every ozone derivation of
A
is Hamiltonian.

Proof.

The Jacobian Poisson structure on
A={\Bbbk}[x,y,z]
is explicitly
given by

\{x,y\}=x^{2}+y^{2},\ \{y,z\}=3x^{2}+2xz,\ \{z,x\}=2yz.

We show that every ozone derivation of
A
is Hamiltonian,
which is based on a tedious computation. Since
A
is graded,
it suffices to check every graded Poisson derivation
\phi
of
degree
n
vanishing on the Poisson center
Z
is Hamiltonian. So
we can write

\phi(x)=\sum_{i=0}^{n+1}\phi_{i}x^{i}\in{\Bbbk}[x,y,z]\text{ of degree $n+1$ with $\phi_{i}\in{\Bbbk}[y,z]_{n+1-i}$. }

Claim 1:
By subtracting a Hamiltonian derivation

H_{g}=\{g,-\}
from
\phi
for some suitable
g\in{\Bbbk}[x,y,z]_{n}
,
we can assume that
\phi_{i}\in{\Bbbk}[z]\bigoplus y^{3}z({\Bbbk}[z,y^{2}z])

for all
0\leq i\leq n-1
.

Proof:
For simplicity, we denote the
{\Bbbk}
-linear map

T_{m}:{\Bbbk}[y,z]_{m}\to{\Bbbk}[y,z]_{m+1}
by

T_{m}(f):=2yz\frac{\partial{f}}{\partial z}-y^{2}\frac{\partial{f}}{\partial y}\text{ for any $m\geq 0$.}

It is clear to check that

•

{\rm ker}(T_{m})=0
if
3\nmid m
and

{\rm ker}(T_{m})={\Bbbk}(y^{2}z)^{\frac{m}{3}}
if
3\mid m
;

•

{\rm img}(T_{m})\bigoplus y({\rm ker}(T_{m}))=y({\Bbbk}[y,z]_{m})
.
In particular
y(y^{2}z)^{\frac{m}{3}}\not\in{\rm img}(T_{m})

if
3\mid m
.

For any homogeneous polynomial
g=\sum g_{i}x^{i}\in{\Bbbk}[x,y,z]_{n}

with
g_{i}\in{\Bbbk}[y,z]_{n-i}
, we get

\displaystyle\{g,x\}

\displaystyle=\frac{\partial g}{\partial y}\{y,x\}+\frac{\partial g}{\partial z}\{z,x\}=-\frac{\partial g}{\partial y}(x^{2}+y^{2})+\frac{\partial g}{\partial z}(2yz)

\displaystyle=-\frac{\partial g_{n-1}}{\partial y}x^{n+1}-\frac{\partial g_{n-2}}{\partial y}x^{n}+(T_{1}(g_{n-1})-\frac{\partial g_{n-3}}{\partial y})x^{n-1}+\cdots

\displaystyle\quad+(T_{n-2}(g_{2})-\frac{\partial g_{0}}{\partial y})x^{2}+T_{n-1}(g_{1})x+T_{n}(g_{0}).

Hence by choosing
g_{0},g_{1},\ldots,g_{n-1}
for the coefficients
of
x^{0},x^{1},\ldots,x^{n-1}
inductively, we can achieve

Claim 1
.

Claim 2:
Up to modulo a Hamiltonian derivation, we
can set

\displaystyle\phi(x)

\displaystyle=xzf(x,z)+y^{3}zg(x,y^{2}z)

\displaystyle\phi(y)

\displaystyle=(\frac{3}{2}xy+yz)f(x,z)+(\frac{3}{2}y^{4}-xy^{2}z)g(x,y^{2}z)+(x^{2}+y^{2})p

\displaystyle\phi(z)

\displaystyle=-(3xz+2z^{2})f(x,z)-3y^{3}zg(x,y^{2}z)-2yzp

where
f(x,z)\in{\Bbbk}[x,z]_{n-1}
,
g(x,y^{2}z)\in{\Bbbk}[x,y^{2}z]_{n-3}

and
p\in{\Bbbk}[x,y,z]_{n-1}
. Furthermore, we can assume
p
does not
contain
x^{n-1}
.

Proof:
By
Claim 1
, up to a Hamiltonian derivation, we
can write

\displaystyle\phi(x)=xzf(x,z)+y^{3}zg(x,y^{2}z)+az^{n+1}+bx^{n}y+cx^{n+1}

for some coefficients
a,b,c\in{\Bbbk}
. Since
{\Bbbk}[\Omega]\subseteq Z
,
we have

\displaystyle\phi(\Omega)=(3x^{2}+2xz)\phi(x)+2yz\phi(y)+(x^{2}+y^{2})\phi(z)=0.

We have three terms
2axz^{n+2}
,
3cx^{n+3}
and
3bx^{n+2}y

appearing in
(3x^{2}+2xz)\phi(x)
, which can only be canceled from the
remaining terms in
2yz\phi(y)+(x^{2}+y^{2})\phi(z)
. We get
a=0

since
xz^{n+2}
is certainly not contained there. For the term

3cx^{n+3}
, it has to be canceled by the terms in
(x^{2}+y^{2})\phi(z)
,
which implies that
\phi(z)=-3cx^{n+1}+\cdots
. But the another term

-3cx^{n+1}y^{2}
appearing in
(x^{2}+y^{2})\phi(z)
has to be canceled
inside the same product
(x^{2}+y^{2})\phi(z)
. Repeating the argument,
we get there is some

such that
3cx^{n+3}+(x^{2}+y^{2})v=0
.
This is absurd unless
c=0
. The same argument shows that
b=0
as well.
So we can write
\phi(x)
as in
Claim 2
and the expressions
of
\phi(y)
and
\phi(z)
follow immediately. Finally, by further

\{ax^{n},-\}
from

, we can replace

with

assume

does not contain

Claim 3

Let

Let

Definition 7.1

It is clear that

For the rest of this section, we only consider locally finite

-graded Poisson algebras

Lemma 7.2

Suppose

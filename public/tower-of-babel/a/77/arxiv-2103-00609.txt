Multifractality Breaking from Bounded Random Measures
L. Moriconi

Abstract
Multifractal systems usually have singularity spectra defined on bounded sets of Hölder exponents. As a consequence, their associated multifractal scaling exponents are expected to depend linearly upon statistical moment orders at high enough orders – a phenomenon referred to as the linearization effect . Motivated by general ideas taken from models of turbulent intermittency and focusing on the case of two-dimensional systems, we investigate the issue within the framework of Gaussian multiplicative chaos. As verified by means of Monte Carlo simulations, it turns out that the linearization effect can be accounted for by Liouville-like random measures defined in terms of upper-bounded scalar fields. The coarse-grained statistical properties of Gaussian multiplicative chaos are furthermore found to be preserved in the linear regime of the scaling exponents. As a related application, we look at the problem of turbulent circulation statistics, and obtain a remarkably accurate evaluation of circulation statistical moments, recently determined with the help of massive numerical simulations.

II
Problem Setup

To set the stage for the issues we aim to address in this paper, let us consider
the example of a
d
-dimensional positive-definite multifractal scalar field

\psi(x)
, described by some translation invariant probability measure, in
such a way that the statistical moments of the renormalized fields

\psi_{a}(x)\equiv\frac{1}{a^{d}}\int_{{\cal{D}}_{a}}d^{d}x\psi(x)\ ,\

(1)

where
{\cal{D}}_{a}
is the spatial domain
|x^{\prime}-x|\leq a
,
behave as

\mathbb{E}[(\psi_{a}(x))^{q}]\sim a^{\tau_{q}}\ .\

(2)

The scaling exponents
\tau_{q}
can be derived within the multifractal
language as follows
frisch-parisi
. Let
D(h)
, referred to as the

singularity spectrum
, be the fractal dimension of the set of points
which have Hölder exponent
h
in the ensemble realizations of
\psi(x)
.
Regarding

as a random variable to be sorted when an arbitrary region
of size

behavior
\psi_{a}(x)\sim a^{h^{\prime}}
where
h^{\prime}\in[h,h+dh]
is, thus,
\rho(h)dh\sim a^{d-D(h)}dh
.
We get, from these assumptions, that

\mathbb{E}[(\psi_{a}(x))^{q}]\sim\int dh\rho(h)a^{qh}\sim\int dha^{hq+d-D(h)}\ .\

(3)

At small enough scales, the dependence of the above expectation values

\tau_{q}={\hbox{inf}}_{h}[hq+d-D(h)]\ .\

The scaling exponent

General arguments

clarity, we adopt here the convention that multifractality refers to the
case of scaling exponents which are strictly concave, that is,

As already alluded in the introductory section, multifractality is expected to be broken

,

is a model-dependent critical moment order,

;

;
ossiander_etal
;
Lahermes_etal1
;

;

;

;

;

Therefore, taking into account that as

\tau_{q}=h_{\ast}q+d-D(h_{\ast})\ ,\

The singularity spectrum can be well approximated in very many instances by a parabolic function of

Centering our attention on two-dimensional modeling, the GMC approximation means, in practice, that

Periodic boundary conditions are assumed for the scalar field

Taking

The bare field

can be identified with the ultraviolet regularized field

A scaling relation similar to (

can then be derived from (

We wonder, thus, if it is possible to implement modifications in the GMC computational

III
Bounded Cascades

We briefly outline, in the following subsections A and B, two phenomenological views on the

formalism, give relevant hints on how to establish the linearization effect in the framework

III.1
The Obukhov-Kolmogorov lognormal model of turbulent intermittency

If
\psi(x)
is used to model the turbulent dissipation field in homogeneous and isotropic
turbulence, commonly denoted by
\epsilon(x)
, relations (
1
) and (
2
)
yield a precise formulation of the Kolmogorov refined similarity hypothesis, a central point
in the Obukhov-Kolmogorov (OK62) modeling of turbulent intermittency

;

.

In the OK62 phenomenology, multiplicative cascade fluctuations of the energy transfer
rates per unit mass and unit time,
\epsilon_{a}
and
\epsilon_{b}
, across two different
length scales
a
and
b
, respectively, are related as

\epsilon_{a}=\epsilon_{b}W_{1}W_{2}...W_{n}\ .\

(12)

The
W^{\prime}s
are lognormally i.i.d. random variables, with unit mean,
and
n=\log_{2}(b/a)
, taken to be a positive integer,
gives the number of modeled steps in the turbulent energy cascade between the scales

a
and
b
. They are assumed to lay within the inertial range scales, that is,

\eta\leq a<b\leq L
, where
L
and
\eta
define the integral and dissipative
length scales, respectively, of the turbulent flow.
Eq. (
12
) is to be understood in the probabilistic sense as an equality
in law for
\epsilon_{a}
and
\epsilon_{b}
.

Considering
b=L
, that is, the scale where energy is injected into the flow with
non-fluctuating energy transfer rate
\epsilon_{L}
, then it is a straightforward exercise
to show, from (
12
), that

\mathbb{E}[\epsilon_{a}^{q}]\sim\epsilon_{L}^{q}\left(\frac{a}{L}\right)^{\tau_{q}}

(13)

holds for
\eta\leq a\leq L
, in the same fashion as (
9
) and
(
11
), where
\tau_{q}
is given as in (
10
), with

\gamma^{2}=2\pi\log_{2}\mathbb{E}[W^{2}]\ .\

(14)

As a relevant note for future use, we introduce the Gaussian random variable
X_{p}
, through

W_{p}\equiv\exp(\gamma X_{p})\ .\

(15)

The OK62 cascade argument (
12
) can in this way be recalled to
suggest, taking a look at (
6
), that pointwise fluctuations of

\psi(x)
can be derived from
\psi\sim\exp(\gamma\phi)
, where

\phi=\sum_{p=1}^{\log_{2}(L/\eta)}X_{p}\ .\

(16)

III.2
The random
\beta
-model of turbulent intermittency

An alternative OK62-like cascade picture of the energy transfer rate fluctuations
across scales, as synthetized in Eq. (
12
), can be put forward in order
to render it closer to contemporary multifractal ideas and in compliance with
general physical principles like energy conservation.

In the random
\beta
-model
benzi_etal
, an arbitrary energy-containing eddy defined
at length scale
a
produces, during its lifetime, a random number
M_{a}\leq 2^{d}

of descendent eddies (in
d
dimensions), all of them defined at length scale
a/2
.
Energy conservation implies that the power supplied by the mother-eddy
to its descendents has to be same as the total power supplied by
the latter ones to their further descendents and, as a consequence,

M_{a}\epsilon_{a/2}(a/2)^{d}=\epsilon_{a}a^{d}
, that is

\epsilon_{a/2}=\beta_{a}^{-1}\epsilon_{a}\ ,\

(17)

where
\beta_{a}=M_{a}/2^{d}
is the fraction of volume that the whole group of
descendent eddies (the “sibling-eddies”) occupy with the respect to the volume
of their mother-eddy.

Assuming that generation after generation the
\beta^{\prime}s
are completely independent
and randomly distributed according to the same probability density function
f(\beta)
,
Eq. (
12
) still holds for the energy transfer rates of each individual eddy, with
(subindices suppressed)

W=\beta^{-1}\ .\

(18)

We have, therefore,

\epsilon_{a}=\left[\prod_{i=1}^{n}\beta_{i}^{-1}\right]\epsilon_{L}\ ,\

(19)

where
n=\log_{2}(L/a)
. Energy transfer rates have, now, statistical moments

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[\epsilon_{a}^{q}]\sim\epsilon_{L}^{q}\left[\int_{0}^{1}d\beta f(\beta)\beta^{1-q}\right]^{n}

\displaystyle=\epsilon_{L}^{q}\mathbb{E}[\beta^{1-q}]^{n}\sim\epsilon_{L}^{q}\left(\frac{a}{L}\right)^{\tau_{q}}\ ,\

(20)

where

\tau_{q}=-\log_{2}\mathbb{E}[\beta^{1-q}]\ .\

(21)

Note that the expectation value (
20
)
takes into account the fact that in the random
\beta
-model
eddies are not space-filling structures.

The particular modeling case where
\beta
is fixed to some arbitrary value

\beta_{0}

, associated to the probability distribution function

f(\beta)=\delta(\beta-\beta_{0})\ ,\

(22)

gives, in view of (
21
), the linear scaling exponents

\tau_{q}=(q-1)\log_{2}\beta_{0}\ .\

(23)

Furthermore, still considering the situation of fixed
\beta
, we infer that
a mother-eddy at the integral scale
L
(the “mother of all mothers”) is the source,
along the turbulent cascade, of a number

N_{a}\sim(2^{d}\beta_{0})^{\log_{2}(L/a)}\sim\left(\frac{L}{a}\right)^{d+\log_{2}\beta_{0}}

(24)

of descendent eddies at length scale
a
. The scaling law (
24
)
indicates that the fractal dimension of the energy-containing eddies is, here,

d_{F}=d+\log_{2}\beta_{0}\ .\

(25)

III.3
The linearization effect in the theory of GMC

As discussed in Sec. II, the linearization
effect takes place when statistical moments get dominated by fluctuations
associated to the most singular set of configurations, which are the ones
which have the minimum available Hölder exponent, denoted in Eq. (
5
)
by
h_{\ast}
. Due to the concavity properties of the singularity spectrum,
we expect the fractal dimension of the most singular set,
D(h_{\ast})
,
to be the smallest allowed one (for the evaluation of positive order moments).

We also note that Eq. (
25
) can be used to establish a mapping
between values of
\beta
, from the side of the random
\beta
-model, to
the fractal dimensions encompassed by the singularity spectrum
D(h)
,
from the side of the multifractal formalism. In the language
of the random
\beta
-model, the linearization effect follows from the
existence of a minimum value of
\beta
, say
\beta_{\ast}
, obtained from

D(h_{\ast})=d+\log_{2}\beta_{\ast}\ .\

(26)

Taking (
18
) into account, we conclude that the cascade factors

W^{\prime}s
are, under these conditions, upper bounded random variables, viz.,

W\leq 1/\beta_{\ast}
. Correspondingly, we see,
from the context of the OK62 phenomenology,
that bounded
W^{\prime}s
should be related to bounded scalar fields

\phi(x)
in the GMC setup, as indicated by (
15
) and
(
16
).

Relying upon the above heuristic considerations, we are, now,
ready to propose a modified version of the two-dimensional GMC,
as given by Eqs. (
6
-
8
),
in order to accommodate in its formal structure the linearization
effect. To do so, we actually keep the definition of the functional
probability measure (
7
), but

(i) replace the Liouville measure (
6
) by the more general expression

\psi(x)=\frac{\psi_{0}}{\mathbb{E}[\tilde{\psi}(x)]}\tilde{\psi}(x)\ ,\

(27)

where

\tilde{\psi}(x)=\exp[\gamma\phi(x)]\ ;\

(28)

(ii) replace the Euclidean action, Eq. (
8
), by

S[\phi]=\int d^{2}x\left[\frac{1}{2}(\partial_{i}\phi)^{2}+V(\phi)\right]\ ,\

(29)

where

V(\phi)=\begin{cases}0\ ,\ \text{if $\phi<\phi_{0}$}\ ,\ \\
V_{0}\ ,\ \text{if $\phi\geq\phi_{0}$}\ ,\ \\
\end{cases}

(30)

with
V_{0}\rightarrow\infty
and

\phi_{0}=C\ln(L/\eta)\ ,\

(31)

where
C
is an adjustable positive constant (observe that
(
31
) follows from (
16
) by taking

X_{p}=C\ln 2
).

In short words, we have just postulated that
the Liouville measure (
27
) gets upper bounded due to the
existence of a scalar field threshold
\phi_{0}
, and that it fluctuates
as usually determined by the free field action (
8
), if

\phi(x)<\phi_{0}
in an arbitrary neighborhood of
x
.

An analytical treatment of the modified GMC scenario, as defined by
Eqs. (
27
-
31
),
is challenging. However, it is possible to proceed along with
Monte Carlo numerical validations, as detailed next.

IV
Monte Carlo Simulations

We have performed Monte Carlo simulations to study the fluctuations of

\gamma=1
,
using (

Statistical ensembles with configurations of

\mathbb{E}_{0}[\phi^{2}]=\frac{1}{2\pi}\ln\left(\frac{L}{\eta}\right)\ .\

Taking,

Each Monte Carlo run consisted of

iterations, sampled at every other

The field derivatives in the action (

evaluated by means of central differences. Monte Carlo variations

(defined at lattice sites) were given by independent

V
Turbulent Circulation Statistics

The relevance of the circulation variable

as a multiscale “mathematical probe” of turbulent vortical
structures, pointed for the first time some 25 years ago

, has recently found renewed interest with the advent of

;

;

, including possible connections between the statistics

Let us center our attention on the particular definition of circulation as

\Gamma_{R}\equiv\int_{\mathcal{C}}d^{2}{\bf{r}}\,\omega({\bf{r}})\ ,\

(35)

where
\mathcal{C}
is a disk of radius

and
\omega({\bf{r}})
is the component of vorticity which is normal
(with arbitrary orientation) to the plane that contains
\mathcal{C}
. The scaling form for the circulation moments,

\mathbb{E}[|\Gamma_{R}|^{q}]\sim R^{\lambda_{q}}\ ,\

(36)

\eta\ll R\ll L

We are here mainly interested to model the scaling exponents

in (

The Kolmogorov phenomenological description of turbulence (K41)

\lambda_{q}=4q/3
, which has been noted to be a very good approximation only for

Tracing back circulation fluctuations to the presence of vortex tubes, it was proposed, in

36

\omega({\bf{r}})\sim\xi_{R}\tilde{\omega}({\bf{r}})\ ,\

(37)

\xi_{R}\equiv\frac{1}{\pi R^{2}}\int_{\mathcal{C}}d^{2}{\bf{r}}\,\sqrt{\epsilon({\bf{r}})}

is a functional of the dissipation field
\epsilon({\bf{r}})
, modeled as a Liouville measure density,
and
\tilde{\omega}({\bf{r}})
is an independent Gaussian random field, with vanishing mean
and correlator

The scaling exponent

VI
Conclusions

We have been able to address a variation of GMC, as described from relations (
27
-
31
),
which gives room for the linearization effect, a phenomenon commonly observed in multifractal systems. The key technical point in the definition of the modified GMC setting is the introduction of upper-bounded Liouville measures.

Our line of reasoning has been closely motivated by cascade models of turbulent intermittency and their connections with the mulifractal language and the theory of GMC. We validated the modified picture of GMC by means of straightforward Monte Carlo simulations and applied it to the problem of turbulent circulation statistics. We developed, in this way, accurate evaluations of the scaling exponents for the
statistical moments of circulation, previously established only through extensive numerical simulations
Iyer_etal
.

Further work is in order. It would be very interesting to devise a mathematically rigorous analysis of the
linearization effect in the GMC, as discussed in this work, and to extend it to general space dimensions. Additional
Monte Carlo simulations are also welcome to explore the validity range (as the field bound
\phi_{0}
, the system size,
and moment orders are changed) of the modified GMC picture. The empirical (numerical) implementation of bounded Liouville measures to models based on the theory of GMC should not present any relevant technical or conceptual difficulty.

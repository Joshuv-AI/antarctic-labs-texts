Cokernel statistics for walk matrices of directed and weighted random graphs
Alexander Van Werde

Abstract
The walk matrix associated to an n × n n\times n integer matrix 𝐗 \mathbf{X} and an integer vector b b is defined by 𝐖 := ( b , 𝐗 ​ b , … , 𝐗 n − 1 ​ b ) \mathbf{W}\vcentcolon=(b,\mathbf{X}b,\ldots,\mathbf{X}^{n-1}b) . We study limiting laws for the cokernel of 𝐖 \mathbf{W} in the scenario where 𝐗 \mathbf{X} is a random matrix with independent entries and b b is deterministic. Our first main result provides a formula for the distribution of the p m p^{m} -torsion part of the cokernel, as a group, when 𝐗 \mathbf{X} has independent entries from a specific distribution. The second main result relaxes the distributional assumption and concerns the ℤ ⁡ [ x ] \mathbb{Z}[x] -module structure. The motivation for this work arises from an open problem in spectral graph theory which asks to show that random graphs are often determined up to isomorphism by their (generalized) spectrum. Sufficient conditions for generalized spectral determinacy can namely be stated in terms of the cokernel of a walk matrix. Extensions of our results could potentially be used to determine how often those conditions are satisfied. Some remaining challenges for such extensions are outlined in the paper.

Results

Given an Abelian group

In a directed and weighted setting, the following result gives a remarkably simple formula for the limiting marginal distribution for a single prime

Theorem 1.2
.

Fix a prime
p
and an integer
m\geq 1
.
For every
n\geq 1
, let
\mathbf{X}
be a
\mathbb{Z}^{n\times n}
-valued random matrix with independent
\operatorname{Unif}\{0,1,\ldots,p^{m}-1\}
-distributed entries and let
b\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}
be a deterministic vector with
b\not\equiv 0\bmod p

Fix some
\ell\geq 1
and

.
Let
i_{0}\vcentcolon=\#\{i\leq\ell:\lambda_{i}=m\}

\delta_{j}\vcentcolon=\lambda_{\ell-j+1}-\lambda_{\ell-j}

Then, as Abelian groups,

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\mathbb{P}\Bigl(\operatorname{coker}(\mathbf{W})_{p^{m}}\cong\bigoplus_{i=1}^{\ell}\frac{\mathbb{Z}}{p^{\lambda_{i}}\mathbb{Z}}\Bigr)=\prod_{i=i_{0}}^{\infty}\Bigl(1-p^{-(i+1)}\Bigr)\prod_{j=1}^{\ell}p^{-j\delta_{j}}.

Note in particular that
Theorem

1.2
predicts that
\operatorname{coker}(\mathbf{W})_{p^{2}}\in\{0,\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z}\}
with nonvanishing probability, at least for random directed and weighted graphs.
For example, the limiting probabilities associated with the primes

, and

We here emphasize odd primes because, while

1.2
also applies when

\operatorname{coker}(\mathbf{W})_{2^{m}}
is very different for simple and non-simple graphs; see

It would hence be ill-advised to use directed graphs as a model for the condition
\operatorname{coker}(\mathbf{W})_{2^{2}}\cong(\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z})^{\lfloor n/2\rfloor}
.

The proof of

The downside is that the distributional assumption on the entries of

Fortunately, a different proof approach allows us to study

Additionally, this allows us to gain insight on the joint law across different primes and the result even applies to sparse graphs.

Remark 1.5
.

The restriction that
\alpha_{n}\gg\ln(n)/n
in
Theorem

1.3
, which limits how sparse the matrices are allowed to be, is close to optimal.
The conclusion of
Theorem

1.3
namely has to fail when
\mathbf{X}
has independent entries satisfying
\mathbb{P}(\mathbf{X}_{i,j}=0)\geq 1-(1-\varepsilon)\ln(n)/n
for
\varepsilon>0
.

Indeed, the coupon collector theorem then implies that
\mathbf{X}
has many rows equal to zero so that
\operatorname{rank}(\mathbf{X})\leq n-2
with high probability.
The latter implies that
\mathbf{X}(\mathbb{F}_{p}^{n})+\mathbb{F}_{p}b\neq\mathbb{F}_{p}^{n}
.
Hence, since
\operatorname{coker}(\mathbf{W})_{p,x}\cong\mathbb{Z}^{n}/(\mathbf{W}(\mathbb{Z}^{n})+p\mathbb{Z}^{n}+x\mathbb{Z}^{n})
is isomorphic to
\mathbb{F}_{p}^{n}/(\mathbf{X}(\mathbb{F}_{p}^{n})+\mathbb{F}_{p}b)
, it would follow that
\lim_{n\to\infty}\mathbb{P}(\operatorname{coker}(\mathbf{W})_{p,x}=\{0\})=0
.
This is incompatible with the conclusion of
Theorem

1.3
since
\mu_{p,x}(\{0\})=\prod_{j=1}^{\infty}(1-p^{-1-j})\neq 0
.

1.2
Proof techniques

The proof of
Theorem

1.2
relies on an analysis of the sequence of random vectors
(\mathbf{X}^{t-1}b)_{t=1}^{n}
, viewed as a stochastic process.
More precisely, we show in
Corollary

2.2
that the group structure of
\operatorname{coker}(\mathbf{W})_{p^{m}}
can be computed in terms of the sequence of random variables
0=U_{1}\leq U_{2}\leq\ldots\leq U_{n}\leq\infty
defined by

\displaystyle U_{t}\vcentcolon=\sup\bigl\{j\geq 0:\mathbf{X}^{t-1}b\in\operatorname{span}_{\mathbb{Z}}(\mathbf{X}^{i-1}b:1\leq i\leq t-1)+p^{j}\mathbb{Z}^{n}\bigr\}.

(1.1)

Here, given a ring
R
and
v_{1},\ldots,v_{t}\in R^{n}
we write
\operatorname{span}_{R}(v_{1},\ldots,v_{t})\vcentcolon=\{\sum_{i=1}^{t}c_{i}v_{i}:c_{i}\in R\}
.
The remaining difficulty is then to study the joint law of the
U_{j}
.
The distributional assumption in
Theorem

1.2
plays an important role for the latter task: the independence and equidistribution of the entries implies that
\mathbf{X}
induces a uniform random endomorphism of
(\mathbb{Z}/p^{m}\mathbb{Z})^{n}
which can be used to establish Markovian dynamics for
\min\{U_{t},m\}
; see
Lemma

2.4
.

This proof yields a direct combinatorial interpretation for the formula in
Theorem

1.2
.
Given
U_{t}
, a counting argument implies that the probability that
U_{t+1}\geq U_{t}+\delta
is
p^{-\delta(n-t)}
for any
\delta
satisfying
U_{t}+\delta\leq m
.
Taking
j=n-t
then explains the factors of the form
p^{-j\delta_{j}}
in
Theorem

1.2
.
Factors of the form
1-p^{-j}
arise when we additionally have to enforce that
U_{t+1}\leq U_{t}+\delta
.
A further benefit of the approach is that it can also be used to establish the law of
\operatorname{coker}(\mathbf{W})_{p^{m}}
for finite
n
; see
Proposition

2.5
.

The proof of
Theorem

1.3
relies on more sophisticated techniques.
In particular, we employ the
category-theoretic moment method
.
In classical probability theory, the moment method allows one to establish convergence in distribution of a sequence of
\mathbb{R}
-valued random variables
(Y_{i})_{i=1}^{\infty}
by showing that the moments
\mathbb{E}[Y_{i}^{n}]
converge to those of the desired limiting law provided that some mild conditions are satisfied.
Results of Sawin and Wood
[
20
]
similarly allow one to establish limiting laws for random algebraic objects such as groups and modules by showing that category-theoretic moments converge.
Here, given a ring
R
and a deterministic
R
-module
N
, the
N
-moment
3
3

3

moment
may be found in
[
4
, Section 3.3]
.
of a random
R
-module
Y
is given by
\mathbb{E}[\#\operatorname{Sur}_{R}(Y,N)]
where
\operatorname{Sur}_{R}(Y,N)
denotes the set of surjective
R
-module morphisms from
Y
to
N
.

The main challenge is hence to estimate the
N
-moments of the random
\mathbb{Z}[x]
-module
\operatorname{coker}(\mathbf{W})
.
To this end, we employ a strategy developed by Wood
[
32
,
33
]
and Nguyen and Wood
[
15
]
for the estimation of moments of random algebraic objects associated with random matrices with
\alpha
-balanced entries.
A key difference between our setting and the one in
[
32
,
33
,
15
]
is that we have little control over the joint law of the entries
\mathbf{W}_{i,j}
of our matrix of interest since these are nontrivial algebraic combinations of the entries of
\mathbf{X}
.
This is why it is essential to view
\operatorname{coker}(\mathbf{W})
as a
\mathbb{Z}[x]
-module, not only as a group.
That is, the
\mathbb{Z}[x]
-module-theoretic viewpoint allows us to untangle the algebraic dependencies and hence estimate the category-theoretic moments; see (
3.3
) and the subsequent remarks.

The advantage of the category-theoretic approach is that it is robust, as is demonstrated by the general distributional assumptions in
Theorem

1.3
.
For comparison, the proof approach for
Theorem

1.2
is highly non-robust.
Indeed, as mentioned above, that proof uses that
\mathbf{X}
induces a uniform random endomorphism of
(\mathbb{Z}/p^{m}\mathbb{Z})^{n}
; a property which is only satisfied when
\mathbf{X}\bmod p^{m}
has independent and uniformly distributed entries.
The robustness of the category-theoretic approach makes us hopeful that we will be able to generalize it in future work, although this remains a nontrivial task; see
Section

4
.

2.1
Computing
\operatorname{coker}(\mathbf{W})_{p^{m}}
in terms of
U_{1},\ldots,U_{n}

Recall the definition of
U_{t}
from (
1.1
).
Fix an integer
m\geq 1
and abbreviate
R\vcentcolon=\mathbb{Z}/p^{m}\mathbb{Z}
.
The following lemma then produces an
R
-module basis for
R^{n}
which is well-adapted to the computation of
\operatorname{coker}(\mathbf{W})_{p^{m}}
.

Lemma 2.1
.

Write
\Lambda_{t}\vcentcolon=\min\{U_{t},m\}
.
Then, there exist
v_{1},\ldots,v_{n}\in R^{n}
such that for every
t\leq n
the following properties are satisfied:

(i)

The reduction of the matrix
(v_{1},\ldots,v_{t})
modulo
p
has rank
t
over
\mathbb{F}_{p}
.

(ii)

It holds that
\operatorname{span}_{R}(\mathbf{X}^{i-1}b\bmod p^{m}:1\leq i\leq t)=\operatorname{span}_{R}(p^{\Lambda_{i}}v_{i}:1\leq i\leq t)
.

Proof.

We proceed by induction on
t
.
If
t=1
, then
U_{1}=0
so
v_{1}\equiv b\bmod p^{m}
satisfies both properties.
Now suppose that
t>1
and assume that there exist
v_{1},\ldots,v_{t-1}
such that both properties are satisfied.
We prove the existence of some
v_{t}
.

First, suppose that
U_{t}\geq m
.
Then, the definition (
1.1
) yields
\operatorname{span}_{R}(\mathbf{X}^{i-1}b\bmod p^{m}:i\leq t)=\operatorname{span}_{R}(\mathbf{X}^{i-1}b\bmod p^{m}:i\leq t-1)
and
p^{\Lambda_{t}}v=p^{m}v=0
for every
v\in R^{n}
.
Consequently, due to the induction hypothesis, both properties are satisfied if we let
v_{t}\in R^{n}
be an arbitrary vector which is not in
\operatorname{span}_{R}(v_{1},\ldots,v_{t-1})+pR^{n}
.
Such a vector exists because
t-1<n
.

Now suppose that
U_{t}<m
.
By definition of
U_{t}
there then exist
w\in p^{U_{t}}R^{n}
and
r\in\operatorname{span}_{R}(\mathbf{X}^{i-1}b\bmod p^{m}:i\leq t-1)
such that
\mathbf{X}^{t-1}b\equiv w+r\bmod p^{m}
.
Pick some
v_{t}\in R^{n}
with
p^{U_{t}}v_{t}=w
.
Then, due to the induction hypothesis,
item

(ii)
is satisfied and
item

(i)
is equivalent to the statement that
v_{t}\not\in\operatorname{span}_{R}(v_{1},\ldots,v_{t-1})+pR^{n}
.
The latter statement is true.
Indeed, if not, then
w\in\operatorname{span}_{R}(p^{U_{t}}v_{1},\ldots,p^{U_{t}}v_{t-1})\allowbreak+p^{U_{t}+1}R^{n}
.
Then, considering that
\operatorname{span}_{R}(p^{U_{t}}v_{i}:i\leq t-1)\subseteq\operatorname{span}_{R}(\mathbf{X}^{i-1}b\bmod p^{m}:i\leq t-1)
by
item

(ii)
of the induction hypothesis and the fact that the
U_{i}
are nondecreasing, it follows from
\mathbf{X}^{t-1}b\equiv w+r\bmod p^{m}
that
\mathbf{X}^{t-1}b\in\operatorname{span}_{\mathbb{Z}}(\mathbf{X}^{i-1}b:i\leq t-1)+p^{U_{t}+1}\mathbb{Z}^{n}
contradicting the maximality of
U_{t}
in (
1.1
).
This shows that both properties are satisfied.
∎

Corollary 2.2
.

Adopt the notation of
Lemma

2.1
.
Then,
\operatorname{coker}(\mathbf{W})_{p^{m}}\cong\oplus_{t=1}^{n}\mathbb{Z}/p^{\Lambda_{t}}\mathbb{Z}
.

Proof.

The case with
t=n
in
item

(i)
of
Lemma

2.1
implies that the vectors
v_{1},\ldots,v_{n}\in R^{n}
determine an
R
-module basis for
R^{n}
.
Further,
item

(ii)
yields that
\mathbf{W}(R^{n})=\operatorname{span}_{R}(p^{\Lambda_{1}}v_{1},\allowbreak\ldots,p^{\Lambda_{n}}v_{n})

The claim is hence immediate since
\operatorname{coker}(\mathbf{W})_{p^{m}}\cong R^{n}/\mathbf{W}(R^{n})

∎

2.2
Markovian dynamics

Given a matrix
\mathbf{M}
, abbreviate
\operatorname{rank}_{p}(\mathbf{M})
for the rank of
\mathbf{M}\bmod p
over
\mathbb{F}_{p}
.
Recall that
R=\mathbb{Z}/p^{m}\mathbb{Z}
.
The following lemma provides a converse for
Lemma

2.1
:

Lemma 2.3
.

Consider some
t\leq n
and integers
0=u_{1}\leq u_{2}\leq\ldots\leq u_{t}<m
.
Then, it holds that
U_{i}=u_{i}
for every
i\leq t
if and only if there exist
v_{1},\ldots,v_{t}\in R^{n}
with
\operatorname{rank}_{p}(v_{1},\ldots,v_{t})=t
such that for every
i\leq t
one has

\operatorname{span}_{R}(\mathbf{X}^{j-1}b\bmod p^{m}:j\leq i)=\operatorname{span}_{R}(p^{u_{j}}v_{j}:j\leq i)
.

Proof.

If
U_{i}=u_{i}
for every
i\leq t
then the existence of
v_{1},\ldots,v_{t}
with the claimed properties follows from
Lemma

2.1
.
Conversely, assume that such
v_{1},\ldots,v_{t}
exist.
Then, for every
i\leq t
,

\displaystyle\operatorname{span}_{R}(\mathbf{X}^{j-1}b\bmod p^{m}:j\leq i)

\displaystyle=\operatorname{span}_{R}(p^{u_{j}}v_{j}:j<i)+\operatorname{span}_{R}(p^{u_{i}}v_{i})

(2.1)

\displaystyle\subseteq\operatorname{span}_{R}(\mathbf{X}^{j-1}b\bmod p^{m}:j<i)+p^{u_{i}}R^{n}.

Considering that
R=\mathbb{Z}/p^{m}\mathbb{Z}
with
m\geq u_{i}
, it follows that
\mathbf{X}^{i-1}b\in\operatorname{span}_{\mathbb{Z}}(\mathbf{X}^{j-1}b:j<i)+p^{u_{i}}\mathbb{Z}^{n}
.
The definition (
1.1
) hence yields
U_{i}\geq u_{i}
.
On the other hand, since
\operatorname{rank}_{p}(v_{1},\ldots,v_{t})=t
and
u_{i}<m
, we have
p^{u_{i}}v_{i}\not\in\operatorname{span}_{R}(p^{u_{j}}v_{j}:j<i)+p^{u_{i}+1}R^{n}
.
Hence,

\displaystyle\operatorname{span}_{R}(p^{u_{j}}v_{j}:j<i)+\operatorname{span}_{R}(p^{u_{i}}v_{i})

\displaystyle\not\subseteq\operatorname{span}_{R}(p^{u_{j}}v_{j}:j<i)+p^{u_{i}+1}R^{n}

(2.2)

\displaystyle=\operatorname{span}_{R}(\mathbf{X}^{j-1}b\bmod p^{m}:j<i)+p^{u_{i}+1}R^{n}.

Given the equality in (
2.1
) and the fact that
u_{i}+1\leq m
, this implies that
\mathbf{X}^{i-1}b\not\in\operatorname{span}_{\mathbb{Z}}(\mathbf{X}^{j-1}b:j<i)+p^{u_{i}+1}\mathbb{Z}^{n}
.
This means that
U_{i}\leq u_{i}
.
Combine the inequalities
U_{i}\geq u_{i}
and
U_{i}\leq u_{i}
to conclude the proof.
∎

Lemma 2.4
.

Assume that
\mathbf{X}
has independent and
\operatorname{Unif}\{0,1,\ldots,p^{m}-1\}
-distributed entries.
Consider some
t\leq n-1
.
Then, for every
0=u_{1}\leq\ldots\leq u_{t}<m
and
u_{t+1}\leq m
,

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\bigl(U_{t+1}\geq u_{t+1}\mid U_{i}=u_{i},\,\forall i\in\{1,\ldots,t\}\bigr)=p^{-(n-t)(u_{t+1}-u_{t})}.

Proof.

Two ordered sets of vectors
v_{1},\ldots,v_{t}\in R^{n}
and
w_{1},\ldots,w_{t}\in R^{n}
are said to be
equivalent
if
\operatorname{span}_{R}(p^{u_{j}}v_{j}:j\leq i)=\operatorname{span}_{R}(p^{u_{j}}w_{j}:j\leq i)
for every
i\leq t
.

Lemma

2.3
implies that the event
\{U_{i}=u_{i}:\forall i\leq t\}
can be written as a union of events of the form
\{\operatorname{span}_{R}(\mathbf{X}^{j-1}b\bmod p^{m}:j\leq i)=\operatorname{span}_{R}(p^{u_{j}}v_{j}:j\leq i),\,\forall i\leq t\}
indexed by vectors
v_{1},\ldots,v_{t}\in R^{n}
with
\operatorname{rank}_{p}(v_{1},\ldots,v_{t})=t
and
\operatorname{span}_{R}(v_{1})=\operatorname{span}_{R}(b\bmod p^{m})
.
Two such events are equal if the corresponding sets of vectors are equivalent and mutually exclusive otherwise.
Hence, by conditioning on the equivalence class of
v_{1},\ldots,v_{t}
, it suffices to show that for every
v_{1},\ldots,v_{t}\in R^{n}
with
\operatorname{rank}_{p}(v_{1},\ldots,v_{t})=t
and
\operatorname{span}_{R}(v_{1})=\operatorname{span}_{R}(b\bmod p^{m})
,

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\bigl(U_{t+1}\geq u_{t+1}\mid\operatorname{span}_{R}(\mathbf{X}^{j-1}b\bmod p^{m}:j\leq i)=\operatorname{span}_{R}({}

\displaystyle{}p^{u_{j}}v_{j}:j\leq i),\,\forall i\leq t\bigr)

(2.3)

\displaystyle\ =p^{-(n-t)(u_{t+1}-u_{t})}.

Fix
v_{1},\ldots,v_{t}
and let
E\vcentcolon=\{\operatorname{span}_{R}(\mathbf{X}^{j-1}b\bmod p^{m}:j\leq i)=\operatorname{span}_{R}(p^{u_{j}}v_{j}:j\leq i),\,\forall i\leq t\}
denote the event in the condition of (
2.3
).

It follows from the definition that
U_{t+1}\geq u_{t+1}
if and only if
\mathbf{X}(\operatorname{span}_{\mathbb{Z}}(\mathbf{X}^{i-1}b:i\leq t))\subseteq\operatorname{span}_{\mathbb{Z}}(\mathbf{X}^{i-1}b:i\leq t)+p^{u_{t+1}}\mathbb{Z}^{n}
.
Hence, conditional on
E
, one has
U_{t+1}\geq u_{t+1}
if and only if
\mathbf{X}(p^{u_{t}}v_{t})\in\operatorname{span}_{R}(p^{u_{j}}v_{j}:j\leq t)+p^{u_{t+1}}R^{n}
.
Considering that
\operatorname{rank}_{p}(v_{1},\ldots,v_{t})=t
and that the
u_{i}
are nondecreasing, the latter occurs if and only if
\mathbf{X}(v_{t})\in\operatorname{span}_{R}(v_{1},\ldots,v_{t})+p^{u_{t+1}-u_{t}}R^{n}
.
Hence,

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\bigl(U_{t+1}\geq u_{t+1}\mid E\bigr)=\mathbb{P}\bigl(\mathbf{X}(v_{t})\in\operatorname{span}_{R}(v_{1},\ldots,v_{t})+p^{u_{t+1}-u_{t}}R^{n}\mid E\bigr).

(2.4)

The assumption
\operatorname{span}_{R}(v_{1})=\operatorname{span}_{R}(b\bmod p^{m})
implies that the event
\operatorname{span}_{R}(\mathbf{X}^{j-1}b\bmod p^{m}:j\leq 2)=\operatorname{span}_{R}(p^{u_{j}}v_{1}:j\leq 2)
only depends on
\mathbf{X}(v_{1})
.
Similarly, continuing in an inductive fashion, the event
E
only depends on
\mathbf{X}(v_{1}),\ldots,\mathbf{X}(v_{t-1})
.
Hence, by the law of total probability,

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\bigl(\mathbf{X}(

\displaystyle v_{t})\in\operatorname{span}_{R}(v_{1},\ldots,v_{t})+p^{u_{t+1}-u_{t}}R^{n}\mid E\bigr)

(2.5)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\Bigl[\mathbb{P}\bigl(\mathbf{X}(v_{t})\in\operatorname{span}_{R}(v_{1},\ldots,v_{t})+p^{u_{t+1}-u_{t}}R^{n}\mid\mathbf{X}(v_{1}),\ldots,\mathbf{X}(v_{t-1})\bigr)\ \big|\ E\ \Bigr].

Recall that the entries of
\mathbf{X}
are independent and
\operatorname{Unif}\{0,1,\ldots,p^{m}-1\}
-distributed.
This implies that
\mathbf{X}
induces a uniform random endomorphism of
R^{n}
.
Hence, since it was assumed that
\operatorname{rank}_{p}(v_{1},\ldots,v_{t})=t
, it holds that
\mathbf{X}(v_{t})
has a uniform distribution on
R^{n}
and is independent of
\mathbf{X}(v_{1}),\ldots,\mathbf{X}(v_{t-1})
.
Consequently, a counting argument yields that

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\bigl(\mathbf{X}(v_{t})\in\operatorname{span}_{R}(v_{1},\ldots,v_{t})+p^{u_{t+1}-u_{t}}R^{n}\mid\mathbf{X}(v_{1}),\ldots,\mathbf{X}(v_{t-1})\bigr)=p^{-(n-t)(u_{t+1}-u_{t})}.

(2.6)

Combine (
2.4
)–(
2.6
) to establish (
2.3
).
This concludes the proof.
∎

2.3
The law of
\operatorname{coker}(\mathbf{W})_{p^{m}}

It now only remains to combine
Corollary

2.2
and
Lemma

2.4
.
This allows us to also determine the law of
\operatorname{coker}(\mathbf{W})_{p^{m}}
when
n
is finite:

Proposition 2.5
.

Fix some
n\geq 1
, let
\mathbf{X}
be a
\mathbb{Z}^{n\times n}
-valued random matrix with independent
\operatorname{Unif}\{0,1,\ldots,\allowbreak p^{m}-1\}
-distributed entries and let
b\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}
be a deterministic vector with
b\not\equiv 0\bmod p
.
Fix an integer
0\leq i_{0}\leq n-1
.

Pick integers
0=\lambda_{1}\leq\lambda_{2}\leq\ldots\leq\lambda_{n}\leq m
and denote
\delta_{i}=\lambda_{n-i+1}-\lambda_{n-i}
.
Assume that
\lambda_{i}<m
if and only if
i\leq n-i_{0}
.
Then, as Abelian groups,

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\Bigl(\operatorname{coker}(\mathbf{W})_{p^{m}}\cong\bigoplus_{i=1}^{n}\frac{\mathbb{Z}}{p^{\lambda_{i}}\mathbb{Z}}\Bigl)=\prod_{i=i_{0}}^{n-2}\Bigl(1-p^{-(i+1)}\Bigr)\prod_{j=1}^{n-1}p^{-j\delta_{j}}.

Proof.

By
Corollary

2.2
and the assumption that
\lambda_{i}<m
for every
i\leq n-i_{0}
and
\lambda_{i}=m
for every
i>n-i_{0}
,

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\bigl(\operatorname{coker}(\mathbf{W})_{p^{m}}\cong{}

\displaystyle{}\oplus_{i=1}^{n}\mathbb{Z}/p^{\lambda_{i}}\mathbb{Z}\bigl)=\mathbb{P}\bigl(U_{i}=\lambda_{i},\,\forall i\in\{1,\ldots,n-i_{0}\}\bigr)

(2.7)

\displaystyle\quad\times\mathbb{P}\bigl(U_{i}\geq m,\,\forall i\in\{n-i_{0}+1,\ldots,n\}\mid U_{i}=\lambda_{i},\,\forall i\in\{1,\ldots,n-i_{0}\}\bigr).

Recall that
\delta_{i}=\lambda_{n-i+1}-\lambda_{n-i}
.
Hence, using that
U_{1}=0
together with
Lemma

2.4
and the assumption that
\lambda_{i}<m
for every
i\leq n-i_{0}
,

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\bigl(U_{i}=\lambda_{i},\,\forall i\in\{1,\ldots,n-i_{0}\}\bigr)

\displaystyle=\prod_{i=i_{0}}^{n-2}\mathbb{P}(U_{n-i}=U_{n-i-1}+\delta_{i+1}\mid U_{j}=\lambda_{j},\,\forall j<n-i)

(2.8)

\displaystyle=\prod_{i=i_{0}}^{n-2}(1-p^{-(i+1)})p^{-(i+1)\delta_{i+1}}.

If
i_{0}=0
, then the second probability in (
2.7
) is equal to one since there is no
i
satisfying
n+1\leq i\leq n
.
In this case, the combination of (
2.7
) and (
2.8
) concludes the proof.

Now suppose that
i_{0}>0
.
Then, it holds that
U_{i}\geq m
for all
i>n-i_{0}
if and only if
U_{n-i_{0}+1}\geq m
.
Hence, by
Lemma

2.4
and the fact that
m=\lambda_{n-i_{0}}+\delta_{i_{0}}
,

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\bigl(U_{i}\geq m,\,\forall i\in\{n-i_{0}+1,\ldots,n\}{}

\displaystyle{}\mid U_{i}=\lambda_{i},\,\forall i\in\{1,\ldots,n-i_{0}\}\bigr)

(2.9)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{P}(U_{n-i_{0}+1}\geq m\mid U_{i}=\lambda_{i},\,\forall i\in\{1,\ldots,n-i_{0}\}\bigr)

\displaystyle=p^{-i_{0}\delta_{i_{0}}}.

Remark
p^{-i_{0}\delta_{i_{0}}}=\prod_{i=1}^{i_{0}}p^{-i\delta_{i}}
since the assumption that
\lambda_{i}=m=\lambda_{i+1}
for all
i>n-i_{0}
ensures that
\delta_{i}=0
for every
i<i_{0}
.
The combination of (
2.7
)–(
2.9
) hence concludes the proof.
∎

Proof of

We establish a more general result than

1.3
and study the limiting law of the
\mathbb{Z}[x]
-module
\operatorname{coker}(\tilde{\mathbf{W}})
where
\tilde{\mathbf{W}}\vcentcolon=(\mathbf{X}^{j-1}\mathbf{B})_{j=1}^{n}
is the

matrix associated to a deterministic

\mathbf{B}
for some fixed

For future reference, let us here state all relevant assumptions:

(A1)

For every

let
\mathbf{X}
be a
\mathbb{Z}^{n\times n}
-valued random matrix with independent entries such that each entry is
\alpha_{n}
-balanced mod
\mathscr{P}
.

(A2)

Fix some

.
For every

\mathbf{B}\in\mathbb{Z}^{n\times k}

\mathbf{B}\bmod p

We denote

and write
\operatorname{coker}(\tilde{\mathbf{W}})\vcentcolon=\mathbb{Z}^{n}/\tilde{\mathbf{W}}(\mathbb{Z}^{nk})
.

(A3)

Assume that
\lim_{n\to\infty}n\alpha_{n}/\ln(n)=\infty
.

The desired result, describing the limiting distribution of
\operatorname{coker}(\tilde{\mathbf{W}})_{p^{m},Q}
under these assumptions, is given in

3.13
.

As was outlined in
Section

1.2
, we rely on the category-theoretic moment method.
The main ingredient required for the proof is correspondingly an estimate on the moments of
\operatorname{coker}(\tilde{\mathbf{W}})
:

Proposition 3.1
.

Adopt assumptions
(A1)
to
(A3)
.
Then, for every finite
\mathbb{Z}[x]
-module
N
such that all prime divisors of
\#N
are in
\mathscr{P}
,

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\mathbb{E}[\#\operatorname{Sur}_{\mathbb{Z}[x]}(\operatorname{coker}(\tilde{\mathbf{W}}),N)]=(\#N)^{-k}.

(3.1)

We prove
Proposition

3.1
in
Section

3.1
and then use a general-purpose result of Sawin and Wood
[
20
, Lemma 6.3]
to solve the associated moment problem in
Section

3.2
.

Remark 3.2
.

The assumption in
Proposition

3.1
that all prime divisors of
\#N
are in
\mathscr{P}
can not be removed.
Indeed, recall that
(A1)
and
(A2)
only make assumptions regarding
\operatorname{rank}_{p}(\mathbf{B})
and the balanced nature of the entries of
\mathbf{X}
at primes

.

So, for instance, at

it could occur that
\mathbb{P}(\mathbf{X}\equiv 0\bmod p)=1
and
\mathbf{B}=0\bmod p
in which case
\mathbb{E}[\#\operatorname{Sur}_{\mathbb{Z}[x]}(\operatorname{coker}(\tilde{\mathbf{W}}),N)]=p^{n}-1
with

.
In particular, it then holds that
\lim_{n\to\infty}\mathbb{E}[\#\operatorname{Sur}_{\mathbb{Z}[x]}(\operatorname{coker}(\tilde{\mathbf{W}}),N)]=\infty
which is incompatible with the conclusion of
Proposition

3.1
.

Remark 3.3
.

There is a sense in which
\operatorname{coker}(\tilde{\mathbf{W}})
is a fairly natural random algebraic object to study.
Note that
\mathbf{W}(\mathbb{Z}^{nk})
is exactly the
\mathbb{Z}[x]
-submodule of
\mathbb{Z}^{n}
generated by the columns of
\mathbf{B}
.
Hence, introducing formal symbols
e_{1},\ldots,e_{n}
, we have

\displaystyle\operatorname{coker}(\tilde{\mathbf{W}})\cong\Bigl(e_{1},\ldots,e_{n}:xe_{j}=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mathbf{X}_{i,j}e_{i},\,\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mathbf{B}_{i,r}e_{i}=0,\,\forall i\leq n,\,\forall r\leq k\Bigr)

(3.2)

as
\mathbb{Z}[x]
-modules.
So,
\operatorname{coker}(\tilde{\mathbf{W}})
corresponds to the finitely presented
\mathbb{Z}[x]
-module which is found when one considers
n

specified by
\mathbf{X}
, and imposes
k\geq 1
additional deterministic constraints specified by
\mathbf{B}
.

3.1
Computing the limiting
N
-moments

Let
N
be a deterministic
\mathbb{Z}[x]
-module such that all prime divisors of
\#N
are in
\mathscr{P}
.
We may consider
\mathbf{X}
as a random element of
\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbb{Z}}(\mathbb{Z}^{n},\mathbb{Z}^{n})
, consider
\mathbf{B}
as a deterministic element of
\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbb{Z}}(\mathbb{Z}^{k},\mathbb{Z}^{n})
, and consider
x
as inducing an element of
\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbb{Z}}(N,N)
.
Here, note that
\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbb{Z}}(\cdot,\cdot)
simply returns the set of group morphisms since a
\mathbb{Z}
-module and an Abelian group are the same thing.

Now observe that a morphism of Abelian groups
F:\mathbb{Z}^{n}\to N
descends to a morphism of
\mathbb{Z}[x]
-modules
\overline{F}:\operatorname{coker}(\tilde{\mathbf{W}})\to N
if and only if the compositions of
F
with
\mathbf{B},\mathbf{X}
, and
x
satisfy
F\mathbf{B}=0
and
F\mathbf{X}=xF
.
Moreover, every
\mathbb{Z}[x]
-module morphism
\overline{F}:\operatorname{coker}(\tilde{\mathbf{W}})\to N
arises from some unique
F:\mathbb{Z}^{n}\to N
in this fashion.
Consequently, since surjectivity is conserved,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[\#\operatorname{Sur}_{\mathbb{Z}[x]}(\operatorname{coker}(\tilde{\mathbf{W}}),N)]

\displaystyle=\sum_{F\in\operatorname{Sur}_{\mathbb{Z}}(\mathbb{Z}^{n},N):F\mathbf{B}=0}\mathbb{P}(F\mathbf{X}=xF).

(3.3)

Let us here emphasize that, while (
3.3
) was relatively easy to prove, the simplification which this step offers is significant.
Indeed, observe that the joint law of the entries of
\tilde{\mathbf{W}}
is not easy to understand since these entries are nontrivial algebraic combinations of the entries of
\mathbf{X}
and
\mathbf{B}
.
On the other hand,
F\mathbf{X}=xF
is a linear equation in terms of
\mathbf{X}
and hence fairly explicit.

The strategy which we use to estimate the
N
-moments from here on is as follows.
We show that there are approximately
(\#N)^{n-k}
surjections
F:\mathbb{Z}^{n}\to N
with
F\mathbf{B}=0
in
Section

3.1.1
.
Subsequently, we show that
\mathbb{P}(F\mathbf{X}=xF)\approx(\#N)^{-n}
for most terms in (
3.3
) in
Section

3.1.2
, and we show that the remaining terms give a negligible contribution in
Section

3.1.3
.
Finally, we combine these ingredients to conclude the proof of
Proposition

3.1
in
Section

3.1.4
.

3.1.1
Estimate on the number of surjections satisfying
F\mathbf{B}=0

The
exponent
of a finite Abelian group
G
is the smallest positive integer
\exp(G)\geq 1
such that
\exp(G)G=0
.
Note that
p\mid\exp(G)
if and only if
p\mid\#G
.

Lemma 3.4
.

Let
G
be a finite Abelian group and let
\mathbf{B}\in\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbb{Z}}(\mathbb{Z}^{k},\mathbb{Z}^{n})
be such that
\operatorname{rank}_{p}(\mathbf{B})=k
for every prime divisor
p
of
\exp(G)
.
Then, there exists a constant
C>0
depending only on
G
such that for all
n\geq k

\displaystyle\lvert\#\{F\in\operatorname{Sur}_{\mathbb{Z}}(\mathbb{Z}^{n},G):F\mathbf{B}=0\}-(\#G)^{n-k}\rvert\leq C\Bigl(\frac{\#G}{2}\Bigr)^{n}.

Proof.

We first argue that we can replace
\operatorname{Sur}_{\mathbb{Z}}(\mathbb{Z}^{n},G)
by
\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbb{Z}}(\mathbb{Z}^{n},G)
up to a negligible error.
If a morphism
F:\mathbb{Z}^{n}\to G
is not surjective then there exists some proper subgroup
H\subsetneq G
such that
F(\mathbb{Z}^{n})=H
.
Hence, since
\#\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbb{Z}}(\mathbb{Z}^{n},H)=(\#H)^{n}
,

\displaystyle\#(\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbb{Z}}(\mathbb{Z}^{n},G)\setminus\operatorname{Sur}_{\mathbb{Z}}(\mathbb{Z}^{n},G))

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{H\subsetneq G}\#\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbb{Z}}(\mathbb{Z}^{n},H)=\sum_{H\subsetneq G}(\#H)^{n}.

(3.4)

Denote
S_{G}
for the number of proper subgroups of
G
.
Then, since
\#H\leq\#G/2
by
H
being a proper subgroup,

\displaystyle\lvert\#\{F\in\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbb{Z}}(\mathbb{Z}^{n},G)

\displaystyle:F\mathbf{B}=0\}-\#\{F\in\operatorname{Sur}_{\mathbb{Z}}(\mathbb{Z}^{n},G):F\mathbf{B}=0\}\rvert

(3.5)

\displaystyle\leq\#(\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbb{Z}}(\mathbb{Z}^{n},G)\setminus\operatorname{Sur}_{\mathbb{Z}}(\mathbb{Z}^{n},G))\leq S_{G}\Bigl(\frac{\#G}{2}\Bigr)^{n}.

We next argue that
\#\{F\in\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbb{Z}}(\mathbb{Z}^{n},G):F\mathbf{B}=0\}=(\#G)^{n-k}
.
(This is not immediate because the columns
b_{1},\ldots,b_{k}
of
\mathbf{B}
are not necessarily part of a
\mathbb{Z}
-module basis for
\mathbb{Z}^{n}
.)

Let
\mathbf{B}=\mathbf{U}\mathbf{D}\mathbf{V}
be the Smith normal form of
\mathbf{B}
.
This means that
\mathbf{U}\in\mathbb{Z}^{n\times n}
and
\mathbf{V}\in\mathbb{Z}^{k\times k}
are matrices with
\det(\mathbf{U}),\det(\mathbf{V})\in\{-1,1\}
and
\mathbf{D}
is an
n\times k
diagonal matrix with integer diagonal entries satisfying
d_{1}\mid d_{2}\mid\ldots\mid d_{k}
.
For brevity, denote
a\vcentcolon=\exp(G)
.
For every
p\mid a
, the assumption that
\operatorname{rank}_{p}(\mathbf{B})=k
implies that
d_{i}\not\equiv 0\bmod p
.
It follows that the
d_{i}
are multiplicative units for
\mathbb{Z}/a\mathbb{Z}
.
Hence, the matrix
\mathbf{D}^{\prime}\vcentcolon=\operatorname{diag}(d_{1},\ldots,d_{k})
is invertible in
(\mathbb{Z}/a\mathbb{Z})^{k\times k}
.
Further, note that
\mathbf{U}
and
\mathbf{V}
are invertible over
\mathbb{Z}
.
Hence, if
u_{1},\ldots,u_{n}
are the columns of
\mathbf{U}
then the reductions to
(\mathbb{Z}/a\mathbb{Z})^{n}
determine a
(\mathbb{Z}/a\mathbb{Z})
-module basis, and the reduction of
\mathbf{D}^{\prime}\mathbf{V}
to
(\mathbb{Z}/a\mathbb{Z})^{k\times k}
is invertible.
Consequently, since
\mathbf{B}=(u_{1},\ldots,u_{k})\mathbf{D}^{\prime}\mathbf{V}
, the reductions of
b_{1},\ldots,b_{k}
together with the reductions of the
u_{i}
with
i\geq k+1
determine a
(\mathbb{Z}/a\mathbb{Z})
-module basis for
(\mathbb{Z}/a\mathbb{Z})^{n}
.

Denote
\pi:\mathbb{Z}^{n}\to(\mathbb{Z}/a\mathbb{Z})^{n}
for the reduction map.
Then, since
a=\exp(G)
, it holds for every
F\in\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbb{Z}}(\mathbb{Z}^{n},G)
that there is some unique
\overline{F}\in\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbb{Z}/a\mathbb{Z}}((\mathbb{Z}/a\mathbb{Z})^{n},G)
with
F=\overline{F}\circ\pi
.
Recall that for any ring
R
an
R
-module morphism from a free
R
-module to an arbitrary
R
-module may be specified uniquely by arbitrarily specifying the images of the basis elements.
Consequently, since the
\pi(b_{i})
are part of a
(\mathbb{Z}/a\mathbb{Z})
-module basis,

\displaystyle\#\{F\in\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbb{Z}}(\mathbb{Z}^{n},G):F\mathbf{B}=0\}

\displaystyle=\#\{\overline{F}\in\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbb{Z}/a\mathbb{Z}}((\mathbb{Z}/a\mathbb{Z})^{n},G):\overline{F}\circ\pi\circ\mathbf{B}=0\}

\displaystyle=(\#G)^{n-k}.

(3.6)

Combine (
3.5
) with (
3.6
) and set
C\vcentcolon=S_{G}
to conclude the proof.
∎

We next estimate
\mathbb{P}(F\mathbf{X}=xF)
.
The quality of the estimates will be better when
F
is “very surjective”.
To make this precise, we rely on a notion of
codes
which is due to Wood
[
32
]
and a notion of
robust morphisms
which is due to Nguyen and Wood
[
15
]
.

3.1.2
Estimate for codes

Let
e_{1},\ldots,e_{n}\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}
be the standard basis vectors.
For any
\sigma\subseteq\{1,\ldots,n\}
write
V_{\sigma}\vcentcolon=\operatorname{span}_{\mathbb{Z}}(e_{i}:i\in\sigma)
for the
\mathbb{Z}
-submodule of
\mathbb{Z}^{n}
consisting of vectors whose nonzero coordinates are in
\sigma
.
We abbreviate
V_{\setminus\sigma}\vcentcolon=V_{\{1,\ldots,n\}\setminus\sigma}
.

Definition 3.5
.

Let
G
be an Abelian group.
Then,
F\in\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbb{Z}}(\mathbb{Z}^{n},G)
is called a
code of distance
w\geq 1
if for every
\sigma\subseteq\{1,\ldots,n\}
with
\#\sigma<w
one has
F(V_{\setminus\sigma})=G
.

The foregoing definition may also be applied to
\mathbb{Z}[x]
-modules since these can be viewed as Abelian groups through the
\mathbb{Z}
-module structure.

Lemma 3.6
.

Adopt assumptions
(A1)
and
(A3)
and fix a
\mathbb{Z}[x]
-module
N
such that all prime divisors of
\#N
are in
\mathscr{P}
.
Then, for every
\delta>0
there exist constants
C,c>0
such that for all
n\geq 1

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}F\in\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbb{Z}}(\mathbb{Z}^{n},N)\\
F\text{ a code of distance }\delta n\end{subarray}}\bigl\lvert\mathbb{P}(F\mathbf{X}=xF)-(\#N)^{-n}\bigr\rvert\leq Cn^{-c}.

(3.7)

Proof.

For any code
F
and any
A\in\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbb{Z}}(\mathbb{Z}^{n},N)
it is shown in
[
15
, Lemma 4.7]
that
\lvert\mathbb{P}(F\mathbf{X}=A)-(\#N)^{-n}\rvert\leq C(\#N)^{-n}n^{-c}
.
Actually, strictly speaking,
[
15
, Lemma 4.7]
is stated for matrices which are
\alpha_{n}
-balanced at all primes, but only balancedness at primes dividing
\#N
is necessary for its proof.
(Indeed,
[
15
, Lemma 4.7]
follows from
[
15
, Lemma 4.5]
whose proof may be found in
[
33
, Lemma 2.1]
and only requires the weaker condition; see
[
33
, Definition 1]
.)
The result now follows since there are at most
\#\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbb{Z}}(\mathbb{Z}^{n},N)=(\#N)^{n}
summands in (
3.7
).
∎

3.1.3
Estimate for non-codes

The contribution of terms in (
3.3
) corresponding to non-codes turns out to be negligible.
It is however delicate to make this rigorous.
The estimate which can be achieved on
\mathbb{P}(F\mathbf{X}=xF)
for a generic non-code
F
is namely insufficient to beat the combinatorial factor corresponding to the number of non-codes.
Hence, a subdivision of the non-codes is required to balance the quality of the estimates against the combinatorial costs.

For an integer
d
with prime factorization
d=\prod_{i}p_{i}^{e_{i}}
denote
\ell(d)\vcentcolon=\sum_{i}e_{i}
.
Given a subgroup
H\subseteq G
, let
[G:H]\vcentcolon=\#G/\#H
denote the index of
H
in
G
.

Definition 3.7
.

Let
G
be a finite Abelian group and let
\delta>0
be a scalar.
Then,
F\in\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbb{Z}}(\mathbb{Z}^{n},G)
is called
\delta
-robust for a subgroup
H\subseteq G
if
H
is minimal with the property that

\displaystyle\#\bigl\{i\in\{1,\ldots,n\}:F(e_{i})\notin H\bigr\}\leq\ell([G:H])\delta n

(3.8)

That is,
H
satisfies (
3.8
) and no strict subgroup
H^{\prime}\subsetneq H
satisfies (
3.8
).

The main motivation for
Definition

3.7
is the following property: if
F
is
\delta
-robust for
H
then the restriction of
F
to
V_{\sigma}
with
\sigma\vcentcolon=\{i:F(e_{i})\in H\}
is a code of distance
\delta n
when
H
is viewed as the codomain of this restriction.
Indeed, suppose this were not the case.
Then, there exists
\mu\subseteq\sigma
with
\#\mu<\delta n
such that
H^{\prime}\vcentcolon=F(V_{\sigma\setminus\mu})
is a strict subgroup of
H
.
So, since
[G:H^{\prime}]\geq[G:H]+1
,

\displaystyle\#\{i\in\{1,\ldots,n\}:F(e_{i})\not\in H^{\prime}\}

\displaystyle\leq\#\{i\in\{1,\ldots,n\}:F(e_{i})\not\in H\}+\#\mu

(3.9)

\displaystyle\leq\ell([G:H^{\prime}])\delta n

(3.10)

contradicting the minimality of
H
.

In particular, any
F\in\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbb{Z}}(\mathbb{Z}^{n},G)
which is not a code of distance
\delta n
is not
\delta
-robust for
G
.
However, (
3.8
) is always satisfied when
G=H
.
This implies that any non-code has to be
\delta
-robust for some, not necessarily unique, proper subgroup of
G
.
Hence,

\displaystyle\{F\in\operatorname{Sur}_{\mathbb{Z}}(\mathbb{Z}^{n},G):F

\displaystyle\text{ not a code of distance }\delta n\}

(3.11)

\displaystyle\subseteq\bigcup_{H\subsetneq G}\{F\in\operatorname{Sur}_{\mathbb{Z}}(\mathbb{Z}^{n},G):F\text{ is }\delta\text{-robust for }H\}.

We next establish an estimate on
\mathbb{P}(F\mathbf{X}=xF)
when
F
is
\delta
-robust for some
H
.
The following lemma generalizes
[
15
, Lemma 4.11]
which concerns a similar bound for
\mathbb{P}(F(Y)=0)
.

Lemma 3.8
.

Fix scalars
\delta,\alpha>0
, an integer
n\geq 1
, and a finite Abelian group
G
.
Fix a proper subgroup
H\subsetneq G
, denote
d\vcentcolon=[G:H]
, and consider a maximal chain of proper subgroups

\displaystyle H=G_{\ell(d)}\subsetneq\cdots\subsetneq G_{2}\subsetneq G_{1}\subsetneq G_{0}=G.

(3.12)

Consider a
\delta
-robust morphism
F\in\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbb{Z}}(\mathbb{Z}^{n},G)
for
H
.
For every
1\leq j\leq\ell(d)
denote
p_{j}\vcentcolon=[G_{j-1}:G_{j}]
and

\displaystyle w_{j}\vcentcolon=\#\bigl\{i\in\{1,\ldots,n\}:F(e_{i})\in G_{j-1}\setminus G_{j}\bigr\}.

(3.13)

Abbreviate
a\vcentcolon=\exp(G)
for the exponent of
G
.
Then, for every
g\in G
and every
\mathbb{Z}^{n}
-valued random vector
Y
whose entries are independent and
\alpha
-balanced modulo all prime divisors of
a
,

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\bigl(F(Y)=g\bigr)\leq

\displaystyle\Bigl((\#G)^{-1}d+\exp(-\alpha\delta n/a^{2})\Bigr)\prod_{j=1}^{\ell(d)}\Bigl(p_{j}^{-1}+\frac{p_{j}-1}{p_{j}}\exp(-\alpha w_{j}/p_{j}^{2})\Bigr).

(3.14)

Proof.

The strategy in this proof is to reduce to the case of codes where estimates are available from
[
15
, Lemma 4.5]
.
Again, similar to the remarks in the proof of
Lemma

3.6
, that lemma is stated for the case where
Y
is balanced at all primes, but the case where
Y
is merely balanced at prime divisors of
a
follows from its proof.

For every
j\in\{1,2,\ldots,\ell(d)\}
define a set of indices by

\displaystyle\sigma_{j}\vcentcolon=\bigl\{i\in\{1,\ldots,n\}:F(e_{i})\in G_{j-1}\setminus G_{j}\bigr\}.

(3.15)

Then, for every
r\leq\ell(d)
the set of indices
i
with
F(e_{i})\notin G_{r}
is given by
\Sigma_{r}\vcentcolon=\cup_{j=1}^{r}\sigma_{j}
.
Write
Y=(y_{1},\ldots,y_{n})
and observe that
\sum_{i\notin\Sigma_{r}}y_{i}F(e_{i})\in G_{r}
for any
r\leq\ell(d)
.
Consequently, since
F(Y)=\sum_{i=1}^{n}y_{i}F(e_{i})
, it is only possible to have
F(Y)=g
if
\sum_{i\in\Sigma_{r}}y_{i}F(e_{i})-g\in G_{r}
for all
r\leq\ell(d)
.
Hence, by definition of conditional probability,

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\Bigl(F(Y)=g\Bigr)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{P}\Bigl(\sum_{i\in\Sigma_{1}}y_{i}F(y_{i})-g\in G_{1}\Bigr)\mathbb{P}\Bigl(F(Y)=g\,\Big|\,\sum_{i\in\Sigma_{1}}y_{i}F(y_{i})-g\in G_{1}\Bigr)

\displaystyle=\prod_{j=1}^{\ell(d)}\mathbb{P}\Bigl(\sum_{i\in\Sigma_{j}}y_{i}F(y_{i})-g\in G_{j}\,\Big|\,\forall r<j:\sum_{i\in\Sigma_{r}}y_{i}F(y_{i})-g\in G_{r}\Bigr)

(3.16)

\displaystyle\quad\times\mathbb{P}\Bigl(F(Y)=g\,\Big|\,\forall r\leq\ell(d):\sum_{i\in\Sigma_{r}}y_{i}F(y_{i})-g\in G_{r}\Bigr).

We next bound the probabilities occurring in (
3.16
).
Recall that the
y_{i}
are independent.
Hence, if we fix some
j\leq\ell(d)
and condition on the values achieved by the
y_{i}
with
i\in\Sigma_{j-1}
, then

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}

\displaystyle\Bigl(\sum_{i\in\Sigma_{j}}y_{i}F(e_{i})-g\in G_{j}\,\Big|\,\forall r<j:\sum_{i\in\Sigma_{r}}y_{i}F(y_{i})-g\in G_{r}\Bigr)

(3.17)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\Bigl[\mathbb{P}\Bigl(\sum_{i\in\Sigma_{j}}y_{i}F(e_{i})-g\in G_{j}\,\Big|\,y_{i}:i\in\Sigma_{j-1}\Bigr)\,\Big|\,\forall r<j:\sum_{i\in\Sigma_{r}}y_{i}F(y_{i})-g\in G_{r}\Bigr]

\displaystyle\leq\max_{h\in G_{j-1}}\mathbb{P}\Bigl(\sum_{i\in\sigma_{j}}y_{i}F(e_{i})-h\in G_{j}\Bigr).

Here, the final step used that
\Sigma_{j}\setminus\Sigma_{j-1}=\sigma_{j}
and
\sum_{i\in\Sigma_{j-1}}y_{i}F(e_{i})-g
was identified with
h
.
Denote
F_{j}:V_{\sigma_{j}}\to G_{j-1}/G_{j}
for the map found by restricting
F
to
V_{\sigma_{j}}
and reducing modulo
G_{j}
.
Recall (
3.13
) and note that
w_{j}=\#\sigma_{j}
.
We claim that
F_{j}
is a code of distance
w_{j}
; recall
Definition

3.5
.
Indeed, the maximality of (
3.12
) ensures that
G_{j-1}/G_{j}
is a cyclic group of prime order and consequently, for every
i\in\sigma_{j}
,
F_{j}(e_{i})
generates
G_{j-1}/G_{j}
since
F_{j}(e_{i})\not\equiv 0\bmod G_{j}
by definition of
\sigma_{j}
; recall (
3.15
).
Now apply
[
15
, Lemma 4.5]
to
F_{j}
and use that
p_{j}=\#(G_{j-1}/G_{j})
to find

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\Bigl(\sum_{i\in\sigma_{j}}y_{i}F(e_{i})-h\in G_{j}\Bigr)\leq\frac{1}{p_{j}}+\frac{p_{j}-1}{p_{j}}\exp\Bigl(-\frac{\alpha w_{j}}{p_{j}^{2}}\Bigr).

(3.18)

Using this bound on the product in (
3.16
) yields the product in (
3.14
).
It remains to bound the remaining factor.
Here, similarly to (
3.17
), we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\Bigl(F(Y)=g\,\Big|\,\forall r\leq\ell(d):\sum_{i\in\Sigma_{r}}y_{i}F(y_{i})-g\in G_{r}\Bigr)\leq\max_{h\in G_{\ell(d)}}\mathbb{P}\Bigl(\sum_{i\not\in\Sigma_{\ell(d)}}y_{i}F(e_{i})=h\Bigr).

By the argument preceding (
3.9
), the restriction of
F
to
V_{\setminus\Sigma_{\ell(d)}}
defines a code of distance
\delta n
.
Hence, by
[
15
, Lemma 4.5]
and the fact that
\exp(H)\mid\exp(G)
,

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\Bigl(\sum_{i\not\in\Sigma_{\ell(d)}}y_{i}F(e_{i})=h\Bigr)\leq(\#H)^{-1}+\exp(-\alpha\delta n/a^{2}).

(3.19)

It was here used that
(\#H-1)/\#H\leq 1
.
Use that
\#H=\#G/d
to conclude the proof.
∎

Corollary 3.9
.

Adopt assumption
(A1)
and let
N
be a
\mathbb{Z}[x]
-module such that all prime divisors of
\#N
are in
\mathscr{P}
.
Then, for every subgroup
H\subseteq N
and every
F\in\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbb{Z}}(\mathbb{Z}^{n},N)
which is
\delta
-robust for
H
,

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}(F\mathbf{X}=xF)\leq

\displaystyle\bigl((\#N)^{-1}d+\exp\bigl(-\alpha_{n}\delta n/a^{2}\bigr)\bigr)^{n}\prod_{j=1}^{\ell(d)}\Bigl(p_{j}^{-1}+\frac{p_{j}-1}{p_{j}}\exp(-\alpha_{n}w_{j}/p_{j}^{2})\Bigr)^{n}

where
d,p_{j},w_{j}
, and
a
are defined as in
Lemma

3.8
with
G=N
.

Proof.

Since the entries of
\mathbf{X}
are assumed to be independent, one has that
\mathbb{P}(F\mathbf{X}=xF)=\prod_{i=1}^{n}\mathbb{P}(F(\mathbf{X}e_{i})=xF(e_{i}))
.
The result is hence immediate from
Lemma

3.8
applied with
Y\vcentcolon=\mathbf{X}e_{i}
and
g\vcentcolon=xF(e_{i})
.
∎

Lemma 3.10
.

Let
N
be a
\mathbb{Z}[x]
-module such that all prime divisors of
\#N
are in
\mathscr{P}
and adopt assumptions
(A1)
and
(A3)
.
Then, there exists
\delta_{0}>0
such that for every
\delta<\delta_{0}
there exist constants
C,c>0
such that for all
n\geq 1

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}F\in\operatorname{Sur}_{\mathbb{Z}}(\mathbb{Z}^{n},N)\\
F\text{ not a code of distance }\delta n\end{subarray}}\hskip-10.00002pt\mathbb{P}(F\mathbf{X}=xF)\leq Cn^{-c}.

Proof.

By (
3.11
), one may upper bound the sum as

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}F\in\operatorname{Sur}_{\mathbb{Z}}(\mathbb{Z}^{n},N)\\
F\text{ not a code of distance }\delta n\end{subarray}}\hskip-10.00002pt\mathbb{P}(F\mathbf{X}=xF)\leq\sum_{H\subsetneq N}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}F\in\operatorname{Sur}_{\mathbb{Z}}(\mathbb{Z}^{n},N)\\
F\text{ is }\delta\text{-robust for }H\end{subarray}}\mathbb{P}(F\mathbf{X}=xF).

(3.20)

Fix some proper subgroup
H\subsetneq N
and pick a maximal chain of subgroups
G_{j}
as in (
3.12
) with
G=N
.
We denote
d\vcentcolon=[N:H]
.

By
[
15
, Lemma 4.10]
, the number of
F\in\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbb{Z}}(\mathbb{Z}^{n},N)
which are
\delta
-robust for
H
and satisfy that there are exactly
w_{j}
indices
i\leq n
with
F(e_{i})\in G_{j-1}\setminus G_{j}
for
1\leq j\leq\ell(d)
is at most
(\#H)^{n-\sum_{j=1}^{\ell(d)}w_{j}}\prod_{j=1}^{\ell(d)}\binom{n}{w_{j}}(\#G_{j-1})^{w_{j}}
.
When
F
is surjective we have
w_{1}\neq 0
.
Further, when
F
is
\delta
-robust we have
w_{j}\leq\ell(d)\delta n
for all
j\leq\ell(d)
.
Indeed, if this were not the case then we would have
\#\{i\leq n:F(e_{i})\not\in H\}>\ell(d)\delta n
which contradicts (
3.8
).
Now, by the combination of
Corollary

3.9
with the aforementioned count on the number of
\delta
-robust morphisms,

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}F\in\operatorname{Sur}_{\mathbb{Z}}(\mathbb{Z}^{n},N)\\
F\text{ is }\delta\text{-robust for }H\end{subarray}}

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}(F\mathbf{X}=xF)\leq\hskip-2.5pt\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}0\leq w_{1},\ldots,w_{\ell(d)}\leq\ell(d)\delta n\\
w_{1}\neq 0\end{subarray}}\hskip-2.5pt(\#H)^{n-\sum_{j=1}^{\ell(d)}w_{j}}\prod_{j=1}^{\ell(d)}\binom{n}{w_{j}}(\#G_{j-1})^{w_{j}}

(3.21)

\displaystyle\times\bigl((\#N)^{-1}d+\exp\bigl(-\alpha_{n}\delta n/a^{2}\bigr)\bigr)^{n}\prod_{j=1}^{\ell(d)}\Bigl(p_{j}^{-1}+\frac{p_{j}-1}{p_{j}}\exp(-\alpha_{n}w_{j}/p_{j}^{2})\Bigr)^{n}.

It remains a nontrivial task to compute the right-hand side of (
3.21
).
Fortunately, a related sum was considered by Nguyen and Wood
[
15
]
and we can extract the relevant estimate from their proofs.
More precisely, the sum in (
3.21
) is a special case of the sum which occurs in the first centered equation of the proof of
[
15
, Theorem 4.12]
: take
u=0
in their notation.
Following the arguments word-for-word up to the centered inequality at the end of page 23 in
[
15
]
now yields the desired result.
∎

Lemma 3.11
.

Adopt assumptions
(A1)
and
(A3)
and fix a
\mathbb{Z}[x]
-module
N
such that all prime divisors of
\#N
are in
\mathscr{P}
.
Then, there exists
\delta_{0}>0
such that for every
\delta<\delta_{0}
there exist constants
C,c>0
such that for all
n\geq 1

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}F\in\operatorname{Sur}_{\mathbb{Z}}(\mathbb{Z}^{n},N)\\
F\text{ not a code of distance }\delta n\end{subarray}}\hskip-10.00002pt\lvert\mathbb{P}(F\mathbf{X}=xF)-(\#N)^{-n}\rvert\leq Cn^{-c}.

Proof.

By
Definition

3.5
, if
F
is not a code of distance
\delta n
then we can find some
\sigma\subseteq\{1,\ldots,n\}
and a proper subgroup
H\subsetneq N
such that
F(V_{\setminus\sigma})\subseteq H
and
\#\sigma=\lfloor\delta n\rfloor
.
Hence, since
F
is uniquely determined by the images of the basis elements of
\mathbb{Z}^{n}
,

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}F\in\operatorname{Sur}_{\mathbb{Z}}(\mathbb{Z}^{n},N)\\
F\text{ not a code of distance }\delta n\end{subarray}}\hskip-10.00002pt\hskip-10.00002pt(\#N)^{-n}\leq\sum_{H\subsetneq N}\binom{n}{\lfloor\delta n\rfloor}(\#H)^{n-\lfloor\delta n\rfloor}(\#N)^{-n+\lfloor\delta n\rfloor}.

(3.22)

The binary entropy bound
[
6
, Eq.(7.14), p.151]
implies that
\binom{n}{\lfloor\delta n\rfloor}\leq 2^{nE(\lfloor\delta n\rfloor/n)}
where
E(y)\vcentcolon=-y\log_{2}(y)+(1-y)\log_{2}(1-y)
.
Note that
\lim_{y\to 0}E(y)=0
.
We can hence find some sufficiently small
\delta_{0}
such that
Lemma

3.10
is applicable and
\delta_{0}+E(\lfloor\delta_{0}n\rfloor/n)<1/2
for all
n\geq 1
.
Then, since
\#H\leq\#N/2
, we have for every
\delta<\delta_{0}
that

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}F\in\operatorname{Sur}_{\mathbb{Z}}(\mathbb{Z}^{n},N)\\
F\text{ not a code of distance }\delta n\end{subarray}}\hskip-10.00002pt\hskip-10.00002pt(\#N)^{-n}\leq S_{N}2^{(E(\lfloor\delta n\rfloor/n)+\delta-1)n}\leq S_{N}2^{-n/2}

(3.23)

where
S_{N}
is the number of proper subgroups of
N
.
Let
C^{\prime},c^{\prime}>0
be such that
S_{N}2^{-n/2}\leq C^{\prime}n^{-c^{\prime}}
for all
n\geq 1
and use
Lemma

3.10
together with the triangle inequality to conclude the proof.
∎

3.1.4
Combining the estimates

We finally combine all preceding estimates to complete the proof of
Proposition

3.1
.

Proof of
Proposition

3.1
.

By (
3.3
) and the triangle inequality,

\displaystyle\lvert\mathbb{E}[\#\operatorname{Sur}_{\mathbb{Z}[x]}(\operatorname{coker}(\tilde{\mathbf{W}}),N)]-(\#{}

\displaystyle{}N)^{-k}\rvert\leq\hskip-2.5pt\sum_{F\in\operatorname{Sur}_{\mathbb{Z}}(\mathbb{Z}^{n},N):F\mathbf{B}=0}\left\lvert(\#N)^{-n}-\mathbb{P}(F\mathbf{X}=xF)\right\rvert

(3.24)

\displaystyle\quad+\left\lvert(\#N)^{-n}\#\{F\in\operatorname{Sur}_{\mathbb{Z}}(\mathbb{Z}^{n},N):F\mathbf{B}=0\}-(\#N)^{-k}\right\rvert.

Pick some
\delta>0
which is sufficiently small to ensure that
Lemma

3.11
is applicable.
Then, by the triangle inequality,

\displaystyle\sum_{F\in\operatorname{Sur}_{\mathbb{Z}}(\mathbb{Z}^{n},N):F\mathbf{B}=0}\lvert(\#N)^{-n}-\mathbb{P}(F\mathbf{X}=xF)\rvert

(3.25)

\displaystyle\quad\leq\hskip-2.5pt\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}F\in\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbb{Z}}(\mathbb{Z}^{n},N)\\
F\text{ a code of distance }\delta n\end{subarray}}\hskip-10.00002pt\lvert(\#N)^{-n}-\mathbb{P}(F\mathbf{X}=xF)\rvert+\hskip-2.5pt\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}F\in\operatorname{Sur}_{\mathbb{Z}}(\mathbb{Z}^{n},N)\\
F\text{ not a code of distance }\delta n\end{subarray}}\hskip-10.00002pt\hskip-10.00002pt\lvert(\#N)^{-n}-\mathbb{P}(F\mathbf{X}=xF)\rvert.

Let

3.6
and let

be the constants from

.
Then, the right-hand side of (

Cn^{-c}+C^{\prime}n^{-c^{\prime}}
and hence tends to zero as

tends to infinity.
Further, by

3.4
, there exists a constant
C^{\prime\prime}>0

\displaystyle\left\lvert(\#N)^{-n}\#\{F\in\operatorname{Sur}_{\mathbb{Z}}(\mathbb{Z}^{n},N):F\mathbf{B}=0\}-(\#N)^{-k}\right\rvert\leq C^{\prime\prime}2^{-n}.

(3.26)

3.2
Solving the moment problem

We next apply a general result concerning measures on categories of
[
20
, Theorem 1.6]
to invert the moment problem.
Using
[
20
, Lemma 6.1]
and
[
20
, Lemma 6.3]
, that result may be specialized to our context—namely to limiting measures on the category of finite modules with
N
-moments given by
(\#N)^{-k}
.
Let us state this specialization explicitly for the sake of definiteness:

Lemma 3.12

(Special case of
[
20
, Theorem 1.6]
)
.

Let
R
be a ring and consider a sequence of random finite
R
-modules
X_{n}
such that for every fixed finite
R
-module
N

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\mathbb{E}[\#\operatorname{Sur}_{R}(X_{n},N)]=(\#N)^{-k}.

Let
S
be quotient ring of
R
with
\#S<\infty
and let
L_{1},\ldots,L_{r}
be representatives of the isomorphism classes of finite simple
S
-modules.
Further, denote
q_{i}
for the cardinality of the endomorphism field of
L_{i}
for every
i\leq r
.
Then, for every finite
S
-module
N
,

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\mathbb{P}(X_{n}\otimes_{R}S\cong N)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{(\#N)^{k}\#\operatorname{Aut}_{S}(N)}\prod_{i=1}^{r}\prod_{j=1}^{\infty}\Bigl(1-\frac{q_{i}^{-j}\#\operatorname{Ext}^{1}_{S}(N,L_{i})}{\#\operatorname{Hom}_{S}(N,L_{i})(\#L_{i})^{k}}\Bigr).

Let us further remark that it follows from the statement of
[
20
, Theorem 1.6]
that the limiting measure in
Lemma

3.12
has
N
-moments given by
(\#N)^{-k}
.
In particular, the
N
-moment for
N=\{0\}
is equal to one which implies that the limiting measure is a probability measure.

It now remains to combine
Proposition

3.1
with
Lemma

3.12
and to simplify the results.
This can be accomplished with a direct computation.
Note that the following result implies
Theorem

1.3
as the special case with
k=1
.

Proposition 3.13
.

Adopt assumptions
(A1)
to
(A3)
.
Fix a positive integer
m\geq 1
, a monic polynomial
Q\in\mathbb{Z}[x]
of degree
\geq 1
, and introduce
Q_{i,p}\in\mathbb{F}_{p}[x]
,
r_{p}\geq 1
, and
d_{i,p}=\deg Q_{i,p}
using the factorization of
Q
modulo
p
as in
Theorem

1.3
.

For every
p\in\mathscr{P}
denote
S_{p^{m}}\vcentcolon=\mathbb{Z}[x]/(p^{m}\mathbb{Z}[x]+Q(x)\mathbb{Z}[x])
.
Then, given a finite
S_{p^{m}}
-module
N_{p^{m},Q}
for every
p\in\mathscr{P}
, it holds that

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\mathbb{P}(\forall p\in\mathscr{P}:

\displaystyle\operatorname{coker}(\tilde{\mathbf{W}})_{p^{m},Q}\cong N_{p^{m},Q})=\prod_{p\in\mathscr{P}}\biggl(\frac{1}{(\#N_{p^{m},Q})^{k}\#\operatorname{Aut}_{S_{p^{m}}}(N_{p^{m},Q})}

\displaystyle\quad\times\prod_{i=1}^{r_{p}}\prod_{j=1}^{\infty}\Bigl(1-\frac{\#\operatorname{Ext}_{S_{p^{m}}}^{1}\bigl(N_{p^{m},Q},\mathbb{F}_{p}[x]/(Q_{i,p}(x)\mathbb{F}_{p}[x])\bigr)}{\#\operatorname{Hom}_{S_{p^{m}}}\bigl(N_{p^{m},Q},\mathbb{F}_{p}[x]/(Q_{i,p}(x)\mathbb{F}_{p}[x])\bigr)}p^{-(k+j)d_{i,p}}\Bigr)\biggr).

Proof.

Denote
R\vcentcolon=\mathbb{Z}[x]/((\prod_{p\in\mathscr{P}}p^{m})\mathbb{Z}[x])
and note that for any
\mathbb{Z}[x]
-module
M
and any
R
-module
N
one has a bijection between
\operatorname{Sur}_{R}(M\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}[x]}R,N)
and
\operatorname{Sur}_{\mathbb{Z}[x]}(M,N)
.
Consequently, by
Proposition

3.1
, we have that for every finite
R
-module
N

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\mathbb{E}[\#\operatorname{Sur}_{R}(\operatorname{coker}(\tilde{\mathbf{W}})\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}[x]}R,N)]=(\#N)^{-k}.

(3.27)

Let
S\vcentcolon=R/(Q(t)R)
and define a finite
S
-module by
N\vcentcolon=\oplus_{p\in\mathscr{P}}N_{p^{m},Q}
.
The Chinese remainder theorem yields
R\cong\oplus_{p\in\mathscr{P}}\mathbb{Z}[x]/(p^{m}\mathbb{Z}[x])
which implies that
S=\oplus_{p\in\mathscr{P}}S_{p^{m}}
and
\operatorname{coker}(\tilde{\mathbf{W}})\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}[x]}S\cong\oplus_{p\in\mathscr{P}}\operatorname{coker}(\tilde{\mathbf{W}})_{p^{m},Q}
.
Consequently, by
Lemma

3.12
and the fact that isomorphism occurs if and only if the corresponding summands are isomorphic,

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\bigl(\forall p\in\mathscr{P}:

\displaystyle\operatorname{coker}(\tilde{\mathbf{W}})_{p^{m},Q}\cong N_{p^{m},Q}\bigr)=\mathbb{P}\bigl((\operatorname{coker}(\tilde{\mathbf{W}})\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}[x]}R)\otimes_{R}S\cong N\bigr)

(3.28)

\displaystyle\quad=\frac{1}{(\#N)^{k}\#\operatorname{Aut}_{S}(N)}\prod_{i=1}^{r}\prod_{j=1}^{\infty}\Bigl(1-\frac{\#\operatorname{Ext}^{1}_{S}(N,L_{i})}{\#\operatorname{Hom}_{S}(N,L_{i})(\#L_{i})^{k}}q_{i}^{-j}\Bigr).

Simple modules over
S
are precisely the modules of the form
S/m
with
m
a maximal ideal of
S
.
Further, maximal ideals of
\mathbb{Z}[x]
are of the form
m=p\mathbb{Z}[x]+f(x)\mathbb{Z}[x]
with
p
a prime and
f(x)\in\mathbb{Z}[x]
irreducible modulo
p
of degree
\geq 1

[
19
, p.22]
.
Hence, since maximal ideals of
S
are in one-to-one correspondence with maximal ideals of
\mathbb{Z}[x]
which contain
\prod_{p\in\mathscr{P}}p^{m}
and
Q
, the modules
L_{i}
in (
3.28
) are of the form
\mathbb{F}_{p}[x]/(Q_{i,p}(x)\mathbb{F}_{p}[x])
.
Consequently, by (
3.28
) and the fact that the endomorphism field of
\mathbb{F}_{p}[x]/(Q_{i,p}(x)\mathbb{F}_{p}[x]))
is isomorphic to
\mathbb{F}_{p}[x]/(Q_{i,p}(x)\mathbb{F}_{p}[x])
which is a finite field of order
p^{d_{i,p}}
,

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\mathbb{P}\bigl(\forall p\in\mathscr{P}:\operatorname{coker}(\tilde{\mathbf{W}})_{p^{m},Q}\cong N_{p^{m},Q}\bigr)

(3.29)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{(\#N)^{k}\#\operatorname{Aut}_{S}(N)}\prod_{p\in\mathscr{P}}\prod_{i=1}^{r_{p}}\prod_{j=1}^{\infty}\Bigl(1-\frac{\#\operatorname{Ext}_{S}^{1}\bigl(N,\mathbb{F}_{p}[x]/(Q_{i,p}(x)\mathbb{F}_{p}[x])\bigr)}{\#\operatorname{Hom}_{S}\bigl(N,\mathbb{F}_{p}[x]/(Q_{i,p}(x)\mathbb{F}_{p}[x])\bigr)}p^{-(k+j)d_{i,p}}\Bigr).

Here, observe that
\#N=\prod_{p\in\mathscr{P}}\#N_{p^{m},Q}
,
\#\operatorname{Aut}_{S}(N)=\prod_{p\in\mathscr{P}}\#\operatorname{Aut}_{S_{p^{m}}}(N_{p^{m},Q})
, and note that
\#\operatorname{Hom}_{S}\bigl(N,\mathbb{F}_{p}[x]/(Q_{i,p}(x)\mathbb{F}_{p}[x])\bigr)=\#\operatorname{Hom}_{S_{p^{m}}}(N_{p^{m},Q},\mathbb{F}_{p}[x]/(Q_{i,p}(x)\mathbb{F}_{p}[x]))
.
Further, since
\operatorname{Ext}
takes with direct sums in the first argument to products
[
31
, Proposition 3.3.4]
,

\displaystyle\#\operatorname{Ext}_{S}^{1}(N,\mathbb{F}_{p}[x]/(Q_{i,p}(x)\mathbb{F}_{p}[x]))

\displaystyle=\prod_{q\in\mathscr{P}}\#\operatorname{Ext}_{S}^{1}(N_{q^{m},Q},\mathbb{F}_{p}[x]/(Q_{i,p}(x)\mathbb{F}_{p}[x])).

(3.30)

Finally, using the definition of
\operatorname{Ext}^{1}
in terms of short exact sequences
[
31
, Theorem 3.4.3]
together with the fact that finite
S
-modules correspond to tuples of
S_{p^{m}}
-modules, one can verify that

\displaystyle\#\operatorname{Ext}_{S}^{1}(N_{q^{m},Q},\mathbb{F}_{p}[x]/(Q_{i,p}(x)\mathbb{F}_{p}[x]))

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}\#\operatorname{Ext}_{S_{p^{m}}}^{1}(N_{p^{m},Q},\mathbb{F}_{p}[x]/(Q_{i,p}(x)\mathbb{F}_{p}[x]))&\text{if }p=q,\\
1&\text{else}.\end{cases}

(3.31)

Combine (
3.29
)–(
3.31
) to conclude the proof.
∎

4
Future work

Ultimately, we would like to show that the conditions of
Theorem

1.1
are satisfied with nonvanishing probability.
The current paper makes progress in this direction: we now have concrete proof techniques which can be used to study cokernels of walk matrices.
There are however still a number of interesting open problems.

For instance, it remains entirely open to understand whether the condition
\operatorname{coker}(\mathbf{W})_{2^{2}}\cong(\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z})^{\lfloor n/2\rfloor}
is often satisfied, even heuristically.
This is because the distribution of
\operatorname{coker}(\mathbf{W})_{2^{m}}
is highly sensitive to the graph being simple which makes approximation by results for directed graphs inadequate.
Indeed, when
\mathbf{X}
is the adjacency matrix of a simple graph and
b=e
is the all-ones vector, then
[
26
, Lemma 14]
implies that
\operatorname{rank}_{2}\mathbf{W}\leq\lceil n/2\rceil
.
Equivalently, it holds that
\operatorname{coker}(\mathbf{W})_{2}\cong(\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z})^{\ell}
for some
\ell\geq\lfloor n/2\rfloor
.
This is very different behavior from the distribution for directed graphs in
Theorem

1.2
with
p=2
since the latter is concentrated on small groups.

For odd primes, numerical evidence suggest that the difference is not as severe.

Table

1
namely suggest that the distribution of
\operatorname{coker}(\mathbf{W})_{p^{m}}
has qualitatively similar behavior for simple and directed graphs.
Quantitatively, however, a close inspection shows that there is a small but nonzero difference which does not seem to disappear when
n
grows large, suggesting that the limiting distribution for simple graphs differs from the one for random directed and weighted graphs.
For instance, the estimated probabilities that
\operatorname{coker}(\mathbf{W})_{p^{2}}\in\{0,\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z}\}
for
p=3
is
0.758\pm 0.002
whereas
Theorem

1.2
predicts
0.747
.

So, the extension of our results to the setting of simple graphs poses an interesting problem, both for odd and even primes.
We intend to pursue this in future work.

In support of this conjecture, we present numerical data in

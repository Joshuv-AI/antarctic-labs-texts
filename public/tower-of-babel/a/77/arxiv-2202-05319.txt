Strong persistence and associated primes of powers of monomial ideals
Amir Mafi and Hero Saremi

Abstract
. Let R = K ⁡ [ x 1 , … , x n ] R=K[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}] be the polynomial ring in n n variables over a field K K and I I be a monomial ideal of degree d ≤ 2 d\leq 2 . We show that ( I k + 1 : I ) = I k (I^{k+1}:I)=I^{k} for all k ≥ 1 k\geq 1 and we disprove a motivation question that was asked by of Carlini, Hà, Harbourne and Van Tuyl by providing of a counterexample. Also, by this counterexample, we give a negative answer to the question that the depth function of square-free monomial ideals are non-increasing.

1.
Introduction

Throughout this paper, we assume that
R=K[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}]
is the polynomial ring in
n
variables over the field
K
with the maximal ideal
\mathfrak{m}=\langle x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}\rangle
and
I
is a monomial ideal of
R
. Let
\operatorname{Ass}(I)
be the set of associated primes of
R/I
. Brodmann
[
4
]
showed that there exists an integer
k_{0}
such that
\operatorname{Ass}(I^{k})=\operatorname{Ass}(I^{k_{0}})
for all
k\geq k_{0}
. This stable set of associated primes is denoted by
\operatorname{Ass}^{\infty}(I)
. Recently there have been several publications in
[
1
,
2
,
3
,
6
,
14
,
16
,
18
,
20
]
about the set of associated primes of monomial ideals. An ideal
I
is said to satisfy the
strong persistence property
if
(I^{k+1}:I)=I^{k}
for all
k\geq 1
. Ratliff
[
25
]
proved that
(I^{k+1}:I)=I^{k}
for all large
k
.
There are some interesting classes of monomial ideals that satisfy the strong persistence property. These include normal ideals, edge ideals of graphs, vertex cover ideals of perfect graphs (or chordal graphs), polymatroidal ideals, vertex cover ideals of cycle graphs of odd orders, vertex cover ideals of wheel graphs of even orders and all square-free monomial ideals in
R
with
n\leq 4
, see for details
[
8
,
9
,
16
,
21
,
24
,
25
,
26
,
29
]
.

An ideal
I
is said to satisfy the
persistence property
if
\operatorname{Ass}(I^{k})\subseteq\operatorname{Ass}(I^{k+1})
for all
k\geq 1
. It is known that
the strong persistence property yields the persistence property, see
[
15
]
.
Brodmann
[
5
]
showed that there exists an integer
k_{0}
such that
\operatorname{depth}R/I^{k}=\operatorname{depth}R/I^{k_{0}}
for all
k\geq k_{0}
. Herzog and Hibi
[
11
]
proved that if
I
is a graded ideal such that
I^{k}
has a linear resolution for all
k\geq 1
, then
\operatorname{depth}R/I^{k}
is a non-increasing function for all
k\geq 1
.
The following questions were raised in
[
11
,
16
,
23
]
:

Question 1.1
.

(i)
Do all square-free monomial ideals satisfy the strong persistence property?

(ii)
Do all square-free monomial ideals satisfy the persistence property?

(iii)
Do all square-free monomial ideals have non-increasing depth function?

A remarkable example of a vertex cover ideal
I
was given in
[
17
]
with the property that
(I^{4}:I)\neq I^{3}
,
\operatorname{Ass}(I^{3})\not\subseteq\operatorname{Ass}(I^{4})
and
\operatorname{depth}R/I^{3}\not\geq\operatorname{depth}R/I^{4}
. This gives a negative answer to the above questions.
This counterexample has the property that
\operatorname{Ass}(I^{2})\subseteq\operatorname{Ass}(I^{n})
for all
n\geq 2
. After that Carlini, Hà, Harbourne and Van Tuyl in
[
6
, Question 2.51]
asked the following question:

Question 1.2
.

Let
I
be any square-free monomial ideal. Does
\operatorname{Ass}(I^{2})\subseteq\operatorname{Ass}(I^{k})
for all
k\geq 2
?

In this paper, we disprove Question
1.2
, in general, by providing a counterexample, and also we give some positive answers in some special cases. Furthermore, we show that the strong persistence property holds for all monomial ideals of degree
d\leq 2
. This is a generalization of
[
21
, Theorem 2.15]
.
For unexplained notation or terminology, we refer the reader to
[
12
]
.

2.
The results

We start this section with the following definition:

Definition 2.1
.

Let
I
be an ideal of
R
. Then an element
x\in R
is
integral over

I
, if there is an equation

{x}^{k}+a_{1}{x}^{k-1}+\dots+a_{k-1}{x}+a_{k}=0,

with
a_{i}\in I^{i}
. The set of elements
\overline{I}
in
R
which are integral over
I
is the integral closure of
I
. It is clear

I\subseteq\overline{I}\subseteq\sqrt{I}
. The ideal
I
is integrally closed if
I=\overline{I}
, and
I
is normal if all powers of
I
are integrally closed.

It is clear that if
I
is a square-free monomial ideal, then
I=\sqrt{I}
and so
I
is integrally closed.

The following result is known but for readers we give an easy proof:

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
I
be a monomial ideal of
R
. If
(I^{k+1}:I)=I^{k}
for some
k\geq 1
, then
\operatorname{Ass}(I^{k})\subseteq\operatorname{Ass}(I^{k+1})
.
In particular, if
I
satisfies the strong persistence property, then
I
satisfies the persistence property.

Proof.

Suppose
\mathfrak{p}\in\operatorname{Ass}(I^{k})=\operatorname{Ass}(I^{k+1}:I)
. Then
\mathfrak{p}=((I^{k+1}:I):\alpha)
for some monomial element
\alpha\in R
and so
\mathfrak{p}=(I^{k+1}:{\alpha}I)
. Thus there is a monomial
u\in I
such that
\mathfrak{p}=(I^{k+1}:{\alpha}u)
and hence
\mathfrak{p}\in\operatorname{Ass}(I^{k+1})
.
Therefore
\operatorname{Ass}(I^{k})\subseteq\operatorname{Ass}(I^{k+1})
. For the remaining proof, by hypothesis we have
(I^{k+1}:I)=I^{k}
for all
k\geq 1
and this implies
\operatorname{Ass}(I^{k})\subseteq\operatorname{Ass}(I^{k+1})
for all
k\geq 1
, as required.
∎

Lemma 2.3
.

(
[
22
, Lemma 11.27]
)
Let
I
be an ideal of
R
. Then, for all
n\geq m\geq 1
,
(\overline{I^{n}}:\overline{I^{m}})=(\overline{I^{n}}:I^{m})=\overline{I^{n-m}}
.

Remark 2.4
.

Let
I
be a square-free monomial ideal of
R
. Then, for all
k\geq 1
,

I\subseteq(I^{2}:I)\subseteq(I^{k}:I^{k-1})\subseteq(\overline{I^{k}}:I^{k-1})=\overline{I}=I

the first equality follows by Lemma
2.3
. Thus
(I^{k}:I^{k-1})=I
and so by the same argument of Lemma
2.2
we have
\operatorname{Ass}(I)\subseteq\operatorname{Ass}(I^{k})
for all
k\geq 1
.

Definition 2.5
.

For a square-free monomial ideal
I=\langle\{x_{i1}\dots x_{in_{i}}\mid i=1,\dots,t\}\rangle
of
R
the
Alexander dual
of
I
, denoted by
I^{\vee}
, is is the square-free monomial ideal
I^{\vee}=\cap_{i=1}^{t}\langle x_{i1},\dots,x_{in_{i}}\rangle
.

Following
[
7
]
let
G
be a finite simple graph on the vertex set
V=\{x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}\}
with edge set
E
. By identifying the vertices with the variables in the polynomial ring
R=K[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}]
, one can associate to
G
a square-free quadratic monomial ideal
I(G)=\langle x_{i}x_{j}|~\{x_{i},x_{j}\}\in E\rangle.

The ideal
I(G)
is called the
edge ideal
of
G
which was first introduced by Villarreal
[
28
]
. The ideal
I(G)^{\vee}
is referred to as the
cover ideal
because of the well-known fact that the generators of
I(G)^{\vee}
correspond to vertex covers (see
[
13
]
).

Theorem 2.6
.

(
[
7
, Theorem 3.2]
)
Let
G
be a finite simple graph and
J=I(G)^{\vee}
. Then the irredundant irreducible decomposition of
J^{2}
is

J^{2}=\bigcap_{\{x_{i},x_{j}\}\in E}\langle x_{i},x_{j}\rangle^{2}\cap\bigcap_{\{x_{i_{1}},\ldots,x_{i_{s}}\}~is~an~induced~odd~cycle}\langle x_{i_{1}}^{2},\ldots,x_{i_{s}}^{2}\rangle.

Simis, Vasconcelos and Villarreal in
[
27
, Proposition 6.6]
proved that if
I
is an edge ideal of a finite simple graph, then
I^{2}
is integrally closed. In the following proposition, we prove a similar result for the vertex cover ideal of an edge ideal.

Proposition 2.7
.

Let
I
be an edge ideal of a finite simple graph
G
and
J
be a vertex cover ideal of
I
. Then
\overline{J^{2}}=J^{2}
. In particular,
(J^{3}:J)=J^{2}
.

Proof.

It is enough to prove that
\operatorname{Ass}(\overline{J^{2}}/J^{2})=\emptyset
. By Theorem
2.6
,
\operatorname{Ass}(R/J^{2})=\{(x_{i},x_{j})\mid~\{x_{i},x_{j}\}\in E\}\cup\{(x_{i_{1}},\ldots,x_{i_{s}})\mid~

\{x_{i_{1}},\ldots,x_{i_{s}}\}
is an induced odd cycle of
G

\}
. Since
\operatorname{Ass}(\overline{J^{2}}/J^{2})\subseteq\operatorname{Ass}(R/J^{2})
, it readily follows
(x_{i},x_{j})\not\in\operatorname{Ass}(\overline{J^{2}}/J^{2})
and so we may assume
\mathfrak{p}=(x_{i_{1}},\ldots,x_{i_{s}})\in\operatorname{Ass}(\overline{J^{2}}/J^{2})
.
Therefore
\overline{J^{2}_{\mathfrak{p}}}/J^{2}_{\mathfrak{p}}\neq 0
and we may assume
J_{\mathfrak{p}}
is a vertex cover ideal of an odd cycle graph. By using

[
1
, Theorem 1.10]
we have
\overline{J^{2}_{\mathfrak{p}}}=J^{2}_{\mathfrak{p}}
and this is a contradiction. Thus
\operatorname{Ass}(\overline{J^{2}}/J^{2})=\emptyset
and so

\overline{J^{2}}=J^{2}
. For the remaining of the proof, we have
J^{2}\subseteq(J^{3}:J)\subseteq(\overline{J^{3}}:J)=\overline{J^{2}}
the last equality follows by Lemma
2.3
. Since
\overline{J^{2}}=J^{2}
, we have the result. This completes the proof.
∎

The following corollary gives a partially positive answer to Question
1.2
.

Corollary 2.8
.

Let
I
be a square-free monomial ideal of
R
such that
\overline{I^{2}}=I^{2}
. Then
\operatorname{Ass}(I^{2})\subseteq\operatorname{Ass}(I^{k})
for all
k\geq 2
. In particular,
if
I
is a vertex cover ideal of a finite simple graph then
\operatorname{Ass}(I^{2})\subseteq\operatorname{Ass}(I^{k})
for all
k\geq 2
.

Proof.

By our hypothesis and Lemma
2.3
, we have
I^{2}\subseteq(I^{k}:I^{k-2})\subseteq(\overline{I^{k}}:I^{k-2})=I^{2}
for all
k\geq 2
.
Therefore
(I^{k}:I^{k-2})=I^{2}
. Now, by using the same argument as Lemma
2.2
we have
\operatorname{Ass}(I^{2})\subseteq\operatorname{Ass}(I^{k})
for all
k\geq 2
.
In particular, if
I
is a vertex cover ideal of a finite simple graph, then by Proposition
2.7
we have the result, as required.
∎

By the following example we show that Question
1.2
and also Question
1.1
in general have negative answers:

Example 2.9
.

Let
R=K[x_{1},\ldots,x_{7}]
and
I=\langle x_{1}x_{4}x_{5}x_{7},x_{2}x_{3}x_{6},x_{2}x_{3}x_{7},x_{2}x_{4}x_{5},\\
x_{2}x_{4}x_{7},x_{2}x_{5}x_{6},x_{3}x_{4}x_{5},x_{3}x_{4}x_{6},x_{3}x_{5}x_{7},x_{4}x_{6}x_{7},x_{5}x_{6}x_{7}\rangle

be the square-free monomial ideal of
R
. By using Macaulay2 we have
\mathfrak{m}\in\operatorname{Ass}(I^{2})\setminus\operatorname{Ass}(I^{3})
and by the algorithm
[
2
]
it follows
\mathfrak{m}\notin\operatorname{Ass}^{\infty}(I)
. Therefore
\operatorname{Ass}(I^{2})\not\subseteq\operatorname{Ass}(I^{3})
,
(I^{3}:I)\neq I^{2}
,
\operatorname{depth}(R/I^{3})\not\leq\operatorname{depth}(R/I^{2})
and
\operatorname{Ass}^{\infty}(I)\neq\bigcup_{k=1}^{\infty}\operatorname{Ass}(I^{k})
.

For the next result we introduce the following terminology: let
I=\langle u_{1},\ldots,u_{t}\rangle
be a monomial ideal of
R
. Then we set
\operatorname{supp}(I)=\bigcup_{i=1}^{t}\operatorname{supp}(u_{i})
and
\deg(I)=\max\{\deg(u_{i})\mid 1\leq i\leq t\}
, where
\operatorname{supp}(u)=\{x_{i}|~u=x_{1}^{a_{1}}\ldots x_{n}^{a_{n}},a_{i}\neq 0\}

and
\deg(x_{1}^{a_{1}}\ldots x_{s}^{a_{s}})=\sum_{i=1}^{s}a_{i}
.

Lemma 2.10
.

Let
\underline{x}=\langle x_{1},\ldots,x_{t}\rangle
and
J
be a monomial ideal of
R
such that
x_{i}\not\in\operatorname{supp}(J)
for all
1\leq i\leq t
.
Then
(\underline{x}^{k+1}J:x_{i})=\underline{x}^{k}J
for all
k\geq 1
.

Proof.

Suppose
\alpha\in(\underline{x}^{k+1}J:x_{i})
and so
\alpha x_{i}\in\underline{x}^{k+1}J
.
Since
\underline{x}^{k+1}J=(\underline{y}^{k+1}+x_{i}\underline{x}^{k})J
where
\underline{y}=\langle x_{1},\ldots,x_{i-1},x_{i+1},\ldots,x_{t}\rangle
, it therefore follows that
\alpha x_{i}\in x_{i}\underline{x}^{k}J
and so
\alpha\in\underline{x}^{k}J
.
Hence
(\underline{x}^{k+1}J:x_{i})=\underline{x}^{k}J
for all
k\geq 1
, as required.
∎

Proposition 2.11
.

Let
I
be a monomial ideal of
R
such that
\deg(I)\leq 2
. If
I=\langle x_{1},\ldots,x_{t}\rangle+J
such that
J
is a monomial ideal of
R
with
\deg(J)\leq 2
and
x_{i}\notin\operatorname{supp}(J)
for all
1\leq i\leq t
, then
(I^{k+1}:I)=I^{k}
for all
k\geq 1
and
1\leq i\leq t
.

Proof.

First we prove that
(I^{k+1}:x_{i})=I^{k}
for all
k\geq 1
and
1\leq i\leq t
.
It is clear
I^{k+1}=\sum_{j=0}^{k+1}\underline{x}^{j}J^{k+1-j}
, where
\underline{x}=\langle x_{1},\ldots,x_{t}\rangle
.
It therefore follows
(I^{k+1}:x_{i})=J^{k+1}+\sum_{j=1}^{k+1}(\underline{x}^{j}J^{k+1-j}:x_{i})
. Now by using Lemma
2.10
and
[
19
, Lemma 2.1]

we get
(I^{k+1}:x_{i})=\sum_{j=0}^{k}\underline{x}^{j}J^{k-j}=I^{k}
. Since
I^{k}\subseteq(I^{k+1}:I)\subseteq(I^{k+1}:\underline{x})\subseteq(I^{k+1}:x_{i})=I^{k}
, we have
(I^{k+1}:I)=I^{k}
for all
k\geq 1
, as required.

∎

The following result is a generalization of
[
21
, Theorem 2.15]
.

Theorem 2.12
.

Let
I
be a monomial ideal of
R
such that
\deg(I)\leq 2
. Then
(I^{k+1}:I)=I^{k}
for all
k\geq 1
. In particular,
\operatorname{Ass}(I^{k})\subseteq\operatorname{Ass}(I^{k+1})

for all
k\geq 1
.

Proof.

If
I
is a monomial ideal of single degree
2
, then the result follows by
[
26
, Theorem 3]
. Now, we may assume that
I=\langle x_{1},\ldots,x_{t}\rangle+J
such that
\deg(J)\leq 2
. Therefore by Proposition
2.11
we have
(I^{k+1}:I)=I^{k}
. The proof then follows by Lemma
2.2
.

∎

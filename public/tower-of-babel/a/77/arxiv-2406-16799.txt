Correlation functions for the Gross-Neveu model
J. Dimock

Abstract
This is a non-perturbative treatment of correlation functions for the weakly coupled massless Gross-Neveu model in a finite volume. The main result is that all correlation functions, treated as distributions, are uniformly bounded in the ultraviolet cutoff.



1
Introduction

The Gross-Neveu model is a quantum field theory on a two dimensional spacetime, here taken to be a torus with Euclidean metric. It describes fermi fields with
n\geq 2
internal components
and a quartic self interaction
g\int(\bar{\psi}\psi)^{2}
. In a previous paper
[
6
]

we gave a non-perturbative treatment of the renormalization group (RG) flow and used it
to prove bounds on the partition function uniform in the ultraviolet cutoff. In the present paper we study correlation functions
<\psi(x_{1})\cdots\psi(x_{n})\bar{\psi}(y_{1})\cdots\bar{\psi}(y_{n})>
regarded as distributions. We find that the established results on the RG flow are sufficient to bound the correlation functions uniformly in an ultraviolet cutoff (but not in the volume).

Our results are complementary to earlier results due to Gawedzki, Kupiainen
[
12
]
and
Feldman, Magnen, Rivasseau, Seneor
[
7
]
as well as subsequent papers referenced in
[
6
]
. These papers
take advantage of the fact that perturbation theory for the model is convergent. They expand to all orders in the coupling constant
and analyze the
n^{th}
order contribution with a detailed study of Feynman graphs. From this it is possible to extract information
about the correlation functions.

We regard both methods as useful. The present non-perturbative treatment seems simpler while the complete perturbative treatment
carries more information in principle. We should add that the purpose of the present paper is not just to generate results for the Gross-Neveu model,
but also to develop techniques that work for models with boson fields where perturbation theory typically does not converge.

The paper is organized as follows. In section
2
we define the model and recall the renormalization group results.
In section
3
we establish some identities for Grassmann integration which play a key role in the analysis. In section
4

we prove the bounds on the correlation functions.

2
Preliminaries

We start in a finite volume by working on the torus
{\mathbb{T}}_{M}={\mathbb{R}}^{2}/L^{M}{\mathbb{Z}}^{2}

with volume
L^{2M}
. Here
L
is a fixed positive integer and
M
is
the long distance cutoff. The fields are elements on an infinite dimensional Grassmann algebra generated by elements

\psi^{i}_{a}(x),\bar{\psi}^{i}_{a}(x)
where
x\in{\mathbb{T}}_{M}
, the spinor index is
a=1,2
, and
i=1,\dots,n
is the internal index.
The algebra is constructed in
[
6
]
and consists of formal expressions of the form

\begin{split}&F(\psi)=F(\psi,\bar{\psi})\\
=&\sum_{n,m}\frac{1}{n!m!}\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{n}_{M}\times{\mathbb{T}}^{m}_{M}}F_{nm}(x_{1},\dots,x_{n},y_{1},\dots y_{n})\psi(x_{1})\cdots\psi(x_{n})\bar{\psi}(y_{1})\cdots\bar{\psi}(y_{n})dxdy\\
\end{split}

(1)

We usually have
n=m
.
Here the kernels
F_{nm}
are elements of a Banach space of distributions
{\cal C}^{\prime}
which is dual to a Banach space
{\cal C}
of differentiable functions. We take

{\cal C}={\cal C}_{3}({\mathbb{T}}^{n+m})
to be functions which are three times continuously differentiable in each variable, supplied with a sup norm.
The
F_{nm}
are anti-symmetric in the
x_{i}
and
y_{j}
separately.

A norm with positive parameters
h,\bar{h}
is defined by

\|F\|_{h,\bar{h}}=\sum_{n,m}\frac{h^{n}\bar{h}^{m}}{n!m!}\|F_{nm}\|_{{\cal C}^{\prime}}

(2)

where
\|F_{nm}\|_{{\cal C}^{\prime}}=\sup_{f\in{\cal C},\|f\|\leq 1}|<F_{nm},f>|
.
We usually have
h=\bar{h}
in which case the norm is denoted
\|F\|_{h}
.
We define
{\cal G}_{h}
to be the Banach space of all such objects with
\|F\|_{h}<\infty
.
Multiplication is defined by anti-symmetrizing the kernels, and
the norm satisfies
\|FH\|_{h}\leq\|F\|_{h}\|H\|_{h}
,
so
{\cal G}_{h}
is a Banach algebra.

A Gaussian integral with covariance
G(x,y)
is defined on monomials by

\int\ \psi(x_{1})\cdots\psi(x_{n})\bar{\psi}(y_{1})\cdots\bar{\psi}(y_{m})\ d\mu_{G}(\psi)=\epsilon_{n}\det\Big\{G(x_{i},y_{j})\Big\}\delta_{nm}

(3)

with
\epsilon_{n}=\pm 1
. The choice of sign is fixed by specifying

\int\ \psi(x_{1})\bar{\psi}(y_{1})\cdots\psi(x_{n})\bar{\psi}(y_{n})\ d\mu_{G}(\psi)=\det\Big\{G(x_{i},y_{j})\Big\}

(4)

We consider particularly covariances which can be written in the form

G(x,y)=\int G_{1}(x,z)G_{2}(z,y)dz
with

h(G)\equiv\sup_{|\alpha|\leq 3}\Big\{\|\partial^{\alpha}G_{1}\|_{2},\|\partial^{\alpha}G_{2}\|_{2}\Big\}<\infty

(5)

Then the determinants can be estimated by Gram’s inequality, and one can show that the integral extends to a linear functional
F\to\int Fd\mu_{G}
on
{\cal G}_{h(G)}

which satisfies

|\int Fd\mu_{G}|\leq\|F\|_{h(G)}

(6)

If
h(G)<h
then
{\cal G}_{h}\subset{\cal G}_{h(G)}
and the integral is defined on
{\cal G}_{h}
with
|\int Fd\mu_{G}|\leq\|F\|_{h}
.

The Euclidean Dirac operator is
\not{\partial}=\sum_{\mu}\gamma_{\mu}\partial_{\nu}=\gamma_{0}\partial_{0}+\gamma_{1}\partial_{1}

where the
\gamma_{\mu}
are self-adjoint Dirac matrices satisfying
\{\gamma_{\mu},\gamma_{\nu}\}=\delta_{\mu\nu}
.
We define
G
to be the inverse Dirac operator with a momentum cutoff at approximately
|p|=L^{N}
.
It has a kernel defined by the Fourier series

G(x-y)=L^{-2M}\sum_{p\in{\mathbb{T}}_{M}^{*},p\neq 0}e^{ip(x-y)}\frac{-i\not{p}}{p^{2}}e^{-p^{2}/L^{2N}}

(7)

where
{\mathbb{T}}_{M}^{*}=2\pi L^{-M}{\mathbb{Z}}^{2}
.

The partition function for the Gross-Neveu model is a Gaussian integral with covariance
G
on the Grassmann algebra
{\cal G}_{h}
of the form

\mathsf{Z}=\int e^{S(\psi)}d\mu_{G}(\psi)\hskip 28.45274pt\hskip 28.45274ptS(\psi)=\int(\varepsilon-z\bar{\psi}\not{\partial}\psi+g(\bar{\psi}\psi)^{2})dx

(8)

Here
(\bar{\psi}\psi)(x)=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\sum_{a=1,2}\bar{\psi}^{i}_{a}(x)\psi^{i}_{a}(x)
with
n\geq 2
. The parameters in the action
S(\psi)
are the coupling constant
g
, incremental field strength
z
,
and energy density
\varepsilon
.

Our goal is to get non-perturbative bounds uniform in the ultraviolet cutoff
N
as
N\to\infty
. In an earlier paper this was accomplished for the partition function.
Now we are interested in accomplishing the same for correlation functions

\begin{split}&\Big\langle\psi(x_{1})\cdots\psi(x_{n})\bar{\psi}(y_{1})\cdots\bar{\psi}(y_{n})\Big\rangle\\
\equiv&\mathsf{Z}^{-1}\int\psi(x_{1})\cdots\psi(x_{n})\bar{\psi}(y_{1})\cdots\bar{\psi}(y_{n})e^{S(\psi)}d\mu_{G}(\psi)\\
\end{split}

(9)

It is convenient to scale up to the torus
{\mathbb{T}}_{M+N}
with a unit momentum cutoff.
This is accomplished by introducing
\psi
on
{\mathbb{T}}_{M+N}
, and replacing the former
\psi
on
{\mathbb{T}}_{M}
by
\psi_{L^{-N}}
where

\psi_{L^{-N}}(x)=L^{N/2}\psi(L^{N}x)\hskip 28.45274pt\bar{\psi}_{L^{-N}}(x)=L^{N/2}\bar{\psi}(L^{N}x)

(10)

We find a new covariance

G_{0}(x-y)=L^{-2(M+N)}\sum_{p\in{\mathbb{T}}_{M+N}^{*},p\neq 0}e^{ip(x-y)}\frac{-i\not{p}}{p^{2}}e^{-p^{2}}

(11)

and for the partition function

\mathsf{Z}=\int e^{S_{0}(\psi)}d\mu_{G_{0}}(\psi)\hskip 28.45274pt\hskip 28.45274ptS_{0}(\psi)=\int(\varepsilon_{0}-z_{0}\bar{\psi}\not{\partial}\psi+g_{0}(\bar{\psi}\psi)^{2})dx

(12)

where the new action has parameters
\varepsilon_{0}=L^{-3N}\varepsilon,z_{0}=z,g_{0}=g
.
The new correlation functions are

L^{Nn}\ \mathsf{Z}^{-1}\int\psi(L^{N}x_{1})\cdots\psi(L^{N}x_{n})\bar{\psi}(L^{N}y_{1})\cdots\bar{\psi}(L^{N}y_{n})e^{S_{0}(\psi)}d\mu_{G_{0}}(\psi)

(13)

We make a series of RG transformations. In each step we integrate out the highest momentum modes, approximately those in
L^{-1}\leq|p|\leq 1
.
Then we scale the volume down by
L^{-1}
and momentum up by
L
restoring the unit momentum cutoff.
We review how this plays out for the partition function, leaving correlation functions to section
4
.

After
k
steps one has fields
\psi
on
{\mathbb{T}}_{M+N-k}
and the expression

\mathsf{Z}=\int e^{S_{k}(\psi)}d\mu_{G_{k}}(\psi)

(14)

The covariance is now given by

G_{k}(x-y)={\sum}^{\prime}_{p\in{\mathbb{T}}^{*}_{N+M-k}}e^{ip(x-y)}\frac{-i\not{p}}{p^{2}}e^{-p^{2}}

(15)

Here
{\sum}^{\prime}
means the sum is weighted by
L^{-2(M+N-k)}
and
p=0
is excluded.
The effective action
S_{k}
is specified below.

Given the representation on
{\mathbb{T}}_{M+N-k}
we generate the representation on
{\mathbb{T}}_{M+N-k-1}
as follows.
Split off the highest momentum piece of the covariance by

G_{k}=G_{k+1,L}+C_{k}

(16)

where
G_{k+1,L}(x,y)=L^{-1}G_{k+1}(L^{-1}x,L^{-1}y)
. Then

\begin{split}G_{k+1,L}(x-y)=&{\sum}^{\prime}_{p\in{\mathbb{T}}_{M+N-k}^{*}}e^{ip(x-y)}\frac{-i\not{p}}{p^{2}}e^{-L^{2}p^{2}}\\
C_{k}(x-y)=&{\sum}^{\prime}_{p\in{\mathbb{T}}_{M+N-k}^{*}}e^{ip(x-y)}\frac{-i\not{p}}{p^{2}}\Big(e^{-p^{2}}-e^{-L^{2}p^{2}}\Big)\\
\end{split}

(17)

The integral splits as

\mathsf{Z}=\int\Big[\int e^{S_{k}(\psi+\eta)}d\mu_{C_{k}}(\eta)\Big]d\mu_{G_{k+1},L}(\psi)

(18)

The expression in brackets is the fluctuation integral, and a basic problem is to construct a new action
\tilde{S}_{k+1}

such that

e^{\tilde{S}_{k+1}(\psi)}=\int e^{S_{k}(\psi+\eta)}d\mu_{C_{k}}(\eta)

(19)

We scale this by taking
\psi
on
{\mathbb{T}}_{M+N-k-1}
, and replacing
\psi(x)
on
{\mathbb{T}}_{M+N-k}
by
\psi_{L}(x)=L^{-\frac{1}{2}}\psi(L^{-1}x)
.
Then
G_{k+1,L}
scales to
G_{k+1}
and
\tilde{S}_{k+1}
scales to

S_{k+1}(\psi)=\tilde{S}_{k+1}(\psi_{L})
, and we have

\mathsf{Z}=\int e^{S_{k+1}(\psi)}d\mu_{G_{k+1}}(\psi)

(20)

The effective action after
k
steps has the form

\begin{split}S_{k}(\psi)=&\int\Big(\varepsilon_{k}-z_{k}\bar{\psi}\not{\partial}\psi+g_{k}(\bar{\psi}\psi)^{2}\Big)+Q_{k}^{\textrm{reg}}(\psi)+E_{k}(\psi)\\
&+p_{k}\int(\bar{\psi}\gamma_{5}\psi)^{2}+v_{k}\int\sum_{\mu}(\bar{\psi}\gamma_{\mu}\psi)^{2}+{Q_{k}^{\textrm{reg}}}^{\prime}(\psi)\\
\end{split}

(21)

This is established in
[
6
]
, see particularly section 3.1.
The various terrms are defined as follows.
The main term is
g_{k}\int(\bar{\psi}\psi)^{2}\equiv V_{k}(\psi)
.
It is again the quartic interaction, now with a new coupling constant
g_{k}
. Also new are
\varepsilon_{k},z_{k}={\cal O}(g_{k})
.

The
Q_{k}
are
{\cal O}(g_{k}^{2})
terms accumulated after
k
steps. They have the form

Q_{k}(\psi)=\frac{1}{2}\int V^{2}_{k}(\psi+\eta)d\mu_{w_{k}}(\eta)-\frac{1}{2}\Big(\int V_{k}(\psi+\eta)d\mu_{w_{k}}(\eta)\Big)^{2}

(22)

Here
w_{k}
is defined inductively by
w_{k+1}=(w_{k}+C_{k})_{L^{-1}}

and is given explicitly by

w_{k}(x-y)=\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}C_{j,L^{-(k-j}}(x-y)={\sum}^{\prime}_{p}e^{ip(x-y)}\frac{-i\not{p}}{p^{2}}\Big(e^{-p^{2}/L^{2k}}-e^{-p^{2}}\Big)

(23)

The function
w_{k}(x-y)
develops short distance singularities
{\cal O}(|x-y|)^{-1}
as
k
increases, and this infects
Q_{k}
rendering it singular.
The
Q_{k}^{\textrm{reg}}
is
Q_{k}
with relevant pieces removed leaving a non singular piece. The relevant pieces
are corrections to the coupling constant, field strength, and energy density.

The
E_{k}
is an
{\cal O}(g_{k}^{3})
element of the Grassmann algebra of the form

E_{k}(\psi)=\sum_{X}E_{k}(X,\psi)

(24)

where the sum is over paved sets
X\subset{\mathbb{T}}_{M+N-k}
. A paved set is a union of unit blocks (squares), not necessarily connected.
The
E_{k}(X,\psi)
only depend on
\psi
in
X
, so this expansion localizes the dependence on
\psi
.
We employ a norm on functions
X\to E(X)
from paved sets to
Grassmann elements of the form

\|E\|_{h,\Gamma}=\sup_{\square}\sum_{X\supset\square}\|E(X)\|_{h}\Gamma(X)

(25)

where
\square
is a unit block. For translation invariant functions like
E_{k}(X)
the sum is independent of
\square
and the supremum can be omitted.
The weight function

\Gamma(X)
grows in the size of the paved set
X
, so
\|E\|_{h,\Gamma}<\infty
enforces that
E_{k}(X)
decays in the size of
X
. We
give the exact definition of
\Gamma(X)
in Appendix
A
. We also use
\Gamma_{n}(X)=e^{n|X|}\Gamma(X)
where
|X|
is the number of unit blocks in
X
.

The last three terms in (
21
) are rogue pseudoscalar and vector terms which do not appear in the original action.
Here
p_{k},v_{k}={\cal O}(g_{k}^{2})
and
{Q_{k}^{\textrm{reg}}}^{\prime}={\cal O}(g_{k}^{3})
is generated from various quartic terms as above.
These terms may actually be zero, but cannot be ruled out at present. (Gawedzki and Kupiainen
[
12
]
have an argument that such terms do
not occur in a fully developed perturbation theory. But this does not settle the question in the present context.)

With this form for
S_{k}
an RG transformation generates an action
S_{k+1}
of the same form but with new parameters. More precisely we have:

Theorem 1
.

[
6
]

Let
L,h,C_{Z},C_{E}
be sufficiently large and chosen in that order, and let
g_{\max}
be sufficiently small depending on these constants.
Suppose
S_{k}
has the form
(
21
) with

0<g_{k}<g_{\max}\hskip 28.45274pt\|E_{k}\|_{h,\Gamma_{4}}\leq C_{E}g_{k}^{3}\hskip 28.45274pt|z_{k}|\leq C_{Z}g_{k}\hskip 28.45274pt|p_{k}|,|v_{k}|\leq C_{E}g_{k}^{2}

(26)

Then
S_{k+1}
has the same form with new parameters

\begin{split}E_{k+1}=&{\cal L}({\cal R}E_{k}+E_{k}^{*})\\
g_{k+1}=&g_{k}+\beta_{k}g^{2}_{k}-2\beta^{\prime}_{k}g_{k}p_{k}-4\beta^{\prime}_{k}g_{k}v_{k}+g^{*}_{k}\\
z_{k+1}=&z_{k}+\theta_{k}g_{k}^{2}-\theta^{p}_{k}g_{k}p_{k}-\theta^{v}_{k}g_{k}v_{k}+z^{*}_{k}\\
p_{k+1}=&p_{k}-2\beta_{k}^{\prime}g_{k}v_{k}+p_{k}^{*}\\
v_{k+1}=&v_{k}-\beta^{\prime}_{k}g_{k}p_{k}+v_{k}^{*}\\
\varepsilon_{k+1}=&L^{2}(\varepsilon_{k}+\varepsilon_{k}^{*})\\
\end{split}

(27)

The coefficient
\beta_{k}>0
is bounded above and below which ensures ultraviolet asymptotic freedom. The other coefficients
\beta^{\prime}_{k},\theta_{k},\theta^{p}_{k},\theta^{v}_{k}
are bounded.
The starred quantities here are all
{\cal O}(g_{k}^{3})
and have specific estimates.
{\cal R}E_{k}
is
E_{k}
with relevant parts removed. The relevant parts show up
in the starred quantities.
{\cal L}
is a reblocking and scaling operation on functions
E(X,\psi)
.

The new parameters
g_{k+1},z_{k+1},\dots
do not necessarily satisfy the bounds (
26
) for
k+1
, but we do have:

Theorem 2
.

[
6
]
Assume the hypotheses of theorem
1
and suppose
g_{f}<\frac{1}{2}g_{\max}
. Then the flow equations (
27
)
have a unique solution
\{g_{k},z_{k},E_{k},p_{k},v_{k},\varepsilon_{k}\}_{0\leq k\leq N}
satisfying the bounds (
26
) for all
k

with boundary conditions

g_{N}=g_{f},\ \varepsilon_{N}=0\ \ \textrm{ and }\ \ E_{0}=0,\ z_{0}=0,\ p_{0}=0,\ v_{0}=0

(28)

This is renormalization, and we assume hereafter that we are talking about this solution.
After
N
steps we are back on the original torus
{\mathbb{T}}_{M}
and
the partition function is given by

\mathsf{Z}=\int e^{S_{N}(\psi)}d\mu_{G_{N}}(\psi)

(29)

The UV singularities have disappeared from this expression and in
[
6
]
it is proved that this is bounded uniformly in

N
.

3
Properties of the Grassmann algebra

We digress to develop some further properties of our Grassmann algebra. Most of these are well-known in the finite dimensional case,
but need fresh proofs in our infinite dimensional setting. A good general reference is
[
8
]
, which however does not exactly cover the case at hand.

3.1
derivatives

Again an element of the Grassmann algebra
{\cal G}_{h}
is
written as

\begin{split}F=&\sum_{n,m}\frac{1}{n!m!}\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{n+m}}F_{nm}(x_{1},\dots,x_{n},y_{1},\dots y_{m})\psi(x_{1})\cdots\psi(x_{n})\bar{\psi}(y_{1})\cdots\bar{\psi}(y_{m})dxdy\\
\end{split}

(30)

We generalize a bit. For this section
{\mathbb{T}}={\mathbb{T}}_{M}={\mathbb{R}}^{d}/L^{M}{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}
is any
d
-dimensional torus, and the kernels
F_{nm}
are elements of
{\cal C}^{\prime}={\cal C}_{k}^{\prime}({\mathbb{T}}^{n+m})
now the dual space of
the Banach space
{\cal C}={\cal C}_{k}({\mathbb{T}}^{n+m})
, not just
k=3
. Here
{\cal C}_{k}({\mathbb{T}}^{n})

is defined to be all functions on
{\mathbb{T}}^{n}
which are
k
-times continuously differentiable functions in each of the
n
variables. The norm is

\|f\|_{{\cal C}}=\sup_{|\alpha|\leq k}\|\partial^{\alpha}f\|_{\infty}

(31)

Here
\partial^{\alpha}=\partial^{\alpha_{1}}_{x_{1}}\cdots\partial^{\alpha_{n}}_{x_{n}}
with
\partial^{\alpha_{i}}_{x_{i}}=\prod_{\mu=1}^{d}\partial^{\alpha_{i,\mu}}_{x_{i,\mu}}
and
|\alpha|=\sup_{i}\sum_{\mu}|\alpha_{i,\mu}|
. Compare the more common
{\cal C}^{k}({\mathbb{T}}^{n})
which would have
|\alpha|=\sum_{i,\mu}|\alpha_{i,\mu}|
. For
n=1
we have
{\cal C}_{k}({\mathbb{T}})={\cal C}^{k}({\mathbb{T}})
.

The norm
\|F\|_{h,\bar{h}}
or
\|F\|_{h}=\|F\|_{h,h}
is still defined by (
2
).

The (formal) derivative of
F
with respect to
\psi(x)
is defined by
\partial\psi(x^{\prime})/\partial\psi(x)=\delta(x-x^{\prime})
and
\partial\bar{\psi}(x^{\prime})/\partial\psi(x)=0
and it anticommutes
with fields when distributed across a product. So the definition is

\begin{split}\frac{\partial F}{\partial\psi(x)}=&\sum_{n,m}\frac{1}{n!m!}\sum_{j=1}^{n}(-1)^{j+1}\int F_{nm}(x_{1},\dots x_{n},y_{1},\dots y_{m})|_{x_{j}=x}\\
&\psi(x_{1})\cdots\widehat{\psi(x_{j})}\cdots\psi(x_{n})\bar{\psi}(y_{1})\cdots\bar{\psi}(y_{m})d\hat{x}dy\\
\end{split}

(32)

where
d\hat{x}=dx_{1}\cdots\widehat{dx_{j}}\cdots dx_{n+1}
, and the hat indicates omission.
This is meant to define a
{\cal C}^{\prime}_{k}({\mathbb{T}})
distribution with values in the algebra. The precise meaning is that for
f\in{\cal C}_{k}({\mathbb{T}})={\cal C}^{k}({\mathbb{T}})

\begin{split}&\int f(x)\frac{\partial F}{\partial\psi(x)}dx=\sum_{n,m}\frac{1}{n!m!}\sum_{j=1}^{n}(-1)^{j+1}\int F_{nm}(x_{1},\dots x_{n},y_{1},\dots y_{m})|_{x_{j}=x}\\
&\hskip 28.45274pt\hskip 28.45274pt\hskip 28.45274pt\psi(x_{1})\cdots f(x)\cdots\psi(x_{n})\bar{\psi}(y_{1})\cdots\bar{\psi}(y_{m})dxdy\\
\end{split}

(33)

where the
f(x)
appears in the
j^{th}
entry. The norm of the derivative is defined as the supremum over
\|f\|_{{\cal C}^{k}}\leq 1
of the norm of this expression.
Derivatives
\partial/\partial\bar{\psi}(x)
are defined similarly.

Proposition 1
.

If
F\in{\cal G}_{h}
then
\partial F/\partial\psi
and
\partial F/\partial\bar{\psi}
define
{\cal G}_{h^{\prime}}
valued distributions for any
h^{\prime}<h
and

\|\frac{\partial F}{\partial\psi}\|_{h^{\prime}}\leq\frac{1}{h-h^{\prime}}\|F\|_{h}\hskip 28.45274pt\|\frac{\partial F}{\partial\bar{\psi}}\|_{h^{\prime}}\leq\frac{1}{h-h^{\prime}}\|F\|_{h}

(34)

Proof
. First suppose there are no
\bar{\psi}
fields, that is
m=0
.
We have

\|\frac{\partial F}{\partial\psi}\|_{h^{\prime}}\equiv\sup_{\|f\|_{{\cal C}^{k}}\leq 1}\|\int f(x)\frac{\partial F}{\partial\psi(x)}dx\|_{h^{\prime}}

(35)

where

\begin{split}&\int f(x)\frac{\partial F}{\partial\psi(x)}dx=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{n!}\sum_{j=1}^{n}(-1)^{j+1}\int F_{n}(x_{1},\dots x_{n})|_{x_{j}=x}\psi(x_{1})\cdots f(x)\cdots\psi(x_{n})dx\\
\end{split}

(36)

In
F_{n}
move the
x
to the left at a cost
(-1)^{j+1}
and then relabel
(x_{1},\dots,x_{j-1},x_{j+1}\dots x_{n})
as
(y_{1},\dots y_{n-1})
. Then

\begin{split}&\int f(x)\frac{\partial F}{\partial\psi(x)}dx=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{(n-1)!}\int F_{n}(x,y_{1},\dots y_{n-1})f(x)\psi(y_{1})\cdots\psi(y_{n-1})dy\\
\end{split}

(37)

This says

\Big(\int f(x)\frac{\partial F}{\partial\psi(x)}dx\Big)_{n-1}(y_{1},\dots y_{n-1})=\int F_{n}(x,y_{1},\dots y_{n-1})f(x)dx

(38)

With
\|f\|_{{\cal C}^{k}}\leq 1
this implies

\|\Big(\ \frac{\partial F}{\partial\psi}\Big)_{n-1}\|_{{\cal C}^{\prime}}\leq\|F_{n}\|_{{\cal C}^{\prime}}

(39)

Then

\|\frac{\partial F}{\partial\psi}\|_{h^{\prime}}\leq\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{{h^{\prime}}^{n-1}}{(n-1)!}\|F_{n}\|_{{\cal C}^{\prime}}=\frac{d}{d{h^{\prime}}}\|F\|_{h^{\prime}}

(40)

But since
F\in{\cal G}_{h}
the function
h^{\prime}\to\|F\|_{h^{\prime}}
extends to a complex analytic function for
|h^{\prime}|<h
. Then by a Cauchy inequality

\frac{d}{d{h^{\prime}}}\|F\|_{h^{\prime}}\leq\frac{1}{h-h^{\prime}}\|F\|_{h}

(41)

which gives the result in this case.

In the general case with both
\psi,\bar{\psi}
the same argument would give

\|\frac{\partial F}{\partial\psi}\|_{h^{\prime},\bar{h}^{\prime}}\leq\frac{\partial}{\partial h^{\prime}}\|F\|_{h^{\prime},\bar{h}^{\prime}}\leq\frac{1}{h-h^{\prime}}\|F\|_{h,\bar{h}^{\prime}}\leq\frac{1}{h-h^{\prime}}\|F\|_{h,\bar{h}}

(42)

Then we would specialize to
\bar{h}^{\prime}=h^{\prime}
and
\bar{h}=h
to complete the proof for
\psi
derivatives. Derivatives in
\bar{\psi}
are
treated similarly.
\blacksquare

For the next result we define

Proposition 2
.

1.

If

and
F
is even/odd then

\frac{\partial}{\partial\psi(x)}FH=\Big(\frac{\partial F}{\partial\psi(x)}\Big)H\pm F\Big(\frac{\partial H}{\partial\psi(x)}\Big)

(43)

2.

If
F\in{\cal G}_{h}
is even

\frac{\partial F^{n}}{\partial\psi(x)}=nF^{n-1}\frac{\partial F}{\partial\psi(x)}

(44)

3.

Suppose
h^{\prime}<h
so
{\cal G}_{h}\subset{\cal G}_{h^{\prime}}
. Let
F=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}a_{n}A^{n}
be an absolutely convergent power series in an even element of
A\in{\cal G}_{h}
.
Then
\partial F/\partial\psi(x)
in
{\cal G}_{h^{\prime}}
is

\frac{\partial F}{\partial\psi(x)}=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}a_{n}nA^{n-1}\frac{\partial A}{\partial\psi(x)}

(45)

Proof
. The first follows from the definition of the derivative. The second follows by induction on
n
, starting with
n=1
.

Now consider the third statement. If
A
is constant it is trivial. Otherwise we have
\|A\|_{h^{\prime}}<\|A\|_{h}
.
The series (

) converges in
{\cal G}_{h^{\prime}}
since by Proposition
1

\|a_{n}nA^{n-1}\frac{\partial A}{\partial\psi}\|_{h^{\prime}}\leq\frac{1}{h-h^{\prime}}|a_{n}|n\|A\|^{n-1}_{h^{\prime}}\|A\|_{h}=\frac{1}{h-h^{\prime}}|a_{n}|\|A\|^{n}_{h}\left[n\left(\frac{\|A\|_{h^{\prime}}}{\|A\|_{h}}\right)^{(n-1)}\right]

(46)

and the bracketed expression is eventually less than one.
To establish the identity take the partial sum

\frac{\partial F_{N}}{\partial\psi(x)}=\sum_{n=1}^{N}a_{n}nA^{n-1}\frac{\partial A}{\partial\psi(x)}

As

\partial F/\partial\psi(x)

\|\frac{\partial F}{\partial\psi}-\frac{\partial F_{N}}{\partial\psi}\|_{h^{\prime}}=\|\frac{\partial}{\partial\psi}(F-F_{N})\|_{h^{\prime}}\leq\frac{1}{h-h^{\prime}}\|F-F_{N}\|_{h}\to 0

. Here are some examples of interest, always assuming

3.2

An integral on our Banach algebra
{\cal G}_{h}
is a continuous linear functional.
Integrals are written
F\to\int Fd\mu
(although there is no measure).
Let
\EuFrak{J}(x,y)
be a smooth function on
{\mathbb{T}}\times{\mathbb{T}}

<\bar{\psi},\EuFrak{J}\psi>=\int\bar{\psi}(x)\EuFrak{J}(x,y)\psi(y)dxdy

(53)

Then
\|<\bar{\psi},\EuFrak{J}\psi>\|_{h}\leq h^{2}\|\EuFrak{J}\|_{{\cal C}^{\prime}}<\infty
so this is an element of
{\cal G}_{h}
for any

. The same is true
for
\exp(\pm<\bar{\psi},\EuFrak{J}\psi>)
with norm bounded by
\exp(\|<\bar{\psi},\EuFrak{J}\psi>\|_{h})
. So for any integral on any
{\cal G}_{h}
we can define

\int e^{-<\bar{\psi},\EuFrak{J}\psi>}d\mu(\psi)

(54)

Let
G

{\mathbb{T}}\times{\mathbb{T}}
(perhaps taking advantage of an ultraviolet cutoff), and suppose we have an assignment of
h(G)
as in (
5
). Then the Gaussian integral
\int Fd\mu_{G}

is defined for
F\in{\cal G}_{h(G)}
and it is shown in
[
6
]
that

\int e^{-<\bar{\psi},\EuFrak{J}\psi>}d\mu_{G}(\psi)=\det(I+\EuFrak{J}G)

(55)

On the right

is the operator
(Gf)(x)=\int G(x,y)f(y)dy
.
The determinant is the Fredholm determinant which exists since
\EuFrak{J}G
is trace class on
L^{2}({\mathbb{T}})
. If

then
{\cal G}_{h}\subset{\cal G}_{h(G)}
and the integral

Conversely, in the same circumstance, it is shown in
[
6
]

\int Fd\mu

{\cal G}_{h}
satisfying

\int e^{-<\bar{\psi},\EuFrak{J}\psi>}d\mu(\psi)=\det(I+\EuFrak{J}G)

then it is the Gaussian integral with covariance
G
. Thus this integral is in a certain sense a characteristic function of
the Gaussian integral.

Assume
F\to\int Fd\mu_{G}
is a Gaussian integral on
{\cal G}_{h}
as above. Then we have:

Proposition 3
.

Let
K(x,y)
be a smooth function on
{\mathbb{T}}\times{\mathbb{T}}
and
K
the associated bounded operator on
L^{2}({\mathbb{T}})
.
Suppose
\|KG\|<1
so
(I-KG)^{-1}
exists. Then

\int Fd\mu_{G,K}(\psi)\equiv\frac{\int Fe^{<\bar{\psi},K\psi>}d\mu_{G}(\psi)}{\int e^{<\bar{\psi},K\psi>}d\mu_{G}(\psi)}

(57)

is the Gaussian integral on
{\cal G}_{h}
with covariance
G(I-KG)^{-1}
.

Proof
. The denominator is
\det(I-KG)
and it is not zero under the assumption
\|KG\|<1
(Theorem 3.5 in
[
13
]
).
The numerator is defined on
{\cal G}_{h}
since

. So it suffices to check
that the characteristic function is
\det(I+\EuFrak{J}G(I-KG)^{-1})
.
We have

\begin{split}\int e^{-<\bar{\psi},\EuFrak{J}\psi>}d\mu_{G,K}(\psi)=&\frac{\int e^{-<\bar{\psi},(\EuFrak{J}-K)\psi>}d\mu_{G}(\psi)}{\int e^{<\bar{\psi},K\psi>}d\mu_{G}(\psi)}\\
=&\frac{\det(I+(\EuFrak{J}-K)G)}{\det(I-KG)}\\
=&\det\Big(I+\EuFrak{J}G(I-KG)^{-1}\Big)\\
\end{split}

(58)

3.3

Next we include some auxiliary Grassmann fields
J(x)=\{J_{a}^{i}(x)\}
and
\bar{J}(x)=\{\bar{J}_{a}^{i}(x)\}

\begin{split}&F(\psi,J)\\
&=\sum_{n,m,k,\ell}\frac{1}{n!m!k!\ell!}\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{n}\times{\mathbb{T}}^{m}\times{\mathbb{T}}^{k}\times{\mathbb{T}}^{\ell}}F_{nmk\ell}(x,y,z,w)\psi(x)\bar{\psi}(y)J(z)\bar{J}(w)dxdydzdw\\
\end{split}

(59)

but usually we have
n=m
and
k=\ell
.
Here if

\psi(x)=\psi(x_{1})\cdots\psi(x_{n})

The kernels

\|F\|_{h,h^{\prime}}=\sum_{k,\ell,n,m}\frac{h^{n+m}h^{\prime k+\ell}}{n!m!k!\ell!}\|F_{nmk\ell}\|_{{\cal C}^{\prime}}

(60)

We define
{\cal G}_{h,h^{\prime}}
as the Banach algebra of all such objects with

A Gaussian integral over
\psi
yields an object in the algebra generated by

only and is defined as in (
3

\begin{split}&\int F(\psi,J)d\mu_{G}(\psi)\\
=&\sum_{n,m,k,\ell}\frac{1}{n!m!k!\ell!}\int F_{nmk\ell}(x,y,z,w)\Big[\int\psi(x)\bar{\psi}(y)d\mu_{G}(\psi)\Big]J(z)\bar{J}(w)dxdydzdw\\
=&\sum_{n,m,k,\ell}\frac{\delta_{nm}\epsilon_{n}}{n!m!k!\ell!}\int F_{nmk\ell}(x,y,z,w)\det\{G(x_{i},y_{j})\}J(z)\bar{J}(w)dxdydzdw\\
\end{split}

(61)

For the rest of this section we continue to assume
G
on
{\mathbb{T}}\times{\mathbb{T}}
is a smooth function with an assignment

h(G)
as in (
5
).

Proposition 4
.

If
F\in{\cal G}_{h(G),h^{\prime}}
then

\int F(\psi,J)d\mu_{G}(\psi)
exists and

\|\int F(\psi,J)d\mu_{G}(\psi)\|_{h^{\prime}}\leq\|F\|_{h(G),h^{\prime}}

Proof
. The integral has the kernel

F_{k\ell}(z,w)=\sum_{n,m}\frac{\delta_{nm}\epsilon_{n}}{n!m!}\int F_{nmk\ell}(x,y,z,w)\det\{G(x_{i},y_{j})\}dxdy

(63)

and the norm

\|\int F(\psi,J)d\mu_{G}(\psi)\|_{h^{\prime}}=\sum_{k,\ell}\frac{h^{\prime k+\ell}}{k!\ell!}\|F_{k\ell}\|_{{\cal C}^{\prime}}

By Grams inequality as in Lemma 7 in
[
6
]
:

\|\det\{G(x_{i},y_{j})\}\|_{{\cal C}}\leq h(G)^{n+m}
.
Hence

\begin{split}\|F_{k\ell}\|_{{\cal C}^{\prime}}&=\sup_{\|f\|_{{\cal C}}\leq 1}\Big|\Big\langle F_{k\ell},f\Big\rangle\Big|\\
&\leq\sup_{\|f\|_{{\cal C}}\leq 1}\sum_{n=m}\frac{1}{n!m!}\Big|\int F_{nmk\ell}(x,y,z,w)\det\{G(x_{i},y_{j})\}f(z,w)dxdy\Big|\\
&\leq\sum_{n=m}\frac{h(G)^{n+m}}{n!m!}\|F_{nmk\ell}\|_{{\cal C}^{\prime}}\\
\end{split}

(65)

Put this in (

) and get the result.
\blacksquare

Next note that

Proposition 5
.

\int e^{<\bar{J},\psi>+<\bar{\psi},J>}d\mu_{G}(\psi)=e^{<\bar{J},GJ>}

Proof
. We have

\begin{split}&e^{<\bar{J},\psi>+<\bar{\psi},J>}=\sum_{n,m}\frac{1}{n!m!}<\bar{J},\psi>^{n}<\bar{\psi},J>^{m}\\
&=\sum_{n,m}\frac{1}{n!m!}\int\psi(x_{1})\cdots\psi(x_{n})\bar{\psi}(y_{1})\cdots\bar{\psi}(y_{m})\bar{J}(x_{1})\cdots\bar{J}(x_{n})J(y_{1})\cdots J(y_{m})dxdy\\
\end{split}

(68)

The integral selects
n=m
and is

\begin{split}&\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{(n!)^{2}}\ \epsilon_{n}\int\bar{J}(x_{1})\cdots\bar{J}(x_{n})\det\{G(x_{i},y_{j})\}J(y_{1})\cdots J(y_{n})dxdy\\
\end{split}

If
\pi

and then

\begin{split}&\int\bar{J}(x_{1})\cdots\bar{J}(x_{n})\det\{G(x_{i},y_{j})\}J(y_{1})\cdots J(y_{n})dxdy\\

=&n!\epsilon_{n}<\bar{J},GJ>^{n}\\
\end{split}

(70)

Substitute this in (

We now assume the operator

We use the notation

(Gf)_{a}(x)=\sum_{b}\int G_{ab}(x-x^{\prime})f_{b}(x^{\prime})dx^{\prime}\hskip 28.45274pt(fG)_{b}(y)=\sum_{a}\int f_{a}(y^{\prime})G_{ab}(y^{\prime}-y)dy^{\prime}

with the internal index still suppressed. A norm on the function

\|G\|=\max\left\{\sup_{a}\sum_{b}\int|G_{ab}(x)|dx,\ \ \sup_{b}\sum_{a}\int|G_{ab}(x)|dx\right\}

\|Gf\|_{\infty}\leq\|G\|\|f\|_{\infty}

\|Gf\|_{{\cal C}^{k}}\leq\|G\|\|f\|_{{\cal C}^{k}}\hskip 28.45274pt\|fG\|_{{\cal C}^{k}}\leq\|G\|\|f\|_{{\cal C}^{k}}

Proposition 6
.

\|F(GJ,\bar{J}G)\|_{h}\leq\|F\|_{\|G\|h}

(74)

\|F(\psi+GJ,\bar{\psi}+\bar{J}G)\|_{h,h^{\prime}}\leq\|F\|_{h+\|G\|h^{\prime}}

(75)

Proof
.
F^{\prime}(J,\bar{J})=F(GJ,\bar{J}G)

\begin{split}&F^{\prime}_{nm}(x^{\prime}_{1},\dots,x^{\prime}_{n},y^{\prime}_{1},\dots,y^{\prime}_{m})\\
=&\int F_{nm}(x_{1},\dots,x_{n},y_{1},\dots,y_{m})\prod_{i=1}^{n}G(x_{i}-x_{i}^{\prime})\prod_{j=1}^{m}G(y^{\prime}_{i}-y_{i})dxdy\\
\end{split}

(77)

where the pairing of spinor indices matches the pairing of spatial variables.
Then for

\begin{split}|<F^{\prime}_{nm},f>|=&\left|\Big\langle F_{nm},(G\otimes\cdots\otimes G)f\Big\rangle\right|\\

\leq&\|F_{nm}\|_{{\cal C}^{\prime}}\|G\|^{n+m}\|f\|_{{\cal C}}\end{split}

(78)

which gives the first estimate (

\|(F^{+})^{\prime}\|_{h,h^{\prime}}\leq\|F^{+}\|_{h,\|G\|h^{\prime}}=\|F\|_{h+\|G\|h^{\prime}}

Proposition 7
.

Let
F\in{\cal G}_{h}
and
h(G)+\|G\|h^{\prime}<h

\int e^{<\bar{J},\psi>+<\bar{\psi},J>}F\Big(\psi,\bar{\psi}\Big)d\mu_{G}(\psi)=e^{<\bar{J},GJ>}\int F\Big(\psi+GJ,\bar{\psi}+\bar{J}G\Big)d\mu_{G}(\psi)

Remarks
.

1.

This is the key identity for subsequent developments. Formally the result follows

\psi\to\psi+GJ

(C.)
We need the fact that for any distribution

On the left side then consider

4.1

Now we return to the two dimensional torus

We study correlation functions following a method used in Dimock,Hurd

[

for scalar fields.

A generating function for correlations is

\Omega(J)=\int e^{<\bar{J},\psi>+<\bar{\psi},J>}e^{S(\psi)}d\mu_{G}(\psi)

This is well-defined in

for any

since the integrand is in
{\cal G}_{h(G),h^{\prime}}
.
But

and

are not bounded in
the ultraviolet cutoff

, so this is not the end of the story.
As an element of

it has a representation

\Omega(J)=\sum_{k,\ell}\frac{1}{k!\ell!}\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{k}_{M}\times{\mathbb{T}}^{\ell}_{M}}\Omega_{k\ell}(z_{1},\dots,z_{k},w_{1},\dots,w_{\ell})J(z_{1})\cdots J(z_{n})\bar{J}(w_{1})\cdots\bar{J}(w_{\ell})dzdw

(97)

with kernels
\Omega_{k\ell}
anti-symmetric in the
z

w

Only terms with

are non-zero.

The normalized 2n-point function is

\Big\langle\psi(x_{1})\cdots\psi(x_{n})\bar{\psi}(y_{1})\cdots\bar{\psi}(y_{n})\Big\rangle=\mathsf{Z}^{-1}\int\psi(x_{1})\cdots\psi(x_{n})\bar{\psi}(y_{1})\cdots\bar{\psi}(y_{n})e^{S(\psi)}d\mu_{G}(\psi)

(98)

where now
\mathsf{Z}=\Omega(0)
is the partition function.
These can be obtained from the generating function using as in (
49
)

\begin{split}\frac{\partial}{\partial\bar{J}(x)}e^{<\bar{J},\psi>}=&e^{<\bar{J},\psi>}\psi(x)\hskip 28.45274pt\frac{\partial}{\partial J(y)}e^{<\bar{\psi},J>}=e^{<\bar{\psi},J>}(-\bar{\psi}(y))\\
\end{split}

(99)

This yields

\begin{split}&\Big\langle\psi(x_{1})\cdots\psi(x_{n})\bar{\psi}(y_{1})\cdots\bar{\psi}(y_{n})\Big\rangle\\
=&\Omega(0)^{-1}\left[\frac{\partial^{2n}\Omega(J)}{\partial\bar{J}(x_{1})\cdots\partial\bar{J}(x_{n})\partial J(y_{1})\cdots\partial J(y_{n})}\right]_{J=0}(-1)^{n}\\
=&\Omega(0)^{-1}\Omega_{nn}(y_{1}\cdots y_{n},x_{1},\cdots x_{n})(-1)^{n}\\
\end{split}

(100)

Now suppose we can find
\Lambda(J)=\Lambda(J,\bar{J})
so that
\Omega(J)=e^{\Lambda(J)}
. Then

\Lambda(J)
generates the truncated
2n
point functions by

\begin{split}\Big\langle\psi(x_{1})\cdots\psi(x_{n})\bar{\psi}(y_{1})\cdots\bar{\psi}(y_{n})\Big\rangle^{T}=&\left[\frac{\partial^{2n}\Lambda(J)}{\partial\bar{J}(x_{1})\cdots\partial\bar{J}(x_{n})\partial J(y_{1})\cdots\partial J(y_{n})}\right]_{J=0}(-1)^{n}\\
=&\Lambda_{nn}(y_{1}\cdots y_{n},x_{1},\cdots x_{n})(-1)^{n}\\
\end{split}

(101)

These can be expressed in terms of the ordinary
2n
-point functions.
Here are some examples.

0=\Big\langle\psi(x)\Big\rangle=e^{-\Lambda(0)}\left[\frac{\partial e^{\Lambda(J)}}{\partial\bar{J}(x)}\right]_{J=0}=\Big\langle\psi(x)\Big\rangle^{T}

(102)

and

\begin{split}\Big\langle\psi(x)\bar{\psi}(y)\Big\rangle=&e^{-\Lambda(0)}\left[\frac{\partial e^{\Lambda(J)}}{\partial\bar{J}(x)\partial J(y)}\right]_{J=0}(-1)\\
=&\left[\frac{\partial^{2}\Lambda(J)}{\partial\bar{J}(x)\partial J(y)}-\frac{\partial\Lambda(J)}{\partial\bar{J}(x)}\frac{\partial\Lambda(J)}{\partial J(y)}\right]_{J=0}(-1)\\
=&\Big\langle\psi(x)\bar{\psi}(y)\Big\rangle^{T}\end{split}

(103)

Similarly

\begin{split}&\Big\langle\psi(x_{1})\psi(x_{2})\bar{\psi}(y_{1})\bar{\psi}(y_{2})\Big\rangle=\Big\langle\psi(x_{1})\psi(x_{2})\bar{\psi}(y_{1})\bar{\psi}(y_{2})\Big\rangle^{T}\\
&-\Big\langle\psi(x_{1})\bar{\psi}(y_{1})\Big\rangle\Big\langle\psi(x_{2})\bar{\psi}(y_{2})\Big\rangle+\Big\langle\psi(x_{1})\bar{\psi}(y_{2})\Big\rangle\Big\langle\psi(x_{2})\bar{\psi}(y_{1})\Big\rangle\end{split}

(104)

gives an expression for
\Big\langle\psi(x_{1})\psi(x_{2})\bar{\psi}(y_{1})\bar{\psi}(y_{2})\Big\rangle^{T}
, and so forth.

2.

The weight function

The weight factor
\Gamma(X)
is the same as that used in
[
1
]
-
[
5
]
. We give the definition in dimension
d
. It is defined by

\Gamma(X)=A^{|X|}\Theta(X)

(154)

Here
|X|
is the number of unit squares in the paved set
X
, so
|X|
is the volume of
X
.
In
A^{|X|}
the
A
is a sufficiently large constant depending on
L
, say
A=L^{d+2}
. Finally

\Theta(X)
has the form

\Theta(X)=\inf_{T}\prod_{\ell\in T}\theta(|\ell|)

(155)

where the infimum is over all tree graphs
T
on the centers of the blocks in
X
. The
\ell
are the lines in the graph
and
|\ell|
is the length in an
\ell^{\infty}
norm. The function
\theta(s)
is defined by
\theta(0)=\theta(1)=1
and for
s=2,3,\dots

\theta(s)=(2L^{d+1})^{n}\hskip 28.45274pt\textrm{ if }\hskip 28.45274ptL^{n}<s\leq L^{n+1}

(156)

Then if
L^{n}<s\leq L^{n+1}

we have
L^{n-1}<s/L\leq L^{n}
and so
L^{n-1}<\{s/L\}\leq L^{n}
where
\{x\}
is the smallest integer greater than or equal to
x
.
Therefore
\theta
has the useful scaling behavior

\theta(\{s/L\})=(2L^{d+1})^{n-1}=(2L^{d+1})^{-1}\theta(s)

(157)

Note also that for
L^{n}<s\leq L^{n+1}

\theta(s)=(2L^{d+1})^{n}=2^{n}(L^{n})^{d+1}\leq 2^{n}s^{d+1}\leq L^{n}s^{d+1}\leq s^{d+2}

(158)

Hence the bound holds for
s=1,2,3\dots
.

We also will use a modification defining for positive integer
n

\Gamma_{n}(X)=e^{n|X|}\Gamma(X)

Appendix B
Smooth approximations to distributions

Let
f
be a smooth function on a torus
{\mathbb{T}}
. Define Fourier coefficients for
p\in{\mathbb{T}}^{*}
by

\tilde{f}(p)=<f,e_{-p}>\hskip 28.45274pt\hskip 28.45274pte_{p}(x)=e^{ipx}

(160)

Then
\tilde{f}(p)
is rapidly decreasing in
p
and
it is a standard fact that the partial sum of the Fourier series

f_{N}(x)\equiv\textrm{Vol}({\mathbb{T}})^{-1}\sum_{|p|\leq N}\tilde{f}(p)e_{p}(x)

(161)

converges uniformly to
f(x)
, also for any number of derivatives.

Now let
F
be a distribution on
{\mathbb{T}}
. Then
F
is continuous on some
{\cal C}^{k}({\mathbb{T}})
, and there are constants
C,k
such that

|<F,f>|\leq C\sup_{|\alpha|\leq k}\|\partial^{\alpha}f\|_{\infty}

(162)

Fourier coefficients defined by
\tilde{F}(p)=<F,e_{-p}>
satisfy

|\tilde{F}(p)|\leq C\sup_{|\alpha|\leq k}\|\partial^{\alpha}e_{p}\|_{\infty}\leq C^{\prime}(1+p^{2})^{k/2}

(163)

Then

<F,f_{N}>=\textrm{Vol}({\mathbb{T}})^{-1}\sum_{|p|\leq N}<F,e_{p}>\tilde{f}(p)=\textrm{Vol}({\mathbb{T}})^{-1}\sum_{|p|\leq N}\tilde{F}(-p)\tilde{f}(p)

(164)

By the uniform convergence of
f_{N}
and its derivatives, and the fact that
\tilde{F}(-p)\tilde{f}(p)
is rapidly decreasing

<F,f>=\lim_{N\to\infty}<F,f_{N}>=\textrm{Vol}({\mathbb{T}})^{-1}\sum_{p}\tilde{F}(-p)\tilde{f}(p)

(165)

Finally
F_{N}
defined by

F_{N}=\textrm{Vol}({\mathbb{T}})^{-1}\sum_{|p|\leq N}\tilde{F}(p)e_{p}

(166)

satisfies
<F_{N},f>=<F,f_{N}>
and
is a smooth function which converges weakly to
F
.

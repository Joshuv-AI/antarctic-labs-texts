The twisted 1-loop invariant and the Jacobian of Ptolemy coordinates
Seokbeom Yoon

Abstract
. We present an alternative definition of the twisted 1-loop invariant in terms of the Jacobian of Ptolemy coordinates. As an application, we prove that the twisted 1-loop invariant is equal to the adjoint twisted Alexander polynomial for all hyperbolic once-punctured torus bundles. This implies that the 1-loop conjecture proposed by Dimofte and Garoufalidis holds for all hyperbolic once-punctured torus bundles.



1.
Introduction

An ideal triangulation of a cusped hyperbolic 3-manifold is an effective tool to describe the hyperbolic structure of the manifold: each tetrahedron is assigned to a complex variable which describes its shape and each edge has an equation to guarantee that the tetrahdra around the edge are well-glued compatibly
[
Thu77
]
.
These equations can be encoded into integer matrices called the gluing equation matrices.
Surprisingly, (a slight modification of) the gluing equation matrices turned out to have the symplectic property
[
NZ85
]
, and this led to some new invariants of 3-manifolds defined in terms of ideal triangulations.
The
1-loop invariant
introduced in
[
DG13
]
is one of such invariants which is also motivated by the generalized Volume conjecture
[
Guk05
]
.
Although it is defined in terms of ideal triangulations, it has topological invariance, namely, it is invariant under 2–3 Pachner moves.
Furthermore, the 1-loop conjecture
[
DG13
, Conj.1.8]
predicts that the 1-loop invariant coincides with the adjoint Reidemeister torsion
[
Por97
]
.
To state the conjecture precisely, let
M
be a 1-cusped hyperbolic 3-manifold and
\mathcal{T}
be an ideal triangulation of
M
.
We denote by
\tau_{\gamma}(M)
the adjoint Reidemeister torsion of
M
and by
\tau_{\gamma}^{\mathrm{CS}}(\mathcal{T})
the 1-loop invariant of
\mathcal{T}
(here
\mathrm{CS}
stands for the Chern–Simons theory, as the 1-loop invariant is derived from its perturbative partition function). Note that both are elements of the trace field of
M
well-defined up to sign and depend on a choice of a peripheral curve
\gamma
of
M
.

Conjecture 1.1

(
[
DG13
]
)
.

The 1-loop invariant
\tau^{\mathrm{CS}}_{\gamma}(\mathcal{T})
of
\mathcal{T}
is equal to the adjoint Reidemeister torsion
\tau_{\gamma}(M)
of
M
for all peripheral curves
\gamma
.

Garoufalidis and the author showed in
[
GY21
]
that the gluing equation matrices can be generalized to
twisted gluing equations matrices
(whose entries are in
\mathbb{Z}[t^{\pm 1}]
) by
choosing an infinite cyclic cover of
M
, or an epimorphism
\alpha:\pi_{1}(M)\twoheadrightarrow\mathbb{Z}
equivalently, and additionally recording how the gluing data are lifted to a fundamental domain. They also defined
twisted 1-loop invariant
using a similar construction to the 1-loop invariant and conjectured that it is equal to the adjoint twisted Alexander polynomial (associated with the geometric representation and the epimorphism
\alpha
). Note that both are Laurent polynomials in one variable
t
over the trace field and well-defined up to multiplication of units in
\mathbb{Z}[t^{\pm 1}]
.

Conjecture 1.2

(
[
GY21
]
)
.

The twisted 1-loop invariant
\tau^{\mathrm{CS}}(\mathcal{T},t)
of
\mathcal{T}
is equal to the adjoint twisted Alexander polynomial
\tau(M,t)
of
M
.

Our original motivation was to study Conjecture
1.2
for punctured surface bundles over
S^{1}
.
As is well-known, twisted Alexander polynomials of those manifolds are simply determined by monodromies.
In particular,
adjoint twisted Alexander polynomials are determined by how monodromies act on the
\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{C})
-character varieties of punctured surfaces.
This leads us to consider so-called Ptolemy coordinates or assignments, instead of shape parameters of tetrahedra, as we need coordinates for the character varieties of punctured surfaces.
Ptolemy coordinates
are well-studied objects in both 2 and 3-dimensional hyperbolic geometry but in different languages (see, for instance,
[
FG06
,
Pen12
,
GTZ15
]
).
An essential property of Ptolemy coordinates is that they effectively parameterize (certain subsets of) character varieties of both punctured surfaces and cusped 3-manifolds in the same fashion.
This allows us to compute adjoint twisted Alexander polynomials of punctured surface bundles easily in terms of Ptolemy coordinates (see Theorem
4.11
for once-punctured torus bundles).
Note that we will use a sign-extended version of Ptolemy assignments,
\sigma
-Ptolemy assignments, developed in
[
Yoo19
]
(there, these were called
\sigma
-deformed Ptolemy assignments) due to a technical reason that the geometric representation of a cusped hyperbolic manifold does not admit an
\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{C})
-lift which is boundary-unipotent
[
Cal06
]
.

The goal of this paper is to relate the twisted 1-loop invariant with Ptolemy coordinates. Precisely, we present an alternative definition of the twisted 1-loop invariant as well as the 1-loop invariant in terms of the Jacobian of Ptolemy coordinates (see Theorem
3.7
and Corollary
3.8
).
An advantage of this definition is that it does not require a combinatorial flattening, an extra datum which is needed in the original definition of (twisted) 1-loop invariant in
[
DG13
]
and
[
GY21
]
.
Interestingly, this resembles the fact that we need a combinatorial flattening to compute the Chern-Simons invariant in terms of the shapes of tetrahedra
[
Neu04
]
but we do not if we use Ptolemy coordinates
[
Zic09
,
GTZ15
]
.

As an application of Theorem
3.7
,
we prove that Conjecture
1.2
is true for all hyperbolic once-punctured torus bundles (which was our original motivation; most of the techniques used in the proof can be applied to higher genus surface bundles). To state the theorem precisely, let
M_{\varphi}
be the once-punctured torus bundle over
S^{1}
with monodromy
\varphi
and
\alpha:\pi_{1}(M_{\varphi})\twoheadrightarrow\mathbb{Z}
be the epimophism induced from the projection map
M_{\varphi}\rightarrow S^{1}
.
We choose an ideal triangulation
\mathcal{T}_{\varphi}
of
M_{\varphi}
as the canonical triangulation (also called the monodromy triangulation)
[
FH82
,
Lac03
,
Gue06
]
.

Theorem 1.3
.

The twisted 1-loop invariant
\tau^{\mathrm{CS}}(\mathcal{T}_{\varphi},t)
equals to the adjoint twisted Alexander polynomial
\tau(M_{\varphi},t)
of
M_{\varphi}
for all hyperbolic once-punctured torus bundles
M_{\varphi}
.

As a corollary of Theorem
1.3
, we obtain that the 1-loop conjecture
[
DG13
, Conj.1.8]
holds for all hyperbolic once-punctured torus bundles.

Corollary 1.4
.

The 1-loop invariant
\tau^{\mathrm{CS}}_{\gamma}(\mathcal{T}_{\varphi})
equals to the adjoint Reidemeister torsion
\tau_{\gamma}(M_{\varphi})
of
M_{\varphi}
for all peripheral curves
\gamma
and hyperbolic once-punctured torus bundles
M_{\varphi}
.

The paper is organized as follows. In Section
2
, we briefly recall the definition of the twisted 1-loop invariant.
In Section
3
, we present an alternative definition of the twisted 1-loop invariant in terms of the Jacobian of Ptolemy coordinates.
In Section
4
, we explicitly compute the twisted 1-loop invariant (Section
4.2
) and Ptolemy assignments (Section
4.3
) for once-punctured torus bundles
and present a formula for the adjoint twisted Alexander polynomial in terms of Ptolemy assignments (Section
4.4
).
Based on computations in Section
4
, we prove Theorem
1.3
and Corollary
1.4
in Section
5
.

2.
The twisted 1-loop invariant

Let
M
be a 1-cusped hyperbolic 3-manifold equipped with an epimorphism
\alpha:\pi_{1}(M)\twoheadrightarrow\mathbb{Z}

and
\mathcal{T}
be an ideal triangulation of
M
.
Let
\widetilde{M}
be the infinite cyclic cover of
M
corresponding to the kernel of
\alpha
and
\widetilde{\mathcal{T}}
be the ideal triangulation of
\widetilde{M}
induced from
\mathcal{T}
.
We index the edges
e_{i}
and the tetrahedra

\Delta_{j}
of
\mathcal{T}
by
1\leq i,j\leq N
(the number of tetrahedra is equal to that of edges, as the Euler characteristic of
M
is zero).
We choose a fundamental domain of
M
in
\widetilde{M}
and denote by
\widetilde{e}_{i}
and
\widetilde{\Delta}_{j}
the lifts of
e_{i}
and
\Delta_{j}
, respectively, to the fundamental domain. Then an edge and a tetrahedron of
\widetilde{\mathcal{T}}
are given by
g^{k}\cdot\widetilde{e}_{i}
and
g^{k}\cdot\widetilde{\Delta}_{j}
for some
k\in\mathbb{Z}
and
1\leq i,j\leq N
where
g
is a generator of the deck transformation group
\mathbb{Z}
of
\widetilde{M}
.

A quad type
of a tetrahedron is a pair of opposite edges; hence each tetrahedron has three different
quad types. We choose a quad type and an orientation of each
\Delta_{j}
so that each edge of

\Delta_{j}
admits a shape parameter among

z_{j},\quad z^{\prime}_{j}=\frac{1}{1-z_{j}},\quad z^{\prime\prime}_{j}=1-\frac{1}{z_{j}}\in\mathbb{C}\setminus\{0,1\}

with opposite edges having same parameters (see Figure
1
).
We denote by
\square
the three pairs of opposite edges of a tetrahedron
so that the edges of
\square
are assigned to the shape parameter
z^{\square}
.

Figure 1.
An ideal tetrahedron with shape parameters
z^{\square}
.

The gluing equation matrices
\mathbf{G}^{\square}
of
\mathcal{T}
are the
N\times N
matrices (whose rows and columns are indexed by the edges and by the tetrahedra of
\mathcal{T}

respectively and) whose
(i,j)
-entry
\mathbf{G}^{\square}_{ij}
is the number
of edges of
\Delta_{j}
with parameter
z^{\square}_{j}
that are incident to
e_{i}
in
\mathcal{T}
.
The gluing equation matrices basically show which tetrahedra are attached to
e_{i}
. In particular, the gluing equation of
e_{i}
is given by

(1)

e_{i}:\quad\prod_{j=1}^{N}z_{j}^{\mathbf{G}_{ij}}{z_{j}^{\prime}}^{\mathbf{G}_{ij}^{\prime}}{z_{j}^{\prime\prime}}^{\mathbf{G}_{ij}^{\prime\prime}}=1.

In a similar manner, we consider tetrahedra of
\widetilde{\mathcal{T}}
that are attached to
\widetilde{e}_{i}
. Precisely, for
k\in\mathbb{Z}
let
\mathbf{G}_{k}^{\square}
be the

N\times N
matrix
whose
(i,j)
-entry is the number of edges of
g^{k}\cdot\widetilde{\Delta}_{j}
with
parameter
z_{j}^{\square}
that are incident to
\widetilde{e}_{i}
in
\widetilde{\mathcal{T}}
. We then define the
twisted gluing equation matrices

\mathbf{G}^{\square}(t)
of
\mathcal{T}
by

\mathbf{G}^{\square}(t):=\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\mathbf{G}^{\square}_{k}\,t^{k}.

Note that entries of
\mathbf{G}^{\square}(t)
are in
\mathbb{Z}[t^{\pm 1}]
and that
\mathbf{G}^{\square}(t)
reduces to
\mathbf{G}^{\square}
when
t=1
.

A peripheral curve is an oriented, homotopically non-trivial,
simple closed curve in the peripheral torus of
M
. Each peripheral curve

\gamma
is associated with three row vectors
\mathbf{C}_{\gamma}^{\square}\in\mathbb{Z}^{N}
that describe its completeness equation as

(2)

\gamma:\quad\prod_{j=1}^{N}z_{j}^{\mathbf{C}_{\gamma j}}{z_{j}^{\prime}}^{\mathbf{C}_{\gamma j}^{\prime}}{z_{j}^{\prime\prime}}^{\mathbf{C}_{\gamma j}^{\prime\prime}}=1

where
\mathbf{C}^{\square}_{\gamma j}
is the
j
-th component of
\mathbf{C}^{\square}_{\gamma}
. We say that a point
(z_{1},\ldots,z_{N})\in(\mathbb{C}\setminus\{0,1\})^{N}
is a
solution
of
\mathcal{T}
if it satisfies the gluing equation (
1
) for all edges of
\mathcal{T}
and the completeness equation (
2
) for all peripheral curves of
M
and that a solution is
geometric
if its holonomy representation
\pi_{1}(M)\rightarrow\mathrm{PSL}_{2}(\mathbb{C})
is the geometric representation of
M
.
Throughout the paper, we assume that a geometric solution of
\mathcal{T}
exists.

A
combinatorial flattening
of
\mathcal{T}
consists of three column vectors
f^{\square}\in\mathbb{Z}^{N}
satisfying

(3)

\displaystyle f+f^{\prime}+f^{\prime\prime}

\displaystyle=(1,\ldots,1)^{T},

(4)

\displaystyle\mathbf{G}f+\mathbf{G}^{\prime}f^{\prime}+\mathbf{G}^{\prime\prime}f^{\prime\prime}

\displaystyle=(2,\ldots,2)^{T},

(5)

\displaystyle\mathbf{C}_{\gamma}f+\mathbf{C}^{\prime}_{\gamma}f^{\prime}+\mathbf{C}^{\prime\prime}_{\gamma}f^{\prime\prime}

\displaystyle=0

for all peripheral curves
\gamma
.
Note that every ideal triangulation has a combinatorial flattening
[
Neu92
, Thm.4.5]
.

Definition 2.1

(
[
GY21
]
)
.

With the above notation, the
twisted 1-loop invariant

\tau^{\mathrm{CS}}(\mathcal{T},t)
of
\mathcal{T}
is defined as

(6)

\tau^{\mathrm{CS}}(\mathcal{T},t):=\frac{\det\big(\mathbf{G}(t)\,\mathrm{diag}(\zeta)+\mathbf{G}^{\prime}(t)\,\mathrm{diag}(\zeta^{\prime})+\mathbf{G}^{\prime\prime}(t)\,\mathrm{diag}(\zeta^{\prime\prime})\big)}{\displaystyle\prod_{j=1}^{N}\zeta_{j}^{f_{j}}{\zeta_{j}^{\prime}}^{f_{j}^{\prime}}{\zeta_{j}^{\prime\prime}}^{f_{j}^{\prime\prime}}}\in\mathbb{C}[t^{\pm 1}]

where the right-hand side is evaluated at the geometric solution of
\mathcal{T}
,

(7)

\zeta_{j}=\frac{d\log z_{j}}{dz_{j}}=\frac{1}{z_{j}},\quad\zeta_{j}^{\prime}=\frac{d\log z_{j}^{\prime}}{dz_{j}}=\frac{1}{1-z_{j}},\quad\zeta_{j}^{\prime\prime}=\frac{d\log z_{j}^{\prime\prime}}{dz_{j}}=\frac{1}{z_{j}(z_{j}-1)},

and
\mathrm{diag}(\zeta^{\square})
is the diagonal matrix with diagonal entries
\zeta^{\square}_{1},\ldots,\zeta^{\square}_{n}
.

Several choices, such as quad types of
\Delta_{j}
, a fundamental domain of
M
and a flattening of
\mathcal{T}
, are involved in the right-hand side of Equation (
6
). However, it turns out that
the twisted 1-loop invariant
\tau^{\mathrm{CS}}(\mathcal{T},t)
is well-defined up to multiplication of units in
\mathbb{Z}[t^{\pm 1}]
. Furthermore, it is invariant under 2–3 Pachner moves.
We refer to
[
GY21
]
for details.

Remark 2.2
.

The logarithmic form of the gluing equation (
1
) is

(8)

e_{i}:\quad\sum_{j=1}^{N}\mathbf{G}_{ij}\log z_{j}+{\mathbf{G}_{ij}^{\prime}}\log{z_{j}^{\prime}}+{\mathbf{G}_{ij}^{\prime\prime}}\log{z_{j}^{\prime\prime}}=2\pi\sqrt{-1}\,.

Since we fixed a fundamental domain, say
D
, of
M
, the infinite cyclic cover
\widetilde{M}
is divided into
g^{k}\cdot D
for
k\in\mathbb{Z}
. Accordingly, the left-hand side of Equation (
8
) is divided with respect to
k\in\mathbb{Z}
: the contribution from
g^{k}\cdot D
is

\sum_{j=1}^{N}(\mathbf{G}_{k})_{ij}\log z_{j}+{(\mathbf{G}^{\prime}_{k})_{ij}}\log{z_{j}^{\prime}}+{(\mathbf{G}^{\prime\prime}_{k})_{ij}}\log{z_{j}^{\prime\prime}}\,.

It naturally allows us to associate a Laurent polynomial
E_{i}
to each edge
e_{i}

E_{i}=\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\left(\sum_{j=1}^{N}(\mathbf{G}_{k})_{ij}\log z_{j}+{(\mathbf{G}^{\prime}_{k})_{ij}}\log{z_{j}^{\prime}}+{(\mathbf{G}^{\prime\prime}_{k})_{ij}}\log{z_{j}^{\prime\prime}}\right)t^{k}

which reduces to the left-hand side of Equation (
8
) when
t=1
. It follows from the definition (
7
) that

\big(\mathbf{G}(t)\,\mathrm{diag}(\zeta)+\mathbf{G}^{\prime}(t)\,\mathrm{diag}(\zeta^{\prime})+\mathbf{G}^{\prime\prime}(t)\,\mathrm{diag}(\zeta^{\prime\prime})\big)_{ij}=\frac{\partial E_{i}}{\partial z_{j}}

for
1\leq i,j\leq N
.

3.
The Jacobian of
\sigma
-Ptolemy coordinates

In this section, we present an alternative definition of the twisted 1-loop invariant in terms of the Jacobian of
\sigma
-Ptolemy coordinates (see Theorem
3.7
below).

3.1.
A single tetrahedron

We first consider a single tetrahedron
\Delta
with its
truncation

\overline{\Delta}
, a polyhedron obtained from
\Delta
by chopping off its vertices. There are two types of edges in
\overline{\Delta}
: an edge of a triangular face is called a
short-edge
and an edge that is not a short-edge is called a
long-edge
.
Let
\delta\overline{\Delta}
be the union of four triangular faces of
\overline{\Delta}
so that the edges of
\delta\overline{\Delta}
are the short-edges of
\overline{\Delta}
.
We denote by
\mathcal{E}(X)
for
X\in\{\Delta,\overline{\Delta},\delta\overline{\Delta}\}
the set of oriented edges of
X
.
It is clear that
\mathcal{E}(\delta\overline{\Delta})\subset\mathcal{E}(\overline{\Delta})
and that
\mathcal{E}(\Delta)
is identified with
\mathcal{E}(\overline{\Delta})\setminus\mathcal{E}(\delta\overline{\Delta})
in an obvious way.

We say that an assignment
\sigma:\mathcal{E}(\delta\overline{\Delta})\rightarrow\{\pm 1\}
of a sign to each oriented short-edge is a
cocycle
if

•

\sigma(-e)=\sigma(e)^{-1}
for
e\in\mathcal{E}(\delta\overline{\Delta})
where
-e
is the same edge
e
but with the reversed orientation;

•

\sigma(e_{1})\sigma(e_{2})\sigma(e_{3})=1
if
e_{1},e_{2}
and
e_{3}
form the boundary of a 2-cell of
\delta\overline{\Delta}
in any cyclic order.

In fact, the orientations of short-edges do not matter as the target group is
\{\pm 1\}
.

Definition 3.1

(
[
Yoo19
]
)
.

For a cocycle
\sigma:\mathcal{E}(\delta\overline{\Delta})\rightarrow\{\pm 1\}

an assignment
c:\mathcal{E}(\Delta)\rightarrow\mathbb{C}^{\ast}
is called a
\sigma
-Ptolemy assignment (or coordinates)
if
c(-e)=-c(e)
for all
e\in\mathcal{E}(\Delta)
and

(9)

c(l_{02})c(l_{13})-\frac{\sigma(s_{031})\sigma(s_{302})}{\sigma(s_{312})\sigma(s_{021})}c(l_{03})c(l_{12})-\frac{\sigma(s_{013})\sigma(s_{102})}{\sigma(s_{132})\sigma(s_{023})}c(l_{01})c(l_{23})=0

where
l_{ij}
is the oriented edge of
\Delta=[v_{0},v_{1},v_{2},v_{3}]
starting from the vertex
v_{i}
and heading to
v_{j}
and
s_{ijk}
is the oriented short-edge starting from
l_{ij}
and heading to
l_{jk}
as in Figure
2
. We refer to Equation (

) as the
\sigma
-Ptolemy equation

p^{\sigma}_{\Delta}
of
\Delta
.

It is shown in

\rho:\mathcal{E}(\overline{\Delta})\rightarrow\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{C})
and the shape of
\Delta
.
Precisely, the cocycle

(10)

\rho(l_{ij})=\begin{pmatrix}0&-c(l_{ij})^{-1}\\
c(l_{ij})&0\end{pmatrix},\quad\rho(s_{ijk})=\begin{pmatrix}\sigma(s_{ijk})&-\frac{\sigma(s_{kij})}{\sigma(s_{jki})}\frac{c(l_{ki})}{c(l_{ij})\,c(l_{jk})}\\

for Figure

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
(f,f^{\prime},f^{\prime\prime})\in\mathbb{Z}^{3}
be a triple of integers satisfying
f+f^{\prime}+f^{\prime\prime}=1
. Then we have

(14)

\frac{c(e^{\square}_{i})}{c^{f}_{\Delta}}\frac{\partial p^{\sigma}_{\Delta}}{\partial c(e^{\square}_{i})}=\pm\frac{\zeta^{\square}}{\zeta^{f}_{\Delta}}

for
i=0,1
and any
\square
where the sign
\pm
does not depend on
i
and
\square

\displaystyle c^{f}_{\Delta}

\displaystyle:=c(e_{0})^{f}c(e_{1})^{f}c(e^{\prime}_{0})^{f^{\prime}}c(e^{\prime}_{1})^{f^{\prime}}c(e^{\prime\prime}_{0})^{f^{\prime\prime}}c(e^{\prime\prime}_{1})^{f^{\prime\prime}},

\displaystyle\zeta^{f}_{\Delta}

\displaystyle:=\zeta^{f}\zeta^{\prime f^{\prime}}\zeta^{\prime\prime f^{\prime\prime}}.

Proof.

As in the proof of Lemma
3.2

\Delta

(e_{0},e_{1})=(l_{02},l_{13})

(e^{\prime}_{0},e^{\prime}_{1})=(l_{01},l_{23})
and
(e^{\prime\prime}_{0},e^{\prime\prime}_{1})=(l_{03},l_{12})

\displaystyle\zeta^{f}\zeta^{\prime f^{\prime}}\zeta^{\prime\prime f^{\prime\prime}}

\displaystyle=\zeta^{1-f^{\prime}-f^{\prime\prime}}\zeta^{\prime f^{\prime}}\zeta^{\prime\prime f^{\prime\prime}}

\displaystyle=\zeta\left(\frac{\zeta^{\prime}}{\zeta}\right)^{f^{\prime}}\left(\frac{\zeta^{\prime\prime}}{\zeta}\right)^{f^{\prime\prime}}

\displaystyle=\pm\zeta\left(\frac{c(l_{01})c(l_{23})}{c(l_{02})c(l_{13})}\right)^{f^{\prime}}\left(\frac{c(l_{03})c(l_{12})}{c(l_{02})c(l_{13})}\right)^{f^{\prime\prime}}

\displaystyle=\pm\zeta\cdot\frac{c(e_{0})^{f}c(e_{1})^{f}c(e^{\prime}_{0})^{f^{\prime}}c(e^{\prime}_{1})^{f^{\prime}}c(e^{\prime\prime}_{0})^{f^{\prime\prime}}c(e^{\prime\prime}_{1})^{f^{\prime\prime}}}{c(e_{0})c(e_{1})}.

where the sign

Combining this with Lemma

3.2.
\sigma
-Ptolemy assignments for a 3-manifold

The notion of
\sigma
-Ptolemy assignment in Section
3.1
naturally extends to an ideal triangulation of a 3-manifold
[
Yoo19
]
. We briefly recall its definition with some properties that we need in the following sections.

Let
M
be a 1-cusped hyperbolic 3-manifold and
\overline{M}
be the compact manifold with a torus boundary whose interior is homeomorphic to
M
.
An ideal triangulation
\mathcal{T}
of
M
endows
\overline{M}
with a decomposition into truncated tetrahedra whose triangular faces triangulate the boundary
\partial\overline{M}
of
\overline{M}
.
For simplicity we confuse this decomposition of
\overline{M}
as well as that of
\partial\overline{M}
with its underlying space.
We denote by
\mathcal{E}(X)
for
X\in\{\mathcal{T},\overline{M},\partial\overline{M}\}
the set of oriented edges of
X
.

Definition 3.4

(
[
Yoo19
]
)
.

For a cocycle
\sigma:\mathcal{E}(\partial\overline{M})\rightarrow\{\pm 1\}
an assignment

is called a
\sigma
-Ptolemy assignment (or coordinates)
if it satisfies
c(-e)=-c(e)
for all
e\in\mathcal{E}(\mathcal{T})
and the
\sigma
-Ptolemy equation
for all tetrahedra of
\mathcal{T}
.

As in Equation (
10
), a
\sigma
-Ptolemy assignment
c:\mathcal{E}(\mathcal{T})\rightarrow\mathbb{C}^{\ast}
determines a cocycle
\rho:\mathcal{E}(\overline{M})\rightarrow\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{C})
and thus induces a representation
\rho:\pi_{1}(M)\rightarrow\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{C})
(also denoted by
\rho
) up to conjugation.
We obtain a map
\Phi
from the set
P^{\sigma}(\mathcal{T})
of all
\sigma
-Ptolemy assignments of
\mathcal{T}
to the
\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{C})
-character variety
\mathcal{X}(M)
of
M

(15)

\Phi:P^{\sigma}(\mathcal{T})\rightarrow\mathcal{X}(M)

by sending
c\in P^{\sigma}(\mathcal{T})
to the conjugacy class of
\rho
.
Note that by the definition (
10
) the representation
\rho
satisfies (up to conjugation)

(16)

\rho(\gamma)=\begin{pmatrix}\sigma(\gamma)&\ast\\
0&\sigma(\gamma)^{-1}\end{pmatrix}\quad\textrm{for all }\gamma\in\pi_{1}(\partial\overline{M})

where
\sigma:\pi_{1}(\partial\overline{M})\rightarrow\{\pm 1\}
is the homomorphism (also denoted by
\sigma
) induced from the cocycle
\sigma
.

Remark 3.5
.

As the
\sigma
-Ptolemy equation (
9
) is homogeneous, the set
P^{\sigma}(\mathcal{T})
admits the multiplication by a non-zero complex number acting diagonally.
It is proved in
[

, Prop.2.6]
that

for all

and
k\in\mathbb{C}^{\ast}
.

As in Equation (
11
), a
\sigma
-Ptolemy assignment

determines the shape parameters of tetrahedra of
\mathcal{T}
.
These shape parameters automatically form a solution of
\mathcal{T}
, and its holonomy representation admits an
\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{C})
-lift whose conjugacy class is
\Phi(c)\in\mathcal{X}(M)
.
Conversely, a standard pseudo-developing map argument shows the following (see
[
Yoo19
, Sec.2.3]
or
[
GTZ15
, Thm.1.12]
for details).

Proposition 3.6

(
[
Yoo19
]
)
.

Let
z_{1},\ldots,z_{N}
be a solution of
\mathcal{T}
whose holonomy representation admits an
\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{C})
-lift

.
Then for any cocycle
\sigma:\mathcal{E}(\partial\overline{M})\rightarrow\{\pm 1\}
satisfying Equation (
16
) there exists

corresponding to the solution.

The geometric representation of a hyperbolic 3-manifold always has an
\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{C})
-lift
[
CS83
, Prop.3.1.1]
.
We say that a cocycle
\sigma:\mathcal{E}(\partial\overline{M})\rightarrow\{\pm 1\}
is an

if it satisfies Equation (

) for a lift

of the geometric representation of

.
It follows from Proposition

that for any obstruction cocycle

there exists

corresponding to the geometric solution of
\mathcal{T}
.

3.3.
An alternative definition of the twisted 1-loop invariant

We now fix an epimorphism
\alpha:\pi_{1}(M)\twoheadrightarrow\mathbb{Z}
.
Let
\widetilde{M}
be the infinite cyclic cover of
M
corresponding to the kernel of
\alpha
and
\widetilde{\mathcal{T}}
be the ideal triangulation of
\widetilde{M}
induced from
\mathcal{T}
.
We choose a fundamental domain of
M
in
\widetilde{M}
and denote by
\widetilde{e}_{i}
and
\widetilde{\Delta}_{j}
the lifts of
e_{i}
and
\Delta_{j}
, respectively, to the fundamental domain (recall that
e_{1},\ldots,e_{N}
and
\Delta_{1},\ldots,\Delta_{N}
are the edges and the tetrahedra of
\mathcal{T}
).
It is clear that
\widetilde{e}_{1},\ldots,\widetilde{e}_{N}
are edges of
\widetilde{\Delta}_{1},\ldots,\widetilde{\Delta}_{N}
but those are not all of the edges of
\widetilde{\Delta}_{1},\ldots,\widetilde{\Delta}_{N}
. We denote by
\widetilde{e}_{N+1},\ldots,\widetilde{e}_{N+L}
the other edges of
\widetilde{\Delta}_{1},\ldots,\widetilde{\Delta}_{N}
.
It follows that for
N+1\leq i\leq N+L
there exists a unique pair of
1\leq j(i)\leq N
and
k(i)\in\mathbb{Z}
satisfying

(17)

\widetilde{e}_{i}=g^{k(i)}\cdot\widetilde{e}_{j(i)}

where
g
is a generator of the deck transformation group
\mathbb{Z}
of
\widetilde{M}
.

We choose an orientation of each edge of
\mathcal{T}
and orient the edges of
\widetilde{\mathcal{T}}
accordingly.
We then assign a variable
c_{i}
to each
\widetilde{e}_{i}

(1\leq i\leq N+L
) and
define an element
p^{\sigma}_{i}\in\mathbb{Z}[c_{1},\ldots,c_{N+L},t^{\pm 1}]
as follows.

•

let
p^{\sigma}_{i}
be the left-hand side of the
\sigma
-Ptolemy equation (
9
) of
\widetilde{\Delta}_{i}
for
1\leq i\leq N
;

•

let
p^{\sigma}_{i}=c_{i}-t^{-k(i)}c_{j(i)}
for
N+1\leq i\leq N+L
.

For
N+1\leq i\leq N+L
we often write
p^{\sigma}_{i}(t)
instead of
p^{\sigma}_{i}
if we need to specify the value of the variable
t
.
For instance, there is an obvious bijection

P^{\sigma}(\mathcal{T})\simeq\{(c_{1},\ldots,c_{N+L})\in(\mathbb{C}^{\ast})^{N+L}:p^{\sigma}_{1}=\cdots=p^{\sigma}_{N}=p^{\sigma}_{N+1}(1)=\cdots=p^{\sigma}_{N+L}(1)=0\}

sending
c\in P^{\sigma}(\mathcal{T})
to
(c_{1},\ldots,c_{N+L})
where
c_{i}=c(e_{i})
for
1\leq i\leq N
and
c_{i}=c_{j(i)}
for
N+1\leq i\leq N+L
. In particular, if
\sigma
is an obstruction cocycle, then there exists a point
(c_{1},\ldots,c_{N+L})\in(\mathbb{C}^{\ast})^{N+L}
corresponding to the geometric solution of
\mathcal{T}
.

Theorem 3.7
.

For an obstruction cocycle
\sigma
we have

\tau^{\mathrm{CS}}(\mathcal{T},t)=\pm\frac{1}{c_{1}\cdots c_{N}}\det\left(\frac{\partial p^{\sigma}_{i}}{\partial c_{j}}\right),\quad 1\leq i,j\leq N+L

where the right-hand side is evaluated at a point
(c_{1},\ldots,c_{N+L})
corresponding to the geometric solution of
\mathcal{T}
.

Proof.

For
N+1\leq i\leq N+L
we have

(18)

\frac{\partial p^{\sigma}_{i}}{\partial c_{j}}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}1&\textrm{if }j=i\\
-t^{-k(i)}&\textrm{if }j=j(i)\\
0&\textrm{otherwise}\end{array}.\right.

Thus adding
t^{-k(i)}
times the
i
-column of the jacobian matrix
(\partial p^{\sigma}_{i}/\partial c_{j})
to the
j(i)
-th column, we have

\begin{pmatrix}\mathbf{Y}&\hskip-5.0pt\vline\hskip-5.0pt&*\\
\hline\cr\mbox{\Large 0}&\hskip-5.0pt\vline\hskip-5.0pt&\mathrm{Id}_{L}\end{pmatrix}

where
\mathrm{Id}_{L}
is the identity matrix of size
L
and
\mathbf{Y}
is a square matrix of size
N
with
\det\mathbf{Y}=\det(\frac{\partial p_{i}^{\sigma}}{\partial c_{j}})
.

Let
f^{\square}=(f^{\square}_{1},\ldots,f^{\square}_{N})\in\mathbb{Z}^{N}
be a combinatorial flattening of
\mathcal{T}
. We claim that

(19)

\begin{array}[]{ll}&\mathrm{diag}(\pm c_{1},\ldots,\pm c_{N})\,\mathbf{Y}^{T}\,\mathrm{diag}(c^{f_{1}}_{\widetilde{\Delta}_{1}},\ldots,c^{f_{N}}_{\widetilde{\Delta}_{N}})^{-1}\\[3.0pt]
&=\big(\mathbf{G}(t)\,\mathrm{diag}(\zeta)+\mathbf{G}^{\prime}(t)\,\mathrm{diag}(\zeta^{\prime})+\mathbf{G}^{\prime\prime}(t)\,\mathrm{diag}(\zeta^{\prime\prime})\big)\,\mathrm{diag}(\zeta^{f_{1}}_{\widetilde{\Delta}_{1}},\ldots,\zeta^{f_{N}}_{\widetilde{\Delta}_{N}})^{-1}\end{array}

where
c^{f_{i}}_{\widetilde{\Delta}_{i}}
and
\zeta^{f_{i}}_{\widetilde{\Delta}_{i}}
are the notations defined in Lemma
3.3
.
We fix
1\leq I\leq N
and let
N+1\leq I_{1},\ldots,I_{m}\leq N+L
be the indices satisfying
j(I_{1})=\cdots=j(I_{m})=I
.
This means that the edges
\widetilde{e}_{I_{1}},\ldots,\widetilde{e}_{I_{m}}
are lifts of the edge
e_{I}
.
Then we have

\mathbf{Y}_{JI}=\frac{\partial p^{\sigma}_{J}}{\partial c_{I}}+\frac{\partial p^{\sigma}_{J}}{\partial c_{I_{1}}}t^{-k(I_{1})}+\cdots+\frac{\partial p^{\sigma}_{J}}{\partial c_{I_{m}}}t^{-k(I_{m})}

for
1\leq J\leq N
, and since
c_{I}=c_{I_{1}}=\cdots=c_{I_{m}}
,

(20)

\frac{c_{I}}{c^{f_{J}}_{\widetilde{\Delta}_{J}}}\mathbf{Y}_{JI}=\frac{c_{I}}{c^{f_{J}}_{\widetilde{\Delta}_{J}}}\frac{\partial p^{\sigma}_{J}}{\partial c_{I}}+\frac{c_{I_{1}}}{c^{f_{J}}_{\widetilde{\Delta}_{J}}}\frac{\partial p^{\sigma}_{J}}{\partial c_{I_{1}}}t^{-k(I_{1})}+\cdots+\frac{c_{I_{m}}}{c^{f_{J}}_{\widetilde{\Delta}_{J}}}\frac{\partial p^{\sigma}_{J}}{\partial c_{I_{m}}}t^{-k(I_{m})}.

A tetrahedron
\Delta_{J}
is attached to
e_{I}
if and only if
\widetilde{\Delta}_{J}
is attached to one of
\widetilde{e}_{I},\widetilde{e}_{I_{1}},\ldots,\widetilde{e}_{l_{m}}
.
Also, a tetrahedron
\widetilde{\Delta}_{J}
is attached to
\widetilde{e}_{I_{l}}
for some
1\leq l\leq m
if and only if
g^{-k(I_{l})}\cdot\widetilde{\Delta}_{J}
is attached to
\widetilde{e}_{I}
.
Combining this fact with Equation (
20
), we obtain Equation (
19
) from Lemma
3.3
.
Therefore,

(21)

\pm\frac{c_{1}\cdots c_{N}}{c^{f_{1}}_{\widetilde{\Delta}_{1}}\cdots c^{f_{N}}_{\widetilde{\Delta}_{N}}}\det\mathbf{Y}=\frac{\det\big(\mathbf{G}(t)\,\mathrm{diag}(\zeta)+\mathbf{G}^{\prime}(t)\,\mathrm{diag}(\zeta^{\prime})+\mathbf{G}^{\prime\prime}(t)\,\mathrm{diag}(\zeta^{\prime\prime})\big)}{\zeta^{f_{1}}_{\widetilde{\Delta}_{1}}\cdots\zeta^{f_{N}}_{\widetilde{\Delta}_{N}}}=\tau^{\mathrm{CS}}(\mathcal{T},t).

On the other hand, we have

{c^{f_{1}}_{\widetilde{\Delta}_{1}}}\cdots{c^{f_{N}}_{\widetilde{\Delta}_{N}}}=c_{1}^{2}\cdots c_{N}^{2}

since
(f,f^{\prime},f^{\prime\prime})
satisfies
\mathbf{G}f+\mathbf{G}^{\prime}f^{\prime}+\mathbf{G}^{\prime\prime}f^{\prime\prime}=(2,\ldots,2)^{T}
.
This completes the proof:

(22)

\tau^{\mathrm{CS}}(\mathcal{T},t)=\pm\frac{c_{1}\cdots c_{N}}{{c^{f_{1}}_{\widetilde{\Delta}_{1}}}\cdots{c^{f_{N}}_{\widetilde{\Delta}_{N}}}}\det\mathbf{Y}=\pm\frac{1}{c_{1}\cdots c_{N}}\det\mathbf{Y}=\pm\frac{1}{c_{1}\cdots c_{N}}\det\left(\frac{\partial p^{\sigma}_{i}}{\partial c_{j}}\right).

∎

Combining Theorem
3.7
with
[
GY21
, Thm.1.7]
, we also obtain an alternative expression of the 1-loop invariant
[
DG13
]
in terms of
\sigma
-Ptolemy coordinates:

We have

\tau_{\lambda}^{\mathrm{CS}}(\mathcal{T})=\pm\frac{1}{c_{1}\cdots c_{N}}\left.\dfrac{d}{dt}\right|_{t=1}\det\left(\frac{\partial p^{\sigma}_{i}}{\partial c_{j}}\right),\quad 1\leq i,j\leq N+L

Dynamics of charge imbalance resolved negativity after a local joining quench
Hui-Huang Chen, Zun-Xian Huang

Abstract
In this paper, we consider the dynamics of charge imbalance resolved negativity after a local joining quench in the 1 + 1 1+1 dimensional free complex boson CFT. In the first part, we study the local joining quench by applying conformal maps, obtaining analytical universal results. We first calculate the quench dynamics of charged logarithmic negativity. Then using the Fourier transformation, we obtain the charge imbalance resolved negativity. The total negativity can be recovered from the charge-resolved ones. In the second part, we test our CFT predictions against the underlying lattice model numerically. Finally, we explain our results based on the quasi-particle picture.

Among the aforesaid research progresses, entanglement entropy is the most successful entanglement measure to characterize the bipartite entanglement of a subsystem

S_{n}=\frac{1}{1-n}\log{\textrm{Tr}}\rho_{A}^{n}.

S_{1}\equiv\lim_{n\rightarrow 1}S_{n}=-\lim_{n\rightarrow 1}\frac{\partial}{\partial n}{\textrm{Tr}}\rho_{A}^{n}=-{\textrm{Tr}}(\rho_{A}\log\rho_{A}).

When two subsystems

\mathcal{N}=\frac{{\textrm{Tr}}|\rho_{A}^{T_{2}}|-1}{2},

(1.3)

where

denotes the trace norm of the operator
O
and
\rho_{A}^{T_{2}}
is the partial transpose of RDM
\rho_{A}
with respect to the degree of freedom of subsystem
A_{2}

A_{1}
and

A_{2}
is defined as

\bra{e_{i}^{(1)}e_{j}^{(2)}}\rho_{A}^{T_{2}}\ket{e_{k}^{(1)}e_{l}^{(2)}}=\bra{e_{i}^{(1)}e_{l}^{(2)}}\rho_{A}\ket{e_{k}^{(1)}e_{j}^{(2)}}.

(1.4)

The Rényi negativity and Rényi logarithmic negativity are defined as

\mathcal{N}_{n}=\frac{N_{n}-1}{2},\quad\mathcal{E}_{n}=\log N_{n},\quad\text{with}\quad N_{n}={\textrm{Tr}}[(\rho_{A}^{T_{2}})^{n}],

(1.5)

from which the negativity and logarithmic negativity are obtained by using the analytic continuation of the even integer at
n_{e}=1

\mathcal{N}=\lim_{n_{e}\to 1}\mathcal{N}_{n_{e}},\quad\mathcal{E}=\lim_{n_{e}\to 1}\mathcal{E}_{n_{e}}.

(1.6)

Local joining quench

In this paper, we will consider the
1+1
dimensional complex free scalar field theory
with the Euclidean action given by

\mathcal{A}=\int d^{2}x\partial_{\mu}\phi^{\dagger}\partial_{\mu}\phi.

(2.1)

This action exhibit a
U(1)
symmetry, i.e. the phase transformation of the field
\phi\rightarrow e^{i\theta}\phi,\phi^{\dagger}\rightarrow e^{-i\theta}\phi^{\dagger}
leaves the action invariant. Initially, we have two independent systems with one spatial dimension and prepare them in their own ground states. At
t=0
, we join the two systems at their endpoints together.

Now, we briefly review the CFT approach to the local joining quench following Ref.
[
57
]
. We first introduce the time evolution of the density matrix
\rho(t)=|\phi(x,t)\rangle\langle\phi(x,t)|
with
|\phi(x,t)\rangle=e^{-\mathrm{i}Ht}|\phi_{0}(x)\rangle
. Then

\langle\phi^{\prime\prime}(x^{\prime\prime})|\rho(t)|\phi^{\prime}(x^{\prime})\rangle=Z^{-1}\langle\phi^{\prime\prime}(x^{\prime\prime})|e^{-\mathrm{i}Ht-\epsilon H}|\phi_{0}(x)\rangle\langle\phi_{0}(x)|e^{+\mathrm{i}Ht-\epsilon H}|\phi^{\prime}(x^{\prime})\rangle,

(2.2)

where two factors
e^{-\epsilon H}
are used to make the path integral representation of this element absolutely convergent, and

Z=\langle\phi_{0}|e^{-2\epsilon H}|\phi_{0}\rangle
is the normalization factor.

3
Symmetry resolved entanglement

In this section, we will briefly review the decomposition of entanglement entropy and negativity under a global internal symmetry.

3.1
Symmetry resolved entropies

Let us first review the basic idea about the symmetry resolved entanglement entropy. If our system exhibit a global
U(1)
symmetry generated by a local charge
Q
which commute with the density matrix, i.e.
[\rho,Q]=0
, then we have
[\rho_{A},Q_{A}]=0
. Thus
\rho_{A}
admits charge decomposition according to eigenvalues
q
of the local charge
Q_{A}

\rho_{A}=\oplus_{q}\mathcal{P}_{q}\rho_{A}=\oplus_{q}p(q)\rho_{A}(q),\quad p(q)={\textrm{Tr}}(\mathcal{P}_{q}\rho_{A}),

(3.1)

where
\mathcal{P}_{q}
is the projection operator which projects the space to the eigenspace corresponding to eigenvalue
q
. The projector
\mathcal{P}_{q}
has the following integral represent

\mathcal{P}_{q}=\int_{0}^{2\pi}\frac{d\mu}{2\pi}e^{-\mathrm{i}\mu q}e^{\mathrm{i}\mu Q_{A}}.

(3.2)

The symmetry resolved Rényi entropies are defined as

S_{n}(q)=\frac{1}{1-n}\log{\textrm{Tr}}[\rho_{A}(q)]^{n}.

(3.3)

Symmetry resolved entanglement entropy can be obtained by taking the replica limit
S(q)=\lim_{n\rightarrow 1}S_{n}(q)
.

Regarding the integral represent of the project operator, it’s convenient to first introduce the charged moments of
\rho_{A}
,

Z_{n}(\mu)={\textrm{Tr}}(e^{\mathrm{i}\mu Q_{A}}\rho_{A}^{n}).

(3.4)

Then it’s sufficient to compute its Fourier transform

Z_{n}(q)=\int_{0}^{2\pi}\frac{d\mu}{2\pi}e^{-\mathrm{i}q\mu}Z_{n}(\mu)

(3.5)

to obtain the Rényi entropies of the sector with charge
q
as

S_{n}(q)=\frac{1}{1-n}\log\left[\frac{Z_{n}(q)}{Z_{1}(q)^{n}}\right].

(3.6)

3.2
Charge imbalance resolved negativity

Now we turn to the symmetry decomposition of entanglement negativity. We briefly recall some results about the charge imbalance resolved negativity under the
U(1)
symmetry generated by a local charge
Q
(see
[
51
,
52
,
53
,
54
,
55
]
for more details). We consider the case of
a bi-partition in two complementary subsystems
A
and
B
, in which the subsystem
A
is partitioned into two complementary subsystems
A_{1}
and
A_{2}
, we denote the corresponding charge operators as
Q_{1}
and
Q_{2}
. Because of locality, we can get
Q=Q_{A}+Q_{B}
and
Q_{A}=Q_{1}+Q_{2}
.
From the relation
[\rho_{A},Q_{A}]=0
, performing the partial transpose with respect to the degrees of freedom of subsystem
A_{2}
, we have

[\rho_{A}^{T_{2}},\mathcal{Q}_{A}]=0,\quad\mathcal{Q}_{A}=Q_{1}-Q_{2}^{T_{2}},

(3.7)

where we define the operator
\mathcal{Q}_{A}
as charge imbalance operator. We denote the eigenvalue of the charge imbalance operator
\mathcal{Q}_{A}
by
\mathrm{q}
, which is an integer. We also denote the projectors on the corresponding eigenspace by
\mathcal{P}_{\mathrm{q}}
.
Then
\rho_{A}^{T_{2}}
has a form of block matrix, each block is characterized by different eigenvalues
{\mathrm{q}}
, thus we can write

\rho_{A}^{T_{2}}({\mathrm{q}})=\frac{\mathcal{P}_{\mathrm{q}}\rho_{A}^{T_{2}}}{{\textrm{Tr}}(\mathcal{P}_{\mathrm{q}}\rho_{A}^{T_{2}})}.

(3.8)

It is enough to write

\rho_{A}^{T_{2}}=\bigoplus_{{\mathrm{q}\in\mathbb{Z}}}p({\mathrm{q}})\rho_{A}^{T_{2}}({\mathrm{q}}),

(3.9)

where
p({\mathrm{q}})={\textrm{Tr}}(\mathcal{P}_{\mathrm{q}}\rho_{A}^{T_{2}})
is the probability of finding
{\mathrm{q}}
as the outcome of a measurement of
\mathcal{Q}_{A}
.
The charge imbalance resolved negativity is defined as

\mathcal{N}(\mathrm{q})=\frac{\mathrm{Tr}|\rho_{A}^{T_{2}}(\mathrm{q})|-1}{2}.

(3.10)

The total negativity obtained by the sum of charge imbalance resolved negativity weighted by the corresponding probability

\mathcal{N}=\sum_{\mathrm{q}}p(\mathrm{q})\mathcal{N}(\mathrm{q}).

(3.11)

It is useful to define the charge imbalance resolved Rényi negativity as

\mathcal{N}_{n}(\mathrm{q})=\frac{N_{n}(\mathrm{q})-1}{2},\quad\text{with}\quad N_{n}(\mathrm{q})={\textrm{Tr}}[(\rho_{A}^{T_{2}}({\mathrm{q}}))^{n}]=\frac{{\textrm{Tr}}[\mathcal{P}_{\mathrm{q}}(\rho_{A}^{T_{2}})^{n}]}{p({\mathrm{q}})^{n}}.

(3.12)

Then the charged imbalance resolved negativity is obtained by taking limit

\mathcal{N}(\mathrm{q})=\lim_{n_{e}\to 1}\mathcal{N}_{n_{e}}(\mathrm{q}).

(3.13)

Based on the integral representation of the projector
\mathcal{P}_{\mathrm{q}}
, one finds that it’s useful to define the charged Rényi negativity and its Fourier transform

N_{n}(\mu)={\textrm{Tr}}[(\rho_{A}^{T_{2}})^{n}e^{\mathrm{i}\mu\mathcal{Q}_{A}}],\quad\mathcal{Z}_{T_{2},n}(\mathrm{q})=\int_{0}^{2\pi}\frac{d\mu}{2\pi}e^{-\mathrm{i}\mu\mathrm{q}}N_{n}(\mu).

(3.14)

The charged Rényi logarithmic negativity is simply given by

\mathcal{E}_{n}(\mu)=\log N_{n}(\mu).

(3.15)

The charged negativity is obtained by taking the replica limit
n_{e}\rightarrow 1
in
N_{n_{e}}(\mu)

N(\mu)=\lim_{n_{e}\to 1}N_{n_{e}}(\mu),\qquad\mathcal{E}(\mu)=\log N(\mu).

(3.16)

The probability distribution can be obtained as

p(\mathrm{q})=\int_{0}^{2\pi}\frac{d\mu}{2\pi}e^{-\mathrm{i}\mu{\mathrm{q}}}N_{1}(\mu)=\int_{0}^{2\pi}\frac{d\mu}{2\pi}e^{-\mathrm{i}\mu{\mathrm{q}}}e^{\mathcal{E}_{1}(\mu)}.

(3.17)

Then it is easy to derive

N_{n}({\mathrm{q}})=\frac{\mathcal{Z}_{T_{2},n}(\mathrm{q})}{p(\mathrm{q})^{n}}.

(3.18)

We also introduce the quantity

\mathcal{Z}_{T_{2}}(\mathrm{q})\equiv\lim_{n_{e}\rightarrow 1}\mathcal{Z}_{T_{2},n_{e}}(\mathrm{q})=\int_{0}^{2\pi}\frac{d\mu}{2\pi}e^{-\mathrm{i}\mu\mathrm{q}}e^{\mathcal{E}(\mu)},

(3.19)

from which we can obtain the charge imbalance resolved negativity as

\mathcal{N}(\mathrm{q})=\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{\mathcal{Z}_{T_{2}}(\mathrm{q})}{p(\mathrm{q})}-1\right)=\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{\int_{0}^{2\pi}\frac{d\mu}{2\pi}e^{-\mathrm{i}\mu\mathrm{q}}e^{\mathcal{E}(\mu)}}{\int_{0}^{2\pi}\frac{d\mu}{2\pi}e^{-\mathrm{i}\mu{\mathrm{q}}}e^{\mathcal{E}_{1}(\mu)}}-1\right).

(3.20)

Charged Rényi negativity and fluxed twist field

As shown in Fig.
3
, we consider the cases of two semi-infinite intervals (up), two adjacent intervals (middle), and two disjoint intervals (bottom). In these three cases, we will respectively calculate the dynamics of charged logarithmic negativity after a local joining quench in CFT. In this section, we will briefly review the method of computing the charged logarithmic negativity using the fluxed twist field
[
51
]
.

The connection between the charged negativity and the fluxed twist fields was first derived in reference
[
51
]
. We will review some basic facts about the charged logarithmic negativity in the
(1+1)
dimensional CFT (see
[
58
]
for more details). We assume that our system exhibits a global
U(1)
symmetry and the subsystem
A
consists of
N
disjoint intervals.

We can view the charged moments of
\rho_{A}
i.e.
Z_{n}(\mu)
(cf. eq. (
3.4
)) as the partition function on the Riemann surface
\mathcal{R}_{n,N}

pierced by an Aharonov-Bohm flux, such that the total phase accumulated by the field upon going through the entire surface is
\mu
. We can define the operator
\mathcal{V}_{\mu}
and the local twist field
\mathcal{T}_{n}
which generate the total phase
\mu
and the
n
-sheet Riemann surface
\mathcal{R}_{n,N}
, respectively. We call the fusion of
\mathcal{V}_{\mu}
and
\mathcal{T}_{n}
as fluxed twist field denoted by
\mathcal{T}_{n,\mu}

[
40
]
. Then the partition function on the fluxed Riemann surface
\mathcal{R}^{(\mu)}_{n,N}
is obtained from the
2N
-point function of these fluxed twist operators. By using the conformal mapping defined in Eq. (
2.3
), the problem of local joining quench is reduced to the computation of the correlation functions of the fluxed twist fields in the RHP. In free complex boson CFT, the conformal dimension of the fluxed twist field and the fluxed anti-twist field are the same, it reads
[
58
]

\Delta_{n,\mu}=\frac{1}{6}\left(n-\frac{1}{n}\right)-\frac{\mu^{2}}{4\pi^{2}n}+\frac{\mu}{2\pi n}.

(4.1)

Different cases we consider here: semi-infinite intervals (up), two adjacent intervals (middle) and two disjoint intervals (bottom).

For simplicity, in the following part, we will focus on the case
N=2
, i.e. we will consider the case where the subsystem
A
consists of two disjoint intervals:
A_{1}=[l_{1},l_{2}]
,
A_{2}=[l_{3},l_{4}]
and
A=A_{1}\cup A_{2}
.

For two adjacent intervals, the charged moments of the partially transposed RDM or the charged Rényi negativity equivalently has the following structure

\begin{aligned} \operatorname{Tr}[\left(\rho_{A}^{T_{2}}\right)^{n}e^{\mathrm{i}\mu\mathcal{Q}_{A}}]&=\left\langle\mathcal{T}_{n,\mu}\left(z_{1}\right)\tilde{\mathcal{T}}_{n,\mu}^{2}\left(z_{2}\right)\mathcal{T}_{n,\mu}\left(z_{3}\right)\right\rangle\end{aligned},\quad z_{i}=l_{i}+i\tau.

(4.2)

By using the conformal mapping defined in Eq. (
2.3
), the three-point function on the
z
-plane is mapped onto the RHP, we have

\displaystyle\operatorname{Tr}[\left(\rho_{A}^{T_{2}}\right)^{n}e^{\mathrm{i}\mu\mathcal{Q}_{A}}]

\displaystyle=\prod_{i=1}^{3}\left|\frac{dw}{dz}\right|_{z_{i}}^{\Delta_{(i)}}\left\langle\mathcal{T}_{n,\mu}\left(w_{1}\right)\tilde{\mathcal{T}}_{n,\mu}^{2}\left(w_{2}\right)\mathcal{T}_{n,\mu}\left(w_{3}\right)\right\rangle_{\mathrm{RHP}},

(4.3)

where the scaling dimensions
\Delta_{(1)}=\Delta_{(3)}=\Delta_{n,\mu}
and
\Delta_{(2)}=\Delta_{n,\mu}^{(2)}
. The scaling dimension
\Delta^{(2)}_{n,\mu}
of the operator
\mathcal{T}^{2}_{n,\mu}
reads
[
55
]

\Delta_{n,\mu}^{(2)}=\begin{cases}\Delta_{n,2\mu},\quad\text{odd}~n\\
2\Delta_{\frac{n}{2},\mu},\quad\text{even}~n\end{cases}

(4.4)

The three-point function on the RHP has the standard form

\begin{split}\langle\mathcal{T}_{n,\mu}(w_{1})\tilde{\mathcal{T}}^{2}_{n,\mu}(w_{2})\mathcal{T}_{n,\mu}(w_{3})\rangle_{\text{RHP}}=&\frac{c_{n,\mu}}{\prod_{i=1}^{3}|(w_{i}-\widetilde{w}_{i})/a|^{\Delta_{(i)}}}\left(\frac{\eta_{1,3}^{\Delta_{n,\mu}^{(2)}-2\Delta_{n,\mu}}}{\eta_{1,2}^{\Delta_{n,\mu}^{(2)}}\eta_{2,3}^{\Delta_{n,\mu}^{(2)}}}\right)^{1/2}\mathcal{F}_{n}(\{\eta_{j,k}\}).\end{split}

(4.5)

where the constant
c_{n,\mu}
known for some specific theories are nonuniversal and
a
is an UV cutoff. The cross ratios
\eta_{i,j}=\frac{(w_{i}-w_{j})(\widetilde{w}_{i}-\widetilde{w}_{j})}{(w_{i}-\widetilde{w}_{j})(\widetilde{w}_{i}-w_{j})}
with
\widetilde{w}_{i}=-\bar{w}_{i}
, which is the image of
w_{i}
as shown in Fig.
2
. The nonuniversal function
\mathcal{F}_{n}(\{\eta_{j,k}\})
depends on the full operator content of the theory and is very difficult to calculate.

For two disjoint intervals, the charged moments of the partially transposed RDM after doing a conformal map onto the RHP, we have

\displaystyle{\textrm{Tr}}[(\rho_{A}^{T_{2}})^{n}e^{\mathrm{i}\mu\mathcal{Q}_{A}}]

\displaystyle=\langle\mathcal{T}_{n,\mu}(z_{1})\tilde{\mathcal{T}}_{n,\mu}(z_{2})\tilde{\mathcal{T}}_{n,\mu}(z_{3})\mathcal{T}_{n,\mu}(z_{4})\rangle

(4.6)

\displaystyle=\prod_{i=1}^{4}\left|\frac{dw}{dz}\right|_{z_{i}}^{\Delta_{(i)}}\left\langle\mathcal{T}_{n,\mu}\left(w_{1}\right)\tilde{\mathcal{T}}_{n,\mu}\left(w_{2}\right)\tilde{\mathcal{T}}_{n,\mu}\left(w_{3}\right)\mathcal{T}_{n,\mu}\left(w_{4}\right)\right\rangle_{\mathrm{RHP}}

where the scaling dimensions
\Delta_{(1)}=\Delta_{(2)}=\Delta_{(3)}=\Delta_{(4)}=\Delta_{n,\mu}
.
Here, comparing to the original charged moments of the RDM, we have exchanged the fluxed twist field
\mathcal{T}_{n,\mu}
and
\tilde{\mathcal{T}}_{n,\mu}
at the endpoints of
A_{2}
while keep the others unchanged.
By global conformal symmetry, the four-point function on the RHP has the following form

\begin{split}&\left\langle\mathcal{T}_{n,\mu}\left(w_{1}\right)\tilde{\mathcal{T}}_{n,\mu}\left(w_{2}\right)\tilde{\mathcal{T}}_{n,\mu}\left(w_{3}\right)\mathcal{T}_{n,\mu}\left(w_{4}\right)\right\rangle_{\mathrm{RHP}}\\
=&\frac{c_{n,\mu}^{2}}{\prod_{i=1}^{4}|(w_{i}-\widetilde{w}_{i})/a|^{\Delta_{n,\mu}}}\frac{1}{\eta_{1,2}^{\Delta_{n,\mu}}\eta_{3,4}^{\Delta_{n,\mu}}}\left(\frac{\eta_{1,4}\eta_{2,3}}{\eta_{1,3}\eta_{2,4}}\right)^{\Delta_{n,\mu}^{(2)}/2-\Delta_{n,\mu}}\mathcal{F}_{n}(\{\eta_{j,k}\}),\end{split}

(4.7)

where the
\Delta_{n,\mu}^{(2)}
appears since that the Eq. (
4.5
) should be reproduced when we take the adjacent limit
z_{3}\rightarrow z_{2}
in Eq. (
4.7
).

5
Charged logarithmic negativity after a local joining quench

5.1
Two adjacent intervals

5.1.1
Semi-infinite intervals

As shown in Fig.
3
, it is convenient to consider the simplest case, in which the total system is made of two semi-infinite parts
A_{1}
and
A_{2}
. In this case, the time evolution of charged Rényi negativity
is governed by
\left\langle\mathcal{T}^{2}_{n,\mu}(z_{1})\right\rangle
on the strip where
\mathcal{T}^{2}_{n,\mu}(z_{1})
is a single fluxed twist field inserted at

z_{1}=l+\mathrm{i}\tau
in the z-plane. Here we choose
l=0
, we get
z_{1}=\mathrm{i}\tau
. The expectation value of
\mathcal{T}^{2}_{n,\mu}(z_{1})
has the standard form

\left\langle\mathcal{T}^{2}_{n,\mu}\left(z_{1}\right)\right\rangle={c}_{n,\mu}\left(\left|\frac{dw}{dz}\right|_{z_{1}}\frac{a}{2\operatorname{Re}\left(w_{1}\right)}\right)^{\Delta_{n,\mu}^{(2)}}.

(5.1)

After the conformal mapping defined in Eq. (
2.3
), we obtain

w_{1}=\mathrm{i}\frac{\tau}{\epsilon}+\frac{1}{\epsilon}\sqrt{\epsilon^{2}-\tau^{2}},

(5.2)

then we can get

\left|\frac{dw}{dz}\right|_{z_{1}}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{\epsilon^{2}-\tau^{2}}}.

(5.3)

Considering
\tau
as a real number, and only at the end of the computation we analytically continue
\tau\to\mathrm{i}t
. Finally, we find

\left\langle\mathcal{T}^{2}_{n,\mu}(z_{1})\right\rangle=c_{n,\mu}\left(\frac{a\epsilon}{2(\epsilon^{2}+t^{2})}\right)^{\Delta^{(2)}_{n,\mu}}.

(5.4)

Now it is straightforward to derive the charged Rényi logarithmic negativity

\mathcal{E}_{n}(\mu)=\Delta_{n,\mu}^{(2)}\ln\frac{a\epsilon}{2\left(\epsilon^{2}+t^{2}\right)}+\ln c_{n,\mu}.

(5.5)

6.3
Total negativity

To check our results, we should recover the total negativity from the charge imbalance resolved negativity from the formula

\sum_{\mathrm{q}=-\infty}^{+\infty}p(\mathrm{q})\mathcal{N}(\mathrm{q})=\frac{e^{-\frac{f}{2}}-1}{2}=\frac{e^{\mathcal{E}(\mu=0)}-1}{2}=\mathcal{N},

(6.22)

where
\mathcal{E}(\mu=0)=\mathcal{E}
is the total logarithmic negativity defined in Eq. (
1.6
).
Here we have used the following formula

\begin{split}\sum_{\mathrm{q}=-\infty}^{+\infty}p(\mathrm{q})=-\sum_{\mathrm{q}=-\infty}^{+\infty}\frac{f}{\pi^{2}\mathrm{q}^{2}+f^{2}}=-\coth(f)\xrightarrow{f\rightarrow-\infty}1.\\
\end{split}

(6.23)

Indeed we have recovered the known quench dynamics of the total negativity from the charge imbalance resolved ones, confirming our results.

7.2
Comparison between CFT predictions and numerical results

The quasi-particle picture of entanglement negativity after a local joining quench has been proposed in Ref.
[
57
]
. In CFT all the quasi-particles propagate at the speed of light
v_{c}
, while in the lattice model the situation is different. The dispersion relation in Eq. (
7.5
) can be written as

\omega(p)=\sqrt{\omega^{2}+4\sin(\frac{p}{2})^{2}},\quad p\equiv\frac{\pi k}{L},

(7.22)

from which it is easy to calculate the group velocities

v\equiv\frac{\partial\omega(p)}{\partial p}=\frac{\sin(p)}{\sqrt{\omega^{2}+4\sin^{2}(p/2)}}.

(7.23)

It’s easy to see that not all the quasi-particles have the same group velocities. In detail, the high-energy quasi-particles have the group velocities
v<v_{c}
. Following Ref.
[
57
]
, as shown in Fig.
8
, we can divide the entangled pairs into four classes according to their group velocities
(v_{L},v_{R})

(v_{L},v_{R})\simeq\left\{\begin{aligned} &(-v,v),&\text{slow-slow pair}\\
&(-v,v_{c}),&\text{slow-fast pair}\\
&(-v_{c},v),&\text{fast-slow pair}\\
&(-v_{c},v_{c}),&\text{fast-fast pair}\\
\end{aligned}\right.

(7.24)

8
Conclusion

In this paper, we studied the dynamics of the charge imbalance resolved entanglement negativity after a local joining quench in
1+1
dimensional free complex boson CFT. Firstly, we use the conformal mapping approach of local joining quenches to calculate the time evolution the charge-resolved negativity. In this quench protocol, the entangled pairs are generated at the joining point and then propagate freely through the system. Secondly, we compare our CFT predictions with the numerical results for quench dynamics of charged logarithmic negativity and probability. Finally, we explained the phenomenon based on the quasi-particle picture.

It will be an interesting problem to see how our results are changed in finite temperatures in the same quench protocol discussed in this paper. More interestingly, one could consider the non-equilibrium evolution of charge resolved entanglement where two CFTs are initially prepared at different temperatures suddenly joined together. In this setting, a non-equilibrium steady state should exist at late times
[
65
]
.

Intrinsic phenomena of delta shock waves in a more realistic Chaplygin Aw-Rascle model
Priyanka, M. Zafar

Abstract
. The motivation of this study is to find the Riemann solutions of Aw-Rascle model with a more realistic version of extended Chaplygin gas. Firstly, we establish the Riemann solutions with two different structures, viz., a shock wave followed by the contact discontinuity and a rarefaction wave followed by the contact discontinuity. Further, by analyzing the limiting behavior, it is found that one of the Riemann solutions converges to δ \delta -shock solution as the pressure approaches to generalized Chaplygin gas pressure. Moreover, numerical simulations have been performed to validate the theoretical analysis. Keywords. Riemann problem; Chaplygin gas; Delta shock; Transport equations; Contact discontinuity

S:\begin{cases}\sigma_{1}=\upsilon_{l}-\frac{\varrho_{*}(p_{*}-p_{l})}{\varrho_{*}-\varrho_{l}}=\upsilon_{*}-\frac{\varrho_{l}(p_{*}-p_{l})}{\varrho_{*}-\varrho_{l}},\\
\upsilon_{*}+p_{*}=\upsilon_{l}+p_{l},\qquad\varrho_{*}>\varrho_{l},\end{cases}

and

(33)

J:\,\sigma_{2}=\upsilon_{*}=\upsilon_{r},\qquad\upsilon_{*}=\upsilon_{r}.

From
\eqref{p28}_{2}
and
(
33
)
,
\varrho_{*}
satisfies

(34)

\upsilon_{r}=-p_{*}+\upsilon_{l}+p_{l}.

On employing
\lim_{a,A\to 0}
in (
34
), with the assumption that
\lim_{a,A\to 0}\varrho_{*}=\infty,
one can obtain
\upsilon_{r}\leqslant\upsilon_{l}-\frac{B}{\varrho_{l}^{\kappa}};
which gives a contradiction with
\upsilon_{l}-\frac{B}{\varrho_{l}^{\kappa}}<\upsilon_{r}.
Hence,
\lim_{a,A\to 0}\varrho_{*}=\text{finite}.

3.2.
Limit of Riemann solutions when (
\varrho_{r}
,
\upsilon_{r}
) lies in region-II

We study the limit
a,A\to 0
of the Riemann solution to (
1
)-(
2
) with (
4
) in the case
\upsilon_{l}\leq\upsilon_{r}
. For any
a>0
and
A>0
, the intermediate state
(\varrho_{*},\upsilon_{*})
is connected with
(\varrho_{l},\upsilon_{l})
by
R
and
(\varrho_{r},\upsilon_{r})
by
J
, respectively. Then, we have

(35)

R:\begin{cases}\zeta=\lambda_{1}=\upsilon-\varrho p^{\prime},\\
\upsilon+p=\upsilon_{l}+p_{l},\qquad\varrho_{*}<\varrho<\varrho_{l},\end{cases}

and

(36)

J:\,\sigma_{2}=\upsilon_{*}=\upsilon_{r},\qquad\upsilon_{*}=\upsilon_{r}.

From
\eqref{p31}_{2}
and
(
36
)
,
\varrho_{*}
satisfies

(37)

\upsilon_{r}=-p_{*}+\upsilon_{l}+p_{l}.

Taking
\lim_{a,A\to 0}
in (
37
), with the consideration that
\lim_{a,A\to 0}\varrho_{*}=0,
one can obtain
\upsilon_{r}=+\infty,
which is absurd. Hence, there is no vacuum in the Riemann solution of the system (
1
)-(
2
) and (
4
).

Numerical Simulations

Here, we present numerical simulations to verify our theoretical analysis. The system (
1
)-(
2
) can be written as

(38)

U_{t}+BU_{x}=0,

where

(39)

U=\begin{bmatrix}\varrho\\
\upsilon\end{bmatrix},\qquad B=\begin{bmatrix}\upsilon&\varrho\\
0&\upsilon-\varrho p^{\prime}\end{bmatrix}.

Further, we have

(40)

B=R\Lambda L,

with

(41)

R=\begin{bmatrix}\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}&-\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}\\
0&1\end{bmatrix},\qquad\Lambda=\begin{bmatrix}\upsilon&0\\
0&\upsilon-\varrho p^{\prime}\end{bmatrix},\qquad L=\begin{bmatrix}p^{\prime}&1\\
0&1\end{bmatrix}.

Now, we employ the first order upwind scheme (see,
[
19
,
4
,
18
]

(42)

(43)

B_{j}^{-}=\frac{B_{j}^{n}-|B_{j}^{n}|}{2},\qquad B_{j}^{+}=\frac{B_{j}^{n}+|B_{j}^{n}|}{2},\qquad|B_{j}^{n}|=R_{j}^{n}|\Lambda_{j}^{n}|L_{j}^{n}.

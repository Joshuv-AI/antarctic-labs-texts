Single-quadrature quantum magnetometry in cavity electromagnonics
M. S. Ebrahimi, Ali Motazedifard, M. Bagheri Harouni

Abstract
A scheme of an ultrasensitive magnetometer in the cavity quantum electromagnonics is proposed, where the intracavity microwave mode is coupled to a magnonic mode via magnetic dipole interaction. It is shown that by driving both magnonic and microwave modes with external classical fields and controlling the system parameters, one can reduce the added noise of magnetic field measurement below the standard quantum limit (SQL). Surprisingly, we show that beyond the rotating wave approximation (RWA), not only can the added noise be suppressed but also the output cavity response to the input signal can be substantially amplified in order to achieve a precise magnetic field measurement. The estimated theoretical sensitivity of the proposed magnetic amplifier-sensor is approximately on the order of 10 − 18 ​ T / Hz 10^{-18}\rm{T/\sqrt{\rm Hz}} , which is competitive compared to the current state-of-the-art magnetometers like superconducting quantum interference devices (SQUIDs) and atomic magnetometers. The advantages of the proposed sensor in comparison with the other magnetometers is its high sensitivity at room temperature, sensing in a wide range of frequencies up to MHz, and its capability for signal-response amplification.

Motivated by the above-mentioned investigations on force sensing in optomechanics and capability of magnetic sensing in electromagnonic systems as well as the current challenges in magnetometers such as low frequency and high temperature, here, we theoretically propose a feasible experimental scheme of a sensor for high-precision quantum magnetometry. We consider an electromagnonic system whose magnons as quantum excitations of spin wave interact with microwave photons via the magnetic dipole interaction.
In the proposed system, both magnonic and microwave cavity modes are externally driven by classical fields. We analyze the system both beyond and under the RWA and show that beyond the RWA by controlling system parameters, the added noise of magnetic measurement can be suppressed far below the SQL. SENSITIVITY, SIGNAL-TO-NOISE RATIO, and experimental discussion

In this section, we calculate the SNR and also the sensitivity of the system. Beyond the RWA, we can write the output phase quadrature of the cavity field as

\displaystyle P^{\mathrm{out}}_{a}(\omega)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle P^{\mathrm{out}}_{a}(\omega)|_{B=0}+i\mathcal{A}(\omega)\tilde{B}_{1}(\omega)\eta\sqrt{\frac{2}{\kappa_{m}}}

(38)

\displaystyle+\mathcal{B}(\omega)\tilde{B}_{2}(\omega)\eta\sqrt{\frac{2}{\kappa_{m}}},\

where

\displaystyle P^{\mathrm{out}}_{a}(\omega)|_{B=0}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}(\omega)X^{\mathrm{in}}_{m}(\omega)+\mathcal{B}(\omega)P^{\mathrm{in}}_{m}(\omega)+\mathcal{C}(\omega)X^{\mathrm{in}}_{a}(\omega)

(39)

\displaystyle+\mathcal{D}(\omega)P^{\mathrm{in}}_{a}(\omega)

is the contribution of the output phase quadrature in the absence of the external magnetic field (that we are going to sense).
\tilde{B}_{1}(\omega)
and
\tilde{B}_{2}(\omega)
are the contribution of the external magnetic field and are defined as follows

\displaystyle\tilde{B}_{1}(\omega)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}[B(\omega+\omega_{d})-B(\omega-\omega_{d})],

(40)

\displaystyle\tilde{B}_{2}(\omega)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}[B(\omega+\omega_{d})+B(\omega-\omega_{d})].

(41)

In order to calculate the sensitivity of the device to the external magnetic field and the SNR, we follow the method used in Ref.
5
. To this end, we define the magnetic field operators as

\displaystyle\delta\tilde{B}_{1}(\omega)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{P^{\mathrm{out}}_{a}(\omega)}{\partial P^{\mathrm{out}}_{a}(\omega)/\partial\tilde{B}_{1}(\omega)}=\delta\hat{N}_{1}+\tilde{B}_{1}(\omega),

(42)

\displaystyle\delta\tilde{B}_{2}(\omega)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{P^{\mathrm{out}}_{a}(\omega)}{\partial P^{\mathrm{out}}_{a}(\omega)/\partial\tilde{B}_{2}(\omega)}=\delta\hat{N}_{2}+\tilde{B}_{2}(\omega),

(43)

in which
\delta\hat{N}_{1}(\omega)
and
\delta\hat{N}_{2}(\omega)
are the noise operators and affect the system through two different channels
\hat{X}^{\mathrm{in}}_{m}(\omega)
and
\hat{P}^{\mathrm{in}}_{m}(\omega)
independently and are defined as follows:

\displaystyle\delta\hat{N}_{1}(\omega)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\mathcal{A}(\omega)\hat{X}^{\mathrm{in}}_{m}+\mathcal{B}(\omega)\hat{P}^{\mathrm{in}}_{m}+\mathcal{C}(\omega)\hat{X}^{\mathrm{in}}_{a}+\mathcal{D}(\omega)\hat{P}^{\mathrm{in}}_{a}}{i\mathcal{A}(\omega)\eta\sqrt{\frac{2}{\kappa_{m}}}},

(44)

\displaystyle\delta\hat{N}_{2}(\omega)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\mathcal{A}(\omega)\hat{X}^{\mathrm{in}}_{m}+\mathcal{B}(\omega)\hat{P}^{\mathrm{in}}_{m}+\mathcal{C}(\omega)\hat{X}^{\mathrm{in}}_{a}+\mathcal{D}(\omega)\hat{P}^{\mathrm{in}}_{a}}{\mathcal{B}(\omega)\eta\sqrt{\frac{2}{\kappa_{m}}}}.

(45)

Considering the probability amplitudes of the noise injection through different channels, the total noise operator can be written as follows

\delta\hat{N}(\omega)=P_{1}\delta\hat{N}_{1}(\omega)+P_{2}\delta\hat{N}_{2}(\omega),

(46)

where the probability amplitudes of noise injection (
P_{1}
and
P_{2}
) through
\hat{X}^{\mathrm{in}}_{m}
and
\hat{P}^{\mathrm{in}}_{m}
are given by

\displaystyle P_{1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\mathcal{A}(\omega)}{\sqrt{|\mathcal{A}(\omega)|^{2}+|\mathcal{B}(\omega)|^{2}}},

(47)

\displaystyle P_{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\mathcal{B}(\omega)}{\sqrt{|\mathcal{A}(\omega)|^{2}+|\mathcal{B}(\omega)|^{2}}}.

(48)

Therefore, the total noise operator is obtained as follows:

\displaystyle\delta\hat{N}(\omega)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{(1-i)}{\eta}\sqrt{\frac{\kappa_{m}}{2}}

\displaystyle(\frac{\mathcal{A}(\omega)\hat{X}^{\mathrm{in}}_{m}+\mathcal{B}(\omega)\hat{P}^{\mathrm{in}}_{m}+\mathcal{C}(\omega)\hat{X}^{\mathrm{in}}_{a}+\mathcal{D}(\omega)\hat{P}^{\mathrm{in}}_{a}}{\sqrt{|\mathcal{A}(\omega)|^{2}+|\mathcal{B}(\omega)|^{2}}}),

which yields to noise power spectrum as

\displaystyle S_{N}(\omega)=\frac{1}{2}\langle\delta\hat{N}(\omega)\delta\hat{N}(\omega)^{\dagger}\rangle,

(50)

which can be simplified as

S_{N}(\omega)=\frac{\kappa_{m}}{\eta^{2}}[(\bar{n}_{m}+\frac{1}{2})+n_{\mathrm{add}}(\omega)].

(51)

SNR, the ratio of the signal to the variance of the noise, can be written as

r(\omega)=\frac{|\tilde{B}(\omega)|}{\sqrt{S_{N}(\omega)}}=\frac{|\tilde{B}(\omega)|}{\frac{\sqrt{\kappa_{m}}}{\eta}[(\bar{n}_{m}+\frac{1}{2})+n_{\mathrm{add}}(\omega)]^{\frac{1}{2}}}.

(52)

The sensitivity or the minimum detectable input of the device is the minimum magnitude of the input signal required to produce an output with
r(\omega)=1
. Therefore, sensitivity of the system beyond the RWA,
\mathcal{S}(\omega)=\sqrt{S_{N}(\omega)}
, is simplified as

\mathcal{S}(\omega)^{\rm bRWA}=\frac{\sqrt{\kappa_{m}}}{\eta}[(\bar{n}_{m}+\frac{1}{2})+n_{\mathrm{add}}(\omega)]^{\frac{1}{2}}.

(53)

The feasible experimental parameters beyond the RWA
17
are
\omega_{m}/2\pi\simeq\omega_{a}/2\pi=37.5~\rm GHz
,
g_{0}/2\pi=2.5~\rm GHz
,
\kappa_{m}/2\pi=15~\rm MHz
, and
\kappa_{a}/2\pi=33~\rm MHz
. This coupled microwave photon–magnon system that yields to a ratio of
g_{0}/\omega_{a(m)}=0.067
has been realized by placing a YIG sphere (which spin and orbital angular momentum of its unit cell are respectively
s=\frac{5}{2}
and
l=0

26
) with diameter of
2.5~\rm mm
inside a microwave cavity with dimension

and with a bias magnetic field as

in which
\mathcal{E}
can be controlled so that the condition
g=g_{0}\mathcal{E}\ll\ \Omega(\Omega=2\omega_{L})
to be satisfied.
It is worth pointing out that in practice, for controlling the
\mathcal{E}
and therefore the magnon–photon coupling strength one can use a Josephson parametric amplifier (JPA). VI
SUMMARY, CONCLUSION, and outlooks

In the present contribution, a scheme of a magnetic sensor based on the cavity electromagnonic has been proposed in which magnons and photons interact via the magnetic dipole interaction. It has been shown that beyond the RWA by choosing the suitable system parameters, one can simultaneously achieve signal amplification and added noise suppression below the SQL in order to precise magnetic measurement. The advantages of the proposed sensor is that our magnetometer can work in a wide range of frequencies at the room temperature with high sensitivity, which is even better than the current SQUIDs and atomic-based magnetometers that need cooling and work at high-frequency regimes, respectively.
These properties means that the proposed sensor can be competitive compared to the SQUIDs and atomic-based systems and might be commercial in the future, or used for fundamental applications such as searching the dark matter axion.

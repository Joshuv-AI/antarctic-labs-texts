Abstract
This article focuses on Chemin’s space 𝒮 h ′ \mathcal{S}^{\prime}_{h} of homogeneous distributions, which was introduced to serve as a basis for realizations of subcritical homogeneous Besov spaces. We will discuss how this construction fails in multiple ways for supercritical spaces. In particular, we study its intersection X h := 𝒮 h ′ ∩ X X_{h}:=\mathcal{S}^{\prime}_{h}\cap X with various Banach spaces X X , namely supercritical homogeneous Besov spaces and the Lebesgue space L ∞ L^{\infty} . For each X X , we find out if the intersection X h X_{h} is dense in X X . If it is not, then we study its closure C = clos ⁡ ( X h ) C={\rm clos}(X_{h}) and prove that the quotient X / C X/C is not separable and that C C is not complemented in X X .

Proposition 11

Consider

Proof.

The link between the two statements is this: the brackets of

All we have to do is show that the value of

\begin{split}\psi_{\lambda}*f(x)&=\psi_{\lambda}*f(0)+x\cdot\int_{0}^{1}\nabla\psi_{\lambda}*f(tx){\rm d}t=\psi_{\lambda}*f(0)+\frac{x}{\lambda}\cdot\int_{0}^{1}(\nabla\psi)_{\lambda}*f(tx){\rm d}t\\
&=\psi_{\lambda}*f(0)+O\left(\frac{|x|}{\lambda}\right),\end{split}

where the constant in the

(A_{\theta})_{\theta\in\mathbb{R}}
are all infinite and any two of these sets must have finite intersection.
∎

In particular, the subspaces
\ell^{\infty}(A_{i})
of
\ell^{\infty}
sequences which are compactly supported in
A_{i}
have the following properties: for
i\neq j
,

\ell^{\infty}(A_{i})\nsubseteq c_{0}\qquad\text{and}\qquad\ell^{\infty}(A_{i})\cap\ell^{\infty}(A_{j})\subset c_{0}.

In what follows, we will transport these spaces into
\dot{B}^{s}_{p,\infty}
by means of a well chosen map: recall
J:\ell^{\infty}\tend\dot{B}^{s}_{p,\infty}
from Theorem
16
, which we have seen to satisfy
J^{-1}(C^{s}_{p})=c_{0}
so that
Ju\in C^{s}_{p}
if and only if
u\in c_{0}
. This implies that the image spaces
J\big(\ell^{\infty}(A_{i})\big)
satisfy

J\big(\ell^{\infty}(A_{i})\big)\nsubseteq C^{s}_{p}\qquad\text{and}\qquad J\big(\ell^{\infty}(A_{i})\cap\ell^{\infty}(A_{j})\big)\subset C^{s}_{p}

and we see that the spaces
J\big(\ell^{\infty}(A_{i})\big)
will be well-suited for our purpose.

STEP 2
. Consider a nonzero bounded operator
T:\dot{B}^{s}_{p,\infty}\tend\dot{B}^{s}_{p,\infty}
such that
C^{s}_{p}\subset\ker(T)
. We will prove the existence of a
i\in I
such that
J\big(\ell^{\infty}(A_{i})\big)\subset\ker(T)
.

Assume, in order to obtain a contradiction, that none of the
J\big(\ell^{\infty}(A_{i})\big)
lie in the kernel of
T
. Therefore, for every
i\in I
, we may find a
u_{i}\in\ell^{\infty}(A_{i})
such that
TJu_{i}\neq 0
. In addition, we may assume
u
to be in the unit ball
\|u\|_{\ell^{\infty}}\leq 1
.

Next, because the predual space
\dot{B}^{-s}_{p^{\prime},1}
of
\dot{B}^{s}_{p,\infty}
is separable (remember that
p>1
for now), we may fix a sequence
(g_{n})_{n\geq 1}
which forms a dense subset of the unit ball of
\dot{B}^{-s}_{p^{\prime},1}
. Then

\begin{split}I&=\{i\in I,\;TJu_{i}\neq 0\}=\bigcup_{n\geq 0}\left\{i\in I,\quad\left\langle TJu_{i},g_{n}\right\rangle_{\dot{B}^{s}_{p,\infty}\times\dot{B}^{-s}_{p^{\prime},1}}\neq 0\right\}\\
&=\bigcup_{n,k\geq 0}\left\{i\in I,\quad\left|\left\langle TJu_{i},g_{n}\right\rangle_{\dot{B}^{s}_{p,\infty}\times\dot{B}^{-s}_{p^{\prime},1}}\right|\geq\frac{1}{k+1}\right\}:=\bigcup_{n,k\geq 0}I_{n,k}.\end{split}

Because
I
is uncountable and is the countable union of the
I_{n,k}
, there must exist indices
n,k\geq 0
such that the set
I_{n,k}
is also uncountable: in particular, there exists an infinite number of
i\in I
such that the bracket
\left\langle TJu_{i},g_{n}\right\rangle_{\dot{B}^{s}_{p,\infty}\times\dot{B}^{-s}_{p^{\prime},1}}
is not small.

To take advantage of this last fact, we will construct a linear combination of the
u_{i}
which will make the bracket become arbitrarily large. Fix a finite subset
F\subset I_{k,n}
and define the sequence

y=\sum_{i\in F}\alpha_{i}u_{i},

where the
\alpha_{i}
are chosen so that the bracket
\langle TJy,g_{n}\rangle_{\dot{B}^{s}_{p,\infty}\times\dot{B}^{-s}_{p^{\prime},1}}
becomes large:

\alpha_{i}=\frac{\left\langle TJu_{i},g_{n}\right\rangle_{\dot{B}^{s}_{p,\infty}\times\dot{B}^{-s}_{p^{\prime},1}}}{|\left\langle TJu_{i},g_{n}\right\rangle_{\dot{B}^{s}_{p,\infty}\times\dot{B}^{-s}_{p^{\prime},1}}|}=\pm 1.

Therefore

(14)

\left|\langle TJy,g_{n}\rangle_{\dot{B}^{s}_{p,\infty}\times\dot{B}^{-s}_{p^{\prime},1}}\right|\geq\frac{|F|}{k+1},

and this lower bound may be made as large as desired by taking
|F|
as large as needed, the set
I_{k,n}
being uncountably infinite. On the other hand, because the subsets
A_{i}
have finite intersection, we may decompose the union of the
A_{i}
as
\cup_{i\in F}A_{i}=A\sqcup B
where

(15)

B=\bigcup_{i\neq j}(A_{i}\cap A_{j})

the union ranging on all
i,j\in F
such that
i\neq j
, is a finite set and any
m\in A
is in exactly one of the
A_{i}
. By setting
a=\mathds{1}_{A}y
and
b=\mathds{1}_{B}y
, we see that
y=a+b
with
\|a\|_{\ell^{\infty}}\leq 1
and
b
having finite support. Since
b
has finite support,
Jb\in C^{s}_{p}
and
TJy=TJa
, so

(16)

\left|\left\langle TJu_{i},g_{n}\right\rangle_{\dot{B}^{s}_{p,\infty}\times\dot{B}^{-s}_{p^{\prime},1}}\right|\leq\|T\|.

By comparing (
14
) and (
16
), we have obtained the contradiction we were seeking, since the set
F
can be chosen as large as desired,
I_{k,n}
being uncountably infinite.

STEP 3
. We may now end the proof of Theorem
17
.

Proof of Theorem
17
.

Assume on the contrary that
C^{s}_{p}
has a topological supplementary
\dot{B}^{s}_{p,\infty}=C^{s}_{p}\oplus G
and let
T:\dot{B}^{s}_{p,\infty}\tend G
be the associated projection on

and step 2 gives a

. But this is a contradiction: Theorem

cannot lie in

.
∎

7
The Critical Case: the Intersection
\mathcal{S}^{\prime}_{h}\cap L^{\infty}

So far in our study, it appears that the space
L^{\infty}
of bounded functions plays a critical role in that it lies at the interface between the two very different behaviors the homogeneous Besov spaces have:
L^{\infty}
is in the center of the chain of embeddings

\textstyle{\dot{B}^{0}_{\infty,1}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\subset}
\textstyle{L^{\infty}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{L^{\infty}/\,\mathbb{R}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\subset}
\textstyle{\dot{B}^{0}_{\infty,\infty},}

while on the one hand
\dot{B}^{0}_{\infty,1}\subset\mathcal{S}^{\prime}_{h}
, and on the other
\dot{B}^{0}_{\infty,\infty}
gathers all the properties described in Theorem
16
and
17
. A very natural question is whether the space
\mathcal{S}^{\prime}_{h}\cap L^{\infty}
has an analogous role in the structure of
L^{\infty}
as
C^{0}_{\infty}
did for the Besov space
\dot{B}^{0}_{\infty,\infty}
.

7.1
The Intersection is Closed

Our first answer shows a difference between
L^{\infty}
and Besov spaces. While
\mathcal{S}^{\prime}_{h}\cap\dot{B}^{0}_{\infty,\infty}
was not closed in
\dot{B}^{0}_{\infty,\infty}
, the intersection
\mathcal{S}^{\prime}_{h}\cap L^{\infty}
is.

Proposition 19
.

The space
L^{\infty}\cap\mathcal{S}^{\prime}_{h}
is closed in
L^{\infty}
for the strong topology.

Proof.

Let
(f_{n})_{n\geq 0}
be a converging sequence of functions in
L^{\infty}\cap\mathcal{S}^{\prime}_{h}
whose limit is
f\in L^{\infty}
. We have, for all
\phi\in\mathcal{S}
,

\displaystyle\left|\left\langle\chi(\lambda D)f,\phi\right\rangle_{L^{\infty}\times L^{1}}\right|

\displaystyle\leq\left|\left\langle\chi(\lambda D)(f-f_{n}),\phi\right\rangle_{L^{\infty}\times L^{1}}\right|+\left|\left\langle\chi(\lambda D)f_{n},\phi\right\rangle_{L^{\infty}\times L^{1}}\right|

\displaystyle\leq\left\|\chi(\lambda D)(f-f_{n})\right\|_{L^{\infty}}\|\phi\|_{L^{1}}+\left|\left\langle\chi(\lambda D)f_{n},\phi\right\rangle_{\mathcal{S}^{\prime}\times\mathcal{S}}\right|.

The fact that the
\chi(\lambda D)f_{n}
converge to
0
in
\mathcal{S}^{\prime}
as
\lambda\rightarrow+\infty
shows that we have,

\forall n\geq 0,\qquad\limss_{\lambda\rightarrow+\infty}\left|\left\langle\chi(\lambda D)f,\phi\right\rangle_{\mathcal{S}^{\prime}\times\mathcal{S}}\right|\leq C\left\|(f-f_{n})\right\|_{L^{\infty}}\|\phi\|_{L^{1}}.

This term has limit
0
as
n\rightarrow+\infty
so that
f
indeed lies in
L^{\infty}\cap\mathcal{S}^{\prime}
.
∎

7.2
Non-Separability of the Quotient

Theorem 20
.

The quotient space
L^{\infty}/(\mathcal{S}^{\prime}_{h}\cap L^{\infty})
is not separable. In fact, there exists an embedding
J:\ell^{\infty}\tend L^{\infty}
which defines a quasi-isometry between the (non-separable) quotient spaces
J^{\prime}:\ell^{\infty}/c_{0}\tend L^{\infty}/(\mathcal{S}^{\prime}_{h}\cap L^{\infty})
.

Proof.

To construct the map
J:\ell^{\infty}\tend L^{\infty}
, the idea is to take advantage of Proposition
11
which states that the space
\mathcal{S}^{\prime}_{h}
is characterized by convergence of a family of average values: if
\chi
is the Littlewood-Paley decomposition function, as in Subsection
2.2
and
\psi\in\mathcal{S}
such that
\widehat{\psi}=\chi
, then a bounded function
f\in L^{\infty}
is in
\mathcal{S}^{\prime}_{h}
if and only if there is convergence of the average values

\langle f,\psi_{\lambda}\rangle_{L^{\infty}\times L^{1}}=\int f(x)\,\psi\left(\frac{x}{\lambda}\right)\frac{\,{\rm d}x}{\lambda^{d}}\tend_{\lambda\rightarrow+\infty}0.

For any
u\in\ell^{\infty}
, we will construct a function
Hu\in L^{\infty}
whose average values
\langle f,\psi_{\lambda}\rangle_{L^{\infty}\times L^{1}}
will share accumulation points with the sequence
u
when
\lambda\rightarrow+\infty
.

Consider an increasing sequence
(r_{m})_{m}
of radii (which we will fix later on) with
r_{0}=0
and define a family of annuli by
\mathcal{C}_{m}=\{r_{m}\leq|x|\leq r_{m+1}\}
. For every sequence
u\in\ell^{\infty}
, we set

Ju=\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}u(m)\mathds{1}_{\mathcal{C}_{m}},

where the sum is to be understood in the sense of pointwise convergence. First of all, the map
J:\ell^{\infty}\tend L^{\infty}
is bounded. Next, if
u\in c_{0}
, then
Ju
has limit zero at
|x|\rightarrow+\infty
, and so
Ju\in C_{0}\subset\mathcal{S}^{\prime}_{h}\cap L^{\infty}
(Example
9
shows that the space
C_{0}
of continuous function that tend to zero at infinity lies in
\mathcal{S}^{\prime}_{h}
). The inclusion
J(c_{0})\subset\ker(J)
allows us to define a quotient map
J^{\prime}
such that the following diagram commutes:

\textstyle{\ell^{\infty}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{J}
\textstyle{L^{\infty}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{\ell^{\infty}/c_{0}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{J^{\prime}}
\textstyle{L^{\infty}/(\mathcal{S}^{\prime}_{h}\cap L^{\infty})}

To fall back on the arguments of Theorem
16
, we must now show the converse: that if
Ju\in\mathcal{S}^{\prime}_{h}\cap L^{\infty}
then we must have
u\in c_{0}
. For this, we prove an inequality that is quite analogous to (
11
). We show that

(17)

\forall f\in L^{\infty},\qquad\limss_{\lambda\rightarrow+\infty}\left|\langle f,\psi_{\lambda}\rangle_{L^{\infty}\times L^{1}}\right|\leq{\rm dist}(f,\mathcal{S}^{\prime}_{h}\cap L^{\infty}):=\inf_{h\in\mathcal{S}^{\prime}_{h}\cap L^{\infty}}\|f-h\|_{L^{\infty}}.

The argument is
mutatis mutandi
the same as for (
11
). On the one hand, in light of Proposition
11
, it is clear that for any
h\in\mathcal{S}^{\prime}_{h}\cap L^{\infty}
we must have
\langle h,\psi_{\lambda}\rangle_{L^{\infty}\times L^{1}}\tend 0
. On the other hand, for
f\in L^{\infty}
and
h\in\mathcal{S}^{\prime}_{h}\cap L^{\infty}
, we have

\left|\langle f,\psi_{\lambda}\rangle_{L^{\infty}\times L^{1}}\right|\leq\|f-h\|_{L^{\infty}}\|\psi\|_{L^{1}}+\left|\langle h,\psi_{\lambda}\rangle_{L^{\infty}\times L^{1}}\right|.

By taking the limit
\lambda\rightarrow+\infty
, we deduce (
17
).

With (
17
) at our disposal, we may show that
Ju\notin\mathcal{S}^{\prime}_{h}\cap L^{\infty}
if
u
does not belong to
c_{0}
. We start by fixing a
u\in\ell^{\infty}
. Consider a
\lambda>0
whose value will be decided later, we have

(18)

\int\psi_{\lambda}Ju=\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}u(m)\int_{\mathcal{C}_{m}}\psi_{\lambda}=\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}u(m)\int_{\lambda^{-1}\mathcal{C}_{m}}\psi.

Let
\epsilon>0
and fix a
R>0
such that

\|\mathds{1}_{|x|\geq R}\psi(x)\|_{L^{1}}=\|\mathds{1}_{|x|\geq R\lambda}\psi_{\lambda}(x)\|_{L^{1}}\leq\epsilon.

On the one hand, the terms of (
18
) at high indices
m
will be small. More precisely,

(19)

\left|\sum_{r_{m}\geq\lambda R}u(m)\int_{\lambda^{-1}\mathcal{C}_{m}}\psi\right|\leq\|u\|_{\ell^{\infty}}\int_{|x|\geq R}|\psi(x)|{\rm d}x\leq\epsilon\|u\|_{\ell^{\infty}}.

On the other hand, terms at low indices will also be small if
\lambda
is large, because the annuli
\lambda^{-1}\mathcal{C}_{m}
have a small measure: fix a
M\geq 0
, then

(20)

\left|\sum_{m=0}^{M-1}u(m)\int_{\lambda^{-1}\mathcal{C}_{m}}\psi\right|\leq\|u\|_{\ell^{\infty}}\|\psi\|_{L^{\infty}}\frac{1}{\lambda^{d}}\sum_{m=0}^{M-1}|\mathcal{C}_{m}|=\|u\|_{\ell^{\infty}}\|\psi\|_{L^{\infty}}\left(\frac{r_{M}}{\lambda}\right)^{d}.

We set the values of the
r_{m}
, and
\lambda
so that both sums (
19
) and (
20
) are negligible and only the contribution of one term matters. Fix a
M\geq 0
and set

r_{m}=2^{m^{2}}\qquad\text{and}\qquad\lambda=r_{M+1}

so that
r_{M}/\lambda=O(4^{-M})
. The sum in (
19
) ranges on all indices
m
such that
r_{m}\geq r_{M+1}R
, that is all
m\geq 0
with

2^{m^{2}}\geq 2^{2M+1}2^{M^{2}}R.

Therefore, any
m\geq M+1
is included in that sum if
M
is taken large enough that
2^{2M+1}R>1
. For such
M
, we may bound the difference between the full sum (
18
) and the
M
-th term:

\left|\int\psi_{\lambda}Ju-u(M)\int_{\mathcal{C}_{M}}\psi_{\lambda}\right|\leq\|u\|_{\ell^{\infty}}\left(\epsilon+\frac{\|\psi\|_{L^{\infty}}}{4^{M}}\right).

Finally, the principal term is equal to
u(M)\int\psi
up to a small remainder: by using exactly the same bound as in (
19
) and (
20
), we see that

\left|\int_{{}^{c}\mathcal{C}_{M}}\psi_{\lambda}\right|\leq\epsilon+\frac{\|\psi\|_{L^{\infty}}}{4^{M}}.

Therefore, by taking small
\epsilon
, large
M
and
\lambda=r_{M+1}
, we see that we can make the bracket
\langle Ju,\psi_{\lambda}\rangle_{L^{\infty}\times L^{1}}
arbitrarily close to any
u(M)
, so all the accumulation points of
u
are also accumulation points of the bracket as
\lambda\rightarrow+\infty
. We deduce:

\|u\|_{\ell^{\infty}/c_{0}}=\limss_{M\rightarrow+\infty}|u(M)|\leq\limss_{\lambda\rightarrow+\infty}\left|\langle Ju,\psi_{\lambda}\rangle_{L^{\infty}\times L^{1}}\right|,

which ends the proof.
∎

7.3
Non-Complementation of the Intersection

In this final Section, we exploit the embedding
J:\ell^{\infty}/c_{0}\tend L^{\infty}/(\mathcal{S}^{\prime}_{h}\cap L^{\infty})
we have constructed in Theorem
20
above to show that
\mathcal{S}^{\prime}_{h}\cap L^{\infty}
is not complemented in
L^{\infty}
.

Theorem 21
.

The space
\mathcal{S}^{\prime}_{h}\cap L^{\infty}
is not complemented in
L^{\infty}
. There exists no decomposition
L^{\infty}=(\mathcal{S}^{\prime}_{h}\cap L^{\infty})\oplus G
with continuous projections.

The proof of Theorem
21
will be very similar to that of Theorem
17
. In fact, we may give an abstract general principle which captures the essence of Whitley’s argument and “lifts” it to another Banach space
X
provided it contains a “suitable” copy of
\ell^{\infty}
. Theorem
21
is a direct consequence of Theorem
20
and the following Proposition.

Proposition 22
.

Let
X
be a Banach space that has the following property: there exists a countable family
(g_{n})_{n\geq 1}
of bounded linear functionals
g_{n}\in X^{\prime}
such that

(21)

\forall f\in X,\qquad\Big(\forall n\geq 1,\;\langle g_{n},f\rangle_{X^{\prime}\times X}=0\Big)\;\Rightarrow\;f=0.

In particular, if
X
has a separable predual, then this property is fulfilled. Now, consider a closed subspace
E\subset X
and assume the existence of a bounded map
J:\ell^{\infty}\tend X
that defines an embedding of the quotient
J^{\prime}:\ell^{\infty}/c_{0}\tend X/E
such that the diagram

\textstyle{\ell^{\infty}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{J}
\textstyle{X\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{\ell^{\infty}/c_{0}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{J^{\prime}}
\textstyle{X/E}

commutes (the vertical arrows are again the natural projections). Then
E
is not complemented in
X
.

Proof of Proposition
22
.

Consider
T:X\tend X
a bounded operator such that
E\subset\ker(T)
and let
(A_{i})_{i\in I}
be the family of subspaces given by Lemma
18
. We show that there must be a
i\in I
with
J\big(\ell^{\infty}(A_{i})\big)\subset\ker(T)
. Assume on the contrary that for all
i\in I
there is a
u_{i}
such that
TJu_{i}\neq 0
. Then, if
(g_{n})_{n}
is as in the statement of Proposition
22
,

I=\{i\in I,\;TJu_{i}\neq 0\}=\bigcup_{k,n\geq 0}\left\{i\in I,\quad\left|\langle g_{n},TJu_{i}\rangle_{X^{\prime}\times X}\right|\geq\frac{1}{k+1}\right\}:=\bigcup_{k,n\geq 0}I_{k,n}.

Now, since
I
is uncountable, there must be indices
k,n\geq 0
such that
I_{k,n}
is also uncountable. Next, for any finite
F\subset I_{k,n}
, let

y=\sum_{i\in F}\alpha_{i}u_{i},\qquad\text{where}\qquad\alpha_{i}=\frac{\langle g_{n},TJu_{i}\rangle_{X^{\prime}\times X}}{\left|\langle Tg_{n},Ju_{i}\rangle_{X^{\prime}\times X}\right|}.

In particular,

(22)

\left|\langle g_{n},TJy\rangle_{X^{\prime}\times X}\right|\geq\frac{|F|}{k+1}.

Next, thanks to the properties of the
A_{i}
given by Lemma

, we can decompose the union of the

in

, where
A
and

are given exactly as in (

, we have
TJy=TJa
and

\left|\langle TJy,g_{n}\rangle_{X^{\prime}\times X}\right|\leq\|T\|,

which contradicts (
22
), since the set
F
can be chosen as large as desired.
∎

Another consequence of the abstract principle of Proposition

is that we may prove Theorem

in the case of the Lebesgue exponent

Corollary 23
.

Let

. Then

is not complemented in

: there is no decomposition
\dot{B}^{s}_{1,\infty}=C^{s}_{1}\oplus G
with continuous projections.

Proof.

We aim at applying Proposition
22
. By virtue of Theorem
16
, we already have constructed a map
J:\ell^{\infty}\tend\dot{B}^{s}_{1,\infty}
that satisfies the assumptions of Proposition
22
. It only remains to show that (

\dot{B}^{s}_{1,\infty}
.

This is an easy consequence of the embedding properties of homogeneous spaces (see Proposition 2.20 p. 64 in

). Since

and because

has a separable predual

that satisfies

\forall f\in\dot{B}^{s}_{1,\infty},\qquad\Big(\forall n\geq 1,\quad\big\langle g_{n},f\big\rangle_{\dot{B}^{s-d}_{\infty,\infty}\times\dot{B}^{d-s}_{1,1}}=0\Big)\;\Rightarrow\;f=0.

.
∎

Remark 24
.

Of course, the abstract principle of Proposition
22
is in reality a small part of the proof, the core of the argument is the construction of the map
J^{\prime}
. Nevertheless, the same arguments may be used in a number of different frameworks. Let us give an example of a seemingly very different situation where this principle works.

Let
H
be a real separable Hilbert space and
X:=\mathcal{L}(H)
the space of bounded linear operators on

of compact operators. N.J. Kalton showed (see Theorem 6 in

]

\mathcal{L}(H)

11

11

\mathcal{L}(X,Y)
contains or not a copy of

. The proof in
[

]
is adapted to the simpler framework of Hilbert spaces.

(Theorem 6.1, pp. 351-354), although phrased differently, can be reformulated to fit in our framework. Define, for any

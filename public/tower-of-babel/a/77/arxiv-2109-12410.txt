Limits and colimits, generators and relations of partial groups
Edoardo Salati

Abstract
. We analyse limits and colimits in the category P ​ a ​ r ​ t Part of partial groups, algebraic structures introduced by A. Chermak. We will prove that P ​ a ​ r ​ t Part is both complete and cocomplete and, in addition, that the full subcategory of finite partial groups is both finitely complete and finitely cocomplete. Cocompleteness is then used in order to define quotients of partial groups. We will also identify a category richer than S ​ e ​ t Set (the category of sets and set-maps) and build the free partial groups over objects is such category; this yields a larger class of free partial groups, eventually allowing to prove that every partial group is the quotient of a free partial group.



Introduction

The notion of a partial group was developed in connection with fusion systems; roughly speaking, a fusion system is a category whose objects are the subgroups of a finite
p
-group
S
and whose morphisms are injective group homomorphisms, including at least all the conjugations by elements of
S
. Research on fusion systems is currently very active in connection to Local Finite Group Theory, Modular Representation Theory and Algebraic Topology. In this last field it has been a tool employed in the proof of the Martino-Priddy conjecture, asserting that, if
p
is a prime, the
p
-completed classifying space associated to a finite group
G
is homotopy equivalent to the classifying space associated to the fusion system
\mathcal{F}_{S}(G)
(described, for example, in
[
AKO11
, Part I, Section 1]
), where
S\in\mathrm{Syl}_{p}(G)
. With regard to this, partial groups played a prominent role in the definition of the classifying space associated to an abstract saturated fusion system
\mathcal{F}
; this was achieved by replacing
\mathcal{F}
with a different, slightly richer, category, the so-called
centric linking system
, and then defining the classifying space of
\mathcal{F}
as the
p
-completed geometric realization of such associated centric linking system, whose existence and uniqueness are therefore necessary conditions to the definition. Partial groups provide a translation of the categorical language into one more similar to that of groups, which in turn allowed A. Chermak in
[
Che13
]
to tackle and solve the problem by means of the well established group-theoretic machinery. The interested reader may find a beautiful survey in
[
AO16
]
, with Sections 3 and 4 covering the interplay between Algebra and Homotopy Theory.

There is a natural notion of morphisms of partial groups, as defined in
[
Che22
]
, and in the same paper Chermak deals with further properties of the category
Part
of partial groups, including completeness and cocompleteness. In particular, in
[
Che22
]
the existence of colimits is proved exclusively over diagrams with specific properties; the main goal of this paper is to correct a mistake appeared in the proof of the cocompleteness of
Part
in an unpublished early work of Chermak and, at the same time, prove the following result.

Theorem A
.

The category
Part
is complete and cocomplete. In addition, if
FinPart
is the full subcategory of
Part
with objects the finite partial groups, then
FinPart
is finitely complete and finitely cocomplete.

Early examples of how colimits in
Part
may be exploited and used as a tool are the
elementary expansions
defined by Chermak in
[
Che16
]
, which consist in the main construction appearing in
[
Che13
, Section 5]
and building the centric linking system over a fusion system, as well as the construction realized by A. Díaz Ramos, R. Molinier and A. Viruel in
[
RMV21
]
for proving that the category
Part
is universal.

We will also take advantage of the categorical setting in order to define quotients for all partial groups and to study free partial groups: this will set the necessary background for the following result.

Proposition B
.

Every partial group is a quotient of a free partial group.

The notion of free partial groups over sets is analyzed very early, in Section
1
, as it is used to build a counterexample to Chermak’s construction of colimits of partial groups. Such counterexample is presented in Section
2
, together with a proof of the cocompleteness of
Part
. We will also report a proof of the completeness of
Part
in Section
3
, although this can also be found in
[
Che22
]
, with the purpose of collecting all coherent results in one single paper. Combining results of Sections
2
and
3
yields a proof of Theorem
A
.

Existence of colimits is exploited in Section
4
in order to define quotients of partial groups. Quotients were previously defined only for very specific subclasses, namely for groups and for the so-called localities, as defined in
[
Che22
, Sections 2 to 4]
and it will be proved that the definition given in this paper specializes to the already existing notions. Section
5
is dedicated to the proof of Proposition
B
; as a consequence of the analysis undertaken in Section
1
it turns out that the notion of free partial groups over sets is too weak for such a result. After replacing the category
Set
of sets and set-maps with a better suited category, denoted by
Set^{s}
, the notions of quotients and of free partial groups over
Set^{s}
allow a proof in terms of generators and relations, in analogy with what happens for groups. The section is ended with an example of how these tools may be used: in detail, it is shown that Chermak’s elementary expansions construction may be realized in terms of generators and relations.

1.
Partial groups and free partial groups

Roughly speaking, a
partial group
is a set
\mathcal{L}
equipped with a
partial

\mathcal{L}
; in other words, it may not be possible to multiply all elements as it happens with groups.

A little remark about the notation adopted throughout the paper: we will use right-hand notation for functions and functors, i.e. writing

\qquad u\circ v\in D\quad\implies\quad u,v\in D

\quad(u\circ v\circ w)\Pi=(u\circ(v)\Pi\circ w)\Pi

In analogy with terminology for groups, we call the element

The partial group

For a survey of some elementary properties of partial groups we refer to

Define the category of pointed sets,

, as the category whose objects are pairs

is a non-empty set and

U:Grp\longrightarrow Set^{*},\quad(G)U=(G,1_{G})

is a homomorphism between groups. Since morphisms of partial group have the same property, we can similarly define a forgetful functor

U:Part\longrightarrow Set^{*},\quad(\mathcal{L},D,\Pi,i)U=(\mathcal{L},1_{\mathcal{L}}).

It is a classical situation that in which

is a right adjoint to the free-construction functor; indeed, we will prove that

is a right adjoint by building free partial groups over pointed sets. It will become clear that the construction may be carried in a very similar way also when dealing with the category

and yields the same partial group.

3.1.
Partial groups as simplicial sets

It has been initially conjectured by C. Broto and then formalized in joint work with A. González that partial groups carry a natural structure of simplicial sets. Indeed it turns out that
Part
is equivalent to a full subcategory of the category

of simplicial sets, as described in
[
Gon15
]
. We wish to show that such subcategory is not closed under formation of colimits in
sSet
, so the well-understood structure of colimits of simplicial sets cannot be used for computing colimits of partial groups. We begin with recalling some definitions and elementary results; more details may be found in
[
GJ99
]
.

Let
\Delta
be the category of finite partially ordered sets with monotone, non-decreasing functions as morphisms. It is possible to show that
\Delta
is equivalent to the category whose objects are the finite ordered sequences

and whose morphisms are all non-decreasing functions. This is known as the skeletal subcategory; in the following we will restrict ourselves to this subcategory and denote it as well by
\Delta
.

Definition 3.1
.

A simplicial set is a functor

X:\Delta^{op}\longrightarrow Set;

hence we may think at a simplicial set as a sequence of sets
X_{n}:=[n]X
with morphisms induced by the monotone functions in
\Delta
taken with opposite direction.

The elements of
X_{0}
are called
vertices
of the simplicial set
X
; if
X_{0}=\{v\}
(i.e.
X
has a single vertex), then
X
is said to be
reduced
.

Simplicial sets together with natural transformations form a category, usually denoted by
sSet
. The structure of colimits of simplicial sets is well known; if
T:\mathcal{C}\longrightarrow sSet
is a diagram over the small category
\mathcal{C}
, then

colim(T):\Delta^{op}\longrightarrow Set

is the simplicial set with
colim(T)_{n}=\underset{c\in\mathcal{C}}{colim}\;[n](cT)
and suitable morphism induced by the universal property of colimits (we will not need to study in detail the morphisms).

In
[
Gon15
, Theorem 4.8]
González proves that partial groups, when regarded as simplicial sets, are characterized by being reduced, among other properties. Therefore, if
\mathcal{L}
and
\mathcal{M}
are partial groups, viewed as simplicial sets they are reduced, i.e.
\mathcal{L}_{0}=\{\star\}
and
\mathcal{M}_{0}=\{*\}
; however the coproduct computed in

is not reduced, since
(\mathcal{L}\sqcup\mathcal{M})_{0}=\mathcal{L}_{0}\sqcup\mathcal{M}_{0}=\{\star,*\}
, thus it cannot be a partial group.

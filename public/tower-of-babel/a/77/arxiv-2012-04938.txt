Affine symmetries in quantum cohomology: corrections and new results
P.–E. Chaput, N. Perrin

Abstract
. In [ CMP09 ] a general formula was given for the multiplication by some special Schubert classes in the quantum cohomology of any homogeneous space. Although this formula is true in the non equivariant setting, the stated equivariant version was wrong. We provide correction for the equivariant formula, thus giving a correct argument for the non equivariant formula. We also give new formulas in the equivariant homology of the affine grassmannian that could lead to non-equivariant Pieri formulas.

1.
Introduction

In
[
CMP09
]
a general formula was given for the multiplication in the quantum cohomology of any homogeneous space by some special Schubert classes coming from cominuscule weights. Although this formula is true in the non equivariant setting, the stated equivariant version is wrong. We provide correction for the equivariant formula, thus giving a correct argument for the non equivariant formula. We also provide new product formulas in the equivariant homology of the affine grassmannian.

Let
G
be a semisimple simply connected algebraic group and fix
T\subset B
a maximal torus and a Borel subgroup containing it. Denote by
P^{\vee}
and
Q^{\vee}
be the coweight and corrot lattices. A dominant coweight
\lambda^{\vee}\in P^{\vee}
is minuscule if
\langle\lambda^{\vee},\alpha\rangle\in\{0,1\}
for any positive root
\alpha
. A minuscule dominant coweight is a fundamental coweight. Denote by
I_{m}
the subset of the set
I
of vertices of the Dynkin diagram of
G
parametrising minuscule coweights.

We consider a finite group
Z
which has several interpretation. Define
Z
has

Z:=P^{\vee}/Q^{\vee}.

Representatives for this quotient are for example the opposites of the minuscule fundamental coweights
(-\varpi^{\vee}_{i})_{i\in I_{m}}
. The group
Z
is also the center of
G
and if
G^{\rm ad}\ \!
the the adjoint group associated to
G
, then
Z=\pi_{1}(G^{\rm ad}\ \!)
.

The group
Z
can be realised as a subgroup of the Weyl group
W
of
G
as follows. Let
w_{0}
be the longest element in
W
. For
i\in I_{m}
define
v_{i}\in W
to be the smallest element in
W
such that
v_{i}\varpi_{i}=w_{0}\varpi_{i}
. Then the family
(v_{i})_{i\in I_{m}}
forms a finite subgroup of
W
isomorphic to
Z
. Finally
Z
can be realised as a subgroup of the extended affine Weyl group
{W_{\rm aff}}=W\ltimes P^{\vee}
(see Section
2.2
below) by
i\mapsto\tau_{i}:=v_{i}t_{-\varpi_{i}}
.

For
P\subset G
a parabolic subgroup, let
I_{P}
be the set of vertices in the Dynkin diagram such that, for
i\in I
, the simple root
\alpha_{i}
is a root of
P
if and only if
i\in I_{P}
. For
w\in W
, denote by
\sigma^{P}(w)
the Schubert class in
H^{2\ell(w)}(G/P,{\mathbb{Z}})
defined by
w
. Denote by
Q_{P}^{\vee}
the coroot lattice of
P
and consider
\eta_{P}:Q^{\vee}\to Q^{\vee}/Q_{P}^{\vee}
the quotient map. We define an action of the Weyl group
W
of
G
on the equivariant cohomology
H^{*}_{T}(G/P)
using, for
w\in W
, the pull-back in cohomology of the left multiplication by
w
(see Subsection
6.1
). We denote this action by
w^{*}
. This action is trivial in non-equivariant cohomology and extends to an action on equivariant quantum cohomology
\QH_{T}^{*}(G/P)
. In this paper we obtain the following formula in the quantum equivariant cohomology
\QH^{*}_{T}(G/P)
for any parabolic subgroup
P\subset G
(see Theorem
6.9
).

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
i
be a cominuscule node. In
QH^{*}_{T}(G/P)
we have

\sigma^{P}(v_{i})\times v_{i}^{*}(\sigma^{P}(w))=q_{\eta_{P}(\varpi_{i}^{\vee}-w^{-1}(\varpi_{i}^{\vee}))}\sigma^{P}(v_{i}w).

This result corrects our formula in
[
CMP09
, Theorem 1]
which was wrong in the equivariant setting (the action
v_{i}^{*}
on the second factor on the LHS was missing). The error in
[
CMP09
]
comes from an incorrect description of the ring structure of
H_{*}^{T}({\Omega K^{\rm ad}})
the equivariant homology of the adjoint affine grassmannian (see Section
5
). If
{\Omega K}
is the affine grassmannian for
G
, the incorrect claim (
[
CMP09
, Page 12]
) was that
H_{*}^{T}({\Omega K})
should be isomorphic to
Z\otimes H_{*}^{T}({\Omega K})
. This is not true as explained in Section
5
(see Remark
5.6
). This is corrected in the present paper. Especially, in Proposition
5.2
, we prove the
S
-algebra isomorphism (here
S=H^{*}_{T}({\rm pt})
):

H_{*}^{T}({\Omega K^{\rm ad}})\simeq S[P^{\vee}]\otimes_{S[{Q^{\vee}}]}H_{*}^{T}({\Omega K}).

The incorrect product formula was then used only once in
[
CMP09
, Proposition 3.16]
. We give a correct version of Proposition 3.16 in
[
CMP09
]
in Proposition
5.14
.

We tried to write this paper as independently from
[
CMP09
]
as possible and included many preliminary results on the algebra and the module structure of the extended affine Hecke algebra
{\widetilde{\mathbb{A}}_{\rm aff}}
(see Section
3
) and on its module structure
{\widetilde{M}}
which is isomorphic to
H_{*}^{T}({\Omega K^{\rm ad}})
the homology of the adjoint affine grassmannian. We also added new results. Especially we provide a generalization of a formula in
[
Lam08
, Proposition 5.4]
to coweights for the map
j^{\rm ad}\ \!:H_{*}^{T}({\Omega K^{\rm ad}})\to Z_{{\widetilde{\mathbb{A}}_{\rm aff}}}(S)
(see Proposition
7.1
).

Proposition 1.2
.

Let
{\mu^{\vee}}\in P^{\vee}
be antidominant and set
W_{\mu^{\vee}}=\langle s_{\alpha_{i}}\ |\ i\in[1,r]\textrm{ and }\langle\alpha_{i},{\mu^{\vee}}\rangle=0\rangle=\{w\in W\ |\ w({\mu^{\vee}})={\mu^{\vee}}\}
. Then

{j^{\rm ad}}(\xi_{t_{\mu^{\vee}}})=\sum_{w\in W/W_{\mu^{\vee}}}{\widetilde{A}}_{t_{w({\mu^{\vee}})}}\,.

Finally, we use this formula to give an explicit formula for the image of the map
j:H_{*}^{T}({\Omega K})\to Z_{{\widetilde{\mathbb{A}}_{\rm aff}}}(S)
for the special elements
\tau_{i}(v_{i})=\tau_{i}v_{i}\tau_{i}^{-1}
(see Proposition
7.4
). Here
{{{\widetilde{W}_{\rm aff}}^{-}}}
denotes the set of minimal representatives of the quotient
{\widetilde{W}_{\rm aff}}/W
.

Proposition 1.3
.

We have
\tau_{i}(v_{i})\in{{{\widetilde{W}_{\rm aff}}^{-}}}
and

j(\xi_{\tau_{i}(v_{i})})=\sum_{w\leq_{L}v_{i}}\sum_{v\leq v_{I}^{-1}}\tau_{i}(\xi^{v}(v_{i}^{-1}))A_{\tau_{i}(w)v_{i}w^{-1}}A_{v},

where
\leq
is the Bruhat order and
\leq_{L}
the weak left Bruhat order.

We hope to use the above formula to prove Pieri type formulas in
H_{*}^{T}({\Omega K})
in the spirit of what Lam, Lapointe, Morse and Shimozono
[
LLMS10
]
did in type
A
.

we thank Elizabeth Milićević for showing us a counterexample to
[
CMP09
, Theorem 1]
which lead to the
present correction and development.

2.
Notations

In this section, we fix notation for affine Kac-Moody Lie algebras, we introduce the finite group
Z
and define the extended affine Weyl group
{\widetilde{W}_{\rm aff}}
.

2.1.
Affine Lie algebras

We denote by
\mathfrak{g}
a simple finite-dimensional Lie algebra of rank
r
, and by
\mathfrak{h}
a Cartan subalgebra.
We denote by
G
the simply-connected group corresponding to
\mathfrak{g}
and by
{G^{\rm ad}}
the adjoint group.
The affine Kac-Moody group corresponding to
G
will be denoted by
\mathcal{G}
and
\mathcal{P}\subset\mathcal{G}
is the parabolic subgroup such that

\mathcal{G}/\mathcal{P}
is the affine Grassmannian.

The corresponding affine Lie algebra will be denoted by
\mathfrak{g}_{\rm aff}
, with Cartan subalgebra
\mathfrak{h}_{\rm aff}
. The simple roots are denoted
(\alpha_{i})_{i\in[1,r]}
and the null-root, orthogonal to all the simple roots
(\alpha_{i})_{i\in[1,r]}
, will be denoted by
\epsilon
. Recall that we have the equality
\epsilon=\Theta+\alpha_{0}
, where
\Theta
is the highest root of
\mathfrak{g}
.
As in
[
Kac90
, p.82]
we will use the decompositions

\mathfrak{h}_{\rm aff}^{\vee}=\mathfrak{h}^{\vee}\oplus{\mathbb{C}}\Lambda_{0}\oplus{\mathbb{C}}\epsilon
and

\mathfrak{h}_{\rm aff}=\mathfrak{h}\oplus{\mathbb{C}}K\oplus{\mathbb{C}}d
.
We denote by
{R_{\rm aff}}
the set of roots of
\mathfrak{g}_{\rm aff}
and by
R
those of
\mathfrak{g}
.

We denote by
Q,P,{Q^{\vee}},{P^{\vee}}
the root, weight, coroot, coweight lattices of
\mathfrak{g}
.
We also denote by
S
the symmetric algebra on
P
.

2.2.
Affine Weyl groups

Let
W
be the Weyl group of
\mathfrak{g}
and
let
{W_{\rm aff}}=Q^{\vee}\rtimes W
be the affine Weyl group.
For
\lambda^{\vee}\in Q^{\vee}
, the corresponding element in
{W_{\rm aff}}
will be denoted by

t_{\lambda^{\vee}}
.
The reflection associated to a root
\alpha
will be denoted by
s_{\alpha}
.
The group
{W_{\rm aff}}
is a Coxeter group with Coxeter generators

s_{i}
for
1\leq i\leq r
and
s_{0}=t_{\Theta^{\vee}}s_{\Theta}

(
[
Kum02
, Prop 13.1.7]
, see also Lemma
2.7
).

Define the extended affine Weyl group
{\widetilde{W}_{\rm aff}}:=P^{\vee}\rtimes W\supset{W_{\rm aff}}
. The group
{W_{\rm aff}}
acts on
P\oplus{\mathbb{Z}}\epsilon
while the group by
{\widetilde{W}_{\rm aff}}
acts only on
Q\oplus{\mathbb{Z}}\epsilon
via

(1)

\begin{array}[]{c}wt_{\lambda^{\vee}}\cdot(\mu+n\epsilon)=w(\mu)+(n-\langle\mu,{\lambda^{\vee}}\rangle)\epsilon\\
wt_{\mu^{\vee}}\cdot(\lambda+n\epsilon)=w(\lambda)+(n-\langle\lambda,{\mu^{\vee}}\rangle)\epsilon\end{array}

where we have
\lambda\in Q,{\lambda^{\vee}}\in{Q^{\vee}},\mu\in P,{\mu^{\vee}}\in{P^{\vee}}
. Note that in general
{\widetilde{W}_{\rm aff}}
does not act on
P\oplus{\mathbb{Z}}\epsilon
since
\langle P,P^{\vee}\rangle\not\subset{\mathbb{Z}}
in general.

We may however define an actions of
{\widetilde{W}_{\rm aff}}
on
P^{\vee}
(and therefore on
Q^{\vee}
) by prescribing that translations do not act: we simply set
wt_{\lambda^{\vee}}({\mu^{\vee}})=w({\mu^{\vee}})
for
w\in W
and
{\lambda^{\vee}},{\mu^{\vee}}\in P^{\vee}
.

Notation 2.1
.

Since an element in
{Q^{\vee}}
is also an element in
{Q^{\vee}}\oplus{\mathbb{Z}}\delta
, we will denote by
w\cdot{\lambda^{\vee}}
the result of the
action of
w\in{\widetilde{W}_{\rm aff}}
on
{\lambda^{\vee}}
as an element in
{Q^{\vee}}\oplus{\mathbb{Z}}\delta
and by
w({\lambda^{\vee}})
the element in
{Q^{\vee}}
.

Recall the definition of the fundamental alcove

A_{\circ}=\{\lambda\in\mathfrak{h}_{\mathbb{R}}^{\vee}\ |\ \langle\lambda,\alpha_{i}^{\vee}\rangle\geq 0\textrm{ for all }i\in[1,r]\textrm{ and }\langle\lambda,\Theta^{\vee}\rangle\leq 1\}.

The stabiliser of
A_{\circ}
in
{\widetilde{W}_{\rm aff}}
will be denoted by
Z
; it is a subgroup of
{\widetilde{W}_{\rm aff}}
isomorphic to
{P^{\vee}}/{Q^{\vee}}

[
LS10
, §10.1]
. In
loc. cit.
, the authors also prove the following result.

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
\tau\in Z
.
The conjugation by
\tau
is an automorphism of the Coxeter group
{W_{\rm aff}}
.
In fact, there exists an automorphism
f_{\tau}
of the affine Dynkin diagram such that

\forall i\in I\cup\{0\}\ ,\ \tau s_{\alpha_{i}}\tau^{-1}=s_{\tau\cdot\alpha_{i}}=s_{\alpha_{f_{\tau}(i)}}\ .

In particular, we have
\tau\cdot\epsilon=\epsilon
.

Notation 2.3
.

For
{\widehat{x}}\in{W_{\rm aff}}
, set
\tau({\widehat{x}}):=\tau{\widehat{x}}\tau^{-1}\in{W_{\rm aff}}
. We have
\ell(\tau({\widehat{x}}))=\ell({\widehat{x}})
.

Lemma 2.4
.

An element
\tau
in
Z
permutes the positive real roots.

Proof.

According to Lemma
2.2
, we have

\tau\cdot(\sum n_{j}\alpha_{j}+n\delta)=\sum n_{j}\alpha_{f_{\tau}(j)}+n\delta
.
Since a real root
\alpha+n\delta
is positive if and only if
n>0
or
n=0
and
\alpha>0
,
\tau
indeed permutes
positive roots.

\square

As explained in
[
CMP09
]
,
{\widetilde{W}_{\rm aff}}
is not a Coxeter group, but we have a well defined length function.

Definition 2.5
.

Every element
x\in{\widetilde{W}_{\rm aff}}
can be uniquely written as
\tau{\widehat{x}}
with
\tau\in Z
and

{\widehat{x}}\in{W_{\rm aff}}

(1)

Define the length function by
\ell(x):=\ell({\widehat{x}})
.

(2)

Define a partial order on
{\widetilde{W}_{\rm aff}}
by
\displaystyle\tau{\widehat{x}}\leq\sigma{\widehat{y}}\Longleftrightarrow\tau=\sigma\mbox{ and }{\widehat{x}}\leq{\widehat{y}}.

Covering relations in
{\widetilde{W}_{\rm aff}}
for the above partial order are defined by
x\lessdot y
if
x\leq y
and
\ell(x)=\ell(y)-1
.

Remark 2.6
.

The length of
x\in{\widetilde{W}_{\rm aff}}
is also the number of inversions, namely the cardinal of the set
I(x)=\{\alpha\in{R_{\rm aff}}\ |\ \alpha>0,\alpha\textrm{ is real and }x(\alpha)<0\}
.
Indeed, for
x=\tau{\widehat{x}}
, by Lemma
2.4
, we have
I(x)=I({\widehat{x}})
.

2.3.
Translations

We will need the following lemma.

Lemma 2.7
.

Let
\alpha\in R
. We have
t_{{\alpha^{\vee}}}=s_{\epsilon-\alpha}s_{\alpha}
.

Proof.

Set
K^{\bot}=\{\mu\in\mathfrak{h}_{\rm aff}^{\vee}\ |\ \langle\mu,K\rangle=0\}
. By
[
Kac90
, p.87]
, it is enough to compute

s_{\epsilon-\alpha}s_{\alpha}(\mu)
for
\mu\in K^{\bot}
.
We have

s_{\epsilon-\alpha}s_{\alpha}(\mu)=\mu-\langle\mu,(\epsilon-\alpha)^{\vee}\rangle(\epsilon-\alpha)-\langle\mu,{\alpha^{\vee}}\rangle\alpha+\langle\mu,{\alpha^{\vee}}\rangle\langle\alpha,(\epsilon-\alpha)^{\vee}\rangle(\epsilon-\alpha).

Now, for any
\beta\in{R_{\rm aff}}
, we have by
[
Kac90
, §2.3.5 and §6.2.3]
:

\langle\mu,(\epsilon+\beta)^{\vee}\rangle=\frac{2(\epsilon+\beta,\mu)}{(\epsilon+\beta,\epsilon+\beta)}=\frac{2(\beta,\mu)}{(\beta,\beta)}=\langle\mu,{\beta^{\vee}}\rangle.

Therefore,

\begin{array}[]{rcl}s_{\epsilon-\alpha}s_{\alpha}(\mu)&=&\mu+\langle\mu,\alpha^{\vee}\rangle(\epsilon-\alpha)-\langle\mu,{\alpha^{\vee}}\rangle\alpha-2\langle\mu,{\alpha^{\vee}}\rangle(\epsilon-\alpha)\\
&=&\mu-\langle\mu,{\alpha^{\vee}}\rangle\epsilon=t_{\alpha^{\vee}}(\mu)\ ,\end{array}

where the last equality follows from the definition of
t_{\alpha^{\vee}}
in
[
Kac90
, §6.5.5]
.

\square

Corollary 2.8
.

For
\alpha\in R,k\in{\mathbb{Z}}
and
{\mu^{\vee}}\in P^{\vee}
, we have
s_{\alpha+k\epsilon}({\mu^{\vee}})=s_{\alpha}({\mu^{\vee}})
.

Proof.

We have
s_{\alpha+\epsilon}({\mu^{\vee}})=s_{\alpha+\epsilon}t_{-\alpha}({\mu^{\vee}})=s_{\alpha+\epsilon}s_{\alpha+\epsilon}s_{-\alpha}({\mu^{\vee}})=s_{-\alpha}({\mu^{\vee}})=s_{\alpha}({\mu^{\vee}})
. The result follows by induction.

\square

3.
extended nil-Hecke ring

The goal of this section is to extend the notion of
the nil-Hecke ring defined by Kostant and Kumar
[
KK86
]
.
This ring was used in
[
LS10
]
to compare the quantum cohomology of
G/P

and the homology of affine Grassmannians
{\Omega K}
. We need a refined version of this
nil-Hecke ring that enables dealing with
{\Omega K^{\rm ad}}
the adjoint affine grassmannian (see Section
5
).

3.1.
Definition

We extend several classical object in particular the affine nil-Hecke algebra. Our reference for these classical objects is Kumar’s book
[
Kum02
]
.

Definition 3.1
.

Recall that the ring
{Q_{\rm aff}}
is

(2)

{Q_{\rm aff}}=\bigoplus_{w\in{W_{\rm aff}}}{\rm{Frac}(S)}\,\delta_{w}

We define the following extended version:

(3)

{\widetilde{Q}_{\rm aff}}=\bigoplus_{w\in{\widetilde{W}_{\rm aff}}}{\rm{Frac}(S)}\,\delta_{w}

In both cases, the ring structure is defined by the equations

\delta_{u}\delta_{v}=\delta_{uv}
and
\delta_{u}s=u(s)\delta_{u}
, for
u,v\in{W_{\rm aff}}

resp.
u,v\in{\widetilde{W}_{\rm aff}}
, and
s\in S
.

Definition 3.2
.

As in
[
Kum02
]
, we consider particular elements in
{Q_{\rm aff}}
.

(1)

For
i\in I
, set
A_{i}=\frac{1}{\alpha_{i}}(\delta_{e}-\delta_{s_{i}})
.
For
i=0
, set
A_{0}=\frac{-1}{\Theta}(\delta_{e}-\delta_{s_{0}})
(note that this is coherent with the forthcoming Definition
3.6
).

(2)

For
w\in{W_{\rm aff}}
and for
w=s_{i_{1}}\cdots s_{i_{\ell}}
a reduced expression. We set:

A_{w}=A_{i_{1}}\cdots A_{i_{\ell}}\,.

By
[
Kum02
, Theorem 11.1.2]
, the right hand side does not depend on the chosen reduced expression.

Recall that for
x\in{\widetilde{W}_{\rm aff}}
, there is a unique decomposition
x=\tau{\widehat{x}}
with
\tau\in Z
and
{\widehat{x}}\in{W_{\rm aff}}
.

Definition 3.3
.

Let
x=\tau{\widehat{x}}\in{\widetilde{W}_{\rm aff}}
, we set

{\widetilde{A}}_{w}=\delta_{\tau}A_{\widehat{x}}.

Definition 3.4
.

As in
[
KK86
]
, the (extended) nil-Hecke ring is generated over
S
by the elements
A_{w}
.

(1)

The nil-Hecke ring is

{\mathbb{A}_{\rm aff}}=\bigoplus_{w\in{W_{\rm aff}}}S\cdot A_{w}\subset{Q_{\rm aff}}\,.

(2)

The extended nil-Hecke ring is

{\widetilde{\mathbb{A}}_{\rm aff}}=\bigoplus_{w\in{\widetilde{W}_{\rm aff}}}S\cdot{\widetilde{A}}_{w}\subset{\widetilde{Q}_{\rm aff}}\,.

Remark 3.5
.

We will see below that both are indeed subrings of
{\widetilde{Q}_{\rm aff}}
.

3.2.
Definition and properties of
A_{\alpha}

It will be helpful to generalize the definition of
A_{i}
in the following way.

Definition 3.6
.

For a real root
\alpha=\gamma+k\epsilon
with
\gamma\in R
, set

A_{\alpha}=\frac{1}{\gamma}(\delta_{e}-\delta_{s_{\alpha}}).

These elements satisfy the following properties.

Proposition 3.7
.

Let
w\in{\widetilde{W}_{\rm aff}},\alpha\in{R_{\rm aff}}
a real root and
\lambda\in Q
. Then we have:

(1)

\delta_{w}A_{\alpha}\delta_{w^{-1}}=A_{w(\alpha)}
.

(2)

A_{\alpha}\lambda=s_{\alpha}(\lambda)A_{\alpha}+\langle\lambda,\alpha^{\vee}\rangle 1
.

Proof.

Let
w=ut_{\mu^{\vee}},\alpha=\gamma+k\epsilon,\lambda\in P
be as in the proposition. Then,

\begin{array}[]{rcccl}\delta_{w}A_{\alpha}\delta_{w^{-1}}&=&\delta_{w}\frac{1}{\gamma}(\delta_{e}-\delta_{s_{\alpha}})\delta_{w^{-1}}&=&\frac{1}{u(\gamma)}\delta_{w}(\delta_{e}-\delta_{s_{\alpha}})\delta_{w^{-1}}\\
&=&\frac{1}{u(\gamma)}(\delta_{e}-\delta_{ws_{\alpha}w^{-1}})&=&\frac{1}{u(\gamma)}(\delta_{e}-\delta_{s_{w(\alpha)}})=A_{w(\alpha)}\,.\end{array}

For the second point, we use
[
LS10
, §6.1]
and the above conjugation relation. Let
w\in{W_{\rm aff}}
and

i\in I\cup\{0\}
be such that

\alpha=w(\alpha_{i})
.
We have

\begin{array}[]{rcl}A_{\alpha}\lambda&=&\delta_{w}A_{i}\delta_{w^{-1}}\lambda\\
&=&\delta_{w}A_{i}w^{-1}(\lambda)\delta_{w^{-1}}\\
&=&\delta_{w}(s_{i}w^{-1}(\lambda)A_{i}+\langle w^{-1}(\lambda),\alpha_{i}^{\vee}\rangle)\delta_{w^{-1}}\\
&=&ws_{i}w^{-1}(\lambda)\delta_{w}A_{i}\delta_{w^{-1}}+\langle\lambda,w(\alpha_{i})^{\vee}\rangle\\
&=&s_{\alpha}(\lambda)A_{\alpha}+\langle\lambda,\alpha^{\vee}\rangle\,.\end{array}

\square

Remark 3.8
.

The second formula in the above proposition generalizes the usual relation satisfied by the elements
A_{i}
(see for example
[
LS10
, §6.1]
).

Corollary 3.9
.

For any real root
\alpha
, we have
A_{\alpha}\in{\mathbb{A}_{\rm aff}}
.

Corollary 3.10
.

The (extended) nil Hecke rings
{\mathbb{A}_{\rm aff}}
and
{\widetilde{\mathbb{A}}_{\rm aff}}
are subrings of
{\widetilde{Q}_{\rm aff}}
.

Proof.

The second formula above shows that for
s,s^{\prime}\in S
and
u,v\in{W_{\rm aff}}
, the product
sA_{u}s^{\prime}A_{v}
lies in
{\mathbb{A}_{\rm aff}}
therefore
{\mathbb{A}_{\rm aff}}
is a ring. The first formula proves that for
\tau,\sigma\in Z
and for
u,v\in{W_{\rm aff}}
, we have
\delta_{\tau}A_{u}\delta_{\sigma}A_{v}=\delta_{\tau}\delta_{\sigma}A_{\sigma^{-1}(u)}A_{v}\in{\widetilde{\mathbb{A}}_{\rm aff}}
proving that
{\widetilde{\mathbb{A}}_{\rm aff}}
is a ring.

\square

4.
Module and ring structures of
{\widetilde{\mathbb{A}}_{\rm aff}}

In this section we present three different descriptions of
{\widetilde{\mathbb{A}}_{\rm aff}}
and describe its
S
-module structure and its ring structure in each case.

4.1.
S
-module structure of
{\widetilde{\mathbb{A}}_{\rm aff}}

Recall that we have an injection of
{W_{\rm aff}}
in the group of invertibles of
{\mathbb{A}_{\rm aff}}
, given by

w\mapsto\delta_{w}
: in fact
\delta_{s_{i}}=1-\alpha_{i}A_{i}\in{\mathbb{A}_{\rm aff}}
thus
\delta_{w}\in{\mathbb{A}_{\rm aff}}
for all
w\in{W_{\rm aff}}
. Therefore the subgroup
Q^{\vee}\subset{W_{\rm aff}}
also injects in
{\mathbb{A}_{\rm aff}}
, and
since
{\mathbb{A}_{\rm aff}}
is a ring we have an injection of the Laurent polynomial algebra
{\mathbb{Z}}[{Q^{\vee}}]
inside
{\mathbb{A}_{\rm aff}}
.
Thus
{\mathbb{A}_{\rm aff}}
is
{\mathbb{Z}}[{Q^{\vee}}]
-module via left mutliplication. The natural
{\mathbb{Z}}
-module basis of

{\mathbb{Z}}[{Q^{\vee}}]
will be denoted by
(h_{\lambda^{\vee}})_{{\lambda^{\vee}}\in{Q^{\vee}}}
.

We now introduce two new algebraic models of
{\widetilde{\mathbb{A}}_{\rm aff}}
.

Definition 4.1
.

Let
\varphi_{1},\varphi_{2}
be the following morphisms of
{\mathbb{Z}}
-modules:

\begin{array}[t]{rrcll}\varphi_{1}&:&{\mathbb{Z}}[{P^{\vee}}]\otimes_{{\mathbb{Z}}[{Q^{\vee}}]}{\mathbb{A}_{\rm aff}}&\rightarrow&{\widetilde{Q}_{\rm aff}}\\
&&h_{{\lambda^{\vee}}}\otimes A_{w}&\mapsto&\delta_{t_{\lambda^{\vee}}}A_{w}\,,\end{array}

\begin{array}[t]{rrcll}\varphi_{2}&:&{\mathbb{Z}}[Z]\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}{\mathbb{A}_{\rm aff}}&\rightarrow&{\widetilde{Q}_{\rm aff}}\\
&&\tau\otimes A_{w}&\mapsto&\delta_{\tau}A_{w}\,.\end{array}

Note that
{\mathbb{A}_{\rm aff}}
has a structure of
S
-bimodule, thus also the two tensor products in this definition.
Both maps

\varphi_{1}
and
\varphi_{2}
are
S
-linear on the right,
moreover
\varphi_{1}
is also
S
-linear on the left
whereas
\varphi_{2}
is not.

Proposition 4.2
.

With the above notations,
{\rm Im}(\varphi_{1})={\rm Im}(\varphi_{2})={\widetilde{\mathbb{A}}_{\rm aff}}
. Moreover, if
J\subset{\mathbb{A}_{\rm aff}}
is a left ideal, then

Proposition 4.3

(1) From the equality

Consider

as the dual of

(w\bullet f)(x)=f(\delta_{w^{-1}}x)\textrm{ for $x\in H_{*}^{T}(G/B)={\mathbb{A}}$}.

Proposition 6.4
.

For

and

Proof.

Using

\square

Corollary 6.5
.

Let
\alpha
be a simple root and
w\in W^{P}
. We have

(s_{\alpha})^{*}\sigma^{P}(w)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\sigma^{P}(w)&\textrm{if }s_{\alpha}w>w;\\
\sigma^{P}(w)-\alpha\sigma^{P}(s_{\alpha}w)&\textrm{if }s_{\alpha}w<w.\\
\end{array}\right.

Proof.

We compute
((s_{\alpha})^{*}\sigma^{P}(w))(A_{u})=\sigma^{P}(w)(\delta_{s_{\alpha}}A_{u})=\sigma^{P}(w)((1-\alpha A_{\alpha})A_{u})=\sigma^{P}(w)(A_{u})-\alpha\sigma^{P}(w)(A_{\alpha}A_{u})
. Now we have

A_{\alpha}A_{u}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}0&\textrm{if }s_{\alpha}u<u\\
A_{s_{\alpha}u}&\textrm{if }s_{\alpha}u>u.\\
\end{array}\right.

Since
\sigma^{P}(w)(A_{v})=\delta_{v,w}
, we get

((s_{\alpha})^{*}\sigma^{P}(w))(A_{u})=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\delta_{u,w}&\textrm{if }s_{\alpha}u<u\\
\delta_{u,w}-\alpha\delta_{s_{\alpha}u,w}&\textrm{if }s_{\alpha}u>u.\\
\end{array}\right.

This in turn gives the result.

\square

Lemma 6.7

.

First assume that

and

thus
\beta^{\prime}\not\in R_{P}
. Now, since

, this implies
\beta<0
.

Conversely, assume
\beta<0

and thus
s_{\alpha}x\in{(W^{P})_{\rm aff}}
. This also implies
\beta^{\prime}\not\in R_{P}
and since

and

\begin{array}[]{rcl}j(\xi_{\tau_{i}(v_{i})})&=&\sum_{w\leq_{L}v_{i}}A_{\tau_{i}(w)v_{i}w^{-1}}\delta_{v_{i}^{-1}}\\
&=&\sum_{w\leq_{L}v_{i}}A_{\tau_{i}(w)v_{i}w^{-1}}\sum_{v\leq v_{i}^{-1}}\xi^{v}(v_{i}^{-1})A_{v}\\

\square

Remark 7.5
.

Let
x\in{W_{{\rm aff}}^{-}}
. In the non equivariant homology, we thus have

\xi_{\tau_{i}(v_{i})}\cdot\xi_{x}=\sum\xi_{\tau_{i}(w)v_{i}w^{-1}x}\,,

where the sum is over
w\leq_{L}v_{i}
such that
\ell(\tau_{i}(w)v_{i}w^{-1}x)=\ell(v_{i})+\ell(x)
and
\tau_{i}(w)v_{i}w^{-1}x\in{W_{{\rm aff}}^{-}}
.

By Corollary
5.15
, we know that there is only one Schubert class in the product

\xi_{\tau_{i}(v_{i})}\cdot\xi_{x}
, from which we deduce that there is exactly one

w\leq_{L}v_{i}
such that
\ell(\tau_{i}(w)v_{i}w^{-1}x)=\ell(v_{i})+\ell(x)
and
\tau_{i}(w)v_{i}w^{-1}x\in{W_{{\rm aff}}^{-}}
.

Asymptotics of some integrals involving modified Bessel and hyper-Bessel functions
R. B. Paris

Abstract
We investigate the asymptotic expansion of integrals analogous to Ball’s integral ∫ 0 ∞ ( Γ ⁡ ( 1 + ν ) ​ | J ν ​ ( x ) | ( x / 2 ) ν ) n ​ 𝑑 x \int_{0}^{\infty}\biggl(\frac{\Gamma(1+\nu)|J_{\nu}(x)|}{(x/2)^{\nu}}\biggr)^{\!n}dx for large n n in which the Bessel function J ν ​ ( x ) J_{\nu}(x) is replaced by the modified Bessel functions I ν ​ ( x ) I_{\nu}(x) and K ν ​ ( x ) K_{\nu}(x) together with appropriate exponential factors e ∓ x e^{\mp x} , respectively. The above integral with J ν ​ ( x ) J_{\nu}(x) replaced by a hyper-Bessel function of the type recently discussed in Aktas et al. [The Ramanujan J., 2019] and taken over a finite interval determined by the first positive zero of the function is also considered for n → ∞ n\to\infty . We give the leading asymptotic behaviour of the hyper-Bessel function for x → + ∞ x\to+\infty in an appendix. Numerical examples are given to illustrate the accuracy of the various expansions obtained. 1. Introduction

The asymptotic expansion of Ball’s integral
[
2
]
for large positive values of
n

\int_{0}^{\infty}\biggl(\frac{\Gamma(1+\nu)|J_{\nu}(x)|}{(x/2)^{\nu}}\biggr)^{\!n}x^{2\nu-1}dx,\qquad n\geq 2,\ \nu\geq\mbox{${\textstyle\frac{1}{2}}$},

(1.1)

where
J_{\nu}(x)
is the Bessel function of the first kind, was investigated by Kerman
et al.
in
[
4
]
. In a recent note
[
6
]
it was shown that the above integral could be replaced by

\int_{0}^{j_{\nu,1}}\biggl(\frac{\Gamma(1+\nu)J_{\nu}(x)}{(x/2)^{\nu}}\biggr)^{\!n}x^{2\nu-1}dx

(1.2)

to within exponentially small terms when
n
is large, where
j_{\nu,1}
is the first positive zero of
J_{\nu}(x)
. The large-
n
expansion was found in the form

2^{2\nu-1}(1+\nu)^{\nu}\Gamma(\nu)\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{(-)^{k}c_{k}}{n^{k+\nu}}\qquad(n\to\infty),

where the leading coefficient
c_{0}=1
; explicit values of
c_{k}
for
k\leq 3
were obtained in
[
4
]
and for
k\leq 6
in
[
6
]
.

Following a suggestion of T. Pogány
[
9
]
, we consider analogous integrals involving the modified Bessel functions
I_{\nu}(x)
and
K_{\nu}(x)
and also the hyper-Bessel function
J_{\sigma_{1},\ldots,\sigma_{m}}(x)
defined in Section 4.
In Section 2, we consider the expansion of the integral

{\cal I}_{n}=\int_{0}^{\infty}\biggl(e^{-x}\frac{\Gamma(1+\nu)I_{\nu}(x)}{(x/2)^{\nu}}\biggr)^{\!n}dx\qquad(\nu>-\mbox{${\textstyle\frac{1}{2}}$})

for
n\to\infty
. Since
I_{\nu}(x)\sim e^{x}/\sqrt{2\pi x}
as
x\to+\infty
, it is necessary to add the factor
e^{-x}
to cancel the exponential growth of
I_{\nu}(x)
. The integrand is then of O(
x^{-\nu-1/2})
as
x\to\infty
so that
{\cal I}_{n}
converges for
\nu>-\mbox{${\textstyle\frac{1}{2}}$}
. In Section 3, a similar process is adopted to determine the large-
n
expansion of

{\cal K}_{n}=\int_{0}^{\infty}\biggl(\frac{2e^{x}}{\Gamma(\nu)}(x/2)^{\nu}K_{\nu}(x)\biggr)^{\!-n}dx\qquad(\nu>\mbox{${\textstyle\frac{1}{2}}$}),

where
n>0
.
From the small and large argument behaviours

K_{\nu}(x)\sim\frac{1}{2}\Gamma(\nu)(x/2)^{-\nu}\quad(x\to 0),\qquad K_{\nu}(x)\sim\sqrt{\frac{\pi}{2x}}\,e^{-x}\quad(x\to+\infty),

it is seen that the integrand has the value unity at
x=0
and is O(
x^{\nu-1/2})
as
x\to\infty
, thereby necessitating the condition
\nu>\mbox{${\textstyle\frac{1}{2}}$}
for convergence.

In the final section, we consider an integral analogous to (
1.1
) in which the classical Bessel function
J_{\nu}(x)
is replaced by a hyper-Bessel function. A significant difference, however, is that the interval of integration cannot be taken as
[0,\infty)
on account of the asymptotic structure of the particular hyper-Bessel function under consideration. It is necessary to take a finite integration interval analogous to that in (
1.2
) determined by the first positive zero of the function.

3. An integral involving the modified Bessel function
K_{\nu}(x)

In this section we consider an analogous integral to (
2.1
) with the modified Bessel function of the second kind, also known as the Macdonald function
K_{\nu}(x)
, namely

{\cal K}_{n}=\int_{0}^{\infty}\biggl(\frac{2e^{x}}{\Gamma(\nu)}(x/2)^{\nu}K_{\nu}(x)\biggr)^{\!-n}dx\qquad(\nu>\mbox{${\textstyle\frac{1}{2}}$}),

(3.1)

where again
n>0
(not necessarily an integer) and we assume throughout this section that
\nu
is not an integer. The quantity in brackets in (
3.1
) is monotonically increasing for
\nu>\mbox{${\textstyle\frac{1}{2}}$}

y(x)=x^{\nu}e^{x}K_{\nu}(x)

y^{\prime}(x)=x^{\nu}e^{x}\{K_{\nu}(x)-K_{\nu-1}(x)\}>0.

The fact that the quantity in braces is positive follows from the result
[
5

K_{\nu}(x)-K_{\nu-1}(x)=\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-x\cosh t}\{\cosh\nu t-\cosh(\nu-1)t\}\,dt>0,\qquad\nu>\mbox{${\textstyle\frac{1}{2}}$}.

With the changes of variable
\psi(x)=x+\log\,\{2(x/2)^{\nu}K_{\nu}(x)/\Gamma(\nu)\}

\tau=\psi(x)

To proceed further we require the inversion of the mapping
\tau\mapsto x

\nu

From the definition

K_{\nu}(x)=\frac{\pi}{2\sin\pi\nu}\{I_{-\nu}(x)-I_{\nu}(x)\},

it is seen that, provided
\nu\neq 1,2,\ldots\

4. An integral involving the hyper-Bessel function

The particular hyper-Bessel function we shall use to replace the Bessel function
J_{\nu}(x)
in the integral (
1.1
) is defined by
[
1
]

J_{\sigma_{1},\ldots,\sigma_{m}}(x)=\frac{(x/(m+1))^{\sigma_{1}+\cdots+\sigma_{m}}}{\prod_{j=1}^{m}\Gamma(\sigma_{j}+1)}\,{}_{0}F_{m}\biggl(\!\!\begin{array}[]{c}-\!\!-\\
\sigma_{1}\!+\!1,\ldots,\sigma_{m}\!+\!1\end{array}\!;-\biggl(\frac{x}{m+1}\biggr)^{\!m+1}\biggr).

(4.1)

Here
{}_{0}F_{m}
denotes the generalised hypergeometric function with
m
denominator parameters

{}_{0}F_{m}(z)=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{(\sigma_{1}\!+\!1)_{k}\ldots(\sigma_{m}\!+\!1)_{k}}\,\frac{z^{k}}{k!}\qquad(\sigma_{j}>-1,\ \ 1\leq j\leq m)

and
(a)_{k}=\Gamma(a+k)/\Gamma(a)=a(a+1)\ldots(a+k-1)
is Pochhammer’s symbol for the rising factorial.
When
m=1
,
\sigma_{1}=\nu
, the definition (
4.1
) reduces to the classical Bessel function
J_{\nu}(x)
, viz.

J_{\nu}(x)=\frac{(\mbox{${\textstyle\frac{1}{2}}$}x)^{\nu}}{\Gamma(1+\nu)}\,{}_{0}F_{1}\biggl(\!\!\begin{array}[]{c}-\!\!-\\
1+\nu\end{array}\!;-\biggl(\frac{x}{2}\biggr)^{\!2}\biggr)=\frac{(\mbox{${\textstyle\frac{1}{2}}$}x)^{\nu}}{\Gamma(1+\nu)}\,\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{(-)^{k}(\mbox{${\textstyle\frac{1}{2}}$}x)^{2k}}{(1+\nu)_{k}k!}~.

Before we can formulate an integral analogous to that in (
1.1
), it is necessary to consider the basic properties and asymptotic behaviour of
J_{\sigma_{1},\ldots,\sigma_{m}}(x)
.
In what follows we write
{\vec{\sigma}}=\{\sigma_{1},\sigma_{2},\ldots,\sigma_{m}\}
and define the quantity

\mu_{k}:=\prod_{j=1}^{m}(\sigma_{j}+k)^{-1}.

(4.2)

Both
J_{\sigma_{1},\ldots,\sigma_{m}}(x)
(for
m\geq 2
) and
J_{\nu}(x)
have an infinite number of zeros on
[0,\infty)
.
If the first such zero of
J_{\sigma_{1},\ldots,\sigma_{m}}(x)
is denoted by
j_{\vec{\sigma},1}
, it was established in
[
1
, Theorem 4]
that

(m+1)\mu_{1}^{-1/(m+1)}<j_{\vec{\sigma},1}<(m+1)(\mu_{1}-\mu_{2})^{-1/(m+1)}.

However, although these two functions possess similar zero properties, their asymptotic structure is quite different.
From (
A.2
) in the appendix, the leading asymptotic behaviour of
J_{\sigma_{1},\ldots,\sigma_{m}}(x)
is

J_{\sigma_{1},\ldots,\sigma_{m}}(x)\sim\frac{2(2\pi)^{-m/2}}{(m+1)^{1/2}}\biggl(\frac{x}{m+1}\biggr)^{\!-m/2}\,e^{x\cos\pi/(m+1)}\,\cos\biggr\{x\sin\frac{\pi}{m+1}+\frac{\pi\vartheta}{m+1}\biggr\}

as
x\to+\infty
, where
\vartheta
is defined in (
A.1
).

Thus, when
m\geq 2
the hyper-Bessel function grows exponentially as
x\to+\infty

and consequently the integral (
1.1

\displaystyle{\cal J}_{\sigma_{1},\ldots,\sigma_{m}}(x)

\displaystyle\frac{\prod_{j=1}^{m}\Gamma(\sigma_{j}+1)}{(x/(m+1))^{\sigma_{1}+\cdots+\sigma_{m}}}\,J_{\sigma_{1},\ldots,\sigma_{m}}(x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{}_{0}F_{m}\biggl(\!\!\begin{array}[]{c}-\!\!-\\
\sigma_{1}\!+\!1,\ldots,\sigma_{m}\!+\!1\end{array}\!;-\biggl(\frac{x}{m+1}\biggr)^{\!m+1}\biggr).

Appendix: The asymptotic behaviour of
J_{\sigma_{1},\ldots,\sigma_{m}}(x)
for
x\to+\infty

The hypergeometric-type function

f(z)=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{z^{k}}{\prod_{j=1}^{m}\Gamma(\sigma_{j}+1+k)k!}

is associated with the parameters
2
2

2

Here we follow the notation of
[
7
, §2.3]
and denote by
\kappa
the quantity
m+1
, although in Section 4 this quantity was denoted by
p
(see
[
7
, §2.3]
)

\kappa=m+1,\quad h=1,\quad\vartheta=-\frac{1}{2}m+\sum_{j=1}^{m}\sigma_{j}.

(A.1)

Define the formal exponential asymptotic sum

E(z):=Z^{\vartheta}e^{Z}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}A_{k}Z^{-k},\qquad Z:=\kappa(hz)^{1/\kappa},

where
A_{k}
are constants independent of
z
with
A_{0}=(2\pi)^{-m/2}\kappa^{-\frac{1}{2}-\vartheta}
.
Then, when
\kappa>2
(that is,
m\geq 2
) the asymptotic expansion of
f(z)
is given by
[
7
, §2.3, Case (iii)]

f(z)\sim\sum_{r=-P}^{P}E(ze^{2\pi ir})\qquad(|z|\to\infty,\ |\arg\,z|\leq\pi),

where
P
is chosen such that
2P+1
is the smallest odd integer satisfying
2P+1>\mbox{${\textstyle\frac{1}{2}}$}\kappa
.

For the hypergeometric function appearing in (
4.1
) we have
Z=xe^{\pi i/\kappa}
. Then, when
m\geq 2
,

{}_{0}F_{m}(-(x/\kappa)^{\kappa})\sim,\prod_{j=1}^{m}\Gamma(\sigma_{j}+1)\,\sum_{r=-P}^{P}E(xe^{(2r+1)\pi i})\qquad(x\to+\infty).

The dominant exponential sums correspond to
r=0
and
r=-1
, whence we obtain

\displaystyle{}_{o}F_{m}(-(x/\kappa)^{\kappa})

\displaystyle\sim

\displaystyle\prod_{j=1}^{m}\Gamma(\sigma_{j}+1)\,\{E(xe^{\pi i})+E(xe^{-\pi i})\}

\displaystyle\sim

\displaystyle 2A_{0}\prod_{j=1}^{m}\Gamma(\sigma_{j}+1)\,x^{\vartheta}e^{x\cos\,\pi/\kappa}\cos\biggl\{x\sin\,\frac{\pi}{\kappa}+\frac{\pi\vartheta}{\kappa}\biggr\}.

Hence the laeding behaviour of
J_{\sigma_{1},\ldots,\sigma_{m}}(x)
is given by

J_{\sigma_{1},\ldots,\sigma_{m}}(x)\sim\frac{2(2\pi)^{-m/2}}{(m+1)^{1/2}}\biggl(\frac{x}{m+1}\biggr)^{\!-m/2}\,e^{x\cos\pi/(m+1)}\,\cos\biggr\{x\sin\frac{\pi}{m+1}+\frac{\pi\vartheta}{m+1}\biggr\}

(A.2)

as
x\to+\infty
; see also
[
8
]
.

When
m=1
,
\sigma_{1}=\nu
, the approximation (
A.2
) reduces to the well-known leading behaviour of the classical Bessel function
[
5

J_{\nu}(x)\sim\sqrt{\frac{2}{\pi x}}\,\cos\biggl\{x-\frac{\pi\nu}{2}-\frac{\pi}{4}\biggr\}\qquad(x\to+\infty).

However, when
m\geq 2

A.2
) that
J_{\sigma_{1},\ldots,\sigma_{m}}(x)
is oscillatory with an exponentially growing amplitude as
x\to+\infty
, and so is of a completely different asymptotic structure to that of
J_{\nu}(x)
.

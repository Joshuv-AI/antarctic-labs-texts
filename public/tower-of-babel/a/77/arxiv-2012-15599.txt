Analytical approximations and Monge-Ampère masses of plurisubharmonic singularities
Chi Li

Abstract
We construct examples of plurisubharmonic functions with isolated singularities at 0 ∈ ℂ n 0\in{\mathbb{C}}^{n} , whose residual Monge-Ampère masses at the origin can not be approximated by masses of canonical analytic approximations obtained via multiplier ideals. This answers negatively a conjecture of Demailly, and shows that residual Monge-Ampère masses are not valuative invariants of plurisubharmonic singularities.



1
Introduction and main results

Let
\varphi
be a plurisubharmonic function in a bounded pseudoconvex open neighborhood
\Omega
of
0\in{\mathbb{C}}^{n}
. Assume that
\varphi
is locally bounded on
\Omega^{*}=\Omega\setminus\{0\}
. For any
\lambda>0
, we have the multiplier ideal sheaf
\mathcal{J}(\lambda\varphi)
: for any open set
U
in the domain of
\varphi
,
\mathcal{J}(\lambda\varphi)(U)
is generated by the space of holomorphic functions
f
such that

\int_{U}|f|^{2}e^{-\lambda\varphi}dV_{n}<+\infty

where
dV_{n}
is the standard volume form on
{\mathbb{C}}^{n}
.
Let
\{f^{(\lambda)}_{k}\}_{k\geq 1}
be an orthonormal basis of
\mathcal{J}(\lambda\varphi)(\Omega)
and set:

\varphi_{\lambda}=\frac{1}{\lambda}\log\sum_{k}|f^{(\lambda)}_{k}|^{2}.

(1)

Demailly used Ohsawa-Takegoshi extension theorem to prove that the singularity of
\varphi
can be approximated by analytic singularities in an accurate manner:

Theorem 1.1

(Demailly, see
[
12
, Theorem 7]
)
.

With the above notations, there exist constants
C_{1},C_{2}>0
such that for
m\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{>0}
,

(a)

\varphi(z)-\frac{C_{1}}{m}\leq\varphi_{m}(z)\leq\sup_{|\zeta-z|<r}\varphi(\xi)+\frac{1}{m}\log\frac{C_{2}}{r^{n}}
for every
z\in\Omega
and
r<d(z,\partial\Omega)
. In particular,
\varphi_{m}
converges to
\varphi
pointwise and in
L^{1}_{\rm loc}
topology on
\Omega
as
m\rightarrow+\infty
, and

(b)

e_{1}(\varphi,z)-\frac{n}{m}\leq e_{1}(\varphi_{m},z)\leq e_{1}(\varphi,z)
, where
e_{1}(\varphi,z)=\liminf_{w\rightarrow z}\frac{\varphi(w)}{\log|w-z|}
is the standard Lelong number of
\varphi
at
z
.

When the unbounded locus of
\varphi
is a compact subset of a Stein manifold, Demailly showed that the Monge-Ampère operator
\varphi\mapsto({\rm dd^{c}}\varphi)^{n}
is well-defined and is continuous with respect to decreasing sequences of psh functions (see
[
8
, III.(4.3)]
). Define the residual Monge-Ampère mass of
\varphi
at
0\in{\mathbb{C}}^{n}
as:

e_{n}(\varphi)=e_{n}(\varphi,0)=\int_{\{0\}}({\rm dd^{c}}\varphi)^{n}.

(2)

This is the generalized Lelong number of the current
({\rm dd^{c}}\varphi)^{n}
at
0\in{\mathbb{C}}^{n}
(see
[
8
, III.§5]
).
Motivated by the above result, Demailly conjectured the following:

Conjecture 1.2

(Demailly,
[
11
]
,
[
13
, Problem 8]
)
.

As
m\rightarrow+\infty
,
e_{n}(\varphi_{m})
converges to
e_{n}(\varphi)
.

In a closely related development, in
[
4
]
Boucksom-Favre-Jonsson carried out a deep study of plurisubharmonic singularities using the tool of valuations. More precisely, for any plurisubharmonic germ
\varphi
defined near
0\in{\mathbb{C}}^{n}
and any divisorial valuation
v
centered at
0
, the authors defined a generalized Lelong number, which we simply denoted by
v(\varphi)
. We state a particular consequence of the main result from
[
4
]
:

Theorem 1.3

(
[
4
]
)
.

Let
\varphi,\psi:({\mathbb{C}}^{n},0)\rightarrow{\mathbb{R}}\cup\{-\infty\}
be two psh germs with isolated singularity at
0\in{\mathbb{C}}^{n}
. The following two statements are equivalent:

1.

v(\varphi)=v(\psi)
for every divisorial valuation centered at
0
(In other words,
\varphi
and
\psi
are valuatively equivalent).

2.

\mathcal{J}(\lambda\varphi)=\mathcal{J}(\lambda\psi)
for any
\lambda>0
.

In
[
15
]
, Kim-Rashkovskii proposed the following

Conjecture 1.4

(
[
15
]
)
.

Let
\varphi
and
\psi
be psh functions with isolated singularities at
0\in{\mathbb{C}}^{n}
. If
v(\varphi)=v(\psi)
for every divisorial valuation
v
centered at
0
, then
e_{n}(\varphi,0)=e_{n}(\psi,0)
.

In other words, this conjecture says that the residual Monge-Ampère masses are valuative invariants of psh singularities.
By Theorem
1.3
, it is easy to see that Conjecture
1.2
implies Conjecture
1.4
. These two conjectures were known to be true for special classes of psh singularities, including analytic psh singularities or a more general class of tame psh singularities (see
[
4
,
15
,
18
]
for details of positive results).

However, we will construct counterexamples to show that neither of these two conjectures is true in general. To construct such an example, we
consider the projection
\pi:X^{*}:={\mathbb{C}}^{2}\setminus\{0\}\rightarrow{\mathbb{P}}^{1}=({\mathbb{C}}^{2}-\{0\})/{\mathbb{C}}^{*}
. Then
X^{*}
can be identified with the complement of the zero section in the total space of the the tautological line bundle
\mathcal{O}(-1)\rightarrow{\mathbb{P}}^{1}
. The function
h_{0}(z)=|z|^{2}
on
{\mathbb{C}}^{2}
can be identified with the standard Hermitian metric on
\mathcal{O}(-1)
whose Chern curvature is give by
-\partial\bar{\partial}\log h_{0}=-\frac{2\pi}{\sqrt{-1}}\omega_{0}
where
\omega_{0}=\frac{\sqrt{-1}}{2\pi}\frac{dw\wedge d\bar{w}}{(1+|w|^{2})^{2}}
with respect to the standard coordinate on
{\mathbb{C}}={\mathbb{P}}^{1}\setminus\{\infty\}
. Moreover any psh Hermitian metric on
\mathcal{O}(-1)
, which may be singular, is of the form
h=h_{0}e^{u}
where
u
is a
\omega_{0}
-psh function on
{\mathbb{P}}^{1}
, i.e.
\omega_{0}+{\rm dd^{c}}u\geq 0
where we set
{\rm dd^{c}}=\frac{\sqrt{-1}}{2\pi}\partial\bar{\partial}
.
h
can be considered as a function on
{\mathbb{C}}^{2}
by the identity
h=|z|^{2}e^{\pi^{*}u}
. Moreover the function
\log h=\log|z|^{2}+\pi^{*}u
is a psh function on
{\mathbb{C}}^{2}
.
Note that although
\pi^{*}u
is not defined at
0\in{\mathbb{C}}^{2}
,
h(0)=0
and
\log h(0)=-\infty
are both well-defined.

With the above data, we get a psh function on
{\mathbb{C}}^{2}
(and hence on any pseudoconvex domain containing
0\in{\mathbb{C}}^{2}
) by setting:

\varphi=\max\{\log h,2\log|z|^{2}\}=\max\{\log|z|^{2}+\pi^{*}u,2\log|z|^{2}\}.

(3)

Because
\varphi\geq 2\log|z|^{2}
,
\varphi
has isolated singularity at
0
, i.e. locally bounded on
\Omega\setminus\{0\}
. In classical potential theory, there is an example of subharmonic function
on
{\mathbb{C}}
whose associated Riesz measure does not have atoms and is supported on a generalized Cantor set which is polar. Similarly one can construct such a
\omega_{0}
-psh function
u
on
{\mathbb{P}}^{1}
(see section
3
). We will use such an
\omega_{0}
-psh function to disprove both Conjecture
1.2
and Conjecture
1.4
:

Theorem 1.5
.

There exists an
\omega_{0}
-psh function
u
on
{\mathbb{P}}^{1}
such that the corresponding
\varphi
defined in (
3
) satisfies

1.

\mathcal{J}(\lambda\varphi)=\mathcal{J}(\lambda\log|z|^{2})
for any
\lambda>0
.

2.

e_{2}(\varphi)=2
and
e_{2}(\varphi_{m})=(\frac{m-1}{m})^{2}
for all
m\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{>0}
.

This example also induces higher dimensional counterexamples on
{\mathbb{C}}^{n}
. Moreover, we can construct examples which are maximal in punctured neighborhoods of
0\in{\mathbb{C}}^{n}
. See section
5
for details.

Our example is motivated by an analogous global example in the study of geodesic rays in the space of finite energy metrics in
[
3
, Example 6.10]
(based on a construction from
[
7
]
). Indeed, ruling out a similar kind of phenomenon for destabilizing geodesic rays in the constant scalar curvature Kähler (cscK) problem is a key step in the author’s recent work in
[
17
]
proving an existence result towards the Yau-Tian-Donaldson conjecture: the K-stability for models (or filtrations associated to models) is a sufficient condition for the existence of cscK metric on any polarized Kähler manifold.

While being a natural question, Demailly actually proposed his conjecture to study Guedj-Rashkovskii’s conjecture (see
[
13
, Problem 7]
) which asks whether
e_{1}(\varphi)=0
implies
e_{n}(\varphi)=0
. The construction in this paper only provides examples of psh functions with positive Lelong numbers and leaves open the latter conjecture.

2
Multiplier ideal approximation

We first study the analytic approximation
\varphi_{m}
. We fix a pseudoconvex domain
\Omega\subset{\mathbb{C}}^{2}
that is
S^{1}
-invariant, i.e.
(e^{i\theta}z_{1},e^{i\theta}z_{2})\in\Omega
for any
e^{i\theta}\in S^{1}
and
(z_{1},z_{2})\in\Omega
. For example, we can just let
\Omega
to be the unit ball
\mathbb{B}_{1}
in
{\mathbb{C}}^{2}
. First note that
\varphi
is
S^{1}
-invariant. So
\mathcal{J}(\lambda\varphi)(\Omega)
is
S^{1}
-invariant. By using the Taylor expansion of holomorphic functions, we see that which implies that
\mathcal{J}(\lambda\varphi)
is homogeneous, i.e.
\mathcal{J}(\lambda\varphi)=\bigoplus_{k}\mathcal{J}(\lambda\varphi)_{k}
where
\mathcal{J}(\lambda\varphi)_{k}
consists of homogeneous holomorphic functions in
\mathcal{J}(\lambda\varphi)
of degree
k
. In other words, any
f\in\mathcal{J}(\lambda\varphi)_{k}
satisfies
f(tz)=t^{k}f(z)
. It is easy to see that
f
must be a homogeneous polynomial of degree
k
. Each such homogeneous polynomial corresponds to a holomorphic section
s_{f}\in H^{0}({\mathbb{P}}^{1},{\mathcal{O}}(k))
.

Proposition 2.1
.

Let
u
be a
\omega_{0}
-psh function and
\varphi
be defined as in (
3
). The following statements are true:

1.

If
\mathfrak{m}
denotes the maximal ideal of
0\in{\mathbb{C}}^{2}
, then

\mathcal{J}(\lambda\varphi)(\Omega)\subseteq\mathfrak{m}^{\lceil\lambda\rceil-1}
.

2.

If
k\geq\lceil\lambda\rceil-1
and
s_{f}\in H^{0}({\mathbb{P}}^{1},{\mathcal{O}}(k)\otimes\mathcal{J}(h^{-\lambda}))
where
\mathcal{J}(h^{-\lambda})
is the multiplier ideal sheaf of the Hermitian metric
h^{-\lambda}
, then
f\in\mathcal{J}(\lambda\varphi)(\Omega)
.

Proof.

Note that
\varphi=\log|z|^{2}+\max\{\pi^{*}u,\log|z|^{2}\}\leq\log|z|^{2}+C
for some constant
C>0
. So
\mathcal{J}(\lambda\log|z|^{2})\supseteq\mathcal{J}(\lambda\varphi)
. On the other hand, it is straightforward to verify that
\mathcal{J}(\lambda\log|z|^{2})(\Omega)
consists of all homogeneous polynomial of degree at least
\lceil\lambda\rceil-1
.

Next we verify the second statement. Let
\rho:X\rightarrow{\mathbb{C}}^{2}
be the standard blowup at
0\in{\mathbb{C}}^{2}
. Denote the exceptional divisor by
D
. Then
X
is also the total space of the tautological line bundle
\pi:\mathcal{O}_{{\mathbb{P}}^{1}}(-1)\rightarrow{\mathbb{P}}^{1}
and can be covered by two affine coordinate charts. Consider one such coordinate chart, for example
\{\mathcal{U}_{2}=\pi^{-1}(U_{2});w=\frac{z_{1}}{z_{2}},z_{2}\}
where
U_{2}=\{z_{2}\neq 0\}\subset{\mathbb{P}}^{1}
. Then
\rho^{*}f=f(wz_{2},z_{2})=z_{2}^{k}f(w,1)
and

\displaystyle\rho^{*}\varphi

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\max\{\log|z_{2}|^{2}+\log(1+|w|^{2})+u(w),2\log|z_{2}|^{2}+2\log(1+|w|^{2})\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\log|z_{2}|^{2}+\varphi^{\prime}(w,z_{2})

where
\varphi^{\prime}(w,z_{2})=\max\{\psi(w),\log|z_{2}|^{2}+2\log(1+|w|^{2})\}

and
\psi(w)=\log(1+|w|^{2})+u(w)
is the local potential of
h=h_{0}e^{u}
over
U_{2}
.
Note that
\rho^{*}dV_{2}=|z_{2}|^{2(n-1)}dV_{2}
with
n=2
. Because
\varphi^{\prime}(w,z_{2})\geq\psi(w)
, we get:

\displaystyle\int_{\mathcal{U}_{2}}|f|^{2}e^{-\lambda\varphi}d\lambda

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathcal{U}_{2}}|f(w,1)|^{2}|z_{2}|^{2k}|z_{2}|^{-2\lambda}e^{-\lambda\varphi^{\prime}(w,z_{2})}|z_{2}|^{2(n-1)}dV

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\int_{\mathcal{U}_{2}}|f(w,1)|^{2}e^{-\lambda\psi(w)}|z_{2}|^{2(k-\lambda+n-1)}\sqrt{-1}^{2}dw\wedge d\bar{w}\wedge dz\wedge d\bar{z}.

The last integral is finite if and only if
f(w,1)\in\mathcal{J}(\lambda\psi)(U_{2})
and
2(k-\lambda+n-1)+1>-1
. The same argument works for the other coordinate chart. So the statement follows.
∎

Corollary 2.2
.

For any
\lambda>0
, if
\mathcal{J}(h^{-\lambda})={\mathcal{O}}_{{\mathbb{P}}^{1}}
, then
\mathcal{J}(\lambda\varphi)(\Omega)=\{f\in{\mathcal{O}}(\Omega);{\rm ord}_{0}(f)\geq\lceil\lambda\rceil-1\}=\mathcal{J}(\lambda\log|z|^{2})(\Omega)
. In this case
e_{2}(\varphi_{\lambda})=\left(\frac{\lceil\lambda\rceil-1}{\lambda}\right)^{2}
. In particular, this holds true if the local potential of
h^{-1}
has zero Lelong numbers.

Proof.

By Skoda’s estimate, if the local potential of the psh metric
h^{-1}
has zero Lelong number, then
\mathcal{J}(h^{-\lambda})=\mathcal{O}_{{\mathbb{P}}^{1}}
for any
\lambda>0
. So the statement follows easily from the above proposition.
∎

A recap on potential theory

We recall some standard potential theory on
{\mathbb{P}}^{1}
. Let
\omega_{0}=\frac{\sqrt{-1}}{2\pi}\frac{dw\wedge d\bar{w}}{(1+|w|^{2})^{2}}
be the round metric with volume 1 on
S^{2}
. Let
u
be any
\omega_{0}
-psh function. With
\Delta u=\Delta_{\omega_{0}}u=\frac{\sqrt{-1}\partial\bar{\partial}u}{\omega_{0}}
we can write:

\omega_{0}+\frac{\sqrt{-1}}{2\pi}\partial\bar{\partial}u=\omega_{0}(1+\frac{1}{2\pi}\Delta u)

(4)

is a positive closed
(1,1)
-current and we call the quantity in (
4
) the (Riesz) probability measure associated to
u
. Note that if
u\in C^{2}({\mathbb{P}}^{1})
, then
\Delta u=(2\pi)(1+|w|^{2})^{2}u_{w\bar{w}}
with respect to the standard coordinate chart
({\mathbb{C}},w)
.

Conversely, we get a
\omega_{0}
-psh function for any probability measure on
{\mathbb{P}}^{1}
. This is obtained by using the
Green function
G(z,w)
of the Laplace operator of
\omega_{0}
.
G(z,w)
satisfies the following equation:

(-\Delta)G(z,w)=\delta_{0}(z)-1,\quad G(z,w)\text{ is locally bounded on }{\mathbb{C}}^{2}\setminus\{z=w\}.

(5)

The following lemma is well known. We give the short proof for the reader’s convenience.

Lemma 3.1
.

The solution to the equation (
5
) is given by:

G(z,w)=-\frac{1}{\pi}\log\frac{|z-w|}{\sqrt{1+|z|^{2}}\sqrt{1+|w|^{2}}}.

(6)

Remark 3.2
.

The expression
\frac{|z-w|}{\sqrt{1+|z|^{2}}\sqrt{1+|w|^{2}}}
is nothing but the chordal distance between
z
and
w
on the Riemann sphere under stereographic projection.

Proof.

First assume that
z=0
. Then by rotational symmetry,
G(0,w)=G(|w|)
. Let
r=|w|
. Then for
r>0
,
G(r)
satisfies the equation:

(2\pi)(1+r^{2})^{2}\frac{1}{4}(G^{\prime\prime}+r^{-1}G^{\prime})=1.

The general solution is given by
G(r)=\frac{1}{2\pi}\left(\log(r^{2}+1)-C\log r^{2}\right)
. Since
G(r)
is bounded as
r\rightarrow+\infty
, we get
C=1
, i.e.
G(r)=\frac{1}{2\pi}\log\frac{1+r^{2}}{r^{2}}
. For general
z
, we consider the unitary Möbius transformation:
w\mapsto\frac{w-z}{1+\bar{z}w}
which preserves the metric. So we get:

G(z,w)=G\left(0,\frac{w-z}{1+\bar{z}w}\right)=\frac{1}{2\pi}\log\frac{(1+|w|^{2})(1+|z|^{2})}{|w-z|^{2}}.

∎

For any probability measure
d\mu
on
{\mathbb{P}}^{1}
, we define the potential of
\mu
to be:

p_{\mu}(z)=2\pi\int_{{\mathbb{P}}^{1}}(-G(z,w))d\mu(w).

(7)

Then
p_{\mu}(z)
is a
\omega_{0}
-psh function satisfying
\omega_{0}+\frac{\sqrt{-1}}{2\pi}\partial\bar{\partial}p_{\mu}=\mu
.

Recall that a subset
E\subset{\mathbb{C}}
is polar if there exists an
\omega_{0}
-psh function
u
such that
E\subseteq\{u=-\infty\}
.
It is well-known that there exist subharmonic functions on
{\mathbb{C}}
whose Riesz measures do not charge isolated points but charge polar sets.

1

1

The author learned the existence of such an example from
[
3
, Example 6.10]
which was also pointed out in
[
14
, paragraph after Corollary 1.8]
.
The corresponding polar sets in these examples are generalized Cantor sets, whose construction we now recall following
[
19
, 5.3]
(see also the earlier reference
[
6
, Theorem 3]
). Let
{\bf s}=\{s_{k}\}_{k\geq 1}
be a sequence of numbers such that
0<s_{k}<1
for all
k
. Define
C(s_{1})
to be the set obtained from
[0,1]
by removing an open interval of length
s_{1}
from the center. At the
k
-th stage, let
C(s_{1},\dots,s_{k})
be the set obtained by removing from the middle of each interval in
C(s_{1},\dots,s_{k-1})
an open subinterval whose length is a proportion
s_{k}
of the whole interval. In this way we get a decreasing sequence of compact sets
(C(s_{1},\dots,s_{k}))_{k\geq 1}
, and the associated
generalized Cantor set
is defined to be:

\mathcal{C}:=C({\bf s})=\bigcap_{m\geq 1}C(s_{1},\dots,s_{k}).

(8)

It can be checked that
\mathcal{C}
is a compact, totally disconnected set of Lebesgue measure
\prod_{k=1}^{+\infty}(1-s_{k})
. By estimating the capacity of the generalized Cantor set, it is shown in
[
19
, Theorem 5.3.7]
, if
1-s_{k}
decays very fast, then
\mathcal{C}
is polar.

On the other hand, by (advanced) real analysis, we can associate to any such generalized Cantor set a generalized Cantor function
f(x)
defined on
[0,1]
. For any
k\geq 1
,
C(s_{1},\dots,s_{k})
consists of
2^{k}
pieces and each piece has length
l_{k}:=\prod_{j=1}^{k}\frac{1-s_{j}}{2}
. For convenience, we also set
\quad l_{0}=1
. The generalized Cantor function
c(x)
can be defined in the following way:

c(x)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\sum_{k=1}^{+\infty}\frac{b_{k}}{2^{k}}&x=\sum_{k=1}^{+\infty}\frac{b_{k}(1+s_{k})}{2}l_{k-1}\text{ for }b_{k}\in\{0,1\}\\
\sup_{y\leq x,y\in\mathcal{C}}c(y)&x\in[0,1]\setminus\mathcal{C}.\end{array}\right.

(9)

The generalized Cantor function
c(x)
is non-decreasing and takes every value between
[0,1]
. Hence it is continuous. It is the distribution function associated to a probability measure
\mu
with support
{\rm supp}(\mu)=\mathcal{C}
. The continuity of
c(x)
means that
\mu
has no atoms. We assume that
s_{k}>1/3
.
Fix any
x\in\mathcal{C}
. Then for any
k\geq 1
, the mass of the subset
[x-l_{k},x+l_{k}]\cap\mathcal{C}
is equal to
2^{-k}
.

Let
p_{\mu}
be the potential of
\mu
, as given by formula (
7
). Then we know that
p_{\mu}
is
\omega_{0}
-harmonic outside
\mathcal{C}
. In other words,
p_{\mu}
is smooth on
{\mathbb{P}}^{1}\setminus\mathcal{C}
and satisfies
\omega_{0}+{\rm dd^{c}}p_{\mu}=0
.

From now on set
l_{k}=e^{-a^{k}}
for
k\geq 1
and define
s_{k}
inductively by the formula:

s_{k}=1-\frac{2l_{k}}{l_{k-1}}=1-2e^{a^{k-1}-a^{k}}.

(10)

It is easy to see that if
a>2
, then
s_{k}\in(0,1)
. We will need the following refined information for
p_{\mu}
which also directly proves that
\mathcal{C}
is polar in this case.

Proposition 3.3
.

With the above choices of
s_{k}
in (
10
) with
a>2
, the following statements are true:

(i)

\mathcal{C}=\{w\in{\mathbb{P}}^{1};p_{\mu}(w)=-\infty\}
. In particular,
\mathcal{C}
is polar.

(ii)

e^{p_{\mu}}
is a continuous function on
{\mathbb{P}}^{1}
.

(iii)

There exists
C>0
such that
p_{\mu}(w)\geq 2\log{\rm dist}_{\rm sph}(w,\mathcal{C})-C
, where
{\rm dist}_{\rm sph}(\cdot,\mathcal{C})
is the spherical distance to the closed set
\mathcal{C}
.

Proof.

Note that we always have
-G(z,w)\leq\frac{1}{2\pi}\log|z-w|^{2}
. Let
d=d(z)={\rm dist}_{\rm Euc}(z,\mathcal{C})
denote the Euclidean distance from
z
to
\mathcal{C}
. For any
n\geq 1
, we have the estimate:

p_{\mu}(z)\leq 2^{-k}\log(d^{2}+l_{k}^{2})+(1-2^{-k})\log(d^{2}+1).

(11)

In particular, when
0\leq d<l_{k}\leq 1
, then there there exists a constant
C>0
independent of
k
such that

p_{\mu}(z)\leq 2^{-k}\cdot 2\log l_{k}+C=-2(\frac{a}{2})^{k}+C.

Since
a>2
, we let
k\rightarrow+\infty
to conclude that
p_{\mu}(z)=-\infty
if
z\in\mathcal{C}
and
p_{\mu}(z)\rightarrow-\infty
uniformly as
d(z)\rightarrow 0
. As mentioned above,
p_{\mu}
is smooth outside
\mathcal{C}
. So we get (i) and (ii).

Moreover, fixing any
b>2
, for any
w\in\mathcal{C}
, we have

\displaystyle-(2\pi)G(z,w)

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\log|z-w|^{2}-\log 2-\log(1+(b+1)^{2})\quad\text{ if }d(z)\leq b,

\displaystyle-(2\pi)G(z,w)

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\log\frac{(|z|-1)^{2}}{|z|^{2}+1}-\log 2\geq-C_{1}\quad\text{ if }d(z)>b

where
C_{1}=\log\frac{(b-2)^{2}}{(b-1)^{2}+1}-\log 2
is a constant independent of
z,w
.
So we can use the formula (
7
) to get that there exists a constant
C=C(b)
such that

\displaystyle p_{\mu}(z)

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle 2\log d-C\quad\text{ if }d(z)\leq b

\displaystyle p_{\mu}(z)

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle-C\quad\text{ if }d(z)>b.

Since
d(z)
is comparable to
{\rm dist}_{\rm sph}
when
d(z)

Lemma 4.1
.

We have the following formula:

({\rm dd^{c}}\varphi)^{2}=\rho_{*}\left([D]\wedge{\rm dd^{c}}\varphi^{\prime}+({\rm dd^{c}}\varphi^{\prime})^{2}\right).

(13)

As a consequence, we have the identity:

({\rm dd^{c}}\varphi)^{2}(\{0\})=1+({\rm dd^{c}}\varphi^{\prime})^{2}(D).

(14)

Proof.

Let
u_{k}
be a sequence of smooth
\omega_{0}
-psh functions decreasing to

. Define

\tilde{\varphi}_{k}=\max\{\log|z|^{2}+\pi^{*}u_{k},2\log|z|^{2}\}

\tilde{\varphi}_{k}
decreases to
\varphi=\max\{\log|z|^{2}+\pi^{*}u,2\log|z|^{2}\}

Since both sides of (

) are continuous with respect to decreasing sequences of psh functions. We just need to verify the identity for
\tilde{\varphi}_{k}
for which the formula (
13
) has been proved in
[
2
, Proposition 4.1]
. For the reader’s convenience, we sketch the proof which is by induction. We change the notation and assume that
\varphi
is one of
\tilde{\varphi}_{k}
such that
\varphi^{\prime}=\log(1+|w|^{2})+u(w)
is a locally bounded psh function. Then first have the identity:

{\rm dd^{c}}\varphi=\rho_{*}({\rm dd^{c}}\rho^{*}\varphi)=\rho_{*}([D]+{\rm dd^{c}}\varphi^{\prime}).

Next, we can verify the equality (

) following
[

, Proof of Proposition 4.1]

\displaystyle({\rm dd^{c}}\varphi)^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\rm dd^{c}}(\varphi{\bf 1}_{{\mathbb{C}}^{2}\setminus\{0\}}({\rm dd^{c}}\varphi))={\rm dd^{c}}(\varphi\rho_{*}({\rm dd^{c}}\varphi^{\prime}))=\rho_{*}{\rm dd^{c}}((\rho^{*}\varphi)({\rm dd^{c}}\varphi^{\prime}))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\rho_{*}{\rm dd^{c}}((\log|z_{2}|^{2}+\varphi^{\prime}){\rm dd^{c}}\varphi^{\prime})=\rho_{*}([D]\wedge{\rm dd^{c}}\varphi^{\prime}+({\rm dd^{c}}\varphi^{\prime})^{2}).

Finally the identity (

Lemma 4.2
.

There is an identity:

({\rm dd^{c}}\varphi^{\prime})^{2}(D)=({\rm dd^{c}}\varphi^{\prime})^{2}(\mathcal{C})={\rm dd^{c}}\psi(\mathcal{C})=1.

(15)

This lemma completes the proof of Theorem
1.5
. Indeed, because

tells us that

) and (

Proof of Lemma
4.2
.

Recall that
\varphi^{\prime}(w,z_{2})=\max\{\psi(w),\log|z_{2}|^{2}+\log(1+|w|^{2})\}
where

\psi(w)=\log(1+|w|^{2})+u(w)
. Because
\varphi^{\prime}
is locally bounded outside
\mathcal{C}=\{\varphi^{\prime}=-\infty\}
, by
[
2
, Corollary 3.3]

({\rm dd^{c}}\varphi)^{2}
does not charge any set contained in
D\setminus\mathcal{C}\subset{\mathbb{C}}^{2}
. This proves the first identity in (
15
).

Next we set
\hat{\varphi}(w,z_{2})=\max\{\psi(w),\log|z_{2}|^{2}\}
. Then because
\log(1+|w|^{2})
is bounded in a neighborhood of
\mathcal{C}
, there exists a constant
C>0
such that
\varphi^{\prime}-C\leq\hat{\varphi}\leq\varphi^{\prime}
. By Remark
3.4
and the comparison theorem for generalized Lelong numbers in
[
8
, III.7.1]
, we know that

({\rm dd^{c}}\varphi^{\prime})^{2}(\mathcal{C})=({\rm dd^{c}}\hat{\varphi})^{2}(\mathcal{C})
. So to prove the second identity of (
15
), it suffices to prove the following identity:

({\rm dd^{c}}\hat{\varphi})^{2}=[D]\wedge{\rm dd^{c}}\psi.

(16)

Next by a formula of Błocki, we get:

({\rm dd^{c}}\hat{\varphi})^{2}={\rm dd^{c}}\hat{\varphi}\wedge{\rm dd^{c}}\psi+{\rm dd^{c}}\hat{\varphi}\wedge{\rm dd^{c}}\log|z_{2}|^{2}-{\rm dd^{c}}\psi\wedge{\rm dd^{c}}\log|z_{2}|^{2}.

This formula was proved in
[
5
, Theorem 4]
when
\hat{\varphi}
is given by a maximum of two locally bounded psh functions. It is still valid in our case because both sides are well-defined (the unbounded locs of
\hat{\varphi}
, i.e.
\mathcal{C}
, is a compact set in
{\mathbb{C}}^{2}
) and are continuous with respect decreasing sequences.

Now we claim that each term on the right-hand-side is equal to
[D]\wedge{\rm dd^{c}}\psi
, which will give us the identity (
16
) thus finishing the proof.

The last term
{\rm dd^{c}}\psi\wedge{\rm dd^{c}}\log|z_{2}|^{2}={\rm dd^{c}}(\psi\log|z_{2}|^{2})={\rm dd^{c}}\psi\wedge[D]
. We will deal with the first two terms using a same method, partly motivated by
[
5
, Proof of Theorem 7]
.

1.

For any
\epsilon>0
set
v_{\epsilon}=\log(|z_{2}|^{2}+\epsilon)
and
\hat{\varphi}_{\epsilon}=\max\{\psi(w),v_{\epsilon}(z_{2})\}
. Then
\hat{\varphi}_{\epsilon}\geq\log\epsilon
and
\hat{\varphi}_{\epsilon}
decreases to
\hat{\varphi}
as
\epsilon\rightarrow 0+
.
Pick any test function
\chi
. Using the fact that
{\rm dd^{c}}\psi\equiv 0
on
\{\psi>\log\epsilon\}
, we get:

\displaystyle\int\chi{\rm dd^{c}}\hat{\varphi}_{\epsilon}\wedge{\rm dd^{c}}\psi

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int\hat{\varphi}_{\epsilon}{\rm dd^{c}}\chi\wedge{\rm dd^{c}}\psi=\int_{\{\psi>\log\epsilon\}}\hat{\varphi}_{\epsilon}{\rm dd^{c}}\chi\wedge{\rm dd^{c}}\psi+\int_{\{\psi\leq\log\epsilon\}}\hat{\varphi}_{\epsilon}{\rm dd^{c}}\chi\wedge{\rm dd^{c}}\psi

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\{\psi\leq\log\epsilon\}}v_{\epsilon}{\rm dd^{c}}\chi\wedge{\rm dd^{c}}\psi=\int v_{\epsilon}{\rm dd^{c}}\chi\wedge{\rm dd^{c}}\psi

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int\chi{\rm dd^{c}}v_{\epsilon}\wedge{\rm dd^{c}}\psi.

So we get
{\rm dd^{c}}\hat{\varphi}_{\epsilon}\wedge{\rm dd^{c}}\psi={\rm dd^{c}}v_{\epsilon}\wedge{\rm dd^{c}}\psi
. Letting

, we get
{\rm dd^{c}}\hat{\varphi}\wedge{\rm dd^{c}}\psi={\rm dd^{c}}\log|z_{2}|^{2}\wedge{\rm dd^{c}}\psi=[D]\wedge{\rm dd^{c}}\psi

2.

To deal with the last term, we set
\psi_{j}=\max\{\psi,-j\}\geq-j
and
\hat{\varphi}_{j}=\max\{\psi_{j},\log|z_{2}|^{2}\}
and use the same argument to get
{\rm dd^{c}}\hat{\varphi}_{j}\wedge{\rm dd^{c}}\log|z_{2}|^{2}={\rm dd^{c}}\psi_{j}\wedge[D]
which converges to
{\rm dd^{c}}\psi\wedge[D]
as

.

∎

5
Examples in higher dimensions and a question

Based on the above example, we can construct counter example to Conjecture
1.2
in higher dimensions. Choose the standard affine coordinate chart on
({\mathbb{C}}^{n-1},\{z_{1},\dots,z_{n-1}\})
of
{\mathbb{P}}^{n-1}
. Let
\mu
be the Cantor probability measure on the generalized Cantor set
\mathcal{C}
which sits inside the
z_{1}
-line
\ell:=\{z_{2}=\cdots=z_{n-1}=0\}
and let
p_{\mu}(z_{1})
be the potential given by (
7
). Set

\phi(z)=\max\{\log(1+|z_{1}|^{2})+p_{\mu}(z_{1}),\log(|z_{2}|^{2}+\cdots+|z_{n-1}|^{2})\}.

(17)

Then
\phi(z)
is a psh function on
{\mathbb{C}}^{n}
such that
\mathcal{C}=\{\phi=-\infty\}
and
\phi
is continuous outside
\mathcal{C}
.
We can extend
\phi
to be a psh metric on
\mathcal{O}_{{\mathbb{P}}^{n-1}}(\gamma)
for
1\ll\gamma\in{\mathbb{N}}
as follows.
Denote by
A(r_{1},r_{2})=\{z\in{\mathbb{C}}^{n-1};r_{1}\leq|z|\leq r_{2}\}
the standard closed spherical annulus centered at
0\in{\mathbb{C}}^{n-1}
such that
A(0,r)=B(r)=\{z\in{\mathbb{C}}^{n-1};|z|\leq r\}
. Set
C_{1}=\min_{A(2,3)}\phi
and
C_{2}=\max_{A(4,5)}\phi
.
Choose
\gamma\gg 1
and then
T\in{\mathbb{R}}
such that

C_{2}-\gamma\log(1+4^{2})<T<C_{1}-\gamma\log(1+3^{2}).

(18)

Set

u(z)=\frac{1}{\gamma}\cdot\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\phi-\gamma\log(1+|z|^{2})&z\in B(2)\\
\max\{\phi-\gamma\log(1+|z|^{2}),T\}&z\in A(2,5)\\
T&z\in{\mathbb{P}}^{n-1}\setminus B(5).\end{array}\right.

(19)

Then
u
is
\omega_{0}
-psh function with zero Lelong number and satisfies
u(z)=\gamma^{-1}\cdot(\phi(z)-\gamma\log(1+|z|^{2}))
in a neighborhood of
\mathcal{C}\subset{\mathbb{C}}^{n-1}
.
Then as before we associate to
u
a psh function

\varphi=\max\{\log|z|^{2}+\pi^{*}u,2\log|z|^{2}\}

(20)

which has an isolated singularity at
0\in{\mathbb{C}}^{n}
. We leave it to the reader to use similar arguments as before to verify that
e_{n}(\varphi_{m})=\left(\frac{m-n+1}{m}\right)^{n}
and
e_{n}(\varphi)=1+\gamma^{-(n-1)}
.

We can also construct counterexamples in neighborhoods of
0\in{\mathbb{C}}^{n}
that are maximal outside
\{0\}
, by using an argument in
[
1
, Lemma 2.1]
as follows. Fix a bounded pseudoconvex domain
\Omega
in
{\mathbb{C}}^{n}
. Set

\varphi^{j}={\rm sup}\left\{v\in{\rm PSH}(\Omega);v\leq\varphi-\sup_{\Omega}\varphi\text{ on }B(0,j^{-1})^{\circ},\text{ and }v\leq 0\ \text{ on }\partial\Omega\right\}.

Then
\{\varphi^{j}\}
is an increasing sequence with limit denoted by
\tilde{\varphi}:=\lim_{j\rightarrow+\infty}\varphi^{j}
. Note that
\varphi^{j}\geq\varphi-\sup_{\Omega}\varphi
. So the limit psh function
\tilde{\varphi}
is less singular than
\varphi
, which implies
\mathcal{J}(m\tilde{\varphi})\supseteq\mathcal{J}(m\varphi)
. So
\tilde{\varphi}_{m}
is also less singular than
\varphi_{m}
, which implies
e_{n}(\tilde{\varphi}_{m})\leq e_{n}(\varphi_{m})
. By
[
1
, Lemma 2.1]
,
\tilde{\varphi}
satisfies
({\rm dd^{c}}\tilde{\varphi})^{n}=({\rm dd^{c}}\varphi)^{n}(\{0\})\delta_{0}
, whose proof we duplicate here for the reader’s convenience. On the one hand, we have
({\rm dd^{c}}\varphi^{j})^{n}=0
on
\Omega\setminus B(0,j^{-1})
and
({\rm dd^{c}}\varphi^{j})^{n}=({\rm dd^{c}}\varphi)^{n}
on
B(0,j^{-1})^{\circ}
. Letting

, we get
({\rm dd^{c}}\tilde{\varphi})^{n}=c\delta_{\{0\}}
with

. On the other hand, since
\tilde{\varphi}
is less singular than
\varphi
, by the comparison theorem of Lelong numbers (
[
8
, III. §7]
), we get
c\geq\int_{\{0\}}({\rm dd^{c}}\varphi)^{n}
. So indeed
c=\int_{\{0\}}({\rm dd^{c}}\varphi^{n})
.
In particular
e_{n}(\tilde{\varphi})=e_{n}(\varphi)>1
when
\varphi
comes from the previous examples.

We make the following conjecture, which could be thought as a local version of a result of Darvas from
[
7
]
.

Conjecture 5.1
.

Assume
u\in{\rm PSH}({\mathbb{P}}^{n-1},\omega_{0})
. Let
\varphi
be defined as in (
20
). Then
e_{n}(\varphi)=1
if and only if
u\in\mathcal{E}(\omega_{0})
, where
\mathcal{E}(\omega_{0})
denotes the set of
\omega_{0}
-psh functions of full mass as studied in
[
14
]
.

By using similar argument like before, one should be able to prove this when
e^{u}
is continuous and
u=\{-\infty\}
is contained in a compact subset of an affine chart.
To establish this in general, it seems to require an appropriate definition of the Monge-Ampère operator for general
u\in{\rm PSH}(\omega_{0})
.

We can also verify the above conjecture when
u
has analytic singularities. In fact we will derive a formula for
e_{n}(\varphi)
in this case, which seems to be interesting in its own right.
Recall that an
\omega_{0}
-psh function

has analytic singularities if there is an ideal sheaf
\mathcal{I}
and
c\in{\mathbb{R}}_{>0}
such that for any point
x\in{\mathbb{P}}^{n-1}
there exists an open set
U
such that
u-c\log\sum_{i}|f_{i}|^{2}
is bounded on
U
where
\{f_{1},\dots,f_{r}\}
are generators of
\mathcal{I}(U)
. In this case, we say that the singularities of
\varphi
are modeled on
\mathcal{I}^{c}
. By the metric characterization of normalization (see
[
16
, Remark 9.6.10]
), we can assume that the ideal sheaf is normal. Let
\rho_{0}:\hat{D}\rightarrow{\mathbb{P}}^{n-1}
be the (normalized) blowup of
\mathcal{I}
with exceptional divisor denoted by
E
. Denote by
H
the hyperplane line bundle of
{\mathbb{P}}^{n-1}
. Then the
{\mathbb{R}}
-line bundle
\rho_{0}^{*}H-cE
admits a bounded psh metric. For simplicity of notations, we will sometimes just use
H
to denote the pull back of hyperplane line bundle under birational morphisms.

Proposition 5.2
.

Assume that
u\in{\rm PSH}(\omega_{0})
has analytic singularities and
\varphi
is defined by the formula (
20
).
With the above notations, we have the identity:

\displaystyle e_{n}(\varphi)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 1+\sum_{p=0}^{n-2}(2^{n-1-p}-1)(H-cE)^{p}\cdot H^{n-2-p}\cdot cE.

(21)

Proof.

We first deal with the case when
A:=\rho_{0}^{*}H-cE
is a semiample
{\mathbb{Q}}
-divisor. The proof in this case is reduced to a purely algbro-geometric calculation by the following construction.
Choose
d\gg 1
such that
cd\in{\mathbb{N}}
and
\mathcal{O}_{\hat{D}}(dA)
is globally generated.
Let
\{s_{1},\dots,s_{N}\}
be a basis of
H^{0}(\hat{D},\mathcal{O}_{\hat{D}}(dA))
and
f_{i}=s_{i}\cdot(dc)s_{E}
be the corresponding homogeneous polynomial of degree
d
. Consider the psh function:

\phi_{d}(z)=\max\left\{\log\sum_{i}|f_{i}|^{2},2d\log|z|^{2}\right\}

(22)

Set
\mathfrak{m}=(z_{1},\dots,z_{n})
and
\mathfrak{a}=\langle f_{i};i=1,\dots,N\rangle+\mathfrak{m}^{2d}
. Then by the comparison theorem for generalized Lelong numbers (
[
8
, III.7.1]
) and
[
9
, Lemma 2.1]
, we get the identity:

e_{n}(\varphi)=e_{n}(d^{-1}\phi_{d})=d^{-n}{\rm mult}(\mathfrak{a})

(23)

where
{\rm mult}(\mathfrak{a})
is the Hilbert-Samuel multiplicity of the primary ideal
\mathfrak{a}
which we now calculate using intersection theory.

Let
\rho_{1}:X\rightarrow{\mathbb{C}}^{n}
be the standard blowup of the origin with exceptional divisor denoted by
D
which is isomorphic to
{\mathbb{P}}^{n-1}
. We get the identity
\rho_{1}^{*}\mathfrak{a}={\mathcal{O}}(-dD)\cdot(\mathcal{I}^{\prime}+\mathcal{I}_{D}^{d})
where
\mathcal{I}^{\prime}=\mathcal{I}^{cd}
. As mentioned above, we assume that
\mathcal{I}
is normal. Let
\rho_{2}:\hat{X}\rightarrow X
be the (normalized) blowup of
\mathcal{I}^{\prime}+\mathcal{I}_{D}^{d}
. Then
\hat{X}
is given by:

\hat{X}={\rm Proj}\left(\bigoplus_{m=0}^{+\infty}{(\mathcal{I}^{\prime}+\mathcal{I}_{D}^{d})^{m}}\right).

Note that
{(\mathcal{I}^{\prime}+\mathcal{I}_{D}^{d})^{m}}=\sum_{k=0}^{m}{\mathcal{I}^{\prime k}}\cdot\mathcal{I}_{D}^{d(m-k)}
.
The exceptional divisor is given by:

\mathcal{E}^{\prime}={\rm Proj}\left(\bigoplus_{m=0}^{+\infty}\sum_{k=0}^{m}{\mathcal{I}^{\prime k}}/{\mathcal{I}^{\prime k+1}}\cdot\mathcal{I}_{D}^{d(m-k)}\right).

Let
\tilde{\xi}
be the line bundle
\mathcal{O}(-\mathcal{E}^{\prime})
. Then
\tilde{\xi}|_{\mathcal{E}^{\prime}}=\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{E}^{\prime}}(1)
.

On the other hand, let
\rho_{0}:\hat{D}\rightarrow D
be the normalized blowup of
D\cong{\mathbb{P}}^{n-1}
along
Z
, which is also strict transform of
D
under
\rho_{2}
. Then
\hat{D}
is given by:

\hat{D}={\rm Proj}\left(\bigoplus_{m=0}^{+\infty}{\mathcal{I}^{\prime m}}\right)

with exceptional divisor given by:

E^{\prime}={\rm Proj}\left(\bigoplus_{m=0}^{+\infty}{\mathcal{I}^{\prime m}}/{\mathcal{I}^{\prime m+1}}\right).

Let
\xi
be the line bundle
\mathcal{O}(-E^{\prime})
. Then
\xi|_{E^{\prime}}=\mathcal{O}_{E^{\prime}}(1)
.

Let
\tilde{\xi}^{\prime}=\tilde{\xi}\otimes\rho_{2}^{*}H^{-d}
. Then
\tilde{\xi}^{\prime}|_{\mathcal{E}^{\prime}}\sim\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{E}^{\prime}}(E^{\prime})
. So we get
\tilde{\xi}|_{\mathcal{E}^{\prime}}=\tilde{\xi}^{\prime}\otimes\rho_{2}^{*}H^{d}|_{\mathcal{E}^{\prime}}=\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{E}^{\prime}}(E^{\prime})\otimes\pi^{*}H^{d}
.

Set
\rho=\rho_{2}\circ\rho_{1}
. Then
\rho^{*}\mathfrak{a}=\rho_{2}^{*}\mathcal{O}_{X}(-dD)\cdot\mathcal{O}_{\hat{X}}(-\mathcal{E}^{\prime})
and the multiplicity of
\mathfrak{a}
is given by:

\left(-\rho_{2}^{*}(dD)-\mathcal{E}^{\prime}\right)^{n-1}\cdot(\rho_{2}^{*}(dD)+\mathcal{E}^{\prime})=(\rho_{2}^{*}(dH)+\tilde{\xi})^{n-1}\cdot(\rho_{2}^{*}(dD)+\mathcal{E}^{\prime}).

(24)

Set
\rho_{2}^{*}(dH)=x
and
a_{k}=(x+\tilde{\xi})^{k}\cdot x^{n-1-k}\cdot\rho_{2}^{*}(dD)
. Then

\displaystyle a_{k}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(x+\tilde{\xi})^{k-1}\cdot x^{n-k}\cdot\rho_{2}^{*}(dD)+(x+\tilde{\xi})^{k-1}\cdot\tilde{\xi}\cdot x^{n-1-k}\cdot\rho_{2}^{*}(dD)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle a_{k-1}+(x+\tilde{\xi})^{k-1}\cdot x^{n-1-k}\cdot\rho_{2}^{*}(dD)\cdot(-\mathcal{E}^{\prime})=a_{k-1}+b_{k-1}

where

.
Note that
a_{0}=x^{n-1}\cdot\rho_{2}^{*}(dD)=d^{n}H^{n-1}\cdot{\mathbb{P}}^{n-1}=d^{n}

On the other hand, we have:

\displaystyle b_{k}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(x+\tilde{\xi})^{k-1}\cdot x^{n-k}\cdot\mathcal{E}^{\prime}+(x+\tilde{\xi})^{k-1}\cdot x^{n-1-k}\cdot(\tilde{\xi}^{\prime}+x)\cdot\mathcal{E}^{\prime}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2(x+\tilde{\xi})^{k-1}\cdot x^{n-k}\cdot\mathcal{E}^{\prime}+(dH-E^{\prime})^{k-1}\cdot(dH)^{n-1-k}\cdot E^{\prime}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2b_{k-1}+(dH-E^{\prime})^{k-1}\cdot H^{n-1-k}\cdot E^{\prime}.

\displaystyle b_{k}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{k}2^{j-1}(dH-E^{\prime})^{k-j}\cdot(dH)^{n-2-k+j}\cdot E^{\prime}

\displaystyle d^{n}\sum_{j=1}^{k}2^{j-1}(H-cE)^{k-j}\cdot H^{n-2-k+j}\cdot(cE).

\displaystyle{\rm mult}(\mathfrak{a})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle a_{n-1}+b_{n-1}=H^{n}+\sum_{k=1}^{n-2}b_{k}+b_{n-1}=d^{n}+\delta

where we have:

\displaystyle d^{-n}\delta

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{n-1}d^{-n}b_{k}=\sum_{k=1}^{n-1}\sum_{j=1}^{k}2^{j-1}(H-cE)^{k-j}\cdot H^{n-2-k+j}\cdot(cE)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{p=0}^{n-2}\sum_{j=1}^{n-p}2^{j-1}(H-cE)^{p}\cdot H^{n-2-p}\cdot(cE)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{p=0}^{n-2}(2^{n-1-p}-1)(H-cE)^{p}\cdot H^{n-2-p}\cdot cE.

\rho^{*}\varphi=\log|F|^{2}+\varphi^{\prime}=\log|F_{1}|^{2}+\log|F_{2}|^{2c}+\varphi^{\prime}

where

with

(resp.

) being a locally defined holomorphic functions on

defining the Cartier divisor
\rho_{2}^{*}D
(resp.
\mathcal{E}
), and
\varphi^{\prime}
is a bounded psh function locally defined up to a pluriharmonic function.
{\rm dd^{c}}\varphi^{\prime}

-line bundle associated to the
{\mathbb{R}}
-Cartier divisor

. By the same argument as in

, Proof of (4.5)]
, we can calculate as follows:

\displaystyle({\rm dd^{c}}\varphi)^{n}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\rho_{*}{\rm dd^{c}}((\rho^{*}\varphi)({\bf 1}_{\hat{X}\setminus{\rm Supp}(\rho_{2}^{*}D\cup\mathcal{E})}({\rm dd^{c}}\varphi)^{n-1})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\rho_{*}{\rm dd^{c}}((\log|F|^{2}+\varphi^{\prime})({\rm dd^{c}}\varphi^{\prime})^{n-1})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\rho_{*}(([\rho_{2}^{*}D]+c[\mathcal{E}])\wedge({\rm dd^{c}}\varphi^{\prime})^{n-1}+({\rm dd^{c}}\varphi^{\prime})^{n})

So we get:

({\rm dd^{c}}\varphi)^{n}(\{0\})=\int_{\rho_{2}^{*}D+c\mathcal{E}}({\rm dd^{c}}\varphi^{\prime})^{n-1}=(-\rho_{2}^{*}D-c\mathcal{E})^{n-1}\cdot(\rho_{2}^{*}D+\mathcal{E}).

Now the same method for calculating (
24
) applies without change to any
c\in{\mathbb{R}}_{>0}
, and hence we have proved our formula (
21
).
∎

Corollary 5.5
.

Assume that
u\in{{\rm PSH}}(\omega_{0})
has analytic singularities.
Then
e_{n}(\varphi)=1
if and only if
\mathcal{I}=\mathcal{O}_{{\mathbb{P}}^{n-1}}
, if and only if
u\in\mathcal{E}(\omega_{0})
.

Proof.

If
\mathcal{I}=\mathcal{O}_{{\mathbb{P}}^{n-1}}
then
\varphi
has the same type of singularity as
\log|z|^{2}
and hence
e_{n}(\varphi)=1
.
Formula (
21
) implies that
e_{n}(\varphi)=1
only if

(H-cE)^{p}\cdot H^{n-2-p}\cdot cE=0,\text{ for all }p=0,\dots,n-2.

(25)

Note that

\delta:=H^{n-1}-(\rho_{0}^{*}H-cE)^{n-1}=\sum_{p=0}^{n-2}(H-cE)^{p}\cdot H^{n-2-p}\cdot(cE).

On the other hand, the non-pluripolar volume of
\omega_{0}+{\rm dd^{c}}u
is equal to
(\rho_{0}^{*}H-cE)^{n-1}
.
So
u\in\mathcal{E}(\omega_{0})
if and only if
\delta=0
, if and only if the condition (
25
) holds true.

Note that

\delta=H^{n-1}-(H-cE)^{n-1}=(n-1)\int_{0}^{c}(H-tE)^{n-2}\cdot Edt.

Because
H-\epsilon E
is ample for
0<\epsilon\ll 1
,
\delta=0
if and only if
E=\emptyset
, i.e.
\mathcal{I}=\mathcal{O}_{{\mathbb{P}}^{n-1}}
.

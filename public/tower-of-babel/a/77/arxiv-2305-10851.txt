Variational Bihamiltonian Cohomologies and Integrable Hierarchies III: Linear Reciprocal Transformations
Si-Qi Liu, Zhe Wang, Youjin Zhang

Abstract
. For an integrable hierarchy which possesses a bihamiltonian structure with semisimple hydrodynamic limit, we prove that the linear reciprocal transformation with respect to any of its symmetry transforms it to another bihamiltonian integrable hierarchy. Moreover, we show that the central invariants of the bihamiltonian structure are preserved under such a linear reciprocal transformation.



1.
Introduction

Reciprocal transformations are transformations of independent variables of evolutionary PDEs, which originally appear in the study of gas dynamics, see
[
40
]
and references therein. Such transformations also play important roles in the study of the theory of integrable hierarchies. For example, they are used to establish relations between different integrable hierarchies and reveal geometric structures behind them.

Consider a system of evolutionary PDEs of the form

(1.1)

\frac{\partial u^{\alpha}}{\partial t}=A^{\alpha}_{\beta}(u)u^{\beta}_{x}+\varepsilon\left(B^{\alpha}_{\beta}(u)u^{\beta}_{xx}+C^{\alpha}_{\beta\gamma}(u)u^{\beta}_{x}u^{\gamma}_{x}\right)+\dots,

here and henceforth we assume summations over repeated upper and lower Greek indices.
If it has a nontrivial conservation law

\displaystyle\frac{\partial a(u,u_{x},u_{xx},\dots)}{\partial t}=\frac{\partial b(u,u_{x},u_{xx},\dots)}{\partial x},

then we can define a reciprocal transformation
(x,t)\mapsto(y,s)
as follows:

dy=a(u)dx+b(u)dt,\quad s=t,

and we call such a reciprocal transformation a nonlinear one. We can also define a linear reciprocal transformation by exchanging the spatial and time variables, i.e., the transformation
(x,t)\mapsto(y,s)
given by

y=t,\quad s=x.

We can compose these two kinds of reciprocal transformations to obtain general ones. Typical examples of nonlinear reciprocal transformations include the one which transforms the Camassa-Holm equation to the first negative flow of the KdV hierarchy, and the one which transforms the 2-component Camassa-Holm equation to the first negative flow of the AKNS hierarchy
[
4
,
5
,
9
,
23
,
24
]
.
An example of linear reciprocal transformation is given by the transformation between the extended Toda hierarchy and the nonlinear Schrödinger hierarchy
[
6
]
. Linear reciprocal transformations are also used in
[
18
]
to establish relations between a certain type of bihamiltonian integrable hierarchies and Frobenius manifolds.

An important problem concerned with reciprocal transformations is whether such transformations preserve the Hamiltonian property of Hamiltonian evolutionary PDEs. This problem is studied and an affirmative answer is obtained in
[
22
,
39
,
44
,
46
]

when the system (
1.1
) is of hydrodynamic type, i.e., of the form

\frac{\partial u^{\alpha}}{\partial t}=A^{\alpha}_{\beta}(u)u^{\beta}_{x}.

It is shown in
[
22
]
that after performing a nonlinear reciprocal transformation, a Hamiltonian system of hydrodynamic type is transformed to a Hamiltonian system but with a non-local Hamiltonian structure of a certain special type
[
20
]
. These non-local Hamiltonian structures are generalized to Jacobi structures
[
34
]
which are certain non-local Hamiltonian structures for deformations of non-local Hamiltonian systems of hydrodynamic type, and their transformation rules under nonlinear reciprocal transformations are given in
[
34
]
. More general non-local Hamiltonian structures are introduced and studied in
[
37
]
and the problem of their classification under reciprocal transformations is studied in
[
36
]
.

Unlike nonlinear ones, linear reciprocal transformations preserve the locality of Hamiltonian structures of hydrodynamic type, as it is shown in
[
39
,
44
]
. Furthermore, if the original system admits a semisimple bihamiltonian structure of hydrodynamic type, then the transformed system again possesses a semisimple bihamiltonian structure of hydrodynamic type
[
46
]
. However, it is not easy to generalize this result to the bihamiltonian dispersionful systems with hydrodynamic limits.

In this paper we study the transformation rule of the bihamiltonian structure of a bihamiltonian integrable hierarchy which possesses hydrodynamic limit under
linear reciprocal transformations.
As an illustrative example, we recall here the linear reciprocal transformation which relates the Toda hierarchy to the nonlinear Schrödinger hierarchy, as it is given in
[
6
]
. The Toda hierarchy can be described by the following Lax pairs:

(1.2)

\varepsilon\frac{\partial L}{\partial t_{k}}=[L^{k}_{+},L],\quad k\geq 0,

with the Lax operator

L=\Lambda+v+e^{u}\Lambda^{-1},

here
\Lambda=\exp(\varepsilon\partial_{x})
is the shift operator along the spatial variable. For example, we have

\frac{\partial v}{\partial t_{1}}=\frac{1}{\varepsilon}(\Lambda-1)e^{u(x)},\quad\frac{\partial u}{\partial t_{1}}=\frac{1}{\varepsilon}(1-\Lambda^{-1})v(x).

Introduce new dependent variables

\varphi(x)=\Lambda^{-1}v(x),\quad\rho(x)=e^{u(x)},

then we have

(1.3)

\varepsilon\frac{\partial\varphi}{\partial t_{1}}=\rho-\rho^{-},\quad\varepsilon\frac{\partial\rho}{\partial t_{1}}=\rho(\varphi^{+}-\varphi).

Here and henceforth we use the notation

\varphi^{+}:=\Lambda\varphi,\quad\rho^{+}:=\Lambda\rho,\quad\varphi^{-}:=\Lambda^{-1}\varphi,\quad\rho^{-}:=\Lambda^{-1}\rho.

Similarly, we have the following second flow of the Toda hierarchy (
1.2
):

(1.4)

\displaystyle\varepsilon\frac{\partial\varphi}{\partial t_{2}}

\displaystyle=\varphi^{+}\rho+\varphi\rho-\varphi\rho^{-}-\varphi^{-}\rho^{-}

(1.5)

\displaystyle\varepsilon\frac{\partial\rho}{\partial t_{2}}

\displaystyle=\rho\varphi^{+}\varphi^{+}-\rho\varphi^{2}+\rho\rho^{+}-\rho\rho^{-}.

Let us perform the linear reciprocal transformation with respect to the flow (
1.3
), i.e., we represent
\frac{\partial}{\partial x}
in terms of the derivatives with respect to the flow
\frac{\partial}{\partial t_{1}}
:

\displaystyle\frac{\partial\varphi}{\partial x}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\rho}\frac{\partial\rho}{\partial t_{1}}+\varepsilon\left(\frac{1}{2\rho^{2}}\frac{\partial\rho}{\partial t_{1}}\frac{\partial\varphi}{\partial t_{1}}-\frac{1}{2\rho}\frac{\partial^{2}\varphi}{\partial t_{1}^{2}}\right)+O(\varepsilon^{2}),

\displaystyle\frac{\partial\rho}{\partial x}

\displaystyle=\frac{\partial\varphi}{\partial t_{1}}+\varepsilon\left(-\frac{1}{2\rho^{2}}\left(\frac{\partial\rho}{\partial t_{1}}\right)^{2}+\frac{1}{2\rho}\frac{\partial^{2}\rho}{\partial t_{1}^{2}}\right)+O(\varepsilon^{2}).

This linear reciprocal transformation can be applied to other flows of the Toda hierarchy. For example, a straightforward computation yields

\frac{\partial\varphi}{\partial t_{2}}=2\varphi\frac{\partial\varphi}{\partial t_{1}}+2\frac{\partial\rho}{\partial t_{1}}-\varepsilon\frac{\partial^{2}\varphi}{\partial t_{1}^{2}},\quad\frac{\partial\rho}{\partial t_{2}}=2\varphi\frac{\partial\rho}{\partial t_{1}}+2\rho\frac{\partial\varphi}{\partial t_{1}}+\varepsilon\frac{\partial^{2}\rho}{\partial t_{1}^{2}}.

If we rewrite
t_{1}
as the new spatial variable
X
, the above flow coincides with the nonlinear Schrödinger equation:

\frac{\partial\varphi}{\partial t_{2}}=2\varphi\varphi_{X}+2\rho_{X}-\varepsilon\varphi_{XX},\quad\frac{\partial\rho}{\partial t_{2}}=2\varphi\rho_{X}+2\rho\varphi_{X}+\varepsilon\rho_{XX}.

Both the Toda hierarchy and the nonlinear Schrödinger hierarchy are bihamiltonian. The bihamiltonian structure of the Toda hierarchy can be represented by the following Hamiltonian operators in terms of the variables
(\varphi,\rho)
by

\mathcal{P}_{0}=\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\begin{pmatrix}0&\rho-\rho^{-}\Lambda^{-1}\\[5.0pt]
-\rho+\rho\Lambda&0\end{pmatrix},\quad\mathcal{P}_{1}=\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\begin{pmatrix}\rho\Lambda-\rho^{-}\Lambda^{-1}&\varphi\rho-\varphi\rho^{-}\Lambda^{-1}\\[5.0pt]
-\varphi\rho+\varphi^{+}\rho\Lambda&\rho\rho^{+}\Lambda-\rho\rho^{-}\Lambda^{-1}\end{pmatrix}.

And the Hamiltonian operators of bihamiltonian structure of the nonlinear Schrödinger equation in terms of
(\varphi,\rho)
reads

\mathcal{P}_{0}=\begin{pmatrix}0&\partial_{X}\\[5.0pt]
\partial_{X}&0\end{pmatrix},\quad\mathcal{P}_{1}=\begin{pmatrix}2\partial_{X}&\varphi\partial_{X}+\varphi_{X}-\varepsilon\partial_{X}^{2}\\[5.0pt]
\varphi\partial_{X}+\varepsilon\partial_{X}^{2}&2\rho\partial_{X}+\rho_{X}\end{pmatrix}.

For a general bihamiltonian integrable hierarchy we prove, under the condition of semisimplicity, that linear reciprocal transformations always preserve the bihamiltonian property. To state this result in a more precise way, let us consider the following bihamiltonian evolutionary PDEs

(1.6)

\frac{\partial u^{\alpha}}{\partial t}=\mathcal{P}^{\alpha\beta}_{0}\frac{\delta H_{0}}{\delta u^{\beta}}=\mathcal{P}^{\alpha\beta}_{1}\frac{\delta H_{1}}{\delta u^{\beta}}=A^{\alpha}_{\beta}u^{\beta}_{x}+O(\varepsilon)

with Hamiltonian operators
\mathcal{P}_{i}=(\mathcal{P}^{\alpha\beta}_{i})
and Hamiltonians
H_{i}
of the form

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}^{\alpha\beta}_{i}

\displaystyle=g^{\alpha\beta}_{i}\partial_{x}+\Gamma^{\alpha\beta}_{\gamma}u^{\gamma}_{x}+\varepsilon(A^{\alpha\beta}_{i}\partial_{x}^{2}+B^{\alpha\beta}_{i;\gamma}u^{\gamma}_{x}\partial_{x}+C^{\alpha\beta}_{i;\gamma\lambda}u^{\gamma}_{x}u^{\lambda}_{x})+O(\varepsilon^{2}),

\displaystyle H_{i}

\displaystyle=\int h_{i}(u)+\varepsilon a_{i;\alpha}(u)u^{\alpha}_{x}+O(\varepsilon^{2}),

where
\varepsilon
is the dispersion parameter. We require that the leading terms of the above bihamiltonian structure are semisimple, i.e., the roots of characteristic equation

\det(g_{1}^{\alpha\beta}-\lambda g_{0}^{\alpha\beta})=0

are distinct and non-constant. By exchanging the spatial and time variables of the system (
1.6
), we arrive at

\frac{\partial u^{\alpha}}{\partial x}=(A^{-1})^{\alpha}_{\beta}\frac{\partial u^{\beta}}{\partial t}+O(\varepsilon).

If we still use
x
to denote the spatial variable and use
\tilde{t}
to denote the time variable of the above system, we obtain from (
1.6
) by the linear reciprocal transformation the following system of evolutionary PDEs:

(1.7)

\frac{\partial u^{\alpha}}{\partial\tilde{t}}=(A^{-1})^{\alpha}_{\beta}u^{\beta}_{x}+O(\varepsilon).

Let us state the main result of the present paper.

Theorem 1.1
.

The system of evolutionary PDEs (
1.7
) has the following properties:

(1)

It possesses a bihamiltonian structure with semisimple hydrodymanic limit. The central invariants of this bihamiltonian structure coincide with those of the bihamiltonian structure of (
1.6
).

(2)

After performing the linear reciprocal transformation, any symmetry of the system (
1.6
) of the form

\frac{\partial u^{\alpha}}{\partial s}=\mathcal{P}^{\alpha\beta}_{0}\frac{\delta G_{0}}{\delta u^{\beta}}=\mathcal{P}^{\alpha\beta}_{1}\frac{\delta G_{1}}{\delta u^{\beta}}

is transformed to a symmetry of (
1.7
) which shares the same bihamiltonian structure with (
1.7
).

This result provides us a convenient tool to classify bihamiltonian integrable hierarchies with semisimple hydrodynamic limits under linear reciprocal transformations and Miura-type transformations. More precisely, given two bihamiltonian integrable hierarchies with hydrodynamic limits, we obtain a very simple method to determine whether we can transform one hierarchy to the other by performing a linear reciprocal transformation composed by a Miura-type transformation. For example, we show that the equivalence classes of scalar bihamiltonian integrable hierarchy under linear reciprocal transformations and Miura-type transformations are parametrized by smooth functions of a single variable.

This paper is organized as follows. In Sect.
2
, we review the theory of variational bihamiltonian cohomology which is our main tool to prove that linear reciprocal transformations preserve bihamiltonian property. In Sect.
3
we give the proof of Theorem
1.1
and use it to study the problem of classification of bihamiltonian integrable hierarchies under linear reciprocal transformations and Miura-type transformations. In Sect.
4
we present some examples, and prove some general results on relations of linear reciprocal transformations with the theory of Frobenius manifolds and Dubrovin-Zhang hierarchies. Finally in Sect.
5
we give some concluding remarks.

2.
Variational bihamiltonian cohomology

This section is a technical preparation for proving the main result of the present paper.

2.1.
Infinite jet spaces and bihamiltonian structures

We start by recalling the representation of bihamiltonian structures in terms of local functionals on infinite jet spaces of super manifolds, which is also called the
\theta
-formalism in the literature
[
8
,
26
,
34
,
35
]
. One can refer to
[
28
]
for a detailed introduction.

Let
M
be a smooth manifold of dimension
n
and let
\hat{M}=\Pi T^{*}M
be the super manifold of dimension
(n|n)
obtained by reversing the parity of the fiber of
T^{*}M
. Denote by
(u^{1},\dots,u^{n};\theta_{1},\dots,\theta_{n})
a system of local canonical coordinates on
T^{*}M
, then the manifold
\hat{M}
can be described locally by the same coordinates with the parity of the fiber coordinates
\theta_{\alpha}
reversed, i.e., they satisfy the anti-commuting relations

\theta_{\alpha}\theta_{\beta}+\theta_{\beta}\theta_{\alpha}=0.

These fiber coordinates are hence called odd variables. We denote by
J^{\infty}(\hat{M})
the infinite jet space of
\hat{M}
which can be described by local coordinates
(u^{\alpha,s};\theta_{\alpha}^{s})
, where
\alpha=1,\dots,n
,
s\geq 0
and
u^{\alpha,0}=u^{\alpha}
,
\theta_{\alpha}^{0}=\theta_{\alpha}
. Let us denote by
\hat{\mathcal{A}}
the space of differential polynomials on
J^{\infty}(\hat{M})
, locally it is the ring

C^{\infty}(u)[[u^{\alpha,s+1},\theta_{\alpha}^{s}\mid\alpha=1,\dots,n;s\geq 0]].

There exists a global vector field
\partial_{x}
on
J^{\infty}(\hat{M})
defined by

\partial_{x}=\sum_{s\geq 0}u^{\alpha,s+1}\frac{\partial}{\partial u^{\alpha,s}}+\theta_{\alpha}^{s+1}\frac{\partial}{\partial\theta_{\alpha}^{s}}.

By using this vector field, we define the space
\hat{\mathcal{F}}
of local functionals as

\hat{\mathcal{F}}:=\hat{\mathcal{A}}/\partial_{x}\hat{\mathcal{A}},

and for
f\in\hat{\mathcal{A}}
, we denote by
\int f
the image of
f
in
\hat{\mathcal{F}}
. Furthermore, we define two gradations on
\hat{\mathcal{A}}
and
\hat{\mathcal{F}}
by setting

\deg_{x}u^{\alpha,s}=s,\quad\deg_{x}\theta_{\alpha}^{s}=s,\quad\deg_{\theta}u^{\alpha,s}=0,\quad\deg_{\theta}\theta_{\alpha}^{s}=1.

We denote the homogeneous subspaces with respect to these gradations by

\hat{\mathcal{A}}^{p}=\{f\in\hat{\mathcal{A}}\mid\deg_{\theta}f=p\},\quad\hat{\mathcal{A}}_{d}=\{f\in\hat{\mathcal{A}}\mid\deg_{x}f=d\},\quad\hat{\mathcal{A}}^{p}_{d}=\hat{\mathcal{A}}^{p}\cap\hat{\mathcal{A}}_{d}.

Since the vector field
\partial_{x}
is homogeneous with respect to both
\deg_{x}
and
\deg_{\theta}
, the quotient space
\hat{\mathcal{F}}
has natural gradations induced from those on
\hat{\mathcal{A}}
. We use
\hat{\mathcal{F}}^{p}
,
\hat{\mathcal{F}}_{d}
and
\hat{\mathcal{F}}^{p}_{d}
to denote the corresponding subspaces of homogeneous elements.

There is a graded Lie algebra structure on
\hat{\mathcal{F}}
given by the so-called Schouten-Nijenhuis bracket. Define the bilinear bracket
[-,-]:\hat{\mathcal{F}}\times\hat{\mathcal{F}}\to\hat{\mathcal{F}}
by

(2.1)

[P,Q]=\int\frac{\delta P}{\delta\theta_{\alpha}}\frac{\delta Q}{\delta u^{\alpha}}+(-1)^{p}\frac{\delta P}{\delta u^{\alpha}}\frac{\delta Q}{\delta\theta_{\alpha}},\quad P\in\hat{\mathcal{F}}^{p},\quad Q\in\hat{\mathcal{F}},

here the variational derivative is defined for any
Q\in\hat{\mathcal{F}}
by

\frac{\delta Q}{\delta u^{\alpha}}=\sum_{s\geq 0}(-\partial_{x})^{s}\frac{\partial f}{\partial u^{\alpha,s}},\quad\frac{\delta Q}{\delta\theta_{\alpha}}=\sum_{s\geq 0}(-\partial_{x})^{s}\frac{\partial f}{\partial\theta_{\alpha}^{s}},\quad Q=\int f.

This bracket is antisymmetric and satisfies the graded Jacobi identity. More precisely, for
P\in\hat{\mathcal{F}}^{p}
,
Q\in\hat{\mathcal{F}}^{q}
and
R\in\hat{\mathcal{F}}^{r}
, we have

(2.2)

\displaystyle[P,Q]=(-1)^{pq}[Q,P],

(2.3)

\displaystyle(-1)^{pr}[[P,Q],R]+(-1)^{qp}[[Q,R],P]+(-1)^{rq}[[R,P],Q]=0.

The Schouten-Nijenhuis bracket is preserved under Miura-type transformations. A Miura-type transofrmation is a change of coordinate on
J^{\infty}(M)
that preserves the action of
\partial_{x}
, i.e., it is of the form

(2.4)

u^{\alpha,s}\mapsto w^{\alpha,s}=\sum_{k\geq 0}\partial_{x}^{s}f^{\alpha}_{k}(u),\quad f^{\alpha}_{k}(u)\in\hat{\mathcal{A}}^{0}_{k},\quad s\geq 0,

where the leading terms satisfy the condition

\det\left(\frac{\partial f^{\alpha}_{0}(u)}{\partial u^{\beta}}\right)\neq 0.

Given a Miura-type transformation of the form (
2.4
), it is proved in
[
34
]
that the following transformation rules for odd variables preserve the Schouten-Nijenhuis bracket:

\sigma_{\alpha}^{s}=\partial_{x}^{s}\sum_{k\geq 0}(-\partial_{x})^{k}\left(\frac{\partial u^{\beta}}{\partial w^{\alpha,k}}\theta_{\beta}\right),\quad s\geq 0,

i.e., if we represent two local functionals
P,Q\in\hat{\mathcal{F}}
in terms of the new coordinates
w^{\alpha,s}
and
\sigma_{\alpha}^{s}
, then their Schouten-Nijenhuis bracket can be computed by using the formula (
2.1
) with
(u^{\alpha};\theta_{\alpha})
replaced by
(w^{\alpha};\sigma_{\alpha})
.

We define a Hamlitonian structure to be a local functional
P\in\hat{\mathcal{F}}^{2}
such that
[P,P]
= 0 and a bihamiltonian structure
(P_{0},P_{1})
to be a pair of Hamiltonian structures such that
[P_{0},P_{1}]=0
. We also define a system of evolutionary PDEs to be a local functional
X\in\hat{\mathcal{F}}^{1}
, and we call it is Hamiltonian if there exists a Hamiltonian structure
P
and a local functional
H\in\hat{\mathcal{F}}^{0}
such that
X=-[P,H]
. Similarly we call
X
is bihamiltonian if there exists a bihamiltonian structure
(P_{0},P_{1})
and two local functionals
H_{0},H_{1}\in\hat{\mathcal{F}}^{0}
such that
X=-[P_{0},H_{0}]=-[P_{1},H_{1}]
.

In the literature, a system of Hamiltonian evolutionary PDEs is usually represented as follows:

\frac{\partial u^{\alpha}}{\partial t}=X^{\alpha}=\mathcal{P}^{\alpha\beta}\frac{\delta H}{\delta u^{\beta}},\quad X^{\alpha}\in\hat{\mathcal{A}}^{0},\quad H\in\hat{\mathcal{F}}^{0},

where
\mathcal{P}=(\mathcal{P}^{\alpha\beta})
is the Hamiltonian operator which has the form

\mathcal{P}^{\alpha\beta}=\sum_{s\geq 0}P^{\alpha\beta}_{s}\partial_{x}^{s},\quad P^{\alpha\beta}_{s}\in\hat{\mathcal{A}}^{0}.

Then in the language of local functionals, the above system of evolutionary PDEs corresponds to the local functional

X=\int X^{\alpha}\theta_{\alpha}\in\hat{\mathcal{F}}^{1}

and the Hamiltonian operator
\mathcal{P}
corresponds to a local functional

P=\frac{1}{2}\int\sum_{s\geq 0}P^{\alpha\beta}_{s}\theta_{\alpha}\theta_{\beta}^{s}\in\hat{\mathcal{F}}^{2}

which satisfies the condition
[P,P]=0
. These lcoal functionals are related by the formula

X=-[P,H].

Conversely, given local functionals
H\in\hat{\mathcal{F}}^{0}
,
X\in\hat{\mathcal{F}}^{1}
and
P\in\hat{\mathcal{F}}^{2}
which satisfy the conditions
X=-[H,P]
and
[P,P]=0
, then we can define a Hamiltonian operator
\mathcal{P}=(\mathcal{P}^{\alpha\beta})
with

(2.5)

\mathcal{P}^{\alpha\beta}=\sum_{s\geq 0}\frac{\partial}{\partial\theta_{\beta}^{s}}\left(\frac{\delta P}{\delta\theta_{\alpha}}\right)\partial_{x}^{s},

and a system of evolutionary PDEs

\frac{\partial u^{\alpha}}{\partial t}=\frac{\delta X}{\delta\theta_{\alpha}},

which is a Hamiltonian system in the sense that

\frac{\partial u^{\alpha}}{\partial t}=\mathcal{P}^{\alpha\beta}\frac{\delta H}{\delta u^{\beta}}.

In the remaining part of the subsection, we review the deformation theory of (bi)Hamiltonian structures. We start by recalling the concept of Hamiltonian structures of hydrodynamic type. For a Hamiltonian structure
P\in\hat{\mathcal{F}}^{2}_{1}
, the corresponding Hamiltonian operator
\mathcal{P}
defined in (
2.5
) has the form

\mathcal{P}^{\alpha\beta}=g^{\alpha\beta}\partial_{x}+\Gamma^{\alpha\beta}_{\gamma}u^{\gamma,1}.

If
\det(g^{\alpha\beta})\neq 0
, then we call
P
is of hydrodynamic type. It is proved in
[
17
]

that in this case
(g_{\alpha\beta}):=(g^{\alpha\beta})^{-1}
defines a flat metric on
M
and
\Gamma^{\alpha}_{\beta\gamma}=-g_{\beta\mu}\Gamma^{\mu\alpha}_{\gamma}
are the Christoffel symbols of the Levi-Civita connection of
(g_{\alpha\beta})
.

Given a bihamiltonian structure
(P_{0},P_{1})
of hydrodynamic type, then we have a pair of flat (contravariant) metrics
(g_{0}^{\alpha\beta},g_{1}^{\alpha\beta})
which forms a flat pencil
[
12
]
. This bihamiltonian structure is called semisimple if the roots of the characteristic equation

\det(g_{1}^{\alpha\beta}-\lambda g_{0}^{\alpha\beta})=0

are all distinct and non-constant. In this case, if we denote by
\lambda^{1},\dots,\lambda^{n}
the
n
distinct solutions of the above equation, then it is proved in
[
21
]
that they can be used as local coordinates of
M
, and they are called the canonical coordinates of the semisimple bihamiltonian structure
(P_{0},P_{1})
. In terms of the canonical coordinates, the two metrics
g_{0}^{ij},g_{1}^{ij}
are diagonal and have the form

g_{0}^{ij}(\lambda)=f^{i}(\lambda)\delta^{ij},\quad g_{1}^{ij}(\lambda)=\lambda^{i}f^{i}(\lambda)\delta^{ij},

here
f^{i}(\lambda)
are some non-zero functions.

An important problem in the theory of integrable hierarchy is to classify the Hamiltonian (bihamiltonian) structures with hydrodynamic limits, or equivalently, to study the deformation theory of hydrodynamic Hamiltonian (bihamiltonian) structures. The classification is completed in a series of papers
[
7
,
8
,
10
,
15
,
18
,
26
,
35
]
by computing the so called Hamiltonian (bihamiltonian) cohomology. The deformation problem can be described as follows. Given a Hamiltonian structure
P
of hydrodynamic type, we want to classify all the possible deformations

\tilde{P}=P+Q,\quad Q\in\hat{\mathcal{F}}^{2}_{\geq 2}

such that
\tilde{P}
is a Hamiltonian structure. We regard two deformations to be equivalent if one can be obtained from the other by a Miura-type transformation. Similarly we study the deformation problem of a semisimple bihamiltonian structure
(P_{0},P_{1})
of hydrodynamic type. The classification results of Hamiltonian (bihamiltonian) structures are summarized in the following theorem.

Theorem 2.1

(
[
8
,
10
,
15
,
26
]
)
.

Let
P
be a Hamiltonian structure of hydrodynamic type and
(P_{0},P_{1})
be a semisimple bihamiltonian structure of hydrodynamic type.

(1)

All the deformations of
P
are trivial, i.e.,they are Miura-equivalent to the trivial deformation
\tilde{P}=P
.

(2)

Equivalent classes of deformations of
(P_{0},P_{1})
are parmeterized by
n
functions of a single variable. These functions are called the central invariants of a deformation.

Let us give a more detailed description on the central invairants of any deformation
(\tilde{P}_{0},\tilde{P}_{1})
of a semisimple bihamiltonian structure
(P_{0},P_{1})
of hydrodynamic type. In terms of the canonical coordinates
(\lambda^{1},\dots,\lambda^{n})
of
(P_{0},P_{1})
, the Hamiltonian operators
\tilde{\mathcal{P}}_{0}
and
\tilde{\mathcal{P}}_{1}
of
\tilde{P}_{0}
and
\tilde{P}_{1}
can be represented in the form

\tilde{\mathcal{P}}_{a}^{ij}=\sum_{k\geq 1}\sum_{s=0}^{k}\tilde{P}^{ij}_{a;k,s}\partial_{x}^{s},\quad\tilde{P}^{ij}_{a;k,s}\in\hat{\mathcal{A}}^{0}_{k-s},\quad a=0,1,

in particular we know that

\tilde{P}^{ij}_{0;1,1}=f^{i}(\lambda)\delta^{ij},\quad\tilde{P}^{ij}_{1;1,1}=\lambda^{i}f^{i}(\lambda)\delta^{ij}.

Then the central invariants can be explicitly computed by the following formulae:

(2.6)

c_{i}(\lambda^{i})=\frac{1}{3(f^{i})^{2}}\left(\tilde{P}^{ii}_{1;3,3}-\lambda^{i}\tilde{P}^{ii}_{0;3,3}+\sum_{j\neq i}\frac{\left(\tilde{P}^{ji}_{1;2,2}-\lambda^{i}\tilde{P}^{ji}_{0;2,2}\right)^{2}}{f^{j}(\lambda^{j}-\lambda^{i})}\right),\quad i=1,\dots,n.

Note that
c_{i}(\lambda^{i})
is a function depending only on the variable
\lambda^{i}
. Conversely, given
n
functions
c_{1}(\lambda^{1}),\dots,c_{n}(\lambda^{n})
of a single variable, we can construct a unique equivalence class of deformations of
(P_{0},P_{1})
such that its central invariants are exactly given by
c_{1}(\lambda^{1}),\dots,c_{n}(\lambda^{n})
. Such a deformation, up to Miura-type transformations, can be represented by

\tilde{P}_{0}=P_{0},\quad\tilde{P}_{1}=P_{1}+[P_{0},Q]+R,\quad R\in\hat{\mathcal{F}}^{2}_{\geq 4},

where
Q\in\hat{\mathcal{F}}^{1}_{2}
is given by

(2.7)

Q=\left[P_{1},\int\sum_{i}c_{i}(\lambda^{i})\lambda^{i,1}\log\lambda^{i,1}\right]-\left[P_{0},\int\sum_{i}\lambda^{i}c_{i}(\lambda^{i})\lambda^{i,1}\log\lambda^{i,1}\right].

The following theorem says that any deformation of a semisimple bihamiltonian structure induces a unique deformation of the corresponding bihamiltonian hierarchy of hydrodynamic type.

Theorem 2.2

(
[
15
]
)
.

Let
(P_{0},P_{1})
be a semisimple bihamiltonian structure of hydrodynamic type and let
X\in\hat{\mathcal{F}}^{1}_{1}
satisfy the condition
[X,P_{0}]=[X,P_{1}]=0
. If
(\tilde{P}_{0},\tilde{P}_{1})
is any deformation of
(P_{0},P_{1})
, then there exists a unique deformation
\tilde{X}
of
X
such that
[\tilde{X},\tilde{P}_{0}]=[\tilde{X},\tilde{P}_{1}]=0
.

2.2.
Variational bihamiltonian cohomology

In the previous subsection, we mainly consider the space of local functionals
\hat{\mathcal{F}}
. In this subsection, we focus on the space of derivations
\mathrm{Der}^{\partial_{x}}(\hat{\mathcal{A}})
and it is defined by

\mathrm{Der}^{\partial_{x}}(\hat{\mathcal{A}})=\bigoplus_{p\geq-1}\mathrm{Der}^{\partial_{x}}(\hat{\mathcal{A}})^{p},

where
\mathrm{Der}^{\partial_{x}}(\hat{\mathcal{A}})^{p}
is the space of derivations with
\deg_{\theta}=p
that commute with
\partial_{x}
. In another word, an element
X\in\mathrm{Der}^{\partial_{x}}(\hat{\mathcal{A}})^{p}
is a linear map
\hat{\mathcal{A}}\to\hat{\mathcal{A}}
such that

X(\hat{\mathcal{A}}^{q})\subseteq\hat{\mathcal{A}}^{p+q},\quad[X,\partial_{x}]=0,

and for any differential polynomials
f\in\hat{\mathcal{A}}^{q}
and
g\in\hat{\mathcal{A}}
we have

X(fg)=X(f)g+(-1)^{pq}fX(g).

The differential degree
\deg_{x}
of
\hat{\mathcal{A}}
induces another gradation on
\mathrm{Der}^{\partial_{x}}(\hat{\mathcal{A}})
, in terms of which a homogeneous element
X
of degree
d
is specified by the condition
X(\hat{\mathcal{A}}_{t})\subseteq\hat{\mathcal{A}}_{d+t}
. We denote by
\mathrm{Der}^{\partial_{x}}(\hat{\mathcal{A}})_{d}
the space of derivations
X
with differential degree
d
and by
\mathrm{Der}^{\partial_{x}}(\hat{\mathcal{A}})^{p}_{d}
the space
\mathrm{Der}^{\partial_{x}}(\hat{\mathcal{A}})^{p}\cap\mathrm{Der}^{\partial_{x}}(\hat{\mathcal{A}})_{d}
.

Remark 2.1
.

In what follows we will call an element of
\mathrm{Der}^{\partial_{x}}(\hat{\mathcal{A}})
a vector field or a flow. In particular, we call an element of
\mathrm{Der}^{\partial_{x}}(\hat{\mathcal{A}})^{2n-1}
for
n\geq 0
an odd flow.

Given a local functional
X\in\hat{\mathcal{F}}^{p}
, we can define a derivation
D_{X}\in\mathrm{Der}^{\partial_{x}}(\hat{\mathcal{A}})^{p-1}
as follows:

(2.8)

D_{X}=\sum_{s\geq 0}\partial_{x}^{s}\left(\frac{\delta X}{\delta\theta_{\alpha}}\right)\frac{\partial}{\partial u^{\alpha,s}}+(-1)^{p}\partial_{x}^{s}\left(\frac{\delta X}{\delta u^{\alpha}}\right)\frac{\partial}{\partial\theta_{\alpha}^{s}}.

Such a derivation is actually the Hamiltonian vector field of the local functional
X
with respect to the canonical symplectic structure on
\hat{M}
. The definition (
2.8
) yields a map

(2.9)

\hat{\mathcal{F}}\to\mathrm{Der}^{\partial_{x}}(\hat{\mathcal{A}}):\quad X\mapsto D_{X}

and we denote by
\mathrm{Der}^{D}(\hat{\mathcal{A}})
the image of this map. We call elements in
\mathrm{Der}^{D}(\hat{\mathcal{A}})
as
derivations of
D
-type
.

It is proved in
[
28
]
that the map (
2.9
) is a homomorphism of graded Lie algebra, where the graded Lie algebra structure on
\mathrm{Der}^{\partial_{x}}(\hat{\mathcal{A}})
is given by the natural graded commutator of derivations. More precisely, for
P\in\hat{\mathcal{F}}^{p}
and
Q\in\hat{\mathcal{F}}
, we have

(2.10)

D_{[P,Q]}=(-1)^{p-1}[D_{P},D_{Q}].

This relation allows us to make super extensions for any evolutionary Hamiltonian PDEs. Given the following system of evolutionary Hamiltonian PDEs

\frac{\partial u^{\alpha}}{\partial t}=X^{\alpha}=\mathcal{P}^{\alpha\beta}\frac{\delta H}{\delta u^{\beta}},

we have seen in the previous subsection that it corresponds to local functionals
H\in\hat{\mathcal{F}}^{0}
,
X\in\hat{\mathcal{F}}^{1}
and
P\in\hat{\mathcal{F}}^{2}
such that
X=-[P,H]
. Then it follows from the graded Jacobi identity (
2.3
) and the condition
[P,P]=0
that
[X,P]=0
, hence
[D_{X},D_{P}]=0
. Therefore the above Hamiltonian system admits the following compatible super extension

\displaystyle\frac{\partial u^{\alpha}}{\partial t}

\displaystyle=D_{X}u^{\alpha},\quad\frac{\partial\theta_{\alpha}}{\partial t}=D_{X}\theta_{\alpha},

\displaystyle\frac{\partial u^{\alpha}}{\partial\tau}

\displaystyle=D_{P}u^{\alpha},\quad\frac{\partial\theta_{\alpha}}{\partial\tau}=D_{P}\theta_{\alpha}.

Conversely if we have two derivations
X\in\mathrm{Der}^{\partial_{x}}(\hat{\mathcal{A}})^{0}
and
P\in\mathrm{Der}^{\partial_{x}}(\hat{\mathcal{A}})^{1}
such that
[X,P]=0
and
[P,P]=0
, then if both
X
and
P
are
D
-type, we conclude that the following system of evolutionary PDEs

\frac{\partial u^{\alpha}}{\partial t}=X(u^{\alpha})

is a Hamiltonian system.

Remark 2.2
.

When
P\in\hat{\mathcal{F}}^{2}
is a Hamiltonian structure with hydrodynamic leading term, then for
X\in\hat{\mathcal{F}}^{1}
, the existence of
H\in\hat{\mathcal{F}}^{0}
such that
X=-[H,P]
is equivalent to the condition that
[X,P]=0
.

Given a Hamiltonian structure
P\in\hat{\mathcal{F}}^{2}
, it follows from the equation (
2.10
) that
[D_{P},D_{P}]=0
. Let us denote by
d_{P}
the adjoint operator
[D_{P},-]:\mathrm{Der}^{\partial_{x}}(\hat{\mathcal{A}})\to\mathrm{Der}^{\partial_{x}}(\hat{\mathcal{A}})
, then we see that
d_{P}^{2}=0
and hence we have a complex
(\mathrm{Der}^{\partial_{x}}(\hat{\mathcal{A}}),d_{P})
. Similarly, for a bihamiltonian structure
(P_{0},P_{1})
, we have a bi-complex
(\mathrm{Der}^{\partial_{x}}(\hat{\mathcal{A}}),d_{P_{0}},d_{P_{1}})
. We can define the cohomology groups for these complexes as follows:

\displaystyle H^{p}_{d}(\mathrm{Der}^{\partial_{x}}(\hat{\mathcal{A}}),P)

\displaystyle:=\frac{\mathrm{Der}^{\partial_{x}}(\hat{\mathcal{A}})^{p}_{d}\cap\ker d_{P}}{\mathrm{Der}^{\partial_{x}}(\hat{\mathcal{A}})^{p}_{d}\cap\ima d_{P}},

\displaystyle VBH^{p}_{d}(\mathrm{Der}^{\partial_{x}}(\hat{\mathcal{A}}),P_{0},P_{1})

\displaystyle:=\frac{\mathrm{Der}^{\partial_{x}}(\hat{\mathcal{A}})^{p}_{d}\cap\ker d_{P_{0}}\cap\ker d_{P_{1}}}{\mathrm{Der}^{\partial_{x}}(\hat{\mathcal{A}})^{p}_{d}\cap\ima d_{P_{0}}d_{P_{1}}}.

These cohomology groups are called variational Hamiltonian (bihamiltonian) cohomology groups and are introduced and studied in the paper
[
33
]
, also see the relevant sections in
[
32
]
. They are originally introduced to study Virasoro symmetries of bihamiltonian integrable hierarchies. In this paper we will use them to study properties of linear reciprocal transformations, to this end we need the following theorem.

Theorem 2.3

(
[
33
]
)
.

Let
P
be a Hamiltonian structure of hydrodynamic type and
(P_{0},P_{1})
be a semisimple bihamiltonian structure. Then we have

(1)

H^{p}_{d}(\mathrm{Der}^{\partial_{x}}(\hat{\mathcal{A}}),P)=0
for any
p\geq-1
and
d>0
,

(2)

VBH^{0}_{\geq 2}(\mathrm{Der}^{\partial_{x}}(\hat{\mathcal{A}}),P_{0},P_{1})=0
and
VBH^{1}_{\geq 4}(\mathrm{Der}^{\partial_{x}}(\hat{\mathcal{A}}),P_{0},P_{1})=0
,

(3)

VBH^{1}_{3}(\mathrm{Der}^{\partial_{x}}(\hat{\mathcal{A}}),P_{0},P_{1})\cong\bigoplus_{i=1}^{n}C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})
.

Let us describe the cohomology group
VBH^{1}_{3}(\mathrm{Der}^{\partial_{x}}(\hat{\mathcal{A}}),P_{0},P_{1})
in a more detailed way. Given a derivation
X\in\mathrm{Der}^{\partial_{x}}(\hat{\mathcal{A}})^{1}_{3}\cap\ker d_{P_{0}}\cap\ker d_{P_{1}}
, there exist
n
functions
c_{i}(\lambda^{i})
of a single variable and a unique derivation
Y\in\mathrm{Der}^{\partial_{x}}(\hat{\mathcal{A}})^{-1}_{1}
such that

(2.11)

X=D_{[P_{0},Q]}+d_{P_{0}}d_{P_{1}}Y,

where
Q\in\hat{\mathcal{F}}^{1}_{2}
is given by the formula (
2.7
). And conversely, for any
n
functions
c_{i}(\lambda^{i})
of a single variable, the derivation
D_{[P_{0},Q]}
defines a cohomology class in the group
VBH^{1}_{3}(\mathrm{Der}^{\partial_{x}}(\hat{\mathcal{A}}),P_{0},P_{1})
.

3.
Proofs of the main result

In this section we prove Theorem
1.1
. To explain the basic ideas of the proof, we first consider the linear reciprocal transformation of the KdV equation

\frac{\partial u}{\partial t}=uu_{x}+\frac{\varepsilon^{2}}{12}u_{xxx}.

It has a bihamiltonian structure with the Hamiltonian operators

(3.1)

\mathcal{P}_{0}=\partial_{x},\quad\mathcal{P}_{1}=u\partial_{x}+\frac{1}{2}u_{x}+\frac{\varepsilon^{2}}{8}\partial_{x}^{3}.

Note that in this example,
u
is already the canonical coordinate of the bihamiltonian structure and the central invariant is
\frac{1}{24}
.

According to the discussion given in Sect.
2
, by introducing the odd variable
\theta
, the above bihamiltonian system can be represented by the local functionals

X=\int\left(uu_{x}+\frac{\varepsilon^{2}}{12}u_{xxx}\right)\theta,\quad P_{0}=\frac{1}{2}\int\theta\theta^{1},\quad P_{1}=\frac{1}{2}\int u\theta\theta^{1}+\frac{\varepsilon^{2}}{8}\theta\theta^{3}.

Therefore the KdV equation admits the following compatible super extension

(3.2)

\displaystyle\frac{\partial u}{\partial t}

\displaystyle=uu_{x}+\frac{\varepsilon^{2}}{12}u_{xxx},

\displaystyle\frac{\partial\theta}{\partial t}=u\theta^{1}+\frac{\varepsilon^{2}}{12}\theta^{3},

(3.3)

\displaystyle\frac{\partial u}{\partial\tau_{0}}

\displaystyle=\theta^{1},

\displaystyle\frac{\partial\theta}{\partial\tau_{0}}=0,

(3.4)

\displaystyle\frac{\partial u}{\partial\tau_{1}}

\displaystyle=u\theta^{1}+\frac{1}{2}u_{x}\theta+\frac{\varepsilon^{2}}{8}\theta^{3},

\displaystyle\frac{\partial\theta}{\partial\tau_{0}}=\frac{1}{2}\theta\theta^{1}.

We perform the linear reciprocal transformation by exchanging the spatial variable
x
and the time variable
t
. In another word, we represent
u_{x}
and
\theta^{1}
by derivatives of
u
and
\theta
with respect to
t
. A straightforward computation shows that

\displaystyle\frac{\partial u}{\partial x}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{u}\frac{\partial u}{\partial t}+\varepsilon^{2}\left(-\frac{1}{12u^{4}}\frac{\partial^{3}u}{\partial t^{3}}+\frac{3}{4u^{5}}\frac{\partial u}{\partial t}\frac{\partial^{2}u}{\partial t^{2}}-\frac{1}{u^{6}}\left(\frac{\partial u}{\partial t}\right)^{3}\right)+O(\varepsilon^{4}),

\displaystyle\frac{\partial\theta}{\partial x}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{u}\frac{\partial\theta}{\partial t}+\varepsilon^{2}\left(-\frac{1}{12u^{4}}\frac{\partial^{3}\theta}{\partial t^{3}}+\frac{1}{2u^{5}}\frac{\partial u}{\partial t}\frac{\partial^{2}\theta}{\partial t^{2}}+\frac{1}{4u^{5}}\frac{\partial\theta}{\partial t}\frac{\partial^{2}u}{\partial t^{2}}-\frac{1}{u^{6}}\left(\frac{\partial u}{\partial t}\right)^{2}\frac{\partial\theta}{\partial t}\right)+O(\varepsilon^{4}).

Let us adopt the usual convention and still use
x
to denote the spatial variable and
\tilde{t}
to denote the time variable, then the linear reciprocal transformation of the equation (
3.2
) reads

(3.5)

\displaystyle\frac{\partial u}{\partial\tilde{t}}

\displaystyle=\frac{u_{x}}{u}+\varepsilon^{2}\left(-\frac{u_{xxx}}{12u^{4}}+\frac{3u_{x}u_{xx}}{4u^{5}}-\frac{u_{x}^{3}}{u^{6}}\right)+O(\varepsilon^{4}),

(3.6)

\displaystyle\frac{\partial\theta}{\partial\tilde{t}}

\displaystyle=\frac{\theta^{1}}{u}+\varepsilon^{2}\left(-\frac{\theta^{3}}{12u^{4}}+\frac{u_{x}\theta^{2}}{2u^{5}}+\frac{u_{xx}\theta^{1}}{4u^{5}}-\frac{u_{x}^{2}\theta^{1}}{u^{6}}\right)+O(\varepsilon^{4}).

Now we can apply the reciprocal transformation to the odd flows (
3.3
) and (
3.4
) by using the substitution

\partial_{x}^{s}u\mapsto\frac{\partial^{s}u}{\partial\tilde{t}^{s}},\quad\theta^{s}\mapsto\frac{\partial^{s}\theta}{\partial\tilde{t}^{s}},

where
\frac{\partial}{\partial\tilde{t}}
is the flow given by the equations (
3.5
) and (
3.6
), and we obtain

(3.7)

\displaystyle\frac{\partial u}{\partial\tilde{\tau}_{0}}=

\displaystyle\,\frac{\theta^{1}}{u}+\varepsilon^{2}\left(-\frac{\theta^{3}}{12u^{4}}+\frac{u_{x}\theta^{2}}{2u^{5}}+\frac{u_{xx}\theta^{1}}{4u^{5}}-\frac{u_{x}^{2}\theta^{1}}{u^{6}}\right)+O(\varepsilon^{4}),

(3.8)

\displaystyle\frac{\partial\theta}{\partial\tilde{\tau}_{0}}=

\displaystyle\,0,

(3.9)

\displaystyle\frac{\partial u}{\partial\tilde{\tau}_{1}}=

\displaystyle\,\theta^{1}+\frac{u_{x}\theta}{2u}+\varepsilon^{2}\left(\frac{\theta^{3}}{24u^{3}}-\frac{u_{x}\theta^{2}}{4u^{4}}-\frac{u_{xx}\theta^{1}}{8u^{4}}+\frac{u_{x}^{2}\theta^{1}}{2u^{5}}\right.

\displaystyle\left.-\frac{u_{xxx}\theta}{24u^{4}}+\frac{3u_{x}u_{xx}\theta}{8u^{5}}-\frac{u_{x}^{3}\theta}{2u^{6}}\right)+O(\varepsilon^{4}),

(3.10)

\displaystyle\frac{\partial\theta}{\partial\tilde{\tau}_{1}}=

\displaystyle\,\frac{\theta\theta^{1}}{2u}+\varepsilon^{2}\left(-\frac{\theta\theta^{3}}{24u^{4}}+\frac{u_{x}\theta\theta^{2}}{4u^{5}}+\frac{u_{xx}\theta\theta^{1}}{8u^{5}}-\frac{u_{x}^{2}\theta\theta^{1}}{2u^{6}}\right)+O(\varepsilon^{4}).

Thus by performing the linear reciprocal transformation to the system (
3.2
)–(
3.4
), we arrive at another system of evolutionary PDEs (
3.5
)–(
3.10
). Since the linear reciprocal transformation preserves the commutativity of the flows, the system of evolutionary PDEs (
3.5
)–(
3.10
) is compatible. Therefore if these flows are of
D
-type, then from the discussion given in Sect.
2
, it follows that the equation (
3.5
) is bihamiltonian.

However, it is easy to see that the flows (
3.5
)–(
3.10
) are NOT of
D
-type. To solve this problem, we introduce a new odd variable
\sigma
by

\sigma=u\theta+\varepsilon^{2}\left(\frac{\theta^{2}}{4u^{2}}-\frac{u_{x}\theta^{1}}{4u^{3}}\right)+O(\varepsilon^{4}),

or equivalently we have

\theta=\frac{\sigma}{u}+\varepsilon^{2}\left(-\frac{\sigma^{2}}{4u^{4}}+\frac{3u_{x}\sigma^{1}}{4u^{5}}+\frac{u_{xx}\sigma}{4u^{5}}-\frac{3u_{x}^{2}\sigma}{4u^{6}}\right)+O(\varepsilon^{4}).

We can rewrite the system (
3.5
)–(
3.10
) in terms of the variables
u
and
\sigma
as

(3.11)

\displaystyle\frac{\partial u}{\partial\tilde{t}}=

\displaystyle\,\frac{u_{x}}{u}+\varepsilon^{2}\left(-\frac{u_{xxx}}{12u^{4}}+\frac{3u_{x}u_{xx}}{4u^{5}}-\frac{u_{x}^{3}}{u^{6}}\right)+O(\varepsilon^{4}),

(3.12)

\displaystyle\frac{\partial\sigma}{\partial\tilde{t}}=

\displaystyle\,\frac{\sigma^{1}}{u}+\varepsilon^{2}\left(-\frac{\sigma^{3}}{12u^{3}}+\frac{u_{x}\sigma^{2}}{4u^{5}}+\frac{u_{xx}\sigma^{1}}{4u^{5}}-\frac{u_{x}^{2}\sigma^{1}}{2u^{6}}\right.

\displaystyle\left.+\frac{u_{x}u_{xx}\sigma}{4u^{6}}-\frac{u_{x}^{3}\sigma}{2u^{7}}\right)+O(\varepsilon^{4}),

(3.13)

\displaystyle\frac{\partial u}{\partial\tilde{\tau}_{0}}=

\displaystyle\,\frac{\sigma^{1}}{u^{2}}-\frac{u_{x}\sigma}{u^{3}}+\varepsilon^{2}\left(-\frac{\sigma^{3}}{3u^{5}}+\frac{5u_{x}\sigma^{2}}{2u^{6}}+\frac{3u_{xx}\sigma^{1}}{2u^{6}}-\frac{7u_{x}^{2}\sigma^{1}}{u^{7}}\right.

\displaystyle\left.+\frac{u_{xxx}\sigma}{3u^{6}}-\frac{4u_{x}u_{xx}\sigma}{u^{7}}+\frac{7u_{x}^{3}\sigma}{u^{8}}\right)+O(\varepsilon^{4}),

(3.14)

\displaystyle\frac{\partial\sigma}{\partial\tilde{\tau}_{0}}=

\displaystyle\,-\frac{\sigma\sigma^{1}}{u^{3}}+\varepsilon^{2}\left(\frac{\sigma\sigma^{3}}{3u^{6}}-\frac{\sigma^{1}\sigma^{2}}{2u^{6}}-\frac{2u_{x}\sigma\sigma^{2}}{u^{7}}-\frac{2u_{xx}\sigma\sigma^{1}}{u^{7}}+\frac{7u_{x}^{2}\sigma\sigma^{1}}{u^{8}}\right)+O(\varepsilon^{4}),

(3.15)

\displaystyle\frac{\partial u}{\partial\tilde{\tau}_{1}}=

\displaystyle\,\frac{\sigma^{1}}{u}-\frac{u_{x}\sigma}{2u^{2}}+\varepsilon^{2}\left(-\frac{5\sigma^{3}}{24u^{4}}+\frac{5u_{x}\sigma^{2}}{4u^{5}}+\frac{3u_{xx}\sigma^{1}}{4u^{5}}-\frac{23u_{x}^{2}\sigma^{1}}{8u^{6}}\right.

\displaystyle\left.+\frac{u_{xxx}\sigma}{6u^{5}}-\frac{13u_{x}u_{xx}\sigma}{8u^{6}}+\frac{19u_{x}^{3}\sigma}{8u^{7}}\right)+O(\varepsilon^{4}),

(3.16)

\displaystyle\frac{\partial\sigma}{\partial\tilde{\tau}_{1}}=

\displaystyle\,-\frac{\sigma\sigma^{1}}{2u^{2}}+\varepsilon^{2}\left(\frac{\sigma\sigma^{3}}{6u^{5}}-\frac{\sigma^{1}\sigma^{2}}{4u^{5}}-\frac{7u_{x}\sigma\sigma^{2}}{8u^{6}}-\frac{7u_{xx}\sigma\sigma^{1}}{u8^{6}}+\frac{21u_{x}^{2}\sigma\sigma^{1}}{8u^{7}}\right)+O(\varepsilon^{4}).

Then we see that the flows
\frac{\partial}{\partial\tilde{t}}
,
\frac{\partial}{\partial\tilde{\tau}_{0}}
and
\frac{\partial}{\partial\tilde{\tau}_{1}}
defined by (
3.11
)–(
3.16
) are now of
D
-type. More precisely, we have

\frac{\partial}{\partial\tilde{t}}=D_{\tilde{X}},\quad\frac{\partial}{\partial\tilde{\tau}_{0}}=D_{\tilde{P}_{0}},\quad\frac{\partial}{\partial\tilde{\tau}_{1}}=D_{\tilde{P}_{1}},

where the local functionals are given by

\displaystyle\tilde{X}

\displaystyle=\int\frac{u_{x}\sigma}{u}+\varepsilon^{2}\left(-\frac{u_{xxx}\sigma}{12u^{4}}+\frac{3u_{x}u_{xx}\sigma}{4u^{5}}-\frac{u_{x}^{3}\sigma}{u^{6}}\right)+O(\varepsilon^{4}),

\displaystyle\tilde{P}_{0}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\int\frac{\sigma\sigma^{1}}{u^{2}}+\varepsilon^{2}\left(-\frac{\sigma\sigma^{3}}{3u^{5}}+\frac{5u_{x}\sigma\sigma^{2}}{2u^{6}}+\frac{3u_{xx}\sigma\sigma^{1}}{2u^{6}}-\frac{7u_{x}^{2}\sigma\sigma^{1}}{u^{7}}\right)+O(\varepsilon^{4}),

\displaystyle\tilde{P}_{1}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\int\frac{\sigma\sigma^{1}}{u}+\varepsilon^{2}\left(-\frac{5\sigma\sigma^{3}}{24u^{4}}+\frac{5u_{x}\sigma\sigma^{2}}{4u^{5}}+\frac{3u_{xx}\sigma\sigma^{1}}{4u^{5}}-\frac{23u_{x}^{2}\sigma\sigma^{1}}{8u^{6}}\right)+O(\varepsilon^{4}).

Hence the equation (
3.5
) is bihamiltonian, with the Hamiltonian operators given by

\displaystyle\tilde{\mathcal{P}}_{0}=

\displaystyle\,\frac{\partial_{x}}{u^{2}}-\frac{u_{x}}{u^{3}}+\varepsilon^{2}\left(-\frac{\partial_{x}^{3}}{3u^{5}}+\frac{5u_{x}\partial_{x}^{2}}{2u^{6}}+\frac{3u_{xx}\partial_{x}}{2u^{6}}-\frac{7u_{x}^{2}\partial_{x}}{u^{7}}\right.

\displaystyle\left.+\frac{u_{xxx}}{3u^{6}}-\frac{4u_{x}u_{xx}}{u^{7}}+\frac{7u_{x}^{3}}{u^{8}}\right)+O(\varepsilon^{4}),

\displaystyle\tilde{\mathcal{P}}_{1}=

\displaystyle\,\frac{\partial_{x}}{u}-\frac{u_{x}}{2u^{2}}+\varepsilon^{2}\left(-\frac{5\partial_{x}^{3}}{24u^{4}}+\frac{5u_{x}\partial_{x}^{2}}{4u^{5}}+\frac{3u_{xx}\partial_{x}}{4u^{5}}-\frac{23u_{x}^{2}\partial_{x}}{8u^{6}}\right.

\displaystyle\left.+\frac{u_{xxx}}{6u^{5}}-\frac{13u_{x}u_{xx}}{8u^{6}}+\frac{19u_{x}^{3}}{8u^{7}}\right)+O(\varepsilon^{4}).

By using the definition (
2.6
), it is easy to see that the central invariant of the above bihamiltonian structure is again
\frac{1}{24}
.

We see that the key point of finding the bihamiltonian structure of the equation (
3.5
) is to find the new super variable
\sigma
such that the flows (
3.5
)–(
3.7
) are of
D
-type. This is the key step of proving Theorem
1.1
.

3.1.
Proofs for the hydrodynamic cases

We first prove Theorem
1.1
for the hydrodynamic bihamiltonian system. In this case the theorem has already been proved in
[
46
]
. However, in order to prepare for the proof of the general cases, we need to re-prove this result using the
\theta
-formalism for Hamiltonian structures.

Let
(P_{0},P_{1})
be a semisimple bihamiltonian structure,
(u^{1},\dots,u^{n})
be its canonical coordinates and
(\theta_{1},\dots,\theta_{n})
be the corresponding odd variables. In terms of these coordinates, we have
[
21
]

(3.17)

P_{0}=\frac{1}{2}\int\sum_{i,j}\left(\delta^{ij}f^{i}(u)\theta_{i}\theta_{i}^{1}+A^{ij}\theta_{i}\theta_{j}\right),\ P_{1}=\frac{1}{2}\int\sum_{i,j}\left(\delta^{ij}u^{i}f^{i}(u)\theta_{i}\theta_{i}^{1}+B^{ij}\theta_{i}\theta_{j}\right),

where

A^{ij}=\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{f^{i}}{f^{j}}\frac{\partial f^{j}}{\partial u^{i}}u^{j,1}-\frac{f^{j}}{f^{i}}\frac{\partial f^{i}}{\partial u^{j}}u^{i,1}\right),\ B^{ij}=\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{u^{i}f^{i}}{f^{j}}\frac{\partial f^{j}}{\partial u^{i}}u^{j,1}-\frac{u^{j}f^{j}}{f^{i}}\frac{\partial f^{i}}{\partial u^{j}}u^{i,1}\right).

Introduce the notation
f_{i}:=(f^{i})^{-1}
, then we have rotation coefficients

(3.18)

\gamma_{ij}(u)=\frac{1}{2\sqrt{f_{i}f_{j}}}\partial_{j}f_{i},\quad i\neq j

for the diagonal metric
\mathrm{diag}(f_{1},\dots,f_{n})
, here and henceforth we use
\partial_{i}
to denote
\frac{\partial}{\partial u^{i}}
. The rotation coefficients satisfy the following equations
[
21
]
:

(3.19)

\displaystyle\partial_{k}\gamma_{ij}=\gamma_{ik}\gamma_{kj},\quad i,j,k\ \text{distinct},

(3.20)

\displaystyle\partial_{i}\gamma_{ij}+\partial_{j}\gamma_{ji}+\sum_{k\neq i,j}\gamma_{ki}\gamma_{kj}=0,\quad i\neq j,

(3.21)

\displaystyle u^{i}\partial_{i}\gamma_{ij}+u^{j}\partial_{j}\gamma_{ji}+\sum_{k\neq i,j}u^{k}\gamma_{ki}\gamma_{kj}+\frac{1}{2}(\gamma_{ij}+\gamma_{ji})=0,\quad i\neq j.

These equations are also defining equations for semisimple bihamiltonian structures. In another word, given
n
nonzero functions
(f_{1},\dots,f_{n})
and define
\gamma_{ij}
by (
3.18
), then the local functionals given by (
3.17
) form a semisimple bihamiltonian structure if and only if the functions
\gamma_{ij}
satisfy the equations (
3.19
)–(
3.21
) (see the appendix of
[
15
]
for details).

Assume that we have a local functional
X\in\hat{\mathcal{F}}^{1}
. It is proved in
[
16
]
that
X
is a bihamiltonian vector field of
(P_{0},P_{1})
, i.e.,
[X,P_{0}]=[X,P_{1}]=0
, if and only if

X=\int\sum_{i}A^{i}(u)u^{i,1}\theta_{i},

where the functions
A^{i}(u)
satisfy the equations

(3.22)

\partial_{j}A^{i}=\sqrt{\frac{f_{j}}{f_{i}}}\,\gamma_{ij}(A^{j}-A^{i}),\quad i\neq j.

Hence a hydrodynamic bihamiltonian system must be of the form

\frac{\partial u^{i}}{\partial t}=D_{X}(u^{i})=A^{i}u^{i,1}.

It admits the following super extension:

(3.23)

\displaystyle\frac{\partial u^{i}}{\partial t}

\displaystyle=D_{X}(u^{i})=A^{i}u^{i,1},

(3.24)

\displaystyle\frac{\partial\theta_{i}}{\partial t}

\displaystyle=D_{X}(\theta_{i})=A^{i}\theta_{i}^{1}+\sum_{j\neq i}\left(\partial_{j}A^{i}\theta_{i}-\partial_{i}A^{j}\theta_{j}\right)u^{j,1},

(3.25)

\displaystyle\frac{\partial u^{i}}{\partial\tau_{0}}

\displaystyle=D_{P_{0}}(u^{i}),\quad\frac{\partial\theta_{i}}{\partial\tau_{0}}=D_{P_{0}}(\theta_{i}),

(3.26)

\displaystyle\frac{\partial u^{i}}{\partial\tau_{1}}

\displaystyle=D_{P_{1}}(u^{i}),\quad\frac{\partial\theta_{i}}{\partial\tau_{1}}=D_{P_{1}}(\theta_{i}).

We will need the following explicit expressions later:

(3.27)

Lemma 3.1
.

Given a derivation
\frac{\partial}{\partial\tau}\in\mathrm{Der}^{\partial_{x}}(\hat{\mathcal{A}})^{1}_{1}
which satisfies the condition
[\frac{\partial}{\partial\tau},\frac{\partial}{\partial\tau}]=0
, then we have the flow of the form

\frac{\partial u^{i}}{\partial\tau}=\sum_{j}g^{ij}\theta_{j}^{1}+\sum_{j,k}\Gamma^{ij}_{k}u^{k,1}\theta_{j}

in terms of some local coordinates
(u^{i},\theta_{i})
. If
\det(g^{ij})\neq 0
and
(g_{ij}):=(g^{ij})^{-1}
defines a flat metric with the Christoffel symbols of its Levi-Civita connection given by
\Gamma^{i}_{jk}=-\sum_{\ell}g_{j\ell}\Gamma^{\ell i}_{k}
, then
\frac{\partial}{\partial\tau}
is of
D
-type, i.e. there is a local fcuntional

P=\frac{1}{2}\int\sum_{i,j}g^{ij}\theta_{i}\theta_{j}^{1}+\sum_{i,j,k}\Gamma^{ij}_{k}u^{k,1}\theta_{i}\theta_{j}

such that

\frac{\partial}{\partial\tau}=D_{P}.

Proof.

Consider the following Miura-type transformation from coordinates
(u^{i},\theta_{i})
to
(\tilde{u}^{i},\tilde{\theta}_{i})
:

\tilde{u}^{i}=\tilde{u}^{i}(u),\quad\tilde{\theta}_{i}=\sum_{j}\frac{\partial u^{j}}{\partial\tilde{u}^{i}}\theta_{j}.

By a straightforward computation, we have

\frac{\partial\tilde{u}^{i}}{\partial\tau}=\sum_{j}\tilde{g}^{ij}\tilde{\theta}_{j}^{1}+\sum_{j,k}\tilde{\Gamma}^{ij}_{k}\tilde{u}^{k,1}\tilde{\theta}_{j},

where the functions
\tilde{g}^{ij}
and
\tilde{\Gamma}^{ij}_{k}
are given by

\displaystyle\tilde{g}^{ij}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k,\ell}\frac{\partial\tilde{u}^{i}}{\partial u^{k}}\frac{\partial\tilde{u}^{j}}{\partial u^{\ell}}g^{k\ell},

\displaystyle\tilde{\Gamma}^{ij}_{k}

\displaystyle=-\sum_{\ell}\tilde{g}^{i\ell}\left(\sum_{p}\frac{\partial\tilde{u}^{j}}{\partial u^{p}}\frac{\partial^{2}u^{p}}{\partial\tilde{u}^{\ell}\tilde{u}^{k}}+\sum_{p,q,r}\frac{\partial u^{p}}{\partial\tilde{u}^{\ell}}\frac{\partial u^{q}}{\partial\tilde{u}^{k}}\frac{\partial\tilde{u}^{j}}{\partial u^{r}}\Gamma^{r}_{pq}\right).

Therefore if
(g_{ij})
defines a flat metric and
\Gamma^{i}_{jk}
are the Christoffel symbols of its Levi-Civita connection, then by choosing
\tilde{u}^{i}
to be its flat coordinates we may assume that
\tilde{g}^{ij}
are constants and all the corresponding Christoffel symbols vanish. Thus we have

\frac{\partial\tilde{u}^{i}}{\partial\tau}=\sum_{j}\tilde{g}^{ij}\tilde{\theta}_{j}^{1}.

By using the fact that

\left[\frac{\partial}{\partial\tau},\frac{\partial}{\partial\tau}\right]\tilde{u}^{i}=2\sum_{j}\tilde{g}^{ij}\partial_{x}\frac{\partial\tilde{\theta}_{j}}{\partial\tau}=0,

it follows that

\frac{\partial\tilde{\theta}_{i}}{\partial\tau}=0.

Therefore in terms of coordinates
(\tilde{u}^{i},\tilde{\theta}_{i})
, the flow
\frac{\partial}{\partial\tau}
is of
D
-type, i.e., it can be represented as

\frac{\partial}{\partial\tau}=D_{\tilde{P}},\quad\tilde{P}=\frac{1}{2}\int\sum_{ij}\tilde{g}^{ij}\tilde{\theta}_{i}\tilde{\theta}_{j}^{1}.

Since the property of being
D
-type is preserved under Miura-type transformations, we conclude that in terms of the original coordinates
(u^{i},\theta_{i})
,
\frac{\partial}{\partial\tau}
is also of
D
-type. The lemma is proved.
∎

To complete the proof of Theorem

We can further describe transformation rules of Hamiltonians under linear reciprocal transformations. Let us write the flow (

\frac{\partial u^{i}}{\partial s}=B^{i}u^{i,1}=\sum_{j}\mathcal{P}_{0}^{ij}\frac{\delta H}{\delta u^{j}},

given in (

for

Theorem 3.3
.

The function
g
can be chosen to be a solution of the following system of PDEs:

\frac{\partial g}{\partial u^{i}}=A^{i}\frac{\partial h}{\partial u^{i}},\quad i=1,\dots,n.

Proof.

The identity

B^{i}u^{i,1}=\sum_{j}\mathcal{P}_{0}^{ij}\frac{\delta H}{\delta u^{j}}

yields the following identities satisfied by the function

\partial_{i}\partial_{j}h=\sqrt{\frac{f^{i}}{f^{j}}}\,\gamma_{ij}\partial_{i}h+\sqrt{\frac{f^{j}}{f^{i}}}\,\gamma_{ji}\partial_{j}h

Then the theorem is proved by a direct computation by using the above identities.

4.
Applications and examples

4.1.
Classification of bihamiltonian integrable hierarchies by linear reciprocal transformations

In this subsection, we classify, under the actions of both Miura-type transformations and linear reciprocal transformations, bihamiltonian integrable hierarchies whose bihamiltonian structures admit semisimple hydrodynamic limits.

It follows from the theory of bihamiltonian cohomology that the Miura-equivalent classes of bihamiltonian structures are parameterized by their leading terms and central invariants, and leading terms are in one-to-one correspondence with flat pencils of metrics of the base manifolds. Therefore for two bihamiltonian integrable hierarchies, if the leading terms of their bihamiltonian structures, represented in their canonical coordinates, coincide and their bihamiltonian structures share the same central invariants, then each flow of one bihamiltonian integrable hierarchy is a symmetry of the other bihamiltonian integrable hierarchy.

Next we consider the actions of both Miura-type transformations and linear reciprocal transformations on bihamiltonian integrable hierarchies. Since linear reciprocal transformations preserve central invariants, we conclude that two bihamiltonian integrable hierarchies are equivalent under actions of Miura-type transformations and linear reciprocal transformations if and only if the central invariants of their bihamiltonian structures coincide and their leading terms can be related by linear reciprocal transformations in their canonical coordinates. So we only need to consider the classification of bihamiltonian hierarchies of hydrodynamic type.

Assume that we have two
n
-component semisimple bihamiltonian structures of hydrodynamic type
(P_{0},P_{1})
and
(Q_{0},Q_{1})
which, in terms of their canonical coordinates, are given by two metrics
\mathrm{diag}(f^{1},\dots,f^{n})
and
\mathrm{diag}(g^{1},\dots,g^{n})
. As we have shown in Theorem (
3.2
), the necessary condition for the existence of a linear reciprocal transformation relating them is that these two metrics share a same set of rotation coefficients. Let us show that this is also a sufficient condition. Indeed, assume that their rotation coefficients coincide, i.e.,

(4.1)

\gamma_{ij}(u)=\frac{1}{2\sqrt{f_{i}f_{j}}}\partial_{j}f_{i}=\frac{1}{2\sqrt{g_{i}g_{j}}}\partial_{j}g_{i},\quad i\neq j,

here as before we denote
f_{i}=(f^{i})^{-1}
and
g_{i}=(g^{i})^{-1}
. If we can show that the flow

\frac{\partial u^{i}}{\partial t}=\sqrt{\frac{f^{i}}{g^{i}}}u^{i,1}

is a bihamiltonian vector field of
(P_{0},P_{1})
, then it follows from Theorem
3.2
that

is transformed to
(Q_{0},Q_{1})
after performing the linear reciprocal transformation with respect to the flow
\frac{\partial}{\partial t}
. According to the equation (
3.22

\partial_{j}\sqrt{\frac{f^{i}}{g^{i}}}=\sqrt{\frac{f_{j}}{f_{i}}}\gamma_{ij}\left(\sqrt{\frac{f^{j}}{g^{j}}}-\sqrt{\frac{f^{i}}{g^{i}}}\right),\quad i\neq j,

which is easy to verify by using equations (

The equivalent classes of

Then the flow (

\displaystyle\frac{\partial v}{\partial t}=

\displaystyle\,-\frac{v_{x}}{2v\sqrt{v}}+\varepsilon^{2}\frac{1}{\sqrt{v}}\left(\frac{v^{(3)}}{16v^{2}}-\frac{19v_{x}v_{xx}}{32v^{3}}+\frac{107v_{x}^{3}}{128v^{4}}\right)+\varepsilon^{4}\frac{1}{\sqrt{v}}\left(-\frac{v^{(5)}}{128v^{3}}+\frac{9v_{x}v^{(4)}}{64v^{4}}\right.

\displaystyle\left.+\frac{64v_{xx}v^{(3)}}{256v^{4}}-\frac{1293v_{x}^{2}v^{(3)}}{1024v^{5}}-\frac{909v_{x}v_{xx}^{2}}{512v^{5}}+\frac{14193v_{x}^{3}v_{xx}}{2048v^{6}}-\frac{76467v_{x}^{5}}{16384v^{7}}\right)+O(\varepsilon^{6}).

\displaystyle v\mapsto w=

\displaystyle\frac{\partial w}{\partial t}=

\displaystyle\,-\frac{w_{x}}{2w\sqrt{w}}+\varepsilon^{2}\frac{1}{\sqrt{w}}\left(\frac{w^{(3)}}{16w^{2}}-\frac{5w_{x}w_{xx}}{16w^{3}}+\frac{35w_{x}^{3}}{128w^{4}}\right)+\varepsilon^{4}\frac{1}{\sqrt{w}}\left(-\frac{w^{(5)}}{128w^{3}}+\frac{21w_{x}w^{(4)}}{256w^{4}}\right.

Let us verify that the flow (

) is exactly the first negative flow of the KdV hierarchy. Recall that the negative flows of the KdV hierarchy are constructed in

]
with the first negative flow given by

\frac{\partial w}{\partial t}=p_{x},\quad w_{x}=\left(w\partial_{x}+\frac{1}{2}w_{x}+\frac{\varepsilon^{2}}{8}\partial_{x}^{3}\right)p.

Other negative flows are defined recursively by the recursion operator

w+\frac{1}{2}w_{x}\partial_{x}^{-1}+\frac{\varepsilon^{2}}{8}\partial_{x}^{2}.

We can solve the variable
p
as a differential polynomial in
w
from the second equation of (

). For example, when we take the dispersionless limit
\varepsilon=0
, we obtain

w_{x}=wp_{x}+\frac{1}{2}w_{x}p,

which can be integrated to obtain

2\sqrt{w}+C=\sqrt{w}p,

here
C
is the integral constant and we take
C=1
for simplicity. Then we can recursively solve the dispersion part of

to obtain

\displaystyle p=

\displaystyle\,2+\frac{1}{\sqrt{w}}+\varepsilon^{2}\frac{1}{\sqrt{w}}\left(\frac{w_{xx}}{16w^{2}}-\frac{w_{x}^{2}}{64w^{3}}\right)+\varepsilon^{4}\frac{1}{\sqrt{w}}\left(-\frac{w^{(4)}}{128w^{3}}+\frac{7w^{(3)}w_{x}}{128w^{4}}\right.

\displaystyle\left.+\frac{21w_{xx}^{2}}{512w^{4}}-\frac{231w_{xx}w_{x}^{2}}{1024w^{5}}+\frac{1155w_{x}^{4}}{8192w^{6}}\right)+O(\varepsilon^{6}).

After substituting the expression of

into

\frac{\partial w}{\partial t}=p_{x},

we obtain the flow (
4.9
).

By a further computation, we can prove that actually the hierarchy (
4.2
) is transformed to the negative KdV hierarchy by performing the linear reciprocal transformation given by (
4.5
). Since we have already seen that the bihamiltonian structure of the Volterra hierarchy is transformed to that of the KdV hierarchy after the linear reciprocal transformation together with the Miura-type transformation (
4.8
), we only need to check the above-mentioned relation for the leading terms of the two integrable hierarchies. Firstly, it is easy to see that the Volterra hierarchy (
4.2
) has leading terms

\frac{\partial u}{\partial t_{k}}=-\frac{k}{4^{k}}\binom{2k}{k}e^{ku}u_{x}+O(\varepsilon).

In terms of the canonical coordinate
v=e^{-u}
we have

\frac{\partial v}{\partial t_{k}}=-\frac{k}{4^{k}}\binom{2k}{k}\frac{v_{x}}{v^{k}}+O(\varepsilon).

After performing the linear reciprocal transformation, i.e., after making the substitution

v_{x}\mapsto\frac{v_{x}}{\sqrt{v}}+O(\varepsilon),

we obtain

(4.11)

\frac{\partial v}{\partial t_{k}}=-\frac{k}{4^{k}}\binom{2k}{k}\frac{v_{x}}{v^{k}\sqrt{v}}+O(\varepsilon),\quad k\geq 1.

These flows (
4.11
) satisfy the recursion relations

\left(v+\frac{1}{2}v_{x}\partial_{x}^{-1}+\frac{\varepsilon^{2}}{8}\partial_{x}^{2}\right)\frac{\partial v}{\partial t_{k+1}}=\frac{\partial v}{\partial t_{k}}.

When

Reciprocal transformations of the Ablowitz–Ladik hierarchy

In this subsection we consider the linear reciprocal transformations of the Ablowitz–Ladik hierarchy. The tri-Hamiltonian structure is given in

\displaystyle\frac{\partial A^{1}}{\partial\lambda^{1}}

4.3.
Reciprocal transformations and Dubrovin–Zhang hierarchies

We can generalize the discussion given in Sect.
4.2
and ask the following question: when a bihamiltonian integrable hierarchy can be transformed to symmetries of some Dubrovin–Zhang hierarchy
[
18
]
by performing Miura-type transformations and linear reciprocal transformations?

Recall that the Dubrovin–Zhang hierarchies can be described as tau-symmetric bihamiltonian deformations of the Principal Hierarchies of semisimple Frobenius manifolds with the property that the central invariants of the deformed bihamiltonian structures are all equal to
\frac{1}{24}

[
31
]
. Therefore we see that flows of a bihamiltonian integrable hierarchy can be transformed to symmetries of a certain Dubrovin–Zhang hierarchy if and only if the central invariants of their bihamiltonian structure are all equal to
\frac{1}{24}
, and their leading terms can be transformed to symmetries of the Principal Hierarchy of a certain semisimple Frobenius manifold. So again it suffices to consider only the hydrodynamic cases.

We will not review the theory of Frobenius manifolds and associated Principal Hierarchies in this paper, one may refer to
[
11
,
12
,
13
,
18
]
for details. Instead, we review some results given in
[
16
]
. It is proved in
[
16
]
that a flat exact irreducible semisimple bihamiltonian structure
(P_{0},P_{1})
must be the bihamiltonian structure of the Principal Hierarchy of a certain semisimple Frobenius manifold, and conversely bihamiltonian structures of Principal Hierarchies of semisimple Frobenius manifolds are always flat exact. More precisely, assume that, in terms of the canonical coordinate
(u^{1},\dots,u^{n})
, the flat pencil corresponding to
(P_{0},P_{1})
is given by
\mathrm{diag}(f^{1},\dots,f^{n})
and
\mathrm{diag}(u^{1}f^{1},\dots,u^{n}f^{n})
, then the flat exact condition means that

\sum_{j}\frac{\partial f^{i}}{\partial u^{j}}=0,\quad i=1,\dots,n,

and the rotation coefficients satisfy the condition
\gamma_{ij}=\gamma_{ji}
for
i\neq j
. The irreducible condition means that for any disjoint nonempty partition
I,J
of the set
\{1,\dots,n\}
and any index
i\in I
, there exists
j\in J
such that
\gamma_{ij}\neq 0
.

Now we consider a bihamiltonian integrable hierarchy of hydrodynamic type with semisimple bihamiltonian structure
(P_{0},P_{1})
. We assume that
(P_{0},P_{1})
corresponds to the flat pencil given by
\mathrm{diag}(f^{1},\dots,f^{n})
and
\mathrm{diag}(u^{1}f^{1},\dots,u^{n}f^{n})
. Since linear reciprocal transformations preserve the rotation coefficients, the necessary condition of the existence of a linear reciprocal transformation to transform the flows of this integrable hierarchy to symmetries of the Principal Hierarchy of a semisimple Frobenius manifold is
\gamma_{ij}=\gamma_{ji}
. In what follows, we prove that this is also a sufficient condition under the irreducibility assumption.

Assume that
\gamma_{ij}=\gamma_{ji}
. In this case, the equations (
3.19
)–(
3.21
) read

(4.17)

\displaystyle\partial_{k}\gamma_{ij}=\gamma_{ik}\gamma_{kj},\quad i,j,k\ \text{distinct},

(4.18)

\displaystyle\sum_{k}\partial_{k}\gamma_{ij}=0,\quad i\neq j,

(4.19)

\displaystyle\sum_{k}u^{k}\partial_{k}\gamma_{ij}=-\gamma_{ij},\quad i\neq j.

Our goal is then to find a bihamiltonian vector field

\frac{\partial u^{i}}{\partial t}=A^{i}u^{i,1}

of
(P_{0},P_{1})
such that

(4.20)

\sum_{j}\partial_{j}\tilde{f}^{i}=0,\quad i=1,\dots,n,

where

\tilde{f}^{i}=\frac{f^{i}}{(A^{i})^{2}}.

From the the equations (
3.22
) and (
4.20
), we obtain the following system of PDEs for
A^{i}
:

(4.21)

\displaystyle\partial_{i}A^{i}

\displaystyle=\frac{A^{i}}{2f^{i}}\partial_{i}f^{i}-\sum_{j\neq i}\sqrt{\frac{f^{i}}{f^{j}}}\gamma_{ij}A^{j},

(4.22)

\displaystyle\partial_{j}A^{i}

\displaystyle=\sqrt{\frac{f_{j}}{f_{i}}}\gamma_{ij}(A^{j}-A^{i}),\quad i\neq j.

By a direct computation, we can show that these equations are compatible, so the solution space is of dimension
n
. Let us prove that if the rotation coefficients
\gamma_{ij}
are irreducible, then we can find a solution
A^{1},\dots,A^{n}
with
A^{i}\neq 0
for each
i=1,\dots,n
. The argument is similar to that of Lemma 3.3 in
[
16
]
. For a nonempty subset
S\subseteq\{1,\dots,n\}
, let us denote

A_{S}:=\prod_{i\in S}A^{i},

then we will prove that if
A_{\{1,\dots,n\}}=0
, then for any nonempty subset
S\subseteq\{1,\dots,n\}
,
A_{S}=0
and in particular each
A^{i}=0
. Let us prove by induction on
|S|
. For
|S|=n
, this is just our assumption that
A_{\{1,\dots,n\}}=0
. Assume that
A_{S}=0
for any
|S|\geq m
for some
m\leq n
. Now take a nonempty subset
T\subseteq\{1,\dots,n\}
with
|T|=m-1
and take
i\in T
. By the irreducibility assumption, we can find
j\notin T
such that
\gamma_{ij}\neq 0
. Denote
S=T\cup\{j\}
, then it follows from the induction hypothesis that
A_{S}=0
. By using the equations (
4.21
) and (
4.22
) we obtain

\displaystyle 0=

\displaystyle\,\frac{\partial A_{S}}{\partial u^{i}}=\sum_{k\in S}A_{S-\{k\}}\frac{\partial A^{k}}{\partial u^{i}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\,\sum_{k\in S,k\neq i}A_{S-\{k\}}\sqrt{\frac{f_{i}}{f_{k}}}\gamma_{ij}(A^{i}-A^{k})

\displaystyle+A_{S-\{i\}}\frac{A^{i}}{2f^{i}}\partial_{i}f^{i}-A_{S-\{i\}}\sum_{k\neq i}\sqrt{\frac{f^{i}}{f^{k}}}\gamma_{ik}A^{k}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\,\sum_{k\in S,k\neq i}A_{S-\{k\}}\sqrt{\frac{f_{i}}{f_{k}}}\gamma_{ij}A^{i}-A_{S-\{i\}}\sum_{k\in S,k\neq i}\sqrt{\frac{f^{i}}{f^{k}}}\gamma_{ik}A^{k}.

Thus it follows from the induction hypothesis that

A_{T}A_{S-\{i\}}=0;\quad A_{T}A_{S-\{k\}}=0,\quad k\neq j,

and we arrive at

0=A_{T}\frac{\partial A_{S}}{\partial u^{i}}=A_{T}A_{S-\{j\}}\sqrt{\frac{f_{i}}{f_{j}}}\gamma_{ij}A^{i}=A_{T}^{2}\sqrt{\frac{f_{i}}{f_{j}}}\gamma_{ij}A^{i}.

By multiplying
A_{T-\{i\}}
on both sides of the above identity, we obtain
A_{T}^{3}\gamma_{ij}=0
and it follows that
A_{T}=0
.

We summarize the results obtained in this subsection in the following theorem.

Theorem 4.2
.

Assume that we have a bihamiltonian integrable hierarchy and the central invariants of its bihamiltonian structure are all equal to
\frac{1}{24}
. If the rotation coefficients
\gamma_{ij}
of the flat pencil associated with the leading term of the bihamiltonian structure satisfy the conditions

\gamma_{ij}=\gamma_{ji},\quad i\neq j,

and if these coefficients are irreducible, then flows of this integrable hierarchy can be transformed to symmetries of a certain Dubrovin–Zhang hierarchy by a linear reciprocal transformation.

In the remaining part of this subsection, we show that two Dubrovin-Zhang hierarchies can be related by a linear reciprocal transformation if and only if their corresponding Frobenius manifolds can be related by a Legendre transformation.

We start by recalling the definitions of Legendre transformations for Frobenius manifolds
[
12
,
18
]
. Let
M
be a Frobenius manifold with flat coordinates
v^{1},\dots,v^{n}
. Let us denote by
\eta_{\alpha\beta}
the flat metric on
TM
and by
c^{\alpha}_{\beta\gamma}(v)
the structure constants of the Frobenius algebra on
T_{v}M
, i.e., on
TM
we have

\langle\partial_{\alpha},\partial_{\beta}\rangle=\eta_{\alpha\beta},\quad\partial_{\alpha}\cdot\partial_{\beta}=c_{\alpha\beta}^{\gamma}\partial_{\gamma}.

Choose a flat invertible vector field
b=b^{\alpha}\partial_{\alpha}
, then a Legendre transformation can be defined by

v^{\alpha}\mapsto\hat{v}^{\alpha}=b^{\beta}\eta^{\alpha\gamma}\partial_{\gamma}\partial_{\beta}F,

where
F
is the potential of the Frobenius manifold
M
. Note that we have the relation

\partial_{\alpha}=b\cdot\hat{\partial}_{\alpha},

here
\hat{\partial}_{\alpha}=\frac{\partial}{\partial\hat{v}^{\alpha}}
. It is proved in
[
12
]
that there exists a function
\hat{F}(\hat{v})
such that

\hat{\partial}_{\alpha}\hat{\partial}_{\beta}\hat{F}=\partial_{\alpha}\partial_{\beta}F

and that
\hat{F}
defines a new Frobenius manifold structure on
M
with a new flat metric on
\hat{M}
given by

(4.23)

\langle X,Y\rangle_{b}:=\langle b\cdot X,b\cdot Y\rangle,\quad X,Y\in\Gamma(TM),

and the multiplication on
TM
is not changed. Note that the metric
\langle-,-\rangle_{b}
has flat coordinates
\hat{v}^{1},\dots,\hat{v}^{n}
and

\langle\hat{\partial}_{\alpha},\hat{\partial}_{\beta}\rangle_{b}=\eta_{\alpha\beta}.

In the semisimple case, it is proved that Legendre transformations preserve the rotation coefficients
[
42
]
, therefore it follows from Theorem
4.2
that if two Frobenius manifolds are related by a Legendre transformation, then their corresponding Dubrovin-Zhang hierarchy is related by a linear reciprocal transformation.

Now let us assume that the Principal Hierarchy of a semisimple Frobenius manifold
M
can be transformed to symmetries of the Principal Hierarchy of another semisimple Frobenius manifold
M^{\prime}
. Let us use
(u^{1},\dots,u^{n})
to denote the canonical coordinates of
M
, then we know that the metric
\langle-,-\rangle
is of diagonal form and we denote

(4.24)

\langle du^{i},du^{j}\rangle=\frac{\delta_{i,j}}{\psi_{i1}^{2}}.

We define functions
\psi_{i\alpha}
by

\frac{\partial v^{\alpha}}{\partial u^{i}}=\psi_{i1}\psi_{i}^{\alpha},\quad\psi_{i}^{\alpha}=\eta^{\alpha\beta}\psi_{i\beta}.

The following identities are useful
[
12
,
18
]
:

(4.25)

\displaystyle c^{\alpha}_{\beta\gamma}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i}\frac{\psi_{i}^{\alpha}\psi_{i\beta}\psi_{i\gamma}}{\psi_{i1}},

(4.26)

\displaystyle\frac{\partial v^{\alpha}}{\partial u^{i}}

\displaystyle=\psi_{i1}\psi_{i}^{\alpha},\quad\frac{\partial u^{i}}{\partial v^{\alpha}}=\frac{\psi_{i\alpha}}{\psi_{i1}},

(4.27)

\displaystyle\frac{\partial\psi_{i\alpha}}{\partial u^{j}}

\displaystyle=\gamma_{ij}\psi_{j\alpha},\quad i\neq j,\quad\sum_{j}\frac{\partial\psi_{i\alpha}}{\partial u^{j}}=0.

If the Principal Hierarchy of
M
can be transformed to symmetries of the Principal Hierarchy of
M^{\prime}
by performing the linear reciprocal transformation with respect to the flow

(4.28)

\frac{\partial u^{i}}{\partial s}=A^{i}u^{i,1},

then in this case the equations (
4.21
), (
4.22
) satisfied by
A^{i}
read

\displaystyle\partial_{j}A^{i}=\frac{\psi_{j1}}{\psi_{i1}}\gamma_{ij}(A^{j}-A^{i}),\quad i\neq j,

\displaystyle\sum_{j}\partial_{j}A^{i}=0.

One can show that the general solution of the above PDEs have the from

A^{i}=\frac{b^{\alpha}\psi_{i\alpha}}{\psi_{i1}},

where
b^{\alpha}
are certain constants. So we assume that the functions
A^{i}
in (
4.28
) are given by the formulae above. It follows from Theorem (
3.2
) that after performing linear reciprocal transformation with respect to the flow

\frac{\partial u^{i}}{\partial s}=\frac{b^{\alpha}\psi_{i\alpha}}{\psi_{i1}}u^{i,1},

the metric (
4.24
) is transformed to

\langle-,-\rangle\mapsto\langle-,-\rangle^{\prime}_{b},

where

(4.29)

\langle du^{i},du^{j}\rangle^{\prime}_{b}=\frac{\delta_{i,j}}{b^{\alpha}b^{\beta}\psi_{i\alpha}\psi_{i\beta}}.

Let us check that this metric is exactly the metric defined by (
4.23
) and hence we can choose
\hat{v}^{1},\dots,\hat{v}^{n}
as flat coordinates of
M^{\prime}
and this defines a Legendre transformation from
M
to
M^{\prime}
. For this purpose, we only need to compute the metric (
4.23
) in terms of the canonical coordinate and prove that it coincides with (
4.29
). Firstly, we see that

\langle\partial_{\alpha},\partial_{\beta}\rangle_{b}=\langle b\cdot\partial_{\alpha},b\cdot\partial_{\beta}\rangle=b^{\mu}b^{\gamma}\eta_{\lambda\delta}c^{\lambda}_{\alpha\mu}c^{\delta}_{\beta\gamma}.

Thus by using the identities (
4.25
) and (
4.26
) we arrive at

\left\langle\frac{\partial}{\partial u^{i}},\frac{\partial}{\partial u^{j}}\right\rangle_{b}=\psi_{i1}\psi_{j1}\psi_{i}^{\alpha}\psi_{j}^{\beta}\langle\partial_{\alpha},\partial_{\beta}\rangle_{b}=\delta_{i,j}{b^{\alpha}b^{\beta}\psi_{i\alpha}\psi_{i\beta}},

here we also used the identities

\psi_{i\alpha}\psi_{j}^{\alpha}=\delta_{i,j},

which can be obtained from (
4.26
). Therefore we see that
\langle-,-\rangle^{\prime}_{b}=\langle-,-\rangle_{b}
.

Example 4.3
.

It is proved in
[
6
]
that the Toda hierarchy and the nonlinear Schrödinger hierarchy can be related by a linear reciprocal transformation, therefore their associated Frobenius manifolds must be related by a Legendre transformation. Indeed, the associated Frobenius manifolds are given by the potentials

F_{\textrm{Toda}}=\frac{1}{2}v^{2}u+e^{u},\quad F_{\textrm{NLS}}=\frac{1}{2}v^{2}u+\frac{1}{2}u^{2}\log u,

and by choosing
b=\partial_{u}
, the potential
F_{\textrm{Toda}}
can be transformed to
F_{\textrm{NLS}}
, see
[
12
,
18
,
16
]
for details.

5.
Conclusion

In this paper we study the actions of linear reciprocal transformations on bihamiltonian integrable hierarchies. We prove that these transformations preserve the bihamiltonian property, and the only invariants are the central invariants of the bihamiltonian structures and rotation coefficients of the flat pencils associated with their leading terms.

One may notice that bihamiltonian cohomologies and their variational generalizations play important rules in the proof. So it is natural to ask if the methods presented in this paper work for an integrable hierarchy with only one Hamiltonian structure. More precisely, given flows
\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\in\mathrm{Der}^{\partial_{x}}(\hat{\mathcal{A}})^{0}_{\geq 1}
and
\frac{\partial}{\partial\tau}\in\mathrm{Der}^{\partial_{x}}(\hat{\mathcal{A}})^{1}_{\geq 1}
satisfying the condition

\left[\frac{\partial}{\partial t},\frac{\partial}{\partial\tau}\right]=\left[\frac{\partial}{\partial\tau},\frac{\partial}{\partial\tau}\right]=0,

we ask whether one can find transformations of odd variables to turn them to vector fields of
D
-type if their leading terms are already of
D
-type. Unfortunately, this is not correct and one can easily write down some examples to justify this. So the method of transformations of odd variables cannot be used to answer the question whether linear reciprocal transformations preserve the Hamiltonian property for integrable hierarchies with only one Hamiltonian structure.

Another open problem is to study the general reciprocal transformations of integrable hierarchies. In
[
22
]
, the reciprocal transformations of Hamiltonian integrable hierarchies of hydrodynamic type are studied and it is proved that in general Hamiltonian structures are transformed to non-local ones. We hope that we can develop a general theory for non-local Hamiltonian structures and study general reciprocal transformations by using the idea of super tau-cover introduced in
[
30
]
.

The study of linear reciprocal transformations may also find its applications in the study of Gromov–Witten invariants. For example, in Sect.
4.2
, we relate the Ablowitz–Ladik hierarchy to the extended Toda hierarchy. It is well-known that the Gromov–Witten potential of
\mathbb{P}^{1}
is a particular tau-function of the extended Toda hierarchy
[
6
,
25
]
, and it is conjectured by Brini that the Gromov–Witten potential of local
\mathbb{P}^{1}
is a tau-function of Ablowitz–Ladik hierarchy
[
2
]
. Note that linear reciprocal transformations should preserve tau-functions, so the results in Sect.
4.2
probably suggest relations among Gromov–Witten invariants of
\mathbb{P}^{1}
and those of local
\mathbb{P}^{1}
.

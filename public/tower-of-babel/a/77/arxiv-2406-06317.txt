Effect of graph operations on graph associahedra
Ana Gargantini, Adrián Pastine, Pablo Torres

Abstract
Given a graph G G , we determine the structure of the rotation graph of a graph obtained by applying certain operations to G G . Specifically, we consider the operations of adding a simplicial vertex, adding a true twin to a vertex, and the two closely related operations of deleting the set of edges from a subgraph induced by a set of true twins, and adding a false twin to a vertex. We describe how applying these operations to a graph affects the structure of its rotation graph. Furthermore, using this description, we study chromatic number, distance, and diameter in rotation graphs. In particular, we establish conditions under which the chromatic number of the rotation graphs is preserved. As an interesting consequence, we obtain that the chromatic number of the rotation graphs of threshold graphs (which includes complete split graphs and star graphs) and of complete bipartite graph is 3. We also provide a new lower bound for diam ​ ( ℛ ​ ( G − S ) ) \text{diam}(\mathcal{R}(G-S)) in terms of diam ​ ( ℛ ​ ( G ) ) \text{diam}(\mathcal{R}(G)) , where S S is the set of edges of the subgraph of G G induced by a set of true twins. As a consequence, we improve the known lower bound for the diameter of the rotation graph of balanced complete bipartite graphs, allowing us to compute the exact value of diam ​ ( ℛ ​ ( K 2 , q ) ) \text{diam}(\mathcal{R}(K_{2,q})) for q ∈ { 3 , 4 , 5 , 6 , 7 , 8 } q\in\{3,4,5,6,7,8\} .

Introduction

Given a connected graph
G
, the graph associahedron
\mathcal{A}(G)
of

is the convex polytope whose face poset is isomorphic to the inclusion order of tubings on

[
7
]
. Several families of graph associahedra correspond to well-known polytopes. For instance, when
G
is a complete graph, a path, a cycle or a star, the graph associahedron
\mathcal{A}(G)
is the permutohedron, the classical associahedron, the cyclohedron, or the stellohedron, respectively.

Moreover, graph associahedra find relevance beyond generalization since they provide a tool for the study of combinatorial structures and their geometric representation, finding applications in various domains including optimization
[
13
]
, hierarchical visualization systems
[
19
]
, random structure generation
[
9
]
, and probabilistic methods
[
15
]
. Additionally, they hold significance in algebra and physics as specific instances of generalized permutohedra
[
1
,
16
]
. One particularly compelling direction of study is the investigation on the graph
\mathcal{R}(G)
determined by the
1
-skeleton of
\mathcal{A}(G)
, and its graph properties. There is a number of equivalent formulations for this structure. Among them, in this article we approach the study of
\mathcal{R}(G)
following
[
3
]
, considering its representation as the rotation graph of
G
, that is, as the graph whose vertex set is the set of search trees on
G
and whose edges are determined by rotations on search trees.

Graph properties of
\mathcal{R}(G)
, such as hamiltonicity, connectivity and diameter have been extensively considered. For example, the diameter of permuthohedra, classical associahedra
[
18
]
, cyclohedra
[
17
]
and stellohedra
[
14
]
were computed. Besides, in
[
6
]
the authors give the exact diameter of the rotation graph of complete split graphs and of unbalanced complete bipartite graphs. In the same article, lower and upper bounds are provided for the diameter of the rotation graph of trivially perfect graphs and for the diameter of rotation graphs
\mathcal{R}(G)
in terms of the order of
G
and its tree-depth, treewidth, and pathwidth. Another challenging problem on rotation graphs is coloring. In
[
11
]
the authors consider the chromatic number of the classical associahedron. They prove that it is bounded above by

. Apart from these results for the classical associahedra and the fact that permutohedra are bipartite, computing the chromatic number of
\mathcal{R}(G)
for the general case remains an open problem.

An interesting tool to study the structure of rotation graphs is based on the reflection on
\mathcal{R}(G)
of an operation on
G
. For instance, in
[
8
]
, pseudograph associahedra are introduced, and consider deformations of pseudograph associahedra as their underlying graphs are altered by edge contractions and edge deletions. Manneville and Pilaud
[
14

proved that if

is in general exponential in

. In 2022, Cardinal et al.
[
5

obtained an efficient algorithm to construct Hamiltonian paths in rotation graphs of chordal graphs by applying a combinatorial generation framework proposed in
[
12
]
. For this graph class, the authors studied search trees of
G
and the relationship with a perfect elimination ordering of the vertices of
G
.

In this regard, we consider the effect of three different graph operations on

on the structure of
\mathcal{R}(G)
. More specifically, given a subset

that induces a clique, we consider the graph

obtained by adding a simplicial vertex whose neighbourhood is

, and given

we consider the graphs

and

obtained from

by adding a true twin of

, and adding a false twin of

can be obtained from

by deleting and edge connecting true twins, serves as a motivation to consider the operation on a graph

In Section

In Section

Remark 2.3

It is not hard to see that a vertex

3
Chromatic number of rotation graphs

In this section we establish results that link the application of the considered graph operations with the chromatic number of the corresponding rotation graphs. Consequently, we deduce that the chromatic number of rotation graphs of both non-complete threshold graphs and complete bipartite graphs is 3.

We start by stating a lemma characterizing permutohedra as bipartite rotation graphs, as well as rotation graphs free of 5-cycles. Although the result seems to be known, we could not find a clear reference. Thus, we include a proof for completeness.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
G
be a connected graph with
|V(G)|\geq 3
. The following are equivalent.

(a)

G
is a complete graph.

(b)

\chi(\mathcal{R}(G))=2
.

(c)

\mathcal{R}(G)
is free of 5-cycles.

Proof.

Let
n\in\mathbb{N}
and
G=K_{n}
. Note that the search trees on
G
are paths, and that there is a bijective correspondence between the vertices of
\mathcal{R}(G)
and permutations of the set
\{1,\ldots,n\}
(elements of the symmetric group
S_{n}
). For
T\in V(\mathcal{R}(G))
, let
\sigma_{T}\in S_{n}
be the permutation associated with
T
. Consider the function
c\colon V(\mathcal{R}(G))\rightarrow\{-1,1\}
defined by
c(T)=\text{sgn}(\sigma_{T})
, where
\text{sgn}(\sigma_{T})
denotes the sign of the permutation. If
T,T^{\prime}\in V(\mathcal{R}(G))
differ by a rotation, then
\sigma_{T}
and
\sigma_{T^{\prime}}
differ by one transposition. Therefore,
c
is a coloring of
\mathcal{R}(G)
and
\chi(\mathcal{R}(G))=2
, that is,
(a)
implies
(b)
.

Assume
G
is not a complete graph. Let us prove that
\mathcal{R}(G)
has a 5-cycle. Since
G
is connected and not complete, there exists
a,c,b\in V(G)
such that
ab,bc\in E(G)
but
ac\notin E(G)
. Let
n=|V(G)|
and label the vertices in
V(G)-\{a,b,c\}
as
v_{1},\ldots,v_{n-3}
. Consider the
six vertex elimination orders

\displaystyle v_{1},\ldots,v_{n-3},a,b,c

\displaystyle v_{1},\ldots,v_{n-3},a,c,b

\displaystyle v_{1},\ldots,v_{n-3},c,a,b

\displaystyle v_{1},\ldots,v_{n-3},c,b,a

\displaystyle v_{1},\ldots,v_{n-3},b,c,a

\displaystyle v_{1},\ldots,v_{n-3},b,a,c.

Since
ac\notin E(G)
, the last two vertex elimination orders determine the same search tree on
G
. Then, these are five different search trees two consecutive of them are adjacent (the adjacencies are determined by rotations of the pairs
bc,ac,ab,cb
, respectively). Also, the first and the last are adjacent since they differ by one

-rotation. Therefore, the five search trees form a 5-cycle in
\mathcal{R}(G)
. We have proved that (
c
) implies (
a
).

Lastly, it is clear that if
\chi(\mathcal{R}(G))=2
, then
\mathcal{R}(G)
is bipartite and, therefore, is free of 5-cycles.
∎

Corollary 3.3
.

Let

In the subsequent result, it is proved that if

Proposition 3.4

It is known that a connected threshold graph

Remark 3.6

4
On distances and diameter of rotation graphs

The problem of finding distances in rotation graphs, as well as determining or bounding their diameter, has attracted attention in recent research. To address this, we begin by presenting a result that connects the adjacency of vertex pairs in a graph
G
with the number of rotations they undergo in sequences of rotations in

\mathcal{R}(G)
. This result will be useful for the analysis of distances and diameters in rotation graphs that follows.

If
T,T^{\prime}\in V(\mathcal{R}(G))
and
u,v\in V(G)
are vertices such that
u
is an ancestor of
v
in
T
and a descendant of
v
in
T^{\prime}
, then we say that
u,v
have different relative order in
T
and
T^{\prime}
. If
u
is an ancestor (alternatively descendant) of

in both

and
T^{\prime}
, then we say that
u,v
have the same relative order in
T
and
T^{\prime}
.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
G
be a graph and let
u,v\in V(G)
.

(a)

Suppose
u
and
v
are adjacent in
G
and let

. Then,
u
and
v
have different relative order in
T
and
T^{\prime}
if and only if any sequence of rotations that transforms
T
into
T^{\prime}
has an odd number of
uv
-rotations.

(b)

If
u
and
v
are true twins
in
G
and
T,T^{\prime}\in V(\mathcal{R}(G))

TT^{\prime}
-path in
\mathcal{R}(G)

uv
-rotation if

and

have different relative orders in
T
and in

uv
-rotations if

and

have the same relative order in

and in
T^{\prime}
.

Proof.

(

) The result is a direct consequence of Remark
1.2
.

(

) Let

and

a minimum lenght

has vertices

and that
T_{i}T_{i+1}
are adjacent for

Let

be true twins in
G
and assume two or more edges in

correspond to
uv
-rotations. Let
i,j\in\{0,\ldots,n-1\}
be indices such that
i+1<j

and
T_{j}T_{j+1}
are edges determined by two of these rotations.

Consider
\rho:V(G)\rightarrow V(G)

\rho(u)=v
,
\rho(v)=u

is a search tree on

We have that the search trees

form a

-walk with exactly two edges (determined by

Therefore,

-rotation. By item (

, and zero otherwise.
∎

a

\displaystyle\text{dist}_{\mathcal{R}(G)}(T,T^{\prime})

\displaystyle=\text{len}(A)

\displaystyle\leq\text{len}(B)

\displaystyle=\text{dist}_{\mathcal{R}(G-S)}(\pi(T),\pi(T^{\prime}))+\binom{|W|}{2}

(1)

Cardinal et al. show in
[

\text{diam}(\mathcal{R}(\text{SPK}_{p,q}))=\begin{cases}2pq+\binom{p}{2}=2m-\binom{p}{2},\text{ if }q\geq 4p+1\\

They also show that

for unbalanced complete bipartite graphs, that is, for

(2)

Lemma 4.9

Let

5
Further remarks and future work

The graph operations introduced in this article provide a method for generating several families of graphs. As a result, the findings presented here offer tools for investigating the structural and combinatorial properties of their corresponding rotation graphs, as well as key graph parameters such as chromatic number and diameter

From the results concerning chromatic numbers, interesting problems arise:

•

We observed that a rotation graph has chromatic number 2 if and only if it is a permutohedron. Since the chromatic number of rotation graphs for threshold graphs and complete bipartite graphs is 3, it would be interesting to characterize all rotation graphs with chromatic number 3. In particular, do rotation graphs of trivially perfect graphs also have chromatic number 3?

•

It is well-established that trivially perfect graphs can be constructed from a single-vertex graph through the operations of disjoint union of trivially perfect graphs and addition of a vertex whose open neighborhood forms a trivially perfect graph. Therefore, a compelling approach to addressing the previous question is to determine whether adding a universal vertex to a graph preserves the chromatic number of its corresponding rotation graph.

•

We have established that under certain conditions, adding a vertex to a graph preserves the chromatic number of the corresponding rotation graph. For example, adding a pendant vertex to a universal vertex
v
preserves the chromatic number, though this might not be the case if
v
is not universal. As noted in
[
2
]
,
\chi(\mathcal{R}(P_{10}))\geq 4
, where
P_{10}
is the path on 10 vertices. A natural next step is to identify which operations on a graph lead to an increase in the chromatic number of its corresponding rotation graph.

Further questions on distances and diameter of rotation graphs emerge:

•

An appealing direction for future work would be to derive bounds for distances, similar to those established in Theorem
4.7
, where analyzing the structure of modified rotation graphs enables bounding the distances between pairs of vertices. Ultimately, determining precise distances in rotation graphs generated through the described graph operations could provide deeper insights into their structural properties, and the computation of their diameters.

•

Let
G
be a graph with a subset
W
of true twins and
S=E(G[W])
.
By Theorem
4.7
we have that
0\leq\text{diam}(\mathcal{R}(G))-\text{diam}(\mathcal{R}(G-S))\leq\binom{|W|}{2}
. As we saw for the case of
K_{2,q}
and
\text{SPK}_{2,q}
, when
|W|=2
both possible values for
\text{diam}(\mathcal{R}(G))-\text{diam}(\mathcal{R}(G-S))
are achieved. Is this the case for every size of
W
? In other words, given
j
and
0\leq k\leq\binom{j}{2}
, is there a graph
G
with a subset
W
of true twins such that
|W|=j
and that
\text{diam}(\mathcal{R}(G))-\text{diam}(\mathcal{R}(G-S))=k
?

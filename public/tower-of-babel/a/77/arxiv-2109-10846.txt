1.
Introduction

The primary motivation for the present work comes from Shimorin’s analytic model for a left-invertible analytic operator
[
15
]
, which facilitates us to model a left-invertible analytic operator
T
as the operator of multiplication by the co-ordinate function on the Hilbert space
\mathscr{H}
of
\ker T^{*}
-valued holomorphic functions defined on
\mathbb{D}\big(0,r(T^{\prime})^{-1}\big)
, where
r(T^{\prime})
is the spectral radius of the Cauchy dual
T^{\prime}
of
T
. This analytic model gives that the open disc
\mathbb{D}\big(0,r(T^{\prime})^{-1}\big)
is contained in the set of all bounded point evaluations on
\mathscr{H}
. On the other hand, the example
[
5
, Example 2]
shows that the disc
\mathbb{D}\big(0,r(T^{\prime})^{-1}\big)
is not optimum and may be properly contained in the set of all bounded point evaluations on
\mathscr{H}
in general. Therefore, it comes very naturally to inquire about the set of all bounded point evaluations on
\mathscr{H}
. For this, we adapt the abstract approach of Williams
[
18
]
who, motivated by the work of Trent
[
16
]
, introduced and studied the notion of bounded point evaluation for a cyclic operator. His idea was extended by Miller-Miller-Neumann
[
13
]
for rationally cyclic operators which was further generalized by the trio Guendafi-Mbekhta-Zerouali
[
8
]
for finitely multicyclic operators. Later, Mbekhta-Ourchane-Zerouali
[
12
]
extended and studied the notion of bounded point evaluation for rationally multicyclic operators. A contribution in the study of bounded point evaluations for a cyclic operator was also made by Bourhim-Chidume-Zerouali
[
3
]
.

In this paper, we extend and study the notion of bounded point evaluation for an operator with the wandering subspace property. Besides, studying the general properties of the set
bpe(T)
of all bounded point evaluations for an operator
T
with the wandering subspace property, we also describe
bpe(T)
in terms of the invertibility of the restriction of specific projections (see Theorem
2.4
). In addition, we give the complete description of
bpe(T)
for a left-invertible operator
T
with the wandering subspace property. This comprises a major part of Section 2. In the third section, we study the notion of analytic bounded point evaluation and characterize the set
abpe(T)
of all analytic bounded point evaluations for a left-invertible operator
T
with the wandering subspace property. This, in particular, recovers the Shimorin’s analytic model for a left-invertible operator with the wandering subspace property. We wind up this paper with examples of left-invertible operator
T
with the wandering subspace property for which
\mathbb{D}\big(0,r(T^{\prime})^{-1}\big)\subsetneqq abpe(T)\subseteq bpe(T)
.

We set below the notations to be used in the posterior sections. The set of non-negative integers, the field of real numbers and the field of complex numbers are denoted by
\mathbb{N},\mathbb{R}
and
\mathbb{C}
respectively. The complex conjugate of a complex number
w
is denoted by
\overline{w}
, and for a non-empty subset
F
of
\mathbb{C}
, the complex conjugate of
F
is given by
\overline{F}:=\{\overline{w}:w\in F\}
. The interior of
F
is denoted by
\mbox{int}\,F
. An open disc in the complex plane centred at
w
with radius
r>0
is denoted by
\mathbb{D}(w,r)
whereas the closed disc centred at
w
with radius
r>0
is denoted by
\overline{\mathbb{D}}(w,r)
. For a polynomial
p
,
\mbox{deg}\,p
denotes the degree of
p
. Let
\mathcal{H}
be a complex separable Hilbert space. By a subspace of
\mathcal{H}
we do not mean a closed subspace of
\mathcal{H}
. If
\mathcal{M}
is a subspace of
\mathcal{H}
, then its closure is denoted by
\mbox{cl}\,\mathcal{M}
. Further, if
\mathcal{M}
is a subspace of
\mathcal{H}
, then by
\dim\mathcal{M}
we mean the Hilbert space dimension of
\mathcal{M}
. The orthogonal complement of a subspace
\mathcal{M}
is denoted by
\mathcal{M}^{\bot}
. If
\mathcal{M}
is a closed subspace of
\mathcal{H}
, then
P_{\mathcal{M}}
stands for the orthogonal projection of
\mathcal{H}
onto
\mathcal{M}
. For a subset
S
of
\mathcal{H}
, the linear span and the closed linear span of
S
are denoted by
\mbox{span}\{x:x\in S\}
and
\bigvee\{x:x\in S\}
respectively. Let
\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})
denote the algebra of bounded linear operators on
\mathcal{H}
. For
T\in\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})
, the kernel, range and adjoint of
T
are denoted by
\ker T
,
\mbox{ran}\,T
and
T^{*}
respectively. The spectrum and the point spectrum of
T\in\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})
are respectively denoted by
\sigma(T)
and
\sigma_{p}(T)
whereas
r(T)
stands for the spectral radius of
T
. If
T\in\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})
is left-invertible, then
T^{*}T
is invertible. In this case, the Cauchy dual
T^{\prime}
of
T
is defined as
T^{\prime}:=T(T^{*}T)^{-1}
. Further, we say that
T\in\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})
is analytic if
\bigcap_{n\in\mathbb{N}}T^{n}(\mathcal{H})=\{0\}
.

2.
Bounded point evaluation: General properties

Let
T
be a bounded linear operator on a complex separable Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}
. A closed subspace
\mathcal{M}
of
\mathcal{H}
is said to be a
cyclic subspace
for
T
if

\displaystyle\bigvee_{n\in\mathbb{N}}T^{n}(\mathcal{M})=\mathcal{H}.

If
\dim\mathcal{M}
is finite, then
T
is said to be
finitely cyclic
. We say that
T
has the
wandering subspace property
if
\ker T^{*}
is a cyclic subspace for
T
. A rich supply of examples of operators with the wandering subspace property is provided by the class of operator-valued unilateral shift
[
9
, Theorem 4.5]
. Let
T\in\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})
have the wandering subspace property. Then for a
\ker T^{*}
-valued polynomial
p(z)=\sum_{n=0}^{k}x_{n}z^{n}
,
x_{n}\in\ker T^{*}
, we set

\displaystyle p(T):=\sum_{n=0}^{k}T^{n}x_{n}.

If
\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{C},\ker T^{*})
denotes the vector space of all
\ker T^{*}
-valued polynomials, then
\{p(T):p\in\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{C},\ker T^{*})\}
is a dense subspace of
\mathcal{H}
. Thus, we have the following definition.

Definition 2.1
.

Let
T
be a bounded linear operator on a complex separable Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}
. Suppose that
T
has the wandering subspace property. A point
w\in\mathbb{C}
is said to be a
bounded point evaluation for

T
if there exists a positive constant
c
such that

\displaystyle\|p(w)\|\leqslant c\,\|p(T)\|\ \mbox{for all }p\in\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{C},\ker T^{*}).

The set of all bounded point evaluations for
T
is denoted by
bpe(T)
.

The notation
bpe(T)
was used for the set of all bounded point evaluations for a finitely cyclic operator (with respect to some cyclic subspace)
[
8
]
. It can be easily seen that if
T
is finitely cyclic with the wandering subspace property, then
bpe(T)
given by the preceding definition coincides with the
bpe(T)
defined in
[
8
]
.

Let
w\in bpe(T)
. Then the map
E_{w}:\{p(T):p\in\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{C},\ker T^{*})\}\rightarrow\ker T^{*}
defined by

\displaystyle E_{w}(p(T))=p(w),\quad p\in\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{C},\ker T^{*}),

is well-defined, linear, surjective and continuous. Also, it is easy to see that
E_{w}|_{\ker T^{*}}=I_{\ker T^{*}}
. Since
\{p(T):p\in\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{C},\ker T^{*})\}
is a dense subspace of
\mathcal{H}
,
E_{w}
can be extended continuously on
\mathcal{H}
. We denote the continuous extension of
E_{w}
on
\mathcal{H}
by
E_{w}
itself. The following proposition studies the general properties of
E_{w}
.

Proposition 2.2
.

Let
T
be a bounded linear operator on a complex separable Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}
. Suppose that
T
has the wandering subspace property. Then the following statements hold:

(i)

0\in bpe(T)\subseteq\overline{\sigma_{p}(T^{*})}
.

(ii)

If
w\in bpe(T)
, then
E_{w}^{*}(\ker T^{*})=\ker(T^{*}-\overline{w})
. Moreover,
E_{w}^{*}:\ker T^{*}\rightarrow\ker(T^{*}-\overline{w})
is invertible.

(iii)

\ker E_{w}=\textup{cl\,ran}(T-w)
for each
w\in bpe(T)
.

(iv)

For each
w\in bpe(T)
,
E_{w}E^{*}_{w}
is invertible on
\ker T^{*}
. Moreover,
E_{0}E^{*}_{0}=I_{\ker T^{*}}
.

(v)

If
w\in bpe(T)
, then
E_{w}T^{n}x=w^{n}E_{w}x
for all
x\in\mathcal{H}
and
n\in\mathbb{N}
.

Proof.

The proof of (i) goes exactly along the lines of the proofs of
[
17
, Proposition 2.4(i),(ii)]
with minor modifications. To see (ii), let
w\in bpe(T)
. For
p\in\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{C},\ker T^{*})
and
x\in\ker T^{*}
, we have

\displaystyle\langle{T^{*}E_{w}^{*}x},\,{p(T)}\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\langle{E_{w}^{*}x},\,{Tp(T)}\rangle=\langle{x},\,{E_{w}(Tp(T))}\rangle=\langle{x},\,{wp(w)}\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\langle{\overline{w}x},\,{p(w)}\rangle=\langle{\overline{w}E_{w}^{*}x},\,{p(T)}\rangle.

Since
\{p(T):\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{C},\ker T^{*})\}
is a dense subspace of
\mathcal{H}
, it follows that
T^{*}E_{w}^{*}x=\overline{w}E_{w}^{*}x
. Thus
E_{w}^{*}(\ker T^{*})\subseteq\ker(T^{*}-\overline{w})
. Note that
E_{w}^{*}:\ker T^{*}\rightarrow\ker(T^{*}-\overline{w})
is expansive. Indeed, for each
x\in\ker T^{*}
, we have

\displaystyle\|x\|^{2}=\langle{x},\,{E_{w}x}\rangle=\langle{E_{w}^{*}x},\,{x}\rangle\leqslant\|E_{w}^{*}x\|\|x\|.

Thus
E_{w}^{*}
is injective and has closed range. Suppose that there exists
h\in\ker(T^{*}-\overline{w})
such that
\langle{E_{w}^{*}x},\,{h}\rangle=0
for all
x\in\ker T^{*}
. This, in turn, implies that
\langle{x},\,{E_{w}h}\rangle=0
for all
x\in\ker T^{*}
, and hence,
E_{w}h=0
. Also, for any
p(z)=\sum_{n=0}^{k}x_{n}z^{n}\in\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{C},\ker T^{*})
, we get

\displaystyle\Big\langle h,\sum_{n=0}^{k}T^{n}x_{n}\Big\rangle=\sum_{n=0}^{k}\langle{T^{*n}h},\,{x_{n}}\rangle=\sum_{n=0}^{k}\langle{\overline{w}^{n}h},\,{x_{n}}\rangle=\langle{h},\,{p(w)}\rangle.

(2.1)

Let
\{p_{n}\}_{n\geqslant 1}
be a sequence of polynomials in
\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{C},\ker T^{*})
such that
p_{n}(T)\rightarrow h
as
n\rightarrow\infty
. Then

\displaystyle\|h\|^{2}=\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\langle{h},\,{p_{n}(T)}\rangle\overset{\eqref{h-pw}}{=}\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\langle{h},\,{p_{n}(w)}\rangle=\langle{h},\,{E_{w}h}\rangle=0.

Thus
E_{w}^{*}(\ker T^{*})=\ker(T^{*}-\overline{w})
and hence (ii) stands verified.

For (iii), observe that for each
w\in bpe(T)
, we have

\displaystyle\ker E_{w}={\mbox{ran}\,E^{*}_{w}}^{\bot}\overset{(ii)}{=}\ker(T^{*}-\overline{w})^{\bot}=\mbox{cl}\,\mbox{ran}\,(T-w).

To see (iv), let
w\in bpe(T)
. Note that
E_{w}|_{\ker(T^{*}-\overline{w})}:\ker(T^{*}-\overline{w})\rightarrow\ker T^{*}
is an invertible operator by virtue of (iii). Further, it follows from (ii) that
E_{w}^{*}:\ker T^{*}\rightarrow\ker(T^{*}-\overline{w})
is invertible. Thus
E_{w}E^{*}_{w}
is invertible on
\ker T^{*}
. For the moreover part, let
x\in\ker T^{*}
and
p(z)=\sum_{n=0}^{k}x_{n}z^{n}\in\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{C},\ker T^{*})
. Then we get

\displaystyle\langle{E^{*}_{0}x},\,{p(T)}\rangle=\langle{x},\,{E_{0}p(T)}\rangle=\langle{x},\,{p(0)}\rangle=\langle{x},\,{p(T)}\rangle.

Since
\{p(T):p\in\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{C},\ker T^{*})\}
is a dense subspace of
\mathcal{H}
, it follows that
E^{*}_{0}x=x
for all
x\in\ker T^{*}
. This gives that
E_{0}E^{*}_{0}x=x
for all
x\in\ker T^{*}
, and hence, establishes (iv).

The conclusion of (v) was derived in the proof of
[
17
, Proposition 2.2]
. Here we provide a different proof. Suppose that
w\in bpe(T)
. Then
E_{w}:\mathcal{H}\rightarrow\ker T^{*}
is continuous. Let
x\in\mathcal{H}
. Then for each
n\in\mathbb{N}
and
y\in\ker T^{*}
, we have

\displaystyle\langle{E_{w}T^{n}x},\,{y}\rangle=\langle{x},\,{T^{*n}E^{*}_{w}y}\rangle=\langle{x},\,{\overline{w}^{n}E^{*}_{w}y}\rangle=\langle{w^{n}E_{w}x},\,{y}\rangle,

where in the second equality, we used (ii). Thus, we must have
E_{w}T^{n}x=w^{n}E_{w}x
for each
n\in\mathbb{N}
. This completes the proof.
∎

A bounded linear operator
T
on a complex separable Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}
is said to be
circular
if for each real number
\theta
there exists a unitary
U_{\theta}
on
\mathcal{H}
such that
U_{\theta}T=e^{i\theta}TU_{\theta}
. Circular operators were introduced and studied in
[
2
]
. Later, Gellar
[
7
]
characterized all the circular operators and proved that an operator is circular if and only if it is an operator-valued shift
[
7
, Proposition 1]
. The following proposition shows that if
T
is circular, then
bpe(T)
has circular symmetry.

Proposition 2.3
.

Let
T
be a bounded linear operator on a complex separable Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}
with the wandering subspace property. If
T
is circular, then the set
bpe(T)
of all bounded point evaluations for
T
has circular symmetry.

Proof.

Suppose that
T
is circular. Let
\theta
be an arbitrary but fixed real number. We first show that

\displaystyle bpe(T)=bpe(e^{i\theta}T).

(2.2)

The proof of (
2.2
) follows by imitating the arguments of the proof of
[
17
, Proposition 2.3]
but we include the details for the sake of completeness. To this end, let
U_{\theta}
be the unitary on
\mathcal{H}
such that
U_{\theta}T=e^{i\theta}TU_{\theta}
. Since
\ker T^{*}=\ker(e^{i\theta}T)^{*}
, we must have
U_{\theta}(\ker T^{*})=\ker T^{*}
. Now suppose that
w\in bpe(T)
. Let
p(z)=\sum_{n=0}^{k}x_{n}z^{n}\in\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{C},\ker T^{*})
. Then
p(z)=\sum_{n=0}^{k}U_{\theta}y_{n}z^{n}
,
y_{n}\in\ker T^{*}
. Thus, we have

\displaystyle\|p(w)\|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Big\|\sum_{n=0}^{k}U_{\theta}y_{n}w^{n}\Big\|=\Big\|\sum_{n=0}^{k}y_{n}w^{n}\Big\|\leqslant c\Big\|\sum_{n=0}^{k}T^{n}y_{n}\Big\|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle c\Big\|\sum_{n=0}^{k}U_{\theta}T^{n}y_{n}\Big\|=c\Big\|\sum_{n=0}^{k}e^{in\theta}T^{n}U_{\theta}y_{n}\Big\|=c\Big\|\sum_{n=0}^{k}e^{in\theta}T^{n}x_{n}\Big\|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle c\|p(e^{i\theta}T)\|.

This shows that
w\in bpe(e^{i\theta}T)
, and hence,
bpe(T)\subseteq bpe(e^{i\theta}T)
. The other way inclusion can be obtained similarly. This completes the verification of (
2.2
).

Further, suppose that
w\in bpe(T)
. Let
p(z)=\sum_{n=0}^{k}x_{n}z^{n}\in\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{C},\ker T^{*})
. Then
p(e^{i\theta}w)=\sum_{n=0}^{k}e^{in\theta}w^{n}x_{n}=\tilde{p}(w)
, where
\tilde{p}(z)=\sum_{n=0}^{k}y_{n}z^{n}
with
y_{n}=e^{in\theta}x_{n}
. Thus, we get

\displaystyle\|p(e^{i\theta}w)\|=\|\tilde{p}(w)\|\leqslant c\Big\|\sum_{n=0}^{k}T^{n}y_{n}\Big\|=c\Big\|\sum_{n=0}^{k}e^{in\theta}T^{n}x_{n}\Big\|=c\|p(e^{i\theta}T)\|.

This shows that
e^{i\theta}w\in bpe(e^{i\theta}T)\overset{\eqref{bpe-eq}}{=}bpe(T)
, and hence, completes the proof.
∎

We are now ready to give a complete description of
bpe(T)
. The following theorem extends
[
8
, Theorem 4]
.

Theorem 2.4
.

Let
T
be a bounded linear operator on a complex separable Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}
with the wandering subspace property. Let
\{x_{n}:1\leqslant n\leqslant\dim\ker T^{*}\}
be an orthonormal basis of
\ker T^{*}
. For
w\in\mathbb{C}
, let
P_{w}
denote the orthogonal projection of
\mathcal{H}
onto
\ker(T^{*}-\overline{w})
. Then the following statements are equivalent:

(i)

A complex number
w
is a bounded point evaluation for
T
.

(ii)

P_{w}|_{\ker T^{*}}:\ker T^{*}\rightarrow\ker(T^{*}-\overline{w})
is invertible.

(iii)

There exists a sequence of vectors
\{y_{n}:1\leqslant n\leqslant\dim\ker T^{*}\}
in
\ker(T^{*}-\overline{w})
such that
\langle{x_{i}},\,{y_{j}}\rangle=\delta_{ij}\ (\mbox{the Kronecker delta})
for all
i,j=1,\ldots,\dim\ker T^{*}
.

Proof.

We first establish the equivalence of (i) and (ii). To this end, let
w\in bpe(T)
. Then it can be easily shown that

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}=\bigvee_{n\in\mathbb{N}}(T-w)^{n}(\ker T^{*}).

Applying
P_{w}
on both sides of the above identity, we get that
\ker(T^{*}-\overline{w})=P_{w}(\ker T^{*})
. Thus,
P_{w}|_{\ker T^{*}}
is onto. To see that
P_{w}|_{\ker T^{*}}
is one-one, assume that
P_{w}x=0
for some
x\in\ker T^{*}
. Then
x\in\textup{cl\,ran}(T-w)
. Let
\{y_{n}\}_{n\geqslant 1}
be a sequence in
\mathcal{H}
such that
(T-w)y_{n}\rightarrow x
as
n\rightarrow\infty
. This, together with Proposition
2.2
(v), gives that

\displaystyle 0=E_{w}(T-w)y_{n}\rightarrow E_{w}x=x\mbox{ as }n\rightarrow\infty.

Thus
x=0
.

Conversely, suppose that
P_{w}|_{\ker T^{*}}:\ker T^{*}\rightarrow\ker(T^{*}-\overline{w})
is invertible for some
w\in\mathbb{C}
. Let
p(z)=\sum_{n=0}^{k}x_{n}z^{n}\in\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{C},\ker T^{*})
. Then for all
y\in\ker(T^{*}-\overline{w})
, we have

\displaystyle\langle{p(T)},\,{y}\rangle=\Big\langle\sum_{n=0}^{k}T^{n}x_{n},y\Big\rangle=\sum_{n=0}^{k}\langle{T^{n}x_{n}},\,{y}\rangle=\sum_{n=0}^{k}\langle{w^{n}x_{n}},\,{y}\rangle=\langle{p(w)},\,{y}\rangle.

The above equality gives that
\|P_{w}p(T)\|=\|P_{w}p(w)\|
. Since
P_{w}|_{\ker T^{*}}
is invertible, we get that

\displaystyle\|p(w)\|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\|(P_{w}|_{\ker T^{*}})^{-1}P_{w}p(w)\|\leqslant\|(P_{w}|_{\ker T^{*}})^{-1}\|\|P_{w}p(T)\|

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle\|(P_{w}|_{\ker T^{*}})^{-1}\|\|P_{w}\|\|p(T)\|.

Hence,
w\in bpe(T)
.

To see the equivalence of (ii) and (iii), suppose that
P_{w}|_{\ker T^{*}}:\ker T^{*}\rightarrow\ker(T^{*}-\overline{w})
is invertible for some
w\in\mathbb{C}
. Let
\{h_{n}:1\leqslant n\leqslant\dim\ker T^{*}\}
be an orthonormal basis of
\ker(T^{*}-\overline{w})
. Let
(a_{ij})_{1\leqslant i,j\leqslant\dim\ker T^{*}}
be the matrix representation of
(P_{w}|_{\ker T^{*}})^{-1}
with respect to the bases
\{h_{n}:1\leqslant n\leqslant\dim\ker T^{*}\}
and
\{x_{n}:1\leqslant n\leqslant\dim\ker T^{*}\}
of
\ker(T^{*}-\overline{w})
and
\ker T^{*}
respectively. Set

\displaystyle y_{j}:=\sum_{k=1}^{\dim\ker T^{*}}a_{kj}h_{k},\quad j=1,\ldots,\dim\ker T^{*}.

Then it can be easily verified that
y_{j}\in\ker(T^{*}-\overline{w})
and
\langle{x_{i}},\,{y_{j}}\rangle=\delta_{ij}
for all
i,j=1,\ldots,\dim\ker T^{*}
.

Conversely, assume that (iii) holds. It was already seen in the beginning of the proof that
P_{w}(\ker T^{*})=\ker(T^{*}-\overline{w})
. Thus it only remains to show that
P_{w}|_{\ker T^{*}}
is one-one. To this end, let
P_{w}x=0
for some
x\in\ker T^{*}
. Then for all
j=1,\ldots,\dim\ker T^{*}
, we get

\displaystyle 0=\langle{P_{w}x},\,{y_{j}}\rangle=\langle{x},\,{y_{j}}\rangle=\Big\langle\sum_{i=1}^{\dim\ker T^{*}}\langle{x},\,{x_{i}}\rangle x_{i},y_{j}\Big\rangle=\langle{x},\,{x_{j}}\rangle.

Hence
x=0
. This completes the proof.
∎

We characterize below
bpe(T)
for a left-invertible operator
T
with the wandering subspace property (cf.
[
9
, Proposition 5.1]
).

Theorem 2.5
.

Let
T
be a left-invertible operator on a complex separable Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}
and let
T^{\prime}
denote the Cauchy dual of
T
. Suppose that
T
has the wandering subspace property. For each
w\in\mathbb{C}
and
n\in\mathbb{N}
, set

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}_{n}:=\bigvee_{k=0}^{n}T^{k}(\ker T^{*})\quad\mbox{and}\quad S_{n}(w):=\sum_{k=0}^{n}\overline{w}^{k}T^{\prime k}.

Then
w\in bpe(T)
if and only if
\displaystyle\sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\|P_{\mathcal{M}_{n}}S_{n}(w)|_{kerT^{*}}\|<\infty
. Moreover, if
w\in bpe(T)
, then
\|E_{w}^{*}\|=\displaystyle\sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\|P_{\mathcal{M}_{n}}S_{n}(w)|_{kerT^{*}}\|
.

Proof.

Suppose that
w\in bpe(T)
. Then
E_{w}:\mathcal{H}\rightarrow\ker T^{*}
is continuous. Let
n\in\mathbb{N}
. Then for each
\ker T^{*}
-valued polynomial
p
with degree up to
n
and for each
x\in\ker T^{*}
, we get

\displaystyle\langle{S_{n}(w)x},\,{p(T)}\rangle=\langle{x},\,{p(w)}\rangle=\langle{E_{w}^{*}x},\,{p(T)}\rangle.

(2.3)

Let
y\in\mathcal{M}_{n}
. Then there exists a sequence of
\ker T^{*}
-valued polynomials
p_{m}
with degree up to
n
such that
p_{m}(T)\rightarrow y
as
m\rightarrow\infty
. Thus for each
x\in\ker T^{*}
, we get

\displaystyle\langle{S_{n}(w)x},\,{y}\rangle=\lim_{m\rightarrow\infty}\langle{S_{n}(w)x},\,{p_{m}(T)}\rangle\overset{\eqref{Sn-Ew}}{=}\lim_{m\rightarrow\infty}\langle{E_{w}^{*}x},\,{p_{m}(T)}\rangle=\langle{E_{w}^{*}x},\,{y}\rangle.

This gives that
P_{\mathcal{M}_{n}}(S_{n}(w)x-E_{w}^{*}x)=0
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
. Consequently, we obtain

\displaystyle\|P_{\mathcal{M}_{n}}S_{n}(w)x\|\leqslant\|E_{w}^{*}x\|\mbox{ for all }n\in\mathbb{N}\mbox{ and for all }x\in\ker T^{*}.

(2.4)

Application of the uniform boundedness principle in the above inequality yields the desired conclusion.

Conversely, suppose that
w\in\mathbb{C}
is such that
\sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\|P_{\mathcal{M}_{n}}S_{n}(w)|_{kerT^{*}}\|<\infty
. Let
p
be any
\ker T^{*}
-valued polynomial. Then for any
n\geqslant\mbox{deg}\,p
, we have

\displaystyle\|p(w)\|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sup_{x\in\ker T^{*},\ \|x\|=1}|\langle{x},\,{p(w)}\rangle|=\sup_{x\in\ker T^{*},\ \|x\|=1}|\langle{S_{n}(w)x},\,{p(T)}\rangle|

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle\|P_{\mathcal{M}_{n}}S_{n}(w)|_{kerT^{*}}\|\|p(T)\|\leqslant\Big(\sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\|P_{\mathcal{M}_{n}}S_{n}(w)|_{kerT^{*}}\|\Big)\|p(T)\|.

Thus
w\in bpe(T)
.

For the moreover part, assume that
w\in bpe(T)
. Then (
2.4
) implies that
\displaystyle\sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\|P_{\mathcal{M}_{n}}S_{n}(w)|_{kerT^{*}}\|\leqslant\|E_{w}^{*}\|
. To see the reverse inequality, let
y\in\mathcal{H}
with
\|y\|=1
. Then there exists a sequence
\{p_{n}(T)\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
with
\|p_{n}(T)\|=1
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
such that
p_{n}(T)\rightarrow y
as
n\rightarrow\infty
. For all
x\in\ker T^{*}
with
\|x\|=1
, we get

\displaystyle|\langle{E_{w}^{*}x},\,{y}\rangle|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}|\langle{E_{w}^{*}x},\,{p_{n}(T)}\rangle|\overset{\eqref{Sn-Ew}}{=}\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}|\langle{S_{deg\,p_{n}}(w)x},\,{p_{n}(T)}\rangle|

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\|P_{\mathcal{M}_{deg\,p_{n}}}S_{deg\,p_{n}}(w)x\|\leqslant\sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\|P_{\mathcal{M}_{n}}S_{n}(w)|_{kerT^{*}}\|.

As a consequence, we get
\|E_{w}^{*}\|\leqslant\sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\|P_{\mathcal{M}_{n}}S_{n}(w)|_{kerT^{*}}\|
. This completes the proof.
∎

The following corollary shows that the
\mbox{int}\,bpe(T)
is non-empty for a left-invertible operator
T
with the wandering subspace property. In fact, it shows that
bpe(T)
contains an open disc centred at the origin which is in general bigger that
\mathbb{D}\big(0,r(T^{\prime})^{-1}\big)
. To see this, we need the following definition: For
T\in\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})
and
x\in\mathcal{H}
, we set
r_{T}(x):=\displaystyle\limsup_{n\rightarrow\infty}\|T^{n}x\|^{\frac{1}{n}}
. In the literature,
r_{T}(x)
is known as
local spectral radius
of
T
at
x

[
11
]
. Clearly,
r_{T}(x)\leqslant r(T)
.

Corollary 2.6
.

Let
T
be a left-invertible operator on a complex separable Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}
and let
T^{\prime}
denote the Cauchy dual of
T
. Suppose that
T
has the wandering subspace property. Then the open disc
\mathbb{D}(0,r)
is contained in the
bpe(T)
, where
\displaystyle r:=\inf_{x\in\ker T^{*}}\frac{1}{r_{T^{\prime}}(x)}
. In particular,
\mathbb{D}\big(0,r(T^{\prime})^{-1}\big)\subseteq bpe(T)
. Moreover, for each
w\in\mathbb{D}(0,r)
, we have
E_{w}^{*}x=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\overline{w}^{n}T^{\prime n}x
for all
x\in\ker T^{*}
.

Proof.

For
x\in\ker T^{*}
, the radius of convergence of the power series
\sum_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\overline{w}^{n}T^{\prime n}x
is
\big(\limsup_{n\rightarrow\infty}\|T^{\prime n}x\|^{\frac{1}{n}}\big)^{-1}=\frac{1}{r_{T^{\prime}}(x)}
. If we set
r:=\inf_{x\in\ker T^{*}}\frac{1}{r_{T^{\prime}}(x)}
, then it follows that if
w\in\mathbb{D}(0,r)
, then
\sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\|S_{n}(w)x\|<\infty
for all
x\in\ker T^{*}
. Hence, by the uniform boundedness principle, we get that
\sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\|S_{n}(w)|_{\ker T^{*}}\|<\infty
. Thus,
\mathbb{D}(0,r)\subseteq bpe(T)
. For the moreover part, let
p
be any
\ker T^{*}
-valued polynomial and
w\in\mathbb{D}(0,r)
. Then for each
n\geqslant\mbox{deg}\,p
, by (
2.3
), we get
\langle{S_{n}(w)x},\,{p(T)}\rangle=\langle{E_{w}^{*}x},\,{p(T)}\rangle
. Taking
n\rightarrow\infty
, we get the desired conclusion.
∎

The subspace
\mathcal{M}:=\bigvee_{w\in bpe(T)}E^{*}_{w}(\ker T^{*})
plays a central role in studying the function theoretic behaviour of
T
. In fact, if
\mathcal{M}=\mathcal{H}
, then it turns out that
T
can be modelled as
\mathscr{M}_{z}
on a reproducing kernel Hilbert space of
\ker T^{*}
-valued functions defined on
bpe(T)
(see Theorem
3.3
). The following proposition studies the general properties of
\mathcal{M}
and also gives a dichotomy that
\mathcal{M}
is either whole
\mathcal{H}
or
\mathcal{M}
skips an infinite dimensional subspace if
T
is injective and analytic.

Proposition 2.7
.

Let
T
be a bounded linear operator on a complex separable Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}
with the wandering subspace property. Let
\mathcal{M}:=\bigvee_{w\in bpe(T)}E^{*}_{w}(\ker T^{*})
. Then the following statements hold:

(i)

\mathcal{M}^{\bot}
is
T
-invariant.

(ii)

If
T
is injective and analytic, then either
\mathcal{M}=\mathcal{H}
or
\mathcal{M}^{\bot}
is infinite dimensional.

(iii)

If
T
is circular, then
T|_{\mathcal{M}^{\bot}}
is circular.

(iv)

If
T
is analytic, then
T|_{\mathcal{M}^{\bot}}
is analytic.

(v)

P_{\mathcal{M}^{\bot}}(\ker T^{*})\subseteq\ker(T|_{\mathcal{M}^{\bot}})^{*}
.

Proof.

Observe that
\mathcal{M}^{\bot}=\bigcap_{w\in bpe(T)}\ker E_{w}
. Let
x\in\mathcal{M}^{\bot}
. Then by Proposition
2.2
(v), we have
E_{w}Tx=wE_{w}x=0
for all
w\in bpe(T)
. Thus
Tx\in\mathcal{M}^{\bot}
. This proves (i).

Suppose that
T
is injective and analytic. Let
\mathcal{M}^{\bot}
be finite dimensional. Since
T
is injective, it follows from (i) that
T|_{\mathcal{M}^{\bot}}
is invertible. But then analyticity of
T
gives that

\mathcal{M}^{\bot}\subseteq\bigcap_{n\in\mathbb{N}}T^{n}(\mathcal{H})=\{0\}.

This completes the verification of (ii).

To see (iii), assume that
T
is circular. In view of Proposition
2.2
(iii), we have

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}^{\bot}=\bigcap_{w\in bpe(T)}\ker E_{w}=\bigcap_{w\in bpe(T)}\textup{cl\,ran}(T-w).

Let
\theta\in\mathbb{R}
. Since
T
is circular, there exists a unitary
U_{\theta}
on
\mathcal{H}
such that
U_{\theta}T=e^{i\theta}TU_{\theta}
. Let
x\in\mbox{ran}\,(T-w)
. Then
x=(T-w)y
for some
y\in\mathcal{H}
. This gives that

\displaystyle U_{\theta}x=U_{\theta}(T-w)y=(e^{i\theta}T-w)U_{\theta}y.

Thus
U_{\theta}\mbox{ran}\,(T-w)\subseteq\mbox{ran}\,(e^{i\theta}T-w)
. Consequently,
U_{\theta}\textup{cl\,ran}(T-w)\subseteq\textup{cl\,ran}(e^{i\theta}T-w)
, which implies that

\displaystyle U_{\theta}\bigcap_{w\in bpe(T)}\textup{cl\,ran}(T-w)\subseteq\bigcap_{w\in bpe(T)}\textup{cl\,ran}(e^{i\theta}T-w).

(2.5)

Since
bpe(T)
has circular symmetry (by Proposition
2.3
), we have
e^{i\theta}bpe(T)=bpe(T)
. This gives that

\displaystyle\bigcap_{w\in bpe(T)}\textup{cl\,ran}(e^{i\theta}T-w)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\bigcap_{u\in bpe(T)}\textup{cl\,ran}(e^{i\theta}T-e^{i\theta}u)

(2.6)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\bigcap_{u\in bpe(T)}\textup{cl\,ran}(T-u)=\mathcal{M}^{\bot}.

The identities (
2.5
) and (
2.6
) together show that
U_{\theta}\mathcal{M}^{\bot}\subseteq\mathcal{M}^{\bot}
. Similarly, using
U^{*}_{\theta}T=e^{-i\theta}TU^{*}_{\theta}
, we get that
U^{*}_{\theta}\mathcal{M}^{\bot}\subseteq\mathcal{M}^{\bot}
. Thus
U_{\theta}|_{\mathcal{M}^{\bot}}
is a unitary on
\mathcal{M}^{\bot}
, and for all
x\in\mathcal{M}^{\bot}
, we have
U_{\theta}Tx=e^{i\theta}TU_{\theta}x
. Hence,
T|_{\mathcal{M}^{\bot}}
is circular. This establishes (iii). The conclusion in (iv) follows easily from the following observation:

\displaystyle\bigcap_{n\in\mathbb{N}}T^{n}(\mathcal{M}^{\bot})\subseteq\bigcap_{n\in\mathbb{N}}T^{n}(\mathcal{H})=\{0\}.

For the proof of (v), let
x\in\ker T^{*}
. Then we get

\displaystyle 0=T^{*}x=T^{*}P_{\mathcal{M}}x+T^{*}P_{\mathcal{M}^{\bot}}x,

which, in turn, implies that
0=P_{\mathcal{M}^{\bot}}T^{*}x=P_{\mathcal{M}^{\bot}}T^{*}P_{\mathcal{M}^{\bot}}x
. Thus
P_{\mathcal{M}^{\bot}}x\in\ker(T|_{\mathcal{M}^{\bot}})^{*}
. This completes the proof.
∎

The following corollary is an immediate fallout of the preceding proposition and the fact that the spectrum of an analytic operator is connected
[
5
, Lemma 5.2]
.

Corollary 2.8
.

Let
T
be an analytic and circular operator on a complex separable Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}
with the wandering subspace property. Let
\mathcal{M}:=\bigvee_{w\in bpe(T)}E^{*}_{w}(\ker T^{*})
. Then the spectrum of
T|_{\mathcal{M}^{\bot}}
is a closed disc centred at the origin with radius being equal to the spectral radius of
T|_{\mathcal{M}^{\bot}}
.

As mentioned earlier, the investigation of the equality
\bigvee_{w\in\sigma(T)}\ker(T^{*}-\overline{w})=\mathcal{H}
is a pivotal part in the study of the function theoretic behaviour of
T
. Clancey and Rogers proved that the aforementioned equality holds for a completely non-normal hyponormal operator whose approximate point spectrum has area measure zero
[
6
, Theorem 2]
. We give below another sufficient condition which ensures that the said equality holds for a left-invertible operator with the wandering subspace property. This condition was also derived in
[
15
, Proposition 2.7]
but here we provide a different proof. Before proceeding towards the result, we need the following prerequisites from the local spectral theory.

Let
X
be a Banach space and
T
be a bounded linear operator on
X
. For
x\in X
, the local resolvent of
T
at
x
, denoted by
\rho_{T}(x)
, is the union of all open subsets
G
of
\mathbb{C}
for which there is an analytic function
f:G\longrightarrow X
satisfying
(\lambda-T)f(\lambda)=x
for all
\lambda\in G
. The complement of
\rho_{T}(x)
is called the local spectrum of
T
at
x
and is denoted by
\sigma_{T}(x)
. The following theorem will be needed in the subsequent proposition.

Theorem 2.9
.

[
11
, Theorem 1.6.3]

Let
T
be a bounded linear operator on a complex Banach space
X
. If
T
is analytic, then
0\in\sigma_{T}(x)
for all non-zero
x\in X
.

Proposition 2.10
.

Let
T
be a left-invertible operator on a complex separable Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}
and let
T^{\prime}
denote the Cauchy dual of
T
. Suppose that
T
has the wandering subspace property. If
T
is analytic, then
\bigvee_{w\in\mathbb{D}(0,r(T^{\prime})^{-1})}E^{*}_{w}(\ker T^{*})=\mathcal{H}
.

Proof.

Suppose that
T
is analytic. Note that
(T^{*}T)^{-1}=T^{\prime*}T^{\prime}\leqslant\|T^{\prime}\|^{2}I
. This gives that
T-w
is left-invertible for all
w\in\mathbb{D}(0,\|T^{\prime}\|^{-1})
. In view of Corollary
2.6
, set

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}:=\bigvee_{w\in\mathbb{D}(0,\|T^{\prime}\|^{-1})}E^{*}_{w}(\ker T^{*}).

Let
x\in\mathcal{M}^{\bot}
. Then
x\in\mbox{ran}\,(T-w)
for all
w\in\mathbb{D}(0,\|T^{\prime}\|^{-1})
, and hence,
x=(T-w)x(w)
for all
w\in\mathbb{D}(0,\|T^{\prime}\|^{-1})
. Consequently, we get

\displaystyle T^{\prime*}x=T^{\prime*}(T-w)x(w)=(I-wT^{\prime*})x(w)\mbox{ for all }w\in\mathbb{D}(0,\|T^{\prime}\|^{-1}).

Since
(I-wT^{\prime*})
is invertible for all
w\in\mathbb{D}(0,\|T^{\prime}\|^{-1})
, we have

\displaystyle x(w)=(I-wT^{\prime*})^{-1}T^{\prime*}x=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{N}}w^{n}T^{\prime*n+1}x\mbox{ for all }w\in\mathbb{D}(0,\|T^{\prime}\|^{-1}).

This shows that the map
w\mapsto x(w)
is analytic on
\mathbb{D}(0,\|T^{\prime}\|^{-1})
which satisfies that
(T-w)x(w)=x
for all
w\in\mathbb{D}(0,\|T^{\prime}\|^{-1})
. Thus
\mathbb{D}(0,\|T^{\prime}\|^{-1})\subseteq\rho_{T}(x)
. On the other hand, by Theorem
2.9
, we have that
0\in\sigma_{T}(x)
for all non-zero
x
. Hence, we must have
x=0
. Thus, we get

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}=\mathcal{M}\subseteq\bigvee_{w\in\mathbb{D}(0,r(T^{\prime})^{-1})}E^{*}_{w}(\ker T^{*}).

This completes the proof.
∎

3.
Analytic bounded point evaluation

Let
T
be a bounded linear operator on a complex separable Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}
with the wandering subspace property. If
bpe(T)
has non-empty interior, then define the map
U:\{p(T):p\in\mathcal{P}(bpe(T),\ker T^{*})\}\rightarrow\mathcal{P}(bpe(T),\ker T^{*})
by

\displaystyle U(p(T))=p,\quad p\in\mathcal{P}(bpe(T),\ker T^{*}),

(3.1)

where
\mathcal{P}(bpe(T),\ker T^{*})
is the vector space of all
\ker T^{*}
-valued polynomials defined on
bpe(T)
. It is easy to see that
U
is a well-defined bijective linear map. Thus, we can define the inner product on
\mathcal{P}(bpe(T),\ker T^{*})
by

\displaystyle\langle{p},\,{q}\rangle:=\langle{p(T)},\,{q(T)}\rangle_{\mathcal{H}},\quad p,q\in\mathcal{P}(bpe(T),\ker T^{*}).

(3.2)

Let
\mathscr{H}
be the completion of
\mathcal{P}(bpe(T),\ker T^{*})
with respect to the inner product given by (
3.2
). Since
\{p(T):p\in\mathcal{P}(bpe(T),\ker T^{*})\}
is a dense subspace of
\mathcal{H}
and
U
is unitary on this subspace,
U
extends to a unitary from
\mathcal{H}
onto
\mathscr{H}
. We denote this extension of
U
by
U
itself. We say that
w\in\mathbb{C}
is a
bounded point evaluation on

\mathscr{H}
if there exists a positive constant
c
such that
\|p(w)\|_{\mathcal{H}}\leqslant c\|p\|_{\mathscr{H}}
for all
p\in\mathcal{P}(bpe(T),\ker T^{*})
. If
w
is a bounded point evaluation on
\mathscr{H}
, then the evaluation map
\mathscr{E}_{w}:\mathscr{H}\rightarrow\ker T^{*}
is continuous. In the following proposition, besides recording the properties of
U
, we also show that the notion of bounded point evaluation defined for
T
agrees with the usual notion of bounded point evaluation on
\mathscr{H}
.

Remark 3.1
.

The Corollary
2.6
shows that the interior of
bpe(T)
is non-empty for every operator
T
in the class of left-invertible operators with the wandering subspace property.

Proposition 3.2
.

Let
T
be a bounded linear operator on a complex separable Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}
with the wandering subspace property. Suppose that
bpe(T)
has non-empty interior. Let
\mathscr{H}
be the Hilbert space as described above and
U:\mathcal{H}\rightarrow\mathscr{H}
be the unitary given by (
3.1
). Then the following statements hold:

(i)

U
maps
\ker T^{*}
onto the subspace of constant polynomials in
\mathscr{H}
and
U|_{\ker T^{*}}
is the identity map.

(ii)

The set of all bounded point evaluations on
\mathscr{H}
is equal to
bpe(T)
.

(iii)

For
w\in bpe(T)
, if
E_{w}
and
\mathscr{E}_{w}
are respective evaluation maps on
\mathcal{H}
and
\mathscr{H}
, then
UE_{w}=\mathscr{E}_{w}U
.

Proof.

The proofs of (i) and (ii) are immediate from the definition of
U
and that of bounded point evaluation on
\mathscr{H}
. To see (iii), let
w\in bpe(T)
and
x\in\mathcal{H}
. Then there exists a sequence of polynomials
\{p_{n}\}_{n\geqslant 1}
in
\mathcal{P}(bpe(T),\ker T^{*})
such that
p_{n}(T)\rightarrow x
as
n\rightarrow\infty
. This, together with (i), gives that

\displaystyle p_{n}(w)=Up_{n}(w)\rightarrow UE_{w}x\mbox{ as }n\rightarrow\infty.

Further, we also have

\displaystyle p_{n}(w)=\mathscr{E}_{w}p_{n}=\mathscr{E}_{w}Up_{n}(T)\rightarrow\mathscr{E}_{w}Ux\mbox{ as }n\rightarrow\infty.

Thus we get that
UE_{w}x=\mathscr{E}_{w}Ux
. This completes the proof.
∎

Theorem 3.3
.

Let
T
be a bounded linear operator on a complex separable Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}
with the wandering subspace property. Suppose that
bpe(T)
has non-empty interior. Let
\mathscr{H}
be the Hilbert space as described after (
3.2
). Then the following statements are true:

(i)

The operator
\mathscr{M}_{z}
of multiplication by the coordinate function defined on the polynomials in
\mathscr{H}
extends continuously on
\mathscr{H}
. If the continuous extension of
\mathscr{M}_{z}
is denoted by
\mathscr{M}_{z}
itself, then
\mathscr{M}_{z}U=UT
, where
U:\mathcal{H}\rightarrow\mathscr{H}
is the unitary operator as defined by (
3.1
).

(ii)

The elements of
\mathscr{H}
are well-defined
\ker T^{*}
-valued functions on
bpe(T)
if and only if
\bigvee_{w\in bpe(T)}E^{*}_{w}(\ker T^{*})=\mathcal{H}
.

Proof.

The proof of (i) follows by imitating the arguments of the proof of
[
17
, Theorem 3.1]
. For the proof of (ii), assume that
\bigvee_{w\in bpe(T)}E^{*}_{w}(\ker T^{*})=\mathcal{H}

and

for all
w\in bpe(T)
. Then there exists a vector
x\in\mathcal{H}
such that

. Using Proposition

\displaystyle 0=f(w)=\mathscr{E}_{w}Ux=UE_{w}x=E_{w}x\mbox{ for all }w\in bpe(T).

Thus we get

\displaystyle x\in\bigcap_{w\in bpe(T)}\ker E_{w}=\Big(\bigvee_{w\in bpe(T)}E^{*}_{w}(\ker T^{*})\Big)^{\bot}=\mathcal{H}^{\bot}=\{0\}.

This gives that

. The proof of the converse is obvious.
∎

Let
\mathscr{H}

Theorem 3.5
.

n\in\mathbb{N}
, set

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}_{n}:=\bigvee_{k=0}^{n}T^{k}(\ker T^{*})\quad\mbox{and}\quad S_{n}(w):=\sum_{k=0}^{n}\overline{w}^{k}T^{\prime k}.

Then
u\in abpe(T)
if and only if there exists an open set
\mathcal{O}\subseteq bpe(T)
containing
u
such that the map
w\mapsto\displaystyle\sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\|P_{\mathcal{M}_{n}}S_{n}(w)|_{kerT^{*}}\|
of
\mathcal{O}
into
[0,\infty)
is bounded on compact subsets of
\mathcal{O}
.

The proof of the following corollary is immediate from Corollary
2.6
and the preceding theorem.

Corollary 3.6
.

Let
T
be a left-invertible operator on a complex separable Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}
with the wandering subspace property. Let
T^{\prime}
denote the Cauchy dual of
T
. Then the open disc
\mathbb{D}(0,r)
is contained in the
abpe(T)
, where
\displaystyle r:=\inf_{x\in\ker T^{*}}\frac{1}{r_{T^{\prime}}(x)}
. In particular,
\mathbb{D}\big(0,r(T^{\prime})^{-1}\big)\subseteq abpe(T)
. Moreover, for each
w\in\mathbb{D}(0,r)
, we have
\mathscr{E}_{w}^{*}x=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\overline{w}^{n}T^{\prime n}x
for all
x\in\ker T^{*}
.

It can be easily seen from Theorem
3.3
, Proposition
2.10
and the preceding corollary that if
T
is a left-invertible analytic operator with the wandering subspace property, then
T
is unitarily equivalent to
\mathscr{M}_{z}
on the Hilbert space of
\ker T^{*}
-valued holomorphic functions defined on
\mathbb{D}\big(0,r(T^{\prime})^{-1}\big)
with the reproducing kernel given by

\displaystyle\kappa(z,w)=\mathscr{E}_{z}\mathscr{E}_{w}^{*}=(I-zT^{\prime*})^{-1}(I-\overline{w}T^{\prime})^{-1}\mbox{ for all }z,w\in\mathbb{D}\big(0,r(T^{\prime})^{-1}\big).

This is precisely Shimorin’s model
[
15
]
for a left-invertible analytic operator with the wandering subspace property. We conclude this paper with a couple of examples of left-invertible operators
T
with the wandering subspace property for which
\mathbb{D}\big(0,r(T^{\prime})^{-1}\big)\subsetneqq bpe(T)
.

The following example is motivated from
[
5
, Example 2]
and
[
1
, Example 3.1]
.

Example 3.7
.

Consider the operator
T
on
\ell^{2}(\mathbb{N})
defined as
Te_{0}=\lambda_{0}e_{0}+\lambda_{1}e_{1}
and
Te_{n}=\lambda_{n+1}e_{n+1}
for all
n\geqslant 1
, where
\{e_{n}:n\in\mathbb{N}\}
is the standard orthonormal basis of
\ell^{2}(\mathbb{N})
and the weights
\lambda_{n}
are described as follows:

\lambda_{0}=\lambda_{1}=\lambda_{2}=\lambda_{3}=\lambda_{4}=1,\ \mbox{and}\ \lambda_{k}=\begin{cases}\frac{1}{2},&\mbox{if}\ 2^{n}+1\leq k\leq 3.2^{n-1},\ n\geq 2,\\
1,&\mbox{otherwise.}\end{cases}

The operator
T
is nothing but a weighted composition operator on
\ell^{2}(\mathbb{N})
with the symbol
\phi:\mathbb{N}\rightarrow\mathbb{N}
defined as
\phi(0)=0
and
\phi(n)=n-1
for all
n\geqslant 1
. It can also be realized as a weighted shift on the following directed graph:

0
1
2
\ldots

Note that
\inf_{n\geq 1}\lambda_{n}=\frac{1}{2}
. Thus
T
is left-invertible and the action of the Cauchy dual
T^{\prime}
of
T
is same as that of
T
with weights
\{\lambda^{\prime}_{n}:n\in\mathbb{N}\}
described as follows:

\displaystyle\lambda^{\prime}_{0}=\lambda^{\prime}_{1}=\frac{1}{2}\quad\mbox{and}\quad\lambda^{\prime}_{n}=\frac{1}{\lambda_{n}}\mbox{ for all }n\geqslant 2.

Note that the series
\sum_{m\in\mathbb{N}}\frac{\lambda^{\prime}_{1}\cdots\lambda^{\prime}_{m+1}}{(\lambda^{\prime}_{0})^{m+1}}
diverges. Therefore, by
[
1
, Theorem 2.1]
,
T^{\prime}
is analytic, and hence, by
[
15
, Proposition 2.7]
,
T
has the wandering subspace property. Further,
\ker T^{*}=\mbox{span}\{e_{0}-e_{1}\}
. Also, observe that for any
n\geqslant 1
, the total number of
2
’s occurring in first
2^{n}
places in
\{\lambda^{\prime}_{n}:n\geqslant 1\}
is equal to
2^{n-1}-2^{n-2}+2^{n-2}-2^{n-3}+\cdots+4-2=2^{n-1}-2
. Therefore, we get

\lambda^{\prime}_{1}\lambda^{\prime}_{2}\cdots\lambda^{\prime}_{2^{n}}=\frac{2^{2^{n-1}-2}}{2}=\frac{2^{2^{n-1}}}{8}.

Let
n
be any positive integer. Then there are unique non-negative integers
m_{n}
and
k_{n}
such that
n=2^{m_{n}}+k_{n}
with
0\leqslant k_{n}<2^{m_{n}}
. Thus, we have

\displaystyle\lambda^{\prime}_{1}\lambda^{\prime}_{2}\cdots\lambda^{\prime}_{n}=\frac{2^{2^{m_{n}-1}-2+\alpha_{k_{n}}}}{2}=\frac{2^{2^{m_{n}-1}+\alpha_{k_{n}}}}{8},

(3.3)

where

\displaystyle\alpha_{k_{n}}=\begin{cases}k_{n}&\mbox{ if }0\leqslant k_{n}\leqslant 2^{m_{n}-1},\\
2^{m_{n}-1}&\mbox{ if }2^{m_{n}-1}<k_{n}<2^{m_{n}}.\end{cases}

Also, observe that if
0\leqslant k_{n}<2^{m_{n}-1}
, then

\displaystyle\frac{2^{m_{n}-1}+\alpha_{k_{n}}}{2^{m_{n}}+k_{n}}=\frac{1}{2}\frac{2^{m_{n}}+2k_{n}}{2^{m_{n}}+k_{n}}=\frac{1}{2}+\frac{1}{2}\frac{1}{\frac{2^{m_{n}}}{k_{n}}+1}<\frac{1}{2}+\frac{1}{2}\frac{1}{3}=\frac{2}{3}.

Further, for
2^{m_{n}-1}\leqslant k_{n}<2^{m_{n}}
, we have

\displaystyle\frac{2^{m_{n}-1}+\alpha_{k_{n}}}{2^{m_{n}}+k_{n}}=\frac{2^{m_{n}}}{2^{m_{n}}+k_{n}}\leqslant\frac{2^{m_{n}}}{3\cdot 2^{m_{n}-1}}=\frac{2}{3}.

Consequently, we get

\displaystyle\frac{2^{m_{n}-1}+\alpha_{k_{n}}}{2^{m_{n}}+k_{n}}\leqslant\frac{2}{3}\mbox{ for all }n\geqslant 1.

(3.4)

Let
x=e_{0}-e_{1}
. Then for each
n\geqslant 1
, we get

\displaystyle\|T^{\prime n}x\|^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\|T^{\prime n}e_{0}\|^{2}+\|T^{\prime n}e_{1}\|^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle|\lambda^{\prime}_{0}|^{2n}+|\lambda^{\prime}_{0}|^{2(n-1)}|\lambda^{\prime}_{1}|^{2}+\cdots+|\lambda^{\prime}_{1}\lambda^{\prime}_{2}\cdots\lambda^{\prime}_{n}|^{2}+|\lambda^{\prime}_{2}\lambda^{\prime}_{3}\cdots\lambda^{\prime}_{n+1}|^{2}

\displaystyle\overset{\eqref{prod-eq-ex}}{\leqslant}

\displaystyle(n+1)\Big(\frac{2^{2^{m_{n}-1}+\alpha_{k_{n}}}}{8}\Big)^{2}+16\Big(\frac{2^{2^{m_{n}-1}+\alpha_{k_{n}}}}{8}\Big)^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(n+17)\Big(\frac{2^{2^{m_{n}-1}+\alpha_{k_{n}}}}{8}\Big)^{2}.

This together with (
3.4
) implies that
\limsup_{n\rightarrow\infty}\|T^{\prime n}x\|^{\frac{1}{n}}\leqslant 2^{\frac{2}{3}}
. On the other hand, it is not difficult to see that
r(T^{\prime})=2
. Thus, in view of Corollary
3.6
, we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{D}\big(0,r(T^{\prime})^{-1}\big)\subsetneqq\mathbb{D}(0,2^{-\frac{2}{3}})\subseteq abpe(T)\subseteq bpe(T).

The foregoing example suggests a way to construct a class of examples for which
\mathbb{D}\big(0,r(T^{\prime})^{-1}\big)\subsetneqq abpe(T)\subseteq bpe(T)
. For this, we refer the reader to
[
10
]
for details about the weighted shift on directed trees.

Example 3.8
.

Consider the sequence
\{\lambda_{n}\}_{n\geqslant 1}
of positive numbers with the following properties:

(i)

\lambda_{n}\leqslant 1
;

(ii)

\displaystyle\inf_{n\geqslant 1}\lambda_{n}>0
;

(iii)

\displaystyle\limsup_{n\rightarrow\infty}(\lambda_{1}\cdots\lambda_{n})^{-\frac{1}{n}}<\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\big(\sup_{m\in\mathbb{N}}(\lambda_{m+1}\cdots\lambda_{m+n})^{-1}\big)^{\frac{1}{n}}
.

One such example of a sequence satisfying above three conditions has been exhibited in the previous example. Let
k
be any positive integer. Then consider the rooted directed tree
\mathscr{T}_{k,0}=(V,\mathcal{E})
described as follows (the reader is referred to
[
10
, Chapter 6]
for more details about such trees):

\displaystyle V

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\{(0,0),(m,n):m,n\geqslant 1\};

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\{\big((0,0),(1,j)\big):1\leqslant j\leqslant k\}\cup\{\big((m,n),(m+1,n)\big):m,n\geqslant 1\}.

An example of
\mathscr{T}_{3,0}
is depicted in the following figure.

(0,0)
(1,1)
(1,2)
(1,3)
(2,1)
(2,2)
(2,3)
\ldots
\ldots
\ldots

The weights on the vertices of the tree
\mathscr{T}_{k,0}
are described as follows:

\displaystyle\lambda_{(n,1)}=\lambda_{n},\ n\geqslant 1\quad\mbox{and}\quad\lambda_{(m,n)}=1\mbox{ for all }m\geqslant 1\mbox{ and }n=2,\ldots,k.

Consider the Hilbert space
\ell^{2}(V)
. Let
T:\ell^{2}(V)\rightarrow\ell^{2}(V)
be the weighted shift whose action on the basis vectors is defined as follows:

\displaystyle Te_{(0,0)}=\sum_{n=1}^{k}\lambda_{(1,n)}e_{(1,n)}\quad\mbox{and}\quad Te_{(m,n)}=\lambda_{(m+1,n)}e_{(m+1,n)}\mbox{ for all }m,n\geqslant 1.

Then
T
is left-invertible. That
T
has the wandering subspace property follows from
[
4
, Proposition 1.3.4]
. The Cauchy dual
T^{\prime}
of
T
is again a weighted shift on
\ell^{2}(V)
with weights
\{\lambda^{\prime}_{(m,n)}:m,n\geqslant 1\}
given by

\displaystyle\lambda^{\prime}_{(1,n)}=\frac{\lambda_{(1,n)}}{\|Te_{(0,0)}\|^{2}},\ 1\leqslant n\leqslant k,\mbox{ and }\lambda^{\prime}_{(m,n)}=\frac{1}{\lambda_{(m,n)}},\ m\geqslant 2,\ n=1,\ldots,k.

Further,

\displaystyle\ker T^{*}=\mbox{span}\{e_{(0,0)}\}\oplus\big(\ell^{2}(\{(1,n):1\ \leqslant n\leqslant k\})\ominus\mbox{span}\{Te_{(0,0)}\}\big).

Routine calculations show that for each
x=\alpha e_{(0,0)}+y
in
\ker T^{*}
and for each
n\geqslant 1
,

\displaystyle\|T^{\prime n}x\|^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle|\alpha|^{2}\|T^{\prime n}e_{(0,0)}\|^{2}+\|T^{\prime n}y\|^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle|\alpha|^{2}\big((\lambda^{\prime}_{(1,1)})^{2}(\lambda_{2}\ldots\lambda_{n})^{-2}+k-1\big)+a(\lambda_{2}\ldots\lambda_{n})^{-2}+b

for some non-negative scalars
a,b
. Consequently,
\|T^{\prime n}x\|^{2}=c(\lambda_{1}\ldots\lambda_{n})^{-2}+d\ \mbox{ for some non-negative scalars }c,d
. This gives that for all
x\in\ker T^{*}
,

\displaystyle r_{T^{\prime}}(x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\limsup_{n\rightarrow\infty}\|T^{\prime n}x\|^{\frac{1}{n}}\leqslant\limsup_{n\rightarrow\infty}(\lambda_{1}\cdots\lambda_{n})^{-\frac{1}{n}}

\displaystyle<

\displaystyle\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\big(\sup_{m\in\mathbb{N}}(\lambda_{m+1}\cdots\lambda_{m+n})^{-1}\big)^{\frac{1}{n}}\leqslant r(T^{\prime}).

Let
\displaystyle r:=\inf_{x\in\ker T^{*}}\frac{1}{r_{T^{\prime}}(x)}
. Then by Corollary
3.6
, we get

\displaystyle\mathbb{D}\big(0,r(T^{\prime})^{-1}\big)\subsetneqq\mathbb{D}(0,r)\subseteq abpe(T)\subseteq bpe(T).

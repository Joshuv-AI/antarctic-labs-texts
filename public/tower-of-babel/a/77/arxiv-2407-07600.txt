Pointwise and Oscillation Estimates via Riesz Potentials for Mixed Local and Nonlocal Parabolic Equations
Lingwei Ma, Qi Xiong, Zhenqiu Zhang

Abstract
We establish a class of pointwise estimates for weak solutions to mixed local and nonlocal parabolic equations involving measure data and merely measurable coefficients via caloric Riesz potentials. Such estimates effectively bound the sizes and oscillations of weak solutions, respectively. The proof relies on demonstrating a new local Hölder estimate with an optimal L q L^{q} -Tail for weak solutions to the corresponding homogeneous problem, which remarkably extends the L ∞ L^{\infty} -Tail in previous work. It is worth mentioning that our main results capture both local and nonlocal features of the double phase parabolic equations and, more importantly, remain valid for SOLA (Solutions Obtained by Limit of Approximations). Mathematics Subject classification (2020): 35K55; 35R09; 35R05; 31C45; 35B65. Keywords: Mixed local-nonlocal parabolic equations; Measure data; Riesz potential; Pointwise estimates; Oscillation estimates.

Introduction

In this paper, we consider the following measure data parabolic problem, which involves a mixed diffusion characterized by the coexistence of local and nonlocal operators

\partial_{t}u-\operatorname{div}\left(\mathcal{A}(t,x,Du)\right)+\mathcal{L}u=\mu~~\text{in}~~\Omega_{T}.

(1.1)

Here
\Omega_{T}=I\times\Omega
denotes the space-time cylinder formed by
the time interval
I=(0,T)
and the bounded domain
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
with
d\geq 2
, and
\mu\in\mathcal{M}(\mathbb{R}^{d+1})
is a general Radon measures with finite total mass on

\mathbb{R}^{d+1}
.
The vector field
\mathcal{A}(t,x,\xi):\Omega_{T}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{d}
is a Carathéodory function satisfying the following growth and ellipticity conditions

\left|\mathcal{A}(t,x,\xi)\right|\leq\Lambda|\xi|,

(1.2)

\left(\mathcal{A}(t,x,\eta)-\mathcal{A}(t,x,\xi)\right)\cdot(\eta-\xi)\geq\Lambda^{-1}|\eta-\xi|^{2}

(1.3)

for almost all
(t,x)\in\Omega_{T}
and every
\xi,\,\eta\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
with the structure constant
\Lambda\geq 1
. The nonlocal operator
\mathcal{L}
is defined by

\mathcal{L}u(t,x)=\text{P.V.}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}(u(t,x)-u(t,y)){K}(t,x,y)\operatorname{d}\!y,

(1.4)

where the kernel
{K}:\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\to[0,\infty)
is assumed to be measurable, and fulfilling the following ellipticity condition

\Lambda^{-1}|x-y|^{-d-2s}\leq{K}(t,x,y)\leq\Lambda|x-y|^{-d-2s}

(1.5)

for a.e.
(t,x,y)\in I\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}
with the fractional order
s\in(0,1)
.
The prototype of such mixed operators is
-\Delta+(-\Delta)^{s}
, which arises naturally from the stochastic processes that encompass both classical random walk and Lévy flight. This type of operator can be used
to model many important physical phenomena, such as heat transport in magnetized plasmas
[
4
]
, the spread of pandemics
[
17
]
, and population dynamics
[
13
]
. Consequently, significant attention has been devoted to exploring the qualitative properties and regularity theory of solutions to mixed local-nonlocal elliptic and parabolic equations, please refer to
[

A powerful approach to studying the regularity of solutions to the linear Poisson equation
-\Delta u=\mu
is based on an explicit representation formula via the fundamental solution. As an immediate consequence, the solution and its gradient can be controlled pointwise by Riesz potentials. Due to the behaviour of Riesz potentials in various relevant function spaces is known, such potential estimates provide a unified framework for exploring the regularity properties of solutions at every function space scale.
A natural question is whether these pointwise potential estimates are still available for general partial differential equations that lack representation formulas for solutions.

In the context of elliptic equations, the first affirmative answer can be traced back to the pioneering work of Kilpeläinen and Malý
[
23
,
24

p

via the nonlinear Wolff potential of the nonhomogeneous term. Afterwards Trudinger and Wang
[

offered an alternative approach. Nearly twenty years later, Mingione and his groups
[

,

,

upgraded the previous pointwise estimates to gradient as well as oscillation levels, provided the coefficients are appropriately regular.
Subsequently, these types of pointwise potential estimates have been extended to various kinds of elliptic equations and systems, we refer to

\partial_{t}u-\operatorname{div}\left(\mathcal{A}(t,x,Du)\right)=\mu~~\text{in}~~\Omega_{T}

|u(t_{0},x_{0})|\leq C\fint_{Q_{R}(t_{0},x_{0})}|u|\operatorname{d}\!x\operatorname{d}\!t+C\mathcal{I}^{\mu}_{2}(t_{0},x_{0};2R),

In the nonlocal parabolic setting,

\partial_{t}u+\mathcal{L}u=\mu~~\text{in}~~\Omega_{T},

possesses a symmetric kernel satisfying (

They demonstrated the following pointwise bound for the solution in terms of a parabolic Riesz potential of order

\displaystyle|u(t_{0},x_{0})|\leq C\left[\left(\fint_{\tilde{Q}_{R}(t_{0},x_{0})}|u|^{q}\operatorname{d}\!x\operatorname{d}\!t\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}+\left(\fint_{{\tilde{I}^{\ominus}_{R}(t_{0})}}\operatorname{\widetilde{Tail}}(u(t);x_{0},R)^{q}\operatorname{d}\!t\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}+\tilde{\mathcal{I}}^{\mu}_{2s}(t_{0},x_{0};2R)\right]

\displaystyle\tilde{\mathcal{I}}^{\mu}_{2s}(t_{0},x_{0};2R)=\int_{0}^{2R}\frac{|\mu|(\tilde{Q}_{\rho}(t_{0},x_{0}))}{\rho^{d}}\frac{\operatorname{d}\!\rho}{\rho}.

To the best of our knowledge, there remains a notable absence of potential theory for solutions of parabolic equations involving local and nonlocal diffusion operators. Our primary focus is on establishing both pointwise and oscillatory potential estimates for solutions of the double phase parabolic equation (
1.1
) in terms of caloric Riesz potentials of measure data
\mu

To illustrate the main results of this paper, we start by presenting the definition of local weak solutions to the mixed local and nonlocal parabolic equation (
1.1
).

Definition 1.1
.

Let
\mu\in\mathcal{M}(\mathbb{R}^{d+1})
. A function

u\in L_{\rm loc}^{2}(I;W^{1,2}_{\rm loc}(\Omega))\cap L_{\rm loc}^{\infty}(I;L^{2}_{\rm loc}(\Omega))\cap L^{2}_{\rm loc}(I;{\mathcal{L}}^{1}_{2s}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))

is a local weak subsolution to (
1.1
), if

\displaystyle-\int_{t_{1}}^{t_{2}}\int_{\Omega}u\partial_{t}\varphi\operatorname{d}\!x\operatorname{d}\!t+\int_{\Omega}u(t_{2},x)\varphi(t_{2},x)\operatorname{d}\!x-\int_{\Omega}u(t_{1},x)\varphi(t_{1},x)\operatorname{d}\!x

\displaystyle+\int_{t_{1}}^{t_{2}}\int_{\Omega}\mathcal{A}(t,x,Du)\cdot D\varphi\operatorname{d}\!x\operatorname{d}\!t+\int_{t_{1}}^{t_{2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}(u(t,x)-u(t,y))(\varphi(t,x)-\varphi(t,y))K(t,x,y)\operatorname{d}\!x\operatorname{d}\!y\operatorname{d}\!t

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\int_{t_{1}}^{t_{2}}\int_{\Omega}\varphi\operatorname{d}\!\mu.

holds for all
[t_{1},t_{2}]\subset I
and nonnegative test functions

\varphi\in W_{\rm loc}^{1,2}(I;L^{2}(\Omega))\cap L_{\rm loc}^{2}(I;W^{1,2}(\Omega))

with compact spatial support contained in
\Omega
, where
{\mathcal{L}}^{1}_{2s}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
represents the nonlocal tail space defined as

{\mathcal{L}}^{1}_{2s}(\mathbb{R}^{d}):=\left\{v\in L^{1}_{\operatorname{loc}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\mathrel{\Big|}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\frac{|v(y)|}{1+|y|^{d+2s}}\operatorname{d}\!y<\infty\right\}.

A function
u
is called a local weak supersolution to (
1.1
) if the previous formula holds for every nonpositive test function
\varphi
. If a function
u
is both a local weak subsolution and a local weak supersolution, then it is referred to as a local weak solution to (
1.1
).

Here we do not need to impose the additional symmetry assumption on the kernel
K
that

{K}(t,x,y)={K}(t,y,x)\,\,\mbox{for a.e.}\,\,(t,x,y)\in I\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d},

(1.6)

as in
[
39
]
. Indeed, without loss of generality, we may assume that the kernel
K
is symmetric. Otherwise,
let us rename variables to show that

\displaystyle\int_{t_{1}}^{t_{2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}(u(t,x)-u(t,y))(\varphi(t,x)-\varphi(t,y))K(t,x,y)\operatorname{d}\!x\operatorname{d}\!y\operatorname{d}\!t

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{t_{1}}^{t_{2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}(u(t,x)-u(t,y))(\varphi(t,x)-\varphi(t,y))K(t,y,x)\operatorname{d}\!y\operatorname{d}\!x\operatorname{d}\!t

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{t_{1}}^{t_{2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}(u(t,x)-u(t,y))(\varphi(t,x)-\varphi(t,y))\frac{K(t,x,y)+K(t,y,x)}{2}\operatorname{d}\!x\operatorname{d}\!y\operatorname{d}\!t.

It is obvious that the new kernel
\bar{K}(t,x,y):=\frac{K(t,x,y)+K(t,y,x)}{2}
is symmetric in the sense of (
1.6
) and satisfies the ellipticity condition

\Lambda^{-1}|x-y|^{-d-2s}\leq{\bar{K}}(t,x,y)\leq\Lambda|x-y|^{-d-2s}
for a.e.
(t,x,y)\in I\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}

by (
1.5
). Therefore, we can replace the original kernel
K
with a symmetric kernel
\bar{K}
.

In what follows, we denote the parabolic cylinder
Q_{r}(t_{0},x_{0}):=I_{r}^{\ominus}(t_{0})\times B_{r}(x_{0})
, where the backward time interval
I_{r}^{\ominus}(t_{0}):=(t_{0}-r^{2},t_{0})
.
We proceed by introducing the definition of the caloric Riesz potential of measure
\mu
, which will be utilized in our potential estimates.

Definition 1.2
.

Let
\alpha\in(0,d+2)
, the parabolic Riesz potential
\mathcal{I}^{\mu}_{\alpha}(t_{0},x_{0};R)
of
\mu\in\mathcal{M}(\mathbb{R}^{d+1})
with order
\alpha
is defined by

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}^{\mu}_{\alpha}(t_{0},x_{0};R)=\int_{0}^{R}\frac{|\mu|(Q_{\rho}(t_{0},x_{0}))}{\rho^{d+2-\alpha}}\frac{\operatorname{d}\!\rho}{\rho}

for any
(t_{0},x_{0})\in I\times\Omega
such that the parabolic cylinder
Q_{R}(t_{0},x_{0})\subset I\times\Omega
.

We are now in a position to state the first result of this paper, specifically, a pointwise potential estimate of solutions to the mixed local and nonlocal parabolic equation (
1.1
).

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
u
be a local weak solution to (
1.1
) with
\mu\in\mathcal{M}(\mathbb{R}^{d+1})
in
\Omega_{T}
,
then for any
q\in(1,2]
, any
R\in(0,1]
and almost all
(t_{0},x_{0})\in I\times\Omega
with
Q_{2R}(t_{0},x_{0})\Subset I\times\Omega
, there exists a
positive constant
C
depending only on
d,\,s,\,\Lambda,\,q

such that

\displaystyle|u(t_{0},x_{0})|\leq C\left[\left(\fint_{Q_{R}(t_{0},x_{0})}|u|^{q}\operatorname{d}\!x\operatorname{d}\!t\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}+\left(\fint_{{I^{\ominus}_{R}(t_{0})}}\operatorname{Tail}(u(t);x_{0},R)^{q}\operatorname{d}\!t\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}+\mathcal{I}^{\mu}_{2}(t_{0},x_{0};2R)\right],

(1.7)

where the nonlocal tail term is defined as

\operatorname{Tail}(u(t);x_{0},R)=r^{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus B_{R}(x_{0})}\frac{|u(t,y)|}{|x_{0}-y|^{d+2s}}\operatorname{d}\!y.

We emphasize that the pointwise potential estimate (
1.7
) captures both local and nonlocal characters inherent in the mixed operator.
Given the nonlocal nature of the fractional term in (
1.1
), it is essential to include a nonlocal tail term in the derivation of the potential estimate to effectively control the solutions.
Nevertheless, the leading contributor in (
1.1
) is the higher order local term, thereby our potential estimate involves the caloric Riesz potential
\mathcal{I}^{\mu}_{2}(t_{0},x_{0};2R)
of order
2
, and the nonlocal tail term used here differs slightly from that discussed in
[
39
]
in the context of purely nonlocal parabolic equations.

The most critical ingredient in the construction of potential estimates is to establish an excess decay estimate for the solution of the

The fundamental scheme to prove this decay estimate comprises two key ingredients,
a comparison estimate given in Lemma

, and
the following local Hölder continuity with an optimal tail for the weak solution of the homogeneous problem corresponding to (
1.1

\partial_{t}v-\operatorname{div}\left(\mathcal{A}(t,x,Dv)\right)+\mathcal{L}v=0~~\text{ in }\Omega_{T}.

.

Let
v

This result might also be interesting for its own sake. In contrast to Hölder regularity of solutions to mixed local and nonlocal parabolic equations mentioned above
[
18
,
20
]
, they share a common additional assumption regarding the boundedness of the nonlocal tail term with respect to the time variable. However, we find that further proving potential estimates based on the local Hölder estimate with an

-Tail is unfeasible, and this assumption often does not hold in the solution space associated with weak solutions. Inspired by the recent work of Kassman and Weidner
[
22

, who obtained Hölder regularity of solutions to nonlocal parabolic equations without an a priori bounded assumption on the nonlocal tail term, here we improve the tail term in the previous Hölder estimates of solutions to mixed problems with respect to the time variable from
L^{\infty}
to
L^{q}
for any
q\in(1,2]

belonging to

apparently guarantees the nonlocal tail term
\operatorname{Tail}(v(t);x_{0},2R)\in L^{q}_{\rm loc}(I)
for any

, our result does not impose any unnatural restriction on solutions. When
q=1
in (

), analogous to the discussion in
[
22
]
, there exists a counterexample showing that the solution

is not Hölder continuous, thus justifying the optimality of the nonlocal tail term in the Hölder continuity estimate (
1.9
).

Lemma 3.1

\displaystyle\sup_{I_{\frac{R}{2}}^{\ominus}(t_{0})\times B_{\frac{R}{2}}(x_{0})}v_{+}(t,x)

3.2
Local Hölder regularity with an optimal Tail

With the Caccioppoli inequality and local boundedness at our disposal,
it is crucial for us to further establish a growth lemma to prove Hölder continuity. Now we start with a so-called De Giorgi type of lemma, which states that if a measure density is achieved in a parabolic cylinder, then pointwise information can be extracted in a smaller parabolic cylinder with the same vertex.

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
v
be a local weak supersolution to (
1.8
) in
\Omega_{T}
.
For every
R\in(0,1]
and
(t_{0},x_{0})\in I\times\Omega
with
I^{\ominus}_{2R}(t_{0})\times B_{2R}(x_{0})\Subset I\times\Omega
, any
\delta\in(0,1]
,
q\in(1,2]
and
H>0
, there exists
\lambda\in(0,1)
depending only on
d,\,s,\,\Lambda,\,q,\,\delta
, such that if
v\geq 0
in
Q_{2R}(t_{0},x_{0})
, as well as

\left|\left\{v\leq H\right\}\cap I^{\ominus}_{\delta R}(t_{0})\times B_{R}(x_{0})\right|\leq\lambda|I^{\ominus}_{\delta R}(t_{0})\times B_{R}(x_{0})|

(3.15)

and

\left(\fint_{I_{2R}^{\ominus}(t_{0})}\operatorname{Tail}(v_{-}(t);x_{0},2R)^{q}\operatorname{d}\!t\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}\leq H,

(3.16)

then it holds that

v(t,x)\geq\frac{H}{2}~~\text{ in }I^{\ominus}_{\frac{\delta R}{2}}(t_{0})\times B_{\frac{R}{2}}(x_{0}).

Proof.

For
i\in\mathbb{N_{+}}
, we define the following sequences

\displaystyle k_{i}=\frac{H}{2}+\frac{H}{2^{i}},\,\,w_{i}=(v-k_{i})_{-},\,\,R_{i}=\frac{R}{2}+\frac{R}{2^{i}},

\displaystyle A_{i}=\frac{\left|\left\{v\leq k_{i}\right\}\cap I^{\ominus}_{\delta R_{i}}(t_{0})\times B_{R_{i}}(x_{0})\right|}{|I^{\ominus}_{\delta R_{i}}\times B_{R_{i}}|},

\displaystyle B_{i}=\left(\fint_{I_{\delta R_{i}}^{\ominus}(t_{0})}\left(\frac{|\{v(t)\leq k_{i}\}\cap B_{R_{i}}(x_{0})|}{|B_{R_{i}}|}\right)^{\frac{q}{q-1}}\operatorname{d}\!t\right)^{\frac{(q-1)d}{q(d+2)}}.

Combining the Caccioppoli inequality (
2.1
) with Hölder’s inequality and (
3.16
), we directly compute

\displaystyle\sup_{t\in I_{\delta R_{i+1}}^{\ominus}(t_{0})}\int_{B_{R_{i+1}}(x_{0})}w^{2}_{i}(t,x)\operatorname{d}\!x+\int_{I_{\delta R_{i+1}}^{\ominus}(t_{0})}\int_{B_{R_{i+1}}(x_{0})}|Dw_{i}(t,x)|^{2}\operatorname{d}\!x\operatorname{d}\!t

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C2^{2i}(\delta R)^{-2}\int_{I_{\delta R_{i}}^{\ominus}(t_{0})}\int_{B_{R_{i}}(x_{0})}w^{2}_{i}(t,x)\operatorname{d}\!x\operatorname{d}\!t

\displaystyle+C2^{i(d+2)}R^{-2}\int_{I_{\delta R_{i}}^{\ominus}(t_{0})}\left(\int_{B_{R_{i}}(x_{0})}w_{i}(t,x)\operatorname{d}\!x\right)\operatorname{Tail}(w_{i}(t);x_{0},R_{i})\operatorname{d}\!t

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C2^{i(d+2)}R^{-2}H^{2}|Q_{R_{i}}|A_{i}+C2^{i(d+2)}R^{-2}H\int_{I_{\delta R_{i}}^{\ominus}(t_{0})}\left(\int_{B_{R_{i}}(x_{0})}\chi_{\{v\leq k_{i}\}}(t,x)\operatorname{d}\!x\right)\operatorname{Tail}(w_{i}(t);x_{0},2R)\operatorname{d}\!t

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C2^{i(d+2)}R^{-2}H^{2}|Q_{R_{i}}|A_{i}+C2^{i(d+2)}R^{-2}H\int_{I_{\delta R_{i}}^{\ominus}(t_{0})}\left(\int_{B_{R_{i}}(x_{0})}\chi_{\{v\leq k_{i}\}}(t,x)\operatorname{d}\!x\right)\operatorname{Tail}(v_{-}(t);x_{0},2R)\operatorname{d}\!t

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C2^{i(d+2)}R^{-2}H^{2}|Q_{R_{i}}|\left(A_{i}+B_{i}^{1+\frac{2}{d}}\right)\,.

Applying Hölder’s inequality repeatedly, in combination with the Sobolev embedding theorem, Young’s inequality and (
3.17
), we deduce that

\displaystyle A_{i+1}|I^{\ominus}_{\delta R_{i+1}}\times B_{R_{i+1}}|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\int_{I_{\delta R_{i+1}}^{\ominus}(t_{0})}\int_{B_{R_{i+1}}(x_{0})}\frac{w_{i}^{2}}{(k_{i}-k_{i+1})^{2}}\operatorname{d}\!x\operatorname{d}\!t

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C2^{2i}H^{-2}\left(A_{i}|I^{\ominus}_{\delta R_{i}}\times B_{R_{i}}|\right)^{\frac{2}{d+2}}\left[\int_{I_{\delta R_{i+1}}^{\ominus}(t_{0})}\left(\int_{B_{R_{i+1}}(x_{0})}w_{i}^{2}\operatorname{d}\!x\right)^{\frac{2}{d}}\left(\int_{B_{R_{i+1}}(x_{0})}w_{i}^{\frac{2d}{d-2}}\operatorname{d}\!x\right)^{\frac{d-2}{d}}\operatorname{d}\!t\right]^{\frac{d}{d+2}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C2^{2i}H^{-2}\left(A_{i}|I^{\ominus}_{\delta R_{i}}\times B_{R_{i}}|\right)^{\frac{2}{d+2}}

\displaystyle\times\left[\int_{I_{\delta R_{i+1}}^{\ominus}(t_{0})}\left(\int_{B_{R_{i+1}}(x_{0})}w_{i}^{2}\operatorname{d}\!x\right)^{\frac{2}{d}}\left(R_{i+1}^{-2}\int_{B_{R_{i+1}}(x_{0})}w_{i}^{2}\operatorname{d}\!x+\int_{B_{R_{i+1}}(x_{0})}|Dw_{i}|^{2}\operatorname{d}\!x\right)\operatorname{d}\!t\right]^{\frac{d}{d+2}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C2^{2i}H^{-2}\left(A_{i}|I^{\ominus}_{\delta R_{i}}\times B_{R_{i}}|\right)^{\frac{2}{d+2}}\left(\sup_{t\in I_{\delta R_{i+1}}^{\ominus}(t_{0})}\int_{B_{R_{i+1}}(x_{0})}w^{2}_{i}\operatorname{d}\!x+\int_{I_{\delta R_{i+1}}^{\ominus}(t_{0})}\int_{B_{R_{i+1}}(x_{0})}|Dw_{i}|^{2}\operatorname{d}\!x\operatorname{d}\!t\right)

\displaystyle C2^{(d+4)i}\delta^{\frac{4}{d+2}}|Q_{R_{i}}|\left(A_{i}^{1+\frac{2}{d+2}}+A_{i}^{\frac{2}{d+2}}B_{i}^{1+\frac{2}{d}}\right).

Then it follows that

A_{i+1}\leq\frac{C_{3}}{\delta^{2}}2^{(d+4)i}\left(A_{i}^{1+\frac{2}{d+2}}+A_{i}^{\frac{2}{d+2}}B_{i}^{1+\frac{2}{d}}\right),

(3.18)

where the positive constant

only depends on
d
,
s
and
\Lambda
.
By analogy with the estimates of

in (

(3.19)

where the constant

. Note that

From this, combining (

to derive that there exists

the sequences

and

Hence, we conclude that

v(t,x)\geq\frac{H}{2}~~\text{ in }I^{\ominus}_{\frac{\delta R}{2}}(t_{0})\times B_{\frac{R}{2}}(x_{0}),

which completes the proof of Lemma

.
∎

In the next step we establish a lemma that shows the forward propagation of the measure theoretical information in time.

Lemma 3.4
.

Let
\alpha\in(0,1]
and

v
be a local weak supersolution to (
1.8
) in
\Omega_{T}
.
For every
R\in(0,1]
and
(t_{0},x_{0})\in I\times\Omega
with
I^{\ominus}_{2R}(t_{0})\times B_{2R}(x_{0})\Subset I\times\Omega
, any
q\in(1,2]
and
H>0
, if
v\geq 0
in
Q_{2R}(t_{0},x_{0})

and

\left|\left\{v(t_{1},\cdot)\geq H\right\}\cap B_{R}(x_{0})\right|\geq\alpha|B_{R}(x_{0})|\,\,\mbox{for some}\,\,t_{1}\in I_{2R}^{\ominus}(t_{0}),

(3.20)

then either

\left(\fint_{I_{2R}^{\ominus}(t_{0})}\operatorname{Tail}(v_{-}(t);x_{0},2R)^{q}\operatorname{d}\!t\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}>H,

(3.21)

or there exists
\varepsilon,\,\delta\in(0,1)
depending only on
d,\,s,\,\Lambda,\,q,\,\alpha
, such that

\left|\left\{v(t,\cdot)\geq\varepsilon H\right\}\cap B_{R}(x_{0})\right|\geq\frac{\alpha}{2}|B_{R}(x_{0})|

for almost everywhere
t\in I_{\delta R}^{\oplus}(t_{1})\subset I_{2R}^{\ominus}(t_{0})
.

Proof.

We may assume that (
3.21
) is invalid, then a combination of the Caccioppoli inequality (
2.2
), Hölder’s inequality and the assumption (
3.20
) yields that

\displaystyle\int_{B_{(1-\epsilon)R}(x_{0})}(v-H)^{2}_{-}(t,x)\operatorname{d}\!x

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\int_{B_{R}(x_{0})}(v-H)^{2}_{-}(t_{1},x)\operatorname{d}\!x+C(\epsilon R)^{-2}\int_{I_{\delta R}^{\oplus}(t_{1})}\int_{B_{R}(x_{0})}(v-H)^{2}_{-}(t,x)\operatorname{d}\!x\operatorname{d}\!t

\displaystyle+C\epsilon^{-d-2}R^{-2}\int_{I_{\delta R}^{\oplus}(t_{1})}\left(\int_{B_{R}(x_{0})}(v-H)_{-}(t,x)\operatorname{d}\!x\right)\operatorname{Tail}((v-H)_{-}(t);x_{0},R)\operatorname{d}\!t

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left((1-\alpha)+C\epsilon^{-2}\delta^{2}+C\epsilon^{-d-2}\delta^{\frac{2(q-1)}{q}}\right)H^{2}|B_{R}|

holds for almost everywhere
t\in I_{\delta R}^{\oplus}(t_{1})
, where the constant
\epsilon\in(0,1)
is to be determined later.
From this, a direct calculation leads to

\displaystyle\left|\left\{v(t,\cdot)\leq\varepsilon H\right\}\cap B_{R}(x_{0})\right|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left|\left\{v(t,\cdot)\leq\varepsilon H\right\}\cap B_{(1-\epsilon)R}(x_{0})\right|+\left|B_{R}(x_{0})\setminus B_{(1-\epsilon)R}(x_{0})\right|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle((1-\varepsilon)H)^{-2}\int_{B_{(1-\epsilon)R}(x_{0})}(v-H)^{2}_{-}(t,x)\operatorname{d}\!x+d\epsilon|B_{R}|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle(1-\varepsilon)^{-2}\left((1-\alpha)+C\epsilon^{-2}\delta^{2}+C\epsilon^{-d-2}\delta^{\frac{2(q-1)}{q}}+d\epsilon\right)|B_{R}|.

To proceed, we choose appropriate
\epsilon
,
\delta
and
\varepsilon
, depending only on
d,\,s,\,\Lambda,\,q,\,\alpha
, such that

\left|\left\{v(t,\cdot)\leq\varepsilon H\right\}\cap B_{R}(x_{0})\right|\leq(1-\frac{\alpha}{2})|B_{R}|

for almost all
t\in I_{\delta R}^{\oplus}(t_{1})
.
This implies that the desired result is true, thus completing the proof of Lemma
3.4
.
∎

In the sequel, we establish a measure shrinking lemma.

Lemma 3.5
.

Let
\alpha\in(0,1]
and let

v
be a local weak supersolution to (
1.8
) in
\Omega_{T}
.
For every
R>0
and
(t_{0},x_{0})\in I\times\Omega
with
I^{\ominus}_{2R}(t_{0})\times B_{2R}(x_{0})\Subset I\times\Omega
, and any
\delta,\,\sigma\in(0,1]
,
q\in(1,2]
, and
H>0
, if
v\geq 0
in
Q_{2R}(t_{0},x_{0})

and

\left|\left\{v(t,\cdot)\geq H\right\}\cap B_{R}(x_{0})\right|\geq\alpha|B_{R}(x_{0})|\,\,\mbox{for a.e.}\,\,t\in I_{\delta R}^{\ominus}(t_{0}),

(3.22)

then there exists a positive constant
C
depending only on
d,\,s,\,\Lambda,\,q
, such that either

\left(\fint_{I_{2R}^{\ominus}(t_{0})}\operatorname{Tail}(v_{-}(t);x_{0},2R)^{q}\operatorname{d}\!t\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}>\sigma H,

(3.23)

or

\left|\left\{v\leq\frac{\sigma H}{4}\right\}\cap I^{\ominus}_{\delta R}(t_{0})\times B_{R}(x_{0})\right|\leq\frac{C}{\alpha\delta(\log_{2}\frac{2}{\sigma})^{\frac{1}{2}}}|I^{\ominus}_{\delta R}(t_{0})\times B_{R}(x_{0})|.

Proof.

Without loss of generality, we may assume that (
3.23
) does not hold true. For any fixed
\sigma\in(0,1]
, there exists
j^{\ast}\in\mathbb{N}_{+}
such that

\frac{1}{2^{j^{\ast}+1}}\leq\frac{\sigma}{4}<\frac{1}{2^{j^{\ast}}}
. We denote
k_{j}:=\frac{H}{2^{j}}
, then it is easy to see that
k_{j}
is a decreasing sequence and satisfies
k_{j}>\frac{\sigma H}{4}
for
j=0,1,2,...,j^{\ast}
.
Let
w(t,x)=v(t,x)-k_{j}
, then
applying the Caccioppoli inequality (
2.2
) with
l=k_{j}
and combining with Hölder’s inequality, we obtain

\displaystyle\int_{I_{\delta R}^{\ominus}(t_{0})}\int_{B_{R}(x_{0})}|Dw_{-}(t,x)|^{2}\operatorname{d}\!x\operatorname{d}\!t

(3.24)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\int_{B_{2R}(x_{0})}w^{2}_{-}(t_{0}-(\delta R)^{2},x)\operatorname{d}\!x+C\ R^{-2}\int_{I_{\delta R}^{\ominus}(t_{0})}\int_{B_{2R}(x_{0})}w^{2}_{-}(t,x)\operatorname{d}\!x\operatorname{d}\!t

\displaystyle+CR^{-2}\int_{I_{\delta R}^{\ominus}(t_{0})}\left(\int_{B_{2R}(x_{0})}w_{-}(t,x)\operatorname{d}\!x\right)\operatorname{Tail}(w_{-}(t);x_{0},2R)\operatorname{d}\!t

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle Ck_{j}^{2}(\delta R)^{-2}|I^{\ominus}_{\delta R}\times B_{R}|.

We define a set

A_{j}:=\{v\leq k_{j}\}\cap I_{\delta R}^{\ominus}(t_{0})\times B_{R}(x_{0}),

then it is derived from Lemma
2.4
, the condition (
3.22
), Hölder’s inequality and (
3.24
) that

\displaystyle(k_{j}-k_{j+1})|A_{j}|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle CR^{d+1}\int_{I_{\delta R}^{\ominus}(t_{0})}\frac{\int_{\{k_{j+1}<v(t,\cdot)\leq k_{j}\}\cap B_{R}(x_{0})}|Dv|\operatorname{d}\!x}{|\{v(t,\cdot)\geq k_{j}\}\cap B_{R}(x_{0})|}\operatorname{d}\!t

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle CR^{d+1}\int_{I_{\delta R}^{\ominus}(t_{0})}\frac{\int_{\{k_{j+1}<v(t,\cdot)\leq k_{j}\}\cap B_{R}(x_{0})}|Dw_{-}|\operatorname{d}\!x}{|\{v(t,\cdot)\geq H\}\cap B_{R}(x_{0})|}\operatorname{d}\!t

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{CR^{d+1}}{\alpha|B_{R}|}\left(\int_{I_{\delta R}^{\ominus}(t_{0})}\int_{B_{R}(x_{0})}|Dw_{-}|^{2}\operatorname{d}\!x\operatorname{d}\!t\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}|A_{j}-A_{j+1}|^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{C}{\alpha\delta}k_{j}|I^{\ominus}_{\delta R}\times B_{R}|^{\frac{1}{2}}|A_{j}-A_{j+1}|^{\frac{1}{2}}.

Note that
k_{j}-k_{j+1}=\frac{k_{j}}{2}
and
|A_{j}|>|A_{j+1}|
, then summing both sides of the above inequality with respect to
j
from
0
to
j^{\ast}-1
results in

|A_{j^{\ast}}|^{2}\leq\frac{\displaystyle\sum_{j=0}^{j^{\ast}-1}|A_{j+1}|^{2}}{j^{\ast}}\leq\frac{C}{(\alpha\delta)^{2}j^{\ast}}|I^{\ominus}_{\delta R}\times B_{R}|\displaystyle\sum_{j=0}^{j^{\ast}-1}|A_{j}-A_{j+1}|\leq\frac{C}{(\alpha\delta)^{2}j^{\ast}}|I^{\ominus}_{\delta R}\times B_{R}|^{2}.

Finally, the above estimate and
\frac{1}{2^{j^{\ast}+1}}\leq\frac{\sigma}{4}<\frac{1}{2^{j^{\ast}}}
guarantee that
j^{\ast}\geq\log_{2}\frac{2}{\sigma}
and then

\displaystyle\left|\left\{v\leq\frac{\sigma H}{4}\right\}\cap I^{\ominus}_{\delta R}(t_{0})\times B_{R}(x_{0})\right|\leq|A_{j^{\ast}}|\leq\frac{C}{\alpha\delta(\log_{2}\frac{2}{\sigma})^{\frac{1}{2}}}|I^{\ominus}_{\delta R}(t_{0})\times B_{R}(x_{0})|.

Thus, we complete the proof of Lemma
3.5
.
∎

to derive

∎

We are now ready to prove the Hölder estimate with an optimal tail term for the weak solution of the homogeneous mixed local and nonlocal parabolic equation (

), which is given in Theorem

.

Proof of Theorem
1.4
.

The proof is by constructing two sequences, a non-increasing sequence
\{M_{j}\}
and a non-decreasing sequence
\{m_{j}\}
, which satisfy

m_{j}\leq v\leq M_{j}~~\text{ in }Q_{\nu^{-j}R}(t_{0},x_{0}),~~\text{ and }~~M_{j}-m_{j}=L\nu^{-\gamma_{0}j},

(3.27)

for any

, some small
\gamma_{0}\in(0,1)
and large

,
where

L:=C_{5}\|v\|_{L^{\infty}(Q_{R}(t_{0},x_{0}))}+\left(\fint_{I_{2R}^{\ominus}(t_{0})}\operatorname{Tail}(v(t);x_{0},2R)^{q}\operatorname{d}\!t\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}

for some positive constant
C_{5}
to be determined later. Once (
3.27
) is achieved, we immediately obtain the desired Hölder continuity by using the definition of
L
and the boundedness of
v
established in Lemma
3.2
.

For any fixed
j_{0}
, if we set
M_{j}=\frac{\nu^{-\gamma_{0}j}L}{2}
and
m_{j}=-\frac{\nu^{-\gamma_{0}j}L}{2}
, and choose
C_{5}\geq 2\nu^{\gamma_{0}j_{0}}
, then it is not difficult to verify that (
3.27
) is valid for every

.
Next, we adopt induction to prove that (
3.27
) holds true for
j>j_{0}
.
More precisely, assuming that (
3.27
) is valid up to some
j\geq j_{0}
, we will show that (
3.27
) holds true for
j+1
.
To proceed, we need to construct appropriate
M_{j+1}
and
m_{j+1}
.
Without loss of generality, we suppose that

\big|\{v(t_{0}-(\delta\nu^{-j}R)^{2},\cdot)\geq m_{j}+\frac{M_{j}-m_{j}}{2}\}\cap B_{\nu^{-j}R}(x_{0})\big|\geq\frac{1}{2}|B_{\nu^{-j}R}(x_{0})|.

(3.28)

Through a straightforward calculation, we can verify that

\left(\fint_{I_{2\nu^{-j}R}^{\ominus}(t_{0})}\operatorname{Tail}((v-m_{j})_{-}(t);x_{0},2\nu^{-j}R)^{q}\operatorname{d}\!t\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}\leq\frac{\theta(M_{j}-m_{j})}{2}

holds true by first choosing a sufficiently small
\gamma_{0}\in(0,1)
, followed by selecting a sufficiently large
\nu>\frac{2}{\delta}
, both of which depend only on
d,\,s,\,q,\,\theta
.
Then it follows from Corollary
3.6
with
v:=v-m_{j}
,
R:=\nu^{-j}R
,
\alpha=\frac{1}{2}
,
t_{1}=t_{0}-(\delta\nu^{-j}R)^{2}
and

that

v(t,x)\geq m_{j}+\frac{\theta L\nu^{-\gamma_{0}j}}{16}~~\text{ in }Q_{\nu^{-(j+1)}R}(t_{0},x_{0}),

(3.29)

where the constant
\theta=\theta(d,s,\Lambda,q)\in(0,1)
. Thus, we define

M_{j+1}=M_{j}\,\,\mbox{and}\,\,m_{j+1}=M_{j}-L\nu^{-\gamma_{0}(j+1)},

and further choose
\gamma_{0}\leq\log_{\nu}(\frac{16}{16-\theta})
sufficiently small such that the chosen
M_{j+1}
and
m_{j+1}
satisfy (
3.27
). On the other hand, if (
3.28
) fails, we proceed analogously, but instead apply Corollary
3.6
with
v:=M_{j}-v
. In this case, let
M_{j+1}=L\nu^{-\gamma_{0}(j+1)}+m_{j}
and

as desired, then we complete the proof of Theorem

.
∎

4
Excess decay estimate

This section is devoted to proving an excess decay estimate for the mixed local and nonlocal parabolic equation (
1.1
), which is a crucial ingredient in the proof of our Riesz potential results. In this context, we introduce the following global excess functional to encode the information about the oscillations of solutions, taking into account simultaneously the behaviour in small parabolic cylinders and the long-range interactions

\displaystyle E(u,t_{0},x_{0},R)

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle\left[\fint_{{I^{\ominus}_{R}(t_{0})}}\left(\fint_{{B_{R}(x_{0})}}|u-(u)_{Q_{R}(t_{0},x_{0})}|\operatorname{d}\!x\right)^{q}\operatorname{d}\!t\right]^{\frac{1}{q}}

\displaystyle+\left(\fint_{{I^{\ominus}_{R}(t_{0})}}\operatorname{Tail}(u(t)-(u)_{Q_{R}(t_{0},x_{0})};x_{0},R)^{q}\operatorname{d}\!t\right)^{\frac{1}{q}},

where the symbol
(u)_{Q_{R}(t_{0},x_{0})}
denotes the integral average of a locally integrable function
u
over the parabolic cylinder
Q_{R}(t_{0},x_{0})
,
defined as

(u)_{Q_{R}(t_{0},x_{0})}:=\fint_{I^{\ominus}_{R}(t_{0})}\fint_{B_{R}(x_{0})}u(t,x)\operatorname{d}\!x\operatorname{d}\!t=\frac{1}{|Q_{R}|}\int_{I^{\ominus}_{R}(t_{0})}\int_{B_{R}(x_{0})}u(t,x)\operatorname{d}\!x\operatorname{d}\!t.

We first need to establish a comparison estimate between the weak solution of

\partial_{t}u-\operatorname{div}\left(\mathcal{A}(t,x,Du)\right)+\mathcal{L}u=\mu~~\text{in}~~Q_{2R}(t_{0},x_{0}),

(4.1)

for any
R>0
and
(t_{0},x_{0})\in I\times\Omega
with
Q_{2R}(t_{0},x_{0})\Subset I\times\Omega
,
and that of the desired problem

\left\{\begin{array}[]{r@{\ \ }c@{\ \ }ll}\partial_{t}v-\operatorname{div}\left(\mathcal{A}(t,x,Dv)\right)+\mathcal{L}v&=&0&\mbox{in}\ \ Q_{R}(t_{0},x_{0})\,,\\[1.42271pt]
v&=&u&\mbox{in}\ \ I_{R}^{\ominus}(t_{0})\times(\mathbb{R}^{d}\backslash B_{R}(x_{0}))\,,\\[1.42271pt]
v&=&u&\mbox{in}\ \ \{t_{0}-R^{2}\}\times B_{R}(x_{0})\,,\end{array}\right.

(4.2)

for which we have known Hölder regularity result.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
u
and
v
be weak solutions to (
4.1
) and (
4.2
) respectively, then there holds that

\sup_{t\in I_{R}^{\ominus}(t_{0})}\fint_{B_{R}(x_{0})}|u(t,x)-v(t,x)|\operatorname{d}\!x\leq\frac{2|\mu|(Q_{R}(t_{0},x_{0}))}{|B_{R}|}.

(4.3)

Proof.

The proof is done by applying the truncation method.
Let
w(t,x):=u(t,x)-v(t,x)
,
we first select
\phi_{\epsilon}^{\pm}=\pm\left(1\wedge\frac{w_{\pm}}{\epsilon}\right)
as a test function for
w
with respect to the spatial variable, then it yields that

\displaystyle\int_{B_{R}(x_{0})}(\partial_{t}w)\phi^{\pm}_{\epsilon}\operatorname{d}\!x+\int_{B_{R}(x_{0})}\left(\mathcal{A}(t,x,Du)-\mathcal{A}(t,x,Dv)\right)\cdot D\phi^{\pm}_{\epsilon}\operatorname{d}\!x

\displaystyle+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}(w(t,x)-w(t,y))(\phi^{\pm}_{\epsilon}(t,x)-\phi^{\pm}_{\epsilon}(t,y))K(t,x,y)\operatorname{d}\!x\operatorname{d}\!y

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{B_{R}(x_{0})}\phi_{\epsilon}^{\pm}\operatorname{d}\!\mu\leq|\mu(t)|(B_{R}(x_{0})),

where last inequality is guaranteed by
|\phi_{\epsilon}^{\pm}|\leq 1
. Utilizing the ellipticity condition (
1.3
) of
\mathcal{A}
and the definition of
\phi_{\epsilon}^{\pm}
, we obtain

\left(\mathcal{A}(t,x,Du)-\mathcal{A}(t,x,Dv)\right)\cdot D\phi^{\pm}_{\epsilon}\geq 0\,\,\mbox{and}\,\,(w(t,x)-w(t,y))(\phi^{\pm}_{\epsilon}(t,x)-\phi^{\pm}_{\epsilon}(t,y))\geq 0.

Then it follows that

\int_{B_{R}(x_{0})}(\partial_{t}w)\phi^{\pm}_{\epsilon}\operatorname{d}\!x\leq|\mu(t)|(B_{R}(x_{0})).

(4.4)

Next, for any given
\hat{t}\in I_{R}^{\ominus}(t_{0})
and
\delta>0
with
\hat{t}+\delta<t_{0}
, we define the test function for
w
with respect to the time variable as

\eta_{\delta}(t)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{r@{\ \ }c@{\ \ }ll}1,&t<\hat{t}\,,\\[1.42271pt]
1-\frac{t-\hat{t}}{\delta},&\hat{t}\leq t\leq\hat{t}+\delta\,,\\[1.42271pt]
0,&t>\hat{t}+\delta\,.\end{array}\right.

It is obvious that
\eta_{\delta}(t_{0})=0
and
\eta_{\delta}\in[0,1]
.
A combination of integration by parts and
w(t_{0}-R^{2},x)=0
in
B_{R}(x_{0})
yields that

\displaystyle\int_{I_{R}^{\ominus}(t_{0})}\eta_{\delta}(t)\int_{B_{R}(x_{0})}(\partial_{t}w)\phi^{\pm}_{\epsilon}\operatorname{d}\!x\operatorname{d}\!t

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{I_{R}^{\ominus}(t_{0})}\int_{B_{R}(x_{0})}\eta_{\delta}(t)\partial_{t}\left(\int_{0}^{w_{\pm}}\left(1\wedge\frac{\sigma}{\epsilon}\right)\operatorname{d}\!\sigma\right)\operatorname{d}\!x\operatorname{d}\!t

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\int_{I_{R}^{\ominus}(t_{0})}\partial_{t}\eta_{\delta}(t)\int_{B_{R}(x_{0})}\int_{0}^{w_{\pm}}\left(1\wedge\frac{\sigma}{\epsilon}\right)\operatorname{d}\!\sigma\operatorname{d}\!x\operatorname{d}\!t.

Then by virtue of (
4.4
), we derive

-\int_{I_{R}^{\ominus}(t_{0})}\partial_{t}\eta_{\delta}(t)\int_{B_{R}(x_{0})}\int_{0}^{w_{\pm}}\left(1\wedge\frac{\sigma}{\epsilon}\right)\operatorname{d}\!\sigma\operatorname{d}\!x\operatorname{d}\!t\leq\int_{I_{R}^{\ominus}(t_{0})}\eta_{\delta}(t)|\mu(t)|(B_{r}(x_{0}))\operatorname{d}\!t\leq|\mu|(Q_{R}(t_{0},x_{0})).

We first take the limit
\epsilon\rightarrow 0
of the above inequality, then it follows from the dominated convergence theorem that

\displaystyle\int_{I_{R}^{\ominus}(t_{0})}\left(-\partial_{t}\eta_{\delta}(t)\right)\int_{B_{R}(x_{0})}w_{\pm}(t,x)\operatorname{d}\!x\operatorname{d}\!t\leq|\mu|(Q_{R}(t_{0},x_{0})).

We proceed by letting
\delta\rightarrow 0
, since

-\partial_{t}\eta_{\delta}\to\delta_{\hat{t}}
as
\delta\rightarrow 0
, then it follows from Levi’s theorem that

\fint_{B_{R}(x_{0})}w_{\pm}(\hat{t},x)\operatorname{d}\!x\leq\frac{|\mu|(Q_{R}(t_{0},x_{0}))}{|B_{R}|}

for any
\hat{t}\in I_{R}^{\ominus}(t_{0})
. By the arbitrariness of
\hat{t}
, we deduce that

\sup_{t\in I_{R}^{\ominus}(t_{0})}\fint_{B_{R}(t_{0},x_{0})}w_{\pm}(t,x)\operatorname{d}\!x\operatorname{d}\!t\leq\frac{|\mu|(Q_{R}(t_{0},x_{0}))}{|B_{R}|}.

(4.5)

Finally, by summing (
4.5
) for the positive and negative parts, we arrive at the desired comparison estimate (
4.3
). Hence, we complete the proof of Lemma
4.1
.
∎

With the aid of the Hölder continuity for the solution
v
of the homogeneous mixed parabolic equation (
1.8
) obtained in Theorem
1.4
and the comparison estimate (
4.3
) between the solution
u
of the nonhomogeneous mixed parabolic equation (
1.1
) and
v
, we are now able to derive the excess decay estimate for
u
.

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
u
be a local weak solution to (
1.1
) with
\mu\in\mathcal{M}(\mathbb{R}^{d+1})
in
\Omega_{T}
,
then for any
q\in(1,2]
, any integer
m\geq 1
, any
R\in(0,1]
and
(t_{0},x_{0})\in I\times\Omega
with
I^{\ominus}_{2R}(t_{0})\times B_{2R}(x_{0})\Subset I\times\Omega
, there exists a positive constant
C_{0}
depending only on
d,\,s,\,\Lambda,\,q
such that

E(u,t_{0},x_{0},2^{-m}R)\leq C_{0}2^{-\gamma_{0}m}E(u,t_{0},x_{0},R)+C_{0}2^{2m(\frac{1}{q}+s+d)}R^{-d}|\mu|(Q_{R}(t_{0},x_{0})),

(4.6)

where the constant
\gamma_{0}=\gamma_{0}(d,s,\Lambda,q)
is the Hölder continuity exponent for the homogeneous mixed parabolic equation (
1.8
) given by Theorem
1.4
.

Proof.

Let
v
be a weak solution of the comparison equation (
4.2
), we first establish an excess decay estimate for
v
.
By performing a direct calculation and utilizing Hölder’s inequality and the Hölder estimate of
v
established in Theorem
1.4
, we derive

\displaystyle E(v,t_{0},x_{0},2^{-m}R)

(4.7)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left[\fint_{I^{\ominus}_{2^{-m}R}(t_{0})}\left(\fint_{B_{2^{-m}R}(x_{0})}|v(t,x)-(v)_{Q_{2^{-m}R}(t_{0},x_{0})}|\operatorname{d}\!x\right)^{q}\operatorname{d}\!t\right]^{\frac{1}{q}}

\displaystyle+(2^{-m}R)^{2}\sum_{k=1}^{m}\left[\fint_{I^{\ominus}_{2^{-m}R}(t_{0})}\left(\int_{B_{2^{k-m}R}(x_{0})\setminus B_{2^{k-m-1}R}(x_{0})}\frac{|v-(v)_{Q_{2^{-m}R}(t_{0},x_{0})}|}{|x_{0}-x|^{d+2s}}\operatorname{d}\!x\right)^{q}\operatorname{d}\!t\right]^{\frac{1}{q}}

\displaystyle+(2^{-m}R)^{2}\left[\fint_{I^{\ominus}_{2^{-m}R}(t_{0})}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus B_{R}(x_{0})}\frac{|v-(v)_{Q_{2^{-m}R}(t_{0},x_{0})}|}{|x_{0}-x|^{d+2s}}\operatorname{d}\!x\right)^{q}\operatorname{d}\!t\right]^{\frac{1}{q}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\sum_{k=0}^{m-1}2^{-2sk}\left[\fint_{I^{\ominus}_{2^{-m}R}(t_{0})}\left(\fint_{B_{2^{k-m}R}(x_{0})}|v(t,x)-(v)_{Q_{2^{-m}R}(t_{0},x_{0})}|\operatorname{d}\!x\right)^{q}\operatorname{d}\!t\right]^{\frac{1}{q}}

\displaystyle+2^{-2m(1-\frac{1}{q})}\left[\fint_{I^{\ominus}_{R}(t_{0})}\left(\fint_{B_{R}(x_{0})}|v(t,x)-(v)_{Q_{2^{-m}R}(t_{0},x_{0})}|\operatorname{d}\!x\right)^{q}\operatorname{d}\!t\right]^{\frac{1}{q}}

\displaystyle+2^{-2m(1-\frac{1}{q})}\left(\fint_{{I^{\ominus}_{R}(t_{0})}}\operatorname{Tail}(v(t)-(v)_{Q_{R}(t_{0},x_{0})};x_{0},R)^{q}\operatorname{d}\!t\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\sum_{k=0}^{m-1}2^{-2sk}\underset{{Q_{2^{k-m}R}(t_{0},x_{0})}}{\operatorname{osc}}v+C\sum_{k=0}^{m-1}2^{-2(1-\frac{1}{q})k}\underset{{Q_{2^{k-m}R}(t_{0},x_{0})}}{\operatorname{osc}}v+C\left(2^{-2sm}+2^{-2m(1-\frac{1}{q})}\right)E(v,t_{0},x_{0},R)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C2^{-\gamma_{0}m}E(v,t_{0},x_{0},R)\left(\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}2^{(\gamma_{0}-2s)k}+\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}2^{(\gamma_{0}-2(1-\frac{1}{q}))k}\right)+C2^{-\gamma_{0}m}E(v,t_{0},x_{0},R)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C2^{-\gamma_{0}m}E(v,t_{0},x_{0},R),

where the constant
C=C(d,s,\Lambda,q)
, and the Hölder continuity exponent
\gamma_{0}
of Theorem
1.4
may assume that
\gamma_{0}<\min\{2s,2(1-\frac{1}{q})\}
, which ensures that the last inequality is true.

We now turn to the proof of the excess decay estimate for
u
.
Note that

v=u\,\,\mbox{in}\,\,I_{R}^{\ominus}(t_{0})\times(\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus B_{R}(x_{0})),

and combining (
4.7
) with the comparison estimate given in Lemma
4.1
, we conclude that

\displaystyle E(u,t_{0},x_{0},2^{-m}R)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle E(v,t_{0},x_{0},2^{-m}R)+E(u-v,t_{0},x_{0},2^{-m}R)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C2^{-\gamma_{0}m}E(v,t_{0},x_{0},R)+C2^{2m(\frac{1}{q}+s+d)}E(u-v,t_{0},x_{0},R)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C2^{-\gamma_{0}m}E(v,t_{0},x_{0},R)+C2^{2m(\frac{1}{q}+s+d)}\sup_{t\in I_{R}^{\ominus}(t_{0})}\fint_{B_{R}(x_{0})}|u(t,x)-v(t,x)|\operatorname{d}\!x

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C_{0}2^{-\gamma_{0}m}E(u,t_{0},x_{0},R)+C_{0}2^{2m(\frac{1}{q}+s+d)}R^{-d}|\mu|(Q_{R}(t_{0},x_{0})),

where the positive constant
C_{0}
depends only on
d,\,s,\,\Lambda,\,q
.
Therefore, we conclude that the proof of Lemma
4.2
is complete.
∎

5
Riesz potential estimates

We are now in a position to prove our main results concerning the pointwise estimate and the oscillation estimate for the weak solution of the mixed local and nonlocal parabolic equation (
1.1
) via caloric Riesz potentials.
These results are given by Theorem
1.3
and Theorem
1.5
, respectively.

5.1
Pointwise estimate

In this subsection, we devoted ourselves to the proof of Theorem
1.3
, which relates to the zero-order pointwise Riesz potential estimate.

Proof of Theorem
1.3
.

We choose
m\geq 1
sufficiently large such that

C_{0}2^{-\gamma_{0}m}\leq\frac{1}{2},

(5.1)

then it is evident that
m
only depends on
d,\,s,\,\Lambda,\,q
.
For any
i\in\mathbb{N}
, we denote

E_{i}:=E(u,t_{0},x_{0},2^{-im}R),

then a combination of Lemma
4.2
with (
5.1
) yields that

E_{i+1}\leq\frac{1}{2}E_{i}+C_{0}2^{2m(\frac{1}{q}+s+d)}(2^{-im}R)^{-d}|\mu|(Q_{2^{-im}R}(t_{0},x_{0})).

(5.2)

For
l\in\mathbb{N}_{+}
, summing (
5.2
) over
i\in\{0,...,l-1\}
results in

\sum_{i=1}^{l}E_{i}\leq\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i=0}^{l-1}E_{i}+C_{0}2^{2m(\frac{1}{q}+s+d)}\sum_{i=0}^{l-1}(2^{-im}R)^{-d}|\mu|(Q_{2^{-im}R}(t_{0},x_{0})).

Note that the first term on the right side of the above inequality can be absorbed by the left side, then we obtain

\sum_{i=1}^{l}E_{i}\leq 2E_{0}+2C_{0}2^{2m(\frac{1}{q}+s+d)}\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}(2^{-im}R)^{-d}|\mu|(Q_{2^{-im}R}(t_{0},x_{0})).

Combining with Hölder’s inequality, we deduce that

\displaystyle|(u)_{Q_{2^{-(l+1)m}R}(t_{0},x_{0})}|

(5.3)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sum_{i=0}^{l}(|(u)_{Q_{2^{-(i+1)m}R}(t_{0},x_{0})}-(u)_{Q_{2^{-im}R}(t_{0},x_{0})}|)+|(u)_{Q_{R}(t_{0},x_{0})}|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\sum_{i=0}^{l}\fint_{Q_{2^{-im}R}(t_{0},x_{0})}|u(t,x)-(u)_{Q_{2^{-im}R}(t_{0},x_{0})}|\operatorname{d}\!x\operatorname{d}\!t+\fint_{Q_{R}(t_{0},x_{0})}|u(t,x)|\operatorname{d}\!x\operatorname{d}\!t

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\sum_{i=1}^{l}E_{i}+C\fint_{Q_{R}(t_{0},x_{0})}|u(t,x)|\operatorname{d}\!x\operatorname{d}\!t

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\left[\left(\fint_{Q_{R}(t_{0},x_{0})}|u(t,x)|^{q}\operatorname{d}\!x\operatorname{d}\!t\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}+\left(\fint_{{I^{\ominus}_{R}(t_{0})}}\operatorname{Tail}(u(t);x_{0},R)^{q}\operatorname{d}\!t\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}\right.

\displaystyle\left.+\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}(2^{-im}R)^{-d}|\mu|(Q_{2^{-im}R}(t_{0},x_{0}))\right],

where the positive constant
C
depends only on
d,\,s,\,\Lambda,\,q
. Furthermore, it remains to estimate the last term in (
5.3
). Let
H:=2^{m}
, then it follows that

\displaystyle\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}(H^{-i}R)^{-d}|\mu|(Q_{H^{-i}R}(t_{0},x_{0}))

(5.4)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle R^{-d}|\mu|(Q_{R}(t_{0},x_{0}))+\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}(H^{-i-1}R)^{-d}|\mu|(Q_{H^{-i-1}R}(t_{0},x_{0}))

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\ln 2}\int_{R}^{2R}\frac{|\mu|(Q_{R}(t_{0},x_{0}))}{R^{d}}\frac{\operatorname{d}\!\rho}{\rho}+\frac{1}{\ln H}\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}\int_{H^{-i-1}R}^{H^{-i}R}(H^{-i-1}R)^{-d}|\mu|(Q_{H^{-i-1}R}(t_{0},x_{0}))\frac{\operatorname{d}\!\rho}{\rho}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{2^{d}}{\ln 2}\int_{R}^{2R}\frac{|\mu|(Q_{\rho}(t_{0},x_{0}))}{\rho^{d}}\frac{\operatorname{d}\!\rho}{\rho}+\frac{H^{d}}{\ln H}\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}\int_{H^{-i-1}R}^{H^{-i}R}\frac{|\mu|(Q_{\rho}(t_{0},x_{0}))}{\rho^{d}}\frac{\operatorname{d}\!\rho}{\rho}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C(d,s,\Lambda,q)\int_{0}^{2R}\frac{|\mu|(Q_{\rho}(t_{0},x_{0}))}{\rho^{d}}\frac{\operatorname{d}\!\rho}{\rho}=C\mathcal{I}^{\mu}_{2}(t_{0},x_{0};2R).

Finally, substituting (
5.4
) into (
5.3
) and letting

l\rightarrow\infty
, then we conclude from the Lebesgue differentiation theorem that the pointwise Riesz potential estimate (
1.7
) holds true. Hence, the proof of Theorem
1.3
is complete.
∎

5.2
Oscillation estimate

In the last subsection, we prove the oscillation estimate given in Theorem
1.5
by introducing two appropriate fractional maximal operators.

Proof of Theorem
1.5
.

At this stage, we shall use two fractional maximal operators, one being the restricted fractional maximal function of order
2-\gamma
for
\mu
, defined by

{\bf M}_{2-\gamma,R}(\mu)(t,x):=\sup_{0<r\leq R}r^{2-\gamma}\frac{|\mu|(Q_{r}(t,x))}{r^{d+2}},

the other is the restricted nonlocal fractional sharp maximal function of order
\gamma
for
u
, defined by

{\bf M}^{\sharp}_{\gamma,R}(u)(t,x):=\sup_{0<r\leq R}r^{-\gamma}E(u,t,x,r).

Let us first claim that there exists a positive constant
C=C(d,s,\Lambda,q,\tilde{\gamma})
such that

{\bf M}^{\sharp}_{\gamma,R}(u)(t,x)\leq C{\bf M}_{2-\gamma,R}(\mu)(t,x)+CR^{-\gamma}E(u,t,x,R)

(5.5)

holds for any
\gamma\in[0,\tilde{\gamma}]
and any parabolic cylinder
Q_{2R}(t,x)\Subset I\times\Omega
.

For any fixed
\rho\in(0,R]
, and
\sigma\in(0,\frac{1}{2})
to be determined later, then Lemma
4.2
implies that

(\sigma\rho)^{-\gamma}E(u,t,x,\sigma\rho)\leq C_{0}(d,s,\Lambda,q)\sigma^{\gamma_{0}-\gamma}\rho^{-\gamma}E(u,t,x,\rho)+C(d,s,\Lambda,q,\sigma)\rho^{-d-\gamma}|\mu|(Q_{\rho}(t,x)).

Selecting
\sigma=\sigma(d,s,\Lambda,q,\tilde{\gamma})
small enough such that
C_{0}\sigma^{\gamma_{0}-\tilde{\gamma}}=\frac{1}{2}
, then
C_{0}\sigma^{\gamma_{0}-\gamma}\leq\frac{1}{2}
ensured by
\gamma\leq\tilde{\gamma}
. It follows that

\displaystyle(\sigma\rho)^{-\gamma}E(u,t,x,\sigma\rho)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\rho^{-\gamma}E(u,t,x,\rho)+C\rho^{2-\gamma}\frac{|\mu|(Q_{\rho}(t,x))}{\rho^{d+2}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}{\bf M}^{\sharp}_{\gamma,R}(u)(t,x)+C{\bf M}_{2-\gamma,R}(\mu)(t,x),

where the positive constant
C=C(d,s,\Lambda,q,\tilde{\gamma})
. Due to the arbitrariness of
\rho\in(0,R]
, we further obtain

\sup_{0<r\leq\sigma R}r^{-\gamma}E(u,t,x,r)\leq\frac{1}{2}{\bf M}^{\sharp}_{\gamma,R}(u)(t,x)+C{\bf M}_{2-\gamma,R}(\mu)(t,x).

(5.6)

Meanwhile, by a direct calculation, we can derive

\sup_{\sigma R<r\leq R}r^{-\gamma}E(u,t,x,r)\leq C(d,s,\Lambda,q,\tilde{\gamma})R^{-\gamma}E(u,t,x,R).

(5.7)

A combination of (
5.6
) and (
5.7
) yields that

{\bf M}^{\sharp}_{\gamma,R}(u)(t,x)\leq\frac{1}{2}{\bf M}^{\sharp}_{\gamma,R}(u)(t,x)+C{\bf M}_{2-\gamma,R}(\mu)(t,x)+CR^{-\gamma}E(u,t,x,R),

where the first term on the right side can be absorbed by the left side, and thus the assertion (
5.5
) is valid.

Let
(t,x),\,(\tau,y)\in Q_{\frac{R}{8}}(t_{0},x_{0})
be Lebesgue points of
u
and
r\in(0,\frac{R}{8}]
, then for any
0<\rho\leq\frac{r}{8}
, there exist
k\in\mathbb{N}
and
\theta\in(\frac{1}{4},\frac{1}{2}]
such that
\rho=\theta^{k}\frac{r}{4}
.
A direct calculation shows that

\displaystyle|(u)_{Q_{\frac{r}{4}}(t,x)}-(u)_{Q_{\rho}(t,x)}|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sum_{i=0}^{k-1}|(u)_{Q_{\theta^{i}\frac{r}{4}}(t,x)}-(u)_{Q_{\theta^{i+1}\frac{r}{4}}(t,x)}|

(5.8)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\theta^{-\frac{2}{q}-d}\sum_{i=0}^{k-1}E(u,t,x,\theta^{i}\frac{r}{4})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\theta^{-\frac{2}{q}-d}}{\ln(\theta^{-1})}\sum_{i=0}^{k-1}\int_{\theta^{i}\frac{r}{4}}^{\theta^{i-1}\frac{r}{4}}E(u,t,x,\theta^{i}\frac{r}{4})\frac{\operatorname{d}\!\varrho}{\varrho}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C(d,s,q)\frac{4^{\frac{2}{q}+d}}{\ln 2}\sum_{i=0}^{k-1}\int_{\theta^{i}\frac{r}{4}}^{\theta^{i-1}\frac{r}{4}}E(u,t,x,\varrho)\frac{\operatorname{d}\!\varrho}{\varrho}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C(d,s,q)\int_{\rho}^{r}E(u,t,x,\varrho)\frac{\operatorname{d}\!\varrho}{\varrho}.

To estimate the last term, we apply Lemma
4.2
again, together with the choice of
\sigma
, to deduce that

\displaystyle\int_{\rho}^{r}E(u,t,x,\varrho)\frac{\operatorname{d}\!\varrho}{\varrho}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\int_{\sigma\rho}^{r}E(u,t,x,\varrho)\frac{\operatorname{d}\!\varrho}{\varrho}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\rho}^{r}E(u,t,x,\sigma\varrho)\frac{\operatorname{d}\!\varrho}{\varrho}+\int_{\sigma r}^{r}E(u,t,x,\varrho)\frac{\operatorname{d}\!\varrho}{\varrho}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\int_{\rho}^{r}E(u,t,x,\varrho)\frac{\operatorname{d}\!\varrho}{\varrho}+C\int_{\rho}^{r}\frac{|\mu|(Q_{\varrho}(t,x))}{\varrho^{d}}\frac{\operatorname{d}\!\varrho}{\varrho}+CE(u,t,x,r)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 2C\int_{\rho}^{r}\frac{|\mu|(Q_{\varrho}(t,x))}{\varrho^{d}}\frac{\operatorname{d}\!\varrho}{\varrho}+2CE(u,t,x,r)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle Cr^{\gamma}\mathcal{I}^{\mu}_{2-\gamma}(t,x;R)+CE(u,t,x,r),

where the positive constant
C=C(d,s,\Lambda,q,\tilde{\gamma})
. Substituting the above inequality into (
5.8
), we derive

|(u)_{Q_{\frac{r}{4}}(t,x)}-(u)_{Q_{\rho}(t,x)}|\leq Cr^{\gamma}\mathcal{I}^{\mu}_{2-\gamma}(t,x;R)+CE(u,t,x,r).

Letting
\rho\rightarrow 0
, then it follows from the Lebesgue differentiation theorem that

|(u)_{Q_{\frac{r}{4}}(t,x)}-u(t,x)|\leq Cr^{\gamma}\mathcal{I}^{\mu}_{2-\gamma}(t,x;R)+CE(u,t,x,r).

Treating in a completely similar manner, we also have

|(u)_{Q_{\frac{r}{4}}(\tau,y)}-u(\tau,y)|\leq Cr^{\gamma}\mathcal{I}^{\mu}_{2-\gamma}(\tau,y;R)+CE(u,\tau,y,r).

We now take
r=\frac{|t-\tau|^{\frac{1}{2}}+|x-y|}{3}<\frac{R}{8}
, then it is easy to verify that

Q_{r}(\tau,y)\subset Q_{4r}(t,x)
. Combining the last two inequalities with (
5.5
), the fact that
Q_{\frac{R}{2}}(t,x)\subset Q_{R}(t_{0},x_{0})
, Hölder’s inequality and Lemma 4.1 presented in
[
25
]
, we conclude that

\displaystyle|u(t,x)-u(\tau,y)|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle|(u)_{Q_{\frac{r}{4}}(t,x)}-(u)_{Q_{\frac{r}{4}}(\tau,y)}|+CE(u,t,x,r)+CE(u,\tau,y,r)+Cr^{\gamma}\left[\mathcal{I}^{\mu}_{2-\gamma}(t,x;R)+\mathcal{I}^{\mu}_{2-\gamma}(\tau,y;R)\right]

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle CE(u,t,x,4r)+Cr^{\gamma}\left[\mathcal{I}^{\mu}_{2-\gamma}(t,x;R)+\mathcal{I}^{\mu}_{2-\gamma}(\tau,y;R)\right]

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle Cr^{\gamma}{\bf M}^{\sharp}_{\gamma,\frac{R}{2}}(u)(t,x)+Cr^{\gamma}\left[\mathcal{I}^{\mu}_{2-\gamma}(t,x;R)+\mathcal{I}^{\mu}_{2-\gamma}(\tau,y;R)\right]

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle Cr^{\gamma}\left[{\bf M}_{2-\gamma,\frac{R}{2}}(\mu)(t,x)+R^{-\gamma}E(u,t,x,\frac{R}{2})\right]+Cr^{\gamma}\left[\mathcal{I}^{\mu}_{2-\gamma}(t,x;R)+\mathcal{I}^{\mu}_{2-\gamma}(\tau,y;R)\right]

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C(\frac{r}{R})^{\gamma}E(u,t_{0},x_{0},R)+Cr^{\gamma}\left[\mathcal{I}^{\mu}_{2-\gamma}(t,x;R)+\mathcal{I}^{\mu}_{2-\gamma}(\tau,y;R)\right]

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\left[\left(\fint_{Q_{R}(t_{0},x_{0})}|u|^{q}\operatorname{d}\!x\operatorname{d}\!t\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}+\left(\fint_{{I^{\ominus}_{R}(t_{0})}}\operatorname{Tail}(u(t);x_{0},R)^{q}\operatorname{d}\!t\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}\right]\left(\frac{|t-\tau|^{\frac{1}{2}}+|x-y|}{R}\right)^{\gamma}

\displaystyle+C\left[\mathcal{I}^{\mu}_{2-\gamma}(t,x;R)+\mathcal{I}^{\mu}_{2-\gamma}(\tau,y;R)\right]\left(|t-\tau|^{\frac{1}{2}}+|x-y|\right)^{\gamma}.

Therefore, the proof of Theorem
1.5
is complete.
∎

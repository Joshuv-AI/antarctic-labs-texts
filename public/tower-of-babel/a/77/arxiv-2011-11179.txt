1. INTRODUCTION

In recent years, the
q
-calculus (also called quantum calculus) and
q
-fractional calculus have attracted much attention in mathematical and physical fields, such as number theory, special functions, basic hyper-geometric functions, operator theory, quantum dynamics and stochastic analysis
[
1
,
2
,
3
,
4
,
5
,
6
,
7
]
.
The
q
-calculus is a class of calculus defined on a
q
-geometry set
T_{q}
where
q
is the scale index of set
T_{q}
. The
q
-fractional differential equations usually describe some physics processes imposed on one time scale set
T_{q,b}=\{q^{n}b,n=0,1,\cdots\}\cup\{0\}
where
q
is used to indicate the discrete time path on which the corresponding physical process varies. The
q
-derivative was originally presented by Jackson
[
8
]
in 1908 and the
q
-integral originated from a
q
-analogue of the usual integral proposed by Al-Salam
[
9
]
in 1966. Following Al-Salam’s work, Agarwal
[
10
]
further gave the definition of
q
-fractional calculus. We refer the readers to
[
1
,
2
,
4
,
11
,
12
,
13
]
for some basic introduction on
q
-calculus and
q
-fractional calculus. Compared with the classical fractional calculus, the study of
q
-fractional calculus is still immature.

The existence of solutions for the Caputo type
q
-fractional initial value and boundary value problems has been studied by many researches, see
[
14
,
15
,
16
,
17
,
19
]
. By using the Lyapunov’s direct method, Jarad et. al.
[
6
]
studied the stability of Caputo type
q
-fractional non-autonomous systems. Wang et. al.
[
20
]
investigated the existence of extremal solutions of the Caputo type
q
-fractional differential equation. Zhang et. al.
[
18
,
19
]
gave new existence theorems for the initial value problem of
q
-fractional differential equation under different conditions of source term functions. On the discrete or numerical methods, Abdeljawad and Baleanu
[
21
]
first presented the successive approximation method to obtain an approximate solution of the Caputo type
q
-fractional differential equation. Then, Salahshour et. al.
[
22
]
investigated the convergence of the successive approximation method presented by Abdeljawad. Wu and Baleanu
[
23
]
used the variational iteration method and the Lagrange multiplier method to study the Caputo type
q
-fractional initial value problem. Recently, Zhang and Tang
[
24
]
established a difference method for solving the initial problem of the
q
-fractional differential equation, but no stability and convergence analysis are given for this method. At present, only a few discrete or approximate methods are proposed for the
q
-fractional problems. In particular, almost no difference method is analyzed theoretically for solving the
q
-fractional differential equations.

In this article, we first present a difference formula (called the
L_{1,q}
formula) to discretize the fractional
q
-derivative
{}^{c}D_{q}^{\alpha}x(t)
of Caputo type on the time scale set
T_{q,b}=\{q^{n}b,n=0,1,\cdots\}\cup\{0\}
. Different from the standard difference formula, we must establish the difference formula on set
T_{q,b}
, that is, the mesh points
\{t_{k}\}
should be in set
T_{q,b}
. Thus, the mesh partition with nodes
\{t_{k}=bq^{k},\,k=0,\cdots,N\}
is nonuniform and the maximal mesh step does not vanish as
N\rightarrow\infty
. This makes the error analysis and stability estimate much more difficult than that of the standard difference methods which can be established on any proper meshes, for example, the uniform mesh. Our difference formula is constructed by using the piecewise linear interpolation to approximate the integrand function on set
T_{q,b}
. The coefficients of this formula are analyzed in detail. We derive the truncation error boundness and prove that this difference formula is unconditionally stable. Then, using this difference formula, we establish a difference method for solving the initial problem of
q
-fractional differential equation:
{}^{c}D_{q}^{\alpha}x(t)=f(t,x(t)),t\in T_{q,b}
. We prove that this difference method is stale and give an error estimation of
\triangle t_{n}^{2}
-order. Numerical experiments show the high accuracy and effectiveness of this difference formula. To the authors’ best knowledge, it is the first time that an unconditionally stable difference formula is presented and analyzed for the
q
-fractional problems. Our work provides a numerical approach for solving the
q
-fractional problems.

This paper is organized as follows. In Section 2, we introduce some basic notations and operations on
q
-fractional calculus. In Section 3, we present the
L_{1,q}
difference formula and derive the truncation error boundness. Section 4 is contributed to the stability analysis of this difference formula. In Section 5, this difference formula is used to solve the initial value problem of
q
-fractional differential equation and the unique existence of the difference solution, stability and error estimation are given. Numerical experiments are provided in Section 6. Section 7 gives some conclusions.

2. THE
q
-CALCULUS AND
q
-FRACTIONAL CALCULUS

In this section, we introduce some notations and operations on the
q
-calculus and the
q
-fractional calculus.

Let
\mathbb{N}=\left\{1,2,3,...\right\}
be the positive integer set. Define the
q
-shifted factorial operation by

(t-s)^{(0)}=1,(t-s)^{(k)}=\prod\limits_{i=0}^{k-1}(t-q^{i}s),\,k\in\mathbb{N}.

(1)

If
\alpha\in R
and
\pm\alpha\notin\mathbb{N}
, then

(t-s)^{(\alpha)}=t^{\alpha}\prod\limits_{i=0}^{\infty}\frac{t-q^{i}s}{t-q^{\alpha+i}s},\,0\leq s\leq t.

(2)

Let
\mathbb{C}
be the complex set. For
0<q<1
and
\alpha\in\mathbb{C}\setminus\{-n:n\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}\}
, the
q
-gamma function
\Gamma_{q}(\alpha)
is defined by

\Gamma_{q}(\alpha)=(1-q)^{(\alpha-1)}(1-q)^{1-\alpha}.

(3)

Introduce the notations

[\alpha]_{q}=\frac{1-q^{\alpha}}{1-q},\;\;[n]_{q}!=[n]_{q}[n-1]_{q}\cdots[1]_{q}.

By a straightforward computation, it holds that

\Gamma_{q}(1)=1,\;\Gamma_{q}(\alpha+1)=[\alpha]_{q}\Gamma_{q}(\alpha),\;\Gamma_{q}(n+1)=[n]_{q}!\,.

For
\mathrm{Re}(\alpha)>0
,
\mathrm{Re}(\beta)>0
, the
q
-beta function is defined by

B_{q}(\alpha,\beta)=\int_{0}^{1}t^{\alpha-1}(1-qt)^{(\beta-1)}d_{q}t,

(4)

where the
q
-integral
\int\,d_{q}t
is defined by (
5
)–(
6
). The relation between the
q
-beta function and the
q
-gamma function is given as follows
[
25
]
.

B_{q}(\alpha,\beta)=\frac{\Gamma_{q}(\alpha)\Gamma_{q}(\beta)}{\Gamma_{q}(\alpha+\beta)}.

For a fixed point
q\in R
, a subset
A\subset R
is called
q
-geometric if
qt\in A
whenever
t\in A
.
It is easy to see that
\forall\,t\in A
,
A
contains all geometric sequences
\{tq^{n}\}_{n=0}^{\infty}
. A typical
q
-geometry set is the time scale set
T_{q}
defined by
T_{q}=\left\{q^{n}:n\in\mathbb{Z}\right\}\cup\left\{0\right\}
, where
0<q<1
,
\mathbb{Z}=\left\{0,\pm 1,\pm 2,...\right\}
.

Definition 1.

[
8
]

Let
f(t)
be a real valued function on set
A
,
0<q<1
. The
q
-derivative of
f(t)
is defined by

\displaystyle D_{q}f(t)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{d_{q}f(t)}{d_{q}t}=\frac{f(qt)-f(t)}{(q-1)t},\,\,t\in A\backslash\left\{0\right\},

\displaystyle D_{q}f(0)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{d_{q}f(t)}{d_{q}t}|_{t=0}=\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{f(tq^{n})-f(0)}{tq^{n}},\,\,0<q<1,\,t\neq 0.

The
q
-derivative is a discrete analogy of the classical derivative. Obviously, for a function
f(t)
defined on
R
, its
q
-derivative
D_{q}f(t)
always exists for
t\not=0
and
D_{q}f(0)
is well defined if the classical derivative
f^{\prime}(0)
exists.

The high order
q
-derivative
D_{q}^{n}f(t)
is defined by
D_{q}^{n}f(t)=D_{q}(D^{n-1}_{q}f(t)),\,n\geq 2
.

Let
f(t)
and
g(t)
be real valued functions on
R
and both
f(t)
and
g(t)
are differentiable at
t=0
. Then, the following
q
-derivative operation rules hold
[
1
]

\displaystyle D_{q}\left(af(t)\pm bg(t)\right)=aD_{q}f(t)\pm bD_{q}g(t),\,a,b\in R,

\displaystyle D_{q}\left(f(t)g(t)\right)=g(t)D_{q}f(t)+f(qt)D_{q}g(t),

\displaystyle D_{q}\big(\frac{f(t)}{g(t)}\big)=\frac{g(t)D_{q}f(t)-f(t)D_{q}g(t)}{g(t)g(qt)},\,g(t)\not=0,\,g(qt)\not=0.

Definition 2.

[
26
]

The
q
-integral on interval
(a,b)
is defined by

\int_{a}^{b}f(t)d_{q}t=\int_{0}^{b}f(t)d_{q}t-\int_{0}^{a}f(t)d_{q}t,\,\,a,\,b\in A,

(5)

where

\int_{0}^{x}f(t)d_{q}t=(1-q)\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}xq^{n}f(xq^{n}),\,\,x\in A.

(6)

By this definition, it is easy to see that

\displaystyle\Big|\int_{0}^{b}f(t)d_{q}t\Big|\leq\int_{0}^{b}|f(t)|d_{q}t,\;0<b.

(7)

\displaystyle\int_{a}^{b}f(t)d_{q}t=\int_{a}^{c}f(t)d_{q}t+\int_{c}^{b}f(t)d_{q}t,\;a<c<b.

(8)

The following lemma gives the
q
-integration by parts rule.

Lemma 1.

Let
f(t)
and
g(t)
are real valued functions on set
A
,
0\leq a<b,\,a,b\in A
and
0<q<1
, then

\displaystyle\int_{a}^{b}g(t)D_{q}f(t)d_{q}t=(fg)(b)-(fg)(a)-\int_{a}^{b}f(qt)D_{q}g(t)d_{q}t,

(9)

\displaystyle\int_{a}^{b}g(qt)D_{q}f(t)d_{q}t=(fg)(b)-(fg)(a)-\int_{a}^{b}f(t)D_{q}g(t)d_{q}t.

(10)

Proof.
We only prove conclusion (
9
), the proof of conclusion (
10
) is completely similar. When
a=0
, we have from (
6
) that

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{b}g(t)D_{q}f(t)d_{q}t

\displaystyle=(1-q)\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}bq^{n}g(bq^{n})D_{q}f(bq^{n})

\displaystyle=(1-q)b\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}q^{n}g(bq^{n})\frac{f(bq^{n})-f(bq^{n+1})}{(1-q)bq^{n}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}g(bq^{n})\left(f(bq^{n})-f(bq^{n+1})\right),

and

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{b}f(qt)D_{q}g(t)d_{q}t

\displaystyle=(1-q)\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}bq^{n}f(bq^{n+1})D_{q}g(bq^{n})

\displaystyle=(1-q)b\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}q^{n}f(bq^{n+1})\frac{g(bq^{n})-g(bq^{n+1})}{(1-q)bq^{n}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}f(bq^{n+1})\left(g(bq^{n})-g(bq^{n+1})\right).

So

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{b}g(t)D_{q}f(t)d_{q}t+\int_{0}^{b}f(qt)D_{q}g(t)d_{q}t=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\Big(f(bq^{n})g(bq^{n})-f(bq^{n+1})g(bq^{n+1})\Big)

\displaystyle=f(b)g(b)-\lim_{n\to\infty}(fg)(bq^{n+1})=(fg)(b)-(fg)(0).

(11)

When
a>0
, we have from (
5
) and (
11
)

\displaystyle\int_{a}^{b}g(t)D_{q}f(t)d_{q}t

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{b}g(t)D_{q}f(t)d_{q}t-\int_{0}^{a}g(t)D_{q}f(t)d_{q}t

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(fg)(b)-(fg)(0)-\int_{0}^{b}f(qt)D_{q}g(t)d_{q}t

\displaystyle-(fg)(a)+(fg)(0)+\int_{0}^{a}f(qt)D_{q}g(t)d_{q}t

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(fg)(b)-(fg)(a)-\int_{a}^{b}f(qt)D_{q}g(t)d_{q}t.

The proof is completed.
\hfill\Box

Now we introduce the
q
-fractional calculus.

Definition 3.

[
3
]

Let
t\in A
,
\alpha\neq-1,-2,...
and
a\geq 0
. The
\alpha
-order
q
-fractional integral of the Riemann-Liouville type with the lower limit point
a
is defined by
I_{q,a}^{0}f(t)=f(t)
and

I_{q,a}^{\alpha}f(t)=\frac{1}{\Gamma_{q}(\alpha)}\int_{a}^{t}(t-qs)^{(\alpha-1)}f(s)d_{q}s.

(12)

Definition 4.

[
27
]

Let
a\geq 0,a\in A,n=\lceil\alpha\rceil
. The
\alpha
-order Riemann-Liouville type fractional
q
-derivative of function
f(t):(a,\infty)\rightarrow\mathrm{R}
is defined by

\displaystyle D_{q,a}^{\alpha}f(t)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{lll}&I_{q,a}^{-\alpha}f(t),&\alpha\leq 0,\\
&D_{q}^{n}I_{q,a}^{n-\alpha}f(t),&\alpha>0,\end{array}\right.

where
\lceil\alpha\rceil
denotes the smallest integer that is greater or equal to
\alpha
.

Definition 5.

[
27
]

Let
a\geq 0,\,a\in A,\,n=\lceil\alpha\rceil
. The
\alpha
-order Caputo type fractional
q
-derivative of function
f(t):(a,\infty)\rightarrow\mathrm{R}
is defined by

{}^{c}D_{q,a}^{\alpha}f(t)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{lll}&I_{q,a}^{-\alpha}f(t),&\alpha\leq 0,\\
&I_{q,a}^{n-\alpha}D_{q}^{n}f(t),&\alpha>0.\end{array}\right.

For simplicity, we often use the notations
I_{q}^{\alpha}f(t)
instead of
I_{q,0}^{\alpha}f(t)
,
D_{q}^{\alpha}f(t)
instead of
D_{q,0}^{\alpha}f(t)
and
{}^{c}D_{q}^{\alpha}f(t)
instead of
{}^{c}D_{q,0}^{\alpha}f(t)
, respectively.

Under a certain condition, the following relations between the Caputo type fractional
q
-derivative and the Riemann-Liouville type fractional
q
-derivative hold
[
1
,
21
]

{}^{c}D_{q}^{\alpha}f(t)=D_{q}^{\alpha}\Big(f(t)-\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}\frac{D_{q}^{j}f(0)}{\Gamma_{q}(j+1)}t^{j}\Big),\;n=\lceil\alpha\rceil,\,\alpha>0.

(19)

In particular, for
0<\alpha<1
,

{}^{c}D_{q}^{\alpha}f(t)=D_{q}^{\alpha}f(t)-\frac{t^{-\alpha}}{\Gamma_{q}(1-\alpha)}f(0).

(20)

3. THE
L_{1,q}
DIFFERENCE FORMULA

In this section, we establish a difference formula (called the
L_{1,q}
formula) for discretizing the Caputo type fractional
q
-derivative
{}^{c}D_{q}^{\alpha}x(t)
with
0<\alpha,q<1
and give the truncation error boundness.

Let us consider the Caputo type fractional
q
-derivative:

{}^{c}D_{q}^{\alpha}x(t)=\frac{1}{\Gamma_{q}(1-\alpha)}\int_{0}^{t}(t-qs)^{(-\alpha)}D_{q}x(s)d_{q}s,\,0<\alpha,q<1,\;0<t\leq b,\,t\in T_{q,b}.

(21)

We need to construct a difference formula to approximate
{}^{c}D_{q}^{\alpha}x(t)
on the discrete point set
\{t_{k}\}\subset T_{q,b}
where the time scale set
T_{q,b}=\{bq^{n}:n=0,1,\dots\}\bigcup\{0\}
.

Let
0=t_{0}<t_{1}<\cdots<t_{N}=b
be a nonuniform partition of
[0,b]
with mesh point
t_{k}=bq^{N-k}\in T_{q,b}
and step sizes
\Delta t_{k}=t_{k}-t_{k-1},\,1\leq k\leq N
where
N\geq 1
is a positive integer. Introduce the piecewise linear interpolation of function
x(s)
:

L_{1,k}(s)=\frac{s-t_{k-1}}{\Delta t_{k}}x(t_{k})+\frac{t_{k}-s}{\Delta t_{k}}x(t_{k-1}),\,s\in[t_{k-1},t_{k}],\,k=1,2,...,N,

(22)

with the interpolation error

R_{k}(s)=x(s)-L_{1,k}(s),s\in[t_{k-1},t_{k}],\;k=1,2,...,N.

(23)

Since

{}^{c}D_{q}^{\alpha}x(t_{n})=\frac{1}{\Gamma_{q}(1-\alpha)}\sum_{k=1}^{n}\int_{t_{k-1}}^{t_{k}}(t_{n}-qs)^{(-\alpha)}D_{q}x(s)d_{q}s,

(24)

replacing
D_{q}x(s)
by
D_{q}L_{1,k}x(s)
in (
24
) and noting that
D_{q}L_{1,k}x(s)=(x(t_{k})-x(t_{k-1}))/\triangle t_{k}
, we obtain

{}^{c}D_{q}^{\alpha}x(t_{n})=\frac{1}{\Gamma_{q}(1-\alpha)}\sum_{k=1}^{n}b_{k}\big(x(t_{k})-x(t_{k-1})\big)+R^{n}_{q},

(25)

\displaystyle b_{k}=\frac{1}{\triangle t_{k}}\int_{t_{k-1}}^{t_{k}}(t_{n}-qs)^{(-\alpha)}d_{q}s,\;\,k=1,2,\dots,n\,,

(26)

where the truncation error

R^{n}_{q}=\frac{1}{\Gamma_{q}(1-\alpha)}\sum_{k=1}^{n}\int_{t_{k-1}}^{t_{k}}(t_{n}-qs)^{(-\alpha)}D_{q}R_{k}(s)d_{q}s.

(27)

Thus, we derive the
L_{1,q}
difference formula:

\triangle_{q}^{\alpha}x^{n}\doteq\frac{1}{\Gamma_{q}(1-\alpha)}\sum_{k=1}^{n}b_{k}\big(x^{k}-x^{k-1}),\;\;^{c}D_{q}^{\alpha}x(t_{n})=\triangle^{\alpha}_{q}x(t_{n})+R^{n}_{q}\,,

(28)

where
x^{k}
is any one mesh function defined on
\{t_{k}\}
and when
x(t)
is continuous on
[0,t_{N}]
, we set
x^{k}=x(t_{k})
.

Remark 1.
In constructing the difference scheme, we used
D_{q}L_{1,k}(t)
to approximate the integrand function
D_{q}x(t)
on interval
[t_{k-1},t_{k}]
in (20). Since (noting that the
q
-mesh point
t_{k-1}=qt_{k}
)

D_{q}L_{1,k}(t)=\frac{x(t_{k})-x(t_{k-1})}{t_{k}-t_{k-1}}=\frac{x(t_{k})-x(qt_{k})}{(1-q)t_{k}}=D_{q}x(t_{k}),\;t\in[t_{k-1},t_{k}],

so actually, we used the
q
-rectangle quadrature formula to discretize the integral on
[t_{k-1},t_{k}]
.

Below we estimate the truncation error
R^{n}_{q}
. We first give several lemmas.

Lemma 2.
(
q
-Rolle lemma
[
28
]
)
Let
x(t)
be a continuous function on
[a,b]
and
x(a)=x(b)
. Then, for
q\in(0,1)
, there exists point
\xi\in(a,b)
such that
D_{q}x(\xi)=0
.

By means of Lemma 2, we can establish the following interpolation error formula.

Lemma 3.

Assume that
x(s)
and
D_{q}x(s)
is continuous on
[t_{k-1},t_{k}]
. Then, the linear interpolation error function
R_{k}(s)=x(s)-L_{1,k}(s)
has the following expression

R_{k}(s)=\frac{1}{1+q}D_{q}^{2}x(\xi_{k})(s-t_{k-1})(s-t_{k}),s\in[t_{k-1},t_{k}],\,\xi_{k}\in(t_{k-1},t_{k}),\,1\leq k\leq N.

Proof.
Since
R_{k}(t_{k-1})=R_{k}(t_{k})=0
, we may assume that

R_{k}(s)=K_{q}(s)(s-t_{k-1})(s-t_{k}),\,s\in[t_{k-1},t_{k}],

(29)

where
K_{q}(s)
is an undetermined function. For any fixed point
s\in(t_{k-1},t_{k})
, let
\varphi_{q}(t)=x(t)-L_{1,k}(t)-K_{q}(s)(t-t_{k-1})(t-t_{k})
. Since
\varphi_{q}(t_{k-1})=\varphi_{q}(t_{k})=\varphi_{q}(s)=0
, by using the
q
-Rolle lemma twice, we know that
D_{q}^{2}\varphi_{q}(t)
has at least one zero point
\xi_{k}\in(t_{k-1},t_{k})
. So

\displaystyle D_{q}^{2}\varphi_{q}(\xi_{k})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle D^{2}_{q}\Big(x(t)-L_{1,k}(t)-K_{q}(s)(t-t_{k-1})(t-t_{k})\Big)\Big|_{t=\xi_{k}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle D_{q}^{2}x(\xi_{k})-(1+q)K_{q}(s)=0.

Hence, we obtain
K_{q}(s)=D_{q}^{2}x(\xi_{k})/(1+q)
. Substituting
K_{q}(s)
into (
29
), the proof is completed.
\hfill\Box

Lemma 4.

Let
0<\alpha,q<1
and
D_{q}
be the
q
-derivative operator with respect to variable
s
. Then

\displaystyle D_{q}(t-s)^{(-\alpha)}=-[-\alpha]_{q}(t-qs)^{(-\alpha-1)},\,0\leq s\leq t,

(30)

\displaystyle|(t-qs)^{(-\alpha-1)}|\leq t^{-\alpha-1}\frac{1}{1-q^{\alpha}}\frac{1}{1-q^{1-\alpha}},\;0\leq s\leq t.

(31)

Proof.
We first prove conclusion (
30
). By the definition of operator
D_{q}
and (
2
), we have

\displaystyle D_{q}(t-s)^{(-\alpha)}=\frac{(t-qs)^{(-\alpha)}-(t-s)^{(-\alpha)}}{(q-1)s}

(32)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{t^{-\alpha}}{(q-1)s}\lim_{m\rightarrow\infty}S_{m},\;S_{m}=\prod_{i=0}^{m}\frac{(t-q^{i+1}s)}{(t-q^{i+1-\alpha}s)}-\prod_{i=0}^{m}\frac{(t-q^{i}s)}{(t-q^{i-\alpha}s)}.

By a straightforward computation, it yields

\displaystyle S_{m}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\prod_{i=1}^{m}\frac{(t-q^{i}s)}{(t-q^{i-\alpha}s)}\Big[\frac{(t-q^{m+1}s)}{(t-q^{m+1-\alpha}s)}-\frac{(t-s)}{(t-q^{-\alpha})s}\Big]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\prod_{i=1}^{m}\frac{(t-q^{i}s)}{(t-q^{i-\alpha}s)}\Big[\frac{st(1-q^{-\alpha})(1-q^{m+1})}{(t-q^{m+1-\alpha}s)(t-q^{-\alpha}s)}\Big]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\prod_{i=0}^{m}\frac{(t-q^{i+1}s)}{(t-q^{i-\alpha}s)}\Big[\frac{st(1-q^{-\alpha})(1-q^{m+1})}{(t-q^{m+1-\alpha}s)(t-q^{m+1}s)}\Big]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\prod_{i=0}^{\infty}\frac{(t-q^{i+1}s)}{(t-q^{i-\alpha}s)}\Big[\frac{s(1-q^{-\alpha})}{t}\Big],\;m\rightarrow\infty.

Substituting this into (
32
), we obtain

D_{q}(t-s)^{(-\alpha)}=\frac{(1-q^{-\alpha})}{(q-1)}t^{-\alpha-1}\prod_{i=0}^{\infty}\frac{(t-q^{i+1}s)}{(t-q^{i-\alpha}s)}=-[-\alpha]_{q}(t-qs)^{(-\alpha-1)}.

Next, we consider estimation (
31
). Since

\displaystyle(t-qs)^{(-\alpha-1)}=t^{-\alpha-1}\lim_{m\rightarrow\infty}S_{m}^{\prime},\;S^{\prime}_{m}=\prod_{i=0}^{m}\frac{(t-q^{i+1}s)}{(t-q^{i-\alpha}s)},

(33)

and

\displaystyle\max_{0\leq s\leq t}\big|\frac{(t-qs)}{(t-q^{-\alpha}s)}\big|=\max\big\{1,\big|\frac{1-q}{1-q^{-\alpha}}\big|\big\}\leq\frac{1-q}{1-q^{\alpha}},

\displaystyle\max_{0\leq s\leq t}\frac{(t-q^{i+1}s)}{(t-q^{i-\alpha}s)}=\frac{(1-q^{i+1})}{(1-q^{i-\alpha})},\;i\geq 1,

so

\displaystyle|S^{\prime}_{m}|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{1-q}{1-q^{\alpha}}\prod_{i=1}^{m}\frac{(1-q^{i+1})}{(1-q^{i-\alpha})}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{1-q^{\alpha}}\frac{1}{1-q^{1-\alpha}}\frac{1-q}{1-q^{2-\alpha}}\frac{1-q^{2}}{1-q^{3-\alpha}}\cdots\frac{1-q^{m-1}}{1-q^{m-\alpha}}\frac{1-q^{m}}{1}\frac{1-q^{m+1}}{1}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{1-q^{\alpha}}\frac{1}{1-q^{1-\alpha}}.

Substituting this into (
33
), the proof is completed.
\hfill\Box

Now we can give the truncation error estimation.

Theorem 1.

Let
x(t)
and
D_{q}x(t)
are continuous on
[0,b]
. Then, the truncation error of the
L_{1,q}
difference formula (
28
) satisfies the following estimate

|R_{q}^{n}|\leq\frac{1}{4\Gamma_{q}(1-\alpha)}\frac{1}{1-q^{2}}\frac{1}{q^{\alpha}-q}\,t_{n}^{-\alpha}\triangle t_{n}^{2}\max_{0\leq t\leq t_{n}}|D_{q}^{2}x(t)|,\;\,n\geq 1.

(34)

Proof.
Let
\bar{R}(s)=R_{k}(s),\,s\in[t_{k-1},t_{k}],\,1\leq k\leq N
. From (
27
), the
q
-integration by parts formula (
10
) and Lemma 4, we obtain

\displaystyle R^{n}_{q}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\Gamma_{q}(1-\alpha)}\sum_{k=1}^{n}\int_{t_{k-1}}^{t_{k}}(t_{n}-qs)^{(-\alpha)}D_{q}R_{k}(s)d_{q}s

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{(t_{n}-qs)^{(-\alpha)}}{\Gamma_{q}(1-\alpha)}R_{k}(s)\big|_{t_{k-1}}^{t^{k}}-\frac{1}{\Gamma_{q}(1-\alpha)}\sum_{k=1}^{n}\int_{t_{k-1}}^{t_{k}}D_{q}(t_{n}-s)^{(-\alpha)}R_{k}(s)d_{q}s

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{[-\alpha]_{q}}{\Gamma_{q}(1-\alpha)}\int_{0}^{t_{n}}(t_{n}-qs)^{(-\alpha-1)}\bar{R}(s)d_{q}s.

Hence, using (
7
)–(
8
), Lemma 3 and (
31
) it yields

\displaystyle|R_{q}^{n}|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{|[-\alpha]_{q}|}{\Gamma_{q}(1-\alpha)}\int_{0}^{t_{n}}|(t_{n}-qs)^{(-\alpha-1)}\bar{R}(s)|d_{q}s

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{|[-\alpha]_{q}|}{\Gamma_{q}(1-\alpha)}\sum_{k=1}^{n}\int_{t_{k-1}}^{t_{k}}|(t_{n}-qs)^{(-\alpha-1)}R_{k}(s)|d_{q}s

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{|[-\alpha]_{q}|}{\Gamma_{q}(1-\alpha)}\frac{1}{1+q}\frac{1}{4}\max_{1\leq k\leq n}|\triangle t_{k}|^{2}\max_{0\leq t\leq t_{n}}|D_{q}^{2}x(t)|\int_{0}^{t_{n}}|(t_{n}-qs)^{(-\alpha-1)}|d_{q}s

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{|[-\alpha]_{q}|}{\Gamma_{q}(1-\alpha)}\frac{1}{1+q}\frac{1}{4}\max_{1\leq k\leq n}|\triangle t_{k}|^{2}\max_{0\leq t\leq t_{n}}|D_{q}^{2}x(t)|t_{n}^{-\alpha}\frac{1}{1-q^{\alpha}}\frac{1}{1-q^{1-\alpha}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\Gamma_{q}(1-\alpha)}\frac{q^{-\alpha}}{1-q^{2}}\frac{1}{4}\frac{1}{1-q^{1-\alpha}}t_{n}^{-\alpha}\triangle t_{n}^{2}\max_{0\leq t\leq t_{n}}|D_{q}^{2}x(t)|\,.

This gives the desired estimate.

4. STABILITY OF THE
L_{1,q}
DIFFERENCE FORMULA

We first give two useful lemmas.

Lemma 5.

For
0<q,\alpha<1
, the following inequality holds

t_{n}^{-\alpha}<(t_{n}-q^{i+1}s)^{(-\alpha)}\leq(t_{n}-qs)^{(-\alpha)},\;i\geq 0,\;0\leq s\leq t_{n}.

(35)

Proof.
For the left side inequality, since
(t_{n}-q^{i+j+1}s)/(t_{n}-q^{i+j+1-\alpha}s)>1
for any
i,j\geq 0
, we have

(t_{n}-q^{i+1}s)^{(-\alpha)}=t_{n}^{-\alpha}\prod_{j=0}^{\infty}\frac{(t_{n}-q^{i+j+1}s)}{(t_{n}-q^{i+j+1-\alpha}s)}>t_{n}^{-\alpha},\,i\geq 0,\,0\leq s\leq t_{n}.

Next, by a straightforward computation, it yields

\displaystyle(t_{n}-q^{i+1}s)^{(-\alpha)}-(t_{n}-qs)^{(-\alpha)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle t_{n}^{-\alpha}\prod_{j=0}^{\infty}\frac{(t_{n}-q^{i+j+1}s)}{(t_{n}-q^{i+j+1-\alpha}s)}-t_{n}^{-\alpha}\prod_{j=0}^{\infty}\frac{(t_{n}-q^{j+1}s)}{(t_{n}-q^{j+1-\alpha}s)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle t_{n}^{-\alpha}\prod_{j=i}^{\infty}\frac{(t_{n}-q^{j+1}s)}{(t_{n}-q^{j+1-\alpha}s)}\Big(1-\prod_{j=0}^{i-1}\frac{(t_{n}-q^{j+1}s)}{(t_{n}-q^{j+1-\alpha}s)}\Big)<0,\,i\geq 1.

The proof is completed.
\hfill\Box

Lemma 6.

Let coefficient series
\{b_{k}\}
be defined by (
26
). Then, it holds

\displaystyle b_{k}=(t_{n}-qt_{k})^{(-\alpha)},\;k\geq 2,

(36)

\displaystyle t_{n}^{-\alpha}<b_{1}<b_{2}<\cdots<b_{k-1}<b_{k},\;2\leq k\leq n\,.

(37)

Proof.
Since
\triangle t_{k}=t_{k}-t_{k-1}=t_{k}(1-q),\,t_{k-1}=qt_{k},\,k\geq 2
, then by (
26
) we have

\displaystyle b_{k}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\triangle t_{k}}\int_{t_{k-1}}^{t_{k}}(t_{n}-qs)^{(-\alpha)}d_{q}s

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\triangle t_{k}}\int_{0}^{t_{k}}(t_{n}-qs)^{(-\alpha)}d_{q}s-\frac{1}{\triangle t_{k}}\int^{t_{k-1}}_{0}(t_{n}-qs)^{(-\alpha)}d_{q}s

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(1-q)\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}\frac{t_{k}}{\triangle t_{k}}q^{i}(t_{n}-q^{i+1}t_{k})^{(-\alpha)}-(1-q)\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}\frac{t_{k-1}}{\triangle t_{k}}q^{i}(t_{n}-q^{i+1}t_{k-1})^{(-\alpha)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}q^{i}(t_{n}-q^{i+1}t_{k})^{(-\alpha)}-\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}q^{i+1}(t_{n}-q^{i+2}t_{k})^{(-\alpha)}=(t_{n}-qt_{k})^{(-\alpha)}.

This gives equality (
36
). For (
37
), we first obtain from Lemma 5

\displaystyle b_{1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\triangle t_{1}}\int_{0}^{t_{1}}(t_{n}-qs)^{(-\alpha)}d_{q}s=(1-q)\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}\frac{t_{1}}{\triangle t_{1}}q^{i}(t_{n}-q^{i+1}t_{1})^{(-\alpha)}

(38)

\displaystyle>

\displaystyle t_{n}^{-\alpha}(1-q)\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}q^{i}=t_{n}^{-\alpha},

and

\displaystyle b_{1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(1-q)\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}\frac{t_{1}}{\triangle t_{1}}q^{i}(t_{n}-q^{i+1}t_{1})^{(-\alpha)}

(39)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle(1-q)\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}q^{i}(t_{n}-qt_{1})^{(-\alpha)}=(t_{n}-qt_{1})^{(-\alpha)}.

Next, it follows from (
36
), (
39
) and Lemma 5

b_{2}-b_{1}\geq(t_{n}-qt_{2})^{(-\alpha)}-(t_{n}-qt_{1})^{(-\alpha)}=(t_{n}-qt_{2})^{(-\alpha)}-(t_{n}-q^{2}t_{2})^{(-\alpha)}>0.

(40)

Moreover, from (
36
) and Lemma 5, it yields

\displaystyle b_{k}-b_{k-1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(t_{n}-qt_{k})^{(-\alpha)}-(t_{n}-qt_{k-1})^{(-\alpha)}

(41)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(t_{n}-qt_{k})^{(-\alpha)}-(t_{n}-q^{2}t_{k})^{(-\alpha)}>0,\;k\geq 3.

Combining (
38
), (
40
) and (
41
), estimation (
37
) is derived.
\hfill\Box

Now let us consider the stability of the
L_{1,q}
difference formula (
28
). For this end, we consider the following difference equation:

\triangle_{q}^{\alpha}x^{n}=f^{n},\;n=1,2,\dots,N,\;\;\;\triangle_{q}^{\alpha}x^{n}\doteq\frac{1}{\Gamma_{q}(1-\alpha)}\sum_{k=1}^{n}b_{k}(x^{k}-x^{k-1}),

(42)

where the initial value
x^{0}
and the source term
f^{n}
are given. Using the identity

\sum_{k=1}^{n}b_{k}(x^{k}-x^{k-1})=b_{n}x^{n}+\sum_{k=1}^{n-1}(b_{k}-b_{k+1})x^{k}-b_{1}x^{0},

difference equation (
42
) can be rewritten as

b_{n}x^{n}=b_{1}x^{0}+\sum_{k=1}^{n-1}(b_{k+1}-b_{k})x^{k}+\Gamma_{q}(1-\alpha)f^{n},\;n\geq 1\,.

(43)

Theorem 2.

The
L_{1,q}
difference formula is unconditionally stable such that the solution of difference equation (
43
) satisfies

|x^{n}|\leq|x^{0}|+\Gamma_{q}(1-\alpha)t_{n}^{\alpha}\max_{1\leq k\leq n}|f^{k}|,\;n\geq 1.

Proof.
It follows from (
43
) and Lemma 6 that

\displaystyle b_{n}|x^{n}|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle b_{1}|x^{0}|+\sum_{k=1}^{n-1}(b_{k+1}-b_{k})\max_{1\leq k\leq n}|x^{k}|+\Gamma_{q}(1-\alpha)|f^{n}|

(44)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle b_{1}|x^{0}|+(b_{n}-b_{1})\max_{1\leq k\leq n}|x^{k}|+\Gamma_{q}(1-\alpha)|f^{n}|\,.

Let
1\leq n_{0}\leq n
be such that
|x^{n_{0}}|=\displaystyle{\max_{1\leq k\leq n}}|x^{k}|
. Taking
n=n_{0}
in (
44
), it yields

b_{1}\max_{1\leq k\leq n}|x^{n}|\leq b_{1}|x^{0}|+\Gamma_{q}(1-\alpha)\max_{1\leq k\leq n}|f^{k}|.

Hence, using (
37
), we complete the proof.
\hfill\Box

5. DIFFERENCE METHOD FOR THE
q
-FRACTIONAL

DIFFERENTIAL EQUATION

Consider the initial value problem of
q
-fractional differential equation:

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{lll}&{}^{c}D_{q}^{\alpha}x(t)=f(t,x(t)),\;0<t\leq b,\;0<q,\,\alpha<1,\;t\in T_{q,b},\\
&x(0)=x^{0},\end{array}\right.

where
f:[0,b]\times R^{d}\rightarrow R^{d}
is continuous,
d\geq 1
. Let the partition of interval
[0,b]
be given as that in Section 3. By means of the
L_{1,q}
difference formula, we establish the difference method for solving problem (
5. DIFFERENCE METHOD FOR THE
q
-FRACTIONAL

DIFFERENTIAL EQUATION
) as follows (see (
43
))

\displaystyle b_{n}x^{n}=b_{1}x^{0}+\sum_{k=1}^{n-1}(b_{k+1}-b_{k})x^{k}+\Gamma_{q}(1-\alpha)f(t_{n},x^{n}),\;n=1,2,\dots,N.

(48)

We first consider the unique existence of the difference solution.

Theorem 3.

Assume that
f:[0,b]\times R^{d}\rightarrow R^{d}
is continuous and satisfies the Lipschitz condition:

|f(t,x)-f(t,x^{\prime})|\leq L|x-x^{\prime}|,\;x,x^{\prime}\in R^{d},\;L_{1}=L\Gamma_{q}(1-\alpha)b^{\alpha}<1.

(49)

Then, difference equation (
48
) has a unique solution.

Proof.
We only need to prove that for any fixed
n\geq 1
, when
\{x^{k}\}_{k=0}^{n-1}
are known, there exists a unique
x^{n}
satisfying Eq. (
48
). Consider the iteration scheme:

b_{n}x^{n,l}=b_{1}x^{0}+\sum_{k=1}^{n-1}(b_{k+1}-b_{k})x^{k}+\Gamma_{q}(1-\alpha)f(t_{n},x^{n,l-1}),\,l=1,2,\dots,\;x^{n,0}=x^{n-1}.

(50)

Noting that
b_{n}\geq t_{n}^{-\alpha}\geq b^{-\alpha}
, we obtain from (
49
)–(
50
) that

\displaystyle|x^{n,l+1}-x^{n,l}|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle b_{n}^{-1}\Gamma_{q}(1-\alpha)|f(t_{n},x^{n,l})-f(t_{n},x^{n,l-1})|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle L_{1}|x^{n,l}-x^{n,l-1}|\leq\cdots\leq L_{1}^{l}|x^{n,1}-x^{n,0}|,\;l\geq 1.

Since
L_{1}<1
, this implies that the series
\{x^{n,l}\}
is convergent. Letting
l\rightarrow\infty
in Eq. (
50
), we see that
x^{n}\doteq x^{n,\infty}
is the solution of difference equation (
48
). Now for fixed
n
, assume that Eq. (
48
) has two solutions
x^{n}
and
\bar{x}^{n}
. Then,
x^{n}
and
\bar{x}^{n}
satisfy

|x^{n}-\bar{x}^{n}|=b_{n}^{-1}\Gamma_{q}(1-\alpha)|f(t_{n},x^{n})-f(t_{n},\bar{x}^{n})|\leq L_{1}|x^{n}-\bar{x}^{n}|<|x^{n}-\bar{x}^{n}|\,.

This implies
x^{n}=\bar{x}^{n}
, the uniqueness is proved.
\hfill\Box

Next, we consider the stability of the difference solution.

Theorem 4.

Assume that condition (
49
) holds. Then, the solution of difference equation (
48
) satisfies the stability estimation

|x^{n}|\leq\frac{1}{1-L_{1}}\big[\,|x^{0}|+\Gamma_{q}(1-\alpha)t_{n}^{\alpha}\max_{1\leq k\leq n}|f(t_{k},0)|\,\big],\;n\geq 1\,.

Proof.
Let
x^{n}
be the solution of Eq. (
48
). By a similar argument to that of Theorem 2, we have

|x^{n}|\leq|x^{0}|+\Gamma_{q}(1-\alpha)t_{n}^{\alpha}\max_{1\leq k\leq n}|f(t_{k},x^{k})|,\;n\geq 1.

(51)

Using condition (
49
), it yields

|f(t_{k},x^{k})|=|f(t_{k},x^{k})-f(t_{k},0)|+|f(t_{k},0)|\leq L|x_{k}|+|f(t_{k},0)|\,.

Combining this with (
51
), we arrive at

|x^{n}|\leq|x^{0}|+L_{1}\max_{1\leq k\leq n}|x^{k}|+\Gamma_{q}(1-\alpha)t_{n}^{\alpha}\max_{1\leq k\leq n}|f(t_{k},0)|,\,n\geq 1\,.

(52)

Now, let
1\leq n_{0}\leq n
be such that
|x^{n_{0}}|=\displaystyle{\max_{1\leq k\leq n}}|x^{k}|
. Then, taking
n=n_{0}
in (
52
), we can derive

(1-L_{1})\max_{1\leq k\leq n}|x^{k}|\leq|x^{0}|+\Gamma_{q}(1-\alpha)t_{n}^{\alpha}\max_{1\leq k\leq n}|f(t_{k},0)|,\;n\geq 1\,.

This gives the desired estimation.
\hfill\Box

Finally, we can give the error estimation.

Theorem 5.

Let
x(t)
and
x^{n}
be the solution of Eqs. (

q
-FRACTIONAL

DIFFERENTIAL EQUATION
) and (
48
), respectively. Assume that
x(t)
and

are continuous on
[0,b]
and condition (
49
) holds. Then, we have the following error estimation.

|x(t_{n})-x^{n}|\leq\frac{1}{1-L_{1}}\frac{1}{4}\frac{1}{1-q^{2}}\frac{1}{q^{\alpha}-q}\triangle t_{n}^{2}\max_{0\leq t\leq t_{n}}|D_{q}^{2}x(t)|,\;n\geq 1.

(53)

Proof.
From Eq. (
5. DIFFERENCE METHOD FOR THE
q
-FRACTIONAL

DIFFERENTIAL EQUATION
), we see that the solution
x(t)
satisfies the discrete equation:
\triangle_{q}^{\alpha}x(t_{n})=f(t_{n},x(t_{n}))-R_{q}^{n}
, or

b_{n}x(t_{n})=b_{1}x^{0}+\sum_{k=1}^{n-1}(b_{k+1}-b_{k})x(t_{k})+\Gamma_{q}(1-\alpha)f(t_{n},x(t_{n}))-\Gamma_{q}(1-\alpha)R_{q}^{n},

(54)

where the truncation error
R_{q}^{n}=^{c}\!D_{q}^{\alpha}x(t_{n})-\triangle_{q}^{\alpha}x(t_{n})
is given by (
27
). Let the error function
e_{n}=x^{n}-x(t_{n})
. From Eqs. (

) and (

), we obtain

\displaystyle b_{n}e_{n}=\sum_{k=1}^{n-1}(b_{k+1}-b_{k})e_{k}+\Gamma_{q}(1-\alpha)\big(f(t_{n},x^{n})-f(t_{n},x(t_{n}))\big)+\Gamma_{q}(1-\alpha)R_{q}^{n}\,.

(55)

Hence, using condition (

), it yields

Substituting this into (

The proof is completed, noting that
\displaystyle{\max_{1\leq k\leq n}}\triangle t_{k}=\triangle t_{n},\,t_{n}=\triangle t_{n}/(1-q)

7. CONCLUSION

An unconditionally stable difference formula is first presented to discretize the fractional
q
-derivative
{}^{c}D^{\alpha}_{q}x(t)
of Caputo type with
0<q,\,\alpha<1
on the time scale set
T_{q,b}
. The rigours truncation error boundness is derived. This difference formula can be used to solve the
q
-fractional differential equation:
{}^{c}D^{\alpha}_{q}x(t)=f(t,x(t))
on set
T_{q,b}
. We prove the unique existence and stability of the difference solution and derive an error estimate of
\triangle t_{n}^{2}
-order. Numerical experiments show the effectiveness and high accuracy of this difference method. This difference formula provides a useful tool for solving numerically the
q
-fractional problems on the time scale set.

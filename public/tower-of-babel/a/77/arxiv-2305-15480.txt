Non-Abelian transport distinguishes three usually equivalent notions of entropy production
Twesh Upadhyaya, William F. Braasch, Jr, Gabriel T. Landi, Nicole Yunger Halpern

Abstract
We extend entropy production to a deeply quantum regime involving noncommuting conserved quantities. Consider a unitary transporting conserved quantities (“charges”) between two systems initialized in thermal states. Three common formulae model the entropy produced. They respectively cast entropy as an extensive thermodynamic variable, as an information-theoretic uncertainty measure, and as a quantifier of irreversibility. Often, the charges are assumed to commute with each other (e.g., energy and particle number). Yet quantum charges can fail to commute. Noncommutation invites generalizations, which we posit and justify, of the three formulae. Charges’ noncommutation, we find, breaks the formulae’s equivalence. Furthermore, different formulae quantify different physical effects of charges’ noncommutation on entropy production. For instance, entropy production can signal contextuality—true nonclassicality—by becoming nonreal. This work opens up stochastic thermodynamics to noncommuting—and so particularly quantum—charges.

To accommodate noncommuting charges, we generalize the SEP.
In conventional quantum thermodynamics, three common SEP formulae equal each other

by a “surprisal formula,” and as quantifying irreversibility by a “trajectory formula.”
We generalize all three formulae using KDQs.
If the charges commute, the generalizations reduce to the usual formulae.
The generalizations
satisfy four sanity checks, including by equaling each other when the charges commute.
Yet we find deductively that noncommuting charges break the equivalence, generating three species of SEP.

Different SEPs, we find, highlight different ways in which charges’ noncommutation impacts transport:

1.

Charge SEP:
Charges’ noncommutation enables individual stochastic trajectories to violate charge conservation. These violations underlie commutator-dependent corrections to a fluctuation theorem.

2.

Surprisal SEP:
Initial coherences, relative to eigenbases of the charges, enable the average surprisal entropy production to become negative. Such negativity simulates a reversal of time’s arrow. Across thermodynamics, a ubiquitous initial state is a product of thermal states. Only if the charges fail to commute can such a state have the necessary coherences. Hence charges’ noncommutation enables a resource, similar to work, for effectively reversing time’s arrow in a common setup.

3.

Trajectory SEP:
The generalized trajectory SEP can become nonreal, thanks to the KDQ. Such nonreality signals contextuality—a strong form of nonclassicality

Kochen and Specker 1967
;
Spekkens 2005

III A
Charge stochastic entropy production

First, we motivate the charge SEP’s definition (Sec.
III A 1
).
\sigma_{\text{chrg}}
portrays entropy as an extensive thermodynamic quantity (Sec.
III A 2
).
Furthermore,
\sigma_{\text{chrg}}
satisfies a fluctuation theorem whose corrections depend on commutators of the
Q_{\alpha}
’s (Sec.
III A 3
).
Corrections arise because noncommuting charges enable individual stochastic trajectories to violate a “microscopic,” or “detailed,” notion of charge conservation. [Nevertheless, charge conservation as defined in Eq. (
3

III A 1
Charge SEP formula

The fundamental relation of statistical mechanics
Pathria 2001
motivates
\sigma_{\text{chrg}}
’s definition. In this paragraph, we reuse quantum notation (Sec.
II A
) to denote classical objects, for simplicity. The fundamental relation governs large, classical systems
{\tt X}={{\tt A}},{{\tt B}}
that have extensive charges
{Q}^{{\tt X}}_{\alpha}
and intensive parameters
{\beta}^{{\tt X}}_{\alpha}
.
Let an infinitesimal interaction conserve each

{Q}^{{\tt A}}_{\alpha}+{Q}^{{\tt B}}_{\alpha}
.
Since
d{Q}_{\alpha}^{{\tt B}}=-d{Q}_{\alpha}^{{\tt A}}
,
the total entropy changes by

\displaystyle dS^{{\tt A}{\tt B}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\alpha}\left(\beta_{\alpha}^{{\tt A}}\,d{Q}_{\alpha}^{{\tt A}}+\beta_{\alpha}^{{\tt B}}\,d{Q}_{\alpha}^{{\tt B}}\right)

(5)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\alpha}\Delta{\beta}_{\alpha}\,d{Q}_{\alpha}^{{\tt A}}\,.

(6)

According to the second law of thermodynamics,
dS^{{\tt A}{\tt B}}\geq 0
during spontaneous processes
Callen 1985
.
We posit that
\expectationvalue{\schrg}
should assume the form (
6
).
We reverse-engineer such a formula, using the charges’ eigenvalues:

\sigma_{\text{chrg}}\coloneqq\sum_{\alpha}\left[\beta^{\tt A}_{\alpha}\left(\lambda_{\alpha,f^{\tt A}_{\alpha}}-\lambda_{\alpha,i^{\tt A}_{\alpha}}\right)+\beta^{\tt B}_{\alpha}\left(\lambda_{\alpha,f^{\tt B}_{\alpha}}-\lambda_{\alpha,i^{\tt B}_{\alpha}}\right)\right].

(7)

III A 2
Average of charge SEP

By design, the charge SEP averages to

\expectationvalue{\schrg}=\sum_{\alpha}\Delta\beta_{\alpha}\,\Delta\expectationvalue{Q_\alpha}.

(8)

This average is non-negative, equaling a relative entropy
Landi and Paternostro 2021
:

\expectationvalue{\schrg}=D(\rho_{\rm f}||\rho)\geq 0\,.

(9)

This inequality echoes the second-law statement written just below Eq. (
6
).

III A 3
Fluctuation theorem for charge SEP

\sigma_{\text{chrg}}
obeys the fluctuation theorem

\displaystyle\expectationvalue{e^{-\schrg}}

(10)

\displaystyle=\Tr( U^\dagger\left[ e^{-\Delta\beta_1 Q_1^{\as}} \ldots e^{-\Delta\beta_c Q_c^{\as} } \right]
U
\left[ e^{\Delta\beta_c Q_c^{\as}} \ldots e^{\Delta\beta_1 Q_1^{\as}} \right]
\rho)

\displaystyle\quad\;+\left(\text{$c-1$ terms dependent on
commutators}\right).

We prove the theorem and present the correction’s form in App.
C 1
. Below, we show that the right-hand side (RHS) evaluates to 1 in the commuting case. In the noncommuting case, corrections arise from two physical sources: (i)
\rho^{\tt A}
and
\rho^{\tt B}
are non-Abelian thermal states. (ii) Individual stochastic trajectories can violate charge conservation.

In the commuting case, the first term in Eq. (
10
) equals 1. The reason is, in
\rho=\rho_{\rm GGE}^{\tt A}\otimes\rho_{\rm GGE}^{\tt B}
,
each
\rho_{\rm GGE}^{\tt X}\propto\exp\left(-\sum_{\alpha}\beta^{\tt X}_{\alpha}Q_{\alpha}^{\tt X}\right)\propto\prod_{\alpha}\exp( -\beta^{\tt X}_\alpha Q_\alpha^{\tt X} )
.
The
{\tt A}
exponentials cancel the
e^{\beta_{\alpha}^{\tt A}Q_{\alpha}^{\tt A}}
factors in Eq. (
10
).
No such cancellation occurs in the noncommuting case, since
\rho_{\rm GGE}^{\tt X}\not\propto\prod_{\alpha}\exp( -\beta^{\tt X}_\alpha Q_\alpha^{\tt X} )
.
The first term in Eq. (
10
) can therefore deviate from 1, quantifying the noncommutation of the charges in
\rho_{\rm GGE}^{\tt X}
.

The second term vanishes in the commuting case, since all commutators of charges vanish. This second term can deviate from 0 in the noncommuting case, due to
nonconserving trajectories
, which we introduce now. Define a trajectory

(i^{\tt A}_{1},i^{\tt B}_{1},i^{\tt A}_{2},i^{\tt B}_{2},\ldots,i^{\tt B}_{c})

\mapsto

(f^{\tt A}_{c},f^{\tt B}_{c},f^{\tt A}_{c-1},f^{\tt B}_{c-1},\ldots,f^{\tt B}_{1}
)
as
conserving
if the corresponding charge eigenvalues satisfy

\lambda_{\alpha,i^{\tt A}_{\alpha}}+\lambda_{\alpha,i^{\tt B}_{\alpha}}=\lambda_{\alpha,f_{\alpha}^{\tt A}}+\lambda_{\alpha,f_{\alpha}^{\tt B}}\quad\forall\alpha.

(11)

Any trajectory that violates this condition, we call
nonconserving
. Loosely speaking, under (
11
),
{\tt A}{\tt B}
has the same amount of
\alpha
-type charge at the trajectory’s start and end.

In the commuting case,
\tilde{p}_{\rm F}=0

when evaluated on nonconserving trajectories. We call this vanishing
detailed charge conservation
. Earlier work has relied on detailed charge conservation
Allahverdyan 2014
;
Levy and Lostaglio 2020
.
In the noncommuting case,
\tilde{p}_{\rm F}
need not vanish when evaluated on nonconserving trajectories (App.
C 2
).
The mathematical reason is, different charges’ eigenprojectors in
\tilde{p}_{\rm F}
[Eq. (
II B
)] can fail to commute.
A physical interpretation is that measuring any
Q_{\alpha}^{\tt X}
disturbs any later measurements of noncommuting
Q_{\alpha^{\prime}}^{\tt X}
’s. Nonconserving trajectories resemble classically forbidden trajectories in the path-integral formulation of quantum mechanics.

We now show that nonconserving trajectories underlie the final term in Eq. (
10
).
Every stochastic function
g(i_{1},i_{2},\ldots,i_{c}\,;\,f_{c},f_{c-1},\ldots,f_{1})
averages to

\expectationvalue{g}=\expectationvalue{g}_{\text{cons}}+\expectationvalue{g}_{\text{noncons}}
.
Each term equals an average over just the conserving or nonconserving trajectories. For example, the average (
8
) decomposes as
\expectationvalue{\schrg}=\expectationvalue{\schrg}_{\text{cons}}+\expectationvalue{\schrg}_{\text{noncons}}
.
The second term on the RHS of Eq. (
10
) takes the form
\expectationvalue{g}_{\text{noncons}}
(App.
C 2 iii
).
In the commuting case, all trajectories are conserving, so the second term equals zero—as expected, since the term depends on commutators
[Q_{\alpha}^{\tt A},Q_{\alpha^{\prime}}^{\tt A}]
.
Therefore, the fluctuation theorem’s final term (second correction) stems from violations of detailed charge conservation. The average (
8
) contains contributions from conserving and nonconserving trajectories.

We have identified two corrections to the fluctuation theorem (
10
). On the equation’s RHS, the first term can deviate from 1, and the second term can deviate from 0. The first deviation originates in noncommutation of the charges in
\rho^{\tt X}_{\rm GGE}
. The second deviation stems from nonconserving trajectories. An example illustrates the distinction between noncommuting charges’ influences on initial conditions and dynamical trajectories.
Let
c=3
, and let

[Q_{1},Q_{2}]\neq 0
, while
[Q_{1},Q_{3}],[Q_{2},Q_{3}]=0
.
Let the noncommuting charges correspond to uniform

\beta
’s:

\beta^{\tt A}_{1}=\beta^{\tt B}_{1}\,,\quad\text{and}\quad\beta^{\tt A}_{2}=\beta^{\tt B}_{2}\,.

Thus, there exists no temperature gradient that directly drives noncommuting-charge currents. Accordingly, the fluctuation theorem’s first term equals 1. Yet the second term—an average over nonconserving trajectories—is nonzero.

III B
Surprisal stochastic entropy production

\sigma_{\text{surp}}
casts entropy as missing information (Sec.
III B 1
). The average
\langle\sigma_{\text{surp}}\rangle
can be expressed in terms of relative entropies (Sec.
III B 2
), as one might expect from precedent
Landi and Paternostro 2021
. Yet initial coherences, relative to charges’ product bases, can render
\langle\sigma_{\text{surp}}\rangle
negative. If
\rho
is a product
\rho^{\tt A}_{\rm GGE}\otimes\rho^{\tt B}_{\rm GGE}
—as across much of thermodynamics—
\rho
has such coherences only if the
Q_{\alpha}
’s fail to commute. Furthermore, positive
\langle\sigma_{\text{surp}}\rangle
values accompany time’s arrow. Hence charges’ noncommutation enables a resource, similar to work, for effecting a seeming reversal of time’s arrow. Charges’ noncommutation also engenders a correction to the
\sigma_{\text{surp}}
fluctuation theorem (Sec.
III B 3
).

III B 1
Surprisal SEP formula

Information theory motivates the surprisal SEP formula. Averaging the surprisal
-\log(p_j)
yields
the Shannon entropy (Sec.
II C
), so the surprisal is a stochastic (associated-with-one-trial) entropic quantity. A difference of two surprisals forms our
\sigma_{\text{surp}}
formula. The probabilities follow from preparing
\rho
and projectively measuring the
\alpha^{\rm th}
product basis, for any
\alpha
. Outcome

i_{\alpha}\coloneqq(i_{\alpha}^{\tt A},i_{\alpha}^{\tt B})

obtains with a probability

p_{\alpha}(i_{\alpha}^{\tt A},i_{\alpha}^{\tt B})\coloneqq\Tr( \Pi_{\alpha, i_\alpha} \rho)
;
and outcome

f_{\alpha}\coloneqq(f_{\alpha}^{\tt A},f_{\alpha}^{\tt B})
,
with a probability

p_{\alpha}(f_{\alpha}^{\tt A},f_{\alpha}^{\tt B})\coloneqq\Tr( \Pi_{\alpha, f_\alpha} \rho)
.
Appendix
D 1
shows how these probabilities generalize those in the conventional surprisal-SEP formula
Landi and Paternostro 2021
. The surprisal SEP quantifies the information gained if we expect to observe
i_{\alpha}
but we obtain
f_{\alpha}
:

\displaystyle\sigma_{\text{surp}}\coloneqq\log\left(\frac{p_{\alpha}(i^{\tt A}_{\alpha},i^{\tt B}_{\alpha})}{p_{\alpha}(f^{\tt A}_{\alpha},f^{\tt B}_{\alpha})}\right).

(12)

All results below hold for arbitrary
\alpha
. Nevertheless, App.
F
introduces a variation on Eq. (
12
)—an alternative definition that contains an average over all
\alpha
.
Appendix
D 2
confirms that
\sigma_{\text{surp}}
reduces to
\sigma_{\text{chrg}}
if the
Q_{\alpha}
’s commute.

III B 2
Average of surprisal SEP

\langle\sigma_{\text{surp}}\rangle
demonstrates that charges’ noncommutation can enable a seeming reversal of time’s arrow.
Time’s arrow manifests in, e.g., spontaneous flows of heat from hot to cold bodies. This arrow accompanies positive average entropy production. Hence negative
\langle\sigma\rangle

Quantum phenomena, such as entanglement, serve as such resources, too

Jennings and Rudolph 2010

We identify another such resource:

1

\Phi_{\alpha}(\omega)\coloneqq\sum_{k}\Pi_{\alpha,k}\,\omega\,\Pi_{\alpha,k}

\sigma_{\text{surp}}

\hskip-17.07182pt\expectationvalue{\ssurp}=D\bm{(}\rho_{\rm f}||\Phi_{\alpha}\left(\rho\right)\bm{)}-D\bm{(}\rho||\Phi_{\alpha}(\rho)\bm{)}

(App.

\expectationvalue{\ssurp}

\expectationvalue{\ssurp}

Since
\expectationvalue{\schrg}\geq 0
[Eq. (
9

\sigma_{\text{surp}}

\sigma_{\text{chrg}}

This result progresses beyond three existing results. First, coherences engender a correction to a heat-exchange fluctuation theorem

1

\rho^{\tt A}_{\rm GGE}\otimes\rho^{\tt B}_{\rm GGE}

Across thermodynamics,

is ubiquitous as an initial state. Hence noncommuting charges underlie a resource for effectively reversing time’s arrow in a common thermodynamic setup.

Second,
Manzano et al. 2022
shows that noncommuting charges can reduce entropy production in the linear-response regime. Our dynamics
U
can be arbitrarily far from equilibrium.
Third, just as we attribute
\expectationvalue{\ssurp}<0

III B 3
Fluctuation theorem for surprisal SEP

To formulate the fluctuation theorem, we define the coherent difference

\Delta\rho_{\alpha}\coloneqq\Phi_{\alpha}(\rho)^{-1}-\rho^{-1}
.
It quantifies the coherences of
\rho
with respect to the
\alpha^{\rm th}
product basis. If and only if
\rho
is diagonal with respect to this basis,
\Delta\rho_{\alpha}=0
. The surprisal SEP obeys the fluctuation theorem

\displaystyle\expectationvalue{e^{-\ssurp}}=1+\Tr\bm{(}U^{\dagger}\Phi_{\alpha}(\rho)U\,\Delta\rho_{\alpha}\,\rho\bm{)}

(14)

(App.
D 4
). The second term—the correction—arises from
\rho
’s coherences relative to the charges’ product eigenbases. If
\rho=\rho^{\tt A}_{\rm GGE}\otimes\rho^{\tt B}_{\rm GGE}
, as throughout much of thermodynamics, then
\rho
can have such coherences only if charges fail to commute.
Our correction resembles that in
Levy and Lostaglio 2020

but arises from distinct physics: noncommuting charges, rather than initial correlations.

VI
Outlook

Noncommuting charges challenge common expectations about entropy production.
Three common SEP formulae, though equal when charges commute, separate when charges do not.
The formulae also offer different physical insights into how noncommuting charges impact entropy production.
First, the noncommutation enables stochastic trajectories to violate charge conservation individually. We introduce these nonconserving trajectories, possible only if charges fail to commute, as quantum phenomena in stochastic thermodynamics.
The violations of detailed charge conservation underlie commutator-dependent corrections to a fluctuation theorem.
Second, initial coherences relative to charges’ eigenbases can render
\expectationvalue{\ssurp}
negative. A common (two-thermal-reservoir) setup can entail such coherences only if the charges fail to commute. Hence, charges’ noncommutation source a resource that can, in a sense, effectively reverse time’s arrow.
Third, nonreality of
\sigma_{\text{traj}}
signals contextuality—provable nonclassicality—in a noncommuting-current experiment.
Such thermodynamic signatures of contextuality—a stringent criterion for nonclassicality—are rare.
These results hold arbitrarily far from equilibrium.
In addition to proving these results deductively, we illustrated them numerically and sketched a trapped-ion test.

Our work introduces noncommuting charges into stochastic thermodynamics
Strasberg 2022
. The field’s results now merit reevaluation, in case charges’ noncommutation alters them.
For example, thermodynamic uncertainty relations bound a current’s relative variance with entropy production
Barato and Seifert 2015
;
Gingrich et al. 2016
;
Timpanaro et al. 2019
;
Horowitz and Gingrich 2020
. Lowering entropy production, noncommuting charges may increase the relative variance, increasing currents’ unpredictability. Our work therefore motivates the derivation of thermodynamic uncertainty relations that highlight exchanges of noncommuting charges, using KDQs. Such relations may follow as extensions of
Timpanaro et al. 2019
.

As another avenue opened by our work within stochastic thermodynamics, nonconserving trajectories provide a new tool for unearthing genuinely quantum effects. Consider any charge exchange modeled with a KDQ. The KDQ decomposes into a conserving and nonconserving part. The former is the only piece that survives when the process has a classical (probabilistic) description. The average of every stochastic physical variable (analogous to entropy production), with respect to the KDQ, decomposes likewise. Hence the contribution of charge noncommutation to the average can be delineated clearly. For example, this technique could be applied to decompose the averaged stochastic work performed by a monitored quantum engine operating between noncommuting-charge reservoirs.

Quantum engines offer another possible application
Ito and Hayashi 2018b
:
The more entropy an engine produces, the lesser the engine’s efficiency. We showed that charges’ noncommutation can lower entropy production even on average: Coherences relative to charges’ eigenbases serve as a resource for reducing average entropy production (Sec.
III B
). Hence engines might leverage such coherences. Such an engine could exchange not only heat, but also noncommuting charges, with reservoirs. One could extend the engine, proposed in
Wright et al. 2018
, that leverages a spin reservoir in place of a heat reservoir.

To put the charges on even more-equal footing, one could average over them in the KDQ’s and SEPs’ definitions (App.
F
).
Finally, calculating a closed form for the average trajectory SEP, for mixed global states, remains an open problem.

Experimental opportunities complement the theoretical ones.
In Sec.
V
, we sketched a trapped-ion experiment for testing our results. Such a test would highlight the quantum features manifested by SEPs when charges fail to commute.

\displaystyle=\log\left(\frac{\Tr\left(\left[\Pi_{i^{\tt A}}^{\tt A}\otimes\Pi_{i^{\tt B}}^{\tt B}\right]\rho\right)}{\Tr\left([\Pi_{f^{\tt A}}^{\tt A}\otimes\Pi_{f^{\tt B}}^{\tt B}]\rho\right)}\right)

(B1)

\displaystyle=\log\Big(
\exp\LParen\beta^\as\left[ \rank(\Pi_{f^\as} ) \lambda_{f^\as}
- \rank(\Pi_{i^\as}) \lambda_{i^\as} \right] \RParen\;
\exp\LParen\beta^\bs\left[ \rank(\Pi_{f^\bs} ) \lambda_{f^\bs}
- \rank(\Pi_{i^\bs}) \lambda_{i^\bs} \right] \RParen\Big)

(B2)

\displaystyle=\beta^{\tt A}\left[\rank(\Pi_{f^\as} )\lambda_{f^{\tt A}}-\rank(\Pi_{i^\as})\lambda_{i^{\tt A}}\right]+\beta^{\tt B}\left[\rank(\Pi_{f^\bs} )\lambda_{f^{\tt B}}-\rank(\Pi_{i^\bs})\lambda_{i^{\tt B}}\right].

(B3)

Due to the rank factors,
\sigma_{\text{surp}}\neq\sigma_{\text{chrg}}
.

Second, we can define
\tilde{p}_{\rm F}
and
\sigma_{\text{surp}}
in terms of rank-one projectors only. If a
Q_{\alpha}
has a degenerate eigenspace, we must choose an eigenbasis for the space. The SEP formulae will remain equal in the commuting case. However, different choices of projectors engender different correction terms in the
\sigma_{\text{chrg}}
fluctuation theorem (
10
) and in the
\sigma_{\text{surp}}
fluctuation theorem (
14
). The corrections’ varying with the basis choice suggests unphysicality.

For example, let each of
{\tt A}
and
{\tt B}
be a qubit. We express operators relative to an arbitrary basis:
Q_{1}=\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\
0&1\end{pmatrix}
,
and
Q_{2}=\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\
0&2\end{pmatrix}
.
Choosing
\Pi_{1,1}=\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\
0&0\end{pmatrix}
and
\Pi_{1,2}=\begin{pmatrix}0&0\\
0&1\end{pmatrix}
engenders different correction terms than

\Pi_{1,1}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\begin{pmatrix}1&1\\
1&1\end{pmatrix}
and
\Pi_{1,2}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\begin{pmatrix}1&-1\\
-1&1\end{pmatrix}

C 2
Detailed charge conservation

The KDQ obeys detailed charge conservation if
\tilde{p}_{\rm F}
is nonzero only when evaluated on indices that satisfy

\lambda_{\alpha,i^{\tt A}_{\alpha}}+\lambda_{\alpha,i^{\tt B}_{\alpha}}=\lambda_{\alpha,f_{\alpha}^{\tt A}}+\lambda_{\alpha,f_{\alpha}^{\tt B}},\quad\forall\alpha.

(C14)

If the dynamics conserve just one charge, the KDQ obeys detailed charge conservation, we show in App.
C 2 i
. Appendix
C 2 ii
generalizes the argument to multiple commuting charges.

C 2 i
Detailed charge conservation in the presence of only one charge

Here, we prove that the KDQ obeys detailed charge conservation in the presence of only one charge. We omit the charge’s index to simplify notation.
The total charge eigendecomposes as
Q^{\tt A}+Q^{\tt B}=\sum_{k}\lambda_{k}\Pi_{k}
.
The eigenprojectors decompose as

\Pi_{k}=\sum_{i^{\tt A},i^{\tt B}\,:\,\lambda_{i^{\tt A}}+\lambda_{i^{\tt B}}=\lambda_{k}}\Pi_{i^{\tt A}}\otimes\Pi_{i^{\tt B}}
.
Since
U
commutes with
Q
,
U

has the same block-diagonal structure:

U=\sum_{k}B_{k}
, wherein
B_{k}
has support only on the subspace projected onto by
\Pi_{k}
.

The relevant KDQ is

\tilde{p}_{\rm F}(i^{\tt A},i^{\tt B};f^{\tt A},f^{\tt B})=\Tr( U^\dagger\Pi_f U \Pi_i \rho)
.
By the projectors’ orthogonality,

\Pi_{f}U=\Pi_{f}B_{k}
, wherein
k
satisfies
\lambda_{k}=\lambda_{f^{\tt A}}+\lambda_{f^{\tt B}}
. By the same logic,

B_{k}\Pi_{i}=0
, unless
\lambda_{k}=\lambda_{i^{\tt A}}+\lambda_{i^{\tt B}}
. Hence
\Pi_{f^{\tt A}f^{\tt B}}U\Pi_{i}
and so

\tilde{p}(i^{\tt A},i^{\tt B};f^{\tt A},f^{\tt B})
is nonzero only if

\lambda_{f^{\tt A}}+\lambda_{f^{\tt B}}=\lambda_{k}=\lambda_{i^{\tt A}}+\lambda_{i^{\tt B}}
,
satisfying detailed charge conservation.

C 2 ii
Detailed charge conservation in the presence of commuting charges

Suppose that the dynamics conserve multiple charges that commute with each other. We show that the KDQ satisfies detailed charge conservation.
Recall the form of
\tilde{p}_{\rm F}
in Eq. (
II B
).
Since the charges commute, every eigenprojector in
\tilde{p}_{\rm F}
commutes with every other.
Thus, rearranging the projectors does not alter the quasiprobability.
We bring the initial and final charge-
\alpha
eigenprojectors
beside
U
:

\displaystyle\tilde{p}_{\rm F}(i^{\tt A}_{1},i^{\tt B}_{1},\ldots,i^{\tt A}_{c},i^{\tt B}_{c}\;;\;f^{\tt A}_{c},f^{\tt B}_{c},\ldots,f^{\tt A}_{1},f^{\tt B}_{1})

\displaystyle=\Tr( U^\dagger\left[ \Pi_{1, f_1 } \ldots\Pi_{c, f_c } \right]
U
\left[ \Pi_{c, i_c } \ldots\Pi_{1, i_1 } \right]
\rho)

(C15)

\displaystyle=\Tr( U^\dagger\ldots\Pi_{\alpha, f_\alpha} U \Pi_{\alpha, i_\alpha} \ldots\rho).

(C16)

By the reasoning for one charge,
unless
\lambda_{\alpha,i^{\tt A}_{\alpha}}+\lambda_{\alpha,i_{\alpha}^{\tt B}}=\lambda_{\alpha,f_{\alpha}^{\tt A}}+\lambda_{\alpha,f_{\alpha}^{\tt B}}
,
the projected unitary

\Pi_{\alpha,f_{\alpha}}U\Pi_{\alpha,i_{\alpha}}=0
, and hence
\tilde{p}_{\rm F}=0
.
This conclusion governs an arbitrary

\tilde{p}_{\rm F}
satisfies detailed charge conservation.

C 2 iii
Fluctuation theorem’s connection to nonconserving trajectories

We now show how the two terms in the
\sigma_{\text{chrg}}
fluctuation theorem [Eq. (
C1
)] are related to (non)conserving trajectories.
According to App.
C 1
, the first term on the fluctuation theorem’s RHS is

, wherein
\kappa\coloneqq\sum_{\alpha=1}^{c}\Delta\beta_{\alpha}(\lambda_{\alpha,f_{\alpha}^{\tt A}}-\lambda_{\alpha,i_{\alpha}^{\tt A}})

\displaystyle\expectationvalue{e^{-\schrg}}

\displaystyle=\expectationvalue{e^{-\kappa}}+\expectationvalue{e^{-\schrg}-e^{-\kappa}}

\displaystyle=\expectationvalue{e^{-\kappa}}+\expectationvalue{e^{-\schrg}-e^{-\kappa}}_{\text{cons}}+\expectationvalue{e^{-\schrg}-e^{-\kappa}}_{\text{noncons}}

\displaystyle=\expectationvalue{e^{-\kappa}}+\expectationvalue{e^{-\schrg}-e^{-\kappa}}_{\text{noncons}}\,.

(C19)

The third line follows because

D 1
Motivation for the suprisal SEP formula

Section
III B 1
partially motivated the
\sigma_{\text{surp}}
definition (
12
). Information theory suggested that the SEP depend on surprisals. The surprisals are of particular probabilities. Why those probabilities? This appendix motivates the choice.

We build on the first paragraph in Sec.
III C 1
. For simplicity, we suppose that only energy is ever measured. A classical thermodynamic story motivated the conventional SEP formula,

\displaystyle\log\left(p_{\rm F}(E^{{\tt A}}_{i},E^{{\tt B}}_{i}\,;\,E^{{\tt A}}_{f},E^{{\tt B}}_{f})/p_{\rm R}(E^{{\tt A}}_{f},E^{{\tt B}}_{f}\,;\,E^{{\tt A}}_{i},E^{{\tt B}}_{i})\right).

(D1)

The
p_{\rm F}
denotes the probability of observing the forward trajectory—the probability of observing
E^{{\tt A}}_{i}
and
E^{{\tt B}}_{i}
, times the probability of observing
E^{{\tt A}}_{f}
and
E^{{\tt B}}_{f}
, conditioned on the initial observations and on the forward dynamics:

\displaystyle p_{\rm F}(E^{{\tt A}}_{i},E^{{\tt B}}_{i}\,;\,E^{{\tt A}}_{f},E^{{\tt B}}_{f})=p(E^{{\tt A}}_{i},E^{{\tt B}}_{i})\times p(E^{{\tt A}}_{f},E^{{\tt B}}_{f}\,|\,E^{{\tt A}}_{i},E^{{\tt B}}_{i},\text{forward dynamics}).

(D2)

The reverse probability decomposes analogously:

\displaystyle p_{\rm R}(E^{{\tt A}}_{f},E^{{\tt B}}_{f}\,;\,E^{{\tt A}}_{i},E^{{\tt B}}_{i})=p(E^{{\tt A}}_{f},E^{{\tt B}}_{f},)\times p(E^{{\tt A}}_{i},E^{{\tt B}}_{i},\,|\,E^{{\tt A}}_{f},E^{{\tt B}}_{f},\text{reverse dynamics}).

(D3)

The dynamics are reversible, so the conditional probabilities cancel in (
D1
)
Landi and Paternostro 2021
. The trajectory SEP formula reduces to

\log\left(p(E^{{\tt A}}_{i},E^{{\tt B}}_{i})/p(E^{{\tt A}}_{f},E^{{\tt B}}_{f})\right).

This SEP formula is the difference of two surprisals. Each is evaluated on the probability of, upon preparing each system and measuring its energy, observing some outcome. The analog, in the noncommuting case, is Eq. (
12
).

D 2

\sigma_{\text{surp}}

\sigma_{\text{chrg}}

We establish that
\sigma_{\text{surp}}

\sigma_{\text{chrg}}

\sigma_{\text{surp}}=\sigma_{\text{chrg}}

For convenience, we assume that charges’ eigenvalues are ordered as follows.

Up to a relabeling of eigenspaces,
\Pi_{\alpha,i_{\alpha}}\Pi_{\zeta,i_{\zeta}}=0

.
The claim now follows immediately
from the KDQ’s definition [Eq. (
II B

\displaystyle\tilde{p}_{\rm F}(i_{1},i_{2},\ldots,i_{c}\,;\,f_{c},f_{c-1},\ldots,f_{1})\coloneqq\Tr\left(U^{\dagger}\left[\Pi_{1,f_{1}}\Pi_{2,f_{2}}\ldots\Pi_{c,f_{c}}\right]U\left[\Pi_{c,i_{c}}\ldots\Pi_{2,i_{2}}\Pi_{1,i_{1}}\right]\rho\right).

(D4)

On the trajectories on which
\tilde{p}_{\rm F}\neq 0

7

i^{\tt B}

\sigma_{\text{chrg}}=\sum_{\zeta=1}^{c}\left[\beta^{\tt A}_{\zeta}\left(\lambda_{\zeta,f^{\tt A}_{\alpha}}-\lambda_{\zeta,i^{\tt A}_{\alpha}}\right)+\beta^{\tt B}_{\zeta}\left(\lambda_{\zeta,f^{\tt B}_{\alpha}}-\lambda_{\zeta,i^{\tt B}_{\alpha}}\right)\right],\;\;\text{if}\;\;\tilde{p}_{\rm F}\neq 0.

(D5)

Consider now the surprisal SEP, which is defined in terms of measurement probabilities [Eq. (
12

{\tt X}

i_{\alpha}^{\tt X}
obtains with a probability

\displaystyle\Tr( \Pi_{\alpha,i_\alpha^\xs}^\xs\rho^\xs)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{Z^{\tt X}}\Tr( \Pi_{\alpha,i_\alpha^\xs}^\xs e^{-\sum_{\zeta=1}^c \beta_\zeta^\xs Q^\xs_\zeta} )=\frac{1}{Z^{\tt X}}e^{-\sum_{\zeta}\beta_{\zeta}^{\tt X}\lambda_{\zeta,i^{\tt X}_{\alpha}}}\Tr( \Pi_{\alpha,i_\alpha^\as}^\as)=\frac{1}{Z^{\tt X}}e^{-\sum_{\zeta}\beta_{\zeta}^{\tt X}\lambda_{\zeta,i^{\tt X}_{\alpha}}}\,.

(D6)

The last equality follows from the charges’ nondegeneracy: The eigenprojectors are rank-one. The choice of
\alpha
is arbitrary, because all the charges have the same product bases.
The surprisal SEP is therefore

\displaystyle\sigma_{\text{surp}}

\displaystyle=\log(
\frac{e^{ -\sum_{\zeta} \beta_\zeta^\as\lambda_{\zeta, i^\as_\alpha}} \ e^{-\sum_{\zeta} \beta_{\zeta}^\as\lambda_{\zeta, i^\as_\alpha}}}
{ e^{ -\sum_{\zeta} \beta_\zeta^\as\lambda_{\zeta, f^\as_\alpha}} \ e^ { -\sum_{\zeta} \beta_{\zeta}^\as\lambda_{\zeta, f^\as_\alpha}}} )

(D7)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\zeta}\left[\beta^{\tt A}_{\zeta}\left(\lambda_{\zeta,f^{\tt A}_{\alpha}}-\lambda_{\zeta,i^{\tt A}_{\alpha}}\right)+\beta^{\tt B}_{\zeta}\left(\lambda_{\zeta,f^{\tt B}_{\alpha}}-\lambda_{\zeta,i^{\tt B}_{\alpha}}\right)\right]\,,

(D8)

in agreement with Eq. (
D5
).

D 3
Calculation of
\langle\sigma_{\text{surp}}\rangle

Here, we prove Eq. (
13
),

\hskip-17.07182pt\expectationvalue{\ssurp}=D\bm{(}\rho_{\rm f}||\Phi_{\alpha}\left(\rho\right)\bm{)}-D\bm{(}\rho||\Phi_{\alpha}(\rho)\bm{)}\,.

(D9)

\sigma_{\text{surp}}
[Eq. (
12
)] depends implicitly on fewer indices than
\tilde{p}_{\rm F}
depends on [Eq. (
II B
)].
Therefore, some of the indices disappear (yield factors of unity) when summed over in
\expectationvalue{\ssurp}
.
Let us substitute
\sigma_{\text{surp}}
’s definition into the LHS of (
D9
), then substitute in each probability’s definition. Finally, we rearrange terms:

\displaystyle\langle\sigma_{\text{surp}}\rangle

\displaystyle=\left\langle\log(
\frac{p_\alpha(i^\as_\alpha, i^\bs_\alpha) }{p_\alpha(f^\as_\alpha,f^\bs_\alpha) } )\right\rangle=\sum_{i_{\alpha},f_{\alpha}}\Tr( U^\dagger\Pi_{\alpha, f_\alpha} U \Pi_{\alpha, i_\alpha} \rho)\log( \frac{\Tr(\Pi_{\alpha, i_\alpha} \rho)
}{\Tr(\Pi_{\alpha, f_\alpha} \rho) } )

\displaystyle=\sum_{i_{\alpha}}\Tr( \Pi_{\alpha, i_\alpha} \rho)\log\bm{(}\Tr(\Pi_{\alpha, i_\alpha} \rho)\bm{)}-\sum_{f_{\alpha}}\Tr( \Pi_{\alpha, f_\alpha} \rho_{\rm f})\log\bm{(}\Tr(\Pi_{\alpha, f_\alpha} \rho)\bm{)}

(D10)

\displaystyle=\Tr( \left[ \sum_{i_\alpha} \log\LParen\Tr(\Pi_{\alpha, i_\alpha} \rho) \RParen\Pi_{\alpha, i_\alpha}\right] \rho)-\Tr( \left[\sum_{f_\alpha}
\Pi_{\alpha, f_\alpha} \log\LParen\Tr(\Pi_{\alpha, f_\alpha} \rho) \RParen\right] \rho_{\rm f}).

(D11)

To simplify the traces, we recall the channel that dephases
\rho
with respect to
Q_{\alpha}
’s product basis:

\Phi_{\alpha}(\rho)\coloneqq\sum_{i_{\alpha}}\Pi_{\alpha,i_{\alpha}}\,\rho\,\Pi_{\alpha,i_{\alpha}}=\sum_{i_{\alpha}}\Tr(\Pi_{\alpha, i_\alpha} \, \rho)\,\Pi_{\alpha,i_{\alpha}}
.
In terms of this channel,

\log\bm{(}\Phi_{\alpha}(\rho)\bm{)}=\sum_{i_{\alpha}}\log\bm{(}\Tr(\Pi_{\alpha, i_\alpha} \, \rho)\bm{)}\,\Pi_{\alpha,i_{\alpha}}.

(D12)

Substituting into (
D11
) yields

\langle\sigma_{\text{surp}}\rangle=\Tr(\rho\log\LParen\Phi_\alpha(\rho) \RParen)-\Tr(\rho_{\rm f} \log\LParen\Phi_\alpha(\rho) \RParen)
.
This expression can be rewritten in terms of relative entropies.
By the von Neumann entropy’s unitary invariance,

0=S_{\rm vN}(\rho)-S_{\rm vN}(\rho_{\rm f})=\Tr\bm{(}\rho_{\rm f}\log(\rho_{\rm f})\bm{)}-\Tr\bm{(}\rho\log(\rho)\bm{)}
.
Adding this
0
onto our
\langle\sigma_{\text{surp}}\rangle
expression yields Eq. (
D9
).

D 4
Proof of fluctuation theorem for
\sigma_{\text{surp}}

Here, we prove the fluctuation theorem (
14
),

\displaystyle\expectationvalue{e^{-\ssurp}}=1+\Tr( U^\dagger\Phi_\alpha(\rho) U \Delta\rho_\alpha\rho).

(D13)

Also, we present the correction’s form.

Recall the definition of the coherent difference
\Delta\rho_{\alpha}=\Phi_{\alpha}(\rho)^{-1}-\rho^{-1}
.
We substitute into
\expectationvalue{e^{-\ssurp}}
the definitions of
\sigma_{\text{surp}}
[Eq. (
12
)] and
\tilde{p}_{\rm F}
[Eq. (
II B
)]. As with the derivation of
\expectationvalue{\ssurp}
, some of the indices are marginalized over.
Rearranging factors:

\displaystyle\expectationvalue{e^{-\ssurp}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i_{1}^{\tt A},i_{2}^{\tt A},\ldots,i_{c}^{\tt A},\;f_{1}^{\tt A},f_{2}^{\tt A},\ldots,f_{c}^{\tt A}}\Tr( U^\dagger\Pi_{1, f_1} \ldots\Pi_{c, f_c} U \Pi_{c, i_c} \ldots\Pi_{1, i_1} \rho)\left(\frac{p_{\alpha}(f^{\tt A}_{\alpha},f^{\tt B}_{\alpha})}{p_{\alpha}(i^{\tt A}_{\alpha},i^{\tt B}_{\alpha})}\right)

(D14)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i_{\alpha}^{\tt A},i_{\alpha}^{\tt B},\;\;f_{\alpha}^{\tt A},f_{\alpha}^{\tt B}}\Tr\left(U^{\dagger}\left[\Pi_{\alpha,f^{\tt A}_{\alpha}}^{\tt A}\otimes\Pi_{\alpha,f^{\tt B}_{\alpha}}^{\tt B}\right]U\left[\Pi_{\alpha,i^{\tt A}_{\alpha}}^{\tt A}\otimes\Pi_{\alpha,i^{\tt B}_{\alpha}}^{\tt B}\right]\rho\right)\left(\frac{p_{\alpha}(f^{\tt A}_{\alpha},f^{\tt B}_{\alpha})}{p_{\alpha}(i^{\tt A}_{\alpha},i^{\tt B}_{\alpha})}\right)

(D15)

\displaystyle=\Tr\left(U^{\dagger}\left\{\sum_{f_{\alpha}^{\tt A},f_{\alpha}^{\tt B}}\left[\Pi_{\alpha,f^{\tt A}_{\alpha}}^{\tt A}\otimes\Pi_{\alpha,f^{\tt B}_{\alpha}}^{\tt B}\right]\ p_{\alpha}(f^{\tt A}_{\alpha},f^{\tt B}_{\alpha})\right\}U\left\{\sum_{i_{\alpha}^{\tt A},i_{\alpha}^{\tt B}}\left[\Pi_{\alpha,i^{\tt A}_{\alpha}}^{\tt A}\otimes\Pi_{\alpha,i^{\tt B}_{\alpha}}^{\tt B}\right]\ p_{\alpha}(i^{\tt A}_{\alpha},i^{\tt B}_{\alpha})^{-1}\right\}\rho\right)\,.

(D16)

The summands simplify to dephased states and inverses thereof:

\sum_{f_{\alpha}^{\tt A},f_{\alpha}^{\tt B}}(\Pi_{\alpha,f^{\tt A}_{\alpha}}^{\tt A}\otimes\Pi_{\alpha,f^{\tt B}_{\alpha}}^{\tt B})\ p_{\alpha}(f^{\tt A}_{\alpha},f^{\tt B}_{\alpha})=\Phi_{\alpha}(\rho)
,
and

\sum_{i_{\alpha}^{\tt A},i_{\alpha}^{\tt B}}(\Pi_{\alpha,i^{\tt A}_{\alpha}}^{\tt A}\otimes\Pi_{\alpha,i^{\tt B}_{\alpha}}^{\tt B})\ p_{\alpha}(i^{\tt A}_{\alpha},i^{\tt B}_{\alpha})^{-1}=\Phi_{\alpha}(\rho)^{-1}=\rho^{-1}+\Delta\rho_{\alpha}
.
We substitute into Eq. (
D16
), multiply out, and simplify:

\displaystyle\expectationvalue{e^{-\ssurp}}

\displaystyle=\Tr\bm{(}U^{\dagger}\Phi_{\alpha}(\rho)U\left[\rho^{-1}+\Delta\rho_{\alpha}\right]\rho\bm{)}=\Tr\bm{(}U^{\dagger}\Phi_{\alpha}(\rho)U\rho^{-1}\rho\bm{)}+\Tr\bm{(}U^{\dagger}\Phi_{\alpha}(\rho)U\Delta\rho_{\alpha}\rho\bm{)}

(D17)

\displaystyle=1+\Tr( U^\dagger\Phi_\alpha(\rho) U \Delta\rho_\alpha\rho)\,.

(D18)

The second term is the correction sourced by coherences. It vanishes if
\Delta\rho_{\alpha}=0
.

\displaystyle\tilde{p}_{\rm R}(f_{1},f_{2},\ldots,f_{c}\,;\,i_{c},i_{c-1},\ldots,i_{1})\coloneqq\Tr\left(\left[\Pi_{1,f_{1}}\Pi_{2,f_{2}}\ldots\Pi_{c,f_{c}}\right]U\left[\Pi_{c,i_{c}}\ldots\Pi_{2,i_{2}}\Pi_{1,i_{1}}\right]U^{\dagger}\rho\right)\,.

We motivated

Prior literature features nonunique reverse distributions (of probabilities, rather than quasiprobabilities)
Manzano et al. 2018

Similarly, we specify a reverse protocol from which

We therefore regard

We can associate each KDQ with a protocol—the key stage of an experiment used to infer the quasiprobability (see App.
A
and
Yunger Halpern et al. 2018

One begins at one end of the trace’s argument and proceeds toward the opposite end. If a projector appears on just one side of
\rho

\tilde{p}_{\text{R}}

\tilde{p}_{\text{F}}

We associate
\tilde{p}_{\text{F}}
with a protocol by reading the indices in (
E1
) from right to left:
One measures charges
1
through
c
weakly, evolves the state forward in time, and then measures charges
c
through
1
weakly.
Before interpreting
\tilde{p}_{\text{R}}

we cycle
\rho
to the trace’s LHS:

\displaystyle\tilde{p}_{\rm R}(f_{1},f_{2},\ldots,f_{c}\,;\,i_{c},i_{c-1},\ldots,i_{1})\coloneqq\Tr\left(\rho\left[\Pi_{1,f_{1}}\ldots\Pi_{c,f_{c}}\right]U\left[\Pi_{c,i_{c}}\ldots\Pi_{1,i_{1}}\right]U^{\dagger}\right).

(E3)

Then, we read from left to right. In the associated protocol, one measures charges
1
through
c
weakly, evolves the state backward in time (under

through
1
weakly.
Relative to the forward protocol, the time evolution is reversed, as is the list of measurement outcomes.

E 2
Equivalence of
\sigma_{\text{traj}}
and
\sigma_{\text{surp}}
in the commuting case

In this appendix, we show that
\sigma_{\text{traj}}=\sigma_{\text{surp}}
in the commuting case.
Equation (
16
) defines the trajectory SEP:

\displaystyle\sigma_{\text{traj}}

\displaystyle=\log\left(\bra{i_1}\rho U^{\dagger}\ket{f_1}/\bra{i_1}U^{\dagger}\rho\ket{f_1}\right).

(E4)

Since the initial state is diagonal with respect to the
1^{\rm{st}}
product basis,

\bra{i_1}\rho=\Tr(\Pi_{i_1} \rho)\bra{i_1}
,
and

\rho\ket{f_1}=\Tr(\Pi_{f_1} \rho)\ket{f_1}.

Therefore,

\displaystyle\sigma_{\text{traj}}

\displaystyle=\log\left(\frac{\Tr(\Pi_{i_1} \rho)\bra{i_1}U^{\dagger}\ket{f_1}}{\Tr(\Pi_{f_1} \rho)\bra{i_1}U^{\dagger}\ket{f_1}}\right)=\log\left(\frac{\Tr(\Pi_{i_1} \rho)}{\Tr(\Pi_{f_1} \rho)}\right).

(E5)

The final expression is
\sigma_{\text{surp}}
[Eq. (
12
)] with
\alpha=1
. (The choice of
\alpha
is irrelevant in the commuting case, since all the product bases coincide.)
We have already established the equivalence of
\sigma_{\text{surp}}
and
\sigma_{\text{chrg}}
in the commuting case (App.
D 2
). Therefore, we have shown that all three SEP formulae agree when charges commute.

E 3
Calculation of
\langle\sigma_{\text{traj}}\rangle
when the initial state
\rho
is pure

Let the initial state in Sec.
II A
be pure:

\rho=|\psi\rangle\langle\psi|
.
Under this assumption, we derive Eq. (
III C 3
):

\displaystyle\expectationvalue{\straj}

\displaystyle=\tfrac{1}{2}\big[D\bm{(}\rho||\Phi^{1}(U^{\dagger}\rho U)\bm{)}+D\bm{(}\rho||U^{\dagger}\Phi^{1}(\rho)U\bm{)}-D\bm{(}\rho||\Phi^{1}(\rho)\bm{)}-D\bm{(}\rho_{\text{f}}||\Phi^{1}(\rho_{\text{f}})\bm{)}\big]+i\langle\phi_{\text{F}}-\phi_{\text{R}}\rangle\,.

(E6)

Our proof consists of three steps. First, we express
\sigma_{\text{traj}}
in terms of weak values,
slightly rewriting Eq. (
III C 2
):

\displaystyle\sigma_{\text{traj}}

\displaystyle=\log\left(\frac{\big\lvert{}_{f_{1}}\langle\Pi_{1,i_{1}}\rangle_{\rho}\big\rvert}{\big\lvert{}_{i_{1}}\langle\Pi_{1,f_{1}}\rangle_{\rho}\big\rvert}\,\frac{\Tr\big( \Pi''_{1,f_1} \rho\big)}{\Tr\big( \Pi_{1, i_1} \rho\big)}\right)+i(\phi_{\text{F}}-\phi_{\text{R}}).

(E7)

Second, we prove that
i\langle\phi_{\rm F}-\phi_{\rm R}\rangle
is imaginary.
Finally, we prove that the logarithm’s average is real and equals the relative-entropy sum in Eq. (
E6
). By implication,

i\langle\phi_{\rm F}-\phi_{\rm R}\rangle=\imaginary(\langle\sigma_{\text{traj}}\rangle)
.
This result supports the main text’s claim that

\imaginary(\langle\sigma_{\text{traj}}\rangle)\neq 0
signals contextuality.

Let us show that

i\langle\phi_{\text{F}}-\phi_{\text{R}}\rangle

is imaginary.
Let
z
denote any complex number. We denote its argument’s (its complex phase’s) principal value by
{\rm Arg}(z)
and the multivalued argument function by
{\rm arg}(z)
.

\phi_{\rm F}-\phi_{\rm R}
equals an argument by Eq. (
16
) and the polar forms in Sec.
III C 1
:
For each index pair
(i_{1},f_{1})
, there is a branch of

\rm{arg}
such that

\displaystyle\phi_{\rm F}-\phi_{\rm R}

\displaystyle={\rm arg}\left(\frac{\langle i_{1}|\psi\rangle\langle\psi|U^{\dagger}|f_{1}\rangle}{\langle i_{1}|U^{\dagger}|\psi\rangle\langle\psi|f_{1}\rangle}\right)={\rm Arg}\left(\langle i_{1}|\psi\rangle\right)+{\rm Arg}\left(\langle\psi|U^{\dagger}|f_{1}\rangle\right)-{\rm Arg}\left(\langle i_{1}|U^{\dagger}|\psi\rangle\right)-{\rm Arg}\left(\langle\psi|f_{1}\rangle\right)\,.

(E8)

Each summand in Eq. (
E8
)’s RHS depends on one index,
i_{1}
or
f_{1}
. (If
\rho
is mixed,
\sigma_{\text{traj}}
does not decompose in this manner. Hence new techniques are required to compute the average.)
Therefore, averaging
\phi_{\rm F}-\phi_{\rm R}
with respect to
\tilde{p}_{\rm F}
yields separate averages with respect to probability distributions. Hence
\langle\phi_{\rm F}-\phi_{\rm R}\rangle
is real, and
i\langle\phi_{\rm F}-\phi_{\rm R}\rangle
is imaginary.

Our task reduces to showing that

\displaystyle\left\langle\log\Bigg( \frac{
\big\lvert{}_{f_1}\langle\Pi_{1,i_1} \rangle_{\rho} \big\rvert}{
\big\lvert{}_{i_1}\langle\Pi_{1, f_1}
\rangle_{\rho} \big\rvert}
\frac{ \Tr\big(\Pi_{1, f_1}'' \rho\big)}{
\Tr\big(\Pi_{1, i_1}' \rho\big) } \Bigg)\right\rangle=\tfrac{1}{2}\big[D\bm{(}\rho||\Phi^{1}(U^{\dagger}\rho U)\bm{)}+D\bm{(}\rho||U^{\dagger}\Phi^{1}(\rho)U\bm{)}-D\bm{(}\rho||\Phi^{1}(\rho)\bm{)}-D\bm{(}\rho_{\rm f}||\Phi^{1}(\rho_{\rm f})\bm{)}\big].

(E9)

The weak values have magnitudes of

\big\lvert{}_{f_{1}}\langle\Pi_{1,i_{1}}\rangle_{\rho}\big\rvert=\frac{\left[\Tr(\Pi_{1, f_1}'' \Pi_{1, i_1}\rho)\Tr(\rho\Pi_{1, i_1} \Pi_{1, f_1}'')\right]^{\frac{1}{2}}}{\Tr(\Pi_{1, f_1}'' \rho)}\quad{\rm and}\quad\big\lvert{}_{i_{1}}\langle\Pi_{1,f_{1}}\rangle_{\rho}\big\rvert=\frac{\left[\Tr(\Pi_{1, i_1}' \Pi_{1, f_1}\rho)\Tr(\rho\Pi_{1, f_1} \Pi_{1, i_1}')\right]^{\frac{1}{2}}}{\Tr(\Pi_{1, i_1}'\rho)}\,.

(E10)

When we insert these expressions into Eq. (
E9
), the denominators in (
E10
) cancel.
To simplify Eq. (
E9
)’s LHS more,
we express

and the rank-1 projectors as outer products:
\rho=|\psi\rangle\langle\psi|

\Pi_{1,i_{1}}=|i_{1}\rangle\langle i_{1}|
, etc.
Factors
\langle f_{1}|U|i_{1}\rangle
and

\langle i_{1}|U^{\dagger}|f_{1}\rangle
appear in the numerator and denominator. Canceling the factors, we compute Eq. (
E9
)’s LHS:

\displaystyle\left\langle\log\left(\frac{\left[\Tr(\Pi_{1, f_1}'' \Pi_{1, i_1}\rho)\Tr(\rho\Pi_{1, i_1} \Pi_{1, f_1}'')\right]^{\frac{1}{2}}}{\left[\Tr(\Pi_{1, i_1}' \Pi_{1, f_1}\rho)\Tr(\rho\Pi_{1, f_1}

(E11)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i_{1},\ldots,i_{c},\;f_{1},\ldots,f_{c}}\frac{1}{2}\Tr(U^\dagger\Pi_{1, f_1} \ldots\Pi_{1, f_c} U \Pi_{c, i_c} \ldots\Pi_{1, i_1} \rho)\log\left(\frac{\Tr( \Pi_{1, f_1} U\rho U^\dagger)\Tr( \Pi_{1, i_1} \rho)}{\Tr(\Pi_{1, f_1} \rho)\Tr(\Pi_{1, i_1} U^\dagger\rho U)}\right)

(E12)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\Bigg[\sum_{f_{1}}\Tr( \Pi_{1, f_1} \rho_{\rm f})\log\bm{(}\Tr( \Pi_{1, f_1} \rho_{\rm f})\bm{)}+\sum_{i_{1}}\Tr( \Pi_{1, i_1} \rho)\log\bm{(}\Tr( \Pi_{1, i_1} \rho)\bm{)}

Appendix F
Symmetrized definitions

In defining the KDQ and some SEPs, we choose projectors and the order in which to arrange them.
Here, we discuss alternative KDQ and SEP definitions based on symmetrizing over these choices. We present the corresponding averages
\expectationvalue{\sigma}
. Via arguments similar to the ones for unsymmetrized
\sigma
’s, one can derive fluctuation theorems.

F 1
Symmetrized Kirkwood-Dirac quasiprobability

A primary motivation for using the KDQ is a desire to describe charges flowing in the absence of measurement disturbance.
Though based on weak measurements, the KDQ
depends on the measurements’ ordering, i.e., how the charges are labeled.
But if the systems interact unitarily without being measured,
relabeling the charges does not change any physical observables. This process’s stochastic description, too, should respect this symmetry.
We take this for granted in the commuting case, in which
the measurements’ ordering does not change the joint probability distribution. We can enforce this symmetry in the noncommuting case by averaging over all possible orderings of the initial projectors. (Once the initial projectors are ordered, the final projectors’ ordering is fixed by the fourth sanity check in Sec.
III
.) We define the
symmetrized KDQ
(SKDQ) as

\tilde{p}_{F}^{\text{symm}}\coloneqq\frac{1}{c!}\sum_{\tau\in S_{c}}\Tr(U^\dagger\left[\Pi_{\tau(1), f_{\tau(1)}}\ldots\Pi_{\tau(c), f_{\tau(c)}} \right] U \left[\Pi_{\tau(c), i_{\tau(c)}} \ldots\Pi_{\tau(1), i_{\tau(1)}}\right] \rho).

(F1)

S_{c}
is the symmetric group of degree
c
, i.e., the set of all permutations of
\mathbbm{Z}_{c}
.

\tilde{p}_{F}^{\text{symm}}
is invariant under every possible relabeling of the charges, respecting a fundamental symmetry of the undisturbed-charge-flow process. The symmetrized KDQ’s single-index marginals are those of

\tilde{p}_{\text{F}}
. In the commuting case,
\tilde{p}_{F}^{\text{symm}}=\tilde{p}_{F}
.

F 2
Charge stochastic entropy production

The charge SEP is already symmetric under every possible relabeling of the charges. Since the SKDQ has the same marginals as the KDQ,
\sigma_{\text{chrg}}
has the same average:

\expectationvalue{\schrg}_{{\text{symm}}}=\expectationvalue{\schrg}=\sum_{\alpha}\Delta\beta_{\alpha}\,\Delta\expectationvalue{Q_\alpha}.

(F2)

F 3
Surprisal stochastic entropy production

To obtain the symmetrized surprisal SEP, we average over the different bases:

\sigma_{\text{surp}}^{\text{symm}}\coloneqq\frac{1}{c}\sum_{\alpha=1}^{c}\log\left(\frac{p_{\alpha}(i^{\tt A}_{\alpha},i^{\tt B}_{\alpha})}{p_{\alpha}(f^{\tt A}_{\alpha},f^{\tt B}_{\alpha})}\right).

(F3)

The average now involves dephasing in all bases:

\expectationvalue{\ssurp^{\text{symm}}}_{{\text{symm}}}=\frac{1}{c}\sum_{\alpha=1}^{c}\left[D\bm{(}\rho_{\rm f}||\Phi_{\alpha}\left(\rho\right)\bm{)}-D\bm{(}\rho||\Phi_{\alpha}(\rho)\bm{)}\right].

(F4)

F 4
Trajectory stochastic entropy production

A natural definition for the symmetrized trajectory SEP is

\sigma_{\text{traj}}^{\text{symm}}=\frac{1}{c}\left(\sum_{\alpha=1}^{c}\log\frac{\bra{i_\alpha}\rho U^{\dagger}\ket{f_\alpha}}{\bra{i_\alpha}U^{\dagger}\rho\ket{f_\alpha}}\right).

(F5)

This symmetrized SEP is closely related to the symmetrized KDQ. As in the unsymmetrized case, define the trajectory SEP as the ratio of forward and reverse quasiprobabilities. However, perform the averaging after taking the ratio:

\displaystyle\sigma_{\text{traj}}^{\text{symm}}

\displaystyle\coloneqq\frac{1}{c!}\sum_{\tau\in S_{c}}\log\left(\frac{\tilde{p}_{\rm F}(\tau)}{\tilde{p}_{\rm R}(\tau)}\right)=\frac{1}{c!}\sum_{\tau\in S_{c}}\log\left(\frac{\Tr(U^\dagger\left[\Pi_{\tau(1), f_{\tau(1)}}\ldots\Pi_{\tau(c), f_{\tau(c)}} \right] U \left[\Pi_{\tau(c), i_{\tau(c)}} \ldots\Pi_{\tau(1), i_{\tau(1)}}\right] \rho)}{\Tr(\left[\Pi_{\tau(1), f_{\tau(1)}}\ldots\Pi_{\tau(c), f_{\tau(c)}} \right] U \left[\Pi_{\tau(c), i_{\tau(c)}} \ldots\Pi_{\tau(1), i_{\tau(1)}}\right] U^\dagger\rho)}\right)

(F6)

Success probability of the L 0 L_{0} -regularized box-constrained Babai point and column permutation strategies
Xiao-Wen Chang and Yingzi Xu

Abstract
We consider the success probability of the L 0 L_{0} -regularized box-constrained Babai point, which is a suboptimal solution to the L 0 L_{0} -regularized box-constrained integer least squares problem and can be used for MIMO detection. First, we derive formulas for the success probability of both L 0 L_{0} -regularized and unregularized box-constrained Babai points. Then we investigate the properties of the L 0 L_{0} -regularized box-constrained Babai point, including the optimality of the regularization parameter, the monotonicity of its success probability, and the monotonicity of the ratio of the two success probabilities. A bound on the success probability of the L 0 L_{0} -regularized Babai point is derived. After that, we analyze the effect of the LLL-P permutation strategy on the success probability of the L 0 L_{0} -regularized Babai point. Then we propose some success probability based column permutation strategies to increase the success probability of the L 0 L_{0} -regularized box-constrained Babai point. Finally, we present numerical tests to confirm our theoretical results and to show the advantage of the L 0 L_{0} regularization and the effectiveness of the proposed column permutation algorithms compared to existing strategies.



I
Introduction

In many applications, the unknown parameter vector
{\mathbf{x}}^{\ast}
and the observation vector
{\mathbf{y}}
satisfy
the following linear relation:

{\mathbf{y}}=\mathbf{A}{\mathbf{x}}^{\ast}+{\mathbf{v}},\ \ {\mathbf{v}}\sim N({\boldsymbol{0}},\sigma^{2}\mathbf{I}),

(1)

where
\mathbf{A}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{m\times n}
is a model matrix and
{\mathbf{v}}
is an
m
dimensional noise vector.
In this paper, we consider the case that
{\mathbf{x}}^{\ast}
is random,
and given integer
M\geq 1
, the entries of
{\mathbf{x}}^{\ast}
are independently distributed over the set

{\cal X}=\{0\}\cup\{-2M+1,-2M+3,\ldots,-1,1,\ldots,2M-3,2M-1\},

(2)

and the elements in the set are chosen independently
with probability

\Pr(x^{\ast}_{k}=i)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}p/(2M),&i\in{\cal X},i\neq 0\\
1-p,&i=0,\end{array}\right.

(3)

where
p
is a positive constant satisfying

p\leq\frac{2M}{2M+1}.

(4)

The inequality (
4
) is equivalent to

p/(2M)\leq 1-p,

(5)

i.e., the probability for
x_{k}^{*}
to take any nonzero integer in the set
{\mathcal{X}}
is the same,
and it is smaller than or equal to the probability for
x_{k}^{*}
to take 0.
If
p
is small,
{\mathbf{x}}^{\ast}
is sparse.
Note that all numbers except zero in
{\cal X}
are odd numbers.
This model has applications in multiple user detection in code-division multiple
access (CDMA) communications, e.g., see
[
1
]
,
in which
{\cal X}
is a
2M
-ary constellation.
When
x_{k}^{\ast}
takes 0, the
k
-th user is said to be inactive.

To recover
{\mathbf{x}}^{\ast}
from the observation
{\mathbf{y}}
, one can apply the maximum a posteriori (MAP) estimation method, which solves

\max_{{\mathbf{x}}\in{\cal X}^{n}}\ln\left(\varphi({\mathbf{y}}|{\mathbf{x}}^{\ast}={\mathbf{x}})\Pr({\mathbf{x}}^{\ast}={\mathbf{x}})\right),

(6)

where
\varphi({\mathbf{y}}|{\mathbf{x}}^{\ast}={\mathbf{x}})
is the probability density function of
{\mathbf{y}}
when
{\mathbf{x}}^{\ast}={\mathbf{x}}

(here for the sake of notational simplicity,
{\mathbf{y}}
is also used for its realized value)
and
\Pr({\mathbf{x}}^{\ast}={\mathbf{x}})
is the probability that
{\mathbf{x}}^{\ast}={\mathbf{x}}
for
{\mathbf{x}}\in{\cal X}^{n}
.
It is shown in
[
1
]
that (
6
) is equivalent to
the following regularized optimization problem:

\min_{{\mathbf{x}}\in{\cal X}^{n}}\frac{1}{2}\|{\mathbf{y}}-\mathbf{A}{\mathbf{x}}\|_{2}^{2}+\lambda\|{\mathbf{x}}\|_{0},

(7)

where the
L_{0}
-“norm”
\|{\mathbf{x}}\|_{0}
is the number of nonzero elements of
{\mathbf{x}}
, and the regularization parameter
\lambda=\lambda^{*}
with

\lambda^{*}:=\sigma^{2}\ln\frac{1-p}{p/(2M)}\geq 0.

(8)

In
[
1
]
, the inequality (
4
) is strict, then
\lambda^{*}>0
.
We allow (
4
) to be an equality so that
\lambda^{*}
can be zero.
This gives us more flexibility.
To make some of our results more applicable, in this paper we assume that the
\lambda
in (
7
)
is only a given nonnegative parameter, unless we state explicitly that it is defined by (
8
).
This unifies some results for
\lambda=\lambda^{*}
and
\lambda=0
.
We refer to (
7
) as the
L_{0}
-“norm” regularized “box” constrained integer least squares (
L_{0}
-RBILS) problem.
(Although the constraint set
{\cal X}^{n}
is not a regular box, its geometric shape is still a box.)

If no
a priori
information about
{\mathbf{x}}^{\ast}
in (
1
) is available, to estimate
{\mathbf{x}}^{\ast}
, one applies the maximum likelihood (ML)
estimation method,
leading to the box constrained integer least squares (BILS) problem:

\min_{{\mathbf{x}}\in{\cal X}^{n}}\frac{1}{2}\|{\mathbf{y}}-\mathbf{A}{\mathbf{x}}\|_{2}^{2}.

(9)

Note that this problem is just (
7
) without the regularization term.
If
{\mathbf{x}}^{\ast}
is uniformly distributed over
{\cal X}^{n}
, then (
4
) is an equality, and then
\lambda^{*}=0

and (
7
) with
\lambda=\lambda^{*}
just becomes (
9
).
We can regard (
9
) as a special case of (
7
).
If we replace the constraint box
{\cal X}
by
{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}
, (
9
) becomes the ordinary integer least squares (OILS) problem

\min_{{\mathbf{x}}\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}}\frac{1}{2}\|{\mathbf{y}}-\mathbf{A}{\mathbf{x}}\|_{2}^{2}.

(10)

The OILS problem (
10
) is NP-hard
[
2
,
3
]
and there are a few approaches
to finding the optimal solution (see, e.g.,
[
4
,
5
]
).
The widely used approach in applications is the enumeration approach originally proposed in
[
6
]

and later improved in
[
7
]
,
which enumerates integer points in an ellipsoid and is called sphere decoding in communications.
This approach has been extended to constrained problems
[
8
,
9
,
10
]

and to the
L_{0}
-RBILS problem
[
1
,
11
,
12
]
.
To make the search faster, typically the original problem is transformed to a new problem
by lattice reduction or column permutations.
Some examples of commonly used strategies include
LLL reduction
[
13
]
,
V-BLAST
[
14
]
and sorted QR decomposition (SQRD)
[
15
]

Since an ILS problem is NP-hard, often a suboptimal solution instead of the optimal one is found in some applications.
One often used suboptimal solution to the ILS problem is the Babai point
[
16
]

which was originally introduced for the OILS problem (

[

9
]

[

It is the first integer point found by the Schnorr-Euchner type search methods

To see how good the Babai point is as a detector,

If the SP of the Babai point is high, one will not need to try to find

In this paper we conduct systematic studies of the SP of the
L_{0}
-regularized Babai point
{\mathbf{x}}^{\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{RB}}
(here the superscripts RB stands for “regularized Babai”) for the
L_{0}
-RBILS problem (
7
).
The main contributions are summarized as follows.

1.

We establish a formula for the SP of the
L_{0}
-regularized
Babai point
{\mathbf{x}}^{\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{RB}}

and a formula for the SP of the (unregularized) Babai point
{\mathbf{x}}^{\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{BB}}
(here the superscripts BB standard for “box-constrained Babai”)
for the BILS problem (
9
) in Section
III
.

2.

The properties of the SP of

.

in

,

3.

We analyse the effect of the column permutation strategy LLL-P on the SP of
{\mathbf{x}}^{\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{RB}}

in Section

Specifically we show that the LLL-P strategy is guaranteed

4.

Based on the formula of the SP of

, we propose

The LSP strategy uses the local information of the SP,

-regularized box-constrained Babai point

II
Preliminaries

In this section, we first transform the
L_{0}
-RBILS problem (
7
) to an equivalent one
by the QR factorization of
\mathbf{A}
and define the corresponding Babai point.
Then we give some background for the column permutation strategies
to be discussed in this paper.

II-A

The Babai points

We first transform (
7
) to an equivalent new problem by an orthogonal transformation.
Let the QR factorization of
\mathbf{A}
be

\mathbf{A}=[\mathbf{Q}_{1},\mathbf{Q}_{2}]\begin{bmatrix}\mathbf{R}\\
{\boldsymbol{0}}\end{bmatrix}=\mathbf{Q}_{1}\mathbf{R},

(11)

where
[\mathbf{Q}_{1},\mathbf{Q}_{2}]\in{\mathbb{R}}^{m\times m}
is orthogonal and
\mathbf{R}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n\times n}
is upper triangular with
positive
diagonal entries.
Given the matrix
\mathbf{A}
, such
\mathbf{R}
is unique and can be found easily and efficiently using algorithms such as the Householder transformations.

With
{\tilde{{\mathbf{y}}}}=\mathbf{Q}_{1}^{\mkern-1.5mu\mathsf{T}}{\mathbf{y}}
and
{\tilde{{\mathbf{v}}}}=\mathbf{Q}_{1}^{\mkern-1.5mu\mathsf{T}}{\mathbf{v}}
, it is easy to show the model (
1
) can be transformed to

{\tilde{{\mathbf{y}}}}=\mathbf{R}{\mathbf{x}}^{\ast}+{\tilde{{\mathbf{v}}}},\ \ {\tilde{{\mathbf{v}}}}\sim N({\boldsymbol{0}},\sigma^{2}\mathbf{I}),

(12)

and the
L_{0}
-RBILS problem (
7
) is equivalent to

\min_{{\mathbf{x}}\in{\cal X}^{n}}\frac{1}{2}\|{\tilde{{\mathbf{y}}}}-\mathbf{R}{\mathbf{x}}\|_{2}^{2}+\lambda\|{\mathbf{x}}\|_{0}.

(13)

Here we define the
L_{0}
-regularized Babai point
{\mathbf{x}}^{\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{RB}}=[x_{1}^{\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{RB}},\ldots,x_{n}^{\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{RB}}]^{\mkern-1.5mu\mathsf{T}}

corresponding to (
13
).
Note that the objective function in (
13
) can be rewritten as

\min_{{\mathbf{x}}\in{\cal X}^{n}}\sum_{k=1}^{n}\bigg(\frac{1}{2}\Big(\tilde{y}_{k}-r_{kk}x_{k}-\sum_{j=k+1}^{n}r_{kj}x_{j}\Big)^{2}+\lambda\|x_{k}\|_{0}\bigg).

Suppose that
x_{n}^{\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{RB}},\ldots,x_{k+1}^{\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{RB}}
have been defined and
we would like to define
x_{k}^{\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{RB}}
as an estimator of
x_{k}^{*}
.
The idea is to choose
x_{k}^{\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{RB}}
to be the solution of the optimization problem:

\min_{x_{k}\in{\cal X}}\Big\{f_{k}(x_{k}):=\frac{1}{2}\Big(\tilde{y}_{k}-r_{kk}x_{k}-\sum_{j=k+1}^{n}r_{kj}x_{j}^{\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{RB}}\Big)^{2}+\lambda\|x_{k}\|_{0}\Big\},

(14)

where
\sum_{k+1}^{n}\cdot=0
if
k=n
.
To simplify the objective function in (
14
),
write

c_{k}:=\Big(\tilde{y}_{k}-\sum_{j=k+1}^{n}r_{kj}x_{j}^{\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{RB}}\Big)/r_{kk}.

(15)

Then we have

f_{k}(x_{k})=\frac{1}{2}r_{kk}^{2}(x_{k}-c_{k})^{2}+\lambda\|x_{k}\|_{0}.

(16)

From (
16
) we observe that the solution to (
14
) is either 0 or
\lfloor c_{k}\rceil_{\cal X}
.
Of course the two are identical if
\lfloor c_{k}\rceil_{\cal X}=0
.
To see which one is the solution.
Define

g_{k}:=\frac{1}{2}r_{kk}^{2}\lfloor c_{k}\rceil_{\cal X}^{2}-r_{kk}^{2}\lfloor c_{k}\rceil_{\cal X}c_{k}+\lambda.

(17)

Observe that
g_{k}=f_{k}(\lfloor c_{k}\rceil_{\cal X})-f_{k}(0)
when
\lfloor c_{k}\rceil_{\cal X}\neq 0
, and
g_{k}\geq 0
when
\lfloor c_{k}\rceil_{\cal X}=0
.
Thus, the solution to (
14
) is

x_{k}^{\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{RB}}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{@{}cl}0,&\text{if }g_{k}\geq 0,\\
\lfloor c_{k}\rceil_{\cal X},&\text{if }g_{k}<0.\end{array}\right.

(18)

If we set
\lambda=0
in (
7
),
{\mathbf{x}}^{\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{RB}}
just becomes
the (unregularized) box-constrained Babai point
{\mathbf{x}}^{\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{BB}}
of the BILS problem (
9
):

x_{k}^{\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{BB}}=\Big\lfloor\Big(\tilde{y}_{k}-\sum_{j=k+1}^{n}r_{kj}x_{j}^{\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{BB}}\Big)/r_{kk}\Big\rceil_{\cal X},\ \ k=n,n-1,\ldots,1,

which is an extension of the ordinary Babai point for the OILS problem (
10
):

x_{k}^{\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{OB}}=\Big\lfloor\Big(\tilde{y}_{k}-\sum_{j=k+1}^{n}r_{kj}x_{j}^{\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{OB}}\Big)/r_{kk}\Big\rceil,\ \ k=n,n-1,\ldots,1.

Here the
L_{0}
-regularized Babai point
{\mathbf{x}}^{\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{RB}}
is a further extension of the ordinary Babai point
{\mathbf{x}}^{\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{OB}}
.
In
[
1
]
an equivalent point is defined, and it is referred to as the decision-directed detector.

II-B

Column permutation strategies

In OILS problems, a commonly used lattice reduction strategy is LLL reduction, which consists of two types of operations: size reduction and column permutation.
In BILS problems, applying size reduction would make the transformed constraints difficult to handle in the search process.
However we can still use the permutation strategy in the reduction process,
referred to as LLL-P
[
17
]
.
The resulting matrix
\mathbf{R}
satisfies the Lovász condition

\delta\,r_{k-1,k-1}^{2}\leq r_{k-1,k}^{2}+r_{kk}^{2},\quad k=2,3,\ldots,n,

(19)

where
\delta\in(\frac{1}{4},1]
is a parameter.

The reduction process works with the upper triangular matrix
\mathbf{R}
obtained by the QR factorization (
11
).
If the Lovász condition (
19
) is violated
for some
k
, then the algorithm interchanges columns
k-1
and
k
of
\mathbf{R}

and uses a Givens rotation to restore the upper-triangular structure of
\mathbf{R}
:

\hat{\mathbf{R}}:=\mathbf{G}^{\mkern-1.5mu\mathsf{T}}_{k-1,k}\mathbf{R}\mathbf{P}_{k-1,k},

(20)

where
\mathbf{G}_{k-1,k}
is a Givens rotation matrix and
\mathbf{P}_{k-1,k}

is a permutation matrix.
Then it is easy to show that

\displaystyle\hat{r}_{k-1,k-1}^{2}

\displaystyle=r_{k-1,k}^{2}+r_{kk}^{2},

(21)

\displaystyle\hat{r}_{k-1,k}^{2}+\hat{r}_{kk}^{2}

\displaystyle=r_{k-1,k-1}^{2},

\displaystyle\hat{r}_{k-1,k-1}\hat{r}_{kk}

\displaystyle=r_{k-1,k-1}r_{kk},

which will be used in our later analysis.
Then it is easy to see that
\hat{\mathbf{R}}
satisfies the Lovász condition for that
k
.

On the other hand, the SQRD strategy acts on
\mathbf{A}
directly
and the original implementation is built on the basis of the modified Gram-Schmidt process
for the QR factorization,
which determines the columns of the permuted
\mathbf{R}
from the first to the last.
For

and the

-RBILS problem (

Furthermore, we let the

-regularized Babai point

.

III
SP of the
L_{0}
-regularized Babai point
{\mathbf{x}}^{\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{RB}}

We would like to see how good the Babai detector
{\mathbf{x}}^{\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{RB}}
is.
One measure is the SP
\Pr({\mathbf{x}}^{\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{RB}}={\mathbf{x}}^{\ast})
.
In this section we derive a formula for it.

In our probability analysis, we need to use the well-known error function and related probability formulas.
The error function is defined as

\mathrm{erf}(\zeta)=\frac{2}{\sqrt{\pi}}\int_{0}^{\zeta}\exp(-t^{2})dt.

(25)

Note that
\mathrm{erf}(\infty)=1
.
Given
a
and
b
, if
x\sim N(0,\sigma^{2})
, then

\Pr(x\leq a)=\frac{1}{2}\left(1+\mbox{erf}\left(\frac{a}{\sqrt{2}\sigma}\right)\right),

(26)

\Pr(x\geq a)=\frac{1}{2}\left(1-\mbox{erf}\left(\frac{a}{\sqrt{2}\sigma}\right)\right),

(27)

\displaystyle\Pr(a\leq x\leq b)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\left(\!\mbox{erf}\left(\frac{(b-a)_{+}+a}{\sqrt{2}\sigma}\right)\!\!-\!\!\mbox{erf}\left(\frac{a}{\sqrt{2}\sigma}\right)\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\left(\!\mbox{erf}\left(\frac{b}{\sqrt{2}\sigma}\right)\!\!-\!\!\mbox{erf}\left(\frac{b-(b-a)_{+}}{\sqrt{2}\sigma}\right)\right).

(28)

where
(b-a)_{+}=b-a
when
b-a>0
, otherwise
(b-a)_{+}=0
.

From (
12
), we have

\tilde{y}_{k}=r_{kk}x^{\ast}_{k}+\sum_{j=k+1}^{n}r_{kj}x^{\ast}_{j}+\tilde{v}_{k},\ \ \tilde{v}_{k}\sim N(0,\sigma^{2}).

Substituting it into (
15
), we obtain

c_{k}=x^{\ast}_{k}+\sum_{j=k+1}^{n}\frac{r_{kj}}{r_{kk}}(x^{\ast}_{j}-x_{j}^{\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{RB}})+\frac{\tilde{v}_{k}}{r_{kk}}.

If
x_{j}^{\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{RB}}=x^{\ast}_{j}
for
j=k+1,k+2,\ldots,n
, then

c_{k}-x^{\ast}_{k}\sim N(0,\sigma^{2}/r_{kk}^{2}).

(29)

The key to finding
\Pr({\mathbf{x}}^{\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{RB}}={\mathbf{x}}^{\ast})
is to find the the range of
c_{k}-x_{k}^{\ast}

for which
x_{k}^{\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{RB}}=x_{k}^{\ast}
for
k=n,n-1,\ldots,1
.
When
x_{k}^{\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{RB}}=x_{k}^{\ast}
,
c_{k}-x_{k}^{\ast}=c_{k}-x_{k}^{\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{RB}}
.
Thus we will find the range of
c_{k}-x_{k}^{\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{RB}}
for which
x_{k}^{\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{RB}}=x_{k}^{\ast}
.

Since the elements of the constraint set
{\cal X}
are symmetric with respect to 0
and the distribution of
x_{k}^{\ast}
is symmetric with respect to 0,
we only focus on the nonnegative case that
x_{k}^{\ast}\geq 0
and
c_{k}\geq 0
in our following analysis,
and we can simply double the relevant probabilities when we also take the nonpositive case into account.

If
\lfloor c_{k}\rceil_{\cal X}=0
, i.e.,
0\leq c_{k}\leq 1/2
, obviously in this case from (
18

In the rest of this paragraph we assume that

From (

(30)

), (

\begin{split}&x_{k}^{\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{RB}}=\lfloor c_{k}\rceil_{\cal X}\Leftrightarrow g_{k}<0\Leftrightarrow c_{k}-\lfloor c_{k}\rceil_{\cal X}>\frac{\lambda}{r_{kk}^{2}\lfloor c_{k}\rceil_{\cal X}}-\frac{1}{2}\lfloor c_{k}\rceil_{\cal X},\\
&x_{k}^{\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{RB}}=0\Leftrightarrow g_{k}\geq 0\Leftrightarrow c_{k}-\lfloor c_{k}\rceil_{\cal X}\leq\frac{\lambda}{r_{kk}^{2}\lfloor c_{k}\rceil_{\cal X}}-\frac{1}{2}\lfloor c_{k}\rceil_{\cal X}.\end{split}

(31)

In addition, from the definition of
\lfloor c_{k}\rceil_{\cal X}
, if
M\geq 2
,
c_{k}-\lfloor c_{k}\rceil_{\cal X}
has to satisfy

\begin{cases}-1/2<c_{k}-\lfloor c_{k}\rceil_{\cal X}\leq 1,&\lfloor c_{k}\rceil_{\cal X}=1,\\
-1<c_{k}-\lfloor c_{k}\rceil_{\cal X}\leq 1,&\lfloor c_{k}\rceil_{\cal X}=2j-1,j=2,\ldots,M-1,\\
-1<c_{k}-\lfloor c_{k}\rceil_{\cal X},&\lfloor c_{k}\rceil_{\cal X}=2M-1,\end{cases}

(32)

and if

To derive the formula for the SP of

we define

when

j_{k}=1,\ \ \alpha_{k}^{(1)}=-\frac{1}{2}.

Then (

\displaystyle\rho_{k}^{\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{BB}}=\

\displaystyle\frac{p}{2M}+\frac{(M-1)p}{M}\mathrm{erf}({\bar{r}}_{kk})-\frac{p}{2M}\mathrm{erf}\Big(-\frac{1}{2}{\bar{r}}_{kk}\Big)+(1-p)\mathrm{erf}\Big(\frac{1}{2}{\bar{r}}_{kk}\Big).

Thus, (
44
) holds, completing the proof.
∎

Here we make some remarks about Theorem

and its proof.

Remark 1
.

In

]
a formula for the symbol error rate (SER)

It is stated in

Here our formula for

Remark 2
.

In

a formula for

is derived for the case that

is uniformly distributed over a box

Remark 3

An earlier version of Theorem

]

Here the expression of

Corollary 1
.

Let the regularization parameter
\lambda
be any positive number.
We have

\displaystyle\lim_{r_{kk}\rightarrow 0}\rho_{k}^{\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{RB}}=1-p,\ \ \lim_{r_{kk}\rightarrow\infty}\rho_{k}^{\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{RB}}=1,

\displaystyle\lim_{r_{kk}\rightarrow 0}\rho_{k}^{\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{BB}}=\frac{p}{2M},\ \ \lim_{r_{kk}\rightarrow\infty}\rho_{k}^{\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{BB}}=1.

Proof.

When
r_{kk}
is small enough, from (

) we see

.
Then from (

\lim_{r_{kk}\rightarrow 0}\alpha_{k}^{(j_{k})}=\infty.

Thus, from (

\lim_{r_{kk}\rightarrow 0}\rho_{k}^{\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{RB}}=\frac{p}{2M}-\frac{p}{2M}\cdot 1+(1-p)\cdot 1=1-p.

When

j_{k}=1
.
From (

\lim_{r_{kk}\rightarrow\infty}\alpha_{k}^{(j_{k})}=-1/2
,
and then from (

\lim_{r_{kk}\rightarrow\infty}\rho_{k}^{\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{RB}}=\frac{p}{2M}+\frac{(M-1)p}{M}+\frac{p}{2M}+(1-p)=1.

From (

\lim_{r_{kk}\rightarrow 0}\rho_{k}^{\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{BB}}=\frac{p}{2M},

\lim_{r_{kk}\rightarrow\infty}\rho_{k}^{\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{BB}}=\frac{p}{2M}+\frac{(M-1)p}{M}+(1-\frac{2M-1}{2M}p)=1.

∎

We make a remark about this corollary.

Under the condition (

Some basic properties of
\rho_{k}^{\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{RB}}

In this subsection, we give some results on the continuity of the function
\rho_{k}^{\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{RB}}
, which will be used in some later proofs.

Lemma 2
.

Let the regularization parameter
\lambda=\lambda^{*}
(see (
8
)).
With the same notation as in Theorem
1
,

\displaystyle\frac{\partial\rho_{k}^{\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{RB}}}{\partial{\bar{r}}_{kk}}=\frac{2}{\sqrt{\pi}}\Bigg[\frac{M-j_{k}}{M}pe^{-{\bar{r}}_{kk}^{2}}+(1-p)(2j_{k}-1)e^{-\left(\frac{1}{2(2j_{k}-1)}\frac{\bar{\lambda}}{{\bar{r}}_{kk}}+\frac{1}{2}(2j_{k}-1){\bar{r}}_{kk}\right)^{2}}\Bigg],

(59)

and it is continuous.

Proof.

To simplify notation, we denote
\gamma={\bar{r}}_{kk}
as in (
49
), and
{\bar{\lambda}}=\lambda^{*}/\sigma^{2}
.
To emphasize that
\rho_{k}^{\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{RB}}
is a function of
\gamma
, we rewrite it as
\rho_{k}^{\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{RB}}(\gamma)

In order to prove our claim, we need only to show that
\rho_{k}^{\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{RB}}(\gamma)
and its first derivative with respect to

\gamma>0

From (
39
), when
\gamma
is increasing,
j_{k}
either remains the same or is decreasing.
Let
\gamma^{(i)}

M-i+1=\frac{1}{2}\sqrt{1+\frac{\bar{\lambda}}{(\gamma^{(i)})^{2}}}+1,\ \ i=1,\ldots,M-1.

(60)

Denote

(61)

Then, from (

\gamma\in(\gamma^{(i-1)},\gamma^{(i)}]\Rightarrow j_{k}=M-i+1,\ \ i=1,\ldots,M.

(62)

Note that if

but (
62

(\gamma^{(0)},\gamma^{(1)})=(0,\infty)
(here the
right parenthesis instead of the right bracket has to be used).
Here we consider a general
M
, but the proof can be applied
to the special case that
M=1
by some simplification.

From (
60

i=1,\ldots,M-1
,

\gamma^{(i)}=\sqrt{\frac{{\bar{\lambda}}}{(2(M-i+1)-1)(2(M-i+1)-3)}},

(63)

which will be used later.

From (
41
) and (
42
), it is easy to see that
for any
i=1,\ldots,M
, when
\gamma\in(\gamma^{(i-1)},\gamma^{(i)})

the function
\rho^{\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{RB}}_{k}
is differentiable, and we have

\displaystyle\rho_{k}^{\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{RB}}(\gamma)=\

\displaystyle\frac{p}{2M}+\frac{M-j_{k}}{M}p\,\mathrm{erf}\left(\gamma\right)-\frac{p}{2M}\,\mathrm{erf}\left(\frac{1}{2(2j_{k}-1)}\frac{\bar{\lambda}}{\gamma}-\frac{1}{2}(2j_{k}-1)\gamma\right)

\displaystyle+(1-p)\,\mathrm{erf}\left(\frac{1}{2(2j_{k}-1)}\frac{\bar{\lambda}}{\gamma}+\frac{1}{2}(2j_{k}-1)\gamma\right),

(64)

\displaystyle\frac{\partial\rho_{k}^{\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{RB}}(\gamma)}{\partial\gamma}=\frac{2}{\sqrt{\pi}}\Big[\frac{M-j_{k}}{M}pe^{-\gamma^{2}}+(1-p)(2j_{k}-1)e^{-\left(\frac{1}{2(2j_{k}-1)}\frac{\bar{\lambda}}{\gamma}+\frac{1}{2}(2j_{k}-1)\gamma\right)^{2}}\Big].

(65)

Furthermore, it is easy to see that

\partial\rho_{k}^{\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{RB}}(\gamma)/\partial\gamma
is continuous
when
\gamma\in(\gamma^{(i-1)},\gamma^{(i)})
.
Therefore to prove our claim,
it suffices to show that
\rho^{\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{RB}}_{k}(\gamma)

and
\partial\rho_{k}^{\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{RB}}(\gamma)/\partial\gamma

are continuous at
\gamma^{(i)}
for
i=1,\ldots,M-1
, where
j_{k}
changes,
i.e.,
we need only to show that
for
i=1,\ldots,M-1
,

\displaystyle\rho_{k}^{\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{RB}}(\gamma^{(i)})=\lim_{\gamma\to\gamma^{(i)-}}\rho_{k}^{\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{RB}}(\gamma)=\lim_{\gamma\to\gamma^{(i)+}}\rho_{k}^{\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{RB}}(\gamma),

(66)

\displaystyle\frac{\partial\rho_{k}^{\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{RB}}(\gamma^{(i)})}{\partial\gamma}=\lim_{\gamma\to\gamma^{(i)-}}\frac{\partial\rho_{k}^{\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{RB}}(\gamma)}{\partial\gamma}=\lim_{\gamma\to\gamma^{(i)+}}\frac{\partial\rho_{k}^{\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{RB}}(\gamma)}{\partial\gamma}.

(67)

The first equality of (

62

From (
64
), (
62
) and (
63

can be rewritten as

\displaystyle\frac{p}{2M}+\frac{i-1}{M}p\,\mathrm{erf}\big(\gamma^{(i)}\big)+\frac{p}{2M}\,\mathrm{erf}\big(\gamma^{(i)}\big)+(1-p)\,\mathrm{erf}\big(2(M-i)\gamma^{(i)}\big)

\displaystyle=\

\displaystyle\frac{p}{2M}+\frac{i}{M}p\,\mathrm{erf}\big(\gamma^{(i)}\big)-\frac{p}{2M}\,\mathrm{erf}\big(\gamma^{(i)}\big)+(1-p)\,\mathrm{erf}\big(2(M-i)\gamma^{(i)}\big),

which obviously holds.

Now, to show that the second equality of
(

\displaystyle\frac{i-1}{M}pe^{-\left(\gamma^{(i)}\right)^{2}}+(1-p)(2M-2i+1)e^{\left(2(M-i)\gamma^{(i)}\right)^{2}}

\displaystyle=\

\displaystyle\frac{i}{M}pe^{-\left(\gamma^{(i)}\right)^{2}}+(1-p)(2M-2i-1)e^{\left(2(M-i)\gamma^{(i)}\right)^{2}},

or equivalently,

Taking logarithm on both sides and substituting

\displaystyle-\frac{{\bar{\lambda}}}{(2(M-i+1)-1)(2(M-i+1)-3)}={\bar{\lambda}}-\frac{4(M-i)^{2}{\bar{\lambda}}}{(2(M-i+1)-1)(2(M-i+1)-3)}

which holds for all

To show the first equality of (

In fact,

\displaystyle\lim_{\gamma\to\gamma^{(i)}}\frac{\partial\rho_{k}^{\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{RB}}(\gamma)}{\partial\gamma}

\displaystyle=\lim_{\gamma\to\gamma^{(i)}}\lim_{\epsilon\to 0}\frac{\rho^{\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{RB}}_{k}(\gamma+\epsilon)-\rho^{\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{RB}}_{k}(\gamma)}{\epsilon}=\lim_{\epsilon\to 0}\frac{\rho^{\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{RB}}_{k}(\gamma^{(i)}+\epsilon)-\rho^{\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{RB}}_{k}(\gamma^{(i)})}{\epsilon}=\frac{\partial\rho_{k}^{\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{RB}}(\gamma^{(i)})}{\partial\gamma},

completing the proof.
∎

Proof.

\displaystyle i=\frac{1}{2}\sqrt{1+\frac{{\bar{\lambda}}^{(i)}}{\gamma^{2}}},\quad i=1,\ldots,M-1.

(68)

IV-B

The superiority of
{\mathbf{x}}^{\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{RB}}

In the following we show that
\lambda^{*}
is optimal in maximizing
the SP of the
L_{0}
-regularized Babai point
{\mathbf{x}}^{\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{RB}}
.

Theorem 2
.

Let
\lambda\in[0,\infty)
be an arbitrary regularization parameter
used in
{\mathbf{x}}^{\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{RB}}

and let
\lambda^{*}
be defined in (
8
).
When
\lambda=\lambda^{*}
, the SP of the
L_{0}
-regularized Babai point
{\mathbf{x}}^{\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{RB}}
reaches the global maximum with respect to
\lambda
.
In particular, when
\lambda=\lambda^{*}
, for the box-constrained Babai point
{\mathbf{x}}^{\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{BB}}
,

\Pr({\mathbf{x}}^{\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{RB}}={\mathbf{x}}^{*})\geq\Pr({\mathbf{x}}^{\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{BB}}={\mathbf{x}}^{*}),

(74)

with equality if and only
\lambda^{*}=0
, i.e.,
p=2M/(2M+1)
.

Proof.

In the following we show that for any fixed
{\bar{r}}_{kk}
,
\rho_{k}^{\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{RB}}
reaches the global maximum with respect to
\lambda
when
\lambda=\lambda^{*}
.
This is the key part of the whole proof.

In Lemma
3
we have shown that
\rho^{\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{RB}}_{k}
is continuous with respect to
\lambda
.
From (
69
) we observe that
j_{k}
does not change
when
{\bar{\lambda}}\in[{\bar{\lambda}}^{(i-1)},{\bar{\lambda}}^{(i)})
for any
i=1,\ldots,M
.
Therefore, by (
41
)
\rho_{k}^{\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{RB}}
is differentiable
with respect to
{\bar{\lambda}}\in({\bar{\lambda}}^{(i-1)},{\bar{\lambda}}^{(i)})
.
To simplify notation, let
\eta:=(2j_{k}-1)\gamma
.
When
{\bar{\lambda}}\in[{\bar{\lambda}}^{(i-1)},{\bar{\lambda}}^{(i)})
, from (
41
)
and (
69
) we have

\displaystyle\frac{\partial\rho_{k}^{\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{RB}}}{\partial{\bar{\lambda}}}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{\pi}\eta}\Big[(1-p)e^{-\left(\frac{{\bar{\lambda}}}{2\eta}+\frac{1}{2}\eta\right)^{2}}-\frac{p}{2M}e^{-\left(\frac{{\bar{\lambda}}}{2\eta}-\frac{1}{2}\eta\right)^{2}}\Big],

(75)

which is a right derivative if
{\bar{\lambda}}={\bar{\lambda}}^{(i-1)}
.
Setting
\frac{\partial\rho_{k}^{\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{RB}}}{\partial{\bar{\lambda}}}=0
,
we obtain

\displaystyle(1-p)e^{-\left(\frac{{\bar{\lambda}}}{2\eta}+\frac{1}{2}\eta\right)^{2}}-\frac{p}{2M}e^{-\left(\frac{{\bar{\lambda}}}{2\eta}-\frac{1}{2}\eta\right)^{2}}=0.

(76)

leading to

\displaystyle{\bar{\lambda}}=\ln\frac{1-p}{p/(2M)}.

(77)

Note that
\lambda^{*}=\sigma^{2}\ln\frac{1-p}{p/(2M)}

(see (
8
)).
Thus, from (
77
), the stationary point of
\rho_{k}^{\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{RB}}
with respect to
{\bar{\lambda}}

is
{\bar{\lambda}}^{*}:=\lambda^{*}/\sigma^{2}
.

Now we show that
{\bar{\lambda}}^{*}
is the maximizer of
\rho_{k}^{\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{RB}}
.
Assume that
{\bar{\lambda}}^{\ast}\in[{\bar{\lambda}}^{(i^{\ast}-1)},{\bar{\lambda}}^{(i^{\ast})})

for some
i^{\ast}\in\{1,\ldots,M\}
.
For any
{\bar{\lambda}}\in[{\bar{\lambda}}^{(i-1)},{\bar{\lambda}}^{(i)})
with
i<i^{\ast}
,
we have
j_{k}=i
and
e^{\bar{\lambda}}<\frac{1-p}{p/(2M)}
.
Therefore

\displaystyle\frac{1-p}{p/(2M)}e^{-\left(\frac{{\bar{\lambda}}}{2\eta}+\frac{1}{2}\eta\right)^{2}}-e^{\bar{\lambda}}e^{-\left(\frac{{\bar{\lambda}}}{2\eta}+\frac{1}{2}\eta\right)^{2}}>0.

Then from (
75
) we observe that

\frac{\partial\rho_{k}^{\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{RB}}}{\partial{\bar{\lambda}}}>0
.
Using a symmetrical argument, we can show that for any
{\bar{\lambda}}\in[{\bar{\lambda}}^{(i-1)},{\bar{\lambda}}^{(i)})
with
i>i^{\ast}
,
\frac{\partial\rho_{k}^{\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{RB}}}{\partial{\bar{\lambda}}}<0
.
We can also use the same argument to show that
when
{\bar{\lambda}}\in[{\bar{\lambda}}^{(i^{\ast}-1)},{\bar{\lambda}}^{(i^{\ast})})
,

\frac{\partial\rho_{k}^{\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{RB}}}{\partial{\bar{\lambda}}}>0
when
{\bar{\lambda}}<{\bar{\lambda}}^{\ast}
,
and
\frac{\partial\rho_{k}^{\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{RB}}}{\partial{\bar{\lambda}}}<0
when
{\bar{\lambda}}>{\bar{\lambda}}^{\ast}
.
With the above results and the continuity of
\rho_{k}^{\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{RB}}
with respect to
{\bar{\lambda}}
from Lemma
3
,
we can conclude that
\rho_{k}^{\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{RB}}
is strictly increasing
when
{\bar{\lambda}}<{\bar{\lambda}}^{\ast}
,
and strictly decreasing when
{\bar{\lambda}}>{\bar{\lambda}}^{\ast}
.
Thus,
{\bar{\lambda}}^{\ast}
is the only
global maximizer of
\rho_{k}^{\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{RB}}
with respect to
{\bar{\lambda}}
,
or equivalently
\lambda^{\ast}
is the only
global maximum of
\rho_{k}^{\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{RB}}
with respect to
\lambda
.
This concludes the proof for our claim.

Using the result above, from (
40
) we see
it is obvious that the SP of
{\mathbf{x}}^{\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{RB}}

reaches the global maximum with respect to
\lambda
if and only if
\lambda=\lambda^{*}
.

Recall that when
\lambda=0
,
{\mathbf{x}}^{\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{RB}}
becomes
{\mathbf{x}}^{\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{BB}}
.
Thus the inequality (
74
) must hold
and it becomes an equality if and only if
\lambda^{*}=0
,
i.e.,
p=2M/(2M+1)
.
∎

Theorem 4
.

Let the regularization parameter
\lambda
used by

be chosen as

defined by (

The ratio
\Pr({\mathbf{x}}^{\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{RB}}={\mathbf{x}}^{\ast})/\Pr({\mathbf{x}}^{\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{BB}}={\mathbf{x}}^{\ast})

Proof.

From Theorem

, we have

\frac{\Pr({\mathbf{x}}^{\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{RB}}={\mathbf{x}}^{\ast})}{\Pr({\mathbf{x}}^{\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{BB}}={\mathbf{x}}^{\ast})}=\prod_{k=1}^{n}\frac{\rho^{\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{RB}}_{k}}{\rho^{\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{BB}}_{k}}.

Since

\rho^{\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{RB}}_{k}/\rho^{\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{BB}}_{k}=(\rho^{\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{RB}}_{k}-\rho^{\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{BB}}_{k})/\rho^{\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{BB}}_{k}+1

it suffices to show that

is

\rho^{\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{BB}}_{k}

To simplify notation, we use
\gamma
to replace

in the expressions for

\rho^{\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{BB}}_{k}

From (

\displaystyle>0.

The remaining part is to show that

is

In the proof we will use the following relation between

and

\displaystyle\hskip-5.69054pt\frac{\partial\rho^{\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{RB}}_{k}}{\partial p}=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2M}+\frac{M-j_{k}}{M}\mathrm{erf}(\gamma)-\frac{1}{2M}\mathrm{erf}\left(\frac{{\bar{\lambda}}}{2\eta}-\frac{1}{2}\eta\right)+\frac{1}{2\sqrt{\pi}M(1-p)\eta}e^{-\left(\frac{{\bar{\lambda}}}{2\eta}-\frac{1}{2}\eta\right)^{2}}

-regularized Babai point

In this section, we give a bound on the SP of
{\mathbf{x}}^{\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{RB}}
, which is an upper bound and a lower bound under different conditions respectively.
The bound is invariant with respect to the column permutations of
\mathbf{A}

and it will help us to understand the limit on SP that column permutations
can achieve, as well as to provide us a motivation for the application of LLL-P strategy.
The approach we use in our proof can be regarded as an
extension of that used in
[
18
]
, which deals with an un-regularized problem.
Here the proof is more complicated due to the discontinuity issue.

To prepare for the proof of our theorem, we first define our target function and introduce a lemma.
We are interested in the effect of
{\bar{r}}_{kk}
on
\rho^{\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{RB}}_{k}
in (
41
).
For convenience, we use
\gamma
to replace
{\bar{r}}_{kk}

in (

as

\displaystyle\rho(\gamma)=\

\displaystyle\frac{p}{2M}+\frac{M-j_{\gamma}}{M}p\,\mathrm{erf}\left(\gamma\right)-\frac{p}{2M}\,\mathrm{erf}\left(\frac{1}{2(2j_{\gamma}-1)}\frac{\bar{\lambda}}{\gamma}-\frac{1}{2}(2j_{\gamma}-1)\gamma\right)

\displaystyle+(1-p)\,\mathrm{erf}\left(\frac{1}{2(2j_{\gamma}-1)}\frac{\bar{\lambda}}{\gamma}+\frac{1}{2}(2j_{\gamma}-1)\gamma\right),

(84)

where we have abused the notation a little bit by replacing the original

\displaystyle\min\left\{\frac{r_{k-1,k-1}}{\sqrt{2}\sigma},\frac{r_{kk}}{\sqrt{2}\sigma}\right\}\leq\sqrt{\beta}\leq\max\left\{\frac{r_{k-1,k-1}}{\sqrt{2}\sigma},\frac{r_{kk}}{\sqrt{2}\sigma}\right\}.

Replacing
r_{k-1,k-1}
and
r_{kk}
with
\hat{r}_{k-1,k-1}
and
\hat{r}_{kk}
respectively, the above inequalities still hold.
Using the condition that
\delta\,r_{k-1,k-1}^{2}>r_{k-1,k}^{2}+r_{kk}^{2}

with
\delta\in(1/4,1]
and the equalities in (
21
), we can obtain

\displaystyle\frac{\beta}{r_{k-1,k-1}/(\sqrt{2}\sigma)}

\displaystyle=\min\left\{\frac{r_{k-1,k-1}}{\sqrt{2}\sigma},\frac{r_{kk}}{\sqrt{2}\sigma}\right\}<\min\left\{\frac{\hat{r}_{k-1,k-1}}{\sqrt{2}\sigma},\frac{\hat{r}_{kk}}{\sqrt{2}\sigma}\right\}\leq\sqrt{\beta},

(94)

\displaystyle\sqrt{\beta}

\displaystyle\leq\max\left\{\frac{\hat{r}_{k-1,k-1}}{\sqrt{2}\sigma},\frac{\hat{r}_{kk}}{\sqrt{2}\sigma}\right\}<\max\left\{\frac{r_{k-1,k-1}}{\sqrt{2}\sigma},\frac{r_{kk}}{\sqrt{2}\sigma}\right\}=\frac{\beta}{r_{kk}/(\sqrt{2}\sigma)}.

(95)

First, we assume
r_{k-1,k-1}\leq\sqrt{2}\sigma\mu_{1}
. Since the permutation only affects columns
k
and
k-1
, other diagonal elements remain the same. By Theorem
1
, to prove (
92
) it suffices to show

\displaystyle\rho(r_{k-1,k-1}/(\sqrt{2}\sigma))\cdot\rho(r_{k,k}/(\sqrt{2}\sigma))\geq\rho(\hat{r}_{k-1,k-1}/(\sqrt{2}\sigma))\cdot\rho(\hat{r}_{k,k}/(\sqrt{2}\sigma)),

which is equivalent to

\displaystyle\Phi\left(\min\left\{\frac{r_{k-1,k-1}}{\sqrt{2}\sigma},\frac{r_{kk}}{\sqrt{2}\sigma}\right\},\beta\right)\geq\Phi\left(\min\left\{\frac{\hat{r}_{k-1,k-1}}{\sqrt{2}\sigma},\frac{\hat{r}_{kk}}{\sqrt{2}\sigma}\right\},\beta\right).

(96)

When
r_{k-1,k-1}\leq\sqrt{2}\sigma\mu_{1}
, then
\frac{\beta}{\mu_{1}}\leq\frac{\beta}{r_{k-1,k-1}/(\sqrt{2}\sigma)}
. Thus from (
94
),

\displaystyle\frac{\beta}{\mu_{1}}

\displaystyle\leq\min\left\{\frac{r_{k-1,k-1}}{\sqrt{2}\sigma},\frac{r_{kk}}{\sqrt{2}\sigma}\right\}<\min\left\{\frac{\hat{r}_{k-1,k-1}}{\sqrt{2}\sigma},\frac{\hat{r}_{kk}}{\sqrt{2}\sigma}\right\}\leq\sqrt{\beta},

and by Lemma
5
the strict inequality in (
96
) holds.

Now we assume
r_{kk}\geq\sqrt{2}\sigma\mu_{2}
.
Using the same reasoning as before, to prove (
93
) it suffices to show

\displaystyle\Phi\left(\max\left\{\frac{r_{k-1,k-1}}{\sqrt{2}\sigma},\frac{r_{kk}}{\sqrt{2}\sigma}\right\},\beta\right)\leq\Phi\left(\max\left\{\frac{\hat{r}_{k-1,k-1}}{\sqrt{2}\sigma},\frac{\hat{r}_{kk}}{\sqrt{2}\sigma}\right\},\beta\right).

(97)

When
r_{kk}\geq\sqrt{2}\sigma\mu_{2}
, we have
\frac{\beta}{r_{kk}/(\sqrt{2}\sigma)}\leq\frac{\beta}{\mu_{2}}
. Thus from (
95
),

\displaystyle\sqrt{\beta}

\displaystyle\leq\max\left\{\frac{\hat{r}_{k-1,k-1}}{\sqrt{2}\sigma},\frac{\hat{r}_{kk}}{\sqrt{2}\sigma}\right\}<\max\left\{\frac{r_{k-1,k-1}}{\sqrt{2}\sigma},\frac{r_{kk}}{\sqrt{2}\sigma}\right\}\leq\frac{\beta}{\mu_{2}}.

By Lemma
5
the strict inequality in (
97
) holds.
∎

VI
Success probability based column permutation strategies

In this section, we propose three SP based column permutation strategies to increase the SP of the Babai point
{\mathbf{x}}^{\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{RB}}
.
Specially, we propose a local greedy strategy, a global greedy strategy and a mixed one.

VI-A

A local success probability based strategy LSP

In this subsection, we propose a local success probability based strategy,
abbreviated as LSP,
which turns out to be the V-BLAST permutation strategy.

We would like to develop a greedy permutation strategy using
\rho_{k}^{\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{RB}}
(see (
41
)) as our measure.
To determine the last column,
for
j=1,2,\ldots,n
, we interchange columns
j
and
n
of
\mathbf{R}
(when
j=n
,
\mathbf{R}
is just
the original one)
and then return the matrix to upper triangular
by a series of Givens rotations applied from the left.
Denote the resulting upper triangular matrix by
\mathbf{R}^{(n,j)}
.
Then we can obtain
\rho_{n}^{\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{RB}}

by the formula (
41
) (i.e., the SP of the
L_{0}
-regularized Babai point at level
n
).
We rewrite it as
\rho_{n}^{\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{RB}}(r_{nn}^{(n,j)})
as it is a function of
r_{nn}^{(n,j)}
.
We would like to find column
j
such that
\rho_{n}^{\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{RB}}(r_{nn}^{(n,j)})
is maximal, i.e.,

p_{n}=\argmax_{j}\rho_{n}^{\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{RB}}(r_{nn}^{(n,j)}).

(101)

Then the
p_{n}
-th column of
\mathbf{R}
is put as the last column
and
\mathbf{R}
is updated to be upper triangular.
After that we recurse on the submatrix
(r_{ij})_{i,j\in\{1,\ldots,n-1\}}
.
We repeat the process until all
columns of the matrix are reordered.

By Theorem
3
,
\rho_{n}^{\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{RB}}(r_{nn}^{(n,j)})
is strictly increasing
for
r_{nn}\in(0,\infty)
.
Therefore, we have

p_{n}:=\argmax_{j}r_{nn}^{(n,j)}.

(102)

This indicates that this LSP permutation strategy is just the V-BLAST strategy, see Algorithm
2
.

Remark 14
.

The LSP permutation strategy was proposed in the conference paper
[
36
]
,
but it was not realized there that it is just the V-BLAST strategy,
because in
[
36
]
the SP formula of
{\mathbf{x}}^{\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{RB}}

is more complicated and the monotonicity of
\rho_{k}^{\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{RB}}
with respect to
r_{kk}

was not established.

VI-B

A global success probability based strategy GSP

When determining the
k
-th column of
\mathbf{R}
, the LSP strategy or the V-BLAST strategy chooses a column to
maximize the SP of
x_{k}^{\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{RB}}
at level
k
,
and it ignores
its impact on the SP of
x_{i}^{\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{RB}}
for
i<k
.
It turns out that applying LSP may decrease the SP of
{\mathbf{x}}^{\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{RB}}
.

To guarantee that the SP will increase (not strictly) after the columns of
\mathbf{R}
are permuted,
we propose a new strategy based on the SP formula (
40
).

Here we show how to determine the last column. Other columns can be determined similarly.
For
j=1,2,\ldots,n
we interchange the columns
j
and
n
of
\mathbf{R}
,
and then return the matrix to upper triangular by a series of Givens rotations applied from the left.
Denote the resulting upper triangular matrix by
\mathbf{R}^{(n,j)}
.
Then we can obtain
P^{\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{RB}}(\mathbf{R}^{(n,j)})
,
the SP of the
L_{0}
-regularized Babai point corresponding to
\mathbf{R}^{(n,j)}
, see (
40
).
We would like to find column
p_{n}
such that
P^{\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{RB}}(\mathbf{R}^{(n,p_{n})})
is maximal, i.e.,

p_{n}=\argmax_{j}P^{\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{RB}}(\mathbf{R}^{(n,j)}).

(103)

Note that

P^{\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{RB}}(\mathbf{R}^{(n,p_{n})})\geq P^{\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{RB}}(\mathbf{R}^{(n,n)}),

where
\mathbf{R}^{(n,n)}
is the original
\mathbf{R}
.
Thus by interchanging columns
p_{n}
and
n
, the SP of the Babai point increases.
Then we work with the
(n-1)\times(n-1)
leading principle submatrix of

to determine its last column in the same way.
We repeat the process until all columns are reordered.
We refer to this as the global success probability based
permutation strategy (in short GSP),
and the algorithm is described in Algorithm

VI-C

As we will see later in the numerical experiments in Section

\sigma

However, the former does not guarantee that the SP will increase, while the latter does.
This motivates us to propose a mixed strategy, which has the advantages

As before, we describe how to determine the

The procedure can be applied recursively to determine other columns.

\mathbf{R}

VIII
Summary and Conclusion

We have derived a formula for
the SP of the
L_{0}
-regularized Babai point
{\mathbf{x}}^{\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{RB}}
under
an
L_{0}
-RBILS problem setting to detect
{\mathbf{x}}^{*}
in the linear model
(
1
).
We have shown that
with the regularization parameter
\lambda
given by (
8
),

{\mathbf{x}}^{\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{RB}}
is more preferable than the unregularized
box-constrained Babai point
{\mathbf{x}}^{\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{BB}}

in terms of success probability.
Furthermore, we have shown that the SP of
{\mathbf{x}}^{\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{RB}}

monotonically increases with respect to the magnitude of the diagonal entries of the factor
\mathbf{R}
obtained from the QR factorization of the model matrix
\mathbf{A}
,
and that the ratio of
the SP of
{\mathbf{x}}^{\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{RB}}
to that of
{\mathbf{x}}^{\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{BB}}

monotonically decreases as
p
increases within its domain.
Additionally, a bound on the SP of
{\mathbf{x}}^{\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{RB}}
has been derived,
which acts as a lower or upper bound
depending on the conditions satisfied.

When columns of the model matrix
\mathbf{A}
are re-ordered,

{\mathbf{x}}^{\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{RB}}
changes, so does its SP.
We have shown that applying the LLL-P permutation strategy guarantees
to increase or decrease the SP depending on
the conditions satisfied.
Then three SP based permutation strategies have been proposed,
aiming to increase the SP of
{\mathbf{x}}^{\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{RB}}
.
The first strategy LSP chooses the ordering greedily
according to the local SP value at each level,
which produces the same ordering as the existing V-BLAST strategy.
LSP can have great performance, however a major disadvantage
is that it can decrease the SP.
The second strategy GSP, on the other hand,
chooses the ordering greedily according to the global SP
and guarantees the SP not to decrease.
The third strategy MSP combines the advantages of LSP and GSP,
having performance close to LSP when LSP performs well,
while guaranteeing the SP not to decrease.

The idea of the proposed approach for column permutations is quite general. It can be applied to other Babai estimation problems, such as the box constrained Babai estimation, where each entry of the true integer parameter vector is
uniformly distributed over
{\mathcal{X}}/\{0\}
, i.e., the alphabet set
{\mathcal{X}}
without 0 included.

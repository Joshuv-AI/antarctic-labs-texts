Abstract
We propose and analyze a mixed finite element method for the spatial approximation of a time-fractional Fokker–Planck equation in a convex polyhedral domain, where the given driving force is a function of space. Taking into account the limited smoothing properties of the model, and considering an appropriate splitting of the errors, we employed a sequence of clever energy arguments to show optimal convergence rates with respect to both approximation properties and regularity results. In particular, error bounds for both primary and secondary variables are derived in L 2 L^{2} -norm for cases with smooth and nonsmooth initial data. We further investigate a fully implicit time-stepping scheme based on a convolution quadrature in time generated by the backward Euler method. Our main result provides pointwise-in-time optimal L 2 L^{2} -error estimates for the primary variable. Numerical examples are then presented to illustrate the theoretical contributions.



Keywords.

fractional Fokker–Planck equation, mixed finite element method, convolution quadrature, error analysis, smooth and nonsmooth initial data.

Introduction

Consider a bounded convex polygonal domain
\Omega
in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
with a boundary
\partial\Omega
, and let
T>0
be a fixed value. The primary objective of this paper is to develop and analyze a mixed finite element method (FEM) for solving the time-fractional Fokker-Planck model problem
[
27
,
11
]
: with
0<\alpha<1,

\partial_{t}u-\nabla\cdot\bigl(^{R}\partial_{t}^{1-\alpha}\kappa\nabla u-{\bf F}\,^{R}\partial_{t}^{1-\alpha}u\bigr)=0,\quad\mbox{ in }\Omega\times(0,T],

(1)

subject to homogeneous Dirichlet boundary conditions, with
u(x,0)=u_{0}(x)
at the initial time
t=0.
Here
\partial_{t}=\partial/\partial t
, the time-independent diffusivity
\kappa\in L^{\infty}(\Omega)
and bounded below by
c_{0}>0
. In (
1
), the Riemann-Liouville fractional derivative
{}^{R}\partial_{t}^{1-\alpha}=\frac{d}{dt}\mathcal{I}^{\alpha}
, where the time-fractional integral

\mathcal{I}^{\alpha}{\varphi}(t):=\int_{0}^{t}\omega_{\alpha}(t-s)\varphi(s)\,ds\quad\text{with}\quad\omega_{\alpha}(t):=\frac{t^{\alpha-1}}{\Gamma(\alpha)}.

We assume that the time-independent driving force
{\bf F}
and its divergence
\nabla\cdot{\bf F}
are bounded in
\Omega
. Since
{\bf F}
is independent of
t
, applying
\mathcal{I}^{1-\alpha}
to (
1
) and using
\mathcal{I}^{1-\alpha}(^{R}\partial_{t}^{1-\alpha}u)=u

yield the alternative formulation

\partial_{t}^{\alpha}u+{\cal L}u=0\quad\mbox{ on }\Omega\times(0,T],

(2)

where the second-order non-selfadjoint operator
{\cal L}u=-\nabla\cdot(\kappa\nabla u)+\nabla\cdot({\bf F}u)
. In (
2
),
\partial_{t}^{\alpha}u(t):=\mathcal{I}^{1-\alpha}{\partial_{t}u}(t)
is the
time-fractional Caputo derivative of order
\alpha
(
0<\alpha<1
). As
\alpha
approaches
1^{-}
,
\partial_{t}^{\alpha}u
converges to
\partial_{t}u
, and consequently, problem (
1
) (equivalently (
2
)) reduces to the standard Fokker-Planck equation. For a comprehensive analysis of the well-posedness of the model problem (
1
) with a general driving force
{\bf F}
, we refer the reader to
[
25
]
.

Various authors have studied the numerical solution of (
2
), mostly for a
1D spatial domain and a smooth continuous solution (which is not practically the case). For instance, Deng
[
7
]
transferred (
2
) to a system of fractional ordinary differential equations (ODEs) by discretizing the spatial derivatives, and then applied a predictor-corrector approach combined with the method of lines. Cao et al.
[
4

adopted a similar approach and solved the resulting system using a second-order backward Euler scheme.
Chen et al.
[
5
]
investigated three different implicit finite difference techniques, in each of which the diffusion term was approximated by the standard
second-order difference approximation at the advanced time level. In contrast, Jiang
[
13
]
established monotonicity properties of the numerical solutions obtained by using these schemes, and so showed that the time-stepping preserves the non-negativity of the solution. Jiang and Xu
[
14
]
proposed a finite volume method, and Yang et al.
[
34
]
a spectral collocation method. Fairweather et al.
[
9
]
developed an orthogonal spline collocation method in space combined with the backward Euler method in time. Saadmandi
[
31
]
studied a collocation method based on shifted Legendre polynomials in time and Sinc functions in space. Vong and Wang
[
33
]
have analyzed a high-order, compact difference scheme. Cui
[
6
]
applied a high-order approximation for the time-fractional derivative combined with a compact exponential finite difference scheme.

For problem (
1
) with a time-space dependent
{\bf F}
, first-order backward Euler and second-order corrected Crank-Nicolson time-stepping schemes were studied in
[
20
,
12
]
and
[
29
]
, respectively. The stability and error analyses were carried out under certain regularity assumptions on the given data excluding the case of nonsmooth initial data, this is in addition to some restrictions on the range of the fractional exponent
\alpha.
For the spatial discretization, a continuous, piecewise-linear Galerkin FEM was considered in a series of papers, see the recent papers
[
21
,
26
]
and related references therein. In
[
26
]
, based on novel energy arguments,
\alpha
-robust stability, and optimal convergence results were investigated for both cases of smooth and nonsmooth initial data.

In the special case
{\bf F}\equiv 0
in (
1
), mixed FEM were studied only in
[
35
,
16
,
17
,
18
]
. In
[
35
]
, the authors established mixed finite element error analysis under higher (unpractical) regularity assumption on the continuous solution
u
. Subsequently, Karaa
[
16
,
17
]
has exploited the inverse of two mixed operators and derived optimal error estimates allowing nonsmooth initial data. The studies included both semidiscrete and fully discrete schemes. Later on, an alternative approach was adopted in
[
18
]
to handle the case of the time-dependent diffusivity coefficient. The error analyses in these papers are not applicable for the case of non-zero
{\bf F}
, and extending it to cover this case is not feasible.

The main aim of this work is: (1) develop a spatial mixed FEM for problem (
2
) and derive
\alpha
-robust (that is, remains valid as
\alpha
approaches
1
) error estimates which are not only optimal in approximation property but also in regularity of the solution; (2) combine our mixed FEM with a convolution quadrature generated by the backward Euler method in time and show optimal error estimates for the primary variable. Our convergence analysis approach involves splitting the errors into two distinct components. Then, a suitable exploitation of the results
[
16
]
and
[
17
]
, and the use of a sequence of challenging energy arguments in combination with general weakly singular Gronwall inequalities (see
[

and
[
8

for the continuous version and discrete version, respectively) allow us to prove the required optimal error bounds for both smooth and nonsmooth initial data.

The paper is organized as follows. In Section

-error estimate is proved in Theorem

3
Mixed FEM

In this section, we consider the mixed form of problem (
2
). Introduce

{\boldsymbol{\sigma}}=-\kappa\nabla u+{\bf F}u
so that
\kappa^{-1}{\boldsymbol{\sigma}}+\nabla u=\beta u
where
\beta=\kappa^{-1}{\bf F}.
Our model problem can now be formulated as

\partial_{t}^{\alpha}u+\nabla\cdot{\boldsymbol{\sigma}}=0,\quad u=0\;\mbox{ on }\partial\Omega,\quad{\rm with}~~u(0)=u_{0}.

Let
H(div;\Omega)=\{{\bf w}\in(L^{2}(\Omega))^{2}:\nabla\cdot{\bf w}\in L^{2}(\Omega)\}

be a Hilbert space equipped with the norm
\|{\bf w}\|_{H(div;\Omega)}=(\|{\bf w}\|^{2}+\|\nabla\cdot{\bf w}\|^{2})^{\frac{1}{2}}
. Then, with
V=L^{2}(\Omega)
and
{\bf W}=H(div;\Omega)

1

(u,{\boldsymbol{\sigma}}):(0,T]\to V\times{\bf W}
such that

\displaystyle(\partial_{t}^{\alpha}u,v)+(\nabla\cdot{\boldsymbol{\sigma}},v)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 0\quad\forall v\in V,

(9)

\displaystyle(\kappa^{-1}{\boldsymbol{\sigma}},{\bf w})-(u,\nabla\cdot{\bf w})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(\beta u,{\bf w})\quad\forall{\bf w}\in{\bf W},

(10)

with
u(0)=u_{0}
. Note that the homogeneous Dirichlet boundary conditions of
u

\partial\Omega
are implicitly contained in (
10

For the semidiscrete mixed formulation corresponding to (
9
)-(
10

of the polygonal convex domain

V_{h}=\{v\in L^{2}(\Omega):\;v|_{K}\in P_{\ell}(K)\;\forall K\in{\mathcal{T}}_{h}\}

{\bf W}_{h}=\{{\bf v}\in{\bf W}:\;{\bf v}|_{K}\in RT_{\ell}(K)\;\forall K\in{\mathcal{T}}_{h}\},

RT_{\ell}(K)=(P_{\ell}(K))^{2}+\boldsymbol{x}P_{\ell}(K),

, where

Further, the following approximation properties hold:

||{\bf w}-\Pi_{h}{\bf w}||\leq Ch^{r}||\nabla\cdot{\bf w}||_{r-1}~~{\rm and}~~\|v-P_{h}v\|\leq Ch^{r}\|v\|_{r}\;\forall~1\leq r\leq\ell+1.

(11)

Additionally, it’s worth noting that

(\nabla\cdot(\Pi_{h}{\bf w}-{\bf w}),v_{h})=0\;\forall v_{h}\in V_{h}\quad\text{and}\quad(P_{h}v-v,\nabla\cdot{\bf w}_{h})=0\;\forall{\bf w}_{h}\in{\bf W}_{h}.

\displaystyle(\kappa^{-1}{\boldsymbol{\sigma}}_{h},{\bf w}_{h})-(u_{h},\nabla\cdot{\bf w}_{h})

\displaystyle=

10
]
. For convenience, define

\displaystyle(\partial_{t}^{\alpha}\bar{u}_{h},v_{h})+(\nabla\cdot\bar{\boldsymbol{\sigma}}_{h},v_{h})

\displaystyle 0\quad\forall v_{h}\in V_{h},

\displaystyle(\kappa^{-1}\bar{\boldsymbol{\sigma}}_{h},{\bf w}_{h})-(\bar{u}_{h},\nabla\cdot{\bf w}_{h})

\displaystyle=

(15)

with

\tilde{\phi}_{u}=\bar{u}_{h}-u

\displaystyle(\kappa^{-1}\bar{\phi}_{\boldsymbol{\sigma}},{\bf w}_{h})-(\tilde{\phi}_{u},\nabla\cdot{\bf w}_{h})

From (

)-(

) and (

\displaystyle(\partial_{t}^{\alpha}\bar{e}_{u},v_{h})+(\nabla\cdot\bar{e}_{\boldsymbol{\sigma}},v_{h})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 0\quad\forall v_{h}\in V_{h},

\displaystyle(\kappa^{-1}\bar{e}_{\boldsymbol{\sigma}},{\bf w}_{h})-(\bar{e}_{u},\nabla\cdot{\bf w}_{h})

Theorem 5

Let the pairs

\partial_{\tau}^{\alpha}(\|(t\bar{\cal E}_{u})^{n}\|^{2})+2\|\sqrt{\kappa^{-1}}(t_{n}\bar{\cal E}_{\boldsymbol{\sigma}}^{n})\|^{2}\leq C\|t_{n}{\cal E}_{u}^{n}\|^{2}+\|\sqrt{\kappa^{-1}}(t_{n}\bar{\cal E}_{\boldsymbol{\sigma}}^{n})\|^{2}+C{\|\partial_{\tau}^{-1}\bar{\cal E}_{\boldsymbol{\sigma}}^{n-1}\|^{2}}.

Cancelling similar terms, applying
\partial_{\tau}^{-\alpha}
and using the estimate in Lemma
8
,

\|t_{n}\bar{\cal E}_{u}^{n}\|^{2}\leq C\partial_{\tau}^{-\alpha}(\|(t\bar{\cal E}_{u})^{n}\|^{2})+C\max_{1\leq j\leq n}\Big(\partial_{\tau}^{-\alpha}(\|(t\bar{\Phi}_{u})^{j}\|^{2})+t_{j-1}^{1-\alpha}\partial_{\tau}^{-1}(\|(\partial_{\tau}^{-\alpha}\bar{\Phi}_{u})\|^{2})^{j-1}\Big).

If the time step
\tau
satisfies
Ca_{0}^{(-\alpha)}\tau^{\alpha}<1
, then an application of a weakly singular discrete Gronwall inequality yields
the desired estimate in the lemma.
∎

We are ready to show optimal error estimates for the fully discrete solution.

Theorem 10
.

Let
(u,{\boldsymbol{\sigma}})
be the solutions of (
9
)-(
10
). Let
(U_{h}^{n},\Sigma_{h}^{n})
be the solution of (
33
)-(
34
) with
u_{0h}=P_{h}u_{0}
.
Then, for a small time step
\tau
, we have

\|U_{h}^{n}-u(t_{n})\|\leq C(\tau t_{n}^{\alpha\delta/2-1}+h^{2}t_{n}^{-\alpha(2-\delta)/2})\|u_{0}\|_{\dot{H}^{\delta}(\Omega)},\quad\delta\in[0,2].

(49)

Proof.

Using the first estimate in Theorem
7
and the fact that

\partial_{\tau}^{-\alpha}(t_{n}^{\beta})\leq Ct_{n}^{\alpha+\beta}
for
\beta>-1
, we find that

\partial_{\tau}^{-\alpha}(\|(t\bar{\Phi}_{u})^{n}\|^{2})\leq C\left(\tau^{2}t_{n}^{\alpha(\delta+1)}+h^{4}t_{n}^{-\alpha(1-\delta)+2}\right),

and

\partial_{\tau}^{-1}(\|\partial_{\tau}^{-\alpha}\bar{\Phi}_{u}\|^{2})^{n}\leq C\left(\tau^{2}t_{n}^{\alpha(\delta+2)-1}+h^{4}t_{n}^{\alpha\delta+1}\right),

for
\delta\in[0,2]
. Then, by Lemma
9
, we see that

\|t_{n}\bar{\cal E}_{u}^{n}\|^{2}\leq C\left(\tau^{2}t_{n}^{\alpha(\delta+1)}+h^{4}t_{n}^{-\alpha(1-\delta)+2}\right).

(50)

The desired estimate (
49
) follows now by using the triangle inequality and the first estimate in Theorem
7
.
∎

Remark 11
.

To show the estimate for
\bar{\cal E}_{\boldsymbol{\sigma}}^{n}
, we operate
\partial_{\tau}^{\alpha}
on (
47
) so that

(\kappa^{-1}\partial_{\tau}^{\alpha}(t\bar{\cal E}_{\boldsymbol{\sigma}})^{n},{\bf w}_{h})-(\partial_{\tau}^{\alpha}(t\bar{\cal E}_{u})^{n},\nabla\cdot{\bf w}_{h})=(\beta\partial_{\tau}^{\alpha}(t{\cal E}_{u})^{n},{\bf w}_{h}).

Next, we choose
{\bf w}_{h}=t_{n}\bar{\cal E}_{\boldsymbol{\sigma}}^{n}
and
v_{h}=\partial_{\tau}^{\alpha}(t\bar{\cal E}_{u})^{n}
in (
46
) to arrive at

\|\partial_{\tau}^{\alpha}(t\bar{\cal E}_{u})^{n}\|^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\partial_{\tau}^{\alpha}\|\sqrt{\kappa^{-1}}t_{n}\bar{\cal E}_{\boldsymbol{\sigma}}^{n}\|^{2}\\
\leq C\left(\|\partial_{\tau}^{\alpha}(t\bar{\Phi}_{u})^{n}\|^{2}+\|\partial_{\tau}^{\alpha-1}\bar{\cal E}_{u}^{{n-1}}\|^{2}+\|\sqrt{\kappa^{-1}}t_{n}\bar{\cal E}_{\boldsymbol{\sigma}}^{n}\|^{2}\right)+\frac{1}{2}\|\partial_{\tau}^{\alpha}(t\bar{\cal E}_{u})^{n})\|^{2}.

Canceling the similar terms, then applying
\partial_{\tau}^{-\alpha}
to both sides,

\|\sqrt{\kappa^{-1}}t_{n}\bar{\cal E}_{\boldsymbol{\sigma}}^{n}\|^{2}\leq C\max_{1\leq j\leq n}\Big(\partial_{\tau}^{-\alpha}\Big(\|\partial_{\tau}^{\alpha}(t\bar{\Phi}_{u})\|^{2}\Big)^{j}+\partial_{\tau}^{-\alpha}\Big(\|\partial_{\tau}^{\alpha-1}\bar{\cal E}_{u}\|^{2}\Big)^{j-1}\Big)\\
+C\partial_{\tau}^{-\alpha}\left(\|\sqrt{\kappa^{-1}}(t\bar{\cal E}_{\boldsymbol{\sigma}})^{n}\|^{2}\right)\,.

Thus, if the time step
\tau
is such that
Ca_{0}^{(-\alpha)}\tau^{\alpha}<1
, then an application of a weakly singular discrete Gronwall inequality yields

t_{n}^{2}\|\sqrt{\kappa}\bar{\cal E}_{\boldsymbol{\sigma}}^{n}\|^{2}\leq C\,\max_{1\leq j\leq n}\Big(\partial_{\tau}^{-\alpha}\Big(\|\partial_{\tau}^{\alpha}(t\bar{\Phi}_{u})\|^{2}\Big)^{j}+\partial_{\tau}^{-\alpha}\Big(\|\partial_{\tau}^{\alpha-1}\bar{\cal E}_{u}\|^{2}\Big)^{j-1}\Big),\quad n\geq 1.

The second term
\|\partial_{\tau}^{\alpha-1}\bar{\cal E}_{u}^{j-1}\|
on the right-hand side can be readily bounded using the estimate (
50
). However, deriving an appropriate bound for the first term
\partial_{\tau}^{-\alpha}\left(\|\partial_{\tau}^{\alpha}(t\bar{\Phi}_{u})\|\right)^{j}
which preserves the convergence rate seems to be a challenging task.

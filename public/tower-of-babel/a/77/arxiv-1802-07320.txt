Degrees of extensionality in the theory of Böhm trees and Sallé’s conjecture.
Benedetto Intrigila, Giulio Manzonetto, Andrew Polonsky

Abstract
. The main observational equivalences of the untyped λ \lambda -calculus have been characterized in terms of extensional equalities between Böhm trees. It is well known that the λ \lambda -theory ℋ ∗ \mathcal{H^{*}} , arising by taking as observables the head normal forms, equates two λ \lambda -terms whenever their Böhm trees are equal up to countably many possibly infinite η \eta -expansions. Similarly, two λ \lambda -terms are equal in Morris’s original observational theory ℋ + \mathcal{H^{+}} , generated by considering as observable the β \beta -normal forms, whenever their Böhm trees are equal up to countably many finite η \eta -expansions. The λ \lambda -calculus also possesses a strong notion of extensionality called the ω \omega -rule , which has been the subject of many investigations. It is a longstanding open problem whether the equivalence ℬ ​ ω {\mathcal{B}\omega} obtained by closing the theory of Böhm trees under the ω \omega -rule is strictly included in ℋ + \mathcal{H^{+}} , as conjectured by Sallé in the seventies. In this paper we demonstrate that the two aforementioned theories actually coincide, thus disproving Sallé’s conjecture. The inclusion ℬ ​ ω ⊆ ℋ + {\mathcal{B}\omega}\subseteq\mathcal{H^{+}} is a consequence of the fact that the λ \lambda -theory ℋ + \mathcal{H^{+}} satisfies the ω \omega -rule. This result follows from a weak form of separability enjoyed by λ \lambda -terms whose Böhm trees only differ because of some infinite η \eta -expansions, a property which is proved through a refined version of the famous Böhm-out technique. The inclusion ℋ + ⊆ ℬ ​ ω \mathcal{H^{+}}\subseteq{\mathcal{B}\omega} follows from the fact that whenever two λ \lambda -terms are observationally equivalent in ℋ + \mathcal{H^{+}} their Böhm trees have a common “ η \eta -supremum” that can be λ \lambda -defined starting from a stream (infinite sequence) of η \eta -expansions of the identity. It turns out that in ℬ ​ ω {\mathcal{B}\omega} such a stream is equal to the stream containing infinitely many copies of the identity, a peculiar property that actually makes the two theories collapse. The proof technique we develop for proving the latter inclusion is general enough to provide as a byproduct a new characterization, based on bounded η \eta -expansions, of the least extensional equality between Böhm trees. Together, these results provide a taxonomy of the different degrees of extensionality in the theory of Böhm trees.

We build on the characterizations of

In Section

It is worth mentioning that such characterizations of

A key ingredient in our proof of

is the fact that
\lambda

This is related to the theory of self-interpreters in

9

As a presentation choice, we decided to use the encoding described in Barendregt’s book

1.1.
Coinduction

Throughout this paper, we often consider
possibly infinite trees as coinductive objects and perform coinductive reasoning.
Here we recall some basic facts and introduce some terminology, but we mainly assume that the reader is familiar with these concepts.
If that is not the case, we suggest the following tutorials on the subject
[
24
,
25
]
.

A
coinductive structure
, also known as
coinductive datatype
, is the greatest fixed point over a grammar, or equivalently the final coalgebra over the corresponding signature.
We also consider
coinductive relations
, that are the greatest relations over such coinductive structures that respect the structural constraints.
A coinductive proof that two elements of the structures stand in
relation to one another is given by an infinite derivation tree, which is a coinductive structure itself.

Since structural coinduction has been around for decades and many efforts have been made within the community to explain why it should be used as innocently as structural induction, in our proofs we will not reassert the coinduction principle every time it is used.
Borrowing the terminology from
[
25
]
, we say that we apply the “coinductive hypothesis” whenever the coinduction principle is applied.
The idea is that one can appeal to the coinductive hypothesis as long
as there has been progress in producing the nodes of a tree and there is no further analysis of the subtrees.
We believe that this style of mathematical writing greatly improves the readability of our proofs without compromising their correctness; the suspicious reader can study
[
25
]
where it is explained how this informal terminology actually corresponds to a formal application of the coinduction principle.

The

Inequational theories and

-terms,

•

\langle\!\!\,\langle\mathtt{1}\rangle\!\!\,\rangle=_{\mathcal{B}}\mathtt{Y}(\lambda mx.[\lambda z.xz,mx])\rightarrow_{\eta}\mathtt{Y}(\lambda mx.[x,mx])=_{\mathcal{B}}\langle\!\!\,\langle\mathtt{I}\rangle\!\!\,\rangle

\langle\!\!\,\langle\mathtt{1}\rangle\!\!\,\rangle

\eta
-expansions.

entails that
\mathrm{BT}(M)
can be obtained from
\mathrm{BT}(N)
by performing at most
one

\eta
-expansion at every position.

{lemC}
[
[

]
If

, then
\mathrm{BT}(M)
is obtained from
\mathrm{BT}(N)
by replacing in the latter some

~\quad U_{1}\quad\cdots\quad U_{k}\ z

In particular, no finite amount of
\eta
-expansions in

can turn its Böhm tree into
\mathrm{BT}(\langle\!\!\,\langle\mathbf{\mathtt{1}}^{*}\rangle\!\!\,\rangle)

Corollary 6

{\mathcal{B}\eta}\vdash\langle\!\!\,\langle\mathtt{I}\rangle\!\!\,\rangle=\langle\!\!\,\langle\mathtt{1}\rangle\!\!\,\rangle

2.2.
\mathcal{H^{+}}
: Countably Many Finite
\eta
-Expansions

By definition,
\mathcal{H^{+}}
is the
\lambda
-theory corresponding to Morris’s original observational equivalence where the observables are the
\beta
-normal forms
[
32
]
.
The
\lambda
-theory
\mathcal{H^{+}}
and its inequational version have been studied both from a syntactic and from a semantic point of view.
We refer to
[
18
,
27
,
13
]
for some standard literature and to
[
30
,
7
]
for more recent work.
The properties that we present here can be found in
[
36
, §11.2]
.
Two
\lambda
-terms having the same Böhm tree cannot be distinguished by any context
C[]
, so we have
\mathcal{B}\subseteq\mathcal{H^{+}}
.
Since
\mathcal{H^{+}}
is an extensional
\lambda
-theory, we get
{\mathcal{B}\eta}\subseteq\mathcal{H^{+}}
.

The question naturally arising is whether there are
\lambda
-terms different in
{\mathcal{B}\eta}
that become equal in
\mathcal{H^{+}}
.
It turns out that
\mathcal{H^{+}}\vdash M=N
holds exactly when
\mathrm{BT}(M)
and
\mathrm{BT}(N)
are equal up to countably many
\eta
-expansions of finite size.
A typical example of this situation is given by the streams
\langle\!\!\,\langle\mathtt{I}\rangle\!\!\,\rangle
and
\langle\!\!\,\langle\mathbf{\mathtt{1}}^{*}\rangle\!\!\,\rangle
since the Böhm tree of the latter can be obtained from the Böhm tree of the former by performing infinitely many finite
\eta
-expansions.

{defi}

Given two Böhm-like trees
U
and
V
, we define coinductively the relation
U\leq^{\eta}V
expressing the fact that
V
is a
finitary
\eta
-expansion
of
U
.
We let
\leq^{\eta}
be the greatest relation between Böhm-like trees such that
U\leq^{\eta}V
entails that

•

either
U=V=\bot
,

•

or, for some
k,m,n\geq 0
:

U=\lambda x_{1}\dots x_{n}.yU_{1}\cdots U_{k}\ \textrm{ and }\ V=\lambda x_{1}\dots x_{n}z_{1}\dots z_{m}.yV_{1}\cdots V_{k}Q_{1}\cdots Q_{m}

where
\vec{z}\notin\mathsf{FV}(yU_{1}\cdots U_{k}V_{1}\cdots V_{k})
,
U_{j}\leq^{\eta}V_{j}
for all
j\leq k
and
Q_{i}\twoheadrightarrow_{\eta}z_{i}
for all
i\leq m
.

It is easy to check that
\mathrm{BT}(\langle\!\!\,\langle\mathtt{I}\rangle\!\!\,\rangle)\leq^{\eta}\mathrm{BT}(\langle\!\!\,\langle\mathbf{\mathtt{1}}^{*}\rangle\!\!\,\rangle)
holds.

{nota}

For
M,N\in\Lambda
, we write
M\leq^{\eta}N
if and only if
\mathrm{BT}(M)\leq^{\eta}\mathrm{BT}(N)
.

Note that
M\leq^{\eta}N
and
N\leq^{\eta}M
entail
\mathrm{BT}(M)=\mathrm{BT}(N)
, therefore the equivalence corresponding to
\leq^{\eta}
and capturing
=_{\mathcal{H^{+}}}
needs to be defined in a more subtle way.

Theorem 7

(Hyland
[
18
]
, see also
[
27
]
)
.

For all
M,N\in\Lambda
, we have

\mathcal{H^{+}}\vdash M=N
if and only if there exists a Böhm-like tree
U\in\mathbb{BT}
such that
\mathrm{BT}(M)\leq^{\eta}U\geq^{\eta}\mathrm{BT}(N)
.

This means that in general, when
M=_{\mathcal{H^{+}}}N
, one may need to perform countably many
\eta
-expansions of finite size both in
\mathrm{BT}(M)
and in
\mathrm{BT}(N)
to find the common “
\eta
-supremum”.

Corollary 8
.

\mathcal{H^{+}}\vdash\langle\!\!\,\langle\mathtt{I}\rangle\!\!\,\rangle=\langle\!\!\,\langle\mathbf{\mathtt{1}}^{*}\rangle\!\!\,\rangle
, while
\mathcal{H^{+}}\vdash\langle\!\!\,\langle\mathtt{I}\rangle\!\!\,\rangle\neq\langle\!\!\,\langle\mathtt{J}\rangle\!\!\,\rangle
.

2.3.
\mathcal{H^{*}}
: Countably Many Infinite
\eta
-Expansions

The
\lambda
-theory
\mathcal{H^{*}}
and its inequational version are, by far, the most well studied theories of the untyped
\lambda
-calculus
[
3
,
17
,
15
,

,

,

Recall that
\mathcal{H^{*}}
corresponds to the observational equivalence where the observables are the head normal forms.
Two

if their Böhm trees are equal up to countably many

-expansions of possibly infinite depth.
The typical example is
\mathtt{I}=_{\mathcal{H^{*}}}\mathtt{J}

{defi}

Given two Böhm-like trees

and

expressing the fact that
V
is a
possibly infinite
\eta

of

We let

be the greatest relation between Böhm-like trees such that

•

,

•

or (for some

where
\vec{z}\notin\mathsf{FV}(yU_{1}\cdots U_{k}V_{1}\cdots V_{k})
,

for all

and
z_{i}\leq^{\eta}_{\omega}V^{\prime}_{i}
for all

.

For instance, we have
\mathrm{BT}(\langle\!\!\,\langle\mathtt{I}\rangle\!\!\,\rangle)\leq^{\eta}_{\omega}\mathrm{BT}(\langle\!\!\,\langle\mathtt{J}\rangle\!\!\,\rangle)
.

{nota}
For
M,N\in\Lambda
, we write
M\leq^{\eta}_{\omega}N
if and only if
\mathrm{BT}(M)\leq^{\eta}_{\omega}\mathrm{BT}(N)
.

{defi}

Given two Böhm-like trees
U
and

we write
U\leq_{\bot}V
whenever

U
results from

by replacing some subtrees by
\bot
(possibly infinitely many).

Theorem 9

(Hyland
[
19
]
/Wadsworth
[
41
]
)
.

For all

, we have that:

(i)

\mathcal{H^{*}}\vdash M\sqsubseteq N
if and only if there exist two Böhm-like trees

such that
\mathrm{BT}(M)\leq^{\eta}_{\omega}U\leq_{\bot}V\geq^{\eta}_{\omega}\mathrm{BT}(N)
.

(ii)

\mathcal{H^{*}}\vdash M=N
if and only if there exists a Böhm-like tree
U\in\mathbb{BT}
such that
\mathrm{BT}(M)\leq^{\eta}_{\omega}U\geq^{\eta}_{\omega}\mathrm{BT}(N)
.

Exercise 10.6.7 in Barendregt’s book
[

]
consists in showing
that the
\eta
-supremum

always be chosen to be the Böhm tree of some
\lambda
-term.
As we will prove in Section

of Theorem

Corollary 10
.

\langle\!\!\,\langle\mathtt{I}\rangle\!\!\,\rangle,\langle\!\!\,\langle\mathtt{1}\rangle\!\!\,\rangle,\langle\!\!\,\langle\mathbf{\mathtt{1}}^{*}\rangle\!\!\,\rangle,\langle\!\!\,\langle\mathtt{J}\rangle\!\!\,\rangle
are all equal in

.

(\mathtt{P}_{k}N^{\prime\prime}_{1}\cdots N^{\prime\prime}_{m+p})\Omega^{\sim k-m-p}\,\mathtt{U}^{k}_{i+1}\vec{Y}=_{\beta}&\textrm{where }N^{\prime\prime}_{\ell}=N^{\prime}_{\ell}\,[\mathtt{P}_{k}/\vec{x}\,],\\
(\lambda z.zN^{\prime\prime}_{1}\cdots N^{\prime\prime}_{m+p}\Omega^{\sim k-m-p})\,\mathtt{U}^{k}_{i+1}\vec{Y}=_{\beta}&\textrm{by Lemma~\ref{lemma:bcprop}\eqref{lemma:bcprop1}},\\
N^{\prime\prime}_{i+1}\vec{Y}=N_{i+1}[\mathtt{P}_{k}/\vec{y},\vec{x}\,]\vec{Y}=_{\mathcal{B}}&\textrm{by Lemma~\ref{lemma:bcprop}\eqref{lemma:bcprop2}},\\
\mathtt{J}_{T}&\textrm{by the induction hypothesis.}\\
\end{array}

Thanks to the Context Lemma we obtain the Morris-separation result in its full generality.

Theorem 21

(Morris Separation)
.

Let
M,N\in\Lambda
be such that
\mathcal{H^{*}}\vdash M=N
while
\mathcal{H^{+}}\vdash M\not\sqsubseteq N
.
There is a closed head context
C[]
such that

C[M]=_{\beta\eta}\mathtt{I}\qquad\textrm{ and }\qquad C[N]=_{\mathcal{B}}\mathtt{J}_{T}\textrm{ for some $T\in\mathbb{T}^{\infty}_{\textrm{\sf rec}}$.}

When
M,N\in\Lambda^{\!o}
the context

can be chosen applicative.

Proof 4.5
.

By Lemma
5
for
\mathcal{H^{+}}
, we get that
\mathcal{H^{+}}\vdash M\not\sqsubseteq N
entails the existence of a closed head context
C_{2}[]
such
that
C_{2}[M]\in_{\beta}\mathtt{NF}_{\beta}
, while
C_{2}[N]
does not have a
\beta
-normal form.
From
\mathcal{H^{*}}\vdash M=N
we obtain
\mathcal{H^{*}}\vdash C_{2}[M]=C_{2}[N]
.
Therefore, by Proposition
18
there exists a Morris separator
\sigma:C_{2}[M]\not\sqsubseteq C_{2}[N]
.
We can now apply Lemma
20
, and get a head context
C_{1}[]=(\lambda\vec{y}.[])\vec{\mathtt{P}}_{k}\vec{X}
, where
\vec{y}=\mathsf{FV}(C_{2}[M]C_{2}[N])
, and such that
C_{1}[C_{2}[M]]=_{\beta}\mathtt{I}
and
C_{1}[C_{2}[N]]=_{\mathcal{B}}\mathtt{J}_{T}
for some

.
Hence the closed head context

we are looking for is actually

.
When

, all the contexts can be chosen applicative.

4.3.

-Theory

The fact that

Lemma 22
.

Let

Proof 4.6
.

By hypothesis

Case

.
Since

If

Case

By the usual semi-separability theorem, there are

When

Lemma 23
.

Let

.
If

\forall\vec{P}\in\Lambda^{o}(M\vec{P}\in_{\beta}\mathtt{NF}_{\beta}\iff N\vec{P}\in_{\beta}\mathtt{NF}_{\beta}).

.

Suppose that for all

We show by induction on the length

of

that

Base:

. Since the contrapositive holds by Lemma

.

Induction:

As a consequence, we get the main result of this section.

Theorem 24
.

\mathcal{H^{+}}

-rule, therefore
{\mathcal{B}\omega}\subseteq\mathcal{H^{+}}
.

.

5.
Building Böhm Trees by Codes and Streams

Now that we have shown
{\mathcal{B}\omega}\subseteq\mathcal{H^{+}}
, we focus our efforts to prove the converse inclusion.
The key step in our proof is to show that when
\mathcal{H^{+}}\vdash M=N
holds the Böhm-like tree

of Theorem

giving the “
\eta
-supremum” of
\mathrm{BT}(M)
and
\mathrm{BT}(N)
can always be chosen to be the Böhm tree of a
\lambda
-term

(Proposition

).
Intuitively, the
\lambda
-term

that we construct is going to inspect the structure of

and

looking at their codes and retrieve the correct
\eta
-expansion to apply from a suitable stream.

5.1.
Building Böhm Trees by Codes

We start by showing that the Böhm tree of a
\lambda
-term can be reconstructed from its code.

{defi}

•

Let us fix an effective one-to-one encoding
\#:\Lambda\to{\rm Nature}
associating with every
\lambda
-term
M
its
code

\#M
(the Gödel number of
M
).

•

The
quote

\lceil M\rceil
of
M
is the corresponding numeral
\mathrm{c}_{\#M}
.

We recall some well established facts from
[
3
, §8.1]
.
By
[
3
, Thm. 8.1.6]
, there is a
\lambda
-term
\mathtt{E}\in\Lambda^{\!o}
such that
\mathtt{E}\lceil M\rceil=M
for all
M\in\Lambda^{\!o}
. This is false in general for open
\lambda
-terms
M
.

Remark 25
.

The following operations are effective:

•

from
\#M
compute
\#M^{\prime}
where
M\rightarrow_{h}M^{\prime}
(since the head-reduction is an effective reduction strategy),

•

from
\#(\lambda\vec{x}.yM_{1}\cdots M_{k})
compute
\#M_{i}
for
i\leq k
,

•

from
\#M
compute
\#(\lambda x_{1}\dots x_{n}.M)
for
x_{1},\dots,x_{n}\in\mathrm{Var}
.

From Remark
25
, it follows that the combinator
\Phi
below exists and can be defined using the fixed point combinator
\mathtt{Y}
.

{defi}

Let
\Phi\in\Lambda^{\!o}
be such that for all
M\in\Lambda^{\!o}
:

•

if
M\twoheadrightarrow_{h}\lambda x_{1}\dots x_{n}.x_{j}M_{1}\cdots M_{k}
, then

\Phi\lceil M\rceil=_{\beta}\lambda\underline{x}_{1}\dots\underline{x}_{n}.\underline{x}_{j}(\Phi\lceil\lambda\vec{x}.M_{1}\rceil\underline{x}_{1}\cdots\underline{x}_{n})\cdots(\Phi\lceil\lambda\vec{x}.M_{k}\rceil\underline{x}_{1}\cdots\underline{x}_{n})

where the
\underline{x}_{i}
are underlined to stress the fact that they are fresh variables.

•

\Phi\lceil M\rceil
is unsolvable whenever
M
is unsolvable.

The term
\Phi
builds the Böhm tree of

from its code
\#M
.
Notice that the closure
\lambda\vec{x}.M_{i}
on the recursive calls is needed to obtain a closed term (since

entails
\mathsf{FV}(M_{i})\subseteq\vec{x}
).
In the definition above we use the fact that
\mathrm{BT}(\lambda\vec{x}.M)=\lambda\vec{x}.\mathrm{BT}(M)
, under the hypotheses that
\lambda x.\bot=\bot
and
\bot M=\bot
, thus the free variables

can be reapplied externally.

Lemma 26
.

For all
M\in\Lambda^{\!o}
, we have
\mathcal{B}\vdash\Phi\lceil M\rceil=M
.

Proof 5.1
.

We prove by coinduction the generalization of the statement to open
\lambda
-terms, namely: for all
M\in\Lambda
and
\{y_{1},\dots,y_{m}\}\supseteq\mathsf{FV}(M)
, we have
\mathrm{BT}(\Phi\lceil\lambda\vec{y}.M\rceil y_{1}\cdots y_{m})=\mathrm{BT}(M)
.

If
M
is unsolvable then
\mathrm{BT}(\Phi\lceil\lambda\vec{y}.M\rceil y_{1}\cdots y_{m})=\mathrm{BT}(M)=\bot
.

Otherwise
M
is solvable, so we have
M\twoheadrightarrow_{h}\lambda x_{1}\dots x_{n}.yM_{1}\cdots M_{k}
and
\Phi\lceil\lambda\vec{y}.M\rceil\vec{y}=_{\beta}\lambda x_{1}\dots x_{n}.y(\Phi\lceil\lambda\vec{y}\vec{x}.M_{1}\rceil y_{1}\cdots y_{m}x_{1}\cdots x_{n})\cdots(\Phi\lceil\lambda\vec{y}\vec{x}.M_{k}\rceil y_{1}\cdots y_{m}x_{1}\cdots x_{n})
.
We concludesince by coinductive hypothesis we have
\mathrm{BT}(\Phi\lceil\lambda\vec{y}\vec{x}.M_{i}\rceil\vec{y}\vec{x})=\mathrm{BT}(M_{i})
for every
i\leq k
.

The generalization to open terms above is formally needed to apply the coinductive hypothesis, but complicates the statements and the corresponding demonstrations.
Hereafter, it will be omitted and our proofs will be slightly more informal for the sake of readability.

5.2.
\eta
-Expanding Böhm Trees from Streams

The existence of
\Phi

being an example of such a
\lambda

The recursive equation of Definition

has the advantage of exposing the structure of the Böhm tree and, doing so, opens the way for altering the tree.
For instance, it is possible to modify Definition
5.1
in order to obtain a
\lambda

which builds a finitary
\eta
-expansion of
\mathrm{BT}(M)
starting from the code of

and a stream of finite
\eta

{defi}

(1)

Let
[\mathtt{I}]_{n\in{\rm Nature}}
be the stream containing infinitely many copies of
\mathtt{I}
:

[\mathtt{I}]_{n\in{\rm Nature}}=[\mathtt{I},[\mathtt{I},[\mathtt{I},\dots]]]

(2)

Let
\vec{\eta}=(\eta_{0},\eta_{1},\eta_{2},\dots)
be an effective enumeration of all (closed) finite
\eta
-expansions of the identity, i.e.
\eta_{i}\in\mathcal{I}^{\eta}\cap\Lambda^{\!o}
for all
i\in{\rm Nature}

[\mathbf{\eta}_{n}]_{n\in{\rm Nature}}=[\eta_{0},[\eta_{1},[\eta_{2},\dots]]].

Notice that, from now on, we consider the enumeration
\vec{\eta}
and the stream
[\mathbf{\eta}_{n}]_{n\in{\rm Nature}}

In order to decide what
\eta

\mathrm{BT}(M)

and extract the
\eta

Since

We recall that an effective encoding

of all finite sequences of natural numbers has been fixed in Section

\lceil\sigma\rceil

In the following definition

{defi}

Let

be a computable function, and

be such that for all

and

•

\Psi_{f}\lceil M\rceil\lceil\sigma\rceil s=_{\beta}\lambda\vec{\underline{x}}.\pi_{f(\sigma)}s\,\big(\underline{x}_{j}(L_{1}\,\underline{x}_{1}\cdots\underline{x}_{n})\cdots(L_{k}\,\underline{x}_{1}\cdots\underline{x}_{n})\big)

We now verify that the

Lemma 27
.

Let
f:{\rm Nature}^{*}\to{\rm Nature}
be a computable function.
For all
M\in\Lambda^{\!o}
and
\sigma\in{\rm Nature}^{*}
:

M\leq^{\eta}\Psi_{f}\lceil M\rceil\lceil\sigma\rceil[\mathbf{\eta}_{n}]_{n\in{\rm Nature}}.

Proof 5.2
.

We prove by coinduction that
\mathrm{BT}(M)\leq^{\eta}\mathrm{BT}(\Psi_{f}\lceil M\rceil\lceil\sigma\rceil[\mathbf{\eta}_{n}]_{n\in{\rm Nature}})
.

If
M
is unsolvable, then
\mathrm{BT}(M)=\mathrm{BT}(\Psi_{f}\lceil M\rceil\lceil\sigma\rceil[\mathbf{\eta}_{n}]_{n\in{\rm Nature}})=\bot
.

Otherwise
M\twoheadrightarrow_{h}\lambda\vec{x}.x_{j}M_{1}\cdots M_{k}
.
Thus, for
f(\sigma)=q
and
\eta_{q}=_{\beta}\lambda yz_{1}\cdots z_{m}.yQ_{1}\cdots Q_{m}
where
\lambda z_{i}.Q_{i}\in\mathcal{I}^{\eta}
for all

we have:

\begin{array}[]{rl}\Psi_{f}\lceil M\rceil\lceil\sigma\rceil[\mathbf{\eta}_{n}]_{n\in{\rm Nature}}=_{\beta}&\lambda\vec{x}.\pi_{q}[\mathbf{\eta}_{n}]_{n\in{\rm Nature}}(x_{j}(L_{1}\vec{x})\cdots(L_{k}\vec{x}))\\
=_{\beta}&\lambda\vec{x}.\eta_{q}(x_{j}(L_{1}\vec{x})\cdots(L_{k}\vec{x}))\\
=_{\beta}&\lambda\vec{x}z_{1}\dots z_{m}.x_{j}(L_{1}\vec{x})\cdots(L_{k}\vec{x})Q_{1}\cdots Q_{m}\\
\end{array}

where
L_{i}=\Psi_{f}\lceil\lambda\vec{x}.M_{i}\rceil\lceil\sigma.i\rceil[\mathbf{\eta}_{n}]_{n\in{\rm Nature}}
for all
i\leq k
.
We conclude because by coinductive hypothesis we get for each
i\leq k
that
\mathrm{BT}(\lambda\vec{x}.M_{i})\leq^{\eta}\mathrm{BT}(L_{i})
holds.

Since
\Psi_{f}
picks the
\eta
-expansion to apply from the input stream, we can retrieve the behaviour of
\Phi
by applying
[\mathtt{I}]_{n\in{\rm Nature}}
.

Lemma 28
.

Let
f:{\rm Nature}^{*}\to{\rm Nature}
be computable.
For all
M\in\Lambda^{\!o}
and
\sigma\in{\rm Nature}^{*}
, we have

\mathcal{B}\vdash M=\Psi_{f}\lceil M\rceil\lceil\sigma\rceil[\mathtt{I}]_{n\in{\rm Nature}}.

Proof 5.3
.

We prove by coinduction that
\mathrm{BT}(M)=\mathrm{BT}(\Psi_{f}\lceil M\rceil\lceil\sigma\rceil[\mathtt{I}]_{n\in{\rm Nature}})
.

If
M
is unsolvable, then
\mathrm{BT}(M)=\mathrm{BT}(\Psi_{f}\lceil M\rceil\lceil\sigma\rceil[\mathtt{I}]_{n\in{\rm Nature}})=\bot
.

Otherwise
M\twoheadrightarrow_{h}\lambda\vec{x}.x_{j}M_{1}\cdots M_{k}
.
Since
\pi_{q}[\mathtt{I}]_{n\in{\rm Nature}}=_{\beta}\mathtt{I}
for all

we have:

\begin{array}[]{rl}\Psi_{f}\lceil M\rceil\lceil\sigma\rceil[\mathtt{I}]_{n\in{\rm Nature}}=_{\beta}&\lambda\vec{x}.\pi_{f(\sigma)}[\mathtt{I}]_{n\in{\rm Nature}}(x_{j}(L_{1}\vec{x})\cdots(L_{k}\vec{x}))\\
=_{\beta}&\lambda\vec{x}.\mathtt{I}(x_{j}(L_{1}\vec{x})\cdots(L_{k}\vec{x}))\\
=_{\beta}&\lambda\vec{x}.x_{j}(L_{1}\vec{x})\cdots(L_{k}\vec{x})\\
\end{array}

where
L_{i}=\Psi_{f}\lceil\lambda\vec{x}.M_{i}\rceil\lceil\sigma.i\rceil[\mathtt{I}]_{n\in{\rm Nature}}
for all
i\leq k
.
We conclude because by coinductive hypothesis we get for each

that
\mathrm{BT}(\lambda\vec{x}.M_{i})=\mathrm{BT}(L_{i})
holds.

We would like to draw the attention to the fact that the
\lambda
-term
\Psi_{f}
does not inspect the structure of the input stream to select one of its elements, it blindly relies on the function

This is the key property that opens the way to modify the behaviour of

by merely changing the input stream as in Lemma

We mention this fact since the

5.3.
Building the
\eta
-Supremum

Using similar techniques, we now define a
\lambda
-term
\mathtt{\Xi}
that builds from the codes of
M,N
and the stream
[\mathbf{\eta}_{n}]_{n\in{\rm Nature}}
the (smallest)
\eta
-supremum satisfying

M\leq^{\eta}\mathtt{\Xi}\lceil M\rceil\lceil N\rceil[\mathbf{\eta}_{n}]_{n\in{\rm Nature}}\geq^{\eta}N

when such an
\eta
-supremum exists, that is whenever
M
and
N
are compatible (Proposition
34
).
Intuitively, at every common position
\sigma
, the
\lambda
-term
\mathtt{\Xi}
needs to compare the structure of the subterms of
M,N
at
\sigma
and apply the correct expansion
\eta_{i}
taken from
[\mathbf{\eta}_{n}]_{n\in{\rm Nature}}
.

Rather than proving that there exists a computable function
f:{\rm Nature}^{*}\to{\rm Nature}
associating to every
\sigma
the corresponding
\eta_{i}
(which can be tedious) we use the following property of
\vec{\eta}=(\eta_{0},\eta_{1},\dots,\eta_{i},\dots)
: since every closed
\eta
-expansion

is
\beta
-normalizable and the enumeration
\vec{\eta}
is effective, it is possible to decide starting from the code
\#Q
the index
i
of
Q
in
\vec{\eta}
.
Moreover, it is possible to choose such an index

minimal.

Lemma 29
.

There exists a computable function
\iota:{\rm Nature}\to{\rm Nature}
such that, for all
M\in\Lambda^{\!o}
,
if
M=_{\beta}\eta_{i}
and
M\neq_{\beta}\eta_{k}
for all
k<i
then
\iota(\#M)=i
.

Proof 5.4
.

Let
\delta(m,n)
be the partial computable map satisfying for all normalizing
M,N\in\Lambda^{\!o}
:

\delta(\#M,\#N)=\begin{cases}0&\textrm{if $M$ and $N$ have the same $\beta$-normal form,}\\
1&\textrm{otherwise.}\\
\end{cases}

Then
\iota
can be defined as
\iota(n):=\mu k.\delta\big(\#(\pi_{k}\circ\vec{\eta}\,),n\big)=0
.
I.e.,
\iota(n)=k
if
k
is the smallest index such that
\delta(\#\eta_{k},n)=0
, equivalently
\mathtt{nf}(\eta_{k})=\mathtt{nf}(\mathtt{E}\,\mathrm{c}_{n})
; otherwise
\iota(n)=\ \uparrow
.

From now on we consider fixed such a function
\iota
, which depends on the enumeration
\vec{\eta}
generating the stream
[\mathbf{\eta}_{n}]_{n\in{\rm Nature}}
.

{defi}

For
M,N\in\Lambda
, we define:

(i)

M\leq_{h}N
whenever
M\twoheadrightarrow_{h}\lambda\vec{x}.yM_{1}\cdots M_{k}
and
N\twoheadrightarrow_{h}\lambda\vec{x}z_{1}\dots z_{m}.yN_{1}\cdots N_{k}Q_{1}\cdots Q_{m}
with
\lambda z_{i}.Q_{i}\in\mathcal{I}^{\eta}
for all
i\leq m
,

(ii)

M\sim_{h}N
if both
M\leq_{h}N
and
N\leq_{h}M
hold,

(iii)

M<_{h}N
if
M\leq_{h}N
holds but
M\not\sim_{h}N
.

Whenever
M\leq_{h}N
holds, we say that
N

looks like an
\eta
-expansion of
M
.
This does not necessarily mean that it actually is, as shown by the following examples.

{exas}

We have:

•

\lambda z.x\mathtt{F}z\leq_{h}\lambda z.x\mathtt{K}z
since we do not require that
\mathtt{F}\leq_{h}\mathtt{K}
holds;

•

z\leq_{h}\lambda z.zz
since in Definition
5.3
(i)
we do not check that

.

Therefore, compared with the relation
\leq^{\eta}
of Definition
2.2
, the relation
\leq_{h}
is weaker since it lacks the coinductive calls and the occurrence check on the

’s.
This is necessary to ensure the following semi-decidability property.

Remark 30
.

The property
M\leq_{h}N
can be semi-decided by the following procedure:

•

first head-reduce in parallel
M,N
until they reach a head normal form,

•

if both reductions terminate, compare the two hnf’s and check whether they have the shape of Definition
5.3
(i)
,

•

then semi-decide whether
Q_{i}\twoheadrightarrow_{\beta\eta}z_{i}
for all
i\leq m
.

This procedure might fail to terminate when
M\not\leq_{h}N
.

By Remarks
25
and
30
and the fact that
\iota
is computable (Lemma
29
), the
\lambda
-term
\mathtt{\Xi}_{\iota}
below exists.

{defi}

Let

be the computable function from Lemma

We define a combinator

such that for all

The following commutativity property follows from the second condition of Definition

Lemma 31
.

For all

, we have:

Proof 5.5
.

We prove by coinduction that

.

If
M,N
are unsolvable or neither

nor

The cases

and

If

Another property that we expect is that whenever

6.
\mathcal{B}\omega=\mathcal{H^{+}}
and Sallé’s Conjecture is False

This section is devoted to proving that

As mentioned earlier, the first step is to show that the term

Proposition 34

For all

\mathcal{H^{+}}\vdash M=N\iff M\leq^{\eta}\mathtt{\Xi}_{\iota}\lceil M\rceil\lceil N\rceil[\mathbf{\eta}_{n}]_{n\in{\rm Nature}}\geq^{\eta}N

Proof 6.1
.

(\Leftarrow)

(\Rightarrow)
By Theorem

As usual, we proceed by coinduction on the Böhm(-like) trees.

If

Otherwise, from

\begin{array}[]{c}M\twoheadrightarrow_{h}\lambda\vec{x}.x_{j}M_{1}\cdots M_{k},\qquad\qquad N\twoheadrightarrow_{h}\lambda\vec{x}z_{1}\dots z_{m}.x_{j}N_{1}\cdots N_{k}Q_{1}\cdots Q_{m},\\

•

and

•

•

By Lemma

looks like an
\eta
-expansion of

Put
q=\iota\big(\#(\mathtt{nf}(\lambda yz_{1}\dots z_{m}.yQ_{1}\cdots Q_{m}))\big)

\Upsilon_{i}=\mathtt{\Xi}_{\iota}\lceil\lambda\vec{x}.M\rceil\lceil\lambda\vec{x}.N\rceil[\mathbf{\eta}_{n}]_{n\in{\rm Nature}}

\begin{array}[]{rl}\mathtt{\Xi}_{\iota}\lceil M\rceil\lceil N\rceil[\mathbf{\eta}_{n}]_{n\in{\rm Nature}}=_{\beta}&\lambda\vec{x}.\pi_{q}[\mathbf{\eta}_{n}]_{n\in{\rm Nature}}(x_{j}(\Upsilon_{1}\vec{x})\cdots(\Upsilon_{k}\vec{x}))\\

{i\leq k}

The symmetric case

is treated analogously, using Lemma

In the proof above, it is easy to check that

\eta

\eta
-supremum of
\mathrm{BT}(M)

The second step towards the proof of Theorem

[\mathtt{I}]_{n\in{\rm Nature}}

{defi}

7.
A Characterization of
{\mathcal{B}\eta}

As discussed in Section
2.1
,
{\mathcal{B}\eta}
equates strictly less than
{\mathcal{B}\omega}
, and by Theorem
38
than
\mathcal{H^{+}}
.
However, to the best of our knowledge, in the literature there is no formal characterization of
{\mathcal{B}\eta}
in terms of some extensional equality between Böhm trees.
The only property shown in
[
3
, §16.4]
concerning the interaction between
\mathrm{BT}(-)
and
\rightarrow_{\eta}
is Lemma
2.1
.

In this section we show that the approach described in
Section
5.3
is general enough to prove that

{\mathcal{B}\eta}\vdash M=N
holds exactly when
\mathrm{BT}(M)
and
\mathrm{BT}(N)
are equal
up to countably many
\eta
-expansions having
uniformly bounded size
(Theorem
49
).

7.1.
Bounded
\eta
-Expansions of Böhm Trees

We start by defining the
size
of a finite
\eta
-expansion
Q
of the identity.
Intuitively, the size of
Q
is the maximum between its height and its width (namely, the maximal number of branching in its Böhm tree).

{defi}

The
size
of
Q\in\mathcal{I}^{\eta}
is defined inductively as follows:

|Q|=\begin{cases}0&\textrm{if }Q\twoheadrightarrow_{\beta}\mathtt{I},\\
\max\{m,\max_{i\leq m}\{|\lambda z_{i}.Q_{i}|\}\!+1\}&\textrm{if }Q\twoheadrightarrow_{\beta}\lambda yz_{1}\dots z_{m}.yQ_{1}\cdots Q_{m}.\\
\end{cases}

For
Q,Q^{\prime}\in\mathcal{I}^{\eta}
, we have that
Q=_{\beta}Q^{\prime}
entails
|Q|=|Q^{\prime}|
since the size
|-|
is determined by their
\beta
-normal forms.
It is easy to verify that for each
n\in{\rm Nature}
the size of
\mathtt{1}^{n}
is
|\mathtt{1}^{n}|=n
.

{defi}

For
p\in{\rm Nature}
, let
\mathcal{I}^{\eta}_{p}=\{Q\in\mathcal{I}^{\eta}:|Q|<p\}
be the set of
\eta
-expansions of
\mathtt{I}
whose size is bounded by
p
.

We have
\mathcal{I}^{\eta}_{0}=\emptyset
,
\mathcal{I}^{\eta}_{1}=\{\mathtt{I}\}
,
\mathcal{I}^{\eta}_{2}=\{\mathtt{I},\lambda yz.yz\}
, and so on.
Therefore
\mathcal{I}^{\eta}_{p}\subseteq\mathcal{I}^{\eta}_{p+1}
and the sequence
\big(\mathcal{I}^{\eta}_{p}\big)_{p\in{\rm Nature}}
is increasing.

The following lemma will be useful for studying the behaviour of
\mathtt{\Xi}_{\iota}
, when applied to the codes of
\lambda
-terms whose Böhm trees differ because of bounded
\eta
-expansions.

Lemma 39
.

Let
m,p\in{\rm Nature}
.
If
m\leq p
and
\lambda z_{i}.Q_{i}\in\mathcal{I}^{\eta}_{p}
holds for all
i\leq m
, then we have that
\lambda yz_{1}\dots z_{m}.yQ_{1}\cdots Q_{m}\in\mathcal{I}^{\eta}_{p+1}
.

Proof 7.1
.

Since
m\leq p
and
|\lambda z_{i}.Q_{i}|<p
for each
i\leq m
, it follows that
|\lambda yz_{1}\dots z_{m}.yQ_{1}\cdots Q_{m}|=\max\{m,\max\{|\lambda z_{1}.Q_{1}|,\dots,|\lambda z_{m}.Q_{m}|\}+1\}\leq p<p+1
.

Now that we have formalized when
Q\in\mathcal{I}^{\eta}
has size bounded by a certain
p
, we specify when two Böhm-like trees
U,V
are such that
V
is an
\eta
-expansion of
U
bounded by
p
.

{defi}

For all
p\in{\rm Nature}
, we define the greatest relation
\leq^{\eta}_{p}
between Böhm-like trees such that
U\leq^{\eta}_{p}V
entails that:

•

either
U=V=\bot
,

•

or for some
m\leq p

U=\lambda\vec{x}.yU_{1}\cdots U_{k}\textrm{ and }V=\lambda\vec{x}z_{1}\dots z_{m}.yV_{1}\cdots V_{k}Q_{1}\cdots Q_{m},

where
z_{\ell}\notin\mathsf{FV}(yU_{1}\cdots U_{k}V_{1}\cdots V_{k})
,
\lambda z_{i}.Q_{i}\in\mathcal{I}^{\eta}_{p}
for all
i\leq m
and
U_{j}\leq^{\eta}_{p}V_{j}
for all
j\leq k
.

Remark that in the definition above we verify not only that the size of each
\lambda z_{i}.Q_{i}
is bounded by
p
, but also that their number
m
is.
Notice the asymmetry between the strict bound
|\lambda z_{i}.Q_{i}|<p
and the bound

, which arises naturally from Lemma
39
.

{nota}

For
M,N\in\Lambda
and
p\in{\rm Nature}
, we write
M\leq^{\eta}_{p}N
whenever
\mathrm{BT}(M)\leq^{\eta}_{p}\mathrm{BT}(N)
.

The next lemma follows straightforwardly from the definition.

Lemma 40
.

For
M,N\in\Lambda
and
p\in{\rm Nature}
. If
M\leq^{\eta}_{p}N
then:

(i)

M\leq^{\eta}N
,

(ii)

\mathcal{B}\vdash M=M^{\prime}
entails
M^{\prime}\leq^{\eta}_{p}N
,

(iii)

for all
p^{\prime}\geq p
, we have
M\leq^{\eta}_{p^{\prime}}N
.

We need a couple of technical lemmas.
The first one exhibits the interaction between
\leq^{\eta}_{p}
and the size
{|-|}
, the intuition being that
\leq^{\eta}_{p}
can increase the size of
Q\in\mathcal{I}^{\eta}
by at most

.

Lemma 41
.

Given
p,p^{\prime}\in{\rm Nature}
, we have that
\lambda y.Q\in\mathcal{I}^{\eta}_{p}
and
Q\leq^{\eta}_{p^{\prime}}Q^{\prime}
imply
\lambda y.Q^{\prime}\in\mathcal{I}^{\eta}_{p+p^{\prime}}
.

Proof 7.2
.

We proceed by induction on the structure of
\mathtt{nf}_{\beta}(Q)
.

From
\lambda y.Q\in\mathcal{I}^{\eta}_{p}
we get
\mathtt{nf}_{\beta}(Q)=\lambda z_{1}\dots z_{m}.yQ_{1}\cdots Q_{m}
for some
m<p
and
Q_{i}
’s such that
|\lambda z_{i}.Q_{i}|<p
.
Since
Q\leq^{\eta}_{p^{\prime}}Q^{\prime}
holds we have
\mathtt{nf}_{\beta}(Q^{\prime})=\lambda z_{1}\dots z_{m}w_{1}\dots w_{m^{\prime}}.yQ^{\prime}_{1}\cdots Q^{\prime}_{m+m^{\prime}}
where
m^{\prime}\leq p^{\prime}
,
Q_{i}\leq^{\eta}_{p^{\prime}}Q^{\prime}_{i}
for all
i\leq m
and
\lambda w_{j}.Q^{\prime}_{j}\in\mathcal{I}^{\eta}_{p^{\prime}}
for all
j>m
.
Therefore
m+m^{\prime}<p+p^{\prime}
,
|\lambda w_{j}.Q^{\prime}_{j}|<p^{\prime}
for all
j>m
and, by inductive hypothesis,
|\lambda z_{i}.Q^{\prime}_{i}|<p+p^{\prime}
for all
i\leq m
.
We conclude that
|\lambda y.Q^{\prime}|<p+p^{\prime}
, which entails
\lambda y.Q^{\prime}\in\mathcal{I}^{\eta}_{p+p^{\prime}}
.

The next lemma is devoted to showing that the relation
\leq^{\eta}_{p}
enjoys the following “weighted” transitivity property.

Lemma 42
.

For all
M,N\in\Lambda
,
p_{1},p_{2}\in{\rm Nature}
we have that
M\leq^{\eta}_{p_{1}}P
and
P\leq^{\eta}_{p_{2}}N
entail
M\leq^{\eta}_{p_{1}+p_{2}}N
.

Proof 7.3
.

We proceed by coinduction on their Böhm trees.

If one among
M,N,P
is unsolvable, then all their Böhm trees are
\bot
and we are done.

Otherwise from
M\leq^{\eta}_{p_{1}}P
we get

M\twoheadrightarrow_{h}\lambda\vec{x}.yM_{1}\cdots M_{k},\textrm{ and }P\twoheadrightarrow_{h}\lambda\vec{x}z_{1}\dots z_{m_{1}}.yP_{1}\cdots P_{k}Q_{1}\cdots Q_{m_{1}}

where
\vec{z}\notin\mathsf{FV}(\mathrm{BT}(y\vec{M}\vec{P}))
,
m_{1}\leq p_{1}
,
\lambda z_{i}.Q_{i}\in\mathcal{I}^{\eta}_{p_{1}}
for
i\leq m_{1}
and
{M_{j}\leq^{\eta}_{p_{1}}P_{j}}
for
j\leq k
.

From
P\leq^{\eta}_{p_{2}}N
we obtain (for
\vec{w}\notin\mathsf{FV}(\mathrm{BT}(y\vec{P}\vec{Q}\vec{N}\vec{Q}^{\prime}))
):

N\twoheadrightarrow_{h}\lambda\vec{x}z_{1}\dots z_{m_{1}}w_{1}\dots w_{m_{2}}.yN_{1}\cdots N_{k}Q^{\prime}_{1}\cdots Q^{\prime}_{m_{1}}Q^{\prime\prime}_{1}\cdots Q^{\prime\prime}_{m_{2}}

with
m_{2}\leq p_{2}
,
P_{j}\leq^{\eta}_{p_{2}}N_{j}
for
j\leq k
,
Q_{i}\leq^{\eta}_{p_{2}}Q^{\prime}_{i}
for
i\leq m_{1}
and
\lambda w_{\ell}.Q^{\prime\prime}_{\ell}\in\mathcal{I}^{\eta}_{p_{2}}
for
\ell\leq m_{2}
.We notice that
m_{1}\leq p_{1}
and
m_{2}\leq p_{2}
imply
m_{1}+m_{2}\leq p_{1}+p_{2}
, and that
\mathcal{I}^{\eta}_{p_{1}}\cup\,\mathcal{I}^{\eta}_{p_{2}}\subseteq\mathcal{I}^{\eta}_{p_{1}+p_{2}}
.From
\lambda z_{i}.Q_{i}\in\mathcal{I}^{\eta}_{p_{1}}
and
Q_{i}\leq^{\eta}_{p_{2}}Q^{\prime}_{i}
we obtain by Lemma
41
that
\lambda z_{i}.Q^{\prime}_{i}\in\mathcal{I}^{\eta}_{p_{1}+p_{2}}
for
i\leq m_{1}
.
Since, for all
j\leq k
,
M_{j}\leq^{\eta}_{p_{1}}P_{j}
and
P_{j}\leq^{\eta}_{p_{2}}N_{j}
we conclude that
M_{j}\leq^{\eta}_{p_{1}+p_{2}}N_{j}
holds by applying the coinductive hypotheses.

The following is an easy corollary of Lemma
2.1
.

Corollary 43
.

If
M\twoheadrightarrow_{\eta}N
then there exists a bound
p\in{\rm Nature}
such that
N\leq^{\eta}_{p}M
.

Proof 7.4
.

We perform an induction loading and prove that if the reduction
M\twoheadrightarrow_{\eta}N
has length
p
, then
N\leq^{\eta}_{p}M
holds.

If
p=0
then
\mathrm{BT}(M)=\mathrm{BT}(N)
so we have
N\leq^{\eta}_{0}M
.

If
p>0
then
M\twoheadrightarrow_{\eta}N^{\prime}
in
p-1
steps and
N^{\prime}\rightarrow_{\eta}N
.
From Lemma
2.1
,
N\leq^{\eta}_{1}N^{\prime}
since at every position
\sigma
of their Böhm trees the lengths of
x_{1},\dots,x_{n}
and
x_{1},\dots,x_{n}z
differ at most by 1, and
\lambda z.z\in\mathcal{I}^{\eta}_{1}
.
By induction hypothesis we have
N^{\prime}\leq^{\eta}_{p-1}M
, so we conclude by applying Lemma
42
.

7.2.
The Behaviour of
\mathtt{\Xi}_{\iota}
on Bounded
\eta
-Expansions

Recall that in Definition
5.2
we have fixed an effective enumeration
\vec{\eta}=(\eta_{0},\eta_{1},\dots)
of the set
\mathcal{I}^{\eta}
, together with the corresponding stream
[\mathbf{\eta}_{n}]_{n\in{\rm Nature}}=[\eta_{0},[\eta_{1},[\eta_{2},\dots]]]
.
Moreover, we consider fixed a map
\iota
satisfying the properties of Lemma
29
.
We start proving the following technical lemma concerning bounded
\eta
-expansions of the identity.

Lemma 44
.

Let
Q,Q^{\prime}\in\Lambda
,
\vec{z}\supseteq\mathsf{FV}(QQ^{\prime})
.
If
\lambda z_{\ell}.Q,\lambda z_{\ell}.Q^{\prime}\in\mathcal{I}^{\eta}_{p}
for some index
\ell
then

Q\leq^{\eta}_{p}\mathtt{\Xi}_{\iota}\lceil\lambda\vec{z}.Q\rceil\lceil\lambda\vec{z}.Q^{\prime}\rceil[\mathbf{\eta}_{n}]_{n\in{\rm Nature}}\,\vec{z}\geq^{\eta}_{p}Q^{\prime}.

Proof 7.5
.

Since
Q,Q^{\prime}
are normalizing, we assume they are in
\beta
-normal form and proceed by structural induction.
We have, say:

Q=\lambda\vec{y}.z_{\ell}Q_{1}\cdots Q_{m}\quad Q^{\prime}=\lambda\vec{y}\vec{w}.z_{\ell}Q^{\prime}_{1}\cdots Q^{\prime}_{m+m^{\prime}}

where
m+m^{\prime}\leq p
,
\lambda y_{i}.Q_{i},\lambda y_{i}.Q^{\prime}_{i}\in\mathcal{I}^{\eta}_{p}
for
i\leq m
and
\lambda w_{j}.Q^{\prime}_{j}\in\mathcal{I}^{\eta}_{p}
for
j>m
.

We split into two subcases.

•

If
m=0
then
Q=z_{\ell}
and
\lambda\vec{z}.z_{\ell}\leq^{\eta}_{p}\lambda\vec{z}.Q^{\prime}
, so by Lemma
32
we get that
\mathcal{B}\vdash\lambda\vec{z}.Q^{\prime}=\mathtt{\Xi}_{\iota}\lceil\lambda\vec{z}.Q\rceil\lceil\lambda\vec{z}.Q^{\prime}\rceil[\mathbf{\eta}_{n}]_{n\in{\rm Nature}}
and the case follows by applying the variables
\vec{z}
to both sides.

•

Otherwise
m>0
, so for
q=\iota(\#(\lambda x\vec{w}.xQ^{\prime}_{m+1}\cdots Q^{\prime}_{m+m^{\prime}}))
we have:

\begin{array}[]{rcl}\mathtt{\Xi}_{\iota}\lceil\lambda\vec{z}.Q\rceil\lceil\lambda\vec{z}.Q^{\prime}\rceil[\mathbf{\eta}_{n}]_{n\in{\rm Nature}}\vec{z}&=_{\beta}&\lambda\vec{y}.\pi_{q}[\mathbf{\eta}_{n}]_{n\in{\rm Nature}}(z_{\ell}(\Upsilon_{1}\vec{z}\vec{y})\cdots(\Upsilon_{m}\vec{z}\vec{y}))\\
&=_{\beta}&\lambda\vec{y}.(\lambda x\vec{w}.xQ^{\prime}_{m+1}\cdots Q^{\prime}_{m+m^{\prime}})(z_{\ell}(\Upsilon_{1}\vec{z}\vec{y})\cdots(\Upsilon_{m}\vec{z}\vec{y}))\\
&=_{\beta}&\lambda\vec{y}w.z_{\ell}(\Upsilon_{1}\vec{z}\vec{y})\cdots(\Upsilon_{m}\vec{z}\vec{y})Q^{\prime}_{m+1}\cdots Q^{\prime}_{m+m^{\prime}}\end{array}

where
\Upsilon_{i}=\mathtt{\Xi}_{\iota}\lceil\lambda\vec{z}\vec{y}.Q_{i}\rceil\lceil\lambda\vec{z}\vec{y}.Q^{\prime}_{i}\rceil[\mathbf{\eta}_{n}]_{n\in{\rm Nature}}
and the case follows by induction hypothesis, thus concluding the proof of the lemma.

As shown in Section
6
, given two
\lambda
-terms
M
and
N
whose Böhm trees differ because of countably many
\eta
-expansions, the
\lambda
-term
\mathtt{\Xi}_{\iota}\lceil M\rceil\lceil N\rceil
of Definition
5.3
builds their
\eta
-supremum from the stream
[\mathbf{\eta}_{n}]_{n\in{\rm Nature}}
(Proposition
34
).
We now prove that when the size of such
\eta
-expansions is bounded by
p
, then the Böhm trees of
M,N
also differ from
\mathrm{BT}(\mathtt{\Xi}_{\iota}\lceil M\rceil\lceil N\rceil[\mathbf{\eta}_{n}]_{n\in{\rm Nature}})
because of
\eta
-expansions bounded by
p
.

Lemma 45
.

Let
M,N,P\in\Lambda^{\!o}
. If
M\geq^{\eta}_{p}P\leq^{\eta}_{p}N
, then:

M\leq^{\eta}_{p}\mathtt{\Xi}_{\iota}\lceil M\rceil\lceil N\rceil[\mathbf{\eta}_{n}]_{n\in{\rm Nature}}\geq^{\eta}_{p}N.

Proof 7.6
.

We proceed by coinduction on their Böhm trees.

If
P
is unsolvable, then also
M,N
and
\mathtt{\Xi}_{\iota}\lceil M\rceil\lceil N\rceil
are unsolvable and we are done.

Otherwise
P\twoheadrightarrow_{h}\lambda\vec{x}.x_{j}P_{1}\cdots P_{k}
and from
P\leq^{\eta}_{p}M
we obtain for some
m\leq p
:

M\twoheadrightarrow_{h}\lambda\vec{x}z_{1}\dots z_{m}.x_{j}M_{1}\cdots M_{k}Q_{1}\cdots Q_{m}

where
P_{i}\leq^{\eta}_{p}M_{i}
for
i\leq k
and
\lambda z_{\ell}.Q_{\ell}\in\mathcal{I}^{\eta}_{p}
for
\ell\leq m
.
Similarly, from
P\leq^{\eta}_{p}N
we get, say,

N\twoheadrightarrow_{h}\lambda\vec{x}z_{1}\dots z_{m}w_{1}\dots w_{m^{\prime}}.x_{j}N_{1}\cdots N_{k}Q^{\prime}_{1}\cdots Q^{\prime}_{m+m^{\prime}}

where
m+m^{\prime}\leq p
,
P_{i}\leq^{\eta}_{p}N_{i}
for
i\leq k
,
\lambda z_{\ell}.Q^{\prime}_{\ell}\in\mathcal{I}^{\eta}_{p}
for
\ell\leq m
and
\lambda w_{\ell}.Q^{\prime}_{m+\ell}\in\mathcal{I}^{\eta}_{p}
for
\ell\leq m^{\prime}
, so we have
M\leq_{h}N
.
(Notice that the case
N<_{h}M
is symmetrical, one just needs to apply Lemma
31
.)
For
q=\iota(\#(\mathtt{NF}_{\beta}(\lambda y\vec{w}.yQ^{\prime}_{m+1}\cdots Q^{\prime}_{m+m^{\prime}})))
, we have that:

\begin{array}[]{rcl}\mathtt{\Xi}_{\iota}\lceil M\rceil\lceil N\rceil[\mathbf{\eta}_{n}]_{n\in{\rm Nature}}&=_{\beta}&\lambda\vec{x}\vec{z}.\pi_{q}[\mathbf{\eta}_{n}]_{n\in{\rm Nature}}(x_{j}(\Upsilon_{1}\,\vec{x}\vec{z}\,)\cdots(\Upsilon_{k}\,\vec{x}\vec{z}\,)(\Upsilon^{\prime}_{1}\,\vec{x}\vec{z}\,)\cdots(\Upsilon^{\prime}_{m}\,\vec{x}\vec{z}\,))\\
&=_{\beta}&\lambda\vec{x}\vec{z}\vec{w}.x_{j}(\Upsilon_{1}\vec{x}\vec{z}\,)\cdots(\Upsilon_{k}\vec{x}\vec{z}\,)(\Upsilon^{\prime}_{1}\vec{x}\vec{z}\,)\cdots(\Upsilon^{\prime}_{m}\vec{x}\vec{z}\,)Q^{\prime}_{m+1}\cdots Q^{\prime}_{m+m^{\prime}}\end{array}

where
\Upsilon_{i}=\mathtt{\Xi}_{\iota}\lceil\lambda\vec{x}\vec{z}.M_{i}\rceil\lceil\lambda\vec{x}\vec{z}.N_{i}\rceil[\mathbf{\eta}_{n}]_{n\in{\rm Nature}}
for
i\leq k
, and
\Upsilon^{\prime}_{\ell}=\mathtt{\Xi}_{\iota}\lceil\lambda\vec{x}\vec{z}.Q_{\ell}\rceil\lceil\lambda\vec{x}\vec{z}.Q^{\prime}_{\ell}\rceil[\mathbf{\eta}_{n}]_{n\in{\rm Nature}}
for
\ell\leq m
.
By applying Lemma
44
, we get
Q_{\ell}\leq^{\eta}_{p}\Upsilon^{\prime}_{\ell}\vec{x}\vec{z}\geq^{\eta}_{p}Q^{\prime}_{\ell}
. By coinductive
hypotheses, we get
M_{i}\leq^{\eta}_{p}\Upsilon_{i}\vec{x}\vec{z}\geq^{\eta}_{p}N_{i}
.
Since
m\leq m+m^{\prime}\leq p
and
Q^{\prime}_{m+\ell}\in\mathcal{I}^{\eta}_{p}
we conclude that
M\leq^{\eta}_{p}\mathtt{\Xi}_{\iota}\lceil M\rceil\lceil N\rceil[\mathbf{\eta}_{n}]_{n\in{\rm Nature}}\geq^{\eta}_{p}N
holds.

7.3.
A New Characterization of the Equality in
{\mathcal{B}\eta}

We are now ready to provide a characterization of the equality
M=_{\mathcal{B}\eta}N
in terms of equality between
\mathrm{BT}(M)
and
\mathrm{BT}(N)
up to bounded
\eta
-expansions.
The key idea we exploit is the fact that, under these hypotheses,
\mathtt{\Xi}_{\iota}\lceil M\rceil\lceil N\rceil
only depends on a finite restriction of its input stream
S
and that all finite restrictions of
[\mathbf{\eta}_{n}]_{n\in{\rm Nature}}
and
[\mathtt{I}]_{n\in{\rm Nature}}
of the same length are
\beta\eta
-convertible with each other (since they are finite).

{defi}

Let
S=[S_{i}]_{i\in{\rm Nature}}
be a stream of
\lambda
-terms.
For
n\in{\rm Nature}
, define the
n
-truncation of
S
as the following sequence:

S\,\hskip-4.0pt\upharpoonright_{\!n}\ =\ [S_{0},[S_{1},[\,\cdots,[S_{n},\Omega]\cdots]]]

It is easy to check that the
i
-th projection
\pi_{i}
defined for
S
also works on
S\,\hskip-4.0pt\upharpoonright_{\!n}
for all
i\leq n
.
Notice that the
\Omega
at the end of the sequence
S\hskip-4.0pt\upharpoonright_{\!n}
does not have a profound meaning: we just need to have an
n+1
-component since
\pi_{n}=\lambda y.y\mathtt{F}^{\sim n}\mathtt{K}
needs something to erase in that position.

We have seen in Lemma
29
that
\iota(\#Q)
corresponds to the
smallest
index
i
such that
Q
occurs in
[\mathbf{\eta}_{n}]_{n\in{\rm Nature}}
.
The following property is a consequence of such a minimality condition.

Lemma 46
.

Let
p\in{\rm Nature}
. There exists an index
n
such that for every closed
\lambda
-term
Q\in\mathcal{I}^{\eta}_{p}
we have
\iota(\#Q)\leq n
.

Proof 7.7
.

Since the size of each
Q\in\mathcal{I}^{\eta}_{p}
is bounded by
p
, the set
\mathtt{nf}_{\beta}(\mathcal{I}^{\eta}_{p})=\{\mathtt{nf}_{\beta}(Q):Q\in\mathcal{I}^{\eta}_{p}\}
is finite.
By Lemma
29
,
\iota(\#Q)
gives the smallest index
i
such that
Q=_{\beta}\eta_{i}
, therefore the set
\iota[\mathcal{I}^{\eta}_{p}]=\{\iota(\#Q):Q\in\mathcal{I}^{\eta}_{p}\cap\Lambda^{\!o}\}
is finite.
We conclude by considering
n=\max(\iota[\mathcal{I}^{\eta}_{p}])
.

As a corollary we get that if
M\leq^{\eta}_{p}N
holds, then
\mathtt{\Xi}_{\iota}\lceil M\rceil\lceil N\rceil
only uses a finite portion of its input stream.

Corollary 47
.

For all
M,N\in\Lambda^{\!o}
, if
M\leq^{\eta}_{p}N
then there exists an index
n\in{\rm Nature}
such that for every stream
S=[S_{i}]_{i\in{\rm Nature}}
we have:

\mathcal{B}\vdash\mathtt{\Xi}_{\iota}\lceil M\rceil\lceil N\rceil S=\mathtt{\Xi}_{\iota}\lceil M\rceil\lceil N\rceil(S\hskip-4.0pt\upharpoonright_{\!n}).

Proof 7.8
.

By Lemma
46
, there exists
n
such that for every
Q\in\mathcal{I}^{\eta}_{p+1}\cap\Lambda^{\!o}
we have
\iota(\#Q)\leq n
.
We prove the statement by coinduction for that particular
n
.

If
M
or
N
are unsolvable, than so is
\mathtt{\Xi}_{\iota}\lceil M\rceil\lceil N\rceil
and we are done.

Otherwise, we have
M\twoheadrightarrow_{h}\lambda\vec{x}.x_{j}M_{1}\cdots M_{k}
and
N\twoheadrightarrow_{h}\lambda\vec{x}\vec{z}.x_{j}N_{1}\cdots N_{k}Q_{1}\cdots Q_{m}
for
m\leq p
and
\lambda z_{\ell}.Q_{\ell}\in\mathcal{I}^{\eta}_{p}
for all
\ell\leq m
.
By Lemma
39
, the
\lambda
-term defined as
Q=\mathtt{nf}_{\beta}(\lambda y\vec{z}.yQ_{1}\cdots Q_{m})
belongs to the set
\mathcal{I}^{\eta}_{p+1}
.
Hence, for some index
q=\iota(\#Q)\leq n
, we have on the one side:

\begin{array}[]{rl}\mathtt{\Xi}_{\iota}\lceil M\rceil\lceil N\rceil S=_{\beta}&\lambda\vec{x}.\pi_{q}S(x_{j}(\Upsilon_{1}\vec{x})\cdots(\Upsilon_{k}\vec{x}))\\
=_{\beta}&\lambda\vec{x}.S_{q}(x_{j}(\Upsilon_{1}\vec{x})\cdots(\Upsilon_{k}\vec{x}))\\
\end{array}

where
\Upsilon_{i}=\mathtt{\Xi}_{\iota}\lceil M_{i}\rceil\lceil N_{i}\rceil{S}
. On the other side, we have:

\begin{array}[]{rl}\mathtt{\Xi}_{\iota}\lceil M\rceil\lceil N\rceil(S\,\hskip-4.0pt\upharpoonright_{\!n})=_{\beta}&\lambda\vec{x}.\pi_{q}(S\,\hskip-4.0pt\upharpoonright_{\!n})(x_{j}(\Upsilon^{\prime}_{1}\vec{x})\cdots(\Upsilon^{\prime}_{k}\vec{x}))\\
=_{\beta}&\lambda\vec{x}.S_{q}(x_{j}(\Upsilon^{\prime}_{1}\vec{x})\cdots(\Upsilon^{\prime}_{k}\vec{x}))\\
\end{array}

where
\Upsilon^{\prime}_{i}=\mathtt{\Xi}_{\iota}\lceil M_{i}\rceil\lceil N_{i}\rceil(S\,\hskip-4.0pt\upharpoonright_{\!n})
.
We can conclude since, by coinductive hypothesis,
\mathrm{BT}(\Upsilon_{i})=\mathrm{BT}(\Upsilon^{\prime}_{i})
holds for all
i\leq k
.

Since
M=_{\mathcal{B}\eta}N
holds exactly when there is an alternating sequence of shape
M=_{\mathcal{B}}M_{0}=_{\eta}M_{1}=_{\mathcal{B}}\cdots=_{\eta}M_{k}=_{\mathcal{B}}~N
, “zig-zag” sequences like the one in the following lemma naturally arise (for instance, in the proof of Theorem
49
).

Lemma 48
.

Let
M_{1},\dots,M_{k+1}\in\Lambda^{\!o}
and let
p\in{\rm Nature}
.
If, for some
N_{i}\in\Lambda^{\!o}
, there is a zig-zag sequence such that:
~M_{1}\qquad\qquad M_{2}\quad\cdots\quad M_{k-1}\qquad\qquad M_{k}\qquad\qquad M_{k+1}
N_{1}\qquad\qquad N_{2}\qquad\cdots\quad N_{k-1}\qquad\quad\ N_{k}\ \
\geq^{\eta}_{p}
\leq^{\eta}_{p}
\geq^{\eta}_{p}
\leq^{\eta}_{p}
\geq^{\eta}_{p}
\leq^{\eta}_{p}
\geq^{\eta}_{p}
\leq^{\eta}_{p}

then there exists
P\in\Lambda^{\!o}
such that
M_{1}\leq^{\eta}_{kp}P\geq^{\eta}_{kp}M_{k}
.

Proof 7.9
.

We proceed by induction on
k
.

Case
k=0
. Trivial, just take
P=M_{1}
.

Case
k>0
. By applying Lemma
45
to each pair
M_{i}
and
M_{i+1}
we get, setting
N^{\prime}_{i}=\mathtt{\Xi}_{\iota}\lceil M_{i}\rceil\lceil M_{i+1}\rceil[\mathbf{\eta}_{n}]_{n\in{\rm Nature}}
, the sequence:

M_{1}\leq^{\eta}_{p}N^{\prime}_{1}\geq^{\eta}_{p}M_{2}\cdots\leq^{\eta}_{p}N^{\prime}_{k}\geq^{\eta}_{p}M_{k+1}.

As the subsequence
N^{\prime}_{1}\geq^{\eta}_{p}M_{2}\cdots\leq^{\eta}_{p}N^{\prime}_{k}
is shorter and satisfies the hypotheses of the lemma, we get from the inductive hypothesis a
\lambda
-term
P\in\Lambda^{\!o}
such that
N^{\prime}_{1}\leq^{\eta}_{(k-1)p}P\geq^{\eta}_{(k-1)p}N^{\prime}_{k}
.
We conclude by Lemma
42
since
(k-1)p+p=kp
.

We are now able to provide the following characterization of
{\mathcal{B}\eta}
,
which constitutes the second main result of the paper.

Theorem 49
.

For all
M,N\in\Lambda^{\!o}
, the following are equivalent:

(1)

{\mathcal{B}\eta}\vdash M=N
,

(2)

there exist
P\in\Lambda^{\!o}
and
p\in{\rm Nature}
such that
M\leq^{\eta}_{p}P\geq^{\eta}_{p}N
.

Proof 7.10
.

(1\Rightarrow 2)
Since
{\mathcal{B}\eta}
is the join of two congruences, namely
=_{\mathcal{B}}
and
=_{\eta}
, we have that
{\mathcal{B}\eta}\vdash M=N
holds if and only if there are
M_{0},\dots,M_{k}\in\Lambda^{\!o}
such that

M=_{\mathcal{B}}M_{0}=_{\eta}M_{1}=_{\mathcal{B}}\cdots=_{\eta}M_{k}=_{\mathcal{B}}N
(cf.

10
, Thm. 4.6]
).
Since
\eta
-reduction is Church-Rosser and the
M_{j}
’s are closed, for even indices
i
(as odd indices correspond to
=_{\mathcal{B}}
steps), we have
M_{i}=_{\eta}M_{i+1}
if and only if
M_{i}\twoheadrightarrow_{\eta}N_{i}\ {}_{\eta}\!\!\twoheadleftarrow M_{i+1}
for some
N_{i}\in\Lambda^{\!o}
. By Corollary
43
there are
p_{i},q_{i}
such that
M_{i}\geq^{\eta}_{p_{i}}N_{i}\leq^{\eta}_{q_{i}}M_{i+1}
.
By Lemma
40
(iii)
, setting
p^{\prime}=\max_{i}\{p_{i},q_{i}\}
we get
M_{i}\geq^{\eta}_{p^{\prime}}N_{i}\leq^{\eta}_{p^{\prime}}M_{i+1}
.
By applying Lemma
40
(ii)
we can get rid of the equality
=_{\mathcal{B}}
and obtain the sequence:

M\geq^{\eta}_{p^{\prime}}N_{1}\leq^{\eta}_{p^{\prime}}M_{2}\geq^{\eta}_{p^{\prime}}N_{2}\cdots N_{k-1}\leq^{\eta}_{p^{\prime}}N.

Therefore this implication follows from Lemma
48
.

(2\Rightarrow 1)
We assume that
M\leq^{\eta}_{p}P\geq^{\eta}_{p}N
holds.
From Lemma
40
(i), we obtain
M\leq^{\eta}P\geq^{\eta}N
as well.
Therefore (for some
k,k^{\prime}\in{\rm Nature}
):

\begin{array}[]{rll}\quad\ M\ =_{\mathcal{B}}&\mathtt{\Xi}_{\iota}\lceil M\rceil\lceil P\rceil[\mathtt{I}]_{n\in{\rm Nature}}&\textrm{by Lemma~\ref{lem:Mwins}}\\
=_{\mathcal{B}}&\mathtt{\Xi}_{\iota}\lceil M\rceil\lceil P\rceil(([\mathtt{I}]_{n\in{\rm Nature}})\hskip-4.0pt\upharpoonright_{\!k})&\textrm{by Corollary~\ref{cor:restricting_stream}}\\
=_{\beta\eta}&\mathtt{\Xi}_{\iota}\lceil M\rceil\lceil P\rceil(([\mathbf{\eta}_{n}]_{n\in{\rm Nature}})\hskip-4.0pt\upharpoonright_{\!k})&\textrm{by $\beta\eta$-conversion}\\
=_{\mathcal{B}}&\mathtt{\Xi}_{\iota}\lceil M\rceil\lceil P\rceil[\mathbf{\eta}_{n}]_{n\in{\rm Nature}}&\textrm{by Corollary~\ref{cor:restricting_stream}}\\
=_{\mathcal{B}}&P&\textrm{by Lemma~\ref{lem:Nwins}}\\
=_{\mathcal{B}}&\mathtt{\Xi}_{\iota}\lceil N\rceil\lceil P\rceil[\mathbf{\eta}_{n}]_{n\in{\rm Nature}}&\textrm{by Lemma~\ref{lem:Nwins}}\\
=_{\mathcal{B}}&\mathtt{\Xi}_{\iota}\lceil N\rceil\lceil P\rceil(([\mathbf{\eta}_{n}]_{n\in{\rm Nature}})\hskip-4.0pt\upharpoonright_{\!k^{\prime}})&\textrm{by Corollary~\ref{cor:restricting_stream}}\\
=_{\beta\eta}&\mathtt{\Xi}_{\iota}\lceil N\rceil\lceil P\rceil(([\mathtt{I}]_{n\in{\rm Nature}})\hskip-4.0pt\upharpoonright_{\!k^{\prime}})&\textrm{by $\beta\eta$-conversion}\\

=_{\mathcal{B}}&N&\textrm{by Lemma~\ref{lem:Mwins}}\\

We conclude that
{\mathcal{B}\eta}\vdash M=N
.

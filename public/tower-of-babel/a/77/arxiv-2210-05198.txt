Optimal geodesics for boundary points of the Gardiner-Masur compactification
Xiaoke Lou, Weixu Su, Dong Tan

Abstract
. The Gardiner-Masur compactification of Teichmüller space is homeomorphic to the horofunction compactification of the Teichmüller metric. Let ξ \xi and η \eta be a pair of boundary points in the Gardiner-Masur compactification that fill up the surface. We show that there is a unique Teichmüller geodesic which is optimal for the horofunctions corresponding to ξ \xi and η \eta . In particular, when ξ \xi and η \eta are Busemann points that fill up the surface, the geodesic converges to ξ \xi in forward direction and to η \eta in backward direction. As an application, we show that if 𝐆 n \mathbf{G}_{n} is a sequence of Teichmüller geodesics passing through X n X_{n} and Y n Y_{n} such that X n → ξ X_{n}\to\xi and Y n → η Y_{n}\to\eta , then 𝐆 n \mathbf{G}_{n} converges to a unique Teichmüller geodesic.



1.
Introduction

1.1.
Background

Let
S=S_{g,n}
be an oriented closed surface of genus
g
with
n
punctures, where
3g-3+n\geq 2
.
Let
\mathcal{T}(S)
be the Teichmüller space of
S
.
It is naturally a complex manifold of dimension
3g-3+n
.

We shall endow
\mathcal{T}(S)
with the Teichmüller metric, which is equal to the Kobayashi metric
[
29
]
.
It is known that the Teichmüller space of torus is isometric to the hyperbolic plane. In higher dimensions, the situation is very different:

(1)

Royden
[
29
]
proved that the
Teichmüller space endowed with the Teichmüller metric is not homogeneous and there is no point about which it is symmetric. Moreover, Royden’s theorem (extended by Earle-Kra
[
7
]
) about automorphisms of Teichmüller space states that any isometry of the Teichmüller metric is induced by an element of the mapping class group.

(2)

Masur
[
19
]
showed that the Teichmüller metric does not have negative curvature in the sense of Busemann;
Masur and Wolf
[
21
]
showed that the Teichmüller metric is not Gromov hyperbolic. See also
[
23
]
.

(3)

The Teichmüller space is biholomorphic to a bounded pseudoconvex domain
[
4
]
. Recently, Markovic
[
18
]
showed that
the Teichmüller space is not
biholomorphic to a bounded convex domain in
\mathbb{C}^{3g-3+n}
.

Nevertheless, there is a strong analogy between the geometry of Teichmüller space and that of a hyperbolic space

[
1
,
3
,
20
,
28
]
.
The aim of this paper is to explore some properties of the horofunction boundary of
\mathcal{T}(S)
, inspired by analogies with
negatively curved spaces.

1.2.
Gardiner-Masur boundary

Horofunction compactification and the corresponding boundary of a proper metric space were introduced by Gromov
[
12
]
. These notions have been recently studied in several research
areas, including geometry of bounded convex domains with the Hilbert metric, Teichmüller spaces, and random walks on groups of isometries
[
32
,
11
]
.

Liu-Su
[
17
]
showed that the horofunction compactification of
\mathcal{T}(S)
equipped with the Teichmüller metric is homeomorphic to the Gardiner-Masur compactification.

Unlike the Thurston boundary, which is homeomorphic to a sphere of dimension
6g-7+2n
,
the topology of the Gardiner-Masur boundary is more complicated. Note that every Teichmüller geodesic ray converges to a unique limit on
the Gardiner-Masur boundary. Limits of geodesic rays are called
Busemann points
.

Busemann points in the Gardiner-Masur boundary are completely characterized by Walsh
[
33
]
.
There exist non-Busemann points in the Gardiner-Masur boundary
[
26
]
.
Recently, Azemar
[
2
]
showed that
Busemann points are not dense in the Gardiner-Masur boundary.

In a proper
\delta
-hyperbolic metric space,
any interior point and any
boundary point can be connected by a geodesic ray.
Moreover, any two distinct boundary points
can be connected by a (bi-infinite) geodesic.
See
[
13
]
.

By identifying the Gardiner-Masur compactification with the horofunction compactification of
\mathcal{T}(S)
,
each boundary point
is corresponding to a horofunction.
Azemar
[
2
]
showed that,
for any given horofunction
\Psi
and
any given
X\in\mathcal{T}(S)
, there is a unique
optimal geodesic
for

\xi
passing through
X
.
By definition, a geodesic
\mathbf{G}(t)
is optimal for
\Psi
if
for every
t\in\mathbb{R}
,

\Psi(\mathbf{G}(t))-\Psi(\mathbf{G}(0))=-t.

Equivalently, the optimal geodsic is a gradient line along which the horofunction decreases fastest.

When
\Psi
is a Busemann function (horofunction function corresponding to Busemann point),
\mathbf{G}(t)
is exactly the geodesic that converges to
\Psi
in forward direction.

1.3.
Main results

The main result in this paper is to determine when
two distinct boundary points of
\mathcal{T}(S)
are “connected” by a Teichmüller geodesic.

Let
\mathcal{MF}
be the space of measured foliations on
S
.
Every point
\xi
in the Gardiner-Masur boundary is
determined by a nonnegative homogeneous function

i(\xi,\cdot)\colon\mathcal{MF}\to\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}.

The representation is unique up to multiplication by a positive constant
[
24
, Theorem 1.1]
.

The following definition is a generalization of a pair of measured foliations that fill up the surface.

Definition 1.1
.

Let
\xi
and
\eta
be a pair of points in the Gardiner-Masur boundary. We say that
\xi
and
\eta

fill up the surface if

i(\xi,\mu)+i(\eta,\mu)>0

for all
\mu\in\mathcal{MF}\setminus\{0\}.

Let
\xi
and
\eta
be a pair of points in the Gardiner-Masur boundary.
A Teichmüller geodesic
\mathbf{G}(t)
is said to be
optimal
for
\xi
and

\eta
if

\mathbf{G}(t)
is an optimal geodesic for
\xi
and
the reverse geodesic
\widetilde{\mathbf{G}}(t)=\mathbf{G}(-t)
is an optimal geodesic for
\eta
.
We prove

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
\xi
and
\eta
be a pair of points in the Gardiner-Masur boundary that fill up the surface. There is a unique Teichmüller geodesic that is optimal for
\xi
and
\eta
.

Theorem
1.2
does not indicate that the geodesic converges to
\xi
or
\eta
, in case that
\xi
and
\eta
are not Busemann points.

A special case of Theorem
1.2
is

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
\xi,\eta
be a pair of Busemann points
in the Gardiner-Masur boundary that fill up the surface. There exists a unique Teichmüller geodesic
\mathbf{G}(t)
such that

\lim_{t\rightarrow+\infty}\mathbf{G}(t)=\xi\quad\text{ and }\quad\lim_{t\rightarrow-\infty}\mathbf{G}(t)=\eta.

The assumption that
\xi
and
\eta

fill up the surface is necessary both in Theorem
1.2
and
Theorem
1.3
.

Remark 1.4
.

Gardiner-Masur
[
10
, Theorem 5.1]
proved that,
given any pair of measured foliations
\mu,\nu
that fill up the surface, there is a unique
Teichmüller geodesic determined by a holomorphic quadratic differetnial whose horizontal and vertical
foliations are in the projective classes of
\mu
and
\nu
.
However, the geodesic may not converges to the
\mu
or
\nu
in general.

1.4.
Idea of the proofs

Theorem
1.3
can be proved directly.
The set of Busemann points in the
Gardiner-Masur boundary is completely characterized by Walsh
[
33
, Theorem 8]
.
Given a Teichmüller geodesic ray, the limited Busemann point
can be represented explicitly in terms of the decomposition of initial quadratic differential.
The problem of existence (and uniqueness) of
geodesic in Theorem
1.3
is reduced to the existence (and uniqueness) of positive eigenvector of a Perron-Frobenius matrix.

In general, there is no explicit representation for a horofunction,
thus the previous argument for Theorem
1.3
doesn’t work.
Instead, we consider level sets of the horofunction
\xi
, defined by

\mathrm{L}(\xi,s)=\{X\in\mathcal{T}(S)\ :\Psi_{\xi}(X)=s\},

where
\Psi_{\xi}
is the horofunction corresponding to
\xi
.

The filling condition of
\xi
and
\eta
implies that each
\mathrm{L}(\xi,s)

is tangent to some
\mathrm{L}(\eta,t)
. We observe that, if we take
X
to be an intersection point of
\mathrm{L}(\xi,s)
and
\mathrm{L}(\eta,t)
, then the optimal geodesic
for
\xi
passing through
X
, by changing its direction, is the optimal geodesic for
\eta
passing through
X
.

The key proof is based on the following inequality.
Associated with a Teichmüller geodesic, there
are two natural horofunctions
\Psi_{{F}}
and
B_{\xi}
, where
\Psi_{{F}}
is corresponding to the vertical foliation of the quadratic differential determining the geodesic, and
B_{\xi}
is the limited Busemann function.
Then we have

(1)

\Psi_{F}\leq\Psi_{\xi}\leq B_{\xi}.

Note that the level sets of
\Psi_{F}
are corresponding to level sets of the extremal length function
\mathrm{Ext}_{F}
, which was studied in
[
30
]
. In this paper, we will give a proof that the Busemann function
B_{\xi}
is
C^{1}
.
Using the above inequality, we are able to show that for each
s
, the level set
\mathrm{L}(\xi,s)
intersects with
\mathrm{L}(\eta,t)
at a unique point
X_{s}
,
where
t
depends on
s
. Consequently, the loci of intersection points

\{X_{s}\}
define the optimal geodesic.

1.5.
An application

We apply Theorem
1.2
to show

Theorem 1.5
.

Let
\xi
and
\eta
be a pair of Gardiner-Masur boundary points that fill up the surface.
For any sequences
X_{n},Y_{n}\in\mathcal{T}(S)
such that
X_{n}\to\xi
and
Y_{n}\to\eta
,
let
\mathbf{G}_{n}
be the Teichmüller geodesics passing through
X_{n},Y_{n}\in\mathcal{T}(S)
.
Then
\mathbf{G}_{n}
converge to a unique geodesic as
n\to\infty
. The limited geodesic is exactly the optimal geodesic in Theorem
1.2
.

To prove Theorem
1.5
, we first show that there is a compact set
\mathcal{K}\subset\mathcal{T}(S)
such that each geodesic
\mathbf{G}_{k}
intersects
\mathcal{K}
.
This is confirmed by the assumption that
\xi
and
\eta
fill up the surface.
Up to a subsequence,
\mathbf{G}_{k}
converges to some geodeic
\mathbf{G}
.
Finally, we show that
\mathbf{G}
is an optimal geodesic for
\xi
and
\eta
.
Then Theorem
1.5
follows from the uniqueness of optimal geodesic.

A corollary of Theorem
1.5

is that Teichmüller geodesics are “sticky”.
See Theorem
6.5
.
The notion is introduced by Duchin and Fisher
[
5
]
.
They considered the Thurston compactification of
\mathcal{T}(S)
and made the following conjecture.

Conjecture 1.6

(Duchin-Fisher)
.

Let
\mathbf{G}
be a Teichmüller geodesic ending at two simple closed curves
\alpha
and
\beta
(it is necessary that
\alpha
and
\beta
fill up the surface). There exists a compact set
\mathcal{K}\subset\mathcal{T}(S)
such that for any sequences
X_{n}\to\alpha
and
Y_{n}\to\beta
in the Thurston compactification, the geodesic segments connecting
X_{n}
and
Y_{n}
intersect
\mathcal{K}
for sufficiently large
n
.

Theorem
1.5
shows that the above conjecture is true if we replace the Thurston compactification by the Gardiner-Masur compactification.
However, we don’t know whether
X_{n}\to\alpha
(where
\alpha
is a simple closed curve) in the Thurston compactification implies
X_{n}\to\alpha
in the Gardiner-Masur compactification or not.
The two convergences are equivalent if the limit is a uniquely ergodic measured foliation
[
25
, Corollary 1]
.

2.
Preliminaries

In this section, we give a brief exposition of Teichmüller theory of Riemann surfaces, quadratic differentials and measured foliations.
More details can be found in the books
[
8
,
9
]
.

2.1.
Teichmüller space

Let
S
be an oriented closed surface of genus
g
with
n
punctures,
where
3g-3+n\geq 2
.
The
Teichmüller space

\mathcal{T}(S)
is the space of equivalence classes of marked Riemann surfaces

(X,f)
, where
X
is a Riemann surface of analytically finite type

(g,n)
and
f:S\to X

is an orientation-preserving homeomorphism. The equivalence relation is given by
(X_{1},f_{1})\sim(X_{2},f_{2})
if there is a conformal mapping

h:X_{1}\rightarrow X_{2}
homotopic to
f_{2}\circ f_{1}^{-1}
.

For any two equivalence classes

[(X_{1},f_{1})],[(X_{2},f_{2})]\in\mathcal{T}(S)
,
their
Teichmüller distance
is defined by

d_{\mathcal{T}}([(X_{1},f_{1})],[(X_{2},f_{2})])=\frac{1}{2}\inf_{\phi}\log K(\phi),

where
\phi
is taken over all quasiconformal mappings

\phi:X_{1}\rightarrow X_{2}

homotopic to
f_{2}\circ f_{1}^{-1}
, and
K(\phi)
is the maximal quasiconformal
dilatation of
\phi
.

For simplicity of notation, we shall denote a point in
\mathcal{T}(S)
by a Riemann surface
X
,
without explicit reference to the marking or to the equivalence relation.

The Teichmüller metric is a complete Finsler metric on
\mathcal{T}(S)
. Any two distinct points in
\mathcal{T}(S)
are connected by a unique geodesic.
Teichmüller geodesic will be defined in §
2.3
.

2.2.
Measured foliations

Recall that a
measured foliation

F
on
S
is a foliation (with a finite number of singularities) with a transverse invariant measure. This means that if the local coordinates send the regular leaves of
F
to horizontal
\operatorname{arcs}
in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
, then the transition functions on
\mathbb{R}^{2}
are of the form
(f(x,y),\pm y+c
) where
c
is a constant, and the measure is given by
|dy|
. The allowed singularities of
\mathcal{F}
are topologically the same as those that occur at
z=0
in the line field of
z^{p-2}dz^{2},p\geq 3
or
p=1
at the puncture of
S
.

Let
\mathcal{S}
be the set of free homotopy classes of essential (non-trivial, non-peripheral) simple closed curves on
S
. The intersection number
i(\gamma,F)
of a simple closed curve
\gamma
with a measured foliation
F
endowed with transverse measure
\mu
is defined by

i(\gamma,F)=\inf_{\gamma^{\prime}}\int_{\gamma^{\prime}}d\mu,

where the infimum is taken over all simple closed curves
\gamma^{\prime}
in the isotopy class of
\gamma
.
Two measured foliations
F
and
F^{\prime}
are measure equivalent if, for all
\gamma\in\mathcal{S}
,

i(\gamma,F)=i\left(\gamma,F^{\prime}\right).

Denote by
\mathcal{MF}=\mathcal{MF}(S)
the space of equivalence classes of measured foliations on
S
.

Two measured foliations
{F}
and
{F}^{\prime}
are projectively equivalent if there is a constant
b>0
such that
{F}=b\cdot{F}^{\prime}
, i.e.
i(\gamma,{F})=b\cdot i\left(\gamma,{F}^{\prime}\right)
for all
\gamma\in\mathcal{S}
. The space of projective equivalence classes of foliations is denoted by
\mathcal{PMF}
.

Thurston showed that
\mathcal{MF}
is homeomorphic to a
6g-6+2n
dimensional ball and
\mathcal{PM\mathcal{F}}
is homeomorphic to a
6g-7+2n
dimensional sphere,
and the set
\mathcal{S}
(corresponding to
one-cylinder measured foliations) is dense in
\mathcal{PMF}
. We refer to
[
9
]

for more details.

The space
\mathcal{PMF}
can be considered as a completion of
\mathcal{S}
.
It plays an important role in the work of Thurston on classification of mapping classes.
Following Thurston,

\mathcal{PMF}
is a natural boundary of
\mathcal{T}(S)
called the Thurston boundary.
In §
3
, we will introduce a larger boundary of
\mathcal{T}(S)
called the
Gardiner-Masur boundary.

Given a conformal metric
\rho
on a Riemann surface
X
, denote the
\rho
-area of
X
by

\operatorname{Area}_{\rho}(X)
.
The extremal length of
\gamma\in\mathcal{S}
on
X
is defined by

\operatorname{Ext}_{X}(\gamma)=\sup_{\rho}\frac{\left({\inf_{\gamma^{\prime}\simeq\gamma}\int_{\gamma^{\prime}}\rho(z)|\mathrm{dz}|}\right)^{2}}{\operatorname{Area}_{\rho}(X)},

where
\rho(z)|\mathrm{d}z|
ranges over all conformal metrics on
X
with

0<\operatorname{Area}_{\rho}(X)<\infty.

Kerckhoff
[
14
]
showed that the notion
of extremal length extends continuously to the space of measured foliations. Moreover, he proved the following important distance formula for the Teichmüller metric.

Theorem 2.1
.

The Teichmüller distance between any
X,Y\in\mathcal{T}(S)
is equal to

\displaystyle d_{\mathcal{T}}(X,Y)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\log\sup_{\gamma\in\mathcal{S}}\frac{\operatorname{Ext}_{X}(\gamma)}{\operatorname{Ext}_{Y}(\gamma)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\log\sup_{\mu\in\mathcal{MF}}\frac{\operatorname{Ext}_{X}(\mu)}{\operatorname{Ext}_{Y}(\mu)}.

2.3.
Quadratic differentials and Teichmüller geodesics

A
holomorphic quadratic differential

q
on
X\in\mathcal{T}_{g,n}
is a tensor which is locally represented by
q=q(z)dz^{2}
, where
q(z)
is a holomorphic function on the local conformal coordinate
z
of
X
. We allow holomorphic quadratic differentials to have at most simple poles at the punctures of
X
. Denote the vector space of holomorphic quadratic differentials on
X
by
Q(X)
.

For
q\in Q(X)
, its area is defined by

\|q\|=\int_{X}|q(z)|\ |dz|^{2}.

We denote by
Q^{1}(X)
the subset of quadratic differentials on

X
of unit area.

A quadratic differential
q
determines a pair of transverse measured foliations
{F}_{h}(q)
and
{F}_{v}(q)
, called the
horizontal and vertical foliations
for
q
. Away from the zeroes of

q
, we can choose natural coordinates
z=x+iy
such that

q=dz^{2}
. Then the leaves of
{F}_{h}(q)
are given by

y=\text{const;}

and the leaves of
{F}_{v}(q)
are given by

x=\text{const},

and the transverse measures are
|dy|
and
|dx|
. The foliations
{F}_{h}(q)
and
{F}_{v}(q)
have the zero set of
q
as their common singular set, and at each zero of order
k
they have a
(k+2)
-pronged singularity, locally modelled on the singularity at the origin of
z^{k}dz^{2}
.

Teichmüller geodesic rays are parameterized by quadratic differentials. Given a Riemann surface
X\in\mathcal{T}(S)
and a quadratic differential
q\in Q^{1}(X)
, there is a family of Riemann surfaces
X_{t}
(where
t\geq 0
) such that

d_{\mathcal{T}}\left(X,X_{t}\right)=t.

Moreover, the Teichmüller maps (i.e., extremal quasiconformal map)

f_{t}:X\to X_{t}

expands along the leaves of the horizontal foliation by
e^{t}
and contracts along the leaves of the vertical foliation by
e^{-t}
.
The family
\{X_{t}\}_{t\geq 0}
is called the Teichmüller geodesic ray determined by
q
, denoted by
\mathbf{R}_{q}(t)
. We can extend

\mathbf{R}_{q}(t)
to a complete geodesic
\mathbf{G}_{q}(t)
by allowing
t<0
.

A pair
{F}_{+},{F}_{-}
of measured foliations is called
fill up the surface
if for any
\mu\in\mathcal{MF}\setminus\{0\}
,

i\left({F}_{+},\mu\right)+i\left({F}_{-},\mu\right)\neq 0.

It is straightforward that the vertical and horizontal foliations of a quadratic differential fill up the surface. The converse is a theorem of Gardiner-Masur
[
10
]
:

Theorem 2.2
.

If a pair
{F}_{+},{F}_{-}
of measured foliations on
S
fill up the surface, then there is a Riemann surface
X
and a holomorphic quadratic differential
q
on
X
such that

{F}_{+}={F}_{v}(q),{F}_{-}={F}_{h}(q).

Moreover, both
X
and
q
are unique.

When
{F}_{+},{F}_{-}
fill up the surface, they
determine a unique Teichmüller geodesic

\mathbf{G}_{q}(t)
, where for each
t
,
\mathbf{G}_{q}(t)
is corresponding
the pair of measured foliations
e^{t}{F}_{+},e^{-t}{F}_{-}
.

3.
Gardiner-Masur compactification

In this section, we fix a base point
X_{0}
in
\mathcal{T}(S)
.
Let
\mathbb{R}_{+}^{\mathcal{S}}
be the space of nonnegative functions
on
\mathcal{S}
, endowed with the weak topology.
Let
\mathrm{P}\mathbb{R}_{+}^{\mathcal{S}}
be the set of projective classes of

\mathbb{R}_{+}^{\mathcal{S}}\setminus\{0\}
.

3.1.
Gardiner-Masur boundary

We define the embedding
\Phi:\mathcal{T}(S)\to\mathrm{P}\mathbb{R}_{+}^{\mathcal{S}}
by
taking

\Phi(X)=[(\sqrt{\operatorname{Ext}_{X}(\alpha)})_{\alpha\in\mathcal{S}}].

Gardiner and Masur
[
10
]
showed that the
closure of
\Phi(\mathcal{T}(S))
in
\mathrm{P}\mathbb{R}_{+}^{\mathcal{S}}

is compact. The boundary of
\Phi(\mathcal{T}(S))
is called
the
Gardiner-Masur boundary
, denoted by
\partial_{\mathrm{GM}}\mathcal{T}(S)
.
This boundary properly contains
\mathcal{PMF}
.

Miyachi
[
24
, Theorem 1.1]
proved

Theorem 3.1
.

Any point
\xi\in\partial_{\mathrm{GM}}\mathcal{T}(S)
can be represented as
a nonnegative continuous function
i(\xi,\mu),\ \mu\in\mathcal{MF}
on
such that

(2)

i(\xi,r\cdot\mu)=r\cdot i(\xi,\mu)

for all
r>0
and
\mu\in\mathcal{MF}
.
The assignment
\mathcal{S}\ni\alpha\mapsto i(\xi,\alpha)

determines
\xi
as a point of
\partial_{\mathrm{GM}}\mathcal{T}(S)
.
Furthermore, the function
i(\xi,\cdot)
is unique up to
multiplication by a positive constant.

We don’t have an explicit formula for a general

\xi
. The following inequality is useful.

Proposition 3.2
.

Let
X_{n}\in\mathcal{T}(S)
converge to a boundary point
\xi\in\partial_{\mathrm{GM}}\mathcal{T}(S)
. Assume that
q_{n}\in{Q^{1}(X_{0})}
is the initial quadratic differential associated to the Teichmüller map from
X_{0}
to
X_{n}
. Up to a subsequence, we also assume that the vertical measured foliations
{F}_{v}(q_{n})
of
q_{n}
converge to a measured foliation

{F}
. Then we have

i({F},\mu)\leq i(\xi,\mu)

for any
\mu\in\mathcal{MF}
.

In fact, the limit of
{F}_{v}(q_{n})

is unique, see
[
2
, Theorem 1.1]
and
[
16
, Theorem 3.4]
. Proposition
3.2
can be proved by using Minsky’s inequality.
See for instance Miyachi
[
24
, Proposition 5.1]
, Liu and Shi
[
16
, Lemma 3.10]
.
We also provide a proof, see Lemma
A.1
in the Appendix.

Remark 3.3
.

Note that the function
i(\xi,\cdot)
is unique up to
multiplication by a positive constant. In Proposition
3.2
,

i(\xi,\cdot)
is chosen as following.
For each
X_{n}
, we associate with a function on
\mathcal{MF}
:

(3)

i(X_{n},\mu)=\sqrt{\frac{\mathrm{Ext}_{X_{n}}(\mu)}{K(X_{0},X_{n})}},

where
K(X_{0},X_{n})
is the quasiconformal dilatation of the Teichmüller map from

X_{0}
to
X_{n}
. Then
i(X_{n},\mu)\to i(\xi,\mu)

uniformly on compact subsets of
\mathcal{MF}

as
X_{n}\to\xi
.

Following Miyachi
[
27
]
,
for any
X,Y\in\mathcal{T}(S)
, we set

\langle X|Y\rangle=\frac{1}{2}\left(d_{\mathcal{T}}(X_{0},X)+d_{\mathcal{T}}(X_{0},Y)-d_{\mathcal{T}}(X,Y)\right).

The intersection number between

X
and
Y
is defined by

(4)

i(X,Y)=e^{-2\langle X|Y\rangle}.

The next theorem, proved by Miyachi
[
27
, Theorem 4]
, unifies the above notions
(
2
) (
3
) and (
4
).

Theorem 3.4
.

The intersection number
i(X,Y)
extends continuously to the Gardiner-Masur compactification.
In particular, for any two sequences
X_{n}
and
Y_{n}
in
\mathcal{T}(S)
such that
X_{n}\to\xi\in\partial_{\mathrm{GM}}\mathcal{T}(S)
and
Y_{n}\to\eta\in\partial_{\mathrm{GM}}\mathcal{T}(S)
, we have

i(X_{n},Y_{n})\to i(\xi,\eta).

Im particular, we consider
\mathcal{PMF}
as a subset of the Gardiner-Masur boundary. Then each element in
\mathcal{PMF}

is represented by a measured foliations
\mu
such that

\mathrm{Ext}_{X_{0}}(\mu)=1
. For any

,

3.2.
Horofunction compactification

Fix a base point
X_{0}\in\mathcal{T}(S)
. To each
Z\in\mathcal{T}(S)
we assigned a function
\Psi_{Z}:\mathcal{T}(S)\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
, given by

\Psi_{Z}(X)=d_{\mathcal{T}}(X,Z)-d_{\mathcal{T}}\left(X_{0},Z\right).

Let
C(\mathcal{T}(S))
be the space of continuous functions on
\mathcal{T}(S)
endowed with the topology of locally uniformly convergence on
\mathcal{T}(S)
. Then the map

\displaystyle\Psi:\mathcal{T}(S)

\displaystyle\rightarrow

\displaystyle C(\mathcal{T}(S))

\displaystyle Z

\displaystyle\mapsto

\displaystyle\Psi_{Z}(\cdot)

is an embedding from
\mathcal{T}(S)
into
C(\mathcal{T}(S))
. The closure
\overline{\Psi(\mathcal{T}(S))}
of
\Psi(\mathcal{T}(S))
is compact, which is called the
horofunction compatification
of
\mathcal{T}(S)
with Teichmüller metric. The
horofunction boundary
is defined to be

\partial_{h}\mathcal{T}(S)=\overline{\Psi(\mathcal{T}(S))}-\Psi(\mathcal{T}(S))

and its elements are called
horofunctions
.

Remark 3.5
.

Note that here the definition of
\partial_{h}\mathcal{T}(S)
depends on the choice of the base point
X_{0}
. If one changes to an alternative base point
X_{1}
, then the assignment of the new function
\Psi_{Z}^{\prime}
is related to
\Psi_{Z}
by
\Psi_{Z}^{\prime}(\cdot)=\Psi_{Z}(\cdot)-\Psi_{Z}\left(X_{1}\right)
. Thus there is a natural identification between
\overline{\Psi(\mathcal{T}(S))}
and
\overline{\Psi^{\prime}(\mathcal{T}(S))}
,
and elements in
\partial_{h}\mathcal{T}(S)
are defined up to additive constants. We refer to
[
17
,
33
]
for more detail about horofunction boundary.

Liu and Su
[
17
]
proved that:

Theorem 3.6
.

The Gardiner-Masur compactification of
\mathcal{T}(S)
is homeomorphic to the horofunction compactification of
\left(\mathcal{T}(S),d_{\mathcal{T}}\right)
. For each
\xi\in\partial_{\mathrm{GM}}\mathcal{T}(S)
,
the corresponding horofunction is represented by

\Psi_{\xi}(X):=\log\sup_{\mu\in\mathcal{MF}}\frac{i(\xi,\mu)}{\sqrt{\operatorname{Ext}_{X}(\mu)}}-\log\sup_{\mu\in\mathcal{MF}}\frac{i(\xi,\mu)}{\sqrt{\operatorname{Ext}_{X_{0}}(\mu)}}.

4.
A pair of Busemann points that fill up the surface

In this section, we give a direct proof of Theorem
1.3
,
independent of Theorem
1.2
.

As an immediate corollary of Theorem
3.6
,
every Teichmüller geodesic ray has a unique limit on the Gardiner-Masur boundary.
The limit point of a geodesic ray is called
a
Busemann point
.

Let
\mathbf{R}_{q}:[0,\infty)\rightarrow\mathcal{T}(S)
be
the Teichmüller geodesic ray determined by
q\in Q^{1}(X)
.
Let
F_{v},F_{h}\in\mathcal{MF}
be the vertical and horizontal foliations of
q
.
Denote the limit of
\mathbf{R}_{q}

in
\partial_{\mathrm{GM}}\mathcal{T}(S)
by
\xi
.

With the above notations, we have (see Walsh
[
33
, Theorem 1]
)

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
\sum\limits_{i=1}^{k}F_{i}
be the ergodic decomposition
of
F_{v}
. Then the Busemann point is represented by

i(\xi,\mu)=\Big\{\sum_{i=1}^{k}\frac{i({F}_{i},\mu)^{2}}{i({F}_{i},{F}_{h})}\Big\}^{1/2},\ \mu\in\mathcal{MF}.

Proof of Theorem
1.3
.

Let
\xi,\eta\in\partial_{\mathrm{GM}}\mathcal{T}(S)
be a pair of Busemann points that fill up the surface.

First, we observe that any possible geodesic need to satisfy a system of equations.
Assume that a pair of measured foliations
{F}_{+},{F}_{-}
fill up the surface, and they determine a Teichmüller geodesic
\mathbf{G}_{q}

with

\lim_{t\to+\infty}\mathbf{G}_{q}(t)=\xi,\lim_{t\to-\infty}\mathbf{G}_{q}(t)=\eta.

Using Theorem
4.1
, we can write

i(\xi,\cdot)=\sqrt{\sum_{i=1}^{k}i(\mu_{i},\cdot)^{2}}\ \ \ \mathrm{and}\ \ \ i(\eta,\cdot)=\sqrt{\sum_{j=1}^{l}i(\nu_{j},\cdot)^{2}}.

Then the foliations
{F}_{+},{F}_{-}
have ergodic decompositions

{F}_{-}=\sum_{i=1}^{k}x_{i}\mu_{i}\ \ \ \mathrm{and}\ \ \ {F}_{+}=\sum_{j=1}^{l}y_{j}\nu_{j},

where the unknown coefficients
x_{i}>0,y_{j}>0
and they must satisfy the equations

i(\mu_{i},\cdot)^{2}=\frac{x_{i}i(\mu_{i},\cdot)^{2}}{i(\mu_{i},{F}_{+})}\ \ \ \mathrm{and}\ \ \ i(v_{j},\cdot)^{2}=\frac{y_{j}i(\nu_{j},\cdot)^{2}}{i(\nu_{j},{F}_{-})},

i.e.,

x_{i}={i(\mu_{i},{F}_{+})}\ \ \ \mathrm{and}\ \ \ y_{j}={i(\nu_{j},{F}_{-})}.

Thus we obtain a system of linear equations:

(5)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{lr}x_{i}=i(\mu_{i},{F}_{+})=\sum_{j=1}^{l}y_{j}i(\mu_{i},\nu_{j})=\sum_{j=1}^{l}y_{j}n_{ij}\\
\\
y_{j}=i(\nu_{j},{F}_{-})=\sum_{i=1}^{k}x_{i}i(\nu_{j},\mu_{i})=\sum_{i=1}^{k}x_{i}n_{ij}\end{array}\right.

where
1\leq i\leq k
,
1\leq j\leq l
and
n_{ij}=i(\mu_{i},\nu_{j})
.
Denote the matrix of intersection by

N=\left(n_{ij}\right)_{1\leq i\leq k,1\leq j\leq l}.

Now we
can reduce the construction of Teichmüller geodesic to
the problem of finding a vector
\mathbf{x}=(x_{1},\cdots,x_{k})
such that

\mathbf{x}=NN^{*}\ \mathbf{x},\ \mathrm{where}\ \mathbf{x}>0.

(All the entries
x_{i}
are positive.)

In general, the solution does not exist. However, note that the representation of
a Busemann point is defined up to multiplication by a positive constant.
Thus it remains to show that the matrix
T=NN^{*}
has a positive eigenvector. This is trivial when
k=1
. Thus we assume that
k\geq 2
.

To see this, we observe that the matrix
T
is non-negative (all the entries
of
T
are non-negative), symmetric and positive semi-definite. Thus all the eigenvalues of
T
are non-negative. Denote the maximal eigenvalue of
T

by
\lambda
. Then
\lambda>0
(otherwise,
T\equiv 0
).

Now we use the assumption that
F_{+}
and
F_{-}
fill up the surface to show that the matrix
T

is primitive, that is, some power of
T
is positive.

In the case that
{F}_{-}
and
{F}_{+}
are rational,
i.e., all the components are weighted simple closed curves,

T
is primitive if and only if the union of the support of
{F}_{-}
and
{F}_{+}

is a connected graph on the surface (see Thurston
[
31
, Section 6]

for more precise description).
Here, the connectedness is confirmed by filling condition.

In general, we can use a theorem of Lezhen-Masur
[
15
, Theorem C]
to approximate the foliations
\sum_{i=1}^{k}\mu_{i}

and
\sum_{j=1}^{l}\nu_{j}
by a pair of rational measured foliations

\alpha=\sum_{i=1}^{k}c_{i}\alpha_{i}
and
\beta=\sum_{j=1}^{l}d_{j}\beta_{j}
,
which satisfy the following properties:

•

\alpha
and
\beta
fill up the surface.

•

i(\mu_{i},\mu_{j})=0
if and only if
i(\alpha_{i},\beta_{j})=0
.

By a similar argument, we can show that the matrix
T
is primitive.

We can now proceed by using the Perron-Frobenius theorem to conclude that there exists some positive vector
\mathbf{x}>0

such that
T\mathbf{x}=\lambda\mathbf{x}
. Moreover,
\lambda
is the only eigenvalue of
T

with a non-negative eigenvector and
\lambda
is a simple eigenvalue.

By our discussion,
the existence of
\mathbf{x}
implies the existence of
\mathbf{G}_{q}
,
and the uniqueness of the geodesic follows from the uniqueness of
\mathbf{x}

(up to a positive constant). This completes the proof.
∎

We obtain an analogue of Theorem
2.2
.

Corollary 4.2
.

Let
\xi,\eta\in\partial_{\mathrm{GM}}\mathcal{T}(S)
be two Busemann points. Then there is a Teichmüller geodesic
\mathbf{G}(\cdot):\mathbb{R}\rightarrow\mathcal{T}(S)
such that

\lim_{t\rightarrow+\infty}\mathbf{G}(t)=\xi,\ \ \lim_{t\rightarrow-\infty}\mathbf{G}(t)=\eta

if and only if

\xi,\eta

fill up the surface.
Moreover, the geodesic is unique.

In this section we prove Theorem

.

(Azemar)
.

. For each

for
\xi

with
\mathbf{G}(0)=X_{0}
.
Moreover, let
\{X_{n}\}\subset\mathcal{T}(S)
be any sequence that converges to

.
Let
\mathbf{G}_{n}
be the Teichmüller geodesic associated to

\mathbf{G}_{n}
passing through

in the positive direction. Then

converges to some

is optimal for

.

Now we have a pair of boundary points

and

that fill up the surface.
The main idea to prove the existence in Theorem

is as following.
Given

, we have geodesics

\mathbf{G}_{1}
and
\mathbf{G}_{2}
, passing through

, which is optimal for
\xi

and
\eta
, respectively.
If the geodesics

and

\bullet

X_{0}
\mathbf{G}_{1}
\mathbf{G}_{2}

Figure 1.
Assume that

and

Unfortunately, if the point

5.1.
Upper and lower bounds for horofunctions.

By Lemma
5.1
, for each horofunction
\xi\in\partial_{\mathrm{GM}}\mathcal{T}(S)
,
we can associate with an optimal geodesic
\mathbf{G}=\mathbf{G}_{q}
with
\mathbf{G}(0)=X_{0}
. We shall denote the
limited Busemann function (in the positive direction) of
\mathbf{G}
by

B_{\xi}
.

The Busemann function
B_{\xi}
provides an upper bound
for the horofunction corresponding to
\xi
.
The next lemma was proved by Azemar
[
2
, Proposition 3.9]
. See also
[
16
, Lemma 3.10]
.

Lemma 5.2
.

For every
X\in\mathcal{T}(S)
, we have

\Psi_{\xi}(X)\leq B_{\xi}(X).

Let
F=F_{v}(q)
be the vertical measured foliation of
q
.
We consider the projective class of
F
as an element of the Gardiner-Masur boundary. The corresponding horofunction will be denoted by

\Psi_{F}
. By definition,

\Psi_{F}(X)=\log\sup_{\mu\in\mathcal{MF}}\frac{i({F},\mu)}{\sqrt{\operatorname{Ext}_{X}(\mu)}}-\log\sup_{\mu\in\mathcal{MF}}\frac{i({F},\mu)}{\sqrt{\operatorname{Ext}_{X_{0}}(\mu)}}.

Lemma 5.3
.

With the above notation, for all
X\in\mathcal{T}(S)
, we have

\Psi_{{F}}(X)\leq\Psi_{\xi}(X).

Proof.

Note that

\Psi_{\xi}(X)=\log\sup_{\mu\in\mathcal{MF}}\frac{i(\xi,\mu)}{\sqrt{\operatorname{Ext}_{X}(\mu)}}-\log\sup_{\mu\in\mathcal{MF}}\frac{i(\xi,\mu)}{\sqrt{\operatorname{Ext}_{X_{0}}(\mu)}}.

It is not hard to check that (see Remark
3.3
)

\log\sup_{\mu\in\mathcal{MF}}\frac{i(\xi,\mu)}{\sqrt{\operatorname{Ext}_{X_{0}}(\mu)}}=\log\sup_{\mu\in\mathcal{MF}}\frac{i(F,\mu)}{\sqrt{\operatorname{Ext}_{X_{0}}(\mu)}}=0.

Moreover, by Proposition
3.2
,
i({F},\mu)\leq i(\xi,\mu)
for all
\mu\in\mathcal{MF}
, which implies that

\Psi_{{F}}(X)\leq\Psi_{\xi}(X).

∎

The above two lemmas give sharp upper and lower bound of
\Psi_{\xi}
, in terms of the data associated to the optimal geodesic. We remark that
\Psi_{\xi}
,
\Psi_{{F}}

and
B_{\xi}
are comparable to each other, that is,

\frac{B_{\xi}}{C}\leq\Psi_{F}\leq\Psi_{\xi}\leq B_{\xi},

where

is a constant depends on
X_{0}
.
In the following, we shall denote by
\Psi_{F}\asymp\Psi_{\xi}\asymp B_{\xi}
.

5.2.
Level sets and sub-level sets

We need some properties on the level sets of horofunctions.

Definition 5.4

(Level set and sub-level set)
.

Let
f
be a function defined on
\mathcal{T}(S)
. For each
r\in\mathbb{R}
,
the level set and sub-level set of
f
are defined by

\mathrm{L}(f,r)=\{X\in\mathcal{T}(S)\ |\ f(X)=r\}

and

\mathrm{SL}(f,r)=\{X\in\mathcal{T}(S)\ |\ f(X)<r\}.

Assume that
Y\in\mathcal{T}(S)
and it is contained in a level set

\mathrm{L}(f,r)
, that is,
f(Y)=r
. Then we also let

\mathrm{L}(f,Y)=\{X\in\mathcal{T}(S)\ |\ f(X)=f(Y)=r\}

denotes the level set passing through
Y
.

When
f
is a horofunction function, level sets of
f
are called
horospheres
and sub-level sets of
f
are called
horoballs
.

In particular, let
F\in\mathcal{MF}
be a measured foliation.
The corresponding horofunction is given by

\Psi_{F}(X)=\frac{1}{2}\log\sup_{\mu\in\mathcal{MF}}\frac{i({F},\mu)^{2}}{\operatorname{Ext}_{X}(\mu)}-\frac{1}{2}\log\sup_{\mu\in\mathcal{MF}}\frac{i({F},\mu)^{2}}{\operatorname{Ext}_{X_{0}}(\mu)}.

We may assume that
\mathrm{Ext}_{X_{0}}({F})=1
. Then,
by an inequality of Gardiner-Masur
[
10
]
,

\Psi_{{F}}(X)=\frac{1}{2}\log\operatorname{Ext}_{X}({F}).

This implies that

\mathrm{L}(\Psi_{{F}},r)=\{X\in\mathcal{T}(S)\ |\ \mathrm{Ext}_{X}({F})=e^{2r}\}.

Definition 5.5
.

Let
\xi,\eta\in\partial_{h}\mathcal{T}(S)
be two horofunctions. We say that the level sets

\mathrm{L}(\Psi_{\xi},r)
and
\mathrm{L}(\Psi_{\eta},s)
are tangent to each other at
X\in\mathcal{T}(S)
if

•

X\in\mathrm{L}(\Psi_{\xi},r)\cap\mathrm{L}(\Psi_{\eta},s)
.

•

\mathrm{SL}(\Psi_{\xi},r)
and
\mathrm{SL}(\Psi_{\eta},s)
are disjoint.

We remark that the above definition doesn’t assume that
\mathrm{L}(\Psi_{\xi},r)\cap\mathrm{L}(\Psi_{\eta},s)
consists of a single point.

Lemma 5.6
.

Let
\xi
and
\eta
be a pair of horofunctions that fill up the surface.
For any given
s_{0}\in\mathbb{R}
, there exists some
t_{0}\in\mathbb{R}
(depending on
s_{0}
) such that
\mathrm{L}(\xi,s_{0})

and
\mathrm{L}(\eta,t_{0})
are tangent to each other.

Proof.

Let
\mu
and
\nu
be the vertical measured foliations
of the quadratic differentials that define the optimal geodesics
for
\xi
and
\eta
, respectively. By our assumption,

\mu
and
\nu
also fill up the surface.

By
[
30
, Lemma 3.10]
, for given
s_{0}
,
there is a constant
t_{0}
such that

(1)

for all
t<t_{0}
,
\mathrm{SL}(\Psi_{\nu},t)
is disjoint from
\mathrm{SL}(\Psi_{\mu},s_{0})
.

(2)

for all

, the intersection of
\overline{\mathrm{SL}(\Psi_{\nu},t)}
and
\overline{\mathrm{SL}(\Psi_{\mu},s_{0})}
is non-empty and compact.

Since
\Psi_{\xi}\asymp\Psi_{\mu}
and
\Psi_{\eta}\asymp\Psi_{\nu}
,
the same statement holds if we replace

and

by

and

As a result, there is some maximal number

\mathrm{L}(\Psi_{\xi},s_{0})

and

∎

5.3.
Busemann functions are
C^{1}
.

Note that for any measured foliation
\mathcal{F}
, the horofunction
\Psi_{\mathcal{F}}
is
C^{1}
.
This follows from the fact that extremal length functions are
C^{1}
.

Lemma 5.7
.

Let
B
be a Busemann function. Then
B
is
C^{1}
in the Teichmüller space.

Proof.

Consider any
X\in\mathcal{T}(S)
. Let
\mu
be a Beltrami differential on

X
, and let
X_{s\mu}
be the quasiconformal deformation of
X
with Beltrami differential

s\mu
, where
-\epsilon<s<\epsilon
.

We may assume that
B(X)=0
. There is a unique quadratic differential
q=q(X)\in Q^{1}(X)
such that
the geodesic ray induced by
q
converges to the Busemann point (function)
B
.
Take some
r>0
. Then the above geodesic intersects the geodesic sphere of radius
r
(centered at
X
) at two points, denoted by
X_{1}
and
X_{2}
. We assume that
B(X_{1})=-r,B(X_{2})=r
.

Define two auxiliary functions

A_{1}(s)=d_{\mathcal{T}}(X_{s\mu},X_{1})-r,

A_{2}(s)=-d_{\mathcal{T}}(X_{s\mu},X_{2})+r.

Note that
A_{1}(0)=A_{2}(0)=0
.

Since the Busemann function is
1
-Lipschitz
and
B(X_{1})=-r,B(X_{2})=r
, we have

\displaystyle B(X_{s\mu})+r

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle d_{\mathcal{T}}(X_{s\mu},X_{1})

\displaystyle-d_{\mathcal{T}}(X_{s\mu},X_{2})

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle B(X_{s\mu})-r.

Thus

A_{2}(s)\leq B(X_{s\mu})\leq A_{1}(s).

Note that
A_{1},A_{2}
are differentiable at
s=0
. In fact, there is a formula due to Earle
[
6
]
:

\displaystyle\lim_{s\to 0}\frac{A_{1}(s)}{s}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lim_{s\to 0}\frac{d(X_{s\mu},X_{1})}{s}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\operatorname{Re}\int_{X}\mu(-q)=-\operatorname{Re}\int_{X}\mu q.

Similarly,

\displaystyle\lim_{s\to 0}\frac{A_{2}(s)}{s}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\lim_{s\to 0}\frac{d(X_{s\mu},X_{2})}{s}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\operatorname{Re}\int_{X}\mu q.

As a result, we obtain

-\operatorname{Re}\int_{X}\mu q\leq\liminf_{s\to 0}\frac{B(X_{s\mu})}{s}\leq\limsup_{s\to 0}\frac{B(X_{s\mu})}{s}\leq-\operatorname{Re}\int_{X}\mu q.

This implies that

\lim_{s\to 0}\frac{B(X_{s\mu})}{s}=-\operatorname{Re}\int_{X}\mu q.

We have shown that the Busemann function is differentiable at every
X\in\mathcal{T}(S)
,
and the differential of
B
is given by the contangent vector
q=q(X)
.

We conclude the proof by showing that
q(X)
depends continuously on
X
.
In fact, for any sequence
X_{n}\in\mathcal{T}(S)
that converges to
X
,

q_{n}=q(X_{n})\in Q^{1}(X_{n})
is the quadratic differential which induces the geodesic ray starting from

X_{n}
and converging to the Busemann point
B
.
It was proved by Walsh
[
33
, Theorem 4]
that each
q_{n}
is modular equivalent to
q=q(X)
.

Assume that
\sum_{j}a^{n}_{j}{F}_{j}
is the ergodic decomposition of the vertical foliations
of
q_{n}
. Denote by
H(q_{n})
the horizontal foliations of
q_{n}
. Then, by definition,

\frac{a_{j}^{n}}{i({F}_{j},H(q_{n}))}=c_{j},

where
c_{j}>0
is a constant independent of
n
.
Up to a subsequence, we may assume that
q_{n}
converges to some
\phi\in Q^{1}(X)
.
Then

i({F}_{j},H(q_{n}))\to i({F}_{j},H(\phi)).

On the other hand, the vertical foliation of
\phi
is given by
\sum_{j}a_{j}{F}_{j}
,
where
a_{j}=\lim_{n\to\infty}a^{n}_{j}\geq 0
.
Note that
i({F}_{j},H(\phi))
is the length of
{F}_{j}
in the flat metric
|\phi|
, which must be positive. This implies that
a_{j}>0
.

As a result, we have shown that
\phi
is modular equivalent to each
q_{n}
, and then it is modular equivalent to

q
. Since on
X
the modular equivalence class in
Q^{1}(X)
is unique
[
33
, Theorem 5]
, we have
q=\phi
.

∎

Remark 5.8
.

We don’t know whether any horofunction is
C^{1}
in the Teichmüller space or not.

Theorem 5.9
.

Let

\mathbf{G}(t)
that is optimal for

. That is, along the geodesic

We first give a proof of Lemma

For any

Lemma A.1
.

Let

converge to a boundary point

. Assume that

Recall the following inequality, due to Minsky

Since
\sqrt{\operatorname{Ext}_{X_{0}}(\nu_{n})}=1

and

.

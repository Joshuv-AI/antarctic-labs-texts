Winding of geodesic rays chosen by a harmonic measure
Timothée Bénard

Abstract
We prove limit theorems for the homological winding of geodesic rays distributed via a harmonic measure on a Gromov hyperbolic space. We obtain applications to the inverse problem for the harmonic measure, and winding statistics for Patterson-Sullivan measures.

Introduction

1.1
The inverse problem for the harmonic measure

Let
\Omega
be a proper geodesic hyperbolic metric space endowed with a basepoint
o
, and
\Gamma
be a finitely generated subgroup of isometries of
\Omega
. Given a non-elementary probability measure
\mu
on
\Gamma
, it is known that for
\mu^{\otimes\mathbb{N}^{*}}
-almost every
b=(b_{i})_{i\geq 1}\in\Gamma^{\mathbb{N}^{*}}
, the sample path
(b_{1}\dots b_{n}.o)_{n\geq 0}
on
\Omega
converges to a point
\xi_{b}
in the Gromov boundary
\partial\Omega
. The distribution
\nu
of the exit point
\xi_{b}
is called the
harmonic measure
or the
Furstenberg measure
of generator
\mu
. It is the only probability measure on
\partial\Omega
satisfying the stationarity equation

\mu*\nu=\nu

The properties of regularity of
\nu
are very much studied
[
33
,
48
]

[
37
,
46
,
47
]

[
13
,
10
]
, and can be used to describe the asymptotic properties of
\mu
-sample paths on
\Omega

[
9
,
36
,
8
,
48
]
.

The motivation for this text is to address the following inverse problem for the harmonic measure:

How much information on
\mu
can be read in (the measure class of) its harmonic measure
\nu
on the boundary?

We will shed some light on this question by examining the homological winding of
\nu
-typical geodesic rays on
\Gamma\backslash\Omega
. As the statement of the main result requires a rather substantial introduction, we start with a few remarkable corollaries. The main result, as well as standard terminology concerning
\mu
, are presented in
Section

1.2
.

(I) Expectation and covariance of
\mu_{ab}
. In the context of random walks on hyperbolic groups, we see that
\nu
informs on the half lines spanned by the expectation and the covariance of
\mu
in the abelianization.

Corollary 1.1
.

Let
(\Gamma,d_{\Gamma})
be a word hyperbolic group equipped with a word metric,
\mu
a non-elementary measure on
\Gamma
, and
\nu
its harmonic measure on the Gromov boudary
\partial\Gamma
. Write
H_{\Gamma}
the torsion-free part of the abelianization of
\Gamma
, and
\mu_{ab}
the projection of
\mu
to
H_{\Gamma}
.

—

If
\mu
has finite first moment, then the measure class of
\nu
determines

\lambda_{\mu}^{-1}\mathbb{E}(\mu_{ab})

where
\lambda_{\mu}>0
is the rate of escape of the
\mu
-walk on
(\Gamma,d_{\Gamma})
.

—

If
\mu
has finite second moment and centered projection to
H_{\Gamma}
, then the measure class of
\nu
determines

\lambda_{\mu}^{-1}Cov(\mu_{ab})

In the above statement,
\mathbb{E}(\mu_{ab}),Cov(\mu_{ab})
are respectively the expectation and the covariance of
\mu_{ab}
in the real vector space spanned by the free abelian group
H_{\Gamma}
. These quantities would not make sense on the torsion part of
\Gamma/[\Gamma,\Gamma]
.

It is remarkable that these formulas link objects which have very different origins. The rate of escape
\lambda_{\mu}
arises from hyperbolicity, and depends strongly on the word distance
d_{\Gamma}
on
\Gamma
. In contrast,
\mu_{ab}
is independent from the metric and lives in a commutative setting.

1.1
is a direct consequence of
1.4
below and the formulas for the drift and the limit covariance.

(II) Non-centered walks are singular at infinity
.
Assume
\Omega
is the real hyperbolic space
\mathbb{H}^{N}
of dimension
N\geq 2
, and
\Gamma
is a convex cocompact non-elementary discrete subgroup of orientation preserving isometries. An established question is to understand the properties of measures
\mu
for which
\nu
is in the class of Patterson-Sullivan on
\partial\mathbb{H}^{N}
. Note that such measures do exist by a result of Connell-Muchnik
[
18
]
, and can even be chosen with finite exponential moment by results of Lalley and Li
[
44
,
49
]
. An active conjecture, commonly attributed to Kaimanovich-Le Prince, states that

cannot be finitely supported
[
39
,
42
,
43

. We prove here that

Corollary 1.2
.

Let
\Gamma\leq Isom^{+}(\mathbb{H}^{N})
be a convex cocompact discrete subgroup,
\mu
a non-elementary probability measure on
\Gamma
with finite first moment in a word metric. If the harmonic measure of
\mu
is in the class of Patterson-Sullivan, then the projection of
\mu
to the torsion-free part of
\Gamma/[\Gamma,\Gamma]
is centered.

1.2
is a direct consequence of
1.4
and
Lemma

5.2

(III) Walks with cusps are singular at infinity
.
Furstenberg’s discretization of the Brownian motion
[

,

implies that any (possibly non-uniform) lattice of

is the support of a probability measure

of

Let

1.2
Homological winding of
\nu
-typical rays

We address the inverse problem for the harmonic measure by reading information about
\mu
in the winding statistics of
\nu
-typical geodesic rays on
\Gamma\backslash\Omega
. More precisely, for every
\xi\in\partial\Omega
, call
r_{\xi}:\mathbb{R}^{+}\rightarrow\Omega
a geodesic ray from
o
to
\xi
on
\Omega
. Letting
\xi
vary with law
\nu
, we obtain a family of random segments
(r_{\xi}([0,t]))_{t\in\mathbb{R}^{+}}
indexed by the time parameter. We prove limit laws for the winding of
(r_{\xi}([0,t]))_{t\in\mathbb{R}^{+}}
on
\Gamma\backslash\Omega
. In those limit theorems, natural numerical quantities appear (drift, limit covariance) and have explicit formulas involving
\mu
. Those formulas only depend on
\nu
so they yield numerical invariants for all possible generators
\mu
of
\nu
. This strategy is motivated by the note of Guivarc’h-Le Jan
[
34
]
. It is also related to Gadre-Maher-Tiozzo
[
30
]
and Randecker-Tiozzo
[
53
]
, as we discuss below.

Homological projection

The winding number of a geodesic segment
[o,z]
on
\Gamma\backslash\Omega
will be formally incarnated by a vector
i(z)
where
i
is a
\Gamma
-equivariant map from
\Omega
to a vector space. Examples below justify the relation with homology and the term winding.

Definition
.

A measurable map
i:\Omega\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{d}
(
d\geq 1
) is
\Gamma
-equivariant
if there exists a morphism
\pi:\Gamma\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{d}
satisfying for all
\gamma\in\Gamma
,
x\in\Omega
,

i(\gamma.x)=\pi(\gamma)+i(x)

Note that
\pi
is unique.

We consider two natural levels of regularity on
i
:

—

We say
i
is
locally bounded
if for every ball
B
of
\Omega
, we have
\sup_{B}\|i\|<\infty
.

—

We say
i
is
quasi-Lipschitz
if there exists
R>0
such that for all
x,y\in\Omega
, we have

\|i(x)-i(y)\|\leq R\,d_{\Omega}(x,y)+R

Examples
.

1. (
Abelianization of hyperbolic group
). Let
\Gamma
be a word hyperbolic group, and
\pi:\Gamma\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{d}
any group morphism. Given any Cayley graph
\Omega
of
\Gamma
, we can extend
\pi
into
i:\Omega\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{d}
, say by
affine
interpolation. The regularity of
i
is always Lipschitz.

2. (
Homological winding on Gromov hyperbolic manifolds
). Let
\Omega
be a simply connected complete Riemannian manifold which is Gromov hyperbolic, and
\Gamma
a subgroup of isometries of
\Omega
. Given a basepoint
o\in\Omega
and a family
{\omega}_{1},\dots,{\omega}_{d}
of
\Gamma
-invariant closed
1
-forms on
\Omega
, we can define a

\Gamma
-equivariant map
i:\Omega\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{d}
by setting

i(z)=\left(\int_{[o,z]}{\omega}_{1}\,,\,\dots\,,\,\int_{[o,z]}{\omega}_{d}\right)

where
[o,z]
stands for any smooth path from
o
to
z
.

Observe that
i
is smooth, whence locally bounded. It can be quasi-Lipschitz, for instance if
\sup_{x\in\Omega,i\leq d}\|\omega_{i}\|_{x}<\infty
. However, if
\Gamma\backslash\Omega=M
is a manifold and some
\omega_{i}
has non zero integral on a loop
l
of
M
that may be shrinked arbitrarily by homotopy, then
i
cannot be quasi-Lipschitz.
Indeed, first note that
\omega_{i}
has the same integral
c\neq 0
on all the homotopic deformations of
l
in
M
, then for any
\varepsilon>0
, choose such a deformation of length at most
\varepsilon
, lift the path following this loop
\lfloor\varepsilon^{-1}\rfloor
times into a path in
\Omega
with endpoints
z,z^{\prime}
satisfying
d(z,z^{\prime})\leq 1
, and observe however that
\|i(z)-i(z^{\prime})\|\geq\lfloor\varepsilon^{-1}\rfloor c\neq 0
can be made arbitrarily large by choosing
\varepsilon
small, thus causing
i
to be non quasi-Lipschitz.
For instance, this occurs if
M
admits a cusp containing a loop with non trivial homology, as in the hyperbolic
3
-horned sphere (but not the once-punctured torus).

When
M=\Gamma\backslash\Omega
is a manifold, the vector
i(z)
is related to the
homological winding
of the path
[o,z]
projected to
M
. Indeed, assume that
\omega_{1},\dots,\omega_{d}
project to a basis of
H^{1}(M,\mathbb{R})
. This yields a representative
\omega
for any cohomology class in
H^{1}(M,\mathbb{R})
, that we see as a
\Gamma
-invariant closed
1
-form on
\Omega
. Using that the integral pairing
H^{1}(M,\mathbb{R})\times H_{1}(M,\mathbb{R})\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
is non-degenerate (De Rham Theorem), we then have

i:\Omega\rightarrow H_{1}(M,\mathbb{R}),\,\,z\mapsto\int_{[o,z]}

Here we use that we specified representatives for a basis of
H^{1}(M,\mathbb{R})
because the integral of an element of
H^{1}(M,\mathbb{R})
is not well defined on a arbitrary path in
M
or
\Omega
.
A priori,
i
depends on the choice of basepoint
o
and on the basis representatives
\omega_{i}
. However, any other choice will lead to a
\Gamma
-equivariant map
i^{\prime}
sharing the same morphism
\pi:\Gamma\rightarrow H_{1}(M,\mathbb{R})
. In this sense, the construction is canonical. The theorems below will not distinguish
i
from
i^{\prime}
.

Historical comments
. The homological winding for the geodesic flow on a negatively curved manifold has been thoroughly investigated from the point of view of
invariant measures
(say Liouville measure or Bowen-Margulis-Sullivan measures). For the compact case, see
[
57
,
54
,
21
]
. In presence of cusps, see
[
35
,
45
]
. Homological winding is also related to the problem of counting closed geodesics as in
[
41
]
. A more precise form of winding, involving
1
-forms which are only closed in a neighborhood of the cusps, is studied in
[
25
,
24
,
27
,
1
]
.

Homological winding for rays chosen with a
Furstenberg measure
has not been much studied. It is hinted at in the seminal note of Guivarc’h-Le Jan
[
34
]
. It is also related to the works of Gadre-Maher-Tiozzo
[
30
]
and Randecker-Tiozzo
[
53
]
who consider a typical geodesic ray on a hyperbolic manifold and show that the non-oriented winding around a given cusp satisfies a law of large numbers with finite mean. In this paper, we establish a wide range of limit theorems for the homological winding of Furstenberg-typical geodesic rays. Contrary to
[
30
,
53
]
which rely on Kingman’s subbaditive ergodic theorem, we compare directly geodesic rays with an abelian random walk (or martingale in the non-centered case) in the homology group. This new approach allows a precise control of the winding statistics and to free oneself from explicit geometrical computations, yielding with no extra cost the case of an arbitrary hyperbolic geodesic metric space. This level of generality, notably describing abelianized geodesic rays in the context of a word hyperbolic group, does not seem to have prior history.

Statistics of
\nu
-typical rays

We now present the main result of the paper, describing the winding statistics of geodesic rays on
\Gamma\backslash\Omega
chosen by a Furstenberg measure
\nu
of generator
\mu
supported by
\Gamma
. Several conditions on
\mu
will come up:

—

\mu
is
non-elementary
if the semigroup generated by its support contains two hyperbolic isometries with disjoint sets of fixed points.

—

\mu
has
finite
p
-th moment
if
\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma}d_{\Gamma}(e,\gamma)^{p}\mu(\gamma)<\infty
where
d_{\Gamma}
is any word distance on
\Gamma
.

—

\mu
has
finite exponential moment
if
\exists s>0
such that
\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma}e^{sd_{\Gamma}(e,\gamma)}\mu(\gamma)<\infty
.

We divide our result in two statements, assuming different levels of regularity for the projection.

1.4
assumes
i
to be quasi-Lipschitz. This situation occurs if
i
is the projection of a hyperbolic group to its abelianization, or the homological projection for some convex cocompact hyperbolic manifold. We prove a law of large numbers (LLN), and a central limit theorem (CLT), a law of the iterated logarithm (LIL), a principle a large deviations (PLD), and a gambler’s ruin estimate (GR).

Theorem 1.4

(Limit laws for geodesic rays I)
.

Let
\Omega
be a proper geodesic hyperbolic metric space
with a basepoint
, and
\Gamma
a finitely generated subgroup of isometries of
\Omega
. Fix a
quasi-Lipschitz

\Gamma
-equivariant map
i:\Omega\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{d}
. Let
\nu
be a Furstenberg measure on
\partial\Omega
for some non-elementary probability measure
\mu
on
\Gamma
.

—

(LLN)
If
\mu
has finite first moment, then there is a vector
e_{\nu}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
such that for
\nu
-almost every
\xi\in\partial\Gamma
,

\frac{i\circ r_{\xi}(t)}{t}\underset{t\to+\infty}{\longrightarrow}e_{\nu}

—

(CLT)
If
\mu
has finite second moment, then as
\xi
varies with law
\nu
, we have the convergence in distribution,

\frac{i\circ r_{\xi}(t)-te_{\nu}}{\sqrt{t}}\underset{t\to+\infty}{\overset{dist}{\longrightarrow}}\mathscr{N}(0,A_{\nu})

for some positive semidefinite symmetric matrix
A_{\nu}\in M_{d}(\mathbb{R})
.

—

(LIL)
If
\mu
has finite second moment, then for
\nu
-almost every
\xi\in\partial\Gamma
,

\left\{\text{ Accumulation points of }\left(\frac{i\circ r_{\xi}(t)-te_{\nu}}{\sqrt{2t\log\log t}}\right)_{t>2}\right\}=A^{1/2}_{\nu}(\overline{B}(0,1))

where
\overline{B}(0,1)=\{t\in\mathbb{R}^{d},\sum_{i}t_{i}^{2}\leq 1\}
.

—

(PLD)
If
\mu
has finite exponential moment, then for all
\alpha>0
, there exists
D,\delta>0
such that

\nu\{\xi:\|i\circ r_{\xi}(t)-te_{\nu}\|\geq\alpha t\}\leq De^{-\delta t}

—

(GR)
If
\mu
has finite exponential moment,
d=1
, and
A_{\nu}>0
, then for any
k,l>0
and any
(k_{s})_{s>0},(l_{s})_{s>0}\in\mathbb{R}^{\mathbb{R}_{>0}}
such that
k_{s}\sim sk
,
l_{s}\sim sl
, we have

\nu\left\{\xi:(i\circ r_{\xi}(t)-te_{\nu})_{t\geq 0}\text{ leaves $[-k_{s},l_{s}]$ via $(l_{s},+\infty)$}\right\}\,\underset{s\to+\infty}{\longrightarrow}\,\frac{k}{k+l}

Remarks
.

1) We recall that the notation
r_{\xi}
refers to a choice of geodesic ray from the basepoint of
\Omega
to
\xi\in\partial\Omega
. The theorem does not depend on the choice geodesics rays as any two geodesic rays on
\Omega
with same starting point and same exit point in the Gromov boundary remain at distance bounded by a constant depending only on
\Omega
.

2) In the second statement, we wrote
\mathscr{N}(0,A_{\nu})
for the (possibly degenerate) centered gaussian measure on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
of covariance matrix
A_{\nu}
.

3) The LLN and the LIL imply that the mean
e_{\nu}
and the covariance matrix
A_{\nu}
only depend on the measure class of
\nu
. We will also give explicit formulas in terms of
\mu
.

1.5
below is more general, for it only assumes
i
to be locally bounded. As observed earlier in Example 2., it is particularly relevant when
i
is the homological projection to a hyperbolic manifold with a cusp. We prove a weak law of large numbers (WLLN), and a central limit theorem (CLT) encapsulating that of
1.4
.

Theorem 1.5

(limit laws for geodesic rays II)
.

Let
\Omega
be a proper geodesic hyperbolic metric space
with a basepoint
, and
\Gamma
a finitely generated subgroup of isometries of
\Omega
. Fix a
locally bounded

\Gamma
-equivariant map
i:\Omega\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{d}
. Let
\nu
be a Furstenberg measure on
\partial\Omega
for some non-elementary probability measure
\mu
on
\Gamma
.

—

(WLLN)
If
\mu
has finite first moment, then as
\xi
varies with law
\nu
, we have the convergence in probability

\frac{i\circ r_{\xi}(t)}{t}\underset{t\to+\infty}{\overset{prob}{\longrightarrow}}e_{\nu}

for some vector
e_{\nu}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
.

—

(CLT)
If
\mu
has finite second moment, then as
\xi
varies with law
\nu
, we have the convergence in distribution,

\frac{i\circ r_{\xi}(t)-te_{\nu}}{\sqrt{t}}\underset{t\to+\infty}{\overset{dist}{\longrightarrow}}\mathscr{N}(0,A_{\nu})

for some positive semidefinite symmetric matrix
A_{\nu}\in M_{d}(\mathbb{R})
.

Mean and covariance matrix
. The mean
e_{\nu}
and the covariance matrix
A_{\nu}
appearing in Theorems
1.4
,
1.5
have explicit formulas in terms of the measure
\mu
. As
e_{\nu},A_{\nu}
only depend on
\nu
,
those formulas give numerical invariants on all possible
\mu
such that
\mu*\nu=\nu
.

To express
e_{\nu},A_{\nu}
we need introduce the
rate of escape

\lambda_{\mu}
of the
\mu
-sample paths on
\Omega
:

\lambda_{\mu}=\lim_{n\to+\infty}\frac{d_{\Omega}(o,b_{1}\dots b_{n}o)}{n}

where
d_{\Omega}
is the distance on
\Omega
, and
(b_{i})_{n\geq 1}\in\Gamma^{\mathbb{N}^{*}}
is
\mu^{\otimes\mathbb{N}^{*}}
-typical.

An application of Kingman’s subbaditive ergodic theorem to the probability space
(\Gamma^{\mathbb{N}^{*}},\mu^{\otimes\mathbb{N}^{*}})
endowed with the shift shows that if
\mu
has finite first moment, then
\lambda_{\mu}
is well defined and the convergence also holds in
L^{1}(\Gamma^{\mathbb{N}^{*}},\mu^{\otimes\mathbb{N}^{*}})
. If
\mu
is also non-elementary, then
\lambda_{\mu}
is non-zero, see for instance
[
8
, Proposition 3.3]
.

Letting
\mu_{ab}=\pi_{\star}\mu
, we prove

e_{\nu}=\lambda_{\mu}^{-1}\mathbb{E}(\mu_{ab})

To describe
A_{\nu}
, we introduce the Busemann boundary
X
of
(\Omega,d_{\Omega})
, the Busemann cocycle
\sigma:\Gamma\times X\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
. We prove in
Section

4.2
that uniformly in
x\in X
,

\lambda_{\mu}A_{\nu}=\lim_{n\to+\infty}\frac{1}{n}\int_{\Gamma}(\pi(\gamma)-\sigma(\gamma^{-1},x)e_{\nu})\,{{}^{t}(\pi(\gamma)-\sigma(\gamma^{-1},x)e_{\nu})}\,d\mu^{*n}(\gamma)

(1)

where
v\mapsto{{}^{t}v}
refers to the transposition map.
In particular, we get the complement information that

—

A_{\nu}
coincides with the covariance matrix
\lambda_{\mu}^{-1}Cov(\mu_{ab})
if
\mu_{ab}
is centered (but not in general).

—

A_{\nu}
is non-degenerate if
\langle\text{supp }\mu_{ab}\rangle_{\text{Vect}}=\mathbb{R}^{d}
and the restriction of the stable length function
l:\Gamma\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{+},\gamma\mapsto\lim_{n\to+\infty}\frac{1}{n}d_{\Omega}(o,\gamma^{n}.o)
to the semigroup generated by the support of
\mu
does not factor via
\pi
(
Lemma

4.1
)

The connection between the statistics of the winding
i\circ r_{\xi}
and that of the
\mu_{ab}
-random walk on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
arises from the strategy of the proof, which is to approximate
\nu
-typical geodesic rays on
\Omega
by
\mu
-sample paths then to project them to
\mathbb{R}^{d}
.
It came thus as a surprise, while working on this paper, to find that
A_{\nu}
may not be proportional to
Cov(\mu_{ab})
. Even worse:
it is possible that
Cov(\mu_{ab})
has rank
d-1
while
A_{\nu}
has rank
d
. This is the case if
\Gamma
is the free group
F_{2}=\langle u,v\rangle
,
\pi:F_{2}\rightarrow\mathbb{Z}^{2},u,v\mapsto(1,0),(0,1)
, and
\mu=\frac{1}{3}(\delta_{u}+\delta_{uv}+\delta_{uv^{-1}})
, (see
Lemma

4.2
). More explanations on this peculiarity are given in the strategy of proof below.

1.3
Winding of Patterson-Sullivan measures

Applying Theorems
1.4
,
1.5
to the context of Example 2 above, we obtain
winding statistics for the geodesic flow directed by a Furstenberg measure on a general manifold
M
with Gromov hyperbolic universal cover
. The particular case of Patterson-Sullivan measures for convex cocompact discrete subgroups yields the following. Recall that
\mathbb{H}^{N}
is the
N
-dimensional real hyperbolic space.

Corollary 1.6

(Homological winding for PS-measures)
.

Let
\Gamma\leq Isom^{+}(\mathbb{H}^{N})
be a convex cocompact torsion-free non-elementary discrete subgroup, and
m
be any probability measure on
\partial\mathbb{H}^{N}
that is absolutely continuous with respect to the class of Patterson-Sullivan of
\Gamma
.

Then the homological projection of
m
-typical geodesic rays to
H_{1}(\Gamma\backslash\mathbb{H}^{N},\mathbb{R})
has sublinear escape, satisfies a central limit theorem with non-degenerate covariance matrix, the law of the iterated logarithm, and the gambler’s ruin estimate (for any
1
-dimensional projection). It also satisfies the principle of large deviations if
m
has
L^{1+\varepsilon}
-Radon Nikodym derivatives with respect to Patterson-Sullivan measures.

The proof is given in
Section

5.1
. It combines
1.4
, the fact that PS-measures arise as Furstenberg measures for walks with finite exponential moment
[
49
]
, and -to establish sublinear escape- the fact that PS-measures represent the geodesic projection of the Bowen-Margulis-Sullivan measure, which is symmetric and flow-invariant on
\Gamma\backslash\mathbb{H}^{N}
.

The CLT (for
m
general), the (multi-dimensional) LIL, the GR, appear to be new, even in the most simple case of real hyperbolic surfaces, i.e. when
N=2
.

The LLN, the PLD, and the CLT when
m
is a Patterson-Sullivan measure, can be obtained by combining results already present in the literature. The seminal case of the CLT on a compact hyperbolic manifold follows from Sinai’s paper
[
57
]
. For the general case, the argument consists in coding the geodesic flow by a suspension flow above a shift of finite type

1.4
Strategy and organization of the paper

The guiding principle to prove Theorems

,
1.5
is simple. Let

denote the space of instructions. Typical

on

(for

by comparison with that of
\pi(b_{1}\dots b_{n})

\pi(b_{1}\dots b_{n})

around

. This approximation is not precise enough to prove second-order limit theorems. For this reason, we introduce the stopping times

leaves the ball of radius

, and we study how

, where we also need take into acount the recentering parameter. This forces to deal with martingales instead of an i.i.d. additive Markov chain on
\mathbb{R}^{d}

.

The goal of this section is to prove Corollaries

) (use the zero drift condition and the cocycle relation).

Let us check (i). We observe that
W_{n}(b)=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{1\leq k\leq n}M(b_{k-1}\dots b_{1}x_{0})
where
M:X\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{+}
is the bounded function defined by

\displaystyle M(y)=\int_{G}\sigma(g,y)^{2}d\mu(g)

(
y\in X
)

Choose
\nu
to be any ergodic
\mu
-stationary probability measure on
X
. In other terms, the dynamical system
(B\times X,\beta\otimes\nu,T^{X})

T^{X}:(b_{i})_{i\geq 1},x)\mapsto((b_{i})_{i\geq 2},b_{1}x)
is measure preserving ergodic
[

. By Birkhoff Ergodic Theorem, for all

in a set

\nu
-measure
1
,
\beta
-almost every

, one has
W_{n}(b)\rightarrow\nu(M)
where
\nu(M)
is the
\nu
-average of

\displaystyle\nu(M)=\int_{G\times X}\sigma(g,x)^{2}\,d\mu(g)d\nu(x)=:A

To get (i), choose
x_{0}
in
E
, and note that almost sure convergence implies convergence in
L^{1}(B,\beta)
for sequence of functions that are uniformly bounded.

For item (ii), observe the equality
W_{\varepsilon,n}(b)=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{1\leq k\leq n}u_{n}(b_{k-1}\dots b_{1}x_{0})
where

\displaystyle u_{n}(y)

\displaystyle=\int_{G}\sigma(g,y)^{2}{\bf 1}_{\{|\sigma(g,y)|>\varepsilon\sqrt{n}\}}\,d\mu(g)

(

\displaystyle\leq\int_{G}|g|^{2}{\bf 1}_{\{|g|>\varepsilon\sqrt{n}\}}\,d\mu(g)

By the

.
∎

We now upgrade
Lemma

A.3
to
A.1
.

Proof of
A.1
.

Let
x_{0}\in X
and
A
be as in
Lemma

A.3
. The uniform CLT follows directly from (

(\mu,X)
-control assumption on
\sigma
.

To check the convergence of variances, we must prove that

\displaystyle\frac{1}{n}\int_{G}\sigma(g,x)^{2}\,d\mu^{*n}(g)\longrightarrow A

uniformly in

. Using the cocycle relation and the zero drift assumption, we can rewrite the left-hand side

\displaystyle\frac{1}{n}\int_{G}\sigma(g,x)^{2}\,d\mu^{*n}(g)=\underbrace{\left(\frac{1}{n}\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\mu^{*k}*\delta_{x}\right)}_{\kappa_{n,x}}(M)

where

is the function defined previously. To conclude, we need to check the following lemma.

Lemma A.4
.

The sequence of empirical averages
(\kappa_{n,x}(M))_{n\geq 1}

A
uniformly in
x\in X
.

Proof of
Lemma

A.4
.

We first observe that for any
\mu
-stationary probability measure
\nu^{\prime}
on

X
, the
\nu^{\prime}
-average of
M
is equal to
A
. Indeed, one can assume
\nu^{\prime}
ergodic. The proof of
Lemma

A.3
shows that
\nu^{\prime}(M)
is the limit covariance in the CLT for
\sigma(.,x^{\prime}_{0})
where

is typical for

. But we also note that the
(\mu,X)
-control assumption on
\sigma
implies that a CLT for

implies the same CLT for

\nu^{\prime}
. This justifies that
\nu^{\prime}(M)=A
for all stationary probability measure

on

To conclude, we now assume by contradiction that
\kappa_{n,x}(M)
does not converge to
A
uniformly in

, i.e. we have some sequence
\kappa_{n,x_{n}}(M)
at distance

from

. But

is bounded continous and any accumulation point of

is stationary, whence a contradiction with the first paragraph.
∎

This concludes the proof of
A.1
.

∎

We now prove the CLT with stopping times.

Proof of
A.2
.

Let
R>0
be some (large) fixed parameter and set,
q_{n}=\lfloor n+R\sqrt{n}\lx@nobreakspace\rfloor
, then
\tau^{\prime}_{n}=\min(\tau_{n},q_{n})
. By the tightness assumption,
\tau^{\prime}_{n}=\tau_{n}
on a set of
\beta
-measure arbitrarily close to

as long as

is large enough. Hence, it is sufficient to check the above convergence for the stopping times
\tau^{\prime}_{n}
, or in other words, we may assume
\tau_{n}\leq q_{n}
. Now we can use the cocycle property to write

\displaystyle\frac{\sigma(b_{\tau_{n}(b)}\dots b_{1},x)}{\sqrt{n}}=\frac{\sigma(b_{q_{n}}\dots b_{1},x)}{\sqrt{n}}-\frac{\sigma(b_{q_{n}}\dots b_{\tau_{n}(b)+1},\,b_{\tau_{n}(b)}\dots b_{1}x)}{\sqrt{n}}

By the CLT in

, the first term on the right-hand side converges in law to

, uniformly in
x\in X
. We only need to check that the second term goes to
0
in probability and uniformly in

. Denote by
T:B\rightarrow B,(b_{i})_{i\geq 1},\mapsto(b_{i+1})_{i\geq 1}
the one-sided shift. Observing that

has law
\beta
, we just need to check that as
a,b
vary independently in
B
with law
\beta
, we have the convergence in probability

\frac{\sigma(a_{q_{n}-\tau_{n}(b)}\dots a_{1},\,y)}{\sqrt{n}}\,\overset{prob}{\longrightarrow}\,0

uniformly in

. This follows from the tightness condition on the
\tau_{n}
and the uniformlity of the CLT in

.

∎

A.2
Law of the iterated logarithm

We prove the law of the iterated logarithm for cocycles.

Proposition A.5
.

Assume
\mu
has finite second moment,
\sigma
has constant zero drift and is
(\mu,X)
-controlled. Let
A
be the limit covariance matrix in the CLT (
A.1
).
Then, for every
x\in X
, for
\beta
-almost every
b\in B
,

\left\{\text{ Accumulation points of }\left(\frac{\sigma(b_{n}\dots b_{1},x)}{\sqrt{2n\log\log n}}\right)_{n\geq 3}\right\}=A^{1/2}(\overline{B}(0,1))

where
\overline{B}(0,1)=\{t\in\mathbb{R}^{d},\sum_{i}t_{i}^{2}\leq 1\}
.

A similar LIL is presented in the book
[
9
]
under the assumption
\mu
has finite exponential moment. To reach the optimal generality of
\mu
having only finite second moment, we build on truncation techniques used by de Acosta
[
20
]
in his proof of the LIL for sum of i.i.d random variables.

The upper bound

We now prove the first half of
A.5
: the set of accumulation points of the renormalized cocycle is included in
A^{1/2}(\overline{B}(0,1))
. For this purpose, we may reduce the problem to dimension
1
.

Lemma A.6
.

We may assume without loss of generality that
d=1
and
A=1
.

Proof.

Fix
x\in X
. If the case
(d=1,A=1)
holds, then for every linear form
\varphi
on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, for
\beta
-almost every
b\in B
, the accumulation points of
\left(a_{n}^{-1}\varphi\circ\sigma(b_{n}\dots b_{1},x)\right)_{n\geq 3}
are included in
\varphi A^{1/2}(\overline{B}(0,1))
. Now use that the inclusion in
A^{1/2}(\overline{B}(0,1))
can be characterized via a countable set of linear forms on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
to conclude.
∎

From now on, we write
a_{n}=\sqrt{2n\log\log n}
.

Lemma A.7
.

Fix
r>1
arbitrary. It is enough to show that for all
x\in X
, for
\beta
-almost every
b\in B
,

\displaystyle\limsup\frac{1}{a_{n}}\sigma(b_{n}\dots b_{1},x)\leq r^{3}

(11)

Proof.

The upper bound in the LIL follows by applying (
11
) to
\sigma
and
-\sigma
, and by letting
r\to 1^{+}
along a countable sequence.
∎

Lemma A.8
.

Let
\delta>0
. To prove (
11
), we may assume

\displaystyle\int_{G}\sigma(g,x)^{2}\,d\mu(g)\leq 1+\delta

(12)

Proof.

Notice that for fixed
k\geq 1
, and
\beta
-almost every
b\in B
, the sequences

\displaystyle a_{n}^{-1}\sigma(b_{n}\dots b_{1},x)\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,a_{nk}^{-1}\sigma(b_{nk}\dots b_{1},x)

(
n\geq 1
)

have the same accumulation points. Hence, we can replace
(\sigma,\mu)
by
(\frac{1}{\sqrt{k}}\sigma,\mu^{*k})
without loss of generality. By the assumption
A=1
, and the uniform convergence of variances in
A.1
, there exists
k\geq 1
such that for all
x\in X
,

\int_{G}\frac{1}{k}\sigma(g,x)^{2}\,d\mu^{*k}(g)\leq 1+\delta

whence the result.
∎

We now introduce truncations of
\sigma
. These are crucial to deal with the weak moment condition on
\sigma
.

Let
\alpha>0
be some parameter to be specified later. For
j\geq 1
, set
\text{LL}j=\log\log j
. We define
X^{(j)}_{1}
as the
\alpha\sqrt{j/\text{LL}j}
-truncation of
\sigma
, and call
X^{(j)}
the corresponding recentered variable:

\displaystyle X^{(j)}_{1}(g,x)=\sigma(g,x){\bf 1}_{|g|\leq\alpha\sqrt{\frac{j}{\text{LL}j}}}\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,X^{(j)}(g,x)=X^{(j)}_{1}(g,x)-\mathbb{E}_{x}(X^{(j)}_{1})

where
\mathbb{E}_{x}
refers to the
\mu\otimes\delta_{x}
-average.

For
(b,x)\in B\times X
, we set

S_{n}(b,x)=\sum_{j=1}^{n}X^{(j)}(b_{j},b_{j-1}\dots b_{1}.x)

S_{n}(b,x)
corresponds to a version of
\sigma(b_{n}\dots b_{1},x)
where the
n
-increments arising from the cocycle relation have been truncated with increasing marge of error and (slightly) recentered. We first note that
S_{n}(b,x)
is a good approximation of
\sigma(b_{n}\dots b_{1},x)
, at least for the purpose of proving the LIL:

Lemma A.9
.

For every
\alpha>0
, for all
x\in X
,
\beta
-almost every

,

\displaystyle\sigma(b_{n}\dots b_{1},x)=S_{n}(b,x)+o(a_{n})

(13)

Proof.

Using the inequality
|\sigma(g,x)|\leq|g|
and the zero drift assumption, we can bound the difference

\displaystyle|\sigma(b_{n}\dots b_{1},x)-S_{n}(b,x)|\leq\sum_{j=1}^{n}|b_{j}|{\bf 1}_{|b_{j}|>\alpha\sqrt{\frac{j}{\text{LL}j}}}+\sum_{j=1}^{n}\mathbb{E}(|g|{\bf 1}_{|g|>\alpha\sqrt{\frac{j}{\text{LL}j}}})

(14)

The second moment assumption and
[
20
, Lemma 2.3]
imply that the right-hand side is
\beta
-almost surely of the form
o(a_{n})
.
∎

We now play on the variance parameter
\delta
and the truncation parameter
\alpha
to prove the upper bound (
11
).

Lemma A.10
.

We can choose the parameters
\delta,\alpha>0
so that for some
c>1
, all
n\geq 1
,

\sup_{x\in X}\mathbb{P}_{x}(S_{n}\geq ra_{n})=O(\exp(-c\text{LL}n))

Proof.

Using the relation
e^{s}\leq 1+s+\frac{s^{2}}{2}e^{|s|}
for any
s\in\mathbb{R}
and the fact that
X^{(j)}
has zero average, we get for
t>0
,
x\in X

\mathbb{E}_{x}(e^{tX^{(j)}})\leq 1+\frac{t^{2}}{2}\mathbb{E}_{x}(|{X^{(j)}}|^{2})\exp(t\alpha\frac{j}{\sqrt{\text{LL}j}})

The relation (
12
) allows to bound the second moment of
X^{(j)}
: for
j
large enough (say

),

\mathbb{E}_{x}(|{X^{(j)}}|^{2})\leq\int_{G}\sigma(g,x)^{2}\,d\mu(g)+\delta\leq 1+2\delta:=a^{2}

hence

\mathbb{E}_{x}(e^{tX^{(j)}})\leq\exp\left(\frac{t^{2}a^{2}}{2}\exp(t\alpha\frac{j}{\sqrt{\text{LL}j}})\right)

We can now bound the exponential moments of

. For

,
t>0
, using that

\mathbb{E}_{x}(e^{tX^{(j)}(b_{j},b_{j-1}\dots b_{1}x)}\,|\,\mathcal{B}_{j-1})\leq\sup_{y\in X}\mathbb{E}_{y}(e^{tX^{(j)}}),

we get

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{x}(e^{tS_{n}})

\displaystyle\leq\prod_{j=1}^{n}\sup_{y\in X}\mathbb{E}_{y}(e^{tX^{(j)}})

\displaystyle\leq\exp\left(\frac{t^{2}a^{2}}{2}\sum_{j=1}^{n}\exp(t\alpha\frac{j}{\sqrt{\text{LL}j}})\right)

So by Chebychev inequality

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}_{x}(S_{n}>ra_{n})\leq\exp(-tra_{n}+\frac{t^{2}a^{2}}{2}\sum_{j=1}^{n}\exp(t\alpha\frac{j}{\sqrt{\text{LL}j}}))

Setting

, a direct computation yields

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}_{x}(S_{n}>ra_{n})\leq\exp(-(r/a)^{2}(2-e^{ra^{-2}\alpha})\text{LL}n)

As
\delta,\alpha\to 0^{+}
, we have

. This concludes the proof.
∎

The rest of the proof is dedicated to showing that for
\delta,\alpha
as in
Lemma

A.10
, we have the upper bound (

).

Lemma A.11
.

For all
\varepsilon>0
, there exists
n_{0}\geq 0
such that for
n\geq n_{0}
,

,

\min_{0\leq k\leq n}\mathbb{P}_{x}(|S_{n}-S_{k}|\leq\varepsilon a_{n}\,|\,\mathcal{B}_{k})\geq 1/2

Proof.

Call

the right-hand side of (

|S_{n}-S_{k}|\leq|\sigma(b_{n}\dots b_{k+1},b_{k}\dots b_{1}x)|+2r_{n}(b)

As

almost-surely, we only need to check that for large

\min_{0\leq l\leq n}\beta(|\sigma(b_{l}\dots b_{1},y)|\leq\frac{\varepsilon}{2}a_{n})\geq 2/3

For

, this follows from the CLT assumption. For

, this follows from the

-domination assumption.
∎

Set

Lemma A.12
.

For all
c,\varepsilon>0
, for

,

,

\mathbb{P}_{x}(S_{n}^{*}\geq(c+\varepsilon)a_{n})\leq 2\mathbb{P}_{x}(|S_{n}|\geq ca_{n})

Proof.

It is enough to check that

\mathbb{P}_{x}(S_{n}\geq ca_{n}\,\,|\,\,S_{n}^{*}\geq(c+\varepsilon)a_{n})\geq\frac{1}{2}

This follows from
Lemma

A.11
.
∎

Lemma A.13
.

Choose
\delta,\alpha>0
as in
Lemma

A.10
. For all
x\in X
, for

-almost every

\displaystyle\limsup\frac{1}{a_{n}}S_{n}(b,x)\leq r^{3}

Proof.

Fix some

and set

. Writing

for

, we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}_{x}(S_{n}\geq r^{3}a_{n}\,\,i.o.\,)

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{P}_{x}(S^{*}_{k_{n}}\geq r^{3}a_{k_{n-1}}\,\,i.o.\,)

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{P}_{x}(S^{*}_{k_{n}}\geq r^{2}a_{k_{n}}\,\,i.o.\,)

where the second inequality uses that

A.3
Principle of large deviations

We show the principle of large deviations for cocycles. The proof is classic but also very short, so we include it for completeness.

Proposition A.14
.

Assume
\mu
has finite exponential moment:
\int_{G}e^{\alpha|g|}\,d\mu(g)<\infty
for some
\alpha>0
, and
\sigma
has constant zero drift. Then for all
\varepsilon>0
there exists
D,\delta>0
such that for any
n\geq 1
,
x\in X
,

\beta(b\,:\,\|\sigma(b_{n}\dots b_{1},x)\|>\varepsilon n)\leq De^{-\delta n}

Proof.

We can assume that
\sigma
is
\mathbb{R}
-valued. Let
t\in(0,\alpha)
be a parameter,
n\geq 1
,
x\in X
. Using the cocycle relation and Fubini Theorem,

\displaystyle\beta(b\,:\,\sigma(b_{n}\dots b_{1},x)>\varepsilon n)

\displaystyle\leq e^{-t\varepsilon n}\int_{B}e^{t\sigma(b_{n}\dots b_{1},x)}d\beta(b)

\displaystyle\leq e^{-t\varepsilon n}\left(\sup_{y\in X}\int_{G}e^{t\sigma(g,y)}d\mu(g)\right)^{n}

Using the bound
e^{s}\leq 1+s+s^{2}e^{|s|}
for any
s\in\mathbb{R}
, we get for
y\in X
,

\int_{G}e^{t\sigma(g,y)}d\mu(g)\leq 1+t^{2}C\leq\exp(t^{2}C)

where
C=\mathbb{E}_{\mu}(|g|^{2}e^{t|g|})
is finite. Combining inequalities, we get

\displaystyle\beta(b\,:\,\sigma(b_{n}\dots b_{1},x)>\varepsilon n)\leq\exp(-(\varepsilon-tC)tn)

Choosing
t<\varepsilon/C
, we have an exponential bound independent of
x
. Applying the argument to
-\sigma
, we ge the desired bound for
|\sigma(b_{n}\dots b_{1},x)|
.
∎

A.4
Gambler’s ruin

We show grambler’s ruin estimates for cocycles.

Proposition A.15
.

Assume
\mu
has finite exponential moment,
\sigma
has constant zero drift and is
(\mu,X)
-controlled,
d=1
, and the limit variance
A
in the CLT (
A.1
) is non-zero. Fix
k,l>0
and sequences
(k_{s})_{s\geq 0},(l_{s})_{s\geq 0}\in\mathbb{R}^{\mathbb{N}}
such that
k_{s}\sim sk
,
l_{s}\sim sl
. Then for every
x\in X
,

\beta\left\{b:(\sigma(b_{n}\dots b_{1},x))_{n\geq 1}\text{ meets $[l_{s},+\infty)$ before $(-\infty,-k_{s}]$}\right\}\,\underset{s\to+\infty}{\longrightarrow}\,\frac{k}{k+l}

For the proof, we fix
x\in X
and write
M_{n}(b)=\sigma(b_{n}\dots b_{1},x)
the associated martingale.

We will need to know that
M_{n}
does leave the window
(-k_{s},l_{s})
, and the exit time is bounded polynomially in
s
with high probability.

Lemma A.16
.

\beta\{b:(M_{n}(b))_{n\geq 1}\text{ leaves $(-k_{s},l_{s})$ for some $n\leq s^{3}$}\}\underset{s\to+\infty}{\longrightarrow}1

Proof.

\displaystyle\beta\{b:M_{\lfloor s^{3}\rfloor}(b)\in(-k_{s},l_{s})\}

\displaystyle=\beta\{b:s^{-3/2}M_{\lfloor s^{3}\rfloor}(b)\in(-s^{-3/2}k_{s},s^{-3/2}l_{s})\}

\displaystyle\underset{s\to+\infty}{\longrightarrow}0

by the CLT for
M_{n}
proven in
A.1
.
∎

Proof of
A.15
.

Set

\tau^{\prime}_{s}:=\inf\{n\geq 1,\,(M_{n}(b))_{n\geq 1}\text{ meets $[l_{s},+\infty)$ or $(-\infty,-k_{s}]$}\}

and
\tau_{s}:=\min(\lfloor s^{3}\rfloor,\tau^{\prime}_{s})
. By the optional sampling theorem applied to the zero-mean martingale
M_{n}
, we have

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}(M_{\tau_{s}})

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}(M_{\tau_{s}}{\bf 1}_{M_{\tau_{s}}\geq l_{s}})+\mathbb{E}(M_{\tau_{s}}{\bf 1}_{M_{\tau_{s}}\leq-k_{s}})+\mathbb{E}(M_{\tau_{s}}{\bf 1}_{M_{\tau_{s}}\in(-k_{s},l_{s})})

Let us estimate each term individually.

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}(M_{\tau_{s}}{\bf 1}_{M_{\tau_{s}}\geq l_{s}})=l_{s}\beta(M_{\tau_{s}}\geq l_{s})+\mathbb{E}((M_{\tau_{s}}-l_{s}){\bf 1}_{M_{\tau_{s}}\geq l_{s}})

We show the overshoot is negligible compared to
s
. We first notice the upper bound

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}((M_{\tau_{s}}-l_{s}){\bf 1}_{M_{\tau_{s}}\geq l_{s}})

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{E}(\sup_{k\leq s^{3}}|M_{k+1}-M_{k}|)

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{+}}\beta(\sup_{k\leq s^{3}}|M_{k+1}-M_{k}|>t)\,dt

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{+}}1-\beta(\sup_{k\leq s^{3}}|M_{k+1}-M_{k}|\leq t)\,dt

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{+}}1-\beta(E_{s^{3}}(t))\,dt

(15)

where for
r>0
, we write
E_{r}(t):=\{b\in B\,:\,\sup_{k\leq r}|M_{k+1}(b)-M_{k}(b)|\leq t\}
. Arguing conditionally and observing that the cocycle relation gives
M_{k+1}(b)-M_{k}(b)=\sigma(b_{k+1},b_{k}\dots b_{1}x)
, we get

\displaystyle\beta(E_{s^{3}}(t))=\beta(|M_{\lfloor s^{3}\rfloor+1}-M_{\lfloor s^{3}\rfloor}|\leq t\,|\,E_{s^{3}-1}(t))\beta(E_{s^{3}-1}(t))\geq(1-e^{-\alpha t})\beta(E_{s^{3}-1}(t))

for some
\alpha=\alpha(\mu,|.|)
coming from the exponential moment assumption on
\mu
. Iterating this inequality and plugging it into (
15
), we get

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}((M_{\tau_{s}}-l_{s}){\bf 1}_{M_{\tau_{s}}\geq l_{s}})\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{+}}1-(1-e^{-\alpha t})^{s^{3}}\,dt

Lemma

A.17
below tells us that
\sup_{s>0}\int_{[\varepsilon s,+\infty]}1-(1-e^{-\alpha t})^{s^{3}}\,dt<\infty
for any
\varepsilon>0
, whence we can finally control the overshoot by:

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}((M_{\tau_{s}}-l_{s}){\bf 1}_{M_{\tau_{s}}\geq l_{s}})=o(s)

To sum up, we proved that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}(M_{\tau_{s}}{\bf 1}_{M_{\tau_{s}}\geq l_{s}})=l_{s}\beta(M_{\tau_{s}}\geq l_{s})+o(s)

Using either the same line of argument, or
Lemma

A.16
, we get as well that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}(M_{\tau_{s}}{\bf 1}_{M_{\tau_{s}}\leq-k_{s}})=-k_{s}\beta(M_{\tau_{s}}\leq-k_{s})+o(s)

\displaystyle{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}|\mathbb{E}(M_{\tau_{s}}{\bf 1}_{M_{\tau_{s}}\in(-k_{s},l_{s})})|\leq\max(k_{s},l_{s})\beta(M_{\tau_{s}}\in(-k_{s},l_{s}))=o(s)}

In conclusion,

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}(M_{\tau_{s}})

\displaystyle=l_{s}\beta(M_{\tau_{s}}\geq l_{s})-k_{s}\beta(M_{\tau_{s}}\leq-k_{s})+o(s)

so as
s\to+\infty
, one has
\beta(M_{\tau_{s}}\geq l_{s})\rightarrow\frac{k}{k+l}
as desired.
The result for
\tau^{\prime}_{s}
follows because
\beta(\tau_{s}\neq\tau^{\prime}_{s})\to 0
as
s\to+\infty
by
Lemma

A.16
.

∎

We record the following estimate used in the proof of
A.15
above.

Lemma A.17
.

For all
\varepsilon>0
, one has

\sup_{s>0}\int_{[\varepsilon s,+\infty]}1-(1-e^{-\alpha t})^{s^{3}}\,dt<\infty.

Proof.

The integral over the larger domain
\int_{\mathbb{R}^{+}}1-(1-e^{-\alpha t})^{s^{3}}\,dt
is finite for every
s>0
and increases with
s
. Hence, we only need to check the bound for
s
large enough. Now rewrite uniformly for
t\in[\varepsilon s,+\infty)
,

(1-e^{-\alpha t})^{s^{3}}=\exp(s^{3}\log(1-e^{-\alpha t}))=\exp(s^{3}(e^{-\alpha t}+o(e^{-\alpha t})))=1+s^{3}e^{-\alpha t}+o(s^{3}e^{-\alpha t})

where
o(.)
goes to zero as
s\to+\infty
. The result then follows because

\sup_{s>0}\int_{[\varepsilon s,+\infty]}s^{3}e^{-\alpha t}\,dt<\infty.

∎

A.5
Exit estimates for drifting cocycles

For this last section, we replace the zero mean condition on
\sigma
by assuming a uniform lower bound on the drift: for all
x\in X
,

\displaystyle\int_{G}\sigma(g,x)d\mu(g)\geq 1

(16)

For
b\in B,x\in X
, we set

S_{n}(b,x)=\sigma(b_{n}\dots b_{1},x)

and for
s>0
, we write

\tau_{s}(b,x)=\inf\{n\geq 1,\,S_{n}(b,x)\geq s\}

Foster’s criterion (see
[
26
]
or
[
6
, Lemma 2.4]
) implies that for any
x
, the exit time
\tau_{s}
is finite, and even has bounded expectation
\mathbb{E}_{x}(\tau_{s})\leq s
. The goal of the section is to show that the overshoot
S_{\tau_{s}}-s
is essentially bounded. Assuming equality in (
16
), we also prove that
\tau_{s}
lands roughly in a window of size
\sqrt{s}
around
s
.
Throughout the section, given a formula
I(n)
representing an inequality involving (among others) a parameter
n\in\mathbb{N}^{*}
, we will lighten notation by writing

\mathbb{P}_{x}(I(\tau_{s}))=\beta(b\in B\,:\,I(\tau_{s}(b,x)))

Proposition A.18
.

1.

(Overshoot control)

Assume that
\sigma
satisfies (
16
).

\sup_{x\in X,s>0}\mathbb{P}_{x}(S_{\tau_{s}}>s+R)\underset{R\to+\infty}{\longrightarrow}0

2.

(Time control)
Assume
\mu
has finite second moment, and
\sigma
is
(\mu,X)
-controlled and has constant drift equal to
1
. Then we have:
\forall\varepsilon>0
,
\exists R>0
,

\sup_{x\in X,s>1}\mathbb{P}_{x}(|\tau_{s}-s|>R\sqrt{s})\leq\varepsilon

Remark
. The control of the overshoot implies that, given any large enough interval
I
in
\mathbb{R}^{+}
, the
\beta
-probability that the random sequence
(S_{n})_{n\geq 0}
intersects
I
is close to
1
. This can be interpreted as a weak form of renewal estimate for
(S_{n})_{n\geq 0}
.

Let us begin with the overshoot estimate.
Our first step is to use the cocycle property of
S_{n}
to reduce the overshoot control for
S_{\tau_{s}}
to that of a stronger control for
S_{\tau_{1}}
.

Lemma A.19
.

For
R>0
, we have

\sup_{x\in X,s>1}\mathbb{P}_{x}(S_{\tau_{s}}>s+R)\,\,\leq\,\,\sum_{n\geq 1}\sup_{y\in X}\mathbb{P}_{y}(S_{\tau_{1}}>n+R)

Proof.

Fix
R>0
. Let
n\geq 1
, and assume
H_{n}
is a constant such that

\displaystyle\sup_{x\in X,s\in(1,n]}\mathbb{P}_{x}(S_{\tau_{s}}>s+R)\leq H_{n}

(17)

It is enough to show that

\displaystyle\sup_{x\in X,s\in(1,n+1]}\mathbb{P}_{x}(S_{\tau_{s}}>s+R)\leq\sup_{y\in X}\mathbb{P}_{y}(S_{\tau_{1}}>n+R)+H_{n}

(18)

Let
x\in X
,
s\in(n,n+1]
. In the event that
S_{\tau_{s}}>s+R
, the sequence
(S_{k})_{k\geq 1}
does not meet the interval
[s,s+R]
, in particular we get

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}_{x}(S_{\tau_{s}}>s+R)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{P}_{x}(S_{\tau_{1}}>s+R)+\mathbb{P}_{x}(S_{\tau_{s}}>s+R;\,S_{\tau_{1}}<s)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{P}_{x}(S_{\tau_{1}}>s+R)

\displaystyle\,\,\,+\mathbb{P}_{x}(S_{\tau_{s}}>s+R\,|\,S_{\tau_{1}}<s)\mathbb{P}_{x}(S_{\tau_{1}}<s)

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{P}_{x}(S_{\tau_{1}}>n+R)+\mathbb{P}_{x}(S_{\tau_{s}}>s+R\,|\,S_{\tau_{1}}<s)

Let us bound above the second term. The cocycle property allows to write for
0\leq k\leq n

S_{n}(b,x)=S_{n-k}(T^{k}b,\,b_{k}\dots b_{1}x)+S_{k}(b,x)

where we recall that
T:B\rightarrow B,(b_{i})_{i\geq 1}\mapsto(b_{i+1})_{i\geq 1}
is the one-sided shift. Applying this to
k=\tau_{1}(b,x)
and
n=\tau_{s}(b,x)
, we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}_{x}(S_{\tau_{s}}>s+R\,\,|\,\,S_{\tau_{1}}<s)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{P}_{x}(S_{\tau_{s}-\tau_{1}}(T^{\tau_{1}}b,\,b_{\tau_{1}}\dots b_{1}x)>s+R-S_{\tau_{1}}\,\,|\,\,S_{\tau_{1}}<s)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{P}_{x}(S_{\tau_{s-S_{\tau_{1}}}}(T^{\tau_{1}}b,\,b_{\tau_{1}}\dots b_{1}x)>s+R-S_{\tau_{1}}\,\,|\,\,S_{\tau_{1}}<s)

As
S_{\tau_{1}}\geq 1
, the lower bound
s+R-S_{\tau_{1}}
is in the initial segment
[0,n+R]
so we may apply the hypothesis (
17
) to get

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}_{x}(S_{\tau_{s}}>s+R\,\,|\,\,S_{\tau_{1}}<s)\leq H_{n}

In sum, we have proven for any
x\in X
,
s\in(n,n+1]
,

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}_{x}(S_{\tau_{s}}>s+R)\leq\mathbb{P}_{x}(S_{\tau_{1}}>n+R)+H_{n}

and the bound (
18
) follows, whence the lemma.
∎

We now need to show that the sum
\sum_{n\geq 1}\sup_{x\in X}\mathbb{P}_{x}(S_{\tau_{1}}>n+R)
appearing in
Lemma

A.19
is small as long as
R
is large. We first check it is finite.

Lemma A.20
.

Assume that
\sigma
satisfies (
16
).
Then

\sum_{n\geq 1}\sup_{x\in X}\mathbb{P}_{x}(S_{\tau_{1}}>n)<\infty

Proof.

For
x\in X
,
n\geq 1
, using that
|\sigma(g,x)|\leq|g|
for all
g\in G
and that
\tau
is a stopping time, we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}_{x}(S_{\tau_{1}}>n)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k}\mathbb{P}_{x}(S_{k}>n,\,\,\tau_{1}=k)

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{k}\mathbb{P}_{x}(S_{k}-S_{k-1}>n-1,\,\,\tau_{1}=k)

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{k}\mathbb{P}_{x}(|b_{k}|>n-1,\,\,\tau_{1}=k)

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{k}\mathbb{P}_{x}(|b_{k}|>n-1,\,\,\tau_{1}\geq k)

\displaystyle=\mu(|g|>n-1)\sum_{k}\mathbb{P}_{x}(\tau_{1}\geq k)

\displaystyle=\mu(|g|>n-1)\mathbb{E}_{x}(\tau_{1})

Foster’s criterion (see
[
26
]
or
[
6
, Lemma 2.4]
) yields the bound
\mathbb{E}_{x}(\tau_{1})\leq 1
. It follows that

\sum_{n\geq 2}\sup_{x\in X}\mathbb{P}_{x}(S_{\tau_{1}}>n)\leq\mathbb{E}_{\mu}(|g|)

which finishes the proof.
∎

We now conclude the proof of the overshoot estimate in
A.18
.

Proof of
A.18
(overshoot control).

The estimate for the supremum involving only
s\in(0,1]
follows from
Lemma

A.20
. Indeed, for
R>1
,

\sup_{x\in X,s\in(0,1]}\mathbb{P}_{x}(S_{\tau_{s}}>s+R)\leq\sup_{x\in X}\mathbb{P}_{x}(S_{\tau_{1}}>R)\underset{R\to+\infty}{\longrightarrow}0

Now assume
s>1
. Using
Lemma

A.19
, we just need to check that

\sum_{n\geq 1}\sup_{x\in X}\mathbb{P}_{x}(S_{\tau_{1}}>n+R)\underset{R\to+\infty}{\longrightarrow}0

For
R=0
, this sum is finite by
Lemma

A.20
, and the result follows by dominated convergence (using again that for
n\geq 0
,
\sup_{x\in X}\mathbb{P}_{x}(S_{\tau_{1}}>n+R)
goes to
0
as
R
goes to
+\infty
by
Lemma

A.20
).
∎

We now deal with the time estimate in
A.18
.

Proof of
A.18
(time control).

Let
R>0
be a parameter to be specified below.

For
s>1
, set
q_{s}=\lfloor s+R\sqrt{s}\rfloor
. We first show that for large
R>0
, we have

\sup_{x\in X,s>1}\mathbb{P}_{x}(\tau_{s}>q_{s})\leq\varepsilon/2

Indeed, if
\tau_{s}(b,x)>q_{s}
, then by definition
S_{q_{s}}(b,x)<s
, so
|S_{q_{s}}(b,x)-q_{s}|>R\sqrt{s}
. The central limit theorem for
S_{n}-n
(
A.1
) guarantees this event only occurs on a set of measure at most
\varepsilon/2
as long as
R
is large enough (independently of
x
and
s>1
), hence the claim.

For
s>1
, set
q^{\prime}_{s}=\lfloor s-R\sqrt{s}\rfloor
. We now show that for large
R>0
, we have

\sup_{x\in X,s>1}\mathbb{P}_{x}(\tau_{s}\leq q^{\prime}_{s})\leq\varepsilon/2

Using the cocycle property and the Markov property, we see that for every
\alpha\in(0,1)
, there exists
R^{\prime}>0
such that for any
x\in X
,
s>1
,

\mathbb{P}_{x}(S_{q^{\prime}_{s}}\geq s-R^{\prime}\,|\,\tau_{s}\leq q^{\prime}_{s})\,\geq\,1-\sup_{y\in X,n\geq 0}\mathbb{P}_{y}(S_{n}\leq-R^{\prime})\geq 1-\alpha

where the inequality on the very right is justified by the central limit theorem for
S_{n}-n
(
A.1
) and the choice of a large enough
R^{\prime}
.

This leads to

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}_{x}(\tau_{s}\leq q^{\prime}_{s})

\displaystyle\leq(1-\alpha)^{-1}\mathbb{P}_{x}(S_{q^{\prime}_{s}}\geq s-R^{\prime})

\displaystyle\leq(1-\alpha)^{-1}\mathbb{P}_{x}(|S_{q^{\prime}_{s}}-q^{\prime}_{s}|\geq R\sqrt{s}-R^{\prime})

Choosing
\alpha
small enough,
R^{\prime}
accordingly, and applying the CLT for
S_{n}-n
from
A.1
bounds the right-hand side by
\varepsilon/2
for
R,s
large enough. We can increase
R
even more to deal with all
s>1
.

∎

Variation of GIT and Variation of Lagrangian Skeletons II: Quasi-Symmetric Case
Jesse Huang and Peng Zhou

Abstract
. Consider ( ℂ ∗ ) k (\mathbb{C}^{*})^{k} acting on ℂ N \mathbb{C}^{N} satisfying certain ’quasi-symmetric’ condition which produces a class of toric Calabi-Yau GIT quotient stacks. Using subcategories of Coh ⁡ ( [ ℂ N / ( ℂ ∗ ) k ] ) \Coh([\mathbb{C}^{N}/(\mathbb{C}^{*})^{k}]) generated by line bundles whose weights are inside certain zonotope called the ’magic window’, Halpern-Leistner and Sam give a combinatorial construction of equivalences between derived categories of coherent sheaves for various GIT quotients. We apply the coherent-constructible correspondence for toric varieties to the magic windows and obtain a non-characteristic deformation of Lagrangian skeletons in ℝ N − k \mathbb{R}^{N-k} parameterized by ℝ k \mathbb{R}^{k} , exhibiting derived equivalences between A-models of the various phases. Moreover, by translating the magic window zonotope in ℝ k \mathbb{R}^{k} , we obtain a universal skeleton over ℝ k × ℝ k \ 𝒟 \mathbb{R}^{k}\times\mathbb{R}^{k}\backslash\mathcal{D} for some fattening of hyperplane arrangements 𝒟 \mathcal{D} , and we show that the the universal skeleton induces a local system of categories over ℝ k × ℝ k \ 𝒟 \mathbb{R}^{k}\times\mathbb{R}^{k}\backslash\mathcal{D} . We also connect our results to the perverse schober structure identified by Špenko and Van den Bergh.



1.
Introduction

This is the second paper in a series
[
Zho20
]
where we construct deformation family of Lagrangian skeletons in the toric variation of GIT setting. Consider a complex torus
\mathbb{T}=(\mathbb{C}^{*})^{k}
acting on a vector space
V=\mathbb{C}^{N}
. There are possibly many GIT quotient stacks
[V/_{\theta}\mathbb{T}]
depending on a character
\theta
of
\mathbb{T}
. If the
\mathbb{T}
action preserves the standard volume form on
\mathbb{C}^{N}
, then the resulting quotients are (non-compact) toric Calabi-Yau stacks whose derived categories of coherent sheaves are equivalent, although the equivalences are not canonical. The lack of uniqueness of GIT quotients and their equivalences is a feature instead of a bug, and one expects a local system of categories over the complexified Kähler moduli space minus some discriminant, such that away from the discriminant, various asymptotic regions in the parameter space correspond to various GIT quotients; parallel transports along paths between the regions correspond to derived equivalences.

In general, it is difficult to construct such a local system of categories explicitly, since the discriminant can be complicated. However, under the ’quasi-symmetric’ condition (see Definition
1.1
below), the discriminant is an affine hyperplane arrangement, and we are able to construct the local system of categories using microlocal sheaf theory and Lagrangian skeletons.

The Coherent-Constructible-Correspondence (CCC) for toric varieties was initiated by Bondal
[
Bon06
]
and Fang-Liu-Treumann-Zaslow
[
FLTZ11
,
FLTZ12
]
, and finalized in the paper by Kuwagaki
[
Kuw16
]
using wrapped constructible sheaves developed in
[
Nad16
]
. These results relate A-model to B-model within each GIT chamber, and our work here interpolates the various A-models across different chambers.

1.1.
Toric GIT

Let

Definition 1.1

(Quasi-Symmetric Condition)
.

Let
\mathcal{L}=\{L_{1},\cdots,L_{m}\}

\mathbb{R}^{k}

Remark 1.2
.

This condition may seem unnatural at first sight, since there is no natural meaning of the lines

\prod_{L\in\mathcal{L}}\mathbb{Z}^{[N]_{L}}\xrightarrow{\pi_{\mathbb{Z}}}\prod_{L\in\mathcal{L}}\mathbb{Z}\xrightarrow{q}\mathbb{Z}^{k},

where we denote

[N]=\{1,\cdots,N\},\quad[N]_{L}=\{i\in[N]:\beta_{i}\in L\}.

Definition 1.3

(Window skeletons)
.

Let
\delta\in\mathbb{R}^{k}
be a shift parameter and
W_{\delta}
be a window. Then the window skeleton of
W_{\delta}
is defined as the union

\Lambda_{W_{\delta}}=\bigcup_{w\in\widetilde{W}_{\delta}}\Lambda_{w}\subset T^{*}\mathbb{R}^{N},

\widetilde{W}_{\delta}=\mu_{\mathbb{Z}}^{-1}(W_{\delta})
. We also define the non-equivariant window skeleton

\overline{\Lambda}_{W_{\delta}}=\Lambda_{W_{\delta}}/\mathsf{M}\subset T^{*}(\mathbb{R}^{N}/\mathsf{M})\simeq T^{*}(T^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{k}),

where
\mathsf{M}
acts on
\mathbb{R}^{N}

Recall
\mu_{\mathbb{R}}:\mathbb{R}^{N}\to\mathbb{R}^{k}

(\mathbb{C}^{*})^{k}
on
\mathbb{C}^{N}
. For any

, we define the restrction of
\Lambda_{W_{\delta}}
to the fiber

to be the symplectic reduction

\Lambda_{\delta,l}:=\Lambda_{W_{\delta}}|_{X_{l}}\subset T^{*}X_{l}\simeq T^{*}\mathbb{R}^{n},

where we use the notion of restricting a conical Lagrangian
\Lambda\subset T^{*}M
to the cotangent bundle
T^{*}S
of a smooth submanifold

\Lambda|_{S}:=(\Lambda\cap T^{*}M|_{S})/T^{*}_{S}M\subset(T^{*}M|_{S})/T_{S}^{*}M\simeq T^{*}S.

Similarly, we define the non-equivariant version of fiber skeleton

\overline{\Lambda}_{\delta,l}=\Lambda_{\delta,l}/\mathsf{M}\subset T^{*}(X_{l}/\mathsf{M})\simeq T^{*}T^{n}.

1.3.
Constructible sheaf of categories

We first present our result about
\Lambda_{W_{\delta}}
for a fixed
\delta
.

We first state a result saying
\Lambda_{W_{\delta}}
over various asymptotic region are the mirror skeletons to the various GIT quotients. Recall that
\mathbb{R}^{k}
admits stratification according to how the fiber of
\mu_{\mathbb{R}}
intersects with the positive quadrant
\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}^{N}
. This stratification fits into a GKZ fan (secondary fan), where each maximal cone’s interior
C
is called a GKZ chamber, and labels the GIT quotient
X_{C}
. In Section
3
, we prove that for any
\delta\in\mathbb{R}^{k}
and chamber
C
, there is an stable region
C_{\nabla_{\delta}}
in the direction
C
,
such that
\Lambda_{W_{\delta}}
restricted over
C_{\nabla_{\delta}}
is the mirror to the GIT quotient
X_{C}
.

Theorem 1.4

(Theorem
3.4
)
.

For any
\delta\in\mathbb{R}^{k}
and any GKZ chamber
C
, if we define

C_{\nabla_{\delta}}=\bigcap_{p\in\nabla_{\delta}}p+C

and the skeleton for GIT quotient in chamber
C
,

\Lambda_{C}=\bigcup_{I\in\mathfrak{I}_{C}}(\mathbb{Z}^{N}+\mathbb{R}^{I})\times(-(\mathbb{R}^{\vee}_{\geq 0})^{I^{c}})\subset T^{*}\mathbb{R}^{N},\quad\mathfrak{I}_{C}=\{I\subset[N]\mid\mu_{\mathbb{R}}^{-1}(C)\cap\mathbb{R}_{>0}^{I}\neq\emptyset\},

then we have

\Lambda_{W_{\delta}}=\Lambda_{C},\quad\text{ inside }\mu_{\mathbb{R}}^{-1}(C_{\nabla_{\delta}}).

In particular, if
l\in C_{\nabla_{\delta}}\cap\mathbb{Z}^{k}
, then
\overline{\Lambda}_{\delta,l}\subset T^{*}T^{n}
is the (non-equivariant) FLTZ skeleton mirror to the GIT quotient
\mathbb{C}^{N}//_{l}(\mathbb{C}^{*})^{k}
.

In fact, Theorem
3.4
holds for general toric GIT, without toric CY or quasi-symmetric condition.

Next, we prove a result showing that, for
\delta
generic,
\Lambda_{W_{\delta}}
induces an equivalence among all skeletons
\Lambda_{\delta,l}
for
l\in\mathbb{R}^{k}
. We recall the notion of non-characteristic deformation of Lagrangian. For any smooth manifold
M
and conical Lagrangian
\Lambda\subset T^{*}M
, we follow the notation of Nadler
[
Nad16
]
and consider the cocomplete dg derived category of sheaves on
M
that are cohomologically constructible and have singular support contained in
\Lambda
, and denote it by
Sh^{\diamondsuit}(M,\Lambda)
. The compact objects in
Sh^{\diamondsuit}(M,\Lambda)
are called wrapped constructible sheaves, and constitute the full subcategory
Sh^{w}(M,\Lambda)
. It is proven recently
[
GPS18
,
NS20
]
that the wrapped constructible sheaves (and more generally wrapped microlocal sheaves) and the corresponding wrapped Fukaya categories
\mathcal{W}(T^{*}M,\Lambda)
are equivalent. We follow
[
Nad15
]
, and say a family of Lagrangian skeletons
\{\Lambda_{t}\subset T^{*}M\}
parametrized by
t
is a non-characteristic deformation family, if the corresponding categories
Sh^{\diamondsuit}(M,\Lambda_{t})
are independent of
t
.

Theorem 1.5

(Theorem
7.1
)
.

If
\delta\in\mathbb{R}^{k}
is generic, then
\{\Lambda_{\delta,l}\}_{l\in\mathbb{R}^{k}}
and
\{\overline{\Lambda}_{\delta,l}\}_{l\in\mathbb{R}^{k}}
are non-characteristic deformations of Lagrangian skeletons parametrized by
l\in\mathbb{R}^{k}
.
More precisely, for any
l\in\mathbb{R}^{k}
the restriction functors are equivalence of categories

\rho_{\delta,l}:Sh^{\diamondsuit}(\mathbb{R}^{N},\Lambda_{W_{\delta}})\xrightarrow{\sim}Sh^{\diamondsuit}(\mathbb{R}^{n},\Lambda_{\delta,l}),\quad\overline{\rho}_{\delta,l}:Sh^{\diamondsuit}(T^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{k},\overline{\Lambda}_{W_{\delta}})\xrightarrow{\sim}Sh^{\diamondsuit}(T^{n},\overline{\Lambda}_{\delta,l}).

Hence given a choice of the window
W_{\delta}
for
\delta
generic, there is a simultaneous non-characteristic interpolation among the universal FLTZ skeletons
\Lambda_{C}
in all chambers. Consequently, the A-model categories are all equivalent through parallel transport.

Our next result is about what happens when
\delta
is non-generic. On the B-side, the story is explained by

]
and
[

]
, and we have the ’window inclusion functors’ and their left and right adjoints. For each
\delta\in\mathbb{R}^{k}
, we have
\nabla_{\delta},W_{\delta}
as before, and we have the window category
\mathcal{B}_{\delta}
(denoted as

generated by line bundles

,

\mathcal{B}_{\delta}:=\langle\{\mathcal{L}_{w}\mid w\in W_{\delta}\}\rangle.

(1.5)

If

Remark 1.7
.

We do not have a good way to prove that

It would be interesting to develope a general method to check this, even just for piecewise linear conical Lagrangians in
\mathbb{R}^{2n}

For any
\Lambda\subset T^{*}M
conical Lagrangian, we define the contact sphere bundle

\Lambda^{\infty}=(\Lambda\backslash T_{M}^{*}M)/\mathbb{R}_{>0}
. We define the jumping loci
\mathcal{S}(\mathcal{C}_{\delta})
for
\mathcal{C}_{\delta}
as the projection image of the Legendrian

to the base
\mathbb{R}^{k}
. By the above theorem,

\mathcal{S}(\mathcal{C}_{\delta})=\bigcup_{F_{\delta,\sigma}:F_{\delta,\sigma}\cap\mathbb{Z}^{k}\neq\emptyset}\Aff(F_{\delta,\sigma}).

If

is not in the jumping loci
\mathcal{S}(\mathcal{C}_{\delta})
, we say

is a regular value for
\mathcal{C}_{\delta}
.

With the knowledge of singular support, we may deform a convex open set

without changing the categorical output
\mathcal{C}_{\delta}(U)
as along as the exterior unit conormal of
U
is disjoint from
SS^{\infty}(\mathcal{C}_{\delta})
. The following corolloary is immediate, noting the connected components in the complement of the jumping loci are convex hence contractible.

Corollary 1.8
.

If
l_{1},l_{2}\in\mathbb{R}^{k}
are in the same connected component in the complement of the jumping loci for
\mathcal{C}_{\delta}
, then we have a canonical isomorphism of stalks
\mathcal{C}_{\delta}|_{l_{1}}\simeq\mathcal{C}_{\delta}|_{l_{2}}
.

More usefully, we have the following result identifying the stalk of
\mathcal{C}_{\delta}|_{l}
at a regular value

with the constructible sheaf category on the fiber over
l
.

Proposition 1.9
.

If
l
is a regular value of
C_{\delta}
, then we have

\mathcal{C}_{\delta}|_{l}\simeq Sh^{\diamondsuit}(\mathbb{R}^{n},\Lambda_{\delta,l}).

Similar statement holds if we change
Sh^{\diamondsuit}
to
Sh^{w}
and/or
\Lambda_{\delta,l}
with
\overline{\Lambda}_{\delta,l}
.

Proof.

By Proposition
4.23
and the triviality of
SS_{Hom}^{L}(\mathcal{C}_{\delta})
, we may extend a sheaf in
Sh^{\diamondsuit}(\mu_{\mathbb{R}}^{-1}(l),\Lambda_{\delta,l})
to a tubular neighborhood of
\mu_{\mathbb{R}}^{-1}(l)
, e.g.
\mu_{\mathbb{R}}^{-1}(B_{x})
. By regularity of

, the restriction from
\mu_{\mathbb{R}}^{-1}(B_{x})
back to
\mu_{\mathbb{R}}^{-1}(l)
is non-characteristic with respect to

, hence the extension is unique, and we have proven that the restriction from

1.4.
Window Skeleton and Perverse Schober

Perverse schober
[
KS14
]
on a disk is a nice combinatorial book-keeping tool to organize several spherical functors, and to localize the categorical computation to small open sets. More generally, perverse schobers with complex hyperplane arrangements
[
KS16
]
can be defined.
One area of motivation and appliation is the Fukaya category with coefficients, the idea of which has been adopted by
[
Nad19
,
Nad18
]
for example.

The connection with GIT has been made in
[
Don19
,
DK19
]
, and the connection with quasi-symmetric window categories has been explained in

give rises to a perverse schober.

By Theorem
1.14
, we automatically have a skeletal realization of the perverse schober, using Spenko and Van den Bergh’s result.

A slightly different point of view is to recognize

\mathcal{B}=\mathbb{R}^{k}_{\delta}\times\mathbb{R}^{k}_{l},\quad\mathcal{X}=\mathbb{R}^{k}_{\delta}\times\mathbb{R}_{x}^{N}

\widetilde{\mu}:\mathcal{X}\to\mathcal{B},\quad(\delta,x)\mapsto(\delta,\mu(x)).

For any
\delta\in\mathbb{R}^{k}
, let

\mathcal{D}_{\nabla_{\delta}}=\bigcup\{\nabla_{\delta}+TF\mid\text{F is a facet of $\nabla_{\delta}$ containing lattice point}\}

(1.11)

where

F

\mathbb{R}^{k}

\delta
,
\partial\nabla_{\delta}
does not contain any lattice point, and
\mathcal{D}_{\nabla_{\delta}}=\emptyset
.
Consider all
\delta

\mathcal{D}=\bigcup_{\delta\in\mathbb{R}^{k}}\{\delta\}\times\mathcal{D}_{\nabla_{\delta}}.

Define

\mathcal{B}^{o}=\mathcal{B}\backslash\mathcal{D},\quad\mathcal{X}^{o}=\widetilde{\mu}^{-1}(\mathcal{B}^{o}).

Proposition 1.16
.

There is a universal skeleton
\Lambda
define over
\mathcal{X}^{o}

\Lambda|_{(\delta,l)}=\Lambda_{\delta,l}.

Proof.

For any
(\delta,l)\in\mathcal{B}^{o}
, there is a small product neighborhood

1.6.2.
Windows and Quasi-Symmetric GIT

We first recall the notion of a B-side window subcategory in GIT problem. Rouhgly speaking, if
Y
is a GIT quotient stack of
Y=[X^{ss}_{\mathcal{L}}/G]
determined by some
G
-ample equivariant line bundle
\mathcal{L}
, then one has a restriction functor on the derived category of coherent sheaves
r:Coh[X/G]\to Coh[X^{ss}_{\mathcal{L}}/G]
. A window subcategory for
Y
is the image of a fully faithful embedding
Coh[X^{ss}_{\mathcal{L}}/G]
back into
Coh[X/G]
. In the case of
\mathbb{C}^{*}
-action on linear space
\mathbb{C}^{n}
with weights
(a_{1},\cdots,a_{n})
, such that
\eta=\sum_{i:a_{i}>0}a_{i}=\sum_{i:a_{i}<0}|a_{i}|
, the subcategory in
[\mathbb{C}^{n}/\mathbb{C}^{*}]
is given by
[a,a+\eta]\cap\mathbb{Z}
for some
a\in\mathbb{R}\backslash\mathbb{Z}
, hence the terminology of ’window’.

The notion of window, or ’grade restriction rules’ was first discovered by physicists
[
HHP08
]
, then imported to math by the seminal work of Ed Segal
[
Seg11
]
. The work in
[
BFK19
]
and
[
HL15
]
then shows that window subcategories exist for a general GIT setup. There the window subcategories are constructed by first stratifying the unstable locus by the Kempf-Ness (KN) strata ordered Morse theoretically via the Hilbert-Mumford function and then eliminating the obstruction to extend coherent sheaves on
X^{ss}
to the KN strata iteratively. A choice is made in each extension step, and the final result of the composition of embeddings depends on the many choices made thus can be complicated, even for a general toric Calabi-Yau setting where
(\mathbb{C}^{*})^{k}
acts on
\mathbb{C}^{N}
preserving the volume form.

The quasi-symmetric case is much simpler and sufficiently rich to be interesting. It is simple because one can choose the window to be defined by a polytope; it is interesting since it includes the case of symplectic representation
T^{*}V
of a reductive group
G
, and the adjoint representation
\mathfrak{g}
of
G
is also quasi-symmetric. The quasi-symmetric case is studied by
[
ŠVdB17
,
ŠVdB19
]
and
[
HLS20
]
. For quasi-symmetric torus actions, a mirror calculation to the window equivalence in
[
HLS20
]
is carried out by the first author in her Ph.D thesis
[
Hua21
]
using periodic FLTZ skeletons.

Another important feature of quasi-symmetry pertains to mirror symmetry expectations. It was shown in
[
Kit17
]
that the complex moduli space of the A-model mirror of a quasi-symmetric GIT quotient admits a combinatorial description as the complement of the complexified
\mathbb{Z}^{k}
-periodic hyperplane arrangement parallel to facets of
\nabla
, which agrees topologically with
\mathcal{B}^{o}
where the universal skeleton
\Lambda
lives over.

4.

In this section, we setup some notations and establish some basic properties of constructible sheaves in Euclidean space adapted to the coordinate hyperplane stratifications.

Let

of

and

Then

Let

.
We also define the sheaf categories

Let

We define the cochain complex of a partially ordered set

C^{0}(P)\to C^{1}(P)\to\cdots

If

5.
Local Window Skeleton and the Restriction Functor

In this section, we will assume quasi-symmetry.

Recall that the zonotope
\nabla
is a symmetric polytope
\nabla=(1/2)\mu([0,1]^{N})
. For a shift parameter
\delta\in\mathbb{R}^{k}
, we have the shifted zonotope
\nabla_{\delta}=\delta+\nabla\subset\mathbb{R}^{k}
and the shifted window

\delta\mapsto W_{\delta}
is locally constant and induces a stratification on

. We want to describe how the skeleton
\Lambda_{W_{\delta}}
changes as
\delta
varies.

5.1.
Window skeleton near a point

Let
\Lambda=\Lambda_{W_{\delta}}
be some window skeleton, or any skeleton adapted to the grid stratification of
\mathbb{R}^{N}
.

For any
x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
, we consider the specialization
\Lambda_{x}\subset T^{*}(T_{x}\mathbb{R}^{N})
of
\Lambda
at
x
. The specialization
\Lambda_{x}
can be obtained in the following way: take a small ball
B_{\epsilon}(x)
around
x
so that
B_{\epsilon}(x)
only intersects with strata that contains
x
in the closure, then restrict
\Lambda
to
B_{\epsilon}(x)
, finally identify
B_{\epsilon}(x)
with
B_{\epsilon}(0)
in
\mathbb{R}^{N}
and extrapolate
\Lambda
from the ball to
\mathbb{R}^{N}
as a bi-conical Lagrangian. Here we identified
T_{x}\mathbb{R}^{N}
with
\mathbb{R}^{N}
in the obvious way.

For any
x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
, let
\nbhd(x)
denote the union of strata that contain
x
in the closure. One can see
\nbhd(x)
is indeed an open neighborhood of
x
. The specialization
\Lambda_{x}
determines the restriction
\Lambda|_{\nbhd(x)}
.
Let
\widetilde{v}\in\mathbb{Z}^{N}
and

\Lambda_{W_{\delta},\widetilde{v}}
only depends on

, since
\Lambda_{W_{\delta}}

\Lambda_{W_{\delta},v}=\Lambda_{W_{\delta},\widetilde{v}}

5.2.
Intermediate Level

It turns out many discussions would simplify if we consider the factorization of
\mu

\mathbb{R}^{N}\xrightarrow{\pi}\mathbb{R}^{m}\xrightarrow{q}\mathbb{R}^{k},

and work with the map
\pi
first.

Let
E_{1},\cdots,E_{m}
be basis of
\mathbb{R}^{m}
. Define

\mathbb{B}=(1/2)\pi([0,1]^{N}).

For any closed convex polytope

We define the Lagrangian skeleton in

as

\Lambda_{\widehat{W}_{K}}=\bigcup_{w\in\widetilde{W}_{K}}\Lambda_{w}.

Remark 5.1
.

If we let
K=q^{-1}(\delta)
, then
\Lambda_{\widehat{W}_{K}}=\Lambda_{W_{\delta}}
defined before. For now, we allow
K
to be any closed convex polytope to keep the discussion more general and independent of the map
q
.

Let
\widehat{v}\in\mathbb{Z}^{m}
and

be a closed convex polytope.
We first give the combinatorial data that describe the skeleton
\Lambda_{\widehat{W}_{K},\widehat{v}}
. Define an index set

\mathfrak{I}(\widehat{W}_{K},\widehat{v})=\{I\subset[N]\mid\Lambda_{I}\subset\Lambda_{\widehat{W}_{K},\widehat{v}}\}.

Lemma 5.2
.

I\in\mathfrak{I}(\widehat{W}_{K},\widehat{v})
if and only if there exists a

and

, such that
w_{i}=v_{i}
for
i\notin I
and
w_{i}<v_{i}
for

Proof.

Suffice to consider for all

\Lambda_{w}
constribute to skeleton
\Lambda_{w,v}
near

for all

∎

Next, we relate the index set
\mathfrak{I}(\widehat{W}_{K},\widehat{v})
to the geometry of

. Recall that

is a basis of
\mathbb{R}^{N}
,

Proof.

Without loss of generality, we only consider the case
\widehat{v}=0
.

By Lemma

I\in\mathfrak{I}(\widehat{W}_{K},0)
if and only if
-\pi(\gamma_{I})\cap\widehat{W}_{K}\neq\emptyset
. Since
\widehat{W}_{K}=(\mathbb{B}+K)\cap\mathbb{Z}^{m}
, then

(-\pi(\gamma_{I}))\cap W_{K}=(-\pi(\gamma_{I}))\cap(\mathbb{B}+K).

If

x=b+y
for some

. Hence

If

, and let

Therefore, in each direction

Indeed, if

with step size

is a discrete subset in the ray

Here we consider the singular support of push-forward of hom sheaves,

Let
\Sigma_{\nabla}
be the exterior conormal fan of
\nabla

\sigma\in\Sigma_{\nabla}

denote the corresponding (closed) face whose exterior conormal is
\sigma

For any nonzero

[N]=[N]_{\xi,-}\sqcup[N]_{\xi,0}\sqcup[N]_{\xi,+},\quad[N]_{\xi,s}=\{i\in[N]\mid\sign(\langle\beta_{i},\xi\rangle)=s\}.

Clearly, the splitting is a locally constant function on

as

Theorem 5.6
.

(1) For any nonzero
\xi\in(\mathbb{R}^{k})^{\vee}
, let
\sigma
denote the cone of the exterior normal fan
\Sigma_{\nabla}
such that
\Int(\sigma)\ni\xi
. Then
\xi\in\mu_{*}(SS_{Hom}(\Lambda_{W_{\delta},v}))_{0}
if and only if
\delta\in v+\Aff(F_{\sigma})

(2) For any

,
\delta\in\mathbb{R}^{k}
, we have

\mu_{*}(SS_{Hom}(\Lambda_{W_{\delta},v}))=T_{\mathbb{R}^{k}}^{*}\mathbb{R}^{k}\quad\cup\,\bigcup_{\stackrel{{\scriptstyle 0\neq\sigma\in\Sigma_{\nabla}}}{{\delta\in v+\Aff(F_{\sigma})}}}\sigma^{\perp}\times\sigma.

(3) For any
\delta\in\mathbb{R}^{k}
,

\mu_{*}(SS_{Hom}(\Lambda_{W_{\delta}}))=T_{\mathbb{R}^{k}}^{*}\mathbb{R}^{k}\quad\cup\,\bigcup_{\sigma:(\delta+F_{\sigma})\cap\mathbb{Z}^{k}\neq\emptyset}\Aff(\delta+F_{\sigma})\times(-\sigma)

Proof.

(1) Let
\widetilde{\xi}=\mu^{*}(\xi)
, and
s(\xi)=\sign(\widetilde{\xi})
. Suppose
\xi\in\mu_{*}(SS_{Hom}(\Lambda_{W_{\delta}}))_{0}
, then there exists

, such that
s(\xi)\leq\sign(I,J)
. This means, for any

, if
\sign(\widetilde{\xi})_{i}=+

s(I,J)_{i}=+
, and if
\sign(\widetilde{\xi})_{i}=-
, then
s(I,J)_{i}=-
. Using Lemma
4.18

I^{c}\supset[N]_{\xi,+},\quad\text{and}\quad I\backslash J\supset[N]_{\xi,-},

[N]_{\xi,-}\subset I\subset[N]_{\xi,-}\sqcup[N]_{\xi,0},\quad\text{and}\quad J\subset[N]_{\xi,+}\sqcup[N]_{\xi,0}.

Since
\delta-v\in A_{I}\cap A_{J}
, and

A_{I}\subset-\beta_{[N]_{\xi,-}}-C_{[N]_{\xi,-}\sqcup[N]_{\xi,0}}+\nabla=-\beta_{[N]_{\xi,-}}/2-C_{[N]_{\xi,-}\sqcup[N]_{\xi,0}}

A_{J}\subset-C_{[N]_{\xi,+}\sqcup[N]_{\xi,0}}+\nabla=\beta_{[N]_{\xi,+}}/2-C_{[N]_{\xi,+}\sqcup[N]_{\xi,0}}

Since

Thus
\delta\in v+\Aff(F_{\sigma})
.

In the other direction, if

\widetilde{\xi}\in SS({\mathcal{H}om}(P_{I},P_{J}))_{0}
and
\xi\in\mu_{*}(SS_{Hom}(\Lambda_{W_{\delta}}))
.

(2) If
\delta-v\in\Aff(F_{\sigma})
, then
\sigma^{\perp}\times\sigma\subset\mu_{*}(SS_{Hom}(\Lambda_{W_{\delta}}))
. If
\delta-v\in\Aff(F_{\sigma})
and

. Finally, since

, the zero section

is included. Hence the direction

(3) One can verify the statement near each lattice point

In this section we prove

Totally Disconnected Semigroup Compactifications of Topological Groups
Alexander Stephens, Ross Stokke

Abstract
We introduce the notion of an introverted Boolean algebra ℬ \cal B of closed-and-open subsets of a topological group G G , show that the associated Stone space ( ν ℬ ​ G , ν ℬ ) (\nu_{\cal{B}}G,\nu_{\cal{B}}) is a totally disconnected semigroup compactification of G G , and show that every totally disconnected semigroup compactification of G G takes this form. We identify and study the universal totally disconnected semigroup compactification, the universal totally disconnected semitopological semigroup compactification and the universal totally disconnected group compactification of G G . Our main results are obtained independently of Gelfand theory and well-known properties of the (typically non-totally disconnected) universal compactifications G L ​ U ​ C G^{LUC} , G W ​ A ​ P G^{WAP} and G A ​ P G^{AP} , though we do employ Gelfand theory to clarify the relationship between these familiar universal compactifications and their totally disconnected counterparts. Totally disconnected compactifications as Stone spaces

A readable account of the following ideas, based on the work of M.H. Stone

Let
{\cal{B}}
be a Boolean algebra, i.e., suppose that

A subset

[
22

Definition 2.1
.

Letting

denote the collection of all ultrafilters on

\lambda_{\mathcal{B}}(B)=\{{\mathcal{U}}\in{\bf S}(B):B\in{\mathcal{U}}\}\quad(B\in{\cal{B}})

form an open base for a topology on
{\bf S}({\cal{B}})
;
{\bf S}({\cal{B}})
equipped with this topology is called the

{\cal{B}}

[
22

The Stone Representation Theorem

[
22
, Theorem 3.2(d)]

Let
{\cal{B}}
be a Boolean algebra. Then:

(i)

{\bf S}({\cal{B}})
is a compact zero-dimensional Hausdorff space;

(ii)

\lambda_{\mathcal{B}}[{\cal{B}}]=\{\lambda_{\mathcal{B}}(B):B\in{\cal{B}}\}={\mathboondoxfrak B}({\bf S}({\cal{B}}))
, the collection of
all
clopen subsets of
{\bf S}({\cal{B}})
; and

(iii)

\lambda_{\mathcal{B}}:{\cal{B}}\rightarrow{\mathboondoxfrak B}({\bf S}({\cal{B}}))
is a Boolean algebra isomorphism.

Thus, up to a Boolean algebra isomorphism, Boolean algebras are precisely the collections of all clopen subsets,
{\mathboondoxfrak B}(X)

will be called
G
-invariant
if it is closed under these group actions;
{\cal{B}}
will be called

if
B^{-1}\in{\cal{B}}
whenever
B\in{\cal{B}}
. For example,
{\mathboondoxfrak B}(G)
is
G
-invariant and inversion invariant. For any collection
{\cal C}
of subsets of
G

Definition 3.1
.

We will say that a
G
-invariant Boolean subalgebra
{\cal{B}}
of
{\mathboondoxfrak B}(G)
is

(respectively

)

{\mathcal{V}}\cdot B\in{\cal{B}}
(respectively

{\mathcal{V}}\in\nu_{\mathcal{B}}G
and

is

When
{\cal{B}}
is left (respectively right) introverted, we define the

(respectively
right
)

on

by

{\mathcal{U}}{\scriptscriptstyle\square}{\mathcal{V}}=\{B\in{\cal{B}}:{\mathcal{V}}\cdot B\in{\mathcal{U}}\}\quad\text{(respectively }{\mathcal{U}}\diamond{\mathcal{V}}=\{B\in{\cal{B}}:B\cdot{\mathcal{U}}\in{\mathcal{V}}\}).

We collect some elementary properties of these operations:

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
{\cal{B}}
be a
G
-invariant Boolean subalgebra of
{\mathboondoxfrak B}(G)
.

(i)

For each
s\in G
,
B\mapsto B\cdot s
and
B\mapsto s\cdot B
are Boolean algebra isomorphisms of
{\cal{B}}
onto itself.

(ii)

For
{\mathcal{C}}\subseteq{\cal{B}}
,
B\in{\cal{B}}
and
s\in G
,
({\mathcal{C}}\cdot B)\cdot s={\mathcal{C}}\cdot(B\cdot s)
and
s\cdot(B\cdot{\mathcal{C}})=(s\cdot B)\cdot{\mathcal{C}}
.

(iii)

For
B\in{\cal{B}}
and
s\in G
,
\nu_{\mathcal{B}}(s)\cdot B=s\cdot B
and
B\cdot\nu_{\mathcal{B}}(s)=B\cdot s
.

(iv)

For
{\mathcal{V}}\in\nu_{\mathcal{B}}G
,
B\mapsto{\mathcal{V}}\cdot B
(
B\mapsto B\cdot{\mathcal{V}}
) defines a Boolean algebra homomorphism of
{\cal{B}}
into
{\cal P}(G)
; when
{\cal{B}}
is left (right) introverted, we have a Boolean algebra homomorphism of
{\cal{B}}
into itself.

(v)

When
{\cal{B}}
is left (right) introverted,
({\mathcal{U}}{\scriptscriptstyle\square}{\mathcal{V}})\cdot B={\mathcal{U}}\cdot({\mathcal{V}}\cdot B)
(
B\cdot({\mathcal{U}}\diamond{\mathcal{V}})=(B\cdot{\mathcal{U}})\cdot{\mathcal{V}}
) for
{\mathcal{U}},{\mathcal{V}}\in\nu_{\mathcal{B}}G
and
B\in{\cal{B}}
.

Proof.

Part (i) is clear and parts (ii) and (iii) are simple calculations. For example,
t\in({\mathcal{C}}\cdot B)\cdot s=s^{-1}({\mathcal{C}}\cdot B)
if and only if
st\in{\mathcal{C}}\cdot B
if and only if
(B\cdot s)\cdot t=B\cdot(st)\in{\mathcal{C}}
if and only if
t\in{\mathcal{C}}\cdot(B\cdot s)
. Statement (iv) asserts that
{\mathcal{V}}\cdot\emptyset=\emptyset
,
{\mathcal{V}}\cdot G=G
, and for
B,C\in{\cal{B}}
,
{\mathcal{V}}\cdot(B\cup C)={\mathcal{V}}\cdot B\cup{\mathcal{V}}\cdot C
,
{\mathcal{V}}\cdot(B\cap C)={\mathcal{V}}\cdot B\cap{\mathcal{V}}\cdot C
and
{\mathcal{V}}\cdot(G\backslash B)=G\backslash({\mathcal{V}}\cdot B)
, all of which follow from (i) and properties of ultrafilters. For example, ultrafilters are prime, so
s\in{\mathcal{V}}\cdot(B\cup C)
if and only if
B\cdot s\cup C\cdot s=(B\cup C)\cdot s\in{\mathcal{V}}
if and only if
B\cdot s\in{\mathcal{V}}
or
C\cdot s\in{\mathcal{V}}
if and only if
s\in{\mathcal{V}}\cdot B\cup{\mathcal{V}}\cdot C
. Statement (v) follows from (ii):
s\in({\mathcal{U}}{\scriptscriptstyle\square}{\mathcal{V}})\cdot B
if and only if
B\cdot s\in{\mathcal{U}}{\scriptscriptstyle\square}{\mathcal{V}}
if and only if
({\mathcal{V}}\cdot B)\cdot s={\mathcal{V}}\cdot(B\cdot s)\in{\mathcal{U}}
if and only if
s\in{\mathcal{U}}\cdot({\mathcal{V}}\cdot B)
.
∎

Theorem 3.3
.

Let

(i)

(iii)

is left introverted and the product on

Proof.

(i) By Proposition
2.3
,

. Let
{\mathcal{U}},{\mathcal{V}},{\mathcal{W}}\in\nu_{\mathcal{B}}G

is a consequence of Lemma

(iv):

, so

{\mathcal{U}}{\scriptscriptstyle\square}{\mathcal{V}}
is a
{\cal{B}}
-ultrafilter.
Lemma
3.2
(v) yields associativity of
{\scriptscriptstyle\square}

if and only if
B\in{\mathcal{U}}{\scriptscriptstyle\square}({\mathcal{V}}{\scriptscriptstyle\square}{\mathcal{W}})
.
To see that
\nu_{\mathcal{B}}G
is a right topological semigroup, suppose that
{\mathcal{U}}_{i}\rightarrow{\mathcal{U}}
in

\lambda_{\mathcal{B}}(B)

(iii). Finally, to see that

maps
G

{\mathcal{V}}_{i}\rightarrow{\mathcal{V}}
in
\nu_{\mathcal{B}}G

(ii) By Proposition
2.3
(v), we only need to show that

is left introverted. Let

,

(iii) By statement (ii) and Proposition

Proposition 3.5
.

Suppose that
{\cal{B}}
is an introverted Boolean subalgebra of
{\mathboondoxfrak B}(G)
,
{\mathcal{U}}\in\nu_{\mathcal{B}}G
. Then
{\mathcal{U}}\in Z_{t}(\nu_{\mathcal{B}}G,{\scriptscriptstyle\square})
if and only if
{\mathcal{U}}{\scriptscriptstyle\square}{\mathcal{V}}={\mathcal{U}}\diamond{\mathcal{V}}
for every
{\mathcal{V}}\in\nu_{\mathcal{B}}G
.

Proof.

Suppose that
{\mathcal{U}}\in Z_{t}(\nu_{\mathcal{B}}G,{\scriptscriptstyle\square})
and let
{\mathcal{V}}\in\nu_{\mathcal{B}}G
. Choose a net
(s_{i})
in
G
such that
\nu_{\mathcal{B}}(s_{i})\rightarrow{\mathcal{V}}
, so
{\mathcal{U}}{\scriptscriptstyle\square}\nu_{\mathcal{B}}(s_{i})\rightarrow{\mathcal{U}}{\scriptscriptstyle\square}{\mathcal{V}}
in
\nu_{\mathcal{B}}G
. Let
B\in{\cal{B}}
and suppose that
B\in{\mathcal{U}}{\scriptscriptstyle\square}{\mathcal{V}}
. Then
{\mathcal{U}}{\scriptscriptstyle\square}{\mathcal{V}}\in\lambda_{\mathcal{B}}(B)
, so
{\mathcal{U}}{\scriptscriptstyle\square}\nu_{\mathcal{B}}(s_{i})\in\lambda_{\mathcal{B}}(B)
, eventually. Hence,
B\in{\mathcal{U}}{\scriptscriptstyle\square}\nu_{\mathcal{B}}(s_{i})
, meaning
\nu_{\mathcal{B}}(s_{i})\cdot B=s_{i}\cdot B\in{\mathcal{U}}
eventually; thus,
s_{i}\in B\cdot{\mathcal{U}}
, or
B\cdot{\mathcal{U}}\in\nu_{\mathcal{B}}(s_{i})
, and therefore
B\in{\mathcal{U}}\diamond\nu_{\mathcal{B}}(s_{i})
eventually. Thus,
{\mathcal{U}}\diamond\nu_{\mathcal{B}}(s_{i})
eventually belongs to the closed set
\lambda_{\mathcal{B}}(B)
, and therefore
{\mathcal{U}}\diamond{\mathcal{V}}\in\lambda_{\mathcal{B}}(B)
since
{\mathcal{U}}\diamond\nu_{\mathcal{B}}(s_{i})\rightarrow{\mathcal{U}}\diamond{\mathcal{V}}
. Hence
B\in{\mathcal{U}}\diamond{\mathcal{V}}
. If we begin by assuming that
B\in{\mathcal{U}}\diamond{\mathcal{V}}
, a symmetric argument shows that
B\in{\mathcal{U}}{\scriptscriptstyle\square}{\mathcal{V}}
. This proves the forward implication and the converse direction is trivial.
∎

Proposition 3.7

for each

if and only if

{\mathcal{U}}{\scriptscriptstyle\square}{\mathcal{V}}_{i}\rightarrow{\mathcal{U}}{\scriptscriptstyle\square}{\mathcal{V}}

\nu_{\mathcal{B}}G
for each

, and suppose that

. Then

, so

eventually, whence
{\mathcal{U}}{\scriptscriptstyle\square}{\mathcal{V}}_{i}\in\lambda_{\mathcal{B}}(B)
eventually. Now assume that the second of these conditions holds, and let
B\in{\cal{B}}
. Let
{\mathcal{U}}\in\nu_{\mathcal{B}}G
. If
{\mathcal{V}}\cdot B\in{\mathcal{U}}
, then
{\mathcal{U}}{\scriptscriptstyle\square}{\mathcal{V}}\in\lambda_{\mathcal{B}}(B)
, whence
{\mathcal{U}}{\scriptscriptstyle\square}{\mathcal{V}}_{i}\in\lambda_{\mathcal{B}}(B)
eventually; therefore,
{\mathcal{V}}_{i}\cdot B\in{\mathcal{U}}
eventually. If
{\mathcal{V}}\cdot B\notin{\mathcal{U}}
, then
{\mathcal{U}}{\scriptscriptstyle\square}{\mathcal{V}}\in\nu_{\mathcal{B}}G\backslash\lambda_{\mathcal{B}}(B)
, so
{\mathcal{U}}{\scriptscriptstyle\square}{\mathcal{V}}_{i}\in\nu_{\mathcal{B}}G\backslash\lambda_{\mathcal{B}}(B)
eventually; therefore
{\mathcal{V}}_{i}\cdot B\notin{\mathcal{U}}
eventually.
∎

The following is an immediate corollary of Theorem
3.3
and Proposition
3.7
.

Corollary 3.8
.

Let
G
be a topological group. Then
(\alpha G,\alpha)
is a totally disconnected semitopological semigroup compactification of
G
if and only if
(\alpha G,\alpha)\equiv_{G}(\nu_{\mathcal{B}}G,\nu_{\mathcal{B}})
for some (necessarily unique) introverted Arens regular Boolean subalgebra
{\cal{B}}
of
{\mathboondoxfrak B}(G)
.

Recall that
(\alpha G,\alpha)\in{\cal SK}(G)
is an
involutive semigroup compactification of
G

if has a continuous involution
z\mapsto z^{*}
— meaning,
(z^{*})^{*}=z
and
(yz)^{*}=z^{*}y^{*}
for all
y,z\in\alpha G
— such that
\alpha(s^{-1})=\alpha(s)^{*}
for all
s\in G
.

Remark 3.9
.

Suppose that
(\alpha G,\alpha)
,
(\gamma G,\gamma)
are involutive semigroup compactifications of
G
with
(\alpha G,\alpha)\preceq_{G}(\gamma G,\gamma)
; say
h:\gamma G\rightarrow\alpha G
is a continuous (homomorphic surjective) map such that
h\circ\gamma=\alpha
. One can readily check that
h
automatically satisfies
h(x^{*})=h(x)^{*}
for
x\in\gamma G
.

Proposition 3.10
.

Let
(\alpha G,\alpha)
be a totally disconnected involutive semigroup compactification of
G
. Then
(\alpha G,\alpha)
is a semitopological semigroup compactification of
G
and
(\alpha G,\alpha)\equiv_{G}(\nu_{\mathcal{B}}G,\nu_{\mathcal{B}})
for a unique introverted, Arens regular, inversion-invariant Boolean subalgebra
{\cal{B}}
of
{\mathboondoxfrak B}(G)
.

Proof.

Take
x\in\alpha G
. Since
\alpha G
is right topological,
\alpha G\rightarrow\alpha G:y\mapsto y^{*}\mapsto y^{*}x^{*}\mapsto(y^{*}x^{*})^{*}=xy
is continuous. Hence,
(\alpha G,\alpha)
is semitopological, so
(\alpha G,\alpha)\equiv_{G}(\nu_{{\cal{B}}^{\alpha}}G,\nu_{{\cal{B}}^{\alpha}})
where
{\cal{B}}^{\alpha}=\{\alpha^{-1}(D):D\in{\mathboondoxfrak B}(\alpha G)\}
is an introverted Arens regular Boolean subalgebra of
{\mathboondoxfrak B}(G)
by Theorem
3.3
and Corollary
3.8
. Let
D\in{\mathboondoxfrak B}(\alpha G)
. Since
x\mapsto x^{*}
is a homeomorphism on
\alpha G
,
D^{*}=\{x^{*}:x\in D\}\in{\mathboondoxfrak B}(\alpha G)
and
\alpha^{-1}(D^{*})=\alpha^{-1}(D)^{-1}
, so
{\cal{B}}^{\alpha}
is inversion invariant.
∎

When
(\nu_{\mathcal{B}}G,\nu_{\mathcal{B}})
is an involutive semigroup, we now describe the involution.

Proposition 3.11
.

Let
{\cal{B}}
be an inversion-invariant left introverted Boolean subalgebra of
{\mathboondoxfrak B}(G)
.

(i)

For
{\mathcal{U}}\in\nu_{\mathcal{B}}G
,
{\mathcal{U}}^{*}:=\{B^{-1}:B\in{\mathcal{U}}\}\in\nu_{\mathcal{B}}G
;
({\mathcal{U}}^{*})^{*}={\mathcal{U}}
;
{\mathcal{U}}\mapsto{\mathcal{U}}^{*}
is a homeomorphism of
\nu_{\mathcal{B}}G
; and for each
s\in G
,
\nu_{\mathcal{B}}(s^{-1})=\nu_{\mathcal{B}}(s)^{*}
. Moreover,
{\cal{B}}
is introverted and
({\mathcal{U}}{\scriptscriptstyle\square}{\mathcal{V}})^{*}={\mathcal{V}}^{*}\diamond{\mathcal{U}}^{*}
for
{\mathcal{U}},{\mathcal{V}}\in\nu_{\mathcal{B}}G
.

(ii)

Hence,
{\cal{B}}
is Arens regular if and only if
(\nu_{\mathcal{B}}G,\nu_{\mathcal{B}},{\scriptscriptstyle\square})
— equivalently
(\nu_{\mathcal{B}}G,\nu_{\mathcal{B}},\diamond)
— is an involutive semigroup compactification of
G
.

Proof.

(i) Since
B\mapsto B^{-1}
is a Boolean algebra isomorphism on
{\cal{B}}
,
{\mathcal{U}}^{*}\in\nu_{\mathcal{B}}G
for
{\mathcal{U}}\in\nu_{\mathcal{B}}G
; clearly
({\mathcal{U}}^{*})^{*}={\mathcal{U}}
and
\nu_{\mathcal{B}}(s^{-1})=\nu_{\mathcal{B}}(s)^{*}
for
s\in G
. To see that
{\mathcal{U}}\mapsto{\mathcal{U}}^{*}
is continuous (and therefore a homeomorphism), observe that the pre-image of
\lambda_{\mathcal{B}}(B)
under this map is
\lambda_{\mathcal{B}}(B^{-1})
. Let
{\mathcal{U}},{\mathcal{V}}\in\nu_{\mathcal{B}}G
. For
B\in{\cal{B}}
, observe that
{\mathcal{V}}\cdot B^{-1}=(B\cdot{\mathcal{V}}^{*})^{-1}
. Since
{\cal{B}}
is inversion invariant and left introverted, we deduce that
{\cal{B}}
is also right introverted. Moreover, for
B\in{\cal{B}}
,
B\in({\mathcal{U}}{\scriptscriptstyle\square}{\mathcal{V}})^{*}
if and only if
(B\cdot{\mathcal{V}}^{*})^{-1}={\mathcal{V}}\cdot B^{-1}\in{\mathcal{U}}
; equivalently,
B\in{\mathcal{V}}^{*}\diamond{\mathcal{U}}^{*}
.

(ii) If
{\cal{B}}
is Arens regular, we obtain
({\mathcal{U}}{\scriptscriptstyle\square}{\mathcal{V}})^{*}={\mathcal{V}}^{*}\diamond{\mathcal{U}}^{*}={\mathcal{V}}^{*}{\scriptscriptstyle\square}\,{\mathcal{U}}^{*}
. If
(\nu_{\mathcal{B}}G,{\scriptscriptstyle\square})
is involutive,
{\mathcal{U}}{\scriptscriptstyle\square}{\mathcal{V}}=({\mathcal{U}}{\scriptscriptstyle\square}{\mathcal{V}})^{**}=({\mathcal{V}}^{*}{\scriptscriptstyle\square}\,{\mathcal{U}}^{*})^{*}={\mathcal{U}}^{**}\diamond{\mathcal{V}}^{**}={\mathcal{U}}\diamond{\mathcal{V}}
. ∎

In Section 4 we will observe that there exist non-Arens regular introverted inversion-invariant Boolean subalgebras of
{\mathboondoxfrak B}(G)
, and we will identify the largest Arens regular (inversion-invariant) Boolean subalgebra of
{\mathboondoxfrak B}(G)
.

4
Universal compactifications

For a topological group
G
, we will identify several totally disconnected semigroup compactifications as
Stone spaces of associated left introverted Boolean subalgebras of
{\mathboondoxfrak B}(G)
. All proofs are independent of function spaces and Gelfand theory, and of previously known universal properties of other (not necessarily totally disconnected) semigroup compactifications. Broad ideas from the non-totally disconnected theory, e.g. see
[
1
]
, are sometimes employed.

4.1
The universal TD semigroup compactification

Let
G
be a topological group. Writing
\nu_{{\mathboondoxfrak B}}
,
\sigma_{{\mathboondoxfrak B}}
in place of
\nu_{{\mathboondoxfrak B}(G)}
,
\sigma_{{\mathboondoxfrak B}(G)}
(and so on), let

{\cal{B}}^{LMC}:=\{B\in{\mathboondoxfrak B}(G):s\mapsto B\cdot s:G\rightarrow({\mathboondoxfrak B}(G),\sigma_{{\mathboondoxfrak B}})\text{ is continuous}\}.

Using Lemma
3.6
(iv) and (vi), one sees that
{\cal{B}}^{LMC}
is a
G
-invariant Boolean subalgebra of
{\mathboondoxfrak B}(G)
.

Notation
: We will write
(\nu_{LMC}G,\nu_{LMC})
,
\lambda_{LMC}(B)
,
\sigma_{LMC}
, etc., in place of
(\nu_{{\cal{B}}^{LMC}}G,\nu_{{\cal{B}}^{LMC}})
,
\lambda_{{\cal{B}}^{LMC}}(B)
,
\sigma_{{\cal{B}}^{LMC}}
, etc. Without further comment, we will employ similar notation after later introducing the Boolean subalgebras
{\cal{B}}^{LUC}
,
{\cal{B}}^{WAP}
and
{\cal{B}}^{AP}
of
{\mathboondoxfrak B}(G)
.

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
G
be a topological group. Then
{\cal{B}}^{LMC}
is the largest left introverted Boolean subalgebra of
{\mathboondoxfrak B}(G)
and
(\nu_{LMC}G,\nu_{LMC})
is the universal totally disconnected semigroup compactification of
G
; i.e.,
(\nu_{LMC}G,\nu_{LMC})
is the projective maximum of
{\cal SK}_{0}(G)
.

Proof.

Let
{\mathcal{V}}\in\nu_{LMC}G
,
B\in{\cal{B}}^{LMC}
. Let
(t_{i})
be a net in
{\mathcal{V}}\cdot B
such that
t_{i}\rightarrow t
in
G
. Then each
B\cdot t_{i}\in{\mathcal{V}}
and, by Lemma
3.6
(v),
B\cdot t_{i}\rightarrow B\cdot t
in
\sigma_{LMC}
; hence
B\cdot t\in{\mathcal{V}}
, meaning that
t\in{\mathcal{V}}\cdot B
. Thus,
{\mathcal{V}}\cdot B
is closed. Since
G\backslash({\mathcal{V}}\cdot B)={\mathcal{V}}\cdot(G\backslash B)
is also closed,
{\mathcal{V}}\cdot B\in{\mathboondoxfrak B}(G)
. To see that
{\mathcal{V}}\cdot B\in{\cal{B}}^{LMC}
, suppose that
s_{i}\rightarrow s
in
G
and let
{\mathcal{U}}\in\nu_{{\mathboondoxfrak B}}G
. Define
{\mathcal{U}}\tilde{{\scriptscriptstyle\square}}{\mathcal{V}}=\{A\in{\cal{B}}^{LMC}:{\mathcal{V}}\cdot A\in{\mathcal{U}}\}
. By Lemma
3.2
(iv),
A\mapsto{\mathcal{V}}\cdot A:{\cal{B}}^{LMC}\rightarrow{\mathboondoxfrak B}(G)
is a Boolean algebra homomorphism, so the argument found in the first paragraph of the proof of Theorem
3.3
(i) showing there that
{\mathcal{U}}{\scriptscriptstyle\square}{\mathcal{V}}
is a
{\cal{B}}
-ultrafilter, shows here that
{\mathcal{U}}\tilde{{\scriptscriptstyle\square}}{\mathcal{V}}
is a
{\cal{B}}^{LMC}
-ultrafilter, i.e.,
{\mathcal{U}}\tilde{{\scriptscriptstyle\square}}{\mathcal{V}}\in\nu_{LMC}G
. If
({\mathcal{V}}\cdot B)\cdot s={\mathcal{V}}\cdot(B\cdot s)\in{\mathcal{U}}
, then
B\cdot s\in{\mathcal{U}}\tilde{{\scriptscriptstyle\square}}{\mathcal{V}}
and therefore, since
B\cdot s_{i}\rightarrow B\cdot s
in
\sigma_{LMC}
(again by Lemma
3.6
(v)),
B\cdot s_{i}\in{\mathcal{U}}\tilde{{\scriptscriptstyle\square}}{\mathcal{V}}
, eventually; hence
({\mathcal{V}}\cdot B)\cdot s_{i}={\mathcal{V}}\cdot(B\cdot s_{i})\in{\mathcal{U}}
, eventually. If
({\mathcal{V}}\cdot B)\cdot s={\mathcal{V}}\cdot(B\cdot s)\notin{\mathcal{U}}
, then
B\cdot s\notin{\mathcal{U}}\tilde{{\scriptscriptstyle\square}}{\mathcal{V}}
, so
B\cdot s_{i}\notin{\mathcal{U}}\tilde{{\scriptscriptstyle\square}}{\mathcal{V}}
, eventually; hence
({\mathcal{V}}\cdot B)\cdot s_{i}={\mathcal{V}}\cdot(B\cdot s_{i})\notin{\mathcal{U}}
, eventually. Thus,
({\mathcal{V}}\cdot B)\cdot s_{i}\rightarrow({\mathcal{V}}\cdot B)\cdot s
in
\sigma_{{\mathboondoxfrak B}}
, and we conclude that
{\mathcal{V}}\cdot B\in{\cal{B}}^{LMC}
. Thus,
{\cal{B}}^{LMC}
is a left introverted Boolean subalgebra of
{\mathboondoxfrak B}(G)
and, therefore,
(\nu_{LMC}G,\nu_{LMC})\in{\cal SK}_{0}(G)
.

Let
(\alpha G,\alpha)\in{\cal SK}_{0}(G)
. By Theorem
3.3
,
(\alpha G,\alpha)\equiv_{G}(\nu_{\mathcal{B}}G,\nu_{\mathcal{B}})
for some left introverted Boolean subalgebra
{\cal{B}}
of
{\mathboondoxfrak B}(G)
. Let
B\in{\cal{B}}
, suppose that
s_{i}\rightarrow s
in
G
, and take
{\mathcal{V}}\in\nu_{\mathcal{B}}G
. If
B\cdot s\in{\mathcal{V}}
, then
s\in{\mathcal{V}}\cdot B
, meaning
{\mathcal{V}}\cdot B\in\nu_{\mathcal{B}}(s)
, and therefore
B\in\nu_{\mathcal{B}}(s){\scriptscriptstyle\square}{\mathcal{V}}
; hence
\nu_{\mathcal{B}}(s){\scriptscriptstyle\square}{\mathcal{V}}\in\lambda_{\mathcal{B}}(B)
. Since
\nu_{\mathcal{B}}(s_{i}){\scriptscriptstyle\square}{\mathcal{V}}\rightarrow\nu_{\mathcal{B}}(s){\scriptscriptstyle\square}{\mathcal{V}}
in
\nu_{\mathcal{B}}G
,
\nu_{\mathcal{B}}(s_{i}){\scriptscriptstyle\square}{\mathcal{V}}\in\lambda_{\mathcal{B}}(B)
eventually, and therefore
B\cdot s_{i}\in{\mathcal{V}}
eventually. If
B\cdot s\notin{\mathcal{V}}
, then
\nu_{\mathcal{B}}(s){\scriptscriptstyle\square}{\mathcal{V}}
belongs to the clopen set
\nu_{\mathcal{B}}G\backslash\lambda_{\mathcal{B}}(B)
, whence
\nu_{\mathcal{B}}(s_{i}){\scriptscriptstyle\square}{\mathcal{V}}\in\nu_{\mathcal{B}}G\backslash\lambda_{\mathcal{B}}(B)
eventually; therefore,
B\cdot s_{i}\notin{\mathcal{V}}
eventually. Thus,
B\cdot s_{i}\rightarrow B\cdot s
in
\sigma_{\cal{B}}
, so
B\cdot s_{i}\rightarrow B\cdot s
in
\sigma_{{\mathboondoxfrak B}}
. Hence,
{\cal{B}}
is contained in
{\cal{B}}^{LMC}
and therefore
(\nu_{\mathcal{B}}G,\nu_{\mathcal{B}})\preceq_{G}(\nu_{LMC}G,\nu_{LMC})
.∎

4.2
Universality with respect to the joint continuity property

For a subset
E
of
G
, let

{}_{E}G=\{s\in G:E\cdot s=E\}\quad\text{ and }\quad G_{E}=\{s\in G:s\cdot E=E\}

be the right and left stabilizer subgroups of
G
. Let

{\cal{B}}^{LUC}=\{B\in{\mathboondoxfrak B}(G):{{}_{B}G}\text{ is open}\}.

Lemma 4.2
.

(a) Let
B\in{\mathboondoxfrak B}(G)
. The following statements are equivalent:

(i)

B\in{\cal{B}}^{LUC}
;

(ii)

G\rightarrow{\mathboondoxfrak B}(G):s\mapsto B\cdot s
is continuous (at
e_{G}
) with respect to the discrete topology on
{\mathboondoxfrak B}(G)
;

(iii)

for each
{\cal A}\subseteq{\mathboondoxfrak B}(G)
,
{\cal A}\cdot B=\{s\in G:B\cdot s\in{\cal A}\}
is open (equivalently closed, equivalently clopen) in
G
;

(iv)

for each
C\in{\mathboondoxfrak B}(G)
,
\{C\}\cdot B=\{s\in G:B\cdot s=C\}
is open in
G
.

(b) The collection of sets
{\cal{B}}^{LUC}
is a left introverted Boolean subalgebra of
{\mathboondoxfrak B}(G)
.

Proof.

(a) Suppose that
B\in{\cal{B}}^{LUC}
and
s_{i}\rightarrow e_{G}
in
G
. Since
e_{G}
belongs to the open subgroup
{}_{B}G
,
s_{i}\in{{}_{B}G}
, and therefore
B\cdot s_{i}=B\cdot e_{G}
, eventually. If
s_{i}\rightarrow s
, then
s_{i}s^{-1}\rightarrow e_{G}
and therefore
B\cdot(s_{i}s^{-1})=B
, whence
B\cdot s_{i}=B\cdot s
, eventually. Hence, (i) implies (ii). Since
{\cal A}\cdot B
is the pre-image of
\cal A
under the map
s\mapsto B\cdot s
, statements (ii) and (iii) are equivalent. It is clear that (iii) implies (iv) and, since
\{B\}\cdot B={{}_{B}G}
, (iv) implies (i).

(b) Observe that
{{}_{\emptyset}G}=G
and
{{}_{G}G}=G
, so
\emptyset,G\in{\cal{B}}^{LUC}
. Suppose that
A,B\in{\cal{B}}^{LUC}
. Then
{}_{A}G\cap{{}_{B}G}
is an open subgroup of both
{}_{A\cap B}G
and
{}_{A\cup B}G
, so
A\cap B,A\cup B\in{\cal{B}}^{LUC}
; also
{}_{G\backslash B}G={{}_{B}G}
, so
G\backslash B\in{\cal{B}}^{LUC}
. Hence,
{\cal{B}}^{LUC}
is a Boolean subalgebra of
{\mathboondoxfrak B}(G)
. For
t\in G
, observe that
{}_{t\cdot B}G={{}_{B}G}
and
{}_{B\cdot t}G=t^{-1}{{}_{B}G}t
; therefore
{\cal{B}}^{LUC}
is
G
-invariant. For
{\mathcal{V}}\in\nu_{LUC}G
,
B\in{\cal{B}}^{LUC}
and
t\in G
,
{\mathcal{V}}\cdot B\in{\mathboondoxfrak B}(G)
by part (a), and
({\mathcal{V}}\cdot B)\cdot t={\mathcal{V}}\cdot(B\cdot t)
, so
{{}_{B}G}\subseteq{{}_{{\mathcal{V}}\cdot B}G}
; hence
{\mathcal{V}}\cdot B\in{\cal{B}}^{LUC}
.
∎

A semigroup compactification
(\alpha G,\alpha)
has the
joint continuity property
if the map
G\times\alpha G\rightarrow\alpha G:(s,z)\mapsto\alpha(s)z
is jointly continuous
[
1
, Section 4.4]
.

Remark 4.3
.

Suppose that
(\gamma G,\gamma)\in{\cal SK}(G)
has the joint continuity property/is a semitopological semigroup/is a topological group. Then any quotient of
(\gamma G,\gamma)
in
{\cal SK}(G)
has the same property: (Though this is likely known, we do not have a reference.) Suppose that
(\alpha G,\alpha)\preceq_{G}(\gamma G,\gamma)
in
{\cal SK}(G)
,
h:\gamma G\rightarrow\alpha G
the associated compactification homomorphism. Assume first that
(\gamma G,\gamma)
has the joint continuity property. To see that
(\alpha G,\alpha)
has the joint continuity property, assuming that
(s_{i},z_{i})\rightarrow(s,z)
in
G\times\alpha G
, it suffices to show that
\alpha(s_{i_{j}})z_{i_{j}}\rightarrow\alpha(s)z
for some subnet
((s_{i_{j}},z_{i_{j}}))_{j}
of
((s_{i},z_{i}))_{i}
. For each
i
, take
x_{i}
in
\gamma G
such that
h(x_{i})=z_{i}
; take
(x_{i_{j}})_{j}
to be a subnet of
(x_{i})_{i}
and
x\in\gamma G
such that
\lim x_{i_{j}}=x
. Then
\gamma(s_{i_{j}})x_{i_{j}}\rightarrow\gamma(s)x
in
\gamma G
, so
\alpha(s_{i_{j}})z_{i_{j}}=h(\gamma(s_{i_{j}})x_{i_{j}})\rightarrow h(\gamma(s)x)=\alpha(s)z
, as needed. A similar argument shows that
\alpha G
is semitopological when
\gamma G
is so. If
\gamma G
is a topological group, then
\alpha G
is a semitopological compact group, therefore a topological group by
[
1
, Corollary 1.4.5]
.

Theorem 4.4
.

Let
{\cal{B}}
be a Boolean subalgebra of
{\mathboondoxfrak B}(G)
. The following statements are equivalent:

(i)

(\nu_{\mathcal{B}}G,\nu_{\mathcal{B}})
is a semigroup compactification of
G
with the joint continuity property;

(ii)

{\cal{B}}
is left introverted and contained in
{\cal{B}}^{LUC}
.

In particular,
(\nu_{LUC}G,\nu_{LUC})
is universal among all totally disconnected semigroup compactifications of
G
with the joint continuity property.

Proof.

Suppose that
(s_{i},{\mathcal{V}}_{i})\rightarrow(s,{\mathcal{V}})
in
G\times\nu_{LUC}G
and let
B\in{\cal{B}}^{LUC}
be such that
\nu_{LUC}(s){\scriptscriptstyle\square}{\mathcal{V}}\in\lambda_{LUC}(B)
; equivalently,
{\mathcal{V}}\in\lambda_{\mathcal{B}}(B\cdot s)
. Take
i_{0}
such that
B\cdot s_{i}=B\cdot s
for
i\succeq i_{0}
, and take
i_{1}\succeq i_{0}
such that
{\mathcal{V}}_{i}\in\lambda_{LUC}(B\cdot s)
for
i\succeq i_{1}
. Then for
i\succeq i_{1}
,
B\cdot s_{i}=B\cdot s\in{\mathcal{V}}_{i}
, and therefore
\nu_{LUC}(s_{i}){\scriptscriptstyle\square}{\mathcal{V}}_{i}\in\lambda_{LUC}(B)
. Hence,
(\nu_{LUC}G,\nu_{LUC})
has the joint continuity property.

Assume that
(\nu_{\mathcal{B}}G,\nu_{\mathcal{B}})
is a semigroup compactification of
G
with the joint continuity property. By Theorem
3.3
,
{\cal{B}}
is left introverted. Suppose towards a contradiction that
B\notin{\cal{B}}^{LUC}
for some
B\in{\cal{B}}
. Then by Lemma
4.2
there is a net
(s_{i})
converging to
e_{G}
in
G
such that
B\cdot s_{i}
does not equal
B\cdot e_{G}=B
eventually; passing to a subnet if needed, we can assume that
B\cdot s_{i}\neq B
for all
i
. For each
i
, either
B\cdot s_{i}\backslash B\neq\emptyset
or
B\backslash B\cdot s_{i}\neq\emptyset
, so there is some
t_{i}\in B
such that either

\displaystyle s_{i}^{-1}t_{i}\notin B\qquad\text{or}\qquad s_{i}t_{i}\notin B.

(1)

For each
i
,
\nu_{\mathcal{B}}(t_{i})\in\lambda_{\mathcal{B}}(B)
, a closed subset of the compact space
\nu_{\mathcal{B}}G
. Passing to a subnet again, if necessary,
\nu_{\mathcal{B}}(t_{i})\rightarrow{\mathcal{V}}
in
\nu_{\mathcal{B}}G
for some
{\mathcal{V}}\in\lambda_{\mathcal{B}}(B)
. Using (
1
) and passing yet again to a subnet if required, we can assume that either
s_{i}^{-1}t_{i}\notin B
for each
i
, or
s_{i}t_{i}\notin B
for each
i
. Suppose first that
s_{i}^{-1}t_{i}\notin B
for each
i
. Since
(s_{i}^{-1},\nu_{\mathcal{B}}(t_{i}))\rightarrow(e_{G},{\mathcal{V}})
in
G\times\nu_{\mathcal{B}}G
and
(\nu_{\mathcal{B}}G,\nu_{\mathcal{B}})
has the joint continuity property,
\nu_{\mathcal{B}}(s_{i}^{-1}t_{i})=\nu_{\mathcal{B}}(s_{i}^{-1}){\scriptscriptstyle\square}\nu_{\mathcal{B}}(t_{i})\rightarrow\nu_{\mathcal{B}}(e_{G}){\scriptscriptstyle\square}{\mathcal{V}}={\mathcal{V}}
in
\nu_{\mathcal{B}}G
. But
{\mathcal{V}}\in\lambda_{\mathcal{B}}(B)
, so for large
i
we must have
\nu_{\mathcal{B}}(s_{i}^{-1}t_{i})\in\lambda_{\mathcal{B}}(B)
, meaning
s_{i}^{-1}t_{i}\in B
, a contradiction. One similarly obtains a contradiction if
s_{i}t_{i}\notin B
for each
i
. We conclude that
{\cal{B}}
is contained in
{\cal{B}}^{LUC}
, and therefore
(\nu_{\mathcal{B}}G,\nu_{\mathcal{B}})\preceq_{G}(\nu_{LUC}G,\nu_{LUC})
.
By Theorem
3.3
we conclude that
(\alpha G,\alpha)\preceq_{G}(\nu_{LUC}G,\nu_{LUC})
for any
(\alpha G,\alpha)\in{\cal SK}_{0}(G)
with the joint continuity property. We have established the last statement of the theorem and that (i) implies (ii); (ii) implies (i) is a consequence of Proposition
2.3
(iv) and Remark
4.3
.
∎

When

). We therefore have the following immediate consequence of Theorems

,

and Proposition

(iv).

Corollary 4.5
.

Let

be a locally compact group. Then
(\nu_{LUC}G,\nu_{LUC})\equiv_{G}(\nu_{LMC}G,\nu_{LMC})
, the universal totally disconnected semigroup compactification of

, and
{\cal{B}}^{LUC}={\cal{B}}^{LMC}
, the largest left introverted Boolean subalgebra of

.

Remark 4.6
.

Symmetric arguments show that
{\cal{B}}^{RUC}=\{B\in{\mathboondoxfrak B}(G):G_{B}\text{ is open}\}
is a right introverted Boolean subalgebra of

such that the obvious versions of

and

hold for the left topological semigroup compactification

.

4.3
The universal TD semitopological semigroup compactification

Let
G
be a topological group. For
B\in{\mathboondoxfrak B}(G)
, let

{\cal LO}(B)={\cal LO}_{G}(B)=\{s\cdot B:s\in G\}\quad\text{ and }\quad{\cal RO}(B)={\cal RO}_{G}(B)=\{B\cdot s:s\in G\}

be the left and right
G
-orbits of
B
. Let

{\cal{B}}^{WAP}=\{B\in{\cal{B}}^{LUC}:{\cal LO}(B)\text{ is relatively }\sigma_{{\mathboondoxfrak B}}-\text{compact}\}.

Remark 4.7
.

(a) Our goal is to identify
{\cal{B}}
such that
(\nu_{\mathcal{B}}G,\nu_{\mathcal{B}})
is the universal semitopological semigroup compactification of
G
. By
[
1
, Corollary 1.4.4]
, every semitopological semigroup compactification of
G
has the joint continuity property, so
{\cal{B}}
will necessarily be contained in
{\cal{B}}^{LUC}
by Theorem
4.4
. Equivalently, we could have required that sets in
{\cal{B}}^{WAP}
belong to
{\cal{B}}^{LMC}
; indeed readers wishing to avoid using
[
1
, Corollary 1.4.4]
can, throughout this subsection, replace all references to
{\cal{B}}^{LUC}
,
\sigma_{LUC}
, etc. with
{\cal{B}}^{LMC}
,
\sigma_{LMC}
, etc.

(b) For
B\in{\cal{B}}^{LUC}
,
{\cal LO}(B)\subseteq{\cal{B}}^{LUC}
, so in our definition of
{\cal{B}}^{WAP}
we could have required that
{\cal LO}(B)
be relatively
\sigma_{LUC}
-compact and, arguing as below, obtained the same compactification of
G
and, therefore, the same collection of sets
{\cal{B}}^{WAP}
. We chose the above definition because we do not know if
{\cal{B}}^{LUC}
is
\sigma_{{\mathboondoxfrak B}}
-closed in
{\mathboondoxfrak B}(G)
, so our definition is formally weaker than the alternative.

Lemma 4.8
.

The collection of sets
{\cal{B}}^{WAP}
is a
G
-invariant Boolean subalgebra of
{\mathboondoxfrak B}(G)
. Hence, the totally disconnected compactification
(\nu_{WAP}G,\nu_{WAP})\in{\mathcal{K}}_{0}(G)
exists.

Proof.

Since
{\cal LO}(\emptyset)=\{\emptyset\}
and
{\cal LO}(G)=\{G\}
,
\emptyset,G\in{\cal{B}}^{WAP}
. Let
A,B\in{\cal{B}}^{WAP}
,
t\in G
. Then
G\backslash A,A\cup B,A\cap B,t\cdot A,A\cdot t\in{\cal{B}}^{LUC}
. Let
(s_{i})
to be a net in
G
. Then there is a subnet
(s_{i_{j}})
of
(s_{i})
and
C,D\in{\mathboondoxfrak B}(G)
such that
s_{i_{j}}\cdot A\rightarrow C
in
\sigma_{{\mathboondoxfrak B}}
and — passing to another subnet if necessary —
s_{i_{j}}\cdot B\rightarrow D
in
\sigma_{{\mathboondoxfrak B}}
. By Lemma
3.6
(iv),
s_{i_{j}}\cdot(G\backslash A)=G\backslash(s_{i_{j}}\cdot A)\rightarrow G\backslash C
in
\sigma_{{\mathboondoxfrak B}}
and

s_{i_{j}}\cdot(A\cup B)=s_{i_{j}}\cdot A\cup s_{i_{j}}\cdot B\rightarrow C\cup D
in
\sigma_{{\mathboondoxfrak B}}
, so
G\backslash A,A\cup B\in{\cal{B}}^{WAP}
; similarly,
A\cap B\in{\cal{B}}^{WAP}
and, by Lemma
3.6
(vi),
A\cdot t\in{\cal{B}}^{WAP}
. Since
{\cal LO}(t\cdot A)={\cal LO}(A)
,
t\cdot A\in{\cal{B}}^{WAP}
as well.
∎

Theorem 4.9
.

Let
{\cal{B}}
be a Boolean subalgebra of
{\mathboondoxfrak B}(G)
. The following statements are equivalent:

(i)

(\nu_{\mathcal{B}}G,\nu_{\mathcal{B}})
is a semitopological semigroup compactification of
G
;

(ii)

{\cal{B}}
is left introverted and contained in
{\cal{B}}^{WAP}
;

(iii)

{\cal{B}}
is introverted and Arens regular.

In particular,
{\cal{B}}^{WAP}
is the largest introverted Arens regular Boolean subalgebra of
{\mathboondoxfrak B}(G)
and
(\nu_{WAP}G,\nu_{WAP})
is the universal totally disconnected semitopological semigroup compactification of
G
.

Proof.

Let
B\in{\cal{B}}^{WAP}
. If
{\mathcal{V}}\in\nu_{WAP}G
, then
{\mathcal{V}}={\mathcal{U}}\cap{\cal{B}}^{WAP}
for some
{\mathcal{U}}\in\nu_{LUC}G
by Proposition
2.3
(iv), and
{\mathcal{V}}\cdot B={\mathcal{U}}\cdot B\in{\cal{B}}^{LUC}
since
{\cal{B}}^{WAP}
is
G
-invariant and
{\cal{B}}^{LUC}
is left introverted. We
claim
that
T_{B}:\nu_{WAP}G\rightarrow({\cal{B}}^{LUC},\sigma_{LUC}):{\mathcal{V}}\mapsto{\mathcal{V}}\cdot B
is continuous.

We first observe that
T_{B}
is continuous with respect to the point topology
\rho_{LUC}
on
{\cal{B}}^{LUC}
. Indeed, if
{\mathcal{V}}_{i}\rightarrow{\mathcal{V}}
in
\nu_{WAP}G
and
s\in{\mathcal{V}}\cdot B
, then
{\mathcal{V}}\in\lambda_{WAP}(B\cdot s)
, so
{\mathcal{V}}_{i}\in\lambda_{WAP}(B\cdot s)
, and therefore
s\in{\mathcal{V}}_{i}\cdot B
, eventually; similarly if
s\notin{\mathcal{V}}\cdot B
,
s\notin{\mathcal{V}}_{i}\cdot B
eventually. By Lemma
3.6
(iii),
{\mathcal{V}}_{i}\cdot B\rightarrow{\mathcal{V}}\cdot B
in
\rho_{LUC}
.
It follows that
{\cal LO}_{\nu_{WAP}G}(B):=\{{\mathcal{V}}\cdot B:{\mathcal{V}}\in\nu_{WAP}G\}
is a
\rho_{LUC}
-compact subset of
{\cal{B}}^{LUC}
. Since
\rho_{LUC}
is Hausdorff,
{\cal LO}_{\nu_{WAP}G}(B)
is
\rho_{LUC}
-closed and therefore, since
s\cdot B=\nu_{WAP}(s)\cdot B
,
\overline{{\cal LO}_{G}(B)}^{\rho_{LUC}}\subseteq{\cal LO}_{\nu_{WAP}G}(B)
. From the density of
\nu_{WAP}(G)
in
\nu_{WAP}G
and
\rho_{LUC}
-continuity of
T_{B}
, we obtain
{\cal LO}_{\nu_{WAP}G}(B)=T_{B}(\nu_{WAP}G)\subseteq\overline{T_{B}(\nu_{WAP}(G))}^{\rho_{LUC}}=\overline{{\cal LO}_{G}(B)}^{\rho_{LUC}}
, and therefore
\overline{{\cal LO}_{G}(B)}^{\rho_{LUC}}={\cal LO}_{\nu_{WAP}G}(B)
is a
\rho_{LUC}
-compact subset of
{\cal{B}}^{LUC}
. By Lemma
3.6
(v),
\overline{{\cal LO}_{G}(B)}^{\rho_{{\mathboondoxfrak B}}}={\cal LO}_{\nu_{WAP}G}(B)
is a
\rho_{\mathboondoxfrak B}
-compact subset of
{\mathboondoxfrak B}(G)
.
However,
{\cal LO}_{G}(B)
is relatively
\sigma_{{\mathboondoxfrak B}}
-compact by hypothesis, and the Hausdorff topology
\rho_{{\mathboondoxfrak B}}
is coarser than the Hausdorff topology
\sigma_{{\mathboondoxfrak B}}
, so
\overline{{\cal LO}_{G}(B)}^{\sigma_{LUC}}=\overline{{\cal LO}_{G}(B)}^{\rho_{LUC}}={\cal LO}_{\nu_{WAP}G}(B)
and the
\sigma_{{\mathboondoxfrak B}}
and
\rho_{\mathboondoxfrak B}
topologies agree on this set; as
{\cal LO}_{\nu_{WAP}G}(B)\subseteq{\cal{B}}^{LUC}
,
\sigma_{LUC}
and
\rho_{LUC}
agree on
{\cal LO}_{\nu_{WAP}G}(B)
. Hence,
T_{B}:\nu_{WAP}G\rightarrow({\cal{B}}^{LUC},\sigma_{LUC})
is continuous, as claimed.

Let
{\mathcal{V}}\in\nu_{WAP}G
. Then
{\mathcal{V}}\cdot B\in{\cal{B}}^{LUC}
. For
s\in G
,
R_{s^{-1}}({\mathcal{V}})\in\nu_{WAP}G
by Lemma
3.6
(vi), and it is easy to check that
s\cdot({\mathcal{V}}\cdot B)=R_{s^{-1}}({\mathcal{V}})\cdot B
; hence,
{\cal LO}_{G}({\mathcal{V}}\cdot B)\subseteq{\cal LO}_{\nu_{WAP}G}(B)
, which is
\sigma_{LUC}
-compact. Thus,
{\mathcal{V}}\cdot B\in\nu_{WAP}G
. It follows that
{\cal{B}}^{WAP}
is left introverted and for each
B\in{\cal{B}}^{WAP}
,
T_{B}:\nu_{WAP}G\rightarrow({\cal{B}}^{WAP},\sigma_{WAP}):{\mathcal{V}}\mapsto{\mathcal{V}}\cdot B
is continuous. By Proposition
3.7
,
{\cal{B}}^{WAP}
is introverted and Arens regular.

Let
{\cal{B}}
be a Boolean subalgebra of
{\mathboondoxfrak B}(G)
. Assume that
{\cal{B}}
is introverted and Arens regular, and let
B\in{\cal{B}}
. By Proposition
3.7
(ii) and Lemma
3.2
(iii),
\{{\mathcal{V}}\cdot B:{\mathcal{V}}\in\nu_{\mathcal{B}}G\}
is
\sigma_{\cal{B}}
-compact — therefore
\sigma_{\mathboondoxfrak B}
-compact — and contains
{\cal LO}_{G}(B)
; hence,
B\in{\cal{B}}^{WAP}
. By Corollary
3.8
, we have established the last statement of the theorem and that (iii) implies (ii). The implications (ii) implies (i) and (i) implies (iii) are consequences of Proposition
2.3
(iv), Remark
4.3
and Corollary
3.8
.
∎

Corollary 4.10
.

Let
B\in{\mathboondoxfrak B}(G)
. The following statements are equivalent:

(i)

B\in{\cal{B}}^{WAP}
;

(ii)

B\in{\cal{B}}^{LUC}
and
{\cal LO}_{\nu_{WAP}G}(B):=\{{\mathcal{V}}\cdot B:{\mathcal{V}}\in\nu_{WAP}G\}
is a
\sigma_{{\mathboondoxfrak B}}
-compact subset of
{\mathboondoxfrak B}(G)
;

(iii)

B\in{\cal{B}}^{RUC}
and
{\cal RO}_{G}(B)
is a relatively
\sigma_{{\mathboondoxfrak B}}
-compact subset of
{\mathboondoxfrak B}(G)
;

(iv)

B\in{\cal{B}}^{RUC}
and
{\cal RO}_{\nu_{WAP}G}(B):=\{B\cdot{\mathcal{U}}:{\mathcal{U}}\in\nu_{WAP}G\}
is a
\sigma_{{\mathboondoxfrak B}}
-compact subset of
{\mathboondoxfrak B}(G)
.

Proof.

By Theorem
4.9
and Proposition
3.7
(also from the proof of Theorem
4.9
), statement (ii) follows from statement (i) and, since
{\cal LO}_{G}(B)
is contained in
{\cal LO}_{\nu_{WAP}G}(B)
, statement (ii) implies statement (i). Also by Theorem
4.9
and Proposition
3.7
, statement (i) implies statement (iv), which in turn implies statement (iii) because
{\cal RO}_{G}(B)
is contained in
{\cal RO}_{\nu_{WAP}G}(B)
. Symmetric arguments to those found in the proof of Lemma
4.8
and Theorem
4.9
show that
{\cal{B}}^{RWAP}
, the collection of sets
B\in{\cal{B}}^{RUC}
for which
{\cal RO}_{G}(B)
is
\sigma_{{\mathboondoxfrak B}}
-compact, is an introverted Arens regular Boolean subalgebra of
{\mathboondoxfrak B}(G)
. Hence
{\cal{B}}^{RWAP}\subseteq{\cal{B}}^{WAP}
by Theorem
4.9
.
∎

Remark 4.11
.

For
B\in{\cal{B}}^{LUC}\cap{\cal{B}}^{RUC}
, we have established the equivalence of relative
\sigma_{{\mathboondoxfrak B}}
-compactness of
{\cal LO}(B)
and
{\cal RO}(B)
without help from the Grothendieck double-limit criteria, the Eberlein-Smulyan theorem, or any pre-existing theory of
WAP(G)
or
G^{WAP}
, (much of which relies on these and other deep theorems).

Corollary 4.12
.

The Arens regular introverted Boolean subalgebra
{\cal{B}}^{WAP}
of
{\mathboondoxfrak B}(G)
is inversion invariant and
(\nu_{WAP}G,\nu_{WAP})
is the universal totally disconnected involutive semigroup compactification of
G
.

Proof.

Let
B\in{\cal{B}}^{WAP}
. Then
B^{-1}\in{\cal{B}}^{RUC}
because
B\in{\cal{B}}^{LUC}
. Since
{\cal RO}(B^{-1})=\{C^{-1}:C\in{\cal LO}(B)\}={\cal LO}(B)^{-1}
,
{\cal RO}(B^{-1})
is relatively
\sigma_{{\mathboondoxfrak B}}
-compact by Lemma
3.6
(vii); by Corollary
4.10
,
B^{-1}\in{\cal{B}}^{WAP}
. By Proposition
3.11
,
(\nu_{WAP}G,\nu_{WAP})
is an involutive semigroup compactification of
G
. Universality follows from Proposition
3.10
and Theorem
4.9
.
∎

Remark 4.13
.

If
G
is a discrete group,
{\cal{B}}^{LUC}={\cal{B}}^{RUC}={\cal P}(G)={\mathboondoxfrak B}(G)
and
\beta G\equiv_{G}\nu_{{\mathboondoxfrak B}(G)}G
by
[
22
, Proposition 4.7(g)]
. Hence,
G^{LUC}\equiv_{G}\beta G\equiv_{G}\nu_{LUC}G
and therefore, by the main result in
[
17
]
,
Z_{t}(\nu_{LUC}G)=\nu_{LUC}(G)
. By Proposition
3.5
,
{\cal{B}}^{LUC}
is an example of a non-Arens regular introverted Boolean subalgebra of
{\mathboondoxfrak B}(G)
. In particular,
(\nu_{WAP}G,\nu_{WAP})\precneqq_{G}(\nu_{LUC}G,\nu_{LUC})
in this case.

Let
\Omega=\Omega(G)
denote the “open coset ring of
G
”, i.e., the Boolean subalgebra of
{\mathboondoxfrak B}(G)
generated by the open cosets in
G
.

Proposition 4.14
.

The open coset ring
\Omega
is an inversion-invariant, Arens regular, introverted Boolean subalgebra of
{\cal{B}}^{WAP}
. Thus,
(\nu_{\Omega}G,\nu_{\Omega})
is a totally disconnected involutive semitopological semigroup compactification of
G
and
(\nu_{\Omega}G,\nu_{\Omega})\preceq_{G}(\nu_{WAP}G,\nu_{WAP})
.

Proof.

We first show that
{\cal{B}}^{WAP}
contains
\Omega
. Taking
H
to be an open subgroup of
G
, it suffices to show that
H\in{\cal{B}}^{WAP}
; to this end we will establish that
{\cal C}:={\cal LO}(H)\cup\{\emptyset\}
is
\sigma_{\mathboondoxfrak B}
-compact: Let
(B_{i})_{i\in I}
be a net in
{\cal C}
. If
(B_{i})
contains a constant subnet, it contains a convergent subnet. Supposing that this is not the case, we
claim
that
B_{i}\rightarrow\emptyset
in
\sigma_{{\mathboondoxfrak B}}
. Let
{\mathcal{V}}\in\nu_{{\mathboondoxfrak B}}G
. Then
\emptyset\notin{\mathcal{V}}
. If
I_{0}:=\{i\in I:B_{i}\in{\mathcal{V}}\}
is cofinal in
I
, then, since the sets in
{\cal C}
are disjoint,
B_{i}=B
for some
B\in{\cal LO}(H)
and all
i\in I_{0}
, contradicting our assumption that
(B_{i})_{i\in I}
has no constant subnet. Hence,
B_{i}\notin{\mathcal{V}}
eventually, and the claim follows by Lemma
3.6
(ii). Hence,
{\cal C}
is
\sigma_{{\mathboondoxfrak B}}
-compact.

Obviously
\Omega
is
G
-invariant and inversion-invariant. To see that
\Omega
is left introverted, let
B\in\Omega
,
{\mathcal{V}}\in\nu_{\Omega}G
. Taking
{\mathcal{U}}\in\nu_{WAP}G
such that
{\mathcal{U}}\cap\Omega={\mathcal{V}}
(via Proposition
2.3
(iv)),
{\mathcal{V}}\cdot B={\mathcal{U}}\cdot B\in{\cal{B}}^{WAP}
; in particular
{\mathcal{V}}\cdot B\in{\mathboondoxfrak B}(G)
. Supposing now that
B
is a left coset of the open subgroup
H
of
G
, by Lemma
3.2
(iv) it suffices to show that
{\mathcal{V}}\cdot B
is an open coset. Let
r,s,t\in{\mathcal{V}}\cdot B
. Then each of
B\cdot r
,
B\cdot s
and
B\cdot t
are left cosets of
H
belonging to
{\mathcal{V}}
, so we must have
B\cdot r=B\cdot s=B\cdot t\in{\mathcal{V}}
. Therefore,
B\cdot(rs^{-1}t)=(B\cdot s)(s^{-1}t)=B\cdot t\in{\mathcal{V}}
, whence
rs^{-1}t\in{\mathcal{V}}\cdot B
. It follows that
{\mathcal{V}}\cdot B
is an open coset in
{\mathboondoxfrak B}(G)
, as needed. The proposition now follows from Theorem
4.9
.
∎

A topological group
G
is
non-Archimedean
if its open cosets form an open base for
G
. For example, every totally disconnected locally compact group is non-Archimedean
[
13
, Theorem 7.7]
. For more information about non-Archimedean topological groups, including many examples, the reader is referred to
[
20
]
.

Corollary 4.15
.

Let
G
be a non-Archimedean topological group. Then the semigroup compactifications
(\nu_{\Omega}G,\nu_{\Omega})
,
(\nu_{WAP}G,\nu_{WAP})
,
(\nu_{LUC}G,\nu_{LUC})
and
(\nu_{LMC}G,\nu_{LMC})
are all topologist’s compactifications of
G
.

Proof.

In this case,
\Omega
is an open base for
G
and
\Omega\subseteq{\cal{B}}^{WAP}\subseteq{\cal{B}}^{LUC}\subseteq{\cal{B}}^{LMC}
, so this is a consequence of Proposition
2.3
(ii).
∎

Remark 4.16
.

Our original version of Corollary
4.15
was stated only for totally disconnected locally compact groups and was proved in Section 5 with the aid of function-space arguments. We are grateful to the referee for suggesting the argument found in the first paragraph of the proof of Proposition
4.14
, and for bringing the class of non-Archimedean groups to our attention.

4.4
The universal TD topological group compactification of
G

Let
G
be a topological group. Let

{\cal{B}}^{AP}=\{B\in{\mathboondoxfrak B}(G):|G:{{}_{B}G}|<\infty\}

where
|G:H|
is the index of a subgroup
H
in
G
. Let
{{\cal N}_{f}}(G)
be the set of all finite-index closed normal subgroups of
G
.

Proposition 4.17
.

Let
B
be a subset of
G
. The following statements are equivalent:

(i)

B\in{\cal{B}}^{AP}
;

(ii)

B\in{\mathboondoxfrak B}(G)
and
{\cal RO}(B)
is finite;

(iii)

B\in{\mathboondoxfrak B}(G)
and
|G:G_{B}|
is finite;

(iv)

B\in{\mathboondoxfrak B}(G)
and
{\cal LO}(B)
is finite;

(v)

B=\emptyset
or
B
is a (finite) union of cosets of some
N\in{{\cal N}_{f}}(G)
.

Proof.

(i)
\Leftrightarrow
(ii): Let
T
be a complete set of right coset representatives of
{{}_{B}G}
without redundancies. Since
B\cdot s=B\cdot t
exactly when
{{}_{B}G}s={{}_{B}G}t
, the map
T\rightarrow{\cal RO}(B):t\mapsto B\cdot t
is a bijection.

(i)
\Rightarrow
(v): Suppose that
B\in{\cal{B}}^{AP}
is nonempty. Since
|G:{{}_{B}G}|
is finite,
{{}_{B}G}
has only finitely many conjugates and therefore its normal core in
G
,
N=\bigcap_{g\in G}g({{}_{B}G})g^{-1}
, is a finite-index normal subgroup of
G
. One can readily check that
{{}_{B}G}
is closed because
B
is so; hence
N\in{{\cal N}_{f}}(G)
. As
N
is contained in
{{}_{B}G}
,
B=NB
, and we have statement (v).

(v)
\Rightarrow
(i): Assume (v) and that
B
is nonempty. Then
N\in{\mathboondoxfrak B}(G)
and is contained in
{{}_{B}G}
, so
B\in{\cal{B}}^{AP}
.

A symmetric argument establishes the equivalence of statements (iii), (iv) and (v).
∎

Lemma 4.18
.

The collection
{\cal{B}}^{AP}
is an Arens regular introverted inversion-invariant Boolean subalgebra of
\Omega
, the open coset ring of
G
, (and therefore of
{\cal{B}}^{WAP}
).

Proof.

Since
{{\cal N}_{f}}(G)
is contained in
\Omega
, so too is
{\cal{B}}^{AP}
by Proposition
4.17
. Hence,
{\cal{B}}^{WAP}
contains
{\cal{B}}^{AP}
, so by Theorem
4.9
it suffices to show that
{\cal{B}}^{AP}
is an inversion-invariant left introverted Boolean subalgebra of
{\mathboondoxfrak B}(G)
. Clearly,
\emptyset,G\in{\cal{B}}^{AP}
. Let
B,C\in{\cal{B}}^{AP}
. Then
{{}_{B}G}\cap{{}_{C}G}
is a finite-index subgroup of
G
that is contained in both
{}_{B\cap C}G
and
{}_{B\cup C}G
. It follows that
B\cap C,B\cup C\in{\cal{B}}^{AP}
. Also,
{}_{G\backslash B}G={{}_{B}G}
, so
G\backslash B\in{\cal{B}}^{AP}
and
B^{-1}\in{\cal{B}}^{AP}
by Proposition
4.17
(v). Thus,
{\cal{B}}^{AP}
is an inversion-invariant Boolean subalgebra of
{\mathboondoxfrak B}(G)
and, since
{\cal LO}(t\cdot B)={\cal LO}(B)
and
{\cal RO}(B\cdot t)={\cal RO}(B)
, Proposition
4.17
also tells us that
{\cal{B}}^{AP}
is
G
-invariant. Finally, let
{\mathcal{V}}\in\nu_{AP}G
,
B\in{\cal{B}}^{AP}
. Then
B\in{\cal{B}}^{LUC}
, so
{\mathcal{V}}\cdot B\in{\mathboondoxfrak B}(G)
by Lemma
4.2
(iii). By Lemma
3.2
(ii)
{{}_{B}G}\leq_{{\mathcal{V}}\cdot B}G
, so
{\mathcal{V}}\cdot B\in{\cal{B}}^{AP}
.
∎

Thus,
(\nu_{AP}G,\nu_{AP})
is a semitopological semigroup compactification of
G
. For the next lemma, note that because
{\cal{B}}^{AP}\subseteq{\cal{B}}^{LUC}
,
{{}_{B}G}\in{\cal{B}}^{AP}
whenever
B\in{\cal{B}}^{AP}
.

Lemma 4.19
.

Let
{\mathcal{U}},{\mathcal{V}}\in\nu_{AP}G
,
B\in{\cal{B}}^{AP}
. Then
B\in{\mathcal{U}}{\scriptscriptstyle\square}{\mathcal{V}}
if and only if
B\cdot a\in{\mathcal{V}}
and
{{}_{B}G}a\in{\mathcal{U}}
for some
a\in G
.

Proof.

Let
T
be a complete (finite) set of right coset representatives of
{{}_{B}G}
without redundancies. Letting
T_{\mathcal{V}}=\{a\in T:B\cdot a\in{\mathcal{V}}\}
,
{\mathcal{V}}\cdot B=\mathop{\vphantom{\bigcup}\mathchoice{\vtop{\halign{\hfil$\m@th\displaystyle#$\hfil\cr\bigcup\cr\cdot\crcr}}}{\vtop{\halign{\hfil$\m@th\textstyle#$\hfil\cr\bigcup\cr\cdot\crcr}}}{\vtop{\halign{\hfil$\m@th\scriptstyle#$\hfil\cr\bigcup\cr\cdot\crcr}}}{\vtop{\halign{\hfil$\m@th\scriptscriptstyle#$\hfil\cr\bigcup\cr\cdot\crcr}}}}\displaylimits_{a\in T_{\mathcal{V}}}{{}_{B}G}a
because
{\cal RO}(B)=\{B\cdot a:a\in T\}
and
B\cdot s=B\cdot a
exactly when
s\in{{}_{B}G}a
. Therefore, if
B\in{\mathcal{U}}{\scriptscriptstyle\square}{\mathcal{V}}
,
{\mathcal{V}}\cdot B=\mathop{\vphantom{\bigcup}\mathchoice{\vtop{\halign{\hfil$\m@th\displaystyle#$\hfil\cr\bigcup\cr\cdot\crcr}}}{\vtop{\halign{\hfil$\m@th\textstyle#$\hfil\cr\bigcup\cr\cdot\crcr}}}{\vtop{\halign{\hfil$\m@th\scriptstyle#$\hfil\cr\bigcup\cr\cdot\crcr}}}{\vtop{\halign{\hfil$\m@th\scriptscriptstyle#$\hfil\cr\bigcup\cr\cdot\crcr}}}}\displaylimits_{a\in T_{\mathcal{V}}}{{}_{B}G}a
belongs to the (prime) ultrafilter
{\mathcal{U}}
, so

5
TD semigroup compactifications as Gelfand compactifications

Let
G
be a topological group. Until now, we have developed all properties of totally disconnected compactifications without employing function spaces. However, every semigroup compactification of
G
is, up to equivalence, a Gelfand compactification, i.e., the Gelfand spectrum of a left
m
-introverted
C^{*}
-subalgebra of
CB(G)
. In this section we describe the relationship between the totally disconnected semigroup compactifications
(\nu_{\mathcal{B}}G,\nu_{\mathcal{B}})
and Gelfand compactifications of
G
, and we identify
(\nu_{*}G,\nu_{*})
with quotients of the familiar compactifications
(G^{*},\delta_{*})
, where
*
is
LMC
,
LUC
,
WAP
or
AP
.

For the convenience of the reader, we recall some basic definitions and facts from the Gelfand theory of commutative
C^{*}
-algebras, e.g., see
[
16
]
. Taking
A
to be a commutative, unital Banach algebra, its Gelfand spectrum,
\Delta(A)
, is the set of all non-zero algebra homomorphisms of
A
into
\mathbb{C}
endowed with the topology of pointwise convergence on
A
, (equivalently the relative weak
∗
-topology inherited from the dual space
A^{*}
), with respect to which
\Delta(A)
is a compact Hausdorff space. The Gelfand representation
\Gamma:A\rightarrow CB(\Delta(A)):a\mapsto\hat{a}
, defined by
\hat{a}(\varphi)=\varphi(a)
for
a\in A
and
\varphi\in\Delta(A)
, is a norm-decreasing algebra homomorphism. In the case that
A
is a unital commutative
C^{*}
-algebra,
\Gamma
is an isometric
*
-isomorphism of
A
onto
CB(\Delta(A))

[
16
, Theorem 2.4.5]
.

Let
X
be a topological space,
{\cal{B}}
a Boolean subalgebra of
{\mathboondoxfrak B}(X)
. Let
{\cal S}^{\cal{B}}(X)
be the
\|\cdot\|_{\infty}
-closed linear span of

in

, a

-subalgebra of

since

is self-adjoint and closed under multiplication. Letting

and
\delta_{\cal{B}}:X\rightarrow{\Delta_{\cal{B}}}
the point evaluation map, the Gelfand transform
\Gamma:{\cal S}^{\cal{B}}(X)\rightarrow CB({\Delta_{\cal{B}}}):f\mapsto\hat{f}
is an isometric
*
-isomorphism with
\Gamma^{-1}=j_{\delta_{\cal{B}}}:CB({\Delta_{\cal{B}}})\rightarrow{\cal S}^{\cal{B}}(X):g\mapsto g\circ\delta_{\cal{B}}
. Thus,

is generated by its idempotents

, and it follows that

separates the points of
{\Delta_{\cal{B}}}
. Consequently,
({\Delta_{\cal{B}}},\delta_{\cal{B}})
is a totally disconnected compactification of

.

For

, let
{\mathcal{U}}_{m}=\{B\in{\cal{B}}:m(1_{B})=1\}
. For

,

, which can readily be used to show that

is an ultrafilter on

, i.e.,

. (For example, for

,

or

, so

by Corollary
5.2
. Letting

,
m_{\mathcal{U}}\cdot 1_{B}(s)=m_{\mathcal{U}}(1_{B\cdot s})=1
if and only if
B\cdot s\in{\mathcal{U}}
, i.e., exactly when

; otherwise

, which happens exactly when

, an idempotent in
{\cal S}^{\cal{B}}(G)
; that
{\mathcal{U}}\cdot B\in{\cal{B}}
follows from Corollary
5.2
. Conversely, suppose that
{\cal{B}}
is left introverted and let
m\in{\Delta_{\cal{B}}}
,
B\in{\cal{B}}
,
s\in G
. Then

and one can check that

, so
m\cdot 1_{B}\in{\cal S}^{\cal{B}}(G)
. By linearity and continuity of the maps
{\cal S}^{\cal{B}}(G)\rightarrow\ell^{\infty}(G):f\mapsto s\cdot f,f\cdot s,m\cdot f
,

is left
m
-introverted.

In either case,

is a compactification isomorphism of semigroup compactifications, so it is automatically a semigroup homomorphism. However, this is also easy to see directly:
B\in{\mathcal{U}}_{m}{\scriptscriptstyle\square}\,{\mathcal{U}}_{n}
if and only if
{\mathcal{U}}_{n}\cdot B\in{\mathcal{U}}_{m}
, which happens exactly when
1=m(1_{{\mathcal{U}}_{n}\cdot B})=m(n\cdot 1_{B})=m{\scriptscriptstyle\square}n(1_{B})
; this happens if and only if

.
∎

Proposition 5.5
.

. Then

(ii)

(a)(ii). ∎

Theorem 5.6
.

Let

(i)

(ii)

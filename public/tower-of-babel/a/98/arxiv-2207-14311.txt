Scale-separated AdS 3 vacua from G 2 G_{2} -orientifolds using bispinors
Vincent Van Hemelryck

Abstract
In this paper, minimal supersymmetric AdS 3 solutions on G 2 G_{2} -orientifolds of type IIA and IIB supergravity are studied. These are obtained by techniques of the bispinor formalism. A straightforward argument is derived against AdS 3 solutions in massless type IIA string theory on such G 2 G_{2} -backgrounds. Furthermore, it is shown that some of the AdS 3 solutions in massive type IIA do admit a separation of scales, whereas the same cannot be said for the type IIB vacua. Finally, a formula for the 3d superpotential in the bispinor language is given that is valid for both type IIA and type IIB configurations. It is shown that the supersymmetry conditions of this superpotential lead back to the bispinor equations.



1
Introduction

The swampland program sheds new light on the field of string compactifications. Its aim of delineating which low energy effective field theories (EFTs) can be completed into a consistent UV theory of quantum gravity and which not, has led to many different insights. However, the criteria for EFTs to belong to the Swampland are formulated as conjectures. Some of these conjectures have been extensively studied in the context of ten-dimensional string theory, e.g. the No Global Symmetries conjecture
Banks:1988yz
;
Banks:2010zn
, the Weak Gravity Conjecture
ArkaniHamed:2006dz
, etc. for which no counterexamples have been found and arguments close to a proof have been formulated
Harlow:2018jwu
;
Harlow:2018tng
;
Montero:2018fns
. Unfortunately other conjectures, with more phenomenological impact, do not share the same fate. An example is the strong AdS distance conjecture (ADC)
Lust:2019zwm
, which forbids supersymmetric AdS vacua that exhibit a feature called scale separation. Similar statements were already raised before in
Gautason:2015tig
;
Gautason:2018gln
and more recently in
Collins:2022nux
.
A string compactification is said to be scale-separated whenever its Kaluza-Klein (KK) energy scale is much larger than the cosmological constant scale. For phenomenological purposes, this feature is vital, as only scale-separated vacua can be considered genuinely lower-dimensional.
It has been shown for
d=4
that scale separation is in tension with the magnetic weak gravity conjecture for
\mathcal{N}>1
supersymmetric AdS
d
vacua purely from the lower-dimensional gauged supergravity point of view
Cribiori:2022trc
. Similar arguments could rule out any supersymmetric
d>4
theory, but has to be investigated further.
For
d\leq 4
the situation is a bit more involved. Indeed, there are a couple of counterexamples to the ADC. In type IIB string theory one has e.g. the four-dimensional supersymmetric KKLT
Kachru:2003aw
vacuum where scale separation is achieved by a small flux superpotential, where one has to rely on non-perturbative physics to stabilise the moduli. Finding vacua with such a small flux superpotential has been the topic of many recent works
Demirtas:2019sip
;
Demirtas:2020ffz
;
Alvarez-Garcia:2020pxd
;
Demirtas:2021nlu
;
Demirtas:2021ote
;
Gendler:2022qof
;
Blumenhagen:2022dbo
, but has been criticised in
Lust:2022lfc
;
Blumenhagen:2022dbo
. Another class of models with apparent scale separation is the (non-supersymmetric) Large Volume Scenario (LVS)
Balasubramanian:2005zx
;
Conlon:2005ki
;
Cicoli:2007xp
;
Cicoli:2008va
, although it lately has been criticised in
Junghans:2022exo
;
Junghans:2022kxg
;
Gao:2022fdi
;
Gao:2022uop
.

In type massive IIA string theory, the celebrated four-dimensional DGKT construction
DeWolfe:2005uu
also serves as a counterexample to the strong ADC (however see
Buratti:2020kda
for a modification to the conjecture). There all the moduli are stabilised with classical ingredients such as smeared orientifold-6 (O6-)planes on a
T^{6}/\mathbb{Z}_{3}\times\mathbb{Z}_{3}
orbifold. More generally this can be extended to other constructions on manifolds with SU(3)-structure in type IIA. Such solutions were pioneered by
Lust:2004ig
, furthermore studied in references like
Behrndt:2004km
;
Behrndt:2004mj
;
Camara:2005dc
and extended in other works, e.g.
Grana:2006kf
;
Caviezel:2008ik
;
Lust:2009mb
;
Lust:2009zb
and many others. See also
Andriot:2022way
;
Andriot:2022yyj
for an extensive search of scale-separated solutions amongst type II vacua on group manifolds. The fact that these models have no M-theory uplift and that O6-planes are treated as non-local objects has led to a lot of debate about the consistency of these solutions. Advancements on the latter issue have been made, treating the O6-planes as local sources by using a perturbative scheme
Junghans:2020acz
;
Marchesano:2020qvg
. Furthermore, a double T-dual version of
DeWolfe:2005uu
has been studied on the Iwasawa nilmanifold in type IIA
Cribiori:2021djm
. These solutions have a separation of the KK and AdS scale, but not of the curvature scales as expected from
Tsimpis:2012tu
;
Andriot:2018tmb
and hence circumventing the nogo-theorem formulated in
Gautason:2015tig
. The strongly coupled solutions of
Cribiori:2021djm
can then further be uplifted to M-theory. Such M-theory solutions also exhibit scale separation and predict the existence of seven-dimensional positively curved Einstein manifolds where the Kaluza-Klein length scale also decouples from the curvature scale. Thus the uplift circumvents the nogo-theorem of
Gautason:2015tig
as well and goes against the claims of
Collins:2022nux
.

The ultimate goal is to settle the debate by the use of holography. Indeed, the CFT
3
dual statement of scale separation is quite simple: it requires that the CFT
3
has a parametrically large gap. Whether such CFTs exist has not been settled yet, but pieces of the puzzle have been studied in e.g.
Polchinski:2009ch
;
Alday:2019qrf
. The first steps towards studying the putative CFT
3
duals of the models described above can be found in
Conlon:2018vov
;
Conlon:2020wmc
;
Conlon:2021cjk
;
Apers:2022zjx
;
Apers:2022tfm
. This is often referred to as the ‘holographic swampland’ program. However, CFT
2
’s are easier to study and hence it would be interesting to test the same statements for AdS
3

An example of such scale-separated vacua are compactifications of massive type IIA on manifolds with
G_{2}
-holonomy, studied in
Farakos:2020phe
;
Farakos:2020idt
. These can be regarded as the 3d analogue of the 4d DGKT solutions. These setups also contain O6-planes, wrapping 4-cycles in the 7d manifold. For some particular choices of the four-form fluxes, scale separation can be achieved in these models as well. A similar construction has been considered in type IIB on manifolds with
G_{2}
-structure but scale separation could not be obtained

.
In order to obtain all these vacuum solutions, the authors had to go through the process of dimensionally reducing the ten-dimensional type IIA/B action, constructing the scalar potential, deriving a 3d real superpotential from that and then minimising this superpotential (in 4d referred to as ‘solving the F-term equations’) to obtain supersymmetric solutions. This was a quite laborious procedure, and the main point of this paper is to demonstrate that such solutions can be found more elegantly by considering the bispinor equations of
Dibitetto:2018ftj
. These techniques were already applied to
\mathcal{N}=1

-vacua in

;

. In the
\mathcal{N}=1

In section
2

G_{2}
-structure are reviewed. In section
3
this is applied to type IIA backgrounds, in section
4
for type IIB ones. Section
5
discusses the 3d superpotential in the bispinor formalism and its variations are shown to be equivalent with the bispinor equations.

2
Bispinors in 7d

A standard way to find vacuum solutions in string theory is dimensionally reducing the ten-dimensional theory to a lower dimensional effective field theory (EFT) and then minimising the scalar potential
V
for the moduli. If one insists on keeping some supersymmetry, it is useful to express the scalar potential in terms of a superpotential. A supersymmetric solution is then found by minimising this superpotential.

However, one can alternatively work directly in ten dimensions and find vacuum solutions by solving the 10d vacuum equations of motion. The solutions one obtains in this way are not necessarily supersymmetric either.
Fortunately it has been known that solving the supersymmetry conditions or Killing spinor equations and imposing the Bianchi identities for the RR fluxes is equivalent to solving the equations of motion and guarantees supersymmetry (see
Tomasiello:2022dwe
and references therein for an extensive treatment). Furthermore, these Killing spinor equations can be recast into form- rather than spinor equations, and these are called the bispinor equations. In this formalism, all the data from the metric is encoded into the bispinors. Many works on this technology have appeared in the context of 4d, i.e.
Grana:2004bg
;
Grana:2005sn
;
Grana:2006kf
;
Tomasiello:2007zq
;
Koerber:2007xk
. More recently, a similar exercise has been made for three dimensions in
Dibitetto:2018ftj
and was applied to many different backgrounds in e.g.
Passias:2019rga
;
Passias:2020ubv
;
Couzens:2022agr
. The basics about these setups are reviewed in the remainder of this section, which relies on
Passias:2019rga
.
The 3d compactifications of interest have the following 10d product metric in string frame

\mathrm{d}s_{10}^{2}=e^{2A}\mathrm{d}s_{3}^{2}+\mathrm{d}s_{7}^{2},

(1)

where the warp factor
e^{A}
is a function on the 7d space.
The 10d spinors of type IIA/B are therefore accordingly decomposed into a three-dimensional (
\zeta
) and seven-dimensional (
\eta_{1,2}
) part, being

\epsilon_{1}=\zeta\otimes\eta_{1}\otimes\begin{pmatrix}1\\
-i\end{pmatrix},\qquad\epsilon_{2}=\zeta\otimes\eta_{2}\otimes\begin{pmatrix}1\\
\mp i\end{pmatrix}.

(2)

The upper (lower) sign corresponds to type IIA (IIB) string theory. In a supersymmetric AdS
3
background, the external 3d spinor
\zeta
has to satisfy

\nabla_{\nu}\zeta=\frac{\mu}{2}\gamma_{\nu}\zeta,

(3)

where
\mu
parametrises the AdS scale in string units, i.e.
\mu^{2}=L_{\text{AdS}}^{-2}
and
\Lambda=-2\mu^{2}
. Evidently one should take
\mu=0
for Minkowski backgrounds.
The supersymmetry conditions that involve these spinors, can be written in terms of polyform equations. Indeed, the bispinor constructed out of
\eta_{1}
and
\eta_{2}
can be related through the Clifford map to an even polyform
\Psi_{+}
and an odd
\Psi_{-}
which are often called the bispinors:

\eta_{1}\otimes\eta_{2}^{\dagger}=\Psi_{+}=i\Psi_{-}.

(4)

Unlike the for 6d Euclidean spinors,
\Psi_{+}
and
\Psi_{-}
in 7d are related to each other through the Clifford map, i.e.

\star\lambda\Psi_{\pm}=\mp\Psi_{\mp}.

(5)

The
\star
is the usual Hodge operator and
\lambda
represents the reversal operator on a
p
-form with
\lambda A_{p}=(-1)^{\lfloor\frac{p}{2}\rfloor}A_{p}
.
These bispinors encode all the information of the 7d internal metric and are subject to the normalisation condition

\left<\Psi_{+},\Psi_{-}\right>=\frac{e^{2A}}{8}\text{vol}_{7},

(6)

where
\text{vol}_{7}
is the volume form (not with unit volume). Also,
\left<\Xi,\Upsilon\right>=(\Xi\wedge\lambda\Upsilon)|_{\text{top}}
is the Mukai-Chevally pairing for the two polyforms
\Xi
and
\Upsilon
. In 7d, the
\star\lambda
- and pairing operator have useful properties:

\left<\Xi,\Upsilon\right>=-\left<\Upsilon,\Xi\right>,\qquad\left<\star\lambda\Xi,\star\lambda\Upsilon\right>=\left<\Xi,\Upsilon\right>,\qquad(\star\lambda)^{2}=-1.

(7)

As said before, the supersymmetry conditions for type II theories can be reformulated in terms of the bispinors. It is a laborious procedure, but the result is rather elegant. For orientifold vacua, for which the two spinors
\eta_{1,2}
have the same norm, they are
Dibitetto:2018ftj
:

\displaystyle\mathrm{d}_{H}(e^{A-\phi}\Psi_{\mp})=0

(8)

\displaystyle\mathrm{d}_{H}(e^{2A-\phi}\Psi_{\pm})\mp 2\mu e^{A-\phi}\Psi_{\mp}=\frac{1}{8}e^{3A}\star\lambda F

(9)

\displaystyle\left<\Psi_{\mp},F\right>=\mp\frac{\mu}{2}e^{-\phi}\text{vol}_{7},

(10)

where the upper (lower) sign has to be taken for type IIA (IIB),
\mathrm{d}_{H}=\mathrm{d}-H\wedge
is the
H
-twisted exterior derivative and the fluxes
F
have only legs in the 7 internal dimensions.
The last equation, (
10
), is referred to as the pairing equation and is absent for 4d supergravities.
Supplemented with the RR Bianchi identities,

\mathrm{d}_{H}F=j_{\text{source}},

(11)

the bispinor equations solve the 10d equations of motion. The beauty of this system of equations is that the local sources like D-branes or O-planes only directly affect the Bianchi identities and not the bispinor equations. However, since O-planes do project out certain fluxes and forms, one has to make sure that the chosen orbifold and orientifold involutions are compatible with the bispinors
\Psi_{\pm}
of a given group structure.

In the case of interest, being a 7d manifold with
G_{2}
-structure, the bispinors are
Dibitetto:2018ftj
;
Passias:2019rga

\Psi_{-}=\frac{e^{A}}{8}\left(\Phi-\text{vol}_{7}\right),\qquad\Psi_{+}=\frac{e^{A}}{8}\left(1-\star\Phi\right),

(12)

where
\Phi
is the
G_{2}
-invariant three-form. Together with its associate four-form
\Psi=\star\Phi
it can be used to reconstruct the metric by
Tomasiello:2022dwe

g_{mn}=\frac{1}{24}\Phi_{mpq}\Phi_{nrs}\Psi^{prqs}.

(13)

The last object
\Psi^{prqs}
has to be seen as a ‘quadruvector’ whose elements are defined by
\Psi^{pqrs}=-(\Psi_{pqrs})^{-1}
. Both the three- and four-form can be parametrised in terms of the vielbein one-forms
e^{a}
,
a=1...7
as

\displaystyle\Phi

\displaystyle=e^{147}+e^{257}+e^{367}+e^{123}-e^{345}+e^{246}-e^{156}

(14)

\displaystyle\star\Phi

\displaystyle=e^{2356}+e^{1346}+e^{1245}+e^{4567}-e^{1267}+e^{1357}-e^{2347}.

Whenever the three-form and its associate four-form are not closed, their departure from non-closure can be parametrised by torsion classes
W_{j}
which are classified in in the
G_{2}
-irreducible representations. These comprise the trivial
1
-, the fundamental
7
-, the adjoint
14
- and symmetric traceless
27
-representations which correspond to the torsion classes
W_{1}
,
W_{7}
,
W_{14}
and
W_{27}
respectively. Such torsion classes are often referred to as geometric flux. In summary, non-closure of the three- and four-form is given by

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\mathrm{d}\Phi=W_{1}\star\Phi+W_{7}\wedge\Phi+W_{27}\\
&\mathrm{d}\star\Phi=\frac{4}{3}W_{7}\wedge\star\Phi+W_{14}\wedge\Phi.\end{split}

(15)

The
W_{1}
- and
W_{7}
-torsion classes are a 0- and 1-form respectively, and the
W_{14}
and
W_{27}
are a 2- and 4-form that satisfy

\star W_{14}=W_{14}\wedge\Phi,\qquad W_{14}\wedge\star\Phi=0,\qquad W_{27}\wedge\Phi=0=W_{27}\wedge\star\Phi.

(16)

We now established the necessary tools to discuss AdS
3
solutions with
G_{2}
-structure, alike
Farakos:2020phe
;
Emelin:2021gzx
, in the language of the bispinors. This will be discussed in the next sections.

3
Type IIA AdS
3
solutions

In this section we look at AdS
3
backgrounds in type IIA with bispinor techniques. Some of these setups were previously considered in
Farakos:2020phe
. A beautiful feature of the bispinor language is that the bispinor equations leave us with conditions on the fluxes and geometric forms for which the presence of sources like O-planes is not directly invoked. Besides the requirement that they have to be compatible with a
G_{2}
-structure and preserve the right amount of supersymmetry, the sources only play an explicit role in the Bianchi identities. This allows us to make general statements about the solution without specifying the sources at the beginning.

When we consider type IIA supergravity, the first bispinor equation (
8
) with the
G_{2}
-structure expressions for
\Psi_{\pm}
(
12
) implies that

\displaystyle\mathrm{d}\left(e^{2A-\phi}\Phi\right)=0,\qquad\Phi\wedge H=0.

(17)

On top of that, by the virtue of (
9
) the RR fluxes appear to be

\displaystyle e^{3A}\star F_{6}=-\mathrm{d}\left(e^{3A-\phi}\right)

(18)

\displaystyle e^{3A}\star F_{4}=-e^{3A-\phi}H-2\mu e^{2A-\phi}\Phi

(19)

\displaystyle e^{3A}\star F_{2}=\mathrm{d}\left(e^{3A-\phi}\star\Phi\right)

(20)

\displaystyle e^{3A}\star F_{0}=e^{3A-\phi}H\wedge\star\Phi+2\mu e^{2A-\phi}\text{vol}_{7}

(21)

The pairing equation (
10
) puts additional constraints on the fluxes, i.e.

\displaystyle\Phi\wedge F_{4}-F_{0}\text{vol}_{7}=-4\mu e^{-A-\phi}\text{vol}_{7}.

(22)

Furthermore, it is interesting to combine eq. (
19
), (
21
) and (
22
) to obtain

H\wedge\star\Phi=-6\mu e^{-A}\text{vol}_{7},\qquad e^{\phi}F_{0}=-4\mu e^{-A}.

(23)

From the latter condition we derive that
\textrm{e}^{\phi+A}
is a constant, since
F_{0}
needs to be a quantised constant. More importantly we conclude that

There are no supersymmetric AdS
3
compactifications of massless type IIA string theory with
G_{2}
-structure.

This is because massless type IIA needs
F_{0}=0
and that requires
\mu=0
through eq. (
23
), leaving us only with Minkowski solutions in massless type IIA.
For the 4d DGKT vacua in massive type IIA on manifolds with SU(3)-structure it was possible to perform two T-dualities and end up with vacua in massless type IIA, again on manifolds with SU(3)-structure
Caviezel:2008ik
;
Cribiori:2021djm
. This scenario is therefore not possible in 3d vacua from manifolds with
G_{2}
-structure.

In what follows we are mainly interested in setups with smeared sources.
For those the warping and dilaton profile are trivial. From eq. (
17
), this means that all torsion classes except
W_{14}
vanish:

\mathrm{d}\Phi=0,\qquad\mathrm{d}\star\Phi=W_{14}\wedge\Phi.

(24)

These are the so-called closed
G_{2}
-structures. A manifold with such
W_{14}
torsion cannot be a toroidal orbifold
DallAgata:2005zlf
, but it can be realised as a compact solvmanifold
Fernandez:2018aaa
;
Fino:2021
. By (
20
) the inclusion of
W_{14}
also means that
F_{2}=e^{-\phi}W_{14}
.

Smeared O-planes have to be introduced to solve the Bianchi identities, and hence the equations of motion. In its most general form, they are:

\displaystyle\mathrm{d}F_{0}=0,\qquad

\displaystyle\mathrm{d}F_{2}=HF_{0}+j_{\text{O6/D6}},

(25)

\displaystyle\mathrm{d}F_{4}=H\wedge F_{2},\qquad

\displaystyle\mathrm{d}F_{6}=H\wedge F_{4}+j_{\text{O2/D2}}.

However, it turns out that turning on
W_{14}
and hence
F_{2}
does not solve the supersymmetric equations of motion for AdS
3
backgrounds. One can see this more easily from the on-shell scalar potential one obtains from dimensional reduction. By definition it must be equal to
V_{\text{on-shell}}=-\mu^{2}M_{\textrm{Pl}}^{3}
, however with the inclusion of
W_{14}
this rather becomes
V_{\text{on-shell}}=(-\mu^{2}+\left|W_{14}\right|^{2})M_{\textrm{Pl}}^{3}
, the second contribution coming from the curvature
R_{7}
- and
F_{2}
-contribution to the scalar potential. It should be cancelled by the negative energy of localised sources, in this case spacetime-filling O4-planes, but such sources have no calibrated two-cycles to wrap in the
G_{2}
-compactification at hand
Fino:2021
. Hence it is only consistent to put
W_{14}=0
. As a consequence, all
14
-components of the fluxes are projected out.

From now on we write
e^{\phi}=g_{s}
and put
e^{A}=1
without loss of generality for the rest of this section.
One remarks from (
18
) that with these quantities being constant,
F_{6}
necessarily vanishes.

A toroidal orientifold example and scale separation

As a concrete example we consider a toroidal orientifold
T^{7}/\mathbb{Z}_{2}\times\mathbb{Z}_{2}\times\mathbb{Z}_{2}
with
G_{2}
-structure as was studied in
Farakos:2020phe
. This is a seven-dimensional orbifold that has orbifold singularities that can be chosen to be blown up. For such toroidal orbifolds it is known that the torsion class
W_{14}
vanishes as stated before
DallAgata:2005zlf
. The three orbifold actions can be parametrised as follows:

\displaystyle\Theta_{\alpha}\left(e^{1},e^{2},e^{3},e^{4},e^{5},e^{6},e^{7}\right)=\left(e^{1},e^{2},e^{3},e^{4},-e^{5},-e^{6},-e^{7}\right)

(26)

\displaystyle\Theta_{\beta}\left(e^{1},e^{2},e^{3},e^{4},e^{5},e^{6},e^{7}\right)=\left(-e^{1},-e^{2},e^{3},e^{4},e^{5},-e^{6},-e^{7}\right)

(27)

\displaystyle\Theta_{\gamma}\left(e^{1},e^{2},e^{3},e^{4},e^{5},e^{6},e^{7}\right)=\left(-e^{1},e^{2},-e^{3},e^{4},-e^{5},e^{6},-e^{7}\right).

(28)

There are 7 sets of O6-planes that are generated by these orbifold actions and the following orientifold action:

\sigma\left(e^{1},e^{2},e^{3},e^{4},e^{5},e^{6},e^{7}\right)=\left(-e^{1},-e^{2},-e^{3},-e^{4},-e^{5},-e^{6},-e^{7}\right).

(29)

It also generates spacetime-filling O2-planes.
The O6-planes source the
F_{2}
Bianchi identity, the O2-planes the one of
F_{6}
as in (
25
). We allow for D2-branes to potentially cancel the O2-charge.

For the remainder of this section, it is useful to write the fluxes and geometric forms in terms of the three-form basis
\{\Phi_{i}\}
and four-form basis
\{\Psi_{i}\}
for
i=1,...,7
that satisfy

\Phi_{i}\wedge\Psi_{j}=\delta_{ij}\tilde{\star}1,\qquad\Phi_{i}\wedge\Phi_{j}=0,\qquad\Psi_{i}\wedge\Psi_{j}=0.

(30)

Unit volume form is understood for
\tilde{\star}1
. The
G_{2}
-invariant three-form can then be decomposed in the three-form basis as

\Phi=s^{i}\Phi_{i},

(31)

where the
s_{i}
are the geometric moduli which can be reformulated into the radii of the different circles in the
T^{7}
. With these moduli, another useful identity is

\star\Phi_{i}=\frac{V_{7}}{(s^{i})^{2}}\Psi_{i},

(32)

with
V_{7}=\left(\Pi_{i=1}^{7}s^{i}\right)^{1/3}
the volume.
The fluxes can similarly be decomposed in the three- and four-form basis:

H=h^{i}\Phi_{i},\qquad F_{4}=F_{4A}+F_{4B}=f_{A}^{i}\Psi_{i}+f_{B}^{i}\Psi_{i},

(33)

with
F_{4A}\wedge H=0,F_{4B}\wedge H\neq 0
. In other words,
F_{4A}
does not participate in the
F_{6}
Bianchi identity whereas
F_{4B}
does.
With all of these redefinitions, the bispinor equations become the algebraic conditions

\displaystyle g_{s}(f_{A}^{i}+f_{B}^{i})\frac{(s^{i})^{2}}{V_{7}}=-h^{i}-2\mu s^{i}\quad\text{no sum}

(34)

\displaystyle g_{s}F_{0}=\sum_{i}\left(\frac{h^{i}}{s^{i}}\right)+2\mu

(35)

\displaystyle g_{s}\sum_{i}(f_{A}^{i}+f_{B}^{i})s^{i}-g_{s}F_{0}V_{7}=-4\mu V_{7}.

(36)

It is now time to look whether the equations above exhibit a scaling symmetry with the unconstrained four-form flux quanta.
Let us simplify by assuming that all the moduli
s^{i}
scale in the same manner, which we will refer to as uniform scalings. Then
s^{i}\sim V_{7}^{3/7}
. We make the same assumption for the
h_{i}
’s,
f_{A}^{i}
’s and
f_{B}^{i}
’s.
We know that
F_{0}
should not scale. Then from the
F_{2}
Bianchi identity we know that the
h^{i}
’s should not scale either, since the O6 source cannot scale. This then means that the
f_{B}^{i}
’s can scale neither, seen from the
F_{6}
Bianchi identity. By investigation of the equations above, we notice that there is no scaling symmetry unless all the
f_{B}^{i}
vanish, and hence
F_{4}
does not participate in the
F_{6}
Bianchi identity. But the O2-planes and D2-branes do, so we can only allow for setups with eight times more O2s than D2s such that their total charge vanishes. With this requirement, a scaling symmetry of the bispinor equations and Bianchi identities can be found with a new scaling parameter
f_{A}
:

\displaystyle f_{A}^{i}\sim f_{A},\qquad

\displaystyle F_{0}\sim f_{A}^{0},\qquad

\displaystyle h^{i}\sim f_{A}^{0}.

(37)

\displaystyle g_{s}\sim f_{A}^{-3/4},\qquad

\displaystyle\mu\sim f_{A}^{-3/4},\qquad

\displaystyle s^{i}\sim f_{A}^{3/4}.

When we compare the KK-scale to the AdS scale, we find

\frac{L_{\text{KK}}^{2}}{L_{\text{AdS}}^{2}}=V_{7}^{2/7}\mu^{2}\sim f^{-1}

(38)

which goes to zero in the large four-form flux limit and hence scale separation is achieved once a solution can be found.

Let us now look at the same example as in
Farakos:2020phe
with the following flux choices

\displaystyle\tilde{f}^{i}=f_{A}(-1,-1,-1,-1,-1,-1,6),\qquad h^{i}=h(1,1,1,1,1,1,1).

(39)

This leads to a six-fold symmetry for the
s^{i}
moduli in the first six directions, but not the seventh. This is parametrised by

s^{i}=(s,s,s,s,s,s,s_{7}).

(40)

With the introduction of the following non-scaling quantities

\displaystyle m=\frac{g_{s}F_{0}}{2\mu},\qquad

\displaystyle f=\frac{hg_{s}f_{A}}{(2\mu)^{2}V_{7}}.

(41)

\displaystyle x=2\mu\frac{s}{h},\qquad

\displaystyle y=2\mu\frac{s_{7}}{h},

the algebraic equations (
34
)-(
36
) become the following simple set of equations

\displaystyle 1+x-fx^{2}=0,\qquad

\displaystyle(m-1)xy-6y-x=0.

(42)

\displaystyle 1+y+6fy^{2}=0,\qquad

\displaystyle 6f(y-x)-m+2=0.

This system can be solved explicitly, with solutions:

\displaystyle m=-2,\qquad

\displaystyle f=\frac{1}{49}\left(-10+\sqrt{2}\right)

(43)

\displaystyle x=-3-\sqrt{2},\qquad

\displaystyle y=\frac{1}{3}\left(1-2\sqrt{2}\right).

All these values are negative, and if we want positive
s
and
s_{7}
it means that we have to take the signs of
\mu
,
h
,
F_{0}
and
f_{A}
appropriately. One can show that these numerical values agree with the solution found in
Farakos:2020phe
after a change to Einstein frame for the dilaton.

There is no guarantee that a solution can be found for any
F_{4A}
- and
H
-flux choice. It would therefore be interesting to chart the landscape of scale-separated AdS
3
vacua in future work.

4
Type IIB AdS
3
solutions

One can do the same exercise for type IIB to find AdS
3
vacua. For that one should take the same bispinor equations (
8
)-(
10
) but now with the lower signs. In
Emelin:2021gzx
it was observed that it is hard to achieve scale-separated solutions in the type IIB setup. Unsurprisingly we will obtain the same conclusion with the bispinor techniques in a faster fashion.

From the first bispinor equation (
8
) one finds that

\mathrm{d}(e^{2A-\phi})=0,\qquad\mathrm{d}(e^{2A-\phi}\star\Phi)=0,\qquad H=0.

(44)

The RR fields can be determined with the second bispinor equation (
9
):

\displaystyle e^{3A}\star F_{1}=0

(45)

\displaystyle e^{3A}\star F_{3}=-\mathrm{d}(e^{3A-\phi}\Phi)+2\mu e^{2A-\phi}\star\Phi

(46)

\displaystyle e^{3A}\star F_{5}=0

(47)

\displaystyle e^{3A}\star F_{7}=-e^{2A-\phi}2\mu.

(48)

Here eq. (
44
) was already used to arrive at these expressions. Some of these RR fields are subject to another constraint through the pairing equation (
10
):

\star\Phi\wedge F_{3}-F_{7}=4\mu e^{-A-\phi}\text{vol}_{7}.

(49)

It serves well to combine (
46
), (
48
) and (
49
) to find

\displaystyle\star\Phi\wedge F_{3}=2\mu e^{-A-\phi}\text{vol}_{7},\qquad\Phi\wedge\left(e^{-3A}\mathrm{d}\left(e^{3A-\phi}\Phi\right)\right)=12\mu e^{-A-\phi}\text{vol}_{7}.

(50)

From the latter equation the torsion class
W_{1}
can be determined:

W_{1}=\frac{12}{7}\mu\>e^{-A}.

(51)

Again, we are interested in smeared sources such that the warping and dilaton profile are trivial and we therefore put
e^{\phi}=g_{s}
and
e^{A}=1
. Since this implies
\mathrm{d}\star\Phi=0
we find
W_{7}=0=W_{14}
due to eq. (
15
). Then we can write
\mathrm{d}\Phi=W_{1}\star\Phi+W_{27}
, which results for (
46
) in

\displaystyle g_{s}F_{3}=\frac{2\mu}{7}\Phi-\star W_{27}.

(52)

The bispinor equations have to be supplemented with the Bianchi identities here as well.
Because
H
,
F_{1}
and
F_{5}
are identically vanishing but
F_{3}
not, there is only the potential need of O5/D5 sources when solving the Bianchi identities and hence only those sources are considered.
Therefore, the only non-trivial Bianchi identity is the one for
F_{3}
:

\mathrm{d}F_{3}=j_{\text{O5/D5}}.

(53)

When looking whether scale separation is possible in these type IIB setups, it is again useful to benefit from a scaling symmetry. Here we only look at setups with uniform scalings, meaning that all volume
p
-forms dual to
p
-cycles scale as
L^{p}
, where
L=V_{7}^{1/7}
is the volume scale and serves as our proxy for the KK scale. This
L
is then a natural choice to parametrise the scaling symmetry.
By examining the scaling properties of the solutions, some conclusions of
Emelin:2021gzx
are revisited. A first remark is that we need configurations where
F_{3}
is closed and thus the D5-branes cancel the O5-charge in order to allow for weakly coupled solutions. Indeed, if not the
F_{3}
Bianchi identity tells us that
F_{3}
cannot scale.
We learn further that with uniform scalings
\Phi\sim L^{3}
,
\star\Phi\sim L^{4}
and hence through the torsion equation
\mathrm{d}\Phi=W_{1}\star\Phi+W_{27}
that
\mu\sim W_{1}\sim L^{-1}
and
W_{27}\sim L^{3}
or
\star W_{27}\sim L^{2}
when respecting geometric flux quantisation. All these scalings indicate that the RHS of (
52
) scales like
L^{2}
, and hence
g_{s}\sim L^{2}
because
F_{3}
does not scale. Thus a large volume limit forces us to a strongly coupled regime. Therefore we need the D5-charge cancelling the O5-charge such
\mathrm{d}F_{3}=0
is enforced. Taking the easy route and requiring
F_{3}
to vanish would lead us to Minkowski compactifications and hence are not of interest here.
So in the case
F_{3}\neq 0
and
\mathrm{d}F_{3}=0
, we conclude from above that

\mu\sim L^{-1},\qquad g_{s}\sim L^{2-a},\qquad F_{3}\sim L^{a},

(54)

with
a>2
.
However, scale separation cannot be achieved, because the ratio

\frac{L_{\text{KK}}^{2}}{L_{\text{AdS}}^{2}}=L^{2}\mu^{2}\sim L^{0}

(55)

is of order one and cannot be made parametrically small. Hence there are no scale-separated AdS
3
solutions of type IIB on manifolds with
G_{2}
-structures for uniform scalings, just as was observed in
Emelin:2021gzx
. The fact that one cannot find such scale-separated 3d vacua with weak coupling and large volume in classical type IIB is quite interesting. Similarly, in
Cribiori:2021djm
it was observed that, despite earlier claims, the classical type IIB AdS
4
vacua with O5/O7-planes of
Petrini:2013ika
and
Caviezel:2009tu
do not admit scale separation, weak coupling and large cycles at the same time. Therefore one could suggest that classical type IIB string theory does not harbour such vacuum solutions.

5
The 3d superpotential

The type II solutions discussed above benefit from
\mathcal{N}=1
supersymmetry in 3d. Just as in the 4d case, the scalar sector of such a supergravity theory is governed by a superpotential. For these type II orientifolds on manifolds with
G_{2}
-structure, the real superpotential
\mathcal{P}
was computed for type IIA and type IIB in
Farakos:2020phe
and
Emelin:2021gzx
separately. Alternatively one can define a normal superpotential
\mathcal{W}
and a Kähler-like potential
\mathcal{K}
(just as in 4d) from this real superpotential through the relation
\mathcal{P}=\left|e^{\mathcal{K}/2}\mathcal{W}\right|
. In 4d, the superpotential is the celebrated Gukov-Vafa-Witten superpotential and extensions thereof
Gukov:1999ya
;
Grimm:2004ua
;
Grimm:2004uq
. Moreover, in
Koerber:2007xk
the 4d off-shell Kähler- and superpotential were determined using the language of bispinors. This has the benefit that expressions for type IIA and IIB are the same upon the exchange of the odd and even bispinors. In what follows it is argued that the same can be done for these 3d setups with
G_{2}
-structure.

To put it simply, the 3d superpotential
\mathcal{W}
and Kähler potential
\mathcal{K}
are

\displaystyle\mathcal{W}

\displaystyle=\pm 2\int\left<e^{A-\phi}\Psi_{\mp},F\right>-8\int e^{-4A}\left<\mathrm{d}_{H}\left(e^{2A-\phi}\Psi_{\pm}\right),e^{2A-\phi}\Psi_{\pm}\right>

(56)

\displaystyle\mathcal{K}

\displaystyle=-4\log\left[8\int e^{-A-2\phi}\left<\Psi_{+},\Psi_{-}\right>\right]

(57)

It is important to note that in 3d there is no notion of complex moduli fields, and hence the superpotential we have written down is not to be understood as a holomorphic function anymore. Furthermore, it was shown in the previous sections that the warping should be trivial for the models under consideration. However, the warp factors are kept explicitly to make contact with the bispinor equations (
8
)-(
10
). The expression for the Kähler potential is similar to its 4d counterpart in
Koerber:2007xk
. With the constant profiles, the Kähler potential can be neatly written as

\mathcal{K}=-4\log\left[e^{A-2\phi}V_{7}\right]=-4\log\left[e^{A}V_{7}\right]+8\phi.

(58)

The super- and Kähler potential given here are in agreement with
Farakos:2020phe
;
Emelin:2021gzx
after a change to Einstein frame for the dilaton.

One can show that the usual supersymmetry conditions for this Kähler- and superpotential are equivalent to the bispinor equations (
8
)-(
10
). With the real moduli
\varphi_{i}
, these conditions imply

0=D_{\varphi_{i}}\mathcal{W}\equiv\frac{\delta}{\delta\varphi_{i}}\left(e^{\frac{\mathcal{K}}{2}}\mathcal{W}\right)=\frac{\delta\mathcal{W}}{\delta\varphi_{i}}+\frac{1}{2}\frac{\delta\mathcal{K}}{\delta\varphi_{i}}\mathcal{W}_{\text{on-shell}}.

(59)

The on-shell superpotential
\mathcal{W}_{\text{on-shell}}
has to satisfy
\Lambda=-8e^{\mathcal{K}}|\mathcal{W}_{\text{on-shell}}|^{2}
, which then can be derived from eq. (
57
) to be

\mathcal{W}_{\text{on-shell}}=\frac{\mu}{2}e^{A-2\phi}V_{7}.

(60)

Alternatively, one gets the same result when plugging in the bispinor equations (
8
)-(
10
) into (
56
).

With the expression for the on-shell superpotential at hand, one can show that the supersymmetry condition for the dilaton
D_{\phi}\mathcal{W}=0
is equivalent to the pairing equation (
10
):

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle=D_{\phi}W

\displaystyle\Rightarrow 0

\displaystyle=\left<e^{A-\phi}\Psi_{\mp},F\right>\mp 8e^{-4A}\left<\mathrm{d}_{H}\left(e^{2A-\phi}\Psi_{\pm}\right),e^{2A-\phi}\Psi_{\pm}\right>\mp 2\mathcal{W}_{\text{on-shell}}\frac{\text{vol}_{7}}{V_{7}}

(61)

\displaystyle=-\left<e^{A-\phi}\Psi_{\mp},F\right>\mp\mathcal{W}_{\text{on-shell}}\frac{\text{vol}_{7}}{V_{7}},

(62)

and hence with (
60
) we recover the pairing equation (
10
)

\left<\Psi_{\mp},F\right>=\mp\frac{\mu}{2}e^{-\phi}\text{vol}_{7}.

(63)

The first bispinor equation (
8
) is obtained by requiring that the Kähler- and superpotential are invariant under deformations of the RR gauge potential
C
. Consider such an infinitesimal deformation acting as
F\to F+\mathrm{d}_{H}\delta C
with arbitrary gauge potential
\delta C
. Invariance of the superpotential requires

0=\delta\mathcal{W}=\pm 2\int\left<e^{A-\phi}\Psi_{\mp},\mathrm{d}_{H}\delta C\right>=\pm 2\int\left<\mathrm{d}_{H}\left(e^{A-\phi}\Psi_{\mp}\right),\delta C\right>.

(64)

Integration by parts was used in the last step.
Because
\delta C
is arbitrary, we recover the first bispinor equation (
8
):

\mathrm{d}_{H}\left(e^{A-\phi}\Psi_{\mp}\right)=0.

(65)

It can also be obtained by acting with
\mathrm{d}_{H}
on the second bispinor (
9
) and requiring the equations of motion for the RR fluxes
F
.

The second bispinor equation (
9
) can be derived from deformations of the bispinors in the super- and Kähler potential. For that it is useful to classify all possible forms into the irreducible representations of the
G_{2}
-group. These consist of the trivial
1
-, the fundamental
7
-, the adjoint
14
- and the symmetric traceless
27
-representation. The spaces of
p
-forms
\Lambda^{p}
are categorised under these representations as follows

\displaystyle\Lambda^{0},\>\Lambda^{7}

\displaystyle:\quad{\bf 1}

(66)

\displaystyle\Lambda^{1},\>\Lambda^{6}

\displaystyle:\quad{\bf 7}

\displaystyle\Lambda^{2},\>\Lambda^{5}

\displaystyle:\quad{\bf 7}\oplus{\bf 14}

\displaystyle\Lambda^{3},\>\Lambda^{4}

\displaystyle:\quad{\bf 1}\oplus{\bf 7}\oplus{\bf 27}.

The bispinors
\Psi_{\pm}
both belong to the trivial representation
1
. One can then define projectors
\pi^{\bf i}
that project a form to its component along the representation
i
=
1, 7, 14, 27
. With such a projector any two polyforms
\Xi
and
\Upsilon
satisfy

\left<\pi^{\bf i}(\Xi),\pi^{\bf j}(\Upsilon)\right>=0\quad\text{for }{\bf i}\neq{\bf j}.

(67)

The deformations of the bispinors themselves can thus also be categorised in these representations. Since the bispinors encode all the metric information, deforming them also means deforming the metric and hence the Hodge dual operator. We then write such a deformation as
\Psi_{\pm}\to\Psi_{\pm}^{\prime}=\Psi_{\pm}+\delta_{\bf i}\Psi_{\pm}
and
\star\to\star^{\prime}=\star+\star^{(\delta)}
where
\star
still corresponds to the undeformed Hodge star. The bispinors are still dependent on each other and hence we require that
\star^{\prime}\lambda\Psi_{\pm}^{\prime}=\mp\Psi_{\mp}^{\prime}
. To first order in the deformation this means that

\star^{(\delta)}\lambda\Psi_{\pm}+\star\lambda\left(\delta_{\bf i}\Psi_{\pm}\right)=\mp\delta_{\bf i}\Psi_{\mp}.

(68)

It is worth to notice that generally the first term, corresponding to deformations of the Hodge operator, does not need to vanish. For the
1
- and
27
-deformations this turns out to be the case. For
7
- and
14
-deformations the first term needs to vanish, since such deformations involve 1-, 2-, 5-, and 6-forms, which the deformed Hodge operator cannot generate when acting on the bispinors since these contain only 0-, 3-, 4- and 7-forms.

1
- and
27
-deformations

A deformation in the trivial representation
1
corresponds to a uniform volume rescaling of all the
G_{2}
-structure forms. An infinitesimal deformation parametrised by
\delta L
acts on the forms as follows:

\delta_{\bf 1}\Phi=3\>\delta L\>\Phi,\qquad\delta_{\bf 1}\left(\star\Phi\right)=4\>\delta L\>\star\Phi,\qquad\delta_{\bf 1}\text{vol}_{7}=7\>\delta L\>\text{vol}_{7}.

(69)

With these we can verify that

\delta_{\bf 1}\Psi_{\mp}=\pm\star\lambda(\delta_{\bf 1}\Psi_{\pm})+7\>\delta L\>\Psi_{\mp},\qquad 7\>\delta L\>\text{vol}_{7}=\mp 16e^{-2A}\left<\Psi_{\mp},\delta_{\bf 1}\Psi_{\pm}\right>.

(70)

The supersymmetry condition
\delta_{\bf 1}\mathcal{W}+(\mathcal{W}/2)\delta_{\bf 1}K=0
for such a deformation then results in

0=\mp\left<e^{A-\phi}\delta_{\bf 1}\Psi_{\mp},F\right>+8e^{-4A}\left<\mathrm{d}_{H}\left(e^{2A-\phi}\Psi_{\pm}\right),e^{2A-\phi}\delta_{\bf 1}\Psi_{\pm}\right>+\frac{\mathcal{W}_{\text{on-shell}}}{V_{7}}(7\>\delta L\>\text{vol}_{7}).

(71)

The first term can now be rewritten with eq. (
63
) and (
70
), and one finds after multiplying with
e^{2A+\phi}
that

\displaystyle 0=

\displaystyle-\left<\star\lambda(\delta_{\bf 1}\Psi_{\pm}),e^{3A}F\right>+8\left<\mathrm{d}_{H}\left(e^{2A-\phi}\Psi_{\pm}\right),\delta_{\bf 1}\Psi_{\pm}\right>

\displaystyle+\left(\frac{\mu}{2}e^{3A-\phi}+e^{2A+\phi}\frac{\mathcal{W}_{\text{on-shell}}}{V_{7}}\right)(7\>\delta L\>\text{vol}_{7}).

(72)

Now using (
7
), (
60
) and (
70
), we arrive at

0=\left<-\frac{1}{8}e^{3A}\star\lambda F+\mathrm{d}_{H}\left(e^{2A-\phi}\Psi_{\pm}\right)\mp 2\mu e^{A-\phi}\Psi_{\mp},\delta_{\bf 1}\Psi_{\pm}\right>.

(73)

In order for this to vanish, the
1
-component of the first argument of the pairing needs to vanish because of eq. (
67
). Similarly, one could manipulate (
71
) such that
\delta_{\bf 1}\Psi_{\mp}
appears in the second argument of the pairing. Together, the two conditions reproduce the
1
-component of the bispinor equation (
9
).

Let us now look at deformations in the
27
-representation. Such deformations can be generated by a traceless symmetric matrix
\beta_{m}{}^{n}
through

\delta_{\bf 27}\Psi_{\pm}=\beta_{m}{}^{n}e^{m}\wedge\iota_{n}\Psi_{\pm},

(74)

with
\iota_{n}=e^{n}\lrcorner
is a contraction operator.
Such deformations satisfy

\star\lambda\left(\delta_{\bf 27}\Psi_{\pm}\right)=\pm\delta_{\bf 27}\Psi_{\mp}.

(75)

The volume is only subject to deformations in the trivial representation, and hence the supersymmetry condition becomes
\delta_{\bf 27}\mathcal{W}=0
. This is equivalent to

0=\mp\left<e^{A-\phi}\delta_{\bf 27}\Psi_{\mp},F\right>+8e^{-4A}\left<\mathrm{d}_{H}\left(e^{2A-\phi}\Psi_{\pm}\right),e^{2A-\phi}\delta_{\bf 27}\Psi_{\pm}\right>.

(76)

By using (
7
) and (
75
) this condition is simplified to:

0=\left<-\frac{1}{8}e^{3A}\star\lambda F+\mathrm{d}_{H}\left(e^{2A-\phi}\Psi_{\pm}\right),\delta_{\bf 27}\Psi_{\pm}\right>.

(77)

Again, this has to vanish for all deformations
\delta_{\bf 27}\Psi_{\pm}
, such that we recover the
27
-component of the bispinor equation (
9
).

7- and 14-deformations

One could wonder about the deformations of the superpotential in the fundamental
7
- and adjoint
14
-representations. Such deformations do not affect the volume and hence the Kähler potential neither. As argued above, those deformations also do not affect the Hodge operator and thus satisfy

\star\lambda\left(\delta_{\bf i}\Psi_{\pm}\right)=\mp\delta_{\bf i}\Psi_{\mp}.

(78)

The effect of such deformations on the superpotential is similar to the
27
-case, but with a sign difference:

0=\left<+\frac{1}{8}e^{3A}\star\lambda F+\mathrm{d}_{H}\left(e^{2A-\phi}\Psi_{\pm}\right),\delta_{\bf i}\Psi_{\pm},\right>,\qquad{\bf i}={\bf 7,14}.

(79)

This comes with the wrong sign when compared to (
9
). Does this then invalidate the results? Fortunately it does not by the virtue of the first bispinor equation (
8
), which we independently derived from the superpotential in (
65
). We argued in section
4
that
H
and the torsion classes
W_{7}
and
W_{14}
vanish for constant warping and dilaton in type IIB due to the first bispinor equation (
65
). In type IIA we concluded in section
3
from the same bispinor equation (
65
) that
H
has no
7
-component (otherwise
\Phi\wedge H\neq 0
) and that
W_{7}
vanishes. Due to the necessary absence of O4-planes it was argued that
W_{14}
also needs to vanish. From all this, we conclude that
\mathrm{d}_{H}\left(e^{2A-\phi}\Psi_{\pm}\right)
has no
7
- and no
14
-component in both type IIA and IIB due to the first bispinor equation we derived in (
65
). Hence, the second term in (
79
) vanishes due to eq. (
67
). This leaves us with

0=\left<\star\lambda F,\delta_{\bf i}\Psi_{\pm}\right>,\qquad{\bf i}={\bf 7,14}.

(80)

It leads us to conclude that the fluxes cannot have components in the
7
- and
14
-representations, i.e.

\pi^{\bf 7}(F)=0,\qquad\pi^{\bf 14}(F)=0.

(81)

This is perfectly consistent with what we have found earlier. Another interesting remark is that the sign difference with (
9
) serves in a way as a prediction that
W_{14}
needs to vanish, as an alternative to the O4-argument used earlier.

Lastly we remark that a
7
-deformation is parametrised by an infinitesimal one-form which we call
\delta\alpha_{1}
. It acts on the bispinors by

\delta_{\bf 7}\Psi_{\pm}=\left(\delta\alpha_{1}\lrcorner-\delta\alpha_{1}\wedge\right)\Psi_{\mp}.

(82)

with
\lrcorner
the contraction operator.
It is interesting to note that such a
7
-transformation transforms the bispinors of a
G_{2}
-structure into those of an SU(3)-structure (see e.g.
Witt:2004vr
;
Dibitetto:2018ftj
). Indeed, for a one-form
v
, one can regard

\Psi_{\pm}^{\text{SU(3)}}=(v\lrcorner-v\wedge)\Psi_{\mp}^{G_{2}}.

(83)

Furthermore, it is a special O(7,7)-transformation that corresponds to T-duality along the vector dual to
v
.

6
Summary

In this paper it was demonstrated that AdS
3
vacua of type II orientifolds with
G_{2}
-structure can easily be captured by the bispinor techniques of
Dibitetto:2018ftj
. Finding such vacuum solutions with minimal supersymmetry amounts to solving the bispinor equations and the Bianchi identities for the Ramond-Ramond fluxes. This approach leads to the same conclusions as in
Farakos:2020phe
;
Emelin:2021gzx
for specific types of vacua. The type IIA vacua can be seen as the 3d analogues of the 4d DGKT solutions, they can exhibit scale separation as well and are therefore in tension with the strong ADC
Lust:2019zwm
. It was also argued in
Apers:2022zjx
that they also violate the
\mathbb{Z}_{k}
-refined strong ADC of
Buratti:2020kda
.
On the other hand, the type IIB solutions do not exhibit scale separation.

Finally a general formula was given for the 3d superpotential in terms of the
G_{2}
-structure bispinors and its supersymmetry conditions were shown to be equivalent to the bispinor equations. Although the latter was shown in the context of
G_{2}
-structures, it is to be expected that the Kähler- and superpotential should have very similar expressions for 3d
\mathcal{N}=1
backgrounds with SU(3)-structure or generalised
G_{2}
-structure
Witt:2004vr
. T-duality gives a hint for this, as it transforms a
G_{2}
-structure into a 7d SU(3)-structure but it does not change the final form of the Kähler- and superpotential. It would therefore be interesting to derive the 3d superpotential from the gravitino mass, as was done for heterotic 3d supergravity in
deIaOssa:2019cci
. Furthermore, it was shown in
Emelin:2022cac
that the local backreaction of the O6-planes can be computed using the techniques of
Junghans:2020acz
that were developed for 4d DGKT vacua. It would be interesting to do the same exercise with the bispinors of a generalised
G_{2}
-structure, along the same lines of
Marchesano:2020qvg
where this was done with generalised SU(3)-structures for 4d DGKT solutions.

Abstract
The global dynamics of the two-species Lotka-Volterra competition patch model with asymmetric dispersal is classified under the assumptions of weak competition and the weighted digraph of the connection matrix is strongly connected and cycle-balanced. We show that in the long time, either the competition exclusion holds that one species becomes extinct, or the two species reach a coexistence equilibrium, and the outcome of the competition is determined by the strength of the inter-specific competition and the dispersal rates. Our main techniques in the proofs follow the theory of monotone dynamical systems and a graph-theoretic approach based on the Tree-Cycle identity. Keywords : Lotka-Volterra competition patch model; global dynamics; weighted digraph; monotone dynamical system. MSC 2020 : 92D25, 92D40, 34C12, 34D23, 37C65.

Introduction

Spatial dispersal of organisms in a heterogeneous environment with uneven resource distribution and varying connectivity have long been recognized as key components of ecological interactions

4
,

For example, metapopulation models have been used to study the effects of habitat fragmentation on biodiversity

[

]

[

Typically a patch model consists of a system of ordinary differential equations with local dynamics in each patch coupled with dispersal dynamics between patches.

A prominent example of patch models is the Lokta-Volterra competition model on a set of discrete set of habitats
[

,
29
,
40
]

2.6
) admits a trivial equilibrium
\mathbf{0}=(0,0,\ldots,0)
. Suppose that
L
is irreducible. Since the Jacobian matrix of the right hand side of (
2.6
) is cooperative (off-diagonal entries are nonnegative) and irreducible, the solutions of (
2.6
) have strongly monotonicity
[
39
, Theorem 4.1.1]
, i.e. for any two solutions
w^{1}(t)
and
w^{2}(t)
of (
2.6
), if
w^{1}(0)>w^{2}(0)\geq\bm{0}
then
w^{1}(t)\gg w^{2}(t)
for all
t>0
. In particular, if
w^{1}(0)>\bm{0}
then
w^{1}(t)\gg\bm{0}
for all
t>0
. The global dynamics of (
2.6
) is as follows
[
6
,
8
,
30
,
34
]
, which is essentially similar to the dynamics of the logistic model, i.e. the case
n=1
:

Lemma 2.4
.

Suppose that the connection matrix
L
as defined in (
1.2
) is irreducible, and
r_{i}>0
for all
i=1,2,...,n
. Then the equilibrium
\mathbf{0}
is unstable, and (
2.6
) admits a unique positive equilibrium
w^{*}(\mu,r)=(w^{*}_{1},\dots,w^{*}_{n})
, which is globally asymptotically stable in
\mathbb{R}_{+}^{n}-\{\mathbf{0}\}
.

Throughout this paper, we assume that
p_{i},q_{i}>0
for all
i=1,2,...,n
. By Lemma
2.4
, (
1.3
) has two semi-trivial equilibria,
E_{1}=(w^{*}(\mu_{u},p),\mathbf{0})
and
E_{2}=(\mathbf{0},w^{*}(\mu_{v},q))
, and one trivial equilibria
E_{0}=(\mathbf{0},\mathbf{0})
. A positive equilibrium (or coexistence equilibrium) of (
1.3
), if exists, is denoted by
E=(u,v)
when not causing confusion.

An important tool to investigate the global dynamics of the Lotka-Volterra competition system (
1.3
) is the monotone dynamical system theory
[
18
,
21
,
27
,
39
]
. Let
X=\mathbb{R}_{+}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}_{+}^{n}
equipped with an order
\leq_{K}
generated by the cone
K=\mathbb{R}_{+}^{n}\times\{-\mathbb{R}_{+}^{n}\}
. That is, for
x=(\bar{u},\bar{v}),y=(\tilde{u},\tilde{v})\in X
, we say
x\leq_{K}y
if
\bar{u}\leq\tilde{u}
and
\bar{v}\geq\tilde{v}
;
x<_{K}y
if
x\leq_{K}y
and
x\neq y
. Then (
1.3
) is cooperative with respect to
K

[
39
, Section 4.4]
, and the solutions of (
1.3
) induce a strictly monotone dynamical system in
X
in the sense that for any two initial data
(u_{0}^{1},v_{0}^{1})<_{K}(u_{0}^{2},v_{0}^{2})
, the corresponding solutions satisfy
(u^{1}(t),v^{1}(t))<_{K}(u^{2}(t),v^{2}(t))
for all
t\geq 0
. Moreover since the Jacobian matrix of (
1.3
) is irreducible in
int(X)
, the dynamical system is strongly monotone in
int(X)
, i.e. for any two initial data
(u_{0}^{1},v_{0}^{1})<_{K}(u_{0}^{2},v_{0}^{2})
with at least one of them in
int(X)
, the corresponding solutions satisfy
(u^{1}(t),v^{1}(t))\ll_{K}(u^{2}(t),v^{2}(t))
for all
t>0

[
39
, Theorem 4.1.1, Remark 4.1.1]
.

The following two results derived from the monotone dynamical system theory can be found in
[
39
]
:

Lemma 2.5

(
[
39
, Theorem 4.4.2]
)
.

Suppose that the connection matrix
L
is irreducible and
E_{1}
is linearly unstable. Then one of the following holds:

(i)

E_{2}
attracts all solutions with initial data
(u_{0},v_{0})\in X
satisfying
v_{0}>0
. In this case,
E_{2}
is linearly stable or neutrally stable;

(ii)

There exists a positive equilibrium
E
satisfying
E_{2}\ll_{K}E\ll_{K}E_{1}
such that
E
attracts all solutions with initial data
(u_{0},v_{0})\in X
satisfying
E\leq_{K}(u_{0},v_{0})<_{K}E_{1}
.

Lemma 2.6

(
[
39
, Corollary 4.4.3]
)
.

Suppose that the connection matrix
L
is irreducible and
E_{1}
and
E_{2}
are linearly unstable. Then there exist positive equilibria
E^{1}
and
E^{2}
of (
1.3
) satisfying
E_{2}\ll_{K}E^{2}\leq_{K}E^{1}\ll_{K}E_{1}
such that the order interval

[E^{2},E^{1}]:=\{(u,v)\in X:\ E^{2}\leq_{K}(u,v)\leq_{K}E^{1}\}

attracts all solutions with initial data
(u_{0},v_{0})
satisfying
u_{0}>0
and
v_{0}>0
. In particular, if
E^{1}=E^{2}
, then
E^{1}
attracts all solutions as above.

The two Lemmas
2.5
-
2.6
are stated for the case
n=2
in
[
39
]
, but as remarked in
[
39
, Page 70]
, they hold for any
n\geq 2
. The following result can be derived by
[
18
, Theorem I.6.1]
:

Lemma 2.7
.

Suppose that the connection matrix
L
is irreducible and
E^{1}
and
E^{2}
are equilibria of (
1.3
) satisfying
E_{2}\leq_{K}E^{2}<_{K}E^{1}\leq_{K}E_{1}
. If
E^{1}
and
E^{2}
are stable and at least one of them is an isolated equilibrium, then there exists an unstable positive equilibrium
E\in[E^{2},E^{1}]
.

In view of previous lemmas, a key step as in
[
17
,
42
]
is to show that every positive equilibrium, if exists, is stable.

3
Stability of coexistence equilibrium

In this section we state our main results on the global dynamics of (
1.3
).
Recall that
w^{*}(\mu,r)
is the unique positive steady state of (
2.6
). Then the following result states that every positive equilibrium of (
1.3
), if exists, is locally asymptotically stable except for the degenerate case, when the competition is weak and the network
\mathcal{G}
is cycle-balanced.

Theorem 3.1
.

Suppose that (A1)-(A3) hold. A positive equilibrium
E=(u,v)
of (
1.3
), if exists, is locally asymptotically stable except for the case
bc=1
and
w^{*}(\mu_{u},p)=cw^{*}(\mu_{v},q)
at which case
E
is linearly neutrally stable.

Proof.

Let
E=(u,v)
be a positive equilibrium of (
1.3
). Then it satisfies the following equations:

\begin{cases}\mu_{u}\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{n}L_{ij}u_{j}+u_{i}(p_{i}-u_{i}-cv_{i})=0,&i=1,\dots,n,\\
\mu_{v}\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{n}L_{ij}v_{j}+v_{i}(q_{i}-bu_{i}-v_{i})=0,&i=1,\dots,n.\end{cases}

(3.1)

Linearizing (
1.3
) at
E
, we have the following eigenvalue problem

\begin{cases}\mu_{u}\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{n}L_{ij}\phi_{j}+(p_{i}-u_{i}-cv_{i})\phi_{i}-u_{i}(\phi_{i}+c\psi_{i})+\lambda\phi_{i}=0,&i=1,\dots,n,\\
\mu_{v}\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{n}L_{ij}\psi_{j}+(q_{i}-bu_{i}-v_{i})\psi_{i}-v_{i}(b\phi_{i}+\psi_{i})+\lambda\psi_{i}=0,&i=1,\dots,n.\end{cases}

(3.2)

Here
(\phi,\psi)=((\phi_{1},...,\phi_{n}),(\psi_{1},...,\psi_{n}))
is a principal eigenvector associated with the principal eigenvalue
\lambda
of (
3.2
). We normalize
(\phi,\psi)
such that
\phi_{i}>0
and
\psi_{i}<0
for all
i=1,...,n
. We will show that
\lambda\geq 0
, where the equality holds if and only if
bc=1
and
w^{*}(\mu_{u},p)=cw^{*}(\mu_{v},q)
.

Multiplying the first equation of (
3.2
) by
u_{i}
, the first equation of (
3.1
) by
\phi_{i}
and taking the difference, we have

\mu_{u}\displaystyle\sum_{j=1,j\neq i}^{n}L_{ij}(\phi_{j}u_{i}-\phi_{i}u_{j})=u_{i}^{2}(\phi_{i}+c\psi_{i})-\lambda u_{i}\phi_{i},\ \ \ \ i=1,\dots,n.

(3.3)

Let
(\alpha_{1},...,\alpha_{n})
be the left eigenvector of
\mathcal{L}
as defined in (
2.2
). Multiplying both sides of (
3.3
) by
\alpha_{i}\phi_{i}^{2}/u_{i}^{2}
and summing up all the equations, we obtain

\mu_{u}\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{n}\displaystyle\sum_{j=1,j\neq i}^{n}\alpha_{i}L_{ij}\left(\frac{\phi_{i}^{2}\phi_{j}}{u_{i}}-\frac{\phi_{i}^{3}u_{j}}{u_{i}^{2}}\right)=\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{n}\alpha_{i}\phi_{i}^{2}(\phi_{i}+c\psi_{i})-\lambda\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{n}\alpha_{i}\frac{\phi_{i}^{3}}{u_{i}}.

(3.4)

Let
\{F_{ij}:D\times D\to\mathbb{R}\}_{1\leq i,j\leq n}
be a family of functions defined as

\displaystyle F_{ij}(x_{i},x_{j})=\frac{\phi_{i}^{2}\phi_{j}}{u_{i}}-\frac{\phi_{i}^{3}u_{j}}{u_{i}^{2}}
, where
D=(0,\infty)\times(0,\infty)
and
x_{i}=(\phi_{i},u_{i})\in D
.
It can be verified that

\displaystyle F_{ij}(x_{i},x_{j})+F_{ji}(x_{j},x_{i})=

\displaystyle\,\left(\frac{\phi_{i}^{2}\phi_{j}}{u_{i}}-\frac{\phi_{i}^{3}u_{j}}{u_{i}^{2}}\right)+\left(\frac{\phi_{i}\phi_{j}^{2}}{u_{j}}-\frac{\phi_{j}^{3}u_{i}}{u_{j}^{2}}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\,\,u_{i}u_{j}\frac{\phi_{i}^{2}}{u_{i}^{2}}\left(\frac{\phi_{j}}{u_{j}}-\frac{\phi_{i}}{u_{i}}\right)+u_{i}u_{j}\frac{\phi_{j}^{2}}{u_{j}^{2}}\left(\frac{\phi_{i}}{u_{i}}-\frac{\phi_{j}}{u_{j}}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\,-u_{i}u_{j}\left(\frac{\phi_{i}}{u_{i}}-\frac{\phi_{j}}{u_{j}}\right)^{2}\left(\frac{\phi_{i}}{u_{i}}+\frac{\phi_{j}}{u_{j}}\right)\leq 0,

and the equal sign holds if and only if
\phi_{i}/u_{i}=\phi_{j}/u_{j}
.
Since
\mathcal{G}
is cycle-balanced, it follows from Theorem
2.2
that

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{n}\displaystyle\sum_{j=1,j\neq i}^{n}\alpha_{i}L_{ij}F_{ij}(x_{i},x_{j})=\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{n}\displaystyle\sum_{j=1,j\neq i}^{n}\alpha_{i}L_{ij}\left(\frac{\phi_{i}^{2}\phi_{j}}{u_{i}}-\frac{\phi_{i}^{3}u_{j}}{u_{i}^{2}}\right)\leq 0,

(3.5)

and the equal sign holds if and only if

\frac{\phi_{1}}{u_{1}}=\frac{\phi_{2}}{u_{2}}=\cdots=\frac{\phi_{n}}{u_{n}}.

(3.6)

Similarly, by using (
3.1
) and (
3.2
), we obtain

\mu_{v}\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{n}\displaystyle\sum_{j=1,j\neq i}^{n}\alpha_{i}L_{ij}\left(\frac{\psi_{i}^{2}\psi_{j}}{v_{i}}-\frac{\psi_{i}^{3}v_{j}}{v_{i}^{2}}\right)=\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{n}\alpha_{i}\psi_{i}^{2}(b\phi_{i}+\psi_{i})-\lambda\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{n}\alpha_{i}\frac{\psi_{i}^{3}}{v_{i}}.

(3.7)

Similar to (
3.5
), it follows from Theorem
2.2
that

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{n}\displaystyle\sum_{j=1,j\neq i}^{n}\alpha_{i}L_{ij}\left(\frac{\psi_{i}^{2}\psi_{j}}{v_{i}}-\frac{\psi_{i}^{3}v_{j}}{v_{i}^{2}}\right)\geq 0,

(3.8)

where the equal sign holds if and only if

\frac{\psi_{1}}{v_{1}}=\frac{\psi_{2}}{v_{2}}=\cdots=\frac{\psi_{n}}{v_{n}}.

(3.9)

Multiplying (
3.7
) by
c^{3}
and subtracting it from (
3.4
), and noticing (
3.5
) and (
3.8
), we have

\displaystyle-\lambda\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{n}\alpha_{i}\left(\frac{\phi_{i}^{3}}{u_{i}}-c^{3}\frac{\psi_{i}^{3}}{v_{i}}\right)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle-\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{n}\alpha_{i}\phi_{i}^{2}(\phi_{i}+c\psi_{i})+\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{n}\alpha_{i}c^{2}\psi_{i}^{2}(bc\phi_{i}+c\psi_{i})

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle-\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{n}\alpha_{i}\phi_{i}^{2}(\phi_{i}+c\psi_{i})+\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{n}\alpha_{i}c^{2}\psi_{i}^{2}(\phi_{i}+c\psi_{i})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{n}\alpha_{i}(\phi_{i}-c\psi_{i})(\phi_{i}+c\psi_{i})^{2}\leq 0,

where we have used
bc\leq 1
. This implies
\lambda\geq 0
and the equality holds if and only if
bc=1
,
\phi_{i}=c\psi_{i}
for all
i
, and (
3.6
) and (
3.9
) hold.

Now we consider the situation when
\lambda=0
. It follows that
u=kv
for some
k>0
, and
bc=1
. By
u=kv
and the first equation of (
3.1
), we have

w^{*}(\mu_{u},p)=\left(1+\frac{c}{k}\right)u

By the second equation of (
3.1
), we have

w^{*}(\mu_{v},q)=\left(kb+1\right)v.

Therefore,

\frac{w^{*}(\mu_{u},p)}{w^{*}(\mu_{v},q)}=\frac{\left(1+\frac{c}{k}\right)u}{\left(kb+1\right)v}=c.

∎

Denote by
\lambda_{1}(\mu,h)
the principal eigenvalue of

\mu\sum_{j=1}^{n}L_{ij}\psi_{j}+h_{i}\psi_{i}+\lambda\psi_{i}=0,\;\;i=1,\dots,n,

where
h=(h_{1},\dots,h_{n})
. Then
\lambda_{1}(\mu,h)=-s(\mu L+diag(h_{i}))
. Let
E_{1}=(w^{*}(\mu_{u},p),\mathbf{0})
and
E_{2}=(\mathbf{0},w^{*}(\mu_{v},q))
be the two semi-trivial equilibria of (
1.1
).
Following
[
17
]
we define the following parameter subsets of
Q=\{(\mu_{u},\mu_{v}):\mu_{u},\mu_{v}>0\}
.

\begin{split}&S_{u}=\{(\mu_{u},\mu_{v}):(w^{*}(\mu_{u},p),\mathbf{0})\text{ is linearly stable}\}=\{(\mu_{u},\mu_{v}):\lambda_{1}(\mu_{v},q-bw^{*}(\mu_{u},p))>0\},\\
&S_{v}=\{(\mu_{u},\mu_{v}):(\mathbf{0},w^{*}(\mu_{v},q))\text{ is linearly stable}\}=\{(\mu_{u},\mu_{v}):\lambda_{1}(\mu_{u},p-cw^{*}(\mu_{v},q))>0\},\\
&S_{-}=\{(\mu_{u},\mu_{v}):\lambda_{1}(\mu_{v},q-bw^{*}(\mu_{u},p))<0,\;\lambda_{1}(\mu_{u},p-cw^{*}(\mu_{v},q))<0\},\\
&S_{u,0}=\{(\mu_{u},\mu_{v}):\lambda_{1}(\mu_{v},q-bw^{*}(\mu_{u},p))=0\},\\
&S_{v,0}=\{(\mu_{u},\mu_{v}):\lambda_{1}(\mu_{u},p-cw^{*}(\mu_{v},q))=0\},\\
&S_{0,0}=\{(\mu_{u},\mu_{v}):\lambda_{1}(\mu_{v},q-bw^{*}(\mu_{u},p))=\lambda_{1}(\mu_{u},p-cw^{*}(\mu_{v},q))=0\}.\end{split}

We classify the global dynamics of (
1.3
) according to the diffusion coefficients as follows.

Theorem 3.2
.

Suppose that (A1)-(A3) hold. Then we have the following mutually disjoint decomposition of
Q
:

Q=\left(S_{u}\cup S_{u,0}\setminus S_{0,0}\right)\bigcup\left(S_{v}\cup S_{v,0}\setminus S_{0,0}\right)\bigcup S_{-}\bigcup S_{0,0}.

(3.10)

Moreover, the following statements hold for system (
1.3
):

(i)

For any
(\mu_{u},\mu_{v})\in S_{u}\cup S_{u,0}\setminus S_{0,0}
,
E_{1}=(w^{*}(\mu_{u},p),\mathbf{0})
is globally asymptotically stable.

(ii)

For any
(\mu_{u},\mu_{v})\in S_{v}\cup S_{v,0}\setminus S_{0,0}
,
E_{2}=(\mathbf{0},w^{*}(\mu_{v},q))
is globally asymptotically stable.

(iii)

For any
(\mu_{u},\mu_{v})\in S_{-}
, (
1.3
) has a unique positive equilibrium
(u,v)
, which is globally asymptotically stable.

(iv)

For any
(\mu_{u},\mu_{v})\in S_{0,0}
, we have
bc=1
,
w^{*}(\mu_{u},p)\equiv cw^{*}(\mu_{v},q)
and (
1.3
) has a compact global attractor consisting of a continuum of equilibria

\{\left(\rho w^{*}(\mu_{u},p),(1-\rho)w^{*}(\mu_{u},p)/c\right):\rho\in[0,1]\}.

(3.11)

Proof.

Step 1
We first show the mutually disjoint decomposition of
Q
. For simplicity of notations, we denote
u^{*}=w^{*}(\mu_{u},p)
and
v^{*}=w^{*}(\mu_{v},q)
.
Denote by
\psi=(\psi_{1},\dots,\psi_{n})>\mathbf{0}
(respectively,
\phi=(\phi_{1},\dots,\phi_{n})>\mathbf{0}
) the principal eigenvector with respect to
\lambda_{1}(\mu_{v},q-bu^{*})
(respectively,
\lambda_{1}(\mu_{u},p-cv^{*})
).
Then we have

\begin{cases}\mu_{u}\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{n}L_{ij}\phi_{j}+(p_{i}-cv^{*}_{i})\phi_{i}+\lambda_{1}(\mu_{u},p-cv^{*})\phi_{i}=0,&i=1,\dots,n,\\
\mu_{v}\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{n}L_{ij}\psi_{j}+(q_{i}-bu^{*}_{i})\psi_{i}+\lambda_{1}(\mu_{v},q-bu^{*})\psi_{i}=0,&i=1,\dots,n.\end{cases}

(3.12)

Note that
u^{*}=(u^{*}_{1},\cdots,u^{*}_{n})
and
v^{*}=(v^{*}_{1},\cdots,v^{*}_{n})
satisfy

\begin{cases}\mu_{u}\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{n}L_{ij}u^{*}_{j}+u_{i}(p_{i}-u^{*}_{i})=0,&i=1,\dots,n,\\
\mu_{v}\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{n}L_{ij}v^{*}_{j}+v_{i}(q_{i}-v^{*}_{i})=0,&i=1,\dots,n.\end{cases}

(3.13)

Multiplying the first equation of (
3.12
) by

u^{*}_{i}
, the first equation of (
3.13
) by
\phi_{i}
and taking the difference, we have

\mu_{u}\displaystyle\sum_{j=1,j\neq i}^{n}L_{ij}(\phi_{j}u^{*}_{i}-\phi_{i}u^{*}_{j})=(cv^{*}_{i}-u^{*}_{i})u_{i}^{*}\phi_{i}-\lambda_{1}(\mu_{u},p-cv^{*})\phi_{i}u^{*}_{i},\;\;i=1,\dots,n

(3.14)

Let
(\alpha_{1},...,\alpha_{n})
be the left eigenvector of
\mathcal{L}
as defined in (
2.2
). Multiplying both sides of (
3.14
) by
\alpha_{i}u_{i}^{*}/\phi_{i}
, and taking the sum, we obtain

\begin{split}&\mu_{u}\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{n}\ \displaystyle\sum_{j=1,j\neq i}^{n}\alpha_{i}L_{ij}\left(\frac{\phi_{j}(u^{*}_{i})^{2}}{\phi_{i}}-u_{i}^{*}u^{*}_{j}\right)\\
=&\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{n}\alpha_{i}(cv^{*}_{i}-u^{*}_{i})(u_{i}^{*})^{2}-\lambda_{1}(\mu_{u},p-cv^{*})\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{n}\alpha_{i}(u_{i}^{*})^{2}.\end{split}

(3.15)

Let
F_{ij}=F_{ij}(\phi_{i},\phi_{j})=\displaystyle\frac{\phi_{j}(u^{*}_{i})^{2}}{\phi_{i}}-u_{i}^{*}u^{*}_{j}
. Since

\displaystyle F_{ij}+F_{ji}=\frac{\phi_{j}(u^{*}_{i})^{2}}{\phi_{i}}-u_{i}^{*}u^{*}_{j}+\frac{\phi_{i}(u^{*}_{j})^{2}}{\phi_{j}}-u_{j}^{*}u^{*}_{i}=\left(\frac{\sqrt{\phi_{j}}}{\sqrt{\phi_{i}}}u_{i}^{*}-\frac{\sqrt{\phi_{i}}}{\sqrt{\phi_{j}}}u_{j}^{*}\right)^{2}\geq 0,

and the equality holds if and only if
\phi_{i}/u^{*}_{i}=\phi_{j}/u^{*}_{j}
, it follows from Theorem
2.2
that

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{n}\displaystyle\sum_{j=1,j\neq i}^{n}\alpha_{i}L_{ij}\left(\frac{\phi_{j}(u^{*}_{i})^{2}}{\phi_{i}}-u_{i}^{*}u^{*}_{j}\right)\geq 0,

(3.16)

and the equal sign holds if and only if

\frac{\phi_{1}}{u^{*}_{1}}=\frac{\phi_{2}}{u^{*}_{2}}=\cdots=\frac{\phi_{n}}{u^{*}_{n}}.

(3.17)

Similarly, by using (
3.12
) and (
3.13
), we have

\begin{split}&\mu_{v}\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{n}\displaystyle\sum_{j=1,j\neq i}^{n}\alpha_{i}L_{ij}\left(\frac{\psi_{j}(v^{*}_{i})^{2}}{\psi_{i}}-v_{i}^{*}v^{*}_{j}\right)\\
=&\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{n}\alpha_{i}(bu^{*}_{i}-v^{*}_{i})(v_{i}^{*})^{2}-\lambda_{1}(\mu_{v},q-bu^{*})\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{n}\alpha_{i}(v_{i}^{*})^{2},\end{split}

(3.18)

and

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{n}\displaystyle\sum_{j=1,j\neq i}^{n}\alpha_{i}L_{ij}\left(\frac{\psi_{j}(v^{*}_{i})^{2}}{v_{i}}-v_{i}^{*}v^{*}_{j}\right)\geq 0,

(3.19)

where the equal sign holds if and only if

\frac{\psi_{1}}{v^{*}_{1}}=\frac{\psi_{2}}{v^{*}_{2}}=\cdots=\frac{\psi_{n}}{v^{*}_{n}}.

(3.20)

Multiplying (
3.18
) by
c^{3}
and subtracting it from (
3.15
), and noticing (
3.16
) and (
3.15
), we have

\begin{split}&\lambda_{1}(\mu_{u},p-cv^{*})\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{n}\alpha_{i}(u_{i}^{*})^{2}+\lambda_{1}(\mu_{v},q-bu^{*})c^{3}\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{n}\alpha_{i}(v_{i}^{*})^{2}\\
\leq&\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{n}\alpha_{i}(cv^{*}_{i}-u^{*}_{i})(u_{i}^{*})^{2}+\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{n}\alpha_{i}(bcu^{*}_{i}-cv^{*}_{i})(cv_{i}^{*})^{2}\\
\leq&-\sum_{i=1}^{n}\alpha_{i}(cv_{i}^{*}-u_{i}^{*})^{2}(cv_{i}^{*}+u^{*}_{i})\leq 0,\end{split}

(3.21)

where the last two inequalities are equalities if and only if
cv^{*}=u^{*}
and
bc=1
.
This implies that

\left(S_{u}\cup S_{u,0}\setminus S_{0,0}\right)\cap\left(S_{v}\cup S_{v,0}\setminus S_{0,0}\right)=\emptyset,

(3.22)

which proves (
3.10
).

Step 2
We show that if
(\mu_{u},\mu_{v})\in S_{u}
then
E_{1}
is globally asymptotically stable. If
(\mu_{u},\mu_{v})\in S_{u}
, then
E_{1}
is linearly asymptotically stable (so it is an isolated equilibrium) and
E_{2}
is linearly unstable by (

). By Lemma
2.5
, either
E_{1}
is globally asymptotically stable or there exists a positive equilibrium
E\in[E_{2},E_{1}]
. If such a positive equilibrium
E
exists, it is stable by Theorem

. Then by Lemma
2.7
, there exists an unstable positive equilibrium in
[E,E_{1}]
, which contradicts Theorem
3.1
. This proves the global stability of
E_{1}
. Similarly, we can prove the case
(\mu_{u},\mu_{v})\in S_{v}
.

Step 3
As in
[
17
]
, we rule out the possibility of coexistence equilibrium in the following two cases:

(A)

(\mu_{u},\mu_{v})\in S_{u,0}\setminus S_{0,0}
;

(B)

(\mu_{u},\mu_{v})\in S_{v,0}\setminus S_{0,0}
.

It suffices to consider case (A).
To the contrary, we assume that there exists a coexistence
equilibrium
(U^{*},V^{*})
for some
(\mu_{u},\mu_{v})\in S_{u,0}\setminus S_{0,0}
and
(b,c)=(b_{0},c_{0})
satisfying
b_{0}c_{0}\leq 1
. So,
\lambda_{1}(\mu_{v},q-b_{0}u^{*})=0
and
\lambda_{1}(\mu_{u},p-c_{0}v^{*})<0
. We define

G(b,c,u,v)=\begin{pmatrix}\mu_{u}\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{n}L_{1j}u_{j}+u_{1}(p_{1}-u_{1}-cv_{1})\\
\vdots\\
\mu_{u}\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{n}L_{nj}u_{j}+u_{n}(p_{n}-u_{n}-cv_{n})\\
\mu_{v}\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{n}L_{1j}v_{j}+v_{1}(q_{1}-bu_{1}-v_{1})\\
\vdots\\
\mu_{v}\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{n}L_{nj}v_{j}+v_{n}(q_{n}-bu_{n}-v_{n})\end{pmatrix}.

Then we compute the Jacobian matrix of

evaluated at
(b,c,u,v)=(b_{0},c_{0},U^{*},V^{*})
:

DG_{(u,v)}(b_{0},c_{0},U^{*},V^{*})=\begin{bmatrix}\mu_{u}L+\text{diag}(p-2U^{*}-c_{0}V^{*})&-\text{diag}(c_{0}U^{*})\\
-\text{diag}(b_{0}V^{*})&\mu_{v}L+\text{diag}(q-b_{0}U^{*}-2V^{*})\end{bmatrix}.

By Theorem
3.1
, the principal eigenvalue of
DG_{(u,v)}(b_{0},c_{0},U^{*},V^{*})
is negative and so all of its eigenvalues are on the left half plane of the complex plane. Therefore,
DG_{(u,v)}(b_{0},c_{0},U^{*},V^{*})
is invertible. By
G(b_{0},c_{0},U^{*},V^{*})=0
and the implicit function theorem, there exist positive solutions
(u(b,c),v(b,c))
of
G(b,c,u,v)=0
for
(b,c)
close to
(b_{0},c_{0})
, where
(u(b,c),v(b,c))
is continuously differentiable in
(b,c)
with
(u(b_{0},c_{0}),v(b_{0},c_{0}))=(U^{*},V^{*})
. By the definition of
G
,
(u(b,c),v(b,c))
is a positive equilibrium of (
1.3
). Noticing
\lambda_{1}(\mu_{u},p-c_{0}v^{*})<0
, we may choose
(\check{b},\check{c})
close to
(b_{0},c_{0})
with
0<\check{c}<c_{0}
,
\check{b}>b_{0}
, and
\check{b}\check{c}\leq 1
such that (
1.3
) has a positive equilibrium
(u(\check{b},\check{c}),v(\check{b},\check{c}))
with
\lambda_{1}(\mu_{u},p-\check{c}v^{*})<0
. Since
\check{b}>b_{0}
, we have

\lambda_{1}(\mu_{v},q-\check{b}u^{*})>\lambda_{1}(\mu_{v},q-b_{0}u^{*})=0.

Then
(\check{b},\check{c})\in S_{u}
, which means
(u^{*},0)
is globally asymptotically stable. This contradicts that (
1.3
) has a positive equilibrium
(u(\check{b},\check{c}),v(\check{b},\check{c}))
.

Step 4
If
(\mu_{u},\mu_{v})\in S_{u,0}\setminus S_{0,0}
, by Step 3 and Lemma
2.5
,
E_{1}
is globally asymptotically stable; Similarly if
(\mu_{u},\mu_{v})\in S_{v,0}\setminus S_{0,0}
,
E_{2}
is globally asymptotically stable.

Suppose
(\mu_{u},\mu_{v})\in S_{-}
. By Lemma
2.6
, there exist positive equilibiria
E^{1}
and
E^{2}
satisfying
E_{2}\ll_{K}E^{2}\leq_{K}E^{1}\ll_{K}E_{1}
such that the order interval
[E^{2},E^{1}]

attracts all solutions with initial data
(u_{0},v_{0})
satisfying
u_{0}>0
and
v_{0}>0
. By Theorem
3.1
,
E^{1}
and
E^{2}
are locally asymptotically stable. If

, by Lemma
2.7
, there exists an unstable equilibrium in

, which contradicts Theorem
3.1
. Therefore,

and it is globally asymptotically stable.

Step 5
Finally, we show (iv). By (
3.21
), if
\lambda_{1}(\mu_{u},p-cv^{*})=\lambda_{1}(\mu_{v},q-bu^{*})=0
then
u^{*}=cv^{*}
and
bc=1
, i.e.

S_{0,0}\subset\{(\mu_{u},\mu_{v}):u^{*}=cv^{*}\text{ and }bc=1\}.

On the other hand, if
u^{*}=cv^{*}
and
bc=1
, from
\mu_{u}\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{n}L_{ij}u_{j}^{*}+u_{i}^{*}(p_{i}-u_{i}^{*})=0
, we have

\mu_{u}\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{n}L_{ij}v_{j}^{*}+v_{i}^{*}(p_{i}-cv_{i}^{*})=0,

which implies
\lambda_{1}(\mu_{u},p-cv^{*})=0
. Similarly,
\lambda_{1}(\mu_{v},q-bu^{*})=0
. So we have

\{(\mu_{u},\mu_{v}):u^{*}=cv^{*}\text{ and }bc=1\}\subset S_{0,0}.

Hence,

S_{0,0}=\{(\mu_{u},\mu_{v}):u^{*}=cv^{*}\text{ and }bc=1\}.

(3.23)

It is easy to check that (
1.3
) has a continuum of equilibria (
3.11
) when
(\mu_{u},\mu_{v})\in S_{0,0}
, which is a global attractor by

[

, Theorem 3]
in the sense that every solution of (
1.3
) converges to an equilibrium in (
3.11
). The result in
[
19
, Theorem 3]
is for reaction-diffusion competition systems, which also holds for patch models.
∎

Lemma 4.1

Suppose that

2. We first claim:

\lim_{\mu\rightarrow\infty}u^{*}=\frac{\sum_{i=1}^{n}\theta_{i}p_{i}}{\sum_{i=1}^{n}\theta_{i}^{2}}\theta.

(4.8)

To see that, for any
\mu_{k}\rightarrow\infty
, there exists a subsequence, still denoting by itself, such that the corresponding positive solution
u_{k}^{*}=(u_{1k},...,u_{nk})
of (
4.7
) satisfies
u_{k}^{*}\rightarrow u^{\infty}\geq 0
as
k\rightarrow\infty
. Dividing both sides of (
4.7
) by
\mu_{k}
and taking
k\rightarrow\infty
, we obtain that
\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{n}L_{ij}u^{\infty}_{j}=0
, which means that
u^{\infty}=l\theta
for some
l\geq 0
. Summing up all the
n
equations in (
4.7
) and noticing
\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{n}L_{ij}=0
for all
1\leq j\leq n
, we have
\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{n}u_{ik}(p_{i}-u_{ik})=0
. Taking
k\rightarrow\infty
, we have
\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{n}l\theta_{i}(p_{i}-l\theta_{i})=0
. Since

by

, we have
l={\sum_{i=1}^{n}\theta_{i}p_{i}}/{\sum_{i=1}^{n}\theta_{i}^{2}}
. This proves (
4.8
).

Then by Lemma
4.1
and (
4.8
), we have

\displaystyle\lim_{\mu\rightarrow\infty}\lambda_{1}(\mu,q-u^{*})={\sum_{i=1}^{n}\theta_{i}(p_{i}-q_{i})}.

Similarly,

\displaystyle\lim_{\mu\rightarrow\infty}\lambda_{1}(\mu,p-v^{*})={\sum_{i=1}^{n}\theta_{i}(q_{i}-p_{i})}.

If
\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{n}\theta_{j}(p_{j}-q_{j})>0
, there exists
\mu_{2}>0
such that
\lambda_{1}(\mu,q-u^{*})>0
and
\lambda_{1}(\mu,p-v^{*})<0
for all

. Therefore, by Theorem
3.2
,
E_{1}
is globally asymptotically stable for
\mu>\mu_{2}
. The case
\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{n}\theta_{j}(p_{j}-q_{j})<0
can be proved similarly.
∎

Remark 4.6
.

By Proposition
4.5
, one may expect that there exists a critical value

such that if
\mu<\mu^{*}
, (
4.4
) has a unique globally stable positive equilibrium while if

either

). However, this result does not hold in general. Indeed, in

The following result characterizes the asymptotic limit of the unique positive equilibrium of (

) when the diffusion rate

Proposition 4.7
.

Suppose that (A1)-(A3) hold. Let
\Omega_{u}=\{i:1\leq i\leq n,p_{i}>q_{i}\}
and
\Omega_{v}=\{i:1\leq i\leq n,p_{i}<q_{i}\}

\Omega_{u}
and

are not empty with

Let

and

, where

u_{0i}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{cc}p_{i},&\text{if}\ i\in\Omega_{u},\\
0,&\text{if}\ i\in\Omega_{u},\end{array}\right.\ \ \ \ \text{and}\ \ \ \ v_{0i}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{cc}0&\text{if}\ i\in\Omega_{u},\\

be the unique positive equilibrium of (

) when

is small, then
\displaystyle\lim_{\mu\rightarrow 0}(u,v)=(u_{0},v_{0})

Proof.

By Proposition
4.5
, there exists
\mu_{1}>0
such that (
4.4
) has a unique positive equilibrium

that is globally asymptotically stable. By the definition, when
\mu=0

is a solution of the following system

\begin{cases}\mu\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{n}L_{ij}u_{j}+u_{i}(p_{i}-u_{i}-v_{i})=0,&i=1,\dots,n,\\
\mu\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{n}L_{ij}v_{j}+v_{i}(q_{i}-u_{i}-v_{i})=0,&i=1,\dots,n.\end{cases}

(4.9)

We show that (

) has a continuum of solutions emanating

F(\mu,(u,v))=\begin{pmatrix}\mu\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{n}L_{1j}u_{j}+u_{1}(p_{1}-u_{1}-v_{1})\\
\vdots\\
\mu\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{n}L_{nj}u_{j}+u_{n}(p_{n}-u_{n}-v_{n})\\
\mu\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{n}L_{1j}v_{j}+v_{1}(q_{1}-u_{1}-v_{1})\\

\mu\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{n}L_{nj}v_{j}+v_{n}(q_{n}-u_{n}-v_{n})\end{pmatrix}.

Then we compute the Jacobian matrix of

evaluated at
(\mu,(u,v))=(0,(u_{0},v_{0}))

DF_{(u,v)}(0,(u_{0},v_{0}))=\begin{bmatrix}\text{diag}(p-2u_{0}-v_{0})&-\text{diag}(u_{0})\\
-\text{diag}(v_{0})&\text{diag}(q-u_{0}-2v_{0})\end{bmatrix}.

By the assumption

for all

u_{0}
and
v_{0}

, we may choose

and

for each

for all

.

We show that
u_{i}(\mu)>0
for each
i\in\Omega_{v}
and
v_{i}(\mu)>0
for each
i\in\Omega_{u}
for
\mu
close to zero. To see that, fix
i_{0}\in\Omega_{v}
. Then,
p_{i_{0}}<q_{i_{0}}
,
u_{0i_{0}}=0
and

. Differentiating
\mu\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{n}L_{i_{0}j}u_{j}+u_{i_{0}}(p_{i_{0}}-u_{i_{0}}-v_{i_{0}})=0
with respect to

and evaluating at

u^{\prime}_{i_{0}}(0)=\frac{\sum_{j=1}^{n}L_{i_{0}j}u_{0j}}{q_{i_{0}}-p_{i_{0}}}=\frac{\sum_{j\in\Omega_{u}}L_{i_{0}j}u_{0j}}{q_{i_{0}}-p_{i_{0}}}.

By the assumption,

We can find
\mu^{*}<\mu^{*}_{1}
such that the solution

of (

. Then the conclusion follows from the uniqueness of the positive solution of (

) and the continuity of

in

.
∎

5
Conclusion

In this paper, we analyze the global dynamics of a Lotka-Volterra competition model in patchy environment with asymmetric dispersal. Under the assumption of weak competition and the weighted digraph of the connection matrix is strongly connected and cycle-balanced, we classify the global dynamics of the model. In particular, in Theorem
3.2
, we show that either the model has a globally stable coexistence steady state or one species competitively exclude the other one except for the special case that both semi-trivial equilibria are neutrally stable. Theorem
3.2
has been applied to two special cases, in which we are able to determine the explicit parameter ranges for coexistence verse competitive exclusion.

Our results use techniques from two fields. We use matrix theory and graph theory techniques to deal with the asymmetry of the connection matrix
L
. Due to the limitation of this method, the weighted digraph of
L
needs to be cycle-balanced. Though cycle-balanced digraph covers some important types of the configurations of patches, it is desirable to see whether this condition can be removed. We conjecture that Theorem
3.2
still holds without this technical assumption.

The second technique that we rely on is the well-developed monotone dynamical system theory. According to this theory, the dynamics of the model is essentially determined by the local dynamics of the equilibria. An essential step in our analysis is to prove that every coexistence steady state is locally asymptotically stable except for the special case that both semi-trivial equilibria are neutrally stable. This approach has been adopted in two recent articles
[
17
,
42
]
on Lotka-Volterra reaction-diffusion competition models. We remark that the patch model may not be simply regarded as the discretization of the reaction-diffusion model as the connection matrix
L
is not assumed to be symmetric.

Finally, we want to point out that we only consider the weak competition case in the paper, and the dynamics of the strong competition case remains an open problem.

Orientation Memory of Magnetic Dipoles
Blagoje Oblak, Ali Seraj

Abstract
We study the precession caused by electromagnetic radiation on a magnetic dipole located far from the source. As we show, this entails a net rotation of the dipole in the plane orthogonal to the direction of wave propagation, providing an electromagnetic analogue of gyroscopic gravitational memory. Like its gravitational cousin, the precession rate falls off with the square of the distance to the source, and is related to electric-magnetic duality and optical helicity on the celestial sphere. We use a multipolar expansion to compute the memory effect due to localized sources such as moving point charges, and highlight its occurrence in setups that break parity symmetry.

II
Gyromagnetic precession

and memory

Here we use the asymptotic behaviour of electrodynamics near null infinity to predict the precession rate of a magnetic dipole located far away from a source of electromagnetic waves, at leading order in the inverse distance from the source. For radiation bursts that are compactly supported in time, this leads to a net gyromagnetic memory, which we compute.

Asymptotic electromagnetic field.

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{e}_{r}

\displaystyle=n^{i}\partial_{i}\,,\qquad\boldsymbol{e}_{a}=rE_{a}{}^{B}\frac{\partial n^{i}}{\partial\theta^{B}}\partial_{i}

(1)

Now let

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}_{\mu}(t,\boldsymbol{x})

\displaystyle=\int\!\text{d}^{3}\boldsymbol{y}\int\limits_{-\infty}^{t}\!\!\text{d}t^{\prime}\,\frac{\delta(t^{\prime}-t+|\boldsymbol{x}-\boldsymbol{y}|)}{|\boldsymbol{x}-\boldsymbol{y}|}J_{\mu}(t^{\prime},\boldsymbol{y}).

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}_{\mu}(t,\boldsymbol{x})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{r}A_{\mu}(u,\boldsymbol{n})+\mathcal{O}(r^{-2})

\displaystyle A_{\mu}(u,\boldsymbol{n})

\displaystyle=\int\text{d}^{3}\boldsymbol{y}\,J_{\mu}(u+\boldsymbol{n}\cdot\boldsymbol{y},\boldsymbol{y})\,.

Precession and memory.

Consider a static magnetic dipole

III
Magnetic field,

duality and helicity

Here we study the two terms of the gyromagnetic memory effect (
12

Then we turn to the nonlinear term and show that it can be interpreted in two equivalent ways: either as a generator of local electric-magnetic duality on a celestial sphere, or as a measure of the difference between the numbers of left- and right-handed photons crossing a given point on the celestial sphere. As before, analogous interpretations hold in the gravitational version of the setup

;
Seraj and Oblak 2022

Now consider the time integral of

Memory and duality.
Let us now focus on the nonlinear term of the gyromagnetic memory (
12

To prove this, we begin with a reminder: electric-magnetic duality is a manifest symmetry of the vacuum Maxwell equations; it mixes electric and magnetic fields through rotations of the electromagnetic field
\mathcal{F}

IV
Gyromagnetic memory

from localized sources

We conclude this work with an estimate of the gyromagnetic effect produced by nonrelativistic localized sources. This is achieved in two different ways. First, we directly solve (
2
) for a point charge and exhibit the result for two kinds of oscillatory motion. Second, we consider more general, arbitrary localized sources in the multipolar expansion and find the leading nonrelativistic effect in the gyromagnetic memory. This paves the way for similar gravitational analyses in the post-Newtonian framework
Blanchet 2014
.

Point-like sources.
Consider a particle with charge
q
that follows some (accelerated) path
\boldsymbol{R}(t)
in space. The corresponding charge density is
\rho(\boldsymbol{x},t)=q\,\delta^{(3)}(\boldsymbol{x}-\boldsymbol{R}(t))
and the current density is
\boldsymbol{J}(\boldsymbol{x},t)=q\,\boldsymbol{v}(t)\,\delta^{(3)}(\boldsymbol{x}-\boldsymbol{R}(t))
, where
\boldsymbol{v}=\dot{\boldsymbol{R}}
is the particle’s velocity. As a result, the
\boldsymbol{y}
integral of the electromagnetic field (
2
) is straightforward and one finds

\mathcal{A}_{\mu}(\boldsymbol{x},t)=q\int\limits_{-\infty}^{t}\!\!\text{d}t^{\prime}\,v_{\mu}(t^{\prime})\,\frac{\delta(t^{\prime}-t+|\boldsymbol{x}-\boldsymbol{R}(t^{\prime})|)}{|\boldsymbol{x}-\boldsymbol{R}(t^{\prime})|}

(20)

where
v_{0}\equiv 1
and
v_{i}=v^{i}=\dot{R}^{i}
. It now remains to carry out the time integral, taking into account the nonlinear dependence of the delta function on the integration variable
t^{\prime}
. This is most easily done near null infinity, where one may expand
t-|\boldsymbol{x}-\boldsymbol{R}(t^{\prime})|=u+\boldsymbol{n}\cdot\boldsymbol{R}(t^{\prime})+\mathcal{O}(1/r)
and the electromagnetic field (
20
) gives the following special case of eq. (
3
):

\mathcal{A}_{\mu}(\boldsymbol{x},t)=\frac{q}{r}\frac{v_{\mu}(t_{*})}{1-\boldsymbol{n}\cdot\boldsymbol{v}(t_{*})}+\mathcal{O}(r^{-2})\,.

(21)

Here

is the root of the transcendental equation

(22)

\displaystyle t_{*}

\displaystyle=u+\boldsymbol{n}\cdot\boldsymbol{R}\bigg(u+\boldsymbol{n}\cdot\boldsymbol{R}\Big(u+\boldsymbol{n}\cdot\boldsymbol{R}\big(\cdots(u)\cdots\big)\Big)\bigg)\,.

(23)

V
Conclusion

This paper was devoted to a subleading electromagnetic memory effect that affects the orientation of magnetic dipoles; it mimics the gyroscopic gravitational memory effect of
Seraj and Oblak 2021
;
Seraj and Oblak 2022
. Our main results are the precession rate (
10
) and the ensuing memory (
12
), both of which are deeply related to the magnetic (parity-odd) structure of the electromagnetic radiative phase space.

A sharp difference between the electromagnetic and gravitational setups is the simplicity of the former. In particular, we provided explicit formulas [
e.g.
(
28
) and (
37
)] for the precession rate of magnetic dipoles due to bounded sources of electromagnetic radiation. Carrying out the analogous computation for gravity will undoubtedly be illuminating, but it is also much harder. We hope to address this problem in the future.

A natural question is whether gyromagnetic memory and/or its gravitational version can be observed. Again, the electromagnetic setup has a clear advantage here, since it can be produced in highly controlled experiments while the detection of gravitational waves has only been possible for a few years
Abbott et al. 2016
. Note that electromagnetic effects sensitive to the helicity (
19
) are known to occur in Nature,
e.g.
in magnetized plasmas (see
Maleknejad 2023
and references therein). One may thus expect gyromagnetic memory to be very much within the reach of current experiments.

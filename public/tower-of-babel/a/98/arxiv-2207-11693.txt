The following benchmark results showcase the relationship between uniform reflection and induction in the context of first order arithmetic:

\mathsf{EA}+\mathrm{RFN}(\mathsf{EA})\ =\ \mathsf{PA},

\mathsf{EA}

\mathsf{PA}+\mathrm{RFN}(\mathsf{PA})\ =\ \mathsf{PA}+\mathrm{TI}(\varepsilon_{0}).

This note is concerned with uniform reflection in the context of second order arithmetic.

Anyone familiar with (a bit of) ordinal analysis will readily realize that (3) and (4) hold because of the unlimited amount of induction, respectively transfinite induction up to

T_{0}+\mathrm{WF}(\varepsilon_{0})\vdash\mathrm{RFN}(T)

A more subtle point is that the restriction to finitely axiomatizable theories is a necessary one, as the result does not apply to recursively enumerable theories
T_{0}

The assumption plays a role in the direction from (transfinite) induction to reflection. The reason, roughly speaking, is that an infinite theory may not prove that all its axioms are true. In fact, for sufficiently strong theories, such condition entails finite
axiomatizability.
Note that main subsystems of reverse mathematics, the so-called

We refer to Section

2.
Basics

2.1.
First order arithmetic

For the purposes of this paper, let the language of first order arithmetic consist of finitely many symbols including
0,1,+,\times,=,<
. As usual,
\varDelta_{0}=\varPi_{0}=\varSigma_{0}
denotes the class of formulas built up from atomic formulas by means of boolean connectives and bounded quantifiers of the form
\exists x<t
and
\forall x<t
, where
t
is a term. The arithmetic hierarchy for
n>0
is thus defined:
\varPi_{n+1}=\{\forall x\,\varphi(x)\mid\varphi(x)\in\varSigma_{n}\}
and dually
\varSigma_{n+1}=\{\exists x\,\varphi(x)\colon\varphi(x)\in\varPi_{n}\}
.

2.2.
Reflection

We assume a primitive recursive Gödel numbering
\raisebox{-0.2639pt}{$\ulcorner$}\hskip-0.99998pt\varphi\hskip-0.99998pt\raisebox{-0.2639pt}{$\urcorner$}
of formulas.
The uniform reflection principle
\mathrm{RFN}(T)
for a theory
T
is a schema consisting of formulas

\forall x\,(\mathrm{Pr}_{T}(\raisebox{1.74994pt}{$\ulcorner$}\hskip-2.5pt\varphi(\dot{x})\hskip-2.5pt\raisebox{1.74994pt}{$\urcorner$})\rightarrow\varphi(x)),

where
\varphi(x)
is a formula with at most the displayed free variable and
\mathrm{Pr}_{T}(y)
is a canonical provability predicate for
T
(cf.
[
5
,
6
]
). The formula
\mathrm{Pr}_{T}(\raisebox{1.74994pt}{$\ulcorner$}\hskip-2.5pt\varphi(\dot{x})\hskip-2.5pt\raisebox{1.74994pt}{$\urcorner$})
can be seen as an abbreviation of

\exists y\,(\mathrm{Pr}_{T}(y)\land\sigma(\raisebox{-0.2639pt}{$\ulcorner$}\hskip-0.99998pt\varphi\hskip-0.99998pt\raisebox{-0.2639pt}{$\urcorner$},x,y)).

Here,
\sigma(u,x,y)
is a
\varSigma_{1}
formula defining the primitive recursive operation
(\raisebox{-0.2639pt}{$\ulcorner$}\hskip-0.99998pt\varphi\hskip-0.99998pt\raisebox{-0.2639pt}{$\urcorner$},n)\mapsto\raisebox{1.74994pt}{$\ulcorner$}\hskip-2.5pt\varphi(\bar{n}/v)\hskip-2.5pt\raisebox{1.74994pt}{$\urcorner$}
, where
v
is the only free variable of
\varphi
and
\varphi(\bar{n}/v)
denotes the substitution of the numeral
\bar{n}
for
v
in
\varphi
. For ease of notation, we omit the overline notation for numerals when writing
\raisebox{-0.2639pt}{$\ulcorner$}\hskip-0.99998pt\varphi\hskip-0.99998pt\raisebox{-0.2639pt}{$\urcorner$}
inside a formula.

2.3.
Second order arithmetic

The language of second order arithmetic is two-sorted, with
first order variables
x,y,z,\ldots
intended to range over natural numbers, and
second order variables
X,Y,Z,\ldots
intended to range over sets of natural numbers. It is obtained from the language of first order arithmetic by adding a membership relation symbol
\in
connecting the two sorts. The equality relation symbol
=
only applies to first order terms.

The classes of formulas
\varDelta^{0}_{0}
,
\varPi^{0}_{n}
,
\varSigma^{0}_{n}
, are defined just as in the first order case, but now set parameters are allowed. In this context, a formula is arithmetic if it does not contain set quantifiers, that is, quantifications of the form
\forall X
and
\exists X
. Note that an arithmetic formula may contain free set variables. A formula is
\varPi^{1}_{n}
if it is of the form

\forall X_{1}\,\exists X_{2}\cdots QX_{n}\,\varphi
,
where
\varphi
is arithmetic. The definition of
\varSigma^{1}_{n}
formulas is dual.

The theory known as (full) second order arithmetic, often denoted
{\sf Z}_{2}
, is given by the usual first order axioms for zero, successor, addition, multiplication, and less than relation, together with comprehension and induction schemata

\exists X\,\forall x\,(x\in X\leftrightarrow\varphi(x)),

\varphi(0)\land\forall x\,(\varphi(x)\rightarrow\varphi(x+1))\rightarrow\forall x\,\varphi(x),

where
\varphi
ranges over all formulas.
Note that every instance of induction can be obtained from comprehension and the induction axiom

0\in X\land\forall x\,(x\in X\rightarrow x+1\in X)\rightarrow\forall x\,(x\in X).

The main subsystems of reverse mathematics
[
21
]
, also known as the Big Five, are obtained by prescribing how much induction one can use and what sets one can form
(in terms of comprehension or set existence axioms). These are

\mathsf{RCA}_{0}
(Recursive Comprehension Axiom),
\mathsf{WKL}_{0}
(Weak König’s Lemma),
\mathsf{ACA}_{0}
(Arithmetical Comprehension Axiom),
\mathsf{ATR}_{0}
(Arithmetical Transfinite Recursion) and
\varPi^{1}_{1}
-
\mathsf{CA}_{0}
(Comprehension for
\varPi^{1}_{1}
formulas).
\mathsf{RCA}_{0}
, the standard base theory of reverse mathematics, consists of
\varDelta^{0}_{1}
comprehension and
\varSigma^{0}_{1}
induction. It is well known that
\mathsf{RCA}_{0}
is a conservative extension of
\mathrm{I}\varSigma_{1}
, that is, Peano arithmetic
\mathsf{PA}
with induction restricted to
\varSigma_{1}
formulas. The theory
\mathsf{ACA}_{0}
consists of comprehension and induction for all arithmetic formulas.

2.4.
Partial truth definitions

In first order arithmetic one has access for any
n\geq 1
to a
\varSigma_{n}
(resp.
\varPi_{n}
) partial truth definition for the class
\varSigma_{n}
(resp.
\varPi_{n}
) over
\mathrm{I}\varSigma_{1}
. The construction involves a partial truth definition for
\varDelta_{0}
formulas, of complexity
\varDelta_{1}
over
\mathrm{I}\varSigma_{1}
(cf.
[
9
, Ch. 1, Sect. 1(d)]
). By the same token, one can construct partial truth definitions for formulas of bounded complexity, say of bounded rank, over
\mathrm{I}\varSigma_{1}
(cf.
[
23
, 1.5.4]
).

In the context of second order arithmetic, one can define truth predicates for arithmetic formulas and
\varPi^{1}_{n}
formulas but they fail to be provably so over weak fragments such as
\mathsf{RCA}_{0}
. However, for the purposes of this paper, it will be sufficient to work with partial truth definitions for formulas of bounded complexity. Let
R_{m}
denote the class of formulas of rank at most
m
and let
P_{n,m}
denote the (closure under subformulas of the) class of
\varPi^{1}_{n}
formulas with arithmetic matrix of rank at most
m
. We define the rank as usual: the rank is a natural number and the rank of a compound formula is bigger than the rank of its components. Notice that we make no distinction between number and set quantifiers. The construction of partial truth definitions in first order arithmetic easily extends to arithmetic formulas with set parameters.
In particular, for any choice of
n
and
m
, one obtains a partial truth predicate for
R_{m}
and a
\varPi^{1}_{n}
truth predicate for
P_{n,m}
formulas over
\mathsf{RCA}_{0}
.

We will use partial truth definitions of the first kind (for
R_{m}
) in the proof of Theorem
3.1
. In Theorem
5.1
we will choose a
\varPi^{1}_{n}
partial truth definition for sufficiently many
\varPi^{1}_{n}
formulas, namely, for a sufficiently large class of the form
P_{n,m}
.

At one point (see Lemma
5.2
) we will use the fact that
\varPi^{1}_{n}
(resp.
\varSigma^{1}_{n}
) uniform reflection can be finitely axiomatized over
\mathsf{ACA}_{0}
for each
n\geq 1
. This can be attained by means of universal formulas. We say that a
\varPi^{1}_{n}
formula
\varphi_{U}(e,x)
with displayed free variables is universal
\varPi^{1}_{n}
in a theory
T
if for all
\varPi^{1}_{n}
formulas
\varphi(x)
,
T
proves
\forall x\,(\varphi(x)\leftrightarrow\varphi_{U}(\raisebox{-0.2639pt}{$\ulcorner$}\hskip-0.99998pt\varphi\hskip-0.99998pt\raisebox{-0.2639pt}{$\urcorner$},x))
.
Universal
\varSigma^{1}_{n}
formulas are defined in the exact same way. The existence in
\mathsf{ACA}_{0}
of universal
\varPi^{1}_{n}
and
\varSigma^{1}_{n}
formulas for every
n\geq 1
is basically folklore. There is some leeway in designing universal formulas. For example, one possible route involves the following two steps in the construction of a universal
\varPi^{1}_{1}
formula with set parameters; it is then routine to build up universal
\varPi^{1}_{n}
and
\varSigma^{1}_{n}
formulas for all
n\geq 1
. First, given a
\varSigma^{0}_{1}
formula
\varphi(x,f,X_{1},\ldots X_{k})
, where
f
denotes a function from
\omega
to
\omega
, one can primitive recursively find an index
e
such that

\displaystyle\varphi(x,f,X_{1},\ldots,X_{k})

\displaystyle\leftrightarrow\{e\}^{f\oplus X_{1}\oplus\cdots\oplus X_{k}}(x)\downarrow\,

\displaystyle\leftrightarrow\exists s\,\vartheta(e,x,s,\bar{f}(s),\bar{X}_{1}(s),\ldots,\bar{X}_{k}(s)),

where
\vartheta(e,x,s,z)
is a fixed
\varDelta^{0}_{0}
formula,
\bar{f}(s)
encodes the sequence
{\langle f(0),\ldots,f(s-1)\rangle}
and
\bar{X}_{i}(s)
encodes the first
s
bits of the characteristic function of
X_{i}
. This version of the enumeration theorem can be formalized in
\mathsf{RCA}_{0}
(cf.
[
21
, Theorem II.2.7, p. 68]
). Second, given a
\varPi^{1}_{1}
formula
\varphi(x,X_{1},\ldots,X_{k})
, one can primitive recursively construct a
\varSigma^{0}_{1}
formula
\varphi_{0}(x,f,X_{1},\ldots,X_{k})
such that
\varphi(x,X_{1},\ldots,X_{k})\leftrightarrow\forall f\,\varphi_{0}(x,f,X_{1},\ldots,X_{k})
. This is Kleene normal form theorem for
\varPi^{1}_{1}
formulas and can be formalized in
\mathsf{ACA}_{0}
(cf.
[
21
, Lemma V.1.4, p. 169]
). Another approach (cf.
[
16
, Sect. 2.1]
) is to further formalize the second step with the help of a partial truth definition for
\varSigma^{0}_{1}
formulas.

We refer the reader to
[
16
, Sect. 2.1]
for more background on partial truth predicates in second order arithmetic.

3.
The uniform reflection principle in analysis

In this section we outline a proof of the following theorem.

Theorem 3.1

(Essentially, Kreisel and Lévy
[
12
]
)
.

Let
T_{0}\supseteq\mathsf{RCA}_{0}
be a finitely axiomatizable second order arithmetic theory.
Let
T
be
T_{0}
plus the schema of full induction. Then

(3)

T_{0}+\mathrm{RFN}(T_{0})=T.

(4)

T_{0}+\mathrm{RFN}(T)=T_{0}+\mathrm{TI}(\varepsilon_{0}).

Proof.

(3) For the forward direction note that for every standard
n
the formula

\varphi(0)\land\forall x\,(\varphi(x)\rightarrow\varphi(x+1))\rightarrow\varphi(\bar{n})

is provable in classical logic, and hence in any theory whatsoever. The construction of such a proof is primitive recursive (indeed elementary recursive) in
n
. This can be formalized in
\mathsf{RCA}_{0}
. Therefore

T_{0}\vdash\forall x\,\mathrm{Pr}_{T_{0}}(\raisebox{1.74994pt}{$\ulcorner$}\hskip-2.5pt\varphi(0)\land\forall x\,(\varphi(x)\rightarrow\varphi(x+1))\rightarrow\varphi(\dot{x})\hskip-2.5pt\raisebox{1.74994pt}{$\urcorner$}).

It follows that

T_{0}+\mathrm{RFN}(T_{0})\vdash\forall x\,(\varphi(0)\land\forall x\,(\varphi(x)\rightarrow\varphi(x+1))\rightarrow\varphi(x)),

which is classically equivalent to induction for
\varphi
.

For the other direction, we use cut elimination for classical logic, which is available in
\mathsf{RCA}_{0}
. More in detail, fix a sentence
\psi
axiomatizing
T_{0}
and let
\varphi(x)
be given . We aim to show

T\vdash\mathrm{Pr}_{T_{0}}(\raisebox{1.74994pt}{$\ulcorner$}\hskip-2.5pt\varphi(\dot{x})\hskip-2.5pt\raisebox{1.74994pt}{$\urcorner$})\rightarrow\varphi(x).

For convenience, let us consider a Tait style sequent calculus for classical logic.
4
4

4

Tait one-sided calculi for first order classical logic (with equality) and cut elimination thereof are presented in e.g.
[
22
, Sect. 3.6]
and
[
1
, Sects. 1.2, 2.1.2 and 3.1.1]
. Here, of course, we are dealing with a two-sorted variant of any such calculus.

We reason informally in
T
. If
\varphi(\bar{n})
is provable in
T_{0}
, then there is a finite cut-free proof of the sequent
\neg\psi,\varphi(\bar{n})
. By induction on the height of the proof, one shows that every sequent in the proof is true. Now,
\psi
is true, and so
\varphi(\bar{n})
must be true. The induction argument can be formalized in
\sf RCA
with the aid of a partial truth predicate for
R_{m}
such that both
\neg\psi
and
\varphi(x)
are in
R_{m}
. Note in fact that by the subformula property a cut-free proof consists only of subformulas of the end sequent.

(4) The proof that
T_{0}+\mathrm{RFN}(T)
entails
\mathrm{TI}(\varepsilon_{0})
is based on a straightforward generalization of Gentzen’s lifting result
[
8
, p. 293]
(i.e., the closure of provable transfinite induction under
\alpha\mapsto\omega^{\alpha}
over
\mathsf{PA}
). Let
\mathrm{Prog}(\varphi)
be a shorthand for
\forall x\,(\forall y\prec x\,\varphi(y)\rightarrow\varphi(x))
. Then for every formula
\varphi(x)
and for every standard number
k
,
T
proves

\mathrm{Prog}(\varphi)\rightarrow\forall x\prec\omega_{k}\,\varphi(x),

where
\omega_{0}=1
and
\omega_{k+1}=\omega^{\omega_{k}}
. The construction of such a proof is uniform and gives a primitive recursive function that accepts a
\varPi^{1}_{n}
formula
\varphi(x)
and a standard number
k>0
as inputs and outputs a proof in
\mathsf{RCA}_{0}
plus induction for
\varPi^{1}_{n+k}
formulas of
\mathrm{Prog}(\varphi)\rightarrow\forall x\prec\omega_{k+1}\,\varphi(x)
. This formalizes in
\mathsf{RCA}_{0}
. The upshot is that

T_{0}\vdash\forall z\,\mathrm{Pr}_{T}(\raisebox{1.74994pt}{$\ulcorner$}\hskip-2.5pt\mathrm{Prog}(\varphi)\rightarrow\forall x\prec\omega_{\dot{z}}\,\varphi(x)\hskip-2.5pt\raisebox{1.74994pt}{$\urcorner$}).

Therefore

T_{0}+\mathrm{RFN}(T)\vdash\forall z\,(\mathrm{Prog}(\varphi)\rightarrow\forall x\prec\omega_{z}\,\varphi(x)).

The conclusion follows.

The converse direction (cf. the proof of
[
12
, Theorem 14]
) is based on the fact that in a suitable infinitary system for
\omega
-arithmetic one can eliminate cuts and then prove by transfinite induction on

\varepsilon_{0}
that every sequent in a cut-free
\omega
-proof is true.

More in detail. Let
\psi
be a sentence axiomatizing
T_{0}
. Given a formula
\varphi
, we aim to show

T_{0}+\mathrm{TI}(\varepsilon_{0})\vdash\mathrm{Pr}_{T}(\raisebox{1.74994pt}{$\ulcorner$}\hskip-2.5pt\varphi(\dot{x})\hskip-2.5pt\raisebox{1.74994pt}{$\urcorner$})\rightarrow\varphi(x).

Again, let us consider a two-sorted extension of a finitary Tait sequent calculus for
\mathsf{PA}
, call it
\mathsf{PA}^{2}
, and its infinitary counterpart with the
\omega

\mathsf{PA}^{2}_{\infty}

The proof of uniform reflection from (transfinite) induction relies on partial truth definitions and the assumption that

The theory

Spectral extrema of K s , t K_{s,t} -minor free graphs–On a conjecture of M. Tait Supported by the National Natural Science Foundation of China (Nos. 11971445, 11771141 and 12011530064).
Mingqing Zhai, Huiqiu Lin

Abstract




Abstract

Minors play an important role in extremal graph theory and spectral extremal graph theory.
Tait [The Colin de Verdière parameter, excluded minors, and the spectral radius,
J. Combin. Theory Ser. A 166 (2019) 42–58] determined the maximum spectral radius and characterized the unique extremal graph
for
K_{r}
-minor free graphs of sufficiently large order
n
,
he also made great progress on

K_{s,t}
-minor free graphs and posed a conjecture: Let
2\leq s\leq t
and
n-s+1=pt+q
,
where
n
is sufficiently large and
1\leq q\leq t.

Then
K_{s-1}\nabla(pK_{t}\cup K_{q})
is the unique extremal graph with the maximum spectral radius
over all
n
-vertex
K_{s,t}
-minor free graphs.
In this paper, Tait’s conjecture is completely solved.
We also determine the maximum spectral radius and its extremal graphs for
n
-vertex
K_{1,t}
-minor free graphs.
To prove our results, some spectral and structural tools, such as,
local edge maximality, local degree sequence majorization,
double eigenvectors transformation, are used to deduce structural properties of extremal graphs.

Keywords:

K_{s,t}
; minor; spectral radius; extremal graph; majorization

05C50; 05C35

Introduction

Given a graph
H
, a graph is said to be
H
-free
if it does not contain
H
as a subgraph.
The classic
Turán’s problem
asks what is the maximum size of an
H
-free graph of order
n
,
where the maximum size is known as the
Turán number
of
H
and denoted by
ex(n,H)
.
The study of Turán’s problem can be dated back at least to Mantel
[
22
]
in 1907,
who showed that
ex(n,K_{3})\leq\lfloor n^{2}/4\rfloor
.
Mantel’s theorem was extended by Turán’s theorem in 1941
[
33
]
.
Since then, Turán’s problem and many kinds of its variations have been paid much attention
and a considerable number of influential results in extremal graph theory have been obtained
(see for example, a survey,
[
14
]
).
In contrast, the spectral extremal problem asks: given a graph
H
,
what is the maximum spectral radius of an
H
-free graph of order
n
?
In the past decades much research has been done on spectral extremal graph theory,
see
K_{r}

[
2
,
36
]
,
K_{s,t}

[
1
,
29
]
,
M_{k}

[
13
]
,
C_{k,q}

[
21
]
,

P_{k}

[
27
]
,
F_{k}

[
6
]
,
W_{2k+1}

[
5
]
,
\bigcup_{i=1}^{k}S_{a_{i}}

[
7
]
,

\bigcup_{i=1}^{k}P_{a_{i}}

[
8
]
,
C_{4}

[
28
,
40
]
,
C_{6}

[
38
]
,
consecutive cycles
[
15
,
19
,
25
,
23
,
39
]
and a survey
[
26
]
.

Given two graphs
H
and
G
,
H
is a
minor
of
G
if
H
can be obtained from a
subgraph of
G
by contracting edges. A graph is said to be
H-minor free
,
if it does not contain
H
as a minor.
Let
A(G)
be the adjacency matrix of
G
and
\rho(G)
be its spectral radius.
Recently, Nikiforov
[
24
]
and Tait
[
31
,
32
]

studied the following spectral extremal problem.

Problem 1.1
.

Given a graph
H
or a family
\mathbb{H}
,
what is the maximum spectral radius of an
H
-minor (
\mathbb{H}
-minor) free graph of order
n
?

Problem
1.1
was initially paid attention in 1990.
Cvetković and Rowlinson
[
10
]
conjectured
that
\rho(G)\leq\rho(K_{1}\nabla P_{n-1})
for any outerplanar graph
G

with equality if and only if
G\cong K_{1}\nabla P_{n-1}
.
Boots and Royle
[
3
]
and independently Cao and Vince
[
4
]
conjectured
that
\rho(G)\leq\rho(K_{2}\nabla P_{n-2})
for any planar graph
G
of order
n\geq 9

with equality if and only if
G\cong K_{2}\nabla P_{n-2}
.
Subsequently, many scholars contributed to these two conjectures
(see
[
4
,
16
,
17
,
30
]
).
Ellingham and Zha
[
12
]
showed that
\rho(G)\leq 2+\sqrt{2n-6}
for a planar graph
G
.
Dvořák and Mohar
[
42
]
proved that
\rho(G)\leq\sqrt{8\Delta-16}+3.47

for a planar graph
G
with maximum degree
\Delta
.
In 2017, Tait and Tobin
[
31
]
confirmed these two old conjectures for sufficiently large
n
.
Recently, Lin and Ning
[
20
]
confirmed Cvetković-Rowlinson conjecture completely.
In 2004, Hong
[
18
]

proved that
K_{3}\nabla(n-3)K_{1}
uniquely attains the maximum spectral radius over all
K_{5}
-minor free graphs.
Tait
[
32
]
extended Hong’s result to
K_{r}
-minor free graphs
by showing the unique extremal graph is
K_{r-2}\nabla(n-r+2)K_{1}
.
In 2017, Nikiforov
[
24
]
contributed to
K_{2,t}
-minor free graphs,
and the result was extended to
K_{s,t}
-minor free graphs by Tait as shown in the following theorem.

Theorem 1.1
.

[
32
]

Let
2\leq s\leq t
,
n
be large enough and
G
be an
n
-vertex
K_{s,t}
-minor free graph.
Then

\rho(G)\leq\frac{1}{2}\left(s+t-3+\sqrt{(s+t-3)^{2}+4(s-1)(n-s+1)-4(s-2)(t-1)}\right),

with equality if and only if
t\mid n-s+1
and
G\cong K_{s-1}\nabla\frac{n-s+1}{t}K_{t}
.

It should be noted that,
if
t\nmid n-s+1
then the maximum spectral radius together
with its extremal graph is still unknown for
K_{s,t}
-minor free graphs.
To this end, Tait posed the following conjecture.

Conjecture 1.1
.

[
32
]

Let
2\leq s\leq t
,
n
be large enough and
n-s+1=pt+q
, where
1\leq q\leq t
.
Then, the maximum spectral radius of
n
-vertex
K_{s,t}
-minor free graphs
is attained by the join of
K_{s-1}
with
p
copies of
K_{t}
and a copy of
K_{q}
.

Up to now, Conjecture
1.1
has been confirmed for
s+t=4

[
28
,
40
]
;
s+t=5

[
24
]
;

s+t=6

[

]
; and

(see Theorem
1.1
).
For a graph
G
, let
\overline{G}
be its complement and
S^{k}(G)
be a graph obtained by subdividing
k
times of
an edge
uv
with the minimum degree sum
d_{G}(u)+d_{G}(v)
.
Let
H^{\star}
be the Petersen graph, and
H_{s,t}
be a star forest of order
t+1
, precisely,
the disjoint union of
\lfloor\frac{t+1}{s+1}\rfloor
stars
in which all but at most one are isomorphic to
K_{1,s}
.
In this paper, Conjecture
1.1
is solved.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
2\leq s\leq t
,
n-s+1=pt+q
and
\beta=\lfloor\frac{t+1}{s+1}\rfloor
, where
n
is large enough and
1\leq q\leq t
.
Let
G^{\star}
attain the maximum spectral radius over all
n
-vertex
K_{s,t}
-minor free graphs.
Then

G^{\star}\cong\left\{\begin{aligned} &K_{s-1}\nabla\left((p-1)K_{t}\cup\overline{H^{\star}}\right)&&\hbox{if $q=2$, $t=8$ and $\beta=1$};\\
&K_{s-1}\nabla\left((p-1)K_{t}\cup S^{1}\left(\overline{H_{s,t}}\right)\right)&&\hbox{if $q=\beta=2$};\\
&K_{s-1}\nabla\left((p-q)K_{t}\cup q\overline{H_{s,t}}\right)&&\hbox{if $q\leq 2(\beta-1)$ except $q=\beta=2$};\\
&K_{s-1}\nabla\left(pK_{t}\cup K_{q}\right)&&\hbox{otherwise}.\end{aligned}\right.

It remains
K_{1,t}
-minor free graphs.
Let us first consider connected case. A nice result, due to
Ding, Johnson and Seymour
[
11
]
,
determined the maximum size and constructed its extremal graphs for connected
K_{1,t}
-minor free graphs.
However, it seems difficult to completely characterize the extremal graphs.
Inspired by Ding, Johnson and Seymour, we obtain the following spectral extremal result.

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
t\geq 3
, and
G^{*}
attain
the maximum spectral radius over all
n
-vertex connected
K_{1,t}
-minor free graphs.
Then

G^{*}\cong\left\{\begin{aligned} &\overline{H_{1,t}}&&\hbox{if $n=t+1$};\\
&S^{n-t}(K_{t})&&\hbox{if $n\geq t+2$.}\end{aligned}\right.

By the connected case in Theorem
1.3
, we further solve general case.

Theorem 1.4
.

Let
n\geq t\geq 1
, and
G
be an
n
-vertex
K_{1,t}
-minor free graph.
Then
\rho(G)\leq t-1
, with equality if and only if

G
contains a component either isomorphic to
K_{t}
,
or isomorphic to
K_{t+1}
by deleting
\frac{t+1}{2}
independent edges.

Combining with above results,
the spectral extremal problem on
K_{s,t}
-minor free graphs is completely solved for large enough
n
.
To prove our results, we use some spectral and structural tools,
such as, local edge maximality (see Lemma
2.3
),
local degree sequence majorization (see Lemma
2.7
)
and double eigenvectors transformation to deduce structural properties of extremal graphs.

2
Preliminaries

As usual,
V(G)
is the vertex set and
E(G)
is the edge set of a graph
G
.
The number of vertices and edges of
G
are called its
order
and
size
,
and denoted by
|G|
and
e(G)
, respectively.
Given
u\in V(G)
and a subgraph
H\subseteq G
(possibly
u\notin V(H)
),

N_{V(H)}(u)
is the set of neighbors of
u
in
V(H)
and
d_{V(H)}(u)
is its cardinality.
If
u\in V(H)
, we also use
N_{H}(u)
and
d_{H}(u)
for convenience.
If
S\subseteq V(G)
, then
G[S]
and
G-S
stand for the subgraphs of
G
induced by
S

and
V(G)\setminus S
, respectively.
If
S\subseteq E(G)
, then
G-S
denotes the subgraph obtained from deleting all edges in
S
.
If
A,B\subseteq V(G)
, then
e_{G}(A,B)
denotes the number of edges with one endpoint in
A
and the other in
B
,
and particularly,
e_{G}(A,A)
is simplified by
e_{G}(A)
.

Throughout this section, let
s,t,n
be integers with
2\leq s\leq t
and
n
sufficiently large,

G^{\star}
be the extremal graph with the maximum spectral radius
\rho

over all
n
-vertex
K_{s,t}
-minor free graphs,
and
X=(x_{1},x_{2},\ldots,x_{n})^{T}
be the Perron vector of
G^{\star}
.
Now let us introduce some important lemmas.
The first is due to Chudnovsky, Reed and Seymour.

Lemma 2.1
.

[
11
,
9
]

Let
t\geq 3
and
n\geq t+2
. If
G
is an
n
-vertex connected graph
with no
K_{1,t}
-minor, then
e(G)\leq{t\choose 2}+n-t
, and this is best possible for all
n,t
.

For
s=2
, Nikiforov
[
24
]
proved that
G^{\star}
contains a dominating vertex.
Tait showed the following result for general
s
.
This gives a very important information for
G^{\star}
.

Lemma 2.2
.

[
32
]

G^{\star}
contains a clique dominating set
K

with
|K|=s-1
.

By Lemma
2.2
,
we can observe that
G^{\star}
is
K_{s,t}
-minor free
if and only if
G^{\star}-K
satisfies the following property:

K_{a,b}
-minor free for all positive integers
a,b
with
a+b=t+1
and
1\leq a\leq\min\{s,\lfloor\frac{t+1}{2}\rfloor\}
.
For convenience, we call it
(s,t)
-property
.

Many known results indicate that an extremal graph with the maximum size
usually is not an extremal graph with the maximum spectral radius.
This observation also happens on
K_{s,t}
-minor free graphs for general
s
and
t
.
However, the following lemma implies that
G^{\star}
has a
local edge maximality
.

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
H
be a disjoint union of several components of
G^{\star}-K
such that
|H|\leq N
(a constant).
If
H^{\prime}
also has
(s,t)
-property with
V(H^{\prime})=V(H)
, then
e(H^{\prime})\leq e(H)
.

Proof.

For convenience, let

X_{0}=\sum_{v\in K}x_{v},~~~x_{1}=\max_{v\in V(H)}x_{v}~~~\hbox{and}~~~x_{2}=\min_{v\in V(H)}x_{v}.

Since
H
has
(s,t)
-property, then
H
is
K_{1,t}
-minor free
and hence
\Delta(H)<t
. So,
\rho x_{1}<X_{0}+tx_{1}
and
\rho x_{2}\geq X_{0}
.
It follows that

\displaystyle x_{1}<\frac{X_{0}}{\rho-t}~~~\hbox{and}~~~x_{2}\geq\frac{X_{0}}{\rho}.

(1)

We now give a claim, which will be frequently used in the subsequent proof.

Claim 2.1
.

Let
a,b
be two constants with
a>b
. Then
ax_{2}>bx_{1}
and
ax_{2}^{2}>bx_{1}^{2}
.

Proof.

By Lemma
2.2
,

\Delta(G^{\star})=n-1
and thus
\rho\geq\rho(K_{1,n-1})=\sqrt{n-1}.

Since
n
is large enough and
a,b,t
are constants,
we can easily have

ax_{2}-bx_{1}>X_{0}\left(\frac{a}{\rho}-\frac{b}{\rho-t}\right)>0,

and similarly,
ax_{2}^{2}>bx_{1}^{2}
.
∎

Let
G^{\prime}=G^{\star}-E(H)+E(H^{\prime})
and
\rho^{\prime}=\rho(G^{\prime})
.
Then
G^{\prime}
is also
K_{s,t}
-minor free.
By the way of contradiction, assume that
e(H^{\prime})\geq e(H)+1
.
Then by Claim
2.1
,

\displaystyle\rho^{\prime}-\rho

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle X^{T}(A(G^{\prime})-A(G^{\star}))X=2\sum\limits_{uv\in E(H^{\prime})}x_{u}x_{v}-2\sum\limits_{uv\in E(H)}x_{u}x_{v}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle 2e(H^{\prime})x_{2}^{2}-2e(H)x_{1}^{2}

\displaystyle>

\displaystyle 0,

a contradiction with the maximality of
\rho(G^{\star})
. So
e(H^{\prime})\leq e(H)
.
∎

For a graph
H
and two vertices
u,v\in V(H)
(possibly,
uv\notin E(H)
),
we use
d_{H}(uv)
to denote
d_{H}(u)+d_{H}(v)
for convenience.

Lemma 2.4
.

Let
H
be a disjoint union of several components of
G^{\star}-K
such that
|H|\leq N
(a constant),
If
H^{\prime}
also has
(s,t)
-property with
V(H^{\prime})=V(H)
and
e(H^{\prime})=e(H)
,
then

\sum\limits_{uv\in E(H^{\prime})}d_{H}(uv)\leq\sum\limits_{uv\in E(H)}d_{H}(uv),

and if equality holds, then

\sum\limits_{uv\in E(H^{\prime})}d_{H^{\prime}}(uv)\leq\sum\limits_{uv\in E(H)}d_{H}(uv).

Proof.

Let
G^{\prime}=G^{\star}-E(H)+E(H^{\prime})
and
\rho^{\prime}=\rho(G^{\prime})
.
Then
\rho\geq\rho^{\prime}
and

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\rho^{2}(\rho^{\prime}-\rho)\geq\frac{1}{2}\rho^{2}X^{T}(A(G^{\prime})-A(G^{\star}))X=\sum\limits_{uv\in E(H^{\prime})}\rho x_{u}\rho x_{v}-\sum\limits_{uv\in E(H)}\rho x_{u}\rho x_{v}.

(2)

Recall that

X_{0}=\sum_{v\in K}x_{v},~~~x_{1}=\max_{v\in V(H)}x_{v}~~~\hbox{and}~~~x_{2}=\min_{v\in V(H)}x_{v}.

Thus, for any
v\in V(H)
, we have

\displaystyle X_{0}+d_{H}(v)x_{2}\leq\rho x_{v}\leq X_{0}+d_{H}(v)x_{1}.

(3)

It follows that

\sum\limits_{uv\in E(H^{\prime})}\rho x_{u}\rho x_{v}\geq\sum\limits_{uv\in E(H^{\prime})}(X_{0}+d_{H}(u)x_{2})(X_{0}+d_{H}(v)x_{2}),

\sum\limits_{uv\in E(H)}\rho x_{u}\rho x_{v}\leq\sum\limits_{uv\in E(H)}(X_{0}+d_{H}(u)x_{1})(X_{0}+d_{H}(v)x_{1}).

Now let

a=\sum\limits_{uv\in E(H^{\prime})}d_{H}(uv),~~b=\sum_{uv\in E(H)}d_{H}(uv),~~c=\sum\limits_{uv\in E(H)}d_{H}(u)d_{H}(v),

and suppose to the contrary that
a\geq b+1.
Then

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{uv\in E(H^{\prime})}\rho x_{u}\rho x_{v}-\sum\limits_{uv\in E(H)}\rho x_{u}\rho x_{v}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle aX_{0}x_{2}-bX_{0}x_{1}-cx_{1}^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle X_{0}\left(\left(a-\frac{1}{2}\right)x_{2}-bx_{1}\right)+\left(\frac{1}{2}X_{0}x_{2}-cx_{1}^{2}\right).

By Claim
2.1
,
\left(a-\frac{1}{2}\right)x_{2}>bx_{1}.

And by (
1
),

\frac{1}{2}X_{0}x_{2}-cx_{1}^{2}>X^{2}_{0}\left(\frac{1}{2\rho}-\frac{c}{(\rho-t)^{2}}\right)>0,

since
\rho\geq\sqrt{n-1}
and
n
is large enough.
Combining with (
2
), we have
\rho^{\prime}>\rho
,
a contradiction. Hence, the first inequality holds.

We now prove the second inequality.
Let
a=b
and
Y=(y_{1},y_{2},\ldots,y_{n})^{T}
be the Perron vector of
G^{\prime}
such that

y_{i}
and
x_{i}
correspond the the same vertex
i
.
Suppose to the contrary that
a^{\prime}\geq b+1
,
and assume that

Y_{0}=\sum_{v\in K}y_{v},~~~y_{1}=\max_{v\in V(H^{\prime})}y_{v}~~~\hbox{and}~~~y_{2}=\min_{v\in V(H^{\prime})}y_{v}.

Similar with (
3
), we have

\displaystyle Y_{0}+d_{H^{\prime}}(v)y_{2}\leq\rho^{\prime}y_{v}\leq Y_{0}+d_{H^{\prime}}(v)y_{1}.

(4)

for any
v\in V(H^{\prime})
. It follows that

\displaystyle y_{1}<\frac{Y_{0}}{\rho^{\prime}-t}~~~\hbox{and}~~~y_{2}\geq\frac{Y_{0}}{\rho^{\prime}}.

(5)

Now we can see that

\displaystyle\rho^{\prime}\rho(\rho^{\prime}-\rho)Y^{T}X

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\rho^{\prime}\rho\left((A(G^{\prime})Y)^{T}X-Y^{T}(A(G)X)\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{uv\in E(H^{\prime})}(\rho x_{u}\rho^{\prime}y_{v}+\rho x_{v}\rho^{\prime}y_{u})-\sum\limits_{uv\in E(H)}(\rho x_{u}\rho^{\prime}y_{v}+\rho x_{v}\rho^{\prime}y_{u}).

Now let

a^{\prime}=\sum_{uv\in E(H^{\prime})}d_{H^{\prime}}(uv),b^{\prime}=\sum_{uv\in E(H)}d_{H^{\prime}}(uv),c^{\prime}=\sum\limits_{uv\in E(H)}\left(d_{H}(u)d_{H^{\prime}}(v)+d_{H}(v)d_{H^{\prime}}(u)\right).

Note that
e(H)=e(H^{\prime})
. By (

\displaystyle\rho^{\prime}\rho(\rho^{\prime}-\rho)Y^{T}X

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{uv\in E(H^{\prime})}\left((X_{0}+d_{H}(u)x_{2})(Y_{0}+d_{H^{\prime}}(v)y_{2})+(X_{0}+d_{H}(v)x_{2})(Y_{0}+d_{H^{\prime}}(u)y_{2})\right)

\displaystyle-\sum\limits_{uv\in E(H)}\left((X_{0}+d_{H}(u)x_{1})(Y_{0}+d_{H^{\prime}}(v)y_{1})+(X_{0}+d_{H}(v)x_{1})(Y_{0}+d_{H^{\prime}}(u)y_{1})\right)

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle aY_{0}x_{2}+a^{\prime}X_{0}y_{2}-bY_{0}x_{1}-b^{\prime}X_{0}y_{1}-c^{\prime}x_{1}y_{1}.

Combining with

, we have

\displaystyle\rho^{\prime}\rho(\rho^{\prime}-\rho)Y^{T}X

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle aY_{0}x_{2}+(a+1)X_{0}y_{2}-aY_{0}x_{1}-aX_{0}y_{1}-c^{\prime}x_{1}y_{1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle X_{0}\left(\left(a+\frac{1}{2}\right)y_{2}-ay_{1}\right)+\left(\frac{X_{0}y_{2}}{2}+aY_{0}\left(x_{2}-x_{1}\right)-c^{\prime}x_{1}y_{1}\right).

By Claim
2.1
, we have

\left(a+\frac{1}{2}\right)y_{2}>ay_{1}.

And by (

\frac{X_{0}y_{2}}{2}+aY_{0}\left(x_{2}-x_{1}\right)-c^{\prime}x_{1}y_{1}>X_{0}Y_{0}\left(\frac{1}{2\rho^{\prime}}+\frac{a}{\rho}-\frac{a}{\rho-t}-\frac{c^{\prime}}{(\rho-t)(\rho^{\prime}-t)}\right)>0,

since

and

are constants.
It follows that
\rho^{\prime}\rho(\rho^{\prime}-\rho)Y^{T}X>0
, that is,
\rho^{\prime}>\rho
, a contradiction.
This completes the proof.

Definition 2.1
.

Let
X=(x_{1},x_{2},\ldots,x_{n})^{T}
and
Y=(y_{1},y_{2},\ldots,y_{n})^{T}
be two decreasing real vectors.
If

\sum_{i=1}^{k}x_{i}\leq\sum_{i=1}^{k}y_{i},~~k=1,2,\ldots,n,

then we say that
X
is
weakly majorized
by

Y
and denote it by
X\prec_{w}Y
. If
X\prec_{w}Y

and
\sum_{i=1}^{n}x_{i}=\sum_{i=1}^{n}y_{i}
, then we say
that
X
is
majorized
by
Y
and denote it by

x\prec y
.

The following two lemmas are needed in the proof of Lemma
2.7
,
which is one of our main tools in this paper.

Lemma 2.5
.

[
20
]

Let
X=(x_{1},x_{2},\ldots,x_{n})^{T}
,
Y=(y_{1},y_{2},\ldots,y_{n})^{T}
be two nonnegative
decreasing real vectors. If
X\prec_{w}Y
,
then
\|X\|_{p}\leq\|Y\|_{p}
for
p>1
, with equality holding if and only if
X=Y
.

Lemma 2.6

(Exercises 5 (i), P74,
[
41
]
)
.

Let
X,Y,Z\in R^{n}
be three
decreasing vectors.
If
X\prec Y
, then
X^{T}\cdot Z\leq Y^{T}\cdot Z
.

Let
\pi(G)
be the decreasing sequence of vertex degrees of a graph
G
.
By using majorization, Bıyıkoǧlu and Leydold
[
34
]
showed that,
for two trees
T
and
T^{\prime}
with
|T|=|T^{\prime}|
,
if
\pi(T^{\prime})\prec\pi(T)
, then
\rho(T^{\prime})\leq\rho(T).

Unfortunately, this nice tool does not work for general graphs, even for unicyclic graphs.
However, the following lemma implies that
G^{\star}
has a local degree sequence majorization.

Lemma 2.7
.

Let
H
and
H^{\prime}
be defined as in Lemma
2.4
.
Let
\pi(H)=(d_{1},\ldots,d_{|H|})
and
\pi(H^{\prime})=(d^{\prime}_{1},\ldots,d^{\prime}_{|H|})
.
If
\pi(H)\prec\pi(H^{\prime})
, then
\pi(H)=\pi(H^{\prime})
.

Proof.

Let
V(H)=\{v_{1},v_{2}\ldots,v_{|H|}\}
with
d_{H}(v_{i})=d_{i}
.
Since
V(H)=V(H^{\prime})
, by graph isomorphism, we may let
d_{H^{\prime}}(v_{i})=d^{\prime}_{i}
for
i=1,2,\ldots,|H|
.
Suppose to the contrary that
\pi(H)\neq\pi(H^{\prime})
. Then by Lemma
2.5
, we have

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{i=1}^{|H|}{d^{\prime}}_{i}^{2}>\sum\limits_{i=1}^{|H|}d_{i}^{2}.

(6)

Now let
X=Z=\pi(H)
and
Y=\pi(H^{\prime})
in Lemma
2.6
. Then we have

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{i=1}^{|H|}d_{i}^{2}\leq\sum\limits_{i=1}^{|H|}d_{i}d^{\prime}_{i}.

(7)

Furthermore, we see that

\sum\limits_{i=1}^{|H|}d_{i}d^{\prime}_{i}=\sum\limits_{v\in V(H^{\prime})}d_{H}(v)d_{H^{\prime}}(v)=\sum\limits_{uv\in E(H^{\prime})}(d_{H}(u)+d_{H}(v))=\sum\limits_{uv\in E(H^{\prime})}d_{H}(uv).

And similarly,

\sum\limits_{i=1}^{|H|}d_{i}^{2}=\sum\limits_{uv\in E(H)}(d_{H}(u)+d_{H}(v))=\sum\limits_{uv\in E(H)}d_{H}(uv).

It follows from the first inequality of Lemma
2.4
that

\sum\limits_{i=1}^{|H|}d_{i}d^{\prime}_{i}\leq\sum\limits_{i=1}^{|H|}d_{i}^{2}.

Combining with (
7
), we have
\sum\limits_{i=1}^{|H|}d_{i}d^{\prime}_{i}=\sum\limits_{i=1}^{|H|}d_{i}^{2}.

By the second inequality of Lemma
2.4
, we have

\sum\limits_{uv\in E(H^{\prime})}d_{H^{\prime}}(uv)\leq\sum\limits_{uv\in E(H)}d_{H}(uv),

that is,

\sum\limits_{i=1}^{|H|}{d^{\prime}}_{i}^{2}\leq\sum\limits_{i=1}^{|H|}d_{i}^{2},

which contradicts (
6
). So,
\pi(H)=\pi(H^{\prime})
.
∎

|N_{A}(w^{\prime})\cup N_{A}(w^{\prime\prime})|=t-2

and
d_{A}(v)=t-3
for any
v\in N_{A}(w^{\prime})\cup N_{A}(w^{\prime\prime})
.
Now
N_{A}(w^{\prime})\cup N_{A}(w^{\prime\prime})=A\setminus\{v_{0}\}
.
Since
v_{0}\in A_{1}
,
d_{A}(v_{0})\leq t-3
and thus
v_{0}
has a non-neighbor
v_{1}\in N_{A}(w^{\prime})\cup N_{A}(w^{\prime\prime})
.
Therefore,
d_{A}(v_{1})=t-3
, and
v_{1}
dominates
N_{A}(w^{\prime})\cup N_{A}(w^{\prime\prime})
.
Let
v_{1}v_{2}
be an edge between
N_{A}(w^{\prime})
and
N_{A}(w^{\prime\prime})
.
Then
v_{0}
is also a non-neighbor of
v_{2}

(Otherwise,
|N_{G^{*}}(v_{1})\cup N_{G^{*}}(v_{2})|=|A\cup\{u^{*},w^{\prime},w^{\prime\prime}\}|=t+2

and we get a
K_{1,t}
-minor).
Now we see that
d_{A}(v_{0})\leq t-4
and thus
v_{0}\in A_{11}
.
On the other hand, by Lemma
5.4
, we have
x_{w^{\prime\prime}}\leq x_{v_{0}}
.
It follows that
x_{w^{\prime}}+x_{w^{\prime\prime}}\leq x_{u^{*}}+\sum_{v\in A_{11}}x_{v}.

∎

Claim 5.3
.

d_{B}(v)=1
for each vertex
v\in A_{1}
, and thus
e(A,B)=|A_{1}|
.

Proof.

By Lemma
5.3
,
d_{B}(v)\leq 1

for each vertex
v\in A_{1}
.
Suppose that
d_{B}(v_{0})=0
for some
v_{0}\in A_{1}
.
We can see that
\rho x_{u^{*}}=\sum\limits_{v\in A}x_{v}
and

\displaystyle\rho^{2}x_{u^{*}}=\sum\limits_{v\in A}\sum\limits_{u\in N_{G^{*}}(v)}x_{u}=|A|x_{u^{*}}+\sum\limits_{v\in A}d_{A}(v)x_{v}+\sum\limits_{w\in B}d_{A}(w)x_{w}.

(26)

From (
26
) and the definitions of
A_{0}
and
A_{11}
, we have

\displaystyle(\rho^{2}-(t-3)\rho)x_{u^{*}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\rho^{2}x_{u^{*}}-(t-3)\sum\limits_{v\in A}x_{v}

(27)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle|A|x_{u^{*}}+\sum\limits_{v\in A}(d_{A}(v)-t+3)x_{v}+\sum\limits_{w\in B}d_{A}(w)x_{w}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle|A|x_{u^{*}}+\sum\limits_{v\in A_{0}}x_{v}-\sum\limits_{v\in A_{11}}x_{v}+\sum\limits_{w\in B}d_{A}(w)x_{w}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle(|A|+|A_{0}|+e(A,B)-2)x_{u^{*}}+x_{w^{\prime}}+x_{w^{\prime\prime}}-\sum\limits_{v\in A_{11}}x_{v}.

Combining Claim
5.2
with
(\ref{a006})
, we have

(\rho^{2}-(t-3)\rho)x_{u^{*}}\leq(|A|+|A_{0}|+e(A,B)-1)x_{u^{*}}.

Recall that
d_{B}(v)=0
for any
v\in A_{0}
, and
d_{B}(v)\leq 1
for any
v\in A_{1}
. Therefore,

e(A,B)=e(A_{1},B)=\sum\limits_{v\in A_{1}\setminus\{v_{0}\}}d_{B}(v)\leq|A_{1}\setminus\{v_{0}\}|=|A_{1}|-1.

It follows that
\rho^{2}-(t-3)\rho\leq 2(|A|-1)=2(t-2),

which contradicts (
20
).
So,
d_{B}(v)=1
for any
v\in A_{1}
and thus
e(A,B)=e(A_{1},B)=|A_{1}|
.
∎

Claim 5.4
.

|A_{11}|\leq 2
.

Proof.

Suppose to the contrary that
|A_{11}|\geq 3
.
Then
|A\setminus A_{11}|\leq t-4
,
and thus

\rho x_{u^{*}}=\sum\limits_{v\in A_{11}}x_{v}+\sum\limits_{v\in A\setminus A_{11}}x_{v}\leq\sum\limits_{x\in A_{11}}x_{v}+(t-4)x_{u^{*}}.

Therefore, by Lemma
5.2
, we have

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{v\in A_{11}}x_{v}\geq(\rho-t+4)x_{u^{*}}\geq 2x_{u^{*}}.

(28)

Combining with (
27
) and (
28
), we have

\displaystyle(\rho^{2}-(t-3)\rho)x_{u^{*}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle|A|x_{u^{*}}+\sum\limits_{v\in A_{0}}x_{v}-\sum\limits_{v\in A_{11}}x_{v}+\sum\limits_{w\in B}d_{A}(w)x_{w}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle(|A|+|A_{0}|-2)x_{u^{*}}+e(A,B)x_{u^{*}}.

By Claim
5.3
, we have
e(A,B)=|A_{1}|
.
Hence,

\rho^{2}-(t-3)\rho\leq(|A|+|A_{0}|+|A_{1}|-2)=2|A|-2=2(t-2),

which contradicts (
20
).
Thus,
|A_{11}|\leq 2
, as claimed.
∎

Claim 5.5
.

If
|A_{1}|\geq 3
, then
A_{10}
is a clique, where
A_{10}=\{v\in A|d_{A}(v)=t-3\}

Proof.

Recall that
A_{11}=\{v\in A|d_{A}(v)\leq t-4\}
. Then
A_{10}=A_{1}\setminus A_{11}.

Suppose to the contrary that there exist two non-adjacent vertices
v_{1},v_{2}\in A_{10}
.
Since
d_{A}(v_{1})=d_{A}(v_{2})=t-3
, both
v_{1}
and
v_{2}
dominate
A\setminus\{v_{1},v_{2}\}
.
Since
|A_{1}|\geq 3
, we can take a vertex
v_{3}\in A_{1}\setminus\{v_{1},v_{2}\}
.
Furthermore, by Claim
5.3
, there exists a unique vertex
w_{i}\in N_{B}(v_{i})
for
i\in\{1,2,3\}
.
If
w_{3}\not=w_{1}
, then

|N_{G^{*}}(v_{1})\cup N_{G^{*}}(v_{3})|\geq|A\cup\{u^{*},w_{1},w_{3}\}|=t+2.

If
w_{3}\not=w_{2}
, then we similarly have
|N_{G^{*}}(v_{2})\cup N_{G^{*}}(v_{3})|\geq t+2.

If
w_{1}=w_{2}=w_{3}
, then by Claim
5.1
,
|N^{2}(u^{*})|\geq 2

and thus there exist a vertex
w_{4}\in N^{2}(u^{*})\setminus\{w_{1}\}
and a vertex

|N_{G^{*}}(v_{1})\cup N_{G^{*}}(v_{4})|\geq|A\cup\{u^{*},w_{1},w_{4}\}|=t+2.

Thus we always find a
K_{1,t}
-minor, a contradiction.
∎

Now we are ready to give the final proof of the theorem.
By Claim
5.1
,
|A_{1}|\geq|N^{2}(u^{*})|\geq 2
.
If

.
Now we can see that

d_{B}(w)\leq 2
for any

.
Since
G^{*}
is an extremal graph, we can observe that

is a path with both endpoints in

.
This implies that
G^{*}\cong S^{n-t}(K_{t})

It remains the case
|A_{1}|\geq 3
. We shall prove that this is impossible.
By Claim
5.4
,
|A_{11}|\leq 2
. Suppose that
|A_{11}|=|\{v_{1},v_{2}\}|=2
.
For

, since
d_{A}(v_{i})\leq t-4

has at least one non-neighbor

By the definition of
A_{10}
,

By Claim
5.3

Recall that

. Then by

\displaystyle(\rho^{2}-(t-3)\rho)x_{u^{*}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle(|A|+|A_{0}|+e(A,B)-2)x_{u^{*}}+x_{w_{1}}+x_{w_{2}}-\sum\limits_{v\in A_{11}}x_{v}

\displaystyle 2(t-2))x_{u^{*}},

|A_{11}|\leq 1

According to Claim
5.5
,

A_{10}=\{v\in A|d_{A}(v)=t-3\}

Therefore, each vertex

Recall that

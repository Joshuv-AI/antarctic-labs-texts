Von Neumann entropy in a dispersive cavity
Ram Narayan Deb

Abstract
We study the von Neumann entropy of the partial trace of a system of two two-level atoms (qubits) in a dispersive cavity where the atoms are interacting collectively with a single mode electromagnetic field in the cavity. We make a contrast of this entanglement entropy with the spin squeezing property of the system. We find a close relationship between this von Neumann entropy and the spin squeezing of the system. We observe that this entanglement entropy attains its maximum value when the spin squeezing in the system goes to its maximum and is zero when the spin squeezing in the system vanishes. Keywords: Von Neumann entropy, two-level atom, dispersive cavity



I
I. INTRODUCTION

Von Neumann entropy, also called the entanglement entropy, forms an important measure of quantum entanglement in bipartite states of quantum systems
Nielsen
.

Optical cavity, in which the atoms interact with the electromagnetic field, is an important experimental tool through which we can study the various aspects of the atom-field interaction and its dynamics. Cavity QED and cavity related systems are widely studied for producing correlated photons or two-level atoms or both. Dispersive cavity plays a significant role in this field
[2-14]
.

Spin squeezing of a system of two-level atoms is an important experimentally measurable quantity that depends on the amount of quantum correlations established among the atoms
Kitagawa
,

Wineland
. So, there exists a close relationship between the von Neumann entropy of the partial trace of a system of two two-level atoms and spin squeezing in that system.
The problem of finding the entropy for the interaction of a single atom and a quantized field in a dispersive cavity has been considered in Ref.
Moya
.

In this paper we consider a system of two identical two-level atoms each of frequency
\omega_{0}
interacting collectively with a single mode electromagnetic field in a dispersive cavity whose characteristic frequency nearest to
\omega_{0}
is
\omega_{c}

Agarwal
.
The cavity temperature is related with the average number of thermal photons
\bar{n}
present in the cavity. The photon lifetime is represented by

\frac{1}{2k}
, where
k
is the cavity bandwidth.
If
\hat{J}_{+}
,

\hat{J}_{-}
and
\hat{J}_{z}
are the atomic operators and
\hat{a}
,
\hat{a}^{\dagger}
are the bosonic operators of the cavity field mode then the atom-field interaction is governed by the Hamiltonian
Agarwal

\hat{H}_{af}=\hbar\omega_{0}\hat{J}_{z}+\hbar\omega_{c}\hat{a}^{\dagger}\hat{a}+\hbar g(\hat{J}_{+}\hat{a}+\hat{a}^{\dagger}\hat{J}_{-}),

(1)

where
g
is the atom-field coupling constant.

We consider the cavity to be highly detuned, that is,

\delta=\omega_{c}-\omega_{0}
is very large satisfying

|i\delta+k|>>g\sqrt{2}
.
The solution to the master equation for a quantized cavity mode has been described in Ref.
Arevalo
. Quantum state reconstruction in the presence of dissipation has been described in Ref.
Moya1
, that takes into account cavity losses. Now, for the present case the derivation of the effective Hamiltonian describing the dynamics of the atoms in the cavity, using the master equation approach, has been done by Agarwal et al. in Ref.
Agarwal
. If
\delta>>k
, then the contribution due to damping is very small and the effective Hamiltonian
takes the form
Agarwal

\displaystyle\hat{H}_{eff}=\hbar\Delta_{0}[\hat{J}_{+}\hat{J}_{-}+2\bar{n}\hat{J}_{z}]=\hbar\Delta_{0}[\hat{J}^{2}-\hat{J}z^{2}+2\bar{n}\hat{J}_{z}],

(2)

where

\Delta_{0}=\frac{g^{2}\delta}{k^{2}+\delta^{2}}.

(3)

This Hamiltonian is quadratic in population inversion operator
\hat{J}_{z}
. The temperature dependent term in the Hamiltonian produces a simple rotation of the quantum state that has no physical significance. Therefore, we drop it by setting
\bar{n}=0
. So, the effective Hamiltonian reduces to

\hat{H}_{eff}=\hbar\Delta_{0}(\hat{J}^{2}-\hat{J}_{z}^{2}).

(4)

We consider the initial state of the two two-level atoms, before they enter the cavity, to be an atomic coherent state

Radcliffe
,
Arecchi
and after they enter the dispersive cavity the time evolution of the state
is governed by the above Hamiltonian in Eq. (
4
). The resulting state is an
atomic Schr
\ddot{o}
dinger cat state
Agarwal
. In this paper we investigate the dynamics of the von Neumann entropy of partial trace of this state and make a contrast with the spin squeezing dynamics of the system.

The organization of the paper is as follows. In Sec. II
we study the von Neumann entropy of the partial traces of the two two-level atoms in the dispersive cavity. In Sec. III we make a contrast of this entanglement entropy with the spin squeezing dynamics of the whole system.

II
II. Von Neumann entropy of partial traces of two two-level atoms in a dispersive cavity

We assume the initial state of the two two-level atoms, before they enter the dispersive cavity, to be an atomic coherent state which is represented as

|j,\chi\rangle=\frac{1}{(1+\chi^{2})^{j}}\sum_{n=0}^{2j}\sqrt{{}^{2j}C_{n}}\chi^{n}|j,j-n\rangle.

(5)

The quantum number
j=N/2
, where
N
is the number of atoms.
Since, in this case the number of atoms
N=2
, the quantum number
j=\frac{N}{2}=1
. So, the above state reduces to

|1,\chi\rangle=\frac{1}{(1+\chi^{2})}\big[|1,1\rangle+\sqrt{2}\chi|1,0\rangle+\chi^{2}|1,-1\rangle\big].

(6)

Here
\chi=\tan(\theta/2)e^{i\phi}
.
\chi=0
represents all the atoms in their upper states. In the following we assume
\phi=0
as it has no effect on the problem we study here. This state is achieved by, first, sending the atoms in their upper states through an auxillary cavity. The quantity
\theta
is decided by the duration of atom-field interaction in this cavity. These atoms then enter the dispersive cavity where their time evolution is governed by the Hamiltonian in Eq. (
4
).

In the
\{m_{1},m_{2}\}
representation, the time evolved state (with
\hbar=1
) is

\displaystyle|1,\chi,t\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{(1+|\chi|^{2})}\bigg[e^{-i\Delta_{0}t}\bigg|\frac{1}{2},\frac{1}{2}\bigg\rangle+\chi e^{-2i\Delta_{0}t}\bigg|\frac{1}{2},-\frac{1}{2}\bigg\rangle+\chi e^{-2i\Delta_{0}t}\bigg|-\frac{1}{2},\frac{1}{2}\bigg\rangle

(7)

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\chi^{2}e^{-i\Delta_{0}t}\bigg|-\frac{1}{2},-\frac{1}{2}\bigg\rangle\bigg].

Now, if
\hat{J}_{1_{x}}
,
\hat{J}_{1_{y}}
and
\hat{J}_{1_{z}}
are the operators of atom 1 and

\hat{J}_{2_{x}}
,
\hat{J}_{2_{y}}
and

\hat{J}_{2_{z}}
are the operators of atom 2, then,
the mean spin vectors of atoms 1 and 2 are

\langle\hat{\overrightarrow{J}}_{1}\rangle=\langle\hat{J}_{1_{x}}\rangle\hat{i}+\langle\hat{J}_{1_{y}}\rangle\hat{j}+\langle\hat{J}_{1_{z}}\rangle\hat{k}

(8)

and

\langle\hat{\overrightarrow{J}}_{2}\rangle=\langle\hat{J}_{2_{x}}\rangle\hat{i}+\langle\hat{J}_{2_{y}}\rangle\hat{j}+\langle\hat{J}_{2_{z}}\rangle\hat{k},

(9)

where
\hat{i}
,
\hat{j}
and
\hat{k}
are the unit vectors along the positive
x
,
y
and
z
axes respectively.

Now, the density matrix for a pair of qubits
A
and

B
can be written in the Bloch-Fano decomposition as
[22-25]

\displaystyle\rho_{AB}=\frac{1}{4}\bigg({\bf 1}\otimes{\bf 1}+\overrightarrow{\sigma}^{A}.{\bf u}\otimes{\bf 1}+{\bf 1}\otimes\overrightarrow{\sigma}^{B}.{\bf v}+\sum_{i,j=1}^{3}\beta_{ij}\sigma_{i}\sigma_{j}\bigg),

(10)

where

\displaystyle{\bf u}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\langle\sigma_{x}^{A}\rangle\hat{i}+\langle\sigma_{y}^{A}\rangle\hat{j}+\langle\sigma_{z}^{A}\rangle\hat{k},

(11)

\displaystyle{\bf v}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\langle\sigma_{x}^{B}\rangle\hat{i}+\langle\sigma_{y}^{B}\rangle\hat{j}+\langle\sigma_{z}^{B}\rangle\hat{k}.

(12)

From Eq. (
10
), we can obtain two reduced density matrices

\displaystyle\rho_{A}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle Tr_{B}(\rho_{AB})=\frac{1}{2}({\bf 1}+\overrightarrow{\sigma}^{A}.{\bf u})

(13)

\displaystyle\rho_{B}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle Tr_{A}(\rho_{AB})=\frac{1}{2}({\bf 1}+\overrightarrow{\sigma}^{B}.{\bf v}).

(14)

For pure states
|{\bf u}|=|{\bf v}|
. The well known measure of concurrence, for pure states, can be calculated as

\displaystyle C=\sqrt{2[1-Tr{(\rho^{A})}^{2}]}=\sqrt{2[1-Tr{(\rho^{B})}^{2}]}=\sqrt{1-|{\bf u}|^{2}}=\sqrt{1-|{\bf v}|^{2}}.

(15)

The entanglement of formation is given by

\displaystyle F(C)=H\bigg(\frac{1+\sqrt{1-C^{2}}}{2}\bigg)=H\bigg(\frac{1+\sqrt{|{\bf u}|^{2}}}{2}\bigg),

(16)

where

H(x)=-xlog_{2}x-(1-x)log_{2}(1-x).

(17)

Now, for pure states the entanglement of formation is same as the von Neumann entropy of the reduced density matrix of either
A
or
B
.
So, using Eqs. (
16
) and (
17
) the von Neumann entropy of the reduced
density matrix takes the form

\displaystyle E=-\bigg(\frac{1}{2}+\frac{1}{2}|{\bf u}|\bigg)log_{2}\bigg(\frac{1}{2}+\frac{1}{2}|{\bf u}|\bigg)-\bigg(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{2}|{\bf u}|\bigg)log_{2}\bigg(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{2}|{\bf u}|\bigg).

(18)

Now, the mean spin vector for atom 1 is related with
\langle\overrightarrow{\sigma}_{1}\rangle
(with
\hbar=1
) as

\displaystyle\langle\hat{\overrightarrow{J_{1}}}\rangle=\langle\hat{J}_{1_{x}}\rangle\hat{i}+\langle\hat{J}_{1_{y}}\rangle\hat{j}+\langle\hat{J}_{1_{z}}\rangle\hat{k}=\frac{1}{2}\bigg(\langle{\sigma}_{1_{x}}\rangle\hat{i}+\langle{\sigma}_{1_{y}}\rangle\hat{j}+\langle{\sigma}_{1_{z}}\rangle\hat{k}\bigg)=\frac{1}{2}\langle\overrightarrow{\sigma}_{1}\rangle,

(19)

So, the von Neumann entropy of the partial traces of the system can be represented in terms of these mean spin vectors of either atom 1 or atom 2 and is given as
[9-12]

\displaystyle E=-\bigg(\frac{1}{2}+|\langle\hat{\overrightarrow{J}}_{1}\rangle|\bigg)log_{2}\bigg(\frac{1}{2}+|\langle\hat{\overrightarrow{J}}_{1}\rangle|\bigg)-\bigg(\frac{1}{2}-|\langle\hat{\overrightarrow{J}}_{1}\rangle|\bigg)log_{2}\bigg(\frac{1}{2}-|\langle\hat{\overrightarrow{J}}_{1}\rangle|\bigg)

(20)

or,

\displaystyle E=-\bigg(\frac{1}{2}+|\langle\hat{\overrightarrow{J}}_{2}\rangle|\bigg)log_{2}\bigg(\frac{1}{2}+|\langle\hat{\overrightarrow{J}}_{2}\rangle|\bigg)-\bigg(\frac{1}{2}-|\langle\hat{\overrightarrow{J}}_{2}\rangle|\bigg)log_{2}\bigg(\frac{1}{2}-|\langle\hat{\overrightarrow{J}}_{2}\rangle|\bigg).

III
III. Von Neumann entropy of the partial traces versus spin squeezing

The spin squeezing of the system of two-level atoms is defined as follows.

If
\hat{J}_{x}
,
\hat{J}_{y}
and
\hat{J}_{z}
are the collective spin operators of a system of two-level atoms, then the collective mean spin vector is

\langle\hat{\bf J}\rangle=\langle\hat{J}_{x}\rangle\hat{i}+\langle\hat{J}_{y}\rangle\hat{j}+\langle\hat{J}_{z}\rangle\hat{k}.

(28)

Now, if
\hat{J}_{1_{\perp}}
and
\hat{J}_{2_{\perp}}

are the two mutually orthogonal components
of
\hat{\bf J}
in a plane perpendicular to

\langle{\hat{\bf J}}\rangle
, satisfying

\Delta J_{1_{\perp}}\Delta J_{2_{\perp}}\geq\frac{|\langle\hat{\bf J}\rangle|}{2},

(29)

we say that there is spin squeezing in the system if

\sqrt{2}\Delta J_{1_{\perp}}/\sqrt{|\langle\hat{\bf J}\rangle|}<1

at the expense of
\Delta J_{2_{\perp}}
or,

\sqrt{2}\Delta J_{2_{\perp}}/\sqrt{|\langle\hat{\bf J}\rangle|}<1

at the expense of
\Delta J_{1_{\perp}}
.

Now, in general
\langle\hat{\bf J}\rangle
points
in an arbitrary direction. So, conventionally we rotate our coordinate system
\{x,y,z\}
to

\{x^{\prime},y^{\prime},z^{\prime}\}
such that the mean spin vector
\langle\hat{\bf J}\rangle
points
along the
z^{\prime}
axis. In that case we say that there is spin squeezing in the system if

S_{x}=\frac{\sqrt{2}\Delta J_{x^{\prime}}}{\sqrt{|\langle\hat{\bf J}\rangle|}}<1

(30)

or,

S_{y}=\frac{\sqrt{2}\Delta J_{y^{\prime}}}{\sqrt{|\langle\hat{\bf J}\rangle|}}<1.

(31)

Spin squeezing properties of this system has been studied in Ref.
Ram
. In this paper we study the dynamics of the von Neumann entropy of this system and make a contrast with the spin squeezing.

Now, in Fig. 3, we plot the spin squeezing parameters
S_{x}
,
S_{y}
and the entanglement entropy

with respect to the dimensionless interaction time
\Delta_{0}t
. We observe that when the entanglement entropy goes to maximum,
S_{x}
becomes very much less than 1, implying maximum spin squeezing in the

S_{y}
is never squeezed and it is always greater than 1.
S_{y}
goes to maximum when the entanglement entropy
E
goes to maximum.

We conclude that when the correlations among the two atoms is maximum, showing maximum squeezing, the entanglement entropy is also maximum. Thus, there is a close relationship between spin squeezing and the quantum entanglement.

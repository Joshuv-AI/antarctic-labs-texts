Loop quantum deparametrized Schwarzschild interior and discrete black hole mass
Cong Zhang, Yongge Ma, Shupeng Song, Xiangdong Zhang

Abstract
We present the detailed analyses of a model of loop quantum Schwarzschild interior coupled to a massless scalar field and extend the results in our previous rapid communication Zhang et al. 2020 to more general schemes. It is shown that the spectrum of the black hole mass is discrete and does not contain zero. This supports the existence of a black hole remnant after Hawking evaporation due to loop quantum gravity effects. Besides to show the existence of a non-vanishing minimal black hole mass in the vacuum case, the quantum dynamics for the non-vacuum case is also solved and compared with the effective one.

I
Introduction

As a prediction of general relativity (GR), the existence of black holes has a broad base of support from observations
Akiyama et al. 2019
. However, our understanding on black hole (BH) is still far from the end. Among those challenging topics on BH, its quantum nature is particularly interesting. By studying quantum BHs, one could not only solve puzzles originating from the classical theory, but also achieve more understandings on the theory of quantum gravity.

As a background independent approach to quantum gravity, loop quantum gravity (LQG) has been widely studied in the past 30 years
Ashtekar and Tate 1991
;
Rovelli 2004
;
Ashtekar and Lewandowski 2004
;
Han et al. 2007
;
Thiemann 2008
;
Abhay and Jorge 2017
.
Although some important breakthroughs have been made in LQG
Rovelli and Smolin 1995
;
Ashtekar et al. 1995
;
Ashtekar and Lewandowski 1997a
;
Ashtekar and Lewandowski 1997b
;
Thiemann 1998a
;
Thiemann 1998b
;
Ma and Ling 2000
;
Lewandowski et al. 2006
;
Yang and Ma 2016
, its dynamics is still an open issue. The obstacle of LQG can be bypassed through applying the loop quantization techniques to the symmetry-reduced sectors of GR, where the expression of the Hamiltonian constraint becomes much simpler than that in the full theory. The resulting quantum models are expected to reflect some quantum features of full LQG, in spite of the fact that they might not be equivalent to the direct symmetric sector of full LQG. An improved treatment of quantum-reduced LQG has also been proposed to study the symmetric sectors
Alesci et al. 2013
. These ideas were applied to study loop quantum Schwarzschild BH recently with different perspectives
Ashtekar and Bojowald 2005
;
Modesto 2006
;
Boehmer and Vandersloot 2007
;
Campiglia et al. 2008
;
Chiou 2008
;
Gambini and Pullin 2013
;
Corichi and Singh 2016
;
Bojowald et al. 2018
;
Ashtekar et al. 2018a
;
Ashtekar et al. 2018b
;
Rovelli and Vidotto 2018
;
Bianchi et al. 2018
;
Achour et al. 2018
;

;
Alesci et al. 2019
;
Bodendorfer et al. 2019a
;
Bodendorfer et al. 2019b
;
Kelly et al. 2020
;
Han and Liu 2020
. However, most of these studies focused on the effective dynamics, where one considered the Hamiltonian constraint with the holonomy correction and solved the effective Hamiton’s equations. This treatment resulted in several important achievements. In particular, it resolves the singularity inside the Schwarzschild BH
and predicts certain extensions of the Schwarzschild interior beyond the singularity (see, for instance,
Ashtekar et al. 2018a
;
Ashtekar et al. 2018b
;
Bodendorfer et al. 2019b
;

;
Han and Liu 2020
). However, in the effective prescription one cannot see more intrinsic quantum natures of BH, such as the ground state of quantum BHs and the discreteness of the spectrum of Dirac observables. After all, it is necessary to consider the quantum dynamics.

There are several crucial topics on the quantum dynamics of BH. One is the issue of the final state of BH evaporation which is related to the constituent of dark matter and the puzzle of information loss.
According to the Hawking radiation
Hawking 1974
, the primordial mini BHs in the very early universe should be completely evaporated by now. However, if the BH evaporation is halted at some stable state by some quantum gravity effect, which is called the BH remnant, these remnants would result in important cosmological consequences
Barrow et al. 1992
;
Carr et al. 1994
;
Dalianis and Tringas 2019
. Remarkably, the remnants originating from these primordial BHs could even comprise the entire dark matter in the universe
Barrow et al. 1992
;
Carr et al. 1994
. Moreover, thanks to the remnant, one could argue that the information fallen into a BH with matters could be stored in the remnant after its evaporation. This provides a possible approach to solve the puzzle of information loss
Preskill 1992
;
Chen et al. 2015
. Furthermore, the distortion of the semiclassical Hawking spectrum resulted from certain discreteness of the BH mass was studied
Bekenstein 1997
;
Pranzetti 2012
;
Barrau et al. 2015
;
Lochan and Chakraborty 2016
.
It was argued that in certain cases, the distortion could be observable even for macroscopic BHs
Bekenstein 1997
. Although these debates are crucial and long standing, there was no systematic study by quantum gravity to lay a solid theoretical foundation for the arguments until the prediction of a BH remnant in LQG models by
Zhang et al. 2020
. The purpose of this paper is to provide the detailed constructions in
Zhang et al. 2020
and extend the results to more general schemes. Moreover, the quantum dynamics of the model will be further studied in detail.

We study the model of loop quantum Schwarzschild interior coupled to a massless scalar field. The quantum Schwarzschild BH, as the vacuum case of this model, can be resulted by vanishing the scalar field.
The phase space of this system contains three pairs of canonical variables,
(b,p_{b})
and
(c,p_{c})
for gravity, and
(\varphi,p_{\varphi})
for the scalar field. By deparametrizing this model, one gets the physical Hamiltonian
\sqrt{\mathfrak{h}}
of the relational evolution with respect to the scalar field
\varphi

Alesci et al. 2015
;
Dapor et al. 2013
;
Zhang and Zhang 2020
. In the classical theory, the Poisson bracket between
\mathfrak{h}
and the ADM mass
M
vanishes. This indicates a classical Dirac observable
m=cp_{c}
proportional to
M
.
However, this commutativity may no longer be kept by the corresponding operators
\hat{\mathfrak{h}}
and
\hat{m}
in the quantum theory,
which is relevant to the choice of schemes for the quantization of
\mathfrak{h}
.
We only focus on the schemes such that
\hat{\mathfrak{h}}
and
\hat{m}
are still commutative, since the commutativity means the existence of a Dirac observable
\hat{m}
.
Note that
a general class of schemes adopted for the loop quantization of the current model can meet our requirement. In particular, it is valid for the
\mu_{o}
-scheme
Ashtekar and Bojowald 2005
;
Modesto 2006
and the new scheme balancing the
\mu_{o}
scheme and
\bar{\mu}
scheme
Corichi and Singh 2016
;
Ashtekar et al. 2018a
;
Ashtekar et al. 2018b
. However, it cannot be met by the
\bar{\mu}
scheme
Boehmer and Vandersloot 2007
;
Chiou 2008
.

We will first construct the Hamiltonian operator
\hat{\mathfrak{h}}
and study its properties analytically. Thanks to these analytical results, a numerical method to diagonalize
\hat{\mathfrak{h}}
is proposed so that the dynamics is computable.
Then the quantum dynamics of the model can be solved for the non-vacuum case, and it can be compared with the effective one. For the vacuum case,
the Hilbert space consisting of the physical states of the Schwarzschild BH
is built up. In this Hilbert space
m
is promoted to an operator which has discrete spectrum
\overline{\sigma_{\xi}}
with
0\notin\overline{\sigma_{\xi}}
.
This result supports the existence of a stable BH remnant.

This paper is organized as follows. In Sec
II
, the theory of loop quantum Schwarzschild BH interior coupled to a massless scalar field is briefly reviewed, including the deparametrization and the polymer quantization of this model. In Sec.
III
, we construct the physical Hamiltonian operator and study its properties analytically. Then the quantum dynamics for both non-vacuum and vacuum cases are solved in Sec.
V
. Finally, in Sec.
VI
, our results are summarized and discussed.

II
Preliminaries

II.1
Deparametrization of the model

Given a spatially homogeneous 3-manifold
\Sigma
of topology
\mathbb{R}\times S^{2}
. Because of the homogeneity,
\Sigma
is endowed with a fiducial metric

\mathring{q}_{ab}{\rm d}x^{a}{\rm d}x^{b}={\rm d}x^{2}+r_{o}^{2}({\rm d}\theta^{2}+\sin^{2}\theta{\rm d}\phi^{2})

(1)

where
(x,\theta,\phi)
are the natural coordinates adapted to the topology, and
r_{o}
is a constant with dimension of length. Since
\Sigma
is non-compact in the
x
direction, we introduce an elementary cell
\mathcal{C}\cong(0,L_{0})\times\mathbb{S}^{2}
in
\Sigma
and restrict all integrals to this elemental cell to avoid the divergence of integrations.

The classical phase space of gravity coupled to a massless scalar field contains the Ashtekar-Barbero canonical conjugate pairs
(A_{a}^{i}(x),E^{a}_{i}(x))
for gravity and
(\varphi(x),\pi(x))
for scalar field. As far as the homogeneous states are concerned, the scalar filed
\varphi
is reduced to a constant and the fields
A_{a}^{i}(x)
,
E^{a}_{i}(x)
and
\pi_{\varphi}(x)
take the forms
Zhang and Zhang 2020

\displaystyle A_{a}^{i}\tau_{i}{\rm d}x^{a}

\displaystyle=\frac{c}{L_{0}}\tau_{3}{\rm d}x+b\tau_{2}{\rm d}\theta-b\tau_{1}\sin\theta{\rm d}\phi+\tau_{3}\cos\theta{\rm d}\phi

(2)

\displaystyle E_{i}^{a}\tau^{i}\partial_{a}

\displaystyle=p_{c}\tau_{3}\sin\theta\partial_{x}+\frac{p_{b}}{L_{0}}\tau_{2}\sin\theta\partial_{\theta}-\frac{p_{b}}{L_{0}}\tau_{1}\partial_{\phi}

\displaystyle\pi_{\varphi}

\displaystyle=\frac{p_{\varphi}}{4\pi r_{0}^{2}}\sqrt{\mathring{q}}=\frac{p_{\varphi}}{4\pi}\sin\theta

where
\tau_{j}=-i\sigma_{j}/2

(j=1,2,3)
with
\sigma_{j}
being the Pauli matrix and,
c,\ b,\ p_{c},\ p_{b}
and
p_{\varphi}
are all constants.
According to (
2
), the symmetry-reduced phase space is coordinatized by the pairs
(c,p_{c})
,
(b,p_{b})
and
(\varphi,p_{\varphi})
. The non-vanishing Poisson brackets read

\{c,p_{c}\}=2G\gamma,\ \{b,p_{b}\}=G\gamma,\ \{\varphi,p_{\varphi}\}=1.

(3)

By the symmetry-reduced expression (
2
), the Gaussian and diffeomorphism constraints vanish automatically.
The dynamics of this model is encoded in the Hamiltonian constraint. In the full theory of gravity coupled to a massless scalar field, the Hamiltonian constraint can be deparametrized as
Domagała et al. 2010

\displaystyle C(x)=\pi_{\varphi}(x)\pm\sqrt{h(x)}=0,

(4)

where
h(x)=-2\sqrt{|\det(E(x))|}C_{\rm gr}(x)
, with
C_{\rm gr}
being the vacuum-gravity Hamiltonian constraint. The so-called physical Hamiltonian can be written as

h_{\rm phy}=\frac{\sqrt{G}\gamma}{\sqrt{4\pi}}\int_{\Sigma}{\rm d}^{3}x\sqrt{h(x)}=\frac{\gamma}{4\sqrt{2}\pi}\int_{\Sigma}{\rm d}^{3}x\sqrt{E_{i}^{a}E_{j}^{b}\left(-F^{k}_{ab}\epsilon_{ijk}+2(1+\gamma^{2})K_{[a}^{i}K_{b]}^{j}\right)}

(5)

where
F^{k}_{ab}
denotes the curvature of the connection
A_{a}^{i}
,
K_{a}^{i}
is the extrinsic curvature and
the prefactor is adapted for convenience. It generates the relational evolution with respect to the scalar field.
Substituting (
2
) in to (
4
) and integrating both sides in the elementary cell
\mathcal{C}
, one finally obtains the Hamiltonian constraint of our model as

p_{\varphi}\pm\frac{\sqrt{4\pi}}{\sqrt{G}L_{0}\gamma}\sqrt{\mathfrak{h}}=0

(6)

where
\mathfrak{h}
is given by

\mathfrak{h}:=p_{b}\left(\left(b^{2}+\gamma^{2}\right)p_{b}+2bcp_{c}\right)

(7)

In this work, we will also refer to the physical Hamiltonian as
\mathfrak{h}
though it is related to the true physical Hamiltonian
h_{\rm phy}
by
\sqrt{\mathfrak{h}}=h_{\rm phy}
.

The quantum states of the model are described by vectors in the Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}_{t}=\mathcal{H}_{\rm mat}\otimes\mathcal{H}_{\rm gr}
, where
\mathcal{H}_{\rm mat}
and
\mathcal{H}_{\rm gr}
are the Hilbert spaces describing the matter and gravity respectively. The physical states
|\psi\rangle_{\rm phy}
should satisfy the quantum version of the Hamiltonian constraint, i.e.,

\left(\hat{p}_{\varphi}\pm\frac{\sqrt{4\pi}}{L_{0}\gamma\sqrt{G}}\widehat{\sqrt{\mathfrak{h}}}\right)|\psi\rangle_{\rm phy}=0.

(8)

It gives us a Schödinger-like equation. Therefore, in the case that the operator
\widehat{\sqrt{\mathfrak{h}}}
is self-adjoint, the physical states, i.e., the solutions of (
8
), can be expressed as

|\psi\rangle_{\rm phy}=e^{\mp i\frac{\sqrt{4\pi G}}{L_{0}\gamma\ell_{\mathrm{p}}^{2}}\varphi\widehat{\sqrt{\mathfrak{h}}}}|\psi\rangle_{\rm gr}

(9)

where
|\psi\rangle_{\rm gr}\in\mathcal{H}_{\rm gr}
is a state of pure gravity and
\ell_{\mathrm{p}}:=\sqrt{G\hbar}
is the Plank length. A Dirac observable
\mathcal{O}_{\rm phy}
takes the form

\mathcal{O}_{\rm phy}=e^{\mp i\frac{\sqrt{4\pi G}}{L_{0}\gamma\ell_{\mathrm{p}}^{2}}\varphi\widehat{\sqrt{\mathfrak{h}}}}\mathcal{O}_{\rm gr}e^{\pm i\frac{\sqrt{4\pi G}}{L_{0}\gamma\ell_{\mathrm{p}}^{2}}\varphi\widehat{\sqrt{\mathfrak{h}}}}

(10)

with an operator
\mathcal{O}_{\rm gr}
in
\mathcal{H}_{\rm gr}
.

To carry out the above deparametrization procedure, in next subsection we will introduce the polymer quantization for the gravity and obtain its Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}
. Then a self-adjoint operator
\hat{\mathfrak{h}}
on
\mathcal{H}
is proposed by the loop quantization procedure. Finally we restrict ourselves to the subspace consisting of non-negative spectra of
\hat{\mathfrak{h}}
to define the Hilbert space

\mathcal{H}_{\rm gr}:=\hat{P}_{[0,\infty)}\mathcal{H}

(11)

where
\hat{P}_{[0,\infty)}:=\chi_{[0,\infty)}(\hat{\mathfrak{h}})
is the projection operator with respect to the spectrum decomposition of
\hat{\mathfrak{h}}
.

II.2
The polymer quantization

The polymer quantization of the gravity in this model leads to the Hilbert space
Ashtekar and Bojowald 2005

\tilde{\mathcal{H}}=\tilde{\mathcal{H}}_{\mathfrak{b}}\otimes\tilde{\mathcal{H}}_{\mathfrak{c}}=L^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{\rm Bohr},{\rm d}\mu_{0})\otimes L^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{\rm Bohr},{\rm d}\mu_{0}),

(12)

where
{\rm d}\mu_{0}
is the Haar measure on the Bohr compactification
\mathbb{R}_{\rm Bohr}
of the real line
\mathbb{R}

(
Thiemann 2008
, Chapter 28)
. The two spaces
\tilde{\mathcal{H}}_{\mathfrak{b}}
and
\tilde{\mathcal{H}}_{\mathfrak{c}}
correspond to the canonical conjugate pairs
(b,p_{b})
and
(c,p_{c})
respectively. The standard basis of these two Hilbert spaces are denoted by
|\mu\rangle\in\tilde{\mathcal{H}}_{\mathfrak{b}}
and
|\tau\rangle\in\tilde{\mathcal{H}}_{\mathfrak{c}}
. Their inner products read

\langle\mu^{\prime}|\mu\rangle=\delta_{\mu^{\prime},\mu},\ \langle\tau^{\prime}|\tau\rangle=\delta_{\tau^{\prime},\tau},

(13)

where the Kronecker-
\delta
symbol is employed.

There are two types of basic operators in the Hilbert spaces. One is the momentum-variable operators
\hat{p}_{b}
and
\hat{p}_{c}
, whose actions on
|\mu\rangle
and
|\tau\rangle
are given by

\displaystyle\hat{p}_{b}\,|\mu\rangle

\displaystyle=\frac{\gamma\ell_{\mathrm{p}}^{2}}{2}\mu|\mu\rangle

(14)

\displaystyle\hat{p}_{c}\,|\tau\rangle

\displaystyle=\gamma\ell_{\mathrm{p}}^{2}\,\tau|\tau\rangle.

The other is the configuration-variable operators
\widehat{e^{i\lambda b}}
and
\widehat{e^{i\lambda c}}
, whose actions read

\displaystyle\widehat{e^{i\lambda b}}|\mu\rangle

\displaystyle=|\mu+2\lambda\rangle

(15)

\displaystyle\widehat{e^{i\lambda c}}|\tau\rangle

\displaystyle=|\tau+2\lambda\rangle.

As in the model of loop quantum cosmology (LQC), the operators
\widehat{e^{i\lambda b}}
and
\widehat{e^{i\lambda c}}
correspond to the holonomies along the edges parallel to the
\mathbb{R}
direction and the equator (or the longitude because of the homogeneity) of
\mathbb{S}^{2}
respectively.
Moreover,
\widehat{e^{i\lambda b}}
and
\widehat{e^{i\lambda c}}
are not strongly continuous with respect to
\lambda
. Therefore there are no operators corresponding to the configuration variables
b
and
c
in our model. This respect to the fact that there does not exist operator corresponding to the connection itself in the full theory.

III
The physical Hamiltonian operator

III.1
A separable subspace
\mathcal{H}
of
\tilde{\mathcal{H}}

According to (
13
) , the Hilbert space
\tilde{\mathcal{H}}
possesses a non-countable orthonormal basis, and hence it is non-separable. The problem that the kinematical Hilbert space is non-separable has appeared in both LQG and LQC. In LQG, the non-separability is caused by the uncountability of the graphs based on which the Hilbert space is defined. In LQC, it is caused by the polymer quantization procedure which leads to an uncountable orthonormal basis as the case of the present model. In LQG, one proposed to employ the diffeomorphism invariance to identify the diffeomorphism equivalent graphs in order to obtain a separable diffeomorphism invaraint Hilbert space
Ashtekar and Lewandowski 2004
.
In LQC, the problem is tackled by the super-selection feature of the Hamiltonian constraint. More precisely, because the Hamiltonian constraint operator preserves some separable Hilbert subspaces, one can confine the study in certain separable subspace
Ashtekar et al. 2003
;
Ashtekar et al. 2006
. The situation in the present model is very similar to that in LQC. As shown below, a separable subspace
\mathcal{H}\subset\tilde{\mathcal{H}}
can be selected, which is preserved by the Hamiltonian operator.

Classically, the physical Hamiltonian (
7
), re-denoted by
\mathfrak{h}_{c}
, can be rewritten as

\mathfrak{h}_{c}=2p_{b}bp_{c}c+p_{b}^{2}b^{2}+\gamma^{2}p_{b}^{2},

(16)

where
p_{c}c
is a Dirac observable. Actually, for vacuum gravity, one has
p_{c}c=L_{0}\gamma GM
with
M
being the ADM mass of Schwarzschild BH
Zhang and Zhang 2020
. Since in the Hilbert space
\tilde{\mathcal{H}}
there are no operators corresponding to
b
and
c
, the expression (
16
) cannot be promoted to an operator directly. We thus return to the integral expression (
5
) of the full deparametrized theory and follow
Ashtekar and Bojowald 2005
to express
C_{\rm gr}
in terms of
\mathring{F}={\rm d}(\gamma K)+[\gamma K,\gamma K]
and the spatial curvature
\Omega=-\sin(\theta){\rm d}\theta\wedge{\rm d}\phi\tau_{3}
, with

K=\frac{1}{\gamma}\left(\frac{c}{L_{0}}\tau_{3}{\rm d}x+b\tau_{2}{\rm d}\theta-b\tau_{1}\sin\theta{\rm d}\phi\right)

(17)

denoting the extrinsic curvature.
Then the physical Hamiltonian
h_{\rm phy}
is expressed by

h_{\rm phy}=\frac{1}{4\sqrt{2}\pi}\int_{\mathcal{C}}{\rm d}^{3}x\sqrt{E^{a}_{i}(x)E^{b}_{j}(x)(\mathring{F}^{k}_{ab}(x)-\gamma^{2}\Omega_{ab}^{k}(x))}.

(18)

Note that, in comparison with the expression (
2
) of
A
, the expression (
17
) does not contain the term
\tau_{3}\cos(\theta){\rm d}\phi
. Thus the “holonomy” of
K
along the
\phi
-direction is much simpler than that of
A
. Moreover, since
\Omega
does not depend on dynamical variables, one needs not to regularize it by holomolies.
To regularize (
18
), one fixes three edges intersecting at a point
x_{0}\in\mathcal{C}
, where
the first edge
e_{1}
is along the
\mathbb{R}
direction of
\Sigma
taking length
\ell_{1}=\delta_{c}L_{0}
and the other two edges

and
e_{3}
are along the equator and the longitude of

with the same radians

. By defining the “holonomies”

one can regularize
\mathring{F}_{ab}^{k}

\mathring{F}_{ab}^{k}(x_{0})=-\sum_{i,j}\frac{2}{\ell_{i}\ell_{j}}\mathrm{tr}\left(h_{i}h_{j}h_{i}^{-1}h_{j}^{-1}\tau_{k}\right)\mathring{\omega}_{a}^{i}\mathring{\omega}_{b}^{j}+O(\sqrt{\ell_{a}\ell_{b}})

where
\mathring{\omega}_{a}^{i}
denotes the co-triad of the fiducial metric (
1

e_{i}

2

\mathfrak{h}=2p_{b}\frac{\sin(\delta_{b}b)}{\delta_{b}}p_{c}\frac{\sin(\delta_{c}c)}{\delta_{c}}+p_{b}^{2}\frac{\sin^{2}(\delta_{b}b)}{\delta_{b}^{2}}+\gamma^{2}p_{b}^{2}.

(21)

The expression (
21
) returns to
\mathfrak{h}_{c}
in (
16
) at the limit of
\delta_{b}\to 0
and
\delta_{c}\to 0
.

To quantize the regularized physical Hamiltonian (
21
) instead of (
16
),
various strategies have been proposed in choosing the two quantum parameters
\delta_{b}
and
\delta_{c}
. Roughly speaking, the choices can be classified into three schemes. The first one is the
\mu_{0}
-scheme where
\delta_{b}
and
\delta_{c}
are chosen as constants

;
Modesto 2006
;
Campiglia et al. 2008
. The second one is the

-scheme which allows
\delta_{b}
and
\delta_{c}
to be any functions of
p_{b}
and
p_{c}

Boehmer and Vandersloot 2007
;

. The third one, referred to as the modified scheme, is developed recently where

and
\delta_{c}
are phase space dependent only through Dirac observables

;
Ashtekar et al. 2018a
;
Ashtekar et al. 2018b
. Since the expression

in (

is composed of a Dirac observable

.
This assumption rules out certain strategies such as the
\bar{\mu}
-scheme where
\delta_{c}
depends on
\hat{p}_{b}
and
\delta_{b}
depends on
\hat{p}_{c}

.
Furthermore, we assume that
\delta_{b}
is a constant or any function of the Dirac observable

Similar assumptions were adopted in
\mu_{o}
-scheme and the modified scheme. They are sufficient but not necessary to obtain a Dirac observable
\widehat{p_{c}\sin(\delta_{c}c)/\delta_{c}}

(i)

a separable Hilbert subspace
\mathcal{H}_{\mathfrak{c}}\subset\tilde{\mathcal{H}}_{\mathfrak{c}}
can be chosen to define an operator
\widehat{p_{c}\sin(\delta_{c}c)/\delta_{c}}
corresponding to

which is self-adjoint, commutates with the physical Hamiltonian
\hat{\mathfrak{h}}
.

(ii)

the quantum parameter
\delta_{b}
is a constant or any function of

.

Denote
\sigma_{\mathfrak{c}}
as the spectrum of the Dirac observable
\widehat{p_{c}\sin(\delta_{c}c)/\delta_{c}}
. Then
\mathcal{H}_{\mathfrak{c}}
is isometric to the Hilbert space

with the spectral measure

. The Hilbert space
\tilde{\mathcal{H}}_{\mathfrak{b}}\otimes\mathcal{H}_{\mathfrak{c}}\subset\tilde{\mathcal{H}}
can be represented by
L^{2}(\sigma_{\mathfrak{c}},{\rm d}\mu_{\mathfrak{c}};\tilde{\mathcal{H}}_{\mathfrak{b}})
in which each element is a
\tilde{\mathcal{H}}_{\mathfrak{b}}
-valued function on
\sigma_{\mathfrak{c}}
. The representation is defined by

U:\tilde{\mathcal{H}}_{\mathfrak{b}}\otimes L^{2}(\sigma_{\mathfrak{c}},{\rm d}\mu_{\mathfrak{c}})\ni\psi_{b}\otimes\psi_{c}\mapsto\psi_{c}(\cdot)\psi_{b}\in L^{2}(\sigma_{\mathfrak{c}},{\rm d}\mu_{\mathfrak{c}};\tilde{\mathcal{H}}_{\mathfrak{b}}).

(22)

The inner product in
L^{2}(\sigma_{\mathfrak{c}},{\rm d}\mu;\tilde{\mathcal{H}}_{\mathfrak{b}})
is given by

\left(\psi^{(1)},\psi^{(2)}\right)=\int_{\sigma_{\mathfrak{c}}}{\rm d}\mu_{\mathfrak{c}}\langle\psi^{(1)}(x)|\psi^{(2)}(x)\rangle

(23)

where
\langle\psi^{(1)}(x)|\psi^{(2)}(x)\rangle
denotes the inner produce of
\psi^{(1)}(x),\psi^{(2)}(x)\in\tilde{\mathcal{H}}_{\mathfrak{b}}
.

For convenience, the elements in the spectrum space can be denoted by
L_{o}\gamma m\in\sigma_{\mathfrak{c}}
with
m\in\mathbb{R}
in analogy with their classical correspondence
p_{c}c=L_{o}\gamma GM
. We also define

\hat{\beta}_{\lambda}:=\widehat{p_{b}\sin(\lambda b)}.

(24)

For a state
\psi\in L^{2}(\sigma_{\mathfrak{c}},{\rm d}\mu_{\mathfrak{c}};\tilde{\mathcal{H}}_{\mathfrak{b}})
,
\psi(L_{o}\gamma m)\in\tilde{\mathcal{H}}_{\mathfrak{b}}
is abbreviated to
\psi(m)
.
Then the action of
\hat{\mathfrak{h}}
on
\psi
reads

(\hat{\mathfrak{h}}\psi)(m)=\left(\frac{2L_{o}\gamma m}{\delta_{b}^{(m)}}\hat{\beta}_{\delta_{b}^{(m)}}+\frac{1}{(\delta_{b}^{(m)})^{2}}\hat{\beta}_{\delta_{b}^{(m)}}^{2}+\gamma^{2}\hat{p}_{b}^{2}\right)\psi(m)

(25)

where
\delta_{b}^{(m)}\equiv\delta_{b}(L_{o}\gamma m)
due to the dependence of
\delta_{b}
on
\widehat{p_{c}\sin(\delta_{c}c)/\delta_{c}}
.
By (
25
), the separable Hilbert subspace
\mathcal{H}\subset L^{2}(\sigma_{\mathfrak{c}},{\rm d}\mu_{\mathfrak{c}};\tilde{\mathcal{H}}_{\mathfrak{b}})
preserved by
\hat{\mathfrak{h}}
is constructed as follows. Given
L_{o}\gamma m\in\sigma_{\mathfrak{c}}
, let
\mathcal{H}_{\mathfrak{b}}(m)\subset\tilde{\mathcal{H}}_{\mathfrak{b}}
be a separable Hilbert space preserved by
\hat{\beta}_{\delta_{b}^{(m)}}
. Then the Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}\subset L^{2}(\sigma_{\mathfrak{c}},{\rm d}\mu_{\mathfrak{c}};\tilde{\mathcal{H}}_{\mathfrak{b}})
contains the state
\psi
such that
\psi(m)\in\mathcal{H}_{\mathfrak{b}}(m)
for all
L_{o}\gamma m\in\sigma_{\mathfrak{c}}
. For convenience, the Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}
constructed by
\mathcal{H}_{\mathfrak{b}}(\cdot):m\mapsto\mathcal{H}_{\mathfrak{b}}(m)
through this procedure will be denoted by
L^{2}(\sigma_{\mathfrak{c}},{\rm d}\mu_{\mathfrak{c}};\mathcal{H}_{\mathfrak{b}}(\cdot))
.

III.2
The operators
\hat{\beta}_{\lambda}

According to (
25
), the action of
\hat{\mathfrak{h}}
is determined by the property of
\hat{\beta}_{\delta_{b}^{(m)}}
. Since
\delta_{b}^{(m)}
is constant for a given
m\in\mathbb{R}
, we will drop the superscript in
\delta_{b}^{(m)}
and consider
\hat{\beta}_{\delta_{b}}
with a constant
\delta_{b}
.
Classically, one has

\displaystyle p_{b}\sin(\delta_{b}b)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2i}\left(p_{b}e^{i\delta_{b}b}-p_{b}e^{-i\delta_{b}b}\right).

(26)

A well-known ambiguity in the quantization procedure is caused by the operator ordering. By definition, one has

\widehat{e^{i\delta_{b}b}}\hat{p}_{b}=(\hat{p}_{b}-\delta_{b}\gamma\ell_{\mathrm{p}}^{2})\widehat{e^{i\lambda b}}.

(27)

We hereby introduce a parameter
\mathfrak{b}
to parameterize various operator-ordering strategies and define

\widehat{p_{b}e^{i\delta_{b}b}}=(\hat{p}_{b}+\gamma\ell_{\mathrm{p}}^{2}\mathfrak{b})\widehat{e^{i\delta_{b}b}}.

(28)

Then, we can define a symmetric operator corresponding to (
26
) as

\displaystyle\hat{\beta}_{\delta_{b}}

\displaystyle:=\frac{1}{2i}\left((\hat{p}_{b}+\gamma\ell_{\mathrm{p}}^{2}\mathfrak{b})\widehat{e^{i\delta_{b}b}}-\widehat{e^{-i\delta_{b}b}}(\hat{p}_{b}+\gamma\ell_{\mathrm{p}}^{2}\mathfrak{b})\right).

(29)

Its action is given by

\displaystyle\hat{\beta}_{\delta_{b}}|\mu\rangle

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2i}\frac{\gamma\ell_{\mathrm{p}}^{2}}{2}\Big((\mu+2\delta_{b}+2\mathfrak{b})|\mu+2\delta_{b}\rangle-(\mu+2\mathfrak{b})|\mu-2\delta_{b}\rangle\Big).

(30)

Thus the separable Hilbert subspaces of
\tilde{\mathcal{H}}_{\mathfrak{b}}
preserved by
\hat{\beta}_{\delta_{b}}
are given by

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}_{\mathfrak{b}}^{(\varepsilon_{b})}

\displaystyle=\overline{\{\psi\in\tilde{\mathcal{H}}_{\mathfrak{b}},\psi(\mu)\neq 0\text{ only for }\mu=\varepsilon_{b}+2n\delta_{b},n\in\mathbb{Z}\}}

(31)

for some constant
\varepsilon_{b}\in[0,2\delta_{b})
. We will show in below that the separable Hilbert subspace preserved by the physical Hamiltonian
\hat{\mathfrak{h}}
can be constructed with
\mathcal{H}_{\mathfrak{b}}^{\varepsilon_{b}}
.

Denote the restriction of
\hat{\beta}_{\delta_{b}}
on
\mathcal{H}_{\mathfrak{b}}^{(\varepsilon_{b})}
by
\hat{\beta}_{\delta_{b}}\restriction\mathcal{H}_{\mathfrak{b}}^{(\varepsilon_{b})}
.
Given
\mathcal{H}_{\mathfrak{b}}^{\varepsilon_{b}}
and
\mathcal{H}_{\mathfrak{b}}^{\tilde{\varepsilon}_{b}}
, a natural isomorphism between them can be defined by

i:\mathcal{H}_{\mathfrak{b}}^{(\varepsilon_{b})}\ni|\varepsilon_{b}+2n\delta_{b}\rangle\mapsto|\tilde{\varepsilon}_{b}+2n\delta_{b}\rangle\in\mathcal{H}_{\mathfrak{b}}^{(\tilde{\varepsilon}_{b})}.

(32)

It introduces an operator
i(\hat{\beta}_{\delta_{b}}\restriction\mathcal{H}_{\mathfrak{b}}^{(\varepsilon_{b})})i^{-1}
in
\mathcal{H}_{\mathfrak{b}}^{(\varepsilon_{b})}
whose action reads

i(\hat{\beta}_{\delta_{b}}\restriction\mathcal{H}_{\mathfrak{b}}^{(\varepsilon_{b})})i^{-1}|\tilde{\varepsilon}_{b}+2n\delta_{b}\rangle=\frac{1}{2i}\frac{\gamma\ell_{\mathrm{p}}^{2}}{2}\Big((2n\delta_{b}+\tilde{\varepsilon}_{b}+2\delta_{b}+2\tilde{\mathfrak{b}})|\tilde{\varepsilon}_{b}+2n\delta_{b}+2\delta_{b}\rangle-(2n\delta_{b}+\tilde{\varepsilon}_{b}+2\tilde{\mathfrak{b}})|\tilde{\varepsilon}_{b}+2n\delta_{b}-2\delta_{b}\rangle\Big),

(33)

where
\tilde{\mathfrak{b}}:=\mathfrak{b}+(\varepsilon_{b}-\tilde{\varepsilon}_{b})/2
. For clarity, let us use
\hat{\beta}_{\delta_{b}}^{(x)}
to denote the operator
\hat{\beta}_{\delta_{b}}
with respect to the constant
\mathfrak{b}=x
. According to (
33
), to study the operator
\hat{\beta}_{\delta_{b}}^{(x)}\restriction\mathcal{H}_{\mathfrak{b}}^{(\varepsilon_{b})}
with respect to
x\neq 0
, one can use the operator
i(\hat{\beta}_{\delta_{b}}^{(x)}\restriction\mathcal{H}_{\mathfrak{b}}^{(\varepsilon_{b})})i^{-1}
on
\mathcal{H}_{\mathfrak{b}}^{\tilde{\varepsilon}_{b}}
with
\tilde{\varepsilon}_{b}=\varepsilon_{b}+2x
, and thus has

i(\hat{\beta}_{\delta_{b}}^{(x)}\restriction\mathcal{H}_{\mathfrak{b}}^{(\varepsilon_{b})})i^{-1}=\hat{\beta}_{\delta_{b}}^{(0)}\restriction\mathcal{H}_{\mathfrak{b}}^{(\tilde{\varepsilon}_{b})}.

(34)

Therefore, without loss of generality, we can set
\mathfrak{b}=0
and study properties of
\hat{\beta}_{\delta_{b}}\restriction\mathcal{H}_{\mathfrak{b}}^{(\varepsilon_{b})}
for any given
\varepsilon_{b}
.

From now on, let us refer to the restriction
\hat{\beta}_{\delta_{b}}\restriction\mathcal{H}_{\mathfrak{b}}^{(\varepsilon_{b})}
for some
\mathcal{H}_{\mathfrak{b}}^{(\varepsilon_{b})}
as
\hat{\beta}_{\delta_{b}}
unless being specially noted. Because
\hat{\beta}_{\delta_{b}}
is unbounded, one has to assign certain domain to complete its definition. A natural choice of the domain
D(\hat{\beta}_{\delta_{b}})
reads

D(\hat{\beta}_{\delta_{b}})=\{\psi\in\mathcal{H}_{\mathfrak{b}}^{(\varepsilon_{b})},|\mathrm{supp}(\psi)|<\infty\}

(35)

where
|\mathrm{supp}(\psi)|
denotes the cardinality of the support of
\psi
. This implies that
D(\hat{\beta}_{\delta_{b}})
only consists of finite linear combination of the basis
|\mu\rangle\in\mathcal{H}_{\mathfrak{b}}^{(\varepsilon_{b})}
.
The essential self-adjointness of
\hat{\beta}_{\delta_{b}}
with the domain
D(\hat{\beta}_{\delta_{b}})
can be proven. Define a self-adjoint operator
\hat{N}(\geq 1)
as
\hat{N}|\mu\rangle=(1+\mu^{2})|\mu\rangle
. Then it is straight-forward to verify that there exist numbers
c,d\in\mathbb{R}
such that

\displaystyle\|\hat{\beta}_{\delta_{b}}\psi\|

\displaystyle\leq c\|N\psi\|,\forall\psi\in D

(36)

\displaystyle\left|\langle\hat{\beta}_{\delta_{b}}\psi,\hat{N}\psi\rangle-\langle\hat{N}\psi,\hat{\beta}_{\delta_{b}}\psi\rangle\right|

\displaystyle\leq d\|\hat{N}^{1/2}\psi\|^{2},\ \forall\psi\in D.

where
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
denotes the inner product in
\mathcal{H}_{\mathfrak{b}}^{(\varepsilon_{b})}
. Therefore
\hat{\beta}_{\delta_{b}}
is essentially self-adjoint with the domain
D(\hat{\beta}_{\delta_{b}})
according to Theorem X.37 in
Reed 2003
.

By expanding the eigen-equation
\hat{\beta}_{\delta_{b}}|\psi\rangle=\omega|\psi\rangle
with the basis
|\mu\rangle
, one has

\omega\psi(\mu)=\frac{1}{2i}\frac{\gamma\ell_{\mathrm{p}}^{2}}{2}\Big((\mu+2\delta_{b})\psi(\mu+2\delta_{b})-\mu\psi(\mu-2\delta_{b})\Big).

(37)

As shown in appendix
A
,
\psi(\mu)
behaves asymptotically as

\psi(\mu)=\left\{\begin{aligned} &\frac{1}{\sqrt{|\mu|}}\left(\chi_{+}e^{ik\ln(|\mu|)}+\chi_{-}e^{-ik\ln(|\mu|)}\right)+O(|\mu|^{-1}),\ \mu=\varepsilon_{b}+4n\delta_{b}\\
&\frac{1}{\sqrt{|\mu|}}\left(\chi_{+}e^{ik\ln(|\mu|)}-\chi_{-}e^{-ik\ln(|\mu|)}\right)+O(|\mu|^{-1}),\ \mu=\varepsilon_{b}+(4n+2)\delta_{b}\end{aligned}\right.

(38)

where
k=\frac{\omega}{\gamma\ell_{\mathrm{p}}^{2}\delta_{b}}
and
n\in\mathbb{Z}
. This implies that

\int|\psi(\mu)|^{2}{\rm d}\mu=\infty.
and hence

\sum_{\mu=\varepsilon_{b}+2n}|\psi(\mu)|^{2}=\infty.

Thus
\psi(\mu)
is unnormalizable.
Moreover, since
\psi(\mu)
behaves asymptoticly as a plane wave of
\ln(|\mu|)
, we can show by using the same techniques as in
Zhang et al. 2018
and the Weyl’s criterion
Reed 2003
, that the spectrum of
\widehat{p_{b}\sin(\delta_{b}b)}
is the entire real line
\mathbb{R}
.
Furthermore, the two leading-order functions in (
38
), denoted by

\psi_{0}^{\pm}(\mu):=\frac{1}{\sqrt{|\mu|}}\left(\chi_{+}e^{ik\ln(|\mu|)}\pm\chi_{-}e^{-ik\ln(|\mu|)}\right),

satisfy

-i\gamma\ell_{\mathrm{p}}^{2}\delta_{b}\mathrm{sgn}(\mu)\sqrt{|\mu|}\frac{{\rm d}}{{\rm d}\mu}\sqrt{|\mu|}\psi_{0}^{\pm}(\mu)=\omega\psi_{0}^{\mp}(\mu),

(39)

where
\mathrm{sgn}(\mu)
denotes the sign function of
\mu
. This implies that the operator
\hat{\beta}_{\delta_{b}}
, in the large
\mu
limit, returns to the Schödinger quantization of
p_{b}b
,

\widehat{p_{b}b}=-i\gamma\ell_{\mathrm{p}}^{2}\mathrm{sgn}(\mu)\sqrt{|\mu|}\frac{{\rm d}}{{\rm d}\mu}\sqrt{|\mu|}.

(40)

Therefore, the classical limit of
\hat{\beta}_{\delta_{b}}
is correct. This finishes the quantization procedure of
p_{b}\sin(\delta_{b}b)
and the study of the properties of the corresponding operator
\hat{\beta}_{\delta_{b}}
. The same discussion can be transported analogously to
p_{c}\sin(\delta_{c}c)
for constant
\delta_{c}
. The resulting operator
\widehat{p_{c}\sin(\delta_{c}c)/\delta_{c}}
is self-adjoint and possesses the entire line as its spectrum. These properties of
\widehat{p_{c}\sin(\delta_{c}c)/\delta_{c}}
are compatible with our general assumptions.

III.3
The operator
\hat{\mathfrak{h}}

Let us denote the operator in the RHS of (
25
) as

\hat{\mathfrak{h}}^{(m)}:=\frac{2L_{o}\gamma m}{\delta_{b}}\hat{\beta}_{\delta_{b}}+\frac{1}{\delta_{b}^{2}}\hat{\beta}_{\delta_{b}}^{2}+\gamma^{2}\hat{p}_{b}^{2}.

(41)

It should be noted that the operator
\hat{\beta}_{\delta_{b}}
in (
41
) is the original one defined by (
30
) with non-vanishing
\mathfrak{b}
. However,
as discussed below (
33
), the identity map
i
from
\mathcal{H}_{\mathfrak{b}}^{(\varepsilon_{b})}
to the specific Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}_{\mathfrak{b}}^{(\tilde{\varepsilon}_{b})}
with
\tilde{\varepsilon}_{b}=\varepsilon_{b}+2\mathfrak{b}
ensures that we can set
\mathfrak{b}=0
. Thus, by this treatment we define an operator in
\mathcal{H}_{\mathfrak{b}}^{(\tilde{\varepsilon}_{b})}
corresponding to
\hat{\mathfrak{h}}^{(m)}
as

i(\hat{\mathfrak{h}}^{(m)}\restriction\mathcal{H}_{\mathfrak{b}}^{(\varepsilon_{b})})i^{-1}=\frac{2L_{o}\gamma m}{\delta_{b}}i(\hat{\beta}_{\delta_{b}}\restriction\mathcal{H}_{\mathfrak{b}}^{(\varepsilon_{b})})i^{-1}+\frac{1}{\delta_{b}^{2}}i(\hat{\beta}_{\delta_{b}}^{2}\restriction\mathcal{H}_{\mathfrak{b}}^{(\varepsilon_{b})})i^{-1}+\gamma^{2}i(\hat{p}_{b}^{2}\restriction\mathcal{H}_{\mathfrak{b}}^{(\varepsilon_{b})})i^{-1}.

(42)

where the map
i
, defined by (
32
), identifies the Hilbert spaces
\mathcal{H}_{\mathfrak{b}}^{(\varepsilon_{b})}
and
\mathcal{H}_{\mathfrak{b}}^{(\tilde{\varepsilon}_{b})}
with
\tilde{\varepsilon}_{b}=\varepsilon_{b}+2\mathfrak{b}
. For the first two terms in this operator, one has

i((\hat{\beta}_{\delta_{b}})^{n}\restriction\mathcal{H}_{\mathfrak{b}}^{(\varepsilon_{b})})i^{-1}=(\hat{\beta}_{\delta_{b}}^{(0)})^{n}\restriction\mathcal{H}_{\mathfrak{b}}^{(\tilde{\varepsilon}_{b})}.

(43)

where
\hat{\beta}_{\delta_{b}}^{(0)}
is defined by (
34
). For the last term, we have

i(\hat{p}_{b}^{2}\restriction\mathcal{H}_{\mathfrak{b}}^{(\varepsilon_{b})})i^{-1}|\tilde{\varepsilon}_{b}+2n\delta_{b}\rangle=[(\hat{p}_{b}-\gamma\ell_{\mathrm{p}}^{2}\mathfrak{b})^{2}\restriction\mathcal{H}_{\mathfrak{b}}^{(\tilde{\varepsilon}_{b})}]|\tilde{\varepsilon}_{b}+2n\delta_{b}\rangle.

(44)

Therefore, (
42
) can be expressed as

i(\hat{\mathfrak{h}}^{(m)}\restriction\mathcal{H}_{\mathfrak{b}}^{(\varepsilon_{b})})i^{-1}=\frac{2L_{o}\gamma m}{\delta_{b}}(\hat{\beta}_{\delta_{b}}^{(0)}\restriction\mathcal{H}_{\mathfrak{b}}^{(\tilde{\varepsilon}_{b})})+\frac{1}{\delta_{b}^{2}}[(\hat{\beta}_{\delta_{b}}^{(0)})^{2}\restriction\mathcal{H}_{\mathfrak{b}}^{(\tilde{\varepsilon}_{b})}]+\gamma^{2}[(\hat{p}_{b}-\gamma\ell_{\mathrm{p}}^{2}\mathfrak{b})^{2}\restriction\mathcal{H}_{\mathfrak{b}}^{(\tilde{\varepsilon}_{b})}].

(45)

That is, by setting
\mathfrak{b}=0
for the
\hat{\beta}_{\delta_{b}}
in
\hat{\mathfrak{h}}^{(m)}
,
\hat{p}_{b}
has to be replaced by
\hat{p}_{b}-\gamma\ell_{\mathrm{p}}^{2}\mathfrak{b}
. Thus we re-define
\hat{\mathfrak{h}}^{(m)}
as

\displaystyle\hat{\mathfrak{h}}^{(m)}=

\displaystyle\frac{2L_{o}\gamma m}{\delta_{b}}\hat{\beta}_{\delta_{b}}+\frac{1}{\delta_{b}^{2}}\hat{\beta}_{\delta_{b}}^{2}+\gamma^{2}(\hat{p}_{b}-\gamma\ell_{\mathrm{p}}^{2}\mathfrak{b})^{2},

(46)

where
\hat{\beta}_{\delta_{b}}
is defined by (
30
) with
\mathfrak{b}=0
. As a consequence, the operator
\hat{\mathfrak{h}}
is changed correspondingly to

(\hat{\mathfrak{h}}\psi)(m)=\hat{\mathfrak{h}}^{(m)}\psi(m)

(47)

with the re-defined
\hat{\mathfrak{h}}^{(m)}
.

Now let us construct the separable Hilbert
L^{2}(\sigma_{\mathfrak{c}},{\rm d}\mu_{\mathfrak{c}};\mathcal{H}_{\mathfrak{b}}(\cdot))
as mentioned below (
25
). To do this, we only need to assign to each
m
the Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}_{\mathfrak{b}}^{(\varepsilon_{b}(m))}
, i.e., to define
\mathcal{H}_{\mathfrak{b}}(\cdot)
as

\mathcal{H}_{\mathfrak{b}}(\cdot):m\mapsto\mathcal{H}_{\mathfrak{b}}(m):=\mathcal{H}_{\mathfrak{b}}^{(\varepsilon_{b}(m))},

(48)

where
\varepsilon_{b}:m\mapsto\varepsilon_{b}(m)\in[0,2\delta_{b})
is assumed to be some sufficiently well-behaved function. Then the resulting Hilbert space
L^{2}(\sigma_{\mathfrak{c}},{\rm d}\mu_{\mathfrak{c}};\mathcal{H}_{\mathfrak{b}}(\cdot))
is supposed to be acted by
\hat{\mathfrak{h}}
. To define the domain of the unbounded operator
\hat{\mathfrak{h}}
, we first define the domain
D_{\mathfrak{b}}(m)
of
\hat{\mathfrak{h}}^{(m)}\restriction\mathcal{H}_{\mathfrak{b}}^{(\varepsilon_{b}(m))}
for each
m
by

D_{\mathfrak{b}}(m):=\{\psi\in\mathcal{H}_{\mathfrak{b}}^{(\varepsilon_{b}(m))},\langle\mu|\psi\rangle\neq 0\text{ for finite numbers of }\mu\}.

(49)

Then the domain of
\hat{\mathfrak{h}}
, denoted by
D(\hat{\mathfrak{h}})
, reads

D(\hat{\mathfrak{h}})=\{\psi\in L^{2}(\sigma_{\mathfrak{c}},{\rm d}\mu_{\mathfrak{c}};\mathcal{H}_{\mathfrak{b}}(\cdot)),\psi(m)\in D_{\mathfrak{b}}(m)\ \forall m\}.

(50)

We now complete the rigorous definition of the operator
\hat{\mathfrak{h}}
. Since
\hat{\mathfrak{h}}
is identical to the operator-valued function
\hat{\mathfrak{h}}^{(\cdot)}:m\mapsto\hat{\mathfrak{h}}^{(m)}
, instead of investigating
\hat{\mathfrak{h}}
itself, it is sufficient to study
\hat{\mathfrak{h}}^{(m)}
for all
m
.
In the following, to simplify our notions, we will abbreviate
\varepsilon_{b}(m)
to
\varepsilon_{b}
if the dependence of
\varepsilon_{b}
on
m
does not matter. Moreover, the restricted operator
\hat{\mathfrak{h}}^{(m)}\restriction\mathcal{H}_{\mathfrak{b}}^{(\varepsilon_{b})}
with the domain
D_{\mathfrak{b}}(m)
is denoted simply by
\hat{\mathfrak{h}}^{(m)}
. Furthermore, when
\hat{\mathfrak{h}}
is mentioned, it refers to the operator
\hat{\mathfrak{h}}
with domain
D(\hat{\mathfrak{h}})
.

The operator
\hat{\mathfrak{h}}
and
\hat{\mathfrak{h}}^{(m)}
are necessary to be self-adjoint to govern a well-defined dynamics. By using the Kato-Rellich Theorem
Reed et al. 2003a
, we can prove that both
\hat{\mathfrak{h}}
and
\hat{\mathfrak{h}}^{(m)}
are essentially self-adjoint (see Appendix
B
for details). Because of the essential self-adjointness of
\hat{\mathfrak{h}}
and
\hat{\mathfrak{h}}^{(m)}
, their closure, denoted by
\overline{\hat{\mathfrak{h}}^{(m)}}
and
\overline{\hat{\mathfrak{h}}}
respectively, can be regarded as the Hamiltonian operator of the current model with the desired properties. We use
\overline{D_{\mathfrak{b}}(m)}
and
\overline{D(\hat{\mathfrak{h}})}
to denote the domains of
\overline{\hat{\mathfrak{h}}^{(m)}}
and
\overline{\hat{\mathfrak{h}}}
respectively.

IV
Dynamics govern by
\overline{\hat{\mathfrak{h}}}

To solve the dynamics governed by
\overline{\hat{\mathfrak{h}}}
, one needs to diagonalize
\overline{\hat{\mathfrak{h}}}
, or equivalently, to diagonalize
\overline{\hat{\mathfrak{h}}^{(m)}}
for all
m
. To begin with, we first study the discreteness of the spectrum of
\overline{\hat{\mathfrak{h}}^{(m)}}
.
By definition,
\hat{\mathfrak{h}}^{(m)}
can be re-expressed as

\hat{\mathfrak{h}}^{(m)}=-L_{0}^{2}m^{2}\gamma^{2}+\left(L_{0}m\gamma+\frac{1}{\delta_{b}}\hat{\beta}_{\delta_{b}}\right)^{2}+\gamma^{2}(\hat{p}_{b}-\gamma\ell_{\mathrm{p}}^{2}\mathfrak{b})^{2}.

(51)

Thus, one has

\langle\psi|\overline{\hat{\mathfrak{h}}^{(m)}}|\psi\rangle\geq\gamma^{2}\langle\psi|(\hat{p}_{b}-\gamma\ell_{\mathrm{p}}^{2}\mathfrak{b})^{2}|\psi\rangle-L_{0}^{2}m^{2}\gamma^{2}\langle\psi|\psi\rangle,\ \forall|\psi\rangle\in\overline{D_{\mathfrak{b}}(m)}.

(52)

Hence
\overline{\hat{\mathfrak{h}}^{(m)}}
is bounded from below. By (
52
), we can obtain

\mu_{n}(\overline{\hat{\mathfrak{h}}^{(m)}})\geq-L_{0}^{2}m^{2}\gamma^{2}+\gamma^{2}\mu_{n}((\hat{p}_{b}-\gamma\ell_{\mathrm{p}}^{2}\mathfrak{b})^{2})

(53)

where, for an operator
\hat{A}
,
\mu_{n}(\hat{A})
denotes

\mu_{n}(\hat{A}):=\sup_{\varphi_{1},\cdots,\varphi_{n-1}\in\mathcal{H}_{\mathfrak{b}}^{\varepsilon_{b}}}\inf_{\begin{subarray}{c}\psi\in\overline{D_{\mathfrak{b}}(m)};\|\psi\|=1;\\
\langle\varphi_{i},\psi\rangle=0,\forall i=1,\cdots,n-1\end{subarray}}\langle\psi,\hat{A}\psi\rangle.

(54)

Note that
(\hat{p}_{b}-\gamma\ell_{\mathrm{p}}^{2}\mathfrak{b})^{2}
can be defined on
\overline{D_{\mathfrak{b}}(m)}
by definition. Hence
\mu_{n}((\hat{p}_{b}-\gamma\ell_{\mathrm{p}}^{2}\mathfrak{b})^{2})
is well-defined.
Since
\mu_{n}((\hat{p}_{b}-\gamma\ell_{\mathrm{p}}^{2}\mathfrak{b})^{2})\to\infty
as
n\to\infty
, one has that

\lim_{n\to\infty}\mu_{n}(\overline{\hat{\mathfrak{h}}^{(m)}})=\infty.

(55)

Hence, according to the min-max principle (see, e.g., Theorem XIII.1 in
Reed et al. 2003b
),
\overline{\hat{\mathfrak{h}}^{(m)}}
has purely discrete spectrum. In other words, each element in the spectrum of
\overline{\hat{\mathfrak{h}}^{(m)}}
, denoted by
\sigma(\overline{\hat{\mathfrak{h}}^{(m)}})
, is an eigenvalue of
\overline{\hat{\mathfrak{h}}^{(m)}}
with finite multiplicity.

Given the significance of
\overline{\hat{\mathfrak{h}}^{(m)}}
, it is desirable to understand the properties of
\sigma(\hat{\mathfrak{h}}^{(m)})
. In particular, one may ask whether the eigenvalue
\omega(m)
as a function of
m
is analytic or not. This issue is closely related to the analyticity of
\overline{\hat{\mathfrak{h}}^{(m)}}
on
m
in the sense of Kato
Reed et al. 2003b
;
Kato 2013
.
To overcome the technical difficulty that
\overline{\hat{\mathfrak{h}}^{(m)}}
for different
m
are defined in different Hilbert spaces, we employ the following unitary map for a given
m_{o}
,

\mathfrak{i}_{m}:\mathcal{H}_{\mathfrak{b}}^{(\varepsilon_{b}(m))}\ni|\varepsilon_{b}(m)+2n\delta_{b}^{(m)}\rangle\mapsto|\varepsilon_{b}(m_{o})+2n\delta_{b}^{(m_{o})}\rangle\in\mathcal{H}_{\mathfrak{b}}^{(\varepsilon_{b}(m_{o}))}

(56)

where the dependence of
\varepsilon_{b}
and
\delta_{b}
on
m
is written explicitly. The issue on the analyticity of
\omega(m)
can be equivalently discussed by that of
\mathfrak{i}_{m}\overline{\hat{\mathfrak{h}}^{(m)}}\mathfrak{i}_{m}^{-1}
which is defined on
\mathfrak{i}_{m}\overline{D_{\mathfrak{b}}(m)}=\overline{D_{\mathfrak{b}}(m_{o})}
. For simplicity, let us use
\delta_{b}^{o}
and
\varepsilon_{b}^{o}
to denote
\delta_{b}^{(m_{o})}
and
\varepsilon_{b}(m_{o})
respectively.

An sesquilinear form
T_{m}(\cdot,\cdot)
associated to
\mathfrak{i}_{m}\hat{\mathfrak{h}}^{(m)}\mathfrak{i}_{m}^{-1}
can be defined on
D_{\mathfrak{b}}(m_{o})
by

T_{m}(\psi,\phi)=\langle\psi|\mathfrak{i}_{m}\hat{\mathfrak{h}}^{(m)}\mathfrak{i}_{m}^{-1}|\phi\rangle,\ \psi,\phi\in D_{\mathfrak{b}}(m_{o}).

(57)

Then (
52
) implies that
T_{m}
is semibounded, i.e.,

T_{m}(\psi,\psi)\geq-L_{0}^{2}m^{2}\gamma^{2}\|\psi\|^{2},\ \forall\psi\in D_{\mathfrak{b}}(m_{o}).

(58)

Hence,
T_{m}
is closable. Its closure, denoted by
\overline{T_{m}}
, is the extension of
T_{m}
on the closure of
D_{\mathfrak{b}}(m_{o})
with respect to the norm

\|\psi\|_{m}=\sqrt{T_{m}(\psi,\psi)+(L_{0}^{2}m^{2}\gamma^{2}+1)\langle\psi|\psi\rangle}.

(59)

Since the norm
\|\psi\|_{m}
depends on
m
, the resulting closures
Q(m)
of
D_{\mathfrak{b}}(m_{o})
could depend on
m
in general. Suppose that
\delta_{b}^{(m)}
and
\varepsilon_{b}(m)
are analytic functions of
m
at
m_{o}
and
\delta_{b}^{(m_{o})}\neq 0
.
It turns out that
\overline{T_{m}}
is an analytic family of type (a) in the sense of Kato (see section VII.4 in
Kato 2013
). This, by definition, is ensured by that i)
Q(m)=Q(m_{o})
for all
m
sufficiently close to
m_{o}
, and ii)
\overline{T_{m}}(\psi,\psi)
is analytic on
m
for all
\psi\in Q(m_{o})
. The detailed proof of this conclusion is presented in Appendix
C
.

Because
\overline{T_{m}}
is symmetric and closed, there is a self-adjoint operator
\hat{\mathfrak{t}}_{m}
associated to it, which is indeed the Friedrichs extension of
\hat{\mathfrak{h}}^{(m)}
. By the analyticity of
\overline{T_{m}}
,
\hat{\mathfrak{t}}_{m}
forms an analytic family of type (B) in the sense of Kato (see Section VII.4 in
Kato 2013
). As a consequence,
\mathfrak{i}_{m}\overline{\hat{\mathfrak{h}}^{(m)}}\mathfrak{i}_{m}^{-1}
and thus
\overline{\hat{\mathfrak{h}}^{(m)}}
carry the same property because of
\hat{\mathfrak{t}}_{m}=\mathfrak{i}_{m}\overline{\hat{\mathfrak{h}}^{(m)}}\mathfrak{i}_{m}^{-1}
, which can be proven as follows. Firstly, the domain
\overline{D_{\mathfrak{b}}(m_{o})}
of
\mathfrak{i}_{m}\overline{\hat{\mathfrak{h}}^{(m)}}\mathfrak{i}_{m}^{-1}
is the closure of
D_{\mathfrak{b}}(m_{o})
with respect to the graph norm

\|\psi\|_{g}:=\sqrt{\|\mathfrak{i}_{m}\hat{\mathfrak{h}}^{(m)}\mathfrak{i}_{m}^{-1}\psi\|^{2}+\|\psi\|^{2}}.

Secondly, applying the same techniques in the proof of Lemma
C.1
in Appendix
C
, one can show straight-forwardly that
\|\cdot\|_{g}
is equivalent to the norm
\|\cdot\|_{g}^{\prime}
defined by

\|\psi\|_{g}^{\prime}:=\sqrt{\langle\psi|\hat{p}_{b}^{4}|\psi\rangle+\langle\psi|\psi\rangle}.

Finally, because of

\|\psi\|_{+}\leq\|\psi\|_{g}^{\prime}
for all
\psi\in D_{\mathfrak{b}}(m_{o})
, one concludes
\overline{D_{\mathfrak{b}}(m_{o})}\subset Q(m)
, which results in
\hat{\mathfrak{t}}_{m}=\mathfrak{i}_{m}\overline{\hat{\mathfrak{h}}^{(m)}}\mathfrak{i}_{m}^{-1}
according to the uniqueness of the Friedrichs extension.

Due to the analyticity of
\overline{\hat{\mathfrak{h}}^{(m)}}
, the analyticity of
\omega(m)
can obtained directly (see e.g. Chapter XII of
Reed et al. 2003b
and Theorem VII.1.8 in
Kato 2013
), which is summarized precisely as the following theorem.

Theorem IV.1
.

Suppose that
\delta_{b}^{(m)}
and
\varepsilon_{b}(m)
are analytic functions of
m
at
\mathring{m}
and
\delta_{b}^{(\mathring{m})}\neq 0
.
Given an eigenvalue
\mathring{\omega}
of
\overline{\hat{\mathfrak{h}}^{(\mathring{m})}}
which is of an algebraic multiplicity
k
, for each
m
which is sufficiently close to
\mathring{m}
,

\overline{\hat{\mathfrak{h}}^{(m)}}
has exactly
k
eigenvalues (counting multiplicity) near
\mathring{\omega}
. These eigenvalues are given by
p\ (\leq k)
distinct, single-valued and analytic functions
\omega_{1}(m),\cdots,\omega_{p}(m)
.

While this theorem is valid for the general cases that the eigenvalue
\omega
possesses the algebraic multiplicity
k\geq 1
, it can be seen from the numerical results in the next section that each eigenspace of
\hat{\mathfrak{h}}^{(m)}
for all
m
is exactly one-dimensional.

IV.1
Numerical approach to compute the eigenvalues and eigenstates

Since
\overline{\hat{\mathfrak{h}}^{(m)}}
has only discrete spectrum, the eigenvector
|\psi\rangle
associated to each
\omega\in\sigma(\overline{\hat{\mathfrak{h}}^{(m)}})
is normalizable. Hence the function
\psi(\mu):=\langle\mu|\psi\rangle
decreases rapidly for sufficiently large values of
|\mu|
. This fact motivates us to use the finite-dimensional cut-off approximation to collect eigenvalues of
\overline{\hat{\mathfrak{h}}^{(m)}}
. More precisely, we consider a finitely dimensional subspace, denoted by
\mathcal{H}_{\mathfrak{b}}^{(\varepsilon_{b},k)}
, spanned by
|\mu_{\eta}\rangle=|\varepsilon_{b}+2\eta\delta_{b}\rangle\in\mathcal{H}_{\mathfrak{b}}^{(\varepsilon_{b})}
with
|\eta|\leq k
for some large
k
.
Let
\hat{P}^{(k)}
be the projection operator to
\mathcal{H}_{\mathfrak{b}}^{(\varepsilon_{b},k)}
such that

\hat{P}^{(k)}|\mu_{\eta}\rangle=\left\{\begin{array}[]{cc}|\mu_{\eta}\rangle,&|\eta|\leq k,\\
0,&\text{otherwise}.\end{array}\right.

(60)

Given an eigenvector
|\psi\rangle
of
\overline{\hat{\mathfrak{h}}^{(m)}}
with respect to an eigenvalue
\omega
, we have

\displaystyle(\hat{P}^{(k)}\overline{\hat{\mathfrak{h}}^{(m)}}\hat{P}^{(k)}-\omega)|\psi\rangle=(\hat{P}^{(k)}\overline{\hat{\mathfrak{h}}^{(m)}}\hat{P}^{(k)}-\overline{\hat{\mathfrak{h}}^{(m)}})|\psi\rangle

(61)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-(1-\hat{P}^{(k)})\overline{\hat{\mathfrak{h}}^{(m)}}\hat{P}^{(k)}|\psi\rangle-\hat{P}^{(k)}\overline{\hat{\mathfrak{h}}^{(m)}}(1-\hat{P}^{(k)})|\psi\rangle-(1-\hat{P}^{(k)})\overline{\hat{\mathfrak{h}}^{(m)}}(1-\hat{P}^{(k)})|\psi\rangle.

Since
\psi(\mu):=\langle\mu|\psi\rangle
rapidly decreases for sufficiently large values of
|\mu|
, the term
1-\hat{P}^{(k)}
in the RHS of (
61
) indicates that
(\hat{P}^{(k)}\overline{\hat{\mathfrak{h}}^{(m)}}\hat{P}^{(k)}-\omega)|\psi\rangle
should be very small for large
k
. Thus it is reasonable to expect that
\omega
and
|\psi\rangle
can be approximated by certain eigenvalue and its corresponding eigenvector of
\hat{P}^{(k)}\overline{\hat{\mathfrak{h}}^{(m)}}\hat{P}^{(k)}
respectively for large
k
. This is the reason for our finite-dimensional cut-off approximation method. To apply this method, we need to identify those
eigenvalues of
\hat{P}^{(k)}\overline{\hat{\mathfrak{h}}^{(m)}}\hat{P}^{(k)}
suitable for approximating the eigenvalues of
\hat{\mathfrak{h}}^{(m)}
, and check whether all eigenvalues of
\hat{\mathfrak{h}}^{(m)}
can be approximated by this way.

Since
\hat{P}^{(k)}\overline{\hat{\mathfrak{h}}^{(m)}}\hat{P}^{(k)}
is a
(2k+1)
dimensional symmetric matrix under the basis
|\mu\rangle
, it has
(2k+1)
eigenvalues. We denote them as
\lambda_{i}^{(k)}
with
\lambda_{1}^{(k)}\leq\lambda_{2}^{(k)}\leq\cdots\leq\lambda_{2k+1}^{(k)}
, where the subscript
i
denoted that
\lambda_{i}^{(k)}
is the
i
th eigenvalue from the least and the superscript
k
corresponds to the superscript of
\hat{P}^{(k)}
. Obviously, we have
i\leq 2k+1
in
\lambda_{i}^{(k)}
. Thus, once the
i
th eigenvalue of
\hat{P}^{(k)}\overline{\hat{\mathfrak{h}}^{(m)}}\hat{P}^{(k)}
for some
k
is mentioned,
k
should satisfy
k\geq k_{o}(i)
with
k_{o}(i):=(i-1)/2
.
Moreover, because
\overline{\hat{\mathfrak{h}}^{(m)}}
is semibounded, it has the minimal eigenvalue. Thus its eigenvalues can be denoted by
\omega_{i}
with
i=1,2,\cdots
such that
\omega_{1}\leq\omega_{2}\leq\cdots\leq\omega_{n}\leq\cdots
. Then by using the Rayleigh-Reitz technique (see, e.g., Theorem XIII.3 in
Reed et al. 2003b
and Appendix
D
), one can obtain

\lambda_{i}^{(k_{o}(i))}\geq\lambda_{i}^{(k_{o}(i)+1)}\geq\cdots\geq\lambda_{i}^{(k_{o}(i)+n)}\geq\cdots\geq\omega_{i}.

(62)

As a consequence, the limit of
\lambda_{i}^{(k)}
as
k\to\infty
exists, i.e.,

\lambda_{i}:=\lim_{k\to\infty}\lambda_{i}^{(k)}<\infty.

(63)

Consider the sequence
\{\psi_{i}^{(k_{o}(i))},\psi_{i}^{(k_{o}(i)+1)},\cdots,\psi_{i}^{(k_{o}(i)+n)},\cdots\}
, where, for each
k
,
\psi_{i}^{(k)}
is an eigenvector of
\hat{P}^{(k)}\hat{\mathfrak{h}}^{(m)}\hat{P}^{(k)}
with the eigenvalue
\lambda_{i}^{(k)}
and satisfies
\|\psi_{i}^{(k)}\|=1
. Because of
\|\psi_{k}^{(k)}\|=1
, the sequence
\{\psi_{i}^{(k_{o}(i))},\psi_{i}^{(k_{o}(i)+1)},\cdots,\psi_{i}^{(k_{o}(i)+n)},\cdots\}
contains a subsequence by the Banach-Alaoglu theorem, i.e., there exists
\psi_{i}\in\mathcal{H}_{\mathfrak{b}}^{(\varepsilon_{b})}
such that

\lim_{l\to\infty}\langle\psi_{i}^{(n_{l})}|\varphi\rangle=\langle\psi_{i}|\varphi\rangle,\ \forall\varphi\in\mathcal{H}_{\mathfrak{b}}^{(\varepsilon_{b})}.

(64)

Consider all weakly convergent subsequences of
\{\psi_{i}^{(k_{o}(i))},\psi_{i}^{(k_{o}(i)+1)},\cdots,\psi_{i}^{(k_{o}(i)+n)},\cdots\}
and collect their limits defined by (
64
). Denote the space spanned by these limits as
\Lambda_{i}
. It turns out that each
\lambda_{i}
is an eigenvalue of
\overline{\hat{\mathfrak{h}}^{(m)}}
and all the elements in
\Lambda_{i}
are eigenvectors corresponding to the eigenvalue
\lambda_{i}
(see Theorem
E.1
in Appendix
E
for proof). Therefore, the
i
th eigenvalue and its corresponding eigenvectors of
\hat{P}^{(k)}\overline{\hat{\mathfrak{h}}^{(m)}}\hat{P}^{(k)}
with
k\gg i
approximate some eigenvalue and eigenvectors of
\overline{\hat{\mathfrak{h}}^{(m)}}
respectively. To check whether all eigenvalues of
\hat{\mathfrak{h}}^{(m)}
can be approximated by the finite-dimensional cut-off approximation, we can show that

\sigma(\overline{\hat{\mathfrak{h}}^{(m)}})\cap(\lambda_{i},\lambda_{i+1})=\emptyset,

(65)

if
\lambda_{i}\neq\lambda_{i+1}
(see Theorem
E.2
in Appendix
E
for more details). In other words, each eigenvalue of
\overline{\hat{\mathfrak{h}}^{(m)}}
is a limit point of the sequence
\{\lambda_{j}^{k_{o}(j)},\lambda_{j}^{k_{o}(j)+1},\cdots,\lambda_{j}^{k_{o}(j)+n},\cdots\}
for some
j
.

The accuracy of the above approximation can be discussed by the following procedure as in
Kato 1949
;
Harrell 1978
. Let
|\psi_{i}^{(k)}\rangle
be a normalized eigenvector of
\hat{P}^{(k)}\overline{\hat{\mathfrak{h}}^{(m)}}\hat{P}^{(k)}
corresponding to the eigenvalue
\lambda_{i}^{(k)}
. One has

\langle\psi_{i}^{(k)}|\overline{\hat{\mathfrak{h}}^{(m)}}|\psi_{i}^{(k)}\rangle=\lambda_{k}^{(k)}.

(66)

Defining

\epsilon_{i}^{(k)}:=\|(\overline{\hat{\mathfrak{h}}^{(m)}}-\lambda_{i}^{(k)})\psi_{i}^{(k)}\|,

(67)

we will show

\lambda_{i}\in(\lambda_{i}^{(k)}-\epsilon_{i}^{(k)},\lambda_{i}^{(k)}+\epsilon_{i}^{(k)}).

(68)

Let
\{|\omega,\delta\rangle\}
be an orthonormal basis of the Hilbert space, consisting of eigenvectors of
\overline{\hat{\mathfrak{h}}^{(m)}}
, where
\omega
denotes the eigenvalue and
\delta
represents other quantum numbers. Given
\lambda_{i}^{(k)}
, for arbitrary real numbers
\alpha
and
\beta
with
\alpha<\lambda_{i}^{(k)}<\beta
, we have

\langle\psi_{i}^{(k)}|(\overline{\hat{\mathfrak{h}}^{(m)}}-\alpha)(\overline{\hat{\mathfrak{h}}^{(m)}}-\beta)|\psi_{i}^{(k)}\rangle=\sum_{\omega}\sum_{\delta}(\omega-\alpha)(\omega-\beta)|\langle\psi_{i}^{(k)}|\omega,\delta\rangle|^{2}.

(69)

Then supposing
(\alpha,\beta)\cap\sigma(\overline{\hat{\mathfrak{h}}^{(m)}})=\emptyset
, one will get

\langle\psi_{i}^{(k)}|(\overline{\hat{\mathfrak{h}}^{(m)}}-\alpha)(\overline{\hat{\mathfrak{h}}^{(m)}}-\beta)|\psi_{i}^{(k)}\rangle\geq 0.

(70)

Because of
\langle\psi_{i}^{(k)}|(\hat{\mathfrak{h}}^{(m)}-\lambda_{i}^{(k)})|\psi_{i}^{(k)}\rangle=0
,
\langle\psi_{i}^{(k)}|(\overline{\hat{\mathfrak{h}}^{(m)}}-\alpha)(\overline{\hat{\mathfrak{h}}^{(m)}}-\beta)|\psi_{i}^{(k)}\rangle
can be expanded as

\langle\psi_{i}^{(k)}|(\overline{\hat{\mathfrak{h}}^{(m)}}-\alpha)(\overline{\hat{\mathfrak{h}}^{(m)}}-\beta)|\psi_{i}^{(k)}\rangle=(\epsilon_{i}^{(k)})^{2}+(\lambda_{i}^{(k)}-\alpha)(\lambda_{i}^{(k)}-\beta).

(71)

Substituting (
71
) into (
70
), we get

\beta\leq\lambda_{i}^{(k)}+\frac{(\epsilon_{i}^{(k)})^{2}}{\lambda_{i}^{(k)}-\alpha}.

(72)

Note that (
72
) holds under the assumption
(\alpha,\beta)\cap\sigma(\overline{\hat{\mathfrak{h}}^{(m)}})=\emptyset
. Thus, if
\beta>\lambda_{i}^{(k)}+(\epsilon_{i}^{(k)})^{2}/(\lambda_{i}^{(k)}-\alpha)
, one will get
(\alpha,\beta)\cap\sigma(\overline{\hat{\mathfrak{h}}^{(m)}})\neq\emptyset
, which, together with the fact that
\sigma(\overline{\hat{\mathfrak{h}}^{(m)}})
is closed, ensures

\left(\alpha,\lambda_{i}^{(k)}+\frac{(\epsilon_{i}^{(k)})^{2}}{\lambda_{i}^{(k)}-\alpha}\right]\cap\sigma(\overline{\hat{\mathfrak{h}}^{(m)}})\neq\emptyset.

(73)

Choosing
\alpha
as
\alpha=\lambda_{i}^{(k)}-\epsilon_{i}^{(k)}
, we get

\left(\lambda_{i}^{(k)}-\epsilon_{i}^{(k)},\lambda_{i}^{(k)}+\epsilon_{i}^{(k)}\right]\cap\sigma(\overline{\hat{\mathfrak{h}}^{(m)}})\neq\emptyset.

(74)

Noting that (
74
) is true for all
k\geq k_{o}(i)
and
\lim_{k\to\infty}\lambda_{i}^{(k)}\pm\epsilon_{i}^{(k)}=\lambda_{i}
, one finally has

\lambda_{i}\in(\lambda_{i}^{(k)}-\epsilon_{i}^{(k)},\lambda_{i}^{(k)}+\epsilon_{i}^{(k)}].

(75)

Similar to the derivation of (
72
), one can also obtain

\alpha\geq\lambda_{i}^{(k)}+\frac{(\epsilon_{i}^{(k)})^{2}}{\lambda_{i}^{(k)}-\beta},

(76)

By choosing
\beta=\lambda_{i}^{(k)}+\epsilon_{i}^{(k)}
, (
76
) will finally lead to

\lambda_{i}\in[\lambda_{i}^{(k)}-\epsilon_{i}^{(k)},\lambda_{i}^{(k)}+\epsilon_{i}^{(k)}).

(77)

Then (
68
) is obtained by combining (
75
) and (
77
).

The above discussion can also be applied to estimate the accuracy of approximating
|\psi_{i}\rangle
by
|\psi_{i}^{(k)}\rangle
. Given an interval
(\alpha_{o},\beta_{o})\ni\lambda_{i}
such that
\lambda_{i}
is the only eigenvalue contained in it, we have

\displaystyle\langle\psi_{i}^{(k)}|(\hat{\mathfrak{h}}^{(m)}-\alpha_{o})(\hat{\mathfrak{h}}^{(m)}-\beta_{o})|\psi_{i}^{(k)}\rangle-(\lambda_{i}-\alpha_{o})(\lambda_{i}-\beta_{o})\sum_{\delta}|\langle\psi_{i}^{(n)}|\lambda_{i},\delta\rangle|^{2}

(78)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{\lambda\neq\lambda_{i}}\sum_{\delta}(\lambda-\alpha)(\lambda-\beta)\|\langle\psi_{k}^{(n)}|\lambda,\delta\rangle\|\geq 0.

Substituting (
71
) into (
78
), one obtains

(\epsilon_{i}^{(k)})^{2}+(\lambda_{i}^{(k)}-\alpha)(\lambda_{i}^{(k)}-\beta)\geq(\lambda_{i}-\alpha_{o})(\lambda_{i}-\beta_{o})\sum_{\delta}|\langle\psi_{i}^{(n)}|\lambda_{i},\delta\rangle|^{2}

(79)

which leads to

1-\sum_{\delta}\left|\langle\psi_{i}^{(k)}|\lambda_{i},\delta\rangle\right|^{2}\leq\frac{(\varepsilon_{i}^{(k)})^{2}}{(\lambda_{i}^{(k)}-\alpha_{o})(\beta_{o}-\lambda_{i}^{(k)})}.

(80)

Now let us assume
\lambda_{i-1}^{(k)}\neq\lambda_{i}^{(k)}\neq\lambda_{i+1}^{(k)}
without loss of generality. Then one can choose a sufficiently large
k
such that

\lambda_{i-1}^{(k)}+\epsilon_{i-1}^{(k)}<\lambda_{i}^{(k)}-\epsilon_{i}^{(k)},\ \lambda_{i}^{(k)}+\epsilon_{i}^{(k)}<\lambda_{i+1}^{(k)}-\epsilon_{i+1}^{(k)},

(81)

which, together with (
68
), implies that the interval
(\lambda_{i-1}^{(k)}+\epsilon_{i-1}^{(k)},\lambda_{i+1}^{(k)}-\epsilon_{i+1}^{(k)})
contains the single eigenvalue
\lambda_{i}
. Then, according to (
80
), we get

1-\sum_{\delta}\left|\langle\psi_{i}^{(k)}|\lambda_{i},\delta\rangle\right|^{2}\leq\frac{(\epsilon_{i}^{(k)})^{2}}{(\lambda_{i}^{(k)}-\lambda_{i-1}^{(k)}-\epsilon_{i-1}^{(k)})(\lambda_{i+1}^{(k)}-\lambda_{i}^{(k)}-\epsilon_{i+1}^{(k)})}.

(82)

The left hand side of (
82
) measures the accuracy of approximating
|\psi_{i}\rangle
by
|\psi_{i}^{(k)}\rangle
because
|\psi_{i}^{(k)}\rangle
is normalized.

V
The quantum dynamics

We now study the dynamics of the model for the cases of
\hat{p}_{\varphi}\neq 0
and
\hat{p}_{\varphi}=0
respectively. For
\hat{p}_{\varphi}\neq 0
, the corresponding classical solution is an extension of the Schwarzschild interior with an extra minimally coupled massless scalar field. This extension is referred to as the Janis-Newman-Winicour (JNW) interior which differs from the usual JNW spacetime as an extension of Schwarzschild exterior
Janis et al. 1968
. In the classical JNW interior
Zhang and Zhang 2020
, characterized by a parameter
B=2\sqrt{m^{2}+Gp_{\varphi}^{2}/4\pi}
, there are two singularities at
r=0
and
r=B
respectively, where
r
is the radial coordinate. Once
p_{\varphi}
vanishes, the singularity at
r=B
will disappear and is replaced by the Schwarzschild horizon, so that the JNW interior becomes the Schwarzschild interior.

In Section
II.1
, gravity is deparametrized by the scalar field which provides a material reference frame of time.
However, for the case of
\hat{p}_{\varphi}=0
, the reference frame of time will disappear. The physical Hamiltonian becomes the Hamiltonian constraint. However, it is still necessary to understand the dynamics of the system as the relational evolution with respect to certain gravitational degree of freedom. The information of the dynamics is encoded in the solutions to
\hat{\mathfrak{h}}\psi=0
. Thus we need to solve this equation.

V.1
Dynamics for
\hat{p}_{\varphi}\neq 0

By (
22
), the Hilbert space
L^{2}(\sigma_{\mathfrak{c}},{\rm d}\mu_{\mathfrak{c}};\mathcal{H}_{\mathfrak{b}}(\cdot))
of the model consists of functions
\psi:m\mapsto\psi(m)\in\mathcal{H}_{\mathfrak{b}}(m)
.
As shown in Section
III.2
,
\mathcal{H}_{\mathfrak{b}}(m)
can be chosen as the one defined by (
31
) with
\varepsilon_{b}=0
and some
m
-dependent
\delta_{b}
, denoted by
\delta^{(m)}_{b}
whose explicit expression depends on the schemes to quantize
\mathfrak{h}
. With this convention, a state
\psi\in L^{2}(\sigma_{\mathfrak{c}},{\rm d}\mu_{\mathfrak{c}};\mathcal{H}_{\mathfrak{b}}(\cdot))
can be represented by a family of functions

\psi(m,\cdot):\eta\to\psi(m,2\delta_{b}^{(m)}\eta)

with
\eta\in\mathbb{Z}
. Given a state
\psi
, according to (
9
) and (
11
), an associated dynamical state reads

\psi(\varphi,m)=e^{\mp i\frac{\sqrt{4\pi G}}{L_{0}\gamma\ell_{\mathrm{p}}^{2}}\varphi\sqrt{\hat{\mathfrak{h}}^{(m)}}}\hat{P}_{[0,\infty)}\psi(m).

(92)

Let
|\omega(m)\rangle\in\mathcal{H}_{\mathfrak{b}}(m)
be the normalized eigenvector of
\hat{\mathfrak{h}}^{(m)}
with respect to the eigenvalue
\omega(m)
. Then (
92
) is simplified as

\psi(\varphi,m)=\sum_{\omega(m)\geq 0}e^{\mp i\frac{\sqrt{4\pi G\omega(m)}}{L_{0}\gamma\ell_{\mathrm{p}}^{2}}\varphi}\langle\omega(m)|\psi(m)\rangle|\omega(m)\rangle.

(93)

We choose
\psi(m)
as

\psi(m,\eta)=e^{-\frac{(m-m_{0})^{2}}{2\sigma_{m}^{2}}+i\lambda m}e^{-\frac{(\eta-\eta_{0})^{2}}{2\sigma_{\eta}^{2}}-i\beta\eta}

(94)

which carries some semiclassical features. According to (
92
), the initial state evolved by the Hamiltonian is
\hat{P}_{[0,\infty)}\psi(m)
. Then, it is possible that
\hat{P}_{[0,\infty)}\psi(m)
is no longer a semiclassical wave packet even though
\psi(m)
is. To see how to avoid this possibility, we introduce the expectation value of
\hat{\mathfrak{h}}^{(m)}
,

\omega_{o}(m):=\langle\psi(m)|\hat{\mathfrak{h}}^{(m)}|\psi(m)\rangle

and its uncertainty

\Delta\omega(m):=\sqrt{\langle\psi(m)|(\hat{\mathfrak{h}}^{(m)})^{2}|\psi(m)\rangle-\langle\psi(m)|\hat{\mathfrak{h}}^{(m)}|\psi(m)\rangle^{2}}.

For each
m
, we think of
\psi(m)
as a wave function of the eigenvalues
\omega
of
\hat{\mathfrak{h}}^{(m)}
, so it is some wave packet peaked at
\omega_{0}(m)
with fluctuation
\Delta\omega(m)
.
The projection
\hat{P}_{[0,\infty)}
cuts off
\psi(m)
at
m=0
and vanishes it for all
\omega(m)\leq 0
. Therefore,
\hat{P}_{[0,\infty)}\psi(m)
can keep the wave-packet feature of
\psi(m)
only if
|\omega_{o}(m)|\gg\Delta\omega(m)
. This condition is the criterion to choose the parameters in (
94
).

For a properly chosen
\psi(m)
, its evolution reads

\psi(m,\eta,\varphi)=e^{-\frac{(m-m_{0})^{2}}{2\sigma_{m}^{2}}+i\lambda m}\sum_{\omega(m)\geq 0}\langle\mu_{\eta}^{(m)}|\omega(m)\rangle e^{i\frac{\sqrt{4\pi G\omega(m)}}{L_{0}\gamma\ell_{\mathrm{p}}^{2}}\varphi}\sum_{\eta^{\prime}}e^{-\frac{(\eta^{\prime}-\eta_{0})^{2}}{2\sigma_{\eta}^{2}}-i\beta\eta^{\prime}}\langle\omega(m)|\mu_{\eta^{\prime}}^{(m)}\rangle.

(95)

To check the consistence between the quantum dynamics and effective dynamics, we calculate the expectation value of the operator
\hat{p}_{b}
as

\displaystyle\frac{\langle\hat{p}_{b}\rangle}{\gamma\delta_{b}\ell_{\mathrm{p}}^{2}}=\mathcal{N}^{2}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}{\rm d}me^{-\frac{(m-m_{0})^{2}}{\sigma_{m}^{2}}}\sum_{\eta}\eta\left|\sum_{\omega(m)\geq 0}\langle\mu_{\eta}^{(m)}|\omega(m)\rangle e^{i\frac{\sqrt{4\pi G\omega(m)}}{\ell_{\mathrm{p}}^{2}\gamma L_{0}}\varphi}\sum_{\eta^{\prime}}e^{-\frac{(\eta^{\prime}-\eta_{0})^{2}}{2\sigma_{\eta}^{2}}-i\beta\eta^{\prime}}\langle\omega(m)|\mu_{\eta^{\prime}}^{(m)}\rangle\right|^{2}

(96)

\displaystyle=:

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\sqrt{\pi}\,\sigma_{m}}\sum_{\eta}\eta\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}{\rm d}me^{-\frac{(m-m_{0})^{2}}{\sigma_{m}^{2}}}\mathcal{P}(\eta,m,\varphi)

where the normalization factor
\mathcal{N}
is given by

\mathcal{N}^{-2}=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}{\rm d}me^{-\frac{(m-m_{0})^{2}}{\sigma_{m}^{2}}}\sum_{\omega(m)\geq 0}\left|\sum_{\eta}e^{-\frac{(\eta-\eta_{0})^{2}}{2\sigma_{\eta}^{2}}-i\beta\eta}\langle\omega(m)|\mu_{\eta}^{(m)}\rangle\right|^{2}=:\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}{\rm d}me^{-\frac{(m-m_{0})^{2}}{\sigma_{m}^{2}}}\mathfrak{n}(m)

(97)

The integral in (
96
) can be calculated by using the saddle point approximation as

\displaystyle\frac{\langle\hat{p}_{b}\rangle}{\gamma\delta_{b}\ell_{\mathrm{p}}^{2}}=

\displaystyle\sum_{\eta}\eta\,\mathcal{P}(\eta,m_{0},\varphi)\left(1+\frac{\sigma_{m_{0}}^{2}}{4}\frac{\mathcal{P}^{\prime\prime}(\eta,m_{0},\varphi)}{\mathcal{P}(\eta,m_{0},\varphi)}+\cdots\right).

(98)

Therefore, as far as the leading order of the evolution is concerned, we only need to compute
\mathcal{P}(\eta,m_{0},\varphi)
. The result of (
97
) reads

\mathcal{N}^{-2}=\sqrt{\pi}\,\sigma_{m_{0}}\mathfrak{n}(m_{0})\left(1+\frac{\sigma_{m}^{2}}{4}\frac{\mathfrak{n}^{\prime\prime}(m_{0})}{\mathfrak{n}(m_{0})}+\cdots\right).

(99)

Hence one has

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}(\eta,m_{0},\varphi)\cong\frac{\left|\sum_{\omega(m_{0})\geq 0}\langle\mu_{\eta}^{(m_{0})}|\omega(m_{0})\rangle e^{i\frac{\sqrt{4\pi G\omega(m_{0})}}{\ell_{\mathrm{p}}^{2}\gamma L_{0}}\varphi}\sum_{\eta^{\prime}}e^{-\frac{(\eta^{\prime}-\eta_{0})^{2}}{2\sigma_{\eta}^{2}}-i\beta\eta^{\prime}}\langle\omega(m_{0})|\mu_{\eta^{\prime}}^{(m_{0})}\rangle\right|^{2}}{\sum_{\omega(m_{0})\geq 0}\left|\sum_{\eta}e^{-\frac{(\eta-\eta_{0})^{2}}{2\sigma_{\eta}^{2}}-i\beta\eta}\langle\omega(m_{0})|\mu_{\eta}^{(m_{0})}\rangle\right|^{2}}.

(100)

By this formula,
\mathcal{P}(\eta,m_{0},\varphi)
can be computed numerically easily. The numerical results of

\mathcal{P}(\eta,m_{0},\varphi)^{1/2}
and
\langle\hat{p}_{b}\rangle/(\gamma\delta_{b}\ell_{\mathrm{p}}^{2})\cong\sum_{\eta}\eta\mathcal{P}(\eta,m_{0},\varphi)
are shown in Fig.
1
, where we choose
\delta_{b}=\sqrt{\Delta}
with
\Delta
being the area gap in LQG.
Moreover, one can also calculate the evolution of
p_{b}
with respect to the effective Hamiltonian

H=\frac{4\pi}{L_{0}G\gamma}\sqrt{\frac{2}{\delta_{b}\delta_{c}}p_{b}\sin(\delta_{b}b)p_{c}\sin(\delta_{c}c)+\frac{1}{\delta_{b}^{2}}p_{b}^{2}\sin(\delta_{b}b)^{2}+\gamma^{2}p_{b}^{2}}.

(101)

As a comparison, the results of the effective dynamics and the classical dynamics are also plotted in Fig.
1
.

As shown in Fig.
1
, both of the classical singularities could be resolved by the effective dynamics where
|\eta|
is prevented from reaching
0
by the bounces at the local minimums. Then the classical spacetime is extended periodically. The quantum evolution matches very well with the effective dynamics for several periods around
\varphi=0
when the semiclassical feature of the coherent state is well kept. The effective evolution and thus the quantum evolution match well with the classical dynamics in the classical regime.
Thus the current quantum theory has a correct semiclassical limit and its semiclassical features can be responded properly by the effective dynamics. However, the coherent
property of the state
cannot be kept along the whole evolution, since the width of the wave packet grows as the time
\varphi
runs far away from the initial value
\varphi=0
. This leads to a significant difference between the quantum and effective dynamics in late time.

Plots of the evolution of the wave packet (top panel) and the evolution of

goes beyond the domain. Then the effective dynamics is no longer valid.

V.2
Dynamics for
\hat{p}_{\varphi}=0

In the case of
\hat{p}_{\varphi}=0
, the dynamics is encoded in the equation

\hat{\mathfrak{h}}\psi=0.

(102)

Alternatively, one could also consider the Hamiltonian constraint operator corresponding to the vacuum Hamiltonian constraint multiplied by volume as a lapse function
Zhang et al. 2020
.
To solve (
102
), it is convenient to represent
\psi(m)
for each
m
by
\psi(m,\cdot):\omega(m)\mapsto\psi(m,\omega(m))\in\mathbb{C}
with
\omega(m)\in\sigma(\overline{\hat{\mathfrak{h}}^{(m)}})
. Then the action of
\overline{\hat{\mathfrak{h}}}
on
\psi
reads

(\hat{\mathfrak{h}}\psi)(m,\omega(m))=\omega(m)\psi(m,\omega(m)).

(103)

By (
23
) the inner product of two states
\psi_{1}
and
\psi_{2}
reads

\left(\psi_{1},\psi_{2}\right)=\int_{\sigma_{\mathfrak{c}}}{\rm d}\mu_{\mathfrak{c}}\sum_{\omega(m)\in\sigma(\overline{\hat{\mathfrak{h}}^{(m)}})}\psi_{1}(m,\omega(m))^{*}\psi_{2}(m,\omega(m))

(104)

where
∗
means the complex conjugate.

Given a solution
\psi
to (
102
), Eq. (
103
) implies

\psi(m,\omega(m))=0,\forall\omega(m)\neq 0.

(105)

Thus the support of the solution
\psi
is contained in the set

\mathcal{S}_{0}=\{(m,\omega(m))|m\in\sigma_{\mathfrak{c}},\omega(m)\in\sigma(\hat{\mathfrak{h}}^{(m)}),\omega(m)=0\},

(106)

which is identified naturally to the set

\sigma_{\xi}:=\{m\in\sigma_{\mathfrak{c}},0\in\sigma(\hat{\mathfrak{h}}^{(m)})\}.

(107)

Given two functions
\psi_{i}
with
i=1,2
on
\sigma_{\xi}
, (
104
) can be expressed by

(\psi_{1},\psi_{2})=\int_{\sigma_{\xi}}{\rm d}\mu_{\mathfrak{c}}\psi_{1}(m)^{*}\psi_{2}(m).

(108)

Thus, one could naively deem that the functions on
\sigma_{\xi}
with the inner product (
108
) would constitute the physical Hilbert space. However, depending on the explicit expression of
\mu_{\mathfrak{c}}
, it could occur that the right hand side of (
108
) vanishes for regular functions
\psi_{1}
and
\psi_{2}
. To see how this happens,
let us assume
\sigma_{\mathfrak{c}}=\mathbb{R}
at first.
Because the eigenvalues of
\hat{\mathfrak{h}}^{(m)}
can be expanded at some
m_{o}
closed to
m
by a power series of
m-m_{o}
and
\sigma(m_{o})
is discrete, in general they are not
0
.
Hence it is reasonable to expect that there are only countably many
m\in\sigma_{\mathfrak{c}}
such that
0\in\sigma(\hat{\mathfrak{h}}^{(m)})
. Then, both
\sigma_{\xi}
and
\mathcal{S}_{0}
are countable sets.
This speculation is confirmed by our numerical computation in the
\mu_{o}
-scheme as we as the scheme with
\delta_{c}=\sqrt{\Delta}
and
\delta_{b}=\sqrt{\Delta}/(2|m|)
, as shown in Fig.
2
.
For the case of
\sigma_{\mathfrak{c}}\neq\mathbb{R}
, one has
\sigma_{\mathfrak{c}}\subset\mathbb{R}
because
\widehat{p_{c}\sin(\delta_{c}c)/\delta_{c}}
was assumed to be self-adjoint. Then the resulting
\sigma_{\xi}
is just a subset of that for the case of
\sigma_{\xi}=\mathbb{R}
. Therefore,
\sigma_{\xi}
is always countable. Since
\sigma_{\xi}
is countable, it could occur that
\sigma_{\xi}\subset\sigma_{\mathfrak{c}}
is of vanishing measure, i.e.
\mu_{\mathfrak{c}}(\sigma_{\xi})=0
. For this case, the right hand side of (
108
) will vanish and thus it cannot define an inner product for the physical Hilbert space.
Hence we introduce the following procedure to define the inner product in the solution space, which is valid not only for the case of
\mu_{\mathfrak{c}}(\sigma_{\xi})=0
but also for the case of
\mu_{\mathfrak{c}}(\sigma_{\xi})\neq 0
. Let
\tilde{\delta}(m_{o},\cdot)
for each
m_{o}\in\sigma_{\mathfrak{c}}
be the function on
\sigma_{\mathfrak{c}}
such that (i)
\tilde{\delta}(m_{o},m)=0
for all
m\neq m_{o}
and (ii)
\int_{\sigma_{\mathfrak{c}}}{\rm d}\mu_{\mathfrak{c}}\tilde{\delta}(m_{o},m)=1
. Thus
\tilde{\delta}(m_{o},\cdot)
is the Dirac-
\delta
distribution for
\mu_{\mathfrak{c}}(\sigma_{\xi})=0
and proportional to the Kronecker
\delta
function otherwise.
Given a regular function
\psi(m,\omega(m))
, a solution to (
102
) can be generated as

\Psi(m)=\sum_{n}\tilde{\delta}(m_{o}^{(n)},m)\delta_{0,\omega_{m}}\psi(m,\omega(m)),

(109)

where
m_{o}^{(n)}\in\sigma_{\xi}\subset\sigma_{\mathfrak{c}}
.
By choosing an appropriate dense subspace
\mathcal{S}\subset\mathcal{H}
, these
\Psi
of (
109
) are indeed anti-linear functionals on

\mathcal{S}
as

\Psi:\phi\mapsto\Psi[\phi]:=\int_{\sigma_{\mathfrak{c}}}{\rm d}\mu_{\mathfrak{c}}\sum_{\omega_{m}}\Psi(m)^{*}\phi(m,\omega_{m})=\sum_{m_{o}^{(n)}}\psi(m_{o}^{(n)},0)^{*}\phi(m_{o}^{(n)},0)

(110)

for all
\phi\in\mathcal{S}
. Hence
\Psi
is the solution to (
102
) in the sense that
\Psi[\hat{\mathfrak{h}}\phi]=0
for all
\phi\in\mathcal{S}
. Thus (
109
) defines a rigging map

\mathbb{P}:\psi\mapsto\mathbb{P}\psi
on
\mathcal{S}
. Therefore, by the refined algebraic quantization procedure
Thiemann 2008
, the physical inner product of two solutions
\Psi_{i}=\mathbb{P}\psi_{i}
with
i=1,2
reads

(\Psi_{1}|\Psi_{2})=\Psi_{1}[\psi_{2}]=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\psi_{1}(m_{o}^{(n)})^{*}\psi_{2}(m_{o}^{(n)}),

(111)

which coincides with (
108
) if
\mu_{\mathfrak{c}}(\sigma_{\xi})\neq 0
.
Hence the physical Hilbert space of the solutions is given by

\mathcal{H}_{\rm BH}:=\overline{\{f:\sigma_{\xi}\to\mathbb{C},\ \sum_{n}|f(m_{o}^{(n)})|^{2}<\infty\}}.

(112)

The Dirac observable
\hat{\xi}_{\delta_{c}}:=\widehat{p_{c}\sin(\delta_{c}c)/\delta_{c}}
in
\mathcal{H}
can be promoted to an operator
\hat{\xi}^{\prime}_{\delta_{c}}
in
\mathcal{H}_{\rm BH}
by the dual action such that

(\hat{\xi}_{\delta_{c}}^{\prime}f)(m_{o}^{(n)})=L_{0}\gamma\delta_{c}m_{o}^{(n)}f(m_{o}^{(n)}),\ \forall\ m_{o}^{(n)}.

(113)

Eq. (
113
) implies that each
m_{o}^{(n)}
is an eigenvalue of
\xi_{\delta_{c}}^{\prime}
, and
\xi_{\delta_{c}}^{\prime}
is self-adjoint in
\mathcal{H}_{\rm BH}
with the spectrum
\overline{\sigma_{\xi}}
as the closure of
\sigma_{\xi}
.

Given
\psi\in\mathcal{H}_{\mathfrak{b}}(m)
, Eq. (
83
) indicates

(\overline{\hat{\mathfrak{h}}^{(-m)}}\psi)(\eta)^{*}=(\overline{\hat{\mathfrak{h}}^{(m)}}\psi^{*})(\eta)

(114)

where
\psi^{*}\in\mathcal{H}_{\mathfrak{b}}(m)
is defined by
\psi^{*}(\eta)=\psi(\eta)^{*}
. For a given
m_{o}^{(n)}\in\sigma_{\xi}
, let
\psi_{0}
be the eigenvector of
\overline{\hat{\mathfrak{h}}^{(m_{o}^{(n)})}}
with the eigenvalue
0
. Then (
114
) ensures that

\overline{\hat{\mathfrak{h}}^{(-m_{o}^{(n)})}}\psi_{0}^{*}=\overline{\hat{\mathfrak{h}}^{(m_{o}^{(n)})}}\psi_{0}=0.

(115)

Thus,
\psi_{0}^{*}
is an eigenvector of
\hat{\mathfrak{h}}^{(-m_{o}^{(n)})}
with the eigenvalue
0
. Therefore, one has
-m_{o}^{(n)}\in\overline{\sigma_{\xi}}
provided
m_{o}^{(n)}\in\overline{\sigma_{\xi}}
.

For
m=0
, one has the operator

\hat{\mathfrak{h}}^{(0)}=\frac{1}{\delta_{b}^{2}}\hat{\beta}_{\delta_{b}}^{2}+\gamma^{2}\hat{p}_{b}^{2}.

Assume that there is an eigenvector
\phi
of
\overline{\hat{\mathfrak{h}}^{(0)}}
with the eigenvalue
0
. Because of
\hat{\beta}_{\delta_{b}}^{2}\geq 0
and
\hat{p}_{b}^{2}\geq 0
,
\phi
would satisfy

\hat{\beta}_{\delta_{b}}^{2}\phi=0=\hat{p}_{b}^{2}\phi.

(116)

By the definitions of
\hat{\beta}_{\delta_{b}}
and
\hat{p}_{b}^{2}
, one can easily check that there is no nontrivial
\phi
satisfying (
116
). Hence,
0
is not an eigenvalue of
\overline{\hat{\mathfrak{h}}^{(0)}}
.
Taking account of Theorem
IV.1
, one gets the conclusion that
0\notin\sigma(\hat{\mathfrak{h}}^{(m)})
for sufficiently small
|m|
. Therefore, there exists a gap between the spectrum
\overline{\sigma_{\xi}}

and
0
, i.e.
0\notin\overline{\sigma_{\xi}}
. Note that
\overline{\sigma_{\xi}}
is the spectrum of
\widehat{p_{c}\sin(\delta_{c}c)/\delta_{c}}
whose classical limit is proportional to the ADM mass of the Schwarzschild BH. Thus, this analysis shows the existence of discrete mass spectrum of the BH. If certain mechanism of BH evaporation could be introduced into our quantum model consistently, the BH would have to evaporate its masses discretely, and the evaporation would have to eventually halt at some state corresponding to the non-vanishing minimal mass, which is referred to as the BH remnant.

VI
Concluding Remark

The loop quantization of the model of Schwarzschild interior coupled to a massless scalar field has been studied in the previous sections. By applying the deparametrization procedure, we get the physical Hamiltonian
\mathfrak{h}
of this model with respect to the scalar field. Since
p_{c}c
is a Dirac observable in the classical theory,
\mathfrak{h}
is promoted to an operator
\hat{\mathfrak{h}}
commutating with the operator
\widehat{p_{c}\sin(\delta_{c}c)/\delta_{c}}
which corresponds to
p_{c}c
. Replacing
\widehat{p_{c}\sin(\delta_{c}c)/\delta_{c}}
in
\hat{\mathfrak{h}}
with its spectrum
L_{o}\gamma m\in\sigma_{\mathfrak{c}}
, we obtain a family of operators
\hat{\mathfrak{h}}^{(m)}
. It is shown that both
\hat{\mathfrak{h}}^{(m)}
and
\hat{\mathfrak{h}}
are self-adjoint. The spectrum of
\hat{\mathfrak{h}}^{(m)}
and its analyticity with respect to
m
are studied. Moreover, we develop a numerical method to diagonalize
\hat{\mathfrak{h}}^{(m)}
. Based on these results, the dynamics for the cases of
\hat{p}_{\varphi}\neq 0
and
\hat{p}_{\varphi}=0
are studied respectively.

For the case of
\hat{p}_{\varphi}\neq 0
, the evolution of a wave packet is considered and the results are compared with the effective dynamics governed by the effective Hamiltonian. It turns out that the quantum evolution matches well with the effective dynamics in the domain where the wave packet is sharply peaked. However, the width of the wave packet would increase as the relational time
\phi
evolves. Thus an inconsistence between the quantum dynamic and the effective dynamics would occur at late time. The numerical codes to compute the evolution can be found in
git
.

For the case of
\hat{p}_{\varphi}=0
, the constraint
\hat{\mathfrak{h}}=0
is imposed to get the physical states of the loop quantum Schwarzschild interior model. Its physical Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}_{\rm BH}
is obtained. The spectrum
\overline{\sigma_{\xi}}
of the Dirac observable
\widehat{p_{c}\sin(\delta_{c}c)/\delta_{c}}
in
\mathcal{H}_{\rm BH}
is analyzed by both analytical and numerical methods. It turns out that the
\overline{\sigma_{\xi}}
is discrete and it does not contain
0
, provided that the parameters
\delta_{b}^{(m)}
and
\varepsilon_{b}(m)
satisfy the suitable analyticity. Thus, there exists a gap between
\overline{\sigma_{\xi}}
and
0
. Moreover, by the numerical method to diagonalize
\hat{\mathfrak{h}}
, we can also compute
\overline{\sigma_{\xi}}
numerically
git
. Some interesting results would be obtained if certain mechanism of BH evaporation could be introduced into our quantum model consistently. The only possibility is that the BH evaporates its mass discretely since
\overline{\sigma_{\xi}}
is discrete. Moreover, because of
0\notin\overline{\sigma_{\xi}}
, the evaporation would eventually halt at the state corresponding to the non-vanishing minimal mass. Hence, our result supports the existence of a black hole remnant after Hawking evaporation.

It should be noted that the analyses in the current paper are valid for a quite general class of schemes such that (i) a separable Hilbert subspace
\mathcal{H}_{\mathfrak{c}}\subset\tilde{\mathcal{H}}_{\mathfrak{c}}
can be chosen to define an operator
\widehat{p_{c}\sin(\delta_{c}c)/\delta_{c}}
corresponding to
p_{c}\sin(\delta_{c}c)/\delta_{c}
, which is self-adjoint and commutates with the physical Hamiltonian
\hat{\mathfrak{h}}
; and (ii)
the quantum parameter
\delta_{b}
is a constant or any function of
p_{c}\sin(\delta_{c}c)/\delta_{c}
.
With the numerical method developed in this paper, it becomes possible to further study the Hawking radiation with the matter backreaction and the distortion of the Hawking spectrum in details. We leave this open issue for our future works. Thus the discrete mass spectrum predicted by our LQG model provides a solid starting point to study the possibilities of considering the BH remnants as dark matter candidates, as well as solving the puzzle of information loss in BH
evaporation.

Generalized Almost Complete Revivals in quantum spin chains
I.V. Ermakov

Abstract
The conception of almost complete revivals has been introduced recently. In a quantum many-body system local observable may exhibit an almost complete revival to its maximal value at the predetermined moment of time. In this paper we extend the original procedure such that the revival may be from an arbitrary point on the Bloch sphere to the arbitrary point. Furthermore in the proposed procedure the reviving and collapsing sites are not necessarily the same. We also demonstrate that for spins S S higher than 1 / 2 1/2 almost complete revivals are suppressed as 1 / S 1/S .

1
Introduction

Local observables of non-integrable quantum many-body systems are expected to quickly reach their equilibrium values. This process of reaching thermal equilibrium is also known as themalization. It’s also usually implied that once some observable has reached thermal equilibrium it stays there practically forever exhibiting only meaningless fluctuations upon its equilibrium value. There is however a number of physical mechanisms when thermalization is either slowed down or not present at all. Among such mechanisms are many-body localization
nandkishore2015many
, spin-glasses
rademaker2020slow
;
stein2013spin
, systems with long-range interactions
gong2013prethermalization
;
neyenhuis2017observation
or systems with constrains
turner2018weak
;
bernien2017probing
;
lin2019exact
. All these mechanisms however are associated with the properties of the system under consideration, an alternative approach is to consider specially designed initial states without putting significant restrictions on the system itself.

In our recent work
ermakov2021almost
we proposed a mechanism of constructing special initial states such that the selected local observable after relaxation exhibits almost complete revival (ACR) to its initial value at the predetermined moment of time. In a closed many-body system
C=A\cup B
for a small subsystem
A
in a pure state the rest of the system
B
usually serves as a thermal reservoir, which leads to the equilibration of
A
. However the reservoir
B
can be finely tuned such that at some predetermined revival time
\tau
, subsystem
A
will be out of equilibrium. So in fact the reservoir can work both ways: thermalize the subsystem or on the contrary push it out of equilibrium. The latter however is exponentially rare event, yet if one can has access all the degrees of freedom of
B
, then the
B
can be tuned such that this event will take place at any desired time
\tau
.

In
ermakov2021almost
we also suggested several applications of ACR to benchmarking of quantum similators. In particular, successful preparation of ACR state can be proved with only one local measurement. We had also proposed to utilize ACR for entanglement assisted sensing and delayed disclosure of a secret. The latter is a scheme which allows one to encode a piece of information into the quantum system such that this information will be accessible only in the short vicinity of the revival moment
\tau
see Figure
1
. Any attempt to extract the information earlier will lead to its permanent destruction. In this regard a system of 5-15 qubits prepared in the ACR state represents some sort of ’’Quantum time capsule’’ which cannot be opened before some specified time.

Figure 1:
Schematic illustration of delayed disclosure of a secret. The information is available only at two moments of time
t=0
and
t=\tau
. Any attempt to extract the information before the
\tau
leads to its destruction.

In
ermakov2021almost
we considered only revivals of spins polarized along
z
-axis to the same value. In the present paper we provide a general prescription on how to construct ACR from an arbitrary point of a Block Sphere to another arbitrary point. Furthermore the collapsing and reviving spins should not necessarily coincide. In this regard, it is not a revival of some local observable as such, but rather a recovery of purity in local subsystem.

We also consider spin chains with spin
S>1/2
, and show that in this case ACR are suppressed by factor
1/S
. This result is in agreement with the classical picture where the ACR are impossible due to the fact that classical spin chains are chaotic in general.

2
General revival scheme in chains of spin 1/2

In this section we generalize the prescription offered in
ermakov2021almost
such that the initial and revived values of selected local spin are both arbitrary points on a Bloch Sphere. Furthermore, we show that the revival may occur on a site different from initial one.

Let us consider a lattice of
L
interacting spins
1/2
. Each spin is describes by the operator
\{S^{\alpha}_{i}\}
where
i
is the lattice index and
\alpha=x,y,z
is the spin projection index. As a collapsing observable we pick the spin
\vec{S}_{q}
and
\vec{S}^{\prime}_{p}
as a reviving one. Notice that while values of
q
and
p
as well as
\vec{S}_{q}
and
\vec{S}^{\prime}_{p}
are different in general, there are no restrictions for them to coincide.

One-spin Hilbert spaces are defined as
|1_{i}\rangle
and
|0_{i}\rangle
, such that
\langle 1_{i}|S^{z}_{i}|1_{i}\rangle=1/2
and
\langle 0_{i}|S^{z}_{i}|0_{i}\rangle=-1/2
. Let us introduce the basis
\mathcal{B}=\{|\varphi_{j}\rangle\}^{2^{L}}_{j=1}
, here
|\varphi_{j}\rangle
is a many-body basis vector. Let us use the following ordering for the basis
\mathcal{B}
:

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}=\{|1_{1}\ 1_{2}\dots 1_{L}\rangle,|1_{1}\ 1_{2}\dots 0_{L}\rangle,\ \dots\ ,|0_{1}\ 0_{2}\dots 1_{L}\rangle,|0_{1}\ 0_{2}\dots 0_{L}\rangle\}.

(1)

Choice of basis determines further construction of ACR states. In our case it is convenient to quantize basis along
z
-axis and order it such as each many-body vector
|\varphi_{j}\rangle
corresponds to the base-
2
form of an integer
j
.

Let us use the following parametrization of the ACR state:

\displaystyle|\Phi_{\text{ACR}}(0)\rangle=\sum\limits^{2^{q-1}}_{k=1}\sum\limits^{2^{L-q}}_{n=1}\left(A_{s(k,n)}|\varphi_{s(k,n)}\rangle+\alpha A_{s(k,n)}|\varphi_{s(k,n)+2^{L-q}}\rangle\right),

(2)

here
q
is the collapsing site,
\alpha
- complex parameter defining the state of the collapsing site on a Bloch Sphere, and function
s(k,n)
is defined as:

\displaystyle s(k,n)=2^{L-q+1}(k-1)+n.

(3)

The ansatz (
2
) for the initial wavefunction guarantees that it has a form of tensor product
|\Phi_{\text{ACR}}(0)\rangle=|l_{q}\rangle\otimes|\Psi_{\text{res}}\rangle
, where
|l_{q}\rangle
is the wavefunction of the
q
-th site which has the form:

\displaystyle|l_{q}\rangle=\frac{|0_{q}\rangle+\alpha|1_{q}\rangle}{\sqrt{1+|\alpha|^{2}}},

(4)

|\Psi_{\text{res}}\rangle
describes the rest of the system, we refer to
|\Psi_{\text{res}}\rangle
as to a ’reservoir’. The parameter
\alpha
is a complex number which determines the position of
q
-th spin on a Bloch sphere as below:

\displaystyle\langle S^{x}_{q}\rangle=\frac{\text{Re}\alpha}{1+|\alpha|^{2}},\qquad\langle S^{y}_{q}\rangle=\frac{\text{Im}\alpha}{1+|\alpha|^{2}},\qquad\langle S^{z}_{q}\rangle=-\frac{1}{2}\cdot\frac{1-|\alpha|^{2}}{1+|\alpha|^{2}}.

Let us take a closer look at the expression (
2
). In general, a wavefunction in a form of tensor product
|l_{q}\rangle\otimes|\Psi_{\text{res}}\rangle
has
2^{L-1}
independent parameters
\mathcal{A}=\{\{A_{s(k,n)}\}^{2^{L-1}}_{n=1}\}^{2^{q-1}}_{k=1}
. Function (
3
) gives us pairs of basis vectors
|\varphi_{s(k,n)}\rangle
and
|\varphi_{s(k,n)+2^{L-q}}\rangle
such that these two vectors are identical for all but one
q
-th site. For example if we set
q=L
, then for
k=1
we will have
|\varphi_{s(1,1)}\rangle=|1_{1}\ 1_{2}\dots 1_{L}\rangle
and
|\varphi_{s(1,1)+1}\rangle=|1_{1}\ 1_{2}\dots 0_{L}\rangle
, taking all the
k=\overline{1,2^{q-1}}
we will end up with
2^{L-1}
basis vectors which are different only for the
L
-th site. Now to have a
q
-th spin in a pure state (
4
) we need to demand that
A_{s(k,n)+2^{L-q}}=\alpha A_{s(k,n)}
. If this condition is satisfied, then initial wavefunction always has a form of tensor product
|\Phi_{\text{ACR}}(0)\rangle=|l_{q}\rangle\otimes|\Psi_{\text{res}}\rangle
for any set of parameters
\mathcal{A}
. In Figure
2
(a) we schematically illustrate the ansatz (
2
).

The set of parameters
\mathcal{A}
defines the state of the reservoir
|\Psi_{\text{res}}\rangle
. If all the parameters are chosen randomly then the
q
-th spin will quickly entangle with the reservoir and will remain entangled virtually forever, same applies to all other spins. Our goal is to choose such set
\mathcal{A}
that at the specified ’’revival’’ time
\tau
wavefunction would split into a tensor product again
|\Phi_{\text{ACR}}(\tau)\rangle=|\bar{l}_{p}\rangle\otimes|\bar{\Psi}_{\text{res}}\rangle
.

Let us demand:

\displaystyle|\Phi_{\text{ACR}}(\tau)\rangle

\displaystyle=e^{-iH\tau}|\Phi_{\text{ACR}}(0)\rangle=

\displaystyle\sum\limits^{2^{p-1}}_{k=1}\sum\limits^{2^{L-p}}_{n=1}\left(C_{\bar{s}(k,n)}|\varphi_{\bar{s}(k,n)}\rangle+\beta C_{\bar{s}(k,n)}|\varphi_{\bar{s}(k,n)+2^{L-p}}\rangle\right),

(5)

this wavefunction has a form similar to (
2
), with the difference that now it is
p
-th spin in the pure state. The state
|\bar{l}_{p}\rangle
is parametrized by the complex number
\beta
similarly to (
4
):

\displaystyle|l_{q}\rangle=\frac{|0_{q}\rangle+\beta|1_{q}\rangle}{\sqrt{1+|\beta|^{2}}},

we also modified
s(k,n)
to
\bar{s}(k,n)=2^{L-p+1}(k-1)+n
, to have a revival on
p
-th site.

To find such set
\mathcal{A}
that (
2
) is satisfied we need to know the full form of the evolution operator at the revival moment
u\equiv e^{-iH\tau}
. Let us take a look at the full form of the condition (
2
):

\displaystyle u_{1,1}A_{1}+\cdots+u_{1,2^{L}}A_{2^{L}}=C_{1}

\displaystyle u_{2,1}A_{2}+\cdots+u_{2,2^{L}}A_{2^{L}}=C_{2}

(6)

\displaystyle u_{2^{L},1}A_{2}+\cdots+u_{2^{L},2^{L}}A_{2^{L}}=C_{2^{L}}.

By substituting
A_{s(k,n)+2^{L-q}}=\alpha A_{s(k,n)}
into (
2
), for
n=\overline{1,2^{L-q}}
and
k=\overline{1,2^{q-1}}
, we eliminate
2^{L-1}
variables from the system (
2
). We can also eliminate
2^{L-1}
equations from (
2
) by using the fact that
C_{\bar{s}(k,n)+2^{L-p}}=\alpha C_{\bar{s}(k,n)}
. Thus we obtain a set of conditions:

\displaystyle\hat{V}\mathcal{A}=0,

(7)

where the matrix
\hat{V}
is given by:

\displaystyle V_{ki}=u_{d[k],\bar{d}[i]}-\beta^{-1}u_{d[k]+2^{L-p},\bar{d}[i]}+\alpha u_{d[k],\bar{d}[i]+2^{L-q}}-\alpha\beta^{-1}u_{d[k]+2^{L-p},\bar{d}[k]+2^{L-q}},

(8)

where indexes
k,j=\overline{1,2^{L-1}}
, and sets of indexes
d
and
\bar{d}
are ordered sets:

\displaystyle d=\{\{s(k,n)\}^{2^{q-1}}_{k=1}\}^{2^{L-q}}_{n=1},

\displaystyle\bar{d}=\{\{\bar{s}(k,n)\}^{2^{p-1}}_{k=1}\}^{2^{L-p}}_{n=1}.

(9)

If matrix (
8
) is degenerate, then (
7
) has a solution and therefore (
2
) is satisfied exactly. However we argued in
ermakov2021almost
that in case of interacting non-integrable Hamiltonian (
8
) must always be non-degenerate. In this case the only solution of
\mathcal{A}=0
which is irrelevant. Let us allow one equation from (
7
) have non-zero right-hand side

\displaystyle\hat{V}\mathcal{A}=(\delta,0,\dots,0)^{T},

(10)

here
\delta
is a parameter which will be determined from normalization condition on (
4
).

Let us assume that typical matrix element of
v
have absolute value
|v_{ki}|\sim 1/\sqrt{2^{L}}
and a largely random phase. In this case we can estimate typical values of
\mathcal{A}
and
\mathcal{C}
as
|A_{0}|\sim 1/\sqrt{2^{L-1}}
and
|C_{0}|\sim 1/\sqrt{2^{L-1}}
. Now if we substitute these typical values into the left-hand side of the first equation in (
10
) we obtain
\delta\sim 1/\sqrt{2^{L-1}}
. This is a key assumption for the existence of ACR, we will discuss it further in the paper.

Let us formulate the procedure of ACR construction as step by step algorithm. In order to construct ACR one needs to:

1.

Pick a collapsing
q
and reviving
p
sites.

2.

Choose a position of collapsing
\vec{S}_{q}
and reviving
\vec{S}^{\prime}_{p}
spins on a Bloch Sphere. Determine corresponding parameters
\alpha
and
\beta
.

3.

Compute sets of indexes

and
\bar{d}
(
2
).

4.

Compute matrix (
8
).

5.

Solve set of equations (
10
). In practice one can set
\delta=1
to obtain a non-normalized solution.

6.

When set of parameters
\mathcal{A}
is determined, construct wavefunction (
2
) and normalize it.

and that
\vec{S}_{p}=\vec{S}^{\prime}_{q}=(0,0,1/2)

2.1
Example of ACR

Let us now construct ACR for the Hamiltonian:

\displaystyle H_{1}=

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{L}\left(g\sigma^{x}_{j}+h\sigma^{z}_{j}+J\sigma^{z}_{j}\sigma^{z}_{j+1}\right),

;

Consider the initial state

\alpha=i
therefore
\vec{S}_{1}=(0,1/2,0)

\tau=10
, and pick

and
\beta=-\sqrt{2/9}-1/3i
. By solving the system of equations (

spins we find such

that

, see Fig.
3

is close to

4
Discussion

We proposed a general scheme which allows one to construct a many-body state which is out of equilibrium on a level of local observables at the two moment of times: the initial
t=0
and the revival one
t=\tau
.

The key assumption which we made in the paper is that the unitary which drives the system is similar to a random rotation in a many-body Hilbert space. In particular we assumed that absolute values of
|u_{mn}|\sim 1/\sqrt{2^{L}}
are all of the same order and have random phases which are uncorrelated. We believe that this assumption holds for any Hamiltonian which satisfies ETH. We don’t have a rigorous proof of this assumption. Nevertheless if we assume that it is correct, then we can suggest a practically useful criteria of ACR reachability. Let us introduce the matrix participation ratio
MPR
as

\displaystyle\text{MPR}=\left(\sum\limits_{ij}|u_{ij}|^{2}\right)^{2}\Biggm/\sum\limits_{ij}|u_{ij}|^{4},

if for given unitary
u
the value of
\text{MPR}\rightarrow 1
, then the ACR is reachable.

The unitary
u
is not necessarily should describe quantum evolution or be associated with any physical Hamiltonian. The interesting direction is to study ACR for more experimentally relevant unitaries. It is particularly interesting to look at unitaries which can be implemented on existing quantum computers.

Remarks on QCD 4 with fundamental and adjoint matter
Mohamed M. Anber, Nakarin Lohitsiri, Tin Sulejmanpasic

Abstract
We study 4-dimensional SU ​ ( N ) \text{SU}(N) gauge theory with one adjoint Weyl fermion and fundamental matter – either bosonic or fermionic. Symmetries, their ’t Hooft anomalies, and the Vafa-Witten-Weingarten theorems strongly constrain the possible bulk phases. The first part of the paper is dedicated to a single fundamental fermion or boson. As long as the adjoint Weyl fermion is massless, this theory always possesses a ℤ 2 ​ N χ \mathbb{Z}_{2N}^{\chi} chiral symmetry, which breaks spontaneously, supporting N N vacua and domain walls between them for a generic mass of the matter fields. We argue, however, that the domain walls generically undergo a phase transition, and we establish the corresponding 3d gauge theories on the walls. The rest of the paper is dedicated to studying the multi-flavor fundamental matter. Here, the phases crucially depend on the ratio of the number of colors and the number of fundamental flavors. We also discuss the limiting scenarios of heavy adjoint and fundamentals, which align neatly with our current understanding of QCD and 𝒩 = 1 {\cal N}=1 super Yang-Mills theory.

Introduction

Quantum field theories (QFTs), fundamental or emergent, are important in modern physics. They are the cornerstone of the Standard Model of particles. In statistical mechanics, they are an efficient way to capture universal features of phase transitions. At the same time, in condensed matter, they are useful to parametrize the important, low-energy features of various materials.

Non-abelian gauge theories are the most infamous of QFTs. They describe Quantum Chromodynamics (QCD). Quarks, labeled by 3 colors, interact with non-abelian gauge fields to produce a theory that makes sense as a genuine quantum field theory (i.e., has a continuum limit) and, in addition, has highly nontrivial features in the infrared, such as chiral symmetry breaking and confinement which have yet to have a complete theoretical understanding.

This is why understanding QCD-like theories has a special place in theoretical physics. Most recently, a popular method of analyzing such theories was pioneered by the discovery of novel generalized global symmetries and ’t Hooft anomalies

2.4
Comments on the large-
N
scaling

We will here briefly comment on the large
N
scaling of the effective Goldstone theory (
31
). To take the large
N
limit, we normalize the UV Lagrangian in a standard way

\mathcal{L}=\frac{N}{\lambda_{t}}\left(\frac{1}{2}\tr{|F|^2}+i\tr\bar{\lambda}D\lambda+i\bar{\psi}D\psi+i\bar{\tilde{\psi}}D\tilde{\psi}\right)

(40)

where
\lambda_{t}
is the ’t Hooft coupling.
The current associated with the chiral symmetry is given by

j^{\mu}_{\chi}\propto N\tr\bar{\lambda}\bar{\sigma}^{\mu}\lambda-N^{2}\bar{\psi}\bar{\sigma}^{\mu}\psi-N^{2}\bar{\tilde{\psi}}\sigma^{\mu}\tilde{\psi}

(41)

where the extra factor of
N
in the last two terms are because the fields
\psi
and
\tilde{\psi}
are charged with charge
N
under the
\text{U}(1)_{\chi}
. The current current correlator then has three pieces, which are schematically given by

\left\langle j^{\mu}j^{\nu}\right\rangle\propto AN^{4}\left\langle J^{\mu}_{\psi}J^{\nu}_{\psi}\right\rangle+BN^{3}\left\langle J^{\mu}_{\psi}J^{\nu}_{\lambda}\right\rangle+CN^{2}\left\langle J^{\mu}_{\lambda}J^{\nu}_{\lambda}\right\rangle\;.

(42)

where
J^{\mu}_{\lambda}=\tr\bar{\lambda}\bar{\sigma}^{\mu}\lambda
,
J^{\mu}_{\psi}=\bar{\psi}\bar{\sigma}^{\mu}\psi+\bar{\tilde{\psi}}\bar{\sigma}^{\mu}\tilde{\psi}
and
A,B,C
are some order one coefficients. We have that
\left\langle J^{\mu}_{\psi}J^{\nu}_{\psi}\right\rangle,\left\langle J^{\mu}_{\psi}J^{\nu}_{\lambda}\right\rangle\sim 1/N
,
\left\langle J^{\mu}_{\lambda}J^{\nu}_{\lambda}\right\rangle\sim 1
. So despite the correlator in the first term above being suppressed by
1/N
, the enhancing factor of
N^{4}
causes the first term to dominate, so

\left\langle j^{\mu}j^{\nu}\right\rangle\sim N^{3}\;.

(43)

To reproduce this, we have to take that in the large
N
limit the coefficient of the kinetic term
(\partial_{\mu}\varphi)^{2}
of the effective Goldstone theory needs to scale as
N^{3}
.

Let us give another, more heuristic, way to understand this result. Namely if we note that there are two fermion-bilinear order parameters of
\text{U}(1)_{\chi}
symmetry breaking
\tr\lambda\lambda\sim e^{i\varphi}=U_{\lambda}
and
\psi\tilde{\psi}\sim e^{iN\varphi}=U_{\psi}
, we then expect that the effective Lagrangian is given by

N^{2}|\partial_{\mu}U_{\lambda}|^{2}+N|\partial_{\mu}U_{\psi}|^{2}\;.

(44)

In other words, the order parameter
U_{\lambda}
will have an a priori dominant kinetic terms, while the
U_{\psi}
is expected to be
1/N
suppressed because it couples to fundamental matter. The second term however is not sub-leading and is of order
N^{3}
because the order parameter
U_{\psi}
carries charge
N
, i.e.
|\partial_{\mu}U_{\psi}|^{2}=N^{2}(\partial_{\mu}\varphi)^{2}
. So the second term above is dominant and is given by
\sim N^{3}(\partial_{\mu}\varphi)^{2}
in the large
N
limit.

It is perhaps not surprising that the fundamental contribution is important even at leading order, because the
\text{U}(1)_{\chi}
symmetry crucially depends on the presence of massless fundamentals. Indeed the way that importance shows up in the large
N
limit is through the dominance of the charge of the fundamentals under the
\text{U}(1)_{\chi}
.

Now if one inserts the mass terms for the adjoint fields and the fundamental fields, we expect them to scale as
N^{2}
and
N
respectively, i.e. the effective theory schematically becomes

\mathcal{L}\propto N^{3}(\partial_{\mu}\varphi)^{2}-m_{adj}\Lambda N^{2}\cos\varphi-m\Lambda N\cos(N\varphi)\;.

(45)

Notice that if we set the fundamental mass to zero, i.e.
m=0
, then the pseudo-Goldstone has a mass
1/N
allowing an analysis to be carried out for large
N
at any
m_{adj}
. This is exactly what one expects when the adjoint fermion decouples. On the other hand setting
m_{adj}=0
, the pseudo-Goldstone mode gets an order one mass in the large
N
limit, and the analysis breaks down when
m
is of order
\Lambda
–the strong scale.

This section explains the origin behind the mixed anomalies discussed in the previous sections. In doing so, we give the details that link the above derivations to the CFU anomaly computations of

The theory possesses the following traditional ’t Hooft anomalies:

\begin{split}\left[\text{SU}(N_{f})_{L}\right]^{3}=-\left[\text{SU}(N_{f})_{R}\right]^{3}:&\quad\mathcal{C}_{L^{3}}=-\quad\mathcal{C}_{R^{3}}=N\,,\\
\left[\text{U}(1)_{\chi}\right]^{3}:&\quad\mathcal{C}_{A^{3}}=\frac{-2N^{4}N_{f}+N_{f}^{3}(N^{2}-1)}{\text{gcd}(N,N_{f})^{3}}\,,\\
\left[\text{U}(1)_{\chi}\right]\left[\text{SU}(N_{f})_{L}\right]^{2}=\left[\text{U}(1)_{\chi}\right]\left[\text{SU}(N_{f})_{R}\right]^{2}:&\quad\mathcal{C}_{AL^{2}}=\mathcal{C}_{AR^{2}}=-\frac{N^{2}}{\text{gcd}(N,N_{f})}\,,\\

\left[\text{U}(1)_{\chi}\right]\left[\mbox{gravity}\right]:&\quad\mathcal{C}_{A\text{-grav}}=-\frac{N_{f}\left(1+N^{2}\right)}{\text{gcd}(N,N_{f})}\,.\end{split}

(64)

More constraining anomalies can be found by utilizing the faithful global group. To find the latter, one first needs to determine a subgroup in the center of

G=\text{SU}(N)\times\text{SU}(N_{f})_{L}\times\text{SU}(N_{f})_{R}\times\text{U}(1)_{q}\times\text{U}(1)_{\chi}

(65)

that acts trivially on all the fields. Consider the transformation

\left(e^{\frac{2\pi in_{c}}{N}}\textbf{1}_{N},e^{\frac{2\pi in_{L}}{N_{f}}}\textbf{1}_{N_{f}},e^{\frac{2\pi in_{R}}{N_{f}}}\textbf{1}_{N_{f}},e^{2\pi i\beta},e^{2\pi i\alpha}\right)\in G

The condition that it acts trivially on all the fields
\psi
,

\tilde{\psi}
, and
\lambda
are :

\begin{split}\psi\;:&\quad\underbrace{e^{\frac{i2\pi n_{c}}{N}}}_{\text{SU}(N)}\underbrace{e^{\frac{i2\pi n_{L}}{N_{f}}}}_{\text{SU}(N_{f})_{L}}\underbrace{e^{i2\pi\beta}}_{\text{U}(1)_{B}}\underbrace{e^{-i2\pi\alpha N}}_{\text{U}(1)_{\chi}}=1\,,\\
\tilde{\psi}\;:&\quad\underbrace{e^{\frac{-i2\pi n_{c}}{N}}}_{\text{SU}(N)}\underbrace{e^{\frac{-i2\pi n_{R}}{N_{f}}}}_{\text{SU}(N_{f})_{R}}\underbrace{e^{-i2\pi\beta}}_{\text{U}(1)_{B}}\underbrace{e^{-i2\pi\alpha N}}_{\text{U}(1)_{\chi}}=1\,,\\
\lambda\;:&\quad\underbrace{e^{i2\pi\alpha N_{f}}}_{\text{U}(1)_{\chi}}=1\,,\end{split}

(66)

where
n_{c}
are integers
\mbox{mod}\,N
,
n_{L,R}
are integers

\mbox{mod}\,N_{f}
, and
\alpha,\beta
are
\text{U}(1)
phases. The conditions (
66
) ensure that the transition functions of the gauge and global symmetry bundles satisfy the cocycle conditions. The set of solutions
to these conditions forms a subgroup of
G
that must be quotiented
out from
G
to determine the faithful global symmetry group. We find
that this subgroup is
\mathbb{Z}_{N}\times\mathbb{Z}_{N_{f}}\times\mathbb{Z}_{Q}
, where
Q=\frac{N_{f}}{\gcd(N,N_{f})}
is the charge of
\lambda
under the global
\text{U}(1)_{\chi}

\begin{split}&\mathbb{Z}_{N}\;\;:\left(e^{\frac{2\pi i}{N}}\textbf{1}_{N},\textbf{1}_{N_{f}},\textbf{1}_{N_{f}},e^{-\frac{2\pi i}{N}},1\right),\\
&\mathbb{Z}_{N_{f}}\;:\left(\textbf{1}_{N},e^{\frac{2\pi i}{N_{f}}}\textbf{1}_{N_{f}},e^{\frac{2\pi i}{N_{f}}}\textbf{1}_{N_{f}},e^{-\frac{2\pi i}{N_{f}}},1\right),\\
&\mathbb{Z}_{Q}\;\;:\left(\textbf{1}_{N},e^{\frac{2\pi iN}{N_{f}}}\textbf{1}_{N_{f}},e^{-\frac{2\pi iN}{N_{f}}}\textbf{1}_{N_{f}},1,e^{\frac{2\pi i}{Q}}\right),\qquad Q=\frac{N_{f}}{\gcd(N,N_{f})}\end{split}

(67)

Thus, the faithful global symmetry of the theory is

\displaystyle G^{\scriptsize\mbox{Global}}=\frac{\text{SU}(N_{f})_{L}\times\text{SU}(N_{f})_{R}\times\text{U}(1)_{q}\times\text{U}(1)_{\chi}}{\mathbb{Z}_{N}\times\mathbb{Z}_{N_{f}}\times\mathbb{Z}_{Q}}\,.

(68)

To fully study the anomalies we note that the symmetries allow us to define a

-

structure on our manifold and not just a
Spin

that acts identically to

\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{F}\subset\text{Spin}(4)

(69)

acts identically to
(-1)^{F}
if the following conditions are
satisfied:

\begin{split}\psi\;:&\quad e^{\frac{2\pi in_{L}}{N_{f}}}e^{i\beta}e^{-iq\alpha}=-1\,,\\
\tilde{\psi}\;:&\quad e^{\frac{-2\pi in_{R}}{N_{f}}}e^{-i\beta}e^{-iq\alpha}=-1\,,\\
\lambda\;:&\quad e^{iQ\alpha}=-1\,.\end{split}

(70)

When both

is odd and

as a

-

\text{SU}(N_{f})_{R}

\mathbb{CP}^{2}

Again, since the quotient

4.2
The IR phases

In the infinite mass limit, we can again integrate out the Dirac
fermions, leaving us with
\mathcal{N}=1
SYM with
gauge group
\text{SU}(N)
. As discussed in §
2.3
, there are
N
degenerate
vacua and domain walls connecting them.

At the massless point, the
\mathbb{Z}_{2N}^{\chi}
discrete chiral symmetry enhances
to
\text{U}(1)_{\chi}
. All anomalies are now given in terms of the anomaly
polynomial. Saturating the anomalies can be achieved in the IR in one
of three ways:

1.

Composite massless fermions charged under the global symmetry.

2.

Spontaneous symmetry breaking (SSB).

3.

Interacting conformal field theory.

The first choice can be ruled out by Weingarten’s theorem

Weingarten1983
because the measure of the theory is positive
definite (see Appendix
A
). Let’s
now discuss the second option, where anomalies are saturated by
breaking the global symmetries

\text{SU}(N_{f})_{L}\times\text{SU}(N_{f})_{R}\times\text{U}(1)_{\chi}\times\text{U}(1)_{B}
down to

\text{SU}(N_{f})_{V}\times\text{U}(1)_{B}
, giving rise to
N_{f}^{2}-1
Goldstones and a

\text{U}(1)
vortex. The condensate
\tilde{\psi}\psi
is charged under the full

\text{SU}(N_{f})_{L}\times\text{SU}(N_{f})_{R}\times\text{U}(1)_{\chi}
, and breaks the group down to

\text{SU}(N_{f})_{V}\times\mathbb{Z}_{2q}
. The
\mathbb{Z}_{2q}
phase is the
unbroken subgroup of
\text{U}(1)_{\chi}
under
\tilde{\psi}\psi
. However, the unbroken
phase is anomalous, as can be checked using

\left[\text{U}(1)_{\chi}\right][\mbox{gravity}]
and the CFU anomalies given by
(
84
) by setting
\alpha=\frac{2\pi}{2q}
. To avoid
this problem, another condensate has to form in order to break
\text{U}(1)_{\chi}

to a non-anomalous group. The minimal choice is
\lambda\lambda
, which transforms
under
\text{U}(1)_{\chi}
as:

\lambda\lambda\xrightarrow{\text{U}(1)_{\chi}}e^{-i2Q\alpha}\lambda\lambda
, and thus, the formation of

\lambda\lambda
breaks
\text{U}(1)_{\chi}
down to
\mathbb{Z}_{2Q}
. The combined condensates

\lambda\lambda
and
\tilde{\psi}\psi
break
\text{U}(1)_{\chi}
down to

\mathbb{Z}_{2\text{gcd}(q,Q)}=\mathbb{Z}_{2}
. it is easy to see that this

\mathbb{Z}_{2}
has no mixed anomaly with
\text{SU}(N_{f})_{V}
nor
\text{U}(1)_{B}
by setting

\alpha=\pi
and
k_{L}=k_{R}
in (

). Moreover, since

\Omega^{\text{Spin}}_{5}(B\mathbb{Z}_{2})=0

, there are no global
anomalies in
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
itself. So for sufficiently small number or flavors
N_{f}<N_{f}^{*}
we expect that the theory flows to a Goldstone phase corresponding to
N_{f}
Goldstone bosons.

Another scenario is that the theory flows to a phase that
preserves all the global symmetries, e.g., a conformal window. One may wonder which scenario is
preferred. The answer to this question comes from comparing the number of
effective massless degrees of freedom (DOF) between the UV theory and
the SSB scenario

. The, effective degrees of
freedom
\mathcal{A}
of
n_{B}
massless real scalars and
n_{f}

massless Weyl fermions are given in terms of the free energy density

, where
\Lambda
is the strong
scale)

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}=\frac{90}{\pi^{2}T^{4}}F=n_{B}+\frac{7n_{f}}{4}\,.

(90)

The effective degrees of freedom defined via the free energy is not guaranteed to decrease
along RG flows. Yet, as we shall see, the outcomes of this method are consistent with the findings via renormalization group analysis. It is expected that the phase with lower free energy, i.e., a smaller
number of DOF, is preferred. Define the difference between the number
of DOF in the UV and the SSB scenario by

\begin{split}\Delta{\cal A}:={\cal A}^{\scriptsize\mbox{Goldstones}}-{\cal A}^{\scriptsize\mbox{UV}}&=(N_{f}^{2}-1)-\left[\underbrace{2(N^{2}-1)}_{\text{gluons DOF}}+\frac{7}{4}\left(N^{2}-1+2NN_{f}\right)\right]\\
&=-\frac{7NN_{f}}{2}-\frac{15}{4}N^{2}+N_{f}^{2}+\frac{11}{4}\,.\end{split}

(91)

6
Conclusions and future prospects

In this work, we analyzed the
\text{SU}(N)
gauge theory with one massless adjoint fermion and massive matter, either bosonic or fermionic. The special case of one fundamental flavor of varying mass was analyzed in detail. The abundance of ’t Hooft anomalies involving discrete chiral symmetry
\mathbb{Z}_{2N}^{\chi}
, combined with Vafa-Witten-Weingarten theorems, restricts the bulk phase quite strongly, which results in spontaneously broken discrete chiral symmetry for any mass of the fundamental fermion or boson, leaving
N
degenerate vacua in the bulk. Further, the bosonic theory is related to fermionic theory in the sense that when the boson condenses, the theory is higgsed down to
\text{SU}(N-1)
with one fundamental fermion. So most conclusions about the theory with fundamental bosons can be drawn by studying the fermionic theory.

The massless fundamental fermion limit is particularly interesting, where the discrete chiral symmetry enhances to
\text{U}(1)_{\chi}
, and the domain walls melt into Goldstone bosons. Studying the small fundamental mass regime can be done systematically by perturbing the Goldstone theory. In particular, we studied the domain walls between the
N
vacua and, with the exception of the one
T
-preserving domain wall, found them all to be trivial. On the other hand, in the opposite limit of infinite fundamental fermion mass, the theory becomes Super Yang-Mills, of which many things are known. The domain walls of Super Yang-Mills are conjectured to hold a TQFT. As the fundamental fermion mass is dialed from small to large, no bulk phase transition occurs, but our analysis shows that a transition must occur on the domain wall. Further, we conjectured that the domain wall theory is IR dual to the corresponding
\text{SU}(N)
gauge theory with both fundamental and adjoint matter in 3d. Of particular interest is the
T
-preserving domain wall, which exists if
N
is even. This domain wall cannot be made trivial as it carries a
\text{U}(1)_{B}-T
mixed anomaly. The corresponding 3d theories were studied in
Lohitsiri:2022jyz
, where it was proposed that anomalies can be saturated either by composite fermions or spontaneous
T
-breaking, with large
N
arguments favoring the latter. The 4d domain wall analysis indicates that
T
breaking is preferred, at least for a small enough mass of the fundamentals. We, however, speculate that there may be a composite fermion phase on the domain wall for some intermediate mass of the fundamentals for
N=2
. We also discuss the decoupling limit of adjoints which results in the usual QCD.

We generalized the one fundamental flavor case to multi-flavors and discussed the bounds on the conformal window from the a-theorem and the Banks-Zacks 2-loop fixed point. For a sufficiently low number of flavors, we analyze the chiral Lagrangian and map out the phase diagram in the bulk and on the domain wall. As with one flavor, we discuss the decoupling limit of adjoints.

We end this section by discussing some future prospects. The conjectures about the bulk phase analyticity can be tested on the lattice. More interesting would be to study domain walls on the lattice, or the corresponding 3d gauge theories. The lattice studies of domain walls would require using twisted boundary conditions or spatially varying
\theta
-term, which generically introduce a complex action problem that hinders numerical simulations. Studies of corresponding 3d theories directly also generically require bare Chern-Simons terms and complex fermionic measures, which again hinders lattice studies. Another interesting approach is to study soft SUSY deformations of super QCD setup, and see carefully what happens on the domain walls as supersymmetry is broken.

\int d^{4}x(\psi_{n}^{i})^{T}C\psi_{m}^{j}=0\;\quad\text{ if $n\neq m$}\;.

(146)

On the other hand if
n=m
then we see that the expression
\int d^{4}x(\psi_{n}^{i})^{T}C\psi_{n}^{j}
is anti-symmetric in
i
and
j
. We use a natural normalization

\int d^{4}x(\psi_{n}^{i})^{T}C\psi_{n}^{j}=\epsilon^{ij}\;.

(147)

Then the action becomes

S=\sum_{n}\lambda_{n}\alpha_{n}^{i}\alpha_{n}^{j}\epsilon_{ij}\;.

(148)

Now we define the measure to be

\prod_{n}(id\alpha_{n}^{1}d\alpha_{n}^{2})\;,

(149)

so that the weight is

\prod_{n}(i\lambda_{n})=\prod_{n|\lambda_{n}>0}\lambda_{n}^{2}\;.

(150)

where the product over
\lambda_{n}
is only over one of the Kramers doublet eigenvalue. The above is manifestly positive.

Appendix B
Spectral flow

Consider a first-order differential operator

D=\mathbb{I}\partial_{\tau}+A(\tau)\;.

(151)

where
A(\tau)
and
\mathbb{I}
are an
N\times N
Hermitian and identity matrices respectively. We want to look for the zero modes of the above operator. We solve the differential equation

D\psi=0\;.

(152)

Now let us decompose
\psi=\sum_{n=1}^{N}c_{n}(\tau)\chi_{n}(\tau)
into the instantaneous eigenvectors
\chi_{n}(\tau)
of
A(\tau)
, i.e.

A(\tau)\chi_{n}(\tau)=\lambda_{n}(\tau)\chi_{n}(\tau)\;.

(153)

We have that the zero-mode equation becomes equivalent to

\partial_{\tau}c_{n}+\lambda_{n}c_{n}+\sum_{m=1}^{N}M_{nm}c_{m}=0

(154)

where the matrix
M_{nm}
is an anti-Hermitian matrix given by

M_{nm}=\chi^{\dagger}_{m}\partial_{\tau}\chi_{n}\;.

(155)

Let us put the coefficients
c_{n}
into a complex vector
\bm{c}
, and
\lambda_{n}
into a diagonal matrix
A_{D}
. Then we have that

\bm{c}(\tau)=Pe^{-\int_{0}^{\tau}d\tilde{\tau}(A_{D}(\tilde{\tau})+M(\tilde{\tau}))}\bm{c}(0)\;.

(156)

Now let us discuss this solution in the adiabatic approximation limit. Namely if we differentiate the equation (
153
), ignoring the derivative of
A
and the derivative of
\lambda_{n}
as small, we have that (sum over
m
implied)

\lambda_{m}M_{mn}-M_{mn}\lambda_{n}=0\Leftrightarrow\Lambda M-MA_{D}=0\;,

(157)

so
M_{mn}
, commuting with
A_{D}
must be diagonal unless
A_{D}
has exact degeneracies. Let us assume that this is the case. Then the equation (
156
) implies that if we start with
c_{n}(0)
not equal to zero for only some
n
and zero for others, it will stay that way. The diagonal matrix
M
is just the Barry phase of individual eigenstates. Now notice that only
c_{n}(\tau)
for which
\lambda_{n}
is positive for
\tau\rightarrow\infty
and negative for
\tau\rightarrow-\infty
can be kept if we want normalizable
\psi(\tau)
. Hence we conclude that the operator
D
has as many zero modes as the net spectral flow. If operator
A(\tau)
still has some degeneracies, the story is similar because we can always diagonalize
M
in the subspace of the degeneracies without affecting the discussion.

Appendix C
\beta
-function

The 3-loop
\beta
function for
n_{R}
Weyl fermions in representation
R
of
\text{SU}(N)
Yang-Mills theory is given by (see
Dietrich:2006cm
;
Zoller:2016sgq
)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\beta(g)=&-\beta_{0}\frac{g^{3}}{(4\pi)^{2}}-\beta_{1}\frac{g^{5}}{(4\pi)^{4}}-\beta_{2}\frac{g^{7}}{(4\pi)^{6}}~,\\[3.0pt]
\beta_{0}=&\frac{11}{6}C_{2}(G)-\sum_{R}\frac{1}{3}T_{R}n_{R}~,\\[3.0pt]
\beta_{1}=&\frac{34}{12}C_{2}^{2}(G)-\sum_{{R}}n_{R}\left\{\frac{5}{6}C_{2}(G)T_{R}+\frac{1}{2}C_{2}({R})T_{R}\right\}~,\\[3.0pt]
\beta_{2}=&\frac{2857}{432}C_{2}^{3}(G)-\sum_{{R}}\frac{n_{R}T_{R}}{4}\left[-\frac{C_{2}^{2}({R})}{2}+\frac{205C_{2}(G)C_{2}({R})}{36}+\frac{1415C_{2}^{2}(G)}{108}\right]\\[3.0pt]
&+\quad\sum_{{R},{R}^{\prime}}\frac{n_{R}n_{R^{\prime}}T_{R}T_{R^{\prime}}}{16}\left[\frac{44C_{2}({R})}{18}+\frac{158C_{2}(G)}{54}\right]~,\end{split}

(158)

where
G
denotes the adjoint representation.
The quadratic Casimir of representation
R
,
C_{2}({R})
, is

\displaystyle t^{a}_{R}t^{a}_{R}=C_{2}({R})\mathbf{1}_{R}~,

(159)

and
C_{2}(G)
is the quadratic Casimir of the adjoint representation.
T_{R}
is the Dynkin index, which is defined via

\displaystyle\mbox{tr}\left[t^{a}_{R}t^{b}_{R}\right]=T_{R}\delta^{ab}~.

(160)

From Eqs. (
159
) and (
160
), we obtain the relation

\displaystyle T_{R}\mbox{dim}_{G}=C_{2}({R})\mbox{dim}_{R}~,

(161)

where
\mbox{dim}_{R}
is the dimension of
R
. In particular, using the convention
T_{R}=1
for the fundamental representation
R={\tiny\hbox{}\hskip 0.0pt{\vbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.3pt\hbox{\vrule height=4.35048pt,width=0.3pt,depth=1.0876pt\hbox to5.4381pt{\hfil}\vrule height=4.35048pt,width=0.3pt,depth=1.0876pt}\hrule height=0.3pt}\vskip-0.3pt}}\hskip 0.0pt}
, we have
C_{2}(G)=2N
,
\mbox{dim}_{G}=N^{2}-1
.

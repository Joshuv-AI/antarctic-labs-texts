Radiative heat transfer with a cylindrical waveguide decays logarithmically slow
Kiryl Asheichyk, Matthias Krüger

Abstract
Radiative heat transfer between two far-field-separated nanoparticles placed close to a perfectly conducting nanowire decays logarithmically slow with the interparticle distance. This makes a cylinder an excellent waveguide which can transfer thermal electromagnetic energy to arbitrary large distances with almost no loss. It leads to a dramatic increase of the heat transfer, so that, for almost any (large) separation, the transferred energy can be as large as for isolated particles separated by a few hundred nanometers. A phenomenologically found analytical formula accurately describes the numerical results over a wide range of parameters.

I.2.2
Analytical approximation

General remarks

Assuming that the slow decay in Fig.
S1
has a logarithmic form, we found that
\operatorname{Tr}\left(\mathbb{G}\mathbb{G}^{\dagger}\right)
can be approximated by Eq. (
4
) in the main text. We note that, for any GF, the trace can be decomposed into three contributions
Messina et al. 2018
:

\operatorname{Tr}\big(\mathbb{G}\mathbb{G}^{\dagger}\big)=\operatorname{Tr}\big(\mathbb{G}_{0}\mathbb{G}_{0}^{\dagger}\big)+\operatorname{Tr}\big(\mathbb{G}_{\mathbb{T}}\mathbb{G}_{\mathbb{T}}^{\dagger}\big)+2\operatorname{Re}\big[\operatorname{Tr}\big(\mathbb{G}_{0}\mathbb{G}_{\mathbb{T}}^{\dagger}\big)\big].

(S6)

In Eq. (
4
) in the main text, the first (power-law) term is the exact result for
\operatorname{Tr}\big(\mathbb{G}_{0}\mathbb{G}_{0}^{\dagger}\big)
. The second (logarithmic) term is the far-field approximation for
\operatorname{Tr}\big(\mathbb{G}_{\mathbb{T}}\mathbb{G}_{\mathbb{T}}^{\dagger}\big)
. The contribution of
2\operatorname{Re}\big[\operatorname{Tr}\big(\mathbb{G}_{0}\mathbb{G}_{\mathbb{T}}^{\dagger}\big)\big]
is missing in Eq. (
4
) in the main text, such that the full trace is larger than the vacuum part, leading to the corresponding property of the approximated HT. This is, however, not true in general for exact values of
\operatorname{Tr}\left(\mathbb{G}\mathbb{G}^{\dagger}\right)
and HT, as can be seen from the figures. Note that the near-field contribution of
\operatorname{Tr}\big(\mathbb{G}_{\mathbb{T}}\mathbb{G}_{\mathbb{T}}^{\dagger}\big)
is absent in Eq. (
4
) in the main text, such that the equation is expected to work worse for near-field
d
, especially when one considers low frequencies (which is equivalent to using small
T_{1}
in the HT). Indeed, we observed that formula (
5
) in the main text approximates the HT worse if
T_{1}
decreases. However, the approximation is still good even for
T_{1}
which is
100
times smaller than room temperature.

Overall, Eq. (
4
) in the main text is a good approximation for the trace, as Figs.
S1
,
S2
,
S3
, and
S4
show. Although strong deviations from numerical data appear for near-field interparticle distances, especially regarding
R
and
h
dependence (see top panels of Figs.
S3
and
S4
), the relative discrepancy is not large. For
d\gtrsim\lambda
, formula (
4
) in the main text approximates
\operatorname{Tr}\left(\mathbb{G}\mathbb{G}^{\dagger}\right)
very good, especially within certain ranges of the parameters, as discussed below.

Three cases for the far field

The condition
d\gtrsim\lambda
allows to simplify and analyze Eq. (
4
) in the main text. Rewriting the formula in terms of the wavelength,

\operatorname{Tr}\left(\mathbb{G}\mathbb{G}^{\dagger}\right)\approx\frac{1}{8\pi^{2}d^{2}}\left[1+\frac{\lambda^{2}}{4\pi^{2}d^{2}}+\frac{3\lambda^{4}}{16\pi^{4}d^{4}}\right]+\frac{\lambda^{2}}{16\pi^{4}(R+h)^{4}\ln^{2}\left[1+\frac{\lambda\sqrt{d^{2}+4h^{2}}}{\sqrt{2}\pi R^{2}}\right]},

(S7)

and taking the far-field limit (
d\gtrsim\lambda
), we get

\operatorname{Tr}\left(\mathbb{G}\mathbb{G}^{\dagger}\right)\approx\frac{1}{8\pi^{2}d^{2}}+\frac{\lambda^{2}}{16\pi^{4}(R+h)^{4}\ln^{2}\left[1+\frac{\lambda\sqrt{d^{2}+4h^{2}}}{\sqrt{2}\pi R^{2}}\right]}.

(S8)

On the other hand, for the far-field-separated particles above a plate, the trace can be approximated as

\operatorname{Tr}\big(\mathbb{G}_{\textrm{pl}}\mathbb{G}_{\textrm{pl}}^{\dagger}\big)\approx\frac{1}{8\pi^{2}d^{2}}+\frac{1}{8\pi^{2}\left(d^{2}+4h^{2}\right)},

(S9)

where, as in the cylinder approximation (
S8
), the first term is the vacuum part, while the second term is the approximation for the scattering part.

It is insightful to introduce three cases, based on Eqs. (
S8
) and (
S9
), and on the plots. The first case is when
\operatorname{Tr}\left(\mathbb{G}\mathbb{G}^{\dagger}\right)\gg 2\operatorname{Tr}\big(\mathbb{G}_{0}\mathbb{G}_{0}^{\dagger}\big)\geq\operatorname{Tr}\big(\mathbb{G}_{\textrm{pl}}\mathbb{G}_{\textrm{pl}}^{\dagger}\big)
, such that the logarithmic term in Eq. (
S8
) dominates. This corresponds to a large enhancement of the trace compared to isolated particles and to a slow stable decay with
d
, observed for large
d
, small
R
, and small
h
(see Figs.
S1
,
S3
, and
S4
), which can be regarded as the features of a cylindrical waveguide. Therefore, we call this case
“cylinder case”
. A more precise condition for this situation can be written as

\ln\left[1+\frac{\lambda\sqrt{d^{2}+4h^{2}}}{\sqrt{2}\pi R^{2}}\right]\ll\frac{\lambda d}{2\pi\left(R+h\right)^{2}}.

(S10)

For an important scenario where
h\ll\lambda
, considering typical
\lambda
(around
\lambda_{T_{1}}
) and physical
R
, Eq. (
S10
) can be approximately simplified to

\lambda d\gg 16R^{2}.

(S11)

From Eq. (
S11
), it follows that, for a fixed
R
, the cylinder case is given when
d\gtrsim d_{\textrm{c}}
, where

d_{\textrm{c}}=\frac{160R^{2}}{\lambda}.

(S12)

This quantity is marked on horizontal axes of Fig.
S2
(b). Notably, approximation (
4
) in the main text works very good when
d\gtrsim d_{\textrm{c}}
, but not as good for smaller
d
. Overall, the agreement is expected to be better when
d\notin[d_{\textrm{p}},d_{\textrm{c}}]
, where
d_{\textrm{p}}
is given in Eq. (
S16
), than in case
d\in[d_{\textrm{p}},d_{\textrm{c}}]
. Similarly, for a fixed
d
, we have the cylinder case if
R\lesssim R_{\textrm{c}}
, where

R_{\textrm{c}}=\frac{\sqrt{\lambda d}}{4\sqrt{10}},

(S13)

marked on horizontal axes of Fig.
S3
(bottom plots). For
R\lesssim R_{\textrm{c}}
, the trace is much larger than the plate trace, and analytical curves are in an excellent agreement with numerical data.

For large
R
, a cylinder should give the same effect as a plate does. Indeed, taking the limit
R\to\infty
in Eq. (
S8
) leads to the plate trace in Eq. (
S9

“plate case”

\ln\left[1+\frac{\lambda\sqrt{d^{2}+4h^{2}}}{\sqrt{2}\pi R^{2}}\right]\approx\frac{\lambda\sqrt{d^{2}+4h^{2}}}{\sqrt{2}\pi R^{2}},\ \ \ \ R\gg h.

(S14)

Note that equality
\operatorname{Tr}\left(\mathbb{G}\mathbb{G}^{\dagger}\right)\approx\operatorname{Tr}\big(\mathbb{G}_{\textrm{pl}}\mathbb{G}_{\textrm{pl}}^{\dagger}\big)
is necessary but not sufficient: It can be achieved also for small
R

R\gg h
is required in Eq. (
S14
). If, furthermore,
h\ll\lambda
, Eq. (
S14

\lambda d\ll 4R^{2},

(S15)

which, for a given
R
, works if
d\lesssim d_{\textrm{p}}
, where

d_{\textrm{p}}=\frac{2R^{2}}{5\lambda}.

(S16)

For a fixed
d
, the plate case corresponds to
R\gtrsim R_{\textrm{p}}
, where

R_{\textrm{p}}=\frac{\sqrt{10}}{2}\sqrt{\lambda d},

(S17)

marked on horizontal axes of Fig.
S3
(bottom plots). One can see that, for
R\gtrsim R_{\textrm{p}}
, the cylinder curves bend over the plate results, and the agreement with the analytical approximation becomes better. Note that, overall, the agreement is better when
R\notin[R_{\textrm{c}},R_{\textrm{p}}]
.

The third case is the vacuum case, where the logarithmic term in (
S8
) is much smaller than the quadratic one, or equivalently,

\ln\left[1+\frac{\lambda\sqrt{d^{2}+4h^{2}}}{\sqrt{2}\pi R^{2}}\right]\gg\frac{\lambda d}{\sqrt{2}\pi\left(R+h\right)^{2}}.

(S18)

It is achieved at large

Figure

\operatorname{Tr}\left(\mathbb{G}\mathbb{G}^{\dagger}\right)

On the other hand, for far-field distance with cylinder

\operatorname{Tr}\left(\mathbb{G}\mathbb{G}^{\dagger}\right)\approx\frac{\lambda^{2}}{16\pi^{4}(R+h)^{4}\ln^{2}\left[1+\frac{\lambda d}{\sqrt{2}\pi R^{2}}\right]}.

\frac{3}{8\pi^{2}}\ln^{2}\left[1+\frac{\lambda d}{\sqrt{2}\pi R^{2}}\right]\lambda^{2}(R+h)^{4}\lesssim d_{\textrm{nf}}^{6}.

(S21)

In the cylinder case,

\lambda^{2}(R+h)^{4}\ll d_{\textrm{nf}}^{6}.

(S22)

Importantly, if Eq. (

I.4
Comparison between the amounts of the transferred and radiated energy

The energy transferred from SiC PP 1 at
T_{1}=300\ \textrm{K}
to SiC PP 2 in the presence of a cylinder can be approximated by Eq. (
6
) in the main text. On the other hand, the total energy emitted by PP 1 (taking into account the presence of a cylinder and PP 2) is given by
Asheichyk et al. 2017

H_{\textrm{total}}=\frac{8\hbar}{c^{2}}\int_{0}^{\infty}d\omega\frac{\omega^{3}}{e^{\frac{\hbar\omega}{k_{\textrm{B}}T_{1}}}-1}\operatorname{Im}(\alpha_{1})\operatorname{Tr}\operatorname{Im}\mathbb{G},

(S23)

where
\mathbb{G}
is the GF of a cylinder and PP 2. We consider
d\gtrsim\lambda_{0}
; it can be shown that, in this case, the contribution of PP 2 to
\operatorname{Tr}\operatorname{Im}\mathbb{G}
, and hence to
H_{\textrm{total}}
, is negligible. Therefore, we consider that
\mathbb{G}
is the GF of a cylinder (i.e., without PP 2).

Similarly to the HT, the spectrum of
H_{\textrm{total}}
is strongly peaked at
\omega_{0}
, allowing us to approximate Eq. (
S23
) as

H_{\textrm{total}}\approx\frac{8\hbar}{c^{2}}\left[\operatorname{Tr}\operatorname{Im}\mathbb{G}(\omega_{0})\right]\int_{0}^{\infty}d\omega\frac{\omega^{3}}{e^{\frac{\hbar\omega}{k_{\textrm{B}}T_{1}}}-1}\operatorname{Im}(\alpha_{1}).

(S24)

Therefore, the ratio between the HT and HR can be written as (since
d\gtrsim\lambda_{0}
, only the

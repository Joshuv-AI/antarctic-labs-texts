Fast Successive-Cancellation Decoding of 2 × 2 2\times 2 Kernel Non-Binary Polar Codes: Identification, Decoding and Simplification
Ali Farsiabi, Hamid Ebrahimzad, Masoud Ardakani, Chuandong Li

Abstract
Non-binary polar codes (NBPCs) decoded by successive cancellation (SC) algorithm have remarkable bit-error-rate performance compared to the binary polar codes (BPCs). Due to the serial nature, SC decoding suffers from large latency. The latency issue in BPCs has been the topic of extensive research and it has been notably resolved by the introduction of fast SC-based decoders. However, the vast majority of research on NBPCs is devoted to issues concerning design and efficient implementation. In this paper, we propose fast SC decoding for NBPCs constructed based on 2 × 2 2\times 2 kernels. In particular, we identify various non-binary special nodes in the SC decoding tree of NBPCs and propose their fast decoding. This way, we avoid traversing the full decoding tree and significantly reduce the decoding delay compared to symbol-by-symbol SC decoding. We also propose a simplified NBPC structure that facilitates the procedure of non-binary fast SC decoding. Using our proposed fast non-binary decoder, we observed an improvement of up to 95 % 95\% in latency concerning the original SC decoding. This is while our proposed fast SC decoder for NBPCs incurs no error-rate loss.

Now that we are equipped with the above definitions and lemmas, in the following, we introduce different non-binary special nodes and their associated decoding algorithms. The proposed special nodes can significantly reduce the latency as well as the complexity of the LNBSC decoder.

IV-A

Rate-0 Node

A node
(\nu,s)
is identified as a Rate-0 node if
\mathcal{A}_{(\nu,s)}=\{\}
. The output vector of a Rate-0 node is
\boldsymbol{c}^{(\nu,s)^{T}}=\mathbf{0}^{T}_{N_{s}\times 1}{\mathbf{G}}^{\bigotimes s}_{2}
, where
\mathbf{0}_{N_{s}\times 1}
denotes an all-zero vector of size
N_{s}
. Hence, the estimated output vector is
\boldsymbol{\chi}^{(\nu,s)}=\mathbf{0}_{N_{s}\times 1}
.

It is noted that if the left child of a node is a Rate-0 node, then the CN equation in (
7
) (or (
9
)) can be skipped and the VN operation can be executed directly.

IV-B

Rate-1 Node

This node corresponds to
\mathcal{A}^{c}_{(\nu,s)}=\{\}
. Suppose the LLR matrix at the top of a Rate-1 node is
\mathbf{L}^{(\nu,s)}
. Then, the non-binary output vector is estimated as

\boldsymbol{\chi}^{(\nu,s)}=\mathcal{H}(\mathbf{L}^{(\nu,s)}),

(23)

where
\mathcal{H}(\cdot)
(defined in (
16
)) represents the non-binary hard-decision on different columns of
\mathbf{L}^{(\nu,s)}
.

IV-C

M-REP Node

A node
(\nu,s)
is identified as a multiplicative repetition (M-REP) node if
\mathcal{A}_{(\nu,s)}=\{N_{s}-1\}
.
Suppose
\mathbf{r}^{(s)^{T}}_{N_{s}-1}=[{r}^{(s)}_{N_{s}-1,0},\dots,{r}^{(s)}_{N_{s}-1,N_{s}-1}]
be the last row of the generator matrix
{\mathbf{G}}^{\bigotimes s}_{2}
. The output vector of an M-REP node is obtained as

\mathbf{c}^{(\nu,s)}=u_{N_{s}(\nu+1)-1}\mathbf{r}^{(s)}_{N_{s}-1},

(24)

where
{u}_{N_{s}(\nu+1)-1}
is the symbol located at leaf-node position
{N_{s}(\nu+1)-1}
. It is observed that the output vector consists of symbol
{u}_{N_{s}(\nu+1)-1}
multiplied by different elements of
\mathbf{r}^{(s)}_{N_{s}-1}
, hence the term “multiplicative repetition”.

Lemma 5
.

If a node
(\nu,s)
is identified as an M-REP node, the vector
\tilde{\mathbf{c}}^{(\nu,s)}
defined as

\tilde{\mathbf{c}}^{(\nu,s)}=\diag(\mathbf{g}^{(s)^{-1}}_{0})\mathbf{c}^{(\nu,s)},

(25)

is a codeword of a repetition (REP) code with rate
1/N_{s}
.

Proof.

Based on Lemma
2
, multiplying Equation (
24
) from left by
\diag(\mathbf{g}^{(s)^{-1}}_{0})
results in

\tilde{\mathbf{c}}^{(\nu,s)}=\frac{\gamma^{s}}{\mu^{s}}{u}_{N_{s}(\nu+1)-1}\mathbf{1}_{N_{s}\times 1}.

(26)

It is observed that
\tilde{\mathbf{c}}^{(\nu,s)}
consists of
N_{s}
repetitions of symbol
\frac{\gamma^{s}}{\mu^{s}}{u}_{N_{s}(\nu+1)-1}
which makes it the codeword of a repetition code with rate
1/N_{s}
.
∎

The result of Lemma
5
can be used to decode an M-REP node. First, different columns of the LLR matrix
\mathbf{L}^{(\nu,s)}
are permuted as

\tilde{\boldsymbol{\ell}}_{i}^{(\nu,s)}=\boldsymbol{\Pi}^{\times}_{g_{0,i}^{(s)^{-1}}}\boldsymbol{\ell}_{i}^{(\nu,s)},\>\>0\leq i\leq N_{s}-1,

(27)

where
\boldsymbol{\Pi}^{\times}_{g_{0,i}^{(s)^{-1}}}
is a
q\times q
permutation matrix corresponding to
i
th element of
\mathbf{g}^{(s)^{-1}}_{0}
. Then, a hard decision is made on the element-wise summation of the vectors
\tilde{\boldsymbol{\ell}}_{i}^{(\nu,s)}
as

{\chi}^{\prime}=\mathcal{H}(\sum_{i=0}^{N_{s}-1}\tilde{\boldsymbol{\ell}}_{i}^{(\nu,s)}),

(28)

where
\chi^{\prime}
is an estimate of the symbol
\frac{\gamma^{s}}{\mu^{s}}{u}_{N_{s}(\nu+1)-1}
in (
26
). The estimated vector
\boldsymbol{\chi}^{(\nu,s)}
corresponding to the M-REP node output is then obtained as

\boldsymbol{\chi}^{(\nu,s)}=\chi^{\prime}\gamma^{-s}\mu^{s}\mathbf{r}^{(s)}_{N_{s}-1}.

(29)

Note that
\gamma^{-s}
is the inverse of
\gamma^{s}
with respect to
\mathbb{GF}(q)
.

IV-D

M-SPC Node

The multiplicative single parity check (M-SPC) node corresponds to
\mathcal{A}^{c}_{(\nu,s)}=\{0\}
.

Theorem 1
.

Let
\mathbf{c}^{(\nu,s)}
be the output of the node
(\nu,s)
with
\mathcal{A}^{c}_{(\nu,s)}=\{0\}
. Then, the elements of the vector
\tilde{\mathbf{c}}^{(\nu,s)}=\diag(\mathbf{g}^{(s)^{-1}}_{0})\mathbf{c}^{(\nu,s)}
form a non-binary SPC equation or, equivalently, the node
(\nu,s)
is identified as a multiplicative SPC (M-SPC) node.

Proof.

Multiplying (
20
) by
\diag(\mathbf{g}^{(s)^{-1}}_{0})
from right results in

\tilde{\mathbf{c}}^{(\nu,s)^{T}}=\mathbf{u}_{N_{s}\nu}^{{N_{s}(\nu+1)-1}^{T}}\overline{\mathbf{G}}^{\bigotimes s}_{2},

(30)

where
\overline{\mathbf{G}}^{\bigotimes s}_{2}
is defined in Lemma
1
. Since
\mathcal{A}^{c}_{(\nu,s)}=\{0\}
, the input vector
\mathbf{u}_{N_{s}\nu}^{{N_{s}(\nu+1)-1}}=[0,u_{N_{s}\nu+1},\dots,u_{N_{s}(\nu+1)-1}]^{T}
, meaning that the first row of
\overline{\mathbf{G}}^{\bigotimes s}_{2}
would not contribute to
\tilde{\mathbf{c}}^{(\nu,s)}=[\tilde{{c}}^{(\nu,s)}_{0},\dots,\tilde{{c}}^{(\nu,s)}_{N_{s}-1}]^{T}
. Also, according to Lemma
1
, all the nonzero elements of row
j
,
0\leq j\leq N_{s}-1
, in
\overline{\mathbf{G}}^{\bigotimes s}_{2}
are equal. On the other hand, Lemma
3
implies that the number of nonzero elements at each row of
\overline{\mathbf{G}}^{\bigotimes s}_{2}
(excluding the first row) is even. Therefore, in the summation of elements in
\tilde{\mathbf{c}}^{(\nu,s)}
, different terms would cancel each other out and we have

\tilde{{c}}^{(\nu,s)}_{0}+\dots+\tilde{{c}}^{(\nu,s)}_{N_{s}-1}=0.

(31)

∎

The result of Theorem
1

27

\tilde{\mathbf{L}}^{(\nu,s)}
from
{\mathbf{L}}^{(\nu,s)}

corresponding to
\tilde{\mathbf{c}}^{(\nu,s)}

{\boldsymbol{\chi}}^{(\nu,s)}

{\boldsymbol{\chi}}^{(\nu,s)}=\gamma^{-s}\mu^{s}\diag(\mathbf{r}^{(s)}_{N_{s}-1})\tilde{\boldsymbol{\chi}}^{(\nu,s)},

(32)

where
\diag(\mathbf{r}^{(s)}_{N_{s}-1})
is a

diagonal matrix whose diagonal entries are the
2^{s}
elements in the last row of
\mathbf{G}^{\bigotimes s}_{2}

IV-E

Type-I Node

A node
(\nu,s)
is identified as a Type-I node if
\mathcal{A}_{(\nu,s)}=\{N_{s}-2,N_{s}-1\}
.

The following result is simple to prove.

Theorem 2
.

Let
\mathbf{c}^{(\nu,s)}_{e}
and
\mathbf{c}^{(\nu,s)}_{o}
be the symbols located at even and odd indexes of the output codeword of a Type-I node. Then, the elements of
\mathbf{c}^{(\nu,s)}_{e}
and
\mathbf{c}^{(\nu,s)}_{o}
form two separate M-REP code as

\begin{split}\mathbf{c}^{(\nu,s)}_{e}&=(\mu u_{N_{s}(\nu+1)-2}+\gamma u_{N_{s}(\nu+1)-1})\mathbf{r}^{(s-1)}_{N_{s}/2-1},\\
\mathbf{c}^{(\nu,s)}_{o}&=\delta u_{N_{s}(\nu+1)-1}\mathbf{r}^{(s-1)}_{N_{s}/2-1},\end{split}

(33)

where
\mathbf{r}^{(s-1)}_{N_{s}/2-1}
is the last row of the generator matrix
\mathbf{G}^{\bigotimes s-1}_{2}
.

In order to decode a Type-I node, the matrix
\mathbf{L}^{(\nu,s)}
is divided into two submatrices,
\mathbf{L}^{(\nu,s)}_{e}
and
\mathbf{L}^{(\nu,s)}_{o}
, that consist of the even and odd indexed columns of
\mathbf{L}^{(\nu,s)}
, respectively. Then,
\mathbf{L}^{(\nu,s)}_{e}
and
\mathbf{L}^{(\nu,s)}_{o}
along with the M-REP decoding of Section
IV-C
are used to decode two separate M-REP codes with length
N_{s}/2
. Eventually, the estimated output vectors, denoted by
\boldsymbol{\chi}^{(\nu,s)}_{e}
and
\boldsymbol{\chi}^{(\nu,s)}_{o}
, are placed at the corresponding even and odd positions of
{\boldsymbol{\chi}}^{(\nu,s)}
to obtain the total output vector.

IV-F

Type-II Node

A node
(\nu,s)
is identified as a Type-II node if
\mathcal{A}_{(\nu,s)}=\{N_{s}-3,N_{s}-2,N_{s}-1\}
.

Theorem 3
.

If a node
(\nu,s)
is identified as a Type-II node, its output vector is

\mathbf{c}^{(\nu,s)^{T}}=[{r}^{(s-2)}_{N_{s}/4-1,0}\mathbf{c}^{(2)^{T}},\dots,{r}^{(s-2)}_{N_{s}/4-1,N_{s}/4-1}\mathbf{c}^{(2)^{T}}],

(34)

where
\mathbf{r}^{(s-2)}_{N_{s}/4-1}=[{r}^{(s-2)}_{N_{s}/4-1,0},\dots,{r}^{(s-2)}_{N_{s}/4-1,N_{s}/4-1}]^{T}
is the last row of
\mathbf{G}^{\bigotimes s-2}_{2}
and
\mathbf{c}^{(2)}=[{c}^{(2)}_{0},{c}^{(2)}_{1},{c}^{(2)}_{2},{c}^{(2)}_{3}]^{T}
is the output vector of an M-SPC node with length
4
, i.e.,
g^{(2)^{-1}}_{0,0}{c}^{(2)}_{0}+g^{(2)^{-1}}_{0,1}{c}^{(2)}_{1}+g^{(2)^{-1}}_{0,2}{c}^{(2)}_{2}+g^{(2)^{-1}}_{0,3}{c}^{(2)}_{3}=0

Proof.

The theorem can be proved using mathematical induction. For
s=2
,
\mathbf{c}^{(\nu,2)}=\mathbf{c}^{(2)}
which is an M-SPC node. For

, we have
\mathbf{c}^{(\nu,s)^{T}}=[\mathbf{0}_{2^{s-1}\times 1}^{T}+\gamma\mathbf{c}^{(\nu,s-1)^{T}},\delta\mathbf{c}^{(\nu,s-1)^{T}}]
whose non-recursive form can be written as (
34
).
∎

In order to decode a Type-II node, the columns of the soft input matrix
\mathbf{L}^{(\nu,s)}
are permuted as

\dot{\boldsymbol{\ell}}_{i}^{(\nu,s)}=\boldsymbol{\Pi}^{\times^{-1}}_{r_{N_{s}/4-1,\lfloor i/4\rfloor}^{(s-2)}}\boldsymbol{\ell}_{i}^{(\nu,s)},\>\>0\leq i\leq N_{s}-1.

\mathbf{L}^{(2)}=[\boldsymbol{\ell}^{(2)}_{0},\dots,\boldsymbol{\ell}^{(2)}_{3}]

\boldsymbol{\ell}^{(2)}_{j}=\sum_{k=0}^{N_{s}/4-1}\dot{\boldsymbol{\ell}}_{4k+j}^{(\nu,s)},\>j=0,\dots,3.

(36)

The calculated matrix
\mathbf{L}^{(2)}

\boldsymbol{\chi}^{(2)}=[{\chi}^{(2)}_{0},\dots,{\chi}^{(2)}_{3}]^{T}
. The output vector,
\boldsymbol{\chi}^{(\nu,s)}
, is then computed as

\boldsymbol{\chi}^{(\nu,s)^{T}}=[{r}^{(s-2)}_{N_{s}/4-1,0}\boldsymbol{\chi}^{(2)^{T}},\dots,{r}^{(s-2)}_{N_{s}/4-1,N_{s}/4-1}\boldsymbol{\chi}^{(2)^{T}}].

(37)

Type-III Node

A node
(\nu,s)
,
s\geq 2

There is a one-to-one correspondence between the factor graph of a polar code with its binary tree representation. For example, in the factor graph of Fig.
5

\gamma^{(\nu,s)}

\delta^{(\nu,s)}

\boldsymbol{\mathcal{G}}^{(\nu,s)}=\begin{bmatrix}\mu^{(\nu,s)}\boldsymbol{\mathcal{G}}^{(2\nu,s-1)}&0\\

\boldsymbol{\mathcal{G}}^{(2\nu,s-1)}

(\nu,s)

\boldsymbol{\mathcal{G}}^{(\nu,1)}=\begin{bmatrix}\mu^{(\nu,1)}&0\\
\gamma^{(\nu,1)}&\delta^{(\nu,1)}\end{bmatrix}.

(53)

\mu^{(\nu,s)}=1,\gamma^{(\nu,s)}=1,\delta^{(\nu,s)}=1\>\>\>\>\text{for}\>\>\>\>1\leq s<s_{0},

VIII
Conclusion

In this work, a fast successive-cancellation (SC) decoding algorithm for non-binary polar codes (NBPCs), which we refer to as fast LLR-domain non-binary SC (LNBSC) decoder, is proposed. The main underlying idea behind our proposed method is the identification and fast decoding of certain non-binary special nodes in the decoding tree of the NBPC. To the best of our knowledge, this is the first work in which the identification of a variety of non-binary special nodes along with their decoding algorithms is proposed. We also proposed a simplified NBPC structure that facilitates the procedure of our proposed non-binary fast SC decoding. Our results show that the proposed fast non-binary decoder achieves significant decoding speed improvement with respect to the original SC decoder. This is while a significant reduction in the decoding latency is observed without sacrificing the error-rate performance of the code.

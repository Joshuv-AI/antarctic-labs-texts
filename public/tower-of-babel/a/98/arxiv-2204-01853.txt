𝒪 \mathcal{O} -operators on Lie triple systems
Taoufik Chtioui , Atef Hajjaji , Sami Mabrouk and Abdenacer Makhlouf 1. University of Sfax, Faculty of Sciences, BP 1171, 3038 Sfax, Tunisia2. University of Gafsa, Faculty of Sciences, 2112 Gafsa, Tunisia3. IRIMAS - Département de Mathématiques, 18, rue des frères Lumière, F-68093 Mulhouse, France

Abstract
The purpose of this paper is to study cohomology and deformations of 𝒪 \mathcal{O} -operators on Lie triple systems. We define a cohomology of an 𝒪 \mathcal{O} -operator T T as the Lie-Yamaguti cohomology of a certain Lie triple system induced by T T with coefficients in a suitable representation. Then we consider infinitesimal and formal deformations of 𝒪 \mathcal{O} -operators from cohomological viewpoint. Moreover we provide relationships between 𝒪 \mathcal{O} -operators on Lie algebras and associated Lie triple systems.



Key words
: Lie triple systems,
\mathcal{O}
-operator, representation, Lie-Yamaguti cohomology, deformation.

(2020): 17A40, 17B56, 17B10, 17B38.

Introduction

The concept of Lie triple system was introduced first by Jacobson
[
15
]
. The present formulation is due to Yamguti
[
29
]

[
6

and was strongly developed for Symmetric spaces and related spaces. Indeed, the tangent space of a symmetric space is a Lie triple system. It turns out that they have important applications in physics, in particular, in elementary particle theory and the theory of quantum mechanics, as well as numerical analysis of differential equations. They have become an interesting subject in mathematics, their structure have been studied first by Lister in
[
20
]
.

The concept of
\mathcal{O}
-operators on Lie algebras appeared first, in a paper of
B. A. Kupershmidt
[
17
]
as an operator analogue of classical
r
-matrices and Poisson structures. However,
Rota-Baxter operators were introduced by G. Baxter
[
5
]
in his study of fluctuation theory
in probability. Then developed by G.-C. Rota
[
24
]
in Combinatorics. They have important applications in the algebraic aspects of the renormalization
in quantum field theory
[
7
]
. Rota-Baxter operators on Lie algebras (resp. associative algebras)
are also related to the splitting of algebraic structures. In
[
4
]
, The authors introduced the notion of a Rota-Baxter operator on a
3
-Lie algebra. Recently, in order to construct solutions of the classical
3
-Lie
Yang-Baxter equation, the authors in
[
3
]
introduced a more general notion called
relative Rota-Baxter operator on a
3
-Lie algebra with respect to a representation .

The deformation theory using formal power series and suitable cohomologies was initiated by Gerstenhaber

[
11
]
for associative algebras. Nijenhuis and Richardson extended this study to Lie algebras
[
23
]
. Deformations of
3
-Lie algebras and Lie triple systems were studied in
[
9
]
and
[
16
,
30
]
respectively.
Recently deformations of certain operators, e.g. morphisms and Rota-Baxter operators
(
\mathcal{O}
-operators) were deeply studied, see
[
1
,
8
,
10
,
21
,
26
]
.
One needs a cohomology to control deformations and extension problems of a given
algebraic structure. Cohomologies of various algebraic structures, such as associative
algebras, Lie algebras, Leibniz algebras, pre-Lie algebras and
n
-Lie algebras, are well known. Cohomology of Lie triple systems was introduced by K. Yamaguti in
[
29
]
. Recently, cohomology of Rota-Baxter operators (
\mathcal{O}
-operators) on Lie algebras and associative
algebras were developed in
[
26
,
8
]
respectively. In
[
28
]
,

Inspired by these works, we tackle in this paper the cohomology theory of

In Section 2, we summarize som basics and briefly recall representations and cohomology

2
Preliminaries

In this section, we recall representations and cohomology theory of Lie triple systems.

Definition 2.1
.

A Lie triple systems (L.t.s) is a vector space
L
endowed with a ternary bracket
[\cdot,\cdot,\cdot]:\wedge^{2}L\otimes L\to L
satisfying:

\displaystyle[x,y,z]+[y,z,x]+[z,x,y]=0,

(2.1)

\displaystyle[x,y,[z,t,e]]=[[x,y,z],t,e]+[z,[x,y,t],e]+[z,t,[x,y,e]],

(2.2)

for any
x,y,z,t,e\in L
.

Example 2.1
.

Let
(L,[\cdot,\cdot])
be a Lie algebra. We define
[\cdot,\cdot,\cdot]:(\wedge^{2}L)\otimes L\rightarrow L
by

[x,y,z]=[[x,y],z],\quad\forall x,y,z\in L.

(2.3)

Then
(L,[\cdot,\cdot,\cdot])
becomes a L.t.s naturally.

A morphism
f:(L,[\cdot,\cdot,\cdot])\rightarrow(L^{{}^{\prime}},[\cdot,\cdot,\cdot]^{{}^{\prime}})
of L.t.s is a linear map satisfying

f([x,y,z])=[f(x),f(y),f(z)]^{{}^{\prime}},\quad\forall x,y,z\in L.

An isomorphism is a bijective morphism.

Denote by
X=(x,y)\in L\otimes L
and
\mathcal{L}(X):L\rightarrow L
defined by
\mathcal{L}(X)z_{i}=[x,y,z_{i}]
, then the identity (
2.2
) can be rewritten
in the form

\mathcal{L}(X)[z_{1},z_{2},z_{3}]=[\mathcal{L}(X)z_{1},z_{2},z_{3}]+[z_{1},\mathcal{L}(X)z_{2},z_{3}]+[z_{1},z_{2},\mathcal{L}(X)z_{3}],

which means that
\mathcal{L}(X)
is a derivation with respect to the bracket
[\cdot,\cdot,\cdot
].

In
[
29
]
, K. Yamaguti introduced the notion of representation and cohomology theory of L.t.s. Later, the authors in
[
12
,
14
]
and
[
16
]
studied the cohomology theory of L.t.s from a different point of view. K. Yamaguti’s
work can be described as follows.

Definition 2.2
.

A representation of a L.t.s
L
on a vector space
V
is a bilinear map
\theta:\otimes^{2}L\rightarrow End(V)
, such that the following conditions are satisfied:

\displaystyle\theta(z,t)\theta(x,y)-\theta(y,t)\theta(x,z)-\theta(x,[y,z,t])+D(y,z)\theta(x,t)=0,

(2.4)

\displaystyle\theta(z,t)D(x,y)-D(x,y)\theta(z,t)+\theta([x,y,z],t)+\theta(z,[x,y,t])=0,

(2.5)

where
D(x,y)=\theta(y,x)-\theta(x,y)
, for all
x,y,z,t\in L
. We denote by

(L,[\cdot,\cdot,\cdot];\theta)
, the
L.t.sRep
pair.

Remark 2.1
.

Let
L
be a L.t.s and
\theta(x,y)
be the linear map
z\mapsto[z,x,y]
of
L
into itself for all
x,y\in L
. Using (
2.2
), then
we can prove that
L
is an
L
-module. Moreover, by (
2.1
),
D(x,y)
becomes a linear map
z\mapsto[x,y,z]
(inner derivation). In this paper, we define
\theta(x,y):=\mathcal{R}(x,y),D(x,y):=\mathcal{L}(x,y)
where
\mathcal{L}(x,y)(z)=[x,y,z],\;\mathcal{R}(x,y)(z)=[z,x,y]
.

As usual, we have a characterisation of a representation by a semi-direct product i.e.
(V,\theta)
is a representation of a L.t.s
L
if and only if
L\oplus V
is a L.t.s under the following bracket

\displaystyle[x+u,y+v,z+w]_{L\oplus V}=[x,y,z]+\theta(y,z)u-\theta(x,z)v+D(x,y)w,

(2.6)

for any
x,y,z\in L
and
u,v,w\in V
.

Let

(L,[\cdot,\cdot,\cdot];\theta)
be a
L.t.sRep
pair. For each
n\geqslant 0
, we denote by
C^{2n+1}(L,V)
, the vector space of
(2n+1)
-cochains of
L
with coefficients in
V
:
f\in C^{2n+1}(L,V)
is a multilinear function of
\times^{2n+1}L
into
V
satisfying

f(x_{1},x_{2},\cdots,x_{2n-2},x,x,y)=0,

and

f(x_{1},x_{2},\cdots,x_{2n-2},x,y,z)+f(x_{1},x_{2},\cdots,x_{2n-2},y,z,x)+f(x_{1},x_{2},\cdots,x_{2n-2},z,x,y)=0.

The Yamaguti coboundary operator
\delta^{2n-1}:C^{2n-1}(L,V)\rightarrow C^{2n+1}(L,V)
is defined by

\displaystyle\delta^{2n-1}f(x_{1},x_{2},\cdots,x_{2n+1})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\theta(x_{2n},x_{2n+1})f(x_{1},x_{2},\cdots,x_{2n-1})-\theta(x_{2n-1},x_{2n+1})f(x_{1},x_{2},\cdots,x_{2n-2},x_{2n})

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{n}(-1)^{n+k}D(x_{2k-1},x_{2k})f(x_{1},x_{2},\cdots,\widehat{x}_{2k-1},\widehat{x}_{2k},\cdots,x_{2n+1})

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{n}\sum_{j=2k+1}^{2n+1}(-1)^{n+k+1}f(x_{1},x_{2},\cdots,\widehat{x}_{2k-1},\widehat{x}_{2k},\cdots,[x_{2k-1},x_{2k},x_{j}],\cdots,x_{2n+1})

(2.7)

for all
f\in C^{2n-1}(L,V),\ n\geqslant 1
, where
\;\widehat{}\;
denotes omission. The Yamaguti cochain forms a complex with this coboundary as follow

C^{1}(L,V)\overset{\delta^{1}}{\longrightarrow}C^{3}(L,V)\overset{\delta^{3}}{\longrightarrow}C^{5}(L,V)\longrightarrow\cdots,

and
\delta^{2n+1}\circ\delta^{2n-1}=0\;for\;n=1,2,\cdots
( see
[
29
]
for more details). Hence we get the
Yamaguti cohomology group
H^{\bullet}(L,V)=Z^{\bullet}(L,V)/B^{\bullet}(L,V)
, where
Z^{\bullet}(L,V)
is the space of
cocycles and
B^{\bullet}(L,V)
is the space of coboundaries.

Definition 2.3
.

[
30
]

Let
(V,\theta)
be a representation of a L.t.s
L
.

1.

A linear map
f\in C^{1}(L,V)

is a
1
-cocycle (closed) if

D(x_{1},x_{2})f(x_{3})-\theta(x_{1},x_{3})f(x_{2})+\theta(x_{2},x_{3})f(x_{1})-f([x_{1},x_{2},x_{3}])=0,

(2.8)

and a map
f\in C^{3}(L,V)
is a
3
-coboundary if there exists a map
g\in C^{1}(L,V)
such that
f=\delta^{1}g
.

2.

A map
f\in C^{3}(L,V)
is called a
3
-cocycle if
\forall x_{1},x_{2},x_{3},y_{1},y_{2},y_{3}\in L
,

\displaystyle f(x_{1},x_{1},x_{2})=0,

(2.9)

\displaystyle f(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3})+f(x_{2},x_{3},x_{1})+f(x_{3},x_{1},x_{2})=0,

(2.10)

\displaystyle f(x_{1},x_{2},[y_{1},y_{2},y_{3}])+D(x_{1},x_{2})f(y_{1},y_{2},y_{3})

\displaystyle=f([x_{1},x_{2},y_{1}],y_{2},y_{3})+f(y_{1},[x_{1},x_{2},y_{2}],y_{3})+f(y_{1},y_{2},[x_{1},x_{2},y_{3}])

\displaystyle+\theta(y_{2},y_{3})f(x_{1},x_{2},y_{1})-\theta(y_{1},y_{3})f(x_{1},x_{2},y_{2})+D(y_{1},y_{2})f(x_{1},x_{2},y_{3}).

(2.11)

3
\mathcal{O}
-operators on Lie triple systems

In this section, we recall the notion of pre-Lie triple system structure introduced in
[
22
]
and define Rota-Baxter operators,
\mathcal{O}
-operators on L.t.s, then
we study their morphisms. Moreover, we give some characterisations of
\mathcal{O}
-operators in terms of Nijenhuis operators and graphs.

Definition 3.1
.

Let
L
be a L.t.s. A linear map
R:L\rightarrow L
is said to be a
Rota-Baxter operator of weight
0
if it satisfies

[R(x),R(y),R(z)]=R\Big([R(x),R(y),z]+[R(x),y,R(z)]+[x,R(y),R(z)]\Big),\;\forall x,y,z\in L.

The notion of
\mathcal{O}
-operator (also called relative Rota-Baxter
operator or Kupershmidt operator) is a generalization of Rota-Baxter operators in the presence of arbitrary representation.

Definition 3.2
.

Let

(L,[\cdot,\cdot,\cdot];\theta)
be a
L.t.sRep
pair. A linear map
T:V\to L
is called an
\mathcal{O}
-operator of
L
with respect to
\theta
if it satisfies

\displaystyle[Tu,Tv,Tw]=T\Big(D(Tu,Tv)w+\theta(Tv,Tw)u-\theta(Tu,Tw)v\Big),\quad\forall u,v,w\in V.

(3.1)

If
V=L
, then
T
is a Rota-Baxter operator of weight
0
on
L
with respect to the adjoint representation. Thus
\mathcal{O}
-operators are generalization of Rota-Baxter operators.

Now we give the following characterization of an
\mathcal{O}
-operator in terms of graphs.

Proposition 3.1
.

A linear map
T:V\rightarrow L
is an
\mathcal{O}
-operator if and only if the graph
Gr(T)=\{(Tu,u)|\;u\in V\}
is a subalgebra of the
semi-direct product
L\oplus V
.

Proof.

Let
(Tu,u)
,
(Tv,v)
and
(Tw,w)

\in Gr(T)
. Then we have

\displaystyle\quad\;[Tu+u,Tv+v,Tw+w]_{L\oplus V}=[Tu,Tv,Tw]+\theta(Tv,Tw)u-\theta(Tu,Tw)v+D(Tu,Tv)w.

Assume that
Gr(T)
is a subalgebra of the
semi-direct product
L\oplus V
, then we have

\displaystyle[Tu,Tv,Tw]=T\Big(\theta(Tv,Tw)u-\theta(Tu,Tw)v+D(Tu,Tv)w\Big).

On the other hand, if
T
is an
\mathcal{O}
-operator, we obtain

\displaystyle\quad\;[Tu+u,Tv+v,Tw+w]_{L\oplus V}

\displaystyle=T\Big(\theta(Tv,Tw)u-\theta(Tu,Tw)v+D(Tu,Tv)w\Big)

\displaystyle+\theta(Tv,Tw)u-\theta(Tu,Tw)v+D(Tu,Tv)w\in Gr(T).

Hence
Gr(T)
is a subalgebra of the
semi-direct product
L\oplus V
.
∎

In the following proposition, we show that an
\mathcal{O}
-operator can be lifted up to a
Rota-Baxter operator.

Proposition 3.2
.

Let

(L,[\cdot,\cdot,\cdot];\theta)
be a
L.t.sRep
pair and

T:V\rightarrow L
be a linear map. Define
\widehat{T}\in End(L\oplus V)
by
\widehat{T}(x,u)=(T(u),0)
. Then
T
is an

\mathcal{O}
-operator if and only
\widehat{T}
is a Rota-Baxter operator on
L\oplus V
.

Proof.

For any
x,y,z\in L
and
u,v,w\in V
, we have

\displaystyle[\widehat{T}(x,u),\widehat{T}(y,v),\widehat{T}(z,w)]_{L\oplus V}

\displaystyle-\widehat{T}\Big([\widehat{T}(x,u),\widehat{T}(y,v),(z,w)]_{L\oplus V}+[\widehat{T}(x,u),(y,v),\widehat{T}(z,w)]_{L\oplus V}+[(x,u),\widehat{T}(y,v),\widehat{T}(z,w)]_{L\oplus V}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle[(Tu,0),(Tv,0),(Tw,0)]_{L\oplus V}

\displaystyle-\widehat{T}\Big([(Tu,0),(Tv,0),(z,w)]_{L\oplus V}+[(Tu,0),(y,v),(Tw,0)]_{L\oplus V}+[(x,u),(Tv,0),(Tw,0)]_{L\oplus V}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Big([Tu,Tv,Tw],0\Big)-\Big(T(\theta(Tv,Tw)u-\theta(Tu,Tw)v+D(Tu,Tv)w),0\Big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Big([Tu,Tv,Tw]-T(\theta(Tv,Tw)u-\theta(Tu,Tw)v+D(Tu,Tv)w),0\Big).

Then
\widehat{T}
is a Rota-Baxter operator on
L\oplus V
if and only if
T
is an
\mathcal{O}
-operator.
∎

Example 3.3
.

Let
L
be a 2-dimensional L.t.s with a basis
\{e_{1},e_{2}\}
and a bracket defined by

[e_{1},e_{2},e_{2}]=e_{1}.

The operator

T=\begin{pmatrix}0&a\\

Example 3.4
.

Let
L

\{e_{1},e_{2},e_{3},e_{4}\}

[e_{1},e_{2},e_{1}]=e_{4}.

\displaystyle T=\begin{pmatrix}0&a&0&0\\

Definition 3.4

Let

\displaystyle\{x_{5},x_{1},[x_{2},x_{3},x_{4}]_{C}\}=\{\{x_{5},x_{1},x_{2}\},x_{3},x_{4}\}-\{\{x_{5},x_{1},x_{3}\},x_{2},x_{4}\}+\{x_{2},x_{3},\{x_{5},x_{1},x_{4}\}\}^{*},

(3.4)

\displaystyle\{x_{1},x_{2},\{x_{5},x_{3},x_{4}\}\}^{*}\;=\{\{x_{1},x_{2},x_{5}\}^{*},x_{3},x_{4}\}+\{x_{5},[x_{1},x_{2},x_{3}]_{C},x_{4}\}+\{x_{5},x_{3},[x_{1},x_{2},x_{4}]_{C}\},

\displaystyle\{x,y,z\}^{*}=\{z,y,x\}-\{z,x,y\},

\displaystyle[x,y,z]_{C}\;=\{z,y,x\}-\{z,x,y\}+\{x,y,z\}-\{y,x,z\}

\displaystyle\quad\quad\quad\;\;\;\;=\{x,y,z\}^{*}+\{x,y,z\}-\{y,x,z\},

5
Deformation of an
\mathcal{O}
-operator on a Lie triple system

In this section, we will apply the classical deformation theory due to Gerstenhaber (infinitesimal and formal deformations) to an
\mathcal{O}
-operator on a L.t.s. We will introduce the notion of
Nijenhuis element associated to an
\mathcal{O}
-operator that arise from trivial deformations.
We also consider the rigidity of an
\mathcal{O}
-operator and provide a sufficient condition in terms of Nijenhuis elements.

5.1
Infinitesimal deformations

Let

(L,[\cdot,\cdot,\cdot],\theta)
be a
L.t.sRep
pair. Suppose
T:V\rightarrow L
is an
\mathcal{O}
-operator associated to representation
(V,\theta)
.

Definition 5.1
.

A parametrized sum
T_{t}=T+tT_{1}
, for some
T_{1}\in Hom(V,L)
, is called an infinitesimal deformation of
T
if
T_{t}
is an
\mathcal{O}
-operator for all values of
t
. In this case, we say that

T_{1}
generates an infinitesimal deformation of
T
.

Suppose
T_{1}
generates an infinitesimal deformation of
T
. Then we have

[T_{t}(u),T_{t}(v),T_{t}(w)]=T_{t}\Big(D(T_{t}(u),T_{t}(v))w+\theta(T_{t}(v),T_{t}(w))u-\theta(T_{t}(u),T_{t}(w))v\Big),\quad\forall u,v,w\in V.

This is equivalent to the following conditions

\displaystyle[Tu,Tv,T_{1}(w)]+[Tu,T_{1}(v),Tw]+[T_{1}(u),Tv,Tw]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle T\Big(D(Tu,T_{1}(v))w+D(T_{1}(u),Tv)w+\theta(Tv,T_{1}(w))u+\theta(T_{1}(v),Tw)u

\displaystyle-\theta(Tu,T_{1}(w))v-\theta(T_{1}(u),Tw)v\Big)+T_{1}\Big(D(Tu,Tv)w+\theta(Tv,Tw)u-\theta(Tu,Tw)v\Big),

(5.1)

\displaystyle[Tu,T_{1}(v),T_{1}(w)]+[T_{1}(u),Tv,T_{1}(w)]+[T_{1}(u),T_{1}(v),Tw]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle T\Big(D(T_{1}(u),T_{1}(v))w+\theta(T_{1}(v),T_{1}(w))u-\theta(T_{1}(u),T_{1}(w))v\Big)

\displaystyle+T_{1}\Big(D(Tu,T_{1}(v))w+D(T_{1}(u),Tv)w+\theta(Tv,T_{1}(w))u

\displaystyle+\theta(T_{1}(v),Tw)u-\theta(Tu,T_{1}(w))v-\theta(T_{1}(u),Tw)v\Big),

(5.2)

and

\displaystyle[T_{1}(u),T_{1}(v),T_{1}(w)]=T_{1}\Big(D(T_{1}(u),T_{1}(v))w+\theta(T_{1}(v),T_{1}(w))u-\theta(T_{1}(u),T_{1}(w))v\Big).

(5.3)

Note that, by Eq.(
2.1
), we have

\displaystyle[Tu,Tv,T_{1}(w)]=-[Tv,T_{1}(w),Tu]-[T_{1}(w),Tu,Tv]=[T_{1}(w),Tv,Tu]+[Tu,T_{1}(w),Tv].

Then the identity (
5.1
) implies that
T_{1}
is a
1
-cocycle (closed) in the cohomology of
T
. Hence,
T_{1}
defines a
cohomology class in
\mathcal{H}^{1}(V,L)
. Eq. (
5.3
) means that

is an
\mathcal{O}
-operator on the
L.t.sRep
pair

(L,[\cdot,\cdot,\cdot];\theta)
.

Definition 5.2
.

Let
T_{t}=T+tT_{1}
and
T^{{}^{\prime}}_{t}=T+tT^{{}^{\prime}}_{1}
be

\mathcal{O}
-operator

and

\mathcal{O}
-operators from

to

Since

\begin{cases}[z_{1},[\mathfrak{X},z_{2}],[\mathfrak{X},z_{3}]]+[[\mathfrak{X},z_{1}],z_{2},[\mathfrak{X},z_{3}]]+[[\mathfrak{X},z_{1}],[\mathfrak{X},z_{2}],z_{3}]=0,\\
[[\mathfrak{X},z_{1}],[\mathfrak{X},z_{2}],[\mathfrak{X},z_{3}]]=0,\;\text{for}\;z_{1},z_{2},z_{3}\in L.\end{cases}

(5.5)

The condition

\begin{cases}\theta(z_{1},[\mathfrak{X},z_{2}])D(\mathfrak{X})+\theta([\mathfrak{X},z_{1}],z_{2})D(\mathfrak{X})+\theta([\mathfrak{X},z_{1}],[\mathfrak{X},z_{2}])=0,\\

From the first condition of (

\displaystyle T_{1}(u)-T^{{}^{\prime}}_{1}(u)=TD(\mathfrak{X})u-[\mathfrak{X},Tu]=(d_{T}\mathfrak{X})(u).

Therefore, we get the following result.

Theorem 5.1
.

Let
T_{t}=T+tT_{1}
and

be two equivalent infinitesimal deformations of an

\mathcal{O}
-operator

. Then
T_{1}
and
T^{{}^{\prime}}_{1}
defines the same cohomology class in
{H}_{T}^{1}(V;L)
.

Definition 5.3
.

Let
T_{t}=T+tT_{1}
be an infinitesimal deformation of an
\mathcal{O}
-operator
T
. The deformation

is said to be

is a morphism
from
T_{t}
to

Now we will define Nijenhuis elements associated to an

5.2

\mathcal{O}
-operators generalizing the

\mathcal{O}

By applying Eqs.(

Theorem 5.4

If two formal deformations of an

Definition 5.8

An
\mathcal{O}
-operator

is trivial.

Now, we give a cohomological characterization of rigidity of an

-operator involving the set of Nijenhuis elements.

Theorem 5.5
.

Let
T
be an
\mathcal{O}
-operator. If
Z_{T}^{1}(V,L)=d_{T}(Nij(T))
, then
T
is rigid.

Proof.

Let
T_{t}=\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}t^{i}T_{i}
be any formal deformation of

. We have seen in Proposition

that

is a

-cocycle in the

, i.e.

. Thus, from the hypothesis, we get a Nijenhuis element
\mathfrak{X}\in Nij(T)

such that

\phi_{t}=Id_{L}+t[\mathfrak{X},-],\quad\quad\psi_{t}=Id_{V}+tD(\mathfrak{X})(-),

and define

is a deformation equivalent to

\displaystyle T^{{}^{\prime}}_{t}(u)=(Id_{L}+t[\mathfrak{X},-])(T_{t}\Big(Id_{V}-tD(\mathfrak{X})+t^{2}D^{2}(\mathfrak{X})+\cdots+(-1)^{i}t^{i}D^{i}(\mathfrak{X})+\cdots)(u)\Big))

\displaystyle\quad\;\;\;\;\;=T(u)+t^{2}T^{{}^{\prime}}_{2}(u)+\cdots\quad(as\;T_{1}(u)=d_{T}(\mathfrak{X})(u)).

6
From cohomology groups of
\mathcal{O}
-operators on Lie algebras to those on Lie triple systems

Motivated by the construction of L.t.s from Lie algebras. We give some connections between
\mathcal{O}
-operators on Lie algebras and L.t.s.

A representation of a Lie algebra
(L,[\cdot,\cdot])
on a vector space
V
is a linear map
\rho:L\rightarrow End(V)
such that

\rho([x,y])=\rho(x)\rho(y)-\rho(y)\rho(x),\;\forall x,y\in L.

Similarly, we call the pair of Lie algebra
(L,[\cdot,\cdot])
and
the representation
\rho
a
LieRep
pair, denoted by
(L,[\cdot,\cdot];\rho)
.
Let
(L,[\cdot,\cdot],\rho)
be a
LieRep
pair. Define a bilinear bracket
[\cdot,\cdot]_{\rho}
on
L\oplus V
by

[x+u,y+v]_{\rho}=[x,y]+\rho(x)v-\rho(y)u,\;\forall x,y\in L,\;u,v\in V.

Then
(L\oplus V,[\cdot,\cdot]_{\rho})
is a semi-direct product Lie algebra, denoted by
L\ltimes_{\rho}V
.

Let
(L,[\cdot,\cdot];\rho)
be a
LieRep
pair. The space of
p
-cochains is
C_{Lie}^{p}(L,V)=Hom(\wedge^{p}L,V)
for
p\geq 0
. The
coboundary operator
\partial_{\rho}:C_{Lie}^{p}(L,V)\rightarrow C_{Lie}^{p+1}(L,V)
is defined by

\displaystyle\partial_{\rho}(f)(x_{1},\cdots,x_{p+1})

\displaystyle=\sum_{1\leq i<j\leq p+1}(-1)^{i+j}f([x_{i},x_{j}],x_{1},\cdots,\widehat{x_{i}},\cdots,\widehat{x_{j}},\cdots,x_{p+1})

\displaystyle+\sum_{i=1}^{p+1}(-1)^{i+1}\rho(x_{i})f(x_{1},\cdots,\widehat{x_{i}},\cdots,x_{p+1}),

for
f\in C_{Lie}^{p}(L,V)(p\geq 0)
. We denote the corresponding
p
-th cohomology group by
H_{Lie}^{p}(L,V)
.

Definition 6.1
.

A linear map
T:V\rightarrow L
is called an
\mathcal{O}
-operator on a
LieRep
pair
(L,[\cdot,\cdot];\rho)
if
T
satisfies

[Tu,Tv]=T\Big(\rho(Tu)v-\rho(Tv)u\Big),\;\forall u,v\in V.

(6.1)

Let
T
be an
\mathcal{O}
-operator on a LieRep pair
(L,[\cdot,\cdot];\rho)
. There is a Lie algebra structure on
(V,[\cdot,\cdot]_{T})
, where the bracket
[\cdot,\cdot]_{T}:V\times V\rightarrow V
is given by

[u,v]_{T}=\rho(Tu)v-\rho(Tv)u\;\forall u,v\in V.

(6.2)

Now, we recall some results from
[
26
]
. There is a representation of
(V,[\cdot,\cdot]_{T})
,
\rho_{T}:V\rightarrow gl(L)
, defined by

\rho_{T}(u)x=[Tu,x]+T\rho(x)u,\;\forall u\in V,x\in L.

Let
T
be an
\mathcal{O}
-operator on a LieRep pair
(L,[\cdot,\cdot];\rho)
. Consider the set of
p
-cochains
C_{T}^{p}(V,L)=Hom(\wedge^{p}V,L)
. Let
\widetilde{d}_{T}:C_{T}^{p}(V,L)\rightarrow C_{T}^{p+1}(V,L)(p\geq 0)
be the corresponding coboundary operator of the
Lie algebra
(V,[\cdot,\cdot]_{T})
with coefficients in the representation
(L,\rho_{T})
, defined for all
f\in C_{T}^{p}(V,L)
and
u_{1},\cdots,u_{p+1}\in V
by

\displaystyle\widetilde{d}_{T}(f)(u_{1},\cdots,u_{p+1})

\displaystyle=\sum_{1\leq i<j\leq p+1}(-1)^{i+j}f(\rho(Tu_{i})u_{j}-\rho(Tu_{j})u_{i},u_{1},\cdots,\widehat{u_{i}},\cdots,\widehat{u_{j}},\cdots,u_{p+1})

\displaystyle+\sum_{i=1}^{p+1}(-1)^{i+1}\Big([Tu_{i},f(u_{1},\cdots,\widehat{u_{i}},\cdots,u_{p+1})]+T\rho(f(u_{1},\cdots,\widehat{u_{i}},\cdots,u_{p+1}))(u_{i})\Big).

Then the cochain complex

(C_{T}^{p}(V,L),)
is called a cochain complex of the
\mathcal{O}
-operator
T
on a LieRep pair
(L,[\cdot,\cdot];\rho)
.
We denote the corresponding
p
-th cohomology group by
H_{T}^{p}(V,L)
.

As we know, there is a method of constructing L.t.s from Lie algebras given in Example
2.1
.

Theorem 6.1
.

Let
(L,[\cdot,\cdot];\rho)
be a LieRep pair. Define
\theta_{\rho}:\otimes^{2}L\rightarrow gl(V)
by

\theta_{\rho}(x,y)=\rho(y)\rho(x).

(6.3)

Then
(L,[\cdot,\cdot,\cdot]=[\cdot,\cdot]\circ([\cdot,\cdot]\otimes Id_{L});\theta_{\rho})
is a
L.t.sRep
pair.

Proof.

Let
(L,[\cdot,\cdot];\rho)
be a LieRep pair. We know that there is a L.t.s structure on
L
given by the bracket
[\cdot,\cdot,\cdot]=[[\cdot,\cdot],\cdot]
. Now,
for all
x,y,z\in L
and
u,v,w\in V
, we have

\displaystyle[x+u,y+v,z+w]_{L\oplus V}

\displaystyle=[[x+u,y+v]_{L\oplus V},z+w]_{L\oplus V}

\displaystyle=[[x,y]+\rho(x)(v)-\rho(y)(u),z+w]_{L\oplus V}

\displaystyle=[[x,y],z]+\rho([x,y])(w)-\rho(z)\rho(x)(v)+\rho(z)\rho(y(u)

\displaystyle=[x,y,z]+\Big(\rho(x)\rho(y)-\rho(y)\rho(x)\Big)(w)-\rho(z)\rho(x)(v)+\rho(z)\rho(y(u)

\displaystyle=[x,y,z]+\Big(\theta_{\rho}(y,x)-\theta_{\rho}(x,y)\Big)(w)-\theta_{\rho}(x,z)(v)+\theta_{\rho}(y,z)(u)

\displaystyle=[x,y,z]+D_{\rho}(x,y)(w)-\theta_{\rho}(x,z)(v)+\theta_{\rho}(y,z)(u).

Then by Eq.(
2.6
),
(L,[\cdot,\cdot,\cdot]=[\cdot,\cdot]\circ([\cdot,\cdot]\otimes Id_{L});\theta_{\rho})
is a
L.t.sRep
pair.
∎

Theorem 6.2
.

Every
1
-cocycle for the cohomology of LieRep pair
(L,[\cdot,\cdot];\rho)
is a
1
-cocycle for the cohomology of the
L.t.sRep

(L,[\cdot,\cdot,\cdot]=[\cdot,\cdot]\circ([\cdot,\cdot]\otimes Id_{L});\theta_{\rho})

Proof.

Let
\varphi
be a

-cocycle of the cohomology of the LieRep pair
(L,[\cdot,\cdot],\rho)

\forall x,y\in L,\;\partial_{\rho}(\varphi)(x,y)=\rho(x)\varphi(y)-\rho(y)\varphi(x)-\varphi([x,y])=0.

Now, for any

\displaystyle\delta^{1}(\varphi)(x,y,z)=

\displaystyle D_{\rho}(x,y)\varphi(z)-\theta_{\rho}(x,z)\varphi(y)+\theta_{\rho}(y,z)\varphi(x)-\varphi([x,y,z])

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\rho([x,y])\varphi(z)-\rho(z)\rho(x)\varphi(y)+\rho(z)\rho(y)\varphi(x)-\varphi([[x,y],z])

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\rho([x,y])\varphi(z)-\rho(z)\rho(x)\varphi(y)+\rho(z)\rho(y)\varphi(x)-\rho([x,y])\varphi(z)+\rho(z)\varphi([x,y])

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\rho([x,y])\varphi(z)-\rho(z)\rho(x)\varphi(y)+\rho(z)\rho(y)\varphi(x)-\rho([x,y])\varphi(z)

\displaystyle+\rho(z)\rho(x)\varphi(y)-\rho(z)\rho(y)\varphi(x)

which means that

-cocycle for the cohomology of the

pair
(L,[\cdot,\cdot,\cdot]=[\cdot,\cdot]\circ([\cdot,\cdot]\otimes Id_{L});\theta_{\rho})
.
∎

Theorem 6.3
.

Let
\varphi\in Z^{2}_{Lie}(L,V)
. Then
\omega(x,y,z)=\varphi([x,y],z)-\rho(z)\varphi(x,y)

(L,[\cdot,\cdot,\cdot]=[\cdot,\cdot]\circ([\cdot,\cdot]\otimes Id_{L});\theta_{\rho})

\displaystyle\omega(x,y,z)=\varphi([x,y],z)-\rho(z)\varphi(x,y)

\displaystyle\quad\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\quad=-\Big(\varphi([y,x],z)-\rho(z)\varphi(y,z)\Big)=-\omega(y,x,z).

and

\displaystyle\omega(x,y,z)+\omega(y,z,x)+\omega(z,x,y)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\varphi([x,y],z)-\rho(z)\varphi(x,y)+\varphi([y,z],x)-\rho(x)\varphi(y,z)+\varphi([z,x],y)-\rho(y)\varphi(z,x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\partial_{\rho}(\varphi)(x,y,z)=0.

Now, for any

\displaystyle\delta^{3}(\omega)(x,y,z,t,e)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\omega(x,y,[z,t,e])+D_{\rho}(x,y)\omega(z,t,e)-\omega([x,y,z],t,e)-\omega(z,[x,y,t],e)

\displaystyle-\omega(z,t,[x,y,e])-\theta_{\rho}(t,e)\omega(x,y,z)+\theta_{\rho}(z,e)\omega(x,y,t)-D_{\rho}(z,t)\omega(x,y,e)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\varphi([x,y],[[z,t],e])-\rho([[z,t],e])\varphi(x,y)+\rho([x,y])\varphi([z,t],e)-\rho([x,y])\rho(e)\varphi(z,t)

\displaystyle-\varphi([[[x,y],z],t],e)+\rho(e)\varphi([[x,y],z],t)-\varphi([z,[[x,y],t]],e)+\rho(e)\varphi(z,[[x,y],t])

\displaystyle-\varphi([z,t],[[x,y],e])+\rho([[x,y],e])\varphi(z,t)-\rho(e)\rho(t)\varphi([x,y],z)+\rho(e)\rho(z)\varphi([x,y],t)

\displaystyle+\rho(e)\rho([t,z])\varphi(x,y)-\rho([z,t])\varphi([x,y],e)+\rho([z,t])\rho(e)\varphi(x,y)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\varphi([x,y],[[z,t],e])-\varphi([z,t],[[x,y],e])+\varphi([[x,y],[t,z]],e)+\rho([x,y])\varphi([z,t],e)

\displaystyle-\rho([z,t])\varphi([x,y],e)+\rho(e)\varphi([[x,y],z],t)+\rho(e)\varphi(z,[[x,y],t])-\rho(e)\rho(t)\varphi([x,y],z)

\displaystyle+\rho(e)\rho(z)\varphi([x,y],t)-\rho(e)\rho([x,y])\varphi(z,t)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\varphi([x,y],[[z,t],e])-\varphi([z,t],[[x,y],e])+\varphi([[x,y],[t,z]],e)+\rho([x,y])\varphi([z,t],e)

\displaystyle-\rho([z,t])\varphi([x,y],e)+\rho(e)\varphi([x,y],[z,t]).

Since

\delta^{3}(\omega)(x,y,z,t,e)=0

∎

Lemma 6.4
.

Let
\alpha\in C^{1}_{Lie}(L,V)
. Then

\delta^{1}(\alpha)(x,y,z)=\partial_{\rho}(\alpha)([x,y],z)-\rho(z)\partial_{\rho}(\alpha)(x,y).

(6.4)

Proof.

For any
x,y,z\in L
, we have

\displaystyle D_{\rho}(x,y)\alpha(z)-\theta_{\rho}(x,z)\alpha(y)+\theta_{\rho}(y,z)\alpha(x)-\alpha([x,y,z])

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\rho([x,y])\alpha(z)-\rho(z)\rho(x)\alpha(y)+\rho(z)\rho(y)\alpha(x)-\alpha([[x,y],z])

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\rho([x,y])\alpha(z)-\rho(z)\alpha([x,y])-\alpha([[x,y],z])

\displaystyle-\rho(z)\Big(\rho(x)\alpha(y)-\rho(y)\alpha(x)-\alpha([x,y])\Big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\partial_{\rho}(\alpha)([x,y],z)-\rho(z)\partial_{\rho}(\alpha)(x,y).

∎

Proposition 6.5
.

Let
\varphi_{1},\varphi_{2}\in Z^{2}_{Lie}(L,V)
. If
\varphi_{1},\varphi_{2}
are in the same cohomology class then
\omega_{1},\omega_{2}
defined by:

\omega_{i}(x,y,z)=\varphi_{i}([x,y],z)-\rho(z)\varphi_{i}(x,y),\;i=1,2

(6.5)

are in the same cohomology class of the associated L.t.s.

Proof.

Let
\varphi_{1},\varphi_{2}\in Z^{2}_{Lie}(L,V)
be two cocycles in the same cohomology class, that is

\varphi_{2}-\varphi_{1}=\partial_{\rho}(\alpha),\;\alpha\in C^{1}_{Lie}(L,V).

and

\omega_{i}(x,y,z)=\varphi_{i}([x,y],z)-\rho(z)\varphi_{i}(x,y),\;i=1,2.

According to Lemma
6.4

\displaystyle\omega_{2}(x,y,z)-\omega_{1}(x,y,z)=(\varphi_{2}-\varphi_{1})([x,y],z)-\rho(z)(\varphi_{2}-\varphi_{1})(x,y)

\omega_{1}

Proposition 6.6
.

Let

\mathcal{O}

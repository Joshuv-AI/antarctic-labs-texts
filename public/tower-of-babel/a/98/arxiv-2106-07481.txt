Blowup solutions for the shadow limit model of a singular Gierer-Meinhardt system with critical parameters Thanks: August 10, 2026


Abstract
. We consider a nonlocal parabolic PDE, which may be regarded as the standard semilinear heat equation with power nonlinearity, where the nonlinear term is divided by some Sobolev norm of the solution. Unlike the earlier work in [ DKZ ] where we consider a subcritical regime of parameters, we focus here on the critical regime, which is much more complicated. Our main result concerns the construction of a blow-up solution with the description of its asymptotic behavior. Our method relies on a formal approach, where we find an approximate solution. Then, adopting a rigorous approach, we linearize the equation around that approximate solution, and reduce the question to a finite dimensional problem. Using an argument based on index theory, we solve that finite-dimensional problem, and derive an exact solution to the full problem. We would like to point out that our constructed solution has a new blowup speed with a log \log correction term, which makes it different from the speed in the subcritical range of parameters and the standard heat equation.

Introduction

In the current work, we consider the following nonlocal parabolic equation

\left\{\begin{array}[]{rcl}\partial_{t}u&=&\Delta u-u+{\displaystyle\frac{u^{p}}{\left(\mathop{\,\hbox to0.0pt{-\hss}\!\!\int}\nolimits_{\Omega}u^{r}dr\right)^{\gamma}}}\quad\text{in}\quad\Omega\times(0,T),\\
\frac{\partial u}{\partial\nu}&=&0\text{ on }\partial\Omega\times(0,T)\\
u(0)&=&u_{0}\geq 0\quad\text{in }\quad\Omega,\end{array}\right.

(1.1)

\Omega
is a bounded domain in
\mathbb{R}^{N}
with smooth boundary, and the parameters satisfy
p>1,\gamma>0
and
r>0

1.1

[
KS17
,

]

[
KS17
]
, where in particular global-in-time existence as well as blowup results were derived according to the range of the involved parameters

and

The case of an isotropically evolving domain was also considered in
[
KBM21
]
, where an analytical and numerical study was delivered. In the aforementioned works, it was pointed out that for the limiting problem (
1.1
),
diffusion-driven (Turing) instability
occurs, an intriguing phenomenon which was introduced in the seminal paper
[
Tur52
]
, for a specific range of the parameters. In particular, the observed
diffusion-driven (Turing) instability
for (

p-r\gamma<1,

(1.2)

Now, we come back to the (nonlocal) equation (
1.1
) when
\gamma\neq 0
. Recently, inspired by the Type I construction mentioned above for equation (
1.3
), the authors of
[
DKZ
]
constructed a blowup solution to equation (
1.1
) via a rigorous analysis, giving the exact form of the
blowup profile

in some subcritical regime of the parameters, namely when

\frac{r}{p-1}<\frac{N}{2}\text{ and }\gamma r\neq p-1,

(1.5)

being in agreement with
Turing condition
(
1.2
). Specifically, under (
1.5
), the authors constructed a blowup solution to (
1.1
) with the blowup profile (pattern) as follows

u(x,t)\sim(\theta^{*})^{-\frac{1}{p-1}}(T-t)^{-\frac{1}{p-1}}\left(p-1+\frac{(p-1)^{2}}{4p}\frac{|x|^{2}}{(T-t)|\ln(T-t)|}\right)^{-\frac{1}{p-1}}\text{ as }t\to T,

(1.6)

where

\theta^{*}:=\lim_{t\to T}\left(\mathop{\,\hbox to0.0pt{-\hss}\!\!\int}\nolimits_{\Omega}u^{r}dr\right)^{-\gamma}.

Note that if the constant
\theta^{*}
is ignored, then the solution has the same structure of blowup solutions, as the solution constructed by
[
BK94
]
and
[
MZ97b
]
for the standard (local) equation (
1.3
).
In fact, the approach in that work imposed a special analysis to control the non-local term and make it converge to a nonzero constant. This way, we reasonably see that in this regime, equation (
1.1
) will behave like the standard equation (
1.3
). In particular, up to some natural scaling, both equations show the same profile
(see (
1.4
) and (
1.6
)). Inspired by the analysis in
[
DKZ
]
, it naturally arises the need for the consideration of another regime where the non-local term has a different limit, in particular it converges towards “zero”, leading hopefully to a different blow-up speed.
The following is our main result:

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
\Omega
be a smooth and bounded domain in
\mathbb{R}^{N}
containing the origin and consider equation (
1.1
) in the following critical regime:

\frac{r}{p-1}=\frac{N}{2}\text{ with }p\geq 3.

(1.7)

Then, there exists
\gamma_{0}
small such that for all
\gamma\in(0,\gamma_{0})
, we can construct initial data
u_{0}\geq 0
such that the solution of (
1.1
) blows up in finite time
T(u_{0})
, only at the origin. Moreover, we have the following blowup asymptotics:

(i)

Behavior of
\theta(t)=\left(\mathop{\,\hbox to0.0pt{-\hss}\!\!\int}\nolimits_{\Omega}u^{r}dr\right)^{-\gamma}
. It holds that

\theta(t)=\theta_{\infty}|\ln(T-t)|^{-\beta}\left(1+O\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{|\ln(T-t)|}}\right)\right),\text{ as }t\to T,

(1.8)

where

\theta_{\infty}=\left(\frac{|\Omega|\left(1+\frac{N}{2}\right)}{1-\frac{\gamma N}{2}}2b^{\frac{N}{2}}\right)^{\frac{\gamma}{1-\frac{\gamma N}{2}}},

\beta=\left(\frac{N}{2}+1\right)\frac{\gamma}{1-\frac{\gamma N}{2}},\;\;b=\frac{(p-1)^{2}}{4p}(1+\beta).

(ii)

The intermediate blowup profile
. For all
t\in(0,T)
, we have

\displaystyle\left\|(T-t)^{\frac{1}{p-1}}|\ln(T-t)|^{-\nu}u(\cdot,t)-(\theta_{\infty})^{-\frac{1}{p-1}}\varphi_{0}\left(\frac{|.|}{\sqrt{(T-t)|\ln(T-t)|}}\right)\right\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}

(1.9)

\displaystyle\hskip 199.16928pt\leq\frac{C}{1+\sqrt{|\ln(T-t)|}}.

where

\varphi_{0}\left(z\right)=\left(p-1+b|z|^{2}\right)^{-\frac{1}{p-1}}\text{ and }\nu=\frac{\beta}{p-1}.

(iii)

The final blowup profile
. It holds that

u(x,t)\to u^{*}(x)\in C^{2}(\Omega\setminus\{0\})
as
t\to T
, uniformly on compact sets of
\Omega\setminus\{0\}
. In particular, we have

u^{*}(x)\sim(\theta_{\infty})^{-\frac{1}{p-1}}\left[\frac{b}{2}\frac{|x|^{2}}{|\ln|x|}\right]^{-\frac{1}{p-1}}\left[2|\ln|x||\right]^{\nu}\text{ as }x\to 0.

(1.10)

Remark 1.2

(Stability)
.

Following the interpretation of
N+1
parameters for the blowup time and the blowup point, originally done in
[
MZ97b
]
, and then applied in
[
DKZ
]
, we can prove that behaviors (
1.9
) and (

) are stable under perturbation of initial data, the readers can find more details in Remark 2.3 of
[
DKZ
]
.

Remark 1.3

(On the smallness of
\gamma
)
.

We mention that in the proof of the Theorem, we need
\gamma
to be very small. This is due to the limitation of our method, which is inspired by the case of the standard equation (
1.3
) treated in
[
BK94
]
and
[
MZ97b
]
. However, regardless of the method, we suspect that the described behavior
will not occur when
\gamma
large, since
\theta
may act against blowup in equation (
1.1
) in that case.

Furthermore, we would like to point out that the condition on the smallness of parameter
\gamma
is consistent with analogous conditions on the exponents of non-local terms that guarantee finite-time blowup for parabolic and hyperbolic non-local problems, for more details see
[
BL97
,
Kav04
,
KE04
,
KN07
,
KS07
,
KT02a
,
KT02b
,
KT06
]
and references therein.

Remark 1.4

(Novelty)
.

The constructed solution in Theorem
1.1
has the following blowup speed

\|u(t)\|_{L^{\infty}}\sim\kappa(\theta_{\infty})^{-\frac{1}{p-1}}(T-1)^{-\frac{1}{p-1}}|\ln(T-t)|^{\nu},\kappa=(p-1)^{-\frac{1}{p-1}},

which is different from the subcritical regime treated in
[
DKZ
]
, and also from the case of the standard heat equation (
1.3
), where no
|\ln(T-t)|
correction appears.

In fact,
our problem is non-local, as it involves the following term

\theta(t)=\frac{1}{\left(\mathop{\,\hbox to0.0pt{-\hss}\!\!\int}\nolimits_{\Omega}u^{r}(t)dx\right)^{\gamma}}

and thus we first observe that once
u
blows up, the non-local integral will affect the solution’s asymptotics. Let us now mention the pioneering papers
[
DKZ
]
and
[
DZ19
]
, where the authors construct blowing up
and quenching solutions to equations involving non-local terms, and describe their blowing up and quenching profile respectively.
However, in the those works,
the authors only handled the regime where the non-local term stays away from
0
and infinity, in the sense that

\theta(t)\to\theta^{*}>0,\text{ as }t\to T.

Then, thanks to a natural scaling, the non-local term has no big impact on the solution’s blowing up or queching behavior.
In the current paper, we unveil a

new phenomenon
,
where

\theta(t)\to 0

which clearly affects the nonlinear term, in the sense that

\theta(t)u^{p}\ll u^{p}.

Naturally, the solution’s behavior will be more affected here than in the subcritical case treated in
[
DKZ
]
.
In the present paper, we mainly rely on the construction method of
[
MZ97a
]
(also
[
BK94
]
and
[
MZ97b
]
), however, we need new ideas to carefully control the behavior of
\theta(t)
, so that it fits the description given in statement
(i)
of Theorem
1.1
.

Remark 1.5
.

Remarkably we should have that

1-\frac{\gamma N}{2}>0\implies\gamma<\frac{2}{N}=\frac{p-1}{r}\implies p-r\gamma>1,

and thus we sit in the regime where
Turing condition
(
1.2
) is not satisfied. Therefore our main result given by Theorem
1.1

describes the occurrence of a
reaction driven
blowup.

2.
Formal approach

In this section, we aim at giving a formal approach which explains how the blowup profile in Theorem
1.1
is derived. Firstly, let us denote

\theta(t)=\frac{1}{\left(\mathop{\,\hbox to0.0pt{-\hss}\!\!\int}\nolimits_{\Omega}u^{r}(x,t)dx\right)^{\gamma}}.

(2.1)

Henceforth, we rewrite equation (
1.1
) by the following

\partial_{t}u=\Delta u-u+\theta(t)u^{p}.

(2.2)

Through a formal observation, we focus on the following three interesting situations:

\displaystyle\theta(t)

\displaystyle\to

\displaystyle 0\text{ as }t\to T,

(2.3)

\displaystyle\theta(t)

\displaystyle\to

\displaystyle\theta^{*}>0\text{ as }t\to T,

(2.4)

\displaystyle\theta(t)

\displaystyle\to

\displaystyle+\infty\text{ as }t\to T.

(2.5)

We point out that (
2.5
) is excluded by Theorem 3.1 and Remark 3.2 given in
[
KS17
]
. Recently, (
2.4
) was handled in
[
DKZ
]
under some sub-critical regimes (see more in (
1.5
)). Thus in the current work we provide analytically a construction of blowup solution, satisfying (
2.3
). In the following, we aim to deliver a formal study deriving the proper blowing behavior.

Here we outline our formal approach into two steps:

- Step 1: A prescribed asymptotic of the solution on
\Omega
. More precisely, we are inspired from
[
DKZ
]
, and
[
DZ19
]
(see also in
[
MZ97a
]
), to control the solution on the three domains under dynamical hypotheses on the non-local term
\theta(t)
, given in (
2.19
):

\bullet

Blowup region
P_{1}
defined by

P_{1}(t)=\left\{|x|\leq K_{0}\sqrt{(T-t)|\ln(T-t)|}\right\}.

(2.6)

\bullet

Intermediate region
P_{2}(t)
defined by

P_{2}(t)=\left\{\frac{K_{0}}{4}\sqrt{(T-t)|\ln(T-t)|}\leq|x|\leq\epsilon_{0}\right\},

(2.7)

for some
\epsilon_{0}
small enough.

\bullet

Regular domain
P_{3}
defined by

P_{3}=\{x\in\Omega\text{ such that }|x|\geq\epsilon_{0}\}.

(2.8)

- Step 2: We give a formal justification to show derived behaviors of
u
on each
P_{j},j=1,2,3
which is adapted to (
2.3
).

2.1.
Control of the solution’s asymptotics on
\Omega

As we mentioned above, in this paragraph we will provide explicit behavior of
u
on regions
P_{1},P_{2}
and
P_{3}
. Now let us assume that
u
is a blowing up solution in finite time
T
at the origin
0\in\Omega
, and (
2.3
) is satisfied.

Asymptotic of the intermediate profile in region
P_{1}

Let us introduce the following similarity variable:

y=\frac{x}{\sqrt{T-t}},\quad s=-\ln(T-t)\text{ and }W(y,s)=(T-t)^{\frac{1}{p-1}}U(x,t),

(2.9)

with

U(x,t):=\theta(t)^{\frac{1}{p-1}}u(x,t),

(2.10)

which by virtue of (
2.1
) entails

\displaystyle\theta(t)=\left(\mathop{\,\hbox to0.0pt{--\hss}\!\!\int}\nolimits_{\Omega}U^{r}\,dx\right)^{-\frac{\gamma}{1-\frac{r\gamma}{p-1}}}.

(2.11)

Next using equation (
1.1
),
U
reads

\partial_{t}U=\Delta U+U^{p}+\left(\frac{1}{p-1}\frac{\theta^{\prime}(t)}{\theta(t)}-1\right)U,

(2.12)

where
\theta(t)
is defined as in (
2.11
).
Using (
2.9
) and (
2.12
),
W
solves

\displaystyle\partial_{s}W=\Delta W-\frac{1}{2}y\cdot\nabla W-\frac{W}{p-1}+W^{p}+\left(\frac{1}{p-1}\frac{\bar{\theta}^{\prime}(s)}{\bar{\theta}(s)}-e^{-s}\right)W,

(2.13)

where

\bar{\theta}(s)=\theta(t(s)),\quad s=-\ln(T-t).

(2.14)

Regarding
\theta
’s evolution, we make a hypothesis as follows near the blowup point

\frac{\theta^{\prime}(t)}{\theta(t)}\ll U^{p-1},

(2.15)

then (
2.12
) is considered as a small perturbation to the following

\partial_{t}U=\Delta U+U^{p},

(2.16)

which has been studied thoroughly in
[
BK94
,
HV92b
,
MZ97b
]
, and the references therein. The authors in those works constructed a blowup solution to (
2.16
), satisfying

U\sim\kappa(T-t)^{-\frac{1}{p-1}}\text{ near the blowup region}\;P_{1},

from which in conjunction with (
2.15
) we derive

\frac{\theta^{\prime}(t)}{\theta(t)}\ll(T-t)^{-1},

(2.17)

and using the fact that
\bar{\theta}(s)=\theta(t)
then (
2.17
) yields

\displaystyle\frac{\bar{\theta}^{\prime}(s)}{\bar{\theta}(s)}\to 0,\text{ as }s\to+\infty.

We remark that the following situation is impossible:

\left|\frac{\bar{\theta}^{\prime}(s)}{\bar{\theta}(s)}\right|\leq\frac{C}{s^{1+\delta}},\text{ for some }\delta>0,

due to the fact that
\bar{\theta}\to 0
as
s\to+\infty.
However, it is hard to classify the asymptotic behavior of this quantity and so we only provide a special case so that (
2.3
) is valid. For example, we consider the following situation

\frac{\bar{\theta}^{\prime}(s)}{\bar{\theta}(s)}=-\beta s^{-1}+O(s^{-1-\delta}),

(2.18)

for some
\beta,\delta>0
. In particular, (
2.18
) has a special solution that

\displaystyle\bar{\theta}(s)=\theta_{\infty}s^{-\beta}\left(1+O\left(\frac{1}{s^{\delta}}\right)\right),\text{ as }s\to+\infty,

This also implies

\theta(t)=\theta_{\infty}|\ln(T-t)|^{-\beta}\left(1+O\left(\frac{1}{|\ln(T-t)|^{\delta}}\right)\right),\text{ as }t\to T.

(2.19)

We remark that (
2.19
) will rigidly be justified in subsection
2.2
.
Plugging (
2.18
) into (
2.13
), we observe that

\displaystyle\left(\frac{\bar{\theta}^{\prime}}{\bar{\theta}}-e^{-s}\right)W=-\frac{\beta}{p-1}\frac{W}{s}+\text{ ``lower order'' }.

Hence, we interested in considering the following proxy equation

\partial_{s}W=\Delta W-\frac{1}{2}y\cdot\nabla W-\frac{W}{p-1}+W^{p}-\frac{\beta}{s(p-1)}W,

(2.20)

for all
(y,s)\in\Omega_{s}\times[-\ln(T-t),+\infty)
and
\Omega_{s}=e^{\frac{s}{2}}\Omega
.

Note that there exists a space-independent solution of (
2.20
) in the following form

W=\kappa+O\left(\frac{1}{s}\right),

recalling that
\kappa=(p-1)^{-\frac{1}{p-1}}.

Now, we consider the linearization around the dominated part
\kappa
by

\bar{W}=W-\kappa,

which yields

\partial_{s}\bar{W}=\mathcal{L}\bar{W}+\bar{B}(\bar{W})-\frac{\beta(\kappa+\bar{W})}{(p-1)s},

(2.21)

where

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Delta-\frac{1}{2}y\cdot\nabla+Id,

\displaystyle\bar{B}(\bar{W})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(\bar{W}+\kappa)^{p}-\kappa^{p}-p\kappa^{p-1}\bar{W}.

In addition to that, we have the fact that for all
|\bar{W}|\leq 1

\left|\bar{B}(\bar{W})-\frac{p}{2\kappa}\bar{W}^{2}\right|\leq C|\bar{W}|^{3}.

We now assume that
\bar{W}
is radial and expressed by

\bar{W}(y,s)=\bar{W}_{0}(s)+\bar{W}_{2}(s)(|y|^{2}-2N)

(the idea behind this argument, i.e. that the solution approaches a radial profile, and
W_{2}(|y|^{2}-2N)
is dominated part, can be found in
[
TZ19
]
).
We expect to have
\bar{W}_{0},\bar{W}_{2}\to 0,\text{ as }s\to+\infty.

Projecting the above expansion on (
2.21
), we get

\displaystyle\bar{W}_{0}^{\prime}(s)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\bar{W}_{0}+\frac{p}{2\kappa}(\bar{W}_{0}^{2}+8N\bar{W}_{2}^{2})-\frac{\beta}{(p-1)s}(\kappa+\bar{W}_{0})+O(|\bar{W}_{0}|^{3}+|\bar{W}_{2}|^{3}),

\displaystyle\bar{W}_{2}^{\prime}(s)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{4p}{\kappa}\bar{W}_{2}^{2}+\frac{p}{\kappa}\bar{W}_{0}\bar{W}_{2}-\frac{\beta}{(p-1)s}\bar{W}_{2}+O(|\bar{W}_{0}|^{3}+|\bar{W}_{2}|^{3}).

Solving the above ODE system, we obtain

\displaystyle\bar{W}_{0}(s)

\displaystyle=

Operator

Let us point out that operator

where

and

The spectrum set is explicitly given by

\text{Spec}(\mathcal{L})=\left\{1-\frac{m}{2}\left|\right.m\in\mathbb{N}\right\}.

The eigenspace corresponding to
\lambda_{m}=1-\frac{m}{2}

\mathcal{E}_{m}=\left<h_{m_{1}}(y_{1}).h_{m_{2}}(y_{2})....h_{m_{N}}(y_{N})\left|\right.m_{1}+...+m_{N}=m\right>,

Potential

(ii)

\sup_{s\geq s_{\epsilon},\frac{|y|}{\sqrt{s}}\geq\mathcal{C}_{\epsilon}}\left|V(y,s)-\left(-\frac{p}{p-1}\right)\right|\leq\epsilon,

for some
\epsilon>0

Therefore, since the largest eigenvalue of
\mathcal{L}

then we have that
\mathcal{L}+V

Thus, we can easily control the solution in that region when

As it is evident form the above,

\mathcal{U}(x,\xi,\tau_{0})=(T-t(x))^{\frac{1}{p-1}}\theta^{\frac{1}{p-1}}(t(x))u(x,0),

and

\hat{\mathcal{U}}(x,\tau_{0}(x))=\left((p-1)(1-\int_{0}^{\tau_{0}(x)}\tilde{\theta}(\tau^{\prime})(\theta(t(x)))^{-1}d\tau^{\prime}\right)^{-\frac{1}{p-1}},

where

\tau_{0}(x)=\frac{-t(x)}{T-t(x)}\in[0,1].

The reader should bear in mind that

\theta(t(x))=\theta(0)\text{ if }t(x)\leq 0,

with
\theta(0)
satisfying (
6.1
). Now, we observe that for all
\tau^{\prime}\in[0,\tau_{0}(x)]
and
|x|\in\left[\frac{K_{0}}{4}\sqrt{T|\ln T|},\epsilon_{0}\right]

\tau^{\prime}\rho+t(x)\leq 0,

which implies

\displaystyle\hat{\mathcal{U}}(x,\tau_{0}(x))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left((p-1)(1-\int_{0}^{\tau_{0}(x)}\theta(0)(\theta(0))^{-1}d\tau^{\prime}+b\frac{K_{0}^{2}}{16}\right)^{-\frac{1}{p-1}}

(6.7)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left((p-1)\left(1-\tau_{0}(x)\right)+b\frac{K_{0}^{2}}{16}\right)^{-\frac{1}{p-1}}.\;\;

Now, we will write
\mathcal{U}(\tau_{0})
with
\tau_{0}(x)=\frac{-t(x)}{T-t(x)}
as follows

\displaystyle\mathcal{U}(x,\xi,\tau_{0})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(T-t(x))^{\frac{1}{p-1}}(\theta(t(x)))^{\frac{1}{p-1}}u(x+\xi\sqrt{T-t(x)},0)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\frac{(T-t(x))\theta(t(x))}{T\theta_{\infty}|\ln T|^{-\beta}}\right)^{\frac{1}{p-1}}\left(p-1+b\frac{|x+\xi\sqrt{T-t(x)}|^{2}}{T|\ln T|}\right)^{-\frac{1}{p-1}}\chi_{1}(x+\xi\sqrt{T-t(x)},0)

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle(T-t(x))^{\frac{1}{p-1}}\theta^{\frac{1}{p-1}}(t(x))H^{*}(x+\xi\sqrt{T-t(x)})(1-\chi_{1}(x+\xi\sqrt{T-t(x)},0))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle I\chi_{1}(x+\xi\sqrt{T-t(x)},0)+II(1-\chi_{1}(x+\xi\sqrt{T-t(x)},0)).

Let us mention that, our functions
\mathcal{U}(x,\xi,\tau_{0})
and
\hat{\mathcal{U}}(x,\tau_{0}(x))
are similar to the ones at
[
MZ97a
, page 1531]
. So, we can apply the process to prove the following estimates (see more details in that work):

- For all
|x|\in\left[\frac{K_{0}}{4}\sqrt{T|\ln T|},2K_{0}\sqrt{T|\ln T|}|\ln T|^{\frac{1}{2}}\right]
and
|\xi|\leq 2\alpha_{0}\sqrt{|\ln\rho(x)|}

\left|I-\hat{\mathcal{U}}(x,\tau_{0}(x))\right|\leq\frac{\delta_{3}}{2}.

(6.8)

- For all
|x|\in\left[K_{0}\sqrt{T|\ln T|}|\ln T|^{\frac{1}{2}},\epsilon_{0}\right]
and
|\xi|\leq 2\alpha_{0}\sqrt{|\ln\rho(x)|}

\left|II-\hat{\mathcal{U}}(x,\tau_{0})\right|\leq\frac{\delta_{3}}{2}.

(6.9)

From (
6.8
) and (
6.9
), we conclude

\left|\mathcal{U}(x,\xi,\tau_{0})-\hat{\mathcal{U}}(x,\tau_{0})\right|\leq\delta_{3}.

In addition to that, the technique at
[
MZ97a
, pages 1533-1535]
can be applied to prove the following:

\left|\nabla_{\xi}\mathcal{U}(x,\xi,\tau_{0})\right|\leq\frac{C_{3}}{\sqrt{|\ln\rho(x)|}}.

Finally, the proof of the required estimates in
P_{2}(0)
immediately follows.

For item
(II)
obviously arises from item
(I)
, see more details in
[
TZ19
]
. We finish the proof of the Proposition.

Now we consider

Lemma C.2

(Bubble integrals)
.

Let us consider
N>0,k\in\mathbb{N}^{*},p>1,
and
b>0
, we now define

I_{b,p,N,k}=\int_{0}^{\infty}(p-1+b\xi^{2})^{-k-\frac{N}{2}}\xi^{N-1}d\xi.

Then, for all
k\geq 1
, we have

\displaystyle I_{b,p,N,1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{(p-1)N}b^{-\frac{N}{2}},

(C.2)

\displaystyle I_{b,p,N,k+1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{p-1}\frac{k}{k+\frac{N}{2}}I_{b,p,N,k}.

(C.3)

Proof.

The result follows the integration by parts. Indeed, we firstly handle (
C.3
). We consider
k\geq 1
, then, we write

I_{b,p,N,k}=\int_{0}^{\infty}(p-1+b\xi^{2})^{-k-\frac{N}{2}}\xi^{N-1}d\xi.

Using integral by parts with
u=(p-1+b\xi^{2})^{-k-\frac{N}{2}},dv=\xi^{N-1}d\xi
, we obtain

\displaystyle I_{b,p,N,k}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{2}{N}\left(k+\frac{N}{2}\right)\int_{0}^{\infty}(p-1+b\xi^{2})^{-k-1-\frac{N}{2}}b\xi^{2}d\xi

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{2}{N}\left(k+\frac{N}{2}\right)\left[\int_{0}^{\infty}(p-1+b\xi^{2})^{-k-\frac{N}{2}}\xi^{N-1}d\xi-(p-1)\int_{0}^{\infty}(p-1+b\xi^{2})^{-k-1-\frac{N}{2}}\xi^{N-1}d\xi\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{2}{N}\left(k+\frac{N}{2}\right)(I_{b,p,N,k}-(p-1)I_{b,p,N,k+1},

which concludes

\displaystyle I_{b,p,N,k+1}=\frac{1}{p-1}\frac{k}{k+\frac{N}{2}}I_{b,p,N,k}.

Thus, (
C.3
) follows.

Next, we prove to (
C.2
). Let
K_{0}
be an arbitrary positive constant and we consider the following integral

\int_{0}^{K_{0}}(p-1+b\xi^{2})^{-\frac{N}{2}}\xi^{N-1}d\xi.

Using integration by parts, we obtain

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{K_{0}}(p-1+b\xi^{2})^{-\frac{N}{2}}\xi^{N-1}d\xi

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\xi^{N}}{N}(p-1+b\xi^{2})^{-\frac{N}{2}}\left.\right|_{0}^{K_{0}}-(p-1)\int_{0}^{K_{0}}(p-1+b\xi^{2})^{-\frac{N}{2}-1}\xi^{N-1}d\xi

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{K_{0}}(p-1+b\xi^{2})^{-\frac{N}{2}}\xi^{N-1}d\xi.

Taking
K_{0}\to+\infty
, we obtain

\displaystyle I_{b,p,N,1}=\frac{1}{p-1}\frac{1}{Nb^{\frac{N}{2}}}.

Finally, we conclude (
C.2
).
∎

Polyharmonic Green Functions and Nonlocal BMS Transformations of a Free Scalar Field
Carles Batlle, Víctor Campello, Joaquim Gomis

Abstract
We express the nonlocal BMS charges of a free massless Klein-Gordon scalar field in 2 + 1 2+1 in terms of the Green functions of the polyharmonic operators. Using the properties of these Green functions, we are able to discuss the asymptotic behaviour of the fields that ensures the existence of the charges, and prove that one obtains a realization of the 2 + 1 2+1 BMS algebra in canonical phase space. We also discuss the transformations in configuration space, and show that in this case the algebra closes only up to skew-symmetric combinations of the equations of motion. The formulation of the charges, in terms of Green functions, opens the way to the generalization of the formalism to other dimensions and systems.

, and the transformations are

\displaystyle\delta^{B}_{1}\phi(x)

\displaystyle=t(\partial_{x_{1}}+i\partial_{x_{2}})\phi(x)+(x_{1}+ix_{2})\dot{\phi}(x),

(4.1)

\displaystyle\delta_{{2\ell}}\phi(x)

\displaystyle=\int\text{d}^{2}y\,\dot{\phi}(y)(\partial_{x_{1}}+i\partial_{x_{2}})^{2\ell}G_{\ell}(\vec{x}-\vec{y}).

(4.2)

Since these are functional variations, the transformation of
\dot{\phi}
is obtained by derivation, and one gets

\displaystyle\delta^{B}_{1}\dot{\phi}(x)

\displaystyle=(\partial_{x_{1}}+i\partial_{x_{2}})\phi(x)+t(\partial_{x_{1}}+i\partial_{x_{2}})\dot{\phi}(x)+(x_{1}+ix_{2})\ddot{\phi}(x),

(4.3)

\displaystyle\delta_{{2\ell}}\dot{\phi}(x)

\displaystyle=\int\text{d}^{2}y\,\ddot{\phi}(y)(\partial_{x_{1}}+i\partial_{x_{2}})^{2\ell}G_{\ell}(\vec{x}-\vec{y}).

(4.4)

Now we can compute the compositions of transformations

\displaystyle\delta_{{2\ell}}\delta^{B}_{1}\phi(x)

\displaystyle=t(\partial_{x_{1}}+i\partial_{x_{2}})\delta_{2\ell}\phi(x)+(x_{1}+ix_{2})\delta_{{2\ell}}\dot{\phi}(x)

\displaystyle\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!=t\int d^{2}y\,\dot{\phi}(y)(\partial_{x_{1}}+i\partial_{x_{2}})^{2\ell+1}G_{\ell}(\vec{x}-\vec{y})+(x_{1}+ix_{2})\int d^{2}y\,\ddot{\phi}(y)(\partial_{x_{1}}+i\partial_{x_{2}})^{2\ell}G_{\ell}(\vec{x}-\vec{y}),

(4.5)

and

\displaystyle\delta^{B}_{1}\delta_{{2\ell}}\phi(x)

\displaystyle=\int d^{2}y\,\delta^{B}_{1}\dot{\phi}(y)(\partial_{x_{1}}+i\partial_{x_{2}})^{2\ell}G_{\ell}(\vec{x}-\vec{y})

\displaystyle=\int d^{2}y\,(\partial_{y_{1}}+i\partial_{y_{2}})\phi(y)(\partial_{x_{1}}+i\partial_{x_{2}})^{2\ell}G_{\ell}(\vec{x}-\vec{y})

\displaystyle+t\int d^{2}y\,(\partial_{y_{1}}+i\partial_{y_{2}})\dot{\phi}(y)(\partial_{x_{1}}+i\partial_{x_{2}})^{2\ell}G_{\ell}(\vec{x}-\vec{y})

\displaystyle+\int d^{2}y\,(y_{1}+iy_{2})\ddot{\phi}(y)(\partial_{x_{1}}+i\partial_{x_{2}})^{2\ell}G_{\ell}(\vec{x}-\vec{y}).

(4.6)

The first term in (
4.6
),

\int d^{2}y\,(\partial_{y_{1}}+i\partial_{y_{2}})\phi(y)(\partial_{x_{1}}+i\partial_{x_{2}})^{2\ell}G_{\ell}(\vec{x}-\vec{y})=\int d^{2}y\,\phi(y)(\partial_{x_{1}}+i\partial_{x_{2}})^{2\ell+1}G_{\ell}(\vec{x}-\vec{y})

is just
-\delta_{2\ell+1}\phi(x)
and, assembling the remaining terms, the commutator of the two transformations turns out to be

\displaystyle[\delta^{B}_{1},\delta_{{2\ell}}]\phi(x)

\displaystyle=-\delta_{{2\ell+1}}\phi(x)-\int d^{2}y\,\big((x_{1}-y_{1})+i(x_{2}-y_{2})\big)\ddot{\phi}(y)(\partial_{x_{1}}+i\partial_{x_{2}})^{2\ell}G_{\ell}(\vec{x}-\vec{y}).

(4.7)

Now we add and subtract
\vec{\nabla}^{2}\phi
and obtain

\displaystyle[\delta^{B}_{1},\delta_{{2\ell}}]\phi(x)=-\delta_{{2\ell+1}}\phi(x)-\int\text{d}^{2}y\,\big((x_{1}-y_{1})+i(x_{2}-y_{2})\big)\vec{\nabla}^{2}_{y}\phi(y)(\partial_{x_{1}}+i\partial_{x_{2}})^{2\ell}G_{\ell}(\vec{x}-\vec{y})

(4.8)

\displaystyle-

\displaystyle\int\text{d}^{2}y\,\big((x_{1}-y_{1})+i(x_{2}-y_{2})\big)\big(\ddot{\phi}(y)-\vec{\nabla}^{2}_{y}\phi(y)\big)(\partial_{x_{1}}+i\partial_{x_{2}})^{2\ell}G_{\ell}(\vec{x}-\vec{y})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\delta_{{2\ell+1}}\phi(x)-\int\text{d}^{2}y\,\big((x_{1}-y_{1})+i(x_{2}-y_{2})\phi(y)(\partial_{x_{1}}+i\partial_{x_{2}})^{2\ell}(\partial_{x_{1}}-i\partial_{x_{2}})G_{\ell}(\vec{x}-\vec{y})

\displaystyle-

\displaystyle 2\int\mbox{d}^{2}y\,\phi(y)(\partial_{x_{1}}+i\partial_{x_{2}})^{2\ell+1}G_{\ell}(\vec{x}-\vec{y})-\int\text{d}^{2}y\,F_{\ell}(x,y)\big(\ddot{\phi}(y)-\vec{\nabla}^{2}_{y}\phi(y)\big),

where we have integrated twice by parts the term
\vec{\nabla}^{2}\phi
and defined

F_{\ell}(x,y)=\big((x_{1}-y_{1})+i(x_{2}-y_{2})\big)(\partial_{x_{1}}+i\partial_{x_{2}})^{2\ell}G_{\ell}(\vec{x}-\vec{y}).

(4.9)

The third term in (
4.8
) is
2\delta_{2\ell+1}\phi(x)
, while the second term, following the same steps that led to (
C.6
), becomes
2(\ell-1)\delta_{2\ell+1}\phi(x)
. Putting everything together one has

\displaystyle[\delta^{B}_{1},\delta_{{2\ell}}]\phi(x)

\displaystyle=(2\ell-1)\delta_{2\ell+1}\phi(x)-\int\text{d}^{2}y\,F_{\ell}(x,y)\big(\ddot{\phi}(y)-\vec{\nabla}^{2}_{y}\phi(y)\big).

(4.10)

Taking into account that, for any transformations generated by charges
A
,
B
, one has that
[\delta_{A},\delta_{B}]\phi=-\delta_{\{A,B\}}\phi
, the first term in (
4.10
) is the one expected from the BMS algebra and, because
F_{\ell}(x,y)=-F_{\ell}(y,x)
, the extra term is a skew-symmetric linear combination of the equations of motion,

\delta_{\ell,\text{trivial}}\phi(x)=\int\text{d}^{2}y\,F_{\ell}(x,y)\big(\ddot{\phi}(y)-\vec{\nabla}^{2}_{y}\phi(y)\big),

(4.11)

which is a trivial symmetry transformation of any system.
Notice that, for
\ell=0
,
F_{\ell}(x,y)=0
and, as it must be, the extra term is not present for the standard commutator of a time translation and a Lorentz boost.

Similar results are obtained for the other commutators of transformations, and hence the algebra closes on-shell in a consistent way.

5
Discussion and outlook

We have obtained an explicit expression for the BMS supertranslation charges of free massless scalar real scalar field in
2+1
space-time, in terms of the Green functions of the polyharmonic operator.

We work first in phase space, and discuss the asymptotic behaviour of the fields that ensures the existence of the charges, as well as that of the symplectic form associated to the Poisson brackets.

The conservation of the charges only depends on general symmetry properties of the involved functions, but the commutative character of the algebra satisfied by these charges relies on a convolution property of the Green functions. Finally, the correct algebra with the Lorentz generators is obtained using more specific properties of the polyharmonic Green functions.

We also discuss the closure of the transformations in configuration space, and it turns out that the correct algebra is obtained modulo transformations given by skew-symmetric combinations of the equations of motion, which are trivial symmetry transformations of any system.

The form of the supertranslation charges presented in this paper, in terms of Green functions of an appropriate operator, opens the way to the generalization to other cases and/or dimensions. For instance, one could consider the extension of the results to the
3+1
space-time dimension case, or to the case of the generators of superrotations which were also constructed in
[
17
]
.

Another subject worth of study is whether the supertranslation transformations of the field
\phi
can be interpreted as a base transformation of the coordinates in Minkowski space, or if additional coordinates, each associated to a supertranslation, must be introduced.

Appendix A
Asymptotic behaviour of the polyharmonic Green functions

The polyharmonic Green function
G_{\ell}
has the form

G_{\ell}(x)=A_{\ell}^{(0)}|\vec{x}|^{2\ell-2}\log|\vec{x}|+B_{\ell}^{(0)}|\vec{x}|^{2\ell-2},

(A.1)

where the constants
A_{\ell}^{(0)}
and
B_{\ell}^{(0)}
can be read from (
2.5
). Successive applications of
\partial_{x_{1}}+i\partial_{x_{2}}
yield expressions of the same form, with decreasing powers of
|\vec{x}|
, until one reaches

(\partial_{x_{1}}+i\partial_{x_{2}})^{\ell-1}G_{\ell}(\vec{x})=(x_{1}+ix_{2})^{\ell-1}\left(A_{\ell}^{(\ell-1)}\log|\vec{x}|+B_{\ell}^{(\ell-1)}\right).

(A.2)

From this point the derivatives cease to content the
\log
term and one can see that the derivative of order
2\ell
is of the form

(\partial_{x_{1}}+i\partial_{x_{2}})^{2\ell}G_{\ell}(\vec{x})=C^{(2\ell)}(x_{1}+ix_{2})^{2\ell}\frac{1}{|\vec{x}|^{2\ell+2}},

(A.3)

with a constant
C^{(2\ell)}
. This is a rational function of
x_{1}
,
x_{2}
with asymptotic behaviour

(\partial_{x_{1}}+i\partial_{x_{2}})^{2\ell}G_{\ell}(\vec{x})\sim\frac{1}{|\vec{x}|^{2}}\quad\forall\ell\geq 1\quad\text{for $|\vec{x}|\to\infty$},

(A.4)

which is independent of
\ell\geq 1
.
This allows us to study the conditions that must be imposed on the fields so that the supertranslation charges are finite. A general supertraslation charge
Q_{\ell}
,
\ell\geq 1
, has integrals of the form

Q_{\ell}(t)=\int\text{d}^{2}x\text{d}^{2}yF(t,\vec{x})G(t,\vec{y})(\partial_{x_{1}}+i\partial_{x_{2}})^{2\ell}G_{\ell}(\vec{x}-\vec{y})

(A.5)

where
F
and
G
are either
\pi
or first order derivatives of
\phi
. Performing a change of variables

\vec{x}=\vec{y}+\vec{r}
and using the asymptotic behaviour (
A.4
), the existence of the charge reduces to the existence of the integral

\int\mbox{d}^{2}r\mbox{d}^{2}yF(\vec{y}+\vec{r})G(\vec{y})\frac{1}{r^{2}}.

(A.6)

Let us assume now that the fields
F
and
G
behave, for large argument, as

F(\vec{y}+\vec{r})\sim\frac{\bar{F}}{|\vec{y}+\vec{r}|^{\alpha}},\quad G(\vec{y})\sim\frac{\bar{G}}{|\vec{y}|^{\beta}},

(A.7)

with
\bar{F}
,
\bar{G}
depending on the angular variable and time. This leads to the study of the integral

\int\mbox{d}^{2}\Omega\,\bar{F}\bar{G}\int\,r\mbox{d}r\,y\mbox{d}y\,\frac{1}{|\vec{y}+\vec{r}|^{\alpha}|\vec{y}|^{\beta}r^{2}},

(A.8)

where
\mbox{d}^{2}\Omega
is the angular measure. Performing a change to polar coordinates in
\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+}
for the
\mbox{d}r\mbox{d}y
measure, one finally gets

\int\mbox{d}^{3}\Omega\,\bar{F}\bar{G}\int\,\rho\mbox{d}\rho\,\frac{1}{\rho^{\alpha+\beta}},

(A.9)

with
\mbox{d}^{3}\Omega
including the additional integration over the angular coordinate of the polar change of variables. For this integral to converge it is necessary that

\alpha+\beta>2.

(A.10)

Considering the forms of
F
and
G
for the different supertranslation charges, one concludes that the asymptotic behaviour of the fields which guarantees the existence of all the
Q_{\ell}
is the one given in
(
2.21
) and (
2.22
).

Appendix B
Brackets between the supertranslation charges

Consider two arbitrary supertranslation charges

\displaystyle Q_{\ell}(t)

\displaystyle=\int\text{d}^{2}x\text{d}^{2}y\,(f_{\ell}(\vec{x}-\vec{y})\pi(x)\phi(y)+\dfrac{1}{2}g_{\ell}(\vec{x}-\vec{y})\pi(x)\pi(y)-\dfrac{1}{2}h_{\ell}(\vec{x}-\vec{y})\phi(x)\phi(y)),

(B.1)

\displaystyle Q_{m}(t)

\displaystyle=\int\text{d}^{2}z\text{d}^{2}w\,(f_{m}(\vec{z}-\vec{w})\pi(z)\phi(w)+\dfrac{1}{2}g_{m}(\vec{z}-\vec{w})\pi(z)\pi(w)-\dfrac{1}{2}h_{m}(\vec{z}-\vec{w})\phi(z)\phi(w)).

(B.2)

Using standard Poisson brackets, one gets

\displaystyle\{Q_{\ell}(t),Q_{m}(t)\}

\displaystyle=\int\text{d}^{2}x\text{d}^{2}y\text{d}^{2}z\,\Big(f_{\ell}(\vec{x}-\vec{y})f_{m}(\vec{y}-\vec{z})\pi(x)\phi(z)-f_{\ell}(\vec{x}-\vec{y})f_{m}(\vec{z}-\vec{x})\phi(y)\pi(z)

\displaystyle+f_{\ell}(\vec{x}-\vec{y})g_{m}(\vec{y}-\vec{z})\pi(x)\pi(z)+f_{\ell}(\vec{x}-\vec{y})h_{m}(\vec{x}-\vec{z})\phi(y)\phi(z)

\displaystyle-g_{\ell}(\vec{x}-\vec{y})f_{m}(\vec{z}-\vec{x})\pi(y)\pi(z)+g_{\ell}(\vec{x}-\vec{y})h_{m}(\vec{x}-\vec{z})\pi(y)\phi(z)

\displaystyle-h_{\ell}(\vec{x}-\vec{y})f_{m}(\vec{x}-\vec{z})\phi(y)\phi(z)-h_{\ell}(\vec{x}-\vec{y})g_{m}(\vec{x}-\vec{z})\phi(y)\pi(z)\Big).

(B.3)

Let us consider first the case
m=0
, that is
Q_{m}(t)=Q_{0}(t)=H(t)
, for which
f_{0}(\vec{x}-\vec{y})=0
,
g_{0}(\vec{x}-\vec{y})=\delta(\vec{x}-\vec{y})
and
h_{0}(\vec{x}-\vec{y})=\vec{\nabla}^{2}_{x}\delta(\vec{x}-\vec{y})
. After integration by parts one gets

\displaystyle\dot{Q}_{\ell}(t)

\displaystyle=\{Q_{\ell}(t),H(t)\}=\int\text{d}^{2}x\text{d}^{2}y\,\Big(f_{\ell}(\vec{x}-\vec{y})\pi(x)\pi(y)+\vec{\nabla}^{2}_{x}f_{\ell}(\vec{x}-\vec{y})\phi(y)\phi(x)

\displaystyle+\vec{\nabla}^{2}_{x}g_{\ell}(\vec{x}-\vec{y})\pi(y)\phi(x)-h_{\ell}(\vec{x}-\vec{y})\phi(y)\pi(x)\Big).

(B.4)

The first two terms are zero, each by itself, due to the skew-symmetry of
f_{\ell}
and its even-order derivatives, while the two last terms cancel each other after using
\vec{\nabla}^{2}(x)g_{\ell}(\vec{x}-\vec{y})=h_{\ell}(\vec{x}-\vec{y})
and the symmetry of
h_{\ell}
.
This shows that the supertraslation charges are conserved by virtue of the symmetry properties of
f_{\ell}
,
g_{\ell}
and
h_{\ell}
, and the relation between
g_{\ell}
and
h_{\ell}
, without using the explicit form of these functions in terms of the polyharmonic Green functions.

For general
\ell
and
m
one must consider the different cases separately.

1.

\ell
and
m
odd. In this case
g_{\ell}=h_{\ell}=g_{m}=h_{m}=0
and, after renaming the variables of integration in the first non-zero contribution,

\displaystyle\{Q_{\ell}(t),Q_{m}(t)\}

\displaystyle=\int\text{d}^{2}x\text{d}^{2}y\text{d}^{2}z\,\Big(f_{\ell}(\vec{z}-\vec{x})f_{m}(\vec{x}-\vec{y})-f_{\ell}(\vec{x}-\vec{y})f_{m}(\vec{z}-\vec{x})\Big)\phi(y)\pi(z).

(B.5)

2.

\ell
even and
m
odd. Now
f_{\ell}=0
and
g_{m}=h_{m}=0
, and the result can be written as

\displaystyle\{Q_{\ell}(t),Q_{m}(t)\}

\displaystyle=-\int\text{d}^{2}x\text{d}^{2}y\text{d}^{2}z\,g_{\ell}(\vec{x}-\vec{y})f_{m}(\vec{z}-\vec{x})\pi(y)\pi(z)

\displaystyle-\int\text{d}^{2}x\text{d}^{2}y\text{d}^{2}z\,h_{\ell}(\vec{x}-\vec{y})f_{m}(\vec{x}-\vec{z})\phi(y)\phi(z).

(B.6)

3.

\ell
and
m
even. We have
f_{\ell}=f_{m}=0
and (
B.3
) boils down to

\displaystyle\{Q_{\ell}(t),Q_{m}(t)\}

\displaystyle=\int\text{d}^{2}x\text{d}^{2}y\text{d}^{2}z\,\Big(g_{\ell}(\vec{x}-\vec{z})h_{m}(\vec{x}-\vec{y})-h_{\ell}(\vec{x}-\vec{y})g_{m}(\vec{x}-\vec{z})\Big)\phi(y)\pi(z).

(B.7)

The elementary symmetry properties used up to now are not enough to show that the above expressions are actually zero. In order to do so, one must use the fact that the functions
f_{\ell}
,
g_{\ell}
and
h_{\ell}
can be written in terms of Green functions that obey the convolution property (
2.7
).

Let us prove, for instance, that the first term in (
B.6
) is zero. Changing
\ell\to 2\ell
and
m\to 2m+1
one has, assuming
l>0
,
m>0

\displaystyle-\int\text{d}^{2}x\text{d}^{2}y\text{d}^{2}z\,g_{\ell}(\vec{x}-\vec{y})f_{m}(\vec{z}-\vec{x})\pi(y)\pi(z)\rightarrow

\displaystyle-

\displaystyle\int\text{d}^{2}x\text{d}^{2}y\text{d}^{2}z\,(\partial_{x_{1}}+i\partial_{x_{2}})^{2\ell}G_{\ell}(\vec{x}-\vec{y})(\partial_{x_{1}}+i\partial_{x_{2}})^{2m+1}G_{m}(\vec{x}-\vec{z})\pi(y)\pi(z)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\int\text{d}^{2}x\text{d}^{2}y\text{d}^{2}z\,G_{\ell}(\vec{x}-\vec{y})G_{m}(\vec{x}-\vec{z})(\partial_{y_{1}}+i\partial_{y_{2}})^{2\ell}\pi(y)(\partial_{z_{1}}+i\partial_{z_{2}})^{2m+1}\pi(z)

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle(\ref{convGG})}}{{=}}

\displaystyle-\int\text{d}^{2}y\text{d}^{2}zG_{\ell+m}(\vec{y}-\vec{z})(\partial_{y_{1}}+i\partial_{y_{2}})^{2\ell}\pi(y)(\partial_{z_{1}}+i\partial_{z_{2}})^{2m+1}\pi(z)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\int\text{d}^{2}y\text{d}^{2}z(\partial_{y_{1}}+i\partial_{y_{2}})^{2\ell+2m+1}G_{\ell+m}(\vec{y}-\vec{z})\pi(y)\pi(z)=0

due to the skew-symmetry of
(\partial_{y_{1}}+i\partial_{y_{2}})^{2\ell+2m+1}G_{\ell+m}(\vec{y}-\vec{z})
, and the second term of (
B.6
) can also be shown to be zero using the same manipulations. Notice that the same reasoning can be used for
\ell
and/or
m
negative, since this amounts to change some

to

Using the same techniques and the convolution property, the two terms which appear in (

Appendix C
Detailed computation of some Poisson brackets

\displaystyle\{L_{0}(t),Q_{2\ell}(t)\}=

One has that

(x_{1}\partial_{x_{2}}-x_{2}\partial_{x_{1}})G_{\ell}(\vec{x}-\vec{z})=G^{\prime}_{\ell}(|\vec{x}-\vec{z}|)\frac{-x_{1}z_{2}+x_{2}z_{1}}{|\vec{x}-\vec{z}|}

\displaystyle\{L_{0}(t),Q_{2\ell}(t)\}

\displaystyle=-2\ell\int\text{d}^{2}x\text{d}^{2}z{\cal H}(x,z)(\partial_{x_{1}}+i\partial_{x_{2}})^{2\ell}G_{\ell}(\vec{x}-\vec{z})

\displaystyle=-2\ell Q_{2\ell}(t),\quad\ell\geq 0.

(C.3)

Let us compute now the Poisson bracket of the boost generator
L_{1}(t)
with an even-order supertranslation charge,

\displaystyle\{L_{1}(t),Q_{2\ell}(t)\}=

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int\text{d}^{2}x\text{d}^{2}y\text{d}^{2}z\Big\{t\pi(x)(\partial_{x_{1}}+i\partial_{x_{2}})\phi(x)+(x_{1}+ix_{2}){\cal H}(x),{\cal H}(y,z)\Big\}(\partial_{y_{1}}+i\partial_{y_{2}})^{2\ell}G_{\ell}(\vec{y}-\vec{z}).

\displaystyle\{L_{1}(t),Q_{2\ell}(t)\}

\displaystyle\quad-\phi(x)\pi(z)(\partial_{x_{1}}+i\partial_{x_{2}})^{2\ell}(\partial_{x_{1}}+i\partial_{x_{2}})G_{\ell}(\vec{x}-\vec{z})\Big).

(C.5)

The second term is just

\displaystyle\{L_{1}(t),Q_{2\ell}(t)\}=-Q_{2\ell+1}(t)

\displaystyle\!+\int\text{d}^{2}x\text{d}^{2}z\Big(\pi(x)\phi(z)(\partial_{x_{1}}+i\partial_{x_{2}})^{2\ell+1}((x_{1}-z_{1})+i(x_{2}-z_{2}))(\partial_{x_{1}}-i\partial_{x_{2}})G_{\ell}(\vec{x}-\vec{z})\Big).

Since we are considering

(the case

Appendix D
Some identities satisfied by the polyharmonic Green functions

From (
2.5
) one has

\displaystyle(\partial_{x_{1}}-i\partial_{x_{2}})G_{\ell}(\vec{x}-\vec{y})

\displaystyle=\frac{|\vec{x}-\vec{y}|^{2(\ell-2)}}{(\ell-1)[(\ell-2)!]^{2}2^{2\ell-2}\pi}((x_{1}-y_{1})-i(x_{2}-y_{2}))\big(\log|\vec{x}-\vec{y}|-H_{\ell-1}\big)

\displaystyle+\frac{|\vec{x}-\vec{y}|^{2(\ell-2)}}{[(\ell-1)!]^{2}2^{2\ell-1}\pi}((x_{1}-y_{1})-i(x_{2}-y_{2})).

(D.1)

Assuming
\ell>1
and using
H_{\ell-1}=H_{\ell-2}+1/(\ell-1)
and re-arranging terms, this can be rewritten as

\displaystyle(\partial_{x_{1}}-i\partial_{x_{2}})G_{\ell}(\vec{x}-\vec{y})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2(\ell-1)}((x_{1}-y_{1})-i(x_{2}-y_{2}))G_{\ell-1}(\vec{x}-\vec{y})

\displaystyle-\frac{|\vec{x}-\vec{y}|^{2(\ell-2)}}{[(\ell-1)!]^{2}2^{2\ell-1}\pi}((x_{1}-y_{1})-i(x_{2}-y_{2})),

(D.2)

which is a recurrence relation valid for
\ell>1
.
Multiplying by
(x_{1}-y_{1})+i(x_{2}-y_{2})
one gets

\displaystyle((x_{1}-y_{1})+i(x_{2}-y_{2}))(\partial_{x_{1}}-i\partial_{x_{2}})G_{\ell}(\vec{x}-\vec{y})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2(\ell-1)}|\vec{x}-\vec{y}|^{2}G_{\ell-1}(\vec{x}-\vec{y})

\displaystyle-\frac{|\vec{x}-\vec{y}|^{2(\ell-1)}}{[(\ell-1)!]^{2}2^{2\ell-1}\pi},

(D.3)

which, except for polynomial terms, has the functional dependence of
G_{\ell}
. Indeed, using again the relation between
H_{\ell-1}
and
H_{\ell-2}
, one obtains

\displaystyle((x_{1}-y_{1})+i(x_{2}-y_{2}))(\partial_{x_{1}}-i\partial_{x_{2}})G_{\ell}(\vec{x}-\vec{y})=2(\ell-1)G_{\ell}(\vec{x}-\vec{y})+\frac{|\vec{x}-\vec{y}|^{2(\ell-1)}}{[(\ell-1)!]^{2}2^{2\ell-1}\pi}.

(D.4)

Although (
D.4
) has been obtained under the assumption that
\ell>1
it can be checked by direct computation that it is also valid for
\ell=1
.

In the computations in Section
3
the left-hand side of this identity appears with derivatives
\partial_{x_{1}}+i\partial_{x_{2}}
acting on it. Since the second term in (
D.4
)
is a polynomial of order
2(\ell-1)
in the components of
\vec{x}
, it turns out that, for
\ell\geq 1
,

(\partial_{x_{1}}+i\partial_{x_{2}})^{n}((x_{1}-y_{1})+i(x_{2}-y_{2}))(\partial_{x_{1}}-i\partial_{x_{2}})G_{\ell}(\vec{x}-\vec{y})=2(\ell-1)(\partial_{x_{1}}+i\partial_{x_{2}})^{n}G_{\ell}(\vec{x}-\vec{y})

(D.5)

for
n>2(\ell-1)
.

Appendix E
Convolution property of the polyharmonic Green functions

One has

\displaystyle\vec{\nabla}_{y}^{2(\ell+m)}\int\text{d}^{2}x\,G_{\ell}(\vec{y}-\vec{x})G_{m}(\vec{z}-\vec{x})

\displaystyle=\int\text{d}^{2}x\,\vec{\nabla}_{y}^{2(\ell+m)}G_{\ell}{\vec{y}-\vec{x}}G_{m}(\vec{z}-\vec{x})=\int\text{d}^{2}x\,\vec{\nabla}_{y}^{2\ell}\vec{\nabla}_{x}^{2m}G_{\ell}{\vec{y}-\vec{x}}G_{m}(\vec{z}-\vec{x})

\displaystyle=\int\text{d}^{2}x\,\vec{\nabla}_{y}^{2\ell}G_{\ell}{\vec{y}-\vec{x}}\vec{\nabla}_{x}^{2m}G_{m}(\vec{z}-\vec{x})=\int\text{d}^{2}x\,\delta(\vec{y}-\vec{x})\delta(\vec{z}-\vec{x})

\displaystyle=\delta(\vec{y}-\vec{z}).

(E.1)

Under standard regularity conditions, the homogeneous polyharmonic problem has only the trivial solution
[
30
]
, and the above computation proves (
2.7
).

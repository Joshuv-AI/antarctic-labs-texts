Revised version Improvement of a conserved current density versus adding a total derivative to a Lagrangian density
François Gieres

Abstract
For classical relativistic field theory in Minkowski space-time, the addition of a superpotential term to a conserved current density is trivial in the sense that it does not modify the local conservation law nor change the conserved charge, though it may allow us to obtain a current density with some improved properties. The addition of a total derivative term to a Lagrangian density is also trivial in the sense that it does not modify the equations of motion of the theory. These facts suggest that both operations are related and possibly equivalent to each other for any global symmetry of an action functional. We address this question following the study of two quite different (and well known) instances: the Callan-Coleman-Jackiw improvement of the canonical energy-momentum tensor for scalar and vector fields (providing an on-shell traceless energy-momentum tensor) and the construction of a current density satisfying a zero curvature condition for two-dimensional sigma models on deformed spaces (notably the squashed three-sphere and warped AdS spaces). These instances correspond to fairly different implementations of the general results. An appendix addresses the precise relationship between the approaches to local conservation laws based on active and passive symmetry transformations, respectively.

Introduction

Noether’s first theorem
[
1
,
2
]

establishing a general relationship between global symmetries of an action functional and local conservation laws
has become a pillar of modern physics, e.g. see reference
[
3
]
for some reviews and reference
[
4
]
for an historical account
up to recent developments. Though the main result is by now part of the standard physics curriculum,
it has taken some time for
achieving a deeper conceptual and mathematical understanding in terms of equivalence classes of currents
and of symmetry transformations
[
5
,
6
,
7
,
8
,
9
]
.

The present article addresses a particular instance of Noether’s first theorem namely the relationship between Lagrangians which are given by a total derivative
and locally conserved current densities which are derivatives of a superpotential (so-called superpotential terms).
The upshot is that the superpotential terms which are generally introduced by hand (in order to obtain conserved current densities
having improved properties with respect to the model under consideration) also follow from Noether’s first theorem
as applied to a Lagrangian density which is given by a total derivative.
Some related results
or examples have previously appeared in the vast literature on field theory
(and will be explicitly indicated in our discussion),
but we are not aware of a complete and general treatment including illustrations of different nature.
Such an investigation represents the main subject of the present text.
We have also included a discussion of the precise relationship between
the approaches based on active and passive symmetry transformations, respectively.
In fact, different authors generally choose either of these two approaches, but the detailed relationship
between both of them requires a bit of care.

We emphasize that we will only be concerned with theories defined on unbounded Minkowski space-time
\mathds{R}^{n}

and not with subsets thereof, henceforth not with boundaries of the latter subsets.
(A simple example
[
10
]
of a subset of
\mathds{R}^{4}
is given by the “spatial upper half-space”

\Omega=\{(t,x,y,z)\,|\,z\geq 0\}
which has a boundary described by
z=0
.)
The presence of such boundaries generally breaks symmetries like translation invariance
and the discussion of local or global conservation laws then has to take into account
boundary conditions of fields as well as boundary terms.
For a discussion of this subject in the framework of supersymmetric field theories,
we refer to
[
10
,
11
]
and references therein.

Our article is organized as follows.
To set the stage, we first recall in section 2 some well known facts concerning Lagrangian densities, Noether’s first theorem
and equivalence classes of conserved current densities.
In section 3, we outline the results which follow from the scale invariance of the action functional for a real scalar field
in
n
space-time dimensions: for this model we discuss the fact that the addition of a particular total derivative
to the Lagrangian density describing the dynamics yields the so-called new improved or Callan-Coleman-Jackiw energy-momentum tensor (EMT)
[
12
]

as well as the fact that this tensor differs from the canonical EMT by a superpotential term.
In the subsequent section,
the results which hold for scale invariance of scalar fields are generalized to the full group of conformal transformations
in
n
-dimensional space-time. These results allow us to apprehend more fully those which hold for scale symmetry
that has been the main focus in the literature in relationship with the EMT.
In section 5,
we show that the total derivative Lagrangian which naturally occurs in
the four-dimensional supersymmetric Wess-Zumino model
yields the familiar improvements of the EMT
and of the supersymmetry current (which are part of the supermultiplet of currents) of this model. In section 6,
we consider active symmetry transformations to
derive a simple
general formula for the current density which is associated to a global symmetry of a Lagrangian density that is given by a total derivative.

This allows us to recover the new improved EMT for a scalar field, but this also leads (by application of the method of Gell-Mann and Lévy
[
13
,
14
]

for deriving Noether current densities) to general expressions for the currents appearing in other classes of models.
The latter include the two-dimensional sigma models with different target spaces that have previously been investigated in the literature
and that we address in section 7.
More precisely, we will provide a short introduction to these models while emphasizing that
the addition of a particular total derivative to the Lagrangian density
induces a superpotential term in the conserved current density: this addition ensures that the total
Lie algebra-valued current density satisfies the
zero curvature condition and thereby permits to establish straightforwardly the integrability of these field theories.
The appendices gather some derivations as well as the discussion of the general relationship between active and passive symmetry transformations
in the implementation of Noether’s first theorem (appendix
C
).
For the sake of completeness, we have also included
a short presentation of the procedure of Gell-Mann and Lévy
which is not always described in great detail or generality in the literature (appendix
D
).

We consider the natural system of units (
c\equiv 1
) and we use standard notation for the coordinates of

-dimensional space-time

x=(t,\vec{x}\,)\equiv(x^{\mu})_{\mu=0,1,\dots,n-1}

Some reminders

2.1
Lagrangian density given by a total derivative

Suppose the Lagrangian density
{\cal L}
for some classical relativistic fields
\varphi
is given by a total derivative,
i.e.
{\cal L}=\partial_{\mu}k^{\mu}
where
k^{\mu}
depends on
\varphi
and/or its derivatives up to some finite order.
A variation
\delta\varphi(x)\equiv\varphi^{\prime}(x)-\varphi(x)
then induces a variation
of the action functional
S\equiv\int_{\Omega}d^{n}x\,{\cal L}
defined on a space-time domain
\Omega\subset\mathds{R}^{n}
:

\displaystyle\delta S=\int_{\Omega}d^{n}x\,\delta{\cal L}=\int_{\Omega}d^{n}x\,\partial_{\mu}(\delta k^{\mu})=\oint_{\partial\Omega}d^{n-1}x_{\mu}\,\delta k^{\mu}\,.

(2.1)

Here, Stokes’ theorem was applied for the last equality, see reference
[
15
]
for the notation
of the hypersurface integration measure.
Thus, if the variation
\delta\varphi
and its derivatives vanish at the boundary
\partial\Omega
of
\Omega
,
the variation
\delta S
vanishes identically for all of these field configurations and so does its functional
derivative with respect to
\varphi
, i.e. we have the
identity

\delta S/\delta\varphi=0
.

2.2
Noether’s first theorem and improvement of currents

Generalities:

For a Lagrangian which is at most of second order,
i.e.
{\cal L}={\cal L}(\varphi,\partial_{\mu}\varphi,\partial_{\mu}\partial_{\nu}\varphi)
,
Noether’s first theorem states: if
\delta{\cal L}=\partial_{\mu}\Omega^{\mu}

under the infinitesimal variation
\delta\varphi(x)\equiv\varphi^{\prime}(x)-\varphi(x)
, then

\displaystyle 0=\frac{\delta S}{\delta\varphi}\,\delta\varphi+\partial_{\mu}j^{\mu}\,,\quad\mbox{with}\ \;\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\frac{\delta S}{\delta\varphi}=\frac{\partial{\cal L}}{\partial\varphi}-\partial_{\mu}\left(\frac{\partial{\cal L}}{\partial(\partial_{\mu}\varphi)}\right)+\partial_{\mu}\partial_{\nu}\left(\frac{\partial{\cal L}}{\partial(\partial_{\mu}\partial_{\nu}\varphi)}\right)\\
j^{\mu}=\left[\frac{\partial{\cal L}}{\partial(\partial_{\mu}\varphi)}-\partial_{\rho}\left(\frac{\partial{\cal L}}{\partial(\partial_{\mu}\partial_{\rho}\varphi)}\right)\right]\delta\varphi+\frac{\partial{\cal L}}{\partial(\partial_{\mu}\partial_{\rho}\varphi)}\,\partial_{\rho}\delta\varphi-\Omega^{\mu}\,.\end{array}\right.

We note that these expressions reduce to the familiar results for a first order Lagrangian.
Since the standard textbook presentations focus on first order Lagrangians, we outline the derivation of (
2.2
)
in the appendices
A
and
B
.
Of course, these results and derivations straightforwardly generalize to a Lagrangian density which depends
on higher than second order derivatives
[
1
,
2
]
,
but we focused on second order derivatives here in view of the physical applications to be addressed.

As a matter of fact, the general formulation
[
5
,
6
,
7
,
8
,
9
]

of Noether’s first theorem
states that there is a one-to-one correspondence between equivalence classes of (global) variational symmetries
and equivalence classes of (on-shell) conserved currents.
(For a review, see for instance references
[
9
,
15
]
.)
More precisely, two infinitesimal global
symmetry transformations are considered to be equivalent if they differ by a gauge symmetry transformation and/or
an “equation of motion symmetry transformation”, i.e. a symmetry transformation which is a linear combination of
Euler-Lagrange derivatives and their space-time derivatives up to a finite order
(with possibly field-dependent coefficients).

The
equivalence of current densities
is defined by

\displaystyle\boxed{\ j^{\mu}\sim j^{\mu}+\underbrace{\partial_{\rho}B^{\rho\mu}}_{\textrm{superpot. term}}+\underbrace{t^{\mu}}_{\approx\,0}\ }\,,\qquad\mbox{where}\ \;B^{\rho\mu}=-B^{\mu\rho}\,.

(2.4)

Here, the so-called
superpotential

B^{\rho\mu}
defines a current density
\partial_{\rho}B^{\rho\mu}

which is identically conserved due to the antisymmetry of
B^{\rho\mu}
.
Moreover, here and in the following, we use Dirac’s notation
F\approx 0
to denote an on-shell equality,
i.e. a relation which holds by virtue of the equations of motion.

For two equivalent currents, say
(j_{1}^{\mu})
and
(j_{2}^{\mu})
, we have

\partial_{\mu}j_{1}^{\mu}\approx\partial_{\mu}j_{2}^{\mu}
which implies that
(j_{1}^{\mu})
is on-shell conserved
if and only if
(j_{2}^{\mu})
is on-shell conserved.
The addition of a trivial term
\partial_{\rho}B^{\rho\mu}+t^{\mu}

to a given (on-shell) conserved current
(j^{\mu})
is generally referred to as an
improvement of the current

since this addition eventually allows us to
obtain a conserved current which has “better properties” than
(j^{\mu})
,
e.g. in a gauge field theory it may be gauge invariant if
(j^{\mu})
does not have this property.
In this respect it is worth recalling the following example.
(Quite generally, in this context we also mention the important fact that the (on-shell value of the)
Noether charge
Q\equiv\int_{\mathds{R}^{n-1}}d^{n-1}x\,j^{0}
is not modified by the addition of an
improvement term to the current density
j^{\mu}
provided the field
B^{i0}
decays sufficiently fast
at spatial infinity
\partial\mathds{R}^{n-1}
.)

Example of EMT of the electromagnetic field:

The
translation invariance of the action
for free Maxwell theory in
n
-dimensional Minkowski space-time,
i.e. of the
functional
S_{Max}[A]\equiv-\frac{1}{4}\int_{\mathds{R}^{n}}d^{n}x\,F^{\mu\nu}F_{\mu\nu}

(with
F_{\mu\nu}\equiv\partial_{\mu}A_{\nu}-\partial_{\nu}A_{\mu}
and equation of motion
\partial_{\mu}F^{\mu\nu}=0
)
leads, by virtue of Noether’s first theorem (
2.2
) to
the local conservation law

\partial_{\mu}T_{\textrm{can}}^{\mu\nu}\approx 0

for the
canonical energy-momentum tensor (EMT)
of the electromagnetic field:

\displaystyle T_{\textrm{can}}^{\mu\nu}=-F^{\mu\rho}\partial^{\nu}A_{\rho}+\frac{1}{4}\,\eta^{\mu\nu}F^{\rho\sigma}F_{\rho\sigma}\,.

(2.5)

Since the first term of this expression is not gauge invariant,
(T_{\textrm{can}}^{\mu\nu})

cannot be viewed as a physically acceptable representative for the EMT of the electromagnetic field
(the components of this tensor being measurable quantities).
This raises the question whether the equivalence class of the on-shell conserved currents
(T_{\textrm{can}}^{\mu\nu})_{\nu=0,1,\dots,n-1}

contain a representative which is gauge invariant.
To find such a representative, we simply express the derivatives
\partial^{\nu}A_{\rho}

in terms of
{F^{\nu}}_{\rho}
:

\displaystyle-F^{\mu\rho}\partial^{\nu}A_{\rho}=F^{\mu\rho}{F_{\rho}}^{\nu}-F^{\mu\rho}\partial_{\rho}A^{\nu}\,.

(2.6)

After applying the Leibniz rule to the last term,

\displaystyle-F^{\mu\rho}\partial_{\rho}A^{\nu}=\partial_{\rho}(-F^{\mu\rho}A^{\nu})+(\partial_{\rho}F^{\mu\rho})A^{\nu}\,,

(2.7)

we find that

\displaystyle T_{\textrm{can}}^{\mu\nu}=T_{\textrm{phys}}^{\mu\nu}+\underbrace{\partial_{\rho}\chi^{\rho\mu\nu}}_{\textrm{superpot. term}}+\underbrace{t^{\mu\nu}}_{\approx\,0}\qquad\mbox{with}\quad\left\{\begin{array}[]{c}\chi^{\rho\mu\nu}\equiv F^{\rho\mu}A^{\nu}=-\chi^{\mu\rho\nu}\\
t^{\mu\nu}\equiv-(\partial_{\rho}F^{\rho\mu})A^{\nu}\approx 0\,,\end{array}\right.

and

\displaystyle\boxed{\ T_{\textrm{phys}}^{\mu\nu}\equiv F^{\mu\rho}{F_{\rho}}^{\nu}+\frac{1}{4}\,\eta^{\mu\nu}F^{\rho\sigma}F_{\rho\sigma}\ }\,.

(2.10)

Thus, for each value of
\nu
,
the currents
(T_{\textrm{can}}^{\mu\nu})
and
(T_{\textrm{phys}}^{\mu\nu})
are equivalent
from the point of view of Noether’s first theorem since they differ only by trivial terms.
While the representative
(T_{\textrm{phys}}^{\mu\nu})
of the equivalence class is
gauge invariant
and
symmetric

as well as
traceless for
n=4
, the representative
(T_{\textrm{can}}^{\mu\nu})
does not have any of these properties.
As a matter of fact, the symmetry of the EMT is also a desired property if the theory in Minkowski space-time is viewed
as the flat space limit of the theory in curved space-time described by general relativity: the EMT in Minkowski space-time
should then coincide with the Einstein-Hilbert EMT, i.e. the flat space limit of the metric EMT

\displaystyle T^{\mu\nu}\equiv\frac{-2}{\sqrt{|g|}}\,\frac{\delta S_{Max}[A,\textbf{{g}}]}{\delta g_{\mu\nu}}\,,

(2.11)

where
S_{Max}[A,\textbf{{g}}]
represents the coupling of the gauge field
(A^{\mu})
to an external gravitational field described
by a fixed, symmetric metric tensor field
\textbf{{g}}(x)\equiv(g_{\mu\nu}(x))

and
g\equiv\textrm{det}\,\textbf{{g}}
(see
[
16
,
17
]
and references therein for further details and subtleties).
As a matter of fact, the improvement (
2.2
) has already been discussed by F. J. Belinfante and L. Rosenfeld
in the 1930s and
is usually referred to by their names.
Here, we simply emphasized the mathematical and physical vision brought about the general formulation of Noether’s first theorem
which describes a correspondence between equivalence classes of global symmetries and on-shell conserved current densities.

2.3
Different implementations of Noether’s first theorem

The fact that relation (
2.2
), i.e.

0=\frac{\delta S}{\delta\varphi}\,\delta\varphi+\partial_{\mu}j^{\mu}
, does not yield a gauge invariant
current density
j^{\mu}=T^{\mu\nu}a_{\nu}
for the case of translations of a gauge field, i.e. of
the infinitesimal symmetry transformations
\delta\varphi=\delta A_{\mu}=a^{\nu}\partial_{\nu}A_{\mu}
,
does not come as a surprise since the latter variation is not gauge invariant.
For this reason various authors have looked for alternative implementations
of Noether’s first theorem which automatically yield a gauge invariant EMT.
A natural procedure (which was rediscovered numerous times over the last decades, e.g. in reference
[
18
]
)
was put forward by E. Bessel-Hagen in his pioneering work
[
2
]

from 1921 in which he introduced divergence symmetries (following the advice of E. Noether)
and applied Noether’s theorems to the invariance of four-dimensional Maxwell’s equations
under the conformal group.
This procedure (qualified as
“Kunstgriff”
, i.e. trick, by E. Bessel-Hagen)
consists in “covariantizing” the variation
\delta A_{\mu}=a^{\nu}\partial_{\nu}A_{\mu}

with the help of the gauge invariant tensor
F_{\nu\mu}=\partial_{\nu}A_{\mu}-\partial_{\mu}A_{\nu}
,
i.e. replacing the gauge variant expression
\delta A_{\mu}
by the gauge invariant one

\displaystyle\delta_{cov}A_{\mu}\equiv a^{\nu}F_{\nu\mu}=\delta A_{\mu}-\partial_{\mu}(a^{\nu}A_{\nu})\,.

(2.12)

Here, the last term represents a local gauge transformation (with field dependent parameter
a^{\nu}A_{\nu}
)
and thereby
it is a trivial contribution to the global symmetry transformation
\delta A_{\mu}

(in the sense of the equivalences of global symmetry transformations defined above).
This procedure directly leads to a gauge invariant EMT, namely to the result (
2.10
).
When applied to the conformal Killing vector fields
\xi\equiv\xi^{\mu}(x)\partial_{\mu}
(of the Minkowski metric)
which parametrize the Lie algebra of the conformal group (rather than the translations
(a^{\mu})
alone),
it yields the
Bessel-Hagen form

T^{\mu\nu}_{\textrm{phys}}\xi_{\nu}
for all of the conserved current densities
associated to conformal invariance (see pages 271-272 of the original work
[
2
]
and
reference
[
19
]
for a recent assessment).

3
Scale invariance for relativistic fields

3.1
Reminder 1: Scale invariance and canonical dilatation current

A
scale transformation
(or
dilatation
or
dilation
)
of the space-time coordinates is defined by

x\mapsto x^{\prime}=\textrm{e}^{\rho}x
where
\rho
is a constant real number.
The induced change of the Minkowski metric is also a rescaling
with a positive factor:

\displaystyle ds^{2}\equiv\eta_{\mu\nu}dx^{\mu}dx^{\nu}\ \leadsto\ ds^{\prime\,2}=\textrm{e}^{2\rho}ds^{2}\,.

(3.1)

A classical relativistic field
\varphi
(like a scalar field
\phi
, a vector field
(A^{\mu})
or a spinor field
\psi
)
transforms under such a rescaling according to
1
1

1

More precisely,
fields transforming in this manner are referred to as
scaling fields

[
20
]
or as
“quasi-primary” fields

in
n
space-time dimensions
[
21
]
.

\displaystyle\boxed{\ \varphi^{\prime}(x^{\prime})=\textrm{e}^{-\rho\,d_{\varphi}}\varphi(x)\ }\qquad\textrm{for}\ \;\boxed{\ x^{\prime}=\textrm{e}^{\rho}x\ }\,.

(3.2)

Here, the natural number
d_{\varphi}
denotes the so-called
scale dimension

of the field
\varphi
. If one chooses this dimension to coincide with the canonical (engineering) dimension
of the field
\varphi
in
n
space-time dimensions (i.e.
d_{\phi}=\frac{n-2}{2}
for a scalar field
\phi
or for a vector field

(A^{\mu})
, and
d_{\psi}=\frac{n-1}{2}
for a spinor field
\psi
), then the action for a
free massless field

\varphi

in
n
dimensions,

\displaystyle S[\varphi]\equiv\int d^{n}x\,{\cal L}(\varphi,\partial_{\mu}\varphi)\,,\qquad{\cal L}^{\prime}(x^{\prime})=\textrm{e}^{-n\rho}\,{\cal L}(x)\qquad\textrm{for}\ \;x^{\prime}=\textrm{e}^{\rho}x\,,

(3.3)

is scale invariant.
However mass terms and in general also interaction terms
involving dimensionful coupling constants
violate scale invariance so that one is not simply dealing
with dimensional analysis.

From the invariance of the action under infinitesimal scale transformations,

\displaystyle\delta_{\rho}x^{\mu}=\rho\,x^{\mu}\,,\qquad\delta_{\rho}\varphi=-\rho\,(x\cdot\partial+d_{\varphi})\varphi\quad\textrm{with}\ \;x\cdot\partial\equiv x^{\mu}\partial_{\mu}\,,

(3.4)

and

\displaystyle\delta_{\rho}{\cal L}=-\rho\,(x\cdot\partial+n){\cal L}=\partial_{\mu}\Omega^{\mu}\qquad\mbox{with}\quad\Omega^{\mu}\equiv-\rho\,x^{\mu}{\cal L}\,,

(3.5)

it follows by virtue of Noether’s first theorem that we have an on-shell conserved
canonical dilatation current
density
of the form

\displaystyle\boxed{\ j^{\mu}_{\textrm{dil,can}}=T^{\mu\nu}_{\textrm{can}}\,x_{\nu}+d_{\varphi}\,\frac{\partial{\cal L}}{\partial(\partial_{\mu}\varphi)}\,\varphi\ }\qquad\textrm{with}\ \;\partial_{\mu}j^{\mu}_{\textrm{dil,can}}\approx 0\,.

(3.6)

Here,
T^{\mu\nu}_{\textrm{can}}\equiv\frac{\partial{\cal L}}{\partial(\partial_{\mu}\varphi)}\,\partial^{\nu}\varphi-\eta^{\mu\nu}{\cal L}

denotes the
canonical EMT
whose conservation law
\partial_{\mu}T^{\mu\nu}_{\textrm{can}}\approx 0
follows
from the invariance of the action under space-time translations.

The result (
3.6
) is reminiscent of the expression
for the
canonical angular momentum tensor:
For non-scalar fields
the latter not only involves the moments of the canonical EMT, but also an additional term, namely the spin density tensor.
This motivated C. Callan, S. Coleman and R. Jackiw
[
12
]
to search for an improvement
such that its addition to
j^{\mu}_{\textrm{dil,can}}
eliminates the second term
in expression (
3.6
).
To achieve this goal, they added an appropriate superpotential term to the canonical EMT
T^{\mu\nu}_{\textrm{can}}

so as to obtain a “new improved” EMT
T^{\mu\nu}_{\textrm{conf}}
which is (on-shell) traceless so that
the improved dilatation current
j^{\mu}_{\textrm{dil,conf}}
is simply given by
the “moments of the EMT”:

\displaystyle\boxed{\ j^{\mu}_{\textrm{dil,conf}}=T^{\mu\nu}_{\textrm{conf}}\,x_{\nu}\ }\,,\qquad\textrm{hence}\ \quad\boxed{\ \partial_{\mu}j^{\mu}_{\textrm{dil,conf}}=T^{\mu}_{\textrm{conf}\,\mu}\approx 0\ }\,.

(3.7)

Thus,
the on-shell tracelessness of the new improved EMT
T^{\mu\nu}_{\textrm{conf}}

represents a mathematical reflection of the scale invariance of the theory under
consideration.

3.2
Reminder 2: New improved EMT for a scalar field

Let us consider the case of a

real free massless scalar field
\phi
in
n
space-time dimensions,

i.e. the action functional

\displaystyle S[\phi]\equiv\int_{M}d^{n}x\,{\cal L}(\partial_{\mu}\phi)\equiv\frac{1}{2}\int_{M}d^{n}x\,(\partial^{\mu}\phi)(\partial_{\mu}\phi)\,.

(3.8)

Then, expression (
3.6
) writes

\displaystyle j^{\mu}_{\textrm{dil,can}}=x_{\nu}T^{\mu\nu}_{\textrm{can}}+d_{\phi}\,\phi\partial^{\mu}\phi\,,\quad\textrm{with}\ \;T_{\textrm{can}}^{\mu\nu}=(\partial^{\mu}\phi)(\partial^{\nu}\phi)-\frac{1}{2}\,\eta^{\mu\nu}(\partial^{\rho}\phi)(\partial_{\rho}\phi)\quad\mbox{and}\ \;d_{\phi}=\frac{n-2}{2}\,.

(3.9)

Following Callan, Coleman and Jackiw
[
12
]

(who studied the four dimensional case),
one adds a particular derivative term to the canonical EMT
T_{\textrm{can}}^{\mu\nu}

of
\phi

so as to obtain the so-called
new improved EMT
or
CCJ tensor

\displaystyle\boxed{\ T^{\mu\nu}_{\textrm{conf}}\equiv T^{\mu\nu}_{\textrm{can}}-\xi_{n}\,(\partial^{\mu}\partial^{\nu}-\eta^{\mu\nu}\Box)\phi^{2}\ }\qquad\textrm{with}\quad\boxed{\ \xi_{n}\equiv\frac{1}{4}\,\frac{n-2}{n-1}\ }\,,

(3.10)

and
\Box\equiv\partial^{\mu}\partial_{\mu}
.
The tensor (
3.10
) is still
symmetric, on-shell conserved

and yields the
same conserved charge as

T^{\mu\nu}_{\textrm{can}}

(upon the assumption that the fields fall off sufficiently fast at spatial infinity).
We have labeled it by ‘conformal’ since it is
on-shell traceless,
i.e.

T^{\mu}_{\textrm{conf}\,\mu}\approx 0
by virtue of the equation of motion
\Box\phi=0
, and it is directly
related to the conformally invariant coupling of scalar fields to
gravity
[
12
,
24
]
.
With (
3.10
) and the redefinition

\displaystyle j^{\mu}_{\textrm{dil,conf}}\equiv j^{\mu}_{\textrm{dil,can}}+\xi_{n}\,\partial_{\rho}\left[(x^{\mu}\partial^{\rho}-x^{\rho}\partial^{\mu})\phi^{2}\right]\,,

(3.11)

we get the expression (
3.7
) for the dilatation current that we looked for, i.e.
j^{\mu}_{\textrm{dil,conf}}

is
on-shell conserved,
yields the
same conserved charge as

j^{\mu}_{\textrm{dil,can}}
and is simply

given by the “moments of the EMT”.

3.3
Derivation of the new improved EMT for a free scalar field

The improved expressions, i.e.
T^{\mu\nu}_{\textrm{conf}}
for the EMT and

j^{\mu}_{\textrm{dil,conf}}
for the dilatation current, respectively, can be straightforwardly obtained as follows.
(Our derivation has been motivated by the appendix of reference
[
25
]
,
but we note that the results are implicit in reference
[
26
]
though its author argues in a different manner.
As we realized quite recently, the result for the EMT in four space-time dimensions is explicit in a work devoted to
a supersymmetric model
[
27
]
which we will discuss in section
5
below.)

First, we recall that, for a given Lagrangian

{\cal L}(\phi,\partial_{\mu}\phi,\partial_{\mu}\partial_{\nu}\phi)
, the addition of a total derivative

{\cal L}_{1}\equiv\partial_{\mu}k^{\mu}
(with
k^{\mu}
depending on
x,\phi,\partial_{\mu}\phi
)
does not modify the equation of motion determined by
{\cal L}
.
Such a trivial addition eventually leads to the addition of a superpotential term to a conserved current associated to
an invariance of the action
S[\phi]\equiv\int d^{n}x\,{\cal L}

(e.g. to the EMT associated to translation invariance).

For the scale invariant action (
3.8
) describing a real free massless scalar field in
n
space-time dimensions,
we can obtain a scale invariant integral over a total derivative by partial integration:

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\int_{M}d^{n}x\,(\partial^{\mu}\phi)(\partial_{\mu}\phi)=-\frac{1}{2}\int_{M}d^{n}x\,\phi\,\Box\phi+\frac{1}{2}\int_{M}d^{n}x\,\partial^{\mu}(\phi\,\partial_{\mu}\phi)\,.

(3.12)

Henceforth, we will consider the
scale invariant action

S_{1}[\phi]\equiv\int d^{n}x\,{\cal L}_{1}

with

\displaystyle\boxed{\ {\cal L}_{1}\equiv\partial_{\mu}k^{\mu}\ }\,,\qquad\mbox{where}\quad\boxed{\ k^{\mu}\equiv-\xi\,\partial^{\mu}\phi^{2}\ }\quad(\xi\in\mathds{R})\,,

(3.13)

i.e.

\displaystyle k^{\mu}=-2\xi\,\phi\,\partial^{\mu}\phi\qquad\mbox{and}\quad{\cal L}_{1}=-2\xi\,[(\partial^{\mu}\phi)(\partial_{\mu}\phi)+\phi\,\Box\phi]\,.

(3.14)

Here, we have introduced an arbitrary real factor
\xi

to keep track of the surface term in the subsequent calculations and in accordance
with the arbitrariness of this term for the equation of motion.
For the symmetries of the second order Lagrangian (
3.13
), we will now apply
Noether’s first theorem (
2.2
).

Space-time translations:

Since
{\cal L}_{1}
does not explicitly depend on
x
, it is invariant under
space-time translations

given at the infinitesimal level by

\delta\phi=a_{\nu}\partial^{\nu}\phi
and

\displaystyle\delta{\cal L}_{1}=a_{\nu}\partial^{\nu}{\cal L}_{1}=\partial_{\mu}\Omega_{1}^{\mu}\qquad\mbox{with}\ \;\Omega_{1}^{\mu}=a_{\nu}\eta^{\mu\nu}{\cal L}_{1}\,.

(3.15)

By virtue of Noether’s theorem (
2.2
), we thus have a
conserved current

j_{1}^{\mu}\equiv T_{1}^{\mu\nu}a_{\nu}
with

\displaystyle T_{1}^{\mu\nu}=\partial^{\nu}k^{\mu}-\eta^{\mu\nu}\partial_{\rho}k^{\rho}\,,

(3.16)

i.e.

\displaystyle\boxed{\ T_{1}^{\mu\nu}=-\partial_{\rho}\chi^{\rho\mu\nu}\ }\qquad\mbox{with}\ \;\boxed{\ \chi^{\rho\mu\nu}\equiv k^{\rho}\eta^{\mu\nu}-k^{\mu}\eta^{\rho\nu}\ }\,.

(3.17)

Since
\chi^{\rho\mu\nu}=-\chi^{\mu\rho\nu}
, this EMT is simply a superpotential term,
i.e. it is identically conserved:
\partial_{\mu}T_{1}^{\mu\nu}=0
.
We note that the tensor (
3.16
) is symmetric since the definition of
k^{\mu}

implies that
\partial^{\nu}k^{\mu}=\partial^{\mu}k^{\nu}
.
This symmetry may be rendered manifest in eqn. (
3.17
) by symmetrizing the second term of

\chi^{\rho\mu\nu}
, i.e. by considering

\chi^{\rho\mu\nu}=k^{\rho}\eta^{\mu\nu}-\frac{1}{2}(k^{\mu}\eta^{\rho\nu}+k^{\nu}\eta^{\rho\mu})

[
26
]
.

By adding the EMT (
3.17
) to
T^{\mu\nu}_{\textrm{can}}
, we obtain a total EMT

T^{\mu\nu}_{\textrm{tot}}\equiv T^{\mu\nu}_{\textrm{can}}+T^{\mu\nu}_{1}

whose trace is given by

\displaystyle T^{\mu}_{\textrm{tot}\,\mu}=-\frac{1}{2}\,\left[(n-2)-4\xi\,(n-1)\right](\partial^{\mu}\phi)(\partial_{\mu}\phi)+2\xi\,(n-1)\,\phi\underbrace{\Box\phi}_{\approx\,0}\,.

(3.18)

Thus, the tensor
T^{\mu\nu}_{\textrm{tot}}
is on-shell traceless for

\xi=\xi_{n}\equiv\frac{1}{4}\,\frac{n-2}{n-1}
:
expression
T^{\mu\nu}_{1}
with
\xi=\xi_{n}

obviously coincides with the additional term in (
3.10
)
and
T^{\mu\nu}_{\textrm{tot}}=T^{\mu\nu}_{\textrm{conf}}
.

Scale transformations:

For the
scale transformation
(
3.4
), we have (
3.5
), i.e.

\delta{\cal L}_{1}=\partial_{\mu}\Omega_{1}^{\mu}
with

\Omega_{1}^{\mu}=-\rho\,x^{\mu}{\cal L}_{1}
: by virtue of (
2.2
) this leads to the conserved Noether current

\displaystyle j^{\mu}_{1}=T^{\mu\nu}_{1}x_{\nu}+(n-1)\,k^{\mu}=\left(\partial^{\nu}k^{\mu}-\eta^{\mu\nu}\partial_{\rho}k^{\rho}\right)x_{\nu}+(n-1)\,k^{\mu}\,,

(3.19)

i.e.

\displaystyle\boxed{\ j^{\mu}_{1}=\partial_{\rho}B^{\rho\mu}\ }\qquad\mbox{with}\ \;\boxed{\ B^{\rho\mu}\equiv-\chi^{\rho\mu\nu}\zeta_{\nu}=x^{\rho}k^{\mu}-x^{\mu}k^{\rho}\ }\,.

(3.20)

Here,
\zeta^{\nu}\equiv x^{\nu}
represents the vector field
\zeta\cdot\partial
generating scale transformations.
Since
B^{\rho\mu}=-B^{\mu\rho}
, the current
j_{1}^{\mu}
also defines a
superpotential term, i.e.
\partial_{\mu}j^{\mu}_{1}=0
.

For
j^{\mu}_{\textrm{tot}}\equiv j^{\mu}_{\textrm{dil,can}}+j^{\mu}_{1}

(with
j^{\mu}_{\textrm{dil,can}}
given by (
3.9
)
and
j^{\mu}_{1}
given by (
3.19
)), we get

\displaystyle j^{\mu}_{\textrm{tot}}=\big[\underbrace{T^{\mu\nu}_{\textrm{can}}+T^{\mu\nu}_{1}}_{=\,T^{\mu\nu}_{\textrm{tot}}}\big]x_{\nu}+\frac{1}{2}\,\left[(n-2)-4\xi\,(n-1)\right]\,\phi\,\partial^{\mu}\phi\,,

(3.21)

hence
j^{\mu}_{\textrm{tot}}=T^{\mu\nu}_{\textrm{tot}}x_{\nu}
for
\xi=\xi_{n}
.
In summary, for
\xi=\xi_{n}
we have
j^{\mu}_{\textrm{tot}}=j^{\mu}_{\textrm{dil,conf}}
and expression
j^{\mu}_{1}
coincides
with the additional term in (
3.11
).

Summary:

For the “conformal value”
\xi=\xi_{n}
, the total Lagrangian has the form

\displaystyle{\cal L}_{\textrm{tot}}\equiv{\cal L}+{\cal L}_{1}=\frac{1}{n-1}\,\Big[\frac{1}{2}\,(\partial^{\mu}\phi)(\partial_{\mu}\phi)-d_{\phi}\,\phi\,\Box\phi\Big]\qquad\mbox{for}\ \ \xi=\xi_{n}\equiv\frac{1}{4}\,\frac{n-2}{n-1}\,.

(3.22)

The total EMT is on-shell traceless and the dilatation current is given by the moments of the the total EMT.

We note that the result (
3.18
) implies that an
off-shell traceless EMT

T^{\mu\nu}_{\textrm{off}}
can be obtained from
T^{\mu\nu}_{\textrm{tot}}=T^{\mu\nu}_{\textrm{conf}}

by the addition of the trivial current

-2\xi\,\frac{n-1}{n}\,\eta^{\mu\nu}\phi\,\Box\phi=-\frac{n-2}{2n}\,\eta^{\mu\nu}\phi\,\Box\phi
which vanishes on-shell
(see eqn. (
2.4
) with
j^{\mu}\equiv T^{\mu\nu}a_{\nu}

in the present case).
In this respect, we mention that the off-shell traceless EMT
T^{\mu\nu}_{\textrm{off}}

can also be obtained by a modification of the Gell-Mann and Lévy procedure
[
22
]
.
In fact, the usual procedure consists in promoting the translation parameters
a^{\mu}
in the
infinitesimal transformation law
\delta\phi=a^{\mu}\partial_{\mu}\phi

to space-time dependent parameters
a^{\mu}(x)
,
but one can also add, more generally, a derivative term
(\partial_{\mu}a_{\nu})\psi^{\mu\nu}(x)

to
a^{\mu}\partial_{\mu}\phi
, this derivative term vanishing if
a^{\mu}(x)
reduces to a constant
a^{\mu}

(see
[
22
]
as well as
[
23
]
for earlier work along the same lines).
A careful exploitation of the derivative term then yields
T^{\mu\nu}_{\textrm{off}}

in a constructive way.

3.4
Generalization to a multiplet of self-interacting real or complex scalar fields

For Minkowski space-time of dimension
n>2
(as well as for a space of dimension
n=1
)
the previous considerations can be generalized to the case where the Lagrangian density
for the real scalar field
\phi
involves a scale invariant self-interaction term
[
12
,
28
]
:

\displaystyle{\cal L}\equiv\frac{1}{2}\,(\partial^{\mu}\phi)(\partial_{\mu}\phi)-V(\phi)\,,\qquad\mbox{with}\quad V(\phi)\equiv\lambda\,\phi^{\frac{2n}{n-2}}\,,

(3.23)

\lambda
being a dimensionless coupling constant. The term
-V(\phi)
yields
a contribution
\eta^{\mu\nu}V
to the canonical EMT

T^{\mu\nu}_{\textrm{can}}
and thus a contribution
nV
to its trace
T^{\,\mu}_{\textrm{can}\,\mu}
.
Thereby, the new improved EMT has a trace given by

T^{\mu\nu}_{\textrm{conf}}=\frac{n-2}{2}\,\phi\,\Box\phi+nV
.
The equation of motion following from the Lagrangian density (
3.23
),
i.e.
\Box\phi+V^{\prime}=0
, now implies that
\frac{n-2}{2}\,\phi\,\Box\phi=-nV
.
By way of consequence,
the EMT
T^{\mu\nu}_{\textrm{conf}}
is still
on-shell traceless
and
thereby reflects the scale invariance of this model.

Instead of a single real self-interacting scalar field
\phi
, we can consider a
multiplet

\Phi
of such fields as well as a
complex-valued field

\phi
or a
multiplet
\Phi
of such fields

with a dynamics described by the scale invariant Lagrangian density

\displaystyle{\cal L}\equiv(\partial^{\mu}\Phi^{\dagger})(\partial_{\mu}\Phi)-\lambda\,(\Phi^{\dagger}\Phi)^{\frac{n}{n-2}}\,.

(3.24)

As a matter of fact, we will consider the case of a complex-valued field in section
5
below
in the context of a four-dimensional supersymmetric model.

3.5
Summary and assessment for a scalar field

The results presented above for the case of a real free massless scalar field
\phi

(i.e. the Lagrangian density
{\cal L}=\frac{1}{2}\,(\partial^{\mu}\phi)(\partial_{\mu}\phi)
)
can be summarized as follows without reference to the method of derivation (addition of the
Lagrangian density
{\cal L}_{1}
to
{\cal L}
).

For such a field, the on-shell conserved canonical dilatation current (
3.6
) has the form

\displaystyle j^{\mu}_{\textrm{dil,can}}=T^{\mu\nu}_{\textrm{can}}\,x_{\nu}-\tilde{J}^{\mu}\,,\qquad\mbox{with}\ \;\tilde{J}^{\mu}\equiv\frac{\partial{\cal L}}{\partial(\partial_{\mu}\phi)}\,\tilde{\delta}_{\textrm{dil}}\phi\,.

(3.25)

Here,
\tilde{\delta}_{\textrm{dil}}\phi=-d_{\phi}\,\phi
describes the
passive
scale transformation

\tilde{\delta}\phi\equiv\rho\,\tilde{\delta}_{\textrm{dil}}\phi=-\rho\,d_{\phi}\phi
(see
equation (
C.6
)
for the passive point of view of scale transformations).
The quantity
(\tilde{J}^{\mu})
may be viewed as the “passive symplectic potential” current density (cf. (
A.5
)
for the general expression of the symplectic potential)
2
2

2

For a scalar field,
the spin matrix
(\Sigma^{\alpha\beta})

vanishes and thereby the current
(\tilde{J}^{\mu})
coincides for these fields with the so-called
virial field

(V^{\mu})

which plays an important role for the invariance of physical models
under both scale and special conformal transformations, e.g. see reference
[
28
]
.
For a vector field
(A^{\mu})
, the spin matrix
(\Sigma^{\alpha\beta})
does not vanish and
the contribution
(\tilde{J}^{\mu})
to the canonical dilatation current
(which we will consider in eqn. (
3.29
) below)
only coincides with the Maxwell virial field up to a (space-time dependent) numerical factor.
.
More explicitly, for the scalar field
\phi
, we have
\tilde{J}^{\mu}=(n-1)\,k^{\mu}

where
k^{\mu}\equiv-\xi_{n}\,\partial^{\mu}\phi^{2}
is the vector field (
3.13
)
on which our derivation of improvements (using the Lagrangian
density
{\cal L}_{1}=\partial_{\mu}k^{\mu}
) was based.

The results for the improvements are summarized by

\displaystyle\boxed{\ j^{\mu}_{\textrm{dil,conf}}=T^{\mu\nu}_{\textrm{conf}}\,x_{\nu}\ }\,,\quad\boxed{\ j^{\mu}_{\textrm{dil,conf}}=j^{\mu}_{\textrm{dil,can}}+\partial_{\rho}B^{\rho\mu}\ }\,,\quad\boxed{\ T^{\mu\nu}_{\textrm{conf}}=T^{\mu\nu}_{\textrm{can}}-\partial_{\rho}\chi^{\rho\mu\nu}\ }\,,

(3.26)

with
B^{\rho\mu}
and
\chi^{\rho\mu\nu}
given by (
3.20
)
and (
3.17
), respectively.
These three relations imply that we have

\displaystyle j^{\mu}_{\textrm{dil,can}}=T^{\mu\nu}_{\textrm{can}}\,x_{\nu}-\left[(\partial_{\rho}\chi^{\rho\mu\nu})\,x_{\nu}+\partial_{\rho}B^{\rho\mu}\right]\,.

(3.27)

Comparison with (
3.25
) now yields the following decomposition for
\tilde{J}^{\mu}=(n-1)\,k^{\mu}
:

\displaystyle(n-1)\,k^{\mu}=\partial_{\rho}(k^{\rho}\eta^{\mu\nu}-k^{\mu}\eta^{\rho\nu})\,x_{\nu}+\partial_{\rho}(x^{\rho}k^{\mu}-x^{\mu}k^{\rho})\,.

(3.28)

Thus, this relation can be used (and in fact was used in reference
[
26
]
)
as a starting point for improving the canonical current densities

T^{\mu\nu}_{\textrm{can}}
and
j^{\mu}_{\textrm{dil,can}}
of a scalar field.

Relation (
3.26
) with
k^{\mu}=\frac{1}{n-1}\tilde{J}^{\mu}

and
\tilde{J}^{\mu}\equiv\frac{\partial{\cal L}}{\partial(\partial_{\mu}\varphi)}\,\tilde{\delta}_{\textrm{dil}}\varphi

can also be applied to the case of a vector field
\varphi=(A^{\mu})
, see next subsection.

3.6
Scale invariance for a vector field

General results:

The Lagrangian density for the free Maxwell field, i.e.

{\cal L}=-\frac{1}{4}\,F^{\mu\nu}F_{\mu\nu}
, is scale invariant in
n
space-time dimensions, the vector field
(A^{\mu})
having a scale
dimension
d_{A}=\frac{n-2}{2}
.
By virtue of relation (
3.25
) and

\frac{\partial{\cal L}}{\partial(\partial_{\mu}A_{\nu})}=-F^{\mu\nu}
, the on-shell conserved canonical dilatation current density
for the Maxwell field
(A^{\mu})
reads

\displaystyle j^{\mu}_{\textrm{dil,can}}=T^{\mu\nu}_{\textrm{can}}\,x_{\nu}-\tilde{J}^{\mu}\,,\qquad\mbox{where}\ \;\tilde{J}^{\mu}=d_{A}\,F^{\mu\nu}A_{\nu}\,,

(3.29)

and where the canonical EMT
T^{\mu\nu}_{\textrm{can}}
is given by (
2.5
).
The latter EMT is not on-shell traceless, but an EMT with this property may be obtained
by virtue of the improvement (
3.26
):

\displaystyle T^{\mu\nu}_{\textrm{conf}}=T^{\mu\nu}_{\textrm{can}}-\frac{1}{n-1}\,\partial_{\rho}\left(\tilde{J}^{\rho}\eta^{\mu\nu}-\tilde{J}^{\mu}\eta^{\rho\nu}\right)\,.

(3.30)

An explicit expression readily follows from
\tilde{J}^{\mu}=d_{A}\,F^{\mu\nu}A_{\nu}=\frac{n-2}{2}\,F^{\mu\nu}A_{\nu}
:

\displaystyle\boxed{\ (n-1)\,T^{\mu\nu}_{\textrm{conf}}=-\frac{n}{2}\,F^{\mu\rho}\partial^{\nu}A_{\rho}+\frac{n-2}{2}\,(\partial^{\nu}F^{\mu\rho})A_{\rho}+\frac{1}{4}\,\eta^{\mu\nu}F^{\rho\sigma}F_{\rho\sigma}\ }\,.

(3.31)

By construction, this EMT is
on-shell conserved and on-shell traceless

[
26
]
,
but it is neither symmetric nor gauge invariant.
For the construction of an
off-shell
traceless EMT (by application of the generalized Gell-Mann and Lévy procedure
mentioned above) and the ensuing lack of symmetry of this tensor, we refer to the general discussion in
[
22
]
.

We note that the combination of expressions (
2.2
)
and (
3.30
) yields
the relation

T^{\mu\nu}_{\textrm{conf}}\approx T^{\mu\nu}_{\textrm{phys}}-\partial_{\rho}\xi^{\rho\mu\nu}\quad\mbox{with}\quad\xi^{\rho\mu\nu}\equiv\frac{1}{2}\frac{n-2}{n-1}\,\left(F^{\rho\sigma}A_{\sigma}\eta^{\mu\nu}-F^{\mu\sigma}A_{\sigma}\eta^{\rho\nu}\right)-F^{\rho\mu}A^{\nu}=-\xi^{\mu\rho\nu}\,,

where
T^{\mu\nu}_{\textrm{phys}}
is the physical EMT of the Maxwell field,
the latter being on-shell conserved, gauge invariant and symmetric as well as traceless for
n=4
.
In four space-time dimensions,
the EMT
T^{\mu\nu}_{\textrm{phys}}
generalizes to the case of a pure non-Abelian Yang-Mills (YM) field, the Lagrangian for the latter theory
being only scale invariant for
n=4
due to the fact that the YM coupling constant is dimensionful for
n\neq 4

(e.g. see appendix of reference
[
29
]
for further discussion).

Derivation from a total derivative:

By combining our previous discussions of scalar and vector fields,
we conclude that the on-shell traceless EMT (
3.31
) for the free Maxwell field
in
n
space-time dimensions can also be obtained from the total Lagrangian

{\cal L}_{\textrm{tot}}={\cal L}+{\cal L}_{1}
with
{\cal L}=-\frac{1}{4}\,F^{\mu\nu}F_{\mu\nu}

and

\displaystyle\boxed{\ {\cal L}_{1}\equiv\partial_{\mu}k^{\mu}\ }\,,\qquad\mbox{with}\quad\boxed{\ k^{\mu}\equiv 2\xi_{n}\,F^{\mu\nu}A_{\nu}\ }\ \quad(\,\xi_{n}\equiv\frac{1}{4}\,\frac{n-2}{n-1}\,)\,.

(3.32)

This Lagrangian is quasi invariant
(i.e.
\delta{\cal L}_{\textrm{tot}}=\partial_{\mu}\Omega^{\mu}
for some vector field
(\Omega^{\mu})
)
under translations and under scale transformations
and it yields the total EMT
T^{\mu\nu}_{\textrm{tot}}=T^{\mu\nu}_{\textrm{conf}}

given in equations (
3.30
),(
3.31
).

To conclude,
we emphasize that the addition of a total derivative to a Lagrangian density (which is quasi invariant
under some global symmetry transformations) only induces the addition of a superpotential term to the Noether currents
(j^{\mu})
: since
the equation of motion following from a total derivative Lagrangian vanishes identically, we cannot generate a contribution
t^{\mu}\approx 0

to currents
(j^{\mu})
,
i.e. an equation of motion term (see eqn. (
2.4
) for the equivalence relation defining currents).
Accordingly, the physical EMT of the Maxwell field cannot be obtained from the Maxwell Lagrangian by adding a total derivative
– see eqn. (
2.2
) which involves an equation of motion term
t^{\mu\nu}\approx 0
.

4
Conformal transformations of scalar fields

In the following we address the invariance of the action for a real, free or self-interacting, massless scalar field in
n
space-time dimensions
under general conformal transformations
following the spirit of the previous section
3
3

3

We wish to thank the anonymous referee for raising this interesting question.
.
Remarkably enough, one obtains analogous results and the broader point of view of conformal symmetry sheds
further light on the general relationships.

Generalities:

We recall that the scale transformations discussed in the previous section
represent a particular instance of conformal transformations in Minkowski space-time
(\mathds{R}^{n},\eta)
.
By definition, the
conformal group associated to
(\mathds{R}^{n},\eta)

consists of all transformations
x\leadsto x^{\prime}(x)
which preserve the angles,
i.e. the Minkowski metric is preserved under these transformations up to a scale factor:

ds^{2}\leadsto\textrm{e}^{\lambda}ds^{2}

where
\lambda
represents a real parameter
[
3
]
.
The associated infinitesimal transformations
x^{\prime\mu}(x)\simeq x^{\mu}-\Xi^{\mu}(x)

are generated by
conformal Killing vector fields

\Xi\equiv\Xi^{\mu}\partial_{\mu}\equiv\Xi\cdot\partial
, i.e. solutions of the

\displaystyle\mbox{conformal Killing equation :}\qquad\partial_{\mu}\Xi_{\nu}+\partial_{\nu}\Xi_{\mu}-\frac{2}{n}\,(\partial_{\rho}\Xi^{\rho})\,\eta_{\mu\nu}=0\,.

(4.1)

The general solution of this equation reads

\displaystyle\Xi_{\mu}=a_{\mu}+\varepsilon_{\mu\nu}x^{\nu}+\rho\,x_{\mu}+2\,(c\cdot x)\,x_{\mu}-c_{\mu}x^{2}\,,

(4.2)

where
a_{\mu}\,,\rho\,,c_{\mu}
and
\varepsilon_{\mu\nu}=-\varepsilon_{\nu\mu}
are constant real parameters.
More precisely, the variables
a_{\mu},\,\varepsilon_{\mu\nu}
parametrize infinitesimal Poincaré transformations
and
\rho
labels
scale transformations (dilatations)

while
(c_{\mu})
labels
special conformal transformations (SCTs)
which are also referred to as
conformal boosts.

We note that expression (
4.2
) implies that
\partial_{\mu}\Xi^{\mu}=n\rho+2n\,(c\cdot x)
.

Under a conformal transformation generated by the vector field

\Xi=\Xi^{\mu}\partial_{\mu}
(with
\Xi^{\mu}
given by (
4.2
)),
a relativistic scalar field
\phi
transforms according to

\displaystyle\boxed{\ \delta_{\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\ }}{{\Xi}}}\phi=\Xi\cdot\partial\,\phi+\frac{1}{n}\,d\,(\partial_{\mu}\Xi^{\mu})\,\phi\ }\,.

(4.3)

As before (see eqn. (
3.2
)),
d\equiv d_{\phi}\equiv\frac{n-2}{2}
denotes the scale dimension of the scalar field
\phi

and the contribution
\Xi\cdot\partial\,\phi=L_{\Xi}\phi
to
\delta_{\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\ }}{{\Xi}}}\phi
may be viewed as the action of the
Lie derivative
L_{\Xi}
with respect to the vector field

\Xi\cdot\partial
on the field
\phi
. For instance, for a dilatation, we have
\Xi^{\mu}=\rho x^{\mu}
(whence
\partial_{\mu}\Xi^{\mu}=n\rho
)
and the transformation law (
4.3
) thus reduces
(up to a global sign factor for
\Xi^{\mu}
)
to the one encountered before, see eqn. (
3.4
).

While we dealt with translations and dilatations in the previous section,
we will now be interested as well in Lorentz transformations and (in particular) in SCTs.
We denote the vector field generating SCTs by
\zeta\cdot\partial
:
by virtue of (
4.2
), the
relation (
4.3
) then yields the following
transformation law of
\phi
under an
infinitesimal SCT parametrized by
c^{\mu}
:

\displaystyle\delta_{\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\ }}{{\zeta}}}\phi=\zeta\cdot\partial\,\phi+2d\,(c\cdot x)\,\phi\,,\qquad\mbox{with}\ \;\zeta^{\mu}=2\,(c\cdot x)\,x^{\mu}-c^{\mu}x^{2}\,.

(4.4)

Here, the last term is related
[
28
]
to the so-called
virial vector field

V^{\nu}\equiv\frac{\partial{\cal L}}{\partial(\partial^{\mu}\phi)}\,(\eta^{\mu\nu}d-\Sigma^{\mu\nu})

that we already mentioned in subsection
3.5
.
In fact, for the dynamics of a real, free (or self-interacting), massless scalar field
\phi
,
i.e. for the Lagrangian density

\displaystyle{\cal L}=\frac{1}{2}\,(\partial^{\mu}\phi)(\partial_{\mu}\phi)-\lambda\,\phi^{\frac{n}{d}}\qquad(\lambda\in\mathds{R})\,,

(4.5)

the virial field represents the divergence of a
“virial potential”
:

\displaystyle V^{\nu}=\partial_{\mu}\sigma^{\mu\nu}\,,\qquad\mbox{with}\ \;\sigma^{\mu\nu}=\frac{1}{4}\,(n-2)\,\eta^{\mu\nu}\phi^{2}\,.

(4.6)

This potential frequently shows up in the context of SCTs and it is worthwhile recalling
that the condition
V^{\nu}=\partial_{\mu}\sigma^{\mu\nu}
(for some tensor field
(\sigma^{\mu\nu})
)
ensures the invariance under SCTs for a field theory which is Poincaré and scale invariant
[
28
]
.

Conformal invariance:

The Lagrangian density (
4.5
)
is quasi invariant under the conformal transformations (
4.3
):

\displaystyle\delta_{\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\ }}{{\Xi}}}{\cal L}=\partial_{\mu}\Omega^{\mu}\,,\qquad\mbox{with}\ \;\Omega^{\mu}\equiv\Xi^{\mu}{\cal L}+d\,c^{\mu}\,\phi^{2}\,.

(4.7)

By virtue of Noether’s first theorem, the
canonical current density

(involving the arbitrary symmetry parameters
a^{\mu},\varepsilon^{\mu\nu},\dots
)

which is associated to the
conformal invariance of the action
corresponding to (
4.5
) is given by

j^{\mu}_{\textrm{can}}=\frac{\partial{\cal L}}{\partial(\partial_{\mu}\phi)}\,\delta_{\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\ }}{{\Xi}}}\phi-\Omega^{\mu}
and reads

\displaystyle\boxed{\ j^{\mu}_{\textrm{can}}=T^{\mu\nu}_{\textrm{can}}\,\Xi_{\nu}+d\,\Big[\frac{1}{2n}\,(\partial_{\nu}\Xi^{\nu})\,\partial^{\mu}-c^{\mu}\Big]\phi^{2}\ }\,.

(4.8)

For dilatations, i.e.
\Xi^{\mu}=\rho x^{\mu}
, this expression coincides with the canonical dilatation current density (
3.9
)
(with
T^{\mu\nu}_{\textrm{can}}
including the self-interaction term).

If we express the canonical EMT
T^{\mu\nu}_{\textrm{can}}
on the right hand side of relation (
4.8
)
in terms of the new improved EMT
T^{\mu\nu}_{\textrm{conf}}
of the scalar field
\phi
(as given by eqn. (
3.10
)),
then one expects to obtain (up to a superpotential term) the Besselhagen form

j^{\mu}_{\textrm{tot}}\equiv j^{\mu}_{\textrm{conf}}\equiv T^{\mu\nu}_{\textrm{conf}}\,\Xi_{\nu}
for the
canonical current
j^{\mu}_{\textrm{can}}
: indeed, since
T^{\mu\nu}_{\textrm{conf}}
is symmetric as well as on-shell conserved
and traceless, we then have (by virtue of the conformal Killing equation (
4.1
))

\displaystyle\partial_{\mu}j^{\mu}_{\textrm{tot}}=(\underbrace{\partial_{\mu}T^{\mu\nu}_{\textrm{conf}}}_{\approx\,0})\,\Xi_{\nu}+T^{\mu\nu}_{\textrm{conf}}\,(\partial_{\mu}\Xi_{\nu})=\frac{1}{2}\,T^{\mu\nu}_{\textrm{conf}}\,(\underbrace{\partial_{\mu}\Xi_{\nu}+\partial_{\nu}\Xi_{\mu}}_{=\,\frac{2}{n}\,(\partial_{\rho}\Xi^{\rho})\,\eta_{\mu\nu}})=\frac{1}{n}\,(\partial_{\rho}\Xi^{\rho})\,\underbrace{T^{\mu}_{\textrm{conf}\,\mu}}_{\approx\,0}\,.

(4.9)

We saw that this is indeed the case for the dilatation current, see eqn. (
3.11
).
In the following we address this issue in complete generality for all conformal transformations.

Derivation of Besselhagen form by adding a total derivative to the Lagrangian:

The Lagrangian density (
3.13
) with

\xi
taking the conformal value

\xi_{n}\equiv\frac{1}{4}\,\frac{n-2}{n-1}
,
i.e.
{\cal L}_{1}\equiv\partial_{\mu}k^{\mu}
with

k^{\mu}\equiv-\xi_{n}\,\partial^{\mu}\phi^{2}
, is quasi invariant under
the conformal transformations (
4.3
):

\displaystyle\delta_{\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\ }}{{\Xi}}}{\cal L}_{1}=\partial_{\mu}\Omega_{1}^{\mu}\,,\qquad\mbox{with}\ \;\Omega_{1}^{\mu}\equiv\Xi^{\mu}{\cal L}_{1}-2\xi_{n}\,(n-2)\,c^{\mu}\,\phi^{2}\,.

(4.10)

Application of relation (
2.2
) for the Noether current density associated to
{\cal L}_{1}

yields a conformal group current density which is a superpotential term (the latter being determined by the
EMT superpotential
\chi^{\rho\mu\nu}
introduced in eqn. (
3.17
)):

\displaystyle\boxed{\ j^{\mu}_{1}=-\partial_{\rho}(\chi^{\rho\mu\nu}\,\Xi_{\nu})\ }\,.

(4.11)

The quasi invariance of the total Lagrangian density
{\cal L}_{\textrm{tot}}\equiv{\cal L}+{\cal L}_{1}

under conformal transformations now yields the
total conformal group current density

\displaystyle\boxed{\ j^{\mu}_{\textrm{tot}}\equiv j^{\mu}_{\textrm{can}}+j^{\mu}_{1}=T^{\mu\nu}_{\textrm{conf}}\,\Xi_{\nu}+\partial_{\nu}Y^{\nu\mu}\ }\,,

(4.12)

with

\displaystyle\boxed{\ Y^{\nu\mu}\equiv-\xi_{n}\,\varepsilon^{\nu\mu}\,\phi^{2}-2\xi_{n}\,\big(x^{\nu}\,c^{\mu}-x^{\mu}c^{\nu}\big)\,\phi^{2}=-Y^{\mu\nu}\ }\,.

(4.13)

Thus,
j^{\mu}_{\textrm{tot}}

has the Besselhagen form up to a superpotential term.

The latter term shows up for Lorentz transformations and for SCTs. This results from the
second term in expression (
4.8
) as well as from the
x
-dependence of
\Xi^{\mu}

which contributes to the total derivative (
4.11
): for dilatations these two different
contributions to
j^{\mu}_{\textrm{tot}}
compensate each other so that it does not contain a superpotential
term (as we already found in the previous section).

In summary,
the addition of the total derivative
{\cal L}_{1}\equiv\partial_{\mu}k^{\mu}

(with
k^{\mu}\equiv-\xi_{n}\,\partial^{\mu}\phi^{2}
)
to the Lagrangian density (
4.5
) of a scalar field allows us to derive the improvements
for the conserved currents associated to conformal invariance in a constructive way.

Return to special conformal transformations:

For SCTs, the current
j^{\mu}_{\textrm{can}}
becomes the so-called
canonical conformal current

which we denote by
K^{\mu}_{\textrm{can}}
:

\displaystyle K^{\mu}_{\textrm{can}}=T^{\mu\nu}_{\textrm{can}}\,\zeta_{\nu}+d\,\big[(c\cdot x)\,\partial^{\mu}-c^{\mu}\big]\phi^{2}\,.

(4.14)

In this case, relation (
4.12
) implies the following expression for the
canonical current density
K^{\mu}_{\textrm{can}}

in terms of the new improved EMT
T^{\mu\nu}_{\textrm{conf}}
:

\displaystyle\boxed{\ K^{\mu}_{\textrm{can}}=T^{\mu\nu}_{\textrm{conf}}\,\zeta_{\nu}+\partial_{\nu}(X^{\nu\mu}-X^{\mu\nu})\ }\,,\qquad\mbox{with}\ \;\boxed{\ X^{\nu\mu}\equiv\xi_{n}\big(\zeta^{\nu}\,\partial^{\mu}+2c^{\nu}x^{\mu}\big)\phi^{2}\ }\,.

(4.15)

The results (
4.14
) and (
4.15
) coincide
in four space-time dimensions
with the corresponding results given in reference
[
30
]

(where they are derived by expressing
T^{\mu\nu}_{\textrm{can}}

in terms of
T^{\mu\nu}_{\textrm{conf}}
).

Application in supersymmetric field theory

Invariance under scale transformations and more generally under the superconformal group
(which also involves the conformal group as well as supersymmetry transformations)
plays an important role in supersymmetric field theories. This fact has already been pointed
out in the pioneering work of J. Wess and B. Zumino
[
31
,
32
]

and has been further explored later on
[
33
]
, e.g. see
[
34
]

and references therein.
In these investigations, the currents (or the current superfield englobing these currents) are improved by hand
rather than dynamically, i.e. by adding a total derivative to the Lagrangian density. (An exception
is the article by S. V. Kuzmin and D. G. C. McKeon
[
27
]

(which has essentially gone unnoticed in the literature)
which has motivated the procedure that we will follow in the sequel and
on which we will comment at the end of this section.)
More precisely, we will show that the results and expressions presented in section
3

provide a simple derivation of the supermultiplet
of currents for the free, massless Wess-Zumino (WZ) model in four space-time dimensions, i.e. the supersymmetric extension of the
Lagrangian density

{\cal L}\equiv(\partial^{\mu}\bar{\phi})(\partial_{\mu}\phi)
for a complex scalar field
\phi
.
We limit ourselves to a concise presentation while postponing a more comprehensive discussion of currents in supersymmetry to a separate
work
[
35
]
. In this section,
we rely on the well-known basics of global supersymmetry as presented for instance in the
textbooks
[
36
,
37
,
38
,
39
,
40
]
whose notation and conventions are also used
here
4
4

4

Thus, in this section, we consider
the mostly plus signature for the Minkowski metric. However, by contrast to the mentioned textbooks, we denote the
indices of space-time coordinates by a greek letter
\mu,\nu,\dots
(as in the rest of our paper)
rather than a latin letter
m,n,\dots
.

Supermultiplet of currents for the WZ model:

For
n=4
, a
scalar superfield

\Phi
satisfies the chirality constraint
\bar{D}_{{\dot{\alpha}}}\Phi=0
and thereby admits the component field expansion

\displaystyle\Phi(y,\theta)=A(y)+\sqrt{2}\,\theta\,\psi(y)+\theta^{2}F(y)\qquad\mbox{with}\ \;y^{\mu}\equiv x^{\mu}+\textrm{i}\,\theta\sigma^{\mu}\bar{\theta}\,.

(5.1)

Here,
A
and
F
denote complex scalar fields and
\psi\equiv(\psi^{\alpha})_{\alpha=1,2}
a Weyl
2
-spinor.
By complex conjugation, we obtain an
anti-chiral superfield
\Phi^{\dagger}
which satisfies
D_{\alpha}\Phi^{\dagger}=0
and which gathers the space-time fields

\bar{A},\bar{F}
and
\bar{\psi}\equiv(\bar{\psi}_{\dot{\alpha}})_{{\dot{\alpha}}=1,2}
.
The product of
\Phi^{\dagger}
and
\Phi
yields the
vector superfield

V\equiv\Phi^{\dagger}\Phi

which is real-valued, i.e.
V^{\dagger}=V
.
The component field expansion of a generic vector superfield reads

\displaystyle V(x,\theta,\bar{\theta})=C(x)+\theta\chi(x)+\bar{\theta}\bar{\chi}(x)+\cdots+\theta^{2}\,\bar{\theta}\bar{\lambda}(x)+\bar{\theta}^{2}\,\theta{\lambda}(x)+\theta^{2}\,\bar{\theta}^{2}\,D(x)\,.

(5.2)

(Here, we do not consider the reparametrization of the higher component fields
\lambda,\bar{\lambda},D
of
V

in terms of the lower ones
C,\chi,\bar{\chi}
which is usually chosen
in relationship with supersymmetric gauge field theories
[
36
]
so as to ensure the supergauge invariance
of the photino field
\lambda
and of the auxiliary field
D

[
39
]
.)
For concreteness and for later reference, we spell out
[
36
]
the explicit expressions of some components of the
superfield
V\equiv\Phi^{\dagger}\Phi
following from the expansion (
5.1
):

\displaystyle C

\displaystyle=\bar{A}A\,,\qquad\chi=\sqrt{2}\,\bar{A}\psi\,,

(5.3a)

\displaystyle\lambda

\displaystyle=-\frac{\textrm{i}}{\sqrt{2}}\,\sigma^{\mu}\left[\bar{\psi}(\partial_{\mu}A)-(\partial_{\mu}\bar{\psi})A\right]+\sqrt{2}\,\psi\bar{F}\,,\dots\,,

(5.3b)

\displaystyle D

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{4}\,\bar{A}\,\Box A+\frac{1}{4}\,(\Box\bar{A})A-\frac{1}{2}\,(\partial^{\mu}\bar{A})(\partial_{\mu}A)-\frac{\textrm{i}}{2}\,\psi\sigma^{\mu}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\leftrightarrow}}{{\partial}}_{\mu}\!\bar{\psi}+\bar{F}F\,.

(5.3c)

Under an
infinitesimal global supersymmetry variation

parametrized by constant Weyl
2
-spinors
\zeta\equiv(\zeta^{\alpha})_{\alpha=1,2}

and
\bar{\zeta}\equiv(\bar{\zeta}_{\dot{\alpha}})_{{\dot{\alpha}}=1,2}
, the auxiliary field
D
transforms
into a total derivative:

\displaystyle\delta_{\zeta}D=\frac{\textrm{i}}{2}\,\partial_{\mu}\left(\zeta\sigma^{\mu}\bar{\lambda}-\lambda\sigma^{\mu}\bar{\zeta}\right)\,.

(5.4)

Thus, a Lagrangian density
{\cal L}
(for the supermultiplet
\Phi
) which is quasi invariant under
global supersymmetry transformations is obtained by considering the highest (i.e.
D
) component
of the real superfield
V\equiv\Phi^{\dagger}\Phi
: by virtue of (
5.3c
) we have

\displaystyle{\cal L}\equiv\left.(\Phi^{\dagger}\Phi)\right|_{\theta^{2}\bar{\theta}^{2}}=D={\cal L}_{\textrm{WZ}}+\tilde{{\cal L}}_{1}\,,

(5.5)

with

\displaystyle{\cal L}_{\textrm{WZ}}

\displaystyle\equiv-\partial^{\mu}\bar{A}\,\partial_{\mu}A-\frac{\textrm{i}}{2}\,\psi\sigma^{\mu}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\leftrightarrow}}{{\partial}}_{\mu}\!\bar{\psi}+\bar{F}F

(5.6a)

\displaystyle\tilde{{\cal L}}_{1}

\displaystyle\equiv\partial_{\mu}\tilde{k}^{\mu}\,,\qquad\qquad\qquad\mbox{where}\quad\tilde{k}^{\mu}\equiv\frac{1}{4}\,\partial^{\mu}(\bar{A}A)\,.

(5.6b)

Here,
{\cal L}_{\textrm{WZ}}
is the standard Lagrangian density for the
free,
massless WZ-model
and
\tilde{{\cal L}}_{1}
represents a total derivative which is usually discarded
in the literature due to the fact that it does not contribute to the
equations of motion.
If we do not discard the contribution
\tilde{{\cal L}}_{1}
to the Lagrangian density (
5.5
),
then we conclude from the considerations of section
3.3

that the EMT following from the translation invariance of
{\cal L}

is the
CCJ-improved EMT for the WZ-model
,

\displaystyle\boxed{\ \begin{array}[]{rl}-T^{\mu\nu}_{\textrm{CCJ}}=(\partial^{\mu}\bar{A})(\partial^{\nu}A)&+\,(\partial^{\nu}\bar{A})(\partial^{\mu}A)-\frac{1}{3}\,(\partial^{\mu}\partial^{\nu}-\eta^{\mu\nu}\Box)(A\bar{A})\\
&+\,\frac{\textrm{i}}{4}\,[\psi\sigma^{\mu}\partial^{\nu}\bar{\psi}+\bar{\psi}\bar{\sigma}^{\mu}\partial^{\nu}{\psi}+(\mu\leftrightarrow\nu)]+\eta^{\mu\nu}{\cal L}_{\textrm{WZ}}\end{array}\ }

provided we rescale
\tilde{{\cal L}}_{1}
(i.e.

\tilde{k}^{\mu}
) by the numerical factor
\frac{4}{3}
, i.e. replace
\tilde{{\cal L}}_{1}
by

\displaystyle{\cal L}_{1}\equiv\frac{4}{3}\,\tilde{{\cal L}}_{1}=\partial_{\mu}{k}^{\mu}\,,\qquad\mbox{with}\quad{k}^{\mu}\equiv\frac{1}{3}\,\partial^{\mu}(\bar{A}A)\,.

(5.9)

In other words, we consider the total Lagrangian density

\displaystyle\boxed{\ {\cal L}_{\textrm{tot}}\equiv{\cal L}_{\textrm{WZ}}+{\cal L}_{1}\ }\qquad\mbox{with ${\cal L}_{1}$ given by~\eqref{eq:ConfTD}}\,.

(5.10)

Since
\frac{1}{3}=\frac{1}{4}+\frac{1}{12}
, this Lagrangian density may as well be thought of
as resulting from
{\cal L}={\cal L}_{\textrm{WZ}}+\tilde{{\cal L}}_{1}

by adding a term
\frac{1}{12}\,\Box(\bar{A}A)
which is of the same form as
\tilde{{\cal L}}_{1}
:
this trivial remark will be exploited in a non-trivial manner at the end of this section.

Next, we determine the supersymmetry current density
(j_{\textrm{tot}}^{\mu})
,
i.e. the on-shell conserved current associated to the invariance of
{\cal L}_{\textrm{tot}}
under supersymmetry transformations
of the multiplet
\Phi
, the latter transformations being given by

\displaystyle\delta_{\zeta}A=\sqrt{2}\,\zeta\psi\,,\qquad\delta_{\zeta}\psi_{\alpha}=\textrm{i}\sqrt{2}\,(\sigma^{\mu}\bar{\zeta})_{\alpha}\partial_{\mu}A+\sqrt{2}\,\zeta_{\alpha}F\,,\qquad\delta_{\zeta}F=\textrm{i}\sqrt{2}\,\bar{\zeta}\bar{\sigma}^{\mu}\partial_{\mu}\psi\,.

(5.11)

For the part
{\cal L}_{\textrm{WZ}}
of the Lagrangian density
{\cal L}_{\textrm{tot}}
,
the results are given in the literature
[
31
,
37
]
:
by writing the on-shell conserved
supersymmetry currents

j^{\mu}\equiv\zeta^{\alpha}\,q^{\mu}_{\ \alpha}+\bar{\zeta}_{{\dot{\alpha}}}\,\bar{q}^{\mu\,{\dot{\alpha}}}
,
we have the
supersymmetry current associated to
{\cal L}_{\textrm{WZ}}
:

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\,q_{\textrm{WZ}}^{\mu}=(\sigma^{\nu}\bar{\sigma}^{\mu}{\psi})\,\partial_{\nu}\bar{A}\,,\qquad\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\,\bar{q}_{\textrm{WZ}}^{\,\mu}=(\bar{\sigma}^{\nu}{\sigma}^{\mu}\bar{\psi})\,\partial_{\nu}{A}\,.

(5.12)

In this respect, we only note that the derivation of these expressions involves
the supersymmetry variation of
{\cal L}_{\textrm{WZ}}

[
37
]

\displaystyle-\delta_{\zeta}{\cal L}_{\textrm{WZ}}=-\partial_{\mu}{\Omega}^{\mu}\equiv\partial_{\mu}\left[\sqrt{2}\,(\zeta\psi)\,\partial^{\mu}\bar{A}-\frac{\textrm{i}}{\sqrt{2}}\,(\zeta\sigma^{\mu}\bar{\psi})\,F+\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\,(\zeta\sigma^{\nu}\bar{\sigma}^{\mu}{\psi})\,\partial_{\nu}\bar{A}\right]+\mbox{c.c.}\,.

(5.13)

The supersymmetry current
\tilde{j}^{\mu}_{1}
associated to the Lagrangian density

\tilde{{\cal L}}_{1}\equiv\partial_{\mu}\tilde{k}^{\mu}
(satisfying
\delta_{\zeta}\tilde{{\cal L}}_{1}=\partial_{\mu}\tilde{\Omega}_{1}^{\mu}

has the general form given by eqn. (

\tilde{j}^{\mu}_{1}=\delta_{\zeta}\tilde{k}^{\mu}-\tilde{\Omega}_{1}^{\mu}

Here, the contribution
\delta_{\zeta}\tilde{k}^{\mu}
readily follows from (

\displaystyle\delta_{\zeta}\tilde{k}^{\mu}=\frac{1}{2\sqrt{2}}\,\zeta\,\partial^{\mu}(\psi\bar{A})+\mbox{c.c.}

(5.14)

The quantity

\displaystyle\delta_{\zeta}\tilde{{\cal L}}_{1}=\delta_{\zeta}D-\delta_{\zeta}{\cal L}_{\textrm{WZ}}\qquad\mbox{with}\ \;D\equiv\left.(\Phi^{\dagger}\Phi)\right|_{\theta^{2}\bar{\theta}^{2}}\,.

The supersymmetry transformation of

\Phi^{\dagger}
and
\Phi

\displaystyle\delta_{\zeta}D=

\displaystyle\;\partial_{\mu}\left[\frac{1}{2\sqrt{2}}\,\zeta\,(\partial^{\mu}\psi)\bar{A}-\frac{1}{2\sqrt{2}}\,(\zeta\psi)\,\partial^{\mu}\bar{A}\right.

(5.16)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\left.\ -\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\,(\zeta\sigma^{\mu\nu}\partial_{\nu}\psi)\bar{A}+\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\,(\zeta\sigma^{\mu\nu}\psi)\,\partial_{\nu}\bar{A}+\frac{\textrm{i}}{\sqrt{2}}\,({\zeta}{\sigma}^{\mu}\bar{\psi})\,F\right]+\mbox{c.c.}\,.

Substitution of this expression and of (

\displaystyle\delta_{\zeta}\tilde{{\cal L}}_{1}=\partial_{\mu}\tilde{\Omega}_{1}^{\mu}\,,\qquad\mbox{with}\quad\tilde{\Omega}_{1}^{\mu}=\frac{1}{2\sqrt{2}}\,\zeta\,\partial^{\mu}(\psi\bar{A})-\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\,(\zeta\sigma^{\mu\nu}\partial_{\nu}({\psi}\bar{A})+\mbox{c.c.}\,.

\displaystyle j^{\mu}_{1}\equiv\frac{4}{3}\,\tilde{j}^{\mu}_{1}=\frac{4}{3}\,(\delta_{\zeta}\tilde{k}^{\mu}-\tilde{\Omega}_{1}^{\mu})=\frac{4}{3}\,\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\,\zeta\sigma^{\mu\nu}\partial_{\nu}({\psi}\bar{A})+\mbox{c.c.}\,.

As expected on general grounds (see next section) it represents a superpotential term.

Assessment:

In summary,
the only details that we have put in by hand is the choice of the numerical factor in front of the total derivative

in eqn. (

for the total EMT (namely an on-shell traceless EMT)
and, upon multiplication by an extra factor two (i.e. addition of an identical term),
the proper expression of the supersymmetry current density (namely a gamma-traceless
expression)

5

We do not have a plausible explanation for
the extra factor of two apart from the fact that it appears to preclude a reformulation
in terms of superfields.
. Henceforth, this derivation of the supermultiplet of conserved currents is both simple and constructive

We will discuss it further in a separate work

6
Main point: Current improvement induced by a total derivative Lagrangian

General result:

Consider a Lagrangian density
{\cal L}
which is quasi invariant
under an infinitesimal symmetry transformation
{\delta}\varphi(x)\equiv\varphi^{\prime}(x)-\varphi(x)
, i.e.

\displaystyle\delta{\cal L}=\partial_{\mu}\Omega^{\mu}\quad\mbox{(off-shell)}\,.

(6.1)

Variation of
{\cal L}
yields (see equations (
A.2
)-(
A.3
))

\displaystyle\delta{\cal L}=\frac{\delta S}{\delta\varphi}\,\delta\varphi+\partial_{\mu}J^{\mu}\,.

(6.2)

Combination of these two equations results in the on-shell conservation equation
\partial_{\mu}j^{\mu}\approx 0

for the current density
j^{\mu}\equiv J^{\mu}-\Omega^{\mu}
.

Now suppose the Lagrangian density is given by a total derivative, i.e.
{\cal L}=\partial_{\mu}k^{\mu}
.
In this case, the derivative
\delta S/\delta\varphi
vanishes identically and we thus have the
off-shell relation

\partial_{\mu}j^{\mu}=0
for
j^{\mu}\equiv J^{\mu}-\Omega^{\mu}
.
Moreover, the variation (
6.2
) of
{\cal L}
now writes

\displaystyle\delta{\cal L}=\delta(\partial_{\mu}k^{\mu})=\partial_{\mu}(\delta k^{\mu})\,.

(6.3)

In summary,
the
current density
(j^{\mu})
that is associated to a Lagrangian
{\cal L}=\partial_{\mu}k^{\mu}
which is quasi invariant
(i.e.
\delta{\cal L}=\partial_{\mu}\Omega^{\mu}
)
reads

\displaystyle\boxed{\ j^{\mu}=\delta k^{\mu}-\Omega^{\mu}\ }\,,

(6.4)

and it is
conserved off-shell
.
More precisely, by virtue of its derivation and of the property
\partial_{\mu}j^{\mu}=0
,

the current density
(j^{\mu})
is defined up to a superpotential term and it represents itself
a superpotential term
.

For instance, if
k^{\mu}
does not explicitly depend on
x
, the Lagrangian density
{\cal L}=\partial_{\mu}k^{\mu}
is
quasi invariant under translations and
for these symmetry transformations we have

\Omega^{\mu}=a^{\mu}{\cal L}
as well as
\delta k^{\mu}=a_{\nu}\partial^{\nu}k^{\mu}
: expression (
6.4
)
now yields the result

\displaystyle j^{\mu}=T^{\mu\nu}a_{\nu}\qquad\mbox{with}\quad T^{\mu\nu}=\partial^{\nu}k^{\mu}-\eta^{\mu\nu}\,\partial_{\rho}k^{\rho}\,,

(6.5)

i.e.

\displaystyle\boxed{\ T^{\mu\nu}=-\partial_{\rho}\psi^{\rho\mu\nu}\ }\,,\qquad\mbox{with}\quad\boxed{\ \psi^{\rho\mu\nu}\equiv k^{\rho}\eta^{\mu\nu}-k^{\mu}\eta^{\rho\nu}=-\psi^{\mu\rho\nu}\ }\,.

(6.6)

Thus,
the EMT associated to a Lagrangian given by a total derivative represents a superpotential term.

The conserved current density (
6.4
) can also be rewritten in terms
of
passive symmetry transformations
of coordinates and fields (see appendix
C
for a discussion of the latter),

\displaystyle\tilde{\delta}x^{\mu}\equiv x^{\prime\mu}-x^{\mu}\,,\qquad\tilde{\delta}\varphi(x)\equiv\varphi^{\prime}(x^{\prime})-\varphi(x)\,.

(6.7)

It then follows from
\delta k^{\mu}=\tilde{\delta}k^{\mu}-\tilde{\delta}x_{\nu}\,\partial^{\nu}k^{\mu}

and
{\Omega}^{\mu}=\tilde{\Omega}^{\mu}-\tilde{\delta}x^{\mu}\,{\cal L}
that

\displaystyle j^{\mu}=-T^{\mu\nu}\,\tilde{\delta}x_{\nu}+\tilde{\delta}k^{\mu}-\tilde{\Omega}^{\mu}\,,\qquad\mbox{with $T^{\mu\nu}$ given by~\eqref{eq:EMTtotDer1} }\,.

(6.8)

This result coincides with the expression for
j^{\mu}
which is given without a detailed derivation in reference
[
26
]
.
A concise derivation from scratch is presented at the end of appendix
C
.

We note that, by virtue of its derivation,
a superpotential term (
6.4
) (which is always conserved without use of the field equations)
can only result from a trivial Lagrangian density, i.e. from a total derivative
{\cal L}=\partial_{\mu}k^{\mu}

for which the field equations are trivially satisfied.

Example of scale invariance:

For the example of a real free massless scalar field
\phi
in
n
space-time dimensions
and the Lagrangian density
{\cal L}_{1}\equiv\partial_{\mu}k^{\mu}
with
k^{\mu}\equiv-\xi\,\partial^{\mu}\phi^{2}

(see eqn. (
3.13
)), relation (
6.4
) (or equivalently (
6.8
))
gives the result (
3.17
) with
\chi^{\rho\mu\nu}=\psi^{\rho\mu\nu}
for the EMT.
Similarly, with
\Omega^{\mu}=-\rho\,x^{\mu}{\cal L}
,
\delta k^{\mu}=-\rho\,[x\cdot\partial+(n-1)]k^{\mu}

and
-\rho\,j^{\mu}\equiv\delta k^{\mu}-\Omega^{\mu}
,
we readily obtain the expression (
3.20
) for the dilatation current.
In this respect we note that the scale transformation of the scalar field
\phi
identifies
it as a scaling or “quasi-primary” field in
n
space-time dimensions
[
21
]

and similarly for
\phi^{2}
, but its derivative
k^{\mu}\propto\partial^{\mu}\phi^{2}
does not represent such a field: this
reflects itself in the fact that the scale factor
(n-1)
in the transformation law of
k^{\mu}

does not coincide with the canonical scale dimension
\frac{n-2}{2}
of a vector field
(A^{\mu})
.

Particular instance:

Though the result (
6.4
) is valid quite generally, it may not be conclusive in some instances
in the sense that it may entail a vanishing expression for
the representative
(j^{\mu})
of the current density.
For instance,
in two space-time dimensions, the Lagrangian density may have a trivial topological form
involving the Levi-Civita symbol
\varepsilon^{\mu\nu}=-\varepsilon^{\nu\mu}
:

\displaystyle\boxed{\ {\cal L}=\partial_{\nu}k^{\nu}\qquad\mbox{with}\quad k^{\nu}=\varepsilon^{\nu\mu}{\cal L}_{\mu}\,,\quad\delta{\cal L}_{\mu}=0\ }

(6.9)

Then, we have
\delta k^{\mu}=0
and
\Omega^{\mu}=0
,
whence
j^{\mu}\equiv\delta k^{\mu}-\Omega^{\mu}=0
.
In this case, the off-shell conserved current density associated to the invariance of
{\cal L}_{\mu}
(and thus of
{\cal L}
)
is expected to have the topological form

\displaystyle\boxed{\ j^{\mu}=\partial_{\nu}(\varepsilon^{\nu\mu}R)\qquad\mbox{for some $R$}\ }\,.

(6.10)

This result may eventually be derived by applying the method of Gell-Mann and
Lévy
[
13
,
14
]
to determine conserved currents, i.e.
one considers symmetry parameters which are space-time dependent.
As a matter of fact, we will encounter this instance in subsection
7.2
below
(see expressions in eqn. (
7.46
))
for the two-dimensional sigma model with values in the Lie group
SU(2)\simeq S^{3}
, the
3
-sphere
S^{3}
being endowed with
the so-called Berger metric (as well as for various generalizations of this model).

In our concluding remarks we will come back to the general results discussed in this section.

7
Two-dimensional integrable models based on a flat improved current

In this section, we will discuss several classes of two-dimensional sigma models where the addition of a total derivative
to the Lagrangian density yields an improvement of the conserved Noether current
(j^{\mu})

which is associated to the natural symmetry of the action functional: this improvement ensures
that the current
(j^{\mu})
satisfies a zero curvature (i.e. flatness) condition whereas the
non-improved current does not do so. The existence of such a flat conserved current allows one to construct an infinite number
of non-local conserved charges and thereby to establish the classical integrability of the model
(by applying for instance the BIZZ-algorithm
[
41
]
).
For the sake of clarity, we first address in some detail
a
1
-parameter deformation of the two-dimensional sigma model with target space
SU(2)\simeq S^{3}
.
A
2
-parameter deformation of this model has already been introduced in 1981 by I. V. Cherednik
[
42
]
who
argued its classical integrability by viewing the classical equation of motion as the quasi-classical limit of a quantum model
which is integrable by the quantum inverse scattering method.
The Lagrangian for the
1
-parameter deformation of the
SU(2)
sigma model has been spelled out
and discussed in references
[
43
,
44
,
45
,
46
]
.
The improvement of the current which ensures the flatness of this current has first been introduced
in reference
[
46
]
. Our presentation rather relies on the more recent work
of I. Kawaguchi and K. Yoshida
[
47
,
48
,
49
]
who
considered the very same improvement for this current so as
to derive the classical integrability of the model and who added a total derivative to the Lagrangian density so as to generate this improved current.
As a matter of fact, the latter argument has been generalized to a variety of similar models and we will briefly outline these
results in section
7.3
so as to emphasize the analogies between these fairly different theories.
Our presentation is self-contained and includes a general proof of the zero curvature condition for the improved current
(appendix
E
).
For further background and details on sigma models, we refer to the works
[
50
]
.
It is worth noting that the afore-mentioned deformations of the two-dimensional
SU(2)
principal model
have triggered the introduction and study of other families of deformations besides those considered
in section
7.3
(in particular following the works
[
51
,
52
]
),
e.g. see the reviews
[
53
]
.

7.1
Reminder: Two-dimensional
SU(2)
principal chiral model

Geometric framework:

The two-dimensional
SU(2)
principal chiral model
[
54
,
55
,
56
]

(also referred to as
SU(2)\otimes SU(2)
-invariant or
O(4)
non-linear sigma model)
is a non-linear sigma model on the
source space

(\mathds{R}^{2},\eta)
(i.e. two-dimensional Minkowski space-time endowed with the metric tensor

\eta\equiv\textrm{diag}\,(+1,-1)
) and with the
target space

G=SU(2)
, i.e. the compact matrix Lie group
SU(2)

which can identified geometrically with the unit sphere
S^{3}

(homeomorphism
SU(2)\simeq S^{3}
).
For the Lie algebra
\textbf{{g}}=su(2)
associated to the Lie group
SU(2)
we consider a basis
(T^{a})_{a=1,2,3}

consisting of anti-Hermitean matrices
T^{a}
satisfying

\displaystyle[T^{a},T^{b}]={\varepsilon^{ab}}_{c}T^{c}\,,\qquad\kappa^{ab}\equiv\textrm{Tr}\,(T^{a}T^{b})=-\frac{1}{2}\,\delta^{ab}\,.

(7.1)

Here,
\varepsilon^{abc}
denotes the components of the Levi-Civita symbol normalized by
\varepsilon^{123}=1

and

(\kappa^{ab})
represents the Cartan-Killing scalar product in
su(2)
for which we have chosen the same
normalisation as in references
[
48
,
57
]
.
The latter implies that

\displaystyle A\in su(2)\qquad\Longleftrightarrow\qquad A=A^{a}T^{a}\quad\mbox{with}\ \;A^{a}=-2\,\textrm{Tr}\,(AT^{a})\,.

(7.2)

An explicit realization of the matrices
T^{a}
(fundamental representation of
su(2)
)
is given by
T^{a}=-\frac{\textrm{i}}{2}\,\sigma^{a}
where
(\sigma^{a})_{a=1,2,3}
denote the
Pauli matrices.

Thus, the
fields
of the model are given by maps

\displaystyle\Phi\,:\,\mathds{R}^{2}\ \longrightarrow

\displaystyle\ \;G

\displaystyle x\ \longmapsto

\displaystyle\ \Phi(x)\equiv g\,,

(7.3)

the space-time coordinates being labeled by
x\equiv(x^{\mu})_{\mu=0,1}\equiv(t,\sigma)
.
The action of the model is defined by considering the
Maurer-Cartan form on
G
,
i.e.
the left-invariant
g
-valued
1
-form on
G
given by

\omega\equiv g^{-1}dg\in\Omega^{1}(G,\textbf{{g}})
.
More precisely, a field
\Phi:\mathds{R}^{2}\to G
allows us to pull back this
1
-form on
G
to a
1
-form on
\mathds{R}^{2}
:

\displaystyle\Phi^{*}\,:\,\Omega^{1}(G,\textbf{{g}})\ \longrightarrow

\displaystyle\ \;\Omega^{1}(\mathds{R}^{2},\textbf{{g}})

\displaystyle\omega\ \longmapsto

\displaystyle\ \Phi^{*}\omega=(g^{-1}\partial_{\mu}g)\,dx^{\mu}\,.

(7.4)

In the last expression and in the sequel, the fields are denoted by
x\mapsto g(x)\in G
(cf. (
7.3
)).
For these fields one imposes the usual
boundary condition

\lim_{\sigma\to\pm\infty}g(t,\sigma)=\mathds{1}_{2}
or
[
48
]
, somewhat more generally,

\lim_{\sigma\to\pm\infty}g(t,\sigma)=g_{\pm}
(= given constant group element).
Here, the convergence is assumed to be given by a
rapid
decrease.
This implies that the

\displaystyle\mbox{ $\textbf{{g}}$-valued \emph{current density} components}\qquad\boxed{\ J^{\mu}\equiv J^{\mu}_{a}T^{a}\equiv g^{-1}\partial^{\mu}g\ }\,,

(7.5)

vanish rapidly at spatial infinity, i.e. for
\sigma\to\pm\infty
.
Since the covariant vector field

(J_{\mu})
corresponds to the components of the Maurer-Cartan
1
-form,
it satisfies the so-called
Maurer-Cartan equation

or
flatness condition
or

\displaystyle\mbox{zero curvature condition for $(J_{\mu})$ :}\qquad\boxed{\ \partial_{\mu}J_{\nu}-\partial_{\nu}J_{\mu}+[J_{\mu},J_{\nu}]=0\ }\,,

(7.6)

(as can readily be checked).
Accordingly, the
g
-valued
1
-form
J_{\mu}dx^{\mu}
on
\mathds{R}^{2}

can be viewed as a flat connection (associated with the symmetry group
SU(2)
).

Dynamics:

The
action functional
of the model is quadratic in
\Phi^{*}\omega
and reads

\displaystyle S_{0}[g]\equiv\int_{\mathds{R}^{2}}\textrm{Tr}\,\big[(\Phi^{*}\omega)\wedge\star(\Phi^{*}\omega)\big]\,,

(7.7)

where
\star
denotes the Hodge dual of differential forms (associated to the Minkowski metric on
\mathds{R}^{2}
).
More explicitly, we have
S_{0}[g]=\int_{\mathds{R}^{2}}dt\,d\sigma\,{\cal L}_{0}

with the
Lagrangian density

\displaystyle\boxed{\ {\cal L}_{0}=\textrm{Tr}\,\big[(g^{-1}\partial^{\mu}g)(g^{-1}\partial_{\mu}g)\big]=\kappa_{ab}(g^{-1}\partial^{\mu}g)^{a}(g^{-1}\partial_{\mu}g)^{b}=\kappa_{ab}J^{a\mu}J_{\mu}^{b}\ }\,.

(7.8)

Under an arbitrary variation
\delta g(x)
of the field
x\mapsto g(x)
subject to the considered boundary condition, the action changes by an amount

\displaystyle\delta S_{0}[g]=-2\int_{\mathds{R}^{2}}d^{2}x\,\textrm{Tr}\,\big[(g^{-1}\delta g)\,\partial^{\mu}(g^{-1}\partial_{\mu}g)\big]\,.

(7.9)

Thus, the field
x\mapsto g(x)
admits the

\displaystyle\mbox{equation of motion}\quad\partial^{\mu}(g^{-1}\partial_{\mu}g)=0\,,\qquad\mbox{i.e.}\quad\boxed{\ \partial^{\mu}J_{\mu}\approx 0\ }\,.

(7.10)

Here,
J_{\mu}
denotes the
g
-valued current introduced in (
7.5
)
and we use Dirac’s notation
\approx
for an equality which holds by virtue of the equations of motion (weak equality).

Equivalent formulation:

Instead of the Maurer-Cartan
1
-form which is left-invariant, one can rewrite
[
57
]
the
Lagrangian density of the model in terms of
the components
K_{\mu}\equiv(\partial_{\mu}g)g^{-1}

of the
right-invariant
1
-form

(dg)g^{-1}
, i.e. of

\Phi^{*}(dgg^{-1})=K_{\mu}dx^{\mu}
:
by virtue of the cyclicity of the trace we have

\displaystyle\boxed{\ {\cal L}_{0}=\textrm{Tr}\,(K^{\mu}K_{\mu})\ }\,,\qquad\mbox{whence}\quad\boxed{\ \partial^{\mu}K_{\mu}\approx 0\ }\,,\qquad\mbox{with}\quad\boxed{\ K_{\mu}\equiv(\partial_{\mu}g)g^{-1}\ }\,.

(7.11)

The covariant vector field
(K_{\mu})
satisfies the

\displaystyle\mbox{zero curvature condition for $(K_{\mu})$ :}\qquad\boxed{\ \partial_{\mu}K_{\nu}-\partial_{\nu}K_{\mu}-[K_{\mu},K_{\nu}]=0\ }\,,

(7.12)

i.e. the zero curvature condition (
7.6
)
with the opposite sign in front of the commutator: relation (
7.6
) is obtained
[
57
]
for
K_{\mu}

by choosing the opposite sign for
K_{\mu}
, i.e. by considering
\tilde{K}_{\mu}\equiv-(\partial_{\mu}g)g^{-1}=g(\partial_{\mu}g^{-1})
.
The variable
\tilde{K}_{\mu}
turns into
J_{\mu}
upon the replacement
g\leadsto g^{-1}
which represents a discrete symmetry of

{\cal L}_{0}=-\textrm{Tr}\,\big[(\partial^{\mu}g)(\partial_{\mu}g^{-1})\big]
.

Symmetries and conservation laws:

The Lagrangian density
{\cal L}_{0}
given by (
7.8
) (and thereby the corresponding action) admits two natural global symmetries.
The fact that the current
J_{\mu}=g^{-1}\partial_{\mu}g
is invariant under the left action of the group
G

(i.e. under
g\leadsto hg
with
h\in G
) implies that
{\cal L}_{0}
is manifestly
left-invariant
.
Analogously, the fact that the current
K_{\mu}\equiv(\partial_{\mu}g)g^{-1}
is invariant under the right action of the group
G

(i.e. under
g\leadsto gh
with
h\in G
) implies that
{\cal L}_{0}

as rewritten in terms of
K_{\mu}
(see eqn. (
7.11
))
is manifestly
right-invariant
.

At the infinitesimal level, the left/right symmetry transformations of the fields
x\mapsto g(x)
are parametrized by
\varepsilon_{L,R}\in\mathds{R}

and read

\displaystyle SU(2)_{L}\;:\qquad

\displaystyle\delta^{a}_{L}g=\varepsilon_{L}\,T^{a}\,g

(7.13a)

\displaystyle SU(2)_{R}\;:\qquad

\displaystyle\delta^{a}_{R}g=\varepsilon_{R}\,g\,T^{a}\,.

(7.13b)

By virtue of Noether’s first theorem, the invariance of
{\cal L}_{0}
under these global
transformations implies the existence of on-shell conserved
current densities.
In the present setting, the latter are conveniently derived by applying the method of Gell-Mann and Lévy
[
13
,
14
]
, i.e.
by rendering the infinitesimal symmetry parameters
\varepsilon_{L,R}
space-time dependent:
one readily finds that

\displaystyle\delta^{a}_{L}{\cal L}_{0}

\displaystyle=2\,(\partial_{\mu}\varepsilon_{L})\,\textrm{Tr}\,\big[(\partial^{\mu}g)g^{-1}\,T^{a}\big]=2\,(\partial_{\mu}\varepsilon_{L})\,\textrm{Tr}\,\big[K^{\mu}T^{a}\big]

(7.14a)

\displaystyle\delta^{a}_{R}{\cal L}_{0}

\displaystyle=2\,(\partial_{\mu}\varepsilon_{R})\,\textrm{Tr}\,\big[g^{-1}(\partial^{\mu}g)\,T^{a}\big]=2\,(\partial_{\mu}\varepsilon_{R})\,\textrm{Tr}\,\big[J^{\mu}T^{a}\big]\,,

(7.14b)

for
a\in\{1,2,3\}
.

Summary:

The Lagrangian density

\displaystyle{\cal L}_{0}(\partial_{\mu}g,\partial_{\mu}g^{-1})\equiv\textrm{Tr}\,\big[J^{\mu}J_{\mu}\big]=\textrm{Tr}\,\big[K^{\mu}K_{\mu}\big]=-\textrm{Tr}\,\big[(\partial^{\mu}g)(\partial_{\mu}g^{-1})\big]\,,

(7.15)

is invariant under the symmetry group
SU(2)_{L}\times SU(2)_{R}
(and under the discrete symmetry

g\leadsto g^{-1}
): the associated (on-shell conserved) Noether current densities read

\displaystyle SU(2)_{L}\;:\qquad

\displaystyle K_{\mu}=(\partial_{\mu}g)g^{-1}=gJ_{\mu}g^{-1}\,,\qquad\ \partial^{\mu}K_{\mu}\approx 0\,,

(7.16a)

\displaystyle SU(2)_{R}\;:\qquad

\displaystyle J_{\mu}=g^{-1}\partial_{\mu}g\,,\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\partial^{\mu}J_{\mu}\approx 0\,.

(7.16b)

The local conservation laws for
(K_{\mu})
and
(J_{\mu})

obviously coincide with the equations of motion of the model, see equations (
7.10
) and (
7.11
),
which reflects the transitivity of the action of symmetry transformations.
Moreover, the components
K_{\mu}
of the right-invariant

1
-form
K_{\mu}dx^{\mu}
satisfy the zero curvature condition (
7.12
)
while the components
J_{\mu}
of the left-invariant

1
-form
J_{\mu}dx^{\mu}
satisfy the zero curvature condition (
7.6
).
This symmetry structure of the
SU(2)
principal chiral model is at the origin of the integrability of the model
at the classical and quantum levels. In fact, the described symmetries allow us to construct
a Lax pair and thereby an infinite number
of (non-local) charges satisfying the so-called
Yangian algebra

[
57
]
.
This construction can equivalently be based on the conserved, flat
SU(2)_{L}
-current

(K_{\mu})
or on the conserved, flat
SU(2)_{R}
-current

(J_{\mu})
, both currents being related by the

\displaystyle\mbox{left-right duality transformation}\qquad J_{\mu}=g^{-1}K_{\mu}g\,.

(7.17)

7.2
Two-dimensional sigma model on the squashed

One may wonder whether the symmetry structure (and thereby the integrability) of the

-principal model survives a deformation thereof.
This motivated
the authors of references
[
42
,
43
,
44
,

,

and later on
I. Kawaguchi and K. Yoshida
[

to consider
a deformation of the group manifold
SU(2)

principal chiral model.
As a matter of fact,

by the French mathematician Marcel Berger (in his work on the classification of simply connected,

The so-called

of dimension

\displaystyle ds^{2}=\underbrace{d\theta^{2}+\sin^{2}\theta\,d\phi^{2}}_{S^{2}}+\;\alpha\,\underbrace{(d\psi+\cos\theta\,d\phi)^{2}}_{S^{1}\ \mbox{fibration}}\,,

(7.25)

denotes the deformation parameter.
(The case

is referred to as the

[

]

Thereby the volume form of

is simply rescaled by

and similarly the constant scalar curvature
of
S^{3}
is rescaled by a constant factor
[

]
.
The Berger
3

[
63
]
:
this Riemannian manifold (and more generally the odd-dimensional spheres endowed with
a metric of this type) have been investigated in mathematics, in particular for constructing counterexamples to several geometric conjectures,
e.g. see the textbooks
[
64
]
as well as the work
[
65
]
and references therein.
In the physics literature, the Berger spheres are known as
squashed spheres
and they have been considered in various contexts,
notably in relationship with integrable models, string theory and black holes
(see
[
66
,
67
,
62
,

,

]
and references therein),

[

]
or supersymmetric models
[

]
.

Geometric set-up and action functional:

Let us consider a deformation of

described by a

-parameter deformation
of the Cartan-Killing metric of
su(2)
:

Equation of motion:

Variation of the field

subject to the same boundary condition as for the undeformed model yields the

\displaystyle\mbox{equation of motion:}\qquad\boxed{\ 0\approx\partial^{\mu}J_{\mu}-2C\,\textrm{Tr}\,(T^{3}J^{\mu})\,[J_{\mu},T^{3}]-2C\,\textrm{Tr}\,(T^{3}\partial_{\mu}J^{\mu})\,T^{3}\ }\,.

By multiplying this relation with

and taking the trace, we obtain the

\displaystyle 0\approx(1+C)\,\partial_{\mu}\textrm{Tr}\,(T^{3}J^{\mu})\,,

which will be related to the symmetries of the model below.
Since

7.36

\partial_{\mu}\textrm{Tr}\,(T^{3}J^{\mu})\approx 0

by virtue of which the equation (
7.35
)
takes a simpler form:

\displaystyle 0\approx\partial^{\mu}J_{\mu}-2C\,\textrm{Tr}\,(T^{3}J^{\mu})\,[J_{\mu},T^{3}]\,.

(7.37)

Symmetries:

For

,
the isometries of the metric and thereby the symmetries of the model described by the Lagrangian

still include

\displaystyle SU(2)_{L}\;:\qquad

\displaystyle\delta^{a}_{L}g=\varepsilon_{L}\,T^{a}g

\displaystyle U(1)_{R}\;:\qquad

\displaystyle\delta_{R}g=-\varepsilon_{R}\,g\,T^{3}\,,

(7.38b)

with

and with a conventional minus sign
in the last equation.
From a geometric point of view (i.e. considering the squashed

-sphere as a principal fiber bundle
over the base space

with rescaled

-fibers), the

and the

the ones of the

\displaystyle SU(2)_{L}\;:\qquad j^{\mu}\equiv\;

\displaystyle j^{\mu}_{L}=gJ^{\mu}g^{-1}-2C\,\textrm{Tr}\,(T^{3}J^{\mu})\,gT^{3}g^{-1}\,,\qquad\ \partial_{\mu}j^{\mu}\approx 0\,,

(7.39a)

\displaystyle U(1)_{R}\;:\qquad\qquad\ \

\displaystyle j_{R}^{\mu}=(1+C)\,\textrm{Tr}\,(T^{3}J^{\mu})\,,\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\partial_{\mu}j_{R}^{\mu}\approx 0\,.

(7.39b)

coincides with eqn. (

)
by projecting out its

-component.
Similarly, from the identities

\displaystyle\partial^{\mu}(gJ_{\mu}g^{-1})=

\displaystyle\,g\,(\partial^{\mu}J_{\mu})\,g^{-1}\,,

\displaystyle\partial^{\mu}(gT^{3}g^{-1})=

\displaystyle\,g\,[J^{\mu},T^{3}]\,g^{-1}=[j^{\mu},g\,T^{3}g^{-1}]\,,

of the Lagrangian.
In the limit

and the

8
Concluding remarks

To conclude, we gather some comments on the general results established in section
6
.

First, we recall that classical mechanics amounts to classical field theory in a space-time
with zero spatial dimension, the corresponding expressions (for the Lagrangian, symmetry transformations, conserved quantities,…)
following directly from field theory by considering such a limit.
More specifically, the Noether current density
(j^{\mu})
then reduces to
j^{0}

and coincides with the Noether charge
Q
. Thus, superpotential terms do not occur in mechanical systems
and our results for the improvement of currents which are based
on such terms do not lead to any contribution for these systems.

Second,
we note that in classical field theory the addition of a total derivative to a Lagrangian density amounts to considering a
canonical transformation in phase space. However, for higher order Lagrangian densities (as considered
for scale invariance in subsection
3.3
),
the Hamiltonian formulation turns out to be quite involved (already in classical mechanics
[
85
]
)
and we have not addressed it here.

Appendix A
Variation of Lagrangian

In the main text, we encounter second order Lagrangians, i.e. Lagrangians

{\cal L}(\varphi,\partial_{\mu}\varphi,\partial_{\mu}\partial_{\nu}\varphi)
which also depend on the second order derivatives
of the field
\varphi
.
An infinitesimal variation of fields,

\displaystyle\delta\varphi(x)\equiv\varphi^{\prime}(x)-\varphi(x)\,,

(A.1)

induces a
variation of the action functional

S[\varphi]\equiv\int d^{n}x\,{\cal L}(\varphi,\partial_{\mu}\varphi,\partial_{\mu}\partial_{\nu}\varphi)

given by

\delta S[\varphi]=\int d^{n}x\,\delta{\cal L}
with

\displaystyle\delta{\cal L}=\frac{\partial{\cal L}}{\partial\varphi}\,\delta\varphi+\frac{\partial{\cal L}}{\partial(\partial_{\mu}\varphi)}\,\delta\partial_{\mu}\varphi+\frac{\partial{\cal L}}{\partial(\partial_{\mu}\partial_{\nu}\varphi)}\,\delta\partial_{\mu}\partial_{\nu}\varphi\,.

(A.2)

Since the variation (
A.1
) is a variation at fixed
x
, it commutes with the partial derivatives with respect to
x^{\mu}
:

\partial_{\mu}\delta\varphi=\delta\partial_{\mu}\varphi
.
By using this fact and by applying the Leibniz rule for partial derivatives to the second and third term
in expression (
A.2
), we obtain

\displaystyle\delta{\cal L}=\frac{\delta S}{\delta\varphi}\,\delta\varphi+\partial_{\mu}J^{\mu}\,,

(A.3)

with

\displaystyle\boxed{\ \frac{\delta S}{\delta\varphi}=\frac{\partial{\cal L}}{\partial\varphi}-\partial_{\mu}\left(\frac{\partial{\cal L}}{\partial(\partial_{\mu}\varphi)}\right)+\partial_{\mu}\partial_{\nu}\left(\frac{\partial{\cal L}}{\partial(\partial_{\mu}\partial_{\nu}\varphi)}\right)\ }

(A.4)

and

\displaystyle J^{\mu}=\left[\frac{\partial{\cal L}}{\partial(\partial_{\mu}\varphi)}-\partial_{\rho}\left(\frac{\partial{\cal L}}{\partial(\partial_{\mu}\partial_{\rho}\varphi)}\right)\right]\delta\varphi+\frac{\partial{\cal L}}{\partial(\partial_{\mu}\partial_{\rho}\varphi)}\,\partial_{\rho}\delta\varphi\,.

(A.5)

The quantity
(J^{\mu})
is also referred to as “symplectic potential” current density
and its expression (
A.5
) amounts to the application of a contracting homotopy operator to the Lagrangian,
see
[
15
]
and references therein.

Appendix B
Noether’s first theorem

Suppose the Lagrangian
{\cal L}
is
quasi invariant

under a global continuous symmetry transformation
(given at the infinitesimal level by (
A.1
)), i.e.

\displaystyle{\cal L}\ \,\mbox{quasi invariant}\;:\qquad\boxed{\ \delta{\cal L}=\partial_{\mu}\Omega^{\mu}\ }\quad\mbox{for some vector field $(\Omega^{\mu})$ }\,.

(B.1)

Then, the action functional
S[\varphi]
is invariant under these variations and
relations (
A.3
)-(
A.5
) yield Noether’s first theorem:

\displaystyle\boxed{\ 0=\frac{\delta S}{\delta\varphi}\,\delta\varphi+\partial_{\mu}j^{\mu}\ }\,,\quad\mbox{with}\ \;j^{\mu}\equiv J^{\mu}-\Omega^{\mu}\,,

(B.2)

i.e.

\displaystyle\boxed{\ j^{\mu}=\left[\frac{\partial{\cal L}}{\partial(\partial_{\mu}\varphi)}-\partial_{\rho}\left(\frac{\partial{\cal L}}{\partial(\partial_{\mu}\partial_{\rho}\varphi)}\right)\right]\delta\varphi+\frac{\partial{\cal L}}{\partial(\partial_{\mu}\partial_{\rho}\varphi)}\,\partial_{\rho}\delta\varphi-\Omega^{\mu}\ }\,.

(B.3)

In particular, this results infers that
\partial_{\mu}j^{\mu}\approx 0
, i.e.
the divergence
\partial_{\mu}j^{\mu}
vanishes for all solutions of the
equation of motion
\frac{\delta S}{\delta\varphi}=0
.

Space-time translations and canonical EMT:

Consider a closed physical system whose dynamics is described by
the second order Lagrangian
{\cal L}(\varphi,\partial_{\mu}\varphi,\partial_{\mu}\partial_{\nu}\varphi)

which does not explicitly depend on space-time coordinates.
Under an infinitesimal space-time translation parametrized by
a constant vector
(a^{\nu})
(with
|a^{\nu}|<\!\!<1
),
any relativistic field transforms as
\delta_{\textrm{trans}}\varphi=a_{\nu}\partial^{\nu}\varphi

and so does the Lagrangian which is a scalar field.
From

\delta_{\textrm{trans}}{\cal L}=a^{\mu}\partial_{\mu}{\cal L}=\partial_{\mu}(a^{\mu}{\cal L})
it thus follows that
the Lagrangian is quasi invariant:

\displaystyle\delta_{\textrm{trans}}{\cal L}=\partial_{\mu}\Omega^{\mu}\qquad\mbox{with}\ \;\Omega^{\mu}=a_{\nu}\eta^{\mu\nu}{\cal L}\,.

(B.4)

Substitution of this expression into (
B.3
)
yields the on-shell conserved current
j_{trans}^{\mu}=T_{\textrm{can}}^{\mu\nu}a_{\nu}

where the fields
T_{\textrm{can}}^{\mu\nu}
are the components of the

\displaystyle\mbox{canonical EMT :}\quad\boxed{\ T_{\textrm{can}}^{\mu\nu}=\left[\frac{\partial{\cal L}}{\partial(\partial_{\mu}\varphi)}-\partial_{\rho}\left(\frac{\partial{\cal L}}{\partial(\partial_{\mu}\partial_{\rho}\varphi)}\right)\right]\partial^{\nu}\varphi+\frac{\partial{\cal L}}{\partial(\partial_{\mu}\partial_{\rho}\varphi)}\,\partial_{\rho}\partial^{\nu}\varphi-\eta^{\mu\nu}{\cal L}\ }\,,

(B.5)

which satisfies the local conservation equation
\partial_{\mu}T_{\textrm{can}}^{\mu\nu}\approx 0
.

Scale transformations and canonical dilatation current:

For a first order Lagrangian
{\cal L}(\varphi,\partial_{\mu}\varphi)
, the invariance of the action functional
S[\varphi]=\int d^{n}x\,{\cal L}(\varphi,\partial_{\mu}\varphi)

under the infinitesimal scale transformations (
3.4
)-(
3.5
)
readily leads to expression (
3.6
)
for the canonical dilatation current.

Appendix C
Passive symmetry transformations

Generalities:

For the infinitesimal symmetry transformations we have considered the active point of view, i.e.
we apply the transformation to the fields (see eqn. (
A.1
)) rather than to the reference system.
The infinitesimal
active symmetry transformations
of fields enjoy various nice properties (see reference
[
86
]

for a praise of the active point of view), in particular with respect to the geometric view-point.
In fact, the operators
\partial_{\mu}
and
\delta
commute with each other since
\delta
represents the variation of fields
at fixed
x
; moreover, for diffeomorphisms generated by a vector field
\xi\equiv\xi^{\mu}(x)\partial_{\mu}
,
the active variation
\delta\varphi
of any field
\varphi
is given by its Lie derivative
L_{\xi}\varphi

with respect to the vector field
\xi
.

Yet, one may equivalently consider the passive point of view,
the infinitesimal
passive symmetry transformations
of coordinates and fields being defined by

\displaystyle\boxed{\ \tilde{\delta}x^{\mu}\equiv x^{\prime\mu}-x^{\mu}\,,\qquad\tilde{\delta}\varphi(x)\equiv\varphi^{\prime}(x^{\prime})-\varphi(x)\ }\,.

(C.1)

The latter transformation of the field
\varphi
is related to its active symmetry transformation

{\delta}\varphi(x)\equiv\varphi^{\prime}(x)-\varphi(x)
by the

\displaystyle\mbox{operatorial identities:}\qquad\boxed{\ \tilde{\delta}={\delta}+\tilde{\delta}x^{\mu}\,\partial_{\mu}\,,\qquad[\partial_{\mu},\tilde{\delta}\,]=\partial_{\mu}(\tilde{\delta}x^{\nu})\,\partial_{\nu}\ }\,,

(C.2)

i.e.
\tilde{\delta}\varphi={\delta}\varphi+\tilde{\delta}x^{\mu}\,\partial_{\mu}\varphi
.

From
\tilde{\delta}(d^{n}x)=d^{n}x\,\partial_{\mu}(\tilde{\delta}x^{\mu})
and

\tilde{\delta}{\cal L}={\delta}{\cal L}+\tilde{\delta}x^{\mu}\,\partial_{\mu}{\cal L}
,
we obtain the passive symmetry transformation of the action functional
S=\int d^{n}x\,{\cal L}
:

\displaystyle\tilde{\delta}S=\int[\tilde{\delta}(d^{n}x)\,{\cal L}+d^{n}x\,\tilde{\delta}{\cal L}]=\int d^{n}x\,[\delta{\cal L}+\partial_{\mu}(\tilde{\delta}x^{\mu}\,{\cal L})]\,.

(C.3)

Thus, the quasi invariance of the Lagrangian density under infinitesimal symmetry transformations
as described by relation (
B.1
) is equivalent to
the

\displaystyle\mbox{invariance condition}\qquad\tilde{\delta}S=\int d^{n}x\;\partial_{\mu}\tilde{\Omega}^{\mu}\qquad\mbox{with}\quad\boxed{\ \tilde{\Omega}^{\mu}={\Omega}^{\mu}+\tilde{\delta}x^{\mu}\,{\cal L}\ }\,.

(C.4)

Substitution of the latter relation for
\tilde{\Omega}^{\mu}
as well as

{\delta}\varphi=\tilde{\delta}\varphi-\tilde{\delta}x^{\nu}\,\partial_{\nu}\varphi

into the Noether current (
B.3
) yields the following expression for this current
(involving the canonical EMT (
B.5
)):

\boxed{\ j^{\mu}=\left[\frac{\partial{\cal L}}{\partial(\partial_{\mu}\varphi)}-\partial_{\rho}\left(\frac{\partial{\cal L}}{\partial(\partial_{\mu}\partial_{\rho}\varphi)}\right)\right]\tilde{\delta}\varphi+\frac{\partial{\cal L}}{\partial(\partial_{\mu}\partial_{\rho}\varphi)}\,\partial_{\rho}(\tilde{\delta}\varphi)-\frac{\partial{\cal L}}{\partial(\partial_{\mu}\partial_{\nu}\varphi)}\,(\partial_{\rho}\varphi)\,\partial_{\nu}(\tilde{\delta}x^{\rho})-T_{\textrm{can}}^{\mu\nu}\,\tilde{\delta}x_{\nu}-\tilde{\Omega}^{\mu}\ }\,.

This result coincides with the expression for this current which follows from a derivation that is
exclusively based on passive symmetry transformations, e.g. see reference
[
26
]

where higher order Lagrangians are also considered.

Examples:

Of course, the latter expression for the Noether current
j^{\mu}

also yields the results (for a free massless field
\varphi

in
n
space-time dimensions) given in subsection
3.1

for the
canonical EMT
T_{\textrm{can}}^{\mu\nu}
associated to (passive) translations,

\displaystyle\tilde{\delta}x^{\mu}=-a^{\mu}\,,\qquad\tilde{\delta}\varphi=0=\tilde{\delta}{\cal L}\,,\qquad\tilde{\Omega}^{\mu}=0\,,

(C.5)

and for the canonical dilatation current

associated to (passive) scale transformations,

\displaystyle\tilde{\delta}x^{\mu}=\rho\,x^{\mu}\,,\qquad\tilde{\delta}\varphi=-\rho\,d_{\varphi}\,\varphi\,,\qquad\tilde{\delta}{\cal L}=-\rho\,n\,{\cal L}\,,\qquad\tilde{\Omega}^{\mu}=0\,.

(C.6)

Case of a Lagrangian density given by a total derivative:

Consider the particular case of a Lagrangian density which is given by a total derivative, i.e.

{\cal L}=\partial_{\mu}k^{\mu}
, and which is quasi invariant under the symmetry transformations (
C.1
),
i.e. we have (

\displaystyle\tilde{\delta}S=\int d^{n}x\;\partial_{\mu}\tilde{\Omega}^{\mu}\qquad\mbox{for some $\,\tilde{\Omega}^{\mu}$}\,.

(C.7)

Substitution of
\delta{\cal L}=\delta(\partial_{\mu}k^{\mu})=\partial_{\mu}(\delta k^{\mu})

\displaystyle\tilde{\delta}S=\int d^{n}x\,\partial_{\mu}[\delta k^{\mu}+\tilde{\delta}x^{\mu}\,{\cal L}\,]\,.

By subtracting (

Appendix D

An alternative way for deriving Noether currents associated to global exact/approximate
symmetries (and of their conservation/balance equations) is due do M. Gell-Mann and M. Lévy
[
13
]
.
It is presented in more or less detail or generality in some textbooks
[

]

and we describe it here since it is repeatedly applied in the main body of the text.

Let us first consider the simple case of global internal symmetries for which the
Lagrangian density
{\cal L}(\varphi,\partial_{\mu}\varphi)
is

constant real parameters
\epsilon^{a}
(with

\delta{\cal L}=0

Then, the infinitesimal variation of

\partial_{\mu}\epsilon^{a}
with coefficients which represent the
r
components of the canonical Noether current
(j^{\mu}_{a})
,
i.e. we have the so-called

\displaystyle\mbox{Gell-Mann and L\'{e}vy result:}\qquad\boxed{\ \delta_{\textrm{loc}}{\cal L}=(\partial_{\mu}\epsilon^{a})\,j^{\mu}_{a}\ }\,.

(D.1)

Moreover, the current densities
(j^{\mu}_{a})
satisfy the conservation law

\partial_{\mu}j^{\mu}_{a}=0
for all solutions of the equations of motion of the fields
\varphi
.
Indeed, any solution of the field equations represents a stationary point of the action functional

S[\varphi]\equiv\int_{\mathds{R}^{n}}d^{n}x\,{\cal L}

and thereby the variation of this functional vanishes for all infinitesimal variations around such stationary points,

\varphi(x)\leadsto\varphi(x)+\delta\varphi(x)
, which vanish at infinity (i.e.
\delta\varphi(x)\to 0

for
\|x\|^{2}\equiv(x^{0})^{2}+\cdots+(x^{n-1})^{2}\to\infty
). A fortiori, the variation
of
S[\varphi]
vanishes for local symmetry
transformations
\varphi(x)\leadsto\varphi(x)+\delta\varphi(x)
if
\varphi
solves the field equations and if one assumes that
the symmetry parameters
\epsilon^{a}(x)
vanish at infinity: for these field variations we therefore have

\displaystyle 0=\delta_{\textrm{loc}}\int_{\mathds{R}^{n}}d^{n}x\,{\cal L}=\int_{\mathds{R}^{n}}d^{n}x\,(\partial_{\mu}\epsilon^{a})\,j^{\mu}_{a}=-\int_{\mathds{R}^{n}}d^{n}x\,\epsilon^{a}\,(\partial_{\mu}j^{\mu}_{a})\,,

(D.2)

and the arbitrariness of the parameters
\epsilon^{a}
now implies that

\partial_{\mu}j^{\mu}_{a}=0
for all solutions of the field equations.

For global geometric symmetries, the Lagrangian density is only quasi invariant
(i.e.
\delta{\cal L}=\partial_{\mu}\Omega^{\mu}
for some vector field
(\Omega^{\mu})
)
and relation (
D.1
) then only holds up to an additional total derivative.
More precisely, let us show that this approach provides the well-known explicit expression
for the Noether current densities
(j^{\mu}_{a})
. To do so, we write

\displaystyle\delta_{\textrm{loc}}\varphi(x)=\epsilon^{a}(x)\,F_{a}(x)\,,

(D.3)

where
F_{a}(x)
is a function of the fields
\varphi
and/or their derivatives at the point
x
.
The induced variation of the first order Lagrangian density
{\cal L}(\varphi,\partial_{\mu}\varphi)
now reads

\delta_{\textrm{loc}}{\cal L}=\frac{\partial{\cal L}}{\partial\varphi}\,\delta_{\textrm{loc}}\varphi+\frac{\partial{\cal L}}{\partial(\partial_{\mu}\varphi)}\,\underbrace{\delta_{\textrm{loc}}(\partial^{\mu}\varphi)}_{=\,\partial^{\mu}(\delta_{\textrm{loc}}\varphi)}\approx\partial_{\mu}\Big(\frac{\partial{\cal L}}{\partial(\partial_{\mu}\varphi)}\Big)\,\epsilon^{a}F_{a}+\frac{\partial{\cal L}}{\partial(\partial_{\mu}\varphi)}\,[(\partial_{\mu}\epsilon^{a})F_{a}+\epsilon^{a}(\partial_{\mu}F_{a})]\,.

For the local transformation (
D.3
), we therefore have (for the solutions of the field equations)

\displaystyle\delta_{\textrm{loc}}{\cal L}\approx\epsilon^{a}\,(\partial_{\mu}J^{\mu}_{a})+(\partial_{\mu}\epsilon^{a})\,J^{\mu}_{a}\,,\qquad\mbox{with}\quad J^{\mu}_{a}\equiv\frac{\partial{\cal L}}{\partial(\partial_{\mu}\varphi)}\,F_{a}\,,

(D.4)

where
(J^{\mu}_{a})
represents the symplectic potential current density, see eqn. (
A.5
).
If
{\cal L}
is quasi invariant under the global transformations corresponding to (
D.3
)
(i.e. for constant
\epsilon^{a}
), viz.
\delta{\cal L}=\partial_{\mu}\Omega^{\mu}
with

\Omega^{\mu}\equiv\epsilon^{a}\Omega^{\mu}_{a}
, then relation (
D.4
) yields the following conservation law (for the solutions of the field equations):

\displaystyle 0\approx\epsilon^{a}\,(\partial_{\mu}j^{\mu}_{a})\,,\qquad\mbox{with}\quad j^{\mu}_{a}\equiv J^{\mu}_{a}-\Omega^{\mu}_{a}\,.

(D.5)

Thus, we obtain the standard expression for the (on-shell conserved) Noether current
densities:

\displaystyle\boxed{\ j^{\mu}\equiv\epsilon^{a}j^{\mu}_{a}=\frac{\partial{\cal L}}{\partial(\partial_{\mu}\varphi)}\,\delta\varphi-\Omega^{\mu}\ }\,,\qquad\mbox{with}\quad\boxed{\ \delta\varphi(x)=\epsilon^{a}\,F_{a}(x)\ }\,.

(D.6)

To conclude, we mention the particular case of
x
-dependent translation parameters
a^{\mu}(x)
,
i.e. the local transformation law
\delta_{\textrm{loc}}\varphi(x)=a^{\mu}(x)\partial_{\mu}\varphi(x)

which may be a source of confusion. In this respect, we emphasize that we do
not

consider the transformations
x^{\mu}\leadsto x^{\mu}-a^{\mu}(x)
of the space-time coordinates themselves here
and thereby the localized transformation law
\delta_{\textrm{loc}}\varphi(x)=a^{\mu}(x)\partial_{\mu}\varphi(x)
is not to be mixed up with the
transformation law of relativistic fields under infinitesimal diffeomorphisms generated by the vector field
a^{\mu}\partial_{\mu}

(from which it differs if
\varphi
is not a scalar field).

Appendix E
Proof of relation (
7.44
)

By virtue of the identity (
7.40b
), the improved left current density (
7.42
) reads

\displaystyle\tilde{j}^{\mu}\equiv j^{\mu}-t^{\mu}\,,\qquad\mbox{with}\quad t^{\mu}\equiv\epsilon\,\sqrt{C}\,\varepsilon^{\mu\nu}\,[j_{\nu},g\,T^{3}g^{-1}]\qquad(\epsilon=\pm 1)\,,

(E.1)

where
j^{\mu}
satisfies (
7.41
), i.e.

\displaystyle\varepsilon^{\mu\nu}(\partial_{\mu}j_{\nu}-j_{\mu}j_{\nu})=C\varepsilon_{\mu\nu}\,\textrm{Tr}\,(T^{3}[J^{\mu},J^{\nu}])\,g\,T^{3}g^{-1}\,.

(E.2)

By applying once more the identity (
7.40b
), one readily finds that

\displaystyle\varepsilon^{\mu\nu}(\partial_{\mu}\tilde{j}_{\nu}-\tilde{j}_{\mu}\tilde{j}_{\nu})=C\varepsilon_{\mu\nu}\,\textrm{Tr}\,(T^{3}[J^{\mu},J^{\nu}])\,g\,T^{3}g^{-1}

\displaystyle\,+\frac{1}{2}\,C\varepsilon_{\mu\nu}\,g\left[[J^{\mu},T^{3}],[J^{\nu},T^{3}]\right]\,g^{-1}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad-\epsilon\,\sqrt{C}\,[\partial_{\mu}j^{\mu},g\,T^{3}g^{-1}]\,.

(E.3)

After proving that the first two terms on the right hand side compensate
each other, we have obtained the desired result (
7.44
).
The proof relies on the particular properties of the elements
A
of the Lie algebra
su(2)
,

\displaystyle A=\underbrace{A_{1}T^{1}+A_{2}T^{2}}_{\equiv\,A_{\perp}}+\underbrace{A_{3}T^{3}}_{\equiv\,A_{\|}}\qquad(\mbox{with}\ A_{1},\,A_{2},\,A_{3}\in\mathds{R})\,,

(E.4)

and, more specifically, on the commutation relations between the components

A_{\perp}
and
A_{\|}
(which are perpendicular and parallel to
T^{3}
, respectively):
from
[T^{1},T^{2}]=T^{3}
and its cyclic permutations, we infer that

\displaystyle[A_{\perp},B_{\perp}]\propto T^{3}\,,

\displaystyle\qquad\mbox{hence}\quad{[A_{\perp},B_{\perp}]}_{\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\ }}{{\perp}}}=0\,,

\displaystyle[A_{\|},B_{\perp}]\propto T^{1},T^{2}\,,

\displaystyle\qquad\mbox{hence}\quad{[A_{\|},B_{\perp}]}_{\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\ }}{{\|}}}=0\,.

Thus, the commutator
\left[[J^{\mu},T^{3}],[J^{\nu},T^{3}]\right]
appearing in the second term of (
E.3
)
is proportional to
T^{3}
with a coefficient that is determined by our normalisation

\textrm{Tr}\,(T^{3}T^{3})=-\frac{1}{2}
:

\displaystyle\left[[J^{\mu},T^{3}],[J^{\nu},T^{3}]\right]=-2\,T^{3}\,\textrm{Tr}\,(T^{3}\left[[J^{\mu},T^{3}],[J^{\nu},T^{3}]\right])=-2\,T^{3}\,\textrm{Tr}\,(\left[T^{3},[J^{\mu},T^{3}]\right][J^{\nu},T^{3}])\,.

(E.5)

From
J^{\mu}=J^{\mu}_{\perp}+J^{\mu}_{\|}
it follows that
\left[T^{3},[J^{\mu},T^{3}]\right]=J^{\mu}_{\perp}
and
thereby the commutator (
E.5
) writes
-2\,T^{3}\,\textrm{Tr}\,(J^{\mu}[J^{\nu},T^{3}])
.
Henceforth

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\,C\varepsilon_{\mu\nu}\,g\left[[J^{\mu},T^{3}],[J^{\nu},T^{3}]\right]\,g^{-1}=-C\varepsilon_{\mu\nu}\,\textrm{Tr}\,(T^{3}[J^{\mu},J^{\nu}])\,g\,T^{3}g^{-1}\,,

(E.6)

which completes the proof that the first two terms in eqn. (
E.3
) compensate each other.

Three-Tone Coherent Microwave Electromechanical Measurement of a Superfluid Helmholtz Resonator Preprint: a
S. Spence, E. Varga, C. A. Potts, J. P. Davis

Abstract
We demonstrate electromechanical coupling between a superfluid mechanical mode and a microwave mode formed by a patterned microfluidic chip and a 3D cavity. The electric field of the chip-cavity microwave resonator can be used to both drive and detect the motion of a pure superflow Helmholtz mode, which is dictated by geometric confinement. The coupling is characterized using a coherent measurement technique developed for measuring weak couplings deep in the sideband unresolved regime. The technique is based on two-probe optomechanically induced transparency/amplification using amplitude modulation. Instead of measuring two probe tones separately, they are interfered to retain only a signal coherent with the mechanical motion. With this method, we measure a vacuum electromechanical coupling strength of g 0 = 2 ​ π × 23.3 ​ μ g_{0}=2\pi\times 23.3\;\mathrm{\mu} Hz, three orders of magnitude larger than previous superfluid electromechanical experiments.

We began by observing electromechanical coupling using a calibrated homodyne measurement:

;
Kumar et al. 2023
;
Spence 2022

\Omega_{\text{m}}

\Omega_{\text{m}}=2\pi\times 1394.7

The homodyne phase-lock was achieved by applying a 10 kHz phase modulation to the signal on the cavity arm of the circuit and then minimizing the 10 kHz signal after down-mixing by varying the LO phase. Phase-sensitive measurement was also possible by instead sending the microwave tone on resonance and using 10 kHz amplitude modulation to phase-lock.

Despite homodyne measurement enabling detection of the mechanical spectrum, the signal is weak, with a small signal-to-noise ratio near the mechanical resonance frequency, mainly due to low electromechanical coupling strength and undercoupled microwave readout, even after more than 450 averages. Moreover, determination of
g_{0}
was not possible due to uncertainty in the effective temperature of the Helmholtz mode since vibrations from the cryocooler drove the superfluid motion in the present experiment.

A coherent measurement scheme can be used to precisely determine the electromechanical coupling rate,
g_{0}
. The scheme in this work is based on OMIT/A:
Weis et al. 2010
;
Agarwal and Huang 2010
;
Safavi-Naeini et al. 2011
;
Kashkanova et al. 2017a
an interference effect where beating between two optical tones causes coherent oscillation of the mechanics via radiation pressure when the two tones are separated in frequency by the mechanical resonance. The oscillation of the mechanics, in turn, scatters photons from the stronger driving ‘pump’ tone to the weak ‘probe’ tone frequency, where they then destructively (constructively) interfere with the probe tone in the case of OMIT (OMIA). In the microwave regime, the equivalent effect is often called electromechanically induced transparency/amplification (EMIT/A).
Zhou et al. 2013
For the Helmholtz devices, mechanical motion is driven via modulation of the electrostatic force between the two capacitive basin plates, proportional to voltage squared. Typically OMIT/A is used for sideband resolved systems (
\kappa\ll\Omega_{\text{m}}
), with stronger optomechanical coupling strengths compared to this work. To overcome these limitations, a ‘two-probe’ measurement scheme is used here. The two probe tones are produced by amplitude modulation of the pump tone, inspired by the work of Kashkanova
et al.
,
Kashkanova et al. 2017b
and adapted from optics to the microwave regime. For this work, modulation of a microwave signal allows straightforward resolution of a mHz mechanical linewidth on a GHz signal and, for kHz mechanics deep into the sideband unresolved regime, enables a unique measurement scheme where the probe tones are canceled to recover only a coherent electromechanical signal, which would not be visible otherwise.

In conclusion, the first superfluid microwave electromechanical coupling was measured for a micromechanical system, using a microfluidic Helmholtz resonator inside a 3D microwave cavity. The mechanical motion was detected using a homodyne noise measurement scheme; however, thermomechanical calibration was not possible due to strong cryocooler vibrations. To measure an electromechanical system with weak coupling, deep in the sideband unresolved regime, a coherent measurement scheme was developed, inspired by OMIT
Weis et al. 2010
and specifically the work of Kashkanova
et al.
Kashkanova et al. 2017b
The scheme uses amplitude modulation of a strong pump tone to create probe sidebands, which experience coherent EMIT/A. The two probe tones are then canceled to recover only signal coherent with the mechanics, allowing an electromechanical calibration of the coupling strength
g_{0}
.

The coherent measurement scheme provides a powerful tool for measuring electro/optomechanical systems deep into the sideband unresolved regime. Moreover, integrating a superfluid Helmholtz device into a microwave cavity provides a new platform for superfluid microwave electromechanics. With three orders of magnitude improvement to
g_{0}
over previous superfluid electromechanical systems,
De Lorenzo 2016
the microwave Helmholtz design shows promise as a sensitive method for measuring the properties of superfluid helium. The sensitivity of this proof of concept can be improved by orders of magnitude in many areas: confinement could decrease
\Gamma_{\text{m}}
while increasing
g_{0}
(possibly with a phononic crystal
Spence et al. 2021
;
Sfendla et al. 2021
), larger antenna or galvanic bonding would increase
\alpha
,
Noguchi et al. 2016
low mK dilution temperatures would decrease superfluid and microwave dissipation, a superconducting microwave cavity could also decrease dissipation, and a stronger coupling into the cavity (
\kappa_{\text{in}}\approx\kappa/2
) would greatly improve signal strength for any given cavity field. The improved system may be useful for studying non-equilibrium thermodynamics,

; and finally
G=\partial\omega_{\rm c}/\partial x
is the electromechanical coupling strength.

For a strong microwave pump (drive) tone at
\omega_{\rm d}
and weak probe tones (one or more), the incident signal can be written
s_{\rm in}(t)=\left(\bar{s}_{\rm in}+\delta s(t)\right)\mathrm{e}^{-i\omega_{\rm d}t}
, where
\delta s_{\rm in}(t)
contains the probe tones and the pump tone amplitude
\bar{s}_{\rm in}
is both positive and real. Then, writing the Langevin equations in a frame rotating at
\Delta=\omega_{\rm d}-\omega_{\rm c}
gives

\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\hat{a}(t)=(+i\Delta-\kappa/2)\hat{a}(t)-iG\hat{x}(t)\hat{a}(t)+\sqrt{\kappa_{\rm in}}s_{\rm in}(t)+\sqrt{\kappa_{\rm 0}}\delta\hat{s}_{\rm vac}(t)\;,

(13)

\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\hat{x}(t)=\frac{\hat{p}(t)}{m_{\rm eff}}\;,

(14)

\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\hat{p}(t)=-m_{\rm eff}\Omega^{2}_{\rm m}\hat{x}(t)-\hbar G\hat{a}^{\dagger}(t)\hat{a}(t)-\Gamma_{\rm m}\hat{p}(t)+\delta\hat{F}_{\rm th}(t)\;,

(15)

where
\delta\hat{s}_{\rm vac}(t)
and
\delta\hat{F}_{\rm th}(t)
are the quantum and thermal noise respectively,
\kappa
is the total cavity decay rate,
\kappa_{\rm 0}
the internal cavity decay rate, and
\Gamma_{\rm m}
the mechanical decay rate. The static cavity field and mechanical displacement are then

\bar{a}=\frac{\sqrt{\kappa_{\rm in}}}{\kappa/2-i(\Delta-G\bar{x})}\bar{s}_{\rm in}=\frac{\sqrt{\kappa_{\rm in}}}{\kappa/2-i\bar{\Delta}}\bar{s}_{\rm in}\;,

(16)

\bar{x}=\frac{-\hbar G\absolutevalue{\bar{a}}^{2}}{m_{\rm eff}\Omega_{\rm m}^{2}}\;.

(17)

Here,
\bar{\Delta}=\Delta-G\bar{x}
is the ‘corrected detuning’, accounting for the average radiation pressure force. For a single probe beam and weak or detuned control fields,
\bar{a}
can be assumed real
Weis et al. 2010
; however, for multiple probe beams
\bar{a}
must be considered complex. Linearizing Eqs.
13
,
14
and
15
for small perturbations using the ansatz
\hat{a}(t)=\bar{a}+\delta\hat{a}(t)
and
\hat{x}(t)=\bar{x}+\delta\hat{x}(t)
, while retaining only first-order terms in the small quantities
\delta\bar{a}
,
\delta\bar{a}^{\dagger}
and
\delta\bar{x}
gives

\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\delta\hat{a}(t)=(+i\bar{\Delta}-\kappa/2)\delta\hat{a}(t)-iG\bar{a}\delta\hat{x}(t)+\sqrt{\kappa_{\rm in}}\delta s_{\rm in}(t)+\sqrt{\kappa_{\rm 0}}\delta\hat{s}_{\rm vac}(t)\;,

(18)

\frac{\mathrm{d}^{2}}{\mathrm{d}t^{2}}\delta\hat{x}(t)+\Gamma_{\rm m}\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\delta\hat{x}(t)+\Omega_{\rm m}^{2}\delta\hat{x}(t)=-\frac{\hbar G}{m_{\rm eff}}(\bar{a}^{\dagger}\delta\hat{a}(t)+\bar{a}\delta\hat{a}^{\dagger}(t))+\delta\hat{F}_{\rm th}(t)\;.

(19)

Now considering two probe tones produced by amplitude modulation of the form
\cos(\Omega_p t)
, where
\pm\Omega_{\rm p}=\omega_{\rm p}^{\pm}-\omega_{\rm d}
is the separation in frequency between the pump and probe tones
\omega_{\rm p}^{\pm}
, the perturbation to the incident field can be written as

\delta s_{\rm in}=\frac{s_{\rm p}}{2}\left(\mathrm{e}^{-i\Omega_{\rm p}t}+\mathrm{e}^{+i\Omega_{\rm p}t}\right)\;.

(20)

Considering the microwave drives as classical coherent fields, expectation values can be used in place of operators
z(t)\equiv\expectationvalue{\hat{z}(t)}
, allowing both noises, which average to zero, to be dropped. For the form of
\delta s_{\rm in}
in Eq.
20
, a general solution can be found using the ansatz

\delta a(t)=A^{+}\mathrm{e}^{-i\Omega_{\rm p}t}+A^{-}\mathrm{e}^{+i\Omega_{\rm p}t}\;,

(21)

\delta a^{*}(t)=(A^{-})^{*}\mathrm{e}^{-i\Omega_{\rm p}t}+(A^{+})^{*}\mathrm{e}^{+i\Omega_{\rm p}t}\;,

(22)

\delta x(t)=X\mathrm{e}^{-i\Omega_{\rm p}t}+X^{*}\mathrm{e}^{+i\Omega_{\rm p}t}\;,

(23)

where Hermitian
\delta x(t)
has been used. Note
A^{+}
is used for
\exp(-i\Omega_p t)
to refer to the upper sideband with
\exp(-i(\omega_d + \Omega_p)t)
. Substituting this ansatz into Eq.
18
and Eq.
19
and sorting by frequency will yield six equations; for a single probe tone, only the three terms at the probe frequency are required
Weis et al. 2010
, while for two probe tones, all six are necessary. The two
\delta a(t)
terms are

(-i(\bar{\Delta}+\Omega_{\rm p})+\kappa/2)A^{+}=-iG\bar{a}X+\sqrt{\kappa_{\rm in}}s_{\rm p}/2\;,

(24)

(-i(\bar{\Delta}-\Omega_{\rm p})+\kappa/2)A^{-}=-iG\bar{a}X^{*}+\sqrt{\kappa_{\rm in}}s_{\rm p}/2\;,\;

(25)

while the
\delta a^{*}(t)
terms are

(+i(\bar{\Delta}-\Omega_{\rm p})+\kappa/2)(A^{-})^{*}=+iG\bar{a}^{*}X+\sqrt{\kappa_{\rm in}}s_{\rm p}/2\;,

(26)

(+i(\bar{\Delta}+\Omega_{\rm p})+\kappa/2)(A^{+})^{*}=+iG\bar{a}^{*}X^{*}+\sqrt{\kappa_{\rm in}}s_{\rm p}/2\;,

(27)

and finally the
\delta x(t)
terms are

m_{\rm eff}(\Omega^{2}_{\rm m}-\Omega_{\rm p}^{2}-i\Gamma_{\rm m}\Omega_{\rm p})X=-\hbar G(\bar{a}^{*}A^{+}+\bar{a}(A^{-})^{*})\;,

(28)

m_{\rm eff}(\Omega^{2}_{\rm m}-\Omega_{\rm p}^{2}+i\Gamma_{\rm m}\Omega_{\rm p})X^{*}=-\hbar G(\bar{a}^{*}A^{-}+\bar{a}(A^{+})^{*})\;.

(29)

These six equations can be used to solve for
A^{+}
and
A^{-}
, but first, we define the cavity and mechanical susceptibilities as

\chi_{\mbox{\scriptsize$\pm$}}=\frac{1}{-i(\bar{\Delta}\pm\Omega_{\rm p})+\kappa/2}\;,

(30)

\chi_{\rm m}=\frac{1}{m_{\rm eff}}\frac{1}{\Omega_{\rm m}^{2}-\Omega_{\rm p}^{2}-i\Omega_{\rm p}\Gamma_{\rm m}}\;.

(31)

Solving Eqs.
24
,
26
and
28
for
A^{+}
, and substituting in the susceptibilities, gives

\sqrt{\kappa_{\rm in}}s_{\rm p}/2-\frac{A^{+}}{\chi_{\rm+}}=-i\hbar G^{2}\absolutevalue{\bar{a}}^{2}\chi_{\rm m}\left[A^{+}+\chi_{\rm-}^{*}\left(1+\frac{\bar{a}^{2}}{\absolutevalue{\bar{a}}^{2}}\right)\sqrt{\kappa_{\rm in}}s_{\rm p}/2\;-\;\frac{\chi_{\rm-}^{*}}{\chi_{\rm+}}A^{+}\right]\;,

(32)

which allows for the substitution near the mechanical resonance (
\Omega_{\rm m}\approx\Omega_{\rm p}
) of

\begin{split}\hbar G^{2}\absolutevalue{\bar{a}}^{2}\chi_{\rm m}&=\frac{\hbar G^{2}\absolutevalue{\bar{a}}^{2}}{m_{\rm eff}}\frac{1}{\Omega_{\rm m}^{2}-\Omega_{\rm p}^{2}-i\Omega_{\rm p}\Gamma_{\rm m}}\\
&\approx\frac{\hbar G^{2}\absolutevalue{\bar{a}}^{2}}{2\Omega_{\rm m}m_{\rm eff}}\frac{1}{\Omega_{\rm m}-\Omega_{\rm p}-i\Gamma/2}\\
&=\frac{\absolutevalue{g}^{2}}{-\delta-i\Gamma_{\rm m}/2}\;,\end{split}

(33)

using
\delta=\Omega_{\rm p}-\Omega_{\rm m}
and
g=\bar{a}g_{\rm 0}
, the photon-enhanced electromechanical coupling in the linearized regime, where
g_{\rm 0}=x_{\rm zpf}G
is the vacuum electromechanical coupling, equivalent to the shift in cavity resonance due to the zero point motion of the mechanical resonator. The standard definition of zero point motion
x_{\rm zpf}=\sqrt{\hbar/2\Omega_{\rm m}m_{\rm eff}}
is used here. Substituting Eq.
33
into Eq.
32
and rearranging gives

A^{+}=\chi_{\rm+}\left(1+\frac{i\absolutevalue{g}^{2}\left(\frac{\bar{a}^{2}}{\absolutevalue{\bar{a}}^{2}}\chi_{\rm-}^{*}+\chi_{\rm+}\right)}{-\delta-i\Gamma_{\rm m}/2\;+\;i\absolutevalue{g}^{2}(\chi_{\rm-}^{*}-\chi_{\rm+})}\right)\sqrt{\kappa_{\rm in}}s_{\rm p}/2\;.

(34)

The same method can be used to solve for
A^{-}
using Eqs.
25
,
27
and
29
, resulting in

A^{-}=\chi_{\rm-}\left(1+\frac{i\absolutevalue{g}^{2}\left(\frac{\bar{a}^{2}}{\absolutevalue{\bar{a}}^{2}}\chi_{\rm+}^{*}+\chi_{\rm-}\right)}{-\delta+i\Gamma_{\rm m}/2\;+\;i\absolutevalue{g}^{2}(\chi_{\rm+}^{*}-\chi_{\rm-})}\right)\sqrt{\kappa_{\rm in}}s_{\rm p}/2\;.

(35)

These forms are similar to other OMIT/A experiments
Weis et al. 2010
;
Zhou et al. 2013
. To obtain forms equivalent to Kashkanova
et al.

Kashkanova et al. 2017b
define the electromechanical self-energy as

i\Sigma(\Omega_{\rm p})=\absolutevalue{g}^{2}(\chi_{\rm+}-\chi_{\rm-}^{*})\;.

(36)

This also defines the electromechanical spring effect
\Delta\Omega_{\rm m(ele)}=\mathbb{R}\left\{\Sigma(\Omega_{\rm p})\right\}
, and the electromechanical damping
\Gamma_{\rm m(ele)}=\mathbb{I}\left\{\Sigma(\Omega_{\rm p})\right\}
. Using the self-energy and
g=\bar{a}/\bar{a}^{*}g^{*}
, Eq.
34
and Eq.
35
can be rewritten as

A^{+}=\chi_{\rm+}\sqrt{\kappa_{\rm in}}s_{\rm p}/2\;-\;\frac{g\chi_{\rm+}(g\chi_{\rm-}^{*}+g^{*}\chi_{\rm+})}{\Gamma_{\rm m}/2-i\delta+i\Sigma(\Omega_{\rm p})}\sqrt{\kappa_{\rm in}}s_{\rm p}/2\;,

(37)

A^{-}=\chi_{\rm-}\sqrt{\kappa_{\rm in}}s_{\rm p}/2\;+\;\frac{g\chi_{\rm-}(g\chi_{\rm+}^{*}+g^{*}\chi_{\rm-})}{\Gamma_{\rm m}/2+i\delta-i\Sigma(-\Omega_{\rm p})}\sqrt{\kappa_{\rm in}}s_{\rm p}/2\;,

(38)

which are
A^{\pm}
in equation (6) of the main paper, effectively the cavity field amplitudes at
\omega_{\rm p}^{\pm}
and equivalent to
a_{\rm+}[\Omega]
and
a_{\rm-}[\Omega]
in Ref.
Kashkanova et al. 2017b
.

A.2
Canceling The Probe Tones

This subsection will take the more general expressions for microwave field amplitude
A^{+}
and
A^{-}
, and develop the specific technique of three-tone measurement used in this work. Using the standard input-output relationship for a single port cavity
\hat{a}_{\rm out}=\hat{a}_{\rm in}-\sqrt{\kappa_{\rm in}}\hat{a}

Aspelmeyer, Kippenberg, and Marquardt 2014
, the choice of
\delta s_{\rm in}
in Eq.
20
, and using
s_{\rm in}(t)=\left(\bar{s}_{\rm in}+\delta s(t)\right)\mathrm{e}^{-i\omega_{\rm d}t}
, the signal reflected from the cavity
s_{\rm out}(t)
is

\begin{split}s_{\rm out}(t)=&\;(\bar{s}_{\rm in}-\sqrt{\kappa_{\rm in}}\bar{a})\mathrm{e}^{-i(\omega_{\rm d}t+\phi)}\\
&+(s_{\rm p}/2-\sqrt{\kappa_{\rm in}}A^{+})\mathrm{e}^{-i([\omega_{\rm d}+\Omega_{\rm p}]t+\phi+\psi)}\\
&+(s_{\rm p}/2-\sqrt{\kappa_{\rm in}}A^{-})\mathrm{e}^{-i([\omega_{\rm d}-\Omega_{\rm p}]t+\phi-\psi)}\;.\end{split}

(39)

Demodulating this signal with frequency
\omega_{\rm d}
and phase
\phi_{\rm ref}
, using a frequency mixer that effectively multiplies by
\cos(\omega_d t + \phi_{ref})
, then filtering out the
2\omega_{\rm d}
and DC terms gives

s_{\rm demod}(t)=\frac{K_{\rm T}}{2}\left[(s_{\rm p}-\sqrt{\kappa_{\rm in}}A^{+})\mathrm{e}^{-i(\Omega_{\rm p}t+\psi)}+(s_{\rm p}-\sqrt{\kappa_{\rm in}}A^{-})\mathrm{e}^{+i(\Omega_{\rm p}t+\psi)}\right]\mathrm{e}^{i(\phi_{\rm ref}-\phi)}\;,

(40)

where
k_{\rm p}
is the transfer function of the circuit after the cavity, including the mixer. The additional 10 kHz amplitude modulation (far from
\Omega_{\rm m}
) sets
\phi_{\rm ref}-\phi=\pi/2
. A double-balanced mixer is used to minimize loss, rather than an IQ mixer as the probe tones in the opposing quadrature would drown out any coherent signal. As a double-balanced mixer is used only the real part of the signal is kept; therefore, expanding the exponentials in
s_{\rm demod}(t)
and keeping only the real terms gives

\begin{split}s_{\rm demod}^{\mathbb{R}}(t)/K_{\rm T}&=-\mathbb{R}\left\{i(A^{+}+A^{-})\right\}\frac{\sqrt{\kappa_{\rm in}}}{2}\cos(\Omega_p t + \psi)+\mathbb{R}\left\{A^{-}-A^{+}\right\}\frac{\sqrt{\kappa_{\rm in}}}{2}\sin(\Omega_p t + \psi)\\
&=\mathbb{I}\left\{A^{+}+A^{-}\right\}\frac{\sqrt{\kappa_{\rm in}}}{2}\cos(\Omega_p t + \psi)+\mathbb{R}\left\{A^{-}-A^{+}\right\}\frac{\sqrt{\kappa_{\rm in}}}{2}\sin(\Omega_p t + \psi)\;,\end{split}

(41)

which is the signal the lock-in amplifier then measures.

A.3
Unresolved-Sideband Limit

The key regime of interest here is deep into sideband-unresolved (
\kappa\gg\Omega_{\rm m}
), and while the technique is also effective for stronger couplings this work focuses on weak an electromechanical coupling (
\Delta\Omega_{\rm m(ele)}\ll\Gamma_{\rm m}
), where standard EMIT/A cannot provide sufficient sensitivity. A sideband-resolved two-probe measurement scheme can be found in Ref.
Buters et al. 2017
, where the cavity filters out the pump and lower probe tone. To simplify the measured signal for the sideband-unresolved regime, first approximate the cavity susceptibilities (using
\Omega_{\rm p}^{2},\Omega_{\rm p}\Delta\ll\kappa^{2}
) to

\chi_{\mbox{\scriptsize$\pm$}}\approx\frac{\kappa/2+i(\Delta\pm\Omega_{\rm p})}{\kappa^{2}/4+\Delta^{2}}\;,

(42)

where
\bar{\Delta}\rightarrow\Delta
has been used, as the coupling is expected to be weak. Using the approximation in Eq.
42
the terms of interest
\mathbb{I}\left\{A^{+}+A^{-}\right\}
and
\mathbb{R}\left\{A^{-}-A^{+}\right\}
can be written:

\mathbb{I}\left\{A^{+}+A^{-}\right\}=\frac{\Delta\sqrt{\kappa_{\rm in}}s_{\rm p}}{\kappa^{2}/4+\Delta^{2}}+\frac{\sqrt{\kappa_{\rm in}}s_{\rm p}}{2}\;\mathbb{I}\left\{\frac{g\chi_{\rm-}(g\chi_{\rm+}^{*}+g^{*}\chi_{\rm-})}{\Gamma_{\rm m}/2+i\delta-i\Sigma(-\Omega_{\rm p})}-\frac{g\chi_{\rm+}(g\chi_{\rm-}^{*}+g^{*}\chi_{\rm+})}{\Gamma_{\rm m}/2-i\delta+i\Sigma(\Omega_{\rm p})}\right\}\;,

(43)

\mathbb{R}\left\{A^{-}-A^{+}\right\}=\frac{\sqrt{\kappa_{\rm in}}s_{\rm p}}{2}\;\mathbb{R}\left\{\frac{g\chi_{\rm-}(g\chi_{\rm+}^{*}+g^{*}\chi_{\rm-})}{\Gamma_{\rm m}/2+i\delta-i\Sigma(-\Omega_{\rm p})}+\frac{g\chi_{\rm+}(g\chi_{\rm-}^{*}+g^{*}\chi_{\rm+})}{\Gamma_{\rm m}/2-i\delta+i\Sigma(\Omega_{\rm p})}\right\}\;.

(44)

To expand further, use the full form of the photon-enhanced coupling

\begin{split}g&=\frac{g_{\rm 0}\sqrt{\kappa_{\rm in}}\bar{s}_{\rm in}}{\kappa/2-i\Delta}\\
&=g_{\rm 0}\sqrt{\kappa_{\rm in}}\bar{s}_{\rm in}\frac{\kappa/2+i\Delta}{\kappa^{2}/4+\Delta^{2}}\;,\end{split}

(45)

along with Eq.
42
and the expected small self-energy
\Sigma(\Omega_{\rm p})\ll\Gamma_{\rm m}
, to expand the factors within
\mathbb{I}\left\{...\right\}
and
\mathbb{R}\left\{..\right\}
in Eq.
43
and Eq.
44
, giving

\frac{g(g\chi_{\rm-}\chi_{\rm+}^{*}+g^{*}\chi_{\rm-}^{2})}{\Gamma_{\rm m}/2+i\delta}=g_{\rm 0}^{2}\kappa_{\rm in}\bar{s}_{\rm in}^{2}(\kappa/2+i\Delta)\frac{\kappa^{3}/4+\kappa\Delta^{2}+\kappa\Delta\Omega_{\rm p}+i(2\Delta^{3}+\kappa^{2}\Delta/2-\kappa^{2}\Omega_{\rm p}-2\Delta^{2}\Omega_{\rm p})}{\left(\kappa^{2}/4+\Delta^{2}\right)^{4}(\Gamma/2+i\delta)}\;,

(46)

\frac{g(g\chi_{\rm+}\chi_{\rm-}^{*}+g^{*}\chi_{\rm+}^{2})}{\Gamma_{\rm m}/2-i\delta}=g_{\rm 0}^{2}\kappa_{\rm in}\bar{s}_{\rm in}^{2}(\kappa/2+i\Delta)\frac{\kappa^{3}/4+\kappa\Delta^{2}-\kappa\Delta\Omega_{\rm p}+i(2\Delta^{3}+\kappa^{2}\Delta/2+\kappa^{2}\Omega_{\rm p}+2\Delta^{2}\Omega_{\rm p})}{\left(\kappa^{2}/4+\Delta^{2}\right)^{4}(\Gamma/2-i\delta)}\;,

(47)

where factors of
\Omega_{\rm p}^{2}\;(\ll\kappa^{2})
have been discarded. Substituting these equations into Eq.
43
and Eq.
44
, then taking the imaginary and real parts respectively gives

\mathbb{I}\left\{A^{+}+A^{-}\right\}=\frac{\Delta\sqrt{\kappa_{\rm in}}s_{\rm p}}{\kappa^{2}/4+\Delta^{2}}-\frac{\sqrt{\kappa_{\rm in}}s_{\rm p}}{2}g_{\rm 0}^{2}\kappa_{\rm in}\bar{s}_{\rm in}^{2}\frac{(\kappa^{3}\Omega_{\rm p})\Gamma_{\rm m}/2+(\kappa^{4}/4-4\Delta^{4})\delta}{\left(\kappa^{2}/4+\Delta^{2}\right)^{4}(\Gamma^{2}/4+\delta^{2})}\;,

(48)

\mathbb{R}\left\{A^{-}-A^{+}\right\}=\frac{\sqrt{\kappa_{\rm in}}s_{\rm p}}{2}g_{\rm 0}^{2}\kappa_{\rm in}\bar{s}_{\rm in}^{2}\frac{(\kappa^{4}/4-4\Delta^{4})\Gamma_{\rm m}/2-(\kappa^{3}\Omega_{\rm p})\delta}{\left(\kappa^{2}/4+\Delta^{2}\right)^{4}(\Gamma^{2}/4+\delta^{2})}\;.

(49)

Ignoring the first term in
\mathbb{I}\left\{A^{+}+A^{-}\right\}
as a constant background which can be subtracted, and using
\Omega_{\rm p}\ll\kappa
to remove all
\Omega_{\rm p}
terms,
s_{\rm demod}^{\mathbb{R}}(t)
can be written as

s_{\rm demod}^{\mathbb{R}}(t)=K_{\rm T}g_{\rm 0}^{2}\kappa_{\rm in}^{2}s_{\rm p}\bar{s}_{\rm in}^{2}\frac{(\kappa^{2}/4-\Delta^{2})}{\left(\kappa^{2}/4+\Delta^{2}\right)^{3}(\Gamma^{2}/4+\delta^{2})}[\sin(\Omega_p t + \psi)\Gamma_{\rm m}/2-\cos(\Omega_p t + \psi)\delta]\;,

(50)

which is the signal reaching the measurement lock-in amplifier. The lock-in takes a signal of form
V_{\rm s}(t)=A_{\rm s}\cos(\Omega_s t + \theta)
and returns

X+iY=\frac{A_{\rm s}}{\sqrt{2}}F(\Omega_{\rm s}-\Omega_{\rm ref})\exp{i[(\Omega_s-\Omega_{ref}t + \theta]}\;.

(51)

So measuring
s_{\rm demod}^{\mathbb{R}}(t)
at frequency
\Omega_{\rm p}
will return a signal

X+iY=\frac{-iK_{\rm T}g_{\rm 0}^{2}\kappa_{\rm in}^{2}s_{\rm p}}{\sqrt{2}(\Gamma/2+i\delta)}\frac{(\kappa^{2}/4-\Delta^{2})}{\left(\kappa^{2}/4+\Delta^{2}\right)^{3}}\frac{P_{\rm in}}{\hbar\omega_{\rm d}}\;,

(52)

where
\bar{s}_{\rm in}^{2}=P_{\rm in}/\hbar\omega_{\rm d}
. This finally recovers equation (10) in the main text, though
K_{\rm T}
is still unknown which prevents calibration of
g_{\rm 0}
from a single measurement. The phase
\mathrm{e}^{i\psi}
has been dropped as it will be small for a low mechanical frequency; in practice, adjustment of the lock-in phase can always set
\psi=0
.

A.4
Calibration Of Vacuum Electromechanical Coupling

To calibrate the three-tone EMIT/A response a measurement of the probe tones interfering constructively is used. This allows for a correction of equation (10) in the main text for
K_{\rm T}
and
s_{\rm p}
, enabling calculation of
g_{\rm 0}
. The calibration is achieved by setting
\phi_{\rm ref}-\phi=0
in Eq.
40
for
s_{\rm demod}
, giving

s_{\rm demod}^{\mathbb{R},cal}(t)/K_{\rm T}=\mathbb{R}\left\{s_{\rm p}-\sqrt{\kappa_{\rm in}}(A^{+}+A^{-})\right\}\frac{1}{2}\cos(\Omega_p t + \psi)+\mathbb{I}\left\{(A^{-}-A^{+})\right\}\frac{\sqrt{\kappa_{\rm in}}}{2}\sin(\Omega_p t + \psi)\;.

(53)

The coherent contribution to the signal from the electromechanical interaction can be ignored here, either because it is small compared to the probe tones (for weak coupling) or because the calibration can be taken detuned from the mechanical resonance. The sine term is also small compared to the cosine as
\Omega_{\rm m}\ll\kappa
. Therefore the signal measured at the lock-in is just the combined probe tones reflected from the cavity (including the cavity susceptibility), which can be written as

X+iY=\frac{s_{\rm p}K_{\rm T}}{2\sqrt{2}}\left(1-\frac{\kappa_{\rm in}\kappa/2}{\kappa^{2}/4+\Delta^{2}}\right)\mathrm{e}^{i\psi}\;.

(54)

The reference value for calibration can be achieved by correcting for the bracketed term, using fits of the microwave resonance and taking the magnitude, giving

R_{\rm cal}=\frac{\absolutevalue{X+iY}}{1-\frac{\kappa_{\rm in}\kappa/2}{\kappa^{2}/4+\Delta^{2}}}=\frac{s_{\rm p}K_{\rm T}}{2\sqrt{2}}\;.

(55)

Normalizing
X+iY
in Eq.
52
by
R_{\rm cal}
, taking the value at
\Gamma_{\rm m}
and rearranging, allows calculation of
g_{\rm 0}
according to

g_{\rm 0}=\frac{\sqrt{\hbar\omega_{\text{d}}}}{2\kappa_{\text{in}}\sqrt{P_{\text{in}}}}\sqrt{\frac{\Gamma_{\rm m}(\kappa^{2}/4+\Delta^{2})^{3}}{(\kappa^{2}/4-\Delta^{2})}\frac{R_{\rm\delta=0}}{R_{\rm cal}}}\;,

(56)

which is equation (11) in the main text. The magnitude of the coherent signal at
\delta=0
is
R_{\rm\delta=0}
, either from the maximum of the coherent spectrum when sweeping
\Omega_{\rm p}
or calculated via fitting of the same spectrum. Backgrounds should be subtracted from calculations of
R_{\rm\delta=0}
, as these normally depend on contributions from imperfect phase-locking. To calculate
R_{\rm cal}
, the phase
\phi_{\rm ref}
is swept and the maximum of the magnitude is taken.

Appendix B
Homodyne Measurement Circuit

Figure
6
shows the microwave measurement setup for amplitude-sensitive calibrated homodyne measurement. The method here broadly follows that of Ref.
Gorodetksy et al. 2010
, adapted for microwave circuits in Ref.
Spence 2022
;
Kumar et al. 2023
. This method allows readout of the Helmholtz resonator’s mechanical noise spectral density, as this noise imparts a time dependence on the microwave resonance via electromechanical coupling. The time-dependent cavity resonance then imparts noise on a pump signal, measured in the time domain by down-mixing with an LO of the same frequency, and Fourier transformed to give the mechanical noise spectral density. The figure shows an amplitude-sensitive scheme, where
\omega_{\rm d}
is placed at the maximum gradient of the microwave resonance, the 10 kHz reference is applied via phase modulation, and the calibration tone via amplitude modulation. Phase-sensitive measurement was also used to measure the mechanics, where
\omega_{\rm d}
is placed at the maximum amplitude of the microwave resonance, the 10 kHz reference is applied via amplitude modulation, and the calibration tone via phase modulation.

Working backward from
g_{\rm 0}
, and using the calibrated homodyne technique
Gorodetksy et al. 2010
;
Spence 2022
, an estimate for effective Helmholtz mode temperature is calculated giving
T_{\text{eff}}\approx 80,000
K, which demonstrates the strong incoherent driving from the cryocooler. While initially, this figure seems high, a silicon nitride membrane driven with white noise via a piezo at 0.001 mV
2
/Hz had an effective temperature of 23,000 K
Kumar et al. 2023
; meanwhile, driving the Helmholtz resonator at over 2.5 mV
2
/Hz in this work could not overcome the cryocooler.

Figure 6:
Amplitude-sensitive calibrated homodyne measurement circuit. The RF Source microwave signal, at frequency
\omega_{\rm d}
, is phase modulated with the 10 kHz reference signal and amplitude modulated with the calibration tone at
\Omega_{\rm calib}
. The RF signal is sent to the experimental ‘Cell’ via a directional coupler. The reflected signal passes back through the directional coupler, is amplified with two microwave amplifiers, and is mixed with the LO source signal at frequency
\omega_{\rm d}
. The mixed signal is low-pass filtered to remove the
2\omega_{\rm d}
components and is then measured on two lock-in amplifiers. The lower lock-in on the diagram is set to 1.4 kHz, near the mechanical frequency
\Omega_{\rm m}
, and acts as a filter plus amplifier. The
X
and
Y
quadrature outputs of this lock-in are measured on a DAQ card, giving a time domain noise measurement of frequency components close to

. The upper lock-in on the diagram measures the 10 kHz reference signal, which is again used to lock the relative phase of mixing via the ‘LO Source’. A piezo is present on the outside of the cell to allow for mechanical driving, either with swept frequency to find mechanical modes, or with white noise to raise the effective mode temperature.

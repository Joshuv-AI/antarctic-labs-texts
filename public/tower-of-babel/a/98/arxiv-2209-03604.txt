Analysis of the local discontinuous Galerkin method with generalized fluxes for 1D nonlinear convection-diffusion systems
Hongjuan Zhang, Boying Wu, Xiong Meng

Abstract
In this paper, we present optimal error estimates of the local discontinuous Galerkin method with generalized numerical fluxes for one-dimensional nonlinear convection-diffusion systems. The upwind-biased flux with adjustable numerical viscosity for the convective term is chosen based on the local characteristic decomposition, which is helpful in resolving discontinuities of degenerate parabolic equations without enforcing any limiting procedure. For the diffusive term, a pair of generalized alternating fluxes are considered. By constructing and analyzing generalized Gauss-Radau projections with respect to different convective or diffusive terms, we derive optimal error estimates for nonlinear convection-diffusion systems with the symmetrizable flux Jacobian and fully nonlinear diffusive problems. Numerical experiments including long time simulations, different boundary conditions and degenerate equations with discontinuous initial data are provided to demonstrate the sharpness of theoretical results.

Introduction

In this paper, we concentrate on optimal error estimates of the local discontinuous Galerkin (LDG) method with generalized numerical fluxes for one-dimensional nonlinear convection-diffusion systems of the form

\displaystyle{\bm{u}}_{t}+{\bm{f}}({\bm{u}})_{x}

\displaystyle=({\bm{A}}({\bm{u}}){\bm{u}}_{x})_{x},\quad(x,t)\in I\times(0,T],

(1.1a)

\displaystyle{\bm{u}}(x,0)

\displaystyle={\bm{u}}_{0}(x),\qquad\quad~~\!x\in I,

(1.1b)

where
{\bm{u}}(x,t)=(u_{1}(x,t),\dots,u_{m}(x,t))^{\mathrm{T}}:\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{+}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{m}
is the vector-valued solution,
{\bm{f}}({\bm{u}})=(f_{1}({\bm{u}}),\dots,

f_{m}({\bm{u}}))^{\mathrm{T}}:\mathbb{R}^{m}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{m}
is a vector-valued flux function,
{\bm{A}}({\bm{u}})=(a_{ij}({\bm{u}}))_{i=1,\dots,m}^{j=1,\dots,m}
is positive semi-definite, and
I=(0,2\pi)
. In our analysis, we mainly consider the periodic boundary conditions and
Dirichlet boundary conditions
, and the problems with mixed boundary conditions are numerically investigated.
We first show optimal error estimate when the flux Jacobian matrix
{\bm{f}}^{\prime}_{{\bm{u}}}
is
symmetric positive definite
in Sect.
2
. Extensions to general nonlinear convection-diffusion systems with the symmetrizable flux Jacobian and fully nonlinear diffusive terms are given in Sect.
3
.

The LDG method is an extension of the discontinuous Galerkin (DG) finite element methods
[
7
,
9
,
8
]
, which is suitable to solve partial differential equations involving high order spatial derivatives. It was first introduced by Cockburn and Shu
[
10
]
to solve convection-diffusion equations. Later, it was actively applied to solve various high order equations, such as the Schrödinger equations
[
28
,
24
]
, the Navier-Stokes-Korteweg equations
[
25
]
and the KdV type equations
[
29
]
. The main idea of the LDG method is to rewrite original high order equation into an equivalent first order system and then the standard DG method can be applied. We refer to papers
[
3
,
27
,
2
,
26
,
5
,
12
]
for some incomplete recent development of the LDG methods.

There have been a wide range of numerical methods available for solving convection-diffusion systems. A fast iterative solver for convection-diffusion systems with
spectral elements was given in
[
16
]
. Rossi et al.
[
23
]
studied the segmented waves in a reaction-diffusion-convection system. In
[
31
]
, a local
L^{2}
-error analysis of the streamline diffusion method for nonstationary convection-diffusion systems was developed. In
[
19
]
, high-order DG schemes for the first-order hyperbolic advection-diffusion system were proposed. Michoski et al.
[
22
]
extended the classical von Neumann analysis to support generalized nonlinear convection-reaction-diffusion equations discretized via high-order accurate DG methods. The entropy stable spatial-temporal DG method was proposed to solve compressible Navier-Stokes equations in
[
18
]
. In light of the sharp discontinuity transition of generalized local Lax-Friedrichs fluxes for nonlinear conservation laws in
[
13
]
and the accurate long time wave resolution of downwind-biased fluxes for KdV equations in
[
14
]
, it would be interesting to investigate the benefit that the generalized flux may bring for solving nonlinear convection-diffusion systems, especially for degenerate equations with discontinuous initial data.

This paper aims to derive optimal error estimates of the LDG method with generalized numerical fluxes for solving nonlinear convection-diffusion systems and to explore the advantages of adjustable numerical viscosities of such fluxes. In particular, due to the difference of the flux Jacobian matrix, we present the optimal error analysis in two different cases, namely the
symmetric positive definite
flux Jacobian and symmetrizable flux Jacobian. For the first case, we apply the local characteristic decomposition
[
8
,
20
,
30
]
and choose suitable generalized Gauss-Radau (GGR) projections for the leading projection errors for convective and diffusive terms to obtain optimal error estimates. For the second case, since the flux Jacobian matrix is symmetrizable, there are mainly two additional difficulties. One is that the standard GGR projections are no longer valid, due to the complexity of the balance of leading errors between the nonlinear convective term and the diffusive term. Inspired by the work in
[
6
]
, we construct a new projection for the auxiliary variable to compensate the error of the nonlinear convection term.
Another one is that some new test functions involving a
symmetric positive definite
matrix pertaining to the symmetrizable theory should be chosen, allowing us to obtain a reasonable bound for the boundary term in
\Pi_{2}
in Sec.
3.1.2
. The analysis is also extended to the fully nonlinear convection-diffusion systems. To show flexibility of generalized numerical fluxes with different weights, a variety of numerical examples are provided, which exhibit smaller long time errors for smooth solutions and steeper discontinuity transitions for degenerate equations with discontinuous initial data when compared to the standard upwind fluxes.

The paper is organized as follows. In Sect.
2
, we show the LDG scheme with generalized numerical fluxes for one-dimensional nonlinear convection-diffusion systems with
symmetric positive definite
flux Jacobian, display the symmetrizable theory, and present the generalized skew-symmetry property of the DG spatial operator. The optimal error estimates for
symmetric positive definite
flux Jacobian are derived, and the case of Dirichlet boundary conditions is discussed. Extensions of the analysis to the case of symmetrizable flux Jacobian and the fully nonlinear diffusive term are carried out in Sect.
3
, in which some new modified GGR projections are constructed and analyzed. In Sect.
4
, numerical experiments are presented to verify the theoretical results. Concluding remarks and comments on future work are given in Sect.
5
.

\displaystyle={\bm{A}}{\bm{u}}_{xx},\quad~~~\!(x,t)\in I\times(0,T],

(2.1a)

\displaystyle{\bm{u}}(x,0)

\displaystyle={\bm{u}}_{0}(x),\qquad x\in I.

(2.1b)

The fully nonlinear case will be discussed in Sect.
3.2
.

2.1
The LDG scheme

2.1.1
Notation

Let
\mathcal{I}_{h}=\{I_{j}=(x_{j-{\frac{1}{2}}},x_{j+{\frac{1}{2}}})\}_{j=1}^{N}
be a partition of
I=(0,2\pi)
. The length of each element is
h_{j}=x_{j+{\frac{1}{2}}}-x_{j-{\frac{1}{2}}}
. The maximum element length is denoted by
h=\displaystyle\max_{1\leq j\leq N}h_{j}
. We assume the partition is quasi-uniform, namely, there exists a positive constant
\gamma
such that
h_{j}\geq\gamma h
for any
j
, as
h
goes to zero. The discontinuous finite element space is defined as

\bm{V}_{h}=\{{\bm{v}}\in[L^{2}(I)]^{m}:{\bm{v}}|_{I_{j}}\in[P^{k}(I_{j})]^{m},~j=1,\dots,N\},

where
P^{k}(I_{j})
denotes the space of polynomials of degree at most
k
on
I_{j}
.

Since functions in
\bm{V}_{h}
may be discontinuous across cell interfaces, denote the jump and the average at
x_{j+{\frac{1}{2}}}
by

\llbracket{{\bm{p}}}\rrbracket_{j+{\frac{1}{2}}}=\bm{p}^{+}_{j+{\frac{1}{2}}}-\bm{p}^{-}_{j+{\frac{1}{2}}},\quad\{\!\!\{{{\bm{p}}}\}\!\!\}_{j+{\frac{1}{2}}}={\frac{1}{2}}(\bm{p}^{+}_{j+{\frac{1}{2}}}+\bm{p}^{-}_{j+{\frac{1}{2}}}),

where
{\bm{p}}_{j+{\frac{1}{2}}}^{\mp}
are values from the left and right elements, respectively.
Furthermore, we define the weighted average as

{\bm{p}}^{(\theta)}_{j+{\frac{1}{2}}}=\theta\bm{p}^{-}_{j+{\frac{1}{2}}}+\bar{\theta}\bm{p}^{+}_{j+{\frac{1}{2}}},\quad{\bm{f}}({\bm{p}})^{(\theta)}_{j+{\frac{1}{2}}}=\theta{\bm{f}}(\bm{p}^{-}_{j+{\frac{1}{2}}})+\bar{\theta}{\bm{f}}(\bm{p}^{+}_{j+{\frac{1}{2}}}),\quad\bar{\theta}=1-\theta.

As usual, we use
\|{\cdot}\|_{M}
to represent the length of a vector, or the spectral norm of a matrix. For any vector
{\bm{p}}=(p_{1},\cdots,p_{m})^{\mathrm{T}}
,
\|{{\bm{p}}}\|_{M}=(\sum_{i=1}^{m}p_{i}^{2})^{1/2}
, and for any matrix
{\bm{C}}
,
\|{{\bm{C}}}\|_{M}=\displaystyle\max_{\|{{\bm{p}}}\|_{M}=1}\|{{\bm{C}}{\bm{p}}}\|_{M}
.
Let
W^{\ell,p}(D)
be the classical Sobolev space equipped with norm
\|{\cdot}\|_{\ell,p,D}
. For any vector-valued function
{\bm{v}}=(v_{1},\ldots,v_{m})^{\mathrm{T}}
, the
L^{2}
norm is denoted by
\|{{\bm{v}}}\|^{2}_{D}=\int_{D}{\|{\bm{v}}\|_{M}^{2}}{\rm d}x
and the
L^{\infty}
norm is denoted by

\|{{\bm{v}}}\|_{\infty,D}=\displaystyle\max_{x\in D}\|{\bm{v}}(x)\|_{M}
. The subscripts
D,p,\ell
will be omitted when
D=I,~p=2
and
\ell=0
. The
broken
Sobolev space
W^{\ell,p}(\mathcal{I}_{h})
and the corresponding norms can be defined in an analogous way. We use
\|{{\bm{v}}}\|_{\Gamma_{\!h}}=\sum\limits_{j=1}^{N}(\|{\bm{v}}^{-}_{j+{\frac{1}{2}}}\|_{M}^{2}+\|{\bm{v}}_{j-{\frac{1}{2}}}^{+}\|_{M}^{2})^{\frac{1}{2}}
to denote the
L^{2}
norm at cell boundaries.

2.1.2
The Cauchy-Schwarz inequality and inverse inequalities

For any vector-valued functions
{\bm{p}}
,
{\bm{q}}
and matrix-valued function
{\bm{C}}
, the following Cauchy-Schwarz inequality holds,

|\bm{p}^{\mathrm{T}}{\bm{C}}{\bm{q}}|\leq\|{{\bm{C}}}\|_{M}\|{{\bm{p}}}\|_{M}\|{{\bm{q}}}\|_{M}.

(2.2)

In what follows, we list some inverse inequalities of the finite element space
[
1
]
. For any
{\bm{p}}\in\bm{V}_{h}
, there exists a positive constant
C
independent of
{\bm{p}}
and
h
, such that

\displaystyle(\mathbf{i})~\|{\partial_{x}{\bm{p}}}\|\leq Ch^{-1}\|{{\bm{p}}}\|,

\displaystyle(\mathbf{ii})~\|{{\bm{p}}}\|_{\Gamma_{\!h}}\leq Ch^{-{\frac{1}{2}}}\|{{\bm{p}}}\|,

\displaystyle(\mathbf{iii})~\|{{\bm{p}}}\|_{\infty}\leq Ch^{-{\frac{1}{2}}}\|{{\bm{p}}}\|.

2.1.3
The symmetrization technique

To deal with the symmetrizable flux Jacobian in Sect.
3
, the following symmetrization procedure is essential. By the symmetrizable theory, e.g.
[
17
]
, if the flux Jacobian matrix
{\bm{f}}^{\prime}_{{\bm{u}}}
is symmetrizable, we can find a mapping
{\bm{u}}({\bm{v}}):\mathbb{R}^{m}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{m}
such that the Jacobian matrix
{\bm{u}}^{\prime}_{{\bm{v}}}=(\partial{u_{i}}/\partial{v}_{j})_{i=1,\ldots,m}^{j=1,\ldots,m}
is
symmetric positive definite
and the Jacobian matrix
{\bm{f}}^{\prime}_{{\bm{v}}}={\bm{f}}^{\prime}_{{\bm{u}}}{\bm{u}}^{\prime}_{{\bm{v}}}
is symmetric. We also have the transformation
{\bm{v}}={\bm{v}}({\bm{u}})
with
{\bm{v}}^{\prime}_{{\bm{u}}}=(\partial{v}_{i}/\partial{u}_{j})_{i=1,\ldots,m}^{j=1,\ldots,m}
being
symmetric positive definite
. If we let
{\bm{Q}}\equiv{\bm{Q}}({\bm{u}})={\bm{v}}^{\prime}_{{\bm{u}}}
, from the symmetrizable theory, we know that
{\bm{f}}^{\prime}_{{\bm{u}}}
has a strong relationship with an important symmetric matrix
{\bm{K}}
, i.e.,

{\bm{Q}}^{1/2}{\bm{f}}^{\prime}_{{\bm{u}}}{\bm{Q}}^{-1/2}={\bm{Q}}^{1/2}{\bm{f}}^{\prime}_{{\bm{v}}}{\bm{Q}}^{1/2}\triangleq{\bm{K}}.

Note that the symmetric matrix
{\bm{K}}
has the same spectrum with
{\bm{f}}^{\prime}_{{\bm{u}}}
. Thus,
{\bm{K}}
can be decomposed to
{\bm{K}}={\bm{X}}^{-1}\tilde{{\bm{K}}}{\bm{X}}
with
\tilde{{\bm{K}}}=diag\left\{{\lambda_{i}}\right\}_{i=1}^{m}
and
\lambda_{i}

(i=1,\ldots,m)
being the eigenvalues of
{\bm{f}}^{\prime}_{{\bm{u}}}
. By the above relationship, we can further obtain

{\bm{Q}}{\bm{f}}^{\prime}_{{\bm{u}}}={\bm{Q}}^{1/2}{\bm{K}}{\bm{Q}}^{1/2}.

(2.3)

The above identity is useful for us to obtain a definite viscosity term for the estimate of
\Pi_{2}
in Sec.
3.1.2
.

2.1.4
The LDG scheme

At first, we introduce an auxiliary variable
{\bm{p}}
to rewrite (
2.1
) as

{\bm{u}}_{t}+{\bm{f}}({\bm{u}})_{x}=\bm{A}^{{\frac{1}{2}}}{\bm{p}}_{x},\quad{\bm{p}}=\bm{A}^{{\frac{1}{2}}}{\bm{u}}_{x},

where
\bm{A}^{{\frac{1}{2}}}=diag({\rm Appl.~Opt.}^{\frac{1}{2}},\dots,a_{m}^{\frac{1}{2}})
. The semi-discrete LDG scheme of (
2.1
) is:
\forall t\in(0,T]
, find
({\bm{u}}_{h},\bm{p}_{h})\in\bm{V}_{h}\times\bm{V}_{h}
such that

\displaystyle\int_{I_{j}}{\bm{v}_{h}^{\mathrm{T}}({\bm{u}}_{h})_{t}}{\rm d}x

\displaystyle=\mathcal{H}_{j}^{\wedge}({{\bm{f}}({\bm{u}}_{h})},{\bm{v}_{h}})-\mathcal{H}_{j}^{\bar{\theta}}({\bm{A}^{{\frac{1}{2}}}\bm{p}_{h}},{\bm{v}_{h}}),

(2.4a)

\displaystyle\int_{I_{j}}{\bm{w}_{h}^{\mathrm{T}}\bm{p}_{h}}{\rm d}x

\displaystyle=-\mathcal{H}_{j}^{\theta}({\bm{A}^{{\frac{1}{2}}}{\bm{u}}_{h}},{\bm{w}_{h}}),

(2.4b)

hold for any
(\bm{v}_{h},\bm{w}_{h})\in\bm{V}_{h}\times\bm{V}_{h}
and
j=1,\dots,N
, where the DG spatial operators
\mathcal{H}_{j}^{\theta}
and
\mathcal{H}_{j}^{\wedge}
depending on the choice of numerical fluxes (specified later) are

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}_{j}^{\theta}({\bm{A}^{{\frac{1}{2}}}{\bm{p}}},{{\bm{q}}})=

\displaystyle\int_{I_{j}}{{\bm{q}}_{x}^{\mathrm{T}}\bm{A}^{{\frac{1}{2}}}{\bm{p}}}{\rm d}x-({\bm{q}}^{-})_{j+{\frac{1}{2}}}^{\mathrm{T}}\bm{A}^{{\frac{1}{2}}}{\bm{p}}^{(\theta)}_{j+{\frac{1}{2}}}+{({\bm{q}}^{+})}_{j-{\frac{1}{2}}}^{\mathrm{T}}\bm{A}^{{\frac{1}{2}}}{\bm{p}}^{(\theta)}_{j-{\frac{1}{2}}},

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}_{j}^{\wedge}({{\bm{p}}},{{\bm{q}}})=

\displaystyle\int_{I_{j}}{{\bm{q}}_{x}^{\mathrm{T}}{\bm{p}}}{\rm d}x-({\bm{q}}^{-})_{j+{\frac{1}{2}}}^{\mathrm{T}}\hat{{\bm{p}}}_{j+{\frac{1}{2}}}+{({\bm{q}}^{+})}_{j-{\frac{1}{2}}}^{\mathrm{T}}\hat{{\bm{p}}}_{j-{\frac{1}{2}}},

and
\mathcal{H}^{\theta}({\bm{A}^{{\frac{1}{2}}}{\bm{p}}},{{\bm{q}}})=\sum\limits_{j=1}^{N}\mathcal{H}_{j}^{\theta}({\bm{A}^{{\frac{1}{2}}}{\bm{p}}},{{\bm{q}}})
,
\mathcal{H}^{\wedge}({{\bm{p}}},{{\bm{q}}})=\sum\limits_{j=1}^{N}\mathcal{H}_{j}^{\wedge}({{\bm{p}}},{{\bm{q}}})
. Using an argument similar to that in
[
6
]
, we have the following generalized skew-symmetry property of the DG spatial operator
\mathcal{H}_{j}^{\theta}
.

Lemma 2.1
.

Under the periodic boundary conditions, one has

\mathcal{H}^{\theta}({\bm{A}^{{\frac{1}{2}}}{\bm{p}}},{{\bm{q}}})+\mathcal{H}^{\bar{\theta}}({\bm{A}^{{\frac{1}{2}}}{\bm{q}}},{{\bm{p}}})=0.

(2.5)

Following
[
20
]
, we consider the Jacobian matrix
{\bm{f}}^{\prime}_{{\bm{u}}}({\bm{u}}_{j+{\frac{1}{2}}})
in the definition of the upwind-biased flux
{\bm{\hat{f}}}
. The corresponding eigenvalues, left and right eigenvectors are denoted by
{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}\lambda_{i,j+{\frac{1}{2}}},\bm{\ell}_{i,j+{\frac{1}{2}}},\bm{r}_{i,j+{\frac{1}{2}}}}

(i=1,\dots,m)
, normalized so that
{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}\bm{\ell}_{l,j+{\frac{1}{2}}}\bm{r}_{n,j+{\frac{1}{2}}}=\delta_{l,n}}
, and
{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}{\bm{R}}_{j+{\frac{1}{2}}}=[\bm{r}_{1,j+{\frac{1}{2}}},\dots,\bm{r}_{m,j+{\frac{1}{2}}}],}

{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}{\bm{L}}_{j+{\frac{1}{2}}}=[\bm{\ell}_{1,j+{\frac{1}{2}}},\dots,}

{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}\bm{\ell}_{m,j+{\frac{1}{2}}}]^{\mathrm{T}}.}
The upwind-biased flux is defined by the following procedure.

1.

Transform
{\bm{f}}({\bm{u}}_{h}^{\pm})
to the eigenspace of
{\bm{f}}^{\prime}_{{\bm{u}}}({\bm{u}}_{j+{\frac{1}{2}}})
,

z_{i}^{\pm}={\color[rgb]{0,0,1}\bm{\ell}_{i,j+{\frac{1}{2}}}}{\bm{f}}({\bm{u}}_{h}^{\pm}),\quad i=1,\dots,m.

2.

Apply the scalar upwind-biased setting to
z_{i}^{\pm}
in the
i
th characteristic field
(i=1,\dots,m)
, and the numerical flux
\hat{z}_{i}
depends on the sign of
{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}\lambda_{i,j+{\frac{1}{2}}}}
,

\hat{z}_{i}=\begin{cases}z_{i}^{(\theta)}&\text{if ${\color[rgb]{0,0,1}\lambda_{i,j+{\frac{1}{2}}}}\geq 0$},\\
z_{i}^{(\bar{\theta})}&\text{if ${\color[rgb]{0,0,1}\lambda_{i,j+{\frac{1}{2}}}}<0$}.\end{cases}

3.

Transform back to the physical field to get
{\bm{\hat{f}}}_{j+{\frac{1}{2}}}
,

{\bm{\hat{f}}}_{j+{\frac{1}{2}}}=\sum\limits_{i=1}^{m}\hat{z}_{i}{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}\bm{r}_{i,j+{\frac{1}{2}}}.}

(2.6)

In particular, when the flux Jacobian
{\bm{f}}^{\prime}_{{\bm{u}}}
is
symmetric positive definite
, eigenvalues of
{\bm{f}}^{\prime}_{{\bm{u}}}({\bm{u}}_{j+{\frac{1}{2}}})
are all positive. It follows from the above procedure that

{\bm{\hat{f}}}_{j+{\frac{1}{2}}}={\bm{f}}({\bm{u}}_{h})_{j+{\frac{1}{2}}}^{(\theta)}.

(2.7a)

For diffusive terms, the following generalized alternating numerical fluxes are chosen

\hat{{\bm{u}}}_{h}={\bm{u}}_{h}^{(\theta)},\quad\hat{{\bm{p}}}_{h}=\bm{p}_{h}^{(\bar{\theta})},

(2.7b)

or

\hat{{\bm{u}}}_{h}={\bm{u}}_{h}^{(\bar{\theta})},\quad\hat{{\bm{p}}}_{h}=\bm{p}_{h}^{(\theta)}

(2.7c)

where
\theta>\frac{1}{2}
and we have omitted the subscript

.
The numerical initial condition is chosen as
{\bm{u}}_{h}(0)={\bm{\pi}}_{h}{\bm{u}}_{0}
, where
{\bm{\pi}}_{h}=(\pi_{h},\ldots,\pi_{h})^{\mathrm{T}}
is the standard

projection in the vector form.

2.2.1
Preliminaries

To define projection for systems, let us first recall scalar GGR projections
[
15
,
6
]
. For
z\in H^{1}(\mathcal{I}_{h})
and an arbitrary cell
I_{j}
, projections
\mathbb{P}_{h}^{\theta}
and
\mathbb{P}_{h}^{\bar{\theta}}
are respectively defined as for any
{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}j=1,\dots,N}

\displaystyle\int_{I_{j}}{({\color[rgb]{0,0,1}\mathbb{P}_{h}^{\theta}z})v}{\rm d}x

\displaystyle=\int_{I_{j}}{zv}{\rm d}x,\quad\forall v\in P^{k-1}(I_{j}),\quad({\color[rgb]{0,0,1}\mathbb{P}_{h}^{\theta}z})_{j+{\frac{1}{2}}}^{(\theta)}=z_{j+{\frac{1}{2}}}^{(\theta)},

(2.8a)

\displaystyle\int_{I_{j}}{({\color[rgb]{0,0,1}\mathbb{P}_{h}^{\bar{\theta}}z})v}{\rm d}x

\displaystyle=\int_{I_{j}}{zv}{\rm d}x,\quad\forall v\in P^{k-1}(I_{j}),\quad({\color[rgb]{0,0,1}\mathbb{P}_{h}^{\bar{\theta}}z})_{j-{\frac{1}{2}}}^{(\bar{\theta})}=z_{j-{\frac{1}{2}}}^{(\bar{\theta})}.

(2.8b)

Next, consider the local linearization of the Jacobian matrix
{\bm{f}}^{\prime}_{{\bm{u}}}({\bm{u}}_{j+{\frac{1}{2}}})
again.

Its eigenvalues, left and right eigenvectors are
\lambda_{i,j+{\frac{1}{2}}},

\bm{\ell}_{i,j+{\frac{1}{2}}},

\bm{r}_{i,j+{\frac{1}{2}}}

(i=1,\dots,m),
and
\bm{\ell}_{l,j+{\frac{1}{2}}}\bm{r}_{n,j+{\frac{1}{2}}}=\delta_{l,n}
respectively. Besides,
{\bm{R}}_{j+{\frac{1}{2}}}=[\bm{r}_{1,j+{\frac{1}{2}}},\dots,\bm{r}_{m,j+{\frac{1}{2}}}]
,
{\bm{\Lambda}}_{j+{\frac{1}{2}}}=diag(\lambda_{i,j+{\frac{1}{2}}})_{i=1}^{m}
, and
{\bm{R}}_{j+{\frac{1}{2}}}^{-1}={\bm{L}}_{j+{\frac{1}{2}}}=[\bm{\ell}_{1,j+{\frac{1}{2}}},\dots,\bm{\ell}_{m,j+{\frac{1}{2}}}]^{\mathrm{T}}
. Clearly,
{\bm{f}}^{\prime}_{{\bm{u}}}({\bm{u}}_{j+{\frac{1}{2}}}){\bm{R}}_{j+{\frac{1}{2}}}={\bm{R}}_{j+{\frac{1}{2}}}{\bm{\Lambda}}_{j+{\frac{1}{2}}}
.
Then, the projection of a vector
{\bm{u}}
, denoted by
\mathsf{P}{\bm{u}}
in
I_{j}
, is the unique function in
[P^{k}(I_{j})]^{m}

determined by the following procedure.

1.

Transform
{\bm{u}}
to the eigenspace of
{\bm{f}}^{\prime}_{{\bm{u}}}({\bm{u}}_{j+{\frac{1}{2}}})
,

z_{i}={\color[rgb]{1,0,0}\bm{\ell}_{i,j+{\frac{1}{2}}}}{\bm{u}},\quad i=1,\dots,m.

2.

Apply the scalar GGR projection (
2.8
) to
z_{i}
for the
i
th characteristic variable
(i=1,\dots,m)
, and the projection
\mathbb{P}z_{i}
depends on the sign of
\lambda_{i,j+{\frac{1}{2}}}
, i.e.,

{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}\mathbb{P}z_{i}}=\begin{cases}{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}\mathbb{P}_{h}^{\theta}z_{i}}&\text{if ${\color[rgb]{1,0,0}\lambda_{i,j+{\frac{1}{2}}}}\geq 0$},\\
{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}\mathbb{P}_{h}^{\bar{\theta}}z_{i}}&\text{if ${\color[rgb]{1,0,0}\lambda_{i,j+{\frac{1}{2}}}}<0$}.\end{cases}

3.

Transform back to the physical field to get
\mathsf{P}{\bm{u}}
,

\mathsf{P}{\bm{u}}=\sum\limits_{i=1}^{m}{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}\mathbb{P}z_{i}}{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}\bm{r}_{i,j+{\frac{1}{2}}}}.

According to the above definition, we have
{\bm{u}}={\color[rgb]{1,0,0}{\bm{R}}_{r}}{\bm{z}}
and
\mathsf{P}{\bm{u}}={\color[rgb]{1,0,0}{\bm{R}}_{r}}\mathbb{P}{\bm{z}}
with
{\bm{R}}_{r}={\bm{R}}_{j+{\frac{1}{2}}}
, where
{\bm{z}}=(z_{1},\dots,z_{m})^{\mathrm{T}}
is the characteristic variable. When the matrix
{\bm{f}}^{\prime}_{{\bm{u}}}
is
symmetric positive definite
, we have

\mathbb{P}{\bm{z}}=(\mathbb{P}_{h}^{\theta}z_{1},\dots,\mathbb{P}_{h}^{\theta}z_{m})^{\mathrm{T}}
and
\mathsf{P}{\bm{u}}\triangleq\mathsf{P}_{h}^{\theta}{\bm{u}}
. Note that
{\bm{R}}_{r}={\bm{R}}_{j+{\frac{1}{2}}}
is a constant matrix in each element
I_{j}
, by the definition and approximation property of scalar GGR projection
\mathbb{P}_{h}^{\theta}
in (

(3.2b)

Similar to the scalar case

Lemma 3.1
.

Assume that
{\bm{u}}\in H^{k+2}
is periodic. Then, the above defined projection is unique and the following approximation property holds

\|{{\bm{p}}-\mathsf{P}{\bm{p}}}\|\leq Ch^{k+1},

(3.3)

where the positive constant
C
is independent of h.

Proof 3.2
.

We first prove the existence and uniqueness of
\mathsf{P}{\bm{p}}
.
Let
{\bm{E}}=\mathsf{P}{\bm{p}}-\mathsf{P}_{h}^{\bar{\theta}}{\bm{p}}=[{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}\mathbb{P}p_{1}-\mathbb{P}_{h}^{\bar{\theta}}p_{1}},\dots,{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}\mathbb{P}p_{m}-}

{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}\mathbb{P}_{h}^{\bar{\theta}}p_{m}}]^{\mathrm{T}}
, where
\mathbb{P}_{h}^{\bar{\theta}}
is the GGR projection defined in (
2.8b
). If we can prove the existence and uniqueness of
{\bm{E}}=(E^{1},\ldots,E^{m})^{\mathrm{T}}
, then the projection
\mathsf{P}{\bm{p}}
will exist and is unique. By the definition of the two projections
\mathbb{P}_{h}^{\bar{\theta}}p_{i},

\mathbb{P}p_{i}~(i=1,\ldots,m)
in (
2.8b
) and (
3.1
), we have

\displaystyle\int_{I_{j}}{E^{i}v_{h}}{\rm d}x

\displaystyle=0,\quad\forall v_{h}\in P^{k-1}(I_{j}),

(3.4a)

\displaystyle(E^{i})_{j-{\frac{1}{2}}}^{(\bar{\theta})}

\displaystyle=c^{i}_{j},\quad j=1,\dots,N.

(3.4b)

Denote the restriction of
E^{i}

(i=1,\ldots,m)
to each element
I_{j}
as

E_{j}^{i}(x)=\sum\limits_{\ell=0}^{k}\alpha^{i}_{j,\ell}P_{j,\ell}(x)=\sum\limits_{\ell=0}^{k}\alpha^{i}_{j,\ell}P_{\ell}(s),

(3.5)

where
P_{\ell}(s)
is the
\ell
th-order orthogonal Legendre polynomials on
[-1,1]
with
s=\frac{2(x-x_{j})}{h_{j}}
. Equality (
3.4a
) and the orthogonality of Legendre polynomials yield

\alpha^{i}_{j,\ell}=0,\quad\ell=0,\dots,k-1,\quad j=1,\dots,N.

Therefore,
E^{i}_{j}(x)=\alpha^{i}_{j,k}P_{k}(s).
It follows from (
3.4b
) that

\bar{\theta}\alpha^{i}_{j-1,k}+(-1)^{k}\theta\alpha^{i}_{j,k}=c^{i}_{j}.

For periodic boundary conditions, it can be written as a linear system

{\bm{B}}\bm{\alpha}^{i}_{k}={\bm{c}}^{i},

(3.6)

where
{\bm{B}}=circ(\theta(-1)^{k},0,\dots,0,\bar{\theta})
is an
N\times N
circulant matrix with first row
(\theta(-1)^{k},0,\dots,0,\bar{\theta})
, and
\bm{\alpha}^{i}_{k}=[\alpha^{i}_{1,k},\dots,\alpha^{i}_{N,k}]^{\mathrm{T}}
,
{\bm{c}}^{i}=[c^{i}_{1},\dots,c^{i}_{N}]^{\mathrm{T}}
. It is easy to compute the determinant of
{\bm{B}}

\det{{\bm{B}}}=\big(\theta(-1)^{k}\big)^{N}-(-\bar{\theta})^{N}.

Since the determinant of
{\bm{B}}
is always not

when
\theta>{\frac{1}{2}}
, the matrix
{\bm{B}}
is invertible. This implies that the existence and uniqueness of
E^{i}_{j}(x)
. Further, we can obtain the existence and uniqueness of
{\bm{E}}
.

Next, we turn to the proof of the approximation result (
3.3
). In
[
11
]
, it is shown that the inverse of a nonsingular circulant matrix is also circulant, and thus

\bm{B}^{-1}=\frac{1}{\theta(-1)^{k}\big(1-q^{N}\big)}circ(1,q,\ldots,q^{N-1}),

where
q=-\frac{\bar{\theta}}{\theta(-1)^{k}},
and
|q|<1
since
\theta>{\frac{1}{2}}
. By some simple manipulations, we can find that the

\infty

are equal and satisfy

\|{\bm{B}^{-1}}\|_{1}=\|{\bm{B}^{-1}}\|_{\infty}\leq\frac{1}{\theta(1-|q|)}.

hence the spectral norm satisfies

\|{\bm{B}^{-1}}\|^{2}\leq\|{\bm{B}^{-1}}\|_{1}\|{\bm{B}^{-1}}\|_{\infty}\leq\frac{1}{\theta^{2}(1-|q|)^{2}}.

Using the approximation property of
\mathsf{P}_{h}^{\theta}{\bm{u}}
in (
2.9b
), we deduce that

\displaystyle\|{\bm{\alpha}^{i}_{k}}\|^{2}

\displaystyle=\|{\bm{B}^{-1}{\bm{c}}^{i}}\|^{2}\leq\|{\bm{B}^{-1}}\|^{2}\|{{\bm{c}}^{i}}\|^{2}\leq C\|{{\bm{c}}^{i}}\|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq Ch^{2k+1}\|{{\bm{u}}}\|_{k+1}^{2},

where the positive constant
C
is independent of
h
. Then,

\|{E^{i}}\|^{2}=\sum\limits_{j=1}^{N}\int_{I_{j}}{\big(E_{j}^{i}(x)\big)^{2}}{\rm d}x=\sum\limits_{j=1}^{N}(\alpha^{i}_{j,k})^{2}\|{P_{j,k}(x)}\|^{2}_{I_{j}}=\sum\limits_{j=1}^{N}\frac{h_{j}(\alpha^{i}_{j,k})^{2}}{2k+1}\leq Ch\|{\bm{\alpha}^{i}_{k}}\|^{2}.

A combination of above two inequalities produces the optimal approximation result of
\|{E^{i}}\|
. Finally, since
\|{{\bm{E}}}\|^{2}=\sum\limits_{i=1}^{m}\|{E^{i}}\|^{2}
and using the approximation property of
\mathsf{P}_{h}^{\bar{\theta}}{\bm{p}}
in (
2.9d
), we arrive at

\|{{\bm{p}}-\mathsf{P}{\bm{p}}}\|\leq\|{{\bm{p}}-\mathsf{P}_{h}^{\bar{\theta}}{\bm{p}}}\|+\|{{\bm{E}}}\|\leq Ch^{k+1}.

This finishes the proof of Lemma
3.1
.

and
j=1,\dots,N
. Different from the linear case, we need to redefine numerical fluxes for diffusive terms, i.e.,

{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}\hat{{\bm{g}}}({\bm{u}}_{h})={\bm{g}}^{(\theta)}({\bm{u}}_{h})},\quad\hat{{\bm{p}}}_{h}=\bm{p}_{h}^{(\bar{\theta})},

and

{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}\hat{{\bm{B}}}({\bm{u}}_{h})=\frac{\llbracket{{\bm{g}}({\bm{u}}_{h})}\rrbracket\llbracket{{\bm{u}}_{h}}\rrbracket^{\mathrm{T}}}{\big\|\llbracket{{\bm{u}}_{h}}\rrbracket\big\|_{M}^{2}},}

where the subscript
j+{\frac{1}{2}}
is omitted.

The projection for the prime variable
{\bm{u}}
can be chosen as that in Sect.
2.2
, namely
\mathsf{P}_{h}^{\theta}{\bm{u}}
. However, since
{\bm{A}}({\bm{u}})
is nonlinear, a modified projection of
{\bm{p}}
, denoted by
{\widetilde{\mathsf{P}}}{\bm{p}}
, shall be introduced satisfying

\displaystyle\int_{I_{j}}{\bm{v}_{h}^{\mathrm{T}}({\bm{p}}-{\widetilde{\mathsf{P}}}{\bm{p}})}{\rm d}x

\displaystyle=0,\quad\forall\bm{v}_{h}\in[P^{k-1}(I_{j})]^{m},

\displaystyle\Big({\color[rgb]{0,0,1}{\bm{B}}({\bm{u}})}({\bm{p}}-{\widetilde{\mathsf{P}}}{\bm{p}})^{(\bar{\theta})}\Big)_{j-{\frac{1}{2}}}

\displaystyle=-(\theta-{\frac{1}{2}})\Big({\color[rgb]{0,0,1}{\bm{B}}^{\prime}({\bm{u}})}\llbracket{{\bm{\eta}}_{{\bm{u}}}}\rrbracket{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}{\bm{g}}({\bm{u}})_{x}}\Big)_{j-{\frac{1}{2}}},\quad j=1,\dots,N,

where
{\bm{B}}^{\prime}({\bm{u}})
is an
m\times m\times m
tensor.

If we decompose
{\bm{e}}_{{\bm{u}}},\bm{e_{p}}
into
{\bm{e}}_{{\bm{u}}}=({\bm{u}}-\mathsf{P}_{h}^{\theta}{\bm{u}})+(\mathsf{P}_{h}^{\theta}{\bm{u}}-{\bm{u}}_{h})\triangleq{\bm{\eta}}_{{\bm{u}}}+{\bm{\xi}}_{{\bm{u}}}
and
\bm{e_{p}}=({\bm{p}}-{\widetilde{\mathsf{P}}}{\bm{p}})+({\widetilde{\mathsf{P}}}{\bm{p}}-\bm{p}_{h})\triangleq\bm{\eta_{p}}+\bm{\xi_{p}}
, and take
\bm{v}_{h}={\bm{\xi}}_{{\bm{u}}},\bm{w}_{h}=\bm{\xi_{p}}
, we can obtain the following error equations

\displaystyle\int_{I}{{\bm{\xi}}_{{\bm{u}}}^{\mathrm{T}}({{\bm{\xi}}_{{\bm{u}}}})_{t}}{\rm d}x

\displaystyle=-\int_{I}{{\bm{\xi}}_{{\bm{u}}}^{\mathrm{T}}({{\bm{\eta}}_{{\bm{u}}}})_{t}}{\rm d}x+\mathcal{H}^{\wedge}({{\bm{f}}({\bm{u}})-{\bm{f}}({\bm{u}}_{h})},{{\bm{\xi}}_{{\bm{u}}}})

\displaystyle\quad~\!-\mathcal{H}^{\bar{\theta}}({{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}{\bm{B}}({\bm{u}})}{\bm{p}}-{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}{\bm{B}}({\bm{u}}_{h})}\bm{p}_{h}},{{\bm{\xi}}_{{\bm{u}}}}),

\displaystyle\int_{I}{\bm{\xi}_{{\bm{p}}}^{\mathrm{T}}\bm{\xi_{p}}}{\rm d}x

\displaystyle=-\int_{I}{\bm{\xi}_{{\bm{p}}}^{\mathrm{T}}\bm{\eta_{p}}}{\rm d}x-\mathcal{H}^{\theta}({{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}{\bm{g}}({\bm{u}})-{\bm{g}}({\bm{u}}_{h})}},{\bm{\xi_{p}}}).

Adding above two equations, we have

\displaystyle{\frac{1}{2}}\frac{\rm d}{{\rm d}t}\|{{\bm{\xi}}_{{\bm{u}}}}\|^{2}+\|{\bm{\xi_{p}}}\|^{2}=\Theta_{1}+\Theta_{2}+\Theta_{3}+\Theta_{4}+\Theta_{5},

where

\displaystyle\Theta_{1}

\displaystyle=-\int_{I}{{\bm{\xi}}_{{\bm{u}}}^{\mathrm{T}}({{\bm{\eta}}_{{\bm{u}}}})_{t}}{\rm d}x-\int_{I}{\bm{\xi}_{{\bm{p}}}^{\mathrm{T}}\bm{\eta_{p}}}{\rm d}x,

\displaystyle\Theta_{2}

\displaystyle=\mathcal{H}^{\wedge}({{\bm{f}}^{\prime}_{{\bm{u}}}({\bm{u}}){\bm{\xi}}_{{\bm{u}}}},{{\bm{\xi}}_{{\bm{u}}}}),

\displaystyle\Theta_{3}

\displaystyle=\mathcal{H}^{\wedge}({{\bm{f}}^{\prime}_{{\bm{u}}}({\bm{u}}){\bm{\eta}}_{{\bm{u}}}},{{\bm{\xi}}_{{\bm{u}}}}),

\displaystyle\Theta_{4}

\displaystyle=-\mathcal{H}^{\wedge}({{\bm{e}}_{{\bm{u}}}^{\mathrm{T}}{\bm{H}}{\bm{e}}_{{\bm{u}}}},{{\bm{\xi}}_{{\bm{u}}}}),

\displaystyle\Theta_{5}

\displaystyle=-\mathcal{H}^{\bar{\theta}}({{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}{\bm{B}}({\bm{u}})}{\bm{p}}-{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}{\bm{B}}({\bm{u}}_{h})}\bm{p}_{h}},{{\bm{\xi}}_{{\bm{u}}}})-\mathcal{H}^{\theta}({{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}{\bm{g}}({\bm{u}})-{\bm{g}}({\bm{u}}_{h})}},{\bm{\xi_{p}}}).

By the choice of numerical fluxes and projections, we can see that the estimates of
\Theta_{1}
–
\Theta_{4}
are exactly the same as that in the proof of Theorem
2.3
. So, we only need to consider the term
\Theta_{5}
.
By Taylor expansion and the mean value theorem, we first have the following expression

\displaystyle{\bm{B}}({\bm{u}}){\bm{p}}-{\bm{B}}({\bm{u}}_{h})\bm{p}_{h}

\displaystyle=({\bm{B}}^{\prime}({\bm{u}}){\bm{e}}_{{\bm{u}}}-{\bm{R}}_{1}){\bm{p}}+({\bm{B}}({\bm{u}})-{\bm{B}}^{\prime}({\bm{u}}){\bm{e}}_{{\bm{u}}}+{\bm{R}}_{1}){\bm{e}}_{p},

\displaystyle{\bm{B}}({\bm{u}}){\bm{p}}-\hat{{\bm{B}}}({\bm{u}}_{h})\hat{{\bm{p}}}_{h}

\displaystyle=({\bm{B}}^{\prime}({\bm{u}})\{\!\!\{{{\bm{e}}_{{\bm{u}}}}\}\!\!\}-{\bm{R}}_{2}){\bm{p}}+({\bm{B}}({\bm{u}})-{\bm{B}}^{\prime}({\bm{u}})\{\!\!\{{{\bm{e}}_{{\bm{u}}}}\}\!\!\}+{\bm{R}}_{2}){\bm{e}}_{p}^{(\bar{\theta})},

and

\displaystyle{\bm{g}}({\bm{u}})-{\bm{g}}({\bm{u}}_{h})

\displaystyle=B({\bm{u}}){\bm{e}}_{{\bm{u}}}-{\bm{R}}_{3},

\displaystyle{\bm{g}}({\bm{u}})-{\bm{g}}^{(\theta)}({\bm{u}}_{h})

\displaystyle=B({\bm{u}}){\bm{e}}^{(\theta)}_{{\bm{u}}}-{\bm{R}}_{4},

where
{\bm{R}}_{i}~(i=1,\dots,4)
are all high order terms derived from Taylor expansion.

Using the same argument as that in
[
4
]
for scalar nonlinear convection-diffusion equations, and by virtue of the newly designed projection
{\widetilde{\mathsf{P}}}{\bm{p}}
and
the symmetric property of
{\bm{B}}({\bm{u}})
, we can easily obtain the estimate for
\Theta_{5}
; it reads,

\Theta_{5}\leq C\|{{\bm{\xi}}_{{\bm{u}}}}\|^{2}+{\frac{1}{2}}\|{\bm{\xi_{p}}}\|^{2}+Ch^{2k+2}.

This, together with the estimates of
\Lambda_{1}
–
\Lambda_{4}
in Theorem
2.3
, produces the desired optimal error estimates.

Remark 3.5
.

For the case when the flux Jacobian is symmetrizable, a modified projection
{\bar{\mathsf{P}}}{\bm{p}}
is defined to satisfy for
j=1,\ldots,N

\displaystyle\int_{I_{j}}{\bm{v}_{h}^{\mathrm{T}}({\bm{p}}-{\bar{\mathsf{P}}}{\bm{p}})}{\rm d}x

\displaystyle=0,\quad\forall\bm{v}_{h}\in[P^{k-1}(I_{j})]^{m},

\displaystyle\Big({\color[rgb]{0,0,1}{\bm{B}}({\bm{u}})}({\bm{p}}-{\bar{\mathsf{P}}}{\bm{p}})^{(\bar{\theta})}\Big)_{j-{\frac{1}{2}}}

\displaystyle=\Big(-(\theta-{\frac{1}{2}}){\color[rgb]{0,0,1}{\bm{B}}^{\prime}({\bm{u}})}\llbracket{{\bm{\eta}}_{{\bm{u}}}}\rrbracket{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}{\bm{g}}({\bm{u}})_{x}}+{\bm{f}}^{\prime}_{{\bm{u}}}({\bm{u}})\hat{\bm{\eta}}_{{\bm{u}}}\Big)_{j-{\frac{1}{2}}}.

The optimal error estimates can be derived analogously, and details are omitted.

5
Concluding Remarks

In this paper, we carry out optimal error analysis of the LDG method using generalized numerical fluxes for nonlinear convection-diffusion systems. For both
symmetric positive definite
and symmetrizable flux Jacobian matrices, we derive optimal error estimates by analyzing suitable GGR projections and using the local characteristic decomposition of the flux Jacobian. A series of numerical experiments are given to validate the theoretical results. In future work, we will concentrate on the LDG method for multi-dimensional nonlinear convection-diffusion systems.

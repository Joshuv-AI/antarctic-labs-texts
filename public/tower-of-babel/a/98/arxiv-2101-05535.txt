On the logistic equation for the fractional p p -Laplacian
Antonio Iannizzotto, Sunra Mosconi, and Nikolaos S. Papageorgiou

Abstract
. We consider a Dirichlet type problem for a nonlinear, nonlocal equation driven by the degenerate fractional p p -Laplacian, with a logistic type reaction depending on a positive parameter. In the subdiffusive and equidiffusive cases, we prove existence and uniqueness of the positive solution when the parameter lies in convenient intervals. In the superdiffusive case, we establish a bifurcation result. A new strong comparison result, of independent interest, plays a crucial role in the proof of such bifurcation result.

35A15, 35B51, 35R11.

1.
Introduction

The present paper is devoted to the study of the following nonlinear elliptic equation of fractional order with Dirichlet type condition:

(P_{\lambda})\qquad\begin{cases}(-\Delta)_{p}^{s}\,u=\lambda u^{q-1}-u^{r-1}&\text{in $\Omega$}\\
u>0&\text{in $\Omega$}\\
u=0&\text{in $\Omega^{c}$.}\end{cases}

Here
\Omega\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{N}
(
N\geqslant 2)
is a bounded domain with
C^{1,1}
boundary
\partial\Omega
,
s\in(0,1)
,
p\geqslant 2
are s.t.
ps<N
, and the leading operator is the degenerate fractional
p
-Laplacian, defined for all
u:{\mathbb{R}}^{N}\to{\mathbb{R}}
smooth enough and
x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{N}
by

(-\Delta)_{p}^{s}\,u(x)=2\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0^{+}}\int_{\{|x-y|>\varepsilon\}}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{p-2}(u(x)-u(y))}{|x-y|^{N+ps}}\,dy

(which for
p=2
reduces to the linear fractional Laplacian, up to a dimensional constant
C(N,s)>0
). The reaction is of logistic type, with powers
1<q<r<p^{*}_{s}
, where
p^{*}_{s}=Np/(N-ps)
denotes the critical exponent for fractional Sobolev spaces, and
\lambda>0
is a parameter. Problem
(P_{\lambda})
may is classified in three different types, according to the principal exponent
q>1
:

(a)

subdiffusive, if
q<p<r
;

(b)

equidiffusive, if
p=q<r
;

(c)

superdiffusive, if
p<q<r
.

Logistic equations are widely studied mainly because of their important applications in mathematical biology. Indeed, the parabolic semilinear logistic equation describes the evolution and spatial distribution of a biological population in the presence of constant rates of reproduction and mortality (Verhulst’s law), see
[
17
]
. This is the obvious reason why, in the study of logistic type equations, authors are usually interested in
positive
solutions. More recently, evolutive systems involving logistic terms have been studied as a model for the biological phenomenon of chemotaxis
[
37
]
, and existence of solutions in the presence of a parameter was studied in
[
1
,
7
]
. Regarding the elliptic counterpart, it models an equilibrium distribution, see
[
10
]
. Several existence results for the equidiffusive cases
(b)
, combining variational an topological methods, can be found in
[
2
,
3
,
36
]
(note that multiplicity often includes negative and nodal solutions). Bifurcation results for the superdiffusive case
(c)
can be found in
[
23
]
for the Dirichlet problem, and in
[
29
]
for the whole space.

Fractional order equations also have a close connection to mathematical biology, since fractional elliptic operators model space diffusion via Lévy type random motion with jumps, and hence they can be used to describe the movement of populations, see
[
4
,
31
]
. Studies on logistic equations with several nonlocal operators of fractional order have appeared in recent years, including the square root of the Dirichlet Laplacian
[
8
]
, the spectral Neumann fractional Laplacian
[
28
]
, and the fractional Laplacian on the whole space
[
35
]
.

The operator we consider here is both nonlinear and nonlocal. It represents the nonlinear generalization of the fractional Laplacian, and it can be seen as the gradient of the functional
u\mapsto[u]_{s,p}^{p}/p
in the fractional Sobolev space
W^{s,p}({\mathbb{R}}^{N})
(see Section
2
below), as first pointed out in
[
5
]
. The corresponding eigenvalue problem was studied in
[
26
]
, which led to existence results for general nonlinear reactions via Morse theory in
[
18
]
. Due to the nature of the operator, regularity theory for weak solutions requires a considerable effort as most usual techniques (including the Caffarelli-Silvestre extension method) do not apply here. For any
p>1
, Hölder continuity of weak solutions in the interior and up to the boundary was studied in
[
14
]
and
[
20
]
, respectively. In the degenerate case
p>2
, optimal interior Hölder regularity was proved in
[
6
]
, while a weighted global Hölder regularity result was proved in
[
21
]
(the singular case
p\in(1,2)
is still open).

The result of
[
21
]
is the fractional counterpart of Lieberman’s
C^{1,\alpha}
-regularity result for the classical
p
-Laplacian
[
25
]
and yields many applications, such as the equivalence of Sobolev and Hölder local minimizers of the energy functional
[
22
]
, the existence of extremal constant sign solutions
[
16
]
, and more recently a Brezis-Oswald type weak comparison principle
[
19
]
. We also recall other interesting related results, such as the study of critical growth and singularity performed in
[
9
]
and the bifurcation results of
[
12
,
32
]
. For further information we refer the reader to the surveys
[
27
,
30
]
.

As far as we know, the present literature includes no specific study on the logistic equation for the fractional
p
-Laplacian. This paper aims at filling the gap, by presenting the following general result for existence of solutions to problem
(P_{\lambda})
(in which
\hat{\lambda}_{1}>0
denotes the principal eigenvalue of
(-\Delta)_{p}^{s}\,
in
\Omega
with Dirichlet conditions):

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
\Omega\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{N}
be a bounded domain with
C^{1,1}
-boundary,
p\geqslant 2
,
s\in(0,1)
s.t.
ps<N
, and
1<q<r<p^{*}_{s}
. Then, the following hold:

(a)

if
q<p
, then for all
\lambda>0
problem
(P_{\lambda})
has a unique solution
u_{\lambda}>0
, with
u_{\lambda}>u_{\mu}
in
\Omega
for all
\lambda>\mu>0
and
u_{\lambda}\to 0
as
\lambda\to 0^{+}
;

(b)

if
q=p
, then for all
\lambda\in(0,\hat{\lambda}_{1}]
problem
(P_{\lambda})
has no solution, while for all
\lambda>\hat{\lambda}_{1}

(P_{\lambda})
has a unique solution
u_{\lambda}>0
, with
u_{\lambda}>u_{\mu}
in
\Omega
for all
\lambda>\mu>\hat{\lambda}_{1}
and
u_{\lambda}\to 0
as
\lambda\to\hat{\lambda}_{1}^{+}
;

(c)

if
q>p
, then there exists
\lambda_{*}>0
s.t. for all
\lambda\in(0,\lambda_{*})
problem
(P_{\lambda})
has no solution, while
(P_{\lambda_{*}})
has at least one solution
u_{*}>0
, and for all
\lambda>\lambda_{*}

(P_{\lambda})
has at least two solutions
u_{\lambda}>v_{\lambda}>0
, with
u_{\lambda}>u_{\mu}
in
\Omega
for all
\lambda>\mu>\lambda_{*}
and
u_{\lambda}\to u_{*}
as
\lambda\to\lambda_{*}^{+}
.

More precise statements of the results above can be found in the subsequent Theorems
3.1
,
4.1
, and
5.5
. Our approach is variational, based on critical point theory and comparison-truncation arguments. For the sub- and equidiffusive cases we apply direct minimization and the weak comparison result of
[
19
]
for uniqueness. In the superdiffusive case, we prove a bifurcation result and detect via the mountain pass theorem a second solution for all
\lambda>\lambda_{*}
.

We remark that our result is new even in the semiliear case
p=2
(fractional Laplacian) and in the local case
s=1
(classical
p
-Laplacian). Bifurcation theorems are proved in
[
8
]
for the superdiffusive logistic equation driven by the square root of the Laplacian, and in
[
23
]
for the classical
p
-Laplacian, but with no information about monotonicity, order between solutions, and convergence. Also, existence and uniqueness for the equidiffusive case with the fractional Laplacian are proved in
[
35
]
.

A crucial role in our arguments is played by new strong maximum and comparison principles for weak sub- and supersolutions, including a Hopf type property (see Theorems
2.6
,
2.7
). Previous results of this type were proved in
[
13
]
and in
[
24
]
, respectively, but our versions involve very general reactions and milder restrictions on the constants
p
,
s
and can be of general interest, since they care applicable to a wide class of problems driven by the fractional
p
-Laplacian.

2.
Preliminaries

Problem
(P_{\lambda})
falls into the following class of Dirichlet problems for the fractional
p
-Laplacian:

(2.1)

\begin{cases}(-\Delta)_{p}^{s}\,u=f(x,u)&\text{in $\Omega$}\\
u=0&\text{in $\Omega^{c}$,}\end{cases}

where
\Omega
,
p
,
s
are as in Section
1
and the general reaction
f
satisfies the following hypothesis:

{\bf H}

f:\Omega\times{\mathbb{R}}\to{\mathbb{R}}
is a Carathéodory function s.t. for a.e.
x\in\Omega
and all
t\in{\mathbb{R}}

|f(x,t)|\leqslant c_{0}(1+|t|^{r-1})\qquad(c_{0}>0,\,r\in(p,p^{*}_{s})).

A variational theory for problem (
2.1
) was established in the recent literature (see for instance
[
16
,
18
,
22
]
). For the reader’s convenience, we recall here some of its main features. First, for all measurable
u:\Omega\to{\mathbb{R}}
we introduce the Gagliardo seminorm

[u]_{s,p,\Omega}=\Big[\iint_{\Omega\times\Omega}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{p}}{|x-y|^{N+ps}}\,dx\,dy\Big]^{\frac{1}{p}}

setting
[u]_{s,p,{\mathbb{R}}^{N}}=[u]_{s,p}
. Then, we define the fractional Sobolev spaces

W^{s,p}(\Omega)=\big\{u\in L^{p}(\Omega):\,[u]_{s,p,\Omega}<\infty\big\},

W^{s,p}_{0}(\Omega)=\big\{u\in W^{s,p}({\mathbb{R}}^{N}):\,u=0\ \text{in $\Omega^{c}$}\big\},

the latter being a uniformly convex, separable Banach space with norm
\|u\|=[u]_{s,p}
, whose dual space is denoted by
W^{-s,p^{\prime}}(\Omega)
(see
[
15
]
). The embedding
W^{s,p}_{0}(\Omega)\hookrightarrow L^{\nu}(\Omega)
is continuous for all
\nu\in[1,p^{*}_{s}]
and compact for all
q\in[1,p^{*}_{s})
. We also recall from
[
20
, Definition 2.1]
the following special space:

\widetilde{W}^{s,p}(\Omega)=\Big\{u\in L^{p}_{\rm loc}({\mathbb{R}}^{N}):\,\exists\,U\Supset\Omega\ \text{s.t. $u\in W^{s,p}(U)$,}\,\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{N}}\frac{|u(x)|^{p-1}}{(1+|x|)^{N+ps}}\,dx<\infty\Big\}.

By
[
20
, Lemma 2.3]
, we can define the fractional
p
-Laplacian as a nonlinear operator
(-\Delta)_{p}^{s}\,:\widetilde{W}^{s,p}(\Omega)\to W^{-s,p^{\prime}}(\Omega)
by setting for all
u,v\in W^{s,p}_{0}(\Omega)

\langle(-\Delta)_{p}^{s}\,u,v\rangle=\iint_{{\mathbb{R}}^{N}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{N}}\frac{(u(x)-u(y))^{p-1}(v(x)-v(y))}{|x-y|^{N+ps}}\,dx\,dy

(with the convention
a^{p-1}=|a|^{p-2}a
established above). Such definition is equivalent to the one given in Section
1
as soon as
u
is smooth enough (for instance, if
u\in\mathcal{S}({\mathbb{R}}^{N})
).

Clearly
W^{s,p}_{0}(\Omega)\subset\widetilde{W}^{s,p}(\Omega)
. The restricted operator
(-\Delta)_{p}^{s}\,:W^{s,p}_{0}(\Omega)\to W^{-s,p^{\prime}}(\Omega)
is continuous, maximal monotone, and enjoys the
(S)_{+}
-property, namely, whenever
(u_{n})
is a sequence in
W^{s,p}_{0}(\Omega)
s.t.
u_{n}\rightharpoonup u
in
W^{s,p}_{0}(\Omega)
and

\limsup_{n}\langle(-\Delta)_{p}^{s}\,u_{n},u_{n}-u\rangle\leqslant 0,

then
u_{n}\to u
in
W^{s,p}_{0}(\Omega)
(see
[
16
, Lemma 2.1]
). From
[
19
, Lemma 2.1]
, for all
u\in W^{s,p}_{0}(\Omega)
we have

(2.2)

\|u^{\pm}\|^{p}\leqslant\langle(-\Delta)_{p}^{s}\,u,\pm u^{\pm}\rangle.

Another useful property, referred to as strict
T
-monotonicity, of
(-\Delta)_{p}^{s}\,
is the following (see
[
16
, proof of Lemma 3.2]
):

Proposition 2.1
.

Let
u,v\in\widetilde{W}^{s,p}(\Omega)
s.t.
(u-v)^{+}\in W^{s,p}_{0}(\Omega)
satisfy

\langle(-\Delta)_{p}^{s}\,u-(-\Delta)_{p}^{s}\,v,(u-v)^{+}\rangle\leqslant 0.

Then,
u\leqslant v
in
\Omega
.

We say that
u\in\widetilde{W}^{s,p}(\Omega)
is a (weak) supersolution of (
2.1
) if
u\geqslant 0
in
\Omega^{c}
and for all
v\in W^{s,p}_{0}(\Omega)_{+}

\langle(-\Delta)_{p}^{s}\,u,v\rangle\geqslant\int_{\Omega}f(x,u)v\,dx,

and similarly we define a (weak) subsolution. Finally,
u\in W^{s,p}_{0}(\Omega)
is a (weak) solution of (
2.1
) if it is both a super- and a subsolution, i.e., if for all
v\in W^{s,p}_{0}(\Omega)

\langle(-\Delta)_{p}^{s}\,u,v\rangle=\int_{\Omega}f(x,u)v\,dx.

In such cases we write that weakly in
\Omega

(-\Delta)_{p}^{s}\,u=\ (\geqslant,\,\leqslant)\ f(x,u).

From
[
9
, Theorem 3.3]
we have the following a priori bound on the solutions:

Proposition 2.2
.

Let
{\bf H}
hold,
u\in W^{s,p}_{0}(\Omega)
be a solution of (
2.1
). Then,
u\in L^{\infty}(\Omega)
with
\|u\|_{\infty}\leqslant C(\|u\|)
.

Classical nonlinear regularity theory does not apply to fractional order equations, whose solutions fail to be
C^{1}
in general. Nevertheless, weighted Hölder continuity can replace higher smoothness in most cases. We set
{\rm d}_{\Omega}(x)={\rm dist}(x,\Omega^{c})
for all
x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{N}
and define the following space:

C_{s}^{0}(\overline{\Omega})=\Big\{u\in C^{0}(\overline{\Omega}):\frac{u}{{\rm d}_{\Omega}^{s}}\ \text{has a continuous extension to}\ \overline{\Omega}\Big\},

a Banach space under the norm
\|u\|_{0,s}=\|u/{\rm d}_{\Omega}^{s}\|_{\infty}
. By
[
18
, Lemma 5.1]
, the positive order cone
C_{s}^{0}(\overline{\Omega})_{+}
has a nonempty interior

{\rm int}(C_{s}^{0}(\overline{\Omega})_{+})=\Big\{u\in C_{s}^{0}(\overline{\Omega}):\,\inf_{\Omega}\frac{u}{{\rm d}_{\Omega}^{s}}>0\Big\}.

Similarly, for any
\alpha\in(0,1)
we set

C_{s}^{\alpha}(\overline{\Omega})=\Big\{u\in C^{0}(\overline{\Omega}):\frac{u}{{\rm d}_{\Omega}^{s}}\ \text{has a $\alpha$-H\"{o}lder continuous extension to}\ \overline{\Omega}\Big\},

a Banach space under the norm

\|u\|_{\alpha,s}=\|u\|_{0,s}+\sup_{x\neq y}\frac{|u(x)/{\rm d}_{\Omega}^{s}(x)-u(y)/{\rm d}_{\Omega}^{s}(y)|}{|x-y|^{\alpha}}.

By the Ascoli-Arzelà theorem,
C_{s}^{\alpha}(\overline{\Omega})\hookrightarrow C_{s}^{0}(\overline{\Omega})
with compact embedding for all
\alpha\in(0,1)
. From Proposition
2.2
and
[
21
, Theorem 1.1]
we have the following weighted Hölder regularity result:

Proposition 2.3
.

Let
{\bf H}
hold,
u\in W^{s,p}_{0}(\Omega)
be a solution of (
2.1
). Then there exists
\alpha\in(0,s]
, independent of
u
, s.t.
u\in C^{\alpha}_{s}(\overline{\Omega})
and
\|u\|_{\alpha,s}\leqslant C(\|u\|)
.

From
[
19
, Proposition 2.8]
we have the following weak comparison principle under a special monotonicity assumption of Brezis-Oswald type:

Proposition 2.4
.

Let
{\bf H}
hold and assume that

t\mapsto\frac{f(x,t)}{t^{p-1}}

is decreasing in
(0,\infty)
for a.e.
x\in\Omega
. Let
u,v\in{\rm int}(C_{s}^{0}(\overline{\Omega})_{+})\cap W^{s,p}_{0}(\Omega)
be a subsolution and a supersolution, respectively, of (
2.1
). Then,
u\leqslant v
in
\Omega
.

The energy functional for problem (
2.1
) is defined by setting for all
u\in W^{s,p}_{0}(\Omega)

\Phi(u)=\frac{\|u\|^{p}}{p}-\int_{\Omega}F(x,u)\,dx,

where we have set for all
(x,t)\in\Omega\times{\mathbb{R}}

F(x,t)=\int_{0}^{t}f(x,\tau)\,dx.

By classical results, we have
\Phi\in C^{1}(W^{s,p}_{0}(\Omega))
, and
u\in W^{s,p}_{0}(\Omega)
is a solution of (
2.1
) iff
\Phi^{\prime}(u)=0
in
W^{-s,p^{\prime}}(\Omega)
. Besides, by
[
18
, Proposition 2.1]

\Phi
satisfies a bounded
(PS)
-condition, namely, whenever
(u_{n})
is a bounded sequence in
W^{s,p}_{0}(\Omega)
s.t.
(\Phi(u_{n}))
is bounded in
{\mathbb{R}}
and
\Phi^{\prime}(u_{n})\to 0
in
W^{-s,p^{\prime}}(\Omega)
, then
(u_{n})
has a convergent subsequence. In this connection, we recall from
[
22
, Theorem 1.1]
the following equivalence principle for Sobolev and Hölder local minimizers of
\Phi
:

Proposition 2.5
.

Let
{\bf H}
hold,
u\in W^{s,p}_{0}(\Omega)
. Then, the following are equivalent:

(i)

there exists
\rho>0
s.t.
\Phi(u+v)\geqslant\Phi(u)
for all
v\in W^{s,p}_{0}(\Omega)\cap C_{s}^{0}(\overline{\Omega})
,
\|v\|_{0,s}\leqslant\rho
;

(ii)

there exists
\sigma>0
s.t.
\Phi(u+v)\geqslant\Phi(u)
for all
v\in W^{s,p}_{0}(\Omega)
,
\|v\|\leqslant\sigma
.

Regarding the spectral properties of the fractional
p
-Laplacian, we refer the reader to
[
26
]
. We just recall that the eigenvalue problem is stated as

(2.3)

\begin{cases}(-\Delta)_{p}^{s}\,u=\lambda u^{p-1}&\text{in $\Omega$}\\
u=0&\text{in $\Omega$.}\end{cases}

The principal eigenvalue of (
2.3
) is defined as

(2.4)

\hat{\lambda}_{1}=\inf_{u\in W^{s,p}_{0}(\Omega)\setminus\{0\}}\frac{\|u\|^{p}}{\|u\|_{p}^{p}},

and it is simple and isolated with a unique positive eigenfunction
\hat{u}_{1}\in{\rm int}(C_{s}^{0}(\overline{\Omega})_{+})
s.t.
\|u\|_{p}=1
.

2.1.
Strong maximum and comparison principles

As mentioned in Section
1
, a strong maximum principle and a Hopf type lemma for the fractional
p
-Laplacian were proved in
[
13
, Theorems 1.2, 1.5]
, while a strong comparison principle was obtained in
[
24
, Theorem 1.1]
. Nevertheless, the strong comparison principle of
[
24
]
does not fit with our purposes for two reasons: first, in the degenerate case
p>2
it requires some special relations between the parameters
p
and
s
which, combined with the optimal Hölder continuity proved in
[
6
]
, lead to the quite restrictive condition
s\leqslant 1/p^{\prime}
; second, the result only ensures that the difference between the super- and the subsolution is positive in
\Omega
, while we need to prove that such difference lies in
{\rm int}(C_{s}^{0}(\overline{\Omega})_{+})
.

Motivated by such difficulties, we present here a new pair of results, following an alternative approach based on the nonlocal superposition principle introduced in
[
21
]
. We begin with a strong maximum principle (including a Hopf type boundary property):

Theorem 2.6
.

Let
g\in C^{0}({\mathbb{R}})\cap BV_{\rm loc}({\mathbb{R}})
,
u\in W^{s,p}_{0}(\Omega)\cap C^{0}(\overline{\Omega})
,
u\not\equiv 0
s.t.

\begin{cases}(-\Delta)_{p}^{s}\,u+g(u)\geqslant g(0)&\text{weakly in $\Omega$}\\
u\geqslant 0&\text{in $\Omega$.}\end{cases}

Then,

\inf_{\Omega}\frac{u}{{\rm d}_{\Omega}^{s}}>0.

In particular, if
u\in C_{s}^{0}(\overline{\Omega})
, then
u\in{\rm int}(C_{s}^{0}(\overline{\Omega})_{+})
.

Proof.

By Jordan’s decomposition, we can find
g_{1},g_{2}\in C^{0}({\mathbb{R}})
nondecreasing s.t.
g(t)=g_{1}(t)-g_{2}(t)
for all
t\in{\mathbb{R}}
, and
g_{1}(0)=0
. So we have weakly in
\Omega

\displaystyle(-\Delta)_{p}^{s}\,u+g_{1}(u)

\displaystyle=(-\Delta)_{p}^{s}\,u+g(u)+g_{2}(u)

\displaystyle\geqslant g(0)+g_{2}(0)=0.

Thus, without loss of generality we may assume that
g
is nondecreasing and
g(0)=0
. In order to prove our assertion, we need a lower barrier for
u
. Let us consider the following torsion problem:

(2.5)

\begin{cases}(-\Delta)_{p}^{s}\,v=1&\text{in $\Omega$}\\
v=0&\text{in $\Omega^{c}$.}\end{cases}

By convexity, (
2.5
) has a unique solution
v\in W^{s,p}_{0}(\Omega)
, which by
[
21
, Lemma 2.3]
satisfies
v\geqslant c{\rm d}_{\Omega}^{s}
in
\Omega
, for some
c>0
. By Proposition
2.3
we have
v\in C^{\alpha}_{s}(\Omega)
, in particular
v
is continuous. So, since
u\not\equiv 0
, we can find
x_{0}\in\Omega
,
\rho,\varepsilon>0
, and
\eta_{0}\in(0,1)
s.t.
\overline{B}_{\rho}(x_{0})\subset\Omega
and

(2.6)

\sup_{\overline{B}_{\rho}(x_{0})}\eta_{0}v<\inf_{\overline{B}_{\rho}(x_{0})}u-\varepsilon.

Set for all
x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{N}
,
\eta\in(0,\eta_{0}]

w_{\eta}(x)=\begin{cases}\eta v(x)&\text{if $x\in\overline{B}_{\rho/2}^{c}(x_{0})$}\\
u(x)&\text{if $x\in\overline{B}_{\rho/2}(x_{0})$.}\end{cases}

First, by (
2.6
) we have
w_{\eta}\leqslant u
in
\overline{B}_{\rho}(x_{0})
. Besides,
w_{\eta}\in\widetilde{W}^{s,p}(\Omega\setminus\overline{B}_{\rho}(x_{0}))
and by the nonlocal superposition principle
[
21
, Proposition 2.6]
we have weakly in
\Omega\setminus\overline{B}_{\rho}(x_{0})

\displaystyle(-\Delta)_{p}^{s}\,w_{\eta}(x)

\displaystyle=(-\Delta)_{p}^{s}\,(\eta v)(x)+2\int_{\overline{B}_{\rho/2}(x_{0})}\frac{(\eta v(x)-u(y))^{p-1}-(\eta v(x)-\eta v(y))^{p-1}}{|x-y|^{N+ps}}\,dy

\displaystyle\leqslant\eta^{p-1}(-\Delta)_{p}^{s}\,v(x)+C\int_{\overline{B}_{\rho/2}(x_{0})}\frac{(\eta v(y)-u(y))^{p-1}}{|x-y|^{N+ps}}\,dy

\displaystyle\leqslant\eta^{p-1}+C\int_{\overline{B}_{\rho/2}(x_{0})}\frac{-\varepsilon^{p-1}}{(\rho/2)^{N+ps}}\,dy

\displaystyle\leqslant\eta^{p-1}-C_{\rho}\varepsilon^{p-1}

for some
C_{\rho}>0
independent of
\varepsilon
, where we have used (
2.5
), (
2.6
), and the following elementary inequality:

(a-b)^{p-1}-(a-c)^{p-1}\leqslant 2^{2-p}(c-b)^{p-1},

holding for all
a,b,c\in{\mathbb{R}}
,
b\geqslant c
(see
[
20
, eq. (2.7)]
). So, for all
\eta\in(0,\eta_{0}]
small enough we have weakly in
\Omega\setminus\overline{B}_{\rho}(x_{0})

(-\Delta)_{p}^{s}\,w_{\eta}<-C\varepsilon^{p-1}.

Since
g(w_{\eta})\to 0
uniformly in
\Omega\setminus\overline{B}_{\rho}(x_{0})
as
\eta\to 0^{+}
, for an even smaller
\eta\in(0,\eta_{0}]
we have (in a weak sense)

\begin{cases}(-\Delta)_{p}^{s}\,w_{\eta}+g(w_{\eta})\leqslant 0\leqslant(-\Delta)_{p}^{s}\,u+g(u)&\text{in $\Omega\setminus\overline{B}_{\rho}(x_{0})$}\\
w_{\eta}\leqslant u&\text{in $(\Omega\setminus\overline{B}_{\rho}(x_{0}))^{c}$.}\end{cases}

Since
w_{\eta}\leqslant u
in
\overline{B}_{\rho}(x_{0})
, we have
(w_{\eta}-u)^{+}\in W^{s,p}_{0}(\Omega\setminus\overline{B}_{\rho}(x_{0}))
. So we can employ such function to test the inequality above and get

\langle(-\Delta)_{p}^{s}\,w_{\eta}-(-\Delta)_{p}^{s}\,u,(w_{\eta}-u)^{+}\rangle\leqslant\int_{\Omega\setminus\overline{B}_{\rho}(x_{0})}(g(u)-g(w_{\eta}))(w_{\eta}-u)^{+}\,dx,

and the latter is negative by monotonicity of
g
. By Proposition
2.1
we have
w_{\eta}\leqslant u
in
\Omega\setminus\overline{B}_{\rho}(x_{0})
. Combining with (
2.6
) we get in
\Omega

u\geqslant\eta v\geqslant\eta c{\rm d}_{\Omega}^{s},

hence the conclusion. In particular, if
u\in C_{s}^{0}(\overline{\Omega})
, then clearly we have
u\in{\rm int}(C_{s}^{0}(\overline{\Omega})_{+})
.
∎

With a similar technique we prove a strong comparison principle for general autonomous reactions:

Theorem 2.7
.

Let
g\in C^{0}({\mathbb{R}})\cap BV_{\rm loc}({\mathbb{R}})
,
u,v\in W^{s,p}_{0}(\Omega)\cap C^{0}(\overline{\Omega})
s.t.
u\not\equiv v
,
K>0
satisfy

\begin{cases}(-\Delta)_{p}^{s}\,v+g(v)\leqslant(-\Delta)_{p}^{s}\,u+g(u)\leqslant K&\text{weakly in $\Omega$}\\
0<v\leqslant u&\text{in $\Omega$.}\end{cases}

Then,
u>v
in
\Omega
. In particular, if
u,v\in{\rm int}(C_{s}^{0}(\overline{\Omega})_{+})
, then
u-v\in{\rm int}(C_{s}^{0}(\overline{\Omega})_{+})
.

Proof.

As in Theorem
2.6
, we may assume
g
nondecreasing. By continuity, we can find
x_{0}\in\Omega
,
\rho,\varepsilon>0
s.t.
\overline{B}_{\rho}(x_{0})\subset\Omega
and

\sup_{\overline{B}_{\rho}(x_{0})}v<\inf_{\overline{B}_{\rho}(x_{0})}u-\varepsilon.

Hence, for all
\eta>1
close enough to
1
we have

(2.7)

\sup_{\overline{B}_{\rho}(x_{0})}\eta v<\inf_{\overline{B}_{\rho}(x_{0})}u-\frac{\varepsilon}{2}.

Define
w_{\eta}\in\widetilde{W}^{s,p}(\Omega\setminus\overline{B}_{\rho}(x_{0}))
as in Theorem
2.6
, so by (
2.7
) we have
w_{\eta}\leqslant u
in
\overline{B}_{\rho}(x_{0})
. Applying nonlocal superposition as in the previous proof we have weakly in
\Omega\setminus\overline{B}_{\rho}(x_{0})

(-\Delta)_{p}^{s}\,w_{\eta}\leqslant\eta^{p-1}(-\Delta)_{p}^{s}\,v-C_{\rho}\varepsilon^{p-1},

for some
C_{\rho}>0
and all
\eta>1
close enough to
1
. Further, we have weakly in
\Omega\setminus\overline{B}_{\rho}(x_{0})

\displaystyle(-\Delta)_{p}^{s}\,w_{\eta}+g(w_{\eta})

\displaystyle\leqslant\eta^{p-1}(-\Delta)_{p}^{s}\,v+g(w_{\eta})-C_{\rho}\varepsilon^{p-1}

\displaystyle\leqslant\eta^{p-1}\big((-\Delta)_{p}^{s}\,v+g(v)\big)+\big(g(w_{\eta})-\eta^{p-1}g(v)\big)-C_{\rho}\varepsilon^{p-1}

\displaystyle\leqslant\eta^{p-1}\big((-\Delta)_{p}^{s}\,u+g(u)\big)+\big(g(w_{\eta})-\eta^{p-1}g(v)\big)-C_{\rho}\varepsilon^{p-1}

\displaystyle\leqslant(-\Delta)_{p}^{s}\,u+g(u)+K(\eta^{p-1}-1)+\big(g(w_{\eta})-\eta^{p-1}g(v)\big)-C_{\rho}\varepsilon^{p-1}.

Since

K(\eta^{p-1}-1)+\big(g(w_{\eta})-\eta^{p-1}g(v)\big)\to 0

uniformly in
\Omega\setminus\overline{B}_{\rho}(x_{0})
as
\eta\to 1^{+}
, we have for all
\eta>1
close enough to
1

\begin{cases}(-\Delta)_{p}^{s}\,w_{\eta}+g(w_{\eta})\leqslant(-\Delta)_{p}^{s}\,u+g(u)&\text{weakly in $\Omega\setminus\overline{B}_{\rho}(x_{0})$}\\
w_{\eta}\leqslant u&\text{in $(\Omega\setminus\overline{B}_{\rho}(x_{0}))^{c}$.}\end{cases}

Testing with
(w_{\eta}-u)^{+}\in W^{s,p}_{0}(\Omega\setminus\overline{B}_{\rho}(x_{0}))
, recalling the monotonicity of
g
, and applying Proposition
2.1
we get
u\geqslant w_{\eta}
in
\Omega\setminus\overline{B}_{\rho}(x_{0})
. So we have in
\Omega

u\geqslant\eta v>v,

hence the conclusion. In particular, if
u,v\in{\rm int}(C_{s}^{0}(\overline{\Omega})_{+})
, then clearly

\inf_{\Omega}\frac{u-v}{{\rm d}_{\Omega}^{s}}\geqslant\inf_{\Omega}\frac{(\eta-1)v}{{\rm d}_{\Omega}^{s}}>0,

so
u-v\in{\rm int}(C_{s}^{0}(\overline{\Omega})_{+})
.
∎

Remark 2.8
.

Both results above exhibit unexpected differences when compared to the corresponding local versions, i.e., the case of the classical
p
-Laplacian. For example, according to Theorem
2.6
, the strong maximum principle holds for supersolutions of the Dirichlet problem

\begin{cases}(-\Delta)_{p}^{s}\,u+u^{\sigma}=0&\text{in $\Omega$}\\
u=0&\text{in $\Omega^{c}$}\end{cases}

for any
\sigma>0
, while for
s=1
the same is not true when
\sigma<p-1
due to the possible presence of dead cores (see
[
34
, p. 204]
). Also, the strong comparison principle of Theorem
2.7
includes cases which are excluded in the local case (see
[
11
, Example 4.1]
). This is essentially due to the nonlocal nature of the operator.

3.
The subdiffusive case

In this section we study problem
(P_{\lambda})
with
1<q<p<r<p^{*}_{s}
. For all
\lambda>0
,
t\in{\mathbb{R}}
we set

f_{\lambda}(t)=\lambda(t^{+})^{q-1}-(t^{+})^{r-1},

F_{\lambda}(t)=\int_{0}^{t}f_{\lambda}(\tau)\,d\tau=\lambda\frac{(t^{+})^{q}}{q}-\frac{(t^{+})^{r}}{r}.

Note that
f_{\lambda}:{\mathbb{R}}\to{\mathbb{R}}
satisfies hypotheses
{\bf H}
as stated in Section
2
. So we may set for all
u\in W^{s,p}_{0}(\Omega)

\Phi_{\lambda}(u)=\frac{\|u\|^{p}}{p}-\int_{\Omega}F_{\lambda}(u)\,dx,

and deduce that
\Phi_{\lambda}\in C^{1}(W^{s,p}_{0}(\Omega))
and the positive critical points of
\Phi_{\lambda}
coincide with the solutions of
(P_{\lambda})
.

In this case we have the following global existence and uniqueness result (corresponding to case
(a)
of Theorem
1.1
):

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
1<q<p<r<p^{*}_{s}
. Then, for all
\lambda>0
problem
(P_{\lambda})
has a unique solution
u_{\lambda}\in{\rm int}(C_{s}^{0}(\overline{\Omega})_{+})
, s.t.
u_{\lambda}-u_{\mu}\in{\rm int}(C_{s}^{0}(\overline{\Omega})_{+})
for all
\lambda>\mu>0
and
u_{\lambda}\to 0
in both
W^{s,p}_{0}(\Omega)
and
C_{s}^{0}(\overline{\Omega})
as
\lambda\to 0^{+}
.

Proof.

Fix any
\lambda>0
. We will find the solution of
(P_{\lambda})
by direct minimization. First we prove that
\Phi_{\lambda}
is coercive. Indeed, since
q<r
, the mapping
F_{\lambda}
is clearly bounded from above, i.e., there exists
C>0
s.t.
F_{\lambda}(t)\leqslant C
for all
t\in{\mathbb{R}}
. So, for all
u\in W^{s,p}_{0}(\Omega)
we have

\Phi_{\lambda}(u)\geqslant\frac{\|u\|^{p}}{p}-C|\Omega|,

and the latter tends to
\infty
as
\|u\|\to\infty
. Besides, by the compact embeddings
W^{s,p}_{0}(\Omega)\hookrightarrow L^{q}(\Omega),\,L^{r}(\Omega)
, it is easily seen that
\Phi_{\lambda}
is sequentially weakly lower semicontinuous in
W^{s,p}_{0}(\Omega)
. So, there exists
u_{\lambda}\in W^{s,p}_{0}(\Omega)
s.t.

(3.1)

\Phi_{\lambda}(u_{\lambda})=\inf_{W^{s,p}_{0}(\Omega)}\Phi_{\lambda}=:m_{\lambda}.

Besides, let
\hat{u}_{1}\in{\rm int}(C_{s}^{0}(\overline{\Omega})_{+})
be defined as in Section
2
, then for all
\tau>0

\Phi_{\lambda}(\tau\hat{u}_{1})=\tau^{p}\frac{\|\hat{u}_{1}\|^{p}}{p}-\lambda\tau^{q}\frac{\|\hat{u}_{1}\|_{q}^{q}}{q}+\tau^{r}\frac{\|\hat{u}_{1}\|_{r}^{r}}{r},

and the latter is negative for all
\tau>0
small enough (recall that
q<p<r
). So, in (
3.1
) we have
m_{\lambda}<0
, implying
u_{\lambda}\not\equiv 0
. From (
3.1
) we deduce that
\Phi^{\prime}_{\lambda}(u_{\lambda})=0
in
W^{-s,p^{\prime}}(\Omega)
, i.e., we have weakly in
\Omega

(3.2)

(-\Delta)_{p}^{s}\,u_{\lambda}=f_{\lambda}(u_{\lambda}).

By Lemma
2.3
we have
u_{\lambda}\in C^{\alpha}_{s}(\overline{\Omega})
. Besides, testing (
3.2
) with
-u_{\lambda}^{-}\in W^{s,p}_{0}(\Omega)
and applying (
2.2
), we have

\|u_{\lambda}^{-}\|^{p}\leqslant\langle(-\Delta)_{p}^{s}\,u_{\lambda},-u_{\lambda}^{-}\rangle=\int_{\Omega}f_{\lambda}(u_{\lambda})(-u_{\lambda}^{-})\,dx=0.

so
u_{\lambda}\geqslant 0
. Now Theorem
2.6
implies
u_{\lambda}\in{\rm int}(C_{s}^{0}(\overline{\Omega})_{+})
, so
u_{\lambda}
solves
(P_{\lambda})
.

Next we prove uniqueness. Let
v_{\lambda}\in{\rm int}(C_{s}^{0}(\overline{\Omega})_{+})
be another solution of
(P_{\lambda})
. We have for all
t>0

\frac{f_{\lambda}(t)}{t^{p-1}}=\lambda t^{q-p}-t^{r-p},

and such mapping is decreasing in
(0,\infty)
. Applying Proposition
2.4
twice, we have
u_{\lambda}=v_{\lambda}
.

To see monotonicity, let
0<\mu<\lambda
, and
u_{\mu},u_{\lambda}\in{\rm int}(C_{s}^{0}(\overline{\Omega})_{+})
be the solutions of
(P_{\mu})
,
(P_{\lambda})
, respectively. We have weakly in
\Omega

(-\Delta)_{p}^{s}\,u_{\mu}<\lambda u_{\mu}^{q-1}-u_{\mu}^{r-1},

so
u_{\mu}
is a strict subsolution of
(P_{\lambda})
. By Proposition
2.4
again we have
u_{\mu}\leqslant u_{\lambda}
in
\Omega
. This in turn implies that weakly in
\Omega

(-\Delta)_{p}^{s}\,u_{\mu}+u_{\mu}^{r-1}=\mu u_{\mu}^{q-1}<\lambda u_{\lambda}^{q-1}=(-\Delta)_{p}^{s}\,u_{\lambda}+u_{\lambda}^{r-1}.

Since
g(t)=t^{r-1}
is continuous and with locally bounded variation, we can apply Theorem
2.7
and see that
u_{\lambda}-u_{\mu}\in{\rm int}(C_{s}^{0}(\overline{\Omega})_{+})
.

Finally, let
(\lambda_{n})
be a decreasing sequence in
(0,\infty)
s.t.
\lambda_{n}\to 0^{+}
, and
u_{n}\in{\rm int}(C_{s}^{0}(\overline{\Omega})_{+})
be the solution of
(P_{\lambda_{n}})
for all
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
, i.e., we have weakly in
\Omega

(3.3)

(-\Delta)_{p}^{s}\,u_{n}=f_{\lambda_{n}}(u_{n}).

Since
q<p
and
(\lambda_{n})
is decreasing, we can find
C>0
s.t. for all
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
,
t\in{\mathbb{R}}

f_{\lambda_{n}}(t)t\leqslant C.

Testing (
3.3
) with
u_{n}\in W^{s,p}_{0}(\Omega)
, for all
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
we have

\|u_{n}\|^{p}=\langle(-\Delta)_{p}^{s}\,u_{n},u_{n}\rangle=\int_{\Omega}f_{\lambda_{n}}(u_{n})u_{n}\,dx\leqslant C|\Omega|.

So,
(u_{n})
is a bounded sequence in
W^{s,p}_{0}(\Omega)
. By reflexivity and the compact embeddings
W^{s,p}_{0}(\Omega)\hookrightarrow L^{q}(\Omega),\,L^{r}(\Omega)
, we can pass to a subsequence s.t.
u_{n}\rightharpoonup u_{0}
in
W^{s,p}_{0}(\Omega)
and
u_{n}\to u_{0}
in both
L^{q}(\Omega)
and
L^{r}(\Omega)
. Testing (
3.3
) with
(u_{n}-u_{0})\in W^{s,p}_{0}(\Omega)
and using Hölder’s inequality, we have for all
n\in{\mathbb{N}}

\displaystyle\langle(-\Delta)_{p}^{s}\,u_{n},u_{n}-u_{0}\rangle

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}(\lambda_{n}u_{n}^{q-1}-u_{n}^{r-1})(u_{n}-u_{0})\,dx

\displaystyle\leqslant\lambda_{1}\|u_{n}\|_{q}^{q-1}\|u_{n}-u_{0}\|_{q}+\|u_{n}\|_{r}^{r-1}\|u_{n}-u_{0}\|_{r},

and the latter tends to
0
as
n\to\infty
. By the
(S)_{+}
-property of
(-\Delta)_{p}^{s}\,
, we have
u_{n}\to u_{0}
in
W^{s,p}_{0}(\Omega)
. So we can pass to the limit in (
3.3
) as
n\to\infty
and get weakly in
\Omega

(-\Delta)_{p}^{s}\,u_{0}=-u_{0}^{r-1}.

Testing with
u_{0}\in W^{s,p}_{0}(\Omega)
we have

\|u_{0}\|^{p}+\|u_{0}\|_{r}^{r}=0,

i.e.,
u_{0}=0
. Plus, we note that, by (
3.3
) and Proposition
2.3
,
(u_{n})
is bounded in
C^{\alpha}_{s}(\overline{\Omega})
, hence, passing to a further subsequence,
u_{n}\to 0
in
C_{s}^{0}(\overline{\Omega})
. Recalling that
\lambda\mapsto u_{\lambda}
is strictly increasing, we conclude that globally
u_{\lambda}\to 0
in both
W^{s,p}_{0}(\Omega)
and
C_{s}^{0}(\overline{\Omega})
, as
\lambda\to 0^{+}
.
∎

4.
The equidiffusive case

In this short section we assume
2\leqslant q=p<r<p^{*}_{s}
, a case that does not differ too much from the previous one, except that the threshold for the parameter
\lambda
turns out to be the principal eigenvalue
\hat{\lambda}_{1}>0
defined in (
2.4
). We define
f_{\lambda}
,
F_{\lambda}
, and
\Phi_{\lambda}
as in Section
3
.

Our existence and uniqueness result (corresponding to case
(b)
of Theorem
1.1
) is the following:

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
2\leqslant q=p<r<p^{*}_{s}
. Then, for all
\lambda\in(0,\hat{\lambda}_{1}]
problem
(P_{\lambda})
has no solution, while for all
\lambda>\hat{\lambda}_{1}
problem
(P_{\lambda})
has a unique solution
u_{\lambda}\in{\rm int}(C_{s}^{0}(\overline{\Omega})_{+})
, s.t.
u_{\lambda}-u_{\mu}\in{\rm int}(C_{s}^{0}(\overline{\Omega})_{+})
for all
\lambda>\mu>\hat{\lambda}_{1}
and
u_{\lambda}\to 0
in both
W^{s,p}_{0}(\Omega)
and
C_{s}^{0}(\overline{\Omega})
as
\lambda\to\hat{\lambda}_{1}^{+}
.

Proof.

First, fix
\lambda\in(0,\hat{\lambda}_{1}]
. Assume that
u\in W^{s,p}_{0}(\Omega)_{+}
satisfies weakly in
\Omega

(4.1)

(-\Delta)_{p}^{s}\,u=\lambda u^{p-1}-u^{r-1}.

Testing (
4.1
) with
u\in W^{s,p}_{0}(\Omega)
and applying (
2.4
), we have

0=\|u\|^{p}-\lambda\|u\|_{p}^{p}+\|u\|_{r}^{r}\geqslant(\hat{\lambda}_{1}-\lambda)\|u\|_{p}^{p}+\|u\|_{r}^{r}\geqslant\|u\|_{r}^{r},

hence
u=0
. So
(P_{\lambda})
admits no solution.

Now let
\lambda>\hat{\lambda}_{1}
. Arguing as in Theorem
3.1
, we see that
\Phi_{\lambda}
has a global minimizer
u_{\lambda}\in W^{s,p}_{0}(\Omega)_{+}
. Besides, for all
\tau>0
we have

\displaystyle\Phi_{\lambda}(\tau\hat{u}_{1})

\displaystyle=\tau^{p}\Big[\frac{\|\hat{u}_{1}\|^{p}}{p}-\lambda\frac{\|\hat{u}_{1}\|_{p}^{p}}{p}\Big]+\tau^{r}\frac{\|\hat{u}_{1}\|_{r}^{r}}{r}

\displaystyle=\tau^{p}\frac{\hat{\lambda}_{1}-\lambda}{p}+\tau^{r}\frac{\|\hat{u}_{1}\|_{r}^{r}}{r},

and the latter is negative for
\tau>0
small enough (as
p<r
). So,
u_{\lambda}\not\equiv 0
. The rest of the proof follows exactly as in Theorem
3.1
.
∎

5.
The superdiffusive case

In this final section we assume
2\leqslant p<q<r<p^{*}_{s}
and define
f_{\lambda}
,
F_{\lambda}
,
\Phi_{\lambda}
as in Section
3
. Let

\Lambda=\big\{\lambda>0:\,\text{$(P_{\lambda})$ has a solution $u_{\lambda}\in{\rm int}(C_{s}^{0}(\overline{\Omega})_{+})$}\big\}.

In the following lemmas we shall investigate the structure of the set
\Lambda
and additional properties of solutions. We begin with a lower bound:

Lemma 5.1
.

We have
\Lambda\neq\emptyset
and
\lambda_{*}:=\inf\Lambda>0
.

Proof.

Fix
\lambda>0
. As in the proof of Theorem
3.1
we find
u_{\lambda}\in W^{s,p}_{0}(\Omega)_{+}
s.t.

(5.1)

\Phi_{\lambda}(u_{\lambda})=\inf_{W^{s,p}_{0}(\Omega)}\Phi_{\lambda}=:m_{\lambda}.

Let
\hat{u}_{1}\in{\rm int}(C_{s}^{0}(\overline{\Omega})_{+})
be defined as in Section
2
, then we have

\Phi_{\lambda}(\hat{u}_{1})=\frac{\|\hat{u}_{1}\|^{p}}{p}-\lambda\frac{\|\hat{u}_{1}\|_{q}^{q}}{q}+\frac{\|\hat{u}_{1}\|_{r}^{r}}{r},

which tends to
-\infty
as
\lambda\to\infty
. So, for all
\lambda>0
big enough we have
m_{\lambda}<0
in (
5.1
), hence
u_{\lambda}\neq 0
. As in Theorem
3.1
we see that
u_{\lambda}\in{\rm int}(C_{s}^{0}(\overline{\Omega})_{+})
and it solves
(P_{\lambda})
. Thus
\lambda\in\Lambda
.

We claim that there exists
\lambda_{0}>0
s.t. for all
t\geqslant 0

(5.2)

f_{\lambda_{0}}(t)\leqslant\hat{\lambda}_{1}t^{p-1},

with
\hat{\lambda}_{1}>0
defined by (
2.4
). Indeed, since
p<q<r
we have for any
\lambda>0

\lim_{t\to 0^{+}}\frac{f_{\lambda}(t)}{t^{p-1}}=0,\ \lim_{t\to\infty}\frac{f_{\lambda}(t)}{t^{p-1}}=-\infty.

So we can find
\delta\in(0,1)
s.t. for all
t\in(0,\delta)\cup(\delta^{-1},\infty)
and all
\lambda\in(0,1]

f_{\lambda}(t)\leqslant\hat{\lambda}_{1}t^{p-1}.

Now set

\lambda_{0}=\min\{\hat{\lambda}_{1}\delta^{q-p},1\}>0.

Then, for all
t\in[\delta,\delta^{-1}]
we have

f_{\lambda_{0}}(t)<\lambda_{0}t^{q-1}\leqslant\hat{\lambda}_{1}t^{p-1},

hence (
5.2
) holds for all
t\geqslant 0
. We prove that
\inf\Lambda\geqslant\lambda_{0}
, arguing by contradiction. Assume that for some
\lambda\in(0,\lambda_{0})
problem
(P_{\lambda})
has a solution
u_{\lambda}\in{\rm int}(C_{s}^{0}(\overline{\Omega})_{+})
. Testing with
u_{\lambda}\in W^{s,p}_{0}(\Omega)
and using (
5.2
) we get

\|u_{\lambda}\|^{p}=\int_{\Omega}f_{\lambda}(u_{\lambda})u_{\lambda}\,dx<\int_{\Omega}f_{\lambda_{0}}(u_{\lambda})u_{\lambda}\,dx\leqslant\hat{\lambda}_{1}\|u_{\lambda}\|_{p}^{p},

against (
2.4
).
∎

Next we prove that
\Lambda
is a half-line and the mapping
\lambda\mapsto u_{\lambda}
is strictly increasing:

Lemma 5.2
.

If
\lambda>\lambda_{*}
then
\lambda\in\Lambda
, besides
u_{\lambda}-u_{\mu}\in{\rm int}(C_{s}^{0}(\overline{\Omega})_{+})
for all
\lambda>\mu>\lambda_{*}
.

Proof.

Fix
\lambda>\lambda_{*}
. Then we can find
\mu\in\Lambda
s.t.
\mu<\lambda
, and a solution
u_{\mu}\in{\rm int}(C_{s}^{0}(\overline{\Omega})_{+})
of
(P_{\mu})
. We have weakly in
\Omega

(5.3)

(-\Delta)_{p}^{s}\,u_{\mu}=f_{\mu}(u_{\mu})<f_{\lambda}(u_{\mu}),

i.e.,
u_{\mu}
is a strict subsolution of
(P_{\lambda})
. We use
u_{\mu}
to truncate the reaction
f_{\lambda}
. Set for all
(x,t)\in\Omega\times{\mathbb{R}}

\hat{f}_{\lambda}(x,t)=\begin{cases}f_{\lambda}(u_{\mu}(x))&\text{if $t\leqslant u_{\mu}(x)$}\\
f_{\lambda}(t)&\text{if $t>u_{\mu}(x)$}\end{cases}

and

\hat{F}_{\lambda}(x,t)=\int_{0}^{t}\hat{f}_{\lambda}(x,\tau)\,d\tau.

So
\hat{f}_{\lambda}:\Omega\times{\mathbb{R}}\to{\mathbb{R}}
satisfies
{\bf H}
. Set for all
u\in W^{s,p}_{0}(\Omega)

\hat{\Phi}_{\lambda}(u)=\frac{\|u\|^{p}}{p}-\int_{\Omega}\hat{F}_{\lambda}(x,u)\,dx,

then as in Section
2
it is seen that
\hat{\Phi}_{\lambda}\in C^{1}(W^{s,p}_{0}(\Omega))
. Reasoning as in Theorem
3.1
we also see that
\hat{\Phi}_{\lambda}
is coercive and sequentially weakly l.s.c., so there exists
u_{\lambda}\in W^{s,p}_{0}(\Omega)
s.t.

\hat{\Phi}_{\lambda}(u_{\lambda})=\inf_{W^{s,p}_{0}(\Omega)}\hat{\Phi}_{\lambda}.

As a consequence we have
\hat{\Phi}_{\lambda}^{\prime}(u_{\lambda})=0
in
W^{-s,p^{\prime}}(\Omega)
, i.e., weakly in
\Omega

(5.4)

(-\Delta)_{p}^{s}\,u_{\lambda}=\hat{f}_{\lambda}(x,u).

Testing (
5.4
) with
(u_{\mu}-u_{\lambda})^{+}\in W^{s,p}_{0}(\Omega)_{+}
we get

\displaystyle\langle(-\Delta)_{p}^{s}\,u_{\lambda},(u_{\mu}-u_{\lambda})^{+}\rangle

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}\hat{f}_{\lambda}(x,u_{\lambda})(u_{\mu}-u_{\lambda})^{+}\,dx

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}f_{\lambda}(u_{\mu})(u_{\mu}-u_{\lambda})^{+}\,dx,

which along with (
5.3
) gives

\langle(-\Delta)_{p}^{s}\,u_{\mu}-(-\Delta)_{p}^{s}\,u_{\lambda},(u_{\mu}-u_{\lambda})^{+}\rangle\leqslant 0.

By Proposition
2.1
we have
u_{\mu}\leqslant u_{\lambda}
in
\Omega
. So (
5.4
) rephrases as

(-\Delta)_{p}^{s}\,u_{\lambda}=f_{\lambda}(u_{\lambda})

weakly in
\Omega
, and moreover
u_{\lambda}>0
in
\Omega
. As in Lemma
5.1
we see that
u_{\lambda}\in{\rm int}(C_{s}^{0}(\overline{\Omega})_{+})
and it solves
(P_{\lambda})
, so
\lambda\in\Lambda
.

Finally, for all
\lambda>\mu>\lambda_{*}
we have weakly in
\Omega

(-\Delta)_{p}^{s}\,u_{\mu}+u_{\mu}^{r-1}=\mu u_{\mu}^{q-1}<\lambda u_{\lambda}^{q-1}=(-\Delta)_{p}^{s}\,u_{\lambda}+u_{\lambda}^{r-1},

as well as
0<u_{\mu}\leqslant u_{\lambda}
in
\Omega
. By Theorem
2.7
we conclude that
u_{\lambda}-u_{\mu}\in{\rm int}(C_{s}^{0}(\overline{\Omega})_{+})
.
∎

Note that in Lemma
5.2
we cannot use Proposition
2.4
to prove the monotonicity of
\lambda\mapsto u_{\lambda}
, as we did in sub- and equidiffusive cases: this is due to the fact that
t\mapsto f_{\lambda}(t)/t^{p-1}
is not a decreasing mapping in
(0,\infty)
(recall that
q>p
). The same reason prevents the use of Proposition
2.4
to prove uniqueness of the solution.

In fact, for
\lambda>\lambda_{*}
we detect at least one more solution beside
u_{\lambda}
:

Lemma 5.3
.

For all
\lambda>\lambda_{*}
there exists a second solution
v_{\lambda}\in{\rm int}(C_{s}^{0}(\overline{\Omega})_{+})
of
(P_{\lambda})
s.t.
u_{\lambda}-v_{\lambda}\in{\rm int}(C_{s}^{0}(\overline{\Omega})_{+})
.

Proof.

Fix
\lambda>\lambda_{*}
. As in Lemma
5.2
we pick
\mu\in\Lambda
s.t.
\lambda_{*}<\mu<\lambda
, define
\hat{\Phi}_{\lambda}\in C^{1}(W^{s,p}_{0}(\Omega))
, and find a global minimizer
u_{\lambda}\in{\rm int}(C_{s}^{0}(\overline{\Omega})_{+})
, which solves
(P_{\lambda})
and satisfies
u_{\lambda}-u_{\mu}\in{\rm int}(C_{s}^{0}(\overline{\Omega})_{+})
. Set now

V=\big\{u_{\mu}+v:\,v\in{\rm int}(C_{s}^{0}(\overline{\Omega})_{+})\big\},

an open set in
C_{s}^{0}(\overline{\Omega})
containing
u_{\lambda}
. For all
x\in\Omega
,
t>u_{\mu}(x)
we have

\displaystyle\hat{F}_{\lambda}(x,t)

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{u_{\mu}(x)}f_{\lambda}(u_{\mu}(x))\,d\tau+\int_{u_{\mu}(x)}^{t}f_{\lambda}(\tau)\,d\tau

\displaystyle=F_{\lambda}(t)+\big[f_{\lambda}(u_{\mu}(x))u_{\mu}(x)-F_{\lambda}(u_{\mu}(x))\big],

hence for all
u\in V\cap W^{s,p}_{0}(\Omega)
(note that
u>u_{\mu}
in
\Omega
)

\hat{\Phi}_{\lambda}(u)=\frac{\|u\|^{p}}{p}-\int_{\Omega}F_{\lambda}(u)\,dx-\int_{\Omega}\big[f_{\lambda}(u_{\mu})u_{\mu}-F_{\lambda}(u_{\mu})\big]\,dx=\Phi_{\lambda}(u)-C,

with
C\in{\mathbb{R}}
independent of
u
. So, recalling that
u_{\lambda}
minimizes
\hat{\Phi}_{\lambda}
over
W^{s,p}_{0}(\Omega)
, for all
u\in V\cap W^{s,p}_{0}(\Omega)
we have

\Phi_{\lambda}(u)\geqslant\Phi_{\lambda}(u_{\lambda}),

i.e.,
u_{\lambda}
is a local minimizer of
\Phi_{\lambda}
in
C_{s}^{0}(\overline{\Omega})
. By Proposition
2.5
,
u_{\lambda}
is as well a local minimizer of
\Phi_{\lambda}
in
W^{s,p}_{0}(\Omega)
. To proceed with the proof we need to perform a different truncation on the reaction. Set for all
(x,t)\in\Omega\times{\mathbb{R}}

\tilde{f}_{\lambda}(x,t)=\begin{cases}f_{\lambda}(t)&\text{if $t\leqslant u_{\lambda}(x)$}\\
\lambda u_{\lambda}^{q-1}(x)-t^{r-1}&\text{if $t>u_{\lambda}(x)$}\end{cases}

and as usual

\tilde{F}_{\lambda}(x,t)=\int_{0}^{t}\tilde{f}_{\lambda}(x,\tau)\,d\tau.

Clearly
\tilde{f}_{\lambda}:\Omega\times{\mathbb{R}}\to{\mathbb{R}}
satisfies
{\bf H}
. So, we set for all
u\in W^{s,p}_{0}(\Omega)

\tilde{\Phi}_{\lambda}(u)=\frac{\|u\|^{p}}{p}-\int_{\Omega}\tilde{F}_{\lambda}(x,u)\,dx

and thus define a functional
\tilde{\Phi}_{\lambda}\in C^{1}(W^{s,p}_{0}(\Omega))
. We note that for all
(x,t)\in\Omega\times{\mathbb{R}}
we have
\tilde{f}_{\lambda}(x,t)\leqslant f_{\lambda}(t)
and hence
\tilde{F}_{\lambda}(x,t)\leqslant F_{\lambda}(t)
. This in turn implies for all
u\in W^{s,p}_{0}(\Omega)

(5.5)

\tilde{\Phi}_{\lambda}(u)\geqslant\Phi_{\lambda}(u).

Since
u_{\lambda}
is a local minimizer of
\Phi_{\lambda}
, we can find
\rho>0
s.t.
\Phi_{\lambda}(u)\geqslant\Phi_{\lambda}(u_{\lambda})
for all
u\in B_{\rho}(u_{\lambda})
, hence by (
5.5
)

\tilde{\Phi}_{\lambda}(u)\geqslant\Phi_{\lambda}(u)\geqslant\Phi_{\lambda}(u_{\lambda})=\tilde{\Phi}_{\lambda}(u_{\lambda}).

So,
u_{\lambda}
is as well a local minimizer of
\tilde{\Phi}_{\lambda}
. Besides, fix
\varepsilon\in(0,\hat{\lambda}_{1})
(with
\hat{\lambda}_{1}>0
defined by (
2.4
)), then we can find
\delta>0
s.t. for all
x\in{\mathbb{R}}
,
|t|\leqslant\delta

\tilde{F}_{\lambda}(x,t)\leqslant F_{\lambda}(t)\leqslant\varepsilon\frac{(t^{+})^{p}}{p}.

Since
\Omega
is bounded, we can find
\sigma>0
s.t.
\|u\|_{\infty}\leqslant\delta
for all
u\in C_{s}^{0}(\overline{\Omega})
,
\|u\|_{0,s}\leqslant\sigma
. Then, using also (
2.4
), for all
u\in W^{s,p}_{0}(\Omega)\cap C_{s}^{0}(\overline{\Omega})
with
0<\|u\|_{0,s}\leqslant\sigma
we have

\tilde{\Phi}_{\lambda}(u)\geqslant\frac{\|u\|^{p}}{p}-\int_{\Omega}\varepsilon\frac{(u^{+})^{p}}{p}\,dx\geqslant(\hat{\lambda}_{1}-\varepsilon)\frac{\|u\|_{p}^{p}}{p}>0.

So,
0
is a strict local minimizer of
\tilde{\Phi}_{\lambda}
in
C_{s}^{0}(\overline{\Omega})
. By Proposition
2.5
again,
0
is as well a local minimizer of
\tilde{\Phi}_{\lambda}
in
W^{s,p}_{0}(\Omega)
. From Lemma
5.1
we know that
\Phi_{\lambda}
is coercive in
W^{s,p}_{0}(\Omega)
, so by (
5.5
)
\tilde{\Phi}_{\lambda}
is coercive as well. As recalled in Section
2
,
\tilde{\Phi}_{\lambda}
then satisfies the
(PS)
-condition. Thus, we may apply the mountain pass theorem (see
[
33
, Theorem 2.1]
) and deduce the existence of
v_{\lambda}\in W^{s,p}_{0}(\Omega)\setminus\{0,u_{\lambda}\}
s.t.
\tilde{\Phi}_{\lambda}^{\prime}(v_{\lambda})=0
in
W^{-s,p^{\prime}}(\Omega)
. So we have weakly in
\Omega

(5.6)

(-\Delta)_{p}^{s}\,v_{\lambda}=\tilde{f}_{\lambda}(x,v_{\lambda}).

Testing (
5.6
) with
-v_{\lambda}^{-}\in W^{s,p}_{0}(\Omega)
and applying (
2.2
) we have

\|v_{\lambda}^{-}\|^{p}\leqslant\langle(-\Delta)_{p}^{s}\,v_{\lambda},-v_{\lambda}^{-}\rangle=\int_{\Omega}\tilde{f}_{\lambda}(x,v_{\lambda})(-v_{\lambda}^{-})\,dx=0,

so
v_{\lambda}\in W^{s,p}_{0}(\Omega)_{+}\setminus\{0\}
. Recalling the definition of
\tilde{f}_{\lambda}
and testing (
5.6
) with
(v_{\lambda}-u_{\lambda})^{+}\in W^{s,p}_{0}(\Omega)
, we have

\displaystyle\langle(-\Delta)_{p}^{s}\,v_{\lambda},(v_{\lambda}-u_{\lambda})^{+}\rangle

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}\tilde{f}_{\lambda}(x,v_{\lambda})(v_{\lambda}-u_{\lambda})^{+}\,dx

\displaystyle\leqslant\int_{\Omega}f_{\lambda}(u_{\lambda})(v_{\lambda}-u_{\lambda})^{+}\,dx

\displaystyle=\langle(-\Delta)_{p}^{s}\,u_{\lambda},(v_{\lambda}-u_{\lambda})^{+}\rangle,

which by Proposition
2.1
implies
v_{\lambda}\leqslant u_{\lambda}
in
\Omega
. So, (
5.6
) rephrases as

(-\Delta)_{p}^{s}\,v_{\lambda}=f_{\lambda}(v_{\lambda})

weakly in
\Omega
. Using Theorem
2.6
as in Theorem
3.1
, we see that
v_{\lambda}\in{\rm int}(C_{s}^{0}(\overline{\Omega})_{+})
and it solves
(P_{\lambda})
. So we have
v_{\lambda}\leqslant u_{\lambda}
in
\Omega
,
v_{\lambda}\not\equiv u_{\lambda}
, and weakly in
\Omega

(-\Delta)_{p}^{s}\,v_{\lambda}+v_{\lambda}^{r-1}=\lambda v_{\lambda}^{q-1}\leqslant\lambda u_{\lambda}^{q-1}=(-\Delta)_{p}^{s}\,u_{\lambda}+u_{\lambda}^{r-1}.

By Theorem
2.7
we have
u_{\lambda}-v_{\lambda}\in{\rm int}(C_{s}^{0}(\overline{\Omega})_{+})
.
∎

To complete the picture, we examine the limiting case
\lambda=\lambda_{*}
. In such case we can prove existence of at least one solution, to which all principal solutions
u_{\lambda}
converge:

Lemma 5.4
.

There exists a solution
u_{*}\in{\rm int}(C_{s}^{0}(\overline{\Omega})_{+})
of
(P_{\lambda_{*}})
s.t.
u_{\lambda}\to u_{*}
in both
W^{s,p}_{0}(\Omega)
and
C_{s}^{0}(\overline{\Omega})
as
\lambda\to\lambda_{*}^{+}
.

Proof.

We prove a slightly more general assertion. Let
(\lambda_{n})
be a decreasing sequence s.t.
\lambda_{n}\to\lambda_{*}^{+}
, and denote by
u_{n}\in{\rm int}(C_{s}^{0}(\overline{\Omega})_{+})
any solution of
(P_{\lambda_{n}})
, then up to a subsequence
u_{n}\to u_{*}
in both
W^{s,p}_{0}(\Omega)
and
C_{s}^{0}(\overline{\Omega})
as
n\to\infty
, being
u_{*}\in{\rm int}(C_{s}^{0}(\overline{\Omega})_{+})
a solution of
(P_{\lambda_{*}})
. First, for all
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
we have weakly in
\Omega

(5.7)

(-\Delta)_{p}^{s}\,u_{n}=f_{\lambda_{n}}(u_{n}).

Arguing as in the proof of Theorem
3.1
, we find
u_{*}\in W^{s,p}_{0}(\Omega)_{+}
s.t. up to a subsequence
u_{n}\to u_{*}
in both
W^{s,p}_{0}(\Omega)
and
C_{s}^{0}(\overline{\Omega})
, hence we can pass to the limit in (
5.7
) and get weakly in
\Omega

(5.8)

(-\Delta)_{p}^{s}\,u_{*}=f_{\lambda_{*}}(u_{*}).

We claim that
u_{*}\not\equiv 0
. Arguing by contradiction, assume that
u_{n}\to 0
in both
W^{s,p}_{0}(\Omega)
and
C_{s}^{0}(\overline{\Omega})
, hence in particular
u_{n}\to 0
uniformly in
\Omega
. Then, for all
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
big enough we have
0<u_{n}\leqslant 1
in
\Omega
. Set for all
n\in{\mathbb{N}}

v_{n}=\frac{u_{n}}{\|u_{n}\|}\in W^{s,p}_{0}(\Omega)\cap{\rm int}(C_{s}^{0}(\overline{\Omega})_{+}).

The sequence
(v_{n})
is obviously bounded in
W^{s,p}_{0}(\Omega)
. By reflexivity and the compact embedding
W^{s,p}_{0}(\Omega)\hookrightarrow L^{p}(\Omega)
, passing to a subsequence we have
v_{n}\rightharpoonup v
in
W^{s,p}_{0}(\Omega)
,
v_{n}\to v
in
L^{p}(\Omega)
. Besides, by (
5.7
), for all
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
we have weakly in
\Omega

(5.9)

(-\Delta)_{p}^{s}\,v_{n}=\lambda_{n}\frac{u_{n}^{q-1}}{\|u_{n}\|^{p-1}}-\frac{u_{n}^{r-1}}{\|u_{n}\|^{p-1}}.

Consider the first term in the right hand side of (
5.9
). Since
0<u_{n}\leqslant 1
in
\Omega
and
p<q
, we have

0<\frac{u_{n}^{q-1}}{\|u_{n}\|^{p-1}}\leqslant\frac{u_{n}^{p-1}}{\|u_{n}\|^{p-1}}=v_{n}^{p-1},

so
(u_{n}^{q-1}/\|u_{n}\|^{p-1})
is bounded in
L^{p^{\prime}}(\Omega)
. Passing to a subsequence, we have
u_{n}^{q-1}/\|u_{n}\|^{p-1}\rightharpoonup w
in
L^{p^{\prime}}(\Omega)
, hence
a fortiori
in
L^{1}(\Omega)
. By Hölder’s inequality and the continuous embedding
W^{s,p}_{0}(\Omega)\hookrightarrow L^{q}(\Omega)
we have

\displaystyle\|w\|_{1}

\displaystyle\leqslant\liminf_{n}\int_{\Omega}\frac{u_{n}^{q-1}}{\|u_{n}\|^{p-1}}\,dx

\displaystyle\leqslant\limsup_{n}\frac{\|u_{n}\|_{q}^{q-1}|\Omega|^{\frac{1}{q}}}{\|u_{n}\|^{p-1}}

\displaystyle\leqslant C\limsup_{n}\|u_{n}\|^{q-p}=0.

So we get
w=0
, i.e.,

(5.10)

\frac{u_{n}^{q-1}}{\|u_{n}\|^{p-1}}\rightharpoonup 0\ \text{in $L^{p^{\prime}}(\Omega)$.}

An entirely similar argument proves that
(u_{n}^{r-1}/\|u_{n}\|^{p-1})
is bounded in
L^{p^{\prime}}(\Omega)
and, up to a subsequence,

(5.11)

\frac{u_{n}^{r-1}}{\|u_{n}\|^{p-1}}\rightharpoonup 0\ \text{in $L^{p^{\prime}}(\Omega)$.}

Testing (
5.9
) with
(v_{n}-v)\in W^{s,p}_{0}(\Omega)
and using Hölder’s inequality, we have for all
n\in{\mathbb{N}}

\displaystyle\langle(-\Delta)_{p}^{s}\,v_{n},v_{n}-v\rangle

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}\Big[\lambda_{n}\frac{u_{n}^{q-1}}{\|u_{n}\|^{p-1}}-\frac{u_{n}^{r-1}}{\|u_{n}\|^{p-1}}\Big](v_{n}-v)\,dx

\displaystyle\leqslant\lambda_{1}\Big\|\frac{u_{n}^{q-1}}{\|u_{n}\|^{p-1}}\Big\|_{p^{\prime}}\|v_{n}-v\|_{p}-\Big\|\frac{u_{n}^{r-1}}{\|u_{n}\|^{p-1}}\Big\|_{p^{\prime}}\|v_{n}-v\|_{p},

and the latter tends to
0
as
n\to\infty
by the relations above. By the
(S)_{+}
-property of
(-\Delta)_{p}^{s}\,
we have
v_{n}\to v
in
W^{s,p}_{0}(\Omega)
, hence
\|v\|=1
. On the other hand, testing (
5.9
) with
v\in W^{s,p}_{0}(\Omega)
, we have for all
n\in{\mathbb{N}}

\langle(-\Delta)_{p}^{s}\,v_{n},v\rangle=\int_{\Omega}\Big[\lambda_{n}\frac{u_{n}^{q-1}}{\|u_{n}\|^{p-1}}-\frac{u_{n}^{r-1}}{\|u_{n}\|^{p-1}}\Big]v\,dx.

Passing to the limit as
n\to\infty
and recalling (
5.10
) and (
5.11
) we get
\|v\|^{p}=0
, a contradiction. Summarizing,
u_{*}\in W^{s,p}_{0}(\Omega)_{+}\setminus\{0\}
and satisfies (
5.8
). As in Lemma
5.1
we see that
u_{*}\in{\rm int}(C_{s}^{0}(\overline{\Omega})_{+})
solves
(P_{\lambda_{*}})
.

Finally, taking into account the monotonicity property of Lemma
5.2
, we conclude that globally
u_{\lambda}\to u_{*}
in both
W^{s,p}_{0}(\Omega)
and
C_{s}^{0}(\overline{\Omega})
, with monotone convergence, as
\lambda\to\lambda_{*}^{+}
, for some
u_{*}\in{\rm int}(C_{s}^{0}(\overline{\Omega})_{+})
solving
(P_{\lambda_{*}})
.
∎

Looking at the proof of Lemma
5.4
above, we can easily argue that, for any sequence
(\lambda_{n})
s.t.
\lambda_{n}\to\lambda_{*}^{+}
, the sequence of solutions
(v_{\lambda_{n}})
provided by Lemma
5.3
has a subsequence which converges to a solution of
(P_{\lambda_{*}})
, which might differ from the global limit of
u_{\lambda}
.

Combining Lemmas
5.1
–
5.4
, we obtain the following bifurcation result for the superdiffusive case (corresponding to case
(c)
of Theorem
1.1
):

Theorem 5.5
.

Let
2\leqslant p<q<r<p^{*}_{s}
. Then, there exists
\lambda_{*}>0
with the following properties: for all
\lambda\in(0,\lambda_{*})
problem
(P_{\lambda})
has no solution;
(P_{\lambda_{*}})
has at least one solution
u_{*}\in{\rm int}(C_{s}^{0}(\overline{\Omega})_{+})
; and for all
\lambda>\lambda_{*}
problem
(P_{\lambda})
has at least two solutions
u_{\lambda},v_{\lambda}\in{\rm int}(C_{s}^{0}(\overline{\Omega})_{+})
s.t.
u_{\lambda}-v_{\lambda}\in{\rm int}(C_{s}^{0}(\overline{\Omega})_{+})
,
u_{\lambda}-u_{\mu}\in{\rm int}(C_{s}^{0}(\overline{\Omega})_{+})
for all
\lambda>\mu>\lambda_{*}
, and
u_{\lambda}\to u_{*}
in both
W^{s,p}_{0}(\Omega)
and
C_{s}^{0}(\overline{\Omega})
as
\lambda\to\lambda_{*}
.

Remark 5.6
.

For simplicity, we confined our study to the pure power logistic reactions. Nevertheless, most of our Theorem
5.5
can be extended to the following generalized logistic equation:

\begin{cases}(-\Delta)_{p}^{s}\,u=\lambda f(x,u)-g(x,u)&\text{in $\Omega$}\\
u>0&\text{in $\Omega$}\\
u=0&\text{in $\Omega^{c}$,}\end{cases}

where
f,g:\Omega\times{\mathbb{R}}\to{\mathbb{R}}
are Carathéodory mappings, both
(p-1)
-superlinear at
\infty
and at
0
, satisfying a subcritical growth condition like
{\bf H}
, and jointly satisfying a pseudo-monotonicity condition (see
[
23
]
for the case of the
p
-Laplacian).

Conic divisorial ideals of toric rings and applications to Hibi rings and stable set rings
Koji Matsushita

Abstract
. In the present paper, we study conic divisorial ideals of toric rings. We provide an idea to determine them and we give a description of the conic divisorial ideals of Hibi rings and stable set rings of perfect graphs by using this idea. We also characterize when Hibi rings or stable set rings are quasi-symmetric or weakly-symmetric. Moreover, by using the description of the conic divisorial ideals, we construct a non-commutative crepant resolution (NCCR) of a special family of stable set rings.

1.
Introduction

Throughout the present paper, let
\Bbbk
be an algebraically closed field of characteristic
0
, for simplicity.

1.1.
Backgrounds

Toric rings arising from rational polyhedral cones, which are normal affine semigroup rings (and hence Cohen-Macaulay rings), are of particular interest in the area of combinatorial commutative algebra.
Especially, the Ehrhart ring of a lattice polytope is a typical example of toric rings and its commutative ring-theoretical properties have been well investigated.
Recently, conic divisorial ideals, which are a certain class of divisorial ideals (rank one reflexive modules) defined on toric rings, and their applications are well studied (see e.g.,
[
4
,
5
]
, and so on).
Let
R=\Bbbk[C\cap\mathbb{Z}^{d}]
be the toric ring of a strongly convex rational polyhedral cone
C\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
.
It is known that up to isomorphism the conic divisorial ideals of
R
are exactly the direct summands of
R^{1/k}
for
k\gg 0
(
[
5
, Proposition 3.6]
,
[
24
, Proposition 3.2.3]
), where
R^{1/k}=\Bbbk[C\cap(1/k\mathbb{Z})^{d}]
is regarded as an
R
-module.
Since
R^{1/k}
is a maximal Cohen-Macaulay (MCM, for short)
R
-module, conic divisorial ideals of
R
are also MCM
R
-modules.

Conic divisorial ideals play important roles in the theory of non-commutative algebraic geometry as well as commutative algebra.
In fact, the following motivational theorem is given:

Theorem 1.1

(
[
10
, Corollary 6.2]
,
[
25
, Proposition 1.8]
)
.

For
k\gg 0
,
\operatorname{End}_{R}(R^{1/k})
is a non-commutative resolution (NCR) of
R
.

This theorem implies that the endomorphism ring of the direct sum of all conic divisorial ideals of
R
is an NCR of
R
.

Moreover, the endomorphism ring of the direct sum of some conic modules of
R
may be a non-commutative crepant resolution (NCCR), which is a special class of NCRs and was introduced by Van den Bergh (
[
31
]
) in the context of non-commutative algebraic geometry.
In particular, NCCRs constructed in this way are called toric NCCRs, and it is known that the following toric rings have toric NCCRs:

•

Gorenstein toric rings whose class groups are
\mathbb{Z}
(
[
31
]
);

•

Gorenstein Hibi rings whose class groups are
\mathbb{Z}^{2}
(
[
21
]
);

•

3
-dimensional Gorenstein toric rings (
[
3
,
20
,
26
]
);

•

Segre products of polynomial rings (
[
19
]
);

•

Gorenstein edge rings of complete multipartite graphs (
[
17
]
).

However, there is an example of a toric ring which have no toric NCCRs (
[
25
, Example 9.1]
) while toric rings always have NCRs as mentioned in Theorem
1.1
.
In general, the existence of NCCRs for Gorenstein toric rings is still open.

Therefore, in the construction of NCCRs, it is natural and important to classify MCM divisorial ideals (including conic ones) of certain class of toric rings. Indeed, it has been investigated in some classes of toric rings.
For example, a classification of MCM divisorial ideals is given in the case of toric rings whose divisor class group are
\mathbb{Z}
or
\mathbb{Z}^{2}
(
[
28
,
30
]
).
Furthermore, a description of conic divisorial ideals is also given in the case of Hibi rings (
[
19
]
) and edge rings of complete multipartite graphs (
[
17
]
).

On the other hand, any toric ring whose divisor class group is isomorphic to
\mathbb{Z}^{r}
can be described as the ring of invariants under an action of the torus
T\cong(\Bbbk^{\times})^{r}
on the symmetric algebra
\operatorname{Sym}W
of a
T
-representation
W
(see e.g.,
[
4
, Theorem 2.1]
).
Suppose that
W
is generic and unimodular. Then, the following facts are known:

•

If
W
is quasi-symmetric, then the toric ring
\operatorname{Sym}(W)^{T}
has a toric NCCR (
[
25
, Theorem 1.19]
).

•

Suppose that
W
is weakly-symmetric. Then, a description of the MCM divisorial ideals of the toric ring
\operatorname{Sym}(W)^{T}
is given (
[
27
, Corollary 3.7]
).
Moreover, all toric NCCRs of
\operatorname{Sym}(W)^{T}
are derived equivalent (
[
27
, Theorem 1.3]
).

Quasi-symmetric (resp. weakly-symmetric) representations are defined by using its weights (see Definition
2.3
), and other properties associated with them are studied in
[
25
]
(resp.
[
27
]
).
Since we want to determine MCM divisorial ideals and construct NCCRs, we are also interested in when toric rings are quasi-symmetric or weakly-symmetric.

In the present paper, we mainly study conic divisorial ideals of toric rings.
In particular, we focus on two classes of toric rings, Hibi rings and stable set rings.

1.2.
Hibi rings

Hibi introduced a class of normal Cohen-Macaulay domains
\Bbbk[P]
arising from posets
P
(
[
14
]
),
which is a quotient of a polynomial ring.
On the other hand, in this paper, our description of toric ring
\Bbbk[P]
(see Section
2.2
) is based on the order polytope of
P
introduced by Stanley (
[
29
]
), and it seems different from the original one.
However, it is known that they are isomorphic.
Nowadays, the toric rings
\Bbbk[P]
are called Hibi rings of posets
P
.

The Hibi ring of a poset
P
is a typical example of an algebra with straightening laws domain on
P
and is of interest in the area of combinatorial commutative algebra. In fact, algebraic properties of Hibi rings have been well studied as mentioned in the previous subsection.
Moreover, the relationships between Hibi rings and other toric rings, such as stable set rings and edge rings, were investigated when their divisor class groups have small rank (
[
18
]
).

1.3.
Stable set rings

The terminology “stable set ring” is used in
[
16
]
, so we also employ it.
The stable set ring
\Bbbk[\operatorname{Stab}_{G}]
of a finite simple graph
G
is a
\Bbbk
-algebra defined from the stable set polytope
\operatorname{Stab}_{G}
, and stable set polytopes were introduced by Chvátal (
[
8
]
).
It is known that the stable set ring
\Bbbk[\operatorname{Stab}_{G}]
of a perfect graph
G
coincide with the Ehrhart ring of the stable set polytope, hence
\Bbbk[\operatorname{Stab}_{G}]
can be regarded as the toric ring arising from a rational polyhedral cone.
In addition, the facets of stable set polytopes are completely characterized in the case of perfect graphs (
[
8
]
).
Thus, stable set rings and stable set polytopes behave well for perfect graphs.
Moreover, stable set polytopes of perfect graphs include a remarkable class of another kind of polytopes arising from posets, which is called chain polytopes and were also introduced by Stanley (
[
29
]
).

Recently, algebraic properties of stable set rings have been well studied as well as Hibi rings.
For example, in
[
18
]
, divisor class groups of stable set rings of perfect graphs
G
are completely characterized in terms of
G
.
Furthermore, the perfect graphs
G
whose stable set rings have the class groups
\mathbb{Z}
or
\mathbb{Z}^{2}
are also characterized, and in this case, each stable set ring of
G
is isomorphic to a certain Hibi ring.

2.1.
Preliminaries on toric rings and conic divisorial ideals

In this paper, we mainly study two classes of toric rings, Hibi rings and stable set rings. Thus, we start this paper with introducing toric rings.

Let
\langle-,-\rangle
denote the natural inner product of
\mathbb{R}^{d}
and let
[n]=\{1,\ldots,n\}
for
n\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
.
We consider a
d
-dimensional strongly convex rational polyhedral cone

\tau=\mathrm{Cone}(v_{1},\cdots,v_{n})=\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}v_{1}+\cdots+\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}v_{n}

generated by
v_{1},\cdots,v_{n}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}
, where
d\leq n
.
We assume this system of generators is minimal and the generators are primitive, i.e.,
\epsilon v_{i}\notin\mathbb{Z}^{d}
for any
0<\epsilon<1
.
For each generator, we define a linear form
\sigma_{i}(-)=\langle-,v_{i}\rangle
and denote
\sigma(-)=(\sigma_{1}(-),\cdots,\sigma_{n}(-))
.
We also consider the dual cone
\tau^{\vee}
of
\tau
:

\tau^{\vee}=\{{\bf x}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}:\sigma_{i}({\bf x})\geq 0\text{ for all }i\in[n]\}.

We now define the toric ring

\displaystyle R=\Bbbk[\tau^{\vee}\cap\mathbb{Z}^{d}]=\Bbbk[t_{1}^{\alpha_{1}}\cdots t_{d}^{\alpha_{d}}:(\alpha_{1},\cdots,\alpha_{d})\in\tau^{\vee}\cap\mathbb{Z}^{d}].

(2.1)

Note that
R
is a
d
-dimensional Cohen-Macaulay normal domain.
In addition, for each
\mathbf{a}=(a_{1},\cdots,a_{n})\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, we set

\mathbb{T}(\mathbf{a})=\{{\bf x}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}:\sigma_{i}({\bf x})\geq a_{i}\text{ for all }i\in[n]\}.

Then, we define the module
T(\mathbf{a})
generated by all monomials whose exponent vector is in
\mathbb{T}(\mathbf{a})
.
By the definition, we have
\mathbb{T}(0)=\tau^{\vee}\cap\mathbb{Z}^{d}
and
T(0)=R
.
Moreover, we note some facts associated with the module
T(\mathbf{a})
(see e.g.,
[
6
, Section 4.F]
):

•

Since
\sigma_{i}(\mathbf{x})\in\mathbb{Z}
for any
i\in[n]
and any
\mathbf{x}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}
, we can see that
T(\mathbf{a})=T(\ulcorner\mathbf{a}\urcorner)
, where
\ulcorner\;\urcorner
means the round up
and
\ulcorner\mathbf{a}\urcorner=(\ulcorner a_{1}\urcorner,\cdots,\ulcorner a_{n}\urcorner)
.

•

The module
T(\mathbf{a})
is a divisorial ideal and any divisorial ideal of
R
takes this form. Therefore, we can identify each
\mathbf{a}\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}
with the divisorial ideal
T(\mathbf{a})
.

•

It is known that the isomorphic classes of divisorial ideals of
R
one-to-one correspond to the elements of the divisor class group
\operatorname{Cl}(R)
of
R
.
We see that for
\mathbf{a},\mathbf{a}^{\prime}\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}
,
T(\mathbf{a})\cong T(\mathbf{a}^{\prime})
if and only if there exists
{\bf y}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}
such that
a_{i}=a_{i}^{\prime}+\sigma_{i}({\bf y})
for all
i\in[n]
.
Thus, we have
\operatorname{Cl}(R)\cong\mathbb{Z}^{n}/\sigma(\mathbb{Z}^{d})
.

Let
\mathfrak{p}_{i}=T(\mathbf{e}_{i})
, where
\mathbf{e}_{i}
is the
i
-th basic vector in
\mathbb{Z}^{n}
, and consider the prime divisor
\mathcal{D}_{i}=\mathrm{V}(\mathfrak{p}_{i})=\operatorname{Spec}R/\mathfrak{p}_{i}
on
\operatorname{Spec}R
.
Then, the divisorial ideal
T(\mathbf{a})=T(a_{1},\cdots,a_{n})
corresponds to the Weil divisor
-(a_{1}\mathcal{D}_{1}+\cdots+a_{n}\mathcal{D}_{n})
.
By the fact that
\operatorname{Cl}(R)\cong\mathbb{Z}^{n}/\sigma(\mathbb{Z}^{d})
, we obtain that

\sigma_{1}(\mathbf{e}_{j})\mathcal{D}_{1}+\cdots+\sigma_{n}(\mathbf{e}_{j})\mathcal{D}_{n}=v_{1}^{(j)}\mathcal{D}_{1}+\cdots+v_{n}^{(j)}\mathcal{D}_{n}=0

(2.2)

in
\operatorname{Cl}(R)
for all
j\in[d]
, where for a vector
v\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
v^{(j)}
denotes the
j
-th coordinate of
v
.

We are interested in a divisorial ideal called
conic
.

Definition 2.1

(see e.g.,
[
5
, Section 3]
)
.

We say that a divisorial ideal
T(\mathbf{a})
is
conic
if there exists
\mathbf{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
such that
\mathbf{a}=\ulcorner\sigma(\mathbf{x})\urcorner
.

By the observation mentioned above, we see that all isomorphic classes of conic divisorial ideals are contained in
\ulcorner\sigma((-1,0]^{d})\urcorner/\sigma(\mathbb{Z}^{d})
.

In what follows, we assume that
\operatorname{Cl}(R)\cong\mathbb{Z}^{r}
.
Then, we can rewrite
R
as the ring of invariants under the action of
G=\operatorname{Hom}(\operatorname{Cl}(R),\Bbbk^{\times})\cong(\Bbbk^{\times})^{r}
on
S=\Bbbk[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}]
.
Let
X(G)
be the character group of
G
.
We can see that
X(G)\cong\operatorname{Cl}(R)
, and hence we can use the same symbol for both of a character and the corresponding weight.
When we consider the prime divisor
\mathcal{D}_{i}
on
\operatorname{Spec}R
as the element in
X(G)\cong\mathbb{Z}^{r}
via the surjection in (
2.2
), we denote it by
\beta_{i}
.
For a character
\chi\in X(G)
, we also denote by
V_{\chi}
the irreducible
representation corresponding to
\chi
, and we let
W=\bigoplus_{i=1}^{n}V_{\beta_{i}}
.
Then, the symmetric algebra
\operatorname{Sym}W
of the
G
-representation
W
is isomorphic to
S
, and the algebraic torus
G
acts on
S
, which is the action induced by
g\cdot x_{i}=\beta_{i}(g)x_{i}
for
g\in G
. This action gives the
\operatorname{Cl}(R)
-grading on
S
, and the degree zero part coincides with the
G
-invariant components.
In particular, we have
R=S^{G}
(see e.g.,
[
5
, Theorem 2.1]
).
In addition, for a character
\chi
, we define
M_{\chi}=(S\otimes_{\Bbbk}V_{\chi})^{G}
.
This is an
R
-module called the
module of covariants
associated to
V_{\chi}
and is generated by
f\in S
with
g\cdot f=\chi(g)f
for any
g\in G
.
Note that for
\chi=\sum_{i}a_{i}\beta_{i}\in X(G)
, we have
T(a_{1},\ldots,a_{n})=M_{-\chi}
.
Moreover, by the arguments in
[
25
, Section 10.6]
, we can describe the conic divisorial ideals of
R
by using the weights
\beta_{1},\ldots,\beta_{n}
as follows:

Proposition 2.2
.

Let
\chi\in X(G)
. Then,
M_{-\chi}
is conic if and only if
\chi
is a strongly critical character with respect to
\beta_{1},\ldots,\beta_{n}
, that is, one can write
-\chi=\sum_{i}a_{i}\beta_{i}
with
a_{i}\in[0,1)
for all
i\in[n]
.

We introduce notions of quasi-symmetric and weakly-symmetric:

Definition 2.3

(
[
27
, Definition 2.2]
)
.

A
G
-representation
W
is
quasi-symmetric
if for every line
l\subset X(G)_{\mathbb{R}}=X(G)\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathbb{R}
passing through the origin, we have
\sum_{\beta_{i}\in l}\beta_{i}=0
. It is
weakly-symmetric
if for every line
l
, the cone spanned by
\beta_{i}\in l
is either zero or
l
. We say that a toric ring
R
is quasi-symmetric (resp. weakly-symmetric) if
R\cong S^{G}
with
S=\operatorname{Sym}W
and
W
is a quasi-symmetric (resp. weakly-symmetric) representation.

Note that quasi-symmetric representations are weakly-symmetric. If
W
is quasi-symmetric, then the top exterior
W
is the trivial representation, and hence
R=S^{G}
is Gorenstein.

2.2.
Preliminaries on Hibi rings

In this subsection, we recall order polytopes and Hibi rings of posets.

Let
P=\{p_{1},\cdots,p_{d-1}\}
be a finite partially ordered set (poset, for short) equipped with a partial order
\preceq
.
For a subset
I\subset P
, we say that
I
is a
poset ideal
of
P
if
p\in I
and
q\preceq p
then
q\in I
.
For a subset
A\subset P
, we call
A
an
antichain
of
P
if
p\not\preceq q
and
q\not\preceq p
for any
p,q\in A
with
p\neq q
.
Note that
\emptyset
is regarded as a poset ideal and an antichain.
Let

\displaystyle\mathcal{O}_{P}=\{(x_{1},\cdots,x_{d-1})\in\mathbb{R}^{d-1}:x_{i}\geq x_{j}\text{ if }p_{i}\preceq p_{j}\text{ in }P,\;0\leq x_{i}\leq 1\text{ for }i\in[d-1]\}.

This convex polytope
\mathcal{O}_{P}
is called the
order polytope
of a poset
P
.
According to
[
29
]
, it is known that the elements of
\mathcal{O}_{P}\cap\mathbb{Z}^{d-1}
are precisely the vertices of
\mathcal{O}_{P}
and are
(0,1)
-vectors, that is,
\mathcal{O}_{P}
is a
(0,1)
-polytope.
Furthermore, the vertices one-to-one correspond to the poset ideals of
P
.
In fact, a
(0,1)
-vector
(a_{1},\ldots,a_{d-1})
is a vertex of
\mathcal{O}_{P}
if and only if
\{p_{i}\in P:a_{i}=1\}
is a poset ideal.

For a poset
P
, let
\Bbbk[P]
denote a toric ring defined by setting

\Bbbk[P]=\Bbbk[{\bf t}^{\alpha}t_{0}:\alpha\in\mathcal{O}_{P}\cap\mathbb{Z}^{d-1}]=\Bbbk[\;(\prod_{p_{i}\in I}t_{i})t_{0}:\text{$I$ is a poset ideal of $P$}],

where
{\bf t}^{\alpha}=t_{1}^{\alpha_{1}}\cdots t_{d-1}^{\alpha_{d-1}}
for
\alpha=(\alpha_{1},\cdots,\alpha_{d-1})\in\mathbb{Z}^{d-1}
.
This
\Bbbk
-algebra is called the
Hibi ring
of
P
.
The following properties associated with order polytopes and Hibi rings of posets
P
are known:

•

\mathcal{O}_{P}
has the integer decomposition property (IDP, for short), that is, for any
n\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
and any
\alpha\in n\mathcal{O}_{P}\cap\mathbb{Z}^{d-1}
, there exist
\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{n}\in\mathcal{O}_{P}\cap\mathbb{Z}^{d-1}
such that
\alpha=\alpha_{1}+\cdots+\alpha_{n}
, and hence
\Bbbk[P]
coincides with the Ehrhart ring of
\mathcal{O}_{P}
(see e.g.,
[
32
, Section 10.4]
for Ehrhart rings).

•

\Bbbk[P]
is Gorenstein if and only if
P
is pure (
[
14
]
),
where we say that
P
is
pure
if all of the maximal chains
p_{i_{1}}\prec\cdots\prec p_{i_{\ell}}
have the same length.

The Hibi ring of a poset can be described as the toric ring arising from a rational polyhedral cone as follows.
Let
P=\{p_{1},\cdots,p_{d-1}\}
. For
p_{i},p_{j}\in P
with
p_{j}\prec p_{i}
, we say that
p_{i}

covers

p_{j}

if there is
p\in P
with
p_{j}\preceq p\preceq p_{i}
then
p=p_{j}
or
p=p_{i}
.
Thus, the edge
\{p_{i},p_{j}\}
of the Hasse diagram
\mathcal{H}(P)
of
P
if and only if
p_{i}
covers
p_{j}
or
p_{j}
covers
p_{i}
.
Set
\widehat{P}=P\cup\{\hat{0},\hat{1}\}
,
where
\hat{0}
(resp.
\hat{1}
) is the unique minimal (resp. maximal) element not belonging to
P
.
Let us denote
p_{0}=\hat{0}
and
p_{d}=\hat{1}
.
For each edge
e=\{p_{i},p_{j}\}
of
\mathcal{H}(\widehat{P})
with
p_{i}\prec p_{j}
,
let
\sigma_{e}
be a linear form in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
defined by

\displaystyle\sigma_{e}({\bf x})=\begin{cases}x_{i}-x_{j}\;&\text{ if }j\not=d,\\
x_{i}\;&\text{ if }j=d\end{cases}

for
{\bf x}=(x_{0},x_{1},\cdots,x_{d-1})
. Let
\tau_{P}=\mathrm{Cone}(\sigma_{e}:e\text{ is an edge of }\mathcal{H}(\widehat{P}))\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
.
Then, we can see that
\Bbbk[P]=\Bbbk[\tau_{P}^{\vee}\cap\mathbb{Z}^{d}]
.
Let
e_{1},\cdots,e_{n}
be all the edges of
\mathcal{H}(\widehat{P})
. We set a linear form
\sigma_{P}:\mathbb{R}^{d}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{n}
by

\sigma_{P}({\bf x})=(\sigma_{e_{1}}({\bf x}),\cdots,\sigma_{e_{n}}({\bf x}))\in\mathbb{R}^{n}

for
{\bf x}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
.

Let
P
and
Q
be two posets with
P\cap Q=\emptyset
.
The
disjoint union
of
P
and
Q
is the poset
P+Q
on
P\cup Q
such that
x\preceq y
in
P+Q
if (a)
x,y\in P
and
x\preceq y
in
P
, or (b)
x,y\in Q
and
x\preceq y
in
Q
.
The
ordinal sum
of
P
and
Q
is the poset
P\oplus Q
on
P\cup Q\cup\{z\}
such that
x\preceq y
in
P\oplus Q
if
(a)
x,y\in P
and
x\preceq y
in
P
, (b)
x,y\in Q
and
x\preceq y
in
Q
, (c)
x\in P
and
y=z
, or (d)
x=z
and
y\in Q
,
where
z
is a new element which is not contained in
P\cup Q
.
Note that the equality
P\oplus Q=Q\oplus P
does not hold in general while the equality
P+Q=Q+P
holds.
By observing poset ideals of
P+Q
and
P\oplus Q
, the following proposition holds:

Proposition 2.4
.

Let
P
and
Q
be two posets with
P\cap Q=\emptyset
.

(i)

\Bbbk[P+Q]
is isomorphic to the Segre product of
\Bbbk[P]
and
\Bbbk[Q]
.

(ii)

\Bbbk[P\oplus Q]\cong\Bbbk[Q\oplus P]\cong\Bbbk[P]\otimes_{\Bbbk}\Bbbk[Q]
. Moreover,
\Bbbk[P\oplus Q]
is quasi-symmetric (resp. weakly-symmetric) if and only if
\Bbbk[P]
and
\Bbbk[Q]
are quasi-symmetric (resp. weakly-symmetric).

Here, the
Segre product
of two standard algebras
R=\bigoplus_{n\geq 0}R_{n}
and
S=\bigoplus_{n\geq 0}S_{n}
over
\Bbbk
is the graded subalgebra
\bigoplus_{n\geq 0}(R_{n}\otimes_{\Bbbk}S_{n})
of
R\otimes_{\Bbbk}S
.

We also recall another polytope arising from
P
, which is defined as follows:

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}_{P}=\{(x_{1},\ldots,x_{d})\in\mathbb{R}^{d}:\;

\displaystyle x_{i}\geq 0\text{ for }i=1,\ldots,d,

\displaystyle x_{i_{1}}+\cdots+x_{i_{k}}\leq 1\text{ for }p_{i_{1}}\prec\cdots\prec p_{i_{k}}\text{ in }P\}.

A convex polytope
\mathcal{C}_{P}
is called the
chain polytope
of
P
.
Similarly to order polytopes, it is known that
\mathcal{C}_{P}
is a
(0,1)
-polytope and the vertices of
\mathcal{C}_{P}
one-to-one correspond to the antichains of
P
(
[
29
]
).

Theorem 2.5

(
[
15
, Theorem 2.1]
)
.

Let
P
be a poset. Then
\mathcal{O}_{P}
and
\mathcal{C}_{P}
are unimodularly equivalent (i.e., there are a vector
v\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}
and a unimodular transformation
f\in\operatorname{GL}_{d}(\mathbb{Z})
such that
\mathcal{C}_{P}=f(\mathcal{O}_{P})+v
) if and only if
P
does not contain the X-shape subposet.

Here, the
X-shape
poset is a poset
\{p_{1},p_{2},p_{3},p_{4},p_{5}\}
equipped with the partial orders
p_{1}\prec p_{3}\prec p_{4}
and
p_{2}\prec p_{3}\prec p_{5}
.

2.3.
Preliminaries on stable set rings

In this subsection, we recall stable set polytopes and stable set rings of graphs.
For the fundamental materials on graph theory, consult, e.g.,
[
9
]
.

In the present paper, we only treat finite simple graphs, so we simply call graphs instead of finite simple graphs.
For a graph
G
, let
V(G)=\{1,\ldots,d\}
denote the vertex set of
G
and let
E(G)
denote the edge set of
G
.
We say that
S\subset V(G)
is a
stable set
or an
independent set
(resp. a
clique
)
if
\{v,w\}\not\in E(G)
(resp.
\{v,w\}\in E(G)
) for any distinct vertices
v,w\in S
.
Note that the empty set and each singleton are regarded as stable sets.

Given a subset
W\subset V(G)
, let
\rho(W)=\sum_{i\in W}\mathbf{e}_{i}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, where
we let
\rho(\emptyset)
stands for the origin of
\mathbb{R}^{d}
.
We define a lattice polytope associated to a graph
G
as follows:

\displaystyle\operatorname{Stab}_{G}=\operatorname{conv}(\{\rho(S):S\text{ is a stable set}\}).

We call
\operatorname{Stab}_{G}
the
stable set polytope
of
G
.
In addition, let
\Bbbk[\operatorname{Stab}_{G}]
denote a
\Bbbk
-algebra defined by setting

\Bbbk[\operatorname{Stab}_{G}]=\Bbbk[{\bf t}^{\alpha}t_{0}:\alpha\in\operatorname{Stab}_{G}\cap\mathbb{Z}^{d}]=\Bbbk[\;(\prod_{i\in S}t_{i})t_{0}:\text{$S$ is a stable set of $G$}],

which is called the
stable set ring
of
G
.

In what follows, we treat the stable set rings of
perfect graphs
since the following properties hold:

•

\operatorname{Stab}_{G}
is compressed if and only if
G
is perfect (
[
22
,
12
]
).
This implies that
\Bbbk[\operatorname{Stab}_{G}]
is normal if
G
is perfect.
Moreover,
\operatorname{Stab}_{G}
has IDP if
G
is perfect.
Therefore,
\Bbbk[\operatorname{Stab}_{G}]
coincides with the Ehrhart ring of
\operatorname{Stab}_{G}
.

•

\Bbbk[\operatorname{Stab}_{G}]
is Gorenstein if and only if
G
all maximal cliques of
G
have the same cardinality (
[
23
, Theorem 2.1]
).

•

The facets of
\operatorname{Stab}_{G}
are completely characterized when
G
is perfect (
[
8
, Theorem 3.1]
).
From its description, we have

\displaystyle\operatorname{Stab}_{G}=\{(x_{1},\cdots,x_{d})\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\mid\,

\displaystyle x_{i}\geq 0\text{ for }i\in[d],\;

\displaystyle\,1-\sum_{i\in Q}x_{i}\geq 0\text{ for each maximal clique $Q$ of $G$}\}.

From those properties, the stable set ring of a perfect graph can be described as the toric ring arising from a rational polyhedral cone as well as Hibi rings.
For a perfect graph
G
with maximal cliques
Q_{0},Q_{1},\ldots,Q_{n}
and
i\in\{0,1,\ldots,n+d\}
,
let
\sigma_{i}
be a linear form in
\mathbb{R}^{d+1}
defined by

\displaystyle\sigma_{i}({\bf x})=\begin{cases}x_{0}-\sum_{j\in Q_{i}}x_{j}\;&\text{ if }i\in\{0,1,\ldots,n\},\\
x_{i-n}\;&\text{ if }i\in\{n+1,\ldots,n+d\}\end{cases}

for
{\bf x}=(x_{0},x_{1},\cdots,x_{d})
. Let
\tau_{G}=\mathrm{Cone}(\sigma_{i}:i\in\{0,1,\ldots,n+d\})\subset\mathbb{R}^{d+1}
.
Then, we can see that
\Bbbk[\operatorname{Stab}_{G}]=\Bbbk[\tau_{G}^{\vee}\cap\mathbb{Z}^{d}]
.
We set a linear form
\sigma_{G}:\mathbb{R}^{d+1}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{n+d+1}
by

\sigma_{G}({\bf x})=(\sigma_{0}({\bf x}),\cdots,\sigma_{n+d}({\bf x}))\in\mathbb{R}^{n+d+1}

for
{\bf x}\in\mathbb{R}^{d+1}
.

Given a poset
P
, we define the
comparability graph
of
P
, denoted by
G(P)
,
as a graph on the vertex set
V(G(P))=[d]
with the edge set

E(G(P))=\{\{i,j\}:\text{$p_{i}$ and $p_{j}$ are comparable in $P$}\}.

It is known that
G(P)
is perfect for any
P
(see e.g.
[
9
, Section 5.5]
) and the cliques of
G(P)
one-to-one correspond to the chains of
P
.
Moreover, we see that
\mathcal{C}_{P}=\operatorname{Stab}_{G(P)}
and if
P
does not contain the X-shape, then the stable set ring

\Bbbk[\mathcal{C}_{P}]=\Bbbk[\operatorname{Stab}_{G(P)}]
is isomorphic to
\Bbbk[P]
by Theorem
2.5
.

2.4.
Preliminaries on graph theory

At the end of this section, we prepare some more notions and notation on (directed) graphs.
We refer the reader to e.g.,
[
9
,
11
]
for the introduction to graph theory.

Let
G
be a graph.
For a subset
W\subset V(G)
, let
G_{W}
denote the induced subgraph with respect to
W
.
For a vertex
v
, we denote by
G\setminus v
instead of
G_{V(G)\setminus\{v\}}
.
Similarly, for
S\subset V(G)
, we denote by
G\setminus S
instead of
G_{V(G)\setminus S}
.
We sometimes denote a cycle
C
of
G
by
(v_{1},\ldots,v_{m})
(i.e.,
V(C)=\{v_{1},\ldots,v_{m}\}\subset V(G)
,
v_{i}\neq v_{j}
for
i,j\in[m]
and
E(C)=\{\{v_{i},v_{i+1}\}\in E(G):i\in[m]\}
, where
v_{m+1}=v_{1}
).
We say that a cycle
C=(v_{1},\ldots,v_{m})
of
G
is a
circuit
if
C
has no chords, that is,
E(G_{V(C)})=E(C)
.
We also say that a graph is
chordal
if each of its cycles of length at least
4
has a chord. If
G
is a graph with induced subgraphs
G_{1},G_{2}
and
S
, such that
V(G)=V(G_{1})\cup V(G_{2})
and
V(S)=V(G_{1})\cap V(G_{2})
, we say that
G
arises from
G_{1}
and
G_{2}
by
pasting
these graphs together along
S
.
A characterization of chordal graphs is known as follows:

Proposition 2.6

(cf.
[
9
, Proposition 5.5.1]
)
.

A graph is chordal if and only if it can be constructed recursively by pasting along complete subgraphs, starting from complete graphs.

Moreover, it is known that every chordal graph is perfect (cf.
[
9
, Proposition 5.5.2]
).

For a connected graph
G
, a subgraph
T
of
G
is called a
spanning tree
if
T
is a connected graph with
V(T)=V(G)
and contains no cycles.
For each
e\in E(G)\setminus E(T)
, there is a unique cycle
C_{e}
in
T+e
, where
T+e
is the subgraph of
G
on the vertex set
V(T)
with the edge set
E(T)\cup\{e\}
. We call
C_{e}
the
fundamental cycle
of
e
with respect to
T
.

The
flow space
of a directed graph
A
is the subspace of
\mathbb{R}^{E(A)}
generated by the vectors
x\in\mathbb{R}^{E(A)}
such that
D_{A}x=0
, where
D_{A}
is the
incidence matrix
of
A
, which is the
\{0,\pm 1\}
-matrix with rows and columns indexed by the vertices and edges of
A
, respectively, such that the
ve
-entry of
D_{A}
is equal to
1
if the vertex
v
is the head of the edge
e
,
-1
if
v
is the tail of
e
, and
0
otherwise.
Let
C=(v_{1},\ldots,v_{m})
be a cycle in
A
.
Using the orientation of
A
, the cycle
C
determines an element
\mathbf{v}(C)\in\mathbb{R}^{E(A)}
as follows:

\displaystyle\mathbf{v}(C)^{(e)}=\begin{cases}0\;&\text{ if $e\notin E(C)$},\\
1\;&\text{ if $e=\{v_{i},v_{i+1}\}$ and $v_{i+1}$ is the head of $e$},\\
-1\;&\text{ if $e=\{v_{i},v_{i+1}\}$ and $v_{i+1}$ is the tail of $e$}.\end{cases}

We refer to
\mathbf{v}(C)
as the
signed characteristic vector
of
C
.
It is known that the signed characteristic vectors of the fundamental cycles with respect to a spanning tree of
A
form bases of the flow space of
A
. For a cycle
C
in
A
, we set

\displaystyle\operatorname{supp}^{+}(C)=\{e\in E(C):\mathbf{v}(C)^{(e)}>0\}\quad\text{ and }\quad\operatorname{supp}^{-}(C)=\{e\in E(C):\mathbf{v}(C)^{(e)}<0\}.

3.
Conic divisorial ideals of toric rings

In this section, we discuss conic divisorial ideals of toric rings whose divisor class group is a free abelian group and give an idea to describe them.
In particular, we determine conic divisorial ideals of Hibi rings and stable set rings by using the idea.

3.1.
Description of conic divisorial ideals of toric rings

Throughout this subsection, let
R
be the toric ring defined in (
2.1
) and we assume that the divisor class group of
R
is isomorphic to
\mathbb{Z}^{r}
.
Moreover, let
\beta_{1},\ldots,\beta_{n}
be the weights of
R
.
Furthermore, let
\bar{\beta}_{1},\ldots,\bar{\beta}_{n^{\prime}}
be weights of
R
such that
n^{\prime}
is the minimal number with
\{\bar{\beta}_{1},\ldots,\bar{\beta}_{n^{\prime}}\}=\{\beta_{1},\ldots,\beta_{n}\}
, and let
m_{i}
be the multiplicity of
\bar{\beta}_{i}
for
i\in[n^{\prime}]
, that is,
m_{i}=|\{j\in[n]:\beta_{j}=\bar{\beta}_{i}\}|
.
By Proposition
2.2
, each element of
\mathcal{W}(R)\cap\mathbb{Z}^{r}
one-to-one corresponds to a conic divisorial ideal of
R
, where

\displaystyle\mathcal{W}(R)=\Big\{\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_{i}\beta_{i}\in\mathbb{R}^{r}:a_{i}\in[0,1)\Big\}=\Big\{\sum_{i=1}^{n^{\prime}}\bar{a}_{i}\bar{\beta}_{i}\in\mathbb{R}^{r}:\bar{a}_{i}\in[0,m_{i})\Big\}.

On the other hand, we define

\mathcal{W}^{\prime}(R)=\Big\{\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_{i}\beta_{i}\in\mathbb{R}^{r}:a_{i}\in[0,1]\Big\}=\Big\{\sum_{i=1}^{n^{\prime}}\bar{a}_{i}\bar{\beta}_{i}\in\mathbb{R}^{r}:\bar{a}_{i}\in[0,m_{i}]\Big\}.

Note that
\mathcal{W}^{\prime}(R)
is a lattice polytope since it is the Minkowski sum of lattice segments
\{\bar{a}_{i}\bar{\beta}_{i}:\bar{a}_{i}\in[0,m_{i}]\}
.
According to oriented matroid theory, we can determine the faces of
\mathcal{W}^{\prime}(R)
as follows.
We define the sign function
\operatorname{sign}:\mathbb{R}\to\{+,-,0\}
by setting

\displaystyle\operatorname{sign}(x)=\begin{cases}+\;&\text{ if }x>0,\\
0\;&\text{ if }x=0,\\
-\;&\text{ if }x<0,\end{cases}

and define partial order on
\{+,-,0\}
by setting
0\prec+
and
0\prec-
, while
+
and
-
are incomparable.
We consider the subset of
\{+,-,0\}^{n^{\prime}}
:

S=\{(\operatorname{sign}(\langle\bar{\beta}_{1},\mathbf{n}\rangle),\ldots,\operatorname{sign}(\langle\bar{\beta}_{n^{\prime}},\mathbf{n}\rangle)):\mathbf{n}\in\mathbb{R}^{n^{\prime}}\setminus\{0\}\}.

Note that
S
can be regarded as a poset by using componentwise partial ordering: for
s,s^{\prime}\in S
,
s\preceq s^{\prime}
if and only if
s^{(i)}\preceq s^{\prime(i)}
for all
i\in[n^{\prime}]
.
By
[
1
, Proposition 2.2.2]
, there is an order-reversing bijection between
S
and the set of faces of
\mathcal{W}^{\prime}(R)
(except for the empty set and
\mathcal{W}^{\prime}(R)
itself), partially ordered by inclusion.
In particular, by considering the correspondence between the facets of
\mathcal{W}^{\prime}(R)
and the minimal elements of
S
, the following lemma holds:

Lemma 3.1
.

If there exist
\mathbf{n}\in\mathbb{Z}^{r}\setminus\{0\}
and
\bar{\beta}_{i_{1}},\ldots,\bar{\beta}_{i_{r-1}}
such that
\bar{\beta}_{i_{1}},\ldots,\bar{\beta}_{i_{r-1}}
are linearly independent and
\langle\mathbf{n},\bar{\beta}_{i_{j}}\rangle=0
for all
j\in[r-1]
, then

F=\Big\{\sum_{\langle\mathbf{n},\beta_{i}\rangle>0}\beta_{i}+\sum_{\langle\mathbf{n},\beta_{i}\rangle=0}a_{i}\beta_{i}\in\mathbb{R}^{r}:a_{i}\in[0,1]\Big\}

is a facet of
\mathcal{W}^{\prime}(R)
. Conversely, all facets of
\mathcal{W}^{\prime}(R)
are obtained in this way.

Our goal is to determine the facet defining inequalities of a convex polytope representing conic classes. Let
m\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
and let
p_{i},q_{i}\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
for
i\in[m]
.
Moreover, for
i\in[m]
and
j\in[r]
, let
c_{ij}
be an integer such that the greatest common divisor of
c_{i1},\ldots,c_{ir}
is equal to
1
for all
i\in[m]
.
We define two convex polytopes:

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}

\displaystyle=\{(z_{1},\ldots,z_{r})\in\mathbb{R}^{r}:-q_{i}\leq\sum_{j=1}^{r}c_{ij}z_{j}\leq p_{i}\text{ for all }i\in[m]\}\text{ and }

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}^{\prime}

\displaystyle=\{(z_{1},\ldots,z_{r})\in\mathbb{R}^{r}:-q_{i}-1\leq\sum_{j=1}^{r}c_{ij}z_{j}\leq p_{i}+1\text{ for all }i\in[m]\}.

Note that if
\mathcal{C}^{\prime}
is a lattice polytope, then we have
\operatorname{int}(\mathcal{C}^{\prime})\cap\mathbb{Z}^{r}=\mathcal{C}\cap\mathbb{Z}^{r}
, where
\operatorname{int}(\mathcal{C}^{\prime})
denotes the relative interior of
\mathcal{C}^{\prime}
.

The following lemma is useful for describing conic divisorial ideals of toric rings.

Lemma 3.2
.

(i)

If
\mathcal{W}^{\prime}(R)=\mathcal{C}^{\prime}
, then one has
\mathcal{W}(R)\cap\mathbb{Z}^{r}=\mathcal{C}\cap\mathbb{Z}^{r}
.

(ii)

Suppose that
\mathcal{W}^{\prime}(R)\subset\mathcal{C}^{\prime}
. If all vertices of
\mathcal{C}^{\prime}
are in
\mathcal{W}^{\prime}(R)
, then
\mathcal{W}^{\prime}(R)=\mathcal{C}^{\prime}
.

Proof.

(i) We show that
\operatorname{int}(\mathcal{W}^{\prime}(R))=\mathcal{W}(R)
.
This implies

\displaystyle\mathcal{W}(R)\cap\mathbb{Z}^{r}=\operatorname{int}(\mathcal{W}^{\prime}(R))\cap\mathbb{Z}^{r}=\operatorname{int}(\mathcal{C}^{\prime})\cap\mathbb{Z}^{r}=\mathcal{C}\cap\mathbb{Z}^{r}.

(3.1)

Note that
\dim\mathcal{W}^{\prime}(R)=\dim\mathcal{C}^{\prime}=r
.
For any
\beta\in\operatorname{int}(\mathcal{W}^{\prime}(R))
, there exists
k>1
such that
k\beta\in\mathcal{W}^{\prime}(R)
. Thus, we have
\beta\in\mathcal{W}(R)
and hence
\operatorname{int}(\mathcal{W}^{\prime}(R))\subset\mathcal{W}(R)
.

To prove the reverse inclusion, we need only show that if
\beta\in\mathcal{W}^{\prime}(R)
is in the boundary
\partial\mathcal{W}^{\prime}(R)
of
\mathcal{W}^{\prime}(R)
, then
\beta\notin\mathcal{W}(R)
.
Let
\mathcal{S}=\sigma(\tau^{\vee}\cap\mathbb{Z}^{d})
and let
\pi_{i}:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}
be the
i
-th projection for each
i\in[n]
.
Note that the group of differences
\langle\mathcal{S}\rangle
of
\mathcal{S}
coincide with
\sigma(\mathbb{Z}^{d})
and
\pi_{i}(s)=\sigma_{i}(\alpha)
for
s=\sigma(\alpha)\in\mathcal{S}
.
Since
\pi_{i}(\langle\mathcal{S}\rangle)=\sigma_{i}(\mathbb{Z}^{d})=\mathbb{Z}
for all
i\in[n]
and for all
i,j\in[n]
with
i\neq j
, there exists
s=\sigma(\alpha)\in\sigma(\tau^{\vee}\cap\mathbb{Z}^{d})
such that
\pi_{j}(s)=\sigma_{j}(\alpha)>0=\sigma_{i}(\alpha)=\pi_{i}(s)
, the set
T_{i}=\{\mathbf{e}_{j}+\langle\mathcal{S}\rangle:i\neq j\in[n]\}
generates
\mathbb{Z}^{n}/\langle\mathcal{S}\rangle
as a semigroup for every
i\in[n]
(
[
7
, Theorem 2]
).
This implies

\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}\beta_{1}+\cdots+\widehat{\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}\beta_{i}}+\cdots+\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}\beta_{n}=\mathbb{Z}^{r}

for all
i\in[n]
, where
\widehat{\;}
indicates an element to be omitted.
Therefore, for any
\mathbf{n}\in\mathbb{Z}^{r}
, there exists
j\in[n]
such that
\langle\mathbf{n},\beta_{j}\rangle>0
.
Since
\beta\in\partial\mathcal{W}^{\prime}(R)
, there is a facet
F
of
\mathcal{W}^{\prime}(R)
with
\beta\in F
.
From Lemma
3.1
,
\beta_{j}
with
\langle\mathbf{n}_{F},\beta_{j}\rangle>0
must appear as a summand in
\beta=\sum_{i\in[n]}a_{i}\beta_{i}
, where
\mathbf{n}_{F}\in\mathbb{Z}^{r}
is an outer normal vector of the supporting hyperplane defining
F
.
Thus, we have
\beta\notin\mathcal{W}(R)
.

(ii) Let
v_{1},\ldots,v_{s}
be the vertices of
\mathcal{C}^{\prime}
.
Since
v_{k}\in\mathcal{W}^{\prime}(R)
for each
k\in[s]
, we can write
v_{k}=\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_{ki}\beta_{i}
for some
a_{ki}\in[0,1]
.
On the other hand, for any
z\in\mathcal{C}^{\prime}
, we can also write
z=\sum_{k=1}^{s}t_{k}v_{k}
with
t_{k}\in[0,1]
and
\sum_{k=1}^{s}t_{k}=1
.
Thus,
z=\sum_{k=1}^{s}t_{k}(\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_{ki}\beta_{i})=\sum_{i=1}^{n}(\sum_{k=1}^{s}t_{k}a_{ki})\beta_{i}
.
Since
\sum_{k=1}^{s}t_{k}a_{ki}\in[0,1]
for all
i\in[n]
, we have
z\in\mathcal{W}^{\prime}(R)
, and hence
\mathcal{W}^{\prime}(R)=\mathcal{C}^{\prime}
.
∎

3.2.
Conic divisorial ideals of Hibi rings

In this subsection, we consider conic divisorial ideals of Hibi rings.

Let
P
be a poset such that the Hasse diagram
\mathcal{H}(\widehat{P})
of
\widehat{P}
has
d+1
vertices and

For
p\in\widehat{P}\setminus\{\hat{1}\}
, let

.
Similarly, for

, let

.

By definition of
\sigma_{P}(-)
and (

), we see that
the prime divisors
\mathcal{D}_{e}
indexed by the edges

satisfy the relations:

\displaystyle\sum_{e\in U(p)}\mathcal{D}_{e}=\sum_{e^{\prime}\in D(p)}\mathcal{D}_{e^{\prime}}\text{ for }p\in\widehat{P}\setminus\{\hat{0},\hat{1}\},\sum_{e\in U(\hat{0})}\mathcal{D}_{e}=0\;\text{ and }\;\sum_{e\in D(\hat{1})}\mathcal{D}_{e}=0.

(3.2)

In particular, we can take prime divisors corresponding to edges not contained in a spanning tree as generators of
\operatorname{Cl}(\Bbbk[P])
,
thus we obtain that
\operatorname{Cl}(\Bbbk[P])\cong\mathbb{Z}^{n}/\sigma_{P}(\mathbb{Z}^{d})\cong\mathbb{Z}^{n-d}
(

Moreover, any Weil divisor can be described as

and we identify this with

.

Note that
\mathcal{H}(\widehat{P})
can be regarded as a directed graph by orienting the edge

from

to

covers

.
In what follows, we fix a spanning tree

of
\mathcal{H}(\widehat{P})
and let

be its edges. Thus, let
e_{d+1},\cdots,e_{n}
be the remaining edges of
\mathcal{H}(\widehat{P})
.
In addition, for

, let

be the fundamental cycle of

with respect to

.
For
e\in E(\mathcal{H}(\widehat{P}))
, let

be the weight corresponding to the prime divisor
\mathcal{D}_{e}
.
Then,
\beta_{e_{d+i}}=\mathbf{e}_{i}
for

and other weights

).

Proposition 3.3
.

Work with the same notation as above.
Then
\beta_{e}=\sum_{i\in[n-d]}\mathbf{v}(F_{i})^{(e)}\mathbf{e}_{i}
.
Moreover, for
w=\sum_{e\in E(\mathcal{H}(\widehat{P}))}a_{e}\beta_{e}
and a cycle
C
of
\mathcal{H}(\widehat{P})
, we have

\sum_{i\in[n-d]}\mathbf{v}(C)^{(e_{d+i})}w^{(i)}=\sum_{e\in E(\mathcal{H}(\widehat{P}))}a_{e}\mathbf{v}(C)^{(e)}.

Proof.

Let
\gamma_{e}=\sum_{i\in[n-d]}\mathbf{v}(F_{i})^{(e)}\mathbf{e}_{i}
for
e\in E(\mathcal{H}(\widehat{P}))
and we show that
\gamma_{e}=\beta_{e}
.
We see that
\gamma_{e_{d+j}}=\sum_{i\in[n-d]}\mathbf{v}(F_{i})^{(e_{d+j})}\mathbf{e}_{i}=\mathbf{e}_{j}=\beta_{e_{d+j}}
for

.
Thus, it is enough to show that

’s satisfy the relation (
3.2
).
For

and

.
We fix

. If

, then

and

. Thus,

.
Suppose that

. Then, there are exactly two edges

with
e_{1},e_{2}\in E(F_{i})
, and only the following two situations may happen:
(a)

or
e_{1},e_{2}\in D(p)
, or (b)
e_{1}\in U(p)
,
e_{2}\in D(p)
or
e_{2}\in U(p)
,
e_{1}\in D(p)
.
In the case (a), we can see that

or

, and hence

.
In the case (b), we can see that
e_{1}\in\operatorname{supp}^{+}(F_{i}),e_{2}\in\operatorname{supp}^{+}(F_{i})
or
e_{1}\in\operatorname{supp}^{-}(F_{i}),e_{2}\in\operatorname{supp}^{-}(F_{i})
, and hence

or

.
Therefore,

holds. Similarly, we have
u(\hat{0})=d(\hat{1})=0
.

Moreover, for
w=\sum_{e\in E(\mathcal{H}(\widehat{P}))}a_{e}\beta_{e}
, we have

.
Furthermore, we see that
\mathbf{v}(C)=\sum_{i\in[n-d]}\mathbf{v}(C)^{(e_{d+i})}\mathbf{v}(F_{i})
for a cycle

in
\mathcal{H}(\widehat{P})
since

form bases of the flow space of
\mathcal{H}(\widehat{P})
.
Therefore, we obtain that

\displaystyle\sum_{i\in[n-d]}\mathbf{v}(C)^{(e_{d+i})}w^{(i)}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i\in[n-d]}\mathbf{v}(C)^{(e_{d+i})}\Big(\sum_{e\in E(\mathcal{H}(\widehat{P}))}a_{e}\mathbf{v}(F_{i})^{(e)}\Big)

\displaystyle=\sum_{e\in E(\mathcal{H}(\widehat{P}))}a_{e}\Big(\sum_{i\in[n-d]}\mathbf{v}(C)^{(e_{d+i})}\mathbf{v}(F_{i})^{(e)}\Big)=\sum_{e\in E(\mathcal{H}(\widehat{P}))}a_{e}\mathbf{v}(C)^{(e)}.

∎

In what follows, we will give a correspondence of which elements in
\operatorname{Cl}(\Bbbk[P])\cong\mathbb{Z}^{n-d}
describe the conic divisorial ideals.

Let
\mathcal{C}(P)
be a convex polytope defined by

\begin{split}\mathcal{C}(P)=\Bigg\{(z_{1},\cdots,z_{n-d})&\in\mathbb{R}^{n-d}:\\
-|\operatorname{supp}^{-}(C)|+1&\leq\sum_{i\in[n-d]}\mathbf{v}(C)^{(e_{d+i})}z_{i}\leq|\operatorname{supp}^{+}(C)|-1\Bigg\},\end{split}

(3.3)

where
C
runs over all circuits in
\mathcal{H}(\widehat{P})
.
Moreover, let
\mathcal{C}^{\prime}(P)
be a convex polytope defined by

\begin{split}\mathcal{C}^{\prime}(P)=\Bigg\{(z_{1},\cdots,z_{n-d})&\in\mathbb{R}^{n-d}:\\
-|\operatorname{supp}^{-}(C^{\prime})|&\leq\sum_{i\in[n-d]}\mathbf{v}(C^{\prime})^{(e_{d+i})}z_{i}\leq|\operatorname{supp}^{+}(C^{\prime})|\Bigg\},\end{split}

where
C^{\prime}
runs over all cycles in
\mathcal{H}(\widehat{P})
.

Theorem 3.4
.

Let the notation be the same as above.
Then, each point in
\mathcal{C}(P)\cap\mathbb{Z}^{n-d}
one-to-one corresponds to the conic divisorial ideal of
\Bbbk[P]
.

Remark 3.5
.

As mentioned in Section
1.4
, this result has already been given in
[
19
, Theorem 2.4]
.
It was proved that each conic divisorial ideal of
\Bbbk[P]
corresponds to a point in
\mathcal{C}(P)\cap\mathbb{Z}^{n-d}
(the set
\mathcal{C}(P)
defined in
[
19
]
coincides with that of this paper).
In the fifth step of the proof, it seems to prove the converse, that is, for each point
(m_{d+1},\ldots,m_{n})\in\mathcal{C}(P)\cap\mathbb{Z}^{n-d}
, there exist
\mathbf{x}\in(-1,0]^{d}
and
(\overline{m}_{1},\ldots,\overline{m}_{n})\in\sigma_{P}(\mathbb{Z}^{d})
such that
m_{i}-\overline{m}_{i}=\ulcorner\sigma_{e_{i}}(\mathbf{x})\urcorner
for all
i\in[n]
, where

.
But in fact, they have proven that for each point
(m_{d+1},\ldots,m_{n})\in\mathcal{C}(P)\cap\mathbb{Z}^{n-d}
and for each

, there exist
\mathbf{x}\in(-1,0]^{d}
and
(\overline{m}_{1},\ldots,\overline{m}_{n})\in\sigma_{P}(\mathbb{Z}^{d})
such that
m_{i}-\overline{m}_{i}=\ulcorner\sigma_{e_{i}}(\mathbf{x})\urcorner
for
i\in[d]
and
i=j
, which does not guarantee that
m_{i}-\overline{m}_{i}=\ulcorner\sigma_{e_{i}}(\mathbf{x})\urcorner
for

.

In this paper, we re-prove this theorem by a different technique.
We derive it by showing
\mathcal{W}^{\prime}(\Bbbk[P])=\mathcal{C}^{\prime}(P)
and using Lemma
3.2
(i), rather than showing
\mathcal{W}(\Bbbk[P])\cap\mathbb{Z}^{n-d}=\mathcal{C}(P)\cap\mathbb{Z}^{n-d}
directly.

Proof of Theorem
3.4
.

It is enough to show that
\mathcal{W}(\Bbbk[P])\cap\mathbb{Z}^{n-d}=\mathcal{C}(P)\cap\mathbb{Z}^{n-d}
.
We first show that
\mathcal{W}^{\prime}(\Bbbk[P])\subset\mathcal{C}^{\prime}(P)
.
Take
w=\sum_{e\in E(\mathcal{H}(\widehat{P}))}a_{e}\beta_{e}\in\mathcal{W}^{\prime}(\Bbbk[P])

For each cycle
C
of

, it follows from Proposition

that

\displaystyle\sum_{i\in[n-d]}\mathbf{v}(C)^{(e_{d+i})}w^{(i)}=\sum_{e\in E(\mathcal{H}(\widehat{P}))}a_{e}\mathbf{v}(C)^{(e)}=\sum_{e\in\operatorname{supp}^{+}(C)}a_{e}-\sum_{e^{\prime}\in\operatorname{supp}^{-}(C)}a_{e^{\prime}}.

(3.4)

Since

, we have

\displaystyle-|\operatorname{supp}^{-}(C)|\leq\sum_{i\in[n-d]}\mathbf{v}(C)^{(e_{d+i})}w^{(i)}\leq|\operatorname{supp}^{+}(C)|.

(3.5)

Thus,
w\in\mathcal{C}^{\prime}(P)
, and hence
\mathcal{W}^{\prime}(\Bbbk[P])\subset\mathcal{C}^{\prime}(P)
.
Moreover, the hyperplanes

\displaystyle\sum_{i\in[n-d]}\mathbf{v}(C)^{(e_{d+i})}z_{i}=|\operatorname{supp}^{+}(C)|\text{ and }\sum_{i\in[n-d]}\mathbf{v}(C)^{(e_{d+i})}z_{i}=-|\operatorname{supp}^{-}(C)|

(3.6)

are supporting hyperplanes of
\mathcal{C}^{\prime}(P)
because there exist elements in
\mathcal{W}^{\prime}(\Bbbk[P])\subset\mathcal{C}^{\prime}(P)
such that the equality of each side of (

) holds respectively.

To show that
\mathcal{C}^{\prime}(P)\subset\mathcal{W}^{\prime}(\Bbbk[P])
, we prove that any vertex of
\mathcal{C}^{\prime}(P)
is in
\mathcal{W}^{\prime}
. Since the hyperplanes (

) support
\mathcal{C}^{\prime}(P)
, any vertex
v
of
\mathcal{C}^{\prime}(P)
can be represented as the intersection of
n-d
hyperplanes of them.
By reversing the direction of cycles
C_{1},\ldots,C_{n-d}
of
\mathcal{H}(\widehat{P})
, we may assume that these hyperplanes have the following forms:

\displaystyle\sum_{i\in[n-d]}\mathbf{v}(C_{k})^{(e_{d+i})}z_{i}=|\operatorname{supp}^{+}(C_{k})|\quad\text{ for }k\in[n-d].

(3.7)

From Lemma
3.6
below, we have
v\in\mathcal{W}^{\prime}
, and hence
\mathcal{W}^{\prime}(\Bbbk[P])=\mathcal{C}^{\prime}(P)
by Lemma
3.2
(ii).

Moreover, from Lemma
3.2
(i), we have

\displaystyle\mathcal{W}(\Bbbk[P])\cap\mathbb{Z}^{n-d}=\Bigg\{(z_{1},\cdots,z_{n-d})

\displaystyle\in\mathbb{Z}^{n-d}:

\displaystyle-|\operatorname{supp}^{-}(C^{\prime})|+1

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{i\in[n-d]}\mathbf{v}(C^{\prime})^{(e_{d+i})}z_{i}\leq|\operatorname{supp}^{+}(C^{\prime})|-1\Bigg\},

where

runs over all cycles in

.
By the same argument as in the fourth step of the proof of

∎

Lemma 3.6
.

Let
C^{+}=\bigcup_{k\in[n-d]}\operatorname{supp}^{+}(C_{k})
and
C^{-}=\bigcup_{k\in[n-d]}\operatorname{supp}^{-}(C_{k})
. Suppose that the intersection of (
3.7
) is a unique point
v=(v_{1},\ldots,v_{n-d})\in\mathbb{R}^{n-d}
.
Then,
v
is a vertex of
\mathcal{C}^{\prime}(P)
if and only if
C^{+}\cap C^{-}=\emptyset
, in which case
v
is in
\mathcal{W}^{\prime}
.

Proof.

It is enough to show that
v\in\mathcal{W}^{\prime}\subset\mathcal{C}^{\prime}(P)
(resp.
v\notin\mathcal{C}^{\prime}(P)
) if
C^{+}\cap C^{-}=\emptyset
(resp.
C^{+}\cap C^{-}\neq\emptyset
). Suppose that
C^{+}\cap C^{-}=\emptyset
. Then,
\sum_{e\in C^{+}}\beta_{e}\in\mathcal{W}^{\prime}
satisfies (
3.7
) for all
k\in[n-d]
.
In fact, by (
3.4
) and
C^{+}\cap C^{-}=\emptyset
, we have

\sum_{i\in[n-d]}\mathbf{v}(C_{k})^{(e_{d+i})}\Big(\sum_{e\in C^{+}}\beta_{e}\Big)^{(i)}=\sum_{e\in\operatorname{supp}^{+}(C_{k})}1=|\operatorname{supp}^{+}(C_{k})|.

Therefore,
v=\sum_{e\in C^{+}}\beta_{e}\in\mathcal{W}^{\prime}\subset\mathcal{C}^{\prime}(P)
.

If
C^{+}\cap C^{-}\neq\emptyset
, there exist
s,t\in[n-d]
with
\operatorname{supp}^{+}(C_{s})\cap\operatorname{supp}^{-}(C_{t})\neq\emptyset
. We may assume that
s=1
and
t=2
. We set
\mathbf{u}=\mathbf{v}(C_{1})+\mathbf{v}(C_{2})
and

C^{*}=(\operatorname{supp}^{+}(C_{1})\cap\operatorname{supp}^{-}(C_{2}))\cup(\operatorname{supp}^{+}(C_{2})\cap\operatorname{supp}^{-}(C_{1})).

Note that
C^{*}\neq\emptyset
. Since
v
satisfies (
3.7
), we obtain that

\displaystyle\sum_{i\in[n-d]}\mathbf{u}^{(e_{d+i})}v_{i}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i\in[n-d]}\Big(\mathbf{v}(C_{1})^{(e_{d+i})}+\mathbf{v}(C_{2})^{(e_{d+i})}\Big)v_{i}

\displaystyle=|\operatorname{supp}^{+}(C_{1})|+|\operatorname{supp}^{+}(C_{2})|

\displaystyle=|\operatorname{supp}^{+}(C_{1})\setminus C^{*}|+|\operatorname{supp}^{+}(C_{2})\setminus C^{*}|+|C^{*}|.

(3.8)

On the other hand, since
\mathbf{u}
is in the flow space of
\mathcal{H}(\widehat{P})
, we can write
\mathbf{u}=\sum_{i=1}^{m}\mathbf{v}(D_{i})
, where
D_{1},\ldots,D_{m}
are cycles of
\mathcal{H}(\widehat{P})
with
\operatorname{supp}^{+}(D_{k})\subset(\operatorname{supp}^{+}(C_{1})\cup\operatorname{supp}^{+}(C_{2}))\setminus C^{*}
and
\operatorname{supp}^{-}(D_{k})\subset(\operatorname{supp}^{-}(C_{1})\cup\operatorname{supp}^{-}(C_{2}))\setminus C^{*}
for all
k\in[m]
.
This fact follows from a similar argument as in the proof of
[
11
, Theorem 14.2.2]
. If
v\in\mathcal{C}^{\prime}(P)
, then

\displaystyle\sum_{i\in[n-d]}\mathbf{v}(D_{k})^{(e_{d+i})}v_{i}\leq|\operatorname{supp}^{+}(D_{k})|\quad\text{ for all }k\in[m].

Thus, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{i\in[n-d]}\mathbf{u}^{(e_{d+i})}v_{i}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k\in[m]}\Big(\sum_{i\in[n-d]}\mathbf{v}(D_{k})^{(e_{d+i})}v_{i}\Big)

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{k\in[m]}|\operatorname{supp}^{+}(D_{k})|=|\operatorname{supp}^{+}(C_{1})\setminus C^{*}|+|\operatorname{supp}^{+}(C_{2})\setminus C^{*}|,

a contradiction to (
3.2
). Hence, we obtain that
v\notin\mathcal{C}^{\prime}(P)
.
∎

3.3.

In this subsection, we consider conic divisorial ideals of stable set rings of perfect graphs.

In what follows, let

By definition of

\displaystyle w^{(i)}=a_{i}+\sum_{v\in Q_{i}}a_{v+n}-\sum_{u\in Q_{0}}a_{u+n}-a_{0}.

\displaystyle\sum_{i\in I}w^{(i)}-\sum_{j\in J}w^{(j)}

S\cap Q_{0}=\emptyset
, then we have
S=\{v_{1},\ldots,v_{n}\}
for some
v_{i}\in Q_{i}^{+}
(
i\in[n]
) since
S\subset V(G_{r_{1},\ldots,r_{n}})\setminus Q_{0}=Q_{1}^{+}\cup\cdots\cup Q_{n}^{+}
.

(iii) We can see that
G_{r_{1},\ldots,r_{n}}
arises from
n+1
complete graphs with
d
vertices by pasting them.
Thus, by Proposition
2.6
,
G_{r_{1},\ldots,r_{n}}
is chordal.

If
G_{r_{1},\ldots,r_{n}}
is a comparability graph, then so is any induced subgraph of
G_{r_{1},\ldots,r_{n}}
.
However, for any
n\geq 3
and
r_{1},\ldots,r_{n}
, the graph
G_{r_{1},\ldots,r_{n}}
contains
G_{1,1,1}
as an induced subgraph and we can check that
G_{1,1,1}
is not a comparability graph, a contradiction.

(iv) Since
G_{r_{1},\ldots,r_{n}}
is perfect and maximal cliques
Q_{0},Q_{1},\ldots,Q_{n}
of
G_{r_{1},\ldots,r_{n}}
have the same cardinality
d
, the stable set ring
\Bbbk[\operatorname{Stab}_{G_{r_{1},\ldots,r_{n}}}]
is Gorenstein. Moreover, we have
\operatorname{Cl}(\Bbbk[\operatorname{Stab}_{G_{r_{1},\ldots,r_{n}}}])\cong\mathbb{Z}^{n}
because
G_{r_{1},\ldots,r_{n}}
has
n+1
maximal cliques.

(v) From (
3.10
), we can see that for
j\in\{0,1,\ldots,2d+n\}
,

\displaystyle\beta_{i}=\begin{cases}\mathbf{e}_{j}\;&\text{ if $i=j$ or $i-n\in Q_{j}^{+}$};\\
-\mathbf{e}_{j}\;&\text{ if $i-n\in Q_{j}^{-}$}.\end{cases}

(5.1)

Therefore,
\{\bar{\beta}_{1},\ldots,\bar{\beta}_{n^{\prime}}\}=\{\mathbf{e}_{0},\pm\mathbf{e}_{1},\ldots,\pm\mathbf{e}_{n}\}
with
n^{\prime}=2n+1
.
Let
\mathbf{n}\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}
. If there are
i_{1},\ldots,i_{n-1}\subset[2n+1]
such that
\bar{\beta}_{i_{k}}
’s are linearly independent and
\langle\mathbf{n},\bar{\beta}_{i_{k}}\rangle=0
for all
k\in[n-1]
, then
\mathbf{n}
must be the following form:

\mathbf{n}=m\mathbf{e}_{i}\quad\text{ or }\quad\mathbf{n}=m(\mathbf{e}_{i}-\mathbf{e}_{j})\quad(m\in\mathbb{Z}\setminus\{0\})

for some
i,j\in[n]
.
By Lemma
3.1
(the observation mentioned in Remark
3.7
), we have

\begin{split}\mathcal{C}^{\prime}(G_{r_{1},\ldots,r_{n}})&=\mathcal{W}^{\prime}(\Bbbk[\operatorname{Stab}_{G_{r_{1},\ldots,r_{n}}}])\\
&\begin{split}\;=\Big\{(z_{1},\cdots,z_{n})\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:-1-|X_{\{i\}\{j\}}^{-}|\leq z_{i}-z_{j}&\leq 1+|X_{\{i\}\{j\}}^{+}|\\
&\text{ for $i,j\in\{0,1,\ldots,n\}$}\Big\}.\end{split}\end{split}

Moreover, we can determine
X_{\{i\}\{j\}}^{\pm}
defined in (
3.11
) as follows: for
i,j\in[n]
,

X_{\{i\}\{0\}}^{+}=Q_{i}^{+},\quad X_{\{i\}\{0\}}^{-}=Q_{i}^{-},\quad X_{\{i\}\{j\}}^{+}=Q_{i}^{+}\cup Q_{j}^{-}\;\text{ and }\;X_{\{i\}\{j\}}^{-}=Q_{j}^{+}\cup Q_{i}^{-}.

Hence, we get

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}(G_{r_{1},\ldots,r_{n}})=\{(z_{1},\cdots,z_{n})

\displaystyle\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:-r_{i}\leq z_{i}\leq r_{i}\text{ for $i\in[n]$},

\displaystyle-r_{i}-r_{j}\leq z_{i}-z_{j}\leq r_{i}+r_{j}\text{ for $i,j\in[n]$}\}.

Clearly, the inequality
-r_{i}-r_{j}\leq z_{i}-z_{j}\leq r_{i}+r_{j}
can be omitted, and hence we obtain the desired result.
∎

5.2.
Preliminaries on non-commutative resolutions

Before proving Theorem
1.6
, we recall the definition of NCCRs and prepare some notation and lemmas.

Definition 5.3
.

Let
R
be a CM normal domain, let
M\neq 0
be a reflexive
R
-module, and let
E=\operatorname{End}_{R}(M)
.
Moreover, let
\operatorname{gldim}E
denote the global dimension of
E
.

•

We call
E
a
non-commutative resolution
(
NCR
, for short) of
R
if
\operatorname{gldim}E<\infty
.

In addition, suppose that
R
is Gorenstein.

•

We call
E
a
non-commutative crepant resolution
(
NCCR
, for short) of
R

if
E
is an NCR and is an MCM
R
-module.

•

Moreover, we say that an NCCR
E
is
splitting
if
M
is a finite direct sum of rank one reflexive
R
-modules. A splitting NCCR is also called “toric NCCR” when
R
is a toric ring (see
[
2
]
).

Since conic divisorial ideals of a toric ring
R
are rank one reflexive MCM
R
-modules, the endomorphism ring
E
of the finite direct sum of some of them is a toric NCCR if
E
is an MCM
R
-module and
\operatorname{gldim}E<\infty
.

We use the same notation as in Section
2.1
.
Let
\mathcal{A}=\operatorname{mod}(G,S)
be the category of finitely generated
G
-equivariant
S
-modules. Given
\chi\in X(G)
, let

P_{\chi}=V_{\chi}\otimes_{\Bbbk}S
.
Note that
P_{\chi}\in\mathcal{A}
and
M_{\chi}=P_{\chi}^{G}
.
For a subset
\mathcal{L}\subset X(G)
, we set

\displaystyle P_{\mathcal{L}}=\bigoplus_{\chi\in\mathcal{L}}P_{\chi}\;\text{ and }\;\Lambda_{\mathcal{L}}=\operatorname{End}_{\mathcal{A}}(P_{\mathcal{L}}).

Moreover, for
\chi\in X(G)
, let
P_{\mathcal{L},\chi}=\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal{A}}(P_{\mathcal{L}},P_{\chi})
.

Let
Y(G)
denote the group of one-parameter subgroups of
G
and let
Y(G)_{\mathbb{R}}=Y(G)\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathbb{R}
.
Note that
Y(G)\cong\mathbb{Z}^{r}
and
Y(G)_{\mathbb{R}}\cong\mathbb{R}^{r}
.
We say that
\chi\in X(G)
is
separated from
\mathcal{L}
by
\lambda\in Y(G)_{\mathbb{R}}

if it holds that
\langle\lambda,\chi\rangle<\langle\lambda,\chi^{\prime}\rangle
for each
\chi^{\prime}\in\mathcal{L}
.

Our goal is to choose
\mathcal{L}\subset\mathcal{W}(R)\cap X(G)
such that
E=\operatorname{End}_{R}(M_{\mathcal{L}})
becomes an NCCR, where
R=S^{G}
and
M_{\mathcal{L}}=\bigoplus_{\chi\in\mathcal{L}}M_{\chi}
.
To show
\operatorname{gldim}E<\infty
, we use the following facts:

•

We note that
\Lambda_{\mathcal{L}}^{G}=\operatorname{End}_{R}(M_{\mathcal{L}})=E
and that
\operatorname{gldim}\Lambda_{\mathcal{L}}<\infty
implies
\operatorname{gldim}E<\infty
(see
[
19
, Section 3]
).

•

If
\operatorname{pdim}_{\Lambda_{\mathcal{L}}}P_{\mathcal{L},\chi}<\infty
for all
\chi\in X(G)
, where
\operatorname{pdim}P_{\mathcal{L},\chi}
denotes the projective dimension of
P_{\mathcal{L},\chi}
over
\Lambda_{\mathcal{L}}
, then
\operatorname{gldim}\Lambda_{\mathcal{L}}<\infty
(
[
25
, Lemma 10.1]
).

•

By the same argument as in
[
25
, Section 10.3]
, we can see that if
\operatorname{pdim}_{\Lambda_{\mathcal{L}}}P_{\mathcal{L},\chi}<\infty
for each
\chi\in\widetilde{\mathcal{L}}
, where
\mathcal{L}\subset\mathcal{W}(R)\cap X(G)\subset\widetilde{\mathcal{L}}\subset X(G)
,
then
\operatorname{pdim}_{\Lambda_{\mathcal{L}}}P_{\mathcal{L},\chi}<\infty
for all
\chi\in X(G)
.

Moreover, we note the following fact to show that
E
is an MCM
R
-module:

•

Since
\operatorname{End}_{R}(M_{\mathcal{L}})\cong\bigoplus_{\chi,\chi^{\prime}\in\mathcal{L}}M_{\chi-\chi^{\prime}}
, the endomorphism ring
E
is an MCM
R
-module if
M_{\chi-\chi^{\prime}}
is an MCM
R
-module for any
\chi,\chi^{\prime}\in\widetilde{\mathcal{L}}
.

By summarizing those facts, we obtain the following lemma:

Lemma 5.4
.

Let the notation be the same as above.
Then,
E
is an NCCR of
R
if there exists
\widetilde{\mathcal{L}}\subset X(G)
satisfying the following two conditions:

(a)

\chi-\chi^{\prime}\in\widetilde{\mathcal{L}}
for any
\chi,\chi^{\prime}\in\mathcal{L}
, and
M_{\chi}
is an MCM
R
-module for any
\chi\in\widetilde{\mathcal{L}}
.

(b)

\mathcal{W}(R)\cap X(G)\subset\widetilde{\mathcal{L}}
and
\operatorname{pdim}_{\Lambda_{\mathcal{L}}}P_{\mathcal{L},\chi}<\infty
for each
\chi\in\widetilde{\mathcal{L}}
.

In addition, we give another lemma to verify the condition (b) in Lemma
5.4
.

Lemma 5.5
.

Let the notation be the same as above.

(i)

(
[
25
, Section 10.1]
)
If
\chi
is in
\mathcal{L}
, then
P_{\mathcal{L},\chi}
is a right projective
\Lambda_{\mathcal{L}}
-module, and hence
\operatorname{pdim}_{\Lambda_{\mathcal{L}}}P_{\mathcal{L},\chi}<\infty
.

(ii)

(
[
25
, Lemma 10.2]
)
Let
\chi\in X(G)
be separated from
\mathcal{L}
by
\lambda\in Y(G)_{\mathbb{R}}
. Then, we obtain the acyclic complex

\displaystyle 0\rightarrow\bigoplus_{\mu_{d_{\lambda}}}P_{\mathcal{L},\mu_{d_{\lambda}}}\rightarrow\cdots\rightarrow\bigoplus_{\mu_{1}}P_{\mathcal{L},\mu_{1}}\rightarrow P_{\mathcal{L},\chi}\rightarrow 0,

where for each
p\in[d_{\lambda}]
with
d_{\lambda}=|\{i\in[m]:\langle\beta_{i},\lambda\rangle>0\}|
, we let

\mu_{p}=\chi+\beta_{i_{1}}+\cdots+\beta_{i_{p}}
with
\{i_{1},\ldots,i_{p}\}\subset[m]
and
\langle\beta_{i_{j}},\lambda\rangle>0
for all
j\in[p]
.

This implies that
\operatorname{pdim}_{\Lambda_{\mathcal{L}}}P_{\mathcal{L},\chi}<\infty
if
\operatorname{pdim}_{\Lambda_{\mathcal{L}}}P_{\mathcal{L},\mu_{p}}<\infty
for each
\mu_{p}
.

5.3.
Proof of Theorem
1.6

This subsection is devoted to giving a proof of Theorem
1.6
.

We recall

\mathcal{L}=\{(z_{1},\cdots,z_{n})\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}:0\leq z_{i}\leq r_{i}\text{ for $i\in[n]$}\}

and set

\widetilde{\mathcal{L}}=\mathcal{C}(G_{r_{1},\ldots,r_{n}})\cap\mathbb{Z}^{n}.

Note that
\widetilde{\mathcal{L}}=\{(z_{1},\cdots,z_{n})\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}:-r_{i}\leq z_{i}\leq r_{i}\text{ for $i\in[n]$}\}
from Proposition
5.2
(v).

In
[
19
, Theorem 3.6]
, a toric NCCR is given for the Segre product of polynomial rings by taking
\mathcal{L},\widetilde{\mathcal{L}}\subset X(G)
(these are different from
\mathcal{L}
and
\widetilde{\mathcal{L}}
defined above, but very similar) and using the same arguments as in Lemma
5.5
.
Indeed, the proof of Theorem
1.6
can be obtained by the same procedure as that of
[
19
, Theorem 3.6]
.
However, we give a self-contained proof since
\Bbbk[\operatorname{Stab}_{G_{r_{1},\ldots,r_{n}}}]
is not the Segre product of polynomial rings.

Proof of Theorem
1.6
.

It is enough to show that
\widetilde{\mathcal{L}}
satisfies the conditions (a) and (b) in Lemma
5.4
.
First, we can check (a) since

\{\chi-\chi^{\prime}:\chi,\chi^{\prime}\in\mathcal{L}\}=\{(z_{1},\cdots,z_{n})\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}:-r_{i}\leq z_{i}\leq r_{i}\text{ for $i\in[n]$}\}=\widetilde{\mathcal{L}},

and
M_{\chi}
is a conic divisorial ideal of
R
for any
\chi\in\widetilde{\mathcal{L}}
.

Next, we show (b).
We see that
\mathcal{L}\subset\mathcal{W}(R)\cap X(G)=\mathcal{C}(G_{r_{1},\ldots,r_{n}})\cap X(G)=\widetilde{\mathcal{L}}
.
We have already computed the weights of
R
in the proof of Proposition
5.2
(v).
We set
\bar{\beta}_{i}=\mathbf{e}_{i}
,
\bar{\beta}_{n+i}=-\mathbf{e}_{i}
for
i\in[n]
and
\bar{\beta}_{2n+1}=\mathbf{e}_{0}
.
Note that for
i\in[n]
, the multiplicity
m_{i}
of
\bar{\beta}_{i}
is equal to
r_{i}+1
.
We also set

\widetilde{\mathcal{L}}_{j}=\{(z_{1},\cdots,z_{n})\in\widetilde{\mathcal{L}}:z_{j}\geq 0,\cdots,z_{n}\geq 0\}

for
j\in[n]
and

\widetilde{\mathcal{L}}_{j}(k)=\{(z_{1},\cdots,z_{n})\in\widetilde{\mathcal{L}}_{j}:z_{j}\geq-k\}.

for
0\leq k\leq r_{j}
.
Note that
\mathcal{L}=\widetilde{\mathcal{L}}_{1}\subset\widetilde{\mathcal{L}}_{2}\subset\cdots\subset\widetilde{\mathcal{L}}_{n}\subset\widetilde{\mathcal{L}}
,

\widetilde{\mathcal{L}}_{j+1}(0)=\widetilde{\mathcal{L}}_{j}(r_{j})
for
j\in[n-1]
and
\widetilde{\mathcal{L}}=\widetilde{\mathcal{L}}_{n}(r_{n})
.
Moreover, for any
j\in[n]
,
k\in[r_{j}]
and any
\chi\in\widetilde{\mathcal{L}}_{j}(k)\setminus\widetilde{\mathcal{L}}_{j}(k-1)
, we can see that

\langle\mathbf{e}_{j},\chi\rangle<\langle\mathbf{e}_{j},\chi^{\prime}\rangle\;\;\text{for any }\chi^{\prime}\in\widetilde{\mathcal{L}}_{j}(k-1).

Hence,
\chi
is separated from
\widetilde{\mathcal{L}}_{j}(k-1)
by
\mathbf{e}_{j}
,
and we have that
\langle\mathbf{e}_{j},\bar{\beta}_{j}\rangle>0
and
\langle\mathbf{e}_{j},\bar{\beta}_{i}\rangle\leq 0
for any
i\not=j
.

We prove that
\operatorname{pdim}_{\Lambda_{\mathcal{L}}}P_{\mathcal{L},\chi}<\infty
for any
j\in[n]
,
k\in\{0,1,\ldots,r_{j}\}
and
\chi\in\widetilde{\mathcal{L}}_{j}(k)
by the induction on
j
and
k
.

(
j=1
and
k=0
)
In this case, we have
\widetilde{\mathcal{L}}_{1}(0)=\mathcal{L}
.
Hence,
\operatorname{pdim}_{\Lambda_{\mathcal{L}}}P_{\mathcal{L},\chi}<\infty
for any
\chi\in\mathcal{L}
by Lemma
5.5
(i).

(
j=1
and
k>0
)
Assume that
\operatorname{pdim}_{\Lambda_{\mathcal{L}}}P_{\mathcal{L},\chi}<\infty
for any
\chi\in\widetilde{\mathcal{L}}_{1}(k-1)
.
Then, for any
\chi^{\prime}\in\widetilde{\mathcal{L}}_{1}(k)\setminus\widetilde{\mathcal{L}}_{1}(k-1)
, we see that

\chi^{\prime}+\beta_{i_{1}}+\cdots+\beta_{i_{p}}\in\widetilde{\mathcal{L}}_{1}(k-1)
, where
\beta_{i_{1}}=\cdots=\beta_{i_{p}}=\bar{\beta}_{1}
and
p\in[r_{1}+1]
.
Hence,
\operatorname{pdim}_{\Lambda_{\mathcal{L}}}P_{\mathcal{L},\chi^{\prime}}<\infty
by Lemma
5.5
(ii).

(
j>1
and
k\geq 0
)
Assume that
\operatorname{pdim}_{\Lambda_{\mathcal{L}}}P_{\mathcal{L},\chi}<\infty
for any
\chi\in\widetilde{\mathcal{L}}_{j-1}(r_{j-1})
.
The case
k=0
is trivial since
\widetilde{\mathcal{L}}_{j}(0)=\widetilde{\mathcal{L}}_{j-1}(r_{j-1})
.
Suppose that
k>0
and
\operatorname{pdim}_{\Lambda_{\mathcal{L}}}P_{\mathcal{L},\chi}<\infty
for any
\chi\in\widetilde{\mathcal{L}}_{j}(k-1)
.
Then, for any
\chi^{\prime}\in\widetilde{\mathcal{L}}_{j}(k)\setminus\widetilde{\mathcal{L}}_{j}(k-1)
, we see that

\chi^{\prime}+\beta_{i_{1}}+\cdots+\beta_{i_{p}}\in\widetilde{\mathcal{L}}_{j}(k-1)
, where
\beta_{i_{1}}=\cdots=\beta_{i_{p}}=\bar{\beta}_{j}
and
p\in[r_{j}+1]
.
Hence,
\operatorname{pdim}_{\Lambda_{\mathcal{L}}}P_{\mathcal{L},\chi^{\prime}}<\infty
by Lemma
5.5
(ii).

Consequently, we obtain that
\operatorname{pdim}_{\Lambda_{\mathcal{L}}}P_{\mathcal{L},\chi}<\infty
for any
\chi\in\widetilde{\mathcal{L}}_{n}(r_{n})=\widetilde{\mathcal{L}}
.

∎

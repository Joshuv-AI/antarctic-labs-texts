Partial Scalar Curvatures and Topological Obstructions for Submanifolds
C.-R. Onti, K. Polymerakis, Th. Vlachos

Abstract
We investigate specific intrinsic curvatures ρ k \rho_{k} , 1 ≤ k ≤ n 1\leq k\leq n , that interpolate between the minimum Ricci curvature ρ 1 \rho_{1} and the normalized scalar curvature ρ n = ρ \rho_{n}=\rho of n n -dimensional Riemannian manifolds. For n n -dimensional submanifolds in space forms, these curvatures satisfy an inequality involving the mean curvature H H and the normal scalar curvature ρ ⟂ \rho^{\perp} , which reduces to the well-known DDVV inequality when k = n k=n . We derive topological obstructions for compact n n -dimensional submanifolds based on universal lower bounds of the L n / 2 L^{n/2} -norms of certain functions involving ρ k , H \rho_{k},H and ρ ⟂ \rho^{\perp} . These obstructions are expressed in terms of the Betti numbers. Our main result applies for any 1 ≤ k ≤ n − 1 1\leq k\leq n-1 , but it generally fails for k = n k=n , where the involved norm vanishes precisely for Wintgen ideal submanifolds. We demonstrate this by providing a method of constructing new compact 3-dimensional minimal Wintgen ideal submanifolds in even-dimensional spheres. Specifically, we prove that such submanifolds exist in 𝕊 6 \mathbb{S}^{6} with arbitrarily large first Betti number.

1
Introduction

A fundamental problem in differential geometry is to investigate
the intricate relationship between the geometry and topology
of Riemannian manifolds. From the perspective of submanifold
theory, there is a profound interplay between the intrinsic and
extrinsic geometries of a submanifold, significantly influencing
its topological properties. In particular, the intrinsic geometry,
governed by metrics and curvature tensors intrinsic to the
submanifold itself, interacts with the extrinsic geometry,
characterized by how the submanifold is embedded within a
higher-dimensional ambient space. This interaction can lead
to profound insights into the topological structure of the
submanifold.

One of the key questions in this area involves understanding
how intrinsic curvature invariants, such as the Ricci curvature
and scalar curvature, relate to extrinsic ones,
including the mean curvature and normal curvature.
These relationships often manifest through inequalities that
bind intrinsic quantities to extrinsic ones, providing
constraints that influence the topological classification
of the submanifolds.

In the case of surfaces
f\colon M^{2}\to\mathbb{Q}_{c}^{2+m}

isometrically immersed into the complete simply connected
space form
\mathbb{Q}_{c}^{2+m}
of constant curvature
c
, such
a pointwise relation is Wintgen’s inequality

K\leq c+H^{2}-|K_{N}|.

This inequality, first proved by Wintgen
[
Wi
]
for surfaces in

\mathbb{R}^{4}
, provides an extrinsic upper bound for the Gaussian curvature

K
of the surface in terms of the length

H
of the mean curvature vector field and the normal curvature

K_{N}
of
f
, which is related to the area of the curvature ellipse.
Furthermore, Wintgen demonstrated that equality holds
precisely at the points where the ellipse of curvature is a circle.
Guadalupe and Rodriguez
[
GR
]
extended Wintgen’s inequality
in the aforementioned form and straightforwardly derived an
inequality for compact surfaces. This inequality establishes a
relationship between the integral of
c+H^{2}-|K_{N}|
and
the Euler-Poincaré characteristic of the surface.

Wintgen’s inequality has been generalized to any
isometric immersion
f\colon M^{n}\to\mathbb{Q}_{c}^{n+m}
with arbitrary
dimension into space forms, resulting in what is known as the
DDVV (cf.
[
DDVV
,
GT
,
Lu
]
) inequality

\rho\leq c+H^{2}-\rho^{\perp}.

This pointwise inequality provides an upper bound
for the normalized scalar curvature
\rho
of
M^{n}
in
terms of the length
H
of the mean curvature vector field,
and of the normal scalar curvature of
f
given by

\rho^{\perp}=\|R^{\perp}\|/n(n-1)
, where
R^{\perp}
is the
normal curvature tensor of
f
. Submanifolds for which
the DDVV inequality holds as equality at every point are
termed Wintgen ideal submanifolds. These submanifolds
often exhibit highly symmetric structures, and their
classification remains an area of active research,
especially in higher dimensions (see
[
XLMW
]
and references therein).
In the case
n=2
, these precisely correspond to superconformal surfaces,
which are surfaces with curvature ellipses becoming circles
at every point. However, in dimensions
n\geq 3
, the
classification of Wintgen ideal submanifolds remains an
intriguing yet challenging problem.

In this paper, we are interested in a type of partial scalar
curvatures essentially introduced by Wolfson in
[
Wo
]
.
More precisely, let
M^{n}
be a Riemannian
manifold and denote by
\lambda_{1}\leq\dots\leq\lambda_{n}

the eigenvalues of the normalized Ricci tensor at each point.
The
k
-th scalar curvature
of
M^{n}
is defined
pointwise as

\rho_{k}=\frac{1}{k}\sum_{i=1}^{k}\lambda_{i},\;\;1\leq k\leq n.

At any point, the
k
-th scalar curvature interpolates between the
minimum
\rho_{1}
of the normalized Ricci curvature for
k=1
,
and the normalized scalar curvature
\rho=\rho_{n}
.
In fact,
\rho_{k}
is the minimum of the average of the sum of
Ricci curvatures in
k
orthonormal vectors,
and it is a continuous function on
M^{n}
for every
k
.

Using the DDVV inequality and its equality case (cf.
[
GT
,
Lu
]
),
one can show that for every
n
-dimensional submanifold
f\colon M^{n}\to\mathbb{Q}^{n+m}_{c}
,
n\geq 3
,
the following inequality holds

\rho_{k}\leq c+H^{2}-\rho^{\perp},\;\;1\leq k\leq n-1,

at every point of
M^{n}
, where the equality holds precisely at the umbilical points.

The aim of the present paper is to derive topological obstructions for compact

n
-dimensional submanifolds based on universal
lower bounds of the
L^{n/2}
-norms of certain functions involving

\rho_{k},H
and
\rho^{\perp}
.
These obstructions are expressed in terms of the Betti numbers.
For compact submanifolds into space forms
\mathbb{Q}^{n+m}_{c}
with
c\geq 0
,
we prove the following theorem that can be regarded as a generalization of the
result of Guadalupe-Rodriguez
[
GR
]
in the context of higher-dimensional
submanifolds. Throughout this paper, we assume all considered manifolds
to be connected without boundary and oriented, with
i
-th
Betti number
b_{i}(M^{n};\mathbb{F})
over an arbitrary coefficient
field
\mathbb{F}
.

Theorem 1
.

Given integers
n\geq 3
and
m\geq 1
, there exists for every

k\in\{1,\dots,n-1\}
a positive constant
\varepsilon_{k}(n,m)
,
depending only on
n
and
m
, such that if
M^{n}
is a compact
Riemannian manifold that admits an isometric immersion into

\mathbb{Q}_{c}^{n+m}
with
c\geq 0
,
then

\int_{M^{n}}(c+H^{2}-\rho^{\perp}-\rho_{k})^{n/2}dM\geq\varepsilon_{k}(n,m)\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}b_{i}(M^{n};\mathbb{F})

for any coefficient field
\mathbb{F}
. Moreover, if

\int_{M^{n}}(c+H^{2}-\rho^{\perp}-\rho_{k})^{n/2}dM<2\varepsilon_{k}(n,m)

for some
k\in\{1,\dots,n-1\}
, then
M^{n}
is either homeomorphic to

\mathbb{S}^{n}
, or it is an Eells-Kuiper manifold.
In particular,
M^{n}
is homeomorphic to
\mathbb{S}^{n}
if

\int_{M^{n}}(c+H^{2}-\rho^{\perp}-\rho_{k})^{n/2}dM<\varepsilon_{k}(n,m).

We recall that an Eells-Kuiper manifold
[
EK
]
of dimension
n
is a
compactification of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
by a sphere of dimension
n/2
, where

n=2,4,8
, or
16
. For
n\geq 4
, it is simply connected and has the integral
cohomology structure of the complex (
n=4
), quaternionic (
n=8
), or the
Cayley (
n=16
) projective plane.

We point out that the assumption
n\geq 3
in the above theorem is
essential, and the result does not hold in the case
n=2
.
In fact, the involved integral vanishes for every compact
superconformal surface, and there exist an abundance of such
surfaces with arbitrary genus in any codimension
m\geq 2
.

Chen and Wei in
[
CW
]
obtained a geometric rigidity result
for compact submanifolds
f\colon M^{n}\to\mathbb{Q}^{n+m}_{c},n\geq 4
,
with parallel mean curvature vector field, in terms of the lowest
eigenvalue of the Ricci tensor. More precisely, for
c\geq 0
, they
proved that such a submanifold is totally umbilical if
the
L^{n/2}
-norm of the nonnegative part of the function

\lambda-(n-1)\rho_{1}
is sufficiently pinched,
where
\lambda
is a constant satisfying

(n-2)(c+H^{2})<\lambda\leq(n-1)(c+H^{2})
.
The following immediate consequence of Theorem
1

is the analogous topological rigidity result for submanifolds of
dimension
n\geq 3
, without imposing any assumption on
the mean curvature.

Corollary 2
.

Let
f\colon M^{n}\to\mathbb{Q}_{c}^{n+m},n\geq 3,c\geq 0
, be an isometric
immersion of a compact Riemannian manifold. If

\int_{M^{n}}(c+H^{2}-\rho^{\perp}-\rho_{1})^{n/2}dM<\varepsilon_{1}(n,m),

then
M^{n}
is homeomorphic to
\mathbb{S}^{n}
.

The following result is a consequence
of Theorem
1
that applies to Einstein submanifolds.

Corollary 3
.

Let
f\colon M^{n}\to\mathbb{Q}_{c}^{n+m},n\geq 3,c\geq 0
, be an isometric
immersion of a compact Einstein manifold with Ricci
curvature
\rho
. Then

\int_{M^{n}}(c+H^{2}-\rho^{\perp}-\rho)^{n/2}dM\geq\varepsilon_{1}(n,m)\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}b_{i}(M^{n};\mathbb{F})

for any coefficient field
\mathbb{F}
. Moreover, if

\int_{M^{n}}(c+H^{2}-\rho^{\perp}-\rho)^{n/2}dM<2\varepsilon_{1}(n,m),

then
M^{n}
is either homeomorphic to
\mathbb{S}^{n}
, or it is an
Eells-Kuiper manifold. In particular,
M^{n}
is homeomorphic to
\mathbb{S}^{n}
if

\int_{M^{n}}(c+H^{2}-\rho^{\perp}-\rho)^{n/2}dM<\varepsilon_{1}(n,m).

The above result demonstrates that Theorem
1
is also valid for
k=n

in the case of any Einstein submanifold, as
\rho_{k}=\rho
for any

1\leq k\leq n
in this scenario. However, Theorem
1
doesn’t hold
for
k=n
in general, in which case the involved integral vanishes for
Wintgen ideal submanifolds. We note that apart from superconformal
surfaces and totally umbilical submanifolds,
there are only a few known compact Wintgen ideal submanifolds
(cf.
[
DDVV
,
XLMW
]
) and all of them have dimension
n=3
.
In Section 5, we provide counterexamples to Theorem 1 for

k=n=3
. In fact, we present a method to generate new compact
Wintgen ideal submanifolds with positive first Betti number,
which is an interesting result in its own right.
These Wintgen ideal submanifolds
are unit bundles of plane subbundles of the normal bundle of
appropriate compact minimal surfaces in even-dimensional
spheres. This class of minimal surfaces encompasses
pseudoholomorphic curves in the nearly Kähler sphere

\mathbb{S}^{6}
. This justifies the inclusion of the additional term in
the following result when considering the case
k=n
.

Theorem 4
.

Given integers
n\geq 2
and
m\geq 1
, there exists for every

\lambda\in[0,1)
a positive constant
\varepsilon_{\lambda}(n,m)
,
depending only on
n
and
m
,
such that if
M^{n}
is a compact
Riemannian manifold that admits an isometric immersion
into
\mathbb{Q}_{c}^{n+m}
, then

\int_{M^{n}}(c+H^{2}-\lambda\rho^{\perp}-\rho)^{n/2}dM\geq\varepsilon_{\lambda}(n,m)\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}b_{i}(M^{n};\mathbb{F})

for any coefficient field
\mathbb{F}
. Moreover, if

\int_{M^{n}}(c+H^{2}-\lambda\rho^{\perp}-\rho)^{n/2}dM<2\varepsilon_{\lambda}(n,m)

for some
\lambda\in[0,1)
, then
M^{n}
is either homeomorphic
to
\mathbb{S}^{n}
, or it is an Eells-Kuiper manifold. In particular,
M^{n}
is homeomorphic to
\mathbb{S}^{n}
if

\int_{M^{n}}(c+H^{2}-\lambda\rho^{\perp}-\rho)^{n/2}dM<\varepsilon_{\lambda}(n,m).

It is worth noting that the integral in Theorem
4
is conformally
invariant, rendering Theorem
4
applicable to submanifolds
in any space form. Moreover, the above theorem may be regarded
as an enhancement of a topological result by Shiohama and Xu

[
SX2
]
for submanifolds in any space form, where the case

\lambda=0
is considered.

The paper is organized as follows: In Section 2, we prove
an auxiliary lemma that is essential for the proofs of our
results and may have potential applications in other contexts.
In Section 3, we derive algebraic inequalities
regarding symmetric bilinear forms, which are crucial
to our proofs. Additionally, we provide examples that
suggest Theorem
1
may not
hold in general for
k=n>3
. In Section 4, we give the
detailed proofs of Theorems
1
and
4
.
The final Section 5 is devoted to the construction of the
aforementioned new compact 3-dimensional Wintgen ideal
submanifolds with arbitrarily large first Betti number, providing
counterexamples to Theorem
1
for
k=n=3
.

2
An auxiliary lemma

The aim of this section is to prove an algebraic lemma essential
to our results. In the following, let
V
and
W
be
finite-dimensional real vector spaces with dimensions
n\geq 2

and
m\geq 1
respectively. Both spaces are equipped with
positive definite inner products, which, for convenience, we
denote by the same symbol
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
. We
denote the space of all symmetric bilinear forms from
V
to
W

by
\mathrm{Sym}(V\times V,W)
, and the space of all
self-adjoint endomorphisms of
V
by
\mathrm{End}(V)
.
We recall that the
index
of an element
A\in\mathrm{End}(V)

is the number of the negative eigenvalues of
A
, and it is
denoted by
\mathrm{Index}\,A
.
Given
\beta\in\mathrm{Sym}(V\times V,W)
, we associate
to each
\xi\in W
the formal shape operator

A_{\xi}(\beta)\in\mathrm{End}(V)
, defined by

{\langle}\beta(x,y),\xi{\rangle}={\langle}A_{\xi}(\beta)x,y{\rangle},\;\;x,y\in V.

The space
\mathrm{Sym}(V\times V,W)
is a complete metric
space with respect to the norm
\lVert\cdot\rVert
defined by

\|\beta\|^{2}=\sum_{i,j=1}^{n}\|\beta(e_{i},e_{j})\|^{2}=\sum_{a=1}^{m}\|A_{\xi_{a}}(\beta)\|^{2},

where
\{e_{i}\}_{1\leq i\leq n}
and
\{\xi_{a}\}_{1\leq a\leq m}
are
orthonormal bases of
V
and
W
, respectively.

A function
\varphi\colon\mathrm{Sym}(V\times V,W)\to\mathbb{R}
is called

homogeneous of degree

d\in\mathbb{R}^{+}
if

\varphi(t\beta)=t^{d}\phi(\beta)
for every
t\geq 0
and
any
\beta\in\mathrm{Sym}(V\times V,W)
. The following lemma,
that may be useful for other purposes,
is crucial for the proofs of our results.

Lemma 5
.

Let
n\geq 2,m\geq 1,0\leq p<n/2
be integers and
d\in\mathbb{R}^{+}
.
Suppose that there is a map that assigns to
each pair
V,W
of vector spaces of dimensions
n,m
respectively,
and equipped with positive definite inner products, a nonnegative continuous
function
\varphi_{V,W}\colon\mathrm{Sym}(V\times V,W)\to\mathbb{R}

that is homogeneous of degree
d
and satisfies the following conditions:

(i)

For any isometries
\mathsf{i}\colon\tilde{V}\to V
and
\mathsf{j}\colon\tilde{W}\to W
we have

\varphi_{V,W}(\beta)=\varphi_{\tilde{V},\tilde{W}}(\mathsf{j}^{-1}\circ\beta\circ(\mathsf{i}\times\mathsf{i}))\;\;\text{for all}\;\;\beta\in\mathrm{Sym}(V\times V,W).

(ii)

If
\varphi_{V,W}(\beta)=0
for some
\beta\in\mathrm{Sym}(V\times V,W)
,
then the endomorphism
A_{u}(\beta)
has an eigenvalue of multiplicity
at least
n-p
for every unit vector
u\in W
, the vanishing of which
implies that
A_{u}(\beta)=0
.

Then there exists a positive constant

\delta(n,m)
, depending only on
n
and
m
, such
that the following inequality holds for any vector spaces
V,W
and all
\beta\in\mathrm{Sym}(V\times V,W)

\varphi_{V,W}(\beta)\geq\delta(n,m)\left(\psi_{p}(\beta)\right)^{d/n},

where the function
\psi_{p}\colon\mathrm{Sym}(V\times V,W)\to\mathbb{R}
is given by

\psi_{p}(\beta)=\int_{\Lambda_{p}(\beta)}|\det A_{u}(\beta)|dS_{u},\;\;\Lambda_{p}(\beta)=\left\{u\in\mathbb{S}^{m-1}:p<\mathrm{Index}\,A_{u}(\beta)<n-p\right\},

and
dS_{u}
stands for the volume
element
1
1

1

In case
m=1
, integration reduces to summation.

of the unit
(m-1)
-sphere
\mathbb{S}^{m-1}
in
W
.

To simplify the proof of the above lemma, we first prove the following result.

Sublemma 6
.

Let
\{\beta_{r}\}_{r\in\mathord{\mathbb{N}}}
be a sequence in

\psi_{p}^{-1}(1)\subset\mathrm{Sym}(V\times V,W)

and
\{s_{r}\}_{r\in\mathord{\mathbb{N}}}
a sequence
of positive real numbers. Assume that the sequence

\gamma_{r}=s_{r}\beta_{r}

converges to some
\gamma\in\mathrm{Sym}(V\times V,W)

with
\varphi_{V,W}(\gamma)=0
. Then
\gamma=0
.

Proof:

For every
r\in\mathord{\mathbb{N}}
, since
\beta_{r}\in\psi_{p}^{-1}(1)
and

\Lambda_{p}(\beta_{r})=\Lambda_{p}(\gamma_{r})
,
there exists an open subset
\mathcal{U}_{r}\subset\mathbb{S}^{m-1}\subset W

such that
\mathcal{U}_{r}\subset\Lambda_{p}(\gamma_{r})
and

\det A_{\xi}(\gamma_{r})\neq 0
for all
\xi\in\mathcal{U}_{r}
.
Let
\{u_{r}\}
be any convergent sequence such that
u_{r}\in\mathcal{U}_{r}

for all
r\in\mathbb{N}
, and set
u=\lim_{r\rightarrow\infty}u_{r}
.
We claim that

\lim_{r\rightarrow\infty}A_{u_{r}}(\gamma_{r})=0.

(1)

Since
\varphi_{V,W}(\gamma)=0
, by our assumption
A_{u}(\gamma)

has an eigenvalue
\mu(u)
with multiplicity at least
n-p
.

We argue that
\mu(u)=0
. Using
that
\lim_{r\rightarrow\infty}A_{u_{r}}(\gamma_{r})=A_{u}(\gamma)

and since
u_{r}\in\mathcal{U}_{r}
, we obtain

\mathrm{Index}\,A_{u}(\gamma)<n-p
(see
[
A
]
).
Assuming that
\mu(u)<0
, since its multiplicity is at least
n-p
,
it follows that
\mathrm{Index}\,A_{u}(\gamma)\geq n-p
and this
is a contradiction. Therefore
\mu(u)\geq 0
and this implies
that
\mathrm{Index}\,A_{u}(\gamma)\leq p
.
Assuming now that
\mu(u)>0
, from

\lim_{r\rightarrow\infty}A_{u_{r}}(\gamma_{r})=A_{u}(\gamma)
it
follows that
\mathrm{Index}\,A_{u_{r}}(\gamma_{r})\leq p

for
r
large enough, and this contradicts the fact that

u_{r}\in\Lambda_{p}(\gamma_{r})
. Therefore,
\mu(u)=0
and
the second condition for
\varphi_{V,W}
yields that

\lim_{r\rightarrow\infty}A_{u_{r}}(\gamma_{r})=A_{u}(\gamma)=0
.

We may choose convergent sequences

\{u_{r}^{(1)}\},\dots,\{u_{r}^{(m)}\}
in
\mathcal{U}_{r}

such that the vectors
u_{r}^{(1)},\dots,u_{r}^{(m)}
span

W
for all
r\in\mathbb{N}
. Using the Gram-Schmidt process
we obtain sequences
\{\xi_{r}^{(1)}\},\dots,\{\xi_{r}^{(m)}\}
with

\xi_{r}^{(a)}\in{\rm{span}}\{u_{r}^{(1)},\dots,u_{r}^{(a)}\}
for each
1\leq a\leq m
.
Then
\xi_{r}^{(a)}=\sum_{\ell=1}^{a}x_{r}^{(\ell)}u_{r}^{(\ell)}
,
where
\{x_{r}^{(\ell)}\}
are convergent sequences for each

1\leq\ell\leq m
. Consequently, we have that

\gamma_{r}(\cdot,\cdot)=\sum_{a=1}^{m}{\langle}A_{{\xi}_{r}^{(a)}}(\gamma_{r})\cdot,\cdot{\rangle}{\xi}_{r}^{(a)},\;\;\text{where}\;\;A_{{\xi}_{r}^{(a)}}(\gamma_{r})=\sum_{\ell=1}^{a}x_{r}^{(\ell)}A_{u_{r}^{(\ell)}}(\gamma_{r}).

(2)

Using (
1
), we obtain

\lim_{r\rightarrow\infty}A_{{\xi}_{r}^{(a)}}(\gamma_{r})=\lim_{r\rightarrow\infty}\sum_{\ell=1}^{a}x_{r}^{(\ell)}A_{u_{r}^{(\ell)}}(\gamma_{r})=0\;\;\text{for all}\;\;1\leq a\leq m.

From (
2
) and the above it follows that

\lim_{r\rightarrow\infty}\gamma_{r}=0
and thus,
\gamma=0
.

Proof of Lemma
5
.

Let
V,W
be vector spaces as in the statement of the lemma.
We first show that the set
\psi_{p}^{-1}(1)
in nonempty. Indeed,
since the function
\psi_{p}
is homogeneous of degree
n
,
it follows that for an arbitrary
\beta\in\mathrm{Sym}(V\times V,W)
with

\psi_{p}(\beta)\neq 0

\beta/(\psi_{p}(\beta))^{1/n}\in\psi_{p}^{-1}(1)
.

Let
\{\beta_{r}\}
be a sequence in
\psi_{p}^{-1}(1)
such that

\lim_{r\rightarrow\infty}\varphi_{V,W}(\beta_{r})=\inf\varphi_{V,W}(\psi_{p}^{-1}(1))\geq 0.

We claim that the sequence
\{\beta_{r}\}
is bounded.
Arguing indirectly, assume that there exists a subsequence, which by abuse of
notation is again denoted by
\{\beta_{r}\}
, such that

\lim_{r\rightarrow\infty}\|\beta_{r}\|=\infty
.
Since
\beta_{r}\in\psi_{p}^{-1}(1)
implies that
\beta_{r}\neq 0
for all
r\in\mathord{\mathbb{N}}
,
we can consider the sequence
\gamma_{r}=s_{r}\beta_{r}
, where
s_{r}=1/\|\beta_{r}\|
.
Then
\{\gamma_{r}\}
is bounded with
\|\gamma_{r}\|=1
and, by passing to a
subsequence if necessary, we may assume that it converges to some

\gamma\in\mathrm{Sym}(V\times V,W)
with
\|\gamma\|=1
.
From
\varphi_{V,W}(\gamma_{r})=\varphi_{V,W}(\beta_{r})/\|\beta_{r}\|^{d}
it follows that

\lim_{r\rightarrow\infty}\varphi_{V,W}(\gamma_{r})=0
.
Then, the continuity of
\varphi_{V,W}
yields that
\varphi_{V,W}(\gamma)=0

and Sublemma
6
implies that
\gamma=0
.
This contradicts the fact that
\|\gamma\|=1
, and the proof of the claim follows.

Thus we may assume that
\{\beta_{r}\}
converges to some

\beta_{\infty}\in\mathrm{Sym}(V\times V,W)
.
We argue that
\varphi_{V,W}(\beta_{\infty})>0
. Suppose to the contrary
that
\varphi_{V,W}(\beta_{\infty})=0
. Then, Sublemma
6
applied
to
\gamma_{r}=\beta_{r}
yields that
\beta_{\infty}=0
.
Therefore
A_{u}(\beta_{\infty})=0
for every
u\in\mathbb{S}^{m-1}
.
On the other hand, since
\beta_{r}\in\psi_{p}^{-1}(1)
, the mean value
theorem implies that there exists
\xi_{r}\in\mathbb{S}^{m-1}

such that

1=\psi_{p}(\beta_{r})\leq\int_{\mathbb{S}^{m-1}}|\det A_{u}(\beta_{r})|dS_{u}=\left|\det A_{\xi_{r}}(\beta_{r})\right|\mathrm{Vol}(\mathbb{S}^{m-1})\;\;\text{for all}\;\;r\in\mathbb{N}.

Since the sequence
\{\xi_{r}\}
is bounded, we may assume that
it converges to some
\xi\in\mathbb{S}^{m-1}
. Then, by letting
r\to\infty
in
the above, we conclude that
\det A_{\xi}(\beta_{\infty})\neq 0

which contradicts the fact that
A_{u}(\beta_{\infty})=0
for every
u\in\mathbb{S}^{m-1}
.

Thus, the function
\varphi_{V,W}
attains a positive minimum

\delta(n,m)=\varphi_{V,W}(\beta_{\infty})
on
\psi_{p}^{-1}(1)
,
which by condition
(i)
depends only on
n
and
m
.
Therefore, for an arbitrary
\beta\in\mathrm{Sym}(V\times V,W)

with
\psi_{p}(\beta)\neq 0
, since

\gamma=\beta/(\psi_{p}(\beta))^{1/n}\in\psi_{p}^{-1}(1)
,
it follows that
\varphi_{V,W}(\gamma)\geq\delta(n,m)
.
Then, the homogeneity of

implies the desired inequality.

3
Algebraic preliminaries

Let
V
and
W
be real vector spaces of dimensions
n\geq 2

and
m\geq 1
respectively, equipped with positive definite inner products.

The
Kulkarni-Nomizu product
of two bilinear forms

\phi,\psi\in\mathrm{Hom}(V\times V,\mathbb{R})

is the
(0,4)
-tensor

\phi\varowedge\psi\colon V\times V\times V\times V\to\mathbb{R}

defined by

\displaystyle\phi\varowedge\psi(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3},x_{4})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\phi(x_{1},x_{3})\psi(x_{2},x_{4})+\phi(x_{2},x_{4})\psi(x_{1},x_{3})

\displaystyle-\phi(x_{1},x_{4})\psi(x_{2},x_{3})-\phi(x_{2},x_{3})\psi(x_{1},x_{4}).

Using the inner product of
W
, we
extend the Kulkarni-Nomizu product to bilinear forms

\beta,\gamma\in\mbox{Hom}(V\times V,W)
, as the
(0,4)
-tensor

\beta\varowedge\gamma\colon V\times V\times V\times V\to\mathbb{R}

defined by

\displaystyle\beta\varowedge\gamma(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3},x_{4})\!\!\!

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\!\!\!\langle\beta(x_{1},x_{3}),\gamma(x_{2},x_{4})\rangle+\langle\beta(x_{2},x_{4}),\gamma(x_{1},x_{3})\rangle

\displaystyle\!\!\!-\langle\beta(x_{1},x_{4}),\gamma(x_{2},x_{3})\rangle-\langle\beta(x_{2},x_{3}),\gamma(x_{1},x_{4})\rangle.

For any
c\in\mathbb{R}
, we define the formal Ricci tensor as the map

{\sf Ric}_{c}\colon\mathrm{Sym}(V\times V,W)\to\mathrm{Sym}(V\times V,\mathbb{R})

given by

{\sf Ric}_{c}(\beta)(x,y)=\mathrm{tr}\ {\sf R}_{c}(\beta)(\cdot,x,\cdot,y),\;\;x,y\in V,

where

{\sf R}_{c}(\beta)=\frac{1}{2}\big(c{\langle}\cdot,\cdot{\rangle}\varowedge{\langle}\cdot,\cdot{\rangle}+\beta\varowedge\beta\big).

For any
\beta\in\mathrm{Sym}(V\times V,W)
, we denote by

\boldsymbol{\lambda}_{c,1}(\beta)\leq\dots\leq\boldsymbol{\lambda}_{c,n}(\beta)

the eigenvalues of the self-adjoint operator
{\sf T}_{c}(\beta)\in{\rm End}(V)

determined by

{\langle}{\sf T}_{c}(\beta)x,y{\rangle}=\frac{1}{n-1}{\sf Ric}_{c}(\beta)(x,y).

For each
1\leq k\leq n
, we consider the functions

\boldsymbol{\rho}_{c,k}\colon\mathrm{Sym}(V\times V,W)\to\mathbb{R}

defined by

\boldsymbol{\rho}_{c,k}(\beta)=\frac{1}{k}\sum_{i=1}^{k}\boldsymbol{\lambda}_{c,i}(\beta).

For
k=n
, we set
\boldsymbol{\rho}_{c}(\beta)=\boldsymbol{\rho}_{c,n}(\beta)
.
Clearly we have

\boldsymbol{\rho}_{c}(\beta)=\frac{1}{n(n-1)}\mathrm{tr}\ {\sf Ric}_{c}(\beta).

Furthermore, we define the function

\boldsymbol{\rho}^{\perp}\colon\mathrm{Sym}(V\times V,W)\to\mathbb{R}
by

\boldsymbol{\rho}^{\perp}(\beta)=\frac{1}{n(n-1)}\|{\sf R}^{\perp}(\beta)\|=\frac{1}{n(n-1)}\Big(\sum_{i,j=1}^{n}\sum_{a,b=1}^{m}\left({\sf R}^{\perp}(\beta)(e_{i},e_{j},\xi_{a},\xi_{b})\right)^{2}\Big)^{1/2},

where
\{e_{i}\}_{1\leq i\leq n}
and
\{\xi_{a}\}_{1\leq a\leq m}
are orthonormal bases
of
V
and
W
, respectively.
Here, the tensor

{\sf R}^{\perp}(\beta)\colon V\times V\times W\times W\to\mathbb{R}
is given by

{\sf R}^{\perp}(\beta)(x,y,\xi,\eta)={\langle}[A_{\xi}(\beta),A_{\eta}(\beta)]x,y{\rangle},\;\;x,y\in V,\;\xi,\eta\in W,

being
[A,B]=A\circ B-B\circ A
the commutator of
A,B\in{\rm End}(V)
.

For every
\beta\in\mathrm{Sym}(V\times V,W)
, we set

\mathcal{H}_{\beta}=\left(1/n\right)\mathrm{tr}\beta,{\sf H}_{\beta}=\|\mathcal{H}_{\beta}\|

and we denote by
\mathring{\beta}=\beta-{\langle}\cdot,\cdot{\rangle}{\cal H}_{\beta}

the traceless part of
\beta
. The form
\beta
is called
umbilical

if
\mathring{\beta}=0
. It is clear that
\beta
is umbilical if and only if

A_{\xi}(\beta)={\langle}{\cal H}_{\beta},\xi{\rangle}\operatorname{Id}_{V}
for every
\xi\in W
.

Lemma 7
.

For every
\beta\in\mathrm{Sym}(V\times V,W)
and any
c\in\mathbb{R}
,
the following hold:

(i)

The formal Ricci tensor is given by

{\sf Ric}_{c}(\beta)(x,y)=c(n-1){\langle}x,y{\rangle}+n{\langle}{\cal H}_{\beta},\beta(x,y){\rangle}-\sum_{i=1}^{n}{\langle}\beta(x,e_{i}),\beta(y,e_{i}){\rangle},

for

\{e_{i}\}_{1\leq i\leq n}
is an orthonormal basis of

.

(ii)

The DDVV inequality is valid

\boldsymbol{\rho}_{c}(\beta)\leq c+{\sf H}_{\beta}^{2}-\boldsymbol{\rho}^{\perp}(\beta).

\{e_{i}\}_{1\leq i\leq n}

of
V
and

of

A_{\xi_{1}}(\beta)=\operatorname{diag}(\lambda_{1}+\mu,\lambda_{1}-\mu,\lambda_{1},\dots,\lambda_{1}),\;\;A_{\xi_{2}}(\beta)=\operatorname{diag}\left(\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}\lambda_{2}&\mu\\
\mu&\lambda_{2}\end{array}\right),\lambda_{2},\dots,\lambda_{2}\right),

and

for
3\leq a\leq m
, where

\mu,\lambda_{1},\dots,\lambda_{m}
are real numbers.

Proof:

Part (i) follows by a straightforward computation.
For the second part, we choose an orthonormal basis
\{u_{a}\}_{1\leq a\leq m}
of

such that
u_{1}={\cal H}_{\beta}/{\sf H}_{\beta}
if

Let

Using part (i) and taking into account that

(3)

On the other hand, it follows easily that

Therefore, the desired inequality is equivalent to

\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}b_{i}(M^{n};\mathbb{F})=1
, then
M^{n}
is an Eells-Kuiper manifold.

Proof:

The assumption is equivalent to

\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n+m-1}}\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}\mu_{i}(u)dS<2\mathrm{Vol}(\mathbb{S}^{n+m-1}).

This implies that there exists a unit vector
u_{0}
such that the
height function
h_{u_{0}}
is a Morse function satisfying

\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}\mu_{i}(u_{0})<2
, or equivalently

\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}\mu_{i}(u_{0})\leq 1.

(12)

The above and (
9
) yield that
\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}b_{i}(M^{n};\mathbb{F})\in\{0,1\}

for every coefficient field
\mathbb{F}
.

(i)
Suppose that

b_{i}(M^{n};\mathbb{F})=0\;\;{\text{for every}}\;\;1\leq i\leq n-1.

(13)

We claim that the homology groups of
M^{n}
over the integers satisfy

H_{k}(M^{n};\mathord{\mathbb{Z}})=0
for any
1\leq k\leq n-1
, which in view of (
13
),
is equivalent to the claim that
M^{n}
has no torsion. Indeed,
if
H_{k}(M^{n};\mathord{\mathbb{Z}})
contains torsion for some
1\leq k\leq n-1
, then

H_{k}(M^{n};\mathord{\mathbb{Z}}_{p})\neq 0
for some prime
p
, which contradicts (
13
).
Hence
M^{n}
is a homology sphere over the integers.

We now prove that
M^{n}
is simply connected. This is clear if
n=2
.
Assume that
n\geq 3
and suppose, to the contrary,
that the fundamental group is
\pi_{1}(M^{n})\neq 0
.
It follows from
[
AD
, Proposition 4.5.7, p. 90]
that
\mu_{1}(u_{0})\neq 0
.
Thus, (
12
) yields that
\mu_{1}(u_{0})=1
and
\mu_{i}(u_{0})=0

for any
2\leq i\leq n-1
. Then, by Morse theory, it follows that the
manifold
M^{n}
has the homotopy type of a CW-complex with no cells
of dimension
2\leq i\leq n-1
. In particular, there are no
2
-cells,
and thus, by the cellular approximation theorem, the inclusion
of the
1
-skeleton
\mathrm{X}^{(1)}\hookrightarrow M^{n}

induces isomorphism between the fundamental groups. Therefore,

\pi_{1}(M^{n})
is a free group on
b_{1}(M^{n};\mathord{\mathbb{Z}})=0
elements. Hence,

\pi_{1}(M^{n})=0
, which is a contradiction.

Thus,
M^{n}
is a simply connected homology sphere over the
integers and therefore a homotopy sphere. By the (generalized)
Poincaré conjecture (Smale
n\geq 5
, Freedman
n=4
,
Perelman
n=3
),
M^{n}
is
homeomorphic to
\mathbb{S}^{n}
.

(ii)
Assume that
\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}b_{i}(M^{n};\mathbb{F})=1.

From Poincaré duality it follows that
n
is even and the Betti
numbers are

b_{n/2}(M^{n};\mathbb{F})=1\;\;\mbox{and}\;\;b_{i}(M^{n};\mathbb{F})=0,\;1\leq i\leq n-1,\;i\neq n/2.

Jointly with (
9
) and (
12
), the above implies that

\mu_{n/2}(u_{0})=1\;\;\mbox{and}\;\;\mu_{i}(u_{0})=0,\;1\leq i\leq n-1,\;i\neq n/2.

(14)

Since
\mu_{1}(u_{0})=0
, it follows from
[
CE75
, Lemma 4.11, p. 85]

that
\mu_{0}(u_{0})\leq 1
. Hence from (
9
), we obtain

\mu_{0}(u_{0})=1.

(15)

The Euler-Poincaré characteristic of

M^{n}
is given by

\chi(M^{n})=\sum_{i=0}^{n}(-1)^{i}b_{i}(M^{n};\mathbb{F})=3.

On the other hand, using (
14
) and (
15
), from
[
Milnor63
, Theorem 5.2, p. 29]
we have

\chi(M^{n})=\sum_{i=0}^{n}(-1)^{i}\mu_{i}(u_{0})=2+\mu_{n}(u_{0}),

and thus
\mu_{n}(u_{0})=1
. Taking into account (
14
) and
(
15
), this implies that the height function
h_{u_{0}}
is a Morse function with
three critical points. Therefore,
M^{n}
is an Eells-Kuiper manifold.

\tilde{f}\colon M^{n}\to\mathbb{R}^{n+m+1}

Proof of Theorem

where

and

its normal curvature tensor.

\widetilde{\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle}=e^{2u}\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle

\alpha_{\tilde{f}}=\alpha_{f}-\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle(\mbox{grad\,}u)^{\perp}\;\;\text{and}\;\;{\cal H}_{\tilde{f}}=e^{-2u}\left({\cal H}_{f}-(\mbox{grad\,}u)^{\perp}\right),

\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle

\tilde{f},f

5
Counterexamples to Theorem
1
for
k=n

5.1
Minimal surfaces in spheres

In this subsection, we discuss some properties of the isotropic surfaces
in spheres, which are the basic tool for the construction of new
compact 3-dimensional Wintgen ideal submanifolds.

Let
f\colon L^{2}\to\mathbb{S}^{n+2}
denote an isometric immersion of a
two-dimensional oriented Riemannian manifold into the sphere
\mathbb{S}^{n+2}
.
The
k^{th}
-normal space
of
f
at
x\in L^{2}
for
k\geq 1
is
given by

N^{f}_{k}(x)=\mbox{span}\big\{\alpha_{f}^{k+1}(X_{1},\ldots,X_{k+1}):X_{1},\ldots,X_{k+1}\in T_{x}L\big\}

where
\alpha_{f}^{2}=\alpha_{f}\colon TL\times TL\to N_{f}L
is the standard
second fundamental form with values in the normal bundle and

\alpha_{f}^{s}\colon TL\times\cdots\times TL\to N_{f}L,\;\;s\geq 3,

is the symmetric tensor called
the
s^{th}
-fundamental form
defined inductively by

\alpha_{f}^{s}(X_{1},\ldots,X_{s})=\big(\nabla^{\perp}_{X_{s}}\ldots\nabla^{\perp}_{X_{3}}\alpha_{f}(X_{2},X_{1})\big)^{\perp}.

Here
\nabla^{\perp}
is the induced connection in the normal bundle
N_{f}L

and
(\;\;)^{\perp}
means taking the projection onto the normal complement of

N^{f}_{1}\oplus\cdots\oplus N^{f}_{s-2}
in
N_{f}L
.

Now suppose that
f\colon L^{2}\to\mathbb{S}^{n+2}
denotes an oriented minimal and
substantial surface. The latter means that the codimension cannot
be reduced, in fact, not even locally since minimal surfaces are real
analytic. It is known (cf.
[
v
,
df
]
) that the normal bundle

N_{f}L
of
f
splits along an open dense subset of
L^{2}
as

N_{f}L=N_{1}^{f}\oplus\dots\oplus N_{m}^{f},\;\;\;m=[(n+1)/2],

since all higher normal bundles have rank two except possibly the last
one that has rank one if
n
is odd. The orientation of
L^{2}
induces
an orientation on each plane vector bundle
N_{s}^{f}
given by the
ordered pair

\xi_{1}^{s}=\alpha_{f}^{s+1}(X,\ldots,X),\;\;\;\xi_{2}^{s}=\alpha_{f}^{s+1}(JX,\ldots,X)

where
0\neq X\in TL
and
J
is the complex structure of
L^{2}

determined by the metric and orientation.

For each
1\leq k\leq m
, the
k^{th}
-order curvature ellipse

{\cal E}^{f}_{k}(x)\subset N^{f}_{k}(x)
is defined by

{\cal E}^{f}_{k}(x)=\big\{\alpha_{f}^{k+1}(Z^{\varphi},\ldots,Z^{\varphi})\colon\,Z^{\varphi}=\cos\varphi Z+\sin\varphi JZ\;\mbox{and}\;\varphi\in[0,2\pi)\big\}

where
Z\in T_{x}L
is any vector of unit length.

A surface
f\colon L^{2}\to\mathbb{S}^{n+2}
is called

r
-
isotropic
if it is minimal and at any
x\in L^{2}

and for any
1\leq k\leq r
the ellipses of curvature
{\cal E}^{f}_{k}(x)

contained in all two-dimensional
N^{f}_{k}
\,{}^{\prime}
s
are circles.
We point out that there are alternative ways to define
isotropy for surfaces, for instance, in terms of the vanishing of higher order Hopf
differentials
[
v1
]
.

We consider the open and dense subset
L_{1}
of
L^{2}
defined by

L_{1}=\big\{x\in L^{2}:\dim N^{f}_{1}(x)\text{ is maximal}\big\}.

Then
the 1st normal spaces along
L_{1}
form a subbundle
N^{f}_{1}|_{L_{1}}

of the normal bundle
N_{f}L
. Inductively, we define the open and dense
subset

L_{s}=\big\{x\in L_{s-1}:\dim N^{f}_{s}(x)\text{ is maximal}\big\}

and similarly the
s
-th normal spaces along
L_{s}
form a subbundle

N^{f}_{s}|_{L_{s}}
of the normal bundle. The following result was proved
in
[
11
]
(see also
[
v
,
dv
]
).

Proposition 14
.

If the surface
f
is
r
-isotropic, then each

L^{2}\smallsetminus L_{s},1\leq s\leq r
, consists of isolated points
and the vector bundle
N^{f}_{s}|_{L_{s}}
smoothly extends
to a vector bundle over
L^{2}
still denoted by
N^{f}_{s}
.

5.2
A class of compact 3-dimensional Wintgen submanifolds

In the sequel, let
f\colon L^{2}\to\mathbb{S}^{2n+2},n\geq 2
, be a substantial

(n-1)
-isotropic
surface. Then Proposition
14
implies
that each plane bundle
N^{f}_{s}|_{L_{s}},1\leq s\leq n-1
, smoothly extends
to a plane bundle over
L^{2}
. Clearly,
N^{f}_{n}
is the orthogonal complement of

N^{f}_{1}\oplus\dots\oplus N^{f}_{n-1}
in
N_{f}L
.
There are plenty of compact
(n-1)
-isotropic surfaces in
\mathbb{S}^{2n+2},n\geq 2
.
This is the case of pseudoholomorphic curves in the nearly
Kähler
\mathbb{S}^{6}
. In fact, it is known that they are
1
-isotropic
[
8
,
19
,
v1
]
.
Moreover, there are compact substantial pseudoholomorphic curves in the nearly
Kähler
\mathbb{S}^{6}
of positive genus (see
[
8
]
). Additionally, all
1
-isotropic tori
in
\mathbb{S}^{6}
were described in
[
5
]
.

The following result provides a method to produce new compact Wintgen ideal
submanifolds.

Theorem 15
.

Let
f\colon L^{2}\to\mathbb{S}^{2n+2},n\geq 2
, be a compact oriented substantial

(n-1)
-isotropic surface of genus
g(L)
and let
M^{3}
be
the total space of the unit bundle
p\colon UN^{f}_{n}\rightarrow L^{2}
of
the plane bundle
N^{f}_{n}
.
Then
\phi_{f}\colon M^{3}\to\mathbb{S}^{2n+2}
given by

\phi_{f}(x,w)=w
is a minimal Wintgen ideal submanifold,
whose first Betti number satisfies
b_{1}(M^{3})\geq 2g(L)
.

Proof:
It follows from Proposition 4.2 in
[
15
]

that the map
\phi_{f}\colon M^{3}\to\mathbb{S}^{2n+2}
is a minimal immersion
of rank two with polar surface
f
. Moreover, Proposition 8 in
[
df
]
implies
that the first curvature ellipse (as defined in
[
df
]
) of
\phi_{f}
is a circle at any point. It follows
from Corollary 1.2 in
[
GT
]
that a minimal
n
-dimensional submanifold
of a space form with relative nullity
n-2
is a Wintgen ideal submanifold if
and only if its first curvature ellipse is a circle at any point. Hence
\phi_{f}

is a Wintgen ideal submanifold.

Now we argue that
b_{1}(M^{3})\geq 2g(L)
.
Clearly, the circle bundle
p\colon UN^{f}_{n}\rightarrow L^{2}

is oriented (as defined in
[
BT
, p. 114]
).
Then we have from
[
B
, Theorem 13.2, p. 390]

or
[
Hat
, p. 438]
that the cohomology rings of

M^{3}
and
L^{2}
are related by the following long exact
sequence known as the Gysin sequence:

\!\!\!\cdots\xrightarrow{\Psi_{i-1}}H^{i}(L;\mathord{\mathbb{Z}})\xrightarrow{p^{*}_{i}}H^{i}(M;\mathord{\mathbb{Z}})\xrightarrow{\Phi_{i}}H^{i-1}(L;\mathord{\mathbb{Z}})\xrightarrow{\Psi_{i}}H^{i+1}(L;\mathord{\mathbb{Z}})\xrightarrow{p^{*}_{i+1}}\cdots

The exactness gives that
p^{*}_{1}
is a monomorphism
and
{\rm{Im}}\,p^{*}_{1}=\ker\Phi_{1}
. On the other hand we have

{\rm{Im}}\,p^{*}_{1}\cong H^{1}(L;\mathord{\mathbb{Z}})/\ker\,p^{*}_{1}\cong H^{1}(L;\mathord{\mathbb{Z}})\cong\mathord{\mathbb{Z}}^{2g(L)}.

Thus
\ker\Phi_{1}\cong\mathord{\mathbb{Z}}^{2g(L)}
. The desired inequality follows
from the fact that
H^{1}(M;\mathord{\mathbb{Z}})
contains a subgroup isomorphic to

\mathord{\mathbb{Z}}^{2g(L)}
.

Theorem
15
shows that each compact oriented substantial

(n-1)
-isotropic surface
f\colon L^{2}\to\mathbb{S}^{2n+2},n\geq 2
, of
positive genus
g(L)
gives rise to a compact 3-dimensional Wintgen ideal
submanifold with positive first Betti number. For instance,
f
can be chosen
to be a compact substantial pseudoholomorphic curve in the nearly
Kähler
\mathbb{S}^{6}
of positive genus (see
[
8
]
), or a
1
-isotropic torus
in
\mathbb{S}^{6}
as described in
[
5
]
. Clearly, these Wintgen ideal submanifolds
violate the inequality in Theorem
1
for
k=n=3
.

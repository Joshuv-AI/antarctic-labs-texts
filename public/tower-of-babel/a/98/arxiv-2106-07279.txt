On a nonhierarchical generalization of the Perceptron GREM Thanks: This work has been supported by a DFG research grant, contract number 2337/1-1.
Nicola Kistler, Giulia Sebastiani

Abstract
. We introduce a nonlinear, nonhierarchical generalization of Derrida’s GREM and establish through a Sanov-type large deviation analysis both a Boltzmann-Gibbs principle as well as a Parisi formula for the limiting free energy. In line with the predictions of the Parisi theory, the free energy is given by the minimal value over all Parisi functionals/hierarchical structures in which the original model can be coarse-grained.

1.
Introduction

The study of mean field spin glasses, started by physicists in the 1970’s and culminated in the Parisi theory
[
6
]
, has over time revealed a rich and challenging mathematical structure. Despite the enormous progress over the last decades, the whole Parisi picture and its presumed universality for mean field models still remains not fully understood. Indeed, for the prototypical models of the theory such as the Sherrington-Kirkpatrick [SK] model
[
15
]
, a rigorous treatment via large deviations techniques within the classical Gibbs-Boltzmann formalism is still lacking, and one has to resort to sophisticated machinery, in particular Guerra’s interpolations
[
8
,
16
]
and the Ghirlanda-Guerra identities
[
7
]
. These tools are remarkably efficient but cannot provide, by their own nature, insights for given realization of the disorder. We refer the reader to
[
9
]
and references therein for a comprehensive account of the state of the art for the SK-model
1
1

1

A treatment of spin glasses which is somewhat complementary to the Parisi theory goes through the TAP-Plefka framework
[
17
,
11
]
. The rigorous studies which bypass the interpolations/GG-identities employ the Kac-Rice formula and some ensuing large deviations estimates for the complexity/number of critical points of Gaussian random fields. This type of analysis is to date however limited to spherical models with REM-like ultrametric structure, see
[
13
,
14
]
and references.
.

One of the most puzzling features of the Parisi theory is the
ultrametricity
, namely the emergence in the infinite volume limit of hierarchical structures closely related to the generalized random energy models, the GREM, introduced by Derrida in the 1980’s
[
4
,
5
]
. For a large class of SK-type Hamiltonians, the ultrametricity has meanwhile been put on rigorous ground by Panchenko
[
10
]
. The analysis relies crucially on the Ghirlanda-Guerra identities.

In order to shed some light on the
origin
of ultrametricity, simplified models for spin glasses which are amenable to a rigorous solution via large deviation techniques have been introduced by Bolthausen and the first author in
[
1
,
2
,
3
]
: the first paper deals with a nonhierarchical yet linear superposition of Derrida’s GREMs, whereas the second paper analyses a non-linear version of the GREM which is closely related to the perceptron model from neural networks
[
6
]
and which we refer to henceforth as
Perceptron GREM
.

In the present paper we introduce a random Hamiltonian which is neither hierarchical from the start nor given by linear superpositions of GREM-like models. For such nonhierarchical, nonlinear generalization of the random energy models we establish a quenched, Sanov-type large deviation principle for the empirical distribution of the random energies which in turn allows to derive a Boltzmann-Gibbs principle, as well as a Parisi formula for the free energy in the thermodynamical limit. The limiting free energy turns out to be given by the minimal value over
multiple
nonlinear Parisi functionals / hierarchical structures.

What is perhaps more, our analysis suggests that the onset of ultrametricity is intimately related to certain monotonicity properties of relative entropies/Kullback-Leibler functionals, and shows how these monotonicities lead to the optimal balance in the energy-entropy competition.

We emphasize that the spin glass we investigate here is
nonlinear
insofar the sub-energies interact via a perceptron-like function, see (
2.2
) below for the definition of the model, but the Hamiltonian still is a linear functional of the empirical measures of the random energies. The case of nonlinear functionals, which give rise to models which better approximate realistic spin glasses such as the SK-model, will be addressed in a forthcoming work.

Let

We first need some infrastructure: for an open subset
U\subset\mathcal{M}^{+}_{1}(S^{2^{n}-1})

The following two lemmata state some well known properties of relative entropies. The short and elementary proofs are given for completeness.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
S
be a Polish space,
d\in\mathbb{N}
,
\nu,\mu\in\mathcal{M}^{+}_{1}\left(S^{d}\right)
with
\nu\ll\mu
,
\mu=\bigotimes_{k=1}^{d}\mu_{k}
for some
\mu_{k}\in\mathcal{M}^{+}_{1}(S)
,
k\in\{1,\cdots,d\}
. Then for every nonempty
B\subseteq\{1,\cdots,d\}
,

H(\nu\,|\,\mu)=H\left(\,\nu^{(B)}\,|\,\mu^{(B)}\,\right)+H\left(\,\nu\,|\,\nu^{(B)}\otimes\mu^{(B^{c})}\,\right)

(3.1)

H\left(\nu^{(B)}\,|\,\mu^{(B)}\right)\leq H\left(\nu\,|\,\mu\right)

(3.2)

where
B^{c}=\{1,\cdots,d\}\setminus B
.

Proof.

The second claim (
3.2
) is an immediate consequence of (
3.1
), together with the positivity of relative entropies. In order to see the first claim, let
\{S_{k}\}_{k=1}^{d}
be
d
copies of
S
and
B=\{b_{1},\cdots,b_{M}\}
,
B^{c}=\{a_{1},\cdots,a_{d-M}\}
.
Since the product space of Polish spaces is Polish, specifically Radon,the disintegration Theorem holds for every
\nu\in\mathcal{M}^{+}_{1}\left(S^{d}\right)
and we can define the conditional distribution
\nu^{(B^{c}|B)}
of
\nu
on
S_{a_{1}}\times\cdots\times S_{a_{d-M}}
given the projection on
S_{b_{1}}\times\cdots\times S_{b_{M}}
. Expressing
\nu=\nu^{(B)}\otimes\nu^{(B^{c}\mid B)}
as a semi-direct product, it holds

\displaystyle H(\nu\,|\,\mu)=\int\log\left(\frac{d\nu}{d\mu}\right)d\nu

\displaystyle=\int\log\left(\frac{d\nu^{(B)}}{d\mu^{(B)}}\right)d\nu+\int\log\left(\frac{d\nu^{(B^{c}|B)}}{d\mu^{(B^{c})}}\right)d\nu

(3.3)

\displaystyle=H\left(\,\nu^{(B)}\,|\,\mu^{(B)}\,\right)+\int\log\left(\frac{d\nu^{(B^{c}|B)}}{d\mu^{(B^{c})}}\right)d\nu\,.

We now focus on the second term on the r.h.s. above: by well-known properties of Radon-Nikodym derivatives
4
4

4

This step is somewhat informal, but see e.g.
[
12
]
.
we may write

\displaystyle\frac{d\nu^{(B^{c}|B)}}{d\mu^{(B^{c})}}

\displaystyle=\frac{d\nu^{(B)}}{d\nu^{(B)}}\cdot\frac{d\nu^{(B^{c}|B)}}{d\mu^{(B^{c})}}=\frac{d\nu}{d\left(\nu^{(B)}\otimes\mu^{(B^{c})}\right)}\,,

(3.4)

and therefore

\int\log\left(\frac{d\nu^{(B^{c}|B)}}{d\mu^{(B^{c})}}\right)d\nu=\int\log\left(\frac{d\nu}{d\left(\nu^{(B)}\otimes\mu^{(B^{c})}\right)}\right)d\nu=H\left(\,\nu\,|\,\nu^{(B)}\otimes\mu^{(B^{c})}\,\right).

(3.5)

Plugging this in (
3.3
) then yields (
3.1
).

∎

Lemma 3.2
.

(Semicontinuity of relative entropies)
With the notation of the previous Lemma, the functions
\nu\to H(\nu\,|\,\mu)
,
\nu\to H\left(\,\nu\,|\,\nu^{(B)}\otimes\mu^{(B^{c})}\,\right)
are lower semicontinuous in the weak topology.

Proof.

It is well known that for relative entropies the following representations hold:

H(\nu\,|\,\mu)=\sup\limits_{u\in\mathcal{U}}\left[\int ud\nu-\log\int e^{u}d\mu\right],

(3.6)

where
\mathcal{U}
is the set of all bounded continous functions

, and analogously for

H\left(\,\nu\,|\,\nu^{(B)}\otimes\mu^{(B^{c})}\,\right)=\sup\limits_{u\in\mathcal{U}}\left[\int ud\nu-\log\int e^{u}d\left(\nu^{(B)}\otimes\mu^{(B^{c})}\right)\right].

(3.7)

But for

, the functionals

\nu\to\int ud\nu,\quad\nu\to\log\int e^{u}d\mu,\quad\nu\to\log\int e^{u}d\left(\nu^{(B)}\otimes\mu^{(B^{c})}\right)

are all continuous: the claim of the Lemma thus steadily follows from this observation and the representations

3.6
) and (
3.7
).

Proof.

be a Polish space,

,

\mathcal{P}^{\prime}=\{b_{1},\cdots,b_{M}\}\subseteq\{1,\cdots,d\},\mathcal{P}^{\prime}\neq\emptyset,

and
\{\bm{X}_{i}\}
,
\{\bm{Y}_{i}\}

We consider the empirical distribution

associated to vectors

and the natural projections

\displaystyle\pi^{\prime}

S^{\prime}_{k}\quad&\text{if}\quad k\notin\mathcal{P}\\
\end{cases}\quad,\qquad\mathcal{P}\overset{\text{\text{def}}}{=}\{1,\cdots,d\}\setminus\mathcal{P}^{\prime}=\{c_{1},c_{2},\cdots,c_{d-M}\}.

We define

(L_{N},R_{N})\overset{\text{\text{def}}}{=}M_{N}\circ(\pi,\pi^{\prime})^{-1},

which is a random element in
\mathcal{M}_{1}^{+}(S_{1}\times\cdots\times S_{d})\times\mathcal{M}_{1}^{+}(\tilde{S}_{1}\times\cdots\times\tilde{S}_{d})
, and

\mathfrak{J}(\nu,\theta)\overset{\text{\text{def}}}{=}\begin{cases}H(\nu^{\left(\mathcal{P}\right)}\,|\,\mu^{\left(\mathcal{P}\right)})+H\left(\,\nu\,|\,\nu^{\left(\mathcal{P}\right)}\otimes\mu^{\left(\mathcal{P}^{\prime}\right)}\,\right)+H\left(\,\theta\,|\,\theta^{\left(\mathcal{P}\right)}\otimes\mu^{\prime}\,\right)&\mbox{if}\quad\nu^{\left(\mathcal{P}\right)}=\theta^{\left(\mathcal{P}\right)}\\
\infty&\mbox{otherwise.}\end{cases}

(3.11)

Applying Sanov’s Theorem to (

), and by continuous projection, we obtain that the couple

and the following implication is immediate

\nu^{(\mathcal{P})}\neq\theta^{(\mathcal{P})}\Longrightarrow\mathfrak{J}^{\prime}(\nu,\theta)=\infty.

We may thus henceforth assume
\nu^{(\mathcal{P})}=\theta^{(\mathcal{P})}
, and consider
\tilde{\rho}=\tilde{\rho}(\nu,\theta)
with marginal
\nu^{(\mathcal{P})}=\theta^{(\mathcal{P})}
on

, and conditional distribution on
S_{b_{1}}\times\cdots\times S_{b_{M}}\times S^{\prime}_{b_{1}}\times\cdots\times S^{\prime}_{b_{M}}
given the projection on
S_{c_{1}}\times\cdots\times S_{c_{d-M}}
as the product of the conditional distribution of
\nu
on
S_{b_{1}}\times\cdots\times S_{b_{M}}
given the projection on
S_{c_{1}}\times\cdots\times S_{c_{d-M}}
, and of the conditional distribution
\theta
on
S^{\prime}_{b_{1}}\times\cdots\times S^{\prime}_{b_{M}}
given the projection on
S_{c_{1}}\times\cdots\times S_{c_{d-M}}
. In which case, applying (

H\left(\,\tilde{\rho}\,|\,\mu\otimes\mu^{\prime}\,\right)=H\left(\,\nu^{(\mathcal{P})}\,|\,\mu^{(\mathcal{P})}\,\right)+H\left(\,\nu\,|\,\nu^{(\mathcal{P})}\otimes\mu^{(\mathcal{P}^{\prime})}\,\right)+H\left(\,\theta\,|\,\theta^{(\mathcal{P})}\otimes\mu^{\prime}\,\right)

and therefore
\mathfrak{J}\geq\mathfrak{J}^{\prime}
. Conversely, for any
\rho

\rho\pi^{-1}=\nu
,
\rho\pi^{\prime-1}=\theta
, we claim that

(3.12)

(Remark that we can assume that the right hand side is finite). To see this, we first write

H\left(\,\rho\,|\,\mu\otimes\mu^{\prime}\,\right)=H\left(\,\rho\,|\,\tilde{\rho}(\nu,\theta)\,\right)+\int d\rho\log\frac{d\tilde{\rho}(\nu,\theta)}{d(\mu\otimes\mu^{\prime})}.

The first term is non-negative, while the second equals

\int d\tilde{\rho}(\nu,\theta)\log\frac{d\tilde{\rho}(\nu,\theta)}{d(\mu\otimes\mu^{\prime})}=\mathfrak{J}(\nu,\theta),

and (
3.12
) follows. The statement of the Lemma corresponds then to the case

d=2^{n}-1,\qquad\mathcal{P}^{\prime}=\mathcal{P}_{A}^{c}.

Proposition 3.4
.

,
V
we may find

the last step by Sanov’s theorem together with (

).

The claim of the Proposition steadily follows from Markov’s inequality, and

Proof of Theorem

.

We begin with a couple of elementary observations.

•

First recall that for
J\in\mathcal{P}_{n}
,

C_{J}=\left\{\nu\in\mathcal{M}^{+}_{1}(S^{2^{n}-1}):H(\nu^{(\mathcal{P}_{J})}\,|\,\mu^{(\mathcal{P}_{J})})\leq\frac{|J|}{n}\log 2\right\}\,.

(3.33)

These sets are compact thanks to the semicontinuity of

from Lemma

. It thus follows that the intersection
\bigcap_{J\in\mathcal{P}_{n}}C_{J}\subset\mathcal{M}^{+}_{1}(S^{2^{n}-1})

•

It is well known that
\nu\to H(\nu\,|\,\mu)
is convex.

•

It clearly holds that
\mu\in\bigcap_{J\in\mathcal{P}_{n}}C_{J}

Given a convex combination
\nu_{\lambda}\overset{\text{\text{def}}}{=}\lambda\mu+(1-\lambda)\nu^{\prime}

We also know that as

\int\phi d\nu_{\lambda}-H(\nu_{\lambda}\,|\,\mu)\geq\int\phi d\nu^{\prime}-H(\nu^{\prime}\,|\,\mu)-\epsilon.

Moreover, since the map

3.6

\nu_{\lambda}

\delta>0

\prob\left[M_{N}(U)\leq 2^{N}e^{-H(\nu_{\lambda}\,|\,\mu)N-\epsilon N)}\right]\leq e^{-\delta N}.

With this choice, we thus have for the partition function of our spin glass (

\displaystyle Z_{N}(\phi)

\displaystyle=2^{-N}\sum\limits_{\alpha=1}^{2^{N}}\exp\left[N\int\phi(x)L_{N,\alpha}(dx)\right]

\displaystyle\geq\exp\left[N\int\phi(x)\nu_{\lambda}(dx)-\epsilon N\right]\exp\left[-H(\nu_{\lambda}\,|\,\mu)N-\epsilon N\right]

almost surely. This settles the ”first half of the theorem”, i.e. the lower bound to the free energy.

In order to establish the upper bound, we distinguish two cases:

i)

For

(which is possible thanks to the aforementioned continuity of the map
\rho\mapsto\int\phi(x)\rho(dx)
).

ii)

For
\nu\in C_{I}\setminus\left(\bigcap\limits_{J\in\mathcal{P}_{n}}C_{J}\right)
we still have
H(\nu\,|\,\mu)\leq\log 2
and we can resort to Proposition
3.5
to find
r_{\nu}>0
such that

\prob\left[M_{N}(B_{r_{\nu}}(\nu))\neq 0\right]\leq e^{-\delta_{\nu}N},

(3.37)

for large enough
N
, and some
\delta_{\nu}>0
.

Using that
C_{I}\subset\bigcup_{\nu\in C_{I}}B_{r_{\nu}}(\nu)
, by compactness we may extract a finite sequence
\{\nu_{j}\}_{j=1}^{m}\subset C_{I}
for which covering still holds, to wit

C_{I}\subset\bigcup_{j=1}^{m}B_{r_{j}}(\nu_{j}),

where we have also shortened
r_{j}\overset{\text{\text{def}}}{=}r_{\nu_{j}}
. Moreover, if we set

U\overset{\text{\text{def}}}{=}\bigcup_{j=1}^{m}B_{r_{j}}(\nu_{j}),\qquad\delta\overset{\text{\text{def}}}{=}\min\limits_{j\in\{1,\cdots,m\}}\delta_{\nu_{j}},

it follows from Sanov theorem that

\limsup\limits_{N\to\infty}\frac{1}{N}\log\prob\left[L_{N,\alpha}\notin U\right]\leq-\inf\limits_{\nu\notin U}H(\nu\,|\,\mu)<-\log 2.

(3.38)

Splitting the contributions to the partition function then steadily yields the upper bound

\displaystyle Z_{N}(\phi)

\displaystyle\leq 2^{-N}\sum_{l=1}^{m}\,\sum\limits_{\alpha:L_{N,\alpha}\in B_{r_{l}}(\nu_{l})}\,\exp{\left[N\int\phi(x)L_{N,\alpha}(dx)\right]}+

(3.39)

\displaystyle+2^{-N}\sum\limits_{\alpha:L_{N,\alpha}\notin U}\exp{\left[\int\phi(x)L_{N,\alpha}(dx)\right]}\,.

By (
3.38
), the second summand yields no contribution in the large
N
-limit. Analogously, (
3.37
) establishes that the same applies to those terms in the first summand for which
\nu_{l}\notin\bigcap\limits_{J\in\mathcal{P}_{n}}C_{J}
. All in all we have

\displaystyle Z_{N}

\displaystyle\leq 2^{-N}\sum\limits_{l:\,\,\nu_{l}\in\bigcap\limits_{J\in\mathcal{P}_{n}}C_{J}}\,\,\sum\limits_{\alpha:L_{N,\alpha}\in B_{r_{l}}(\nu_{l})}\,\exp{\left[N\int\phi(x)L_{N,\alpha}(dx)\right]}

(3.40)

\displaystyle\leq e^{\epsilon N}\sum\limits_{l:\,\,\nu_{l}\in\bigcap\limits_{J\in\mathcal{P}_{n}}C_{J}}\exp{\left[N\int\phi d\nu_{l}\right]}M_{N}\left(B_{r_{l}}(\nu_{l})\right)

\displaystyle\leq e^{2\epsilon N}\sum\limits_{l:\,\,\nu_{l}\in\bigcap\limits_{J\in\mathcal{P}_{n}}C_{J}}\exp{\left[N\int\phi d\nu_{l}-NH(\nu_{l}\,|\,\mu)\right]}

\displaystyle\leq e^{2\epsilon N}m\exp{\left[N\sup\limits_{\nu\in\bigcap\limits_{J\in\mathcal{P}_{n}}C_{J}}\left\{\int\phi d\nu-H(\nu\,|\,\mu)\right\}\right]}

almost surely, for large enough
N
. Since
\epsilon
is arbitrary we thus have

\limsup\limits_{N\to\infty}F_{N}(\phi)=\limsup\limits_{N\to\infty}\frac{1}{N}\log Z_{N}(\phi)\leq\sup\limits_{\nu\in\bigcap\limits_{J\in\mathcal{P}_{n}}C_{J}}\left\{\int\phi d\nu-H(\nu\,|\,\mu)\right\},

almost surely. This being the sought upperbound, Theorem
2.1
follows.
∎

Coming back to our main task, a proof that the minimal chain satisfies all side constraints in the Boltzmann-Gibbs principle, let

In light of (

First case:
\partial_{k_{J}}P^{\mathcal{T}}(\bm{m}^{\prime})\leq 0

i)

When

on

\displaystyle H\left((G^{\mathcal{T}})^{(\mathcal{P}_{J})}\,|\,\mu^{(\mathcal{P}_{J})}\right)

ii)

For sets of cardinality

hence by the monotonicity property (

\displaystyle

\displaystyle H\left((G^{\mathcal{T}})^{(\mathcal{P}_{J})}\,|\,\mu^{(\mathcal{P}_{J})}\right)\leq H\left((G^{\mathcal{T}})^{(\mathcal{P}_{{\mathfrak{O}}}\,\cup\,\mathcal{T}_{k_{J}})}\,|\,\mu^{(\mathcal{P}_{{\mathfrak{O}}}\,\cup\,\mathcal{T}_{k_{J}})}\right)

\displaystyle H\left((G^{\mathcal{T}})^{(\mathcal{P}_{{\mathfrak{O}}}\,\cup\,\mathcal{T}_{k_{J}})}\,|\,(G^{\mathcal{T}})^{(\mathcal{P}_{\mathfrak{O}})}\otimes\mu^{(\mathcal{T}_{k_{J}})}\right)

Second case:
\partial_{k_{J}}P^{\mathcal{T}}(\bm{m}^{\prime})>0

\displaystyle P^{\mathcal{T}}(\bm{m}^{\prime})

\displaystyle=\frac{\log 2}{n}\sum_{k=1}^{n}\frac{1}{m_{k}^{\prime}}+\phi_{0}^{\mathcal{T}}(\bm{m}^{\prime})=\frac{\log 2}{n}\sum_{k=1}^{n}\frac{1}{m_{k}^{\prime}}+\phi_{0}^{\mathcal{R}}(\bm{m}^{\prime})=P^{\mathcal{R}}(\bm{m}^{\prime})\,,

(4.36)

and by similar reasoning,

From (

\mathcal{T}
, i.e.

\displaystyle=H\left(\,[G^{\mathcal{R}}(\bm{m}^{\prime\prime})]^{(\mathcal{P}_{M}^{\mathcal{R}})}\,|\,\mu^{(\mathcal{P}_{M}^{\mathcal{R}})}\,\right)\leq\frac{M}{n}\log 2,

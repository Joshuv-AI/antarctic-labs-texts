Abstract
. The present paper refers to the knot theory and is devoted to the study of global properties of Gordian graphs of various local moves. In 2005, Gambaudo and Ghys raised the question of the behavior at infinity of the crossing change Gordian graph. They proposed studying its “ends”, that is, unbounded connected components of complements of bounded subsets. We provide a complete description of the behavior at infinity for local moves from three well-known infinite families, namely, rational moves, C ⁡ ( n ) \Cm(n) -moves, and H ⁡ ( n ) \Hm(n) -moves (note that each of the first two families contains the crossing change). Also, in 2005, Marché gave a different perspective on the behavior of Gordian graphs at infinity, proposing to consider complements of finite subsets. We describe the behavior at infinity in this sense for all local moves with the infinite neighborhood of the unknot in the corresponding Gordian graph.

The present paper refers to the classical theory of knots and is devoted to the study of knot transformations. A knot and link transformation is any geometric procedure that transforms a given link into some new link, possibly the same. The most well-studied class of knot transformations is the class of local moves. 1.
Preliminaries

In this section, we recall some of the basic concepts, objects, and constructions of knot theory, that we need. By a link we mean a piecewise-smooth embedding of a disjoint union of a finite number of circles into an oriented three-dimensional sphere
S^{3}
. We also use the term link to refer to the image of this embedding considered up to ambient isotopy. In this paper, all links are assumed to be tame and unoriented unless said otherwise. By a knot we mean a one-component link. We use the notation
\U
for the unknot and denote by
\bL
the set of all links, by
\bL^{\circ}
the set of all oriented links, and by
\bK
the set of all knots (here, by the set of all links we mean, of course, the countable set of all isotopy classes of links). Let
K
be a knot in
S^{3}
. An orientable surface

in
S^{3}
is called a
Seifert surface
for

if
\partial M=K
. It is known that any knot has an associated Seifert surface, see
[
59
]
.

Definition 1

(connected sum)
.

Let

and

be knots in

. We say that a knot

in

is a

of

and

if there are knots
K^{\prime}
,
Q^{\prime}
, and
W^{\prime}
in
S^{3}
and a
3
-ball

in
S^{3}
such that
K
,
Q
, and
W
are ambient isotopic to
K^{\prime}
,
Q^{\prime}
,
and
W^{\prime}
, respectively,
K^{\prime}
lies in
B
,
Q^{\prime}
lies in
S^{3}\setminus\Int(B),

K^{\prime}\cap Q^{\prime}
is a simple arc
\gamma
lying in
\partial B
, and
W^{\prime}=(K^{\prime}\cup Q^{\prime})\setminus\Int(\gamma).

Remark
.

Note that in the general case there can be two distinct knots each of which is a connected sum of
K
and
Q
. This problem of ambiguity is solved by choosing an orientation on
K
and
Q
, but due to the specifics of our further reasoning and the desire to work with unoriented knots we are also satisfied with this not a very clear definition.

Definition 2

(tangle)
.

An

-tangle
is a pair
B_{A}=(B,A)
, where

is a three-dimensional ball and

is a collection of

intersects with each arc at the endpoints of this arc and only at them. We call

of

. The

of

are connected components of

Remark
.

Two

and

are said to be

if the set of endpoints
\partial A
coincides with
\partial C
, and if there is an ambient isotopy of

(B,C)
that is the identity on the boundary
(\partial B,\partial A)=(\partial B,\partial C)
. Note that we also use the same term

for a pair of isotopic tangles

3.
Gordian graphs

In this section, we define the notion of a Gordian graph for a knot transformation and introduce some related notation. In addition, we introduce four new (global) knot transformation invariants that reflect the behavior of the corresponding Gordian graph at infinity. In a sense, these invariants can be considered a modification of the classical notion of ends of a topological space, see
[
18
]
.

Definition 3

(Gordian graph)
.

Let
\delta
be a knot transformation, and let
M
be a subset of
\bL
. Then we denote by
\G(\delta,M)
such a graph whose vertex set is in one-to-one correspondence with
M
, and two vertices are connected by an edge if and only if the corresponding links are obtained from each other (sic!) by a single application of
\delta
. The graph
\G(\delta,M)
is called the
Gordian graph
for
\delta
and
M
. We consider this graph with a natural metric.
The distance between two vertices
K
and
Q
in this metric is denoted by
\dt_{\delta}^{M}(K,Q)
and is called the
Gordian distance
. Note that
\dt_{\delta}^{M}(K,Q)
is the number of edges in any shortest path connecting
K
and
Q
, if
K
and
Q
are in the same connected component. If
K
and
Q
lie in distinct connected components of
\G(\delta,M)
, then we assume that

\dt_{\delta}^{M}(K,Q)=\infty.

Further, we neglect the difference between a vertex of a Gordian graph and its corresponding link, identifying these objects.

Definition 4

(unknotting distance, balls and spheres)
.

Let
\delta
be a knot transformation, and let
M
be a subset of
\bL
. For a link
L\in M
we define the
unknotting distance
as the distance from
L
to the unknot and denote it by

\un_{\delta}^{M}(L)=\dt_{\delta}^{M}(L,\U).

If
n\in\mathbb{N}
and
K\in M
then we denote by
{\Sf}_{n,\delta}^{M}(K)
the set of all
Q\in M
such that
\dt_{\delta}^{M}(K,Q)=n
, that is, the sphere in
\G(\delta,M)
of radius
n
centered at
K
. Also we denote by
{\B}_{n,\delta}^{M}(K)
the set of all
Q\in M
such that
\dt_{\delta}^{M}(K,Q)\leq n
.

Remark
.

In what follows, we mainly consider either
\bK
or
\bL
as
M
, so for these cases we use slightly less-cluttered notation. We omit the reference to
M
in the case of
M=\bK
, that is, we denote

\dt_{\delta}^{\bK}(K,Q)\,\,by\,\,\dt_{\delta}(K,Q),\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\un_{\delta}^{\bK}(K)\,\,by\,\,\un_{\delta}(K),

{\Sf}_{n,\delta}^{\bK}(K)\,\,\,by\,\,\,{\Sf}_{n}^{\delta}(K),\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,{\B}_{n,\delta}^{\bK}(K)\,\,\,by\,\,\,{\B}_{n}^{\delta}(K),

for
K,Q\in\bK
, and we replace the reference to
M
by
\circ
in the case of
M=\bL
, that is, we denote

\dt_{\delta}^{\bL}(K,Q)\,\,by\,\,{{{\accentset{\circ}{\dt}_{\delta}}}(K,Q)},\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\un_{\delta}^{\bL}(K)\,\,by\,\,{{\accentset{\circ}{\un}_{\delta}}}(K),

{\Sf}_{n,\delta}^{\bL}(K)\,\,\,by\,\,\,{{{\accentset{\circ}{\Sf}_{n}^{\delta}}}}(K),\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,{\B}_{n,\delta}^{\bL}(K)\,\,\,by\,\,\,{{\accentset{\circ}{\B}_{n}^{\delta}}}(K),

for
K,Q\in\bL
.

Further, when it comes to removing a certain set of vertices
X\subset\Vrt(G)
from a graph
G
, we always mean that all edges adjacent to these vertices are also removed. For the resulting space we use the same notation as for the ordinary complement, that is,
G\setminus X
, but we never mean the ordinary complement in this context.

Definition 5

(equivalence)
.

We say that two knot transformations
\delta
and
\zeta
are
equivalent
if
\G(\delta,\bL)
coincides with
\G(\zeta,\bL)
.

We now introduce four indicators of graph behavior at infinity. Definitions of these indicators are based on a general principle but differ in detail.

Definition 6

(ends)
.

Let
G
be a connected finite or countable graph (in what follows, all occurring graphs are assumed to be finite or countable), and let
\dt_{G}
be the natural metric on
G
. Then we define
\BU(G),\BI(G),\FU(G),\FI(G)\in\{0\}\cup\mathbb{N}\cup\{\infty\}
as follows:

\BU(G)=\sup_{V\,{\in}\,{\PB}(G)}\UCC\Big(G\setminus V\Big),

\BI(G)=\sup_{V\,{\in}\,{\PB}(G)}\ICC\Big(G\setminus V\Big),

\FU(G)=\sup_{V\,{\in}\,{\PF}(G)}\UCC\Big(G\setminus V\Big),

\FI(G)=\sup_{V\,{\in}\,{\PF}(G)}\ICC\Big(G\setminus V\Big),

where
\UCC(G\setminus V)
is the cardinality of the set of unbounded connected components of
G\setminus V
,
\ICC(G\setminus V)
is the cardinality of the set of infinite connected components of
G\setminus V
,
{\PF}(G)
is the set of all finite subsets of
\Vrt(G)
, and
{\PB}(G)
is the set of all bounded (as subsets of
G
) subsets of
\Vrt(G)
.

Let
x
be an arbitrary vertex of
G
, then it is easy to see that
\BU(G)
can be reformulated as follows

\BU(G)=\sup_{n\,{\in}\,\mathbb{N}}\UCC\Big(G\setminus\B_{n}^{G}(x)\Big),

where
\B_{n}^{G}(x)
is the set of all
v\in\Vrt(G)
such that
\dt_{G}(v,x)\leq n
. And in fact the construction does not depend on the choice of
x
.

We can extend our definitions to the case of graphs with more than one connected component, taking as a value the sum of the corresponding values over all connected components. Let
G
be a graph, then we say that
\BU(G)
is the number of
\BU
-ends
,
\BI(G)
is the number of
\BI
-ends
,
\FU(G)
is the number of
\FU
-ends
, and
\FI(G)
is the number of
\FI
-ends
of
G
.

Remark
.

Note that for any graph
H
we have

\FU(H)\leq\BU(H)\leq\BI(H)\,\,and\,\,\FU(H)\leq\FI(H)\leq\BI(H),

and all inequalities can be strict (for example, let
X_{i}
be copies of an infinite complete graph with
i\in\mathbb{Z}
, and let each vertex
x\in X_{i}
be connected by edges with its copies in
X_{i-1}
and
X_{i+1}
. Then the graph
A
obtained by gluing an infinite complete graph to an arbitrary vertex of this graph has
\FU(A)=1
,
\FI(A)=2
,
\BU(A)=2
, and
\BI(A)=\infty
).

Local moves

In this section, we define the most well-studied and natural class of knot transformations. Knot transformations included in this class are called local moves. Note that often in works devoted to local moves, the definitions are not strict and partly rely on intuition, which is why the narrative acquires many subtle and unclear moments. To avoid confusion, we give a complete formal definition of a local transformation, which, nevertheless, fully corresponds to intuition. In addition, we give some examples of well-known local moves and families of local moves. Also, we introduce a new family of local moves called almost trivial moves.

Definition 18

(branched covering (
[
59
]
))
.

Let

be compact
n

(n-2)
-submanifolds

is

-to-one near

-fold branched covering

-fold covering.

The Path Shifting Lemma.

Let
\delta
be a local move. Let
W
be an arbitrary knot in
S^{3}
, and let
\gamma
be a path in
\G(\delta,\bK)
such that
\{V_{0},V_{1},\dots,V_{n}\}
is the set of vertices of
\gamma
,
\{e_{1},e_{2},\dots,e_{n}\}
is the set of edges of
\gamma
, and

is incident to

and

, where

. Then there is a path
\gamma^{\prime}
in
\G(\delta,\bK)
, such that
\{V_{0}^{\prime},V_{1}^{\prime},\dots,V_{n}^{\prime}\}
is the set of vertices of
\gamma^{\prime}

\{e_{1}^{\prime},e_{2}^{\prime},\dots,e_{n}^{\prime}\}
is the set of edges of
\gamma^{\prime}
,

is a connected sum of

and

, and

is incident to

and

, where

. In other words, any path in a local-move Gordian graph can be "shifted" by some given knot.

Proof.

First, we prove the Lemma in the case of a single-edge path. Let

and

be knots in

corresponding to adjacent vertices of

, and let

be an arbitrary knot in

.
Let

be a

-ball in

, and let
B
be the base ball of
\delta
. By definition, there are knots
K^{\prime}
and
Q^{\prime}
such that
K^{\prime}
is ambient isotopic to
K
, and
Q^{\prime}
is ambient isotopic to
Q
,
K^{\prime}
and
Q^{\prime}
coincide outside the interior of
B
in
S^{3}
, and the pair
((B,B\cap K^{\prime}),(B,B\cap Q^{\prime}))
coincides with
\delta
. Let
E
be a
3
-ball in
S^{3}
such that
E\cap B=\varnothing
,
\partial E\cap K^{\prime}
is two points and
E\cap K^{\prime}
is an unknotted arc connecting these two points. Let
E^{\prime}
be a
3
-ball in
S^{3}
such that
\partial E^{\prime}\cap W
is two points and
E^{\prime}\cap W
is an unknotted arc connecting these two points. Denote by
S^{\prime}
the adjunction space

\Big(S^{3}\setminus\Int(E)\Big)\bigsqcup_{\ff}\Big(S^{3}\setminus\Int(E^{\prime})\Big),

where
\ff\colon\partial E\rightarrow\partial E^{\prime}
is a glueing homeomorphism such that
\ff(\partial E\cap K^{\prime})=\partial E^{\prime}\cap W
.
Then we introduce the notation

K_{1}=\Big(K^{\prime}\cap\big(S^{3}\setminus\Int(E)\big)\Big)\cup\Big(W\cap\big(S^{3}\setminus\Int(E^{\prime})\big)\Big)\subset S^{\prime}\cong S^{3},

Q_{1}=\Big(Q^{\prime}\cap\big(S^{3}\setminus\Int(E)\big)\Big)\cup\Big(W\cap\big(S^{3}\setminus\Int(E^{\prime})\big)\Big)\subset S^{\prime}\cong S^{3}.

By construction,
K_{1}
is a connected sum of
K
and
W
, and
Q_{1}
is a connected sum of
Q
and
W
. Moreover,
K_{1}
and
Q_{1}
coincide outside the interior of
B
, and the pair
((B,B\cap K^{\prime}),(B,B\cap Q^{\prime}))
coincides with
\delta
, which implies that
K_{1}
and
Q_{1}
correspond to adjacent vertices of
\G(\delta,\bK)
. This argument completes the proof in this case.

We now prove the Lemma in the general case. Let
\gamma
be a path in
\G(\delta,\bK)
such that
\{V_{0},V_{1},\dots,V_{n}\}
is the set of vertices of
\gamma
,
\{e_{1},e_{2},\dots,e_{n}\}
is the set of edges of
\gamma
, and
e_{i}
is incident to
V_{i}
and
V_{i-1}
, where
i\in\{1,2,\dots,n\}
. The Lemma in the case of a single-edge path says that there are adjacent vertices
V_{0}^{\prime}
and
V_{1}^{\prime}
such that
V_{0}^{\prime}
is a connected sum of
V_{0}
and
W
, and
V_{1}^{\prime}
is a connected sum of
V_{1}
and
W
, and there are adjacent vertices
V_{1}^{\prime\prime}
and
V_{2}^{\prime}
such that
V_{1}^{\prime\prime}
is a connected sum of
V_{1}
and
W
, and
V_{2}^{\prime}
is a connected sum of
V_{2}
and
W
. Since a connected sum of unoriented knots is not uniquely defined (see Remark after Definition
1
), it may be that
V_{1}^{\prime}
and
V_{1}^{\prime\prime}
are not ambient isotopic, but the control over the choice of the gluing homeomorphism in the proof of the Lemma in the case of a single-edge path allows us to assume that the choice of the corresponding connected sums is consistent, that is,
V_{1}^{\prime}
coincides with
V_{1}^{\prime\prime}
. Similarly, we can choose each next pair of vertices
(V_{i-1}^{\prime},V_{i}^{\prime})
in such a way that the choice of
V_{i-1}^{\prime}
is consistent with the previous choice that defines the pair
(V_{i-2}^{\prime},V_{i-1}^{\prime})
.
This argument completes the proof.
∎

The Basic Lemma.

Let
\delta
be an
n
-string almost trivial move. Suppose that there is a knot
Q
such that
Q\in{\Sf}_{1}^{\delta}(\U)
and
\e_{2}(Q)\geq 1
. Then the number of
\BU
-ends of each connected component of
\G(\delta,\bK)
is equal to one.

Proof.

Let
C
be a connected component of
\G(\delta,\bK)
, and let
E
be an arbitrary vertex in
C
. Let us show that for any
r\in\mathbb{N}
and any knots
K
and
S
such that

nr+e\leq\dt_{\delta}(K,E)<\infty\,\,\,and\,\,\,nr+e\leq\dt_{\delta}(S,E)<\infty,

where
e=\e_{2}(E)
, there is a path in
\G(\delta,\bK)
that connects
K
and
S
and does not intersect
\B_{r-1}^{\delta}(E)
. This immediately implies that the number of
\BU
-ends of
C
is exactly one, since for any
r\in\mathbb{N}
the complement
C\setminus\B_{r-1}^{\delta}(E)
contains only one unbounded connected component containing the set
C\setminus\B_{nr+e}^{\delta}(E)
.

Let
r\in\mathbb{N}
, and let
K
be a knot such that
nr+e\leq\dt_{\delta}(K,E)<\infty,
and let
e_{1}
be an edge incident to vertices
\U
and
Q
.
The Path Shifting Lemma.
says that
e_{1}
can be "shifted" by
Q
, that is, there are a knot
Q^{2}
and an edge
e_{2}
, such that
Q^{2}
is a connected sum of
Q
and
Q
, and
e_{2}
is incident to
Q
and
Q^{2}
. Now
e_{2}
can be "shifted" in a similar way, that is, there are knots
Q^{3}
and
J
, and an edge
e_{3}
, such that
Q^{3}
is a connected sum of
Q^{2}
and
Q
,
J
is a connected sum of
Q
and
Q
, and
e_{3}
is incident to
J
and
Q^{3}
. Moreover, as in the proof of
The Path Shifting Lemma.
, we can choose a gluing homeomorphism in such a way that
J
is ambient isotopic to
Q^{2}
. Thus,
e_{1}
,
e_{2}
, and
e_{3}
form a path in
\G(\delta,\bK)
connecting
\U
and
Q^{3}
. Continuing to "shift" the edges in the same way so that they are consistent with each other, we obtain a path
\gamma
in
\G(\delta,\bK)
connecting
\U
and
Q^{(n-1)r+e}
such that
\{\U=Q^{0},Q=Q^{1},Q^{2},\dots,Q^{(n-1)r+e}\}
is the set of vertices of
\gamma
,
\{e_{1},e_{2},\dots,e_{(n-1)r+e}\}
is the set of edges of
\gamma
,
Q^{i}
is a connected sum of
Q^{i-1}
and
Q
, and
e_{i}
is incident to
Q^{i}
and
Q^{i-1}
, where
i\in\{1,2\dots,(n-1)r+e\}
.

The Path Shifting Lemma.
says that
\gamma
can be "shifted" by
K
, that is, there is a path
\gamma^{\prime}
in
\G(\delta,\bK)
, such that
\{K=W_{0},W_{1},\dots,W_{(n-1)r+e}\}
is the set of vertices of
\gamma^{\prime}
,
\{e_{1}^{\prime},e_{2}^{\prime},\dots,e_{(n-1)r+e}^{\prime}\}
is the set of edges of
\gamma^{\prime}
,
W_{i}
is a connected sum of
Q^{i}
and
K
, and
e_{i}^{\prime}
is incident to
W_{i}
and
W_{i-1}
, where
i\in\{1,2\dots,(n-1)r+e\}
.

Note that since
\dt_{\delta}(K,E)<\infty
, there is a path
\zeta
in
\G(\delta,\bK)
connecting
E
and
K
, such that
\{E=V_{0},V_{1},\dots,K=V_{m}\}
is the set of vertices of
\zeta
,
\{f_{1},f_{2},\dots,f_{m}\}
is the set of edges of
\zeta
, and
f_{i}
is incident to
V_{i}
and
V_{i-1}
, where
i\in\{1,2\dots,m\}
.
The Path Shifting Lemma.
says that
\zeta
can be "shifted" by
Q^{(n-1)r+e}
, that is, there is a path
\zeta^{\prime}
, such that
\{V_{0}^{\prime},V_{1}^{\prime},\dots,V_{m}^{\prime}\}
is the set of vertices of
\zeta^{\prime}
,
\{f_{1}^{\prime},f_{2}^{\prime},\dots,f_{m}^{\prime}\}
is the set of edges of
\zeta^{\prime}
,
V_{i}^{\prime}
is a connected sum of
V_{i}
and
Q^{(n-1)r+e}
, and
f_{i}^{\prime}
is incident to
V_{i}^{\prime}
and
V_{i-1}^{\prime}
, where
i\in\{1,2\dots,m\}
.

Note that we can choose the gluing homeomorphisms of the "shifts" in such a way that
W_{(n-1)r+e}
and
V_{m}^{\prime}
, each of which is a connected sum of
K
and
Q^{(n-1)r+e}
, are ambient isotopic. In this case, paths
\gamma^{\prime}
and
\zeta^{\prime}
form a path connecting
K
and
V_{0}^{\prime}
.

Now we need some technical details.

The Lower Estimates Lemma.
shows that for arbitrary knots
A
and
B
we have

\dt_{\delta}(A,B)\geq\frac{|\e_{2}(A)-\e_{2}(B)|}{n-1}.

Note that if a knot
W
is a connected sum of knots
A
and
B
, then it follows from the construction of a
2
-fold branched cover that
\Sigma_{2}(W)
is a connected sum of
\Sigma_{2}(A)
and
\Sigma_{2}(B)
. This implies that

{\bf H}_{1}(\Sigma_{2}(W))={\bf H}_{1}(\Sigma_{2}(A))\oplus{\bf H}_{1}(\Sigma_{2}(B)).

By construction and by the Schubert theorem (see
[
61
]
,
[
59
, p. 150]
),
Q^{i}
is a connected sum of
i
copies of
Q
. Then we have

{\bf H}_{1}(\Sigma_{2}(Q^{i}))=\bigoplus_{i}{\bf H}_{1}(\Sigma_{2}(Q)),

and therefore
\e_{2}(Q^{i})\geq i
. Note that since each vertex
V_{i}^{\prime}
of the path
\zeta^{\prime}
is a connected sum of
V_{i}
and
Q^{(n-1)r+e}
, we have

{\bf H}_{1}(\Sigma_{2}(V_{i}^{\prime}))={\bf H}_{1}(\Sigma_{2}(V_{i}))\oplus{\bf H}_{1}(\Sigma_{2}(Q^{(n-1)r+e}))

and therefore

\dt_{\delta}(V_{i}^{\prime},E)\geq\frac{|\e_{2}(V_{i}^{\prime})-e|}{n-1}\geq\frac{|(n-1)r+e-e|}{n-1}\geq r.

Moreover, since each vertex
W_{i}
of the path
\gamma^{\prime}
is a connected sum of
K
and
Q^{i}
, we have

{\bf H}_{1}(\Sigma_{2}(W_{i}))={\bf H}_{1}(\Sigma_{2}(K))\oplus{\bf H}_{1}(\Sigma_{2}(Q^{i})).

If
i\geq(n-1)r+e
, then

\dt_{\delta}(W_{i},E)\geq\frac{|\e_{2}(W_{i})-e|}{n-1}\geq\frac{|i-e|}{n-1}\geq\frac{|(n-1)r+e-e|}{n-1}\geq r.

Suppose
i<(n-1)r+e
and
\dt_{\delta}(W_{i},E)<r
, then by construction of
\gamma^{\prime}
and by definition of distance we have
\dt_{\delta}(K,W_{i})\leq i
and by the triangle inequality we have

nr+e\leq\dt_{\delta}(K,E)\leq\dt_{\delta}(K,W_{i})+\dt_{\delta}(W_{i},E)<i+r<(n-1)r+e+r=nr+e,

which leads to a contradiction. Therefore if
i<(n-1)r+e
then it must be
\dt_{\delta}(W_{i},E)\geq r
. Thus, we have obtained that any vertex of the path
\gamma^{\prime}\cup\,\zeta^{\prime}
, connecting
K
and
V_{0}^{\prime}
(where
V_{0}^{\prime}
is a connected sum of
E
and
Q^{(n-1)r+e}
, and therefore does not depend on the choice of
K
), lies outside the ball
\B_{r-1}^{\delta}(E)
. Note that
K
is chosen arbitrarily among those such that
nr+e\leq\dt_{\delta}(K,E)<\infty
. This remark completes the proof.
∎

.

.

,

. Let

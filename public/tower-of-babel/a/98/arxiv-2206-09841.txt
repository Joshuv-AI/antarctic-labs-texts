Label noise (stochastic) gradient descent implicitly solves the Lasso for quadratic parametrisation
Loucas Pillaud-Vivien, Julien Reygner, Nicolas Flammarion

Abstract
Understanding the implicit bias of training algorithms is of crucial importance in order to explain the success of overparametrised neural networks. In this paper, we study the role of the label noise in the training dynamics of a quadratically parametrised model through its continuous time version. We explicitly characterise the solution chosen by the stochastic flow and prove that it implicitly solves a Lasso program. To fully complete our analysis, we provide nonasymptotic convergence guarantees for the dynamics as well as conditions for support recovery. We also give experimental results which support our theoretical claims. Our findings highlight the fact that structured noise can induce better generalisation and help explain the greater performances of stochastic dynamics as observed in practice.

1
Introduction

The many successes of deep learning are undoubtedly equal to the theoretical mysteries that surround it. However, while theoretical explanations were quite weak a decade ago, some recent progresses have refined our understanding of neural networks: by proving convergence in some cases
(
Mei et al. 2018
;
Chizat and Bach 2018
)
, or clarifying the role of initialisation
(
Jacot et al. 2018
;
Chizat et al. 2019
)
. Still, one of their most surprising and unexplained aspect is their ability to generalise without explicit regularisation despite large overparametrisation
(
Zhang et al. 2017
)
.

In fact, due to the high expressivity of overparametrised neural networks, they carry a large freedom while fitting a data set, yet without any hurt in the generalisation performance. That is to say that the way they are trained (initialisation, algorithm, specific architecture) specifies a large part of their generalisation abilities.
This crucial aspect, often referred to as the
implicit bias
or
algorithmic regularisation
, has been a major line of research lately. For example, in the simple and prototypical least-square framework, it has been shown that both gradient descent and stochastic gradient descent converge towards the global solution which has the lowest squared distance from the initialisation
(
Zhang et al. 2017
)
. For a linear parametrisation,
Soudry et al. 2018
show in a seminal paper that gradient descent selects the max-margin classifier for logistic regression on separable data. Since then, many works have tried to characterise the implicit bias of specified settings: for classification with neural networks
(
Lyu and Li 2020
;
Chizat and Bach 2020
)
, and regression for a large variety of nonconvex models
(
Woodworth et al. 2020
;
Arora et al. 2019
)
for which initialisation always plays a central role
(
Maennel et al. 2018
)
.

A common feature of these analyses is that they study (deterministic) gradient descents (GD), while it has been shown empirically that stochasticity may be of primary importance to match best generalisation guarantees
(
Keskar et al. 2017
)
. Hence, it is natural to try to understand the role of stochasticity induced by the mini-batch training procedure of stochastic gradient decent (SGD). It is often shown that SGD tends to move towards
flat regions
of the training loss
(
Zhu et al. 2019
;
Chaudhari and Soatto 2018
)
. However,
the flat minima selection phenomenon does not appear very clearly and noise models taken to rigorously prove these are disputable. In this perspective, specific noise models to understand the role of stochasticity are primordial: an example of that is the fact that minibatch stochasticity of SGD is state dependent and cancels itself at global optima
(
Wojtowytsch 2021
;
Pesme et al. 2021
;
Ali et al. 2020
)
. Another important feature is that the noise has a specific geometry, e.g. in the least-square model it belongs to the span of the data inputs
(
Zhang et al. 2017
)
.

To understand this without suffering from the degeneracy at global optima one may resort to
label noise
SGD, where some noise is systematically added to the output at each step of the descent. This injected noise has been shown to be a good surrogate model that exemplifies the geometry and the state dependence of the noise carried by SGD before reaching zero training loss
(
HaoChen et al. 2021
)
. In this perspective, local implicit bias criteria have been sketched
(
Damian et al. 2021
;
Blanc et al. 2020
)
and notably, a limiting process has been introduced lately to formally explain how the label noise drives the dynamics
(
Li et al. 2022
)
. However, all these correspond to local, nonexplicit and asymptotic results that might not be as satisfying as those proven in the deterministic case. We study such a label noise procedure in a nonconvex model, and provide an explicit, nonasymptotic description of the dynamics.

The quadratic parametrisations which we consider have become popular lately
(
Vaškevičius et al. 2019
)
since, despite their simplicity, they already enable to grasp the complexity of more general networks. Indeed, they highlight important aspects of the theoretical concerns of modern machine learning: the neural tangent kernel regime, the roles of overparametrisation and of the
initialisation
(
Woodworth et al. 2020
)
. In this literature, it has been shown that a
\ell_{1}
-sparcifying regularisation rules the implicit biasing, together with initialisation: indeed, when the initialisation goes infinitely small, GD
(
Woodworth et al. 2020
)
and SGD
(
Pesme et al. 2021
)
select a sparse interpolator of the data. However, an important drawback is that as initialisation gets very small, optimisation time gets very large. Hence the following question

Does label noise help in recovering a sparse interpolator without infinitely small initialisation?

To tackle this question, we study the label noise (stochastic) gradient descent through its continuous version, namely the
stochastic gradient flow (SGF). We stress that in our work, we attach
peculiar attention to the adequate modelling of the noise. Tools from Itô calculus are then leveraged
in order to derive exact formulas, quantitative bounds and interesting interpretations for our problem.

1.1
Main contribution and paper organisation

In Section
2
, we start by introducing the setup of our problem as well as the continuous stochastic model. Then, in Section
3
, we state the main results on the dynamics convergence, deriving precise nonasymptotic statements, both in terms of time and noise. We informally formulate it here:

Theorem

(
Informal).

For any initialisation, the label noise stochastic gradient flow for quadratic parametrisation implicitly solves a weighted Lasso program. In consequence, under conditions on the design matrix, it recovers exactly the support of the ground-truth sparse estimator of the model.

To reach this goal, we analyse thoroughly the dynamics, handling precisely its stochastic fluctuations. The proof sketch is depicted in Section
4
. We support our results experimentally and validate our model in Section
5
.

1.2
Additional related work

A large part of the related work has already been covered in the introduction. Let us complete it here. Our study leverages continuous time stochastic differential equation modelling of discrete time dynamics. We refer to
Li et al. 2019
for a technical introduction to these techniques when related to machine learning problems.

Let us also compare the present work with the recent literature on label noise driven GD. Two different points of view are taken in the literature. The aim of the first one pioneered by
Blanc et al. 2020
is to show that such stochastic dynamics are biased towards optimising a hidden objective related to the curvature of the loss. However, it seems hard to conclude as their calculations are essentially both local and asymptotic. In the same spirit, one of the most conclusive works related to this approach is certainly the recent work of
Li et al. 2022
in which the authors exhibit a proper limiting dynamics upon the manifold of interpolators thanks to a time rescaling. Once again, the results shown are only asymptotic, nonquantitative and difficult to apprehend. In contrast, the aim of the present paper is to characterise quantitatively the convergence without resorting to any limiting argument. Finally,
HaoChen et al. 2021
show a similar collapsing effect due to the label noise. However, their analysis relies on an extremely large noise (at least square of the dimension), so that our result on the large noise regime alone can be considered finer.

2
Setup and preliminaries

The aim of the study is to show that the geometry of the noise induced by SGD can bias the dynamics towards sparse data interpolators. To precisely support this claim, we introduce now the model and the algorithms we consider.

2.1
Overparametrised noiseless sparse regression

We consider a linear regression problem with inputs-outputs
(\mathsf{x}_{i},\mathsf{y}_{i})_{1\leqslant i\leqslant n}
in
\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}
, and loss function

\mathsf{L}(\beta):=\frac{1}{4n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left(\langle\beta,\mathsf{x}_{i}\rangle-\mathsf{y}_{i}\right)^{2}=\frac{1}{4n}\|\mathsf{X}\beta-\mathsf{y}\|_{2}^{2},

(1)

where
\mathsf{X}\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times d}
is the data matrix whose rows are the data vectors
(\mathsf{x}_{i}^{\top})_{1\leqslant i\leqslant n}
and
\mathsf{y}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
is the vector of outputs
(\mathsf{y}_{i})_{1\leqslant i\leqslant n}
. We study the overparametrised setting
d\geqslant n
and assume that there exists at least one
nonnegative
interpolating parameter which perfectly fits the training set, namely:

(A.1)

(Existence of nonnegative interpolators)
.

The set
\mathcal{I}_{+}:=\{\beta\in\mathbb{R}_{+}^{d}:\mathsf{L}(\beta)=0\}
is nonempty.

Since we work in the overparametrised setting, the set
\mathcal{I}_{+}
may in principle be a high-dimensional polyhedron. We now provide assumptions on
\mathsf{X}
and
\mathsf{y}
ensuring the existence and characterisation of particular
sparse
interpolators. The first one is very mild and ensures that the vector
\mathsf{h}:=\textrm{diag}(\mathsf{X}^{\top}\mathsf{X})\in\mathbb{R}_{+}^{d}
has only positive coordinates.

(A.2)

(No degenerate coordinate)
.

The data matrix
\mathsf{X}
has no identically
0
column.

For the following, let
S
be a nonempty subset of
\llbracket 1,d\rrbracket
with cardinality
s
and define
\mathsf{X}_{S}\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times s}
as the matrix constituted by the coordinates of the inputs
(\mathsf{x}_{i})_{1\leqslant i\leqslant n}
solely in
S
. We use a similar notation for the vector
\mathsf{h}_{S}\in\mathbb{R}_{+}^{s}
. Furthermore, the support of any
\beta\in\mathbb{R}_{+}^{d}
is denoted and defined by
\mathrm{supp}(\beta):=\{k\in\llbracket 1,d\rrbracket:\beta_{k}>0\}
, and we introduce the set
\mathcal{S}_{+}:=\{\mathrm{supp}(\beta),\beta\in\mathcal{I}_{+}\}
.

Lemma 1

(Domination condition)
.

Under Assumptions
(A.1)
and
(A.2)
, there is at most one
S\in\mathcal{S}_{+}
such that either
S=\varnothing
, or
\mathsf{X}_{S}^{\top}\mathsf{X}_{S}
is invertible and the domination condition

\mathsf{h}_{S^{c}}>\mathsf{X}^{\top}_{S^{c}}\mathsf{X}_{S}(\mathsf{X}^{\top}_{S}\mathsf{X}_{S})^{-1}\mathsf{h}_{S}

(2)

holds, where the latter inequality is understood coordinatewise in
\mathbb{R}^{d-s}
.

Lemma
1
is proved in Appendix
A
. Let us brefiely discuss some of its consequences. First, when
S\neq\varnothing
, the invertibility condition on
\mathsf{X}_{S}^{\top}\mathsf{X}_{S}
implies that
|S|\leqslant n
, which gives that the interpolator associated with
S\in\mathcal{S}_{+}
is at worse
n
-sparse. To understand the uniqueness property of
S
stated in the Lemma let us detail the case with one data point in dimension two.

Example 2

(Case
n=1,d=2
)
.

Let us set
\mathsf{x}=(\mathsf{x}^{1},\mathsf{x}^{2})\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
and e.g.
\mathsf{y}=1
. Clearly
(A.1)
and
(A.2)
are verified if and only if
\mathsf{x}^{1},\mathsf{x}^{2}\neq 0
and either
\mathsf{x}^{1}>0
or
\mathsf{x}^{2}>0
. If either one of the coordinate at least is negative, then the domination condition Eq. (
2
) is always fulfilled and uniqueness of
S
is obvious. Now suppose that
\mathsf{x}^{1},\mathsf{x}^{2}>0
: if
S=\{1\}
, then Eq. (
2
) is equivalent to
\mathsf{x}^{2}>\mathsf{x}^{1}
and in the case
S=\{2\}
, Eq. (
2
) is true if and only if
\mathsf{x}^{1}>\mathsf{x}^{2}
: hence, one possibility rejects the other one and uniqueness of

holds. Finally, in the particular case where
\mathsf{x}^{1}=\mathsf{x}^{2}
, Eq. (
2
) is never satisfied; we exclude this pathological case thanks to the following assumption.

(A.3)

(Existence of the ground-truth)
.

There exists exactly one set
S^{\mathsf{o}}\in\mathcal{S}_{+}
which satisfies the conditions of Lemma
1
.

Notice that

if and only if

. Note additionally that under Assumption

, we may define

as the unique element of

and when

\beta^{\mathsf{o}}=[(\mathsf{X}^{\top}_{S^{\mathsf{o}}}\mathsf{X}_{S^{\mathsf{o}}})^{-1}\mathsf{X}^{\top}_{S^{\mathsf{o}}}\mathsf{y},0_{S^{c}}]

2.2
Architecture and algorithm

A two-homogeneous reparametrisation.

We reparametrise the linear prediction
\mathsf{x}\mapsto\langle\beta,\mathsf{x}\rangle
using the nonlinear parametrisation
\mathsf{x}\mapsto\langle\theta^{2},\mathsf{x}\rangle
, where the square of the vector
\theta
stands for the coordinatewise square. This
2
-positive homogeneous model can be viewed as a simple linear network with only pairwise connections and is often used as a first step towards understanding more general neural networks
(
Woodworth et al. 2020
;
Li et al. 2022
)
. It is worth noting that the parametrisation
\beta=\theta_{+}^{2}-\theta_{-}^{2}
could be considered in order to attain negative values. It would only make the analysis more technical and therefore we prefer to restrict ourselves to the simpler setting. By a slight abuse of notation we rewrite the training loss (
1
) as a function of
\theta
as

\mathsf{L}(\theta):=\frac{1}{4n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left(\langle\theta^{2},\mathsf{x}_{i}\rangle-\mathsf{y}_{i}\right)^{2}.

(3)

Even if the overall function space expressivity has not changed, the reparametrisation makes the least-square problem associated with Eq. (
3
) nonconvex. Thus minimising it with gradient based procedures is not guaranteed to converge to global optima anymore.

Label noise gradient descent.

We minimise the training loss in Eq. (
3
) with GD and
Label Noise
(LNGD). Namely, at each gradient step
t>0
, we deliberately add a random noise
\xi(t)\sim\mathcal{N}(0,\updelta I_{n})
to the label
\mathsf{y}
. The algorithm is started from
\theta_{0}
and used with a constant step size
\gamma>0
. Noting explicitly the loss with input
\mathsf{X}
and output
\mathsf{y}
as
\mathsf{L}(\theta;\mathsf{X},\mathsf{y})
, the update rule corresponds to

\begin{split}\theta(t+1)&=\theta(t)-\gamma\nabla_{\theta}\mathsf{L}(\theta(t);\mathsf{X},\mathsf{y}+\xi(t))\\
&=\theta(t)-\frac{\gamma}{n}\left[\mathsf{X}^{\top}\left(\mathsf{X}\theta(t)^{2}-\mathsf{y}\right)\right]\odot\theta(t)+\frac{\gamma}{n}\left[\mathsf{X}^{\top}\xi(t)\right]\odot\theta(t),\end{split}

(4)

where
\odot
stands for the coordinatewise product between two vectors.
In the previous literature, LNGD was often studied together with SGD.
However the stochasticity coming from the sampling procedure of SGD is rapidly negligible compared to the one triggered by the label noise. Indeed the label noise SGD update writes

\begin{split}\theta(t+1)&=\theta(t)-\gamma(\langle\theta(t)^{2},\mathsf{x}_{i(t)}\rangle-\mathsf{y}_{i(t)})\mathsf{x}_{i(t)}\odot\theta(t)\\
&=\theta(t)-\frac{\gamma}{n}\left[\mathsf{X}^{\top}\left(\mathsf{X}\theta(t)^{2}-\mathsf{y}\right)\right]\odot\theta(t)+\frac{\gamma}{n}\left[\mathsf{X}^{\top}(\xi(t)+\varepsilon_{i(t)})\right]\odot\theta(t),\end{split}

where for all
t>0
,
i(t)
is sampled from the uniform distribution over
\llbracket 1,n\rrbracket
, and we have defined
\varepsilon_{i(t)}:=\mathbb{E}_{i(t)}[(\langle\theta(t)^{2},\mathsf{x}_{i(t)}\rangle-\mathsf{y}_{i(t)})e_{i(t)}]-(\langle\theta(t)^{2},\mathsf{x}_{i(t)}\rangle-\mathsf{y}_{i(t)})e_{i(t)}
where
(e_{i})_{1\leqslant i\leqslant n}
is the canonical basis of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
.
With this notation,
\varepsilon_{i(t)}
corresponds to the SGD noise: it is multiplicative, crucially vanishes at optimum and is rapidly negligible or comparable to
\sqrt{\updelta}
. Thus there should be no qualitative difference between SGD and GD when both are used with label noise (see Figure
1
for an empirical validation of this fact).
To understand the LNGD dynamics on the nonconvex objective Eq. (
3
), we resort to its continuous time model. This approach has the advantage of leading to clean calculations while comprehending the complexity of the model.

2.3
Label noise stochastic gradient flow

Continuous time stochastic dynamics modelling.

Continuous time modelling of sequential processes provides a large set of tools, such as differential calculus, which are valuable when trying to understand the dynamics of a process.
For this reason, many recent works have considered gradient flows with the aim of grasping the behaviour of gradient descent on complex nonconvex problems such as neural networks training.
However the modelling of stochastic dynamics is more demanding.
Indeed these dynamics are better modelled by stochastic processes which are solutions of stochastic differential equations (SDEs):
\mathrm{d}\theta(t)=b(t,\theta(t))\mathrm{d}t+\sigma(t,\theta(t))\mathrm{d}B(t)
, where
(B(t))_{t\geqslant 0}
is a standard Brownian motion.
For a proper model, the drift term
b
and the noise
\sigma
need to be set in a particular manner:

(i)

The drift term
b
should match the negative gradient:
b=-\nabla\mathsf{L}
.

(ii)

The noise covariance
\sigma\sigma^{\top}(t,\theta)
should match
\mathrm{Cov}[\frac{\gamma}{n}\left[\mathsf{X}^{\top}\xi\right]\odot\theta(t)|\theta(t)=\theta]
, where we have set
\xi\sim\mathcal{N}(0,\updelta I_{n})
independent of
\theta(t)
.

(iii)

The noise should belong to the correct space, i.e. to the manifold
\{\theta\odot\mathrm{span}[\mathsf{X}^{\top}],\textrm{ for }\theta\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\}
.

Stochastic process model.

Following these rules, we propose the following SDE to model LNGD in continuous time. This gives the label noise gradient flow (LNGF)

\mathrm{d}\theta(t)=-\frac{1}{n}[\mathsf{X}^{\top}(\mathsf{X}\theta(t)^{2}-\mathsf{y})]\odot\theta(t)\mathrm{d}t+\frac{\sqrt{\updelta\gamma}}{n}\ \theta(t)\odot[\mathsf{X}^{\top}\mathrm{d}B(t)],

(5)

with
(B(t))_{t\geqslant 0}
a standard Brownian motion in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Since LNGD is the Euler-Maruyama discretisation with step-size
\gamma
of the SDE (
5
), the SDE and the discrete models match perfectly for infinitesimal step-sizes (up to first order terms in
\gamma
). The model is said to be
consistent
.
We also note that the same SDE is obtained for any label noise distribution
\xi
with zero-mean and
\updelta
times identity covariance. We simply assume that the injected label noise is Gaussian for clearness of exposition.
In the same way, we could consider time-dependent covariance
\Sigma(t)\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}
by replacing the noise term in the SDE by
\frac{\sqrt{\gamma}}{n}\ \theta(t)\odot[\mathsf{X}^{\top}\Sigma(t)^{1/2}\mathrm{d}B(t)]
.

Drift-Variance tradeoff.

For the sake of clarity, we introduce the following renormalised variables:
X=\mathsf{X}/\sqrt{n},y=\mathsf{y}/\sqrt{n},h=\mathsf{h}/n,\delta=\gamma\updelta/n
. The SDE model we study reads

\mathrm{d}\theta(t)=\underbrace{-[X^{\top}(X\theta(t)^{2}-y)]\odot\theta(t)}_{\textrm{drift term}}\mathrm{d}t+\underbrace{\sqrt{\delta}\ \theta(t)\odot[X^{\top}\mathrm{d}B(t)]}_{\textrm{noise term}}.

(6)

As often with state-dependent noise SDEs, there is a competition between the noise and the drift components.
On the one hand, considering the drift term alone amounts to only take into account the
gradient flow
. As analysed by
Woodworth et al. 2020
;
Wu and Rebeschini 2020
, the dynamics is, in that case, driven to a certain interpolator related to the initialisation.
On the other hand, the noise term acts as a multiplicative shrinking force akin to the one in the geometric Brownian motion
\mathrm{d}S(t)=\mu S(t)\mathrm{d}t+\sigma S(t)\mathrm{d}W(t)
.
For this dynamics, the noise can counter the repulsive force of the drift and drive
S(t)
to
0
almost surely if its scale satisfies
\sigma^{2}>2\mu
.
Thus, if the noise
\delta
dominates the dynamics:
\delta\gg\sup_{t\geqslant 0}\|X^{\top}(X\theta(t)^{2}-y)\|_{\infty}
, the process is similarly driven to
0
almost surely. We note that this argument is at the crux of the analysis of
HaoChen et al. 2021
.
However, when the noise level
\delta
is not infinitely large, the drift and the noise balance each other out and the dynamics becomes much more intricate to analyse.

2.4
Hidden mirror flow structure and Lasso

Itô calculus and hidden mirror flow.

Let us recall that, for such reparametrised model, when
\theta
follows a gradient flow
\mathrm{d}\theta(t)=-\nabla\mathsf{L}(\theta(t))\mathrm{d}t
, then the corresponding iterate
\beta(t)=\theta(t)^{2}
follows a mirror descent with potential defined through
\nabla\psi(\beta)=\log(\beta)
, where the
\log
is taken componentwise
(
Ghai et al. 2020
)
.
This result is easily obtained using the chain rule on
\nabla\psi(\beta(t))
.
This hidden mirror structure is then used to describe the implicit bias of such gradient flows.
It turns out that the exact same procedure can be done based on Itô calculus (i.e. chain rule for stochastic processes) to exhibit a stochastic mirror flow.
Indeed, setting
\beta(t)=\theta(t)^{2}
, where
(\theta(t))_{t\geqslant 0}
is the solution to (
6
), we have

\mathrm{d}\beta(t)=\beta(t)\odot\left[-2X^{\top}(X\beta(t)-y)+\delta h\right]\mathrm{d}t+2\sqrt{\delta}\beta(t)\odot[X^{\top}\mathrm{d}B(t)],

(7)

and this entails the following stochastic differential equation for
\log(\beta)

\mathrm{d}\log\beta(t)=-\left(2X^{\top}(X\beta(t)-y)+\delta h\right)\mathrm{d}t+2\sqrt{\delta}X^{\top}\mathrm{d}B(t).

(8)

This observation has already been used to understand the implicit bias due to the sampling noise of SGD
(
Pesme et al. 2021
)
, where the authors resort to the unsigned parametrisation (
\beta=\theta^{2}_{+}-\theta^{2}_{-}
) changing the mirror map to the
\mathrm{argsh}
; without affecting qualitatively the following discussion.

Stochastic mirror on the weighted lasso.

The equation (
8
) can be explicitly rewritten as a stochastic
mirror-like
flow

\mathrm{d}\nabla\psi(\beta(t))=-\nabla L_{\delta}(\beta(t))\mathrm{d}t+2\sqrt{\delta}X^{\top}\mathrm{d}B(t),

(9)

with

L_{\delta}(\beta):=\|X\beta-y\|_{2}^{2}+\delta\langle h,\beta\rangle,\qquad\nabla\psi(\beta)=\log\beta.

(10)

The objective
L_{\delta}
is importantly related to the celebrated
weighted
Lasso problem:

\min_{\beta\geqslant 0}\ L_{\delta}(\beta),

(
\text{WL}_{\delta}
)

where the quadratic loss is regularised by a weighted
\ell_{1}
-norm with weight
h
and regularisation parameter
\delta
.
Hence, the change of variable together with the mirror interpretation provides the following intuition:

The label noise gradient flow can be cast as a stochastic mirror flow on the weighted Lasso with weight
h

\delta

This picture serves as the main guideline for our study.
However, the stochasticity present in the mirror prevents from directly applying mirror-based optimisation techniques.
Instead, our approach is based on a direct analysis of the dynamics of
\beta(t)
. Our results are gathered in the next section.

3
Main results

3.1
The recovery problem for the weighted Lasso

3.2
SDE convergence results

Before presenting the main results, we first recall the most important observations stated in the previous sections. First, as drift and noise parts are locally Lipschitz continuous, for any initial condition
\theta(0)\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, Eq. (
6

\tau_{\infty}

(
Khasminskii 2012

. Throughout the sequel, we shall work with a fixed initial condition
\theta(0)
such that
\beta(0)=\theta^{2}(0)>0

\tau_{\infty}=+\infty
, almost surely. Remarkably, Eq. (
9

2.4
shows that it can be cast as a Lasso stochastic mirror flow on the linear predictor
\beta=\theta^{2}
.
Then, conditions are given in Theorem
3
under which support recovery is achieved by the minimiser
\beta^{\mathsf{L}}
of the weighted Lasso program (

WL
δ

) .
Here, we naturally distinguished between two different regimes: (i) the large noise regime where the best predictor is uniformly zero (Section
3.2.1
) and (ii) the standard noise regime, when the signal to noise ratio is high enough to allow for support recovery (Section
3.2.2
).

Our main results show that
(\beta(t))_{t\geqslant 0}
recovers perfectly the support of
\beta^{\mathsf{L}}
in these two regimes, namely that, first,
\beta(t)\to 0
on
{S^{\mathsf{o}}}^{c}
. Second, on the support
S^{\mathsf{o}}
, in the long run,
\beta(t)
fluctuates in a neighbourhood of size
\sqrt{\delta}
around
\beta^{\mathsf{L}}
and hence of
\beta^{\mathsf{o}}
(by Theorem

To quantify the noise that remains inherently in
\beta(t)
, we introduce comparison processes that allow to precisely specify the scale of the fluctuations.
In the large noise regime, it is ruled by Brownian fluctuations, whereas in the standard noise regime, it is ruled by a rapidly mixing process which concentrates around
\beta^{\mathsf{o}}
at
\sqrt{\delta}

3.2.1
The large noise regime

We first place ourselves in the large noise regime from Theorem
3
. We recall that we take the convention that this regime contains the case
S^{\mathsf{o}}=\varnothing
(that is to say
y=0
), for which we define
\delta_{+}=0
.
In this regime, note that
\beta^{\mathsf{L}}
is uniformly zero. In the following result, we show that the stochastic gradient flow we consider goes to zero almost surely at exponential speed.

Theorem 4

(Large noise regime convergence)
.

Let the assumptions of Theorem
3
hold, with
\delta>\delta_{+}
. Recall that
\mu^{\mathsf{L}}=\delta h-2X^{\top}y>0
. Then
\tau_{\infty}=+\infty
, almost surely; besides, there exists
C\geqslant 0
depending on the data
X
,
y
and on
\beta(0)
, and a one-dimensional process
(\zeta^{\delta}(t))_{t\geqslant 0}
, which has the same law as
(4\delta\|B(t)\|^{2}_{2})_{t\geqslant 0}
,
such that almost surely,

\forall t\geqslant 0,\qquad\beta(t)\leqslant C\exp\left(\|X^{\top}\|\sqrt{\zeta^{\delta}(t)}\mathds{1}-\mu^{\mathsf{L}}t\right).

(11)

In consequence,

\lim_{t\to+\infty}\beta(t)=\beta^{\mathsf{L}}=0,\qquad\text{almost surely.}

(12)

The theorem shows quantitatively that all the coordinates of our predictor go to zero exponentially fast at some speed governed by
\delta
(through the variable
\mu^{\mathsf{L}}
). Roughly speaking, as the fluctuations of the
Bessel process

\sqrt{\zeta^{\delta}(t)}
are of order
\sqrt{t}

(
Revuz and Yor 2013
, Chapter XI)
, we see that they are negligible in front of the linear deterministic term
-\mu^{\mathsf{L}}t
. These two terms in the exponential are quite reminiscent of the one dimensional geometric Brownian motion (GBW). Thus, we can see this regime as a multidimensional generalisation of the GBW. Note that the same idea has been expressed in
HaoChen et al. 2021
but with an unnecessarily large noise
\delta\geqslant\mathcal{O}(\beta(0)d^{2})
. To conclude on the almost sure convergence, a precise analysis is conducted in the proof using the law of the iterated logarithm
(
Revuz and Yor 2013
, Corollary 1.12, Chapter II)
. The proof as well as the explicit value of
C
can be found in Appendix
B.1
.

3.2.2
The standard noise regime

In this regime, recall that by Theorem
3
,
\beta^{\mathsf{L}}
has the same support as
\beta^{\mathsf{o}}
, denoted by
S^{\mathsf{o}}\subset\llbracket 1,d\rrbracket
. For technical reasons we need to assume that the initial condition
\beta(0)
satisfies the condition that
\beta_{S^{\mathsf{o}}}(0)\not=\beta^{\mathsf{L}}_{S^{\mathsf{o}}}
, and moreover that

. In the following result, we prove that the stochastic gradient flow we consider goes to zero almost surely at exponential speed on
{S^{\mathsf{o}}}^{c}
. We then show precisely that, on the support

, the stochastic flow fluctuates in a region of size
\sqrt{\delta}
around
\beta^{\mathsf{L}}
. To quantitatively support this claim we introduce a comparison process
(\xi^{\delta}(t))_{t\geqslant 0}
which quantifies the scale of the fluctuations.

Theorem 5

(Standard noise regime convergence)
.

Let the assumptions of Theorem
3
hold, with
S^{\mathsf{o}}\not=\varnothing
and
\delta<\delta_{-}
. Recall that
\mu^{\mathsf{L}}_{{S^{\mathsf{o}}}^{c}}>0
. Then
\tau_{\infty}=+\infty
, almost surely; besides, there exists a one dimensional stochastic process
(\xi^{\delta}(t))_{t\geqslant 0}

(i)

On the support
S^{\mathsf{o}}
, almost surely,

\forall t\geqslant 0,\qquad\beta^{\mathsf{L}}_{S^{\mathsf{o}}}\mathrm{e}^{-\|X_{S^{\mathsf{o}}}^{\top}\|\sqrt{\xi^{\delta}(t)}}\leqslant\beta_{S^{\mathsf{o}}}(t)\leqslant\beta^{\mathsf{L}}_{S^{\mathsf{o}}}\mathrm{e}^{\|X_{S^{\mathsf{o}}}^{\top}\|\sqrt{\xi^{\delta}(t)}}.

(13)

Furthermore,
\xi^{\delta}(t)
converges in distribution towards a random variable

which satisfies the concentration property

\forall u>0,\qquad\mathbb{P}\left(\xi^{\delta}_{\infty}\geqslant u\delta\right)\leqslant 6\exp\left(-\sqrt{\frac{u\delta}{\kappa^{\delta}}}\right),

(14)

for some
\kappa^{\delta}>0
such that

(ii)

Outside the support

\forall t\geqslant 0,\qquad\beta_{{S^{\mathsf{o}}}^{c}}(t)\leqslant C\exp\left(\|X_{{S^{\mathsf{o}}}^{c}}^{\top}\|\sqrt{\xi^{\delta}(t)}\mathds{1}_{{S^{\mathsf{o}}}^{c}}-\mu^{\mathsf{L}}_{{S^{\mathsf{o}}}^{c}}t\right).

\lim_{t\to+\infty}\beta_{{S^{\mathsf{o}}}^{c}}(t)=0,\qquad\text{almost surely.}

We make the following remarks on the theorem. In the long term limit, for
\delta
small, Equation (

is of order
\mathcal{O}(\delta)

n

\beta^{\mathsf{o}}

\beta_{S^{\mathsf{o}}}(t)\simeq\beta^{\mathsf{L}}_{S^{\mathsf{o}}}+\mathcal{O}\left(\sqrt{\delta}\right)=\beta_{S^{\mathsf{o}}}^{\mathsf{o}}+\mathcal{O}\left(\sqrt{\delta}\right),

is of order
\delta

On the other hand, from Equation (
15

at rate
\mu^{\mathsf{L}}_{S^{c}}>0

(\xi^{\delta}(t))_{t\geqslant 0}

effective
strong convexity effect that prevents the noise from truly fluctuating far from some deterministic trajectory.

Precise derivations on
(\xi^{\delta}(t))_{t\geqslant 0}
and explicit constants can be found in Appendix
B.2

4
Dynamics description and proof sketch

4.1
Going beyond the mirror shape: a dual process

The time dependent mirror.

Previous analysis
(
Gunasekar et al. 2018
;
Woodworth et al. 2020
;
Pesme et al. 2021
)
on the implicit bias of such models rely on the crucial observation that the dual iterates
\nabla\psi(\beta(t))
belong to the linear span of the observations
\mathrm{span}(X^{\top})
.
However, in our case, the additional drift term does not belong to such a space: i.e.
\delta h\notin\mathrm{span}(X^{\top})
. This is why we introduce a time dependent mirror map
\nabla_{\beta}\psi(t,\beta)=\log(\beta\mathrm{e}^{\delta ht})
. By Eq. (
8
), the process
(\beta(t))_{0\leqslant t<\tau_{\infty}}
then satisfies

\mathrm{d}\nabla_{\beta}\psi(t,\beta(t))=-2X^{\top}(X\beta(t)-y)\mathrm{d}t+2\sqrt{\delta}X^{\top}\mathrm{d}B(t),

which is a stochastic mirror-like descent with a geometry that depends on time. The vector
\nabla_{\beta}\psi(t,\beta(t))
can then be decomposed in a unique way along
\mathrm{span}(X^{\top})
and
(\mathrm{span}(X^{\top}))^{\perp}=\mathrm{Ker}X
.
More precisely, there exist
u_{*}\in\mathrm{Ker}X
and
v(0)\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
such that
\log\beta(0)=u_{*}+X^{\top}v(0)
. We next consider the unique strong solution to the SDE

\mathrm{d}v(t)=-2\left(X\exp(X^{\top}v(t)+u_{*}-\delta ht)-y\right)\mathrm{d}t+2\sqrt{\delta}\mathrm{d}B(t),

and notice that
\exp(u_{*}+X^{\top}v(t)-\delta ht)
then satisfies the SDE (
7
).
Therefore, by pathwise uniqueness,
\beta(t)=\exp(u_{*}+X^{\top}v(t)-\delta ht)
and both processes
\beta
and
v
share the same explosion time
\tau_{\infty}
.

Defining a dual process.

We next remove the constant term
2y
in the drift of
v(t)
by defining
\widetilde{v}(t):=v(t)-2yt\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Denoting by
F(t,\widetilde{v}):=\|\exp(X^{\top}\widetilde{v}+u_{*}-(\delta h-2X^{\top}y)t)\|_{1}
, we remark that
(\widetilde{v}(t))_{0\leqslant t<\tau_{\infty}}
satisfies the SDE

\mathrm{d}\widetilde{v}(t)=-2\nabla_{v}F(t,\widetilde{v}(t))\mathrm{d}t+2\sqrt{\delta}\mathrm{d}B(t).

(16)

We define as well the associated gradient flow
(\widetilde{w}(t))_{0\leqslant t<\widetilde{\tau}_{\infty}}
:

\mathrm{d}\widetilde{w}(t)=-2\nabla_{v}F(t,\widetilde{w}(t))\mathrm{d}t,

(17)

initialised similarly, that is
\widetilde{w}(0)=\widetilde{v}(0)=v(0)
, and defined up to some (deterministic) explosion time
\widetilde{\tau}_{\infty}
.
For
t\geqslant 0
we also introduce, with a slight abuse of notation, the map
\beta(t,\cdot)
defined from
\mathbb{R}^{n}
to
\mathbb{R}^{d}
as
\beta(t,\widetilde{v}):=\exp(X^{\top}\widetilde{v}+u_{*}-(\delta h-2X^{\top}y)t)
.
This map represents the unique way to go from the process
\widetilde{v}(t)
, solution of the SDE (
16
), to the associated process of linear predictors
\beta(t)
that follows the SDE (
7
).
Thus, we refer to
\widetilde{v}(t)
as
the dual process
associated to
\beta(t)
.
It is also worth noting that
F(t,\widetilde{v})=\|\beta(t,\widetilde{v})\|_{1}
is a convex function of the second variable. This equality emphasises the sparse promoting effect of the dynamics on the linear predictor. Finally, an important quantity appearing in the exponential is the vector

c:=\delta h-2X^{\top}y.

In the two next sections, we show that, depending on its sign, the behaviour of Eq. (
16
) and (
17
) is very different.

4.2
Large noise regime and the lack of strong convexity

In the setting of Theorem
4
, the vector
c
coincides with
\mu^{\mathsf{L}}
defined in Theorem
3
, and thus satisfies
c>0
.
Hence the noise dominates the dynamics and entails that all coordinates of
\beta(t,\widetilde{v}(t))
are shrunk by a
\mathrm{e}^{-\mu^{\mathsf{L}}t}
factor. Here, we proceed in two steps:

(i)

First, we show that the gradient flow (
17
) on the dynamics of the process
(\widetilde{w}(t))_{0\leqslant t<\widetilde{\tau}_{\infty}}
is bounded.
Therefore,
\widetilde{\tau}_{\infty}=+\infty
and the associated linear estimator
\beta(t,\widetilde{w}(t))
goes to
0
at rate
\mathrm{e}^{-\mu^{\mathsf{L}}t}
.

(ii)

Then, we show that the stochastic process (
16
) on
\widetilde{v}(t)
fluctuates from the gradient flow
\widetilde{w}(t)
at a distance controlled by the norm of an
n
-dimensional Brownian motion, i.e. a Bessel process. Hence, as Brownian fluctuations are of order
\sqrt{t}
, these are negligible in front of the
-\mu^{\mathsf{L}}t
gradient flow decay rate, which yields in particular
\tau_{\infty}=+\infty
.

We put emphasis on the fact that this regime suffers form a lack of strong convexity. This is reminiscent of optimizing the Lasso outside of the support of the sparse ground-truth
\beta^{\mathsf{o}}
(see the discussion section 2.2 of
Agarwal et al. 2012
on
Restricted strong convexity and smoothness
).

4.3
Standard noise regime and strong convexity

Failing of the previous reasoning.

In the setting of Theorem
5
, it is expected that the dual gradient flow (
17
) drives the primal iterate
\beta(t,\widetilde{w}(t))
towards the solution of the weighted Lasso
\beta^{\mathsf{L}}
.
The strategy of comparison with the gradient flow, outlined in the previous section, only works for the coordinates of
\beta(t,\widetilde{v}(t))
outside of the support
S^{\mathsf{o}}
of
\beta^{\mathsf{L}}
.
For these coordinates, the fluctuations are still dominated by the linear exponential shrinking.
Unfortunately, when applying the same technique for coordinates in the support
S^{\mathsf{o}}
, the fluctuations totally blur the sparse recovery problem.
To overcome this obstacle, we leverage the strong convexity of the function
\widetilde{v}\mapsto F(t,\widetilde{v})
on the support
S^{\mathsf{o}}
, as explained in the following paragraph.

A linear classification problem structure.

The reasoning rests on the following observation: the dual gradient flow (
17
) is similar to the one that solves a linear classification problem with the exponential loss
on the data points given by the coordinates of the
(x_{i})_{1\leqslant i\leqslant n}
.
More precisely, let us define the
d
data
(z_{k})_{1\leqslant k\leqslant d}\in(\mathbb{R}^{n})^{d}
such that
Z=X^{\top}
, i.e for all
k\in\llbracket 1,d\rrbracket
,
i\in\llbracket 1,n\rrbracket

[z_{k}]_{i}=[x_{i}]_{k}
. Then (
17
) is equivalent to

\mathrm{d}\widetilde{w}(t)=-2Z^{\top}\exp(Z\widetilde{w}(t)+u_{*}-ct)\mathrm{d}t=-2\sum_{k=1}^{d}z_{k}\exp\left(\langle z_{k},\widetilde{w}(t)\rangle+[u_{*}]_{k}-c_{k}t\right)\mathrm{d}t,

and the same goes for (
16
). Note that this observation is reminiscent of the primal-dual analysis presented in
Ji and Telgarsky 2021
.
We leverage this equivalence and show that the selection of the support vectors (i.e, the samples with the smallest margin) of separable linear classification problem
(
Soudry et al. 2018
)
enables to recover the true support
S^{\mathsf{o}}
.
In this aim, we show that the only terms that matter in the exponential sum are those in the set
S^{\prime}
of indices
k
for which
\langle z_{k},\widetilde{w}(t)\rangle\sim c_{k}t
when
t\to+\infty
,
since the other terms go to zero exponentially fast.
Hence,
\beta(t,\widetilde{w}(t))
is asymptotically supported by the coordinates of
S^{\prime}
. As in the separable classification problem
(
Soudry et al. 2018
)
, it turns out that we can totally identify
S^{\prime}
as in fact
S^{\prime}=S^{\mathsf{o}}
. Finally, we show that the fluctuations due to the stochastic remaining terms are controlled by the strong convexity upon the support and are of order
\sqrt{\delta}
.

6
Conclusion

In this paper, we have shown that, for quadratically parametrised predictors, the label noise gradient descent solves implicitly a weighted Lasso optimisation program. Hence, this stochastic descent is able to perfectly recover the support of the sparse ground-truth when the injected noise is not too large. In contrast with previous works, we derived precise nonasymptotic results, both it terms of time and noise. Surprisingly, the heart of the proof is based on an equivalence between the selection of support vectors for a classification problem and the one of the nonzero coordinates of the sparse ground-truth. Whether this is only a technical equivalence or a deep relationship could be of great interest. Also, we characterise the equivalence between our label noise dynamics and an optimisation problem enforcing sparsity. Whether it can be done for other architecture remains an interesting open question.

Proposition 9

Let

be the solution of the gradient flow (

\displaystyle\forall w\in\mathbb{R}^{n},\qquad\|\widetilde{w}(t)-w\|_{2}\leqslant\|w(0)-w\|_{2}+2\|\mathrm{e}^{X^{\top}w+u_{*}}\|_{2}\frac{\|X\|}{\uline{c}}.

Proof.

Thanks to Lemma
8

t\in[0,\widetilde{\tau}_{\infty})

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\frac{1}{2}\|\widetilde{w}(t)-w\|_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle=\left\langle\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\widetilde{w}(t),\widetilde{w}(t)-w\right\rangle

\displaystyle=-2\langle\nabla_{v}F(t,\widetilde{w}(t)),\widetilde{w}(t)-w\rangle

\displaystyle=-2\langle\nabla_{v}F(t,\widetilde{w}(t))-\nabla_{v}F(t,w),\widetilde{w}(t)-w\rangle-2\langle\nabla_{v}F(t,w),\widetilde{w}(t)-w\rangle

\displaystyle\leqslant-2\langle\nabla_{v}F(t,w),\widetilde{w}(t)-w\rangle

\displaystyle\leqslant 2\|\nabla_{v}F(t,w)\|_{2}\|\widetilde{w}(t)-w\|_{2}

\displaystyle=2\|X\mathrm{e}^{X^{\top}w+u_{*}-ct}\|_{2}\|\widetilde{w}(t)-w\|_{2}

. Then, denote

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}f^{\prime}(t)

\displaystyle\leqslant C\mathrm{e}^{-\uline{c}t}\sqrt{f(t)},

so that, for any

satisfies

\displaystyle[g^{\varepsilon}(t)]^{\prime}=\frac{f^{\prime}(t)}{2\sqrt{f(t)+\varepsilon}}

\displaystyle\leqslant C\mathrm{e}^{-\uline{c}t}\frac{\sqrt{f(t)}}{\sqrt{f(t)+\varepsilon}}\leqslant C\mathrm{e}^{-\uline{c}t},

and by direct integration,

\displaystyle\leqslant g^{\varepsilon}(0)+C\int_{0}^{t}\mathrm{e}^{-\uline{c}s}\mathrm{d}s=g^{\varepsilon}(0)+\frac{C}{\uline{c}}(1-\mathrm{e}^{-\uline{c}t})\leqslant\sqrt{f(0)+\varepsilon}+\frac{C}{\uline{c}}.

B.1.2
Control of the stochasticity: comparison between gradient and stochastic gradient flow

The aim of this section is to bound the difference between the SDE solution of Eq. (
16
) and the gradient flow solution of Eq. (
17
). By Itô calculus, we have for
t\in[0,\tau_{\infty})
,

\displaystyle\mathrm{d}\|\widetilde{v}(t)-\widetilde{w}(t)\|_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle=2\langle\widetilde{v}(t)-\widetilde{w}(t),\mathrm{d}\widetilde{v}(t)-\mathrm{d}\widetilde{w}(t)\rangle+4n\delta\mathrm{d}t

We begin by rewriting the previous equation with a one dimensional Brownian motion.

Lemma 10
.

There exists a one dimensional Brownian motion
(W(t))_{t\geqslant 0}
such that, almost surely, for
t\in[0,\tau_{\infty})
,

\displaystyle\mathrm{d}\|\widetilde{v}(t)-\widetilde{w}(t)\|_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle=-4\left(\langle\widetilde{v}(t)-\widetilde{w}(t),\nabla_{v}F(t,\widetilde{v}(t))-\nabla_{v}F(t,\widetilde{w}(t))\rangle-n\delta\right)\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle\quad+4\sqrt{\delta}\|\widetilde{v}(t)-\widetilde{w}(t)\|_{2}\mathrm{d}W(t).

Proof.

From regularity theory of elliptic equations, for any
t>0
the law of
\widetilde{v}(t)
on the event
\{t<\tau_{\infty}\}
is absolutely continuous with respect to the Lebesgue measure and hence
\mathbb{P}(t<\tau_{\infty},\widetilde{v}(t)=\widetilde{w}(t))=0
. As a consequence,
\mathbb{E}[\int_{0}^{t}\mathds{1}_{\{s<\tau_{\infty},\widetilde{v}(s)=\widetilde{w}(s)\}}\mathrm{d}s]=\int_{0}^{t}\mathbb{P}(s<\tau_{\infty},\widetilde{v}(s)=\widetilde{w}(s))\mathrm{d}s=0
, hence
\int_{0}^{t}\mathds{1}_{\{s<\tau_{\infty},\widetilde{v}(s)=\widetilde{w}(s)\}}\mathrm{d}s=0
, almost surely. Therefore, by Levy’s characterisation, the local martingale
(W(t))_{t\geqslant 0}
defined by

\forall t\geqslant 0,\qquad W(t)=\int_{0}^{t}\left(\mathds{1}_{\{s<\tau_{\infty}\}}\frac{\langle\widetilde{v}(s)-\widetilde{w}(s),\mathrm{d}B(s)\rangle}{\|\widetilde{v}(s)-\widetilde{w}(s)\|_{2}}+\mathds{1}_{\{s\geqslant\tau_{\infty}\}}\mathrm{d}B^{1}(s)\right),

where

is the first coordinate of

, is a Brownian motion. This completes the proof.
∎

From this, using Lemma
8

\displaystyle\mathrm{d}\|\widetilde{v}(t)-\widetilde{w}(t)\|_{2}^{2}\leqslant 4n\delta\mathrm{d}t+4\sqrt{\delta}\|\widetilde{v}(t)-\widetilde{w}(t)\|_{2}\mathrm{d}W(t).

n

Proposition 11

The stochastic differential equation

\mathrm{d}\zeta^{\delta}(t)=4n\delta\mathrm{d}t+4\sqrt{\delta\zeta^{\delta}(t)}\mathrm{d}W(t),\qquad\zeta^{\delta}(0)=0,

(i)

(ii)

almost surely, for any

Proof.

First, we define

and deduce from Itô’s formula that

\mathrm{d}\widetilde{\zeta}^{\delta}(t)=4n\delta\mathrm{d}t+8\delta\langle B(t),\mathrm{d}B(t)\rangle.

By the same arguments as in the proof of Lemma
10

(\widetilde{W}(t))_{t\geqslant 0}
such that

\displaystyle=-4\langle X\left(\mathrm{e}^{X^{\top}r(t)+X^{\top}v^{\mathsf{L}}+u_{*}-u_{*}^{\prime}-\mu^{\mathsf{L}}t}-\beta^{\mathsf{L}}\right),\,r(t)\rangle\mathrm{d}t+4n\delta\mathrm{d}t+4\sqrt{\delta}\langle r(t),\mathrm{d}B(t)\rangle

\displaystyle=-4\sum_{k\in S^{\mathsf{o}}}\mathrm{e}^{\langle z_{k},v^{\mathsf{L}}\rangle}\left(\mathrm{e}^{\langle z_{k},r(t)\rangle}-1\right)\langle z_{k},r(t)\rangle\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle\quad-4\sum_{k\in{S^{\mathsf{o}}}^{c}}\mathrm{e}^{\langle z_{k},v^{\mathsf{L}}\rangle+[u_{*}]_{k}-[u_{*}^{\prime}]_{k}-\mu^{\mathsf{L}}_{k}t}\mathrm{e}^{\langle z_{k},r(t)\rangle}\langle z_{k},r(t)\rangle\mathrm{d}t+4n\delta\mathrm{d}t+4\sqrt{\delta}\langle r(t),\mathrm{d}B(t)\rangle.

By the same argument as in Lemma
10
, there exists a one dimensional Brownian motion
(W(t))_{t\geqslant 0}
such that for all
t\in[0,\tau_{\infty})
,

\displaystyle\mathrm{d}\|r(t)\|_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle=-4\sum_{k\in S^{\mathsf{o}}}\beta^{\mathsf{L}}_{k}\left(\mathrm{e}^{\langle z_{k},r(t)\rangle}-1\right)\langle z_{k},r(t)\rangle\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle\quad-4\sum_{k\in{S^{\mathsf{o}}}^{c}}\mathrm{e}^{\langle z_{k},v^{\mathsf{L}}\rangle+[u_{*}]_{k}-[u_{*}^{\prime}]_{k}-\mu^{\mathsf{L}}_{k}t}\mathrm{e}^{\langle z_{k},r(t)\rangle}\langle z_{k},r(t)\rangle\mathrm{d}t+4n\delta\mathrm{d}t+4\sqrt{\delta}\|r(t)\|\mathrm{d}W(t).

Using that for any
x\in\mathbb{R}
,
-x\mathrm{e}^{x}\leqslant|x|
, defining
\uline{\mu}=\min_{k\in{S^{\mathsf{o}}}^{c}}\mu^{\mathsf{L}}_{k}
, and finally noting the sum
b=\sum_{k\in{S^{\mathsf{o}}}^{c}}\mathrm{e}^{\langle z_{k},v^{\mathsf{L}}\rangle+[u_{*}]_{k}-[u_{*}^{\prime}]_{k}}\|z_{k}\|_{2}
, we get that the second term is upper bounded by
b\mathrm{e}^{-\uline{\mu}t}\|r(t)\|_{2}
.

The first term is a bit more involved and crucially rests on the strong convexity on the support given by the invertibility of
X^{\top}_{S^{\mathsf{o}}}X_{S^{\mathsf{o}}}
. Indeed, first note that for any
x\in\mathbb{R}
,
x(\mathrm{e}^{x}-1)\geqslant x^{2}/(1+|x|)
. Moreover if we denote
a=\min_{k\in S^{\mathsf{o}}}{\beta^{\mathsf{L}}_{k}}
,
\Omega=\sup_{k\in S^{\mathsf{o}}}\|z_{k}\|_{2}
, and let
\rho_{S^{\mathsf{o}}}>0
such that
X^{\top}_{S^{\mathsf{o}}}X_{S^{\mathsf{o}}}\geqslant\rho_{S^{\mathsf{o}}}I_{S^{\mathsf{o}}}
,

\displaystyle-4\sum_{k\in S^{\mathsf{o}}}\beta^{\mathsf{L}}_{k}\left(\mathrm{e}^{\langle z_{k},r(t)\rangle}-1\right)\langle z_{k},r(t)\rangle

\displaystyle\leqslant-4a\sum_{k\in S^{\mathsf{o}}}\frac{\langle z_{k},r(t)\rangle^{2}}{1+|\langle z_{k},r(t)\rangle|}

\displaystyle\leqslant-4a\sum_{k\in S^{\mathsf{o}}}\frac{\langle z_{k},r(t)\rangle^{2}}{1+\Omega\|r(t)\|_{2}}

\displaystyle\leqslant-\frac{4a}{1+\Omega\|r(t)\|_{2}}\sum_{k\in S^{\mathsf{o}}}\langle z_{k},r(t)\rangle^{2}

\displaystyle\leqslant-4a\rho_{S^{\mathsf{o}}}\frac{\|r(t)\|_{2}^{2}}{1+\Omega\|r(t)\|_{2}}.

Finally,

\mathrm{d}\|r(t)\|_{2}^{2}\leqslant\left(4n\delta-4a\rho_{S^{\mathsf{o}}}\frac{\|r(t)\|_{2}^{2}}{1+\Omega\|r(t)\|_{2}}+4b\mathrm{e}^{-\uline{\mu}t}\|r(t)\|_{2}\right)\mathrm{d}t+4\sqrt{\delta}\|r(t)\|_{2}\mathrm{d}W(t).

(19)

B.2.3
The comparison process
(\xi^{\delta}(t))_{t\geqslant 0}
and the first part of the theorem

Everything is now in order to apply the same SDE comparison argument we detailed in the nonstrongly convex case in Subsection
B.1.2
.

Proposition 14
.

Assume that
n\geqslant 2
and
r(0)\neq 0
. The stochastic differential equation

\mathrm{d}\xi^{\delta}(t)=\left(4n\delta-4a\rho_{S^{\mathsf{o}}}\frac{\xi^{\delta}(t)}{1+\Omega\sqrt{\xi^{\delta}(t)}}+4b\mathrm{e}^{-\uline{\mu}t}\sqrt{\xi^{\delta}(t)}\right)\mathrm{d}t+4\sqrt{\delta\xi^{\delta}(t)}\mathrm{d}W(t),

(20)

initialised at
\xi^{\delta}(0)=\|r(0)\|_{2}^{2}
, has a unique strong solution
(\xi^{\delta}(t))_{t\geqslant 0}
, which is defined globally in time. Besides:

(i)

the process
(\xi^{\delta}(t))_{t\geqslant 0}
has the same law as
(\|R^{\delta}(t)\|_{2}^{2})_{t\geqslant 0}
, where
(R^{\delta}(t))_{t\geqslant 0}
is the unique strong solution to the
n
-dimensional SDE

\mathrm{d}R^{\delta}(t)=\left(-2a\rho_{S^{\mathsf{o}}}\frac{R^{\delta}(t)}{1+\Omega\|R^{\delta}(t)\|_{2}}+2b\mathrm{e}^{-\uline{\mu}t}\frac{R^{\delta}(t)}{\|R^{\delta}(t)\|_{2}}\right)\mathrm{d}t+2\sqrt{\delta}\mathrm{d}B(t),

(21)

initialised at
R^{\delta}(0)=r(0)
;

(ii)

almost surely, for any
t\in[0,\tau_{\infty})
,
\|r(t)\|_{2}^{2}\leqslant\xi^{\delta}(t)
.

Proof.

The well-posedness of (
21
) follows from
Veretennikov 1981
, and Itô’s formula together with Lévy’s characterisation show that the process
(\|R^{\delta}(t)\|_{2}^{2})_{t\geqslant 0}
is a weak solution to (
20
). However, in contrast with the proof of Proposition
11
, the presence of the square root in the drift of the SDE (
20
) prevents us from using standard results to claim pathwise uniqueness. To recover this property, we note that since the drift remains Lipschitz continuous, uniformly in time, on all sets of the form
[\varepsilon,+\infty)
,
\varepsilon>0
, any two strong solutions to (
20
) coincide, and have the same law as the process
\|R^{\delta}(t)\|_{2}
, until they hit
0
. As a consequence, to obtain pathwise uniqueness for (
20
) it suffices to check that, almost surely,
R^{\delta}(t)
never hits
0
. But since the drift in (
21
) is bounded, uniformly in time, the Girsanov theorem shows that for any
T>0
, the laws of
(R^{\delta}(t))_{t\in[0,T]}
and
(r(0)+2\sqrt{\delta}B(t))_{t\in[0,T]}
are equivalent, and therefore

\mathbb{P}\left(\exists t\in[0,T]:R^{\delta}(t)=0\right)=\mathbb{P}\left(\exists t\in[0,T]:r(0)+2\sqrt{\delta}B(t)=0\right)=0,

where the second equality is well-known in dimension
n\geqslant 2
.
This implies that, almost surely,
R^{\delta}(t)\not=0
, for any
t\geqslant 0
, and therefore completes the proof of pathwise uniqueness for (
20
).

We now detail the comparison between
\|r(t)\|_{2}^{2}
and
\xi^{\delta}(t)
. For any
M>M_{0}:=1\vee\|r(0)\|_{2}^{-2}
, we set
\tau^{\prime}_{M}:=\inf\{t\in[0,\tau_{\infty}):\|r(t)\|^{2}_{2}\wedge\xi^{\delta}(t)\leqslant 1/M\text{ or }\|\beta(t)\|_{2}\geqslant M\}
, so that
\limsup_{M\to+\infty}\tau^{\prime}_{M}=\tau_{0}\wedge\tau_{\infty}
, with
\tau_{0}:=\inf\{t\in[0,\tau_{\infty}):\|r(t)\|_{2}=0\}
(we recall that, almost surely,
\xi^{\delta}(t)
never hits
0
). With similar arguments to
(
Revuz and Yor 2013
, Theorem 3.7, Chapter IX)
, we may write

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\left(\|r(t\wedge\tau^{\prime}_{M})\|^{2}_{2}-\xi^{\delta}(t\wedge\tau^{\prime}_{M})\right)_{+}\right]

\displaystyle\leqslant\mathbb{E}\left[\int_{0}^{t\wedge\tau^{\prime}_{M}}\mathds{1}_{\{\|r(s)\|^{2}_{2}\geqslant\xi^{\delta}(s)\}}\left|g(s,\|r(s)\|^{2}_{2})-g(s,\xi^{\delta}(s))\right|\mathrm{d}s\right],

where
g(t,\xi)=4n\delta-4a\rho_{S^{\mathsf{o}}}\xi/(1+\Omega\sqrt{\xi})+4b\mathrm{e}^{-\uline{\mu}t}\sqrt{\xi}
is the drift of (
20
). Denoting by
C_{M}
the Lipschitz constant of
g(s,\cdot)
on
[1/M,+\infty)
, which is uniform in
s
, we get

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\left(\|r(t\wedge\tau^{\prime}_{M})\|^{2}_{2}-\xi^{\delta}(t\wedge\tau^{\prime}_{M})\right)_{+}\right]

\displaystyle\leqslant C_{M}\mathbb{E}\left[\int_{0}^{t\wedge\tau^{\prime}_{M}}\mathds{1}_{\{\|r(s)\|^{2}_{2}\geqslant\xi^{\delta}(s)\}}\left|\|r(s)\|^{2}_{2}-\xi^{\delta}(s)\right|\mathrm{d}s\right]

\displaystyle=C_{M}\mathbb{E}\left[\int_{0}^{t\wedge\tau^{\prime}_{M}}\left(\|r(s)\|^{2}_{2}-\xi^{\delta}(s)\right)_{+}\mathrm{d}s\right]

\displaystyle\leqslant C_{M}\int_{0}^{t}\mathbb{E}\left[\left(\|r(s\wedge\tau^{\prime}_{M})\|^{2}_{2}-\xi^{\delta}(s\wedge\tau^{\prime}_{M})\right)_{+}\right]\mathrm{d}s,

which by Gronwall’s lemma and the continuity of the trajectories of
\|r(t\wedge\tau^{\prime}_{M})\|^{2}_{2}-\xi^{\delta}(t\wedge\tau^{\prime}_{M})
yields, almost surely,

\forall t\geqslant 0,\quad\forall M>M_{0},\qquad\|r(t\wedge\tau^{\prime}_{M})\|^{2}_{2}\leqslant\xi^{\delta}(t\wedge\tau^{\prime}_{M}),

and therefore, almost surely,

\forall t\in[0,\tau_{\infty}\wedge\tau_{0}),\qquad\|r(t)\|^{2}_{2}\leqslant\xi^{\delta}(t).

To complete the proof, let us fix
T\in[0,\tau_{\infty})
and call
z:=\inf_{t\in[0,T]}\xi^{\delta}(t)>0
. The previous argument shows that
\|r(t)\|^{2}_{2}\leqslant\xi^{\delta}(t)
for all
t\leqslant T\wedge\tau_{0}
, and if
\tau_{0}<T
, this inequality remains trivially true as long as
t\leqslant\tau^{(1)}_{z}:=\inf\{t\in[\tau_{0},T]:\|r(t)\|_{2}^{2}\geqslant z\}
. But if
\tau^{(1)}_{z}<T
, the argument above can be repeated to show that the inequality holds up to
\tau^{(1)}_{0}:=\inf\{t\in[\tau^{(1)}_{z},T]:\|r(t)\|_{2}=0\}
, and then, if
\tau^{(1)}_{0}<T
, up to
\tau^{(2)}_{z}:=\inf\{t\in[\tau^{(1)}_{0},T]:\|r(t)\|_{2}^{2}\geqslant z\}
. Iterating the argument, we thus construct two sequences
\tau^{(l)}_{0},\tau^{(l)}_{z}\leqslant T
such that
\|r(\tau^{(l)}_{0})\|_{2}=0
if
\tau^{(l)}_{0}<T
, and
\|r(\tau^{(l)}_{z})\|_{2}^{2}\geqslant z
if
\tau^{(l)}_{z}<T
, and such that the inequality
\|r(t)\|^{2}_{2}\leqslant\xi^{\delta}(t)
holds on
[0,\tau^{(l)}_{0}]
. By continuity of the trajectory of
r(t)
on
[0,T]
, there are only finitely many
\tau^{(l)}_{0},\tau^{(l)}_{z}
which are strictly below
T
, and therefore the inequality
\|r(t)\|^{2}_{2}\leqslant\xi^{\delta}(t)
finally holds on
[0,T]
.
∎

We are now in place to give a bound on the iterates
\beta(t,\widetilde{v}(t))
for all
t\in[0,\tau_{\infty})
.

Theorem 15
.

Let
(\xi^{\delta}(t))_{t\geqslant 0}
be defined in Proposition
14
.

(i)

On the support: almost surely,

\forall t\in[0,\tau_{\infty}),\qquad\beta^{\mathsf{L}}_{S^{\mathsf{o}}}\mathrm{e}^{-\|X_{S^{\mathsf{o}}}^{\top}\|\sqrt{\xi^{\delta}(t)}}\leqslant\beta_{S^{\mathsf{o}}}(t)\leqslant\beta^{\mathsf{L}}_{S^{\mathsf{o}}}\mathrm{e}^{\|X_{S^{\mathsf{o}}}^{\top}\|\sqrt{\xi^{\delta}(t)}}.

(ii)

Outside the support: there exists
C\geqslant 0
, depending on
X
,
y
and
\beta(0)
, such that, almost surely,

\forall t\in[0,\tau_{\infty}),\qquad\beta_{{S^{\mathsf{o}}}^{c}}(t)\leqslant C\exp\left(\|X_{{S^{\mathsf{o}}}^{c}}^{\top}\|\sqrt{\xi^{\delta}(t)}\mathds{1}_{{S^{\mathsf{o}}}^{c}}-\mu^{\mathsf{L}}_{{S^{\mathsf{o}}}^{c}}t\right).

In particular,
\tau_{\infty}=+\infty
, almost surely.

Proof.

We transfer the estimates on
\widetilde{v}(t)
to the iterates of the initial flow on the linear predictor
\beta(t)
. Indeed as
X^{\top}\widetilde{v}(t)=X^{\top}r(t)+X^{\top}v^{\infty}t+X^{\top}v^{\mathsf{L}}-[[u_{*}]_{S^{\mathsf{o}}},u_{*}^{\prime}]^{\top}
, we get for
t\in[0,\tau_{\infty})
,

\displaystyle\beta(t)

\displaystyle=\exp\left(X^{\top}\widetilde{v}(t)+u_{*}-ct\right)

\displaystyle=\exp\left(X^{\top}r(t)+X^{\top}v^{\infty}t+X^{\top}v^{\mathsf{L}}-[[u_{*}]_{S^{\mathsf{o}}},u_{*}^{\prime}]^{\top}+u_{*}-ct\right)

\displaystyle=\exp\left(X^{\top}r(t)+X^{\top}v^{\mathsf{L}}+[0_{S^{\mathsf{o}}},[u_{*}]_{{S^{\mathsf{o}}}^{c}}-u_{*}^{\prime}]^{\top}+(X^{\top}v^{\infty}-c)t\right)

\displaystyle=\exp\left(X^{\top}r(t)+X^{\top}v^{\mathsf{L}}+[0_{S^{\mathsf{o}}},[u_{*}]_{{S^{\mathsf{o}}}^{c}}-u_{*}^{\prime}]^{\top}-\mu^{\mathsf{L}}t\right),

thanks to Lemma
13
. On the support, we have
[\mathrm{e}^{X^{\top}v^{\mathsf{L}}}]_{S^{\mathsf{o}}}=\beta^{\mathsf{L}}_{S^{\mathsf{o}}}
and
\mu^{\mathsf{L}}_{S^{\mathsf{o}}}=0
. Hence,
\beta_{S^{\mathsf{o}}}(t)=\beta^{\mathsf{L}}_{S^{\mathsf{o}}}\odot\exp\left(X_{S^{\mathsf{o}}}^{\top}r(t)\right)
, and the first part of the Theorem follows from Proposition
14
. The second part of the Theorem follows similarly, with
C=\max_{k\in{S^{\mathsf{o}}}^{c}}\exp([X^{\top}v^{\mathsf{L}}]_{k}+[u_{*}]_{k}-[u_{*}^{\prime}]_{k})
. Finally, the fact that
\xi^{\delta}(t)
does not explode yields
\tau_{\infty}=+\infty
, which completes the proof.
∎

B.2.4
Study of the process
(R^{\delta}(t))_{t\geqslant 0}
and the second part of the theorem

This section is dedicated to a detailed study of the long-time behaviour of the process
(R^{\delta}(t))_{t\geqslant 0}
, and therefore of
(\xi^{\delta}(t))_{t\geqslant 0}
. To proceed, we first rewrite (
20
) under the form

\mathrm{d}R^{\delta}(t)=G(t,R^{\delta}(t))\mathrm{d}t+2\sqrt{\delta}\mathrm{d}B(t),\qquad G(t,r):=-\nabla V(r)+2b\mathrm{e}^{-\uline{\mu}t}\frac{r}{\|r\|_{2}},

where
V:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}
is the
C^{1}
function with globally bounded gradient defined by

V(r):=\frac{2a\rho_{S^{\mathsf{o}}}}{\Omega^{2}}\left(\Omega\|r\|_{2}-\log(1+\Omega\|r\|_{2})\right).

(22)

We also introduce the time-homogeneous diffusion process

\mathrm{d}\overline{R}^{\delta}(t)=-\nabla V(\overline{R}^{\delta}(t))\mathrm{d}t+2\sqrt{\delta}\mathrm{d}B(t),\qquad\overline{R}^{\delta}(0)=r(0).

We first state a trajectorial comparison result between
R^{\delta}(t)
and
\overline{R}^{\delta}(t)
.

Proposition 16
.

Almost surely,

\sup_{t\geqslant 0}\|\overline{R}^{\delta}(t)-R^{\delta}(t)\|_{2}\leqslant\frac{b}{\uline{\mu}},\qquad\text{and}\qquad\lim_{t\to+\infty}\|\overline{R}^{\delta}(t)-R^{\delta}(t)\|_{2}=0.

Proof.

Step 1. Convexity of
V
.
We start the proof by showing that
V
is convex on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
and bounding
\nabla^{2}V(r)
from below. To proceed, we compute

\nabla^{2}V(r)=\frac{2a\rho_{S^{\mathsf{o}}}}{(1+\Omega\|r\|_{2})^{2}}\left((1+\Omega\|r\|_{2})I_{n}-\Omega\frac{rr^{\top}}{\|r\|_{2}}\right),

(23)

so that, for any
u\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
,

\displaystyle\langle u,\nabla^{2}V(r)u\rangle

\displaystyle=\frac{2a\rho_{S^{\mathsf{o}}}}{(1+\Omega\|r\|_{2})^{2}}\left((1+\Omega\|r\|_{2})\|u\|_{2}^{2}-\Omega\frac{\langle r,u\rangle^{2}}{\|r\|_{2}}\right)

\displaystyle\geqslant\frac{2a\rho_{S^{\mathsf{o}}}}{(1+\Omega\|r\|_{2})^{2}}\left((1+\Omega\|r\|_{2})\|u\|_{2}^{2}-\Omega\|r\|_{2}\|u\|_{2}^{2}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{2a\rho_{S^{\mathsf{o}}}}{1+\Omega\|r\|_{2}}\|u\|_{2}^{2}.

As a consequence, for any
r,\overline{r}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
,

\displaystyle\langle\overline{r}-r,\nabla V(\overline{r})-\nabla V(r)\rangle

\displaystyle=\left\langle\overline{r}-r,\int_{0}^{1}\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\nabla V(t\overline{r}+(1-t)r)\mathrm{d}t\right\rangle

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{1}\left\langle\overline{r}-r,\nabla^{2}V(t\overline{r}+(1-t)r)(\overline{r}-r)\right\rangle\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle\geqslant 2a\rho_{S^{\mathsf{o}}}\int_{0}^{1}\frac{\|\overline{r}-r\|^{2}_{2}}{1+\Omega\|t\overline{r}+(1-t)r\|_{2}}\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle\geqslant 2a\rho_{S^{\mathsf{o}}}\frac{\|\overline{r}-r\|^{2}_{2}}{1+\Omega(\|\overline{r}\|_{2}\vee\|r\|_{2})}.

Step 2. Global estimate.
Using only the nonnegativity of
\langle\overline{R}^{\delta}(t)-R^{\delta}(t),-\nabla V(\overline{R}^{\delta}(t))+\nabla V(R^{\delta}(t))\rangle
, we get, for any
t\geqslant 0
,

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\|\overline{R}^{\delta}(t)-R^{\delta}(t)\|^{2}_{2}

\displaystyle=2\left\langle\overline{R}^{\delta}(t)-R^{\delta}(t),-\nabla V(\overline{R}^{\delta}(t))+2b\mathrm{e}^{-\uline{\mu}t}\frac{R^{\delta}(t)}{\|R^{\delta}(t)\|_{2}}+\nabla V(R^{\delta}(t))\right\rangle

\displaystyle\leqslant 2b\mathrm{e}^{-\uline{\mu}t}\|\overline{R}^{\delta}(t)-R^{\delta}(t)\|_{2},

and therefore the global estimate on
\|\overline{R}^{\delta}(t)-R^{\delta}(t)\|_{2}
follows from Lemma
17
.

Step 3. Long time convergence.
We shall make use of this global estimate to prove the long time convergence of
\|\overline{R}^{\delta}(t)-R^{\delta}(t)\|_{2}
to
0
. We first note that, for any
r,\overline{r}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, if
\|\overline{r}-r\|_{2}\leqslant b/\uline{\mu}
, then
\|\overline{r}\|_{2}\vee\|r\|_{2}\leqslant\|\overline{r}\|_{2}+b/\uline{\mu}
and thus

\langle\overline{r}-r,\nabla V(\overline{r})-\nabla V(r)\rangle\geqslant 2a\rho_{S^{\mathsf{o}}}\frac{\|\overline{r}-r\|^{2}_{2}}{1+\Omega(\|\overline{r}\|_{2}+b/\uline{\mu})}.

Let us now fix
M>1
large enough for the inequality

\mu^{\prime}:=2a\rho_{S^{\mathsf{o}}}/(1+\Omega(M+b/\uline{\mu}))<\uline{\mu}

to hold, and define

\displaystyle\tau^{-}_{0}

\displaystyle:=\inf\{t\geqslant 0:\|\overline{R}^{\delta}(t)\|_{2}\leqslant M-1\},

\displaystyle\tau^{+}_{\ell}

\displaystyle:=\inf\{t\geqslant\tau^{-}_{\ell}:\|\overline{R}^{\delta}(t)\|_{2}\geqslant M\},\qquad\ell\geqslant 0,

\displaystyle\tau^{-}_{\ell}

\displaystyle:=\inf\{t\geqslant\tau^{+}_{\ell-1}:\|\overline{R}^{\delta}(t)\|_{2}\leqslant M-1\},\qquad\ell\geqslant 1.

Since
\|\overline{R}^{\delta}(t)\|_{2}^{2}
satisfies a time-homogeneous SDE and is ergodic, the sequences of positive random variables
(\tau^{+}_{\ell}-\tau^{-}_{\ell})_{\ell\geqslant 0}
and
(\tau^{-}_{\ell+1}-\tau^{+}_{\ell})_{\ell\geqslant 0}
are well-defined, and by the strong Markov property they are iid and therefore
\tau^{-}_{\ell},\tau^{+}_{\ell}\to+\infty
with
\ell
. Besides, on account on the previous discussion, we have

\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\|\overline{R}^{\delta}(t)-R^{\delta}(t)\|^{2}_{2}\leqslant-2\mu^{\prime}\|\overline{R}^{\delta}(t)-R^{\delta}(t)\|^{2}_{2}+2b\mathrm{e}^{-\uline{\mu}t}\|\overline{R}^{\delta}(t)-R^{\delta}(t)\|_{2},\qquad\text{on $[\tau^{-}_{\ell},\tau^{+}_{\ell}]$,}

and

\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\|\overline{R}^{\delta}(t)-R^{\delta}(t)\|^{2}_{2}\leqslant 2b\mathrm{e}^{-\uline{\mu}t}\|\overline{R}^{\delta}(t)-R^{\delta}(t)\|_{2},\qquad\text{on $[\tau^{+}_{\ell},\tau^{-}_{\ell+1}]$.}

We deduce from Lemma
17
that

\|\overline{R}^{\delta}(\tau^{-}_{0})-R^{\delta}(\tau^{-}_{0})\|_{2}\leqslant\frac{b}{\uline{\mu}}\left(1-\mathrm{e}^{-\uline{\mu}\tau^{-}_{0}}\right),

and for any
t\in[\tau^{-}_{\ell},\tau^{+}_{\ell}]
,

\|\overline{R}^{\delta}(t)-R^{\delta}(t)\|_{2}\leqslant\mathrm{e}^{-\mu^{\prime}(t-\tau^{-}_{\ell})}\|\overline{R}^{\delta}(\tau^{-}_{\ell})-R^{\delta}(\tau^{-}_{\ell})\|_{2}+\frac{b\mathrm{e}^{-\mu^{\prime}t}}{\uline{\mu}-\mu^{\prime}}\left(\mathrm{e}^{-(\uline{\mu}-\mu^{\prime})\tau^{-}_{\ell}}-\mathrm{e}^{-(\uline{\mu}-\mu^{\prime})t}\right),

and for any
t\in[\tau^{+}_{\ell},\tau^{-}_{\ell+1}]
,

\|\overline{R}^{\delta}(t)-R^{\delta}(t)\|_{2}\leqslant\|\overline{R}^{\delta}(\tau^{+}_{\ell})-R^{\delta}(\tau^{+}_{\ell})\|_{2}+\frac{b}{\uline{\mu}}\left(\mathrm{e}^{-\uline{\mu}\tau^{+}_{\ell}}-\mathrm{e}^{-\uline{\mu}t}\right).

As a consequence, the sequence
Q_{\ell}:=\|\overline{R}^{\delta}(\tau^{-}_{\ell})-R^{\delta}(\tau^{-}_{\ell})\|_{2}
satisfies the recursive inequation
Q_{\ell+1}\leqslant\alpha_{\ell}Q_{\ell}+\beta_{\ell}
, where

\alpha_{\ell}:=\mathrm{e}^{-\mu^{\prime}(\tau^{+}_{\ell}-\tau^{-}_{\ell})},\quad\beta_{\ell}:=\frac{b\mathrm{e}^{-\mu^{\prime}\tau^{+}_{\ell}}}{\uline{\mu}-\mu^{\prime}}\left(\mathrm{e}^{-(\uline{\mu}-\mu^{\prime})\tau^{-}_{\ell}}-\mathrm{e}^{-(\uline{\mu}-\mu^{\prime})\tau^{+}_{\ell}}\right)+\frac{b}{\uline{\mu}}\left(\mathrm{e}^{-\uline{\mu}\tau^{+}_{\ell}}-\mathrm{e}^{-\uline{\mu}\tau^{-}_{\ell+1}}\right),

and moreover we have the intermediate (rough) control

\sup_{\tau^{-}_{\ell}\leqslant t\leqslant\tau^{-}_{\ell+1}}\|\overline{R}^{\delta}(t)-R^{\delta}(t)\|_{2}\leqslant Q_{\ell}+\frac{b}{\uline{\mu}-\mu^{\prime}}\mathrm{e}^{-(\uline{\mu}-\mu^{\prime})\tau^{-}_{\ell}}+\frac{b}{\uline{\mu}}\mathrm{e}^{-\uline{\mu}\tau^{+}_{\ell}},

so that, since
\tau^{-}_{\ell},\tau^{+}_{\ell}\to+\infty
, to show that
\|\overline{R}^{\delta}(t)-R^{\delta}(t)\|_{2}\to 0
is suffices to show that
Q_{\ell}\to 0
. The recursive inequation yields, for any
\ell\geqslant 1
,

Q_{\ell}\leqslant\left(\prod_{m=0}^{\ell-1}\alpha_{m}\right)Q_{0}+\sum_{m=0}^{\ell-1}\left(\prod_{k=m+1}^{\ell-1}\alpha_{k}\right)\beta_{m}.

On the one hand,

\prod_{m=0}^{\ell-1}\alpha_{m}=\exp\left(-\mu^{\prime}\sum_{m=0}^{\ell-1}(\tau^{+}_{m}-\tau^{-}_{m})\right),

and since the sequence
(\tau^{+}_{m}-\tau^{-}_{m})_{m\geqslant 0}
is iid with
\mathbb{P}(\tau^{+}_{0}-\tau^{-}_{0}>0)=1
, we have
\sum_{m=0}^{\ell-1}(\tau^{+}_{m}-\tau^{-}_{m})\to+\infty
, almost surely. On the other hand, we first note that

\displaystyle\beta_{m}

\displaystyle=\frac{b}{\uline{\mu}-\mu^{\prime}}\left(\mathrm{e}^{-\mu^{\prime}(\tau^{+}_{m}-\tau^{-}_{m})}\mathrm{e}^{-\uline{\mu}\tau^{-}_{m}}-\mathrm{e}^{-\uline{\mu}\tau^{+}_{m}}\right)+\frac{b}{\uline{\mu}}\left(\mathrm{e}^{-\uline{\mu}\tau^{+}_{m}}-\mathrm{e}^{-\uline{\mu}\tau^{-}_{m+1}}\right)

\displaystyle\leqslant\frac{b}{\uline{\mu}-\mu^{\prime}}\left(\mathrm{e}^{-\uline{\mu}\tau^{-}_{m}}-\mathrm{e}^{-\uline{\mu}\tau^{-}_{m+1}}\right),

so that

\displaystyle\sum_{m=0}^{\ell-1}\left(\prod_{k=m+1}^{\ell-1}\alpha_{k}\right)\beta_{m}

\displaystyle\leqslant\frac{b}{\uline{\mu}-\mu^{\prime}}\sum_{m=0}^{\ell-1}\left(\prod_{k=m+1}^{\ell-1}\alpha_{k}\right)\left(\mathrm{e}^{-\uline{\mu}\tau^{-}_{m}}-\mathrm{e}^{-\uline{\mu}\tau^{-}_{m+1}}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{b}{\uline{\mu}-\mu^{\prime}}\sum_{m=0}^{\ell-1}\mathrm{e}^{-\uline{\mu}\tau^{-}_{m}}(1-\alpha_{m})\left(\prod_{k=m+1}^{\ell-1}\alpha_{k}\right)-\mathrm{e}^{-\uline{\mu}\tau^{-}_{\ell}}.

Since
\tau^{-}_{\ell}\to+\infty
when
\ell\to+\infty
, the remainder
\mathrm{e}^{-\uline{\mu}\tau^{-}_{\ell}}
vanishes when
\ell\to+\infty
. Besides, for any
\varepsilon>0
, there exists
m_{0}
such that for all
m\geqslant m_{0}
,
\mathrm{e}^{-\uline{\mu}\tau^{-}_{m}}\leqslant\varepsilon
. We may then write, for
\ell-1\geqslant m_{0}
,

\sum_{m=0}^{\ell-1}\mathrm{e}^{-\uline{\mu}\tau^{-}_{m}}(1-\alpha_{m})\left(\prod_{k=m+1}^{\ell-1}\alpha_{k}\right)\leqslant\sum_{m=0}^{m_{0}-1}\mathrm{e}^{-\uline{\mu}\tau^{-}_{m}}(1-\alpha_{m})\left(\prod_{k=m+1}^{\ell-1}\alpha_{k}\right)+\varepsilon\sum_{m=m_{0}}^{\ell-1}(1-\alpha_{m})\left(\prod_{k=m+1}^{\ell-1}\alpha_{k}\right).

Each one of the
m_{0}
terms of the sum in the first term of the right-hand side goes to
0
when
\ell\to+\infty
, while the second term is telescopic and rewrites

\varepsilon\sum_{m=m_{0}}^{\ell-1}(1-\alpha_{m})\left(\prod_{k=m+1}^{\ell-1}\alpha_{k}\right)=\varepsilon\sum_{m=m_{0}}^{\ell-1}\left(\prod_{k=m+1}^{\ell-1}\alpha_{k}-\prod_{k=m}^{\ell-1}\alpha_{k}\right)\leqslant\varepsilon.

Therefore, for any
\varepsilon>0
, we get
\limsup_{\ell\to+\infty}Q_{\ell}\leqslant\varepsilon
, which proves that
Q_{\ell}\to 0
, almost surely, and finally
\|\overline{R}^{\delta}(t)-R^{\delta}(t)\|_{2}\to 0
as well.
∎

Lemma 17

(A Gronwall-type estimate)
.

Let
0\leqslant\mu^{\prime}<\mu
and
b\geqslant 0
. Assume that
u(t)\geqslant 0
satisfies

u^{\prime}(t)\leqslant-2\mu^{\prime}u(t)+2b\mathrm{e}^{-\mu t}\sqrt{u(t)}

on some interval
[t_{1},t_{2}]
. Then

\forall t\in[t_{1},t_{2}],\qquad\sqrt{u(t)}\leqslant\mathrm{e}^{-\mu^{\prime}(t-t_{1})}\sqrt{u(t_{1})}+\frac{b\mathrm{e}^{-\mu^{\prime}t}}{\mu-\mu^{\prime}}\left(\mathrm{e}^{-(\mu-\mu^{\prime})t_{1}}-\mathrm{e}^{-(\mu-\mu^{\prime})t}\right).

Proof.

We first set
\widehat{u}(t)=\mathrm{e}^{2\mu^{\prime}t}u(t)
, so that

\widehat{u}^{\prime}(t)\leqslant 2b\mathrm{e}^{(2\mu^{\prime}-\mu)t}\sqrt{u(t)}=2b\mathrm{e}^{(\mu^{\prime}-\mu)t}\sqrt{\widehat{u}(t)}.

For any
\varepsilon>0
, we therefore deduce that the function
\widehat{u}_{\varepsilon}
defined by
\widehat{u}_{\varepsilon}(t)=\widehat{u}(t)+\varepsilon
satisfies
\widehat{u}_{\varepsilon}(t)>0
and, for any
t\in[t_{1},t_{2}]
,

\widehat{u}_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}(t)=\widehat{u}^{\prime}(t)\leqslant 2b\mathrm{e}^{(\mu^{\prime}-\mu)t}\sqrt{\widehat{u}(t)}\leqslant 2b\mathrm{e}^{(\mu^{\prime}-\mu)t}\sqrt{\widehat{u}_{\varepsilon}(t)}.

Therefore

\sqrt{\widehat{u}_{\varepsilon}(t)}-\sqrt{\widehat{u}_{\varepsilon}(t_{1})}=\int_{t_{1}}^{t}\frac{\widehat{u}^{\prime}_{\varepsilon}(s)}{2\sqrt{\widehat{u}_{\varepsilon}(s)}}\mathrm{d}s\leqslant b\int_{t_{1}}^{t}\mathrm{e}^{(\mu^{\prime}-\mu)s}\mathrm{d}s.

We deduce that

\sqrt{\mathrm{e}^{2\mu^{\prime}t}u(t)+\varepsilon}\leqslant\sqrt{\mathrm{e}^{2\mu^{\prime}t_{1}}u(t_{1})+\varepsilon}+b\int_{t_{1}}^{t}\mathrm{e}^{(\mu^{\prime}-\mu)s}\mathrm{d}s,

in which we take the
\varepsilon\to 0
limit to rewrite

\sqrt{u(t)}\leqslant\mathrm{e}^{-\mu^{\prime}(t-t_{1})}\sqrt{u(t_{1})}+b\mathrm{e}^{-\mu^{\prime}t}\int_{t_{1}}^{t}\mathrm{e}^{(\mu^{\prime}-\mu)s}\mathrm{d}s,

and obtain the claimed estimate.
∎

We are now ready to complete the proof of Theorem
5
(see next subsection for the concentration property). To proceed, we first note that it is easily checked that

Z^{\delta}:=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\exp\left(-\frac{V(r)}{2\delta}\right)\mathrm{d}r<+\infty,

which allows to define the probability measure

\mu^{\delta}_{\infty}(\mathrm{d}r):=\frac{1}{Z^{\delta}}\exp\left(-\frac{V(r)}{2\delta}\right)\mathrm{d}r

on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. By standard arguments, the process
(\overline{R}^{\delta}(t))_{t\geqslant 0}
is ergodic with respect to the probability measure
\mu^{\delta}_{\infty}
. In particular,
\overline{R}^{\delta}(t)
converges in distribution towards
R^{\delta}_{\infty}\sim\mu^{\delta}_{\infty}
, which combined with Proposition
16
entails that
R^{\delta}(t)
converges in distribution to
R^{\delta}_{\infty}
, and therefore
\xi^{\delta}(t)
converges in distribution to
\xi^{\delta}_{\infty}=\|R^{\delta}_{\infty}\|_{2}^{2}
.

Outside the support, it remains to show that

\limsup_{t\to+\infty}\|X_{{S^{\mathsf{o}}}^{c}}^{\top}\|\sqrt{\xi^{\delta}(t)}\mathds{1}_{{S^{\mathsf{o}}}^{c}}-\mu^{\mathsf{L}}_{{S^{\mathsf{o}}}^{c}}t=-\infty,\qquad\text{almost surely.}

We start by noting that, for any
t\geqslant 0
,

\frac{\overline{R}^{\delta}(t)}{t}=\frac{r(0)}{t}-\frac{1}{t}\int_{0}^{t}\nabla V(\overline{R}^{\delta}(s))\mathrm{d}s+2\sqrt{\delta}\frac{B(t)}{t}.

Since
V
is even, by the ergodic theorem,

\lim_{t\to+\infty}\frac{1}{t}\int_{0}^{t}\nabla V(\overline{R}^{\delta}(s))\mathrm{d}s=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\nabla V(r)\mu^{\delta}_{\infty}(\mathrm{d}r)=0,\qquad\text{almost surely,}

while by the law of the iterated logarithm,

\lim_{t\to+\infty}\frac{r(0)}{t}+2\sqrt{\delta}\frac{B(t)}{t}=0,\qquad\text{almost surely.}

Therefore,

\lim_{t\to+\infty}\frac{\overline{R}^{\delta}(t)}{t}=0,\qquad\text{almost surely,}

and by Proposition
16
we deduce that

\lim_{t\to+\infty}\frac{R^{\delta}(t)}{t}=0,\qquad\text{almost surely.}

Since the processes
(\|R^{\delta}(t)\|)_{t\geqslant 0}
and
(\sqrt{\xi^{\delta}(t)})_{t\geqslant 0}
have the same law, we deduce that

\lim_{t\to+\infty}\frac{\sqrt{\xi^{\delta}(t)}}{t}=0,\qquad\text{almost surely,}

which proves the claim.

B.2.5
Poincaré inequality and concentration properties

We first show, in Lemma
18
, that the probability measure
\mu^{\delta}_{\infty}
satisfies a Poincaré inequality with a constant
\kappa^{\delta}
on which we provide an explicit bound. We recall that it means that, for any smooth function
f
on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
,

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\left(f(r)-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}f(r^{\prime})\mu^{\delta}_{\infty}(\mathrm{d}r^{\prime})\right)^{2}\mu^{\delta}_{\infty}(\mathrm{d}r)\leqslant\kappa^{\delta}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\|\nabla f(r)\|^{2}\mu^{\delta}_{\infty}(\mathrm{d}r).

From this result, the concentration inequality (
14
) stated in Theorem
5
follows from Eq. (4.4.6), p. 192 in
Bakry et al. 2014
. Last, the fact that
\kappa^{\delta}=\mathcal{O}(\delta)
in the
\delta\to 0
regime follows from a basic application of the Laplace method, which is detailed in Remark
19
.

Lemma 18

(Poincare constant)
.

The probability measure
\mu^{\delta}_{\infty}
satisfies a Poincaré inequality with constant
\kappa^{\delta}\leqslant 13(\sigma^{\delta})^{2}/n
, where

\sigma^{\delta}:=\sqrt{\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\|r\|^{2}_{2}\mu^{\delta}_{\infty}(\mathrm{d}r)}=\sqrt{\mathbb{E}[\|R^{\delta}_{\infty}\|_{2}^{2}]}.

Proof.

Let us denote by
\mu^{\delta}_{\infty}(r)
the density of the probability measure
\mu^{\delta}_{\infty}(\mathrm{d}r)
with respect to the Lebesgue measure on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. It is an immediate computation to show that the probability density
\widetilde{\mu}^{\delta}_{\infty}
defined by

\widetilde{\mu}^{\delta}_{\infty}(\widetilde{r}):=\left(\frac{\sigma^{\delta}}{\sqrt{n}}\right)^{n}\mu^{\delta}_{\infty}\left(\frac{\sigma^{\delta}}{\sqrt{n}}\widetilde{r}\right)

satisifes

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\|\widetilde{r}\|_{2}^{2}\widetilde{\mu}^{\delta}_{\infty}(\widetilde{r})\mathrm{d}\widetilde{r}=n,

and that this density writes as a log-concave function of
\|\widetilde{r}\|_{2}
. Therefore, by Theorem 1 in
Bobkov 2003
, it satisfies a Poincaré inequality with constant
\widetilde{\kappa}^{\delta}\leq 13
, and the final statement follows from an elementary rescaling argument for Poincaré inequalities.
∎

Remark 19

(Estimate of the Poincare constant)
.

Finally let us give some estimate on the Poincaré constant when
\delta
is small. To give an equivalent of
(\sigma^{\delta})^{2}=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\|r\|^{2}_{2}\mu^{\delta}_{\infty}(\mathrm{d}r)
with the Laplace method, we approximate
V
by a quadratic near its minimum in
0_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}
,
V(r)\simeq V(0)+\frac{1}{2}\langle r,\nabla^{2}V(0)r\rangle=a\rho_{S^{\mathsf{o}}}\|r\|^{2}_{2}
by the equation (
22
). Therefore by the Laplace method, in the small
\delta
regime, the distribution with density
\mu_{\infty}^{\delta}
should approximately behave as a centered Gaussian random variable, with covariance matrix
\delta/(a\rho_{S^{\mathsf{o}}})I_{n}
. In particular, this gives an upperbound of the Poincaré constant:

\kappa^{\delta}\leqslant\frac{13(\sigma^{\delta})^{2}}{n}\simeq\frac{13\delta}{a\rho_{S^{\mathsf{o}}}}=\mathcal{O}(\delta).

Finally remark that in the small
\delta
limit, this estimate is tight up to the numerical constant as a lower bound with numerical constant
1
is also given in
Bobkov 2003
.

Local multiboson factorization of the quark determinant
\firstname Marco \lastname Cè \fnsep, \firstname Leonardo \lastname Giusti, \firstname Stefan \lastname Schaefer

Abstract
We discuss the recently proposed multiboson domain-decomposed factorization of the gauge-field dependence of the fermion determinant in lattice QCD. In particular, we focus on the case of a lattice divided in an arbitrary number of thick time slices. As a consequence, multiple space-time regions can be updated independently. This allows to address the exponential degradation of the signal-to-noise ration of correlation functions with multilevel Monte Carlo sampling. We show numerical evidence of the effectiveness of a two-level integration for pseudoscalar propagators with momentum and for vector propagators, in a two active regions setup. These results are relevant to lattice computation of the hadronic contributions to the anomalous magnetic moment of the muon and to heavy meson decay form factors.

Introduction

It is well known that the standard Monte Carlo (MC) evaluation of a generic multi-point function of QCD has an exponential signal-to-noise ratio (
\text{S}/\text{N}
) problem. Namely, the signal decays with the distance between points with a faster exponential rate than its statistical error, or noise
Parisi:1983ae
;
Lepage:1989hd
. This currently limits the obtainable statistical accuracy at long distances. A way to tackle the signal-to-noise problem that involves a modification of the MC integration procedure, the so-called
multilevel
algorithm
Parisi:1983hm
;
Luscher:2001up
, is briefly introduced in Section
3
.

Algorithms of this kind have been applied to lattice Yang–Mills theory
Parisi:1983hm
;
Luscher:2001up
;
Meyer:2002cd
;
DellaMorte:2007zz
;
DellaMorte:2008jd
;
DellaMorte:2010yp
, thanks to the fact that the action of a purely bosonic theory is
manifestly local
. However, in numerical lattice QCD the Grassmann quark fields are first integrated out analytically. This leads to a
non-local
fermion determinant and
non-local
Wick contractions. Both non-localities prevent a straightforward application of multilevel sampling.

In the first part of these proceedings (Sec.
4
), we present the factorization introduced in Refs
Ce:2016ajy
;
Giusti:2017ksp
, focusing on the case of
independent updates
of an
arbitrary number
of thick-time-slice regions. To this purpose, the fermion determinant is factorized in contributions that are localized to a spacetime region of a domain decomposed (DD) lattice
Luscher:2005rx
, while interactions between different domains are mediated by multiboson (MB) fields
Luscher:1993xx
;
Borici:1995np
. In the second part of these proceedings (Sec.
5
) we show new numerical evidence that multilevel sampling can solve the
\text{S}/\text{N}
problem for the connected pseudoscalar correlator with momentum and for the vector correlator.

2
The signal-to-noise ratio problem

As a prototypical manifestation of the
\text{S}/\text{N}
problem, consider the mesonic correlator on a fixed background gauge field configuration

W_{\Gamma}(x_{0},y_{0},\mathbf{p})=\frac{1}{L^{3}}\sum_{\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y}}\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\mathbf{p}\cdot(\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{y})}\expectationvalue{ \bar{d}(y)\Gamma u(y) \; \bar{u}(x)\Gamma d(x) }_{f}=-\frac{1}{L^{3}}\sum_{\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y}}\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\mathbf{p}\cdot(\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{y})}\Tr{ \Gamma\gamma_5 S(x,y)^\dagger\gamma_5 \Gamma S(x,y) },

(1)

where
\expectationvalue{\bullet}_{f}
denotes the fermion path integral. The mesonic two-point function is estimated by averaging Eq. (
1
) on gauge field configurations. At large distances, it decays exponentially with the energy of the lightest state with the specified quantum numbers

C_{\Gamma}(x_{0},y_{0},\mathbf{p})=\expectationvalue{ W_\Gamma(x_0,y_0,\vb{p}) }\propto\exp{-E_\Gamma(\vb{p})\abs{x_0-y_0}}.

(2)

At the same time, the variance decays exponentially with the lightest state with at least four quark lines, i.e. a pair of zero-momentum pions, irrespective of
\Gamma
and
\mathbf{p}
,

\sigma^{2}\left[C_{\Gamma}\right](x_{0},y_{0},\mathbf{p})=\expectationvalue{ \abs{W_\Gamma(x_0,y_0,\vb{p})}^2 }-\expectationvalue{ W_\Gamma(x_0,y_0,\vb{p}) }^{2}\propto\exp{-M_{\pi\pi}\abs{x_0-y_0}}.

(3)

Therefore, neglecting finite volume effects on the
\pi\pi
state energy, any connected mesonic two-point function with the exclusion of pseudoscalar ones, e.g.
\Gamma=\gamma_{5}
,
\gamma_{0}\gamma_{5}
, has an expectation value that is decaying faster than its standard deviation. Assuming sampling over
n
independent configurations with a standard MC, the
\text{S}/\text{N}
is

\frac{C_{\Gamma}(x_{0},y_{0},\mathbf{p})}{\sigma\left[C_{\Gamma}\right](x_{0},y_{0},\mathbf{p})/n^{1/2}}\propto n^{1/2}\exp{-(E_\Gamma(\vb{p})-M_\pi) \abs{x_0-y_0}}.

(4)

The multilevel Monte Carlo algorithm

The multilevel algorithm addresses the
\text{S}/\text{N}
problem modifying the MC sampling and boosting the standard
n^{-1/2}
scaling of the statistical error in Eq. (
4

0

n_{0}
configurations that span the whole lattice gauge field. At level-
1
(and higher),
n_{1}
gauge field configurations are generated for each level-
0
configuration. The level-
1
updates are characterized by the independent update of different spacetime regions of the lattice, and they are used to independently average the observable in the distinct regions.

The multilevel estimator for

\bar{C}^{\text{mlv}}_{\text{fact},\Gamma}(x_{0},y_{0},\mathbf{p})=\frac{1}{n_{0}}\sum_{i=1}^{n_{0}}\left\{\bar{O}_{1}\bar{O}_{2}\dots\bar{O}_{m}\right\},\qquad\bar{O}_{k}=\frac{1}{n_{1}}\sum_{j=1}^{n_{1}}O_{k}[U_{i,j}].

Each factor

exponentially faster
with respect to the standard MC. We expect to have

\frac{\bar{C}^{\text{mlv}}_{\text{fact},\Gamma}(x_{0},y_{0},\mathbf{p})}{\sigma\left[\bar{C}^{\text{mlv}}_{\text{fact},\Gamma}\right](x_{0},y_{0},\mathbf{p})/n_{0}^{1/2}}\propto n_{0}^{1/2}\exp{-\left[ E_\Gamma(\vb{p})-M_\pi-(\bar{M}\ln{n_1})/2 \right]\abs{x_0-y_0}}.

(7)

Provided that is possible to boost the exponential rate of the noise to the level of the one of the signal, the
\text{S}/\text{N}
problem is completely solved. In general, the optimal value of
n_{1}
is observable specific.

The factorization of the lattice QCD action

In order to implement multilevel sampling in QCD with dynamical fermions, we need to be able to update the gauge field in different spacetime regions independently. To this purpose, in Ref.
Ce:2016ajy
we introduced a factorization of the fermion determinant and its dependency on gauge links in different regions.

4.2
The multiboson representation

The fact that the condition number of
\mathds{1}-w
is
\simeq(1+\bar{\delta})/(1-\bar{\delta})=\order{1}
suggests as a next step to apply to it the MB technique
Luscher:1993xx
, in its complex-valued variant
Borici:1995np
. This is obtained by approximating the operator inverse with a polynomial

P_{N}(z)=\frac{1-R_{N+1}(z)}{z}=c_{N}\prod_{k=1}^{N}(z-z_{k}),\qquad z_{k}=\{z\neq 0:R_{N+1}(z)=1\},\qquad u_{k}=1-z_{k}.

(11)

A simple and good choice is a truncated geometric series
P_{N}(z)=\sum_{p=1}^{N}(1-z)^{p}
, which has the nice property of being exponentially more accurate close to
z=1
, where the bulk of the UV eigenvalues of
\mathds{1}-w
are concentrated. This polynomial choice has a constant accuracy on circumferences in the complex plane
\absolutevalue{1-z}=\text{const}
, but different choices might provide slightly better approximations.

Chosing
N
even, we can write down the MB action for
\det{1-w}

\frac{\det{1-w}}{\det{\Id-R_{N+1}(\Id-w)}}\propto\prod_{k=1}^{N/2}\det{W_{\sqrt{u_k}}^\dagger W_{\sqrt{u_k}}}^{-1}\propto\prod_{k=1}^{N/2}\int\mathinner{\mathcal{D}\argopen*[\chi_{k},\chi_{k}^{\dagger}\argclose*]}\mathrm{e}^{-\absolutevalue{W_{\sqrt{u_k}}\chi_k}^{2}},

(12)

where the MB fields

6
Conclusions

We showed in Ref.
Ce:2016ajy
that it is possible to factorize the gauge field dependence of the quark determinant, introducing a domain decomposition in overlapping domains and representing the residual contribution with multiboson fields. Combined with the quark propagator factorization introduced in Ref.
Ce:2016idq
, the quark determinant factorization allows multilevel sampling of fermionic correlators, for the first time with dynamical fermions. In these proceedings, we focused on a setup with multiple independent thick time slices, that results in the action in Eq. (
13
).

The effectiveness of two-level sampling has been tested in Refs
Ce:2016idq
;
Ce:2016qto
;
Ce:2016ajy
for the baryon propagator and for the disconnected contribution to the flavour-singlet pseudoscalar propagator. In these proceedings, we tested in addition the two-point functions of pseudoscalar densities at non-zero momentum and of vector currents in a two-region setup. Results show an exponential gain in the
\text{S}/\text{N}
in both cases. This suggests new ways to approach computations such as the hadronic vacuum polarization contribution to the anomalous magnetic moment of the muon.

Variants on the combined domain decomposition and multiboson scheme might also address the limitations of standard HMC algorithms in master field simulations with dynamical fermions
Luscher:2017cjh
.

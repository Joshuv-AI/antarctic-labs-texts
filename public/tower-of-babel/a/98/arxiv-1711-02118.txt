Simultaneous sign change and equidistribution of signs of Fourier coefficients of two cusp forms
Mohammed Amin Amri

Abstract
We study the simultaneous sign change of Fourier coefficients of a pair of distinct normalized newforms of integral weight supported on primes power indices, we also prove some equidistribution results. Finally, we consider an analogous question for Fourier coefficients of a pair of half-integral weight Hecke eigenforms.

11F03, 11F30, 11F37

Simultaneous sign changes, Fourier coefficients, Sato-Tate conjecture

Introduction and statements of results

Throughout the paper we denote by
\mathbb{P}
the set of all prime numbers, for a set
S\subset\mathbb{P}
we denote by
\delta(S)
its natural density. We write
S_{k}^{\mathrm{new}}(N)
for the space of newforms of even integral weight
k
with trivial Dirichlet character over the group
\Gamma_{0}(N)
. The letter
\mathcal{H}
stands for the upper half-plane, for
z\in\mathcal{H}
we set

.

The study of sign changes of Fourier coefficients of a single cusp form has been the focus of much recent study (cf.
[
1
,
3
,
4
,
8
,
7
,
13
,
10
]
) due to their various number theoretic applications (see for instance
[
5
]
) and his long history which goes back at least to Siegel
[
17
]
. The question of simultaneous sign change of Fourier coefficients of modular forms was first considered by Kohnen and Sengupta in
[
9
]
. Indeed, they proved that for given two cusp forms of different weights and the same level with real algebraic Fourier coefficients, there exists a Galois automorphism
\sigma
such that
f_{1}^{\sigma}
and
f_{2}^{\sigma}
have infinitely many Fourier coefficients of opposite sign using analytic properties of the Rankin–Selberg zeta function, Landau’s theorem on Dirichlet series with non-negative coefficients, and the bounded denominator principle. Recently, this result was extended to two cusp forms with arbitrary real Fourier coefficients in
[
6
]
by Gun, Kohnen, and Rath. Moreover, they show for any pair
(f_{1},f_{2})
of distinct newforms with trivial Dirichlet character that the sequence
\{\lambda_{1}(p^{\nu})\lambda_{2}(p^{\nu})\}_{\nu\in\mathbb{N}}
changes signs infinitely often for an infinite set of primes
p
, where
\lambda_{i}(n)
is the
n
-th Fourier coefficient of
f_{i}
.

The primary result of this paper is to calculate the proportion of integers for which the product
\lambda_{1}(p^{\nu})\lambda_{2}(p^{\nu})
has the same sign. More precisely, we shall show the following equidistribution result.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let

f_{i}(z)=\sum_{n\geq 1}\lambda_{i}(n)n^{(k_{i}-1)/2}q^{n}\in S_{k_{i}}^{\mathrm{new}}(N_{i})\quad i=1,2,

be two distinct normalized newforms of level
N_{1},N_{2}
and weights
k_{1},k_{2}\geq 2
respectively. For any prime
p\nmid N_{i}
, define
\theta_{i}(p)\in[0,\pi]
by the relation
\lambda_{i}(p)=2\cos\theta_{i}(p)
with
i=1,2
. Suppose that
1,\frac{\theta_{1}(p)}{2\pi},\frac{\theta_{2}(p)}{2\pi}
are linearly independent over
\mathbb{Q}
, then we have

\lim_{x\to\infty}\dfrac{\#\{v\leq x:\lambda_{1}(p^{\nu})\lambda_{2}(p^{\nu})\lessgtr 0\}}{x}=\frac{1}{2}.

The strategy of the proof is inspired by the one used by Kohnen et al., in
[
10

and involve essentially a two dimensional variant of Weyl’s equidistribution theorem (see ?THM?
for a precise statement).

Remark 1.1
.

It should be noted that if one of the numbers
\frac{\theta_{1}(p)}{2\pi},\frac{\theta_{2}(p)}{2\pi}
is rational and the other is irrational, then we have

\lim_{x\to\infty}\dfrac{\#\{v\leq x:\lambda_{1}(p^{\nu})\lambda_{2}(p^{\nu})\lessgtr 0\}}{\#\{v\leq x:\lambda_{1}(p^{\nu})\lambda_{2}(p^{\nu})\neq 0\}}=\frac{1}{2},

which may be proved in much the same way used by the author in

Our next purpose is to deal with the sign changes of the sequence
\{\lambda_{1}(p^{\nu})\lambda_{2}(p^{\nu})\}_{p\in\mathbb{P}}

Suppose that
k_{1},k_{2}\geq 2
and
N_{1},N_{2}
are distinct natural numbers. Suppose that

f_{i}(z):=\sum_{n\geq 1}\lambda_{i}(n)n^{(k_{i}-1)/2}q^{n}\in S_{k_{i}}^{\mathrm{new}}(N_{i})\quad i=1,2,

are normalized newforms without complex multiplication which are not twists of each other. Let

are distinct natural numbers,
N_{1},N_{2}

\pi_{>0}(x):=\{p\leq x:p\nmid N,\;a_{1}(tp^{2\nu})a_{2}(tp^{2\nu})>0\}
. Dividing the above inequality by
\pi(x)
(the prime-counting function), we obtain

\frac{\pi_{>0}(x)}{\pi(x)}+\dfrac{\pi\left(\frac{1}{\epsilon^{2}}\right)}{\pi(x)}\geq\dfrac{S(I_{\epsilon},I_{\epsilon})(x)+S(I^{\prime}_{\epsilon},I^{\prime}_{\epsilon})(x)}{\pi(x)}.

(3.8)

By ?THM?
we have

\lim_{x\to\infty}\dfrac{S(I_{\epsilon},I_{\epsilon})(x)}{\pi(x)}=\mu_{\mathrm{ST}}^{\otimes_{2}}\left(I_{\epsilon}\times I_{\epsilon}\right)\;\;\text{and}\;\;\lim_{x\to\infty}\dfrac{S(I^{\prime}_{\epsilon},I^{\prime}_{\epsilon})(x)}{\pi(x)}=\mu_{\mathrm{ST}}^{\otimes_{2}}\left(I^{\prime}_{\epsilon}\times I^{\prime}_{\epsilon}\right).

(3.9)

Since
\pi\left(\frac{1}{\epsilon^{2}}\right)
, is finite, the term
\frac{\pi\left(\frac{1}{\epsilon^{2}}\right)}{\pi(x)}
tends to zero as
x
tends to infinity. Taking into account (
3.9
), a passage to the limit in (
3.8
) implies that

\liminf_{x\to\infty}\frac{\pi_{>0}(x)}{\pi(x)}\geq\mu_{\mathrm{ST}}^{\otimes_{2}}\left(I_{\epsilon}\times I_{\epsilon}\right)+\mu_{\mathrm{ST}}^{\otimes_{2}}\left(I^{\prime}_{\epsilon}\times I^{\prime}_{\epsilon}\right).

(3.10)

Letting
\epsilon
to zero in (
3.10
), we find

\liminf_{x\to\infty}\frac{\pi_{>0}(x)}{\pi(x)}\geq\mu_{\mathrm{ST}}^{\otimes_{2}}\left(I\times I\right)+\mu_{\mathrm{ST}}^{\otimes_{2}}\left(I^{\prime}\times I^{\prime}\right),

where

I:=\bigcup_{j=1}^{\frac{\nu+1}{2}}\left(\frac{(2j-2)\pi}{\nu+1},\frac{(2j-1)\pi}{\nu+1}\right)\;\;\text{and}\;\;I^{\prime}:=\bigcup_{j=1}^{\frac{\nu+1}{2}}\left(\frac{(2j-1)\pi}{\nu+1},\frac{2j\pi}{\nu+1}\right).

From
[
14
]
we have

\mu_{\mathrm{ST}}^{\otimes_{2}}\left(I\times I\right)=\mu_{\mathrm{ST}}^{\otimes_{2}}\left(I^{\prime}\times I^{\prime}\right)=\frac{1}{4}.

It follows that

\liminf_{x\to\infty}\frac{\pi_{>0}(x)}{\pi(x)}\geq\frac{1}{2}.

The same arguments yield
\liminf\limits_{x\to\infty}\frac{\pi_{\leq 0}(x)}{\pi(x)}\geq\frac{1}{2}
.

Finally, upon using
\pi_{\leq 0}(x)=\pi(x)-\pi_{>0}(x)
, we deduce that

\frac{1}{2}\leq\liminf\limits_{x\to\infty}\frac{\pi_{>0}(x)}{\pi(x)}\leq\limsup\limits_{x\to\infty}\frac{\pi_{>0}(x)}{\pi(x)}\leq\frac{1}{2},

and hence
\lim\limits_{x\to\infty}\frac{\pi_{>0}(x)}{\pi(x)}
exists and is equal to
\frac{1}{2}
. The rest of the proof runs as before. Consequently we have
\delta(\mathbb{P}^{\prime}_{=0})=0
.

Shifted twisted Yangians and Slodowy slices in classical Lie algebras Thanks: Mathematics Subject Classification (2000 revision ). Primary 17B63, 17B37. Secondary 17B45, 17B08.
Lukas Tappeiner & Lewis Topley

Abstract
. In this paper we introduce the shifted twisted Yangian of type AI , following the work of Lu–Wang–Zhang, and we study their semiclassical limits, a class of Poisson algebras. We demonstrate that they coincide with the Dirac reductions of the semiclassical shifted Yangian for 𝔤 ​ 𝔩 n \mathfrak{gl}_{n} . We deduce that these shifted twisted Yangians admit truncations which are isomorphic to Slodowy slices for many non-rectangular nilpotent elements in types B , C , D . As a direct consequence we obtain parabolic presentations of the semiclassical shifted twisted Yangian, analogous to those introduced by Brundan–Kleshchev for the Yangian of type A . Finally we give Poisson presentations of Slodowy slices for all even nilpotent elements in types B , C , D , generalising the recent work of the second author.



1.
Introduction

1.1.
Background

To each nilpotent orbit in the Lie algebra of a complex reductive group one can associate a Slodowy slice, which is a certain affine Poisson variety, and these slices are quantized by finite
W
-algebras
[
GG02
,
Pr02
]
. These slices and their quantizations play several interesting roles in representation theory, algebraic geometry and mathematical physics.

The representation theory of the finite
W
-algebra has furnished new methods for studying the invariants of primitive ideals of enveloping algebras
[
Br11
,
Lo10a
,
Lo15
,
Pr07a
,
Pr10
,
Pr14
,
PT14
]
, as well as the quantizations of nilpotent orbits and their covers
[
Lo10b
,
Lo22
,
To23
]
.

At the same time, a Slodowy slice can be viewed as a flat deformation of its nilpotent part
[
Sl80
, Corollary 7.4.1]
, and the latter is an example of a conic symplectic singularity
[
Gi09
, Proposition 2.1.2]
. Recently it has been conjectured that these nilpotent slices are the symplectic duals of certain affinizations of covers of nilpotent orbits
[
LMM21
, § 9.3]
, and this duality is the central motif in the field of symplectic representation theory; see
[
BLPW16
]
for an introduction.

Finite
W
-algebras and Slodowy slices also play a vital role in the theory of vertex algebras. Affine
W
-algebras form a large class of vertex algebras, which have been studied intensively in recent years
[
Ar15
,
Ar17
,
AF19
,
DSKV16
,
Fr02
,
AKMFP24
]
. By taking the Zhu algebra or the
C_{2}
-algebra of an universal affine
W
-algebra at any level, one obtains a finite
W
-algebra or a Slodowy slice, respectively
[
Ar17
, §5.7]
. In this way, the algebras discussed in the present paper are the key tools for studying conformally graded simple modules and associated varieties of the simple affine vertex algebras. These are some of the key challenges in this field.

Yangians are a family of associative algebras of a very different pedigree.
They were first introduced by the St. Petersburg school in the 1980s and are one of the fundamental examples of quantum groups defined using the RTT formalism
[
FRT90
]
. Drinfeld made an extensive study of the representation theory of the Yangian associated to
\mathfrak{gl}_{n}
, and introduced a new presentation to classify their finite dimensional modules
[
Dr87
,
Dr88
]
. Later Olshanski introduced twisted Yangians, which are analogous to the twisted affine Lie algebras
[
Ol92
]
, one of the key ingredients in the current article.

In
[
RS99
]
Ragoucy and Sorba showed that if
N=nl
for
n,l>0
and we choose the nilpotent orbit
\mathbb{O}\subseteq\mathfrak{gl}_{N}
which has all blocks of size
l
(the so-called
rectangular
case) then the finite
W
-algebra associated to
\mathbb{O}
can be realised as a quotient of a Yangian associated to
\mathfrak{gl}_{n}
. Brundan and Kleshchev extended this relationship between Yangians and
W
-algebras, by introducing the shifted Yangians
[
BK06
]
. They showed that for every
N>0
and to every nilpotent orbit
\mathbb{O}\subseteq\mathfrak{gl}_{N}
we can associate a
shift matrix

\sigma
and a
level

l
, and that the truncated shifted Yangian
Y_{n,l}(\sigma)
is isomorphic to the finite
W
-algebra for
\mathbb{O}
. The (doubled) canonical filtration on
Y_{n,l}(\sigma)
lines up with the Kazdan filtration on the finite
W
-algebra and so, as a corollary, the semiclassical truncated shifted Yangian is isomorphic to the Slodowy slice, as a Poisson algebra. One of the key features of their argument was the introduction of
parabolic presentations
of Yangians
[
BK05
]
, which we discuss later in this article.

In types
B
,
C
and
D
much less is known. Ragoucy showed that certain truncations of semiclassical twisted Yangians are isomorphic (as Poisson algebras) to the Slodowy slices associated to rectangular nilpotent orbits
[
Ra01
]
. Brown proved the quantum analogue for finite
W
-algebras using quite different methods
[
Br09
]
. There has been a long-standing hope that some variant of twisted Yangians could be related to Slodowy slices and finite
W
-algebras associated to non-rectanguar cases. In this paper we make the first progress in this problem by introducing shifted twisted Yangians of type
AI
, and relating them to Slodowy slices. Our main tools are the Drinfeld presentation of twisted Yangians recently introduced by Lu, Wang and Zhang
[
LWZ23
]
, as well as the methods of
[
To23
]
and the parabolic presentations of Yangians introduced by Brundan–Kleshchev
[
BK05
]
.

1.2.
Statement of results

We introduce some notation related to Yangians, and we refer the reader to
[
Mo07
]
for a good introduction. Fix
n>0
, let
Y_{n}
denote the Yangian associated to
\mathfrak{gl}_{n}
and let
y_{n}
denote the (semiclassical) Yangian
\operatorname{gr}Y_{n}
, with respect to the canonical filtration. Since this algebra is commutative it carries a natural Poisson structure, and because this paper focuses on
y_{n}
rather than
Y_{n}
, we will usually drop the prefix ‘semiclassical’ in what follows.

Pick a symmetric shift matrix
\sigma\in\operatorname{Mat}_{n}(\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0})
(see §
2.1
) and let
y_{n}(\sigma)
be the shifted Yangian, which is presented by generators and relations in
[
To23
, §3.3]
. According to Lemma
2.1
there is a natural bijection between pairs
(\sigma,\ell)
, where
\ell>0
is an integer we call the
level
, and partitions of positive integers with precisely
n
parts, all of the same parity.

Let
\mathcal{Y}_{n}
denote the (non-commutative) twisted Yangian for the orthogonal Lie algebra
\mathfrak{o}_{n}
, where the twisting is performed with respect to the Cartan involution. Note that
\mathcal{Y}_{n}
is denoted
Y_{G}(\mathfrak{o}_{N})
in
[
Mo07
, Ch. 2]
, where
G
is the identity matrix in
\operatorname{Mat}_{n}(\mathbb{C})
. Let
y_{n}^{+}
denote the semiclassical limit of
\mathcal{Y}_{n}
with respect to the canonical filtration, which we equip with its natural Poisson structure. In Section
3.2
we introduce the shifted twisted Yangian
\mathcal{Y}_{n}(\sigma)
as well as the Poisson algebra
y_{n}^{+}(\sigma)=\operatorname{gr}\mathcal{Y}_{n}(\sigma)
.

In Section
2.5
we recall the method of Dirac reduction for complex affine Poisson algebras. If
A
is a countably generated complex Poisson algebra with a reductive group
H
of Poisson automorphisms then
R(A,H)
is a Poisson algebra such that
\operatorname{Spec}R(A,H)\cong X^{H}
. The Cartan involution on
\mathfrak{gl}_{n}
induces an involution on
y_{n}
which we denote by
\tau
, see (
3.3
). Since
\sigma
is symmetric the involution restricts to
y_{n}(\sigma)
.

Our first main theorem is the following, see Theorem
3.9
.

Theorem A
.

We have a Poisson isomorphism

R(y_{n}(\sigma),\tau)\overset{\sim}{\longrightarrow}y_{n}^{+}(\sigma).

The theorem is proven by defining a map on generators, checking relations, and then applying the Poincaré–Birhoff–Witt (PBW) theorem on both sides to see that a basis maps to a basis. We prove a PBW theorem for
\mathcal{Y}_{n}(\sigma)
, drawing heavily on the unshifted case (see
[
LWZ23
, §5.4]
) using an argument analogous to
[
BK06
, Theorem 2.1]
, and we deduce the PBW theorem for
y_{n}^{+}(\sigma)
from the non-commutative setting. The PBW theorem for
R(y_{n}(\sigma),\tau)
is
[
To23
, Theorem 3.4]
.

The main result of
[
To23
, Part I]
states that
R(y_{n}(\sigma),\tau)
admits certain truncations which are isomorphic to Slodowy slices as Poisson algebras. Consequently we obtain the following.

Corollary
.

Suppose that either:

(i)

\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{so}_{n}
and all parts of the partition are odd; or

(ii)

\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{sp}_{n}
and all parts of the partition are even.

Then the Poisson structure on the Slodowy slice is isomorphic to a truncation of the shifted twisted Yangian for
\mathfrak{o}_{n}
.

One of the key tools in the proofs of
[
BK06
]
are the parabolic presentations of shifted Yangians. These interpolate between the RTT presentation and the Drinfeld presentation. The former should be thought of as analogous to presenting a simple Lie algebra via a Chevalley
\mathbb{Z}
-form, whilst the latter is akin to the Chevalley–Serre presentation. For future developments it is important to discover parabolic presentations of twisted Yangians
\mathcal{Y}_{n}
and their shifted analogues (see
[
LWZ23
, §1.3]
). Our second main theorem provides these presentations at the semiclassical level. We state the result informally now, and provide a more detailed statement and proof in Remark
5.11
.

Theorem B
.

We introduce parabolic presentations of
y_{n}^{+}(\sigma)
.

In the Corollary to Theorem A we discussed Poisson presentations of a certain class of even orbit in types
B
,
C
and
D
. Our final main theorem extends this presentation to all even orbits, by using the parabolic presentations of semiclassical shifted Yangians, generalising
[
To23
, Part I]
. The key difference between Theorems A and C is that we cannot yet relate the new Poisson presentations below to a shifted twisted Yangian.

The full statement and proof of the following theorem can be assembled from Theorem
5.13
and Theorem
6.2
.

Theorem C
.

A presentation of the Poisson structure on the Slodowy slice associated to any even orbit in
\mathfrak{so}_{n}
or
\mathfrak{sp}_{n}
is given by the truncation of the Dirac reduction of the semiclassical shifted Yangian, with respect to any parabolic presentation.

Surprisingly, the truncation relations are significantly simpler when, either: the Lie algebra is not of type
C
, or; the smallest two parts of partition of the nilpotent orbit have distinct sizes. We note that the the Corollary to Theorem A is significantly stronger than the result obtained in
[
To23
, Theorem 1.3]
thanks to these simplified truncation relations. This simplification depends crucially on the parabolic presentations of Theorem B.

One technical detail which we have suppressed in this introduction is the notion of an admissible shape for
\sigma
, introduced by Brundan–Kleshcev in order to define parabolic presentations. In Section
2.2
we recall this definition and introduce the new notion of a
(\beta,\sigma)
-admissible shape, where
\beta=\pm 1
. The parabolic presentations in Theorem B apply to the case
\beta=1
.

We conjecture that a version of Theorems A and B should hold true for
\beta=-1
, when
y_{n}^{+}(\sigma)
is replaced by the as-yet-undefined shifted twisted Yangian for
\mathfrak{sp}_{n}
(Satake diagram of type
AII
). We also expect that the shifted twisted Yangian
\mathcal{Y}_{n}(\sigma)
should quantize certain slices in the twisted affine Grassmanian associated to
\operatorname{SO}_{n}
, in parallel with
[
KWWY14
]
.

2.
Preliminaries

In this section we record the combinatorics, as well as some preliminary definitions and theorems to be used in the sequel. All vector spaces, algebras and algebraic varieties are defined over
\mathbb{C}
.

2.1.
Symmetric shift matrices and partitions

Fix
n>0
. A shift matrix is an
n\times n
matrix
\sigma=(s_{i,j})
with entries in
\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
satisfying the condition

(2.1)

\displaystyle s_{i,k}=s_{i,j}+s_{j,k}

whenever
|i-k|=|i-j|+|j-k|
and
1\leq i,j,k\leq n
. We observe that every such shift matrix has zeroes on the diagonal, and is determined entirely by the sub- and super-diagonal entries, which can be chosen arbitrarily. We will always assume that our shift matrix is
symmetric
meaning
s_{i,j}=s_{j,i}
for all
i,j
. Such a matrix is determined by
(s_{i,i+1}\mid i=1,...,n-1)
.

Now fix an integer
\ell>0
, which we refer to as
the level
. If we set

(2.2)

\displaystyle N=n\ell+2(n-1)s_{1,2}+2(n-2)s_{2,3}+\cdots+2s_{n-1,n}

then the data
(\sigma,\ell)
give rise to a partition
\lambda=(\lambda_{1}\leq\lambda_{2}\leq\cdots\leq\lambda_{n})\vdash N
by

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{l}\lambda_{1}=\ell,\\
\lambda_{i}=\lambda_{i-1}+2s_{i-1,i}\text{ \ for \ }i=2,...,n.\end{array}

We remark that all parts of
\lambda
have the same parity.

The following easy lemma will be used often later on.

Lemma 2.1
.

The assignment
(\sigma,\ell)\mapsto\lambda
determined by (
2.1
) defines a bijection from symmetric shift matrices with a fixed level, to partitions of positive integers with precisely
n
parts, all of the same parity.
\hfill\qed

2.2.
Admissible shapes

Keep a choice of
n\times n
shift matrix
\sigma
. A
shape of size
n
is a tuple
\nu=(\nu_{1},\nu_{2},...,\nu_{m})
of positive integers such that
\sum_{i}\nu_{i}=n
, and we write
\bar{\nu}=(\bar{\nu}_{1},...,\bar{\nu}_{m})
where
\bar{\nu}_{a}=\Sigma_{b=1}^{a}\nu_{b}
. We refer to
m
as the
length
of
\nu
.

We say that
\nu
is a
\sigma
-admissible shape
if
s_{i,j}=0
for all
\bar{\nu}_{a}+1\leq i,j<\bar{\nu}_{a+1}
and
a=1,...,m-1
. In
[
BK06
]
these were simply referred to as admissible shapes. We note that
(1^{n})
is
\sigma
-admissible for every
\sigma
, and that for each
\sigma
there is a unique
\sigma
-admissible shape of minimal length, which is known as the
minimal admissible shape
.

From a
\sigma
-admissible shape we can construct a
relative shift matrix

\sigma(\nu)
, which is an
m\times m
-matrix with entries
s(\nu)_{i,j}
in
\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
determined by

(2.6)

\displaystyle s(\nu)_{a,b}=s_{\bar{\nu}_{a},\bar{\nu}_{b}}\text{ \ for all \ }1\leq a,b\leq m.

Since
s_{i,i+1}=0
for all
i=\bar{\nu}_{a},\bar{\nu}_{a}+1,...,\bar{\nu}_{a+1}-1
, and
\sigma
can be recovered from its super-diagonal entries, it follows that
\sigma
can be recovered from the pair
(\sigma(\nu),\nu)
.

Similarly, if we fix a level
\ell
and let
\lambda=(\lambda_{1},...,\lambda_{n})
be the partition determined by the data
(\sigma,\ell)
then we can define the
relative partition

\lambda(\nu)=(\lambda(\nu)_{1},...,\lambda(\nu)_{m})
by
\lambda(\nu)_{a}=\lambda_{\bar{\nu}_{a}}
. As above, we may recover
\lambda
from the pair
(\lambda(\nu),\nu)
.

Now fix
\beta=\pm 1
. We say that a
\sigma
-admissible shape
\nu
is
(\beta,\sigma)
-admissible
if
\beta^{\nu_{a}}=\beta
for
a=1,...,m
. When
\beta=-1
this ensures that
\nu_{a}
are all even, whilst for
\beta=1
any
\sigma
-admissible shape is
(\beta,\sigma)
-admissible.

The notation of a
(\beta,\sigma)
-admissible shape is key to our later constructions and so we would like to motivate it here. In Theorem
5.3
we introduce an involution on the shifted current Lie algebra
\mathfrak{c}_{n}(\sigma)
, and the involution extends to the current algebra
\mathfrak{c}_{n}
. On the zero graded piece
\mathfrak{gl}_{n}\subseteq\mathfrak{c}_{n}
, the matrix of the involution is symmetric when
\beta=1
and antisymmetric when
\beta=-1
. This is a shadow of the fact, central to our work: the (semiclassical) shifted twisted Yangian of type
AI
surjects onto classical finite
W
-algebras for which there are no multiplicity constraints on the partition of the nilpotent element, whilst the (as-yet-undefined) shifted twisted Yangian of type
AII
surjects onto those algebras for which there are multiplicity constraints (Cf. Section
2.4
). In this paper the role of the shifted twisted Yangian of type
AII
is played by the Dirac reductions with
\beta=-1
appearing in Theorem C.

2.3.
Classical finite
W
-algebras

Let
\mathfrak{g}
be the Lie algebra of a connected complex reductive group
G
. A nilpotent orbit of
\mathbb{O}\subseteq\mathfrak{g}
is a
G
-orbit consisting of elements which act nilpotently on every finite dimension representation. Let
\kappa
be a choice of
G
-invariant non-degenerate bilinear form on
\mathfrak{g}
.

The Jacobson–Morozov theorem states that nilpotent
G
-orbits are in 1-1 correspondence with the
G
-orbits in the space of Lie algebra homomorphisms
\operatorname{Hom}(\mathfrak{sl}_{2},\mathfrak{g})
. The bijection is provided by choosing a non-zero nilpotent element
e\in\mathfrak{sl}_{2}
and sending
\phi\in\operatorname{Hom}(\mathfrak{sl}_{2},\mathfrak{g})
to
G\cdot\phi(e)
. Inverting this map we may associate an
\mathfrak{sl}_{2}
-triple
(e,h,f)
to any nilpotent element
e\in\mathfrak{g}
, unique up to conjugacy.

The grading
\mathfrak{g}=\bigoplus_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}\mathfrak{g}(i)
induced by
\operatorname{ad}(h)
is called the Dynkin grading, and the variety
e+\mathfrak{g}^{f}\subseteq\mathfrak{g}
is called the Slodowy slice to
\mathbb{O}
. By
\mathfrak{sl}_{2}
-theory
T_{e}\mathbb{O}=\operatorname{ad}(\mathfrak{g})e
and
T_{e}(e+\mathfrak{g}^{f})=\mathfrak{g}^{f}
are mutually orthogonal complements inside
\mathfrak{g}
with respect to the Killing form, and so the slice is transversal to the orbit of
e
(in fact, to every orbit which it intersects).

Let
G(<0)
be the unipotent subgroup of
G
with Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}(<0)=\bigoplus_{i\leq 0}\mathfrak{g}(i)
. Thanks to
[
GG02
, Lemma 2.1]
we have an isomorphism

(2.7)

\displaystyle G(<0)\times(e+\mathfrak{g}^{f})\to e+\mathfrak{g}(\leq 1)

of
G(<0)
-varieties, where
G(<0)
acts by left multiplication on the left hand factor, and via the adjoint action on the right hand side.
We define the
classical finite
W
-algebra
to be the subquotient

(2.8)

\displaystyle S(\mathfrak{g},e):=(S(\mathfrak{g})/S(\mathfrak{g})\mathfrak{g}(\leq-1)_{e})^{G(<0)}

where
\mathfrak{g}(\leq 0)_{e}:=\{x-\kappa(e,x)\mid x\in\mathfrak{g}(\leq 0)\}
. Using (
2.7
) we identify
S(\mathfrak{g},e)
with
\mathbb{C}[e+\mathfrak{g}^{f}]
as commutative algebras.

We can equip
S(\mathfrak{g},e)
with a Poisson structure, using a version of Hamiltonian reduction (see
[
ACET20
, §3.1]
, for example). Describing this Poisson structure is the main goal of the present article.

2.4.
Nilpotent orbits in classical Lie algebras

Pick
\varepsilon=\pm 1
and an integer
N>0
satisfying
\varepsilon^{N}=1
. Suppose that
(\cdot,\cdot):\mathbb{C}^{N}\times\mathbb{C}^{N}\to\mathbb{C}
is a form satisfying
(u,v)=\varepsilon(v,u)
for all
u,v\in\mathbb{C}^{N}
. The Lie algebra subalgebra
\mathfrak{g}_{0}
of
\mathfrak{gl}_{N}
consisting of skew-self adjoint matrices with respect to
(\cdot,\cdot)
is a classical simple Lie algebra. More precisely
\mathfrak{g}_{0}\cong\mathfrak{sp}_{N}
when
\varepsilon=-1
and
\mathfrak{g}_{0}\cong\mathfrak{so}_{N}
when
\varepsilon=1
. The connected component of the subgroup of
\operatorname{GL}_{N}
preserving the form
(\cdot,\cdot)
is denoted
G_{0}
.

The nilpotent
\operatorname{GL}_{N}
-orbits in
\mathfrak{gl}_{N}
are parameterised by partitions
\lambda\vdash N
, thanks to the theory of Jordan normal form. If
\mathbb{O}_{\lambda}
is the orbit corresponding to
\lambda\vdash N
then
\mathbb{O}_{\lambda}\cap\mathfrak{g}_{0}
is either empty or is a union of at most two
G_{0}
-orbits
[
CM93
, §5]
. In particular,
\mathbb{O}_{\lambda}\cap\mathfrak{g}_{0}\neq\emptyset
if and only if:

•

(\varepsilon=1)
all even parts of
\lambda
occur with even multiplicity,

•

(\varepsilon=-1)
all odd parts of
\lambda
occur with even multiplicity.

We denote the set of partitions satisfying these conditions by
\mathcal{P}_{\varepsilon}(N)
. A partition
\lambda\in\mathcal{P}_{\varepsilon}(N)
is called
even
if all parts of
\lambda
have the same parity.

Also for
\lambda\in\mathcal{P}_{\varepsilon}(N)
we note that
\mathbb{O}_{\lambda}\cap\mathfrak{g}_{0}
is a union of two
G_{0}
-orbits if and only if
\varepsilon=1
and all parts of
\lambda
are even; such partitions are called
very even
. In all other cases,
\mathbb{O}_{\lambda}\cap\mathfrak{g}_{0}
is a single orbit.

2.5.
Dirac reduction for fixed points

Let
A
be an affine Poisson algebra with a group
H
of Poisson automorphisms. The ideal of coinvariants is denoted
I_{H}=(h\cdot a-a\mid h\in H,\ a\in A)
. The
H
-fixed points
I_{H}^{H}\subseteq I_{H}
is a Poisson ideal of
A^{H}
and the Dirac reduction of
A
by
H
is the Poisson algebra

R(A,H):=A^{H}/I_{H}^{H}.

It is explained in
[
To23
, §2.1]
that when
X=\operatorname{Spec}(A)
is an affine Poisson variety the Dirac reduction equips
X^{H}
with the structure of an affine Poisson scheme, see also
[
LPV13
, §5.4.3]
.

Now retain the notation of Section
2.3
. We suppose that
H\subseteq\operatorname{Aut}(\mathfrak{g})
is a reductive group of automorphisms fixing the
\mathfrak{sl}_{2}
-triple pointwise. Then
H
induces a group of Poisson automorphisms of
S(\mathfrak{g},e)
. We write
\mathfrak{g}_{0}=\mathfrak{g}^{H}
for the fixed point subalgebra, which is also the Lie algebra of a reductive group
[
To23
, Lemma 2.5(2)]
. One of the main applications of Dirac reduction, in the context of the present work, is the following.

Theorem 2.2
.

[
To23
, Theorem 2.6]

There is an isomorphisms of Poisson algebras

\displaystyle R(S(\mathfrak{g},e),H)\overset{\sim}{\longrightarrow}S(\mathfrak{g}_{0},e).

3.
Shifted twisted Yangian versus Dirac reduction of shifted Yangians

3.1.
Twisted Yangian

We refer the reader to
[
Mo07
, Ch. 2]
for a good introduction to Olshanski’s theory of twisted Yangians.

Recall that the twisted Yangian
\mathcal{Y}_{n}
associated to
\mathfrak{o}_{n}
is generated by elements
\{s_{i,j}^{(r)}\mid 1\leq i,j\leq n,\ \ r\geq 0\}
subject to the quaternary and symmetry relation
[
Mo07
, Proposition 2.2.1]
. In
[
LWZ23
]
those authors solved the long-standing problem of providing a Drinfeld presentation of
\mathcal{Y}_{n}
associated to the split bilinear form on
\mathbb{C}^{n}
. These are known as twisted Yangians of type
AI
, and from now on we will focus on type
AI
type.

We recall the main results of their their paper, which is the Drinfeld presentation of
\mathcal{Y}_{n}
, see
[
LWZ23
, Theorem 5.1]
.

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
C=(c_{ij})_{1\leq i,j\leq n-1}
be the Cartan matrix of type
{\sf A}_{n-1}
, and set
h_{i,-1}=1
,
c_{0j}=-\delta_{j,1}
. Then the twisted Yangian
\mathcal{Y}_{n}
is generated by

(3.1)

\displaystyle\{h_{i,r},b_{j,s}\mid 0<i<N\ \ 1\leq j<N,\ \ r,s\geq 0\}

subject only to the following relations, for
r,s\in\mathbb{N}
:

(3.2)

\displaystyle[h_{i,r},h_{j,s}]

\displaystyle=0,\ \ \ \ \ {h_{i,2r}=0,}

(3.3)

\displaystyle[h_{i,r+1},b_{j,s}]-[h_{i,r-1},b_{j,s+2}]

\displaystyle=c_{ij}[h_{i,r-1},b_{j,s+1}]_{-}+\frac{1}{4}c_{ij}^{2}[h_{i,r-1},b_{j,s}],

(3.4)

\displaystyle[b_{i,r+1},b_{j,s}]-[b_{i,r},b_{j,s+1}]

\displaystyle=\frac{c_{ij}}{2}[b_{i,r},b_{j,s}]_{-}-2\delta_{ij}(-1)^{r}h_{i,r+s+1},

(3.5)

\displaystyle[b_{i,r},b_{j,s}]

\displaystyle=0,\qquad\text{ for }|i-j|>1,

\displaystyle\mathrm{Sym}_{k_{1},k_{2}}\big[b_{i,k_{1}},[b_{i,k_{2}},b_{j,r}]\big]

.

and define

(5.60)

\displaystyle\widetilde{d}_{a,i,j}^{(r)}:=-\sum_{g=1}^{\nu_{a}}\sum_{t=1}^{r}d_{a,i,g}^{(t)}\widetilde{d}_{a,g,j}^{(r-t)}

\displaystyle e_{a,i,a+1,j}^{(r)}:=e_{a,i,j}^{(r)}\text{ for }1\leq a<m,\ 1\leq i\leq\nu_{a},\ 1\leq j\leq\nu_{a+1},\ s(\nu)_{a,a+1}<r;

For

Theorem 5.6

Let

\sigma

Let

Consider now the map
\tau_{\nu}:Y_{n}\to Y_{n}:T(u)\mapsto J_{\nu}^{-1}T(-u)^{t}J_{\nu}

(5.82)

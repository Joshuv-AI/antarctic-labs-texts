Xiaoyue XIA

Abstract
Recently in a paper by Lin, Dai and Tibboel, it was shown that the third and fourth Painlevé equations have tronquée and tritronquée solutions. We obtain global information about these tronquée and tritronquée solutions. We find their sectors of analyticity, their Borel summed representations in these sectors as well as the asymptotic position of the singularities near the boundaries of the analyticity sectors. We also correct slight errors in the paper mentioned.

Introduction

The well-known Painlevé equations were first introduced by Painlevé more than a century ago and have been investigated by many researchers. The Painlevé equations define new functions called Painlevé transcendents, which are considered as special nonlinear functions and their asymptotic behavior is of particular importance. For an overview of Painlevé equations and the asymptotic behavior of Painlevé transcendents please see, e.g.,
[

[

For the first Painlevé equation some pioneering works based on the powerful techniques of isomonodromic deformation and reduction to Riemann–Hilbert problem were done in the study of tronquée solutions by Kapaev and coauthors. In

In the following, we denote by
\mathcal{L}_{\phi}
the Laplace transform

\displaystyle f\longmapsto\int_{0}^{\infty{\rm e}^{{\rm i}\phi}}f(p){\rm e}^{-xp}{\rm d}p,

where

Assume that we have a formal series

\displaystyle\tilde{f}(w)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}a_{n}w^{-r-n},\qquad\operatorname{Re}(r)>0,

where the series
\sum\limits_{n=0}^{\infty}a_{n}x^{n}
has a positive radius of convergence. The Borel transform of
\tilde{f}
is defined to be the formal power series

\displaystyle\big(\mathcal{B}\tilde{f}\big)(p):=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{a_{n}p^{n+r-1}}{\Gamma(n+r)}.

In most cases the explicit solution of a differential equation is not known. We may obtain classical asymptotic series as formal power series solutions, but these formal solutions do not contain parameters that help us distinguish between actual solutions. This is illustrated in the following simple ordinary differential equation at the irregular singularity at
x=\infty

\displaystyle y^{\prime}+y=\frac{1}{x^{2}}.

(1.4)

The unique formal power series solution for
x\to\infty
is

\displaystyle\tilde{y}_{0}(x)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{n!}{x^{n+1}}

and the general solution to (
1.4
) is

\displaystyle y(x;C)=y_{0}(x)+C{\rm e}^{-x},\qquad\textrm{where}\quad y_{0}(x)={\rm e}^{-x}\int_{x_{1}}^{x}\frac{{\rm e}^{s}}{s^{2}}{\rm d}s\sim\tilde{y}_{0}(x)\quad\textrm{as}\quad x\to+\infty.

The idea of transseries solution is a completion of classical formal power series solution in the sense that the transseries solution representation includes the free parameters which appear in the actual solutions. In the example above, if we let

\displaystyle\tilde{y}(x)=\tilde{y}_{0}(x)+C{\rm e}^{-x}\qquad\textrm{for}\quad x\to+\infty,

(1.5)

then (
1.5
) is a formal solution to (
1.4
) and the simplest example of a transseries.

Under appropriate conditions (see
[
3
,
4
,
6
]
), given
\phi
, the operator
\mathcal{L}_{\phi}\mathcal{B}
is a one-to-one map between the transseries solutions and actual solutions. In the example (
1.4

\displaystyle y(x)=\begin{cases}\mathcal{L}_{\phi}\mathcal{B}\tilde{y}_{0}(x)+C_{+}{\rm e}^{-x},&-\phi=\arg(x)\in\big(0,\tfrac{\pi}{2}\big),\\
\mathcal{L}_{\phi}\mathcal{B}\tilde{y}_{0}(x)+C_{-}{\rm e}^{-x},&-\phi=\arg(x)\in\big({-}\frac{\pi}{2},0\big).\end{cases}

The value
C_{+}-C_{-}
is called the Stokes constant. This representation is a trivial example of Borel summed representation of solutions. In the case of nonlinear systems such as (
1.8

4.1

4.3

Recall that the Borel transform of a formal series

\displaystyle\tilde{f}(w)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}a_{n}w^{-r-n},\qquad\operatorname{Re}(r)>0,

where the series
\sum\limits_{n=0}^{\infty}a_{n}x^{n}
has a positive radius of convergence, is defined to be the formal power series

\displaystyle\big(\mathcal{B}\tilde{f}\big)(p):=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{a_{n}p^{n+r-1}}{\Gamma(n+r)}.

Lemma A.1
.

Assume that we have two formal series
\tilde{f}
and
\tilde{g}
,

\displaystyle\tilde{f}(w)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}a_{n}w^{-r-n},\qquad\operatorname{Re}(r)>0,

\displaystyle\tilde{g}(w)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}b_{n}w^{-r-s},\qquad\operatorname{Re}(s)>0,

where both series
\sum\limits_{n=0}^{\infty}a_{n}x^{n}
and
\sum\limits_{n=0}^{\infty}b_{n}x^{n}
have positive radii of convergence. Then

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}\big(\tilde{f}\tilde{g}\big)(p)=\big(\mathcal{B}\tilde{f}\ast\mathcal{B}\tilde{g}\big)(p)=p^{r+s-1}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\left(\sum_{k=0}^{n}a_{k}b_{n-k}\right)\frac{p^{n}}{\Gamma(n+r+s)},

where

\displaystyle\big(\mathcal{B}\tilde{f}\ast\mathcal{B}\tilde{g}\big)(p):=\int_{0}^{p}\big(\mathcal{B}\tilde{f}\big)(t)\big(\mathcal{B}\tilde{g}\big)(p-t)\mathrm{d}t.

Recall that the Laplace transform
\mathcal{L}_{\phi}
is defined as the following

\displaystyle f\longmapsto\int_{0}^{\infty{\rm e}^{{\rm i}\phi}}f(p){\rm e}^{-xp}{\rm d}p,

where
\phi\in\mathbb{R}
.

Lemma A.2
.

Assume that the function
f
is integrable over the ray
{\rm e}^{{\rm i}\phi}\mathbb{R}^{+}
, namely

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty{\rm e}^{{\rm i}\phi}}|f(p)||\mathrm{d}p|<\infty.

Then for
\operatorname{Re}(w{\rm e}^{{\rm i}\phi})>0

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{\phi}(1\ast f)(w)=\frac{1}{w}\mathcal{L}_{\phi}(f)(w).

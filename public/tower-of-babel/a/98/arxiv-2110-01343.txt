Taming singular stochastic differential equations: A numerical method
Khoa Lê and Chengcheng Ling

Abstract
. We consider a generic and explicit tamed Euler–Maruyama scheme for multidimensional time-inhomogeneous stochastic differential equations with multiplicative Brownian noise. The diffusive coefficient is uniformly elliptic, Hölder continuous and weakly differentiable in the spatial variables while the drift satisfies the strict Ladyzhenskaya–Prodi–Serrin condition, as considered by Krylov and Röckner (2005). In the discrete scheme, the drift is tamed by replacing it by an approximation. A strong rate of convergence of the scheme is provided in terms of the approximation error of the drift in a suitable and possibly very weak topology. A few examples of approximating drifts are discussed in detail. The parameters of the approximating drifts can vary and—under suitable conditions— be fine-tuned to achieve a strong convergence rate which is arbitrarily close to the benchmark 0.5 0.5 rate. The result is then applied to provide numerical solutions for stochastic transport equations with singular vector fields satisfying the aforementioned condition. Mathematics Subject Classification (2020): Primary 60H35, 60H10; Secondary 60H50, 60L90, 35B65. Keywords: Singular SDEs; strong approximation; tamed Euler scheme; regularization by noise; stochastic sewing; Zvonkin’s transformation; quantitative Khasminskii’s lemma.

Introduction

The aim of this article is to devise a numerical scheme and obtain its strong convergence rate for stochastic differential equations (SDEs) with integrable drift coefficients and elliptic regular diffusive coefficients.
We consider the SDE

(1.1)

\displaystyle dX_{t}=b(t,X_{t})dt+\sigma(t,X_{t})dB_{t},\quad X_{0}=x_{0},\quad t\in[0,1],

Let us briefly explain our general method and strategy.
Starting from (
1.1
) and (
1.3
), we decompose the difference

into three types of differences:

•

differences between functionals of

and

,

•

differences between functionals of
X_{t}
and functionals of

,

•

differences between functionals of
X^{n}_{t}
and functionals of

.

At this stage, our strategy aligns with the classical works
[
KP91
,
JP98
]
for SDE’s with Lipschitz coefficients. However, in order to utilize the regularizing effect of the noise in compensation for the lack of regularity of the drift, our treatments for these functionals are different and follow the recent approach of
[
DGL23
]
.
The differences of the first type can be easily estimated from above by
\varpi_{n}
.
For the differences of the second type, we use a Zvonkin-type transformation to show that they depend on
\sup_{t\in[0,1]}|X_{t}-X^{n}_{t}|
in a Lipschitz sense.
The differences of the last type contain, for instance, the functional

\sup_{t\in[0,1]}\left|\int_{0}^{t}[b^{n}(s,X^{n}_{k_{n}(s)})-b^{n}(s,X^{n}_{s})]ds\right|.

Because

and

are not continuous (uniformly in

), estimation for the above functional is a challenging problem and one has to utilize the regularizing effect from the noise, an important observation made by Dareiotis and Gerencsér in
[

]
.
For these differences, we use stochastic sewing techniques—originated from
[
Lê20
]
and further extended in
[

,

]
—to estimate them by a constant multiple of

.
From here, we obtain an integral inequality for the moment of

. An application of the stochastic Grönwall inequality yields the desired estimate in

2.2
.
From this analysis, one observes that the strong rate of convergence for (

The estimates for

in

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
{\mathcal{A}}=({\mathcal{A}}_{t})_{t\in[0,1]}
be a continuous adapted stochastic process and let
p,N\in(0,\infty)
be some fixed constants. Assume that
{\mathcal{A}}_{0}=0
and

\displaystyle\sup_{0\leqslant s\leqslant t\leqslant 1}\|\delta{\mathcal{A}}_{s,t}\|_{L_{p}(\Omega|{\mathcal{F}}_{s})}\leqslant N.

Then the following statements hold.

(1)

There exists a constant
c(p)
such that
\|{\mathcal{A}}_{\tau}\|_{L_{p}(\Omega)}\leqslant c(p)N
for any stopping time
\tau\leqslant 1
.

(2)

For every
\bar{p}\in(0,p)
, there exists a constant
c(\bar{p},p)
such that

\displaystyle\|\sup_{t\in[0,1]}|{\mathcal{A}}_{t}|\|_{L_{\bar{p}}(\Omega)}\leqslant c(\bar{p},p)N.

Proof.

Let
\tau
be a stopping time taking finitely many values
\{t_{j}\}\subset[0,1]
. By assumption, we have

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}(|\delta{\mathcal{A}}_{\tau,1}|^{p})={\mathbb{E}}\sum_{j}{\mathbf{1}}_{(\tau=t_{j})}{\mathbb{E}}(|\delta{\mathcal{A}}_{t_{j},1}|^{p}|{\mathcal{F}}_{t_{j}})\leqslant{\mathbb{E}}\sum_{j}{\mathbf{1}}_{(\tau=t_{j})}N^{p}\leqslant N^{p}.

Using the elementary inequality

, we have

\displaystyle\|{\mathcal{A}}_{\tau}\|_{L_{p}(\Omega)}\lesssim\|\delta{\mathcal{A}}_{\tau,1}\|_{L_{p}(\Omega)}+\|{\mathcal{A}}_{1}\|_{L_{p}(\Omega)}\lesssim N.

By approximations and continuity of
{\mathcal{A}}
, the above inequality also holds for all stopping times
\tau\leqslant 1
. This shows
1
. Part
2
is a consequence of part
1
and Lenglart inequality.
∎

+\Gamma_{1}w(s,t)^{1+\varepsilon}+h\Gamma_{2}w(s,t)^{\frac{1}{2}}+C_{3}\Gamma_{2}w(s,t)^{\frac{1}{2}+\varepsilon}\,.

Lemma 3.4

Let

(3.9)

\displaystyle\|Y_{t}-Y_{s}\|\leqslant\sum_{i=1}^{h}C_{i}s^{-\eta_{i}}(t-s)^{\tau_{i}}\quad\forall s_{-1}\leqslant s\leqslant t\leqslant 1,s\neq 0

for some constant

Assume that
\tau_{i}-\eta_{i}>0
for each

\displaystyle\|Y_{t}-Y_{s}\|\leqslant\sum_{i=1}^{h}(1-2^{\eta_{i}-\tau_{i}})^{-1}C_{i}(t-s)^{\tau_{i}-\eta_{i}}\quad\forall s_{-1}\leqslant s\leqslant t\leqslant 1,s\neq 0.

Proof.

Observe that (

Lemma 3.5

(Quantitative Khasminskii’s lemma)
.

Let

be such that

be a nonnegative measurable

-adapted process. Assume that for all

(3.10)

\displaystyle\left\|\int_{s}^{t}\beta(r)dr\right\|_{L_{1}(\Omega|{\mathcal{F}}_{s})}\leqslant\rho(s,t),

where
(s,t)\mapsto\rho(s,t)
is a nonrandom function on

satisfying the following conditions:

(1)

\rho(t_{1},t_{2})\leqslant\rho(t_{3},t_{4})

(2)

Suppose additionally that there exist

and a continuous control

\rho(s,t)\leqslant w(s,t)^{\gamma}
for each

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}\exp\Big(\lambda\int_{S}^{T}\beta(r)dr\Big)\leqslant 2^{1+(2\lambda)^{1/\gamma}w(S,T)}.

Proof.

The former statement is an excerpt from

In the above,

To obtain the estimate in

Lemma 3.8

(Stochastic Grönwall inequality)
.

Let
\xi_{t},V_{t}
be nonnegative nondecreasing processes,
let
A_{t}
be a continuous nondecreasing
{\mathcal{F}}_{t}
-adapted process with
A_{0}=0
, and let
M_{t}
be
{\mathcal{F}}_{t}
-local martingale with
M_{0}=0
. Suppose that there exists a constant
\theta\in(0,\infty)
such that with probability one,

(3.13)

\displaystyle\xi_{t}\leqslant\left(\int^{t}_{0}\xi_{s}^{1/\theta}dA_{s}\right)^{\theta}+M_{t}+V_{t},\quad\forall t\geqslant 0.

Then for any bounded stopping time
\tau
, we have

(3.14)

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}2^{-2^{1/\theta}A_{\tau}}\xi_{\tau}\leqslant 2{\mathbb{E}}V_{\tau}\quad\text{when}\quad\theta\leqslant 1

and

(3.15)

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}2^{-2A_{\tau}^{\theta}}\xi_{\tau}\leqslant 2{\mathbb{E}}V_{\tau}\quad\text{when}\quad\theta>1.

Proof.

We put
G=M+V
and consider two cases.

Case 1: when
\theta\leqslant 1
. Define

\displaystyle\bar{\xi}_{t}=\left(\int^{t}_{0}\xi_{s}^{1/\theta}dA_{s}\right)^{\theta}+G_{t}\quad\text{so that}\quad 0\leqslant\xi_{t}\leqslant\bar{\xi}_{t}.

We assume first that
M
is a uniformly integrable martingale.
For any
t\geqslant s\geqslant 0
, we have

\displaystyle\delta\bar{\xi}_{s,t}=\left(\int^{t}_{0}\xi_{r}^{1/\theta}dA_{r}\right)^{\theta}-\left(\int^{s}_{0}\xi_{r}^{1/\theta}dA_{r}\right)^{\theta}+\delta G_{s,t}.

We use the inequality
a^{\theta}-b^{\theta}\leqslant(a-b)^{\theta}
(valid for any
a\geqslant b\geqslant 0
) to obtain from the previous identity that

(3.16)

\displaystyle\delta\bar{\xi}_{s,t}\leqslant\left(\int^{t}_{s}\xi_{r}^{1/\theta}dA_{r}\right)^{\theta}+\delta G_{s,t}.

Define
t_{0}=0
and for each integer
j\geqslant 1
, the stopping time

\displaystyle t_{j}=\inf\{t>t_{j-1}\ :\ A_{t}-A_{t_{j}-1}\geqslant 2^{-1/\theta}\}.

Let
j\geqslant 1
be fixed. For every
t\in[t_{j-1},t_{j}]
, we derive from (
3.16
) that

\displaystyle\delta\bar{\xi}_{t_{j-1},t}\leqslant\frac{1}{2}\xi_{t}+\delta G_{t_{j-1},t}\leqslant\frac{1}{2}\bar{\xi}_{t}+\delta G_{t_{j-1},t}

which yields
\bar{\xi}_{t}\leqslant 2\bar{\xi}_{t_{j-1}}+2\delta G_{t_{j-1},t}
.
By iteration and the fact that
\bar{\xi}_{0}=V_{0}
, we have

(3.17)

\displaystyle\bar{\xi}_{t}\leqslant 2^{j}V_{0}+\sum_{i=1}^{j}2^{j-i+1}\delta G_{t_{i-1},t_{i}\wedge t},\quad\forall t\in[t_{j-1},t_{j}].

Next, let
\tau
be a bounded stopping time and let
N
be an (random) integer such that
t_{N}>\tau
. We have

\displaystyle 2^{-2^{1/\theta}A_{\tau}}\bar{\xi}_{\tau}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{N}{\mathbf{1}}_{[t_{j-1},t_{j})}(\tau)2^{-2^{1/\theta}A_{\tau}}\bar{\xi}_{\tau}\leqslant\sum_{j=1}^{N}{\mathbf{1}}_{[t_{j-1},t_{j})}(\tau)2^{-(j-1)}\bar{\xi}_{\tau}.

Using (
3.17
), we have

\displaystyle 2^{-2^{1/\theta}A_{\tau}}\bar{\xi}_{\tau}

\displaystyle\leqslant 2\sum_{j=1}^{N}{\mathbf{1}}_{[t_{j-1},t_{j})}(\tau)\left(V_{0}+\sum_{i=1}^{j}2^{1-i}\delta G_{t_{i-1},\tau\wedge t_{i}}\right)

\displaystyle=2V_{0}+2\sum_{i=1}^{N}\sum_{j=i}^{N}{\mathbf{1}}_{[t_{j-1},t_{j})}(\tau)2^{1-i}\delta G_{t_{i-1},\tau\wedge t_{i}}

\displaystyle=2V_{0}+2\sum_{i=1}^{N}{\mathbf{1}}_{[t_{i-1},t_{N})}(\tau)2^{1-i}\delta G_{t_{i-1},\tau\wedge t_{i}},

which is rewritten as

(3.18)

2^{-2^{1/\theta}A_{\tau}}\bar{\xi}_{\tau}\leqslant 2V_{0}+2\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}{\mathbf{1}}_{[t_{i-1},\infty)}(\tau)2^{1-i}\delta V_{\tau\wedge t_{i-1},\tau\wedge t_{i}}\\
+2\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}{\mathbf{1}}_{[t_{i-1},\infty)}(\tau)2^{1-i}\delta M_{\tau\wedge t_{i-1},\tau\wedge t_{i}}.

By martingale property, boundedness of
\tau
and uniform integrability,
{\mathbb{E}}|\delta M_{\tau\wedge t_{i-1},\tau\wedge t_{i}}|\leqslant{\mathbb{E}}|M_{\tau\wedge t_{i-1}}|+{\mathbb{E}}|M_{\tau\wedge t_{i}}|\leqslant 2\sup_{t\geqslant 0}{\mathbb{E}}|M_{t}|<\infty
.
Hence, by Fubini theorem and martingale property,

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}{\mathbf{1}}_{[t_{i-1},\infty)}(\tau)2^{1-i}\delta M_{\tau\wedge t_{i-1},\tau\wedge t_{i}}={\mathbb{E}}\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}{\mathbf{1}}_{[t_{i-1},\infty)}(\tau)2^{1-i}{\mathbb{E}}(\delta M_{\tau\wedge t_{i-1},\tau\wedge t_{i}}|{\mathcal{F}}_{\tau\wedge t_{i-1}})=0.

Taking expectation in (
3.18
) gives

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}2^{-2^{1/\theta}A_{\tau}}\bar{\xi}_{\tau}

\displaystyle\leqslant{\mathbb{E}}\left(2V_{0}+2\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}{\mathbf{1}}_{[t_{i-1},\infty)}(\tau)2^{1-i}\delta V_{\tau\wedge t_{i-1},\tau\wedge t_{i}}\right)

\displaystyle\leqslant{\mathbb{E}}\left(2V_{0}+2\sum_{i=1}^{N}\delta V_{\tau\wedge t_{i-1},\tau\wedge t_{i}}\right)=2{\mathbb{E}}V_{\tau}.

In the general case when
M
is a local martingale, let
\{\tau_{n}\}
be a sequence of increasing stopping times such that
\lim_{n}\tau_{n}=\infty
a.s. and for each
n
,
M_{\tau_{n}\wedge\cdot}
is a uniformly integrable martingale. For a bounded stopping time
\tau
, the previous case implies that

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}2^{-2^{1/\theta}A_{\tau\wedge\tau_{n}}}\bar{\xi}_{\tau\wedge\tau_{n}}

\displaystyle\leqslant 2{\mathbb{E}}V_{\tau\wedge\tau_{n}}.

Sending
n\to\infty
yields (
3.14
).

Case 2: when
\theta>1
. Using Hölder inequality and integration by parts

\displaystyle\left(\int_{0}^{t}\xi^{1/\theta}dA\right)^{\theta}\leqslant\left(\int_{0}^{t}\xi dA\right)A_{t}^{\theta-1}=\int_{0}^{t}\int_{0}^{s}\xi dA(\theta-1)A_{s}^{\theta-2}dA_{s}+\int_{0}^{t}A^{\theta-1}\xi dA.

By monotonicity,
\int_{0}^{s}\xi dA\leqslant\xi_{s}A_{s}
so that

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}\int_{0}^{s}\xi dA(\theta-1)A_{s}^{\theta-2}dA_{s}\leqslant\int_{0}^{t}\xi_{s}(\theta-1)A_{s}^{\theta-1}dA_{s}.

Hence, we have

\displaystyle\left(\int_{0}^{t}\xi^{1/\theta}dA\right)^{\theta}\leqslant\int_{0}^{t}\xi_{s}\theta A_{s}^{\theta-1}dA_{s}=\int_{0}^{t}\xi_{s}dA^{\theta}_{s}.

Together with (
3.13
), we have

\displaystyle\xi_{t}\leqslant\int_{0}^{t}\xi dA^{\theta}+M_{t}+V_{t},\quad\forall t\geqslant 0.

Using the result from the previous case, we obtain (
3.15
). ∎

(4.3)

\|\int_{S}^{T}(f(r,B_{r})-f(r,B_{k_{n}(r)}))dr\|_{L_{p}(\Omega)}\\
\leqslant N(1/n)^{\frac{1}{2}}\log(n)\|f\|_{\mathbb{L}_{p}^{q}([S,T])}\left[S^{-\frac{d}{2p}}|T-S|^{\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{q}}+S^{-\frac{d}{p}}|T-S|^{1-\frac{2}{q}}\right]

and

(4.4)

\displaystyle\|\int_{S}^{T}(f(r,B_{r})-f(r,B_{k_{n}(r)}))dr\|_{L_{p}(\Omega)}\leqslant N(1/n)^{\frac{1}{2}}\log(n)\|f\|_{\mathbb{L}_{p}^{q}([S,T])}|T-S|^{\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{q}-\frac{d}{2p}}.

Proof.

(
4.4
) is a direct consequence of (
4.3
) and
Lemma

3.4
.
We show (
4.3
) below.
Let
2/n\leqslant S\leqslant T\leqslant 1
be fixed. By linearity, we can assume that
\|f\|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{q}_{p}([S,T])}=1
.
For
S\leqslant s\leqslant t\leqslant T
, let

\displaystyle A_{s,t}:={\mathbb{E}}_{s}\int_{s}^{t}(f(r,B_{r})-f(r,B_{k_{n}(r)}))dr.

We treat two cases
t\leqslant k_{n}(s)+\frac{2}{n}
and
t>k_{n}(s)+\frac{2}{n}
separately as following.

Case 1.
For
t\in(s,k_{n}(s)+\frac{2}{n}]
, by triangle inequality and (
4.1
) we have

\displaystyle\|A_{s,t}\|_{L_{p}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\leqslant\int_{s}^{t}\|f(r,B_{r})\|_{L_{p}(\Omega)}+\|f(r,B_{k_{n}(r)})\|_{L_{p}(\Omega)}dr

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{s}^{t}k_{n}(r)^{-\frac{d}{2p}}\|f(r,\cdot)\|_{L_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}dr.

Note that
k_{n}(r)\geqslant k_{n}(s)\geqslant s/2
, applying Hölder inequality and the fact that
t-s\leqslant 2/n
, we have

\displaystyle\int_{s}^{t}k_{n}(r)^{-\frac{d}{2p}}\|f(r,\cdot)\|_{L_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}dr

\displaystyle\lesssim s^{-\frac{d}{2p}}\|f\|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{q}_{p}([s,t])}(t-s)^{1-\frac{1}{q}}

\displaystyle\lesssim(1/n)^{1/2}s^{-\frac{d}{2p}}\|f\|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{q}_{p}([s,t])}(t-s)^{\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{q}}.

This gives

(4.5)

\displaystyle\|A_{s,t}\|_{L_{p}(\Omega)}\lesssim(1/n)^{\frac{1}{2}}s^{-\frac{d}{2p}}\|f\|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{q}_{p}([s,t])}(t-s)^{\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{q}}.

Case 2.
When
t\in(k_{n}(s)+\frac{2}{n},1]
, by triangle inequality,

\displaystyle\|A_{s,t}\|_{L_{p}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\leqslant\int_{s}^{k_{n}(s)+\frac{2}{n}}\|{\mathbb{E}}_{s}(f(r,B_{r})-f(r,B_{k_{n}(r)}))\|_{L_{p}(\Omega)}dr

\displaystyle\quad+\int_{k_{n}(s)+\frac{2}{n}}^{t}\|{\mathbb{E}}_{s}(f(r,B_{r})-f(r,B_{k_{n}(r)}))\|_{L_{p}(\Omega)}dr=:I_{1}+I_{2}.

For
I_{1}
, from (
4.5
) we know that

\displaystyle I_{1}\lesssim(1/n)^{\frac{1}{2}}s^{-\frac{d}{2p}}\|f\|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{q}_{p}([s,t])}\left(k_{n}(s)-s+\frac{2}{n}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{q}}.

Because
k_{n}(s)-s+\frac{2}{n}\leqslant t-s
,
we get

\displaystyle I_{1}\lesssim(1/n)^{\frac{1}{2}}s^{-\frac{d}{2p}}\|f\|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{q}_{p}([s,t])}(t-s)^{\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{q}}.

By (
4.1
) and (
4.2
) we have for
I_{2}

\displaystyle I_{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{k_{n}(s)+\frac{2}{n}}^{t}\|P_{s,r}f(r,B_{s})-P_{s,k_{n}(r)}f(r,B_{s})\|_{L_{p}(\Omega)}dr

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{k_{n}(s)+\frac{2}{n}}^{t}s^{-\frac{d}{2p}}\|P_{s,r}f(r,\cdot)-P_{s,k_{n}(r)}f(r,\cdot)\|_{L_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}dr

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{k_{n}(s)+\frac{2}{n}}^{t}s^{-\frac{d}{2p}}n^{-\frac{1}{2}}(k_{n}(r)-s)^{-\frac{1}{2}}\|f(r,\cdot)\|_{L_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}dr

\displaystyle\lesssim(1/n)^{\frac{1}{2}}s^{-\frac{d}{2p}}\|f\|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{q}_{p}([s,t])}(t-s)^{\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{q}}.

Combining these two cases together we obtain that for
S\leqslant s\leqslant t\leqslant T
,

(4.6)

\displaystyle\|A_{s,t}\|_{L_{p}(\Omega)}\lesssim(1/n)^{\frac{1}{2}}s^{-\frac{d}{2p}}\|f\|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{q}_{p}([s,t])}(t-s)^{\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{q}}.

Furthermore, for
u\in(s,t)
, we have
{\mathbb{E}}_{s}\delta A_{s,u,t}=0
.
Let
w
be the continuous control defined by

\displaystyle w(s,t)=\left[s^{-\frac{d}{2p}}\|f\|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{q}_{p}([s,t])}(t-s)^{\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{q}}\right]^{2}+s^{-\frac{d}{2p}}\|f\|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{q}_{p}([s,t])}(t-s)^{1-\frac{1}{q}}.

(See
Example

3.3
for a justification that
w
is a control.)
Denote

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}_{t}:=\int_{0}^{t}(f(r,B_{r})-f(r,B_{k_{n}(r)}))dr,\quad J_{s,t}:=\delta\mathcal{A}_{s,t}-A_{s,t}.

Using similar estimates leading to (
4.5
), we have

\displaystyle\|J_{s,t}\|_{L_{p}(\Omega)}\lesssim s^{-\frac{d}{2p}}\|f\|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{q}_{p}([s,t])}(t-s)^{1-\frac{1}{q}}\lesssim w(s,t).

Furthermore,
\delta J_{s,u,t}=-\delta A_{s,u,t}
and we derive from (
4.6
) that

\displaystyle\|\delta J_{s,u,t}\|_{L_{p}(\Omega)}\lesssim(1/n)^{\frac{1}{2}}w(s,t)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

It is obvious that
{\mathbb{E}}_{s}J_{s,t}=0
and hence
{\mathbb{E}}_{s}\delta J_{s,u,t}=0
.
Applying
Lemma

3.2
, we have

\displaystyle\|J_{s,t}\|_{L_{p}(\Omega)}\lesssim(1/n)^{\frac{1}{2}}\log(n)\left[w(s,t)^{\frac{1}{2}}+w(s,t)\right]

for every
S\leqslant s\leqslant t\leqslant T
.
By triangle inequality and (
4.6
), this implies that

\displaystyle\|\delta{\mathcal{A}}_{s,t}\|_{L_{p}(\Omega)}\lesssim(1/n)^{\frac{1}{2}}\log(n)\left[w(s,t)^{\frac{1}{2}}+w(s,t)\right].

Because
\|f\|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{q}_{p}([s,t])}\leqslant\|f\|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{q}_{p}([S,T])}=1
and
t-s\leqslant 1
, we have
w(s,t)\leqslant 2s^{-\frac{d}{p}}(t-s)^{1-\frac{2}{q}}
. Hence, we deduce (
4.3
) from the above estimate.
∎

In the following result, the
L_{p}(\Omega)
-norm in (
4.4
) is improved to
L_{p}(\Omega|{\mathcal{F}}_{v})
-norm.

Proposition 4.3
.

Let
f\in\mathbb{L}_{p}^{q}([0,1])
, with
p,q\in[2,\infty)
satisfying
\frac{d}{p}+\frac{2}{q}<1
. Let
v\in[0,1-2/n]
be a fixed number. Then for all
v+2/n\leqslant S\leqslant T\leqslant 1
and all
n
, one has the bound

(4.7)

\displaystyle\|\int_{S}^{T}[f(r,B_{r})-f(r,B_{k_{n}(r)})]dr\|_{L_{p}(\Omega|{\mathcal{F}}_{v})}\leqslant N(1/n)^{\frac{1}{2}}\log(n)\|f\|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{q}_{p}([S,T])}|T-S|^{\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{q}-\frac{d}{2p}},

where
N=N(p,d)
is a constant.

Proof.

We follow the argument used in
Proposition

4.2
, replacing the
L_{p}(\Omega)
-norm by the
L_{p}(\Omega|{\mathcal{F}}_{v})
-norm. The estimate (
4.1
) used therein (whose purpose is to deduce the analytic
L_{p}({{\mathbb{R}}^{d}})
-norm from the probabilistic
L_{p}(\Omega)
-norm) is replaced by the following estimate

(4.8)

\displaystyle\|g(B_{t})\|_{L_{p}(\Omega|{\mathcal{F}}_{v})}\leqslant N(t-v)^{-\frac{d}{2p}}\|g\|_{L_{p}({{\mathbb{R}}^{d}})}\quad\forall g\in L_{p}({{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}),t>v,

with the same constant
N
as in (
4.1
).
This yields the following estimate, which corresponds to (
4.3
),

(4.9)

\|\int_{S}^{T}[f(r,B_{r})-f(r,B_{k_{n}(r)})]dr\|_{L_{p}(\Omega|{\mathcal{F}}_{v})}\\
\leqslant N(1/n)^{\frac{1}{2}}\log(n)\|f\|_{\mathbb{L}_{p}^{q}([S,T])}\left[(S-v)^{-\frac{d}{2p}}|T-S|^{\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{q}}+(S-v)^{-\frac{d}{p}}|T-S|^{1-\frac{2}{q}}\right].

Applying
Lemma

3.4
, we obtain (
4.7
).
∎

An advantage of the conditional norms over the usual ones is realized the next result.

Proposition 4.4
.

Let
f
be a Borel function in
{\mathbb{L}}_{p}^{q}([0,1])\cap{\mathbb{L}}_{\infty}^{q}([0,{1}])
for some
p,q\in[2,\infty)
satisfying
\frac{d}{p}+\frac{2}{q}<1
.

We put
\beta_{n}(f)=\sup_{r\in D_{n}}\|f\|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{q}_{\infty}([r,r+1/n])}
.
Then for any
\bar{p}\in(0,p)
, there exists a constant
N=N(d,p,q,\bar{p})
such that

\|\sup_{t\in[0,1]}|\int_{0}^{t}[f(r,B_{r})-f(r,B_{k_{n}(r)})]dr|\|_{L_{\bar{p}}(\Omega)}\\
\leqslant N\left[(1/n)^{1-\frac{1}{q}}\beta_{n}(f)+(1/n)^{\frac{1}{2}}\log(n)\|f\|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{q}_{p}([0,1])}\right].

Proof.

Put
{\mathcal{A}}_{t}=\int_{0}^{t}(f(r,B_{r})-f(r,B_{k_{n}(r)}))dr
which has continuous sample paths by (
4.11
).
In view of
Lemma

3.1
, it suffices to show that there exists a constant
N=N(d,p,q)
such that

(4.10)

\displaystyle\|\delta{\mathcal{A}}_{s,t}\|_{L_{p}(\Omega|{\mathcal{F}}_{s})}\leqslant N\left[(1/n)^{1-\frac{1}{q}}\beta_{n}(f)+(1/n)^{\frac{1}{2}}\log(n)\|f\|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{q}_{p}([0,1])}\right]

for every
(s,t)\in\Delta
.

Indeed, by assumption and Hölder inequality, we have

(4.11)

\displaystyle|\delta{\mathcal{A}}_{s,t}|\leqslant 2\int_{s}^{t}\|f_{r}\|_{L_{\infty}({{\mathbb{R}}^{d}})}dr\lesssim\|f\|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{q}_{\infty}([s,t])}(t-s)^{1-\frac{1}{q}}

for every
(s,t)\in\Delta
.
For every
(s,t)\in\Delta
satisfying
t-s\geqslant 2/n
, we obtain from (
4.11
) and (
4.7
) that

\displaystyle\|\delta{\mathcal{A}}_{s,t}\|_{L_{p}(\Omega|{\mathcal{F}}_{s})}

\displaystyle\leqslant\|\delta{\mathcal{A}}_{s,s+2/n}\|_{L_{p}(\Omega|{\mathcal{F}}_{s})}+\|\delta{\mathcal{A}}_{s+2/n,t}\|_{L_{p}(\Omega|{\mathcal{F}}_{s})}

\displaystyle\lesssim(1/n)^{1-\frac{1}{q}}\|f\|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{q}_{\infty}([s,s+2/n])}+(1/n)^{\frac{1}{2}}\log(n)\|f\|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{q}_{p}([0,1])}.

Note that
\|f\|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{q}_{\infty}([s,s+2/n])}\lesssim\beta_{n}(f)
.
For every
(s,t)\in\Delta
satisfying
t-s\leqslant 2/n
, (
4.11
) trivially implies that

\displaystyle\|\delta{\mathcal{A}}_{s,t}\|_{L_{p}(\Omega|{\mathcal{F}}_{s})}\lesssim(1/n)^{1-\frac{1}{q}}\|f\|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{q}_{\infty}([s,s+2/n])}\lesssim(1/n)^{1-\frac{1}{q}}\beta_{n}(f).

Hence, in both cases, we have obtained (
4.10
).
∎

Next, we turn to the functional
\int_{s}^{t}g(r,B_{r})dr
.

Lemma 4.5
.

Let
g
be a function in
{\mathbb{L}}^{q}_{p}([0,1])
for some
p,q\in[1,\infty]
satisfying
\frac{d}{p}+\frac{2}{q}<2
. Then for every
m\geqslant 1
, there exists a constant
N=N(m,d,p,q)
such that for every
(s,t)\in\Delta
,

\displaystyle\|\int_{s}^{t}g(r,B_{r})dr\|_{L_{m}(\Omega)}\leqslant N\|g\|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{q}_{p}([s,t])}(t-s)^{1-\frac{d}{2p}-\frac{1}{q}}.

Proof.

We can assume that
g
is nonnegative.
Using standard estimate for the heat kernel and Hölder inequality, we have for every
(s,t)\in\Delta
that

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}_{s}\int_{s}^{t}g(r,B_{r})dr

\displaystyle=\int_{s}^{t}P_{s,r}g(r,B_{s})dr\lesssim\int_{s}^{t}(r-s)^{-\frac{d}{2p}}\|g_{r}\|_{L_{p}({{\mathbb{R}}^{d}})}dr

\displaystyle\lesssim\|g\|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{q}_{p}([s,t])}(t-s)^{1-\frac{d}{2p}-\frac{1}{q}}.

Applying
Lemma

3.5
, we obtain the stated estimate for every integer
m\geqslant 1
. This and the Hölder interpolation inequality implies the estimate for any real
m\geqslant 1
.
∎

Proposition 4.6
.

Let
p\in[2,\infty)
and
q\in[2,\infty]
. Let
\Gamma
be a positive number,
w_{0}
be a continuous control on
\Delta
and
g
be a function in
{\mathbb{L}}^{q}_{p}([0,1])
such that for any
(s,t)\in\Delta
,

\displaystyle\|g\|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{q}_{-1,p}([s,t])}\leqslant\Gamma w_{0}(s,t)^{\frac{1}{q}}\quad\text{and}\quad\|g\|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{q}_{p}([s,t])}\leqslant w_{0}(s,t)^{\frac{1}{q}}.

(a) Then for every
0\leqslant v<S\leqslant T\leqslant 1

(4.12)

\|\int_{S}^{T}g(r,B_{r})dr\|_{L_{p}(\Omega|{\mathcal{F}}_{v})}\leqslant Nw_{0}(S,T)^{\frac{1}{q}}\Gamma(1+|\log(\Gamma)|)\\
\times\left[(S-v)^{-\frac{d}{2p}}(T-S)^{\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{q}}+(S-v)^{-\frac{d}{p}}(T-S)^{1-\frac{2}{q}}\right].

(b) If furthermore
p,q
satisfy
\frac{d}{p}+\frac{2}{q}<1
, then for any
\bar{p}\in(0,p)
, there exists a constant
N=N(d,p,q,\bar{p})
such that

\displaystyle\|\sup_{t\in[0,1]}|\int_{0}^{t}g(r,B_{r})dr|\|_{L_{\bar{p}}(\Omega)}\leqslant N\Gamma(1+|\log(\Gamma)|)w_{0}(0,1).

Proof.

(a) Let
v,S,T
be fixed such that
0\leqslant v<S<T\leqslant 1
. We can assume without loss of generality that
w_{0}(S,T)=1
.
For each
(s,t)\in\Delta([S,T])
, let

\displaystyle A_{s,t}={\mathbb{E}}_{s}\int_{s}^{t}g(r,B_{r})dr=\int_{s}^{t}P_{s,r}g(r,B_{s})dr\quad\text{and}\quad J_{s,t}=\int_{s}^{t}g(r,B_{r})dr-A_{s,t}.

In the above, we can interchange the conditional expectation and the integration due to Fubini theorem and
Lemma

4.5
.
Define the continuous control
w
on
\Delta([S,T])
by

\displaystyle w(s,t)=\left[(s-v)^{-\frac{d}{2p}}(t-s)^{\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{q}}w_{0}(s,t)^{\frac{1}{q}}\right]^{2}+(s-v)^{-\frac{d}{2p}}(t-s)^{1-\frac{1}{q}}w_{0}(s,t)^{\frac{1}{q}}.

Applying Minkowski inequality, (
4.1
) and Hölder inequality, we have

\displaystyle\|J_{s,t}\|_{L_{p}(\Omega|{\mathcal{F}}_{v})}\leqslant 2\int_{s}^{t}\|g(r,B_{r})\|_{L_{p}(\Omega|{\mathcal{F}}_{v})}dr

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{s}^{t}(r-v)^{-\frac{d}{2p}}\|g(r,\cdot)\|_{L_{p}({{\mathbb{R}}^{d}})}dr

\displaystyle\lesssim(s-v)^{-\frac{d}{2p}}\|g\|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{q}_{p}([s,t])}(t-s)^{1-\frac{1}{q}}\lesssim w(s,t).

Furthermore,
{\mathbb{E}}_{s}J_{s,t}=0
, showing that (
3.1
) is satisfied.

On the other hand, we have

\displaystyle\|A_{s,t}\|_{L_{p}(\Omega|{\mathcal{F}}_{v})}

\displaystyle\leqslant\int_{s}^{t}\|P_{s,r}g(r,B_{s})\|_{L_{p}(\Omega|{\mathcal{F}}_{v})}dr

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{s}^{t}(s-v)^{-\frac{d}{2p}}\|P_{s,r}g(r,\cdot)\|_{L_{p}({{\mathbb{R}}^{d}})}dr

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{s}^{t}(s-v)^{-\frac{d}{2p}}(r-s)^{-\frac{1}{2}}\|g(r,\cdot)\|_{L_{-1,p}({{\mathbb{R}}^{d}})}dr

\displaystyle\lesssim(s-v)^{-\frac{d}{2p}}(t-s)^{\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{q}}\|g\|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{q}_{-1,p}([s,t])}.

Combining with our assumption on
g
leads to
\|A_{s,t}\|_{L_{p}(\Omega)}\lesssim\Gamma w(s,t)^{1/2}
. Since
\delta J_{s,u,t}=-\delta A_{s,u,t}
, this implies that
J
satisfies (
3.2
). The condition (
3.3
) is trivial because
{\mathbb{E}}_{s}J_{s,t}=0
.
Applying
Lemma

3.2
, we have for every
(s,t)\in\Delta([S,T])
,

\displaystyle\|J_{s,t}\|_{L_{p}(\Omega|{\mathcal{F}}_{v})}\lesssim\Gamma(1+|\log\Gamma|)w(s,t)^{\frac{1}{2}}+\Gamma w(s,t)

and by triangle inequality,

\displaystyle\|\int_{s}^{t}g(r,B_{r})dr\|_{L_{p}(\Omega)}\lesssim\Gamma(1+|\log\Gamma|)w(s,t)^{\frac{1}{2}}+\Gamma w(s,t).

Because
w_{0}(s,t)\leqslant 1
and
t-s\leqslant 1
, we have
w(s,t)\leqslant 2(s-v)^{-\frac{d}{p}}(t-s)^{1-\frac{2}{q}}
. Hence, we deduce (
4.12
) from the above estimate by taking
(s,t)=(S,T)
.

(b) Applying
Lemma

3.4
and part (a), we have

\displaystyle\|\int_{s}^{t}g(r,B_{r})dr\|_{L_{p}(\Omega|{\mathcal{F}}_{v})}\lesssim\Gamma(1+|\log(\Gamma)|)w_{0}(0,1)

for every
v<s\leqslant t\leqslant 1
.
In view of
Lemma

4.5
and Kolmogorov continuity theorem, it is easy to see that the process
t\mapsto\int_{0}^{t}g(r,B_{r})dr
has a continuous version.
Hence, the above inequality holds for every
0\leqslant v=s\leqslant t\leqslant 1
. Applying
Lemma

3.1
, we obtain the result.
∎

Proposition 4.7
.

Let
\nu\in[0,1)
,
p\in[2,\infty)
and

,
\frac{d}{p}+\frac{2}{q}+\nu<2

Then for any

Proof.

In view of
Lemma

3.1
, it suffices to show that

(4.13)

\displaystyle\sup_{(s,t)\in\Delta}\|\int_{s}^{t}g(r,B_{r})dr\|_{L_{p}(\Omega|{\mathcal{F}}_{s})}\lesssim\|g\|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{q}_{-\nu,p}([0,1])}.

The proof is similar to that of
Proposition

4.6
, however, the control can be chosen in a simpler way.
Let
v\in[0,1]
be fixed but arbitrary. For each
(s,t)\in\Delta
,
s>v
, define

and

as in the proof of
Proposition

4.6
. As in the aforementioned proof,

and

\displaystyle\|J_{s,t}\|_{L_{p}(\Omega|{\mathcal{F}}_{v})}+\|A_{s,t}\|_{L_{p}(\Omega|{\mathcal{F}}_{v})}\lesssim(s-v)^{-\frac{d}{2p}}(t-s)^{1-\frac{\nu}{2}-\frac{1}{q}}\|g\|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{q}_{-\nu,p}([s,t])}.

Let
w
be the control on
\Delta((v,1])

\displaystyle w(s,t)=\left[(s-v)^{-\frac{d}{2p}}(t-s)^{1-\frac{\nu}{2}-\frac{1}{q}}\|g\|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{q}_{-\nu,p}([s,t])}\right]^{1/(1-\nu/2)}.

and applying

3.2

\displaystyle\|J_{s,t}\|_{L_{p}(\Omega|{\mathcal{F}}_{v})}\lesssim w(s,t)^{1-\frac{\nu}{2}}.

By triangle inequality and the previous estimate for

, we have for every

,

,

\displaystyle\|\int_{s}^{t}g(r,B_{r})dr\|_{L_{p}(\Omega|{\mathcal{F}}_{v})}\lesssim w(s,t)^{1-\frac{\nu}{2}}\lesssim(s-v)^{-\frac{d}{2p}}(t-s)^{1-\frac{\nu}{2}-\frac{1}{q}}\|g\|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{q}_{p}([0,1])}.

An application of

3.4
gives

\displaystyle\|\int_{s}^{t}g(r,B_{r})dr\|_{L_{p}(\Omega|{\mathcal{F}}_{v})}\lesssim\|g\|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{q}_{p}([0,1])}

,

.

4.5
and Kolmogorov continuity theorem, the process

(s,t)\in\Delta
and

, which shows (
4.13
).
∎

Analysis of the discrete paths

Theorem 5.1
.

\beta_{n}(f)=\sup_{t\in D_{n}}\|f\|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{q}_{\infty}([t,t+1/n])}

Then for any

(5.1)

\displaystyle\Big\|\sup_{t\in[0,1]}

\rho<1
.
Observe furthermore that
(t-s)/(t-\bar{s})\leqslant 2
. Thus we have

\displaystyle\int_{s+2/n}^{t}e^{\lambda(k_{n}(r)-s)}(k_{n}(r)-s)^{-\rho}(t-k_{n}(r))^{\frac{\alpha}{2}-1}dr\lesssim\lambda^{-\frac{\alpha}{2}}e^{\lambda(t-s)}(t-s)^{-\rho}.

Putting the previous estimates altogether into (
5.17
), we have

\displaystyle\|Q^{n}_{s,t}f\|_{L_{p_{2}}({{\mathbb{R}}^{d}})}\lesssim(t-s)^{-\rho}(1+m^{*}_{t}\lambda^{-\frac{\alpha}{2}}e^{\lambda(t-s)}).

By approximations, the above estimate also holds for any function
f\in L_{p_{1}}({{\mathbb{R}}^{d}})
with
\|f\|_{L_{p_{1}}({{\mathbb{R}}^{d}})}=1
.
It follows that
m_{t}\lesssim 1+\lambda^{-\frac{\alpha}{2}}m^{*}_{t}
for every
t\geqslant s+2/n
. By choosing
\lambda
sufficiently large, we conclude that
m^{*}_{1}
is bounded by a constant independent from
n
and thus obtain (
5.15
).

Step 3.
We remove the restriction
\rho<1
in the previous step.

Suppose that
\rho:=\frac{d}{2p_{1}}-\frac{d}{2p_{2}}<2
. Define
p_{3}\in[p_{1},p_{2}]
by
\frac{d}{p_{3}}=\frac{d}{2p_{1}}+\frac{d}{2p_{2}}
so that
\frac{d}{2p_{1}}-\frac{d}{2p_{3}}=\frac{d}{2p_{3}}-\frac{d}{2p_{2}}=\frac{\rho}{2}<1
. Let
t\geqslant s+4/n
and
u=(s+t)/2
. Then by Markov property of the Euler–Maruyama scheme, we have
Q^{n}_{s,t}=Q^{n}_{k_{n}(u),t}Q^{n}_{s,k_{n}(u)}
. It is easy to see that
t>k_{n}(u)>s
so that by (
5.15
), we have for every
f\in L_{p_{1}}({{\mathbb{R}}^{d}})
that

\displaystyle\|Q^{n}_{s,t}f\|_{L_{p_{2}}({{\mathbb{R}}^{d}})}

\displaystyle\lesssim(t-k_{n}(u))^{-\frac{\rho}{2}}\|Q^{n}_{s,k_{n}(u)}f\|_{L_{p_{3}}({{\mathbb{R}}^{d}})}

\displaystyle\lesssim(t-k_{n}(u))^{-\frac{\rho}{2}}(k_{n}(u)-s)^{-\frac{\rho}{2}}\|f\|_{L_{p_{1}}({{\mathbb{R}}^{d}})}.

It is straightforward to see that
t-k_{n}(u)\geqslant(t-s)/2
and
k_{n}(u)-s\geqslant(t-s)/4
. Hence, from the above estimate, we deduce that
\|Q^{n}_{s,t}f\|_{L_{p_{2}}({{\mathbb{R}}^{d}})}\lesssim(t-s)^{-\rho}\|f\|_{L_{p_{1}}({{\mathbb{R}}^{d}})}
for any
t\geqslant s+4/n
. Combining with (
5.13
) from step 1., we see that (
5.15
) holds for any
p_{1},p_{2}\in[1,\infty]
satisfying
\frac{d}{2p_{1}}-\frac{d}{2p_{2}}<2
.
We iterate the argument. After
\lfloor\log_{2}(d/2)\rfloor+1
iterations, we see that (
5.15
) holds whenever
\rho\leqslant d/2
, which is trivially satisfied for any
p_{1},p_{2}\in[1,\infty]
. Hence, we have shown (
5.12
).
∎

Corollary 5.9
.

Let
g
be a function in
L_{1,p}({{\mathbb{R}}^{d}})
for some
p\in[1,\infty)

(5.20)

\displaystyle\|Q^{n}_{s,t}g-g\|_{L_{p}({{\mathbb{R}}^{d}})}\lesssim\|g\|_{L_{1,p}({{\mathbb{R}}^{d}})}(t-s)^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}.

Proof.

By approximation, we can assume
g
is continuously differentiable and has bounded derivatives.
From (
5.10
), we have
Q^{n}_{s,t}g-g=I_{1}+I_{2}
, where
I_{1}=T_{s,t}g-g
and
I_{2}=\int_{s}^{t}Q^{n}_{s,k_{n}(r)}[H^{n}_{r,t}g]dr
.
From (
5.4
), we have

\displaystyle I_{1}

\displaystyle=\int_{{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}}[p_{\Sigma_{s,t}(y)}(y-x)g(y)-p_{\Sigma_{s,t}(x)}(y-x)g(x)]dy

\displaystyle=\int_{{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}}[p_{\Sigma_{s,t}(y)}(y-x)-p_{\Sigma_{s,t}(x)}(y-x)]g(y)dy+\int_{{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}}[g(y)-g(x)]p_{\Sigma_{s,t}(x)}(y-x)dy

\displaystyle=I_{11}+I_{12}.

Using

A

1

, it is straightforward to verify that
\lambda^{-1}I\leqslant\Sigma_{s,t}(y)\Sigma_{s,t}(x)^{-1}\leqslant\lambda I
and
\|I-\Sigma_{s,t}(x)\Sigma_{s,t}(y)^{-1}\|\leqslant\lambda|x-y|^{\alpha}
for some finite constant

\displaystyle|I_{11}|\lesssim\int_{{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}}|x-y|^{\alpha}p_{N(t-s)}(x-y)|g(y)|dy.

\displaystyle|I_{12}|

\displaystyle\leqslant\int_{{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}}|x-y|(h(x)+h(y))p_{\Sigma_{s,t}(x)}(x-y)dy

5.5

5.3

\displaystyle\|I_{2}\|_{L_{p}({{\mathbb{R}}^{d}})}\lesssim\int_{s}^{t}\|H^{n}_{r,t}g\|_{L_{p}({{\mathbb{R}}^{d}})}dr\lesssim\int_{s}^{t}(t-r)^{\frac{\alpha}{2}-1}\|g\|_{L_{p}({{\mathbb{R}}^{d}})}dr\lesssim(t-s)^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}\|g\|_{L_{p}({{\mathbb{R}}^{d}})}.

Combining the estimates for

and
I_{2}

8.
Application: stochastic transport equations

Let
(W_{t})
be a standard
d
-dimensional Brownian motion on a filtered probability space
(\Omega,\mathcal{F},(\mathcal{F}_{t})_{t\in[0,1]},{\mathbb{P}})
and let
b:[0,1]\times{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\to{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}
be a Borel measurable function satisfying (
1.2
). In this section, we propose a numerical scheme for the following (forward) stochastic linear transport equation

(8.1)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}u+b\cdot\nabla u+\nabla u\circ dW_{t}=0,\quad u(0,x)=\rho(x),

where
\rho\in\cap_{r\geqslant 1}L_{1,r}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
and
\nabla u\circ dW_{t}
is interpreted in Stratonovich sense. As in
[
FF13
]
, we say
u
is a
weakly differentiable solution
to (
8.1
) if

•

u:\Omega\times[0,1]\times{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\rightarrow{\mathbb{R}}
is measurable,
\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}u(t,x)\psi(x)dx
is progressively measurable for each
\psi\in C_{0}^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
;

•

{\mathbb{P}}(u(t,\cdot)\in\cap_{r\geqslant 1}L_{1,r}^{loc}({\mathbb{R}}^{d}))=1
for
t\in[0,1]
and both
u
and
\nabla u
are in
C([0,1];\cap_{r\geqslant 1}L^{r}({\mathbb{R}}^{d}\times\Omega))
;

•

for any
\psi\in C_{0}^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
and
t\in[0,1]
with probability one the following holds

\displaystyle\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}u(t,x)\psi(x)dx+

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}b(s,x)\cdot\nabla u(s,x)\psi(x)dxds

\displaystyle=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\rho(x)\psi(x)dx+\sum_{i=1}^{d}\int_{0}^{t}\Big(\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}u(s,x)\partial_{x_{i}}\psi(x)dx\Big)dW_{s}^{i}

\displaystyle\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad+\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{t}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}u(s,x)\Delta\psi(x)dxds.

It is known from
[
FF13
, Theorems 10, 11]
that a weakly differentiable solution
u
to (
8.1
) exists uniquely and has the representation
u(\tau,x)=\rho(\phi_{0}^{\tau}(x))
, where
\phi_{0}^{\tau}(x)
is the inverse of the stochastic flow of homeomorphisms generated by the solution
(X_{\tau}(x))_{\tau\in[0,1]}
to the SDE

(8.2)

\displaystyle dX_{\tau}(x)=b(\tau,X_{\tau}(x))d\tau+dW_{\tau},\quad\tau\in[0,1],\quad X_{0}(x)=x.

For each fixed
\tau\in[0,1]
, consider the backward-in-time SDE

(8.3)

\displaystyle X_{\tau,s}(x)

\displaystyle=x-\int_{s}^{\tau}b(r,X_{\tau,r}(x))dr+W_{s}-W_{\tau},\quad 0\leqslant s\leqslant\tau,\quad X_{\tau,\tau}(x)=x.

The inverse flow
\phi^{\tau}_{0}(x)
is directly related to the solution to the previous SDE through the relation
\phi_{0}^{\tau}(x)=X_{\tau,0}(x)
. Indeed, when
(
b
)
is a smooth bounded function with bounded derivatives, this relation is classical, see
[
Kun19
, Theorem 3.7.1]
. When

belongs to

are stable under this approximation (see
[
FF13

and
[
GL23

respectively), the relation holds true in this case. Alternatively, another argument for this fact is recently provided in
[
ALL23
]
utilizing

of (
8.2
).

To devise a numerical scheme for (
8.1
), it convenes to introduce

X_{s}^{\tau}(x):=X_{\tau,\tau-s}(x)
. By a change of variables, we find that

(8.4)

\displaystyle X_{s}^{\tau}(x)=x-\int_{0}^{s}b^{\tau}(r,X^{\tau}_{r}(x))dr+W^{\tau}_{s},\quad 0\leqslant s\leqslant\tau,

where
b^{\tau}(r,x):=b(\tau-r,x)
and
W^{\tau}_{r}:=W_{\tau}-W_{\tau-r}
for
r\in[0,\tau]
. Observe that
(W_{t}^{\tau})_{t\in[0,\tau]}
is a
(\mathcal{F}_{t}^{\tau})
-Brownian motion with
\mathcal{F}_{t}^{\tau}:=\sigma(W_{\tau-r_{1}}-W_{\tau-r_{2}},0\leqslant r_{1}\leqslant r_{2}\leqslant t)
for

.
Hence, we have the representation

which implies (

∎

\displaystyle\|u\|_{{\mathbb{L}}_{p}^{np}([s,1])}\lesssim C_{\rho}\|u\|_{{\mathbb{L}}_{-1,p}^{np}([s,1])}+o_{\rho}\|u\|_{{\mathbb{L}}_{1,p}^{np}([s,1])},

we get

\displaystyle\|\nabla^{2}u\|_{{\mathbb{L}}_{-1,p}^{np}([s,1])}\lesssim\|f\|_{{\mathbb{L}}_{-1,p}^{np}([s,1])}+C_{\rho}\|u\|_{{\mathbb{L}}_{-1,p}^{np}([s,1])}+o_{\rho}\|u\|_{{\mathbb{L}}_{1,p}^{np}([s,1])}.

In view of (
.25
), we have

\displaystyle\|u\|_{{\mathbb{L}}_{1,p}^{np}([s,1])}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|f\|_{{\mathbb{L}}_{-1,p}^{np}([s,1])}+C_{\rho}\|u\|_{{\mathbb{L}}_{-1,p}^{np}([s,1])}+o_{\rho}\|u\|_{{\mathbb{L}}_{1,p}^{np}([s,1])}.

By choosing
\rho
sufficiently small, we derive derive (
.24
) from the above estimate.

Step 3.
We show that

(.28)

\displaystyle\|u\|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{\infty}_{-1,p}([0,1])}\lesssim\|f\|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{p}_{-1,p}([0,1])}.

From (
.21
), we have

\displaystyle u^{z}_{s}=\int_{s}^{1}P_{\Sigma_{s,t}(z)}F^{z}_{t}dt,\quad\text{where}\quad\Sigma_{s,t}(z)=2\int_{s}^{t}a(r,z)dr.

Then Minkowski inequality and
[
Tri13
, Theorem 5.30]
yield

\displaystyle\|u_{s}^{z}\|_{L_{-1,p}({{\mathbb{R}}^{d}})}\lesssim\int_{s}^{1}\|F^{z}_{t}\|_{L_{-1,p}({{\mathbb{R}}^{d}})}dt.

Applying Hölder inequality,
Lemma

.6
, (
.22
) and the interpolation inequality (
.27
), we obtain from the above that

\displaystyle\|u_{s}\|_{L_{-1,p}({{\mathbb{R}}^{d}})}^{p}

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{s}^{1}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\|F^{z}_{t}\|_{L_{-1,p}({{\mathbb{R}}^{d}})}^{p}dzdt

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{s}^{1}\left[\|f_{t}\|^{p}_{L_{-1,p}({{\mathbb{R}}^{d}})}+\|u_{t}\|^{p}_{L_{-1,p}({{\mathbb{R}}^{d}})}+\|u_{t}\|^{p}_{L_{1,p}({{\mathbb{R}}^{d}})}\right]dt.

Applying (
.24
) (with
n=1
), we have

\displaystyle\|u_{s}\|^{p}_{L_{-1,p}({{\mathbb{R}}^{d}})}\lesssim\|f\|^{p}_{{\mathbb{L}}^{p}_{1,p}([0,1])}+\int_{s}^{1}\|u_{t}\|_{L_{-1,p}({{\mathbb{R}}^{d}})}^{p}dt,

which implies (
.28
) by Grönwall inequality.

Step 4.

Using (
.28
) in (
.24
) yields

\displaystyle\|u\|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{np}_{1,p}([0,1])}\lesssim\|f\|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{np}_{1,p}([0,1])}+\|f\|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{p}_{1,p}([0,1])}\lesssim\|f\|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{np}_{1,p}([0,1])},

which implies (
.17
).
∎

Proof.

The proof is similar to that of

The main differences are the computations in step 1 of the proof of

Let

Define

\displaystyle\partial_{t}v^{z}-a^{ij}(z)\partial^{2}_{ij}v^{z}+G^{z}=0,\quad v^{z}(0,\cdot)=0,

\displaystyle G^{z}

\displaystyle=g^{z}+a^{ij}(z)\partial^{2}_{ij}v^{z}-\partial^{2}_{ij}(a^{ij}v)\phi^{z}

Applying

.3

\|\partial_{ij}((a^{ij}-a^{ij}(z))v^{z})\|_{L_{-1,p^{\prime}}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})}\lesssim\|(a^{ij}-a^{ij}(z))v^{z}\|_{L_{1,p^{\prime}}({{\mathbb{R}}^{d}})}\\
\lesssim\|(a^{ij}-a^{ij}(z))\psi^{z}\|_{L_{1,p_{0}}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})}\|v^{z}\|_{L_{1,p^{\prime}}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})}\leqslant o_{\rho}\|v^{z}\|_{L_{1,p^{\prime}}({{\mathbb{R}}^{d}})}

\displaystyle\|a^{ij}v\partial^{2}_{ij}\phi^{z}\|_{L_{-1,p^{\prime}}({{\mathbb{R}}^{d}})}\lesssim\|a^{ij}\psi^{z}\|_{L_{1,p_{0}}({{\mathbb{R}}^{d}})}\|v\partial^{2}_{ij}\phi^{z}\|_{L_{-1,p^{\prime}}({{\mathbb{R}}^{d}})}\lesssim\|v\partial^{2}_{ij}\phi^{z}\|_{L_{-1,p^{\prime}}({{\mathbb{R}}^{d}})}.

Similar to (

\displaystyle\left(\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\|v^{z}\|_{L_{1,p}({{\mathbb{R}}^{d}})}^{p^{\prime}}\right)^{1/p^{\prime}}\leqslant\|v\|_{L_{1,p^{\prime}}({{\mathbb{R}}^{d}})}+C_{\rho}\|v\|_{L_{p}^{\prime}({{\mathbb{R}}^{d}})}.

\left(\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\|\partial_{ij}((a^{ij}-a^{ij}(z))v^{z})+a^{ij}v\partial^{2}_{ij}\phi^{z}\|_{L_{-1,p^{\prime}}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})}^{p^{\prime}}dz\right)^{1/p^{\prime}}\\
\lesssim C_{\rho}\|v\|_{L_{p^{\prime}}({{\mathbb{R}}^{d}})}+o_{\rho}\|v\|_{L_{1,p^{\prime}}({{\mathbb{R}}^{d}})}.

Applying

\displaystyle\left(\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\|g^{z}+2\partial_{i}(a^{ij}v)\partial_{j}\phi^{z}\|_{L_{-1,p^{\prime}}({{\mathbb{R}}^{d}})}^{p^{\prime}}dz\right)^{{1/p^{\prime}}}

\displaystyle\left(\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\|G^{z}\|^{p^{\prime}}_{L_{-1,p^{\prime}}({{\mathbb{R}}^{d}})}dz\right)^{1/p^{\prime}}\lesssim\|g\|_{L_{-1,p^{\prime}}({{\mathbb{R}}^{d}})}+C_{\rho}\|v\|_{L_{p^{\prime}}({{\mathbb{R}}^{d}})}+o_{\rho}\|v\|_{L_{1,p^{\prime}}({{\mathbb{R}}^{d}})}.

Using the interpolation inequality

\displaystyle\|v\|_{L_{p^{\prime}}({{\mathbb{R}}^{d}})}\leqslant C_{\rho}\|v\|_{L_{-1,p^{\prime}}({{\mathbb{R}}^{d}})}+o_{\rho}\|v\|_{L_{1,p^{\prime}}({{\mathbb{R}}^{d}})},

Lemma .12
.

Let
q\in(2,\infty)
and
p\in(1,\infty)

for

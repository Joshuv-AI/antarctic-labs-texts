Abstract
. In this note, I will prove a conjecture in [ 1 ] , which is related to the invariants of a maximal parabolic subgroup of GL n + 1 \text{GL}_{n+1} . Consequently, the natural tensor invariants of this typical maximal parabolic subgroup of GL n + 1 \text{GL}_{n+1} are determined when n ≥ r n\geq r .



0. Introduction

The purpose of this note is to prove Conjecture (
0.1
) proposed in
[
1
]
, which is actually much related to tensor invariants of an distinguished maximal parabolic subgroup of
\text{GL}_{n+1}
. The authors in
[
1
]
defined an enhanced reductive algebraic group
\underline{G}
, which is naturally a semi-reductive group. In order to illustrate the tensor invariants of
\underline{\text{GL}_{n}}=\text{GL}_{n}\ltimes\mathbb{C}^{n}
in
\text{End}_{\mathbb{C}}(\underline{V}^{\otimes r})
with
\underline{V}=\mathbb{C}^{n+1}
, they introduced a finite-dimensional degenerate double Hecke algebra
D(n,r)
, and got a Levi Schur-Weyl duality for
\underline{V}=\mathbb{C}^{n+1}
in
[
1
]
(see
[
1
, Theorem 4.3]
) which is an analog of classical Schur-Weyl duality.

By
[
1
, Example 5.2]
,
\mathbb{C}\Psi(\mathfrak{S}_{r})\subsetneq\text{End}_{\underline{\text{GL}_{n}}\rtimes\textbf{G}_{m}}(\underline{V}^{\otimes r})
when
n<r
. However in the case
n\geq r
, there is a conjecture

\text{End}_{\underline{\text{GL}_{n}}\rtimes\textbf{G}_{m}}(\underline{V}^{\otimes r})=\mathbb{C}\Psi(\mathfrak{S}_{r})

(0.1)

(see
[
1
, Conjecture 5.4]
). In §
2
of this article, I will give a proof of (
0.1
). Then it is a position to
investigate the enhanced tensor invariants, and this is listed in §
3
.

Note that
\underline{\text{GL}_{n}}\rtimes\textbf{G}_{m}
is a canonical maximal parabolic subgroup of
\text{GL}_{n+1}
. It is worth mentioning that the study of invariants beyond reductive groups is a challenge (see
[
3
]
-
[
5
]

\underline{G}\rtimes\textbf{G}_{m}
has its own interest.

1.
Levi Schur-Weyl duality.

1.1.
A general setting-up.

In this note, all vector spaces are over a complex number field of
\mathbb{C}
. Let
G
be a connected reductive algebraic group over
\mathbb{C}
, and
(V,\rho)

\mathbb{C}

\underline{G}=G\times_{\rho}V
as follows (see

Regard
\underline{G}
as a set, we have
\underline{G}=G\times V
; For any

,

\displaystyle(g_{1},v_{1})\cdot(g_{2},v_{2}):=(g_{1}g_{2},\rho(g_{1})(v_{2})+v_{1}).

(1.1)

From now on, we will write down
e^{v}

(where

\textsf{{dim}}V=n

In particular, assume that

becomes a
\underline{G}
-module that is defined for any

,

and

\displaystyle\underline{g}\cdot\underline{u}:=\rho(g)(u)+av+a\eta.

(1.2)

It is easy to see that this module is a rational module of
\underline{G}
.

In particular, if
G

. Since
\text{GL}(V)
can naturally act on

\underline{G}

\underline{G}

Since

\text{GL}_{n}

\displaystyle g\mapsto\text{diag}(g,1):=\left(\begin{matrix}g&0\\

(1.3)

and

\displaystyle c\mapsto\text{diag}(1,\cdots,1,c):=\left(\begin{matrix}I_{n}\\

where

1.2.
Classical Schur-Weyl duality.

We can define a representation
(\underline{V}^{\otimes r},\Phi)
of
\text{GL}(\underline{V})
on
\underline{V}^{\otimes r}

\Phi(g)(v_{1}\otimes v_{2}\otimes\cdots\otimes v_{r})=g(v_{1})\otimes g(v_{2})\otimes\cdots\otimes g(v_{r}).

(1.5)

for any
g\in\text{GL}(\underline{V})
and any monomial tensor product
v_{1}\otimes v_{2}\otimes\cdots\otimes v_{r}\in\underline{V}^{\otimes r}
.

In the meanwhile,
(\underline{V}^{\otimes r},\Psi)
naturally becomes a representation of
\mathfrak{S}_{r}
with following permutation action

\Psi(\sigma)(v_{1}\otimes v_{2}\otimes\cdots\otimes v_{r})=v_{\sigma^{-1}(1)}\otimes v_{\sigma^{-1}(2)}\otimes\cdots\otimes v_{\sigma^{-1}(r)}.

(1.6)

for any
\sigma\in\mathfrak{S}_{r}
and any monomial tensor product
v_{1}\otimes v_{2}\otimes\cdots\otimes v_{r}\in\underline{V}^{\otimes r}
.

The classical Schur-Weyl duality implies that the images of
\Phi
and
\Psi
are double centralizers in
\text{End}_{\mathbb{C}}(\underline{V}^{\otimes r})
, namely

\text{End}_{\text{GL}(\underline{V})}(V^{\otimes r})=\mathbb{C}\Psi(\mathfrak{S}_{r}),\text{End}_{\mathfrak{S}_{r}}(\underline{V}^{\otimes r})=\mathbb{C}\Phi(\mathfrak{\text{GL}}(\underline{V})).

(1.7)

Here
\mathbb{C}\Psi(\mathfrak{S}_{r})
and
\mathbb{C}\Phi(\mathfrak{\text{GL}}(\underline{V}))
are the subalgebras of
\text{End}_{\mathbb{C}}(\underline{V}^{\otimes r})
that generated by
\Psi(\mathfrak{S}_{r})
and
\Phi(\mathfrak{\text{GL}}(\underline{V}))
respectively.

1.3.
Degenerate double Hecke algebras.

From now on, we always assume that
G=\text{GL}(V)\cong\text{GL}_{n}
.

For given positive integers
r
and
l
with
r>l
, we define the
l

\mathcal{H}^{l}_{r}
of
\mathfrak{S}_{r}

\{{\rm{\textbf{x}}}_{\sigma}\mid\sigma\in\mathfrak{S}_{l}\}
and
\{\rm{\textbf{s}}_{\textit{i}}\mid\textit{i}=1,2,\cdots,\textit{r}-1\}

\displaystyle\rm{\textbf{s}}_{\textit{i}}^{2}=1,\rm{\textbf{s}}_{\textit{i}}\rm{\textbf{s}}_{\textit{j}}=\rm{\textbf{s}}_{\textit{j}}\rm{\textbf{s}}_{\textit{i}}\text{ for }0<\textit{i}\neq\textit{j}\leq\textit{r}-1,|\textit{j}-\textit{i}|>1;

(1.8)

\displaystyle\rm{\textbf{s}}_{\textit{i}}\rm{\textbf{s}}_{\textit{j}}\rm{\textbf{s}}_{\textit{i}}=\rm{\textbf{s}}_{\textit{j}}\rm{\textbf{s}}_{\textit{i}}\rm{\textbf{s}}_{\textit{j}}\text{ for }0<\textit{i}\neq\textit{j}\leq\textit{r}-1,|\textit{j}-\textit{i}|=1;

(1.9)

\displaystyle{\rm{\textbf{x}}}_{\sigma}{\rm{\textbf{x}}}_{\mu}={\rm{\textbf{x}}}_{\sigma\circ\mu}\text{ for }\sigma,\mu\in\mathfrak{S}_{l};

(1.10)

\displaystyle\rm{\textbf{s}}_{\textit{i}}{\rm{\textbf{x}}}_{\sigma}={\rm{\textbf{x}}}_{\rm{\textbf{s}}_{\textit{i}}\circ\sigma},{\rm{\textbf{x}}}_{\sigma}\rm{\textbf{s}}_{\textit{i}}={\rm{\textbf{x}}}_{\sigma\circ\rm{\textbf{s}}_{\textit{i}}}\text{ for }\sigma\in\mathfrak{S}_{\textit{{l}}},\textit{i}<\textit{l};

(1.11)

\displaystyle\rm{\textbf{s}}_{\textit{i}}{\rm{\textbf{x}}}_{\sigma}={\rm{\textbf{x}}}_{\sigma}={\rm{\textbf{x}}}_{\sigma}\rm{\textbf{s}}_{\textit{i}}\text{ for }\sigma\in\mathfrak{S}_{\textit{{l}}},\textit{i}>\textit{l}.

(1.12)

This is an infinite-dimensional associative algebra.

The degenerate double Hecke algebra

of

(

\displaystyle{\rm{\textbf{x}}}_{\delta}^{(l)}{\rm{\textbf{x}}}_{\gamma}^{(k)}=0\text{ for }\delta\in\mathfrak{S}_{l},\gamma\in\mathfrak{S}_{k}\text{ with }l\neq k.

(1.13)

For any

2.
Tensor invariant of enhanced reductive group
\textbf{G}_{m}\ltimes\underline{\text{GL}(V)}

Keep the notations as in Section

G=\text{GL}(V)\cong\text{GL}_{n}

\displaystyle\sigma^{[I]}|_{\underline{V}_{J}^{\otimes r}}:=\left\{\begin{aligned} \sigma|_{\underline{V}_{I}^{\otimes r}},\text{ }I=J;\\

By definition,
\sigma^{[I]}(\underline{V}_{l}^{\otimes r})\subseteq\underline{V}_{l}^{\otimes r}

\varepsilon_{J,I}

.

D(n,r)_{l}=\bigoplus\limits_{I\subseteq\underline{r},~\sharp I=l}D_{[I]}

Proof.

Since
n\geq r

[

\displaystyle\text{If }i\in J,\text{ set }b_{i}:=\eta;\text{ if }i\notin J,\text{ set }b_{i}:=v_{i}.

Let

Lemma 2.3
.

Suppose

. For
\delta\in\mathbb{C}\Psi(\mathfrak{S}_{r})

\phi\neq J\subseteq\underline{r}
, if

Proof.

We assume that
\sharp J=r-k

A_{w}^{J}=v_{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes v_{k}\otimes(v_{k+1}+\cdots+v_{r}+\eta)^{\otimes(r-k)}-v_{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes v_{k}\otimes\eta^{\otimes(r-k)}
. Set

and
G_{2}\cong\mathfrak{S}_{r-k}

\bar{s}:=\{s(x)\mid x\in\underline{r}\backslash\underline{k}\text{ and }1\leq s(x)\leq k\}
. It is clear that
\underline{s}=(\underline{r}\backslash\underline{k})\backslash\{{s(x)\mid x\in\underline{r}\backslash\underline{k}\text{ and }s(x)\in\underline{r}\backslash\underline{k}}\}
,
\bar{s}=\underline{k}\backslash\{s(x)\mid x\in\underline{k}\text{ and }s(x)\in\underline{k}\}
and
\sharp\underline{s}=\sharp\bar{s}
. For

and
A\subseteq\underline{r}\backslash\underline{k}=\{k+1,\cdots,r\}

\sharp A=\sharp B

\Psi_{A,B}:=\sum\limits_{s\in\mathfrak{S}_{r}\atop\underline{s}=A,~\bar{s}=B}\mathbb{C}\Psi(s)
; if
\sharp A\neq\sharp B
, there does not exist
s\in\mathfrak{S}_{r}
satisfying
\underline{s}=A
and
\bar{s}=B

\Psi_{A,B}:=0
. Obviously,
\mathbb{C}\Psi(\mathfrak{S}_{r})=\sum\limits_{A\subseteq\underline{r}}\sum\limits_{B\subseteq\underline{r}}\Psi_{A,B}=\sum\limits_{A\subseteq\underline{r}\backslash\underline{k},~B\subseteq\underline{k}\atop\sharp A=\sharp B}\Psi_{A,B}
.

Clearly, for
s\in\mathfrak{S}_{r}
,
\underline{s}=\phi\Leftrightarrow\bar{s}=\phi\Leftrightarrow s\in G_{1}G_{2}
. Given
A\subseteq\underline{r}\backslash\underline{k}
and

(
\Rightarrow A\neq\phi,B\neq\phi

and
B=\{j_{1},\cdots j_{q}\}
, now we set
\theta=(i_{1},j_{1})\cdots(i_{q},j_{q})
. For all
\varepsilon\in\mathfrak{S}_{r}
with
\underline{\varepsilon}=A
and
\bar{\varepsilon}=B
, we have
\theta\cdot\varepsilon\in G_{1}G_{2}=\langle G_{1},G_{2}\rangle
.

Hence fix
A\subseteq\underline{r}\backslash\underline{k}
and
B\subseteq\underline{k}
with
\sharp A=\sharp B
,
\forall\varepsilon\in\mathfrak{S}_{r}
, we have
\theta\cdot\varepsilon\in G_{1}G_{2}
(Here
\theta:=1=\text{id}
, if
A=B=\phi
). Namely,

\displaystyle\varepsilon=\theta^{-1}\cdot\varepsilon^{\prime}=\theta\cdot\varepsilon^{\prime},\text{ where }\varepsilon^{\prime}=\theta\varepsilon\in G_{1}G_{2},\text{ and note that }\theta=\theta^{-1}\in\mathfrak{S}_{r}\backslash(G_{1}G_{2}).

(2.2)

The terms in
A_{w}^{J}
containing
v_{1},\cdots,v_{r}

\Psi(s)(v_{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes v_{r})
, where
s

G_{2}
. Hence
\delta(A_{w}^{J})=0

\delta(\sum_{s\in G_{2}}\Psi(s)(v_{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes v_{r}))=0
. Thanks to
\mathbb{C}\Psi(\mathfrak{S}_{r})=\sum\limits_{A\subseteq\underline{r}}\sum\limits_{B\subseteq\underline{r}}\Psi_{A,B}
and (
2.2
), there exist
m\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
,
\phi_{1},\cdots,\phi_{m}\in\mathfrak{S}_{r}\backslash(G_{1}G_{2})
and
T_{0},T_{1},\cdots,T_{m}\in\mathbb{C}\Psi(G_{1}G_{2})
, such that
\delta=T_{0}+\sum_{i=1}^{m}\Psi(\phi_{i})\circ T_{i}=\sum_{i=0}^{m}\Psi(\phi_{i})\circ T_{i}
(Here

), and satisfying

\displaystyle\Psi(\phi_{i})\circ T_{i}\in\Psi_{A_{i},B_{i}}\text{ and }\Psi(\phi_{i})\in\Psi_{A_{i},B_{i}}\text{, with }0\leq i\leq m,A_{i}\subseteq\underline{r}\backslash\underline{k},B_{i}\subseteq\underline{k},\sharp A_{i}=\sharp B_{i}\neq 0.

(2.3)

as well as
A_{i}\neq A_{j}
or
B_{i}\neq B_{j}
,
\forall 0\leq i\neq j\leq m
, i.e.

\displaystyle(A_{i},B_{i})\neq(A_{j},B_{j})\text{ for all }0\leq i\neq j\leq m.

(2.4)

By (

\displaystyle\Psi(\phi_{i})\circ T_{i}\circ(\sum_{s\in G_{2}}\Psi(s))\in\Psi_{A_{i},B_{i}}.

(2.5)

We have
(T_{0}\circ(\sum_{s\in G_{2}}\Psi(s)))(v_{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes v_{r})+(\Psi(\phi_{1})\circ T_{1}\circ(\sum_{s\in G_{2}}\Psi(s)))(v_{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes v_{r})+\cdots+(\Psi(\phi_{m})\circ T_{m}\circ(\sum_{s\in G_{2}}\Psi(s)))(v_{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes v_{r})=0
. Due to (
2.4

\displaystyle(\Psi(\phi_{i})\circ T_{i}\circ(\sum_{s\in G_{2}}\Psi(s)))(v_{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes v_{r})=0,\text{ }\forall i=0,1,\cdots,m.

Thus we have

\displaystyle(\Psi(\phi_{i}^{-1})\circ\Psi(\phi_{i})\circ T_{i}\circ(\sum_{s\in G_{2}}\Psi(s)))(v_{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes v_{r})=0,\text{ }\forall i=0,1,\cdots,m.

Namely

\displaystyle(T_{i}\circ(\sum_{s\in G_{2}}\Psi(s)))(v_{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes v_{r})=0,\text{ }\forall i=0,1,\cdots,m.

(2.6)

Since
T_{i}\in\mathbb{C}\Psi(G_{1}G_{2})
and

, there exist

,

\displaystyle T_{i}\circ(\sum_{s\in G_{2}}\Psi(s))=(\sum_{j=1}^{l}k_{j}\Psi(g_{1}^{(j)})\circ\Psi(g_{2}^{(j)}))\circ(\sum_{s\in G_{2}}\Psi(s))=\sum_{j=1}^{l}\sum_{s\in G_{2}}k_{j}\Psi(g_{1}^{(j)})\circ\Psi(g_{2}^{(j)})\circ\Psi(s)

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{l}k_{j}\Psi(g_{1}^{(j)})(\sum_{s\in G_{2}}\Psi(g_{2}^{(j)}\cdot s))=(\sum_{j=1}^{l}k_{j}\Psi(g_{1}^{(j)}))(\sum_{s\in G_{2}}\Psi(s)).

So
(\sum_{j=1}^{l}k_{j}\Psi(g_{1}^{(j)}))\circ(\sum_{s\in G_{2}}\Psi(s))(v_{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes v_{r})=0
. Thanks to
G_{1}\cap G_{2}=\{1\}
, we can conclude that

\displaystyle(\sum_{j=1}^{l}k_{j}\Psi(g_{1}^{(j)}))(v_{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes v_{r})=0.

(2.7)

Since
\sum_{j=1}^{l}k_{j}\Psi(g_{1}^{(j)})\in\mathbb{C}\Psi(G_{1})
, there exists
h_{i}\in G_{1},c_{i}\in\mathbb{C}
with

for all

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{l}k_{j}\Psi(g_{1}^{(j)})=0.

(2.8)

Note that

\displaystyle T_{i}|_{\underline{V}_{\underline{k}}^{\otimes r}}=\sum_{j=1}^{l}(k_{j}\Psi(g_{1}^{(j)})\circ\Psi(g_{2}^{(j)}))|_{\underline{V}_{\underline{k}}^{\otimes r}}=\sum_{j=1}^{l}k_{j}\Psi(g_{1}^{(j)})|_{\underline{V}_{\underline{k}}^{\otimes r}}{\mathrel{\to}^{\text{By }(\ref{0})}}0.

(

Clearly, Schur-Weyl duality implies that

\forall\sigma\in D(n,r)^{V}

2.2

\tau_{I}^{[I]}

2.1

If
\sharp I=r

with

(

2.1

. Recall that

\displaystyle(\sigma\circ\Phi(e^{w}))(v_{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes v_{k}\otimes\eta^{\otimes(r-k)})=\sigma(v_{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes v_{k}\otimes(w+\eta)^{\otimes(r-k)})

\displaystyle=(\sum\limits_{I\subseteq\underline{r},~\sharp I\leq k}\tau_{I}^{[I]}+\sum\limits_{I\subseteq\underline{r},~\sharp I>k}\tau^{[I]})(v_{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes v_{k}\otimes(w+\eta)^{\otimes(r-k)})

\displaystyle=(\tau_{\underline{k}}^{[\underline{k}]}+\sum\limits_{I\subseteq\underline{r},~\sharp I>k}\tau^{[I]})(v_{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes v_{k}\otimes(w+\eta)^{\otimes(r-k)})

\displaystyle=\tau_{\underline{k}}^{[\underline{k}]}(v_{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes v_{k}\otimes\eta^{\otimes(r-k)})+\tau(A_{w}^{\underline{r}\backslash\underline{k}})

\displaystyle=\tau_{\underline{k}}(v_{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes v_{k}\otimes\eta^{\otimes(r-k)})+\tau(A_{w}^{\underline{r}\backslash\underline{k}}).

(2.9)

\displaystyle(\Phi(e^{w})\circ\sigma)(v_{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes v_{k}\otimes\eta^{\otimes(r-k)})=\Phi(e^{w})(\tau_{\underline{k}}^{[\underline{k}]}(v_{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes v_{k}\otimes\eta^{\otimes(r-k)}))

\tau^{[I]}=\tau_{I}^{[I]}
. By induction, we have

for all

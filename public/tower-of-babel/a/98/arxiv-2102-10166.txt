Mixed generalized fractional Brownian motion
Shaykhah Alajmi 1 and Ezzedine Mliki 1,2

Abstract
. To extend several known centered Gaussian processes, we introduce a new centred mixed self-similar Gaussian process called the mixed generalized fractional Brownian motion, which could serve as a good model for a larger class of natural phenomena. This process generalizes both the well-known mixed fractional Brownian motion introduced by Cheridito [ 10 ] and the generalized fractional Brownian motion introduced by Zili [ 31 ] . We study its main stochastic properties, its non-Markovian and non-stationarity characteristics and the conditions under which it is not a semimartingale. We prove the long range dependence properties of this process.

1.
Introduction

Fractional Brownian motion on the whole real line (fBm for short)
B^{H}=\{B_{t}^{H},\,t\in\mathbb{R}\}
of Hurst parameter
H
is the best known centered Gaussian process with long-range dependence. Its covariance function is

(1.1)

\displaystyle\mbox{Cov}(B_{t}^{H},\,B_{s}^{H})=\frac{1}{2}[|t|^{2H}+|s|^{2H}-|t-s|^{2H}]

where
H
is a real number in
(0,1)
and the case
H=\frac{1}{2}
corresponds to the Brownian motion. It is the unique continuous Gaussian process starting from zero, the self-similarity and stationarity of the increments are two main properties for which fBm enjoyed successes as modeling tool in finance and telecommunications. Researchers have applied fractional Brownian motion to a wide range of problems, such as bacterial colonies, geophysical data, electrochemical deposition, particle diffusion, DNA sequences and stock market indicators
[
2
]
and
[
23
]
. In particular, computer science applications of fBm include modeling network traffic and generating
graphical landscapes
[
22
]
and
[
26
]
. The fBm was investigated in many papers (e.g.
[
3
,
4
,
14
,
17
,
19
]
). The main difference between fBm and regular Brownian motion is that the increments in Brownian motion are independent, increments for fBm are not.

In
[
6
]
, the authors suggested another kind of extension of the Brownian motion, called the sub-fractional
Brownian motion (sfBm for short), which preserves most properties of the fBm, but not the stationarity of the increments. It is a centered Gaussian process
\xi^{H}=\left\{\xi_{t}^{H},\ t\geq 0\right\},
defined by:

(1.2)

\displaystyle\xi_{t}^{H}=\frac{B_{t}^{H}+B_{-t}^{H}}{\sqrt{2}},\quad t\geq 0,

where
H\in(0,1)
. The case
H=\frac{1}{2}
corresponds to the Brownian motion.

The sfBm is intermediate between Brownian motion and fractional Brownian motion
in the sense that it has properties analogous to those of fBm, self-similarity, not Markovian but the increments
on nonoverlapping intervals are more weakly correlated, and their covariance decays
polynomially at a higher rate in comparison with fBm (for this reason in
[
6
]
is called sfBm). So the sfBm does not generalize the fBm.
The sfBm was investigated in many papers (e.g.
[
5
,
6
,
25
,
28
]
).

An extension of the sfBm was introduced by Zili in
[
30
]
as
a linear combination of a finite number of independent sub-fractional Brownian motions.
It was called the mixed sub-fractional Brownian motion (msfBm for short). The msfBm
is a centered mixed self-similar Gaussian process and does not have stationary increments.
The msfBm do not generalize the fBm.

In
[
31
]
Zili introduced new model called the generalized fractional Brownian motion (gfBm for short) which is an extension of both sub-fractional Brownian motion and fractional Brownian motion. A gfBm with parameters
a,b,\ and\ H
, is a process
Z^{H}=\left\{Z_{t}^{H}(a,b),t\geq 0\right\}

defined by

(1.3)

\displaystyle Z_{t}^{H}(a,b)=aB_{t}^{H}+bB_{-t}^{H},\quad t\geq 0

The gfBm was investigated in
[
32
]
and
[
13
]
. The gfBm generalize the sfBm but not the the mixed fractional Brownian motion.

The mixed fractional Brownian motion (mfBm for short) is a linear combination between a Brownian motion and an independent fractional Brownian motion of Hurst parameter
H
. It was introduced by Cheridito
[
10
]
to present a stochastic model of
the discounted stock price in some arbitrage-free and complete financial markets. The mfBm is a centered Gaussian process starting from
zero with covariance function

(1.4)

\displaystyle\mbox{Cov}(N_{t}^{H}(a,b),N_{s}^{H}(a,b))=a^{2}(t\wedge s)+\frac{b^{2}}{2}\left(t^{2H}+s^{2H}-|t-s|^{2H}\right),

with
H\in(0,1).
When
a=1
and
b=0,
the mfBm is the Brownian motion and when
a=0
and
b=1,
is the fBm.
We refer also to
[
11
,
10
,
29
,
27
]
for further information on this process.

In this paper, we introduce a new stochastic model, which we call the mixed generalized fractional Brownian motion.

Definition 1.1
.

A mixed generalized fractional Brownian motion (mgfBm for short) of parameters
a,b,c
and
H\in(0,\,1)
is a centred
Gaussian process
M^{H}(a,b,c)=\{M_{t}^{H}(a,b,c),\,t\geq 0\},
defined on a probability space
(\Omega,\mathcal{F},\mathbb{P}),
with the covariance function

(1.5)

\displaystyle C(t,s)=a^{2}(t\wedge s)+\frac{(b+c)^{2}}{2}(t^{2H}+s^{2H})-bc(t+s)^{2H}-\frac{(b^{2}+c^{2})}{2}|t-s|^{2H}

where
t\wedge s=\frac{1}{2}\left(t+s-|t-s|\right)
.

The mgfBm is completely different from all the extensions mentioned above. The process
M^{H}(a,b,c)
is motivated by the fact that this process already introduced for specific values of
a
,
b
and
c
. Indeed
M^{H}(a,b,0)
is the mixed fractional Brownian motion and
M^{H}(0,b,c),
is the generalized fractional Brownian motion. This why we will name
M^{H}(a,b,c)
the mixed generalized fractional Brownian motion. It allows to deal with a larger class of modeled natural phenomena, including those
with stationary or non-stationary increments.

Our goal is to study the main stochastic properties of this new model, paying attention to the long-range dependence, self-similarity,
increment stationary, Markovity and semi-martingale properties.

2.
The main properties

Existence of the mixed generalized fractional Brownian motion
M^{H}(a,b,c)
for any
H\in(0,1)
can be shown in the following way, consider the process

(2.1)

\displaystyle M_{t}^{H}(a,b,c)=aB_{t}+bB_{t}^{H}+cB_{-t}^{H},\quad t\geq 0,

where
B=\{B_{t},\,t\in\mathbb{R}\}
is a Brownian motion and
B^{H}=\{B_{t}^{H},\,t\in\mathbb{R}\}
is an independent fractional Brownian
motion with Hurst parameter
H\in(0,1).

Using (
1.1
) and since
B
and
B^{H}
are independent we obtain the following lemma.

Lemma 2.1
.

For all
s,\ t\geq 0
, the process (
2.1
) is a centered Gaussian process with covariance function given by (
1.5
).

Proof.

Let
s,\ t\geq 0
and
C(t,s)=Cov\left(M_{t}^{H}(a,b,c),M_{s}^{H}(a,b,c)\right).
Then

\displaystyle C(t,s)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle Cov\left[\left(aB_{t}+bB_{t}^{H}+cB_{-t}^{H}\right),\left(aB_{s}+bB_{s}^{H}+cB_{-s}^{H}\right)\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle a^{2}(t\wedge s)+b^{2}Cov(B_{t}^{H},B_{s}^{H})+bc[Cov(B_{t}^{H},B_{-s}^{H})]+cb[Cov(B_{-t}^{H},B_{s}^{H})]

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle c^{2}Cov(B_{-t}^{H},B_{-s}^{H})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle a^{2}(t\wedge s)+\frac{b^{2}}{2}\left(t^{2H}+s^{2H}-|t-s|^{2H}\right)+\frac{bc}{2}\left(t^{2H}+s^{2H}-|t+s|^{2H}\right)

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\frac{cb}{2}\left(t^{2H}+s^{2H}-|-(t+s)|^{2H}\right)+\frac{c^{2}}{2}\left(t^{2H}+s^{2H}-|-(t-s)|^{2H}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle a^{2}(t\wedge s)+\frac{b^{2}}{2}t^{2H}+\frac{b^{2}}{2}s^{2H}-\frac{b^{2}}{2}|t-s|^{2H}+\frac{bc}{2}t^{2H}+\frac{bc}{2}s^{2H}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle a^{2}(t\wedge s)+\frac{(b+c)^{2}}{2}(t^{2H}+s^{2H})-bc|t+s|^{2H}-\frac{(b^{2}+c^{2})}{2}|t-s|^{2H}.

Hence the covariance function of the process (
2.1
) is precisely
C(t,s)
given by (
1.5
).
Therefore the
M^{H}(a,b,c)
exists.
∎

Remarks 2.1
.

Some special cases of the mixed generalized fractional Brownian motion:

(1)

If
a=0,b=1,c=0,
then
M^{H}(0,1,0)
is a fBm.

(2)

If
a=0,b=c=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}},
then
M^{H}(0,\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}},\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}})

is a sfBm.

(3)

If
a=1,b=0,c=0,
then
M^{H}(1,0,0)
is a Bm.

(4)

If
a=0,
then
M^{H}(0,b,c),
is a gfBm.

(5)

If
c=0,
then
M^{H}(a,b,0),
is a mfBm.

(6)

If
b=c,
then
M^{H}(a,\frac{b}{\sqrt{2}},\frac{b}{\sqrt{2}}),
is the smfBm.

So the mixed generalized fractional Brownian motion is, at the same, a generalization of the fractional Brownian motion, sub-fractional Brownian motion, the sub-mixed fractional Brownian motion, generalized fractional Brownian motion, mixed fractional Brownian motion and of course of the standard Brownian motion.

Proposition 2.1
.

The mgfBm satisfies the following properties:

(1)

For all
t\geq 0
,

\displaystyle E\left(M_{t}^{H}(a,b,c)\right)^{2}=a^{2}t+\left(b^{2}+c^{2}-(2^{2H}-2)bc\right)t^{2H}.

(2)

Let
0\leq s<t
. Then

\displaystyle E\left(M_{t}^{H}(a,b,c)-M_{s}^{H}(a,b,c)\right)^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle a^{2}|t-s|-2^{2H}bc(t^{2H}+s^{2H})

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle(b^{2}+c^{2})|t-s|^{2H}+2bc|t+s|^{2H}.

(3)

We have for all
0\leq s<t
,

\displaystyle a^{2}(t-s)+\gamma_{(b,c,H)}(t-s)^{2H}\leq E\left(M_{t}^{H}(a,b,c)-M_{s}^{H}(a,b,c)\right)^{2}\leq a^{2}(t-s)+\nu_{(b,c,H)}(t-s)^{2H}

where

\displaystyle\gamma_{(b,c,H)}=(b^{2}+c^{2}-2bc(2^{2H-1}-1))\,\mathbf{1}_{\mathcal{C}}(b,c,H)+(b^{2}+c^{2})\,\mathbf{1}_{\mathcal{D}}(b,c,H),

\displaystyle\nu_{(b,c,H)}=(b^{2}+c^{2})\,\mathbf{1}_{\mathcal{C}}(a,b,H)+(b^{2}+c^{2}-2bc(2^{2H-1}-1))\,\mathbf{1}_{\mathcal{D}}(b,c,H),

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}=\{(b,c,H)\in\mathbb{R}^{2}\times]0,1[;\;(H>\frac{1}{2},\;bc\geq 0)\;or\;(H<\frac{1}{2},\;bc\leq 0)\},

and

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}=\{(b,c,H)\in\mathbb{R}^{2}\times]0,1[;\;(H>\frac{1}{2},\;bc\leq 0)\;or\;(H<\frac{1}{2},\;bc\geq 0)\}.

Proof.

(1)

It is a direct consequence of (
1.5
).

(2)

Let
0\leq s<t
and
\alpha(t,s)=E\left(M_{t}^{H}(a,b,c)-M_{s}^{H}(a,b,c))^{2}\right)
. Then

\displaystyle\alpha(t,s)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle E\left(M_{t}^{H}(a,b,c)\right)^{2}\ +E\left(M_{s}^{H}(a,b,c)\right)^{2}-2E\left(M_{t}^{H}(a,b,c)M_{s}^{H}(a,b,c)\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle a^{2}t+b^{2}t^{2H}+2bct^{2H}-2^{2H}bct^{2H}+c^{2}t^{2H}+a^{2}s+b^{2}s^{2H}+2bcs^{2H}-2^{2H}bcs^{2H}

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle c^{2}s^{2H}-2a^{2}(t\wedge s)-b^{2}t^{2H}-b^{2}s^{2H}+b^{2}|t-s|^{2H}-bct^{2H}-bcs^{2H}+bc|t+s|^{2H}

\displaystyle-

\displaystyle cbt^{2H}-cbs^{2H}+cb|t+s|^{2H}-c^{2}t^{2H}-c^{2}s^{2H}+c^{2}|t-s|^{2H}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle a^{2}(t+s)-2^{2H}bc(t^{2H}+s^{2H})-2a^{2}(t\wedge s)+(b^{2}+c^{2})|t-s|^{2H}+2bc|t+s|^{2H}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle a^{2}|t-s|-2^{2H}bc(t^{2H}+s^{2H})+(b^{2}+c^{2})|t-s|^{2H}+2bc|t+s|^{2H}.

(3)

It is a direct consequence of the second item of Proposition
2.1
and Lemma 3 in
[
31
]
.

∎

Proposition 2.2
.

For all
(a,b,c)\in\mathbb{R}^{3}\setminus\{(0,0,0)\}
and
H\in(0,1)\setminus\{\frac{1}{2}\}
the mgfBm is not a self-similar process.

Proof.

This follows from the fact that, for fixed
h>0
, the processes
\left\{M_{ht}^{H}(a,b,c),\ t\geq 0\right\}

and
\left\{h^{H}M_{t}^{H}(a,b,c),\ t\geq 0\right\}
are Gaussian, centered, but don’t have the same covariance function. Indeed

\displaystyle C\left(ht,hs\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle a^{2}(ht\wedge hs)+\frac{b^{2}}{2}\left((ht)^{2H}+(hs)^{2H}-|ht-hs|^{2H}\right)

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\frac{bc}{2}\left((ht)^{2H}+(hs)^{2H}-|ht+hs|^{2H}\right)

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\frac{cb}{2}\left((ht)^{2H}+(hs)^{2H}-|-(ht+hs)|^{2H}\right)

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\frac{c^{2}}{2}\left((ht)^{2H}+(hs)^{2H}-|-(ht-hs)|^{2H}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle a^{2}(ht\wedge hs)+\frac{b^{2}}{2}(ht)^{2H}+\frac{b^{2}}{2}(hs)^{2H}-\frac{b^{2}}{2}|ht-hs|^{2H}

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\frac{bc}{2}(ht)^{2H}+\frac{bc}{2}(hs)^{2H}-\frac{bc}{2}|ht+hs|^{2H}+\frac{bc}{2}(ht)^{2H}+\frac{bc}{2}(hs)^{2H}

\displaystyle-

\displaystyle\frac{bc}{2}|ht+hs|^{2H}+\frac{c^{2}}{2}(ht)^{2H}+\frac{c^{2}}{2}(hs)^{2H}-\frac{c^{2}}{2}|ht-hs|^{2H}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle a^{2}h(t\wedge s)+h^{2H}\frac{(b+c)^{2}}{2}\left((t)^{2H}+(s)^{2H}\right)-bch^{2H}|t+s|^{2H}-h^{2H}\frac{(b^{2}+c^{2})}{2}|t-s|^{2H}.

On the other hand,

\displaystyle Cov\left(h^{H}M_{t}^{H}(a,b,c),h^{H}M_{s}^{H}(a,b,c)\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle h^{2H}Cov\left(M_{t}^{H}(a,b,c),M_{s}^{H}(a,b,c)\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle a^{2}h^{2H}(t\wedge s)+h^{2H}\frac{(b+c)^{2}}{2}\left((t)^{2H}+(s)^{2H}\right)-bch^{2H}|t+s|^{2H}

\displaystyle-

\displaystyle h^{2H}\frac{(b^{2}+c^{2})}{2}|t-s|^{2H}.

Then the mgfBm is not a self-similar process for all
(a,b,c)\in\mathbb{R}^{3}\setminus\{(0,0,0)\}.

∎

Remarks 2.2
.

As a consequence of Proposition
2.2
, we see that:

(1)

M^{H}(0,b,c)
is a self-similar process for all
(b,c)\in\mathbb{R}^{2}.

(2)

Now, we will study the Markovian property.

Theorem 2.1
.

Assume
H\in(0,1)\setminus\left\{{\frac{1}{2}}\right\},a\in\mathbb{R}\ and\ (b,c)\in\mathbb{R}^{2}\setminus\left\{(0,0)\right\}.

Then
M^{H}(a,b,c)
is not a Markovian process.

Proof.

The process
M^{H}(a,b,c)
is a centered Gaussian.
Then, if
M_{t}^{H}(a,b,c)
is a Markovian process, according to Revuz and Yor
[
24
]
, for all
s<t<u
, we would have

\displaystyle C(s,u)C(t,t)=C(s,t)C(t,u).

We will only prove the theorem in the case where
a\neq 0
, the result with
a=0
is known in
[
31
]
. For the proof we follow the proof of Proposition 1 given in
[
31
]
.

Using Proposition
2.1
we get

\displaystyle C(s,u)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle a^{2}s+\frac{(b+c)^{2}}{2}(u^{2H}+s^{2H})-bc|u+s|^{2H}-\frac{(b^{2}+c^{2})}{2}|u-s|^{2H},

\displaystyle C(t,t)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle a^{2}t+\left(b^{2}+c^{2}-(2^{2H}-2)bc\right)t^{2H},

\displaystyle C(s,t)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle a^{2}s+\frac{(b+c)^{2}}{2}(t^{2H}+s^{2H})-bc|t+s|^{2H}-\frac{(b^{2}+c^{2})}{2}|t-s|^{2H},

\displaystyle C(t,u)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle a^{2}t+\frac{(b+c)^{2}}{2}(u^{2H}+t^{2H})-bc|u+t|^{2H}-\frac{(b^{2}+c^{2})}{2}|u-t|^{2H}.

In the particular case where
1<s=\sqrt{t}<t<u=t^{2}
, we have

\displaystyle C(\sqrt{t},t^{2})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle a^{2}t^{\frac{1}{2}}+\frac{(b+c)^{2}}{2}(t^{4H}+t^{H})-bc|t^{2}+t^{\frac{1}{2}}|^{2H}-\frac{(b^{2}+c^{2})}{2}|t^{2}-t^{\frac{1}{2}}|^{2H},

\displaystyle C(t,t)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle a^{2}t+\left(b^{2}+c^{2}-(2^{2H}-2)bc\right)t^{2H},

\displaystyle C(\sqrt{t},t)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle a^{2}t^{\frac{1}{2}}+\frac{(b+c)^{2}}{2}(t^{2H}+t^{H})-bc|t+t^{\frac{1}{2}}|^{2H}-\frac{(b^{2}+c^{2})}{2}|t-t^{\frac{1}{2}}|^{2H},

\displaystyle C({t},t^{2})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle a^{2}t+\frac{(b+c)^{2}}{2}(t^{4H}+t^{2H})-bc|t^{2}+t|^{2H}-\frac{(b^{2}+c^{2})}{2}|t^{2}-t^{\frac{1}{2}}|^{2H}.

Then by using that,

\displaystyle C(\sqrt{t},t^{2})C(t,t)=C(\sqrt{t},t)C(t,t^{2}),

we have

\displaystyle\left[a^{2}t^{\frac{1}{2}}+\frac{(b+c)^{2}}{2}(t^{4H}+t^{H})-bc|t^{2}+t^{\frac{1}{2}}|^{2H}-\frac{(b^{2}+c^{2})}{2}|t^{2}-t^{\frac{1}{2}}|^{2H}\right]

\displaystyle\times

\displaystyle\left[a^{2}t+\left(b^{2}+c^{2}-(2^{2H}-2)bc\right)t^{2H}\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left[a^{2}t^{\frac{1}{2}}+\frac{(b+c)^{2}}{2}(t^{2H}+t^{H})-bc|t+t^{\frac{1}{2}}|^{2H}-\frac{(b^{2}+c^{2})}{2}|t-t^{\frac{1}{2}}|^{2H}\right]

\displaystyle\times

\displaystyle\left[a^{2}t+\frac{(b+c)^{2}}{2}(t^{4H}+t^{2H})-bc|t^{2}+t|^{2H}-\frac{(b^{2}+c^{2})}{2}|t^{2}-t^{\frac{1}{2}}|^{2H}\right].

It follows that

\displaystyle\left[a^{2}t^{\frac{1}{2}}+t^{4H}\left(\frac{(b+c)^{2}}{2}(1+t^{-3H})-bc|1+t^{-\frac{3}{2}}|^{2H}-\frac{(b^{2}+c^{2})}{2}|1-t^{-\frac{3}{2}}|^{2H}\right)\right]

\displaystyle\times

\displaystyle\left[a^{2}t+\left(b^{2}+c^{2}-(2^{2H}-2)bc\right)t^{2H}\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left[a^{2}t^{\frac{1}{2}}+t^{2H}\left(\frac{(b+c)^{2}}{2}(1+t^{-H})-bc|1+t^{-\frac{1}{2}}|^{2H}-\frac{(b^{2}+c^{2})}{2}|1-t^{-\frac{1}{2}}|^{2H}\right)\right]

\displaystyle\times

\displaystyle\left[a^{2}t+t^{4H}\left(\frac{(b+c)^{2}}{2}(1+t^{-2H})-bc|1+t^{-1}|^{2H}-\frac{(b^{2}+c^{2})}{2}|1-t^{-1}|^{2H}\right)\right].

Hence

\displaystyle a^{4}t^{\frac{3}{2}}

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle a^{2}\left(\frac{(b+c)^{2}}{2}(1+t^{-3H})-bc|1+t^{-\frac{3}{2}}|^{2H}-\frac{(b^{2}+c^{2})}{2}|1-t^{-\frac{3}{2}}|^{2H}\right)t^{4H+1}

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\left(\frac{(b+c)^{2}}{2}(1+t^{-3H})-bc|1+t^{-\frac{3}{2}}|^{2H}-\frac{(b^{2}+c^{2})}{2}|1-t^{-\frac{3}{2}}|^{2H}\right)

\displaystyle\times

\displaystyle\left(b^{2}+c^{2}-(2^{2H}-2)bc\right)t^{6H}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle a^{4}t^{\frac{3}{2}}

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle a^{2}\left(\frac{(b+c)^{2}}{2}(1+t^{-2H})-bc|1+t^{-1}|^{2H}-\frac{(b^{2}+c^{2})}{2}|1-t^{-1}|^{2H}\right)t^{4H+\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle a^{2}\left(\frac{(b+c)^{2}}{2}(1+t^{-H})-bc|1+t^{-\frac{3}{2}}|^{2H}-\frac{(b^{2}+c^{2})}{2}|1-t^{-\frac{3}{2}}|^{2H}\right)t^{2H+1}

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\left(\frac{(b+c)^{2}}{2}(1+t^{-H})-bc|1+t^{-\frac{3}{2}}|^{2H}-\frac{(b^{2}+c^{2})}{2}|1-t^{-\frac{3}{2}}|^{2H}\right)

\displaystyle\times

\displaystyle\left(\frac{(b+c)^{2}}{2}(1+t^{-2H})-bc|1+t^{-1}|^{2H}-\frac{(b^{2}+c^{2})}{2}|1-t^{-1}|^{2H}\right)t^{6H}

Take

\displaystyle a^{2}\left(b^{2}+c^{2}-(2^{2H}-2)bc\right)t^{-4H+\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle a^{2}\left(\frac{(b+c)^{2}}{2}(1+t^{-3H})-bc|1+t^{-\frac{3}{2}}|^{2H}-\frac{(b^{2}+c^{2})}{2}|1-t^{-\frac{3}{2}}|^{2H}\right)t^{-2H+1}

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\left(\frac{(b+c)^{2}}{2}(1+t^{-3H})-bc|1+t^{-\frac{3}{2}}|^{2H}-\frac{(b^{2}+c^{2})}{2}|1-t^{-\frac{3}{2}}|^{2H}\right)

\displaystyle\times

\displaystyle\left(b^{2}+c^{2}-(2^{2H}-2)bc\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle a^{2}\left(\frac{(b+c)^{2}}{2}(1+t^{-2H})-bc|1+t^{-1}|^{2H}-\frac{(b^{2}+c^{2})}{2}|1-t^{-1}|^{2H}\right)t^{-2H+\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle a^{2}\left(\frac{(b+c)^{2}}{2}(1+t^{-H})-bc|1+t^{-\frac{1}{2}}|^{2H}-\frac{(b^{2}+c^{2})}{2}|1-t^{-\frac{1}{2}}|^{2H}\right)t^{-4H+1}

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\left(\frac{(b+c)^{2}}{2}(1+t^{-H})-bc|1+t^{-\frac{1}{2}}|^{2H}-\frac{(b^{2}+c^{2})}{2}|1-t^{-\frac{1}{2}}|^{2H}\right)

\displaystyle\times

\displaystyle\left(\frac{(b+c)^{2}}{2}(1+t^{-2H})-bc|1+t^{-1}|^{2H}-\frac{(b^{2}+c^{2})}{2}|1-t^{-1}|^{2H}\right).

Therefore

\displaystyle a^{2}\left(b^{2}+c^{2}-(2^{2H}-2)bc\right)t^{-4H+\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle a^{2}[\frac{1}{2}(b+c)^{2}\left(1+t^{-3H}\right)-bc\left(1+2Ht^{-\frac{3}{2}}+H(2H-1)t^{-3}+\circ(t^{-3})\right)

\displaystyle-

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}(b^{2}+c^{2})\left(1-2Ht^{-\frac{3}{2}}+H(2H-1)t^{-3}+\circ(t^{-3})\right)]t^{-2H+1}

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle[\frac{1}{2}(b+c)^{2}\left(1+t^{-3H}\right)-bc\left(1+2Ht^{-\frac{3}{2}}+H(2H-1)t^{-3}+\circ(t^{-3})\right)

\displaystyle-

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}(b^{2}+c^{2})\left(1-2Ht^{-\frac{3}{2}}+H(2H-1)t^{-3}+\circ(t^{-3})\right)]

\displaystyle\times

\displaystyle[b^{2}+c^{2}-(2^{2H}-2)bc]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle a^{2}[\frac{1}{2}(b+c)^{2}\left(1+t^{-2H}\right)-bc\left(1+2Ht^{-1}+H(2H-1)t^{-2}+\circ(t^{-2})\right)

\displaystyle-

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}(b^{2}+c^{2})\left(1-2Ht^{-1}+H(2H-1)t^{-2}+\circ(t^{-2})\right)]t^{-2H+\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle a^{2}[\frac{1}{2}(b+c)^{2}\left(1+t^{-H}\right)-bc\left(1+2Ht^{-1/2}+H(2H-1)t^{-1}+\circ(t^{-1})\right)

\displaystyle-

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}(b^{2}+c^{2})\left(1-2Ht^{-\frac{1}{2}}+H(2H-1)t^{-1}+\circ(t^{-1})\right)]t^{-4H+1}

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle[\frac{1}{2}(b+c)^{2}\left(1+t^{-H}\right)-bc\left(1+2Ht^{-\frac{1}{2}}+H(2H-1)t^{-1}+\circ(t^{-1})\right)

\displaystyle-

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}(b^{2}+c^{2})\left(1-2Ht^{-\frac{1}{2}}+H(2H-1)t^{-1}+\circ(t^{-1})\right)]

\displaystyle\times

\displaystyle[\frac{1}{2}(b+c)^{2}\left(1+t^{-2H}\right)-bc\left(1+2Ht^{-1}+H(2H-1)t^{-2}+\circ(t^{-2})\right)

\displaystyle-

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}(b^{2}+c^{2})\left(1-2Ht^{-1}+H(2H-1)t^{-2}+\circ(t^{-2})\right)].

First case:
0<H<\frac{1}{2}
,
a\neq 0
and
b+c\neq 0
. By Taylor’s expansion we get, as
t\rightarrow\infty
,

\displaystyle a^{2}\left(b^{2}+c^{2}-(2^{2H}-2)bc\right)t^{-4H+\frac{1}{2}}+a^{2}\frac{1}{2}(b+c)^{2}t^{-5H+1}

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{2}(b+c)^{2}[b^{2}+c^{2}-(2^{2H}-2)bc]t^{-3H}

\displaystyle\approx a^{2}\frac{1}{2}(b+c)^{2}t^{-4H+\frac{1}{2}}+a^{2}\frac{1}{2}(b+c)^{2}t^{-5H+1}+\frac{1}{4}(b+c)^{4}t^{-3H}.

Therefore

\displaystyle a^{2}\left(b^{2}+c^{2}-(2^{2H}-2)bc\right)t^{-4H+\frac{1}{2}}+\frac{1}{2}(b+c)^{2}[b^{2}+c^{2}-(2^{2H}-2)bc]t^{-3H}

\displaystyle\approx a^{2}\frac{1}{2}(b+c)^{2}t^{-4H+\frac{1}{2}}+\frac{1}{4}(b+c)^{4}t^{-3H},

which is true if and only if

\displaystyle\frac{(b-c)^{2}}{2}-(2^{2H}-2)bc=0\quad\mbox{and}\quad a^{2}\frac{(b-c)^{2}}{2}-a^{2}(2^{2H}-2)bc=0.

However, it is easy to check that
\frac{(b-c)^{2}}{2}-(2^{2H}-2)bc>0
and
a^{2}\frac{(b-c)^{2}}{2}-a^{2}(2^{2H}-2)bc>0
for fixed
c,b
and every real
a
.

Second case:
0<H<\frac{1}{2}
,
a\neq 0
and
b+c=0
. By Taylor’s expansion we get, as
t\rightarrow\infty
,

\displaystyle a^{2}[b^{2}+c^{2}-(2^{2H}-2)bc]t^{-4H+\frac{1}{2}}+a^{2}\left[-2Hbc+(b^{2}+c^{2})H\right]t^{-2H-\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle+\left[-2Hbc+(b^{2}+c^{2})H\right]\times\left[b^{2}+c^{2}-(2^{2H}-2)bc\right]t^{-\frac{3}{2}}

\displaystyle\approx a^{2}\left[-2Hbc+(b^{2}+c^{2})H\right]t^{-2H-\frac{1}{2}}+a^{2}\left[-2Hbc+(b^{2}+c^{2})H\right]t^{-4H+\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle+\left[-2Hbc+(b^{2}+c^{2})H\right]^{2}t^{-\frac{3}{2}}.

Hence

\displaystyle a^{2}[b^{2}+c^{2}-(2^{2H}-2)bc]t^{-4H+\frac{1}{2}}+\left[-2Hbc+(b^{2}+c^{2})H\right]\times\left[b^{2}+c^{2}-(2^{2H}-2)bc\right]t^{-\frac{3}{2}}

\displaystyle\approx a^{2}\left[-2Hbc+(b^{2}+c^{2})H\right]t^{-4H+\frac{1}{2}}+\left[-2Hbc+(b^{2}+c^{2})H\right]^{2}t^{-\frac{3}{2}},

which is true if and only if
b=c=0
. This is a contradiction.

Third case:
\frac{1}{2}<H<1
,
a\neq 0
and
b-c\neq 0
. By Taylor’s expansion we get, as
t\rightarrow\infty
,

\displaystyle a^{2}[b^{2}+c^{2}-(2^{2H}-2)bc]t^{-4H+\frac{1}{2}}+a^{2}H(b-c)^{2}t^{-2H-\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle+H(b-c)^{2}[b^{2}+c^{2}-(2^{2H}-2)bc]t^{-\frac{3}{2}}

\displaystyle\approx a^{2}H(b-c)^{2}t^{-2H-\frac{1}{2}}+a^{2}H(b-c)^{2}t^{-4H+\frac{1}{2}}+H^{2}(b-c)^{4}t^{-\frac{3}{2}}.

Then

\displaystyle a^{2}[b^{2}+c^{2}-(2^{2H}-2)bc]t^{-4H+\frac{1}{2}}+H(b-c)^{2}[b^{2}+c^{2}-(2^{2H}-2)bc]t^{-\frac{3}{2}}

\displaystyle\approx a^{2}H(b-c)^{2}t^{-4H+\frac{1}{2}}+H^{2}(b-c)^{4}t^{-\frac{3}{2}},

which is true if and only if

\displaystyle\left[b^{2}(1-H)+c^{2}(1-H)+(2-2^{2H}+2H)bc\right]=0.

However, it is easy to check that
b^{2}(1-H)+c^{2}(1-H)+(2-2^{2H}+2H)bc>0
for fixed
c,b
and every real
a
.

Fourth case:
\frac{1}{2}<H<1
,
a\neq 0
and
b-c=0
. By Taylor’s expansion we get, as
t\rightarrow\infty
,

\displaystyle a^{2}\left(b^{2}+c^{2}-(2^{2H}-2)bc\right)t^{-4H+\frac{1}{2}}+\frac{1}{2}(b+c)^{2}[b^{2}+c^{2}-(2^{2H}-2)bc]t^{-3H}

\displaystyle\approx a^{2}\frac{1}{2}(b+c)^{2}t^{-4H+\frac{1}{2}}+\frac{1}{4}(b+c)^{4}t^{-3H},

which is true if and only if
2-2^{2H}=0.
This contradicts the fact that
H\neq\frac{1}{2}
.
∎

Let us check the mixed-self-semilarity property of the mgfBm. This property was introduced in
[
29
]
for the mfBm and investigated to show the Hölder continuity of the mfBm. See also
[
12
]
for the sfBm case.

Proposition 2.3
.

For any
h>0
,
\left\{M^{H}_{ht}(a,b,c)\right\}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\bigtriangleup}}{{=}}\left\{M^{H}_{t}(ah^{\frac{1}{2}},bh^{H},ch^{H})\right\}.

where
\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\bigtriangleup}}{{=}}
”to have the same law”.

Proof.

For fixed
h>0
, the processes
\{M_{ht}^{H}(a,b,c)\}
and
\{M_{t}^{H}(ah^{\frac{1}{2}},bh^{H},ch^{H})\}
are Gaussian and centered. Therefore, one only have to prove that they have the same covariance function.
But, for any
s
and
t
in
\mathbb{R}_{+}
, since
B
and
B^{H}
are independent, then

\displaystyle Cov\left(M_{ht}^{H}(a,b,c),M_{hs}^{H}(a,b,c)\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle a^{2}h(t\wedge s)+\frac{(b^{2}+c^{2})}{2}\left[h^{2H}\left(t^{2H}+s^{2H}-|t-s|^{2H}\right)\right]

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle bc\left[h^{2H}\left(t^{2H}+s^{2H}-|t+s|^{2H}\right)\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle Cov\left(M_{t}^{H}(ah^{\frac{1}{2}},bh^{H},ch^{H}),M_{s}^{H}(ah^{\frac{1}{2}},bh^{H},ch^{H})\right).

∎

Proposition 2.4
.

For all
(a,b,c)\in\mathbb{R}^{3}\setminus\{(0,0,0)\},
the increments of the
M^{H}(a,b,c)
are not stationary.

Proof.

Let
(a,b,c)\in\mathbb{R}^{3}\setminus\{(0,0,0)\}.

For a fixed
t\geq 0
consider the processes
\{P_{t},\,t\geq 0\}
define by
P_{t}=M_{t+s}^{H}(a,b,c)-M_{s}^{H}(a,b,c).

Using Proposition
2.1
we get

\displaystyle Cov(P_{t},P_{t})]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle E\left[(M_{t+s}^{H}(a,b,c)-M_{s}^{H}(a,b,c))^{2}\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle a^{2}(t+s+s)-2^{2H}bc(({t+s})^{2H}+s^{2H})-2a^{2}s

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle(b^{2}+c^{2})|t+s-s|^{2H}+2bc|t+s+s|^{2H}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle a^{2}(t+2s)-2^{2H}bc(({t+s})^{2H}+s^{2H})-2a^{2}s+(b^{2}+c^{2})t^{2H}+2bc|t+2s|^{2H}.

Using Proposition
2.1
we get

\displaystyle Cov(M_{t}^{H}(a,b,c),M_{t}^{H}(a,b,c))=a^{2}t+\left(b^{2}+c^{2}-(2^{2H}-2)bc\right)t^{2H}.

Since both processes are centered Gaussian, the inequality of covariance functions implies that
P_{t}
does not have the same distribution as
M_{t}^{H}(a,b,c)
. Thus, the incremental behavior of
M^{H}(a,b,c)
at any point in the future is not the same. Hence the increments of
M^{H}(a,b,c)
are not stationary.
∎

3.
Long range dependence of the mgfBm

Definition 3.1
.

We say that the increments of a stochastic process
X
are long-range dependent if for every integer
p\geq 1
, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{n\geq 1}R_{X}(p,p+n)=\infty,

where
R_{X}(p,p+n)=E\left((X_{p+1}-X_{p})(X_{p+n+1}-X_{p+n})\right).

This property was investigated in many papers (e.g.
[
2
,
8
,
9
,
5
,
14
]
).

Theorem 3.1
.

For every
a\in\mathbb{R}
and
(b,c)\in\mathbb{R}^{2}\setminus\left\{(0,0)\right\}
the increments of
M^{H}(a,b,c)
are long-range dependent if and only if
H>\frac{1}{2}
and
b\neq c
.

Proof.

For all
n\in\mathbb{N}\setminus\{0\}
and
p\geq 1
we have

\displaystyle R_{M}(p,p+n)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle E\left((M_{p+1}^{H}(a,b,c)-M_{p}^{H}(a,b,c))(M_{p+n+1}^{H}(a,b,c)-M_{p+n}^{H}(a,b,c))\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle E\left(M_{p+1}^{H}(a,b,c)M_{p+n+1}^{H}(a,b,c)\right)-E\left(M_{p+1}^{H}(a,b,c)M_{p+n}^{H}(a,b,c)\right)

\displaystyle-

\displaystyle E\left(M_{p}^{H}(a,b,c)M_{p+n+1}^{H}(a,b,c)\right)+E\left(M_{p}^{H}(a,b,c)M_{p+n}^{H}(a,b,c)\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle C({p+1},{p+n+1})-C({p+1},{p+n})-C({p},{p+n+1})+C({p},{p+n})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle a^{2}(p+1)+\frac{(b+c)^{2}}{2}\left((p+1)^{2H}+(p+n+1)^{2H}\right)-bc(2p+n+2)^{2H}

\displaystyle-

\displaystyle\frac{(b^{2}+c^{2})}{2}n^{2H}-a^{2}(p+1)-\frac{(b+c)^{2}}{2}\left((p+1)^{2H}+(p+n)^{2H}\right)

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle bc(2p+n+1)^{2H}+\frac{(b^{2}+c^{2})}{2}|n-1|^{2H}-a^{2}p

\displaystyle-

\displaystyle\frac{(b+c)^{2}}{2}\left((p)^{2H}+(p+n+1)^{2H}\right)+bc(2p+n+1)^{2H}+\frac{(b^{2}+c^{2})}{2}|n+1|^{2H}.

Hence

\displaystyle R_{M}(p,p+n)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{(b^{2}+c^{2})}{2}\left((n+1)^{2H}-2n^{H}+(n-1)^{2H}\right)

\displaystyle-

\displaystyle bc\left((2p+n+2)^{2H}-2(2p+n+1)^{2H}+(2p+n)^{2H}\right).

Then for every integer
p\geq 1
, by Taylor’s expansion, as
n\rightarrow\infty
, we have

\displaystyle R_{M}(p,p+n)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{b^{2}+c^{2}}{2}n^{2H}\left[\left(1+\frac{1}{n}\right)^{2H}-2+\left(1-\frac{1}{n}\right)^{2H}\right]

\displaystyle-

\displaystyle bcn^{2H}\left[\left(1+\frac{2p+2}{n}\right)^{2H}-2\left(1+\frac{2p+1}{n}\right)+\left(1+\frac{2p}{n}\right)^{2H}\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle H(2H-1)n^{2H-2}(b-c)^{2}

\displaystyle-

\displaystyle 4H(2H-1)(H-1)bc(2p+1)n^{2H-3}(1+\circ(1)).

If
b\neq c
, we see that as
n\rightarrow\infty
,

\displaystyle R_{M}(p,p+n)\approx H(2H-1)n^{2H-2}(b-c)^{2}.

Then

\displaystyle\sum_{n\geq 1}R_{M}(p,p+n)=\infty\ \ \Leftrightarrow\ 2H-2>-1\ \ \Leftrightarrow H>\frac{1}{2}.

If
b=c
, then, as
n\rightarrow\infty
,

\displaystyle R_{M}(p,p+n)\approx 4H(2H-1)(H-1)a^{2}(2p+1)n^{2H-3}.

For every
H\in(0,1)
, we have
2H-3<-1
and, consequently,

\displaystyle\sum_{n\geq 1}R_{M}(p,p+n)<\infty.

∎

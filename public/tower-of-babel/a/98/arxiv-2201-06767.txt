Timing-residual power spectrum of a polarized stochastic gravitational-wave background in pulsar-timing-array observation
Guo-Chin Liu 1 and Kin-Wang Ng 2,3

Abstract
We study the observation of stochastic gravitational-wave background (SGWB) made by pulsar-timing arrays in the spherical harmonic space. Instead of using the Shapiro time delay, we keep the Sachs-Wolfe line-of-sight integral for the timing residual of an observed pulsar. We derive the power spectrum of the timing residual, from which the overlap reduction functions and the bipolar spherical harmonics coefficients are constructed for the SGWB intensity and polarization anisotropies. We have reproduced the previous results, noting that we have developed a fast algorithm for computing accurate overlap reduction functions and the bipolar spherical harmonics coefficients for the linear-polarization anisotropy are worked out for the first time. Our harmonic-space method is useful for future pulsar-timing-array observation on a few thousand pulsars and provides optimal estimators for testing the statistical isotropy of the SGWB.

Polarized SGWB

In the Minkowskian spacetime

\langle h_{A}(f,\hat{k})h^{*}_{A^{\prime}}(f^{\prime},\hat{k}^{\prime})\rangle=\delta(f-f^{\prime})\delta(\hat{k}-\hat{k}^{\prime})P_{AA^{\prime}}(f,\hat{k})\,,

(2)

where the spatial translational invariance dictates the delta function of their 3-momenta,

remain to be direction dependent.

For GWs coming from the sky direction

with wave vector

\displaystyle\mathbf{e}^{+}(\hat{k})

\displaystyle=\hat{\mathbf{e}}_{\theta}\otimes\hat{\mathbf{e}}_{\theta}-\hat{\mathbf{e}}_{\phi}\otimes\hat{\mathbf{e}}_{\phi}\,,

\displaystyle\mathbf{e}^{\times}(\hat{k})

\displaystyle=\hat{\mathbf{e}}_{\theta}\otimes\hat{\mathbf{e}}_{\phi}+\hat{\mathbf{e}}_{\phi}\otimes\hat{\mathbf{e}}_{\theta}\,,

in which

,

, and

Also, we can define the complex circular polarization basis tensors as

\displaystyle\mathbf{e}_{R}

\displaystyle=\frac{(\mathbf{e}_{+}+i\mathbf{e}_{\times})}{\sqrt{2}}\,,

\displaystyle\mathbf{e}_{L}

\displaystyle=\frac{(\mathbf{e}_{+}-i\mathbf{e}_{\times})}{\sqrt{2}}\,,

(4)

where
\mathbf{e}_{R}
stands for the right-handed GW with a positive helicity while

stands for the left-handed GW with a negative helicity. The corresponding amplitudes in Eq. (
1
) in the two different bases are related to each other via

\displaystyle h_{R}

\displaystyle=\frac{(h_{+}-ih_{\times})}{\sqrt{2}}\,,

\displaystyle h_{L}

\displaystyle=\frac{(h_{+}+ih_{\times})}{\sqrt{2}}\,.

(5)

Analogous to the case in electromagnetic waves

, the coherency matrix
P_{AA^{\prime}}
in Eq. (
2
) is related to the Stokes parameters,

,
Q
,

, and

as

\displaystyle I

\displaystyle=\left[\langle h_{R}h_{R}^{*}\rangle+\langle h_{L}h_{L}^{*}\rangle\right]/2\,,

\displaystyle Q+iU

\displaystyle=\langle h_{L}h_{R}^{*}\rangle\,,

\displaystyle Q-iU

\displaystyle=\langle h_{R}h_{L}^{*}\rangle\,,

\displaystyle V

\displaystyle=\left[\langle h_{R}h_{R}^{*}\rangle-\langle h_{L}h_{L}^{*}\rangle\right]/2\,,

which are functions of the frequency

III
Pulsar timing

In the pulsar-timing observation, radio pulses from an array of roughly 100 Galactic millisecond pulsars are being monitored with ground-based radio telescopes. The redshift fluctuation of a pulsar in the pointing direction

\hat{e}
on the sky is given by the Sachs-Wolfe effect

which is of order

The quantity that is actually observed in the pulsar-timing observation is the timing residual counted as

Using the laboratory time

where the detector tensor is

d^{ij}=\hat{e}^{i}\hat{e}^{j}\,.

IX
Conclusion

We have studied the pulsar-timing-array observation of the Stokes parameters of an anisotropic stochastic gravitational-wave background, based on the spherical harmonic expansion of the pulsar timing residual.
A numerical scheme to compute the overlap reduction functions (ORFs) and
the bipolar spherical harmonics (BiPoSH) coefficients has been developed.
We have used the Sachs-Wolfe line-of-sight integral for the time residual of a Galactic millisecond pulsar, which properly accounts for the power contribution of the pulsar term to the ORF multipoles at small angular separation of the pulsar pair. Using a spherical harmonic analysis, we can compute an ORF multipole with a desired angular resolution.
Our method also gives ORFs for a Galactic pulsar pair at different distances from the Earth. Indeed, the Sachs-Wolfe line-of-sight integral can be generalized to considering extragalactic pulsars at high redshifts.
In future pulsar-timing-array observation on a large number of pulsars,
our method provides a fast algorithm for computing accurate ORF multipoles
and BiPoSH coefficients.

Spin-Weighted Spherical Harmonics

The explicit form of the spin-weighted spherical harmonics that we use is

\displaystyle{}_{s}Y_{\ell m}(\theta,\phi)=(-1)^{m}e^{im\phi}\sqrt{\frac{(2\ell+1)}{(4\pi)}\frac{(\ell+m)!(\ell-m)!}{(\ell+s)!(\ell-s)!}}\sin^{2\ell}\!\left(\frac{\theta}{2}\right)\sum_{r}\binom{\ell-s}{r}\binom{\ell+s}{r+s-m}(-1)^{\ell-r-s}\cot^{2r+s-m}\!\left(\frac{\theta}{2}\right)\,.

(60)

When
s=0
, it reduces to the ordinary spherical harmonics,

Y_{\ell m}(\hat{n})=\sqrt{\frac{(2\ell+1)}{(4\pi)}\frac{(\ell-m)!}{(\ell+m)!}}P^{m}_{\ell}(\cos\theta)e^{im\phi}\,.

(61)

Spin-weighted spherical harmonics satisfy the orthogonal relation,

\int_{S^{2}}\mathbf{d}{\hat{n}}\;{}_{s}Y^{*}_{\ell m}(\hat{n}){}_{s}Y_{\ell^{\prime}m^{\prime}}(\hat{n})=\delta_{\ell\ell^{\prime}}\delta_{mm^{\prime}}\,,

(62)

and the completeness relation,

\displaystyle\sum_{\ell m}{}_{s}Y^{*}_{\ell m}(\hat{n}){}_{s}Y_{\ell m}(\hat{n}^{\prime})

\displaystyle=\delta(\hat{n}-\hat{n}^{\prime})

\displaystyle=\delta(\phi-\phi^{\prime})\delta(\cos\theta-\cos\theta^{\prime})\,.

(63)

Its complex conjugate is

{}_{s}Y^{*}_{\ell m}(\hat{n})=(-1)^{s+m}{}_{-s}Y_{\ell-m}(\hat{n})\,,

(64)

and its parity is given by

{}_{s}Y_{\ell m}(-\hat{n})\equiv{}_{s}Y_{\ell m}(\pi-\theta,\phi+\pi)=(-1)^{\ell}{}_{-s}Y_{\ell m}(\hat{n})\,.

(65)

Also, we have the spherical wave expansion:

e^{i\vec{k}\cdot\vec{r}}=4\pi\sum_{l=0}^{\infty}\sum_{m=-\ell}^{\ell}i^{\ell}j_{\ell}(kr)Y_{\ell m}^{*}(\hat{k})Y_{\ell m}(\hat{r})\,,

(66)

where
j_{\ell}(x)
is the spherical Bessel function.

We can calculate the integral of a product of three spin-weighted spherical harmonics using the formula:

\int_{S^{2}}\mathbf{d}\hat{e}\;{}_{s_{1}}Y_{l_{1}m_{1}}(\hat{e})\;{}_{s_{2}}\!Y_{l_{2}m_{2}}(\hat{e})\;{}_{s_{3}}\!Y_{l_{3}m_{3}}(\hat{e})=\sqrt{\frac{(2l_{1}+1)(2l_{2}+1)(2l_{3}+1)}{4\pi}}\begin{pmatrix}l_{1}&&l_{2}&&l_{3}\\
-s_{1}&&-s_{2}&&-s_{3}\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}l_{1}&&l_{2}&&l_{3}\\
m_{1}&&m_{2}&&m_{3}\end{pmatrix}\,,

(67)

which involves two Wigner-3j symbols representing the coupling coefficients between different spherical harmonics
book:Varshalovich
. The Wigner-3j symbol is zero unless it satisfies:
l_{1}
,
l_{2}
, and
l_{3}
have to meet the triangular condition, i.e.
l_{1}+l_{2}\geq l_{3}\geq|l_{1}-l_{2}|
, while

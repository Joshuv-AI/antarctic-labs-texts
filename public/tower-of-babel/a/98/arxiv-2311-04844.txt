On well-posedness and maximal regularity for parabolic Cauchy problems on weighted tent spaces
Pascal Auscher, Hedong Hou

Abstract
. We prove well-posedness in weighted tent spaces of weak solutions to the Cauchy problem ∂ t u − div A ∇ u = f , u ( 0 ) = 0 \partial_{t}u-\Div A\nabla u=f,u(0)=0 , where the source f f also lies in (different) weighted tent spaces, provided the complex coefficient matrix A A is bounded, measurable, time-independent, and uniformly elliptic. To achieve this, we extend the theory of singular integral operators on tent spaces via off-diagonal estimates introduced by [ 5 ] to obtain estimates on solutions u u , and also ∇ u \nabla u , ∂ t u \partial_{t}u , and div A ∇ u \Div A\nabla u in weighted tent spaces, showing at the same time maximal regularity. Uniqueness follows from a different strategy using interior representation for weak solutions and boundary behavior.

1.
Introduction

The main goal of this work is to study some parabolic equations in weighted tent spaces. We prove the following well-posedness and maximal regularity result.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
A\in L^{\infty}({\mathds{R}}^{n};\mat_{n}({\mathds{C}}))
be uniformly elliptic.
Let
\beta>-1/2
and
p_{L}(\beta)<p\leq\infty
. For any
f\in T^{p}_{\beta}
, there is a unique global weak solution
u\in T^{p}_{\beta+1}
to the equation

(1.1)

\partial_{t}u(t,x)-\Div_{x}(A(x)\nabla_{x}u)(t,x)=f(t,x),\quad(t,x)\in(0,\infty)\times{\mathds{R}}^{n},

Moreover, it satisfies the estimate

\|u\|_{T^{p}_{\beta+1}}+\|\nabla u\|_{T^{p}_{\beta+{1}/{2}}}\lesssim\|f\|_{T^{p}_{\beta}},

and both of
\partial_{t}u
and
\Div(A\nabla u)
belong to
T^{p}_{\beta}
with estimate

\|\partial_{t}u\|_{T^{p}_{\beta}}+\|\Div(A\nabla u)\|_{T^{p}_{\beta}}\lesssim\|f\|_{T^{p}_{\beta}}.

The definition of weak solutions is recalled in Section
4
and that of the weighted tent spaces
T^{p}_{\beta}
in Section
3.1
. For the time being, it suffices to know that
p
is an integrability index and
s:=2\beta+1
is a regularity index. The number
p_{L}(\beta)
will be given later and depends on the elliptic operator
L:=-\Div(A\nabla)
. We mention that this number is sharp in the case of the heat equation, with value
\frac{n}{n+2\beta+2}
.

Let us first clarify the absence of an initial condition in the statement. Actually, when
\beta>-1/2
and
0<p\leq\infty
, we shall show that all
T^{p}_{\beta+1}
-functions have zero Whitney trace. This notion of trace, which differs from the usual one taking completion from the usual restriction of test functions, is a sort of non-tangential convergence at
t=0
: it consists in taking the limit of averages on parabolic Whitney cubes as
t\to 0
for a.e.
x\in{\mathds{R}}^{n}
, see Theorem
4.2
for a precise description. In particular, the solution in the statement must have zero Whitney trace. It also implies that there is no non-trivial global weak solution in
T^{p}_{\beta+1}
to
\partial_{t}u-\Div(A\nabla u)=0
, and hence the initial value problem with non-zero datum cannot be posed in
T^{p}_{\beta+1}
when
\beta>-1/2
.

That the initial condition must be zero suggests that one may solve the equation only using appropriate estimates for the Duhamel operator

(1.2)

{\mathcal{L}}_{1}(f)(t):=\int_{0}^{t}e^{-(t-s)L}f(s)ds,

its gradient
\nabla{\mathcal{L}}_{1}
, and the maximal regularity operator

(1.3)

{\mathcal{M}}_{L}(f)(t):=\int_{0}^{t}Le^{-(t-s)L}f(s)ds.

Here, the semigroup is defined on
L^{2}({\mathds{R}}^{n})
when realizing
L
as a maximal accretive operator by Kato’s theory. Note that the operator norms of the first two integrands in
L^{2}({\mathds{R}}^{n})
have integrable singularities, while that of the maximal regularity operator is on the order of
|t-s|^{-1}
. Still, it is bounded on
L^{2}((0,\infty);L^{2}({\mathds{R}}^{n}))
by de Simon’s theorem
[
18
]
. This corresponds here to the tent space
T^{2}_{0}
. Classical maximal regularity theory (
i.e.
, boundedness of
{\mathcal{M}}_{L}
on
L^{q}((0,\infty);X)
for
1<q<\infty
) also applies, provided the semigroup has the
R
-boundedness property and that
X
is a UMD space, see
[
24
]
. It produces what is usually called unique mild solutions to the abstract Cauchy problem

(1.4)

\begin{cases}\partial_{t}u(t)+Lu(t)=f(t)\\
u(0)=0\end{cases}.

In the case of
L=-\Div(A\nabla)
,
R
-boundedness on
X=L^{p}({\mathds{R}}^{n})
only holds in a range of
p
which can be limited
[
15
]
. Indeed, it is the interval
(p_{-}(L),p_{+}(L))
introduced by
[
12
]
as the largest open interval in
(1,\infty)
so that the semigroup
(e^{-tL})_{t\geq 0}
, initially defined on
L^{2}({\mathds{R}}^{n})
, is uniformly bounded on
L^{p}({\mathds{R}}^{n})
. For negative Laplacian
-\Delta
,
p_{-}(-\Delta)=1
,
p_{+}(-\Delta)=\infty
. Examples show that
\frac{2n}{n\mp 2}\mp\varepsilon
(when
n\geq 3
) are best possible in full generality.

The reader may wonder why we attempt to use tent spaces. The interest is that their norms first use local
L^{2}
integrability in the upper half-space no matter what
p
and
\beta
are. Thus, we can be dispensed with checking
R
-boundedness and work with general non-smooth coefficients. To our knowledge, the first use of tent spaces (in the form of Carleson measures) to solve evolution equations is due to
[
22
]
on Navier–Stokes equations. See also a different proof in
[
2
]
and more recently
[
19
]
for inhomogeneous incompressible Navier–Stokes equations.

Getting away from the Laplacian, a series of pioneering works of the first author
et al.

[
8
,
5
]
proves boundedness of maximal regularity operators associated with such
L
on a family of weighted tent spaces
T^{p}_{\beta}
, completely avoiding the notion of
R
-boundedness. Here we prove the following result.

Proposition 1.2
.

Let
\beta>-1/2
and
p_{L}(\beta)<p\leq\infty
. Then,
{\mathcal{M}}_{L}
can be extended to a bounded operator on
T^{p}_{\beta}
. In addition,
{\mathcal{L}}_{1}
is bounded from
T^{p}_{\beta}
to
T^{p}_{\beta+1}
and
\nabla{\mathcal{L}}_{1}
is bounded from
T^{p}_{\beta}
to
T^{p}_{\beta+1/2}
.

We now define

(1.5)

p_{L}(\beta):=\frac{np_{-}(L)}{n+(2\beta+1)p_{-}(L)},

where
p_{-}(L)
is introduced above. Observe that
{\dot{H}}^{2\beta+1,p_{L}(\beta)}({\mathds{R}}^{n})
embeds in
L^{p_{-}(L)}({\mathds{R}}^{n})
by Sobolev embedding, so
p_{L}(\beta)
is in fact a Sobolev exponent of
p_{-}(L)
at regularity
2\beta+1
. We also see that information of boundedness of the semigroup on
L^{p}({\mathds{R}}^{n})
for
p<2
helps improving the range.

Compared to
[
5
]
, we improve here the lower limit of tent space boundedness to
p_{L}(\beta)
in all cases, thanks to a new extrapolation argument. We additionally treat here the operators
{\mathcal{L}}_{1}
and
\nabla{\mathcal{L}}_{1}
. Some results in this direction with application towards stochastic analysis are in
[
14
]
and
[
23
]
. For this, we not only extend the singular integral theory to different (singular) kernels, but also correct an inaccuracy in the definition of singular integral operators on tent spaces. As we include different singularities, it is somehow difficult to explain the correction without going again through the technical lemmas, so we take this opportunity to do it here. In the end, it streamlines the presentation and allows for improvement, see Section
2.3
.

Correctly reframed using the point of view of weak solutions, this proposition gives us existence.

Uniqueness relies on a method already used in
[
9
]
, which can be thought as an elaboration of Green’s identity in the context of rough coefficients. It allows one to show that any global (or in a strip) weak solution to (
1.4
) with null source term in a tent space satisfies an interior representation, which we may call “
homotopy identity
”,

u(t)=e^{-(t-s)L}u(s)\quad\text{ in }{\mathscr{D}}^{\prime}({\mathds{R}}^{n})

when
0<s<t
, in the sense that

\int_{{\mathds{R}}^{n}}u(t,x){\overline{h}}(x)dx=\int_{{\mathds{R}}^{n}}u(s,x)\overline{((e^{-(t-s)L})^{\ast}h)}(x)dx

for any
h\in C_{\mathsf{c}}^{\infty}({\mathds{R}}^{n})
. With this identity, the problem is reduced to understand the boundary behavior of solutions when
s\to 0
and use regularity
(e^{-(t-s)L})^{\ast}h
to
(e^{-tL})^{\ast}h
in appropriate topology as
s\to 0
, which only depends on functional calculus of the operator
L
.

To finish this introduction, we mention the results addressed in a second work
[
3
]
. As we observed after Theorem
1.1
, the weak solutions to (
1.1
) in
T^{p}_{\beta+1}
have null Whitney trace when
2\beta+1>0
(and also zero distributional limit). However, if
2\beta+1=0
, then the initial value problem can be uniquely posed with non-zero Whitney trace, even for time-dependent coefficients, see
[
9
]
and
[
25
]
. The solution class is given by
\nabla u\in T^{p}_{0}
. For time-independent coefficients, we will generalize it to
-1<2\beta+1<1
, where the initial data can be taken with Sobolev regularity
2\beta+1
.

At the same time, other source terms in divergence form
\Div F
can be considered, in the spirit of the Lions’ theory. The function
F
will be taken in
T^{p}_{\beta+{1}/{2}}
for
2\beta+1>-1
, extending
[
11
]
which considers
2\beta+1=0
(but for non-autonomous equations). In particular, this exhibits a range of
(\beta,p)
for which the Cauchy problem

\begin{cases}\partial_{t}u-\Div(A\nabla u)=\Div F\\
u(0)=u_{0}\end{cases}

can be uniquely posed for solutions
\nabla u\in T^{p}_{\beta+1/2}
provided
F\in T^{p}_{\beta+{1}/{2}}
and
u_{0}\in{\dot{H}}^{2\beta+1,p}

when
-1<2\beta+1<1
. When
2\beta+1<0
, the solution
u
will also lie in
T^{p}_{\beta+1}
, but no maximal regularity estimate.

2.
Singular integral operators

In this section, we rectify and generalize the abstract model for singular integral operators in
[
5
]
via off-diagonal decay.

Let
\pi_{1},\pi_{2}
be the projections of
{\mathds{R}}^{1+n}={\mathds{R}}\times{\mathds{R}}^{n}
to
{\mathds{R}}
and
{\mathds{R}}^{n}
components, respectively. Denote by
\Delta
the set
\{(t,s)\in(0,\infty)\times(0,\infty):t=s\}
and write
\Delta^{c}:=((0,\infty)\times(0,\infty))\setminus\Delta
.

Definition 2.1

(Off-diagonal decay)
.

Let
\kappa\in{\mathds{R}},m\in{\mathds{N}},1\leq q\leq r\leq\infty
, and
M>0
be constants. Let
\{K(t,s)\}_{(t,s)\in\Delta^{c}}
be a family of bounded operators on
L^{2}({\mathds{R}}^{n})
. We say that it has
L^{q}-L^{r}
off-diagonal decay of type
(\kappa,m,M)
if there exists a constant
C>0
such that for any Borel sets
E,F\subset{\mathds{R}}^{n}
,
f\in L^{2}({\mathds{R}}^{n})\cap L^{q}({\mathds{R}}^{n})
, and a.e.
(t,s)\in\Delta^{c}
,

(2.1)

\|\mathds{1}_{E}K(t,s)\mathds{1}_{F}f\|_{r}\leq C|t-s|^{-1+\kappa-\frac{n}{m}[q,r]}\left(1+\frac{\dist(E,F)^{m}}{|t-s|}\right)^{-M}\|\mathds{1}_{F}f\|_{q}.

2.1.
Singular kernels

Let
{\mathscr{L}}(X)
be the space of bounded linear operators on a (quasi-)Banach space
X
.

Definition 2.2

(Singular kernel)
.

Let
\kappa\in{\mathds{R}},m\in{\mathds{N}},1\leq q\leq\infty
, and
M>0
be constants. We say that an operator-valued function
K:\Delta^{c}\rightarrow{\mathscr{L}}(L^{2}({\mathds{R}}^{n}))
is a
singular kernel
(SK)
of type
(\kappa,m,q,M)
, if

(1)

K
is strongly measurable;

(2)

There exists some constant
C>0
such that for a.e.
(t,s)\in\Delta^{c}
,

(2.2)

\|K(t,s)\|_{{\mathscr{L}}(L^{2}({\mathds{R}}^{n}))}\leq C|t-s|^{-1+\kappa};

(3)

The family
\{K(t,s)\}_{(t,s)\in\Delta^{c}}
has off-diagonal
L^{q}-L^{2}
decay of type
(\kappa,m,M)
if
1\leq q\leq 2
, and off-diagonal
L^{2}-L^{q}
decay of type
(\kappa,m,M)
if
2\leq q\leq\infty
.

Let
\sk^{\kappa}_{m,q,M}
be the set of singular kernels of type
(\kappa,m,q,M)
and
\sk^{\kappa}_{m,q,\infty}
be the intersection
\bigcap_{M>0}\sk^{\kappa}_{m,q,M}
.

Remark 2.3
.

The class
\sk^{\kappa}_{m,q,M}
is stable with respect to truncation in the sense that, if
K\in\sk^{\kappa}_{m,q,M}
, then
\mathds{1}_{A}K\in\sk^{\kappa}_{m,q,M}
for any measurable set
A\subset\Delta^{c}
. The two cases,
A=\{t<s\}
and
A=\{t>s\}
are of particular interest.

Denote by
T^{\ast}
the adjoint of a linear operator
T
on
L^{2}({\mathds{R}}^{n})
.

Lemma 2.4
.

Let
1\leq q\leq r\leq\infty
. Let
\{K(t,s)\}_{(t,s)\in\Delta^{c}}
be a family of bounded operators on
L^{2}({\mathds{R}}^{n})
with
L^{q}-L^{r}
off-diagonal decay of type
(\kappa,m,M)
. Then
\{K(s,t)^{\ast}\}_{(t,s)\in\Delta^{c}}
has
L^{r^{\prime}}-L^{q^{\prime}}
off-diagonal decay of type
(\kappa,m,M)
.

Proof.

Let
E,F\subset{\mathds{R}}^{n}
be two Borel sets. For any
f\in L^{2}({\mathds{R}}^{n})\cap L^{r^{\prime}}({\mathds{R}}^{n})
and a.e.
(t,s)\in\Delta^{c}
,

\displaystyle\|\mathds{1}_{F}K(s,t)^{\ast}(\mathds{1}_{E}f)\|_{q^{\prime}}

\displaystyle=\sup_{\phi}\left\langle\mathds{1}_{F}\phi,K(s,t)^{\ast}(\mathds{1}_{E}f)\right\rangle_{L^{2}({\mathds{R}}^{n})}

\displaystyle=\sup_{\phi}\left\langle K(s,t)(\mathds{1}_{F}\phi),\mathds{1}_{E}f\right\rangle_{L^{2}({\mathds{R}}^{n})}

\displaystyle\leq\sup_{\phi}\|\mathds{1}_{E}K(s,t)(\mathds{1}_{F}\phi)\|_{r}\|\mathds{1}_{E}f\|_{r^{\prime}}

\displaystyle\lesssim|t-s|^{-1+\kappa-\frac{n}{m}[q,r]}\left(1+\frac{\dist(E,F)^{m}}{|t-s|}\right)^{-M}\|\mathds{1}_{E}f\|_{r^{\prime}},

where
\phi
is taken in
L^{2}({\mathds{R}}^{n})\cap L^{q}({\mathds{R}}^{n})
with support in
F
and
\|\phi\|_{q}=1
. Note that
[q,r]=[r^{\prime},q^{\prime}]
to conclude the proof.
∎

Corollary 2.5
.

If
K
belongs to
\sk^{\kappa}_{m,q,M}
, then
K^{\ast}:(t,s)\mapsto K(s,t)^{\ast}
belongs to
\sk^{\kappa}_{m,q^{\prime},M}
.

Proof.

The strong measurability of
K^{\ast}
follows from Pettis’ measurability theorem (see
[
21
, Theorem 1.1.6]
), since
L^{2}({\mathds{R}}^{n})
is separable. The boundedness condition is obvious, while the off-diagonal decay follows from Lemma
2.4
.
∎

2.2.
Integrals associated with singular kernels

For any function
f
on
{\mathds{R}}^{1+n}_{+}
, denote by
f(s)
the function
x\mapsto f(s,x)
. Let us first consider a non-singular integral. Let
K
be in
\sk^{\kappa}_{m,q,M}
with
\kappa>0
. For any
\beta>-1/2
and
f\in L^{2}_{\beta}({\mathds{R}}^{1+n}_{+})
, we claim that the integral

(2.3)

\int_{0}^{t}K(t,s)f(s)ds

makes sense as a Bochner integral on
L^{2}({\mathds{R}}^{n})
for a.e.
t\in(0,\infty)
. Indeed, (
2.2
) implies

\displaystyle\left(\int_{0}^{\infty}\left(t^{-\beta-\kappa}\int_{0}^{t}\|K(t,s)f(s)\|_{2}\penalty\ ds\right)^{2}dt\right)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\quad\lesssim\left(\int_{0}^{\infty}\left(\int_{0}^{t}s^{\beta+\frac{1}{2}}t^{-\beta-\kappa+\frac{1}{2}}(t-s)^{-1+\kappa}\|s^{-\beta+\frac{1}{2}}f(s)\|_{2}\frac{ds}{s}\right)^{2}\frac{dt}{t}\right)^{1/2}.

Write
k(t,s)=\mathds{1}_{\{s<t\}}(t,s)s^{\beta+\frac{1}{2}}t^{-\beta-\kappa+\frac{1}{2}}(t-s)^{-1+\kappa}
. For a.e.
t>0
,

\int_{0}^{\infty}k(t,s)\frac{ds}{s}=\int_{0}^{1}\lambda^{\beta+\frac{1}{2}}(1-\lambda)^{\kappa-1}\frac{d\lambda}{\lambda}\leq C(\beta,\kappa).

With the same conditions, for a.e.
s>0
,

\int_{0}^{\infty}k(t,s)\frac{dt}{t}=\int_{0}^{\infty}\lambda^{\kappa-1}(\lambda+1)^{-\beta-\kappa-\frac{1}{2}}d\lambda\leq C(\beta,\kappa).

Schur test hence implies

\left(\int_{0}^{\infty}\left(t^{-\beta-\kappa}\int_{0}^{t}\|K(t,s)f(s)\|_{2}\penalty\ ds\right)^{2}dt\right)^{1/2}\lesssim\|f\|_{L^{2}_{\beta}({\mathds{R}}^{1+n}_{+})}<\infty.

This proves our claim. It also shows that the function

(t,y)\mapsto\left(\int_{0}^{t}K(t,s)f(s)ds\right)(y)

belongs to
L^{2}_{\beta+\kappa}({\mathds{R}}^{1+n}_{+})
. Thanks to the strong measurability of
K
, we have the pointwise evaluation
(see
[
21
, Proposition 1.2.25]
) in the sense that for a.e.
(t,y)\in{\mathds{R}}^{1+n}_{+}
,

(2.4)

\left(\int_{0}^{t}K(t,s)f(s)ds\right)(y)=\int_{0}^{t}(K(t,s)f(s))(y)ds.

For
f\in L^{2}_{-\beta-\kappa}({\mathds{R}}^{1+n}_{+})
, by a duality argument, we can also define the
L^{2}({\mathds{R}}^{n})
-valued Bochner integral

(2.5)

\int_{t}^{\infty}K(t,s)f(s)ds

for a.e.
t\in(0,\infty)
, and obtain similarly

(2.6)

\left(\int_{t}^{\infty}K(t,s)f(s)ds\right)(y)=\int_{t}^{\infty}(K(t,s)f(s))(y)ds

for a.e.
(t,y)\in{\mathds{R}}^{1+n}_{+}
. We summarize the above discussion in the following lemma.

Lemma 2.6
.

Let
K
be in
\sk^{\kappa}_{m,q,M}
with
\kappa>0
. For any
\beta>-1/2
,

(1)

(
2.3
) defines a bounded operator from
L^{2}_{\beta}({\mathds{R}}^{1+n}_{+})
to
L^{2}_{\beta+\kappa}({\mathds{R}}^{1+n}_{+})
, and (
2.4
) holds;

(2)

(
2.5
) defines a bounded operator from
L^{2}_{-\beta-\kappa}({\mathds{R}}^{1+n}_{+})
to
L^{2}_{-\beta}({\mathds{R}}^{1+n}_{+})
, and (
2.6
) holds.

We then turn to
\kappa\leq 0
, where a non-integrable singularity occurs at
t=s
. Still, as we are interested in handling functions of
(t,x)
, we adopt a refined strategy based on the following lemma. A subset
Q\subset{\mathds{R}}^{1+n}_{+}
is called a
rectangle
if it is of product form
Q=\pi_{1}(Q)\times\pi_{2}(Q)
. It is called a
bounded
rectangle if both components are bounded.

Lemma 2.7
.

Let
K\in\sk^{\kappa}_{m,q,M}
with
\kappa\leq 0
and
\frac{n}{m}|[q,2]|-\kappa<M\leq\infty
. Let
Q_{0},Q_{1}
be two bounded rectangles in
{\mathds{R}}^{1+n}_{+}
with

\max\{\dist(\pi_{i}(Q_{0}),\pi_{i}(Q_{1})):i=1,2\}\geq\epsilon>0.

For a.e.
(t,s)\in{\mathds{R}}_{+}\times{\mathds{R}}_{+}
, define

\omega_{Q_{0},Q_{1}}(t,s):=\|\mathds{1}_{Q_{0}}(t)K(t,s)\mathds{1}_{Q_{1}}(s)\|_{{\mathscr{L}}(L^{2}({\mathds{R}}^{n}))}.

Then, it holds that

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\omega_{Q_{0},Q_{1}}(t,s)ds

\displaystyle\leq C(\epsilon,Q_{0},Q_{1},\kappa,m,M,q),

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\omega_{Q_{0},Q_{1}}(t,s)dt

\displaystyle\leq C(\epsilon,Q_{0},Q_{1},\kappa,m,M,q).

Proof.

By symmetry, it suffices to prove the first inequality. Recall that
\mathds{1}_{Q_{0}}(t)(x)=\mathds{1}_{\pi_{1}(Q_{0})}(t)\mathds{1}_{\pi_{2}(Q_{0})}(x)
and

\mathds{1}_{Q_{1}}(s)(y)=\mathds{1}_{\pi_{1}(Q_{1})}(s)\mathds{1}_{\pi_{2}(Q_{1})}(y)
.

If
\dist(\pi_{1}(Q_{0}),\pi_{1}(Q_{1}))\geq\epsilon/2
, for any
t\in\pi_{1}(Q_{0})
, we have

\int_{0}^{\infty}\omega_{Q_{0},Q_{1}}(t,s)ds\lesssim\int_{\pi_{1}(Q_{1})}|t-s|^{-1+\kappa}ds\leq(\epsilon/2)^{\kappa-1}|\pi_{1}(Q_{1})|.

Otherwise,
\dist(\pi_{1}(Q_{0}),\pi_{1}(Q_{1}))\leq\epsilon/2
but then
\dist(\pi_{2}(Q_{0}),\pi_{2}(Q_{1}))\geq\epsilon
. Off-diagonal decay of
K(t,s)
implies

\omega_{Q_{0},Q_{1}}(t,s)\lesssim\begin{cases}\epsilon^{-mM}|t-s|^{-1+\kappa-\frac{n}{m}[q,2]+M}|\pi_{2}(Q_{1})|^{[q,2]}&\text{ if }1\leq q\leq 2\\
\epsilon^{-mM}|t-s|^{-1+\kappa-\frac{n}{m}[2,q]+M}|\pi_{2}(Q_{0})|^{[2,q]}&\text{ if }2\leq q\leq\infty\end{cases}

by Hölder’s inequality. In summary, we get

\int_{0}^{\infty}\omega_{Q_{0},Q_{1}}(t,s)ds\lesssim_{Q_{0},Q_{1},q}\epsilon^{-mM}\int_{\pi_{1}(Q_{1})}|t-s|^{-1+\kappa-\frac{n}{m}|[q,2]|+M}ds,

which converges as
M>\frac{n}{m}|[q,2]|-\kappa
.
∎

Let
L^{2}_{\mathsf{b}}({\mathds{R}}^{1+n}_{+})
be the subspace of
L^{2}({\mathds{R}}^{1+n}_{+})
consisting of functions with bounded support in
\overline{{\mathds{R}}^{1+n}_{+}}
. For any
f\in L^{2}_{\mathsf{b}}({\mathds{R}}^{1+n}_{+})
, define
\pi(f):=\pi_{1}(\supp f)\times\pi_{2}(\supp f)
as a bounded rectangle. Let
K,\kappa
, and
M
be as in Lemma
2.7
. For any bounded rectangle
Q\subset{\mathds{R}}^{1+n}_{+}
with
\dist(Q,\pi(f))>0
and a.e.
t\in\pi_{1}(Q)
,

\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathds{1}_{\pi_{2}(Q)}K(t,s)f(s)ds

is defined as a Bochner integral valued in
L^{2}({\mathds{R}}^{n})
, since

\int_{0}^{\infty}\|\mathds{1}_{\pi_{2}(Q)}(K(t,s)f(s))\|_{2}\penalty\ ds\leq\int_{0}^{\infty}\omega_{Q,\pi(f)}(t,s)\|f(s)\|_{2}\penalty\ ds,

which is finite thanks to Lemma
2.7
and Schur test. Pointwise evaluation also holds,
i.e.
, for a.e.
(t,y)\in Q
,

\left(\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathds{1}_{\pi_{2}(Q)}K(t,s)f(s)ds\right)(y)=\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathds{1}_{\pi_{2}(Q)}(y)(K(t,s)f(s))(y)ds.

Furthermore, if
Q,Q^{\prime}\subset({\mathds{R}}^{1+n}_{+}\setminus\pi(f))
are bounded rectangles with
Q\cap Q^{\prime}\neq\varnothing
, then for a.e.
(t,y)\in Q\cap Q^{\prime}
, we have

(2.7)

\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathds{1}_{\pi_{2}(Q)}(y)(K(t,s)f(s))(y)dy=\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathds{1}_{\pi_{2}(Q^{\prime})}(y)(K(t,s)f(s))(y)ds.

Indeed, as
t\in\pi_{1}(Q)\cap\pi_{1}(Q^{\prime})
, we have when
x\in\pi_{2}(Q)\cap\pi_{2}(Q^{\prime})

\lhs=\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathds{1}_{\pi_{2}(Q^{\prime})}(y)\mathds{1}_{\pi_{2}(Q)}(y)(K(t,s)f(s))(y)ds=\rhs.

Thus, we can define the function

(t,y)\mapsto\int_{0}^{\infty}(K(t,s)f(s))(y)ds

almost everywhere on
\pi(f)^{c}
as follows. Pick an
exhaustion
of
\pi(f)^{c}
by bounded rectangles
\{Q_{i}\}_{i\in{\mathds{N}}}
,
i.e.
,
\bigcup_{i}Q_{i}=\pi(f)^{c}
. Then, we set almost everywhere

(2.8)

\int_{0}^{\infty}(K(t,s)f(s))(y)ds:=\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathds{1}_{\pi_{2}(Q_{i})}(y)(K(t,s)f(s))(y)ds,

if
(t,y)\in Q_{i}
. This definition makes sense almost everywhere and is clearly independent of the choice of exhaustion
\{Q_{i}\}_{i\in{\mathds{N}}}
by (
2.7
). We summarize the above discussion in the following lemma.

Lemma 2.8
.

Let
\kappa\leq 0
,
\frac{n}{m}|[q,2]|-\kappa<M\leq\infty
, and
K\in\sk^{\kappa}_{m,q,M}
. Then for any
f\in L^{2}_{\mathsf{b}}({\mathds{R}}^{1+n}_{+})
,

(t,x)\mapsto\int_{0}^{\infty}(K(t,s)f(s))(x)ds

defines a function in
L^{2}_{\loc}(\overline{{\mathds{R}}^{1+n}_{+}}\setminus\pi(f))
. Moreover, for any
g\in L^{2}_{\mathsf{b}}({\mathds{R}}^{1+n}_{+})
with
\pi(g)\cap\pi(f)=\varnothing
,

(t,s)\mapsto\langle K(t,s)f(s),g(t)\rangle_{L^{2}({\mathds{R}}^{n})}

defines a function in
L^{1}((0,\infty)\times(0,\infty))
, and

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{0}^{\infty}\langle K(t,s)f(s),g(t)\rangle_{L^{2}({\mathds{R}}^{n})}dsdt

\displaystyle\quad=\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{{\mathds{R}}^{n}}\left(\int_{0}^{\infty}(K(t,s)f(s))(x)ds\right)\cdot{\overline{g}}(t,x)dtdx.

Proof.

The first part follows from (
2.8
), and the second part follows from the first part by writing

\langle K(t,s)f(s),g(t)\rangle_{L^{2}({\mathds{R}}^{n})}=\langle\mathds{1}_{\pi(g)}(t)K(t,s)\mathds{1}_{\pi(f)}(s)f(s),g(t)\rangle_{L^{2}({\mathds{R}}^{n})}

and using pointwise evaluation and Fubini’s theorem.
∎

2.3.
Singular integral operators

Define

(2.9)

M_{\kappa,q}:=\begin{cases}0&\text{ if }\kappa>0\\
\frac{n}{m}|[q,2]|-\kappa&\text{ if }\kappa\leq 0\end{cases}\ .

Definition 2.9

(SIO of type
+(\kappa,m,q,M)
)
.

Let
\kappa\in{\mathds{R}},m\in{\mathds{N}},1\leq q\leq\infty
, and
M_{\kappa,q}<M\leq\infty
be constants. An operator
T
is called a
singular integral operator
(SIO)
of type
+(\kappa,m,q,M)
, if

(1)

T
is a bounded operator from
L^{2}({\mathds{R}}^{1+n}_{+})
to
L^{2}_{\kappa}({\mathds{R}}^{1+n}_{+})
;

(2)

There exists
K\in\sk^{\kappa}_{m,q,M}
, called the
kernel
of
T
, such that the representation

(2.10)

Tf(t,y)=\int_{0}^{t}(K(t,s)f(s))(y)ds

holds

•

for any
f\in L^{2}({\mathds{R}}^{1+n}_{+})
and a.e.
(t,y)\in{\mathds{R}}^{1+n}_{+}
, if
\kappa>0
;

•

for any
f\in L^{2}_{\mathsf{b}}({\mathds{R}}^{1+n}_{+})
and a.e.
(t,y)\in\pi(f)^{c}
, if
\kappa\leq 0
.

Denote by
\sio^{\kappa+}_{m,q,M}
the set of all SIOs of type
+(\kappa,m,q,M)
.

There is a slight abuse of terminology since the operator is not singular when
\kappa>0
.

The next class of singular integral operators is concerned with negative type. We shall present results for it since it is instrumental for duality. It can be used to obtain estimates for backward Cauchy problems, but we leave to the reader the care of stating them.

Definition 2.10

(SIO of type
-(\kappa,m,q,M)
)
.

Let
\kappa\in{\mathds{R}},m\in{\mathds{N}},1\leq q\leq\infty
, and
M_{\kappa,q}<M\leq\infty
be constants. An operator
T
is called a
singular integral operator of type
-(\kappa,m,q,M)
, if

(1)

T
is a bounded operator from
L^{2}_{-\kappa}({\mathds{R}}^{1+n}_{+})
to
L^{2}({\mathds{R}}^{1+n}_{+})
;

(2)

There exists
K\in\sk^{\kappa}_{m,q,M}
, called the
kernel
of
T
, such that the representation

(2.11)

Tf(t,y)=\int_{t}^{\infty}(K(t,s)f(s))(y)ds

holds

•

for any
f\in L^{2}_{-\kappa}({\mathds{R}}^{1+n}_{+})
and a.e.
(t,y)\in{\mathds{R}}^{1+n}_{+}
, if
\kappa>0
;

•

for any
f\in L^{2}_{-\kappa}({\mathds{R}}^{1+n}_{+})
with bounded support in
\overline{{\mathds{R}}^{1+n}_{+}}
and a.e.
(t,y)\in\pi(f)^{c}
, if
\kappa\leq 0
.

Denote by
\sio^{\kappa-}_{m,q,M}
the set of all SIOs of type
-(\kappa,m,q,M)
.

For
\kappa>0
, the integrals in (
2.10
) and (
2.11
) are well-defined by Lemma
2.6
. For
\kappa\leq 0
, one uses Lemma
2.8
. In the case
\kappa=0
, compared with Definition 2.1 in
[
5
]
, our definitions seem more restrictive, but in fact, (
2.10
) and (
2.11
) are all what is needed in their proofs to obtain tent space estimates.

These two types of SIOs are linked via
L^{2}({\mathds{R}}^{1+n}_{+})
-duality given by

\langle f,g\rangle_{L^{2}({\mathds{R}}^{1+n}_{+})}:=\int_{{\mathds{R}}^{1+n}_{+}}f(t,y){\overline{g}}(t,y)dtdy.

Proposition 2.11
.

Let
\kappa\in{\mathds{R}},m\in{\mathds{N}},1\leq q\leq\infty
, and
M_{\kappa,q}<M\leq\infty
. Let
T
be in
\sio^{\kappa\pm}_{m,q,M}
with kernel
K
and
T^{\ast}
be the adjoint of
T
with respect to
L^{2}({\mathds{R}}^{1+n}_{+})
-duality. Then
T^{\ast}
lies in
\sio^{\kappa\mp}_{m,q^{\prime},M}
with kernel
K^{\ast}
.

Proof.

We only prove the case assuming
T\in\sio^{\kappa+}_{m,q,M}
with
\kappa\leq 0
. The other cases are left to the reader. The boundedness of
T^{\ast}
is clear, and Corollary
2.5
shows
K^{\ast}\in\sk^{\kappa}_{m,q^{\prime},M}
, so it suffices to prove the representation. For any
f\in L^{2}_{-\kappa}({\mathds{R}}^{1+n}_{+})
with bounded support in
\overline{{\mathds{R}}^{1+n}_{+}}
and
g\in L^{2}_{\mathsf{b}}({\mathds{R}}^{1+n}_{+})
so that
\pi(g)
is disjoint with
\pi(f)
, we get by (
2.10
)

\left\langle Tg,f\right\rangle_{L^{2}({\mathds{R}}^{1+n}_{+})}=\int_{{\mathds{R}}^{1+n}_{+}}\left(\int_{0}^{s}(K(s,t)g(t))(y)dt\right){\overline{f}}(s,y)dsdy.

Thanks to Lemma
2.7
, Schur test implies

\displaystyle\int_{{\mathds{R}}^{1+n}_{+}}\int_{0}^{s}|(K(s,t)g(t))(y)||f(s,y)|dtdsdy

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}ds\int_{0}^{s}dt\int_{{\mathds{R}}^{n}}|\mathds{1}_{\pi(f)}(s,y)(K(s,t)g(t))(y)||f(s,y)|dy

\displaystyle\leq\int_{0}^{\infty}ds\int_{0}^{s}\omega_{\pi(f),\pi(g)}(s,t)\|g(t)\|_{2}\|f(s)\|_{2}\penalty\ dt

\displaystyle\lesssim\|g\|_{L^{2}({\mathds{R}}^{1+n}_{+})}\|f\|_{L^{2}({\mathds{R}}^{1+n}_{+})}\lesssim\|g\|_{L^{2}({\mathds{R}}^{1+n}_{+})}\|f\|_{L^{2}_{-\kappa}({\mathds{R}}^{1+n}_{+})}<\infty,

using that
f
has bounded support. Fubini’s theorem ensures that

\displaystyle\left\langle g,T^{\ast}f\right\rangle_{L^{2}({\mathds{R}}^{1+n}_{+})}

\displaystyle=\left\langle Tg,f\right\rangle_{L^{2}({\mathds{R}}^{1+n}_{+})}

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}ds\int_{0}^{s}\left\langle K(s,t)g(t),f(s)\right\rangle_{L^{2}({\mathds{R}}^{n})}dt

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}dt\int_{t}^{\infty}\left\langle g(t),K(s,t)^{\ast}f(s)\right\rangle_{L^{2}({\mathds{R}}^{n})}ds

\displaystyle=\int_{{\mathds{R}}^{1+n}_{+}}g(t,x)\left(\int_{t}^{\infty}\overline{(K(s,t)^{\ast}f(s))}(x)ds\right)dtdx.

We conclude by arbitrariness of
g
that for a.e.
(t,x)\in\pi(f)^{c}
,

T^{\ast}(f)(t,x)=\int_{t}^{\infty}(K(s,t)^{\ast}f(s))(x)ds.

Hence (
2.11
) holds for
T^{\ast}
with kernel
K^{\ast}
.
∎

3.
Extensions of SIOs to tent spaces

The bounded extensions of SIOs of type
\pm(0,m,q,M)
on tent spaces have been studied in
[
5
]
. In this section, we improve their results in our framework, simplify some proofs, and generalize to SIOs of type
\pm(\kappa,m,q,M)
for
\kappa\neq 0
.

3.1.
Basic properties of tent spaces

Readers can refer to
[
16
]
for the original definitions of tent spaces and proofs of the following facts. Some needed refinements can be found in
[
13
,
7
]
.

For
0<p<\infty
,
m\in{\mathds{N}}
,
\beta\in{\mathds{R}}
, the
tent space

T^{p;m}_{\beta}
consists of (possibly vector-valued, strongly) measurable functions
f
on
{\mathds{R}}^{1+n}_{+}
for which

\|f\|_{T^{p;m}_{\beta}}:=\left(\int_{{\mathds{R}}^{n}}\left(\int_{0}^{\infty}\fint_{B(x,t^{1/m})}|t^{-\beta}f(t,y)|^{2}dtdy\right)^{p/2}dx\right)^{1/p}<\infty.

Our notation for weighted tent spaces is different from that in
[
5
]
and in fact corresponds to
T^{p,2;m}_{-\beta/2}
there.

Note that
T^{2;m}_{\beta}
can be identified with
L^{2}_{\beta}({\mathds{R}}^{1+n}_{+})
for any
m\in{\mathds{N}}
by Fubini’s theorem. Let
L^{2}_{\mathsf{c}}({\mathds{R}}^{1+n}_{+})
be the space of
L^{2}({\mathds{R}}^{1+n}_{+})
-functions with compact support in
{\mathds{R}}^{1+n}_{+}
. It is dense in
T^{p;m}_{\beta}
. In fact, for any
f\in T^{p;m}_{\beta}
and compact subset
K\subset{\mathds{R}}^{1+n}_{+}
,
\mathds{1}_{K}f
lies in
L^{2}({\mathds{R}}^{1+n}_{+})
with

(3.1)

\|\mathds{1}_{K}f\|_{L^{2}({\mathds{R}}^{1+n}_{+})}\eqsim_{p,m,\beta,K}\|\mathds{1}_{K}f\|_{T^{p;m}_{\beta}}\leq\|f\|_{T^{p;m}_{\beta}}.

For any
p\in(1,\infty)
and
\beta\in{\mathds{R}}
, the dual of
T^{p;m}_{\beta}
can be identified with
T^{p^{\prime};m}_{-\beta}
via
L^{2}({\mathds{R}}^{1+n}_{+})
-duality. It also identifies the dual of
T^{p;m}_{\beta}
with
T^{\infty;m}_{-\beta,([p,1])}
when
p\leq 1
. The space
T^{\infty;m}_{-\beta,([p,1])}
consists of measurable functions
f
on
{\mathds{R}}^{1+n}_{+}
for which

\|f\|_{T^{\infty;m}_{-\beta,([p,1])}}:=\sup_{B}\frac{1}{|B|^{[p,1]}}\left(\int_{0}^{r(B)^{m}}\fint_{B}|t^{\beta}f(t,y)|^{2}dtdy\right)^{1/2}<\infty,

where
B
describes all balls of
{\mathds{R}}^{n}
.

Note that
L^{2}_{\mathsf{c}}({\mathds{R}}^{1+n}_{+})
is not dense in
T^{\infty;m}_{-\beta,([p,1])}
for any
p\leq 1
and
\beta\in{\mathds{R}}
with respect to the topology induced by its norm. However, it is dense in the
\weakstar
topology.

Lemma 3.1
.

For any
0<p\leq 1
and
\beta\in{\mathds{R}}
,
L^{2}_{\mathsf{c}}({\mathds{R}}^{1+n}_{+})
is dense in
T^{\infty;m}_{-\beta,([p,1])}
with respect to the weak
∗
topology as dual of
T^{p;m}_{\beta}
.

Proof.

Let
f
be in
T^{\infty;m}_{-\beta,([p,1])}
. For any
R>1
, define
C_{R}:=[1/R,R]\times B(0,R)
and
f_{R}:=f\mathds{1}_{C_{R}}
. For any
g\in T^{p;m}_{\beta}
,

\left|\langle f-f_{R},g\rangle_{L^{2}({\mathds{R}}^{1+n}_{+})}\right|\leq\int_{(C_{R})^{c}}|f||g|\lesssim\|f\|_{T^{\infty;m}_{-\beta,([p,1])}}\|\mathds{1}_{(C_{R})^{c}}g\|_{T^{p;m}_{\beta}},

which tends to 0 as
R\to\infty
by dominated convergence. This proves the convergence of
f_{R}
to
f
as
R\to\infty
for the
\weakstar
topology.
∎

An important tool in the study of tent spaces is change of aperture. For any
\alpha>0
and
x\in{\mathds{R}}^{n}
, the
cone

\Gamma_{\alpha}^{m}(x)
with
aperture

\alpha
and
homogeneity

m
at
x
is given by

(3.2)

\Gamma_{\alpha}^{m}(x):=\{(t,y)\in{\mathds{R}}^{1+n}_{+}:|x-y|<\alpha t^{1/m}\}.

Notice that the inner integral in the
T^{p;m}_{\beta}
-norm is an
L^{2}
-norm on
\Gamma_{1}^{m}(x)
. Changing aperture gives an equivalent norm. More precisely, define the
conical square function
as

(3.3)

{\mathcal{A}}^{(\alpha)}_{\beta;m}(f)(x):=\left(\int_{0}^{\infty}dt\int_{B(x,\alpha t^{1/m})}|t^{-\beta}f(t,y)|^{2}\frac{dy}{t^{n/m}}\right)^{1/2}.

For
p\in(0,\infty)
, define

\|f\|_{T^{p;m}_{\alpha,\beta}}:=\|{\mathcal{A}}^{(\alpha)}_{\beta;m}(f)\|_{p}.

For
\alpha>0
and
p\in(0,\infty)
, one has
T^{p;m}_{\beta}=T^{p;m}_{\alpha,\beta}
and

(3.4)

\min\{\alpha^{\frac{n}{2}},\alpha^{\frac{n}{p}}\}\|f\|_{T^{p;m}_{\beta}}\lesssim\|f\|_{T^{p;m}_{\alpha,\beta}}\lesssim\max\{\alpha^{\frac{n}{2}},\alpha^{\frac{n}{p}}\}\|f\|_{T^{p;m}_{\beta}}.

We shall omit
\alpha
if
\alpha=1
, and also write
{\mathcal{A}}_{\beta;m}:={\mathcal{A}}^{(1)}_{\beta;m}
.

For
0<p\leq 1
, a measurable function
a
on
{\mathds{R}}^{1+n}_{+}
is called a
T^{p;m}_{\beta}
-atom
, if there exists a ball
B\subset{\mathds{R}}^{n}
such that
\supp(a)\subset[0,r(B)^{m}]\times B
, and

\|a\|_{L^{2}_{\beta}({\mathds{R}}^{1+n}_{+})}\leq|B|^{-[p,2]}.

Such a ball
B
is said to be
associated
to
a
. Using the notion of atomic decomposition, the following result is a verbatim adaptation of
[
10
, Theorem 4.9]
, left to the reader.

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
\beta,\gamma\in{\mathds{R}}
and
0<p\leq 1
. Let
T
be a bounded linear operator from
L^{2}_{\beta}({\mathds{R}}^{1+n}_{+})
to
L^{2}_{\gamma}({\mathds{R}}^{1+n}_{+})
. If
T
is uniformly bounded from
T^{p;m}_{\beta}
-atoms to
T^{p;m}_{\gamma}
, then
T
can be extended to a bounded linear operator from
T^{p;m}_{\beta}
to
T^{p;m}_{\gamma}
.

3.2.
Main results

In this section, we state our main results on the extension of SIOs and their adjoints to tent spaces
T^{p;m}_{\beta}
. This is divided into four statements depending on the type of SIOs and the range of
p
. Proofs are postponed to Section

.

Throughout the section,
\kappa\in{\mathds{R}}
and

The first statement concerns the extension of operators in

. Define

(3.5)

.

Let

, and

. Let

);

(2)

2\leq q^{\prime}\leq\frac{2n}{m(2\beta+1)}
(or equivalently

) and

.

The next statement establishes the extension of operators in

.

.

The third statement describes the extension of operators in

.

.

, and

.

(1)

From the definition (

\displaystyle\|\mathds{1}_{B_{0}}T_{z}(\mathds{1}_{C_{j}}f)(t)\|_{2}

\displaystyle\leq\int_{0}^{t/2}\left(\frac{s}{t}\right)^{\Re(z)}\|\mathds{1}_{B_{0}}K(t,s)(\mathds{1}_{C_{j}}f(s))\|_{2}\penalty\ ds

4.
Inhomogeneous Cauchy problems

We may now begin the study of well-posedness in weighted tent spaces of Cauchy problems for the inhomogeneous parabolic equation

(IC)

\partial_{t}u(t,x)-\Div_{x}(A(x)\nabla_{x}u)(t,x)=f(t,x),\quad(t,x)\in(0,\infty)\times{\mathds{R}}^{n},

where
f\in{\mathscr{D}}^{\prime}({\mathds{R}}^{1+n}_{+})
is called the
source term
. Assume that
A\in L^{\infty}({\mathds{R}}^{n};\mat_{n}({\mathds{C}}))
is
uniformly elliptic
,
i.e.
, there exist
\Lambda_{0},\Lambda_{1}>0
such that, for a.e.
x\in{\mathds{R}}^{n}
and any
\xi,\eta\in{\mathds{C}}^{n}
,

(4.1)

\Re(\langle A(x)\xi,\xi\rangle)\geq\Lambda_{0}|\xi|^{2},\quad|\langle A(x)\xi,\eta\rangle|\leq\Lambda_{1}|\xi||\eta|.

Let us recall the classical definition of weak solutions to (
IC
). Let
0\leq a<b\leq\infty
be constants,
\Omega
be an open subset of
{\mathds{R}}^{n}
, and
Q:=(a,b)\times\Omega
. Let
f
be in
{\mathscr{D}}^{\prime}(Q)
. We say that a function
u\in L^{2}_{\loc}((a,b);H^{1}_{\loc}(\Omega))
is a
weak solution to (
IC
) with source term
f
, if

(4.2)

-\int_{Q}u(t,x)\overline{\partial_{t}\phi}(t,x)+\int_{Q}(A(x)\nabla u(t,x))\cdot\overline{\nabla\phi}(t,x)=(f,\overline{\phi})

holds for any
\phi\in C_{\mathsf{c}}^{\infty}(Q)
, where the right-hand side is understood as the pairing between distributions and test functions on
Q
. Here and often in the sequel, when unspecified, the measures in the integrals are unweighted Lebesgue measures. We say
u
is a
global weak solution to (
IC
) with source term
f
when
Q={\mathds{R}}^{1+n}_{+}=(0,\infty)\times{\mathds{R}}^{n}
.

We shall use an extension of Duhamel’s formula to build weak solutions. Define the operator
L:=-\Div(A(x)\nabla)
on
L^{2}({\mathds{R}}^{n})
with domain

D(L):=\{f\in H^{1}({\mathds{R}}^{n}):\Div(A\nabla f)\in L^{2}({\mathds{R}}^{n})\}.

It is well-known that
-L
is a densely-defined closed operator on
L^{2}({\mathds{R}}^{n})
that generates a bounded analytic semigroup
(e^{-tL})_{t\geq 0}
on
L^{2}({\mathds{R}}^{n})
.

Let
1\leq p\leq q\leq\infty
and
M>0
be constants. We say that a family of bounded operators
(T_{t})_{t\geq 0}
on
L^{2}({\mathds{R}}^{n})
has
(parabolic)
L^{p}-L^{q}
off-diagonal estimates
of
order

M
, if there is a constant
C>0
so that, for any Borel sets
E,F\subset{\mathds{R}}^{n}
,
f\in L^{2}({\mathds{R}}^{n})\cap L^{p}({\mathds{R}}^{n})
, and a.e.
t>0
,

(4.3)

\|\mathds{1}_{E}T_{t}\mathds{1}_{F}f\|_{q}\leq Ct^{-\frac{n}{2}[p,q]}\left(1+\frac{\dist(E,F)^{2}}{t}\right)^{-M}\|\mathds{1}_{F}f\|_{p}.

Let
p_{\pm}(L),q_{\pm}(L)\in[1,\infty]
be the critical numbers introduced by
[
12
]
. The semigroup
(e^{-tL})_{t\geq 0}
has
L^{p}-L^{q}
off-diagonal estimates of any order
M
when
p_{-}(L)<p\leq q<p_{+}(L)
, and the same holds for the family
(t^{1/2}\nabla e^{-tL})_{t>0}
when
q_{-}(L)<p\leq q<q_{+}(L)
. Moreover,
p_{-}(L)=q_{-}(L)<2
and
p_{+}(L)\geq q_{+}(L)>2
.

Let us also recall a family of slice spaces. Readers can refer to
[
6
, §3]
for the proof of the following facts. For any
p\in[1,\infty]
and
\delta>0
, the
(parabolic) slice space
E^{p}_{\delta}
consists of measurable functions
f
for which

\|f\|_{E^{p}_{\delta}}:=\left\|\left(\fint_{B(\cdot,\delta^{1/2})}|f(y)|^{2}dy\right)^{1/2}\right\|_{p}<\infty.

Let
\delta,\delta^{\prime}>0
be constants. Then, for any measurable function
f
on
{\mathds{R}}^{n}
,

(4.4)

\|f\|_{E^{p}_{\delta}}\lesssim\max\left\{1,\left(\frac{\delta^{\prime}}{\delta}\right)^{\frac{n}{2}[2,p]}\right\}\|f\|_{E^{p}_{\delta^{\prime}}}.

Fix
\delta>0
. For any
p\in[1,\infty)
, the dual of
E^{p}_{\delta}
can be identified with
E^{p^{\prime}}_{\delta}
via
L^{2}({\mathds{R}}^{n})
-duality. Moreover, let
1\leq p_{0}<p<p_{1}\leq\infty
be with
1/p=(1-\theta)/p_{0}+\theta/p_{1}
for some
\theta>0
. Then, the complex interpolation space
[E^{p_{0}}_{\delta},E^{p_{1}}_{\delta}]_{\theta}
can be identified with
E^{p}_{\delta}
.

4.1.
Main results

Define the operator
{\mathcal{L}}_{1}
on
L^{2}({\mathds{R}}^{1+n}_{+})
by

(4.5)

{\mathcal{L}}_{1}(f)(t,y):=\int_{0}^{t}(e^{-(t-s)L}f(s))(y)ds.

For any

Lemma 4.6

. Then, the sequence of pairing

converges to

We now prove Theorem

.

Proof of Theorem
4.3
.

Let

to (

) with same source term

. It lies in

) with null source term. For

\int_{{\mathds{R}}^{n}}\left(\int_{a}^{b}\int_{B(x,b^{1/2})}|v(t,y)|^{2}dtdy\right)^{1/2}e^{-\gamma|x|^{2}}dx\lesssim_{\gamma,p,a,b,\beta}\|v\|_{T^{p}_{\beta}}<\infty

4.8

[

ensures that the homotopy identity holds. More precisely, for
0<s<t<\infty
and

, we have

(4.17)

\int_{{\mathds{R}}^{n}}v(t,x){\overline{h}}(x)dx=\int_{{\mathds{R}}^{n}}v(s,x)\overline{((e^{-(t-s)L})^{\ast}h)}(x)dx.

We now take limits as
s\to 0
on both sides to show

in
{\mathscr{D}}^{\prime}({\mathds{R}}^{n})
, hence proving

. The proof is divided into three cases.

Case 1:
2\leq p<\infty

Let
\delta>0
be a constant. Using (

\|v(t)\|_{E^{p}_{\delta}}\lesssim\|v(t)\|_{E^{p}_{t/16}}
. Since

\displaystyle\left(\int_{{\mathds{R}}^{n}}\left(\fint_{B(x,\frac{\sqrt{t}}{4})}|v(t,y)|^{2}dy\right)^{p/2}dx\right)^{1/p}

\displaystyle\lesssim t^{\beta+\frac{1}{2}}\|v\|_{T^{p}_{\beta+1}}.

Hence

Thus,

tends to 0 in
E^{p}_{\delta}
as

. Thanks to
[

, Lemma 4.7]
, we have
(e^{-(t-s)L})^{\ast}h

in

.

Case 2:

\delta>0
be a constant. For any

\displaystyle\|v(t)\|_{E^{\infty}_{\delta}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|v(t)\|_{E^{\infty}_{t/16}}=\underset{x\in{\mathds{R}}^{n}}{\mathrm{esssup}}\left(\fint_{B(x,\frac{\sqrt{t}}{4})}|v(t,y)|^{2}dy\right)^{1/2}

We claim that,

(4.19)

\|v(t)\|_{p}\lesssim t^{\beta+\frac{1}{2}}\|v\|_{T^{p}_{\beta+1}},\quad\forall 0<p\leq 2,\penalty\ \forall t>0.

Indeed, using Hölder’s inequality and (

Interpolating (

Maximal regularity on tent spaces

In this section, we prove maximal regularity of the system (

),

, Theorem
4.2
(

5.1.
Maximal regularity operator

Define the
maximal regularity operator

{\mathcal{L}}_{0}
on
L^{2}((0,\infty);D(L))
by

(5.1)

{\mathcal{L}}_{0}(f)(t,y):=\int_{0}^{t}(Le^{-(t-s)L}f(s))(y)ds,\quad\mathrm{a.e.}\ (t,y)\in{\mathds{R}}^{1+n}_{+}.

The integral makes sense as an
L^{2}({\mathds{R}}^{n})
-valued Bochner integral. Recall that the celebrated de Simon’s theorem
[
18
]
asserts
{\mathcal{L}}_{0}
can be extended by density to a bounded operator on
L^{2}({\mathds{R}}^{1+n}_{+})
. Denote by
\tilde{{\mathcal{L}}}_{0}
this extension.

Lemma 5.1
.

\tilde{{\mathcal{L}}}_{0}
belongs to
\sio^{0+}_{2,q,\infty}
for any
q\in(p_{-}(L),p_{+}(L))
.

Proof.

The boundedness of
\tilde{{\mathcal{L}}}_{0}
on
L^{2}({\mathds{R}}^{1+n}_{+})
is clear by construction, and the function
(t,s)\mapsto\mathds{1}_{\{t>s\}}(t,s)Le^{-(t-s)L}
belongs to
\sk^{0}_{2,q,\infty}
for any
q\in(p_{-}(L),p_{+}(L))
by
[
12
, Proposition 3.15]
. Thus, Lemma
2.8
yields for any
f\in L^{2}_{\mathsf{b}}({\mathds{R}}^{1+n}_{+})
,

(t,x)\mapsto\int_{0}^{t}(Le^{-(t-s)L}f)(x)ds

defines a function in
L^{2}_{\loc}(\overline{{\mathds{R}}^{1+n}_{+}}\setminus\pi(f))
. It only remains to prove the representation (
2.10
), or equivalently, for any
f\in L^{2}_{\mathsf{b}}({\mathds{R}}^{1+n}_{+})
and
g\in C_{\mathsf{c}}^{\infty}({{\mathds{R}}^{1+n}_{+}})
with
\pi(g)\cap\pi(f)=\varnothing
,

(5.2)

\left\langle\tilde{{\mathcal{L}}}_{0}(f),g\right\rangle_{L^{2}({\mathds{R}}^{1+n}_{+})}=\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{{\mathds{R}}^{n}}\left(\int_{0}^{t}(Le^{-(t-s)L}f(s))(x)ds\right){\overline{g}}(t,x)dtdx.

To prove (
5.2
), we proceed by putting the following two observations together. Fix
g\in C_{\mathsf{c}}^{\infty}({{\mathds{R}}^{1+n}_{+}})
.

The first observation is that for any
f\in L^{2}((0,\infty);D(L))
,

(5.3)

\left\langle{\mathcal{L}}_{0}(f),g\right\rangle_{L^{2}({\mathds{R}}^{1+n}_{+})}=\left\langle A{\mathcal{L}}_{1/2}(f),\nabla g\right\rangle_{L^{2}({\mathds{R}}^{1+n}_{+})}.

Indeed, since
{\mathcal{L}}_{1/2}(f)
is given by an
L^{2}({\mathds{R}}^{n})
-valued Bochner integral, all the following integrals converge absolutely, so Fubini’s theorem ensures

\displaystyle\left\langle{\mathcal{L}}_{0}(f),g\right\rangle_{L^{2}({\mathds{R}}^{1+n}_{+})}

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{0}^{t}\left\langle Le^{-(t-s)L}f(s),g(t)\right\rangle_{L^{2}({\mathds{R}}^{n})}dsdt

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{0}^{t}\left\langle A\nabla e^{-(t-s)L}f(s),\nabla g(t)\right\rangle_{L^{2}({\mathds{R}}^{n})}dsdt

\displaystyle=\left\langle A{\mathcal{L}}_{1/2}(f),\nabla g\right\rangle_{L^{2}({\mathds{R}}^{1+n}_{+})}.

The second observation is that when
f
lies in
L^{2}_{\mathsf{b}}({\mathds{R}}^{1+n}_{+})
with
\pi(f)
disjoint with
\pi(g)
, we have the identity

(5.4)

\displaystyle\left\langle A{\mathcal{L}}_{1/2}(f),\nabla g\right\rangle_{L^{2}({\mathds{R}}^{1+n}_{+})}

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{0}^{t}\int_{{\mathds{R}}^{n}}(A\nabla e^{-(t-s)L}f(s))(x)\cdot\overline{\nabla g}(t,x)dxdsdt

\displaystyle=-\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{0}^{t}\int_{{\mathds{R}}^{n}}\Div(A\nabla e^{-(t-s)L}f(s))(x)\overline{g}(t,x)dxdsdt=:I

Indeed, all the integrals converge absolutely as for fixed
(t,s)
, both
x\mapsto(A\nabla e^{-(t-s)L}f(s))(x)
and
x\mapsto\Div(A\nabla e^{-(t-s)L}f(s))(x)
are integrable on the support of
g(t)
, using Lemma
2.6
with
\kappa=1/2
and Lemma
2.8
with
\kappa=0
. Thus, the second equality follows by integration by parts for the divergence.

We now prove (
5.2
) as follows. By a limiting argument from the first observation, we have that for any
f\in L^{2}({\mathds{R}}^{1+n}_{+})
, (
5.3
) becomes

(5.5)

\left\langle\tilde{{\mathcal{L}}}_{0}(f),g\right\rangle_{L^{2}({\mathds{R}}^{1+n}_{+})}=\left\langle A{\mathcal{L}}_{1/2}(f),\nabla g\right\rangle_{L^{2}({\mathds{R}}^{1+n}_{+})}

Indeed, the argument is similar to Lemma
4.5
, but using the continuity of
{\mathcal{L}}_{1/2}
and
\tilde{{\mathcal{L}}}_{0}
. We hence leave details to the reader.

Next, for any
f\in L^{2}_{\mathsf{b}}({\mathds{R}}^{1+n}_{+})
with
\pi(f)
disjoint with
\pi(g)
, (
5.5
) and (
5.4
) yield

\left\langle\tilde{{\mathcal{L}}}_{0}(f),g\right\rangle_{L^{2}({\mathds{R}}^{1+n}_{+})}=I.

So it only remains to observe that for a.e.

and
x\in{\mathds{R}}^{n}
,

\Div(A\nabla e^{-(t-s)L}f(s))(x)=(Le^{-(t-s)L}f(s))(x)

by definition of
L
, as

belongs to
L^{2}({\mathds{R}}^{n})
.
∎

We then consider a further extension of
\tilde{{\mathcal{L}}}_{0}
to tent spaces.

Proposition 5.2

Let
\beta>-1/2
and

Lemma

says

The extension of

in the sequel. Write

(

Proof of Theorem

(

Let

5.2.
Boundary behavior

In this section, we prove Theorem
4.2
(

) and (

). The first result is valid for arbitrary function in
T^{p}_{\beta+1}
.

Lemma 5.3
.

Let
0<p\leq\infty
and
\beta>-1/2
. Let
u
be in
T^{p}_{\beta+1}
. Then, for a.e.
x\in{\mathds{R}}^{n}
,

\lim_{t\rightarrow 0}\left(\fint_{W(t,x)}|u(s,y)|^{2}dsdy\right)^{1/2}=0.

Proof.

First consider
0<p<\infty
. For any
t>0
and
x\in{\mathds{R}}^{n}
, we have

\fint_{W(t,x)}|u(s,y)|^{2}dsdy\lesssim\fint_{t}^{2t}ds\fint_{B(x,s^{1/2})}|u(s,y)|^{2}dy\lesssim t^{2\beta+1}{\mathcal{A}}_{\beta+1;2}(u)(x)^{2},

where
{\mathcal{A}}_{\beta+1;2}
is defined in (
3.3
). Since
\|{\mathcal{A}}_{\beta+1;2}(u)\|_{p}\eqsim\|u\|_{T^{p}_{\beta+1}}<\infty
,
{\mathcal{A}}_{\beta+1}(u)(x)
is finite for a.e.
x\in{\mathds{R}}^{n}
, so the right-hand side tends to 0 when
t\to 0
as
\beta>-1/2
.

Then for
p=\infty
, note that
W(t,x)\subset(0,2t)\times B(x,(2t)^{1/2})
, so

\fint_{W(t,x)}|u(s,y)|^{2}dsdy\lesssim t^{2\beta+1}\int_{t}^{2t}ds\fint_{B(x,(2t)^{1/2})}|s^{-(\beta+1)}u(s,y)|^{2}dy.

Taking esssup on
x\in{\mathds{R}}^{n}
, we get

\underset{x\in{\mathds{R}}^{n}}{\mathrm{esssup}}\fint_{W(t,x)}|u(s,y)|^{2}dsdy\lesssim t^{2\beta+1}\|u\|_{T^{\infty}_{\beta+1}}^{2}.

This completes the proof.
∎

Lemma 5.4
.

For any
p\in[2,\infty]
and
\beta\geq-1
,
T^{p}_{\beta+1}
is contained in
L^{2}_{\loc}(\overline{{\mathds{R}}^{1+n}_{+}})
. More precisely, for ball
B\subset{\mathds{R}}^{n}
and
0<T<r(B)^{2}
,

(5.8)

\left(\int_{0}^{T}\int_{B}|u(t,y)|^{2}dtdy\right)^{1/2}\lesssim_{p}T^{\beta+1}|B|^{[2,p]}\|u\|_{T^{p}_{\beta+1}}.

Proof.

By definition, it is trivial if
p=\infty
. We thus assume
2\leq p<\infty
. For any
\alpha>0
and ball
B\subset{\mathds{R}}^{n}
, define the
tent
on
B
of
aperture

\alpha
(and
homogeneity

m=2
) as

T_{\alpha}(B):=\{(t,y)\in{\mathds{R}}^{1+n}_{+}:0<\alpha t^{1/2}\leq\dist(y,B^{c})\}.

Observe that
(0,T)\times B\subset T_{\alpha}(2B)
with
\alpha:=T^{-1/2}r(B)>1
. Moreover, for any
(t,y)\in T_{\alpha}(2B)
, since
\alpha t^{1/2}<r(B)<\dist(y,(2B)^{c})
, we know that
B(y,\alpha t^{1/2})\subset 2B
. In other words,

\int_{2B}\mathds{1}_{B(0,1)}\left(\frac{x-y}{\alpha t^{1/2}}\right)dx\eqsim(\alpha t^{1/2})^{n}.

With the help of these two observations, we get

\displaystyle\|u\|_{L^{2}((0,T)\times B)}^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim\alpha^{-n}\int_{T_{\alpha}(2B)}|u(t,y)|^{2}dt\frac{dy}{t^{n/2}}\int_{2B}\mathds{1}_{B(0,1)}\left(\frac{x-y}{\alpha t^{1/2}}\right)dx

\displaystyle\leq\alpha^{-n}\int_{2B}dx\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{B(x,\alpha t^{1/2})}|u(t,y)|^{2}dt\frac{dy}{t^{n/2}}

\displaystyle\leq T^{2(\beta+1)}\alpha^{-n}\int_{2B}{\mathcal{A}}^{(\alpha)}_{\beta+1;2}(u)(x)^{2}dx

\displaystyle\lesssim_{p}T^{2(\beta+1)}\alpha^{-n}|B|^{2[2,p]}\|{\mathcal{A}}^{(\alpha)}_{\beta+1;2}(u)\|_{p}^{2}\lesssim T^{2(\beta+1)}|B|^{2[2,p]}\|u\|_{{T^{p}_{\beta+1}}}^{2}.

The last inequality follows from the change of aperture, cf. (
3.4
).
∎

Corollary 5.5
.

Let
2\leq p\leq\infty
and
\beta\geq-1
. Let
B\subset{\mathds{R}}^{n}
be a ball and
0<T<r(B)^{2}
. For any
u\in T^{p}_{\beta+1}
,

(5.9)

\fint_{0}^{T}\|u(t)\|_{L^{2}(B)}dt\lesssim_{\beta}T^{\beta+\frac{1}{2}}|B|^{[2,p]}\|u\|_{T^{p}_{\beta+1}}.

In particular, if
\beta>-1/2
,
u(t)
tends to 0 in a Cesàro mean in
L^{2}_{\loc}({\mathds{R}}^{n})
as
t\to 0
.

Proof.

It suffices to prove (
5.9
), which is a direct consequence of Lemma
5.4
by Jensen’s inequality as

\displaystyle\fint_{0}^{T}\|u(t)\|_{L^{2}(B)}dt\leq\left(\fint_{0}^{T}\|u(t)\|_{L^{2}(B)}^{2}dt\right)^{1/2}\lesssim T^{\beta+\frac{1}{2}}|B|^{[2,p]}\|u\|_{T^{p}_{\beta+1}}.

This completes the proof.
∎

Corollary 5.6
.

Let
2\leq p\leq\infty
and
\beta\geq-1
. Let
u
be in
T^{p}_{\beta+1}
with
\partial_{t}u\in T^{p}_{\beta}
. Then, for any
\delta>0
and
t\in(0,\delta)
,

(5.10)

\|u(t)\|_{E^{\infty}_{\delta}}\lesssim\delta^{-\frac{n}{2p}}t^{\beta+\frac{1}{2}}\big(\|u\|_{T^{p}_{\beta+1}}+\|\partial_{t}u\|_{T^{p}_{\beta}}\big).

Proof.

Fix a ball
B\subset{\mathds{R}}^{n}
. For any
T>0
, by (
3.1
), both of
u
and
\partial_{t}u
belong to
L^{2}((T/8,2T)\times B)
. Thus, for any
T^{\prime}\in[T/4,T/2]
, we have

u(T)=u(T^{\prime})+\int_{T^{\prime}}^{T}\partial_{t}u(t)dt

valued in
L^{2}(B)
. Taking average with respect to
T^{\prime}
, we get

u(T)=\fint_{T/4}^{T/2}u(T^{\prime})dT^{\prime}+\fint_{T/4}^{T/2}dT^{\prime}\int_{T^{\prime}}^{T}\partial_{t}u(t)dt.

Since
T/4\leq T^{\prime}\leq\min\{t,T/2\}
, Fubini’s theorem yields

\|u(T)\|_{L^{2}(B)}\lesssim\fint_{T/4}^{T/2}\|u(T^{\prime})\|_{L^{2}(B)}dT^{\prime}+\fint_{T/4}^{T}t\|\partial_{t}u(t)\|_{L^{2}(B)}dt.

Note that both of
u
and
t\partial_{t}u
lie in
T^{p}_{\beta+1}
. Using Corollary
5.5
, we deduce

\|u(t)\|_{E^{\infty}_{\delta}}\eqsim\delta^{-\frac{n}{2}}\sup_{B}\|u(t)\|_{L^{2}(B)}\lesssim\delta^{-\frac{n}{2p}}t^{\beta+{\frac{1}{2}}}\big(\|u\|_{T^{p}_{\beta+1}}+\|\partial_{t}u\|_{T^{p}_{\beta}}\big),

where
B
describes all balls in
{\mathds{R}}^{n}
with
r(B)=\delta^{1/2}
.
∎

We may finish the proof of Theorem
4.2
.

Proof of Theorem
4.2
(

) and (
e
).

Statement (

) is clear by Lemma
5.3
. For (

), first note that the same deduction as for (
4.18
) implies

(5.11)

\|u(t)\|_{E^{p}_{t/16}}\lesssim t^{\beta+\frac{1}{2}}(\|u\|_{T^{p}_{\beta+1}}+\|f\|_{T^{p}_{\beta}}).

Then the discussion is divided into two cases.

Case 1:
p_{L}(\beta)<p\leq 2

Observe that

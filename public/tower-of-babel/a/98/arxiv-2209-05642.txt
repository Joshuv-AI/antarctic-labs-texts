Nonlinear variable exponent Picone identity and p ⁡ ( x ) p(x) -sub-Laplacian first eigenvalue for general vector fields
Abimbola Abolarinwa

Abstract
. In this paper, we establish a new generalized nonlinear variable exponent Picone identities for p ⁡ ( x ) p(x) -sub-Laplacian. As applications we prove uniqueness, simplicity, monotonicity and isolatedness of the first nontrivial Dirichlet eigenvalue of p ⁡ ( x ) p(x) -sub-Laplacian with respect to the general vector fields. Further applications yield Hardy type inequalities and Caccioppoli estimates with variable exponents.

1.
Introduction

This paper is concerned with variable exponent Picone identity in the context of sub-Riemannian geometry. We derive a nonlinear Picone identity which allows us to study some qualitative properties of the principal eigenvalue of
p(x)
-sub-Laplacian with respect to the general vector fields on smooth manifolds. As by-products, we also derive Hardy type inequalities and Caccioppoli estimates with variable exponents. These results are appearing for the first time, even in the Euclidean setting. In recent years, several authors have devoted their researches towards the study of variable exponent elliptic equations and systems with
p(x)
-growth condition in Euclidean setting with many interesting results
[
6
,
9
,
12
,
14
,
18
,
22
]
. Models involving
p(x)
-growth condition arise from physical processes such as nonlinear elasticity theory, electrorheological fluids, image processing, etc
[
1
,
2
,
28
]
. It has been observed that
p(x)
-Laplacian is similar in many respect to the classical
p
-Laplacian (
p
-constant) but it lacks certain vital properties such as homogeneity. This therefore makes the nonlinearity so much complicated and many of known approaches to
p
-Laplacian can no longer hold for
p(x)
-Laplacian. It is interesting to consider
p(x)
-Laplacian in the sub-elliptic setting and investigate which of the known results for
p
-constant hold for variable exponents.

Let
M
be an
n
-dimensional smooth manifold equipped with a volume form
dx
and
\{X_{k}\}_{k=1}^{N}
,
n\geq N
, be a family of vector fields defined on
M
. Consider the operator

\mathscr{L}_{X}:=\sum_{k=1}^{N}X^{*}_{k}X_{k},

which is a second-order differential operator usually called canonical sub-Laplacian. This operator is related to the operator for the sum of squares of vector fields and it is well known to be locally hypoelliptic if the commutators of the vector fields
\{X_{k}\}_{k=1}^{N}
generate the tangent space of
M
as the Lie algebra, due to Hörmander’s pioneering work
[
19
]
. We denote the horizontal gradients for general vector fields by

\nabla_{X}=(X_{1},\cdots,X_{N})\ \ \ \text{and}\ \ \ \nabla_{X}^{*}=(X_{1}^{*},\cdots,X_{N}^{*}),

where
X_{k}
and its formal adjoint
X_{k}^{*}
are respectively given by

X_{k}=\sum_{j=1}^{n}a_{kj}(x)\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{j}}\ \ \text{and}\ \ X_{k}^{*}=-\sum_{j=1}^{n}\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{j}}(a_{kj}(x)),\ \ k=1,\cdots,N.

There are numbers of examples of sub-manifolds where vector fields can be defined. For examples, we list among others, the Carnot groups, Heisenberg groups, Engel groups, and Grushin plane (which does not even posses a group structure). Interested readers can see the book
[
24
]
for more examples and detail discussions on the sub-Laplacian and its various extensions in each case. In the case
M=\mathbb{R}^{n}
, then
dx
is the Lebesgue measure,
\nabla_{X}=\nabla
and
\mathscr{L}_{X}=\Delta
are the usual Euclidean gradient and Laplacian, respectively.

Let
p:\bar{\Omega}\to\mathbb{R}
be a continuous function and
p(x)>1
for
x\in\bar{\Omega}\subset M
. We define the
p(x)
-sub-Laplacian for general vector fields on

by the formula

\mathscr{L}_{p}u:=\nabla^{*}_{X}(|\nabla_{X}u|^{p(x)-2}\nabla_{X}u),

where

is a smooth function. If
p(x)=p
(

=constant), the operator
\mathscr{L}_{p}u
becomes the
p
-sub-Laplacian,
\nabla^{*}_{X}(|\nabla_{X}u|^{p-2}\nabla_{X}u)
and

stands for the Euclidean length of

.

-sub-Laplacian on

,

,

\displaystyle\left.\begin{array}[]{ll}-\nabla^{*}_{X}(|\nabla_{X}u|^{p(x)-2}\nabla_{X}u)=\lambda g(x)|u|^{p(x)-2}u,&\ x\in\Omega,\\
\ \ \ u>0,&\ x\in\Omega,\\
\ \ \ u=0,&\ x\in\partial\Omega,\end{array}\right.

and discuss some properties of the eigenvalue

and the corresponding eigenfunction

in certain Sobolev spaces with variable exponents
[

which grows to

, the authors studied (

\lambda_{1,p}=\inf_{u\neq 0}\frac{\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u|^{p(x)}dx}{\int_{\Omega}|u|^{p(x)}dx},\ \ \ p(x)>1,

to be zero and positive, respectively. The properties that

is very useful in analysis and applications. Motivated by

Picone identity is a very useful tool in the study of qualitative properties of solutions of differential equations, and for this, several linear and nonlinear Picone type identities have been derived to handle differential equations of various type. Picone identity was originally developed by Mauro Picone in 1910 to prove Sturm Comparison principle and oscillation theory for a system of differential equations. This identity was later extended to partial differential equation involving Laplacian by Allegretto
[
3
]
and
p
-Laplacian by Allegretto and Huang
[
4
]
to establish among others, existence and nonexistence of positive solutions, Sturmian comparison principle, Liouville type theorems, Hardy inequalities and some profound results involving
p
-Laplace equations and systems. Precisely, Allegretto
[
3
]
proved that, for nonnegative differentiable functions
u
and
v
with
v\neq 0
, the following formula

(1.4)

\displaystyle|\nabla u|^{2}+\frac{u^{2}}{v^{2}}|\nabla v|^{2}-2\frac{u}{v}\nabla u\nabla v=|\nabla u|^{2}-\nabla\left(\frac{u^{2}}{v}\right)\nabla v\geq 0

holds. Allegretto and Huang
[
4
]
extended (
1.4
) to handle
p
-Laplace equations and eigenvalue problems involving
p
-Laplacian. Their identity reads as follows, for
u\geq 0
,
v>0
, then

(1.5)

\displaystyle|\nabla u|^{p}+(p-1)\frac{u^{p}}{v^{p}}|\nabla v|^{p}

\displaystyle-p\frac{u^{p-1}}{v^{p-1}}|\nabla v|^{p-2}\nabla v\nabla u=R_{p}(u,v),

where

\displaystyle R_{p}(u,v):=|\nabla u|^{p}-\nabla\left(\frac{u^{p}}{v^{p-1}}\right)|\nabla v|^{p-2}\nabla v\geq 0.

Several extensions and generalization of Picone identity have been established in order to handle more general elliptic operators. Tyagi
[
34
]
and Bal
[
7
]
established nonlinear versions of (
1.4
) and its

-Laplace analogue (
1.5
), respectively, with several applications, (see also
[

,

,
33
]
). For other interesting extension of Picone type identities one can find

,

]
(for Finsler

-Laplacian with application to Caccioppoli inequality),
[

,

,

]
(for general vector fields and

-sub-Laplacian with applications to Grushin plane, Heisenberg group, Stratified Lie groups),
[

(for

[

(for nonlinear Picone identities for anisotropic

Allegretto
[
5

established variable exponent Picone type identity for differentiable functions

,

and continuous

as follows:

\displaystyle\frac{|\nabla u|^{p(x)}}{p(x)}

\displaystyle-\nabla\left[\frac{u^{p(x)}}{p(x)v^{p(x)-1}}\right]|\nabla v|^{p(x)-2}\nabla v

(1.6)

\displaystyle=\frac{|\nabla u|^{p(x)}}{p(x)}-\left(\frac{u}{v}\right)^{p(x)-1}|\nabla v|^{p(x)-2}\nabla v\nabla u+\frac{p(x)-1}{p(x)}\left(\frac{u}{v}|\nabla v|\right)^{p(x)}

\displaystyle\hskip 28.45274pt+\frac{1}{p(x)}\frac{u^{p(x)}}{v^{p(x)-1}}|\nabla v|^{p(x)-2}\left[\frac{1}{p(x)}-\ln\left(\frac{u}{v}\right)\right]\nabla v\nabla p(x)\geq 0

on the assumption that
\nabla v\nabla p(x)=0
. He used the inequality to prove Barta theorem and some other results. Later, Yoshida
[
30
]
(see also
[

,
32
]
) established similar Picone identities for quasilinear and half-linear elliptic equations involving
p(x)
-Laplacian and pseudo

-Laplacian, and consequently developed Sturmian comparison theory. Most recently, Feng and Han
[

]
, motivated by Allegretto
[

]
proved a modified form of (

) and showed that

(1.7)

\displaystyle|\nabla u|^{p(x)}-\nabla\left(\frac{u^{p(x)}}{v^{p(x)-1}}\right)|\nabla v|^{p(x)-2}\nabla v\geq 0

if
\nabla v\nabla p(x)=0
a.e in
\Omega
, with equality if and only if
\nabla(u/v)=0
in
\Omega
. They proved monotonicity of principal eigenvalue
\lambda_{1,p}
and a variable exponent Barta inequality for

-Laplacian in the form

\displaystyle\lambda_{1,p}\geq\inf_{x\in\Omega}\left[\frac{\Delta_{p}v}{v^{p(x)-1}}\right],\ \ \ \Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n},

where
\Delta_{p}:=-\nabla(|\nabla v|^{p(x)-2}\nabla v)
, on the assumption that
\nabla v\nabla p(x)=0
.

In this paper, we derive new generalized variable exponent Picone type identities for general vector fields in the sub-Riemannian settings. The derived generalized identity contains some known identities in various setting as will be discussed in Section

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\phi^{p(x)}|\nabla_{X}v|^{p(x)}dx\leq(p^{+})^{p^{+}}\int_{\Omega}v^{p(x)}|\nabla_{X}\phi|^{p(x)}dx

for every nonnegative test function
\phi\in C^{\infty}_{0}(\Omega)
, where

is a sub-solution in
\Omega\subset M
and
p^{+}:=ess\sup p(x)
.

2.
Nonlinear variable exponent Picone identity

Here we give the statement and the proof of the nonlinear Picone identity with variable exponent, which is the main result of this section. First, we state some hypotheses as adopted in this section (and ofcourse throughout the paper) and Young’s inequality in the forms that will be applied here and later.

Let
M
be an
n
-dimensional smooth manifold and
\Omega
any domain in
M
,
p(x)>1
is a continuous function on
\bar{\Omega}
,
p^{\prime}(x)=1/(p(x)-1)
is Hölder conjugate to
p(x)
.

Lemma 2.1
.

(Classical Young’s inequality)
Let
s\geq 0
,
t\geq 0
, and
p(x)>1
such that
1/p(x)+1/p^{\prime}(x)=1
. There holds the inequality

(2.1)

\displaystyle st\leq\frac{s^{p(x)}}{p(x)}+\frac{t^{p^{\prime}(x)}}{p^{\prime}(x)}

with equality if and only if
s^{p(x)}=t^{p^{\prime}(x)}
.

Inequality (
2.1
) is the classical Young’s inequality which can be varied in the following form.

Lemma 2.2
.

(Modified Young’s inequality)
Let
\Phi(x),\Psi(x)\geq 0
,
p(x)>1
such that
1/p(x)+1/p^{\prime}(x)=1
and
\varepsilon:\Omega\to\mathbb{R}^{+}
be a continuous and bounded function. There holds the inequality

(2.2)

\displaystyle\Phi\Psi^{p(x)-1}\leq\frac{\Phi^{p(x)}}{p(x)\varepsilon(x)^{p(x)-1}}+\frac{p(x)-1}{p(x)}\varepsilon(x)\Psi^{p(x)}

for a.e.
x\in\Omega
.

Proof.

Applying the classical Young’s inequality (
2.1
) with

s=\frac{\Phi}{\varepsilon(x)^{\frac{p(x)}{p(x)-1}}}\ \ \ \text{and}\ \ \ t=\left(\Psi\varepsilon(x)^{\frac{1}{p(x)}}\right)^{p(x)-1},

we have

\displaystyle\Phi\Psi^{p(x)-1}

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{\Phi}{\varepsilon(x)^{\frac{p(x)}{p(x)-1}}}\right)\left(\Psi\varepsilon(x)^{\frac{1}{p(x)}}\right)^{p(x)-1}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\Phi^{p(x)}}{p(x)\varepsilon(x)^{p(x)-1}}+\frac{p(x)-1}{p(x)}\left(\Psi\varepsilon(x)^{\frac{1}{p(x)}}\right)^{p(x)}.

∎

The next is the variable exponent Picone identity.

Theorem 2.3
.

Let
u\geq 0
and
v>0
be nonconstant differentiable functions a.e. in
\Omega
. Suppose
p:\bar{\Omega}\to(0,\infty)
is a
C^{1}
-function for
p(x)>1
, and
f:(0,\infty)\to(0,\infty)
is a
C^{1}
-function satisfying
f(y)>0
and
f^{\prime}(y)\geq(p(x)-1)\left[f(y)^{\frac{p(x)-2}{p(x)-1}}\right]
for
y>0
. Define

\displaystyle L(u,v)

\displaystyle=|\nabla_{X}u|^{p(x)}-\frac{u^{p(x)}\ln u}{f(v)}|\nabla_{X}v|^{p(x)-2}\nabla_{X}v\nabla_{X}p(x)

(2.3)

\displaystyle-p(x)\frac{u^{p(x)-1}}{f(v)}|\nabla_{X}v|^{p(x)-2}\nabla_{X}v\nabla_{X}u+\frac{u^{p(x)}f^{\prime}(v)}{(f(v))^{2}}|\nabla_{X}v|^{p(x)}

and

(2.4)

\displaystyle R(u,v)=|\nabla_{X}u|^{p(x)}-\nabla_{X}\left(\frac{u^{p(x)}}{f(v)}\right)|\nabla_{X}v|^{p(x)-2}\nabla_{X}v.

Then

(1)

L(u,v)=R(u,v)
.

(2)

Moreover
L(u,v)\geq 0
if
\nabla_{X}v\nabla_{X}p(x)\equiv 0
.

(3)

Furthermore,
L(u,v)=0
a.e. in
\Omega
if and only if
\nabla_{X}(u/v)=0
a.e. in
\Omega
.

Proof.

By direct computation we have

\displaystyle R(u,v)

\displaystyle=|\nabla_{X}u|^{p(x)}-\left(\frac{\nabla_{X}(u^{p(x)})}{f(v)}-\frac{u^{p(x)}\nabla_{X}(f(v))}{(f(v))^{2}}\right)|\nabla_{X}v|^{p(x)-2}\nabla_{X}v

\displaystyle=|\nabla_{X}u|^{p(x)}-\frac{u^{p(x)}\ln u\nabla_{X}p(x)+p(x)u^{p(x)-1}\nabla_{X}u}{f(v)}|\nabla_{X}v|^{p(x)-2}\nabla_{X}v

\displaystyle\hskip 56.9055pt+\frac{u^{p(x)}f^{\prime}(v)}{(f(v))^{2}}|\nabla_{X}v|^{p(x)}

\displaystyle=L(u,v),

which proves
(1)
of the theorem.

Next we verify
L(u,v)\geq 0
. Rewriting the expression for
L(u,v)
as follows

\displaystyle L(u,v)

\displaystyle=|\nabla_{X}u|^{p(x)}-p(x)\frac{u^{p(x)-1}}{f(v)}|\nabla_{X}v|^{p(x)-1}|\nabla_{X}u|+\frac{u^{p(x)}f^{\prime}(v)}{(f(v))^{2}}|\nabla_{X}v|^{p(x)}

\displaystyle\ \ \ +p(x)\frac{u^{p(x)-1}}{f(v)}\left(|\nabla_{X}v||\nabla_{X}u|-\nabla_{X}v\nabla u\right)-\frac{u^{p(x)}\ln u}{f(v)}|\nabla_{X}v|^{p(x)-2}\nabla_{X}v\nabla_{X}p(x)

\displaystyle=p(x)\left(\frac{|\nabla_{X}u|^{p(x)}}{p(x)}+\frac{p(x)-1}{p(x)}\left[\frac{(u|\nabla_{X}v|)^{p(x)-1}}{f(v)}\right]^{\frac{p(x)}{p(x)-1}}\right)+\frac{u^{p(x)}f^{\prime}(v)}{(f(v))^{2}}|\nabla_{X}v|^{p(x)}

\displaystyle\ \ \ -(p(x)-1)\left[\frac{(u|\nabla_{X}v|)^{p(x)-1}}{f(v)}\right]^{\frac{p(x)}{p(x)-1}}-p(x)\frac{u^{p(x)-1}}{f(v)}|\nabla_{X}v|^{p(x)-1}|\nabla_{X}u|

\displaystyle\ \ \ +p(x)\frac{u^{p(x)-1}}{f(v)}\left(|\nabla_{X}v||\nabla_{X}u|-\nabla_{X}v\nabla u\right)-\frac{u^{p(x)}\ln u}{f(v)}|\nabla_{X}v|^{p(x)-2}\nabla_{X}v\nabla_{X}p(x)

\displaystyle=L_{1}(u,v)\ \ +\ \ L_{2}(u,v)\ \ +\ \ L_{3}(u,v)\ \ +\ \ L_{4}(u,v),

where

\displaystyle L_{1}(u,v)

\displaystyle:=p(x)\left(\frac{|\nabla_{X}u|^{p(x)}}{p(x)}+\frac{p(x)-1}{p(x)}\left[\frac{(u|\nabla_{X}v|)^{p(x)-1}}{f(v)}\right]^{\frac{p(x)}{p(x)-1}}\right)

\displaystyle\hskip 85.35826pt-p(x)\frac{u^{p(x)-1}}{f(v)}|\nabla_{X}v|^{p(x)-1}|\nabla_{X}u|,

\displaystyle L_{2}(u,v)

\displaystyle:=\frac{u^{p(x)}f^{\prime}(v)}{(f(v))^{2}}|\nabla_{X}v|^{p(x)}-(p(x)-1)\left[\frac{(u|\nabla_{X}v|)^{p(x)-1}}{f(v)}\right]^{\frac{p(x)}{p(x)-1}},

\displaystyle L_{3}(u,v)

\displaystyle:=p(x)\frac{u^{p(x)-1}}{f(v)}\left(|\nabla_{X}v||\nabla_{X}u|-\nabla_{X}v\nabla u\right),

\displaystyle L_{4}(u,v)

\displaystyle:=-\frac{u^{p(x)}\ln u}{f(v)}|\nabla_{X}v|^{p(x)-2}\nabla_{X}v\nabla_{X}p(x).

Applying the Young’s inequality (
2.1
), choosing
s=|\nabla_{X}u|
and

\displaystyle t=\frac{(u|\nabla_{X}v|)^{p(x)-1}}{f(v)}
, we obtain

\displaystyle p(x)\frac{u^{p(x)-1}}{f(v)}

\displaystyle|\nabla_{X}v|^{p(x)-1}|\nabla_{X}u|

\displaystyle\leq p(x)\left(\frac{|\nabla_{X}u|^{p(x)}}{p(x)}+\frac{p(x)-1}{p(x)}\left[\frac{(u|\nabla_{X}v|)^{p(x)-1}}{f(v)}\right]^{\frac{p(x)}{p(x)-1}}\right),

implying that
L_{1}(u,v)\geq 0
with equality if and only if there is equality in the Young’s inequality, that is,
\Phi=\Psi^{\frac{1}{p(x)-1}}
.

Applying the assumption
f^{\prime}(y)\geq(p(x)-1)\left[f(y)^{\frac{p(x)-2}{p(x)-1}}\right]
, we have

\displaystyle\frac{u^{p(x)}f^{\prime}(v)}{(f(v))^{2}}|\nabla_{X}v|^{p(x)}\geq(p(x)-1)\left[\frac{(u|\nabla_{X}v|)^{p(x)-1}}{f(v)}\right]^{\frac{p(x)}{p(x)-1}},

which implies that
L_{2}(u,v)\geq 0
with equality if and only if

f^{\prime}(y)=(p(x)-1)\left[f(y)^{\frac{p(x)-2}{p(x)-1}}\right]
. Clearly,
L_{3}(u,v)\geq 0
by reverting to the inequality
|\nabla_{X}v||\nabla_{X}u|-\nabla_{X}v\nabla_{X}u\geq 0
. By the virtue of the assumption that
\nabla_{X}v\nabla_{X}p(x)\equiv 0
, we have also
L_{4}(u,v)\equiv 0
. Putting all of these together we obtain that
L(u,v)\geq 0
a.e. in
\Omega
.

Observe that
L(u,v)=0
holds if and only if

(2.5)

\displaystyle|\nabla_{X}u|=\frac{u}{f(v)^{\frac{1}{p(x)-1}}}|\nabla_{X}v|,

(2.6)

\displaystyle f^{\prime}(y)=(p(x)-1)\left[f(y)^{\frac{p(x)-2}{p(x)-1}}\right],

and

(2.7)

\displaystyle|\nabla_{X}v||\nabla_{X}u|=\nabla_{X}v\nabla_{X}u.

Upon solving for (
2.6
) we get
f(v)=v^{p(x)-1}
. If
\nabla_{X}(u/v)=0
then there exists a positive constant, say
\alpha>0
such that
u=\alpha v
, then equality (
2.7
) holds. Combining
f(v)=v^{p(x)-1}
and
u=\alpha v
, then (
2.5
) holds.
We can now conclude that
L(u,v)=0
implies
\nabla_{X}(u/v)=0
. Indeed, if
L(u,v)(x_{0})=0
,
x_{0}\in\Omega
, there are two cases to consider, namely; the case
u(x_{0})\neq 0
and the case
u(x_{0})=0
.

(a) If
u(x_{0})\neq 0
, then
L(u,v)=0
for all
x_{0}\in\Omega
, that is,
L_{1}(u,v)=0
,
L_{2}(u,v)=0
and
L_{3}(u,v)=0
, and we conclude that (
2.5
), (
2.6
) and (
2.7
) hold, which when combined gives
u=\alpha v
a.e. for some constant
\alpha>0
and
\nabla_{X}(u/v)=0
for all
x_{0}\in\Omega
.

(b) If
u(x_{0})=0
, we denote
\Omega^{*}=\{x\in\Omega:u(x)=0\}
, and suppose
\Omega^{*}\neq\Omega
. Here
u(x_{0})=\alpha v(x_{0})
implies
\alpha=0
since
u(x_{0})=0
and
v(x_{0})>0
. By the first case (Case (a)) we know that
u(x)=\alpha v(x)
and
u(x)\neq 0
for all
x\in\Omega\setminus\Omega^{*}
, then it is impossible that
\alpha=0
. This contradiction implies that
\Omega^{*}=\Omega
.

∎

3.
Applications

In order to discuss generalized solutions, we need some concepts from the theory of variable Lebesgue and Sobolev spaces. Detailed description of these spaces can be found in

,

.

Variable Lebesgue spaces

Let
\Omega\subset M
be an open domain and
E(\Omega)
denotes the set of all equivalence classes of measurable real-valued functions defined on
\Omega
being equal almost everywhere.

Definition 3.1
.

The variable exponent Lebesgue space
L^{p(\cdot)}(\Omega)
is defined as

L^{p(\cdot)}(\Omega)=\left\{u\in E(\Omega):\int_{\Omega}|u(x)|^{p(\cdot)}dx<\infty\right\}

equipped with the (Luxemburg) norm

\|u\|_{L^{p(\cdot)}(\Omega)}=\inf\left\{t>0:\int_{\Omega}\left|\frac{u(x)}{t}\right|^{p(x)}dx\leq 1\right\}.

The variable exponent Sobolev space
W^{1,p(\cdot)}(\Omega)
is defined as

W^{1,p(\cdot)}(\Omega)=\{u\in L^{p(\cdot)}(\Omega):\nabla_{X}u\in L^{p(\cdot)}(\Omega)\}

equipped with the norm

\|u\|_{W^{1,p(\cdot)}(\Omega)}=\|u\|_{L^{p(\cdot)}(\Omega)}+\|\nabla_{X}u\|_{L^{p(\cdot)}(\Omega)}.

Denoted by
W_{0}^{1,p(\cdot)}(\Omega)
the closure of
C^{\infty}_{0}(\Omega)
in
W^{1,p(\cdot)}(\Omega)
with respect to the norm

\|u\|_{W_{0}^{1,p(\cdot)}(\Omega)}=\|\nabla_{X}u\|_{L^{p(\cdot)}(\Omega)}.

It can be clearly seen that
L^{p(\cdot)}(\Omega)
,
W^{1,p(\cdot)}(\Omega)
and
W_{0}^{1,p(\cdot)}(\Omega)
are all separable and reflexive Banach spaces in their respectful norms if
1<\inf p(x)<\sup p(x)<\infty
in
\Omega
.

Eigenvalue problem for
p(x)
-Laplacian

Let
\Omega\subset M
be a bounded domain with smooth boundary
\partial\Omega
. We suppose a continuous function
p:\bar{\Omega}\to\mathbb{R}^{+}
,
p(x)>1
is such that

1<p^{-}:=ess\inf_{x\in\bar{\Omega}}p(x)\leq p(x)\leq p^{+}:=ess\sup_{x\in\bar{\Omega}}p(x)<\infty.

Now consider the indefinite weighted Dirichlet eigenvalue problem for
p(x)
-Laplacian

\displaystyle\left.\begin{array}[]{ll}-\nabla^{*}_{X}(|\nabla_{X}u|^{p(x)-2}\nabla_{X}u)=\lambda g(x)|u|^{p(x)-2}u,&\ x\in\Omega,\\
\ \ \ u>0,&\ x\in\Omega,\\
\ \ \ u=0,&\ x\in\partial\Omega,\end{array}\right.

where
\Omega
is as defined above,
g(x)
is a positive bounded function and
p:\bar{\Omega}\to(1,\infty)
is a continuous function for
x\in\bar{\Omega}
.

Definition 3.2
.

Let
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}^{+}
and
u\in W_{0}^{1,p(x)}(\Omega)
, the pair
(u,\lambda)
is called a solution of (
3
) if

(3.4)

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}|\nabla_{X}u|^{p(x)-2}\langle\nabla_{X}u,\nabla_{X}\phi\rangle dx-\lambda\int_{\Omega}g(x)|u|^{p(x)-2}u\phi dx=0

for all
\phi\in W_{0}^{1,p(x)}(\Omega)
.
If
(u,\lambda)
is a solution of (
3
), we call
\lambda
an eigenvalue, and
u
an eigenfunction corresponding to
\lambda
.

Similarly, by the sup-solution and sub-solution of (
3
), we mean the pair
(u,\lambda)
such that

(3.5)

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}|\nabla_{X}u|^{p(x)-2}\langle\nabla_{X}u,\nabla_{X}\phi\rangle dx-\lambda\int_{\Omega}g(x)|u|^{p(x)-2}u\phi dx\geq 0

and

(3.6)

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}|\nabla_{X}u|^{p(x)-2}\langle\nabla_{X}u,\nabla_{X}\phi\rangle dx-\lambda\int_{\Omega}g(x)|u|^{p(x)-2}u\phi dx\leq 0

for all
\phi\in W_{0}^{1,p(x)}(\Omega)
, respectively.

Denote the principal eigenvalue of (
3
) (the least positive eigenvalue) by
\lambda_{1,p}:=\lambda_{1,p}(\Omega)
, clearly for the solution
(u,\lambda)
and
u\neq 0
, we get

\lambda_{1,p}=\inf_{u\in\in W_{0}^{1,p(x)}(\Omega)\setminus\{0\}}\frac{\int_{\Omega}|\nabla_{X}u|^{p(x)}dx}{\int_{\Omega}g(x)|u|^{p(x)}dx}.

In the case
p(x)=p
(constant), it is well known that
\lambda_{1,p}(\Omega)
given above is the first eigenvalue of
p
-Laplacian (with
g(x)=1
,
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
), which must be positive. But this is not true for general
p(x)
in the sense that
\lambda_{1,p}
may be zero
[
13
]
. Nevertheless, Fan, Zhang and Zhao in
[
14
]
have proved the existence of infinitely many eigenvalues
p(x)
-Laplacian and established sufficient conditions for
\lambda_{1,p}(\Omega)>0
(see also Franzina and Lindqvist
[
17
]
). Motivated by
[
14
]
, we are able to assume the existence of
\lambda_{1,p}>0
in the rest of this section.

3.1.
Variable exponent Hardy type inequality

Proposition 3.3
.

Let
\Omega\subset M
be an open bounded domain. Suppose that a function
v\in C^{\infty}_{0}(\Omega)
satisfies
\nabla_{X}v\nabla_{X}p(x)\equiv 0
and

\displaystyle\left.\begin{array}[]{ll}-\mathscr{L}_{p}v=\mu a(x)f(v)&\ \ \text{in}\ \Omega,\\
\ \ \ u>0&\ \ \text{in}\ \Omega,\\
\ \ \ u=0&\ \ \text{on}\ \partial\Omega,\end{array}\right.

where
f:\mathbb{R}^{+}\to\mathbb{R}^{+}
is
C^{1}
and satisfies
f^{\prime}(y)\geq(p(x)-1)\left[f(y)^{\frac{p(x)-2}{p(x)-1}}\right]
,
\mu>0
is a constant,
a(x)
is a positive continuous function. Then there holds

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}|\nabla_{X}u|^{p(x)}dx\geq\mu\int_{\Omega}a(x)|u|^{p(x)}dx

for any
0\leq u\in C^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
.

Proof.

Since
v>0
and solves (
3.3
) in
\Omega
, that is,
v\in W_{0}^{1,p(x)}(\Omega)
. For a given a
\epsilon>0
, we set
\phi=\frac{|u|^{p(x)}}{f(v+\epsilon)}
. By the definition of solution (
3.4
) we compute

\displaystyle\mu\int_{\Omega}a(x)f(v)\frac{|u|^{p(x)}}{f(v+\epsilon)}dx

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\Omega}|\nabla_{X}v|^{p-2}\nabla_{X}v\nabla_{X}\left(\frac{|u|^{p(x)}}{f(v+\epsilon)}\right)dx

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}\left[|\nabla_{X}u|^{p(x)}-R(u,v+\epsilon)\right]dx

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}|\nabla_{X}u|^{p(x)}dx-\int_{\Omega}L(u,v+\epsilon)dx.

Taking the limit as
\epsilon\to 0^{+}
, applying Fatou’s Lemma and Lebesgue dominated convergence theorem respectively on the left hand side and right hand side of the last expression, we obtain

\displaystyle 0\leq\int_{\Omega}|\nabla_{X}u|^{p(x)}-\mu\int_{\Omega}a(x)|u|^{p(x)}dx-\int_{\Omega}L(u,v)dx.

Therefore we have

\displaystyle 0\leq\int_{\Omega}|\nabla_{X}u|^{p(x)}dx-\mu\int_{\Omega}a(x)|u|^{p(x)}dx

since
L(u,v)\geq 0
almost everywhere in
\Omega
. This therefore completes the proof.
∎

Corollary 3.4
.

Suppose there exists
\lambda>0
and a strictly positive sup-solution of (
3
). Then

(3.10)

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}|\nabla_{X}u|^{p(x)}dx\geq\lambda\int_{\Omega}g(x)|u|^{p(x)}dx

for all
u\in W_{0}^{1,p(x)}(\Omega)
.

Proof.

Applying Proposition
3.3
by setting
a(x)\equiv g(x)
,
\mu=\lambda
and
f(v)=|v|^{p(x)-2}v
, then one arrives at the conclusion (
3.3
) at once.
∎

3.2.
Principal frequency and domain monotonicity

Proposition 3.5
.

Let there exists
\lambda
and a strictly positive sup-solution
v\in W_{0}^{1,p(x)}(\Omega)
of (
3
). Then we have

(3.11)

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}|\nabla_{X}u|^{p(x)}dx\geq\lambda\int_{\Omega}g(x)|u|^{p(x)}dx

and

(3.12)

\displaystyle\lambda_{1,p}(\Omega)\geq\lambda

for all
u\in W_{0}^{1,p(x)}(\Omega)
.

Proof.

Suppose there exists
\lambda>0
, since
v
is strictly positive sup-solution of (
3
) in
\Omega
, we have

(3.13)

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}|\nabla_{X}v|^{p(x)-2}\langle\nabla_{X}v,\nabla_{X}\phi\rangle dx\geq\lambda\int_{\Omega}g(x)|v|^{p(x)-2}v\phi dx

for all
\phi\in W_{0}^{1,p(x)}(\Omega)
. For a given small
\epsilon>0
, setting
\phi=\frac{|u|^{p(x)}}{(v+\epsilon)^{p(x)-1}}
into (
3.13
). Then, following the proof of the Proposition
3.3
, we arrive at (
3.11
).

Now, let
u_{1}\in W_{0}^{1,p(x)}(\Omega)
be the eigenfunction corresponding to the principal eigenvalue
\lambda_{1,p}(\Omega)
. We have

(3.14)

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}|\nabla_{X}u_{1}|^{p(x)-2}\langle\nabla_{X}u_{1},\nabla_{X}\phi\rangle dx=\lambda_{1,p}\int_{\Omega}g(x)|u_{1}|^{p(x)-2}u_{1}\phi dx

for any
\phi\in W_{0}^{1,p(x)}(\Omega)
.
Choosing
\epsilon>0
(small) we can define via Picone identity that

(3.15)

\displaystyle 0\leq L(u_{1},v+\epsilon)=R(u_{1},v+\epsilon),\ \ v>0.

Integrating (
3.15
) over
\Omega
and then using (
3.13
) with
\displaystyle\phi=\frac{|u_{1}|^{p}}{f(v+\epsilon)}
and (
3.14
) with
\phi=u_{1}
, we obtain

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\Omega}L(u_{1},v+\epsilon)dx=\int_{\Omega}R(u_{1},v+\epsilon)dx

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}|\nabla_{X}u_{1}|^{p(x)}dx-\int_{\Omega}\nabla_{X}\left(\frac{|u_{1}|^{p}}{f(v+\epsilon)}\right)|\nabla_{X}v|^{p(x)-2}\nabla_{X}vdx

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}|\nabla_{X}u_{1}|^{p(x)}dx+\int_{\Omega}\frac{|u_{1}|^{p}}{f(v+\epsilon)}\nabla_{X}^{*}(|\nabla_{X}v|^{p(x)-2}\nabla_{X})vdx

\displaystyle\leq\lambda_{1,p}(\Omega)\int_{\Omega}g(x)|u_{1}|^{p(x)}dx-\lambda\int_{\Omega}g(x)\frac{|u_{1}|^{p}}{f(v+\epsilon)}|v|^{p(x)-2}vdx.

As usual, taking the limit as
\epsilon\to 0^{+}
, applying Fatou’s Lemma and Lebesgue dominated convergence theorem, setting
f(v)=v^{p(x)-1}
, we arrive at

0\leq(\lambda_{1,p}(\Omega)-\lambda)\int_{\Omega}g(x)|u_{1}|^{p}dx,

which implies
\lambda_{1,p}(\Omega)\geq\lambda
.
∎

As a corollary to the last proposition, we show strict monotonicity of the principal eigenvalue with respect to domain monotonicity.

Corollary 3.6
.

Let
\lambda_{1,p}(\Omega)>0
be the principal eigenvalue of
\mathscr{L}_{p}
on
\Omega
. Suppose
\Omega_{1}\subset\Omega_{2}\subset\Omega
and
\Omega_{1}\neq\Omega_{2}
. Then

\displaystyle\lambda_{1,p}(\Omega_{1})>\lambda_{1,p}(\Omega_{2})

if they both exist.

Proof.

Let
u_{1}
and
u_{2}
be positive eigenfunctions corresponding to
\lambda_{1,p}(\Omega_{1})
and
\lambda_{1,p}(\Omega_{2})
, respectively. Clearly with
\phi\in C^{\infty}_{0}(\Omega)
, we have by Picone identity that

0\leq\int_{\Omega}L(\phi,u_{2})dx=\int_{\Omega}R(\phi,u_{2})dx.

Replacing
\phi
by
u_{1}
and applying Proposition
3.5
we have

\lambda_{1,p}(\Omega_{1})-\lambda_{1,p}(\Omega_{2})\geq 0.

If we have
\lambda_{1,p}(\Omega_{1})=\lambda_{1,p}(\Omega_{2})
, then
L(u_{1},u_{2})=0
a.e. in
\Omega
and thus
u_{1}=\alpha u_{2}
for some constant
\alpha>0
. However, this is impossible when
\Omega_{1}\subset\Omega_{2}
and
\Omega_{1}\neq\Omega_{2}
.
∎

Next is the uniqueness and simplicity results.

3.3.
Uniqueness and simplicity of
\lambda_{1,p}(\Omega)

Proposition 3.7
.

Let there exists
\lambda>0
and a strictly positive solution
v\in W_{0}^{1,p(x)}(\Omega)
of (
3
). Then we have

\lambda_{1,p}(\Omega)=\lambda.

Moreover, let
u_{1}
be the corresponding eigenfunction to
\lambda_{1,p}(\Omega)
. Then any other
u\in W_{0}^{1,p(x)}(\Omega)
corresponding to
\lambda_{1,p}(\Omega)
is a constant multiple of
u_{1}
.

Proof.

Let
u_{1}\in W_{0}^{1,p(x)}(\Omega)
be the eigenfunction corresponding to
\lambda_{1,p}(\Omega)
and
u
be a positive solution of (
3
). Applying Picone identity by choosing
\epsilon>0
(small) as follows:

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\Omega}L(u,u_{1}+\epsilon)dx

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}|\nabla_{X}u|^{p(x)}dx+\int_{\Omega}\frac{u^{p(x)}}{f(u_{1}+\epsilon)}\nabla_{X}^{*}(|\nabla_{X}u_{1}|^{p(x)-2}\nabla_{X})u_{1}dx

\displaystyle=\lambda\int_{\Omega}g(x)|u|^{p(x)}dx-\lambda_{1,p}(\Omega)\int_{\Omega}g(x)\frac{u^{p(x)}}{(u_{1}+\epsilon)^{p(x)-1}}|u_{1}|^{p(x)-2}u_{1}dx,

where we have set
f(u_{1}+\epsilon)=(u_{1}+\epsilon)^{p(x)-1}
. Taking the limit as
\epsilon\to 0^{+}
, applying Fatou’s Lemma and Lebesgue dominated convergence theorem, then

\lambda_{1,p}(\Omega)\leq\lambda.

On the other hand by Proposition
3.5
, we have

\lambda_{1,p}(\Omega)\geq\lambda.

This therefore implies that
\lambda_{1,p}(\Omega)=\lambda
. By this we have proved the uniqueness part.

Now by the hypothesis of the theorem we have for
\phi,\psi\in C^{\infty}_{0}(\Omega)
that

(3.16)

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}|\nabla_{X}u|^{p(x)-2}\langle\nabla_{X}u,\nabla_{X}\phi\rangle dx=\lambda_{1,p}\int_{\Omega}g(x)|u|^{p(x)-2}u\phi dx,

(3.17)

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}|\nabla_{X}u_{1}|^{p(x)-2}\langle\nabla_{X}u_{1},\nabla_{X}\psi\rangle dx=\lambda_{1,p}\int_{\Omega}g(x)|u_{1}|^{p(x)-2}u_{1}\psi dx.

Taking
\phi=u
and
\psi=\frac{|u|^{p}}{(u_{1}+\epsilon)^{p-1}}
into (
3.16
) and (
3.17
), respectively, and sending
\epsilon\to 0^{+}
, we arrive at

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}|\nabla_{X}u|^{p(x)}dx

\displaystyle=\lambda_{1,p}\int_{\Omega}g(x)|u|^{p(x)}dx

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}|\nabla_{X}u_{1}|^{p(x)-2}\nabla_{X}u_{1}\nabla_{X}\Big(\frac{|u|^{p(x)}}{u_{1}^{p(x)-1}}\Big)dx,

which implies (by choosing
f(u_{1})=u_{1}^{p(x)-2}
)

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}R(u,u_{1})dx=\int_{\Omega}L(u,u_{1})dx=0

and consequently,
\nabla_{X}(u/v)=0
, i.e.,
u=\alpha u_{1}
for some positive constant
\alpha>0
.

∎

The next proposition gives the sign changing nature of any other eigenfunction associated to an eigenvalue other than
\lambda_{1,p}(\Omega)
.

Proposition 3.8
.

Any eigenfunction
v
corresponding to an eigenvalue
\lambda\neq\lambda_{1,p}(\Omega)
changes sign.

Proof.

By contradiction we suppose
v>0
does not change sign (the case
v\leq 0
can be handled similarly). Let
\phi>0
be an eigenfunction corresponding to
\lambda_{1,p}(\Omega)
. Choosing any
\epsilon>0
as before, applying Picone identity, we have

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\Omega}L(\phi,v+\epsilon)dx

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}\left[|\nabla_{X}\phi|^{p(x)}-\nabla_{X}\Big(\frac{\phi^{p(x)}}{f(v+\epsilon)}\Big)|\nabla_{X}v|^{p(x)-2}\nabla_{X}v\right]dx

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}|\nabla_{X}\phi|^{p(x)}dx+\int_{\Omega}\frac{\phi^{p(x)}}{f(v+\epsilon)}\mathscr{L}_{p}vdx.

Since
\frac{\phi^{p(x)}}{(v+\epsilon)^{p(x)-1}}
is admissible in the weak formulation of (
3
) satisfied by
(\phi,\lambda)
, we arrive at

0\leq\lambda_{1,p}(\Omega)\int_{\Omega}g(x)|\phi|^{p(x)}dx-\lambda\int_{\Omega}\frac{\phi^{p(x)}}{f(v+\epsilon)}g(x)|v|^{p(x)-2}vdx.

Setting
f(v+\epsilon)=(v+\epsilon)^{p(x)-1}
and letting
\epsilon\to 0^{+}
in the last inequality as usual we obtain

0\leq(\lambda_{1,p}-\lambda)\int_{\Omega}g(x)\phi^{p(x)}dx,

which is a contradiction since
\int_{\Omega}g(x)\phi^{p(x)}dx=1
. Thus
v
must change sign.

∎

4.
variable exponent Caccioppoli estimates for general vector fields

Picone identity is applied to prove some variable exponent Caccioppoli estimates for general vector fields in this section. Recall that

1<p^{-}:=ess\inf_{x\in\bar{\Omega}}p(x)\leq p(x)\leq p^{+}:=ess\sup_{x\in\bar{\Omega}}p(x)<\infty.

Without giving rise to confusion but for simplicity sake we write
p:=p(x)
and
q=:q(x)
. We also denote
q^{-}:=ess\inf_{x\in\bar{\Omega}}q(x)
and
q^{+}:=ess\sup_{x\in\bar{\Omega}}q(x)
.

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
v
be a positive sub-solution of (
3
) in
\Omega\subset M
. Then for every fixed
q(x)>p(x)-1
,
p(x)>1
,
\nabla_{X}v\nabla_{X}p(x)=0
,
\nabla_{X}v\nabla_{X}q(x)=0
and
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
, we have

(4.1)

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}v^{q-p}\phi^{p}|\nabla_{X}v|^{p}dx\leq C^{p^{+}}_{p,q}\int_{\Omega}v^{q}|\nabla_{X}\phi|^{p}dx+C_{\lambda,p,q}\int_{\Omega}g(x)v^{q}\phi^{p}dx

for every nonnegative functions
\phi\in C^{\infty}_{0}(\Omega)
, where

C^{p^{+}}_{p,q}:=\left(\frac{p^{+}}{q^{-}-p^{+}+1}\right)^{p^{+}}\ \ \ \text{and}\ \ \ C_{\lambda,p,q}:=\left(\frac{\lambda p^{+}}{q^{-}-p^{+}+1}\right).

Proof.

Let
u=v^{q/p}\phi
, where
\phi
is a nonnegative test function and
v
is a sub-solution of (
3
), we compute

\displaystyle\nabla_{X}\left(v^{q/p}\phi\right)

\displaystyle=\phi\nabla_{X}(v^{q/p})+v^{q/p}\nabla_{X}\phi

\displaystyle=\phi v^{q/p}\ln v\left(\frac{\nabla_{X}q}{p}-\frac{q\nabla_{X}p}{p^{2}}\right)+\frac{q}{p}v^{\frac{q-p}{p}}\phi\nabla_{X}v+v^{q/p}\nabla_{X}\phi

so that

\displaystyle\langle\nabla_{X}v,\nabla_{X}\left(v^{q/p}\phi\right)\rangle

\displaystyle=\phi v^{q/p}\ln v\left(\frac{\nabla_{X}q}{p}-\frac{q\nabla_{X}p}{p^{2}}\right)\nabla_{X}v

\displaystyle\hskip 28.45274pt+\frac{q}{p}v^{\frac{q-p}{p}}\phi|\nabla_{X}v|^{2}+v^{q/p}\langle\nabla_{X}\phi,\nabla_{X}v\rangle.

Now using the the fact that
v
is a sub-solution of (
3
) and the condition that
\nabla_{X}v\nabla_{X}p(x)\equiv 0
and
\nabla_{X}v\nabla_{X}q(x)\equiv 0
in the Picone identity
L(u,v)\geq 0
, we have

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\Omega}L(v^{q/p}\phi,v)

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}|\nabla_{X}\left(v^{q/p}\phi\right)|^{p}dx+\int_{\Omega}\frac{f^{\prime}(v)}{(f(v))^{2}}|v^{q/p}|^{p}|\phi\nabla_{X}v|^{p}dx

(4.2)

\displaystyle\ \ \ -\int_{\Omega}q\frac{|v^{q/p}\phi|^{p-1}}{f(v)}\phi v^{\frac{q-p}{p}}|\nabla_{X}v|^{p}dx

\displaystyle\ \ \ -\int_{\Omega}p\frac{|v^{q/p}\phi|^{p-1}}{f(v)}v^{q/p}|\nabla_{X}v|^{p-2}\langle\nabla_{X}\phi,\nabla_{X}v\rangle dx.

Considering the condition
f^{\prime}(v)\geq(p(x)-1)\left[f(v)^{\frac{p(x)-2}{p(x)-1}}\right]
, we can then choose
f(v)=v^{p(x)-1}
. Then (
4
) reads

\displaystyle 0\leq

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}|\nabla_{X}\left(v^{q/p}\phi\right)|^{p}dx+\int_{\Omega}(p-1)v^{q-p}|\phi\nabla_{X}v|^{p}dx-\int_{\Omega}qv^{q-p}|\phi\nabla_{X}v|^{p}dx

(4.3)

\displaystyle-\int_{\Omega}p|v^{\frac{q-p}{p}}\phi|^{p-1}v^{q/p}|\nabla_{X}v|^{p-2}\langle\nabla_{X}\phi,\nabla_{X}v\rangle dx.

Using the
\varepsilon(x)
-modified version of the Young’s inequality in Lemma
2.2
with
\Phi=v^{q/p}|\nabla_{X}\phi|
and
\Psi=v^{\frac{q-p}{p}}\phi|\nabla_{X}v|
, we can estimate the last term of (
4
) as follows

\displaystyle-\int_{\Omega}p|v^{\frac{q-p}{p}}\phi|^{p-1}

\displaystyle v^{q/p}|\nabla_{X}v|^{p-2}\langle\nabla_{X}\phi,\nabla_{X}v\rangle dx

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\Omega}p|v^{\frac{q-p}{p}}\phi|^{p-1}|\nabla_{X}v|^{p-1}v^{q/p}\nabla_{X}\phi dx

(4.4)

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\Omega}\varepsilon^{1-p}v^{q}|\nabla_{X}\phi|^{p}dx+\int_{\Omega}\varepsilon(p-1)v^{q-p}|\phi\nabla_{X}v|^{p}dx,

where
\varepsilon(x)
is a continuous bounded function on
\Omega
, which will be chosen later. Substituting (
4
) into (
4
) we get

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\Omega}|\nabla_{X}\left(v^{q/p}\phi\right)|^{p}dx-\int_{\Omega}[q-p+1-\varepsilon(p-1)]v^{q-p}|\phi\nabla_{X}v|^{p}dx

\displaystyle\hskip 28.45274pt+\int_{\Omega}\varepsilon^{1-p}v^{q}|\nabla_{X}\phi|^{p}dx

\displaystyle\leq\lambda\int_{\Omega}g(x)|v^{q/p}\phi|^{p}dx-\mathcal{C}^{1}_{\epsilon,p,q}\int_{\Omega}v^{q-p}|\phi\nabla_{X}v|^{p}dx+\mathcal{C}^{2}_{\epsilon,p}\int_{\Omega}v^{q}|\nabla_{X}\phi|^{p}dx,

where we have used
\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}|\nabla_{X}u|^{p(x)}dx\leq\lambda\int_{\Omega}g(x)|u|^{p(x)}dx
for the sub-solution of (
3
). Here

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}^{1}_{\epsilon,p,q}:=q^{-}-p^{+}+1-\bar{\varepsilon}(p^{+}-1)\ \ \text{and}\ \ \mathcal{C}^{2}_{\epsilon,p}:=\bar{\varepsilon}^{1-p^{+}},

where
\bar{\varepsilon}:=\sup_{\Omega}\varepsilon(x)
.

Rearranging the last inequality we arrive at

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}v^{q-p}|\phi\nabla_{X}v|^{p}dx\leq\frac{\mathcal{C}^{2}_{\epsilon,p}}{\mathcal{C}^{1}_{\epsilon,p,q}}\int_{\Omega}v^{q}|\nabla_{X}\phi|^{p}dx+\frac{\lambda}{\mathcal{C}^{1}_{\epsilon,p,q}}\int_{\Omega}g(x)|v^{q/p}\phi|^{p}dx.

We can now choose a suitable number
\bar{\varepsilon}
as
\displaystyle\bar{\varepsilon}:=\frac{q^{-}-p^{+}+1}{p^{+}}
and then compute

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\mathcal{C}^{1}_{\epsilon,p,q}}

\displaystyle:=\frac{1}{q^{-}-p^{+}+1-\bar{\varepsilon}(p^{+}-1)}=\frac{\lambda p^{+}}{q^{-}-p^{+}+1},

\displaystyle\frac{\mathcal{C}^{2}_{\epsilon,p}}{\mathcal{C}^{1}_{\epsilon,p,q}}

\displaystyle:=\frac{\bar{\varepsilon}^{1-p^{+}}}{q^{-}-p^{+}+1-\bar{\varepsilon}(p^{+}-1)}=\left(\frac{p^{+}}{q^{-}-p^{+}+1}\right)^{p^{+}}.

The proof is therefore complete.
∎

Corollary 4.2
.

Let
v
be a positive sub-solution of (
3
) in
\Omega
. If
g(x)\equiv 0
or
\lambda=0
and
p(x)=q(x)
in
\Omega
. Then we have

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\phi^{p(x)}|\nabla_{X}v|^{p(x)}dx\leq(p^{+})^{p^{+}}\int_{\Omega}v^{p(x)}|\nabla_{X}\phi|^{p(x)}dx

for every nonnegative function
\phi\in C^{\infty}_{0}(\Omega)
.

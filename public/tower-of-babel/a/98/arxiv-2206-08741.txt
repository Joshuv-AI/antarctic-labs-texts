Growth of Solutions of Linear Difference Equations with Meromorphic Coefficients in Terms of Iterated p − ϕ p-\phi Order
Anirban Bandyopadhyay, Chinmay Ghosh, Sanjib Kumar Datta

Abstract
In this article we have studied complex linear homogeneous difference equations where the coefficients are meromorphic functions, having finite iterated p − ϕ p-\phi order. We have made some estimations on the growth of its nontrivial solutions. Also we have extended some of the previous results of Zhou-Zheng (2017), Belaïdi-Benkarouba (2019) and Belaïdi-Bellaama (2021). AMS Subject Classification (2010) : 30D35, 39A06, 39B32. Keywords and phrases : linear difference equation, meromorphic solution, iterated p − ϕ p-\phi order, iterated p − ϕ p-\phi type, iterated p − ϕ p-\phi exponent of convergence.

Introduction

Consider the complex difference equations

A_{k}(z)f(z+c_{k})+A_{k-1}(z)f(z+c_{k-1})+\cdots\\
+A_{1}(z)f(z+c_{1})+A_{0}(z)f(z)=0

(1)

and

A_{k}(z)f(z+c_{k})+A_{k-1}(z)f(z+c_{k-1})+\cdots\\
+A_{1}(z)f(z+c_{1})+A_{0}(z)f(z)=A(z)

(2)

where the coefficients
A_{0},A_{1},\ldots,A_{k}

(A_{k}\neq 0)
and
A

(A\neq 0)
are meromorphic and
c_{k},c_{k-1},\ldots,c_{1}\in\mathbb{C},
the set of finite complex numbers, are all distinct. Recently, study on (
1
) and (
2
) have become a topic of great interests from the
perspective of Nevanlinna’s theory
[
8
,
6
]
. In 2006 Halburd-Korhonen

[
7
]
and in 2008 Chiang-Feng
[
4
]
independently established some
results which are difference analogues of the classical logarithmic
derivative lemma. After that, using those results, many authors (
[
11
]
,

[
15
]
,
[
13
]
,
[
12
]
etc.) studied the growth properties of
the solutions of linear difference equations. In
2017
and in
2019

respectively Zhou, Zheng
[
16
]
and Belaïdi, Benkarouba
[
1
]

investigated higher order linear difference equations and proved some
results estimating the growth of the solutions of those equations using
iterated order
[
10
]
and iterated type
[
3
]
. Again in
2021
, Belaïdi and Bellaama
[
2
]
used iterated lower order
[
9
]
and
iterated lower type
[
9
]
to improve some of the previous theorems.

In
2009,
Chyzhykov et. al.
[
5
]
introduced
\phi-
order for a
meromorphic function in the unit disc. Also in
2014,
Shen et. al.
[
14
]
developed the concept of
\phi-
order of a meromorphic function in
whole complex plane and applied it to the second order linear differential
equation. The
\phi-
order is based on the function
\phi
which is
defined as:
\phi:[0,\infty)\longrightarrow(0,\infty)
is a
nondecreasing, unbounded function which satisfies following two conditions:

i)

There exists
\,k_{\phi}\in\mathbb{N},
such that
\lim\limits_{r\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\log_{p+1}r}{\log\phi(r)}{\small=0}
for all
p\geq k_{\phi}{\small;}

ii)

\lim\limits_{r\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\log\phi(\alpha r)}{\log\phi(r)}=1,
for
\alpha>1.

In this paper, we first introduce the iterated
p-\phi
order, the iterated

p-\phi
type and the iterated
p-\phi
exponent of convergence of a
meromorphic function. Since the concept of iterated
p-\phi
order is more
generalisation than that of iterated order, we are motivated to improvise
some of the results of Zhou-Zheng
[
16
]
, Belaïdi-Benkarouba
[
1
]
and Belaïdi-Bellaama
[
2
]
. Here we obtain our main results
for (
1
) or (
2
), when there may or may not exist exactly one
coefficient having maximal iterated
p-\phi
order (or iterated lower
p-\phi
order).

Let us define for all
r>0,

\log^{+}r=\max\left(0,~\log r\right).

We denote for all
r\in\mathbb{R},

\exp_{1}r=e^{r},~\exp_{n+1}r=\exp(\exp_{n}r),~n\in\mathbb{N}.

Also we denote for all sufficiently large values of
r,

\log_{1}r=\log^{+}r,~\log_{n+1}r=\log^{+}(\log_{n}r),~n\in\mathbb{N}.

Further we denote

\exp_{0}r=\log_{0}r=r,~\exp_{-1}r=\log^{+}r,~\log_{-1}r=e^{r}.

Moreover, in this paper, the linear measure of a set
E\subset(0,+\infty)

is defined by

m\left(E\right)=\int_{0}^{+\infty}\chi_{E}dt

and the logarithmic measure of a set
F\subset(1,+\infty)
is defined by

lm\left(F\right)=\int_{1}^{+\infty}\frac{\chi_{F}}{t}dt,

where
\chi_{G}(t)
is the characteristics function on the set
G
.

2
Basic definitions and lemmas

First we give the following definitions to indicate the rate of growth of
meromorphic functions.

Definition 1
(Iterated
p-\phi
order)

Let
p\in\mathbb{N}
. Then the iterated
p-\phi
order,
\rho_{p}(f,\phi)
of a meromorphic function
f(z)
is defined by

\rho_{p}(f,\phi)=\limsup_{r\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\log_{p}T(r,f)}{\log\phi(r)},

and when
f
is an entire function, we define

\rho_{p}(f,\phi)=\limsup_{r\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\log_{p+1}M_{f}(r)}{\log\phi(r)}.

Definition 2
(Finiteness degree of the
\phi
order)

The finiteness degree of the
\phi
order of a meromorphic function
f(z)

is defined by

i_{\rho}(f,\phi)=\left\{\begin{tabular}[]{ll}$0,$&$\text{if }\rho_{1}(f,\phi)=\infty\text{,}$\\
&\\
$\min\{j\in\mathbb{N}:\rho_{j}(f,\phi)<\infty\},$&$\text{if there exist some }j\in\mathbb{N}\text{ with }$\\
&$\rho_{j}(f,\phi)<\infty\text{ exists,}$\\
&\\
$\infty,$&$\text{if }\rho_{j}(f,\phi)=\infty\text{ for all }j\in\mathbb{N}.$\end{tabular}\right.

Definition 3
(Iterated lower
p-\phi
order)

Let
p\in\mathbb{N}
. Then the iterated lower
p-\phi
order,
\mu_{p}(f,\phi)
of a meromorphic function
f(z)
is defined by

\mu_{p}(f,\phi)=\liminf_{r\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\log_{p}T(r,f)}{\log\phi(r)},

and when
f
is an entire function, we define

\mu_{p}(f,\phi)=\liminf_{r\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\log_{p+1}M_{f}(r)}{\log\phi(r)}.

Remark 4

Let
f
be a meromorphic function with
i_{\rho}(f,\phi)=p
and the
iterated
p-\phi
order of
f
be
\rho_{p}(f,\phi).
Then it follows
from the definition that, for any
\epsilon>0,

T(r,f)=O\left(\exp_{p-1}\{\phi(r)\}^{\rho_{p}(f,\phi)+\epsilon\text{ }}\right).

Remark 5

Let
f
be a meromorphic function such that,
T(r,f)\leq\exp_{p}\{\phi(r)\}^{\alpha}
for some
\alpha>0
and
p\in\mathbb{N},
then we can say

i_{\rho}(f,\phi)\leq p+1\text{ and }\rho_{p+1}(f,\phi)\leq\alpha.

Definition 6
(Iterated
p-\phi
type)

Let
p\in\mathbb{N}.
Then the iterated
p-\phi
type,
\tau_{p}(f,\phi)
of a meromorphic
function
f
with nonzero finite iterated
p-\phi
order
\rho_{p}(f,\phi)
is defined by

\tau_{p}(f,\phi)=\limsup_{r\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\log_{p-1}T(r,f)}{\left\{\phi(r)\right\}^{\rho_{p}(f,\phi)}},

and when
f
is an entire function, then for
p\geq 2
we define

\tau_{p}(f,\phi)=\limsup_{r\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\log_{p}M_{f}(r)}{\left\{\phi(r)\right\}^{\rho_{p}(f,\phi)}}.

Definition 7
(Iterated lower
p-\phi
type)

Let
p\in\mathbb{N}.
Then the iterated lower
p-\phi
type,
t_{p}(f,\phi)
of a meromorphic
function
f
having non-zero finite iterated lower
p-\phi
order
\mu_{p}(f,\phi)
is defined by

t_{p}(f,\phi)=\liminf_{r\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\log_{p-1}T(r,f)}{\left\{\phi(r)\right\}^{\mu_{p}(f,\phi)}},

and when
f
is an entire function, then for
p\geq 2
we define

t_{p}(f,\phi)=\liminf_{r\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\log_{p}M_{f}(r)}{\left\{\phi(r)\right\}^{\mu_{p}(f,\phi)}}.

Remark 8

Let
f
be a meromorphic function with
i_{\rho}(f,\phi)=p
. Also let the
iterated
p-\phi
order and the iterated
p-\phi
type of
f
be
\rho_{p}(f,\phi)
and
\tau_{p}(f,\phi)
respectively. Then it follows from
the definition that, for any
\epsilon>0,

T(r,f)=O\left(\exp_{p-1}\left[\left\{\tau_{p}(f,\phi)+\epsilon\right\}\{\phi(r)\}^{\rho_{p}(f,\phi)\text{ }}\right]\right).

Definition 9
(Iterated
p-\phi
exponent of convergence)

Let
p\in\mathbb{N}.
Then the iterated
p-\phi
exponent of convergence of the sequence of
poles of a meromorphic function
f
is defined by

\lambda_{p}\left(\frac{1}{f},\phi\right)=\limsup_{r\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\log_{p}N(r,f)}{\log_{q}\left\{\phi(r)\right\}}.

Now we give some lemmas which will use to prove our main results.

Lemma 10

[
7
]
Suppose
z_{1}\in\mathbb{C},

\delta<1~
and
\varepsilon>0.
Then for any nonconstant meromorphic function
f
, there
exists a set
F\subset(1,+\infty)
with
lm(F)<\infty
such that

m\left(r,\frac{f\left(z+z_{1}\right)}{f\left(z\right)}\right)=o\left(\frac{\left\{T\left(r+\left|z_{1}\right|,\text{ }f\right)\right\}^{1+\varepsilon}}{r^{\delta}}\right),

for all
r\notin F.

Lemma 11

[
6
]
Let
z_{1},

z_{2}
be nonzero complex constants. Then
for any nonconstant meromorphic function
f
, we have

\left(1+o(1)\right)T\left(r-\left|z_{1}\right|,\text{ }f\right)\leq T\left(r,\text{ }f\left(z+z_{1}\right)\right)\leq\left(1+o(1)\right)T\left(r+\left|z_{1}\right|,\text{ }f\right),

for sufficiently large values of
r.

Consequently we have,
\rho_{p}\left(f\left(z+z_{1}\right),\phi\right)=\rho_{p}\left(f,\phi\right)
and
\mu_{p}\left(f\left(z+z_{1}\right),\phi\right)=\mu_{p}\left(f,\phi\right).

Lemma 12

[
7
]
Suppose
z_{1},z_{2}\in\mathbb{C~}(z_{1}\neq z_{2}),

\delta<1,

\varepsilon>0.
Then, for any nonconstant meromorphic function

f
, there exists a set
F\subset(1,+\infty)
with
lm(F)<\infty
such
that

m\left(r,\frac{f\left(z+z_{1}\right)}{f\left(z+z_{2}\right)}\right)=o\left(\frac{\left\{T\left(r+\left|z_{1}-z_{2}\right|+\left|z_{2}\right|,\text{ }f\right)\right\}^{1+\varepsilon}}{r^{\delta}}\right),

for all
r\notin F.

Lemma 13

Let
f
be a nonconstant meromorphic function with nonzero finite
iterated
p-\phi
order,
\rho_{p}\left(f,\phi\right).
Then for any
given
\varepsilon>0,
there exists a set
F\subset(1,+\infty)
with
lm(F)=+\infty
such that

\log_{p-1}T\left(r,\text{ }f\right)>\left\{\phi(r)\right\}^{\rho_{p}\left(f,\phi\right)-\varepsilon},

for
r\in F.

Proof.
By the definition of iterated
p-\phi
order, there exists an increasing
sequence
\{r_{n}\}~(r_{n}\rightarrow\infty)
satisfying
(1+\frac{1}{n})r_{n}<r_{n+1}~\
and

\lim_{r_{n}\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\log_{p}T(r_{n},f)}{\log\phi(r_{n})}=\rho_{p}(f,\phi).

Then for given
\varepsilon>0
there exists an integer
N_{0}
such that
for all
n>N_{0},

\log_{p-1}T\left(r_{n},\text{ }f\right)>\left\{\phi(r_{n})\right\}^{\rho_{p}\left(f,\phi\right)-\frac{\varepsilon}{2}}

Then for
r\in\left[r_{n},(1+\frac{1}{n})r_{n}\right]
and for
n>N_{0}

\displaystyle\log_{p-1}T\left(r,\text{ }f\right)

\displaystyle>

\displaystyle\log_{p-1}T\left(r_{n},\text{ }f\right)>\left\{\phi(r_{n})\right\}^{\rho_{p}\left(f,\phi\right)-\frac{\varepsilon}{2}}

\displaystyle>

\displaystyle\left\{\phi(\frac{n}{n+1}r)\right\}^{\rho_{p}\left(f,\phi\right)-\frac{\varepsilon}{2}}>\left\{\phi(r)\right\}^{\rho_{p}\left(f,\phi\right)-\varepsilon}.

Set
F=\cup_{n=N_{0}}^{\infty}\left[r_{n},(1+\frac{1}{n})r_{n}\right],

then

\displaystyle lm(F)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{n=N_{0}}^{\infty}lm\left(\left[r_{n},(1+\frac{1}{n})r_{n}\right]\right)=\sum_{n=N_{0}}^{\infty}\int_{1}^{+\infty}\frac{\chi_{\left[r_{n},(1+\frac{1}{n})r_{n}\right]}}{t}dt

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{n=N_{0}}^{\infty}\int_{r_{n}}^{(1+\frac{1}{n})r_{n}}\frac{1}{t}dt=\sum_{n=N_{0}}^{\infty}\log(1+\frac{1}{n})=+\infty.

Thus Lemma
13
is proved.

Lemma 14

Let
f
be a nonconstant meromorphic function with nonzero finite

p-\phi

\rho_{p}\left(f,\phi\right)

p-\phi

\tau_{p}\left(f,\phi\right).

for

Proof.
By the definition of iterated

p-\phi

there exists an increasing sequence
\{r_{n}\}~(r_{n}\rightarrow\infty)

satisfying
(1+\frac{1}{n})r_{n}<r_{n+1}~\
and

\limsup_{r\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\log_{p-1}T(r_{n},f)}{\left\{\phi(r_{n})\right\}^{\rho_{p}(f,\phi)}}=\tau_{p}(f,\phi).

Then for given
\varepsilon>0
there exists an integer
N_{0}
such that
for all

\log_{p-1}T\left(r_{n},\text{ }f\right)>\left(\tau_{p}\left(f,\phi\right)-\frac{\varepsilon}{2}\right)\left\{\phi(r_{n})\right\}^{\rho_{p}\left(f,\phi\right)}

Then for
r\in\left[r_{n},(1+\frac{1}{n})r_{n}\right]
and for

\displaystyle\log_{p-1}T\left(r,\text{ }f\right)>\log_{p-1}T\left(r_{n},\text{ }f\right)>\left(\tau_{p}\left(f,\phi\right)-\frac{\varepsilon}{2}\right)\left\{\phi(r_{n})\right\}^{\rho_{p}\left(f,\phi\right)}

\displaystyle>\left(\tau_{p}\left(f,\phi\right)-\frac{\varepsilon}{2}\right)\left\{\phi(\frac{n}{n+1}r)\right\}^{\rho_{p}\left(f,\phi\right)}>\left(\tau_{p}\left(f,\phi\right)-\varepsilon\right)\left\{\phi(r)\right\}^{\rho_{p}\left(f,\phi\right)}.

F=\cup_{n=N_{0}}^{\infty}\left[r_{n},(1+\frac{1}{n})r_{n}\right],

\displaystyle lm(F)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{n=N_{0}}^{\infty}lm\left(\left[r_{n},(1+\frac{1}{n})r_{n}\right]\right)=\sum_{n=N_{0}}^{\infty}\int_{1}^{+\infty}\frac{\chi_{\left[r_{n},(1+\frac{1}{n})r_{n}\right]}}{t}dt

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{n=N_{0}}^{\infty}\int_{r_{n}}^{(1+\frac{1}{n})r_{n}}\frac{1}{t}dt=\sum_{n=N_{0}}^{\infty}\log(1+\frac{1}{n})=+\infty.

Thus Lemma
14
is proved.

Lemma 19

Let
f
be a nonconstant meromorphic function with

and
\rho_{p}\left(f,\phi\right)=\rho
. Suppose
z_{1}\in\mathbb{C~}(z_{1}\neq 0)

By using similar arguments as in the proof of (
[

]
, Lemma 2.3), there

with

such that for
some

, we have

\left|\log\left|\frac{f\left(z+z_{1}\right)}{f\left(z\right)}\right|\right|\leq B\left\{T\left(\lambda r,f\right)\frac{\log^{\lambda}r}{r}\log T\left(\lambda r,f\right)\right\},

Again since,

\rho_{p}\left(f,\phi\right)=\rho,

\displaystyle B\exp_{p-1}\left\{\phi(\lambda r)\right\}^{\rho+\frac{\varepsilon}{2}}\frac{\log^{\lambda}r}{r}\exp_{p-2}\left\{\phi(\lambda r)\right\}^{\rho+\frac{\varepsilon}{2}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\exp_{p-1}\left\{\phi(r)\right\}^{\rho+\varepsilon},\text{ for }r\notin F\cup\left[0,1\right].

\displaystyle\text{Therefore, }\left|\frac{f\left(z+z_{1}\right)}{f\left(z\right)}\right|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\exp_{p}\left\{\phi(r)\right\}^{\rho+\varepsilon},\text{ for }r\notin F\cup\left[0,1\right].

Thus Lemma

We can write

By Lemma

Again since

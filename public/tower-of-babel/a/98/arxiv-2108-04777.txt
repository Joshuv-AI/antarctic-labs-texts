Approximation and Error Analysis of Forward-Backward SDEs driven by General Lévy Processes using Shot Noise Series Representations
Till Massing

Abstract
We consider the simulation of a system of decoupled forward-backward stochastic differential equations (FBSDEs) driven by a pure jump Lévy process L L and an independent Brownian motion B B . We allow the Lévy process L L to have an infinite jump activity. Therefore, it is necessary for the simulation to employ a finite approximation of its Lévy measure. We use the generalized shot noise series representation method by Rosiński 2001 to approximate the driving Lévy process L L . We compute the L p L^{p} error, p ≥ 2 p\geq 2 , between the true and the approximated FBSDEs which arises from the finite truncation of the shot noise series (given sufficient conditions for existence and uniqueness of the FBSDE). We also derive the L p L^{p} error between the true solution and the discretization of the approximated FBSDE using an appropriate backward Euler scheme.



Keywords:
Decoupled forward-backward SDEs with jumps; Lévy processes; Shot noise series representation; Discrete-time approximation; Euler Scheme

60H10; 60H35; 65C05

Introduction

We consider a system of decoupled forward-backward stochastic differential equations (FBSDE) with jumps of the type

\displaystyle=X_{0}+\int_{0}^{t}b(s,X_{s})\mathrm{d}s+\int_{0}^{t}a(s,X_{s})\mathrm{d}B_{s}+\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}}h(s,X_{s-})e\widetilde{\mu}(\mathrm{d}e,\mathrm{d}s)

\displaystyle Y_{t}

\displaystyle=g(X_{T})+\int_{t}^{T}f(s,X_{s},Y_{s},Z_{s},\Gamma_{s})\mathrm{d}s-\int_{t}^{T}Z_{s}\mathrm{d}B_{s}-\int_{t}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{R}}U_{s}(e)e\widetilde{\mu}(\mathrm{d}e,\mathrm{d}s),

(2)

for
t\in[0,T]
, where
B
is a Brownian motion and

is an independent pure jump Lévy process with Lévy measure
\nu
where
\mu(\mathrm{d}e,\mathrm{d}t)=\nu(\mathrm{d}e)\mathrm{d}t

\widetilde{\mu}(\mathrm{d}e,\mathrm{d}s)=\mu(\mathrm{d}e,\mathrm{d}s)-\nu(\mathrm{d}e)\mathrm{d}s

. (Assumptions on the functions

are discussed below.) For path simulation it is therefore necessary to employ a finite approximation of the Lévy measure first. Afterwards we have to discretize the FBSDE. We are interested in path simulation and the associated error between the true solution and the approximate solution of (

Backward SDEs are a vibrant research topic since the seminal paper of
Pardoux and Peng 1990
. They proved existence and uniqueness in the
L^{2}
sense of a solution of a BSDE (without jumps) under the assumptions of square integrability of the terminal condition and Lipschitz continuity of the generator
f
. Since then BSDEs and/or FBSDEs have been analyzed in many directions.

One strand of the literature treats extensions of the existence and uniqueness result of
Pardoux and Peng 1990
by relaxing the underlying assumptions or extending the BSDE under consideration. For example,
Tang and Li 1994
and
Barles et al. 1997
included jumps into the BSDE.
Briand et al. 2003
discussed the existence and uniqueness in an
L^{p}
sense given a Brownian filtration.
Buckdahn and Pardoux 1994
did the same including jumps. Since then several papers have shown
L^{p}
existence and uniqueness with a generalized filtration under weak assumptions, e.g.,
Kruse and Popier 2016
,
Yao 2017
, and
Eddahbi et al. 2017
.
FBSDEs are the Markovian special case of BSDEs where the terminal condition is determined by the forward SDE.

Another strand of the literature covers possible areas of applications of FBSDEs. For example, FBSDEs turned out to be useful in mathematical finance, see
Karoui et al. 1997
and
Delong 2013
, in optimal control, see
Tang and Li 1994
, or for partial differential equations, see
Pardoux 1999
or also the book
Pardoux and Răşcanu 2014
.

A third strand is about the discrete-time approximation of FBSDEs, which this paper aims to contribute to. A popular approach is a backward Euler scheme, see
Zhang 2004
and
Bouchard and Touzi 2004
who derived the
L^{2}
approximation error of the scheme.
Gobet and Labart 2007
generalized this by computing the
L^{p}
error.
Bouchard and Elie 2008
derived the
L^{2}
error for FBSDEs containing a finite number of jumps. In the case of an infinite jump activity,
Aazizi 2013
proposed a two-step approximation by first approximating the small jumps by a Brownian motion to have only finitely many big jumps, and second by discretizing according to
Bouchard and Elie 2008
.
Aazizi 2013
then derived the
L^{2}
approximation-discretization error. The approach follows
Kohatsu-Higa and Tankov 2010
who approximated forward SDEs with infinitely many jumps by finitely many jumps.

This paper contributes to literature in the following way. First, we extend the results of
Aazizi 2013
for the
L^{2}
approximation-discretization error to a more general
L^{p}
,
p\geq 2
, version by incorporating a jump-adapted Euler scheme. Second, instead of partitioning the Lévy measure into jumps larger or smaller than a certain level we allow for various truncation functions using the approach of shot noise series representations by
Rosiński 2001
, which may be the more efficient way for a certain Lévy process (some examples are provided below). Third, we enlarge the class of pure jump Lévy processes to these which do not fulfill the
Asmussen and Rosiński 2001
assumption for the approximation of small jumps. All in all, we obtain a statement for the
L^{p}
error for general Lévy processes. We find that the error depends on
N^{-1/2}
, where
N
is the number of time steps, and on the
p
th and second moments of the Lévy measure of the discarded jumps.

The remainder of this paper is organized as follows. In Section
2
we discuss the settings in more detail. In Section
3
we derive an upper bound for the error of the approximation with a finite jump measure. In Section
4
we present the discrete Euler scheme and prove an upper bound for the discretization error.

We next mention some important facts on Lévy processes which we need to approximate the infinite Lévy measure. We opt for the approximation using series representations which goes back to
Rosiński 2001
, see
Yuan and Kawai 2021
for a recent overview. Let
L
be a pure jump Lévy process on
(\Omega,\mathcal{F},(\mathcal{F}_{t})_{0\leq t\leq T},\mathbb{P})
with Lévy measure
\nu
as discussed above. The famous Lévy-Itô decomposition states that
L
can be written as

L_{t}=\int_{0}^{t}\int_{|e|\leq 1}e\widetilde{\mu}(\mathrm{d}e,\mathrm{d}s)+\int_{0}^{t}\int_{|e|>1}e\mu(\mathrm{d}e,\mathrm{d}s)

(14)

for
t\in[0,T]
.

Rosiński 2001
proved the useful result that it is possible to express jump-type Lévy processes as an infinite series. We now summarize his theory of
generalized shot noise series representations
. We only present the one-dimensional case (it is of course also available in
d
dimensions). Suppose that the Lévy measure
\nu
can be decomposed as

\nu(B)=\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathbb{P}[H(r,V)\in B]\mathrm{d}r,\ \ \ B\in\mathcal{B},

(15)

where
V
is a random variable in some space
\mathcal{V}
and
H\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}(0,\infty)\times\mathcal{V}\to\mathbb{R}
is a measurable function such that for every
v\in\mathcal{V}
,
r\mapsto|H(r,v)|
is nonincreasing. Then, it holds that

L_{t}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathcal{L}}}{{=}}\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}H\left(\frac{G_{i}}{T},V_{i}\right)\mathds{1}_{[0,t]}(T_{i})-tc_{i},

(16)

for
t\in[0,T]
, where
\{G_{i}\}_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
are the arrival times of a standard Poisson process (i.e., a sequence of partial sums of i.i.d. unit rate exponential random variables),
\{V_{i}\}_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
are i.i.d. copies of the random variable
V
independent of
\{G_{i}\}_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
,
\{T_{i}\}_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
are i.i.d. uniforms on
[0,T]
independent of
\{G_{i}\}_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
and
\{V_{i}\}_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
, and
\{c_{k}\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
are centering constants such that

c_{i}=\int_{i-1}^{i}\int_{|x|\leq 1}x\mathbb{P}[H(r,V)\in\mathrm{d}x]\mathrm{d}r.

(17)

Rosiński’s theorem offers several choices for different series representations. The most convenient representation is case-dependent given the specific Lévy measure. Well-known special cases include the inverse Lévy measure method, the rejection method or the thinning method, see
Yuan and Kawai 2021
for details.

To obtain a feasible numerical algorithm one has to truncate the infinite series in (
16
). Instead of truncating the series deterministically, i.e., after
n
summands, we choose a random truncation

L_{t}^{n}\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}=\sum_{\{i\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}G_{i}\leq nT\}}H\left(\frac{G_{i}}{T},V_{i}\right)\mathds{1}_{[0,t]}(T_{i})-tc_{i},

(18)

where we cut off all summands if
G_{i}>nT
, which depends on the random Poisson arrival times
\{G_{i}\}
. The reason is that with the random truncation
L^{n}
itself is a compound Poisson process and hence a proper Lévy process with Lévy measure

\nu^{n}(B)=\int_{0}^{n}\mathbb{P}[H(r,U)\in B]\mathrm{d}r,\ \ \ B\in\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{R}^{d}).

(19)

Note that the truncated Lévy measure only describes finitely many jumps, i.e.,
\nu^{n}(\mathbb{R})=n<\infty
. In the following we use the notation
\bar{\nu}^{n}(\mathrm{d}e)\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}=\nu(\mathrm{d}e)-\nu^{n}(\mathrm{d}e)
, which is the Lévy measure of the infinitely many small jumps that are discarded.
The important quantity which determines the approximation error of FBSDEs will be in terms of the second and
p
th moments of the Lévy measure
\bar{\nu}^{n}
, which are defined as

\sigma^{p}(n)\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}=\int_{\mathbb{R}}|e|^{p}\bar{\nu}^{n}(\mathrm{d}e),

(20)

for
p\geq 2
. By
\mu^{n}
we denote the Poisson measure with intensity measure
\nu^{n}(\mathrm{d}e)\mathrm{d}t
and by
\bar{\mu}^{n}
the Poisson measure with intensity measure
\bar{\nu}^{n}(\mathrm{d}e)\mathrm{d}t
. Let
\widetilde{\mu^{n}}
and
\widetilde{\bar{\nu}^{n}}
be the corresponding compensated Poisson measures. Clearly,
\nu=\nu^{n}+\bar{\nu}^{n}
,
\mu=\mu^{n}+\bar{\mu}^{n}
and
\widetilde{\mu}=\widetilde{\mu^{n}}+\widetilde{\bar{\mu}^{n}}
.

We give some examples for special Lévy processes with different series representations.

Example 1
.

We first consider the case where
L
is a gamma process, i.e., a Lévy process with Lévy measure
\alpha\frac{\exp(-\beta e)}{e}\mathds{1}(e>0)\mathrm{d}e
, with
\alpha>0,\beta>0
. There are four widely used series representation for the gamma process
Rosiński 2001
.

\displaystyle L_{t}

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathcal{L}}}{{=}}\frac{1}{\beta}\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}E_{1}^{-1}\left(\frac{G_{i}}{\alpha T}\right)\mathds{1}_{[0,t]}(T_{i}),

(inverse Lévy measure method)

(21)

\displaystyle L_{t}

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathcal{L}}}{{=}}\frac{1}{\beta}\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{\mathrm{e}^{G_{i}/(\alpha T)}-1}\mathds{1}\left(\frac{\mathrm{e}^{G_{i}/(\alpha T)}}{\mathrm{e}^{G_{i}/(\alpha T)}-1}\mathrm{e}^{-(\mathrm{e}^{G_{i}/(\alpha T)})^{-1}}\geq V_{i}^{(1)}\right)\mathds{1}_{[0,t]}(T_{i}),

(rejection method)

(22)

\displaystyle L_{t}

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathcal{L}}}{{=}}\frac{1}{\beta}\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}V_{i}^{(2)}\mathds{1}\left(V_{i}^{(2)}G_{i}\leq\alpha T\right)\mathds{1}_{[0,t]}(T_{i}),

(thinning method)

(23)

\displaystyle L_{t}

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathcal{L}}}{{=}}\frac{1}{\beta}\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\mathrm{e}^{-\frac{G_{i}}{\alpha T}}V_{i}^{(2)}\mathds{1}_{[0,t]}(T_{i}),

(Bondesson method)

(24)

for
t\in[0,T]
, where
\{G_{i}\}_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
are the arrival times of a standard Poisson process,
\{V_{i}^{(1)}\}_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
are i.i.d. uniforms on
[0,1]
,
\{V_{i}^{(2)}\}_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
are i.i.d. standard exponential r.v.’s,
\{T_{i}\}_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
are i.i.d. uniforms on
[0,T]
. All sequences are independent of each other.
E_{1}(x)\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}=\int_{u}^{\infty}\frac{\mathrm{e}^{-u}}{u}\mathrm{d}u
is the exponential integral and
E_{1}^{-1}
its inverse. The centering constants
c_{i}
are all equal to zero in this case.
Imai and Kawai 2013
showed the superiority of the inverse Lévy measure method compared with the other methods when employing a finite truncation, i.e., its
\sigma^{p}(n)
is smaller for
p\geq 2
. However, in practice it may be easier to work with the other methods. For example, for the gamma process the inverse Lévy measure method relies on numerical inversion of the exponential integral function. The method of Bondesson may be the easiest to use in this case. Note that the gamma process does not fulfill the
Asmussen and Rosiński 2001
assumption for the approximation of small jumps.

Example 2
.

The second example are tempered stable Lévy processes, see
Rosiński 2007
. For simplicity, we here consider the classical tempered stable subordinator

with Lévy measure
\delta\frac{\exp(-\lambda e)}{e^{-1-\alpha}}\mathds{1}(e>0)\mathrm{d}e

(25)

for
t\in[0,T]
, where
\{G_{i}\}_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
are the arrival times of a standard Poisson process,
\{U_{i}\}_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
are i.i.d. uniforms on
[0,1]
,
\{V_{i}\}_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
are i.i.d. standard exponential r.v.’s,
\{T_{i}\}_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
are i.i.d. uniforms on
[0,T]
. All sequences are independent of each other. The centering constants

Remark 1
.

The Lévy measures
\nu
and
\bar{\nu}^{n}
are assumed to be infinite, i.e.,
\nu(\mathbb{R})=\bar{\nu}^{n}(\mathbb{R})=\infty
and thus we cannot apply Jensen’s inequality to integrals of the type
\int\nu(\mathrm{d}e)
. Assumption
1
.(iii) provides a necessary bound. Indeed, by Hölder’s inequality

\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}}\rho(e)U_{s}(e)e\nu(\mathrm{d}e)\right)^{2}\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}}U_{s}(e)^{2}e^{2}\nu(\mathrm{d}e)\int_{\mathbb{R}}\rho(e)^{2}\nu(\mathrm{d}e)\leq K^{3}\int_{\mathbb{R}}U_{s}(e)^{2}e^{2}\nu(\mathrm{d}e)

(26)

for
U_{s}\in\mathbb{L}_{\mu}^{2}
, because
\int(1\wedge|e|^{2})\nu(\mathrm{d}e)\leq K<\infty
is bounded by a finite constant.

Given the approximation of Lévy processes using truncated series representations we use
\widetilde{\mu^{n}}
to approximate the Poisson measure
\widetilde{\mu}
. We call
(X^{n},Y^{n},Z^{n},U^{n})
the solution of the approximate FBSDE

\displaystyle X_{t}^{n}

\displaystyle=X_{0}+\int_{0}^{t}b(s,X_{s}^{n})\mathrm{d}s+\int_{0}^{t}a(s,X_{s}^{n})\mathrm{d}B_{s}+\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}}h(s,X_{s-}^{n})e\widetilde{\mu^{n}}(\mathrm{d}e,\mathrm{d}s)

(27)

\displaystyle Y_{t}^{n}

\displaystyle=g(X_{T}^{n})+\int_{t}^{T}f(s,X_{s}^{n},Y_{s}^{n},Z_{s}^{n},\Gamma_{s}^{n})\mathrm{d}s-\int_{t}^{T}Z_{s}^{n}\mathrm{d}B_{s}-\int_{t}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{R}}U_{s}^{n}(e)e\widetilde{\mu^{n}}(\mathrm{d}e,\mathrm{d}s),

(28)

where

\Gamma_{s}^{n}=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\rho(e)U_{s}^{n}(e)e\nu^{n}(\mathrm{d}e)
. The aim of the next section is to compute the approximation error between the original FBSDE (
1
)-(
2
) and the approximate FBSDE (
27
)-(
28
).

Remark 2

In this paper we restrict ourselves to one-dimensional FBSDEs. The extension to multidimensional FBSDEs (the comparison principle, see

3
Error of the approximation of the pure jump process

In this section we compute the
L^{p}
approximation error between the original backward SDE (
2
) and the approximate backward SDE (
28
), defined as

\displaystyle Err_{n,p}(Y,Z,U)\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}=

\displaystyle\ \mathbb{E}\left[\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}|Y_{t}-Y_{t}^{n}|^{p}+\left(\int_{0}^{T}|Z_{s}-Z_{s}^{n}|^{2}\mathrm{d}s\right)^{p/2}\right.

(29)

\displaystyle+\left.\left(\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{R}}|U_{s}(e)-U_{s}^{n}(e)|^{2}e^{2}\nu^{n}(\mathrm{d}e)\mathrm{d}s\right)^{p/2}+\left(\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{R}}U_{s}(e)^{2}e^{2}\bar{\nu}^{n}(\mathrm{d}e)\mathrm{d}s\right)^{p/2}\right]^{1/p}.

(30)

Furthermore, we derive an upper bound for the approximation error of the forward SDE defined as

\mathbb{E}\left[\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}|X_{t}-X_{t}^{n}|^{p}\right].

(31)

In the following proofs, the standard estimate for the solution of the FBSDEs is useful:

||(X,Y,Z,U)||_{\mathcal{S}^{p}\times\mathcal{E}^{p}}^{p}\leq C_{p}(1+|X_{0}|^{p}),

(32)

for
p\geq 2
, see
Bouchard and Elie 2008
. In particular, the forward SDE has the estimate

\mathbb{E}\left[\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}|X_{t}|^{p}\right]\leq C_{p}(1+|X_{0}|^{p}).

(33)

Because the FBSDEs are decoupled we can analyze the forward and backward components separately. We begin with an error bound for the forward SDE.

Proposition 1
.

Let
p\geq 2
. Under Assumption
1
on
(\Omega,\mathcal{F},(\mathcal{F}_{t}),\mathbb{P})

•

there exists a unique solution
X
of (
1

,

•

) on
[0,T]

(34)

Proof.

The existence and uniqueness is a standard result, see
Applebaum 2009
. We thus only prove the bound (

Aazizi 2013

Let

. We plug in the SDEs and use the Burkholder-Davis-Gundy (see Theorem 2.11 of

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\sup_{0\leq s\leq t}|X_{s}-X_{s}^{n}|^{p}\right]\leq C_{p}

\displaystyle\left(\mathbb{E}\left[\left(\int_{0}^{t}|b(r,X_{r})-b(r,X_{r}^{n})|\mathrm{d}r\right)^{p}\right]\right.

(35)

\displaystyle+\mathbb{E}\left[\left(\int_{0}^{t}|a(r,X_{r})-a(r,X_{r}^{n})|^{2}\mathrm{d}r\right)^{p/2}\right]

(36)

\displaystyle+\mathbb{E}\left[\left(\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}}|h(r,X_{r})-h(r,X_{r}^{n})|^{2}|e|^{2}\nu^{n}(\mathrm{d}e)\mathrm{d}r\right)^{p/2}\right]

(37)

\displaystyle+\mathbb{E}\left[\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}}|h(r,X_{r})-h(r,X_{r}^{n})|^{p}|e|^{p}\nu^{n}(\mathrm{d}e)\mathrm{d}r\right]

(38)

\displaystyle+\mathbb{E}\left[\left(\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}}|h(r,X_{r})|^{2}|e|^{2}\bar{\nu}^{n}(\mathrm{d}e)\mathrm{d}r\right)^{p/2}\right]

(39)

integral, the Lipschitz assumption (

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\sup_{0\leq s\leq t}|X_{s}-X_{s}^{n}|^{p}\right]

(41)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\left.+\mathbb{E}\left[\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}}(1+|X_{r}|^{p})|e|^{p}\bar{\nu}^{n}(\mathrm{d}e)\mathrm{d}r\right]\right)

(43)

Now the result follows from Gronwall’s lemma.
∎

4
Error of the discretization of the FBSDE

In this section we discretize the approximated FBSDE
(X^{n},Y^{n},Z^{n},U^{n})
and derive error bounds. We use a forward-backward Euler scheme for simulation.
First we define the regular grid
\tilde{\pi}\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}=\left\{\tilde{t}_{k}\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}=\frac{kT}{N},\ k=0,\ldots,N\right\}
on
[0,T]
. We do not discuss the discretization of the original FBSDE because in practice they cannot be simulated and the proofs of this section rely on
\nu^{n}(\mathbb{R})<\infty
.
Starting with the forward Euler scheme for
X^{n}
, we define

\displaystyle\begin{cases}X_{0}^{n,\pi}&\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}=X_{0}\\
X_{\tilde{t}_{k+1}}^{n,\pi}&\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}=X_{\tilde{t}_{k}}^{n,\pi}+\frac{T}{N}b(\tilde{t}_{k},X_{t_{k}}^{n,\pi})+a(\tilde{t}_{k},X_{t_{k}}^{n,\pi})\Delta B_{{k+1}}+\int_{\mathbb{R}}h(\tilde{t}_{k},X_{\tilde{t}_{k}}^{n,\pi})e\widetilde{\mu^{n}}(\mathrm{d}e,(\tilde{t}_{k},\tilde{t}_{k+1}]),\end{cases}

(138)

where
\Delta B_{{k+1}}\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}=B_{\tilde{t}_{k+1}}-B_{\tilde{t}_{k}}
are normal random variables.
We now aim to to derive the discretization error of the forward SDE. Although the techniques are pretty standard, we have to reconsider the result of
Aazizi 2013
because the discretization error depends on the truncation parameter
n
. We here follow
Mrad and Popier 2023
who derived a new approximation-discretization error for a jump-adapted scheme. In order to do so, we define the jump-adapted discretization scheme as

\pi\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}=\{t_{k},k=1,\ldots,N+J^{n}\}\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}=\tilde{\pi}\cup\{T_{i},i\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}G_{i}\leq nT\},

(139)

which is the superposition of the discretization times and the times when large jumps occur, and
J^{n}\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}=|\{T_{i},i\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}G_{i}\leq nT\}|
denotes the number of large jumps.

Let us define the function
\tau
for

by

\tau_{t}\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}=\max\{t_{k},\ k=1,\ldots,N+J^{n}|t_{k}\leq t\},

(140)

(141)

be the jump-adapted Euler-scheme for

This can be written as

(142)

. Let us define

(152)

\bar{Y}_{t_{k}}^{n,\pi}=\bar{Y}_{t_{k+1}}^{n,\pi}+(t_{k+1}-t_{k})f(t_{k},X_{t_{k}}^{n,\pi},\bar{Y}_{t_{k}}^{n,\pi},\bar{Z}_{t_{k}}^{n,\pi},\bar{\Gamma}_{t_{k}}^{n,\pi})-\int_{t_{k}}^{t_{k+1}}Z_{s}^{n,\pi}\mathrm{d}B_{s}-\int_{t_{k}}^{t_{k+1}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}U_{s}^{n,\pi}(e)e\bar{\mu}^{n}(\mathrm{d}e,\mathrm{d}s).

(153)

We define the process
Y^{n,\pi}

Y_{t}^{n,\pi}\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}=\bar{Y}_{t_{k}}^{n,\pi}-(t-t_{k})f(t_{k},X_{t_{k}}^{n,\pi},\bar{Y}_{t_{k}}^{n,\pi},\bar{Z}_{t_{k}}^{n,\pi},\bar{\Gamma}_{t_{k}}^{n,\pi})+\int_{t_{k}}^{t}Z_{s}^{n,\pi}\mathrm{d}B_{s}+\int_{t_{k}}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}}U_{s}^{n,\pi}(e)e\bar{\mu}^{n}(\mathrm{d}e,\mathrm{d}s)

(154)

on
[t_{k},t_{k+1})
and obtain that

(t_{k+1}-t_{k})^{-1}\mathbb{E}\left[\int_{t_{k}}^{t_{k+1}}Y_{s}^{n,\pi}\mathrm{d}s|\mathcal{F}_{t_{k}}\right]=\mathbb{E}\left[\bar{Y}_{t_{k+1}}^{n,\pi}|\mathcal{F}_{t_{k}}\right]+(t_{k+1}-t_{k})f(t_{k},X_{t_{k}}^{n,\pi},\bar{Y}_{t_{k}}^{n,\pi},\bar{Z}_{t_{k}}^{n,\pi},\bar{\Gamma}_{t_{k}}^{n,\pi})=Y_{t_{k}}^{n,\pi}=\bar{Y}_{t_{k}}^{n,\pi}.

(155)

Thus
\bar{Y}_{t_{k}}^{n,\pi}
is the best approximation of
Y^{n,\pi}
on
[t_{k},t_{k+1})
by
\mathcal{F}_{t_{k}}
-measurable random variables (viewed as constant processes on
[t_{k},t_{k+1})
), which explains the notation
\bar{Y}^{n,\pi}
, consistent with the definition of
\bar{Z}^{n,\pi}
and
\bar{\Gamma}^{n,\pi}
.

Furthermore, we need to define the processes
(\bar{Z}^{n},\bar{\Gamma}^{n})
on each interval
[t_{k},t_{k+1})
by

\displaystyle\bar{Z}_{t}^{n}

\displaystyle\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}=(t_{k+1}-t_{k})\mathbb{E}\left[\left.\int_{t_{k}}^{t_{k+1}}Z_{s}^{n}\mathrm{d}s\right|\mathcal{F}_{t_{k}}\right],

(156)

\displaystyle\bar{\Gamma}_{t}^{n}

\displaystyle\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}=(t_{k+1}-t_{k})\mathbb{E}\left[\left.\int_{t_{k}}^{t_{k+1}}\Gamma_{s}^{n}\mathrm{d}s\right|\mathcal{F}_{t_{k}}\right].

(157)

Remark 4
.

\bar{Z}_{t_{k}}^{n}
and
\bar{\Gamma}_{t_{k}}^{n}
are the counterparts of
\bar{Z}_{t_{k}}^{n,\pi}
and
\bar{\Gamma}_{t_{k}}^{n,\pi}
for the original backward SDE. They can be interpreted as the best
\mathbb{H}_{[t_{k},t_{k+1}]}^{2}
-approximations of
(Z_{t}^{n})_{t_{k}\leq t<t_{k+1}}
and
(\Gamma_{t}^{n})_{t_{k}\leq t<t_{k+1}}
by an
\mathcal{F}_{t_{k}}
-measurable random variables (viewed as constant processes on

), i.e.,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\int_{t_{k}}^{t_{k+1}}|Z_{s}^{n}-\bar{Z}_{t_{k}}^{n}|^{2}\mathrm{d}s\right]=\inf_{Z_{k}\in L^{2}(\Omega,\mathcal{F}_{t_{k}})}\mathbb{E}\left[\int_{t_{k}}^{t_{k+1}}|Z_{s}^{n}-Z_{k}|^{2}\mathrm{d}s\right],

(158)

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\int_{t_{k}}^{t_{k+1}}|\Gamma_{s}^{n}-\bar{\Gamma}_{t_{k}}^{n}|^{2}\mathrm{d}s\right]=\inf_{\Gamma_{k}\in L^{2}(\Omega,\mathcal{F}_{t_{k}})}\mathbb{E}\left[\int_{t_{k}}^{t_{k+1}}|\Gamma_{s}^{n}-\Gamma_{k}|^{2}\mathrm{d}s\right].

(159)

We now state our second main theorem, which gives a bound for the discretization error.

Theorem 2
.

Under Assumptions
1
and
2
, the discretization error for the backward SDE is bounded by

Err_{\pi,p}(Y^{n},Z^{n},U^{n})\leq C_{p}\left(N^{-1/2}+N^{-1}\mathfrak{m}^{1}(n)\right),

(160)

where
C_{p}
only depends on constants and not on

or
\pi
.

Proof.

The proof is an
L^{p}
extension of the proofs of
Bouchard and Elie 2008
,
Elie 2006
and
Bouchard and Touzi 2004
. For the sake of brevity we set
\delta^{n}Y_{t}\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}=Y_{t}^{n}-Y_{t}^{n,\pi}
,
\delta^{n}Z_{t}\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}=Z_{t}^{n}-Z_{t}^{n,\pi}
,
\delta^{n}U_{t}(e)\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}=U_{t}^{n}(e)-U_{t}^{n,\pi}(e)
,
\delta^{n}\Gamma_{t}\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}=\Gamma_{t}^{n}-\Gamma_{t}^{n,\pi}
and
\delta^{n}f(\Theta_{t})\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}=f(t,X_{t}^{n},Y_{t}^{n},Z_{t}^{n},\Gamma_{t}^{n})-f(t_{k},X_{t_{k}}^{n,\pi},\bar{Y}_{t_{k}}^{n,\pi},\bar{Z}_{t_{k}}^{n,\pi},\bar{\Gamma}_{t_{k}}^{n,\pi})
. Note that
\bar{Y}_{t_{k}}^{n,\pi}=Y_{t_{k}}^{n,\pi}
by (
155
) which we will use repeatedly.

The proof is divided in four steps. Before turning to the first step, we discuss some bounds which we will need throughout. Let
s\in[t_{k},t_{k+1})

(161)

\mathbb{E}\left[|Y_{s}^{n}-\bar{Y}_{t_{k}}^{n,\pi}|^{p}\right]\leq p\left(\mathbb{E}\left[|Y_{s}^{n}-Y_{t_{k}}^{n}|^{p}\right]+\mathbb{E}\left[|\delta^{n}Y_{t_{k}}|^{p}\right]\right)

(162)

and

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[|Z_{s}^{n}-\bar{Z}_{t_{k}}^{n,\pi}|^{p}\right]

\displaystyle\leq p\left(\mathbb{E}\left[|Z_{s}^{n}-\bar{Z}_{t_{k}}^{n}|^{p}\right]+\mathbb{E}\left[|\bar{Z}_{t_{k}}^{n}-\bar{Z}_{t_{k}}^{n,\pi}|^{p}\right]\right)

(163)

\displaystyle=p\left(\mathbb{E}\left[|Z_{s}^{n}-\bar{Z}_{t_{k}}^{n}|^{p}\right]+\mathbb{E}\left[\left|(t_{k+1}-t_{k})^{-1}\mathbb{E}\left[\left.\int_{t_{k}}^{t_{k+1}}\delta^{n}Z_{s}\mathrm{d}s\right|\mathcal{F}_{t_{k}}\right]\right|^{p}\right]\right)

(164)

\displaystyle\leq C_{p}\left(\mathbb{E}\left[|Z_{s}^{n}-\bar{Z}_{t_{k}}^{n}|^{p}\right]+\mathbb{E}\left[\mathbb{E}\left[\left.(t_{k+1}-t_{k})^{-1}\int_{t_{k}}^{t_{k+1}}|\delta^{n}Z_{s}|^{2}\mathrm{d}s\right|\mathcal{F}_{t_{k}}\right]^{p/2}\right]\right)

(165)

\displaystyle\leq C_{p}\left(\mathbb{E}\left[|Z_{s}^{n}-\bar{Z}_{t_{k}}^{n}|^{p}\right]+(t_{k+1}-t_{k})^{-p/2}\mathbb{E}\left[\left(\int_{t_{k}}^{t_{k+1}}|\delta^{n}Z_{s}|^{2}\mathrm{d}s\right)^{p/2}\right]\right)

(166)

\displaystyle=C_{p}\left(\mathbb{E}\left[|Z_{s}^{n}-\bar{Z}_{t_{k}}^{n}|^{p}\right]+(t_{k+1}-t_{k})^{-p/2}||\delta^{n}Z||_{\mathbb{H}_{[t_{k},t_{k+1}]}^{p}}^{p}\right).

The second equality follows by (

) and (

) and the third and fourth inequalities by Jensen’s inequality. Analogously, using the bound on

Remark 8
.

The proposed scheme is not fully implementable in practice. One key step is the computation of the conditional expectations in (
146
) which has to be performed numerically. There are several methods to estimate these. Among them there are nonparametric kernel regression
Bouchard and Touzi 2004
;
Lemor et al. 2006
, Malliavin regression
Bouchard and Touzi 2004
, quantization
Bally and Pagès 2003
and some other approaches. We discuss the nonparametric regression approach in some more detail which works by simulating
1\leq m\leq M
paths
X^{n,\pi,m}
of
X^{n,\pi}
and initialize
\bar{Y}_{T}^{n,\pi,m}=g(X_{T}^{n,\pi,m})
. Then we regress
\bar{Y}_{t_{k+1}}^{n,\pi,m}
and
\bar{Y}_{t_{k+1}}^{n,\pi,m}\Delta B_{k+1}^{m}
and
\bar{Y}_{t_{k+1}}^{n,\pi,m}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\rho(e)e\widetilde{\mu^{n,m}}(\mathrm{d}e,(t_{k},t_{k+1}])
on
X_{t_{k}}^{n,\pi,m}
. Details are presented in
Elie 2006
.

To compute the
L^{p}
error between the original backward SDE and the numerical backward SDE taking into account approximation of the jump process, discretization and estimation of conditional expectations we have to sum up the error of Corollary
1
, the error of a localization procedure and the statistical error by the kernel regression.
Elie 2006
derived the
L^{p}
error of the localization procedure. Furthermore,
Elie 2006
derived the statistical error which is in terms of the Euclidean norm on
\mathbb{R}^{M}
. Since all norms on
\mathbb{R}^{M}
are equivalent it is not much work to deduce a bound for the error in terms of the
p
-norm. All in all, if we choose some other parameters in the algorithm large enough, we can conclude that the total error is of the order
N^{-1/2}+\sigma^{p}(n)^{1/p}+\sigma^{2}(n)^{1/2}+N^{-1}\mathfrak{m}^{1}(n)
under Assumptions
1
and
2
.

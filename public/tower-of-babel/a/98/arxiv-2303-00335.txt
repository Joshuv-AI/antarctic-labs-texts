Subalgebras of Octonion Algebras
Norbert Knarr, Markus J. Stroppel

Abstract
For an arbitrary octonion algebra, we determine all subalgebras. It turns out that every subalgebra of dimension less than four is associative, while every subalgebra of dimension greater than four is not associative. In any split octonion algebra, there are both associative and non-associative subalgebras of dimension four. Except for one-dimensional subalgebras spanned by idempotents, any two isomorphic subalgebras are in the same orbit under automorphisms.

Introduction

Octonion algebras are the most complicated examples of composition
algebras. Composition algebras are (not necessarily associative) algebras
endowed with a multiplicative quadratic form such that the associated
polar form is not degenerate. As a tool for the investigation of
quadratic forms, they have their roots in number
theory. Four-dimensional composition algebras (also called quaternion
algebras) are ubiquitous in the theory of quadratic forms.
In the present paper, we are mainly concerned with composition
algebras of dimension
8
, also called
octonion algebras
(or
octaves).

It is known (see
[
22
, 1.6.2]
) that composition algebras
only occur in dimension
d\in\{1,2,4,8\}
. A composition algebra is
called
split
if the quadratic form is isotropic. Over any
field
F
, there are split composition algebras of
dimension
2
(isomorphic to
F\times F
with the quadratic form
mapping
(x,y)
to
xy
), of dimension
4
(isomorphic to

F^{2\times 2}
, the quadratic form is the determinant) and of
dimension
8
(obtained by doubling the split quaternion
algebra
F^{2\times 2}
, see
1.4
(i)
below).
These split composition algebras are determined uniquely by their
dimension and the underlying field
F
(see
[
22
, 1.8.1]
).

The existence of non-split composition algebras depends in an
essential way on properties of the ground field
F
. In particular, a
non-split composition algebra of dimension
2
over
F
is a separable
quadratic extension field. A non-split quaternion algebra exists if,
and only if, there is a separable quadratic extension and an
anisotropic hermitian form in two variables over that extension
(cp.
[
15
]
). In particular, every composition algebra of
dimension greater than two over a finite field is split. A quaternion
field (i.e., a non-split quaternion algebra) over
F
exists if, and
only if, there is a non-singular quadratic form on
F^{3}
such that the
polar form is not zero (see
[
13
, Th. 3.1.1]
). A necessary
(but not sufficient, see
[
13
, Th. 3.1.6]
) condition for
the existence of a non-split octonion algebra over
F
is of course
the existence of a non-singular quadratic form on
F^{8}
.

Octonion algebras play a role in group theory: For instance, the
automorphism group of an octonion algebra is a group of type

\mathrm{G}_{2}
, the action of the hyperplane orthogonal to
1
gives
the representation of that group on a vector space of dimension
7
. Several important subgroups of that group can conveniently be seen as
stabilizers of certain elements or subspaces in the octonion algebra
(see
[
3
]
), and various exceptional isomorphisms between classical groups can be
understood using representations as such stabilizers.

We also remark that non-split octonion algebras of dimension
8

coordinatize Moufang planes that are not desarguesian, and that every
non-desarguesian Moufang plane is obtained in this way
(
[
16
]
,
[
18
, Satz 50, p. 106 and Abschnitt 6,
pp. 157–186]
,
[
23
, Ch. 19]
). Split octonion algebras are used to construct generalized hexagons
(namely, the split Cayley hexagons, see
[
21
, p. 207]
).

In the present paper, we study the subalgebras of octonion algebras.
The split case is of special interest; we find many types of
subalgebras in split octonion algebras that do not have counterparts
in non-split algebras. We also prove that subalgebras that are isomorphic as abstract
algebras are in the same orbit under automorphisms of the octonion
algebra (except if they are one-dimensional, and spanned by
1
and an
idempotent
p\neq 1
, respectively). See Figure
1
in Section
8
below for a
graph showing the relative position of (orbits of) subalgebras. As a byproduct, we obtain that every subalgebra of dimension less
than
4
in a composition algebra is associative
(see
7.1
). In split octonion algebras, there do occur
subalgebras of dimension
4
that are not associative
(see
2.3
), and subalgebras of dimensions
5

and
6
, respectively (see
2.2
); no
subalgebra of dimension greater than
4
is associative
(see
7.1
).

Using our list of subalgebras, we identify the commutative
subalgebras, see
7.3
. It turns out that every
commutative subalgebra is associative.

Maximal subalgebras of octonion algebras have been studied
in
[
19
]
, a gap in that paper was noted in
[
6
]

and closed in
[
17
]
. Unary subalgebras (i.e. subalgebras
containing the neutral element of multiplication) of finite octonion
algebras have been determined in
[
8
]
. Our present treatment is independent of those results.

1.1 Definition
.

Let
F
be a field, and let
(A,+,\cdot)
be any (not
necessarily unary, not necessarily associative) algebra over
F
. A quadratic form
q\colon A\to F
is called
multiplicative
if

q(x\cdot y)=q(x)q(y)
holds for all
x,y\in A
.

1.2 Lemma: multiplicative forms
.

Let
(A,+,\cdot)
be any algebra over
F
. Assume that
a,b\in A
are such that the maps

\rho_{a}\colon A\to A\colon x\mapsto xa
and

\lambda_{b}\colon A\to A\colon x\mapsto bx
are bijections, and
define the multiplication
*
on
A
by

x*y:=\rho_{a}^{-1}(x)\cdot\lambda_{b}^{-1}(y)
.

(a)

The element
b\cdot a
is a neutral element for the multiplication
*
.

(b)

The triplet
(\lambda_{b}^{-1}|\mathrm{id}|\rho_{a}^{-1})
is an isotopism from

(A,+,*)
onto
(A,+,\cdot)
.

(c)

Assume that there is a non-zero multiplicative quadratic
form
q
on
(A,+,\cdot)
.

Then
s:=q(b\cdot a)
is not zero, and
sq(x*y)=q(x)q(y)

holds for all
x,y\in A
.

The form
s^{-1}q
is thus a non-zero multiplicative form on

(A,+,*)
.

Proof.

We compute
(b\cdot a)*y=\rho_{a}^{-1}(b\cdot a)\cdot\lambda_{b}^{-1}(y)=b\cdot\lambda_{b}^{-1}(y)=\lambda_{b}(\lambda_{b}^{-1}(y))=y
and
x*(b\cdot a)=\rho_{a}^{-1}(x)\cdot\lambda_{b}^{-1}(b\cdot a)=\rho_{a}^{-1}(x)\cdot a=\rho_{a}(\rho_{a}^{-1}(x))=x
. This shows that
b\cdot a
is a neutral element for the
multiplication
*
.

For
x,y\in A
, we note

\mathrm{id}(x*y)=x*y=\rho_{a}^{-1}(x)\cdot\lambda_{b}^{-1}(y)
. This is the defining property of an isotopism from
(A,+,*)
onto

(A,+,\cdot)
, cp.
[
12
, Section 1]
.

Pick
u\in A
such that
q(u)\neq 0
, and let

v:=\lambda_{b}^{-1}(\rho_{a}^{-1}(u))
. Then

0\neq q(u)=q((b\cdot v)\cdot a)=q(b\cdot v)q(a)=q(b)q(v)q(a)=q(b)q(a)q(v)=q(b\cdot a)q(v)
yields
q(b\cdot a)\neq 0
. We
compute
sq({x*y})=sq\left(\rho_{a}^{-1}(x)\cdot\lambda_{b}^{-1}(y)\right)=\left(q(a)q(b)\right)q\left(\rho_{a}^{-1}(x)\right)q\left(\lambda_{b}^{-1}(y)\right)=q\left(\rho_{a}^{-1}(x)\right)q(a)q(b)q\left(\lambda_{b}^{-1}(y)\right)=q\left(a\cdot\rho_{a}^{-1}(x)\right)q\left(b\cdot\lambda_{b}^{-1}(y)\right)=q(x)q(y)
. This implies the last assertion.
∎

1.3 Definition
.

A
composition algebra
over
F
is a (not necessarily
associative) algebra
C
over
F
with a multiplicative quadratic form

{\mathrm{N}}:={\mathrm{N}}_{C}\colon C\to F
(called the
norm form
) such
that the polar form of
{\mathrm{N}}
is not degenerate. This polar form will be written as

(x|y):={\mathrm{N}}(x+y)-{\mathrm{N}}(x)-{\mathrm{N}}(y)
. We also assume that the algebra contains a neutral element for its
multiplication, denoted by
1
. (However, see
1.2
for the case of isotopes without
1
.)

As usual, the ground field
F
is embedded as
F1
in
C
. The composition algebra is called
split
if it contains divisors
of zero (i.e., non-zero elements of norm zero,
see
1.4
(f)
below).
If no confusion is expected we write multiplication as juxtaposition.

The first chapter of
[
22
]
gives a comprehensive
introduction into composition algebras over arbitrary fields,
including the characteristic two case. We collect the basic facts that we need in the present paper (for
proofs, consult
[
22
]
):

We use the most comprehensive notion of “subalgebra” here:

1.5 Definition
.

A
subalgebra
in a given (not necessarily unary, not necessarily
associative) algebra
B
over
F
is a vector subspace
A

(over
F
) which is closed under multiplication.

In particular, even if
B
contains a neutral element of
multiplication, we do
not
require it to lie in the
subalgebra. A subalgebra

may well contain a neutral element for its
multiplication; this will then be an idempotent centralized by
A
,
but need not be a unit element in
B
(in general, it will not even
be centralized by
B
, and need not be invertible).

We remark that in
[
22
, 1.2.1]
much stronger properties
are required for a
composition subalgebra
; that is a
subalgebra (in the sense defined above) that contains
1
, and such
that the restriction of the norm form has non-degenerate polar form.

Note also that only subalgebras containing
1
are considered
in
[
8
]
.

A vector subspace

\kappa
if, and only if, it either
contains
1
or is contained in
1^{\perp}
.

Proof.

The assertion is obvious if
V\subseteq 1^{\perp}
. If
1\in V
then
{\mathchoice{\vbox{\halign{#\cr\kern 1.0pt\cr\kern 1.0pt\leaders{\hrule}{\hfill}\kern 0.0pt\crcr\kern 1.0pt\cr$\hfil\displaystyle{v}\hfil$\crcr}}}{\vbox{\halign{#\cr\kern 1.0pt\cr\kern 1.0pt\leaders{\hrule}{\hfill}\kern 0.0pt\crcr\kern 1.0pt\cr$\hfil\textstyle{v}$\crcr}}}{\vbox{\halign{#\cr\kern 1.0pt\cr\kern 1.0pt\leaders{\hrule}{\hfill}\kern 0.0pt\crcr\kern 1.0pt\cr$\hfil\scriptstyle{v}\hfil$\crcr}}}{\vbox{\halign{#\cr\kern 1.0pt\cr\kern 1.0pt\leaders{\hrule}{\hfill}\kern 0.0pt\crcr\kern 1.0pt\cr$\hfil\scriptscriptstyle{v}\hfil$\crcr}}}}={\mathrm{T}}(v)-v
lies in
V
, for each

v\in V
. Conversely, if
v\in V\smallsetminus 1^{\perp}
then
0\neq{\mathrm{T}}(v)\in V\cap F
yields
1\in V
.
∎

Note that a subalgebra will, in general, not be a composition algebra
since the polar form of the restriction of the norm form
{\mathrm{N}}
to the
subalgebra may be degenerate.

1.7 Quaternion algebras
.

In the context of abstract algebras over
F
, a
split
quaternion algebra
is an algebra
H
over
F
which is isomorphic
to the matrix algebra
F^{2\times 2}
. A
quaternion field
can
be defined as a skewfield (i.e., an associative division
algebra) with center
F
and dimension
4
over
F
, we call this a

non-split quaternion algebra
over
F
.

Both classes of quaternion algebras are completely contained in the
class of composition algebras; their members are exactly the
composition algebras of dimension
4
. The required (multiplicative)
norm is obtained as the determinant in the split case. For the
non-split case, one uses a two-dimensional subalgebra, and the fact
that the tensor product with that subalgebra yields a split
quaternion algebra. See also
[
15
]
.

A
quaternion subalgebra
of an octonion algebra over
F
is a
subalgebra
H
that is isomorphic (as an abstract algebra) to some
quaternion algebra over
F
.

For a quaternion subalgebra
H
of an octonion algebra
\mathbb{O}
, we do
not require that the restriction of the norm
{\mathrm{N}}_{\mathbb{O}}
to
H

satisfies any non-degeneracy conditions, and we do not require

a priori
that
H
contains the neutral element
1

of
\mathbb{O}
. However, it turns out that the quaternion algebra is
embedded as nice as can be, see
4.1
below.

1.8 Lemma
.

Let
\mathbb{O}
be a
non-split
octonion algebra over a
field
F
with
\operatorname{char}{F}\neq 2
. Then any two elements of
\mathbb{O}
are
contained in a quaternion subalgebra of
\mathbb{O}
.

Proof.

Consider
a,b\in\mathbb{O}
. If
a\in F+Fb
(in particular, if
a\in F
) then the assertion is
obvious from the result that every single element
b
of
\mathbb{O}
is
contained in a quaternion algebra (this even holds if
\mathbb{O}
is
split, or if
\operatorname{char}{F}=2
, see
[
22
, 1.6.4]
). So assume that
a\notin F+Fb
. Then
A:=F+Fa
is a
quadratic extension field of
F
, and
\mathbb{O}
is a vector space
over
A
. So
Ab
has trivial intersection with
A
, and

H:=A+Ab=F+Fa+Fb+Fab
is a vector space of dimension
4

over
F
. Clearly, that vector space coincides with the
(associative) subalgebra generated by
\{a,b\}
in
\mathbb{O}
. The
restriction
{\mathrm{N}}|_{H}
of the norm form
{\mathrm{N}}_{\mathbb{O}}
is non-singular, and
then non-degenerate because
\operatorname{char}{F}\neq 2
. So
H
is a (non-split)
composition algebra (of dimension
4
), and thus a quaternion field.
∎

1.9 Remark
.

If
\mathbb{O}
is a non-split octonion algebra of characteristic
2

then
\mathbb{O}
contains commutative (and associative) subalgebras of
dimension
4
(obtained as fixed point sets of involutory
automorphisms, see
[
11
, 4.5]
). In fact, such a
subalgebra forms a totally inseparable field extension of degree
4

and exponent
1
over
F
. Two elements generating such a subalgebra
do not lie in any common quaternion field, obviously,
so
1.8
does not extend to the characteristic two
case.

1.10 Remark
.

In a split octonion algebra, it is no longer true that any two
elements lie in a quaternion subalgebra. E.g., if
a,b
are two
nilpotent elements such that
ab\neq 0
then the algebra
A_{a,b}

generated by them is a Heisenberg algebra (see
2.7

below). Such a Heisenberg algebra contains two-dimensional
subalgebras with trivial multiplication (such as
Fa+Fab
), and is
therefore not contained in any quaternion algebra.
So
1.8
does not extend to the split case.

2.7 Heisenberg subalgebras
.

Take a nilpotent element
a\neq 0
(i.e., any non-zero element
a
of
norm and trace
0
), and pick a nilpotent element

. Then
{\mathrm{N}}(ab)=0
, and

(see
1.4
(c)
) gives

. Moreover, computation in the (associative) subalgebra generated
by

and

yields

,

(ab)a=-(ba)a{{}=0}
. Analogously, we find
b(ab)=0=(ab)b
and
(ab)(ab)=0
. So the
linear span of
\{a,b,ab\}
is closed under multiplication, and
forms a subalgebra
A_{a,b}
.

There are two cases:

(a)

If
ab=0
then
ba={\mathchoice{\vbox{\halign{#\cr\kern 1.0pt\cr\kern 1.0pt\leaders{\hrule}{\hfill}\kern 0.0pt\crcr\kern 1.0pt\cr$\hfil\displaystyle{ab}\hfil$\crcr}}}{\vbox{\halign{#\cr\kern 1.0pt\cr\kern 1.0pt\leaders{\hrule}{\hfill}\kern 0.0pt\crcr\kern 1.0pt\cr$\hfil\textstyle{ab}$\crcr}}}{\vbox{\halign{#\cr\kern 1.0pt\cr\kern 1.0pt\leaders{\hrule}{\hfill}\kern 0.0pt\crcr\kern 1.0pt\cr$\hfil\scriptstyle{ab}\hfil$\crcr}}}{\vbox{\halign{#\cr\kern 1.0pt\cr\kern 1.0pt\leaders{\hrule}{\hfill}\kern 0.0pt\crcr\kern 1.0pt\cr$\hfil\scriptscriptstyle{ab}\hfil$\crcr}}}}=0
, and

is a

is trivial. We then conclude that the multiplication on

is trivial
(i.e., every product of two elements in

equals
0
).

(b)

If

then

because

See

4
Automorphisms of (split) composition algebras

The following is a variant of the well known Skolem-Noether Theorem
(e.g., see
[
10
, 4.6, p. 222]
or
[
4
, 5.1.7,
p. 183]
). Note, however, that we deal with subalgebras of a non-associative
algebra, and do not require a priori that the subalgebras
contain
1
.

4.1 Lemma
.

Let
\mathbb{O}
and
\mathbb{O}^{\prime}
be octonion algebras over
F
, and let

H<\mathbb{O}
and
H^{\prime}<\mathbb{O}^{\prime}
be quaternion subalgebras. Then both
H
and
H^{\prime}
contain the neutral element
1

of
\mathbb{O}
and of
\mathbb{O}^{\prime}
, respectively. Moreover, we have:

(a)

If
\mathbb{O}
and
\mathbb{O}^{\prime}
are isomorphic then every non-constant

F
-linear multiplicative map from
H
to
H^{\prime}
extends to an
isomorphism from
\mathbb{O}
onto
\mathbb{O}^{\prime}
.

(b)

If
H
or
H^{\prime}
is split then every non-constant

F
-linear multiplicative map from
H
to
H^{\prime}
extends to an
isomorphism from
\mathbb{O}
onto
\mathbb{O}^{\prime}
.

Proof.

Let
e
and
e^{\prime}
be the neutral elements of multiplication in
H

and
H^{\prime}
, respectively. Then
H
is a quaternion subalgebra
of
\mathbb{O}
centralizing the idempotent
e
. From
3.5
we
infer
e\in F
and then
e=1
.

Now consider a non-constant
F
-linear multiplicative map

\beta\colon H\to H^{\prime}
. As
H
is a simple algebra, the ideal

\ker\beta
is trivial, and
\beta
is an isomorphism by dimension
reasons. The restrictions
{\mathrm{N}}_{\mathbb{O}}|_{H}
and
{\mathrm{N}}_{\smash{\mathbb{O}^{\prime}}}|_{\smash{H^{\prime}}}

of the norms are isometric because the quaternion subalgebras are
isomorphic. By Witt’s Cancellation Theorem (
[
25
, Satz 4,
p. 34]
,
[
1
, Folgerung, p. 160]
,
see
[
5
, p. 21, p. 35]
or
[
9
, 5.1,
12.12]
) the restrictions
{\mathrm{N}}_{\mathbb{O}}|_{H^{\perp}}
and

{\mathrm{N}}_{\smash{\mathbb{O}^{\prime}}}|_{\smash{H^{\prime}}^{\perp}}
of the norms are isometric,
as well. We pick any element
w\in H^{\perp}
with
{\mathrm{N}}(w)\neq 0
, and
find
w^{\prime}\in\smash{H^{\prime}}^{\perp}
with

{\mathrm{N}}_{\mathbb{O}}(w^{\prime})={\mathrm{N}}_{\smash{\mathbb{O}^{\prime}}}(w)
. The formula
(see
1.4
(i)
) for the multiplication in

\mathbb{O}=H+Hw
and in
\mathbb{O}^{\prime}=H^{\prime}+H^{\prime}w^{\prime}
, respectively, immediately
yields that mapping
a+xw\in H+Hw
to

\beta(a)+\beta(x)w^{\prime}\in H^{\prime}+H^{\prime}w^{\prime}
is an automorphism of
\mathbb{O}
.

If
H
or
H^{\prime}
is split then the existence of an isomorphism
between the quaternion algebras implies that both are split, and so
are the octonion algebras
\mathbb{O}
and
\mathbb{O}^{\prime}
. So the latter are
isomorphic, and our first assertion applies.
∎

4.2 Remark
.

Let
\mathbb{O}=H+Hw
be obtained by doubling a quaternion subalgebra
H
, using

w\in H^{\perp}
.
The
F
-linear automorphisms of
\mathbb{O}
that leave
H
invariant can
be given explicitly: they are the maps
\alpha_{s,t}\colon H+Hw\to H+Hw\colon a+xw\mapsto sas^{-1}+(txs^{-1})w
, where
s,t
are
invertible elements in
H
with
{\mathrm{N}}(s)={\mathrm{N}}(t)
.

In fact, the restriction of a given automorphism

\alpha\in\operatorname{Aut}_{F}(\mathbb{O})
with
\alpha(H)=H
to
H
gives an
F
-linear
automorphism of
H
, which is inner by the Skolem-Noether Theorem (e.g., see
[
10
, 4.6, p. 222]
or
[
4
, 5.1.7,
p. 183]
). So there exists
s\in H
with
{\mathrm{N}}(s)\neq 0
and
\alpha(x)=sxs^{-1}

for each
x\in H
. Now
\alpha(w)
lies in
H^{\perp}
, and is of the
form
\alpha(w)=uw
with
u\in H
. As
\alpha
preserves the norm,
we have
{\mathrm{N}}(u)=1
, and obtain

\alpha(xw)=\alpha(y)\,\alpha(w)=(sxs^{-1})(uw)=(usxs^{-1})w
,
for each
x\in H
. Putting
t:=us
gives the description as claimed.

4.3 Corollary
.

In any octonion algebra
\mathbb{O}
, two quaternion subalgebras are in the
same orbit under
\operatorname{Aut}_{F}(\mathbb{O})
if, and only if, they are isomorphic
(as
F
-algebras). ∎

4.4 Lemma
.

Let
\mathbb{O}
be an octonion algebra over a field
F
. Then non-central elements of
\mathbb{O}
are in the same orbit under

\operatorname{Aut}_{F}(\mathbb{O})
if, and only if, they have the same norm and the same
trace.

Proof.

Clearly elements in the same orbit have the same norm and trace.

Now consider
a_{1},a_{2}\in\mathbb{O}\smallsetminus F
with
{\mathrm{N}}(a_{1})={\mathrm{N}}(a_{2})
and

{\mathrm{T}}(a_{1})={\mathrm{T}}(a_{2})
. Applying Witt’s theorem, we find
b_{j}\in a_{j}^{\perp}
with
{\mathrm{T}}(b_{1})={\mathrm{T}}(b_{2})=1
and

{\mathrm{N}}(b_{1})={\mathrm{N}}(b_{2})\neq 0
.

If
{\mathrm{N}}(a_{1})\neq 0
then
H_{j}:=F+Fb_{j}+(F+Fb_{j})a_{j}
is a
quaternion algebra, and the
F
-algebras
H_{1}
and
H_{2}
are
isomorphic because their norm forms are isometric
(see
[
22
, 1.7.1]
). By
4.1
(a)
, any
F
-linear
isomorphism from
H_{1}
onto
H_{2}
extends to an automorphism
of
\mathbb{O}
. If
\alpha\in\operatorname{Aut}_{F}(\mathbb{O})
is such an extension then the
image
\alpha(a_{1})\in H_{2}
has the same norm and trace as
a_{2}
,
and is thus a conjugate of
a_{2}
in
H_{2}
. Adapting
\alpha
by the
extension of a suitable (inner) automorphism of
H_{2}
, we thus
obtain an
F
-linear automorphism of
\mathbb{O}
mapping
a_{1}
to
a_{2}
.

If
{\mathrm{N}}(a_{1})=0
then
a_{j}
is contained in a split quaternion
subalgebra
H_{j}

(see
1.4
(j)
). Since any two split quaternion algebras over
F
are isomorphic, we
can proceed as above.
∎

5.9 Lemma
.

Let
m,n
be two non-trivial elements of

with

{\mathrm{N}}(m)=0={\mathrm{N}}(n)
and
mn=0
. Then

, and

is a subalgebra of dimension

. These subalgebras form a single orbit under
\operatorname{Aut}_{F}(\mathbb{O})
, and for

\operatorname{Aut}_{F}(\mathbb{O})
acts

Proof.

We know from
5.3
that
m\mathbb{O}

and

are subalgebras, so
m\mathbb{O}\cap n\mathbb{O}
is a subalgebra, as

lie in

, we have

.

Using

, we may assume

. There are

such that

, and

yields

and

. On the other hand, we have

, and obtain

and

∎

5.10 Corollary
.

The group
\operatorname{Aut}_{F}(\mathbb{O})
acts transitively on the set of all pairs

(Fm,Fn)
of different subspaces spanned by non-trivial elements

,

in
\mathbb{O}
with

. ∎

5.11 Totally singular subalgebras
.

Let
A
be a subalgebra of
\mathbb{O}
, and assume
1\notin A
. Then
A
is totally singular, and
\dim{A}\leq 4
. In order to
give representatives for the orbits of subalgebras, we write

\mathbb{O}=\mathbb{H}+\mathbb{H}w
as the double of the split quaternion algebra

\mathbb{H}:=F^{2\times 2}
with
w
such that
w^{2}=1
, and use

p_{0}:=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}1&0\\
0&0\end{smallmatrix}\right)
and
n_{0}:=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}0&1\\
0&0\end{smallmatrix}\right)
.

(a)

If
\dim{A}=1
then

with
a\in\mathbb{O}
such that

{\mathrm{N}}(a)=0
. These subalgebras form two orbits under
\operatorname{Aut}_{F}(\mathbb{O})
,
represented, respectively, by
Fp_{0}
, and by
Fn_{0}
. Note that
F
and
Fp_{0}
are isomorphic as algebras, under an isomorphism that maps
1\in F
to

p_{0}\in\mathbb{O}\smallsetminus\{1\}
.

(b)

Totally singular subalgebras of dimension
2
form three
orbits under
\operatorname{Aut}_{F}(\mathbb{O})
:

(i)

Algebras with a left identity for the multiplication, such
as
Fp_{0}+Fn_{0}
(see
5.7
).

(ii)

Algebras with a right identity for the multiplication,
obtained by applying some anti-automorphism (such as
\kappa
)
to algebras with left identity (see
5.7
).

(iii)

Algebras with trivial multiplication, such as
(Fp_{0}+Fn_{0})w

(see
5.9
).

Each one of the algebras in the latter orbit is contained
in
1^{\perp}
, while none of those in the first two of these orbits
is contained in
1^{\perp}
.

(c)

Totally singular subalgebras of dimension
3
form two
orbits under
\operatorname{Aut}_{F}(\mathbb{O})
, namely:

(i)

Heisenberg algebras

with

, obtained as

n\mathbb{O}\cap\mathbb{O}n
for

such that

. These subalgebras are contained in

.

(ii)

Algebras of the form

where
m,n\in\mathbb{O}
are
linearly independent, with
m^{2}=0=n^{2}=0=mn=nm
. None of these subalgebras is contained in
1^{\perp}
.

(d)

Totally singular subalgebras of dimension

form two
orbits under
\operatorname{Aut}_{F}(\mathbb{O})
, namely:

(i)

n\mathbb{O}
for
n\in\mathbb{O}
such that
n^{2}=0\neq n
.

(ii)

\mathbb{O}n
for
n\in\mathbb{O}
such that
n^{2}=0\neq n
.

In particular, two totally singular subalgebras of
\mathbb{O}
are
isomorphic if, and only if, they are in the same orbit

6.2 Subalgebras of dimension greater than four
.

Let
A
be a subalgebra of
\mathbb{O}
. If
\dim{A}>4
then either
A=\mathbb{O}
, or
\mathbb{O}
is split and one of
the following cases occurs:

(a)

\dim{A}=5
, and there exists a nilpotent element

such that

A=n\mathbb{O}+\mathbb{O}n
. These subalgebras form a single orbit
under
\operatorname{Aut}_{F}(\mathbb{O})
.

(b)

\dim{A}=6
, and there exist two linearly independent nilpotent

. Then

\operatorname{Aut}_{F}(\mathbb{O})

Proof.

We know that
1\in A
because
\dim{A}>4
implies that
A
is not
totally singular. From
5.1
we know

A\not\subseteq 1^{\perp}
. We show first that there is a two-dimensional subalgebra

B\subseteq A
such that the restriction of the polar form to
B
is
not degenerate; then
B
is a composition algebra.

If
\operatorname{char}{F}=2
we just pick
b\in A
such that
b\notin 1^{\perp}
. Then
b\notin F\subseteq 1^{\perp}
, and

B:=F+Fb\subseteq A
is a two-dimensional composition
algebra, as required.

If
\operatorname{char}{F}\neq 2

is not totally

4
. The

3
. So there exists

{\mathrm{N}}(a)\neq 0
, and

Now consider

. If there exists an invertible element

. If there is still an invertible element

. So we may assume that

of

. Choose

, and

and

(i)

It remains to consider the case where

nilpotent elements. Then the norm vanishes on that subspace, and

6.3 Lemma
.

Let

be a

Proof.

We note first of all that

6.5 Remark
.

The examples in
6.4
(d)(ii)
show
that not every associative subalgebra of a split octonion algebra is
contained in a quaternion algebra.

7
Associative and commutative subalgebras

7.1 Associativity of subalgebras
.

Let
\mathbb{O}
be an octonion algebra.

(a)

Every subalgebra of dimension less than
4
is associative.

(b)

If
\mathbb{O}
is split then there do exist subalgebras of
dimension
4
that are not associative, namely, the totally
singular ones (of the form
n\mathbb{O}

or
\mathbb{O}n
with
{n^{2}=0\neq n\in\mathbb{O}}
). Every other subalgebra of dimension
4
is associative.

(c)

Every subalgebra of dimension greater than
4
contains a
non-associative subalgebra of dimension
4
.

(d)

If
\mathbb{O}
is split then a given subalgebra is not associative if,
and only if, it contains a maximal totally singular subspace.

(e)

If
\mathbb{O}
is not split then every proper subalgebra is
associative.

Proof.

If a subalgebra is not totally singular then it contains
1
. We
have seen in
6.4
that each subalgebra
A
with

1\in A
and
\dim{A}\leq 4
is associative. A totally singular
subalgebra is associative if, and only if, it has dimension less
than
4
; see
5.11
. By
5.11
.
(d)
, the
totally singular subalgebras of dimension
4
are those of the form

n\mathbb{O}
and
\mathbb{O}n
, repsectively, with
n^{2}=0\neq n
. So assertions
(a)
and
(b)
are established.

Each proper subalgebra of dimension greater than
4
contains a
totally singular subalgebra of dimension
4
(of the form
n\mathbb{O}
for
some nilpotent element
n
, see
6.2
). These subalgebras are not associative. This gives assertions
(c)
and
(d)
.

If
\mathbb{O}
is not split then every proper subalgebra has dimension at
most
4
, and there are no nilpotent elements apart from
0
. So
assertions
(a)
and
(b)
yield
assertion
(e)
.
∎

7.2 Corollary
.

A
4
-dimensional subalgebra of
\mathbb{O}
is associative if, and only
if, it contains a neutral element for its multiplication. By
5.5
, such a neutral element in a
4
-dimensional
subalgebra necessarily equals
1
. ∎

7.3 Commutativity of subalgebras
.

Let
\mathbb{O}
be an octonion algebra, and let
A
be a subalgebra. If
\mathbb{O}
is split, we write
\mathbb{O}=\mathbb{H}+\mathbb{H}w
, with

\mathbb{H}=F^{2\times 2}
and
w^{2}=1
. The subalgebra

is commutative in the following cases:

(a)

\dim{A}=1
.

(b)

\dim{A}=2
, and

.

(c)

\dim{A}=2
, and
A
is in the
\operatorname{Aut}_{F}(\mathbb{O})
-orbit of

.

(d)

\dim{A}=3
, and
A
is in the
\operatorname{Aut}_{F}(\mathbb{O})
-orbit of

F+(Fp_{0}+Fn_{0})w
.

If
\operatorname{char}{F}\neq 2
then these are the only commutative
subalgebras.

If
\operatorname{char}{F}=2

others):

(e)

\dim{A}=3
, and
A
is in the
\operatorname{Aut}_{F}(\mathbb{O})

(f)

\dim{A}=4

, where

(g)

\dim{A}=4
, and

is a totally inseparable field extension of
exponent

.

In any case, every commutative subalgebra of

is associative,

We use our classification of subalgebras,

,

, and

-linear

X
in

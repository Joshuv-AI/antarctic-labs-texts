Homology of weighted path complexes and directed hypergraphs
Yuri Muranov, Anna Szczepkowska, Vladimir Vershinin

Abstract
We introduce the weighted path homology on the category of weighted directed hypergraphs and describe conditions of homotopy invariance of weighted path homology groups. We give several examples that explain the nontriviality of the introduced notions.

A

\Pi=(V,P)
consists of a set

V

P
of
elementary paths

(i_{0}\dots i_{n})

of vertices such that one-element sequence
(i)
lies in
P

(i_{0}\dots i_{n})\in P

(i_{0}...i_{n-1})\in P
,
(i_{1}...i_{n})\in P

The

of a path

(i_{0}...i_{n})

n

\Pi_{V}=(V,P_{V})

for which
P_{V}
consists of all paths of finite length on
V

A

of maps

where
f_{V}\colon V\to W

and
f_{P}(i_{0}\dots i_{n})\colon=\left(f_{V}(i_{0})\dots f_{V}(i_{n})\right)
lays in
P
.
Thus we obtain a category
\mathcal{P}
whose objects are path complexes and
whose morphisms are morphisms of path complexes.

Let
J=\{0,1\}
be the two-element set. For any set
V=\{0,\dots,n\}
, let
V\times J

be as usual the Cartesian product.
Let
V^{\prime}
be a copy of the set
V
with the elements
\{0^{\prime},\dots,n^{\prime}\}

corresponds to

V\times J

with

corresponds to
i

(i,1)
corresponds to
i^{\prime}
for all

.
Thus
V
is identified with
V\times\{0\}\subset V\times J
and
V^{\prime}
is
identified with
V\times\{1\}\subset V\times J
.
The natural bijection

defines the set of path

on

by the condition:
(i_{0}^{\prime}\dots i_{n}^{\prime})\in P^{\prime}

\begin{matrix}P^{\uparrow}=P\cup P^{\prime}\cup P^{\#},\\
{P^{\#}}=\{q^{\#}_{k}=(i_{0}\dots i_{k}\,i_{k}^{\prime},i^{\prime}_{k+1}\dots i_{n}^{\prime})\,|\,q=(i_{0}\dots i_{k}\,i_{k+1}\dots i_{n})\in P\}.\\
\end{matrix}

There are morphisms

and

which are induced by the natural inclusion of
V
in
V\times\{0\}
and in
V\times\{1\}
, respectively.

Let

Theorem 2.6
.

Let

Proof.

It is sufficient to consider the case of one-step weighted homotopy

F\colon{\Pi^{\uparrow}}^{\delta}\to\Sigma^{\delta}
between
f^{\delta}

and
g^{\delta}
. By definition

F_{*}\circ{[i_{\bullet}]}_{*}=f_{*}
and

F_{*}\circ{[j_{\bullet}]}_{*}=g_{*}
.
We define a chain
homotopy

L_{n}\colon\Omega_{n}^{w}(P)\rightarrow\Omega_{n+1}^{w}(S)

such that

\partial^{w}L_{n}+L_{n-1}\partial^{w}=g_{\ast}-f_{\ast}.

Let the element

belong to
\Omega_{n}^{w}(P)
.
Then, by definition,
\tau(v)\in\mathcal{R}_{n+1}^{reg}(P^{\uparrow})
.
To prove that

\tau(v)\in\Omega_{n+1}^{w}(P^{\uparrow})

\partial^{w}\tau(v)\in\mathcal{R}_{n}^{reg}(P^{\uparrow})

\partial^{w}v\in\mathcal{R}_{n-1}^{reg}(P)\subset\Omega_{n-1}^{w}(V)

\tau(v)\in\Omega_{n+1}^{w}(P^{\uparrow})

n\geq 0

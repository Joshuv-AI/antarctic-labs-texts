K K -rings of wonderful varieties and matroids
Matt Larson, Shiyue Li, Sam Payne, Nicholas Proudfoot

Abstract
. We study the K K -ring of the wonderful variety of a hyperplane arrangement and give a combinatorial presentation that depends only on the underlying matroid. We use this combinatorial presentation to define the K K -ring of an arbitrary loopless matroid. We construct an exceptional isomorphism, with integer coefficients, to the Chow ring of the matroid that satisfies a Hirzebruch–Riemann–Roch-type formula, generalizing a recent construction of Berget, Eur, Spink, and Tseng for the permutohedral variety (the wonderful variety of a Boolean arrangement). As an application, we give combinatorial formulas for Euler characteristics of arbitrary line bundles on wonderful varieties. We give analogous constructions and results for augmented wonderful varieties, and for Deligne–Mumford–Knudsen moduli spaces of stable rational curves with marked points.



1.
Introduction

Let
L
be a finite dimensional vector space over a field
\mathbb{F}
, and let
\mathcal{A}=\{H_{e}\mid e\in E\}
be a finite
multiset of hyperplanes in
L
intersecting only at the origin.
The wonderful variety
W_{\!\mathcal{A}}
is a smooth compactification of
\mathbb{P}(L)\setminus\bigcup_{H\in\mathcal{A}}\mathbb{P}(H)
,
originally studied by De Concini and Procesi
[
DCP95
]
. The augmented wonderful variety
W_{\!\mathcal{A}}^{{\operatorname{aug}}}
is a smooth
compactification of
L
, introduced in
[
BHM+22a
]
, that contains
W_{\!\mathcal{A}}
as a divisor.
The Chow rings of these spaces have been extensively studied and have combinatorial presentations that depend only on the underlying matroid. As a result, such rings are naturally defined for arbitrary, not necessarily realizable, matroids.
Presentations for the Chow ring of
W_{\!\mathcal{A}}

appear in
[
DCP95
,
FY04
,
BES23
]
, and this ring is used to prove log concavity
of the characteristic polynomial of
\mathcal{A}
(and more generally of any matroid) in
[
AHK18
]
.
A presentation of the Chow ring of
W^{{\operatorname{aug}}}_{\!\mathcal{A}}
appears in
[
BHM+22a
]
, and this ring is used to prove the top-heavy conjecture and the nonnegativity of Kazhdan–Lusztig polynomials of matroids
[
BHM+22b
]
.

Our goal is to study the Grothendieck rings of vector bundles
K(W_{\!\mathcal{A}})
and
K(W^{{\operatorname{aug}}}_{\!\mathcal{A}})
. We first give presentations for the
K
-rings using generators analogous to the generators given by Feichtner–Yuzvinsky for the
Chow ring of
W_{\mathcal{A}}
.
Despite the fact that the (non-homogeneous) relations among the Feichtner–Yuzvinsky generators in the
K
-ring are different from the (homogeneous) relations
among the Feichtner–Yuzvinsky generators in the Chow ring, we
construct
an integral isomorphism from the
K
-ring to the Chow ring which satisfies a Hirzebruch–Riemann–Roch-type formula, generalizing recent results for Boolean
arrangements
[
BEST23
,
EHL23
]
. The
K
-rings admit additional structures,
such as Adams operations, an Euler characteristic map, and Serre duality, which leads us to new results and new questions.
Most of our results can be extended to the moduli space
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{0,n}
of stable rational curves with
n

marked points, which is closely related to the wonderful variety for the braid arrangement
\mathcal{B}_{n-1}
.

1.1.
Definitions of the varieties

For any
S\subset E
, let

L_{S}\coloneqq\bigcap_{e\in S}H_{e}L^{S}\coloneqq L/L_{S}.

The dimension of
L^{S}
is called the
rank
of
S
, and the dimension of
L_{S}
is called the
corank
.
A subset
F\subset E
is called a
flat
if it is maximal within its rank, or, equivalently, if
L_{F}\subset H_{e}
implies that
e\in F
.

The
wonderful variety

W_{\!\mathcal{A}}
is defined as the closure of the image of the rational map

\mathbb{P}(L)\dasharrow\prod_{F\not=\emptyset}\mathbb{P}(L^{F}),

and the
augmented wonderful variety

W^{{\operatorname{aug}}}_{\!\mathcal{A}}
is defined as the closure of the image of the rational map

\mathbb{P}(L\oplus\mathbb{F})\dasharrow\prod_{F\not=\emptyset}\mathbb{P}(L^{F}\oplus\mathbb{F}),

where both products are over the set of nonempty flats.
For any nonempty flat
F
, let

\pi_{F}\colon W_{\!\mathcal{A}}\to\mathbb{P}(L^{F})\pi^{\operatorname{aug}}_{F}\colon W^{{\operatorname{aug}}}_{\!\mathcal{A}}\to\mathbb{P}(L^{F}\oplus\mathbb{F})

be the natural projections. We will write

\mathcal{L}_{F}\coloneqq\pi_{F}^{*}\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}(L^{F})}(1)\mathcal{L}_{F}^{\operatorname{aug}}\coloneqq(\pi_{F}^{\operatorname{aug}})^{*}\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}(L^{F}\oplus\,\mathbb{F})}(1).

The map
\pi_{E}\colon W_{\!\mathcal{A}}\to\mathbb{P}(L)
is an iterated blow-up. It is obtained by blowing up first the points
\mathbb{P}(L_{F})
for all corank 1 flats
F
, then the strict transforms of the lines
\mathbb{P}(L_{F})
for all corank 2 flats
F
, and so on. For any nonempty proper flat
F
, let
D_{F}\subset W_{\!\mathcal{A}}
be the closure of the preimage under the map
\pi_{E}
of the locus

\mathbb{P}(L_{F})\setminus\bigcup_{F\subsetneq G\not=E}\mathbb{P}(L_{G}).

Similarly, the map
\pi^{\operatorname{aug}}_{E}\colon W^{{\operatorname{aug}}}_{\!\mathcal{A}}\to\mathbb{P}(L\oplus\mathbb{F})

is an iterated blow-up, obtained by blowing up first the points
\mathbb{P}(L_{F}\oplus\{0\})
for all corank 1 flats
F
, then the strict transforms of the lines
\mathbb{P}(L_{F}\oplus\{0\})
for all corank 2
flats
F
, and so on. For any proper flat
F
, let
D_{F}^{\operatorname{aug}}\subset W^{{\operatorname{aug}}}_{\!\mathcal{A}}
be the
closure of the preimage under the map
\pi_{E}^{\operatorname{aug}}
of the locus

\mathbb{P}(L_{F}\oplus\{0\})\setminus\bigcup_{F\subsetneq G\not=E}\mathbb{P}(L_{G}\oplus\{0\}).

In addition, for any
e\in E
, let
D_{e}^{\operatorname{aug}}\subset W^{{\operatorname{aug}}}_{\!\mathcal{A}}
be the strict transform of
\mathbb{P}(H_{e}\oplus\mathbb{F})
.
We have a canonical isomorphism
W_{\!\mathcal{A}}\cong D_{\varnothing}^{\operatorname{aug}}\subset W^{{\operatorname{aug}}}_{\!\mathcal{A}}
,
which induces identifications
\mathcal{L}_{F}=\mathcal{L}_{F}^{\operatorname{aug}}|_{W_{\!\mathcal{A}}}
and
D_{F}=D_{F}^{\operatorname{aug}}\cap W_{\!\mathcal{A}}
for all nonempty proper
F
.

1.2.
Feichtner–Yuzvinsky presentations

Let
A(W_{\!\mathcal{A}})
denote the Chow ring of cycles modulo rational equivalence on
W_{\!\mathcal{A}}
, see
[
Ful98
]
. Similarly, let
A(W^{{\operatorname{aug}}}_{\!\mathcal{A}})
denote the Chow ring of
W^{{\operatorname{aug}}}_{\!\mathcal{A}}
. Let

T_{\mathcal{A}}\coloneqq\mathbb{Z}[x_{F}\mid\text{$F$ a flat}]\otimes\mathbb{Z}[y_{e}\mid e\in E].

Consider the map

\Phi_{\operatorname{FY}}^{\operatorname{aug}}\colon T_{\mathcal{A}}\to A(W^{{\operatorname{aug}}}_{\!\mathcal{A}})

that sends
x_{F}
to
[D_{F}^{\operatorname{aug}}]
for all proper
F
,
x_{E}
to
-c_{1}(\mathcal{L}_{E}^{\operatorname{aug}})
, and

y_{e}
to
[D_{e}^{\operatorname{aug}}]
for all
e\in E
. Composing with the pullback along the inclusion
W_{\!\mathcal{A}}\subset W^{{\operatorname{aug}}}_{\!\mathcal{A}}
, we obtain a map

\Phi_{\operatorname{FY}}\colon T_{\mathcal{A}}\to A(W_{\!\mathcal{A}})

that
sends
x_{F}
to
[D_{F}]
for all nonempty proper
F
and
y_{e}
to zero. The maps
\Phi_{\operatorname{FY}}
and
\Phi_{\operatorname{FY}}^{\operatorname{aug}}
are surjective, and their kernels were explicitly described in
[
FY04
,
BHM+22a
]
in terms of the flats of
\mathcal{A}
(Theorem
2.1
).

Next,
consider the map

\Psi_{\operatorname{FY}}^{\operatorname{aug}}\colon T_{\mathcal{A}}\to K(W^{{\operatorname{aug}}}_{\!\mathcal{A}})

that sends
x_{F}
to
[\mathcal{O}_{D_{F}^{\operatorname{aug}}}]
for all proper
F
,
x_{E}
to

1-[\mathcal{L}_{E}^{\operatorname{aug}}]
,
and
y_{e}
to
[\mathcal{O}_{D_{e}^{\operatorname{aug}}}]

for all
e\in E
. Composing with the pullback along the inclusion
W_{\!\mathcal{A}}\subset W^{{\operatorname{aug}}}_{\!\mathcal{A}}
, we obtain a map

\Psi_{\operatorname{FY}}\colon T_{\mathcal{A}}\to K(W_{\!\mathcal{A}})

that
sends
x_{F}
to
[\mathcal{O}_{D_{F}}]
for all nonempty proper
F
and
y_{e}
to zero. We show that the maps
\Psi_{\operatorname{FY}}
and
\Psi_{\operatorname{FY}}^{{\operatorname{aug}}}
are surjective and give an explicit description of the kernels in terms of the flats of
\mathcal{A}
(Theorem
2.3
). The relations generating the kernels of
\Psi_{\operatorname{FY}}
and
\Psi_{\operatorname{FY}}^{{\operatorname{aug}}}
are “inhomogeneous versions” of the relations generating the kernels of
\Phi_{\operatorname{FY}}
and
\Phi_{\operatorname{FY}}^{{\operatorname{aug}}}
.

1.3.
Exceptional isomorphisms and simplicial presentations

For any nonempty flat
F
, we define the following Chow and
K
-classes:

\displaystyle h_{F}

\displaystyle\coloneqq c_{1}(\mathcal{L}_{F})\in A(W_{\!\mathcal{A}})

\displaystyle\quad\quad h_{F}^{\operatorname{aug}}

\displaystyle\coloneqq c_{1}(\mathcal{L}_{F}^{\operatorname{aug}})\in A(W^{{\operatorname{aug}}}_{\!\mathcal{A}})

\displaystyle\eta_{F}

\displaystyle\coloneqq 1-[\mathcal{L}_{F}^{-1}]\in K(W_{\!\mathcal{A}})

\displaystyle\eta_{F}^{\operatorname{aug}}

\displaystyle\coloneqq 1-[(\mathcal{L}_{F}^{\operatorname{aug}})^{-1}]\in K(W^{{\operatorname{aug}}}_{\!\mathcal{A}}).

Remark 1.1
.

The Chow classes
\{h_{F}\}
and
\{h_{F}^{\operatorname{aug}}\}
may be interpreted in terms of divisors on the permutohedral or stellahedral variety coming from simplices and are therefore known as
simplicial generators
. In the non-augmented setting, these classes were studied in
[
Yuz02
,
BES23
]
, and the definitions and basic properties immediately generalize to the augmented setting. See
[
BES23
, Section 3.2]
for more details. We will similarly refer to
\{\eta_{F}\}
and
\{\eta_{F}^{\operatorname{aug}}\}

as the simplicial generators of the
K
-ring. The statement that they generate their respective
K
-rings is true but not obvious.

Our next result
says that there are isomorphisms from the
K
-ring to the Chow ring of the varieties
W_{\!\mathcal{A}}
and
W^{{\operatorname{aug}}}_{\!\mathcal{A}}
that satisfy analogues of the Hirzebruch–Riemann–Roch formula,
in which
\frac{1}{1-h_{E}}
and
\frac{1}{1-h_{E}^{\operatorname{aug}}}
play the roles of the respective Todd classes.
The special case in which
\mathcal{A}
is the Boolean arrangement (i.e., the number of hyperplanes is equal to the dimension of
L
)
appeared in
[
BEST23
, Theorem D]
in the non-augmented setting and in
[
EHL23
, Theorem 1.8]
in the augmented setting. Our techniques give new proofs of these results.

Theorem 1.2
.

There are isomorphisms

\zeta_{\mathcal{A}}\colon K(W_{\!\mathcal{A}})\to A(W_{\!\mathcal{A}})
and
\zeta_{\mathcal{A}}^{\operatorname{aug}}\colon K(W^{{\operatorname{aug}}}_{\!\mathcal{A}})\to A(W^{{\operatorname{aug}}}_{\!\mathcal{A}})

characterized by the property that
\zeta_{\mathcal{A}}(\eta_{F})=h_{F}
and

\zeta_{\mathcal{A}}^{\operatorname{aug}}(\eta^{\operatorname{aug}}_{F})=h^{\operatorname{aug}}_{F}.

For any classes
\xi\in K(W_{\!\mathcal{A}})
and
\xi^{\operatorname{aug}}\in K(W^{{\operatorname{aug}}}_{\!\mathcal{A}})
, we have

\chi(W_{\!\mathcal{A}},\xi)=\deg_{W_{\!\mathcal{A}}}\left(\frac{\zeta_{\mathcal{A}}(\xi)}{1-h_{E}}\right)\chi(W^{{\operatorname{aug}}}_{\!\mathcal{A}},\xi^{\operatorname{aug}})=\deg_{W^{{\operatorname{aug}}}_{\!\mathcal{A}}}\left(\frac{\zeta_{\mathcal{A}}^{\operatorname{aug}}(\xi^{\operatorname{aug}})}{1-h_{E}^{\operatorname{aug}}}\right).

Remark 1.3
.

The isomorphisms of Theorem
1.2
are not related to the Chern character homomorphisms;
in particular, they do not coincide with the respective Chern characters after tensoring with the rational numbers.
If we replaced
\zeta_{\mathcal{A}}
(respectively
\zeta_{\mathcal{A}}^{\operatorname{aug}}
) with the Chern character,
a similar formula would hold with
\frac{1}{1-h_{E}}
(respectively
\frac{1}{1-h_{E}^{\operatorname{aug}}}
)
replaced by the Todd class.

Let
S_{\mathcal{A}}\coloneqq\mathbb{Z}[u_{F}\mid\text{$F$ a nonempty flat}\},
and
consider the map

\Phi_{\nabla}^{\operatorname{aug}}\colon S_{\mathcal{A}}\to A(W^{{\operatorname{aug}}}_{\!\mathcal{A}})

that sends
u_{F}
to
h^{\operatorname{aug}}_{F}
for all
F
. Composing with the pullback along the inclusion
W_{\!\mathcal{A}}\subset W^{{\operatorname{aug}}}_{\!\mathcal{A}}
, we obtain a map

\Phi_{\nabla}\colon S_{\mathcal{A}}\to A(W_{\!\mathcal{A}})

that
sends
u_{F}
to
h_{F}
for all
F
. The maps
\Phi_{\nabla}
and
\Phi_{\nabla}^{{\operatorname{aug}}}
are surjective, and the ideal of relations can be computed explicitly in terms of the arrangement (Theorem
5.3
). This is called the
simplicial presentation
of
A(M)
and
A^{{\operatorname{aug}}}(M)
.

For the
K
-theoretic analogue of the simplicial generators, consider the map

\Psi_{\nabla}^{\operatorname{aug}}\colon S_{\mathcal{A}}\to K(W^{{\operatorname{aug}}}_{\!\mathcal{A}})

that sends
u_{F}
to
\eta^{\operatorname{aug}}_{F}
for all
F
.
Composing with the pullback along the inclusion
W_{\!\mathcal{A}}\subset W^{{\operatorname{aug}}}_{\!\mathcal{A}}
, we obtain a map

\Psi_{\nabla}\colon S_{\mathcal{A}}\to K(W_{\!\mathcal{A}})

that
sends
u_{F}
to
\eta_{F}
for all
F
.
Theorems
1.2
immediately implies that the kernel of
\Psi_{\nabla}
is the same as the kernel of
\Phi_{\nabla}
, and that the kernel of
\Psi_{\nabla}^{{\operatorname{aug}}}
is the same as the kernel of
\Phi_{\nabla}^{{\operatorname{aug}}}
. This kernel is computed explicitly in Theorem
5.3
, giving what we refer to as the
simplicial presentation
of
K(M)
and
K^{{\operatorname{aug}}}(M)
.

1.4.
The
K
-ring of
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{0,n}

Our results generalize to
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{0,n}
, the Deligne–Mumford–Knudsen compactification of the moduli space of stable rational curves with
n
marked points, for
n\geq 3
. For
i\in\{1,\dotsc,n\}
, there is a line bundle
\mathbb{L}_{i}
on
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{0,n}
whose fiber over a point is the cotangent space of the
i
th marked point in the corresponding curve. The first Chern class of
\mathbb{L}_{i}
is denoted
\psi_{i}
.

Kapranov
[
Kap93
]
showed that each
\mathbb{L}_{i}
is a base-point-free line bundle whose complete linear system induces a birational map
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{0,n}\to\mathbb{P}^{n-3}
. For every subset
S
of
[n]\coloneqq\{1,\dotsc,n\}
of size at least
3
, we have a forgetful map
f_{S}\colon\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{0,n}\to\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{0,S}=\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{0,|S|}
. We therefore obtain a map

\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{0,n}\to\prod_{S\subset[n-1],\,|S|\geq 2}\mathbb{P}^{|S|-2}

by composing
f_{S\cup n}
with the map induced by the complete linear system of
\mathbb{L}_{n}
on
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{0,S\cup n}
. This map is a closed embedding.

Consider the braid arrangement
\mathcal{B}_{n-1}
in
\mathbb{F}^{n-1}/\mathbb{F}\cdot(1,\dotsc,1)
, whose hyperplanes are normal to
e_{i}-e_{j}
for
i<j\in[n-1]
. The lattice of flats of
\mathcal{B}_{n-1}
may be identified with the collection of partitions of the set
[n-1]
. For any subset
S\subset[n-1]
of cardinality at least 2,
let
F_{S}
denote the flat corresponding to the partition of
[n-1]
into
S
along with a bunch of singletons.
Projecting onto the factors indexed by flats of
\mathcal{B}_{n-1}
of the form
F_{S}
for some
S
, we have a map

W_{\mathcal{B}_{n-1}}\to\prod_{S\subset[n-1],\,|S|\geq 2}\mathbb{P}(L^{F_{S}})

whose image is
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{0,n}
under the embedding described previously
[
DCP95
, Section 4.3]
. The relation between
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{0,n}
and
W_{\mathcal{B}_{n-1}}
allows us to deduce results about
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{0,n}
from our study of wonderful varieties.

Let
\mathcal{L}_{S}=f_{S\cup n}^{*}\mathbb{L}_{n}
. This bundle is trivial when
|S|=2
, and the bundles corresponding to sets of cardinality at least 3 form a basis for the Picard group of
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{0,n}
. Let

c_{S}\coloneqq c_{1}(\mathcal{L}_{S})=f_{S\cup n}^{*}\psi_{n}\in A^{1}(\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{0,n}).

Theorem 1.4
.

There is an isomorphism
\zeta_{n}\colon K(\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{0,n})\to A(\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{0,n})

that sends

1-[\mathcal{L}_{S}^{-1}]
to
c_{S}
.
For any
\xi\in K(\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{0,n})
, the Euler characteristic of
\xi
is equal to the degree of

\frac{\zeta_{n}(\xi)}{1-c_{[n-1]}}
.

1.5.
Matroids

The presentations of
A(W_{\!\mathcal{A}})
and
A(W^{{\operatorname{aug}}}_{\!\mathcal{A}})
depend only on the matroid associated with
\mathcal{A}
, which
led to the definitions of Chow rings and augmented Chow rings of arbitrary
loopless matroids
[
FY04
,
BHM+22a
]
.
Similarly, our descriptions of
K(W_{\!\mathcal{A}})
and
K(W^{{\operatorname{aug}}}_{\!\mathcal{A}})
, along with their
Euler characteristic functionals,
only depend on the matroid associated with
\mathcal{A}
. With the exception of the latter part of Section
8
, we will always assume that all matroids are loopless.

Let
M
be a matroid on the ground set
E
. Let
\Sigma_{M}
be the
Bergman fan
of
M
, which has rays
\{\rho_{F}\mid\text{$F$ a nonempty proper flat}\}

[
AK06
]
.
Let
\Sigma^{\operatorname{aug}}_{M}
be the

augmented Bergman fan

[
BHM+22a
]
of
M
, which has rays

\{\rho_{F}^{\operatorname{aug}}\mid\text{$F$ a proper flat}\}\cup\{\rho_{e}^{\operatorname{aug}}\mid e\in E\}
. These two fans are related by the fact
that
\Sigma_{M}
is isomorphic to the star of the ray
\rho_{\varnothing}
in
\Sigma_{M}^{\operatorname{aug}}

[
BHM+22a
, Proposition 2.7(2)]
.
The rings
A(M)
and
A^{\operatorname{aug}}(M)
are defined to be the Chow rings of the toric varieties

X_{\Sigma_{M}}
and
X_{\Sigma^{\operatorname{aug}}_{M}}
, respectively.

If
M
is the matroid associated with a hyperplane arrangement
\mathcal{A}
, then, after choosing a linear functional defining each hyperplane in
\mathcal{A}
, we obtain canonical inclusions

W_{\!\mathcal{A}}\subset X_{\Sigma_{M}}
and
W^{{\operatorname{aug}}}_{\!\mathcal{A}}\subset X_{\Sigma^{\operatorname{aug}}_{M}}

with the properties that
D_{F}=W_{\!\mathcal{A}}\cap D_{\rho_{F}}
for any nonempty proper flat
F
,

D_{F}^{\operatorname{aug}}=W^{{\operatorname{aug}}}_{\!\mathcal{A}}\cap D_{\rho_{F}^{\operatorname{aug}}}
for any proper flat
F
, and
D_{e}^{\operatorname{aug}}=W^{{\operatorname{aug}}}_{\!\mathcal{A}}\cap D_{\rho_{e}^{\operatorname{aug}}}
for any
e\in E
.
The restriction maps
A(M)\to A(W_{\!\mathcal{A}})
and
A^{\operatorname{aug}}(M)\to A(W^{{\operatorname{aug}}}_{\!\mathcal{A}})
are both isomorphisms
[
FY04
,
BHM+22a
]
.

Consider the polynomial ring

T_{M}\coloneqq\mathbb{Z}[x_{F}\mid\text{$F$ a flat}]\otimes\mathbb{Z}[y_{e}\mid e\in E]

along with the homomorphism
\Phi_{\operatorname{FY}}^{\operatorname{aug}}\colon T_{M}\to A^{\operatorname{aug}}(M)
taking

to
[D_{\rho_{F}^{\operatorname{aug}}}]
for all proper flats
F
,

x_{E}
to
-\sum_{F\neq E}[D_{\rho_{F}^{\operatorname{aug}}}]
, and

y_{e}
to
[D_{\rho_{e}^{\operatorname{aug}}}]
.
Composing with the pullback along the inclusion

,

\Phi_{\operatorname{FY}}\colon T_{M}\to A(M)
that sends
x_{F}
to

and

We define

and

(1)

If

,

\kappa_{M}(u_{F}^{2})=\left(1-\prod_{e\in H}\left(1-\tau^{\operatorname{aug}}_{H}\right)^{-1}\right)\left(1-\prod_{e\notin K}\left(1-\tau^{\operatorname{aug}}_{K}\right)\right),

which vanishes by Equation (
11
). Now for any flat

, we have

\displaystyle\kappa_{M}\Big(u_{F}(u_{G}-u_{F\vee G})\Big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(1-\prod_{e\notin K}\left(1-\tau^{\operatorname{aug}}_{K}\right)\right)\prod_{F\vee G\subset I}\left(1-\tau^{\operatorname{aug}}_{I}\right)^{-1}\left(\prod_{G\subset J\subsetneq F\vee G}\left(1-\tau^{\operatorname{aug}}_{J}\right)^{-1}-1\right),

which also vanishes by Equation (
11
) because
e\notin J
for any

We have now proved that
\kappa_{M}
descends to a homomorphism
\overline{\kappa}_{M}\colon A^{\operatorname{aug}}(M)\to K^{\operatorname{aug}}(M)
.
Möbius inversion tells us that

1-\tau_{F}^{\operatorname{aug}}=\prod_{F\subset G}\Big(1-\overline{\kappa}_{M}(h_{G})\Big)^{-\mu(F,G)},

where
\mu
is the Möbius function on the lattice of flats of
M
. This, along with the surjectivity statement of Theorem
5.2
(2),
implies that
\overline{\kappa}_{M}
is surjective.
By Lemma
2.5
applied to the variety
X_{\Sigma_{M}^{\operatorname{aug}}}
,
A^{\operatorname{aug}}(M)
and
K^{\operatorname{aug}}(M)
are free abelian groups of the same rank, thus
\overline{\kappa}_{M}

is an isomorphism.
We may then take
\zeta_{M}^{\operatorname{aug}}
to be the inverse of
\overline{\kappa}_{M}
.
∎

6.
Adams operations and Serre duality

We now discuss some additional structures on matroid
K
-rings.
The fact that
K(M)

and
K^{{\operatorname{aug}}}(M)

are (by definition)
K
-rings of toric varieties
X_{\Sigma_{M}}
and
X_{\Sigma^{{\operatorname{aug}}}_{M}}
endows them with the structure of
augmented
\lambda
-rings

[
BGI71
, Exposé V, Exemple 3.9.1]
. This means that we have a rank function
\epsilon
that takes values in
\mathbb{Z}
, and for each natural
number
k
, we have operations
\lambda^{k}
and
\Psi^{k}
(the latter called
Adams operations
)
characterized by the property that

\lambda^{k}([\mathcal{E}])=[\wedge^{k}\mathcal{E}]\Psi^{k}([\mathcal{L}])=[\otimes^{k}\mathcal{L}]

for any vector bundle
\mathcal{E}
and any line bundle
\mathcal{L}
.
Since our simplicial generators
\eta_{F}
are all of the form
1-[\mathcal{L}]
for some line bundle
\mathcal{L}
, we have

\epsilon(\eta_{F})=0
, and

\Psi^{k}(\eta_{F})=\sum_{i=1}^{k}(-1)^{i+1}\binom{k}{i}\eta_{F}^{i}.

The formula for augmented simplicial generators is identical.
Note that the Adams operations are ring homomorphisms, which is not at all combinatorially obvious from the above formula.
The Adams operations become simultaneously diagonalizable after tensoring with
\mathbb{Q}
, and their eigenspaces (which are independent of
k>1
)
map isomorphically to the graded pieces of the Chow ring via
the Chern character.
We also have a
duality automorphism

D
, characterized by the property that

D([\mathcal{E}])=[\mathcal{E}^{\vee}]
for any vector bundle
\mathcal{E}
.
In terms of the simplicial generators, this takes the form

D(\eta_{F})=\frac{-\eta_{F}}{1-\eta_{F}}=-\eta_{F}-\eta_{F}^{2}-\dotsb,

and similarly in the augmented setting.

We note that the operations
\epsilon
,
\lambda^{k}
,
\Psi^{k}
, and
D
all commute with
the maps
\iota^{*}
and
(\iota^{\operatorname{aug}})^{*}
introduced in Section
5
because they are compatible with functorial maps between
K
-rings of varieties.

On a
d
-dimensional smooth projective variety
X
with canonical bundle
\omega_{X}
, Serre duality implies that, for any vector bundle
\mathcal{E}
,

\chi(X,\mathcal{E})=(-1)^{d}\chi(X,\omega_{X}\otimes\mathcal{E}^{\vee}).

In particular, this holds for
X=W_{\!\mathcal{A}}
or
W^{{\operatorname{aug}}}_{\!\mathcal{A}}
. We will show that a similar formula holds on
K(M)
for any matroid
M
,
even if
M
is not realizable. Our first task is to define classes
\omega_{M}\in K(M)
and
\omega_{M}^{{\operatorname{aug}}}\in K^{{\operatorname{aug}}}(M)

that will play the roles of the canonical bundles.

For any matroid on the ground set
E
, the
matroid polytope

P(M)\subset\mathbb{R}^{E}
is defined to be the convex hull
of the indicator functions of bases of
M
, and the
independence polytope

I\!P(M)\subset\mathbb{R}^{E}
is defined
to be the convex hull of indicator functions of independent subsets of
M
.

Recall that, on a smooth projective toric variety with fan
\Sigma
, there is a correspondence between torus equivariant
nef line bundles and lattice polytopes whose normal fans coarsen
\Sigma
. For any matroid on the ground set
E
,

P(M)
coarsens
\Sigma_{U_{E}}
and
I\!P(M)
coarsens
\Sigma_{U_{E}}^{\operatorname{aug}}
, so we obtain line bundles

[P(M)]
and
[I\!P(M)]
on
X_{E}
and
X_{E}^{{\operatorname{aug}}}
, respectively.

Let
M^{\perp}
be the matroid dual to
M
,
characterized by the property that the bases of
M^{\perp}
are the complements of the bases of
M
.
Using the standard description of the canonical bundle of a smooth proper toric variety
[
Ful93
, Section 4.3]
, one can check that
c_{1}(\omega_{X_{E}})=x_{\emptyset}+x_{E}=-\sum_{F\not=\emptyset,E}x_{F}
and
c_{1}(\omega_{X_{E}}^{{\operatorname{aug}}})=x_{E}-\sum_{e\in E}y_{e}=-\sum_{F\not=E}x_{F}-\sum_{e\in E}y_{e}
.
We define the classes

\omega_{M}\coloneqq\iota^{*}\left(\omega_{X_{E}}\cdot[P(M^{\perp})]\right)\in K(M)

and

\omega_{M}^{\operatorname{aug}}\coloneqq(\iota^{\operatorname{aug}})^{*}\left(\omega_{X^{{\operatorname{aug}}}_{E}}\cdot[I\!P(M^{\perp})]\right)\in K^{\operatorname{aug}}(M).

These definitions are motivated by the following proposition.

Proposition 6.1
.

Let
\mathcal{A}
be a hyperplane arrangement with associated matroid
M
.
The isomorphisms
K(M)\cong K(W_{\!\mathcal{A}})
and

K^{\operatorname{aug}}(M)\cong K(W^{{\operatorname{aug}}}_{\!\mathcal{A}})
take
\omega_{M}
and
\omega_{M}^{\operatorname{aug}}

to the canonical bundles of
W_{\!\mathcal{A}}
and
W^{{\operatorname{aug}}}_{\!\mathcal{A}}
, respectively.

Proof.

The adjunction formula states that the canonical class of
W^{{\operatorname{aug}}}_{\!\mathcal{A}}

is equal to the restriction of the canonical class of
X_{E}^{{\operatorname{aug}}}
tensored with the determinant of the
normal bundle to
W^{{\operatorname{aug}}}_{\!\mathcal{A}}
inside of
X_{E}^{{\operatorname{aug}}}
.
The determinant of the normal bundle is equal to the restriction of
[I\!P(M^{\perp})]

by
[
EHL23
, Proposition 4.6 and Corollary 5.4]
. The non-augmented case can be prove similarly, using
[
BEST23
, Theorem 7.10]
.
∎

The following theorem is a combinatorial version of Serre duality for
K
-rings of matroids.

Theorem 6.2
.

For any
\xi\in K(M)
and
\xi^{{\operatorname{aug}}}\in K^{{\operatorname{aug}}}(M)
, we have

\chi(M,\xi)=(-1)^{\operatorname{rk}M-1}\chi(M,\omega_{M}\cdot D(\xi))\chi^{{\operatorname{aug}}}(M,\xi^{{\operatorname{aug}}})=(-1)^{\operatorname{rk}M}\chi^{{\operatorname{aug}}}(M,D(\xi^{{\operatorname{aug}}})).

In the realizable case, Theorem
6.2
follows from Proposition
6.1
and Serre duality for the varieties

W_{\!\mathcal{A}}
and
W^{{\operatorname{aug}}}_{\!\mathcal{A}}
. Our strategy is to deduce the general case using the concept of valuativity.
Given an abelian group
A
, a function
f\colon\{\text{matroids on }E\}\to A
is called
valuative

if it factors through the map that takes
M

to the indicator function of its matroid polytope
P(M)\subset\mathbb{R}^{E}
.
That is, for any matroids
M_{1},\ldots,M_{k}
and integers
a_{1},\ldots,a_{k}
such that
\sum a_{i}1_{P(M_{i})}=0
, we
require that
\sum a_{i}f(M_{i})=0
.

Remark 6.3
.

The notion of valuativity is typically defined on functions that take values
on the set of all matroids on
E
, whereas we are only considering loopless matroids in this paper.
This is not an important distinction, as the valuative group of loopless matroids on
E
is a direct summand of the valuative group
of all matroids on
E
. That is, a function on the set of loopless matroids on
E
is valuative if and only if it extends to a valuative function on the set of all matroids on
E
, if and only if it extends by zero to a valuative function on the set of all matroids on
E
.

Lemma 6.4
.

Fix a pair of classes
\xi\in K(U_{E})
and
\xi^{\operatorname{aug}}\in K^{\operatorname{aug}}(U_{E})
. The following four
\mathbb{Z}
-valued functions are valuative:

\displaystyle M

\displaystyle\mapsto\chi(M,\iota^{*}\xi)

\displaystyle M

\displaystyle\mapsto\chi^{\operatorname{aug}}(M,(\iota^{\operatorname{aug}})^{*}\xi^{\operatorname{aug}})

\displaystyle M

\displaystyle\mapsto(-1)^{\operatorname{rk}M-1}\chi\big(M,\omega_{M}\cdot D(\iota^{*}\xi)\big)

\displaystyle M

\displaystyle\mapsto(-1)^{\operatorname{rk}M}\chi^{\operatorname{aug}}\big(M,\omega_{M}^{\operatorname{aug}}\cdot D((\iota^{\operatorname{aug}})^{*}\xi^{\operatorname{aug}})\big).

Proof.

We begin with the first function.
By Proposition
5.6
,
\chi(M,\iota^{*}\xi)=\chi(X_{E},[\mathcal{O}_{M}]\cdot\xi)
. Recall that
[\mathcal{O}_{M}]=\zeta_{U_{E}}^{-1}(\Delta_{M})
, and the function
M\mapsto\Delta_{M}
is valuative by
[
BEST23
, Corollary 7.11]
. Therefore the function
M\mapsto[\mathcal{O}_{M}]
is valuative, and the result follows by the linearity of the Euler characteristic
map on
K(X_{E})
.
The proof of valuativity of the second function is similar, except that we now use
[
EHL23
, Proposition 4.7]

to establish the valuativity of the function
M\mapsto[\mathcal{O}_{M}^{{\operatorname{aug}}}]
.

After applying
\zeta_{U_{E}}^{\operatorname{aug}}
, it follows from
[
EHL23
, Proposition 4.7 and Corollary 6.5]
that the function

M\mapsto[\mathcal{O}_{M}^{\operatorname{aug}}]\cdot[I\!P(M^{\perp})]
is valuative. This implies the valuativity of the fourth function. One can argue similarly for the third function.
∎

Proof of Theorem
6.2
.

We have already used Proposition
6.1
to prove the theorem for realizable matroids.
We have shown that each side of both claimed equalities is valuative.
The full theorem now follows from a result of Derksen and Fink
[
DF10
]

(see also
[
BEST23
, Lemma 5.9]
), which says that
the matroid polytope of any matroid can be expressed as a linear combination of indicator functions of matroid polytopes of realizable matroids.
∎

Question 6.5
.

Can Theorem
6.2
be applied to give interesting identities for matroids? For example, let
f(\ell)\coloneqq\chi(M,\omega_{M}^{\ell})
; then Theorem
6.2
implies that
f(\ell)=(-1)^{r-1}f(1-\ell)
. Does this statement admit an elementary proof,
without using valuativity and Serre duality for wonderful varieties?

7.
Euler characteristics and simplicial Snapper polynomials

In this section, we give purely combinatorial formulas for the Euler characteristic of a monomial in the simplicial generators
on
K(M)
or
K^{\operatorname{aug}}(M)
.
Given a pair of tuples

\textbf{m}=(m_{F}\mid F\text{ a nonempty flat})\textbf{m}^{\prime}=(m^{\prime}_{F}\mid F\text{ a nonempty flat}),

we say that
\textbf{m}^{\prime}\leq\textbf{m}
if
m^{\prime}_{F}\leq m_{F}
for all
F
.
We say that
m
satisfies the
Hall–Rado condition
if, for all
\textbf{m}^{\prime}\leq\textbf{m}
,
we have

\operatorname{rank}\bigcup_{m^{\prime}_{F}>0}F\;\geq\;\sum_{F\not=\emptyset}m^{\prime}_{F}.

If the inequality is always strict, we say that
m
satisfies the
dragon Hall–Rado condition
.
The following theorem computes the degrees of the monomials
h^{\textbf{m}}
and
(h^{\operatorname{aug}})^{\textbf{m}}
.

Theorem 7.1
.

If
\sum m_{F}=\operatorname{rk}M-1
, then
[
BES23
, Theorem 5.2.4]

\deg_{M}(h^{\textbf{m}})=\begin{cases}1&\text{if $\textbf{m}$ satisfies the dragon Hall--Rado condition}\\
0&\text{otherwise.}\end{cases}

If
\sum m_{F}=\operatorname{rk}M
, then
[
EL23
, Theorem 1.3]

\deg^{\operatorname{aug}}_{M}((h^{\operatorname{aug}})^{\textbf{m}})=\begin{cases}1&\text{if $\textbf{m}$ satisfies the Hall--Rado condition}\\
0&\text{otherwise.}\end{cases}

The
K
-theoretic analogue of Theorem
7.1
is almost exactly the same, except without
the condition on
\sum m_{F}
.

Theorem 7.2
.

For any
m
, we have

\chi(M,\eta^{\textbf{m}})=\begin{cases}1&\text{if $\textbf{m}$ satisfies the dragon Hall--Rado condition}\\
0&\text{otherwise}\end{cases}

and

\chi^{\operatorname{aug}}(M,(\eta^{\operatorname{aug}})^{\textbf{m}})=\begin{cases}1&\text{if $\textbf{m}$ satisfies the Hall--Rado condition}\\
0&\text{otherwise.}\end{cases}

Proof.

We prove the first statement; the augmented case is similar.
If
\sum m_{F}\geq\operatorname{rk}M
, then
\eta^{\textbf{m}}=0
and
m
fails to satisfy the dragon Hall–Rado condition. If
\sum m_{F}<\operatorname{rk}M
,
we let

k=\operatorname{rk}M-1-\sum m_{F}\geq 0,

and
define
\tilde{\textbf{m}}
by putting
\tilde{m}_{E}=m_{E}+k

and
\tilde{m}_{F}=m_{F}
for all
F\neq E
. Then

\chi(M,\eta^{\textbf{m}})=\deg_{M}\left(\frac{\zeta_{M}(\eta^{\textbf{m}})}{1-h_{E}}\right)=\deg_{M}\left(\frac{h^{\textbf{m}}}{1-h_{E}}\right)=\deg_{M}\left(h^{\tilde{\textbf{m}}}\right).

We observe that
m
satisfies dragon Hall–Rado if and only if
\tilde{\textbf{m}}
does, thus our formula follows from Theorem
7.1
.
∎

Given a proper variety
X
and a finite tuple of line bundles
\mathcal{L}=(\mathcal{L}_{1},\ldots,\mathcal{L}_{k})
on
X
,
the function from
\mathbb{Z}^{k}
to
\mathbb{Z}
given by the formula

\textup{Snap}_{\mathcal{L}}(\textbf{a})\coloneqq\chi\left(X,\mathcal{L}_{1}^{a_{1}}\otimes\cdots\otimes\mathcal{L}_{k}^{a_{k}}\right)

is a polynomial of degree at most
\dim X

[
Sna59
, Theorem 9.1]
,
which we call the
Snapper polynomial
.
Given an integer
x
and a natural number
d
, let

x^{(d)}=\frac{x(x+1)\cdots(x+d-1)}{d!}=\binom{d+x-1}{d}=(-1)^{d}\binom{-x}{d}.

If
\textbf{a}\in\mathbb{Z}^{k}
and
\textbf{d}\in\mathbb{N}^{k}
, let

\textbf{a}^{(\textbf{d})}\coloneqq a_{1}^{(d_{1})}\cdots a_{k}^{(d_{k})}.

Lemma 7.3
.

If
\sigma_{i}=1-[\mathcal{L}_{i}^{-1}]
for each
i\in\{1,\ldots,k\}
,
then

\textup{Snap}_{\mathcal{L}}(\textbf{a})=\sum_{\textbf{d}\in\mathbb{N}^{k}}\chi\left(X,\sigma_{1}^{d_{1}}\cdots\sigma_{k}^{d_{k}}\right)\,\textbf{a}^{(\textbf{d})}.

Proof.

We have

[\mathcal{L}_{i}^{a_{i}}]=\frac{1}{(1-\sigma_{i})^{a_{i}}}=\sum_{d_{i}=0}^{\infty}a_{i}^{(d_{i})}\sigma_{i}^{d_{i}}.

The lemma follows.
∎

Let
M
be a matroid, and consider a tuple
\textbf{a}=(a_{F}\mid\text{$F$ a nonempty flat})
.
Let

\textup{Snap}_{M}(\textbf{a})\coloneqq\sum_{m}\chi(M,\eta^{\textbf{m}})\textbf{a}^{(\textbf{m})}\textup{Snap}_{M}^{\operatorname{aug}}(\textbf{a})\coloneqq\sum_{m}\chi^{\operatorname{aug}}(M,(\eta^{\operatorname{aug}})^{\textbf{m}})\textbf{a}^{(\textbf{m})}.

This definition is motivated by the following observation.

Lemma 7.4
.

Let
\mathcal{A}
be a hyperplane arrangement with associated matroid
M
.
Then
\textup{Snap}_{M}
and
\textup{Snap}_{M}^{\operatorname{aug}}

coincide with the geometrically defined Snapper polynomials for the varieties
W_{\!\mathcal{A}}
and
W^{{\operatorname{aug}}}_{\!\mathcal{A}}

with respect to the line bundles
(\mathcal{L}_{F})
and
(\mathcal{L}_{F}^{\operatorname{aug}})
.

Proof.

By definition,
\eta_{F}=1-[\mathcal{L}_{F}^{-1}]
.
The fact that
\textup{Snap}_{M}
coincides with the Snapper polynomial for
W_{\!\mathcal{A}}

with respect to
(\mathcal{L}_{F})
then follows from Lemma
7.3
. The augmented case is identical.
∎

We can now rephrase Theorem
7.2
as a statement about Snapper polynomials.

Corollary 7.5
.

We have

\textup{Snap}_{M}(\textbf{a})\;\;=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\text{$\textbf{m}$ satisfies}\\
\text{dragon Hall--Rado}\end{subarray}}\textbf{a}^{(\textbf{m})}\textup{Snap}_{M}^{\operatorname{aug}}(\textbf{a})\;\;=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\text{$\textbf{m}$ satisfies}\\
\text{Hall--Rado}\end{subarray}}\textbf{a}^{(\textbf{m})}.

Remark 7.6
.

In the special case where
M=U_{E}
is Boolean, the first equality in
Corollary

7.5
appears in
[
Pos09
, Theorem 11.3]
.

8.
Snapper polynomials in the Feichtner–Yuzvinsky generators

Section
7
was about the Snapper polynomial of the wonderful variety of an arrangement
with respect to the line bundles
\{\mathcal{L}_{F}\mid\text{$F$ a nonempty flat}\}
, or of an arbitrary matroid with respect
to the corresponding
K
-classes. This was fundamentally a “simplicial” construction, since the simplicial generators
for
K
-theory were defined in terms of these line bundles. In this section, we consider the Snapper polynomial
with respect to a collection of line bundles related to the Feichtner–Yuzvinsky generators. For simplicity, we work
only in the non-augmented setting.

Recall that, for all flats
F
, we have defined

t_{F}\coloneqq\Phi_{\operatorname{FY}}(x_{F})\in A(M)\tau_{F}\coloneqq\Psi_{\operatorname{FY}}(x_{F})\in K(M).

For any tuple of natural numbers
\textbf{m}=(m_{F}\mid\text{$F$ a flat})
, let

\mathbf{\tau}^{\textbf{m}}\coloneqq\prod_{F}\tau_{F}^{m_{F}}.

For any tuple of integers
\textbf{a}=(a_{F}\mid\text{$F$ a flat})
, we define

\textup{Snap}_{M}^{\operatorname{FY}}(\textbf{a})\coloneqq\sum_{\textbf{m}}\chi(M,\mathbf{\tau}^{\textbf{m}})\textbf{a}^{(\textbf{m})}.

If
M
is the matroid associated with a hyperplane arrangement
\mathcal{A}
,
let
D_{\emptyset}
and
D_{E}
be the divisor classes on
W_{\!\mathcal{A}}
with first Chern classes
t_{\emptyset}
and
t_{E}
respectively, so that we have
\tau_{F}=1-[\mathcal{O}(-D_{F})]
for all flats
F
.
Lemma
7.3
immediately implies that
\textup{Snap}_{M}^{\operatorname{FY}}
is the Snapper polynomial of
W_{\!\mathcal{A}}
with respect to the tuple of line bundles
\left(\mathcal{O}(D_{F})\mid\text{$F$ a flat}\right)
.

We now provide an explicit formula for the polynomial
\textup{Snap}_{M}^{\operatorname{FY}}(\textbf{a})
.
For any natural number
k
, let
\operatorname{Flag}_{k}
be the collection
of flags of flats of the form

\mathcal{F}=\{\varnothing=F_{0}\subsetneq F_{1}\subsetneq\cdots\subsetneq F_{k}=E\}.

If
F
and
G
are incomparable flats, then
\tau_{F}\tau_{G}=0\in K(M)
, therefore
\textup{Snap}_{M}^{\operatorname{FY}}(\textbf{a})
does not contain any monomials that are multiples of
a_{F}a_{G}
.
This implies that we may write

\textup{Snap}_{M}^{\operatorname{FY}}(\textbf{a})=\sum_{k\geq 1}\sum_{\mathcal{F}\in\operatorname{Flag}_{k}}\sum_{\textbf{m}}c(\mathcal{F},\textbf{m})\,a_{F_{0}}^{(m_{0})}a_{F_{1}}^{(m_{1}+1)}a_{F_{2}}^{(m_{2}+1)}\cdots a_{F_{k-1}}^{(m_{k-1}+1)}a_{F_{k}}^{(m_{k})},

where
m
ranges over all tuples
(m_{0},\ldots,m_{k})
of natural numbers.
It remains only to compute the constants
c(\mathcal{F},\textbf{m})
.

Remark 8.1
.

It may be slightly confusing that the first and last exponents are
(m_{0})
and
(m_{k})
, whereas the middle exponents
are
(m_{i}+1)
for
1\leq i<k
. The point is that a particular “monomial” appears in the summand indexed by
\mathcal{F}
if and only if
the nonempty proper flats in its support are precisely
\{F_{1},\ldots,F_{k-1}\}
.
Also, this convention leads to a tidier formula for
c(\mathcal{F},\textbf{m})
.

Given
\mathcal{F}\in\operatorname{Flag}_{k}
and
i\in\{1,\ldots,k\}
, consider the matroid

M^{F_{i}}_{F_{i-1}}
obtained by contracting
F_{i-1}
and deleting the complement of
F_{i}
,
whose poset of flats is canonically isomorphic
to the interval
[F_{i-1},F_{i}]
in the poset of flats of
M
. Let
d_{i}=\operatorname{rk}M^{F_{i}}_{F_{i-1}}-1
.
Let
\mu^{j}(M)
denote the absolute value of the coefficient of
t^{\operatorname{rk}M-1-j}
in the reduced characteristic polynomial of
M
.

Theorem 8.2
.

For any
k\geq 1
,
\mathcal{F}\in\operatorname{Flag}_{k}
, and
\textbf{m}=(m_{0},\ldots,m_{k})
, we have

c(\mathcal{F},\textbf{m})=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\textbf{e},\textbf{f}\in\mathbb{N}^{k}\\
e_{1}=m_{0}\end{subarray}}(-1)^{|\textbf{e}|+|\textbf{f}|}\prod_{i=1}^{k}\binom{d_{i}-e_{i}}{f_{i}}\mu^{e_{i}}(M^{F_{i}}_{F_{i-1}})\binom{m_{i}}{e_{i+1}+f_{i}-m_{i},m_{i}-f_{i},m_{i}-e_{i+1}},

where we adopt the convention that
e_{k+1}=0
.

Corollary 8.3
.

Fix an element
\mathcal{F}\in\operatorname{Flag}_{k}
and a tuple of natural numbers
\textbf{m}=(m_{0},\ldots,m_{k})
with
\sum m_{i}=\operatorname{rk}M-k
. Then the degree

\deg_{M}\left(t_{F_{0}}^{m_{0}}t_{F_{1}}^{m_{1}+1}\cdots t_{F_{k-1}}^{m_{k-1}+1}t_{F_{k}}^{m_{k}}\right)

is equal to

(-1)^{\operatorname{rk}M-k}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\textbf{e}\in\mathbb{N}^{k}\\
e_{1}=m_{0}\end{subarray}}\prod_{i=1}^{k}\mu^{e_{i}}(M^{F_{i}}_{F_{i-1}})\binom{m_{i}}{e_{i+1}-e_{i}+d_{i}-m_{i},m_{i}-d_{i}+e_{i},m_{i}-e_{i+1}},

again with the convention that
e_{k+1}=0
.

Proof.

By the definition of
\chi
and the third item of Lemma
5.8
, we have

\deg_{M}\left(t_{F_{0}}^{m_{0}}t_{F_{1}}^{m_{1}+1}\cdots t_{F_{k-1}}^{m_{k-1}+1}t_{F_{k}}^{m_{k}}\right)=\chi\left(M,\tau_{\varnothing}^{m_{0}}\tau_{F_{1}}^{m_{1}+1}\cdots\tau_{F_{k-1}}^{m_{k-1}+1}\tau_{F_{k}}^{m_{k}}\right)=c(\mathcal{F},\textbf{m}).

In the formula for
c(\mathcal{F},\textbf{m})
in Theorem
8.2
, we see that a particular term vanishes unless
e_{i}+f_{i}\leq d_{i}

for all
i\in\{1,\ldots,k\}
. Taking the sum over all
i
, this implies that

|e|+|f|\leq|d|=\operatorname{rk}M-k=m_{0}+\cdots+m_{k}.

On the other hand, a term also vanishes unless
m_{i}\leq e_{i+1}+f_{i}
for all
i\in\{1,\ldots,k\}
. Taking the sum over all
i
and including the equality
e_{0}=m_{0}
,
this implies that

m_{0}+\cdots+m_{k}\leq|e|+|f|.

Thus the aforementioned inequalities are all equalities, and we can simplify our formula for
c(\mathcal{F},\textbf{m})
by setting
f_{i}=d_{i}-e_{i}
for all
i
.
The result follows.
∎

If we take
m_{0}=0=m_{k}
in Corollary
8.3
, we recover a result of Eur
[
Eur20
, Theorem 3.2]
.

Corollary 8.4
.

Fix an element
\mathcal{F}\in\operatorname{Flag}_{k}
and a tuple of natural numbers
(m_{1},\ldots,m_{k-1})
with
(m_{1}+1)+\cdots+(m_{k-1}+1)=\operatorname{rk}M-1
.
For all
i\in\{1,\ldots,k\}
, let
e_{i}=(m_{1}-d_{1})+\cdots+(m_{i-1}-d_{i-1})
, and let
e_{k+1}=0
.
Then we have

\deg_{M}\left(t_{F_{1}}^{m_{1}+1}\cdots t_{F_{k-1}}^{m_{k-1}+1}\right)=(-1)^{\operatorname{rk}M-k}\prod_{i=1}^{k}\mu^{e_{i}}(M^{F_{i}}_{F_{i-1}})\binom{m_{i}}{e_{i+1}}.

Proof.

The fact that
m_{0}=0=m_{k}
implies that the only nonzero term in Corollary
8.3
is the one in which
e_{i}=(m_{1}-d_{1})+\cdots+(m_{i-1}-d_{i-1})
for all
i
.
∎

The remainder of this section is devoted to the proof of Theorem
8.2
.
We begin by analyzing a particular specialization of
\textup{Snap}_{M}^{\operatorname{FY}}
, where we set all variables except
a_{\emptyset}
and
a_{E}
to
0
.

Lemma 8.5
.

We have

\textup{Snap}_{M}^{\operatorname{FY}}(a_{\emptyset},\mathbf{0},a_{E})=\sum_{e,f\in\mathbb{N}}(-1)^{e+f}\binom{\operatorname{rk}M-1-e}{f}\mu^{e}(M)a_{\emptyset}^{(e)}a_{E}^{(f)}.

Proof.

By definition, we have

\textup{Snap}_{M}^{\operatorname{FY}}(a_{\emptyset},\mathbf{0},a_{E})=\sum_{e,f\in\mathbb{N}}\chi(M,\tau_{\varnothing}^{e}\tau_{E}^{f})a_{\emptyset}^{(e)}a_{E}^{(f)}.

We also have

\chi(M,\tau_{\varnothing}^{e}\tau_{E}^{f})=\deg_{M}\left(\frac{\zeta_{M}(\tau_{\varnothing}^{e}\tau_{E}^{f})}{1-h_{E}}\right)=\deg_{M}\left(\frac{\zeta_{M}(\tau_{\varnothing}^{e}\tau_{E}^{f})}{1+t_{E}}\right).

By Lemma
5.8
, this is equal to

\deg_{M}\left(\frac{t_{\varnothing}^{e}t_{E}^{f}}{(1+t_{E})^{f+1}}\right)=\sum_{\ell}(-1)^{\ell-f}\binom{\ell}{f}\deg_{M}(t_{\varnothing}^{e}t_{E}^{\ell}).

The degree of
t_{\varnothing}^{e}t_{E}^{\ell}
vanishes unless
e+\ell=\operatorname{rk}M-1
, in which case it is equal to
(-1)^{\operatorname{rk}M-1}\mu^{e}(M)

[
AHK18
, Proposition 9.5]
,
thus

\chi(M,\tau_{\varnothing}^{e}\tau_{E}^{f})=(-1)^{e+f}\binom{\operatorname{rk}M-1-e}{f}\mu^{e}(M).

This completes the proof.
∎

For the statement and proof of the next proposition,
it will be convenient to regard
\textup{Snap}_{M}^{\operatorname{FY}}(\textbf{a})
as a function that takes inputs
a_{S}
for all subsets
S\subset E
, with the property
that the coefficient of any monomial involving
a_{S}
is zero if
S
is not a flat of
M
.
It will also be convenient to allow
M
to have loops, with the convention that
\textup{Snap}_{M}^{\operatorname{FY}}(\textbf{a})=0
whenever
M
has a loop.
This allows us to define the matroid
M_{F}^{G}
on the ground set
G\setminus F
with respect to an arbitrary pair of sets
F\subset G
; note that
this matroid is loopless if and only if
F
is a flat.

Fix a subset
G\subset E
, and for any
\textbf{a}=(a_{F})
, define
\textbf{a}^{\prime}
by putting
a^{\prime}_{G}=a_{G}-1
and
a^{\prime}_{F}=a_{F}
for all
F\neq G
.
For any polynomial
P
in
a
, define
\partial_{G}P
by putting

\partial_{G}P(\textbf{a})\coloneqq P(\textbf{a})-P(\textbf{a}^{\prime}).

Note that if
P
does not depend on
a_{G}
, then
\partial_{G}P=0
.
For any pair of subsets
F\subset G
, let
\textbf{a}|_{[F,G]}
be the restriction of
a
to this interval, which we identify with the collection of subsets of
G\setminus F
.

Proposition 8.6
.

For any proper nonempty subset
G\subset E
, we have

\partial_{G}\textup{Snap}_{M}^{\operatorname{FY}}(\textbf{a})=\textup{Snap}^{\operatorname{FY}}_{M_{\varnothing}^{G}}(\textbf{a}|_{[\varnothing,G]})\,\textup{Snap}^{\operatorname{FY}}_{M_{G}^{E}}(\textbf{a}|_{[G,E]}).

Proof.

We will first prove the statement when
M
is the matroid associated with a hyperplane arrangement
\mathcal{A}
, and then deduce the general case from valuativity.
Note that if
G
is not a flat of
M
, then
M_{\emptyset}^{G}
has a loop and both sides vanish. We therefore assume that
G
is a flat.

Consider the hyperplane arrangements

\mathcal{A}_{\varnothing}^{G}=\{H_{e}/L_{G}\mid e\in G\}\mathcal{A}_{G}^{E}\coloneqq\{H_{e}\cap L_{G}\mid e\notin G\}.

We observe that
\mathcal{A}_{\varnothing}^{G}
is an arrangement in the vector space
L^{G}
with associated matroid
M_{\varnothing}^{G}
, and
\mathcal{A}_{G}^{E}

is an arrangement in the vector space
L_{G}
with associated matroid
M_{G}^{E}
.

There is a short exact sequence

0\to\mathcal{O}\left((t_{G}-1)D_{G}+\sum_{F\neq G}t_{F}D_{F}\right)\to\mathcal{O}\left(\sum t_{F}D_{F}\right)\to\mathcal{O}_{D_{G}}\left(\sum t_{F}D_{F}\right)\to 0.

Taking Euler characteristics, we find that

\partial_{G}\textup{Snap}_{M}^{\operatorname{FY}}(\textbf{a})=\textup{Snap}_{M}^{\operatorname{FY}}(\textbf{a})-\textup{Snap}_{M}^{\operatorname{FY}}(\textbf{a}^{\prime})=\chi\left(D_{G},\mathcal{O}_{D_{G}}\left(\sum t_{F}D_{F}\right)\right).

By
[
DCP95
, Page 482]
, the divisor
D_{G}
is isomorphic to
W_{\!\mathcal{A}_{\varnothing}^{G}}\times W_{\!\mathcal{A}_{G}^{E}}
.
Furthermore, the restriction of
\mathcal{O}_{D_{G}}(\sum t_{F}D_{F})
to
D_{G}
is isomorphic to the pullback from
W_{\!\mathcal{A}_{\varnothing}^{G}}
of
\mathcal{O}_{D_{G}}(\sum_{F\leq G}t_{F}D_{F})

tensored with the pullback from
W_{\!\mathcal{A}_{G}^{E}}
of
\mathcal{O}_{D_{G}}(\sum_{G\leq F}t_{F}D_{F})

[
BHM+22a
, Proposition 2.20]
.
Then by the Künneth formula, we have

\begin{split}\chi\left(D_{G},\mathcal{O}_{D_{G}}\left(\sum t_{F}D_{F}\right)\right)&=\chi\left(W_{\!\mathcal{A}_{\varnothing}^{G}},\mathcal{O}_{D_{G}}\left(\sum_{F\leq G}t_{F}D_{F}\right)\right)\cdot\chi\left(W_{\!\mathcal{A}_{G}^{E}},\mathcal{O}_{D_{G}}\left(\sum_{G\leq F}t_{F}D_{F}\right)\right)\\
&=\textup{Snap}^{\operatorname{FY}}_{M_{\varnothing}^{G}}(\textbf{a}|_{[\varnothing,G]})\,\textup{Snap}^{\operatorname{FY}}_{M_{G}^{E}}(\textbf{a}|_{[G,E]}).\end{split}

This completes the proof in the realizable case.
For the general case, it will suffice to show that both sides of the equation in the statement of the proposition are valuative invariants of
M
. It follows from Lemma
6.4
that
\textup{Snap}_{M}^{\operatorname{FY}}(\textbf{a})
is valutive, and therefore that

\partial_{G}\textup{Snap}_{M}^{\operatorname{FY}}(\textbf{a})
is valuative. The valuativity of
\textup{Snap}^{\operatorname{FY}}_{M_{\varnothing}^{G}}(\textbf{a}|_{[\varnothing,G]})\,\textup{Snap}^{\operatorname{FY}}_{M_{G}^{E}}(\textbf{a}|_{[G,E]})
follows from the valuativity of
\textup{Snap}_{M}^{\operatorname{FY}}(\textbf{a})
and general properties of valuativity
[
McM09
, Theorem 4.6]
(see also
[
AS23
, Theorem A]
).
∎

Remark 8.7
.

Our reason for regarding
\textup{Snap}_{M}^{\operatorname{FY}}(\textbf{a})
as a polynomial with variables indexed by arbitrary subsets rather than flats, and for allowing matroids
with loops, is that it would not otherwise make sense to assert that the two sides of the equation in the statement of Proposition
8.6

are valuative invariants of the matroid
M
. When considering all matroids at once, we cannot know in advance whether or not
G
is a flat.

Iterating Proposition
8.6
, we obtain the following corollary.

Corollary 8.8
.

For any
\mathcal{F}\in\operatorname{Flag}_{k}
, we have

\partial_{F_{1}}\cdots\partial_{F_{k-1}}\textup{Snap}_{M}^{\operatorname{FY}}(\textbf{a})=\prod_{i=1}^{k}\textup{Snap}_{M^{F_{i}}_{F_{i-1}}}^{\operatorname{FY}}(\textbf{a}|_{[F_{i-1},F_{i}]}).

Proof of Theorem
8.2
.

We use the fact that

\partial_{F_{1}}\cdots\partial_{F_{k-1}}\left(a_{F_{0}}^{(m_{0})}a_{F_{1}}^{(m_{1}+1)}a_{F_{2}}^{(m_{2}+1)}\cdots a_{F_{k-1}}^{(m_{k-1}+1)}a_{F_{k}}^{(m_{k})}\right)=a_{F_{0}}^{(m_{0})}\cdots a_{F_{k}}^{(m_{k})}

to interpret
c(\mathcal{F},\textbf{m})
as
the coefficient of
a_{F_{0}}^{(m_{0})}\cdots a_{F_{k}}^{(m_{k})}
in the polynomial

\partial_{F_{1}}\cdots\partial_{F_{k-1}}\textup{Snap}_{M}^{\operatorname{FY}}(\textbf{a})
.
Corollary
8.8
provides us with a formula for this polynomial.
Setting
a_{F}=0
for all
F
not appearing in
\mathcal{F}
,
we obtain the polynomial

\prod_{i=1}^{k}\textup{Snap}_{M^{F_{i}}_{F_{i-1}}}^{\operatorname{FY}}(a_{F_{i-1}},\mathbf{0},a_{F_{i}}).

By Lemma
8.5
, we may rewrite this as

(12)

\sum_{\textbf{e},\textbf{f}\in\mathbb{N}^{k}}(-1)^{|\textbf{e}|+|\textbf{f}|}\prod_{i=1}^{k}\binom{d_{i}-e_{i}}{f_{i}}\mu^{e_{i}}(M^{F_{i}}_{F_{i-1}})a_{F_{i-1}}^{(e_{i})}a_{F_{i}}^{(f_{i})}.

To compute the coefficient of
a_{F_{0}}^{(m_{0})}\cdots a_{F_{k}}^{(m_{k})}
,
we make use of the identity

x^{({m})}x^{(n)}=\sum_{\ell=0}^{m}\binom{m+n-\ell}{\ell,m-\ell,n-\ell}x^{(m+n-\ell)}

to rewrite (
12
) as

\sum_{\textbf{e},\textbf{f}\in\mathbb{N}^{k}}(-1)^{|\textbf{e}|+|\textbf{f}|}a_{\varnothing}^{(e_{1})}\prod_{i=1}^{k}\binom{d_{i}-e_{i}}{f_{i}}\mu^{e_{i}}(M^{F_{i}}_{F_{i-1}})\sum_{\ell_{i}=0}^{e_{i+1}}\binom{e_{i+1}+f_{i}-\ell_{i}}{\ell_{i},e_{i+1}-\ell_{i},f_{i}-\ell_{i}}a_{F_{i}}^{(e_{i+1}+f_{i}-\ell_{i})},

with the convention that
e_{k+1}=0
.
The coefficient of
a_{F_{0}}^{(m_{0})}\cdots a_{F_{k}}^{(m_{k})}
consists of those terms for which

e_{1}=m_{0}
and
e_{i+1}+f_{i}-\ell_{i}=m_{i}
for all
0\leq i\leq k
.
Thus

c(\mathcal{F},\textbf{m})=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\textbf{e},\textbf{f}\in\mathbb{N}^{k}\\
e_{1}=m_{0}\end{subarray}}(-1)^{|\textbf{e}|+|\textbf{f}|}\prod_{i=1}^{k}\binom{d_{i}-e_{i}}{f_{i}}\mu^{e_{i}}(M^{F_{i}}_{F_{i-1}})\binom{m_{i}}{e_{i+1}+f_{i}-m_{i},m_{i}-f_{i},m_{i}-e_{i+1}}.\qed

9.
Euler characteristics and Snapper polynomials for
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{0,n}

Last, we turn our attention to the moduli space
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{0,n}
.
Recall that we have defined a tuple of line bundles

\mathcal{L}\coloneqq(\mathcal{L}_{S}\mid S\subset[n-1],|S|\geq 3).

We begin this section by computing the Snapper polynomial
\textup{Snap}_{\mathcal{L}}(\textbf{a})
with respect to these line bundles.
Given a tuple of natural numbers
\textbf{m}=(m_{S}\mid S\subset[n-1]\mid|S|\geq 3)
, we say that

m
satisfies the
Cerberus condition
if, for every
\textbf{m}^{\prime}\leq\textbf{m}
,
we have

\Big|\bigcup_{m^{\prime}_{S}>0}S\cup\{n\}\,\,\Big|-3\;\geq\;\sum_{S}m^{\prime}_{S}.

Theorem 9.1
.

We have

\textup{Snap}_{\mathcal{L}}(\textbf{a})\;\;=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\text{$\textbf{m}$ satisfies}\\
\text{Cerberus}\end{subarray}}\textbf{a}^{(\textbf{m})}.

Proof.

Let
m
be given, and define

\tilde{\textbf{m}}=(\tilde{m}_{F}\mid\text{$F$ a nonempty flat of $\mathcal{B}_{n-1}$})

by putting
\tilde{m}_{F_{S}}=m_{S}
and
\tilde{m}_{F}=0

for all flats
F
not of the form
F_{S}
.
Because the map
p\colon W_{\mathcal{B}_{n-1}}\to\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{0,n}
is a composition of blowups at smooth centers,
the Euler characteristic of a line bundle on
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{0,n}
is equal to the Euler characteristic
of its pullback to
W_{\mathcal{B}_{n-1}}
. Thus, if we can show
that the Cerberus condition for
m
is equivalent to the dragon Hall–Rado condition for
\tilde{\textbf{m}}
,
our result will follow from Theorem
7.2
. Note that
\tilde{\textbf{m}}
fails the dragon Hall–Rado condition if and only if there is
\tilde{\textbf{m}}^{\prime}\leq\tilde{\textbf{m}}
with

\operatorname{rank}\bigcup_{\tilde{m}^{\prime}(F)>0}F\;\leq\;\sum_{F\not=\emptyset}\tilde{m}^{\prime}(F)\text{ and }\bigcup_{\tilde{m}^{\prime}(F)>0}F\text{ is a connected set}.

The remainder of the proof follows by direct
calculation, using the facts that
\operatorname{rk}F_{S}=|S|-1

and
\operatorname{rk}F_{S}\cup F_{T}=\operatorname{rk}F_{S\cup T}
if
S
and
T
are not disjoint.
∎

We conclude by computing the Snapper polynomial with respect to a different tuple of line bundles
on
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{0,n}
.
Recall that
\mathbb{L}_{i}
is the
i^{\text{th}}
cotangent line bundle, whose first Chern class is equal to
\psi_{i}
, and let

\mathbb{L}\coloneqq(\mathbb{L}_{1},\ldots,\mathbb{L}_{n}).
In
[
Pan97
]
, Pandharipande showed that if
a_{1},\dotsc,a_{n}\geq 0
, then
H^{i}(\overline{M}_{0,n},\mathbb{L}_{1}^{\otimes a_{1}}\otimes\dotsb\otimes\mathbb{L}_{n}^{\otimes a_{n}})=0
for
i>0
, and so

\chi(\overline{M}_{0,n},\mathbb{L}_{1}^{\otimes a_{1}}\otimes\dotsb\otimes\mathbb{L}_{n}^{\otimes a_{n}})=h^{0}(\overline{M}_{0,n},\mathbb{L}_{1}^{\otimes a_{1}}\otimes\dotsb\otimes\mathbb{L}_{n}^{\otimes a_{n}}).

In
[
Lee97
]
, Lee gave an expression for the generating function of this Euler characteristic, which is equivalent to the following theorem.

Theorem 9.2
.

[
Lee97
]

We have

\textup{Snap}_{\mathbb{L}}(\textbf{a})=\sum_{|\textbf{d}|\leq n-3}\binom{a_{1}}{d_{1}}\cdots\binom{a_{n}}{d_{n}}\binom{n-3}{n-3-\left\lvert\textbf{d}\right\rvert,d_{1},\ldots,d_{n}}.

We give a new proof of Theorem
9.2
, beginning with the following lemma. Recall the exceptional isomorphism
\zeta_{n}\colon K(\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{0,n})\to A(\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{0,n})

of Theorem
1.4
. Let
z_{i}\coloneqq 1-[\mathbb{L}_{i}]\in K(\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{0,n})
.

Lemma 9.3
.

We have
\zeta_{n}(z_{i})=-\psi_{i}
for all
i<n
, and
\zeta_{n}(z_{n})=-\psi_{n}/(1-\psi_{n})
.

Proof.

We will consider a family of closely related moduli spaces of curves, called the Losev–Manin spaces
[
LM00
]
.
The Losev–Manin space
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{0,w_{k,\ell}}
is a moduli space of stable rational curves with
n
weighted
marked points
[
Has03
]
, with weights 1 for the the
k^{\text{th}}
and
\ell^{\text{th}}
points and

\varepsilon\in\mathbb{Q}\cap(0,\frac{1}{n-2})
for the remaining
n-2
points.
Reduction of weights from
(1,\ldots,1)
to
w_{k,\ell}
induces a morphism
[
Has03
, Theorem 4.1]

\rho_{k,\ell}\colon\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{0,n}\to\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{0,w_{k,\ell}}.

Furthermore,
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{0,w_{k,\ell}}
is isomorphic to the permutohedral variety
X_{{[n]\smallsetminus\{k,\ell\}}}

[
LM00
]
, so we have an exceptional isomorphism

\zeta_{[n]\smallsetminus\{k,\ell\}}\colon K(\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{0,w_{k,\ell}})\to A(\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{0,w_{k,\ell}}).

For
i\not=n
, consider the classes
t_{E},t_{\emptyset}\in A(\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{0,w_{i,n}})\cong A(X_{{[n]\smallsetminus\{i,n\}}})
and
\tau_{E},\tau_{\emptyset}\in K(\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{0,w_{i,n}})\cong K(X_{{[n]\smallsetminus\{i,n\}}})
.
Then we have the following identities:

\displaystyle\rho_{n,i}^{\ast}t_{E}

\displaystyle=-\psi_{n}

\displaystyle\quad\quad\rho_{n,i}^{\ast}\tau_{E}

\displaystyle=1-[\mathbb{L}_{n}]

\displaystyle\rho_{n,i}^{\ast}t_{\emptyset}

\displaystyle=-\psi_{i}

\displaystyle\quad\quad\rho_{n,i}^{\ast}\tau_{\emptyset}

\displaystyle=1-[\mathbb{L}_{i}],

see, for example,
[
DR22
, Section 2]
.
For each
i\neq n
, we have the following diagram

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces K(\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{0,w_{n,i}})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces A(\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{0,w_{n,i}})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces K(\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{0,n})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces A(\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{0,n}),}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\zeta_{[n]\smallsetminus\{n,i\}}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\rho_{n,i}^{\ast}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\rho_{n,i}^{\ast}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\zeta_{n}}

whose commutativity can be checked by using that
\rho_{n,i}^{*}\tau_{E}=1-[\mathbb{L}_{n}^{-1}]
, and that similar formulas hold for pullbacks of the
n
th cotangent line bundle under forgetful maps.

The Lemma
5.8
gives that

\zeta_{n}(z_{i})=\zeta_{n}(1-[\mathbb{L}_{i}])=\zeta_{n}\circ\rho_{n,i}^{\ast}(\tau_{\emptyset})=\rho_{n,i}^{\ast}\circ\zeta_{[n]\smallsetminus\{n,i\}}(\tau_{\emptyset})=\rho_{n,i}^{\ast}(t_{\emptyset})=-\psi_{i},

and similarly

\zeta_{n}(z_{n})=\rho_{n,i}^{\ast}(\frac{t_{E}}{1+t_{E}})=\frac{-\psi_{n}}{1-\psi_{n}}.

∎

Proof of Theorem
9.2
.

By Lemma
7.3
, we have

\textup{Snap}_{\mathbb{L}}(-\textbf{a})=\sum_{\textbf{d}\in\mathbb{N}^{n}}\chi\left(\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{0,n},\,z_{1}^{d_{1}}\cdots z_{n}^{d_{n}}\right)\,\textbf{a}^{(\textbf{d})}.

(Note that the minus sign comes from the fact that
z_{i}=1-[\mathbb{L}_{i}]
rather than
1-[\mathbb{L}_{i}^{-1}]
.)
By Theorem
1.4
and Lemma
9.3
, we have

\chi\left(\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{0,n},\,z_{1}^{d_{1}}\cdots z_{n}^{d_{n}}\right)=\deg\left(\frac{\zeta_{n}(z_{1}^{d_{1}}\cdots z_{n}^{d_{n}})}{1-c_{[n-1]}}\right)=(-1)^{|\textbf{d}|}\deg\left(\frac{\psi_{1}^{d_{1}}\cdots\psi_{n}^{d_{n}}}{(1-\psi_{n})^{d_{n}+1}}\right).

Next, we observe that

\frac{\psi_{1}^{d_{1}}\cdots\psi_{n}^{d_{n}}}{(1-\psi_{n})^{d_{n}+1}}=\psi_{1}^{d_{1}}\cdots\psi_{n}^{d_{n}}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}(d_{n}+1)^{(k)}\psi_{n}^{k},

and therefore the part of this sum in degree
n-3
is equal to

\psi_{1}^{d_{1}}\cdots\psi_{n-1}^{d_{n-1}}\psi_{n}^{n-3-d_{1}-\cdots-d_{n-1}}(d_{n}+1)^{(n-3-|\textbf{d}|)}

if
|\textbf{d}|\leq n-3
, and zero otherwise.
By work of Witten
[
Wit91
]
, the degree of this term is equal to

\binom{n-3}{d_{1},\ldots,d_{n-1},n-3-d_{1}-\cdots-d_{n-1}}(d_{n}+1)^{(n-3-|\textbf{d}|)}.

Putting it all together, we have

\displaystyle\textup{Snap}_{\mathbb{L}}(-\textbf{a})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{d\in\mathbb{N}^{n}}\chi\left(\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{0,n},\,z_{1}^{d_{1}}\cdots z_{n}^{d_{n}}\right)\,\textbf{a}^{(\textbf{d})}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{|\textbf{d}|\leq n-3}(-1)^{|\textbf{d}|}\textbf{a}^{(\textbf{d})}\deg\left(\frac{\psi_{1}^{d_{1}}\cdots\psi_{n}^{d_{n}}}{(1-\psi_{n})^{d_{n}+1}}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{|\textbf{d}|\leq n-3}(-1)^{|\textbf{d}|}\textbf{a}^{(\textbf{d})}(d_{n}+1)^{(n-3-|\textbf{d}|)}\binom{n-3}{d_{1},\ldots,d_{n-1},n-3-d_{1}-\cdots-d_{n-1}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{|\textbf{d}|\leq n-3}(-1)^{|\textbf{d}|}\textbf{a}^{(\textbf{d})}\binom{d_{n}+n-3-|\textbf{d}|}{n-3-|\textbf{d}|}\binom{n-3}{d_{1},\ldots,d_{n-1},n-3-d_{1}-\cdots-d_{n-1}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{|\textbf{d}|\leq n-3}(-1)^{|\textbf{d}|}\textbf{a}^{(\textbf{d})}\binom{n-3}{d_{1},\ldots,d_{n},n-3-|\textbf{d}|}.

Finally, this implies that

\textup{Snap}_{\mathbb{L}}(\textbf{a})=\sum_{|\textbf{d}|\leq n-3}\binom{a_{1}}{d_{1}}\cdots\binom{a_{n}}{d_{n}}\binom{n-3}{d_{1},\ldots,d_{n},n-3-|\textbf{d}|},

which completes the proof.
∎

Appendix A
The simplicial presentation of Chow rings

This appendix is devoted to proving the simplicial presentation for the (augmented) Chow ring of a matroid.
The simplicial generators of
A(M)
were extensively studied in
[
BES23
]
, and the surjectivity of
\Phi_{\nabla}
was proved there. This immediately generalizes to
\Phi_{\nabla}^{{\operatorname{aug}}}
. That work did not give a simple description of the kernel of
\Phi_{\nabla}
.

For any pair of flats
F
and
G
, let

z_{F,G}\coloneqq\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}F\subset F^{\prime}\subsetneq F\vee G\\
G\subset G^{\prime}\subsetneq F\vee G\end{subarray}}x_{F^{\prime}}x_{G^{\prime}}\in T_{M}.

For any element
e\in E
and flat
F\neq\emptyset
, let

w_{e,F}\coloneqq\sum_{F\subset G\not\ni e}y_{e}x_{G}\in T_{M}.

Recall the definitions of the ideals
\mathcal{I}_{1},\mathcal{I}_{2},\mathcal{I}_{3},\mathcal{I}_{4}^{\operatorname{aug}}\subset T_{M}
in Equation (
3
).

Lemma A.1
.

We have the following equalities of ideals in
T_{M}
:

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}_{3}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left\langle z_{F,G}\mid\text{$F,G$ {arbitrary}}\right\rangle

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}_{2}+\mathcal{I}_{4}^{\operatorname{aug}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}_{2}+\langle y_{e}^{2}\mid e\in E\rangle+\left\langle w_{e,F}\mid e\in E,F\neq\varnothing\right\rangle.

Proof.

We begin with the first statement.
Every term in
z_{F,G}
is a product of incomparable flats, thus
z_{F,G}
is contained in
\mathcal{I}_{3}
.
The opposite inclusion follows by induction on sum of the coranks of
F
and
G
, using the observation that, whenever
F
and
G
are incomparable,

z_{F,G}=x_{F}x_{G}+\text{terms with lower sum of coranks.}

For the second statement, we write

\mathcal{I}_{4}^{\operatorname{aug}}=\langle y_{e}x_{\varnothing}\mid e\in E\rangle+\langle y_{e}x_{F}\mid e\notin F\neq\varnothing\rangle.

A similar argument to the one above shows that

\langle y_{e}x_{F}\mid e\notin F\neq\varnothing\rangle=\left\langle w_{e,F}\mid e\in E,F\neq\varnothing\right\rangle,

so it remains only to show that
y_{e}x_{\varnothing}
is congruent to
y_{e}^{2}
modulo the ideal
\mathcal{I}_{2}+\left\langle w_{e,F}\mid e\in E,F\neq\varnothing\right\rangle
.
Indeed, we have

y_{e}^{2}-x_{\varnothing}y_{e}=y_{e}\left(y_{e}-\sum_{e\notin F}x_{F}\right)+\sum_{e\notin F\neq\varnothing}y_{e}x_{F},

which completes the proof.
∎

Recall that we have defined the homomorphism
\Phi_{\nabla}^{\operatorname{aug}}\colon S_{M}\to A^{\operatorname{aug}}(M)
by the formula

\Phi_{\nabla}^{\operatorname{aug}}(u_{F})\coloneqq-\sum_{F\subset G}\Phi_{\operatorname{FY}}(x_{G}).

It is easy to see that the kernel of the map
A^{{\operatorname{aug}}}(M)\to A(M)
is generated by
\langle h_{F}:\operatorname{rk}F=1\rangle
, so the following theorem implies Theorem
5.3
.

Theorem A.2
.

The map
\Phi_{\nabla}^{\operatorname{aug}}
is surjective with kernel
\mathcal{J}_{1}+\mathcal{J}_{2}^{{\operatorname{aug}}}
.

Proof.

Consider the homomorphism
\Theta\colon S_{M}\to T_{M}
defined by the formula

\Theta(u_{F})\coloneqq-\sum_{F\subset G}x_{G}

for all nonempty flats
F
, so that
\Phi_{\nabla}^{\operatorname{aug}}=\Phi_{\operatorname{FY}}^{\operatorname{aug}}\circ\Theta
.
The image of
\Theta
is equal to the subring
\mathbb{Z}[x_{F}\mid\text{$F$ a nonempty flat}]\subset T_{M}
. In particular, the composition

\bar{\Theta}\colon S_{M}\to T_{M}\to T_{M}/(\mathcal{I}_{1}+\mathcal{I}_{2})

is
an isomorphism. This implies that
\Phi_{\nabla}^{\operatorname{aug}}
is surjective,
and its kernel is equal to

\Theta^{-1}(\mathcal{I}_{1}+\mathcal{I}_{2}+\mathcal{I}_{3}+\mathcal{I}_{4}^{\operatorname{aug}}).

For the remainder of the proof, we put a bar over an element of
T_{M}
or an ideal in
T_{M}
to denote its image in
T_{M}/(\mathcal{I}_{1}+\mathcal{I}_{2})
.
Then we need to compute
\bar{\Theta}^{-1}(\bar{\mathcal{I}}_{3}+\bar{\mathcal{I}}_{4}^{\operatorname{aug}})
.
By Lemma
A.1
, we may rewrite this as

\bar{\Theta}^{-1}\Big(\left\langle\bar{z}_{F,G}\mid\text{$F,G$ arbitrary}\right\rangle+\langle\bar{y}_{e}^{2}\mid e\in E\rangle+\left\langle w_{e,F}\mid e\in E,F\neq\varnothing\right\rangle\Big).

We have the following equalities:

\displaystyle\bar{z}_{F,G}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\bar{\Theta}\Big((u_{F}-u_{F\vee G})(u_{G}-u_{F\vee G})\Big),

\displaystyle\bar{y}_{e}^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\bar{\Theta}(u_{\bar{e}}^{2}),

\displaystyle\bar{w}_{e,F}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\bar{\Theta}\Big(u_{\bar{e}}(u_{\bar{e}\vee F}-u_{F})\Big),

which together imply that the kernel of
\Phi_{\nabla}^{\operatorname{aug}}
is equal to

\mathcal{J}_{1}+\langle u_{\bar{e}}^{2}\mid e\in E\rangle+\langle u_{\bar{e}}(u_{\bar{e}\vee F}-u_{F})\mid e\in E,F\neq\varnothing\rangle.

The theorem now follows from the fact that the second and third summand above add to
\mathcal{J}_{2}^{\operatorname{aug}}
.
∎

An esoteric identity with many parameters and other elliptic extensions of elementary identities
Gaurav Bhatnagar, Archna Kumari, Michael J. Schlosser

Abstract
. We provide elliptic extensions of elementary identities such as the sum of the first n n odd or even numbers, the geometric sum and the sum of the first n n cubes. Many such identities, and their q q -analogues, are indefinite sums, and can be obtained from telescoping. So we used telescoping in our study to find elliptic extensions of these identities. In the course of our study, we obtained an identity with many parameters, which appears to be new even in the q q -case. In addition, we recover some q q -identities due to Warnaar.

Introduction

The geometric sum is a staple of high school algebra. It can be written as

\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}q^{k}=\frac{1-q^{n}}{1-q}=:[n]_{q},

-analogue of

[

-case. This work is in the context of the
rapidly developing field of elliptic combinatorics. Some recent references are

To be able to define an elliptic number, we need some notation.
The
modified Jacobi theta function
of the complex number
a

with (fixed) nome

is defined as

\theta\!\left({a};p\right):=\prod\limits_{j=0}^{\infty}(1-ap^{j})(1-p^{j+1}/a)

where
a\neq 0
and
|p|<1
.
When the nome
p=0
, the modified theta function
\theta\!\left({a};p\right)
reduces to
(1-a)
.
We use the shorthand notation

\theta\!\left({a_{1},a_{2},\dots,a_{r}};p\right):=\theta\!\left({a_{1}};p\right)\theta\!\left({a_{2}};p\right)\cdots\theta\!\left({a_{r}};p\right).

The elliptic analogue of a complex number

is defined by
[
19
]
as

[z]_{a,b;q,p}:=\frac{\theta(q^{z},aq^{z},bq^{2},a/b;p)}{\theta(q,aq,bq^{z+1},aq^{z-1}/b;p)}.

(1.2a)

This has additional (complex) parameters
a
and

, in addition to the
base

q
and nome
p
. Note that

[0]_{a,b;q,p}=0
and
[1]_{a,b;q,p}=1
.
Let the elliptic weight be defined by

W_{a,b;q,p}(k):=\frac{\theta(aq^{2k+1},bq,bq^{2},aq^{-1}/b,a/b;p)}{\theta(aq,bq^{k+1},bq^{k+2},aq^{k-1}/b,aq^{k}/b;p)}q^{k},

(1.2b)

for any

.
By the Weierstraß addition formula for theta functions (see (

[x+y]_{a,b;q,p}=[x]_{a,b;q,p}+W_{a,b;q,p}(x)[y]_{aq^{2x},bq^{x};q,p}.

(1.2c)

Note that if we set

, the elliptic weight in (

) reduces to

. In this case (

) reduces to the recurrence relation

[x+y]_{q}=[x]_{q}+q^{x}[y]_{q}.

This, along with the initial conditions
[0]_{q}=0
and
[1]_{q}=1
, is used to define the
q
-number for integers. Thus, the elliptic number is indeed an extension of the
q
-number

for any complex

The analogue of the geometric sum (

n
is assumed to be a non-negative integer.)

1+W_{a,b;q,p}(1)+W_{a,b;q,p}(2)+\cdots+W_{a,b;q,p}(n-1)=[n]_{a,b;q,p}.

(1.3)

Many such elementary results (even in the

[

q
-case.
At this point, we would like to emphasize that the parameters in our identities
should be chosen such that not-removable singularities and poles are avoided,
so that the identities make sense.

Theorem 1
.

For any non-negative integer

and complex numbers

we have the following identity:

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{n}

\displaystyle\Bigg(\frac{\big[2(gk+c)(hk+d)\big]_{a,b;q,p}\,\big[2ghk+ch+dg\big]_{aq^{2(gk-g+c)(hk+d)},bq^{(gk-g+c)(hk+d)};q,p}}{\big[2cd\big]_{a,b;q,p}\,\big[ch+dg\big]_{aq^{2(c-g)d},bq^{(c-g)d};q,p}}

\displaystyle={}

\displaystyle\frac{\big[(gn+c)(hn+h+d)\big]_{a,b;q,p}\,\big[(g+c)d\big]_{aq^{2(c-g)d},bq^{(c-g)d};q,p}}{\big[2cd\big]_{a,b;q,p}\,\big[ch+dg\big]_{aq^{2(c-g)d},bq^{(c-g)d};q,p}}

\displaystyle\times\prod_{j=1}^{n}\frac{\big[(gj+g+c)(hj+d)\big]_{aq^{2(gj-g+c)(hj+d)},bq^{(gj-g+c)(hj+d)};q,p}}{\big[(gj+c)(hj-h+d)\big]_{aq^{2(gj+c)(hj+h+d)},bq^{(gj+c)(hj+h+d)};q,p}}

\displaystyle-\frac{\big[(c-g)d\big]_{a,b;q,p}\,\big[c(d-h)\big]_{aq^{2c(h+d)},bq^{c(h+d)};q,p}}{\big[2cd\big]_{a,b;q,p}\,\big[ch+dg\big]_{aq^{2(c-g)d},bq^{(c-g)d};q,p}}\,W_{aq^{2(c-g)d},bq^{(c-g)d};q,p}\big(ch+dg\big).

(1.4)

The “hypergeometric version” of (
1

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{n}\frac{(gk+c)(hk+d)(2ghk+ch+dg)}{cd(ch+dg)}=\frac{(gn+c)(hn+h+d)(gn+g+c)(hn+d)}{2cd(ch+dg)}

\displaystyle-\frac{(d-h)(c-g)}{2(ch+dg)}.

This is indeed an elementary identity, but

Note, for example, the product

\prod_{j=0}^{k-1}\big[(gj+g+c)(hj+d)\big]_{aq^{2(gj-g+c)(hj+d)},bq^{(gj-g+c)(hj+d)};q,p}

In particular, the ratio

Background information

(1)

Two important properties of the modified theta function are

[

\displaystyle\theta\!\left({a};p\right)=\theta\!\left({p/a};p\right)=-a\theta\!\left({1/a};p\right),

(2)

The following general theorem serves as a justification of

Proposition 2

(

\displaystyle a_{1}a_{2}\cdots a_{r}=b_{1}b_{2}\cdots b_{r}.

This last condition is the

Lemma 3

(Euler’s telescoping lemma)

Let

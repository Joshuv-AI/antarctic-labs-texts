On the connection formula of a higher rank analog of Painlevé VI
Xiaomeng Xu

Abstract
In this paper, we derive the connection formula for the higher rank analog of Painlevé VI arsing from the isomonodromic deformation theory and the study of Frobenius manifolds.



1
Introduction

The six classical Painlevé equations were introduced at the turn of the twentieth century by Painlevé
[
22
]
and Gambier
[
14
]
, in a specific
classification problem for second order ODEs. Since then, they have appeared in the integrable nonlinear PDEs, 2D Ising models, random matrices, topological field theory and so on.
We refer the reader to the book of Fokas, Its, Kapaev and Novokshenov
[
13
]
for a thorough introduction to the history and developments of the study of Painlevé equations. As stressed in
[
13
,
17
]
, the solutions of Painlevé equations (called Painlevé transcendents) are seen as nonlinear special functions, because they play the same role in nonlinear mathematical physics as that of classical special functions, like Airy functions, Bessel functions, etc., in linear physics. And it is the answers of the following questions
(a)
and
(b)
make Painlevé transcendents as efficient in applications as linear special functions (see
[
13
,
17
]
and the references therein for more details):

(a).

The parametrization of Painlevé transcendents by their asymptotic behaviour at critical points;

(b).

The construction of the connection
formula from one critical point to another.

Many global asymptotic results, including the connection formula, of Painlevé transcendents are based on the method of isomonodromic deformation, introduced by Flaschka-Newell
[
12
]
, and by Jimbo-Miwa-Ueno
[
19
]
.

The theory of isomonodromic deformation, as well as the theory of Frobenius manifolds (see the book
[
10
]
of Dubrovin), leads to a natural generalization of a higher rank analog of the sixth Painlevé equation. Let
\mathfrak{h}_{\rm reg}
denote the set of
n\times n
diagonal matrices with distinct eigenvalues. Then, the higher rank analog of the sixth Painlevé equation is the nonlinear differential equation for a
n\times n
matrix valued function
\Phi(u):\mathfrak{h}_{\rm reg}\rightarrow\mathfrak{gl}(n)

\displaystyle\frac{\partial\Phi}{\partial u_{k}}=\frac{1}{2\pi\iota}[\Phi,{\rm ad}^{-1}_{u}{\rm ad}_{E_{kk}}\Phi],\ \text{for all}\ k=1,...,n.

(1)

Here the square brackets take the matrix commutator,
u={\rm diag}(u_{1},...,u_{n})\in\mathfrak{h}_{\rm reg}
, and
\iota:=\sqrt{-1}
,
E_{kk}
is the
n\times n
diagonal matrix whose
(k,k)
-entry is
1
and other entries are
0
.
Note that the adjoint
{\rm ad}_{E_{kk}}\Phi=[E_{kk},\Phi]
takes values in the subspace of off-diagonal matrices, and that the adjoint operator
{\rm ad}_{u}

is invertible when restricted to the subspace. In this sense
{\rm ad}^{-1}_{u}{\rm ad}_{E_{kk}}\Phi
denotes the unique off-diagonal matrix such that
[u,{\rm ad}^{-1}_{u}{\rm ad}_{E_{kk}}\Phi]={\rm ad}_{E_{kk}}\Phi
.

The equation (
1
) is the isomonodromic deformation equation of meromorphic linear systems of ordinary differential equations with Poncaré rank
1
. See Section
3.2
. Following Miwa
[
21
]
, the solutions of the equation (
1
) have the Painlevé property. Furthermore, they are multi-valued meromorphic functions of
u_{1},...,u_{n}
and the branching occurs when
u
moves along a loop around the fat diagonal

\Delta=\{(u_{1},...,u_{n})\in\mathbb{C}^{n}~|~u_{i}=u_{j},\text{for some }i\neq j\}.

Thus, according to the original idea of Painlevé, they can be a new class of special functions. In the meanwhile, these functions have found applications in the theory of Frobenius manifolds
[
10
]
, solving the Witten-Dijkgraaf-Verlinde-Verlinde (WDVV) equations
[
15
]
, stability conditions
[
6
]
, crystal bases in representation theory
[
25
]
, the WKB approximation of linear systems of differential equations
[
1
]
and so on. However, in the literature there is no
general results of the similar questions
(a)
and
(b)
for (
1
).

To fill the gap, in the previous paper
[
25
]
, we studied the problem
(a)
, i.e., characterized the asymptotics of solutions of the equation (
1
) in all asymptotic zones of
u
, using the idea of the Gelfand-Tsetlin theory in representation theory. Furthermore, we obtained the expression of the monodromy of the associated linear problem. We remark that the explicit expression of monodromy data would give some constrains on the distributions of poles of solutions of (
1
).

In this paper, we use the results in
[
25
]
to derive an answer to the problem
(b)
, i.e., obtaining the explicit connection formula between two asymptotic zones, via the method of isomonodromic deformation. As will become clear below, we thus obtain the global information about the equation (
1
) using only a local analysis.

The case
n=3
: Painlevé VI

Before stating our main result, let us give a brief recall of the sixth Painlevé equation (simply denoted by PVI) as an illustrative example. That is the nonlinear differential equation

\displaystyle\frac{d^{2}y}{dx^{2}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\Big[\frac{1}{y}+\frac{1}{y-1}+\frac{1}{y-x}\Big](\frac{dy}{dx})^{2}-\Big[\frac{1}{x}+\frac{1}{x-1}+\frac{1}{y-x}\Big]\frac{dy}{dx}

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\frac{y(y-1)(y-x)}{x^{2}(x-1)^{2}}\Big[\alpha+\beta\frac{x}{y^{2}}+\gamma\frac{x-1}{(y-1)^{2}}+\delta\frac{x(x-1)}{(y-x)^{2}}\Big],\ \alpha,\beta,\gamma,\delta\in\mathbb{C}.

A solution
y(x)
of PVI has
0,1,\infty
as critical points, and can be analytically
continued to a meromorphic function on the universal covering of
\mathbb{P}^{1}\setminus\{0,1,\infty\}
. The function
y(x)
is in general not given in terms of classical functions. Then, following
[
13
]
, to "solve" the Painlevé equation, it means that

•

to find the explicit asymptotics of
y(x)
at the critical points
x=0,1,\infty
:

\displaystyle y(x)\sim y_{p}(x;a_{p},\sigma_{p}),\ \text{as}\ x\rightarrow p,\ p\in\{0,1,\infty\},

(2)

where
a_{p},\sigma_{p}
are the asymptotic parameters;

•

to find the explicit connection formula of
y(x)
between two different critical points
p\neq q\in\{0,1,\infty\}
, i.e., the explicit formula

\displaystyle a_{p}=a_{p}(a_{q},\sigma_{q}),\hskip 14.22636pt\sigma_{p}=\sigma_{p}(a_{q},\sigma_{q}).

(3)

The asymptotics (
2
) and connection formula (
3
) for
y(x)
were evaluated via the isomonodromy approach for the generic case by Jimbo
[
18
]
, and for important special cases, not covered by Jimbo’s
results, by Dubrovin-Mazzocco
[
11
]
and Guzzetti
[
16
]
. The key feature is that the parameters
(a_{p},\sigma_{p})
for
p=0,1,\infty
can be expressed as functions
of the monodromy data of the associated linear system of differential equation, then the isomonodromy method enables one to solve the
connection problem.

It was shown in
[
9
]
that Painlevé VI is equivalent to the equation (
1
) for
n=3
with suitable matrices
\Phi(u)
. In particular, let us assume that there exists parameters
\theta_{1},\theta_{2},\theta_{3},\theta_{\infty}

\displaystyle{\rm diag}(\Phi(u))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2\pi\iota\cdot{\rm diag}(\theta_{1},\theta_{2},\theta_{3}),

(4)

\displaystyle\text{eigenvalues of }\Phi(u)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 0,\ \pi\iota\cdot(\theta_{1}+\theta_{2}+\theta_{3}-\theta_{\infty}),\ \pi\iota\cdot(\theta_{1}+\theta_{2}+\theta_{3}+\theta_{\infty}).

(5)

Then the equation (
1
) is equivalent to the Painlevé VI of
y(x)
with

x=\frac{u_{2}-u_{1}}{u_{3}-u_{1}}

and the parameters

2\alpha=(\theta_{\infty}-1)^{2},\ 2\beta=-\theta_{1}^{2},\ 2\gamma=-\theta_{3}^{2},\ 2\delta=-\theta_{2}^{2}.

Therefore, the asymptotics (
2
) and connection problem (
3
) of Painlevé VI amounts to the study of the asymptotics of solutions of the equation
1
as

\displaystyle\frac{u_{2}-u_{1}}{u_{3}-u_{1}}\rightarrow\infty,\hskip 5.69054pt\frac{u_{3}-u_{1}}{u_{2}-u_{1}}\rightarrow\infty,\hskip 5.69054pt\frac{u_{2}-u_{1}}{u_{2}-u_{3}}\rightarrow\infty,

(6)

respectively, and the connection problem between these asymptotics. This perspective has been taken in
[
15
]
with applications in solving the WDVV equations via the asymptotic properties of the equation (
1
) for
n=3
.

Asymptotics of solutions of the equation (

)

of

3
Connection formula via the method of isomonodromic deformation

The nonlinear differential equation (
1
) governs the isomonodromic deformation of a meromorphic linear system of ordinary differential equations. Furthermore, the monodromy data of the linear system is a complete set of independent first integrals of (
1
). It thus transfers global analytic problems of equation (
1
) to the study of the monodromy of the linear system (we refer the reader to the book
[
17
]
for an introduction to the isomonodromic deformation method in the study of Painlevé equations). The key feature of the associated linear system is the appearance of irregular singularities, which involves the study of Stokes phenomenon. Thus, an analytic basis
for the achievement of the connection formula consists of solving explicitly the Riemann-Hilbert-Birkhoff problem of the linear system in asymptotic zones (see Theorem
3.7
for one example, and see
[
25
]
for more details).

This section applies the idea of isomonodromic deformation to solve the connection problem for (
1
).
Section
3.1
introduces the involved meromorphic linear system of ordinary differential equations and recalls the notion of its Stokes matrices. Section
3.2
explains that in which sense the equation (
1
) is the isomonodromic deformation equation of the linear system. Section
3.3
explicitly expresses the Stokes matrices of the linear system via the asymptotics of solutions of (
1
). In the end, Section
3.4
gives the explicit connection formula between the two asymptotic zones
u_{\rm cat}^{(+)}
to
u_{\rm cat}^{(-)}
, and the last subsection briefly discusses the connection formula between all asymptotic zones.

3.1
Meromorphic linear system of Poncaré rank 1 and Stokes matrices

Let us consider the meromorphic linear system

\displaystyle\frac{dF}{dz}=\Big(\iota u-\frac{1}{2\pi\iota}\frac{A}{z}\Big)\cdot F,

(11)

where
F(z)
is valued in
{\rm GL}_{n}(\mathbb{C})
,
u\in\mathfrak{h}_{\rm reg}(\mathbb{R})
and
A\in{{\rm Herm}}(n)
. The system has an order two pole at
\infty
and (if
A\neq 0
) an order one pole at
0
.

Definition 3.1
.

The
Stokes sectors
of the system
are the right/left half planes
{\rm Sect}_{\pm}=\{z\in\mathbb{C}~|~\pm{\rm Re}(z)>0\}
.

For any two real numbers
a,b
, an open sector and a closed sector with opening
angle
b-a>0
are respectively denoted by

S(a,b):=\{z\in\mathbb{C}~|~a<{\rm arg}(z)<b\},\hskip 14.22636pt\bar{S}(a,b):=\{z\in\mathbb{C}~|~a\leq{\rm arg}(z)\leq b\}.

Let us choose the branch of
{\rm log}(z)
, which is real on the positive real axis, with a cut along the nonnegative imaginary axis
\iota\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
. Then by convention,
{\rm log}(z)
has imaginary part
-\pi
on the negative real axis in
{\rm Sect}_{-}
. The following construction of solutions via the Laplace-Borel transform is standard, see e.g.,
[
2
,
20
,
25
]
.

Theorem 3.2
.

For any
u\in\mathfrak{h}_{\rm reg}(\mathbb{R})
, on
{\rm Sect}_{\pm}
there
is a unique (therefore canonical) fundamental solution
F_{\pm}:{\rm Sect}_{\pm}\to{\rm GL}_{n}(\mathbb{C})
of equation (
11
) such that
F_{+}\cdot e^{-\iota uz}\cdot z^{\frac{[A]}{2\pi\iota}}
and
F_{-}\cdot e^{-\iota uz}\cdot z^{\frac{[A]}{2\pi\iota}}
can be analytically continued to
S(-\pi,\pi)
and
S(-2\pi,0)
respectively, and for every small
\varepsilon>0
,

\displaystyle\lim_{z\rightarrow\infty}F_{+}(z;u)\cdot e^{-\iota uz}\cdot z^{\frac{[A]}{2\pi\iota}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\rm Id}_{n},\ \ \ as\ \ \ z\in\bar{S}(-\pi+\varepsilon,\pi-\varepsilon),

\displaystyle\lim_{z\rightarrow\infty}F_{-}(z;u)\cdot e^{-\iota uz}\cdot z^{\frac{[A]}{2\pi\iota}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\rm Id}_{n},\ \ \ as\ \ \ z\in\bar{S}(-2\pi+\varepsilon,-\varepsilon),

Here
{\rm Id}_{n}
is the rank n identity matrix, and
[A]
is the diagonal part of
A
. The solutions
F_{\pm}
are called the canonical solutions in
{\rm Sect}_{\pm}
.

For any
\sigma\in S_{n}
, let us denote by
U_{\sigma}
the connected component

U_{\sigma}:=\{u={\rm diag}(u_{1},...,u_{n})\in\mathfrak{h}_{\rm reg}(\mathbb{R})~|~u_{\sigma(1)}<\cdot\cdot\cdot<u_{\sigma(n)}\}

, and denote by

Definition 3.3
.

For any

, the
Stokes matrices
of the system (
11
) (with respect
to
{\rm Sect}_{+}
and the chosen branch of
{\rm log}(z)
) are the elements

determined by

where the first (resp. second) identity is understood to hold in
{\rm Sect}_{-}

(resp.
{\rm Sect}_{+}
) after
F_{+}
(resp.
F_{-}
)
has been analytically continued anticlockwise around

According to our definition, the (diagonal part of) Stokes matrices include the information of the formal monodromy data of (

4
]
where Stokes matrices are defined such that all diagonal entries are

The prescribed asymptotics of

Lemma 3.4
.

Let
S_{+}(u,A)^{\dagger}
denote the conjugation transpose of

S_{-}(u,A)=S_{+}(u,A)^{\dagger}
.

3.2
Isomonodromic deformation

In general, the Stokes matrices
S_{\pm}(u,A)
of the system (
11
) will depend on the irregular term
u
. The isomonodromic deformation (also known as monodromy preserving) problem is to find the matrix valued function
A(u)
such that the Stokes matrices
S_{\pm}(u,A(u))
are (locally) constant. In particular, the following proposition is well known. See more detailed discussions in e.g.,
[
5
,
8
,
10
,
19
]
.

Proposition 3.5
.

For any solution
\Phi(u)
of the equation (
1
), the Stokes matrices
S_{\pm}(u,\Phi(u))

are locally constant (independent of
u
).

That is one can associate with initial non-linear differential equation (
1
) a linear system

\displaystyle\frac{dF}{dz}=\Big(\iota u-\frac{1}{2\pi\iota}\frac{\Phi(u)}{z}\Big)\cdot F,

(12)

where the variation of
\Phi(u)
in the
coefficient is described by equation (
1
). Then the monodromy data (Stokes matrices) of system (
12
) is conserved. In this way, (
1
) is explained as the isomonodromic deformation equation of the linear system (
12
). In the following, the equation (
1
) is also called the isomonodromy equation.

3.3

\displaystyle e^{\frac{\small{\lambda^{(k-1)}_{k}-\lambda^{(k)}_{k+1}}}{4}}\sum_{i=1}^{k}\frac{\prod_{l=1,l\neq i}^{k}\Gamma(1-\frac{\lambda^{(k)}_{l}-\lambda^{(k)}_{i}}{2\pi\iota})}{\prod_{l=1}^{k+1}\Gamma(1-\frac{\lambda^{(k+1)}_{l}-\lambda^{(k)}_{i}}{2\pi\iota})}\frac{\prod_{l=1,l\neq i}^{k}\Gamma(1-\frac{\lambda^{(k)}_{l}-\lambda^{(k)}_{i}}{2\pi\iota})}{\prod_{l=1}^{k-1}\Gamma(1-\frac{\lambda^{(k-1)}_{l}-\lambda^{(k)}_{i}}{2\pi\iota})}\cdot\overline{m^{(k)}_{i}}.

Here

Proposition 3.9
.

The Stokes matrices

\displaystyle S_{\pm}\big(u,\Phi_{-}(u;A_{-\infty})\big)=P\cdot S_{\mp}\big(u,\Phi(u;PA_{-\infty}P)\big)\cdot P.

(13)

Here

as every anti-diagonal entry.

Proof.
If

are the canonical solutions of equation (

) in
{\rm Sect}_{\pm}
, then one checks that the functions

\displaystyle F^{\tau}_{-}(z):=P\cdot F_{-}(-z)\cdot P\hskip 5.69054pt\text{and}\hskip 5.69054ptF^{\tau}_{+}(z):=P\cdot F_{+}(-z)\cdot P

(14)

are solutions of

\displaystyle\frac{dF}{dz}=\Big(-\iota PuP-\frac{1}{2\pi\iota}\frac{PAP}{z}\Big)\cdot F.

(15)

Following the asymptotics of
F_{\pm}
at

and the uniqueness of solutions of (

) with prescribed asymptotics by Theorem

,

and

are actually the canonical solutions of (

) in the sectors
{\rm Sect}_{+}
and
{\rm Sect}_{-}

By Definition

, the Stokes matrices
S_{\pm}\Big(-PuP,PAP\Big)

F^{\tau}_{\pm}(z)

Replacing

once the correct defining sectors of

The above identity holds for any

\displaystyle S_{\pm}(u,\Phi_{-}(u;A_{-\infty}))=PS_{\mp}\Big(-PuP,P\Phi_{-}(u;A_{-\infty})P\Big)P.

\displaystyle S_{\pm}(u,\Phi_{-}(u;A_{-\infty}))=PS_{\mp}(v,P\Phi_{-}(v;A_{-\infty})P)P.

3.4
Connection formula between the asymptotic zones
u_{\rm cat}^{(+)}
and
u_{\rm cat}^{(-)}

Recall that the asymptotic zones
u_{\rm cat}^{(+)}
and
u_{\rm cat}^{(-)}
are determined by (
9
) and (
10
). Given a solution
\Phi(u)
of the isomonodromy equation (
1
), we have computed the Stokes matrices as functions of the asymptotics parameter
A_{\infty}
at
u_{\rm cat}^{(+)}
, and of the asymptotics
A_{-\infty}
at
u_{\rm cat}^{(-)}
. As an immediate consequence, the knowledge of the Stokes matrices enables us to connect the two sets of asymptotics parameters.

Theorem 3.10
.

Assume that a solution
\Phi(u)\in{\rm Herm}(n)
of the isomonodromy equation (
1
) on
U_{\rm id}
has

•

the asymptotics
A_{\infty}
at
u_{\rm cat}^{(+)}
;

•

the asymptotics
A_{-\infty}
at
u_{\rm cat}^{(-)}
,

then we have

\displaystyle S_{+}(u,\Phi(u;A_{\infty}))=PS_{-}(u,\Phi(u;PA_{-\infty}P^{-1}))P^{-1}.

(19)

Proof.
By the assumption of
\Phi(u)
and the Definition
3.6
and
3.8
, we have
\Phi(u)=\Phi(u;A_{\infty})=\Phi_{-}(u;A_{-\infty}).

It gives

S_{+}(u,\Phi(u;A_{\infty}))=S_{+}(u,\Phi(u;A_{-\infty})).

Then replacing
S_{+}(u,\Phi(u;A_{-\infty}))
by the right hand side of the identity (
13
) verifies the identity (
19
).

Theorem
3.10
solves the connection problem between the two asymptotic zones of
\Phi(u)
. Indeed, the Stokes matrices give a complete set of independent first integrals of the isomonodromy equation, and they are explicitly expressed by the asymptotics of the same solution

\Phi(u)
in two different zones. Thus the asymptotics
A_{-\infty}
at
u_{\rm cat}^{(-)}
is an implicit function of the asymptotics
A_{\infty}
at
u_{\rm cat}^{(+)}
by the implicit equation (
19
) (independent of
u
). By Theorem
2.1
, the implicit equation (
19
) can be explicitly expressed by
A_{\infty}
and
A_{-\infty}
involving gamma functions, eigenvalue functions and so on. Therefore, the connection formula of the higher rank analog of Painlevé VI, though complicated, is still explicit.

3.5
The connection problem for generic asymptotic zones

As we have seen from the previous subsections, in the study of the connection problem via the method of isomonodromic deformation, the key point is to find the expression of the Stokes matrices
S_{\pm}(u,\Phi(u))
by the asymptotics of the solution
\Phi(u)
of the isomonodormy equation in an asymptotic zone
u_{\rm asy}
. Actually, following
[
25
]
, a generalization of Theorem
3.7
from
u_{\rm cat}^{(+)}
to any other asymptotic zone
u_{\rm asy}
is possible: it consists in reducing the computation of Stokes matrices of (
12
) to the computation of the monodromy data of the equations taking the form (the so-called analytic branching rule in
[
25
]
)

\displaystyle\frac{dF}{dz}=\Big(\iota D_{k}-\frac{1}{2\pi\iota}\frac{A}{z}\Big)\cdot F,

(20)

where
F(z)
is valued in
{\rm GL}_{n}(\mathbb{C})
,
A\in{{\rm Herm}}(n)
and

D_{k}={{\rm diag}(\underbrace{0,...,0}_{k},\underbrace{1,...,1}_{n-k})},\ \text{for}\ k=1,...,n-1.

Roughly speaking, if the irregular term
u
of the system (
12
) depends on a real parameter
t
in the way that
u(t)={\rm diag}(u_{1},...,u_{k},t+u_{k+1},...,t+u_{n})
, then the equation (
20
) was used in
[
25
]
to decompose (or "branch") the
n\times n
system (
12
) to the upper left and lower right subsystems of rank
k
and
n-k
respectively, as
t\rightarrow+\infty
. One sees that along the way the asymptotics of
\Phi(u(t))
as
t\rightarrow\infty
naturally arises.
One can imagine that the
t\rightarrow+\infty
limit in
u(t)
corresponds to a branching of a rooted tree with
k
leaves on the left branch and
n-k
leaves on the right branch. Then to get the expression of Stokes matrices of (
12
) via the asymptotics of
\Phi(u)
at
u_{\rm asy}
, we just iterate the step according to the branching at each of the inner vertex of the rooted planar binary tree representing
u_{\rm asy}
. For example, if the asymptotic zone
u_{\rm asy}
is given by Figure 1, we only need to compute the monodromy data for the type of equation (
20
) with six different pairs
(n,k)
:

\left\{\begin{array}[]{lr}(n=6,k=2)\ \text{corresponding to the root vertex}\\
(n=2,k=1)\ \text{corresponding to the inner vertex with leaves $u_{1}$ and $u_{2}$}\\
(n=4,k=1)\ \text{corresponding to the inner vertex with leaves $u_{3}$, $u_{4}$, $u_{5},u_{6}$}\\
(n=3,k=2)\ \text{corresponding to the inner vertex with leaves $u_{4}$, $u_{5},u_{6}$}\\
(n=2,k=1)\ \text{corresponding to the inner vertex with leaves $u_{4}$, $u_{5}$}\end{array}\right.

As
k=n-1
or
k=1
, the equation (
20
) is solved by the confluent hypergeometric functions. Thanks to the known global analysis of the functions, the monodromy of (
20
) then has a closed formula. According to the analytic branching rule, a manipulation of the iteration of the computation for the equation (
20
) with
(n=m,k=m-1)
and
m=2,...,n
eventually gives Theorem
3.7
. See
[
25
]
for more details.

Although the equation (
20
) for
2\leq k\leq n-2
becomes harder to solve, its monodromy can be understood via the (classical analog of) representations of the Yangian of
\mathfrak{gl}_{k}
. See our paper
[
24
]
. As discussed above, iterating the computation can express the Stokes matrices
S_{\pm}(u,\Phi(u))
by the asymptotics of
\Phi(u)
at any asymptotic zone. It thus helps to answer the connection problem
(b)
of (
1
) between all asymptotic zones in an explicit term.

In the end, we would like to remark that the connection problem considered in this paper can be characterized by the Gelfand-Tsetlin theory in representation theory. And the explicit "algebraic characterization" of the analytic properties has much to do with the (quantization of) Hamiltonian description of the isomonodromy equation
[
5
,
23
]
.

Remark 3.11

3.10

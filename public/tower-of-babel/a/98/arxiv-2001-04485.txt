Comparing the orthogonal and unitary functor calculi
Niall Taggart

Abstract
. The orthogonal and unitary calculi give a method to study functors from the category of real or complex inner product spaces to the category of based topological spaces. We construct functors between the calculi from the complexification-realification adjunction between real and complex inner product spaces. These allow for movement between the versions of calculi, and comparisons between the Taylor towers produced by both calculi. We show that when the inputted orthogonal functor is weakly polynomial, the Taylor tower of the functor restricted through realification and the restricted Taylor tower of the functor agree up to weak equivalence. We further lift the homotopy level comparison of the towers to a commutative diagram of Quillen functors relating the model categories for orthogonal calculus and the model categories for unitary calculus.



1.
Introduction

The orthogonal and unitary calculi allow for the systematic study of functors from either the category of real inner product spaces, or the category of complex inner product spaces, to the category of based topological spaces. The motivating examples are
\mathrm{BO}(-)\colon V\longmapsto\mathrm{BO}(V)
, where
\mathrm{BO}(V)
is the classifying space of the orthogonal group of
V
, and
\mathrm{BU}(-)\colon W\longmapsto\mathrm{BU}(W)
where
\mathrm{BU}(W)
is the classifying space of the unitary group of
W
. The foundations of orthogonal calculus were originally developed by Weiss in
[
9
]
, and later converted to a model category theoretic framework by Barnes and Oman in
[
2
]
. From the unitary calculus perspective, it has long been known to the experts, with the foundations and model category framework developed by the author in
[
8
]
.

In this paper we use the similarity between real and complex vector spaces, namely the complexification-realification adjunction to give a formal comparison of the calculi both on the homotopy level by comparing the towers, and on the model category level by constructing Quillen functors between the model categories for orthogonal calculus and the model categories for unitary calculus. In particular, this paper will allow for a more methodical way of using the calculi together, and to transfer calculations between them.

We cover the basic background of the calculi in Section
2
. With this background in place, we begin with a comparison of the input functors in Section
3
. In particular we construct two Quillen adjunctions between the input categories with precomposition with realification and precomposition with complexification respectively being right Quillen functors.

Denote by
\mathcal{E}_{0}^{\mathbf{O}}
the category of input functors for orthogonal calculus, that is,

the unitary calculus analogue. For a full definition of these categories, see Definition

(1)

In Section

In

Notation and Conventions

The use of a superscript
\mathbf{O}
is to denote the orthogonal calculus, and a superscript
\mathbf{U}
is to denote the unitary calculus. When the superscript is omitted, we mean the statement applies to both orthogonal and unitary calculus.

We will refer to the category of based compactly generated weak Hausdorff spaces as the category of based spaces and denote this category by
\T
. This is a cofibrantly generated model category with weak equivalences the weak homotopy equivalences and fibrations the Serre fibrations. The set of generating cofibrations shall be denoted
I
, and the set of generating acyclic cofibrations, denoted
J
.

2.1.
Input Functors

Let
\mathcal{J}
be the category of finite-dimensional
\mathbb{F}
-inner product subspaces of
\mathbb{F}^{\infty}
, and
\mathbb{F}
-linear isometries. Denote by
\mathcal{J}_{0}

may be topologised as the Stiefel manifold of
\dim_{\mathbb{F}}(U)
-frames in

Definition 2.1
.

Define
\mathcal{E}_{0}
to be the category of
\T
-enriched functors from
\mathcal{J}_{0}
to
\T
.

The category
\mathcal{E}_{0}^{\mathbf{O}}
is category of input functors for orthogonal calculus as studied by Weiss and Barnes and Oman
[
9
,
2
]
. Moreover
\mathcal{E}_{0}^{\mathbf{U}}
is the category of input functors for unitary calculus, studied by the author in
[
8
]
. These input categories are categories of diagram spaces as in
[
7
]
hence they can be equipped with a projective model structure.

Proposition 2.2
.

There is a cellular, proper and topological model category structure on the category
\mathcal{E}_{0}
, with the weak equivalences and fibrations defined to be the levelwise weak homotopy equivalences and levelwise Serre fibrations respectively. The generating (acyclic) cofibrations are of the form
\mathcal{J}_{0}(U,-)\wedge i
where
i
is a generating (acyclic) cofibration in
\T
.

2.2.
Polynomial functors

Arguably the most important class of functors in orthogonal and unitary calculi are the

2.3.
Homogeneous functors

The
n
-th layer of the Taylor tower satisfies the property that it is both
n
-polynomial, and its
(n-1)
-st polynomial approximation vanishes,
[
8
, Example 2.10]
. The class of functors which satisfy this property are called
n
-homogeneous.

Definition 2.6
.

A functor
F\in\mathcal{E}_{0}
is said to be
n
–reduced
if

is trivial, and is said to be

or equivalently

-homogeneous
if it is both
n
-polynomial and

–reduced.

There is a further model structure on

which captures the homotopy theory of
n
-homogeneous functors. Denote by

T_{n}F\longrightarrow T_{n-1}F.

Proposition 2.7

(
[
2
, Proposition 6.9]
,
[
8
, Proposition 3.13]
)
.

There is a topological model structure on
\mathcal{E}_{0}
where the weak equivalences are those maps

is a weak equivalence in
\mathcal{E}_{0}
, the fibrations are the fibrations of the
n
-polynomial model structure and the cofibrations are those maps with the left lifting property with respect to the acyclic fibrations. The fibrant objects are
n
-polynomial and the cofibrant-fibrant objects are the projectively cofibrant

-homogeneous functors.

In
[
8
, §8]
, the author gave further characterisations of the

-homogeneous model structure. These will prove useful in our comparisons. The results hold true for the orthogonal calculus, with all but identical proofs.

Proposition 2.8

(
[
8
, Proposition 8.3]
)

A map

-polynomial model structure and an

-equivalence.

This allows us to characterise the acyclic fibrations between fibrant objects.

Corollary 2.9

(
[
8
, Corollary 8.4]
)

A map

between
n
-polynomial objects is an acyclic fibration in the

-homogeneous model structure if and only if it is a fibration in the

-polynomial model structure.

We now turn our attention to the cofibrations.

Lemma 2.10

(
[
8
, Lemma 8.5]
)

A map

(
[

)

The cofibrant objects of the

-reduced projectively cofibrant objects.

An important class of functors, introduced by the author in

Definition 2.20
.

A map

3.
Comparing the input functors

Let
V\in\mathcal{J}_{0}^{\mathbf{O}}
, then the complexification of
V
,
\mathbb{C}\otimes_{\mathbb{R}}V
, is a complex vector space such that

\dim_{\mathbb{C}}\mathbb{C}\otimes_{\mathbb{R}}V=\dim_{\mathbb{R}}V.

Given a
\mathbb{R}
-inner product
\langle-,-\rangle_{V}
on
V
, there is a well defined
\mathbb{C}
-inner product on
\mathbb{C}\otimes V
, given by

\langle(a+ib)\otimes v,(c+id)\otimes w\rangle=\langle av,cw\rangle_{V}+\langle bv,dw\rangle_{V}+i\langle bv,cw\rangle_{V}-i\langle av,dw\rangle_{V}

where
a+ib,c+id\in\mathbb{C}
, and
v,w\in V.

The complexification of an
\mathbb{R}
-linear map
T
is given by
T_{\mathbb{C}}=\mathbb{C}\otimes T
. Moreover in the finite dimensional case the matrices representing
T
and
\mathbb{C}\otimes T
are equal (corresponding to the inclusion
\mathrm{O}(n)\hookrightarrow\mathrm{U}(n)
) and we get characterisations of images and kernels,

\ker(\mathbb{C}\otimes_{\mathbb{R}}T)=\mathbb{C}\otimes_{\mathbb{R}}\ker(T)\ \text{and}\ \mathrm{im}(\mathbb{C}\otimes_{\mathbb{R}}T)=\mathbb{C}\otimes_{\mathbb{R}}\mathrm{im}(T).

Given a
\mathbb{R}
-linear isometry,
T\colon V\to W
,
\mathbb{C}\otimes T\colon\mathbb{C}\otimes V\to\mathbb{C}\otimes W,c\otimes v\longmapsto c\otimes T(v)
, is a
\mathbb{C}
-linear isometry, that respects the inner product. It follows that complexification gives a well defined functor
c\colon\mathcal{J}_{0}^{\mathbf{O}}\to\mathcal{J}_{0}^{\mathbf{U}}
.

The “opposite operation” to complexification is that of realification. Let
W
be a complex vector space, then its realification
W_{\mathbb{R}}
is the set
W
with vector addition and scalar multiplication by reals inherited unchanged from
W
and the complex multiplication “forgotten”. If
\{e_{1},\dots,e_{n}\}
is a basis for
W
then
\{e_{1},\dots,e_{n},ie_{1},\dots,ie_{n}\}
is a basis for
W_{\mathbb{R}}
. It follows that

\dim_{\mathbb{R}}W_{\mathbb{R}}=2\dim_{\mathbb{C}}W=\dim_{\mathbb{R}}W.

Up to isomorphism it suffices to check that there is a well defined inner product on the realification of
\mathbb{C}^{n}
induced by the Hermitian inner product on
\mathbb{C}^{n}
. Recall for vectors
\mathbf{c}=(c_{i}),\mathbf{c^{\prime}}=(c_{i}^{\prime})
in
\mathbb{C}^{n}
, the Hermitian inner product is given by

\langle\mathbf{c},\mathbf{c^{\prime}}\rangle_{\mathbb{C}}=\sum_{i=1}^{n}c_{i}\overline{c_{i}^{\prime}}.

To obtain a real inner product on
\mathbb{R}^{2n}=(\mathbb{C}^{n})_{\mathbb{R}}
, we realise the vectors
\mathbf{c}
and
\mathbf{c^{\prime}}
as
\mathbf{c}=\mathbf{a}+i\mathbf{b}
and
\mathbf{c^{\prime}}=\mathbf{a^{\prime}}+i\mathbf{b}
, where
\mathbf{a},\mathbf{a^{\prime}},\mathbf{b},\mathbf{b^{\prime}}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
. By taking the real part of
\langle\mathbf{c},\mathbf{c}^{\prime}\rangle_{\mathbb{C}}
, we then define a real inner product on
\mathbb{R}^{2n}
as

\langle(\mathbf{a},\mathbf{b}),(\mathbf{a^{\prime}},\mathbf{b^{\prime}})\rangle_{\mathbb{R}}=\Re\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}c_{i}\overline{c_{i}^{\prime}}\right)=\Re\left(\langle\mathbf{c},\mathbf{c^{\prime}}\rangle\right)

under the identification
c_{j}=a_{j}+ib_{j}
,
c_{j}^{\prime}=a_{j}^{\prime}+ib_{j}^{\prime}
and where
(\mathbf{a},\mathbf{b})
is notation for the vector

(a_{1},b_{1},a_{2},b_{2},\cdots,a_{n},b_{n})\in\mathbb{R}^{2n}.

If
T\colon\mathbb{C}^{k}\to\mathbb{C}^{m}
is a
\mathbb{C}
-linear map then we may view it as a
\mathbb{R}
-linear map
T_{\mathbb{R}}\colon(\mathbb{C}^{k})_{\mathbb{R}}\to(\mathbb{C}^{m})_{\mathbb{R}}
.
It follows that

\ker(T_{\mathbb{R}})=(\ker(T))_{\mathbb{R}}\ \text{and}\ \mathrm{im}(T_{\mathbb{R}})=(\mathrm{im}(T))_{\mathbb{R}}.

If
T\colon V\to W
is a
\mathbb{C}
-linear isometry, then
T_{\mathbb{R}}\colon V_{\mathbb{R}}\to W_{\mathbb{R}}
is a
\mathbb{R}
-linear isometry, and it follows that realification gives a well defined functor
r\colon\mathcal{J}_{0}^{\mathbf{U}}\to\mathcal{J}_{0}^{\mathbf{O}}
.

3.1.
Realification and complexification induce Quillen functors

For an orthogonal functor
F\in\mathcal{E}_{0}^{\mathbf{O}}
, precomposition with
r
, which we call “pre-realification” defines a unitary functor

r^{*}F\colon\mathcal{J}_{0}^{\mathbf{U}}\to\T.

Hence pre-realification defines a functor
r^{*}\colon\mathcal{E}_{0}^{\mathbf{O}}\to\mathcal{E}_{0}^{\mathbf{U}}
, which has a left adjoint
r_{!}
given by the formula,

(r_{!}E)(V)=\int^{W\in\mathcal{J}_{0}^{\mathbf{U}}}E(W)\wedge\mathcal{J}_{0}^{\mathbf{O}}(W_{\mathbb{R}},V),

i.e.
r_{!}
is the left Kan extension along
r
.

Similarly, complexification defines a functor
c^{*}\colon\mathcal{E}_{0}^{\mathbf{U}}\to\mathcal{E}_{0}^{\mathbf{O}}
, which has left adjoint
c_{!}
given by the left Kan extension along
c
. These functors are homotopically meaningful when one considers the projective model structures on the categories of input functors.

Lemma 3.1
.

The adjoint pair

\textstyle{r_{!}:\mathcal{E}_{0}^{\mathbf{U}}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\mathcal{E}_{0}^{\mathbf{O}}:r^{*}}

is a Quillen adjunction, when both categories are equipped with their projective model structures.

Proof.

Let
f\colon E\to F
be a levelwise fibration (resp. levelwise weak equivalence). Then by definition
r^{*}f\colon r^{*}E\to r^{*}F
is a levelwise fibration (resp. levelwise weak equivalence). Hence
r^{*}
preserves fibrations and acyclic fibrations.
∎

Lemma 3.2
.

The adjoint pair

\textstyle{c_{!}:\mathcal{E}_{0}^{\mathbf{O}}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\mathcal{E}_{0}^{\mathbf{U}}:c^{*}}

is a Quillen adjunction, when both categories are equipped with their projective model structures.

Proof.

The proof is all but identical to that of Lemma
3.1
.
∎

4.
Comparing the polynomial and homogeneous functors

4.1.
Homogeneous functors

Heuristically, the homogeneous functors are the building blocks of the Taylor towers of the calculi. As such we start with a direct comparison between these functors. This comparison is reliant on the classifications of homogeneous functors from orthogonal and unitary calculi.

Lemma 4.1
.

If an orthogonal functor
F
is
n
-homogeneous, then
r^{*}F
is
n
-homogeneous.

Proof.

Let
F
be an
n
-homogeneous orthogonal functor. Then by the characterisation, Proposition
2.19
,
F
is levelwise weakly equivalent to the functor

V\longmapsto\Omega^{\infty}[(S^{\mathbb{R}^{n}\otimes_{\mathbb{R}}V}\wedge\Psi_{F}^{n})_{h\mathrm{O}(n)}]

where
\Psi_{F}^{n}
is an orthogonal spectrum with an
O(n)
-action. It follows that pre-realification of
F
is levelwise weakly equivalent to the functor

W\longmapsto\Omega^{\infty}[(S^{\mathbb{R}^{n}\otimes_{\mathbb{R}}W_{\mathbb{R}}}\wedge\Psi^{n}_{F})_{h\mathrm{O}(n)}].

Using the derived change of group functor, we construct an orthogonal spectrum with an action of
\mathrm{U}(n)
,

\mathrm{U}(n)_{+}\wedge_{\mathrm{O}(n)}^{\mathds{L}}\Psi_{F}^{n}:=\mathrm{U}(n)_{+}\wedge_{\mathrm{O}(n)}(E\mathrm{O}(n)_{+}\wedge\Psi_{F}^{n}).

By the classification of
n
-homogeneous unitary functors, Proposition
2.19
, there is an
n
-homogeneous functor
F^{\prime}
associated to the above spectrum, given by

W\longmapsto\Omega^{\infty}[(S^{\mathbb{C}^{n}\otimes_{\mathbb{C}}W}\wedge(\mathrm{U}(n)_{+}\wedge_{\mathrm{O}(n)}(E\mathrm{O}(n)_{+}\wedge\Psi_{F}^{n})))_{h\mathrm{U}(n)}].

By
[
6
, Proposition V.2.3]
,
F^{\prime}(W)
is isomorphic to

\Omega^{\infty}[\mathrm{U}(n)_{+}\wedge_{\mathrm{O}(n)}((\iota^{*}S^{\mathbb{C}^{n}\otimes_{\mathbb{C}}W}\wedge(E\mathrm{O}(n)_{+}\wedge\Psi_{F}^{n})))_{h(\mathrm{U}(n)}].

The
\mathrm{U}(n)
-action on
\mathrm{U}(n)_{+}\wedge_{\mathrm{O}(n)}((\iota^{*}S^{\mathbb{C}^{n}\otimes_{\mathbb{C}}W}\wedge(E\mathrm{O}(n)_{+}\wedge\Psi_{F}^{n})))
is free (
(E\mathrm{O}(n)_{+}
is a free
\mathrm{O}(n)
-space), hence taking homotopy orbits equates to taking strict orbits. Hence there is an isomorphism

F^{\prime}(W)\cong\Omega^{\infty}[(\mathrm{U}(n)_{+}\wedge_{\mathrm{O}(n)}((\iota^{*}S^{\mathbb{C}^{n}\otimes_{\mathbb{C}}W}\wedge(E\mathrm{O}(n)_{+}\wedge\Psi_{F}^{n}))))/\mathrm{U}(n)].

The strict
\mathrm{U}(n)
-orbits of the spectrum
\mathrm{U}(n)_{+}\wedge_{\mathrm{O}(n)}((\iota^{*}S^{\mathbb{C}^{n}\otimes_{\mathbb{C}}W}\wedge(E\mathrm{O}(n)_{+}\wedge\Psi_{F}^{n}))
are isomorphic to the
\mathrm{O}(n)
-orbits of the spectrum,
\iota^{*}S^{\mathbb{C}^{n}\otimes_{\mathbb{C}}W}\wedge(E\mathrm{O}(n)_{+}\wedge\Psi_{F}^{n}))
, hence
F^{\prime}(W)
is isomorphic to

\Omega^{\infty}[(\iota^{*}S^{\mathbb{C}^{n}\otimes_{\mathbb{C}}W}\wedge(E\mathrm{O}(n)_{+}\wedge\Psi_{F}^{n})))/\mathrm{O}(n)].

This last is precisely

\Omega^{\infty}[(\iota^{*}S^{\mathbb{C}^{n}\otimes_{\mathbb{C}}W}\wedge\Psi_{F}^{n})_{h\mathrm{O}(n)}]

as homotopy orbits is the left derived functor of strict orbits and smashing with
E\mathrm{O}(n)_{+}
is a cofibrant replacement in the projective model structure.

Since the action of
\mathrm{O}(n)
on
\iota^{*}S^{\mathbb{C}^{n}\otimes_{\mathbb{C}}W}
is equivalent to the
\mathrm{O}(n)
action on
S^{\mathbb{R}^{n}\otimes_{\mathbb{R}}W_{\mathbb{R}}}
and the one-point compactification are isomorphic, the above infinite loop space is isomorphic to

\Omega^{\infty}[(S^{\mathbb{R}^{n}\otimes_{\mathbb{R}}W_{\mathbb{R}}}\wedge\Psi_{F}^{n})_{h\mathrm{O}(n)}].

By the characterisation of
n
-homogeneous orthogonal functors, we see that this is levelwise weakly equivalent to
F(W_{\mathbb{R}})=(r^{*}F)(W).

∎

Lemma 4.2
.

If a unitary functor
E
is
n
-homogeneous, then
c^{*}E
is
(2n)
-homogeneous.

Proof.

Since
E
is
n
-homogeneous,

E(W)\simeq\Omega^{\infty}[(S^{\mathbb{C}^{n}\otimes W}\wedge\Psi_{E}^{n})_{h\mathrm{U}(n)}].

By definition

(c^{*}E)(V)=E(\mathbb{C}\otimes_{\mathbb{R}}V)\simeq\Omega^{\infty}[(S^{\mathbb{C}^{n}\otimes_{\mathbb{C}}\mathbb{C}\otimes_{\mathbb{R}}V}\wedge\Psi_{E}^{n})_{hU(n})].

Observe that

\mathrm{O}(2n)_{+}\wedge^{\mathds{L}}_{\mathrm{U}(n)}\Psi_{E}^{n}

is an orthogonal spectrum with
\mathrm{O}(2n)
-action. The classification of homogeneous functors in orthogonal calculus, Proposition
2.19
gives a
(2n)
-homogeneous functor,

V\longmapsto\Omega^{\infty}[(S^{\mathbb{R}^{2n}\otimes V}\wedge(\mathrm{O}(2n)\wedge^{\mathds{L}}_{\mathrm{U}(n)}\Psi_{E}^{n}))_{h\mathrm{O}(2n)}].

A similar argument to Lemma
4.1
yields the result.
∎

4.2.
Polynomial functors

Using the above results on pre-realification and pre-complexification of homogeneous functors, we can compare polynomial functors. We must add the technical assumption that
F
is reduced, i.e., that
F(\mathbb{R}^{\infty})
is weakly contractible. Many of the functors which one wishes to consider in the calculi are reduced, and in the situations where they are not, we can take their reduced part to be the homotopy fibre of the map
F\to T_{0}F
, and work relative to the
0
–polynomial approximation.

Theorem 4.3
.

If an orthogonal functor
F
is
n
-polynomial and
F(\mathbb{R}^{\infty})
is weakly contractible, then
r^{*}F
is an
n
-polynomial unitary functor, that is, the map

r^{*}T_{n}^{\mathbf{O}}F\longrightarrow T_{n}^{\mathbf{U}}(r^{*}T_{n}^{\mathbf{O}}F)

is a levelwise weak equivalence for every
F\in\mathcal{E}_{0}^{\mathbf{O}}
.

Proof.

We argue by induction on the polynomial degree. The case
n=0
follows by definition. Assume the map
r^{*}T_{n-1}^{\mathbf{O}}F\to T_{n-1}^{\mathbf{U}}(r^{*}T_{n-1}^{\mathbf{O}}F)
is a levelwise weak equivalence. There is a homotopy fibre sequence

T_{n}^{\mathbf{O}}F\longrightarrow T_{n-1}^{\mathbf{O}}F\longrightarrow R_{n}^{\mathbf{O}}F

where
R_{n}^{\mathbf{O}}F
is
n
-homogeneous, since
F
satisfies the conditions of
[
9
, Corollary 8.3]
. Lemma
4.1
implies that
r^{*}R_{n}^{\mathbf{O}}F
is
n
-homogeneous in
\mathcal{E}_{0}^{\mathbf{U}}
, and in particular
n
-polynomial. As homotopy fibres of maps between
n
-polynomial objects are
n
-polynomial, the homotopy fibre of the map
r^{*}T_{n-1}^{\mathbf{O}}F\to r^{*}R_{n}^{\mathbf{O}}F
is
n
-polynomial. Computation of homotopy fibres is levelwise, hence the homotopy fibre in question is
r^{*}T_{n}^{\mathbf{O}}F
, and it follows that

r^{*}T_{n}^{\mathbf{O}}F\longrightarrow T_{n}^{\mathbf{U}}(r^{*}T_{n}^{\mathbf{O}}F)

is a levelwise weak equivalence.
∎

Theorem 4.4
.

If an unitary functor
E
is
n
-polynomial and
E(\mathbb{C}^{\infty})
is weakly contractible, then
c^{*}F
is
(2n)
-polynomial, that is, the map

c^{*}T_{n}^{\mathbf{U}}E\longrightarrow T_{2n}^{\mathbf{O}}(c^{*}T_{n}^{\mathbf{U}}E)

is a levelwise weak equivalence for all
E\in\mathcal{E}_{0}^{\mathbf{U}}
.

Proof.

The argument follows as in Theorem
4.3
using Lemma
4.2
in place of Lemma
4.1
.
∎

4.3.
Polynomial model structures

We turn our attention to a model structure comparison. Since Theorem
4.3
only applies to reduced functors (those
F
such that
F(\mathbb{R}^{\infty})
is trivial) we have to restrict our attention to reduced functors. We do this by defining the reduced part of
F
, denoted
\mathrm{red}(F)
to be the homotopy fibre of the map
F\to T_{0}F
. Then
\mathrm{red}(F)
is reduced, and defines a functor
\mathrm{red}(-):\mathcal{E}_{0}\to\mathcal{E}_{0}
. The composite

\mathcal{E}_{0}^{\mathbf{O}}\xrightarrow{\mathrm{red}(-)}\mathcal{E}_{0}^{\mathbf{O}}\xrightarrow{\hskip 8.19447ptr^{*}\hskip 8.19447pt}\mathcal{E}_{0}^{\mathbf{U}},

is a right Quillen functor, hence induces a functor on the homotopy categories, although it does not have a left adjoint. In Section
9
we restrict our attention to the homotopy categories, and do not require the existence of a left adjoint to
r^{*}\mathrm{red}(-)
.

Lemma 4.5
.

The composite

n\poly\mathcal{E}_{0}^{\mathbf{O}}\xrightarrow{\mathrm{red}(-)}n\poly\mathcal{E}_{0}^{\mathbf{O}}\xrightarrow{\hskip 8.19447ptr^{*}\hskip 8.19447pt}n\poly\mathcal{E}_{0}^{\mathbf{U}}

preserves acyclic fibrations, and fibrations. In particular, there is an induced functor on homotopy categories.

Proof.

The acyclic fibrations in the
n
–polynomial model structure are levelwise, hence both terms in the composite, and resultingly their composite, preserves these. It suffices by
[
3
, Corollary A.2]
to show that the composite preserves fibrations between fibrant objects, which are the levelwise fibrations by
[
5
, Proposition 3.3.16]
. It hence suffices to show that
F
preserves fibrant objects. Let
F
be a
n
–polynomial orthogonal functor, then
\mathrm{red}(F)
is also
n
–polynomial, and reduced. An application of Theorem
4.3
implies the result.
∎

Lemma 4.6
.

The composite

n\poly\mathcal{E}_{0}^{\mathbf{U}}\xrightarrow{\mathrm{red}(-)}n\poly\mathcal{E}_{0}^{\mathbf{U}}\xrightarrow{\hskip 8.19447ptc^{*}\hskip 8.19447pt}(2n)\poly\mathcal{E}_{0}^{\mathbf{O}}

preserves acyclic fibrations and fibrations. In particular, there is an induced functor on homotopy categories.

Proof.

This follows similarly to Lemma
4.5
, using Theorem
4.4
in place of Theorem
4.3
.
∎

4.4.
Homogeneous model structures

The homogeneous model structures are right Bousfield localisations of the
n
-polynomial model structures. This fact, together with the further characterisations of the homogeneous model structure provided by the author in
[
8
]
, allow for the construction of Quillen functors between the orthogonal
n
-homogeneous model structure and the unitary
n
-homogeneous model structure.

Proposition 4.7
.

The composite

n\homog\mathcal{E}_{0}^{\mathbf{O}}\xrightarrow{\mathrm{red}(-)}n\homog\mathcal{E}_{0}^{\mathbf{O}}\xrightarrow{\hskip 8.19447ptr^{*}\hskip 8.19447pt}n\homog\mathcal{E}_{0}^{\mathbf{U}}

preserves acyclic fibrations and fibrations. In particular, there is an induced functor on homotopy categories.

Proof.

First suppose that
f\colon E\to F
is a fibration in
n\homog\mathcal{E}_{0}^{\mathbf{O}}
. It follows that
f
is a fibration in the
n
-polynomial model structure, and hence
r^{*}\mathrm{red}(f)
is a fibration in
n\poly\mathcal{E}_{0}^{\mathbf{U}}
by Lemma
4.5
, and hence
n\homog\mathcal{E}_{0}^{\mathbf{U}}
.

Suppose further that
f
in an acyclic fibration in
n\homog\mathcal{E}_{0}^{\mathbf{O}}
. By Proposition
2.8
, it follows that
f
is an
(n-1)
-polynomial fibration and an
D_{n}^{\mathbf{O}}
-equivalence. As above it follows that
r^{*}\mathrm{red}(f)
is a fibration in
(n-1)\poly\mathcal{E}_{0}^{\mathbf{U}}
. In particular the homotopy fibre of
r^{*}\mathrm{red}(f)
is
(n-1)
-polynomial. Such objects are trivial in
n\homog\mathcal{E}_{0}^{\mathbf{U}}
hence, since
n\homog\mathcal{E}_{0}^{\mathbf{U}}
is stable,
r^{*}\mathrm{red}(f)
is a weak equivalence in
n\homog\mathcal{E}_{0}^{\mathbf{U}}
.
∎

Proposition 4.8
.

The composite

n\homog\mathcal{E}_{0}^{\mathbf{U}}\xrightarrow{\mathrm{red}(-)}n\homog\mathcal{E}_{0}^{\mathbf{U}}\xrightarrow{\hskip 8.19447ptc^{*}\hskip 8.19447pt}(2n)\homog\mathcal{E}_{0}^{\mathbf{O}}

preserves acyclic fibrations and fibrations. In particular, there is an induced functor on homotopy categories.

Proof.

The proof follows almost verbatim from Proposition
4.7
.
∎

Remark 4.9
.

Without a clearer understanding on how the pre-realification and pre-complexification functors behave with respect to the polynomial approximations, it is not possible to say that they preserve all
n
-homogeneous equivalences. In particular, if
f\colon X\to Y
is an
n
-homogeneous equivalence then

is levelwise weakly equivalent to
D_{n}Y
, and there is a diagram of homotopy fibre sequences the form

\textstyle{D_{n}X\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}

5.
Comparing weakly polynomial functors

5.1.
Agreement

The notion of agreement plays a central role in the theory or orthogonal and unitary calculus, for example it is crucial to the proof that the
n
-th polynomial approximation in
n
-polynomial, see
[
10
]
. The pre-realification and pre-complexification functors behave well with respect to functors which agree to a certain order.

Lemma 5.1
.

If a map
p\colon F\to G
in
\mathcal{E}_{0}^{\mathbf{O}}
is an order
n
orthogonal agreement, then
r^{*}p\colon r^{*}F\to r^{*}G
in
\mathcal{E}_{0}^{\mathbf{U}}
is an order
n
unitary agreement.

Proof.

Since
p
is an order
n
orthogonal agreement, there is an integer
b\in\mathbb{Z}
, such that
p_{V}\colon F(V)\to G(V)
is
(-b+(n+1)\dim_{\mathbb{R}}V)
-connected. It follows by definition that
(r^{*}p)_{W}=p_{W_{\mathbb{R}}}\colon F(W_{\mathbb{R}})\to G(W_{\mathbb{R}})
is
(-b+(n+1)\dim_{\mathbb{R}}W_{\mathbb{R}})
-connected. Since
\dim_{\mathbb{R}}W_{\mathbb{R}}=\dim_{\mathbb{R}}W
, it follows that
(r^{*}p)_{W}
is
(-b+(n+1)\dim_{\mathbb{R}}W)
-connected, and hence
r^{*}p
is an order
n
unitary agreement.
∎

Lemma 5.2
.

If a map
p\colon F\to G
in
\mathcal{E}_{0}^{\mathbf{U}}
is an order
n
unitary agreement, then
c^{*}p\colon c^{*}F\to c^{*}G
in
\mathcal{E}_{0}^{\mathbf{O}}
is an order
2n
unitary agreement.

Proof.

Since
p
is an order
n
unitary agreement, there is an integer
b
, such that
p_{V}\colon F(V)\to G(V)
is
(-b+(n+1)\dim_{\mathbb{R}}V)
-connected. It follows by definition that
(c^{*}p)_{W}=p_{\mathbb{C}\otimes W}\colon F(\mathbb{C}\otimes W)\to G(\mathbb{C}\otimes W)
is
(-b+(n+1)\dim_{\mathbb{R}}(\mathbb{C}\otimes W))
-connected. Since
\dim_{\mathbb{R}}\mathbb{C}\otimes W=2\dim_{\mathbb{C}}\mathbb{C}\otimes W=2\dim_{\mathbb{R}}W
, it follows that
(c^{*}p)_{W}
is
(-b+2(n+1)\dim_{\mathbb{R}}W)
-connected, and hence in particular
(-b+(2n+1)\dim_{\mathbb{R}}W)

is an order
2n
unitary agreement.
∎

Remark 5.3
.

In
[
1
]
Barnes and Eldred give a tower level comparison between Goodwillie calculus and orthogonal calculus. This relies on the functor
F
from Goodwillie calculus being stably
n
-excisive, that is, the functor must behave well with respect to (co)Cartesian cubes, see
[
4
, Definition 4.1]
for the precise definition. The key property gained by a stably
n
-excisive functor is that the polynomial approximation map

in Goodwillie calculus is an agreement of order
n
in the Goodwillie calculus setting. This allows for a clear comparison between the
n
-polynomial approximation functors of Goodwillie and orthogonal calculi. In general, the map

is not an agreement of order
n
in either orthogonal or unitary calculus.

The

for unitary spectra, with the difference coming from the fact that the commutative monoid

for unitary spectra only takes values on even dimensional spheres.

On objects, let

be given by forgetting the complex structure, i.e.,
\mathbb{C}^{k}\longmapsto\mathbb{R}^{2k}

r(\gamma_{n}(f,x))=r((\mathbb{C}^{n}\otimes f,x)=(\mathbb{R}^{n}\otimes f_{\mathbb{R}},rx)=\beta_{n}((f_{\mathbb{R}},rx))=\beta_{n}(r(f,x)).

It follows that
r\gamma_{n}=\beta_{n}r
. Since the right adjoints in the required diagram are defined in terms of precomposition the result follows. Note that the group actions are also compatible since the unitary
\gamma_{n}
has been restricted to
\mathrm{O}(n)
actions.
∎

8.3.
Change of model through complexification

Define a complexification functor
c\colon\mathcal{J}_{2n}^{\mathbf{O}}\to\mathcal{J}_{n}^{\mathbf{U}}
, given on objects by
cV=\mathbb{C}\otimes V
, and on morphisms by sending
(f,x)\in\mathcal{J}_{2n}^{\mathbf{O}}(V,W)
to
(\mathbb{C}\otimes f,cx)\in\mathcal{J}_{n}^{\mathbf{U}}(cV,cW)
, where
cx
is the image of
x
under the composition of isomorphisms,

\mathbb{R}^{2n}\otimes_{\mathbb{R}}\coker(f)\xrightarrow[\cong]{\varphi_{1}}\mathbb{C}^{n}\otimes_{\mathbb{R}}\coker(f)\xrightarrow[\cong]{\varphi_{2}}\mathbb{C}^{n}\otimes_{\mathbb{C}}\mathbb{C}\otimes_{\mathbb{R}}\coker(f)\xrightarrow[\cong]{\varphi_{3}}\mathbb{C}^{n}\otimes_{\mathbb{C}}\coker(\mathbb{C}\otimes f).

where

\begin{split}\varphi_{1}(r_{1},\cdots,r_{2n},f(v))&=(r_{1}+ir_{2},\cdots r_{2n-1}+ir_{2n},f(v));\\
\varphi_{2}(c_{1},\cdots,c_{n},f(v))&=(c_{1},\cdots,c_{n},1,f(v));\ \ \text{and}\\
\varphi_{3}(c_{1},\cdots,c_{n},c,f(v))&=(c_{1},\cdots,c_{n},(\mathbb{C}\otimes f)(c\otimes v)).\\
\end{split}

Restricting from
\mathrm{O}(2n)
to
\mathrm{U}(n)
through the subgroup inclusion
\kappa\colon\mathrm{U}(n)\hookrightarrow\mathrm{O}(2n)
gives a functor

c\colon\kappa^{*}\mathcal{J}_{2n}^{\mathbf{O}}\to\mathcal{J}_{n}^{\mathbf{U}},

and precomposition defines a functor

c^{*}\colon\mathrm{U}(n)\mathcal{E}_{n}^{\mathbf{U}}\to\mathrm{U}(n)\mathcal{E}_{2n}^{\mathbf{O}}.

This functor is well defined as for
X\in\mathrm{U}(n)\mathcal{E}_{n}^{\mathbf{U}}
the map

c^{*}X\colon\kappa^{*}\mathcal{J}_{2n}^{\mathbf{O}}(V,W)\to\T((c^{*}X)(V),(c^{*}X)(W))=\T(X(\mathbb{C}\otimes V),X(\mathbb{C}\otimes W))

is
\mathrm{U}(n)
-equivariant. Indeed, for
\sigma\in\mathrm{U}(n)
,

\begin{split}(c^{*}X)(\sigma(f,x))=(c^{*}X)(f,\sigma x)&=X(\mathbb{C}\otimes f,c(\kappa^{*}(\sigma)x))=X(\mathbb{C}\otimes f,\sigma(cx))\\
&=X(\mathbb{C}\otimes f,\sigma x)=\sigma((c^{*}X)(f,x)).\end{split}

The structure maps of
c^{*}X
are induced by those of
X
, i.e.,

S^{2n}\wedge(c^{*}X)(V)\xrightarrow{=}S^{2n}\wedge X(\mathbb{C}\otimes V)\xrightarrow{\sigma}X((\mathbb{C}\otimes V)\oplus\mathbb{C})\xrightarrow{=}(c^{*}X)(V\oplus\mathbb{R})

where
\sigma\colon S^{2n}\wedge X(W)\to(W\oplus\mathbb{C})
is the structure map of
X\in\mathrm{U}(n)\mathcal{E}_{2n}^{\mathbf{O}}
.

The complexification functor
c^{*}
has a left adjoint,
c_{!}
given by the left Kan extension along
c
. We obtain a similar result to the case of realification, Lemma
8.8
.

Lemma 8.10
.

The functor
c^{*}\colon\mathrm{U}(n)\mathcal{E}_{n}^{\mathbf{U}}\to\mathrm{U}(n)\mathcal{E}_{2n}^{\mathbf{O}}
is a right Quillen functor.

9.
A homotopy category level comparison

We have shown previously that all but the bottom pentagons of Figure
1
commute. Moreover, since all of the commutation results for the sub-diagrams - excluding the lower pentagons - involve composing left (resp. right) Quillen functors with left (resp. right) Quillen functors those sub-diagrams commute on the homotopy category level. Hence, the only sections of Figure

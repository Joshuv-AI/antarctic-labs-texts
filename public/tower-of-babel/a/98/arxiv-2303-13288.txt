Abstract
. We describe Lorentzian manifolds that admit metric connections with parallel torsion having zero twistorial component and non-zero vectorial component. We also describe Lorentzian manifolds admitting metric connections with closed parallel skew-symmetric torsion. Keywords : Lorentzian manifold; parallel torsion; twistor-free torsion; skew-symmetric torsion; holonomy. AMS Mathematics Subject Classification 2020: 53B30; 53C29.



1.
Introduction

Metric connection with torsion appear both in the context of differential geometry and mathematical physics. Under the action of the pseudo-orthogonal group, the torsion of a metric connection on a pseudo-Riemannian manifold decomposes into three components: vectorial, twistorial, and skew-symmetric. Riemannian connections with skew-symmetric torsion have been studied in many works, e.g.,
[
1
,
2
,
9
]
.
Connections with skew-symmetric torsion appear in certain supergravity theories, see, e.g.,
[
12
,
13
,
18
,
21
]
. We studied Lorentzian metric connections with parallel skew-symmetric torsion in

[
11
]
.

Recently Moroianu and Pilca
[
20
]
classified complete simply connected Riemannian manifolds that admit metric connections with parallel torsion having zero twistorial component and non-zero vectorial component. It is shown that each such manifold is a warped product of the line and a complete Riemannian manifold admitting a parallel 3-form
\tau
satisfying the condition
\tau(X)\cdot\tau=0
for all vector fields
X
, see Theorem
1
below.
Then complete Riemannian manifolds carrying such 3-forms were classified, see Theorem
2
below.

In the present paper we consider the corresponding problem in the Lorentzian signature
. The first result (Theorem
3
) states that if a Lorentzian manifold
(M,g)
admits a metric connection with parallel torsion having zero twistorial component and non-zero vectorial component, then
(M,g)
cannot be geodesically complete, and even more it cannot satisfy no weaker condition of geodesic completeness: space-like, time-like, or light-like geodesic completeness.
We distinguish two cases depending on the vector field
\xi
defining the vectorial component of the torsion:
\xi
is isotropic and
\xi
is not isotropic. If
\xi
is non-isotropic and complete, then Theorem
4
provides a result similar to Theorem
1
. Next, Theorem
5
describes Lorentzian manifolds admitting parallel 3-forms
\tau
satisfying the above condition.
In fact, Theorem
5
describes Lorentzian manifolds admitting metric connections
\nabla
with closed
\nabla
-parallel skew-symmetric torsion
.
Next, we assume that the vector field
\xi
is isotropic. If the norm of the skew-symmetric component
S
of the torsion is zero, then the manifold under consideration may be described as a manifold
(M_{0},g_{0})
admitting a parallel isotropic vector field and a parallel 2-form on the corresponding screen bundle (Theorem
6
).
Suppose that the norm of
S
is non-zero. If the dimension of the manifold is 3, then we prove in Theorem
7

that the manifold is a Kundt space of a special form. If the dimension is greater than 3, then the manifold under consideration is a locally warped product of the manifold
(M_{0},g_{0})
and a Riemannian manifold again admitting a 3-form with the above properties.

A large class of metric connections with torsion appear as homogeneous structures.
A homogeneous structure on a pseudo-Riemannian manifold is a metric connection
\nabla
with
\nabla
-parallel curvature and torsion. By the Ambrose-Singer Theorem, a complete pseudo-Riemannian manifold is reductive homogeneous if and only if it admits a homogeneous structure
[
8
,
22
]
. Let us mention several results about homogeneous structures related to the results of the present paper.
In
[
14
]
it is shown that Lorentzian space forms do not admit homogeneous structures of vectorial type. Next, a homogeneous Lorentzian space admitting a homogeneous structure of isotropic vectorial type is a singular homogeneous plane wave
[
19
,
5
]
. The paper
[
17
]
provides a classification of Lorentzian homogeneous structures with the
torsion having zero twistorial component and non-zero vectorial component.
These structures are exhausted by locally symmetric spaces and singular homogeneous plane waves. We thus generalize these results to general connections with parallel twistor-free torsion having non-zero vectorial component.

2.
Preliminaries

Let
(M,g)
be a pseudo-Riemannian manifold. Denote by
\nabla^{g}
the Levi-Civita connection on
(M,g)
. A connection
\nabla
on
(M,g)
is called metric if
\nabla g=0
. Using the metric
g
we identify the tangent and cotangent bundles, and we use the obvious identifications for tensors. In particular,
we identify a field of bivectors
X\wedge Y
with the field of endomorphism

(X\wedge Y)Z=g(X,Z)Y-g(Y,Z)X,

and for a 3-tensor
B
by abuse of notation we will write

B(X,Y,Z)=g(B(X,Y),Z)=g(B(X)Y,Z).

Let
A\in\Gamma(TM)\otimes(\wedge^{2}\Gamma(TM))
. Then the equality

\nabla_{X}Y=\nabla^{g}_{X}Y+A(X,Y),\quad X,Y\in\Gamma(TM),

defines the metric connection
\nabla
with the torsion

T(X,Y,Z)=A(X,Y,Z)-A(Y,X,Z),

see, e.g,
[
2
]
. Let
\xi\in\Gamma(TM)
and
S\in\Omega^{3}(M)
, then the tensor

A(X)=X\wedge\xi+\frac{1}{2}S(X)

defines the metric connection

(1)

\nabla_{X}=\nabla_{X}^{g}+X\wedge\xi+\frac{1}{2}S(X),

with the torsion

T(X,Y)=(X\wedge Y)\xi+S(X,Y).

The torsion
T
has vectorial component
\xi
, skew-symmetric component
S
and zero twistorial component. In particular, a 3-form
\tau
defines the metric connection

(2)

\nabla_{X}=\nabla_{X}^{g}+\frac{1}{2}\tau(X)

with skew-symmetric torsion
\tau
.

Let
\nabla
be given by (
1
). Suppose that
\nabla T=0
. It is clear that this is equivalent to the conditions
\nabla\xi=0
and
\nabla S=0
.
Consider the first Bianchi identity

\underset{XYZ}{\mathfrak{S}}R(X,Y)Z=\underset{XYZ}{\mathfrak{S}}\{T(T(X,Y),Z)+(\nabla_{X}T)(Y,Z)\},

where
\underset{XYZ}{\mathfrak{S}}
denotes the cyclic sum with respect to
X,Y,Z
. It is easy to check that the identity may be rewritten in the form

(3)

\underset{XYZ}{\mathfrak{S}}R(X,Y)Z=\underset{XYZ}{\mathfrak{S}}S(S(X,Y),Z)+\underset{XYZ}{\mathfrak{S}}g(X,\xi)S(Y,Z)+\underset{XYZ}{\mathfrak{S}}S(\xi,Y,Z)X.

For a 3-form
\tau
define the 4-form
\sigma_{\tau}
by the equality

\sigma_{\tau}(X,Y,Z)=\underset{XYZ}{\mathfrak{S}}\tau(\tau(X,Y),Z).

It holds that

(4)

(\tau(X)\cdot\tau)(Y,Z,V)=-\sigma_{\tau}(X,Y,Z,V).

Consider the connection (
2
). It holds

(5)

d\tau(X,Y,Z,V)=\underset{XYZ}{\mathfrak{S}}\{(\nabla_{X}\tau)(Y,Z,V)\}-(\nabla_{V}\tau)(X,Y,Z)+2\sigma_{\tau}(X,Y,Z,V),

see, e.g.,
[
13
]
.
From (
2
) it follows that

(6)

\nabla\tau=\nabla^{g}\tau-\frac{1}{2}\sigma_{\tau}.

If
\nabla\tau=0
, then
the first Bianchi identity for the connection
\nabla
may be written in the form

(7)

\underset{XYZ}{\mathfrak{S}}R(X,Y)Z=\sigma_{\tau}(X,Y,Z).

Note that by (
4
), the condition
\sigma_{\tau}=0
is equivalent to the condition
\tau(X)\cdot\tau=0
for all
X\in\Gamma(TM)
.
From (
5
) and (
6
) easily follows

Lemma 1
.

Let
(M,g)
be a pseudo-Riemannian manifold and
\nabla
a metric connection with skew-symmetric torsion
\tau
on it. Then the following conditions are equivalent

1.

\nabla\tau=0
and
d\tau=0
;

2.

\nabla\tau=0
and
\sigma_{\tau}=0
;

3.

\nabla^{g}\tau=0
and
\sigma_{\tau}=0
.

The following theorem from
[
20
]
describes complete simply connected Riemannian manifolds with parallel twistor-free torsion.

Theorem 1
.

A complete simply connected Riemannian manifold
(M,g_{M})
carries a metric connection with parallel twistor-free torsion if and only if
(M,g_{M})
is homothetic to a warped product
(\mathbb{R}\times N,(dt)^{2}+e^{2t}g_{N})
, where
(N,g_{N})
is a complete simply connected Riemannian manifold carrying a parallel 3-form
\tau\in\Omega^{3}(N)
such that
\sigma_{\tau}=0
.

In
[
3
]
it is shown that an irreducible complete simply connected Riemannian manifold of dimension greater than
4
with parallel skew torsion
\tau
satisfying
\sigma_{\tau}=0
is a simple compact Lie group with bi-invariant metric or its dual noncompact symmetric space.
The following is a more general theorem from
[
20
]
.

Theorem 2
.

Let
(N,g)
be a complete simply connected Riemannian manifold carrying a metric connection with parallel skew-symmetric torsion
\tau
which satisfies
\sigma_{\tau}=0
. Then
(N,g)
is one of the following:

1.

(N,g)
is an oriented 3-dimensional Riemannian manifold and
\tau
is a constant multiple of its Riemannian volume form;

2.

(N,g)
is a simple Lie algebra with an
\operatorname{ad}
-invariant metric
g
and
\tau
is a constant multiple of its canonical 3-form;

3.

(N,g)
is an irreducible symmetric space of type II or type IV and
\tau
is a constant multiple of its canonical 3-form;

4.

(N,g)
is a Riemannian manifold and
\tau=0
;

5.

(N,g)
is a product of some of the manifolds as in 1–4.

Now we recall the definition of Kundt and Walker spaces.

A Kundt space is a Lorentzian manifold
(M,g)
admitting an isotropic vector field
p
satisfying the following conditions:

(8)

\nabla^{g}_{p}p=0,\quad\operatorname{tr}\nabla^{g}p=0,\quad||(\nabla^{g}p)^{sym}||_{g}=0,\quad||(\nabla^{g}p)^{alt}||_{g}=0,

i.e., the vector field
p
is geodesic, expansion-free, shear-free and twist-free,
see, e.g.,
[
10
]
. Locally there exist coordinates
v,x^{1},\dots x^{n},u
such that
g
takes the form

(9)

g=2dvdu+h+2Adu+H(du)^{2},

where
h=\sum_{i,j=1}^{n}h_{ij}(x^{1},\dots,x^{n},u)dx^{i}dx^{j}
is a
u
-family of local Riemannian metrics,

A=\sum_{i=1}^{n}A_{i}(v,x^{1},\dots,x^{n},u)dx^{i}
is a 1-form, and
H=H(v,x^{1},\dots,x^{n},u)
is a local function. The vector field
p
is proportional to
\partial_{v}
.

A Walker manifold (see, e.g.,
[
7
]
) is a Lorentzian manifold
(M,g)
admitting a
\nabla^{g}
-parallel distribution of isotropic lines
\ell
. Locally
g
is given by (
9
) with the functions
A_{i}
independent of
v
. If moreover the induced connection in
\ell
is flat, i.e.,
(M,g)
admits (local)
\nabla^{g}
-parallel isotropic vector field
p
, then in the function
H
may be chosen to be independent of
v
.

Let
\nabla
be a metric connection on a Lorentzian manifold
(M,g)
and suppose that
p
is a
\nabla
-parallel isotropic vector field on
M
.
Since the metric
g
is
\nabla
-parallel, the distribution
p^{\bot}
is
\nabla
-parallel.
The bundle
E=p^{\bot}/\left<p\right>
is called
the screen bundle
, see
[
15
,
11
]
for details. There is the obvious projection
p^{\bot}\to E
. The connection
\nabla
induces a connection on
E
(which we denote by the same symbol): if
X
is a vector field on
M
and
Y
is a section of
E
, then
\nabla_{X}Y
is the projection to
E
of the vector field
\nabla_{X}\tilde{Y}
, where
\tilde{Y}
is an arbitrary section of
p^{\bot}
such that its projection to
E
is
Y
.

We will consider a Witt basis
p,e_{1},\dots,e_{n},q
of the Minkowski space
\mathbb{R}^{1,n+1}
. The non-zero values of the Minkowski metric with respect to such basis are
g(p,q)=g(q,p)=g(e_{i},e_{i})=1
. We will denote by
\mathbb{R}^{n}
the Euclidean subspace of
\mathbb{R}^{1,n+1}
spanned by the vectors

e_{1},\dots,e_{n}
. With respect to the basis
p,e_{1},\dots,e_{n},q
, the subalgebra of
\mathfrak{so}(1,n+1)
preserving the isotropic line
\mathbb{R}p
has the following matrix form:

\mathfrak{so}(1,n+1)_{\mathbb{R}p}=\left\{\left.\begin{pmatrix}a&-X^{t}&0\\
0&A&X\\
0&0&-a\end{pmatrix}\right|\begin{matrix}a\in\mathbb{R}\\
A\in\mathfrak{so}(n)\\
X\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\end{matrix}\right\}.

The above matrix is identified with the bivector

-ap\wedge q+A+p\wedge X,

and we get the decomposition

\mathfrak{so}(1,n+1)_{\mathbb{R}p}=(\mathbb{R}p\wedge q\oplus\mathfrak{so}(n))\ltimes p\wedge\mathbb{R}^{n}.

For the subalgebra of
\mathfrak{so}(1,n+1)
annihilating the isotropic vector
p
we have

\mathfrak{so}(1,n+1)_{p}=\mathfrak{so}(n)\ltimes p\wedge\mathbb{R}^{n}.

3.
Main Results

Let
(M,g)
be a Lorentzian manifold admitting a metric connection with parallel twistor-free torsion having non-vanishing vectorial component
\xi
.

Assuming that
(M,g)
is simply connected and complete and that
g(\xi,\xi)=\varepsilon=\pm 1
, one may apply all arguments from the proof of Theorem
2
and conclude that
(M,g)
must be isometric to the manifold given by (
10
). But such Lorentzian manifold is not complete
[
4
,
6
]
and we see that the statement of Theorem
2
cannot be extended to complete Lorentzian manifolds with non-isotropic
\xi
.
Since assuming geodesic completeness is usually too strong in Lorentzian geometry, one may consider weaker notions of geodesic completeness, such as space-like geodesic completeness time-like geodesic completeness, or light-like geodesic completeness (to be short, we use the notion of
\eta
-completeness, where
\eta=1,-1,0
).
It turned out that assuming a weaker notion of geodesic completeness still does not allow to prove an analogue of Theorem
2
for Lorentzian manifolds and finally we prove

Theorem 3
.

Let
(M,g)
be a Lorentzian manifold admitting a metric connection with parallel twistor-free torsion having non-vanishing vectorial component
\xi
. Then
(M,g)
is not
\eta
-complete for
\eta=1,-1,0
.

Theorem
3
generalizes the following two results. Lorentzian space forms do not admit homogeneous structures of vectorial type
[
14
]
. A homogeneous Lorentzian space admitting a homogeneous structure of isotropic vectorial type is a singular homogeneous plane wave
[
19
,
5
]
.

We prove an analogue of Theorem
1
for Lorentzian manifolds if we just require the completeness of the vectorial component of the torsion in the case when the vectorial component is non-isotropic.

Theorem 4
.

Let
(M,g)
be a simply connected Lorentzian manifold. Let
\varepsilon=\pm 1
. Then

(M,g)
admits a metric connection with parallel twistor-free torsion having the vectorial component given by a non-isotropic complete
vector field

\xi
with
\varepsilon g(\xi,\xi)>0
if and only if
(M,g)
is homothetic to the warped product

(10)

(\mathbb{R}\times N,\varepsilon(dt)^{2}+e^{2\varepsilon t}g_{N}),

where
(N,g_{N})
is a Riemannian (if
\varepsilon=-1
) or a Lorentzian
(if
\varepsilon=1
) manifold carrying a parallel 3-form
\tau\in\Omega^{3}(N)
that satisfies
\sigma_{\tau}=0
.

Example 1
.

The flat slicing coordinates on an open subset of the de Sitter space
dS^{n+2}
allow one to write the de Sitter metric in the form

g=-(dt)^{2}+e^{-2t}\sum_{i=1}^{n+1}(dy^{i})^{2}.

The vector field
\xi=\partial_{t}
defines a metric connection
\nabla
with vectorial torsion. This connection is a homogeneous structure on an open subset of
dS^{n+2}
and, according to
[
14
]
,
\nabla
cannot be extended to the entire
dS^{n+2}
.

Then we prove an analogue of Theorem
2
for
the Lorentzian signature.

Theorem 5
.

Let
(N,g)
be a Lorentzian
manifold carrying a parallel 3-form
\tau
with
\sigma_{\tau}=0
. Then
(N,g)
is locally isometric to one of the following manifolds:

1.

\dim N=3
,
(N,g)
is an oriented 3-dimensional Lorentzian manifold and
\tau
is a constant multiple of its Lorentzian volume form;

2.

\dim N\geqslant 4
,
(N,g)
is a Lorentzian manifold carrying a parallel isotropic vector field
p
, and
\tau=p^{\flat}\wedge\omega
, where
\omega
is a parallel 2-form on the screen bundle
p^{\bot}/\left<p\right>
.

3.

(N,g)
is a Lorentzian manifold and
\tau=0
;

4.

(N,g)
locally is a product of a Lorentzian manifold
from one of the cases 1–3 with a Riemannian manifold from Theorem
2
.

From Lemma
1
it follows that Theorem
5
describes Lorentzian manifolds admitting metric connections
\nabla
with closed
\nabla
-parallel skew-symmetric torsion.

Then we consider the case when the vectorial part of the torsion is isotropic. In that case the
g
-norm of the torsion coincides with the
g
-norm
||S||_{g}
of the skew-symmetric component
S
. We say that the component
S
is degenerate if
||S||_{g}=0
, otherwise we say that
S
is non-degenerate. The first theorem deals with the case of degenerate skew-symmetric component
S
.

Theorem 6
.

Let
(M_{0},g_{0})
be a Lorentzian manifold with a
\nabla^{g_{0}}
-parallel isotropic vector field
p_{0}
and a
\nabla^{g_{0}}
-parallel form

\tau_{0}=p_{0}^{\flat}\wedge\omega
, where
\omega
is a
\nabla^{g_{0}}
-parallel 2-form
on the screen bundle
p^{\bot}/\left<p\right>
.
Suppose that there exists a function
\varphi
on
M_{0}
such that
p_{0}^{\flat}=de^{\varphi}
. Let
\xi=e^{-\varphi}p_{0}
and
S=e^{-\varphi}\tau_{0}
. Then the connection
\nabla
on
M_{0}
given by (
1
) has parallel
twistor-free torsion with isotropic vectorial component
\xi
and the skew-symmetric component
S
.

Conversely, each simply connected Lorentzian manifold admitting a metric connection with parallel twistor-free torsion having isotropic vectorial component and degenerate skew-symmetric component is globally equivalent to a just constructed one.

Remark 1
.

Let
(M_{0},g_{0})
be a Lorentzian manifold with a
\nabla^{g_{0}}
-parallel isotropic vector field
p_{0}
.
Then
dp^{\flat}_{0}=0
, and there exists a function
f
such that
p^{\flat}_{0}=df
. Such a function is defined up to a constant. The condition in the above construction is satisfied whenever each such function is bounded from below. For example, let
(B,b)
be a Riemannian manifold. Consider the Walker manifold

(M_{0}=\mathbb{R}\times B\times\mathbb{R}_{+},\quad g_{0}=2dvdu+b+H(du)^{2}),

where
v
and
u
are the coordinates on
\mathbb{R}
and
\mathbb{R}_{+}
, respectively, and
H
is any function on
B\times\mathbb{R}_{+}
. Then

p_{0}=\partial_{v}
is a parallel isotropic vector, and
p^{\flat}_{0}=du=de^{\varphi}
,
\varphi=\ln u
. The condition on
p^{\flat}_{0}
from the above construction is satisfied.

Example 2
.

Consider the metric

g_{0}=2dvdu+\sum_{i=1}^{n}(dx^{i})^{2}+(H+2v)(du)^{2},\quad\partial_{v}H=0.

The vector field
p_{0}=-e^{-u}\partial_{v}
is
\nabla^{g_{0}}
-parallel and it satisfies
p_{0}^{\flat}=de^{-u}
.
Let

\xi=-\partial_{v},\quad S=du\wedge\sum_{i<j}F_{ij}dx^{i}\wedge dx^{j},

where
F=(F_{ij})
is a constant skew-symmetric matrix. Then the connection
\nabla
given by (
1
) is a metric connection with parallel twistor-free torsion having isotropic vectorial component
\xi
and skew-symmetric component
S
. The connection
\nabla
is a homogeneous structure if and only if
\nabla R=0
. According to
[
5
,
17
,
19
]
, this is the case if and only if

H=A(e^{-uF}x,e^{-uF}x),

i.e,
g
is a singular homogeneous plane-wave metric. Here
A
is a constant symmetric bilinear form.

The following two theorems give a complete solution for non-degenerate
S
.

Theorem 7
.

Let
(M,g)
be a 3-dimensional Lorentzian manifold. Then

(M,g)
admits a metric connection with parallel twistor-free torsion having isotropic vectorial component
p
and non-degenerate skew-symmetric component
S
if and only if
M
is oriented and admits an isotropic vector field
p

such that

(11)

\nabla^{g}_{X}p=-g(X,p)p-\frac{1}{2}S(X,p),\quad\forall\,X\in\Gamma(TM),

where

S=a\text{\rm Vol}_{g},\quad a\in\mathbb{R},\quad a\neq 0.

In particular, such
(M,g)
is a Kundt space, and locally
g
may be written as

g=2dvdu+2avdxdu+(dx)^{2}+(-2ve^{-ax}+C(x,u))(du)^{2},

where
C(x,u)
is an arbitrary function and
p=e^{-ax}\partial_{v}
.

Theorem 8
.

Let
(M_{0},g_{0},p_{0},\tau_{0},\varphi)
be a Lorentzian manifold as in
Theorem
6
.
Let
(N,g_{N})
be a Riemannian manifold with a non-zero
\nabla^{g_{N}}
-parallel 3-form
\tau_{N}
satisfying
\sigma_{\tau_{N}}=0
. Consider the manifold

M=M_{0}\times N

with the Lorentzian metric

g=g_{0}+e^{2\varphi}g_{N}.

Let

\xi=e^{-\varphi}p_{0},\quad S=e^{-\varphi}\tau_{0}+e^{3\varphi}\tau_{N}.

Then the connection
\nabla
on
M
given by (
1
) has parallel
twistor-free torsion with isotropic vectorial component
\xi
and non-degenerate skew-symmetric component
S
.

Conversely, each Lorentzian manifold of dimension different from 3 admitting a metric connection with parallel twistor-free torsion having isotropic vectorial component and non-degenerate skew-symmetric component is locally equivalent to a just constructed one.

4.
Proof of Theorem
4

Let
(M,g)
be a simply connected Lorentzian manifold. Suppose that

(M,g)
admits a metric connection
\nabla
with parallel twistor-free torsion having vectorial component given by a non-isotropic complete
vector field
\xi
. Let us assume that
g(\xi,\xi)=\varepsilon=\pm 1
. The connection
\nabla
is given by (
1
). For the proof we use the ideas from
[
20
]
, and we apply
[
16
, Prop. 8]
to obtain the global decomposition of
M
.

Consider the 1-form
\eta
given by

\eta(X)=\varepsilon g(\xi,X).

It is clear that
\eta(\xi)=1
. As in
[
20
]
it can be shown that

d\eta=S(\xi)=0.

Proposition 8 from
[
16
]
implies that all leaves of the foliation on
M
tangent to the distribution
\ker\eta
are pairwise diffeomorphic, and the manifold
M
is diffeomorphic to the product

M\cong\mathbb{R}\times N,

where
N
is a leaf of the foliation. Under this diffeomorphism, the vector field
\xi
corresponds to
\partial_{t}
. From the definition of
\eta
it follows that the vectors tangent to the leaves of the foliation are orthogonal to
\partial_{t}
. Consequently, the metric
g
may be written in the form

g=\varepsilon(dt)^{2}+h,

where
h
is a
t
-family of metrics on
N
.
Consider the metric

\tilde{g}=e^{-2\varepsilon t}g.

Applying the formula for the Levi-Civita connection under the conformal change, (
1
), and the fact that
S(\xi)=0
, we obtain

\nabla^{\tilde{g}}_{X}\xi=-\varepsilon(Xt)\xi.

This shows that the distribution generated by
\xi
and the distribution
\ker\eta
are
\nabla^{\tilde{g}}
-parallel. The local version of the Wu Theorem implies that

g_{N}=e^{-2\varepsilon t}h

is a metric on
N
independent of
t
.
Thus,

g=\varepsilon(dt)^{2}+e^{2\varepsilon t}g_{N}.

The equality
S(\xi)=0
shows that
S
is a
t
-family of 3-forms on the manifold
N
. As in
[
20
]
it can be shown that the condition
\nabla S=0
is equivalent to the conditions

S=e^{3\varepsilon t}\tau_{N},\quad\tau_{N}\in\Omega^{3}(N),\quad\nabla^{g}\tau_{N}=0,\quad\sigma_{\tau_{N}}=0.

This completes the proof of the theorem.
∎

5.
Proof of Theorem
5

Let
(N,g)
be a Lorentzian
manifold carrying a non-zero parallel 3-form
\tau
such that
\sigma_{\tau}=0
. Consider the metric connection

\nabla=\nabla^{g}+\frac{1}{2}\tau

with skew-symmetric torsion
\tau
.
From Lemma
1
it follows that
\tau
is
\nabla
-parallel.
In
[
11
]

we described holonomy, curvature and torsion of Lorentzian connections with parallel skew-symmetric torsion. We have now to consider the additional condition
\sigma_{\tau}=0
. We assume that
\dim N=n+2\geqslant 3
and denote by

\mathfrak{g}\subset\mathfrak{so}(1,n+1)
the holonomy algebra of the connection
\nabla
.
We fix a point
x\in N
. The tangent space
T_{x}N
may be identified with the Minkowski space
\mathbb{R}^{1,n+1}
.

Recall that a subalgebra
\mathfrak{g}\subset\mathfrak{so}(1,n+1)
is called weakly irreducible if it does not preserve any proper non-degenerate subspace of
\mathbb{R}^{1,n+1}
.
The geometry
(N,g,\nabla)
is called
reducible
if the holonomy algebra
\mathfrak{g}\subset\mathfrak{so}(1,n+1)
of the connection
\nabla
is
not
weakly irreducible, i.e.,
\mathfrak{g}
preserves a proper non-degenerate subspace of the tangent space. In this case there exists a non-trivial
\mathfrak{g}
-invariant orthogonal decomposition of the tangent space

(12)

T_{x}M=L\oplus E.

The geometry
(N,g,\nabla)
is called
decomposable

if the holonomy algebra
\mathfrak{g}\subset\mathfrak{so}(1,n+1)
preserves an orthogonal decomposition (
12
) such that it holds

\tau_{x}\in\wedge^{3}L\oplus\wedge^{3}E.

Otherwise we say that the geometry is
indecomposable
. If the geometry is decomposable, then it is a product of two other geometries, i.e.,
(N,g)
is as in the case 4 from the statement of the theorem. Thus we may assume that the geometry is indecomposable.

In Section 3 from
[
11
]
we proved that if
\nabla
is a metric connection on a Lorentzian manifold
(N,g)
with parallel skew-symmetric torsion
\tau
and weakly irreducible holonomy algebra, then
\tau
automatically satisfies the condition

\sigma_{\tau}=0
, moreover,
(N,g)
is as in the case 1 or 2 from the statement of the theorem.

Now we assume that the geometry
(N,g,\nabla)
is reducible and indecomposable. Then the holonomy algebra
\mathfrak{g}
preserves a decomposition (
12
). We may assume that the induced representation of
\mathfrak{g}
in
L
is weakly irreducible. We consider several cases depending on the dimension of
L
and use the description of
\tau
from
[
11
]
.
We will denote by
\tau
also the value of the field
\tau
at the point
x
.
Since the field
\tau
is
\nabla
-parallel, it is enough to check that its value at the point
x
satisfies the condition

(13)

\tau(X)\cdot\tau=0\quad\text{for all}\quad X\in T_{x}N.

If
\dim L\geqslant 2
, we denote by
p,e_{1},\dots,e_{k},q
a Witt basis in
L
.

Let
\dim L=1
. Then

T_{x}M=\mathbb{R}e_{-}\oplus E,

\tau=e_{-}\wedge\theta+\omega_{E},

where
g(e_{-},e_{-})=-1
,
\theta\in\wedge^{2}E
and
\omega_{E}\in\wedge^{3}E
are annihilated by
\mathfrak{g}
and it holds
\theta\cdot\omega_{E}=0
. The last condition may be written in the form

\underset{XYZ}{\mathfrak{S}}\omega_{E}(\theta(X),Y,Z)=0\quad\text{for all}\quad X,Y,Z\in E.

Let
X\in E
. Then

\tau(X)=\theta(X)\wedge e_{-}+\omega_{E}(X),

\tau(X)\cdot\tau=\theta(X)\wedge\theta+\omega_{E}(X)\cdot\omega_{E}+e_{-}\wedge(\omega_{E}(X)\cdot\theta)=0.

This is equivalent to

(14)

\displaystyle\theta(X)\wedge\theta+\omega_{E}(X)\cdot\omega_{E}

\displaystyle=0,

(15)

\displaystyle\omega_{E}(X)\cdot\theta

\displaystyle=0.

We have

0=\omega_{E}(X)\cdot\theta(Y,Z)=-\theta(\omega_{E}(X,Y),Z)-\theta(Y,\omega_{E}(X,Z))=\\
=\omega_{E}(X,Y,\theta(Z))-\omega_{E}(X,Z,\theta(Y))=\omega_{E}(\theta(Z),X,Y)+\omega_{E}(\theta(Y),Z,X)=\\
=-\omega_{E}(\theta(X),Y,Z),

which means that
\theta\in\wedge^{2}(\ker\omega_{E})
. Since the geometry is indecomposable, this implies that
\omega_{E}=0
.
Equation (
14
) now reads as

\theta(X)\wedge\theta=0,

for all
X\in E
. This is possible only if
\operatorname{rk}\theta\leqslant 2
.
Thus the indecomposability implies that the dimension of
N
is 3.

Let
\dim L=2
. In this case
\tau
has the form

\tau=p\wedge q\wedge v+\theta\wedge v+\omega_{E_{1}},

where
v\in E
is a non-zero vector,
E=\mathbb{R}v\oplus E_{1}
is an orthogonal decomposition,
\mathfrak{g}v=0
,
\omega_{E_{1}}\in\wedge^{3}E_{1}
,
\theta\in\wedge^{2}E_{1}
,
\mathfrak{g}\cdot\omega_{E_{1}}=0
,
\mathfrak{g}\cdot\theta=0
.
Consider the condition (
13
).
It holds

\tau(p)=-p\wedge v,

\tau(p)\cdot\tau=g(v,v)\theta\wedge v=0.

Hence,
\theta=0
. The indecomposability again implies that the dimension of
N
is 3.

Assume that
\dim L=3
. Then, given an arbitrary Witt basis
p,e_{1},q
of
L
, the torsion
\tau
has the form

\tau=p\wedge(\alpha e_{1}\wedge q+e_{1}\wedge v+\lambda)+\theta\wedge v+\omega_{E_{1}},

where
v\in E
is a vector,
E_{1}
is the orthogonal complement of
v
in
E
,
\mathfrak{g}v=0
,
\omega_{E_{1}}\in\wedge^{3}E_{1}
,
\theta\in\wedge^{2}E_{1}
and
\lambda\in\wedge^{2}E
are annihilated by
\mathfrak{g}
.
It holds that

\tau(e_{1})=-\alpha p\wedge q-p\wedge v,

\tau(e_{1})\cdot\tau=p\wedge(\alpha\lambda+g(v,v)\theta)=0.

Consequently,

\alpha\lambda+g(v,v)\theta=0.

First suppose that
\alpha\neq 0
. Let us set

\tilde{v}=v-\frac{g(v,v)}{\alpha}p,\quad\tilde{q}=q+\frac{1}{\alpha}\left(v-\frac{1}{2\alpha}g(v,v)p\right),\quad\tilde{E}=\mathbb{R}\tilde{v}\oplus E,\quad\tilde{L}=\left<p,e_{1},\tilde{q}\right>.

Then
p,e_{1},\tilde{q}
is a Witt basis in
\tilde{L}
and
T_{x}M=\tilde{L}\oplus\tilde{E}
is a new
\mathfrak{g}
-invariant orthogonal decomposition.
The torsion
\tau
now may be written as

\tau=\alpha p\wedge e_{1}\wedge\tilde{q}+\theta\wedge\tilde{v}+\omega_{E_{1}}

and we see that the geometry is decomposable.

Suppose now that
\alpha=0
. The condition (
13
) may be rewritten in the following way:

\theta\wedge v=0,\quad\lambda(v)=0,\quad\lambda\cdot\omega_{E_{1}}=0,\quad\omega_{E_{1}}(X)\cdot\omega_{E_{1}}=0,\quad\forall X\in E_{1}.

The expressions for the curvature tensor from
[
11
, Theorem 5]
imply that

\mathfrak{g}=\mathbb{R}p\wedge e_{1}\oplus\mathfrak{b},

where
\mathfrak{b}
is the projection of
\mathfrak{g}
onto
\mathfrak{so}(E_{1})
. The above equalities show that
\omega_{E_{1}}
defines a Lie algebra structure on
E_{1}
, and
\lambda
is a derivation of the Lie algebra
E_{1}
.
Let
E_{0}=\ker\omega_{E_{1}}\subset E_{1}
and let
E_{1}^{\prime}
be the orthogonal complement to
E_{0}
in
E_{1}
. Then
E_{0}
is a commutative Lie algebra, and
E_{1}^{\prime}
is either the trivial or a semisimple Lie algebra.
Therefore,

\lambda=\omega_{E_{1}}(U_{0})+\lambda_{0},

where
U_{0}\in E^{\prime}_{1}
and
\lambda_{0}\in\wedge^{2}E_{0}
.
Since
\mathfrak{g}
annihilates
\lambda
and
\omega_{E_{1}}
, it annihilates the vector
U_{0}
.

Let

\tilde{E}=\{\tilde{U}=U+g(U,U_{0})p\,|\,U\in E\},\quad\tilde{q}=q-U_{0}-\frac{1}{2}g(U_{0},U_{0})p,\quad\tilde{L}=\left<p,e_{1},\tilde{q}\right>.

Then,
T_{x}M=\tilde{L}\oplus\tilde{E}
is a
\mathfrak{g}
-invariant orthogonal decomposition. Now,

\tau=p\wedge(e_{1}\wedge\tilde{v}+\tilde{\lambda}_{0})+\omega_{\tilde{E}_{1}},

where in the new notation
\tilde{E}=\mathbb{R}\tilde{v}+\tilde{E}_{1}
,
\tilde{E}_{1}=\tilde{E}_{0}\oplus\tilde{E}^{\prime}_{1}
,
\tilde{\lambda}_{0}\in\wedge^{2}\tilde{E}_{0}
,
\omega_{\tilde{E}_{1}}\in\wedge^{3}\tilde{E}^{\prime}_{1}
. The indecomposability implies that
\tilde{E}^{\prime}_{1}=0
. Thus,
(N,g)
is as in the case 1 or 2 from the statement of the theorem.

Finally let us assume that
\dim L\geqslant 4
. Then the torsion
\tau
has the form

(16)

\tau=p\wedge(\omega+\sum_{i=1}^{k}e_{i}\wedge\mu_{i}+\lambda)+\omega_{E},

where
\mu_{i}\in E
,
\omega_{E}\in\wedge^{3}E
,
\omega\in\wedge^{2}\mathbb{R}^{k}
,
\lambda\in\wedge^{2}E
, and it holds that
\lambda\cdot\omega_{E}=0
.
Let us consider the condition (
13
):

\tau(e_{i})=-p\wedge\mu_{i}-p\wedge\omega(e_{i}),

\tau(e_{i})\cdot\tau=-p\wedge\omega_{E}(\mu_{i})=0,

\tau(U)\cdot\tau=\omega_{E}(U)\cdot\omega_{E}=0,\quad U\in E.

The condition
\tau(q)\cdot\tau=0
is equivalent to
\lambda\cdot\omega_{E}=0
.
From these equalities we get
\omega_{E}(\mu_{i})=0
.
Again,
\omega_{E}
defines a Lie algebra structure on
E
, and as in the previous case, the indecomposability of the geometry implies that
\omega_{E}=0
, and
(N,g)
is as in the case 1 or 2 from the statement of the theorem.
∎

6.
Proof of Theorem
7

Let
(M,g)
be a 3-dimensional Lorentzian manifold.
Suppose that
(M,g)
admits a metric connection
\nabla
with parallel twistor-free torsion having isotropic vectorial component
\xi
and non-zero skew-symmetric component
S
.

The connection
\nabla
is given by (
1
).
Since
S
is parallel, it is non-vanishing, and, consequently,
M
is orientable. Fix an orientation of
M
, and let
\text{\rm Vol}_{g}
be the volume form defined by
g
. The torsion is
\nabla
-parallel if and only if
\nabla p=0
and
\nabla S=0
.

It holds

0=\nabla S=\nabla^{g}S,

i.e.,
S
is proportional to the volume form,

S=a\text{\rm Vol}_{g},\quad a\in\mathbb{R},\quad a\neq 0.

Now, the torsion is parallel
if and only if

\nabla p=0
. By (
1
), the last condition is equivalent to (
11
).
It is obvious that the vector field
p
satisfies the conditions (
8
), i.e.,
(M,g)
is a Kundt space.
Consequently, the metric
g
may be locally written in the form

g=2dvdu+2A(v,x,u)dxdu+(dx)^{2}+H(v,x,u)(du)^{2}.

The vector field
p
satisfies

p=\varphi\partial_{v}
, for a function
\varphi=\varphi(x,u)
.
Consider the local field of Witt frames

p=\varphi\partial_{v},\quad e=\partial_{x},\quad q=\frac{1}{\varphi}\left(\partial_{u}-A\partial_{x}-\frac{1}{2}H\partial_{v}\right).

The condition (
11
) is equivalent to

\nabla^{g}_{p}p=0,\quad\nabla^{g}_{e}p=-\frac{1}{2}ap,\quad\nabla_{q}^{g}p=-p+\frac{1}{2}ae.

It is easy to check that

\nabla^{g}_{e}p=\left(\partial_{x}\varphi+\frac{1}{2}\varphi\partial_{v}A\right)\partial_{v}.

This implies that

\frac{\partial_{x}\varphi}{\varphi}+\frac{1}{2}\partial_{v}A=-\frac{1}{2}a.

Next,

\nabla^{g}_{q}p=\left(\frac{1}{\varphi}(\partial_{u}\varphi-A\partial_{x}\varphi)+\frac{1}{2}\partial_{v}H-A\partial_{v}A\right)\partial_{v}+\frac{1}{2}\partial_{v}A\partial_{x}.

We conclude that
\partial_{v}A=a
, i.e.,
A=av+B(x,u)
, and
\partial_{x}\varphi=-\varphi,
i.e.,
\varphi=c(u)e^{-ax}
. A simple coordinate transformation allows to assume that
B(x,u)=0
and
c(u)=1
.
Finally we get that
H=-2e^{-ax}v+C(x,u)
. ∎

7.
Proof of Theorems
6
and
8

Let
(M,g)
be a Lorentzian manifold admitting a metric connection
\nabla
with parallel twistor-free torsion having isotropic vectorial component
p
and skew-symmetric component
S
. If
\dim M=3
, then thanks to Theorem
7

we will assume that

||S||_{g}=0
; since in dimension 3 each 3-form is proportional to the (local) volume form, this implies that
S=0
.
Denote by
\mathfrak{g}\subset\mathfrak{so}(1,n+1)
the holonomy algebra of the connection
\nabla
at a point
x\in M
. By abuse of notation, we denote by
p
,
S
,
R
the values of the corresponding tensor fields at the point
x
.
Then
\mathfrak{g}
preserves the vector
p\in\mathbb{R}^{1,n+1}=T_{x}M
, i.e.,
\mathfrak{g}\subset\mathfrak{so}(1,n+1)_{p}
.

Lemma 2
.

[
11
, Lemma 1]

If
\mathfrak{g}\subset\mathfrak{so}(1,n+1)_{p}
is a weakly irreducible subalgebra, then each
S\in\wedge^{3}\mathbb{R}^{1,n+1}
annihilated by
\mathfrak{g}
is of the form
S=p\wedge\omega

for a bivector
\omega
on
p^{\bot}/\left<p\right>
.

Lemma 3
.

If the holonomy algebra
\mathfrak{g}\subset\mathfrak{so}(1,n+1)
of the connection
\nabla
is not weakly irreducible, then

\mathfrak{g}
preserves an orthogonal decomposition

\mathbb{R}^{1,n+1}=L\oplus E,

where

L=\mathbb{R}p\oplus\mathbb{R}^{k}\oplus\mathbb{R}q,\quad 0\leqslant k\leqslant n,

such that

S=p\wedge\omega+\omega_{E},

where
\omega\in\wedge^{2}\mathbb{R}^{k}
and
\omega_{E}\in\wedge^{3}E
.
Moreover,
\sigma_{\omega_{E}}=0
and
\ker\omega_{E}=0
.

Note that the statement of the lemma includes the case
E=0
.

Proof of Lemma
3
.

If
\mathfrak{g}\subset\mathfrak{so}(1,n+1)
is not weakly irreducible, then it is clear that

\mathfrak{g}
preserves an orthogonal decomposition

\mathbb{R}^{1,n+1}=L\oplus E,\quad\dim L\geqslant 1

such that the induced representation of
\mathfrak{g}
in
L
is weakly irreducible.

First assume that
\dim L\geqslant 4
.
By
[
11
, Lemma 7]
,

S=p\wedge\omega+\omega_{E},

where

\omega=\omega_{\mathbb{R}^{k}}+\mu+\lambda,

here
\omega_{\mathbb{R}^{k}}\in\wedge^{2}\mathbb{R}^{k}
,
\mu\in\mathbb{R}^{k}\wedge E
,
\lambda\in\wedge^{2}E
.
The Bianchi identity (
3
) written for the vectors
U,V\in E
and
q
takes the form

R(U,V)q+R(V,q)U+R(q,U)V=-(\omega\cdot\omega_{E})(U,V)+\lambda(U,V)p+\omega_{E}(U,V).

Since
E
is holonomy-invariant,
R(V,q)U,R(q,U)V\in E
.
Hence, multiplying the above equality scalarly by
q
, we get

\lambda=0.

This implies that
\omega\cdot\omega_{E}=0
, and

R(V,q)U+R(q,U)V=\omega_{E}(U,V).

Since the first prolongation of
\mathfrak{so}(E)
is trivial, the equation

R(V,q)U+R(q,U)V=0
, for all
U,V\in E
, has only trivial solution. This shows that

R(q,U)V=\frac{1}{2}\omega_{E}(U,V),\quad\forall\,U,V\in E.

Since
\mathfrak{g}
annihilates
S
, and
R
takes values in
\mathfrak{g}
, we get

(R(q,U)\cdot S)(U_{1},U_{2},U_{3})=0,\quad\forall\,U,U_{1},U_{2},U_{3}\in E.

This implies that

\omega_{E}(U)\cdot\omega_{E}=0,\quad\forall\,U\in E,

i.e.,

\sigma_{\omega_{E}}=0.

We conclude that the equality

g([U_{1},U_{2}],U_{3})=\omega_{E}(U_{1},U_{2},U_{3}),\quad\forall\,U_{1},U_{2},U_{3}\in E

defines a Lie bracket on the vector space
E
.
Let
E_{0}=\ker\omega_{E}
, and let
E^{\prime}
be the orthogonal complement to
E_{0}
in
E
. Then
E_{0}
and
E^{\prime}
are commutative and semisimple ideals in
E
, respectively.

The Bianchi identity written for the vectors
X\in\mathbb{R}^{k}
,
U\in E
and
q
easily implies that
g(R(q,U)X,Y)=0
for all
Y\in\mathbb{R}^{k}
. Hence,
R(q,U)X\in\mathbb{R}p
. Let us write the bivector
\mu
in the form
\mu=\sum_{i=1}^{k}e_{i}\wedge\mu_{i}
, where
\mu_{i}\in E
. Now,
\mathfrak{g}
annihilates the tensor
p\wedge\mu
, hence,

0=R(q,U)\cdot(p\wedge\mu)=\sum_{i=1}^{k}p\wedge e_{i}\wedge R(q,U)\cdot\mu=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i=1}^{k}p\wedge e_{i}\wedge[U,\mu_{i}].

This implies that
[U,\mu_{i}]=0
for all
U\in E
. Consequently,
\mu_{i}\in E_{0}
. Denoting now
L\oplus E_{0}
by
L
and
E^{\prime}
by
E
, we see that
S
is just as in the statement of the lemma.

Assume now again that the representation of
\mathfrak{g}
in
L
is weakly irreducible.

Suppose that
\dim L=3
. According to
[
11
]
,
S
has the form

S=p\wedge\omega+\omega_{E}+\alpha p\wedge e_{1}\wedge q,

where
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}
and all other elements are as above.
Using the Bianchi identity for the vectors
U\in E
,
e_{1}
and
q
, it is easy to shown that
\alpha=0
, and further considerations are just as in the previous case.

Since
\mathfrak{g}
annihilates the isotropic vector
p
, it is not possible that the representation
of
\mathfrak{g}
in
L
is weakly irreducible and
\dim L=2
. Finally, if
\dim L=1
, then since
\mathfrak{g}
annihilates the isotropic vector
p
, it annihilates a two-dimensional Lorentzian subspace of
\mathbb{R}^{1,n+1}
. Again, it is not hard to show that the statement of the lemma holds true. The lemma is proved. ∎

Corollary 1
.

Under the current assumptions it holds
S(p)=0
unless
\dim M=3
and
(M,g)
is as in Theorem
7
.

Let us suppose that the manifold
M
is simply connected.
Consider the dual 1-form
\eta
to the vector field
p
,

\eta(X)=g(p,X),\quad\forall\,X\in\Gamma(TM).

It holds

d\eta(X,Y)=Xg(p,Y)-Yg(p,X)-g(p,[X,Y])\\
=g(\nabla^{g}_{X}p,Y)+g(p,\nabla^{g}_{X}Y)-g(\nabla^{g}_{Y}p,X)-g(p,\nabla^{g}_{Y}X)-g(p,\nabla^{g}_{X}Y-\nabla^{g}_{Y}X)\\
=g(\nabla^{g}_{X}p,Y)-g(\nabla^{g}_{Y}p,X)=g\left(-(X\wedge p)p-\frac{1}{2}S(X,p),Y\right)-g\left(-(Y\wedge p)p-\frac{1}{2}S(Y,p),X\right)=0.

Then there exists a function
\varphi
such that
\eta=d\varphi
.

Proof of Theorem
6
.
Suppose now that
S
is degenerate. From the above lemmata it follows that
S=p\wedge\omega
.

From (
1
) it follows that

\nabla^{g}_{X}p=-g(X,p)p=-\eta(X)p.

Consequently the vector field

p_{0}=e^{\varphi}p

is
\nabla^{g}
-parallel. Its dual 1-form
p_{0}^{\flat}
is closed, and there exists a function
u
on
M
such that
du=p_{0}^{\flat}
. We conclude that

d\varphi=e^{-\varphi}du,

i.e.,

de^{\varphi}=du.

Since the both functions
\varphi
and
u
are defined up to a constant, we may assume that

u=e^{\varphi},

i.e., the function
u
is positive. By (
1
),
\nabla^{g}\tau_{0}=0
, where
\tau_{0}=e^{\varphi}S
. ∎

Proof of Theorem
8
.

Suppose that the manifold
M
is simply connected. Suppose that
||S||_{g}\neq 0
and
\dim M\neq 3
. Let
L
and
E
be as in Lemma
3
. Since the subspaces
L
and
E
of
T_{x}M
are
\mathfrak{g}
-invariant, they define
\nabla
-parallel distributions
\mathcal{L}
and
\mathcal{E}
on
M
. Moreover,

S=S_{1}+S_{2},\quad S_{1}\in\wedge^{3}\Gamma(\mathcal{L}),\quad S_{2}\in\wedge^{3}\Gamma(\mathcal{E}),\quad\nabla S_{1}=\nabla S_{2}=0,\quad\sigma_{S_{1}}=\sigma_{S_{2}}=0.

Consider the new metric

h=e^{-2\varphi}g,

where the function
\varphi
is as above.

By the standard formula, for all
X,Y\in\Gamma(TM)
it holds

\nabla^{h}_{X}Y=\nabla^{g}_{X}Y-g(p,X)Y-g(p,Y)X+g(X,Y)p.

Combining this equality with (
1
), we get

(17)

\nabla^{h}_{X}Y=\nabla_{X}Y-g(p,X)Y-\frac{1}{2}S(X,Y),

or just

(18)

\nabla^{h}_{X}=\nabla_{X}-g(p,X)\mathop{\text{}}{\rm id}\nolimits-\frac{1}{2}S(X).

This implies that the distributions
\mathcal{L}
and
\mathcal{E}

are
\nabla^{h}
-parallel. By the Wu Theorem,
(M,h)
is locally a product of a Lorentzian manifold
(M_{0},h_{0})
and a Riemannian manifold
(N,g_{N})
, where
M_{0}
and
N
are integral submanifolds of the distributions
\mathcal{L}
and
\mathcal{E}
, respectively. In particular, locally it holds

h=h_{0}+g_{N}.

The vector field
p
is tangent to the distribution
\mathcal{L}
, i.e.,
p
is a family of vector fields on the manifold
M_{0}
depending on the local coordinates on the manifold
N
. If
U\in\Gamma(TN)
, then
\nabla^{h}_{U}p=0
. Thus,
p
is a vector field on
M_{0}
. If
X\in\Gamma(TM_{0})
, then

\nabla^{h_{0}}_{X}p=\nabla^{h}_{X}p=-g(X,p)p=-\eta(X)p=-d\varphi(X)p.

This shows that
\varphi
is a function on the manifold
M_{0}
.
Consequently,

g_{0}=e^{2\varphi}h_{0}

is a Lorentzian metric on
M_{0}
and it coincides with
g
restricted to
M_{0}
. We get that

g=g_{0}+e^{2\varphi}g_{N}.

If
U\in\Gamma(TN)
, then by (
18
),

\nabla^{h}_{U}S_{1}=\nabla_{U}S_{1}=0,

i.e.,
S_{1}
is a 3-form on
M_{0}
.
Next, if
X\in\Gamma(TM_{0})
, then, by (
1
),

\nabla_{X}^{g_{0}}S_{1}=\nabla_{X}^{g}S_{1}=\nabla_{X}S_{1}=0.

We conclude that the data

(M_{0},g_{0},p_{0}=e^{\varphi}p,\tau_{0}=e^{\varphi}S_{1},\varphi)

is as in Theorem
6
.

For
U\in\Gamma(TN)
it holds

\nabla^{g_{N}}_{U}S_{2}=\nabla^{h}_{U}S_{2}=\nabla_{U}S_{2}=0.

If
X\in\Gamma(TM_{0})
, then

\nabla^{h}_{X}S_{2}=-g(p,X)\mathop{\text{}}{\rm id}\nolimits\cdot S_{2}=3g(p,X)S_{2}=3d\varphi(X)S_{2}.

Consequently,

\nabla^{h}_{X}\tau_{N}=0,

where
\tau_{N}=e^{-3\varphi}S_{2}
. Thus,

S=e^{-\varphi}\tau_{0}+e^{3\varphi}\tau_{N},\quad\nabla^{g_{N}}\tau_{N}=0,\quad\sigma_{\tau_{N}}=0.

This proves the theorem. ∎

Difference of Hilbert series of homogeneous monoid algebras and their normalizations
Akihiro Higashitani

Abstract
. Let Q Q be an affine monoid, 𝕜 ⁡ [ Q ] \Bbbk[Q] the associated monoid 𝕜 \Bbbk -algebra, and 𝕜 ⁡ [ Q ¯ ] \Bbbk[\overline{Q}] its normalization, where we let 𝕜 \Bbbk be a field. In this paper, in the case where 𝕜 ⁡ [ Q ] \Bbbk[Q] is homogeneous (i.e., standard graded), a difference of the Hilbert series of 𝕜 ⁡ [ Q ] \Bbbk[Q] and 𝕜 ⁡ [ Q ¯ ] \Bbbk[\overline{Q}] is discussed. More precisely, we prove that if 𝕜 ⁡ [ Q ] \Bbbk[Q] satisfies Serre’s condition ( S 2 ) (S_{2}) , then the degree of the h h -polynomial of 𝕜 ⁡ [ Q ] \Bbbk[Q] is always greater than or equal to that of 𝕜 ⁡ [ Q ¯ ] \Bbbk[\overline{Q}] . Moreover, we also show counterexamples of this statement if we drop the assumption ( S 2 ) (S_{2}) .

1.
Introduction

1.1.
Backgrounds

Let
\Bbbk
be a field throughout this paper.
For the fundamental materials on commutative algebra, see
[
3
]
.

Let
R=\bigoplus_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}R_{i}
be a graded
\Bbbk
-algebra with
\dim_{\Bbbk}R_{i}<\infty
for each
i
.
Hilbert series of
R
is one of the most fundamental invariants in the theory of commutative algebra.
For the introduction to the theory of Hilbert series, see, e.g.,
[
3
, Section 4]
.
Although the Hilbert series just provides the numerical information of
R
as a
\Bbbk
-vector space, it reflects several commutative-algebraic properties.
For example, a classical observation claims that if
R
is Cohen–Macaulay,
then the Laurent polynomial appearing in the numerator of the Hilbert series of
R
has nonnegative coefficients (
[
3
, Corollary 4.1.10]
).
Moreover, the Gorensteinness of
R
is completely characterized in terms of a kind of symmetry of the Hilbert series if
R
is a domain (
[
3
, Corollary 4.4.6]
).
Furthermore, by several recent studies, some connections with certain generalized notions of Gorensteinness (e.g. almost Gorenstein, nearly Gorenstein, level)
and the Hilbert series have been discovered. (See, e.g.,
[
6
,
7
,
10
]
.)
Since the Hilbert series of
R
can be computed from the graded minimal free resolution of
R
,
it also captures other invariants which have been well-discussed in commutative algebra, such as
Krull dimension (or codimension), Koszulness, Castelnuovo-Mumford regularity, multiplicity,
a
-invariant, and so on.

On the other hand, affine monoids and their associated
\Bbbk
-algebras have been well studied by many researchers in several contexts.
For the introduction to the theory of affine monoids and affine monoid
\Bbbk
-algebras, see, e.g.,
[
2
]
and
[
3
, Section 6.1]
.
Since geometric information of affine monoids is applicable for the analysis of algebraic properties of the associated
\Bbbk
-algebras, affine monoid
\Bbbk
-algebras have been regarded as useful objects in commutative algebra.
For example, the celebrated theorem by Hochster claims that if an affine monoid
Q
is normal, i.e.,
\overline{Q}=Q
,
then
\Bbbk[Q]
is Cohen–Macaulay (
[
3
, Theorem 6.3.5 (a)]
).
Moreover, Cohen–Macaulayness of the affine monoid
\Bbbk
-algebra
\Bbbk[Q]
is characterized in terms of geometric information on
Q

together with the information about reduced homology groups over
\Bbbk
of certain simplicial complexes (
[
15
]
).
Furthermore, Katthän reveals a strong connection with the structure of holes of affine monoids
Q
(i.e.,
\overline{Q}\setminus Q
)
and ring-theoretic properties (e.g., Serre’s condition
(S_{2})
,
(R_{1})
and depth). See
[
9
]
.

By taking these backgrounds into account, in this paper, we study the Hilbert series of
\Bbbk[Q]
and
\Bbbk[\overline{Q}]
.
In particular, we focus on the difference of them.

1.2.
Main Results

To explain the main results of this paper, we introduce the notations used throughout this paper.
Given a graded
\Bbbk
-algebra
R=\bigoplus_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}R_{i}
with
\dim_{\Bbbk}R_{i}<\infty
for each
i
,
let
\operatorname{Hilb}(R,t)
denote the Hilbert series of
R
, i.e.,

\operatorname{Hilb}(R,t)=\sum_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}\dim_{\Bbbk}R_{i}t^{i}.

We say that
R
is
homogeneous
(or
standard graded
) if it is generated by degree
1
elements.
If
R
is homogeneous and of dimension
d
, then we see that
\operatorname{Hilb}(R,t)
is of the following form:

\operatorname{Hilb}(R,t)=\frac{h_{R}(t)}{(1-t)^{d}},

where
h_{R}(t)
is a polynomial in
t
with integer coefficients. We call this polynomial
h_{R}(t)
the
h
-polynomial
of
R
.

When
R=\Bbbk[Q]
for some affine monoid
Q
, we call
Q

homogeneous
if
\Bbbk[Q]
is homogeneous.
We use the notation
\operatorname{Hilb}(Q,t)
and
h_{Q}(t)
instead of
\operatorname{Hilb}(\Bbbk[Q],t)
and
h_{\Bbbk[Q]}(t)
, respectively.

The following is the first main theorem of this paper:

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
Q
be a homogeneous affine monoid and assume that
\Bbbk[Q]
satisfies Serre’s condition
(S_{2})
.
Then
\operatorname{deg}(h_{Q}(t))\geq\operatorname{deg}(h_{\overline{Q}}(t))
.

Here,
\operatorname{deg}(f(t))
denotes the degree of the polynomial
f(t)
. For the definition of Serre’s condition, see Subsection
2.2
.

The following second main theorem shows the existence of counterexamples of Theorem
1.1
if we drop the assumption
(S_{2})
.

Theorem 1.2
.

For any positive integer
m
, there exists a homogeneous affine monoid
Q
such that
\operatorname{deg}(h_{\overline{Q}}(t))-\operatorname{deg}(h_{Q}(t))=m
.

.

2.
Preliminaries

In this section, we recall several materials used in this paper.

2.1.
Affine monoids and hole modules

An
affine monoid
is a finitely generated submonoid of
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
for some
d
.
Given an affine monoid
Q\subset\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}^{d}
, we can associate the
\Bbbk
-algebra
\Bbbk[Q]\subset\Bbbk[x_{1},\ldots,x_{d}]
defined by

\Bbbk[Q]=\Bbbk[\mathbf{x}^{\alpha}:\alpha\in Q],

where for
\alpha=(\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{d})\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}^{d}
, we let
\mathbf{x}^{\alpha}=x_{1}^{\alpha_{1}}\cdots x_{d}^{\alpha_{d}}
.
We call this
\Bbbk
-algebra
\Bbbk[Q]
the
monoid algebra
of
Q
.
Affine monoids and monoid algebras have been called as affine semigroups and affine semigroup rings (e.g., in
[
3
]
),
but it is becoming to be called them as affine monoids and monoid algebras, respectively.
Those are the same notions, but we employ the terminology “monoid” in this paper.

We recall some fundamental notions on affine monoids and their monoid
\Bbbk
-algebras.
Let
Q\subset\mathbb{Z}^{d}_{\geq 0}
be an affine monoid. The minimal generating system of
Q
is the minimal finite subset
\{\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{s}\}
of
Q

such that
\displaystyle Q=\left\{\sum_{i=1}^{s}n_{i}\alpha_{i}:n_{i}\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}\right\}
.
In this case, we use the notation
Q=\langle\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{s}\rangle
.

Let
Q=\langle\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{s}\rangle
for some
\alpha_{i}\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}^{d}
.

•

Let
\mathbb{Z}Q
denote the free abelian group generated by
Q
, i.e.,

\displaystyle\mathbb{Z}Q=\left\{\sum_{i=1}^{s}z_{i}\alpha_{i}:z_{i}\in\mathbb{Z}\right\}.

•

Let
\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}Q
denote the polyhedral cone generated by
Q
, i.e.,

\displaystyle\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}Q=\left\{\sum_{i=1}^{s}r_{i}\alpha_{i}:r_{i}\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}\right\}.

•

Let
\overline{Q}
be the
normalization
of
Q
, i.e.,

\overline{Q}=\mathbb{Z}Q\cap\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}Q.

Clearly, we have
Q\subset\overline{Q}
. We say that
Q
is
normal
if
Q=\overline{Q}
holds.
Note that
\Bbbk[\overline{Q}]
coincides with the normalization of
\Bbbk[Q]
.
It is known that
\Bbbk[Q]
is Cohen–Macaulay if
Q
is normal. (See
[
3
, Theorem 6.3.5]
.)

•

A
face

F
of
Q
is a subset of
Q
satisfying the following:
\alpha,\beta\in Q
,

\alpha+\beta\in F\Rightarrow\alpha\in F\text{ and }\beta\in F.

The dimension of a face
F
is defined to be the rank of the free abelian group
\mathbb{Z}F
.

•

We say that
Q
is
positive
if the minimal face of
Q
is
\{0\}
.

•

Regarding
\Bbbk[Q]
, we have
\dim\Bbbk[Q]=\dim Q
.

•

We say that an affine monoid
Q
is
homogeneous
if the minimal generating set of
Q
lies on the same hyperplane not containing the origin.

Throughout this paper, affine monoids are always assumed to be positive.
We recall the following statement, which will play a crucial role in the proof of Theorem
1.1
.

Theorem 2.1

(
[
9
, Theorem 3.1 and Proposition 5.5]
)
.

Let
Q
be an affine monoid. Then there exists a (not-necessarily disjoint) decomposition:

\displaystyle\overline{Q}\setminus Q=\bigcup_{i=1}^{\ell}(q_{i}+\mathbb{Z}F_{i})\cap\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}Q

(2.1)

with
q_{i}\in\overline{Q}
and faces
F_{i}
of
Q
.

Moreover, in the case
\ell=1
, i.e., if
\overline{Q}\setminus Q=(q+\mathbb{Z}F)\cap\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}Q
for some
q\in\overline{Q}
and a face
F
of
Q
,
then
\operatorname{depth}\Bbbk[Q]=\dim F+1
.

We call
q_{i}+\mathbb{Z}F_{i}
appearing in (
2.1
) a
j
-dimensional family of holes
of
Q
if
\dim F_{i}=j
.

Note that Theorem 3.1 and Proposition 5.5 in
[
9
]
claim the similar statement for non-necessarily positive affine monoids,
but we convert the statement into the case of positive affine monoids.

2.2.
Serre’s condition

Let
R
be a Noetherian ring and let
M
be a finitely generated
R
-module.
For a nonnegative integer
m
, we say that
M
satisfies
Serre’s condition
(S_{m})
if

\operatorname{depth}M_{\mathfrak{p}}\geq\min\{m,\dim M_{\mathfrak{p}}\}\text{ for all }\mathfrak{p}\in\operatorname{Spec}R,

where
\operatorname{Spec}R
denotes the set of all prime ideals of
R
.
Namely,
M
satisfies
(S_{m})
if and only if
M_{\mathfrak{p}}
is Cohen–Macaulay for any
\mathfrak{p}\in\operatorname{Spec}R
with
\operatorname{depth}M_{\mathfrak{p}}<m
.

Proposition 2.2

(cf.
[
4
]
)
.

Let
A=\Bbbk[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}]
, let
I
be an ideal of
A
and let
R=A/I
.
Then
R
satisfies
(S_{m})
if and only if
\dim(\operatorname{Ext}^{i}_{A}(R,A))\leq n-m-i
holds for any
i>d-\dim R
.

Regarding
(S_{2})
for monoid algebras
\Bbbk[Q]
, we konw the following:

Theorem 2.3

(
[
9
, Theorem 5.2]
)
.

Let
Q
be a positive affine monoid of dimension
d
.
Then
\Bbbk[Q]
satisfies
(S_{2})
if and only if every family of holes of
Q
is of dimension
d-1
.

2.3.
Ehrhart rings of lattice polytopes

A convex polytope
P\subset\mathbb{R}^{N}
is called a
lattice polytope
if all of its vertices belong to
\mathbb{Z}^{N}
.
If
P\not\subset\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}^{N}
, then we may translate
P
by a certain lattice point
\gamma\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}^{N}
to make
P+\gamma\subset\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}^{N}
.
Since most properties of lattice polytopes are preserved by the traslation by a lattice point,
we may assume that
P\subset\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}^{N}
without loss of generality.

Given a lattice polytope
P\subset\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}^{N}
, we can associate a homogeneous affine monoid
Q_{P}
as follows:

Q_{P}=\langle(\alpha,1):\alpha\in P\cap\mathbb{Z}^{N}\rangle\subset\mathbb{R}^{N+1}.

Hence, we can associate the
\Bbbk
-algebra
\Bbbk[Q_{P}]
, known as the
toric ring
of
P
.

On the other hand, we can also define the
\Bbbk
-algebra associated to
P
as follows:

\operatorname{Ehr}_{\Bbbk}(P):=\Bbbk[{\bf x}^{\alpha}x_{N+1}^{n}:\alpha\in nP\cap\mathbb{Z}^{N}]\subset\Bbbk[x_{1},\ldots,x_{N+1}],

where
nP=\{n\alpha:\alpha\in P\}
.
This
\Bbbk
-algebra
\operatorname{Ehr}_{\Bbbk}(P)
is called the
Ehrhart ring
of
P
.
We see by definition that
\operatorname{Hilb}(\operatorname{Ehr}_{\Bbbk}(P),t)
coincides with the
Ehrhart series
of
P
,
which is the generating function
\sum_{n\geq 0}|nP\cap\mathbb{Z}^{N}|t^{n}
.
For the introduction to Ehrhart theory, see
[
1
]
.

Given a homogeneous affine monoid
Q\subset\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}^{d}
, a
cross section polytope
of
Q
, denoted by
P_{Q}\subset\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}^{d}
,
is the lattice polytope obtained by the intersection of
Q
and a hyperplane including all the generators of
Q
.

Example 2.4
.

The Ehrhart ring of
P
does not necessarily coincide with the normalization of
\Bbbk[Q_{P}]
.
In fact, let
P
be the convex hull of
\{(0,0,0),(1,1,0),(0,1,1),(1,0,1)\}
. Then we see that

\Bbbk[Q_{P}]=\Bbbk[x_{4},x_{1}x_{2}x_{4},x_{2}x_{3}x_{4},x_{1}x_{3}x_{4}]\cong\Bbbk[X_{1},\ldots,X_{4}].

In particular, the normalization
\Bbbk[\overline{Q_{P}}]
is its own.
On the other hand, the following holds:

\displaystyle\operatorname{Ehr}_{\Bbbk}(P)

\displaystyle=\Bbbk[x_{4},x_{1}x_{2}x_{4},x_{2}x_{3}x_{4},x_{1}x_{3}x_{4},\uwave{x_{1}x_{2}x_{3}x_{4}^{2}}]

\displaystyle\cong\Bbbk[X_{1},\ldots,X_{4},Y]/(X_{1}X_{2}X_{3}X_{4}-Y^{2}),

where we regard
\operatorname{deg}X_{i}=1
and
\operatorname{deg}Y=2
.

We say that a lattice polytope
P\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}_{\geq 0}
is
spanning
if
\mathbb{Z}Q_{P}=\mathbb{Z}^{d+1}
.
If
P
is spanning, then we see that
\Bbbk[\overline{Q_{P}}]=\operatorname{Ehr}_{\Bbbk}(P)
.
The notion of spanning polytopes was introduced in
[
8
]
and the Ehrhart theory for spanning polytopes was developed there.

We recall a well-known notion for lattice polytopes
P\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
.
The
codegree of
P
, denoted by
\operatorname{codeg}(P)
, is defined as follows:

\displaystyle\operatorname{codeg}(P):=\min\{\ell\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}:\ell P^{\circ}\cap\mathbb{Z}^{d}\neq\emptyset\},

(2.2)

where
P^{\circ}
denotes the relative interior of
P
.

Assume that
P\subset\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}^{d}
and
P
is spanning.
Then it is known that

\operatorname{deg}(h_{\overline{Q_{P}}}(t))=\dim\Bbbk[\overline{Q_{P}}]-\operatorname{codeg}(P)

and

h_{s}=|\ell P^{\circ}\cap\mathbb{Z}^{d}|,

where
s=\operatorname{deg}(h_{\overline{Q_{P}}}(t))
,
h_{s}
is the leading coefficient of
h_{\overline{Q_{P}}}(t)
and
\ell=\operatorname{codeg}(P)
.
Those are implicitly explained in
[
1
, Section 4]
in terms of lattice polytopes or their Ehrhart series.

2.4.
Edge rings

Throughout this paper, all graphs are finite and simple.
Let
G
be a connected graph on the vertex set
V(G)
with the edge set
E(G)
.
Then we can associate a homogeneous affine monoid
Q_{G}
by setting

Q_{G}=\langle\mathbf{e}_{u,v}:\{u,v\}\in E(G)\rangle\subset\mathbb{R}^{V(G)},

where
\mathbf{e}_{u}
denotes the unit vector of
\mathbb{R}^{V(G)}
and
\mathbf{e}_{u,v}=\mathbf{e}_{u}+\mathbf{e}_{v}
.
We call the associated
\Bbbk
-algebra
\Bbbk[Q_{G}]
the
edge ring
of
G
.

Let
P_{G}
be the convex hull of
\{\mathbf{e}_{u,v}:\{u,v\}\in E(G)\}
.
This polytope is known as the
edge polytope
of
G
.

We know by
[
12
, Proposition 1.3]
that

\dim\Bbbk[G]=\dim P_{G}+1=\begin{cases}|V(G)|-1,&\text{if $G$ is bipartite},\\
|V(G)|,&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

Remark 2.5
.

For a non-bipartite connected graph
G
with
d
vertices, the edge polytope
P_{G}
is always assumed to be spanning in the following sense.
Fix a vertex
v
of
G
. Let
P^{\prime}
be the image of
P_{G}
by the projection
\pi:\mathbb{R}^{V(G)}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{V(G)\setminus\{v\}}

ignoring the entry corresponding to
v
. Then
Q_{G}
is isomorphic to
Q_{P^{\prime}}
as affine monoids.
Moreover, let
S
be a
(d-1)
-simplex in
P_{G}
described in
[
12
, Lemma 1.4]
.
Then we can check that
\{(\alpha,1):\alpha\in\pi(S)\cap\mathbb{Z}^{V(G)\setminus\{v\}}\}
forms a
\mathbb{Z}
-basis for
\mathbb{Z}^{V(G)}

if we choose a vertex
v
properly. Therefore, we conclude that
P^{\prime}
is spanning.
Since
Q_{G}
is isomorphic to
Q_{P^{\prime}}
, we can claim like “
P_{G}
is spanning”. In particular,
\Bbbk[\overline{Q_{G}}]=\operatorname{Ehr}_{\Bbbk}(P_{G})
holds.
Even if
G
is not connected, we may apply the same procedure for each connected component
G_{i}
and obtain a
\mathbb{Z}
-basis for
\mathbb{Z}^{V(G_{i})}
.
By combining these
\mathbb{Z}
-bases for
\mathbb{Z}^{V(G_{i})}
for each
i
, we obtain a
\mathbb{Z}
-basis for
\mathbb{Z}^{V(G)}
.
Namely,
P_{G}
is spanning for any non-bipartite (non-necessarily connected) graph
G
.

A similar discussion can be applied for bipartite graphs and we can also claim the spanning property for edge polytopes of bipartite graphs,
but we omit the detail since we do not use it in this paper.

An
exceptional pair
in
G
is a pair
(C,C^{\prime})
of two odd cycles
C
and
C^{\prime}
in
G
such that

C
and
C^{\prime}
have no common vertex and there is no bridge between
C
and
C^{\prime}
(i.e., no edge
\{v,v^{\prime}\}
in
G
with
v\in V(C)
and
v^{\prime}\in V(C^{\prime})
).

Edge rings have been intensively studied by several people since the following theorem was established:

Theorem 2.6

(
[
12
,
14
]
)
.

Let
G
be a connected graph. Then
\overline{Q_{G}}
is described as follows:

\displaystyle\overline{Q_{G}}=Q_{G}+\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}\{\mathbf{e}_{C}+\mathbf{e}_{C^{\prime}}:(C,C^{\prime})\text{ is an exceptional pair in }G\},

(2.3)

where
\mathbf{e}_{C}=\sum_{v\in V(C)}\mathbf{e}_{v}
. In particlar,
Q_{G}
is normal if and only if there is no exceptional pair in
G
.

We prepare the following proposition for the proof later.

Proposition 2.7
.

For a connected graph with
d
vertices, we have
\operatorname{deg}(h_{\overline{Q_{G}}}(t))\leq d/2
.
Moreover, if the equality holds, then
h_{d/2}=1
, where
h_{d/2}
is the leading coefficient of
h_{\overline{Q_{G}}}(t)
.

Proof.

For the first statement, it is enough to show that
\operatorname{codeg}(P_{G})\geq d/2
by (
2.2
) and Remark
2.5
.

Let
\ell
be a positive integer and assume that
\ell P_{G}^{\circ}\cap\mathbb{Z}^{d}\neq\emptyset
.
Let
\alpha=(\alpha_{v})_{v\in V(G)}\in\ell P_{G}^{\circ}\cap\mathbb{Z}^{d}
.
Then we can write
\alpha
like
\alpha=\sum_{\{u,v\}\in E(G)}a_{u,v}\mathbf{e}_{u,v}
,
where
a_{u,v}>0
for each
\{u,v\}\in E(G)
and
\sum a_{u,v}=\ell
.
Thus, in particular,
\alpha_{u}\geq 1
holds for each
u\in V(G)
since
\alpha\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}
.
Hence,

2\ell=2\sum a_{u,v}=\sum_{v\in V(G)}\alpha_{v}\geq|V(G)|=d.

This means that
\operatorname{codeg}(P_{G})\geq d/2
.

Moreover, by this discussion, if
\operatorname{codeg}(P_{G})=d/2
, then
\alpha
must be
\sum_{v\in V(G)}\mathbf{e}_{u}
. This implies that
h_{d/2}=1
.
∎

3.
Proof of Theorem
1.1

This section is devoted to giving a proof of Theorem
1.1
.

Proof of Theorem
1.1
.

Let
Q\subset\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}^{d}
be a homogeneous affine monoid of dimension
d
.
Then we know the following short exact sequence of graded
\Bbbk[Q]
-modules:

\displaystyle 0\longrightarrow\Bbbk[Q]\longrightarrow\Bbbk[\overline{Q}]\longrightarrow\Bbbk[\overline{Q}]/\Bbbk[Q]\longrightarrow 0

Thus, we have

\displaystyle\operatorname{Hilb}(Q,t)=\operatorname{Hilb}(\overline{Q},t)-\operatorname{Hilb}(\Bbbk[\overline{Q}]/\Bbbk[Q],t).

(3.1)

By the way, Theorem
2.1
describes the structure of
\Bbbk[\overline{Q}]/\Bbbk[Q]
.
Since we assume
(S_{2})
for
\Bbbk[Q]
, we know that each family of holes is of dimension
d-1
(see Theorem
2.3
).
Let us consider the decomposition of
\overline{Q}\setminus Q
in (
2.1
), where
q_{i}\in\overline{Q}
and
F_{i}
is a face of
Q
of dimension
d-1
for each
i
.
Note that this decomposition is not necessarily disjoint. Hence, we have to apply an “inclusion-exclusion type” formula to get the Hilbert series of
\Bbbk[\overline{Q}]/\Bbbk[Q]
.

Here, we recall some materials on hyperplanes arrangements. (See, e.g.,
[
13
]
for the introduction to the theory of hyperplane arrangements.)
Let
H_{i}=q_{i}+\mathbb{R}F_{i}
be an affine hyperplane, and let
\mathcal{A}=\{H_{1},\ldots,H_{\ell}\}
be a hyperplane arrangement.
Let
\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{A})
denote the intersection lattice of
\mathcal{A}
, i.e.,

\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{A})=\left\{\bigcap_{i\in I}H_{i}\neq\emptyset:I\subset\{1,\ldots,\ell\}\right\}

equipped with a partial order defined by reverse inclusion.
We regard
\bigcap_{i\in\emptyset}H_{i}
as
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, i.e.,
\mathbb{R}^{d}\in\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{A})
is the minimal element.
We define a map
\mu:\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{A})\rightarrow\mathbb{Z}
(known as the
Möbius function
) as follows:

\mu(X)=\begin{cases}1,\;&\text{ if }X=\mathbb{R}^{d},\\
-\sum_{X\subsetneq Y}\mu(Y),&\text{ otherwise}.\end{cases}

Then we see from (
2.1
) that

\displaystyle\operatorname{Hilb}(\Bbbk[\overline{Q}]/\Bbbk[Q],t)

\displaystyle=-\sum_{X\in\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{A})\setminus\mathbb{R}^{d}}\mu(X)\frac{t^{a_{i}}h_{\overline{X}}(t)}{(1-t)^{\dim X}}

\displaystyle=-\sum_{X\in\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{A})\setminus\mathbb{R}^{d}}\mu(X)\frac{t^{a_{i}}h_{\overline{X}}(t)(1-t)^{d-\dim X}}{(1-t)^{d}},

where
a_{i}=\operatorname{deg}\mathbf{x}^{q_{i}}
and we let
\overline{X}=X\cap\overline{Q}
. Note that
\overline{X}
is a normal homogeneous submonoid of
\overline{Q}
.
Hence, it follows from (
3.1
) that

h_{Q}(t)=h_{\overline{Q}}(t)+\sum_{X\in\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{A})}(-1)^{d-\dim X}\mu(X)t^{a_{i}}(t-1)^{d-\dim X}h_{\overline{X}}(t).

Since
\overline{X}
is normal, we know that
\Bbbk[\overline{X}]
is Cohen–Macaulay, so
h_{\overline{X}}(t)
has positive coefficients.
Thus, we can conclude the desired conclusion
\operatorname{deg}(h_{Q}(t))\geq\operatorname{deg}(h_{\overline{Q}}(t))
once we check that
(-1)^{d-\dim X}\mu(X)\geq 0
.
Actually, this is known to be true. See, e.g.,
[
13
, Corollary 3.4]
.
∎

Regarding another relationship between
h_{Q}(t)
and
h_{\overline{Q}}(t)
, we know the following:

Proposition 3.1
.

For any homogeneous affine monoid
Q
of dimension
d
,
h_{Q}(1)=h_{\overline{Q}}(1)
holds.

Proof.

Since the structure of
\Bbbk[\overline{Q}]/\Bbbk[Q]
can be captured by the decomposition (
2.1
) and each face
F_{i}
is of dimension at most
d-1
,
we see that

\operatorname{Hilb}(\Bbbk[\overline{Q}]/\Bbbk[Q],t)=\frac{g(t)}{(1-t)^{d-1}},

where
g(t)
is some polynomial in
t
. Then it follows from (
3.1
) that

\displaystyle\operatorname{Hilb}(\Bbbk[\overline{Q}],t)-\operatorname{Hilb}(\Bbbk[Q],t)=\frac{h_{\overline{Q}}(t)-h_{Q}(t)}{(1-t)^{d}}=\frac{(1-t)g(t)}{(1-t)^{d}}.

In particular,
h_{\overline{Q}}(t)-h_{Q}(t)
is divisible by
(1-t)
. This means that
h_{\overline{Q}}(1)=h_{Q}(1)
.
∎

For the proof of Theorem
1.2
, we recall the notion of join for lattice polytopes and apply the same idea to homogeneous affine monoids.
Given two lattice polytopes
P\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
P\subset\mathbb{R}^{d^{\prime}}
, let
P\star P^{\prime}

be te convex hull of

\{(\alpha,{\bf 0}_{d^{\prime}},0):\alpha\in P\}\cup\{({\bf 0}_{d},\alpha,1):\alpha^{\prime}\in P^{\prime}\})\subset\mathbb{R}^{d+d^{\prime}+1}.

We call
P\star P^{\prime}
the
join
of
P
and
P^{\prime}
.

Similarly, given two homogeneous affine monoids
Q\subset\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}^{d}
and
Q^{\prime}\subset\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}^{d^{\prime}}
,
let
\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{s}
(resp.
\alpha_{1}^{\prime},\ldots,\alpha_{s^{\prime}}^{\prime}
) be the minimal generating set of
Q
(resp.
Q^{\prime}
).
We define

Q\star Q^{\prime}=\langle(\alpha_{1},{\bf 0}_{d^{\prime}},0),\ldots,(\alpha_{s},{\bf 0}_{d^{\prime}},0),({\bf 0}_{d},\alpha_{1}^{\prime},1),\ldots,({\bf 0}_{d},\alpha_{s^{\prime}}^{\prime},1)\rangle\subset\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}^{d+d^{\prime}+1}.

Then it is straightforward to see that
Q\star Q^{\prime}
is also a homogeneous affine monoid.
Let us call
Q\star Q^{\prime}
the
join
of homogeneous affine monoids
Q
and
Q^{\prime}
.

Proposition 4.3

(cf.
[
5
, Lemma 1.3]
)
.

Given two homogeneous affine monoids
Q\subset\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}^{d}
and
Q^{\prime}\subset\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}^{d^{\prime}}
,
we have

\operatorname{Hilb}(Q\star Q^{\prime},t)=\operatorname{Hilb}(Q,t)\cdot\operatorname{Hilb}(Q^{\prime},t)

and

\operatorname{Hilb}(\overline{Q\star Q^{\prime}},t)=\operatorname{Hilb}(\overline{Q},t)\cdot\operatorname{Hilb}(\overline{Q^{\prime}},t).

Proof.

Let
P
(resp.
P^{\prime}
) be the cross section polytope of
Q
(resp.
Q^{\prime}
).
Then that of
Q\star Q^{\prime}
is nothing but
P\star P^{\prime}
since the hyperplnae containing the generators of
Q\star Q^{\prime}
is defined by

b^{\prime}\sum_{i=1}^{d}a_{i}x_{i}+b\sum_{i=1}^{d^{\prime}}a_{i}^{\prime}x_{d+i}=bb^{\prime}
, where
\sum_{i=1}^{d}a_{i}x_{i}=b
(resp.
\sum_{i=1}^{d^{\prime}}a_{i}^{\prime}x_{i}=b^{\prime}
)
is the hyperplane containing the generators of
Q
(resp.
Q^{\prime}
).
Hence, the latter equality of the statement is directly obtained from
[
5
, Lemma 1.3]
.

For the former, similarly to the proof of
[
5
, Lemma 1.3]
, it suffices to show that

\dim_{\Bbbk}(\Bbbk[Q\star Q^{\prime}]_{k})=\sum_{i+j=k}\dim_{\Bbbk}(\Bbbk[Q])_{i}\dim_{\Bbbk}(\Bbbk[Q^{\prime}])_{j},

but this directly follows from the description of
Q\star Q^{\prime}
as follows: for each
k\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
,

\displaystyle(Q\star Q^{\prime})

\displaystyle\cap\left\{b^{\prime}\sum_{i=1}^{d}a_{i}x_{i}+b\sum_{i=1}^{d^{\prime}}a_{i}^{\prime}x_{d+i}=bb^{\prime}k\right\}

\displaystyle=\left\{m(\alpha,{\bf 0}_{d^{\prime}},0)+(k-m)({\bf 0}_{d},\alpha^{\prime},1):\alpha\in Q,\alpha^{\prime}\in Q^{\prime},0\leq m\leq k,m\in\mathbb{Z}\right\}.

∎

Now, we are in the position to give a proof of Theorem
1.2
.

Proof of Theorem
1.2
.

Given a positive integer
m
, let

Q^{(m)}=\underbrace{Q\star\cdots\star Q}_{m\text{ times}},

where
Q
is the same homogeneous affine monoid as in Example

It then follows from Proposition

that
\operatorname{Hilb}(Q^{(m)},t)=\operatorname{Hilb}(Q,t)^{m}
and
\operatorname{Hilb}(\overline{Q^{(m)}},t)=\operatorname{Hilb}(\overline{Q},t)^{m}
.
Hence,
\operatorname{deg}(h_{\overline{Q^{(m)}}}(t))-\operatorname{deg}(h_{Q^{(m)}}(t))=m\left(\operatorname{deg}(h_{\overline{Q}}(t))-\operatorname{deg}(h_{Q}(t))\right)=m

∎

Remark 4.4
.

The difference of the degrees of
h_{Q}(t)
and
h_{\overline{Q}}(t)
can be arbitrarily large.
For the case
\operatorname{deg}(h_{Q}(t))<\operatorname{deg}(h_{\overline{Q}}(t))
, this is just what Theorem
1.2
claims.
For the case
\operatorname{deg}(h_{Q}(t))\geq\operatorname{deg}(h_{\overline{Q}}(t))
, we can find such examples, e.g., in
[
11
, Theorem A]
.
In fact, for any nonnegative integer
m
, let

R_{m}:=\langle(0,2m+3),(m+1,m+2),(m+2,m+1),(2m+3,0)\rangle\subset\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}^{2}.

Then
\Bbbk[R_{m}]
is nearly Gorenstein with Cohen–Macaulay type
2
(see
[
11
, Lemma A (
\alpha
)]
).
Moreover, we also see that the projective dimension of
\Bbbk[R_{m}]
is
2
.
Thus, by
[
10
, Theorem 4.10]
, we know that
\displaystyle\operatorname{Hilb}(R_{m},t)=\frac{1+2\sum_{i=1}^{s}t^{i}}{(1-t)^{2}}
for some
s
.
In particular,
h_{R_{m}}(t)=1+2\sum_{i=1}^{s}t^{i}
.
On the other hand, since
\overline{R_{m}}=\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}^{2}\cap\{(x,y):x+y\in(2m+3)\mathbb{Z}\}
,
we also see that
\Bbbk[\overline{R_{m}}]\cong\Bbbk[X,Y]^{(2m+3)}
, where
\Bbbk[X,Y]^{(n)}
stands for the
n
-th Veronese subring of
\Bbbk[X,Y]
.
In particular,
\displaystyle\operatorname{Hilb}(\overline{R_{m}},t)=\frac{1+(2m+2)t}{(1-t)^{2}}
.
Since
h_{R_{m}}(1)=h_{\overline{R_{m}}}(1)=2m+3
(see Proposition
3.1
), we obtain that
s=m+1
.
Therefore,
\operatorname{deg}(h_{R_{m}}(t))-\operatorname{deg}(h_{\overline{R_{m}}}(t))=m
holds, as desired.

Spin-conserving Boltzmann theory for carriers and excitons in organic semiconductors
Charlotte Bäcker, Linus Thummel, Carsten Timm

Abstract
The rise of organic electronics calls for versatile modeling tools. In this context, we develop a semiclassical Boltzmann theory that describes transport and excitonic processes in crystalline organic semiconductors on equal footing. The generation of singlet and triplet excitons out of the ground state, their formation from free electrons and holes, the reverse processes, as well as the fusion and fission of excitons are included. The corresponding scattering integrals respect spin conservation, which requires matrix-valued distribution functions. They also include fermionic and bosonic many-particle effects such as Pauli blocking. We employ a multipole expansion of the distribution functions, where quadrupolar terms turn out to be essential for the triplet excitons. This work provides a basis for the modeling of organic solar cells, in which excitonic processes are crucial for the performance. Moreover, the theory is of general interest for transport and transitions of multiple (quasi-) particle species carrying spin in nonequilibrium systems.

We aim at a unified description of transport and the aforementioned local excitonic processes. A relevant property of organic materials is the typically weak spin-orbit coupling, which ensures that spin is conserved to a good approximation. The description of transport thus has to respect spin rotation symmetry, in particular if singlet and triplet excitons are involved. We note that any violation of spin conservation, i.e., any relaxation of spin, can easily be incorporated by an additional scattering integral.

The excitons in typical devices are far from equilibrium. The quantum master equation constitutes a powerful tool for the description of nonequilibrium processes in quantum systems
BrP02
. It is the equation of motion for the reduced density matrix or statistical operator
\widehat{\rho}
of an open system. This equation is linear in the density matrix since it is derived from the Schrödinger equation, which is linear in the state vector. Linearity is necessary to preserve the unit trace of the density matrix. However, one can relax this trace condition and interpret
\Tr\widehat{\rho}
as a concentration of quasiparticles. The matrix structure of
\widehat{\rho}
then encodes the distribution of spin orientations, where
\widehat{\rho}
is a
(2s+1)\times(2s+1)
matrix for spin-
s

A generalized quantum master equation in this sense was applied to local excitonic processes, such as fission and fusion, in organic crystals as early as 1969 by Johnson and Merrifield
JoM70
. Essentially the same equation was proposed, under the name of
stochastic Liouville equation
, in the context of quantum optics
ScL67

;
Hab76
;
StU89

et al.

SWK11

In case of the stochastic Liouville equation, the nonlinearity is given by a
\widehat{\rho}

;
ScL67
;
SWK11

\frac{\partial\widehat{\rho}}{\partial t}=-\frac{i}{\hbar}\,\big[\widehat{H},\widehat{\rho}\big]-\frac{W}{2}\,\big\{\widehat{P},\widehat{\rho}\big\}+\widehat{\Gamma}.

(1)

The first term on the right-hand side describes the unitary time evolution determined by the Hamiltonian
\widehat{H}
. The second term implements decay processes and is naturally linear in the density. Here
W
is a decay rate and
\widehat{P}
is a projection operator onto the spin state from which a decay is allowed by spin conservation. The specific anticommutator structure was shown by Haberkorn

\widehat{\rho}
—since the eigenvalues of
\widehat{\rho}
are interpreted as concentrations they must not become negative
note.Haberkorn
. The third term describes generation processes and is of order zero in
\widehat{\rho}
since the generation rate is assumed to be independent of the density.

The interpretation of
\widehat{\rho}
as a density also permits terms of higher order. Consider, for example, a pair-annihilation process of the type
A+A\to 0
. For spinless particles,
\widehat{\rho}
just becomes the number density
n
and the process is naturally described by the equation

\frac{\partial n}{\partial t}=-c\,n^{2}.

(2)

This is of course well known in chemical kinetics
McQ67
;
nonlinear
. Nonlinear terms also result from quantum statistics, which show that the rate of a scattering process also depends on the occupation of the final state, e.g., due to the Pauli principle.

The question arises what the corresponding equation looks like for particles with spin. The right-hand side then contains a product of the matrix
\widehat{\rho}
with itself and the question is how this product must be constructed so that spin is conserved. More complicated processes involving multiple species, e.g.,
A+B\to C
, can be described using multiple matrix-valued densities and we have to construct spin-conserving terms out of matrices of generally different sizes
(2s+1)\times(2s+1)
. This issue is central for the description of excitonic processes. For spin-
1/2
quasiparticles, i.e., electrons and holes, the matrix-valued densities are
2\times 2
matrices acting on their spin Hilbert space. For triplet excitons with spin
1
, they are
3\times 3
matrices, whereas for singlet excitons they reduce to scalars.

We combine the spin-rotation-invariant description of local excitonic processes with a spin-rotation-invariant description of carrier and exciton transport. We are interested in bandlike transport in clean organic materials at not very low temperatures. Characteristic quantum effects such as weak localization and universal conductance fluctuations are thus expected to be irrelevant and a semiclassical description is justified. Our goals thus require the derivation of
\mathrm{SU}(2)
spin-rotation-invariant Boltzmann-type kinetic equations for the various quasiparticle species and of scattering integrals describing transitions between them.

As noted above, polaronic effects are typically strong in organic materials

;

;

;

et al.

This suggests that a semiclassical description should be possible, where the effect of the slow modes is incorporated by scattering integrals. Since polaronic effects are not at the focus of the present paper we assume that the polarons are adiabatically connected to the undressed quasiparticles, i.e., that polaronic effects only renormalize the parameters appearing in our description but do not change its structure.

We emphasize that it is not sufficient to use kinetic equations for the densities in each spin channel, say spin-up and spin-down electrons, since this is equivalent to assuming the matrix-valued densities to be diagonal in the standard spin basis. This precludes the description of spin polarization in the

Our program is of general interest beyond the field of organic electronics as the principles developed here are useful for any system showing scattering of and reactions between spin-carrying particles, for example in spintronics and spin chemistry.

II
Spin-conserving kinetic equations

In this section, we present the Boltzmann transport equations for electrons, holes, and singlet and triplet excitons including static, uniform electric and magnetic fields. We concentrate on the drift terms conventionally appearing on the left-hand side of the Boltzmann equation and leave the collision terms for Sec.
III
.

Following Ref.
QiZ03
, we write the general Boltzmann equation for the density
\widehat{f}(\bm{r},\bm{k},t)
as

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\,\widehat{f}(\bm{r},\bm{k},t)+\bm{v}(\bm{k})\cdot\Nabla_{\bm{r}}\widehat{f}(\bm{r},\bm{k},t)

\displaystyle\quad{}+\frac{q}{\hbar}\left(\bm{E}+\bm{v}(\bm{k})\times\bm{B}\right)\cdot\Nabla_{\bm{k}}\widehat{f}(\bm{r},\bm{k},t)

\displaystyle\quad{}+\frac{i}{\hbar}\big[\widehat{H}(\bm{r},\bm{k},t),\widehat{f}(\bm{r},\bm{k},t)\big]=\widehat{I}[\widehat{f}],

(3)

where
q\in\{-e,0,e\}
is the charge of the quasiparticles under consideration and the hat
\widehat{\bullet}
denotes matrices acting on spin space. The density
\widehat{f}(\bm{r},\bm{k},t)
is a
(2s+1)\times(2s+1)
matrix, where
s
is the quasiparticle spin. Note that for
s>0
, the matrix structure leads to an additional commutator term, which describes the dynamics in spin space
QiZ03
. For example, for spin-
1/2
quasiparticles in a magnetic field
\bm{B}
, the Hamiltonian
\widehat{H}
contains a Zeeman term proportional to
\bm{B}\cdot\widehat{\mbox{\boldmath$\sigma$}}
, where
\widehat{\mbox{\boldmath$\sigma$}}
is the vector of Pauli matrices
\widehat{\sigma}_{1}
,
\widehat{\sigma}_{2}
,
\widehat{\sigma}_{3}
. We suppress the arguments
\bm{r}
,
\bm{k}
, and
t
from now on.
\widehat{I}[\widehat{f}]
contains all scattering integrals affecting the distribution.

Since we will later consider nonlinear collision integrals, the normalization of
\widehat{f}
is important. We take the eigenvalues of
\widehat{f}
to be the occupation numbers per quantum state. For example, the density

\widehat{f}_{e}=\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\
0&1\end{pmatrix}

(4)

for all
\bm{r}
and
\bm{k}
describes a filled electron band. This convention will be useful for the description of Fermi and Bose statistics in the collision integrals. As noted above,
\Tr\widehat{f}
is not restricted to unity and
\widehat{f}
is not a density matrix in the sense of a statistical operator
SWK11
.

The velocity in Eq. (
3
) is given by the derivative of the Hamiltonian acting on spin space with respect to momentum,

\bm{v}=\frac{1}{\hbar}\,\frac{\partial\widehat{H}}{\partial\bm{k}},

(5)

and thus, in principle, obtains a matrix structure. This structure is made nontrivial by those terms in the Hamiltonian that contain both the momentum and the spin, i.e., by spin-orbit coupling. Since we are primarily interested in organic materials, which show weak spin-orbit coupling, we neglect them. The velocity
\bm{v}
then becomes proportional to the identity matrix and can be treated as a scalar
note.soc
. The dispersion relation is the only relevant momentum-dependent term in the Hamiltonian so that we can express
\bm{v}
as the group velocity

\bm{v}=\frac{1}{\hbar}\bm{\nabla}_{\bm{k}}\epsilon(\bm{k})

(6)

in terms of the quasiparticle dispersion
\epsilon(\bm{k})
.

II.1
Electrons

The charge carriers in organic semiconductors are electrons and holes. For simplicity, we restrict ourselves to a model with one valence band and one conduction band; the generalization to multiple bands is straightforward. The spin
1/2
of the electrons and holes is essential to understand the related excitonic processes.

The identity matrix
\widehat{\sigma}_{0}
and the three Pauli matrices
\widehat{\sigma}_{x}
,
\widehat{\sigma}_{y}
, and
\widehat{\sigma}_{z}
form a basis of the space of Hermitian
2\times 2
matrices. Hence, for spin-
1/2
quasiparticles, the
2\times 2
matrix
\widehat{f}
can be expanded as

\widehat{f}=\frac{1}{2}\left(n\,\widehat{\sigma}_{0}+\bm{c}\cdot\widehat{\bm{\sigma}}\right).

(7)

Then
n=\Tr\widehat{f}
is the concentration of quasiparticles, summed over spin orientations, and
\bm{c}=\Tr\widehat{f}\,\widehat{\bm{\sigma}}
is twice the spin density (setting
\hbar=1
). Note that
\widehat{f}
must be Hermitian to ensure real densities. We will call relations of the form of Eq. (
7
)
multipole expansions
.

We start with the description of electrons in the conduction band with the Hamiltonian

\widehat{H}_{e}=\widehat{H}_{e0}+\frac{1}{2}\,g_{\text{el}}\mu_{B}\bm{B}\cdot\widehat{\bm{\sigma}},

(8)

with the electronic Landé factor
g_{\text{el}}
and the Bohr magneton
\mu_{B}
. Neglecting spin-orbit coupling, the bare electron Hamiltonian is
\widehat{H}_{e0}=\epsilon_{e}(\bm{k})\,\widehat{\sigma}_{0}
, with the dispersion
\epsilon_{e}(\bm{k})
. The Zeeman term remains the only part with nontrivial matrix structure.

We decompose the electronic distribution function into the equilibrium distribution
\widehat{{f}}_{e0}
and a deviation
\widehat{{g}}_{e}
according to

\widehat{{f}}_{e}=\widehat{{f}}_{e0}+\widehat{{g}}_{e}.

(9)

We assume the distribution function to be close to equilibrium because the equilibrium distribution is perturbed only little by (a) a weak electric field
\bm{E}
and (b) decomposition of a low concentration of excitons. The weak electric field also means that terms involving the product of
\bm{E}
and
\widehat{{g}}_{e}
are small of higher order and can be neglected. This is the assumption of linear response. It would be straightforward to drop this approximation.

The multipole expansion of the deviation is

\widehat{{g}}_{e}=\frac{1}{2}\left(\tilde{n}_{e}\widehat{\sigma}_{0}+\tilde{\bm{c}}_{e}\cdot\widehat{\bm{\sigma}}\right),

(10)

similar to Eq. (
7
). In equilibrium, the distribution is spatially uniform since the external fields and the scattering term are assumed not to depend on position
\bm{r}
, and can thus be written as

\widehat{{f}}_{e0}=\frac{1}{2}\left(n_{e0}\,\widehat{\sigma}_{0}+c_{e0}\bm{B}\cdot\widehat{\bm{\sigma}}\right).

(11)

This yields

\widehat{{f}}_{e}=\frac{1}{2}\,\big[(n_{e0}+\tilde{n}_{e})\,\widehat{\sigma}_{0}+(c_{e0}\bm{B}+\tilde{\bm{c}}_{e})\cdot\widehat{\bm{\sigma}}\big].

(12)

The equilibrium distribution can be derived starting from the specific case of
\bm{B}\parallel\bm{e}_{3}
, where
\bm{e}_{3}
is the unit vector in the
z
direction. Here the Hamiltonian takes a diagonal form and so does the equilibrium function. It is thus given by a linear combination of
\widehat{\sigma}_{0}
and
B\,\widehat{\sigma}_{z}
. The rotation to an arbitrary direction of the magnetic field is then described by the transformation
B\,\widehat{\sigma}_{z}\rightarrow\bm{B}\cdot\widehat{\bm{\sigma}}
. The induced magnetization is along
\bm{B}
since we assume a magnetically isotropic medium
note.nonlinear
. The equilibrium term
n_{e0}
corresponds to the Fermi-Dirac distribution of electrons with energy
\epsilon_{e}(\bm{k})
, chemical potential
\mu
, and temperature
T
,

n_{e0}(\bm{k})=\frac{1}{e^{(\epsilon_{e}(\bm{k})-\mu)/k_{B}T}+1}.

(13)

In the linear-response approximation, the Boltzmann equation for the electrons reads as

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\,\widehat{{g}}_{e}+\frac{1}{\hbar}\,\bm{\nabla}_{\bm{k}}\epsilon_{e}\cdot\bm{\nabla}_{\bm{r}}\widehat{{g}}_{e}-\frac{e}{\hbar}\left(\bm{E}+\frac{1}{\hbar}\,\bm{\nabla}_{\bm{k}}\epsilon_{e}\times\bm{B}\right)\cdot\bm{\nabla}_{\bm{k}}\widehat{{f}}_{e0}

\displaystyle\quad{}-\frac{e}{\hbar^{2}}\left(\bm{\nabla}_{\bm{k}}\epsilon_{e}\times\bm{B}\right)\cdot\bm{\nabla}_{\bm{k}}\widehat{{g}}_{e}+\frac{i}{\hbar}\,\big[\widehat{H}_{e},\widehat{{f}}_{e0}\big]+\frac{i}{\hbar}\,\big[\widehat{H}_{e},\widehat{{g}}_{e}\big]

\displaystyle=2\widehat{I}_{e}\big[f_{s},\widehat{{f}}_{e},\widehat{{f}}_{h},\widehat{{f}}_{t}\big],

(14)

where the factor of 2 is due to the factor of
1/2
in Eq. (
7
) and drops out when the scattering term is also expanded. We now analyze the individual expressions in the multipole expansion.
The derivative

\bm{\nabla}_{\bm{k}}\widehat{{f}}_{e0}=\frac{n_{e0}^{2}-n_{e0}}{2k_{B}T}\,\bm{\nabla}_{\bm{k}}\epsilon_{e}\widehat{\sigma}_{0}+\frac{1}{2}\,\bm{\nabla}_{\bm{k}}c_{e0}\bm{B}\cdot\widehat{\bm{\sigma}}

(15)

can be used to simplify the expression

\left(\bm{\nabla}_{\bm{k}}\epsilon_{e}\times\bm{B}\right)\cdot\bm{\nabla}_{\bm{k}}\widehat{{f}}_{e0}=\frac{1}{2}\,(\bm{\nabla}_{\bm{k}}\epsilon_{e}\times\bm{B})\cdot(\bm{\nabla}_{\bm{k}}c_{e0})\bm{B}\cdot\widehat{\bm{\sigma}}.

(16)

The commutator terms are

\displaystyle\frac{i}{\hbar}\big[\widehat{H}_{e},\widehat{f}_{e0}\big]

\displaystyle=0,

(17)

\displaystyle\frac{i}{\hbar}\big[\widehat{H}_{e},\widehat{g}_{e}\big]

\displaystyle=-\frac{g_{\text{el}}\mu_{B}}{2\hbar}\,(\bm{B}\times\bm{c}_{e})\cdot\widehat{\bm{\sigma}},

(18)

where the latter expression describes the precession of the spin around the direction of the magnetic field.

Inserting these expressions into the Boltzmann equation (
14
) gives

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\,\tilde{n}_{e}\widehat{\sigma}_{0}+\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\,\tilde{\bm{c}}_{e}\cdot\widehat{\bm{\sigma}}

\displaystyle\quad{}+\frac{1}{\hbar}\,\bm{\nabla}_{\bm{k}}\epsilon_{e}\cdot\left[\bm{\nabla}_{\bm{r}}\tilde{n}_{e}\widehat{\sigma}_{0}+\bm{\nabla}_{\bm{r}}(\tilde{\bm{c}}_{e}\cdot\widehat{\bm{\sigma}})\right]

\displaystyle\quad{}-\frac{e}{\hbar}\,\bm{E}\cdot\big[\bm{\nabla}_{\bm{k}}n_{e0}\widehat{\sigma}_{0}+\bm{\nabla}_{\bm{k}}c_{e0}\bm{B}\cdot\widehat{\bm{\sigma}}\big]

\displaystyle\quad{}-\frac{e}{\hbar^{2}}\left(\bm{\nabla}_{\bm{k}}\epsilon_{e}\times\bm{B}\right)\cdot\bm{\nabla}_{\bm{k}}c_{e0}\bm{B}\cdot\widehat{\bm{\sigma}}

\displaystyle\quad{}-\frac{e}{\hbar^{2}}\left(\bm{\nabla}_{\bm{k}}\epsilon_{e}\times\bm{B}\right)\cdot\left[\bm{\nabla}_{\bm{k}}\tilde{n}_{e}\widehat{\sigma}_{0}+\bm{\nabla}_{\bm{k}}(\tilde{\bm{c}}_{e}\cdot\widehat{\bm{\sigma}})\right]

\displaystyle\quad{}-\frac{g_{\text{el}}\mu_{B}}{\hbar}\left(\bm{B}\times\bm{c}_{e}\right)\cdot\widehat{\bm{\sigma}}

\displaystyle=\widehat{I}_{e}\big[f_{s},\widehat{{f}}_{e},\widehat{{f}}_{h},\widehat{{f}}_{t}\big].

(19)

It is useful to also expand the scattering term into multipole components, in analogy to Eq. (
7
),

\displaystyle\widehat{I}_{e}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\left(I^{n_{e}}\,\widehat{\sigma}_{0}+\bm{I}^{\bm{c}_{e}}\cdot\widehat{\bm{\sigma}}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\left(I^{n_{e}}\,\widehat{\sigma}_{0}+I^{c_{e}^{x}}\,\widehat{\sigma}_{x}+I^{c_{e}^{y}}\,\widehat{\sigma}_{y}+I^{c_{e}^{z}}\,\widehat{\sigma}_{z}\right).

(20)

From this, we can obtain equations for the coefficients of the basis matrices. The notation can be shortened using the differential operator

\mathcal{D}_{e}=\frac{\partial}{\partial t}+\frac{1}{\hbar}\,\bm{\nabla}_{\bm{k}}\epsilon_{e}\cdot\bm{\nabla}_{\bm{r}}-\frac{e}{\hbar^{2}}\left(\bm{\nabla}_{\bm{k}}\epsilon_{e}\times\bm{B}\right)\cdot\bm{\nabla}_{\bm{k}}.

(21)

For the coefficient of
\widehat{\sigma}_{0}
, we obtain

\mathcal{D}_{e}\tilde{n}_{e}-\frac{e}{\hbar}\,\bm{E}\cdot\bm{\nabla}_{\bm{k}}n_{e0}=I^{n_{e}}[f_{s},\widehat{{f}}_{e},\widehat{{f}}_{h},\widehat{{f}}_{t}]

(22)

and for the coefficients of
\widehat{\sigma}_{i}
with
i=1,2,3
,

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}_{e}\tilde{c}_{ei}-\frac{e}{\hbar}\left(\bm{E}+\frac{1}{\hbar}\,\bm{\nabla}_{\bm{k}}\epsilon_{e}\times\bm{B}\right)\cdot\bm{\nabla}_{\bm{k}}c_{e0}B_{i}

\displaystyle\quad{}-\frac{g_{\text{el}}\mu_{B}}{\hbar}\left(\bm{B}\times\tilde{\bm{c}}_{e}\right)_{i}=I^{c_{e}^{i}}[f_{s},\widehat{{f}}_{e},\widehat{{f}}_{h},\widehat{{f}}_{t}].

(23)

II.2
Holes

The description of holes in the valence band is analogous. Missing electrons close to the band top are described in terms of holes with positive mass and positive charge. The Hamiltonian for the holes reads as

\widehat{H}_{h}=\epsilon_{h}(\bm{k})\widehat{\sigma}_{0}-\frac{1}{2}\,g_{\textrm{ho}}\mu_{B}\bm{B}\cdot\widehat{\bm{\sigma}},

(24)

where
g_{\mathrm{ho}}>0
is the hole Landé factor.

The matrix-valued distribution function
\widehat{{f}}_{h}
of the holes is decomposed into the equilibrium distribution and a deviation,

\widehat{{f}}_{h}=\widehat{{f}}_{h0}+\widehat{{g}}_{h}.

(25)

The contributions are expanded according to

\displaystyle\widehat{{f}}_{h0}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\left(n_{h0}\,\widehat{\sigma}_{0}+\bm{c}_{h0}\bm{B}\cdot\widehat{\bm{\sigma}}\right),

(26)

\displaystyle\widehat{{g}}_{h}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\left(\tilde{n}_{h}\widehat{\sigma}_{0}+\tilde{\bm{c}}_{h}\cdot\widehat{\bm{\sigma}}\right).

(27)

The kinetic equations for the multipole coefficients
\tilde{n}_{h}
and
\tilde{\bm{c}}_{h}
can then be written down in analogy to the electronic case by replacing subscripts “
e
” by “
h
”,
e
by
-e
, and
g_{\mathrm{el}}
by
-g_{\mathrm{ho}}
. Defining the differential operator

\mathcal{D}_{h}=\frac{\partial}{\partial t}+\frac{1}{\hbar}\,\bm{\nabla}_{\bm{k}}\epsilon_{h}\cdot\bm{\nabla}_{\bm{r}}+\frac{e}{\hbar^{2}}\left(\bm{\nabla}_{\bm{k}}\epsilon_{h}\times\bm{B}\right)\cdot\bm{\nabla}_{\bm{k}},

(28)

we obtain

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}_{h}\tilde{n}_{h}+\frac{e}{\hbar}\,\bm{E}\cdot\bm{\nabla}_{\bm{k}}n_{h0}=I^{n_{h}}[f_{s},\widehat{{f}}_{e},\widehat{{f}}_{h},\widehat{{f}}_{t}],

(29)

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}_{h}\tilde{c}_{hi}+\frac{e}{\hbar}\left(\bm{E}+\frac{1}{\hbar}\,\bm{\nabla}_{\bm{k}}\epsilon_{h}\times\bm{B}\right)\cdot\bm{\nabla}_{\bm{k}}\bm{c}_{h0}B_{i}

\displaystyle\quad{}+\frac{g_{\text{ho}}\mu_{B}}{\hbar}\left(\bm{B}\times\tilde{\bm{c}}_{h}\right)_{i}=I^{c_{h}^{i}}[f_{s},\widehat{{f}}_{e},\widehat{{f}}_{h},\widehat{{f}}_{t}].

(30)

II.3
Singlet excitons

Singlet excitons are spin-
0
quasiparticles. Consequently, the density
f_{s}
and Hamiltonian
H_{s}
are scalars so that the commutator term in Eq. (
3
) vanishes. This simplifies the Boltzmann equation to

\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\,f_{s}+\frac{1}{\hbar}\,\bm{\nabla}_{\bm{k}}\epsilon_{s}\cdot\bm{\nabla}_{\bm{r}}f_{s}=I_{s}\big[f_{s},\widehat{{f}}_{e},\widehat{{f}}_{h},\widehat{{f}}_{t}\big].

(31)

Again, we use the group velocity with regard to the dispersion relation of the singlet excitons,
\epsilon_{s}(\bm{k})
. The description of excitons by a band structure
\epsilon_{s}(\bm{k})
and an associated velocity has a long history
Wan37
;
Dre56
;
Kan75
and has also been applied to organic semiconductors
Dav71
;
VrS03
;
NPT21
. The excitonic band structure in pentacene has been studied experimentally by Schuster
et al.

SKB07
. One should note that the band width and velocity of excitons can be larger than the ones of the charge carriers since the motion of excitons can take place through pure energy (Förster) transfer, without tunneling of electrons.

Similarly to the linear-response theory for electrons, we decompose the distribution function into an equilibrium distribution
f_{s0}
and a deviation
g_{s}
,

f_{s}=f_{s0}+g_{s}.

(32)

However, here the deviation is not related to the electric field and is not necessarily small since we also consider situations with optically excited excitons far from equilibrium. We assume
f_{s0}
to be uniform. In equilibrium, excitons in semiconductors follow the Bose-Einstein distribution

f_{0}(\epsilon)=\frac{1}{e^{(\epsilon-\mu)/k_{B}T}-1},

(33)

where
\epsilon
is the exciton energy and
\mu
is their chemical potential
MoM00
. Inserting Eq. (
32
) into Eq. (
31
) gives

\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\,g_{s}+\frac{1}{\hbar}\,\bm{\nabla}_{\bm{k}}\epsilon_{s}\cdot\bm{\nabla}_{\bm{r}}g_{s}=I_{s}\big[f_{s},\widehat{{f}}_{e},\widehat{{f}}_{h},\widehat{{f}}_{t}\big].

(34)

The scalar form of the Boltzmann equation for singlet excitons allows one to obtain the solutions analytically. This requires specific assumptions for the scattering term. In a simple case, we can neglect interactions with other particle species and use the relaxation-time approximation,
I_{s}[f_{s}]=-R_{s}\,(f_{s}-f_{s0})=-R_{s}\,g_{s}
, to describe the scattering.
R_{s}
is a possibly momentum-dependent relaxation rate. This transforms the Boltzmann equation into a homogeneous partial differential equation for
g_{s}
,

\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\,g_{s}+\frac{1}{\hbar}\,\bm{\nabla}_{\bm{k}}\epsilon_{s}\cdot\bm{\nabla}_{\bm{r}}g_{s}+R_{s}g_{s}=0.

(35)

It is of first order and the coefficients are constant for fixed
\bm{k}
. The analytical solution can be derived using Fourier transformation with respect to
\bm{r}
. The result is

g_{s}=g_{s}^{0}\,e^{-R_{s}t}\int\frac{d^{3}q}{(2\pi)^{3}}\>\exp\left(i\bm{q}\cdot\left[\bm{r}-\frac{1}{\hbar}\,\bm{\nabla}_{\bm{k}}\epsilon_{s}t\right]\right),

(36)

with the integration constant
g_{s}^{0}
. This equation expresses an exponential dampening of the deviation that is superimposed onto a ballistic divergence in position space. This dynamics agrees with the expected behavior. We can take this as a confirmation of the form of the Boltzmann equation derived above.

In this section, we construct spin-conserving scattering integrals for the Boltzmann equations for electrons, holes, and excitons. These scattering integrals should not only describe collisions between quasiparticles and scattering off disorder but, importantly, also generation, decay, and transitions between excitons. An overview of the processes studied here is given in Table
1
. The selection and order of these processes as well as the presentation in this section are partly pedagogical. We progress from conceptually simpler to more complicated cases, where essentially every subsection introduces an additional aspect, usually having to do with conservation of spin or indistinguishability of quasiparticles.
Additional processes can be treated in an analogous manner, e.g., the reverse of some of the processes in Table
1
and processes for holes instead of electrons. Which processes are important of course depends on specific materials. Disorder scattering is expected to be always present. Bipolar semiconductors in which the carrier energies are sufficiently high in comparison to the exciton binding energies will also show electron-hole binding and unbinding,
e+h\leftrightarrow s
or
e+h\leftrightarrow t
. For systems with photogenerated singlet excitons, e.g., photovoltaic devices, the fission
s\to 2t
and the fusion
2t\to s
are crucial, as noted in Sec.
I
. Note that the derivations in this section do not require any quasiparticle species to be close to equilibrium.

Scattering off disorder is described by collision integrals that are linear in the distribution function
Zim72
;
AsM76
. Possanner and Negulescu
PoN11
have studied such terms for both spin-conserving and spin-flip scattering, from a mathematical point of view. El Hajj
Haj14
has studied spin-diffusion models derived from the resulting linear matrix Boltzmann equation in detail.

Our main interest is in the description of transitions between quasiparticle species. The required collision integrals are necessarily nonlinear. Quantum statistics imply that the transition rates depend on the occupation of the final states, contrary to what is assumed in the stochastic Liouville equation. For fermions, complete occupation of the final state prevents a process due to the Pauli principle, whereas for bosons, a large occupation of the final state enhances the rate. In the context of excitonic processes, this has been studied by Bisquert
Bis08
, albeit not using an
\mathrm{SU}(2)
-invariant formalism.

It is useful to first outline the principles behind the construction of the collision integrals. Generation, decay, and transitions between excitons involve quasiparticles on different energy levels. This is reminiscent of laser theory, in which transition rates are derived by considering the occupation of the initial and final states. However, unlike in laser theory, these transition rates depend on momentum. Thus, they take a form similar to the basic scattering term in Boltzmann theory,

\displaystyle I(\bm{k})

\displaystyle=\int\frac{d^{3}k^{\prime}}{(2\pi)^{3}}\,W(\bm{k}^{\prime},\bm{k})\,f(\bm{k}^{\prime})\,[1-f(\bm{k})]

\displaystyle\quad{}-\int\frac{d^{3}k^{\prime}}{(2\pi)^{3}}\,W(\bm{k},\bm{k}^{\prime})\,f(\bm{k})\,[1-f(\bm{k}^{\prime})],

(67)

where
f
is a distribution function. The two integrals describe in-scattering and out-scattering, respectively. The processes considered here are more complicated in a number of ways. First, our Boltzmann equations contain matrix-valued density functions. The idea is to derive the matrix form from the special case of
z
-polarized particles by means of a rotation into a general direction, like discussed for the kinetic term in Sec.
II
.

Second, the initial and final states can consist of more than one particle. We assume the initial state
A
to contain particles of species
a_{1},a_{2},\ldots
(some of which may be the same) and the final state
B
to contain particles of species
b_{1},b_{2},\ldots
(some of which may be the same). The integrand of the scattering integral is then proportional to

N_{a_{1}}N_{a_{2}}\cdots(1\pm N_{b_{1}})(1\pm N_{b_{2}})\cdots,

(68)

where
N_{a_{1}}
etc. are the occupation numbers of states and the sign in factors
1\pm N_{b}
is
+
(
-
) if
b
is bosonic (fermionic). Considering the limit
N_{b}\to 0
yields the classical part of the process. For low concentrations of quasiparticles in the final state, in particular low exciton densities, this case is relevant for applications.

In the following, we will progress from simple to more complicated cases. To fix the notation, we first discuss the essentially trivial case of elastic scattering of electrons off nonmagnetic impurities. It will be beneficial to write the matrix-valued distribution function of the electrons in components,

\widehat{f}_{e}=\begin{pmatrix}f_{e\uparrow\uparrow}&f_{e\uparrow\downarrow}\\
f_{e\downarrow\uparrow}&f_{e\downarrow\downarrow}\end{pmatrix}.

(69)

Since nonmagnetic scattering does not flip the spin, the matrix-valued collision integral appearing in the Boltzmann equation for
\widehat{f}_{e}
is diagonal,

\widehat{I}^{\widehat{f}_{e}}_{e\to e}=\begin{pmatrix}I^{e\uparrow}_{e\to e}&0\\
0&I^{e\downarrow}_{e\to e}\end{pmatrix}.

(70)

The collision integral for spin-up electrons can be written as
Zim72
;
AsM76

\displaystyle I^{e\uparrow}_{e\to e}(\bm{k})

\displaystyle=\int\frac{d^{3}k^{\prime}}{(2\pi)^{3}}\,W_{e^{\prime}\to e}(\bm{k}^{\prime},\bm{k})\,f_{e\uparrow}(\bm{k}^{\prime})\,[1-f_{e\uparrow}(\bm{k})]

\displaystyle\quad{}-\int\frac{d^{3}k^{\prime}}{(2\pi)^{3}}\,W_{e\to e^{\prime}}(\bm{k},\bm{k}^{\prime})\,f_{e\uparrow}(\bm{k})\,[1-f_{e\uparrow}(\bm{k}^{\prime})]

(71)

and analogously for spin-down electrons. Detailed balance for elastic scattering implies that
W_{e^{\prime}\to e}(\bm{k}^{\prime},\bm{k})=W_{e\to e^{\prime}}(\bm{k},\bm{k}^{\prime})
and thus

I^{e\uparrow}_{e\to e}(\bm{k})=\int\frac{d^{3}k^{\prime}}{(2\pi)^{3}}\,W_{e\to e^{\prime}}(\bm{k},\bm{k}^{\prime})\,[f_{e\uparrow}(\bm{k}^{\prime})-f_{e\uparrow}(\bm{k})]

(72)

and analogously for spin down.

In the following, we employ a short-hand notation that suppresses (a) integrals over momenta
\bm{k}^{\prime}
,
\bm{k}^{\prime\prime}
, … and (b) momentum arguments. Functions depending on
\bm{k}
,
\bm{k}^{\prime}
, and
\bm{k}^{\prime\prime}
are denoted by no, one, and two primes, respectively. Hence, we write the collision integrals for elastic impurity scattering as

\displaystyle I^{e\uparrow}_{e\to e}

\displaystyle=W_{e\to e^{\prime}}\,(f_{e\uparrow}^{\prime}-f_{e\uparrow}),

(73)

\displaystyle I^{e\downarrow}_{e\to e}

\displaystyle=W_{e\to e^{\prime}}\,(f_{e\downarrow}^{\prime}-f_{e\downarrow}).

(74)

From Eq. (
20
), we then obtain the collision integrals for the electron number density,

I^{n_{e}}_{e\to e}=I^{e\uparrow}_{e\to e}+I^{e\downarrow}_{e\to e}=W_{e\to e^{\prime}}\,(n_{e}^{\prime}-n_{e}),

(75)

and for the density of the
z
-component of spin,

I^{c_{e}^{z}}_{e\to e}=I^{e\uparrow}_{e\to e}-I^{e\downarrow}_{e\to e}=W_{e\to e^{\prime}}\,(c_{e}^{z\prime}-c_{e}^{z}).

(76)

The crucial next step is to reconstruct the
\mathrm{SU}(2)
-invariant form of the collision integral for the spin by rotating all quantities into an arbitrary direction. In the present case, this is simple. We recognize that the left-hand side and both summands on the right-hand side are
z
-components of vectors. The
\mathrm{SU}(2)
-invariant form is thus

\bm{I}^{\mathbf{c}_{e}}_{e\to e}=W_{e\to e^{\prime}}\,(\bm{c}_{e}^{\prime}-\bm{c}_{e}).

(77)

We can now use the multipole expansions in Eqs. (
7
) and (
20
) to express the collision rate in terms of the matrix densities as

\widehat{I}^{\widehat{f}_{e}}_{e\to e}=W_{e\to e^{\prime}}\,(\widehat{f}_{e}^{\prime}-\widehat{f}_{e}).

(78)

The case of holes is of course analogous.

The result for excitons is also analogous: For example for singlet excitons, the collision integral is

\displaystyle I^{s}_{s\to s}(\bm{k})=\int\frac{d^{3}k^{\prime}}{(2\pi)^{3}}\,W_{s^{\prime}\to s}(\bm{k}^{\prime},\bm{k})\,f_{s}(\bm{k}^{\prime})\,[1+f_{s}(\bm{k})]

\displaystyle\qquad{}-\int\frac{d^{3}k^{\prime}}{(2\pi)^{3}}\,W_{s\to s^{\prime}}(\bm{k},\bm{k}^{\prime})\,f_{s}(\bm{k})\,[1+f_{s}(\bm{k}^{\prime})],

(79)

where we have used that excitons are bosons. The second-order terms again cancel and we obtain

I^{s}_{s\to s}=W_{s\to s^{\prime}}\,(f_{s}^{\prime}-f_{s}).

(80)

III.1
Generation and decay of singlet excitons

Singlet excitons are described by the scalar distribution function
f_{s}
, so
\mathrm{SU}(2)
invariance is automatically maintained. The transition rate describing the generation of singlet excitons only depends on the occupation of the final state. Since singlet excitons are bosons the resulting transition rate reads as

I_{\ket{0}\rightarrow s}^{s}=\widetilde{W}_{\ket{0}\rightarrow s}\left(1+f_{s}\right).

(81)

Here
\ket{0}\rightarrow s
is a short-hand way to denote that the singlet exciton is excited starting from the ground state with all single-electron states below (above) the Fermi energy occupied (empty). This is of course not a spontaneously occurring process but requires coupling to the radiation field, which is suppressed in this notation. We use the symbol
\widetilde{W}_{\ket{0}\rightarrow s}
with a tilde for the rate; this is an effective rate with properties of the radiation field such as occupation numbers of photonic states absorbed. For the decay of a singlet exciton into the Fermi sea, we analogously write

I_{s\rightarrow\ket{0}}^{s}=-\widetilde{W}_{s\rightarrow\ket{0}}f_{s}.

(82)

These transition rates can be added to the right-hand side of the Boltzmann equation (
31
) for the singlet excitons. The corresponding processes for triplet excitons are of course described analogously.

III.2
Binding and unbinding of electrons and holes in a singlet state

The transition between unbound electron-hole pairs and singlet excitons involves three different particle species, which results in a description from three different perspectives. We discuss these in turn.

III.2.1
Point of view of the singlet exciton

In the case of
z
-polarized particles, there are only two different orientations of the electron and hole spins. To form a singlet exciton, a spin-up electron has to be paired with a spin-down hole or vice versa. Hence, the transition rate takes the form

I_{e+h\rightarrow s}^{s}=W_{e+h\rightarrow s}\,\frac{f^{\prime}_{e\uparrow}f^{\prime\prime\prime}_{h\downarrow}+f^{\prime}_{e\downarrow}f^{\prime\prime\prime}_{h\uparrow}}{2}\,(1+f_{s}).

(83)

Here functions depending on the momenta
\bm{k}^{\prime\prime}=\bm{k}+\bm{k}^{\prime}
and
\bm{k}^{\prime\prime\prime}=\bm{k}-\bm{k}^{\prime}
are described by two and three primes, respectively. Note that the momentum arguments of the electron and the hole distribution functions could be interchanged as all possible combinations are covered by integrating over
\bm{k}^{\prime}
. The origin of the factor
1/2
is that the electron-hole states
\ket{\uparrow\downarrow}
and
\ket{\downarrow\uparrow}
can be written as
\ket{\uparrow\downarrow}=(\ket{\psi_{s}}+\ket{\psi_{t0}})/\sqrt{2}
and
\ket{\downarrow\uparrow}=(-\ket{\psi_{s}}+\ket{\psi_{t0}})/\sqrt{2}
, respectively, where
\ket{\psi_{s}}=(\ket{\uparrow\downarrow}-\ket{\downarrow\uparrow})/\sqrt{2}
is the singlet state and
\ket{\psi_{t0}}=(\ket{\uparrow\downarrow}+\ket{\downarrow\uparrow})/\sqrt{2}
is the
m=0
triplet state. Hence, the probability of, say,
\ket{\uparrow\downarrow}
being in the singlet state is
1/2
.

For a spin along the
z
axis, only the diagonal elements of the distribution function
\widehat{f}_{e}
in Eq. (

) in terms of the coefficients of the multipole expansion in Eq. (

) then reads as

I_{e+h\rightarrow s}^{s}=W_{e+h\rightarrow s}\,\frac{n^{\prime}_{e}n^{\prime\prime\prime}_{h}-c_{e}^{z\prime}c_{h}^{z\prime\prime\prime}}{4}\,(1+f_{s}).

(84)

Now it is possible to generalize this transition rate to arbitrary spin directions as there is a unique
\mathrm{SU}(2)
-invariant expression that reduces to Eq. (
84
) for
z
-polarized spins:

I_{e+h\rightarrow s}^{s}=W_{e+h\rightarrow s}\,\frac{n^{\prime}_{e}n^{\prime\prime\prime}_{h}-\bm{c}^{\prime}_{e}\cdot\bm{c}^{\prime\prime\prime}_{h}}{4}\,(1+f_{s}).

(85)

This transition rate
I_{e+h\rightarrow s}^{s}\equiv I_{e+h\to s}^{f_{s}}
appears in the Boltzmann equation (
31
) for the scalar distribution function
f_{s}
of singlet excitons and is thus itself a scalar. It can also be expressed in a basis-independent form in terms of matrix-valued distribution functions as
note.anticomm

I_{e+h\to s}^{f_{s}}=\frac{W_{e+h\to s}}{2}\,\Tr\big\{\widehat{P}_{s},\widehat{f}^{\prime}_{e}\otimes\widehat{f}^{\prime\prime\prime}_{h}\big\}\,(1+f_{s}),

(86)

as can be shown by inserting the multipole expansions of the electron and hole distribution functions. Here
\{\bullet,\bullet\}
is the anticommutator and
\widehat{P}_{s}
is the projection operator onto the spin-singlet subspace of the product Hilbert space of the electron and hole spins. This operator can be written as

\displaystyle\widehat{P}_{s}

\displaystyle=\frac{\mathds{1}_{4}}{4}-\widehat{\bm{S}}_{e}\cdot\widehat{\bm{S}}_{h}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{4}\left(\widehat{\sigma}_{0}\otimes\widehat{\sigma}_{0}-\widehat{\sigma}_{x}\otimes\widehat{\sigma}_{x}-\widehat{\sigma}_{y}\otimes\widehat{\sigma}_{y}-\widehat{\sigma}_{z}\otimes\widehat{\sigma}_{z}\right),

(87)

where
\widehat{\bm{S}}_{e}
and
\widehat{\bm{S}}_{h}
are the spin operators of the electron and the hole, respectively. It expresses the fact that an electron and a hole can only form a singlet exciton if they are in a relative spin-singlet state. The structure of the momentum-dependent transition rate is reminiscent of the Haberkorn approach
JoM70
;
EFL73
;
Hab76
. As mentioned in Sec.
I
, the anticommutator structure ensures positivity of the density matrix on the electron-hole product space. The trace in Eq. (
86
) sums over all contributions allowed by spin conservation and leads to a scalar transition term.

It is useful to restore the momentum arguments and integrals at this point. For the process
e+h\to s
from the point of view of the exciton, the outer momentum
\bm{k}
is the one of the exciton, the momentum
\bm{k}^{\prime}
of the electron needs to be integrated over and the momentum
\bm{k}^{\prime\prime\prime}=\bm{k}-\bm{k}^{\prime}
of the hole is then fixed by momentum conservation. This leads to

\displaystyle I_{e+h\to s}^{f_{s}}(\bm{k})

\displaystyle=\int\frac{d^{3}k^{\prime}}{(2\pi)^{3}}\,\frac{W_{e+h\to s}(\bm{k}^{\prime},\bm{k}-\bm{k}^{\prime})}{2}

\displaystyle\quad{}\times\Tr\big\{\widehat{P}_{s},\widehat{{f}}_{e}(\bm{k}^{\prime})\otimes\widehat{{f}}_{h}(\bm{k}-\bm{k}^{\prime})\big\}\,\big[1+f_{s}(\bm{k})\big].

(88)

The breaking of singlet excitons into unbound electrons and holes can be described in the same way. For this reverse process only the sign and the occupation numbers have to be changed. Now the final state is fermionic, which yields

I_{s\rightarrow e+h}^{s}=-W_{s\rightarrow e+h}\,\frac{(2-n^{\prime}_{e})(2-n^{\prime\prime\prime}_{h})-\bm{c}^{\prime}_{e}\cdot\bm{c}^{\prime\prime\prime}_{h}}{4}\,f_{s}

(89)

in the multipole form and

\displaystyle I_{s\rightarrow e+h}^{f_{s}}

\displaystyle=-\frac{W_{s\to e+h}}{2}

\displaystyle\quad{}\times\Tr\Big\{\widehat{P}_{s},\big(\mathds{1}_{2}-\widehat{f}^{\prime}_{e}\big)\otimes\big(\mathds{1}_{2}-\widehat{f}^{\prime\prime\prime}_{h}\big)\Big\}\,f_{s}

(90)

as the basis-independent expression; compare Eqs. (
7
) and (
20
).

III.2.2
Point of view of the electron

We now consider the perspective of the electron (the hole case is of course analogous). The electron’s momentum is the momentum argument
\bm{k}
of the solution of the Boltzmann equation. The transition rates for spin-up and spin-down electrons read as

\displaystyle I_{e+h\rightarrow s}^{e\uparrow}

\displaystyle=-W_{e+h\rightarrow s}\,\frac{f_{e\uparrow}f^{\prime}_{h\downarrow}}{2}\,(1+f^{\prime\prime}_{s}),

(91)

\displaystyle I_{e+h\rightarrow s}^{e\downarrow}

\displaystyle=-W_{e+h\rightarrow s}\,\frac{f_{e\downarrow}f^{\prime}_{h\uparrow}}{2}\,(1+f^{\prime\prime}_{s}),

(92)

respectively. The transition rate for the electron number density is the sum of these two transition rates, while the transition rate of the density of the
z
-component of the spin is their difference. After generalizing to an arbitrary polarization direction, we obtain

I_{e+h\rightarrow s}^{n_{e}}=-W_{e+h\rightarrow s}\,\frac{n_{e}n^{\prime}_{h}-\bm{c}_{e}\cdot\bm{c}^{\prime}_{h}}{4}\,(1+f^{\prime\prime}_{s})

(93)

and

\bm{I}_{e+h\rightarrow s}^{\bm{c}_{e}}=-W_{e+h\rightarrow s}\,\frac{\bm{c}_{e}n^{\prime}_{h}-\bm{c}^{\prime}_{h}n_{e}}{4}\,(1+f^{\prime\prime}_{s}).

(94)

The transition rate of the electron number density is the negative of the transition rate from the point of view of the singlet exciton given in Eq. (
85
). According to Eqs. (
7
) and (
20
), the matrix-valued transition term then reads as

\displaystyle\widehat{I}^{\widehat{f}_{e}}_{e+h\to s}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\,\big(I_{e+h\rightarrow s}^{n_{e}}\widehat{\sigma}_{0}+\bm{I}_{e+h\rightarrow s}^{\bm{c}_{e}}\cdot\widehat{\bm{\sigma}}\big)

\displaystyle=-\frac{W_{e+h\to s}}{2}\,\Tr_{h}\big\{\widehat{P}_{s},\widehat{{f}}_{e}\otimes\widehat{f}^{\prime}_{h}\big\}\,(1+f^{\prime\prime}_{s}),

(95)

where
\Tr_{h}
is the trace over the hole sector. To restore the momenta and integrals, we note that the outer momentum
\bm{k}
is the one of the electron, the hole momentum
\bm{k}^{\prime}
is integrated over, and the exciton momentum
\bm{k}^{\prime\prime}=\bm{k}+\bm{k}^{\prime}
is then fixed. The rate reads as

\displaystyle\widehat{I}^{\widehat{f}_{e}}_{e+h\to s}(\bm{k})

\displaystyle=-\int\frac{d^{3}k^{\prime}}{(2\pi)^{3}}\,\frac{W_{e+h\to s}(\bm{k},\bm{k}^{\prime})}{2}

\displaystyle\penalty\ {}\times\Tr_{h}\big\{\widehat{P}_{s},\widehat{{f}}_{e}(\bm{k})\otimes\widehat{{f}}_{h}(\bm{k}^{\prime})\big\}\,\big[1+f_{s}(\bm{k}+\bm{k}^{\prime})\big].

(96)

These transition rates can be compared to the transition rates from the point of view of the singlet exciton. This yields

{-}I_{e+h\rightarrow s}^{s}=I_{e+h\rightarrow s}^{n_{e}}=I_{e+h\rightarrow s}^{n_{h}},

(97)

as the creation of one singlet exciton results in the annihilation of a free electron and a free hole. Furthermore, the spin transition rates satisfy

\bm{I}_{e+h\rightarrow s}^{\bm{c}_{e}}+\bm{I}_{e+h\rightarrow s}^{\bm{c}_{h}}=0,

(98)

which shows that spin is conserved, as the singlet exciton does not posses an overall spin.

The reverse unbinding process can be described analogously. It can also be read off from Eq. (
95
), essentially by reversing the sign and interchanging occupied and empty states. The resulting rate reads as

\widehat{I}^{\widehat{f}_{e}}_{s\to e+h}=\frac{W_{s\to e+h}}{2}\,\Tr_{h}\Big\{\widehat{P}_{s},\big(\mathds{1}_{2}-\widehat{{f}}_{e}\big)\otimes\big(\mathds{1}_{2}-\widehat{f}^{\prime}_{h}\big)\Big\}\,f^{\prime\prime}_{s}.

(99)

III.3
Binding and unbinding of electrons and holes in a triplet state

Like for electrons and holes, it is beneficial to write the matrix-valued distribution function of the triplet excitons in components,

\widehat{{f}}_{t}=\begin{pmatrix}f_{t,11}&f_{t,10}&f_{t,1\bar{1}}\\
f_{t,01}&f_{t,00}&f_{t,0\bar{1}}\\
f_{t,\bar{1}1}&f_{t,\bar{1}0}&f_{t,\bar{1}\bar{1}}\end{pmatrix}.

(100)

The indices of the matrix elements denote the magnetic quantum number of the triplet exciton, where we use
\bar{1}=-1
. For a spin along the
z
axis, only the diagonal elements of the matrix are nonzero so that in the multipole representation it takes the form

\widehat{{f}}_{t}=\begin{pmatrix}f_{t,1}&0&0\\
0&f_{t,0}&0\\
0&0&f_{t,\bar{1}}\end{pmatrix}={n_{t}}\widehat{S}_{0}+{c_{t}^{z}}\widehat{S}_{3}+{q_{t5}}{\widehat{Q}}_{5};

(101)

cf. Eq. (
48
). This allows us to express the elements of the density matrix in terms of the diagonal elements of the multipole expansion and vice versa,

\displaystyle{n_{t}}

\displaystyle=\frac{f_{t,1}+f_{t,0}+f_{t,\bar{1}}}{\sqrt{6}},

(102)

\displaystyle{c_{t}^{z}}

\displaystyle=\frac{f_{t,1}-f_{t,\bar{1}}}{2},

(103)

\displaystyle{q_{t5}}

\displaystyle=\frac{f_{t,1}-2f_{t,0}+f_{t,\bar{1}}}{2\sqrt{3}}.

(104)

III.3.1
Point of view of the triplet exciton

The transition term for the triplet-exciton distribution function
\widehat{f}_{t}
also has to be a
3\times 3
matrix on the spin-
1
Hilbert space. For the spin along the
z

\displaystyle I_{e+h\rightarrow t}^{t_{1}}

\displaystyle=W_{e+h\rightarrow t}\,f^{\prime}_{e\uparrow}f^{\prime\prime\prime}_{h\uparrow}\,(1+f_{t,1}),

(105)

\displaystyle I_{e+h\rightarrow t}^{t_{0}}

\displaystyle=W_{e+h\rightarrow t}\,\frac{f^{\prime}_{e\uparrow}f^{\prime\prime\prime}_{h\downarrow}+f^{\prime}_{e\downarrow}f^{\prime\prime\prime}_{h\uparrow}}{2}\,(1+f_{t,0}),

(106)

\displaystyle I_{e+h\rightarrow t}^{t_{\bar{1}}}

\displaystyle=W_{e+h\rightarrow t}\,f^{\prime}_{e\downarrow}f^{\prime\prime\prime}_{h\downarrow}\,(1+f_{t,\bar{1}}).

(107)

Recall that three primes denote a dependence on
\bm{k}^{\prime\prime\prime}=\bm{k}-\bm{k}^{\prime}
. The next step is again to construct the
\mathrm{SU}(2)
-invariant transition rates by rotating the spin into a general direction. In analogy to the multipole expansion of the distribution function in Eq. (
48

\widehat{I}^{\widehat{f}_{t}}_{e+h\rightarrow t}=I^{n_{t}}_{e+h\rightarrow t}\widehat{S}_{0}+\bm{I}^{\bm{c}_{t}}_{e+h\rightarrow t}\cdot\bm{\widehat{S}}+\vec{I}^{\,\vec{q}_{t}}_{e+h\rightarrow t}\cdot\vec{{\widehat{Q}}}.

(108)

For the spin along the
z
direction, this yields

\displaystyle I_{e+h\to t}^{{n_{t}}}

\displaystyle=\frac{I_{e+h\rightarrow t}^{t_{1}}+I_{e+h\rightarrow t}^{t_{0}}+I_{e+h\rightarrow t}^{t_{\bar{1}}}}{\sqrt{6}}

\displaystyle=\frac{W_{e+h\rightarrow t}}{24}\,\big(3\sqrt{6}\,n_{e}^{\prime}n_{h}^{\prime\prime\prime}+\sqrt{6}\,c_{e}^{z\prime}c_{h}^{z\prime\prime\prime}

\displaystyle\quad{}+6\,n_{e}^{\prime}n_{h}^{\prime\prime\prime}n_{t}+2\,c_{e}^{z\prime}c_{h}^{z\prime\prime\prime}n_{t}+2\sqrt{6}\,c_{e}^{z\prime}n_{h}^{\prime\prime\prime}c_{t}^{z}

\displaystyle\quad{}+2\sqrt{6}\,n_{e}^{\prime}c_{h}^{z\prime\prime\prime}c_{t}^{z}+4\sqrt{2}\,c_{e}^{z\prime}c_{h}^{z\prime\prime\prime}q_{t5}\big)

(109)

and analogously

\displaystyle I_{e+h\to t}^{{c_{t}^{z}}}

\displaystyle=\frac{W_{e+h\rightarrow t}}{12}\,\big(3\,n_{e}^{\prime}c_{h}^{z\prime\prime\prime}+3\,c_{e}^{z\prime}n_{h}^{\prime\prime\prime}

\displaystyle\quad{}+3\,n_{e}^{\prime}n_{h}^{\prime\prime\prime}c_{t}^{z}+\sqrt{6}\,n_{e}^{\prime}c_{h}^{z\prime\prime\prime}n_{t}+\sqrt{6}\,c_{e}^{z\prime}n_{h}^{\prime\prime\prime}n_{t}

\displaystyle\quad{}+3\,c_{e}^{z\prime}c_{h}^{z\prime\prime\prime}c_{t}^{z}+\sqrt{3}\,n_{e}^{\prime}c_{h}^{z\prime\prime\prime}q_{t5}+\sqrt{3}\,c_{e}^{z\prime}n_{h}^{\prime\prime\prime}q_{t5}\big),

(110)

\displaystyle I_{e+h\to t}^{{q_{t5}}}

\displaystyle=\frac{W_{e+h\rightarrow t}}{12\sqrt{3}}\,\big(6\,c_{e}^{z\prime}c_{h}^{z\prime\prime\prime}

\displaystyle\quad{}+3\sqrt{3}\,n_{e}^{\prime}n_{h}^{\prime\prime\prime}q_{t5}+3\,n_{e}^{\prime}c_{h}^{z\prime\prime\prime}c_{t}^{z}+3\,c_{e}^{z\prime}n_{h}^{\prime\prime\prime}c_{t}^{z}

\displaystyle\quad{}+2\sqrt{6}\,c_{e}^{z\prime}c_{h}^{z\prime\prime\prime}n_{t}-\sqrt{3}\,c_{e}^{z\prime}c_{h}^{z\prime\prime\prime}q_{t5}\big).

(111)

In these equations, the terms of second order in the coefficients represent the classical part since they do not depend on the occupation of the final state. The terms of third order are the quantum corrections due to the Bose-Einstein statistics of the excitons.

Unlike for the previous examples, the
\mathrm{SU}(2)
-invariant generalization of Eqs. (

{q_{t5}}

under rotations is not clear. On the other hand, the Cartesian quadrupole density

as components of Cartesian matrices and write Eqs. (

This can be done by considering the two-particle state of an electron and a hole. As the unbound electron-hole pair can be of triplet character, it generally has a quadrupole moment. The
projection operator

\widehat{P}_{t}=\mathds{1}_{4}-\widehat{P}_{s}=\frac{3\mathds{1}_{4}}{4}+\widehat{\bm{S}}_{e}\cdot\widehat{\bm{S}}_{h}

projects onto the triplet subspace, in analogy to the singlet projection operator

defined in Eq. (

can be written as a
3\times 3
matrix with respect to a suitable basis of this subspace. To be consistent, we choose the canonical spin-
1

\{|{\uparrow_{e}\uparrow_{h}}\rangle,(|{\uparrow_{e}\downarrow_{h}}\rangle+|{\downarrow_{e}\uparrow_{h}}\rangle)/\sqrt{2},|{\downarrow_{e}\downarrow_{h}}\rangle\}
. The notation
\left[\bullet\right]_{3}
refers to the corresponding matrix. The density matrix for an electron-hole pair in a triplet state is thus written as

\widehat{{f}}_{eh}=\Big[\widehat{P}_{t}\,\big(\widehat{f}^{\prime}_{e}\otimes\widehat{f}^{\prime\prime\prime}_{h}\big)\,\widehat{P}_{t}\Big]_{3}.

(113)

The resulting matrix
\widehat{{f}}_{eh}
is an operator on the Hilbert space of spin-1 particles and can thus be expressed in terms of the nine basis matrices of the multipole expansion.
For a spin along the
z
axis, only three coefficients are nonzero,
\widehat{{f}}_{eh}=n_{eh}\widehat{S}_{0}+c_{eh}^{z}\widehat{S}_{3}+q_{eh5}{\widehat{Q}}_{5}

\displaystyle n_{eh}

\displaystyle=\frac{c_{e}^{z\prime}c_{h}^{z\prime\prime\prime}+3n^{\prime}_{e}n^{\prime\prime\prime}_{h}}{4\sqrt{6}},

(114)

\displaystyle c_{eh}^{z}

\displaystyle=\frac{c_{e}^{z\prime}n^{\prime\prime\prime}_{h}+c_{h}^{z\prime\prime\prime}n^{\prime}_{e}}{4},

(115)

\displaystyle q_{eh5}

\displaystyle=\frac{\sqrt{3}\,c_{e}^{z\prime}c_{h}^{z\prime\prime\prime}}{6}.

(116)

As electron and hole together have the overall momentum
\bm{k}
due to momentum conservation,
\widehat{{f}}_{eh}
and the corresponding multipole coefficients do not carry any primes. By eliminating the variables
n^{\prime}_{e}
,
n^{\prime\prime\prime}_{h}

c_{e}^{z\prime}
, and

from Eqs. (

\displaystyle I_{e+h\to t}^{{n_{t}}}

\displaystyle=\frac{W_{e+h\rightarrow t}}{3}\,\big(3n_{eh}+\sqrt{6}\,n_{eh}n_{t}+\sqrt{6}\,c_{eh}^{z}c_{t}^{z}

\displaystyle\quad{}+\sqrt{6}\,q_{eh5}q_{t5}\big),

(117)

\displaystyle I_{e+h\to t}^{{c_{t}^{z}}}

\displaystyle=\frac{W_{e+h\rightarrow t}}{3}\,\big(3c_{eh}^{z}+\sqrt{6}\,n_{eh}c_{t}^{z}+\sqrt{3}\,q_{eh5}c_{t}^{z}

\displaystyle\quad{}+\sqrt{6}\,c_{eh}^{z}n_{t}+\sqrt{3}\,q_{t5}c_{eh}^{z}\big),

(118)

\displaystyle I_{e+h\to t}^{{q_{t5}}}

\displaystyle=\frac{W_{e+h\rightarrow t}}{3}\,\big(3q_{eh5}+\sqrt{6}\,q_{eh5}n_{t}+\sqrt{6}\,n_{eh}q_{t5}

\displaystyle\quad{}+\sqrt{3}\,c_{eh}^{z}c_{t}^{z}-\sqrt{3}\,q_{eh5}q_{t5}\big).

(119)

\mathrm{SU}(2)
-invariant generalizations without ambiguities. For terms containing quadrupole moments, Eq. (

) should be noted. The transition rate of the triplet exciton number density is

\displaystyle I_{e+h\rightarrow t}^{{n_{t}}}

\displaystyle=\frac{W_{e+h\rightarrow t}}{6}\,\big(6\,n_{eh}+2\sqrt{6}\,n_{eh}{n_{t}}+2\sqrt{6}\,\bm{c}_{eh}\cdot\bm{c}_{t}

\displaystyle\quad{}+\sqrt{6}\,\Tr\underline{q}{}_{eh}\underline{q}{}_{t}\big),

(120)

where the classical part is proportional to
n_{eh}
. For the last term, the identity
\Tr\underline{q}{}_{eh}\underline{q}{}_{t}=2\vec{q}_{eh}\cdot\vec{q}_{t}
has been used. The spin transition rate becomes

\displaystyle\bm{I}_{e+h\rightarrow t}^{\bm{c}_{t}}

\displaystyle=\frac{W_{e+h\rightarrow t}}{6}\,\big(6\,\bm{c}_{eh}+2\sqrt{6}\,n_{eh}\bm{c}_{t}+3\,\underline{q}{}_{eh}\bm{c}_{t}

\displaystyle\quad{}+2\sqrt{6}\,\bm{c}_{eh}{n_{t}}+3\,\underline{q}{}_{t}\bm{c}_{eh}\big),

\bm{c}_{eh}

\displaystyle\underline{I}_{e+h\rightarrow t}^{\underline{q}{}_{t}}

\displaystyle=W_{e+h\rightarrow t}\,\bigg[\underline{q}{}_{eh}+\frac{\sqrt{6}}{3}\,\underline{q}{}_{eh}{n_{t}}+\frac{\sqrt{6}}{3}\,n_{eh}\underline{q}{}_{t}

\displaystyle\quad{}+\frac{1}{2}\,(\bm{c}_{t}\otimes\bm{c}_{eh}+\bm{c}_{eh}\otimes\bm{c}_{t})-\frac{1}{3}\,(\bm{c}_{eh}\cdot\bm{c}_{t})\,\mathds{1}_{3}

\displaystyle\quad{}-\frac{1}{2}\,\big(\underline{q}{}_{eh}\underline{q}{}_{t}+\underline{q}{}_{eh}\underline{q}{}_{t}\big)+\frac{1}{3}\,\big(\Tr\underline{q}{}_{eh}\underline{q}{}_{t}\big)\,\mathds{1}_{3}\bigg],

(122)

with the classical part being proportional to
\underline{q}{}_{eh}

\widehat{I}^{\widehat{f}_{t}}_{e+h\rightarrow t}=\frac{W_{e+h\to t}}{2}\,\Big\{\Big[\widehat{P}_{t}\,\big(\widehat{f}^{\prime}_{e}\otimes\widehat{f}^{\prime\prime\prime}_{h}\big)\,\widehat{P}_{t}\Big]_{3},\mathds{1}_{3}+\widehat{{f}}_{t}\Big\},

\displaystyle\widehat{I}^{\widehat{f}_{t}}_{t\to e+h}

\displaystyle=-\frac{W_{t\to e+h}}{2}

\displaystyle\quad{}\times\Big\{\Big[\widehat{P}_{t}\,\Big[\big(\mathds{1}_{2}-\widehat{f}^{\prime}_{e}\big)\otimes\big(\mathds{1}_{2}-\widehat{f}^{\prime\prime\prime}_{h}\big)\Big]\,\widehat{P}_{t}\Big]_{3},\widehat{{f}}_{t}\Big\},

(125)

from which the rates for the number, spin, and quadrupole densities can be inferred.

III.3.2
Point of view of electron and hole

In the following, we discuss the process
e+h\to t
from the point of view of the electrons; the results for the holes are analogous. The transition rates of the different spin orientations for
z
-polarized electrons,

\displaystyle I_{e+h\rightarrow t}^{e\uparrow}

\displaystyle=-W_{e+h\rightarrow t}\,\bigg[f_{e\uparrow}f^{\prime}_{h\uparrow}\,(1+f^{\prime\prime}_{t,1})

\displaystyle\quad{}+\frac{f_{e\uparrow}f^{\prime}_{h\downarrow}}{2}\,(1+f^{\prime\prime}_{t,0})\bigg],

(126)

\displaystyle I_{e+h\rightarrow t}^{e\downarrow}

\displaystyle=-W_{e+h\rightarrow t}\,\bigg[f_{e\downarrow}f^{\prime}_{h\downarrow}\,(1+f^{\prime\prime}_{t,\bar{1}})

\displaystyle\quad{}+\frac{f_{e\downarrow}f^{\prime}_{h\uparrow}}{2}\,(1+f^{\prime\prime}_{t,0})\bigg],

(127)

can be combined to obtain the transition rates for the electron number and spin densities in analogy to Eqs. (
75
) and (

). The resulting expressions are not symmetric in the coefficients of
\widehat{{f}}_{e}
and

for the simple reason that we are writing down transition rates for the electrons, not for the holes. The rates are not sufficient to read off the
\mathrm{SU}(2)
-invariant form, as we will see shortly.

The basis-independent form of the transition rate is particularly useful for this reason. In analogy to the previous cases, the transition rate in terms of density matrices can be written as

\displaystyle\widehat{I}_{e+h\to t}^{\widehat{{f}}_{e}}

\displaystyle=-\frac{W_{e+h\to t}}{2}\Tr_{h}\bigg[\left(\mathds{1}_{3}+\widehat{f}^{\prime\prime}_{t}\right)_{4}\widehat{P}_{t}\left(\widehat{{f}}_{e}\otimes\widehat{f}^{\prime}_{h}\right)

\displaystyle\quad{}+\left(\widehat{{f}}_{e}\otimes\widehat{f}^{\prime}_{h}\right)\widehat{P}_{t}\left(\mathds{1}_{3}+\widehat{f}^{\prime\prime}_{t}\right)_{4}\bigg].

(128)

The notation
\left(\bullet\right)_{4}

3\times 3

\{\ket{\uparrow_{e}}\ket{\uparrow_{h}},\ket{\uparrow_{e}}\ket{\downarrow_{h}},\ket{\downarrow_{e}}\ket{\uparrow_{h}},\ket{\downarrow_{e}}\ket{\downarrow_{h}}\}

\Tr_{h}\bullet=\bra{\uparrow_{h}}\bullet\ket{\uparrow_{h}}+\bra{\downarrow_{h}}\bullet\ket{\downarrow_{h}}.

(129)

The resulting
\mathrm{SU}(2)
-invariant transition rates given in the multipole expansion are thus

\displaystyle I_{e+h\rightarrow t}^{n_{e}}

\displaystyle=-\frac{W_{e+h\rightarrow t}}{12}\,\big[9n_{e}n^{\prime}_{h}+3\bm{c}_{e}\cdot\bm{c}^{\prime}_{h}+3\sqrt{6}\,n_{e}n^{\prime}_{h}n^{\prime\prime}_{t}

\displaystyle\quad{}+6n_{e}\bm{c}^{\prime}_{h}\cdot\bm{c}^{\prime\prime}_{t}+6n^{\prime}_{h}\bm{c}_{e}\cdot\bm{c}^{\prime\prime}_{t}+\sqrt{6}\,n^{\prime\prime}_{t}\bm{c}_{e}\cdot\bm{c}^{\prime}_{h}

\displaystyle\quad{}+6\bm{c}_{e}\cdot\underline{q}^{\prime\prime}{\hskip-5.0pt}_{t}\hskip 1.49994pt\bm{c}^{\prime}_{h}\big]

(130)

for the electron number density and

\displaystyle\bm{I}_{e+h\rightarrow t}^{\bm{c}_{e}}

\displaystyle=-\frac{W_{e+h\rightarrow t}}{12}\,\big[9\bm{c}_{e}n^{\prime}_{h}+3n_{e}\bm{c}^{\prime}_{h}

\displaystyle\quad{}+3\sqrt{6}\,\bm{c}_{e}n^{\prime}_{h}n^{\prime\prime}_{t}+\sqrt{6}\,n_{e}\bm{c}^{\prime}_{h}n^{\prime\prime}_{t}+6n_{e}n^{\prime}_{h}\bm{c}^{\prime\prime}_{t}

\displaystyle\quad{}+6\bm{c}_{e}\left(\bm{c}^{\prime}_{h}\cdot\bm{c}^{\prime\prime}_{t}\right)+6n_{e}\underline{q}^{\prime\prime}{\hskip-5.0pt}_{t}\hskip 1.49994pt\bm{c}^{\prime}_{h}\big]

(131)

for the spin density. This rate contains a product of three spin densities. The corresponding term is proportional to
c_{e}^{z}c_{h}^{z\prime}c_{t}^{z\prime\prime}
in the limit of
z
-polarized spins. It is thus clear that this limit does not allow to infer the full
\mathrm{SU}(2)
-invariant expression (although one might guess correctly) so that the basis-independent expression (
128
) is required.

The results for the holes are analogous. We thus find that the rates satisfy the consistency relations

\displaystyle-\sqrt{6}\,I_{e+h\to t}^{{n_{t}}}

\displaystyle=I_{e+h\to t}^{n_{e}}=I_{e+h\to t}^{n_{h}},

(132)

\displaystyle-4\bm{I}_{e+h\to t}^{\bm{c}_{t}}\,

\displaystyle=\bm{I}_{e+h\to t}^{\bm{c}_{e}}+\bm{I}_{e+h\to t}^{\bm{c}_{h}}.

(133)

Note that the factors
\sqrt{6}
and
4
in these two equations stem from the different normalization conditions concerning electrons and triplet excitons. The reverse process can be treated similarly.

III.4
Fusion and fission of singlet excitons

In preparation for the important transitions between a singlet exciton and two triplet excitons,
s\leftrightarrow 2t
, we first consider the simpler process
s\leftrightarrow 2s
. In contrast to the processes investigated before, the fusion and fission of singlet excitons involves the same particle species in the initial and final states. Furthermore, the two singlet excitons on the same side of the reaction have to be treated as indistinguishable bosons.

Starting with the fusion, this process can either create or fuse a singlet exciton with the outer momentum
\bm{k}
. This leads to in-scattering and out-scattering terms in the transition rate, which can be written as

I_{2s\to s}^{f_{s}}=W^{\prime\prime\prime}_{2s\to s}f_{s}^{\prime\prime\prime}f_{s}^{\prime}\left(1+f_{s}\right)-2W_{2s\to s}f_{s}f_{s}^{\prime}\left(1+f_{s}^{\prime\prime}\right).

(134)

Here the short-hand notations
W\equiv W(\bm{k}^{\prime},\bm{k})
and
W^{\prime\prime\prime}\equiv W(\bm{k}^{\prime},\bm{k}-\bm{k}^{\prime})\equiv W(\bm{k}^{\prime},\bm{k}^{\prime\prime\prime})
are used. The factor
2
in the out-scattering term results from the two equal contributions of indistinguishable singlet excitons in the initial state. Conversely, the fission of one singlet exciton into two singlet excitons is described by

\displaystyle I_{s\to 2s}^{f_{s}}

\displaystyle=2W_{s\to 2s}\left(1+f_{s}\right)\left(1+f_{s}^{\prime}\right)f_{s}^{\prime\prime}

\displaystyle\quad{}-W^{\prime\prime\prime}_{s\to 2s}\left(1+f_{s}^{\prime\prime\prime}\right)\left(1+f_{s}^{\prime}\right)f_{s}.

(135)

Note that due to the spin-singlet character of all involved quasiparticles,
\mathrm{SU}(2)
symmetry is trivially satisfied.

III.5
Absorption of a singlet exciton by a triplet exciton

In contrast to the previous process, the particles in the initial state are distinguishable. In the limit of
z
polarization, this yields, from the point of view of the singlet exciton,

\displaystyle I_{t+s\to t}^{s}

\displaystyle=-W_{t+s\to t}\,\big[f^{\prime}_{t,1}(1+f^{\prime\prime}_{t,1})+f^{\prime}_{t,0}(1+f^{\prime\prime}_{t,0})

\displaystyle\quad{}+f^{\prime}_{t,\bar{1}}(1+f^{\prime\prime}_{t,\bar{1}})\big]f_{s}.

(136)

In terms of the multipole expansion this becomes

\displaystyle I_{t+s\to t}^{s}

\displaystyle=-W_{t+s\to t}\,\big(\sqrt{6}\,n^{\prime}_{t}

\displaystyle\quad{}+2n^{\prime}_{t}n^{\prime\prime}_{t}+2\bm{c}^{\prime}_{t}\cdot\bm{c}^{\prime\prime}_{t}+2\vec{q}^{\>\prime}_{t}\cdot\vec{q}^{\>\prime\prime}_{t}\big)f_{s}.

(137)

The singlet exciton is described by a scalar distribution function so that the basis-independent transition rate is

I_{t+s\to t}^{s}=-\frac{W_{t+s\to t}}{2}\,\Tr\Big\{\widehat{f}^{\prime}_{t},\mathds{1}_{3}+\widehat{f}^{\prime\prime}_{t}\Big\}\,f_{s}.

(138)

The transition term from the perspective of the triplet exciton features in-scattering and out-scattering terms as the triplet exciton is present on both sides of the reaction. The transition rates for the different spin orientations are

\displaystyle I_{t+s\to t}^{t_{1}}

\displaystyle=W^{\prime\prime\prime}_{t+s\to t}f^{\prime}_{t,1}f_{s}^{\prime\prime\prime}\left(1+f_{t,1}\right)

\displaystyle\quad{}-W_{t+s\to t}f_{t,1}f_{s}^{\prime}\left(1+f^{\prime\prime}_{t,1}\right),

(139)

\displaystyle I_{t+s\to t}^{t_{0}}

\displaystyle=W^{\prime\prime\prime}_{t+s\to t}f^{\prime}_{t,0}f_{s}^{\prime\prime\prime}\left(1+f_{t,0}\right)

\displaystyle\quad{}-W_{t+s\to t}f_{t,0}f_{s}^{\prime}\left(1+f^{\prime\prime}_{t,0}\right),

(140)

\displaystyle I_{t+s\to t}^{t_{\bar{1}}}

\displaystyle=W^{\prime\prime\prime}_{t+s\to t}f^{\prime}_{t,\bar{1}}f_{s}^{\prime\prime\prime}\left(1+f_{t,\bar{1}}\right)

\displaystyle\quad{}-W_{t+s\to t}f_{t,\bar{1}}f_{s}^{\prime}\left(1+f^{\prime\prime}_{t,\bar{1}}\right).

(141)

Since the (positive) in-scattering and the (negative) out-scattering contributions have the same structure it is sufficient to investigate one type. For the out-scattering contributions to the
\mathrm{SU}(2)
-invariant transition rates of the multipole coefficients we obtain

\displaystyle I_{t+s\to t}^{{n_{t}},\,\mathrm{out}}

\displaystyle=-\frac{W_{t+s\to t}}{\sqrt{6}}\,\big(\sqrt{6}\,n_{t}

\displaystyle\quad{}+2n_{t}n^{\prime\prime}_{t}+2\bm{c}_{t}\cdot\bm{c}^{\prime\prime}_{t}+\Tr\underline{q}{}_{t}\underline{q}^{\prime\prime}{\hskip-5.0pt}_{t}\hskip 1.49994pt\big)f_{s}^{\prime},

(142)

\displaystyle\bm{I}_{t+s\to t}^{\bm{c}_{t},\,\mathrm{out}}

\displaystyle=-\frac{W_{t+s\to t}}{6}\,\big(6\bm{c}_{t}+2\sqrt{6}\,n_{t}\bm{c}^{\prime\prime}_{t}+2\sqrt{6}\,\bm{c}_{t}n^{\prime\prime}_{t}

\displaystyle\quad{}+3\underline{q}{}_{t}\bm{c}^{\prime\prime}_{t}+3\underline{q}^{\prime\prime}{\hskip-5.0pt}_{t}\hskip 1.49994pt\bm{c}_{t}\big)f_{s}^{\prime},

(143)

\displaystyle\underline{I}_{t+s\to t}^{\underline{q}{}_{t},\,\mathrm{out}}

\displaystyle=-W_{t+s\to t}\,\bigg[\underline{q}{}_{t}+\frac{\sqrt{6}}{3}\,\big(n_{t}\underline{q}^{\prime\prime}{\hskip-5.0pt}_{t}\hskip 1.49994pt+\underline{q}{}_{t}n^{\prime\prime}_{t}\big)

\displaystyle\quad{}+\frac{1}{2}\,\big(\bm{c}_{t}\otimes\bm{c}^{\prime\prime}_{t}+\bm{c}^{\prime\prime}_{t}\otimes\bm{c}_{t}\big)-\frac{1}{3}\,\bm{c}^{\prime\prime}_{t}\cdot\bm{c}_{t}\mathds{1}_{3}

\displaystyle\quad{}-\frac{1}{2}\,\big(\underline{q}^{\prime\prime}{\hskip-5.0pt}_{t}\hskip 1.49994pt\underline{q}{}_{t}+\underline{q}{}_{t}\underline{q}^{\prime\prime}{\hskip-5.0pt}_{t}\hskip 1.49994pt\big)+\frac{1}{6}\,\Tr{\underline{q}{}_{t}\underline{q}^{\prime\prime}{\hskip-5.0pt}_{t}\hskip 1.49994pt}\mathds{1}_{3}\bigg]f_{s}^{\prime},

(144)

where the quadrupole transition rate is again symmetric and traceless. In a basis-independent form, these relations can be summarized as

\displaystyle I_{t+s\to t}^{\widehat{{f}}_{t}}

\displaystyle=\frac{W^{\prime\prime\prime}_{t+s\to t}}{2}\big\{\widehat{f}^{\prime}_{t},\mathds{1}_{3}+\widehat{{f}}_{t}\big\}f_{s}^{\prime\prime\prime}

\displaystyle\quad{}-\frac{W_{t+s\to t}}{2}\big\{\widehat{{f}}_{t},\mathds{1}_{3}+\widehat{f}^{\prime\prime}_{t}\big\}f_{s}^{\prime}.

(145)

The treatment of singlet-exciton emission is analogous.

III.6
Fusion and fission involving two triplet excitons and one singlet exciton

The important fusion of two triplet excitons forming a singlet exciton
StU89
is similar to the fusion of electron and hole into a singlet exciton. In contrast to the latter process, the two particles forming the singlet exciton are indistinguishable bosons.

III.6.1
Point of view of the singlet exciton

Considering the Clebsch-Gordan coefficients, the transition rate in terms of diagonal elements of the distribution function is

I_{2t\to s}^{s}=\frac{W^{\prime\prime\prime}_{2t\to s}}{3}\,\big(f^{\prime}_{t,1}f^{\prime\prime\prime}_{t,\bar{1}}+f^{\prime}_{t,0}f^{\prime\prime\prime}_{t,0}+f^{\prime}_{t,\bar{1}}f^{\prime\prime\prime}_{t,1}\big)(1+f_{s}).

(146)

Written in the multipole expansion and generalized to the
\mathrm{SU}(2)
-invariant form this yields

I_{2t\to s}^{s}=\frac{2W^{\prime\prime\prime}_{2t\to s}}{3}\,\big(n^{\prime}_{t}n^{\prime\prime\prime}_{t}-\bm{c}^{\prime}_{t}\cdot\bm{c}^{\prime\prime\prime}_{t}+\vec{q}^{\>\prime}_{t}\cdot\vec{q}^{\>\prime\prime\prime}_{t}\big)(1+f_{s}),

(147)

where the relation
\Tr\underline{q}^{\prime}{\hskip-3.00003pt}_{t}\underline{q}^{\prime\prime\prime}_{t}=2\vec{q}^{\>\prime}_{t}\cdot\vec{q}^{\>\prime\prime\prime}_{t}
has been used. The only differences compared to the creation of singlet excitons from free charge carriers are the prefactor and the additional quadrupole term. The basis-independent form is

I_{2t\to s}^{f_{s}}=\frac{W^{\prime\prime\prime}_{2t\to s}}{2}\,\Tr\big\{\widehat{P}_{s},\widehat{f}^{\prime}_{t}\otimes\widehat{f}^{\prime\prime\prime}_{t}\big\}\left(1+f_{s}\right),

(148)

which is analogous to the basis-independent form in the process
e+h\to s
; see Eq. (
86
). Here
\widehat{P}_{s}=|0_{2t}\rangle\langle 0_{2t}|
projects the two-triplet-exciton state onto the singlet sector, where

|0_{2t}\rangle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{3}}\left(|1\bar{1}\rangle-|00\rangle+|\bar{1}1\rangle\right)

(149)

is the singlet state of the two triplet excitons.

The rate for the reverse fission process can be obtained analogously or be read off from Eq. (
148
),

I_{s\to 2t}^{f_{s}}=-\frac{W^{\prime\prime\prime}_{s\to 2t}}{2}\,\Tr\bigg\{\widehat{P}_{s},\Big(\mathds{1}_{3}+\widehat{f}^{\prime}_{t}\Big)\otimes\Big(\mathds{1}_{3}+\widehat{f}^{\prime\prime\prime}_{t}\Big)\bigg\}f_{s}.

(150)

III.6.2
Point of view of the triplet exciton

For the diagonal elements of the distribution function, we obtain the three transition rates

\displaystyle I_{2t\to s}^{t_{1}}

\displaystyle=-\frac{2W_{2t\to s}}{3}\,f_{t,1}f^{\prime}_{t,\bar{1}}\left(1+f_{s}^{\prime\prime}\right),

(151)

\displaystyle I_{2t\to s}^{t_{0}}

\displaystyle=-\frac{2W_{2t\to s}}{3}\,f_{t,0}f^{\prime}_{t,0}\left(1+f_{s}^{\prime\prime}\right),

(152)

\displaystyle I_{2t\to s}^{t_{\bar{1}}}

\displaystyle=-\frac{2W_{2t\to s}}{3}\,f_{t,\bar{1}}f^{\prime}_{t,1}\left(1+f_{s}^{\prime\prime}\right),

(153)

where the factor
2
is due to the indistinguishable triplet excitons in the initial state. The transition rate referring to the triplet-exciton number density is

I_{2t\to s}^{{n_{t}}}=-\frac{2\sqrt{6}\,W_{2t\to s}}{9}\,\big(n_{t}n^{\prime}_{t}-\bm{c}_{t}\cdot\bm{c}^{\prime}_{t}+\vec{q}_{t}\cdot\vec{q}^{\>\prime}_{t}\big)(1+f_{s}^{\prime\prime}).

(154)

Since the actual density is
\sqrt{6}\,n_{t}
, where
n_{t}
is the expansion coefficient in Eq. (
48
), the transition rate for the actual density is twice the negative transition rate for the singlet-exciton density in Eq. (
147
). For the spin transition rate we obtain

\displaystyle\bm{I}_{2t\to s}^{\bm{c}_{t}}

\displaystyle=-\frac{W_{2t\to s}}{3}\,\bigg({-}\frac{2\sqrt{6}}{3}\,n_{t}\bm{c}^{\prime}_{t}+\frac{2\sqrt{6}}{3}\,n^{\prime}_{t}\bm{c}_{t}

\displaystyle\quad{}-\underline{q}{}_{t}\bm{c}^{\prime}_{t}+\underline{q}^{\prime}{\hskip-3.00003pt}_{t}\bm{c}_{t}\bigg)\left(1+f_{s}^{\prime\prime}\right)

(155)

and the traceless and symmetric quadrupole transition rate is

\displaystyle\underline{I}_{2t\to s}^{\underline{q}{}_{t}}

\displaystyle=-\frac{W_{2t\to s}}{3}\,\bigg[{-}\big(\underline{q}{}_{t}\underline{q}^{\prime}{\hskip-3.00003pt}_{t}+\underline{q}^{\prime}{\hskip-3.00003pt}_{t}\underline{q}{}_{t}\big)+\frac{2}{3}\Tr\big(\underline{q}{}_{t}\underline{q}^{\prime}{\hskip-3.00003pt}_{t}\big)\,\mathds{1}_{3}

\displaystyle\quad{}-\big(\bm{c}_{t}\otimes\bm{c}^{\prime}_{t}+\bm{c}^{\prime}_{t}\otimes\bm{c}_{t}\big)+\frac{2}{3}\,\big(\bm{c}_{t}\cdot\bm{c}^{\prime}_{t}\big)\,\mathds{1}_{3}

\displaystyle\quad{}+\frac{2\sqrt{6}}{3}\,\big(\underline{q}{}_{t}n^{\prime}_{t}+n_{t}\underline{q}^{\prime}{\hskip-3.00003pt}_{t}\big)\bigg]\left(1+f_{s}^{\prime\prime}\right).

(156)

The basis-independent transition rate then assumes the form

I_{2t\to s}^{\widehat{{f}}_{t}}=-W_{2t\to s}\Tr_{t^{\prime}}\Big\{\widehat{P}_{s},\widehat{{f}}_{t}\otimes\widehat{f}^{\prime}_{t}\Big\}\left(1+f_{s}^{\prime\prime}\right),

(157)

where
\Tr_{t^{\prime}}
is the partial trace over the sector of the triplet exciton with momentum
\bm{k}^{\prime}
. The reverse fission process is then described by

I_{s\to 2t}^{\widehat{{f}}_{t}}=W_{s\to 2t}\Tr_{t^{\prime}}\bigg\{\widehat{P}_{s},\Big(\mathds{1}_{3}+\widehat{{f}}_{t}\Big)\otimes\Big(\mathds{1}_{3}+\widehat{f}^{\prime}_{t}\Big)\bigg\}f_{s}^{\prime\prime}.

(158)

III.7
Absorption of a triplet exciton by an electron

The process
e+t\leftrightarrow e
is more complicated than the previous cases because both particle species carry spin, in contrast to, e.g.,
t+s\leftrightarrow t
. For the process
e+t\leftrightarrow e
to be possible, the total spin of the electron and triplet exciton on the left-hand side must be
1/2
, i.e., they must form a doublet. The two states are

\displaystyle|\psi^{d}_{\uparrow}\rangle

\displaystyle=\frac{\big|\frac{1}{2}\big\rangle|0\rangle-\sqrt{2}\,\big|{-}\frac{1}{2}\big\rangle|1\rangle}{\sqrt{3}},

(159)

\displaystyle|\psi^{d}_{\downarrow}\rangle

\displaystyle=\frac{\sqrt{2}\,\big|\frac{1}{2}\big\rangle|{-}1\rangle-\big|{-}\frac{1}{2}\big\rangle|0\rangle}{\sqrt{3}}.

(160)

These relations allow us to read off the numerical prefactors in the rates.

III.7.1
Point of view of the triplet excitons

In the limit of
z
polarization, the three transition rates describing triplet excitons are

\displaystyle I_{e+t\to e}^{t_{1}}

\displaystyle=-\frac{2W_{e+t\to e}}{3}\,f^{\prime}_{e\downarrow}f_{t,1}(1-f^{\prime\prime}_{e\uparrow}),

(161)

\displaystyle I_{e+t\to e}^{t_{0}}

\displaystyle=-\frac{W_{e+t\to e}}{3}\,\big[f^{\prime}_{e\uparrow}f_{t,0}(1-f^{\prime\prime}_{e\uparrow})+f^{\prime}_{e\downarrow}f_{t,0}(1-f^{\prime\prime}_{e\downarrow})\big],

(162)

\displaystyle I_{e+t\to e}^{t_{\bar{1}}}

\displaystyle=-\frac{2W_{e+t\to e}}{3}\,f^{\prime}_{e\uparrow}f_{t,\bar{1}}(1-f^{\prime\prime}_{e\downarrow}).

(163)

The
\mathrm{SU}(2)
-invariant transition rates in terms of multipole coefficients are obtained in analogy to the previous cases. For the triplet-exciton number density we find

\displaystyle I_{e+t\to e}^{{n_{t}}}

\displaystyle=-\frac{W_{e+t\to e}}{18}\,\big(6{n_{t}}n^{\prime}_{e}-2\sqrt{6}\,\bm{c}_{t}\cdot\bm{c}^{\prime}_{e}

\displaystyle\quad{}-3n^{\prime}_{e}n^{\prime\prime}_{e}{n_{t}}+{n_{t}}\bm{c}^{\prime}_{e}\cdot\bm{c}^{\prime\prime}_{e}

\displaystyle\quad{}+\sqrt{6}\,\bm{c}_{t}\cdot\bm{c}^{\prime}_{e}n^{\prime\prime}_{e}-\sqrt{6}\,\bm{c}_{t}\cdot\bm{c}^{\prime\prime}_{e}n^{\prime}_{e}+\sqrt{6}\,\bm{c}^{\prime}_{e}\cdot\underline{q}{}_{t}\bm{c}^{\prime\prime}_{e}\big),

(164)

for the spin density

\displaystyle\bm{I}_{e+t\to e}^{\bm{c}_{t}}

\displaystyle=-\frac{W_{e+t\to e}}{18}\,\bigg[6\bm{c}_{t}n^{\prime}_{e}-2\sqrt{6}\,{n_{t}}\bm{c}^{\prime}_{e}-3\underline{q}{}_{t}\bm{c}^{\prime}_{e}

\displaystyle\quad{}+\sqrt{6}\,{n_{t}}\,(\bm{c}^{\prime}_{e}n^{\prime\prime}_{e}-n^{\prime}_{e}\bm{c}^{\prime\prime}_{e})

\displaystyle\quad{}+\frac{3}{2}\,(\bm{c}_{t}\cdot\bm{c}^{\prime}_{e})\bm{c}^{\prime\prime}_{e}+\frac{3}{2}\,(\bm{c}_{t}\cdot\bm{c}^{\prime\prime}_{e})\bm{c}^{\prime}_{e}

\displaystyle\quad{}-3\bm{c}_{t}n^{\prime}_{e}n^{\prime\prime}_{e}+\frac{3}{2}\,n^{\prime\prime}_{e}\underline{q}{}_{t}\bm{c}^{\prime}_{e}-\frac{3}{2}\,n^{\prime}_{e}\underline{q}{}_{t}\bm{c}^{\prime\prime}_{e}\bigg],

(165)

and for the symmetric traceless quadrupole density

\displaystyle\underline{I}_{e+t\to e}^{\underline{q}{}_{t}}=-\frac{W_{e+t\to e}}{18}

\displaystyle{}\times\bigg({-}3\,\bigg[\bm{c}^{\prime}_{e}\otimes\bm{c}_{t}+\bm{c}_{t}\otimes\bm{c}^{\prime}_{e}-\frac{2}{3}\,(\bm{c}^{\prime}_{e}\cdot\bm{c}_{t})\,\mathds{1}_{3}\bigg]+6n^{\prime}_{e}\underline{q}{}_{t}

\displaystyle\quad{}-3n^{\prime}_{e}n^{\prime\prime}_{e}\underline{q}{}_{t}

\displaystyle\quad{}+\sqrt{6}\bigg[\bm{c}^{\prime}_{e}\otimes\bm{c}^{\prime\prime}_{e}+\bm{c}^{\prime\prime}_{e}\otimes\bm{c}^{\prime}_{e}-\frac{2}{3}\,(\bm{c}^{\prime}_{e}\cdot\bm{c}^{\prime\prime}_{e})\,\mathds{1}_{3}\bigg]{n_{t}}

\displaystyle\quad{}+\frac{3}{2}\,\bigg[\bm{c}^{\prime}_{e}\otimes\bm{c}_{t}+\bm{c}_{t}\otimes\bm{c}^{\prime}_{e}-\frac{2}{3}\,(\bm{c}^{\prime}_{e}\cdot\bm{c}_{t})\,\mathds{1}_{3}\bigg]n^{\prime\prime}_{e}

\displaystyle\quad{}-\frac{3}{2}\,\bigg[\bm{c}_{t}\otimes\bm{c}^{\prime\prime}_{e}+\bm{c}^{\prime\prime}_{e}\otimes\bm{c}_{t}-\frac{2}{3}\,(\bm{c}_{t}\cdot\bm{c}^{\prime\prime}_{e})\,\mathds{1}_{3}\bigg]n^{\prime}_{e}

\displaystyle\quad{}+3\bigg[(\bm{c}^{\prime}_{e}\cdot\bm{c}^{\prime\prime}_{e})\underline{q}{}_{t}+\frac{1}{2}\,\bm{c}^{\prime}_{e}\otimes(\underline{q}{}_{t}\bm{c}^{\prime\prime}_{e})+\frac{1}{2}\,(\underline{q}{}_{t}\bm{c}^{\prime\prime}_{e})\otimes\bm{c}^{\prime}_{e}

\displaystyle\qquad{}-\frac{1}{2}\,\bm{c}^{\prime\prime}_{e}\otimes(\underline{q}{}_{t}\bm{c}^{\prime}_{e})-\frac{1}{2}\,(\underline{q}{}_{t}\bm{c}^{\prime}_{e})\otimes\bm{c}^{\prime\prime}_{e}

\displaystyle\qquad{}-(\bm{c}^{\prime}_{e}\otimes\bm{c}^{\prime\prime}_{e})\underline{q}{}_{t}-\underline{q}{}_{t}(\bm{c}^{\prime\prime}_{e}\otimes\bm{c}^{\prime}_{e})+\frac{2}{3}\,\bm{c}^{\prime}_{e}\cdot\underline{q}{}_{t}\bm{c}^{\prime\prime}_{e}\,\mathds{1}_{3}\bigg]\bigg).

(166)

Like for the process
e+h\to t
, one might guess at this
\mathrm{SU}(2)
-invariant form based on the limit of
z
-polarized spins but cannot infer it rigorously. The problem lies in the terms containing three
\mathbf{c}
vectors and in the terms with two
\mathbf{c}
vectors and the triplet-exciton quadrupole tensor
\underline{q}{}_{t}
. The above expressions have thus been checked by comparing them with the basis-independent expression describing this process, which takes the form

\displaystyle I_{e+t\to e}^{\widehat{{f}}_{t}}

\displaystyle=-\frac{W_{e+t\to e}}{2}\,\Tr_{e}\bigg[\Big(\mathds{1}_{2}-\widehat{f}^{\prime\prime}_{e}\Big)_{6}\widehat{P}_{d}\Big(\widehat{f}^{\prime}_{e}\otimes\widehat{{f}}_{t}\Big)

\displaystyle\quad{}+\Big(\widehat{f}^{\prime}_{e}\otimes\widehat{{f}}_{t}\Big)\widehat{P}_{d}\Big(\mathds{1}_{2}-\widehat{f}^{\prime\prime}_{e}\Big)_{6}\bigg],

(167)

where
\widehat{P}_{d}
is the projection operator onto the doublet subspace. This is a
6\times 6
matrix. The notation
\left(\bullet\right)_{6}
means that a
2\times 2
matrix on the doublet subspace is extended to the full two-particle space by adding a
4\times 4
null matrix on the quartet subspace. The matrix is then transformed into the product basis for electron and triplet-exciton spin to be consistent with
\widehat{{f}}_{e}\otimes\widehat{{f}}_{t}
.

III.7.2
Point of view of the electron

The perspective of the electron leads to in-scattering and out-scattering terms. The resulting transition rates for the two spin orientations are

\displaystyle I_{e+t\to e}^{e\uparrow}=-\frac{W_{e+t\to e}}{3}

\displaystyle\quad{}\times\big[2f_{e\uparrow}f^{\prime}_{t,\bar{1}}(1-f^{\prime\prime}_{e\downarrow})+f_{e\uparrow}f^{\prime}_{t,0}(1-f^{\prime\prime}_{e\uparrow})\big]

\displaystyle{}+\frac{W^{\prime\prime\prime}_{e+t\to e}}{3}\,\big[f^{\prime}_{e\uparrow}f^{\prime\prime\prime}_{t,0}(1-f_{e\uparrow})+2f^{\prime}_{e\downarrow}f^{\prime\prime\prime}_{t,1}(1-f_{e\uparrow})\big],

(168)

\displaystyle I_{e+t\to e}^{e\downarrow}=-\frac{W_{e+t\to e}}{3}

\displaystyle\quad{}\times\big[2f_{e\downarrow}f^{\prime}_{t,1}(1-f^{\prime\prime}_{e\uparrow})+f_{e\downarrow}f^{\prime}_{t,0}(1-f^{\prime\prime}_{e\downarrow})\big]

\displaystyle{}+\frac{W^{\prime\prime\prime}_{e+t\to e}}{3}\,\big[f^{\prime}_{e\downarrow}f^{\prime\prime\prime}_{t,0}(1-f_{e\downarrow})+2f^{\prime}_{e\uparrow}f^{\prime\prime\prime}_{t,\bar{1}}(1-f_{e\downarrow})\big],

(169)

where the Clebsch-Gordan coefficients have been used. As the in-scattering and out-scattering terms have the same structure it is sufficient to investigate out-scattering. For the multipole transition rates this yields

\displaystyle I_{e+t\to e}^{n_{e},\,\mathrm{out}}

\displaystyle=-\frac{W_{e+t\to e}}{18}\,\big(6\sqrt{6}\,n_{e}n^{\prime}_{t}-12\bm{c}_{e}\cdot\bm{c}^{\prime}_{t}

\displaystyle\quad{}-3\sqrt{6}\,n_{e}n^{\prime}_{t}n^{\prime\prime}_{e}-6n_{e}\bm{c}^{\prime\prime}_{e}\cdot\bm{c}^{\prime}_{t}+\sqrt{6}\,n^{\prime}_{t}\bm{c}_{e}\cdot\bm{c}^{\prime\prime}_{e}

\displaystyle\quad{}+6n^{\prime\prime}_{e}\bm{c}_{e}\cdot\bm{c}^{\prime}_{t}+6\bm{c}_{e}\cdot\underline{q}^{\prime}{\hskip-3.00003pt}_{t}\bm{c}^{\prime\prime}_{e}\big),

(170)

\displaystyle\bm{I}_{e+t\to e}^{\bm{c}_{t},\,\mathrm{out}}

\displaystyle=-\frac{W_{e+t\to e}}{18}\,\bigg[12n_{e}\bm{c}^{\prime}_{t}-6\sqrt{6}\,\bm{c}_{e}n^{\prime}_{t}

\displaystyle\quad{}-\sqrt{6}\,n_{e}n^{\prime}_{t}\bm{c}^{\prime\prime}_{e}-6n_{e}\bm{c}^{\prime}_{t}n^{\prime\prime}_{e}+3\sqrt{6}\,\bm{c}_{e}n^{\prime}_{t}n^{\prime\prime}_{e}

\displaystyle\quad{}+6\bm{c}_{e}(\bm{c}^{\prime}_{t}\cdot\bm{c}^{\prime\prime}_{e})-\frac{3}{2}\,n_{e}\underline{q}^{\prime}{\hskip-3.00003pt}_{t}\bm{c}^{\prime\prime}_{e}\bigg],

(171)

which can be combined to obtain the full transition rate from the point of view of the electron.

Together with the in-scattering terms the basis-independent form is then

\displaystyle I_{e+t\to e}^{\widehat{{f}}_{e}}

\displaystyle=\frac{W^{\prime\prime\prime}_{e+t\to e}}{2}\,\Tr_{t^{\prime\prime\prime}}\bigg[\Big(\mathds{1}_{2}-\widehat{{f}}_{e}\Big)_{6}\widehat{P}_{d}\Big(\widehat{f}^{\prime}_{e}\otimes\widehat{f}^{\prime\prime\prime}_{t}\Big)

\displaystyle\quad{}+\Big(\widehat{f}^{\prime}_{e}\otimes\widehat{f}^{\prime\prime\prime}_{t}\Big)\widehat{P}_{d}\Big(\mathds{1}_{2}-\widehat{{f}}_{e}\Big)_{6}\bigg]

\displaystyle\quad{}-\frac{W_{e+t\to e}}{2}\,\Tr_{t^{\prime}}\bigg[\Big(\mathds{1}_{2}-\widehat{f}^{\prime\prime}_{e}\Big)_{6}\widehat{P}_{d}\Big(\widehat{{f}}_{e}\otimes\widehat{f}^{\prime}_{t}\Big)

\displaystyle\quad{}+\Big(\widehat{{f}}_{e}\otimes\widehat{f}^{\prime}_{t}\Big)\widehat{P}_{d}\Big(\mathds{1}_{2}-\widehat{f}^{\prime\prime}_{e}\Big)_{6}\bigg].

(172)

The absorption and emission of a triplet exciton by a hole is analogous.

III.8
Fusion of triplet excitons into a triplet exciton

The fusion of two triplet excitons into a triplet exciton
StU89
is the most complex process considered here. In the case of
z
polarization, the transition terms of the components of the density matrix read as

\displaystyle I_{2t\to t}^{t_{1}}=W^{\prime\prime\prime}_{2t\to t}\,\frac{f^{\prime\prime\prime}_{t,1}f^{\prime}_{t,0}}{2}\,(1+f_{t,1})

\displaystyle\quad{}-2W_{2t\to t}\bigg[\frac{f_{t,1}f^{\prime}_{t,\bar{1}}}{2}\,(1+f^{\prime\prime}_{t,0})+\frac{f_{t,1}f^{\prime}_{t,0}}{2}\,(1+f^{\prime\prime}_{t,1})\bigg],

(173)

\displaystyle I_{2t\to t}^{t_{0}}=W^{\prime\prime\prime}_{2t\to t}\,\frac{f^{\prime}_{t,1}f^{\prime\prime\prime}_{t,\bar{1}}}{2}\,(1+f_{t,0})

\displaystyle\quad{}-2W_{2t\to t}\bigg[\frac{f^{\prime}_{t,1}f_{t,0}}{2}\,(1+f^{\prime\prime}_{t,1})+\frac{f^{\prime}_{t,\bar{1}}f_{t,0}}{2}\,(1+f^{\prime\prime}_{t,\bar{1}})\bigg],

(174)

\displaystyle I_{2t\to t}^{t_{\bar{1}}}=W^{\prime\prime\prime}_{2t\to t}\,\frac{f^{\prime\prime\prime}_{t,\bar{1}}f^{\prime}_{t,0}}{2}\,(1+f_{t,\bar{1}})

\displaystyle\quad{}-2W_{2t\to t}\bigg[\frac{f^{\prime}_{t,1}f_{t,\bar{1}}}{2}\,(1+f^{\prime\prime}_{t,0})+\frac{f_{t,\bar{1}}f^{\prime}_{t,0}}{2}\,(1+f^{\prime\prime}_{t,\bar{1}})\bigg].

(175)

In this process, the in-scattering and out-scattering terms differ in structure. This makes it necessary to investigate both terms separately.

III.8.1
In-scattering for
2t\to t

Combining Eqs. (
III.8
)–(
175
), we can obtain the transition rates expressed in the multipole expansion. Their generalization to the
\mathrm{SU}(2)
-invariant form is ambiguous at first glance. However, the
\mathrm{SU}(2)
-invariant form can be obtained in analogy to the formation of triplet excitons out of free charge carriers. We construct the density matrix
\widehat{f}^{\prime}_{t}\otimes\widehat{f}^{\prime\prime\prime}_{t}
on the product space of the two triplet-exciton spins and project it onto the triplet subspace. The resulting
3\times 3
matrix, which will be referred to as
\widehat{f}_{tt}
, can be expressed in terms of coefficients of the nine basis matrices
\widehat{S}_{i}
,
i=1,2,3
, and
\widehat{Q}_{j}
,
j=1,2,3,4,5
.
For a spin along the
z
axis, only three coefficients do not vanish:

\displaystyle n_{tt}

\displaystyle=\frac{2\sqrt{6}\,n^{\prime}_{t}n^{\prime\prime\prime}_{t}-\sqrt{6}\,c_{t}^{z\prime}c_{t}^{z\prime\prime\prime}-\sqrt{6}\,q^{\prime}_{t5}q^{\prime\prime\prime}_{t5}}{6},

(176)

\displaystyle c_{tt}^{z}

\displaystyle=\frac{\sqrt{6}\,c_{t}^{z\prime}n^{\prime\prime\prime}_{t}-2\sqrt{3}\,c_{t}^{z\prime}q^{\prime\prime\prime}_{t5}+\sqrt{6}\,c_{t}^{z\prime\prime\prime}n^{\prime}_{t}-2\sqrt{3}\,c_{t}^{z\prime\prime\prime}q^{\prime}_{t5}}{6},

(177)

\displaystyle q_{tt5}

\displaystyle=\frac{2\sqrt{2}\,c_{t}^{z\prime}c_{t}^{z\prime\prime\prime}-2n^{\prime}_{t}q^{\prime\prime\prime}_{t5}-2n^{\prime\prime\prime}_{t}q^{\prime}_{t5}-2\sqrt{2}\,q^{\prime}_{t5}q^{\prime\prime\prime}_{t5}}{6}.

(178)

Combining these equations and using Eqs. (
III.8
)–(
175
), we can unambiguously generalize the rates to their
\mathrm{SU}(2)
-invariant form:

\displaystyle I_{2t\to t}^{{n_{t}},\,\mathrm{in}}

\displaystyle=W^{\prime\prime\prime}_{2t\to t}\bigg[{n}_{tt}+\frac{\sqrt{6}}{3}\,n_{t}{n}_{tt}+\frac{\sqrt{6}}{3}\,\bm{c}_{t}\cdot\bm{c}_{tt}

\displaystyle\quad{}+\frac{\sqrt{6}}{3}\,\vec{q}_{t}\cdot\vec{q}_{tt}\bigg],

(179)

\displaystyle\bm{I}_{2t\to t}^{\bm{c}_{t},\,\mathrm{in}}

\displaystyle=W^{\prime\prime\prime}_{2t\to t}\bigg[\bm{c}_{tt}+\frac{\sqrt{6}}{3}\,{n}_{tt}\bm{c}^{\prime}_{t}+\frac{\sqrt{6}}{3}\,\bm{c}_{tt}n^{\prime}_{t}

\displaystyle\quad{}+\frac{1}{2}\,\underline{q}{}_{tt}\bm{c}^{\prime}_{t}+\frac{1}{2}\,\underline{q}^{\prime}{\hskip-3.00003pt}_{t}\bm{c}_{tt}\bigg],

(180)

\displaystyle\underline{I}_{2t\to t}^{\underline{q}{}_{t},\,\mathrm{in}}

\displaystyle=W^{\prime\prime\prime}_{2t\to t}\bigg[\underline{q}{}_{tt}+\frac{\sqrt{6}}{3}\,{n}_{tt}\underline{q}{}_{t}+\frac{\sqrt{6}}{3}\,n_{t}\underline{q}{}_{tt}

\displaystyle\quad{}-\frac{1}{2}\,\underline{q}{}_{tt}\underline{q}{}_{t}-\frac{1}{2}\,\underline{q}{}_{t}\underline{q}{}_{tt}+\frac{1}{3}\,\Tr\big(\underline{q}{}_{tt}\underline{q}{}_{t}\big)\mathds{1}_{3}

\displaystyle\quad{}-\frac{1}{3}\,(\bm{c}_{tt}\cdot\bm{c}_{t})\mathds{1}_{3}+\frac{1}{2}\,(\bm{c}_{tt}\otimes\bm{c}_{t}+\bm{c}_{t}\otimes\bm{c}_{tt})\bigg].

(181)

The basis-independent in-scattering term reads as

\displaystyle I_{2t\to t}^{\widehat{{f}}_{t},\,\mathrm{in}}

\displaystyle=\frac{W^{\prime\prime\prime}_{2t\to t}}{2}\,\Tr_{t^{\prime\prime\prime}}\bigg[\Big(\mathds{1}_{3}+\widehat{{f}}_{t}\Big)_{9}\widehat{P}_{t}\Big(\widehat{f}^{\prime}_{t}\otimes\widehat{f}^{\prime\prime\prime}_{t}\Big)

\displaystyle\quad{}+\Big(\widehat{f}^{\prime}_{t}\otimes\widehat{f}^{\prime\prime\prime}_{t}\Big)\widehat{P}_{t}\Big(\mathds{1}_{3}+\widehat{{f}}_{t}\Big)_{9}\bigg].

(182)

The notation
(\bullet)_{9}
is analogous to
(\bullet)_{4}
in Sec.
III.3.2
and to
(\bullet)_{6}
in Sec.
III.7.1
.

III.8.2
Out-scattering for
2t\to t

For out-scattering, the strategy employed for in-scattering does not work since the rates for the case of
z
polarization are not symmetric in the two incoming triplet excitons at the outer momentum
\bm{k}
and the running momentum
\bm{k}^{\prime}
. Hence, the projected density matrix on the product space,
\widehat{f}_{tt}
, is not useful. The situation is analogous to the process
e+h\to t
from the point of view of the electron or hole; see Sec.
III.3.2
. Like for
e+h\to t
, the matrix-valued transition rate in terms of projection operators and density matrices

\displaystyle I_{2t\to t}^{\widehat{{f}}_{t},\,\mathrm{out}}

\displaystyle=-W_{2t\to t}\,\Tr_{t^{\prime}}\bigg[\Big(\mathds{1}_{3}+\widehat{f}^{\prime\prime}_{t}\Big)_{9}\widehat{P}_{t}\Big(\widehat{{f}}_{t}\otimes\widehat{f}^{\prime}_{t}\Big)

\displaystyle\quad{}+\Big(\widehat{{f}}_{t}\otimes\widehat{f}^{\prime}_{t}\Big)\widehat{P}_{t}\Big(\mathds{1}_{3}+\widehat{f}^{\prime\prime}_{t}\Big)_{9}\bigg]

(183)

can be used to read off the correct
\mathrm{SU}(2)
-invariant multipole transition rates. For the triplet exciton number density this yields

\displaystyle I_{2t\to t}^{{n_{t}},\,\mathrm{out}}

\displaystyle=-\frac{W_{2t\to t}}{3}\,\bigg[2\sqrt{6}\,n_{t}n^{\prime}_{t}-\sqrt{6}\,\bm{c}_{t}\cdot\bm{c}^{\prime}_{t}-\frac{\sqrt{6}}{2}\,\Tr\underline{q}{}_{t}\underline{q}^{\prime}{\hskip-3.00003pt}_{t}

\displaystyle\quad{}+4n_{t}n^{\prime}_{t}n^{\prime\prime}_{t}+2\big(n_{t}\bm{c}^{\prime}_{t}\cdot\bm{c}^{\prime\prime}_{t}+n^{\prime}_{t}\bm{c}_{t}\cdot\bm{c}^{\prime\prime}_{t}+n^{\prime\prime}_{t}\bm{c}_{t}\cdot\bm{c}^{\prime}_{t}\big)-\Big(n_{t}\Tr\underline{q}^{\prime}{\hskip-3.00003pt}_{t}\underline{q}^{\prime\prime}{\hskip-5.0pt}_{t}\hskip 1.49994pt+n^{\prime}_{t}\Tr\underline{q}{}_{t}\underline{q}^{\prime\prime}{\hskip-5.0pt}_{t}\hskip 1.49994pt+n^{\prime\prime}_{t}\Tr\underline{q}{}_{t}\underline{q}^{\prime}{\hskip-3.00003pt}_{t}\Big)

\displaystyle\quad{}-\sqrt{6}\,\Big(\bm{c}_{t}\cdot\underline{q}^{\prime}{\hskip-3.00003pt}_{t}\bm{c}^{\prime\prime}_{t}+\bm{c}^{\prime}_{t}\cdot\underline{q}{}_{t}\bm{c}^{\prime\prime}_{t}+\bm{c}_{t}\cdot\underline{q}^{\prime\prime}{\hskip-5.0pt}_{t}\hskip 1.49994pt\bm{c}^{\prime}_{t}\Big)-\frac{\sqrt{6}}{2}\,\Big(\Tr\underline{q}{}_{t}\underline{q}^{\prime}{\hskip-3.00003pt}_{t}\underline{q}^{\prime\prime}{\hskip-5.0pt}_{t}\hskip 1.49994pt+\Tr\underline{q}{}_{t}\underline{q}^{\prime\prime}{\hskip-5.0pt}_{t}\hskip 1.49994pt\underline{q}^{\prime}{\hskip-3.00003pt}_{t}\Big)\bigg].

(184)

The spin transition rate becomes

\displaystyle\bm{I}_{2t\to t}^{\bm{c}_{t},\,\mathrm{out}}

\displaystyle=-\frac{W_{2t\to t}}{6}\,\Big[2\sqrt{6}\,(-2n_{t}\bm{c}^{\prime}_{t}+n^{\prime}_{t}\bm{c}_{t})-3\Big(\underline{q}{}_{t}\bm{c}^{\prime}_{t}+\underline{q}^{\prime}{\hskip-3.00003pt}_{t}\bm{c}_{t}\Big)-4\big(n_{t}n^{\prime\prime}_{t}\bm{c}^{\prime}_{t}-n_{t}n^{\prime}_{t}\bm{c}^{\prime\prime}_{t}-2n_{t}n^{\prime\prime}_{t}\bm{c}^{\prime}_{t}\big)

\displaystyle\quad{}-\sqrt{6}\,\Big(n^{\prime}_{t}\underline{q}^{\prime\prime}{\hskip-5.0pt}_{t}\hskip 1.49994pt\bm{c}_{t}-n^{\prime}_{t}\underline{q}{}_{t}\bm{c}^{\prime\prime}_{t}+n^{\prime\prime}_{t}\underline{q}^{\prime}{\hskip-3.00003pt}_{t}\bm{c}_{t}+n^{\prime\prime}_{t}\underline{q}{}_{t}\bm{c}^{\prime}_{t}+2n_{t}\underline{q}^{\prime}{\hskip-3.00003pt}_{t}\bm{c}^{\prime\prime}_{t}-2n_{t}\underline{q}^{\prime\prime}{\hskip-5.0pt}_{t}\hskip 1.49994pt\bm{c}^{\prime}_{t}\Big)

\displaystyle\quad{}-3\big(\bm{c}^{\prime}_{t}(\bm{c}_{t}\cdot\bm{c}^{\prime\prime}_{t})+\bm{c}^{\prime\prime}_{t}(\bm{c}^{\prime}_{t}\cdot\bm{c}_{t})-2\bm{c}_{t}(\bm{c}^{\prime}_{t}\cdot\bm{c}^{\prime\prime}_{t})\big)-3\Big(\underline{q}^{\prime}{\hskip-3.00003pt}_{t}\underline{q}^{\prime\prime}{\hskip-5.0pt}_{t}\hskip 1.49994pt\bm{c}_{t}+\underline{q}^{\prime\prime}{\hskip-5.0pt}_{t}\hskip 1.49994pt\underline{q}^{\prime}{\hskip-3.00003pt}_{t}\bm{c}_{t}+\underline{q}{}_{t}\underline{q}^{\prime}{\hskip-3.00003pt}_{t}\bm{c}^{\prime\prime}_{t}-\underline{q}{}_{t}\underline{q}^{\prime\prime}{\hskip-5.0pt}_{t}\hskip 1.49994pt\bm{c}_{t}\Big)\Big].

(185)

The quadrupole part reads as

\displaystyle\underline{I}_{2t\to t}^{\underline{q}{}_{t},\,\mathrm{out}}

\displaystyle=-\frac{W_{2t\to t}}{6}\,\bigg(2\sqrt{6}\,\big(-n_{t}\underline{q}^{\prime}{\hskip-3.00003pt}_{t}+2n^{\prime}_{t}\underline{q}{}_{t})-3\big[\bm{c}_{t}\otimes\bm{c}^{\prime}_{t}+\bm{c}^{\prime}_{t}\otimes\bm{c}_{t}-2(\bm{c}^{\prime}_{t}\cdot\bm{c}_{t})\mathds{1}_{3}\big]-3\big(\underline{q}{}_{t}\underline{q}^{\prime}{\hskip-3.00003pt}_{t}+\underline{q}^{\prime}{\hskip-3.00003pt}_{t}\underline{q}{}_{t}\big)-2\Tr\big(\underline{q}{}_{t}\underline{q}^{\prime}{\hskip-3.00003pt}_{t}\big)\mathds{1}_{3}

\displaystyle\quad{}-4\Big(n_{t}n^{\prime}_{t}\underline{q}^{\prime\prime}{\hskip-5.0pt}_{t}\hskip 1.49994pt+n_{t}n^{\prime\prime}_{t}\underline{q}^{\prime}{\hskip-3.00003pt}_{t}-2n^{\prime}_{t}n^{\prime\prime}_{t}\underline{q}{}_{t}\Big)

\displaystyle\quad{}-\sqrt{6}\,\big[2n_{t}(\bm{c}^{\prime}_{t}\otimes\bm{c}^{\prime\prime}_{t}+\bm{c}^{\prime\prime}_{t}\otimes\bm{c}^{\prime}_{t})-n^{\prime}_{t}(\bm{c}_{t}\otimes\bm{c}^{\prime\prime}_{t}+\bm{c}^{\prime\prime}_{t}\otimes\bm{c}_{t})+n^{\prime\prime}_{t}(\bm{c}_{t}\otimes\bm{c}^{\prime}_{t}+\bm{c}^{\prime}_{t}\otimes\bm{c}_{t})\big]

\displaystyle\quad{}+\frac{2\sqrt{6}}{3}\,\Big[2n_{t}(\bm{c}^{\prime}_{t}\cdot\bm{c}^{\prime\prime}_{t})-n^{\prime}_{t}(\bm{c}_{t}\cdot\bm{c}^{\prime\prime}_{t})+n^{\prime\prime}_{t}(\bm{c}_{t}\cdot\bm{c}^{\prime}_{t})\Big]\mathds{1}_{3}

\displaystyle\quad{}-\sqrt{6}\,\Big[2n_{t}\Big(\underline{q}^{\prime}{\hskip-3.00003pt}_{t}\underline{q}^{\prime\prime}{\hskip-5.0pt}_{t}\hskip 1.49994pt+\underline{q}^{\prime\prime}{\hskip-5.0pt}_{t}\hskip 1.49994pt\underline{q}^{\prime}{\hskip-3.00003pt}_{t}\Big)-n^{\prime}_{t}\Big(\underline{q}{}_{t}\underline{q}^{\prime\prime}{\hskip-5.0pt}_{t}\hskip 1.49994pt+\underline{q}^{\prime\prime}{\hskip-5.0pt}_{t}\hskip 1.49994pt\underline{q}{}_{t}\Big)-n^{\prime\prime}_{t}\Big(\underline{q}{}_{t}\underline{q}^{\prime}{\hskip-3.00003pt}_{t}+\underline{q}^{\prime}{\hskip-3.00003pt}_{t}\underline{q}{}_{t}\Big)\Big]

\displaystyle\quad{}+\frac{2\sqrt{6}}{3}\,\Big[2n_{t}\Tr\big(\underline{q}^{\prime}{\hskip-3.00003pt}_{t}\underline{q}^{\prime\prime}{\hskip-5.0pt}_{t}\hskip 1.49994pt\big)-n^{\prime}_{t}\Tr\big(\underline{q}{}_{t}\underline{q}^{\prime\prime}{\hskip-5.0pt}_{t}\hskip 1.49994pt\big)-n^{\prime\prime}_{t}\Tr\big(\underline{q}{}_{t}\underline{q}^{\prime}{\hskip-3.00003pt}_{t}\big)\Big]\mathds{1}_{3}

\displaystyle\quad{}-3\Big[\big(\bm{c}_{t}\otimes\bm{c}^{\prime\prime}_{t}\big)\underline{q}^{\prime}{\hskip-3.00003pt}_{t}+\underline{q}^{\prime}{\hskip-3.00003pt}_{t}\big(\bm{c}^{\prime\prime}_{t}\otimes\bm{c}_{t}\big)-\big(\bm{c}_{t}\otimes\bm{c}^{\prime}_{t}\big)\underline{q}^{\prime\prime}{\hskip-5.0pt}_{t}\hskip 1.49994pt-\underline{q}^{\prime\prime}{\hskip-5.0pt}_{t}\hskip 1.49994pt\big(\bm{c}^{\prime}_{t}\otimes\bm{c}_{t}\big)-\big(\bm{c}^{\prime}_{t}\otimes\bm{c}^{\prime\prime}_{t}\big)\underline{q}{}_{t}-\underline{q}{}_{t}\big(\bm{c}^{\prime\prime}_{t}\otimes\bm{c}^{\prime}_{t}\big)

\displaystyle\qquad{}-\big(\bm{c}^{\prime\prime}_{t}\otimes\bm{c}^{\prime}_{t}\big)\underline{q}{}_{t}-\underline{q}{}_{t}\big(\bm{c}^{\prime}_{t}\otimes\bm{c}^{\prime\prime}_{t}\big)\Big]-2\Big(\bm{c}_{t}\cdot\underline{q}^{\prime\prime}{\hskip-5.0pt}_{t}\hskip 1.49994pt\bm{c}^{\prime}_{t}-\bm{c}_{t}\cdot\underline{q}^{\prime}{\hskip-3.00003pt}_{t}\bm{c}^{\prime\prime}_{t}+\bm{c}^{\prime\prime}_{t}\cdot\underline{q}{}_{t}\bm{c}^{\prime}_{t}\Big)\mathds{1}_{3}

\displaystyle\quad{}+3\Big(\underline{q}{}_{t}\underline{q}^{\prime}{\hskip-3.00003pt}_{t}\underline{q}^{\prime\prime}{\hskip-5.0pt}_{t}\hskip 1.49994pt+\underline{q}^{\prime\prime}{\hskip-5.0pt}_{t}\hskip 1.49994pt\underline{q}^{\prime}{\hskip-3.00003pt}_{t}\underline{q}{}_{t}+\underline{q}^{\prime}{\hskip-3.00003pt}_{t}\underline{q}^{\prime\prime}{\hskip-5.0pt}_{t}\hskip 1.49994pt\underline{q}{}_{t}+\underline{q}{}_{t}\underline{q}^{\prime\prime}{\hskip-5.0pt}_{t}\hskip 1.49994pt\underline{q}^{\prime}{\hskip-3.00003pt}_{t}\Big)-4\Tr\Big(\underline{q}{}_{t}\underline{q}^{\prime}{\hskip-3.00003pt}_{t}\underline{q}^{\prime\prime}{\hskip-5.0pt}_{t}\hskip 1.49994pt\Big)\mathds{1}_{3}-6\Tr\Big(\underline{q}^{\prime}{\hskip-3.00003pt}_{t}\underline{q}^{\prime\prime}{\hskip-5.0pt}_{t}\hskip 1.49994pt\Big)\underline{q}{}_{t}\bigg).

(186)

The derivation of the rates for the spin and for the quadrupole moment again require the basis-independent form in Eq. (
183
).

The reverse fission process
t\to 2t
can be investigated in the same way. In the out-scattering term, the introduction of
\widehat{f}_{1-tt}
leads to the same structure as in the in-scattering term for
2t\to t
.

IV
Examples

In this section, we illustrate the theoretical framework by applying it to a simple model. In order to reduce numerical complications, we consider a one-dimensional toy model containing fission and fusion processes, disorder scattering of singlet and triplet excitons, as well as relaxation of singlet and triplet excitons. All bare rates
W
are assumed to be independent of momentum, except as dictated by scattering being elastic.
The temperature is assumed to be sufficiently low so that the equilibrium thermal population of excitonic states is negligible. A magnetic field, if present, is taken to be uniform, constant in time, and oriented along the
z
direction.

The dispersion relations of singlet and triplet excitons are written as

\displaystyle\epsilon_{s}(k)

\displaystyle=\Delta\epsilon+\frac{\hbar^{2}k^{2}}{2m_{s}},

(187)

\displaystyle\epsilon_{t}(k,m)

\displaystyle=\frac{\hbar^{2}k^{2}}{2m_{t}}-g_{\mathrm{tr}}\mu_{B}Bm,

(188)

respectively, where
m=1,0,-1
specifies the spin state of triplet excitons and

\Delta\epsilon\equiv\frac{\hbar^{2}k_{0}^{2}}{2m_{t}}

(189)

is the energy difference between singlet and triplet excitons, which defines a momentum scale
k_{0}
.

Choosing our units such that
\hbar/m_{t}=1
and defining the parameters

\displaystyle\alpha

\displaystyle\equiv\frac{m_{t}}{m_{s}}=\frac{\hbar}{m_{s}},

(190)

\displaystyle\omega_{L}

\displaystyle\equiv\frac{1}{\hbar}\,g_{\mathrm{tr}}\mu_{B}B,

(191)

the Boltzmann equation for the singlet-exciton distribution function
f_{s}(x,k,t)
can be written as

\frac{\partial f_{s}}{\partial t}+\alpha k\,\frac{\partial f_{s}}{\partial x}=I^{f_{s}},

(192)

and the corresponding equation for the matrix-valued triplet-exciton distribution function
\widehat{f}_{t}(x,k,t)
as

\frac{\partial\widehat{f}_{t}}{\partial t}+k\,\frac{\partial\widehat{f}_{t}}{\partial x}+i\omega_{L}\,[\widehat{S}_{3},\widehat{f}_{t}]=\widehat{I}^{\widehat{f}_{t}}.

(193)

Here
I^{f_{s}}
and
\widehat{I}^{\widehat{f}_{t}}
are the full scattering integrals, which contain the following contributions, where we only make the momentum dependence explicit:

(a) Decay is discussed in Sec.
III.1
. It is described by the scattering integrals

\displaystyle I^{f_{s}}_{s\to\ket{0}}(k)

\displaystyle=-\widetilde{W}_{s\to\ket{0}}\,f_{s}(k)\equiv-\eta_{s0}\,f_{s}(k),

(194)

\displaystyle\widehat{I}^{\widehat{f}_{t}}_{t\to\ket{0}}(k)

\displaystyle=-\widetilde{W}_{t\to\ket{0}}\,\widehat{f}_{t}(k)\equiv-\eta_{t0}\,\widehat{f}_{t}(k).

(195)

x

(b) Disorder scattering is discussion in the introduction to Sec.
III
and is described by

\displaystyle I^{f_{s}}_{s\to s}

\displaystyle=\int\frac{dk^{\prime}}{2\pi}\,w_{s\to s^{\prime}}\,\delta\bigg(\frac{\hbar^{2}(k^{\prime})^{2}}{2m_{s}}-\frac{\hbar^{2}k^{2}}{2m_{s}}\bigg)\,[f_{s}(k^{\prime})-f_{s}(k)]

\displaystyle=w_{s\to s^{\prime}}\,\frac{m_{s}}{2\pi\hbar^{2}}\,\frac{f_{s}(-k)-f_{s}(k)}{|k|}

\displaystyle\equiv\eta^{\mathrm{dis}}_{s}\,\frac{f_{s}(-k)-f_{s}(k)}{|k|},

(196)

\displaystyle\widehat{I}^{\widehat{f}_{t}}_{t\to t}

\displaystyle=\int\frac{dk^{\prime}}{2\pi}\,w_{t\to t^{\prime}}\,\delta\bigg(\frac{\hbar^{2}(k^{\prime})^{2}}{2m_{t}}-\frac{\hbar^{2}k^{2}}{2m_{t}}\bigg)\,\Big[\widehat{f}_{t}(k^{\prime})-\widehat{f}_{t}(k)\Big]

\displaystyle=w_{t\to t^{\prime}}\,\frac{m_{t}}{2\pi\hbar^{2}}\,\frac{\widehat{f}_{t}(-k)-\widehat{f}_{t}(k)}{|k|}

\displaystyle\equiv\eta^{\mathrm{dis}}_{t}\,\frac{\widehat{f}_{t}(-k)-\widehat{f}_{t}(k)}{|k|}.

(197)

Note that the singlet-triplet energy difference
\Delta\epsilon
and the magnetic energy of triplet excitons cancel.

(c) Fusion is discussion in Sec.
III.6
. From the point of view of the singlet excitons, fusion is described by

\displaystyle I^{f_{s}}_{2t\to s}(k)

\displaystyle=\int\frac{dk^{\prime}}{2\pi}\,\frac{w_{2t\to s}}{2}\,\delta\bigg(\frac{\hbar^{2}(k^{\prime})^{2}}{2m_{t}}+\frac{\hbar^{2}(k-k^{\prime})^{2}}{2m_{t}}-\Delta\epsilon-\frac{\hbar^{2}k^{2}}{2m_{s}}\bigg)\Tr\big\{\widehat{P}_{s},\widehat{f}_{t}(k^{\prime})\otimes\widehat{f}_{t}(k-k^{\prime})\big\}\,[1+f_{s}(k)]

\displaystyle=w_{2t\to s}\,\frac{m_{t}}{4\pi\hbar^{2}}\,\frac{1}{\kappa}\,[1+f_{s}(k)]\bigg(\Tr\widehat{P}_{s}\bigg[\widehat{f}_{t}\bigg(\frac{k}{2}+\kappa\bigg)\otimes\widehat{f}_{t}\bigg(\frac{k}{2}-\kappa\bigg)\bigg]+\Tr\widehat{P}_{s}\bigg[\widehat{f}_{t}\bigg(\frac{k}{2}-\kappa\bigg)\otimes\widehat{f}_{t}\bigg(\frac{k}{2}+\kappa\bigg)\bigg]\bigg)

\displaystyle\equiv w_{\mathrm{fusion}}\,\frac{1}{\kappa}\,[1+f_{s}(k)]\bigg(\Tr\widehat{P}_{s}\bigg[\widehat{f}_{t}\bigg(\frac{k}{2}+\kappa\bigg)\otimes\widehat{f}_{t}\bigg(\frac{k}{2}-\kappa\bigg)\bigg]+\Tr\widehat{P}_{s}\bigg[\widehat{f}_{t}\bigg(\frac{k}{2}-\kappa\bigg)\otimes\widehat{f}_{t}\bigg(\frac{k}{2}+\kappa\bigg)\bigg]\bigg),

(198)

with

\kappa\equiv\sqrt{\textstyle k_{0}^{2}+\frac{m_{t}}{2m_{s}}\,k^{2}-\frac{k^{2}}{4}}=\sqrt{\textstyle k_{0}^{2}+\frac{\alpha}{2}\,k^{2}-\frac{k^{2}}{4}},

(199)

if
\kappa
is real, otherwise the scattering integral vanishes. Note that the magnetic energy of triplet excitons cancels. Introducing the unitary part of the antiunitary time-reversal operator,

\widehat{U}_{T}=e^{i\widehat{S}_{2}\pi}=\begin{pmatrix}0&0&1\\
0&-1&0\\
1&0&0\end{pmatrix}=\widehat{U}_{T}^{\dagger},

(200)

we can simplify the expression to

I^{f_{s}}_{2t\to s}(k)=\frac{2}{3}\,w_{\mathrm{fusion}}\,\frac{1}{\kappa}\,[1+f_{s}(k)]\Tr\widehat{f}_{t}\bigg(\frac{k}{2}+\kappa\bigg)\widehat{U}_{T}\widehat{f}_{t}^{\,T}\bigg(\frac{k}{2}-\kappa\bigg)\widehat{U}_{T}^{\dagger}.

(201)

The corresponding scattering integral for the triplet excitons reads as

\displaystyle\widehat{I}^{\widehat{f}_{t}}_{2t\to s}

\displaystyle=-\int\frac{dk^{\prime}}{2\pi}\,w_{2t\to s}\,\delta\bigg(\frac{\hbar^{2}k^{2}}{2m_{t}}+\frac{\hbar^{2}(k^{\prime})^{2}}{2m_{t}}-\Delta\epsilon-\frac{\hbar^{2}(k+k^{\prime})^{2}}{2m_{s}}\bigg)\Tr_{t^{\prime}}\Big\{\widehat{P}_{s},\widehat{f}_{t}(k)\otimes\widehat{f}_{t}(k^{\prime})\Big\}\,[1+f_{s}(k+k^{\prime})]

\displaystyle=-\frac{2w_{\mathrm{fusion}}}{|1-\alpha|}\,\frac{1}{\kappa^{\prime}}\,\bigg(\Tr_{t^{\prime}}\bigg\{\widehat{P}_{s},\widehat{f}_{t}(k)\otimes\widehat{f}_{t}\bigg(\frac{\alpha}{1-\alpha}\,k+\kappa^{\prime}\bigg)\bigg\}\bigg[1+f_{s}\bigg(\frac{1}{1-\alpha}\,k+\kappa^{\prime}\bigg)\bigg]

\displaystyle\quad{}+\Tr_{t^{\prime}}\bigg\{\widehat{P}_{s},\widehat{f}_{t}(k)\otimes\widehat{f}_{t}\bigg(\frac{\alpha}{1-\alpha}\,k-\kappa^{\prime}\bigg)\bigg\}\bigg[1+f_{s}\bigg(\frac{1}{1-\alpha}\,k-\kappa^{\prime}\bigg)\bigg]\bigg),

(202)

with

\kappa^{\prime}\equiv\sqrt{\textstyle\frac{2}{1-\alpha}\,\Big(k_{0}^{2}+\frac{\alpha-1/2}{1-\alpha}\,k^{2}\Big)},

(203)

if
\kappa^{\prime}
is real, otherwise the scattering integral vanishes. With some algebra, the expression can be rewritten as

V
Summary and conclusions

The understanding of transport of charge carriers and excitons in organic semiconductors is crucial for applications. Semiclassical Boltzmann theory is a valuable tool for this since it is formulated in terms of physically intuitive quantities and processes, thereby simplifying the interpretation. In this paper, we have addressed the spin-conserving charge transport and scattering in a semiclassical framework, including the creation, decay, recombination, fission, and fusion of excitons, which are crucial for organic solar cells. However, our results are of more general interest for any system showing scattering of and reactions between spin-carrying particles, for example in spintronics and spin chemistry.

To start with, we have set up kinetic equations for charge carriers as well as for singlet and triplet excitons in uniform electric and magnetic fields, taking care to preserve
\mathrm{SU}(2)
spin-rotation invariance. The case of triplet excitons is the most interesting in that these spin-
1
quasiparticles permit and require a
quadrupolar
contribution to their distribution function. We have obtained coupled equations for the concentration, spin-density, and quadrupole-density components. The generalization to nonuniform systems is conceptually straightforward.

In the second step, we have constructed
\mathrm{SU}(2)
-invariant transition terms or scattering integrals describing disorder scattering, singlet-exciton generation, binding of electrons and holes into singlet and into triplet excitons, and exciton fusion, as well as the reverse processes. The occupation of final states enhances a process for bosons and suppresses it for fermions. These quantum effects are naturally included and lead to higher-order terms in the scattering integrals, which are relevant for large occupation numbers. It proved useful to employ two different representations of distribution functions, namely a multipole expansion and a description as matrix-valued functions acting on the spin Hilbert space. For the simpler processes, the multipole representation allows one to directly write down the
\mathrm{SU}(2)
-invariant terms based on the transition terms under the (symmetry-breaking) assumption of all moments being parallel or antiparallel to the
z
axis. The results can be used to describe real materials when realistic models for the bare transition rates
W
are available. Moreover, our work serves as a guide on how to construct scattering integrals for complex transitions involving several quasiparticles with spin.

Future directions following from this work are quite clear. First, it will be necessary to derive models for the bare transition rates
W
for real materials and link the equations to observables, in order to obtain quantitative descriptions. For the application to photovoltaic devices, it is important to note that the generation of excitons takes place at internal interfaces in organic blends, while the diffusion of (triplet) excitons is a bulk effect. Our framework offers two approaches to this problem: On long length scales relative to the typical scale of the phases in the blend, coarse graining leads to an effective-medium description with an effective exciton-generation rate. On the other hand, on shorter length scales—but still long compared to the single-molecule scale—the spatial structure of the phases can be modeled. The semiclassical approach is in principle suitable for this but of course relies on a good structural model.

Second, additional processes are relevant in certain materials. For example, bound states of two electrons and one hole or vice versa, i.e., trions, can exist
Lam58
;
Gla20
. The formation and decay of trions can easily be described in our framework. Third, we have here assumed that polaronic effects, which are strong in organic materials, can be treated simply be renormalizing model parameters for charge carriers and excitons. This is certainly simplistic, in particular due to the broad distribution of timescales of phonons in organic materials. The connection of transport and excitonic processes with polaronic effects in a semiclassical framework is thus an important goal for the future.

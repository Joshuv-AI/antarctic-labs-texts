TRALALA


Abstract
We derive computational formulas for the generalized Choquet integral based on the novel survival function introduced by M. Boczek et al. [ 1 ] . We demonstrate its usefulness on the Knapsack problem and the problem of accommodation options. Moreover, we describe sufficient and necessary conditions under which novel survival functions based on different parameters coincide. This is closely related to the incomparability of input vectors (alternatives) in decision-making processes.

Introduction

M. Boczek et al.
[
1
]
, inspired by consumer’s problems, aggregation operators, and conditional expectation, introduced a new notion of conditional aggregation operators. Conditional aggregation operators cover many existing aggregations, such as the arithmetic and geometric mean, or plenty of integrals known in the literature
[
12
,
14
,
16
]
. They became the essence of the generalization of survival function (a notion known from
[
7
]
, from
[
9
]
known as the decumulative distribution function or from
[
2
]
known as the strict level measure) introduced by M. Boczek et al.
[
1
]
. Just as the survival function is the basis of the definition of the famous Choquet integral, the generalized survival function enabled the building of a new integral. The generalized Choquet integral
\displaystyle\mathrm{C}_{{\mathscr{A}}}(\mathbf{x},\mu)
based on the generalized survival function
\displaystyle\mu_{\mathscr{A}}(\mathbf{x},\alpha)
has been naturally introduced by

\mathrm{C}_{{\mathscr{A}}}(\mathbf{x},\mu)=\int_{0}^{\infty}\mu_{\mathscr{A}}(\mathbf{x},\alpha)\,\mathrm{d}\alpha.

A discrete form of the famous Choquet integral is of great importance in decision making theory, regarding a finite set
\displaystyle[n]=\{1,\dots,n\}
as criteria set, a vector
\displaystyle\mathbf{x}\in[0,+\infty)^{[n]}
as a score vector, and a capacity
\displaystyle\mu\colon 2^{[n]}\to[0,+\infty)
as the weights of particular sets of criteria.
We provide problems, which complement the ones described in
[
1
]
, and which stress the potential of the novel survival function and integral based on it in decision theory. However, the main aim of the present paper is to provide various computational formulas for
\displaystyle\mu_{\mathscr{A}}(\mathbf{x},\alpha)
, and consequently for
\displaystyle\mathrm{C}_{{\mathscr{A}}}(\mathbf{x},\mu)
, since the computation of
\displaystyle\mathrm{C}_{{\mathscr{A}}}(\mathbf{x},\mu)
has not yet been thoroughly investigated. Thus, we believe that our computational algorithms improve the practical implementation of the novel concept of aggregation in various problems.

The novel survival function
\displaystyle\mu_{\mathscr{A}}(\mathbf{x},\alpha)
is based on an aggregation of an input vector
\displaystyle\mathbf{x}
, applying each conditional aggregation operator from the family
\displaystyle{\mathscr{A}}
of size
\displaystyle\kappa
on
\displaystyle\mathbf{x}
and corresponding set of its indices. To illustrate our results we provide a sample formula for
\displaystyle\mathrm{C}_{{\mathscr{A}}}(\mathbf{x},\mu)
. Indeed, we show that

\displaystyle\displaystyle{\mathrm{C}_{{\mathscr{A}}}(\mathbf{x},\mu)=\sum_{i=0}^{\kappa-1}\mu(F_{\mathbf{i}(i)})(\mathsf{A}_{i+1}-\mathsf{A}_{i}),}

(1)

where
\displaystyle\mathsf{A}_{0}\leq\dots\leq\mathsf{A}_{\kappa-1}
is the arrangement of all the values of the family of conditional aggregation operators
\displaystyle{\mathscr{A}}
applied on
\displaystyle\mathbf{x}
and corresponding sets
\displaystyle E_{0},\dots,E_{\kappa-1}
, and
\displaystyle\mu(F_{0})\leq\dots\leq\mu(F_{\kappa-1})
are all the values of the measure
\displaystyle\mu
.
The index
\displaystyle\mathbf{i}(i)
deciding the value of
\displaystyle\mathrm{C}_{{\mathscr{A}}}(\mathbf{x},\mu)
on the interval
\displaystyle[\mathsf{A}_{i},\mathsf{A}_{i+1})
is the minimum of the set
\displaystyle\{(0),(1),\dots,(i)\}
of the indices such that
\displaystyle F_{(0)}=E_{0}^{c},\dots,F_{(i)}=E_{i}^{c}
. The proof of formula (
1
) as well as many other similar formulas can be found in Theorem
5.2
.

The paper is organized as follows. Section 2 contains necessary terminology on measure and a family of conditional aggregation operators. Moreover, it describes two problems in decision-making theory that can be modeled by the generalized survival function. The first series of formulas for its computation is presented in Section 3. The next section reduces the minimization of the reals in the formulas to the minimization of indices, and shows two graphical approaches to obtain the generalized survival function. In addition, it contains a solution to the first of two mentioned problems in decision-making theory. The formulas for the generalized Choquet integral computation are listed in Section 5, including simplified fomulas for special types of measures. The solution to the second problem from Section 2 is presented here. The study of the conditions such that the novel survival functions based on different parameters coincide is performed in Section 6. Finally, the paper itself contains just proofs of selected results, the remaining proofs are attached in the Appendix.

Background and interpretations

As we have already mentioned, we shall consider a finite set
\displaystyle[n]:=\{1,2,\dots,n\}
,
\displaystyle n\in\mathbb{N}
,
\displaystyle n\geq 1
.
Let us denote
\displaystyle[n]_{0}:=\{0\}\cup[n]
.
By
\displaystyle 2^{[n]}
we mean the power set of
\displaystyle[n]
.
A set function
\displaystyle\mu\colon\mathscr{S}\to[0,+\infty)
,
\displaystyle\{\emptyset\}\subseteq\mathscr{S}\subseteq 2^{[n]}
, such that
\displaystyle\mu(E)\leq\mu(F)
whenever
\displaystyle E\subseteq F
, with
\displaystyle\mu(\emptyset)=0
, we call
monotone measure
on
\displaystyle\mathscr{S}
.
Moreover, if
\displaystyle[n]\in\mathscr{S}
, we assume
\displaystyle\mu([n])>0
, and if
\displaystyle\mu([n])=1
, the monotone measure
\displaystyle\mu
is called
capacity
or
normalized monotone measure
.
Further, we put
\displaystyle\max{\emptyset}=0
,
\displaystyle\min{\emptyset}=+\infty
and
\displaystyle\textstyle\sum_{i\in\emptyset}x_{i}=0
.

We shall work with nonnegative real-valued vectors, we use the notation
\displaystyle\mathbf{x}=(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})
,
\displaystyle x_{i}\in[0,+\infty)
,
\displaystyle i\in[n]
.
The family of all nonnegative real-valued vectors on
\displaystyle[n]
is the set
\displaystyle[0,+\infty)^{[n]}
.
By
\displaystyle\mathbf{1}_{E}
we shall denote
indicator function
of a set
\displaystyle E\subseteq[0,+\infty)
, i.e.
\displaystyle\mathbf{1}_{E}(x)=1
, if
\displaystyle x\in E
, and
\displaystyle\mathbf{1}_{E}(x)=0
, if
\displaystyle x\notin E
.
Especially,
\displaystyle\mathbf{1}_{\emptyset}(x)=0
for each
\displaystyle x\in E
.
Let us consider a set
\displaystyle\mathscr{E}\subseteq 2^{[n]}
.
Unless otherwise stated, for application reasons we assume
\displaystyle\{\emptyset,[n]\}\subseteq\mathscr{E}
.
We call it the
collection
.
Let us denote the number of sets in
\displaystyle\mathscr{E}
by
\displaystyle\kappa
, i.e.
\displaystyle|\mathscr{E}|=\kappa
.
Let
\displaystyle\hat{{\mathscr{E}}}=\{E^{c}:E\in{\mathscr{E}}\}
, i.e.
\displaystyle\hat{{\mathscr{E}}}
contains the complements of the sets from collection
\displaystyle\mathscr{E}
.
The set of all monotone measures on
\displaystyle\hat{\mathscr{E}}
we shall denote by
\displaystyle\mathbf{M}
.

In the following, we present the definition of the conditional aggregation operator.
The inspiration for this concept can be found in probability theory, specifically in conditional expectation.
The idea of aggregating data not on the whole, but on a conditional set is expressed in the terms of this definition.
The conditional aggregation operator generalizes the classical definition of aggregation operator introduced by Calvo et al. in
[
3
]
and forms the basic component of the definition of the generalized survival function.

Definition 2.1

(cf.
[
1
, Definition 4.1.]
)
A
family of conditional aggregation operators
(FCA for short) is a family

{\mathscr{A}}=\{\mathsf{A}(\cdot|E):E\in\mathscr{E}\},

such that each
\displaystyle\mathsf{A}(\cdot|E)
is a map
\displaystyle\mathsf{A}(\cdot|E)\colon[0,+\infty)^{[n]}\to[0,+\infty)
satisfying the following conditions:

(i)

\displaystyle\mathsf{A}(\mathbf{x}|E)\leq\mathsf{A}(\mathbf{y}|E)
for any
\displaystyle\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y}
such that
\displaystyle x_{i}\leq y_{i}
for any
\displaystyle i\in E
,
\displaystyle E\neq\emptyset
;

(ii)

\displaystyle\mathsf{A}(\mathbf{1}_{E^{c}}|E)=0
,
\displaystyle E\neq\emptyset
.

If
\displaystyle\mu
is a monotone measure on
\displaystyle\hat{{\mathscr{E}}}=\{E^{c}:E\in{\mathscr{E}}\}
, i.e.
\displaystyle\mu\in\mathbf{M}
, then the
generalized survival function
with respect to
\displaystyle{\mathscr{A}}
is defined as

\displaystyle\displaystyle\mu_{\mathscr{A}}(\mathbf{x},\alpha):=\min\left\{\mu(E^{c}):\mathsf{A}(\mathbf{x}|E)\leq\alpha,\,E\in\mathscr{E}\right\}

(2)

for any
\displaystyle\alpha\in[0,+\infty)
.

If
\displaystyle E\neq\emptyset
, then
\displaystyle\mathsf{A}(\cdot|E)
is called the
conditional aggregation operator w.r.t.
\displaystyle E
. Moreover, we consider that each element of FCA satisfies
\displaystyle\mathsf{A}(\cdot|\emptyset)=0
.

Remark 2.2

Note that the survival function
\displaystyle\mu(\{\mathbf{x}>\alpha\})=\mu(\{i\in[n]:x_{i}>\alpha\})
can be rewritten as

\displaystyle\displaystyle\mu(\{\mathbf{x}>\alpha\})

\displaystyle\displaystyle=\mu([n]\setminus\{\mathbf{x}\leq\alpha\})=\min\big\{\mu(E^{c}):(\forall i\in E)\,\,x_{i}\leq\alpha,\,E\in 2^{[n]}\big\}

\displaystyle\displaystyle=\min\{\mu(E^{c}):\max_{i\in E}x_{i}\leq\alpha,E\in 2^{[n]}\},

(3)

therefore the introduction of generalized survival function consists in the simple idea of replacing
\displaystyle\max_{i\in E}x_{i}
in (
2.2
) with another functional. Clearly, for
\displaystyle\mathscr{E}=2^{[n]}
and
\displaystyle{\mathscr{A}}^{\mathrm{max}}
we get the original strict survival function.

Example 2.3

Let
\displaystyle\mathbf{x}\in[0,+\infty)^{[n]}
. Typical examples of the FCA are:

(i)

\displaystyle{\mathscr{A}}^{\mathrm{max}}=\{\mathsf{A}^{\mathrm{max}}(\cdot|E):E\in{{\mathscr{E}}}\}
with
\displaystyle\mathsf{A}^{\mathrm{max}}(\mathbf{x}|E)=\max_{i\in E}x_{i}
for
\displaystyle E\neq\emptyset
;

(ii)

\displaystyle{\mathscr{A}}^{\mathrm{min}}=\{\mathsf{A}^{\mathrm{min}}(\cdot|E):E\in{{\mathscr{E}}}\}
with
\displaystyle\mathsf{A}^{\mathrm{min}}(\mathbf{x}|E)=\min_{i\in E}x_{i}
for
\displaystyle E\neq\emptyset
;

(iii)

\displaystyle{\mathscr{A}}^{\mathrm{sum}}=\{\mathsf{A}^{\mathrm{sum}}(\cdot|E):E\in{{\mathscr{E}}}\}
with
\displaystyle\mathsf{A}^{\mathrm{sum}}(\mathbf{x}|E)=\sum_{i\in E}x_{i}
for
\displaystyle E\neq\emptyset
.

Note that the FCA does not have to contain elements of only one type.

Example 2.4

Let
\displaystyle\mathbf{x}\in[0,+\infty)^{[3]}
, and
\displaystyle{{\mathscr{E}}}=\{\emptyset,\{1\},\{2\},\{3\},\{1,2,3\}\}
. We can consider the following FCA

{\mathscr{A}}=\{\mathsf{A}^{\mathrm{max}}(\cdot|E):E\in\{\{1\},\{2\},\{1,2,3\}\}\cup\{\mathsf{A}^{\mathrm{min}}(\cdot|E):E\in\{\{3\}\}\}\cup\{\mathsf{A}(\cdot|\emptyset)\}.

For other examples of FCA we recommend
[
1
]
.
On several places in this paper, we shall work with the FCA that is
nondecreasing
w.r.t sets, i.e. the map
\displaystyle E\mapsto\mathsf{A}(\cdot|E)
will be nondecreasing. E.g., the families
\displaystyle{\mathscr{A}}^{\mathrm{max}}
and
\displaystyle{\mathscr{A}}^{\mathrm{sum}}
in Example
2.3
are nondecreasing w.r.t. sets.

Knapsack problem

Let us imagine a person who is preparing for a holiday. The person plans to travel by plane. Therefore while packing the suitcase, he must keep the rules according to which it is allowed to carry less or equal to
\displaystyle 1
liter of liquids in the suitcase. Moreover, liquids must be in containers with a volume of up to
\displaystyle 100
ml.
The prices of these products (soap, shampoo, cream, etc.) and their possible combinations (packages of products) are more expensive in the destination country than at home.
The person wants to buy such products, or packages of products, while still at home, to minimize the price of the holiday.

Let
\displaystyle[n]
,
\displaystyle n\geq 1
be a set of liquid products that the person needs. Then
\displaystyle\mathscr{E}\subseteq 2^{[n]}
represents all possible combinations of products. Let us consider
\displaystyle\mathbf{x}=(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})\in[0,+\infty)^{[n]}
, where
\displaystyle x_{i}
represents the volume of
\displaystyle i
-th container and let a monotone measure
\displaystyle\mu\in\mathbf{M}
represent the price of a package of products.
Note that the monotone measure
\displaystyle\mu
need not be additive. It is often possible to buy a package of products that is cheaper than the sum of the prices of the individual products.
The task is to choose such a combination
\displaystyle E\in\mathscr{E}
of products that their volume does not exceed the given limit, i.e.
\displaystyle\textstyle\sum_{i\in E}x_{i}\leq 1000
(milliliters), having in mind that we want to minimize the price of those products that will no longer fit in the suitcase and the person will have to buy them during the holiday.
In other words, we are faced to solve the optimization problem

\min\left\{\mu(E^{c}):\sum_{i\in E}x_{i}\leq 1000,\,E\in\mathscr{E}\right\}\text{.}

One can observe that the given formula is a special case of the generalized survival function given in (
2
) with the conditional aggregation operator being the sum.

Last, but not least let us point out the essence of the collection
\displaystyle\mathscr{E}
. There are situations when instead of the whole powerset one is forced to consider a subcollection
\displaystyle\mathscr{E}\subset 2^{[n]}
. E.g. among the products that one is considering can be shampoo and conditioner separately or shampoo containing conditioner. Of course, these products should not be considered together. Thus all sets consisting of these three products are disqualified and it makes sense to take
\displaystyle\mathscr{E}\subset 2^{[n]}
instead of the whole powerset.

The full solution to the problem using our results is accomplished in Subsection
4.1
.

Computational formulas

When working with a family of conditional aggregation operators
\displaystyle{\mathscr{A}}
, an input vector
\displaystyle\mathbf{x}
is aggregated on sets
\displaystyle E
from
\displaystyle{\mathscr{E}}\subseteq 2^{[n]}
. By this procedure, instead of
\displaystyle n
input components of
\displaystyle\mathbf{x}
we obtain
\displaystyle\kappa=|{\mathscr{E}}|
input values of the family of conditional aggregation operators
\displaystyle{\mathscr{A}}
:

\displaystyle\displaystyle 0=\mathsf{A}_{0}\leq\mathsf{A}_{1}\leq\dots\leq\mathsf{A}_{\kappa-2}\leq\mathsf{A}_{\kappa-1}<+\infty,

(4)

with the convention
\displaystyle\mathsf{A}_{\kappa}=+\infty
. This “new” input enters the computation of the generalized survival function. Now we provide two approaches to compute generalized survival function values, the first one is presented in Theorem
3.2
and the other one in Theorem
3.7
. We shall fix a notation.

Let
\displaystyle E_{*}
be any bijection
\displaystyle E_{*}\colon[\kappa-1]_{0}\to{{\mathscr{E}}}
such that

\displaystyle\displaystyle\mathsf{A}_{i}=\mathsf{A}(\mathbf{x}|E_{i}).

(5)

The second bijection is a map
\displaystyle F_{*}\colon[\kappa-1]_{0}\to{\hat{{\mathscr{E}}}}
such that denoting
\displaystyle\mu_{j}=\mu(F_{j})
we have:

\displaystyle\displaystyle 0=\mu_{0}\leq\mu_{1}\leq\dots\leq\mu_{\kappa-2}\leq\mu_{\kappa-1}<+\infty.

(6)

It should be recalled that
\displaystyle\hat{{\mathscr{E}}}
is the collection of complements of
\displaystyle{\mathscr{E}}
, i.e. for every
\displaystyle F_{j}\in\hat{{\mathscr{E}}}
there exists
\displaystyle E_{i}\in{\mathscr{E}}
such that
\displaystyle F_{j}=E_{i}^{c}
. We denote sets in collection
\displaystyle{\mathscr{E}}
by
\displaystyle E_{i}
,
\displaystyle i\in[\kappa-1]_{0}
, and their complements in
\displaystyle\hat{{\mathscr{E}}}
are denoted by
\displaystyle F_{j}
,
\displaystyle j\in[\kappa-1]_{0}
, because of technical details. In the whole paper for ease of writing, we shall use a shortcut notation

\mathsf{A}_{{\langle j\rangle}}:=\mathsf{A}(\mathbf{x}|F_{j}^{c}),\,\,\,\,\text{and}\,\,\,\,\mu_{(i)}:=\mu(E_{i}^{c}).

One can notice that maps
\displaystyle E_{*}
and
\displaystyle F_{*}
need not be unique (they are unique just in case they are injective on
\displaystyle[\kappa-1]_{0}
), but this has no influence on presented results.

In the following example, we only demonstrate the introduction of bijection

The first approach to computing generalized survival function values is based on an (ascending) arrangement (

) of conditional aggregation operator values. Let us note that this approach is directly derived from Definition

. In the computation process using this approach, we look for generalized survival function values that are achieved at intervals

Theorem 3.2

Let
\displaystyle{\mathscr{A}}
be a FCA,
\displaystyle\mu\in\mathbf{M}
,
\displaystyle\mathbf{x}\in[0,+\infty)^{[n]}
.
Then

(i)

for any
\displaystyle i\in[\kappa-1]_{0}
it holds that

\mu_{\mathscr{A}}(\mathbf{x},\alpha)=\min_{k\leq i}\mu_{(k)}\,\,\ \textrm{for any}\,\,\alpha\in[\mathsf{A}_{i},\mathsf{A}_{i+1});

(ii)

\displaystyle\displaystyle\mu_{\mathscr{A}}(\mathbf{x},\alpha)=\sum_{i=0}^{\kappa-1}\min_{k\leq i}\mu_{(k)}\mathbf{1}_{[\mathsf{A}_{i},\mathsf{A}_{i+1})}(\alpha)\,\,\ \textrm{for any}\,\,\alpha\in[0,+\infty).

(7)

Proof.
(i) Let us consider an arbitrary (fixed)
\displaystyle i\in[\kappa-1]_{0}
such that
\displaystyle\mathsf{A}_{i}<\mathsf{A}_{i+1}
, and let us take
\displaystyle\alpha\in[\mathsf{A}_{i},\mathsf{A}_{i+1})
.
Since
\displaystyle 0=\mathsf{A}_{0}\leq\mathsf{A}_{1}\leq\dots\leq\mathsf{A}_{i}\leq\alpha
, then

\mu_{\mathscr{A}}(\mathbf{x},\alpha)=\min\{\mu(E^{c}):\mathsf{A}(\mathbf{x}|E)\leq\alpha,\,E\in\mathscr{E}\}=\min\{\mu_{(k)}:k\in\{0,\dots,i\}\}=\min_{k\leq i}\mu_{(k)}.

The case (ii) immediately follows from (i).

\displaystyle\Box\;\;

Remark 3.3

Let
\displaystyle(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})\in[0,+\infty)^{[n]}
. Let us denote

\displaystyle\mathbf{x}=(x_{\sigma(1)},x_{\sigma(2)},\dots,x_{\sigma(n)})
, with
\displaystyle\sigma\colon[n]\to[n]
being a permutation such that
\displaystyle x_{\sigma(1)}\leq x_{\sigma(2)}\leq\dots\leq x_{\sigma(n)}
with the convention
\displaystyle x_{\sigma(0)}=0
.
Also let
\displaystyle{\mathscr{A}}^{\mathrm{max}}
be a FCA with the collection
\displaystyle\mathscr{E}=\{G_{\sigma{(i+1)}}^{c}:i\in[n]_{0}\}
where
\displaystyle G_{\sigma{(i)}}=\{\sigma{(i)},\dots,\sigma{(n)}\}
for
\displaystyle i\in[n]
and
\displaystyle G_{\sigma(n+1)}=\emptyset
.
Then from the previous proposition we get the standard formula of the survival function
[
11
]
:

\mu_{\mathscr{A}^{\mathrm{max}}}(\mathbf{x},\alpha)=\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}\mu{(G_{\sigma(i+1)})}\mathbf{1}_{[x_{\sigma{(i)}},x_{\sigma{(i+1)}})}(\alpha)\,\,\ \textrm{for any}\,\,\alpha\in[0,+\infty)\text{.}

Indeed,
\displaystyle\mathsf{A}^{\mathrm{max}}(\mathbf{x}|G_{\sigma(i+1)}^{c})=x_{\sigma(i)}
for any
\displaystyle i\in[n]_{0}
.
Moreover, if
\displaystyle x_{\sigma(i)}=\mathsf{A}^{\mathrm{max}}_{l_{i}}
,
\displaystyle l_{i}\in[\kappa-1]_{0}
, then
\displaystyle\min_{k\leq l_{i}}\mu_{(k)}=\min_{k\leq i}\mu(G_{\sigma(k)})=\mu(G_{\sigma(i)})

because of the monotonicity of
\displaystyle\mu
.

Since
\displaystyle[n]\in{\mathscr{E}}
, then the definition of generalized survival function guarantees to achieve zero value.
According to arrangement in (

\mathrm{C}_{{\mathscr{A}}}(\mathbf{x},\bar{\mu})=\min_{E\neq\emptyset}\mathsf{A}(\mathbf{x}|E).

(iii)

Let
\displaystyle{\mathscr{A}}
be FCA monotone w.r.t. sets with
\displaystyle{\mathscr{E}}=2^{[n]}

\displaystyle\mu

\displaystyle\mu^{i}:=\mu(F)

\displaystyle|F|=i

(iv)

Let
\displaystyle{\mathscr{A}}
be FCA monotone w.r.t. sets with
\displaystyle{\mathscr{E}}=2^{[n]}
. Let
\displaystyle\Pi

\displaystyle G_{\sigma(i)}=\{\sigma(i),\dots,\sigma(n)\}

(v)

\displaystyle{\mathscr{A}}

\displaystyle\mathrm{N}

\displaystyle\sigma

Remark 5.4

In the following, we aim to emphasize that the
\displaystyle{\mathscr{A}}
-Choquet integral covers the famuous Choquet integral.
Let us consider a special family
\displaystyle{\mathscr{A}}^{\mathrm{max}}
.
Taking symmetric measures we obtain

\mathrm{C}_{{\mathscr{A}}^{\mathrm{max}}}(\mathbf{x},\mu)=\sum_{i=1}^{n}(\mu^{i}-\mu^{i-1})x_{\sigma(n-i+1)}=\sum_{i=1}^{n}(\mu^{n-i+1}-\mu^{n-i})x_{\sigma(i)},

where
\displaystyle\sigma\colon[n]\to[n]
is a permutation such that
\displaystyle x_{\sigma(1)}\leq\dots\leq x_{\sigma(n)}
with the convention
\displaystyle x_{\sigma(0)}=0
. This is a formula of Yager’s ordered weighted averaging (OWA) operator
[
17
]
as Grabisch has shown, see
[
10
]
. Therefore, the generalized
\displaystyle{\mathscr{A}}
-Choquet integral w.r.t. symmetric measures can be seen as a new type of the OWA operator.

The formula for possibility measure in Corollary
5.3
(iv) simplifies to the form

\displaystyle\displaystyle\mathrm{C}_{{\mathscr{A}}^{\mathrm{max}}}(\mathbf{x},\Pi)=\sum_{i=1}^{n}(\pi(\sigma(i))-\pi(\sigma(i-1)))\max_{k\geq i}x_{\sigma(k)}=\sum_{i=1}^{n}(\pi(\sigma(i))-\pi(\sigma(i-1)))\max_{\pi(k)\geq\pi(\sigma(i))}x_{k},

which is the famous Choquet integral with respect to possibility measure, cf.
[
6
]
, and permutation
\displaystyle\sigma
as in Corollary
3.9
. Similarly, taking necessity measure in Corollary
5.3
(v) we obtain

\displaystyle\displaystyle\mathrm{C}_{{\mathscr{A}}^{\mathrm{max}}}(\mathbf{x},\mathrm{N})=\sum_{i=1}^{n}(\pi(\sigma(i))-\pi(\sigma(i-1)))\min_{k\geq i}x_{\sigma(k)}=\sum_{i=1}^{n}(\pi(\sigma(i))-\pi(\sigma(i-1)))\min_{\pi(k)\geq\pi(\sigma(i))}x_{k}.

6
Searching optimal intervals, and indistinguishibility

The Choquet integral is a basic tool for multicriteria decision making and modeling of decision under risk and uncertainty.
In
[
6
]
, Dubois and Rico studied the equality conditions of Choquet integrals of particular input vectors. They considered Choquet integrals with respect to possibility and necessity measures. In
[
4
]
, Chen et al. continued their research with a view to a wider class of so-called universal integrals
[
12
]
. Universal integrals form one class of utility functions in multicriteria decision making.

In this section, we formulate the equality conditions of generalized survival functions considering arbitrary measures. Naturally, if generalized survival functions coincide, then their Choquet integrals equal. In order to obtain the equality conditions, we study the greatest possible intervals on which the generalized survival function takes its possible values.
For this purpose, for any
\displaystyle j\in[\kappa-1]_{0}
let us set

\displaystyle\displaystyle{\varphi^{*}}(j):=\max\{k\in[\kappa-1]_{0}:\mu_{k}=\mu_{j}\}\quad\text{and}\quad{\varphi_{*}}(j)=\min\{k\in[\kappa-1]_{0}:\mu_{k}=\mu_{j}\}.

(18)

The following proposition summarizes the basic properties of
\displaystyle{\varphi^{*}}
and
\displaystyle{\varphi_{*}}
.

Proposition 6.1

Let
\displaystyle{\mathscr{A}}
be a FCA,
\displaystyle\mu\in\mathbf{M}
,
\displaystyle\mathbf{x}\in[0,\infty)^{[n]}
,
\displaystyle{\varphi^{*}}
and
\displaystyle{\varphi_{*}}
are defined as in (
18
) and
\displaystyle a,b,j\in[\kappa-1]_{0}
. Then the following properties hold.

(i)

\displaystyle{\varphi_{*}}(j)\leq j\leq{\varphi^{*}}(j)
,

(ii)

\displaystyle\mu_{{\varphi^{*}}(j)}=\mu_{k}=\mu_{{\varphi_{*}}(j)}
for any integer
\displaystyle k\in\{{\varphi_{*}}(j),\dots,{\varphi^{*}}(j)\}
,

(iii)

\displaystyle\bigcup_{{\varphi_{*}}(j)\leq l\leq{\varphi^{*}}(j)}\Big[\min_{k\leq l}\mathsf{A}_{{\langle k\rangle}},\min_{k<l}\mathsf{A}_{{\langle k\rangle}}\Big)=\Big[\min_{k\leq{\varphi^{*}}(j)}\mathsf{A}_{{\langle k\rangle}},\min_{k<{\varphi_{*}}(j)}\mathsf{A}_{{\langle k\rangle}}\Big)
,

(iv)

\displaystyle{\varphi_{*}}
and
\displaystyle{\varphi^{*}}
are nondecreasing.

Proof.
The statements (i) and (ii) follow directly from definitions of
\displaystyle{\varphi^{*}}
,
\displaystyle{\varphi_{*}}
, and arrangement (
6
).
The validity of the statement (iii) follows from the fact that

\displaystyle\min_{k<l}\mathsf{A}_{\langle k\rangle}=\min_{k\leq l-1}\mathsf{A}_{\langle k\rangle}
for
\displaystyle l\in\{{\varphi_{*}}(j),\dots,{\varphi^{*}}(j)\},

\displaystyle l\neq 0
, and because of

\min_{k\leq{\varphi^{*}}(j)}\mathsf{A}_{\langle k\rangle}\leq\min_{k<{\varphi^{*}}(j)}\mathsf{A}_{\langle k\rangle}=\min_{k\leq{\varphi^{*}}(j)-1}\mathsf{A}_{\langle k\rangle}\leq\dots\leq\min_{k<{\varphi_{*}}(j)+1}\mathsf{A}_{\langle k\rangle}=\min_{k\leq{\varphi_{*}}(j)}\mathsf{A}_{\langle k\rangle}\leq\min_{k<{\varphi_{*}}(j)}\mathsf{A}_{\langle k\rangle}

with the convention already stated in this paper
\displaystyle\min\limits_{k<0}\mathsf{A}_{\langle k\rangle}=\min\emptyset=+\infty.

Now we show (iv). If
\displaystyle a\leq b
, then from arrangement (
6
) we have
\displaystyle\mu_{a}\leq\mu_{b}
and thus

\max\{k\in[\kappa-1]_{0}:\mu_{a}=\mu_{k}\}\leq\max\{k\in[\kappa-1]_{0}:\mu_{b}=\mu_{k}\},

hence
\displaystyle{\varphi^{*}}(a)\leq{\varphi^{*}}(b)
.
The second part of the statement (iv) can be shown analogously.

\displaystyle\Box\;\;

By means of
\displaystyle{\varphi^{*}}
and
\displaystyle{\varphi_{*}}
we can determine the greatest possible intervals corresponding to given monotone measure. Simultaneously, we show under what conditions the value of a given monotone measure is not achieved by the generalized survival function.

Proposition 6.2

Let
\displaystyle{\mathscr{A}}
be a FCA,
\displaystyle\mu\in\mathbf{M}
,
\displaystyle\mathbf{x}\in[0,+\infty)^{[n]}
,
\displaystyle{\varphi_{*}}
and
\displaystyle{\varphi^{*}}
be given as in (
18
), and
\displaystyle j\in[\kappa-1]_{0}
. Then

\mu_{\mathscr{A}}(\mathbf{x},\alpha)=\mu_{j}\text{ for any }\alpha\in\Big[\min_{k\leq{\varphi^{*}}(j)}\mathsf{A}_{\langle k\rangle},\min_{k<{\varphi_{*}}(j)}\mathsf{A}_{\langle k\rangle}\Big)

with the convention
\displaystyle\min\limits_{k<0}\mathsf{A}_{\langle k\rangle}=\min\emptyset=+\infty
and this interval is the greatest possible.

Proof.

According to Theorem
3.7
, Proposition
6.1
(i), (ii), (iii) the generalized survival function
\displaystyle\mu_{\mathscr{A}}(\mathbf{x},\alpha)
achieves the value
\displaystyle\mu_{j}
on interval
\displaystyle\Big[\min_{k\leq{\varphi^{*}}(j)}\mathsf{A}_{\langle k\rangle},\min_{k<{\varphi_{*}}(j)}\mathsf{A}_{\langle k\rangle}\Big)
. However, it is not clear that this interval is the greatest possible. By contradiction:
So let exist
\displaystyle\tilde{j}\in[\kappa-1]_{0}
such that

\min_{k\leq{\varphi^{*}}(\tilde{j})}\mathsf{A}_{\langle k\rangle}<\min_{k\leq{\varphi^{*}}(j)}\mathsf{A}_{\langle k\rangle}\quad\text{or}\quad\min_{k<{\varphi_{*}}(j)}\mathsf{A}_{\langle k\rangle}<\min_{k<{\varphi_{*}}(\tilde{j})}\mathsf{A}_{\langle k\rangle},

and
\displaystyle\mu_{\mathscr{A}}(\mathbf{x},\alpha)=\mu_{j}
for
\displaystyle\alpha\in\Big[\min_{k\leq{\varphi^{*}}(\tilde{j})}\mathsf{A}_{\langle k\rangle},\min_{k\leq{\varphi^{*}}(j)}\mathsf{A}_{\langle k\rangle}\Big)
or
\displaystyle\alpha\in\Big[\min_{k<{\varphi_{*}}(j)}\mathsf{A}_{\langle k\rangle},\min_{k<{\varphi_{*}}(\tilde{j})}\mathsf{A}_{\langle k\rangle}\Big)
.
Let us discuss these cases.

•

By Theorem
3.7
(i) for
\displaystyle\alpha=\min_{k\leq{\varphi^{*}}(\tilde{j})}\mathsf{A}_{\langle k\rangle}
it holds
\displaystyle\mu_{\mathscr{A}}(\mathbf{x},\alpha)=\mu_{{\varphi^{*}}(\tilde{j})}=\mu_{\tilde{j}}
, where the last equality holds because of Proposition
6.1
(i), (ii).
However,
\displaystyle\mu_{\tilde{j}}\neq\mu_{j}
, otherwise we get a contradiction.
Indeed, if
\displaystyle\mu_{\tilde{j}}=\mu_{j}
, then
\displaystyle{\varphi^{*}}(j)={\varphi^{*}}(\tilde{j})
and thus
\displaystyle\min_{k\leq{\varphi^{*}}(\tilde{j})}\mathsf{A}_{\langle k\rangle}=\min_{k\leq{\varphi^{*}}(j)}\mathsf{A}_{\langle k\rangle}
, which is in conflict with choice of
\displaystyle\tilde{j}
.

•

Using the same arguments as in the previous case, for
\displaystyle\alpha=\min_{k<{\varphi_{*}}(j)}\mathsf{A}_{\langle k\rangle}=\min_{k\leq{\varphi_{*}}(j)-1}\mathsf{A}_{\langle k\rangle}
, we have
\displaystyle\mu_{\mathscr{A}}(\mathbf{x},\alpha)=\mu_{{\varphi_{*}}(j)-1}
.
Further,
\displaystyle\mu_{{\varphi_{*}}(j)-1}<\mu_{{\varphi_{*}}(j)}=\mu_{j}
, thus
\displaystyle\mu_{{\varphi_{*}}(j)-1}\neq\mu_{j}
.

This completes the proof.

\displaystyle\Box\;\;

From the above proposition, we immediately obtain the following result.

Corollary 6.3

Let
\displaystyle{\mathscr{A}}
be a FCA,
\displaystyle\mu\in\mathbf{M}
,
\displaystyle\mathbf{x}\in[0,+\infty)^{[n]}
,
\displaystyle{\varphi_{*}}
and
\displaystyle{\varphi^{*}}
be given as in (
18
), and
\displaystyle j\in[\kappa-1]_{0}
.
The value
\displaystyle\mu_{j}
is not achieved if and only if
\displaystyle\min_{k\leq{\varphi^{*}}(j)}\mathsf{A}_{\langle k\rangle}=\min_{k<{\varphi_{*}}(j)}\mathsf{A}_{\langle k\rangle}
.

Lemma 6.4

Let
\displaystyle{\mathscr{A}}
be a FCA,
\displaystyle\mu\in\mathbf{M}
,
\displaystyle\mathbf{x}\in[0,+\infty)^{[n]}
,
\displaystyle{\varphi_{*}}
and
\displaystyle{\varphi^{*}}
be given as in (

).

(i)

Let
\displaystyle j\in[\kappa-1]
. Then it holds

(i1)

If
\displaystyle\mu_{j}>\mu_{j-1}
, then
\displaystyle{\varphi_{*}}(j)-1={\varphi^{*}}(j-1)
and
\displaystyle\mu_{j}
is achieved on
\displaystyle\big[\min\limits_{k\leq{\varphi^{*}}(j)}\mathsf{A}_{\langle k\rangle},\min\limits_{k\leq{\varphi^{*}}(j-1)}\mathsf{A}_{\langle k\rangle}\big)
. Moreover, this interval is the greatest possible.

(i2)

If
\displaystyle\mu_{j}=\mu_{j-1}
, then
\displaystyle{\varphi^{*}}(j)={\varphi^{*}}(j-1)
.

(ii)

Let
\displaystyle j\in[\kappa-1]_{0}
. Then it holds

(ii1)

If
\displaystyle\mu_{j}<\mu_{j+1}
, then
\displaystyle{\varphi^{*}}(j)+1={\varphi_{*}}(j+1)
and

\displaystyle\mu_{j}
is achieved on
\displaystyle\Big[\min_{k<{\varphi_{*}}(j+1)}\mathsf{A}_{\langle k\rangle},\min_{k<{\varphi_{*}}(j)}\mathsf{A}_{\langle k\rangle}\Big)
, with
\displaystyle\min\limits_{k<{\varphi_{*}}(\kappa)}\mathsf{A}_{\langle k\rangle}=0

(ii2)

If
\displaystyle\mu_{j}=\mu_{j+1}
, then
\displaystyle{\varphi_{*}}(j)={\varphi_{*}}(j+1)
.

Proof.
Let us prove part (i).

(i1)

If
\displaystyle\mu_{j}>\mu_{j-1}
, then directly from definitions of
\displaystyle{\varphi_{*}}
and
\displaystyle{\varphi^{*}}
we have
\displaystyle{\varphi_{*}}(j)=j
and
\displaystyle{\varphi^{*}}(j-1)=j-1
, thus
\displaystyle{\varphi^{*}}(j-1)={\varphi_{*}}(j)-1
.

6.2
, we have

\mu_{\mathscr{A}}(\mathbf{x},\alpha)=\mu_{j}

for each
\displaystyle\alpha\in\big[\min\limits_{k\leq{\varphi^{*}}(j)}\mathsf{A}_{\langle k\rangle},\min\limits_{k\leq{\varphi_{*}}(j)-1}\mathsf{A}_{\langle k\rangle}\big)
and this interval is the greatest possible.
Using the above equality
we have

\big[\min\limits_{k\leq{\varphi^{*}}(j)}\mathsf{A}_{\langle k\rangle},\min\limits_{k\leq{\varphi_{*}}(j)-1}\mathsf{A}_{\langle k\rangle}\big)=\big[\min\limits_{k\leq{\varphi^{*}}(j)}\mathsf{A}_{\langle k\rangle},\min\limits_{k\leq{\varphi^{*}}(j-1)}\mathsf{A}_{\langle k\rangle}\big).

(i2)

It holds trivially.

Part (ii) can be proved analogously.

\displaystyle\Box\;\;

(19)

for any
\displaystyle\alpha\in[0,\infty)

(thus

Proof.

(i1)

, then

Conclusion

In the paper, we dealt with the concept of the generalized survival function and the generalized Choquet integral related to it. Considering their applications, see Section 2, we were mainly interested in their computational formulas on discrete space.
The derivation of these formulas required the introduction of new notations whose idea and practical meaning can be visually interpreted, as we described in Sections 3 and 4. Interesting results are Proposition
3.2
, Proposition
3.7
, Corollary
4.3
and Corollary
4.6
.
In Section 4 we also pointed out the direct and efficient construction of the graph of the generalized survival function.

Motivated by applications we solved the indistinguishability of generalized survival functions. This question is interesting, especially in decision-making processes, because the indistinguishability means incomparability of two inputs (alternatives).
The interesting results given in Proposition
6.5
, Proposition
6.11
and Proposition
6.13
are related to this.

In this paper, we also mentioned the formulas for calculating the generalized Choquet integral with respect to special types of monotone measure, and we solved the introductory problems serving as a motivation to study mentioned concepts.

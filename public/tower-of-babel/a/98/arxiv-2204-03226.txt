Abstract
. 1.
Introduction

1.1.
Monogenity of number fields and polynomials

Let
\displaystyle K
be a number field of degree
\displaystyle n
with ring of integers
\displaystyle\mathbb{Z}_{K}
, and absolute discriminant
\displaystyle d_{K}
.
The number field
\displaystyle K
is called
monogenic
if it admits a

power integral basis
, that is an integral basis of type
\displaystyle(1,\alpha,\ldots,\alpha^{n-1})

for some
\displaystyle\alpha\in\mathbb{Z}_{K}
.
Monogenity of number fields is a classical problem of algebraic number theory, going back to Dedekind, Hasse and Hensel, cf e.g.
[
23
,
25
]
and
[
15
]
for the present state of this area.
It is called a problem of Hasse to give an arithmetic characterization of those number fields which have a power integral basis
[
23
,
25
,
34
]
.
For any primitive element
\displaystyle\alpha
of
\displaystyle\mathbb{Z}_{K}
(that is
\displaystyle\alpha\in\mathbb{Z}_{K}
with
\displaystyle K=\mathbb{Q}(\alpha)
)
we denote by

\text{ind}(\alpha)=(\mathbb{Z}_{K}:\mathbb{Z}[\alpha])

the
index of
\displaystyle\alpha
, that is
the index of the
\displaystyle\mathbb{Z}
-module
\displaystyle\mathbb{Z}[\alpha]
in the free
\displaystyle\mathbb{Z}
-module
\displaystyle\mathbb{Z}_{K}
of rank
\displaystyle n
.
As it is known
[
15
]
, we have

\triangle(\alpha)=\text{ind}(\alpha)^{2}\cdot d_{K}

where
\displaystyle\triangle(\alpha)
is the discriminant of
\displaystyle\alpha
. If
\displaystyle F(x)
is the minimal polynomial of
\displaystyle\alpha
then we have
\displaystyle\Delta(F)=\Delta(\alpha)
and
we also use the
index of

\displaystyle F
,
\displaystyle\text{ind}(F)=\text{ind}(\alpha)
.
We also say that the polynomial
\displaystyle F(x)\in\mathbb{Z}[x]
is
monogenic
, if
\displaystyle\text{ind}(F)=1
, that is a root
\displaystyle\alpha

of
\displaystyle F(x)
generates a power integral basis in
\displaystyle K=\mathbb{Q}(\alpha)
.
Obviously, if
\displaystyle F(x)
is monogenic, then
\displaystyle K
is also monogenic, but the converse is not true:
there may exist a power integral basis in
\displaystyle K
, even if
\displaystyle F(x)
is not monogenic.
Further, note that
\displaystyle F(x)
is monogenic (that is
\displaystyle(1,\alpha,\ldots,\alpha^{n-1})

is an integral basis of
\displaystyle K
) if and only if
\displaystyle\mathbb{Z}[\alpha]

is integrally closed.

The elements
\displaystyle\alpha
and
\displaystyle\beta
of
\displaystyle\mathbb{Z}_{K}
are called
equivalent
if
\displaystyle\alpha\pm\beta\in\mathbb{Z}
.
Obviously, equivalent elements have the same indices.

Let
\displaystyle(1,\omega_{1},\ldots,\omega_{n-1})
be an integral basis of
\displaystyle K
.
The discriminant
\displaystyle\triangle(L(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n-1}))
of the linear form

\displaystyle L(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n-1})=\omega_{1}x_{1}+\ldots+\omega_{n-1}x_{n-1}
can be written (cf.
[
15
]
) as

\triangle(L(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n-1}))=(\text{ind}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n-1}))^{2}\cdot d_{K},

where
\displaystyle\text{ind}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n-1})
is the
index form
corresponding to the
integeral basis
\displaystyle(1,\omega_{1},\ldots,\omega_{n-1})
, having the property that for any

\displaystyle\alpha=x_{0}+\omega_{1}x_{1}+\ldots+\omega_{n-1}x_{n-1}\in\mathbb{Z}_{K}
(with
\displaystyle x_{0},x_{1},\ldots,x_{n-1}\in\mathbb{Z}
)
we have
\displaystyle\text{ind}(\alpha)=|\text{ind}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n-1})|
.

Obviously,
\displaystyle\text{ind}(\alpha)=1
if and only if
\displaystyle(1,\alpha,\ldots,\alpha^{n-1})
is an integral basis
of
\displaystyle K
. Therefore
\displaystyle\alpha
is a
generator of a power integral bases
if and only if

\displaystyle(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n-1})\in\mathbb{Z}^{n-1}
is a solution of the
index form equation

\text{ind}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n-1})=\pm 1\;\;{\rm in}\;\;(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n-1})\in\mathbb{Z}^{n-1}.

1.3.
The index of a number field

The greatest common divisor of the indices of all integral primitive elements of
\displaystyle K
is called the index of
\displaystyle K
, and denoted by
\displaystyle i(K)
. A rational prime
\displaystyle p
dividing
\displaystyle i(K)
is called a
prime common index divisor
of
\displaystyle K
. It is clear that if
\displaystyle\mathbb{Z}_{K}
has a power integral basis, then the index of
\displaystyle K
is trivial, namely
\displaystyle i(K)=1
. Therefore a field having a prime index divisor is not monogenic.

The first number field with non trivial index was given by Dedekind in 1871,
who exhibited examples in cubic and quartic number fields. For example, he considered the cubic field
\displaystyle K
generated by a root of
\displaystyle x^{3}-x^{2}-2x-8
and showed that the prime
\displaystyle 2
splits completely in
\displaystyle\mathbb{Z}_{K}
. So, if
\displaystyle K

were monogenic, then there would be a cubic polynomial, a root of which generating
\displaystyle K
, that splits completely into distinct polynomials of degree
\displaystyle 1
in
\displaystyle\mathbb{F}_{2}[x]
. Since there are only
\displaystyle 2
distinct polynomials of degree
\displaystyle 1
in
\displaystyle\mathbb{F}_{2}[x]
, this is impossible.

Based on these ideas and using Kronecker’s theory of algebraic number fields,
Hensel gave necessary and sufficient conditions
for any prime integer
\displaystyle p
to be a prime common index divisor
[
24
]
.
Hensel
[
25
]
also showed that the prime divisors of
\displaystyle i(K)
must be less than the
degree of the field
\displaystyle K
.

For arbitrary number fields of degree
\displaystyle n\leq 7
, Engstrom
[
8
]
characterized
\displaystyle\nu_{p}(i(K))
,
the highest power of
\displaystyle p
dividing
\displaystyle i(K)
,
by the factorization of
\displaystyle(p)
into powers of prime ideals of
\displaystyle\mathbb{Z}_{K}
for every positive prime
\displaystyle p\leq n
.
Problem 22 of Narkiewicz
[
36
]
asks for an explicit formula for the highest power of a
given prime integer
\displaystyle p
dividing
\displaystyle i(K)
.

In
[
35
]
, Nakahara studied the index of non-cyclic but abelian biquadratic number fields.
In
[
19
]
Gaál, Pethö and Pohst characterized the field indices of biquadratic number fields having Galois group
\displaystyle V_{4}
.
In quintic number field
\displaystyle K
defined by a trinomial
\displaystyle x^{5}+ax^{2}+b
, El Fadil
[
11
]

gave necessary and sufficient conditions for prime integer
\displaystyle p
to divide
\displaystyle i(K)

in terms of
\displaystyle a
,
\displaystyle b
.

1.4.
The purpose of the present paper

In this paper we consider trinomials of type
\displaystyle x^{4}+ax^{2}+b
. This is a special type of trinomials,
but exactly these special properties enable us to formulate results that far exceed
some corresponding statements on general types of trinomials.

Our paper was motivated by some results concerning trinomials of type
\displaystyle x^{4}+ax+b
.
Alaca and Williams
[
2
]
,
[
3
]
constructed
\displaystyle p
-integral bases and integral bases
of quartic fields generated by a root of such a trinomial.
Davis and Spearman
[
6
]
characterized the prime divisors of
quartic number fields
\displaystyle K
generated by a root of
such a trinomial.

In this paper, for any quartic number field
\displaystyle K
generated by a root
\displaystyle\alpha
of an irreducible
trinomial
\displaystyle x^{4}+ax^{2}+b\in\mathbb{Z}[x]
, we give necessary and sufficient conditions
for the integral closedness of
\displaystyle\mathbb{Z}[\alpha]
in terms of the coefficients
\displaystyle a
and
\displaystyle b
.
We also evaluate
\displaystyle\nu_{p}(i(K))
for every prime integer
\displaystyle p
.
Based on Engstrom’s results given in
[
8
]
, the unique prime candidates to divide
\displaystyle i(K)
are
\displaystyle 2
and
\displaystyle 3
, that is
\displaystyle i(K)=2^{\nu_{2}}3^{\nu_{3}}
, with
\displaystyle 0\leq\nu_{2}\leq 2
and
\displaystyle 0\leq\nu_{3}\leq 1
. In
[
6
]
, for a quartic number field
\displaystyle K
defined by a trinomial
\displaystyle x^{4}+ax+b\in\mathbb{Z}[x]
, and for every prime integer
\displaystyle p
, Davis and Spearman gave necessary and sufficient conditions on
\displaystyle a
and
\displaystyle b
so that
\displaystyle p
is a common index divisor of
\displaystyle K
. Their method is based on the calculation of the
\displaystyle p
-index form of
\displaystyle K
, derived from
\displaystyle p
-integral bases of
\displaystyle K
.
Our results in Theorems
2.2
and
2.3

are analogous to that given in
[
6
]
, but our method is totally different;
the proofs of Theorems
2.2
and
2.3
are based on prime ideal factorization, which is performed for quartic number fields in the thesis of Montes (1999). The first author is very thankful to Professor Enric Nart who provided him a copy of Montes’s thesis.

In the present paper, we consider three types of problems: For any quartic number field
\displaystyle K
generated by a root
\displaystyle\alpha
of an irreducible trinomial
\displaystyle x^{4}+ax^{2}+b\in\mathbb{Z}[x]
,

–we characterize when
\displaystyle\mathbb{Z}[\alpha]
is integrally closed (Theorem
2.1
),

–we give necessary and sufficient conditions for
\displaystyle 2
or
\displaystyle 3
to divide the index of
\displaystyle K
, in terms of
\displaystyle a
,
\displaystyle b

–we determine
\displaystyle\nu_{p}(i(K))
for
\displaystyle p=2,3

–we study monogenity of
\displaystyle K
(see Subsection
\displaystyle 4.2
).

–for a wide class of monogenic trinomials of type
\displaystyle x^{4}+ax^{2}+b
we show that up to equivalence
the root of the trinomial is the only generator of power integral bases.

We also provide a series of examples illustrating our results.

2.3.
The number of inequivalent generators of power integral bases

Number fields usually only have a few inequivalent generators of power integral bases
(see e.g. the tables of
[
15
]
). Considering recently some types of monogenic binomials

\displaystyle x^{n}-m
we had the experience that up to equivalence the root of the binomial is
the only generator of power integral bases in the number field generated by the root.
Calculating ”small” solutions (with coefficients
\displaystyle<10^{100}
in absolute values in the integral bases)
we found that this phenomenon occurs in pure sextic fields generated by a root of
a monogenic binomial
\displaystyle x^{6}-m
and in pure octic fields
generated by a root of
a monogenic binomial
\displaystyle x^{8}-m
both for with
\displaystyle 0<m<-5000
.

Therefore we found that it will also
be interesting to consider the number of inequivalent generators of power
integral bases of monogenic trinomials of type
\displaystyle x^{4}+ax^{2}+b
, utilizing the special properties
of this trinomial.
Note that using
[
17
]
,
[
18
]

in any specific quartic field it is possible to calculate
all generators of power integral bases, but we would like to formulate
here a statement in a parametric form covering an infinite number of fields.
We succeeded to cover the cases
\displaystyle a>1,b>1
:

Theorem 2.4
.

Assume
\displaystyle a>1,b>1
and
\displaystyle F(x)=x^{4}+ax^{2}+b\in\mathbb{Z}[x]
is irreducible and monogenic.
If
\displaystyle a,b
are not of type

(1)

a=\frac{u\pm 1}{v},\;\;\;b=\frac{u^{2}-1}{4v^{2}}

for some
\displaystyle u,v\in\mathbb{Z},v\neq 0,u\neq\pm 1
, then up to equivalence, the root
\displaystyle\alpha

of
\displaystyle F(x)
is the only generator of power integral bases in
\displaystyle K=\mathbb{Q}(\alpha)
.

Note that if
\displaystyle a,b
are of type (
1
) then there may be several
inequivalent generators of power integral bases in the corresponding quartic fields. Hence if
\displaystyle a,b
are of type (
1
), then the statement of our Theorem
2.4
is not valid.

We list a few examples of pairs of positive parameters
\displaystyle(a,b)
, represented
in the form (
1
), such that the trinomial
\displaystyle F(x)=x^{4}+ax^{2}+b
is irreducible and monogenic
but the number field
\displaystyle K=\mathbb{Q}(\alpha)
, generated by a root
\displaystyle\alpha
of

\displaystyle F(x)
admits several inequivalent generators of power integral bases.
As in such fields
\displaystyle(1,\alpha,\alpha^{2},\alpha^{3})
is an integral basis, we shall
give the triples
\displaystyle(x,y,z)
such that
\displaystyle\gamma=x\alpha+y\alpha^{2}+z\alpha^{3}
generates
a power integral basis in
\displaystyle K
:

Case 1.

3.
A short introduction to Newton polygons

We use Newton polygon techniques. This is a standard method which is rather technical but
very efficient to apply. We have introduced the corresponding concepts in several former papers.
Here we only give a brief introduction which makes our proofs understandable.
For a detailed description we refer to Section 3 of our paper
[
12
]
,
to Guardia and Nart
[
22
]
and to Guardia, Montes and Nart
[
21
]
.

Let
\displaystyle F(x)\in\mathbb{Z}[x]
be a monic irreducible polynomial with a root
\displaystyle\alpha
and set
\displaystyle K=\mathbb{Q}(\alpha)
.
We shall use Dedekind’s theorem

[
37
, Chapter I, Proposition 8.3]

relating the prime ideal factorization
\displaystyle p\mathbb{Z}_{K}

and the factorization of
\displaystyle F(x)
modulo
\displaystyle p
(for primes
\displaystyle p
not dividing
\displaystyle(\mathbb{Z}_{K}:\mathbb{Z}[\alpha])
).
Also, we shall need Dedekind’s criterion
[
5
, Theorem 6.1.4]
on the divisibility of

\displaystyle(\mathbb{Z}_{K}:\mathbb{Z}[\alpha])
by primes
\displaystyle p
.

For any prime integer
\displaystyle p
, let
\displaystyle\nu_{p}
be the
\displaystyle p
-adic valuation of
\displaystyle\mathbb{Q}
,
\displaystyle\mathbb{Q}_{p}
its
\displaystyle p
-adic completion, and
\displaystyle\mathbb{Z}_{p}
the ring of
\displaystyle p
-adic integers. Let
\displaystyle\nu_{p}
be the Gauss’s extension of
\displaystyle\nu_{p}
to
\displaystyle\mathbb{Q}_{p}(x)
.
For any polynomial
\displaystyle P=\sum_{i=0}^{n}a_{i}x^{i}\in\mathbb{Q}_{p}[x]
we set

\displaystyle\nu_{p}(P)=\mbox{min}(\nu_{p}(a_{i}),\,i=0,\dots,n)
.
For nonzero polynomials
\displaystyle P,Q\in\mathbb{Q}_{p}[x]
, we extend this valuation to

\displaystyle\nu_{p}(P/Q)=\nu_{p}(P)-\nu_{p}(Q)
.

Let
\displaystyle\phi\in\mathbb{Z}_{p}[x]
be a monic polynomial whose reduction is irreducible in

\displaystyle\mathbb{F}_{p}[x]
, let
\displaystyle\mathbb{F}_{\phi}
be
the field
\displaystyle\frac{\mathbb{F}_{p}[x]}{(\overline{\phi})}
. For any
monic polynomial
\displaystyle F(x)\in\mathbb{Z}_{p}[x]
, upon the Euclidean division
by successive powers of
\displaystyle\phi
, we expand
\displaystyle F(x)
as

\displaystyle F(x)=\sum_{i=0}^{l}a_{i}(x)\phi(x)^{i},
called the
\displaystyle\phi
-
expansion
of
\displaystyle F(x)

(for every
\displaystyle i
, deg
\displaystyle(a_{i}(x))<

deg
\displaystyle(\phi)
).
The
\displaystyle\phi
-
Newton polygon

\displaystyle N_{\phi}(F)
of
\displaystyle F(x)
with respect to
\displaystyle p
is the lower boundary convex envelope of the set of points
\displaystyle\{(i,\nu_{p}(a_{i}(x))),\,a_{i}(x)\neq 0\}
. It is the process of joining the obtained edges
\displaystyle S_{1},\dots,S_{r}
ordered by increasing slopes, which can be expressed as
\displaystyle N_{\phi}(F)=S_{1}+\dots+S_{r}
.
For every side
\displaystyle S_{i}
of
\displaystyle N_{\phi}(F)
, the length
\displaystyle l(S_{i})
of
\displaystyle S_{i}
is the length of its projection to the
\displaystyle x
-axis and its height
\displaystyle h(S_{i})
is the length of its projection to the
\displaystyle y
-axis. We call
\displaystyle d(S_{i})=\gcd(l(S_{i}),h(S_{i}))
the degree of
\displaystyle S
.
The
principal

\displaystyle\phi
-
Newton polygon

\displaystyle N_{\phi}^{-}(F)
of
\displaystyle{F}

is the part of the polygon
\displaystyle N_{\phi}(F)
, which is determined by joining all sides of negative slopes.
To every side
\displaystyle S
of
\displaystyle N_{\phi}^{-}(F)
, with initial point
\displaystyle(s,u_{s})
and length
\displaystyle l
, and to every

\displaystyle 0\leq i\leq l
, we attach the following
residue coefficient
\displaystyle c_{i}\in\mathbb{F}_{\phi}
:

c_{i}=\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}0,&\mbox{ if }(s+i,{\it u_{s+i}})\mbox{ lies strictly
above }S,\\
\left(\dfrac{a_{s+i}(x)}{p^{{\it u_{s+i}}}}\right)\,\,\ \mbox{\rm(mod }{(p,\phi(x))}),&\mbox{ if }(s+i,{\it u_{s+i}})\mbox{ lies on }S,\end{array}\right.

where
\displaystyle(p,\phi(x))
is the maximal ideal of
\displaystyle\mathbb{Z}_{p}[x]
generated by
\displaystyle p
and
\displaystyle\phi(x)
.
Let
\displaystyle-\lambda=-h/e
be the slope of
\displaystyle S
, where
\displaystyle h
and
\displaystyle e
are two positive coprime integers. Then
\displaystyle d=l/e
is the degree of
\displaystyle S
. Notice that,
the points with integer coordinates lying on
\displaystyle S
are exactly
\displaystyle(s,u_{s}),(s+e,u_{s}-h),\cdots,(s+de,u_{s}-dh)
. Thus, if
\displaystyle i
is not a multiple of
\displaystyle e
, then

\displaystyle(s+i,u_{s+i})
does not lie in
\displaystyle S
, and so
\displaystyle c_{i}=0
. The polynomial

\displaystyle R_{\lambda}(F)(y)=t_{d}y^{d}+t_{d-1}y^{d-1}+\cdots+t_{1}y+t_{0}\in\mathbb{F}_{\phi}[y],
is called
the
residual polynomial
of
\displaystyle F(x)
associated to the side
\displaystyle S
,
where for every
\displaystyle i=0,\dots,d
,
\displaystyle t_{i}=c_{ie}
.

Let
\displaystyle N_{\phi}^{-}(F)=S_{1}+\dots+S_{r}
be the principal
\displaystyle\phi
-Newton polygon of
\displaystyle f
with respect to
\displaystyle p
. We say that
\displaystyle F
is a
\displaystyle\phi
-
regular polynomial
with respect to
\displaystyle p
, if
\displaystyle F_{S_{i}}(y)
is square free in
\displaystyle\mathbb{F}_{\phi}[y]
for every
\displaystyle i=1,\dots,r
. The polynomial
\displaystyle F(x)
is said to be
\displaystyle p
-regular

if
\displaystyle\overline{F(x)}=\prod_{i=1}^{r}\overline{\phi_{i}}^{l_{i}}
for some monic polynomials
\displaystyle\phi_{1},\dots,\phi_{t}
of
\displaystyle\mathbb{Z}[x]
such that
\displaystyle\overline{\phi_{1}},\dots,\overline{\phi_{t}}
are irreducible coprime polynomials over
\displaystyle\mathbb{F}_{p}
and
\displaystyle F(x)
is a
\displaystyle\phi_{i}
-regular polynomial with respect to
\displaystyle p
for every
\displaystyle i=1,\dots,t
.

Let
\displaystyle\phi\in\mathbb{Z}_{p}[x]
be a monic polynomial, with
\displaystyle\overline{\phi(x)}
irreducible in
\displaystyle\mathbb{F}_{p}[x]
. As defined in
[
14
, Def. 1.3]
, the
\displaystyle\phi
-
index
of
\displaystyle F(x)
, denoted by
\displaystyle\text{ind}_{\phi}(F)
, is deg
\displaystyle(\phi)
times the number of points with natural integer coordinates that lie below or on the polygon
\displaystyle N_{\phi}^{-}(F)
, strictly above the horizontal axis, and strictly beyond the vertical axis.

Let
\displaystyle\overline{F(x)}=\prod_{i=1}^{r}\overline{\phi_{i}}^{l_{i}}
be the factorization of
\displaystyle\overline{F(x)}
in
\displaystyle\mathbb{F}_{p}[x]
, where every
\displaystyle\phi_{i}\in\mathbb{Z}[x]
is monic polynomial, with
\displaystyle\overline{\phi_{i}(x)}
is irreducible in
\displaystyle\mathbb{F}_{p}[x]
,
\displaystyle\overline{\phi_{i}(x)}
and
\displaystyle\overline{\phi_{j}(x)}
are coprime when
\displaystyle i\neq j
and
\displaystyle i,j=1,\dots,t
.
For every
\displaystyle i=1,\dots,t
, let
\displaystyle N_{\phi_{i}}^{+}(F)=S_{i1}+\dots+S_{ir_{i}}
be the principal
\displaystyle\phi_{i}
-Newton polygon of
\displaystyle F(x)
with respect to
\displaystyle p
. For every
\displaystyle j=1,\dots,r_{i}
, let
\displaystyle F_{S_{ij}}(y)=\prod_{k=1}^{s_{ij}}\psi_{ijk}^{a_{ijk}}(y)
be the factorization of
\displaystyle F_{S_{ij}}(y)
in
\displaystyle\mathbb{F}_{\phi_{i}}[y]
.
We shall use the following index theorem of Ore (see e.g.
[
14
, Theorem 1.7 and Theorem 1.9]
):

Theorem 3.1
.

(1)

Using the above notation we have

\nu_{p}(\text{ind}(F))\geq\sum_{i=1}^{r}\text{ind}_{\phi_{i}}(F).

The equality holds if
\displaystyle F(x)
is
\displaystyle p
-regular.

(2)

If
\displaystyle F(x)
is
\displaystyle p
-regular, then

p\mathbb{Z}_{K}=\prod_{i=1}^{r}\prod_{j=1}^{r_{i}}\prod_{k=1}^{s_{ij}}\mathfrak{p}^{e_{ij}}_{ijk},

is the factorization of
\displaystyle p\mathbb{Z}_{K}
into powers of prime ideals of
\displaystyle\mathbb{Z}_{K}
lying above
\displaystyle p
, where
\displaystyle e_{ij}=l_{ij}/d_{ij}
,
\displaystyle l_{ij}
is the length of
\displaystyle S_{ij}
,
\displaystyle d_{ij}
is the degree
of
\displaystyle S_{ij}
, and
\displaystyle f_{ijk}=\mbox{deg}(\phi_{i})\times\mbox{deg}(\psi_{ijk})
is the residue degree of the prime ideal
\displaystyle\mathfrak{p}_{ijk}
over
\displaystyle p
.

Remark 2
.

By Dedekind’s criterion,
\displaystyle\nu_{p}(\text{ind}(F))=0
if and only if
\displaystyle\text{ind}_{\phi_{i}}(F)=0
for every
\displaystyle i=1,\dots,r
, which menas
\displaystyle N_{\phi_{i}}^{-}(F)
has a single side of height
\displaystyle 1
for every
\displaystyle i=1,\dots,r
.

When the program of Ore fails, that is
\displaystyle F(x)
is not
\displaystyle p
-regular, then in order to complete the factorization of
\displaystyle F(x)
, Guardia, Montes, and Nart introduced the notion of
high order Newton polygon
. They showed, thanks to a theorem on the index

[
21
, Theorem 4.18]
, that after a finite number of iterations this process yields all monic irreducible factors of
\displaystyle F(x)
, all prime ideals of
\displaystyle\mathbb{Z}_{K}
lying above a prime
\displaystyle p
, the index
\displaystyle(\mathbb{Z}_{K}:\mathbb{Z}[\alpha])
, and the absolute discriminant of
\displaystyle K
. For more details, we refer to
[
21
]
.

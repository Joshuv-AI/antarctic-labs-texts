Homology of graph burnings
Yuri Muranov, Anna Muranova

Abstract
In this paper we study graph burnings using methods of algebraic topology. We prove that the time function of a burning is a graph map to a path graph. Afterwards, we define a category whose objects are graph burnings and morphisms are graph maps which commute with the time functions of the burnings. In this category we study relations between burnings of different graphs and, in particular, between burnings of a graph and its subgraphs. For every graph, we define a simplicial complex, arising from the set of all the burnings, which we call a configuration space of the burnings. Further, simplicial structure of the configuration space gives burning homology of the graph. We describe properties of the configuration space and the burning homology theory. In particular, we prove that the one-dimensional skeleton of the configuration space of a graph G G coincides with the complement graph of G G . The results are illustrated with numerous examples.



Keywords:

homology of graphs, simplicial homology, simplicial complex, graph burning, path graph, burning time, trees.

In this paper we study graph burning using methods of algebraic topology in graph theory. We consider only

Definition 2.1

A graph

G=(V_{G},E_{G})
is a non-empty finite set
V_{G}
of

E_{G}
of non-ordered pairs
\{v,w\}\in E_{G}

Sometimes we will omit
the subscript

of vertices and from the set

of edges if the graph is clear from the context.

Two different vertices are
adjacent
if they are connected by an edge. All vertices which are adjacent to vertex
v
are called
neighbors
of
v
. A vertex is
incident
with an edge
if the vertex is one of the endpoints of that edge.
Two different edges
e,e^{\prime}\in E_{G}
of a graph
G
are called
incident
if they have one common vertex.

Definition 2.2

We say that a graph
H
is a
subgraph
of a graph
G
and we write
H\subset G
if
V_{H}\subset V_{G}
and
E_{H}\subset E_{G}
. A subgraph

is the
induced subgraph
if for any edge
\{v,w\}\in E_{G}
, such that
v,w\in V_{H}
we have
\{v,w\}\in E_{H}
. In this case we write

. For a subgraph

the induced subgraph which has the same set of vertices as

.

Definition 2.3

A

For a graph

Thus we obtain a category

whose objects are finite graphs
and whose morphisms are graph maps and a category
\mathbb{G}
whose objects are finite graphs
and whose morphisms are homomorphisms.

Definition 2.4

For

For

Definition 2.5

(i) A graph

(ii) A connected graph

(iii) A graph

(iv) A

(v) A graph is

(vi)

Note that the edgeless graph

Definition 2.6

For a graph

Definition 2.7

Definition 2.8

Let

(i) The

is the subgraph of

and with the set of edges

.

(ii) The

.

(iii) The

3
Morphisms of graphs and graph burning

In this section we define a
graph burning
of a graph
G
. Afterwards, we prove that there exists a graph map of the graph
G
to a path graph
P_{n}
, defined by the burning. Further, we describe various relations between burnings of a graph and burnings of its induced subgraphs. At the end of the section we compute burning time for various burnings of several classes of graphs.

Let
G=(V_{G},E_{G})
be a graph and

S_{G}=(v_{1},\dots,v_{k})
be a non-empty ordered set of vertices. For
1\leq j\leq k+1
,
consider a set of induced subgraphs
\mathcal{N}_{j}\sqsubset G
defined by

\mathcal{N}_{j}=\begin{cases}N_{j-1}(v_{1})\Cup\dots\Cup N_{1}(v_{j-1})\Cup N_{0}(v_{j})&\text{for}\ \ 1\leq j\leq k,\\
N_{k}(v_{1})\Cup N_{k-1}(v_{2})\Cup\dots\Cup N_{1}(v_{k})&\text{for}\ j=k+1.\\
\end{cases}

(3.1)

For
2\leq j\leq k+1
, let
U_{j}
be the induced subgraph
of
G
given by

U_{j}=N_{j-1}(v_{1})\Cup N_{j-2}(v_{2})\Cup\dots\Cup N_{1}(v_{j-1}).

(3.2)

We note that for
2\leq j\leq k
, the set of vertices
V_{U_{j}}
of the graph
U_{j}
is obtained from the set of vertices
V_{\mathcal{N}_{j}}
of the graph
\mathcal{N}_{j}
by deleting the vertex
v_{j}
and
U_{k+1}=\mathcal{N}_{k+1}
.

It follows immediately from (
3.1
) and (
3.2
) that we have the following commutative diagram of inclusions of induced subgraphs

\begin{matrix}\mathcal{N}_{1}&\sqsubset&\mathcal{N}_{2}&\sqsubset&\mathcal{N}_{3}&\sqsubset\dots\sqsubset&\mathcal{N}_{k}&\sqsubset&\mathcal{N}_{k+1}&\sqsubset G\\
&&\sqcup&&\sqcup&\dots&\sqcup&&||&\ \ \ ||\\
&&U_{2}&\sqsubset&U_{3}&\sqsubset\dots\sqsubset&U_{k}&\sqsubset&U_{k+1}&\sqsubset G\\
&&\sqcup&&\sqcup&\dots&\sqcup&&\sqcup&\ \ \ \sqcup\\
&&\mathcal{N}_{1}&\sqsubset&\mathcal{N}_{2}&\sqsubset\dots\sqsubset&\mathcal{N}_{k-1}&\sqsubset&\mathcal{N}_{k}&\sqsubset\mathcal{N}_{k+1}\\
\end{matrix}

(3.3)

in which each row is a filtration of the graph
G
by induced subgraphs.

Definition 3.1

Let
G=(V,E)
be a graph and

S_{G}=(v_{1},\dots,v_{k})
be a non-empty ordered set of vertices.
The sequence
S_{G}
is called a
burning sequence
if in diagram (
3.3
)
v_{j}\notin U_{j}
for
2\leq j\leq k
and
U_{k+1}=G
.
The elements of
S_{G}
are called
burning sources
. The vertex
v_{i}
is called the
source at time
i
.

A burning sequence
S_{G}
defines a commutative diagram (
3.3
) and since in this case
\mathcal{N}_{k+1}=U_{k+1}=G

we obtain a filtration of
G

\mathcal{N}_{1}\sqsubset\mathcal{N}_{2}\sqsubset\dots\sqsubset\mathcal{N}_{k}\sqsubset\mathcal{N}_{k+1}=G.

(3.4)

Let
\mathbb{N}
be the set of positive integers. Define a function

\lambda_{G}\colon V_{G}\to\mathbb{N}\;\mbox{ by }\;\lambda_{G}(v)=\min\{i\,|\,v\in\mathcal{N}_{i}\}.

(3.5)

It follows from the filtration (
3.4
) that the function
\lambda_{G}
is well defined.

Lemma 3.2

Let
S_{G}=(v_{1},\dots,v_{n})
be a burning sequence of a graph
G=(V,E)
. Let
T=\max\{\lambda_{G}(v)\;|\;\,v\in V_{G}\}
where
\lambda_{G}
is defined by (
3.5
). Then the function
\lambda_{G}
is a surjective map from the set
V
of vertices to the set
\{1,2,\dots,T\}
,
where
T=k
or
T=k+1
.

Proof.
It follows from (
3.4
) and (
3.5
) that for any vertex
v\in V
we have
\lambda_{G}(v)=j\in\{1,\dots,k+1\}
. By (
3.1
), we obtain that the burning source
v_{j}\in\mathcal{N}_{j}
for
1\leq j\leq k
. By Definition
3.1
,
v_{j}\notin U_{j}
for
2\leq j\leq k
and, hence, by (
3.3
),
v_{j}\notin\mathcal{N}_{j-1}
for
2\leq j\leq k
. Hence
\lambda_{G}(v_{j})=j
for any source

j\in S_{G}
. In the case
\mathcal{N}_{k}=\mathcal{N}_{k+1}
we obtain that
T=k
. Otherwise
\mathcal{N}_{k}\neq\mathcal{N}_{k+1}
and
T=k+1
.

We call a pair
\mathbf{B}(G)\colon=(\lambda_{G},S_{G})
the
burning (process)
for the graph
G
. We call the integer
T
the
end time of the burning
. Further, for a vertex
v\in V_{G}
the value
\lambda_{G}(v)
is called the

. We write

if the process ends in the time

. We denote by

which corresponds to the process

.

Lemma 3.3

Let
G=(V,E)
be a graph with a burning
\mathbf{B}(G)=(\lambda,S_{G})
. Let

be two adjacent vertices. If
v\in\mathcal{N}_{j}
for some

then

.

Proof.
By the definition of
\lambda
in (
3.5
) and using (

) we obtain that

v\in\mathcal{N}_{j}=N_{j-1}(v_{1})\Cup N_{i-2}(v_{2})\Cup\dots\Cup N_{1}(v_{j-1})\Cup N_{0}(v_{j}),

Hence there exists at least one number
i\,(1\leq i\leq j)
such that
v\in~N_{j-i}(v_{i})
, that is
d_{G}(v_{i},v)\leq j-i
. We have

. Hence, by the triangle inequality for the graph distance

we obtain that

d_{G}(v_{i},w)\leq d_{G}(v_{i},v)+d_{G}(v,w)=j-i+1

Lemma 3.4

Let
G=(V,E)
be a graph and
\mathbf{B}^{T}(G)
be a graph burning with
\lambda=\lambda_{G}
. Then for any two adjacent vertices

we have
|\lambda(v)-\lambda(w)|\in\{0,1\}

Proof.
If
\lambda(v)=\lambda(w)
, then there is nothing to prove. Without loss of generality, assume that
\lambda(w)>\lambda(v)
. Then
v\in\mathcal{N}_{\lambda(v)}
and by Lemma
3.3

w\in\mathcal{N}_{\lambda(v)+1}
, and hence, by (
3.3
),
\lambda(w)\leq\lambda(v)+1

Theorem 3.5

Let
G=(V,E)
be a graph and
\mathbf{B}^{T}(G)
be a graph burning. Then the function
\lambda_{G}=\lambda(\mathbf{B}^{T}(G))
defines a unique graph map

which coincides
with
\lambda(\mathbf{B}^{T}(G))
on the set of vertices

. We continue to denote this graph map by

Proof.
Recall that

is the path graph, i.e the graph with the set of vertices

Definition 4.1

of
vertices
and a set

(ii) if

A simplex

A simplicial complex is

Theorem 4.15

Let
H
and
G
be trees and
H\subset G
.
Then
G
is the burning extension of the graph
H
.

Proof.
Consider a burning
\mathbf{B}(H)=(\lambda_{H},S_{H})
where

S_{H}=(v_{1},\dots,v_{n}),\ v_{i}\in V_{H}
and
n\geq 2
. For
n=1
the statement follows from Definition
3.1
. For
2\leq j\leq n+1
we denote by

U_{j}
the induced subgraph of
H
defined in (
3.2
) where all neighborhoods
N_{k}
are neighborhoods in
H
. For
2\leq j\leq n+1
, we define also an induced subgraph of the graph
G
by setting

U_{j}^{G}=N_{j-1}^{G}(v_{1})\Cup N_{j-2}^{G}(v_{2})\Cup\dots\Cup N_{1}^{G}(v_{j-1})

(4.1)

where all neighborhoods
N_{k}^{G}
are the neighborhoods in
G
.
We have evidently
U_{j}\subset U_{j}^{G}
. By Definition
3.1
, we have
v_{j}\notin U_{j}
and
U_{n+1}=G
. Now we prove that
v_{j}\notin U_{j}^{G}
. Indeed,
let us suppose that
v_{j}\in U_{j}^{G}
. Then there exists at least one neighborhood

N_{j-k}^{G}(v_{k})
in (
4.1
) such that
v_{j}\in N_{j-k}^{G}(v_{k})
. Hence there is a path of a minimal length
d\leq j-k
from
v_{k}\in V_{H}
to
v_{j}\in V_{H}
. Since
G
is a tree there is an unique path of minimal length
d
from

v_{k}
to
v_{j}
which lays in the subgraph
H
, since the tree is connected by the definition. Hence
v_{j}\in N_{j-k}(v_{k})
and

v_{j}\in U_{j}
. We obtain a contradiction and, hence,
v_{j}\notin U_{j}^{G}
for
2\leq j\leq n+1
. Now if
U_{n+1}^{G}=G
we have obtained that
(v_{1},\dots,v_{n})
is the burning sequence of the graph
G
. Otherwise, we have

U_{n+1}^{G}\varsubsetneqq G
and we can add an issue vertex
v_{n+1}\in V_{G}
to the burning sequence
S_{H}
. Continuing this process we obtain a burning of
G
which is a burning extension of
S_{H}
.

Definition 4.17

(q-1)
-simplex
\sigma
of
\Delta
.

Definition 4.18

5
Burning homology

In this Section, we define
homology groups of
G
-burnings
as simplicial homology groups of
\Delta_{G}
. We describe properties of introduced homology groups and give several examples

Let
R
be a unitary commutative ring and
\Delta=(V,F)
be a simplicial complex. For
n\geq 0
,
homology groups

H_{n}(\Delta,R)\,
of the complex
\Delta
with coefficients in
R
are defined using a functorially defined chain complex

\Lambda_{*}(\Delta)=\{\Lambda_{n},\partial\}
of
R
modules (see, for example,
[
8
]
,
[
11
]
,
[
13
]
,
[
14
]
). Recall that the module
\Lambda_{n}(\Delta)
is generated by
n
-dimensional simplexes of
\Delta
. The
augmented
chain complex
\widetilde{\Lambda}_{*}(\Delta)

is the complex
\Lambda_{*}(\Delta)
equipped with the
augmentation homomorphism

\varepsilon\colon\Lambda_{0}(\Delta)\to R\ \ \text{where}\ \ \varepsilon\left(\sum_{i}n_{i}\sigma_{i}\right)=\sum_{i}n_{i}

where
\sigma_{i}
are zero dimensional simplexes and
n_{i}\in R
. The homology groups of the augmented chain complex are called
reduced homology groups
and are denoted by
\widetilde{H}_{*}(\Delta,R)
.

Definition 5.1

The
burning homology
of a graph
G
is the simplicial homology of the simplicial complex
\Delta_{G}
. The corresponding homology groups are denoted by
H_{k}(\mathcal{B}(G),R)
where

be a burning extension of a graph

i_{*}\colon H_{k}(\mathcal{B}(H),R)\to H_{k}(\mathcal{B}(G),R)\ \text{for}\ \ k\geq 0.

(5.1)

Proof.
Follows from the definition of homology groups and Proposition
4.14
.

(5.2)

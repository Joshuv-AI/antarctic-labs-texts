On the Northcott property of Dedekind zeta functions
Xavier Généreux and Matilde Lalín

Abstract
. The Northcott property for special values of Dedekind zeta functions and more general motivic L L -functions was defined by Pazuki and Pengo. We investigate this property for any complex evaluation of Dedekind zeta functions. The results are more delicate and subtle than what was proven for the function field case in previous work of Li and the authors, since they include some surprising behavior in the neighborhood of the trivial zeros. The techniques include a mixture of analytic and computer assisted arguments.

Introduction

Recently Pazuki and Pengo
[
18
]
considered a variant of the Northcott property for special values of
L
-functions attached to mixed motives. Usually the Northcott property
[
13
]
refers to the fact that a set of algebraic numbers with bounded height and bounded degree must be finite. In the number field case, the problem that Pazuki and Pengo study concerns special values of the Dedekind zeta function. For a field
K
and
s\in\mathbb{C}
denote by

\zeta_{K}^{*}(s):=\lim_{t\rightarrow s}\frac{\zeta_{K}(t)}{(t-s)^{\text{ord}_{s}(\zeta_{K}(t))}},

the first nonzero coefficient of the Taylor series for
\zeta_{K}
around
s
.

For a fixed
s=n\in\mathbb{Z}
and a fixed positive real number
B
,
Pazuki and Pengo study the set of isomorphism classes of number fields
K
given by

S_{B,n}=\{[K]:|\zeta_{K}^{*}(n)|\leq B\},

and discuss the finiteness of this set under various conditions of
B
and
n
. For number fields, they prove that the Northcott property holds for
n
a negative integer or
n=0
, and it does not hold if
n
is a positive integer. They also estimate the size of this set for the integers
n
such that the Northcott property holds.

In
[
5
]
Li and the authors of this note consider the analogous problem for isomorphism classes of function fields
K
with constant field
\mathbb{F}_{q}
. However, instead of restricting to special values with
s=n\in\mathbb{Z}
, they work directly with
\zeta_{K}^{*}(s)
with
s
a fixed arbitrary complex number. They are able to establish or partially establish the question of the Northcott property
outside
the set
\frac{1}{2}-\frac{\log 2}{\log q}\leq\re(s)<\frac{1}{2}
. More precisely, the Northcott property holds when
\re(s)<\frac{1}{2}-\frac{\log 2}{\log q}
and the set
S_{B,s}
is infinite for
B
larger than a certain constant depending on
s
in

\re(s)\geq\frac{1}{2}
. Moreover, remark that the gap corresponding to
\frac{1}{2}-\frac{\log 2}{\log q}\leq\re(s)<\frac{1}{2}
shrinks to the empty set as

tends to infinity. Figure

2.
Some background

In this section we recall some background regarding the Dedekind zeta function
\zeta_{K}(s)
, the Gamma function, and some inequalities involving trigonometric and hyperbolic functions.
Let
K
be a number field, that is, a finite extension of
\mathbb{Q}
of degree
d_{K}=r_{1}+2r_{2}
, where
r_{1}
denotes the number of real embeddings and
r_{2}
, the number of pairs of complex embeddings. The Dedekind zeta function of
K
is given by

(2)

\zeta_{K}(s):=\sum_{I\subseteq{\mathcal{O}}_{K}}\frac{1}{N_{K/\mathbb{Q}}(I)^{s}}=\prod_{P\subseteq{\mathcal{O}}_{K}}(1-N_{K/\mathbb{Q}}(P)^{-s})^{-1},\qquad\re(s)>1,

where the sum takes place over all the ideals in the integral domain
\mathcal{O}_{K}
and the Euler product goes over the prime ideals of
\mathcal{O}_{K}
.

Let
\Delta_{K}
denote the discriminant of
K/\mathbb{Q}
. The Dedekind zeta function
\zeta_{K}(s)
satisfies the following functional equation

\zeta_{K}(s)=\zeta_{K}(1-s)\frac{\Gamma_{\mathbb{R}}(1-s)^{r_{1}}\Gamma_{\mathbb{C}}(1-s)^{r_{2}}}{\Gamma_{\mathbb{R}}(s)^{r_{1}}\Gamma_{\mathbb{C}}(s)^{r_{2}}}|\Delta_{K}|^{\frac{1}{2}-s},

where

\Gamma_{\mathbb{R}}(s)=\pi^{-s/2}\Gamma(s/2),\qquad\Gamma_{\mathbb{C}}(s)=2(2\pi)^{-s}\Gamma(s).

Here

\Gamma(s):=\int_{0}^{\infty}x^{s-1}e^{-x}dx,\quad\re(s)>1

is the gamma function, which has a meromorphic continuation to the whole complex plane, with simple poles at
0
and at the negative integers. When
n
is a positive integer, we have

\Gamma(n)=(n-1)!

The value at a complex number can be controlled by the value at a real argument. More precisely, writing
s=\sigma+i\tau
, we have,

(3)

|\Gamma(s)|\leq|\Gamma(\sigma)|.

(See
[
1
, Eq. 6.1.26]
.)

Euler’s reflection formula
[
1
, Eq. 6.1.17]
gives

(4)

\Gamma(s)\Gamma(1-s)=\frac{\pi}{\sin(\pi s)},\qquad s\not\in\mathbb{Z}.

Lagrange’s duplication formula
[
1
, Eq. 6.1.18]
yields

(5)

\Gamma(s)\Gamma\left(s+\frac{1}{2}\right)=2^{1-2s}\sqrt{\pi}\Gamma(2s).

For
\sigma\geq\frac{1}{2}
, we have

(6)

|\Gamma(s)|\geq\frac{\Gamma(\sigma)}{|\cosh(\pi\tau)|^{\frac{1}{2}}}.

(See
[
12
, Eq. 5.6.7]
.)

Euler’s infinite product (
[
1
, Eq. 6.1.3]
) gives

(7)

\frac{1}{\Gamma(z)}=ze^{\gamma z}\prod_{k=1}^{\infty}\left(1+\frac{z}{k}\right)e^{-z/k}

The digamma function is the logarithmic derivative of the gamma function:

(8)

\psi(s)=\frac{d}{ds}\log(\Gamma(s)).

It can be expressed with the following series (
[
1
, Eq. 6.3.16]
)

(9)

\psi(s+1)=-\gamma+\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\left(\frac{1}{k}-\frac{1}{k+s}\right),

for
s\not=-1,-2,\dots
.

We will need some bounds relating trigonometric functions and hyperbolic functions.
For example, we have

(10)

|\sin(s)|\geq|\sinh(\tau)|.

(See
[
1
, Eq. 4.3.83]
.)

The following result will be used to bound
|\sin(z)|
and
|\cos(z)|
in terms of
|z|
.

Lemma 2.1
.

For any
z\in\mathbb{C}
, we have

\displaystyle|\sin(|z|)|\leq|\sin(z)|\leq\sinh(|z|).

Similarly we have

\displaystyle|\cos(|z|)|\leq|\cos(z)|\leq\cosh(|z|).

Proof.

Notice that the upper bound on
|\sin(z)|
and
|\cos(z)|
are well-known (see for example
[
1
, Eq. 4.3.87, Eq. 4.3.86]
).

Write for simplicity
z=re^{it}
, where
r\geq 0
and
t\in[0,2\pi)
. Consider

\displaystyle|\sin(z)|^{2}=|\sin(re^{i t})|^{2}=\sin(r\cos(t))^{2}\cosh(r\sin(t))^{2}+\cos(r\cos(t))^{2}\sinh(r\sin(t))^{2}=:f(t).

The derivatives with respect to
t
give

\displaystyle f^{\prime}(t)=

\displaystyle r\sinh(2r\sin(t))\cos(t)-r\sin(2r\cos(t))\sin(t),

\displaystyle f^{\prime\prime}(t)=

\displaystyle 2r^{2}\cosh(2r\sin(t))\cos(t)^{2}+2r^{2}\cos(2r\cos(t))\sin(t)^{2}

\displaystyle-r\sinh(2r\sin(t))\sin(t)-r\sin(2r\cos(t))\cos(t).

We can focus on
[0,2\pi)
. Studying the derivatives, we find minima at
0
and
\pi
as well as maxima at
\frac{1}{2}\pi
and
\frac{3}{2}\pi
. Thus, we obtain

\sin(r)^{2}\leq f(t)\leq\sinh(r)^{2}.

By taking square roots everywhere in the above inequality we obtain the result.

Similarly, consider

\displaystyle|\cos(z)|^{2}=|\cos(re^{i t})|^{2}=\cos(r\cos(t))^{2}\cosh(r\sin(t))^{2}+\sin(r\cos(t))^{2}\sinh(r\sin(t))^{2}=:g(t).

As before, we have

\displaystyle g^{\prime}(t)=

\displaystyle r\sinh(2r\sin(t))\cos(t)+r\sin(2r\cos(t))\sin(t),

\displaystyle g^{\prime\prime}(t)=

\displaystyle 2r^{2}\cosh(2r\sin(t))\cos(t)^{2}-2r^{2}\cos(2r\cos(t))\sin(t)^{2}

\displaystyle-r\sinh(2r\sin(t))\sin(t)+r\sin(2r\cos(t))\cos(t).

We study the derivatives on
[0,2\pi)
, and we find minima at 0 and
\pi
and maxima at
\frac{1}{2}\pi
and
\frac{3}{2}\pi
. This gives

\cos(r)^{2}\leq g(t)\leq\cosh(r)^{2}.

By taking square roots everywhere in the above inequality we obtain the result.
∎

3.
The right side of the critical strip

To begin, we consider the Northcott property on the right side of the critical strip, that is,
\mathbb{C}_{\sigma>1}
, where we obtain a result conditionally on the value of
B
. The result will follow from a comparison between
\zeta_{K}(s)
and
\zeta(\sigma)^{d_{K}}
, where
d_{K}
is the degree of the extension
K/\mathbb{Q}
.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
s=\sigma+i\tau
with
\sigma>1
. Then

\frac{1}{\zeta(\sigma)^{d_{K}}}\leq|\zeta_{K}(s)|\leq\zeta(\sigma)^{d_{K}}.

Notice that this result bounds
|\zeta_{K}(s)|
by small constants except near
\sigma=1
. In addition, since
d_{K}
can be arbitrary, the above bounds are not absolute for
s
.

Proof.

We start by proving bounds in terms of
\zeta_{K}(\sigma)
. Since
\sigma>1
, we have

(11)

|\zeta_{K}(\sigma+i\tau)|=\left|\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}I\subseteq\mathcal{O}_{K}\\
N_{K/\mathbb{Q}}(I)=n\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{n^{\sigma+i\tau}}\right|\leq\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\left(\ \sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}I\subseteq\mathcal{O}_{K}\\
N_{K/\mathbb{Q}}(I)=n\end{subarray}}1\right)\frac{1}{n^{\sigma}}=\zeta_{K}(\sigma),

and this yields an upper bound.

To get a lower bound, we take the logarithm of the Euler product (
2
) and use the fact that
1+\cos(\theta)\geq 0
for any
\theta
to get

(12)

\log\zeta_{K}(\sigma)+\re\log\zeta_{K}(\sigma+i\tau)=\sum_{P}\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\frac{1+\cos(\tau\log|N_{K/\Q}(P)^j|)}{j|N_{K/\mathbb{Q}}(P)^{j}|^{\sigma}}\geq 0.

By combining (
11
) and the exponential of (
12
), we conclude that

(13)

\frac{1}{\zeta_{K}(\sigma)}\leq|\zeta_{K}(\sigma+i\tau)|\leq\zeta_{K}(\sigma).

Finally, using the fact that
N_{K/\mathbb{Q}}(P)
is a power of the prime
p\in\mathbb{Z}
lying under the prime ideal
P\subseteq\mathcal{O}_{K}
,
we have that

\zeta_{K}(\sigma)=\prod_{P\subseteq\mathcal{O}_{K}}(1-N_{K/\mathbb{Q}}(P)^{-\sigma})^{-1}\leq\prod_{p}\left(1-p^{-\sigma}\right)^{-d_{K}}=\zeta(\sigma)^{d_{K}},

since there are at most
d_{K}
prime ideals
P
lying over each
p
. By combining with (
13
), we get the desired result.
∎

Combining the above, we arrive at the following result.

Theorem 3.2
.

Let
s=\sigma+i\tau
with
\sigma>1
.
Then the Northcott property does not hold at
s
for any
B\geq\zeta(\sigma)^{2}
.

Proof.

Fix
B\geq\zeta(\sigma)^{2}
. The upper bound in Lemma
3.1
implies that for any quadratic field
K
,

\displaystyle|\zeta_{K}(s)|=|\zeta_{K}(\sigma+i\tau)|\leq\zeta(\sigma)^{2}.

This gives an infinite family of fields with
|\zeta_{K}(s)|\leq B
and the result follows.
∎

4.
The left side of the critical strip

We now turn our attention to the left side of the critical strip, namely,
\mathbb{C}_{\sigma<0}
. In this set, Pazuki and Pengo
[
18
]
proved that the Northcott property is satisfied at the negative integers and zero. We will extend this result to show that the Northcott property is satisfied away from the negative integers. We will then see that the Northcott property is not satisfied in a neighborhood around each negative integer that excludes the integer itself.

Before proceeding to these considerations, we recall some results giving bounds to discriminants in terms of degrees, and prove some basic lemmas. We start by recalling the following statement.

Theorem 4.1

(
[
16
]
,
[
7
]
)
.

Consider

\delta(n)=\min_{d_{K}=n}|\Delta_{K}|,

that is, the minimum of the absolute values of the discriminants of all the numbers fields of fixed degree
n
over
\mathbb{Q}
. Let

D=\liminf_{n\to\infty}\delta(n)^{1/n}.

Then we have

D_{m}\leq D\leq D_{M},

where

\displaystyle D_{m}:=

\displaystyle 4\pi e^{\gamma}=22.3816\dots,

\displaystyle D_{M}:=

\displaystyle 3^{\frac{1}{8}}\cdot 7^{\frac{1}{12}}\cdot 13^{\frac{1}{12}}\cdot 19^{\frac{1}{6}}\cdot 23^{\frac{1}{3}}\cdot 29^{\frac{1}{12}}\cdot 31^{\frac{1}{12}}\cdot 35509^{\frac{1}{6}}=78.4269\dots,

and
\gamma
is the Euler–Mascheroni constant given by

\gamma=\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\left(-\log n+\sum_{k=1}^{n}\frac{1}{k}\right)=0.57721\dots.

Remark 4.2
.

The lower bound appeared as a culmination of a series of articles by Odlyzko

We begin with a simple lemma that transforms the bounds under consideration from field dependent to degree dependent. Together with Lemma
3.1
, the following statement gives bounds for each of the three factors involved in the functional equation of
\zeta_{K}(s)
.

Lemma 4.3
.

Let
s\in\mathbb{C}
and

\displaystyle\Gamma_{m}(s)

\displaystyle=\min\left\{\left|\frac{\Gamma_{\mathbb{R}}(1-s)}{\Gamma_{\mathbb{R}}(s)}\right|,\sqrt{\left|\frac{\Gamma_{\mathbb{C}}(1-s)}{\Gamma_{\mathbb{C}}(s)}\right|}\right\}.

We have the following bound

\displaystyle\Gamma_{m}(s)^{d_{K}}\leq\left|\frac{\Gamma_{\mathbb{R}}(1-s)^{r_{1}}\Gamma_{\mathbb{C}}(1-s)^{r_{2}}}{\Gamma_{\mathbb{R}}(s)^{r_{1}}\Gamma_{\mathbb{C}}(s)^{r_{2}}}\right|.

We remark that the above bound is only dependent on the degree of the extension
K/\mathbb{Q}
and the number of complex and real embeddings, but it is independent of the field itself.

Proof.

To show this, we consider two cases. Writing
\gamma_{\mathbb{R}}(s)=\left|\frac{\Gamma_{\mathbb{R}}(1-s)}{\Gamma_{\mathbb{R}}(s)}\right|
and
\gamma_{\mathbb{C}}(s)=\left|\frac{\Gamma_{\mathbb{C}}(1-s)}{\Gamma_{\mathbb{C}}(s)}\right|^{\frac{1}{2}}
, we first suppose that
\gamma_{\mathbb{R}}(s)\leq\gamma_{\mathbb{C}}(s)
so that
\Gamma_{m}(s)=\gamma_{\mathbb{R}}(s)
. Then

|\gamma_{\mathbb{R}}^{r_{1}}(s)\gamma_{\mathbb{C}}^{2r_{2}}(s)|\geq|\gamma_{\mathbb{R}}^{r_{1}}(s)\gamma_{\mathbb{R}}^{2r_{2}}(s)|=|\Gamma_{m}(s)|^{d_{K}}.

Similarly, if
\gamma_{\mathbb{C}}(s)\leq\gamma_{\mathbb{R}}(s)
, we find

|\gamma_{\mathbb{R}}^{r_{1}}(s)\gamma_{\mathbb{C}}^{2r_{2}}(s)|\geq|\gamma_{\mathbb{C}}^{r_{1}}(s)\gamma_{\mathbb{C}}^{2r_{2}}(s)|=|\Gamma_{m}(s)|^{d_{K}}.

∎

The combination of Lemmas
3.1
and
4.3
yields the following key result, which gives a sufficient condition for having the Northcott property on the left side of the critical strip.

Proposition 4.4
.

Let
s=\sigma+i\tau
with
\sigma<0
, and suppose that we have

(14)

\displaystyle\frac{\Gamma_{m}(s)}{\zeta(1-\sigma)}D_{m}^{\frac{1}{2}-\sigma}>1.

Then the Northcott property holds at
s
for any
B>0
.

Proof.

First, we notice that, by Theorem
4.1
there are only finitely many number fields such that

\displaystyle\Delta_{K}\leq(D_{m}-\varepsilon)^{d_{K}},

where
\varepsilon>0
is arbitrary.

Therefore, for all but finitely many fields
K
, we have that

(15)

\displaystyle|\zeta_{K}(s)|\geq|\zeta_{K}(1-s)|\Gamma_{m}(s)^{d_{K}}|\Delta_{K}|^{\frac{1}{2}-\sigma}\geq\frac{|\Gamma_{m}(s)|^{d_{K}}}{\zeta(1-\sigma)^{d_{K}}}(D_{m}-\varepsilon)^{d_{K}(\frac{1}{2}-\sigma)},

where we have applied Lemmas
3.1
and
4.3
. Choosing
\varepsilon
sufficiently small, we
can replace
D_{m}-\varepsilon
by
D_{m}
and obtain condition (
14
).

The statement follows from the fact that for large enough degrees, the number on the right hand side of (
15
) is arbitrarily large and that for a fixed degree, there are only finitely many fields with discriminant bounded by any constant.
∎

A natural question is to bound the size of
S_{B,s}
in the cases when it is finite. This was done by Pazuki and Pengo
[
18
]
for the case
s=n\in\mathbb{Z}_{n<0}
using the following result of Couveignes
[
4
]
.

Theorem 4.5
.

[
4
]
*Theorem 4, simplified version There exists an absolute and computable constant
\mathcal{Q}
such that the following is true. Let
K
be a number field of degree
n\geq\mathcal{Q}
and discriminant
\Delta_{K}
. Then, there are at most

(n^{n}|\Delta_{K}|)^{\mathcal{Q}(\log n)^{2}}

possibilities for
K
.

Although Couveignes does not give the value of
\mathcal{Q}
, this constant is only related to the technicalities of the proof and is independent of the field.

Using Theorem
4.5
, we can prove the following bound, which extends the result of
[
18
]
.

Theorem 4.6
.

Let
s=\sigma+i\tau
with
\sigma<0

and suppose that

(16)

\frac{\Gamma_{m}(s)}{\zeta(1-\sigma)}D_{m}^{\frac{1}{2}-\sigma}>1.

Then, we have

\#S_{B,s}\leq\exp\left(2\mathcal{Q}\left(\frac{1}{\frac{1}{2}-\sigma}+\frac{1+\log D_{m}}{a_{s}}\right)(\log B)\left(\log\left(\frac{\log B}{a_{s}}\right)\right)^{3}\right),

where

a_{s}=\log\left(\frac{\Gamma_{m}(s)D_{m}^{\frac{1}{2}-\sigma}}{\zeta(1-\sigma)}\right).

Proof.

By Theorem
4.5
, we have that

\#\{[K]\,:\,|\Delta_{K}|=x,d_{K}=d\}\leq d^{\mathcal{Q}d(\log d)^{2}}x^{\mathcal{Q}(\log d)^{2}}.

Thus, we have

\displaystyle\#\{[K]\,:\,|\Delta_{K}|\leq X,d_{K}\leq D\}\leq

\displaystyle\sum_{x=1}^{X}\sum_{d=1}^{D}d^{\mathcal{Q}d(\log d)^{2}}x^{\mathcal{Q}(\log d)^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle D^{\mathcal{Q}D(\log D)^{2}+1}X^{\mathcal{Q}(\log D)^{2}+1}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\exp\left(2\mathcal{Q}D(\log D)^{3}+2\mathcal{Q}(\log X)(\log D)^{2}\right).

Now suppose that

|\zeta_{K}(s)|\leq B.

By equation (
15
), we must have

|\zeta_{K}(1-s)|\Gamma_{m}(s)^{d_{K}}|\Delta_{K}|^{\frac{1}{2}-\sigma}\leq B.

Combining the above with (
16
), we must have

|\zeta_{K}(1-s)|\left(\frac{\zeta(1-\sigma)}{D_{m}^{\frac{1}{2}-\sigma}}\right)^{d_{K}}|\Delta_{K}|^{\frac{1}{2}-\sigma}\leq B.

Now we apply Lemma
3.1
to conclude

\frac{|\Delta_{K}|^{\frac{1}{2}-\sigma}}{D_{m}^{d_{K}(\frac{1}{2}-\sigma)}}\leq B.

Equation (
15
) also implies that

\left(\frac{|\Gamma_{m}(s)|D_{m}^{\frac{1}{2}-\sigma}}{\zeta(1-\sigma)}\right)^{d_{K}}\leq B.

Thus, we obtain,

d_{K}\leq\frac{\log B}{\log\left(\frac{\Gamma_{m}(s)D_{m}^{\frac{1}{2}-\sigma}}{\zeta(1-\sigma)}\right)}\quad\mbox{ and }\quad|\Delta_{K}|\leq B^{\frac{1}{\frac{1}{2}-\sigma}}B^{\frac{\log D_{m}}{\log\left(\frac{\Gamma_{m}(s)D_{m}^{\frac{1}{2}-\sigma}}{\zeta(1-\sigma)}\right)}}.

Setting

D=\frac{\log B}{a_{s}},\qquad X=B^{\frac{1}{\frac{1}{2}-\sigma}+\frac{\log D_{m}}{a_{s}}},\quad\mbox{ where }\quad a_{s}=\log\left(\frac{\Gamma_{m}(s)D_{m}^{\frac{1}{2}-\sigma}}{\zeta(1-\sigma)}\right),

we obtain

\displaystyle\#S_{B,s}\leq

\displaystyle\exp\left(2\mathcal{Q}\frac{\log B}{a_{s}}\left(\log\left(\frac{\log B}{a_{s}}\right)\right)^{3}+2\mathcal{Q}\left(\frac{1}{\frac{1}{2}-\sigma}+\frac{\log D_{m}}{a_{s}}\right)(\log B)\left(\log\left(\frac{\log B}{a_{s}}\right)\right)^{2}\right)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\exp\left(2\mathcal{Q}\left(\frac{1}{\frac{1}{2}-\sigma}+\frac{1+\log D_{m}}{a_{s}}\right)(\log B)\left(\log\left(\frac{\log B}{a_{s}}\right)\right)^{3}\right).

∎

4.1.
Away from the real line

The next step is to give a general idea of the values of
s
that respect the condition in Proposition
4.4
.
To do this, it is useful to search for lower bounds for
\Gamma_{m}(s)
, which follow from individual bounds for the ratios
\gamma_{\mathbb{R}}(s)=\left|\frac{\Gamma_{\mathbb{R}}(1-s)}{\Gamma_{\mathbb{R}}(s)}\right|
and
\gamma_{\mathbb{C}}(s)=\left|\frac{\Gamma_{\mathbb{C}}(1-s)}{\Gamma_{\mathbb{C}}(s)}\right|^{\frac{1}{2}}
.

Lemma 4.7
.

Let
s=\sigma+i\tau\in\mathbb{C}\setminus\mathbb{Z}
with
\sigma<0
. Then

\displaystyle\gamma_{\mathbb{C}}(s)

\displaystyle\geq\frac{(2\pi)^{\sigma-\frac{1}{2}}}{\sqrt{\pi}}\Gamma(1-\sigma)\tanh(\pi\tau)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

and

\displaystyle\gamma_{\mathbb{R}}(s)

\displaystyle\geq\sqrt{2}\frac{(2\pi)^{\sigma-\frac{1}{2}}}{\sqrt{\pi}}\Gamma\left(1-\sigma\right)\left|\tanh\left(\frac{\pi\tau}{2}\right)\right|.

Proof.

First consider the bound for
\Gamma_{\mathbb{C}}
. By applying Euler’s reflection formula (
4
) as well as inequalities (
6
) and (
10
), we obtain

\displaystyle\gamma_{\mathbb{C}}(s)^{2}

\displaystyle=\left|\frac{2(2\pi)^{s-1}\Gamma(1-s)}{2(2\pi)^{-s}\Gamma(s)}\right|

(17)

\displaystyle=(2\pi)^{2\sigma-1}|\Gamma(1-s)|^{2}\frac{|\sin(\pi s)|}{\pi}

(18)

\displaystyle\geq\frac{(2\pi)^{2\sigma-1}}{\pi}\Gamma(1-\sigma)^{2}\left|\frac{\sin(\pi s)}{\cosh(\pi\tau)}\right|

\displaystyle\geq\frac{(2\pi)^{2\sigma-1}}{\pi}\Gamma(1-\sigma)^{2}|\tanh(\pi\tau)|.

Now consider the bound for
\Gamma_{\mathbb{R}}
. Again we apply Euler’s reflection formula (
4
) as well as inequalities (
6
) and (
10
), together with Lagrange’s duplication formula (
5
).

\displaystyle\gamma_{\mathbb{R}}(s)

\displaystyle=\pi^{\sigma-\frac{1}{2}}\left|\frac{\Gamma\left(\frac{1-s}{2}\right)}{\Gamma\left(\frac{s}{2}\right)}\right|

(19)

\displaystyle=\pi^{\sigma-\frac{1}{2}}\left|\Gamma\left(\frac{1-s}{2}\right)\Gamma\left(1-\frac{s}{2}\right)\right|\frac{\left|\sin(\frac{\pi s}{2})\right|}{\pi}

\displaystyle\geq\pi^{\sigma-\frac{1}{2}}\frac{\Gamma\left(\frac{1-\sigma}{2}\right)}{\left|\cosh\left(\frac{\pi\tau}{2}\right)\right|^{\frac{1}{2}}}\frac{\Gamma\left(1-\frac{\sigma}{2}\right)}{\left|\cosh\left(\frac{\pi\tau}{2}\right)\right|^{\frac{1}{2}}}\frac{\left|\sin(\frac{\pi s}{2})\right|}{\pi}

(20)

\displaystyle{\geq\frac{\pi^{\sigma-\frac{1}{2}}}{\sqrt{\pi}}2^{\sigma}\Gamma\left(1-\sigma\right)\left|\frac{\sin\left(\frac{\pi s}{2}\right)}{\cosh\left(\frac{\pi\tau}{2}\right)}\right|}

\displaystyle\geq\sqrt{2}\frac{(2\pi)^{\sigma-\frac{1}{2}}}{\sqrt{\pi}}\Gamma\left(1-\sigma\right)\left|\tanh\left(\frac{\pi\tau}{2}\right)\right|.

∎

The previous results allow us to give a large region to the left of the critical strip where the Northcott property is satisfied.

Theorem 4.8
.

Let
s=\sigma+i\tau
such that

\sigma<\sigma_{0}:=-1.5\quad\mbox{ and }\quad\tau>\tau_{0}:=\frac{2}{\pi}\tanh^{-1}\left(\frac{\zeta\left(\frac{5}{2}\right)}{3\sqrt{2}e^{2\gamma}}\right)=0.063666\dots.

Then the Northcott property holds at
s
for any
B>0
.

Proof.

In fact, for such values of
\sigma,\tau
we have that

(21)

\displaystyle\frac{\sqrt{2}\Gamma(1-\sigma)\tanh(\frac{\pi\tau}{2})}{\sqrt{\pi}\zeta(1-\sigma)}\left(\frac{D_{m}}{2\pi}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}-\sigma}>1\quad\mbox{ and }\quad\frac{\Gamma(1-\sigma)\tanh(\pi\tau)^{\frac{1}{2}}}{\sqrt{\pi}\zeta(1-\sigma)}\left(\frac{D_{m}}{2\pi}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}-\sigma}>1.

Remark that the functions
\Gamma(1-\sigma)
,
\left(\frac{D_{m}}{2\pi}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}-\sigma}=(2e^{\gamma})^{\frac{1}{2}-\sigma}
, and
\frac{1}{\zeta(1-\sigma)}
all increase as
\sigma
decreases in the negative part of the real axis. Now
\tanh(\frac{\pi\tau}{2})
and
\tanh(\pi\tau)^{\frac{1}{2}}
are increasing functions of
\tau
. Thus, the worse possible case is with
\sigma=\sigma_{0}
and
\tau=\tau_{0}
. Fixing
\sigma=-1.5
, we evaluate in
\sigma_{0}
and choose
\tau_{0}
accordingly so that the inequalities in (
21
) are satisfied.

We then combine the inequalities (
21
) with Proposition
4.4
and Lemma
4.7
to conclude.

∎

Lemma 4.9

Let

\displaystyle\gamma_{\mathbb{C}}(s)

\displaystyle\geq\frac{(2\pi)^{\sigma-\frac{1}{2}}}{\sqrt{\pi}}\Gamma(1-\sigma)\left|\frac{\sin(\pi r)}{\cosh(\pi\tau_0)}\right|^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\gamma_{\mathbb{R}}(s)

\displaystyle\geq\frac{\sqrt{2}}{{\sqrt{\pi}}}(2\pi)^{\sigma-\frac{1}{2}}\Gamma\left(1-\sigma\right)\left|\frac{\sin\left(\frac{\pi r}{2}\right)}{\cosh\left(\frac{\pi\tau_{0}}{2}\right)}\right|.

Remark 4.10
.

Because we have a precise formula for

\cosh\left(\frac{\pi\tau_{0}}{2}\right)=\frac{3\sqrt{2}e^{2\gamma}}{\left(18e^{4\gamma}-\zeta\left(\frac{5}{2}\right)^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}}.

First we consider
\Gamma_{\mathbb{C}}

\displaystyle\gamma_{\mathbb{C}}(s)

\displaystyle\geq\frac{(2\pi)^{\sigma-\frac{1}{2}}}{\sqrt{\pi}}\Gamma(1-\sigma)\left|\frac{\sin(\pi r)}{\cosh(\pi\tau_0)}\right|^{\frac{1}{2}}.

Now we consider
\Gamma_{\mathbb{R}}

\displaystyle\gamma_{\mathbb{R}}(s)

4.3.
The neighborhood of the negative integers

The goal of this section is to prove non-Northcott near odd negative integers. To do this, we use the constant
D_{M}=3^{\frac{1}{8}}\cdot 7^{\frac{1}{12}}\cdot 13^{\frac{1}{12}}\cdot 19^{\frac{1}{6}}\cdot 23^{\frac{1}{3}}\cdot 29^{\frac{1}{12}}\cdot 31^{\frac{1}{12}}\cdot 35509^{\frac{1}{6}}=78.4269\dots
given by Hajir, Maire, and Ramakrishna in
[

The following result is a natural complement to Proposition

Proposition 4.18
.

Let
s=\sigma+i\tau
with
\sigma<0
and suppose that we have

(28)

\displaystyle\gamma_{\mathbb{C}}(s)\zeta(1-\sigma)D_{M}^{\frac{1}{2}-\sigma}<1.

Then the Northcott property does not hold at

for any

Proof.

Using the sequence of totally complex fields

satisfying (

), and fixing an arbitrary

, we notice that there are infinitely many
K_{\ell}
such that

\displaystyle|\zeta_{K_{\ell}}(s)|

\displaystyle=|\zeta_{K_{\ell}}(1-s)|\left|\frac{\Gamma_{\mathbb{R}}(1-s)^{r_{1}}\Gamma_{\mathbb{C}}(1-s)^{r_{2}}}{\Gamma_{\mathbb{R}}(s)^{r_{1}}\Gamma_{\mathbb{C}}(s)^{r_{2}}}\right|\left|\Delta_{K_{\ell}}^{\frac{1}{2}-s}\right|

(29)

\displaystyle\leq\gamma_{\mathbb{C}}(s)^{d_{K_{\ell}}}\zeta(1-\sigma)^{d_{K_{\ell}}}(D_{M}+\varepsilon)^{d_{K_{\ell}}(\frac{1}{2}-\sigma)}

Here we have used Lemma
3.1
together with the fact that the fields
K_{\ell}
are totally complex, and all but finitely many must have root discriminant less than
D_{M}+\varepsilon

Now, by setting

we can write (

\displaystyle|\zeta_{K_{\ell}}(s)|<f_{\varepsilon}(s)^{d_{K_{\ell}}}.

When inequality (

. Therefore we have

, and we find that for every

such that

\displaystyle|\zeta_{K_{\ell}}(s)|<f(s)^{d_{K_{\ell}}}<B

\displaystyle\text{ for all }d_{K_{\ell}}>d_{B}.

∎

Since
\Gamma_{\mathbb{C}}(s)
has poles on the negative integers, the ratio
\frac{\Gamma_{\mathbb{C}}(1-s)}{\Gamma_{\mathbb{C}}(s)}
vanishes on them and we expect the condition of Proposition
4.18
to hold in small discs around them.
We will use similar ideas to those introduced in Section

Consider

Let

be such that it verifies
-n-\frac{1}{2}\leq\sigma\leq-n+\frac{1}{2}

\displaystyle r<\frac{1}{\pi}\sinh^{-1}\left(\frac{\pi}{\Gamma\left(n+\frac{3}{2}\right)^{2}\zeta\left(n+\frac{1}{2}\right)^{2}}\left(\frac{2\pi}{D_{M}}\right)^{2n+2}\right),

s
for any

.

Proof.

Starting from (

and (

\displaystyle\gamma_{\mathbb{C}}(s)\zeta(1-\sigma)D_{M}^{\frac{1}{2}-\sigma}

\displaystyle=|\Gamma(1-s)|\left(\frac{|\sin(\pi s)|}{\pi}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\zeta(1-\sigma)\left(\frac{D_{M}}{2\pi}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}-\sigma}

We can provide an effective result of this statement by slightly modifying the proof of Lemma

Lemma 4.20
.

Let
s=\sigma+i\tau
with
\sigma>1
. Then we have

\displaystyle|\Gamma(\sigma+i\tau)|^{2}\geq|\Gamma(\sigma)|^{2}\left|\frac{\pi\tau}{\sin(\pi i\tau)}\right|.

Proof.

By applying Euler’s infinite product (
7

is strictly increasing for

\left|\frac{\Gamma(\sigma+i\tau)}{\Gamma(\sigma)}\right|^{2}=\prod_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{\left(k+\sigma\right)^{2}}{(k+\sigma)^{2}+\tau^{2}}\geq\prod_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{(k+1)^{2}}{(k+1)^{2}+\tau^{2}}=\prod_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{1+\frac{\tau^{2}}{k^{2}}}=\left|\frac{\pi i\tau}{\sin(\pi i\tau)}\right|.

∎

Lemma 4.21
.

Let
s=\sigma+i\tau
such that
\sigma<0
. Then we have

(31)

\displaystyle\gamma_{\mathbb{C}}(s)^{2}

\displaystyle\geq(2\pi)^{2\sigma-1}\Gamma(1-\sigma)^{2}|\tau|

(32)

\displaystyle\gamma_{\mathbb{R}}(s)^{2}

\displaystyle\geq(2\pi)^{2\sigma-1}\Gamma(1-\sigma)^{2}|\tau|\left|\tanh\left(\frac{\pi\tau}{2}\right)\right|.

Proof.

Equation (
31
) follows immediately from equations (
17
), (
10
), and Lemma
4.20
.

Equation (
32
) follows similarly from (
19
) and the duplication formula (
5
) since

\displaystyle\gamma_{\mathbb{R}}(s)=

\displaystyle\pi^{\sigma-\frac{1}{2}}\left|\Gamma\left(\frac{1-s}{2}\right)\Gamma\left(1-\frac{s}{2}\right)\right|\frac{\left|\sin(\frac{\pi s}{2})\right|}{\pi}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\pi^{\sigma-1}\left|2^{s}\Gamma\left(1-s\right)\right|\left|\sin(\frac{\pi s}{2})\right|

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\pi^{\sigma-\frac{1}{2}}2^{\sigma}\left|\Gamma\left(1-\sigma\right)\right|\frac{\left|\sin(\frac{\pi s}{2})\right|}{\left|\sinh(\pi\tau)\right|^{\frac{1}{2}}}|\tau|^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\pi^{\sigma-\frac{1}{2}}2^{\sigma}\left|\Gamma\left(1-\sigma\right)\right|\frac{\left|\sinh(\frac{\pi\tau}{2})\right|}{\left|\sinh(\pi\tau)\right|^{\frac{1}{2}}}|\tau|^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(2\pi)^{\sigma-\frac{1}{2}}\left|\Gamma\left(1-\sigma\right)\right|\left|\tanh\left(\frac{\pi\tau}{2}\right)\right|^{\frac{1}{2}}|\tau|^{\frac{1}{2}},

Where we have used inequality (

2\frac{\sinh(\frac{\pi\tau}{2})^{2}}{|\sinh(\pi\tau)|}=\left|\tanh(\frac{\pi\tau}{2})\right|
.
∎

Combining the result above with inequality (

), we have the following result.

Theorem 4.22
.

Let
s=\sigma+i\tau
with
\sigma<0
and

|\tau|>\frac{1}{\tanh\left(\frac{\pi}{2}\left(\frac{D_{m}}{2\pi}\right)^{2\sigma-1}\frac{\zeta(1-\sigma)^{2}}{\Gamma(1-\sigma)^{2}}\right)}\left(\frac{D_{m}}{2\pi}\right)^{2\sigma-1}\frac{\zeta(1-\sigma)^{2}}{\Gamma(1-\sigma)^{2}}.

Then, the Northcott property holds at
s
for any
B>0
.

Proof.

It suffices to check inequality (

), and therefore, by Lemma
4.21
, it suffices to check that

|\tau|\min\left\{1,\left|\tanh\left(\frac{\pi\tau}{2}\right)\right|\right\}>\left(\frac{D_{m}}{2\pi}\right)^{2\sigma-1}\frac{\zeta(1-\sigma)^{2}}{\Gamma(1-\sigma)^{2}}.

But this follows from the fact that

|\tau|>\frac{1}{\tanh\left(\frac{\pi}{2}\left(\frac{D_{m}}{2\pi}\right)^{2\sigma-1}\frac{\zeta(1-\sigma)^{2}}{\Gamma(1-\sigma)^{2}}\right)}\left(\frac{D_{m}}{2\pi}\right)^{2\sigma-1}\frac{\zeta(1-\sigma)^{2}}{\Gamma(1-\sigma)^{2}}>\left(\frac{D_{m}}{2\pi}\right)^{2\sigma-1}\frac{\zeta(1-\sigma)^{2}}{\Gamma(1-\sigma)^{2}},

and therefore

\left|\tanh\left(\frac{\pi\tau}{2}\right)\right|\geq\tanh\left(\frac{\pi}{2}\left(\frac{D_{m}}{2\pi}\right)^{2\sigma-1}\frac{\zeta(1-\sigma)^{2}}{\Gamma(1-\sigma)^{2}}\right).

The left side neighborhood of zero

Using numerical methods, we can obtain substantially more precise results that are closer to the boundary given by Proposition

but require a large number of steps, and ultimately rely on the help of a computer.

The strategy in this section is the following. We will numerically construct an approximation of a curve that is close to the boundary of the red region in Figure

, such that we will be able to guarantee that the Northcott property is true for
s=\sigma+i\tau
with

such that
\sigma+it
is on the curve. This curve approximation will be made of small horizontal segments (see Figure

In order to construct this approximation, we consider an interval around
n

The first result will allow us to understand the growth of
|\Gamma(s)|
as
s
moves in a horizontal line, outside a circle of center
\frac{1}{2}

Lemma 5.1
.

Let
s=\sigma+i\tau\in\{z\in\mathbb{C}_{\sigma<0}:|z-0.5|\geq 1.1\}
. We have

\displaystyle\frac{d}{d\sigma}|\Gamma(1-s)|^{2}\leq 0.

Remark 5.2
.

The constant 1.1 has been numerically chosen by numerically adjusting a circle centered at 0.5 so that it satisfies the following conditions.

•

The circle encapsulates the region of
\mathbb{C}_{\sigma<0}
where
\frac{d}{d\sigma}|\Gamma(1-s)|^{2}\geq 0
.

•

The boundary of the red region of Figure
6
entirely lies outside this circle (see Figure
7
for more detail).

Figure 7.
Depiction of the boundary of condition (
14
) and the circle of center
0.5
and radius
1.1
employed in Lemma
5.1

Proof.

After replacing
1-s
by
s
, the statement to be proven is equivalent to
\frac{d}{d\sigma}|\Gamma(\sigma+i\tau)|^{2}\geq 0
for
s=\sigma+i\tau\in\mathcal{C}:=\{z\in\mathbb{C}_{\sigma>1}:|z-0.5|\geq 1.1\}
. We have

\frac{d}{ds}\Gamma(s)\overline{\Gamma(s)}=(\psi(s)+\overline{\psi(s)})|\Gamma(s)|^{2},

where
\psi
denotes the digamma function (
8
). Therefore,
the desired derivative is given by

\displaystyle\frac{d}{d\sigma}|\Gamma(\sigma+i\tau)|^{2}=2\real(\psi(\sigma+i\tau))|\Gamma(\sigma+i\tau)|^{2}.

Our goal is to show that
\real(\psi(\sigma+i\tau))
is positive outside the circle
\mathcal{C}
. We consider the series (
9
) and truncate it to the first four terms:

(33)

\displaystyle\real(\psi(s))=-\gamma+\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{k(\sigma-1)+(\sigma-1)^{2}+\tau^{2}}{k((k+\sigma-1)^{2}+\tau^{2})}\geq-\gamma+\sum_{k=1}^{4}\frac{k(\sigma-1)+(\sigma-1)^{2}+\tau^{2}}{k((k+\sigma-1)^{2}+\tau^{2})},

where we have chosen to keep four terms because numerical estimates suggest this level of truncation yields the needed precision.

Next, since we want to prove that the right-hand side of (
33
) is positive outside the circle
{\mathcal{C}}
, we consider polar coordinates centered at
0.5
. After the change of variables
s=0.5+re^{ix}
, we now have

(34)

\displaystyle-\gamma+\sum_{k=1}^{4}\frac{4r^{2}+4(k-1)r\cos(x)-2k+1}{4kr^{2}+4(2k^{2}-k)r\cos(x)+4k^{3}-4k^{2}+k}.

The derivative with respect to
r
of each term is

\frac{4\left(4k(k-1)\cos\left(x\right)+(4r^{2}+1)\cos\left(x\right)+4r(2k-1)\right)}{\left(4r^{2}+4(2k-1)r\cos\left(x\right)+4k^{2}-4k+1\right)^{2}}.

We see that the term above is always positive for
x\in[-\pi/2,\pi/2]
. Thus, if expression (
34
) is positive on this half circle of fixed radius, the result will follow. To see this, we compute the derivative of (
34
) with respect to
x
and remark that it can be expressed as
\sin(x)R(\cos(x))
, where
R(t)
is a rational function with positive coefficients, and therefore
R(\cos(x))\geq 0
for

x\in[-\pi/2,\pi/2]
. From this we can deduce that for fixed
r
, equation (
34
) reaches its minimum when
x=0
. Now we evaluate at
r=1.1
and

to obtain

\displaystyle-\gamma+\sum_{k=1}^{4}\frac{4(1.1)^{2}+4(k-1)(1.1)-2k+1}{4k(1.1)^{2}+4k^{3}+4(2k^{2}-k)(1.1)-4k^{2}+k}=0.00133\dots.

Thus, we conclude that
\re(\psi(s))\geq 0
outside
\mathcal{C}
, and therefore the same is true for
\frac{d}{ds}|\Gamma(s)|^{2}
.
∎

The next result allows us to understand the growth of
\gamma_{\mathbb{C}}(s)
and

as

moves along a vertical line.

Lemma 5.3
.

Let
\sigma\leq 1/2
be fixed. Then

\displaystyle\frac{d}{d\tau}\gamma_{\mathbb{C}}(s)^{4}\geq 0\text{ and }\frac{d}{d\tau}\gamma_{\mathbb{R}}(s)^{2}\geq 0

for
\tau\in[0,\infty)
.

Proof.

Starting with the case of
\gamma_{\mathbb{C}}
, it suffices to check the sign of the derivative. Thus, we can ignore the positive constants and consider

\displaystyle\frac{d}{d\tau}\frac{|\Gamma(1-s)|^{2}}{|\Gamma(s)|^{2}}.

The above expression has the same sign as

\displaystyle\left(\frac{d}{d\tau}\Gamma(1-s)\overline{\Gamma(1-s)}\right)|\Gamma(s)|^{2}-\left(\frac{d}{d\tau}\Gamma(s)\overline{\Gamma(s)}\right)|\Gamma(1-s)|^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle|\Gamma(1-s)|^{2}\left(-i\psi(1-s)+i\overline{\psi(1-s)}\right)|\Gamma(s)|^{2}-|\Gamma(s)|^{2}\left(i\psi(s)-i\overline{\psi(s)}\right)|\Gamma(1-s)|^{2}.

Ignoring positive terms once again, we are left to consider

\displaystyle\left(-i\psi(1-s)+i\overline{\psi(1-s)}\right)-\left(i\psi(s)-i\overline{\psi(s)}\right),

which is the same as

\displaystyle\im(\psi(s)+\psi(1-s)).

Now, using (

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{\tau}{(k+\sigma-1)^{2}+\tau^{2}}-\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{\tau}{(k-\sigma)^{2}+\tau^{2}}\geq 0.

We can show this by ignoring the sums and comparing the terms for each

\displaystyle\frac{\tau}{(k+\sigma-1)^{2}+\tau^{2}}\geq\frac{\tau}{(k-\sigma)^{2}+\tau^{2}}\text{ when }\sigma\leq 1/2\text{ and }\tau\geq 0.

The case of
\gamma_{\mathbb{R}}
can be proven similarly.

(4)

Suppose
[\alpha,\beta]\subseteq[2n-1,2n]
, then

\displaystyle\frac{\sqrt{2}}{\sqrt{\pi}}\left(\frac{D_{m}}{2\pi}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}-\beta}\frac{|\Gamma(1-\beta-i\tau)|}{\zeta(1-\beta)}\left|\sin(\frac{\pi}{2} (\beta+i\tau))\right|>1\iff\frac{\gamma_{\mathbb{R}}(s)}{\zeta(1-\sigma)}D_{m}^{\frac{1}{2}-\sigma}>1.

Proof.

The proof follows by studying the growth of the terms in condition (
14
).

The functions under consideration are (up to a positive constant) the following.

(1)

The expression

\displaystyle\left(\frac{D_{m}}{2\pi}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}-\sigma}\frac{|\Gamma(1-\sigma-i\tau)|}{\zeta(1-\sigma)}

is monotonously decreasing as a function of
\sigma
when
s\in\{z\in\mathbb{C}_{\sigma<0}:|z-0.5|\geq 1.1\}
by Lemma
5.1
.

(2)

The function
|\sin(\pi\sigma)|
is increasing in
[n,n+\frac{1}{2}]
and decreasing in
[n-\frac{1}{2},n]
for
n\in\mathbb{Z}
. For fixed
\tau
this function has the same increasing/decreasing intervals as
|\sin(\pi(\sigma+i\tau))|^{\frac{1}{2}}
. This follows from the fact that
|\sin(\pi(\sigma+i\tau))|^{\frac{1}{2}}=\sqrt[4]{\sin(\pi\sigma)^{2}+\sinh(\pi\tau)^{2}}
and
\sinh(\pi\tau)^{2}
is constant when
\tau
is fixed.

(3)

Similarly,
\left|\sin(\frac{\pi}{2}(\sigma+i \tau))\right|
is increasing in
[2n,2n+1]
and decreasing in
[2n-1,2n]
for
n\in\mathbb{Z}
.

We get the result by combining the above facts together and by taking the minimum of each component in the interval
[\alpha,\beta]
.
∎

6.1.
The case of
s=1

This case was established in
[
18
]
by using the asymptotics for the moment of the class numbers
h_{\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{D})}
, which are directly related to
\zeta^{*}_{\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{D})}(1)
by the class number formula.

Another strategy is to employ the following result of Chowla.

Theorem 6.1
.

[
2
, Theorem 2]
For any
x
sufficiently large, there is a
x<d<2x
such that there is a real primitive character
\chi_{d}
modulo
d
satisfying

L(1,\chi_{d})\leq(1+o(1))\frac{\zeta(2)}{e^{\gamma}\log(\log d)},

where
\gamma
denotes the Euler–Mascheroni constant.

We then have the following result.

Theorem 6.2
.

s=1
does not satisfy the Northcott property for any
B>0
.

Proof.

Recall that for a quadratic field
K
with corresponding quadratic character
\chi
we have

\zeta_{K}^{*}(1)=\lim_{s\rightarrow 1}(s-1)\zeta_{K}(s)=\lim_{s\rightarrow 1}(s-1)\zeta(s)L(1,\chi)=L(1,\chi).

Given
B>0
, choose
x
such that

(1+o(1))\frac{\zeta(2)}{e^{\gamma}\log(\log x )}\leq B.

By choosing
x
progressively larger, we can construct an infinite sequence of
D_{k}
such that

|\zeta_{\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{D_{k}})}^{*}(1)|\leq B,

and this process can be applied to any
B
arbitrary. Therefore, the Northcott property is not satisfied in this case.

∎

6.2.
The case of
1/2<\sigma<1

Let
\chi_{p}=\left(\frac{\cdot}{p}\right)
denote the Legendre symbol modulo a prime
p
. We have the following result of Lamzouri.

Theorem 6.3
.

[
9
, Theorem 1.8, partial statement]
Assume the Generalized Riemann Hypothesis. Let
s=\sigma+i\tau
where
1/2<\sigma<1
and
\tau\in\mathbb{R}
. Let
x
be large. Then there are
\gg x^{\frac{1}{2}}
primes
p\leq x
such that

\log|L(s,\chi_{p})|\leq-(\beta(s)+o(1))\frac{(\log x)^{1-\sigma}}{(\log(\log x))^{\sigma}},

where

\beta(\sigma)=\frac{(2\log 2)^{\sigma-1}}{1-\sigma},

and for
\tau\not=0
,

\beta(s)=\frac{\beta(\sigma)\tau^{2}}{(1-\sigma)^{2}+4\tau^{2}}.

With this result we can prove the following.

Theorem 6.4
.

Assume the Generalized Riemann Hypothesis.
Let
s=\sigma+i\tau
with
1/2<\sigma<1
, then
s
does not satisfy the Northcott property for any
B>0
.

Proof.

Let
B>0
and
s
fixed with
1/2<\sigma<1
. Choose
x
such that

|\zeta(s)|\exp\left(-(\beta(s)+o(1))\frac{(\log x)^{1-\sigma}}{(\log(\log x))^{\sigma}}\right)\leq B.

Thus, there are
\gg x^{\frac{1}{2}}
primes
p\leq x
such that

|\zeta_{\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{p})}(s)|\leq B.

By choosing
x
progressively larger, we can construct an infinite sequence of primes
p_{k}
such that

|\zeta_{\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{p_{k}})}(s)|\leq B,

and this process can be applied to any
B
arbitrary. Therefore, the Northcott property is not satisfied in this case.
∎

We can also give an unconditional partial result. For this we need the following statement of Sono.

Theorem 6.5
.

[
21
, Theorem 2.2, simplified version]

Let
\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2}\in\mathbb{C}
such that
|\re(\alpha_{j})|<1/2
. Let
\Phi:\mathbb{R}_{>0}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
be a smooth function supported in
[1/2,3]
and, for sufficiently large
X>0
, set

F(x)=\Phi\left(\frac{x}{X}\right).

Let
\chi_{8d}=\left(\frac{8d}{\cdot}\right)
, where
d
is square-free and odd (
8d
is a fundamental discriminant). Then, for any
\varepsilon>0
,

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}d\,\square-\text{free}\\
\text{odd}\end{subarray}}L\left(\textstyle{\frac{1}{2}}+\alpha_{1},\chi_{8d}\right)L\left(\textstyle{\frac{1}{2}}+\alpha_{2},\chi_{8d}\right)F(d)=\sum_{\varepsilon_{1},\varepsilon_{2}\in\{\pm 1\}}A_{\varepsilon_{1}\alpha_{1},\varepsilon_{2}\alpha_{2}}\Gamma_{\alpha_{1}}^{\delta_{1}}\Gamma_{\alpha_{2}}^{\delta_{2}}\frac{2\tilde{F}(1-\delta_{1}\alpha_{1}-\delta_{2}\alpha_{2})}{3\zeta(2)}+O_{\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2}}\left(X^{\frac{1}{2}+\varepsilon}\right),

where
\tilde{F}(w)=\int_{0}^{\infty}F(x)x^{w-1}dx
is the Mellin transform of
F
,

A_{\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2}}=\sum_{(n,2)=1}\frac{\sigma_{\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2}}(n^{2})}{n}\prod_{p\mid n}(1+p^{-1})^{-1},

and

\sigma_{\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2}}(n)=\sum_{n_{1}n_{2}=n}\frac{1}{n_{1}^{\alpha_{1}}n_{2}^{\alpha_{2}}}.

We also have

\delta_{i}=\begin{cases}0&\varepsilon_{i}=1,\\
1&\varepsilon_{i}=-1.\end{cases}

Finally,

\Gamma_{\alpha}=\left(\frac{8}{\pi}\right)^{-\alpha}\frac{\Gamma\left(\frac{1-2\alpha}{4}\right)}{\Gamma\left(\frac{1+2\alpha}{4}\right)}.

The statement of Theorem
6.5
is deduced from the recipe of Conrey, Farmer, Keating, Rubinstein, and Snaith
[
3
]
and rigorously proven as an intermediate step in the estimate for the second moment of quadratic Dirichlet
L
-functions at
s=1/2
with a square-root savings in the error term. The original ideas for the proof are due to Young, who developed this method to obtain similar results for the first and third moments in
[
24
,
25
]
.
In its original statement, Theorem
6.5
has the condition that the
\alpha_{j}
be in the rectangle
|\re(s)|\leq\frac{\varepsilon}{\log X}
,
|\im(s)|\leq X^{\varepsilon}
. However, as explained by Young in
[
24
]
, this condition is imposed to claim uniformity in terms of
\alpha
. In our case, since the
\alpha_{j}
will be fixed, we do not need to impose this condition, but we still need
|\re(\alpha_{j})|<1/2
for all the terms
A_{\varepsilon_{1}\alpha_{1},\varepsilon_{2}\alpha_{2}}
to converge.

We choose a concrete
\Phi(x)
in the next statement to make the result explicit.

Theorem 6.6
.

Let
s\in\mathbb{C}
such that
1/2<\sigma<1
be fixed. Set

\Phi(x)=\begin{cases}\exp\left(-\frac{1}{(2x-1)(3-x)}\right)&\frac{1}{2}<x<3,\\
0&\text{otherwise},\end{cases}\mbox{ and }I=\int_{0}^{\infty}\Phi(y)dy.

Then
s
does not satisfy the Northcott property for any

(37)

B>|\zeta\left(\textstyle{s}\right)|\left(\exp\left(\frac{1}{2}\right)A_{s-\frac{1}{2},\overline{s}-\frac{1}{2}}\frac{8I}{9}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

Proof.

Let
\alpha=s-\frac{1}{2}
. Then
0<\re(\alpha)<1/2
, and we set
\alpha_{1}=\overline{\alpha_{2}}=\alpha
in Theorem
6.5
. We have

\tilde{F}(1-\delta_{1}\alpha-\delta_{2}\overline{\alpha})=\int_{0}^{\infty}\Phi\left(\frac{x}{X}\right)x^{-\delta_{1}\alpha-\delta_{2}\overline{\alpha}}dx=X^{1-\delta_{1}\alpha-\delta_{2}\overline{\alpha}}\int_{0}^{\infty}\Phi\left(y\right)y^{-\delta_{1}\alpha-\delta_{2}\overline{\alpha}}dy.

Remark that the dominant term in the formula for the mixed moment given in Theorem
6.5
occurs with
\delta_{i}=0
. This gives

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}d\,\square-\text{free}\\
\text{odd}\end{subarray}}\left|L\left(\textstyle{\frac{1}{2}}+\alpha,\chi_{8d}\right)\right|^{2}F(d)=A_{\alpha,\overline{\alpha}}\frac{2I}{3\zeta(2)}X+O\left(X^{1-\re(\alpha)}+X^{\frac{1}{2}+\varepsilon}\right).

Then we get

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}d\,\square-\text{free}\\
\text{odd}\\
X\leq d\leq\frac{5}{2}X\end{subarray}}\left|L\left(\textstyle{\frac{1}{2}}+\alpha,\chi_{8d}\right)\right|^{2}\leq\exp\left(\frac{1}{2}\right)A_{\alpha,\overline{\alpha}}\frac{2I}{3\zeta(2)}X+O\left(X^{1-\re(\alpha)}+X^{\frac{1}{2}+\varepsilon}\right).

The number of
d
square-free, odd and such that
X\leq d\leq\frac{5}{2}X
is
\sim\frac{3X}{4\zeta(2)}
(see
[
11
, Ex. 3.2.1.6]
).

Thus, given
\varepsilon>0
, we can guarantee that for
X
large enough there is a
d
such that
X\leq d\leq\frac{5}{2}X
and for which

\left|L\left(\textstyle{\frac{1}{2}}+\alpha,\chi_{8d}\right)\right|\leq\left(\exp\left(\frac{1}{2}\right)A_{\alpha,\overline{\alpha}}\frac{8I}{9}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}+\varepsilon.

Taking
\varepsilon
arbitrarily small we can construct an infinite sequence of
d
’s satisfying this property, and leading to bounded
\left|L\left(\textstyle{\frac{1}{2}}+\alpha,\chi_{8d}\right)\right|
. The conclusion follows by writing

\zeta_{K}\left(\textstyle{\frac{1}{2}}+\alpha,\chi_{8d}\right)=\zeta\left(\textstyle{\frac{1}{2}}+\alpha,\chi_{8d}\right)L\left(\textstyle{\frac{1}{2}}+\alpha,\chi_{8d}\right).

∎

Remark 6.7
.

Notice that

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\left(\left(1-\frac{1}{p^{\frac{1}{2}+\alpha}}\right)^{-1}\left(1-\frac{1}{p^{\frac{1}{2}+\overline{\alpha}}}\right)^{-1}+\left(1+\frac{1}{p^{\frac{1}{2}+\alpha}}\right)^{-1}\left(1+\frac{1}{p^{\frac{1}{2}+\overline{\alpha}}}\right)^{-1}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\left(\sum_{j_{1}=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{p^{j_{1}(\frac{1}{2}+\alpha)}}\sum_{j_{2}=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{p^{j_{2}(\frac{1}{2}+\overline{\alpha})}}+\sum_{j_{1}=0}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^{j_{1}}}{p^{j_{1}(\frac{1}{2}+\alpha)}}\sum_{j_{2}=0}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^{j_{2}}}{p^{j_{2}(\frac{1}{2}+\overline{\alpha})}}\right)

\displaystyle=1+\frac{1}{2}\left(\sum_{\ell=1}^{\infty}\frac{\sigma_{\alpha,\overline{\alpha}}(p^{\ell})}{p^{\frac{\ell}{2}}}+\sum_{\ell=1}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^{\ell}\sigma_{\alpha,\overline{\alpha}}(p^{\ell})}{p^{\frac{\ell}{2}}}\right).

Thus,
A_{\alpha,\overline{\alpha}}
can be expressed as

\displaystyle A_{\alpha,\overline{\alpha}}=

\displaystyle\prod_{p\not=2}\left(1+\left(1+\frac{1}{p}\right)^{-1}\sum_{\ell=1}^{\infty}\frac{\sigma_{\alpha,\overline{\alpha}}(p^{2\ell})}{p^{\ell}}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\prod_{p\not=2}\left[\frac{1}{2}\left(\left(1-\frac{1}{p^{\frac{1}{2}+\alpha}}\right)^{-1}\left(1-\frac{1}{p^{\frac{1}{2}+\overline{\alpha}}}\right)^{-1}+\left(1+\frac{1}{p^{\frac{1}{2}+\alpha}}\right)^{-1}\left(1+\frac{1}{p^{\frac{1}{2}+\overline{\alpha}}}\right)^{-1}\right)+\frac{1}{p}\right]

\displaystyle\times\left(1+\frac{1}{p}\right)^{-1}.

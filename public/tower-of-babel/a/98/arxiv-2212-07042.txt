Massive to Massless by Applying a Nonlocal Field Redefinition
Mojtaba Najafizadeh

Abstract
The invariance of physical observables like particle’s mass under a local field redefinition is a well-known and important property of quantum field theory. In this paper, on the other hand, we investigate nonlocal field redefinitions eliminating a physical observable from a theory. Indeed, by giving explicit examples, we present invertible nonlocal field redefinitions reducing a massive theory to the massless one, and vice versa. The studied examples are: classical harmonic oscillator, the scalar field theory, and the Dirac field theory.



1
Introduction

Some of coupling parameters in any action principle can be removed by a redefinition of the fields, which are known as the redundant parameters
[
1
]
. On the other hand, physical observables like particle masses and scattering amplitudes are independent of field redefinitions which is a well-known and important property of quantum field theory. In order to clarify the issue, let us follow Weinberg’s approach
[
1
]
and consider the action of a scalar field theory in the form

S=\int d^{4}x\left[-\tfrac{\,1\,}{2}\,Z\,(\partial^{\mu}\mathrm{\Phi}\,\partial_{\mu}\mathrm{\Phi}+m^{2}\mathrm{\Phi}^{2})-\tfrac{1}{4!}\,g\,Z^{2}\mathrm{\Phi}^{4}\right].

(1)

The constant
Z
which is called a field renormalization constant is a redundant coupling because
\delta S\scalebox{0.9}{$/$}\delta Z
vanishes when we use the field equation. In other words, the constant
Z
can be removed by redefining the field,
\mathrm{\Phi}\rightarrow(\scalebox{.85}{${1}/{\sqrt{Z}}$})\,\mathrm{\Phi}
, while neither the scalar particle mass
m
nor the coupling
g
can be eliminated by a field redefinition. Indeed, in this example, the field redefinition is continuous and local, but what happens if one applies a nonlocal field redefinition?

In this paper, we observe by applying nonlocal field redefinition a physical parameter of a theory may be redundant as well. The physical parameter we aim to make redundant is angular frequency of the simple harmonic oscillator, as well as mass of the scalar field and the Dirac field. As we know, in the last two examples, massive fields of spin-
0
and spin-
\tfrac{1}{2}
have the same degrees of freedom as their corresponding massless fields, that is, in four dimensions, 1 for the massive

/

massless real scalar field and 8 for the massive

/

massless Dirac field. However, except these two fields, other arbitrary fields of spin
s\,(\,\geqslant 1)
have different degrees of freedom depending on being massive or massless

[

]
for degrees of freedom of a massive

/

2
Harmonic Oscillator

Although our concentration may lie on the classical

/

quantum field theories, it is beneficial to first study classical harmonic oscillator shedding light on our nonlocal field redefinitions discussed in Secs.
3
and
4
. Thus, let us consider a Newtonian massive particle whose position only depends on time and subject to the force law of the usual Hooke’s law. The equation of motion reads

\Big(\,\frac{d^{2}}{dt^{2}}\,+\,\omega^{2}\,\Big)\,\mathrm{X}(t)=0\,,

(2)

where
\omega=\scalebox{0.9}{$\sqrt{{k}/{m}}$}\,,\,m\neq 0,
is angular frequency of a simple harmonic oscillator system. In comparison to the given example in Sec.
1
, here, the angular frequency is not a redundant parameter as it is a physical observable and cannot be removed by a so-called local wave function redefinition. Nevertheless, it may be redundant by performing a nonlocal wave function redefinition. To see this redundancy, we introduce a new wave function
x(t)
through

\displaystyle\mathrm{X}(t)=\bigg(\,\,\underset{n=0}{\overset{\infty}{\textstyle\sum}}\,(\,\omega t\,)^{2n}\,~\frac{\scalebox{0.85}{$(-1)$}^{n}}{4^{n}~n!~(t\,\tfrac{d}{dt}+\tfrac{1}{2})_{n}}\,\,\bigg)\,x(t)\,,

(3)

where
(\cdots)_{n}
in the denominator stands for the rising Pochhammer symbol
[
3
]
with the argument
a
of any
n\in\mathbb{N}

(a)_{n}=a(a+1)\cdots(a+n-1)\,.

(4)

By plugging (
3
) into the equation of motion (
2
), and doing some calculations explained below, we shall arrive at

\Big(\,\frac{d^{2}}{dt^{2}}\,\Big)\,x(t)=0\,.

(5)

Multiplying the latter by
m
,
m\neq 0
, it is nothing but a free Newtonian massive particle subject to no force. As a consequence, the nonlocal wave function redefinition (
3

2
). It is remarkable to mention that relation (
3
) can be also expressed in terms of the Bessel function of the first kind (see e.g.
[
4
]
). However, we shall keep the above form dealing with the Pochhammer symbol properties which makes for easy calculations, as we will see later.

We have found that the above procedure can be invertible. In fact, this time, we consider the free Newtonian massive particle equation (
5
) as our starting point. After that, we introduce a new wave function
\mathrm{X}(t)
via

\displaystyle x(t)=\bigg(\,\,\underset{n=0}{\overset{\infty}{\textstyle\sum}}~\frac{\scalebox{0.85}{$1$}}{~4^{n}\,n!\,(t\,\tfrac{d}{dt}-n-\tfrac{1}{2})_{n}}~(\,\omega t\,)^{2n}\,\bigg)\,\mathrm{X}(t)\,.

(6)

By plugging the latter into (
5
) and performing some calculations we can reach the simple harmonic oscillator equation (
2
). This means as the nonlocal wave function redefinition could remove the angular frequency from the system, invertibly, we could restore it to the system by an inverse operation. Therefore, the angular frequency as a physical observable could be redundant upon applying a nonlocal wave function redefinition.

Let us now go into the details of the above calculations. First of all, we note that the appeared operators in (
2
)-(
6

{d^{2}}/{dt^{2}}
,

and
t\,{d}/{dt}

\ell_{0}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\tfrac{1}{2}\,(t\,\tfrac{d}{dt}+\tfrac{1}{2})\,,\quad\quad\quad\ell_{1}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=-\,\tfrac{1}{2}\,\tfrac{d^{2}}{dt^{2}}\,,\quad\quad\quad\ell_{-1}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=-\,\tfrac{1}{2}\,t^{2}\,.

These generators satisfy the

[\,\ell_{m}\,,\,\ell_{n}\,]=(m-n)\,\ell_{m+n}\,,\qquad m,n=0,\pm 1\,.

It is then convenient to find the following commutation relation for any
n\in\mathbb{N}

\Big[\,\,\frac{d^{2}}{dt^{2}}\,,\,t^{2n}\,\,\Big]=4n\,(a-n+1)\,t^{2n-2}\,,

(8)

where

a\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=t\,\frac{d}{dt}\,+\frac{1}{2}\,.

(9)

At this moment, let us define two nonlocal dimensionless (
[\,t\,]=\mathrm{T}=[\omega]^{-1}
) operators

\displaystyle\mathrm{P}

\displaystyle\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\underset{n=0}{\overset{\infty}{\textstyle\sum}}\,(\,\omega t\,)^{2n}\,~\frac{\scalebox{0.85}{$(-1)$}^{n}}{4^{n}~n!~(a)_{n}}\,,

(10)

\displaystyle\mathrm{Q}

\displaystyle\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\underset{n=0}{\overset{\infty}{\textstyle\sum}}\,(\,\omega t\,)^{2n}\,~\frac{\scalebox{0.85}{$(-1)$}^{n}}{4^{n}~n!~(a+2)_{n}}\,,

(11)

where
(a)_{n}
and
a

4

9

3

8

3
]

(a)_{n}=(a+n-1)\,(a)_{n-1}\,,

(12)

it is straightforward to examine that two operators,
\mathrm{P}

\mathrm{Q}

\Big(\,\frac{d^{2}}{dt^{2}}\,+\,\omega^{2}\,\Big)\,\mathrm{P}=\mathrm{Q}\,\Big(\,\frac{d^{2}}{dt^{2}}\,\Big)\,.

(13)

As a result, by applying the nonlocal wave function redefinition (
3
), or equivalently
\mathrm{X}=\mathrm{P}\,x
, and then using the identity (

), we immediately realize that Eq. (

) can be reduced to (

\Big(\,\frac{d^{2}}{dt^{2}}\,+\,\omega^{2}\,\Big)\mathrm{X}=0~~~~\rightarrow~~~~\Big(\,\frac{d^{2}}{dt^{2}}\,+\,\omega^{2}\,\Big)\mathrm{P}\,x=0~~~~\rightarrow~~~~\mathrm{Q}\,\Big(\,\frac{d^{2}}{dt^{2}}\,\Big)x=0\,.

Let us end this section by discovering inverse operators. This enables us to follow the reverse procedure applied above. One way to find the inverse operators is as follows. Using the fact that
\mathrm{P}\mathrm{P}^{-1}=1=\mathrm{Q}^{-1}\mathrm{Q}
, we can multiply the identity (
13

\mathrm{Q}^{-1}

\mathrm{P}^{-1}
to the right which results in

\mathrm{Q}^{-1}\Big(\,\frac{d^{2}}{dt^{2}}\,+\,\omega^{2}\,\Big)=\Big(\,\frac{d^{2}}{dt^{2}}\,\Big)\mathrm{P}^{-1}\,.

(14)

Here we ask ourselves if it is possible to figure out inverse operators,
\mathrm{P}^{-1}
and
\mathrm{Q}^{-1}
, such that satisfy (
14
)? The answer is positive. In fact, if we introduce

\displaystyle\mathrm{P}^{-1}

\displaystyle\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\underset{n=0}{\overset{\infty}{\textstyle\sum}}~\frac{\scalebox{0.85}{$1$}}{~4^{n}~n!~(a-n-1)_{n}}~(\,\omega t\,)^{2n}\,,

(15)

\displaystyle\mathrm{Q}^{-1}

\displaystyle\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\underset{n=0}{\overset{\infty}{\textstyle\sum}}~\frac{\scalebox{0.85}{$1$}}{~4^{n}~n!~(a-n+1)_{n}}~(\,\omega t\,)^{2n}\,,

(16)

the so-called “inverse identity” (
14
) will be then satisfied. To check this, it is sufficient to use (
8
) and (
12
). We note that the inverse operator in (
15
) is the one that became visible in (
6
). Therefore, in a reverse way, the Eq. (
5
) could be reduced to (
2
), upon taking into account the nonlocal wave function redefinition (
6
), corresponding to
x=\mathrm{P}^{-1}~\mathrm{X}
, and applying the inverse identity (
14
). Inspired by the harmonic oscillator, let us begin studying the classical

3
Scalar field theory

We take into account a real scalar field theory in
d
-dimensional Minkowski spacetime given by the action

S=\tfrac{1}{2}\int d^{d}x\,\,\mathrm{\Phi}\,\big(\,\partial^{\mu}\partial_{\mu}-m^{2}\,\big)\,\mathrm{\Phi}\,,

(17)

where
m
is scalar field’s mass and
\mu=0,1,\ldots,d-1
. We note, to simplify calculations, an integration by part is used by which the standard field derivative term,
-\,\partial^{\mu}\mathrm{\Phi}\,\partial_{\mu}\mathrm{\Phi}
(
\,=\mathrm{\Phi}\,\partial^{\mu}\partial_{\mu}\mathrm{\Phi}\,+\,\hbox{total derivative}
), has been rewritten as the above form. As already mentioned, the mass parameter in the action (
17
) is not redundant and does not eliminate by a local field redefinition
[
1
]
. Nevertheless, we can make it redundant if we introduce a nonlocal field redefinition, which is

\displaystyle\mathrm{\Phi}=\bigg(\,\underset{n=0}{\overset{\infty}{\textstyle\sum}}\,(\,mx\,)^{2n}\,~\frac{\scalebox{0.85}{$(-1)$}^{n}}{4^{n}\,n!~\big(x^{\mu}\,\partial_{\mu}+\tfrac{d}{2}\big)_{n}}\,\bigg)\,\varphi\,.

(18)

Indeed, by plugging the latter into (
17
), the massive scalar field action (
17
) reduces to (details of the calculations are given below)

\mathcal{S}=\tfrac{1}{2}\int d^{d}x\,\,\varphi\,\partial^{\mu}\partial_{\mu}\,\varphi\,,

(19)

which is a massless scalar field action. We notice the action (
19
) is conformal invariant and the scaling dimension of
d
-dimensional scalar field is
\mathrm{\Delta}=(d-2)/2
.

Invertibly, we discover the massless scalar field action (
19
) can be reduced to the massive one (
17
) by applying the following nonlocal field redefinition

\displaystyle\varphi=\bigg(\,\underset{n=0}{\overset{\infty}{\textstyle\sum}}~\frac{\scalebox{0.85}{$1$}}{~4^{n}\,n!~\big(x^{\mu}\partial_{\mu}-n+\tfrac{d-2}{2}\big)_{n}}~(mx)^{2n}\,\bigg)\,\mathrm{\Phi}\,.

(20)

Here, we note that the operator
x^{\mu}\partial_{\mu}
in the denominator, denoting by
\mathrm{D}
, is nothing but the dilatation (rigid scaling) operator. Hence, the argument of the Pochhammer symbol in (
20
) can be taken into account as
\mathrm{D}-n+\mathrm{\Delta}
. As we will see later, we expect a similar structure for the Dirac field as well.

In this place, we give useful relations enabling the reader to simply check the details of calculations. To this end, we first obtain the following commutation relation, for any
n\in\mathbb{N}
, in any spacetime dimension
d

\left[\,\,\partial^{\mu}\partial_{\mu}\,,\,x^{2n}\,\,\right]=4n\,(h-n+1)\,x^{2n-2}\,,

(21)

where

h\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=x^{\mu}\,\partial_{\mu}+\frac{d}{2}\,.

(22)

After that, we define dimensionless (
[m]=\mathrm{M}=[x]^{-1}
) operators in
d
spacetime dimensions

\displaystyle\mathbf{P}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\underset{n=0}{\overset{\infty}{\textstyle\sum}}\,(\,mx\,)^{2n}\,~\frac{\scalebox{0.85}{$(-1)$}^{n}}{4^{n}\,n!~(h)_{n}}\,,

(23)

\displaystyle\mathbf{Q}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\underset{n=0}{\overset{\infty}{\textstyle\sum}}\,(\,mx\,)^{2n}\,~\frac{\scalebox{0.85}{$(-1)$}^{n}}{4^{n}\,n!~(h+2)_{n}}\,,

(24)

\displaystyle\mathbf{P}^{-1}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\underset{n=0}{\overset{\infty}{\textstyle\sum}}\,~\frac{\scalebox{0.85}{$1$}}{4^{n}\,n!~(h-n-1)_{n}}~(\,mx\,)^{2n}\,,

(25)

\displaystyle\mathbf{Q}^{-1}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\underset{n=0}{\overset{\infty}{\textstyle\sum}}\,~\frac{\scalebox{0.85}{$1$}}{4^{n}\,n!~(h-n+1)_{n}}~(\,mx\,)^{2n}\,,

(26)

where the Pochhammer symbol
(h)_{n}
and
h
were introduced in (
4
), (
22
). We note that the operators
\mathbf{P}
and
\mathbf{P}^{-1}
are those employed in (
18
) (
\mathrm{\Phi}=\mathbf{P}\,\varphi
) and (
20
) (
\varphi=\mathbf{P}^{-1}\,\mathrm{\Phi}
) respectively. Now, using the commutation relation (
21
) and the Pochhammer symbol property (
12
), we can conveniently demonstrate that the set of operators (
23
)-(
26
) will satisfy the following identities

\displaystyle\big(\,\partial^{\mu}\partial_{\mu}-m^{2}\,\big)\,\mathbf{P}

\displaystyle=\mathbf{Q}\,\big(\,\partial^{\mu}\partial_{\mu}\,\big)\,,

(27)

\displaystyle\mathbf{Q}^{-1}\,\big(\,\partial^{\mu}\partial_{\mu}-m^{2}\,\big)

\displaystyle=\big(\,\partial^{\mu}\partial_{\mu}\,\big)\,\mathbf{P}^{-1}\,.

(28)

Furthermore, since we argue at the level of the action, Hermitian conjugation of the above operators is needed to calculate. Therefore, we introduce the Hermitian conjugation rules

(x^{\mu})^{\dagger}=x^{\mu}\,,\qquad(\partial_{\mu})^{\dagger}=-\,\partial_{\mu}\,,

(29)

under which, e.g.,
h^{\dagger}=-h
. Using (
29
), we can perform some calculations and realize that the Hermitian conjugation of (
23
) and (
24
) results in

\mathbf{P}^{\dagger}=\mathbf{Q}^{-1}\,,\qquad\mathbf{Q}^{\dagger}=\mathbf{P}^{-1}\,.

(30)

These results could be also obtained without any explicit calculations from (
27
), (
28
). In fact, by taking the Hermitian conjugation of (
27
), and its comparison with (
28
), the relations in (
30
) will be acquired directly.

Using the above facilities, one can conveniently reduce the massive action to the massless one and vice versa. For instance, applying the field redefinition (
18
), which is corresponding to
\mathrm{\Phi}=\mathbf{P}\,\varphi=\varphi\,\mathbf{P}^{\dagger}
, the action (
17
) reduces to (
19
)

\displaystyle S=\tfrac{1}{2}\int d^{d}x\,\,\varphi\,\mathbf{P}^{\dagger}\big(\,\partial^{\mu}\partial_{\mu}-m^{2}\,\big)\,\mathbf{P}\,\varphi=\tfrac{1}{2}\int d^{d}x\,\,\varphi\!\underbrace{\,\mathbf{P}^{\dagger}\,\mathbf{Q}\,}_{=\,1}\big(\,\partial^{\mu}\partial_{\mu}\,\big)\,\varphi=\mathcal{S}\,,

in which relations (
27
), (
30
) have been used.

4
Dirac field theory

In
d
-dimensional flat spacetime, the massive Dirac field is given by the action

S=-\int d^{d}x\,\bar{\mathrm{\Psi}}\big(\,\gamma\cdot\partial+m\,\big)\mathrm{\Psi}

(31)

where the dot stands for the scalar product,
\gamma^{\mu}
are
d
-dimensional Dirac gamma matrices, and
\bar{\mathrm{\Psi}}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}={\mathrm{\Psi}}^{\dagger}\,i\,\gamma^{0}
is the Dirac adjoint. Similar to the scalar field, here, we intend to illustrate that the physical mass parameter
m
can be redundant, by applying a nonlocal field redefinition. For this purpose, we introduce a new field
\psi
by

\displaystyle\hskip-14.22636pt\mathrm{\Psi}=\bigg(\,\underset{k=0}{\overset{\infty}{\textstyle\sum}}\,\Big[(m\,\gamma\cdot x)^{2k}-2k\,(m\,\gamma\cdot x)^{2k-1}\Big]\,\frac{\scalebox{0.85}{$1$}}{~4^{k}~k!~\big(x^{\mu}\,\partial_{\mu}+\tfrac{d}{2}\big)_{k}~}\bigg)\,\psi\,.

(32)

Plugging the latter into the massive action (
31
), and doing some calculations explained below, the mass parameter will be eliminated so as we shall arrive at

\mathcal{S}=-\int d^{d}x~\bar{\psi}~\gamma\cdot\partial~{\psi}\,,

(33)

that is the Dirac action for the massless field
\psi
.

As a matter of fact, we realized that this method can be invertible as well. Indeed, if we introduce the nonlocal field redefinition

\displaystyle\hskip-17.07182pt\psi=\bigg(\,\underset{k=0}{\overset{\infty}{\textstyle\sum}}\,\frac{\scalebox{0.85}{$(-1)$}^{k}}{4^{k}\,k!\,\big(x^{\mu}\,\partial_{\mu}-k+\tfrac{d}{2}\big)_{k}~}\Big[(m\,\gamma\cdot x)^{2k}-2k\,(m\,\gamma\cdot x)^{2k-1}\Big]\bigg)\,\mathrm{\Psi},

(34)

then the mass parameter can be restored to the theory, such that this time by plugging (
34
) into the massless action (
33
), we can arrive back to the massive action (
31
). We note that, similar to the scalar field redefinition (
20
), here, the argument of the Pochhammer symbol in (
34
) can be considered as
\mathrm{D}-k+{\varDelta}+\tfrac{1}{2}
, where
\varDelta=(d-1)/2
is the scaling dimension of
d
-dimensional Dirac spinor field.

The step-by-step details of the calculations are very similar to the case of the scalar field. However, here, we have the Clifford algebra
\{\gamma^{\mu},\gamma^{\nu}\}=2\eta^{\mu\nu}
that using it one can simply find
\{\gamma\cdot\partial,\gamma\cdot x\}=2(x\cdot\partial)+d
and
[\gamma\cdot\partial,(\gamma\cdot x)^{2}]=2(\gamma\cdot x)
. In the same way, one can find a generalization of odd and even powers of
\gamma\cdot x
. Therefore, we acquire commutation (
[a,b]=ab-ba
) and anticommutation (
\{a,b\}=ab+ba
) relations, in any spacetime dimension
d
, for any
k\in\mathbb{N}

\displaystyle\left[\,\gamma\cdot\partial~,~(\gamma\cdot x)^{\,2k}\,\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2k\,(\gamma\cdot x)^{\,2k-1}\,,

(35)

\displaystyle\left\{\gamma\cdot\partial\,,\,(\gamma\cdot x)^{\,2k-1}\right\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2\,(h-k+1)~(\gamma\cdot x)^{\,2k-2}\,,

(36)

where
h
defined in (
22
). After that, we introduce dimensionless operators in
d
spacetime dimensions

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}

\displaystyle\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\underset{k=0}{\overset{\infty}{\textstyle\sum}}\,\Big[(m\,\gamma\cdot x)^{2k}-2k\,(m\,\gamma\cdot x)^{2k-1}\Big]\frac{\scalebox{0.85}{$1$}}{~4^{k}~k!~(h)_{k}~}\,,

(37)

\displaystyle\mathbb{Q}

\displaystyle\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\underset{k=0}{\overset{\infty}{\textstyle\sum}}\,\Big[(m\,\gamma\cdot x)^{2k}+2k\,(m\,\gamma\cdot x)^{2k-1}\Big]\frac{\scalebox{0.85}{$1$}}{~4^{k}~k!~(h+1)_{k}~}\,,

(38)

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}^{-1}

\displaystyle\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\underset{k=0}{\overset{\infty}{\textstyle\sum}}\,\frac{\scalebox{0.85}{$(-1)$}^{k}}{~4^{k}~k!~(h-k)_{k}~}\,\Big[(m\,\gamma\cdot x)^{2k}-2k\,(m\,\gamma\cdot x)^{2k-1}\Big]\,,

(39)

\displaystyle\mathbb{Q}^{-1}

\displaystyle\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\underset{k=0}{\overset{\infty}{\textstyle\sum}}\,\frac{\scalebox{0.85}{$(-1)$}^{k}}{~4^{k}~k!~(h-k+1)_{k}~}\,\Big[(m\,\gamma\cdot x)^{2k}+2k\,(m\,\gamma\cdot x)^{2k-1}\Big]\,,

(40)

with
h
introduced in (
22
). We notice that the operators
\mathbb{P}
and
\mathbb{P}^{-1}
are those emerged in (
32
) (
\mathrm{\Psi}=\mathbb{P}\,\psi
) and (
34
) (
\psi=\mathbb{P}^{-1}\,\mathrm{\Psi}
). Using the commutation (
35
) and anti-commutation (
36
) relations as well as the property of the Pochhammer symbol (
12
), it follows that the above operators satisfy the following identities

\displaystyle\big(\,\gamma\cdot\partial+m\,\big)\,\mathbb{P}

\displaystyle=\mathbb{Q}\,\big(\,\gamma\cdot\partial\,\big)\,,

(41)

\displaystyle\mathbb{Q}^{-1}\,\big(\,\gamma\cdot\partial+m\,\big)

\displaystyle=\big(\,\gamma\cdot\partial\,\big)\,\mathbb{P}^{-1}\,.

(42)

To find the Hermitian conjugate of operators, we use (
29
) accompanied with

(\gamma^{\mu})^{\dagger}=\gamma^{0}\gamma^{\mu}\gamma^{0}\,,\qquad(\gamma^{0})^{\dagger}=-\,\gamma^{0}\,,\qquad(\gamma^{0})^{2}=-1\,.

(43)

As a consequence, the Hermitian conjugates of the operators (
37

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}^{\dagger}

\displaystyle=-\,\gamma^{0}\,\mathbb{Q}^{-1}\,\gamma^{0}\,,\qquad\qquad(\,\mathbb{P}^{-1})^{\dagger}=-\,\gamma^{0}\,\mathbb{Q}\,\gamma^{0}\,,

(44)

\displaystyle\mathbb{Q}^{\dagger}

\displaystyle=-\,\gamma^{0}\,\mathbb{P}^{-1}\,\gamma^{0}\,,\qquad\qquad(\mathbb{Q}^{-1})^{\dagger}=-\,\gamma^{0}\,\mathbb{P}\,\gamma^{0}\,.

(45)

At this stage, we are equipped to reduce each actions, (
31
) or (
33

\psi=\mathbb{P}^{-1}\,\mathrm{\Psi}
(

) (which, using (

\bar{\psi}=\bar{\mathrm{\Psi}}\,\mathbb{Q}

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}

\displaystyle=-\int d^{d}x~\bar{\mathrm{\Psi}}\,\mathbb{Q}~(\gamma\cdot\partial)~\mathbb{P}^{-1}\,\mathrm{\Psi}=-\int d^{d}x~\bar{\mathrm{\Psi}}\,\underbrace{\mathbb{Q}\,\mathbb{Q}^{-1}}_{=\,1}(\gamma\cdot\partial+m)~\mathrm{\Psi}=S\,,

in which relations (
42
) and (
44
) have been utilized.

5
Conclusions and Discussion

[
1
]
. In this paper, we investigated the possibility if a physical mass parameter can be redundant by applying a nonlocal field redefinition. For this purpose, we presented three explicit examples equipped with invertible nonlocal wave

/

field redefinitions. In the case of the classical harmonic oscillator, the angular frequency became redundant using (
3
), while it could be recaptured to the system by (
6
). For the scalar field theory, by applying (
18
), the mass parameter of the spin-
0
field was removed from the theory, while it could be reattached to the theory upon (
20
). In similar fashion, we introduced (
32
) and its inverse (
34
) for the Dirac field theory, using them, the mass parameter of the spin-
\tfrac{1}{2}
spinor field could be eliminated and restored respectively.

0
and spin-
\tfrac{1}{2}

s\,(\,\geqslant 1)

d

1

d-1
) massive degrees of freedom, while it has (
d-2
) massless degrees of freedom. Thus, it is of interest to find invertible operators (if any) making the mass parameter redundant in the Proca action in which the degrees of freedom will change by mass redundancy. Furthermore, our method may establish a duality between theories in flat and curved spacetimes. To be more precise, the scalar field theory in anti-de Sitter spacetime includes the cosmological constant playing a role like mass. Thus, making use of our nonlocal transformations, one can simply drop the cosmological constant from the field equation which this in turn yields the equation of motion in flat spacetime. Therefore, as an application, a suitable generalization of our nonlocal transformations may be beneficial to construct a flat spacetime holographic correspondence

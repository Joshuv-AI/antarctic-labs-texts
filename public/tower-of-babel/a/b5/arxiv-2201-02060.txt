Minimum non-chromatic-choosable graphs with given chromatic number
Jialu Zhu, Xuding Zhu

Abstract
A graph G G is called chromatic-choosable if χ ⁡ ( G ) = c ​ h ​ ( G ) \chi(G)=ch(G) . A natural problem is to determine the minimum number of vertices in a non-chromatic-choosable graph with given chromatic number. It was conjectured by Ohba, and proved by Noel, Reed and Wu that k k -chromatic graphs G G with | V ⁡ ( G ) | ≤ 2 ​ k + 1 |V(G)|\leq 2k+1 are chromatic-choosable. This upper bound on | V ⁡ ( G ) | |V(G)| is tight. It is known that if k k is even, then G = K 3 ⋆ ( k / 2 + 1 ) , 1 ⋆ ( k / 2 − 1 ) G=K_{3\star(k/2+1),1\star(k/2-1)} and G = K 4 , 2 ⋆ ( k − 1 ) G=K_{4,2\star(k-1)} are non-chromatic-choosable k k -chromatic graphs with | V ⁡ ( G ) | = 2 ​ k + 2 |V(G)|=2k+2 . Some subgraphs of these two graphs are also non-chromatic-choosable. The main result of this paper is that all other k k -chromatic graphs G G with | V ⁡ ( G ) | = 2 ​ k + 2 |V(G)|=2k+2 are chromatic-choosable. In particular, if χ ⁡ ( G ) \chi(G) is odd and | V ⁡ ( G ) | ≤ 2 ​ χ ​ ( G ) + 2 |V(G)|\leq 2\chi(G)+2 , then G G is chromatic-choosable, which was conjectured by Noel.

The

List colouring is a natural generalization of classical graph colouring, introduced independently by Erdős-Rubin-Taylor
[
4
]
and Vizing
[
24
]
in 1970’s.
A

of

which
assigns to each vertex

of permissible colours.
An

-colouring
of

is a proper colouring
\phi
of

with
\phi(v)\in L(v)
for each vertex

. We say that

is
L
-colourable
if there exists an
L
-colouring of
G
, and
G

is

-choosable
if

is
L
-colourable for any list assignment

of
G
with
|L(v)|\geq k
for each vertex

generally, for a function

, we say

is

-choosable
if
G
is
L
-colourable for every list assignment
L
with
|L(v)|\geq g(v)
for all

.
The
choice number

{\rm ch}(G)
of

is
the minimum
k
for which

is
k
-choosable.

A
k
-colouring of a graph
G
is a special case of list colouring, where each vertex
v
has the same list
L(v)=\{1,2,\ldots,k\}
. So
k
-choosable implies
k
-colourable. At first glance, one might expect the reverse inequality to hold as well. The smaller intersection between lists would make it easier to assign distinct colours to adjacent vertices. However, the reverse inequality is far from true. It was observed in
[
4
]
and
[
24
]
that for any integer

, there are bipartite graphs that are not
k
-choosable. So the difference
ch(G)-\chi(G)
can be arbitrarily large.

A graph
G
is called
chromatic-choosable
if
\chi(G)=ch(G)
.
Chromatic-choosable graphs have been studied a lot in the literature, and are related to some other difficult problems.
For example, the famous Dinitz problem (see e.g.
[
25
]
) asks the following question:

“Given an
n\times n
array of
n
-sets, is it always possible to choose one from each set, keeping the chosen elements distinct in every row, and distinct in every column?”

This problem can be equivalently stated as whether the line graph of
K_{n,n}
is chromatic-choosable? This problem was solved by Galvin
[
5
]
, who proved a more general result: the line graph of any bipartite multigraph is chromatic-choosable. On the other hand, Galvin’s result is a special case of a more general conjecture - the list colouring conjecture: line graphs of all multigraphs are chromatic-choosable. The list colouring conjecture was posed independently by many diﬀerent
researchers: Albertson and Collins, Bollobás and Harris,
Gupta, and Vizing (see
[
1
,
7
,
10
]
). It has attracted a lot of attention and remains open in general.

Ohba conjecture is another well-known conjecture about chromatic-choosable graphs. It was proved in
[
18
]
that for any graph
G
,
ch(G\vee K_{n})=\chi(G\vee K_{n})
for sufficiently large
n
, where
G\vee H
is the join of
G
and
H
, i.e., the graph obtained from the disjoint union of
G
and
H
by adding edges connecting every vertex of
G
to every vertex of
H
. This means that
graphs
G
with
|V(G)|
“close” to
\chi(G)
are chromatic-choosable. A natural problem is how close should be
|V(G)|
and
\chi(G)
to ensure that
G
be chromatic-choosable. Equivalently, what is the
minimum number of vertices in a non-
k
-choosable
k
-chromatic graph?

We denote by
K_{k_{1}\star n_{1},k_{2}\star n_{2},\ldots,k_{q}\star n_{q}}
the complete multi-partite graph with
n_{i}
parts of size
k_{i}
, for
i=1,2,\ldots,q
. If
n_{j}=1
, then the number

is omitted from the notation.
It was proved in
[
3
]
that if
k
is an even integer, then
K_{4,2\star(k-1)}
and
K_{3\star(k/2+1),1\star(k/2-1)}
are not

-choosable. These two graphs are

-chromatic graphs with
2k+2
vertices.
Ohba
[
18
]
conjectured that for any positive integer

,
k
-chromatic graphs with at most

vertices are

-choosable. This conjecture has attracted considerable attention, and many partial results were proved before it was finally confirmed by Noel, Reed and Wu
[
17
]
.

One approach has been to prove variants of Ohba’s conjecture in which

is replaced by
|V(G)|\leq f(\chi(G))
for some function

.
Ohba
[

]
proved such a variant with

, and Reed and Sudakov
[

]
improved the result to

. By using a sophisticated probabilistic method, Reed and Sudakov
[
20
]
proved that Ohba’s conjecture is asymptotically true: if
|V(G)|\leq(2-o(1))\chi(G)
, then

is chromatic-choosable.

Another approach has been to show the conjecture holds for special families of graphs. He, Li, Shen and Zheng
[
22
]
proved Ohba’s conjecture for graphs
G
with independence number
\alpha(G)\leq 3
, by extending a result of Ohba
[
19
]
who proved that if
|V(G)|\leq 2\chi(G)
and

, then

is chromatic-choosable. Kostochka, Stiebitz and Woodall
[
13
]
improved this result and showed that Ohba’s Conjecture holds for graphs

. Also Ohba’s conjecture were verified for some particular complete multipartite graphs in
[

,
22
,
23
]
.

In 2015, Ohba’s conjecture was finally confirmed by Noel, Reed and Wu
[

]
:

Theorem 1.1
(Noel-Reed-Wu Theorem)

Every
k
-colourable graph with at most
2k+1
vertices is
k
-choosable.

Nevertheless, this is not the end of the story. More problems related to Ohba’s conjecture are posed and studied. One problem is what would be the choice number of
k
-chromatic graphs

with
|V(G)|
slightly bigger than
2k+1
. This question was addressed in
[
16
]
. Another related problem is the online version of Ohba’s conjecture, which was posed in
[
8
]
, and has been studied in a few papers
[

,
12
,
14
]
. Some partial cases are verified and the conjecture remains open in general.

This paper explores the tightness of Ohba’s conjecture. Although Ohba’s conjecture is tight,
K_{4,2\star(k-1)}
and

for even

are the only known

-chormatic graphs with

vertices that are not

-choosable. In particular, Ohba’s conjecture was not known to be tight for odd integer

Noel
[
15
]
conjectured if

is odd, then all

-chromatic graphs with

vertices are
k
-choosable.

Observe that for a
k
-chromatic graph

, by adding edges between vertices of distinct colour classes, the resulting graph has the same chromatic number, and whose choice number is not decreased. Therefore in the study of minimum non-chromatic choosable graphs, it suffices to consider complete multipartite graphs.

The main result of this paper is that
K_{4,2\star(k-1)}
and

Assume

For a positive integer

For an odd integer

\beta(k)=\begin{cases}2k+2,&\text{ if $k$ is even},\cr 2k+3,&\text{ if $k$ is odd}.\end{cases}

6
Near acceptable colourings

We have shown in the previous section that the partition
\mathcal{S}
of

induced by a pseudo-

-colouring of

, one more restriction need to be added to a pseudo-

-colouring.
In this section, we define the concept of

-colouring of

of

-colouring.

Definition 5

A colour

is called frequent if one of the following holds:

(1)

|L^{-1}(c)|\geq k+2
.

(2)

|L^{-1}(c)\cap T|\geq\lambda

(3)

|T|=\lambda-1\geq 1
and

.

A pseudo

-colouring

The concept of near acceptable

-colouring was first used in
[

]
for the proof of Noel-Reed-Wu Theorem.
For the proof of Theorem
1.2
, as

has one more vertex, the definition of frequent colours is different from that in
[

]
. Thus the near acceptable

-colouring in this paper is different from the one in
[
17
]
. The difference makes it more difficult to find a near acceptable

-colouring of

-colouring of

Lemma 6.1

G
has no near acceptable

-colouring.

Proof.

Assume to the contrary that

is a near acceptable

-colouring of

.
Since
|X_{f}|>|Y_{f}|
, there is a colour class

.

, in contrary to Lemma

. Therefore

F\subseteq\bigcap_{v\in T}L(v).

Lemma 8.3

There are at most
k-p_{1}-1
frequent colours.

Proof.

Assume to the contrary that
\{c_{p_{1}+1},c_{p_{1}+2},\ldots,c_{k}\}
is a set of
k-p_{1}
frequent colours.

Assume
T=\{v_{1},v_{2},\ldots,v_{p_{1}}\}
. We choose
p_{1}
colours
c_{1},c_{2},\ldots,c_{p_{1}}
so that for

c_{i}\in L(v_{i})-\{c_{p_{1}+1},\ldots,c_{k}\}-\{c_{1},\ldots,c_{i-1}\}.

As
|L(v_{i})|\geq k
, the colour
c_{i}
exists.

Let
C^{\prime}=\{c_{1},c_{2},\ldots,c_{k}\}
and define
L^{\prime}
as follows:

L^{\prime}(v)=\begin{cases}C^{\prime}&\text{if $v\in T$},\cr L(v)&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

By Lemma

,
p_{1}\geq\lambda-1
. If
p_{1}\geq\lambda
, then each colour in

is Type-2 frequent with respect to
L^{\prime}
. If
p_{1}=\lambda-1

L^{\prime}
.
By Lemma
8.3
,

(G,L^{\prime})
is not a mimnimum counterexample to Theorem
1.2
. Since
C_{L^{\prime}}\subseteq C_{L}
, we know that
(G,L^{\prime})
is not a counterexample to Theorem

. Hence
G
has an
L^{\prime}
-colouring
f
.

Note that if
v\notin T
, then
f(v)\in L(v)
.
We shall modify
f
to obtain a near acceptable
L
-colouring of
G
.

Let
T^{\prime}=\{v_{i}:1\leq i\leq p_{1},c_{i}\in f(T)\}
.
As
|T-T^{\prime}|=|f(T)-\{c_{1},c_{2},\ldots,c_{p_{1}}\}|

there is a bijection

Let

is a near acceptable
L

.

9

of

.

For any colour

Lemma 9.1

There is a subset
X
of
T
such that

(1)

|X|\geq p_{1}-\lambda+1
.

(2)

|L(X)|\leq k+b
.

Moreover, if

or
p_{1}=\lambda-1
, then
|X|\geq p_{1}-\lambda+2
.

Proof.

Let
c^{\prime}\in C_{L}-F
be a colour with
|L^{-1}(c^{\prime})|=k+1-b
. By Lemma
8.2
, there is a vertex
w\in T
such that
c^{\prime}\notin L(w)
.
Define a list assignment
L^{\prime}
as follows:

L^{\prime}(v)=\begin{cases}L(v)\cup\{c^{\prime}\}&\text{$v=w$ },\cr L(v)&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

By the maximality of
\sum_{v\in V(G)}|L(v)|
,
G
has an
L^{\prime}
-colouring
f
.
We must have
f(w)=c^{\prime}
and

is a proper

-colouring of

.

Now
f
is a pseudo
L
-colouring of

. By Lemma
5.1
, in the bipartite graph

V_{f}
has a subset
X_{f}
such that
|X_{f}|>|Y_{f}|=|N_{B_{f}}(X_{f})|

\lambda-1

G
.

It is easy to see that
w\in X_{f}
and
c^{\prime}\notin Y_{f}
.
Let

X=\{v\in T:\{v\}\text{ is an $f$-class in $X_{f}$}\}.

Then
|X|=|T|-|(V_{f}-X_{f})\cap T|\geq p_{1}-\lambda+1
and by Lemma
5.2
, if
p_{1}=\lambda-1
, then
|X|=|T|-|(V_{f}-X_{f})\cap T|\geq p_{1}-\lambda+2
.

Since each
f
-class in
X_{f}
contains a vertex
v
for which
c^{\prime}\notin L(v)
, we have

|L(X)|\leq|Y_{f}|<|X_{f}|\leq|V|-|L^{-1}(c^{\prime})|=k+1+b.

So
|L(X)|\leq k+b.

It remains to prove that if
b=0
, i.e.,
|L^{-1}(c^{\prime})|=k+1
, then
|X|\geq p_{1}-\lambda+2
.

Assume to the contrary that
|L^{-1}(c^{\prime})|=k+1

|X|=p_{1}-\lambda+1
.
By Lemma
5.1
,
|Y_{f}|\geq k+1
and hence
|X_{f}|\geq k+2
, in contrary to

This completes the proof of Lemma
9.1
.

We order the colours in

as

, so that

|L^{-1}(c_{1})\cap X|\geq|L^{-1}(c_{2})\cap X|\geq\ldots\geq|L^{-1}(c_{t})\cap X|,

where

. Let
F^{\prime\prime}=\{c_{1},c_{2},\ldots,c_{k-p_{1}}\}
.

It suffices to show that

|L^{-1}(c_{k-p_{1}})\cap X|\geq\lambda,

and hence each colour
c_{i}\in F^{\prime\prime}
is a frequent of Type (2).

Let
Z=\{c_{k-p_{1}},c_{k-p_{1}+1},\ldots,c_{t}\}
.
For each

.
Hence

|Z||L^{-1}(c_{k-p_{1}})\cap X|\geq\sum_{i=k-p_{1}}^{t}|L^{-1}(c_{i})\cap X|=\sum_{v\in X}|L(v)\cap Z|\geq|X|(p_{1}+1).

(26)

By Lemma
9.1
,

|Z|=|L(X)|-(k-p_{1}-1)\leq p_{1}+1+b.

Plugging this into (

), we have

(p_{1}+1+b)|L^{-1}(c_{k-p_{1}})\cap X|\geq|X|(p_{1}+1).

This implies that

(27)

For each

,

(by definition of

). By Lemma
3.2
, for

(28)

Plugging
|C_{L}|=|V|-\lambda=2k+2-\lambda
and

), we have

(2k+2)k\leq(2k+2-\lambda-(k-p_{1}-1))(k+1-b)+(k-p_{1}-1)(k+p_{1}+2).

(29)

(Note that the coefficient of
|F|
in the right hand side of (

) is positive.)

This implies

b\leq\frac{(p_{1}+3-\lambda-k)(k+1)+(k-p_{1}-1)(k+p_{1}+2)}{k+p_{1}+3-\lambda}.

(30)

If
\lambda=2
, then since
p_{1}\geq 3
, by plugging
|X|\geq p_{1}-\lambda+1
(see Lemma

) into (

), we have

|L^{-1}(c_{k-p_{1}})\cap X|\geq\frac{(p_{1}-\lambda+1)(p_{1}+1)}{p_{1}+1+b}\geq\frac{(p_{1}-1)(p_{1}+1)}{p_{1}+1+\frac{(p_{1}+1)(k-p_{1}-1)}{k+p_{1}+1}}=\frac{(p_{1}-1)(k+p_{1}+1)}{2k}\geq\frac{2(k+p_{1}+1)}{2k}>1.

Since
|L^{-1}(c_{k-p_{1}})\cap X|
is an integer,
|L^{-1}(c_{k-p_{1}})\cap X|\geq 2=\lambda
and we are done.

Therefore
\lambda\geq 3
and
|C_{L}|\leq 2k-1
.
By Lemma
3.2
,
G
has no 2-parts.
By the same reason as (

), we have

p_{1}\geq\frac{k+1}{2}.

Combining (

) with Lemma
8.3
, together with

\frac{k-3}{2}\geq k-p_{1}-1\geq|F^{\prime}|\geq\frac{k\lambda-|C_{L}|}{p_{1}+1}=\frac{k\lambda-(2k+2-\lambda)}{p_{1}+1}=\frac{(k+1)\lambda-2k-2}{p_{1}+1}.

Hence

\displaystyle\lambda\leq\frac{\frac{(k-3)(p_{1}+1)}{2}+2k+2}{k+1}=\frac{p_{1}+1}{2}+2-\frac{2(p_{1}+1)}{k+1}<\frac{p_{1}+1}{2}+1.

Since

is an integer,

(31)

Therefore

Plugging this into (

\displaystyle b

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{(p_{1}+3-\lambda-k)(k+1)+(k-p_{1}-1)(k+p_{1}+2)}{k+p_{1}+3-\lambda}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{(p_{1}+3-\lambda-k)(k+1)+(k-p_{1}-1)(k+p_{1}+2)}{k+4}\ \ (\text{ as $p_{1}\geq\lambda+1$ })

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}(p_{1}+1-2\lambda)+\frac{2k+2+3\lambda-\frac{7}{2}(p_{1}+1)}{k+4}

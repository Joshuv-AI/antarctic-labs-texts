A note on the Winterbottom shape
Leonard Kreutz, Bernd Schmidt

Abstract
. In this short note we review results on equilibrium shapes of minimizers to the sessile drop problem. More precisely, we study the Winterbottom problem and prove that the Winterbottom shape is indeed optimal. The arguments presented here are based on relaxation and the (anisotropic) isoperimetric inequality.



1.
Introduction

For solid crystals with sufficiently small grains, Herring
[
11
]
claims that the bulk contribution to their configurational energy is negligible with respect to the surface tension. Thus, in this setting, when determining the equilibrium shape of a crystal, interfacial energies of the type

\displaystyle P_{\varphi}(E)=\int_{\partial^{*}E}\varphi(\nu_{E})\,\mathrm{d}\mathcal{H}^{d-1}\,

play a fundamental role. Here,
\varphi\colon\mathbb{R}^{d}\to[0,+\infty)
is a convex and positively homogeneous function of degree one that describes the possibly anisotropic surface tension of the crystal. The set
E
is assumed to be sufficiently regular (i.e.
E
is a set of finite perimeter),
\partial^{*}E
denotes its (measure theoretic) boundary, and
\nu_{E}(x)
the outer normal to the set
E
at the point
x\in\partial^{*}E
. The Wulff problem consists in studying solutions of

(1.1)

\displaystyle E\in\mathrm{argmin}\left\{P_{\varphi}(E)\colon|E|=v\right\}\,,

where
v>0
is given. This problem is an anistropic generalization of the classical isoperimetric problem. In the early 1900s Wulff
[
18
]
proposed a geometric construction to (
1.1
) given by

(1.2)

\displaystyle W_{\varphi}=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\colon x\cdot\nu\leq\varphi(\nu)\text{ for all }\nu\in\mathbb{S}^{d-1}\}\,.

This shape is nowadays known as the Wulff set (or Wulff crystal) of
\varphi
. Dinghas proved formally in
[
5
]
that among convex polyhedra the Wulff set is the shape having the least surface integral for the volume it contains. The proof has later been rendered precise by Taylor
[
14
,
15
,
16
]
using arguments from geometric measure theory.

The Wulff variational problem provides a description of an equilibrium crystal shape deep inside a region in the gas phase. This leads to the natural question if, likewise, the shape of a crystal growing on a substrate can be determined that minimizes a suitable combination of its surface tension and the interaction energy with the substrate. Such a situation may be described as follows. For
\varphi
be as above and
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
we set

(1.3)

\displaystyle F_{\lambda,\varphi}(E)=\int_{\partial^{*}E\cap H^{+}}\varphi(\nu_{E})\,\mathrm{d}\mathcal{H}^{d-1}+\lambda\mathcal{H}^{d-1}(\partial^{*}E\cap H)\,,

where
H^{+}=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\colon x_{d}>0\}
,
H=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\colon x_{d}=0\}
and
E
is a set of finite perimeter in
H^{+}
, i.e.
|E\setminus H^{+}|=0
, which we simply denote by
E\subseteq H^{+}
, cf. Fig.
1
.

Here, as in the Wulff problem,
\varphi
represents the (anisotropic) surface tension density between crystal and vapor and
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
is the relative adhesion coefficient between the crystal and the substrate (i.e., the difference of the crystal-substrate and the substrate-vapor interfacial energy per unit surface area). In this setting, the Winterbottom problem
[
17
]
consists in studying the existence and finding the solutions of

(1.4)

\displaystyle E\in\mathrm{argmin}\left\{F_{\lambda,\varphi}(E)\colon E\subseteq H^{+},|E|=v\right\}\,,

where
v>0
. There are three interesting parameter regimes to consider (see Remark
2.4
):

(1)

If
\lambda\geq\varphi(-e_{d})
it is energetically inconvenient for the crystal to attach to the substrate and therefore the solution (
1.4
) coincides (up to translation) with the solution of (
1.1
). This phenomenon is called complete drying.

(2)

If
\lambda\in(-\varphi(e_{d}),\varphi(-e_{d}))
a solution to the Winterbottom exists, but its shape differs from the Wulff shape given in (
1.2
). In fact, the solution shape is now the Winterbottom shape given by

(1.5)

\displaystyle W_{\lambda,\varphi}=W_{\varphi}\cap\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\colon x_{d}\geq-\lambda\}

suitably rescaled and placed in order to be in contact with the substrate. In this case we speak of partial drying/wetting.

(3)

If
\lambda\leq-\varphi(e_{d})
complete wetting occurs: It is energetically favorable to create large surface area in common with the substrate. This allows to create arbitrarily small energy (if
\lambda=-\varphi(e_{d})
) or even energy diverging to
-\infty
(if
\lambda<-\varphi(e_{d})
).

We rigorously study all the cases. From the point of view of the mathematical analysis, the most interesting regime to consider is the partial wetting regime (2). There, our first main result Theorem
2.3
proves that indeed (
1.5
) solves (
1.4
).

In the case that
\varphi
is smooth the solution given in (
1.5
) in particular recovers Young’s law
[
19
]
. This law relates the contact angle of the boundary of equilibrium configurations with the adhesion coefficient
\lambda
. More precisely, if
\partial(\overline{E}\cap H)
denotes the boundary of
\overline{E}\cap H
in
H
, then

(1.6)

\displaystyle\nabla\varphi(\nu_{E}(x))\cdot(-e_{d})=\lambda\,,\quad\text{ for all }x\in\partial(\overline{E}\cap H)\,

(see Remark
2.4
(
4
) below). In the isotropic case, i.e.
\varphi(\nu)=|\nu|
, this reads (cf. Fig.

\displaystyle\nu_{E}(x)\cdot(-e_{d})=\lambda\,,\quad\text{ for all }x\in\partial(\overline{E}\cap H)\,.

The equilibrium condition (
1.6

[
12
]
and for epitaxially-strained thin films in
[

.

The proof that (

2.
Settings and Main results

Notation and Preliminaries

For a measurable subset
B
of
\mathbb{R}^{d}
we denote by
|B|
its
d
-dimensional Lebesgue measure and by
\mathcal{H}^{k}(B)
its
k
-dimensional Hausdorff measure. Given
x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
we denote by
x\cdot y
their scalar product and by
\|x\|
the Euclidean norm of
x
. For

,
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
we write
B_{r}(x)=\{y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\colon\|y-x\|<r\}
and abbreviate
\mathbb{S}^{d-1}=\partial B_{1}(0)
. We also introduce the half space
H^{+}=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\colon x_{d}>0\}
and the hyperplane
H=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\colon x_{d}=0\}
.
If
E\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
is a set of finite perimeter (i.e., its characteristic function is of bounded variation), we write
\partial^{*}E
for the reduced boundary of
E
and denote by
\nu_{E}\colon\partial^{*}E\to\mathbb{S}^{d-1}
the generalized outer normal to
E
. We refer to
[
1
]
for the definition of these objects and basic facts about sets of finite perimeter. In particular, if
K\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d}
is a bounded convex set, then
K
is of finite perimeter and
\nu_{K}(x)\in\mathcal{N}_{K}(x)
for all
x\in\partial^{*}K\subseteq\partial K
, where, for each
x\in\partial K
,
\mathcal{N}_{K}(x):=\{\zeta\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\colon\zeta\cdot(x^{\prime}-x)\leq 0\penalty\ \forall\,x^{\prime}\in K\}
denotes the normal cone to
K
at
x
.

Energy

Let
\varphi\colon\mathbb{R}^{d}\to[0,+\infty)
be a convex and positively homogeneous function of degree one that is bounded from below, i.e. there exists
c>0
such that

(2.1)

\displaystyle\varphi(\nu)\geq c\,\|\nu\|\text{ for all }\nu\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\,.

For a set of finite perimeter
E\subseteq H^{+}
we define
F_{\lambda,\varphi}(E)
as in (
1.3
). Given
v>0
, we are interested in studying the shape of the solutions to (
1.4
).

Remark 2.1

(Scaling)
.

It is obvious that
F_{\lambda,\varphi}(rE)=r^{d-1}F_{\lambda,\varphi}(E)
for any
r>0
and, in particular, the minimal energy
m_{\lambda,\varphi}(v)=\inf\left\{F_{\lambda,\varphi}(E)\colon|E|=v\right\}
satisfies

\displaystyle m_{\lambda,\varphi}(v)=v^{\frac{d-1}{d}}m_{\lambda,\varphi}(1)\,.

Wulff shape

In order to construct solutions to (
1.4
) we first define the
Wulff set
of
\varphi
, yet in a more general set-up: For any positively
1
-homogeneous Borel function
\varphi\colon\mathbb{R}^{d}\to[0,+\infty)
which is bounded from below (cf. (
2.1
)) but not necessarily continuous or even convex, we set

\displaystyle W_{\varphi}:=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\colon\nu\cdot x\leq\varphi(\nu)\penalty\ \forall\,\nu\in\mathbb{S}^{n-1}\}\,.

In fact, for continuous
\varphi
,
W_{\varphi}
is the – up to translations unique – minimizer among sets with equal volume to the problem
E\mapsto\int_{\partial^{*}E}\varphi(\nu_{E})\,\mathrm{d}\mathcal{H}^{d-1}
without substrate, cf.
[
16
,
8
,
9
]
.
We recall some basic facts on Wulff sets.

Proposition 2.2
.

(1)

W_{\varphi}
is a bounded, convex and closed set with
0\in\operatorname{int}W_{\varphi}
.

(2)

The convex conjugate
\varphi^{*}
is equal to the indicator function of
W_{\varphi}
, i.e.,
\varphi^{*}(x)=0
if
x\in W_{\varphi}
and
\varphi^{*}(x)=\infty
if
x\notin W_{\varphi}
.

(3)

For
x\in\partial W_{\varphi}
we have that
\mathcal{N}_{W_{\varphi}}(x)=\partial\varphi^{*}(x)
, where
\partial\varphi^{*}(x)
is the subgradient of
\varphi^{*}
at
x
.

(4)

The Wulff shapes of
\varphi
and its convex envelope
\varphi^{**}
coincide:
W_{\varphi}=W_{\varphi^{**}}
.

(5)

If
x\in\partial W_{\varphi}
and
\zeta
lies in the the normal cone to
W_{\varphi}
at
x
, then
x\cdot\zeta=\varphi^{**}(\zeta)
.

Proof.

Convexity and closedness of
W_{\varphi}
and
0\in B_{c}\subseteq W_{\varphi}
for
c>0
given by (
2.1
) are immediate. Boundedness follows in our set-up from
-\varphi(-e_{k})\leq x_{k}\leq\varphi(e_{k})
for
k=1,\ldots,n
for any
x\in W_{\varphi}
. This proves (
1
). Now, (
2
)–(
5
)
are found in
[
8
, Propositions 3.4 & 3.5]
. A careful insepection of the proofs in
[
8
]
reveals that the relevant assertions do not need continuity of
\varphi
(which is assumed there). The statement in (
5
) follows since, by (
3
),
\zeta\in\partial\varphi^{*}(x)
, whence the Fenchel-Young identity implies
x\cdot\zeta=\varphi^{*}(x)+\varphi^{**}(\zeta)
and so
x\cdot\zeta=\varphi^{**}(\zeta)
by (
2
).
∎

Winterbottom shape

We now return to our convex, positively
1
-homogeneous and lower bounded
\varphi
and set

\displaystyle W_{\lambda,\varphi}=W_{\varphi}\cap\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\colon x_{d}\geq-\lambda\}\,.

If
|W_{\lambda,\varphi}|>0
, the
Winterbottom set
with volume
v>0
is then defined as

(2.2)

\displaystyle W_{\lambda,\varphi}(v)=\left(\frac{v}{|W_{\lambda,\varphi}|}\right)^{\frac{1}{d}}\left(W_{\lambda,\varphi}+\lambda e_{d}\right)\,.

Minimizers

We are describing quickly the different regimes for
\lambda
:

(1)

Complete drying:

\lambda\geq\varphi(-e_{d})
. In this case
W_{\lambda,\varphi}=W_{\varphi}
. In Remark
2.4
below we will see that minimizers to
F_{\lambda,\varphi}
for a given volume are those of the unconstrained system without substrate.

(2)

Partial drying/wetting:

\lambda\in\left(-\varphi(e_{d}),\varphi(-e_{d})\right)
. Here we have
W_{\lambda,\varphi}\subsetneq W_{\varphi}
as shown in Lemma
3.1
below. Minimality of the Winterbottom shape will be established in Theorem
2.3
below.

(3)

Complete wetting:

\lambda\leq-\varphi(e_{d})
. Here
W_{\lambda,\varphi}=\emptyset
. In Remark
2.4
below we will see that solutions to (
1.4
) do not exist if
\lambda<-\varphi(e_{d})
as “wetting” configurations that intersect large parts of
H
have arbitrarily small energy. Generically there are no minimizers in the special case
\lambda=-\varphi(e_{d})
either, while here for particular
\varphi
(even non-unique) minimizers might exist.

The first main theorem is the following characterization of minimizers in the partial drying/wetting regime.

Theorem 2.3
.

Let
\lambda\in\left(-\varphi(e_{d}),\varphi(-e_{d})\right)
and
v>0
. Then

\displaystyle F_{\lambda,\varphi}(W_{\lambda,\varphi}(v))\leq F_{\lambda,\varphi}(E)

for all
E\subseteq H^{+}
sets of finite perimeter such that
|E|=v
. Moreover, equality holds if and only if there exists
\tau\in H
such that
|E\Delta\left(W_{\lambda,\varphi}(v)+\tau\right)|=0
.

Remark 2.4
.

(1)

Complete drying:

\lambda\geq\varphi(-e_{d})
. Comparison to the unconstrained case shows that
E
is a solution to (
1.4
) if and only if
|E\Delta\left(W_{\lambda,\varphi}(v)+\tau\right)|=0
(recall definition (
2.2
) of
W_{\lambda,\varphi}(v)
) for some
\tau\in H^{+}
(if
\lambda>\varphi(-e_{d})
and
\mathcal{H}^{d-1}(\{x\in W_{\varphi}:x_{d}=-\varphi(-e_{d})\})>0
), respectively,
\tau\in H^{+}\cup H
(if
\lambda=\varphi(-e_{d})
or
\mathcal{H}^{d-1}(\{x\in W_{\varphi}:x_{d}=-\varphi(-e_{d})\})=0
).

(2)

Complete drying and partial drying/partial wetting:

\lambda>-\varphi(e_{d})
. For every set of finite perimeter
E\subseteq H^{+}
one has

(2.3)

\displaystyle\int_{\partial^{*}E\cap H^{+}}\varphi(\nu_{E})\,\mathrm{d}\mathcal{H}^{d-1}\geq\varphi\Big(\int_{\partial^{*}E\cap H^{+}}\nu_{E}\,\mathrm{d}\mathcal{H}^{d-1}\Big)=\mathcal{H}^{d-1}(\partial^{*}E\cap H)\varphi(e_{d})\,,

where we have used Jensen’s inequality, the homogeneity of
\varphi
and the fact that, by the Gauss-Green Theorem for sets of finite perimeter,
\int_{\partial^{*}E}\nu_{E}\,\mathrm{d}\mathcal{H}^{d-1}=0
. This shows that
m_{\lambda,\varphi}
(cf. Remark
2.1
) is positive if
\lambda>-\varphi(e_{d})
.

(3)

Complete wetting:

\lambda\leq-\varphi(e_{d})
. In case
\lambda<-\varphi(e_{d})
one may consider cylindrical sets
E_{R}=(0,R)^{d-1}\times(0,v/R^{d-1})
with
R\to\infty
to see that
m_{\lambda,\varphi}=-\infty
. In particular, solutions to (
1.4
) do not exist. In case
\lambda=-\varphi(e_{d})
the trial configurations
E_{R}
show that
m_{\lambda,\varphi}\leq 0
. Together with (
2.3
) this implies
m_{\lambda,\varphi}=0
. However, in this case both existence and non-existence of minimizers might occur: If, e.g.,
\varphi
is strictly convex, the above argument shows that
F_{\lambda,\varphi}(E)>0
for
|E|>0
. If, by way of contrast,
\varphi
is affine near
e_{d}
the above computation shows that for a spherical cap
C_{\varepsilon}=\{x\in B_{1}(0):x_{d}\geq 1-\varepsilon\}
,
0<\varepsilon\ll 1
, the set
E=\frac{v}{|C_{\varepsilon}|}(C_{\varepsilon}-(1-\varepsilon)e_{d})
has
F_{\lambda,\varphi}(E)=0
.

(4)

Young’s law:
The relation (
1.6
) can be seen as follows. Let
x\in\partial W_{\lambda,\varphi}
with
x_{d}=-\lambda
and
\nu(x)\in\mathcal{N}_{W_{\varphi}}(x)
. Then Proposition
2.2
(
3
) and the Fenchel-Young identity imply
x\in\partial\varphi^{**}(\nu(x))=\partial\varphi(\nu(x))
. For smooth
\varphi
this shows
\nabla\varphi(\nu(x))=x
and, hence,

\displaystyle\nabla\varphi(\nu(x))\cdot(-e_{d})=-x_{d}=\lambda\,.

Furthermore, we are able to establish the stability of the Winterbottom shape improving Theorem
2.3
. This is formulated in our second main theorem.

Theorem 2.5
.

Let
\lambda\in\left(-\varphi(e_{d}),\varphi(-e_{d})\right)
and
v>0
. There is a constant
C>0
such that for every set
E\subseteq H^{+}
of finite perimeter with
|E|=v
there exists
\tau\in H
that verifies

\displaystyle\frac{|E\triangle(W_{\lambda,\varphi}(v)+\tau)|^{2}}{v^{2}}\leq C\left(\frac{F_{\lambda,\varphi}(E)-F_{\lambda,\varphi}(W_{\lambda,\varphi}(v))}{F_{\lambda,\varphi}(W_{\lambda,\varphi}(v))}\right)\,.

3.
Proof of the main theorems

In this section we prove Theorem
2.3
and Theorem
2.5
. In the following we will make use of various unconstrained auxiliary functionals. If
\psi:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}
is a homogeneous function of degree one (not necessarily convex or bounded from below), for a set
E\subseteq H^{+}
of finite perimeter we define

\displaystyle P_{\psi}(E)=\int_{\partial^{*}E}\psi(\nu)\,\,\mathrm{d}\mathcal{H}^{d-1}\,.

Now suppose
\varphi_{\lambda}\colon\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}
is given by

\displaystyle\varphi_{\lambda}(\nu)=\begin{cases}\lambda t&\text{if }\nu=-te_{d}\text{ with }t>0\,,\\
\varphi(\nu)&\text{otherwise.}\end{cases}

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
-\varphi(e_{d})<\lambda<\varphi(-e_{d})
. Then,

\displaystyle P_{\varphi_{\lambda}}(E)\leq F_{\lambda,\varphi}(E)

for all sets of finite perimeter
E\subseteq H^{+}
with equality if and only if

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}^{d-1}\left(\{x\in\partial^{*}E\colon\nu_{E}(x)=-e_{d}\}\setminus H\right)=0\,.

In particular,
W_{\lambda,\varphi}(1)\subsetneq W_{\varphi}(1)
and

\displaystyle P_{\varphi_{\lambda}}(W_{\lambda,\varphi}(1))=F_{\lambda,\varphi}(W_{\lambda,\varphi}(1))\,.

Proof.

The first two statements are direct from the definition of
P_{\varphi_{\lambda}}
. As
W_{\lambda,\varphi}(1)
is convex and
W_{\lambda,\varphi}(1)\subseteq\{x_{d}\geq 0\}
, it suffices to show
W_{\lambda,\varphi}(1)\cap H\neq\emptyset
, or equivalently
W_{\varphi}\cap\{x_{d}=-\lambda\}\neq\emptyset
. In fact, if there exists
x\in\partial W_{\lambda,\varphi}(1)
such that
x_{d}>0
and
-e_{d}=\nu(x)\in\mathcal{N}_{W_{\lambda,\varphi}(1)}(x)
, then

\displaystyle-x_{d}=x\cdot(-e_{d})\geq y\cdot(-e_{d})=-y_{d}\quad\forall y\in W_{\lambda,\varphi}(1)\iff y_{d}\geq x_{d}>0\quad\forall y\in W_{\lambda,\varphi}(1)\,.

This contradicts
W_{\lambda,\varphi}(1)\cap H\neq\emptyset
. In order to see that
W_{\varphi}\cap\{x_{d}=-\lambda\}\neq\emptyset
we choose
x_{+},x_{-}\in W_{\varphi}
such that
\pm e_{d}\in\mathcal{N}_{W_{\varphi}}(x_{\pm})
. Then, by Proposition
2.2
(
5
) we have
(x_{\pm})_{d}=\pm x_{\pm}\cdot(\pm e_{d})=\pm\varphi(\pm e_{d})
. Thus by convexity of
W_{\varphi}
there exists
x\in W_{\varphi}
such that
x_{d}=-\lambda
.

∎

.

Let
0<\lambda<\varphi(-e_{d})

\displaystyle P_{\varphi_{\lambda}^{**}}(W_{\lambda,\varphi}(1))=P_{\varphi_{\lambda}}(W_{\lambda,\varphi}(1))\leq P_{\varphi_{\lambda}}(E)

for all sets of finite perimeter

such that

\tau\in H^{+}\cup H
such that

\displaystyle\nu_{W_{\lambda,\varphi}}(x)=\begin{cases}\nu_{W_{\varphi}}(x)\neq-e_{d}&\mbox{if }x_{d}>-\lambda\,,\\
-e_{d}&\mbox{if }x_{d}=-\lambda\,.\end{cases}

Since

is bounded from below and that

is the Wulff set of

. By Proposition

(3.2)

\displaystyle W_{\lambda,\varphi}=W_{\varphi_{\lambda}^{**}}\,.

Let

, we apply Proposition

) to both
\varphi_{\lambda}^{**}
and

\varphi_{\lambda}^{**}(\nu_{W_{\lambda,\varphi}}(x))=x\cdot(-e_{d})=\lambda\,=\varphi_{\lambda}(\nu_{W_{\lambda,\varphi}}(x)),

\displaystyle P_{\varphi_{\lambda}^{**}}(W_{\lambda,\varphi})=P_{\varphi_{\lambda}}(W_{\lambda,\varphi})\,.

We can now conclude by referring to known results

]
on the unconstrained functional

\displaystyle P_{\varphi_{\lambda}}(W_{\lambda,\varphi}(1))=P_{\varphi^{**}_{\lambda}}(W_{\lambda,\varphi}(1))=P_{\varphi^{**}_{\lambda}}(|W_{\varphi_{\lambda}^{**}}|^{-1/d}W_{\varphi_{\lambda}^{**}})\leq P_{\varphi^{**}_{\lambda}}(E)\leq P_{\varphi_{\lambda}}(E)

Let

Furthermore, there exists

we have that

(3.5)

\displaystyle{\rm(i)}\penalty\ 0<\lambda^{\prime}<\varphi_{x_{0}}(-e_{d})\,\quad\text{and}\quad{\rm(ii)}\penalty\ P_{(\varphi_{x_{0}})_{\lambda^{\prime}}}(E)=P_{\varphi_{\lambda}}(E)\,.

In addition, we have

(3.6)

\displaystyle{\rm(i)}\penalty\ x\in W_{\varphi}\iff x-x_{0}\in W_{{\varphi}_{x_{0}}}\quad\text{and}\quad{\rm(ii)}\penalty\ W_{\lambda^{\prime},\varphi_{x_{0}}}(1)=W_{\lambda,\varphi}(1)+\tau

for
\tau=|W_{\lambda,\varphi}|^{-1/d}(-x_{0}+(x_{0}\cdot e_{d})e_{d})\in H
.

Proof.

We first prove (
3.4
). As
\varphi_{x_{0}}
is positively
1
-homogeneous, it suffices to prove the claim for
\nu\in\mathbb{S}^{d-1}
. By construction of
W_{\varphi}
for all
x\in W_{\varphi}
we have
x\cdot\nu\leq\varphi(\nu)
and hence

\displaystyle(x-x_{0})\cdot\nu\leq\varphi(\nu)-x_{0}\cdot\nu=\varphi_{x_{0}}(\nu)\quad\text{for all }\nu\in\mathbb{S}^{d-1}\,.

Since
x_{0}\in\mathrm{int}\,(W_{\varphi})
there is
\varepsilon>0
such that
\overline{B}_{\varepsilon}(x_{0})\subseteq W_{\varphi}
and thus choosing
x_{\varepsilon,\nu}=x_{0}+\varepsilon\nu\in\overline{B}_{\varepsilon}(x_{0})
we find

\displaystyle\varepsilon=(x_{\varepsilon,\nu}-x_{0})\cdot\nu\leq\varphi_{x_{0}}(\nu)\quad\text{for all }\nu\in\mathbb{S}^{d-1}\,.

This shows (
3.4
).
Next, we prove (
3.5
)(i). To see this, observe

\displaystyle 0<\lambda^{\prime}<\varphi_{x_{0}}(-e_{d})\iff-x_{0}\cdot e_{d}<\lambda<\varphi(-e_{d})\,.

By assumption,
\lambda<\varphi(-e_{d})
. By choosing an element
\bar{x}
with maximal last component in the compact set
W_{\varphi}
and observing that
e_{d}\in\mathcal{N}_{W_{\varphi}}(\bar{x})
, with the help of Proposition
2.2
(
5
) we also get

\displaystyle-\max\{x_{0}\cdot e_{d}\colon x_{0}\in W_{\varphi}\}=-\bar{x}\cdot e_{d}=-\varphi(e_{d})\,.

Since
\lambda>-\varphi(e_{d})
, there exists
x_{0}\in\mathrm{int}\,(W_{\varphi})
such that
-x_{0}\cdot e_{d}<\lambda
, too, as claimed. Next, we show (
3.5
)(ii). To this end, note that
(\varphi_{x_{0}})_{\lambda^{\prime}}(\nu)=\varphi_{\lambda}(\nu)-x_{0}\cdot\nu
for all
\nu\in\mathbb{S}^{d-1}
since

(\varphi_{x_{0}})_{\lambda^{\prime}}(\nu)=\left\{\begin{aligned} \varphi_{x_{0}}(\nu)&&=&&\varphi(\nu)-x_{0}\cdot\nu&&=&&\varphi_{\lambda}(\nu)-x_{0}\cdot\nu&&\penalty\ \text{ if }\nu\neq-e_{d}\,,\\
\lambda^{\prime}&&=&&\lambda-x_{0}\cdot(-e_{d})&&=&&\varphi_{\lambda}(\nu)-x_{0}\cdot\nu&&\penalty\ \text{ if }\nu=-e_{d}\,.\end{aligned}\right.

Now, by the Gauss-Green Theorem for sets of finite perimeter, for
E
a set of finite perimeter we obtain

\displaystyle P_{(\varphi_{x_{0}})_{\lambda^{\prime}}}(E)

\displaystyle=\int_{\partial^{*}E}(\varphi_{x_{0}})_{\lambda^{\prime}}(\nu)\,\mathrm{d}\mathcal{H}^{d-1}=\int_{\partial^{*}E}\varphi_{\lambda}(\nu)-x_{0}\cdot\nu\,\mathrm{d}\mathcal{H}^{d-1}

\displaystyle=\int_{\partial^{*}E}\varphi_{\lambda}(\nu)\,\mathrm{d}\mathcal{H}^{d-1}-\int_{E}\mathrm{div}(x_{0})\,\mathrm{d}x=\int_{\partial^{*}E}\varphi_{\lambda}(\nu)\,\mathrm{d}\mathcal{H}^{d-1}=P_{\varphi_{\lambda}}(E)\,,

where we used that
\mathrm{div}(x_{0})=0
. To see (
3.6
)(i), it suffices to note that

\displaystyle x\in W_{\varphi}

\displaystyle\iff x\cdot\nu\leq\varphi(\nu)\quad\forall\,\nu\in\mathbb{S}^{d-1}\iff(x-x_{0})\cdot\nu\leq\varphi(\nu)-x_{0}\cdot\nu\quad\forall\,\nu\in\mathbb{S}^{d-1}

\displaystyle\iff(x-x_{0})\cdot\nu\leq\varphi_{x_{0}}(\nu)\quad\forall\,\nu\in\mathbb{S}^{d-1}\iff x-x_{0}\in W_{\varphi_{x_{0}}}\,.

In order to prove (
3.6
)(ii), Note that

\displaystyle W_{\lambda^{\prime},\varphi_{x_{0}}}

\displaystyle=\{x\in W_{\varphi_{x_{0}}}\colon x_{d}\geq-\lambda^{\prime}\}=\{x\in W_{\varphi_{x_{0}}}\colon x_{d}\geq-\lambda-x_{0}\cdot e_{d}\}

\displaystyle=\{x\in W_{\varphi}-x_{0}\colon(x+x_{0})_{d}\geq-\lambda\}=\{x\in W_{\varphi}\colon x_{d}\geq-\lambda\}-x_{0}=W_{\lambda,\varphi}-x_{0}\,.

Therefore,

\displaystyle W_{\lambda^{\prime},\varphi_{x_{0}}}(1)

\displaystyle=|W_{\lambda^{\prime},\varphi_{x_{0}}}|^{-1/d}(W_{\lambda^{\prime},\varphi_{x_{0}}}+\lambda^{\prime}e_{d})=|W_{\lambda,\varphi}|^{-1/d}(W_{\lambda,\varphi}-x_{0}+(\lambda+x_{0}\cdot e_{d})e_{d})

\displaystyle=|W_{\lambda,\varphi}|^{-1/d}(W_{\lambda,\varphi}+\lambda\,e_{d}-x_{0}+(x_{0}\cdot e_{d})e_{d})=W_{\lambda,\varphi}(1)+\tau\,,

where
\tau=|W_{\lambda,\varphi}|^{-1/d}(-x_{0}+(x_{0}\cdot e_{d})e_{d})\in H
.
∎

We are now in position to prove the main Theorems.

Proof of Theorem
2.3
.

By Remark
2.1
it suffices to prove the theorem only for
v=1
. To this end, let
E\subseteq H^{+}
be such that
|E|=1
. We choose
x_{0}\in\mathrm{int}\,(W_{\varphi})
and
\lambda^{\prime}=\lambda+x_{0}\cdot e_{d}
such that the assertions of Lemma
3.3
hold true. Using Lemma
3.1
,(
3.5
)(ii), and (
3.6
)(ii) we obtain

\displaystyle F_{\lambda,\varphi}(W_{\lambda,\varphi}(1))

\displaystyle=P_{\varphi_{\lambda}}(W_{\lambda,\varphi}(1))=P_{\varphi_{\lambda}}(W_{\lambda^{\prime},\varphi_{x_{0}}}(1))=P_{(\varphi_{x_{0}})_{\lambda^{\prime}}}(W_{\lambda^{\prime},\varphi_{x_{0}}}(1))

and, for any set
E
of finite perimeter,

\displaystyle F_{\lambda,\varphi}(E)

\displaystyle\geq P_{\varphi_{\lambda}}(E)=P_{(\varphi_{x_{0}})_{\lambda^{\prime}}}(E)\,.

Finally applying Lemma
3.2
to
\varphi_{x_{0}}
and the positive
\lambda^{\prime}
gives
P_{(\varphi_{x_{0}})_{\lambda^{\prime}}}(W_{\lambda^{\prime},\varphi_{x_{0}}}(1))\leq P_{(\varphi_{x_{0}})_{\lambda^{\prime}}}(E)
if
|E|=1
, and we have shown

\displaystyle F_{\lambda,\varphi}(W_{\lambda,\varphi}(1))\leq F_{\lambda,\varphi}(E)

for such
E
. In case of equality, we also have
P_{(\varphi_{x_{0}})_{\lambda^{\prime}}}(W_{\lambda^{\prime},\varphi_{x_{0}}}(1))=P_{(\varphi_{x_{0}})_{\lambda^{\prime}}}(E)
and Lemma
3.2
implies
E=W_{\lambda^{\prime},\varphi_{x_{0}}}(1)+\tau^{\prime}=W_{\lambda,\varphi}(1)+\tau+\tau^{\prime}
for
\tau
as above and some
\tau^{\prime}\in H^{+}\cup H
. As also
P_{\varphi_{\lambda}}(E)=F_{\lambda,\varphi}(E)
, necessarily
\tau+\tau^{\prime}\in H
. This concludes the proof.
∎

We are now in position to prove Theorem
2.5
establishing the stability of the Winterbottom shape.

Proof of Theorem
2.5
.

Let
x_{0}\in\mathrm{int}(W_{\varphi})
and
\lambda^{\prime}=\lambda+x_{0}\cdot e_{d}
be such that the assertions of Lemma
3.3
hold true. By properly rescaling we may without loss of generality assume
|E|=1
. In the following, we set
W=W_{\lambda,\varphi}(1)
and
W^{\prime}=W_{\lambda^{\prime},\varphi_{x_{0}}}(1)
. We first want to prove that there exists
\tau\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
such that

(3.7)

\displaystyle|E\triangle(W+\tau)|^{2}\leq C(F_{\lambda,\varphi}(E)-F_{\lambda,\varphi}(W))\,.

The quantitative isoperimetric inequality
[
7
, Theorem 1.1]
for the unconstrained functional
P_{(\varphi_{x_{0}})_{\lambda^{\prime}}^{**}}
yields
C>0
such that for a suitable
\tau^{\prime}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
we have

\displaystyle|E\Delta(W^{\prime}+\tau^{\prime})|^{2}\leq C\left(P_{(\varphi_{x_{0}})_{\lambda^{\prime}}^{**}}(E)-P_{(\varphi_{x_{0}})_{\lambda^{\prime}}^{**}}(W^{\prime})\right)\,.

Recalling that, by (
3.6
)(ii), there holds
W=W^{\prime}+\hat{\tau}
for some
\hat{\tau}\in H
and setting
\tau=\tau^{\prime}-\hat{\tau}
, we obtain

(3.8)

\displaystyle|E\Delta(W+\tau)|^{2}=|E\Delta(W^{\prime}+\tau^{\prime})|^{2}\leq C\left(P_{(\varphi_{x_{0}})_{\lambda^{\prime}}^{**}}(E)-P_{(\varphi_{x_{0}})_{\lambda^{\prime}}^{**}}(W^{\prime})\right)\,.

Now, by Lemma
3.1
, Lemma
3.3
, and, as
\lambda^{\prime}>0
, by Lemma
3.2
we have

\displaystyle P_{(\varphi_{x_{0}})_{\lambda^{\prime}}^{**}}(E)\leq P_{(\varphi_{x_{0}})_{\lambda^{\prime}}}(E)=P_{\varphi_{\lambda}}(E)\leq F_{\lambda,\varphi}(E)

and

\displaystyle P_{(\varphi_{x_{0}})_{\lambda^{\prime}}^{**}}(W^{\prime})=P_{(\varphi_{x_{0}})_{\lambda^{\prime}}}(W^{\prime})=P_{\varphi_{\lambda}}(W^{\prime})=P_{\varphi_{\lambda}}(W)=F_{\lambda,\varphi}(W)\,,

where we again used that
W=W^{\prime}+\hat{\tau}
with
\hat{\tau}\in H
. Therefore

\displaystyle P_{(\varphi_{x_{0}})_{\lambda^{\prime}}^{**}}(E)-P_{(\varphi_{x_{0}})_{\lambda^{\prime}}^{**}}(W^{\prime})\leq F_{\lambda,\varphi}(E)-F_{\lambda,\varphi}(W)\,.

Together with (
3.8
), this yields (
3.7
).

The challenge that remains is to show that
\tau
can be chosen in
H
, i.e., such that
\tau_{d}=0
. In order to do that we distinguish cases.

Case 1.
Assume (
3.7
) holds true with
\tau_{d}<0
. By convexity of
W
we have

\displaystyle|E\triangle(W+\tau)|\geq|\{x\in\tau+W\colon x_{d}<0\}|=|\{x\in W\colon x_{d}<-\tau_{d}\}|\geq c\min\{-\tau_{d},1\}

for a constant
c>0
. Since also

\displaystyle|(W+\tau)\triangle(W+\tau-\tau_{d}e_{d})|\leq C\min\{-\tau_{d},1\}\,

(note that trivially
|(W+\tau)\triangle(W+\tau-\tau_{d}e_{d})|\leq 2|W|\leq C
), we find that

\displaystyle|E\triangle(W+\tau-\tau_{d}e_{d})|\leq|E\triangle(W+\tau)|+|(W+\tau)\triangle(W+\tau-\tau_{d}e_{d})|=(1+Cc^{-1})|E\triangle(W+\tau)|\,.

This implies the claim in view of (
3.7
).

Case 2.
Assume (
3.7
) holds true with
\tau_{d}>0
. In the following we denote by

W_{\varphi}(v)=\left(\frac{v}{|W_{\varphi}|}\right)^{\frac{1}{d}}W_{\varphi}

the rescaled Wulff set of
\varphi
with volume
v>0
. Since in the partial drying/wetting regime
W_{\varphi}(1)
(placed in
H^{+}
) is not optimal for
F_{\lambda,\varphi}
, we have
P_{\varphi}(W_{\varphi}(1))>F_{\lambda,\varphi}(W_{\lambda,\varphi}(1))
and so we can define a positive constant
c_{0}
by asking that

\displaystyle\left(1-c_{0}\right)^{\frac{d-1}{d}}P_{\varphi}(W_{\varphi}(1))-(\varphi(e_{d})+\varphi(-e_{d}))c_{0}=F_{\lambda,\varphi}(W_{\lambda,\varphi}(1))+\delta\,,

where
\delta:=\frac{1}{2}(P_{\varphi}(W_{\varphi}(1))-F_{\lambda,\varphi}(W_{\lambda,\varphi}(1))>0
.

Case 2a.
Suppose that
|\{x\in E\colon x_{d}<\tau_{d}\}|\geq c_{0}\min\{\tau_{d},1\}
. Then

\displaystyle|E\triangle(W+\tau)|\geq c_{0}\min\{\tau_{d},1\}

and, similarly as above, from

\displaystyle|(W+\tau)\triangle(W+\tau-\tau_{d}e_{d})|\leq C\min\{\tau_{d},1\},

we conclude

\displaystyle|E\triangle(W+\tau-\tau_{d}e_{d})|\leq|E\triangle(W+\tau)|+|(W+\tau)\triangle(W+\tau-\tau_{d}e_{d})|=(1+Cc_{0}^{-1})|E\triangle(W+\tau)|\,.

Again this finishes the proof with the help of
(
3.7
).

Case 2b.
Assume that
|\{x\in E\colon x_{d}<\tau_{d}\}|<c_{0}\min\{\tau_{d},1\}
.
Then there is an
\varepsilon\in(0,\tau_{d})
such that

\mathcal{H}^{d-1}(E\cap(\varepsilon e_{d}+H))\leq c_{0}\min\{1,\tau_{d}^{-1}\}.

We cut along this hyperplane and set
\tilde{E}=\{x\in E:x_{d}>\varepsilon\}
. With this set we then get

F_{\lambda,\varphi}(E)\geq F_{\lambda,\varphi}(E\setminus\tilde{E})+P_{\varphi}(\tilde{E})-(\varphi(e_{d})+\varphi(-e_{d}))\mathcal{H}^{d-1}(E\cap(\varepsilon e_{d}+H)).

By Remark
2.4
(
2
), the first term on the right hand side is non-negative and we get the bound

\displaystyle F_{\lambda,\varphi}(E)

\displaystyle\geq P_{\varphi}(\tilde{E})-(\varphi(e_{d})+\varphi(-e_{d}))c_{0}\min\{1,\tau_{d}^{-1}\}

\displaystyle\geq P_{\varphi}(W_{\varphi}(|\tilde{E}|))-(\varphi(e_{d})+\varphi(-e_{d}))c_{0}

\displaystyle=|\tilde{E}|^{\frac{d-1}{d}}P_{\varphi}(W_{\varphi}(1))-(\varphi(e_{d})+\varphi(-e_{d}))c_{0}\,,

where in the second step we have used that the Wulff shape
W_{\varphi}(|\tilde{E}|)
minimizes the unconstrained functional
P_{\varphi}
among sets of volume
|\tilde{E}|
. As by assumption
|\tilde{E}|\geq 1-c_{0}
, it follows that

(3.9)

\displaystyle F_{\lambda,\varphi}(E)

\displaystyle\geq\left(1-c_{0}\right)^{\frac{d-1}{d}}P_{\varphi}(W_{\varphi}(1))-(\varphi(e_{d})+\varphi(-e_{d}))c_{0}\geq F_{\lambda,\varphi}(W_{\lambda,\varphi}(1))+\delta

by our choice of
c_{0}
. Thus, for any
C\geq 4\delta^{-1}
we have even for
\tau=0

|E\triangle W|^{2}\leq(|E|+|W|)^{2}=4\leq C(F_{\lambda,\varphi}(E)-F_{\lambda,\varphi}(W)).\qed

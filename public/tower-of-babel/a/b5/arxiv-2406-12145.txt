Minimax Linear Regression under the Quantile Risk
Ayoub El Hanchi, Chris J. Maddison, Murat A. Erdogdu

Abstract
We study the problem of designing minimax procedures in linear regression under the quantile risk. We start by considering the realizable setting with independent Gaussian noise, where for any given noise level and distribution of inputs, we obtain the exact minimax quantile risk for a rich family of error functions and establish the minimaxity of OLS. This improves on the lower bounds obtained by Lecué and Mendelson 2016 and Mendelson 2017 for the special case of square error, and provides us with a lower bound on the minimax quantile risk over larger sets of distributions. Under the square error and a fourth moment assumption on the distribution of inputs, we show that this lower bound is tight over a larger class of problems. Specifically, we prove a matching upper bound on the worst-case quantile risk of a variant of the procedure proposed by Lecué and Lerasle 2020 , thereby establishing its minimaxity, up to absolute constants. We illustrate the usefulness of our approach by extending this result to all p p -th power error functions for p ∈ ( 2 , ∞ ) p\in(2,\infty) . Along the way, we develop a generic analogue to the classical Bayesian method for lower bounding the minimax risk when working with the quantile risk, as well as a tight characterization of the quantiles of the smallest eigenvalue of the sample covariance matrix.



keywords
minimax procedures, linear regression, sample covariance matrix, quantile risk.

1
Introduction

We study the problem of designing minimax procedures in linear regression under the quantile risk over large classes of distributions. Specifically, for some
d\in\mathbb{N}
, there is an input random vector
X\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
and an output random variable
Y\in\mathbb{R}
, and we are provided with
n\in\mathbb{N}
i.i.d. samples
(X_{i},Y_{i})_{i=1}^{n}
from their joint distribution
P
, with the goal of constructing a predictor of
Y
given
X
. We consider the set of linear predictors
\left\{x\mapsto\langle w,x\rangle\mid w\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\right\}
, and measure the error of a predictor
w\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
on an input/output pair
(X,Y)
through
e(\langle w,X\rangle-Y)
for an error function of our choice
e:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
. We evaluate the overall error of a predictor
w\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
through the expected error
E(w)\vcentcolon=\Exp\left[e(\langle w,X\rangle-Y)\right]
, and define
\mathcal{E}(w)\vcentcolon=E(w)-\inf_{v\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}E(v)
.

For a user-chosen failure probability
\delta\in(0,1)
, we evaluate the performance of a procedure
\hat{w}_{n,\delta}:(\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R})^{n}\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
on a particular distribution
P
through its quantile risk

R_{n,\delta}(P,\hat{w}_{n,\delta})\vcentcolon=Q_{\mathcal{E}(\hat{w}_{n,\delta})}(1-\delta)=\inf\left\{t\geq 0\,\middle|\,\Prob\left\lparen\mathcal{E}(\hat{w}_{n,\delta})\leq t\right\rparen\geq 1-\delta\right\},

(1)

where we shortened
\hat{w}_{n,\delta}((X_{i},Y_{i})_{i=1}^{n})
to
\hat{w}_{n,\delta}
.
We consider the scenario where all that is known about
P
is that it belongs to a class of distributions
\mathcal{P}
on
\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}
. This justifies evaluating the overall performance of a procedure through its worst-case risk

R_{n,\delta}(\mathcal{P},\hat{w}_{n,\delta})\vcentcolon=\sup_{P\in\mathcal{P}}R_{n,\delta}(P,\hat{w}_{n,\delta}).

Our goal is to characterize the minimax risk
R^{*}_{n,\delta}(\mathcal{P})\vcentcolon=\inf_{\hat{w}_{n,\delta}}R_{n,\delta}(\mathcal{P},\hat{w}_{n,\delta})
and design minimax procedures for rich classes of distributions and error functions.

Note on terminology.

In this paper, we reserve the terms ‘risk’ and ‘loss’ to refer to the corresponding decision-theoretic concepts, see e.g.
Lehmann and Casella 2006
for background on these notions. To avoid any confusion, we have used the terms ‘error’ and ‘expected error’ to refer to what is commonly called ‘prediction loss’ and ‘prediction risk’ in statistical learning theory.

Motivation.

Our motivation for studying this problem has its roots in the work of
Catoni 2012
, who showed that the empirical mean is no longer minimax over the set of all distributions with finite variance under the square loss if one replaces the classical notion of risk, given by the expected loss, with the quantile risk, given by the
1-\delta
quantile of the loss, for any user-chosen failure probability
\delta\in(0,1)
. Since then, minimax procedures were discovered for this problem
(
Devroye et al. 2016
;
Lee and Valiant 2022
)
, and there has been a lot of effort to construct minimax procedures under this new notion of risk for a variety of statistical problems
(
Lugosi and Mendelson 2019a
;
Lugosi and Mendelson 2019b
;
Mendelson and Zhivotovskiy 2020
)
. We view our work as part of this larger effort.

Known results.

To understand why previous results are insufficient to accomplish our stated goal, let us briefly review the most relevant ones. Most previous work has focused on the case of square error
e(t)=t^{2}/2

(
Audibert and Catoni 2011
;
Hsu and Sabato 2016
;
Lugosi and Mendelson 2019a
;
Lecué and Lerasle 2020
)
. In this case, a natural class of distributions to consider is

\mathcal{P}_{2}(P_{X},\sigma^{2})\vcentcolon=\left\{P\,\middle|\,(X,Y)\sim P:X\sim P_{X}\text{ and }\esssup(\Exp[\xi^{2}\mid X])\leq\sigma^{2}\right\},

(2)

for a given distribution
P_{X}
of inputs, noise level
\sigma^{2}\in(0,\infty)
, and where
\xi\vcentcolon=Y-\langle w^{*},X\rangle
is the noise and
w^{*}
is the unique minimizer of the expected error
E(w)
under square error. The best lower bound on the minimax risk over this class has been obtained by considering the subclass

\mathcal{P}_{\text{Gauss}}(P_{X},\sigma^{2})\vcentcolon=\{P\mid(X,Y)\sim P:(X,\eta)\sim P_{X}\times\mathcal{N}(0,\sigma^{2}),Y=\langle w^{*},X\rangle+\eta\text{ for }w^{*}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\}.

The following results yield the best upper and lower bounds on the minimax risk over
\mathcal{P}_{2}(P_{X},\sigma^{2})
.

Proposition 1

(
Lecué and Mendelson 2016
;
Mendelson 2017
)
.

Suppose that
e(t)=t^{2}/2
. There exist absolute constants
C,c>0
such that for all
\delta\in(0,1/8)
, it holds that

R^{*}_{n,\delta}(\mathcal{P}_{\textrm{Gauss}}(P_{X},\sigma^{2}))\geq\begin{cases}\infty&if $n\leq d/C$,\\
c\cdot\frac{\sigma^{2}(d+\log(1/\delta))}{n}&otherwise.\end{cases}

Proposition 2

(
Oliveira and Resende 2023
)
.

Suppose that
e(t)=t^{2}/2
. There exists a procedure
\hat{w}_{n,\delta}
and absolute constants
C,c>0
such that the following holds. If

n\geq c\cdot\theta^{2}(P_{X})(d+\log(1/\delta)),\quad\text{ where }\quad\theta(P_{X})\vcentcolon=\sup_{w\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus\left\{0\right\}}\frac{\Exp\left[\langle w,X\rangle^{2}\right]^{1/2}}{\Exp\left[\lvert\langle w,X\rangle\rvert\right]},

then

R_{n,\delta}(\mathcal{P}_{2}(P_{X},\sigma^{2}),\hat{w}_{n,\delta})\leq C\cdot\theta^{2}(P_{X})\cdot\frac{\sigma^{2}\cdot(d+\log(1/\delta))}{n}.

In the prescribed regime
(n,\delta)
stated in Proposition
2
, and on the set of distributions for which
\theta(P_{X})
is upper bounded by an absolute constant, the combination of Propositions
1
and
2
proves the minimaxity, up to an absolute constant, of the procedure in Proposition
2
over
\mathcal{P}_{2}(P_{X},\sigma^{2})
.

Unfortunately, this minimaxity result is unsatisfactory for two important reasons. First, the set of distributions for which
\theta(P_{X})
is bounded by an absolute constant is both difficult to characterize and too small to cover classes of problems of interest. Indeed, by using the relationship between
\theta(P_{X})
and the small-ball constants
(
Lecué and Lerasle 2019
)
, and using the lower bounds derived on the latter by
Saumard 2018
, it is possible to derive dimension-dependent lower bounds on
\theta(P_{X})
for standard linear regression problems with bounded inputs. Second, this minimaxity result is specific to the square error function. While procedures with guarantees have been studied for other error functions
(
Chinot et al. 2020
)
, no lower bounds are known outside of Proposition
1
.

Contributions.

Below we summarize our main results related to linear regression.

•

We compute the
exact
minimax quantile risk over the class
\mathcal{P}_{\text{Gauss}}(P_{X},\sigma^{2})
for a rich set of error functions, and show that OLS is minimax in this setting (Theorem
3
). We deduce from this result the asymptotic minimax quantile risk over this class (Proposition
4
).

•

Focusing on the non-asymptotic setting with
e(t)=t^{2}/2
, we complement our exact computation with tight upper and lower bounds (Proposition
7
). We then recover the lower bound of Proposition
1
and identify a setting in which it is tight (Corollary
9
). We give an analogous result under the error function
e(t)=\lvert t\rvert^{p}/[p(p-1)]
for
p\in(2,\infty)
(Proposition
10
).

•

We then turn to finding minimax procedures on larger classes of distributions. For the square error, we establish the minimaxity, up to an absolute constant, of a variant of the min-max regression procedure
(
Audibert and Catoni 2011
;
Lecué and Lerasle 2020
)
over the class
\mathcal{P}_{2}(P_{X},\sigma^{2})
, and under a fourth moment assumption on
P_{X}
(Theorem
11
).

•

Finally, we study minimax linear regression under the error function
e(t)=\lvert t\rvert^{p}/[p(p-1)]
for
p\in(2,\infty)
. Guided by our results, we identify a rich class of distributions analogous to
\mathcal{P}_{2}(P_{X},\sigma^{2})
, and show that the min-max regression procedure is minimax, up to a constant that depends only on
p
, and under a fourth moment assumption on
P_{X}
(Theorem
12
).

Our contributions on linear regression are supported by the following more general results.

•

We consider the quantile risk in full generality. We develop an analogue to the Bayesian method for lower bounding the minimax quantile risk (Theorem
13
). We then prove that the minimaxity of procedures under the quantile risk is invariant to strictly increasing left-continuous transformations of the loss (Proposition
14
).

•

We illustrate the generality of our methods by applying them to two unrelated problems: multivariate mean estimation with Gaussian data, in which we recover a strengthening of the recent result of
Depersin and Lecué 2022
(Proposition
15
), and variance estimation with Gaussian data and known mean, where we show that, surprisingly, the sample variance is suboptimal, and design a new minimax estimator (Proposition
16
).

•

We conclude by studying the smallest eigenvalue of the sample covariance matrix. We prove a new tight asymptotic lower bound on its quantiles, and a nearly matching fully non-asymptotic upper bound (Proposition
17
), both under a fourth moment assumption on
P_{X}
.

Organization

The rest of the paper is organized as follows. In Section
2
, we present our results on the minimax quantile risk over the class
\mathcal{P}_{\text{Gauss}}(P_{X},\sigma^{2})
. In Section
3
, we present new upper bounds on the worst-case quantile risk of the min-max regression procedure for the error functions
e(t)=\lvert t\rvert^{p}/[p(p-1)]
for
p\in[2,\infty)
, showing its minimaxity over suitable classes of distributions up to constants. In Section
4
we study the quantile risk in full generality. Finally, in Section
5
, we present our results on the smallest eigenvalue of the sample covariance matrix.

Notation.

We call a function
f:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
increasing if
x\leq x^{\prime}
implies
f(x)\leq f(x^{\prime})
. If
f:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
is an increasing function, we define its pseudo-inverse
f^{-}:[-\infty,\infty]\to[-\infty,\infty]
by
f^{-}(y)\vcentcolon=\inf\left\{x\in\mathbb{R}\,\middle|\,f(x)\geq y\right\}
. For a random variable
X:\Omega\to\mathbb{R}
, we denote its CDF by
F_{X}
and its quantile function by
Q_{X}\vcentcolon=F^{-}_{X}
. We allow random variables of the form
X:\Omega\to[0,\infty]
, but we restrict the definition of their CDFs to
[0,\infty)
. Without loss of generality, we assume throughout that the support of the distribution of inputs
P_{X}
is not contained in any hyperplane. We write
\Sigma=\Exp\left[XX^{T}\right]
for the covariance matrix of the random vector
X
. We write
a\asymp b
to mean that there exist absolute constants
C,c>0
such that
c\cdot b\leq a\leq C\cdot b
.

2
Minimax quantile risk over
\mathcal{P}_{{\textrm{Gauss}}}(P_{X},\sigma^{2})

The following is the main result of this section.

Theorem 3
.

Let
P_{X}
be a distribution on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
\sigma^{2}\in(0,\infty)
. Assume that
e
is strictly convex, differentiable, and symmetric i.e.
e(t)=e(-t)
for all
t\in\mathbb{R}
. Define, for
(X,\eta)\sim P_{X}\times\mathcal{N}(0,\sigma^{2})
,

\widetilde{E}(\Delta)\vcentcolon=\Exp\left[e(\langle\Delta,X\rangle+\eta)\right],\quad\quad\widetilde{\mathcal{E}}(\Delta)\vcentcolon=\widetilde{E}(\Delta)-\widetilde{E}(0).

If
P_{X}
is such that
\widetilde{E}
is finite everywhere and differentiable at
0
with
\nabla\widetilde{E}(0)=\Exp\left[\nabla e(\eta)\right]
, then

R_{n,\delta}^{*}(\mathcal{P}_{\text{Gauss}}(P_{X},\sigma^{2}))=Q_{\widetilde{\mathcal{E}}(Z)}(1-\delta),

where the random variable
Z
is jointly distributed with
(X_{i})_{i=1}^{n}\sim P_{X}^{n}
such that
Z\mid(X_{i})_{i=1}^{n}\sim\mathcal{N}(0,\frac{\sigma^{2}}{n}\widehat{\Sigma}_{n}^{-1})
on the event that the sample covariance matrix
\widehat{\Sigma}_{n}\vcentcolon=n^{-1}\sum_{i=1}^{n}X_{i}X_{i}^{T}
is invertible, otherwise
\widetilde{\mathcal{E}}(Z)\vcentcolon=\infty
. Moreover, all procedures satisfying the following condition are minimax

\hat{w}_{n,\delta}((X_{i},Y_{i})_{i=1}^{n})\in\argmin_{w\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}(\langle w,X_{i}\rangle-Y_{i})^{2}.

We make a few remarks about this result before interpreting its content. First, Theorem
3
improves on the best known comparable result, Proposition
1
, in two distinct ways: it provides the
exact
minimax risk over the class
\mathcal{P}_{\text{Gauss}}(P_{X},\sigma^{2})
for the error function
e(t)=t^{2}/2
, and it generalizes this result to a rich collection of error functions. Second, and as can readily be seen from the proof, the strict convexity hypothesis on
e(t)
in Theorem
3
can be weakened to the strict quasiconvexity of
E(w)
, and the strictness can be replaced by the existence of a unique minimizer of
E(w)
. Finally, the proof of Theorem
3
is based on the Bayesian method we develop in Theorem
13
, an adaptation of an argument of
Mourtada 2022
, and Anderson’s Lemma
(
Keener 2010
, e.g.)
.

While exact, the result in Theorem
3
is both difficult to interpret and hard to manipulate. In particular, the dependence of the minimax risk on the problem parameters
(n,\delta,P_{X},\sigma^{2})
as well as the error function
e
remains implicit in Theorem
3
. This is not too surprising as the error function can interact with the parameters of the problems in quite complicated ways.

In the rest of this section, we develop tools to make these dependencies explicit. Specifically, in Section
2.1
, we compute the asymptotic minimax risk for generic error functions as
n\to\infty
and show that it takes on a simple form. In Section
2.2
, we focus on the case of square error function, and identify a setting where the lower bound of Proposition
1
is tight. In Section
2.3
, we extend this result to the case of the
p
-th power error function for
p\in(2,\infty)
.

2.1
General error functions

The following result shows that under a mild assumption on the error function, the asymptotic minimax risk is a pleasingly simple function of the parameters of the problem. In particular, this result shows that the lower bound of Proposition
1
is asymptotically tight.

Proposition 4
.

Under the setup of Theorem
3
, further assume that
e
is twice differentiable and
\widetilde{E}
is twice differentiable at
0
with
\nabla^{2}\widetilde{E}(0)=\Exp\left[\nabla^{2}e(\eta)\right]
, and let
\alpha\vcentcolon=\Exp\left[e^{\prime\prime}(\eta)\right]/2
. Then

\lim_{n\to\infty}n\cdot R_{n,\delta}^{*}(\mathcal{P}_{\text{Gauss}}(P_{X},\sigma^{2}))=\sigma^{2}\alpha\cdot Q_{\lVert Z\rVert_{2}^{2}}(1-\delta)\asymp\sigma^{2}\alpha\cdot\left[d+\log(1/\delta)\right],

where
Z\sim\mathcal{N}(0,I_{d\times d})
, and where the relation
\asymp
holds when
\delta\in(0,1/2)
.

Non-asymptotically, and with no more assumptions on the error function, it is difficult to say much more about the minimax risk than Proposition
4
. However, determining when the minimax risk is infinite is tractable, as the next result shows.

Lemma 5
.

Under the setup of Theorem
3
, let
\varepsilon_{n}\vcentcolon=\Prob\left\lparen\rank(\widehat{\Sigma}_{n})<d\right\rparen
. Then

R_{n,\delta}^{*}(\mathcal{P}_{\text{Gauss}}(P_{X},\sigma^{2}))=\infty\Leftrightarrow\delta\leq\varepsilon_{n}\quad\text{ and }\quad\rho(P_{X})^{n}\leq\varepsilon_{n}\leq\left(\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{n}{d-1}\right)\rho(P_{X})^{n-d-1},

where
\rho(P_{X})\vcentcolon=\sup_{w\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus\left\{0\right\}}\Prob(\langle w,X\rangle=0)<1
.

The upper bound on
\varepsilon_{n}
as well as the statement
\rho(P_{X})<1
in Lemma
5
are due to
El Hanchi and Erdogdu 2023
. At a high level, Lemma
5
says that the range of failure probabilities for which the risk is infinite gets exponentially small as a function of
n
. This is in sharp contrast with the result of
Mourtada 2022
under the classical notion of risk and the square error, where it was shown that the minimax risk in that case is infinite for all sample sizes as soon as
\rho(P_{X})>0
.

2.2
Square error

We assume throughout this section that
e(t)=t^{2}/2
. We derive increasingly loose but more interpretable upper and lower bounds on the minimax risk in this setting. Our motivation is to better understand the influence of each of the parameters
(n,\delta,P_{X},\sigma^{2})
of the problem on the minimax risk. Practically, the main result of this section is the identification of a general sufficient condition under which the lower bound in Proposition
1
is tight. With that achievement to look forward to, we start with a simple Corollary of Theorem
3
.

Corollary 6
.

Under the setup of Theorem
3
,

R_{n,\delta}^{*}(\mathcal{P}_{\text{Gauss}}(P_{X},\sigma^{2}))=\frac{\sigma^{2}}{2n}\cdot Q_{\lVert Z\rVert_{2}^{2}}(1-\delta),

where the random variable
Z
is jointly distributed with
(X_{i})_{i=1}^{n}\sim P_{X}^{n}
such that
Z\mid(X_{i})_{i=1}^{n}\sim\mathcal{N}(0,\widetilde{\Sigma}_{n}^{-1})
on the event that the sample covariance matrix
\widehat{\Sigma}_{n}
is invertible, and where
\widetilde{\Sigma}_{n}=\Sigma^{-1/2}\widehat{\Sigma}_{n}\Sigma^{-1/2}
is the whitened sample covariance matrix; otherwise
\lVert Z\rVert_{2}^{2}\vcentcolon=\infty
.

Corollary
6
already makes explicit the dependence of the minimax risk on
(n,\sigma^{2})
, but the dependence on
(P_{X},\delta)
remains implicit. The next result is a step towards clarifying this relationship.

Proposition 7
.

Under the setup of Theorem
3
, and for all
\delta\in(0,(1-\varepsilon_{n})/4)
,

R_{n,\varepsilon_{n}+\delta}^{*}(\mathcal{P}_{\text{Gauss}}(P_{X},\sigma^{2}))\begin{cases}&\leq 2\cdot\frac{\sigma^{2}}{n}\left[Q_{\Tr\left\lparen\widetilde{\Sigma}_{n}^{-1}\right\rparen}(1-\varepsilon_{n}-\delta/2)+Q_{W}(1-\varepsilon_{n}-\delta/2)\right],\\
&\geq\frac{1}{6428}\cdot\frac{\sigma^{2}}{n}\left[Q_{\Tr\left\lparen\widetilde{\Sigma}_{n}^{-1}\right\rparen}(1-\varepsilon_{n}-4\delta)+Q_{W}(1-\varepsilon_{n}-4\delta)\right],\end{cases}

where we defined
\Tr(\widetilde{\Sigma}_{n}^{-1})\vcentcolon=\infty
when
\widetilde{\Sigma}_{n}
is not invertible, and
W
is a random variable jointly distributed with
(X_{i})_{i=1}^{n}\sim P_{X}^{n}
and with conditional distribution
W\mid(X_{i})_{i=1}^{n}\sim{\text{Exp}}(\lambda_{\textrm{min}}(\widetilde{\Sigma}_{n}))
, with the convention that the exponential distribution
{\text{Exp}}(0)
refers to the unit mass at
\infty
.

It is interesting to compare this result with the exact minimax risk under the classical notion of risk computed by
Mourtada 2022
, and given by
(\sigma^{2}/n)\cdot\Exp[\Tr({\widetilde{\Sigma}_{n}^{-1})}]
. Proposition
7
says that the minimax quantile risk is upper and lower bounded by a ‘strong’ term given by a quantile of
\Tr({\widetilde{\Sigma}_{n}^{-1})}
, and a ‘weak’ term governed by the distribution of
\lambda_{\textrm{min}}(\widetilde{\Sigma}_{n})
. Our next result shows that the lower bound from Proposition
7
improves on the one from Proposition
1
.

Lemma 8
.

Let
\delta\in(\varepsilon_{n},1)
. Then

\displaystyle d\cdot(1-\delta)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle Q_{\Tr\left\lparen\widetilde{\Sigma}_{n}^{-1}\right\rparen}(1-\delta)

\displaystyle\leq Q_{\lambda_{\textrm{max}}(\widetilde{\Sigma}_{n}^{-1})}(1-\delta)\cdot d,

\displaystyle\log(1/\delta)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle Q_{W}(1-\delta)

\displaystyle\leq Q_{\lambda_{\textrm{max}}(\widetilde{\Sigma}_{n}^{-1})}(1-\delta/2)\cdot\log(2/\delta).

This lemma further shows that a sufficient condition for the lower bound of Proposition
1
to be tight is the boundedness of
Q_{\lambda_{\textrm{max}}(\widetilde{\Sigma}_{n}^{-1})}(1-\delta/2)
by an absolute constant. Under what conditions on
(n,\delta,P_{X})
does this hold ? Our results from Section
5
provide a satisfying answer.

Corollary 9
.

Assume that
P_{X}
has fourth moments. If
\delta\in(0,1/2)
and

n\geq\max\left\{128\left[4\log(3d)\lambda_{\textrm{max}}(S(P_{X}))+R(P_{X})\log(2/\delta)\right],\frac{\log(3d)}{18\lambda_{\textrm{max}}(S(P_{X}))},\frac{\log(2/\delta)}{R(P_{X})}\right\},

then

R_{n,\delta}^{*}(\mathcal{P}_{\text{Gauss}}(P_{X},\sigma^{2}))\asymp\frac{\sigma^{2}(d+\log(1/\delta))}{n},

where the parameters
S(P_{X})
,
R(P_{X})
are as defined in (
4
).

Corollary
9
can be interpreted as a non-asymptotic version of Proposition
4
for the square error function. As we argue in Section
5
, the fourth moment assumption is very natural in this setting, and the sample size restriction is, in a sense, optimal. The main restriction on the sample size comes from the first term, as both
\lambda_{\textrm{max}}(S(P_{X}))
and
R(P_{X})
are expected to be large.

2.3
p
-th power error

The results of the last section are quite specific to the case of the square error, and it is a priori unclear how the minimax risk of other error functions can be studied non-asymptotically. Let us build on the observation that Corollary
9
is a non-asymptotic version of Proposition
4
for the square error. Can we do this for more general error functions ?
The underlying proof idea of Proposition
4
is a simple second order Taylor expansion, which becomes exact as
n\to\infty
. If we have non-asymptotic control over the error in this expansion, we can carry out the argument behind Proposition
4
non-asymptotically. We implement this idea here, and conclude this section with the following non-asymptotic lower bound on the minimax risk under a
p
-th power error function.

Proposition 10
.

Assume that
e(t)=\lvert t\rvert^{p}/[p(p-1)]
for some
p\in(2,\infty)
. Under the setup of Theorem
3
, and for
\delta\in(0,1/2)
, we have

R_{n,\delta}^{*}(\mathcal{P}_{\text{Gauss}}(P_{X},\sigma^{2}))\geq\frac{m(p-2)}{16(p-1)}\cdot\frac{\sigma^{p}\left[d+\log(1/\delta)\right]}{n}

where
m(p)\vcentcolon=2^{p/2-1}\Gamma(p/2-1)/\sqrt{\pi}
is the
p
-th absolute moment of a standard normal variable.

3
Minimaxity of the min-max linear regression procedure

In this section, we establish the minimaxity of a variant of the popular min-max regression procedure, e.g.
Audibert and Catoni 2011
;
Lecué and Lerasle 2020
;
Oliveira and Resende 2023
over suitably large classes of problems under the
p
-th power error functions
e(t)=\lvert t\rvert^{p}/[p(p-1)]
, for
p\in[2,\infty)
. Before stating our results, we briefly describe the construction of the procedure.

Let
\alpha,\beta\in\mathbb{R}
such that
\alpha\leq\beta
and define
\phi_{\alpha,\beta}(x)\vcentcolon=\alpha\mathbbm{1}_{(-\infty,\alpha)}(x)+x\mathbbm{1}_{[\alpha,\beta]}(x)+\beta\mathbbm{1}_{(\beta,\infty)}(x)
. For a real valued sequence
a\vcentcolon=(a_{i})_{i=1}^{n}
, define the sequence
a^{*}=(a^{*}_{i})_{i=1}^{n}
by
a^{*}_{i}\vcentcolon=a_{\pi(i)}
where
\pi
is a permutation that orders
a
increasingly.
Fix
k\in\left\{1,\dotsc,\lfloor n/2\rfloor\right\}
, and define
\varphi_{k}[a]\vcentcolon=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\phi_{a^{*}_{1+k},a^{*}_{n-k}}(a_{i})
. Given samples
(X_{i},Y_{i})_{i=1}^{n}
, and for
w,v\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, define

4
The quantile risk

In this section, we study the quantile risk in full generality. Our motivation for doing so is to provide the tools necessary for proving Theorem
3
. The results we obtain are however more general and can be used to tackle other problems. We illustrate this with two examples at the end of the section.

Before we formulate our results, let us briefly introduce some basic decision-theoretic concepts. To avoid overloading the notation, the symbols we introduce here will be specific to this section. A decision problem has the following components: a set of possible observations
\mathcal{O}
, a subset
\mathcal{P}
of probability measures on
\mathcal{O}
, a set of available actions
\mathcal{A}
, a loss function
\ell:\mathcal{P}\times\mathcal{A}\to\mathbb{R}
, and a decision rule
d:\mathcal{O}\to\mathcal{A}
. For a fixed distribution
P
, the performance of a decision rule is classically evaluated through its expected loss
\Exp\left[\ell(P,d(O))\right]
where
O\sim P
. Here instead, for a user-chosen failure probability
\delta\in(0,1)
, we evaluate the performance of a decision rule through its quantile risk
R_{\delta}(\ell,P,d)\vcentcolon=Q_{\ell(P,d(O))}(1-\delta)
. We define the associated worst-case and minimax risks by
R_{\delta}(\ell,d)\vcentcolon=\sup_{P\in\mathcal{P}}R_{\delta}(\ell,P,d)
and
R^{*}_{\delta}(\ell)\vcentcolon=\inf_{d}R_{\delta}(\ell,d)
respectively. Our aim is to develop methods to understand the minimax risk and establish the minimaxity of candidate decision rules.

4.1
A Bayesian criterion for minimaxity and an invariance principle

A classical way to establish the minimaxity of a decision rule is by computing its worst-case risk and showing that it matches the limit of a sequence of Bayes risks
(
Lehmann and Casella 2006
)
. The following result provides an analogue to this method when working under the quantile risk.

Theorem 13
.

For a distribution
\pi
on
\mathcal{P}
, define
F^{\pi}_{\ell(P,d(O))}
to be the cumulative distribution function of the random variable
\ell(P,d(O))
, where
P\sim\pi
and
O\mid P\sim P
.
Let
(\pi_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
be a sequence of distributions on
\mathcal{P}
. For any
t\in\mathbb{R}
, define

p_{\ell,k}(t)\vcentcolon=\sup_{d}F^{\pi_{k}}_{\ell(P,d(O))}(t).

Assume that the functions
(p_{\ell,k})_{k\in\mathbb{R}}
are right-continuous and that the sequence is decreasing, i.e.
p_{\ell,k}\geq p_{\ell,k+1}
and let
p_{\ell}\vcentcolon=\inf_{k\in N}p_{\ell,k}=\lim_{k\to\infty}p_{\ell,k}
. If
\hat{d}
is a decision satisfying

\sup_{P\in\mathcal{P}}R_{\delta}(\ell,P,\hat{d})=p_{\ell}^{-}(1-\delta),

where
p^{-}_{\ell}
is the pseudo-inverse of
p_{\ell}
, then
\hat{d}\in\argmin_{d}R_{\delta}(\ell,d)
, i.e. it is minimax.

We mention that instantiations of the arguments leading to Theorem
13
have been used by
Depersin and Lecué 2022
and
Gupta et al. 2023
to tackle specific problems. The general form we present here is new, and relies on new analytical results concerning collections of quantile functions.

In applications, it is desirable that the optimality of a decision rule depends as little as possible on the loss function, or conversely, that a single decision rule be minimax for as large a number of loss functions as possible. The following result shows that the minimaxity of a decision rule in the quantile risk is invariant to at least one form of transformation of the loss function.

Proposition 14
.

Let
\varphi:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
be a strictly increasing left-continuous function, and define
\varphi(\infty)\vcentcolon=\infty
and
\varphi(-\infty)\vcentcolon=-\infty
. Then
R_{\delta}(\varphi\circ\ell,P,d)=\varphi\left\lparen R_{\delta}(\ell,P,d)\right\rparen
. Furthermore, if
R_{\delta}(\ell,d)<\infty
, then
R_{\delta}(\varphi\circ\ell,d)=\varphi(R_{\delta}(\ell,d))
. Finally, if
R^{*}_{\delta}(\ell)<\infty
, then

d^{*}\in\argmin_{d}R_{\delta}(\ell,d)\implies d^{*}\in\argmin_{d}R_{\delta}(\varphi\circ\ell,d).

4.2
Mean estimation revisited

To exhibit the usefulness of the above results, we briefly revisit the problem of mean estimation under Gaussian data. This problem can be embedded in the above decision-theoretic setting as follows. The observations are random vectors
(X_{i})_{i=1}^{n}\in(\mathbb{R}^{d})^{n}
for some
d,n\in\mathbb{N}
, the subset of distributions is the
n
-product of distributions in the class
\mathcal{P}_{\text{Gauss}}(\Sigma)\vcentcolon=\left\{\mathcal{N}(\mu,\Sigma)\,\middle|\,\mu\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\right\}
, for a fixed
\Sigma\in S_{++}^{d}
. The set of available actions is
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, and the loss function is given by
\ell(P^{n},\mu)\vcentcolon=e(\mu-\mu(P))
for some error function
e
and where
\mu(P)
is the mean of the distribution
P
. Finally, a decision rule is given by an estimator
\hat{\mu}:(\mathbb{R}^{d})^{n}\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
. The following result gives the minimax quantile risk for this problem under a mild assumption on the error function
e
, generalizing the recent result of
Depersin and Lecué 2022
which holds under stronger assumptions on
e
.

Proposition 15
.

Assume that the error function
e
satisfies
e=\varphi\circ s
, where
\varphi
is a left-continuous strictly increasing function, and
s
is both quasiconvex, i.e.
s(tv+(1-t)u)\leq\max\left\{s(v),s(u)\right\}
for all
t\in[0,1]
and
u,v\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, and symmetric, i.e.

\inf_{\hat{\mu}}\sup_{P\in\mathcal{P}_{\text{Gauss}}(\Sigma)}R_{\delta}(\ell,P^{n},\hat{\mu})=Q_{e(Z)}(1-\delta),

where
Z\sim\mathcal{N}(0,\Sigma/n)
. Furthermore, the sample mean
\hat{\mu}((X_{i})_{i=1}^{n})\vcentcolon=n^{-1}\sum_{i=1}^{n}X_{i}
is minimax.

4.3
Minimax estimation of the variance of a Gaussian with known mean

As a second application of our results, we consider the problem of variance estimation with one-dimensional Gaussian data. For this problem, the observations are random variables
(X_{i})_{i=1}^{n}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
for some
n\in\mathbb{N}
, the subset of distributions is the
n

\mathcal{P}_{\text{Gauss}}(\mu)\vcentcolon=\left\{\mathcal{N}(\mu,\sigma^{2})\,\middle|\,\sigma\in(0,\infty)\right\}
,
for a fixed
\mu
. The set of available actions is
(0,\infty)
, and we consider the following loss function which captures the problem of estimating
\sigma^{2}
in relative error:
\ell(P^{n},\sigma^{2})\vcentcolon=\left\lvert\log(\sigma^{2}(P)/\sigma^{2})\right\rvert
where
\sigma^{2}(P)
is the variance of the distribution
P
. Finally, a decision rule is given by an estimator
\hat{\sigma}^{2}:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to(0,\infty)

, we obtain the following result.

Proposition 16
.

For
\alpha\in(0,\infty)
and
Z\sim{\text{Inv-Gamma}}(\alpha,\alpha)

p_{\alpha}(t)\vcentcolon=\Prob\left\lparen\frac{1-\exp(-2t)}{2t}\leq Z\leq\frac{\exp(2t)-1}{2t}\right\rparen.

\inf_{\hat{\sigma}^{2}}\sup_{P\in\mathcal{P}_{\text{Gauss}}(\mu)}R_{\delta}(\ell,P^{n},\hat{\sigma}^{2})=p_{n/2}^{-}(1-\delta).

Furthermore, the sample variance is not minimax and the estimator

\hat{\sigma}^{2}((X_{i})_{i=1}^{n})\vcentcolon=\frac{\sum_{i=1}^{n}(X_{i}-\mu)^{2}}{n}\phi\left\lparen p_{n/2}^{-}(1-\delta)\right\rparen

Surprisingly, Proposition

5
Smallest eigenvalue of the sample covariance matrix

The results of Sections
2
and
3
, and in particular the sample size conditions in Corollary
9
and Theorems
11
and
12
, rely on new high probability lower bounds on the smallest eigenvalue of the sample covariance matrix we describe in this section. We briefly formalize our problem, we then state our main results, and conclude this section by discussing and relating them to the literature.

Let
P_{X}
be a distribution on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
with finite second moments,
X\sim P_{X}
, and denote by
\Sigma\vcentcolon=\Exp\left[XX^{T}\right]
its covariance matrix. For i.i.d. samples
(X_{i})_{i=1}^{n}\sim P_{X}^{n}
, define the sample covariance matrix
\widehat{\Sigma}_{n}\vcentcolon=n^{-1}\sum_{i=1}^{n}X_{i}X_{i}^{T}
. In this section, we want to identify how close
\widehat{\Sigma}_{n}
is to
\Sigma
in a relative error sense and in a one-sided fashion. Specifically, we want to characterize the quantiles of the random variable
\lambda_{\textrm{max}}(I-\Sigma^{-1/2}\widehat{\Sigma}_{n}\Sigma^{-1/2})=1-\lambda_{\textrm{min}}(\Sigma^{-1/2}\widehat{\Sigma}_{n}\Sigma^{-1/2})
. To ease notation, we introduce the whitened random vector
\widetilde{X}\vcentcolon=\Sigma^{-1/2}X
. Notice that
\Exp[\widetilde{X}\widetilde{X}^{T}]=I_{d\times d}
, and that
\widetilde{\Sigma}_{n}\vcentcolon=n^{-1}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\widetilde{X}_{i}\widetilde{X}_{i}^{T}=\Sigma^{-1/2}\widehat{\Sigma}_{n}\Sigma^{-1/2}
. We want to understand the quantiles of
1-\lambda_{\textrm{min}}(\widetilde{\Sigma}_{n})
.

As already mentioned, our motivation for studying this problem stems from the fact that upper bounds on these quantiles form a crucial step in the analysis of linear regression in general, e.g.
Oliveira 2016
;
Mourtada 2022
, and in particular our results from Section
2
and
3
.

We are now ready to state our results. Define the following variance-like parameters

S(P_{X})\vcentcolon=\Exp\left[\left\lparen\widetilde{X}\widetilde{X}^{T}-I\right\rparen^{2}\right],\quad\quad R(P_{X})\vcentcolon=\sup_{v\in S^{d-1}}\Exp\left[\left\lparen\langle v,\widetilde{X}\rangle^{2}-1\right\rparen^{2}\right].

(4)

Our first result is the following proposition, which provides an asymptotic lower bound on the quantiles of
1-\lambda_{\textrm{min}}(\widetilde{\Sigma}_{n})
, and a nearly matching non-asymptotic upper bound.

Proposition 17
.

Assume that
P_{X}
has finite fourth moments. Then for all
\delta\in(0,0.1)
,

\lim_{n\to\infty}\sqrt{n}\cdot Q_{1-\lambda_{\textrm{min}}(\widetilde{\Sigma}_{n})}(1-\delta)\geq\frac{1}{40}\cdot\left\lparen\sqrt{\lambda_{\textrm{max}}(S(P_{X}))}+\sqrt{R(P_{X})\log(1/\delta)}\right\rparen.

Furthermore, for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
and
\delta\in(0,1)
,

Q_{1-\lambda_{\textrm{min}}(\widetilde{\Sigma}_{n})}(1-\delta)\leq\sqrt{\frac{8\lambda_{\textrm{max}}(S(P_{X}))\log(3d)}{n}}+\sqrt{\frac{2R(P_{X})\log(1/\delta)}{n}}+\frac{(2\log(3d)+4\log(1/\delta))}{3n}.

Our second result extends the upper bound in Proposition
17
to the case where
\lambda_{\textrm{min}}(\widetilde{\Sigma}_{n})=\inf_{v\in S^{d-1}}n^{-1}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\langle v,\widetilde{X}_{i}\rangle^{2}
is subject to a direction dependent adversarial truncation. This result is needed in our analyses of Section
3
, from which we recall the definition of
a^{*}
for a sequence
a
.

Proposition 18
.

Let
\delta\in(0,1/2)
such that
k=8\log(2/\delta)
is an integer satisfying
1\leq k\leq\lfloor n/2\rfloor
. For
(v,i)\in S^{d-1}\times[n]
, define
Y_{i,v}\vcentcolon=\langle v,\widetilde{X}_{i}\rangle^{2}
and
\overline{\lambda}_{\text{min}}(\widetilde{\Sigma}_{n})\vcentcolon=\inf_{v\in S^{d-1}}n^{-1}\sum_{i=k+1}^{n-k}Y_{i,v}^{*}
. Then, if
n\geq 8\log(6d)
,

Q_{(1-2k/n)-\overline{\lambda}_{\text{min}}(\widetilde{\Sigma}_{n})}(1-\delta)\leq 100\left\lparen\sqrt{\frac{8\log(6d)(\lambda_{\textrm{max}}(S(P_{X}))+1)}{n}}+\sqrt{\frac{(R(P_{X})+1)\log(1/\delta)}{n}}\right\rparen.

Comparison with existing results.

To the best of our knowledge, the only known lower bound, asymptotic or not, on the quantiles of
1-\lambda_{\textrm{min}}(\widetilde{\Sigma}_{n})
is due to
Mourtada 2022
. This bound however is distribution-free and decays fast, as
\log(1/\delta)/n
. In terms of upper bounds comparable to that of Proposition
17
, the closest result we are aware of is due to
Oliveira 2016
(see also
Zhivotovskiy 2024
), who proved
\sqrt{n}Q_{1-{\lambda}_{\text{min}}(\widetilde{\Sigma}_{n})}(1-\delta)\lesssim\sqrt{(R(P_{X})+1)\left[d+\log(1/\delta)\right]}
. In general, our upper bound and theirs are not comparable, and when combined they yield the best of both. Nevertheless, we suspect that our bound is better on heavy-tailed problems. Indeed, by Jensen’s inequality, it is not hard to see that
\lambda_{\textrm{max}}(S(P_{X}))\leq R(P_{X})\cdot d
, so our upper bound from Proposition
17
can be at most worse by
\sqrt{\log(d)}
. This occurs when
X
is a centred Gaussian, for which it is known that Oliveira’s bound is tight
(
Koltchinskii and Lounici 2017
)
. On the other hand, consider
X
with centred independent coordinates, and where the first coordinate has kurtosis
\kappa\gg 1
, while the other coordinates have constant kurtosis. Then Oliveira’s bound scales as
\sqrt{\kappa\cdot d}
, while ours scales as
\sqrt{\kappa\cdot\log(d)}
. Finally, versions of Proposition
18
that mimic Oliveira’s bound can be deduced from the recent literature
(
Abdalla and Zhivotovskiy 2023
;
Oliveira and Rico 2022
)
. The same considerations apply when comparing these results.

On the fourth moment assumption.

We carried out our analysis under a fourth moment assumption on
P_{X}
. We argue here that this is in some sense the most natural assumption to study this problem. Indeed, recall the fact that
\widetilde{\Sigma}_{n}
is an empirical average of the random matrix
\widetilde{X}\widetilde{X}^{T}
. Therefore, by the law of large numbers,
\widetilde{\Sigma}_{n}\overset{d}{\to}I_{d\times d}
, and by the continuous mapping theorem,
\lambda_{\textrm{min}}(\widetilde{\Sigma}_{n})\overset{d}{\to}1
as
n\to\infty
. To say something about the rate of this convergence, the most natural assumption to make is that the entries of the random matrix
XX^{T}
have finite variance so that the CLT holds. This is equivalent to assuming that
P_{X}
has fourth moments.

On the critical sample size.

Our main application of Propositions
17
and
18
is in providing upper bounds on the critical sample size
n^{*}(P_{X},\delta)\vcentcolon=\min\left\{n\in\mathbb{N}\,\middle|\,Q_{1-\lambda_{\textrm{min}}(\widetilde{\Sigma}_{n})}(1-\delta/2)\leq 1/4\right\}
. In particular, these upper bounds correspond to the sample size restrictions in Corollary
9
and Theorems
11
and
12
. We claimed after the statement of these results that these restrictions were in some sense optimal; we expand on this here. Let
L\vcentcolon=\lim_{n\to\infty}\sqrt{n}\cdot Q_{1-\lambda_{\textrm{min}}(\widetilde{\Sigma}_{n})}(1-\delta)
, and define
n_{0}(P_{X},\delta)\vcentcolon=\min\left\{n\in\mathbb{N}\,\middle|\,m\geq n\Rightarrow Q_{1-\lambda_{\textrm{min}}(\widetilde{\Sigma}_{m})}(1-\delta/2)\geq\frac{L}{4\sqrt{m}}\right\}
. If
n_{0}(P_{X},\delta)\leq n^{*}(P_{X},\delta)
, then we can reverse the above-mentioned upper bounds using the first item of Proposition
17
, up to a
\sqrt{\log(d)}
factor.
In words, if the critical sample size is determined by the asymptotic behaviour of the
1-\delta/2
quantile of
1-\lambda_{\textrm{min}}(\widetilde{\Sigma}_{n})
, then our bounds on the critical sample size are tight. When the hypothesis in this last statement is true remains unclear however. The choice of the constant
1/4
in the above argument is arbitrary, and can be replaced with any absolute constant.

6
Conclusion

In this paper, we studied minimax linear regression under the quantile risk. We gave an in-depth characterization of the minimax risk over the Gaussian class
\mathcal{P}_{\text{Gauss}}(P_{X},\sigma^{2})
, and leveraged these results to establish the minimaxity, up to absolute constants, of the min-max regression procedure for
p
-norm regression problems. While the problem of estimation with high confidence has been studied intensely recently, we are not aware of its formalization through quantiles as was done in this paper. We hope this new perspective proves fruitful in advancing both our understanding of learning problems and our ability to design efficient solutions for them.

Lemma 20
.

The following holds.

•

Let
f:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
be an increasing function. Then for all
x\in\mathbb{R}
,
f^{-}(f(x))\leq x
.

•

Let
f,g
be increasing functions from
\mathbb{R}
to
\mathbb{R}
. If
f\geq g
then
f^{-}\leq g^{-}
.

•

Let
I
be an index set and let
\left\{f_{i}\right\}_{i\in I}
be a collection of increasing functions from
\mathbb{R}
to
\mathbb{R}
. Then

\left\lparen\sup_{i\in I}f_{i}\right\rparen^{-}\leq\inf_{i\in I}f_{i}^{-}\leq\sup_{i\in I}f_{i}^{-}\leq\left\lparen\inf_{i\in I}f_{i}\right\rparen^{-}.

•

Let
f:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
be a strictly increasing function, so that it is a bijection from
\mathbb{R}
to
\im(f)
. Denote by
f^{-1}:\im(f)\to\mathbb{R}
the inverse of
f
. Then for all
y\in\im(f)
,
f^{-}(y)=f^{-1}(y)
.

•

Let
f:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
be an increasing right-continuous function. Then for all
y\in[-\infty,\infty]
,

f^{-}(y)=\min\left\{x\in\mathbb{R}\,\middle|\,f(x)\geq y\right\},

with the conventions
\min\varnothing\vcentcolon=\infty
and
\min\mathbb{R}\vcentcolon=-\infty
.

•

Let
I
be an index set and let
\left\{f_{i}\right\}_{i\in I}
be a collection of increasing right-continuous functions from
\mathbb{R}
to
\mathbb{R}
. Then

\sup_{i\in I}f_{i}^{-}=\left\lparen\inf_{i\in I}f_{i}\right\rparen^{-}.

•

Let
f:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
be increasing and right-continuous, and let
g:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
be increasing. Then

(f\circ g)^{-}=g^{-}\circ f^{-}.

•

Let
(f_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
be a decreasing sequence of increasing right-continuous functions, and assume that
f_{n}\to f
pointwise as
n\to\infty
. Then,

\sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}f_{n}^{-1}=f^{-1}.

Proof.

•

We have, since
f(x)\geq f(x)
,

f^{-}(f(x))=\inf\left\{z\in\mathbb{R}\,\middle|\,f(z)\geq f(x)\right\}\leq x.

•

Fix
y\in[-\infty,\infty]
, and define
S_{f}\vcentcolon=\left\{x\in\mathbb{R}\,\middle|\,f(x)\geq y\right\}
, and
S_{g}\vcentcolon=\left\{x\in\mathbb{R}\,\middle|\,g(x)\geq y\right\}
. We claim that
S_{g}\subset S_{f}
from which the statement follows. If
S_{g}=\varnothing
, the statement follows trivially. Otherwise let
x\in S_{g}
. Then
f(x)\geq g(x)\geq y
, so
x\in S_{f}
.

•

We prove the last inequality. The first follows from a similar argument. By definition, for all
j\in I
,
\sup_{i\in I}f_{i}\geq f_{j}
. Applying the second item yields that for all
j\in I
,
\left\lparen\sup_{i\in I}f_{i}\right\rparen^{-}\leq f_{j}^{-}
. Taking the infimum over
j\in I
yields the result.

•

Let
y\in\im(f)
and let
S\vcentcolon=\left\{x\in\mathbb{R}\,\middle|\,f(x)\geq y\right\}
. We claim that
f^{-1}(y)=\min S
from which the claim follows since then
f^{-}(y)=\min S
. Indeed, since
f(f^{-1}(y))=y
, we have
f^{-1}(y)\in S
. Now suppose that there exists an
x\in S
such that
f^{-1}(y)>x
. Since
f
is strictly increasing, we would have
y=f(f^{-}(y))>f(x)
, which contradicts the fact that
x\in S
. Therefore
f^{-1}(y)\leq x
for all
x\in S
. This proves that
f^{-1}(y)=\min S
.

•

The statement holds trivially if
f^{-}(y)\in\left\{-\infty,\infty\right\}
. Otherwise,
f^{-}(y)\in\mathbb{R}
, and by definition of the infimum, for all
k\in\mathbb{N}
, we have
x_{k}\vcentcolon=f^{-}(y)+1/k\in S
and therefore
f(x_{k})\geq y
. Furthermore,
\lim_{k\to\infty}x_{k}=f^{-1}(y)
and
x_{k}>f^{-1}(y)
, so by the right-continuity of
f
we obtain

f(f^{-1}(y))=\lim_{k\to\infty}f(x_{k})\geq y.

Therefore
f^{-1}(y)\in S
which implies
f^{-1}(y)=\min S
.

•

The inequality
(\leq)
is covered by the third item, therefore it is enough to prove the inequality
(\geq)
. Let
y\in[-\infty,\infty]
. We claim that

\sup_{i\in I}f^{-}_{i}(y)\geq\left\lparen\inf_{i\in I}f_{i}\right\rparen^{-}(y).

The statement follows trivially if
\sup_{i\in I}f^{-}_{i}(y)=\infty
. Otherwise, we have
f^{-}_{i}(y)<\infty
for all
i\in I
. If
\sup_{i\in I}f^{-}_{i}(y)=-\infty
, then
f_{i}^{-}(y)=-\infty
for all
i\in I
, which implies that
f_{i}(x)\geq y
for all
x\in\mathbb{R}
. This in turn implies that for all
x\in\mathbb{R}
,
\inf_{i\in I}f_{i}(x)\geq y
and therefore
\left\lparen\inf_{i\in I}f_{i}\right\rparen^{-}(y)=-\infty
. It remains to consider the case where
\sup_{i\in I}f^{-}_{i}(y)\in\mathbb{R}
. We claim that

\inf_{i\in I}f_{i}\left\lparen\sup_{j\in I}f^{-}_{j}(y)\right\rparen\geq y

from which the main claim follows by definition of the pseudo-inverse. Indeed, let
i\in I
. If
f^{-}_{i}(y)\in\mathbb{R}
, then we have

f_{i}\left\lparen\sup_{j\in I}f^{-}_{j}(y)\right\rparen\geq f_{i}(f_{i}^{-}(y))\geq y

where the first inequality holds since
f_{i}
is increasing, and the second by the fifth item and the fact that
f^{-}_{i}(y)\in\mathbb{R}
. Otherwise
f_{i}^{-}(y)=-\infty
, and therefore
f_{i}(x)\geq y
for all
x\in\mathbb{R}
, which in particular implies the desired statement since
\sup_{i\in I}f^{-}_{i}(y)\in\mathbb{R}
.

•

Let
y\in[-\infty,\infty]
. By the assumed properties of
f
and the fifth item, we have
f(g(x))\geq y
if and only if
g(x)\geq f^{-}(y)
. Therefore

\displaystyle(f\circ g)^{-}(y)

\displaystyle=\inf\left\{x\in\mathbb{R}\,\middle|\,f(g(x))\geq y\right\}

\displaystyle=\inf\left\{x\in\mathbb{R}\,\middle|\,g(x)\geq f^{-}(y)\right\}

\displaystyle=g^{-}(f^{-}(y))=(g^{-}\circ f^{-})(y).

•

We start with the inequality (
\leq
). Let
x\in\mathbb{R}
. Since
(f_{n}(x))_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is decreasing, we have
f(x)=\lim_{n\to\infty}f_{n}(x)=\inf_{n\in\mathbb{N}}f_{n}(x)
. Therefore, for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
, we have
f_{n}\geq f
. By the second item, we therefore have
f^{-}_{n}\leq f^{-}
. Taking supremum over
n
yields the result.

For the inequality
(\geq)
, let
y\in\mathbb{R}
, and suppose that
\sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}f_{n}^{-}(y)<f^{-}(y)
. If
\sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}f_{n}^{-}(y)=-\infty
, then for all
x\in\mathbb{R}
and for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
,
f_{n}(x)\geq y
, which implies that for all
x\in\mathbb{R}
,
f(x)=\lim_{n\to\infty}f_{n}(x)\geq y
, and therefore
f^{-1}(y)=-\infty
, contradicting the strict inequality. Otherwise
x^{*}\vcentcolon=\sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}f_{n}^{-}(y)\in\mathbb{R}
, and either
f^{-}(y)=\infty
or
f^{-}(y)\in\mathbb{R}
.

If
f^{-}(y)=\infty
, then on the one hand, for all
x\in\mathbb{R}
,
\lim_{n\to\infty}f_{n}(x)=f(x)<y
. On the other hand, for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
,
f_{n}\left\lparen x^{*}\right\rparen\geq y
. Indeed, if
f^{-}_{n}(y)\in\mathbb{R}
, then by the fifth item
f_{n}(x^{*})\geq f_{n}(f^{-}_{n}(y))\geq y
. Otherwise,
f^{-}_{n}(y)=-\infty
so that
f(x)\geq y
for all
x\in\mathbb{R}
, and in particular
f_{n}(x^{*})\geq y
. But then we get the contradiction
y>\lim_{n\to\infty}f_{n}\left\lparen x^{*}\right\rparen\geq y
.

Finally, if
f^{-}(y)\in\mathbb{R}
, define
\varepsilon\vcentcolon=f^{-}(y)-x^{*}>0
. By definition of
x^{*}
,
f^{-}(y)-\varepsilon\geq f^{-}_{n}(y)
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
. We claim that for all
n\in\mathbb{N}

f_{n}(f^{-}(y)-\varepsilon)\geq y.

Indeed, if
f^{-}_{n}(y)\in\mathbb{R}
, then by the fifth item
f_{n}(f^{-}(y)-\varepsilon)\geq f_{n}(f^{-}_{n}(y))\geq y
. Otherwise,
f^{-}_{n}(y)=-\infty
so that
f(x)\geq y
for all
x\in\mathbb{R}
, and in particular
f_{n}(f^{-}(y)-\varepsilon)\geq y
since
f^{-}(y)-\varepsilon\in\mathbb{R}
. Taking the limit as
n\to\infty
yields

\lim_{n\to\infty}f_{n}(f^{-}(y)-\varepsilon)=f(f^{-}(y)-\varepsilon)\geq y

contradicting the minimality of
f^{-}(y)
.

∎

For a random variable
X
, we denote by
F_{X}:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
its cumulative distribution function
F_{X}(x)\vcentcolon=\Prob\left\lparen X\leq x\right\rparen
, and by
Q_{X}:[-\infty,\infty]\to[-\infty,\infty]
its quantile function
Q_{X}(p)\vcentcolon=F_{X}^{-}(p)
. Since
F_{X}
is right-continuous, then by the fifth item of Lemma
20
we have

Q_{X}(p)=\min\left\{x\in\mathbb{R}\,\middle|\,F_{X}(x)\geq p\right\}.

Furthermore, since
\lim_{x\to-\infty}F_{X}(x)=0
and
\lim_{x\to\infty}F_{X}(x)=1
, it is easy to verify that
Q_{X}(p)\in\mathbb{R}
for all
p\in(0,1)
and
Q_{X}(0)=-\infty
. If
X,Y
are two random variables, we define the random variable
F_{X\mid Y}(x)\vcentcolon=\Prob\left\lparen X\leq x\,\middle|\,Y\right\rparen
and we note that
F_{X}(x)=\Exp[F_{X\mid Y}(x)]
for all
x\in\mathbb{R}
.

Lemma 21
.

Let
\varphi:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
be strictly increasing and left continuous. Then for all
p\in(0,1)

Q_{\varphi(X)}(p)=\varphi(Q_{X}(p)).

where we define
\varphi(\infty)\vcentcolon=\infty
and
\varphi(-\infty)\vcentcolon=-\infty
.

Proof.

Let
\varepsilon_{-}\vcentcolon=\Prob\left\lparen X=\infty\right\rparen
and
\varepsilon_{+}=\Prob\left\lparen X=\infty\right\rparen
. By definition,
\varphi(X)=\infty\Leftrightarrow X=-\infty
, so the identity holds trivially for all
p\in(0,\varepsilon_{-}]\cup[1-\varepsilon_{+},1)
. Now consider the case
p\in I\vcentcolon=(\varepsilon_{-},1-\varepsilon_{+})
. First, since
\lim_{x\to-\infty}F_{X}(x)=\varepsilon_{-}
and
\lim_{x\to\infty}F_{X}(x)=1-\varepsilon_{+}
by continuity of probability measures, we have
Q_{X}(p)\in\mathbb{R}
. The same argument shows that
Q_{\varphi(X)}(p)\in\mathbb{R}
. Now, since
\varphi
is strictly increasing,

F_{X}(x)=\Prob\left\lparen X\leq x\right\rparen=\Prob\left\lparen\varphi(X)\leq\varphi(x)\right\rparen=F_{\varphi(X)}(\varphi(x))=(F_{\varphi(X)}\circ\varphi)(x).

Therefore, by the penultimate item of Lemma
20
, we have

Q_{X}=F_{X}^{-}=\varphi^{-}\circ F_{\varphi(X)}^{-}=\varphi^{-}\circ Q_{\varphi(X)}.

(5)

We claim that for all
p\in I
,

(\varphi\circ\varphi^{-}\circ Q_{\varphi(X)})(p)=Q_{\varphi(X)}(p).

(6)

By the fourth item of Lemma
20
, it is enough to show that
Q_{\varphi(X)}(p)\in\im(\varphi)
for all
p\in I
. This will be the goal of the proof. Let
p\in I
, and define

S\vcentcolon=\left\{x\in\mathbb{R}\,\middle|\,\varphi(x)\leq Q_{\varphi(X)}(p)\right\}
. We claim that
S
is non-empty and upper bounded. Indeed, suppose not. Then either
\varphi(x)>Q_{\varphi(X)}(p)
for all
x\in\mathbb{R}
or
\varphi(x)\leq Q_{\varphi(X)}(p)
for all
x\in\mathbb{R}
. In the former case, this implies that for all
x\in\mathbb{R}

F_{X}(x)=\Prob\left\lparen X\leq x\right\rparen=\Prob\left\lparen\varphi(X)\leq\varphi(x)\right\rparen\geq\Prob\left\lparen\varphi(X)\leq Q_{\varphi(X)}(p)\right\rparen\geq p>\varepsilon_{-},

where the second inequality follows from the fifth item of Lemma
20
and the fact that
Q_{\varphi(X)}(p)\in\mathbb{R}
. This leads to the contradiction

\varepsilon_{-}=\lim_{x\to-\infty}F_{X}(x)\geq p>\varepsilon_{-}.

In the latter case, we get that for all
x\in\mathbb{R}

F_{X}(x)=\Prob\left\lparen X\leq x\right\rparen=\Prob\left\lparen\varphi(X)\leq\varphi(x)\right\rparen\leq\Prob\left\lparen\varphi(X)\leq Q_{\varphi(X)}(p)\right\rparen.

This leads to

1-\varepsilon_{+}=\lim_{x\to\infty}F_{X}(x)\leq\Prob\left\lparen\varphi(X)\leq Q_{\varphi(X)}(p)\right\rparen\leq 1-\varepsilon_{+}.

where the last inequality follows from the fact that
Q_{\varphi(X)}(p)\in\mathbb{R}
. Now, since

\Prob\left\lparen\varphi(X)\leq\lim_{x\to\infty}\varphi(x)\right\rparen=1-\varepsilon_{+}

and
\varphi(x)\leq Q_{\varphi(X)}(p)
for all
x\in\mathbb{R}
, we get by the minimality property of
Q_{\varphi(X)}(p)

Q_{\varphi(X)}(p)=\lim_{x\to\infty}\varphi(x).

But, on the one hand, we have by continuity of probability,

\lim_{n\to\infty}\Prob\left\lparen\varphi(X)\leq\varphi(n)\right\rparen=\Prob\left\lparen\varphi(X)\leq\lim_{n\to\infty}\varphi(n)\right\rparen=1-\varepsilon_{+}

yet on the other, since
\varphi
is strictly increasing, we have
\varphi(n)<\lim_{x\to\infty}\varphi(x)=Q_{\varphi(X)}(p)
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
, so by the minimality of
Q_{\varphi(X)}(p)
,
\Prob\left\lparen\varphi(X)\leq\varphi(n)\right\rparen<p
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
, from which we obtain the contradiction

1-\varepsilon_{+}=\lim_{n\to\infty}\Prob\left\lparen\varphi(X)\leq\varphi(n)\right\rparen\leq p

This proves that
S
is non-empty and upper bounded. Now define
x_{0}\vcentcolon=\sup S
, which is guaranteed to satisfy
x_{0}\in\mathbb{R}
by the upper boundedness of
S
and its non-emptiness. We claim that
\varphi(x_{0})=Q_{\varphi(X)}(p)
. Indeed, by the left-continuity of
\varphi
, we have, for any sequence
(x_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
in
S
such that
x_{n}\to x_{0}

\varphi(x_{0})=\lim_{n\to\infty}\varphi(x_{n})\leq Q_{\varphi(X)}(p)

(7)

where the last inequality follows from the definition of
S
and the fact that
x_{n}\in S
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
. On the other hand, by the maximality of
x_{0}
, we have for all
x>x_{0}
,
\varphi(x)>Q_{\varphi(X)}(p)
, which implies that

Q_{\varphi(X)}(p)\leq\lim_{x\to x_{0}^{+}}\varphi(x)

(8)

Combining (
7
) and (
8
), we obtain

Q_{\varphi(X)}(p)\in\left[\varphi(x_{0}),\lim_{x\to x_{0}^{+}}\varphi(x)\right]

But for any
y\in\left[{\varphi(x_{0}),\lim_{x\to x_{0}^{+}}\varphi(x)}\right]
, we have

\Prob\left\lparen\varphi(X)\leq y\right\rparen=\Prob\left\lparen\varphi(X)\leq\varphi(x_{0})\right\rparen

Indeed on the one hand

\Prob\left\lparen\varphi(X)\leq y\right\rparen\geq\Prob\left\lparen\varphi(X)\leq\varphi(x_{0})\right\rparen

On the other, if
X>x_{0}
, then since
\varphi
is strictly increasing,
\varphi(X)>\lim_{x\to x_{0}^{+}}\varphi(x)\geq y
. Therefore

\Prob\left\lparen\varphi(X)\leq y\right\rparen\leq\Prob\left\lparen\varphi(X)\leq\lim_{x\to x_{0}^{+}}\varphi(x)\right\rparen\leq\Prob\left\lparen X\leq x_{0}\right\rparen=\Prob\left\lparen\varphi(X)\leq\varphi(x_{0})\right\rparen

but then, by the minimality of
Q_{\varphi(X)}(p)
, we obtain
Q_{\varphi(X)}(p)=\varphi(x_{0})
. This proves (
6
). Now applying
\varphi
to both sides of (
5
) and using (
6
) yields the result.
∎

Definition 22
.

A subset
A\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
is

•

convex if for all
x,y\in A
and
t\in[0,1]
,
(1-t)x+ty\in A
.

•

symmetric if for all
x\in A
,
-x\in A
.

Lemma 23
.

Let
A
be a non-empty convex symmetric set. Then for all
\lambda\geq 1
,
A\subseteq\lambda A
.

Proof.

We start by proving that
\lambda A
is convex. Indeed let
x,y\in\lambda A
and
t\in[0,1]
. Then by definition
x/\lambda,y/\lambda\in A
, so by convexity of
A

(1-t)\frac{x}{\lambda}+t\frac{y}{\lambda}\in A,

which implies

(1-t)x+ty=\lambda\cdot\left\lparen(1-t)\frac{x}{\lambda}+t\frac{y}{\lambda}\right\rparen\in\lambda A.

Next we prove that
0\in A
. Let
v\in A
. Since
A
is symmetric,
-v\in A
, and by convexity of
A

0=\frac{1}{2}x-\frac{1}{2}x=\frac{1}{2}x+\frac{1}{2}(-x)\in A.

Finally, we prove the main claim. Let
x\in A
. Then by definition
\lambda x\in\lambda A
. But then by convexity of
\lambda A
and since
\lambda\geq 1

x=\left\lparen 1-\frac{1}{\lambda}\right\rparen 0+\frac{1}{\lambda}\lambda x\in\lambda A

∎

Definition 24
.

A function
f:\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}
is

•

quasiconvex if for all
x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
t\in[0,1]
,
f((1-t)x+ty)\leq\max\left\lparen f(x),f(y)\right\rparen
.

•

symmetric if for all
v\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
f(v)=f(-v)
.

Remark 25
.

Every convex function is quasiconvex. The function
f(x)=\log(x)
is quasiconvex but not convex. Every norm is quasiconvex (and, in fact, convex) and symmetric.

Lemma 26
.

The following holds.

•

f:\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}
is quasiconvex and symmetric if and only if for all
y\in\mathbb{R}
,
f^{-1}((-\infty,y])
is convex and symmetric.

If

A.3
Gaussian measures

Lemma 27
.

Let
Z\sim\mathcal{N}(0,\sigma^{2})
. Then

\sqrt{1-\exp\left\lparen-\frac{r^{2}}{2\sigma^{2}}\right\rparen}\leq F_{\lvert Z\rvert}(r)\leq\sqrt{1-\exp\left\lparen-\frac{2r^{2}}{\pi\sigma^{2}}\right\rparen}.

Proof.

Consider first the case where
\sigma^{2}=1/2
. Then

\left\lparen F_{\lvert Z\rvert}(r)\right\rparen^{2}=\left\lparen\Prob\left\lparen-r\leq Z\leq r\right\rparen\right\rparen^{2}=\left\lparen\frac{2}{\sqrt{\pi}}\int_{0}^{r}e^{-t^{2}}dt\right\rparen^{2}\\
=\frac{4}{\pi}\int_{0}^{r}\int_{0}^{r}e^{-(t^{2}+s^{2})}dtds=\frac{4}{\pi}\int_{S}e^{-(t^{2}+s^{2})}dtds

where
S\vcentcolon=\left\{(x,y)\in\mathbb{R}^{2}\,\middle|\,0\leq x,y\leq r\right\}
is the square of length
r
whose lower left corner is at
0
. For a radius
\rho>0
, define the quarter disks
D(\rho)\vcentcolon=\left\{(x,y)\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}\,\middle|\,x,y\geq 0,\sqrt{x^{2}+y^{2}}\leq\rho\right\}
. Clearly,
D(r)\subset S
, so that

\displaystyle\frac{4}{\pi}\int_{S}e^{-(t^{2}+s^{2})}dtds

\displaystyle\leq\frac{4}{\pi}\int_{D(r)}e^{-(t^{2}+s^{2})}dtds=1-\exp\left\lparen-r^{2}\right\rparen

where the last equality is obtained by an explicit integration using polar coordinates. On the other hand, consider the quarter disk
D(2r/\sqrt{\pi})
, and define
A\vcentcolon=D(2r/\sqrt{\pi})\setminus S
and
B\vcentcolon=S\setminus D(2r/\sqrt{\pi})
. Since
S
and
D(2r/\sqrt{\pi})
have the same area, so do
A
and
B
. But for all
(t,s)\in A
and all
(x,y)\in B
, we have

t^{2}+s^{2}\leq\frac{4r^{2}}{\pi}\leq x^{2}+y^{2}\Rightarrow\exp\left\lparen-\lparen t^{2}+s^{2}\rparen\right\rparen\geq\exp\left\lparen-\lparen x^{2}+y^{2}\rparen\right\rparen

Therefore

\displaystyle\frac{4}{\pi}\int_{S}e^{-(t^{2}+s^{2})}dtds

\displaystyle=\frac{4}{\pi}\left\lparen\int_{S\cap D(2r/\sqrt{\pi})}e^{-(t^{2}+s^{2})}dtds+\int_{B}e^{-(t^{2}+s^{2})}dtds\right\rparen

\displaystyle\leq\frac{4}{\pi}\left\lparen\int_{S\cap D(2r/\sqrt{\pi})}e^{-(t^{2}+s^{2})}dtds+\int_{A}e^{-(t^{2}+s^{2})}dtds\right\rparen

\displaystyle=\frac{4}{\pi}\int_{D(2r/\sqrt{\pi})}e^{-(t^{2}+s^{2})}dtds=1-\exp\left\lparen-\frac{4r^{2}}{\pi}\right\rparen

This proves the statement for
\sigma^{2}=1/2
. For
\sigma^{2}\in(0,\infty)
, note that
Z\overset{d}{=}\sqrt{2\sigma^{2}}\tilde{Z}
where
\tilde{Z}\sim\mathcal{N}(0,1/2)
, so

F_{\lvert Z\rvert}(r)=\Prob\left\lparen-r\leq Z\leq r\right\rparen=\Prob\left\lparen-\frac{r}{\sqrt{2\sigma^{2}}}\leq\tilde{Z}\leq\frac{r}{\sqrt{2\sigma^{2}}}\right\rparen=F_{\lvert\tilde{Z}\rvert}\left\lparen\frac{r}{\sqrt{2\sigma^{2}}}\right\rparen,

and applying the result for
\sigma^{2}=1/2
yields the general result.
∎

Lemma 28
.

Let
X,Y
be random vectors such that
X\mid Y=y\sim\mathcal{N}(y,\Sigma)
for some fixed
\Sigma\in\mathbb{S}_{++}^{d}
and for all
y
in the image of
Y
. Then
X-Y\sim\mathcal{N}(0,\Sigma)
.

Proof.

Let
B
be a Borel subset of
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, and let
Z\sim\mathcal{N}(0,\Sigma)
. We have

\Prob\left\lparen X-Y\in B\right\rparen=\Exp\left[\Prob\left\lparen X-Y\in B\,\middle|\,Y\right\rparen\right]=\Exp\left[\Prob\left\lparen Z\in B\right\rparen\right]=\Prob\left\lparen Z\in B\right\rparen.

∎

Lemma 30

(Gaussian Poincaré/Concentration)
.

Let

,

, and

be an

-Lipschitz function with respect to the Euclidean metric. Then

A.3.1
Concentration of norms of Gaussian vectors

For this subsection, fix
d\in\mathbb{N}
, an arbitrary norm
\lVert\cdot\rVert
on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, and a covariance matrix
\Sigma\in S_{++}^{d}
. Let
Z\sim\mathcal{N}(0,\Sigma)
, and define
M\vcentcolon=\Exp\left[\lVert Z\rVert\right]
. Let
S
denote the unit sphere of the dual norm
\lVert\cdot\rVert_{*}
, and recall that
\lVert x\rVert=\lVert x\rVert_{**}=\sup_{v\in S}\lvert\langle v,x\rangle\rvert
. Define
R\vcentcolon=\sup_{v\in S}v^{T}\Sigma v
, and
v_{*}=\max_{v\in S}v^{T}\Sigma v
where the maximum is attained since
S
is compact and the function is continuous.

Lemma 31
.

The function
f:\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}
given by
f(x)=\lVert\Sigma^{1/2}x\rVert
is
\sqrt{R}
-Lipschitz in the Euclidean metric.

Proof.

\lvert f(x)-f(y)\rvert=\left\lvert\lVert\Sigma^{1/2}x\rVert-\lVert\Sigma^{1/2}y\rVert\right\rvert\leq\lVert\Sigma^{1/2}(x-y)\rVert\\
=\sup_{v\in S}\langle\Sigma^{1/2}v,x-y\rangle\leq\left\lparen\max_{v\in S}\lVert\Sigma^{1/2}v\rVert_{2}\right\rparen\lVert x-y\rVert_{2}

∎

Lemma 32
.

For all
t\geq 0
,

\displaystyle M^{2}\leq\Exp\left[\lVert Z\rVert^{2}\right]\leq\left\lparen 1+\frac{\pi}{2}\right\rparen M^{2},

\displaystyle\Prob\left\lparen M-\lVert Z\rVert\geq t\right\rparen\leq\exp\left\lparen-\frac{t^{2}}{\pi M^{2}}\right\rparen.

Proof.

Notice that

M=\Exp\left[\lVert Z\rVert\right]=\Exp\left[\sup_{v\in S}\lvert\langle v,Z\rangle\rvert\right]\geq\sup_{v\in S}\Exp\left[\lvert\langle v,Z\rangle\rvert\right]=\sup_{v\in S}\sqrt{\frac{2}{\pi}v^{T}\Sigma v}=\sqrt{\frac{2}{\pi}v_{*}^{T}\Sigma v_{*}}.

where the inequality follows by convexity of the supremum and Jensen’s inequality, and the third equality by the fact that
\langle v,Z\rangle\sim\mathcal{N}(0,v^{T}\Sigma v)
and an explicit calculation of the expectation. We now prove the first item. The first inequality follows from Jensen’s inequality. For the second, notice that
\Sigma^{-1/2}Z\sim\mathcal{N}(0,I_{d\times d})
, so that an application of Lemmas
30
and
31
yields

\Exp\left[\lVert Z\rVert^{2}\right]-(\Exp\left[\lVert Z\rVert\right])^{2}=\Var\left[\lVert Z\rVert\right]=\Var\left[f(\Sigma^{-1/2}Z)\right]\leq R=v_{*}^{T}\Sigma v_{*}\leq\frac{\pi}{2}(\Exp\left[\lVert Z\rVert\right])^{2}.

where
f
is as defined in Lemma
31
. For the second item, notice that
-f
is also
\sqrt{R}
-Lipschitz, so that again an application of Lemma
30
yields

\Prob\left\lparen M-\lVert Z\rVert>t\right\rparen=\Prob\left\lparen-f(\Sigma^{-1/2}Z)-\Exp\left[-f(\Sigma^{-1/2}Z)\right]>t\right\rparen\\
\leq\exp\left\lparen-\frac{t^{2}}{2v_{*}^{T}\Sigma v_{*}}\right\rparen\leq\exp\left\lparen-\frac{t^{2}}{\pi\left\lparen\Exp\left[\lVert Z\rVert\right]\right\rparen^{2}}\right\rparen.

∎

Corollary 33
.

For all
r\in\mathbb{R}
,

l(r)\leq F_{\lVert Z\rVert}(r)\leq\min\left\{u_{1}(r),u_{2}(r)\right\}

where

\displaystyle l(r)\vcentcolon=\left[1-\exp\left\lparen-\frac{(r-M)^{2}}{2R}\right\rparen\right]\mathbbm{1}_{[M,\infty)},

\displaystyle u_{1}(r)\vcentcolon=\exp\left\lparen\frac{-(M-r)^{2}}{\pi M^{2}}\right\rparen\mathbbm{1}_{[0,M)}(r)+\mathbbm{1}_{[M,\infty)}(r),\quad u_{2}(r)\vcentcolon=\sqrt{1-\exp\left\lparen-\frac{2r^{2}}{\pi R}\right\rparen}\mathbbm{1}_{[0,\infty)}(r).

Proof.

We start with the lower bound. Let
r\geq M
. Then

F_{\lVert Z\rVert}(r)=\Prob\left\lparen\lVert Z\rVert\leq r\right\rparen=1-\Prob\left\lparen\lVert Z\rVert>r\right\rparen=1-\Prob\left\lparen\lVert Z\rVert-M>r-M\right\rparen\geq 1-\exp\left\lparen-\frac{(r-M)^{2}}{2R}\right\rparen

where the last inequality follows from Lemmas
30
and
31
. For the lower bound, we have, by Lemma
32

F_{\lVert Z\rVert}(r)=\Prob\left\lparen\lVert Z\rVert\leq r\right\rparen=\Prob\left\lparen\lVert Z\rVert-M\leq r-M\right\rparen=\Prob\left\lparen M-\lVert Z\rVert\geq M-r\right\rparen\leq u_{1}(r).

Furthermore,

F_{\lVert Z\rVert}(r)=\Prob\left\lparen\sup_{v\in S}\lvert\langle v,Z\rangle\rvert\leq r\right\rparen\leq\Prob\left\lparen\lvert\langle v_{*},Z\rangle\rvert\leq r\right\rparen\leq u_{2}(r),

where the second inequality follows from the fact that
\langle v_{*},Z\rangle\sim\mathcal{N}(0,R)
and Lemma
27
.
∎

A.4
Inverse Gamma measure

Let
\alpha,\beta>0
. The inverse gamma measure
\text{Inv-Gamma}(\alpha,\beta)
on
(0,\infty)
has density

f_{\alpha,\beta}(x)\vcentcolon=\frac{\beta^{\alpha}}{\Gamma(\alpha)}x^{-\alpha-1}\exp\left\lparen-\frac{\beta}{x}\right\rparen

with respect to Lebesgue measure, where
\Gamma
is the gamma function.

Lemma 34
.

Let
X\sim\text{Inv-Gamma}(\alpha,\beta)
and
Z\sim\text{Inv-Gamma}(\alpha,\alpha)
. Let
r>0
, and define

x_{\alpha,\beta}(r)\vcentcolon=\frac{\beta\left[\exp(r)-\exp(-r)\right]}{2\alpha r}\quad\quad p_{\alpha}(r)\vcentcolon=\Prob\left\lparen\frac{1-\exp(-2r)}{2r}\leq Z\leq\frac{\exp(2r)-1}{2r}\right\rparen

Then for all
x\in(0,\infty)

p_{\alpha}(r)=\Prob\left\lparen\lvert\log(X/x_{\alpha,\beta}(r))\rvert\leq r\right\rparen\geq\Prob\left\lparen\lvert\log(X/x)\rvert\leq r\right\rparen.

Proof.

Fix
r\in(0,\infty)
. Define
h_{r}(x)\vcentcolon=\Prob\left\lparen\lvert\log(X/x)\rvert\leq r\right\rparen
. Then we have

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dx}(h_{r}(x))

\displaystyle=\frac{d}{dx}\left\lparen\Prob\left\lparen\lvert\log(X/x)\rvert\leq r\right\rparen\right\rparen

\displaystyle=\frac{d}{dx}\left\lparen\Prob\left\lparen x\exp(-r)\leq X\leq x\exp(r)\right\rparen\right\rparen

\displaystyle=\frac{d}{dx}\left\lparen\int_{x\exp(-r)}^{x\exp(r)}f_{\alpha,\beta}(t)dt\right\rparen

\displaystyle=\exp(r)f_{\alpha,\beta}(x\exp(r))-\exp(-r)f_{\alpha,\beta}(x\exp(-r))

where in the last line we used Leibniz integral rule. Setting the derivative to
0
and solving yields
x_{\alpha,\beta}(r)
. Examining its derivative, we notice that
h_{r}
is non-decreasing on
(0,x_{\alpha,\beta}(r)]
and non-increasing on
[x_{\alpha,\beta}(r),\infty)
. Therefore
x_{\alpha,\beta}(r)
is the global maximizer of
h_{r}
. Now

\displaystyle\Prob\left\lparen\lvert\log(X/x_{\alpha,\beta}(r))\rvert\leq r\right\rparen

\displaystyle=\Prob\left\lparen x_{\alpha,\beta}(r)\exp(-r)\leq X\leq x_{\alpha,\beta}(r)\exp(r)\right\rparen

\displaystyle=\Prob\left\lparen\frac{1-\exp(-2r)}{2r}\leq\frac{\alpha}{\beta}X\leq\frac{\exp(2r)-1}{2r}\right\rparen

\displaystyle=p_{\alpha}(r)

where in the last line we used that if
X\sim\text{Inv-Gamma}(\alpha,\beta)
, then
cX\sim\text{Inv-Gamma}(\alpha,c\cdot\beta)
for all
c>0
.
∎

p_{\alpha}(r)
is non-decreasing in
\alpha
for all

.

•

p_{\alpha}(r)

B.1
Truncation function

Let
\alpha,\beta\in\mathbb{R}
such that
\alpha\leq\beta
. Define

\phi_{\alpha,\beta}(x)\vcentcolon=\begin{cases}\beta&\quad if \quad$x>\beta$,\\
x&\quad if \quad$x\in[\alpha,\beta]$,\\
\alpha&\quad if \quad$x<\alpha$.\end{cases}

(9)

Lemma 36
.

The following holds for all
x\in\mathbb{R}
.

•

c\cdot\phi_{\alpha,\beta}(x)=\phi_{c\alpha,c\beta}(cx)
for all
c\in[0,\infty)
.

•

-\phi_{\alpha,\beta}(x)=\phi_{-\beta,-\alpha}(-x)
.

•

\phi_{\alpha,\beta}(x)+y=\phi_{\alpha+y,\beta+y}(x+y)
for all
y\in\mathbb{R}
.

Proof.

Just check the three possible cases for each item.
∎

Fix
n\in\mathbb{N}
. For a real valued sequence
a\vcentcolon=(a_{i})_{i=1}^{n}
, define the sequence
a^{*}=(a^{*}_{i})_{i=1}^{n}
by
a^{*}_{i}\vcentcolon=a_{\pi(i)}
for all
i\in[n]
and where
\pi:[n]\to[n]
is a permutation that orders
a
non-decreasingly, i.e.
a_{\pi(1)}\leq\dotsc\leq a_{\pi(n)}
. Note that this is well-defined since any such permutation gives the same
a^{*}
. Addition and scalar multiplication of sequences are as usual. For two sequences
a,b
, we say that
a\leq b
if
a_{i}\leq b_{i}
for all
i\in[n]
.

Lemma 37
.

Let
a=(a_{i})_{i=1}^{n}
and
b=(b_{i})_{i=1}^{n}
be real valued sequences.

a\leq b\Rightarrow a^{*}\leq b^{*}

Proof.

Let
\pi
and
\sigma
be permutations of
[n]
that order
a
and
b
non-decreasingly, respectively. Let
i\in[n]
. We show that
a_{\pi(i)}\leq b_{\sigma(i)}
. We consider two cases. If
\pi(i)\in\left\{\sigma(1),\dotsc,\sigma(i)\right\}
, then
a_{\pi(i)}\leq b_{\pi(i)}\leq b_{\sigma(i)}
. Otherwise,
\pi(i)\in\left\{\sigma(i+1),\dotsc,\sigma(n)\right\}
. This implies that there exists a
j\in\left\{i+1,\dotsc,n\right\}
such that
\pi(j)\in\left\{\sigma(1),\dotsc,\sigma(i)\right\}
, from which we conclude that
a_{\pi(i)}\leq a_{\pi(j)}\leq b_{\pi(j)}\leq b_{\sigma(i)}
.
∎

Let
k\in\left\{1,\dotsc,\lfloor n/2\rfloor\right\}
. Define

\varphi_{k}(a)\vcentcolon=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\phi_{a^{*}_{1+k},a^{*}_{n-k}}(a_{i}).

(10)

Lemma 38
.

The following holds for all real-valued sequences
a=(a_{i})_{i=1}^{n}
.

•

c\cdot\varphi_{k}(a)=\varphi_{k}(c\cdot a)
for all
c\in\mathbb{R}
.

•

\varphi_{k}(a)+n\cdot c=\varphi_{k}(a+c)
for all
c\in\mathbb{R}
.

•

\varphi_{k}(a)\leq\varphi_{k}(b)
for all sequences
b=(b_{i})_{i=1}^{n}
such that
a\leq b
.

Proof.

We start with the first item. Let
\pi:[n]\to[n]
be a permutation that orders
a
non-decreasingly. The case
c=0
is trivial. Now consider the case
c>0
. Then since
c>0
,
\pi
also orders
c\cdot a
non-decreasingly. Therefore
(c\cdot a)_{i}^{*}=c\cdot a^{*}_{i}
and

c\cdot\varphi_{k}(a)=\sum_{i=1}^{n}c\cdot\phi_{a^{*}_{k},a^{*}_{n-k}}(a_{i})=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\phi_{c\cdot a^{*}_{k},c\cdot a_{n-k}^{*}}(c\cdot a_{i})=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\phi_{(c\cdot a)^{*}_{k},(c\cdot a)_{n-k}^{*}}(c\cdot a_{i})=\varphi_{k}(c\cdot a),

where the second equality follows from the first item of Lemma
36
.
Now consider the case
c=-1
. Then the permutation
\pi
orders
-a
non-increasingly so that
(-a)_{i}^{*}=-a_{n-i}^{*}
and

-\varphi_{k}(a)=\sum_{i=1}^{n}-\phi_{a^{*}_{k},a^{*}_{n-k}}(a_{i})=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\phi_{-a^{*}_{n-k},-a^{*}_{k}}(-a_{i})=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\phi_{(-a)^{*}_{k},(-a)^{*}_{n-k}}(-a_{i})=\varphi_{k}(-a),

where the second equality follows from the second item of Lemma
37
. For the case
c<0
, we have

c\cdot\varphi_{k}(a)=(-c)\cdot-\varphi_{k}(a)=(-c)\cdot\varphi_{k}(-a)=\varphi_{k}(c\cdot a).

For the second item, we have by Lemma
37
that
a^{*}\leq b^{*}
, from which we conclude

\varphi_{k}(a)=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\phi_{a^{*}_{k},a^{*}_{n-k}}(a_{i})=ka^{*}_{k}+\sum_{i=k+1}^{n-k-1}a_{i}^{*}+ka_{n-k}^{*}\leq kb^{*}_{k}+\sum_{i=k+1}^{n-k-1}b_{i}^{*}+kb_{n-k}^{*}=\varphi_{k}(b)

∎

Lemma 39
.

Let
a=(a_{i})_{i=1}^{n}
and
b=(b_{i})_{i=1}^{n}
be real valued sequences such that
b\geq 0
. Then

\varphi_{k}(a+b)\geq\varphi_{k}(a)+\sum_{i=1}^{n-2k}b_{i}^{*}

Proof.

Let
\pi
and
\sigma
be permutations that order
a+b
and
a
non-decreasingly, respectively. By definition

\varphi_{k}(a+b)=k(a+b)_{1+k}^{*}+\sum_{i=k+1}^{n-k}(a+b)_{i}^{*}+k(a+b)_{n-k}^{*}.

We lower bound each of the three terms separately. For the first, define the sets
I_{1}\vcentcolon=\left\{\pi(1),\dotsc,\pi(1+k)\right\}
and
J_{1}\vcentcolon=\left\{\sigma(1+k),\dotsc,\sigma(n)\right\}
, and notice that

\left\lvert I_{1}\cap J_{1}\right\rvert=\lvert I_{1}\rvert+\lvert J_{1}\rvert-\lvert I_{1}\cup J_{1}\rvert\geq(1+k)+(n-k)-n=1.

Therefore, we have

(a+b)_{1+k}^{*}=a_{\pi(1+k)}+b_{\pi(1+k)}=\max\left\{a_{i}+b_{i}\mid i\in I_{1}\right\}\geq\max\left\{a_{i}+b_{i}\mid i\in I_{1}\cap J_{1}\right\}\geq a_{\sigma(1+k)},

where the last inequality uses the non-negativity of
b
. Similarly, for the third term, define the sets
I_{2}\vcentcolon=\left\{\pi(1),\dotsc,\pi(n-k)\right\}
and
J_{2}\vcentcolon=\left\{\sigma(n-k),\dotsc,\sigma(n)\right\}
, and notice that

\left\lvert I_{2}\cap J_{2}\right\rvert=\lvert I_{2}\rvert+\lvert J_{2}\rvert-\lvert I_{2}\cup J_{2}\rvert\geq(n-k)+(1+k)-n=1.

Therefore, we get

(a+b)_{n-k}^{*}=a_{\pi(n-k)}+b_{\pi(n-k)}=\max\left\{a_{i}+b_{i}\mid i\in I_{2}\right\}\geq\max\left\{a_{i}+b_{i}\mid i\in I_{2}\cap J_{2}\right\}\geq a_{\sigma(n-k)},

where again we used the non-negativity of
b
in the last inequality. It remains to lower bound the second term. Let
S^{*}\subset I_{2}
such that
\lvert S\rvert=k
and
\left\{\sigma(1),\dotsc,\sigma(k)\right\}\cap I_{2}\subset S
. Notice that

\displaystyle\sum_{i=k+1}^{n-k}(a+b)_{i}^{*}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=k+1}^{n-k}(a_{\pi(i)}+b_{\pi(i)})

\displaystyle=\max_{\begin{subarray}{c}S\subset I_{2}\\
\lvert S\rvert=k\end{subarray}}\sum_{i\in I_{2}\setminus S}(a_{i}+b_{i})

\displaystyle\geq\sum_{i\in I_{2}\setminus S^{*}}a_{i}+\sum_{i\in I_{2}\setminus S^{*}}b_{i}

Let us further bound each term. For the first, notice that by definition of
S^{*}
, we have
(I_{2}\setminus S^{*})\subset J_{1}
and
\left\lvert I_{2}\setminus S^{*}\right\rvert=n-2k
, therefore

\sum_{i\in I_{2}\setminus S^{*}}a_{i}\geq\min_{\begin{subarray}{c}T\subset J_{1}\\
\left\lvert T\right\rvert=n-2k\end{subarray}}\sum_{i\in T}a_{i}=\sum_{i=k+1}^{n-k}a_{\sigma(i)}.

For the second, we have

\sum_{i\in I_{2}\setminus S^{*}}b_{i}\geq\min_{\begin{subarray}{c}T\subset[n]\\
\left\lvert T\right\rvert=n-2k\end{subarray}}\sum_{i\in T}b_{i}=\sum_{i=1}^{n-2k}b_{i}^{*}

Combining the bounds yields the desired result.
∎

B.2
Suprema of truncated empirical processes

Let
\mathcal{T}
be a countable index set, and let
(\{Z_{i,s}\}_{s\in\mathcal{T}})_{i=1}^{n}
be independent real-valued
\mathcal{T}
-indexed stochastic processes. Define
Z\vcentcolon=\sup_{s\in\mathcal{T}}\sum_{i=1}^{n}Z_{i,s}
. For
s\in\mathcal{T}
, define
Z_{s}\vcentcolon=(Z_{i,s})_{i=1}^{n}
. For i.i.d. Rademacher random variables
(\varepsilon_{i})_{i=1}^{n}
, define
\mu\vcentcolon=\Exp\left[\sup_{s\in\mathcal{T}}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\varepsilon_{i}Z_{i,s}\right]
. We assume throughout that
\sigma^{2}\vcentcolon=\sup_{s\in\mathcal{T}}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\Exp\left[Z_{i,s}^{2}\right]<\infty
. We start by recalling the following result.

Lemma 40

(
Klein and Rio 2005
)
.

Assume that for all
s\in\mathcal{T}
and
i\in[n]
,
\Exp\left[Z_{i,s}\right]=0
, and that
R\vcentcolon=\sup_{(s,i)\in\mathcal{T}\times[n]}\lVert Z_{i,s}\rVert_{\infty}<\infty
. Define
v\vcentcolon=2R\Exp\left[Z\right]+\sigma^{2}
. Then

\Prob\left\lparen Z\geq\Exp\left[Z\right]+t\right\rparen\leq\exp\left\lparen-\frac{4v}{9R^{2}}h\left\lparen\frac{3Rt}{2v}\right\rparen\right\rparen,

where
h(t)\vcentcolon=1+t-\sqrt{1+2t}
with inverse
h^{-1}(t)=t+\sqrt{2t}
. Consequently, with probability at least
1-\delta

Z<\Exp[Z]+\frac{3R\log(1/\delta)}{2}+\sqrt{2v\log(1/\delta)}.

The following result is due to
Lugosi and Mendelson 2021
.

Lemma 41
.

Let
T>0
. Then with probability at least
1-\delta

\sup_{s\in\mathcal{T}}\left\lvert\left\{i\in[n]\mid\lvert Z_{i,s}\rvert>T\right\}\right\rvert\\
<\inf_{\varepsilon\in(0,1)}\left\{\frac{2\mu}{\varepsilon T}+\frac{\sigma^{2}}{(1-\varepsilon)^{2}T^{2}}+\sqrt{\left\lparen\frac{8\mu}{\varepsilon T}+\frac{2\sigma^{2}}{(1-\varepsilon)^{2}T^{2}}\right\rparen\log(1/\delta)}+\frac{3\log(1/\delta)}{2}\right\}.

Proof.

Let
T>0
and
\varepsilon\in(0,1)
, and define the function
\chi_{T,\varepsilon}:\mathbb{R}\to[0,1]
by

\chi_{T,\varepsilon}(x)\vcentcolon=\begin{cases}0&if $x\leq(1-\varepsilon)T$\\
\frac{x}{\varepsilon T}-\frac{1-\varepsilon}{\varepsilon}&if $x\in((1-\varepsilon)T,T]$\\
1&if $x>T$.\end{cases}

Note that
\mathbbm{1}_{(T,\infty)}\leq\chi_{T,\varepsilon}\leq\mathbbm{1}_{((1-\varepsilon)T,\infty)}
and
\chi_{T,\varepsilon}
is
(1/\varepsilon T)
-Lipschitz. Now we have

\sup_{s\in\mathcal{T}}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mathbbm{1}_{(T,\infty)}(\lvert Z_{i,s}\rvert)\leq\sup_{s\in\mathcal{T}}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\chi_{T,\varepsilon}(\lvert Z_{i,s}\rvert)\\
\leq\sup_{s\in\mathcal{T}}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\underbrace{\chi_{T,\varepsilon}(\lvert Z_{i,s}\rvert)-\Exp\left[\chi_{T,\varepsilon}(\lvert Z_{i,s}\rvert)\right]}_{\textstyle W_{i,s}\vcentcolon=}+\sup_{s\in\mathcal{T}}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\Exp\left[\chi_{T,\varepsilon}(\lvert Z_{i,s}\rvert)\right]

(11)

The second term of (
11
) is bounded by

\sup_{s\in\mathcal{T}}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\Exp\left[\chi_{T,\varepsilon}(\lvert Z_{i,s}\rvert)\right]\leq\sup_{s\in\mathcal{T}}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\Prob\left\lparen\lvert Z_{i,s}\rvert>(1-\varepsilon)T\right\rparen\leq\frac{\sigma^{2}}{(1-\varepsilon)^{2}T^{2}}.

(12)

We now turn to the first term of (
11
) which we denote by
W
. We note that
\Exp[W_{i,s}]=0
,
\lvert W_{i,s}\rvert\leq 1
, so by Lemma
28
we have with probability at least
1-\delta

W<\Exp\left[W\right]+\frac{3\log(1/\delta)}{2}+\sqrt{2\left\lparen 2\Exp[W]+\alpha^{2}\right\rparen\log(1/\delta)},

(13)

where
\alpha^{2}\vcentcolon=\sup_{s\in\mathcal{T}}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\Exp[W_{i,s}^{2}]
.
It remains to bound
\Exp[W]
and
\alpha^{2}
. The former is bounded by

\Exp\left[W\right]=\Exp\left[\sup_{s\in\mathcal{T}}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\chi_{T,\varepsilon}(\lvert Z_{i,s}\rvert)-\Exp\left[\chi_{T,\varepsilon}(\lvert Z_{i,s}\rvert)\right]\right]\\
\leq 2\Exp\left[\sup_{s\in\mathcal{T}}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\varepsilon_{i}\chi_{T,\varepsilon}(\lvert Z_{i,s}\rvert)\right]\leq\frac{2}{\varepsilon T}\Exp\left[\sup_{s\in\mathcal{T}}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\varepsilon_{i}Z_{i,s}\right],

(14)

where the first inequality is by symmetrization and the second by the contraction principle and the
(1/\varepsilon T)
-Lipschitzness of
\chi_{T,\varepsilon}\circ\lvert\cdot\rvert
. The latter is bounded by

\alpha^{2}=\sup_{s\in\mathcal{T}}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\Exp\left[W_{i,s}^{2}\right]\leq\sup_{s\in\mathcal{T}}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\Exp\left[\chi^{2}_{T,\varepsilon}(\lvert Z_{i,s}\rvert)\right]\leq\sup_{s\in\mathcal{T}}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\Prob\left\lparen\lvert Z_{i,s}\rvert>(1-\varepsilon)T\right\rparen\leq\frac{\sigma^{2}}{(1-\varepsilon)^{2}T^{2}}.

\Prob\left\lparen\frac{d^{2}}{\Tr(\widetilde{\Sigma}_{n})}\leq t\right\rparen=\Prob\left\lparen\Tr(\widetilde{\Sigma}_{n})\geq\frac{d^{2}}{t}\right\rparen\leq\frac{\Exp[\Tr(\widetilde{\Sigma}_{n})]}{d^{2}/t}=\frac{t}{d}.

20
, we obtain the desired lower bound

Q_{\Tr(\widetilde{\Sigma}_{n}^{-1})}(1-\delta)\geq Q_{d^{2}/\Tr(\widetilde{\Sigma}_{n})}(1-\delta)\geq d\cdot(1-\delta).

The upper bound follows from the simple observation
\Tr(\widetilde{\Sigma}_{n}^{-1})\leq d\cdot\lambda_{\textrm{max}}(\widetilde{\Sigma}^{-1}_{n})
. We now move to bounds on
Q_{W}(1-\delta)
, and we start with the lower bound. By definition, we have

1-\delta\leq\Prob\left\lparen W\leq Q_{W}(1-\delta)\right\rparen=1-\Exp\left[\exp(-Q_{W}(1-\delta)\cdot\lambda_{\textrm{min}}(\widetilde{\Sigma}_{n}))\right],

hence, by Jensen’s inequality

\delta\geq\Exp\left[\exp(-Q_{W}(1-\delta)\cdot\lambda_{\textrm{min}}(\widetilde{\Sigma}_{n}))\right]\geq\exp\left\lparen-Q_{W}(1-\delta)\cdot\Exp\left[\lambda_{\textrm{min}}(\widetilde{\Sigma}_{n})\right]\right\rparen,

and using the variational characterization of the smallest eigenvalue we get, for any
v\in S^{d-1}
,

\Exp\left[\lambda_{\textrm{min}}(\widetilde{\Sigma}_{n})\right]=\Exp\left[\inf_{v\in S^{d-1}}\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\langle v,\Sigma^{-1/2}X_{i}\rangle^{2}\right]\leq\Exp\left[\langle v,\Sigma^{-1/2}X\rangle^{2}\right]=1.

Therefore
Q_{W}(1-\delta)\geq\log(1/\delta)
as desired. For the upper bound, let
q\vcentcolon=Q_{\lambda_{\textrm{max}}(\widetilde{\Sigma}_{n}^{-1})}(1-\delta/2)
and define the event
A\vcentcolon=\left\{\lambda_{\textrm{max}}(\widetilde{\Sigma}^{-1}_{n})\leq q\right\}
which satisfies
\Prob\left\lparen A\right\rparen\geq 1-\delta/2
. Notice that

\Prob\left\lparen W\leq t\right\rparen\geq\Exp\left[\left\{1-\exp\left\lparen-\frac{t}{\lambda_{\textrm{max}}(\widetilde{\Sigma}^{-1}_{n})}\right\rparen\right\}\mathbbm{1}_{A}((X_{i})_{i=1}^{n})\right]\geq(1-\delta/2)\left\lparen 1-\exp(t/q)\right\rparen.

Taking
t\geq q\cdot\log(2/\delta)
ensures that the above probability is at least
1-\delta
. By the minimality of the quantile, we get that
Q_{W}(1-\delta)\leq Q_{\lambda_{\textrm{max}}(\widetilde{\Sigma}_{n}^{-1})}(1-\delta/2)\cdot\log(2/\delta)
, which is the desired upper bound.

Proof of Corollary

Proof.

We claim that for all the allowed sample sizes,

Q_{\lambda_{\textrm{max}}(\widetilde{\Sigma}_{n}))}(1-\delta/2)\leq 2.

Indeed, the restriction on the sample size

is chosen in such a way that by the upper bound in Proposition

Q_{1-\lambda_{\textrm{min}}(\widetilde{\Sigma}_{n})}(1-\delta/2)\leq\frac{1}{2}

∎

Using Lemma 2.5 in

E.1
Proof of Theorem
11

Fix a distribution
P\in\mathcal{P}_{2}(P_{X},\sigma^{2})
. We will prove an upper bound on the risk of the proposed procedure under
P
. We follow the approach developed by
Lugosi and Mendelson 2019a
. Define

\phi(w)=\max_{v\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}\psi_{k}(w,v),

and note that by definition of
\hat{w}_{n,\delta}
, we have

\psi_{k}(\hat{w}_{n,k},w^{*})\leq\phi(\hat{w}_{n,k})\leq\phi(w^{*}).

(28)

The key idea of the proof is to show that
\lVert\hat{w}_{n,k}-w^{*}\rVert_{\Sigma}
is small, by simultaneously showing that

•

For all
w\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, if
\lVert w-w^{*}\rVert_{\Sigma}
is large, then so is
\psi_{k}(w,w^{*})
,

•

\phi(w^{*})
is small.

The combination of these statements combined with (
28
) will show that
\lVert\hat{w}_{n,k}-w^{*}\rVert_{\Sigma}
is indeed small. Define

\Delta(\delta)\vcentcolon=50\sqrt{\frac{\sigma^{2}[d+\log(4/\delta)]}{n}}

All the following lemmas are stated under the conditions of Theorem
11
.
The first step of the proof is a simple application of Lemma
43
.

Lemma 45
.

\Prob\left\lparen\sup_{\lVert v\rVert_{\Sigma}\leq 1}n^{-1}\varphi_{k}\left[(\langle\nabla e(\langle w^{*},X_{i}\rangle-Y_{i}),v\rangle)_{i=1}^{n}\right]>\Delta(\delta)\right\rparen\leq\delta/2

Proof.

For
v\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
such that
\lVert v\rVert_{\Sigma}\leq 1
and
i\in[n]
, define

Z_{i,v}\vcentcolon=\frac{1}{n}\langle\nabla e(\langle w^{*},X_{i}\rangle-Y_{i}),v\rangle=\frac{1}{n}\xi_{i}\langle X_{i},v\rangle

Our aim is to apply Lemma
43
, so we make the necessary computations here. We have

\displaystyle\Exp\left[Z_{i,v}\right]

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{n}\langle\Exp\left[\nabla e(\langle w^{*},X_{i}\rangle-Y_{i})\right],v\rangle=\frac{1}{n}\langle\nabla E(w^{*}),v\rangle=0,

\displaystyle\sup_{\lVert v\rVert_{\Sigma}\leq 1}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\Exp\left[Z_{i,v}^{2}\right]

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{n}\sup_{\lVert v\rVert_{\Sigma}=1}\Exp\left[\xi^{2}\langle X,v\rangle^{2}\right]\leq\frac{\sigma^{2}}{n}.

where the last inequality follows from the assumption
\Exp\left[\xi^{2}\mid X\right]\leq\sigma^{2}
. Now, for independent Rademacher variables
(\varepsilon_{i})_{i=1}^{n}
, we have

\displaystyle\Exp\left[\sup_{\lVert v\rVert_{\Sigma}=1}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\varepsilon_{i}Z_{i,v}\right]

\displaystyle=\Exp\left[\sup_{\lVert v\rVert_{\Sigma}=1}\left\langle\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\varepsilon_{i}\xi_{i}X_{i},v\right\rangle\right]

\displaystyle=\Exp\left[\left\lVert\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\varepsilon_{i}\xi_{i}X_{i}\right\rVert_{\Sigma^{-1}}\right]

\displaystyle\leq\Exp\left[\left\lVert\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\varepsilon_{i}\xi_{i}X_{i}\right\rVert_{\Sigma^{-1}}^{2}\right]^{1/2}

\displaystyle=\sqrt{\frac{\Exp[\xi^{2}\lVert X\rVert_{\Sigma^{-1}}^{2}]}{n}}\leq\sqrt{\frac{\sigma^{2}d}{n}}.

Where again we have used the assumption
\Exp\left[\xi^{2}\mid X\right]\leq\sigma^{2}
. Recalling that
k=8\log(2/(\delta/2))
from the statement of the theorem, and applying Lemma
43
with the above constants yields the result.
∎

From this result, we can deduce the following estimate, which will help us bound
\phi(w^{*})
later on.

Corollary 46
.

For any
r\in(0,\infty)
,

\Prob\left\lparen\sup_{\lVert v-w^{*}\rVert_{\Sigma}<r}\psi_{k}(w^{*},v)>r\cdot\Delta(\delta)\right\rparen\leq\delta/2.

Proof.

We have

\displaystyle\sup_{\lVert v-w^{*}\rVert_{\Sigma}<r}\psi_{k}(w^{*},v)

\displaystyle=\sup_{\lVert v-w^{*}\rVert_{\Sigma}<r}\varphi_{k}\left[\left\lparen e(\langle w^{*},X_{i}\rangle-Y_{i})-e(\langle v,X_{i}\rangle-Y_{i})\right\rparen_{i=1}^{n}\right]

\displaystyle=\sup_{\lVert v-w^{*}\rVert_{\Sigma}<r}\varphi_{k}\left[\left\lparen-\langle\nabla e(\langle w^{*},X_{i}\rangle-Y_{i}),v-w^{*}\rangle-\frac{1}{2}\langle X_{i},v-w^{*}\rangle^{2}\right\rparen_{i=1}^{n}\right]

\displaystyle\leq\sup_{\lVert v-w^{*}\rVert_{\Sigma}<r}\varphi_{k}\left[\left\lparen-\langle\nabla e(\langle w^{*},X_{i}\rangle-Y_{i}),v-w^{*}\rangle\right\rparen_{i=1}^{n}\right]

\displaystyle=\sup_{\lVert v-w^{*}\rVert_{\Sigma}<r}r\cdot\varphi_{k}\left[\left\lparen\left\langle\nabla e(\left\langle w^{*},X_{i}\right\rangle-Y_{i}),\frac{v-w^{*}}{r}\right\rangle\right\rparen_{i=1}^{n}\right]

\displaystyle=r\cdot\sup_{\lVert v\rVert_{\Sigma}<1}\varphi_{k}\left[\left\lparen\langle\nabla e(\left\langle w^{*},X_{i}\right\rangle-Y_{i}),v\rangle\right\rparen_{i=1}^{n}\right]

where the first line is by definition, the second holds since
e
is quadratic so its second order Taylor expansion is exact, the third by the third item of Lemma
38
, and the fourth by the first item of Lemma
38
. Applying Lemma
45
to the last line yields the result.
∎

The key technical novelty of this proof is the following lemma, which uses our new results Proposition
18
and Lemma
39
.

Lemma 47
.

Let
r\in[8\Delta(\delta),\infty)
. Then

\Prob\left\lparen\inf_{\lVert v-w^{*}\rVert\geq r}\psi_{k}(v,w^{*})<\frac{r^{2}}{8}-r\Delta(\delta)\right\rparen\leq\delta

Proof.

We start with the case
\lVert v-w^{*}\rVert_{\Sigma}=r
. We have

\displaystyle\inf_{\lVert v-w^{*}\rVert_{\Sigma}=r}\psi_{k}(v,w^{*})

\displaystyle=\inf_{\lVert v-w^{*}\rVert_{\Sigma}=r}n^{-1}\varphi_{k}\left[\left\lparen e(\langle v,X_{i}\rangle-Y_{i})-e(\langle w^{*},X_{i}\rangle-Y_{i})\right\rparen_{i=1}^{n}\right]

\displaystyle=\inf_{\lVert v-w^{*}\rVert_{\Sigma}=r}n^{-1}\varphi_{k}\left[\left\lparen\langle\nabla e(\langle w^{*},X_{i}\rangle-Y_{i}),v-w^{*}\rangle+\frac{1}{2}\langle v-w^{*},X_{i}\rangle^{2}\right\rparen_{i=1}^{n}\right]

\displaystyle=\inf_{v\in S^{d-1}}n^{-1}\varphi_{k}\left[\left\lparen r\cdot\langle\nabla e(\langle w^{*},X_{i}\rangle-Y_{i}),\Sigma^{-1/2}v\rangle+\frac{r^{2}}{2}\langle v,\Sigma^{-1/2}X_{i}\rangle^{2}\right\rparen_{i=1}^{n}\right].

Define
\widetilde{X}_{i}=\Sigma^{-1/2}X_{i}
, and
Z_{i,v}\vcentcolon=\langle v,\widetilde{X}_{i}\rangle^{2}
for
(i,v)\in[n]\times S^{d-1}
. Then we have by Lemma
39
,

\displaystyle\inf_{\lVert v-w^{*}\rVert_{\Sigma}=r}\psi_{k}(v,w^{*})

\displaystyle\geq r\cdot\inf_{v\in S^{d-1}}n^{-1}\varphi_{k}\left[\left\lparen\langle\nabla e(\langle w^{*},X_{i}\rangle-Y_{i}),\Sigma^{-1/2}v\rangle\right\rparen_{i=1}^{n}\right]+r^{2}\cdot\inf_{v\in S^{d-1}}n^{-1}\sum_{i=1}^{n-2k}Z_{i,v}^{*}

\displaystyle=\frac{r^{2}}{2}\cdot\inf_{v\in S^{d-1}}n^{-1}\sum_{i=1}^{n-2k}Z_{i,v}^{*}-r\cdot\sup_{\lVert v\rVert_{\Sigma}=1}n^{-1}\varphi_{k}\left[\left\lparen\langle\nabla e(\langle w^{*},X_{i}\rangle-Y_{i}),v\rangle\right\rparen_{i=1}^{n}\right]

The second term is bounded with probability
1-\delta/2
by
r\cdot\Delta(\delta)
by Lemma
45
. For the first term, the restriction on the sample size in Theorem
11
is chosen such that by Proposition
17
, with probability at least
1-\delta^{2}/2\geq 1-\delta/2

\inf_{v\in S^{d-1}}n^{-1}\sum_{i=1}^{n-2k}Z_{i,v}^{*}\geq\frac{1}{4}

Therefore, with probability at least
1-\delta

\inf_{\lVert v-w^{*}\rVert_{\Sigma}=r}\psi_{k}(v,w^{*})\geq\frac{r^{2}}{8}-r\Delta(\delta).

We now extend this to all vectors
w\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
such that
\lVert w-w^{*}\rVert_{\Sigma}\geq r
. On the same event, if
\lVert w-w^{*}\rVert_{\Sigma}=R>r
, then
v\vcentcolon=w^{*}+\frac{r}{R}(w-w^{*})
satisfies
\lVert v-w^{*}\rVert_{\Sigma}=r
, and

\displaystyle\psi_{k}(w,w^{*})

\displaystyle=n^{-1}\varphi_{k}\left\lparen(\langle\nabla e(\langle w^{*},X_{i}\rangle-Y_{i}),w-w^{*}\rangle+\frac{1}{2}\langle w-w^{*},X_{i}\rangle^{2})_{i=1}^{n}\right\rparen

\displaystyle=n^{-1}\varphi_{k}\left[\left\lparen\frac{R}{r}\langle\nabla e(\langle w^{*},X_{i}\rangle-Y_{i}),v-w^{*}\rangle+\frac{R^{2}}{r^{2}}\frac{1}{2}\langle v-w^{*},X_{i}\rangle^{2}\right\rparen_{i=1}^{n}\right]

\displaystyle\geq n^{-1}\varphi_{k}\left[\left\lparen\frac{R}{r}\langle\nabla e(\langle w^{*},X_{i}\rangle-Y_{i}),v-w^{*}\rangle+\frac{R}{r}\frac{1}{2}\langle v-w^{*},X_{i}\rangle^{2})_{i=1}^{n}\right\rparen_{i=1}^{n}\right]

\displaystyle=\frac{R}{r}\cdot\psi_{k}(v,w^{*})

\displaystyle\geq\psi_{k}(v,w^{*})

where the first inequality follows from the fact that
R/r>1
,
\langle v-w^{*},X_{i}\rangle^{2}>0
, and Lemma
38
, and the second inequality follows from the fact that by the condition on
r
, we have
\psi_{k}(v,w^{*})\geq 0
on the event we are considering.
∎

We are now ready to state the proof of Theorem
11
. Set
r\vcentcolon=20\Delta(\delta)
, and recall from (
28
) that

\displaystyle\psi_{k}(\hat{w}_{n,k},w^{*})\leq\phi(w^{*})

\displaystyle=\sup_{v\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}\psi_{k}(w^{*},v)

\displaystyle=\max\left\{\sup_{\lVert v-w^{*}\rVert_{\Sigma}\geq r}\psi_{k}(w^{*},v),\sup_{\lVert v-w^{*}\rVert_{\Sigma}<r}\psi_{k}(w^{*},v)\right\}

\displaystyle=\max\left\{-\inf_{\lVert v-w^{*}\rVert\geq r}\psi_{k}(v,w^{*}),\sup_{\lVert v-w^{*}\rVert_{\Sigma}<r}\psi_{k}(w^{*},v)\right\},

where the last line uses the fact that
\psi_{k}(w,v)=-\psi_{k}(v,w)
.
Now by combining Corollary
46
and Lemma
47
, we have with probability
1-\delta
that the first term in the above maximum is negative, while the second is bounded by
r\cdot\Delta(\delta)=20\Delta^{2}(\delta)
. On the other hand, we have on the same event by Lemma
47
that

\inf_{\lVert v-w^{*}\rVert_{\Sigma}\geq r}\psi_{k}(v,w^{*})\geq\frac{r^{2}}{8}-r\Delta(\delta)=30\Delta^{2}(\delta)

Therefore, we conclude that with probability at least
1-\delta

\lVert\hat{w}_{n,k}-w^{*}\rVert_{\Sigma}\leq 20\Delta(\delta).

finally, noticing that this implies, with probability at least

\mathcal{E}(\hat{w}_{n,k})=\frac{1}{2}\lVert\hat{w}_{n,k}-w^{*}\rVert_{\Sigma}^{2}\leq 20^{2}\Delta^{2}(\delta),

finishes the proof.

\displaystyle\Exp\left[\sup_{\lVert v\rVert_{H}=1}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\varepsilon_{i}Z_{i,v}\right]

Exploiting Sign Symmetries in Minimizing Sums of Rational Functions Thanks: Submitted to the editors DATE.
Feng Guo, Jie Wang, Jianhao Zheng

Abstract
This paper is devoted to the problem of minimizing a sum of rational functions over a basic semialgebraic set. We provide a hierarchy of sum of squares (SOS) relaxations that is dual to the generalized moment problem approach due to Bugarin, Henrion, and Lasserre. The investigation of the dual SOS aspect offers two benefits: 1) it allows us to conduct a convergence rate analysis for the hierarchy; 2) it leads to a sign symmetry adapted hierarchy consisting of block-diagonal semidefinite relaxations. When the problem possesses correlative sparsity as well as sign symmetries, we propose sparse semidefinite relaxations by exploiting both structures. Various numerical experiments are performed to demonstrate the efficiency of our approach. Finally, an application to maximizing sums of generalized Rayleigh quotients is presented.



keywords

sum of rational functions, sign symmetry, semidefinite relaxation, correlative sparsity, generalized Rayleigh quotient

Introduction

In this paper, we consider the optimization problem of minimizing a sum of rational functions:

\rho\coloneqq\inf_{x\in\mathbf{K}}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\frac{p_{i}(\mathbf{x})}{q_{i}(\mathbf{x})},

(SRFO)

where

\mathbf{K}\coloneqq\{\mathbf{x}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}\mid g_{j}(\mathbf{x})\geq 0,\ j=1,\ldots,m\},

(1)

and
p_{i},q_{i},g_{j}
are polynomials in variables
\mathbf{x}=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})
. Throughout the paper, we make the following assumption on (
SRFO
):

Assumption 1
.

(i)
\mathbf{K}
is compact; (ii)
q_{i}>0
on
\mathbf{K}
for
i=1,\ldots,N
.

The problem (
SRFO
) has applications in various fields, including computer vision
[
7
,
9
]
, multi-user MIMO systems
[
21
]
, sparse Fisher discriminant analysis in pattern recognition
[
4
,
5
,
32
]
.

Given the potentially large number of terms
N
and the absence of convexity (resp. concavity) assumptions on
p_{i}
(resp.
q_{i}
), globally solving (
SRFO
) or achieving a close approximation of the optimum
\rho
presents significant challenges.
In literature, (
SRFO
) is often called a
sum-of-ratios program
which stands as one of the most complex fractional programs encountered to date. For strategies of tackling sum-of-ratios programs under specific assumptions regarding concavity and linearity of ratios, the reader may refer to the survey
[
24
]
and
[
33
, Table 1]
.

In the scenario where
N=1
, by utilizing the polynomial structure in (
SRFO
) and Positivstellensätze from real algebraic geometry, some hierarchies of semidefinite programming (SDP) relaxations were proposed in
[
8
,
18
]
. The basic idea is to maximizing a number
\gamma\in{\mathbb{R}}
subject to the nonnegativity of
p_{1}-\gamma q_{1}
on
\mathbf{K}
which can be ensured by certain sum of squares (SOS) representations. In
[
6
]
, the problem of minimizing a rational function was reformulated as a polynomial optimization problem and solved by the exact Jacobian SDP relaxation method proposed by Nie
[
17
]
.

For the case that
N>1
, one could attempt to reduce all rational functions
p_{i}/q_{i}

to the same denominator and apply the hierarchies of SDP relaxations mentioned above.
However, due to the potentially large value of
N
, the resulting unified denominator
may have a significantly high degree, which makes it impractical to even solve the
first order relaxations of the hierarchies.
To overcome this difficulty, Bugarin, Henrion, and Lasserre
[
3
]
reformulated (
SRFO
) as an equivalent infinite-dimensional linear program which is a particular instance of the generalized moment problem (GMP) with
N
unknown measures. The GMP can be relaxed into a hierarchy of SDPs which provides increasingly tight lower bounds on
\rho
. When a correlative sparsity pattern is present in the polynomial data of (
SRFO
), a sparse GMP reformulation for (
SRFO
) and a corresponding hierarchy of sparse SDP relaxations were also proposed in
[
3
]
.
By employing pushforward measures, Lasserre et al.
[
12
]
gave an approach yielding convergent upper bounds on
\rho
.

Contributions.
Our main contributions are summarized as follows:

1.

By studying the Lagrange dual problem, we unveil that the core strategy of the GMP approach is to replace the terms
p_{i}/q_{i}
,
i=2,\ldots,N
by polynomial approximations from below on
\mathbf{K}
, leading to a problem with a single denominator. As a result, we provide a hierarchy of SOS relaxations that is dual to the GMP approach due to Bugarin, Henrion, and Lasserre.
Moreover, we are able to conduct a convergence rate analysis for the hierarchy of SDP relaxations for (
SRFO
).

2.

We present a sign symmetry adapted hierarchy consisting of block-diagonal SDP relaxations for (
SRFO
). Furthermore, when both correlative sparsity and sign symmetries are present in the polynomial data of (
SRFO
), we show that the exploitation of sign symmetries can be naturally incorporated into the sparse hierarchy of SDP relaxations for (
SRFO
), thereby further reducing the computational cost. Various numerical experiments demonstrate the efficiency of our approach.

The rest of the paper is organized as follows.
We first recall some preliminaries and the GMP approach for (
SRFO
)
in Section
2
. In Section
3
, we investigate the dual aspect
of the GMP approach and conduct convergence rate analysis for the SDP hierarchy.
In Section
4
, we develop sparse SDP relaxations for (
SRFO
)
by exploiting sign symmetries as well as correlative sparsity present in the problem data. Numerical experiments are presented in Section
5
and an application to maximizing sums of generalized Rayleigh quotients is provided in Section
6
.

2.1
Sums of squares and moments

We recall some background about SOS polynomials
and the dual theory of
moment matrices
.
A polynomial
f(\mathbf{x})\in{\mathbb{R}}[\mathbf{x}]
is said to be a

sum of squares
if it can be written as
f(\mathbf{x})=\sum_{i=1}^{t}f_{i}(\mathbf{x})^{2}
for some
f_{1}(\mathbf{x}),\ldots,f_{t}(\mathbf{x})\in{\mathbb{R}}[\mathbf{x}]
. Let

\Sigma[\mathbf{x}]
denote the set of SOS polynomials in
{\mathbb{R}}[\mathbf{x}]
.

Let
\mathbf{g}\coloneqq\{g_{1},\ldots,g_{m}\}
be the set of polynomials that defines the
semialgebraic set
\mathbf{K}
in (
1
). We denote by

\mathcal{Q}(\mathbf{g})\coloneqq\left\{\sigma_{0}+\sum_{j=1}^{m}\sigma_{j}g_{j}\ \middle|\ \sigma_{j}\in\Sigma[\mathbf{x}],j\in\{0\}\cup[m]\right\}

the
quadratic module
generated by
\mathbf{g}
and denote by

\mathcal{Q}_{k}(\mathbf{g})\coloneqq\left\{\sigma_{0}+\sum_{j=1}^{m}\sigma_{j}g_{j}\ \middle|\ \sigma_{0},\sigma_{j}\in\Sigma[\mathbf{x}],\deg(\sigma_{0}),\deg(\sigma_{j}g_{j})\leq 2k,j\in[m]\right\}

the
k
-th
truncated quadratic module
.
It is clear that if
f\in\mathcal{Q}(\mathbf{g})
, then

f(\mathbf{x})\geq 0
for any
\mathbf{x}\in\mathbf{K}
though the converse is not necessarily
true.

Given a (pseudo-moment) sequence of real numbers
\mathbf{y}\coloneqq(y_{{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}})_{{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}\in\mathbb{N}^{n}}
,
the
k
-th order
moment matrix
is the matrix
\mathbf{M}_{k}(\mathbf{y})

indexed by
\mathbb{N}^{n}_{k}
with the
({\boldsymbol{\alpha}},{\boldsymbol{\beta}})
-th entry being
y_{{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}+{\boldsymbol{\beta}}}
. Given a polynomial
f(\mathbf{x})=\sum_{{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}}f_{{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}}\mathbf{x}^{{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}}
, the
k
-th order
localizing
matrix

\mathbf{M}_{k}(f\mathbf{y})
indexed by
\mathbb{N}^{n}_{k}
is defined by
[\mathbf{M}_{k}(f\mathbf{y})]_{{\boldsymbol{\beta}},{\boldsymbol{\gamma}}}=\sum_{{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}}f_{{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}}y_{{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}+{\boldsymbol{\beta}}+{\boldsymbol{\gamma}}}
.
The Riesz functional
L_{\mathbf{y}}
on
{\mathbb{R}}[\mathbf{x}]
is defined by
L_{\mathbf{y}}\left(\sum_{{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}}f_{{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}}\mathbf{x}^{{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}}\right)\coloneqq\sum_{{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}}f_{{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}}y_{{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}}
.

Definition 2
(Archimedean condition).

We say that
\mathcal{Q}(\mathbf{g})
is Archimedean if
there exists
M>0
such that
M-x_{1}^{2}-\cdots-x_{n}^{2}\in\mathcal{Q}(\mathbf{g})
.

Note that the Archimedean condition implies that
\mathbf{K}
is compact and the converse is not necessarily true. However, for any compact set
\mathbf{K}
, we could always
force the associated quadratic module to be Archimedean
by adding a redundant constraint
M-x_{1}^{2}-\cdots-x_{n}^{2}\geq 0
in the description of

\mathbf{K}
for sufficiently large
M
.

Theorem 3
.

[
22
, Putinar’s Positivstellensatz]

Suppose that
\mathcal{Q}(\mathbf{g})
is Archimedean.
If a polynomial
f\in{\mathbb{R}}[\mathbf{x}]
is positive on
\mathbf{K}
, then
f\in\mathcal{Q}_{k}(\mathbf{g})
for some
k\in\mathbb{N}
.

2.2
The GMP reformulation and SDP relaxations

In
[
3
]
, Bugarin et al. reformulated (
SRFO
) as the following GMP:

\left\{\begin{aligned} \inf_{\mu_{i}\in\mathcal{M}(\mathbf{K})_{+}}&\ \sum_{i=1}^{N}\int_{\mathbf{K}}p_{i}\mathrm{d}\mu_{i}\\
\text{s.t.}\quad\,&\ \int_{\mathbf{K}}q_{1}\mathrm{d}\mu_{1}=1,\\
&\ \int_{\mathbf{K}}\mathbf{x}^{{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}}q_{i}\mathrm{d}\mu_{i}=\int_{\mathbf{K}}\mathbf{x}^{{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}}q_{1}\mathrm{d}\mu_{1},\quad\forall{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}\in\mathbb{N}^{n},i\in[N]\setminus\{1\},\end{aligned}\right.

(P)

where
\mathcal{M}(\mathbf{K})_{+}
denotes the set of finite positive Borel measures
supported on
\mathbf{K}
.

Let

\displaystyle d_{j}

\displaystyle\coloneqq\lceil\deg(g_{j})/2\rceil,\quad j\in[m],

\displaystyle d_{\min}

\displaystyle\coloneqq\max\,\{\lceil\deg(p_{i})/2\rceil,\lceil\deg(q_{i})/2\rceil,i\in[N];\,d_{j},j\in[m]\}.

Based on the reformulation (
P
), Bugarin et al. further proposed the following hierarchy of SDP relaxations for (
SRFO
) (
k\geq d_{\min}
):

\left\{\begin{aligned} \inf_{\mathbf{y}_{i}}&\ \sum_{i=1}^{N}L_{\mathbf{y}_{i}}(p_{i})\\
\text{s.t.}&\ \mathbf{M}_{k}(\mathbf{y}_{i})\succeq 0,\quad i\in[N],\\
&\ \mathbf{M}_{k-d_{j}}(g_{j}\mathbf{y}_{i})\succeq 0,\quad i\in[N],j\in[m],\\
&\ L_{\mathbf{y}_{1}}(q_{1})=1,\\
&\ L_{\mathbf{y}_{i}}(\mathbf{x}^{{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}}q_{i})=L_{\mathbf{y}_{1}}(\mathbf{x}^{{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}}q_{1}),\quad\forall{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}\in\mathbb{N}^{n}_{2k-\max\{\deg(q_{1}),\deg(q_{i})\}},i\in[N]\setminus\{1\}.\end{aligned}\right.

(P
k
)

It was shown in
[
3
]
that under Assumption
1
and the Archimedean condition, the sequence of optima of (
P

k

) converges to
\rho
as
k\to\infty
.

3
The dual perspective and convergence rate analysis

In this section, we will unveil the underlying principle of the GMP reformulation (
P
) for (
SRFO
) from the dual perspective, which enables us to achieve a convergence rate analysis of the hierarchy of SDP relaxations for (
SRFO
).

3.1
The dual perspective

Let us derive the Lagrange dual problem of the GMP reformulation (
P
) for (
SRFO
). Note that there are infinitely many
constraints involved in (
P
).
To formulate the dual problem, we need to embed these constraints into an appropriate functional space paired with a dual space
[
25
]
. Let

\mathcal{Y}
be the space of all functions
\omega:\mathbb{N}^{n}\to{\mathbb{R}}
equipped with natural algebraic operations of addition and
multiplication by a scalar.
We associate this space with the dual space
\mathcal{Y}^{*}
consisting of
functions
\omega^{*}:\mathbb{N}^{n}\to{\mathbb{R}}
such that only a finite number of
values
\omega^{*}({\boldsymbol{\alpha}}),{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}\in\mathbb{N}^{n}
are nonzero. For

\omega\in\mathcal{Y}
and
\omega^{*}\in\mathcal{Y}^{*}
, define the
scalar product

\langle\omega^{*},\omega\rangle\coloneqq\sum_{{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}\in\mathbb{N}^{n}}\omega^{*}({\boldsymbol{\alpha}})\omega({\boldsymbol{\alpha}}),

where the summation is performed over
{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}

in the finite support set of
\omega^{*}
. Equivalently, we can take

\mathcal{Y}^{*}
to be the polynomial ring
{\mathbb{R}}[\mathbf{x}]
.
For any

h(\mathbf{x})=\sum_{{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}\in\mathbb{N}^{n}}h_{{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}}\mathbf{x}^{{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}}\in{\mathbb{R}}[\mathbf{x}]
and

\omega\in\mathcal{Y}
, define the scalar product

\langle h,\omega\rangle\coloneqq\sum_{{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}\in\mathbb{N}^{n}}h_{{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}}\omega({\boldsymbol{\alpha}}).

Clearly, for each
i\in[N]\setminus\{1\}
, the mapping
\omega_{i}:\mathbb{N}^{n}\to{\mathbb{R}}

defined by

\omega_{i}({\boldsymbol{\alpha}})\coloneqq\int_{\mathbf{K}}\mathbf{x}^{{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}}q_{i}\mathrm{d}\mu_{i}-\int_{\mathbf{K}}\mathbf{x}^{{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}}q_{1}\mathrm{d}\mu_{1},\ \ \forall{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}\in\mathbb{N}^{n},

belongs to the space
\mathcal{Y}
, and then the second part of constraints of
(
P
) can be expressed as
\omega_{i}=0,i\in[N]\setminus\{1\}
. Let
h_{i}=\sum_{{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}\in\mathbb{N}^{n}}h^{i}_{{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}}\mathbf{x}^{{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}}\in{\mathbb{R}}[\mathbf{x}]
be the dual variable associated with the constraint
\omega_{i}=0,i\in[N]\setminus\{1\}
,
and let
c
be the dual variable associated with the constraint
\int_{\mathbf{K}}q_{1}\mathrm{d}\mu_{1}=1
.
The Lagrangian of (
P
) now can be written as

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}(\{\mu_{i}\},\{h_{i}\},c)\coloneqq

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{N}\int_{\mathbf{K}}p_{i}\mathrm{d}\mu_{i}-c\left(\int_{\mathbf{K}}q_{1}\mathrm{d}\mu_{1}-1\right)-\sum_{i=2}^{N}\langle h_{i},\omega_{i}\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle c+\sum_{i=1}^{N}\int_{\mathbf{K}}p_{i}\mathrm{d}\mu_{i}-\int_{\mathbf{K}}cq_{1}\mathrm{d}\mu_{1}-\sum_{i=2}^{N}\sum_{{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}\in\mathbb{N}^{n}}h^{i}_{{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}}\left(\int_{\mathbf{K}}\mathbf{x}^{{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}}q_{i}\mathrm{d}\mu_{i}-\int_{\mathbf{K}}\mathbf{x}^{{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}}q_{1}\mathrm{d}\mu_{1}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle c+\int_{\mathbf{K}}\left(p_{1}-cq_{1}+q_{1}\sum_{i=2}^{N}h_{i}\right)\mathrm{d}\mu_{1}+\sum_{i=2}^{N}\int_{\mathbf{K}}\left(p_{i}-q_{i}h_{i}\right)\mathrm{d}\mu_{i}.

The Lagrange dual function of (
P
) is

\inf_{\mu_{i}\in\mathcal{M}(\mathbf{K})_{+}}\mathcal{L}(\{\mu_{i}\},\{h_{i}\},c)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}c,&\text{if}\ p_{1}-q_{1}(c-\sum_{i=2}^{N}h_{i})\geq 0,\ p_{i}-q_{i}h_{i}\geq 0\ \text{on}\ \mathbf{K},\\
-\infty,&\text{otherwise}.\end{array}\right.

Hence, the Lagrange dual problem of (
P
) is

\left\{\begin{aligned} \sup_{c,h_{i}}&\ c\\
\text{s.t.}&\ p_{1}(\mathbf{x})+\left(\sum_{i=2}^{N}h_{i}(\mathbf{x})-c\right)q_{1}(\mathbf{x})\geq 0,\quad\forall\mathbf{x}\in\mathbf{K},\\
&\ p_{i}(\mathbf{x})-h_{i}(\mathbf{x})q_{i}(\mathbf{x})\geq 0,\quad\forall\mathbf{x}\in\mathbf{K},i\in[N]\setminus\{1\},\\
&\ h_{i}\in{\mathbb{R}}[\mathbf{x}],\quad i\in[N]\setminus\{1\}.\end{aligned}\right.

(D)

For each
i\in[N]\setminus\{1\}
, define

\mathcal{H}_{i}\coloneqq\left\{h\in{\mathbb{R}}[\mathbf{x}]\,\middle|\,\frac{p_{i}(\mathbf{x})}{q_{i}(\mathbf{x})}\geq h(\mathbf{x}),\ \ \forall\mathbf{x}\in\mathbf{K}\right\}.

Since each
q_{i}>0
on
\mathbf{K}
, the Lagrange dual problem of (
P
) can be also expressed as

\sup_{h_{i}\in\mathcal{H}_{i}}\inf_{\mathbf{x}\in\mathbf{K}}\ \frac{p_{1}(\mathbf{x})}{q_{1}(\mathbf{x})}+\sum_{i=2}^{N}h_{i}(\mathbf{x}).

Consequently, the underlying principle of the GMP
reformulation (
P
) for (
SRFO
), derived from the dual aspect,
can be unfolded as follows: 1) replacing the terms

\frac{p_{i}}{q_{i}}
,
i\in[N]\setminus\{1\}
by polynomial approximations

h_{i}\in{\mathbb{R}}[\mathbf{x}]
from below on
\mathbf{K}
; 2) computing the infimum of
the resulting function which contains only a single denominator; 3) letting the approximations
h_{i}
’s vary and taking the supremum.

Theorem 4
.

Under Assumption
1
, the optimum of (
D
) equals
\rho
.

Proof.

Denote the optimum of (
D
) by
\tau
.
Clearly, we have
\tau\leq\rho
. Fix an arbitrary
\varepsilon>0
. By the Stone-Weierstrass theorem, for each
i\in[N]\setminus\{1\}
, there exists
\hat{h}_{i}\in{\mathbb{R}}[\mathbf{x}]
such that

\sup_{\mathbf{x}\in\mathbf{K}}\left|\frac{p_{i}(\mathbf{x})}{q_{i}(\mathbf{x})}-\hat{h}_{i}(\mathbf{x})\right|\leq\frac{\varepsilon}{2(N-1)}.

Letting
h_{i}\coloneqq\hat{h}_{i}-\varepsilon/2(N-1)
, we have

\frac{p_{i}(\mathbf{x})}{q_{i}(\mathbf{x})}-\frac{\varepsilon}{N-1}\leq h_{i}(\mathbf{x})\leq\frac{p_{i}(\mathbf{x})}{q_{i}(\mathbf{x})},\quad\forall\mathbf{x}\in\mathbf{K}.

Hence,
h_{i}\in\mathcal{H}_{i}
and

\tau\geq\inf_{\mathbf{x}\in\mathbf{K}}\frac{p_{1}(\mathbf{x})}{q_{1}(\mathbf{x})}+\sum_{i=2}^{N}h_{i}(\mathbf{x})\geq\inf_{\mathbf{x}\in\mathbf{K}}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\frac{p_{i}(\mathbf{x})}{q_{i}(\mathbf{x})}-\varepsilon\geq\rho-\varepsilon.

Thus,
(\rho-\varepsilon,h_{2},\ldots,h_{N})
is feasible to (
D
).
As
\varepsilon
is arbitrary, we obtain
\tau=\rho
.

Remark 5
.

Theorem
4
can also be derived from strong duality between (
P
) and (
D
). In fact, as
\mathbf{K}
is compact, there exist
constant polynomials
\hat{h}_{i}
’s and
\hat{c}\in{\mathbb{R}}
such that
p_{i}/q_{i}>\hat{h}_{i}
,
i\in[N]\setminus\{1\}
, and

p_{1}/q_{1}+\sum_{i=2}^{N}\hat{h}_{i}>\hat{c}
on
\mathbf{K}
.
That is,
(\hat{c},\hat{h}_{2},\ldots,\hat{h}_{N})
is a strictly feasible solution of (
D
) and hence there is no dual gap between (
P
) and (

).

By truncating the polynomial degree, we obtain the following hierarchy of dual SOS relaxations for (
SRFO
) (
k\geq d_{\min}
):

\rho_{k}\coloneqq\left\{\begin{aligned} \sup_{c,h_{i}}&\ c\\
\text{s.t.}&\ p_{1}(\mathbf{x})+\left(\sum_{i=2}^{N}h_{i}(\mathbf{x})-c\right)q_{1}(\mathbf{x})\in Q_{k}(\mathbf{g}),\\
&\ p_{i}(\mathbf{x})-h_{i}(\mathbf{x})q_{i}(\mathbf{x})\in Q_{k}(\mathbf{g}),\quad i\in[N]\setminus\{1\},\\
&\ h_{i}\in{\mathbb{R}}[\mathbf{x}]_{2k-\max\{\deg(q_{1}),\deg(q_{i})\}},\quad i\in[N]\setminus\{1\}.\end{aligned}\right.

(D
k
)

Theorem 6
.

Under Assumption
1
and the Archimedean condition, it holds
\rho_{k}\nearrow\rho
as
k\to\infty
.

Proof.

It is obvious that
\rho_{k}\leq\rho_{k+1}\leq\rho
for all
k\geq d_{\min}
.
Fix an arbitrary
\varepsilon>0
and we will prove that there exists
k_{\varepsilon}\in\mathbb{N}
such that
\rho_{k_{\bar{\varepsilon}}}\geq\rho-\varepsilon
. For
\bar{\varepsilon}\coloneqq\varepsilon/3
, let

(\rho-\bar{\varepsilon},\bar{h}_{2},\ldots,\bar{h}_{N})
be the feasible solution to (
D
) provided in the proof of Theorem
4
. For each
i=2,\ldots,N
,
let

h_{i}\coloneqq\bar{h}_{i}-\frac{\bar{\varepsilon}}{N-1}.

Then, for
\mathbf{x}\in\mathbf{K}
, it holds that

p_{i}(\mathbf{x})-h_{i}(\mathbf{x})q_{i}(\mathbf{x})=p_{i}(\mathbf{x})-\bar{h}_{i}(\mathbf{x})q_{i}(\mathbf{x})+\frac{\bar{\varepsilon}}{N-1}q_{i}(\mathbf{x})\geq\frac{\bar{\varepsilon}}{N-1}q_{i}(\mathbf{x})>0.

(2)

By Theorem
3
, there exists
k_{i}\in\mathbb{N}
such that
h_{i}\in{\mathbb{R}}[\mathbf{x}]_{2k_{i}-\deg(q_{i})}
and
p_{i}-h_{i}q_{i}\in Q_{k_{i}}(\mathbf{g})
. Moreover, for
\mathbf{x}\in\mathbf{K}
, we have

\displaystyle p_{1}(\mathbf{x})+\left(\sum_{i=2}^{N}h_{i}(\mathbf{x})-(\rho-\varepsilon)\right)q_{1}(\mathbf{x})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle p_{1}(\mathbf{x})+q_{1}(\mathbf{x})\sum_{i=2}^{N}\bar{h}_{i}(\mathbf{x})-\bar{\varepsilon}q_{1}(\mathbf{x})-\rho q_{1}(\mathbf{x})+3\bar{\varepsilon}q_{1}(\mathbf{x})

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle(\rho-\bar{\varepsilon})q_{1}(\mathbf{x})-\rho q_{1}(\mathbf{x})+2\bar{\varepsilon}q_{1}(\mathbf{x})\geq\bar{\varepsilon}q_{1}(\mathbf{x})>0.

By Theorem
3
again, there exists
k_{1}\in\mathbb{N}

such that

p_{1}(\mathbf{x})+\left(\sum_{i=2}^{N}h_{i}(\mathbf{x})-(\rho-\varepsilon)\right)q_{1}(\mathbf{x})\in Q_{k_{1}}(\mathbf{g}).

Let
k_{\varepsilon}\coloneqq\max_{1\leq i\leq N}k_{i}
. Then

(\rho-\varepsilon,h_{2},\ldots,h_{N})
is feasible to (

k

) with

6
An application to maximizing sums of generalized Rayleigh quotients

In this section, we apply the sparse SDP relaxations to the problem of maximizing a sum of generalized Rayleigh quotients which arises from signal processing.

In the downlink of a multi-user MIMO system, the base station can multiplex signals intended to different users on the same spectral resource. A challenging problem arising in such a scenario is the joint optimization of channel assignment (scheduling) and beamforming aimed at
maximizing the sum-rate in each time-slot. Assuming zero-forcing linear beamforming at the base station,
Primolevo et al.
[
21
]
addressed this task by investigating a greedy method to approximately maximize the sum-rate.
At each iteration of their method a spatial channel needs to be determined to allocate a specific user, which reduces to
maximizing a sum of generalized Rayleigh quotients of the form

\max_{\mathbf{z}\in{\mathbb{C}}^{n}}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\frac{\mathbf{z}^{\rm{H}}A_{i}\mathbf{z}}{\mathbf{z}^{\rm{H}}B_{i}\mathbf{z}}\quad\text{s.t. }\ \|\mathbf{z}\|^{2}=1,

(18)

where
A_{i},B_{i}\in{\mathbb{C}}^{n\times n}
are positive semidefinite Hermitian matrices,
\mathbf{z}^{\rm{H}}
denotes the conjugate transpose
of
\mathbf{z}
,
n
is the number of assigned spatial channels at the current iteration, and
N
is the number of available users in the time-slot.

When
N=2
, the real counterpart of the problem (
18
) also appears in the sparse Fisher discriminant analysis in pattern recognition (see
[
4
,
5
,
32
]
). The single generalized Rayleigh quotient optimization
problem corresponds to the classical eigenvalue problem and can be solved in polynomial time
[
20
]
.
However, when
N\geq 2
, solving (
18
) is much more challenging and requires sophisticated techniques (see
[
16
,
31
,
34
]
for the real case of
N=2
).
In particular, Primolevo et al.
[
21
]
restricted
\mathbf{z}
in (
18
) to be columns of an identity matrix to give a suboptimal solution.

Here, we convert (
18
) into a real problem by specifying the real and imaginary parts of the complex variables and coefficients appearing in (
18
). Let
\mathbf{z}=\mathbf{x}+\mathbf{i}\mathbf{y}
,
A_{i}=A_{i,1}+\mathbf{i}A_{i,2}
, and

B_{i}=B_{i,1}+\mathbf{i}B_{i,2}
with
\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
and
A_{i,1},A_{i,2},B_{i,1},B_{i,2}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n\times n}
.
Then (
18
) becomes

\max_{\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\frac{\mathbf{x}^{\intercal}A_{i,1}\mathbf{x}-2\mathbf{x}^{\intercal}A_{i,2}\mathbf{y}+\mathbf{y}^{\intercal}A_{i,1}\mathbf{y}}{\mathbf{x}^{\intercal}B_{i,1}\mathbf{x}-2\mathbf{x}^{\intercal}B_{i,2}\mathbf{y}+\mathbf{y}^{\intercal}B_{i,1}\mathbf{y}}\quad\text{s.t. }\ \|\mathbf{x}\|^{2}+\|\mathbf{y}\|^{2}=1.

(19)

In our numerical experiments, we assume that
A_{i}
’s are Hermitian and
B_{i}
’s are positive definite.
To satisfy this condition, we generate random matrices
C_{i,1},C_{i,2},D_{i,1},D_{i,2}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n\times n}
with
each entry being drawn from the uniform distribution on
[0,1]
, and let

A_{i}=(C_{i,1}+\mathbf{i}C_{i,2})+(C_{i,1}+\mathbf{i}C_{i,2})^{\rm{H}},\quad B_{i}=(D_{i,1}+\mathbf{i}D_{i,2})^{\rm{H}}(D_{i,1}+\mathbf{i}D_{i,2}).

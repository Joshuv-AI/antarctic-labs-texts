The immortal dyon index is positive
Martí Rosselló

Abstract
. The microstates of supersymmetric black holes in asymptotically flat four-dimensional spacetime are expected to be bosonic due to the spherical symmetry of their horizons. This implies that the index counting the difference between bosonic and fermionic black hole microstates must be positive, as conjectured by Sen in [ 1 ] . We show that the conjecture is satisfied by the index which counts 1 4 \frac{1}{4} -BPS states in 𝒩 = 4 \mathcal{N}=4 string theory forming single centered black holes, called immortal dyons. The proof relies on the relation between the indexed degeneracies and the coefficients of a family of mock modular forms, thus showing that black holes predict the sign of the Fourier coefficients of mock modular forms.

1.
Introduction and statement of results

Black holes have been a major source of insight into quantum gravity. The holographic principle and its concise realisation in the AdS/CFT correspondence can ultimately be seen as a consequence of the Bekenstein-Hawking area law for black hole entropy.

In this work, black holes are the source of a non-trivial mathematical statement. Namely, we show that black holes predict the sign of the Fourier coefficients of a meromorphic Siegel modular form, as conjectured by Sen in

The enumeration of states (or multiplets) at weak string coupling is performed through a supersymmetric index, which counts the difference between bosonic and fermionic BPS states annihilated by a fraction of the supercharges. The agreement between the number of indexed microstates in the limit of large charges and the semiclassical answer for the entropy indicates that at strong coupling, and in the large charge limit, the number of bosonic states must be much larger than the number of fermionic states.

Heterotic string theory compactified on

The vectors

The

(2.2)

2.4.
Polar coefficients

The coefficients

A weakly holomorphic modular form

, we have

(3.37)

\begin{split}&\frac{{\frac{23}{21}\sqrt{2m}\,d(m)\left(\frac{4m}{\Delta}\right)^{6}I_{12}\left(2\pi\sqrt{\frac{\Delta}{m}}\right)}}{C_{\mathrm{pos.}}(\Delta,m,-1)}\,\leq\,\frac{\frac{23}{21}\sqrt{2m}\,d(m)\left(\frac{4m}{3m^{2}}\right)^{6}I_{12}\left(2\sqrt{3}\pi\sqrt{m}\right)}{C_{\mathrm{pos.}}(3m^{2},m,-1)}\\
&\;=\;\,\frac{\frac{23}{21}\sqrt{2m}\,d(m)\left(\frac{4m}{3m^{2}}\right)^{6}I_{12}\left(2\sqrt{3}\pi\sqrt{m}\right)}{2\pi\,(m+2)\frac{1}{\sqrt{2m}}\left(\frac{4m+m^{2}}{3m^{2}}\right)^{23/4}I_{23/2}\left(\sqrt{3}{\pi}\sqrt{4m+m^{2}}\right)}\,.\end{split}

The last expression is a decreasing function of
m
, being bounded by its value at
m=6
, which is smaller than
5\cdot 10^{-4}
.

The other bound is proved in the same way. From (
3.23
) and
\Delta\geq 3m^{2}
, we have

\frac{\left|\sum\limits_{\gamma=1}^{+\infty}C_{25/2}(m,n,\ell,\gamma)\right|}{C_{\mathrm{pos.}}(3m^{2},m,-1)}\,\leq\,\frac{\left(1+\frac{2}{21}\right){d(m)}\left(\frac{4m}{3m^{2}}\right)^{25/4}m^{3/2}\pi\left(\frac{\pi^{2}}{3}+\frac{1}{3}\right)I_{25/2}\left({2\sqrt{3}\pi}{\sqrt{m}}\right)}{2\pi\,(m+2)\frac{1}{\sqrt{2m}}\left(\frac{4m+m^{2}}{3m^{2}}\right)^{23/4}I_{23/2}\left(\sqrt{3}{\pi}\sqrt{4m+m^{2}}\right)}\,.

The last expression is a decreasing function of
m
, being bounded by its value at
m=6
which is smaller than
0.015
.
∎

3.5.
Polar coefficients bounds

In this subsection we bound the polar coefficients
c_{m}^{F}(\tilde{n},\tilde{\ell})
with
\tilde{\Delta}=4m\tilde{n}-\tilde{\ell}^{2}<0
with respect to the first contribution defined in (
2.54
) coming from the
U
matrix. We start with the case
\tilde{n}=0
for which there is an exact expression for the polar coefficients. This expression can be derived by noticing that the matrices of the form
U^{r}
with
r\geq 1
are enough to find a path from the
\mathcal{R}
chamber to a zero degeneracy chamber. Alternatively, one can also compute the coefficients with
\tilde{n}=0
as the
p^{m}y^{\tilde{\ell}}
coefficient of the function
\frac{d(0)}{\eta^{24}(\rho)}\mathcal{A}_{2,0}(\rho,v)
. The explicit form is,

(3.38)

c^{F}_{m}(0,\tilde{\ell})\;=\;\tilde{\ell}\,d(0)\sum_{r=1}^{\lfloor\frac{m+1}{\tilde{\ell}}\rfloor}d(m-r\tilde{\ell})\,,

which is valid for
0<\tilde{\ell}\leq m
. From this expression we can prove the following.

Lemma 3
.

Let
m\geq 6
and
1\leq k<m
. Then

(3.39)

{c}^{F}_{m}(0,m-k)\,\leq\,\left(1+\frac{25}{d(k)}+\kappa_{1}k^{1/2}\right)\hat{c}^{F}_{m}(0,m-k)\,,

where
\kappa_{1}=\frac{91}{88\pi}
.

Proof.

The ratio vs. the first contribution reads

(3.40)

\frac{c^{F}_{m}(0,\tilde{\ell})}{\hat{c}^{F}_{m}(0,\tilde{\ell})}\;=\;\frac{\sum\limits_{r=1}^{\lfloor\frac{m+1}{\tilde{\ell}}\rfloor}d(m-r\tilde{\ell})}{d(m-\tilde{\ell})}\;=\;\frac{1}{d(k)}\sum_{r=1}^{\lfloor\frac{m+1}{m-k}\rfloor}d(m-rm+rk)\,,

where
k=m-\tilde{\ell}
. There will be extra contributions starting at
r=2
only if

(3.41)

m-2m+2k\,\geq\,-1\Longrightarrow k\,\geq\,\frac{m-1}{2}\,>\,2\,,

since we are only considering
m\geq 6
. Thus, the following equality is satisfied

(3.42)

{c}^{F}_{m}(0,m-k)\;=\;\hat{c}^{F}_{m}(0,m-k)\qquad\text{ for }k=1,2,3\,.

The following analysis is restricted to
k\geq 3
. For these cases, we have

(3.43)

\frac{1}{d(k)}\sum_{r=1}^{\lfloor\frac{m+1}{m-k}\rfloor}d(m-rm+rk)\leq\frac{1}{d(k)}\left(d(-1)+d(0)+\sum_{r=1}^{\lfloor\frac{m-1}{m-k}\rfloor}d(m-rm+rk)\right)\,.

Using the second inequality in (
3.14
), the discrete sum over
r
can be bounded by an integral and we obtain

(3.44)

\begin{split}&\sum_{r=1}^{\lfloor\frac{m-1}{m-k}\rfloor}d(m-rm+rk)\,\leq\,\\
&d(k)+\int_{1}^{\frac{m-1}{m-k}}\left(1+\frac{1}{12}\right)2\pi(m-rm+rk)^{-13/2}I_{13}(4\pi\sqrt{m-rm+rk})dr\,,\end{split}

where we have used that the sum over
r
is monotonically decreasing. The integral can be evaluated to yield the following bound

(3.45)

\frac{1}{d(k)}\sum_{r=1}^{\lfloor\frac{m-1}{m-k}\rfloor}d(m-rm+rk)\leq 1+\frac{1}{d(k)}\left(1+\frac{1}{12}\right)\frac{2\pi}{m-k}\left(\frac{I_{12}(4\pi\sqrt{k})}{2k^{6}\pi}-\frac{I_{12}(4\pi)}{2\pi}\right)\,.

Applying the first inequality in (
3.14
) to
d(k)
in the denominator, together with the bound

(3.46)

\frac{I_{12}(4\pi\sqrt{n})}{I_{13}(4\pi\sqrt{n})}\leq\frac{7}{4}\qquad\text{ for }n\geq 3\,,

we obtain

(3.47)

\sum_{r=1}^{\lfloor\frac{m+1}{m-k}\rfloor}d(m-rm+rk)\,\leq\,d(-1)+d(0)+d(k)+d(k)\frac{1+\frac{1}{12}}{1-\frac{1}{12}}k^{1/2}\frac{7}{8\pi}\,,

where we have used the rough bound
(m-k)^{-1}\leq 1
. We can write this bound as

(3.48)

\frac{{c}^{F}_{m}(0,m-k)}{\hat{c}^{F}_{m}(0,m-k)}\leq\frac{1}{d(k)}\left(d(-1)+d(0)+d(k)+d(k)\frac{1+\frac{1}{12}}{1-\frac{1}{12}}k^{1/2}\frac{7}{8\pi}\right)\,,

which is equivalent to (
3.39
).
∎

We now bound the polar coefficients for
\tilde{n}\geq 1
. For this we use the general form of the polar coefficients (
2.45
).

Lemma 4
.

Let
m\geq 6
,
\tilde{n}\geq 1
,
1\leq k\leq m
and
\tilde{\Delta}=4m\tilde{n}-(m-k)^{2}<0
. Then

(3.49)

c_{m}^{F}(\tilde{n},m-k)\,\leq\,\left(\kappa_{2}+\kappa_{3}k\right)\,\hat{c}_{m}^{F}(\tilde{n},m-k)\,,

where
\kappa_{2}=181/100
and
\kappa_{3}=243/400
.

Proof.

Consider first the second contribution to
W(m,\tilde{n},\tilde{\ell})
, where
\tilde{\ell}=m-k
. For all charge bilinears satisfying
0<\tilde{\ell}<m
, we have
0<\frac{\tilde{\ell}}{2m}<\frac{1}{2}
and therefore the second matrix coming from the continued fraction expansion of
\frac{\tilde{\ell}}{2m}
is always

(3.50)

U^{2}=\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\
2&1\end{pmatrix},\;\;\;\;\text{ with }\;\;\;{\ell}_{U^{2}}d(m_{U^{2}})d({n}_{U^{2}})\;=\;(\tilde{\ell}-4\tilde{n})d(4\tilde{n}+m-2\tilde{\ell})d(\tilde{n})\,.

In terms of
k=m-\tilde{\ell}
the contribution becomes

(3.51)

{\ell}_{U^{2}}d(m_{U^{2}})d(\tilde{n}_{U^{2}})\;=\;(m-k-4\tilde{n})d(4\tilde{n}-m+2k)d(\tilde{n})\,.

The ratio of the second contribution and the first contribution satisfies the following bound,

(3.52)

\frac{(m-k-4\tilde{n})d(4\tilde{n}-m+2k)d(\tilde{n})}{(m-k-2\tilde{n})d(\tilde{n}+k)d(\tilde{n})}\,\leq\,\frac{d(4\tilde{n}-m+2k)}{d(\tilde{n}+k)}\,,

since
\tilde{n}\geq 1
. We use this inequality to bound the rest of the contributions. As explained in Section
2.4
,
m_{G}
and
{n}_{G}
are decreasing in terms of the sequence defined by the continued fraction. Moreover, since
{n}_{U}=\tilde{n}\geq 1
,
m_{U}=\tilde{n}+k\geq 1
and the discriminant
\tilde{\Delta}
is invariant under
SL(2,\mathbb{Z})
transformations,

(3.53)

\tilde{\Delta}\;=\;4m_{U}{n}_{U}-{\ell}_{U}^{2}\;=\;4m_{G}{n}_{G}-{\ell}_{G}^{2}\,,

the value of
{\ell}_{G}
cannot be greater than
{\ell}_{U}
,

(3.54)

{\ell}_{U}\,\geq\,{\ell}_{G}\,.

By the description of the set
W(m,\tilde{n},m-k)
in terms of the continued fraction of
\frac{\tilde{\ell}}{2m}
given in section
2.4
, there are less than
2m
contributions to the polar coefficients, so we can bound the sum of all contributions by

(3.55)

\sum_{G\in W(m,\tilde{n},m-k)\setminus\{U\}}\frac{{\ell}_{G}\,d(m_{G})\,d({n}_{G})}{{\ell}_{U}\,d(m_{U})\,d({n}_{U})}\,<\,2m\frac{d(4\tilde{n}-m+2k)}{d(\tilde{n}+k)}\,.

The right hand side is decreasing with
m
. Thus, we can bound it by the smallest possible value of
m
. From the conditions

(3.56)

\tilde{\Delta}=4m\tilde{n}-(m-k)^{2}<0,\;\;\;1\leq k<m\,,

one finds

(3.57)

m>k+2\tilde{n}+\sqrt{(k+2\tilde{n})^{2}-k^{2}}\,,

and therefore

(3.58)

2m\frac{d(4\tilde{n}-m+2k)}{d(\tilde{n}+k)}\leq 2\left(k+2\tilde{n}+\sqrt{(k+2\tilde{n})^{2}-k^{2}}\right)\frac{d\left(\left\lfloor 2\tilde{n}+k-\sqrt{(k+2\tilde{n})^{2}-k^{2}}\right\rfloor\right)}{d(\tilde{n}+k)}\,.

This quantity is maximized by the value
\tilde{n}=1
,

(3.59)

2m\frac{d(4\tilde{n}-m+2k)}{d(\tilde{n}+k)}\,<\,(6k+8)\frac{d(\lfloor 2+k-2\sqrt{k}\rfloor)}{d(1+k)}\,,

and the ratio of the
\eta^{-24}(\tau)
coefficients is maximized by its value at
k=1
,

(3.60)

\frac{d(\lfloor 2+k-2\sqrt{k}\rfloor)}{d(1+k)}\,\leq\,\frac{d(1)}{d(2)}\;=\;\frac{324}{3200}\,,

yielding the desired bound

(3.61)

\frac{1}{\hat{c}_{m}^{F}(m,\tilde{n},m-k)}\sum_{G\in W(m,\tilde{n},m-k)\setminus\{U\}}\tilde{\ell}_{G}\,d(m_{G})d(\tilde{n}_{G})\,\leq\,\frac{324}{3200}(6k+8)\,.

∎

The previous result is a rough bound, mostly due to the use of the inequality (
3.55
). Numerical experiments show that there is no linear growth in
k
and presumably using the precise form of the polar coefficients (
2.45
) one could prove a tighter bound. For our purposes, however, the bound is enough.

The remaining bounds concern the polar coefficients with
\tilde{n}=-1
.
The coefficients
c_{m}^{F}(-1,\tilde{\ell})
with
0\leq\tilde{\ell}\leq m
have the following exact expression

(3.62)

c_{m}^{F}(-1,\tilde{\ell})\;=\;\sum_{r\geq 1}(2r+\tilde{\ell})d(m-r^{2}-r\tilde{\ell})\,,

where the sum over
r
is bounded by

(3.63)

r_{\max}\;=\;{\lfloor\frac{-\tilde{\ell}+\sqrt{\tilde{\ell}^{2}+4m+4}}{2}\rfloor}\,,

due to the vanishing of
d(n)
for
n<-1
. The expression (
3.62
) can be obtained directly from the product representation of
\Phi_{10}(\rho,\sigma,v)
(
2.16
), since for
\Phi_{10}(\rho,\sigma,v)^{-1}
the
p^{-1}
coefficient is given by the quantities
C_{0}(-1)=1
and
C_{0}(0)=10
, yielding the following negative index Jacobi form

(3.64)

\begin{split}\psi_{-1}(\sigma,v)\;=\;&\frac{q^{-1}y}{(1-y)^{2}}\prod_{n=1}^{\infty}(1-yq^{n})^{-2}(1-y^{-1}q^{n})^{-2}(1-q^{n})^{-20}\\
\;=\;&\frac{1}{\eta^{24}(\sigma)}\frac{1}{\varphi_{-2,1}(\sigma,v)}\,.\end{split}

The function
\varphi_{-2,1}(\sigma,v)^{-1}
can be expressed as a derivative of an Appell-Lerch sum
[
79
,
59
]
, giving the coefficients (
3.62
)
[
37
]
.

In (
3.62
) the second contribution coming from
r=2
is only non-vanishing if

(3.65)

m-4-2\tilde{\ell}=m-4-2(m-k)\geq-1\Rightarrow k\geq\frac{3+m}{2}\geq 5\,,

since
m\geq 6
. Thus, in the following we only consider the case
k\geq 5
. We have

Lemma 5
.

Let
m\geq 6
and
5\leq k\leq m
, then

(3.66)

c_{m}^{F}(-1,m-k)\,\leq\,(\kappa_{4}+\kappa_{5}k^{1/2})\,\hat{c}_{m}^{F}(-1,m-k)\,,

where the constants
\kappa_{4},\kappa_{5}
are
\kappa_{4}=3+2\cdot 10^{-5}
and
\kappa_{5}=\frac{1+\frac{1}{12}}{1-\frac{1}{12}}\frac{1}{4\pi}
.

Proof.

The proof is similar to the one for the
\tilde{n}=0
case. We have

(3.67)

\frac{c_{m}^{F}(-1,m-k)}{\hat{c}_{m}^{F}(-1,m-k)}\,\leq\,1+\sum_{r\geq 2}\frac{(2r+m-k)d(m-r^{2}-r(m-k))}{(2+m-k)d(k-1)}\,.

We isolate the possible contribution containing the coefficient
d(-1)
in order to use the bounds (
3.14
). Denoting by
r_{*}
the positive value of
r
satisfying
m-r^{2}-r(m-k)=-1
, if
r_{*}
is an integer, the corresponding summand in (
3.67
) can be bounded by

\frac{(2r_{*}+m-k)d(m-r_{*}^{2}-r_{*}(m-k))}{(2+m-k)d(k-1)}\;=\;\frac{\sqrt{(m-k)^{2}+4m+4}}{(2+m-k)d(k-1)}\,\leq\,\frac{\sqrt{1+k}}{d(k-1)}<2\cdot 10^{-5}\,,

where the last bound follows from the ratio being decreasing with
k
, with the minimum value given by
k=5
.

It follows that the right hand side of (
3.67
) is less than

(3.68)

1+2\cdot 10^{-5}+\sum_{r\geq 2}^{r_{\max}^{\prime}}\frac{(2r+m-k)d(m-r^{2}-r(m-k))}{(2+m-k)d(k-1)}\,,

where

(3.69)

r_{\max}^{\prime}\;=\;\left\lfloor\frac{-(m-k)+\sqrt{(m-k)^{2}+4m}}{2}\right\rfloor\,,

with the convention that the sum is zero if
r_{\max}^{\prime}<2
. From the second inequality in (
3.14
), the decreasing sum satisfies the following bound

(3.70)

\begin{split}&\sum_{r=2}^{r_{\max}^{\prime}}{(2r+m-k)d(m-r^{2}-r(m-k))}\,\leq\,{(4+m-k)d(m-4-2(m-k))}\\
&+\int_{2}^{r_{max}^{\prime}}dr\,(2r+m-k)\left(1+\frac{1}{12}\right)2\pi\frac{I_{13}(4\pi\sqrt{m-r^{2}-r(m-k)})}{(m-r^{2}-r(m-k))^{13/2}}\,.\end{split}

The integral can be evaluated using the following equality

\int_{2}^{r_{max}^{\prime}}dr\,(2r+m-k)\frac{I_{13}(4\pi\sqrt{m-r^{2}-r(m-k)})}{(m-r^{2}-r(m-k))^{13/2}}\;=\;\frac{I_{12}(4\pi\sqrt{2k-m-4})}{2\pi(2k-m-4)^{6}}-\frac{(2\pi)^{11}}{12!}\,,

which yields,

(3.71)

\begin{split}&\sum_{r\geq 2}^{r_{\max}^{\prime}}\frac{(2r+m-k)d(m-r^{2}-r(m-k))}{(2+m-k)d(k-1)}\\
<\,&\,\frac{(4+m-k)d(2k-m-4)}{(2+m-k)d(k-1)}+\frac{\left(1+\frac{1}{12}\right)\frac{I_{12}(4\pi\sqrt{2k-m-4})}{(2k-m-4)^{6}}}{(2+m-k)d(k-1)}\,.\end{split}

The first summand is decreasing with
m
. Since for fixed
k
the least possible value of
m
is
k
, this term is bounded by
2
,

(3.72)

\frac{(4+m-k)d(2k-m-4)}{(2+m-k)d(k-1)}\,<\,2\,.

Now we examine the second term, which is again decreasing with
m
and bounded by the value
m=k
,

(3.73)

\frac{\left(1+\frac{1}{12}\right)\frac{I_{12}(4\pi\sqrt{2k-m-4})}{(2k-m-4)^{6}}}{(2+m-k)d(k-1)}\,\leq\,\frac{\left(1+\frac{1}{12}\right)\frac{I_{12}(4\pi\sqrt{k-4})}{(k-4)^{6}}}{2d(k-1)}\,.

We use the first inequality in (
3.14
) to write

(3.74)

\frac{\left(1+\frac{1}{12}\right)\frac{I_{12}(4\pi\sqrt{k-4})}{(k-4)^{6}}}{2d(k-1)}\,\leq\,\frac{1+\frac{1}{12}}{1-\frac{1}{12}}\frac{(k-1)^{13/2}}{4\pi{(k-4)^{6}}}\frac{I_{12}(4\pi\sqrt{k-4})}{I_{13}(4\pi\sqrt{k-1})}\,.

This last expression is an unbounded function of
k
which is smaller than the function capturing its asymptotic growth,

(3.75)

\frac{1+\frac{1}{12}}{1-\frac{1}{12}}\frac{(k-1)^{13/2}}{4\pi{(k-4)^{6}}}\frac{I_{12}(4\pi\sqrt{k-4})}{I_{13}(4\pi\sqrt{k-1})}\,\leq\,\frac{1+\frac{1}{12}}{1-\frac{1}{12}}\frac{k^{1/2}}{4\pi}\,.

Putting the previous bounds together, we have

(3.76)

\frac{c_{m}^{F}(-1,m-k)}{\hat{c}_{m}^{F}(-1,m-k)}\,<\,3+2\cdot 10^{-5}+\frac{1+\frac{1}{12}}{1-\frac{1}{12}}\frac{k^{1/2}}{4\pi}\,.

∎

3.6.
Leading term bounds

The remaining bounds concern the the leading term in the Rademacher expansion, by which we mean the first line in the right hand side of (
2.36
). We show that the positive contribution (
3.5
) for each
\tilde{n}
is larger than the sum of the remaining contributions with
\tilde{\ell}<m
. We first derive a bound for the cases
\tilde{n}\geq 0
by considering only the first contribution to the polar coefficients (
2.54
) and then we correct it with the bounds for the polar coefficients derived in the previous subsection separately for
\tilde{n}=0
and
\tilde{n}\geq 1
. The bounds for the more involved case
\tilde{n}=-1
are derived at the end.

Lemma 6
.

Let
m\geq 6
,
n\geq m
,
0\leq\ell\leq m
, and
\tilde{n}\geq 0
. Then

(3.77)

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}0\leq\tilde{\ell}<m\\
\tilde{\Delta}<0\end{subarray}}\frac{2\,\hat{c}_{m}^{F}(\tilde{n},\tilde{\ell})|\tilde{\Delta}|^{23/4}\,{I}_{23/2}\left(\frac{\pi}{m}\sqrt{\Delta|\tilde{\Delta}|}\right)}{{c}_{m}^{F}(\tilde{n},m)|\tilde{\Delta}_{{m}}|^{23/4}\,{I}_{23/2}\left(\frac{\pi}{m}\sqrt{\Delta|\tilde{\Delta}_{{m}}|}\right)}\,<\,0.123\,.

Proof.

Using the inequality (
3.8
),
\Delta\geq 3m^{2}
, we can bound each summand in (
3.77
) by

\frac{2\,\hat{c}_{m}^{F}(\tilde{n},\tilde{\ell})|\tilde{\Delta}|^{23/4}\,{I}_{23/2}\left(\frac{\pi}{m}\sqrt{\Delta|\tilde{\Delta}|}\right)}{{c}_{m}^{F}(\tilde{n},m)|\tilde{\Delta}_{{m}}|^{23/4}\,{I}_{23/2}\left(\frac{\pi}{m}\sqrt{\Delta|\tilde{\Delta}_{{m}}|}\right)}\leq\frac{2\,\hat{c}_{m}^{F}(\tilde{n},\tilde{\ell})|\tilde{\Delta}|^{23/4}\,{I}_{23/2}\left(\sqrt{3}{\pi}\sqrt{|\tilde{\Delta}|}\right)}{{c}_{m}^{F}(\tilde{n},m)|\tilde{\Delta}_{{m}}|^{23/4}\,{I}_{23/2}\left(\sqrt{3}{\pi}\sqrt{|\tilde{\Delta}_{{m}}|}\right)}.

The right hand side of this equation has the following explicit expression

\frac{2\,(m-k-2\tilde{n})d(k+\tilde{n})d(\tilde{n})((m-k)^{2}-4m\tilde{n})^{23/4}\,{I}_{23/2}\left(\sqrt{3}{\pi}\sqrt{(m-k)^{2}-4m\tilde{n}}\right)}{(m-2\tilde{n})d(\tilde{n})d(\tilde{n})(m^{2}-4m\tilde{n})^{23/4}\,{I}_{23/2}\left(\sqrt{3}{\pi}\sqrt{m^{2}-4m\tilde{n}}\right)}

where
k\;=\;m-\tilde{\ell}
. From the inequality (
3.13
) we obtain

(3.78)

\begin{split}&\frac{2\,(m-k-2\tilde{n})d(k+\tilde{n})d(\tilde{n})((m-k)^{2}-4m\tilde{n})^{23/4}\,{I}_{23/2}\left(\sqrt{3}{\pi}\sqrt{(m-k)^{2}-4m\tilde{n}}\right)}{(m-2\tilde{n})d(\tilde{n})d(\tilde{n})(m^{2}-4m\tilde{n})^{23/4}\,{I}_{23/2}\left(\sqrt{3}{\pi}\sqrt{m^{2}-4m\tilde{n}}\right)}\\
&<\frac{2\,(m-k-2\tilde{n})d(k+\tilde{n})d(\tilde{n})\,}{(m-2\tilde{n})d(\tilde{n})d(\tilde{n})}\left(\frac{(m-k)^{2}-4m\tilde{n}}{m^{2}-4m\tilde{n}}\right)^{6}e^{\sqrt{3}{\pi}\left(\sqrt{(m-k)^{2}-4m\tilde{n}}-\sqrt{m^{2}-4m\tilde{n}}\right)}.\end{split}

Since
\tilde{n}\geq 0
, the right hand side of the previous equation is an increasing function of
m
. Therefore, it is smaller or equal than its
m\to\infty
limit, which is given by

(3.79)

\frac{2\,d(k+\tilde{n})}{d(\tilde{n})}e^{-\sqrt{3}{\pi}k}.

The sum over
\tilde{\ell}
is restricted to the values which satisfy
\tilde{\Delta}=4m\tilde{n}-\tilde{\ell}^{2}<0
, so the sum over
k
is from
k=1
to
k=\lfloor m-2\sqrt{m\tilde{n}}\rfloor
. Therefore, by taking the limit
m\to\infty
, we have shown

(3.80)

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}0\leq\tilde{\ell}<m\\
\tilde{\Delta}<0\end{subarray}}\frac{2\,\hat{c}_{m}^{F}(\tilde{n},\tilde{\ell})|\tilde{\Delta}|^{23/4}\,{I}_{23/2}\left(\frac{\pi}{m}\sqrt{\Delta|\tilde{\Delta}|}\right)}{{c}_{m}^{F}(\tilde{n},m)|\tilde{\Delta}_{{m}}|^{23/4}\,{I}_{23/2}\left(\frac{\pi}{m}\sqrt{\Delta|\tilde{\Delta}_{{m}}|}\right)}\,<\,\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{2\,d(k+\tilde{n})}{d(\tilde{n})}e^{-\sqrt{3}{\pi}k}\,.

The last expression is a decreasing function of
\tilde{n}
. We can write it as

(3.81)

\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{2\,d(k+\tilde{n})}{d(\tilde{n})}e^{-\sqrt{3}{\pi}k}\;=\;\frac{2e^{\sqrt{3}\pi\tilde{n}}}{d(\tilde{n})}\left(\eta^{-24}\left(\frac{i\sqrt{3}}{2}\right)-\sum_{r=-1}^{\tilde{n}}d(r)e^{-\sqrt{3}\pi r}\right)\,,

which we can compute for any value of
\tilde{n}
. E.g., for
\tilde{n}=1
the previous quantity is smaller than
0.09
, and for

\tilde{n}=0
it gives the desired bound

(3.82)

\frac{2}{24}\left(\eta^{-24}\left(\frac{i\sqrt{3}}{2}\right)-e^{\sqrt{3}\pi}-24\right)\;=\;0.122...<0.123\,.

∎

The next step consists in using the bounds for the polar coefficients found in the previous subsection to find a bound for the complete contribution.

Lemma 7
.

Let
m\geq 6,n\geq m,0\leq\ell\leq m
, and
\tilde{n}\geq 1
. Then

(3.83)

e_{23/2}(\Delta,m,\tilde{n})\;=\;\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}0\leq\tilde{\ell}<m\\

Richardson extrapolation for the iterated Galerkin solution of Urysohn integral equations with Green’s kernels
Gobinda RAKSHIT, Akshay S. RANE, Kshitij PATIL

Abstract
We consider a Urysohn integral operator 𝒦 \mathcal{K} with kernel of the type of Green’s function. For r ≥ 1 r\geq 1 , a space of piecewise polynomials of degree ≤ r − 1 \leq r-1 with respect to a uniform partition is chosen to be the approximating space and the projection is chosen to be the orthogonal projection. Iterated Galerkin method is applied to the integral equation x − 𝒦 ⁡ ( x ) = f x-\mathcal{K}(x)=f . It is known that the order of convergence of the iterated Galerkin solution is r + 2 r+2 and, at the above partition points it is 2 ​ r 2r . We obtain an asymptotic expansion of the iterated Galerkin solution at the partition points of the above Urysohn integral equation. Richardson extrapolation is used to improve the order of convergence. A numerical example is considered to illustrate our theoretical results.



Key Words : Urysohn integral operator, Green’s kernel, Galerkin method, Richardson extrapolation.

AMS subject classification : 45G10, 65B05, 65J15, 65R20

Introduction

Let
\mathcal{X}=L^{\infty}[0,1]
and
consider the following Urysohn integral operator

\displaystyle\mathcal{K}(x)(s)=\int_{0}^{1}\kappa\left(s,t,x(t)\right)dt,\;\;\;s\in[0,1],\;x\in\mathcal{X},

(1.1)

where
\kappa(s,t,u)
is a real valued continuous function
defined on

\Omega=[0,1]\times[0,1]\times{\mathbb{R}}.

Then
\mathcal{K}
is a compact operator from
L^{\infty}[0,1]
to
C[0,1].

Consider the Urysohn integral equation

\displaystyle x(s)-\int_{0}^{1}\kappa\left(s,t,x(t)\right)dt=f(s),\;\;\;s\in[0,1],

(1.2)

where
f\in\mathcal{X}
is given and
x
is the unknown to be determined. We assume that
\varphi
is an isolated solution of the above equation and consider its numerical approximations.

We are interested in approximate solution which converges to
\varphi
uniformly. We consider some projection methods associated with a sequence of orthogonal projections converging to the Identity operator point-wise.

In this paper, we consider the case when the kernel
\kappa
of the integral operator
\mathcal{K}
, is of the type of Green’s function in its domain. We allow the partial derivatives of the kernel
\kappa
to have jump discontinuities along the diagonal
s=t
. For
r\geq 1,
let
\mathscr{X}_{n}
be a space of piecewise polynomials of degree
\leq r-1
with respect to a uniform partition of
[0,1]
with
n
subintervals each of length
{h=\frac{1}{n}}.

Let
\pi_{n}
be the restriction to
L^{\infty}[0,1]
of the orthogonal projection from
L^{2}[0,1]
onto
\mathscr{X}_{n}.
Galerkin method is a classical projection method for the approximate solution of an integral equation. In this method,
(
1.2
) is approximated by

x_{n}^{G}-\pi_{n}\mathcal{K}(x_{n}^{G})=\pi_{n}f.

(1.3)

The above projection method has been studied extensively in the research literature.
See Krasnoselsii
[
6
]
, Krasnoselskii, Vainikko et al
[
7
]

and Krasnoselskii and Zabreiko
[
8
]
for details.

The iterated Galerkin solution is defined by

x_{n}^{S}=\mathcal{K}(x_{n}^{G})+f.

Note that

x_{n}^{G}=\pi_{n}x_{n}^{S}

and then
the iterated Galerkin solution satisfies the following equation:

x_{n}^{S}-\mathcal{K}(\pi_{n}x_{n}^{S})=f.

(1.4)

From Atkinson-Potra
[
3
]
, we quote the following orders of convergence:

If
r=1,
then

\|x_{n}^{G}-\varphi\|_{\infty}=O(h),\;\;\;\|x_{n}^{S}-\varphi\|_{\infty}=O(h^{2}),

(1.5)

whereas if
r\geq 2,
then

\left\lVert x_{n}^{G}-\varphi\right\rVert_{\infty}=O\left(h^{r}\right)~,\quad\left\lVert x_{n}^{S}-\varphi\right\rVert_{\infty}=O\left(h^{r+2}\right).

(1.6)

Asymptotic error analysis and extrapolation methods are classical topics in numerical analysis. Richardson extrapolation is the popular one. In Ford et al.
[
5
]
, Hammerstein integral equation with Green’s function type of kernel is considered. Composite trapezoidal quadrature method is used to approximate the integral operator, and then an asymptotic error expansion is obtained for the approximate solution at the node points. Richardson extrapolation is applied to improve the orders of convergence. In Kulkarni-Rane
[
12
]
, the authors have defined the Nyström operator based on the
composite midpoint and the composite modified Simpson rules to approximate the integral operator of a Hammerstein integral equation with Green’s function type kernel. Asymptotic expansions for the approximate solution at the node points as well as at the partition points, are obtained and Richardson extrapolation is used to obtain approximate solutions with higher orders of convergence. Hammerstein integral equation is a special case of Urysohn integral equation. The case when the kernel of the Urysohn integral equation is sufficiently smooth, asymptotic error analysis are investigated for various projection methods in Kulkarni-Nidhin
[
10
]
. In the case of a linear integral equation of the second kind with smooth kernel, asymptotic series expansion for the iterated Galerkin solution is proved by McLean
[
14
]
. The case of asymptotic expansion for approximate solution of integral equations with Green’s kernel, at the partition points in the case of Nyström method with midpoint rule, modified Simpsons rule and iterated collocation method is treated in Kulkarni-Rane
[
11
]
.

In Rakshit-Rane
[
15
]
, we considered a Fredholm integral equation with kernel of the type of Green’s function and then asymptotic error analysis is investigated for the iterated Galerkin solution at the partition points. Richardson extrapolation is applied to obtain an approximate solution with higher rate of convergence.

In this paper we shall analyze asymptotic expansion for the iterated Galerkin solution of Urysohn integral equation with Green’s function type kernel. We will use Richardson extrapolation to improve the order of convergence.

The paper is organized as follows. In Section 2, notation is set
and some preliminary results are proved for later use. In Section 3,
asymptotic error analysis for the iterated Galerkin solution at the partition points are investigated. Numerical illustration is given in Section 4.

2
Preliminaries

In this section we describe the Urysohn integral operator with Green’s function type kernel, its Fréchet derivatives and related preliminary results. We introduce the following notations.

For an integer
\alpha\geq 0
, let
C^{\alpha}[0,1]
denotes the space of all real valued
\alpha
-times continuously differentiable functions on
[0,1]
with the following norm.

\left\lVert x\right\rVert_{\alpha,\infty}=\max_{0\leq j\leq\alpha}\left\lVert x^{(j)}\right\rVert_{\infty},

where
x^{(j)}
is the
j^{\text{th}}
derivative of the function
x
and

\left\lVert x^{(j)}\right\rVert_{\infty}=\sup_{0\leq t\leq 1}\left|x^{(j)}(t)\right|.

Define

\left\lVert\kappa\right\rVert_{\alpha,\infty}=\max_{0\leq i+j+k\leq\alpha}\left\lVert D^{(i,j,k)}\kappa(s,t,u)\right\rVert_{\infty},

where

D^{(i,j,k)}\kappa(s,t,u)=\frac{\partial^{i+j+k}\kappa}{\partial s^{i}\partial t^{j}\partial u^{k}}(s,t,u).

2.1
Properties of the kernel (Green’s function type)

Let
r\geq 1
be an integer and assume that the kernel
\kappa
has the following properties.

1.

For
i=1,2,3,4
, the functions
\kappa,\displaystyle{\frac{\partial^{i}\kappa}{\partial u^{i}}\in C(\Omega),}

where
C(\Omega)
denotes the space of all real valued continuous function on
\Omega=[0,1]\times[0,1]\times{\mathbb{R}}
.

2.

Let

\Omega_{1}=\{(s,t,u):0\leq t\leq s\leq 1,\;u\in{\mathbb{R}}\},\;\;\;\Omega_{2}=\{(s,t,u):0\leq s\leq t\leq 1,\;u\in{\mathbb{R}}\}.

There are two functions
\kappa_{j}\in C^{r}(\Omega_{j}),j=1,2,
such that

\kappa(s,t,u)=\left\{{\begin{array}[]{ll}\kappa_{1}(s,t,u),\;\;\;(s,t,u)\in\Omega_{1},\\
\kappa_{2}(s,t,u),\;\;\;(s,t,u)\in\Omega_{2}.\end{array}}\right.

3.

Denote

\displaystyle{\ell(s,t,u)=\frac{\partial\kappa}{\partial u}(s,t,u)}
and
\displaystyle{q(s,t,u)=\frac{\partial^{2}\kappa}{\partial u^{2}}(s,t,u)},

for all
(s,t,u)\in\Omega.
The partial derivatives of
\ell(s,t,u)
and
q(s,t,u)
with respect to
s
and
t
have jump discontinuities on
s=t
.

4.

There are functions
\ell_{j},q_{j}\in C^{r}(\Omega_{j}),j=1,2,
with

\ell(s,t,u)=\left\{{\begin{array}[]{ll}\ell_{1}(s,t,u),\;\;\;(s,t,u)\in\Omega_{1},\\
\ell_{2}(s,t,u),\;\;\;(s,t,u)\in\Omega_{2},\end{array}}\right.

q(s,t,u)=\left\{{\begin{array}[]{ll}q_{1}(s,t,u),\;\;\;(s,t,u)\in\Omega_{1},\\
q_{2}(s,t,u),\;\;\;(s,t,u)\in\Omega_{2}.\end{array}}\right.

Following Atkinson-Potra
[
3
]
, if the kernel
\kappa
satisfies the above conditions, then we say that
\kappa
is of class
\mathscr{G}_{4}(r,0)
.

Under the above assumptions, the operator
\mathcal{K}
is four times Fréchet differentiable and its Fréchet derivatives at
x\in\mathcal{X}
are given by

\mathcal{K}^{\prime}(x)v_{1}(s)=\int_{0}^{1}\frac{\partial\kappa}{\partial u}\left(s,t,x(t)\right)\>v_{1}(t)\>dt

and

\mathcal{K}^{(i)}(x)(v_{1},\ldots,v_{i})(s)=\int_{0}^{1}\frac{\partial^{i}\kappa}{\partial u^{i}}\left(s,t,x(t)\right)\>v_{1}(t)\cdots v_{i}(t)\>dt,\qquad i=2,3,4,

(2.1)

where

\displaystyle\frac{\partial^{i}\kappa}{\partial u^{i}}\left(s,t,x(t)\right)=\frac{\partial^{i}\kappa}{\partial u^{i}}\left(s,t,u\right)|_{u=x(t)},\quad i=1,2,3,4

and
v_{1},v_{2},v_{3},v_{4}\in\mathcal{X}
. Note that
\mathcal{K}^{\prime}(x):\mathcal{X}\rightarrow\mathcal{X}
is linear and
\mathcal{K}^{(i)}(x):\mathcal{X}^{i}\rightarrow\mathcal{X}
are multi-linear operators, where
\mathcal{X}^{i}
is the cartesian product of
i
copies of
\mathcal{X}
. See Rall
[
16
]
.
We define

\left\lVert\mathcal{K}^{(i)}(x)\right\rVert\>=\>\sup_{\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\left\lVert v_{j}\right\rVert_{\infty}\leq 1}}{{j=1,\ldots,i}}}\left\lVert\mathcal{K}^{(i)}(x)(v_{1},\ldots,v_{i})\right\rVert_{\infty},\qquad i=1,2,3,4.

It follows that

\displaystyle\left\lVert\mathcal{K}^{(i)}(x)\right\rVert

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sup_{0\leq s,t\leq 1}\left|\frac{\partial^{i}\kappa}{\partial u^{i}}\left(s,t,x(t)\right)\right|,\qquad i=1,2,3,4.

We rewrite the equation (
1.2
) as

\displaystyle x-\mathcal{K}(x)=f,\quad x\in\mathcal{X}.

Let

\mathcal{T}(x)=\mathcal{K}(x)+f,\quad x\in\mathcal{X}.

(2.2)

Assume that
\varphi
is a fixed point of
\mathcal{T}
. Since
\mathcal{K}
is compact,
\mathcal{K}^{\prime}(\varphi)
is a compact linear operator. See Krasnoselskii
[
6
]
. Assume that
1
is not an eigenvalue of
\mathcal{K}^{\prime}(\varphi).
Then,
\varphi
is an isolated solution of (
1.2
). Let
f\in C^{\alpha}[0,1]
, then by the Corollary 3.2 of Atkinson-Potra
[
3
]
, it follows that
\varphi\in C^{\alpha}[0,1].

2.2
Approximating Space and Projection operator

Let
n\in\mathbb{N}
and consider the following uniform partition of

\Delta:0<\frac{1}{n}<\cdots<\frac{n-1}{n}<1.

(2.3)

Define

t_{j}=\frac{j}{n},\;\;\;j=0,\ldots,n.

(2.4)

Let

\Delta_{j}=[t_{j-1},t_{j}]\;\;\;\mbox{and}\;\;\;h=t_{j}-t_{j-1}=\frac{1}{n},\;\;\;j=1,\ldots,n.

Consider a finite dimensional approximating space as

\mathscr{X}_{n}=\left\{g\in L^{\infty}[0,1]:g\text{ is a polynomial of degree }\leq r-1\text{ on }\Delta_{j},~j=1,2,\dots,n\right\}.

As no continuity conditions are imposed at the partition points, the dimension of

is

\displaystyle{\mathscr{X}_{n}\subset L^{\infty}[0,1].}

Let
\pi_{n}
be the restriction to

onto

, which converges to the Identity operator pointwise. Then

\sup_{n\geq 1}\left\lVert\pi_{n}\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}[0,1]\rightarrow L^{\infty}[0,1]}<\infty.

(2.5)

If
x\in C^{\alpha}[0,1]
, it is well-known that

\displaystyle\left\lVert(I-\pi_{n})x\right\rVert_{\infty}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C_{1}\|x^{(\beta)}\|_{\infty}h^{\beta},

(2.6)

where
\beta=\min\left\{\alpha,r\right\}
and

is a constant independent of

. See Atkinson
[
1
]
, Chatelin-Lebbar
[
4
]
.
Denote

For

we have

\displaystyle\|(I-\pi_{n,j})x\|_{\Delta_{j},\infty}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C_{2}\|x^{(\beta)}\|_{\Delta_{j},\infty}h^{\beta},

(2.7)

where
\beta=\min\left\{\alpha,r\right\}
and
C_{2}
is a constant independent of

[

2.3
Asymptotic Expansions and the higher order terms

Let

\mathcal{B}(\varphi,\delta)=\left\{x\in\mathcal{X}:\left\lVert x-\varphi\right\rVert_{\infty}\leq\delta\right\}

Denote

It follows that

\mathcal{K}^{\prime}(\varphi)v(s)=\int_{0}^{1}\ell_{*}(s,t)\>v(t)\>dt,\qquad v\in\mathcal{X},\>s\in[0,1],

(2.8)

\mathcal{K}^{\prime\prime}(\varphi)(v_{1},v_{2})(s)=\int_{0}^{1}q_{*}(s,t)~v_{1}(t)\>v_{2}(t)\>dt,\qquad v_{1},v_{2}\in\mathcal{X},\>s\in[0,1],

where the kernels
\ell_{*}(\cdot,\cdot),\;q_{*}(\cdot,\cdot)\in C[0,1]\times C[0,1]
are of the type of Green’s function as mentioned in the section
2.1

\left\lVert\mathcal{K}^{\prime}(\varphi)\right\rVert\leq\sup_{0\leq t\leq 1}\int_{0}^{1}\left|\ell_{*}(s,t)\right|\>ds,

\left\lVert\mathcal{K}^{\prime\prime}(\varphi)\right\rVert\leq\sup_{0\leq t\leq 1}\int_{0}^{1}\left|q_{*}(s,t)\right|\>ds.

(2.9)

See Atkinson
[
1
]
.

By assumption,
I-\mathcal{K}^{\prime}(\varphi)
is invertible. Let

\mathcal{M}=\left(I-\mathcal{K}^{\prime}(\varphi)\right)^{-1}\mathcal{K}^{\prime}(\varphi),

\mathcal{M}_{2}=\left(I-\mathcal{K}^{\prime}(\varphi)\right)^{-1}\mathcal{K}^{\prime\prime}(\varphi),

\mathcal{M}_{3}=\left(I-\mathcal{K}^{\prime}(\varphi)\right)^{-1}\mathcal{K}^{(3)}(\varphi).

Then
\mathcal{M}
,
\mathcal{M}_{2}
and
\mathcal{M}_{3}
are respectively compact linear, bi-linear and tri-linear integral operators. See Riesz-Nagy
[
17
]
. For
v\in\mathcal{X}
, let

\mathcal{M}v(s)=\int_{0}^{1}m(s,t)\>v(t)\>dt,\quad s\in[0,1],

Note that the kernels of
\mathcal{M}
,
\mathcal{M}_{2}
and
\mathcal{M}_{3}
inherit the same smoothness properties as the kernels of
\mathcal{K}^{\prime}(\varphi)
,
\mathcal{K}^{\prime\prime}(\varphi)
and
\mathcal{K}^{(3)}(\varphi)
respectively. See Atkinson-Potra [Lemma 5.1]
[
3
]
. Hence, the kernels of the above three operators are of the type of Green’s function as mentioned in section
2.1
.

We quote the following result from Rakshit-Rane
[
15
]
.

\mathcal{M}\varphi(t_{i})=\mathcal{M}\pi_{n}\varphi(t_{i})+\left(\mathcal{A}_{2r}\varphi\right)(t_{i})h^{2r}+O\left(h^{2r+2}\right),\quad i=0,1,\ldots,n,

(2.10)

where

(\mathcal{A}_{2r}\varphi)(t_{i})=\bar{b}_{2r,2r}\int_{0}^{1}m(t_{i},t)(t)~\varphi^{(2r)}(t)~dt\\
~~~~~+\sum_{p=1}^{2r-1}\bar{b}_{2r,p}\Bigg\{\left[\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\right)^{2r-p-1}\left(m(t_{i},t)\varphi^{(p)}(t)\right)\right]_{t=0}^{t=1}\\
-\left[\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\right)^{2r-p-1}\left(m(t_{i},t)\varphi^{(p)}(t)\right)\right]_{t=t_{i}-}^{t=t_{i}+}\Bigg\}

with

\bar{b}_{2r,p}=\int_{0}^{1}\int_{0}^{1}\Lambda_{r}(\sigma,\tau)\frac{(\sigma-\tau)^{p}}{p!}\frac{B_{2r-p}(\tau)}{(2r-p)!}\>d\sigma\>d\tau,

\displaystyle\Lambda_{r}(\sigma,\tau)=\sum_{q=0}^{r-1}e_{q}(\sigma)e_{q}(\tau)
,
\left\{e_{0},e_{1},e_{2},\ldots\right\}
is the sequence of orthonormal polynomials in
L^{2}[0,1]
and
B_{k}
is the Bernoulli polynomial of degree
k
.

As in Kulkarni-Nidhin

\mathcal{M}_{2}(\pi_{n}\varphi-\varphi)^{2}(s)=\mathcal{V}_{1}(\varphi)(s)h^{2r}+O(h^{2r+2})

(2.11)

and

(2.12)

where

\mathcal{V}_{1}(\varphi)=\left(\int_{0}^{1}[\chi_{r}(t)]^{2}dt\right)\mathcal{M}_{2}\left(\varphi^{(r)}\right)^{2},

\mathcal{V}_{2}(\varphi)=\left(\int_{0}^{1}[\chi_{r}(t)]^{3}dt\right)\mathcal{M}_{3}\left(\varphi^{(r)}\right)^{3}\text{ and }~\mathcal{V}_{2}(\varphi)=0\text{ for }~r=1

are independent of

\left\lVert\mathcal{M}_{3}(\pi_{n}\varphi-\varphi)^{3}\right\rVert_{\infty}=O\left(h^{4}\right)\quad\text{ for }~r=1.

C_{3}=\max\left\{\sup_{\stackrel{{\scriptstyle 0\leq t\leq s\leq 1}}{{|u|\leq\|\varphi\|_{\infty}+\delta}}}\left|D^{(1,0,0)}q_{1}(s,t,u)\right|,\sup_{\stackrel{{\scriptstyle 0\leq s\leq t\leq 1}}{{|u|\leq\|\varphi\|_{\infty}+\delta}}}\left|D^{(1,0,0)}q_{2}(s,t,u)\right|\right\},

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
x\in\mathcal{B}(\varphi,\delta)
. Then, for

\left\lVert\left(\mathcal{K}^{\prime\prime}(x)(v_{1},v_{2})\right)^{{}^{\prime}}\right\rVert_{\infty}\leq C_{3}\left\lVert v_{1}\right\rVert_{\infty}\left\lVert v_{2}\right\rVert_{\infty}.

Proof.

Let

. Then

\displaystyle\mathcal{K}^{\prime\prime}(x)(v_{1},v_{2})(s)

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{1}q(s,t,x(t))\>v_{1}(t)\>v_{2}(t)\>dt

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{s}q_{1}(s,t,x(t))\>v_{1}(t)\>v_{2}(t)\>dt+\int_{s}^{1}q_{2}(s,t,x(t))\>v_{1}(t)\>v_{2}(t)\>dt.

It follows that

\displaystyle\left(\mathcal{K}^{\prime\prime}(x)(v_{1},v_{2})\right)^{{}^{\prime}}(s)

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{s}\frac{\partial q_{1}}{\partial s}\left(s,t,x(t)\right)v_{1}(t)\>v_{2}(t)\>dt+q_{1}(s,s,x(s))\>v_{1}(s)\>v_{2}(s)

\displaystyle~~+\int_{s}^{1}\frac{\partial q_{2}}{\partial s}\left(s,t,x(t)\right)v_{1}(t)\>v_{2}(t)\>dt-q_{2}(s,s,x(s))\>v_{1}(s)\>v_{2}(s).

Since

is continuous on
\Omega
,

\displaystyle\left(\mathcal{K}^{\prime\prime}(x)(v_{1},v_{2})\right)^{{}^{\prime}}(s)=\int_{0}^{s}\frac{\partial q_{1}}{\partial s}\left(s,t,x(t)\right)v_{1}(t)v_{2}(t)\>dt+\int_{s}^{1}\frac{\partial q_{2}}{\partial s}\left(s,t,x(t)\right)v_{1}(t)v_{2}(t)\>dt.

Hence,

\left\lVert\left(\mathcal{K}^{\prime\prime}(x)(v_{1},v_{2})\right)^{{}^{\prime}}\right\rVert_{\infty}\leq C_{3}\left\lVert v_{1}\right\rVert_{\infty}\left\lVert v_{2}\right\rVert_{\infty}.

This completes the proof.

), we write

\displaystyle\mathcal{K}^{\prime\prime}(\varphi)(x_{n}^{G}-\varphi)^{2}

\displaystyle=~\mathcal{K}^{\prime\prime}(\varphi)\left[\pi_{n}(x_{n}^{S}-\varphi)-(I-\pi_{n})\varphi\right]^{2}

\displaystyle=~\mathcal{K}^{\prime\prime}(\varphi)\left(\pi_{n}(x_{n}^{S}-\varphi)\right)^{2}

\displaystyle~~~-2~\mathcal{K}^{\prime\prime}(\varphi)\left(\pi_{n}(x_{n}^{S}-\varphi),(I-\pi_{n})\varphi\right)

\displaystyle~~~+~\mathcal{K}^{\prime\prime}(\varphi)\left((I-\pi_{n})\varphi\right)^{2}.

It follows that

\displaystyle\left(I-\mathcal{K}^{\prime}(\varphi)\right)^{-1}\mathcal{K}^{\prime\prime}(\varphi)(x_{n}^{G}-\varphi)^{2}

\displaystyle=~\mathcal{M}_{2}\left(\pi_{n}(x_{n}^{S}-\varphi)\right)^{2}

\displaystyle~~~-2~\left(I-\mathcal{K}^{\prime}(\varphi)\right)^{-1}\mathcal{K}^{\prime\prime}(\varphi)\left(\pi_{n}(x_{n}^{S}-\varphi),(I-\pi_{n})\varphi\right)

\displaystyle~~~+~\mathcal{M}_{2}\left((I-\pi_{n})\varphi\right)^{2}.

(2.22)

Since
\displaystyle{\left(I-\mathcal{K}^{\prime}(\varphi)\right)^{-1}\mathcal{K}^{(3)}(\varphi)}
is bounded, from (
1.6
), (
2.5
), (
2.6
) and (
2.9
), it is easy to see that

\left\lVert\mathcal{M}_{2}\left(\pi_{n}(x_{n}^{S}-\varphi)\right)^{2}\right\rVert_{\infty}=\left\{{\begin{array}[]{ll}O\left(h^{4}\right),~~~~r=1,\\
O\left(h^{2r+4}\right),~~~\>r\geq 2.\end{array}}\right.

(2.23)

and

\left\lVert\mathcal{K}^{\prime\prime}(\varphi)\left(\pi_{n}(x_{n}^{S}-\varphi),(I-\pi_{n})\varphi\right)\right\rVert_{\infty}=\left\{{\begin{array}[]{ll}O\left(h^{3}\right),~~~~r=1,\\
O\left(h^{2r+2}\right),~~~\>r\geq 2.\end{array}}\right.

When
r=1
, that is, when
\mathscr{X}_{n}
is the space of piecewise constant functions, the order of the term
\left\lVert\mathcal{K}^{\prime\prime}(\varphi)\left(\pi_{n}(x_{n}^{S}-\varphi),(I-\pi_{n})\varphi\right)\right\rVert_{\infty}
can be improved to
h^{4}
in the following way. Note that

\displaystyle\mathcal{K}^{\prime\prime}(\varphi)\left(\pi_{n}(x_{n}^{S}-\varphi),(I-\pi_{n})\varphi\right)(s)

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{1}q_{*}(s,t)\>(\pi_{n}(x_{n}^{S}-\varphi))(t)\>(I-\pi_{n})\varphi(t)\>dt

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{n}\int_{t_{j-1}}^{t_{j}}q_{*}(s,t)(\pi_{n,j}(x_{n}^{S}-\varphi))(t)\>(I-\pi_{n,j})\varphi(t)\>dt.

Since, the range of
\pi_{n}
is
\mathscr{X}_{n}
,
\pi_{n,j}(x_{n}^{S}-\varphi)
is a constant on
[t_{j-1},t_{j}]
. It follows that

\mathcal{K}^{\prime\prime}(\varphi)\left(\pi_{n}(x_{n}^{S}-\varphi),(I-\pi_{n})\varphi\right)(s)=\\
\sum_{j=1}^{n}(\pi_{n,j}(x_{n}^{S}-\varphi))\left(\frac{t_{j-1}+t_{j}}{2}\right)\int_{t_{j-1}}^{t_{j}}q_{*}(s,t)\>(I-\pi_{n,j})\varphi(t)\>dt.

As in the proof of Lemma 9 of Chatelin-Lebbar
[
4
]
, it can be shown that

\left|\int_{t_{j-1}}^{t_{j}}q_{*}(s,t)\>(I-\pi_{n,j})\varphi(t)\>dt\right|=O\left(h^{3}\right).

Since
\left\{\pi_{n,j}\right\}
is uniformly bounded and
\left\lVert x_{n}^{S}-\varphi\right\rVert_{\infty}=O\left(h^{2}\right)
, from the above estimate it follows that

\left\lVert\mathcal{K}^{\prime\prime}(\varphi)\left(\pi_{n}(x_{n}^{S}-\varphi),(I-\pi_{n})\varphi\right)\right\rVert_{\infty}=O\left(h^{4}\right),\quad r=1.

Hence,

\left\lVert\mathcal{K}^{\prime\prime}(\varphi)\left(\pi_{n}(x_{n}^{S}-\varphi),(I-\pi_{n})\varphi\right)\right\rVert_{\infty}=\left\{{\begin{array}[]{ll}O\left(h^{4}\right),~~~~~~~r=1,\\
O\left(h^{2r+2}\right),~~~\>r\geq 2.\end{array}}\right.

(2.24)

Hence, (
2.21
) follows from (
2.11
), (
2.3
) and (
2.24
), the proof of the proposition is complete.
∎

Lemma 2.3
.

s\in[0,1]
and

. Then

(2.25)

where

).

\displaystyle~~~-3~\mathcal{M}_{3}\left(\pi_{n}(x_{n}^{S}-\varphi),\pi_{n}(x_{n}^{S}-\varphi),(I-\pi_{n})\varphi\right)

), (

), (

) we have

\left\lVert\mathcal{M}_{3}\left(\pi_{n}(x_{n}^{S}-\varphi),(I-\pi_{n})\varphi,(I-\pi_{n})\varphi\right)\right\rVert_{\infty}=\left\{{\begin{array}[]{ll}O\left(h^{4}\right),\qquad~r=1,\\

On the other hand, from (

3
The Main Result

Recall that the Iterated Galerkin solution is defined by

\displaystyle x_{n}^{S}-\mathcal{K}(\pi_{n}x_{n}^{S})=f

and the exact solution as

\varphi-\mathcal{K}(\varphi)=f.

In this section, we prove our main result about the asymptotic series expansion for
the iterated Galerkin solution
x_{n}^{S}
at the partition points
t_{i},\>i=0,1,\dots,n
. That is, we will show the following.

\displaystyle\varphi(t_{i})-x_{n}^{S}(t_{i})=\mathcal{A}_{2r}(t_{i})h^{2r}+O\left(h^{2r+2}\right),

(3.1)

where
\mathcal{A}_{2r}
is a function independent of
n
.

Then, we can apply Richardson extrapolation to obtain an approximation of
\varphi
with higher order at the partition points. From Ford et al
[
5
, Section 5]
, it can be shown that a continuous function can be reconstructed from the extrapolated discrete values at the partition points and it approximates the exact solution
\varphi
to higher order in the uniform norm. We will not discuss this thing here. Our main aim is to prove (
3.1
).

Recall that

\mathcal{M}=\left(I-\mathcal{K}^{\prime}(\varphi)\right)^{-1}\mathcal{K}^{\prime}(\varphi)

with

\displaystyle(\mathcal{M}x)(s)=\int_{0}^{1}m(s,t)~x(t)~dt,\;\;\;s\in[0,1],\;x\in\mathcal{X},

(3.2)

where the kernel
m
is of the type of Green’s function.

We quote the following expression for the error in the iterated Galerkin solution from Atkinson et al
[
2
, equation (2.28)]
:

\displaystyle x_{n}^{S}-\varphi

\displaystyle~=~\left(I-\mathcal{K}^{\prime}(\varphi)\right)^{-1}\left\{\left[\mathcal{K}(x_{n}^{G})-\mathcal{K}(\varphi)-\mathcal{K}^{\prime}(\varphi)(x_{n}^{G}-\varphi)\right]\right\}

\displaystyle~~~-\mathcal{M}(I-\pi_{n})\left[\mathcal{K}(x_{n}^{G})-\mathcal{K}(\varphi)-\mathcal{K}^{\prime}(\varphi)(x_{n}^{G}-\varphi)\right]

\displaystyle~~~-\mathcal{M}(I-\pi_{n})\mathcal{K}^{\prime}(\varphi)(x_{n}^{G}-\varphi)

\displaystyle~~~-\mathcal{M}(I-\pi_{n})\varphi.

(3.3)

By the following propositions, we will prove that the second and the third terms on the right hand side of the above equation are of the order
2r+2
or higher, and the first and the last term has an asymptotic expansion at the partition points.

Let

\displaystyle C_{5}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\max_{0\leq i\leq 4}\left(\sup_{\stackrel{{\scriptstyle s,t\in[0,1]}}{{|u|\leq\|\varphi\|_{\infty}+\delta}}}\left|\frac{\partial^{i}\kappa}{\partial u^{i}}(s,t,u)\right|\right).

Let us investigate the first term on the right hand side of the equation (
2.27
) for an asymptotic expansion.

Proposition 3.1
.

Let
x_{n}^{G}
be the Galerkin solution defined by the equation (
1.3
) and
s\in[0,1]
. Then for
r\geq 1,

\left(I-\mathcal{K}^{\prime}(\varphi)\right)^{-1}\left[\mathcal{K}(x_{n}^{G})-\mathcal{K}(\varphi)-\mathcal{K}^{\prime}(\varphi)(x_{n}^{G}-\varphi)\right](s)=\mathcal{V}_{1}(\varphi)(s)\>h^{2r}+O\left(h^{2r+2}\right),

where
\mathcal{V}_{1}
is defined by (
2.11
).

Proof.

Using the generalized Taylor’s series expansion (see Linz
[
13
]
) in the neighbourhood
\mathcal{B}(\varphi,\delta)
, we obtain

\displaystyle\mathcal{K}(x_{n}^{G})-\mathcal{K}(\varphi)-

\displaystyle\mathcal{K}^{\prime}(\varphi)(x_{n}^{G}-\varphi)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\mathcal{K}^{\prime\prime}(\varphi)(x_{n}^{G}-\varphi)^{2}+\frac{1}{6}\mathcal{K}^{(3)}(\varphi)(x_{n}^{G}-\varphi)^{3}+\mathcal{R}_{4}\left(x_{n}^{G},\varphi\right),

where

\mathcal{R}_{4}\left(x_{n}^{G},\varphi\right)=\frac{1}{6}\int_{0}^{1}\mathcal{K}^{(4)}\left(\varphi+\theta(x_{n}^{G}-\varphi)\right)(x_{n}^{G}-\varphi)^{4}(1-\theta)^{3}\;d\theta.

By (
2.1
), we have

\displaystyle\mathcal{K}^{(4)}\left(\varphi+\theta(x_{n}^{G}-\varphi)\right)(x_{n}^{G}-\varphi)^{4}(s)=\int_{0}^{1}\frac{\partial^{4}\kappa}{\partial u^{4}}\left(s,t,\varphi(t)+\theta(x_{n}^{G}-\varphi)(t)\right)(x_{n}^{G}-\varphi)^{4}(t)\>dt.

Since
\left\lVert x_{n}^{G}-\varphi\right\rVert_{\infty}\rightarrow 0
as
n\rightarrow\infty
and
\theta\in(0,1)
,

\varphi+\theta(x_{n}^{G}-\varphi)\in\mathcal{B}(\varphi,\delta).

It follows that

\displaystyle\left\lVert\mathcal{K}^{(4)}\left(\varphi+\theta(x_{n}^{G}-\varphi)\right)(x_{n}^{G}-\varphi)^{4}\right\rVert_{\infty}\leq C_{5}\left\lVert x_{n}^{G}-\varphi\right\rVert_{\infty}^{4}

Using (
1.6
) and the above estimate, we obtain

\mathcal{R}_{4}\left(x_{n}^{G},\varphi\right)=O\left(h^{4r}\right).

(3.4)

Note that

\displaystyle\left(I-\mathcal{K}^{\prime}(\varphi)\right)^{-1}

\displaystyle\left[\mathcal{K}(x_{n}^{G})-\mathcal{K}(\varphi)-\mathcal{K}^{\prime}(\varphi)(x_{n}^{G}-\varphi)\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\>\left(I-\mathcal{K}^{\prime}(\varphi)\right)^{-1}\mathcal{K}^{\prime\prime}(\varphi)(x_{n}^{G}-\varphi)^{2}+\frac{1}{6}\>\left(I-\mathcal{K}^{\prime}(\varphi)\right)^{-1}\mathcal{K}^{(3)}(\varphi)(x_{n}^{G}-\varphi)^{3}

\displaystyle~+\left(I-\mathcal{K}^{\prime}(\varphi)\right)^{-1}\mathcal{R}_{4}\left(x_{n}^{G},\varphi\right).

Hence, by Lemma
2.2
, Lemma
2.3
, equation (
3.4
) and and the above estimate, we obtain the followings.

For
r=1
,

\left(I-\mathcal{K}^{\prime}(\varphi)\right)^{-1}\left[\mathcal{K}(x_{n}^{G})-\mathcal{K}(\varphi)-\mathcal{K}^{\prime}(\varphi)(x_{n}^{G}-\varphi)\right](s)=\mathcal{V}_{1}(\varphi)(s)\>h^{2r}+O\left(h^{4}\right),

for
r\geq 2
,

\left(I-\mathcal{K}^{\prime}(\varphi)\right)^{-1}\left[\mathcal{K}(x_{n}^{G})-\mathcal{K}(\varphi)-\mathcal{K}^{\prime}(\varphi)(x_{n}^{G}-\varphi)\right](s)\\
=\mathcal{V}_{1}(\varphi)(s)\>h^{2r}+\mathcal{V}_{2}(\varphi)(s)\>h^{3r}+O\left(h^{2r+2}\right).

Since
3r\geq 2r+2
for
r\geq 2
,

\left(I-\mathcal{K}^{\prime}(\varphi)\right)^{-1}\left[\mathcal{K}(x_{n}^{G})-\mathcal{K}(\varphi)-\mathcal{K}^{\prime}(\varphi)(x_{n}^{G}-\varphi)\right](s)=\mathcal{V}_{1}(\varphi)(s)\>h^{2r}+O\left(h^{2r+2}\right),\quad r\geq 2.

This completes the proof.
∎

Now we investigate the second term on the R.H.S. of the equation (
3
).

Proposition 3.2
.

Let
\displaystyle{\left\{t_{i}:i=0,1,\dots,n\right\}}
be the set of all partition points defined by (
2.4
). Then for
r\geq 1
,

\mathcal{M}(I-\pi_{n})\left[\mathcal{K}(x_{n}^{G})-\mathcal{K}(\varphi)-\mathcal{K}^{\prime}(\varphi)(x_{n}^{G}-\varphi)\right](t_{i})=O\left(h^{2r+2}\right).

Proof.

By the generalized Taylor’s theorem we obtain

\mathcal{K}(x_{n}^{G})-\mathcal{K}(\varphi)-\mathcal{K}^{\prime}(\varphi)(x_{n}^{G}-\varphi)=\int_{0}^{1}\mathcal{K}^{\prime\prime}\left(\varphi+\theta(x_{n}^{G}-\varphi)\right)(x_{n}^{G}-\varphi)^{2}(1-\theta)\;d\theta.

Let

\mathcal{R}_{2}(x_{n}^{G},\varphi)=\int_{0}^{1}\mathcal{K}^{\prime\prime}\left(\varphi+\theta(x_{n}^{G}-\varphi)\right)(x_{n}^{G}-\varphi)^{2}(1-\theta)\;d\theta.

(3.5)

Note that

\displaystyle\mathcal{K}^{\prime\prime}\left(\varphi+\theta(x_{n}^{G}-\varphi)\right)(x_{n}^{G}-\varphi)^{2}(s)=\int_{0}^{1}\frac{\partial^{2}\kappa}{\partial u^{2}}\left(s,t,\varphi(t)+\theta(x_{n}^{G}-\varphi)(t)\right)(x_{n}^{G}-\varphi)^{2}(t)\>dt.

It follows that

\displaystyle\left\lVert\mathcal{K}^{\prime\prime}\left(\varphi+\theta(x_{n}^{G}-\varphi)\right)(x_{n}^{G}-\varphi)^{2}\right\rVert_{\infty}\leq C_{3}\left\lVert x_{n}^{G}-\varphi\right\rVert_{\infty}^{2}.

Since
\displaystyle{\left\lVert x_{n}^{G}-\varphi\right\rVert_{\infty}=O\left(h^{r}\right)}
,

\displaystyle\left\lVert\mathcal{K}^{\prime\prime}\left(\varphi+\theta(x_{n}^{G}-\varphi)\right)(x_{n}^{G}-\varphi)^{2}\right\rVert_{\infty}=O\left(h^{2r}\right).

(3.6)

Let
s\in[0,1]
be fixed and
m_{s}(t)=m(s,t),~t\in[0,1]
, then

\mathcal{M}(I-\pi_{n})\left[\mathcal{K}(x_{n}^{G})-\mathcal{K}(\varphi)-\mathcal{K}^{\prime}(\varphi)(x_{n}^{G}-\varphi)\right](s)\\
=\left<m_{s},\>(I-\pi_{n})\left[\mathcal{K}(x_{n}^{G})-\mathcal{K}(\varphi)-\mathcal{K}^{\prime}(\varphi)(x_{n}^{G}-\varphi)\right]\right>,

where
\left<\cdot,\cdot\right>
is the usual inner product in
L^{2}[0,1]
, i.e.,

\left<x,y\right>=\int_{0}^{1}x(t)\>y(t)\>dt,\quad x,y\in L^{2}[0,1].

Since
I-\pi_{n}
is self-adjoint,

\mathcal{M}(I-\pi_{n})\left[\mathcal{K}(x_{n}^{G})-\mathcal{K}(\varphi)-\mathcal{K}^{\prime}(\varphi)(x_{n}^{G}-\varphi)\right](t_{i})\\
=\left<(I-\pi_{n})m_{t_{i}},\>(I-\pi_{n})\left[\mathcal{K}(x_{n}^{G})-\mathcal{K}(\varphi)-\mathcal{K}^{\prime}(\varphi)(x_{n}^{G}-\varphi)\right]\right>.

(3.7)

Note that
m_{t_{i}}
is continuous on
[t_{j-1},t_{j}]
and
r
times continuously differentiable on
(t_{j-1},t_{j})
for all
j=1,2,\dots,n
. Therefore by (
2.7
)

\displaystyle\left\lVert(I-\pi_{n,j})m_{t_{i}}\right\rVert_{\Delta_{j},\infty}=O\left(h^{r}\right).

(3.8)

Using (
2.5
), (
3.6
), (
3.7
) and the above estimate, we obtain

\mathcal{M}(I-\pi_{n})\left[\mathcal{K}(x_{n}^{G})-\mathcal{K}(\varphi)-\mathcal{K}^{\prime}(\varphi)(x_{n}^{G}-\varphi)\right](t_{i})=O\left(h^{3r}\right),\quad r\geq 2.

(3.9)

Consider the case
\mathbf{r=1}
.

From (
3.5
), it is easy to see that if
\mathcal{K}^{\prime\prime}\left(\varphi+\theta(x_{n}^{G}-\varphi)\right)(x_{n}^{G}-\varphi)^{2}
is differentiable, then
\mathcal{R}_{2}(x_{n}^{G},\varphi)
is differentiable. Since
\varphi+\theta(x_{n}^{G}-\varphi)\in\mathcal{B}(\varphi,\delta)
, from Lemma
2.1
we have
\mathcal{R}_{2}(x_{n}^{G},\varphi)
is differentiable. Thus, from (
3.5
)

\left(\mathcal{R}_{2}(x_{n}^{G},\varphi)\right)^{{}^{\prime}}=\int_{0}^{1}\left(\mathcal{K}^{\prime\prime}\left(\varphi+\theta(x_{n}^{G}-\varphi)\right)(x_{n}^{G}-\varphi)^{2}\right)^{{}^{\prime}}(1-\theta)\;d\theta.

This implies,

\left\lVert\left(\mathcal{R}_{2}(x_{n}^{G},\varphi)\right)^{{}^{\prime}}\right\rVert_{\infty}\leq\frac{1}{2}\left\lVert\left(\mathcal{K}^{\prime\prime}\left(\varphi+\theta(x_{n}^{G}-\varphi)\right)(x_{n}^{G}-\varphi)^{2}\right)^{{}^{\prime}}\right\rVert_{\infty},\qquad 0<\theta<1.

Using Lemma
2.1
,

\left\lVert\left(\mathcal{R}_{2}(x_{n}^{G},\varphi)\right)^{{}^{\prime}}\right\rVert_{\infty}\leq C_{3}\left\lVert x_{n}^{G}-\varphi\right\rVert^{2}.

From (
2.6
), it follows that

\displaystyle\left\lVert(I-\pi_{n})\left[\mathcal{K}(x_{n}^{G})-\mathcal{K}(\varphi)-\mathcal{K}^{\prime}(\varphi)(x_{n}^{G}-\varphi)\right]\right\rVert_{\infty}

\displaystyle~=~\left\lVert(I-\pi_{n})\mathcal{R}_{2}(x_{n}^{G},\varphi)\right\rVert_{\infty}

\displaystyle~\leq~C_{1}C_{3}\left\lVert x_{n}^{G}-\varphi\right\rVert^{2}h.

By (
1.5
), (
3.7
), (
3.8
) and the above estimate, we obtain

\mathcal{M}(I-\pi_{n})\left[\mathcal{K}(x_{n}^{G})-\mathcal{K}(\varphi)-\mathcal{K}^{\prime}(\varphi)(x_{n}^{G}-\varphi)\right](t_{i})=O\left(h^{4}\right),\quad r=1.

(3.10)

Hence, the required result follows from (
3.9
) and (
3.10
). ∎

Next we investigate the third term on the R.H.S. of the equation (
3
).

Proposition 3.3
.

Let
\displaystyle{\left\{t_{i}:i=0,1,\dots,n\right\}}
be the set of all partition points defined by (
2.4
). Then

\mathcal{M}(I-\pi_{n})\mathcal{K}^{\prime}(\varphi)(x_{n}^{G}-\varphi)(t_{i})=O\left(h^{2r+2}\right),\quad\text{ for }r\geq 1.

Proof.

From (
3.2
) we have

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}(I-\pi_{n})\mathcal{K}^{\prime}(\varphi)(x_{n}^{G}-\varphi)(t_{i})

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{1}m(t_{i},t)~(I-\pi_{n})\mathcal{K}^{\prime}(\varphi)(x_{n}^{G}-\varphi)(t)~dt

\displaystyle=\left<m_{t_{i}}~,~(I-\pi_{n})\mathcal{K}^{\prime}(\varphi)(x_{n}^{G}-\varphi)\right>

\displaystyle=\left<(I-\pi_{n})m_{t_{i}}~,~(I-\pi_{n})\mathcal{K}^{\prime}(\varphi)(x_{n}^{G}-\varphi)\right>.

(3.11)

It is easy to see that,
m_{t_{i}}
is continuous on
[t_{j-1},t_{j}]
and
r
times continuously differentiable on
(t_{j-1},t_{j})
for all
j=1,2,\dots,n
. Therefore by (
2.7
)

\displaystyle\left\lVert(I-\pi_{n,j})m_{t_{i}}\right\rVert_{\Delta_{j},\infty}=O\left(h^{r}\right).

(3.12)

Note that

\mathcal{K}^{\prime}(\varphi)(x_{n}^{G}-\varphi)=\mathcal{K}^{\prime}(\varphi)\left(\pi_{n}(x_{n}^{S}-\varphi)\right)-\mathcal{K}^{\prime}(\varphi)(I-\pi_{n})\varphi.

(3.13)

Thus, by (
1.6
), (
2.5
) and (
2.3
) we obtain,

\left\lVert\mathcal{K}^{\prime}(\varphi)(x_{n}^{G}-\varphi)\right\rVert_{\infty}=O\left(h^{r+2}\right),\quad r\geq 2.

Hence, from (
3
), (
3.12
) and the above estimate, we obtain

\mathcal{M}(I-\pi_{n})\mathcal{K}^{\prime}(\varphi)(x_{n}^{G}-\varphi)(t_{i})=O\left(h^{2r+2}\right),\quad r\geq 2.

(3.14)

When
\mathbf{r=1}
, that is, when
\mathscr{X}_{n}
is the space of piecewise constant functions, it is easy to see from (
1.5
), (
2.3
) and (
3.13
) that

\left\lVert\mathcal{K}^{\prime}(\varphi)(x_{n}^{G}-\varphi)\right\rVert_{\infty}=O\left(h^{2}\right),

which is not equal to
O\left(h^{2r+2}\right)
with
r=1
. We consider this case separately.

Note that,

(I-\pi_{n})\mathcal{K}^{\prime}(\varphi)(x_{n}^{G}-\varphi)=(I-\pi_{n})\mathcal{K}^{\prime}(\varphi)\left(\pi_{n}(x_{n}^{S}-\varphi)\right)-(I-\pi_{n})\mathcal{K}^{\prime}(\varphi)(I-\pi_{n})\varphi.

(3.15)

From (
2.16
), we have

\left\lVert(I-\pi_{n})\mathcal{K}^{\prime}(\varphi)\left(\pi_{n}(x_{n}^{S}-\varphi)\right)\right\rVert_{\infty}\leq C_{1}C_{4}\left\lVert\pi_{n}\right\rVert\left\lVert x_{n}^{S}-\varphi\right\rVert_{\infty}h.

From (
1.5
) and (
2.5
), it follows that

\left\lVert(I-\pi_{n})\mathcal{K}^{\prime}(\varphi)\left(\pi_{n}(x_{n}^{S}-\varphi)\right)\right\rVert_{\infty}=O\left(h^{3}\right).

By (
2.19
), (
3
), (
3.12
), (
3.15
) and the above estimate, we obtain

\mathcal{M}(I-\pi_{n})\mathcal{K}^{\prime}(\varphi)(x_{n}^{G}-\varphi)(t_{i})=O\left(h^{4}\right),\quad r=1.

(3.16)

Hence, the required result follows from (
3.14
) and (
3.16
).
∎

Now, we prove our main theorem.

Theorem 3.4
.

Let
f\in C^{2r}[0,1]
, and the kernel of the Urysohn integral operator (
1.1
) be of class
\mathscr{G}_{4}(r,0)
. Let
\varphi
be a fixed point of the operator
\mathcal{T}
defined by (
2.2
), with
1
not an eigenvalue of
\mathcal{K}^{\prime}(\varphi)
. For
r\geq 1
, let
\mathscr{X}_{n}
be the space of piecewise polynomials of degree
\leq r-1
with respect to the partition (
2.3
) and
\pi_{n}
be the orthogonal projection defined by (
2.5
)–(
2.6
). Let
x_{n}^{S}
be the iterated Galerkin solution defined by (
1.4
). Then, for
i=0,1,\ldots,n
,

x_{n}^{S}(t_{i})-\varphi(t_{i})=-\zeta_{2r}(t_{i})\>h^{2r}+O\left(h^{2r+2}\right),

where
\zeta_{2r}
is a function bounded by a constant independent of
h
.

Proof.

From the equation (
3
)

\displaystyle x_{n}^{S}(t_{i})-\varphi(t_{i})

\displaystyle~=~\left(I-\mathcal{K}^{\prime}(\varphi)\right)^{-1}\left[\mathcal{K}(x_{n}^{G})-\mathcal{K}(\varphi)-\mathcal{K}^{\prime}(\varphi)(x_{n}^{G}-\varphi)\right](t_{i})

\displaystyle~~~-\mathcal{M}(I-\pi_{n})\left[\mathcal{K}(x_{n}^{G})-\mathcal{K}(\varphi)-\mathcal{K}^{\prime}(\varphi)(x_{n}^{G}-\varphi)\right](t_{i})

\displaystyle~~~-\mathcal{M}(I-\pi_{n})\mathcal{K}^{\prime}(\varphi)(x_{n}^{G}-\varphi)(t_{i})

\displaystyle~~~-\mathcal{M}(I-\pi_{n})\varphi(t_{i}).

Let

\displaystyle\zeta_{2r}=\mathcal{A}_{2r}+\mathcal{V}_{1}(\varphi).

Hence, the proof of this theorem follows from the equation (
2.10
), Proposition
3.1
, Proposition
3.2
and Proposition
3.3
.

∎

We can now apply one step of Richardson extrapolation and obtain an approximations of
\varphi
of order
h^{2r+2}
at the partition points.

Define

x_{n}^{EX}=\frac{2^{4r}x_{2n}^{S}-x_{n}^{S}}{2^{4r}-1}.

Then under the assumptions of Theorem
3.4
, we have the following result

x_{n}^{EX}{(t_{i})}-\varphi{(t_{i})}=O\left(h^{2r+2}\right),\quad i=0,1,2,\ldots,n.

Numerical Illustration

For the sake of numerical illustration, we consider the following example of a non-linear Hammerstein integral equation from Kulkarni-Rane
[
12
]
.
Consider

\varphi(s)-\int_{0}^{1}\kappa(s,t)\left[\psi\left(t,\varphi(t)\right)\right]\>dt=f(s),\;\;\;0\leq s\leq 1,

(4.1)

where

\kappa(s,t)=\frac{1}{\gamma\sinh\gamma}\left\{{\begin{array}[]{ll}\sinh\gamma s\>\sinh\gamma(1-t),&~0\leq t\leq s\leq 1,\\
\gamma(1-s)\sinh\gamma t,&~0\leq s\leq t\leq 1,\end{array}}\right.

with
\gamma=\sqrt{12},

and

\psi(t,\varphi(t))=\gamma^{2}\varphi(t)-2\left(\varphi(t)\right)^{3},\quad t\in[0,1].

We have
f(s)=\frac{1}{\gamma\sinh\gamma}\left\{2\sinh\gamma(1-s)+\frac{2}{3}\sinh\gamma s\right\}.
The exact solution of (
4.1
) is given by

\displaystyle\varphi(s)=\frac{2}{2s+1},\quad s\in[0,1].

Let
\mathscr{X}_{n}
be the space of piecewise constant polynomials with respect to the uniform partition (
2.3
) of the interval
[0,1]
considered before. Let
\pi_{n}:L^{\infty}[0,1]\rightarrow\mathscr{X}_{n}
be the orthogonal projection defined by (
2.5
)–(
2.6
).

In this case, it is given by

(\pi_{n}\varphi)(s)=\displaystyle\frac{1}{h}\int_{(i-1)h}^{ih}\varphi(t)\>dt,\;s\in[(i-1)h,ih],

where
h=\frac{1}{n}
. In the definition of projection operator defined above, if we replace the integral by the right hand rule, then

(\pi_{n}\varphi)(ih-)=\varphi(ih-).
Let
x_{n}^{S}
be the Sloan solution defined by (
1.4
). Then
x_{n}^{G}(ih)=\displaystyle\frac{\pi_{n}x_{n}^{S}(ih-)+\pi_{n}x_{n}^{S}(ih+)}{2}
at the partition points is obtained by solving the approximate system of non-linear equations which gives the values of
\pi_{n}x_{n}^{S}(ih-)
and
\pi_{n}x_{n}^{S}(ih+).
The system is as follows:

\alpha_{j}=h\sum_{l=1}^{n}\kappa(s_{j},s_{l})\left[\gamma^{2}\alpha_{l}-2\frac{\alpha_{l}^{3}}{h}\right]+\frac{f(s_{j})}{\sqrt{h}},\quad j=1,2,\ldots,n,

(4.2)

where
\alpha_{l}=\pi_{n}x_{n}^{G}(s_{l})
and
s_{l}=(l-\frac{1}{2})h
for
l=1,2,\ldots,n
. The above system is obtained by replacing all the integrals by numerical integration formula.

We have used Picard’s iteration to solve the above system of non-linear equations.

Let
t_{i}=(i-1)/20,i=1,2,\ldots,21
be the partition points with step size
h=\frac{1}{20}.

It is easy to see that

\displaystyle E_{1}^{n}(t_{i})=|\varphi(t_{i})-x_{n}^{S}(t_{i})|=O\left(h^{2}\right).

We define

\displaystyle x_{n}^{EX}(t_{i})=\frac{4x_{2n}^{S}(t_{i})-x_{n}^{S}(t_{i})}{3}.

Then

E_{2}^{n}(t_{i})=\left|\varphi(t_{i})-x_{n}^{EX}(t_{i})\right|=O\left(h^{4}\right).

The orders of convergence are calculated using the formula :

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \alpha_{1}=\frac{log(E_{1}^{n}(t_{i})/E_{1}^{2n}(t_{i}))}{log(2)},\\
\beta=\frac{log(E_{2}^{n}(t_{i})/E_{2}^{2n}(t_{i}))}{log(2)},\end{aligned}~~~~~~~n=40

\displaystyle\alpha_{2}=\frac{log(E_{1}^{n}(t_{i})/E_{1}^{2n}(t_{i}))}{log(2)},\quad n=20.

We expect
\alpha_{1}=\alpha_{2}=2

Abstract
. In this study, we show that all non-trivial solutions of f ′′ + A ⁡ ( z ) ​ f ′ + B ⁡ ( z ) ​ f = 0 f^{\prime\prime}+A(z)f^{\prime}+B(z)f=0 have infinite order, provided that the entire coefficient A ⁡ ( z ) A(z) has certain restrictions and B ⁡ ( z ) B(z) has multiply-connected Fatou component. We also extend these results to higher order linear differential equations.

1.
Introduction

In this paper, we discuss the growth of solutions of

(1)

f^{\prime\prime}+A(z)f^{\prime}+B(z)f=0,

where
A(z)
and
B(z)
are entire functions. We use the notations
\lambda(f)
,
\rho(f)
and
\mu(f)
for the exponent of convergence, order of growth and lower order of growth of the entire function
f(z)
respectively. Since the coefficients are entire functions, all the solutions are also entire functions. If at least one of the coefficients is a transcendental entire, then almost all the non-trivial solutions are of infinite order. The main focus of this study is to find restrictions on
A(z)
and
B(z)
so that all non-trivial solutions of equation (
1
) have infinite order. We collate some well-known results in the following theorem.

Theorem 1.

Suppose that
A(z)
and
B(z)
are non-constant entire functions. Then, all non-trivial solutions of equation (
1
) are of infinite order, if one of the following holds:

(i)

[
7
]

\rho(A)<\rho(B)
;

(ii)

[
7
]

A(z)
is a polynomial and
B(z)
is transcendental;

(iii)

[
9
]

\rho(B)<\rho(A)\leq\frac{1}{2}
.

Example 1
.

The differential equation
f^{\prime\prime}+(ze^{-z}-z-1)f^{\prime}+zf=0
,
\rho(ze^{-z}-z-1)>\rho(z)
has a solution
f(z)=e^{z}-1
of order
1
.

Example 2
.

The order of growth of coefficients of the differential equation
f^{\prime\prime}-ze^{-z}f^{\prime}+e^{-z}f=0
is equal to
1
and it has a solution
f(z)=z
of order
0
.

In the above two examples we observe that
\rho(B)\leq\rho(A)
and the differential equation (
1
) has a non-trivial solution of finite order. Here, we are trying to find the conditions on coefficients
A(z)
and
B(z)
such that all non-trivial solutions of equation (
1
) are of infinite order. Before stating our main results, we invoke the definition of Fatou set for the meromorphic function
f
.

Definition 1
.

The set of family of iterates
\{f^{n}:n\in\mathbb{N}\}
for the transcendental meromorphic function
f
is called a Fatou set if it is well-defined and forms a normal family.

In this paper, we prove the following results.

Theorem A
.

Let
A(z)
satisfy
\lambda(A)<\rho(A)
and
B(z)
be a transcendental entire function with a multiply-connected Fatou component. Then, all non-trivial solutions of equation (
1
) have infinite order of growth.

We illustrate our first result with an example.

Example 3
.

Consider the function

(2)

B(z)=Cz^{2}\displaystyle{\prod_{n=1}^{\infty}\left(1+\frac{z}{a_{n}}\right)},

where
C=(4e)^{-1}
, and

satisfies
1<a_{1}<a_{2}<...

a_{n+1}<B(a_{n})<2a_{n+1}

[

. Here,

has a multiply-connected Fatou component. Let
A(z)=\sin ze^{z^{2}}
. Then using Theorem

, all non-trivial solutions of equation (

Theorem B
.

Let
A(z)
be an entire function having a finite deficient value and
B(z)
be a transcendental entire function with a multiply-connected Fatou component. Then, all non-trivial solutions
f
of equation (
1
) are of infinite order.

Let
A(z)
be an entire function of finite genus
p\geq 1
and all of its zeros lie in the sector

\left\{z:|\arg z|\leq\frac{\pi}{2(p+1)}\right\}.

T. Kobayashi
[
10

proved that such a function has zero as a deficient value. Note that the genus of an entire function

having the representation

where

P(z)=z^{m}\prod\limits_{n=1}^{\infty}\left(1-\frac{z}{z_{n}}\right)\exp\left(\frac{z}{z_{n}}+\cdots+\frac{1}{p}\left(\frac{z}{z_{n}}\right)^{p}\right)

and root of multiplicity

Corollary 1
.

Let

be an entire function of finite genus
p\geq 1
, and let all of its zeros lie in the sector

\left\{z:|\arg z|\leq\frac{\pi}{2(p+1)}\right\}.

Suppose that
B(z)
is a transcendental entire function with a multiply-connected Fatou component. Then, every non-trivial solution of equation (
1

Example 4

We illustrate Theorem B by an example. The differential equation

Before stating our final result, we would like to recall the following definition.

Definition 2
.

For an entire function
A(z)
of order
\rho
with
respect to the proximate order
\rho(r)
, the indicator

is defined by

h(\theta)=\lim_{r\to\infty}\sup\frac{\log|A(re^{\iota\theta})|}{r^{\rho(r)}},

where
\rho(r)\to\rho
as

. The function

is said to have a
completely regular growth if there exist disks
D(a_{k},s_{k})
satisfying

\sum_{|a_{k}|\leq r}s_{k}=o(r),

such that

\log|A(re^{(\iota\theta)})|=h(\theta)r^{\rho(r)}+o(r^{\rho(r)}),

re^{\iota\theta}\not\in\cup_{k}D(a_{k},s_{k})

as
r\to\infty
, uniformly in
\theta.
The set formed by the union of disks
\cup_{k}D(a_{k},s_{k})
is called
C_{0}
-set.

More details can be obtained from the book by B. Ja. Levin
[
15

. Our final result is as follows:

Let

be a completely regular growth entire function. The set

be a transcendental entire function with a multiply-connected Fatou component and
\rho(A)\neq\rho(B)
. Then,
every non-trivial solution of equation (

[

[

Let

be an entire

Consider the

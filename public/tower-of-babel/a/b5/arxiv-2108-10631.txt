On Z-mean reflected BSDEs
Joffrey Derchu, Thibaut Mastrolia

Abstract
In this paper we provide conditions for the existence of supersolutions to BSDEs with mean-reflections on the Z Z component. We show that, contrary to BSDEs with mean-reflections on the Y Y component, we cannot expect a supersolution with a deterministic increasing process K K . Nonetheless, we give conditions for the existence of a supersolution for a stochastic component K K and under various constraints. We formalize some previous arguments on the time-inconsistency of such problems, proving that a minimal supersolution is necessarily a solution in our framework. Keywords: Constrained BSDEs, Malliavin calculus .

Introduction

The aim of this paper is to discuss the existence of supersolutions

(Y,Z,K)
of a backward stochastic differential equation (BSDE in short) of the form

Y_{t}=\xi+\int_{t}^{T}f(s,Y_{s},Z_{s})ds-\int_{t}^{T}Z_{s}dB_{s}+K_{T}-K_{t},0\leq t\leq T

(1.1)

under
weak
constraints on
Z
, where
B
is a one-dimensional Brownian motion, and
\xi
and
f
are potentially non-Markovian. Backward stochastic differential equations were first introduced by
[
Pardoux and Peng, 1990
,
Pardoux and Peng, 1992
]
in a Markovian setting to investigate its connections with viscosity solutions of non-linear PDE, and were subsequently extended to a non-Markovian framework and used for the valuation of contingent claims in complete markets in
[
El Karoui et al., 1997
]
,
[
Antonelli, 1993
]
, or in incomplete markets, see
[
El Karoui and Quenez, 1995
]
,
[
Foellmer and Schweizer, 1991
]
,
[
Ansel and Stricker, 1994
]
,
[
Jacka, 1992
]
. These works was then extended to the problem of hedging a claim under almost-sure constraints on the portfolio. In
[
Cvitanic and Karatzas, 1993
]
, the authors showed that the hedging price of the claim under convex constraints is given, if it exists, by a minimal supersolution of (
1.1
). Also, other works (
[
Peng, 1999
]
,
[
Cvitanić et al., 1997
]
,
[
Peng and Xu, 2013
]
,
[
Peng and Xu, 2010
]
) show that such a minimal supersolution can be well approximated by a sequence of penalized unconstrained BSDEs, if a supersolution exists. We can also cite the relatively similar approach used to deal with optimal stopping problems and the pricing of American options via reflected BSDEs, see
[
Karoui et al., 1997
]
,
[
Essaky and Hassani, 2011
]
,
[
Hamadene and Lepeltier, 1995
]
.

Nevertheless, few papers actually deal with the existence of a supersolution under those constraints. Some interesting works related to this problem include
[
Bouchard et al., 2018b
]
,
[
Bouchard et al., 2018a
]
. The first paper (
[
Bouchard et al., 2018b
]
) shows existence of supersolutions if
\phi
is upper bounded and if the constraint can be written as
Z_{t}\in K
for
K
a convex set of
\mathbb{R}
containing
0
, by relying on a dual formulation of the problem, while
[
Bouchard et al., 2018a
]
shows that, if the constraints are of the form
\ell(Z_{t})\leq 0
with
\ell
the supremum of linear functions, then the existence of a supersolution can be deduced if some particular singular control problem has a value. By and large the existence of a supersolution is not easy to obtain, and we give in this paper some examples of natural problems in which existence does not hold and penalization methods thus yield diverging results.

A possible approach is to relax the constraints by considering constraints in expectation. This idea has been used in
[
Briand et al., 2016
]
and
[
Briand et al., 2020
]
for constraints on the law of
Y
to deal with problems of hedging with constraints on a risk measure or with dynamical systems with constraints on a large number of particles.

We observe three main approaches in the literature to study mean-reflected BSDE theory (with constraints on the distribution of
Y
).

(i)

First, fixed point arguments can be used when the process
Z
plays no role in the generator and the problem can be reformulated as a stopping problem (
[
Djehiche et al., 2019
]
,
[
Chen et al., 2020
]
).

(ii)

Second, a penalization scheme can be used, but requires some monotonicity property (see also
[
Djehiche et al., 2019
]
,
[
Briand et al., 2016
]
,
[
Chen et al., 2020
]
).

(iii)

Third, some papers restrain the problem to deterministic
K
, as in
[
Briand et al., 2016
]
or
[
Briand et al., 2020
]
.

In this paper we address the problem of solving (
1.1
) with law constraints on
Z
of the form
\mathbb{E}[\ell(t,Z_{t})]\geq 0
for some constraint function
\ell
. Beyond the particular case (i) implying no dependence of the
Z
process in the generator of the BSDE, we show in this paper that we cannot follow directly the two other approaches (ii) and (iii). It requires to develop another method to investigate the existence of supersolutions to
Z
-mean reflected BSDE. The approach used in the present paper is based on Malliavin calculus and aims at solving the
Z
-mean reflected BSDE in a smooth class of for the component
K
, which will have a stochastic exponential form.

The structure of the paper is the following. We first state the definitions and notations in Section
2
. Then, we turn to the existence of supersolutions to BSDE (
1.1
) with
Z
-mean reflection
\mathbb{E}[\ell(t,Z_{t})]\geq 0
in Section
3
. This section contains the mains ideas of the paper. In particular, we show in Section
3.1
that contrary to the case of law constraints on
Y
, deterministic non-decreasing processes
K
cannot always be used so that neither the approach (ii) nor the approach (iii) above can be used. However, if non-deterministic
K
processes are allowed, then we give conditions on the moments of the Malliavin derivative of the terminal payoff
\xi
which ensure the existence of a supersolution, see Section
3.2
. Then, we investigate in Section
3.5
conditions ensuring the existence of a minimal supersolution to a
Z
mean reflected BSDE. We follow up on an argument given in
[
Briand et al., 2016
]
about the time inconsistency of the problem if non-deterministic
K
processes are allowed, to fully describe all possible minimal supersolutions in this case. Finally, we give in Section
3.5.2
an application.

Preliminaries

2.1
Spaces and Notations

We are given a finite horizon
T
and a complete probability space
(\Omega,{\cal F},\mathbb{P})
endowed with a Brownian motion
B=(B_{t})_{0\leq t\leq T}
and its usual augmented filtration of
\{{\cal F}_{t}\}_{0\leq t\leq T}
. Let
p>1
, we denote by

•

L^{2}({\cal F}_{T})
the set of real valued
{\cal F}_{T}-
measurable random variables
\xi
such that
\mathbb{E}[|\xi|^{p}]]<\infty
,

•

{\cal S}^{p}

adapted continuous processes

[0,T]
such that

\|Y\|_{{\cal S}^{p}}:=\mathbb{E}[\sup_{0\leq r\leq T}|Y_{r}|^{p}]^{\frac{1}{p}}<\infty,

•

the set of predictable continuous
\mathbb{R}-
valued processes
Z
s.t.

\|Z\|_{{\cal H}^{p}}:=\mathbb{E}[\int_{0}^{T}|Z_{r}|^{p}dr]^{\frac{1}{p}}<\infty.

•

{\cal A}^{2}
is the closed subset of
{\cal S}^{2}
consisting in nondecreasing processes
K=(K_{t})_{0\leq t\leq T}
with
K_{0}=0
.

Let

be the space of continuously differentiable function on
\mathbb{R}^{+}
and twice continuously differentiable function on
\mathbb{R}
. In addition, we denote by

the set of Borel measurable square integrable real valued functions on

and let us consider the following inner product on

\langle f,g\rangle:=\int_{0}^{T}f(t)g(t)dt,\text{ for any Borel measurable functions $(f,g)$ on $[0,T]$},

with associated norm
\|{\cdot}\|_{L^{2}([0,T])}
.

A
cylindrical functional
is a square integrable random variables
F
of the form

F=f(W_{t_{1}},\ldots,W_{t_{n}}),\quad(t_{1},\ldots,t_{n})\in[0,T]^{n}.

(2.1)

where
f
is a continuously differentiable and bounded function on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
with
n\in\mathbb{N}\setminus\{0\}
with bounded derivatives denoted by
f_{x_{i}}
.
For any cylindrical functional
F
of the form (
2.1
), the Malliavin derivative
DF
of
F
is defined as the following
L^{2}([0,T])-
valued random variable:

DF:=\sum_{i=1}^{n}f_{x_{i}}(W_{t_{1}},\ldots,W_{t_{n}})\textbf{1}_{[0,t_{i}]}.

(2.2)

It is then customary to identify
DF
with the stochastic process
(D_{t}F)_{t\in[0,T]}
. Denote then by
\mathcal{D}^{1,2}
the closure of the set of cylindrical functionals with respect to the Sobolev norm
\|\cdot\|_{1,2}
, defined as:

\|F\|_{1,2}:=\mathbb{E}\left[|F|^{2}\right]+\mathbb{E}\left[\int_{0}^{T}|D_{t}F|^{2}dt\right].

2.2
Definitions and settings

Given a terminal condition
\xi\in L^{2}({\cal F}_{T})
, a generator
f:[0,T]\times\Omega\times\mathbb{R}^{2}\longrightarrow\mathbb{R}
and a constraint
\ell:[0,T]\times\mathbb{R}\longrightarrow\mathbb{R}

(Y,Z,K)\in{\cal S}^{2}\times{\cal H}^{2}\times{\cal A}^{2}
to the following BSDE

Y_{t}=\xi+\int_{t}^{T}f(s,Y_{s},Z_{s})ds-\int_{t}^{T}Z_{s}dB_{s}+K_{T}-K_{t},0\leq t\leq T

(2.3)

satisfying the following unilateral constraint

1

1

\mathbb{E}[\ell(t,Z_{t})]\geq 0,\text{ for any }t\in[0,T].

(2.4)

Definition 2.1
.

A triplet of process
(Y,Z,K)\in{\cal S}^{2}\times{\cal H}^{2}\times{\cal A}^{2}
satisfying (
2.3
) under the constraint (
2.4
) is called a supersolution. A supersolution

A supersolution

such that, for any other supersolution

for all

In the following we will often consider the following assumption extracted from Proposition 5.3 of
[
El Karoui et al., 1997
]
ensuring the existence of the

component to BSDE (
2.3
) together with the existence of a version of
(D_{r}Y_{t},D_{r}Z_{t})_{0\leq r\leq t\leq T}
.

Assumption
(\mathbf{A})

(i)

\xi\in{\cal D}^{1,2}
such that
\int_{0}^{T}\mathbb{E}[|D_{t}\xi|^{2}]dt<\infty
,

(ii)

is continuously differentiable in
(y,z)
, with uniformly bounded derivatives denoted by
\partial_{Y}f
and
\partial_{Z}f
respectively in
y
and
z
and for each
(y,z)
,
(t,\omega)\longrightarrow f(t,.,y,z)\in{\cal D}^{1,2}
and its derivative denoted by
Df
admits a progressively measurable version such that
|D_{.}f|
is uniformly bounded by some constant
d\in\mathbb{R}^{+}
,

(iii)

\xi\in L^{4}(\mathcal{F}_{T})
and

,

(iv)

for any
(y,y^{\prime},z,z^{\prime})
and any

D_{r}f(t,\omega,y,z)-D_{r}f(t,\omega,y^{\prime},z^{\prime})|\leq\kappa_{r}(t,\omega)(|y-y^{\prime}|+|z-z^{\prime}|),

where
\{\kappa_{r}(t,\cdot),\;t\in[0,T]\}
is an
\mathbb{R}^{+}-
valued adapted process for almost every
r\in[0,t]
with
\kappa_{r}\in\mathcal{H}^{4}
such that
\int_{0}^{T}\|\kappa_{r}\|_{4}^{4}dr<\infty
.

Note that the properties
(iii)-(iv)
can be relaxed in order to get Proposition 5.3 of
[
El Karoui et al., 1997
]
, see for example
[
Mastrolia et al., 2017
]
.

3
Existence results

In this section we turn to existence of supersolution of BSDE (
2.3
) under
Z
-mean reflection (
2.4
) under the standing assumption
(\mathbf{A})
.

3.1
No restriction to deterministic
K
or penalization schemes

To illustrate that neither the approach (ii) nor the approach (iii) can be used for the existence of supersolution to
Z
mean reflected BSDE, we first consider a simple example. Let
f=0
,
\ell(t,.)=Id(\cdot)-\nu
,
\nu\in\mathbb{R}
,
\xi=\int_{0}^{T}\lambda(t)dB_{t}
for some deterministic function
\lambda
such that there exists
t_{0}\in[0,T]
with
\lambda(t_{0})<\nu
. If we assume that there exists a solution
(Y,Z,K)
to (
2.3
) with deterministic component
K
, then for any
t\in[0,T]
we have

\mathbb{E}[Z_{t}]=\mathbb{E}_{t}[D_{t}\xi]=\lambda(t)
. Hence, the constraint is not satisfied at time
t_{0}
. It is not enough to impose a consistency condition on
D_{T}\xi
only unlike the case of BSDE with mean constraint on the
Y
component, see
[
Briand et al., 2016
, Assumption
(H_{\xi})
]
. We need to assume at least
\lambda(t)\geq\nu
for any
t
.

As a consequence, we cannot expect a penalization scheme to converge to a supersolution. As a matter of fact, consider the following penalized BSDE

Y^{n}_{t}=\xi+\int_{t}^{T}n\mathbb{E}[Z^{n}_{s}]^{-}ds-\int_{t}^{T}Z^{n}_{s}dB_{s},\;n\in\mathbb{N}\,\;\xi\in{\cal D}^{1,2}.

This seems to penalize the fact that
\mathbb{E}[Z^{n}_{s}]<0
when
n
is large. However the penalization is deterministic so if every term converges this would yield a supersolution with deterministic
K
. Note that
Y^{n}_{t}=\mathbb{E}_{t}[\xi+\int_{t}^{T}n\mathbb{E}[Z^{n}_{s}]^{-}ds]=\mathbb{E}_{t}[\xi]+\phi^{n}(t)
for some deterministic function
\phi^{n}
. In particular
Y^{n}_{t}-\phi^{n}(t)=\mathbb{E}_{t}[\xi]=\xi-\int_{t}^{T}D_{s}\xi dB_{s}
so
Z^{n}_{s}=D_{s}\xi
and
Y^{n}_{t}=\mathbb{E}_{t}[\xi]+n\int_{t}^{T}\mathbb{E}[D_{s}\xi]^{-}ds
. Thus
Y^{n}_{0}
diverges if
\int_{0}^{T}\mathbb{E}[D_{s}\xi]^{-}ds>0
. Hence the penalization scheme diverges.

The following proposition provides non trivial examples for which we do not have solutions to BSDE (
2.3
) under the constraint (
2.4
) restricted to the class of deterministic
K
components.

Proposition 3.1
.

Let
\ell
in
C^{1,2}(\mathbb{R}^{+}\times\mathbb{R})
. Let
(Y,Z,K)
be a supersolution to (
1.1
). Assume that one of the two following condition is satisfied.

(a)

\ell(t,x)=x-\nu

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[\Gamma_{t}^{T}D_{t}\xi+\int_{t}^{T}\Gamma_{t}^{s}D_{t}f(s,Y_{s},Z_{s})ds]<\nu

for some
0\leq t\leq T
, with
\Gamma_{t}^{s}=e^{\int_{t}^{s}\partial_{Y}f(r,Y_{r},Z_{r})dr+\partial_{Z}f(r,Y_{r},Z_{r})dB_{r}-\frac{\partial_{Z}f(r,Y_{r},Z_{r})^{2}}{2}dr}
.

(b)

D_{t}\xi
and

are bounded,
\partial_{x}\ell\geq\text{\@text@baccent{$m$}}
and
c\geq-\frac{\partial_{xx}^{2}\ell}{(\partial_{x}\ell)^{2}}
with
\text{\@text@baccent{$m$}},c\in\mathbb{R}_{+}^{*}
. Assume moreover that either
d=0
or
\partial_{x}\ell
bounded and

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\big[\log(\mathbb{E}_{t}[e^{\sqrt{c}\ell(e^{\int_{t}^{T}\partial_{Y}f(s,Y_{s},Z_{s})ds}D_{t}\xi)}e^{\int_{t}^{T}\bar{m}d\sqrt{c}e^{\int_{t}^{s}\partial_{Y}f(u,Y_{u},Z_{u})du}ds+\int_{t}^{T}\partial_{Z}f(s,Y_{s},Z_{s})dB_{s}-\frac{1}{2}\int_{t}^{T}(\partial_{Z}f(s,Y_{s},Z_{s}))^{2}ds}])\big]<0

for some

.

Then

cannot be deterministic.

Proof.

Let
(Y,Z,K)
be such a supersolution with
K
deterministic. Under Assumption
(\mathbf{A})
, up to a change of variable for the
Y
component, and noting that
f(\cdot,K_{T}-K_{\cdot},0)\in\mathcal{H}^{4}
and

is Lipschitz, we deduce from Proposition 5.3 of
[
El Karoui et al., 1997
]
that

D_{r}Y_{t}=D_{r}\xi+\int_{t}^{T}(D_{r}f(s,Y_{s},Z_{s})+\partial_{Y}f(s,Y_{s},Z_{s})D_{r}Y_{s}+\partial_{Z}f(s,Y_{s},Z_{s})D_{r}Z_{s})ds-\int_{t}^{T}D_{r}Z_{s}dB_{s}

for
0\leq r\leq t\leq T
.

Case (a).
Note that

\mathbb{E}[Z_{t}]=\mathbb{E}[D_{t}Y_{t}]=\mathbb{E}[\Gamma_{t}^{T}D_{t}\xi+\int_{t}^{T}\Gamma_{t}^{s}D_{t}f(s,Y_{s},Z_{s})ds]<\nu,

which contradicts (
2.4
).

Case (b).

By using Itô’s Lemma we get for any
0\leq r\leq t\leq T

\displaystyle\ell(t,e^{\int_{r}^{t}\partial_{Y}f(s,Y_{s},Z_{s})ds}D_{r}Y_{t})

\displaystyle=\ell(T,e^{\int_{r}^{T}\partial_{Y}f(s,Y_{s},Z_{s})ds}D_{r}\xi)

\displaystyle+\int_{t}^{T}\big(\partial_{x}\ell(s,e^{\int_{r}^{s}\partial_{Y}f(u,Y_{u},Z_{u})du}D_{r}Y_{s})e^{\int_{r}^{s}\partial_{Y}f(u,Y_{u},Z_{u})du}(D_{r}f(s,Y_{s},Z_{s})+\partial_{Z}f(s,Y_{s},Z_{s})D_{r}Z_{s})

\displaystyle\hskip 30.00005pt-\partial_{t}\ell(s,e^{\int_{r}^{s}\partial_{Y}f(u,Y_{u},Z_{u})du}D_{r}Y_{s})-\frac{1}{2}\partial_{xx}^{2}\ell(s,e^{\int_{r}^{s}\partial_{Y}f(u,Y_{u},Z_{u})du}D_{r}Y_{s})(e^{\int_{r}^{s}\partial_{Y}f(u,Y_{u},Z_{u})du}D_{r}Z_{s})^{2}\big)ds

\displaystyle-\int_{t}^{T}\partial_{x}\ell(s,e^{\int_{r}^{s}\partial_{Y}f(u,Y_{u},Z_{u})du}D_{r}Y_{s})e^{\int_{r}^{s}\partial_{Y}f(u,Y_{u},Z_{u})du}D_{r}Z_{s}dB_{s}.

Hence,

\displaystyle\tilde{Y}_{t}^{r}

\displaystyle=\ell(T,e^{\int_{r}^{T}\partial_{Y}f(s,Y_{s},Z_{s})ds}D_{r}\xi)-\int_{t}^{T}\tilde{Z}_{s}^{r}dB_{s}

\displaystyle+\int_{t}^{T}\big(\partial_{x}\ell(s,e^{\int_{r}^{s}\partial_{Y}f(u,Y_{u},Z_{u})du}D_{r}Y_{s})e^{\int_{r}^{s}\partial_{Y}f(u,Y_{u},Z_{u})du}D_{r}f(s,Y_{s},Z_{s})-\partial_{t}\ell(s,e^{\int_{r}^{s}\partial_{Y}f(u,Y_{u},Z_{u})du}D_{r}Y_{s})

\displaystyle\hskip 25.00003pt+\partial_{Z}f(s,Y_{s},Z_{s})\tilde{Z}_{s}^{r}-\frac{1}{2}\frac{\partial_{xx}^{2}\ell(s,e^{\int_{r}^{s}\partial_{Y}f(u,Y_{u},Z_{u})du}D_{r}Y_{s})}{\partial_{x}\ell(s,e^{\int_{r}^{s}\partial_{Y}f(u,Y_{u},Z_{u})du}D_{r}Y_{s})^{2}}|\tilde{Z}_{s}^{r}|^{2}\big)ds.

the unique solution to the following BSDE

\displaystyle\overline{Y}_{t}^{r}

\displaystyle=\ell(T,e^{\int_{r}^{T}\partial_{Y}f(s,Y_{s},Z_{s})ds}D_{r}\xi)

\displaystyle+\int_{t}^{T}\big(\partial_{x}\ell(s,e^{\int_{r}^{s}\partial_{Y}f(u,Y_{u},Z_{u})du}D_{r}Y_{s})e^{\int_{r}^{s}\partial_{Y}f(u,Y_{u},Z_{u})du}D_{r}f(s,Y_{s},Z_{s})-\partial_{t}\ell(s,e^{\int_{r}^{s}\partial_{Y}f(u,Y_{u},Z_{u})du}D_{r}Y_{s})

\displaystyle\overline{Y}_{t}^{r}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{c}}\log(\mathbb{E}_{t}[

Kobylanski, 2000

for any

. Taking

and recalling that

, we get

\displaystyle\ell(t,Z_{t})\leq\frac{1}{\sqrt{c}}\log(\mathbb{E}_{t}[

\displaystyle e^{\sqrt{c}\ell(T,e^{\int_{t}^{T}\partial_{Y}f(s,Y_{s},Z_{s})ds}D_{t}\xi)}e^{\int_{t}^{T}\partial_{Z}f(s,Y_{s},Z_{s})dB_{s}-\frac{1}{2}\int_{t}^{T}(\partial_{Z}f(s,Y_{s},Z_{s}))^{2}ds}])

\displaystyle e^{\sqrt{c}\int_{t}^{T}\big(\partial_{x}\ell(s,e^{\int_{t}^{s}\partial_{Y}f(u,Y_{u},Z_{u})du}D_{t}Y_{s})e^{\int_{t}^{s}\partial_{Y}f(u,Y_{u},Z_{u})du}D_{t}f(s,Y_{s},Z_{s})-\partial_{t}\ell(s,e^{\int_{r}^{s}\partial_{Y}f(u,Y_{u},Z_{u})du}D_{r}Y_{s})\big)ds}.

We finally deduce from our assumptions that

for some

. Hence, there is no solution with deterministic

2.3
) satisfying the constraint (
2.4
).

3.2
Non-trivial supersolutions

3.2.1
Weak
v.s.

Note that in
[
Peng and Xu, 2010
]
or
[
Peng and Xu, 2013
]
, the authors investigate the existence of a solution which satisfies the constraint in
Z_{s}\in\Gamma_{s}
, where for all

is some convex subset of

, but Example 3.1 in
[

assumes

[
Cvitanić et al., 1997
]
, the authors also assume
0\in\Gamma_{s}
and give other conditions ensuring existence in Section 7, relying either on a boundedness assumption or a convexity assumption on the generator. Again in
[
Buckdahn and Hu, 1998
]
the same assumption on
\Gamma_{s}
is made, in the Markovian case, with also some boundedness assumptions. It is actually very simple to build examples where there is no supersolution which can satisfy an almost sure constraint, if
0\not\in\Gamma_{s}
.

Suppose
\xi
and
f
be lower bounded by reals
\inf\xi
and
\inf f
respectively. Using the arguments of
[
Bouchard et al., 2018a
]
, we see that any supersolution satisfying
Z_{t}\geq 1
for all
t\in[0,T]
is lower bounded for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
by
Y^{n}
where
(Y^{n},Z^{n})\in{\cal S}^{2}\times{\cal H}^{2}
is the solution to the BSDE

\displaystyle Y^{n}_{t}=\xi+\int_{t}^{T}(f(s,Y^{n}_{s},Z^{n}_{s})+n(1-Z^{n}_{s})^{+})ds-\int_{t}^{T}Z_{s}dB_{s}.

Let
(\tilde{Y}^{n},\tilde{Z}^{n})\in{\cal S}^{2}\times{\cal H}^{2}
be the solution to the BSDE

\displaystyle\tilde{Y}^{n}_{t}=\inf\xi+\int_{t}^{T}(\inf f+n(1-\tilde{Z}^{n}_{s}))ds-\int_{t}^{T}\tilde{Z}_{s}dB_{s}.

From comparison Theorem, we see that
Y_{0}^{n}\geq\tilde{Y}^{n}_{0}\geq\inf\xi+(n+\inf f)T\underset{n\rightarrow+\infty}{\longrightarrow}+\infty
so this contradicts the existence of a supersolution satisfying the constraint.

In
[
Bouchard et al., 2018a
]
, the existence of a minimal supersolution is shown to be a consequence of the finiteness of the value of a particular singular control problem, for constraints of the form
q(t,Z_{t})\leq 0
with
q
a supremum over linear functions. All those results except
[
Cvitanić et al., 1997
]
rely on a penalization argument and the classical convergence result of
[
Peng, 1999
]
.

However, taking for example
f=0
, we note that

Z_{t}=D_{t}Y_{t}=D_{t}\xi-\int_{t}^{T}D_{t}Z_{s}dW_{s}.

Hence, if
\mathbb{E}[D_{t}\xi]\geq 1
for any
t\in[0,T]
, so there exists a solution to BSDE (
2.3
) which satisfies
\mathbb{E}[Z_{t}]\geq 1
. As a consequence, we expect that we can obtain existence results of supersolutions to (
2.3
) by relaxing the constraint on the
Z
component.

3.2.2
Existence results

Before turning to the main results of this work, we show two technical lemmas.

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
\mathcal{Y},\mathcal{Z}\in\mathcal{S}^{2}\times\mathcal{H}^{2}
and
f:[0,T]\times\Omega\times\mathbb{R}^{2}\longrightarrow\mathbb{R}
such that the derivative of
f
with respect to the
z
-component is uniformly bounded i.e.
\|\partial_{Z}f\|_{\infty}<\infty
. Let

\displaystyle\tilde{\Gamma}_{t}^{T}:=e^{\int_{t}^{T}\partial_{Z}f(s,\mathcal{Y}_{s},\mathcal{Z}_{s})dB_{s}-\frac{\partial_{Z}f(s,\mathcal{Y}_{s},\mathcal{Z}_{s})^{2}}{2}ds},

for any
t\in[0,T]
. Then

\begin{cases}\mathbb{E}[|\tilde{\Gamma}_{t}^{T}|^{p}]^{1/p}\geq e^{(-1+p)\frac{\|\partial_{Z}f\|^{2}}{2}(T-t)},&\text{for any }p<0,\\
\mathbb{E}[|\tilde{\Gamma}_{t}^{T}|^{p}]^{1/p}\leq e^{(-1+p)\frac{\||\partial_{Z}f\|^{2}}{2}(T-t)}&\text{for any }p>1.\end{cases}

Proof.

Let
q>1
. Then by applying Hölder’s Inequality,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[|\tilde{\Gamma}_{t}^{T}|^{p}]=\mathbb{E}[e^{\int_{t}^{T}p\partial_{Z}f(s,\mathcal{Y}_{s},\mathcal{Z}_{s})dB_{s}-p\frac{\partial_{Z}f(s,\mathcal{Y}_{s},\mathcal{Z}_{s})^{2}}{2}ds}]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}[e^{\int_{t}^{T}p\partial_{Z}f(s,\mathcal{Y}_{s},\mathcal{Z}_{s})dB_{s}-\frac{qp^{2}}{2}\frac{\partial_{Z}f(s,\mathcal{Y}_{s},\mathcal{Z}_{s})^{2}}{2}ds+p(-1+qp)\frac{\partial_{Z}f(s,\mathcal{Y}_{s},\mathcal{Z}_{s})^{2}}{2}ds}]

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{E}[e^{\frac{pq(-1+qp)}{(q-1)}\int_{t}^{T}\frac{\partial_{Z}f(s,y_{s},z_{s})^{2}}{2}ds}]^{\frac{q-1}{q}}.

Choosing
q
such that
\frac{pq(-1+qp)}{(q-1)}>0
whether
p<0
or
p>1
, we have

\mathbb{E}[|\tilde{\Gamma}_{t}^{T}|^{p}]\leq e^{p(-1+qp)\frac{\|\partial_{Z}f\|^{2}}{2}(T-t)}.

We obtain the result by putting this to the power
\frac{1}{p}
and taking
q\longrightarrow 1
.
∎

Remember that the process
K
appearing in the BSDE must be non-decreasing and non-negative. We have seen that we cannot restrict the study to deterministic
K
as in
[
Briand et al., 2016
]
. We thus focus on some particular shapes of
K
with stochastic exponential similarly to
[
Briand et al., 2016
, Remark 6]
.

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
\alpha:[0,T]\mapsto\mathbb{R}
be a deterministic càdlàg function with
\int_{0}^{T}\alpha_{t}^{2}dt<\infty
and
K_{t}=\int_{0}^{t}e^{\int_{0}^{s}\{\alpha_{r}dB_{r}-\frac{1}{2}\alpha_{r}^{2}dr\}}ds
for
0\leq t\leq T
. Then,
K\in{\cal A}^{2}
and
K_{t}\in{\cal D}^{1,2}
for all
0\leq t\leq T
.

Proof.

The process
K
is well defined as
\mathbb{E}[(e^{\int_{0}^{s}\alpha_{r}dB_{r}-\frac{1}{2}\alpha_{r}^{2}dr})^{2}]=e^{\int_{0}^{s}\alpha_{r}^{2}dr}\leq e^{\int_{0}^{T}\alpha_{r}^{2}dr}
for any
s\leq T
. Clearly
K_{0}=0
and
K
is non-decreasing. Also

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[(\sup_{t\in[0,T]}K_{t})^{2}]=\mathbb{E}[K_{T}^{2}]\leq\mathbb{E}[(T\sup_{s\in[0,T]}e^{\int_{0}^{s}\alpha_{r}dB_{r}-\frac{1}{2}\alpha_{r}^{2}dr})^{2}]\leq 4T^{2}\mathbb{E}[(e^{\int_{0}^{T}\alpha_{r}dB_{r}-\frac{1}{2}\alpha_{r}^{2}dr})^{2}]<\infty,

where we have used successively the fact that
K
is non-decreasing, that
K
is pathwise differentiable, and the Doob inequality. Consequently,
K\in{\cal S}^{2}
. Moreover, we note that
D_{u}K_{t}=\mathbf{1}_{u\leq t}\alpha_{u}K_{t}
for all
0\leq u,t\leq T
so
\mathbb{E}[\int_{0}^{T}|D_{u}K_{t}|^{2}du]=\mathbb{E}[K_{t}^{2}]\int_{0}^{t}\alpha_{t}^{2}dt<\infty
so
K_{t}\in{\cal D}^{1,2}
.
∎

These lemmas enable us to construct supersolutions to (
2.3
) with linear constraints and under conditions on the Malliavin derivative of
\xi
.

Proposition 3.4
.

We assume that
\ell(t,.)=Id(\cdot)-\nu(t)
for some real function
\nu
on
[0,T]
with
\nu(T)\leq\mathbb{E}[D_{T}\xi]
. In addition, assume that one of the following condition is satisfied,

1.

\partial_{Z}f=0
, and

\displaystyle t\in[0,T)\mapsto\frac{1}{T-t}\big[\nu(t)-\big(-e^{(T-t)\sup\partial_{Y}f}\mathbb{E}[D_{t}\xi^{-}]+e^{(T-t)\inf\partial_{Y}f}\mathbb{E}[D_{t}\xi^{+}]\big)\big]

is upper bounded;

2.

there exist two map
p_{+},p_{-}
from
[0,T)
into
(1,+\infty)
such that

\displaystyle t\in[0,T)\mapsto

\displaystyle\frac{1}{T-t}\big[\nu(t)-(-e^{(T-t)\sup\partial_{Y}f}e^{\frac{1}{p_{-}(t)-1}\frac{\sup|\partial_{Z}f|^{2}}{2}(T-t)}\mathbb{E}[|D_{t}\xi^{-}|^{p_{-}(t)}]^{\frac{1}{p_{-}(t)}}

\displaystyle+e^{(T-t)\inf\partial_{Y}f}e^{\frac{p_{+}(t)}{1-p_{+}(t)}\frac{\sup|\partial_{Z}f|^{2}}{2}(T-t)}\mathbb{E}[|D_{t}\xi^{+}|^{\frac{1}{p_{+}(t)}}]^{p_{+}(t)})\big]

is upper bounded.

Then there exists a supersolution to BSDE (
2.3
) under the
Z-
mean constraint (
2.4
).

Proof.

Let
\alpha:[0,T]\longrightarrow\mathbb{R}
some deterministic càdlàg function,
k_{s}=e^{\int_{0}^{s}\alpha(u)dB_{u}-\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{s}\alpha(u)^{2}du}
,
dK_{s}=k_{s}ds
. We have
K\in{\cal A}^{2}
. Under Assumption
(\mathbf{A})
and noticing that
f(t,0,0)+k_{t}\in{\cal H}^{4}
, for example by using Minkowski inequality, we get

D_{r}Y_{t}=D_{r}\xi+\int_{t}^{T}(D_{r}f(s,Y_{s},Z_{s})+\partial_{Y}f(s,Y_{s},Z_{s})D_{r}Y_{s}+\partial_{Z}f(s,Y_{s},Z_{s})D_{r}Z_{s})ds-\int_{t}^{T}D_{r}Z_{s}dB_{s}+\int_{t}^{T}D_{r}k_{s}ds

for
0\leq r\leq t\leq T
, so

\mathbb{E}[Z_{t}]=\mathbb{E}[D_{t}Y_{t}]=\mathbb{E}[\Gamma_{t}^{T}D_{t}\xi+\int_{t}^{T}\Gamma_{t}^{s}D_{t}f(s,Y_{s},Z_{s})ds+\int_{t}^{T}\Gamma_{t}^{s}D_{t}k_{s}ds]

with
\Gamma_{t}^{s}=e^{\int_{t}^{s}\partial_{Y}f(r,Y_{r},Z_{r})dr+\partial_{Z}f(r,Y_{r},Z_{r})dB_{r}-\frac{\partial_{Z}f(r,Y_{r},Z_{r})^{2}}{2}dr}
.

Case 1. No dependence with respect to
Z
in the driver.

We have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[Z_{t}]\geq

\displaystyle-e^{(T-t)\sup\partial_{Y}f}\mathbb{E}[D_{t}\xi^{-}]+e^{(T-t)\inf\partial_{Y}f}\mathbb{E}[D_{t}\xi^{+}]-de^{(T-t)\sup|\partial_{Y}f|}(T-t)

\displaystyle+\int_{t}^{T}[\alpha(t)e^{(s-t)\inf\partial_{Y}f}\mathbf{1}_{\alpha(t)\geq 0}+\alpha(t)e^{(s-t)\sup\partial_{Y}f}\mathbf{1}_{\alpha(t)<0}]ds

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle-e^{(T-t)\sup\partial_{Y}f}\mathbb{E}[D_{t}\xi^{-}]+e^{(T-t)\inf\partial_{Y}f}\mathbb{E}[D_{t}\xi^{+}]-de^{(T-t)\sup|\partial_{Y}f|}(T-t)

\displaystyle+\alpha(t)^{+}i(t)-\alpha(t)^{-}s(t)

where, for
0\leq t\leq T
,

\displaystyle s(t)=\begin{cases}\frac{e^{(T-t)\sup\partial_{Y}f}-1}{\sup\partial_{Y}f}&\text{ if }\sup\partial_{Y}f\neq 0\\
T-t&\text{ otherwise}\end{cases}

and

\displaystyle i(t)=\begin{cases}\frac{e^{(T-t)\inf\partial_{Y}f}-1}{\inf\partial_{Y}f}&\text{ if }\inf\partial_{Y}f\neq 0\\
T-t&\text{ otherwise}.\end{cases}

Since
\mathbb{E}[Z_{T}]=\mathbb{E}[D_{T}\xi]\geq\nu(T)
, it remains to choose
\alpha
wisely so that the constraint is satisfied on
[0,T)
.
We thus choose

\displaystyle\alpha(t)=\big(\sup_{0\leq t<T}\frac{1}{i(t)}\big[\nu(t)-(-e^{(T-t)\sup\partial_{Y}f}\mathbb{E}[D_{t}\xi^{-}]+e^{(T-t)\inf\partial_{Y}f}\mathbb{E}[D_{t}\xi^{+}]-de^{(T-t)\sup|\partial_{Y}f|}(T-t))\big]\big)^{+}

for all
0\leq t\leq T
.
Given the assumption and the definition of
i
we have
0\leq\alpha<\infty
, and, by construction,
\mathbb{E}[Z_{t}]\geq\nu(t)
.

Case 2. Z dependence in the driver.

Let now
p>1
and define
k^{p}_{s}=e^{\int_{0}^{s}\alpha(u)dB_{u}-\frac{1}{2p}\int_{0}^{s}\alpha(u)^{2}du}
,
dK_{s}=k^{p}_{s}ds
with
\alpha
being nonnegative. We have
K\in{\cal A}^{2}
. Following the lines of the proof of Case 1., we similarly get

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[Z_{t}]\geq

\displaystyle-e^{(T-t)\sup\partial_{Y}f}\mathbb{E}[\tilde{\Gamma}_{t}^{T}D_{t}\xi^{-}]+e^{(T-t)\inf\partial_{Y}f}\mathbb{E}[\tilde{\Gamma}_{t}^{T}D_{t}\xi^{+}]-de^{(T-t)\sup|\partial_{Y}f|}(T-t)

\displaystyle+\int_{t}^{T}\mathbb{E}[\tilde{\Gamma}_{t}^{s}D_{t}k^{p}_{s}]e^{(s-t)\inf\partial_{Y}f}ds

with
\tilde{\Gamma}_{t}^{s}=e^{\int_{t}^{s}\partial_{Z}f(r,Y_{r},Z_{r})dB_{r}-\frac{\partial_{Z}f(r,Y_{r},Z_{r})^{2}}{2}dr}
.

Applying Hölder’s inequality for some
q>1
, we get

\mathbb{E}[\tilde{\Gamma}_{t}^{T}D_{t}\xi^{-}]\leq\mathbb{E}[|\tilde{\Gamma}_{t}^{T}|^{\frac{q}{q-1}}]^{\frac{1-q}{q}}\mathbb{E}[|D_{t}\xi^{-}|^{q}]^{\frac{1}{q}}\leq e^{\frac{1}{q-1}\frac{\sup|\partial_{Z}f|^{2}}{2}(T-t)}\mathbb{E}[|D_{t}\xi^{-}|^{q}]^{\frac{1}{q}}.

Now, from Reverse Hölder inequality together with Lemma
3.2
we get for any
q>1

\mathbb{E}[\tilde{\Gamma}_{t}^{T}D_{t}\xi^{+}]\geq\mathbb{E}[|\tilde{\Gamma}_{t}^{T}|^{\frac{1}{1-q}}]^{1-q}\mathbb{E}[|D_{t}\xi^{+}|^{\frac{1}{q}}]^{q}\geq e^{\frac{q}{1-q}\frac{\sup|\partial_{Z}f|^{2}}{2}(T-t)}\mathbb{E}[|D_{t}\xi^{+}|^{\frac{1}{q}}]^{q},

and

\mathbb{E}[\tilde{\Gamma}_{t}^{s}D_{t}k^{p}_{s}]\geq\mathbb{E}[|\tilde{\Gamma}_{t}^{s}|^{\frac{1}{1-p}}]^{1-p}\mathbb{E}[|D_{t}k^{p}_{s}|^{\frac{1}{p}}]^{p}\geq e^{\frac{p}{1-p}\frac{\sup|\partial_{Z}f|^{2}}{2}(s-t)}\alpha(t).

Consequently, for any
p_{+},p_{-}:[0,T]\mapsto(1,+\infty)
,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[Z_{t}]\geq

\displaystyle-e^{(T-t)\sup\partial_{Y}f}\mathbb{E}[\tilde{\Gamma}_{t}^{T}D_{t}\xi^{-}]+e^{(T-t)\inf\partial_{Y}f}\mathbb{E}[\tilde{\Gamma}_{t}^{T}D_{t}\xi^{+}]-de^{(T-t)\sup|\partial_{Y}f|}(T-t)

\displaystyle+\int_{t}^{T}e^{\frac{p}{1-p}\frac{\sup|\partial_{Z}f|^{2}}{2}(s-t)}\alpha(t)e^{(s-t)\inf\partial_{Y}f}ds

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle-e^{(T-t)\sup\partial_{Y}f}e^{\frac{1}{p_{-}(t)-1}\frac{\sup|\partial_{Z}f|^{2}}{2}(T-t)}\mathbb{E}[|D_{t}\xi^{-}|^{p_{-}(t)}]^{\frac{1}{p_{-}(t)}}

\displaystyle+e^{(T-t)\inf\partial_{Y}f}e^{\frac{p_{+}(t)}{1-p_{+}(t)}\frac{\sup|\partial_{Z}f|^{2}}{2}(T-t)}\mathbb{E}[|D_{t}\xi^{+}|^{\frac{1}{p_{+}(t)}}]^{p_{+}(t)}

\displaystyle-de^{(T-t)\sup|\partial_{Y}f|}(T-t)+\alpha(t)i(t)

with

\displaystyle i(t)=\begin{cases}\frac{e^{(\frac{p}{1-p}\frac{\sup|\partial_{Z}f|^{2}}{2}+\inf\partial_{Y}f)(T-t)}-1}{\frac{p}{1-p}\frac{\sup|\partial_{Z}f|^{2}}{2}+\inf\partial_{Y}f}&\text{ if }(\frac{p}{1-p}\frac{\sup|\partial_{Z}f|^{2}}{2}+\inf\partial_{Y}f)\neq 0\\
T-t&\text{ otherwise}.\end{cases}

so the same argument applies, with

\displaystyle\alpha(t)=\sup_{0\leq t<T}\frac{1}{i(t)}\big[\nu(t)-(

\displaystyle-e^{(T-t)\sup\partial_{Y}f}e^{\frac{1}{p_{-}(t)-1}\frac{\sup|\partial_{Z}f|^{2}}{2}(T-t)}\mathbb{E}[|D_{t}\xi^{-}|^{p_{-}(t)}]^{\frac{1}{p_{-}(t)}}

\displaystyle+e^{(T-t)\inf\partial_{Y}f}e^{\frac{p_{+}(t)}{1-p_{+}(t)}\frac{\sup|\partial_{Z}f|^{2}}{2}(T-t)}\mathbb{E}[|D_{t}\xi^{+}|^{\frac{1}{p_{+}(t)}}]^{p_{+}(t)}-de^{(T-t)\sup|\partial_{Y}f|}(T-t))\big]

for all
0\leq t\leq T
.
∎

Remark 3.5
.

For a constraint of the type
\mathbb{E}[Z_{t}]\leq\nu(t)
the assumptions in Proposition
3.4
become
\nu(T)\geq\mathbb{E}[D_{T}\xi]
and

1.

if

,

\displaystyle t\in[0,T)\mapsto\frac{1}{T-t}\big[\nu(t)-(-e^{(T-t)\inf\partial_{Y}f}\mathbb{E}[D_{t}\xi^{-}]+e^{(T-t)\sup\partial_{Y}f}\mathbb{E}[D_{t}\xi^{+}])\big]

2.

otherwise

\displaystyle+e^{(T-t)\sup\partial_{Y}f}e^{\frac{p_{+}(t)}{1-p_{+}(t)}\frac{\sup|\partial_{Z}f|^{2}}{2}(T-t)}\mathbb{E}[|D_{t}\xi^{+}|^{\frac{1}{p_{+}(t)}}]^{p_{+}(t)})\big]

We now turn to more general constraints. We can derive from the previous proposition sufficient conditions in the case of concave or convex constraints.

Theorem 1
.

Let
\ell
in
C^{1,2}(\mathbb{R}^{+}\times\mathbb{R})
, with
\partial_{x}\ell\geq K\in\mathbb{R}^{*}_{+}
and
-\text{\@text@baccent{$C$}}\leq\frac{\partial_{xx}^{2}\ell}{(\partial_{x}\ell)^{2}}\leq C

|\partial_{t}\ell|\leq\bar{M}\in\mathbb{R}_{+}
and either

or
\partial_{x}\ell\leq\bar{K}\in\mathbb{R}
, and
D_{t}\xi
is bounded. Let one of the two following condition be satisfied.

•

\ell
is convex and the conditions of Proposition
3.4
associated with the constraint
\mathbb{E}[Z_{t}]\geq\ell(t,.)^{-1}(0)
are satisfied;

•

\ell
is concave with

0\leq\mathbb{E}[\ell(T,D_{T}\xi)]
and either

1.

\partial_{Z}f=0
, and

\displaystyle t\in[0,T)\mapsto\frac{1}{T-t}\big[-\mathbb{E}[\ell(T,-e^{(T-t)\sup\partial_{Y}f}D_{t}\xi^{-}+e^{(T-t)\inf\partial_{Y}f}D_{t}\xi^{+})]\big]

is upper bounded,

or

2.

there exist two maps
p_{+},p_{-}
from
[0,T)
into
(1,+\infty)
such that

\displaystyle t\in[0,T)\mapsto

\displaystyle\frac{1}{T-t}\big[-(-e^{\frac{1}{p_{-}(t)-1}\frac{\sup|\partial_{Z}f|^{2}}{2}(T-t)}\mathbb{E}[|\chi_{t}^{-}|^{p_{-}(t)}]^{\frac{1}{p_{-}(t)}}

\displaystyle+e^{\frac{p_{+}(t)}{1-p_{+}(t)}\frac{\sup|\partial_{Z}f|^{2}}{2}(T-t)}\mathbb{E}[|\chi_{t}^{+}|^{\frac{1}{p_{+}(t)}}]^{p_{+}(t)})\big]

is upper bounded, with
\chi_{t}=\ell(T,-e^{(T-t)\sup\partial_{Y}f}D_{t}\xi^{-}+e^{(T-t)\inf\partial_{Y}f}D_{t}\xi^{+})
.

Proof.

If
\text{\@text@baccent{$C$}}\leq 0
,
i.e.
if
\ell(t,.)
is convex, then
\mathbb{E}[\ell(t,Z_{t})]\geq\ell(t,\mathbb{E}[Z_{t}])
. As
\ell(t,.)
is strictly increasing and continuous the constraint
\mathbb{E}[\ell(t,Z_{t})]\geq 0
is satisfied if
\mathbb{E}[Z_{t}]\geq\ell(t,.)^{-1}(0)
. This can be done under the assumptions of Proposition
3.4
.

In the following we assume
\text{\@text@baccent{$C$}}\geq 0
. Let
\alpha:[0,T]\longrightarrow\mathbb{R}
some non-negative deterministic càdlàg function,
k_{s}=e^{\int_{0}^{s}\alpha(u)dB_{u}-\int_{0}^{s}\frac{\alpha(u)^{2}}{2}du}
,
dK_{s}=k_{s}ds
. We have
K\in{\cal A}^{2}
. Under Assumption
(\mathbf{A})
and noticing that
f(t,\omega,0,0)+k_{t}\in{\cal H}^{4}
we get

D_{r}Y_{t}=D_{r}\xi+\int_{t}^{T}(D_{r}f(s,Y_{s},Z_{s})+\partial_{Y}f(s,Y_{s},Z_{s})D_{r}Y_{s}+\partial_{Z}f(s,Y_{s},Z_{s})D_{r}Z_{s})ds-\int_{t}^{T}D_{r}Z_{s}dB_{s}+\int_{t}^{T}D_{r}k_{s}ds

for
0\leq r\leq t\leq T
, so, using Itô’s Lemma we get

\displaystyle\ell(t,e^{\int_{r}^{t}\partial_{Y}f(s,Y_{s},Z_{s})ds}D_{r}Y_{t})=\ell(T,e^{\int_{r}^{T}\partial_{Y}f(s,Y_{s},Z_{s})ds}D_{r}\xi)

\displaystyle+\int_{t}^{T}\big(\partial_{x}\ell(s,e^{\int_{r}^{s}\partial_{Y}f(u,Y_{u},Z_{u})du}D_{r}Y_{s})e^{\int_{r}^{s}\partial_{Y}f(u,Y_{u},Z_{u})du}(D_{r}f(s,Y_{s},Z_{s})+\partial_{Z}f(s,Y_{s},Z_{s})D_{r}Z_{s})

\displaystyle-\partial_{x}\ell(s,e^{\int_{r}^{s}\partial_{Y}f(u,Y_{u},Z_{u})du}D_{r}Y_{s})-\frac{1}{2}\partial_{xx}^{2}\ell(s,e^{\int_{r}^{s}\partial_{Y}f(u,Y_{u},Z_{u})du}D_{r}Y_{s})(e^{\int_{r}^{s}\partial_{Y}f(u,Y_{u},Z_{u})du}D_{r}Z_{s})^{2}\big)ds

\displaystyle-\int_{t}^{T}\partial_{x}\ell(s,e^{\int_{r}^{s}\partial_{Y}f(u,Y_{u},Z_{u})du}D_{r}Y_{s})e^{\int_{r}^{s}\partial_{Y}f(u,Y_{u},Z_{u})du}D_{r}Z_{s}dB_{s}

\displaystyle+\int_{r}^{T}\partial_{x}\ell(s,e^{\int_{r}^{s}\partial_{Y}f(u,Y_{u},Z_{u})du}D_{r}Y_{s})e^{\int_{r}^{s}\partial_{Y}f(u,Y_{u},Z_{u})du}D_{r}k_{s}ds.

As
\partial_{x}\ell>0
we let
\tilde{Z}^{r}_{s}=\partial_{x}\ell(s,e^{\int_{r}^{s}\partial_{Y}f(u,Y_{u},Z_{u})du}D_{r}Y_{s})e^{\int_{r}^{s}\partial_{Y}f(u,Y_{u},Z_{u})du}D_{r}Z_{s}
and
\tilde{Y}^{r}_{s}=\ell(s,e^{\int_{r}^{s}\partial_{Y}f(u,Y_{u},Z_{u})du}D_{r}Y_{s})
for
r\leq s\leq T
, so we can write

\displaystyle\tilde{Y}_{t}^{r}=\ell(T,e^{\int_{r}^{T}\partial_{Y}f(s,Y_{s},Z_{s})ds}D_{r}\xi)

3.5.2
Application: replication under
\beta
law constraints

Consider a stock market endowed with a bond with a deterministic interest rate
r
and a stock with price dynamics given by

\displaystyle dS_{t}=S_{t}(\mu_{t}dt+\sigma_{t}dB_{t}),0\leq t\leq T,

where the drift
\mu
and the volatility
\sigma
are two square integrable predictable processes, such that
\sigma
is lower bounded by some positive constant. Given an initial wealth
x
and a square integrable claim
\xi
at time
T
, a trader is looking for a hedging price. His wealth process is driven by a consumption-investment strategy
(\pi,K)
with dynamics

\displaystyle dX_{t}^{x,\pi,K}=r_{t}X_{t}^{x,\pi,K}dt+(\mu_{t}-r_{t})\pi_{t}dt+\pi_{t}\sigma_{t}dB_{t}-dK_{t}.

The admissible wealth processes satisfy the constraint

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[\pi_{t}]\geq\beta_{t}

for some deterministic lower bound
\beta
which penalizes portfolios which are too often contrarian but also allows for very contrarian positions in rare circumstances. The results of the paper show that there are admissible strategies, but there may not exist one with a deterministic investment process
K
. In addition the only strategy that might be optimal (in the sense that it is the preferred strategy at all time) if it is admissible is the one with no investment.

Ill/well-posedness of non-diffusive active scalar equations with physical applications
Susan Friedlander, Anthony Suen, Fei Wang

Abstract
. We consider a general class of non-diffusive active scalar equations with constitutive laws obtained via an operator 𝐓 \mathbf{T} that is singular of order r 0 ∈ [ 0 , 2 ] r_{0}\in[0,2] . For r 0 ∈ ( 0 , 1 ] r_{0}\in(0,1] we prove well-posedness in Gevrey spaces G s G^{s} with s ∈ [ 1 , 1 r 0 ) s\in[1,\frac{1}{r_{0}}) , while for r 0 ∈ [ 1 , 2 ] r_{0}\in[1,2] and further conditions on 𝐓 \mathbf{T} we prove ill-posedness in G s G^{s} for suitable s s . We then apply the ill/well-posedness results to several specific non-diffusive active scalar equations including the magnetogeostrophic equation, the incompressible porous media equation and the singular incompressible porous media equation.

1.
Introduction

Active scalar equations belong to a class of partial differential equations where the evolution in time of a scalar quantity is governed by the motion of the fluid in which the velocity itself varies with this scalar quantity. This kind of differential equations comes from numerous physical models such as the Navier-Stokes, Euler, or magneto-hydrodynamic (MHD) equations, all of them have great importance in practical applications ranging from fluid mechanics to atmospheric science, oceanography and geophysics; see for example
[
CCCGW12
,
CFG11
,
CGO07
,
FGSV12
,
FRV12
,
FRV14
,
FS15
,
FS18
,
FS19
,
FS21
,
FV11a
,
FV11b
,
KV09
,
KVW16
]
and the references therein. They have been a topic of considerable study in recent years, in part because they arise in many physical models and in part because they present challenging nonlinear PDEs. The physics of an active scalar equation is encoded in the constitutive law that relates the transport velocity vector
u
with a scalar field
\theta
. This law produces a differential operator that when applied to the scalar field determines the velocity. The singular or smoothing properties of this operator are closely connected with the mathematics of the nonlinear advection equation for
\theta
. From the mathematical point of view, these active scalar equations preserve some essential difficulties of the full fluid equations, which give rise to interesting and challenging problems. In this present paper we study issues of ill/well-posedness in Gevrey spaces when the nonlinear term is singular and there is no compensating effect of diffusion. More precisely, we consider a general class of non-diffusive active scalar equations with constitutive laws obtained via an operator
\mathbf{T}
that is singular of order
r_{0}\in[0,2]
. For
r_{0}\in(0,1]
we prove well-posedness in Gevrey spaces
G^{s}
with
s\in[1,\frac{1}{r_{0}})
, while for
r_{0}\in[1,2]
and further conditions on
\mathbf{T}
we prove ill-posedness in
G^{s}
for some suitable values of
s
. Our main results are given in Theorem
3.1
and Theorem
4.2
.

We address the following abstract class of active equations in
\mathbb{T}^{d}\times[0,\infty)=[0,2\pi]^{d}\times(0,\infty)
, where
d\geq 2
is the spatial dimension:

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\partial_{t}\theta+u\cdot\nabla\theta=0,\\
\nabla\cdot u=0,\,\,\,u=\mathbf{T}\theta,\,\,\,\theta(\textbf{{x}},0)=\theta_{0}(\textbf{{x}}).\end{array}\right.

The
d
-dimensional singular vector field
u
is obtained from the unknown scalar field
\theta
via the Fourier multiplier operator
\mathbf{T}
, which is given explicitly in term of the Fourier symbol

\widehat{\mathbf{T}}=(\widehat{\mathbf{T}}_{1},\dots,\widehat{\mathbf{T}}_{d-1},\widehat{\mathbf{T}}_{d})

with
\widehat{\mathbf{T}}:\mathbb{Z}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
. We also write
\textbf{{k}}^{\prime}
to denote the
d-1
dimensional vector
(k_{1},...,k_{d-1})\in\mathbb{Z}^{d-1}
and we assume the following condition hold:

|\widehat{\mathbf{T}}(\textbf{{k}})|\leq C|\textbf{{k}}|^{r_{0}}
for all
\textbf{{k}}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}
,

(1.3)

where
r_{0}\in[0,2]
and
C>0
. Without loss of generality we assume that
\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\theta(\textbf{{x}},t)d\textbf{{x}}=0
for all
t\geq 0
, since the mean of
\theta
is conserved by the flow.

Our motivation for addressing such a class of active scalar equations mainly comes from two different physical systems. The first example comes from MHD and a physical model proposed by Moffatt and Loper
[
ML94
,
Mof08
]
for magenetostrophic turbulence in the Earth’s fluid core. Under the postulates in
[
ML94
]
, the governing equation reduces to a three dimensional active scalar equation in
\mathbb{T}^{3}\times(0,\infty)
for a temperature field
\theta
, which is given by

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\partial_{t}\theta+u\cdot\nabla\theta=0\\
u=\mathbf{M}\theta,\,\,\,\theta(\textbf{{x}},0)=\theta_{0}(\textbf{{x}}).\end{array}\right.

Here
\mathbf{M}=(\mathbf{M}_{1},\mathbf{M}_{2},\mathbf{M}_{3})
is a Fourier multiplier operator with symbols
\mathbf{M}_{1}
,
\mathbf{M}_{2}
,
\mathbf{M}_{3}
given explicitly by

\displaystyle\widehat{\mathbf{M}}_{1}(\textbf{{k}})

\displaystyle=\frac{2\Omega k_{2}k_{3}|\textbf{{k}}|^{2}-(\beta^{2}/\eta)k_{1}k_{2}^{2}k_{3}}{4\Omega^{2}k_{3}^{2}|\textbf{{k}}|^{2}+(\beta^{2}/\eta)^{2}k_{2}^{4}},

(1.6)

\displaystyle\widehat{\mathbf{M}}_{2}(\textbf{{k}})

\displaystyle=\frac{-2\Omega k_{1}k_{3}|\textbf{{k}}|^{2}-(\beta^{2}/\eta)k_{2}^{3}k_{3}}{4\Omega^{2}k_{3}^{2}|\textbf{{k}}|^{2}+(\beta^{2}/\eta)^{2}k_{2}^{4}},

(1.7)

\displaystyle\widehat{\mathbf{M}}_{3}(\textbf{{k}})

\displaystyle=\frac{(\beta^{2}/\eta)k_{2}^{2}(k_{1}^{2}+k_{2}^{2})}{4\Omega^{2}k_{3}^{2}|\textbf{{k}}|^{2}+(\beta^{2}/\eta)^{2}k_{2}^{4}},

(1.8)

where
\beta
,
\eta
,
\Omega
are some fixed positive constants. The expressions (
1.6
), (
1.7
) and (
1.8
) hold for all
\textbf{{k}}\in\{(k_{1},k_{2},k_{3})\in\mathbb{Z}^{3}:k_{3}\neq 0\}
, and for the self-consistency of the model we take

\displaystyle\widehat{\mathbf{M}}(k_{1},k_{2},0)=(0,0,0),\qquad k_{1},k_{2}\in\mathbb{Z}.

The system (
1
)-(
1.8
) is known as the non-diffusive magenetostrophic (MG) equation. We refer the readers to
[
FV11a
,
FV11b
,
FRV14
,
FS15
,
FS18
,
FS21
]
for more results concerning the MG equation.

The second physical example comes from a two dimensional model derived via Darcy’s law for the evolution of a flow in a porous medium, which is given by an active scalar equation in
\mathbb{T}^{2}\times(0,\infty)

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\partial_{t}\theta+u\cdot\nabla\theta=0\\
u=\mathbf{I}\theta,\,\,\,\theta(\textbf{{x}},0)=\theta_{0}(\textbf{{x}}).\end{array}\right.

Here
\mathbf{I}=(\mathbf{I}_{1},\mathbf{I}_{2})
is a Fourier multiplier operator with symbols
\mathbf{I}_{1}
,
\mathbf{I}_{2}
given explicitly by

\displaystyle\widehat{\mathbf{I}}_{1}(\textbf{{k}})

\displaystyle=\frac{-k_{1}k_{2}}{k_{1}^{2}+k_{2}^{2}},

(1.11)

\displaystyle\widehat{\mathbf{I}}_{2}(\textbf{{k}})

\displaystyle=\frac{k_{1}^{2}}{k_{1}^{2}+k_{2}^{2}},

(1.12)

The expressions (
1.11
) and (
1.12
) hold for all
\textbf{{k}}\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}_{*}
, and for the self-consistency of the model we take

\displaystyle\widehat{\mathbf{I}}(0,0)=(0,0).

The system (
1
)-(
1.12
) is called the incompressible porous media (IPM) equation. We refer the readers to
[
CFG11
,
CGO07
,
FGSV12
,
FS19
]
for more results on the IPM equation and the singular incompressible porous media (SIPM) equation.

The purpose of our current paper is to address the ill/well-posedness of the abstract class of active scalar equations (
1
) under particular regimes for
r_{0}
defined in (
1.3
). The paper is organised as follows. In Section
3
we prove local well-posedness in Gevrey classes for (
1
). For the special case when the symbol of
\mathbf{T}
is
odd
, the authors in
[
CCCGW12
]
showed the local existence and uniqueness of solutions for (
1
) in Sobolev spaces
H^{s}
, the main ingredients in their proof were an estimate for the commutator
\|[\partial_{x_{i}}(-\Delta)^{\frac{s}{2}},g]\|_{L^{2}}
and the identity

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}fAfg=-\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}f[A,g]f,

which holds for smooth
f
and
g
, and
A
is an odd operator. In contrast, when the symbol of
\mathbf{T}
is
even
and singular of order 1, the above method fails and in particular for the case of non-diffusive MG equation described by (
1
), Friedlander and Vicol
[
FV11b
]
established ill-posedness in the sense of Hadamard in Sobolev spaces. The proof of ill-posednes in
[
FV11b
]
relied on the explicit structure of the symbol of
\mathbf{M}
given by (
1.6
)-(
1.8
) in which there is one derivative loss in
x
in the map
\theta\mapsto u
, with the anisotropy of the Fourier symbol of
\mathbf{M}
and the fact that the symbol of
\mathbf{M}
is even. In our current work, we point out that our well-posedness results in Gevrey classes hold for
any
abstract operator
\mathbf{T}
satisfying (
1.3
) and is
independent of
the evenness or oddness of
\mathbf{T}
.

In Section
4
under further conditions (C1)–(C6) on the abstract system (
1
), we prove ill-posedness results in Gevrey classes. For even operators
\mathbf{T}
, ill-posedness results in Sobolev spaces have been proved using techniques of continued fractions
[
FGSV12
,
FRV12
,
FV11b
]
, and such method was later adopted for odd operators as well
[
KVW16
]
. In this current paper we extend such methods to Gevrey spaces. The main ingredients in the proof involve a linear ill-posedness result and a classical linear implies nonlinear ill-posedness argument. The conditions (C1)–(C6) described in Section
4
are somewhat more general than those given in
[
FGSV12
]
as we discuss in Remark
4.1
. Our conditions allow us to obtain sharper results for some physical models. To achieve our goal, we
combine the techniques
used for even operators
[
FGSV12
,
FRV12
,
FV11b
]
as well as odd operators
[
KVW16
]
, which enable us to obtain a fast enough growth rate of the
L^{2}
norm of the solutions in any short period of time.

In Section
5
we apply the results for a general class of active scalar equations to the MG, IPM and SIPM equations. Our end point results are consistent with the literature and give a more complete picture of Gevrey space ill/well-posedness related to strongly singular operators. We point out that our ill-posedness results apply to the physical model of non-diffusive MG equation given by (
1
)-(
1.8
), which show that the non-diffusive MG equation is in fact locally ill-posed in Gevrey spaces
G^{s}

for all

s>1
. Such result is drastically different from the case of analytic spaces (that is the case of
G^{s}
with
s=1
) for which the authors in
[
FV11b
]
proved that the non-diffusive MG equation is locally well-posed in analytic spaces. If the operator
\mathbf{M}
in (
1
) is replaced by
(-\Delta)^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}\mathbf{M}
, then we are able to obtain a
sharp dichotomy
across the value
\alpha=0
that is the case of non-diffusive MG equation. More precisely, if
\alpha<0
the equations are locally well-posed, while if
\alpha>0
they are ill-posed in Gervey classes
G^{s}
for all
s\geq 1
. We refer to Remark
5.3
for further discussion.

2.
Preliminaries

We introduce the following notations used in this paper.
W^{s,p}
is the usual inhomogeneous Sobolev space with norm
\|\cdot\|_{W^{s,p}(\mathbb{T}^{d})}
, and we write
H^{s}=W^{s,2}
. For simplicity, we write
\|\cdot\|_{L^{p}}=\|\cdot\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{T}^{d})}
,
\|\cdot\|_{W^{s,p}}=\|\cdot\|_{W^{s,p}(\mathbb{T}^{d})}
, etc. unless otherwise specified.

We recall the following Sobolev embedding inequality from the literature (see for example Bahouri-Chemin-Danchin
[
BCD11
]
and Ziemer
[
Zie89
]
): For
d\geq 1
, suppose that
k>0
,
p<d
,
1\leq p<q<\infty
with
\frac{1}{p}-\frac{l}{d}=\frac{1}{q}
. If
g\in W^{p,l}
, then
g\in L^{q}
and there exists a constant
C>0
such that

\displaystyle\|g\|_{L^{q}}\leq C\|g\|_{W^{p,l}}.

(2.1)

We state the following definition for Gevrey class
G^{s}
when
s\geq 1
.

Definition 2.1

(Gevrey-space)
.

Fix
r>3
. A function
\theta\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
belongs to the Gevrey class
G^{s}
where
s\geq 1
, if there exists
\tau>0
, known as the Gevrey-class radius, such that the
G^{s}_{\tau}
-norm is finite, i.e.

\displaystyle\|\theta\|^{2}_{G^{s}_{\tau}}=\|(-\Delta)^{\frac{r}{2}}e^{\tau(-\Delta)^{\frac{1}{2s}}}\theta\|^{2}_{L^{2}}=\sum_{\textbf{{k}}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}_{*}}|\textbf{{k}}|^{2r}e^{2\tau|\textbf{{k}}|^{\frac{1}{s}}}|\widehat{\theta}(\textbf{{k}})|^{2}.

(2.2)

Remark 2.2
.

Since we are working in the mean-free setting, we take
\mathbb{Z}^{d}_{*}=\{\textbf{{k}}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}:|\textbf{{k}}|\neq 0\}
and we define
G^{s}:=\cup_{\tau>0}G^{s}_{\tau}
. We point out that for the case when
s=1
,
G^{s}
gives the space of analytic functions.

We also recall the following definition of Lipschitz well-posedness for the nonlinear equation (see
[
FRV12
, Definition 4.4]
for example):

Definition 2.3
.

Let
Y\subset X\subset H^{q}
be Banach spaces with
q>r_{0}+\frac{d}{4}
. The Cauchy problem (
1
) is locally Lipschitz
(X,Y)
well-posed, if there exist continuous functions
T,K:[0,\infty)^{2}\to(0,\infty)
, the time of existence and the Lipschitz constant, so that for every pair of initial data
\theta_{0}^{(1)}
,
\theta_{0}^{(2)}\in Y
, there exist unique solutions
\theta^{(1)}
,
\theta^{(2)}\in L^{\infty}(0,T;X)
of the initial value problem associated with (
1
) that additionally satisfy

\displaystyle\|\theta^{(1)}(\cdot,t)-\theta^{(2)}(\cdot,t)\|_{X}\leq K\|\theta_{0}^{(1)}(\cdot)-\theta_{0}^{(2)}(\cdot)\|_{Y}

(2.3)

for every
t\in[0,T]
, where
T=T(\|\theta_{0}^{(1)}\|_{Y},\|\theta_{0}^{(2)}\|_{Y})
and
K=K(\|\theta_{0}^{(1)}\|_{Y},\|\theta_{0}^{(2)}\|_{Y})
.

Finally, we state the following fact which will become useful in Section
3
.

Proposition 2.4
.

Let
\zeta>0
. Then for any
a,b>0
, we have

\displaystyle(a+b)^{\zeta}\leq C_{\zeta}(a^{\zeta}+b^{\zeta})

(2.4)

where
C_{\zeta}>0
depends only on
\zeta
and is independent of
a
and
b
.

3.
Wellposedness in Gevrey-class of active scalar equations

In this section, we study the active scalar equation (
1
) when
r_{0}\in[0,1]
, where
r_{0}
is given by (
1.3
) in Section
1
. We prove that the equation (
1
) is locally well-posed in Gevrey class
G^{s}
for all
s\in[1,\frac{1}{r_{0}}]
when
r_{0}\in(0,1]
, while for the case
r_{0}=0
, the equation (
1
) is locally well-posed in
G^{s}
for all

. The results are summarised in Thereom

:

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
r_{0}\in[0,1]
and assume that (

) holds. When
r_{0}\in(0,1]
, we fix
s\in[1,\frac{1}{r_{0}}]
and
K_{0}>0
. Let
\theta_{0}\in G^{s}
with radius of convergence
\tau_{0}>0
and

\displaystyle\|(-\Delta)^{\frac{r}{2}}e^{\tau_{0}(-\Delta)^{\frac{1}{2s}}}\theta_{0}\|_{L^{2}}\leq K_{0},

(3.1)

where
r>3
. There exist a positive time
T_{*}=T_{*}(\tau_{0},K_{0})
and a unique solution in
G^{s}
on
[0,T_{*})
to the initial value problem associated to (
1
).

When
r_{0}=0
, there exist a positive time
T_{*}=T_{*}(\tau_{0},K_{0})
and a unique solution in
G^{s}
on
[0,T_{*})
to the initial value problem associated to (
1
) for all
s\geq 1
.

Proof of Theorem
3.1
.

We only give the proof for
r_{0}\in(0,1]

Let
\tau=\tau(t)>0

,
r_{0}\in(0,1]
and
s\in[1,\frac{1}{r_{0}}]
. Throughout this proof,
C
always denotes a positive generic constant which may depend on
r
,
r_{0}

\tau
,
l
,
j

k
, and for simplicity we write

We take

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\|\theta\|^{2}_{G^{s}_{\tau}}-\dot{\tau}\|\Lambda^{\frac{1}{2s}}\theta\|^{2}_{G^{s}_{\tau}}=\langle u\cdot\nabla\theta,\Lambda^{2r}e^{2\tau\Lambda^{\frac{1}{s}}}\theta\rangle.

(3.2)

Write

\displaystyle\langle u\cdot\nabla\Lambda^{r}e^{\tau\Lambda^{\frac{1}{s}}}\theta,\Lambda^{r}e^{\tau\Lambda^{\frac{1}{s}}}\theta\rangle=0

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}=\langle u\cdot\nabla\theta,\Lambda^{2r}e^{2\tau\Lambda^{\frac{1}{s}}}\theta\rangle-\langle u\cdot\nabla\Lambda^{r}e^{\tau\Lambda^{\frac{1}{s}}}\theta,\Lambda^{r}e^{\tau\Lambda^{\frac{1}{s}}}\theta\rangle.

By Plancherel’s theorem,

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}

\displaystyle=i(2\pi)^{d}\sum_{\textbf{{j}}+\textbf{{k}}=\textbf{{l}};\textbf{{j}},\textbf{{k}},\textbf{{l}}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}_{*}}\widehat{u}(\textbf{{j}})\cdot\textbf{{k}}\widehat{\theta}(\textbf{{k}})|\textbf{{l}}|^{2r}e^{2\tau|\textbf{{l}}|^{\frac{1}{s}}}\widehat{\theta}(-\textbf{{l}})

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}_{1}

\displaystyle=i(2\pi)^{d}\sum_{(\textbf{{j}},\textbf{{k}},\textbf{{l}})\in{\mathcal{A}}}\widehat{u}(\textbf{{j}})\cdot\textbf{{k}}\widehat{\theta}(\textbf{{k}})|\textbf{{l}}|^{2r}e^{2\tau|\textbf{{l}}|^{\frac{1}{s}}}\widehat{\theta}(-\textbf{{l}})

Estimates on

In this section, we address the equation (
1

(C1)

Remark 4.1

Conditions (C1)–(C6) are reminiscent of those given in

Under the conditions (C1)–(C6), we claim that solution to (

Theorem 4.2

Remark 4.3

Theorem

In order to prove Theorem

5.
Applications to physical models

5.1.
The non-diffusive MG equations

We consider the following class of active scalar equations in

:

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\partial_{t}\theta+u\cdot\nabla\theta=0\\

where

,
\mathbf{M}_{2}
,

\displaystyle|\widehat{(-\Delta)^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}\mathbf{M}}(\textbf{{k}})|\leq C(\beta,\eta,\Omega)|\textbf{{k}}|^{\alpha+1},

\beta
,

1.6
)-(

and

. Notice that for

) reduces to the non-diffusive MG equation given by (
1
)-(
1.8
).

For the case when

, as a direct consequence of Theorem
3.1
, we immediately obtain the following result:

Theorem 5.1
.

For
\alpha\in(-1,0]
, we fix
s\in[1,\frac{1}{\alpha+1}]
and
K_{0}>0
. Let
\theta_{0}\in G^{s}
with radius of convergence

and

\displaystyle\|(-\Delta)^{\frac{r}{2}}e^{\tau_{0}(-\Delta)^{\frac{1}{2s}}}\theta_{0}\|_{L^{2}}\leq K_{0},

where

and a unique solution in

on

to the initial value problem associated to (
5.1
).

When
\alpha=-1
, there exist
T_{*}=T_{*}(\tau_{0},K_{0})>0
and a unique solution in
G^{s}
on
[0,T_{*})
to the initial value problem associated to (

) for all

When

hold for the symbol

when

. Then conditions (C1) and (C2) follow directly from the definition of

\displaystyle\textbf{{b}}^{(j)}:=(j^{2},j),\qquad j\in\mathbb{N}.

Then it is clear that

Remark 5.3
.

Some remarks on Theorem

and Theorem

•

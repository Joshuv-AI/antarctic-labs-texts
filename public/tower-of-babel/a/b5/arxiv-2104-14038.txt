Riemann-Hilbert problem on an elliptic surface and a uniformly stressed inclusion embedded into a half-plane subjected to antiplane strain
Y.A. Antipov

Abstract
An inverse problem of elasticity of n n elastic inclusions embedded into an elastic half-plane is analyzed. The boundary of the half-plane is free of traction. The half-plane and the inclusions are subjected to antiplane shear, and the conditions of ideal contact hold in the interfaces between the inclusions and the half-plane. The shapes of the inclusions are not prescribed and have to be determined by enforcing uniform stresses inside the inclusions. The method of conformal mappings from a slit domain onto the ( n + 1 ) (n+1) -connected physical domain is worked out. It is shown that to recover the map and therefore the inclusions shapes, one needs to solve a vector Riemann-Hilbert problem on a genus- n n hyperelliptic surface. In a particular case of loading of a single inclusion in a half-plane, the problem is equivalent to two scalar Riemann-Hilbert problems on two slits on an elliptic surface. In addition to three parameters of the model the conformal map possesses a free geometric parameter. Results of numerical tests which show the impact of these parameters on the inclusion shape are presented.

The goal of this paper is to develop a method of conformal mappings for the inverse antiplane problem with an inclusion

It is aimed to construct a conformal map that is capable to recover the shape

In Section 3, we show that if both stresses,

Introduce next two functions, a harmonic conjugate

The first Riemann-Hilbert problem

dV((\xi,v),(\zeta,u))=\frac{1}{2}\left[\frac{\zeta-\xi_{0}}{\xi-\xi_{0}}+\frac{u}{v}\frac{\xi-\xi_{0}}{\zeta-\xi_{0}}\right]\frac{d\xi}{\xi-\zeta}.

6
Two scalar Riemann-Hilbert problems associated with the problem of
n
inclusions

In this section we aim to generalize the method by considering
n\geq 2
uniformly stressed inclusions
D_{j}

embedded into a half-plane
{{R}}_{+}^{2}=\{|x_{1}|<\infty,x_{2}\geq 0\}
.
Suppose that the shear moduli of the inclusions
D_{j}
and the surrounding infinite body
D_{0}={{R}}_{+}^{2}\setminus\cup_{j=1}^{n}D_{j}
are
\mu_{j}
and
\mu_{0}
, respectively. At infinity,
\tau_{13}=\tau_{1}^{\infty}
,
\tau_{23}=0
.
The boundary of the half-plane is free of traction,
\tau_{23}=0
, and inside the inclusions, the stresses are
constant,
\tau_{13}=\tau^{j}_{1}
,
\tau_{23}=\tau^{j}_{2}
,
(x_{1},x_{2})\in D_{j}
,
j=1,2,\ldots,n
. As in Section 2, the interface conditions of ideal contact

w_{0}=w_{j},\quad\mu_{0}\frac{\partial w_{0}}{\partial\nu}=\mu_{j}\frac{\partial w_{j}}{\partial\nu},\quad x\in L_{j},\quad j=1,2,\ldots,n,

(6.1)

hold on the boundary
L_{j}
of an inclusion
D_{j}
.

6.1
Case
\tau_{1}^{j}=\tau_{1}
,
\tau_{2}^{j}=\tau_{2}
,
j=1,2,\ldots,n

By introducing a function
f(z)
by (
2.6
) we rewrite the interface conditions (
6.1
) as

f(z)=\frac{1}{\lambda_{j}}\mathop{\rm Re}\nolimits\frac{\bar{\tau}z}{\mu_{0}}+a_{j}+ib_{j},\quad z\in L_{j},\quad j=1,2,\ldots,n,

(6.2)

where
\lambda_{j}=\kappa_{j}/(1-\kappa_{j})
,
\kappa_{j}=\mu_{j}/\mu_{0}
, and
a_{j}
and
b_{j}
are arbitrary real constants.

Let
z=\omega(\zeta)
be a conformal map
{\cal D}\to D_{0}
from a parametric
\zeta
-plane cut along
segments
l_{0}=[m,\infty)
and
l_{j}=[k_{2j-1},k_{2j}]
,
j=1,2,\ldots,n
, where

m>1
,
k_{1}=0
,
k_{2}=1
,
0>k_{4}>k_{3}>k_{6}>k_{5}>\ldots>k_{2n}>k_{2n-1}
.
The function
\omega(\zeta)
maps the two-sided finite segments
l_{j}
into the contours
L_{j}
and the two-sided semi-infinite
contour
l_{0}
into the
x_{1}
-axis of the physical plane. When there are two inclusions in the half-plane and the domain
is triply-connected, such a map always
exists. In the case when
n\geq 3
, we assume that the inclusions are arranged such that all preimages
l_{j}

of their boundaries
L_{j}
lie in the real axis. As in Section 3, we need to determine two functions,

F(\zeta)=f(\omega(\zeta))
and
\omega(\zeta)
analytic in the
(n+1)
-connected slit domain
{\cal D}

based on the boundary conditions

\mathop{\rm Im}\nolimits F(\zeta)=b_{j},\quad\zeta\in l_{1},\quad\mathop{\rm Re}\nolimits\frac{\bar{\tau}\omega(\zeta)}{\mu_{0}}=\lambda_{j}[\mathop{\rm Re}\nolimits F(\zeta)-a_{j}],\quad\zeta\in l_{j}.\quad j=1,\ldots,n,

\mathop{\rm Im}\nolimits\omega(\zeta)=0,\quad\mathop{\rm Im}\nolimits F(\zeta)=b_{0}-\mathop{\rm Im}\nolimits\frac{\bar{\tau}\omega(\zeta)}{\mu_{0}},\quad\zeta\in l_{0}.

(6.3)

At infinity the functions
F(\zeta)
and
\omega(\zeta)
have to satisfy the conditions (
3.5
).

Let
{\cal R}
be a genus-
n
hyperelliptic surface of the algebraic function

u^{2}=p(\zeta),\quad p(\zeta)=(\zeta-m)\prod_{j=1}^{2n}(\zeta-k_{j}).

(6.4)

Introduce two functions
\Phi_{1}(\zeta,u)
and
\Phi_{2}(\zeta,u)
on the
surface
{\cal R}
. The first function is defined as in (
3.8
), while the second function is

\Phi_{2}(\zeta,u)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{cc}i\mu_{0}^{-1}\bar{\tau}\omega(\zeta),&(\zeta,u)\in{\cal D}^{+}\subset{\cal R},\\
-i\mu_{0}^{-1}\tau\overline{\omega(\bar{\zeta})},&(\zeta,u)\in{\cal D}^{-}\subset{\cal R}.\\
\end{array}\right.

(6.5)

These functions satisfy the symmetry condition (
3.9
), the conditions (
3.14
) at infinity,
analytic on the Riemann surface
{\cal R}\setminus{\cal L}
,

{\cal L}=\cup_{j=0}^{n}l_{j}
, and Hölder-continuous up to the two-sided contour
{\cal L}
. On the contour
{\cal L}
,
they satisfy the vector Riemann-Hilbert boundary condition (
3.11
), where
G(\xi,v)
is a piece-wise constant matrix
given by

G(\xi,v)=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}1&0\\
2i\lambda_{j}&1\\
\end{array}\right),\quad(\xi,v)\in l_{j},\quad G(\xi,v)=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}1&i(1-\bar{\tau}/\tau)\\
0&-\bar{\tau}/\tau\\
\end{array}\right),\quad(\xi,v)\in l_{0},

(6.6)

and
g(\xi,v)
is a piece-wise constant vector

g(\xi,v)=\left(\begin{array}[]{c}2ib_{j}\\
-2i\lambda_{j}(a_{j}-ib_{j})\\
\end{array}\right),\quad(\xi,v)\in l_{j},\quad g(\xi,v)=\left(\begin{array}[]{c}2ib_{0}\\
0\\
\end{array}\right),\quad(\xi,v)\in l_{0}.

(6.7)

When
\tau_{2}=0
, the vector Riemann-Hilbert problem is equivalent to two scalar problems

\Phi_{1}^{+}(\xi,v)-\Phi_{1}^{-}(\xi,v)=2ib_{j},\quad(\xi,v)\in l_{j},\quad j=0,1,\ldots,n,

\Phi_{2}^{+}(\xi,v)+\Phi_{2}^{-}(\xi,v)=0,\quad(\xi,v)\in l_{0},

\Phi_{2}^{+}(\xi,v)-\Phi_{2}^{-}(\xi,v)=2i\lambda_{j}[\mathop{\rm Re}\nolimits\Phi_{1}^{+}(\xi,v)-a_{j}],\quad(\xi,v)\in l_{j},\quad j=1,\ldots,n.

(6.8)

6.2
Case
\tau_{1}^{j}/\mu_{j}=\nu_{1}
,
\tau_{2}^{j}/\mu_{j}=\nu_{2}
,
j=1,2,\ldots,n

In this case we denote
\nu=\nu_{1}+i\nu_{2}
and rewrite the interface conditions
in the form

f(z)=i(\kappa_{j}-1)\mathop{\rm Im}\nolimits(\bar{\nu}z)+a_{j}+ib_{j},\quad z\in L_{j},\quad j=1,2,\ldots,n,

(6.9)

The counterpart of the boundary conditions (
6.3
) on the contours
l_{j}
is

\mathop{\rm Re}\nolimits F(\zeta)=a_{j},\quad(\kappa_{j}-1)\mathop{\rm Im}\nolimits[\bar{\nu}\omega(\zeta)]=\mathop{\rm Im}\nolimits F(\zeta)-b_{j},\quad\zeta\in l_{j}.\quad j=1,\ldots,n,

\mathop{\rm Im}\nolimits\omega(\zeta)=0,\quad\mathop{\rm Im}\nolimits F(\zeta)=b_{0}-\mathop{\rm Im}\nolimits[\bar{\nu}\omega(\zeta)],\quad\zeta\in l_{0}.

(6.10)

To reduce this problem to a vector Riemann-Hilbert problem on the surface
{\cal R}
we
define the function
\Phi_{1}(\zeta,u)
and
\Phi_{1}(\zeta,u)
in a way different from the case of Section 6.1. We put

\Phi_{1}(\zeta,u)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{cc}iF(\zeta),&(\zeta,u)\in{\cal D}^{+}\subset{\cal R},\\
-i\overline{F(\bar{\zeta})},&(\zeta,u)\in{\cal D}^{-}\subset{\cal R},\\
\end{array}\right.

\Phi_{2}(\zeta,u)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{cc}\bar{\nu}\omega(\zeta),&(\zeta,u)\in{\cal D}^{+}\subset{\cal R},\\
\nu\overline{\omega(\bar{\zeta})},&(\zeta,u)\in{\cal D}^{-}\subset{\cal R}.\\
\end{array}\right.

(6.11)

These functions solve the vector Riemann-Hilbert problem (
3.11
) on the contour
{\cal L}

with the matrix coefficient
G(\xi,v)
and the right-hand side
g(\xi,v)
having the form

G(\xi,v)=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}1&0\\
-\frac{2i}{\kappa_{j}-1}&1\\
\end{array}\right),\;(\xi,v)\in l_{j},\quad G(\xi,v)=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}-1&i(1-\frac{\bar{\nu}}{\nu})\\
0&\frac{\bar{\nu}}{\nu}\\
\end{array}\right),\;(\xi,v)\in l_{0},

g(\xi,v)=\left(\begin{array}[]{c}2ia_{j}\\
-\frac{2i}{\kappa_{j}-1}(b_{j}+ia_{j})\\
\end{array}\right),\;(\xi,v)\in l_{j},\quad g(\xi,v)=\left(\begin{array}[]{c}-2b_{0}\\
0\\
\end{array}\right),\;(\xi,v)\in l_{0}.

(6.12)

Again, the vector problem is decoupled if
\tau_{2}^{j}=0
, that is
\nu=\bar{\nu}=\tau_{1}^{j}/\mu_{j}
,
j=1,\ldots,n
.
We deduce the following two scalar Riemann-Hilbert problems on the genus-
n
hyperelliptic surface
{\cal R}
:

\Phi_{1}^{+}(\xi,v)-\Phi_{1}^{-}(\xi,v)=2ia_{j},\quad(\xi,v)\in l_{j},\quad j=1,\ldots,n

\Phi_{1}^{+}(\xi,v)+\Phi_{1}^{-}(\xi,v)=-2b_{0},\quad(\xi,v)\in l_{0},

\Phi_{2}^{+}(\xi,v)-\Phi_{2}^{-}(\xi,v)=-\frac{2i}{\kappa_{j}-1}[\mathop{\rm Re}\nolimits\Phi_{1}^{+}(\xi,v)+b_{j}],\quad(\xi,v)\in l_{j},\quad j=1,\ldots,n,

\Phi_{2}^{+}(\xi,v)-\Phi_{2}^{-}(\xi,v)=0,\quad(\xi,v)\in l_{0}.

(6.13)

In both Riemann-Hilbert problems, (
6.8
) and (
6.13
), the functions
\Phi_{1}(\zeta,u)
and
\Phi_{2}(\zeta,u)
satisfy the symmetry condition (
3.9
) and the conditions at
infinity (
3.14

For the solution of both Riemann-Hilbert problems one need a genus-
n
hyperelliptic analogue of the elliptic kernel (
4.3
).
It has the form

dV((\xi,v),(\zeta,u))=\frac{1}{2}\left[1+\frac{u}{v}\prod_{j=0}^{n}\frac{\xi-\xi_{j}}{\zeta-\xi_{j}}\right]\left(\frac{1}{\xi-\zeta}-\frac{1}{\xi-\xi_{0}}\right)d\xi.

(6.14)

Here,
\xi_{0},\ldots,\xi_{n}
are arbitrary fixed not necessarily distinct points in the real axis not falling on the
contour
{\cal L}
of the Riemann-Hilbert problem.
The solution of the Riemann-Hilbert problems (
6.8
) and (
6.13
) is beyond the scope of this paper and not presented here.

7
Conclusion

In the previous sections a closed-form solution has been derived to the inverse problem of antiplane shear of
an elastic finite domain
D_{1}
embedded into an elastic half-plane
{{R}}_{+}^{2}
. In this model,
the boundary of the half-plane
\{||x_{1}|<\infty,x_{2}=0\}

is kept free of traction
\tau_{23}
, the conditions of ideal contact on the interface hold, and the stress
field inside the inclusion is uniform, while the shape of the inclusion is to be recovered.
This harmonic problem is well suited for the method of conformal mappings.
It has been shown that a map from a parametric plane cut along the segments
[0,1]
and
[m,\infty)

onto the physical doubly-connected domain
{{R}}_{+}^{2}\setminus D_{1}
can be recovered by solving a
vector Riemann-Hilbert problem with a piece-wise constant matrix coefficient on an elliptic
surface
{\cal R}
.
Under the assumption
that
\tau_{23}=0
inside the inclusion
D_{1}
, the vector
problem has been decoupled into two scalar Riemann-Hilbert problems on two slits on the surface
{\cal R}
.
The solution has been determined by proposing and using an analogue of the Cauchy kernel on an elliptic surface and
solving a Jacobi inversion problem associated with the factorization problem, a part of the solution procedure.
Not counting the scaling parameter, the solutions to the Riemann-Hilbert problems
comprise a four-parametric family of conformal mappings which possess three model parameters

\kappa=\mu_{1}/\mu_{0}
,
\tau_{1}/\mu_{0}
, and
\tau_{1}^{\infty}/\mu_{0}
and one geometric parameter
m
.
Here,
\mu_{1}
and
\mu_{0}
are the shear moduli of the inclusion and the half-plane,
\tau_{1}
is the constant stress

\tau_{13}
inside the inclusion, and
\tau_{1}^{\infty}
is the stress
\tau_{13}
applied at infinity.
Numerical implementation of this method has shown that
the inclusion is not symmetric with respect to any line parallel to the
x_{1}
-axis. At the same time,
its shape resembles an ellipse.

The method can be generalized to the problem of
n
inclusions
D_{j}
(
j=1,\ldots,n)
in a half-plane
when uniform stresses inside all the inclusions satisfy one of the conditions,
(i)
\tau_{13}^{j}=\tau_{1}
,
\tau_{23}^{j}=\tau_{2}
, or
(ii)
\tau_{13}^{j}/\mu_{j}=\nu_{1}
,
\tau_{23}^{j}/\mu_{j}=\nu_{2}
.
The conformal map, if available, recoveres the whole family of inclusions in the case
n=2

and a part of the family if
n\geq 3
that is the set of those inclusions whose preimages lie in the real axis
of the parametric plane. If
\tau_{2}\neq 0
(
\nu_{2}\neq 0
), one needs to deal with a vector Riemann-Hilbert problem on a genus-
n

hyperelliptic surface. In the particular case
\tau_{2}=0
(
\nu_{2}=0
), the problem is decoupled, and the corresponding
scalar Riemann-Hilbert problems admit a solution by singular integrals. One of the most difficult issues
in the solution procedure is a genus-
n
Jacobi inversion problem whose solution is determined through
the
n
zeros of the associated Riemann Theta function
[
21
]
,
[
22
]
.

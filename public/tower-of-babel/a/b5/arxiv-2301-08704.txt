Following

]
, a space

of a space

Proposition 2.1
.

There exists a screened space
X
possessing a free
\omega_{1}
-complete closed ultrafilter if and only if there exists a measurable cardinal.

Proof.

The “if” part of the proof follows from the fact that every discrete space is screened.

Assume that a screened space
X
possesses a free
\omega_{1}
-complete closed ultrafilter
\mathcal{F}
. Consider the open cover
\mathcal{V}=\{X\setminus F:F\in\mathcal{F}
,
F
is closed
\}
.
Since the space
X
is screened, the cover
\mathcal{V}
admits a refinement
\mathcal{W}=\bigcup_{n\in\omega}\mathcal{W}_{n}
, where for each
n\in\omega
the family
\mathcal{W}_{n}
consists of pairwise disjoint open sets.
For each
n\in\omega
let
\mathbf{W_{n}}=\bigcup\mathcal{W}_{n}
. Note that if
\mathbf{W_{n}}\notin\mathcal{F}
, then the maximality of
\mathcal{F}
implies that the closed set
X\setminus\mathbf{W_{n}}
belongs to
\mathcal{F}
. Consider the set
M=\{n\in\omega:\mathbf{W_{n}}\in\mathcal{F}\}
. If
\mathbf{W_{n}}\notin\mathcal{F}
for all
n\in\omega
, then, by the
\omega_{1}
-completeness of
\mathcal{F}
,
\emptyset=\bigcap_{n\in\omega}(X\setminus\mathbf{W_{n}})\in\mathcal{F}
which implies a contradiction. Hence the set
M
is not empty.
Fix any
n\in M
.
Enumerate
\mathcal{W}_{n}
as
\{W_{\alpha}:\alpha\in\kappa\}
. For any
F\in\mathcal{F}
define
H_{F}=\{\alpha\in\kappa:F\cap W_{\alpha}\neq\emptyset\}
. Consider the filter
\mathcal{H}
on
\kappa
generated by the base
\{H_{F}:F\in\mathcal{F}\}
. By the definition of the cover
\mathcal{V}
,
W_{\alpha}\notin\mathcal{F}
for any
\alpha\in\kappa
. Therefore, the filter
\mathcal{H}
is nonprincipal. Since the filter
\mathcal{F}
is
\omega_{1}
-complete, then so is
\mathcal{H}
. Pick any subset
A\subset\kappa
.
Assume that
A\notin\mathcal{H}
. Then for every
F\in\mathcal{F}
the set
F\cap(\bigcup_{\alpha\in\kappa\setminus A}W_{\alpha})\neq\emptyset
. It follows that
F\cap(X\setminus(\bigcup_{\alpha\in A})W_{\alpha})\neq\emptyset
for all
F\in\mathcal{F}
. Since the set
G=X\setminus(\bigcup_{\alpha\in A})W_{\alpha}
is closed and
\mathcal{F}
is a closed ultrafilter, we get that

. Then
\mathcal{H}\ni H_{G}\subset\kappa\setminus A
, witnessing that

.
Hence

is a nonprincipal
\omega_{1}
-complete ultrafilter. Theorem
1.1
yields the existence of a measurable cardinal.

Lemma 2.2
.

An
\omega_{1}
-complete filter
\mathcal{F}
on a space
X
is an
F_{\sigma}
ultrafilter if and only if
\mathcal{F}
is a closed ultrafilter.

Proof.

(
\Rightarrow
) Fix an arbitrary
F\in\mathcal{F}
. There exists an
F_{\sigma}
set
B\subset F
which belongs to
\mathcal{F}
. Then
B=\bigcup_{i\in\omega}B_{i}
where the sets
B_{i}
are closed. To derive a contradiction, assume that there exists no
i\in\omega
such that
B_{i}\in\mathcal{F}
. Taking into account that the sets
B_{i}
are closed and
\mathcal{F}
is an
F_{\sigma}
ultrafilter, we get that
X\setminus B_{i}\in\mathcal{F}
for every
i\in\omega
. Since the filter
\mathcal{F}
is
\omega_{1}
-complete, the set
C=\bigcap_{i\in\omega}(X\setminus B_{i})
belongs to
\mathcal{F}
. But
\emptyset=B\cap C\in\mathcal{F}
which implies a contradiction. Hence there exists

such that
B_{i}\in\mathcal{F}
. This provides that
\mathcal{F}
is a closed filter. At this point it is easy to see that
\mathcal{F}
is a closed ultrafilter.

(
\Leftarrow
) Fix any
F_{\sigma}
subset
B\subset X
such that
B\notin\mathcal{F}
. Find closed sets
B_{i}
,
i\in\omega
such that
B=\bigcup_{i\in\omega}B_{i}
. Since
\mathcal{F}
is a closed ultrafilter and

, for each

such that

. Then

and

, witnessing that

is an
F_{\sigma}
ultrafilter.
∎

\mathcal{F}
and the inclusion
\mathcal{F}\subset\mathcal{H}
, we obtain that
\mathcal{F}=\mathcal{H}
is an
\omega_{1}
-complete free closed ultrafilter.
∎

The
club principle
is the following consistent with ZFC statement: There is a sequence
\{A_{\alpha}:\alpha<\omega_{1},\alpha
limit
\}
such that for every
\alpha
,
A_{\alpha}
is cofinal in
\alpha
and for every uncountable
X\subset\omega_{1}
the set
\{\alpha\in\omega_{1}:A_{\alpha}\subset X\}
is not empty. The club principle was introduced by Ostaszewski
[
34
]
and usually is denoted by
\clubsuit
.

According to
[
15
]
an uncountable space
X
is called
sub-Ostaszewski
if every open subset of
X
is either countable or co-countable. By
[
15
, Proposition 2.10]
each sub-Ostaszewski space
X
has size
\omega_{1}
. Proposition 2.2 from
[
15
]
implies that at most one point in
X
has no countable open neighborhood. Note that the subspace
Y=\{x\in X:x
possesses a countable open neighborhood
\}
of
X
is not Lindelöf, witnessing that
X
is not hereditary Lindelöf. By
[
15
, Proposition 2.4]
, any sub-Ostaszewski space is hereditary separable. Recall that a hereditary separable regular space, which is not hereditary Lindelöf, is called an
S-space

[
43
]
. By the renowned result of Todorčević
[
48
]
, there is a model of ZFC which contains no S-spaces and, as a consequence, no regular sub-Ostaszewski spaces.

Lemma 2.10
.

(
\clubsuit
) There exists a perfectly normal hereditary separable first-countable locally compact space
X
which possesses a free
\omega_{1}
-complete filter
\mathcal{F}
which is simultaneously closed
G_{\delta}
,
F_{\sigma}
and Borel ultrafilter.

Proof.

In
[
34
]
it was proven that assuming
\clubsuit
there exists a topology
\tau
on
\omega_{1}
such that
X=(\omega_{1},\tau)
is a perfectly normal first-countable locally compact sub-Ostaszewski space. Moreover, for any
\xi\in\omega_{1}
the initial segment
\{\alpha:\alpha\leq\xi\}
is open in
X
. It is straightforward to check that every Borel subset of any sub-Ostaszewski space is either countable or co-countable.

Consider the filter
\mathcal{F}
generated by the family
\{\omega_{1}\setminus\xi:\xi\in\omega_{1}\}
. Clearly, the filter
\mathcal{F}
is
\omega_{1}
-complete. Since initial segments are open in
X
, the filter
\mathcal{F}
is free and closed. Consequently, the filter
\mathcal{F}
is
F_{\sigma}
and Borel. Since the complement to each element of
\mathcal{F}
is countable,
\mathcal{F}
is a
G_{\delta}
filter. Let us show that
\mathcal{F}
is simultaneously closed,
F_{\sigma}
,
G_{\delta}
and Borel ultrafilter.

Pick any Borel set
A\subset X
such that
A\notin\mathcal{F}
. Since any Borel set in
X
is either countable or co-countable, we get that the set
A
is countable. Then
\omega_{1}\setminus(\sup A+1)
is an element of
\mathcal{F}
disjoint with
A
. Hence
\mathcal{F}
is simultaneously closed,
F_{\sigma}
,
G_{\delta}
and Borel ultrafilter. ∎

The example constructed in the proof of Lemma
2.10
can be made countably compact
[
34
]
. But for this we need to assume
\diamondsuit
instead of
\clubsuit
. For more information about
\diamondsuit
see Chapter II.7 of
[
25
]
. Thus, we get the following:

Proposition 2.11
.

(
\diamondsuit
) There exists a hereditary separable perfectly normal first-countable locally compact countably compact space which possesses a free
\omega_{1}
-complete filter
\mathcal{F}
which is simultaneously closed
G_{\delta}
,
F_{\sigma}
and Borel ultrafilter.

Weiss
[
51
]
showed that MA implies that every perfectly normal countably compact space is compact. Moreover, Nyikos and Zdomskyy
[

]
proved that under PFA every first-countable separable normal countably compact space is compact. Thus, Proposition

cannot be proved within ZFC.

3.
General facts about the poset of free open filters on a given space

We start with the following auxiliary lemma.

Lemma 3.1
.

An open filter
\mathcal{F}
on a space
X
is an open ultrafilter if and only if for any
F\in\mathcal{F}
and open subset
U\subset F
either
U\in\mathcal{F}
or
F\setminus\overline{U}\in\mathcal{F}
.

Proof.

(
\Rightarrow
) Assuming that
\mathcal{F}
is an open ultrafilter, fix any
F\in\mathcal{F}
and an open subset
U\subset F
. Then either
U\in\mathcal{F}
or there exists an open set
H\in\mathcal{F}
such that
H\cap U=\emptyset
. In the latter case
H\cap\overline{U}=\emptyset
, implying that
\mathcal{F}\ni H\cap F\subseteq F\setminus\overline{U}
. Hence
F\setminus\overline{U}\in\mathcal{F}
.

(
\Leftarrow
) Assume that
\mathcal{F}
is an open filter on
X
such that for any
F\in\mathcal{F}
and any open subset
U\subset F
either
U\in\mathcal{F}
or
F\setminus\overline{U}\in\mathcal{F}
. Since
X\in\mathcal{F}
, for any open subset
U\subset X
either
U\in\mathcal{F}
or
X\setminus\overline{U}\in\mathcal{F}
. Then for any open set
U\notin\mathcal{F}
, the set
X\setminus\overline{U}
is an element of
\mathcal{F}
disjoint with
U
. Hence
\mathcal{F}
is an open ultrafilter.
∎

Lemma
3.1
implies that every open ultrafilter on a space
X
contains all dense open subsets of
X
.

The set
\mathbf{H}(X)
of all H-extensions of a space
X
carries a natural partial order
\leq
defined as follows: for any
Y,Z\in\mathbf{H}(X)
,
Y\leq Z
if there exists a continuous surjection
f:Z\rightarrow Y
such that
f(x)=x
for any
x\in X
. It can be checked
[
30
]
that
\mathbf{H}(X)
is a complete upper semilattice with respect to this order, i.e. each subset
A\subset\mathbf{H}(X)
has supremum in
\mathbf{H}(X)
. Order properties of compactifications and H-extensions were investigated in
[
5
,
22
,
28
,
29
,
32
,
42
]
.

Definition 3.2
.

The Katetov H-extension
K(X)
of a space
X
is the set

X\cup\{\mathcal{F}:\mathcal{F}\hbox{ is a free open ultrafilter on }X\}

endowed with the topology
\tau
which satisfies the following conditions:

•

A subset
A\subseteq X
is open in
(K(X),\tau)
if and only if
A
is open in
X
;

•

open neighborhood base at
\mathcal{F}\in K(X)\setminus X
consists of the sets
\{\mathcal{F}\}\cup F
, where
F\in\mathcal{F}
is open.

For any space
X
, the Katetov extension
K(X)
is the supremum of
\mathbf{H}(X)
and it can be considered as a generalization of Čech-Stone compactification (see
[
37
]
or
[
39
]
for more details).

A space
X
is called

•

locally H-closed
if each point
x\in X
possesses an open neighborhood
U
such that
\overline{U}
is H-closed.

•

almost H-closed
if it admits a unique free open ultrafilter.

A simple example of an almost H-closed space is the subspace
K(X)\setminus\{\mathcal{F}\}
of
K(X)
, where
X
is not H-closed, and
\mathcal{F}
is a free open ultrafilter on
X
.
The following lemma is a consequence of
Theorem 4.1 from
[
18
]
.

Lemma 3.3
.

Every almost H-closed space is locally H-closed.

Let
X
be a locally H-closed non-H-closed space. By
\mathcal{F}_{\inf}
we denote the filter generated by the base
\{X\setminus K:K
is an H-closed subspace of
X\}
. Observe that
\mathcal{F}_{\inf}
is well-defined, as the union of finitely many H-closed subspaces remains H-closed.

Lemma 3.4
.

For any locally H-closed non-H-closed space
X
the filter
\mathcal{F}_{\inf}
is the infimum of
\mathbf{OF}(X)
.

Proof.

Clearly,
\mathcal{F}_{\inf}
is a free open filter on
X
. To derive a contradiction, assume that there exists a free open filter
\mathcal{H}
on
X
such that
\mathcal{F}_{\inf}\not\subseteq\mathcal{H}
. Then there exists an H-closed subset
K
of
X
such that
H\cap K\neq\emptyset
for any
H\in\mathcal{H}
. Then the trace of
\mathcal{H}
on
K
is a free open filter, which contradicts the H-closedness of
K
.
∎

Lemma 3.5
.

Let
X
be a locally H-closed space and
\mathcal{T}
be a nonempty subset of
\mathbf{OF}(X)
. Then
\inf\mathcal{T}=\bigcap\mathcal{T}
.

Proof.

It is straightforward to check that the intersection of an arbitrary family of open filters is an open filter.
Hence to prove that
\inf\mathcal{T}=\bigcap\mathcal{T}
, it suffices to show that the open filter
\bigcap\mathcal{T}
is free.
Lemma
3.4
implies that the filter
\mathcal{F}_{\inf}
is the infimum of
\mathbf{OF}(X)
.
Hence
\mathcal{F}_{\inf}\subseteq\mathcal{F}
for each
\mathcal{F}\in\mathcal{T}
. Since the filter
\mathcal{F}_{\inf}
is free we get that so is
\bigcap\mathcal{T}
.
∎

Lemma 3.6
.

Let
X
be an almost H-closed space and
\mathcal{T}
be a nonempty subset of
\mathbf{OF}(X)
. Then
\sup\mathcal{T}
is generated by the family
\bigcup\mathcal{T}
.

Proof.

Let
\mathcal{F}_{\sup}
be the supremum of
\mathbf{OF}(X)
.
Since for each
\mathcal{F}\in\mathcal{T}
,
\mathcal{F}\subset\mathcal{F}_{\sup}
, the family
\bigcup\mathcal{T}
has the finite intersection property. Let
\mathcal{H}
be the filter generated by the family
\bigcup\mathcal{T}
. Since each filter
\mathcal{F}\in\mathcal{T}
is free and
\mathcal{F}\subset\mathcal{H}
, the filter
\mathcal{H}
is free as well. It remains to show that the filter
\mathcal{H}
is open. For this, fix any
H\in\mathcal{H}
. It follows that there exist
\mathcal{F}_{0},\ldots,\mathcal{F}_{n-1}\in\mathcal{T}
and elements
F_{i}\in\mathcal{F}_{i}
,
i\in n
such that
\bigcap_{i\in n}F_{i}\subseteq H
. Since the filters
\mathcal{F}_{i}
,
i\in n
are open, for every
i\in n
there exists an open set
O_{i}\in\mathcal{F}_{i}
such that
O_{i}\subset F_{i}
. Observe that the open set
\bigcap_{i\in n}O_{i}\subseteq H
belongs to
\mathcal{H}
.
∎

Since the increasing union of filters is a filter, Lemma
3.6
implies the following.

Corollary 3.7
.

Let
\mathcal{T}
be a nonempty linearly ordered subset of
\mathbf{OF}(X)
. Then
\sup\mathcal{T}=\bigcup\mathcal{T}
.

A lattice
(L,\vee,\wedge)
is called
distributive
if

x\wedge(y\vee z)=(x\wedge y)\vee(x\wedge z)
. It is well-known that the latter condition is equivalent to its dual:

x\vee(y\wedge z)=(x\vee y)\wedge(x\vee z)
.

Theorem 3.8
.

For a Hausdorff space
X
the following conditions are equivalent:

(1)

X
is almost H-closed;

(2)

the posets
\mathbf{H}(X)
and
\mathbf{OF}(X)
are order isomorphic;

(3)

the poset
\mathbf{OF}(X)
is a complete distributive lattice;

(4)

the poset
\mathbf{OF}(X)
is a lattice.

Proof.

(1)
\Rightarrow
(2). Let us show that any proper extension of
X
has a singleton reminder. To derive a contradiction, assume that
Y
is an extension of
X
such that
Y\setminus X
contains two distinct points
a,b
. By Zorn’s Lemma, the traces of the filters
\mathcal{N}(a)
and
\mathcal{N}(b)
on
X
can be enlarged to open ultrafilters
\mathcal{F}_{a}
and
\mathcal{F}_{b}
, respectively. Since the space
Y
is Hausdorff, there exists
U_{a}\in\mathcal{N}(a)
and
U_{b}\in\mathcal{N}(b)
such that
U_{a}\cap U_{b}=\emptyset
. Therefore,
\mathcal{F}_{a}
and
\mathcal{F}_{b}
are distinct filters, which contradicts the almost H-closedness of
X
. Define
\phi:\mathbf{H}(X)\rightarrow\mathbf{OF}(X)
by
\phi(Y)=\mathcal{F}_{y}
, where
Y\setminus X=\{y\}
and
\mathcal{F}_{y}
is the trace of the filter
\mathcal{N}(y)
(whose base consists of open neighborhoods of
y
) on
X
. Clearly, the filter
\mathcal{F}_{y}
is open and free, witnessing that the map
\phi
is well-defined.
At this point it is straightforward to check that the map
\phi
is injective. Let us check that
\phi
is an order homomorphism. Fix any two H-extensions
Y_{1}
and
Y_{2}
of
X
such that
Y_{1}\leq Y_{2}
. Let
\{y_{1}\}=Y_{1}\setminus X
and
\{y_{2}\}=Y_{2}\setminus X
. There exists a continuous surjective map
h:Y_{2}\rightarrow Y_{1}
fixing elements of
X
. Obviously,
h(y_{2})=y_{1}
. Since the map
h
is continuous,
\phi(Y_{1})\subseteq\phi(Y_{2})
. Hence
\phi
is an injective order homomorphism between posets
\mathbf{H}(X)
and
\mathbf{OF}(X)
.
Fix any free open filter
\mathcal{F}
on
X
. Let
\tau
be the topology on the set
X\cup\{\mathcal{F}\}
which satisfies the following conditions:

•

A subset
A\subseteq X
is open in
(X\cup\{\mathcal{F}\},\tau)
if and only if
A
is open in
X
;

•

the family
\{\{\mathcal{F}\}\cup F:F\in\mathcal{F}
and
F
is open
\}
forms a base at
\mathcal{F}
.

Then
Y=(X\cup\{\mathcal{F}\},\tau)\in\mathbf{H}(X)
and
\phi(Y)=\mathcal{F}
which implies that the map
\phi
is surjective.

(2)
\Rightarrow
(1). Assume that the space
X
possesses two distinct free open ultrafilters
\mathcal{F}_{1}
,
\mathcal{F}_{2}
. Then there exist
F_{1}\in\mathcal{F}_{1}
and
F_{2}\in\mathcal{F}_{2}
such that
F_{1}\cap F_{2}=\emptyset
. It follows that there exists no open filter
\mathcal{H}
on
X
such that
\mathcal{F}_{1}\subset\mathcal{H}
and
\mathcal{F}_{2}\subset\mathcal{H}
. Consequently, the poset
\mathbf{OF}(X)
does not have a supremum. On the other hand, the Katetov extension
K(X)
is the supremum of
\mathbf{H}(X)
(see
[
37
]
). Hence the posets
\mathbf{OF}(X)
and
\mathbf{H}(X)
cannot be order isomorphic.

(2)
\Rightarrow
(3). Since (1)
\Leftrightarrow
(2) the space
X
is almost H-closed. Lemma
3.3
implies that
X
is locally H-closed. By the previous arguments, we have that each H-closed extension of
X
has a singleton remainder. Proposition 1 from
[
19
]
provides that the set of all H-closed extensions with a singleton remainder of a space
X
forms a complete sublattice of
\mathbf{H}(X)
. Hence the lattice
\mathbf{OF}(X)
is complete. Consider any free open filters
\mathcal{F}
,
\mathcal{G}
,
\mathcal{H}
on
X
. Lemmas
3.5
and
3.6
imply that the filter
\mathcal{F}\wedge(\mathcal{G}\vee\mathcal{H})
is generated by the family
\mathcal{A}=\{F\cup(G\cap H):F\in\mathcal{F},G\in\mathcal{G},H\in\mathcal{H}\}
, and the filter
(\mathcal{F}\wedge\mathcal{G})\vee(\mathcal{F}\wedge\mathcal{H})
is generated by the family
\mathcal{B}=\{(F\cup G)\cap(F\cup H):F\in\mathcal{F},G\in\mathcal{G},H\in\mathcal{H}\}
. Obviously,
\mathcal{A}=\mathcal{B}
witnessing that the lattice
\mathbf{OF}(X)
is distributive.

The implication (3)
\Rightarrow
(4) is trivial.

(4)
\Rightarrow
(1). Assume that
\mathbf{OF}(X)
is a nonempty lattice and nonetheless
X
admits two distinct free open ultrafilters
\mathcal{F}_{1},\mathcal{F}_{2}
. Then there exists
F_{1}\in\mathcal{F}_{1}
and
F_{2}\in\mathcal{F}_{2}
such that
F_{1}\cap F_{2}=\emptyset
. Since
\mathbf{OF}(X)
is a lattice
there exists a filter
\mathcal{G}\in\mathrm{OF}(X)
such that
\mathcal{F}_{1}\cup\mathcal{F}_{2}\subset\mathcal{G}
. It follows that
\emptyset=F_{1}\cap F_{2}\in\mathcal{G}
, which implies a contradiction.
∎

The latter fact and Theorem
3.8
imply the following:

Corollary 3.9
.

There exist five-element lattices which are not order isomorphic to

.

Problem 3.10
.

Characterize finite lattices which can be represented as the lattice
\mathbf{OF}(X)
for some Hausdorff space

Lemma 3.11
.

Let
X
be an almost H-closed space and
\mathcal{F}_{1},\mathcal{F}_{2}\in\mathbf{OF}(X)
. Then
\operatorname{Int}(\overline{F})\in\mathcal{F}_{2}
for each
F\in\mathcal{F}_{1}
.

Proof.

By Theorem
3.8
, the poset
\mathbf{OF}(X)
is a complete lattice. Let
\mathcal{F}_{\sup}
and
\mathcal{F}_{\inf}
be the supremum and infimum, respectively, of
\mathbf{OF}(X)
.
Note that for any
F\subset X
and open filter
\mathcal{F}
on
X
,
\overline{F}\in\mathcal{F}
if and only if
\operatorname{Int}(\overline{F})\in\mathcal{F}
.
Taking into account the latter argument, it suffices to check that for each
F\in\mathcal{F}_{\sup}
,
\overline{F}\in\mathcal{F}_{\inf}
. To derive a contradiction, assume that there exists
F\in\mathcal{F}_{\sup}
such that
\overline{F}\notin\mathcal{F}_{\inf}
. It follows that for each
H\in\mathcal{F}_{\inf}
the set
H\setminus\overline{F}
is nonempty. Then the family
\{H\setminus\overline{F}:H\in\mathcal{F}_{\inf}\}
forms a base of some free open filter on
X
which is not contained in the filter
\mathcal{F}_{\sup}
, contradiction.
∎

Corollary 3.12
.

Let
X
be an almost H-closed space and
\mathcal{U}
be the unique free open ultrafilter on
X
. Then the family
\{\operatorname{Int}(\overline{U}):U\in\mathcal{U}\}
is a base of the filter
\mathcal{F}_{\inf}
.

Proof.

Clearly, the filter
\mathcal{W}
generated by the family
\{\operatorname{Int}(\overline{U}):U\in\mathcal{U}\}
is a free open filter. Lemma
3.11
provides that the filter
\mathcal{W}
is contained in any other free open filter on
X
. Lemma
3.4
implies that
\mathcal{W}=\mathcal{F}_{\inf}
.
∎

The following lemma is useful for the investigation of finite and linear posets of free open filters.

Lemma 3.13
.

Let
\mathcal{F},\mathcal{H}
be two distinct free open filters on a space
X
such that
\mathcal{F}\subset\mathcal{H}
and there exists no open filter
\mathcal{G}
satisfying
\mathcal{F}\subsetneqq\mathcal{G}\subsetneqq\mathcal{H}
. Let
P
be any open set which belongs to
\mathcal{H}\setminus\mathcal{F}
. By
\mathcal{T}_{P}
we denote the filter on
X
generated by the family
\{F\cap(X\setminus P):F\in\mathcal{F}\}
. Then the following statements hold:

(1)

\mathcal{H}
is generated by the family
\mathcal{F}\cup\{P\}
;

(2)

\mathcal{F}=\{T\cup H:T\in\mathcal{T}_{P}
and
H\in\mathcal{H}\}
;

(3)

\mathcal{T}_{P}
traces a free open ultrafilter on
X\setminus P
;

(4)

if for any open filter
\mathcal{G}
on
X
the inclusion
\mathcal{F}\subsetneqq\mathcal{G}
implies that
\mathcal{H}\subset\mathcal{G}
, then
\mathcal{F}
is generated by the family
\{U\subset X:U
is open and
U\cap(X\setminus P)\in\mathcal{T}_{P}\}
.

If, moreover, the space
X
is almost H-closed, then

(5)

\mathcal{T}_{P}
traces an open ultrafilter on
\overline{P}\setminus P
.

Proof.

1. Observe that the filter
\mathcal{G}
generated by the family
\mathcal{F}\cup\{P\}
is open and satisfies
\mathcal{F}\subset\mathcal{G}\subset\mathcal{H}
, which implies that either
\mathcal{G}=\mathcal{F}
or
\mathcal{G}=\mathcal{H}
. Since
P\notin\mathcal{F}
, we deduce that
\mathcal{G}=\mathcal{H}
.

2. Let
\mathcal{Z}
be the filter generated by the family
\{T\cup H:T\in\mathcal{T}_{P},H\in\mathcal{H}\}
. Consider any
Z\in\mathcal{Z}
. By the definition of
\mathcal{Z}
, there exist
T\in\mathcal{T}_{P}
and
H\in\mathcal{H}
such that
T\cup H\subset Z
. By item (1), the filter
\mathcal{H}
is generated by the family
\mathcal{F}\cup\{P\}
. It follows that there exists an element
F\in\mathcal{F}
such that
F\cap P\subset H
. By the definition of
\mathcal{T}_{P}
, there exists
G\in\mathcal{F}
such that
G\subset F
and
G\cap(X\setminus P)\subset T
. The choice of
F
implies that
G\cap P\subset F\cap P\subset H
. Thus,
G\subset T\cup H\subset Z
witnessing that
\mathcal{Z}\subset\mathcal{F}
.
To show the converse inclusion, fix any
F\in\mathcal{F}
. Put
H=F\cap P\in\mathcal{H}
and
T=F\cap(X\setminus P)\in\mathcal{T}_{P}
. Obviously,
T\cup H\in\mathcal{Z}
and
T\cup H=F
witnessing that
\mathcal{Z}=\mathcal{F}
.

3. Clearly,
\mathcal{T}_{P}
traces a free open filter on
X\setminus P
. To derive a contradiction, assume that the trace of
\mathcal{T}_{P}
on
X\setminus P
is properly contained in an open filter
\mathcal{U}
on
X\setminus P
. It follows that there exists a set
U\in\mathcal{U}\setminus\mathcal{T}_{P}
which is open in
X\setminus P
. Then there exists an open set
V\subset X
such that
V\cap(X\setminus P)=U
. Let
W=V\cup P
. Consider the filter
\mathcal{Z}
generated by the family
\mathcal{F}\cup\{W\}
. Since the set
W
is open we get that
\mathcal{Z}
is an open filter. By the definition of
\mathcal{Z}
,
\mathcal{F}\subsetneqq\mathcal{Z}\subsetneqq\mathcal{H}
which implies a contradiction.

4. Assume that for any open filter
\mathcal{G}
on
X
the inclusion
\mathcal{F}\subsetneqq\mathcal{G}
implies that
\mathcal{H}\subset\mathcal{G}
. Consider the filter
\mathcal{Z}
which is generated by the family
\{U\subset X:U
is open and
U\cap(X\setminus P)\in\mathcal{T}_{P}\}
. Obviously the filter
\mathcal{Z}
is open and
\mathcal{F}\subset\mathcal{Z}
. If
\mathcal{Z}\neq\mathcal{F}
, the assumption of the statement implies that
\mathcal{H}\subset\mathcal{Z}
. But this is not possible, since
P\notin\mathcal{Z}
. Hence
\mathcal{Z}=\mathcal{F}
.

5. By Lemma
3.11
,
\overline{P}\setminus P\in\mathcal{T}_{P}
. At this point item (5) follows from item (3).
∎

A
character
of a filter
\mathcal{F}
is the cardinal
\chi(\mathcal{F})=\min\{|\mathcal{B}|:\mathcal{B}
is a base of
\mathcal{F}\}
.
Recall that

\mathfrak{u}=\min\{\chi(\mathcal{F}):\mathcal{F}\hbox{ is a nonprincipal ultrafilter on }\omega\}.

A space
X
is called
ccc
if every pairwise disjoint family of open subsets of
X
is countable.
We finish this chapter with an auxiliary proposition about the character of open ultrafilters.

Proposition 3.14
.

Let
\mathcal{F}
be a free open ultrafilter on a ccc space
X
. Then
\chi(\mathcal{F})\geq\mathfrak{u}
.

Proof.

First we construct a special countable family of pairwise disjoint open subsets of
X
. Let
U_{0}
be any open subset of
X
which doesn’t belong to
\mathcal{F}
. Assume that we already constructed pairwise disjoint open subsets
U_{\alpha}
,
\alpha\in\xi\in\omega_{1}
such that for any
\delta\in\xi
,
\bigcup_{\alpha\in\delta}U_{\alpha}\notin\mathcal{F}
. If
\bigcup_{\alpha\in\xi}U_{\alpha}\in\mathcal{F}
, then
\{U_{\alpha}:\alpha\in\xi\}
is the desired family. Otherwise, an open set
T=X\setminus\overline{\bigcup_{\alpha\in\xi}U_{\alpha}}
is nonempty, as it belongs to
\mathcal{F}
. Pick any
x\in T
. Since the filter
\mathcal{F}
is free, there exists an open neighborhood
U(x)\subset T
of
x
such that
U(x)\notin\mathcal{F}
. Then put
U_{\xi}=U(x)
. Note that the ccc property of the space
X
ensures that our induction will stop at some countable limit step, i.e. there exists a countable limit ordinal
\theta
such that
\bigcup_{\alpha\in\theta}U_{\alpha}\in\mathcal{F}
. Enumerate the family
\{U_{\alpha}:\alpha\in\theta\}
as
\{V_{n}:n\in\omega\}
. For any
F\in\mathcal{F}
consider the set
H_{F}=\{n\in\omega:F\cap V_{n}\neq\emptyset\}
. Observe that
\mathcal{H}=\{H_{F}:F\in\mathcal{F}\}
is the filter on
\omega
. Pick any subset
A\subset\omega
. Lemma
3.1
implies that either
\bigcup_{n\in A}V_{n}\in\mathcal{F}
or
\bigcup_{n\in\omega\setminus A}V_{n}\in\mathcal{F}
. In the first case the set
\bigcup_{n\in A}V_{n}\in\mathcal{F}
is a witness for
A\in\mathcal{H}
. In the second case the set
\bigcup_{n\in\omega\setminus A}V_{n}\in\mathcal{F}
is a witness for
\omega\setminus A\in\mathcal{H}
. Hence
\mathcal{H}
is an ultrafilter. Observe that for any base
\mathcal{B}
of
\mathcal{F}
the family
\{H_{B}:B\in\mathcal{B}\}
is a base of
\mathcal{H}
. Since
\chi(\mathcal{H})\geq\mathfrak{u}
we get that
\chi(\mathcal{F})\geq\mathfrak{u}
.
∎

4.
Linearly ordered lattices of free open filters

In what follows, ordinals are assumed to carry the discrete topology, unless stated otherwise.
If
X
is a discrete space, then
\beta(X)
can be described as the set of all ultrafilters on
X
endowed with the topology
\tau
generated by the base
\mathcal{B}=\{\langle A\rangle:A\subseteq X\}
, where
\langle A\rangle=\{u\in\beta(X):A\in u\}
. Recall that each element
x\in X
is identified with the corresponding principal ultrafilter. Further we refer only to the aforementioned representation of
\beta(X)
.
A space
X
is called
scattered
if each nonempty subspace of
X
contains an isolated point. A
height
of a space
X
is the minimal ordinal
ht(X)
such that the
ht(X)
-th Cantor-Bendixson derivative of
X
is empty.
Recall that Cantor-Bendixson derivatives of a scattered space
X
are defined by transfinite induction as follows, where
X^{\prime}
is the set of all accumulation points of
X
:

•

X^{0}=X
;

•

X^{\alpha+1}=\big(X^{\alpha}\big)^{\prime}
;

•

X^{\alpha}=\bigcap_{\beta<\alpha}X^{\beta}
, if
\alpha
is a limit ordinal.

The set
X^{\alpha}\setminus X^{\alpha+1}
is called the
\alpha
-th
Cantor-Bendixson level
of
X
and is denoted by
X^{(\alpha)}
.

A subset
X
of a space
Y
is called
strongly discrete
if there exists a family
(U_{x})_{x\in X}
of open pairwise disjoint subsets of
Y
such that
U_{x}\cap X=\{x\}
for every
x\in X
.
The following lemma is a folklore.
Nonetheless, we give an easy proof of it.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
X=\{x_{\alpha}\}_{\alpha\in\lambda}
be a strongly discrete subset of
\beta(\kappa)
. Then there exists a homeomorphism
h:\overline{X}\to\beta(\lambda)
such that
h(x_{\alpha})=\alpha
for each
\alpha\in\lambda
.

Proof.

Since the set
X
is strongly discrete there exists a family
\{A_{\alpha}:\alpha\in\lambda\}
of open pairwise disjoint subsets of
\beta(\kappa)
such that
X\cap A_{\alpha}=\{x_{\alpha}\}
for any
\alpha\in\lambda
.
Fix any function
f:X\rightarrow[0,1]
. Define the function
f_{1}:\kappa\rightarrow[0,1]
as follows:

f_{1}(\xi)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{cl}f(x_{\alpha}),&\hbox{if~~}\xi\in A_{\alpha}\cap\kappa;\\
0,&\hbox{if~~}\xi\in\kappa\setminus(\bigcup_{\alpha\in\lambda}A_{\alpha}).\end{array}\right.

By the definition of
\beta(\kappa)
, there exists a continuous extension
\hat{f_{1}}:\beta(\kappa)\rightarrow[0,1]
of
f_{1}
. Let
g=\hat{f_{1}}{\restriction}_{\overline{X}}
. Since
\hat{f_{1}}(x_{\alpha})=f(x_{\alpha})
,
g
is a continuous extension of
f
. Hence every function
f:X\rightarrow[0,1]
can be extended to a continuous function
g:\overline{X}\rightarrow[0,1]
. Corollary 3.6.3 from
[
16
]
yields a homeomorphism
h:\overline{X}\to\beta(X)
which fixes points of
X
. Since
X
is a discrete set of cardinality
\lambda
, we can identify
\beta(X)
and
\beta(\lambda)
.
∎

Let
\rho
be an equivalence relation on a set
X
. Then for each
x\in X
such that
x\rho y
if and only if
x=y
, we denote the equivalence class
\{x\}
simply by
x
.
The following scheme was invented by Mooney
[
30
]
and it will be crucial in constructing almost H-closed spaces with certain properties of their lattices of free open filters.

Construction 1

(Mooney
[
30
]
)
.

Let
X
be a non-H-closed topological space,
x^{*}
be a non-isolated point of
X
, and
K(X)
be the Katetov extension of
X
(see Definition
3.2
).

Consider the Tychonoff product
K(X){\times}\{0,1\}
where the set
\{0,1\}
carries the discrete topology. Let
Y
be the quotient space
(K(X){\times}\{0,1\})/\rho
where the equivalence relation
\rho
is defined as follows:
(a,i)\sim(b,j)
if and only if
a=b
and
i=j
, or
a=b\in K(X)\setminus X
. Finally, put
\mathrm{M}(X,x^{*})=Y\setminus\{(x^{*},0)\}
.
One can easily check that the space
\mathrm{M}(X,x^{*})
is scattered if and only if
X
is scattered.

An open filter
\mathcal{F}
on a space
X
is called
regular open
if the family
\{\operatorname{Int}(\overline{F}):F\in\mathcal{F}\}
forms a base of
\mathcal{F}
.

Proposition 4.2
.

Let
\mathcal{F}
be a free regular open filter on a space
X
. Then there exists a space
Y
such that
\mathbf{OF}(Y)
is order isomorphic to the subposet
\{\mathcal{G}\in\mathbf{OF}(X):\mathcal{F}\subseteq\mathcal{G}\}
of
\mathbf{OF}(X)
.

Proof.

By
X_{\mathcal{F}}
we denote the space
X
with an attached singleton
\mathcal{F}
, whose open neighborhood base consists of the sets
F\cup\{\mathcal{F}\}
, where
F\in\mathcal{F}
is open. Let
Y=\mathrm{M}(X_{\mathcal{F}},\mathcal{F})
(see Construction
1
).

Consider any free open filter
\Phi
on
Y
. Note that the subspace
K(X_{\mathcal{F}}){\times}\{1\}
of
Y
, being homeomorphic to
K(X_{\mathcal{F}})
, is H-closed. So,
Y\setminus(K(X_{\mathcal{F}}){\times}\{1\})=X{\times}\{0\}\in\Phi
.
This allows us to define the map
g:\mathbf{OF}(Y)\to\mathbf{OF}(X)
by
g(\Phi)=\{G\subseteq X:G{\times}\{0\}\in\Phi\}
. It is easy to see that the map
g
is injective and order preserving.
Seeking a contradiction, assume that there exists
\Phi\in\mathbf{OF}(Y)
such that
\mathcal{F}\setminus g({\Phi})\neq\emptyset
. Then, taking into account that the filter
\mathcal{F}
is regular open, there exists a set
F=\operatorname{Int}(\overline{F})\in\mathcal{F}
such that
F\notin g(\Phi)
.
Therefore
\overline{F}\notin g(\Phi)
, because otherwise we would have

F=\operatorname{Int}(\overline{F})\in g(\Phi)
, which we assumed to be not the case.
Hence
H\cap((X\setminus\overline{F}){\times}\{0\})\neq\emptyset
for each
H\in\Phi
. Let
\mathcal{U}
be any free open ultrafilter on
Y
such that
\Phi\cup\{(X\setminus\overline{F}){\times}\{0\}\}\subset\mathcal{U}
. It is easy to see that
(\mathcal{U},1)\in Y
is an accumulation point of the filter
\Phi
, which yields a contradiction. Hence
\mathcal{F}\subseteq g(\Phi)
for every
\Phi\in\mathbf{OF}(Y)
. If
\mathcal{G}
is a free open filter on
X
such that
\mathcal{F}\subseteq\mathcal{G}
, then let
\Phi_{\mathcal{G}}
be the filter on
Y
generated by the family
\{G{\times}\{0\}:G\in\mathcal{G}\}
. Obviously,
\Phi_{\mathcal{G}}\in\mathbf{OF}(Y)
and
g(\Phi_{\mathcal{G}})=\mathcal{G}
, which completes the proof.
∎

The following definition is crucial for constructing spaces with linear lattices of free open filters.

Definition 4.3
.

Let
\mathcal{F}
be an ultrafilter on an infinite cardinal
\kappa
. We write that
\mathfrak{h}(\mathcal{F})>\alpha
(and say that the
height
of
\mathcal{F}
is greater than
\alpha
) if there exists a scattered space
X\subset\beta(\kappa)
satisfying the following conditions:

(1)

\{\mathcal{F}\}=X^{(\alpha)}
and for any
\xi\in\alpha
the filter
\mathcal{N}(\mathcal{F})
traces on
X^{(\xi)}
an ultrafilter
\mathcal{F}_{\xi}
;

(2)

for every
0<\gamma\leq\alpha
and for each selector
\langle F_{\xi}\in\mathcal{F}_{\xi}:\xi\in\gamma\rangle
such that the set
\bigcup_{\xi\in\gamma}F_{\xi}
is open in
X
, there exists
F\in\mathcal{F}
satisfying
\langle F\rangle\cap\bigcup_{\xi\in\gamma}X^{(\xi)}\subseteq\bigcup_{\xi\in\gamma}F_{\xi}
.

For a given scattered
space
X
witnessing that
\mathfrak{h}(\mathcal{F})>\alpha
, and fixed
\xi<\alpha
, one may form the
scattered subspace

Y=\bigcup_{\gamma\in\xi}X^{(\gamma)}\cup\{\mathcal{F}\}
of
X
, which witnesses that
\mathfrak{h}(\mathcal{F})>\xi
. Hence the following is well defined:

\mathfrak{h}(\mathcal{F})=\alpha
if
\mathfrak{h}(\mathcal{F})>\xi
for any
\xi\in\alpha
, but
\lnot(\mathfrak{h}(\mathcal{F})>\alpha)
.

Clearly,
\mathfrak{h}(\mathcal{F})=1
if and only if
\mathcal{F}
is a principal ultrafilter. The next lemma shows that consistently there exist ultrafilters of height 2.

Lemma 4.4
.

\mathfrak{h}(\mathcal{F})=2
for each P-point
\mathcal{F}\in\omega^{*}
.

Proof.

To derive a contradiction, assume that there exists a scattered subspace
X\subset\beta(\omega)
and a P-point
\mathcal{F}\in\omega^{*}
such that
\mathcal{F}\in X^{(2)}
and
\mathcal{F}
traces on the set
X^{(1)}
an ultrafilter which we denote by
\mathcal{F}_{1}
. Consider any countable family
\{H_{n}:n\in\omega\}\subset\mathcal{F}_{1}
. By the definition of
\mathcal{F}_{1}
, for every
n\in\omega
there exists
F_{n}\in\mathcal{F}
such that
\langle F_{n}\rangle\cap X^{(1)}\subset H_{n}
. Since
\mathcal{F}
is a P-point, there exists
F\in\mathcal{F}
such that
F\subset^{*}F_{n}
for any
n\in\omega
. Taking into account that
X^{(1)}
consists of free ultrafilters, we obtain that
\langle F\rangle\cap X^{(1)}\subset\langle F_{n}\rangle\cap X^{(1)}\subset H_{n}
for every
n\in\omega
. Thus,
\langle F\rangle\cap X^{(1)}\subset\bigcap_{n\in\omega}H_{n}
, witnessing that the ultrafilter
\mathcal{F}_{1}
is
\omega_{1}
-complete. By
[
20
, Lemma 10.2]
, the cardinality of the set
X^{(1)}
is greater or equal to some measurable cardinal. On the other hand,
|X^{(1)}|\leq\mathfrak{c}
which implies a contradiction.
∎

Note that the above lemma does not immediately follow from the fact that P-points are not in the closure of any countable set, as
X^{(1)}
can be uncountable.

For an ordinal
\alpha
, let
S^{*}(\alpha)=\alpha
if
\alpha
is finite and
S^{*}(\alpha)=\alpha+1
otherwise. Let us note that
S^{*}(\alpha)
equals the order type of the set
\{\delta:0<\delta<\alpha+1\}
.
The following two results justify Definition
4.3
.

Proposition 4.5
.

Let
\mathcal{F}
be an ultrafilter on
\kappa
of height
>\alpha
and
X
be a scattered subspace of
\beta(\kappa)
, which witnesses
\mathfrak{h}(\mathcal{F})>\alpha
. Then
\mathcal{N}(\mathcal{F})
traces on
Y=X\setminus\{\mathcal{F}\}
a free regular open filter
\Phi
, and the subposet
\{\mathcal{G}\in\mathbf{OF}(Y):\Phi\subseteq\mathcal{G}\}
of
\mathbf{OF}(Y)
is order isomorphic to the ordinal
S^{*}(\alpha)
endowed with the reversed order.

Proof.

Since the space
X
is regular and
\mathcal{F}\notin Y
,
\Phi
is a free regular open filter. For each nonzero ordinal
\delta<\alpha+1
let
\mathcal{G}_{\delta}
be the filter on
Y
generated by the family
\Phi\cup\{\bigcup_{\xi\in\delta}X^{(\xi)}\}
. Since the space
Y
is scattered, for each
0<\delta<\alpha+1
the set
\bigcup_{\xi\in\delta}X^{(\xi)}
is open. Thus, for every
0<\delta<\alpha+1
the filter
\mathcal{G}_{\delta}
is open. Since
\Phi
is a free filter, we get that so are the filters
\mathcal{G}_{\delta}
, where
0<\delta<\alpha+1
.

Let
\mathcal{G}
be an arbitrary free open filter on
Y
such that
\Phi\subseteq\mathcal{G}
. Put

\delta=\min\{\xi\in\alpha+1:\hbox{exists }G\in\mathcal{G}\hbox{ such that }G\cap X^{(\xi)}=\emptyset\}.

Since
X^{(0)}
is an open dense subset of
Y
and the filter
\mathcal{G}
is open,
G\cap X^{(0)}\neq\emptyset
for all
G\in\mathcal{G}
. Therefore,
\delta>0
.
We claim that
\mathcal{G}=\mathcal{G}_{\delta}
. The inclusion
\mathcal{G}_{\delta}\subseteq\mathcal{G}
follows from the definition of
\delta
. In order to show the converse inclusion, fix any
G\in\mathcal{G}
. Since the filter
\mathcal{G}
is open and by the definition of
\delta
, we lose no generality assuming that the set
G
is open and
G\subseteq\bigcup_{\xi\in\delta}X^{(\xi)}
. Since
\Phi\subseteq\mathcal{G}
, for every
\xi\in\delta
the trace of
\mathcal{G}
on
X^{(\xi)}
is the ultrafilter
\mathcal{F}_{\xi}
(see Definition
4.3
). Then for each
\xi\in\delta
the set
F_{\xi}=G\cap X^{(\xi)}\in\mathcal{F}_{\xi}
. Since
\mathfrak{h}(\mathcal{F})>\alpha
(see condition (2) in Definition
4.3
), there exists
F\in\mathcal{F}
such that
\langle F\rangle\cap\bigcup_{\xi\in\delta}X^{(\xi)}\subset G
.
It remains to observe that
\langle F\rangle\in\mathcal{N}(\mathcal{F})
,
\langle F\rangle\cap Y\in\Phi
and

G\supseteq\langle F\rangle\cap\bigcup_{\xi\in\delta}X^{(\xi)}=\langle F\rangle\cap Y\cap\bigcup_{\xi\in\delta}X^{(\xi)}\in\mathcal{G}_{\delta}.

Thus,
G\in\mathcal{G}_{\delta}
which implies that
\mathcal{G}=\mathcal{G}_{\delta}
. Hence

\{\mathcal{G}\in\mathbf{OF}(Y):\mathcal{N}(\mathcal{F})\subseteq\mathcal{G}\}=\{\mathcal{G}_{\delta}:0<\delta<\alpha+1\}.

It is easy to see that the subposet
\{\mathcal{G}_{\delta}:0<\delta<\alpha+1\}\subset\mathbf{OF}(Y)
is order isomorphic to
(S^{*}(\alpha),\geq)
.
∎

.

For each positive integer

there exists an ultrafilter
\mathcal{F}
on

Proof.

Fix any positive integer

. First we inductively construct a scattered subspace

. Let

. Assume that an infinite countable discrete subset

to be any countable infinite discrete subset of

, for each

A subset

Theorem 4.10
.

(CH) There exists an ultrafilter
\mathcal{F}
on
\omega
such that
\mathfrak{h}(\mathcal{F})>\omega
.

Proof.

Observe that in the proof of Theorem
4.7

for every
n\in\mathbb{N}
we constructed a countable scattered subspace
X_{n}\subset\beta(\omega)
which satisfies the following conditions:

•

ht(X_{n})=n+1
and
X_{n}^{(n)}=\{\mathcal{F}_{n}\}
;

•

For each
m\in n
the Cantor-Bendixson level

is strongly discrete, implying that the filter
\mathcal{N}(\mathcal{F}_{n})
traces on

an ultrafilter.

Consider any partition
\{P_{n}:n\in\omega\}
of
\omega
into disjoint infinite subsets. For each
n\in\omega
,
X_{n}
is homeomorphic to a subspace of
\langle P_{n}\rangle\cong\beta(\omega)
.
Let
Y
be a topological sum of
\{X_{n}:n\in\omega\}
.
Identify
Y
with a subspace of
\beta(\omega)
such that
X_{n}\subset\langle P_{n}\rangle
.
Using CH, enumerate the set of all high open subsets of
Y
as
\{U_{\alpha}:\alpha\in\omega_{1}\}
. Moreover, we assume that each high open subset of
Y
appears cofinally many times in the enumeration. Also, enumerate all subsets of
\omega
as
\{B_{\alpha}:\alpha\in\omega_{1}\}
. The desired ultrafilter
\mathcal{F}
on
\omega
will be constructed by recursion of length
\omega_{1}
. Fix any
\beta\in\omega_{1}
and assume that we already constructed a family
\mathcal{V}_{\beta}=\{V_{\alpha}:\alpha\in\beta\}
of subsets of
\omega
which satisfies the following conditions:

(a)

the family
\mathcal{V}_{\beta}
is centered and, thus, generates a filter which we denote by
\mathcal{W}_{\beta}
;

(b)

for any
\alpha\in\beta
either
B_{\alpha}\in\mathcal{W}_{\beta}
or
\omega\setminus B_{\alpha}\in\mathcal{W}_{\beta}
;

(c)

for any

the set
\langle W\rangle
is high in
Y
, i.e.,
\langle W\rangle\cap Y^{(m)}\neq\emptyset
for any

.

The filter
\mathcal{W}_{\beta}
is an approximation of the desired filter
\mathcal{F}
. At stage
\beta
two cases are possible:

(i)

for any
n\in\omega
there exists
W_{n}\in\mathcal{W}_{\beta}
such that
\langle W_{n}\rangle\cap Y^{(n)}\subset U_{\beta}
;

(ii)

there exists
n\in\omega
such that
\langle W\rangle\cap(Y^{(n)}\setminus U_{\beta})\neq\emptyset
for any
W\in\mathcal{W}_{\beta}
.

If case (ii) holds, then put
V_{\beta}=\omega
. Informally speaking the set
U_{\beta}
is at this stage “irrelevant” for the filter
\mathcal{W}_{\beta}
.

Assume that case (i) holds. Since the ordinal
\beta
is countable the filter
\mathcal{W}_{\beta}
admits a countable nested base
\mathcal{B}=\{C_{n}:n\in\omega\}
, i.e.,
C_{n}\subset C_{m}

The arguments above imply that

. The set

is nonempty, because otherwise there would exist elements

,

, which is impossible. Since for each

on

For each

The following result was proved by Blass in his PhD-thesis.

Lemma 5.1
.

For ultrafilters

and

and

.

Let

Lemma 5.3

Lemma 5.5
.

Let

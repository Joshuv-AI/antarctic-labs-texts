Abstract
We will show that the 3-4-1 model with heavy right-handed neutrinos can explain the recent experimental data of ( g − 2 ) e , μ (g-2)_{e,\mu} anomalies of charged leptons and neutrino oscillations through the inverse seesaw mechanism. In addition, the model can predict large lepton flavor violating decay rates μ → e ​ γ \mu\to e\gamma and τ → μ ​ γ , e ​ γ \tau\to\mu\gamma,e\gamma up to the recent experimental sensitivities.

2.1
Yukawa couplings and masses for fermions

In this work, we will study the 3-4-1 model with heavy right-handed neutrinos and new singly charged leptons assigned in the three left-handed quadruplets
[
6
,
11
]
. This model is constructed based on the gauge symmetry
SU(3)_{c}\times SU(4)_{L}\times U(1)_{X}
, implying 16 electroweak (EW) gauge bosons. In addition, there are 4 neutral gauge bosons corresponding to the 4 diagonal generators of the EW group. Normally, the EW group is assumed to break to the final electric group through the following pattern:
SU(4)_{L}\times U(1)_{X}\to SU(3)_{L}\times U(1)_{X^{\prime}}\to SU(2)\times U(1)_{Y}\to U(1)_{em}
, i.e, the 3-4-1 model can be considered as the extended version of 3-3-1 models
[
113
,
114
]
. The electric operator is defined as follows:

Q=T_{3}+\frac{1}{\sqrt{3}}T_{8}-\frac{2}{\sqrt{6}}T_{15}+X\mathbb{I},

(4)

where
T_{3,8,15}
are the diagonal generators of the
SU(4)
group and
X
is the
U(1)
charge, see precise forms corresponding to the quadruplet in Ref.
[
115
]
, for example.

The lepton sector consists of three left-handed quadruplets and respective right-handed singlets, namely

\displaystyle L_{a}=(\nu^{\prime}_{a}\,,e^{\prime}_{a}\,,E^{\prime}_{a}\,,N^{\prime}_{a})_{L}^{T}\sim\left(1,4,-\frac{1}{2}\right)\,,\;a=1,2,3,

\displaystyle e^{\prime}_{aR},\;E^{\prime}_{aR}\sim(1,1,-1),\hskip 14.22636pt\nu^{\prime}_{aR},\;N^{\prime}_{aR}\sim(1,1,0).

(5)

\displaystyle\chi

\displaystyle\phi

\displaystyle=\left(\phi_{1}^{+}\,,\phi_{2}^{0}\,,\phi_{3}^{0}\,,\phi_{4}^{+}\right)^{T}\,\sim\left(1,4,\frac{1}{2}\right),\;\langle\phi\rangle=\left(0\,,0\,,\frac{\omega}{\sqrt{2}}\,,0\right)^{T},

\displaystyle\rho

\displaystyle=\left(\rho_{1}^{+}\,,\rho_{2}^{0}\,,\rho_{3}^{0}\,,\rho_{4}^{+}\right)^{T}\sim\left(1,4,\frac{1}{2}\right),\;\langle\rho\rangle=\left(0\,,\frac{v_{1}}{\sqrt{2}}\,,0\,,0\right)^{T},

\displaystyle\eta

\displaystyle=\left(\eta_{1}^{0}\,,\eta_{2}^{-}\,,\eta_{3}^{-}\,,\eta_{4}^{0}\right)^{T}\,\sim\left(1,4,-\frac{1}{2}\right),\;\langle\eta\rangle=\left(\frac{v_{2}}{\sqrt{2}}\,,0\,,0\,,0\right)^{T}.

\displaystyle-\mathcal{L}_{y}=

\displaystyle\;Y^{N}_{ab}\overline{L_{a}}\chi N^{\prime}_{bR}+Y^{E}_{ab}\overline{L_{a}}\phi E^{\prime}_{bR}+Y^{e}_{ab}\overline{L_{a}}\rho e^{\prime}_{bR}+Y^{\nu}_{ab}\overline{L_{a}}\eta\nu^{\prime}_{bR}+\mathrm{H.c.}.

2.2
Gauge boson masses and mixing

Gauge boson masses arise from the covariant kinetic term of Higgs multiplets, namely

L_{\mathrm{Higgs}}=\sum_{H}^{4}\left(D^{\mu}\langle H\rangle\right)^{\dagger}D_{\mu}\langle H\rangle,

(15)

where
H=\chi,\phi,\eta,\rho
. The covariant derivative is defined as

\displaystyle D_{\mu}

\displaystyle=\partial_{\mu}-ig\sum_{a=1}^{15}W_{a\mu}T_{a}-ig_{X}XB^{\prime\prime}_{\mu}T_{16}\equiv\partial_{\mu}-igP_{\mu}^{NC}-igP_{\mu}^{CC}\,,

(16)

where
g,g_{X}
and
W_{a\mu},B^{\prime\prime}_{\mu}
are gauge couplings and fields of the gauge groups
SU(4)_{L}
and
U(1)_{X}
, respectively. The two parts
P_{\mu}^{NC}
and
P_{\mu}^{CC}
relate to the neutral and non-hermitian currents
[
12
]
.
For quadruplet,
T_{16}=\frac{1}{2\sqrt{2}}\textrm{diag}(1,1,1,1)
and

\displaystyle P_{\mu}^{CC}=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\left(\begin{array}[]{cccc}0&W^{+}&W_{13}^{+}&W_{14}^{0}\\
W^{-}&0&W_{23}^{0}&W_{24}^{-}\\
W_{13}^{-}&W_{23}^{0*}&0&W_{34}^{-}\\
W_{14}^{0*}&W_{24}^{+}&W_{34}^{+}&0\end{array}\,\right)_{\mu}\,,

where
t\equiv g_{X}/g
and
\sqrt{2}\,W^{\mu}_{ij}\equiv W^{\mu}_{i}-iW^{\mu}_{j}
with
i<j
.
The upper subscripts label the electric charges of gauge bosons. The relation between the original basis
(W_{3},\;W_{8},W_{15},B^{\prime\prime})
and the mass basis
(A,Z,Z_{3},Z_{4})
of all real neutral boson was determined previously
[
12
]
.
The masses of non-Hermitian (charged) gauge bosons are given by

\displaystyle m^{2}_{W}

\displaystyle=\frac{g^{2}(v_{1}^{2}+v_{2}^{2})}{4}\,,m^{2}_{W_{13}}=\frac{g^{2}(v_{2}^{2}+\omega^{2})}{4}\,,m^{2}_{W_{23}}=\frac{g^{2}(v_{1}^{2}+\omega^{2})}{4}\,,

\displaystyle m^{2}_{W_{14}}

\displaystyle=\frac{g^{2}(v_{2}^{2}+V^{2})}{4}\,,m^{2}_{W_{24}}=\frac{g^{2}(v_{1}^{2}+V^{2})}{4}\,,m^{2}_{W_{34}}=\frac{g^{2}(\omega^{2}+V^{2})}{4}\,.

(21)

By spontaneous symmetry breaking (SSB), the following relation should be in order:

V\gg\omega\gg v_{1},v_{2}
. A consequence from Eq. (
21
) is that
W^{\pm}
must be identified with the singly charged SM gauge boson, namely

v_{1}^{2}+v_{2}^{2}=v^{2}=246^{2}\;\textrm{GeV}^{2}.

(22)

Then neutral gauge boson masses are

\displaystyle m^{2}_{Z}

\displaystyle\simeq\frac{m^{2}_{W}}{c^{2}_{W}},\;m^{2}_{Z_{3}}\simeq\frac{4g^{2}c_{W}^{2}w^{2}}{3(1-4s_{W}^{2})},\;m^{2}_{Z_{4}}\simeq\frac{g^{2}(9V^{2}+w^{2})}{6}.

(23)

The above formula implies that
\eta
and
\rho
play roles as two Higgs doublets in the well-known two Higgs doublet models after the breaking steps to the SM gauge group
SU(2)_{L}\times U(1)_{Y}
. Then we define the mixing angle
\beta
as follows

t_{\beta}\equiv\tan\beta=\frac{v_{2}}{v_{1}},\;v_{1}=vc_{\beta},\;v_{2}=vs_{\beta},

(24)

where
t_{\beta}\geq 0.4
as the perturbative limit of the top quark Yukawa coupling
|Y^{u}_{33}|\simeq\sqrt{2}m_{t}/(vs_{\beta})<\sqrt{4\pi}
. The upper bound of
t_{\beta}
may comes from the tau mass
Y^{\tau}\simeq\sqrt{2}m_{\tau}/(vc_{\beta})<\sqrt{4\pi}
, equivalently
t_{\beta}<390
.

The mixing parameters of the neutral gauge bosons were summarized in appendix
B
, see the details in Ref.
[
12
]
. Because the current study
[

]
shows that the breaking scale of
SU(3)_{L}
and
SU(L)_{L}
is the order of TeV, the mixing parameters between
Z
,
Z_{3}
, and
Z_{4}
are much small, therefore we ignore in this work. But the loop corrections to the

\displaystyle V_{h}=

\displaystyle\;V(\eta,\rho,\phi,\chi)+V(\sigma^{\pm}),

\displaystyle V(\eta,\rho,\phi,\chi)=

\displaystyle\;\mu^{2}_{1}\eta^{\dagger}\eta+\mu^{2}_{2}\rho^{\dagger}\rho+\mu^{2}_{3}\phi^{\dagger}\phi+\mu^{2}_{4}\chi^{\dagger}\chi

\displaystyle+\lambda_{1}(\eta^{\dagger}\eta)^{2}+\lambda_{2}(\rho^{\dagger}\rho)^{2}+\lambda_{3}(\phi^{\dagger}\phi)^{2}+\lambda_{4}(\chi^{\dagger}\chi)^{2}

\displaystyle+(\eta^{\dagger}\eta)[\lambda_{5}(\rho^{\dagger}\rho)+\lambda_{6}(\phi^{\dagger}\phi)+\lambda_{7}(\chi^{\dagger}\chi)]

Conclusion

We have discussed a solution to explain the recent experimental data of the
(g-2)_{e_{a}}
anomalies in the 341ISS framework. We have constructed the Yukawa Lagrangian of leptons and Higgs potential obeying the generalized lepton number
\mathcal{L}
that keeps necessary terms generating the ISS mechanism and large chirally-enhanced one-loop contributions to
(g-2)_{e_{a}}
anomalies. Although the ISS mechanism may result in large cLFV decays
e_{b}\to e_{a}\gamma
, we have shown numerically that there always exist allowed regions of the parameter space guaranteeing these experimental bounds. In addition, these allowed regions will not be excluded totally if the future sensitivities of the cLFV experiments are updated, and no cLFV significations are found. The model can also explain successfully the existence of at least one of the cLFV decays
\tau\to\mu\gamma,e\gamma
, or
\mu\to e\gamma
once they are detected by incoming experiments.

Appendix A
The SM-like Higgs boson

For simplicity in estimating the SM-like Higgs boson
h
found by LHC, we assume some conditions of the Higgs self-couplings as follows:

\displaystyle\lambda_{6}+\frac{fV}{2t_{\beta}w},\;\lambda_{7}+\frac{fw}{2t_{\beta}V},\lambda_{8}+\frac{ft_{\beta}V}{2w},\lambda_{9}+\frac{fwt_{\beta}}{2V}\simeq 0.

(80)

As a result, the SM-like Higgs boson gets dominant contributions from the neutral Higgs basis
(\mathrm{Re}[\rho^{0}_{2}],\;\mathrm{Re}[\eta^{0}_{1}])
, corresponding to the following squared mass matrix:

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}^{2}_{h}=\begin{pmatrix}2\lambda_{2}c^{2}_{\beta}v^{2}-\frac{ft_{\beta}Vw}{2}&\lambda_{5}s_{\beta}c_{\beta}v^{2}+\frac{fVw}{2}\\
\lambda_{5}s_{\beta}c_{\beta}v^{2}+\frac{fVw}{2}&2\lambda_{1}s^{2}_{\beta}v^{2}-\frac{fVw}{2t_{\beta}}\end{pmatrix},

which results in one light CP-even neutral Higgs boson with mass

m_{h}^{2}=2\frac{\mathcal{M}^{2}_{h,11}\mathcal{M}^{2}_{h,22}-\left(\mathcal{M}^{2}_{h,12}\right)^{2}}{\mathcal{M}^{2}_{h,11}+\mathcal{M}^{2}_{h,22}+\sqrt{\left(\mathcal{M}^{2}_{h,11}-\mathcal{M}^{2}_{h,22}\right)^{2}}+4\left(\mathcal{M}^{2}_{h,12}\right)^{2}}\varpropto\mathcal{O}(v^{2}).

(81)

Similarly the 2HDM framework, this CP-even Higgs boson can be identified with the SM-like Higgs boson found from LHC. Denoting the mixing parameter
\alpha
of these two Higgs bosons,

\displaystyle\begin{pmatrix}\mathrm{Re}[\rho^{0}_{2}]\\
\mathrm{Re}[\eta^{0}_{1}]\end{pmatrix}

\displaystyle=\begin{pmatrix}c_{\alpha}&s_{\alpha}\\
-s_{\alpha}&c_{\alpha}\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}H\\
h\end{pmatrix},

(82)

the difference between the tree level couplings
h\overline{e_{a}}e_{a}
predicted by the model under consideration and the SM is a factor
s_{\alpha}/c_{\beta}
, exactly the same as the 2HDM, see Ref.
[
126
]
for example. Therefore, the combination of this factor and the loop corrections can accommodate the experimental data.

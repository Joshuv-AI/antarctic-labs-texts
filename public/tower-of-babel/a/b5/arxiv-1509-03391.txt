Modal Characterisations of Behavioral Pseudometrics
Yuxin Deng, Wenjie Du, Daniel Gebler

Abstract
For the model of probabilistic labelled transition systems that allow for the co-existence of nondeterminism and probabilities, we present two notions of bisimulation metrics: one is state-based and the other is distribution-based. We provide a sound and complete modal characterisation for each of them, using real-valued modal logics based on the Hennessy-Milner logic. The logic for characterising the state-based metric is much simpler than an earlier logic by Desharnais et al. as it uses only two non-expansive operators rather than the general class of non-expansive operators.

Introduction

Bisimulation is an important proof technique for establishing behavioural equivalences of concurrent systems.
In probabilistic concurrency theory, there are roughly two kinds of bisimulations: one is state-based because it is directly defined over states and then lifted to distributions, and the other is distribution-based as it is a relation between distributions. The former is originally defined in
[
LS91
]
to represent a branching time semantics; the latter, as defined in
[
HKK14
,
FZ14
,
DFD15
]
, is strictly coarser and represents a linear time semantics.

In correspondence with those bisimulations, there are two notions of behavioural pseudometrics (simply called metrics). They are more robust ways of formalising behavioural similarity between formal systems than bisimulations because, particularly in the probabilistic setting, bisimulations are too sensitive to probabilities (a very small perturbation of the probabilities would render two systems non-bisimilar). A metric gives a quantitative measure of the distance between two systems and distance
0
usually means that the two systems are bisimilar. A logical characterisation of the state-based bisimulation metric for labelled Markov processes is given in
[
DGJP04
]
. For a more general model of labelled concurrent Markov chains (LCMCs) that allow for the co-existence of nondeterminism and probabilities, a weak bisimulation metric is proposed in
[
DJGP02
]
. Its logical characterisation uses formulae like
h\circ f
, where
f
is a formula and
h
can be any non-expansive operator on
[0,1]
, i.e.
|h(x)-h(y)|\leq|x-y|
for any
x,y\in[0,1]
. A natural question then arises: instead of the general class of non-expansive operators, is it possible to use only a few simple non-expansive operators without losing the capability of characterising the bisimulation metric?

In the current work, we give a positive answer to the above question.
We work in the framework of probabilistic labelled transition systems (pLTSs) that are essentially the same as LCMCs, so the interplay of nondeterminism and probabilities is allowed. We provide a modal characterisation of the state-based bisimulation metric closely in line with the classical Hennessy-Milner logic (HML)
[
HM85
]
. Our variant of the HML makes use of state formulae and distribution formulae, which are formulae evaluated at states and distributions, respectively, and yield success probabilities. We use merely two non-expansive operators: negation (
\neg\phi
) and testing (
\phi\ominus p
). Negation is self-explanatory and the testing operator checks if a state satisfies a property with certain threshold probability. More precisely, if state
s
satisfies formula
\phi
with probability
q
, then it satisfies
\neg\phi
with probability
1-q
, and satisfies
\phi\ominus p
with probability
q-p
if
q>p
and
0
otherwise. In other words, we do not need the general classs of non-expansive operators because negation and testing, together with other modalities in the classical HML, are expressive enough to characterise bisimulation metrics. As regards to the characterisation of distribution-based bisimulation metric, we drop state formulae and use distribution formulae only.

The rest of this paper is organised as follows. Section
2
provides some basic concepts on pLTSs. Section
3
defines a two-sorted modal logic that leads to a sound and complete characterisation of the state-based bisimulation metric. Section
4
gives a similar characterisation for the distribution-based bisimulation metric. In Section
5
we review some related work.
Finally, we conclude in Section
6
.

Let

A

Let

\Kantorovich(d)\colon\mathop{\mbox{$\mathcal{D}$}}({S})\times\mathop{\mbox{$\mathcal{D}$}}({S})\to[0,1]
defined via a linear programming problem as follows:

\Kantorovich(d)(\Delta,\Theta)=\min_{\omega\in\Omega(\Delta,\Theta)}\sum_{s,t\in S}d(s,t)\cdot\omega(s,t)

for
\Delta,\Theta\in\mathop{\mbox{$\mathcal{D}$}}({S})

The dual of the above linear programming problem is the following

\begin{array}[]{rl}\max\ \sum_{s\in S}(\Delta(s)-\Theta(s))x_{s},\text{ subject to }&0\leq x_{s}\leq 1\\
&\forall s,t\in S\colon\ x_{s}-x_{t}\leq d(s,t)\;.\end{array}

The duality theorem in linear programming guarantees that both problems have the same optimal value.

Let
\hat{d}\colon\mathop{\mbox{$\mathcal{D}$}}({S})\times\mathop{\mbox{$\mathcal{D}$}}({S})\to[0,1]

\mathop{\mbox{$\mathcal{D}$}}({S})

\Hausdorff(\hat{d})(\Pi_{1},\Pi_{2})=\max\{\adjustlimits{\sup}_{\Delta\in\Pi_{1}}{\inf}_{\Theta\in\Pi_{2}}\hat{d}(\Delta,\Theta),\ \ \adjustlimits{\sup}_{\Theta\in\Pi_{2}}{\inf}_{\Delta\in\Pi_{1}}\hat{d}(\Theta,\Delta)\}

for all

, whereby

and

.

Definition 3
.

Our metric HML is two-sorted and has the following syntax:

\begin{array}[]{rl}\varphi::=&\top\ |\ \lnot\varphi\ |\ \varphi\ominus p\ |\ \varphi_{1}\land\varphi_{2}\ |\ \langle a\rangle\psi\\[2.15277pt]
\psi::=&\psi\ominus p\ |\ \psi_{1}\land\psi_{2}\ |\ [\varphi]\end{array}

with
a\in A
and
p\in[0,1]
.

Let
\logic
denote the set of all metric HML formulae,
\varphi
range over the set of all
state formulae

\logicstate
, and
\psi
range over the set of all
distribution formulae

\logicdist
. The two kinds of formulae are defined simultaneously. The operator
\phi\ominus p
tests if a state passes
\phi
with probability at least
p
. If
\phi
is a state formula then it immediately induces a distribution formula
[\phi]
.
Sometimes we abbreviate
\langle a\rangle[\varphi]
as
\langle a\rangle\varphi
. All other operators are standard and have appeared in the classical HML.

Definition 4
.

A state formula
\varphi\in\logicstate
evaluates in
s\in S
as follows:

\displaystyle\llbracket{\top}\rrbracket({s})=1
,

\displaystyle\llbracket{\lnot\varphi}\rrbracket({s})=1-\llbracket{\varphi}\rrbracket({s})
,

\displaystyle\llbracket{\varphi\ominus p}\rrbracket({s})=\max(\llbracket{\varphi}\rrbracket({s})-p,~0)
,

\displaystyle\llbracket{\varphi_{1}\land\varphi_{2}}\rrbracket({s})=\min(\llbracket{\varphi_{1}}\rrbracket({s}),\llbracket{\varphi_{2}}\rrbracket({s}))
,

\displaystyle\llbracket{\langle a\rangle\psi}\rrbracket({s})=\max_{s\xrightarrow{\,{a}\,}\Delta}\llbracket{\psi}\rrbracket({\Delta})
,

and a distribution formula
\psi\in\logicdist
evaluates in
\Delta\in\mathop{\mbox{$\mathcal{D}$}}({S})
as follows:

\displaystyle\llbracket{\psi\ominus p}\rrbracket({\Delta})=\max(\llbracket{\psi}\rrbracket({\Delta})-p,0)
,

\displaystyle\llbracket{\psi_{1}\land\psi_{2}}\rrbracket({\Delta})=\min(\llbracket{\psi_{1}}\rrbracket({\Delta}),\llbracket{\psi_{2}}\rrbracket({\Delta}))
,

\displaystyle\llbracket{[\varphi]}\rrbracket({\Delta})=\sum_{s\in S}\Delta(s)\llbracket{\varphi}\rrbracket({s})
.

We often use constant formulae e.g.
\underline{p}
for any
p\in[0,1]
with the semantics
\llbracket{\underline{p}}\rrbracket({s})=p
, which is derivable in the above logic by letting
\underline{p}=\top\ominus(1-p)
. Moreover, we write
\varphi\oplus p
for
\lnot((\lnot\varphi)\ominus p)
which has the semantics
\displaystyle\llbracket{\varphi\oplus p}\rrbracket({s})=\min(\llbracket{\varphi}\rrbracket({s})+p,~1)=1-\max(1-\llbracket{\varphi}\rrbracket({s})-p,0)
. In the presence of negation and conjunction we can derive disjunction by letting
\varphi_{1}\vee\varphi_{2}
to be
\lnot(\lnot\varphi_{1}\wedge\lnot\varphi_{2})
.
Semantics of
\langle a\rangle\varphi
is a translation of
[
DJGP02
, Def. 4.1]
from labelled concurrent Markov chain semantics to pLTSs. Conjunction of distribution formulae
\psi_{1}\land\psi_{2}
could alternatively be replaced by
\psi_{1}\oplus_{p}\psi_{2}
or
\psi_{1}\oplus\psi_{2}

[

, Sec. 4]
with semantics

\begin{array}[]{rl}\llbracket{\psi_{1}\oplus_{p}\psi_{2}}\rrbracket({\Delta})&=\displaystyle\sup_{\Delta=p\Delta_{1}+(1-p)\Delta_{2}}(p\cdot\llbracket{\psi_{1}}\rrbracket({\Delta_{1}})+(1-p)\llbracket{\psi_{2}}\rrbracket({\Delta_{2}})\\
\llbracket{\psi_{1}\oplus\psi_{2}}\rrbracket({\Delta})&=\displaystyle\sup_{p\in(0,1)}\llbracket{\psi_{1}\oplus_{p}\psi_{2}}\rrbracket({\Delta})\end{array}

The above metric HML induces two natural logical metrics
\bisimdlogicstrong
and
\bisimddlogicstrong
on states and distributions respectively, by letting

\begin{array}[]{rcl}\bisimdlogicstrong(s,t)&=&\sup_{\varphi\in\logicstate}|\llbracket{\varphi}\rrbracket({s})-\llbracket{\varphi}\rrbracket({t})|\\
\bisimddlogicstrong(\Delta,\Theta)&=&\sup_{\psi\in\logicdist}|\llbracket{\psi}\rrbracket({\Delta})-\llbracket{\psi}\rrbracket({\Theta})|\end{array}

Lemma 1

1.

\bisimdlogicstrong\sqsubseteq\bisimdstrong

2.

\bisimddlogicstrong\sqsubseteq\Kantorovich(\bisimdstrong)

Proof.

We show the two statements simultaneously by structural induction on formulae. For any two states
s,t\in S
and distributions
\Delta_{1},\Delta_{2}\in\mathop{\mbox{$\mathcal{D}$}}({S})
, we prove that

(1)

|\llbracket{\varphi}\rrbracket({s})-\llbracket{\varphi}\rrbracket({t})|\leq\bisimdstrong(s,t)
for all
\varphi\in\logicstate
;

(2)

|\llbracket{\psi}\rrbracket({\Delta_{1}})-\llbracket{\psi}\rrbracket({\Delta_{2}})|\leq\Kantorovich(\bisimdstrong)(\Delta_{1},\Delta_{2})
for all
\psi\in\logicdist
.

We first analyze the structure of
\varphi
in (1).

•

\varphi\equiv\top
. Then it is trivial to see that
|\llbracket{\varphi}\rrbracket({s})-\llbracket{\varphi}\rrbracket({t})|=|1-1|=0\leq\bisimdstrong(s,t)
.

•

\varphi\equiv\lnot\varphi^{\prime}
. Then
|\llbracket{\varphi}\rrbracket({s})-\llbracket{\varphi}\rrbracket({t})|=|\llbracket{\varphi^{\prime}}\rrbracket({t})-\llbracket{\varphi^{\prime}}\rrbracket({s})|\leq\bisimdstrong(s,t)
where the inequality holds by induction.

•

\varphi\equiv\varphi^{\prime}\ominus p
. There are four subcases and we consider one of them. Suppose
\llbracket{\varphi^{\prime}}\rrbracket({s})>p
and
\llbracket{\varphi^{\prime}}\rrbracket({t})\leq p
, then

|\llbracket{\varphi}\rrbracket({s})-\llbracket{\varphi}\rrbracket({t})|=|\llbracket{\varphi^{\prime}}\rrbracket({s})-p|\leq|\llbracket{\varphi^{\prime}}\rrbracket({s})-\llbracket{\varphi^{\prime}}\rrbracket({t})|\leq\bisimdstrong(s,t)
by induction.

•

\varphi\equiv\varphi_{1}\land\varphi_{2}
. Without loss of generality we assume that
\llbracket{\varphi}\rrbracket({s})\geq\llbracket{\varphi}\rrbracket({t})
. There are two possibilities:

–

If
\llbracket{\varphi_{1}}\rrbracket({t})\leq\llbracket{\varphi_{2}}\rrbracket({t})
, then
\llbracket{\varphi}\rrbracket({s})-\llbracket{\varphi}\rrbracket({t})\leq\llbracket{\varphi_{1}}\rrbracket({s})-\llbracket{\varphi_{1}}\rrbracket({t})\leq\bisimdstrong(s,t)
, where the last inequality holds by induction.

–

Symmetrically, if
\llbracket{\varphi_{2}}\rrbracket({t})\leq\llbracket{\varphi_{1}}\rrbracket({t})
, then
\llbracket{\varphi}\rrbracket({s})-\llbracket{\varphi}\rrbracket({t})\leq\llbracket{\varphi_{2}}\rrbracket({s})-\llbracket{\varphi_{2}}\rrbracket({t})\leq\bisimdstrong(s,t)
.

•

\varphi\equiv\langle a\rangle\psi
. We consider the non-trivial case that both
s
and
t
can perform action
a
. (If either of the two states cannot perform action
a
, the expected result is straightforward.) Let
\Delta_{1}
be the distribution such that
s\xrightarrow{\,{a}\,}\Delta_{1}
and
\llbracket{\langle a\rangle\psi}\rrbracket({s})=\llbracket{\psi}\rrbracket({\Delta_{1}})
.
Since
\bisimdstrong
is a state-based bisimulation metric, by definition there exists some
\Delta_{2}
such that
t\xrightarrow{\,{a}\,}\Delta_{2}
and
\Kantorovich(\bisimdstrong)(\Delta_{1},\Delta_{2})\leq\bisimdstrong(s,t)
. Without loss of generality we assume that
\llbracket{\varphi}\rrbracket({s})\geq\llbracket{\varphi}\rrbracket({t})
. It follows that

\begin{array}[]{rl}&\llbracket{\varphi}\rrbracket({s})-\llbracket{\varphi}\rrbracket({t})\\
=&\llbracket{\psi}\rrbracket({\Delta_{1}})-\max_{s\xrightarrow{\,{a}\,}{\Delta^{\prime}}}\llbracket{\psi}\rrbracket({\Delta^{\prime}})\\
\leq&\llbracket{\psi}\rrbracket({\Delta_{1}})-\llbracket{\psi}\rrbracket({\Delta_{2}})\\
\leq&\Kantorovich(\bisimdstrong)(\Delta_{1},\Delta_{2})\qquad\mbox{by induction on $\psi$}\\
\leq&\bisimdstrong(s,t)\end{array}

Then we analyze the structure of
\psi
in (2).

•

\psi=\psi_{1}\land\psi_{2}
. Without loss of generality we assume that
\llbracket{\psi}\rrbracket({\Delta_{1}})\geq\llbracket{\psi}\rrbracket({\Delta_{2}})
. There are two possibilities:

–

If
\llbracket{\psi_{1}}\rrbracket({\Delta_{2}})\leq\llbracket{\psi_{2}}\rrbracket({\Delta_{2}})
, then
\llbracket{\psi}\rrbracket({\Delta_{1}})-\llbracket{\psi}\rrbracket({\Delta_{2}})\leq\llbracket{\psi_{1}}\rrbracket({\Delta_{1}})-\llbracket{\psi_{1}}\rrbracket({\Delta_{2}})\leq\Kantorovich(\bisimdstrong)(\Delta_{1},\Delta_{2})
, where the last inequality holds by induction.

–

Symmetrically, if
\llbracket{\psi_{2}}\rrbracket({\Delta_{2}})\leq\llbracket{\psi_{1}}\rrbracket({\Delta_{2}})
, then
\llbracket{\psi}\rrbracket({\Delta_{1}})-\llbracket{\psi}\rrbracket({\Delta_{2}})\leq\llbracket{\psi_{2}}\rrbracket({\Delta_{1}})-\llbracket{\psi_{2}}\rrbracket({\Delta_{2}})\leq\Kantorovich(\bisimdstrong)(\Delta_{1},\Delta_{2})
.

•

\psi=\psi^{\prime}\ominus p
for some
p
in
[0,1]
. There are four subcases and we consider one of them. Suppose
\llbracket{\psi^{\prime}}\rrbracket({\Delta_{1}})>p
and
\llbracket{\psi^{\prime}}\rrbracket({\Delta_{2}})\leq p
, then

|\llbracket{\psi}\rrbracket({\Delta_{1}})-\llbracket{\psi}\rrbracket({\Delta_{2}})|=|\llbracket{\psi^{\prime}}\rrbracket({\Delta_{1}})-p|\leq|\llbracket{\psi^{\prime}}\rrbracket({\Delta_{1}})-\llbracket{\psi^{\prime}}\rrbracket({\Delta_{2}})|\leq\Kantorovich(\bisimdstrong)(\Delta_{1},\Delta_{2})
by induction.

•

\psi=[\varphi]
for some
\varphi\in\logicstate
. Without loss of generality we assume that
\llbracket{\varphi}\rrbracket({s})\geq\llbracket{\varphi}\rrbracket({t})
. We infer that

\begin{array}[]{ll}&\llbracket{\psi}\rrbracket({\Delta_{1}})-\llbracket{\psi}\rrbracket({\Delta_{2}})\\
=&\llbracket{[\varphi]}\rrbracket({\Delta_{1}})-\llbracket{[\varphi]}\rrbracket({\Delta_{2}})\\
=&\sum_{u\in S}(\Delta_{1}(u)-\Delta_{2}(u))\llbracket{\varphi}\rrbracket({u})\\
\leq&\max\{\sum_{u\in S}(\Delta_{1}(u)-\Delta_{2}(u))x_{u}\mid x_{u},x_{u^{\prime}}\in[0,1]\land x_{u}-x_{u^{\prime}}\leq\bisimdstrong(u,u^{\prime})\}\\
=&\Kantorovich(\bisimdstrong)(\Delta_{1},\Delta_{2})\end{array}

where the last equality holds because of the Kantorovich-Rubinstein duality theorem
[
KR58
,
vBW01a
]
and the last inequality holds because for any states
u,u^{\prime}\in S
we have
\llbracket{\varphi}\rrbracket({u}),\llbracket{\varphi}\rrbracket({u^{\prime}})\in[0,1]
and
|\llbracket{\varphi}\rrbracket({u})-\llbracket{\varphi}\rrbracket({u^{\prime}})|\leq\bisimdstrong(u,u^{\prime})
by induction on
\varphi
.

Lemma 2

\Kantorovich(\bisimdlogicstrong)\sqsubseteq\bisimddlogicstrong

Proof.

Let
\Delta_{1},\Delta_{2}
be any two distributions in
\logicdist
.
We show that

\Kantorovich(\bisimdlogicstrong)(\Delta_{1},\Delta_{2})~\leq~\sup_{\psi\in\logicdist}|\llbracket{\psi}\rrbracket({\Delta_{1}})-\llbracket{\psi}\rrbracket({\Delta_{2}})|,

using an idea inspired by
[
DJGP02
]
. Let
L(\Delta_{1},\Delta_{2})
be the optimal value of the following linear program

\begin{array}[]{rl}\max\sum_{s\in S}(\Delta_{1}(s)-\Delta_{2}(s))x_{s},\text{ subject to }&0\leq x_{s}\leq 1\\
&\forall s,t\in S\colon\ x_{s}-x_{t}\leq\bisimdlogicstrong(s,t)\end{array}

Suppose
\{k_{s}\}_{s\in S}
be a set of real numbers that maximizes the above linear program to reach
L(\Delta_{1},\Delta_{2})
. Let
e=\min\{1-k_{t}\mid k_{t}<1\mbox{ and }t\in S\}
and

\epsilon>0
be any positive real number smaller than
e
. Hence, if
t\in S
and
k_{t}<1
then

k_{t}+\epsilon<1.

(1)

We construct some formula
\psi
such that

L(\Delta_{1},\Delta_{2})-\epsilon~<~\llbracket{\psi}\rrbracket({\Delta_{1}})-\llbracket{\psi}\rrbracket({\Delta_{2}}).

(2)

For any
s,t\in S
, we distinguish two cases:

1.

If
k_{s}>k_{t}
, then
0<k_{s}-k_{t}\leq\bisimdlogicstrong(s,t)
. It is easy to see that there exists some formula
\varphi_{st}
such that

k_{s}-k_{t}<\llbracket{\varphi_{st}}\rrbracket({s})-\llbracket{\varphi_{st}}\rrbracket({t})+\epsilon.

(3)

or equivalently
\llbracket{\varphi_{st}}\rrbracket({t})-\llbracket{\varphi_{st}}\rrbracket({s})+k_{s}<k_{t}+\epsilon
.
We define a new formula

\varphi^{\prime}_{st}\;=\;\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\varphi_{st}\ominus(\llbracket{\varphi_{st}}\rrbracket({s})-k_{s})&\mbox{if $\llbracket{\varphi_{st}}\rrbracket({s})>k_{s}$}\\
\varphi_{st}\oplus(k_{s}-\llbracket{\varphi_{st}}\rrbracket({s}))&\mbox{otherwise.}\end{array}\right.

Let us compare
\varphi^{\prime}_{st}
with
k_{t}
.

(a)

If
\llbracket{\varphi_{st}}\rrbracket({s})>k_{s}
, then

\begin{array}[]{rcl}\llbracket{\varphi^{\prime}_{st}}\rrbracket({t})&=&\max(\llbracket{\varphi_{st}}\rrbracket({t})-\llbracket{\varphi_{st}}\rrbracket({s})+k_{s},0)\\
&<&\max(k_{t}+\epsilon,0)\qquad\mbox{by (\ref{eq:ks})}\\
&=&k_{t}+\epsilon\end{array}

(b)

Otherwise,

\llbracket{\varphi^{\prime}_{st}}\rrbracket({t})=\min(\llbracket{\varphi_{st}}\rrbracket({t})+k_{s}-\llbracket{\varphi_{st}}\rrbracket({s}),1)
. By (
3
) we infer that
\llbracket{\varphi_{st}}\rrbracket({t})+k_{s}-\llbracket{\varphi_{st}}\rrbracket({s})<k_{t}+\epsilon
. Since
k_{t}<k_{s}\leq 1

we infer by (
1
) that
k_{t}+\epsilon<1
and thus
\llbracket{\varphi^{\prime}_{st}}\rrbracket({t})<k_{t}+\epsilon
.

In both (a) and (b) we have
\llbracket{\varphi^{\prime}_{st}}\rrbracket({t})<k_{t}+\epsilon
, and it is also easy to see that
\llbracket{\varphi^{\prime}_{st}}\rrbracket({s})=k_{s}
.

2.

If
k_{s}\leq k_{t}
, then we simply set
\varphi^{\prime}_{st}
to be the formula
\underline{k_{s}}
. As in the last case, we have
\llbracket{\varphi^{\prime}_{st}}\rrbracket({s})=k_{s}
and
\llbracket{\varphi^{\prime}_{st}}\rrbracket({t})=k_{s}\leq k_{t}<k_{t}+\epsilon
.

In summary, the above reasoning says that for any
s,t\in S
we can construct a formula
\varphi^{\prime}_{st}
such that

\llbracket{\varphi^{\prime}_{st}}\rrbracket({s})=k_{s}
and
\llbracket{\varphi^{\prime}_{st}}\rrbracket({t})<k_{t}+\epsilon
.
Now let us define
\varphi^{\prime}_{s}=\bigwedge_{t\in S}\varphi^{\prime}_{st}
.
It is easy to see that
\llbracket{\varphi^{\prime}_{s}}\rrbracket({s})=k_{s}
and
\llbracket{\varphi^{\prime}_{s}}\rrbracket({t})<k_{t}+\epsilon
for all
t\in S
. The latter implies
\max\{\llbracket{\varphi^{\prime}_{s}}\rrbracket({t})\mid s,t\in S\}<k_{t}+\epsilon
.
Then define
\varphi=\bigvee_{s\in S}\varphi^{\prime}_{s}
. For all
t\in S
, we have

k_{t}~=~\llbracket{\varphi^{\prime}_{t}}\rrbracket({t})~\leq~\llbracket{\varphi}\rrbracket({t})~=~\max\{\llbracket{\varphi^{\prime}_{s}}\rrbracket({t})\mid s,t\in S\}~<~k_{t}+\epsilon.

Finally, we define
\psi=[\varphi]
.
It follows that

\begin{array}[]{rcl}\llbracket{\psi}\rrbracket({\Delta_{1}})-\llbracket{\psi}\rrbracket({\Delta_{2}})&=&\llbracket{[\varphi]}\rrbracket({\Delta_{1}})-\llbracket{[\varphi]}\rrbracket({\Delta_{2}})\\
&=&\sum_{t\in S}\Delta_{1}(t)\cdot\llbracket{\varphi}\rrbracket({t})-\sum_{t\in S}\Delta_{2}(t))\cdot\llbracket{\varphi}\rrbracket({t})\\
&\geq&\sum_{t\in S}\Delta_{1}(t)\cdot k_{t}-\sum_{t\in S}\Delta_{2}(t))\cdot\llbracket{\varphi}\rrbracket({t})\\
&>&\sum_{t\in S}\Delta_{1}(t)\cdot k_{t}-\sum_{t\in S}\Delta_{2}(t))\cdot(k_{t}+\epsilon)\\
&=&\sum_{t\in S}(\Delta_{1}(t)-\Delta_{2}(t))\cdot k_{t}-\sum_{t\in S}\Delta_{2}(t)\cdot\epsilon\\
&=&L(\Delta_{1},\Delta_{2})-\epsilon\end{array}

as required.

The above property will be used to prove the following lemma.

Lemma 3

\bisimdstrong\sqsubseteq\bisimdlogicstrong

Proof.

We show that
\bisimdlogicstrong
is a state-based bisimulation metric. Let
s,t
be any two states in
S
and
\epsilon
be any real number in the interval
[0,1)
with
\bisimdlogicstrong(s,t)\leq\epsilon
. Assume that
s\xrightarrow{\,{a}\,}\Delta_{1}
is an arbitrarily chosen transition from
s
. Then state
t
must be able to perform action
a
too. Otherwise it is easy to see that
\bisimdlogicstrong(s,t)=1>\epsilon
, which contradicts our assumption above. We need to show that there exists some transition
t\xrightarrow{\,{a}\,}\Delta_{2}
with
\Kantorovich(\bisimdlogicstrong)(\Delta_{1},\Delta_{2})\leq\epsilon
.
Suppose for a contradiction that no
a
-transition from
t
satisfies this condition.
In other words, for each
\Delta^{i}_{2}
with
t\xrightarrow{\,{a}\,}\Delta^{i}_{2}
we have
\Kantorovich(\bisimdlogicstrong)(\Delta_{1},\Delta^{i}_{2})>\epsilon
. By Lemma
2
, this means
\bisimddlogicstrong(\Delta_{1},\Delta^{i}_{2})>\epsilon
. Then
there must exist some formula
\psi^{i}_{2}\in\logicdist
such that

|\llbracket{\psi^{i}_{2}}\rrbracket({\Delta_{1}})-\llbracket{\psi^{i}_{2}}\rrbracket({\Delta^{i}_{2}})|>\epsilon
.
Furthermore, we can strengthen this condition to the following one

\llbracket{\psi^{i}_{2}}\rrbracket({\Delta_{1}})-\llbracket{\psi^{i}_{2}}\rrbracket({\Delta^{i}_{2}})>\epsilon

(4)

because we can take the formula
\lnot\psi^{i}_{2}
in place of
\psi^{i}_{2}
in the case that
\llbracket{\psi^{i}_{2}}\rrbracket({\Delta_{1}})<\llbracket{\psi^{i}_{2}}\rrbracket({\Delta_{2}})
.
Let
\varphi=\langle a\rangle\bigwedge_{i}(\psi^{i}_{2}\ominus\llbracket{\psi^{i}_{2}}\rrbracket({\Delta^{i}_{2}}))
. We infer that

\begin{array}[]{rcl}\llbracket{\varphi}\rrbracket({s})&=&\max_{s\xrightarrow{\,{a}\,}\Delta}\llbracket{\bigwedge_{i}\psi^{i}_{2}\ominus\llbracket{\psi^{i}_{2}}\rrbracket({\Delta^{i}_{2}})}\rrbracket({\Delta})\\
&\geq&\llbracket{\bigwedge_{i}(\psi^{i}_{2}\ominus\llbracket{\psi^{i}_{2}}\rrbracket({\Delta^{i}_{2}}))}\rrbracket({\Delta_{1}})\\
&=&\min_{i}\llbracket{\psi^{i}_{2}\ominus\llbracket{\psi^{i}_{2}}\rrbracket({\Delta^{i}_{2}})}\rrbracket({\Delta_{1}})\\
&=&\llbracket{\psi^{k}_{2}\ominus\llbracket{\psi^{k}_{2}}\rrbracket({\Delta^{k}_{2}})}\rrbracket({\Delta_{1}})\qquad\text{for some $k$}\\
&=&\max(\llbracket{\psi^{k}_{2}}\rrbracket({\Delta_{1}})-\llbracket{\psi^{k}_{2}}\rrbracket({\Delta^{k}_{2}}),~0)\\
&>&\epsilon\qquad\mbox{by (\ref{eq:psii})}\end{array}

On the other hand, we have

\begin{array}[]{rcl}\llbracket{\varphi}\rrbracket({t})&=&\max_{t\xrightarrow{\,{a}\,}\Delta^{i}_{2}}\llbracket{\bigwedge_{j}(\psi^{j}_{2}\ominus\llbracket{\psi^{j}_{2}}\rrbracket({\Delta^{j}_{2}}))}\rrbracket({\Delta^{i}_{2}})\\
&=&\max_{t\xrightarrow{\,{a}\,}\Delta^{i}_{2}}\min_{j}\llbracket{\psi^{j}_{2}\ominus\llbracket{\psi^{j}_{2}}\rrbracket({\Delta^{j}_{2}})}\rrbracket({\Delta^{i}_{2}})\\
&=&\max_{t\xrightarrow{\,{a}\,}\Delta^{i}_{2}}\min_{j}\max((\llbracket{\psi^{j}_{2}}\rrbracket({\Delta^{i}_{2}})-\llbracket{\psi^{j}_{2}}\rrbracket({\Delta^{j}_{2}})),~0)\\
&=&0\end{array}

It follows that
\bisimdlogicstrong(s,t)\geq\llbracket{\varphi}\rrbracket({s})-\llbracket{\varphi}\rrbracket({t})>\epsilon
, which gives rise to a contradiction.

By combining the above three technical lemmas we obtain the following logical characterisation of the state-based bisimilairty metric.

Theorem 3.1

\bisimdstrong=\bisimdlogicstrong
and
\Kantorovich(\bisimdstrong)=\bisimddlogicstrong

Remark 1
.

In the proof of Lemma
3
we have constructed the formula

\varphi=\langle a\rangle\bigwedge_{i}(\psi^{i}_{2}\ominus\llbracket{\psi^{i}_{2}}\rrbracket({\Delta^{i}_{2}}))

(5)

by making use of conjunction and minus connectives for distribution formulae. This happens because in the presence of non-determinism state
t
may perform action
a
and then evolves into one of successor distributions
\Delta^{i}_{2}
. If we confine ourselves to deterministic pLTSs, then state
t
will have a unique successor distribution
\Delta^{i}_{2}
and therefore (
5
) can be simplified as

\varphi=\langle a\rangle\psi^{i}_{2}
. In this case, there is no need of conjunction and minus connectives for distribution formulae. Furthermore, if we fold
[\varphi]
into state formulae in Definition
3
, distribution formulae can be completely dropped. In other words, for deterministic pLTSs, the state-based bisimilarity metric can be characterised by the following metric logic

\varphi::=\top\ |\ \lnot\varphi\ |\ \varphi\ominus p\ |\ \varphi_{1}\land\varphi_{2}\ |\ \langle a\rangle\varphi

(6)

Therefore, for deterministic pLTSs, the two-sorted logic in Definition
3
degenerates into the logic considered in
[
DGJP04
,
vBW05
]
, as expected.

In
[
DAMRS08
,
CDAMR10
]
a bisimulation metric for game structures is characterised by a quantitative
\mu
-calculus where formulae are valuated also on states and no distribution formula is needed. This is not surprising because games are deterministic: at any state
s
, if two players have chosen their moves, say
a_{1}
and
a_{2}
, then there is a unique distribution
\delta(s,a_{1},a_{2})
to determine the probabilities of arriving at a set of destination states.

4
Distribution-Based Bisimulation Metric

The bisimilarity metric given in Definition
2
measures the distance between two states. Alternatively, it is possible to directly define a
metric that measures subdistributions. In order to do so, we first define a transition
relation between subdistributions.

Definition 5
.

We write
\Delta\xrightarrow{\,{a}\,}\Delta^{\prime}
if

\Delta^{\prime}=\sum_{s\in\lceil{\Delta}\rceil}\Delta(s)\cdot\Delta_{s}
, where
\Delta_{s}
is
determined as follows:

•

either
s\xrightarrow{\,{a}\,}\Delta_{s}

•

or there is no
\Theta
with
s\xrightarrow{\,{a}\,}\Theta
, and in this case we set

\Delta_{s}=\mathop{\varepsilon}
.

Note that if
\Delta\xrightarrow{\,{a}\,}\Delta^{\prime}
then some (not
necessarily all) states in the support of
\Delta
can perform action

a
. For example, consider the two states
s_{2}
and
s_{3}
in
Figure
1
. Since
s_{2}\xrightarrow{\,{c}\,}\overline{s_{4}}
and
s_{3}

cannot perform action
c
, the distribution

\Delta=\frac{1}{2}\overline{s_{2}}+\frac{1}{2}\overline{s_{3}}
can make the
transition
\Delta\xrightarrow{\,{c}\,}\frac{1}{2}\overline{s_{4}}
to reach the
subdistribution
\frac{1}{2}\overline{s_{4}}
.

Definition 6
.

A
1
-bounded pseudometric
d
on
\mathop{\mbox{$\mathcal{D}$}}({S})
is a
distribution-based bisimulation metric
if
|\;|\Delta_{1}|-|\Delta_{2}|\;|\leq d(\Delta_{1},\Delta_{2})
and
for all
\Delta_{1},\Delta_{2}\in\mathop{\mbox{$\mathcal{D}$}}({S})
and
\epsilon\in[0,1)
with
d(\Delta_{1},\Delta_{2})\leq\epsilon
, if
\Delta_{1}\xrightarrow{\,{a}\,}\Delta^{\prime}_{1}
then there exists an
\Delta_{2}\xrightarrow{\,{a}\,}\Delta^{\prime}_{2}
with
d(\Delta^{\prime}_{1},\Delta^{\prime}_{2})\leq\epsilon
.

The smallest (wrt.
\sqsubseteq
) distribution-based bisimulation metric, notation
\bisimddist
, is called
distribution-based bisimilarity metric
.
Distribution-based bisimilarity
[
DFD15
]
is the kernel of the distribution-based bisimilarity metric.

It is not difficult to see that
\bisimdstrong
is different from
\bisimddist
, as witnessed by the following example.

Example 4

Consider the states in Figure
1
.
We first observe that
\bisimddist(\overline{s_{2}},\overline{t_{3}})=0
because
s_{2}
and
t_{3}
can match each other’s action exactly. Similarly, we have
\bisimddist(\overline{s_{3}},\overline{t_{4}})=0
. Then it is easy to see that
\bisimddist(\frac{1}{2}\overline{s_{2}}+\frac{1}{2}\overline{s_{3}},\frac{1}{2}\overline{t_{3}}+\frac{1}{2}\overline{t_{4}})=0
. Since
s_{1}\xrightarrow{\,{b}\,}\frac{1}{2}\overline{s_{2}}+\frac{1}{2}\overline{s_{3}}
and

\frac{1}{2}\overline{t_{1}}+\frac{1}{2}\overline{t_{2}}
, we infer that

\bisimddist(\overline{s_{1}},\frac{1}{2}\overline{t_{1}}+\frac{1}{2}\overline{t_{2}})=0
. It, in turn, implies
\bisimddist(\overline{s},\overline{t})=0
. We have already seen in Example
1
that
\bisimdstrong(s,t)=\frac{1}{2}
. Therefore, the two distance functions
\bisimdstrong
and
\bisimddist
are indeed different.

The rest of this section is devoted to a logical characterisation of
\bisimddist
.
Consider the metric logic
\logic^{D*}
whose formulae are defined below

\psi::=\top\ |\ \lnot\psi\ |\ \psi\ominus p\ |\ \psi_{1}\land\psi_{2}\ |\ \langle a\rangle\psi

(7)

This logic is the same as that defined in (
6
) except that now we only have distribution formulae. We will show that this logic can capture the distribution-based bisimilarity metric.

Definition 7
.

A formula
\psi\in\logic^{D*}
evaluates in
\Delta\in\mathop{\mbox{$\mathcal{D}$}}({S})
as follows:

\displaystyle\llbracket{\top}\rrbracket({\Delta})=|\Delta|
,

\displaystyle\llbracket{\lnot\psi}\rrbracket({\Delta})=1-\llbracket{\psi}\rrbracket({\psi})
,

\displaystyle\llbracket{\psi\ominus p}\rrbracket({\Delta})=\max(\llbracket{\psi}\rrbracket({\psi})-p,~0)
,

\displaystyle\llbracket{\psi_{1}\land\psi_{2}}\rrbracket({\Delta})=\min(\llbracket{\psi_{1}}\rrbracket({\Delta}),\llbracket{\psi_{2}}\rrbracket({\Delta}))
,

\displaystyle\llbracket{\langle a\rangle\psi}\rrbracket({\Delta})=\max_{\Delta\xrightarrow{\,{a}\,}\Delta^{\prime}}\llbracket{\psi}\rrbracket({\Delta^{\prime}})
,

This induces a natural logical metric
\bisimddistl
over subdistributions defined by

\bisimddistl(\Delta,\Theta)=\sup_{\psi\in\logic^{D}}|[\![\psi]\!](\Delta)-[\![\psi]\!](\Theta)|

It turns out that
\bisimddistl
coincides with
\bisimddist
. We split the proof of this coincidence result into two parts, to show that one metric is dominated by the other and vice versa.

Lemma 4

\bisimddistl\sqsubseteq\bisimddist

Proof.

Similar to the proof of Lemma
1
.
We proceed by structural induction on formulae. For any two subdistributions
\Delta_{1},\Delta_{2}\in\mathop{\mbox{$\mathcal{D}$}}({S})
, we prove that

|\llbracket{\psi}\rrbracket({\Delta_{1}})-\llbracket{\psi}\rrbracket({\Delta_{2}})|\leq\bisimddist(\Delta_{1},\Delta_{2})

for all
\psi\in\logic^{D*}
.

We first analyze the structure of
\psi
.

•

\varphi\equiv\top
. Then it is trivial to see that
|\llbracket{\psi}\rrbracket({\Delta_{1}})-\llbracket{\psi}\rrbracket({\Delta_{2}})|=|\,|\Delta_{1}|-|\Delta_{2}|\,|\leq\bisimddist(\Delta_{1},\Delta_{2})
.

•

\psi\equiv\lnot\psi^{\prime}
. Then
|\llbracket{\psi}\rrbracket({\Delta_{1}})-\llbracket{\psi}\rrbracket({\Delta_{2}})|=|\llbracket{\psi^{\prime}}\rrbracket({\Delta_{2}})-\llbracket{\psi^{\prime}}\rrbracket({\Delta_{1}})|\leq\bisimddist(\Delta_{1},\Delta_{2})
where the inequality holds by induction.

•

\psi\equiv\psi^{\prime}\ominus p
. There are four subcases and we consider one of them. Suppose
\llbracket{\psi^{\prime}}\rrbracket({\Delta_{1}})>p
and
\llbracket{\psi^{\prime}}\rrbracket({\Delta_{2}})\leq p
, then

|\llbracket{\psi}\rrbracket({\Delta_{1}})-\llbracket{\psi}\rrbracket({\Delta_{2}})|=|\llbracket{\psi^{\prime}}\rrbracket({\Delta_{1}})-p|\leq|\llbracket{\psi^{\prime}}\rrbracket({\Delta_{1}})-\llbracket{\psi^{\prime}}\rrbracket({\Delta_{2}})|\leq\bisimddist(\Delta_{1},\Delta_{2})
by induction.

•

\psi\equiv\psi_{1}\land\psi_{2}
. Without loss of generality we assume that
\llbracket{\psi}\rrbracket({\Delta_{1}})\geq\llbracket{\psi}\rrbracket({\Delta_{2}})
. There are two possibilities:

–

If
\llbracket{\psi_{1}}\rrbracket({\Delta_{2}})\leq\llbracket{\psi_{2}}\rrbracket({\Delta_{2}})
, then
\llbracket{\psi}\rrbracket({\Delta_{1}})-\llbracket{\psi}\rrbracket({\Delta_{2}})\leq\llbracket{\psi_{1}}\rrbracket({\Delta_{1}})-\llbracket{\psi_{1}}\rrbracket({\Delta_{2}})\leq\bisimddist(\Delta_{1},\Delta_{2})
, where the last inequality holds by induction.

–

Symmetrically, if
\llbracket{\psi_{2}}\rrbracket({\Delta_{2}})\leq\llbracket{\psi_{1}}\rrbracket({\Delta_{2}})
, then
\llbracket{\psi}\rrbracket({\Delta_{1}})-\llbracket{\psi}\rrbracket({\Delta_{2}})\leq\llbracket{\psi_{2}}\rrbracket({\Delta_{1}})-\llbracket{\psi_{2}}\rrbracket({\Delta_{2}})\leq\bisimddist(\Delta_{1},\Delta_{2})
.

•

\psi\equiv\langle a\rangle\psi^{\prime}
. Let
\Delta^{\prime}_{1}
be the distribution such that
\Delta_{1}\xrightarrow{\,{a}\,}\Delta^{\prime}_{1}
and
\llbracket{\langle a\rangle\psi^{\prime}}\rrbracket({\Delta_{1}})=\llbracket{\psi^{\prime}}\rrbracket({\Delta^{\prime}_{1}})
.
Since
\bisimddist
is a distribution-based bisimulation metric, by definition there exists some
\Delta^{\prime}_{2}
such that
\Delta_{2}\xrightarrow{\,{a}\,}\Delta^{\prime}_{2}
and
\bisimddist(\Delta^{\prime}_{1},\Delta^{\prime}_{2})\leq\bisimddist(\Delta_{1},\Delta_{2})
. Without loss of generality we assume that
\llbracket{\psi}\rrbracket({\Delta_{1}})\geq\llbracket{\psi}\rrbracket({\Delta_{2}})
. It follows that

\begin{array}[]{rl}&\llbracket{\psi}\rrbracket({\Delta_{1}})-\llbracket{\psi}\rrbracket({\Delta_{2}})\\
=&\llbracket{\psi^{\prime}}\rrbracket({\Delta^{\prime}_{1}})-\max_{\Delta_{2}\xrightarrow{\,{a}\,}{\Delta^{\prime\prime}_{2}}}\llbracket{\psi^{\prime}}\rrbracket({\Delta^{\prime\prime}_{2}})\\
\leq&\llbracket{\psi^{\prime}}\rrbracket({\Delta^{\prime}_{1}})-\llbracket{\psi^{\prime}}\rrbracket({\Delta^{\prime}_{2}})\\
\leq&\bisimddist(\Delta^{\prime}_{1},\Delta^{\prime}_{2})\qquad\mbox{by induction on $\psi^{\prime}$}\\
\leq&\bisimddist(\Delta_{1},\Delta_{2})\end{array}

Lemma 5

\bisimddist\sqsubseteq\bisimddistl

Proof.

Similar to the proof of Lemma
3
.
We show that
\bisimddistl
is a distribution-based bisimulation metric. Let
\Delta_{1},\Delta_{2}
be any two subdistributions in
\mathop{\mbox{$\mathcal{D}$}}({S})
and
\epsilon
be any real number in the interval
[0,1)
with
\bisimddistl(\Delta_{1},\Delta_{2})\leq\epsilon
. Assume that
\Delta_{1}\xrightarrow{\,{a}\,}\Delta^{\prime}_{1}
is an arbitrarily chosen transition from
\Delta_{1}
. We need to show that there exists some transition
\Delta_{2}\xrightarrow{\,{a}\,}\Delta^{\prime}_{2}
with
\bisimddistl(\Delta^{\prime}_{1},\Delta^{\prime}_{2})\leq\epsilon
.
Suppose for a contradiction that no
a
-transition from
\Delta_{2}
satisfies this condition.
In other words, for each
\Delta^{i}_{2}
with
\Delta_{2}\xrightarrow{\,{a}\,}\Delta^{i}_{2}
we have
\bisimddistl(\Delta^{\prime}_{1},\Delta^{i}_{2})>\epsilon
. Then
there must exist some formula
\psi^{i}_{2}\in\logicdist
such that

|\llbracket{\psi^{i}_{2}}\rrbracket({\Delta^{\prime}_{1}})-\llbracket{\psi^{i}_{2}}\rrbracket({\Delta^{i}_{2}})|>\epsilon
.
Furthermore, we can strengthen this condition to the following one

\llbracket{\psi^{i}_{2}}\rrbracket({\Delta^{\prime}_{1}})-\llbracket{\psi^{i}_{2}}\rrbracket({\Delta^{i}_{2}})>\epsilon

(8)

because we can take the formula
\lnot\psi^{i}_{2}
in place of
\psi^{i}_{2}
in the case that
\llbracket{\psi^{i}_{2}}\rrbracket({\Delta^{\prime}_{1}})<\llbracket{\psi^{i}_{2}}\rrbracket({\Delta_{2}})

Let
\varphi=\langle a\rangle\bigwedge_{i}(\psi^{i}_{2}\ominus\llbracket{\psi^{i}_{2}}\rrbracket({\Delta^{i}_{2}}))

\begin{array}[]{rcl}\llbracket{\varphi}\rrbracket({\Delta_{1}})&=&\max_{\Delta_{1}\xrightarrow{\,{a}\,}\Delta}\llbracket{\bigwedge_{i}\psi^{i}_{2}\ominus\llbracket{\psi^{i}_{2}}\rrbracket({\Delta^{i}_{2}})}\rrbracket({\Delta})\\

\begin{array}[]{rcl}\llbracket{\psi}\rrbracket({\Delta_{2}})&=&\max_{\Delta_{2}\xrightarrow{\,{a}\,}\Delta^{i}_{2}}\llbracket{\bigwedge_{j}(\psi^{j}_{2}\ominus\llbracket{\psi^{j}_{2}}\rrbracket({\Delta^{j}_{2}}))}\rrbracket({\Delta^{i}_{2}})\\

Related work

Metrics for probabilistic transition systems are first suggested by

to formalize

show that discrete

consider the category of complete ultrametric spaces. Similar
ultrametric spaces are considered by den Hartog in
[
dH02
]
.

Metrics for deterministic systems are extensively studied.
Desharnais
et al.

[
DGJP04
]
propose a logical pseudometric
for labelled Markov chains, which is a reactive model of probabilistic
systems. A similar pseudometric
is defined by van Breugel and Worrell

[
vBW01b
]
via the terminal coalgebra of a functor based on a metric
on the space of Borel probability measures. The metric of
[
DGJP04
,
vBW05
]
works for
continuous probabilistic transition systems, while in this work we
concentrate on discrete systems.
Interestingly, van
Breugel and Worrell
[
vBW01a
]
also present a polynomial-time
algorithm to approximate their coalgebraic distances. Furthermore, van Breugel
et al.
propose an algorithm to approximate a behavioural pseudometric without discount
[
vBSW08
]
. In
[
FPP11
]
a sampling algorithm for calculating bisimulation distances in Markov decision processes is shown to have good performance.
In
[
DAMRS07
,
DAMRS08
]
the probabilistic bisimulation metric on game structures is characterised by a quantitative
\mu
-caluclus. Algorithms for game metrics are proposed in
[
CDAMR10
,
Ram10
]
.

Metrics for nondeterministic probabilistic systems are considered in

[
DJGP02
]
, where Desharnais
et al.
deal with labelled concurrent
Markov chains (similar to pLTSs, this model can be captured by the simple probabilistic
automata of
[
Seg95
]
). They show that the greatest fixed point
of a monotonic function on pseudometrics corresponds to the weak
probabilistic bisimilarity of
[
PLS00
]
.

In
[
SDC07
]
a notion of trace metric is proposed for pLTSs and a tool is developed to compute the trace metric. In
[
DCPP06
]
a notion of bisimulation metric is proposed that extends the approach of
[
DJGP02
,
DGJP04
]
to a more general framework called action-labelled quantitative transition systems.

In
[
DAFS09
]
de Alfaro
et al.
consider metric transition systems in which the propositions at each state are interpreted as elements of metric spaces. In that setting, trace equivalence and bisimulation give rise to linear and branching distances that can be characterised by quantitative versions of linear-time temporal logic

In

In

6
Concluding remarks

We have considered two behavioural pseudometrics for probabilistic labelled transition systems where nondeterminism and probabilities co-exist. They correspond to state-based and distribution-based bisimulations. Our modal characterisation of the state-based bisimulation metric is much simpler than an earlier proposal by Desharnais
et al.
since we only use two non-expansive operators, negation and testing, rather than the general class of non-expansive operators. Our modal characterisaton of the distribution-based bisimulation metric is new. The characterisations are shown to be sound and complete.

In the current work we have not distinguished internal actions from external ones. But it is not difficult to make the distinction and abstract away internal actions so as to introduce weak versions of bisimulation metrics. In a finite-state and finitely branching pLTS, the subdistributions reachable from a state by weak transitions is infinite but can be represented by the convex closure of a finite set
[
Den15
]
. This entails that the logical characterisation of weak bisimulation metrics would be similar to those presented here.

Appendix 0.A
Convex Bisimulation Metric

For
\Pi\subseteq\mathop{\mbox{$\mathcal{D}$}}({S})
we denote by
\cc(\Pi)
the convex closure of
\Pi
. If
\Delta\in\cc(\der(s,a))
then we say
\Delta
is a combined transition of
s

a

s\xrightarrow{\,{a}\,}_{c}\Delta
.

Definition 8
(Convex bisimulation metric).

A
1
-bounded metric
d
on
S
is a
convex bisimulation metric
if for all
s,t\in S
and
\epsilon\in[0,1)
with
d(s,t)\leq\epsilon
, if
s\xrightarrow{\,{a}\,}\Delta
then there exists a
\Delta^{\prime}\in\cc(\der(t,a))
such that
\Kantorovich(d)(\Delta,\Delta^{\prime})\leq\epsilon
.

The smallest (wrt.
\sqsubseteq
) convex bisimulation metric, denoted by
\bisimdconvex
, is called
convex bisimilarity metric
. Convex bisimilarity equivalence (also called probablistic bisimilarity)
[
Seg95
]
is the kernel of the convex bisimilarity metric.

Let us define the functor
\functorconvex\colon[0,1]^{S\times S}\to[0,1]^{S\times S}
for
d\colon S\times S\to[0,1]
and
s,t\in S

\functorconvex(d)(s,t)=\sup_{a\in A}\{\Hausdorff(\Kantorovich(d))(\cc(\der(s,a)),\cc(\der(t,a)))\}\,.

It can be shown that
\functorconvex
is monotone and its least fixed point is the convex bisimilarity metric.

Given a pLTS, we can saturate it with all possible combined transitions. In the saturated pLTS, convex bisimulation metric coincides with state-based bisimulation metric because of the following lemma.

Lemma 6

d
is a convex bisimulation metric if and only if for all
s,t\in S
and
\epsilon\in[0,1)
with
d(s,t)\leq\epsilon
, if
s\xrightarrow{\,{a}\,}_{c}\Delta

then there exists some
t\xrightarrow{\,{a}\,}_{c}\Delta^{\prime}
such that
\Kantorovich(d)(\Delta,\Delta^{\prime})\leq\epsilon
.

Proof.

The “if” direction is trivial. The “only if” direction can be shown by making use of the following inequality:

\Kantorovich(d)(\sum_{i\in I}p_{i}\cdot\Delta_{i},\sum_{i\in I}p_{i}\cdot\Delta^{\prime}_{i})~\leq\sum_{i\in I}p_{i}\cdot\Kantorovich(d)(\Delta_{i},\Delta^{\prime}_{i})

for any pseudometric
d
. This holds because

\begin{array}[]{ll}&\Kantorovich(d)(\sum_{i\in I}p_{i}\cdot\Delta_{i},\sum_{i\in I}p_{i}\cdot\Delta^{\prime}_{i})\\
=&\max\{\sum_{u\in S}((\sum_{i\in I}p_{i}\cdot\Delta_{i})(u)-(\sum_{i\in I}p_{i}\cdot\Delta^{\prime}_{i})(u))x_{u}\mid\\
&\hskip 170.71652ptx_{u},\;x_{u^{\prime}}\in[0,1]\wedge x_{u}-x_{u^{\prime}}\leq d(u,u^{\prime})\}\\
=&\max\{\sum_{u\in S}\sum_{i\in I}p_{i}\cdot(\Delta_{i}(u)-\Delta^{\prime}_{i}(u))x_{u}\mid x_{u},\;x_{u^{\prime}}\in[0,1]\wedge x_{u}-x_{u^{\prime}}\leq d(u,u^{\prime})\}\\
=&\max\{\sum_{i\in I}p_{i}\sum_{u\in S}(\Delta_{i}(u)-\Delta^{\prime}_{i}(u))x_{u}\mid x_{u},\;x_{u^{\prime}}\in[0,1]\wedge x_{u}-x_{u^{\prime}}\leq d(u,u^{\prime})\}\\
\leq&\sum_{i\in I}p_{i}\cdot\max\{\sum_{u\in S}(\Delta_{i}(u)-\Delta^{\prime}_{i}(u))x_{u}\mid x_{u},\;x_{u^{\prime}}\in[0,1]\wedge x_{u}-x_{u^{\prime}}\leq d(u,u^{\prime})\}\\
=&\sum_{i\in I}p_{i}\cdot\Kantorovich(d)(\Delta_{i},\Delta^{\prime}_{i})\end{array}

Appendix 0.B
Trace Metric

In this section we present a notion of trace metric that enjoys a straightforward logical characterisation.

0.B.1
Trace Metric

A trace
{\it tr}
is a string in the set
A^{*}
. We write
\epsilon
for the empty trace, and
{\it tr}_{1}\cdot{\it tr}_{2}
for the concatenation of two traces
{\it tr}_{1}
and
{\it tr}_{2}
. Given a finitely branching pLTS, the maximum probability that state
s
can perform trace
{\it tr}
is defined as follows.

{\it Pr}(s,{\it tr})~=~\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}1&\mbox{if ${\it tr}=\epsilon$}\\
\max_{s\xrightarrow{\,{a}\,}\Delta}\sum_{t\in S}\Delta(t)\cdot{\it Pr}(t,{\it tr}^{\prime})&\mbox{if ${\it tr}=a\cdot{\it tr}^{\prime}$}\end{array}\right.

Definition 9
.

For any two states
s,t\in S
, the
trace distance
between them is given as follows:

\tracedist(s,t)~=~\sup_{{\it tr}\in A^{*}}|{\it Pr}(s,{\it tr})-{\it Pr}(t,{\it tr})|

Intuitively, the trace distance between
s
and
t
is the maximal difference between the maximum probabilities given by
s
and
t
to a same trace.

0.B.2
Logical Characterizations

Let
\logic^{T}
be the set of formulae produced by the following grammar.

\varphi::=\top\mid\langle a\rangle{\varphi}

A formula evaluates in a state
s
as follows:

\begin{array}[]{l}\llbracket{\top}\rrbracket({s})=1\\
\llbracket{\langle a\rangle{\varphi}}\rrbracket({s})=\max_{s\xrightarrow{\,{a}\,}\Delta}\sum_{t\in S}\Delta(t)\cdot\llbracket{\varphi}\rrbracket({t})\end{array}

This logic induces a natural metric
\tracedistl
.

\tracedistl(s,t)~=~\sup_{\varphi\in\mathbb{O}_{tr}}|\llbracket\varphi\rrbracket(s)-\llbracket\varphi\rrbracket(t)|

Theorem 0.B.1

\tracedist=\tracedistl

Proof.

It is easy to see that there is a bijection
f
between
A^{*}
and
\logic^{T}
.

\begin{array}[]{rcl}f(\epsilon)&=&\top\\
f(a\cdot{\it tr})&=&\langle a\rangle{f({\it tr})}\end{array}

For any state
s
and trace
{\it tr}
, we can show that

{\it Pr}(s,{\it tr})=\llbracket{f({\it tr})}\rrbracket({s})

by induction on the length of
{\it tr}
. The required result now easily follows.

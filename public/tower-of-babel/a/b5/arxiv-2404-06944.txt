A negative result on regularity estimates of finite radial Morse index solutions to elliptic problems. Thanks: The authors have been supported by the FEDER-MINECO Grant PID2021-122122NBI00 and by J. Andalucia (FQM-116).
J. Silverio Martínez-Baena, Salvador Villegas

Abstract
. In the regularity theory of solutions to elliptic partial differential equations often the concept of stability plays the role of a sufficient condition for smoothness. It is a natural question to ask if this holds true for nonstable but finite Morse index solutions. We provide a negative answer showing the existence of sequences of solutions with radial Morse index equal to 1 for which regularity estimates can not be satisfied.



1.
Introduction

In this paper we will be concern about the Morse index stability properties of solutions
u:\Omega\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
of the nonlinear elliptic Dirichlet problem:

(1.1)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{rcll}-\Delta u&=&f(u)&\text{ in }\Omega\\
u&=&0&\text{ on }\partial\Omega\end{array}\right.

We will focus on
\Omega=B_{1}
the unit ball in
\mathbb{R}^{N}
with dimension
N\geq 3
and the nonlinearity
f\in C^{1}(\mathbb{R})
being nonnegative and nondecreasing. Under these assumptions, it is well known by the celebrated Gidas-Ni-Nirenberg symmetry result
[
7
]
that
u
is radially symmetric and decreasing. For the sake of generality of the following definitions let us take
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{N}
to be any bounded domain and
\Omega^{\prime}\subset\Omega
a subdomain. The problem (
1.1
) corresponds to the Euler-Lagrange equation for the energy functional

(1.2)

E_{\Omega}[u]=\int_{\Omega}\left(\frac{1}{2}|\nabla u|^{2}-F(u)\right)dx,

where
F(t)=\int_{0}^{t}f(s)ds
. Consider the second variation of (
1.2
) that when
f^{\prime}(u)\in L^{1}_{loc}(\Omega)
is given by

Q_{u,\Omega}[\varphi]:=\left.\frac{d^{2}}{d\varepsilon^{2}}\right|_{\varepsilon=0}E_{\Omega}[u+\varepsilon\varphi]:=\int_{\Omega}\left(|\nabla\varphi|^{2}-f^{\prime}(u)\varphi^{2}\right)dx.

We say that a solution

Q_{u,\omega_{x}}[\varphi]\geq 0
for all
\varphi\in C_{0}^{1}(\omega_{x})
.

Quite recently, in
[
3

the authors have shown that for nonlinearities
f\geq 0

, stable solutions to (

(1.3)

\displaystyle||\nabla u||_{L^{2+\gamma}(B_{1/2})}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C||u||_{L^{1}(B_{1})}\ \ \ (N\geq 1)

(1.4)

\displaystyle||u||_{C^{\alpha}(\overline{B}_{1/2})}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C||u||_{L^{1}(B_{1})}\ \ \ (1\leq N\leq 9)

with

Furthermore, in

These results provide a good control on the behaviour of solutions under the assumption of stability, which can be a too strong assumption in some situations so it is reasonable to require results in the same direction for a larger class of solutions. Since its first introduction by M. Morse in the early 20th century in

Definition 1.2

(Morse Index

Let

We shall use boundedness of radial Morse index as the criterium to the extent the class of solutions under study.

Remark 1.3
.

It is immediate from the definition that,

ii)

ind_{r}(u,\Omega^{\prime})\leq ind(u,\Omega^{\prime})

[

(2)

Morse index had been proposed to characterize the uniform boundedness of a solution for the first time in
[
1

and prove the equivalence between boundedness of solutions and of their Morse index. In
[

They also mention that in the critical case, one can find a counterexample to the boundedness of finite Morse index solutions. More precisely, if one define

U(\lambda,x)=\left(\frac{\sqrt{\lambda N(N-2)}}{\lambda^{2}+|x|^{2}}\right)^{\frac{N-2}{2}}

are Morse index

1.1
) for the critical nonlinearity

that exhibit a singular behaviour
\{||u_{\lambda}||_{L^{\infty}(B_{1})}\}\rightarrow\infty
,
\{||u_{\lambda}||_{L^{1}(B_{1})}\}\rightarrow 0
as
\lambda\rightarrow 0^{+}
. This example shows that one can not expect to get something similar to Theorem

2.
Main Theorem.

Theorem 2.1
.

Let
1\leq q<p\leq\infty
with
p>\frac{N}{N-2}
. For
3\leq N\leq 9
there exists a sequence
\{u_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\subset C^{\infty}(\overline{B_{1}})
of solutions to Dirichlet problems (
1.1
) with nonlinearities
\{f_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\subset C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})
and radial Morse index
ind_{r}(u_{n})=1\ \ \forall n\in\mathbb{N}
, such that:

(2.1)

\frac{||u_{n}||_{L^{p}(B_{1})}}{||u_{n}||_{L^{q}(B_{1})}}\longrightarrow+\infty\ \ \ \ \ \text{as}\ \ n\rightarrow\infty

Remark 2.2
.

It is remarkable the fact that we are not able to find divergent sequences for
q<p\leq\frac{N}{N-2}
. The “raison d’être” of this kind of bound in
p
and its optimality remains as an interesting open question. We want also to emphasize again that this divergent behaviour is contrary to the stability case in which the estimate in the Theorem
1.1

i)
automatically implies the quotient that appears in (
2.1
) to be bounded.

Our main result can be interpreted as the impossibility to find a sort of reciprocal result of Prop.
1.4
-
(
2
)
, i.e., the impossibility to prove certain notion of regularity of a solution only from the boundedness of its Morse index. It also remains as an open problem to find sufficient conditions to ensure such a reciprocal.

3.
Proof of the main theorem.

For the sake of clarity before going into the proof of the main theorem we show the following tree lemmas.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
\Omega=B_{1}
,
u(x)=u(|x|)\equiv u(r)
a solution of (
1.1
). Assume
ind_{r}(u)=0
in
B_{\delta}
and in

for some

ind_{r}(u)\leq 1
in
B_{1}

Proof.

If on the contrary
ind_{r}(u)>1

such that
Q_{u,B_{1}}[\varphi]<0
for any
\varphi\in X\setminus\{0\}
. We choose two linearly independent
\varphi_{1},\varphi_{2}\in X
. Clearly
\varphi_{2}(\delta)\not=0
because otherwise

Q_{u,B_{1}}[\varphi_{2}]=Q_{u,B_{\delta}}\left[\varphi_{2}|_{{}_{B_{\delta}}}\right]+Q_{u,B_{1}\setminus\overline{B_{\delta}}}\left[\varphi_{2}|_{{}_{B_{1}\setminus\overline{B_{\delta}}}}\right]\geq 0,

which is immediately contradictory. We can define,

\varphi=\frac{\varphi_{1}(\delta)}{\varphi_{2}(\delta)}\varphi_{2}-\varphi_{1}

that vanishes at
r=\delta
. Hence

Q_{u,B_{1}}[\varphi]=Q_{u,B_{\delta}}\left[\varphi|_{{}_{B_{\delta}}}\right]+Q_{u,B_{1}\setminus\overline{B_{\delta}}}\left[\varphi|_{{}_{B_{1}\setminus\overline{B_{\delta}}}}\right]\geq 0,

which is a contradiction.∎

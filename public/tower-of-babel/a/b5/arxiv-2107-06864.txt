Abstract.
In this note, we extend the definition of multiple harmonic sums and apply their stuffle relations to obtain explicit evaluations of the sums
R_{n}(p,t)=\displaystyle\sum\nolimits_{m=0}^{n}m^{p}H_{m}^{t}
, where
H_{m}
are harmonic numbers. When
t\leq 4
these sums were first studied by Spieß around 1990 and, more recently, by Jin and Sun. Our key step first is to find an explicit formula of a special type of the extended multiple harmonic sums. This also enables us to provide a general structural result of the sums
R_{n}(p,t)
for all
t\geq 0
.

Keywords
: Bernoulli number; harmonic number; multiple harmonic sum; extended multiple harmonic sum.

05A19; 11B73; 11M32; 68R05.

1.
Introduction

We begin with some basic notations. Let
\mathbb{N}
(resp.
\mathbb{Z}
) be the set of positive integers (resp. integers) and
\mathbb{N}_{0}:=\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}
.
A finite sequence
{\bf{k}}:=(k_{1},\ldots,k_{r})\in\mathbb{N}^{r}
is called a
composition
. We define its
weight
and the
depth
of
{\bf{k}}
, respectively, by

|{\bf{k}}|:=k_{1}+\cdots+k_{r}\quad\text{and}\quad\dep({\bf{k}}):=r.

For any
n\in\mathbb{N}
and
(k_{1},\ldots,k_{r})\in\mathbb{Z}^{r}
, we define the
extended multiple harmonic sums
by

(1.1)

\displaystyle H_{n}(k_{1},\ldots,k_{r}):=\displaystyle\sum\limits_{n\geq n_{1}>\cdots>n_{r}>0}\displaystyle\frac{1}{n_{1}^{k_{1}}\cdots n_{r}^{k_{r}}}.

We set
H_{n}(\emptyset):=1
and
H_{n}(k_{1},\ldots,k_{r}):=0
if
n<r
. When
r=1
and
k>0
,
H_{n}(k)=\displaystyle\sum\nolimits_{j=1}^{n}{1}/{j^{k}}
is the
n
-th generalized harmonic number of order
k
, and furthermore, if
k=1
then
H_{n}:=H_{n}(1)
is the classical
n
-th harmonic number. Identities involving harmonic numbers and multiple harmonic sums (i.e., when all
k_{j}>0
) have been extensively studied in the literature (see, e.g.,
[
6
]
and the references therein).

Let
(k_{1},\ldots,k_{r})\in\mathbb{N}^{r}
. In this note, we are particularly interested in the sums of the form

(1.2)

\displaystyle H_{n}(-k_{1},k_{2},\ldots,k_{r})=\displaystyle\sum\limits_{n\geq n_{1}>\cdots>n_{r}>0}n_{1}^{k_{1}}\displaystyle\frac{1}{n_{2}^{k_{2}}\cdots n_{r}^{k_{r}}}=\displaystyle\sum_{m=1}^{n}m^{k_{1}}H_{m-1}(k_{2},\cdots,k_{r}).

Recall that there are two versions of Bernoulli numbers defined by the generating functions

(1.3)

\displaystyle\frac{te^{t}}{e^{t}-1}=\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}B_{n}\displaystyle\frac{t^{n}}{n!}\qquad\text{and}\qquad\displaystyle\frac{t}{e^{t}-1}=\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}{\widetilde{B}}_{n}\displaystyle\frac{t^{n}}{n!}.

It is well-known that
B_{j}={\widetilde{B}}_{j}
if
j\neq 1
,
B_{1}=1/2
and
{\widetilde{B}}_{1}=-1/2
.
By Faulhaber’s formula,

(1.4)

\displaystyle H_{n}(-k)=\displaystyle\sum_{m=1}^{n}m^{k}=\displaystyle\frac{1}{k+1}\displaystyle\sum_{j=0}^{k}\binom{k+1}{j}B_{j}n^{k+1-j}=n^{k}+\displaystyle\frac{1}{k+1}\displaystyle\sum_{j=0}^{k}\binom{k+1}{j}{\widetilde{B}}_{j}n^{k+1-j}.

In
[
3
]
, Spieß studied the summation

(1.5)

R_{n}(d,t):=\displaystyle\sum_{m=0}^{n}m^{d}H_{m}^{t}\quad(n\in\mathbb{N},d,t\in\mathbb{N}_{0})

and proved the following structure theorem (see
[
3
, Thm. 30]
).

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
p(m)
be a polynomial in
m
of degree
d
. Then for
t=1,2
or
3
, there exist polynomials
q_{0}(n),\ldots,q_{t}(n)
and
C(n)
of degree at most
d+1
such that

(1.6)

\displaystyle\displaystyle\sum_{m=0}^{n}p(m)H_{m}^{t}=\displaystyle\sum_{i=0}^{t}q_{i}(n)H_{n}^{i}+C(n)H_{n}(2),

for all nonnegative integers
n
. Moreover,
C(n)=0
when
t=1,2
.

Spieß also conjectured that Theorem
1.1
holds for any positive integer
t\geq 4
. However, Jin and Sun
[
4
]
showed that the sum
R_{n}(0,4)=\displaystyle\sum\nolimits_{m=0}^{n}H_{m}^{4}
cannot be represented by the form as conjectured by Spieß. Moreover, Jin and Sun
[
4
, Thms. 1.2 and 1.3]
proved that

(1.7)

\displaystyle R_{n}(d,3)=\displaystyle\sum_{m=1}^{n}m^{d}H_{m}^{3}=H_{n}(-d)H_{n}^{3}+q_{1}(n)H_{n}^{2}+q_{2}(n)H_{n}+q_{3}(n)+\displaystyle\frac{{\widetilde{B}}_{d}}{2}H_{n}(2),

where
q_{1}(n),q_{2}(n)
and
q_{3}(n)
are polynomial in
n
of degree at most
d+1
. They also provided similar formulas for the sums

(1.8)

\displaystyle\displaystyle\sum_{m=0}^{n}p(m)H_{m}^{4}\qquad\text{and}\qquad\displaystyle\sum_{m=0}^{n}m^{d}H_{m}H_{m}(2).

Nevertheless, the explicit formula of the polynomials
q_{1}(n),q_{2}(n),q_{3}(n)
were not found.

The primary goal of this note is to establish explicit formulas of (
1.7
) and (
1.8
). Our main idea is to express these sums using (
1.2
) for which
we are able to derive an explicit formula in general (see Theorem
2.1
). At the end of the note, we present a result generalizing Theorem
1.1
to all
t\geq 0
(see Theorem
2.15
).

2.
Explicit Formulas of Spieß’s Results

In this section we will study the sums of the form

(2.9)

\displaystyle\sum_{m=1}^{n}m^{p}H_{m-1}^{t}

for
p,t\geq 0
. We remark that this is slightly different from
R_{n}(p,t)
(see (
1.5
))
considered by Spieß
[
3
]
but
they are closely related. See Remark
2.13
.
The key idea to study (
2.9
) is to express
H_{n}^{t}
by using general multiple harmonic
sums of the form (
1.2
) by applying the stuffle relations, also called quasi-shuffle relations (see

[
1
]
). In fact, more generally, we know
that for any composition
{\bf{k}}=(k_{1},\ldots,k_{r})
, the product
H_{n}(k_{1})\cdots H_{n}(k_{r})
can be expressed in terms of a linear combination of multiple harmonic sums (for the explicit formula, see
[
5
, Eq. (2.4)]
), for example

(2.10)

H_{n}H_{n}(2)=H_{n}(1)H_{n}(2)=H_{n}(1,2)+H_{n}(2,1)+H_{n}(3).

2.1.
Some Explicit Formulas of General MHSs

To derive the general formula for
H_{n}(-p,{\bf{k}})
where
{\bf{k}}=(k_{1},\dots,k_{r})\in\mathbb{N}^{r}
,
we set
{\bf{k}}_{i}=(k_{1},\dots,k_{i})
for all
1\leq i\leq r
and
|{\bf{k}}_{i}|=k_{0}+\dotsm+k_{i}
for all
0\leq i\leq r
.

Theorem 2.1
.

Let
p\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
,
r\in\mathbb{N}
, and
k_{1},\dots,k_{r}\in\mathbb{N}
. Put
k_{0}=j_{0}=0
,
k^{\prime}_{r}=k_{r+1}=1
and
k^{\prime}_{l}=k_{l}
for all
l<r
. Then

\displaystyle H_{n}(-p,k_{1},\ldots,k_{r})=

\displaystyle-

\displaystyle\displaystyle\sum_{l=1}^{r}(-1)^{l}\left(\displaystyle\sum\limits_{0\leq j_{i}\leq p+i-|{\bf{k}}^{\prime}_{i}|-|{\bf{j}}_{i-1}|\ \forall 1\leq i\leq l}\prod_{h=0}^{l-1}\displaystyle\frac{\binom{p+h+1-|{\bf{k}}_{h}|-|{\bf{j}}_{h}|}{j_{h+1}}B_{j_{h+1}}}{p+h+1-|{\bf{k}}_{h}|-|{\bf{j}}_{h}|}\right)n^{p+l-|{\bf{k}}_{l-1}|-|{\bf{j}}_{l}|}\cdot H_{n}(k_{l},\dotsc,k_{r})

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\displaystyle\sum_{l=1}^{r}(-1)^{l}\left(\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}p+l+1-|{\bf{k}}_{l}|\leq|{\bf{j}}_{l}|\\
\leq p+l-1-|{\bf{k}}_{l-1}|\end{subarray}}\prod_{h=0}^{l-1}\displaystyle\frac{\binom{p+h+1-|{\bf{k}}_{h}|-|{\bf{j}}_{h}|}{j_{h+1}}B_{j_{h+1}}}{p+h+1-|{\bf{k}}_{h}|-|{\bf{j}}_{h}|}\right)H_{n}\Big(|{\bf{k}}_{l}|+|{\bf{j}}_{l}|-l-p,k_{l+1},\dotsc,k_{r}\Big)

(2.11)

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle(-1)^{r}\left(\displaystyle\sum\limits_{0\leq j_{l}\leq p+l-|{\bf{k}}_{l}|-|{\bf{j}}_{l-1}|\ \forall 1\leq l\leq r+1}\prod_{h=0}^{r}\displaystyle\frac{\binom{p+h+1-|{\bf{k}}_{h}|-|{\bf{j}}_{h}|}{j_{h+1}}B_{j_{h+1}}}{p+h+1-|{\bf{k}}_{h}|-|{\bf{j}}_{h}|}\right)n^{p+r+1-|{\bf{k}}_{r}|-|{\bf{j}}_{r+1}|},

where we set the binomial coefficients
\binom{a}{b}=0
if
b<0
.

Proof.

According to definition (
1.2
), we have the following recurrence relation

\displaystyle H_{n}(-p,k_{1},\ldots,k_{r})

\displaystyle=\displaystyle\sum_{m=1}^{n}m^{p}H_{m-1}(k_{1},\ldots,k_{r})=\displaystyle\sum_{m=1}^{n}m^{p}\displaystyle\sum\limits_{m>n_{1}>\cdots>n_{r}\geq 1}\displaystyle\frac{1}{n_{1}^{k_{1}}\cdots n_{r}^{k_{r}}}

\displaystyle=\displaystyle\sum_{m=1}^{n}m^{p}\left(H_{m}(k_{1},\ldots,k_{r})-\displaystyle\frac{H_{m-1}(k_{2},\ldots,k_{r})}{m^{k_{1}}}\right)

\displaystyle=\displaystyle\sum_{m=1}^{n}m^{p}H_{m}(k_{1},\ldots,k_{r})-\displaystyle\sum_{m=1}^{n}m^{p-k_{1}}H_{m-1}(k_{2},\ldots,k_{r})

\displaystyle=\displaystyle\sum_{m=1}^{n}m^{p}\displaystyle\sum\limits_{m\geq n_{1}>\cdots>n_{r}\geq 1}\displaystyle\frac{1}{n_{1}^{k_{1}}\cdots n_{r}^{k_{r}}}-\displaystyle\sum_{m=1}^{n}m^{p-k_{1}}H_{m-1}(k_{2},\ldots,k_{r})

\displaystyle=\displaystyle\sum_{n_{1}=1}^{n}\displaystyle\frac{H_{n_{1}-1}(k_{2},\ldots,k_{r})}{n_{1}^{k_{1}}}\displaystyle\sum_{m=n_{1}}^{n}m^{p}-\displaystyle\sum_{m=1}^{n}m^{p-k_{1}}H_{m-1}(k_{2},\ldots,k_{r})

\displaystyle=\displaystyle\sum_{n_{1}=1}^{n}\displaystyle\frac{H_{n_{1}-1}(k_{2},\ldots,k_{r})}{n_{1}^{k_{1}}}\left(\displaystyle\sum_{m=1}^{n}m^{p}-\displaystyle\sum_{m=1}^{n_{1}-1}m^{p}-n_{1}^{p}\right)

\displaystyle=H_{n}(-p)H_{n}(k_{1},\ldots,k_{r})-\displaystyle\sum_{n_{1}=1}^{n}\displaystyle\frac{H_{n_{1}-1}(k_{2},\ldots,k_{r})}{n_{1}^{k_{1}}}\displaystyle\sum_{m=1}^{n_{1}}m^{p}

\displaystyle=H_{n}(-p)H_{n}(k_{1},\ldots,k_{r})-\displaystyle\sum_{j=0}^{p}\binom{p+1}{j}\displaystyle\frac{B_{j}}{p+1}\displaystyle\sum_{n_{1}=1}^{n}\displaystyle\frac{H_{n_{1}-1}(k_{2},\ldots,k_{r})}{n_{1}^{k_{1}}}n_{1}^{p+1-j}

(2.12)

\displaystyle=H_{n}(-p)H_{n}(k_{1},\ldots,k_{r})-\displaystyle\sum_{j=0}^{p}\binom{p+1}{j}\displaystyle\frac{B_{j}}{p+1}H_{n}(k_{1}+j-p-1,k_{2},\ldots,k_{r}).

If
k_{1}>p+1
in (
2.1
) then
k_{1}+j-p-1\in\mathbb{N}
, so
H_{n}(k_{1}+j-p-1,k_{2},\ldots,k_{r})
is the classical multiple harmonic sum. If
1\leq k_{1}\leq p+1
then (
2.1
) can be rewritten in the following form

\displaystyle H_{n}(-p,{\bf{k}})=H_{n}(-p)H_{n}({\bf{k}})

\displaystyle-\displaystyle\sum_{j=p+2-k_{1}}^{p}\binom{p+1}{j}\displaystyle\frac{B_{j}}{p+1}H_{n}(k_{1}+j-p-1,k_{2},\ldots,k_{r})

(2.13)

\displaystyle-\displaystyle\sum_{j=0}^{p+1-k_{1}}\binom{p+1}{j}\displaystyle\frac{B_{j}}{p+1}H_{n}(k_{1}+j-p-1,k_{2},\ldots,k_{r}).

When
r=1
this implies the theorem by replacing
k
by
p
in (
1.4
):

\displaystyle H_{n}(-p,k_{1})=H_{n}(-p)H_{n}(k_{1})-\displaystyle\sum_{j_{1}=p+2-k_{1}}^{p}\binom{p+1}{j_{1}}\displaystyle\frac{B_{j_{1}}}{p+1}H_{n}(k_{1}+j_{1}-p-1)

(2.14)

\displaystyle-\displaystyle\sum_{j_{1}=0}^{p+1-k_{1}}\displaystyle\sum_{j_{2}=0}^{p+1-k_{1}-j_{1}}\binom{p+1}{j_{1}}\binom{p+2-k_{1}-j_{1}}{j_{2}}\displaystyle\frac{B_{j_{1}}B_{j_{2}}}{(p+1)(p+2-k_{1}-j_{1})}n^{p+2-k_{1}-j_{1}-j_{2}}.

By applying the above recurrence relation, one can prove the theorem by induction on
r
using (
2.1
).
We leave the detail to the interested reader.
∎

To state our results more concisely, we set
j_{0}=a_{1}=0
and for all
a_{2},\dots,a_{r}\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
we define the polynomials

C^{(p)}_{a_{2},\dots,a_{r}}(x):=\displaystyle\sum\limits_{j_{1}+\dots+j_{r}\leq p-a_{r}\atop j_{1},\dotsc,j_{r}\geq 0}\left(\prod_{i=1}^{r}\displaystyle\frac{\binom{p+1-a_{i}-j_{1}-\dotsm-j_{i-1}}{j_{i}}B_{j_{i}}}{p+1-a_{i}-j_{1}-\dotsm-j_{i-1}}\right)x^{p+1-a_{r}-j_{1}-\dotsm-j_{r}}.

In particular, we see that the subscript on the left-hand side is vacuous if
r=1
and in this case
C^{(p)}(n)=H_{n}(-p)
.

Corollary 2.2
.

For a composition
{\bf{k}}=(k_{1},\ldots,k_{r})
and
p\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
, the sum
H_{n}(-p,{\bf{k}})
can be expressed in terms of a combination of products of polynomial in
n
of degree
\leq p+1
and multiple harmonic sums with depth
\leq r
. In particular, if
{\bf{k}}=(\{1\}_{r})
then we have

(2.15)

\displaystyle H_{n}(-p,\{1\}_{r})=H_{n}(-p)H_{n}(\{1\}_{r})+\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{r}(-1)^{i}C^{(p)}_{\underbrace{\scriptstyle 0,\dotsc,0}_{i}}(n)H_{n}(\{1\}_{r-i}),

where
\{1\}_{r}
means the string obtained by repeating
1
exactly
r
times.

Proof.

Observe that the
l
-th term in the second sum of (
2.1
) is vacuous if
k_{l}=1
. The rest of the proof
is straight-forward.
∎

Setting
r=2
in (
2.15
) and noting the fact that

(2.16)

H_{n}(1,1)=\displaystyle\frac{H_{n}^{2}-H_{n}(2)}{2},

we get the following corollary.

Corollary 2.3
.

For
p\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
,

\displaystyle H_{n}(-p,1,1)

\displaystyle=H_{n}(-p)H_{n}(\{1\}_{2})-C^{(p)}_{0}(n)H_{n}+C^{(p)}_{0,0}(n)

(2.17)

\displaystyle=\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}H_{n}(-p)(H_{n}^{2}-H_{n}(2))-C^{(p)}_{0}(n)H_{n}+C^{(p)}_{0,0}(n).

Taking
r=2
in (
2.1
) we can get the following corollary.

Corollary 2.4
.

For positive integers
k_{1},k_{2}
and nonnegative
p
, we have

\displaystyle H_{n}(-p,k_{1},k_{2})=H_{n}(-p)H_{n}(k_{1},k_{2})-\displaystyle\frac{1}{p+1}\displaystyle\sum_{j=p+2-k_{1}}^{p}\binom{p+1}{j}B_{j}H_{n}(k_{1}+j-p-1,k_{2})

\displaystyle\quad+\displaystyle\frac{1}{p+1}\displaystyle\sum_{j_{1}=0}^{p+1-k_{1}}\displaystyle\sum_{j_{2}=p+3-k_{1}-k_{2}-j_{1}}^{p+1-k_{1}-j_{1}}\displaystyle\frac{\binom{p+1}{j_{1}}\binom{p+2-k_{1}-j_{1}}{j_{2}}}{p+2-k_{1}-j_{1}}B_{j_{1}}B_{j_{2}}H_{n}(k_{1}+k_{2}+j_{1}+j_{2}-p-2)

\displaystyle\quad+\displaystyle\frac{1}{p+1}\displaystyle\sum_{j_{1}=0}^{p+1-k_{1}}\displaystyle\sum_{j_{2}=0}^{p+2-k_{1}-k_{2}-j_{1}}\displaystyle\sum_{j_{3}=0}^{p+2-k_{1}-k_{2}-j_{1}-j_{2}}\displaystyle\frac{\binom{p+1}{j_{1}}\binom{p+2-k_{1}-j_{1}}{j_{2}}\binom{p+3-k_{1}-k_{2}-j_{1}-j_{2}}{j_{3}}}{(p+2-k_{1}-j_{1})(p+3-k_{1}-k_{2}-j_{1}-j_{2})}

\displaystyle\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\times B_{j_{1}}B_{j_{2}}B_{j_{3}}n^{p+3-k_{1}-k_{2}-j_{1}-j_{2}-j_{3}}

(2.18)

\displaystyle\quad-\displaystyle\frac{1}{p+1}\displaystyle\sum_{j=0}^{p+1-k_{1}}\displaystyle\sum_{l=0}^{p+1-k_{1}-j}\displaystyle\frac{\binom{p+1}{j}\binom{p+2-k_{1}-j}{l}}{p+2-k_{1}-j}n^{p+2-k_{1}-j-l}B_{j}B_{l}H_{n}(k_{2}).

For
p\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
, put
D^{(p)}_{a_{2},\dots,a_{r}}(B):=C^{(p)}_{a_{2},\dots,a_{r}}(B)/B
with
B^{i}
understood as
B_{i}

which is the convention in umbral calculus.
Setting
(k_{1},k_{2})=(1,2)
and
(2,1)
in (
2.4
), respectively, we get the following two formulas:

(2.19)

\displaystyle H_{n}(-p,1,2)

\displaystyle=H_{n}(-p)H_{n}(1,2)+D^{(p)}(B)H_{n}-C^{(p)}_{0}(n)H_{n}(2)+C^{(p)}_{0,1}(n),

(2.20)

\displaystyle H_{n}(-p,2,1)

\displaystyle=H_{n}(-p)H_{n}(2,1)-B_{p}H_{n}(1,1)-C^{(p)}_{1}(n)H_{n}+C^{(p)}_{1,1}(n).

For
p=0,1
in (
2.19
) and (
2.20
), we get

\displaystyle H_{n}(0,1,2)

\displaystyle=nH_{n}(1,2)-nH_{n}(2)+H_{n},

\displaystyle H_{n}(0,2,1)

\displaystyle=nH_{n}(2,1)-\displaystyle\frac{H_{n}^{2}-H_{n}(2)}{2},

\displaystyle H_{n}(-1,1,2)

\displaystyle=\displaystyle\frac{n(n+1)}{2}H_{n}(1,2)-\displaystyle\frac{n(n+3)}{4}H_{n}^{2}+\displaystyle\frac{3}{4}H_{n}+\displaystyle\frac{1}{4}n,

\displaystyle H_{n}(-1,2,1)

\displaystyle=\displaystyle\frac{n(n+1)}{2}H_{n}(2,1)-\displaystyle\frac{1}{4}(H_{n}^{2}-H_{n}(2))-\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}nH_{n}+\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}n.

2.2.
Explicit Formulas of Spieß’s results

We now present some explicit evaluations of Spieß’s results on harmonic numbers (Theorem
1.1
).

Theorem 2.5
.

For
p\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
, we have

(2.21)

\displaystyle\displaystyle\sum_{m=1}^{n}m^{p}H_{m-1}^{2}=C^{(p)}(n)H_{n}^{2}-(2C_{0}^{(p)}(n)+B_{p})H_{n}+2C_{0,0}^{(p)}(n)-C_{1}^{(p)}(n).

Proof.

By the stuffle relation (
2.16
) we have

\displaystyle\sum_{m=1}^{n}m^{p}H_{m-1}^{2}=2H_{n}(-p,1,1)+H_{n}(-p,2).

Taking
k=2
in (
2.1
) we see that

\displaystyle H_{n}(-p,2)=H_{n}(-p)H_{n}(2)-B_{p}H_{n}-C_{1}^{(p)}(n).

Combining the above with (
2.3
), we arrive at (
2.21
) immediately.
∎

Example 2.6
.

Letting
p=0,1
in (
2.21
) we get

\displaystyle\displaystyle\sum_{m=1}^{n}H_{m-1}^{2}

\displaystyle=nH_{n}^{2}-(2n+1)H_{n}+2n,

\displaystyle\displaystyle\sum_{m=1}^{n}mH_{m-1}^{2}

\displaystyle=\displaystyle\frac{n(n+1)}{2}H_{n}^{2}-\displaystyle\frac{n^{2}+3n+1}{2}H_{n}+\displaystyle\frac{n(n+5)}{4}.

Theorem 2.7
.

For
p\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
, we have

\displaystyle\displaystyle\sum_{m=1}^{n}m^{p}H_{m-1}H_{m-1}(2)

\displaystyle=H_{n}(-p)H_{n}H_{n}(2)-\displaystyle\frac{B_{p}}{2}H_{n}^{2}+\bigg(D^{(p)}(B)-C_{1}^{(p)}(n)-\displaystyle\frac{p}{2}B_{p-1}\bigg)H_{n}

(2.22)

\displaystyle\quad-\bigg(C_{0}^{(p)}(n)+\displaystyle\frac{B_{p}}{2}\bigg)H_{n}(2)+C_{0,1}^{(p)}(n)+C_{1,1}^{(p)}(n)-C_{2}^{(p)}(n).

Proof.

Applying identities (
2.10
), (
2.1
), (
2.19
) and (
2.20
), we obtain the explicit evaluation of sum
\displaystyle\sum_{m=1}^{n}m^{p}H_{m-1}H_{m-1}(2)
by a direct calculation. ∎

Example 2.8
.

Letting
p=0,1
in (
2.7
), we get

\displaystyle\displaystyle\sum_{m=1}^{n}H_{m-1}H_{m-1}(2)

\displaystyle=nH_{n}H_{n}(2)-\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}H_{n}^{2}-\displaystyle\frac{2n+1}{2}H_{n}(2)+H_{n},

\displaystyle\displaystyle\sum_{m=1}^{n}mH_{m-1}H_{m-1}(2)

\displaystyle=\displaystyle\frac{n(n+1)}{2}H_{n}H_{n}(2)-\displaystyle\frac{1}{4}H_{n}^{2}-\displaystyle\frac{n^{2}+3n+1}{4}H_{n}(2)+\displaystyle\frac{1-2n}{4}H_{n}+\displaystyle\frac{3}{4}n.

Theorem 2.9
.

For
p\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
, we have

\displaystyle\displaystyle\sum_{m=1}^{n}m^{p}H_{m-1}^{3}=

\displaystyle H_{n}(-p)H_{n}^{3}-3\bigg(C^{(p)}_{0}(n)+\displaystyle\frac{B_{p}}{2}\bigg)H_{n}^{2}+{\displaystyle\frac{B_{p}}{2}}H_{n}(2)

\displaystyle+\bigg(6C^{(p)}_{0,0}(n)+3D^{(p)}(B)-3C^{(p)}_{1}(n)-\displaystyle\frac{p}{2}B_{p-1}\bigg)H_{n}

\displaystyle-6C^{(p)}_{0,0,0}(n)+3C^{(p)}_{0,1}(n)+3C^{(p)}_{1,1}(n)-{C^{(p)}_{2}(n)}.

Proof.

By the stuffle relation we have

u_{i}=a_{i}\eta^{m_{i}}
with
a_{i}\in k(\alpha),

m_{i}
an integer, and
v\in k(\alpha)[\eta]
. Since
u_{i}^{\prime}/u_{i}=a_{i}^{\prime}/a_{i}+m_{i}\eta^{\prime}/\eta=a_{i}^{\prime}/a_{i}+m_{i}\alpha
. We have

f=\sum_{i=1}^{n}c_{i}\frac{a_{i}^{\prime}}{a_{i}}+(\sum_{i=1}^{n}m_{i}c_{i})\alpha+v^{\prime}.

This implies
v^{\prime}\in k(\alpha).
Applying Kolchin-Ostrowski,
[
RS88
, Appendix]
, we have
v\in k(\alpha).
Hence

(3.1)

f=\sum_{i=1}^{n}c_{i}\frac{a_{i}^{\prime}}{a_{i}}+c\alpha+v^{\prime}.

where
a_{1},\dots,a_{n},v\in k(\alpha)
and
c=\sum_{i=1}^{n}m_{i}c_{i}
.

Let
G
be the Galois group of the quadratic extension
k(\alpha)
of
k.
Then, for all
x\in k(\alpha),

\mathrm{tr}(x)=\sum_{\sigma\in G}\sigma(x)\in k,\qquad\mathrm{nr}(x)=\prod_{\sigma\in G}\sigma(x)\in k\quad\text{and}\quad\mathrm{tr}(x)^{\prime}=\mathrm{tr}(x^{\prime}).

Applying the trace map to Equation (
3.1
), we obtain

\displaystyle 2f

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{n}c_{i}\mathrm{tr}\left(\frac{a_{i}^{\prime}}{a_{i}}\right)+c\ \mathrm{tr}(\alpha)+\mathrm{tr}(v^{\prime})

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{n}c_{i}\left(\frac{\mathrm{nr}(a_{i})^{\prime}}{\mathrm{nr}(a_{i})}\right)+c\ \mathrm{tr}(\alpha)+\mathrm{tr}(v)^{\prime}.

Since we have proved in Proposition
2.1
that
\mathrm{tr}(\alpha)=\frac{1}{2}\frac{\gamma^{\prime}}{\gamma}
for some
\gamma\in k,
the proof of the Lemma is now complete. ∎

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
E
be a Picard-Vessiot extension of
k
having a differential Galois group isomorphic to
\mathrm{SL}_{2}(C).
If
f\in k
has an elementary integral over
E
then it has an elementary integral over
k.

Proof.

We first resolve
E
as a tower of differential fields as in Proposition
2.2
(
ii
). Then for
y:=\omega/\xi^{2},
we have

\beta:=\frac{y^{\prime}}{y}=\frac{\omega^{\prime}}{\omega}-2\alpha\in k(\alpha)

and therefore,
k(\alpha)=k(\beta)
and that
k(\alpha,y)
is a differential field. Since
\eta^{\prime}=y
and that
\xi^{2}\in k(\alpha,y),
we have the following tower of differential fields:

k\subsetneqq k(\alpha)\subsetneqq k(\alpha,y)\subsetneqq k(\alpha,y,\eta)\subsetneqq E=k(\alpha,y,\eta)(\xi),

where
\alpha,\xi
and
\eta
are as in Proposition
2.2
(
ii
) and
E
is a quadratic extension of
k(\alpha,y,\eta)
with
\xi^{2}\in k(\alpha,y).
We divide the rest of the proof into four steps.

Step 1.
Let

c_{1}\frac{u_{1}^{\prime}}{u_{1}}+c_{2}\frac{u^{\prime}_{2}}{u_{2}}+\dots+c_{n}\frac{u_{n}^{\prime}}{u_{n}}+v^{\prime}=f,

where
u_{1},\cdots,u_{n}\in E
and
c_{1},\cdots,c_{n}
are
\mathbb{Q}-
linearly independent constants and
v\in E.
Then since
E
is an elementary (algebraic) extension of
k(\alpha,y,\eta),
by Liouville’s theorem, we obtain that
f
admits a similar expression over
k(\alpha,y,\eta).
Thus, we shall further assume that for each
i,

u_{i}\in k(\alpha,y,\eta)
and that
v\in k(\alpha,y,\eta).

Step 2.
Observe that
f\in k\subset k(\alpha,y)[\eta]
and that
\eta^{\prime}=y\in k(\alpha,y).
Therefore, we shall apply Proposition
2.5
and obtain that
u_{1},\dots,u_{n}\in k(\alpha,y)
and that
v\in k(\alpha,y)[\eta]
. Since
v^{\prime}\in k(\alpha,y),
by Kolchin-Ostrowski, there is a constant
e_{1}
such that
v-e_{1}\eta\in k(\alpha,y).
Thus

(3.2)

f=\sum_{i=1}^{n}c_{i}\frac{u_{i}^{\prime}}{u_{i}}+e_{1}y+(v-e_{1}\eta)^{\prime}.

where
u_{i},\dots u_{n},v-e_{1}\eta\in k(\alpha,y)
.

Step 3.
We have
\sum^{n}_{i=1}c_{i}u^{\prime}_{i}/u_{i}+(v-e_{1}\eta)^{\prime}=f-e_{1}y\in k(\alpha)[y],
where
y^{\prime}=\beta y.
Therefore, we shall again apply Proposition
2.5
and obtain that
v-e_{1}\eta\in k(\alpha)[y]
and for each
i=1,2,\dots,n,
there is an integer
m_{i}
and an element
v_{i}\in k(\alpha)
such that
u_{i}=v_{i}y^{m_{i}}.
Let
v-e_{1}\eta=w+a_{1}y+a_{2}y^{2}+\cdots+a_{l}y^{l}.
Then we have

\displaystyle(v-e_{1}\eta)^{\prime}

\displaystyle=w^{\prime}+(a^{\prime}_{1}+\beta a_{1})y+\cdots+(a^{\prime}_{l}+l\beta a_{l})y^{l}

\displaystyle\frac{u^{\prime}_{i}}{u_{i}}

\displaystyle=\frac{v^{\prime}_{i}}{v_{i}}+m_{i}\beta.

Using the above equations, we shall rewrite Equation (
3.2
) and obtain that

(3.3)

f=\sum_{i=1}^{n}c_{i}\frac{v^{\prime}_{i}}{v_{i}}+e\beta+w^{\prime},

where
e=\sum^{n}_{i=1}m_{i}c_{i},

v_{i}\in k(\alpha)=k(\beta)
and
w\in k(\alpha).

Step 4.
We shall now show that the
elements
w,v_{1},\dots,v_{n}
belong to
k
and that
e=0.
This will then complete the proof.

Let
\overline{E}
be an algebraic closure of
E
and
\bar{k}
be the algebraic closure of
k
in
\overline{E}.
For any rational function
x\in k(\alpha)
and
a\in\bar{k},
let

x=r_{\lambda}(\alpha-a)^{\lambda}+r_{\lambda+1}(\alpha-a)^{\lambda+1}+\cdots

be the Laurent series expansion of
x
about
a.
Since

(\alpha-a)^{\prime}=-R(a)-(\alpha-a)^{2}-(2a-r)(\alpha-a),

we have the following Laurent series expansions for
x^{\prime}
and
x^{\prime}/x
:

\displaystyle x^{\prime}

\displaystyle=-\lambda R(a)r_{\lambda}(\alpha-a)^{\lambda-1}+\cdots

\displaystyle\frac{x^{\prime}}{x}

\displaystyle=\lambda R(a)(\alpha-a)^{-1}+\cdots

Thus,
\mathrm{ord}_{a}(x^{\prime})\geq\mathrm{ord}_{a}(x)-1
and that
\mathrm{ord}_{a}(x^{\prime}/x)\geq-1.
In particular,

(3.4)

\mathrm{ord}_{a}\left(\sum^{n}_{i=1}c_{i}(v^{\prime}_{i}/v_{i})\right)\geq-1.

Since
k
is algebraically closed in
E,
we have
\overline{k}E
is a Picard-Vessiot extension of
\overline{k}
with (see
[
Mag94
, Proposition 6.6]
)

\mathscr{G}(\overline{k}E|\overline{k})\cong\mathscr{G}(E|k)\cong\mathrm{SL}_{2}(C).

Therefore, applying Proposition
2.2
with
\overline{k}
in place of
k,
we obtain that
R(a)\neq 0
for all
a\in\overline{k}.
Thus, if
a
is a pole of
x
then
\mathrm{ord}_{a}(x^{\prime})=\mathrm{ord}_{a}(x)-1
and if
a
is either a pole or a zero of
x
then
\mathrm{ord}_{a}(x^{\prime}/x)=-1.
With these observations, we shall move on to show that
v_{i}
and
w
are in
k.

Suppose that
w
has a pole at
a\in\overline{k}.
Then
\mathrm{ord}_{a}(w)<0
and as we observed,
\mathrm{ord}_{a}(w^{\prime})=\mathrm{ord}_{a}(w)-1<-2.
Note that
\mathrm{ord}_{a}(e\beta)=

\mathrm{ord}_{a}(e(\omega^{\prime}/\omega)-e\alpha)=0
or
1,
depending on
e=0
or
e\neq 0
. Therefore, from Equations (
3.3
) and (
3.4
), we obtain

0=\mathrm{ord}_{a}(f)\leq\min\left\{\mathrm{ord}_{a}\left(\sum^{n}_{i=1}c_{i}(v^{\prime}_{i}/v_{i})\right),\mathrm{ord}_{a}(e\beta),\mathrm{ord}_{a}(w^{\prime})\right\}=\mathrm{ord}_{a}(w^{\prime})<-2,

a contradiction. Therefore,
w
is a polynomial in
\alpha
over
k.

Let
A\subseteq\{1,2,\dots,n\}
be the subset containing all
j
such that
v_{j}
has either a zero or a pole at
a\in\overline{k}.
Suppose that
A\neq\emptyset.
Then the Laurent series expansion of
\sum_{j\in A}c_{i}(v^{\prime}_{i}/v_{i})
is

\sum_{j\in A}c_{i}(v^{\prime}_{i}/v_{i})=\left(\sum_{i\in A}c_{i}\lambda_{i}\right)R(a)(\alpha-a)^{-1}+\cdots,

where
\lambda_{i}=\mathrm{ord}_{a}(v_{i}).

Since
c_{1},\dots,c_{n}
are
\mathbb{Q}-
linearly independent, we have
\sum_{i\in A}c_{i}\lambda_{i}\neq 0.
This implies

\mathrm{ord}_{a}\left(\sum_{i\in A}c_{i}(v^{\prime}_{i}/v_{i})\right)=-1.

On the other hand, for each
i\in\{1,2,\dots,n\}\setminus A,
we have
\mathrm{ord}_{a}(v^{\prime}_{i}/v_{i})\geq 0.
Then

\mathrm{ord}_{a}\left(\sum_{i\in\{1,2,\dots,n\}\setminus A}c_{i}(v^{\prime}_{i}/v_{i})\right)\geq 0.

Thus

\mathrm{ord}_{a}\left(\sum^{n}_{i=1}c_{i}(v^{\prime}_{i}/v_{i})\right)=-1.

Since
w\in k[\alpha],
we have
w^{\prime}\in k[\alpha]
and that
\mathrm{ord}_{a}(e\beta+w^{\prime})\geq 0.
Then

0=\mathrm{ord}_{a}(f)\leq\min\left\{\mathrm{ord}_{a}\left(\sum^{n}_{i=1}c_{i}(v^{\prime}_{i}/v_{i})\right),\mathrm{ord}_{a}\left(e\beta+w^{\prime}\right)\right\}=\mathrm{ord}_{a}\left(\sum^{n}_{i=1}c_{i}(v^{\prime}_{i}/v_{i})\right)=-1,

which is absurd. Thus
A=\emptyset
and we obtain that
v_{1},\dots,v_{n}
belongs to
k
and that
e\beta+w^{\prime}=f-\sum^{n}_{i=1}c_{i}(v^{\prime}_{i}/v_{i})\in k.

To complete the proof, we only need to show that
e=0
and that
w\in k.
We have already noted that
w\in k[\alpha].
Now we shall show that
w
has no zeros in
\overline{k},
which would then imply
w\in k.
Suppose
a\in\overline{k}
is a zero of
w
of order
m\geq 1
and that degree of
w
is
l.
Then
w^{\prime}
is of degree
l+1:

\displaystyle w

\displaystyle=a_{m}(\alpha-a)^{m}+\cdots+a_{l}(\alpha-a)^{l},\ \ \text{where}\ a_{l}\neq 0,a_{m}\neq 0\ \ \text{and}

\displaystyle w^{\prime}

\displaystyle=-ma_{m}R(a)(\alpha-a)^{m-1}-\cdots-la_{l}(\alpha-a)^{l+1}.

Since
\beta=(\omega^{\prime}/\omega)-2\alpha
is a polynomial of degree
1
and
e\beta+w^{\prime}=f-\sum^{n}_{i=1}c_{i}(v^{\prime}_{i}/v_{i})\in k,
by comparing the degrees, we obtain that
l=0.
This in turn implies
w\in k
and
e=0.
∎

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
k(\theta,\theta^{\prime})
be a differential field extension of
k
, where
\theta
is weierstrassian over
k.
If
f\in k
has an elementary integral over
k(\theta,\theta^{\prime})
then it has an elementary integral over
k.

Proof.

Let
f\in k
have an elementary integral over
k(\theta,\theta^{\prime}).
Then, there are
\mathbb{Q}-
linearly independent constants
c_{1},\dots,c_{n},
nonzero elements
u_{1},\dots,u_{n}\in k(\theta,\theta^{\prime})
and an element
v\in k(\theta,\theta^{\prime})
such that

c_{1}u^{\prime}_{1}/u_{1}+\cdots+c_{n}u^{\prime}_{n}/u_{n}+v^{\prime}=f\in k.

Fix an algebraic closure of
k(\theta,\theta^{\prime})
and let
\overline{k}
be the relative algebraic closure of
k.
Let
\mathscr{G}
be the group of all differential automorphisms of
\overline{k}(\theta,\theta^{\prime})
fixing elements of
\overline{k}.
For
\tau\in\mathscr{G},
we have

c_{1}\tau(u_{1})^{\prime}/\tau(u_{1})+\cdots+c_{n}\tau(u_{n})^{\prime}/\tau(u_{n})+\tau(v)^{\prime}=f\in k.

From Proposition
2.4
, we see that there is a constant
c_{\tau}\in C
such that

(3.5)

c_{1}\mathrm{d}\tau(u_{1})/\tau(u_{1})+\cdots+c_{n}\mathrm{d}\tau(u_{n})/\tau(u_{n})-c_{\tau}\left(c_{1}\mathrm{d}u_{1}/u_{1}+\cdots+c_{n}\mathrm{d}u_{n}/u_{n}\right)+\mathrm{d}(\tau(v)-c_{\tau}v)=0.

We extend the set
\{c_{1},\dots,c_{n}\}
to a
\mathbb{Q}-
linearly independent set of constants
\{c_{1},\dots,c_{n},\dots,c_{l}\}
so that for each
1\leq i\leq n,

c_{\tau}c_{i}
is a
\mathbb{Q}-
linear combination of
c_{1},\dots,c_{l}.
Now for
1\leq i\leq n
and
1\leq j\leq l,
we can find integers
m_{ij\tau}
and a positive integer
l_{\tau}
such that

-l_{\tau}c_{\tau}c_{i}=\sum^{l}_{j=1}m_{ij\tau}c_{j}.

Then Equation (
3.5
) becomes

(3.6)

c_{1}\mathrm{d}v_{1}/v_{1}+\cdots+c_{l}\mathrm{d}v_{l}/v_{l}+\mathrm{d}(l_{\tau}v_{0})=0,

where for

1\leq j\leq n,
we have

v_{j}=\frac{\tau(u_{j}^{l_{\tau}})}{\prod^{n}_{i=1}u^{m_{ij\tau}}_{i}}

and for each
n+1\leq i\leq l,

v_{i}
is a power product of
u_{1},\dots,u_{n},\tau(u_{1}),\dots,\tau(u_{n})
and
v_{0}=\tau(v)-c_{\tau}v.

Apply Proposition
2.3
to Equation (
3.6
) to obtain that each
v_{0},v_{1},\dots,v_{n}
belongs to
\overline{k}.
Thus, for each
\tau\in\mathscr{G}
and for each
1\leq j\leq n,
there are elements
f_{j\tau}
and
g_{\tau}
in
\overline{k}
such that

(3.7)

\tau(u_{j}^{l_{\tau}})=f_{j\tau}\prod^{n}_{i=1}u^{m_{ij\tau}}_{i}\quad\text{and}\quad\tau(v)=c_{\tau}v+g_{\tau}.

We claim that these equations hold only if each
u_{1},\dots,u_{n},v
belongs to
k,
which will then complete the proof of the lemma. As noted earlier,
k
is algebraically closed in
k(\theta,\theta^{\prime}).
Since each
u_{1},\dots,u_{n},v
belongs to
k(\theta,\theta^{\prime}),
the claim follows once we show that each
u_{1},\dots,u_{n},v
belongs to
\overline{k}.

Suppose that for some
j
we have
u_{j}\in\overline{k}(\theta,\theta^{\prime})
and
u_{j}\notin\overline{k}.
Since
\mathcal{E}=ZY^{2}-4X^{3}+g_{1}Z^{2}X+g_{0}Z^{3}
is an irreducible nonsingular projective curve, every non-constant rational function in
\overline{k}(\mathcal{E})=\overline{k}(\theta,\theta^{\prime})
admits a zero and a pole. Let

T_{j}:=\{y\in\mathcal{E}(\overline{k})\ |\ y\text{ is a pole of }\ \tau(u_{j})\ \text{ for some }\tau\in\mathscr{G}\}.

Recall that
\mathscr{G}
consists of automorphisms induced by the translation maps
\tau_{p}
for
p\in\mathcal{E}(C).
Observe that
\{\tau_{p}(x)=x+p\ |\ p\in\mathcal{E}(C)\}
is an infinite set and that
x\in\mathcal{E}(\overline{k})
is a pole of
u_{j}
if and only if for each
p\in\mathcal{E}(C),

\tau_{-p}(x)=x-p
is a pole of
\tau^{*}_{p}(u_{j}).
Thus
T_{j}
is an infinite set.

For each
1\leq i\leq n,
let
S_{i}
be the finite set of all zeros and poles of
u_{i}.
Since
f_{j\tau}\in\overline{k}
and
m_{ij\tau}
are integers, the set of all poles of
f_{j\tau}\prod^{n}_{i=1}u^{m_{ij\tau}}_{i}
is a subset of the finite set
\cup^{n}_{i=1}S_{i}.
Since
l_{\tau}
is a positive integer, if
y\in T_{j}
is a pole of
\tau(u_{j})
then
y
is also a pole of
\tau(u^{l_{\tau}}_{j}).
Therefore, from Equation (
3.7
), we must have
T_{j}\subset\cup^{n}_{i=1}S_{i},
which contradicts the fact that
T_{j}
is an infinite set. Thus each
u_{1},\cdots,u_{n}
must belong to
\overline{k}.
Similarly, one shows that
v\in\overline{k}.

∎

Hereditarily Rigid Relations Dedicated to Professor I.G. Rosenberg on the occasion of his 80-th birthday.
Miguel Couceiro, Lucien Haddad, Maurice Pouzet, Karsten Schölzel

Abstract
An h h -ary relation ρ \rho on a finite set A A is said to be hereditarily rigid if the unary partial functions on A A that preserve ρ \rho are the subfunctions of the identity map or of constant maps. A family of relations ℱ {\mathcal{F}} is said to be hereditarily strongly rigid if the partial functions on A A that preserve every ρ ∈ ℱ \rho\in{\mathcal{F}} are the subfunctions of projections or constant functions. In this paper we show that hereditarily rigid relations exist and we give a lower bound on their arities. We also prove that no finite hereditarily strongly rigid families of relations exist and we also construct an infinite hereditarily strongly rigid family of relations.

Remark 3

In what follows, we deal only with non-empty relations.

When dealing with partial functions, it seems natural to extend this concept. However, as mentioned in Remark 2, rigid relations with respect to partial functions are trivial.

\displaystyle\{f\in{\rm Par}({\bf k})^{(1)}\mid f\leq\id\}

By Remark 2, we know that for any relation

For

We define

II
Hereditarily rigid relations

In this section we show that non-trivial hereditarily rigid relations exist and we give an upper bound on the size of their domain.

Let
X
be a set and
m\geq 1
. We denote by
{\mathcal{P}}(X)
the powerset of
X
. We denote by
{X\choose m}
the subset of
{\mathcal{P}}(X)
made of
m
-element subsets of
X
.
We recall that an
antichain
of subsets of
X
is a collection of subsets such that none is contained in another.
We recall the famous theorem of Sperner (see
[
3
]
):

Theorem 7
.

Let
n
be a non-negative integer. The largest size of an antichain family of subsets of an
n
-element set
X
is
\binom{n}{\lfloor n/2\rfloor}
. It is only realized by

\binom{X}{\lfloor n/2\rfloor}
and
\binom{X}{\lceil n/2\rceil}
.

Let
n\geq 1
. We set
[n]:=\{1,\dots n\}
; we identify
n
-tuples of elements of
X
with maps from
[n]
to
X
. Let
\vec{x}:=(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})\in X^{n}
; considering it as a map,
{\rm img\;}(\vec{x})=\{x_{1},\dots,x_{n}\}
; we set
|\vec{x}|:=|{\rm img\;}(\vec{x})|
.
Suppose
1\leq m\leq n
. We set
\beta_{m}^{n}(X):=\{\vec{x}\in X^{n}:|\vec{x}|=m\}
and
\beta_{<m}^{n}(X):=\bigcup_{m^{\prime}<m}\beta_{m^{\prime}}^{n}(X)
.
We note that
\beta^{n}_{n}(X)
is the set of all
n
-tuples with pairwise distinct entries, hence if
\ell:=|X|
, then
|\beta^{n}_{n}(X)|
is the falling factorial
\ell^{\underline{n}}:=\ell\cdot(\ell-1)\cdots(\ell-n+1)
. On the other hand,
\beta^{n}_{\ell}(X)
identifies with the set of surjective maps from
[n]
onto
X
. Denote this number by
s(n,\ell)
and recall that it satisfies the formula:

s(n,\ell)=\sum_{j=1}^{\ell}(-1)^{\ell-j}\binom{\ell}{j}j^{n}.

Lemma 8
.

Let
\rho
be an
h
-ary relation on
{\bf k}
,
\ell\geq 1
,
Orb_{<\ell}(\rho):=\{x\circ i:i\in\beta_{<\ell}^{h}({\bf k})\cap\rho\;\text{and}\;x\in{\rm Par}({\bf k})^{(1)}\}
, and
\hat{\rho}:=\rho\cup Orb_{<\ell}(\rho)
. Then

1.

{\Omega_{<\ell}({\bf k})}\cup{\rm pPol}\,^{(1)}\rho\subseteq{\rm pPol}\,^{(1)}\hat{\rho}
;

2.

If
\rho
is hereditarily
\ell
-rigid, then
\hat{\rho}=\rho
;

3.

If
\hat{\rho}
is hereditarily
\ell
-rigid, then
{\rm pPol}\,^{(1)}\rho\subseteq{\Omega_{<\ell}({\bf k})}
;

4.

If
h<\ell
and
{\Omega_{<\ell}({\bf k})}\subseteq{\rm pPol}\,^{(1)}\rho
, then
{\rm pPol}\,^{(1)}\rho={\rm Par}({\bf k})^{(1)}
.

Proof.
For (1), observe first that
f\circ x\circ i\in Orb_{<\ell}(\rho)
for every
f\in{\rm Par}({\bf k})^{(1)}
,
x\circ i\in Orb_{<\ell}(\rho)
such that
{\rm img\;}(f)\subseteq x\circ i
; next prove successively that
{\Omega_{<\ell}({\bf k})}\subseteq{\rm pPol}\,^{(1)}\hat{\rho}
and
{\rm pPol}\,^{(1)}\rho\subseteq{\rm pPol}\,^{(1)}\hat{\rho}
. (2) is immediate. (3) follows from (1). For (4) observe that a partial function
f
belongs to
{\rm pPol}\,^{(1)}\rho
if and only if every subfunction with domain of size at most
h
belongs to
{\rm pPol}\,^{(1)}\rho
.

Corollary 9
.

Let
k\geq 2
and
\ell
,
1\leq\ell\leq k
. If an
h
-ary relation
\rho
on
{\bf k}
is hereditarily
\ell
-rigid, then

.

Let
\Psi_{\ell}({\bf k})
be the set of one-to-one partial unary functions on
\bf k
which are not below the identity and whose domain has size
\ell
.
This definition amounts to:

\Psi_{\ell}({\bf k})=\{f\in{\rm Par}({\bf k})^{(1)}\mid|{\rm dom\;}(f)|={\ell}\;\text{and}\;f\notin{\Omega_{<\ell}({\bf k})}\}.

Lemma 10
.

Let
\rho
be a hereditarily
\ell
-rigid relation and
f\in\Psi_{\ell}({\bf k})
.
Then there is some
\vec{x}\in\rho
with

{\rm img\;}(\vec{x})={\rm dom\;}(f)
.

Proof.
Clearly,
f\not\in{\rm pPol}\,^{(1)}\rho
. Hence, there is

\vec{x}:=(x_{1},\dots,x_{h})\in\rho\cap({\rm dom\;}(f))^{h}

with
f(\vec{x})\not\in\rho
. Set
X:={\rm img\;}(\vec{x})
.
Then
g:=f_{\restriction X}\not\in{\rm pPol}\,^{(1)}\rho
. Since
{\rm pPol}\,^{(1)}\rho={\Omega_{<\ell}({\bf k})}
,
|{\rm img\;}(g)|=\ell
and thus
X={\rm dom\;}(f)
.

The next lemma shows that we only need to consider functions whose domain has size
\ell
.

Lemma 11
.

Let
f\in{\rm Par}({\bf k})^{(1)}
and
{\ell}\geq 1
such that
g\in{\Omega_{<\ell}({\bf k})}
for all
g\leq f
with
|{\rm dom\;}(g)|={\ell}
.
Then
f\in{\Omega_{<\ell}({\bf k})}
.

Proof.
Let
f\in{\rm Par}({\bf k})^{(1)}
. If
|{\rm dom\;}(f)|<\ell
, then
|{\rm img\;}(f)|<\ell
hence
f\in\Omega_{<\ell}(\bf k)
. On the other hand if
|{\rm dom\;}(f)|=\ell
, then, since
f\leq f
,
f\in\Omega_{<\ell}(\bf k)
. So let
|{\rm dom\;}(f)|>\ell
. Assume to the contrary that
f\notin{\Omega_{<\ell}({\bf k})}
, i.e.
f\not\leq\id
and
|{\rm img\;}(f)|\geq\ell
. Then there is some
X\subseteq{\rm dom\;}(f)
with
|X|={\ell}
and
|f(X)|={\ell}
.
By hypothesis,
g:=f_{\restriction X}\in\Omega_{\ell}(\bf k)
. Since
|{\rm img\;}(g)|=\ell
, we have
g\leq\id
, i.e.,
f(x)=x
for all
x\in X
.

(1) Suppose that there exists
y\in{\rm dom\;}(f)\setminus X
such that
f(y)\in X
. Then consider
Y:=X\setminus\{f(y)\}\cup\{y\}
. Let
g:=f_{|Y}
.
Clearly,
g\leq f
and
|{\rm dom\;}(g)|=|Y|=|X|={\ell}
, hence from the hypothesis of the lemma we have
g\in{\Omega_{<\ell}({\bf k})}
. Since
|{\rm img\;}(g)|=|X|={\ell}
we have
g\leq\id
, i.e.,

f(y)=y
, but this contradicts
y\notin X
and
f(y)\in X
.

(2) Otherwise, we have
f(y)\notin X\cup\{y\}
for all
y\in{\rm dom\;}(f)\setminus X
. Since
f\not\leq\id
there exists some
y\in{\rm dom\;}(f)\setminus X
such that
f(y)\not\in X\cup\{y\}
. Then consider
Y:=(X\setminus\{x\})\cup\{y\}
for some
x\in X
and let
g:=f_{|Y}
.
Clearly,
g\leq f
and
|{\rm dom\;}(g)|=|Y|=|X|={\ell}
thus, again,
g\leq\id
, hence
f(y)=y
, but this contradicts
f(y)\notin X\cup\{y\}
.

In both cases, we get a contradiction and, hence,
f\in{\Omega_{<\ell}({\bf k})}
as claimed.

Theorem 12
.

A relation
\rho
is hereditarily
\ell
-rigid iff

{\rm pPol}\,^{(1)}\rho\cap\Psi_{\ell}({\bf k})=\emptyset
and
{\Omega_{<\ell}({\bf k})}\subseteq{\rm pPol}\,^{(1)}\rho
.

Proof.
Trivially, if
\rho
is hereditarily
\ell
-rigid, then the two conditions are satisfied since
{\rm pPol}\,^{(1)}\rho={\Omega_{<\ell}({\bf k})}
. Conversely, let
f\in{\rm pPol}\,^{(1)}\rho
. If
f\not\in{\Omega_{<\ell}({\bf k})}
, then, according to Lemma
18
, there is some
g\leq f
with
|{\rm dom\;}(g)|={\ell}
such that
g\not\in{\Omega_{<\ell}({\bf k})}
. We have
g\in\Psi_{\ell}({\bf k})
hence, according to the first condition,
g\not\in{\rm pPol}\,^{(1)}\rho
. This is impossible since every subfunction of
f
must belong to
{\rm pPol}\,^{(1)}\rho
. This proves that
{\rm pPol}\,^{(1)}\rho\subseteq{\Omega_{<\ell}({\bf k})}
. Since the second condition asserts that
{\Omega_{<\ell}({\bf k})}\subseteq{\rm pPol}\,^{(1)}\rho
, we have
{\rm pPol}\,^{(1)}\rho={\Omega_{<\ell}({\bf k})}
, that is,
\rho
is hereditarily
\ell
-rigid.

It follows from the statements above that we only need to consider the tuples with exactly
\ell
different entries in a hereditarily
\ell
-rigid relation
\rho
.
We connect
\ell
-rigid relations to antichains of subsets.

Definition 13
.

Let
\rho
be an
h
-ary relation on
{\bf k}
, and
{\ell}\geq 1
. We define the function
T_{\rho}^{\ell}
from
\beta_{\ell}^{\ell}(\bf k)
to
{\mathcal{P}}(\beta_{\ell}^{h}([\ell]))
by setting:

\displaystyle\rho^{\ell}((x_{1},\dots,x_{\ell}))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\{(i_{1},\dots,i_{h})

\displaystyle\in

\displaystyle\beta_{\ell}^{h}([\ell])\mid(x_{i_{1}},\dots,x_{i_{h}})\in\rho\}.

Identifying
t
-tuples with functions, and denoting by
\circ

T_{\rho}^{\ell}(\vec{x}):=\{\vec{i}\in\beta_{\ell}^{h}([\ell])\mid\vec{x}\circ\vec{i}\in\rho\}.

In Sections 2 and 4 we will use the following two definitions and Proposition
16
below.

Definition 14
.

Let
T\colon\beta_{\ell}^{\ell}({\bf k})\rightarrow{{\mathcal{P}}}({\beta_{\ell}^{h}([\ell]))}
. We define the function
\hat{T}
from
\beta_{\ell}^{\ell}({\bf k})
to
{{\mathcal{P}}}({\beta_{\ell}^{h}({\bf k}))}
by setting:

\hat{T}(\vec{x}):=\{\vec{x}\circ\vec{i}\mid\vec{i}\in T(\vec{x})\}.

Definition 15
.

Let
T\colon\beta_{\ell}^{\ell}({\bf k})\rightarrow{{\mathcal{P}}}({\beta_{\ell}^{h}([\ell]))}
. We define the
h
-ary relation
\rho_{T}
on
\bf k
by setting:

\rho_{T}:=\beta^{h}_{<\ell}({\bf k})\cup\bigcup_{\vec{x}\in\beta_{\ell}^{\ell}({\bf k})}\hat{T}(\vec{x}).

Proposition 16
.

Let
\rho
be an
h
-ary relation on
{\bf k}
,
\pi
be a permutation on
[\ell]
and
\vec{x}\in\beta_{\ell}^{\ell}({\bf k})
.
Then

\vec{i}\in T^{\ell}_{\rho}(\vec{x}\circ\pi)\;\text{if and only if}\;\pi\circ\vec{i}\in T^{\ell}_{\rho}(\vec{x}).

Proof.
The fact that
\vec{i}\in T_{\rho}^{\ell}(\vec{x}\circ\pi)
amounts to
(\vec{x}\circ\pi)\circ\vec{i}\in\rho
, whereas

\pi\circ\vec{i}\in T^{\ell}_{\rho}(\vec{x})
means that
\vec{x}\circ(\pi\circ\vec{i})\in\rho
.

Members of
\beta_{\ell}^{\ell}({\bf k})
are one-to-one maps hence, as partial functions they are invertible on their image. If
\vec{x}\in\beta_{\ell}^{\ell}({\bf k})
we denote by
\vec{x}^{-1}
its inverse.

Definition 17
.

Let
\vec{x},\vec{y}\in\beta_{\ell}^{\ell}({\bf k})
. We set
F_{\vec{x}\to\vec{y}}:=\vec{y}\circ\vec{x}^{-1}
.

According to this definition
F_{\vec{x}\to\vec{y}}
is a partial unary function on
\bf k
with domain
{\rm img\;}(\vec{x})
and image
{\rm img\;}(\vec{y})
.

Lemma 18
.

Let
\rho
be an
h
-ary relation on
{\bf k}
,
\ell\geq 2
and
\vec{x},\vec{y}\in\beta_{\ell}^{\ell}({\bf k})
.
Then
F_{\vec{x}\to\vec{y}}\in{\rm pPol}\,^{(1)}\rho
implies
T^{\ell}_{\rho}(\vec{x})\subseteq T^{\ell}_{\rho}(\vec{y})
. The converse holds provided that
\Psi_{m}({\bf k})\subseteq{\rm pPol}\,^{(1)}\rho
for all
m<\ell
.

Proof.
By definition, we have
F_{\vec{x}\to\vec{y}}\in{\rm pPol}\,^{(1)}\rho
iff
F_{\vec{x}\to\vec{y}}\circ\vec{u}\in\rho
for every
\vec{u}\in\rho\cap{\rm img\;}(\vec{x})^{h}
.
Suppose that
F_{\vec{x}\to\vec{y}}\in{\rm pPol}\,^{(1)}\rho
. We show that
T_{\rho}^{\ell}(\vec{x})\subseteq T_{\rho}^{\ell}(\vec{y})
holds. Indeed, let
\vec{i}\in T_{\rho}^{\ell}(\vec{x})
. Then
\vec{x}\circ\vec{i}\in\rho
thus
F_{\vec{x}\to\vec{y}}\circ\vec{x}\circ\vec{i}=\vec{y}\circ{i}\in\rho
, proving that
\vec{i}\in T_{\rho}^{\ell}(\vec{y})
.

Conversely, suppose that
T_{\rho}^{\ell}(\vec{x})\subseteq T_{\rho}^{\ell}(\vec{y})
holds. Let
\vec{u}\in\rho\cap{\rm img\;}(\vec{x})^{h}
. Set
m:=|{\rm img\;}(\vec{u})|
. If
m<\ell
, then since
\Psi_{m}(\bf k)\subseteq{\rm pPol}\,\rho^{(1)}
it follows that
F_{\vec{x}\to\vec{y}}\circ\vec{u}\in\rho
.
If
m=\ell
set
\vec{i}:=\vec{x}^{-1}\circ\vec{u}
. Then
\vec{x}\circ\vec{i}=\vec{u}\in\rho
hence
\vec{i}\in T_{\rho}^{\ell}(\vec{x})
. Since
T_{\rho}^{\ell}(\vec{x})\subseteq T_{\rho}^{\ell}(\vec{y})
, then
\vec{i}\in T_{\rho}^{\ell}(\vec{y})
hence
F_{\vec{x}\to\vec{y}}\circ\vec{u}=\vec{y}\circ{i}\in\rho
. And thus
F_{\vec{x}\to\vec{y}}\in{\rm pPol}\,\rho^{(1)}
.

From this, we obtain:

Theorem 19
.

If a relation
\rho
on
{\bf k}
is hereditarily
\ell
-rigid, then
{\mathcal{T}}:=\{T^{\ell}_{\rho}(\vec{x})\mid\vec{x}\in\beta_{\ell}^{\ell}({\bf k})\}
is an antichain with respect to set inclusion. The converse holds provided that
{\Omega_{<\ell}({\bf k})}\subseteq{\rm pPol}\,^{(1)}\rho
.

Proof.
If
{\Omega_{<\ell}({\bf k})}\subseteq{\rm pPol}\,^{(1)}\rho
, then
\Psi_{m}({\bf k})\subseteq{\rm pPol}\,^{(1)}\rho
for all
m<\ell
. Now, if
\rho
is hereditarily
\ell
-rigid, then
{\Omega_{<\ell}({\bf k})}={\rm pPol}\,^{(1)}\rho
. Thus the equivalence in Lemma
18
holds in the cases we are considering and gives the result. For example, let
T^{\ell}_{\rho}(\vec{x})
and
T^{\ell}_{\rho}(\vec{y})\in\mathcal{T}
. Suppose that
T^{\ell}_{\rho}(\vec{x})\subseteq T^{\ell}_{\rho}(\vec{y})
. Applying Lemma
18
we get that the map
F_{\vec{x}\to\vec{y}}\in{\rm pPol}\,\rho^{(1)}
. If
\rho
is hereditarily
\ell
-rigid, this map must be the identity, i.e.,
x=y
hence
T^{\ell}_{\rho}(\vec{x})=T^{\ell}_{\rho}(\vec{y})
. This proves that if
\rho
is hereditarily
\ell
-rigid, then
\mathcal{T}
is an antichain. The converse follows the same line.

Since
T_{\rho}^{\ell}(\vec{x})
is a subset of
\beta_{\ell}^{h}([\ell])
whose cardinality is the number
s(h,\ell)
of surjections of
[h]
onto
[\ell]
, then with the help of Sperner’s theorem we obtain:

Corollary 20
.

Let
\rho
be an
h
-ary hereditarily
\ell
-rigid relation on
{\bf k}
. Then
k^{\underline{\ell}}\leq{s(h,\ell)\choose s(h,\ell)/2}
where
k^{\underline{\ell}}=k\cdot(k-1)\cdots(k-\ell+1)
and
s(h,\ell)
is the number of surjections of
[h]
onto
[\ell]
.

The aim of the next section is to show that for
\ell=2
, this bound can be attained, and that for
\ell>2

the actual upper bound is not much lower than the one in the previous corollary.

III
The case
\ell=2

For
h\geq 2
, we have
s(h,2)=2^{h}-2
, and thus
s(h,2)/2=2^{h-1}-1
is odd. Hence, there is just one antichain of maximal size on the set
\beta_{2}^{h}([2])
,
namely
{\mathcal{X}}_{2}^{h}:=\binom{\beta_{2}^{h}([2])}{2^{h-1}-1}
. Therefore, for any
X\in{\mathcal{X}}_{2}^{h}
the dual set
X^{\mathrm{d}}
(interchanging 0’s and 1’s) is different from
X
, but also belongs to
{\mathcal{X}}_{2}^{h}
.
Let
T\colon\beta_{2}^{2}({\bf k})\to{{\mathcal{P}}}({\beta_{2}^{h}([2]))}
be an injective function such that

T(x,y)\in{\mathcal{X}}_{2}^{h}
and
T(y,x)=T(x,y)^{\mathrm{d}}
.
Then
T
fulfills the condition in Proposition
16
. The relation
\rho_{T}
given by Definition
15
satisfies the condition in Theorem
19
,
and thus it is hereditarily
2
-rigid. From this observation we get:

Theorem 21
.

There exists an
h
-ary hereditarily
2
-rigid relation on
{\bf k}
if and only if

k\cdot(k-1)\leq\binom{2^{h}-2}{2^{h-1}-1}.

For example, if
h:=1,2,3,4,5
the largest values of
k
are respectively:
0,2,5,59,12455
.
Using an approximation of the binomial coefficient via Stirling’s formula we get an
h
-ary hereditarily
2
-rigid relation iff

k\lesssim\sqrt{\frac{4^{2^{h-1}-1}}{\sqrt{\pi(2^{h-1}-1)}}}=\frac{2^{2^{h-1}-1}}{\sqrt[4]{\pi(2^{h-1}-1)}},

which basically grows double exponentially.

IV
The case
{\ell}\geq 3

Denote by
\mathfrak{S}_{\ell}
the set of permutations of
[\ell]
. Let
\pi\in\mathfrak{S}_{\ell}
and
X\subseteq\beta_{\ell}^{h}([\ell])
. We set
\pi(X):=\{\pi\circ\vec{i}:\vec{i}\in X\}
.
If
{\ell}\geq 3
, a function
T
constructed from the antichain
{\mathcal{X}}_{\ell}^{h}
similar to the case
\ell=2
,
does not automatically fulfill the condition in Proposition
16
,
because there can be some element
X\in{\mathcal{X}}_{\ell}^{h}
, and some non-identical permutation
\pi
on
[\ell]
,
with
\pi(X)=X
. The element
\vec{x}\in\beta_{\ell}^{\ell}({\bf k})
that would be assigned to this element by
T
would then
have the property that
F_{\vec{x}\to\vec{x}\circ\pi}\in{\rm pPol}\,^{(1)}\rho_{T}
, i.e.,
\rho_{T}
would not be hereditarily
\ell
-rigid.

For an arbitrary
\vec{y}\in\beta_{\ell}^{h}([\ell])
, let
Y:=\{\pi\circ\vec{y}\mid\pi\in\mathfrak{S}_{\ell}\}
and
Y^{\prime}:=\{\{y\}:y\in Y\}
.
Then
|Y^{\prime}|={\ell}!
. Let
{\mathcal{Y}}_{\ell}^{h}
be an antichain of maximal size in
{\mathcal{P}}(\beta_{\ell}^{h}([\ell])\setminus Y)

and construct
T\colon\beta_{\ell}^{\ell}({\bf k})\to{{\mathcal{P}}}({\beta_{\ell}^{h}([\ell]))}
as follows.

Let
T_{1}\colon\beta_{\ell}^{\ell}({\bf k})\to{\mathcal{Y}}_{\ell}^{h}
be an injective function
such that
T_{1}(\vec{x}\circ\pi)=\pi^{-1}T_{1}(\vec{x})
.
Let
T_{2}\colon\beta_{\ell}^{\ell}({\bf k})\to Y^{\prime}
with

T_{2}(\vec{x}\circ\pi)=\pi^{-1}T_{2}(\vec{x})
, and set
T(\vec{x}):=T_{1}(\vec{x})\cup T_{2}(\vec{x})
.

This function
T
fulfills the condition in Proposition
16
, and its range is an antichain. By Theorem
19
,
the relation
\rho_{T}
is hereditarily
\ell
-rigid.

Corollary 22
.

Let
k
,
\ell
, and
h
with
\ell<h
such that

k^{\underline{\ell}}\leq\binom{s(h,\ell)-\ell!}{(s(h,\ell)-\ell!)/2}.

Then there is an
h
-ary hereditarily
\ell
-rigid relation on
{\bf k}
.

This upper bound is not optimal, as the construction above is brute force. But there is only a constant (for constant
\ell
)
factor between this bound, and the one given before.

Theorem 23
.

Let
r(\ell,h)
be the maximum cardinality of
h
-ary hereditarily
\ell
-rigid relations.
Then

\binom{s(h,\ell)-\ell!}{(s(h,\ell)-\ell!)/2}\leq r(\ell,h)\leq\binom{s(h,\ell)}{(s(h,\ell))/2}.

Furthermore, lower and upper bound on
r(\ell,h)
differ approximately by a constant factor for constant
\ell
, and
h\gg\ell
:

\displaystyle\binom{s(h,\ell)-\ell!}{(s(h,\ell)-\ell!)/2}

\displaystyle\approx

\displaystyle\frac{2^{s(h,\ell)-\ell!}}{\sqrt{(\pi/2)(s(h,\ell)-\ell!)}}

\displaystyle\approx

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2^{\ell!}}\frac{2^{s(h,\ell)}}{\sqrt{(\pi/2)s(h,\ell)}}

\displaystyle\approx

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2^{\ell!}}\binom{s(h,\ell)}{(s(h,\ell))/2}.

V
Hereditarily strongly rigid relations

In this section we prove that no finite hereditarily strongly rigid family of relations exists and we also construct an infinite hereditarily strongly rigid family of relations.

Lemma 24
.

Let
k\geq 2
,
n\geq 3
and
n>h\geq 1
. There is an
n
-ary partial function
\phi_{n}
on
\bf k
that is neither a partial projection nor a partial
constant and that preserves all
h
-ary relations on
{\bf k}
.

Proof.

For
n\geq 3
consider the
n
-ary partial function
\phi_{n}
defined by

{\rm dom}\hphantom{.}(\phi_{n}):=\{(0,1,1,\dots,1),(0,1,0,\dots,0),

(0,0,1,0,\dots,0),\dots,(0,0,\dots,0,1)\}

and

\phi_{n}(0,1,\dots,1)=1
and

\phi_{n}({\bf v})=0
for
{\bf v}\in{\rm dom}\hphantom{.}(\phi_{n})
,
{\bf v}\neq(0,1,\dots,1)
. Thus we have

\penalty\ \phi_{n}\left(\begin{array}[]{cccccc}0&1&1&1\dots&1\\
0&1&0&0\dots&0\\
0&0&1&0\dots&0\\
0&0&0&1\dots&0\\
\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\vdots\\
0&0&0&0\dots&1\\
\end{array}\right)=\left(\begin{array}[]{c}1\\
0\\
0\\
0\\
\vdots\\
0\\
\end{array}\right)

Call
M
the above left hand side matrix. The partial function
\phi_{n}
takes two values and so is not a constant function.
Since the tuple
(1,0,\dots,0)^{t}
is not a column of the matrix
M
, the partial function
\phi_{n}
is not a partial projection.
However, if
g\leq\phi_{n}
is a subfunction of
\phi_{n}
with
{\rm dom}\hphantom{.}(g)\neq{\rm dom}\hphantom{.}(\phi_{n})
, then
g
is a partial projection function.
Indeed, it is easy to see that if for some
i=1,\dots,n
, we have
M_{i*}\not\in{\rm dom}\hphantom{.}(g)
, then
g={e_{i}^{n}}_{|{\rm dom}\hphantom{.}(g)}
.

Now let
\rho
be an
h
-ary relation on
{\bf k}
with
h<n
and let
N
be an
h\times n
matrix with all columns

N_{*j}\in\rho
and all rows
N_{i*}\in{\rm dom}\hphantom{.}(\phi_{n})
. Since
h<n
the matrix
N
does not contain all rows of the matrix
M
defined above, and so the partial function
\phi_{n}
restricted to the rows of the matrix
N
is a partial projection function; it therefore preserves the relation
\rho
.

Corollary 25
.

Let
k\geq 2
and
{\mathcal{F}}=\{\rho_{1},\dots,\rho_{t}\}
be a finite family of relations over
{\bf k}
. Then there is a partial function
\phi
that is neither a partial projection nor a partial constant function such that
\phi\in\displaystyle\bigcap_{i=1}^{t}{\rm pPol}\,(\rho_{i}).

Proof.
For
i=1,\dots,t
let
h_{i}
be the arity of the relation
\rho_{i}
and let

n:=\mathrm{max}\{h_{i}\penalty\ |\penalty\ i=1,\dots,t\}+2
. Then the
n
-ary function
\phi_{n}
constructed above preserves all relations
\rho_{i}
.

As a consequence of this, we get:

Theorem 26
.

Let
k\geq 2
. Then there is no hereditarily strongly rigid finite family of relations on
{\bf k}
.

Remark 27
.

Note that some relations that are strongly rigid with respect to total functions are studied and described in
[
2
,
4
,
5
,
9
]
.

In view of the results above, one may ask if there exist a hereditarily strongly rigid infinite family of relations on
{\bf k}
, i.e., a family of relations
{\mathcal{R}}
such that

\bigcap_{\rho\in{\mathcal{R}}}{\rm pPol}\,(\rho)
is the partial clone generated by the constant functions on
{\bf k}
.

In what follows we consider relations and partial clones on
{\bf 2}:=\{0,1\}
.
The construction below can be generalized over any finite set.
Denote by
{\mathcal{C}}
the strong partial clone generated by all partial constant functions on
{\bf 2}
.

Notation.
For
1\leq t<h
define the
h
-tuple

{\bf v}_{t}^{h}:=(\underbrace{1,\dots,1}_{{\rm t\penalty\ times}},0,\dots,0)\in{\bf 2}^{h},

\Delta_{t}^{h}:={\bf 2}^{h}\setminus\{{\bf v}_{t}^{h}\}
, and let
{\mathcal{F}}^{(h)}:=\{\Delta_{1}^{h},\Delta_{2}^{h},\dots,\Delta_{h-1}^{h}\}
.

Furthermore, for
h\geq 2
, let

\displaystyle{\rm pPol}\,({\mathcal{F}}^{(h)}):=\bigcap_{j=1}^{h-1}{\rm pPol}\,(\Delta_{j}^{h})
.

Example 28
.

Consider
{\mathcal{F}}^{(2)}=\{\{(0,0),(1,1),(1,0)\}\}
and

{\mathcal{F}}^{(3)}=\{{\bf 2}^{3}\setminus\{(1,0,0)\},\penalty\ {\bf 2}^{3}\setminus\{(1,1,0)\}\}
.

It is well known that polymorphisms of relations are strong partial clones, i.e., they contain all partial projections. Moreover since
(0,\dots,0),(1,\dots,1)\in\Delta_{t}^{h}
we deduce that
{\rm pPol}\,(\Delta_{t}^{h})
contains all partial constant functions on
{\bf 2}
. Thus for all
h\geq 2
we have
{\mathcal{C}}\subseteq{\rm pPol}\,({\mathcal{F}}^{(h)})
.

Note that if
{\bf w}\in{\bf 2}^{h}
is any tuple with exactly
t
symbols 1 and
h-t
symbols 0, then
{\rm pPol}\,(\Delta_{t}^{h})={\rm pPol}\,({\bf 2}^{h}\setminus\{{\bf w}\})
.
Indeed the relation
\Delta_{t}^{h}
can be obtained from
{\bf 2}^{h}\setminus\{{\bf w}\}
by some permutation of the variables of
{\bf 2}^{h}\setminus\{{\bf w}\}
.

Example 29
.

Let
h=4
. Then
{\bf v}_{2}^{4}=(1,1,0,0)
and

\Delta_{2}^{4}=\{0,1\}^{4}\setminus\{(1,1,0,0)\}
.
Take
{\bf w}=(0,1,0,1)
and
\lambda:={\bf 2}^{4}\setminus\{(0,1,0,1)\}
. It is easy to verify that

(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3},x_{4})\in\lambda\Longleftrightarrow(x_{2},x_{4},x_{1},x_{3})\in\Delta_{2}^{4}

and thus
{\rm pPol}\,(\Delta_{2}^{4})={\rm pPol}\,(\lambda)
. Note that
{\rm pPol}\,(\Delta_{1}^{2})={\rm pPol}\,(\leq)
is a maximal partial clone on
{\bf 2}
.

The following result will be used to show that the family
{\rm pPol}\,({\mathcal{F}}^{(h)})_{h\geq 2}
is a descending chain of partial clones containing all partial constant functions.

Lemma 30
.

Let
1\leq t<h
. Then

\displaystyle\bigcap_{j=1}^{h}{\rm pPol}\,(\Delta_{j}^{h+1})\subseteq{\rm pPol}\,(\Delta_{t}^{h})
.

Proof.

Let
f\in\bigcap_{j=1}^{h}{\rm pPol}\,(\Delta_{j}^{h+1})
be an
n
-ary partial function on
{\bf 2}
and fix
t\in\{1,\dots,h-1\}
.
We show that

f\in{\rm pPol}\,(\Delta_{t}^{h}).

To do so take an
h\times n
matrix
M
with all columns in
\Delta_{t}^{h}
and all rows in
{\rm dom}\hphantom{.}(f)
. Now let
N
be the
(h+1)\times n
matrix whose rows satisfy

N_{i*}=M_{i*}
for
i=1,\dots,h
and
N_{(h+1)*}=M_{h*}
, i.e.,
N
is obtained from
M
by duplicating the last row of
M
. Since all columns of
M
belong to
\Delta_{t}^{h}
, the
h
-tuple
{\bf v}_{t}^{h}
is not a column of
M
and consequently the
(h+1)
-tuple
{\bf v}_{t}^{h+1}
is not a column of
N
. Thus all columns of
N
belong to
\Delta_{t}^{h+1}
. As
f\in{\rm pPol}\,(\Delta_{t}^{h+1})
we have that the
(h+1)
-tuple
(f(N_{1*}),\ldots,f(N_{h*}),f(N_{h*}))\in\Delta_{t}^{h+1}
, i.e.,

(f(N_{1*}),\ldots,f(N_{h*}),f(N_{(h+1)*}))\neq{\bf v}_{t}^{h+1}
and so

(f(M_{1*}),\ldots,f(M_{h*}))\neq{\bf v}_{t}^{h}
, proving that
f\in{\rm pPol}\,(\Delta_{t}^{h})
.

Remark 31
.

The above result can be shown using the representation lemma due to B. Romov (see
[
8
]
Lemma 20.3.4). Indeed for
1<t<h
we have

\Delta_{t}^{h}=\{(x_{1},\dots,x_{h})\mid(x_{1},\dots,x_{h},x_{h})\in\Delta_{t}^{h+1}\},

and so by Lemma 20.3.4 of
[
8
]
we obtain
{\rm pPol}\,(\Delta_{t}^{h+1})\subseteq{\rm pPol}\,(\Delta_{t}^{h})
. Thus

\bigcap_{j=1}^{h}{\rm pPol}\,(\Delta_{j}^{h+1})\subseteq{\rm pPol}\,(\Delta_{t}^{h}).

From Lemma
30
, we get:

Corollary 32
.

Let
h\geq 2
. Then

{\rm pPol}\,({\mathcal{F}}^{(h+1)})\subseteq{\rm pPol}\,({\mathcal{F}}^{(h)})
.

The above inclusion is strict. Indeed consider the partial function
\phi_{n}
defined in Lemma
24
where
n:=h+1
. Then
\phi_{n}
preserves

\Delta_{t}^{h}
for all
t=1,\dots,h-1
but
\phi_{n}
does not preserve
\Delta_{1}^{h+1}
.

Corollary 33
.

{\rm pPol}\,({\mathcal{F}}^{(2)})\supset{\rm pPol}\,({\mathcal{F}}^{(3)})\supset{\rm pPol}\,({\mathcal{F}}^{(4)})\dots
.

We now show that the limit of the above chain is the partial clone generated by the constant functions on
{\bf 2}
.

Lemma 34
.

Let
n\geq 2
and
f
be an
n
-ary partial function that is neither a partial projection nor a partial constant function on
{\bf 2}
. Then there is an
h\geq 2
such that
f\not\in{\rm pPol}\,({\mathcal{F}}^{(h)})
.

Proof.

Set
h:=|{\rm dom}\hphantom{.}f|
and form an
h\times n
matrix
M
whose rows consists of all tuples in the domain of
f
and consider the
h
-tuple
f(M):=(f(M_{1*}),\dots,f(M_{h*}))
.
Since
f
is not a partial constant function, the tuple
f(M)
contains at least one symbol 0 and one symbol 1. Let
t
be the number of symbols 1 in the tuple
f(M)
.
Call
N
the
h\times n
matrix obtained by rearranging the rows of the matrix
M
so that
f(N):=(f(N_{1*}),\dots,f(N_{h*}))={\bf v}_{t}^{h}
.
Since
f
is not a partial projection function, no column of
N
is the tuple
{\bf v}_{t}^{h}
. Thus all columns of
N
belong to the
h
-ary relation
\Delta_{t}^{h}
,
while
(f(N_{1*}),\dots,f(N_{h*}))\not\in\Delta_{t}^{h}
, proving that
f\not\in{\rm pPol}\,(\Delta_{t}^{h})
. Thus
f\not\in{\rm pPol}\,({\mathcal{F}}^{(h)})
.

By combining these results, we get:

Theorem 35
.

\displaystyle{\mathcal{C}}=\bigcap_{h\geq 2}{\rm pPol}\,({\mathcal{F}}^{(h)})
.

The above theorem shows that the family
\bigcup\{{\mathcal{F}}^{(h)}\penalty\ |\penalty\ {h\geq 2}\}
is a hereditarily strongly rigid family over
{\bf 2}
.

Remark 36
.

It is not hard to construct infinite subfamilies of
\{{\mathcal{F}}^{(h)}\penalty\ |\penalty\ {h\geq 2}\}
of relations that are hereditarily strongly rigid. For example consider the family of relations

\{\Delta_{n}^{2n}\mid n\in{\mathbb{N}}\}
. Then by the Romov representation lemma we have
{\rm pPol}\,(\Delta_{1}^{2})\supseteq{\rm pPol}\,(\Delta_{2}^{4})\supseteq{\rm pPol}\,(\Delta_{3}^{6})\dots
Moreover, similar arguments as in Corollary
32
and Lemma

give that the above inclusions are all strict and

\displaystyle{\mathcal{C}}=\bigcap_{n\geq 2}{\rm pPol}\,(\Delta_{n}^{2n}).

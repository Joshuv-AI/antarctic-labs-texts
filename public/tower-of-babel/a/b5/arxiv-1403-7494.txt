Some characterizations of slant and spherical helices due to sabban frame
B. Altunkaya, L. Kula

Abstract
In this paper, we are investigating that under which conditions of the geodesic curvature of unit speed curve γ \gamma that lies on the unit sphere, the curve c c which is obtained by using γ \gamma , is a spherical helix or slant helix.



Key Words:
Helices, Spherical Helices, Slant Helices, Sabban Frame.

1
Introduction

Izumiya and Takeuchi [4],[2] have defined helices, spherical helices, slant helices and conical geodesic curve and given a classification of special developable surfaces under the condition of the existence of such a special curve as a geodesic [1].

Encheva and Georgiev [3] have used a similar method and determined a
Frenet

curve
up to a direct similarity of
R^{3}
.

In this paper we have used the the method in [2], [3], and [4] to construct spherical helices and slant helices.

In section 2, we recall some basic concepts of differential geometry of space curves that we will use later. In the next section we will provide some new theorems and proofs about the construction of spherical helices and slant helices.

2
Basic Concepts

We now recall some basic concepts on classical differential geometry of curves in the Euclidean space
E^{3}
. For a regular curve
c:I\subset R\rightarrow{R}^{3}
with curvature and torsion,
\kappa
and
\tau
, the following Frenet-Serret formulae are given in [5] written in the matrix form for
\kappa>0

\left[\begin{matrix}{T}^{{}^{\prime}}\\
{N}^{{}^{\prime}}\\
{B}^{{}^{\prime}}\end{matrix}\right]=\left[\begin{matrix}0&\kappa\nu&0\\
-\kappa\nu&0&\tau\nu\\
0&-\tau\nu&0\end{matrix}\right]\left[\begin{matrix}T\\
N\\
B\end{matrix}\right]

where

\nu=\nu(t)=\left\|{c}^{{}^{\prime}}(t)\right\|,\kappa=\kappa(t)=\frac{\left\|{c}^{{}^{\prime}}(t)\times{c}^{{}^{\prime\prime}}(t)\right\|}{{\left\|{c}^{{}^{\prime}}(t)\right\|}^{3}},\tau=\tau(t)=\frac{\left<{c}^{{}^{\prime}}(t)\times{c}^{{}^{\prime\prime}}(t),{c}^{{}^{\prime\prime\prime}}(t)\right>}{{\left\|{c}^{{}^{\prime}}(t)\times{c}^{{}^{\prime\prime}}(t)\right\|}^{2}}

(1)

and

T=T(t)=\frac{{c}^{{}^{\prime}}(t)}{\left\|{c}^{{}^{\prime}}(t)\right\|},B=B(t)=\frac{{c}^{{}^{\prime}}(t)\times{c}^{{}^{\prime\prime}}(t)}{\left\|{c}^{{}^{\prime}}(t)\times{c}^{{}^{\prime\prime}}(t)\right\|},N=N(t)=B(t)\times T(t)

(2)

In the formulae above, we denote unit tangent vector with
T
, binormal unit vector with
B
, unit principal normal vector with
N
, cross product with
\times
, and inner product with
<,>
.

A regular curve
c
with
\kappa>0
is a cylindrical helix if and only if the ratio

\frac{\tau}{\kappa}

is constant [5].

A regular curve
c
with
\kappa>0
is a slant helix if and only if the geodesic curvature of the spherical image of the principal normal indicatrix of
c

\sigma(t)=\left(\frac{{\kappa}^{2}}{{{\nu\left({\kappa}^{2}+{\tau}^{2}\right)}}^{{3}/{2}}}{\left(\frac{\tau}{\kappa}\right)}^{{}^{\prime}}\right)\left(t\right)

(3)

is constant [4].

A regular curve
c
with
\kappa>0
lies on the surface of a sphere which has a radius
r
if and only if

{r}^{2}=\left(\frac{1}{{\kappa}^{2}}+{\left(\frac{1}{\nu\tau}{\left(\frac{1}{\kappa}\right)}^{{}^{\prime}}\right)}^{2}\right)\left(t\right)

(4)

satisfies [6]. We can easily simplify this equation as follows

\left(\frac{1}{\nu}\left[\frac{1}{\nu\tau}{\left(\frac{1}{\kappa}\right)}^{{}^{\prime}}\right]^{{}^{\prime}}+\frac{\tau}{\kappa}\right)\left(t\right)=0.

(5)

Let
\gamma:I\rightarrow{S}^{2}
be a unit speed spherical curve with an arc length parameter
s
and denote
{\gamma}^{{}^{\prime}}\left(s\right)=t\left(s\right)
where
{\gamma}^{{}^{\prime}}\left(s\right)=\frac{d\gamma}{ds}
. If we set a vector
p\left(s\right)=\gamma\left(s\right)\times t\left(s\right)
, by definition we have an orthonormal frame
\left\{\gamma\left(s\right),t\left(s\right),p\left(s\right)\right\}
along
\gamma
. This frame is called the
Sabban

frame
of
\gamma
. Then we have the following
Frenet-Serret
formulae of
\gamma

\begin{matrix}{\gamma}^{{}^{\prime}}\left(s\right)=t\left(s\right)\\
{t}^{{}^{\prime}}\left(s\right)=-\gamma\left(s\right)+{k}_{g}\left(s\right)p\left(s\right)\\
{p}^{{}^{\prime}}\left(s\right)=-{k}_{g}\left(s\right)t\left(s\right)\end{matrix}

(6)

where
{k}_{g}\left(s\right)
is the
geodesic

curvature
of the curve
\gamma
on
S^{2}
which is
{k}_{g}\left(s\right)=det\left(\gamma\left(s\right),t\left(s\right),{t}^{{}^{\prime}}\left(s\right)\right)
[2].

In [2] Izuyama and Takeuchi showed a way to construct all
Bertrand

curves
by the following formula

c\left(s\right)=b\int_{{s}_{0}}^{s}{\gamma\left(\varphi\right)d}\varphi+b\cot{\theta}\int_{{s}_{0}}^{s}{p\left(\varphi\right)d}\varphi+a

(7)

where
b,\theta
are constant numbers, a is a constant vector, and
\gamma
is a unit speed curve on
S^{2}
with the
Sabban

frame
above. Also they showed that the spherical curve
\gamma
is a circle if and only if the corresponding
Bertrand

curves
are circular helices.

In [3] Encheva and Georgiev showed a way to construct all
Frenet

curves\left(\kappa>0\right)
by the following formula

c\left(s\right)=b\int{{e}^{\int{k\left(s\right)ds}}\gamma\left(s\right)}ds+a

(8)

where
b
is a constant number, a is a constant vector,
\gamma
is a unit speed curve on
S^{2}
with the
Sabban

frame
above, and
k:I\rightarrow R\quad
is a function of class
C^{1}
. Also they showed that the spherical curve
\gamma
is a circle if and only if the corresponding
Frenet

curves
are cylindrical helices.

If we use the equations in (1), (2), (6), (7), and (8) we can easily see the
Frenet

frame

\left\{T,N,B\right\}
of the curve
c
and the
Sabban

frame

\left\{\gamma,t,p\right\}
of the curve
\gamma
coincides. Therefore we can say the
tangent

indicatrix
of the curve
c
is
\gamma
.

3
Special Results

Now, we can deduce some results from the equations above. First, we want to show, under which circumstances the equation (8) is a spherical helix. As we know before, if
\gamma
is a circle, the geodesic curvature of it is constant. Therefore we can write the theorem below.

Theorem 1
.

If the curve
\gamma
is a circle, the curve c defined by (8) is a spherical helix if and only if the function
k\left(s\right)=-{k}_{g}tan\left[\left({k}_{g}\right)\left(s-{b}_{1}\right)\right]
where
b_{1}\in R
.

Proof.
.

For the curve

c\left(s\right)=b\int{{e}^{\int{k\left(s\right)ds}}\gamma\left(s\right)}ds+a

If we calculate
\kappa
,
\tau
, and
\nu
of the curve
c
by using the equations at (1), we will find

\begin{matrix}\kappa\left(s\right)=\frac{1}{b{e}^{\int{k\left(s\right)ds}}}\\
\tau\left(s\right)=\frac{{k}_{g}\left(s\right)}{b{e}^{\int{k\left(s\right)ds}}}\\
\nu\left(s\right)=b{e}^{\int{k\left(s\right)ds}}\end{matrix}.

(9)

Now, by putting these equations in (5), we have

\left(\frac{1}{\nu}\left[\frac{1}{\nu\tau}{\left(\frac{1}{\kappa}\right)}^{{}^{\prime}}\right]^{{}^{\prime}}+\frac{\tau}{\kappa}\right)\left(s\right)=0

\left(\frac{1}{b{e}^{\int{kds}}}\left[\frac{1}{b{e}^{\int{kds}}\frac{{k}_{g}}{b{e}^{\int{kds}}}}{\left(\frac{1}{\frac{1}{b{e}^{\int{kds}}}}\right)}^{{}^{\prime}}\right]^{{}^{\prime}}+\frac{\frac{{k}_{g}}{b{e}^{\int{kds}}}}{\frac{1}{b{e}^{\int{kds}}}}\right)\left(s\right)=0

\left(\frac{1}{b{e}^{\int{kds}}}\left[\frac{1}{{k}_{g}}{\left(b{e}^{\int{kds}}\right)}^{{}^{\prime}}\right]^{{}^{\prime}}+{k}_{g}\right)\left(s\right)=0

\left(\frac{1}{{k}_{g}{e}^{\int{kds}}}\left[{k}^{{}^{\prime}}{e}^{\int{kds}}+{k}^{2}{e}^{\int{kds}}\right]+{k}_{g}\right)\left(s\right)=0

{k}^{{}^{\prime}}\left(s\right)+{k}^{2}\left(s\right)=-{{k}_{g}}^{2}.

If we solve this differential equation, we will have

k\left(s\right)=-{k}_{g}tan\left[\left({k}_{g}\right)\left(s-{b}_{1}\right)\right]

Conversely, if we take
k\left(s\right)=-{k}_{g}tan\left[\left({k}_{g}\right)\left(s-{b}_{1}\right)\right]
in (8) then

\int{k\left(s\right)ds=\int{-{k}_{g}tan\left[\left({k}_{g}\right)\left(s-{b}_{1}\right)\right]ds}}.

Let
u={k}_{g}\left(s-{b}_{1}\right)={k}_{g}s-{k}_{g}{b}_{1}
then
{k}_{g}ds=du
, by using these equations

\displaystyle{\int k\left(s\right)ds}=\int{-\tan{u}du}

\displaystyle\quad\quad\quad\quad=\ln{\cos{u}}+\ln{{b}_{2}}

\displaystyle\quad\quad\quad\quad=\ln{\left[{b}_{2}\cos{\left\{{k}_{g}\left(s-{b}_{1}\right)\right\}}\right]}

we have

\displaystyle c\left(s\right)=b\int{{e}^{\int{k\left(s\right)ds}}\gamma\left(s\right)}ds+a

\displaystyle\quad\quad=b\int{{e}^{\int{-{k}_{g}tan\left[\left({k}_{g}\right)\left(s-{b}_{1}\right)\right]ds}}\gamma\left(s\right)}ds+a

\displaystyle\quad\quad=b\int{{e}^{\ln{\left[{b}_{2}\cos{\left\{{k}_{g}\left(s-{b}_{1}\right)\right\}}\right]}}\gamma\left(s\right)}ds+a

\displaystyle\quad\quad=b\int{{b}_{2}\cos{\left\{{k}_{g}\left(s-{b}_{1}\right)\right\}}\gamma\left(s\right)}ds+a.

where
b_{1},b_{2}\in R
.

Now, we must show that curve
c
is spherical. If we use (4) to do it, we will have

\displaystyle{r}^{2}=\left(\left(\frac{1}{{\kappa}^{2}}+{\left(\frac{1}{\nu\tau}{\left(\frac{1}{\kappa}\right)}^{{}^{\prime}}\right)}\right)^{2}\right)\left(s\right)

\displaystyle=\left({b}^{2}{e}^{2\int{kds}}+\left(\frac{1}{b{e}^{\int{kds}}\frac{{k}_{g}}{b{e}^{\int{kds}}}}{\left(\frac{1}{\frac{1}{b{e}^{\int{kds}}}}\right)}^{{}^{\prime}}\right)^{2}\right)\left(s\right)

\displaystyle=\left({b}^{2}{e}^{2\int{kds}}+\left(\frac{1}{{k}_{g}}{\left(b{e}^{\int{kds}}\right)}^{{}^{\prime}}\right)^{2}\right)\left(s\right)

\displaystyle=\left({b}^{2}{e}^{2\int{kds}}+\frac{b^{2}k^{2}}{{k}_{g}^{2}}{e}^{2\int{kds}}\right)\left(s\right)

\displaystyle=\left({b}^{2}{e}^{2\int{kds}}\left(1+\frac{k^{2}}{{k}_{g}^{2}}\right)\right)\left(s\right)

\displaystyle={b}^{2}{b_{2}}^{2}\cos^{2}{\left\{{k}_{g}\left(s-b_{1}\right)\right\}}\left(1+\frac{\left(-{k}_{g}tan\left[\left({k}_{g}\right)\left(s-{b}_{1}\right)\right]\right)^{2}}{{k}_{g}^{2}}\right)

\displaystyle={b}^{2}{b_{2}}^{2}\cos^{2}{\left\{{k}_{g}\left(s-b_{1}\right)\right\}}\left(\frac{1}{\cos^{2}{\left\{{k}_{g}\left(s-b_{1}\right)\right\}}}\right)

\displaystyle=b^{2}{b_{2}}^{2}.

Therefore, we can say curve
c
lies on a sphere which has a radius
\left|bb_{2}\right|

∎

Theorem 2
.

Let
\gamma\left(s\right)
be a unit speed spherical curve on

;

if and only if

is a slant helix.

Proof.
.

Let, for
\gamma

{{{k}_{g}}^{2}}\left(s\right)=\frac{\left(ms+n\right)^{2}}{1-{\left(ms+n\right)}^{2}}.

(10)

For the curve
c\left(s\right)
we have

\begin{matrix}{c}^{{}^{\prime}}\left(s\right)=b{e}^{\int{k\left(s\right)ds}}\gamma\left(s\right)\\
{c}^{{}^{\prime\prime}}\left(s\right)=b{e}^{\int{k\left(s\right)ds}}\left\{k\left(s\right)\gamma\left(s\right)+{\gamma}^{{}^{\prime}}\left(s\right)\right\}\\

\tau\left(s\right)=\frac{{k}_{g}\left(s\right)}{b{e}^{\int{k\left(s\right)ds}}}\\
\nu\left(s\right)=b{e}^{\int{k\left(s\right)ds}}.\end{matrix}

The geodesic curvature of the spherical image of the principal normal indicatrix of
c
is as follows

\displaystyle\sigma(s)=\left(\frac{{\kappa}^{2}}{{{\nu\left({\kappa}^{2}+{\tau}^{2}\right)}}^{{3}/{2}}}{\left(\frac{\tau}{\kappa}\right)}^{{}^{\prime}}\right)\left(s\right)

\displaystyle\quad\quad=\left(\frac{\frac{1}{{\nu}^{2}}}{{{\nu\left(\frac{1}{{\nu}^{2}}+\frac{{{k}_{g}}^{2}}{{\nu}^{2}}\right)}}^{{3}/{2}}}{{k}_{g}}^{{}^{\prime}}\right)\left(s\right).

So we have

\sigma(s)=\frac{{{k}_{g}}^{{}^{\prime}}\left(s\right)}{{\left({{k}_{g}}^{2}\left(s\right)+1\right)}^{{3}/{2}}}

(11)

Now, let’s take
u\left(s\right)=ms+n
then we have (11)

{{{k}_{g}}^{2}}\left(s\right)=\frac{u^{2}\left(s\right)}{1-u^{2}\left(s\right)}.

(12)

If we take the derivates of the both sides of (13) for
s
we have

\displaystyle 2{k}_{g}{{\left(s\right)k}_{g}}^{{}^{\prime}}\left(s\right)=\left(\frac{2u{u}^{{}^{\prime}}\left(1-{u}^{2}\right)-\left(-2u{u}^{{}^{\prime}}\right){u}^{2}}{{\left(1-{u}^{2}\right)}^{2}}\right)\left(s\right)

\displaystyle{k}_{g}{{\left(s\right)k}_{g}}^{{}^{\prime}}\left(s\right)=\left(\frac{u{u}^{{}^{\prime}}}{{\left(1-{u}^{2}\right)}^{2}}\right)\left(s\right)

{{k}_{g}}^{{}^{\prime}}\left(s\right)=\left(\left(\frac{u{u}^{{}^{\prime}}}{{\left(1-{u}^{2}\right)}^{2}}\right)\left(\varepsilon\sqrt{\frac{1-{u}^{2}}{{u}^{2}}}\right)\right)\left(s\right)

(13)

where
\varepsilon=\pm 1
. Putting (13) and (14) in (12), we have

\displaystyle\sigma(s)=\frac{{{k}_{g}}^{{}^{\prime}}\left(s\right)}{{\left({{k}_{g}}^{2}\left(s\right)+1\right)}^{{3}/{2}}}

\displaystyle\quad\quad=\left(\varepsilon\frac{\frac{\sqrt{1-{u}^{2}}u{u}^{{}^{\prime}}}{\left|u\right|{\left(1-{u}^{2}\right)}^{2}}}{{\left(\frac{{u}^{2}}{1-{u}^{2}}+1\right)}^{{3}/{2}}}\right)\left(s\right)

\displaystyle\quad\quad=\left(\varepsilon\frac{\sqrt{1-{u}^{2}}u{u}^{{}^{\prime}}}{\left|u\right|{\left(1-{u}^{2}\right)}^{2}}{\left(1-{u}^{2}\right)}^{{3}/{2}}\right)\left(s\right)

\displaystyle\quad\quad=\left(\varepsilon\frac{{\left(1-{u}^{2}\right)}^{2}}{{\left(1-{u}^{2}\right)}^{2}}\frac{u}{\left|u\right|}{u}^{{}^{\prime}}\right)\left(s\right)

\displaystyle\quad\quad=\varepsilon\frac{ms+n}{\left|ms+n\right|}m

\displaystyle\quad\quad=\varepsilon m

which is constant.

Conversely, let
c\left(s\right)
be a
slant

helix
, then the geodesic curvature of the spherical image of the principal normal indicatrix of
c
is a constant function. So we can take

\sigma(s)=\left(\frac{{\kappa}^{2}}{{{\nu\left({\kappa}^{2}+{\tau}^{2}\right)}}^{{3}/{2}}}{\left(\frac{\tau}{\kappa}\right)}^{{}^{\prime}}\right)\left(s\right)=m

where
m\in R
. Therefore, from (12)

\displaystyle m=\left(\frac{{\kappa}^{2}}{{{\nu\left({\kappa}^{2}+{\tau}^{2}\right)}}^{{3}/{2}}}{\left(\frac{\tau}{\kappa}\right)}^{{}^{\prime}}\right)\left(s\right)

\displaystyle\quad=\frac{{{k}_{g}}^{{}^{\prime}}\left(s\right)}{{{\left({{k}_{g}}^{2}\left(s\right)+1\right)}}^{{3}/{2}}}

If we solve this differential equation, we have

\frac{{k}_{g}\left(s\right)}{\sqrt{{{k}_{g}}^{2}\left(s\right)+1}}=ms+n

where

.
Then,

\displaystyle\frac{{{k}_{g}}^{2}\left(s\right)}{{{k}_{g}}^{2}\left(s\right)+1}={\left(ms+n\right)}^{2}

\displaystyle\frac{{{k}_{g}}^{2}\left(s\right)+1-1}{{{k}_{g}}^{2}\left(s\right)+1}={\left(ms+n\right)}^{2}

\displaystyle 1-\frac{1}{{{k}_{g}}^{2}\left(s\right)+1}={\left(ms+n\right)}^{2}

\displaystyle{{k}_{g}}^{2}\left(s\right)=\frac{1}{{1-\left(ms+n\right)}^{2}}-1

\displaystyle{{{k}_{g}}^{2}}\left(s\right)=\frac{\left(ms+n\right)^{2}}{1-{\left(ms+n\right)}^{2}}.

Theorem 3
.

Let
\gamma\left(s\right)
be a unit speed spherical curve on
S^{2}
;

be constant numbers; and a be a constant vector. The
geodesic

curvature

\gamma\left(s\right)
satisfies

{{{k}_{g}}^{2}}\left(s\right)=\frac{\left(ms+n\right)^{2}}{1-{\left(ms+n\right)}^{2}}

if and only if

c\left(s\right)=b\int_{{s}_{0}}^{s}{\gamma\left(\varphi\right)d}\varphi+b\cot{\theta}\int_{{s}_{0}}^{s}{p\left(\varphi\right)d}\varphi+a

is a slant helix.

.

Let, for
\gamma

{{{k}_{g}}^{2}}\left(s\right)=\frac{\left(ms+n\right)^{2}}{1-{\left(ms+n\right)}^{2}}.

(14)

For the curve

\begin{matrix}{c}^{{}^{\prime}}\left(s\right)=b\left(\gamma\left(s\right)+\cot{\theta}p\left(s\right)\right)\\

where
\varepsilon=\pm 1
.

The geodesic curvature of the spherical image of the principal normal indicatrix of

is as follows

\displaystyle\sigma(s)=\left(\frac{{\kappa}^{2}}{{{\nu\left({\kappa}^{2}+{\tau}^{2}\right)}}^{{3}/{2}}}{\left(\frac{\tau}{\kappa}\right)}^{{}^{\prime}}\right)\left(s\right)

\displaystyle\quad\quad=\left(\frac{\varepsilon\sin^{3}{\theta}{{k}_{g}}^{{}^{\prime}}}{{{{b}^{3}\left(\frac{\left({\varepsilon}^{2}+\cot^{2}{\theta}-2\left(-1+{\varepsilon}^{2}\right)\cot{\theta}{k}_{g}+\left(1+{\varepsilon}^{2}\cot^{2}{\theta}\right){{k}_{g}}^{2}\right)\sin^{4}{\theta}}{{a}^{2}}\right)}}^{{3}/{2}}}\right)\left(s\right)

\displaystyle\quad\quad=\left(\frac{\varepsilon{{k}_{g}}^{{}^{\prime}}}{{{\sin^{3}{\theta}\left(\left(1+\cot^{2}{\theta}\right)\left(1+{{k}_{g}}^{2}\right)\right)}}^{{3}/{2}}}\right)\left(s\right)

\displaystyle\quad\quad=\left(\frac{\varepsilon{{k}_{g}}^{{}^{\prime}}}{{{\sin^{3}{\theta}\left(\frac{1}{\sin^{2}{\theta}}\left(1+{{k}_{g}}^{2}\right)\right)}}^{{3}/{2}}}\right)\left(s\right)

\displaystyle\quad\quad=\left(\frac{\varepsilon{{k}_{g}}^{{}^{\prime}}}{{{\left(1+{{k}_{g}}^{2}\right)}}^{{3}/{2}}}\right)\left(s\right)

So we have

\sigma(s)=\frac{\varepsilon{{k}_{g}}^{{}^{\prime}}\left(s\right)}{{\left({{k}_{g}}^{2}\left(s\right)+1\right)}^{{3}/{2}}}

(15)

Now, let’s take
u\left(s\right)=ms+n
then we have (15)

{{{k}_{g}}^{2}}\left(s\right)=\frac{u^{2}\left(s\right)}{1-u^{2}\left(s\right)}.

(16)

If we take the derivates of the both sides of (17) for
s
we have

\displaystyle 2{k}_{g}{{\left(s\right)k}_{g}}^{{}^{\prime}}\left(s\right)=\left(\frac{2u{u}^{{}^{\prime}}\left(1-{u}^{2}\right)-\left(-2u{u}^{{}^{\prime}}\right){u}^{2}}{{\left(1-{u}^{2}\right)}^{2}}\right)\left(s\right)

\displaystyle{k}_{g}{{\left(s\right)k}_{g}}^{{}^{\prime}}\left(s\right)=\left(\frac{u{u}^{{}^{\prime}}}{{\left(1-{u}^{2}\right)}^{2}}\right)\left(s\right)

{{k}_{g}}^{{}^{\prime}}\left(s\right)=\left(\left(\frac{u{u}^{{}^{\prime}}}{{\left(1-{u}^{2}\right)}^{2}}\right)\left(\varepsilon\sqrt{\frac{1-{u}^{2}}{{u}^{2}}}\right)\right)\left(s\right).

(17)

Putting (17) and (18) in (16), we have

\displaystyle\sigma(s)=\frac{\varepsilon{{k}_{g}}^{{}^{\prime}}\left(s\right)}{{\left({{k}_{g}}^{2}\left(s\right)+1\right)}^{{3}/{2}}}

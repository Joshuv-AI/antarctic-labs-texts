A Unified Primal-Dual Algorithm Framework for Inequality Constrained Problems
Zhenyuan Zhu, Fan Chen, Junyu Zhang, Zaiwen Wen

Abstract
In this paper, we propose a unified primal-dual algorithm framework based on the augmented Lagrangian function for composite convex problems with conic inequality constraints. The new framework is highly versatile. First, it not only covers many existing algorithms such as PDHG, Chambolle-Pock (CP), GDA, OGDA and linearized ALM, but also guides us to design a new efficient algorithm called Simi-OGDA (SOGDA). Second, it enables us to study the role of the augmented penalty term in the convergence analysis. Interestingly, a properly selected penalty not only improves the numerical performance of the above methods, but also theoretically enables the convergence of algorithms like PDHG and SOGDA. Under properly designed step sizes and penalty term, our unified framework preserves the 𝒪 ⁡ ( 1 / N ) \mathcal{O}(1/N) ergodic convergence while not requiring any prior knowledge about the magnitude of the optimal Lagrangian multiplier. Linear convergence rate for affine equality constrained problem is also obtained given appropriate conditions. Finally, numerical experiments on linear programming, ℓ 1 \ell_{1} minimization problem, and multi-block basis pursuit problem demonstrate the efficiency of our methods.



∎

Introduction

In this work, we consider the following convex composite optimization problem:

\begin{split}\min_{x}\ &\Phi(x):=f(x)+h(x),\\
\,\textrm{s.t.}\,\ &Ax-b\in\mathcal{K}.\end{split}

(1)

In this problem,
f(x):\mathbb{R}^{n}\mapsto\mathbb{R}
is a differentiable convex function whose gradient
\nabla f(\cdot)
is
L_{f}
-Lipschitz continuous,
h(x):\mathbb{R}^{n}\mapsto\mathbb{R}
is a simple convex function whose proximal operator can be efficiently evaluated, the set
\mathcal{K}
is either
\{0\}
or a proper cone, and
A\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times n}
,
b\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
are some matrix and vector. In particular, (
1
) becomes the standard affine equality constrained problem when
\mathcal{K}=\{0\}
.

For problem (
1
) with
\mathcal{K}=\{0\}
, the augmented Lagrangian method (ALM) and the alternating direction method of multipliers (ADMM) are the most popular algorithms. ALM minimizes the augmented Lagrangian function with respect to primal variables and applies a gradient ascent step on the dual variable. Due to Danskin’s theorem, ALM can thus be interpreted as applying gradient ascent method to the dual function. For multi-block problems with separable structure, ADMM replaces the exact primal minimization of ALM with one cycle of block coordinate minimization, which can be viewed as an approximate dual gradient ascent. However, the approximation in the primal minimization step makes the convergence of ADMM subtle. When the number of blocks equals two, the convergence of ADMM was established in the context of operator splitting
eckstein1992douglas
. When the number of blocks is greater than two, a counterexample was constructed in
chen2016direct
, indicating that the direct extension of ADMM may not necessarily converge for general multi-block problems.
To ensure convergence, one must make further modification to the algorithm
deng2017parallel
;
gao2019randomized
, or assuming certain extra conditions on the problem
cai2017convergence
;
chen2013convergence
;
li2015convergent
;
lin2015global
;
lin2015sublinear
.

In contrast to the (approximate) dual gradient methods that require (approximately) solving the primal problem in each iteration, the primal-dual methods like Chambolle-Pock
chambolle2011first
;
chambolle2016ergodic
update the primal and dual variables equally with the current gradient information. Thus they experience no trouble from the inexact primal minimization.
So far, the primal-dual methods have not been extensively studied in the context of affinely constrained problems. Existing results mainly focus the saddle point problem:

\begin{split}\min_{x\in\mathcal{X}}\max_{y\in\mathcal{Y}}\ h(x)+\Psi(x,y)-s(y),\end{split}

(2)

where efficient projections onto
\mathcal{X}
and
\mathcal{Y}
are available. One early algorithm for solving (
2
) is the Arrow-Hurwicz method
uzawa1958iterative
, which is also known as the primal-dual hybrid gradient (PDHG) method
zhu2008efficient
. The convergence of PDHG has been established when one of
h(\cdot)
and
s(\cdot)
is strongly convex, while a non-convergent example exists if strong convexity is not available
he2014convergence
. The Chambolle-Pock method (CP)
chambolle2011first
is another famous primal-dual algorithm. In terms of the duality gap, the CP method preserves an
\mathcal{O}(1/N)
ergodic convergence rate, and is shown to be optimal
zhang2021lower
.
It is worth mentioning that both PDHG and CP focus on the case where
\Psi(x,y)
is bilinear, that is,
\Psi(x,y)=x^{\mathrm{T}}Ky
.
Recently, the optimistic gradient descent ascent (OGDA) method has gained popularity due to its superior empirical performance in GANs training
daskalakis2017training
.
For bilinear objective function, the convergence of OGDA was studied in
daskalakis2017training
;
liang2019interaction
. By viewing OGDA as an approximate proximal point method, the
\mathcal{O}(1/N)
convergence was established in
mokhtari2020convergence
for smooth convex-concave problem, as well as linear convergence for smooth strongly convex strongly concave problem. Further attempt was made in
wei2020linearOGDA
to prove the last-iterate linear convergence under metric subregularity. To our best knowledge, the OGDA methods has yet been studied for general non-smooth convex-concave problem.

Notice that most of the convergence results of PDHG, Chambolle-Pock and OGDA are established with respect to the duality gap of (
2
). However, for the saddle point problem induced by the (augmented) Lagrangian function of some constrained optimization problem, the duality gap is no longer a valid measure of convergence due to the unboundedness of the dual domain, see
zhang2021primal
. One general way to avoid this issue is artificially adding a suitable norm constraint to the multipliers. However, properly constructing this constraint requires prior knowledge on the magnitude of the optimal Lagrangian multiplier, which is impractical in many applications.
For specific algorithms such as the Chambolle-Pock method
hamedani2021primal
and the linearized ALM
xu2021first
;
luo2021accelerated
, as well as their variants, the convergence without bounded dual domain has been established. However, similar guarantee is still not available for many other primal-dual methods like OGDA, our newly proposed SOGDA, and so on.
In this work, we integrate several primal-dual methods into a unified algorithmic framework and establish a unified ergodic and nonergodic convergence for the main problem (
1
), without requiring prior knowledge on the bound of the optimal Lagrangian multiplier. The main contributions are summarized as follows:

•

We propose a unified primal-dual algorithm framework for solving (
1
). It covers several well-known algorithms, such as PDHG, Chambolle-Pock, GDA, OGDA and linearized ALM. By properly selecting the parameters of our framework, we also introduce a new algorithm called Semi-OGDA, abbreviated as SOGDA, which demonstrates promising performance in the numerical experiments.

•

By analyzing the constraint violation and function value gap instead of the duality gap, we establish a unified analysis for our framework to achieve an
\mathcal{O}(1/N)
ergodic convergence for problem (
1
), without requiring any prior knowledge on the magnitude of the optimal multiplier. Furthermore, the non-ergodic convergence of our algorithm framework can also be established. Under suitable conditions, our algorithm achieves linear convergence.

•

Unlike the standard primal-dual algorithms like PDHG who solve a minimax problem based on the Lagrangian function of problem (
1
), we build our framework upon the augmented Lagrangian function. On the one hand, our analysis shows that adding an augmented penalty term makes the constraint
y\in\mathcal{K}^{*}
optional for the Lagrangian multiplier
y
. This can often improve the flexibility of the algorithm design since one can fully remove this constraint if it causes difficulty in solving subproblems. We also prove that the augmented penalty term can theoretically improve the convergence guarantee for PDHG and SOGDA. On the other hand, algorithms with an augmented Lagrangian function usually converge faster than those based on the Lagrangian function in numerical experiments.

•

Our analysis framework can be easily extended beyond bilinear saddle point problem for augmented Lagrangian function. As a byproduct of our analysis, we derive the convergence result of the proximal OGDA, generalization of OGDA to non-smooth problem.

We use
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
to denote Euclidean inner product, and we use
\|\cdot\|
to denote the Euclidean norm for a vector or the spectral norm for a matrix. For any symmetric matrix
A
, we define the weighted inner product
\langle x,y\rangle_{A}:=\langle x,Ay\rangle=\langle Ax,y\rangle
, and when
A\succeq 0
, we define the corresponding norm
\|x\|_{A}:=\sqrt{\langle x,x\rangle_{A}}
. For any set
\mathcal{X}
, the indicator function is defined as
\mathbbm{1}_{\mathcal{X}}(x)=0
if
x\in\mathcal{X}
and
\mathbbm{1}_{\mathcal{X}}(x)=+\infty
if
x\notin\mathcal{X}
. The distance between the point
x
and the set
\mathcal{X}
is defined as
\mathrm{dist}\left(x,\mathcal{X}\right):=\min_{y\in\mathcal{X}}\|x-y\|_{2}
, and more generally,
\mathrm{dist}_{A}\left(x,\mathcal{X}\right):=\min_{y\in\mathcal{X}}\|x-y\|_{A}
. For any cone
\mathcal{K}
, we denote its dual cone by
\mathcal{K}^{*}
and define the polar cone as
\mathcal{K}^{\circ}:=-\mathcal{K}^{*}
. Let
\mathcal{S}
be a closed set, we use
\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{S}}(\cdot)
to denote the projection operator to
\mathcal{S}
. For simplicity, we will write
\mathcal{P}_{+}\left(\cdot\right):=\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{K}^{\circ}}(\cdot)
and
\mathcal{P}_{-}\left(\cdot\right):=-\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{K}}\left(\cdot\right)
throughout the paper.

1.2
Basic assumptions

Assumption 1.1

f(x)
is a convex differentiable function and
h(x)
is a lower semi-continuous convex function.

Assumption 1.2

The gradient of
f(x)
is
L_{f}
-Lipschitz continuous, that is,

\|\nabla f(x)-\nabla f(x^{\prime})\|\leq L_{f}\|x-x^{\prime}\|,\quad\forall x,x^{\prime}.

For the convenience of notation, we define
f_{\rho}(x):=f(x)+\frac{\rho}{2}\|Ax-b\|^{2}
and let
L_{f_{\rho}}:=L_{f}+\rho\|A\|^{2}
be the Lipschitz constant of
\nabla f_{\rho}(x)
.

Assumption 1.3

The optimal solution of (
1
) is attainable, that is, there exists
x^{*}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
such that
Ax^{*}-b\in\mathcal{K}
and
\Phi(x^{*})
equals to the optimal value
\Phi^{*}
.

Assumption 1.4
(Slater’s condition)

For the convex problem (
1
),
let
\mathcal{D}=\mathrm{dom}\ \Phi
and
\mathrm{relint}\ \mathcal{D}
denote the relative interior of the set
\mathcal{D}
.
There exists
x\in\mathrm{relint}\ \mathcal{D}
such that
Ax-b\in\mathrm{int}\ \mathcal{K}
, where
\mathrm{int}\ \mathcal{K}
denotes the interior of the cone
\mathcal{K}
.
When the cone
\mathcal{K}
is polyhedral (including the case of
\mathcal{K}=\{0\}
), the condition can be relaxed to the existence of
x\in\mathrm{relint}\ \mathcal{D}
such that
Ax-b\in\mathcal{K}
.

2
Primal-dual methods for conic inequality constrained problems

2.1
The augmented Lagrangian duality

When
\mathcal{K}=\{0\}
or
\mathcal{K}
is a proper cone that has nonempty interior, the strong duality holds under Assumptions
1.1
and
1.4
.
It implies that the minimization problem (
1
) is equivalent to a saddle point problem:

\displaystyle\min_{x}\max_{y\in\mathcal{K}^{*}}\ \mathcal{L}(x,y)=f(x)+h(x)-y^{\mathrm{T}}(Ax-b).

(3)

In this section, we generalize the strong duality to the saddle point formulation of problem (
1
) based on the augmented Lagrangian function. Importantly, by incorporating the augmented penalty term, the dual constraint
y\in\mathcal{K}^{*}
becomes optional. One can fully remove this constraint if it causes difficulty in solving subproblems.
Although the augmented duality for inequality constraint has been discussed in
rockafellar2009variational
, we provide a simpler proof for problem (
1
).

Lemma 1

Suppose that
\mathcal{K}
is
\{0\}
or a proper cone. Given any penalty coefficient
\rho>0
, we define the augmented Lagrangian function as

\mathcal{L}_{\rho}(x,y):=f(x)+h(x)+\frac{\rho}{2}\left\|\mathcal{P}_{+}\left(Ax-b-\frac{y}{\rho}\right)\right\|^{2}-\frac{\left\|y\right\|^{2}}{2\rho}.

(4)

Under Assumptions
1.1
and
1.4
, the strong duality holds for
\mathcal{L}_{\rho}(x,y)
, that is,

\min_{x}\max_{y}\ \mathcal{L}_{\rho}(x,y)=\max_{y}\min_{x}\ \mathcal{L}_{\rho}(x,y),

(5)

where both sides of (
5
) are equivalent to problem (
1
).

Proof

We first consider the case where
\mathcal{K}
is a proper cone.
For any given
\rho>0
, we introduce a slack variable
\pi
to the conic inequality constraint and consider the following equivalent formulation of (
1
):

\begin{split}\min_{\pi\in-\mathcal{K},x}\ &f(x)+h(x)+\frac{\rho}{2}\|Ax-b+\pi\|^{2},\\
\,\textrm{s.t.}\,\ &Ax-b+\pi=0,\end{split}

(6)

whose Lagrangian function equals
\mathcal{L}(x,\pi,y):=f(x)+h(x)-y^{\mathrm{T}}(Ax-b+\pi)+\frac{\rho}{2}\|Ax-b+\pi\|^{2}
.
By Assumption
1.4
, there exists
x\in\mathrm{relint}\ \mathcal{D}
and
\pi\in\mathrm{int}\ \mathcal{K}
such that
Ax-b+\pi=0
, which indicates that Slater’s condition holds for problem (
6
).
Thus, the strong duality implies

\min_{\pi\in-\mathcal{K},x}\max_{y}\,\,\mathcal{L}(x,\pi,y)\,\,=\,\,\max_{y}\min_{\pi\in-\mathcal{K},x}\,\,\mathcal{L}(x,\pi,y).

(7)

This saddle point problem (
7
) is equivalent to the original minimization problem (
1
). Note that for any
x
and
y
, the minimization over
\pi
admits a closed form solution:

\argmin_{\pi\in-\mathcal{K}}\ \mathcal{L}(x,\pi,y)=\mathcal{P}_{-}\Big(Ax-b-\frac{y}{\rho}\Big).

Substituting this solution to
\mathcal{L}
gives the function
\mathcal{L}_{\rho}(x,y)
defined by (
4
). That is,

\max_{y}\min_{\pi\in-\mathcal{K},x}\,\,\mathcal{L}(x,\pi,y)\,\,=\,\,\max_{y}\min_{x}\mathcal{L}_{\rho}(x,y).

(8)

To complete the proof, it remains to show:

\min_{x}\max_{y}\ \mathcal{L}_{\rho}(x,y)=\min_{\pi\in-\mathcal{K},x}\max_{y}\ \mathcal{L}(x,\pi,y).

(9)

First, when
Ax-b\notin\mathcal{K}
, it is straightforward to see that

\displaystyle\max_{y}\mathcal{L}_{\rho}(x,y)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\max_{y}\min_{\pi\in-\mathcal{K}}f(x)+h(x)-y^{\mathrm{T}}(Ax-b+\pi)+\frac{\rho}{2}\|Ax-b+\pi\|^{2}

(10)

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\max_{y}\min_{\pi\in-\mathcal{K}}f(x)+h(x)-y^{\mathrm{T}}(Ax-b+\pi)

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\max_{y\in\mathcal{K}^{*}}\min_{\pi\in-\mathcal{K}}f(x)+h(x)-y^{\mathrm{T}}(Ax-b+\pi)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\max_{y\in\mathcal{K}^{*}}f(x)+h(x)-y^{\mathrm{T}}(Ax-b)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle+\infty=\max_{y}\mathcal{L}(x,\pi,y).

Second, when
Ax-b\in\mathcal{K}
, the closed form solution to (
9
) can be obtained given any fixed
x
. In details,
y=0
and
\pi=\mathcal{P}_{-}\left(Ax-b\right)
are the optimal solution to
\min_{\pi\in-\mathcal{K}}\max_{y}\ \mathcal{L}(x,\pi,y)
. Thus, combined with (
10
), the right hand side of (
9
) is equivalent to (
1
). For the left hand side of (
9
), the optimal solution of
y
is
y=0
when
Ax-b\in\mathcal{K}
. Thus, combined with (
10
), the left hand side of (
9
) is also equivalent to (
1
). Therefore, combining (
7
)-(
9
) yields the conclusion.

For the case of
\mathcal{K}=\{0\}
, it always holds that
\pi=0
and the conclusion can be proved similarly.
∎

According to the KKT condition of (

), it can be proved that the optimal dual variable

Therefore, (

) still holds when

\min_{x}\max_{y\in\mathcal{K}^{*}}\ \mathcal{L}_{\rho}(x,y)=\max_{y\in\mathcal{K}^{*}}\min_{x}\ \mathcal{L}_{\rho}(x,y).

(11)

As a result of Lemma
1
, solving the original minimization problem (

2.2
A unified primal-dual algorithm framework

In this section, we propose a unified primal-dual algorithm framework to solve problem (
12
), as well as problem (
3
). For the ease of notation, we define

s(y)=\begin{cases}0,&{\rho>0,}\\
\mathbbm{1}_{\mathcal{K}^{*}}(y),&{\rho=0,}\end{cases}

and

\Psi(x,y)=\begin{cases}{f(x)+\frac{\rho}{2}\left\|\mathcal{P}_{+}\left(Ax-b-\frac{y}{\rho}\right)\right\|^{2}-\frac{\left\|y\right\|^{2}}{2\rho},}&{\rho>0,}\\
{f(x)-y^{\mathrm{T}}(Ax-b),}&{\rho=0.}\end{cases}

Depending on the value of
\rho
, we can rewrite both problem (
3
) and (
12
) as

\min_{x}\max_{y}\ \mathcal{L}_{\rho}(x,y):=h(x)+\Psi(x,y)-s(y).

(13)

Let the proximal operator of an arbitrary function
f
be

\mathbf{prox}_{f}(x):=\argmin_{u}\{f(u)+\frac{1}{2}\|x-u\|^{2}\}.

Given this notation, we propose the following primal-dual algorithm framework:

Input: penalty factor
\rho\geq 0
; primal and dual step sizes
\tau,\sigma>0
;
gradient extrapolation coefficients
\alpha\in[0,1]
and
\beta
;
the ratio of Gauss-Seidel iteration versus Jacobian iteration
\mu\in[0,1]
.

Initialize:
x^{-1}=x^{0},y^{-1}=y^{0}=0
.

for

k=0,1,2,\dots

do

1

Compute
g_{x}^{k}=\nabla_{x}\Psi(x^{k},y^{k}),g_{y}^{k}=\nabla_{y}\Psi(x^{k},y^{k})
.

Perform the update:

\begin{split}x^{k+1}&=\mathbf{prox}_{\tau h}\left[x^{k}-\tau\left((1+\alpha)g_{x}^{k}-\alpha g_{x}^{k-1}\right)\right],\\
y^{k+1}&=\mathbf{prox}_{\sigma s}\left[y^{k}+\sigma\mu\left((1+\beta)g_{y}^{k}-\beta g_{y}^{k-1}\right)\right.\\
&\qquad\qquad\quad\left.+\sigma(1-\mu)\left((1+\beta)g_{y}^{k+1}-\beta g_{y}^{k}\right)\right],\end{split}

(14)

Algorithm 1

A unified primal-dual algorithm framework

When
\rho=0
, the gradients
g_{x}^{k}
and
g_{y}^{k}
are easy to obtain. When
\rho>0
, we have

\displaystyle g_{x}^{k}

\displaystyle=\nabla f(x^{k})+\rho A^{\mathrm{T}}\mathcal{P}_{+}\left(w^{k}\right),

\displaystyle g_{y}^{k}

\displaystyle=-\frac{y^{k}}{\rho}-\mathcal{P}_{+}\left(w^{k}\right)=-(Ax^{k}-b)-\mathcal{P}_{-}\left(w^{k}\right),

where
w^{k}=Ax^{k}-b-\frac{y^{k}}{\rho}
.
Note that the gradient
g_{y}^{k+1}=\nabla_{y}\Psi(x^{k+1},y^{k+1})
in the
y^{k+1}
update rule involves
y^{k+1}
itself if
\rho>0
, the algorithm defined by (
14
) is potentially an implicit scheme. Fortunately, thanks to the fact that we can set
s(y)=0
since
y\in\mathcal{K}^{*}
is optional when
\rho>0
, the
y^{k+1}
update can equivalently be rewritten in an explicit form, which is described as follows.

Lemma 2

Let
s(y)=0
and
\rho>0
, the dual update rule of (
14
) can be written as

y^{k+1}=\omega-\frac{\kappa}{\kappa+1}\cdot\mathcal{P}_{-}\left(\nu-\omega\right),

where
\omega=y^{k}+\sigma\mu\left((1+\beta)g_{y}^{k}-\beta g_{y}^{k-1}\right)-\sigma(1-\mu)\left((1+\beta)(Ax^{k+1}-b)+\beta g_{y}^{k}\right)
,
\kappa=\sigma(1-\mu)(1+\beta)/\rho\geq 0
, and

\nu=\rho(Ax^{k+1}-b)
.

Proof

According to (
14
),
y^{k+1}
is the solution to the following equation:

y^{k+1}=\omega-\kappa\mathcal{P}_{-}\left(\nu-y^{k+1}\right).

Due to Moreau’s decomposition in Theorem
7.1
in appendix, we have

\nu-y^{k+1}=\mathcal{P}_{+}\left(\nu-y^{k+1}\right)-\mathcal{P}_{-}\left(\nu-y^{k+1}\right).

Combining the above two equations gives

\mathcal{P}_{+}\left(\nu-y^{k+1}\right)-(\kappa+1)\mathcal{P}_{-}\left(\nu-y^{k+1}\right)=\nu-\omega,

which further indicates that

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}_{+}\left(\nu-y^{k+1}\right)=\mathcal{P}_{+}\left(\nu-\omega\right)\quad\mbox{and}\quad\mathcal{P}_{-}\left(\nu-y^{k+1}\right)=\frac{\mathcal{P}_{-}\left(\nu-\omega\right)}{\kappa+1}.

Therefore,
y^{k+1}=\omega-\frac{\kappa}{\kappa+1}\cdot\mathcal{P}_{-}\left(\nu-\omega\right)
, which completes the proof.
∎

Remark 2

By Remark
1
, one can also set
s(y)=\mathbbm{1}_{\mathcal{K}^{*}}(y)
when
\rho>0
.
Numerical results show that the performance may benefit from this setting. However, the explicit dual update of (
14
) can only be obtained in specific cases such as
\mathcal{K}=\{x|x\leq 0\}
, see Lemma
10
in the appendix.
Otherwise, we can utilize the following explicit variant of the framework in practice:

\begin{split}x^{k+1}&=\mathbf{prox}_{\tau h}\left[x^{k}-\tau\left((1+\alpha)g_{x}^{k}-\alpha g_{x}^{k-1}\right)\right],\\
y^{k+1}&=\mathbf{prox}_{\sigma s}\left[y^{k}+\sigma\mu\left((1+\beta)g_{y}^{k}-\beta g_{y}^{k-1}\right)\right.\\
&\qquad\qquad\quad\left.+\sigma(1-\mu)\left((1+\beta)g_{y}^{k+1,k}-\beta g_{y}^{k}\right)\right],\end{split}

(15)

where
g_{y}^{k+1,k}:=\nabla_{y}\Psi(x^{k+1},y^{k})
.
It is worth mentioning that the implicit scheme (
14
) and the explicit scheme (
15
) are equivalent when
\mathcal{K}=\{0\}
or
\rho=0
.

2.3
Consequences of the unified framework

In the unified primal-dual algorithm framework (
14
), one is allowed to choose a wide range of algorithmic parameters. In fact, under appropriate parameters, our framework not only covers several well-known primal-dual algorithms such as PDHG, Chambolle-Pock, GDA, OGDA and linearized ALM, but also leads to a new efficient algorithm which we call SOGDA. Next, let us discuss the detailed consequences of our framework under different parameter specifications.

SOGDA
Let us set
\mu=1,\alpha=0,\beta=1,\rho\geq 0
in (
14
), the scheme becomes a new algorithm which we call Semi-OGDA (SOGDA):

\displaystyle x^{k+1}

\displaystyle=\mathbf{prox}_{\tau h}\left[x^{k}-\tau g_{x}^{k}\right],

\displaystyle y^{k+1}

\displaystyle=\mathbf{prox}_{\sigma s}\left[y^{k}+\sigma\left(2g_{y}^{k}-g_{y}^{k-1}\right)\right].

When
\mathcal{K}=\{0\}
and
\rho=0
, this method can be interpreted as the OGDA method with only dual variables extrapolated or a Jacobian variant of the Chambolle-Pock method.

PDHG and Chembolle-Pock
Now let us consider the Gauss-Seidel iteration without primal extrapolation, which corresponds to
\mu=\alpha=0
. If we further remove the dual extrapolation by setting
\beta=0
, then we obtain the following scheme:

\begin{split}x^{k+1}&=\mathbf{prox}_{\tau h}\left[x^{k}-\tau g_{x}^{k}\right],\\
y^{k+1}&=\mathbf{prox}_{\sigma s}\left[y^{k}+\sigma g_{y}^{k+1}\right].\end{split}

(16)

Alternatively, if we allow the dual extrapolation and set
\beta=1
, the scheme becomes

\begin{split}x^{k+1}&=\mathbf{prox}_{\tau h}\left[x^{k}-\tau g_{x}^{k}\right],\\
y^{k+1}&=\mathbf{prox}_{\sigma s}\left[y^{k}+\sigma\left(2g_{y}^{k+1}-g_{y}^{k}\right)\right].\end{split}

(17)

When
\mathcal{K}=\{0\}
and
\rho=0
, the schemes (
16
) and (
17
) are PDHG and Chambolle-Pock, respectively. Therefore, (
16
) and (
17
) can be viewed as the natural extensions of PDHG and Chambolle-Pock to the conic inequality constrained problem with an augmented penalty term.

GDA and OGDA
If we consider the algorithms of Jacobian iteration, then we can set
\mu=1
. In addition, if we omit both primal and dual extrapolation by setting
\alpha=\beta=0
, then (
14
) becomes

\begin{split}x^{k+1}&=\mathbf{prox}_{\tau h}\left[x^{k}-\tau g_{x}^{k}\right],\\
y^{k+1}&=\mathbf{prox}_{\sigma s}\left[y^{k}+\sigma g_{y}^{k}\right].\end{split}

(18)

Alternatively, if we allow primal and dual extrapolation with
\alpha=\beta=1
, we obtain

\begin{split}x^{k+1}&=\mathbf{prox}_{\tau h}\left[x^{k}-\tau\left(2g_{x}^{k}-g_{x}^{k-1}\right)\right],\\
y^{k+1}&=\mathbf{prox}_{\sigma s}\left[y^{k}+\sigma\left(2g_{y}^{k}-g_{y}^{k-1}\right)\right].\end{split}

(19)

The scheme (
18
) is GDA and the scheme (
19
) is a proximal variant of OGDA. Since the existing works of OGDA mainly focus on differentiable problems, (
19
) is a direct extension of OGDA on the non-differentiable saddle point problems.

Linearized ALM and Jacobian linearized ADMM
Lastly, if we further require
\rho>0
and
\mathcal{K}=\{0\}
in the scheme (
16
), it becomes the linearized ALM
yang2013linearized
. If the primal variable can be split into multiple blocks in addition, then algorithm (

deng2017parallel
.

3
The ergodic convergence

3.1
Preliminary result

Note that the general scheme (
14
) covers a wide range of primal-dual algorithms. In this section, we establish an
\mathcal{O}(1/N)
ergodic convergence for (
14
) with a unified analysis. Note that for any
(\bar{x},\bar{y})
, due to (
10
), the duality gap for problem (
12
) remains
+\infty
whenever
A\bar{x}-b\notin\mathcal{K}
:

\max_{y\in\mathcal{Y}}\ \mathcal{L}_{\rho}(\bar{x},y)-\min_{x\in\mathcal{X}}\ \mathcal{L}_{\rho}(x,\bar{y})=+\infty.

(20)

Therefore, the duality gap is not a reasonable measure of the convergence for the conic constrained problems, which invalidates most existing analysis for general saddle point algorithms that establish their convergence in terms of the duality gap. In this paper, we carefully utilize the structure of the original minimization problem and give an error bound in terms of objective function gap and the constraint violation. First of all, let us provide a lemma that controls the objective function gap and the constraint violation separately by bounding their weighted summation.

Lemma 3

Suppose that Assumptions
1.1
,
1.3
and
1.4
are satisfied.
Let
(x^{*},y^{*})
be a pair of primal and dual optimal solution of problem (
1
). For any positive constant
\gamma>\left\|y^{*}\right\|
, if the following inequality holds

\displaystyle\Phi(x)-\Phi(x^{*})+\gamma\left\|\mathcal{P}_{+}\left(Ax-b\right)\right\|\leq\delta,

then it holds that

\displaystyle-\frac{\left\|y^{*}\right\|}{\gamma-\left\|y^{*}\right\|}\delta\leq\Phi(x)-\Phi(x^{*})\leq\delta,\qquad\left\|\mathcal{P}_{+}\left(Ax-b\right)\right\|\leq\frac{\delta}{\gamma-\left\|y^{*}\right\|}.

Proof

First, by direct computation, we have

\displaystyle\Phi(x)-\Phi(x^{*})+\gamma\left\|\mathcal{P}_{+}\left(Ax-b\right)\right\|

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle(i)}}{{\geq}}

\displaystyle\left(\gamma-\left\|y^{*}\right\|\right)\left\|\mathcal{P}_{+}\left(Ax-b\right)\right\|+\Phi(x)-\Phi(x^{*})-\left\langle{y^{*}},{\mathcal{P}_{+}\left(Ax-b\right)}\right\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\gamma-\left\|y^{*}\right\|\right)\left\|\mathcal{P}_{+}\left(Ax-b\right)\right\|+\Phi(x)-\Phi(x^{*})-\left\langle{y^{*}},{Ax-b}\right\rangle

\displaystyle-\left\langle{y^{*}},{\mathcal{P}_{+}\left(Ax-b\right)-(Ax-b)}\right\rangle

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle(ii)}}{{\geq}}

\displaystyle\left(\gamma-\left\|y^{*}\right\|\right)\left\|\mathcal{P}_{+}\left(Ax-b\right)\right\|+\Phi(x)-\Phi(x^{*})-\left\langle{A^{\mathrm{T}}y^{*}},{x-x^{*}}\right\rangle

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle(iii)}}{{\geq}}

\displaystyle\left(\gamma-\left\|y^{*}\right\|\right)\left\|\mathcal{P}_{+}\left(Ax-b\right)\right\|,

where (i) is due to the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality,
(ii) uses the fact that
\mathcal{P}_{+}\left(u\right)-u\in-\mathcal{K}
,
y^{*}\in\mathcal{K}^{*}
and
\left\langle{y^{*}},{Ax^{*}-b}\right\rangle=0
,
(iii) comes from the fact that
A^{\mathrm{T}}y^{*}\in\partial\Phi(x^{*})
and the convexity of
\Phi
, that is,
\Phi(x)\geq\Phi(x^{*})+\left\langle{\partial\Phi(x^{*})},{x-x^{*}}\right\rangle.

Therefore, as long as
\gamma>\left\|y^{*}\right\|
, it holds that
\left\|\mathcal{P}_{+}\left(Ax-b\right)\right\|\leq\frac{\delta}{\gamma-\left\|y^{*}\right\|}
, and hence

\displaystyle\Phi(x)-\Phi(x^{*})\geq\left\langle{y^{*}},{Ax-b}\right\rangle\geq-\left\|y^{*}\right\|\left\|\mathcal{P}_{+}\left(Ax-b\right)\right\|\geq-\frac{\left\|y^{*}\right\|}{\gamma-\left\|y^{*}\right\|}\delta,

which completes the proof of this lemma. ∎

Let
z^{k}=[x^{k};y^{k}]\in\mathbb{R}^{m+n}
be the iterates generated by the general primal-dual method given in (
14
).
Define the operator
F:\mathbb{R}^{m+n}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{m+n}
as

F(z)=[\nabla_{x}\Psi(z);-\nabla_{y}\Psi(z)].

(21)

Define the function
R(z):=h(x)+s(y)
and the scaling matrices

\Lambda=\left[\begin{array}[]{ll}\tau I_{n}&\\
&\sigma I_{m}\\
\end{array}\right],\qquad\Theta=\left[\begin{array}[]{ll}\alpha I_{n}&\\
&\beta I_{m}\\
\end{array}\right],\qquad\Xi=\left[\begin{array}[]{ll}I_{n}&\\
&\mu I_{m}\\
\end{array}\right].

Then our unified primal-dual algorithm framework (
14
) can be rewritten as

\displaystyle z^{k+1}=\mathbf{prox}_{\Lambda R}\big(z^{k}

\displaystyle-\Lambda\Xi\left[\left(I+\Theta\right)F(z^{k})-\Theta F(z^{k-1})\right]

\displaystyle-\Lambda(I-\Xi)\left[\left(I+\Theta\right)F(z^{k+1})-\Theta F(z^{k})\right]\big).

In the next lemma, we characterize the one-step behavior of this algorithm.

Lemma 4

Under Assumption
1.1
, for any
z
, it holds that

\displaystyle R(z^{k+1})-R(z)+\left\langle F(z^{k+1}),z^{k+1}-z\right\rangle

(22)

\displaystyle\leq\

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\|z^{k}-z\|^{2}_{\Lambda^{-1}}-\frac{1}{2}\|z^{k+1}-z\|^{2}_{\Lambda^{-1}}-\frac{1}{2}\left\|z^{k+1}-z^{k}\right\|^{2}_{\Lambda^{-1}}

\displaystyle+\!\left\langle{F(z^{k})-F(z^{k-1})},{z-z^{k}}\right\rangle_{\Xi\Theta}-\left\langle{F(z^{k+1})-F(z^{k})},{z-z^{k+1}}\right\rangle_{\Xi\Theta}

\displaystyle+\!\left\langle{F(z^{k})\!-\!F(z^{k-1})},{z^{k}\!-\!z^{k+1}}\right\rangle_{\!\Xi\Theta}+\langle F(z^{k+1})-F(z^{k}),z-z^{k+1}\rangle_{\Theta-\Xi}.

Proof

By the optimality condition of the proximal subproblem in (
14
), there exists a vector
v^{k+1}\in\partial R(z^{k+1})
such that

\begin{split}0=v^{k+1}+\Lambda^{-1}(z^{k+1}-z^{k})&+\Xi\left[\left(I+\Theta\right)F(z^{k})-\Theta F(z^{k-1})\right]\\
&+(I-\Xi)\left[\left(I+\Theta\right)F(z^{k+1})-\Theta F(z^{k})\right].\end{split}

(23)

Taking inner product between (
23
) and
z^{k+1}-z
and then rearranging the terms, we get

\displaystyle\langle v^{k+1},z^{k+1}-z\rangle+\langle F(z^{k+1}),z^{k+1}-z\rangle

(24)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\langle z^{k+1}-z^{k},z-z^{k+1}\rangle_{\Lambda^{-1}}+\langle F(z^{k})-F(z^{k-1}),z-z^{k+1}\rangle_{\Xi\Theta}

\displaystyle-\langle F(z^{k+1})-F(z^{k}),z-z^{k+1}\rangle_{\Xi\Theta}+\langle F(z^{k+1})-F(z^{k}),z-z^{k+1}\rangle_{\Theta-\Xi}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\|z^{k}-z\|^{2}_{\Lambda^{-1}}-\frac{1}{2}\|z^{k+1}-z\|^{2}_{\Lambda^{-1}}-\frac{1}{2}\left\|z^{k+1}-z^{k}\right\|^{2}_{\Lambda^{-1}}

\displaystyle+\langle F(z^{k})-F(z^{k-1}),z-z^{k+1}\rangle_{\Xi\Theta}-\langle F(z^{k+1})-F(z^{k}),z-z^{k+1}\rangle_{\Xi\Theta}

\displaystyle+\langle F(z^{k+1})-F(z^{k}),z-z^{k+1}\rangle_{\Theta-\Xi},

where the last equality is because

\langle z^{k+1}-z^{k},z-z^{k+1}\rangle_{\Lambda^{-1}}=\frac{1}{2}\|z^{k}-z\|^{2}_{\Lambda^{-1}}-\frac{1}{2}\|z^{k+1}-z\|^{2}_{\Lambda^{-1}}-\frac{1}{2}\left\|z^{k+1}-z^{k}\right\|^{2}_{\Lambda^{-1}}.

Substituting
R(z^{k+1})-R(z)\leq\langle v^{k+1},z^{k+1}-z\rangle
into (
24
) proves the lemma. ∎

Inspired by the above “one-step descent”, we consider the following potential function constructed for an arbitrary reference point
z
:

\begin{split}\Delta_{k}(z):=&\frac{1}{2}\|z^{k}-z\|^{2}_{\Lambda^{-1}}+\frac{c}{2}\|z^{k}-z^{k-1}\|^{2}_{\Lambda^{-1}}\\
&+\langle F(z^{k})-F(z^{k-1}),z-z^{k}\rangle_{\Xi\Theta}+(\mu-\beta)\left\langle{\nabla_{y}\Psi(z^{k})},{y^{k}-y}\right\rangle,\end{split}

(25)

where

c
is a positive constant depending on
\alpha,\beta,\mu,\tau,\sigma,\rho
, which shall be specified later.

3.2
Ergodic convergence for the affine equality constrained problem

In the following analysis, we start with the case
\mathcal{K}=\{0\}
, whose analysis is cleaner and more insightful compared to the general case.
In this case, we specify the weight
c
in the potential as

c=C^{\mathrm{affine}}_{\alpha,\beta,\mu}(\tau,\sigma,\rho):=\alpha\tau L_{f_{\rho}}+\max\{\left|\mu\beta\right|\sqrt{\sigma\tau}\left\|A\right\|,\alpha\sqrt{\sigma\tau}\left\|A\right\|\}.

Actually any
c\geq C^{\mathrm{affine}}_{\alpha,\beta,\mu}(\tau,\sigma,\rho)
suffices.
Given any coefficient
c
, we define the matrix
P_{c}
as

\displaystyle P_{c}:=\begin{bmatrix}\rho I_{m}&0_{m\times n}&\frac{1-\alpha-\beta+\mu}{2}I_{m}\\
0_{n\times m}&\left(\frac{1-2c}{2\tau}-\frac{(1-\alpha)L_{f_{\rho}}}{2}\right)I_{n}&\frac{\beta-\mu}{2}A^{\mathrm{T}}\\
\frac{1-\alpha-\beta+\mu}{2}I_{m}&\frac{\beta-\mu}{2}A&\frac{1-2c}{2\sigma}I_{m}\end{bmatrix}.

As long as
P_{c}\succeq 0
, we derive the following lemma to characterize the monotonicity of the potential function.

Lemma 5

Suppose Assumptions
1.1
-
1.4
hold and let
\mathcal{K}=\{0\}
so that (
1
) is an affine equality constrained problem.
Define
\tilde{z}=(x^{*},y)
,
\forall y\in\mathbbm{R}^{m}
, where
x^{*}
be a primal optimal solution.
Then as long as
0\leq\alpha\leq 1
and
P_{c}\succeq 0
, we have

\displaystyle\Delta_{k}(\tilde{z})-\Delta_{k+1}(\tilde{z})\geq\

\displaystyle\Phi(x^{k+1})-\Phi(x^{*})-\langle Ax^{k+1}-b,y\rangle+\delta^{k},

(26)

where
\delta^{k}\geq 0
is some non-negative term.

Proof

Substituting
\tilde{z}=(x^{*},y)
into Lemma
4
and rearranging the terms yields

\displaystyle h(x^{k+1})-h(x^{*})+\left\langle{F(z^{k+1})},{z^{k+1}-\tilde{z}}\right\rangle

(27)

\displaystyle+(\alpha-1)\left\langle{\nabla_{x}\Psi(z^{k+1})-\nabla_{x}\Psi(z^{k})},{x^{k+1}-x^{*}}\right\rangle

\displaystyle\!\!\leq\

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\|z^{k}-\tilde{z}\|^{2}_{\Lambda^{-1}}-\frac{1}{2}\|z^{k+1}-\tilde{z}\|^{2}_{\Lambda^{-1}}-\frac{1}{2}\left\|z^{k+1}-z^{k}\right\|^{2}_{\Lambda^{-1}}

\displaystyle+\left\langle{F(z^{k})-F(z^{k-1})},{\tilde{z}-z^{k}}\right\rangle_{\Xi\Theta}-\left\langle{F(z^{k+1})-F(z^{k})},{\tilde{z}-z^{k+1}}\right\rangle_{\Xi\Theta}

\displaystyle+(\mu-\beta)\left\langle{\nabla_{y}\Psi(z^{k})},{y^{k}-y}\right\rangle-(\mu-\beta)\left\langle{\nabla_{y}\Psi(z^{k+1})},{y^{k+1}-y}\right\rangle

\displaystyle+\left\langle{F(z^{k})-F(z^{k-1})},{z^{k}-z^{k+1}}\right\rangle_{\Xi\Theta}+(\mu-\beta)\left\langle{\nabla_{y}\Psi(z^{k})},{y^{k+1}-y^{k}}\right\rangle.

Plugging in the definition of
\Delta_{k}(z)
yields

\displaystyle\Delta_{k}(\tilde{z})-\Delta_{k+1}(\tilde{z})\geq h(x^{k+1})-h(x^{*})+\Gamma_{1}+\Gamma_{2}+\Gamma_{3},

(28)

where

\displaystyle\Gamma_{1}

\displaystyle:=\left\langle{F(z^{k+1})},{z^{k+1}-\tilde{z}}\right\rangle+(\alpha-1)\left\langle{\nabla_{x}\Psi(z^{k+1})-\nabla_{x}\Psi(z^{k})},{x^{k+1}-x^{*}}\right\rangle,

\displaystyle\Gamma_{2}

\displaystyle:={\frac{c}{2}\left\|z^{k}\!\!-\!z^{k-1}\right\|^{2}_{\!\Lambda^{-1}}\!+\!\frac{1\!-\!c}{2}\|z^{k+1}\!\!-\!z^{k}\|^{2}_{\!\Lambda^{-1}}}\!-\!\left\langle{F(z^{k})\!-\!F(z^{k-1})},{z^{k}\!\!-\!z^{k+1}}\right\rangle_{\!\Xi\Theta}\!,

\displaystyle\Gamma_{3}

\displaystyle:=-(\mu\!-\!\beta)\!\left\langle{\nabla_{y}\Psi(z^{k})},{y^{k+1}\!-\!y^{k}}\right\rangle\!.

(29)

We first deal with the term
\Gamma_{1}
.
Through a direct computation, we have

\displaystyle\Gamma_{1}=

\displaystyle\,\alpha\left\langle{\nabla f_{\rho}(x^{k+1})-A^{\top}y^{k+1}},{x^{k+1}-x^{*}}\right\rangle+\left\langle{Ax^{k+1}-b},{y^{k+1}-y}\right\rangle

\displaystyle+(1-\alpha)\left\langle{\nabla f_{\rho}(x^{k})-A^{\top}y^{k}},{x^{k+1}-x^{*}}\right\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left\langle{\alpha\nabla f_{\rho}(x^{k+1})+(1-\alpha)\nabla f_{\rho}(x^{k})},{x^{k+1}-x^{*}}\right\rangle-\left\langle{Ax^{k+1}-b},{y}\right\rangle

\displaystyle+(1-\alpha)\left\langle{A^{\top}(y^{k+1}-y^{k})},{x^{k+1}-x^{*}}\right\rangle.

Due to the convexity of
f_{\rho}(x)
and the smoothness of
f_{\rho}(x)
, it holds that

\begin{split}&\langle\nabla f_{\rho}(x^{k+1}),x^{k+1}-x^{*}\rangle\geq f_{\rho}(x^{k+1})-f_{\rho}(x^{*}),\\
&\langle\nabla f_{\rho}(x^{k}),x^{k+1}-x^{*}\rangle\geq f_{\rho}(x^{k+1})-f_{\rho}(x^{*})-\frac{L_{f_{\rho}}}{2}\left\|x^{k+1}-x^{k}\right\|^{2}.\end{split}

(30)

Note that
Ax^{*}=b
, we then have

\displaystyle\Gamma_{1}\geq

\displaystyle\,\,f(x^{k+1})-f(x^{*})-\left\langle{Ax^{k+1}-b},{y}\right\rangle+\rho\left\|Ax^{k+1}-b\right\|^{2}

(31)

\displaystyle-\frac{(1-\alpha)L_{f_{\rho}}}{2}\left\|x^{k+1}-x^{k}\right\|^{2}+(1-\alpha)\left\langle{y^{k+1}-y^{k}},{Ax^{k+1}-b}\right\rangle.

Next, we bound the term
\Gamma_{2}
. By the smoothness of
F
, for any
z=(x,y)
we have

\begin{split}&\left|\left\langle{F(z^{k})-F(z^{k-1})},{z^{k}-z}\right\rangle_{\Xi\Theta}\right|\\
\leq\ &\alpha\left|\langle\nabla f_{\rho}(x^{k})-\nabla f_{\rho}(x^{k-1}),x^{k}-x\rangle\right|+\alpha\left|\langle A^{\mathrm{T}}(y^{k}-y^{k-1}),x^{k}-x\rangle\right|\\
&+\left|\mu\beta\right|\left|\left\langle{A(x^{k}-x^{k-1})},{y^{k}-y}\right\rangle\right|\\
\leq\ &\frac{\alpha L_{f_{\rho}}}{2}\Big(\!\left\|x-x^{k}\right\|^{2}\!\!+\left\|x^{k}-x^{k-1}\right\|^{2}\!\Big)\\
&+\frac{\alpha\|A\|}{2}\Big(\sqrt{\frac{\sigma}{\tau}}\|x^{k}-x\|^{2}+\sqrt{\frac{\tau}{\sigma}}\|y^{k}-y^{k-1}\|^{2}\Big)\\
&+\frac{\left|\mu\beta\right|\!\left\|A\right\|}{2}\Big(\sqrt{\frac{\sigma}{\tau}}\left\|x^{k}-x^{k-1}\right\|^{2}+\sqrt{\frac{\tau}{\sigma}}\left\|y-y^{k}\right\|^{2}\Big)\\
\leq\ &\frac{c}{2}\left\|z^{k}-z^{k-1}\right\|_{\Lambda^{-1}}^{2}+\frac{c}{2}\left\|z^{k}-z\right\|_{\Lambda^{-1}}^{2},\end{split}

(32)

where the last inequality is due to definition of
c
.
Then we can take

\delta_{1}^{k}\!:=\!\frac{c}{2}\left\|z^{k+1}\!-\!z^{k}\right\|^{2}_{\Lambda^{-1}}\!+\!\frac{c}{2}\|z^{k}\!-\!z^{k-1}\|^{2}_{\Lambda^{-1}}\!-\!\left\langle{F(z^{k})\!-\!F(z^{k-1})},{z^{k}\!-\!z^{k+1}}\right\rangle_{\Xi\Theta},

and
\delta_{1}^{k}\geq 0
by (
32
). Combining (
31
) with
\Gamma_{2}=\delta_{1}^{k}+\frac{1-2c}{2}\left\|z^{k+1}-z^{k}\right\|^{2}_{\Lambda^{-1}}
, we see

\displaystyle\Delta_{k}(\tilde{z})-\Delta_{k+1}(\tilde{z})\geq\

\displaystyle\Phi(x^{k+1})-\Phi(x^{*})-\langle Ax^{k+1}-b,y\rangle+\delta_{1}^{k}+\delta_{2}^{k},

where

\displaystyle\delta_{2}^{k}:=

\displaystyle\frac{1-2c}{2}\left\|z^{k+1}-z^{k}\right\|^{2}_{\Lambda^{-1}}+\rho\|Ax^{k+1}-b\|^{2}-\frac{(1-\alpha)L_{f_{\rho}}}{2}\left\|x^{k+1}-x^{k}\right\|^{2}

\displaystyle+(\mu-\beta)\left\langle{Ax^{k}-b},{y^{k+1}-y^{k}}\right\rangle+(1-\alpha)\left\langle{Ax^{k+1}-b},{y^{k+1}-y^{k}}\right\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1-2c}{2}\left\|z^{k+1}-z^{k}\right\|^{2}_{\Lambda^{-1}}+\rho\|Ax^{k+1}-b\|^{2}-\frac{(1-\alpha)L_{f_{\rho}}}{2}\left\|x^{k+1}-x^{k}\right\|^{2}

\displaystyle\!-\!(\mu\!-\!\beta)\!\left\langle{A(x^{k+1}\!-\!x^{k})},{y^{k+1}\!-\!y^{k}}\right\rangle\!+\!(1\!-\!\alpha\!-\!\beta\!+\!\mu)\!\left\langle{Ax^{k+1}\!-\!b},{y^{k+1}\!-\!y^{k}}\right\rangle\!.

The fact
\delta_{2}^{k}\geq 0
directly follows from
P_{c}\succeq 0
, because
\delta_{2}^{k}
is a quadratic form of
Ax^{k+1}-b
,
x^{k+1}-x^{k}
and
y^{k+1}-y^{k}
, with matrix
P_{c}
. We finalize the proof by setting
\delta^{k}:=\delta_{1}^{k}+\delta_{2}^{k}
.
∎

Corollary 1

Under the settings of Lemma
5
with
\tilde{z}=(x^{*},y)
, it holds that

0\leq\Delta_{k}(\tilde{z})\leq\left\|z^{k}-\tilde{z}\right\|^{2}_{\Lambda^{-1}}+c\left\|z^{k}-z^{k-1}\right\|_{\Lambda^{-1}}^{2}.

(33)

Proof

Notice that due to
Ax^{*}=b
, we have

\displaystyle\begin{split}\Delta_{k}(\tilde{z})=&\frac{1}{2}\|z^{k}-z\|^{2}_{\Lambda^{-1}}+\frac{c}{2}\|z^{k}-z^{k-1}\|^{2}_{\Lambda^{-1}}+(\beta-\mu)\left\langle{A(x^{k}-x^{*})},{y^{k}-y}\right\rangle\\
&+\langle F(z^{k})-F(z^{k-1}),\tilde{z}-z^{k}\rangle_{\Xi\Theta}.\end{split}

From (
32
), we know

\displaystyle\left|\left\langle{F(z^{k})-F(z^{k-1})},{z^{k}-\tilde{z}}\right\rangle_{\Xi\Theta}\right|\leq\ \frac{c}{2}\left\|z^{k}-z^{k-1}\right\|_{\Lambda^{-1}}^{2}+\frac{c}{2}\left\|z^{k}-\tilde{z}\right\|_{\Lambda^{-1}}^{2}.

Combining the above two relations, we obtain

\displaystyle\left\|\tilde{z}-z^{k}\right\|_{Q_{c}}^{2}\leq\Delta_{k}(\tilde{z})\leq\left\|\tilde{z}-z^{k}\right\|_{c\Lambda^{-1}+Q_{c}}^{2}+c\left\|z^{k}-z^{k-1}\right\|_{\Lambda^{-1}}^{2},

where we denote

Q_{c}:=\begin{bmatrix}\frac{1-c}{2\tau}I_{n}&\frac{\beta-\mu}{2}A^{\mathrm{T}}\\
\frac{\beta-\mu}{2}A&\frac{1-c}{2\sigma}I_{m}\end{bmatrix}.

(34)

Here
(1-c)\Lambda^{-1}\succeq Q_{c}\succeq 0
due to
P_{c}\succeq 0
. This observation completes the proof.
∎

As a direct result of Lemma
5
, with initialization
x^{-1}=x^{0},y^{-1}=y^{0}=0
, we have the following ergodic convergence result.

Theorem 3.1

For the affine equality constrained problems, suppose that Assumptions
1.1
-
1.4
are satisfied and
0\leq\alpha\leq 1
. If the step sizes
\tau,\sigma
, the penalty factor
\rho
, and the extrapolation coefficients
\alpha,\beta,\mu
are properly chosen so that
P_{c}\succeq 0
, then for
\forall N\geq 1
and
\forall\gamma\geq 0
, we have

\Phi(\bar{x}_{N})-\Phi(x^{*})+\gamma\|A\bar{x}_{N}-b\|\leq\frac{1}{N}\left(\frac{\left\|x^{0}-x^{*}\right\|^{2}}{\tau}+\frac{\gamma^{2}}{\sigma}\right),

(35)

where
\bar{x}_{N}=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{k=1}^{N}x^{k}
. Moreover, it holds that

\displaystyle\left|\Phi(\bar{x}_{N})-\Phi(x^{*})\right|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{4}{N}\left(\frac{\left\|x^{0}-x^{*}\right\|^{2}}{\tau}+\frac{\left\|y^{*}\right\|^{2}}{\sigma}\right),

\displaystyle\left\|A\bar{x}_{N}-b\right\|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{3}{N}\left(\frac{\left\|x^{0}-x^{*}\right\|}{\sqrt{\tau\sigma}}+\frac{\left\|y^{*}\right\|}{\sigma}\right).

Proof

By Lemma
5
, for
k=0,\cdots,N-1
and
y
, it holds that

\begin{split}\Delta_{k}(\tilde{z})-\Delta_{k+1}(\tilde{z})\geq\ &\Phi(x^{k+1})-\Phi(x^{*})-\langle Ax^{k+1}-b,y\rangle.\end{split}

Taking the sum of the above inequality from
0
to
N-1
, we get

\begin{split}\sum_{k=0}^{N-1}\left(\Phi(x^{k+1})-\Phi(x^{*})-\langle Ax^{k+1}-b,y\rangle\right)\leq\Delta_{0}(\tilde{z})-\Delta_{N}(\tilde{z}).\end{split}

(36)

Let
\bar{x}_{N}:=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{k=1}^{N}x^{k}
denote the averaged iterates.
Then, for any
\gamma\geq 0
,
let
\hat{y}=-\frac{\gamma(A\bar{x}_{N}-b)}{\|A\bar{x}_{N}-b\|}

\begin{split}\Phi(\bar{x}_{N})-\Phi(x^{*})+\gamma\|A\bar{x}_{N}-b\|&=\ \Phi(\bar{x}_{N})-\Phi(x^{*})-\left\langle{A\bar{x}_{N}-b},{\hat{y}}\right\rangle\\
\leq\ \frac{1}{N}&\sum_{k=0}^{N-1}\left(\Phi(x^{k+1})-\Phi(x^{*})-\langle A\bar{x}^{k+1}-b,\hat{y}\rangle\right)\\
&\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\leq\ \frac{\Delta_{0}(\hat{z})-\Delta_{N}(\hat{z})}{N}\leq\ \frac{1}{N}\bigg(\frac{\left\|x^{0}-x^{*}\right\|^{2}}{\tau}+\frac{\gamma^{2}}{\sigma}\bigg),\end{split}

(37)

where the last inequality is due to Corollary
1
.
The proof is completed by setting

and applying Lemma

.

CP

3.3

We next consider the general conic affinely constrained problems with

For such problems, we specify the weight

For

\bar{r}_{N}=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{k=0}^{N-1}r^{k}

\hat{y}=-\gamma\mathcal{P}_{+}\left(\bar{r}_{N}\right)/\|\mathcal{P}_{+}\left(\bar{r}_{N}\right)\|
and
\hat{z}=(x^{*},\hat{y})
, we have

\displaystyle\Phi(\bar{x}_{N})-\Phi(x^{*})+\gamma\|\mathcal{P}_{+}\left(\bar{r}_{N}\right)\|

(51)

\displaystyle=\

\displaystyle\Phi(\bar{x}_{N})-\Phi(x^{*})-\left\langle{\bar{r}_{N}},{\hat{y}}\right\rangle

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle(i)}}{{\leq}}\

\displaystyle\frac{1}{N}\sum_{k=0}^{N-1}\left(\Phi(x^{k+1})-\Phi(x^{*})-\langle r^{k+1},\hat{y}\rangle\right)

\displaystyle\leq\

\displaystyle\frac{1}{N}\left(\Delta_{0}(\hat{z})-\Delta_{N}(\hat{z})\right)

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle(ii)}}{{\leq}}\

\displaystyle\frac{1}{N}\left(\frac{\left\|x^{0}-x^{*}\right\|^{2}}{2\tau}+\frac{\gamma^{2}}{2\sigma}+\gamma\left|\mu-\beta\right|\left\|r^{0}\right\|+\frac{\left|\mu-\beta\right|^{2}\sigma}{(a+c)\rho^{2}}\left(\gamma^{2}+\left\|y^{*}\right\|^{2}\right)\right),

where (i) utilizes the convexity of
\Phi
and
r
and (ii) is due to
P_{c}^{\prime}+c\Lambda^{-1}\succ 0
and Lemma
8
.
Since
\bar{r}_{N}=A\bar{x}_{N}-b+\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=0}^{N-1}\mathcal{P}_{-}\left(w^{k}\right)
and
\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=0}^{N-1}\mathcal{P}_{-}\left(w^{k}\right)\in-\mathcal{K}
, using Lemma
11
in appendix, we have

\|\mathcal{P}_{+}\left(\bar{r}_{N}\right)\|\geq\|\mathcal{P}_{+}\left(A\bar{x}_{N}-b\right)\|.

(52)

Combining (
52
) with (
51
) yields

\Phi(\bar{x}_{N})-\Phi(x^{*})+\gamma\|\mathcal{P}_{+}\left(A\bar{x}_{N}-b\right)\|\leq\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{1}{N}\right),

where
\mathcal{O}\left(\cdot\right)
hides constants that depend on
\left\|x^{*}-x^{0}\right\|,\left\|y^{*}\right\|
and the parameters.
The proof is completed by setting some
\gamma>\left\|y^{*}\right\|
and directly applying Lemma
3
.
∎

Next, we substitute the parameters into the step size conditions
P_{c}^{\prime}\succeq 0
and analyze the sufficient conditions on step sizes specifically.

SOGDA
Set
\mu=1,\alpha=0,\beta=1
. For any
\rho>0
,
P_{c}^{\prime}\succeq 0
can be guaranteed if

\sqrt{\sigma\tau}\left\|A\right\|+\frac{\sigma}{\rho}\leq\frac{3}{8},\quad\frac{1}{\tau}\geq 4L_{f}+\rho\left\|A\right\|^{2}\frac{\rho}{\frac{3}{8}\rho-\sigma}.

PDHG
Set
\mu=0,\alpha=0,\beta=0
. For any
\rho>0
,
P_{c}^{\prime}\succeq 0
can be guaranteed if

\sigma<\frac{3}{2}\rho,\quad\frac{1}{\tau}\geq L_{f}+\rho\left\|A\right\|^{2}\frac{\rho}{\frac{3}{2}\rho-\sigma}.

CP
Set
\mu=0,\alpha=0,\beta=1
. For any
\rho>0
,
P_{c}^{\prime}\succeq 0
can be guaranteed if

\frac{1}{\tau}\geq L_{f}+(\rho+\sigma)\left\|A\right\|^{2}.

GDA
Set
\mu=1,\alpha=0,\beta=0
. For any
\rho>0
,
P_{c}^{\prime}\succeq 0
can be guaranteed if

\sigma<\frac{\rho}{4},\quad\frac{1}{\tau}\geq L_{f}+\rho\left\|A\right\|^{2}\frac{\rho-3\sigma}{\rho-4\sigma}.

OGDA
Set
\mu=1,\alpha=1,\beta=1
. For any
\rho>0
,
P_{c}^{\prime}\succeq 0
can be guaranteed if

\max\left\{\tau L_{f_{\rho}},\frac{\sigma}{\rho}\right\}+\sqrt{\sigma\tau}\left\|A\right\|\leq\frac{1}{2}.

Combining the analysis in Section
3.2
, we obtain the complete ergodic convergence of the unified primal-dual framework.
From the results, we can observe that the properly selected penalty enables the convergence of several algorithms.
For example, the PDHG method based on Lagrangian function has no convergence guarantee generally.
However, if we add a penalty term to the Lagrangian function and apply PDHG on the augmented Lagrangian function, the algorithm is guaranteed to converge without further assumptions.
The penalty term makes the convex objective function into a strongly convex function along at least one direction, which brings the benefits of convergence.
The similar benefit from the penalty term is also reflected in GDA and SOGDA.
They converge if and only if the penalty parameter
\rho>0
.

4
The non-ergodic convergence

In the previous section, we have established the sublinear ergodic convergence rate of our primal-dual algorithm framework (
14
). In this section, we establish the non-ergodic convergence for the last iterate of our algorithm. For simplicity, we only consider the affine equality constrained problem where
\mathcal{K}=\{0\}
. Pointwise convergence to a KKT pair of problem (
1
) can be directly obtained from our analysis framework. Furthermore, under the error bound condition (e.g. strong convexity), if we properly choose the algorithmic parameters, then linear convergence to a KKT pair can be achieved. Next, we define the local error bound (LEB) condition of problem (
1
).

Definition 1

For the affine equality constrained problem (
1
) with
\mathcal{K}=\{0\}
, denote
z=[x;y]\in\mathbb{R}^{n+m}
, we define a set-valued operator
T:\mathbb{R}^{n+m}\rightrightarrows\mathbb{R}^{n+m}
as

\displaystyle T:z=[x;y]^{\top}\mapsto[\partial\Phi(x)-A^{\top}y;Ax-b]^{\top}.

Let
\mathcal{Z}^{*}
be the set of all KKT pairs of problem (
1
), we say
T
satisfies (LEB) if for every
z^{*}\in\mathcal{Z}^{*}
, there exists
\epsilon>0,M>0
such that

\displaystyle\mathrm{dist}\left(z,\mathcal{Z}^{*}\right)\leq M\mathrm{dist}\left(T(z),0\right),\qquad\forall z\ \,\textrm{s.t.}\,\mathrm{dist}\left(z,z^{*}\right)\leq\epsilon.

This type of error bound condition is satisfied for a large class of constrained optimization problems. We state a few important examples as follows.

Example 1

Consider the affinely constrained strongly convex problem:

\displaystyle\min_{x}f(x),\quad\,\textrm{s.t.}\,\;Ax=b.

(53)

If
f
is
L_{f}
-smooth and
\mu_{f}
-strongly convex, then (LEB) is satisfied for some
M>0
. Moreover, this result can also be extended to the case where
f
is
\mu_{f}
-strongly convex restricted to the hyperplane
\{x\!:Ax\!=\!b\!\}
.

Example 2

Consider the two-block affinely constrained convex problem:

\min_{x_{1},x_{2}}f(x_{1})\!+\!h(x_{2}),\,\textrm{s.t.}\,A_{1}x_{1}\!+\!A_{2}x_{2}=b.

The operator
T
introduced by Definition
1
agrees with the operator
T_{KKT}
introduced in
yuan2020discerning
. By
(
yuan2020discerning
, Theorem 61)
, (LEB) holds if the following assumptions are satisfied

•

A_{1}
has full row rank,
A_{2}
has full column rank.

•

f(x_{1})=g(Lx_{1})+\left\langle{q},{x_{1}}\right\rangle
with
g
being smooth and strongly convex.

•

h
is either a convex piecewise linear-quadratic function, or a
\ell_{1,q}
-norm regularizer with
q\in[1,2]
, or a sparse-group LASSO regularizer.

In particular, the BP problem and the L1L1 problem considered in the numerical experiments are both included in this class.

More examples can be found in
ye1997necessary
.
Next, let us establish the last-iterate non-ergodic convergence of our method.

Theorem 4.1

For problem (
1
) with
\mathcal{K}=\{0\}
, suppose that Assumptions
1.1
-
1.4
hold and the algorithmic parameters are chosen so that
P_{c}\succ 0
. For the iterate sequence

generated by our algorithm, then there exists

such that
\lim_{k\to\infty}z^{k}=z^{*}
.

Proof

Fix a
z^{*}=(x^{*},y^{*})\in\mathcal{Z}^{*}
, then by Lemma
5
, it holds that

\displaystyle\Delta_{k}(z^{*})-\Delta_{k+1}(z^{*})\geq

\displaystyle\Phi(x^{k+1})-\Phi(x^{*})-\langle Ax^{k+1}-b,y^{*}\rangle+\delta^{k}\geq\delta^{k}

due to the optimality condition
Ax^{*}=b
and
A^{\mathrm{T}}y^{*}\in\partial\Phi(x^{*})
. Note that by Corollary
1
and the analysis of Lemma
5
, we have
\Delta_{k}(z^{*})\geq\|z^{k}-z^{*}\|_{Q_{c}}^{2}
and
\delta_{k}\geq\delta_{2}^{k}=\|u\|_{P_{c}}^{2}
,
u=[Ax^{k+1}-b;z^{k+1}-z^{k}]
, where
Q_{c}
is defined in (
34
). Therefore, because
P_{c}\succ 0
, we know
Q_{c}\succ 0
and there exists
\lambda>0
such that
\delta^{k}_{2}\geq\lambda\left\|z^{k+1}-z^{k}\right\|_{\Lambda^{-1}}^{2}
and
\Delta_{k}(z^{*})\geq\lambda\left\|z^{*}-z^{k}\right\|_{\Lambda^{-1}}^{2}
, for all
k\geq 0
.
Thus the sequence
\{\Delta_{k}(z^{*})\}_{k=0}^{+\infty}
is monotonically decreasing, and hence the sequence
\{z^{k}\}_{k=0}^{+\infty}
is bounded and has a convergent subsequence
\{z^{k_{n}}\}_{n=0}^{+\infty}
. Suppose this subsequence converges to some point
\bar{z}=[\bar{x};\bar{y}]
. Note that

\lambda\sum_{k=0}^{+\infty}\|z^{k+1}-z^{k}\|^{2}_{\Lambda^{-1}}\leq\sum_{k=0}^{+\infty}\delta^{k}\leq\Delta_{0}(z^{*})<+\infty.

Consequently, we have
\lim_{n\to\infty}\left\|z^{k+1}-z^{k}\right\|=0
. Then taking
k=k_{n}
in (
14
) and let
n\to\infty
yields

\displaystyle\bar{x}=\mathbf{prox}_{\tau h}\left[\bar{x}-\tau\nabla_{x}\Psi(\bar{x},\bar{y})\right],\qquad\bar{y}=\mathbf{prox}_{\sigma s}\left[\bar{y}+\eta\nabla_{y}\Psi(\bar{x},\bar{y})\right].

That is,
\bar{z}\in\mathcal{Z}^{*}
. Let us set
z^{*}=\bar{z}
, then
\lim_{n\to\infty}\|z^{k_{n}}-z^{*}\|^{2}_{\Lambda^{-1}}=0
. By Corollary
1
,

\Delta_{k_{n}}(z^{*})\leq\|z^{k_{n}}-z^{*}\|^{2}_{\Lambda^{-1}}+c\|z^{k_{n}}-z^{k_{n}-1}\|_{\Lambda^{-1}}\to 0\quad\mbox{as}\quad n\to\infty.

Finally, due to the fact that the sequence
\{\Delta_{k}(z^{*})\}_{k=0}^{+\infty}
is monotonically decreasing, a subsequence converging to zero also indicates the convergence of the whole sequence to zero. Finally, we conclude that
\|z^{k}\!-\!z^{*}\|_{\Lambda^{-1}}^{2}\!\leq\!\lambda^{-1}\Delta_{k}(z^{*})\!\to\!0
as
k\!\to\!+\infty
.
∎

Next, we provide the analysis of the linear convergence for the scheme (
14
).

Lemma 9

Under the setting of Theorem
4.1
, for
\forall k\geq 0
, there exists
C>0
such that

\displaystyle\rho\left\|Ax^{k+1}\!-\!b\right\|^{2}\!+\!\tau\mathrm{dist}\left(\partial\Phi(x^{k+1})\!-\!A^{\mathrm{T}}y^{k+1},0\right)^{2}\!+\!\left\|z^{k+1}\!-\!z^{k}\right\|_{\Lambda^{-1}}^{2}\!\leq\!C\delta^{k}.

Proof

Because
P_{c}\succ 0
, there exists
\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2}>0
such that
P_{c}\succeq\operatorname{diag}\left(\lambda_{1}\rho,\lambda_{2}\Lambda^{-1}\right)
. Then from the definition of
\delta_{2}^{k}
in Lemma
5
, we have

\displaystyle\delta_{2}^{k}\geq\lambda_{1}\rho\left\|Ax^{k+1}-b\right\|^{2}+\lambda_{2}\left\|z^{k+1}-z^{k}\right\|_{\Lambda^{-1}}^{2}.

It remains to bound
\mathrm{dist}\left(\partial\Phi(x^{k+1})-A^{\mathrm{T}}y^{k+1},0\right)^{2}
by
\delta^{k}
. From (
32
), we have

\displaystyle\delta_{1}^{k}=

\displaystyle\frac{c}{2}\|z^{k+1}\!-\!z^{k}\|^{2}_{\Lambda^{-1}}\!+\!\frac{c}{2}\|z^{k}\!-\!z^{k-1}\|^{2}_{\Lambda^{-1}}\!-\!\left\langle{F(z^{k})\!-\!F(z^{k-1})},{z^{k}\!-\!z^{k+1}}\right\rangle_{\Xi\Theta}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\frac{c}{2}\|z^{k+1}\!-\!z^{k}\|^{2}_{\Lambda^{-1}}\!+\!\frac{c}{2}\|z^{k}\!-\!z^{k-1}\|^{2}_{\Lambda^{-1}}\!-\!\alpha L_{f_{\rho}}\left\|x^{k}\!-\!x^{k-1}\right\|\left\|x^{k+1}\!-\!x^{k}\right\|

\displaystyle-\alpha\left\|A\right\|\left\|y^{k}\!-\!y^{k-1}\right\|\left\|x^{k+1}\!-\!x^{k}\right\|\!-\!\left|\mu\beta\right|\left\|A\right\|\left\|x^{k}\!-\!x^{k-1}\right\|\left\|y^{k+1}\!-\!y^{k}\right\|

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\frac{c}{2}\|z^{k+1}\!-\!z^{k}\|^{2}_{\Lambda^{-1}}\!+\!\frac{c}{2}\|z^{k}\!-\!z^{k-1}\|^{2}_{\Lambda^{-1}}\!-\!\frac{c}{\tau}\left\|x^{k}\!-\!x^{k-1}\right\|\left\|x^{k+1}\!-\!x^{k}\right\|

\displaystyle-\frac{c}{\sqrt{\tau\sigma}}\left\|y^{k}\!-\!y^{k-1}\right\|\left\|x^{k+1}\!-\!x^{k}\right\|\!-\!\frac{c}{\sqrt{\tau\sigma}}\left\|x^{k}\!-\!x^{k-1}\right\|\left\|y^{k+1}\!-\!y^{k}\right\|

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\frac{c}{4\tau}\left\|x^{k}\!-\!x^{k-1}\right\|^{2}\!+\!\frac{c}{4\sigma}\left\|y^{k}\!-\!y^{k-1}\right\|^{2}\!-\!\frac{5c}{2\tau}\left\|x^{k+1}\!-\!x^{k}\right\|^{2}\!-\!\frac{3c}{2\sigma}\left\|y^{k+1}\!-\!y^{k}\right\|^{2}.

Hence, it holds that
c\left\|z^{k}-z^{k-1}\right\|_{\Lambda^{-1}}^{2}\leq 4\delta_{1}^{k}+10c\left\|z^{k+1}-z^{k}\right\|_{\Lambda^{-1}}^{2}
.

Recall the update rule of primal variable in (
14
), we have

x^{k+1}=\mathbf{prox}_{\tau h}\left[x^{k}-\tau\left((1+\alpha)g_{x}^{k}-\alpha g_{x}^{k-1}\right)\right].

(54)

According to optimality condition of (
54
), there exists
v_{x}^{k+1}\in\partial h(x^{k+1})
such that

v_{x}^{k+1}+\frac{1}{\tau}(x^{k+1}-x^{k})+(1+\alpha)g_{x}^{k}-\alpha g_{x}^{k-1}=0.

Rearranging the above equation yields

\displaystyle\|v_{x}^{k+1}+g_{x}^{k+1}\|

\displaystyle=\

\displaystyle\left\|-\frac{1}{\tau}(x^{k+1}-x^{k})+g_{x}^{k+1}-g_{x}^{k}-\alpha(g_{x}^{k}-g_{x}^{k-1})\right\|

\displaystyle\leq\

\displaystyle\!\frac{1}{\tau}\left\|x^{k+1}-x^{k}\right\|+\|g_{x}^{k+1}-g_{x}^{k}\|+\alpha\|g_{x}^{k}-g_{x}^{k-1}\|

\displaystyle\leq\

\displaystyle\!(\frac{1}{\tau}\!+\!L_{f_{\rho}})\!\left\|x^{k+\!1}\!\!-\!x^{k}\right\|\!+\!\left\|A\right\|\!\left\|y^{k+\!1}\!\!-\!y^{k}\right\|\!+\!\alpha L_{\!f_{\!\rho}}\!\left\|x^{k}\!\!-\!x^{k-\!1}\right\|\!+\!\alpha\!\left\|A\right\|\!\left\|y^{k}\!\!-\!y^{k-\!1}\right\|

\displaystyle\leq\

\displaystyle\!(c\!+\!1)\!\left(\frac{\left\|x^{k+1}\!-\!x^{k}\right\|}{\tau}\!+\!\frac{\left\|y^{k+1}\!-\!y^{k}\right\|}{\sqrt{\sigma\tau}}\right)\!+\!c\left(\frac{\left\|x^{k}\!-\!x^{k-1}\right\|}{\tau}\!+\!\frac{\left\|y^{k}\!-\!y^{k-1}\right\|}{\sqrt{\sigma\tau}}\right)

where the last inequality is due to the definition of
c
.
Therefore, we have

\displaystyle\tau\mathrm{dist}\left(\partial h(x^{k+1})+\nabla f(x^{k+1})-A^{\mathrm{T}}y^{k+1},0\right)^{2}

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle(i)}}{{\leq}}\

\displaystyle\tau\|v_{x}^{k+1}+\nabla f(x^{k+1})-A^{\mathrm{T}}y^{k+1}\|^{2}

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle(ii)}}{{\leq}}\,

\displaystyle 4(c+1)^{2}\left\|z^{k+1}-z^{k}\right\|_{\Lambda^{-1}}^{2}+4c^{2}\left\|z^{k}-z^{k-1}\right\|_{\Lambda^{-1}}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\

\displaystyle 4c\delta_{1}^{k}+(10c^{2}+4(c+1)^{2})\left\|z^{k+1}-z^{k}\right\|_{\Lambda^{-1}}^{2}

where (i) is because
v_{x}^{k+1}\in\partial h(x^{k+1})
, (ii) is due to Cauchy inequality.
Then it suffices to take
C=\max\left\{\frac{1}{\lambda_{1}},4c,\frac{10c^{2}+4(c+1)^{2}+1}{\lambda_{2}}\right\}
.
∎

Suppose Assumptions

Assume that the sequence

,
\epsilon_{1}

(55)

Theorem 4.3

Suppose the problem has the form (
53
) and Assumption
1.3
holds. Let the step sizes of (

) be suitably chosen as
\tau=\frac{c_{\tau}}{L_{f}},\sigma=c_{\sigma}\frac{L_{f}}{\left\|A\right\|^{2}},\rho=c_{\rho}\frac{L_{f}}{\left\|A\right\|^{2}}
, where the constants
c_{\tau},c_{\sigma},c_{\rho}
only depend on
\alpha,\beta,\mu
. Then there exists constant
c_{\kappa}
such that for all

,

\displaystyle\left\|x^{k}-x^{*}\right\|\leq\mathcal{O}\left(\exp(-c_{\kappa}\left(\kappa_{f}+\kappa_{A}^{2}\right)k)\right),

where
\mathcal{O}\left(\cdot\right)
hides constants that depend on
x^{0},y^{0}
only.

Proof

We can simply choose
c_{\tau},c_{\sigma},c_{\rho}
such that the constant
C
defined in Lemma

are of constant order. For any fixed
z^{*}=(x^{*},y^{*})\in\mathcal{Z}^{*}
, it holds that

\displaystyle f(x)-f(x^{*})-\left\langle{Ax-b},{y^{*}}\right\rangle

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle(i)}}{{=}}

\displaystyle f(x)-f(x^{*})-\left\langle{\nabla f(x^{*})},{x-x^{*}}\right\rangle

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle(ii)}}{{\geq}}

\displaystyle\max\left\{\mu_{f}\left\|x-x^{*}\right\|^{2},\frac{1}{2L_{f}}\left\|\nabla f(x)-\nabla f(x^{*})\right\|^{2}\right\},

where (i) utilizes the KKT conditions
Ax^{*}=b
and
\nabla f(x^{*})=A^{\mathrm{T}}y^{*}
, (ii) is due to the assumption that

is

-smooth and
\mu_{f}
-strongly convex.
For simplicity, we denote
\delta_{0}^{k}:=f(x^{k+1})-f(x^{*})-\left\langle{Ax^{k+1}-b},{y^{*}}\right\rangle

\displaystyle\left\|x^{k+1}-x^{*}\right\|\leq\sqrt{\frac{\delta_{0}^{k}}{\mu_{f}}},\qquad\left\|\nabla f(x^{k+1})-\nabla f(x^{*})\right\|\leq\sqrt{2L_{f}\delta_{0}^{k}}.

By Lemma

\left\|\nabla f(x^{k+1})-A^{\top}y^{k+1}\right\|\leq\sqrt{\frac{C\delta^{k}}{\tau}}
. Recall the definition of

in (

\lambda_{0}\!>\!0
is the minimum nonzero singular value of

\displaystyle\lambda_{0}\mathrm{dist}\left(y^{k+1},\mathcal{Y}^{*}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\left\|A^{\top}y^{k+1}-\nabla f(x^{*})\right\|

\displaystyle\leq\left\|A^{\top}y^{k+1}-\nabla f(x^{k+1})\right\|+\left\|\nabla f(x^{k+1})-\nabla f(x^{*})\right\|

\displaystyle\leq\sqrt{\frac{C\delta^{k}}{\tau}}+\sqrt{2L_{f}\delta_{0}^{k}}.

Hence

\displaystyle\mathrm{dist}_{\Lambda^{-1}}\left(z^{k+1},\mathcal{Z}^{*}\right)^{2}\leq\frac{\delta_{0}^{k}}{\tau\mu_{f}}+\frac{3}{\lambda_{0}^{2}\sigma}\left(\frac{C\delta^{k}}{\tau}+L_{f}\delta_{0}^{k}\right).

by Lemma

\displaystyle M=\max\left\{\frac{3C}{\lambda_{0}^{2}\sigma\tau}+cC,\frac{1}{\tau\mu_{f}}+\frac{3L_{f}}{\lambda_{0}^{2}\sigma}\right\}

and any

\displaystyle\Delta_{k}(\tilde{z})-\Delta_{k+1}(\tilde{z})\geq

\displaystyle\delta_{0}^{k}+\delta^{k}

\displaystyle{\geq}

\displaystyle\frac{1}{M}\left(\mathrm{dist}_{\Lambda^{-1}}\left(z^{k+1},\mathcal{Z}^{*}\right)^{2}+c\left\|z^{k+1}-z^{k}\right\|_{\Lambda^{-1}}^{2}\right)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{M}\inf_{z\in\mathcal{Z}^{*}}\Delta_{k+1}(z).

Then we have
\Delta^{*}_{k+1}\leq\frac{M}{M+1}\Delta^{*}_{k},

which yields a convergence rate of

clearly.

5
A byproduct: proximal OGDA for nonsmooth saddle point problem

Note that, our analysis framework can be extended beyond bilinear problem or augmented Lagrangian function. As a byproduct of our analysis, we derive the convergence result of the proximal OGDA algorithm, which is a generalization of well-known OGDA algorithm for smooth problem
wei2020linearOGDA
;
mokhtari2020unifiedOGDA
.
Consider the problem

\min_{x}\max_{y}\ \mathcal{L}(x,y):=h(x)+\Psi(x,y)-s(y).

(57)

We propose the proximal OGDA algorithm as

\displaystyle\begin{split}x^{k+1}&=\mathbf{prox}_{\tau h}\left[x^{k}-\tau\left(2g_{x}^{k}-g_{x}^{k-1}\right)\right],\\
y^{k+1}&=\mathbf{prox}_{\sigma s}\left[y^{k}+\sigma\left(2g_{y}^{k}-g_{y}^{k-1}\right)\right].\end{split}

(58)

Especially, when
h(\cdot)=\mathbb{I}_{\mathcal{X}}(\cdot)
and
s(\cdot)=\mathbb{I}_{\mathcal{Y}}(\cdot)
, we recover the OGDA on smooth minimax problem with closed convex domain.
The convergence analysis of the proximal OGDA (
58
) is almost a direct implication of our analysis for (
14
), but much simpler. Consider the potential function

\displaystyle\Delta_{k}(z)=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\|z^{k}-z\|^{2}_{\Lambda^{-1}}+\frac{1}{4}\|z^{k}-z^{k-1}\|^{2}_{\Lambda^{-1}}+\langle F(z^{k})-F(z^{k-1}),z-z^{k}\rangle.

We assume that the gradient
\nabla_{x}\Psi(x,y)
is
L_{xx}
-Lipschitz continuous with respect to
x
and
L_{xy}
-Lipschitz continuous with respect to
y
,
the gradient
\nabla_{y}\Psi(x,y)
is
L_{yx}
-Lipschitz continuous with respect to
x
and
L_{yy}
-Lipschitz continuous with respect to
y
, i.e.,

\displaystyle\left\|\nabla_{x}\Psi(x,y)-\nabla_{x}\Psi(x^{\prime},y)\right\|

\displaystyle\leq L_{xx}\left\|x-x^{\prime}\right\|,\ \forall x,x^{\prime}\in\mathcal{X},\forall y\in\mathcal{Y},

\displaystyle\left\|\nabla_{x}\Psi(x,y)-\nabla_{x}\Psi(x,y^{\prime})\right\|

\displaystyle\leq L_{xy}\left\|y-y^{\prime}\right\|,\ \forall x\in\mathcal{X},\forall y,y^{\prime}\in\mathcal{Y},

\displaystyle\left\|\nabla_{y}\Psi(x,y)-\nabla_{y}\Psi(x^{\prime},y)\right\|

\displaystyle\leq L_{yx}\left\|x-x^{\prime}\right\|,\ \forall x,x^{\prime}\in\mathcal{X},\forall y\in\mathcal{Y},

\displaystyle\left\|\nabla_{y}\Psi(x,y)-\nabla_{y}\Psi(x,y^{\prime})\right\|

\displaystyle\leq L_{yy}\left\|y-y^{\prime}\right\|,\ \forall x\in\mathcal{X},\forall y,y^{\prime}\in\mathcal{Y}.

Theorem 5.1

The sequence
\{z^{n}\}_{n=0}^{+\infty}
is generated by (
58
) with the step sizes satisfying
\tau\leq\frac{1}{2L_{xx}},\sigma\leq\frac{1}{2L_{yy}}
and
\big(\frac{1}{\tau}-2L_{xx}\big)\big(\frac{1}{\sigma}-2L_{yy}\big)>4\max\{L_{xy},L_{yx}\}^{2}
.
Then the sequence
\{z^{n}\}_{n=0}^{+\infty}
converges to a saddle point of problem (
57
). Furthermore, let
(\bar{x}_{N},\bar{y}_{N})=\left(\frac{1}{N}\sum_{k=1}^{N}x^{k},\frac{1}{N}\sum_{k=1}^{N}y^{k}\right)
, then for any
R_{x},R_{y}>0
, it holds that

\displaystyle\max_{y\in\mathcal{Y}\cap\mathbb{B}(y_{0},R_{y})}\mathcal{L}(\overline{x}_{N},y)-\min_{x\in\mathcal{X}\cap\mathbb{B}(x_{0},R_{x})}\mathcal{L}(x,\overline{y}_{N})\leq\frac{1}{N}\left(\frac{R_{x}^{2}}{\tau}+\frac{R_{y}^{2}}{\sigma}\right).

Proof

By Lemma
4
, it holds that for any
z\in\mathcal{Z}=\mathcal{X}\times\mathcal{Y}
that

\displaystyle\Delta_{k}(z)-\Delta_{k+1}(z)\geq R(z^{k+1})-R(z)+\left\langle F(z^{k+1}),z^{k+1}-z\right\rangle

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad+\frac{1}{4}\left\|z^{k+1}\!-\!z^{k}\right\|^{2}_{\Lambda^{-1}}\!+\!\frac{1}{4}\left\|z^{k}\!-\!z^{k-1}\right\|^{2}_{\Lambda^{-1}}\!-\!\left\langle{F(z^{k})\!-\!F(z^{k-1})},{z^{k}\!-\!z^{k+1}}\right\rangle.

By definition, we have

\displaystyle\left\langle{F(z^{k})-F(z^{k-1})},{z^{k}-z^{k+1}}\right\rangle

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left\|x^{k}-x^{k+1}\right\|\left\|\nabla_{x}\Psi(z^{k})-\nabla_{x}\Psi(z^{k-1})\right\|+\left\|y^{k}-y^{k+1}\right\|\left\|\nabla_{y}\Psi(z^{k})-\nabla_{y}\Psi(z^{k-1})\right\|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle L_{xx}\left\|x^{k}-x^{k+1}\right\|\left\|x^{k}-x^{k-1}\right\|+L_{yy}\left\|y^{k}-y^{k+1}\right\|\left\|y^{k}-y^{k-1}\right\|

\displaystyle+L_{xy}\left\|x^{k}-x^{k+1}\right\|\left\|y^{k}-y^{k-1}\right\|+L_{yx}\left\|y^{k}-y^{k+1}\right\|\left\|x^{k}-x^{k-1}\right\|.

Then by
\left(\frac{1}{2\tau}-L_{xx}\right)\left(\frac{1}{2\sigma}-L_{yy}\right)\geq\max\{L_{xy},L_{yx}\}^{2}
, it follows that

\displaystyle\delta^{k}:=\frac{1}{4}\left\|z^{k+1}-z^{k}\right\|^{2}_{\Lambda^{-1}}+\frac{1}{4}\left\|z^{k}-z^{k-1}\right\|^{2}_{\Lambda^{-1}}-\left\langle{F(z^{k})-F(z^{k-1})},{z^{k}-z^{k+1}}\right\rangle\geq 0.

Similarly, we have
\Delta_{k}(z)\geq\frac{1}{4}\left\|z^{k}-z\right\|_{\Lambda^{-1}}^{2}
. Hence, for any
z\in\mathcal{Z}=\mathcal{X}\times\mathcal{Y}
,

\displaystyle R(z^{k+1})-R(z)+\left\langle F(z^{k+1}),z^{k+1}-z\right\rangle\leq\Delta_{k}(z)-\Delta_{k+1}(z).

The desired inequality follows directly by taking average.

Furthermore, when
\left(\frac{1}{2\tau}-L_{xx}\right)\left(\frac{1}{2\sigma}-L_{yy}\right)>\max\{L_{xy},L_{yx}\}^{2}
, there actually exists a
\gamma>0
such that for all
k\geq 0
,
\delta^{k}\geq\gamma\left\|z^{k+1}-z^{k}\right\|^{2}_{\Lambda^{-1}}
, and hence for any saddle point
z^{*}
,

\displaystyle\Delta_{k}(z^{*})-\Delta_{k+1}(z^{*})

\displaystyle\geq\delta_{0}^{k}+R(z^{k+1})-R(z^{*})+\left\langle F(z^{k+1}),z^{k+1}-z^{*}\right\rangle

\displaystyle\geq\gamma\left\|z^{k+1}-z^{k}\right\|^{2}_{\Lambda^{-1}}.

Then we see
\left(\Delta_{k}(z^{*})\right)
is monotonically decreasing, and
\lim_{k}\left\|z^{k+1}-z^{k}\right\|=0
. By the fact
\Delta_{k}(z^{*})\geq\frac{1}{4}\left\|z^{k}-z^{*}\right\|_{\Lambda^{-1}}^{2}
,
(z^{n})
has a subsequence
(z^{k_{n}})
which converges to a
z^{\star}\in\mathcal{Z}
. Then in

\displaystyle z^{k+1}=\mathbf{prox}_{\Lambda R}\left(z^{k}-2\Lambda F(z^{k})+\Lambda F(z^{k-1})\right),

we can plug in
k=k_{n}
and let
n\to\infty
, by continuity it holds
z^{\star}=\mathbf{prox}_{\Lambda R}\left(z^{\star}-\Lambda F(z^{\star})\right)
. Therefore,
z^{\star}\in\mathcal{Z}^{*}
and hence the above argument shows that
\left(\Delta_{k}(z^{\star})\right)
is also monotonically decreasing. Thus it must tend to 0, and
\lim_{k}z^{k}=z^{\star}
.

7
Conclusions

In this paper, we focus on the primal-dual methods for the conic constrained problems.
Several popular algorithms are unified into the proposed framework and
their
\mathcal{O}(1/N)
ergodic convergence and linear convergence are analyzed under the entire framework.
In contrast to the existing theoretical results,
we utilize the suboptimality and primal infeasibility to measure the optimality of the iterates, instead of the duality gap which is invalid without the boundedness assumption on the dual variable.
In addition, both the theory and experiments show that the penalty term added to the Lagrangian saddle point problem is helpful for convergence.
When compared to approximate dual ascent method such as ADMM, the primal-dual methods are easy to implement and enjoy better convergence guarantees, especially in the multi-block cases.
Preliminary numerical experiments verify the above points.

Theorem 7.1
(Moreau’s decomposition theorem
moreau1962decomposition
)

Let
\mathcal{K}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a closed convex cone and
\mathcal{K}^{\circ}
be its polar cone. For
x,y,z\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, the following statements are equivalent:

•

z=x+y,x\in\mathcal{K},y\in\mathcal{K}^{\circ}
and
\langle x,y\rangle=0
,

•

x=\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{K}}(z)
and
y=\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{K}^{\circ}}(z)
.

The proof of basic lemmas is presented as follows. It is worth noting that when
\mathcal{K}=\mathbb{R}^{n}_{\leq 0}
, we have
\mathcal{P}_{+}\left(\cdot\right)=\left[\cdot\right]_{+}
being the standard entry-wise positive part, and
\mathcal{P}_{-}\left(\cdot\right)=\left[\cdot\right]_{-}
also.

Proof
(Proof of Lemma
6
)

By the definition of
\mathcal{P}_{+}\left(\cdot\right)
and
\mathcal{P}_{-}\left(\cdot\right)
, we have

\displaystyle\left\|w-w^{\prime}\right\|^{2}=

\displaystyle\left\|\mathcal{P}_{+}\left(w\right)-\mathcal{P}_{+}\left(w^{\prime}\right)-\mathcal{P}_{-}\left(w\right)+\mathcal{P}_{-}\left(w^{\prime}\right)\right\|^{2}

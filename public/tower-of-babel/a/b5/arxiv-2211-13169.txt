1.
Introduction

An interval exchange transformation of
[0,1)
is a bijection
f:[0,1)\to[0,1)
defined by a finite partition of the unit interval into half-open intervals (i.e.
[b_{i},b_{i+1})
) and rearrangement of these intervals.
More precisely, there exists a permutation
\sigma:\{1,...,n\}\to\{1,...,n\}
and
b_{i}\in[0,1)
, with
b_{1}=0,\,b_{n+1}=1
and
b_{i}<b_{j}
if
i<j
, such that
f(x)=x-b_{j}+\displaystyle\sum_{\sigma(i)<\sigma(j)}(b_{i+1}-b_{i})
if
x\in[b_{j},b_{j+1})
.
Let
IET
be the group of all interval exchange transformations of
[0,1)
.

The dynamic of an interval exchange transformation has been studied since the 70’s. An important result is that almost every interval exchange transformation is uniquely ergodic, proved independently by Masur
[
7
]
and Veech
[
13
]
.

Later
IET
has been studied as a group. For example, in
[
11
]
, Novak proved that
IET
does not contain distortion elements.

An important problem about
IET
is the following question formulated by Katok. Does
IET
contain a non abelian free group?

In
[
4
]
, Dahamani, Fujiwara and Guirardel proved that a group generated by a generic pair of elements of
IET
is not a non abelian free group. Also they proved that a connected Lie group can be embedded in
IET
if and only if it is abelian.
In
[
5
]
these authors worked in solvable groups of
IET
, and proved that some groups cannot be embedded in
IET
.

The group
IET
has canonical inclusions into other groups, like interval exchange transformations with flips (FIET), affine interval exchange transformations (AIET) and permutation of
\mathbb{S}^{1}
that are continuous outside a finite subset (
\widehat{\text{PC}^{\bowtie}}(\mathbb{S}^{1})
).

These groups have been studied in several works.

Unlike the case of
IET
, for FIET that reverse orientation in at least one interval Nogueira proved that almost all have periodic points, in particular they are non-ergodic
[
10
]
.

The group FIET is simple, this is an unpublished result of Arnoux
[
1
]
.
Later, Guelman and Liousse calculated an uniform bound for the commutator length
[
6
]
.

In
[
2
]
, Boulanger, Fougeron and Ghazouani, study the dynamics of a 1-parameter family in
AIET
in which the dynamics is trivial in a full measure set but not on a Cantor set.

Some question are simpler in
AIET
than in
IET
, for example the Katok question has an affirmative answer in
AIET
.
More precisely, via a ping-pong argument, it can be built an explicit example of a non abelian free group in
AIET

[
9
]
.

Let (
\mathcal{G}_{fin}(\mathbb{S}^{1})
) be the group of permutation of
\mathbb{S}^{1}
with finite support. It is a normal subgroup in
\widehat{\text{PC}^{\bowtie}}(\mathbb{S}^{1})
.

In
[
3
]
Cornulier studied when a subgroup of
\widehat{\text{PC}^{\bowtie}}(\mathbb{S}^{1})/\mathcal{G}_{fin}(\mathbb{S}^{1})
can be lifted to
\widehat{\text{PC}^{\bowtie}}(\mathbb{S}^{1})
.
Examples of groups that can be lifted are the subgroups of
\text{PC}_{+}(\mathbb{S}^{1})
, the group of piecewise continuous bijection of
\mathbb{S}^{1}
, that are piecewise orientation-preserving.
In this article we work with a subgroup of
\text{PC}_{+}(\mathbb{S}^{1})
.

Definition 1.1
.

A bijection
f:\mathbb{S}^{1}\to\mathbb{S}^{1}
is called a right piecewise absolutely continuous if

(1)

is right-continuous,

(2)

the set of discontinuity is finite,

(3)

if
f
is continuous in
[a,b)
then
f
is absolutely continuous in
[a,b)
.

(4)

if
f
is continuous in
[a,b)
then
[f(a),f(b))=f([a,b))
.

We denote by
\mathcal{PAC}_{+}(\mathbb{S}^{1})
the group of all bijections
f:\mathbb{S}^{1}\to\mathbb{S}^{1}
such that
f
and
f^{-1}
are right piecewise absolutely continuous.

The definition of intervals in
\mathbb{S}^{1}
is introduced in Notation
2.2
.
For precise definition of right-continuous and absolutely continuous see Definition
2.5
.

If we exclude the item
1
of this definition, we obtain another group denoted by
\widehat{\mathcal{PAC}}_{+}(\mathbb{S}^{1})
, (following the notation in
[
3
]
).
This group contains the normal subgroup of finitely supported permutation in
\mathbb{S}^{1}
denoted by (
\mathcal{G}_{fin}(\mathbb{S}^{1})
).

These three groups are related by
\mathcal{PAC}_{+}(\mathbb{S}^{1})\simeq(\widehat{\mathcal{PAC}}_{+}(\mathbb{S}^{1})/\mathcal{G}_{fin}(\mathbb{S}^{1}))
. In other words, the condition 1 implies the only function with finite support in
\mathcal{PAC}_{+}(\mathbb{S}^{1})
is the identity function.

In the case that
f
is continuous, property 4 is equivalent to orientation preserving. Also, property 4 of this definition implies that
f
preserve orientation on intervals of continuity.
In particular if
f
is continuous in
[a,b]
then
f^{-1}
is continuous in
[f(a),f(b)]
.

The group
\mathcal{PAC}_{+}(\mathbb{S}^{1})
verifies
\mathcal{PAC}_{+}(\mathbb{S}^{1})=\langle\textnormal{IET}(\mathbb{S}^{1}),\mathcal{AC}_{+}(\mathbb{S}^{1})\rangle
, in a similar way that AIET verifies
\textnormal{AIET}=\langle\textnormal{IET},\textnormal{PL}_{+}([0,1))\rangle
.

We focus on the work of Novak in
[
12
]
. The main result of this article is a classification theorem: given a natural unrestricted topology in
IET
, any continuous homomorphism
\tau:\mathbb{R}\to\textnormal{IET}
is conjugated to a standard torus action in one parameter.

Definition 1.2
.

Let
\lambda=(\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2},...,\lambda_{n})\in(0,1)^{n}
with
\sum_{i}\lambda_{i}=1
. Let
\beta_{i}
the sum
\beta_{i}=\sum_{j\leq i}\lambda_{j}
, in particular
\beta_{0}=0
and
\beta_{n}=1
. The standard torus action associated to
\lambda
is the function
\tau_{\lambda}:\mathbb{T}^{n}\to\textnormal{IET}
define by

[\tau_{\lambda}(\alpha)](x)=\left\{\begin{matrix}x+\lambda_{j}\alpha_{j}&\mbox{if }x\in[\beta_{j-1},\beta_{j}-\lambda_{j}\alpha_{j})\\
x+\lambda_{j}\alpha_{j}-\beta_{j}&\mbox{if }x\in[\beta_{j}-\lambda_{j}\alpha_{j},\beta_{j})\end{matrix}\right.

An one parameter standard torus action is a map
\theta:\mathbb{R}\to\textnormal{IET}
such that
\theta(t)=\tau_{\lambda}(t\alpha)
for some
\alpha,\lambda
in the previous conditions.

In other words the standard torus action is a rotation in each interval
[\beta_{j},\beta_{j+1})
of angle
\alpha_{j}
(see figure 1).

Figure 1.
Standard torus action (in this case
\alpha_{1}=\frac{1}{3}
,
\alpha_{2}=0
and
\alpha_{3}=\frac{1}{2}
)

To study continuity in our context we will provide a distance in
\mathcal{PAC}_{+}(\mathbb{S}^{1})
.

Definition 1.3
.

We define
\tilde{d}_{1},d:\mathcal{PAC}_{+}(\mathbb{S}^{1})\times\mathcal{PAC}_{+}(\mathbb{S}^{1})\to\mathbb{R}^{+}\cup\{0\}
by

\tilde{d}_{1}(f,g)=\int_{\mathbb{S}^{1}}\mathfrak{d}(f(x),g(x))d\mu,\hskip 14.22636ptd(f,g)=\tilde{d}_{1}(f,g)+\tilde{d}_{1}(f^{-1},g^{-1})

where
\mathfrak{d}
is the distance in
\mathbb{S}^{1}
, and
\mu
the measure generated by
\mathfrak{d}
(Notation
2.1
).

We study the distance
d
in the section 3. In particular we will prove the following result.

Proposition 1.4
.

The group
(\mathcal{PAC}_{+}(\mathbb{S}^{1}),d)
is a topological group.

Note that the subgroup
\textnormal{AIET}(\mathbb{S}^{1})=\langle\textnormal{IET}(\mathbb{S}^{1}),\textnormal{PL}_{+}(\mathbb{S}^{1})\rangle<\mathcal{PAC}_{+}(\mathbb{S}^{1})
verifies that
(\textnormal{AIET}(\mathbb{S}^{1}),d)
is also a topological group.

The topology induced in
IET
by
d
, and
\tilde{d}_{1}
verifies the hypothesis of Novak’s theorem.

The main result of
[
12
]
is not valid in
(\mathcal{PAC}_{+}(\mathbb{S}^{1}),d)
, for example via differential flow we can construct
\rho:\mathbb{R}\to\mathcal{PAC}_{+}(\mathbb{S}^{1})
with a finite numbers of fix points (Example
6.1
).

Also this result is not valid in
(\textnormal{AIET}(\mathbb{S}^{1}),d)
. More precisely the existence of exotic circles in
\textnormal{PL}_{+}(S^{1})
(
[
8
]
) implies the existence of continuous homomorphisms
\rho:\mathbb{R}\to(\textnormal{AIET}(\mathbb{S}^{1}),d)
such that are not conjugated to a standard torus action.

But we can adapt this result to our context. This theorem can be interpreted in terms of domains. We will use the notation of
[
4
]
to work in domains other than [0,1).

Definition 1.5
.

A domain
\mathcal{D}
is a disjoint finite union of circles
\mathbb{R}/l\mathbb{Z}
and semi-open bounded intervals
[\alpha,\beta)
.
Each component of
\mathcal{D}
has a natural metric and orientation.

We consider any domain
\mathcal{D}
with an order in his components and denote by
\mathcal{I}_{j}
the canonical inclusion of
j
-th component in
\mathcal{D}
.

An interval exchange transformation
h:\mathcal{D}\to\mathcal{D}^{\prime}
between two domains
\mathcal{D},\,\mathcal{D}^{\prime}
is a bijection function which is piecewise isometric, orientation-preserving, right continuous and with only finitely many discontinuity points.
The set of all interval exchange transformation between
\mathcal{D},\,\mathcal{D}^{\prime}
is denote by
\textnormal{IET}(\mathcal{D},\mathcal{D}^{\prime})
. Also we denote
\textnormal{IET}(\mathcal{D})=\textnormal{IET}(\mathcal{D},\mathcal{D})
.

Note that by the previous definition
\textnormal{IET}([0,1))=\textnormal{IET}
.

In this article we work with domains whose components are circles.

The result of
[
12
]
can be viewed as: for any continuous homomorphism
\tau:\mathbb{R}\to\textnormal{IET}
there exist a domain
\mathcal{D}
and
f\in\textnormal{IET}(\mathbb{S}^{1},\mathcal{D})
such that
f\circ\tau(t)\circ f^{-1}\in\textnormal{IET}(\mathcal{D})
is continuous for any
t\in\mathbb{R}
.

We adapt this result to the group
(\mathcal{PAC}_{+}(\mathbb{S}^{1}),d)
. In particular this show that this property does not need the piecewise isometric condition.

Theorem 1.6
.

Let
\rho:\mathbb{R}\to\mathcal{PAC}_{+}(\mathbb{S}^{1})
a continuous homomorphism. Then there exist a domain
\mathcal{D}
and an interval exchange transformation
f\in\textnormal{IET}(\mathbb{S}^{1},\mathcal{D})
such that the map
\hat{\rho}:\mathbb{R}\to\mathcal{P}\mathcal{AC}_{+}(\mathcal{D})
defined by
\hat{\rho}(t)=f\circ\rho(t)\circ f^{-1}
is a continuous homomorphism and verifies
\hat{\rho}(t)
is continuous for any
t\in\mathbb{R}
.

We prove this theorem in section 5,

In the section 6 we exhibit several examples to analyze the hypotheses of the main theorem.

Notation 2.2
.

Let

with
a\neq b
, we denote by
[a,b]=\pi_{l}([\hat{a},\hat{b}])

\pi_{l}(\hat{a})=a,\ \pi_{l}(\hat{b})=b

The measure of intervals and the distance of their endpoints are related in the obvious way.

Remark 2.3

Let

\mathfrak{d}(a,b)=\mu([a,b])

\mathfrak{d}(a,b)=\mu([b,a])

Also, if

\mathfrak{d}(a,b)=\mu([a,b])

Also, we define the left half-interval of

as

[a,b]_{-}=\pi\left(\left[\hat{a},\frac{\hat{a}+\hat{b}}{2}\right]\right)
.

We will use
(a,b)_{+}
,
(a,b)_{-}
,
[a,b)_{+}
,
[a,b)_{-}
,
(a,b]_{-}
and
(a,b]_{+}

.

Let

(1)

\hat{a}
. Also we say

(2)

The function

\hat{c}

Since

Notation 2.6
.

Let

and

Since any map

Definition 2.9

Let

k

\mathbb{S}^{1}

Remark 2.10
.

Let
f\in\mathcal{PAC}_{+}(\mathbb{S}^{1})
.

(1)

If
(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3})
is an ordered
3

f
is continuous on

(2)

Applying the previous item to

is not continuous in

(3)

In summary

is continuous in

Remark 2.13

3.
\mathcal{PAC}_{+}(\mathbb{S}^{1})
is a topological group

In this section we prove that
(\mathcal{PAC}_{+}(\mathbb{S}^{1}),d)
is a topological group (Proposition
1.4
), and we study basic topological properties of it.

In
[
12
]
, Novak considered all topological group structures that satisfy some properties.
These topologies verify the following naive statement. For
f,g\in\textnormal{IET}
, if
\sharp f=\sharp g
,
BP_{0}(f)
is close to
BP_{0}(g)
and
\sigma_{f}=\sigma_{g}
(i.e. has the same rearrangement permutation) then
f
is close to
g
. Since the elements of
IET
are piecewise isometric, this property is closed by inverse. But this is not true in
\mathcal{PAC}_{+}(\mathbb{S}^{1})
.

The distance
\tilde{d}_{1}
(Definition
1.3
) restricted to
IET
(distance
\underline{d}
defined in the Proposition 2.2 of
[
12
]
), verifies this property. However
\tilde{d}_{1}
is not a good distance in
\mathcal{PAC}_{+}(\mathbb{S}^{1})
.

The following example shows that
(\mathcal{PAC}_{+}(\mathbb{S}^{1}),\tilde{d}_{1})
is not a topological group. Also the restriction to
\textnormal{AIET}(\mathbb{S}^{1})
is not a topological group.

Example 3.1
.

For
n\in\mathbb{N}
let denote
a_{n}(k)=\frac{1}{4}+\frac{k}{4n}
and
b_{n}(k)=1-\frac{1}{4n}+\frac{k}{4n^{2}}
.
Note that
a_{n}(k+1)-a_{n}(k)=\frac{1}{4n}
,
\cup_{k<n}[a_{n}(k),a_{n}(k+1))=[\frac{1}{4},\frac{1}{2})
,
b_{n}(k+1)-b_{n}(k)=\frac{1}{4n^{2}}
and
\cup_{k<n}[b_{n}(k),b_{n}(k+1))=[1-\frac{1}{4n},1)

Let
f_{n}\in\textnormal{AIET}(\mathbb{S}^{1})
a sequence defined by

f_{n}(x)=\left\{\begin{matrix}x&\mbox{ if }x\in\left[0,\frac{1}{4}\right)\\
\frac{1}{2}(x-a_{n}(k))+a_{n}(k)&\mbox{ if }x\in[a_{n}(k),a_{n}(k+1))\mbox{ with }k<n\\
\frac{2n}{2n-1}(x-\frac{1}{2})+\frac{1}{2}&\mbox{ if }x\in\left[\frac{1}{2},1-\frac{1}{4n}\right)\\
\frac{n}{2}(x-b_{n}(k))+a_{n}(k)+\frac{1}{8n}&\mbox{ if }x\in[b_{n}(k),b_{n}(k+1))\mbox{ with }k<n\end{matrix}\right.

Figure 4
.
Representation of maps
f_{n}
of Example 3.1.

It is easy to check that
\mathfrak{d}(f_{n}(x),x)\leq\frac{1}{4n},\ \forall x<1-\frac{1}{4n}
, then
\tilde{d}_{1}(f_{n},id)\leq\frac{1}{2n}
, in particular
\tilde{d}_{1}(f_{n},id)\to 0
.

On the other hand,
\mathfrak{d}(f_{n}^{-1}(x),x)\geq\frac{1}{4}
for all

, since
\mu(f_{n}([1-\frac{1}{4n},1))=\frac{1}{8}
so
\tilde{d}_{1}(f_{n}^{-1},id)\geq\frac{1}{24}

This example shows that
(\textnormal{AIET}(\mathbb{S}^{1}),\tilde{d}_{1})
is not topological group.
The reason is that small intervals can be transformed in uniformly big ones.
This fact does not happen in
IET
because its elements are piecewise isometric.

Proposition 3.2
.

Let
\delta>0
. Then there exists

such that

Proof.

Let
I
be an interval, and

.

Let
\delta_{0}=\mu(I),

\delta_{1}=\mu(g(I))
and
\tilde{\delta}=|\delta_{1}-\delta_{0}|

\delta_{1}>\delta_{0}
and
\delta_{1}<\delta_{0}

Case 1:

Let

Also

Geometric characterizations of Lipschitz stability for convex optimization problems
TRAN T. A. NGHIA

Abstract




Abstract.
In this paper, we mainly study tilt stability and Lipschitz stability of convex optimization problems. Our characterizations are geometric and fully computable in many important cases. As a result, we apply our theory to the group Lasso problem and the nuclear norm minimization problem and reveal that the Lipschitz stability of the solution mapping in these problems is automatic whenever the solution mapping is single-valued.

Keywords.
Lipschitz stability, tilt stability, convex optimization, variational analysis and nonsmooth optimization, second-order analysis, group Lasso problems, nuclear norm minimization problems.

(2020). 49J52, 49J53, 49K40, 90C25, 90C31

Introduction

Recovering a signal
x_{0}\in\mathbb{X}
from a noised observation
b\approx\Phi x_{0}
with
\Phi:\mathbb{X}\to\mathbb{Y}
being a linear operator between two Euclidean spaces is an important problem in many different areas of science and engineering. One of the most popular methods for recovering approximate solutions of
x_{0}
is to solve the following regularized optimization problem

\min_{x\in\mathbb{X}}\qquad\frac{1}{2\mu}\|\Phi x-b\|^{2}+g(x),

(1.1)

where
\mu>0
is a regularization (tuning) parameter and
g:\mathbb{X}\to\mathbb{R}
is a convex function (regularizer). The choice of regularizer
g
usually depends on desirable properties of the original signal
x_{0}
. As
b
is also considered a parameter, it is natural to question stability and sensitivity of the solution mapping of problem (
1.1
) with the two parameters
b
and
\mu

[
43
,
50
,
51
,
4
,
2
,
5
]
. In this paper, we mainly study the Lipschitz stability of the solution mapping
S:\mathbb{Y}\times\mathbb{R}_{++}\to\mathbb{X}

S(b,\mu)\mathrel{{\mathop{=}\limits}}{\rm argmin}\,\left\{\frac{1}{2\mu}\|\Phi x-b\|^{2}+g(x)|\;x\in\mathbb{X}\right\}.

(1.2)

Lipschitz stability of this mapping was studied recently in
[
2
]
for a particular class of
g
as the
\ell_{1}
norm. Since the
\ell_{1}
norm is a polyhedral function,
[
2
]
is able to employ some known results on Lipschitz stability
[
38
,
48
]
and available second-order computations on the
\ell_{1}
norm to obtain sufficient conditions for Lipschitz stability of
S
and derive explicit formula for its Lipschitz modulus. However, these second-order computations could be very complicated for nonpolyhedral regularizers such as the
nuclear norm
. In the recent papers
[
4
,
5
]
, sensitivity analysis of solution mapping (
1.2
) was studied via the so-called
conservative Jacobian
, which also needs
S
to be Lipschitz continuous at first. We approach Lipschitz stability differently via the
generalized implicit function theorem

[
44
,
21
]
and
tilt stability

[
36
]
together with the recent geometric characterizations of
strong minima
in
[
23
]
without computing complex second-order structures on
g
. Our ideas are summarized as follows.

The solution mapping (
1.2
) is viewed as a
generalized equation

[
44
]

S(b,\mu)=\left\{x\in\mathbb{X}|\;0\in\frac{1}{\mu}\Phi^{*}(\Phi x-b)+\partial g(x)\right\},

(1.3)

where
\partial g
is the
convex subdifferential mapping
of
g
.
It is well-known from the notable Robinson implicit function theorem
[
44
,
21
]
that
S
has a
Lipschitz continuous single-valued localization
around
(\bar{b},\bar{\mu})\in\mathbb{Y}\times\mathbb{R}_{++}
for
\bar{x}\in S(\bar{b},\bar{\mu})
if the following mapping

\widehat{S}(v)\mathrel{{\mathop{=}\limits}}\left\{x\in\mathbb{X}|\;v\in\frac{1}{\bar{\mu}}\Phi^{*}(\Phi x-\bar{b})+\partial g(x)\right\}\quad\mbox{for}\quad v\in\mathbb{X}^{*},

(1.4)

has a Lipschitz continuous single-valued localization around
0
for
\bar{x}
. This idea dates back to the original work of Robinson
[
44
]
similar to the connection between the classical implicit function theorem and the classical inverse function theorem. Studying Lipschitz stability of
\widehat{S}
in (
1.4
) is easier than that of
S
in (
1.3
). One important observation in our study is that Lipschitz stability of
\widehat{S}
around
0
for
\bar{x}
is equivalent to tilt stability at
\bar{x}
of the below problem

\min_{x\in\mathbb{X}}\qquad\frac{1}{2\bar{\mu}}\|\Phi x-\bar{b}\|^{2}+g(x).

(1.5)

Tilt stability occurs when the solution mapping of an optimization problem is Lipschitzian stable as it is shifted by a small tilt (linear) perturbation. Indeed,
\widehat{S}
in (
1.4
) is the solution mapping of the following problem

\min_{x\in\mathbb{X}}\qquad\frac{1}{2\bar{\mu}}\|\Phi x-\bar{b}\|^{2}+g(x)-\langle v,x\rangle\quad\mbox{for}\quad v\in\mathbb{X}^{*}.

(1.6)

Tilt stability was introduced in the landmark paper of Poliquin and Rockafellar
[
36
]
and developed in different settings
[
3
,
9
,
19
,
20
,
26
,
29
,
30
,
33
,
35
,
41
,
42
]
. The original result in
[
36
, Theorem 1.3]
shows that tilt stability is equivalent to the positive definiteness of the
generalized Hessian/second-order limiting subdifferential
of the cost function defined in
[
37
]
. For example, when
g(x)
is the
\ell_{1}
-norm, by using the explicit formula of the generalized Hessian of
\ell_{1}
norm in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, e.g.,
[
25
, Proposition 8.1]
, one can obtain a full characterization of tilt stability for problem (
1.5
), which turns out to be equivalent to Tibshirani’s condition
[
50
, Lemma 1]
for the corresponding
\ell_{1}
optimization problem (a.k.a. Lasso). Consequently, Tibshirani’s condition is shown to be sufficient for Lipschitz stability of solution mapping (
1.2
) of Lasso problem as in
[
2
]
via a different approach. One of the main disadvantages of using the generalized Hessian is its hard computation in many nonpolyhedral structures. Lots of research papers tried to overcome that obstacle by different ways
[
1
,
9
,
18
,
19
,
29
,
35
,
42
]
. In this paper, we obtain a new characterization of tilt stability via
second subderivatives

[
7
,
48
]
, which seems to be the simplest second order structure for nonsmooth functions. Our approach relies on the primal-dual characterization of tilt stability
[
9
, Theorem 3.3]
that is also used in
[
3
]
to establish explicit conditions for tilt stability in (nonpolyhedral)
second-order cone programming
problems.

A significant class of problem (
1.1
) is the
nuclear norm minimization problem
:

\min_{X\in\mathbb{R}^{n_{1}\times n_{2}}}\quad\frac{1}{2\mu}\|\Phi X-B\|^{2}+\|X\|_{*},

(1.7)

where
\Phi:\mathbb{R}^{n_{1}\times n_{2}}\to\mathbb{R}^{m}
is a linear operator,
B\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
,
\mu>0
, and
\|X\|_{*}
is the nuclear norm of matrix
X
being the sum of all its singular values. This problem has high impact in several important research areas such as
matrix completion
,
multivariate regression
,
matrix compressed sensing

[
11
,
12
,
13
,
54
]
. Unlike the
\ell_{1}
norm, the generalized Hessian of the nuclear norm is very hard to calculate. One of our main motivations is to characterize tilt stability of problem (
1.7
) and Lipschitz stability of the solution mapping (
1.2
) without computing the generalized Hessian.

Our contributions.
As discussed above, tilt stability plays the central role in our paper. More specifically, we study tilt stability of the following problem

\min_{x\in\mathbb{X}}\quad f(x)+g(x),

(1.8)

where
f:\mathbb{X}\to\mathbb{R}
is a twice continuously differentiable convex function and
g:\mathbb{X}\to\overline{\mathbb{R}}
is a proper l.s.c. convex function. This is the general model of problem (
1.5
) when the quadratic loss function is replaced by the function
f
. Characterization of tilt stability for this problem is important for its own sake due to recent developments of employing tilt stability to design nonsmooth Newton’s method to solve many major optimization problems in the format of (
1.8
); see, e.g.,
[
25
,
26
,
27
,
29
,
40
]
. One of our main results is to establish a new geometric characterization of tilt stability with two additional conditions on the function
g
: (a)
g
satisfies the so-called
quadratic growth condition for a given set
\mathcal{M}\subset\mathbb{X}^{*}
and (b)
\partial g
has
relative approximations
by
\mathcal{M}
.
Convex piecewise linear-quadratic functions

[
48
]
, including the
\ell_{1}
norm and convex picecewise linear functions satisfy these two conditions for the whole dual space
\mathbb{X}^{*}
due to polyhedral features in their constructions. We also show that the two conditions are valid in other substantial nonpolyhedral regularizers such as the
\ell_{1}/\ell_{2}
norm (a.k.a the group Lasso regularizer
[
52
]
), the nuclear norm, and the indicator function to the set of positive semi-definite matrices. Constructing the set
\mathcal{M}
in these cases is not trivial, especially for the nuclear norm.

Another crucial part of our paper is to investigate the Lipschitz stability of the optimal solution mapping
S
in (
1.2
). In the case of Lasso problem, the recent paper
[
2
]
shows that Tibshirani’s condition
[
50
]
is sufficient for the latter property. Our geometric characterization of tilt stability for problem (
1.5
) are also sufficient for the Lipschitz stability of
S
as discussed above. In this paper, we prove that it is also necessary when the function
g
is either the
\ell_{1}/\ell_{2}
norm (including the case of
\ell_{1}
norm) or the nuclear norm. Moreover, for the last two important regularizers, we show that if the solution mapping (
1.2
) is single-valued, the Lipschitz continuity follows automatically. This is quite a surprising phenomena, as Lipschitz continuity usually does not occur freely. Consequently, Tibshirani’s sufficient condition
[
50
]
is also necessary for single-valueness and Lipschitz stability of solution mapping of the Lasso problem.

2
Preliminaries

Throughout this paper we suppose that
\mathbb{X}
is an Euclidean finite dimensional space with the norm
\|\cdot\|
. Its dual space
\mathbb{X}^{*}
is endowed by the inner product
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
.
\mathbb{B}_{\varepsilon}(x)
stands for the open ball with radius
\varepsilon>0
and center
x
. The most important geometric structure used in our paper is the (Bouligand/Severi)
tangent/contingent cone

[
48
, Definition 6.1]
to a closed set
\Omega\subset\mathbb{X}
at a point
\bar{x}\in\Omega
defined by

T_{\Omega}(\bar{x})\mathrel{{\mathop{=}\limits}}\{w\in\mathbb{X}|\;\exists t_{k}\downarrow 0,w_{k}\to w,\bar{x}+t_{k}w_{k}\in\Omega\}=\mathop{{\rm Lim}\,{\rm sup}}_{t\downarrow 0}\frac{\Omega-\bar{x}}{t}.

(2.1)

In the convex case, the polar cone of the tangent cone is known as the
normal cone
to
\Omega
at
\bar{x}
:

N_{\Omega}(\bar{x})\mathrel{{\mathop{=}\limits}}\left[T_{\Omega}(\bar{x})\right]^{\circ}=\{v\in\mathbb{X}^{*}|\;\langle v,x-\bar{x}\rangle\leq 0,\forall x\in\Omega\}.

(2.2)

Let
\varphi:\mathbb{X}\to\overline{\mathbb{R}}\mathrel{{\mathop{=}\limits}}\mathbb{R}\cup\{+\infty\}
be a proper lower semi-continuous (l.s.c.) convex function with nonempty domain
\mbox{\rm dom}\,\varphi\mathrel{{\mathop{=}\limits}}\{x\in\mathbb{X}|\;\varphi(x)<\infty\}
. The convex subdifferential of
\varphi
at
\bar{x}\in\mbox{\rm dom}\,\varphi
is

\partial\varphi(\bar{x})\mathrel{{\mathop{=}\limits}}\{v\in\mathbb{X}^{*}|\;\varphi(x)-\varphi(\bar{x})\geq\langle v,x-\bar{x}\rangle,\forall x\in\mathbb{X}\}.

The normal cone
N_{\Omega}(\bar{x})
defined above is indeed the subdifferential of the
indicator function

\delta_{\Omega}(\cdot)
at
\bar{x}
, where
\delta_{\Omega}(x)
is equal to
0
if
x\in\Omega
and equal to
\infty
otherwise.

The Fenchel conjugate of
\varphi
is the function
\varphi^{*}:\mathbb{X}^{*}\to\overline{\mathbb{R}}
defined by

\varphi^{*}(v)\mathrel{{\mathop{=}\limits}}\sup\{\langle v,x\rangle-\varphi(x)|\;x\in\mathbb{X}\}\quad\mbox{for}\quad v\in\mathbb{X}^{*}.

(2.3)

It is well-known from the Fenchel-Young’s identity that

v\in\partial\varphi(\bar{x})\quad\mbox{if and only if}\quad\varphi(\bar{x})+\varphi^{*}(v)=\langle v,\bar{x}\rangle.

(2.4)

Note further that
\partial\varphi:\mathbb{X}\rightrightarrows\mathbb{X}^{*}
is a set-valued mapping whose graph is denoted by

\mbox{\rm gph}\,\partial\varphi\mathrel{{\mathop{=}\limits}}\{(u,v)\in X\times\mathbb{X}^{*}|\;v\in\partial\varphi(u)\}.

Next, let us consider the following convex optimization problem

\min_{x\in\mathbb{X}}\quad\varphi(x).

(2.5)

The point
\bar{x}
is an optimal solution (minimizer) of this problem if and only if
0\in\partial\varphi(\bar{x})
. Moreover, it is called a strong solution if there exist some
\varepsilon>0
and modulus
\kappa>0
such that

\varphi(x)-\varphi(\bar{x})\geq\frac{\kappa}{2}\|x-\bar{x}\|^{2}\quad\mbox{for all}\quad x\in\mathbb{B}_{\varepsilon}(\bar{x}).

One of the simplest ways to characterize strong minima is using the
second subderivatives

[
7
,
48
]
.

Definition 2.1

(Second subderivative)
.

The second subderivative of
\varphi
at
\bar{x}
for
\bar{v}\in\partial\varphi(\bar{x})
is the function
d^{2}\varphi(\bar{x}|\bar{v}):\mathbb{X}\to\overline{\mathbb{R}}
defined by

d^{2}\varphi(\bar{x}|\bar{v})(w)\mathrel{{\mathop{=}\limits}}\liminf_{t\downarrow 0,w^{\prime}\to w}\dfrac{\varphi(\bar{x}+tw^{\prime})-\varphi(\bar{x})-t\langle\bar{v},w^{\prime}\rangle}{\frac{1}{2}t^{2}}\quad\mbox{for}\quad w\in\mathbb{X}.

(2.6)

It is well-known
[
48
, Theorem 13.24]
that
\bar{x}
is a strong minimizer of
\varphi
if and only if
0\in\partial\varphi(\bar{x})
and there exists
\ell>0
such that

d^{2}\varphi(\bar{x}|0)(w)\geq\ell\|w\|^{2}\quad\mbox{for all}\quad w\in\mathbb{X}.

(2.7)

As discussed in the Introduction, tilt stability introduced by Poliquin and Rockafellar
[
36
, Definition 1.1]
plays a central role in our paper. We recall the definition here.

Definition 2.2

(Tilt stability)
.

The point
\bar{x}
is called a tilt-stable solution of the function
\varphi
(or problem (
2.5
)) if there exists
\gamma>0
such that the following optimal solution mapping

M_{\gamma}(v)\mathrel{{\mathop{=}\limits}}{\rm argmin}\;\{\varphi(x)-\langle v,x\rangle|\;x\in\mathbb{B}_{\gamma}(\bar{x})\}\quad\mbox{for}\quad v\in\mathbb{X}^{*}

(2.8)

is single-valued and Lipschitz continuous on some neighborhood of
0\in\mathbb{X}^{*}
with
M_{\gamma}(0)=\bar{x}
.

Tilt stability in the above definition is designed for general (possibly nonconvex) cost function
\varphi
as in
[
36
]
. In this paper, we focus on the case when
\varphi
is a convex function. As local minimizers are also global minimizers in convex programming, it is possible to remove the ball
\mathbb{B}_{\gamma}(\bar{x})
in (
2.8
). Indeed,
\bar{x}
is a tilt stable minimizer of the convex problem (
2.5
) if and only if the solution mapping

M(v)={\rm argmin}\;\{\varphi(x)-\langle v,x\rangle|\;x\in\mathbb{X}\}\quad\mbox{for}\quad v\in\mathbb{X}^{*}

(2.9)

is single-valued and Lipschitz continuous around
0\in\mathbb{X}^{*}
with
M(0)=\bar{x}
. Note that

M(v)=(\partial\varphi)^{-1}(v)=\partial\varphi^{*}(v)

is a convex set for any
v\in\mathbb{X}^{*}
. Thus
\bar{x}
is a tilt-stable solution of problem (
2.5
) if and only if
\partial\varphi
is
strongly metrically regular
at
0
for
\bar{x}
in the sense that
(\partial\varphi)^{-1}(0)=\{\bar{x}\}
and the inverse mapping
(\partial\varphi)^{-1}
is Lipschitz continuous around
0
; see
[
1
,
18
,
19
,
33
,
42
,
55
]
. Note that this definition of strong metric regularity is slightly different from the traditional one in
[
21
, Section 3G]
, which needs a
localization
. In particular,
[
21
, Section 3G]
says
\partial\varphi
is strongly metrically regular at
0
for
\bar{x}
if
(\partial\varphi)^{-1}
has a
Lipschitz continuous singe-valued localization

s
around
0
for
\bar{x}
in the sense that there exists a neighborhood
U\times V\subset\mathbb{X}\times\mathbb{X}^{*}
of
(\bar{x},0)
such that
\mbox{\rm gph}\,s=\mbox{\rm gph}\,(\partial\varphi)^{-1}\cap(V\times U)
and that
s
is single-valued and Lipschitz continuous in
V
with
s(0)=\bar{x}
. In this case, for any
v\in V
,
s(v)
is an isolated point of
(\partial\varphi)^{-1}(v)
. Since
(\partial\varphi)^{-1}(v)
is a convex set, the latter implies that
s(v)=(\partial\varphi)^{-1}(v)
, i.e.,
(\partial\varphi)^{-1}(\cdot)
is single-valued and Lipschitz continuous on
V
.

There are different characterizations of tilt stability. In the original paper
[
36
]
, Poliquin and Rockafellar used the
generalized Hessian
of the function
\varphi
, which is the
limiting coderivative
of the subdifferential
\partial\varphi
introduced by Mordukhovich
[
37
]
to characterize tilt stability. The disadvantage of this construction is that it may be very hard to compute. In this paper, we avoid calculating it and actually employ the second subderivative in Definition
2.1
. Before going there, we recall two known characterizations of tilt stability that are the
uniform quadratic growth condition

[
1
,
18
,
19
]
and the positive definiteness of the
subgradient graphical derivative

[
9
]
, a primal-dual structure that is also used in
[
3
]
to derive explicit conditions for tilt stability of (nonpolyhedral) second-order cone programming problems.

Theorem 2.3

(Characterization of tilt stability via subgradient)
.

Suppose that
0\in\partial\varphi(\bar{x})
. The following are equivalent:

(i)

(Tilt stability)
The point
\bar{x}
is a tilt-stable solution of problem (
2.5
).

(ii)

(Uniform quadratic growth condition)
There exist some
\kappa>0
and an open neighborhood
U\times V\subset\mathbb{X}\times\mathbb{X}^{*}
of
(\bar{x},0)
such that for any
(u,v)\in\mbox{\rm gph}\,\partial\varphi\cap(U\times V)
one has

\varphi(x)-\varphi(u)-\langle v,x-u\rangle\geq\frac{\kappa}{2}\|x-u\|^{2}\quad\mbox{for all}\quad x\in U.

(2.10)

(iii)

(Positive definiteness via the subgradient graphical derivative)
There exist
\kappa>0
and an open neighborhood
U\times V\subset\mathbb{X}\times\mathbb{X}^{*}
of
(\bar{x},0)
such that

\langle z,w\rangle\geq\frac{1}{\kappa}\|w\|^{2}\quad\mbox{whenever}\quad z\in D\partial\varphi(u|v)(w),(u,v)\in{\rm gph}\partial\varphi\cap(U\times V),

(2.11)

where
D\partial\varphi(u|v)(\cdot)
is known as the subgradient graphical derivative defined by

D\partial\varphi(u|v)(w)=\{z\in\mathbb{X}|\;(z,w)\in T_{{\rm gph}\,\partial\varphi}(u,v)\}

(2.12)

with
T_{{\rm gph}\,\partial\varphi}(u,v)
being the contingent cone to
\mbox{\rm gph}\,\partial\varphi
at
(u,v)\in\mbox{\rm gph}\,\partial\varphi
defined in (
2.1
).

Next we connect the second subderivative with the subgradient graphical derivative.

Lemma 2.4
.

Let
\varphi
be a proper l.s.c. convex function. For any
(u,v)\in\mbox{\rm gph}\,\partial\varphi
, we have

2\langle z,w\rangle\geq d^{2}\varphi(u|v)(w)+d^{2}\varphi^{*}(v|u)(z)\quad\mbox{whenever}\quad z\in D\partial\varphi(u|v)(w).

(2.13)

Consequently,

\langle z,w\rangle\geq\frac{1}{2}d^{2}\varphi(u|v)(w)\quad\mbox{whenever}\quad z\in D\partial\varphi(u|v)(w).

(2.14)

Proof.

Pick any
z\in D\partial\varphi(u|v)(w)
, we find sequences
(w_{k},z_{k})\to(w,z)
and
t_{k}\downarrow 0
such that

v+t_{k}z_{k}\in\partial\varphi(u+t_{k}w_{k}).

It follows from the Fenchel-Young’s identity (
2.4
) that

\begin{array}[]{ll}\varphi^{*}(v+t_{k}z_{k})&=\langle v+t_{k}z_{k},u+t_{k}w_{k}\rangle-\varphi(u+t_{k}w_{k})\\
&=\langle v,u\rangle+t_{k}\langle v,w_{k}\rangle+t_{k}\langle z_{k},u\rangle+t_{k}^{2}\langle z_{k},w_{k}\rangle-\varphi(u+t_{k}w_{k})\\
&=\varphi^{*}(v)+\varphi(u)+t_{k}\langle v,w_{k}\rangle+t_{k}\langle z_{k},u\rangle+t_{k}^{2}\langle z_{k},w_{k}\rangle-\varphi(u+t_{k}w_{k}).\end{array}

Hence we have

2\langle z_{k},w_{k}\rangle=\dfrac{\varphi(u+t_{k}w_{k})-\varphi(u)-t_{k}\langle v,w_{k}\rangle}{\frac{1}{2}t_{k}^{2}}+\dfrac{\varphi^{*}(v+t_{k}z_{k})-\varphi^{*}(v)-t_{k}\langle u,z_{k}\rangle}{\frac{1}{2}t_{k}^{2}}\\

By taking the liminf both sides, we obtain (
2.13
). Moreover, (
2.14
) follows directly from (
2.13
), as
\varphi^{*}
is a convex function.

\hfill\Box

When the function
\varphi
is
twice epi-differentiable
at
u\in\mathbb{X}
for
v\in\partial\varphi(u)
in the sense of
[
48
, Definition 13.6]
, it is proved in
[
10
, Lemma 3.6 and Theorem 3.7]
that (
2.14
) can be improved to

\langle z,w\rangle=d^{2}\varphi(u|v)(w)\quad\mbox{whenever}\quad z\in D\partial\varphi(u|v)(w).

(2.15)

We are ready to set up a new characterization of tilt stability via second subderivative as below.

Corollary 2.5

(Characterization of tilt stability via second subderivative)
.

Suppose that
\bar{x}
is a minimizer of
\varphi
. Then
\bar{x}
is a tilt-stable solution of problem (
2.5
) if and only if there exist
\ell>0
and an open neighborhood
U\times V\subset\mathbb{X}\times\mathbb{X}^{*}
of
(\bar{x},0)
such that

d^{2}\varphi(u|v)(w)\geq\ell\|w\|^{2}\quad\mbox{whenever}\quad(u,v)\in\mbox{\rm gph}\,\partial\varphi\cap(U\times V)\quad\mbox{and}\quad w\in\mathbb{X}.

(2.16)

Proof.

Suppose that
\bar{x}
is a tilt-stable solution of problem (
2.5
). By Theorem
2.3
, the uniform quadratic growth condition (
2.10
) holds with some modulus
\kappa>0
and open neighborhood
U\times V
of
(\bar{x},0)
. For any
(u,v)\in\mbox{\rm gph}\,\partial\varphi\cap(U\times V)
, note from (
2.10
) that
u

is a strong solution with modulus
\kappa
of the function
\varphi(\cdot)-\langle v,\cdot\rangle
. It follows from (
2.7
) that (
2.16
) is satisfied.

To prove the converse implication, suppose that inequality (
2.16
) holds for some
\ell>0
and neighborhood
U\times V
of
(\bar{x},0)
. We obtain from Lemma
2.4
that

\langle z,w\rangle\geq\frac{\ell}{2}\|w\|^{2}\quad\mbox{whenever}\quad z\in D\partial\varphi(u|v)(w),(u,v)\in\mbox{\rm gph}\,\partial\varphi\cap(U\times V).

By Theorem
2.3
,
\bar{x}
is a tilt-stable solution of
\varphi
.
\hfill\Box

Second subderivative is also an important tool in
[
35
]
to obtain some new characterizations of tilt stability, but the function
\varphi
in that paper is assumed to be twice epi-differentiable. In this case, Corollary
2.5
follows directly from Theorem
2.3
and equality (
2.15
). Our characterization (
2.16
) is different from those in
[
35
]
and does not need the function
\varphi
to be twice epi-differentiable.

3
Geometric characterizations of Lipschitz stability for convex optimization problems

In this section we study tilt stability of optimization problem (
1.8
)

\min_{x\in\mathbb{X}}\quad\varphi(x)\mathrel{{\mathop{=}\limits}}f(x)+g(x),

where
f,g:\mathbb{X}\to\overline{\mathbb{R}}
are proper l.s.c. convex functions and
f
is twice continuously differentiable in
\mathbb{X}
with full domain. We need some additional conditions on the function
g
.

Definition 3.1

(Quadratic growth condition for a set)
.

Let
g:\mathbb{X}\to\overline{\mathbb{R}}
be a proper l.s.c. convex function with
(\bar{x},\bar{y})\in\mbox{\rm gph}\,\partial g
. The function
g
is said to satisfy the quadratic growth condition at
\bar{x}
for
\bar{y}
with modulus
\kappa>0
if there exists
\varepsilon>0
such that

g(x)-g(\bar{x})-\langle\bar{y},x-\bar{x}\rangle\geq\frac{\kappa}{2}\left[{\rm dist}\,(x;(\partial g)^{-1}(\bar{y}))\right]^{2}\quad\mbox{for all}\quad x\in\mathbb{B}_{\varepsilon}(\bar{x}).

(3.1)

Let
\mathcal{M}
be a subset of
\mathbb{X}^{*}
containing
\bar{y}
. We say
g
satisfies the quadratic growth condition around
(\bar{x},\bar{y})\in\mbox{\rm gph}\,\partial g
for
\mathcal{M}
if there exist
\eta,\kappa>0
such that for any
(x,y)\in\mbox{\rm gph}\,\partial g\cap\mathbb{B}_{\eta}(\bar{x},\bar{y})
with
y\in\mathcal{M}
the function
g
satisfies the quadratic growth condition at
x
for
y
with the same modulus
\kappa.

The quadratic growth condition for convex functions
[
7
]
is known to be equivalent to the
Łojasiewicz inequality with exponent
\frac{1}{2}

[
6
]
and the
metric subregularity of the subdifferential

[
1
,
19
,
55
]
. Many important classes of convex functions satisfy these conditions; see, e.g.,
[
6
,
17
,
57
]
. The quadratic growth condition for a set
\mathcal{M}
introduced here seems new. It means that the quadratic growth condition holds at
x
around
\bar{x}
for any
y\in\partial\varphi(x)\cap\mathcal{M}
around
\bar{y}
with the same modulus, i.e.,
\partial\varphi
is metrically subregular at
x
for
y\in\mathcal{M}
in the sense of (
3.2
) below. Of course, if the function
g
satisfies the quadratic growth condition at
\bar{x}
for
\bar{y}
, it also satisfies the quadratic growth condition around
(\bar{x},\bar{y})
for the trivial set
\mathcal{M}=\{\bar{y}\}
by applying the Fenchel-Young’s identity (
2.4
). Finding the nontrivial set
\mathcal{M}\neq\{\bar{y}\}
is not simple. Since the modulus is uniform when
y\in\mathcal{M}
, computing the modulus
\kappa
in (
3.1
) will help us determine the set
\mathcal{M}
. Next we discuss a few major functions that meet the quadratic growth condition for some nontrivial sets
\mathcal{M}
.

Example 3.2

(Convex piecewise linear-quadratic functions,
[
48
, Definition 10.20]
)
.

The proper l.s.c. convex function
g:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\overline{\mathbb{R}}
is called
convex piecewise linear-quadratic
if
\mbox{\rm dom}\,g
is a union of finitely many polyhedral sets, for each of which the function
g(x)
has a quadratic expression
\frac{1}{2}\langle Ax,x\rangle+\langle b,x\rangle+c
for some scalar
c\in\mathbb{R}
, vector
b\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, and positive semi-definite matrix
A
. This class of functions covers all
convex piecewise linear functions
including some important convex regularizers such as the
\ell_{1}
(sparsity) norm, the
\ell_{\infty}
norm, and the
anisotropic total variation

[
51
]
. It also contains the
elastic net regularizer

[
56
]
, the
\ell_{1}+\ell_{2}

regularizer

[
31
]
, and the
discrete Blake-Zisserman regularizer

[
8
]
. In this case, it is well-known
[
48
, Proposition 12.30]
that the subdifferential mapping
\partial g
is
piecewise polyhedral
, i.e., the graph of
\partial g
is a union of finitely many polyhedral sets. Obviously, the inverse subdifferential mapping
(\partial g)^{-1}
is also piecewise polyhedral. By the landmark result of Robinson
[
45
]
(see also
[
21
, Proposition 3H.1 and Theorem 3H.3]
), the mapping
\partial g
is
metrically subregular
at any
\bar{x}\in\mbox{\rm dom}\,g
for any
\bar{y}\in\partial g(\bar{x})
with the same modulus
\ell>0
in the sense that there exists some
\varepsilon>0
such that

{\rm dist}\,(x;(\partial g)^{-1}(\bar{y}))\leq\ell{\rm dist}\,(\bar{y};\partial g(x))\quad\mbox{for all}\quad x\in\mathbb{B}_{\varepsilon}(\bar{x})\cap\mbox{\rm dom}\,\partial g.

(3.2)

This condition is known to guarantee the quadratic growth condition (
3.1
) with the modulus
\kappa
smaller but arbitrarily close to
\ell^{-1}>0
; see, e.g.,
[
1
,
19
,
55
]
. Thus the function
g
satisfies the quadratic growth condition around
(\bar{x},\bar{y})\in\mbox{\rm gph}\,\partial g
for the whole space
\mathcal{M}=\mathbb{R}^{n}
or any neighborhood of
\bar{y}
.
\triangle

Example 3.3

(
\ell_{1}/\ell_{2}
norm)
.

Suppose that
\mathcal{J}
is a partition of
\{1,2,\ldots,n\}
with
m
distinct groups. The
\ell_{1}/\ell_{2}
norm for this partition (a.k.a. the group Lasso regularizer) is defined by

g(x)=\|x\|_{1,2}\mathrel{{\mathop{=}\limits}}\sum_{J\in\mathcal{J}}\|x_{J}\|\quad\mbox{for}\quad x\in\mathbb{R}^{n},

(3.3)

where
\|x_{J}\|
is the regular Euclidean norm at
x_{J}\in\mathbb{R}^{|J|}
for any
J\in\mathcal{J}
. The subdifferential of this norm is metrically subregular at any
\bar{x}
for any
\bar{y}\in\partial\|\bar{x}\|_{1,2}
by
[
57
, Proposition 9]
. By
[
1
, Theorem 3.3]
, the
\ell_{1}/\ell_{2}
norm satisfies the quadratic growth condition. Here we advance this property by showing that it satisfies the quadratic growth condition around
(\bar{x},\bar{y})\in\mbox{\rm gph}\,\partial g
for a nontrivial set
\mathcal{M}
. In order to find the set
\mathcal{M}
, we compute the modulus
\kappa
in (
3.1
) first. For any
J\in\mathcal{J}
, note that

\displaystyle\partial\|x_{J}\|=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\mathbb{B}_{J}&\mbox{if}\;x_{J}=0_{J}\in\mathbb{R}^{|J|}\\
\frac{x_{J}}{\|x_{J}\|}&\mbox{otherwise},\end{array}\right.

where
\mathbb{B}_{J}
is the unit Euclidean ball in
\mathbb{R}^{|J|}
. Let us write
\bar{y}=(\bar{y}_{J})_{J\in\mathcal{J}}
with
\bar{y}_{J}\in\partial\|\bar{x}_{J}\|
. It follows from (
3.3
) that

\displaystyle(\partial\|\cdot\|)^{-1}(\bar{y}_{J})=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\mathbb{R}_{+}\bar{y}_{J}&\quad\mbox{if}\quad\|\bar{y}_{J}\|=1\\
\{0\}&\quad\mbox{if}\quad\|\bar{y}_{J}\|<1.\end{array}\right.

For any
x_{J}\in\mathbb{R}^{|J|}
, we have

\|x_{J}\|-\|\bar{x}_{J}\|-\langle\bar{y}_{J},x_{J}-\bar{x}_{J}\rangle=\|x_{J}\|-\langle\bar{y}_{J},x_{J}\rangle=\|x_{J}\|(1-\|\bar{y}_{J}\|\cos\theta_{J}),

(3.10)

where
\theta_{J}
is the angle between
x_{J}
and
\bar{y}_{J}
. Let us consider two cases of (
3.3
).

Case I.
If
\|\bar{y}_{J}\|=1
, note from (
3.3
) that

{\rm dist}\,(x_{J};(\partial\|\cdot\|)^{-1}(\bar{y}_{J}))={\rm dist}\,(x_{J};\mathbb{R}_{+}\bar{y}_{J}).

If
\theta_{J}\in[0,\frac{\pi}{2}]
, we have
{\rm dist}\,(x_{J};\mathbb{R}_{+}\bar{y}_{J})=\|x_{J}\|\sin\theta_{J}.

This together with (
3.10
) gives us that

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{ll}\|x_{J}\|-\|\bar{x}_{J}\|-\langle\bar{y}_{J},x_{J}-\bar{x}_{J}\rangle&=\displaystyle\|x_{J}\|(1-\cos\theta_{J})=2\|x_{J}\|\sin^{2}(\theta_{J}/2)\\
&=\displaystyle\dfrac{1}{2\|x_{J}\|\cos^{2}(\theta_{J}/2)}\left[{\rm dist}\,(x_{J};(\partial\|\cdot\|)^{-1}(\bar{y}_{J}))\right]^{2}\\
&\geq\displaystyle\dfrac{1}{2\|x\|}\left[{\rm dist}\,(x_{J};(\partial\|\cdot\|)^{-1}(\bar{y}_{J}))\right]^{2}.\end{array}

If
\theta_{J}\in[\frac{\pi}{2},\pi]
, note that
{\rm dist}\,(x_{J};\mathbb{R}_{+}\bar{y}_{J})=\|x_{J}\|
. It follows from (
3.10
) that

\|x_{J}\|-\|\bar{x}_{J}\|-\langle\bar{y}_{J},x_{J}-\bar{x}_{J}\rangle=\|x_{J}\|(1-\cos\theta_{J})\geq\|x_{J}\|\geq\dfrac{1}{\|x\|}\left[{\rm dist}\,(x_{J};(\partial\|\cdot\|)^{-1}(\bar{y}_{J}))\right]^{2}.

(3.15)

Case II.
If
\|\bar{y}_{J}\|<1
, we obtain from (
3.3
) that
{\rm dist}\,(x_{J};(\partial\|\cdot\|)^{-1}(\bar{y}_{J}))=\|x_{J}\|
. Combing this with (
3.10
) gives us that

\|x_{J}\|-\|\bar{x}_{J}\|-\langle\bar{y}_{J},x_{J}-\bar{x}_{J}\rangle\geq\|x_{J}\|(1-\|\bar{y}_{J}\|)\geq\dfrac{1-\|\bar{y}_{J}\|}{\|x\|}\left[{\rm dist}\,(x_{J};(\partial\|\cdot\|)^{-1}(\bar{y}_{J}))\right]^{2}.

(3.16)

We are ready to show that the
\ell_{1}/\ell_{2}
norm satisfies the quadratic growth condition. Define the following
subdominant number

\gamma
of
\bar{y}
:

\gamma=\gamma(\bar{y})\mathrel{{\mathop{=}\limits}}\|\bar{y}_{\mathcal{H}}\|_{\infty,2}=\max\{\|\bar{y}_{J}\||\,J\in\mathcal{H}\}<1

(3.17)

if
\mathcal{H}:=\{J\in\mathcal{J}|\;\|\bar{y}_{J}\|<1\}\neq\emptyset
and
\gamma=0
if
\mathcal{H}=\emptyset
. It follows from (
3.3
), (
3.15
), and (
3.16
) that

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{ll}\|x\|_{1,2}-\|\bar{x}\|_{1,2}-\langle\bar{y},x-\bar{x}\rangle\displaystyle\geq\frac{1-\gamma}{2\|x\|}\left[{\rm dist}\,(x;(\partial\|\cdot\|_{1,2})^{-1}(\bar{y}))\right]^{2}\end{array}.

This shows the (global) quadratic growth condition at
\bar{x}
for
\bar{y}
with an explicit modulus.

To find the set
\mathcal{M}
, we need to make sure that the subdominant numbers
\gamma(y)
should be close to
\gamma
and should not be close to
1
, when
y\in\mathcal{M}
is around
\bar{y}
. A possible choice for
\mathcal{M}
is

\mathcal{M}_{1,2}\mathrel{{\mathop{=}\limits}}\{y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}|\;\|y_{J}\|=1,J\in\mathcal{K}\}\quad\mbox{with}\quad\mathcal{K}:=\mathcal{J}\setminus\mathcal{H}.

(3.20)

If
\mathcal{H}=\emptyset,
we have
\mathcal{K}=\mathcal{J}
. This is a trivial case as
\gamma(y)=0
for any
\in\mathcal{M}_{1,2}
. It suffices to consider the case
\mathcal{H}\neq\emptyset
.
For any
\varepsilon\in(0,1-\gamma)
and
(x,y)\in\mbox{\rm gph}\,\partial\|\cdot\|_{1,2}\cap\mathbb{B}_{\varepsilon}(\bar{x},\bar{y})
with
y\in\mathcal{M}_{1,2}
, observe that
\bar{y}
and
y
have the same index set
\mathcal{K}
. Indeed, for any
J\in\mathcal{H}
, we have

\|y_{J}\|\leq\|y_{J}-\bar{y}_{J}\|+\|\bar{y}_{J}\|\leq\|y-\bar{y}\|+\gamma\leq\varepsilon+\gamma<1.

As
y\in\mathcal{M}_{1,2}
, the above inequality tells us that

\{J\in\mathcal{J}|\;\|y_{J}\|=1\}=\mathcal{K}.

It follows that

|\gamma(y)-\gamma(\bar{y})|=|\|y_{\mathcal{H}}\|_{\infty,2}-\|\bar{y}_{\mathcal{H}}\|_{\infty,2}|\leq\|y_{\mathcal{H}}-\bar{y}_{\mathcal{H}}\|_{\infty,2}\leq\varepsilon.

Moreover, for any
u\in\mathbb{B}_{\varepsilon}(x)\subset\mathbb{B}_{2\varepsilon}(\bar{x})
, we derive from (
3.3
) that

\begin{array}[]{ll}\|u\|_{1,2}-\|x\|_{1,2}-\langle y,u-x\rangle\displaystyle\geq\frac{1-\gamma-\varepsilon}{2\|\bar{x}\|+4\varepsilon}\left[{\rm dist}\,(u;(\partial\|\cdot\|_{1,2})^{-1}(y))\right]^{2}.\end{array}

As
\varepsilon
can be chosen arbitrarily small, the latter inequality shows that
\|\cdot\|_{1,2}
satisfies the quadratic growth condition around
(\bar{x},\bar{y})
for
\mathcal{M}_{1,2}
with the same modulus.
\triangle

Example 3.4

(The indicator of the positive semidefinite cone
\mathbb{S}^{n}_{+}
)
.

Let
\mathbb{X}=\mathbb{S}^{n}
be the space of all
n\times n
symmetric matrices and
\mathbb{S}^{n}_{+}
be the set of all positive semidefinite matrices. The indicator function
\delta_{\mathbb{S}^{n}_{+}}(X)
is defined by
0
if
X\in\mathbb{S}^{n}_{+}
and
\infty
otherwise. This function is especially important in semidefinite programming. It is also known that
\delta_{\mathbb{S}^{n}_{+}}(\cdot)
satisfies the quadratic growth condition
[
15
, Theorem 2.4]
. For any
(\overline{X},\overline{Y})\in\mbox{\rm gph}\,N_{\mathbb{S}^{n}_{+}}(\cdot)
, we have the spectral decomposition of
\overline{X}
and
\overline{Y}
:

\overline{X}=\overline{P}\begin{pmatrix}\overline{\Lambda}_{\alpha}&0&0\\
0&0_{\beta}&0\\
0&0&0_{\eta}\end{pmatrix}\overline{P}^{T}\qquad\mbox{and}\qquad\overline{Y}=\overline{P}\begin{pmatrix}0_{\alpha}&0&0\\
0&0_{\beta}&0\\
0&0&\overline{\Lambda}_{\eta}\end{pmatrix}\overline{P}^{T}\in N_{\mathbb{S}^{n}_{+}}(\overline{X}),

(3.21)

where
\{\alpha,\beta,\eta\}
forms a partition of
\{1,\ldots,n\}
,

\overline{\Lambda}_{\alpha}={\rm diag}(\bar{\lambda}_{1},\ldots,\bar{\lambda}_{|\alpha|})
of positive non-increasing eigenvalues of
\overline{X}
,
\overline{\Lambda}_{\eta}={\rm diag}(\bar{\lambda}_{|\alpha|+|\beta|+1},\ldots,\bar{\lambda}_{n})
of negative non-increasing eigenvalues of
\overline{Y}
, and
\overline{P}
is an orthogonal matrix. Let us consider the case
\overline{Y}\neq 0
first.
[
15
, Theorem 2.4]
shows that

\delta_{\mathbb{S}^{n}_{+}}(X)-\delta_{\mathbb{S}^{n}_{+}}(\overline{X})-\langle\overline{Y},X-\overline{X}\rangle\geq\frac{-\bar{\lambda}_{|\alpha|+|\beta|+1}}{2\bar{\lambda}_{1}+\frac{3}{2}+n}\big[{\rm dist}(X;(N_{\mathbb{S}^{n}_{+}})^{-1}(\overline{Y}))\big]^{2}\quad\mbox{for all}\quad X\in\mathbb{B}_{\varepsilon}(\overline{X})

(3.22)

for some
\varepsilon>0
. The eigenvalue
\bar{\lambda}_{|\alpha|+|\beta|+1}
is the
subdominant eigenvalue
of
\overline{Y}
. Let us define

\mathcal{M}_{\mathbb{S}}\mathrel{{\mathop{=}\limits}}\{Y\in\mathbb{S}^{n}|\;\lambda_{i}(Y)=0,i\in\alpha\cup\beta\},

(3.23)

where
\lambda_{1}(Y),\ldots,\lambda_{n}(Y)
are the eigenvalue functions in non-increasing order of
Y\in\mathbb{S}^{n}
. For any
(X,Y)\in\mbox{\rm gph}\,N_{\mathbb{S}^{n}_{+}}(\cdot)\cap\mathbb{B}_{\varepsilon}(\overline{X},\overline{Y})
and for some small
\varepsilon>0
with
Y\in\mathcal{M}_{\mathbb{S}}
, it follows from (
3.22
) that there exists some
\nu>0
(possibly depending on
(X,Y)
) such that

\delta_{\mathbb{S}^{n}_{+}}(U)-\delta_{\mathbb{S}^{n}_{+}}(X)-\langle Y,U-X\rangle\geq\frac{-\lambda_{|\alpha|+|\beta|+1}(Y)}{2\lambda_{1}(X)+\frac{3}{2}+n}\big[{\rm dist}(X;(N_{\mathbb{S}^{n}_{+}})^{-1}(Y))\big]^{2}\quad\mbox{for}\quad U\in\mathbb{B}_{\nu}(X).

As
\lambda_{1}(\cdot)
and
\lambda_{|\alpha|+|\beta|+1}(\cdot)
are Lipschitz continuous functions with modulus
1
, we derive from the latter that

\delta_{\mathbb{S}^{n}_{+}}(U)-\delta_{\mathbb{S}^{n}_{+}}(X)-\langle Y,U-X\rangle\geq\frac{-\bar{\lambda}_{|\alpha|+|\beta|+1}-\varepsilon}{2\bar{\lambda}_{1}+2\varepsilon+\frac{3}{2}+n}\big[{\rm dist}(X;(N_{\mathbb{S}^{n}_{+}})^{-1}(Y))\big]^{2}\quad\mbox{for}\quad U\in\mathbb{B}_{\nu}(X).

By choosing
\varepsilon
small enough, the above inequality tells us that the indicator function
\delta_{\mathbb{S}_{+}}(\cdot)
satisfies the quadratic growth condition for the set
\mathcal{M}_{\mathbb{S}}
. When
\overline{Y}=0
, we may choose
\mathcal{M}_{\mathbb{S}}=\{0\}
. In this case, the quadratic growth condition around
(\overline{X},\overline{Y})
for
\mathcal{M}_{\mathbb{S}}
is also true with any positive modulus.
\triangle

It is worth noting that the choice of
\mathcal{M}
in Definition
3.1
is not necessarily unique; for instance,
\mathcal{M}
can be chosen as any subset of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
containing
\bar{y}
in Example
3.2
or simply
\{\bar{y}\}
in Example
3.3
. The nuclear norm also satisfies the quadratic growth condition for some nontrivial set; see our Lemma
4.2
for further details and discussions. Next, we recall the following lemma taken from
[
23
, Lemma 3.2]
that provides an estimate for the second subderivative of
g
.

Lemma 3.5

(Quadratic growth condition and second subderivative)
.

Suppose that the function
g
satisfies the quadratic growth condition at
\bar{x}
for
\bar{y}\in\partial g(\bar{x})
with modulus
\kappa>0
. Then we have

d^{2}g(\bar{x}|\bar{y})(w)\geq\kappa\left[{\rm dist}\,(w;T_{(\partial g)^{-1}(\bar{y})}(\bar{x}))\right]^{2}\qquad\mbox{for all}\qquad w\in\mathbb{X}.

(3.24)

Consequently,

\mbox{\rm Ker}\,d^{2}g(\bar{x}|\bar{y})=\{w\in\mathbb{X}|\;d^{2}g(\bar{x}|\bar{y})(w)=0\}=T_{(\partial g)^{-1}(\bar{y})}(\bar{x}).

(3.25)

To use the quadratic growth condition for a set
\mathcal{M}
in Definition
3.1
, we need
(x,y)\in\mbox{\rm gph}\,\partial g
and
y\in\mathcal{M}
. If
y\notin\mathcal{M}
, we will try to “push” it onto
\mathcal{M}
by approximating it by some element in
\mathcal{M}\cap\partial g(x)
. Such an approximation is introduced below.

Definition 3.6

(Relative approximations of subgradients by a set)
.

Let
g:\mathbb{X}\to\overline{\mathbb{R}}
be a proper l.s.c. convex function with
(\bar{x},\bar{y})\in\mbox{\rm gph}\,\partial g
and
\mathcal{M}
be a subset in
\mathbb{X}^{*}
containing
\bar{y}
. We say the subdifferential mapping
\partial g
has relative approximations by
\mathcal{M}
around
(\bar{x},\bar{y})
if there exists
\varepsilon\in(0,1)
such that for any
(x,y)\in\mbox{\rm gph}\,\partial g\cap\mathbb{B}_{\varepsilon}(\bar{x},\bar{y})
, we can find
\lambda\in(1-\varepsilon,1]
and
\widehat{y}\in\partial g(x)\cap\mathcal{M}
and
\tilde{y}\in\partial g(x)
satisfying

\lambda\widehat{y}+(1-\lambda)\tilde{y}=y.

(3.26)

Here,
\widehat{y}
is called a relative approximation of
y
by
\mathcal{M}
.

In later results of this section, we need the function
g
to satisfy both the quadratic growth condition for a set
\mathcal{M}
and the relative approximations of subgradients by the same set. When
\mathcal{M}
is the whole space
\mathbb{X}^{*}
, the above definition is trivial. This is the case of convex piecewise-linear quadratic functions in Example
3.2
. The choice of
\mathcal{M}
in that example is also not unique, as we can choose
\mathcal{M}
to be any neighborhood of
\bar{y}
and (
3.26
) holds with
\lambda=1
,
\widehat{y}=\tilde{y}=y
. In general, the set
\mathcal{M}
in Definition
3.6
should have a special property that
\partial g(x)\cap\mathcal{M}\neq\emptyset
for
x
near to the given point
\bar{x}
. This does not allow us to choose
\mathcal{M}
as the trivial set
\{\bar{y}\}
like the case of Definition
3.1
.

Example 3.7

(Relative approximations of the subgradients of
\ell_{1}/\ell_{2}
norm)
.

Let
\|\cdot\|_{1,2}
be the
\ell_{1}/\ell_{2}
norm defined in (
3.3
). Given a point
(\bar{x},\bar{y})\in\mbox{\rm gph}\,\partial\|\cdot\|_{1,2}
, we claim that the subdifferential mapping
\partial\|\cdot\|_{1,2}
always has relative approximations by the set
\mathcal{M}_{1,2}
defined in (
3.20
). Pick any
\varepsilon\in(0,1-\gamma)
and any
(x,y)\in\mbox{\rm gph}\,\partial\|\cdot\|_{1,2}\cap\mathbb{B}_{\varepsilon}(\bar{x},\bar{y})
with
\gamma
in (
3.17
). If
y\in\mathcal{M}_{1,2}
, we can choose
\lambda=1
,
\widehat{y}=\tilde{y}=y
so that (
3.26
) holds. If
y\not\in\mathcal{M}_{1,2}
, define

\mathcal{K}_{1}=\{J\in\mathcal{J}|\;\|y_{J}\|=1\}.

As
y\in\partial\|x\|_{1,2}
and
\|y-\bar{y}\|\leq\varepsilon
, observe that
\mathcal{K}_{1}\subset\mathcal{K}
when
\varepsilon
is sufficiently small. Since
y\notin\mathcal{M}_{1,2}
, we have
\mathcal{I}_{1}\mathrel{{\mathop{=}\limits}}\mathcal{K}\setminus\mathcal{K}_{1}\not=\emptyset
and thus
\lambda\mathrel{{\mathop{=}\limits}}\min_{J\in\mathcal{I}_{1}}\|y_{J}\|<1.

For any
J\in\mathcal{I}_{1}
, note further that

\varepsilon\geq\|y_{J}-\bar{y}_{J}\|\geq\|\bar{y}_{J}\|-\|y_{J}\|\geq 1-\|y_{J}\|,

which implies that
\|y_{J}\|\geq 1-\varepsilon
for any
J\in\mathcal{I}_{1}
, i.e.,
\lambda\geq 1-\varepsilon
and
y_{J}\neq 0
.

It is time to find
\widehat{y}
and
\tilde{y}
satisfying (
3.26
). Define

\widehat{y}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\widehat{y}_{J}=y_{J}&\mbox{if}\quad J\in\mathcal{J}\setminus\mathcal{I}_{1}\\
\widehat{y}_{J}=\frac{y_{J}}{\|y_{J}\|}&\mbox{if}\quad J\in\mathcal{I}_{1}\end{array}\right.\qquad\mbox{and}\qquad\tilde{y}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\tilde{y}_{J}=y_{J}&\mbox{if}\quad J\in\mathcal{J}\setminus\mathcal{I}_{1}\\
\tilde{y}_{J}=\frac{\|y_{J}\|-\lambda}{1-\lambda}\cdot\frac{y_{J}}{\|y_{J}\|}&\mbox{if}\quad J\in\mathcal{I}_{1}\end{array}\right.

As
\|y_{J}\|<1
for
J\in\mathcal{I}_{1}
, we have
x_{J}=0
for
J\in\mathcal{I}_{1}
. It follows that
\widehat{y},\tilde{y}\in\partial\|x\|_{1,2}
. Note further that
\widehat{y}\in\mathcal{M}_{1,2}
and that
\lambda\widehat{y}+(1-\lambda)\tilde{y}=y
. This verifies that
\partial\|\cdot\|_{1,2}
has relative approximations by
\mathcal{M}
around
(\bar{x},\bar{y})
.
\triangle

Example 3.8

(Relative approximations of
N_{\mathbb{S}^{n}_{+}}(\cdot)
)
.

We claim that the normal cone mapping
N_{\mathbb{S}^{n}_{+}}(\cdot)
has relative approximations by the set
\mathcal{M}_{\mathbb{S}}
in (
3.23
). Given a pair
(\overline{X},\overline{Y})\in\mbox{\rm gph}\,N_{\mathbb{S}^{n}_{+}}
with decomposition (
3.21
), for any
(X,Y)\in\mbox{\rm gph}\,N_{\mathbb{S}^{n}_{+}}\cap\mathbb{B}_{\varepsilon}(\overline{X},\overline{Y})
with
\varepsilon>0
, it follows from (
3.21
) that

X=P\begin{pmatrix}\Lambda_{\alpha^{\prime}}&0&0\\
0&0_{\beta^{\prime}}&0\\
0&0&0_{\eta^{\prime}}\end{pmatrix}P^{T}\qquad\mbox{and}\qquad Y=P\begin{pmatrix}0_{\alpha^{\prime}}&0&0\\
0&0_{\beta^{\prime}}&0\\
0&0&\Lambda_{\eta^{\prime}}\end{pmatrix}P^{T}

(3.27)

for some orthogonal matrix
P
.
For sufficiently small
\varepsilon>0
,
\eta\subset\eta^{\prime}
. If
\eta=\eta^{\prime}
, i.e.,
Y\in\mathcal{M}_{\mathbb{S}}
, we choose
\widehat{Y}=Y
as a relative approximation of
Y
by
\mathcal{M}_{\mathbb{S}}
. If
\eta\neq\eta^{\prime}
, we define

\widehat{Y}=P\begin{pmatrix}0_{\alpha}&0&0\\
0&0_{\beta}&0\\
0&0&\Lambda_{\eta}\end{pmatrix}P^{T}\quad\mbox{and}\quad\widetilde{Y}=P\begin{pmatrix}0_{\alpha^{\prime}\cup\beta^{\prime}}&0&0\\
0&\sigma^{-1}\Lambda_{\eta^{\prime}\setminus\eta}&0\\
0&0&\Lambda_{\eta}\end{pmatrix}P^{T}

(3.28)

with
\sigma=|\lambda_{|\alpha|+|\beta|}(Y)|>0
. As
\bar{\lambda}_{|\alpha|+|\beta|}(\overline{Y})=0
, we have

0<\sigma=|\lambda_{|\alpha|+|\beta|}(Y)-\bar{\lambda}_{|\alpha|+|\beta|}(\overline{Y})|\leq\|Y-\overline{Y}\|\leq\varepsilon.

Observe further that
\widehat{Y}\in N_{\mathbb{S}^{n}_{+}}(X)\cap\mathcal{M}_{\mathbb{S}}
,
\widetilde{Y}\in N_{\mathbb{S}^{n}_{+}}(X)
, and that

Y=(1-\sigma)\widehat{Y}+\sigma\widetilde{Y}
. This shows that
\widehat{Y}
is a relative approximation of
Y
by
\mathcal{M}_{\mathbb{S}}
.
\triangle

We are ready to set up the central result of this section, which establishes a geometric characterization of tilt stability for problem (
1.8
).

Theorem 3.9

(Geometric characterization of tilt stability)
.

Let
\bar{x}
be an optimal solution of problem (
1.8
). Suppose that there exists a set
\mathcal{M}\subset\mathbb{X}^{*}
containing
\bar{y}=-\nabla f(\bar{x})\in\partial g(\bar{x})
such that the function
g
satisfies the quadratic growth condition around
(\bar{x},\bar{y})
for
\mathcal{M}
and that the subdifferential mapping
\partial g
has relative approximations by
\mathcal{M}
around
(\bar{x},\bar{y})
. Then
\bar{x}
is a tilt-stable solution if and only if

\mbox{\rm Ker}\,\nabla^{2}f(\bar{x})\cap\mathfrak{T}_{\mathcal{M}}=\{0\},

(3.29)

where the set
\mathfrak{T}_{\mathcal{M}}
is defined by

\mathfrak{T}_{\mathcal{M}}\mathrel{{\mathop{=}\limits}}\mathop{{\rm Lim}\,{\rm sup}}_{(x,y)\mathrel{{\mathop{\longrightarrow}\limits}}(\bar{x},\bar{y})}T_{(\partial g)^{-1}(y)}(x),

(3.30)

where the
Limsup
is in the Painlevé-Kuratowski sense, i.e.,

\mathfrak{T}_{\mathcal{M}}=\left\{w\in\mathbb{X}|\;\exists\,(x_{k},y_{k})\in\mbox{\rm gph}\,\partial g\;{\rm and}\;w_{k}\in T_{(\partial g)^{-1}(y_{k})}(x_{k}):y_{k}\in\mathcal{M},(x_{k},y_{k},w_{k})\to(\bar{x},\bar{y},w)\right\}.

Proof.

Let us start by supposing that
\bar{x}
is a tilt-stable solution of problem (
1.8
). For any
w
in the set
\mbox{\rm Ker}\,\nabla^{2}f(\bar{x})\cap\mathfrak{T}_{\mathcal{M}}
, we claim that
w=0
to verify condition (
3.29
). Indeed, take any sequences
w_{k}\to w
and
(x_{k},y_{k})\mathrel{{\mathop{\longrightarrow}\limits}}(\bar{x},\bar{y})
with
y_{k}\in\mathcal{M}
and
w_{k}\in T_{(\partial g)^{-1}(y_{k})}(x_{k})
. Define
v_{k}:=\nabla f(x_{k})+y_{k}\in\partial\varphi(x_{k})
and note that
v_{k}\to 0
. By Corollary
2.5
, we find some
\ell>0
such that

\ell\|w_{k}\|^{2}\leq d^{2}\varphi(x_{k}|\,v_{k})(w_{k})=\langle\nabla^{2}f(x_{k})w_{k},w_{k}\rangle+d^{2}g(x_{k}|\,y_{k})(w_{k})

for sufficiently large
k
. As
w_{k}\in T_{(\partial g)^{-1}(y_{k})}(x_{k})
, it follows from Lemma
3.5
that
d^{2}g(x_{k}|y_{k})(w_{k})=0
. The above inequality gives us that

\ell\|w_{k}\|^{2}\leq\langle\nabla^{2}f(x_{k})w_{k},w_{k}\rangle.

By passing to the limit, we have
\ell\|w\|^{2}\leq\langle\nabla^{2}f(\bar{x})w,w\rangle=0
, i.e.,
w=0
. This ensures (
3.29
).

To justify the converse implication, suppose that condition (
3.29
) holds. If
\bar{x}
is not a tilt-stable solution of problem (
1.8
), by Corollary
2.5
again there exist a sequence
(x_{k},y_{k})\mathrel{{\mathop{\longrightarrow}\limits}}(\bar{x},\bar{y})
and
w_{k}\in\mathbb{X}
with
\|w_{k}\|=1
such that

\frac{1}{k}\geq d^{2}\varphi(x_{k}|\;\nabla f(x_{k})+y_{k})(w_{k})=\langle\nabla^{2}f(x_{k})w_{k},w_{k}\rangle+d^{2}g(x_{k}|\,y_{k})(w_{k}).

(3.31)

Since the function
g
satisfies the quadratic growth condition around
(\bar{x},\bar{y})
for
\mathcal{M}
, there exist
\varepsilon,\kappa>0
such that for any
(x,y)\in\mbox{\rm gph}\,\partial g\cap\mathbb{B}_{2\varepsilon}(\bar{x},\bar{y})
and
y\in\mathcal{M}
, the function
g
satisfies the quadratic growth condition at any
x
for
y
with the same modulus
\kappa>0
. Moreover, as
\partial g
has relative approximations by
\mathcal{M}
around
(\bar{x},\bar{y})
, we find
\lambda_{k}\in(1-\varepsilon,1]
,
\widehat{y}_{k}\in\partial g(x_{k})\cap\mathcal{M}
, and
\tilde{y}_{k}\in\partial g(x_{k})
such that
\lambda_{k}\widehat{y}_{k}+(1-\lambda_{k})\tilde{y}_{k}=y_{k}
when
k
is sufficiently large. Note that
d^{2}g(x_{k}|\cdot)(w_{k})
is a concave function. It follows from (
3.31
) that

\begin{array}[]{ll}\frac{1}{k}&\displaystyle\geq\langle\nabla^{2}f(x_{k})w_{k},w_{k}\rangle+\lambda_{k}d^{2}g(x_{k}|\,\widehat{y}_{k})(w_{k})+(1-\lambda_{k})d^{2}g(x_{k}|\,\tilde{y}_{k})(w_{k}).\\
&\displaystyle\geq\langle\nabla^{2}f(x_{k})w_{k},w_{k}\rangle+(1-\varepsilon)d^{2}g(x_{k}|\,\widehat{y}_{k})(w_{k}).\end{array}

This together with Lemma
3.5
implies that

\frac{1}{k}\geq\langle\nabla^{2}f(x_{k})w_{k},w_{k}\rangle+(1-\varepsilon)\kappa\left[{\rm dist}\,(w_{k};T_{(\partial g)^{-1}(\widehat{y}_{k})}(x_{k}))\right]^{2}.

Hence, there exists
u_{k}\in T_{(\partial g)^{-1}(\widehat{y}_{k})}(x_{k})
such that

\frac{1}{k}\geq\langle\nabla^{2}f(x_{k})w_{k},w_{k}\rangle+(1-\varepsilon)\kappa\|w_{k}-u_{k}\|^{2}\geq(1-\varepsilon)\kappa\|w_{k}-u_{k}\|^{2}.

By passing to subsequences, we may suppose that both
w_{k}
and
u_{k}
converge to some
w\in\mathbb{X}
with
\|w\|=1
. The later inequalities also imply that
0\geq\langle\nabla^{2}f(\bar{x})w,w\rangle
, i.e.,
w\in\mbox{\rm Ker}\,\nabla^{2}f(\bar{x})
. As
\widehat{y}_{k}\in\partial g(x_{k})\cap\mathcal{M}
and
u_{k}\in T_{(\partial g)^{-1}(\widehat{y}_{k})}(x_{k})
, we have
w\in\mathfrak{T}_{\mathcal{M}}
. It follows that
w\in\mbox{\rm Ker}\,\nabla^{2}f(\bar{x})\cap\mathfrak{T}=\{0\}
, which is a contradiction. Hence,
\bar{x}
must be a tilt-stable solution of problem (
1.8
).

\hfill\Box

As the contingent cone
T_{(\partial g)^{-1}(y)}(x)
is usually easy to compute, the set
\mathfrak{T}_{\mathcal{M}}
looks more computable than the generalized Hessian used in
[
36
]
and some other second-order structures in
[
9
,
19
,
42
]
to characterize tilt stability. These second-order structures often lead to the computation of the “sigma-term” or the “curvature” on regularizers
g
that are also complicated to calculate. The “Limsup” in (
3.30
) relies on the set
\mathcal{M}
, but at this stage, we do not know if
\mathfrak{T}_{\mathcal{M}}
actually depends on the choice of
\mathcal{M}
.

Remark 3.10

(Subspace containing derivative)
.

In
[
29
]
, Gfrerer and Outrata introduced a new characterization of tilt stability via the so-called
subspace containing derivative
(SCD, in brief)
[
29
, Definition 3.3 and Theorem 7.9]
for the subdifferential mapping
\partial\varphi
, which has strong connection with the
quadratic bundle
of
\varphi
introduced by Rockafellar
[
47
]
; see aslo
[
29
, Lemma 3.31 and Proposition 3.33]
and the discussion therein. The SCD of
\partial\varphi
takes the “Limsup” of the graphical derivative
D\partial\varphi
over the set
\mathcal{O}_{\partial\varphi}
of
(x,y)
at which the graph of
D\partial\varphi(x|\,y)
(or the tangent cone
T_{{\rm gph}\,\partial\varphi}(x,y)
) is a subspace. The “Limsup” in (
3.30
) shares some similar idea, as we also require
y
to belong to a special set
\mathcal{M}
. However, our set
\mathcal{M}
is mostly about “
y
” and relates more about the quadratic growth condition. Moreover, the tangent cone in (
3.30
) is taken on the subdifferential set
(\partial g)^{-1}(y)=\partial g^{*}(y)
, not on the whole graph of
\partial\varphi
. Although it turn outs that
\mathfrak{T}_{\mathcal{M}}
is a subspace in different classes of regularizers
g
in this paper, we do not know if the
T_{{\rm gph}\,\partial\varphi}(x,y)
is a subspace or not when
y\in\mathcal{M}\cap\partial g(x)
. SCDs on various norm functions (not including the nuclear norm) are computed in
[
26
]
. The characterization of tilt stability for the group Lasso problem (
3.34
) in Corollary
3.11
below can be obtained from
[
26
, Proposition 3.15 and Lemma 3.16]
together with the possible extension of
[
26
, Example 3.11]
to the
\ell_{1}/\ell_{2}
norm. But the characterization of Lipschitz stability for problem (
3.34
) in Theorem
3.13
is new. A deeper analysis understanding the connection between these two approaches and particularly between the set
\mathcal{O}_{\partial\varphi}
and
\mathcal{M}
will be helpful for further research in this direction.

As the function
g
satisfies the quadratic growth condition around
(\bar{x},\bar{y})
for
\mathcal{M}
as in Theorem
3.9
, it follows from
[
28
, Corollary 4.2]
that

T_{\partial g^{*}(y)}(x)=D\partial g^{*}(y,x)(0)

(3.32)

for any
(x,y)\in\mbox{\rm gph}\,\partial g\cap\mathcal{M}
around
(\bar{x},\bar{y})
. The limsup in (
3.30
) tells us that the set
\mathfrak{T}_{\mathcal{M}}
is a subset of
D^{\sharp}\partial g^{*}(\bar{y},\bar{x})(0)
, which is the so-called
outer limiting graphical derivative
of
\partial g^{*}
at
(\bar{y},\bar{x})

[
29
, Definition 3.8]
:

\displaystyle D^{\sharp}\partial g^{*}(\bar{y},\bar{x})(0)=\left\{w\in\mathbb{X}|\;\exists\,(w_{k},v_{k})\mathrel{{\mathop{\to}\limits}}(w,0),(x_{k},y_{k})\mathrel{{\mathop{\to}\limits}}(\bar{x},\bar{y}):w_{k}\in D\partial g^{*}(y_{k},x_{k})(v_{k})\right\}.

(3.33)

For the particular class of convex piecewise linear-quadratic functions, it is not hard to show that
\mathfrak{T}_{\mathcal{M}}
and
D^{\sharp}\partial g^{*}(\bar{y},\bar{x})(0)
are the same, due to the piecewise polyhedral property of its subdifferential and the choice
\mathcal{M}
as the whole space as discussed in Example
3.2
. “Whether are they equal for other classes of
g
?” is the question that we do not have the complete answer yet, as the computation of (
3.33
) is rather involved in our opinion. There are two main differences in their definitions: the choice of sequence
v_{k}
in (
3.33
) is fixed as
0
in (
3.30
) and
y_{k}
in (
3.33
) lies on a special set
\mathcal{M}
in (
3.30
). They essentially make the set
\mathfrak{T}_{\mathcal{M}}
more computable in several important cases in this paper; for example, when
g
is the nuclear norm, it is still hard to obtain the computation of
D\partial g^{*}(y_{k},x_{k})(v_{k})
before taking the limit as in (
3.33
), but the formula
D\partial g^{*}(y_{k},x_{k})(0)
is quite simple (Lemma
4.28
) and allows us to fully calculate the formula
\mathfrak{T}_{\mathcal{M}}
in (
4.30
); see our Section 4 for further details. Moreover, if the computation of
D\partial g^{*}(y_{k},x_{k})(v_{k})
is available, we may use the characterization in Theorem 2.3(iii) from
[
9
]
to derive a full characterization of tilt stability.

\triangle

Corollary 3.11

(Tilt stability for group Lasso problem)
.

Consider the following group Lasso problem

\min_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}\quad f(x)+\|x\|_{1,2},

(3.34)

where
f:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}
is a convex function that is continuously twice differentiable and
\|\cdot\|_{1,2}
is the
\ell_{1}/\ell_{2}
norm defined in (
3.3
). Then
\bar{x}
is a tilt-stable solution if and only if
\bar{y}=-\nabla f(\bar{x})\in\partial\|\bar{x}\|_{1,2}
and

\mbox{\rm Ker}\,\nabla^{2}f(\bar{x})\cap\mathcal{V}=\{0\},

(3.35)

where
\mathcal{V}
is the subspace of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
defined by

\mathcal{V}\mathrel{{\mathop{=}\limits}}\{w\in\mathbb{R}^{n}|\;w_{J}\in\mathbb{R}\{\bar{y}_{J}\}\;\;\mbox{if}\;\;J\in\mathcal{K}\;\;\mbox{and}\;\;w_{J}=0\;\;if\;\;J\in\mathcal{H}\}

(3.36)

with
\mathcal{K}=\{J\in\mathcal{J}|\;\|\bar{y}_{J}\|=1\}
and
\mathcal{H}=\{J\in\mathcal{J}|\;\|\bar{y}_{J}\|_{J}<1\}
.

Proof.

By Example
3.3
, Example
3.7
, and Theorem
3.9
, we only need to show that the set
\mathfrak{T}
defined in (
3.30
) is
\mathcal{V}
. Indeed, for any
(x,y)\in\mbox{\rm gph}\,\partial\|\cdot\|_{1,2}
with
y\in\mathcal{M}_{1,2}
, we obtain from (
3.3
) that

\begin{array}[]{ll}T_{(\partial\|\cdot\|_{1,2})^{-1}(y_{J})}(x_{J})&=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\mathbb{R}_{+}\{y_{J}\}&\mbox{if}\;\;x_{J}=0\\
\mathbb{R}\{y_{J}\}&\mbox{if}\;\;x_{J}\neq 0.\end{array}\right.\subset\mathbb{R}\{y_{J}\}\quad\mbox{for any}\quad J\in\mathcal{K}\\
T_{0_{J}}(x_{J})=0_{J}\quad\mbox{for any}\quad J\in\mathcal{H}.\end{array}\right.\end{array}

(3.37)

It is easy to derive from (
3.30
) that
\mathfrak{T}_{\mathcal{M}_{1,2}}\subset\mathcal{V}
. To verify the opposite inclusion, define
\mathcal{I}\mathrel{{\mathop{=}\limits}}\{J\in\mathcal{J}|\;\bar{x}_{J}\neq 0\}\subset\mathcal{K}
and

x^{k}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}(x^{k})_{J}=\bar{x}_{J}&\mbox{if}\;\;J\in\mathcal{I}\cup\mathcal{H}\\
(x^{k})_{J}=\frac{1}{k}\bar{y}_{J}&\mbox{if}\;\;J\in\mathcal{K}\setminus\mathcal{I}.\end{array}\right.\quad\quad\mbox{and}\quad y^{k}=\bar{y}.

(3.38)

Note that
(x^{k},y^{k})\to(\bar{x},\bar{y})
,
y^{k}\in\partial\|x^{k}\|_{1,2}\cap\mathcal{M}_{1,2}
, and from (
3.37
) that

T_{(\partial\|\cdot\|_{1,2})^{-1}(y^{k})}(x^{k})=T_{(\partial\|\cdot\|_{1,2})^{-1}(\bar{y})}(x^{k})=\mathcal{V},

(3.39)

which implies that
\mathcal{V}\subset\mathfrak{T}_{\mathcal{M}_{1,2}}
and thus
\mathfrak{T}_{\mathcal{M}_{1,2}}=\mathcal{V}
. By Theorem
3.9
,
\bar{x}
is a tilt-stable solution of problem (
3.34
) if and only if condition (
3.35
) is satisfied.

\hfill\Box

When the
\ell_{1}/\ell_{2}
norm reduces to the
\ell_{1}
norm for Lasso problem, the above result can be obtained easily from
[
26
, Proposition 3.15, Lemma 3.16, and Example 3.8]
with a different approach via the SC derivatives. For the case of
\ell_{1}/\ell_{2}
norm as in Corollary
3.11
, the characterization of tilt stability in (
3.35
) can be also derived from
[
26
, Proposition 3.15 and Lemma 3.16]
by computing the SC derivative for the
\ell_{1}/\ell_{2}
norm, which is just the separable sum of
\ell_{2}
norm functions
[
26
, Example 3.11]
. The choice of
(x_{k},y_{k})
in the above computation is to make
T_{(\partial\|\cdot\|_{1,2})^{-1}(y^{k})}(x^{k})
to be a subspace; see also the proof of our Theorem
4.7
for the case of nuclear norm. This is close to the idea of using the
subspace containing derivative
in
[
29
]
. The difference is that we do not compute the tangent cone to the whole graph of
\partial g
as in
[
29
]
, but only “
y
” part of it.

As discussed in the Introduction, a particular problem of (
1.8
) is

\mathcal{P}(b,\mu)\qquad\min_{x\in\mathbb{X}}\quad\frac{1}{2\mu}\|\Phi x-b\|^{2}+g(x),

(3.40)

where
\Phi
is a linear operator between two Euclidean spaces
\mathbb{X}
and
\mathbb{Y}
and
(b,\mu)\in\mathbb{Y}\times\mathbb{R}_{++}
is a parameter pair around some fixed
(\bar{b},\bar{\mu})\in\mathbb{Y}\times\mathbb{R}_{++}
.
The solution set of this problem is defined in (
1.2
) and (
1.3
) by

\displaystyle S(b,\mu)={\rm argmin}\left\{\frac{1}{2\mu}\|\Phi x-b\|^{2}+g(x)|\;x\in\mathbb{X}\right\}=\left\{x\in\mathbb{X}|\;0\in\frac{1}{\mu}\Phi^{*}(\Phi x-b)+\partial g(x)\right\}.

(3.41)

Next we will provide some analysis for Lipschitz continuity of this solution mapping. It is similar to the discussion after Definition
2.2
that in our convex settings, the mapping
S
is single-valued and Lipschitz continuous around
(\bar{b},\bar{\mu})
for
\bar{x}=S(\bar{b},\bar{\mu})
if and only if it has a Lipschitz continuous single-valued localization around
(\bar{b},\bar{\mu})
for
\bar{x}=S(\bar{b},\bar{\mu})
. Moreover, since the function
h(x,b,\mu)=\frac{1}{\mu}\Phi^{*}(\Phi x-b)
is strictly differentiable at
(\bar{x},(\bar{b},\bar{\mu}))
,
S
has single-valued and Lipschitz continuous around
(\bar{b},\bar{\mu})
for
\bar{x}
if the following mapping

X^{*}\ni v\mapsto G(v)\mathrel{{\mathop{=}\limits}}\{x\in\mathbb{X}|\;v\in h(\bar{x},\bar{b},\bar{\mu})+\nabla_{x}h(\bar{x},\bar{b},\bar{\mu})(x-\bar{x})+\partial g(x)\}

single-valued and Lipschitz continuous around
0
for
\bar{x}=G(0)
; see, e.g.,
[
21
, Corrollary 2B.9]
. As
h
is a linear function with respect to
x
, we have

h(\bar{x},\bar{b},\bar{\mu})+\nabla_{x}h(\bar{x},\bar{b},\bar{\mu})(x-\bar{x})=h(x,\bar{b},\bar{\mu})=\frac{1}{\bar{\mu}}\Phi^{*}(\Phi x-\bar{b}).

It follows that

G(v)={\rm argmin}\left\{\frac{1}{2\bar{\mu}}\|\Phi x-\bar{b}\|^{2}+g(x)-\langle v,x\rangle|\;x\in\mathbb{X}\right\}.

Thus
G
is single-valued and Lipschitz continuous around
0
for
\bar{x}=G(0)
if and only if
\bar{x}
is a tilt-stable solution of problem
P(\bar{b},\bar{\mu})
.
These analysis together with Theorem
3.9
gives us a sufficient condition for local Lipschitz continuity of the solution mapping
S
as below.

Corollary 3.12

(Lipschitz stability of solution mapping)
.

Suppose that
\bar{x}
is an optimal solution of problem
\mathcal{P}(\bar{b},\bar{\mu})
in (
3.40
). Suppose further that there exists a set
\mathcal{M}\subset\mathbb{X}^{*}
containing
\bar{y}=-\frac{1}{\bar{\mu}}\Phi^{*}(\Phi\bar{x}-\bar{b})
such that the function
g
satisfies the quadratic growth condition around
(\bar{x},\bar{y})
for
\mathcal{M}
and that the subdifferential mapping
\partial g
has relative approximations by
\mathcal{M}
around
(\bar{x},\bar{y})
. Then the solution mapping
S
is single-valued and Lipschitz continuous around
(\bar{b},\bar{\mu})
with
\bar{x}=S(\bar{b},\bar{\mu})
provided that

\mbox{\rm Ker}\,\Phi\cap\mathfrak{T}_{\mathcal{M}}=\{0\}.

(3.42)

It is worth noting that (
3.42
) and tilt stability of problem
P(\bar{b},\bar{\mu})
are only sufficient for the Lipschitz stability of
S
in general. For some special classes of
g

[
21
,
16
,
39
,
49
]
, sufficient conditions for Lipschitz stability of
S
can be obtained via the
Robinson’s strong regularity
introduced in
[
44
]
. But again, these two kinds of stability are different; see, e.g.,
[
21
]
for further details and discussions. Moreover, Robinson’s strong regularity is usually characterized via the so-called
Strong Second-Order Sufficient Condition
that may contain some second-order structures that are also involved to compute and verify, e.g., when
g
is the nuclear norm. Our approach is different via tilt stability. It does require some special properties on
g
, but the most important thing is condition (
3.42
) can be fully calculated.

Next we will show that condition (
3.42
) is also necessary for the Lipschitz stability of solution mapping
S
in (
3.41
) when the function
g
is the
\ell_{1}/\ell_{2}
norm. Indeed, both of them are equivalent to the single-valuedness of solution mapping
S
. This is quite a surprise, as Lipschitz continuity is not a free property for single-valued mappings in general.

Theorem 3.13

(Full characterization of Lipschitz stability for group Lasso problem)
.

Consider the following parametric group Lasso problem

\min_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}\quad\frac{1}{2\mu}\|\Phi x-b\|^{2}+\|x\|_{1,2},

(3.43)

where
\Phi:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}^{m}
is a linear operator and
(b,\mu)\in\mathbb{R}^{m}\times\mathbb{R}_{++}
is a parameter pair around some fixed
(\bar{b},\bar{\mu})\in\mathbb{R}^{m}\times\mathbb{R}_{++}
. The following are equivalent:

(i)

The following Strong Sufficient Condition holds

\mbox{\rm Ker}\,\Phi\cap\mathcal{V}=\{0\},

(3.44)

where
\mathcal{V}
is defined in (
3.36
) with
\bar{y}=-\frac{1}{\bar{\mu}}\Phi^{*}(\Phi\bar{x}-\bar{b})
.

(ii)

The solution mapping
S
of problem (
3.43
) is single-valued and Lipschitz continuous around
(\bar{b},\bar{\mu})
with
S(\bar{b},\bar{\mu})=\bar{x}
.

(iii)

The solution mapping
S
of problem (
3.43
) is single-valued around
(\bar{b},\bar{\mu})
with
S(\bar{b},\bar{\mu})=\bar{x}
.

Proof.

4
Geometric characterizations of Lipschitz stability for nuclear norm minimization problems

In this section, we consider the following nuclear norm minimization problem

\min_{X\in\mathbb{R}^{n_{1}\times n_{2}}}\qquad f(X)+\|X\|_{*},

(4.1)

where
f:\mathbb{R}^{n_{1}\times n_{2}}\to\mathbb{R}
is a twice continuously differentiable convex function and
g(X)\mathrel{{\mathop{=}\limits}}\|X\|_{*}
is the nuclear norm of
X\in\mathbb{R}^{n_{1}\times n_{2}}
, which is the sum of its singular values
\sigma_{1}(X),\ldots,\sigma_{n_{1}}(X)
with nonincreasing order. For any
\overline{X}\in\mathbb{R}^{n_{1}\times n_{2}}
(
n_{1}\leq n_{2}
), the singular valued decomposition (SVD) of
\overline{X}
is written by

\overline{X}=U\begin{pmatrix}\Sigma&0\\
0&0\end{pmatrix}_{n_{1}\times n_{2}}V^{T},

where
U\in\mathbb{R}^{n_{1}\times n_{1}}
and
V\in\mathbb{R}^{n_{2}\times n_{2}}
are orthogonal matrices,
\Sigma={\rm Diag}\,\{\sigma_{1},\sigma_{2},\ldots,\sigma_{r}\}
is the
r\times r
diagonal matrix with
\sigma_{1}\geq\sigma_{2}\ldots\geq\sigma_{r}>0
being all positive singular values of
\overline{X}
, and
r
is the rank of
\overline{X}
. The set
\mathcal{O}(\overline{X})
contains all such pairs
(U,V)
for
\overline{X}
.

The following lemma provides some well-known formulas for subdifferential of the nuclear norm; see, e.g.,
[
53
, Example 1 and Example 2]
and
[
57
, Proposition 10]
. Recall that
\|X\|
is the spectral norm of
X\in\mathbb{R}^{n_{1}\times n_{2}}
, which is the largest singular value of
X
.

Lemma 4.1

(Subdifferential of the nuclear norm)
.

The subdifferential to nuclear norm at
\overline{X}\in\mathbb{R}^{n_{1}\times n_{2}}
is computed by

\partial\|\overline{X}\|_{*}=\left\{U\begin{pmatrix}\mathbb{I}_{r}&0\\
0&W\end{pmatrix}V^{T}|\;\|W\|\leq 1\right\}\quad\mbox{for any}\quad(U,V)\in\mathcal{O}(\overline{X}).

(4.2)

Moreover,
\overline{Y}\in\partial\|\overline{X}\|_{*}
if and only if
\|\overline{Y}\|\leq 1
and

\|\overline{X}\|_{*}=\langle\overline{Y},\overline{X}\rangle.

(4.3)

Furthermore, for any
\overline{Y}\in\mathbb{B}\mathrel{{\mathop{=}\limits}}\{Z\in\mathbb{R}^{n_{1}\times n_{2}}|\;\|Z\|\leq 1\}
, we have

\partial g^{*}(\overline{Y})=N_{\mathbb{B}}(\overline{Y})=\overline{U}\begin{pmatrix}\mathbb{S}_{+}^{p(\overline{Y})}&0\\
0&0\end{pmatrix}\overline{V}^{T}\quad\mbox{for any}\quad(\overline{U},\overline{V})\in\mathcal{O}(\overline{Y}),

(4.4)

where
p(\overline{Y})
is defined by

p(\overline{Y})\mathrel{{\mathop{=}\limits}}\#\{i\in\{1,\ldots,n_{1}\}|\;\sigma_{i}(\overline{Y})=1\}.

(4.5)

When
\overline{Y}\in\partial\|\overline{X}\|_{*}
,
\overline{X}
and
\overline{Y}
have the
simultaneous
ordered singular value decomposition in the sense that there exists
(\overline{U},\overline{V})\in\mathcal{O}(\overline{X})\cap\mathcal{O}(\overline{Y})
such that

\overline{X}=\overline{U}\begin{pmatrix}\Sigma&0\\
0&0\end{pmatrix}\overline{V}^{T}\qquad\mbox{and}\qquad\overline{Y}=\overline{U}\begin{pmatrix}\mathbb{I}_{p}&0&0\\
0&\Lambda&0\end{pmatrix}\overline{V}^{T},

(4.6)

where
p=p(\overline{Y})
and
\Lambda\in\mathbb{R}^{(n_{1}-p)\times(n_{1}-p)}
is the diagonal matrix containing all singular values of
\overline{Y}
in
[0,1)
;
see, e.g.,
[
23
]
for the way of constructing the pair
(\overline{U},\overline{V})\in\mathcal{O}(\overline{X})
. In the following result, we show that the nuclear norm satisfies the quadratic growth condition for some nontrivial set
\mathcal{M}
. Local quadratic growth condition of the nuclear norm was established in
[
57
]
without providing the exact formula for the modulus. Our approach here is completely different via convex analysis. It reveals not only the modulus but also the
global
quadratic growth condition of the nuclear norm.

Lemma 4.2

(Quadratic growth condition for a set of the nuclear norm)
.

Let
(\overline{X},\overline{Y})\in\mbox{\rm gph}\,\partial\|\cdot\|_{*}
. Then we have

\displaystyle\|X\|_{*}-\|\overline{X}\|_{*}-\langle\overline{Y},X-\overline{X}\rangle\geq\frac{1-\gamma^{2}}{2\|X\|_{*}(1+(1+\gamma)^{2})}\left[{\rm dist}\,(X;(\partial\|\cdot\|_{*})^{-1}(\overline{Y}))\right]^{2}

(4.7)

for any
X\in\mathbb{R}^{n_{1}\times n_{2}}\setminus\{0\}
,
where
\gamma
is the subdominant singular value defined by

\gamma=\gamma(\overline{Y})\mathrel{{\mathop{=}\limits}}\max\{\sigma(\overline{Y})\setminus\{1\},0\}<1.

(4.8)

Consequently, we have

\|X\|_{*}-\|\overline{X}\|_{*}-\langle\overline{Y},X-\overline{X}\rangle\geq\frac{1-\gamma}{5\|X\|_{*}}\left[{\rm dist}\,(X;(\partial\|\cdot\|_{*})^{-1}(\overline{Y}))\right]^{2}\quad\mbox{for all}\quad X\in\mathbb{R}^{n_{1}\times n_{2}}\setminus\{0\}.

(4.9)

Finally, the nuclear norm satisfies the quadratic growth condition around
(\overline{X},\overline{Y})
for the following set

\mathcal{M}_{*}\mathrel{{\mathop{=}\limits}}\{Y\in\mathbb{R}^{n_{1}\times n_{2}}|\;\sigma_{1}(Y)=\ldots=\sigma_{p}(Y)=1\},

(4.10)

where
p=p(\overline{Y})
is the number of singular values of
\overline{Y}
that are equal to
1
defined in (
4.5
).

Proof.

Suppose that
U\overline{\Sigma}V^{T}
is the SVD of
\overline{Y}
with
\overline{\Sigma}=\begin{pmatrix}\mathbb{I}_{p}&0\\
0&\Sigma\end{pmatrix}_{n_{1}\times n_{2}}
,
(U,V)\in\mathcal{O}(\overline{Y})
, and
\|\Sigma\|<1
, where
p
is from (
4.5
).
Pick any
X\in\mathbb{R}^{n_{1}\times n_{2}}\setminus\{0\}
and define
\widehat{X}=\|X\|_{*}^{-1}X
. As
(\partial\|\cdot\|_{*})^{-1}(\overline{Y})
is a convex cone by (
4.4
) and
\|\overline{X}\|_{*}=\langle\overline{Y},\overline{X}\rangle
by (
4.3
), inequality (
4.7
) is equivalent to

\|\widehat{X}\|_{*}-\langle\overline{Y},\widehat{X}\rangle\geq\frac{c}{2}\left[{\rm dist}\,(\widehat{X};(\partial\|\cdot\|_{*})^{-1}(\overline{Y}))\right]^{2}\quad\mbox{with}\quad c\mathrel{{\mathop{=}\limits}}\frac{(1-\gamma^{2})}{1+(1+\gamma)^{2}}.

(4.11)

Define
\widetilde{X}=U^{T}\widehat{X}V
and note that

\|\widehat{X}\|_{*}-\langle\overline{Y},\widehat{X}\rangle=\|\widetilde{X}\|_{*}-{\rm Tr}(V\overline{\Sigma}^{T}U^{T}\widehat{X})=\|\widetilde{X}\|_{*}-{\rm Tr}(\overline{\Sigma}^{T}U^{T}\widehat{X}V)=\|\widetilde{X}\|_{*}-\langle\overline{\Sigma},\widetilde{X}\rangle.

Moreover, it follows from (
4.4
) that

{\rm dist}\,(\widehat{X};(\partial\|\cdot\|_{*})^{-1}(\overline{Y}))={\rm dist}\,\left(\widetilde{X};\begin{pmatrix}\mathbb{S}^{p}_{+}&0\\
0&0\end{pmatrix}\right).

Thus, to prove (
4.7
), we only need to show that

h(X)\mathrel{{\mathop{=}\limits}}\|X\|_{*}-\langle\overline{\Sigma},X\rangle+\delta_{B^{*}}(X)\geq k(X)\mathrel{{\mathop{=}\limits}}\frac{c}{2}\left[{\rm dist}\left(X;\begin{pmatrix}{\mathbb{S}}^{p}_{+}&0\\
0&0\end{pmatrix}\right)\right]^{2}\quad\mbox{for}\quad X\in\mathbb{R}^{n_{1}\times n_{2}},

(4.12)

where
B_{*}
is the unit ball with the nuclear norm, i.e.,
{\mathbb{B}}_{*}\mathrel{{\mathop{=}\limits}}\{X\in\mathbb{R}^{n_{1}\times n_{2}}|\;\|X\|_{*}\leq 1\}
. By the Biconjugate Theorem of convex analysis, inequality (
4.12
) is equivalent to

h^{*}(Y)\leq k^{*}(Y)\quad\mbox{for all}\quad Y\in\mathbb{R}^{n_{1}\times n_{2}}.

(4.13)

Note that

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{ll}h^{*}(Y)&=\displaystyle\sup_{X\in{\mathbb{B}}_{*}}\langle Y+\overline{\Sigma},X\rangle-\|X\|_{*}=\max\{\|Y+\overline{\Sigma}\|-1,0\}=\displaystyle(\|Y+\overline{\Sigma}\|-1)_{+}.\end{array}

By writing
Y=\begin{pmatrix}A&B\\
C&D\end{pmatrix}\in\mathbb{R}^{n_{1}\times n_{2}}
as a block matrix with
A\in\mathbb{R}^{p\times p}
, we have

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{ll}k^{*}(Y)&=\displaystyle\sup_{X\in\mathbb{R}^{n_{1}\times n_{2}}}\sup_{Z\in{\mathbb{S}}^{p}_{+}}\langle Y,X\rangle-\frac{c}{2}\left\|X-\begin{pmatrix}Z&0\\
0&0\end{pmatrix}\right\|_{F}^{2}\\
&=\displaystyle\sup_{Z\in{\mathbb{S}}^{p}_{+}}\sup_{X\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times n}}\left\langle A,Z\right\rangle+\langle Y,X-\begin{pmatrix}Z&0\\
0&0\end{pmatrix}\rangle-\frac{c}{2}\left\|X-\begin{pmatrix}Z&0\\
0&0\end{pmatrix}\right\|^{2}_{F}\\
&=\displaystyle\sup_{Z\in{\mathbb{S}}^{p}_{+}}\langle A,Z\rangle+\dfrac{1}{2c}\|Y\|^{2}_{F}\\
&=\displaystyle\sup_{Z\in{\mathbb{S}}^{p}_{+}}\frac{1}{2}\langle A+A^{T},Z\rangle+\dfrac{1}{2c}\|Y\|^{2}_{F}\\
&=\displaystyle\delta_{{\mathbb{S}}^{p}_{-}}(A+A^{T})+\dfrac{1}{2c}\|Y\|^{2}_{F},\end{array}

where the last equality holds due to
A+A^{T}\in\mathbb{S}^{p}
. By (
4
) and (
4
), we only need to verify (
4.13
) when
A+A^{T}\in{\mathbb{S}}^{p}_{-}
. Recall that for a symmetric matrix
Z\in\mathbb{R}^{n_{2}\times n_{2}}
, we write
Z\succeq 0
if
Z\in\mathbb{S}^{n_{2}}_{+}
and
Z\preceq 0
if
-Z\succeq 0
. Note from the representation
\overline{\Sigma}=\begin{pmatrix}\mathbb{I}_{p}&0\\
0&\Sigma\end{pmatrix}
that

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{ll}(Y+\overline{\Sigma})^{T}(Y+\overline{\Sigma})&=Y^{T}Y+\overline{\Sigma}^{T}Y+Y^{T}\overline{\Sigma}+\overline{\Sigma}^{T}\overline{\Sigma}\\
&=Y^{T}Y+\begin{pmatrix}A+A^{T}&B+C^{T}\Sigma\\
B^{T}+\Sigma^{T}C&\Sigma^{T}D+D^{T}\Sigma\end{pmatrix}+\begin{pmatrix}\mathbb{I}_{p}&0\\
0&\Sigma^{T}\Sigma\end{pmatrix}\\
&\preceq(\|Y\|^{2}+1)\mathbb{I}_{n_{2}}+\begin{pmatrix}0&B+\Sigma^{T}C\\
B^{T}+C^{T}\Sigma&\Sigma^{T}D+D^{T}\Sigma+\Sigma^{T}\Sigma-\mathbb{I}_{n_{2}-p}\end{pmatrix}.\end{array}

Next we claim that

\begin{pmatrix}0&B+\Sigma^{T}C\\
B^{T}+C^{T}\Sigma&\Sigma^{T}D+D^{T}\Sigma+\Sigma^{T}\Sigma-\mathbb{I}_{n_{2}-p}\end{pmatrix}\preceq d\|Y\|^{2}\mathbb{I}_{n_{2}}\quad\mbox{with}\quad d\mathrel{{\mathop{=}\limits}}\dfrac{\gamma^{2}+(1+\gamma)^{2}}{(1-\gamma^{2})},

(4.26)

which is equivalent to

\begin{pmatrix}d\|Y\|^{2}\mathbb{I}_{p}&-B-\Sigma^{T}C\\
-B^{T}-C^{T}\Sigma&(d\|Y\|^{2}+1)\mathbb{I}_{n_{2}-p}-\Sigma^{T}D-D^{T}\Sigma-\Sigma^{T}\Sigma\end{pmatrix}\succeq 0.

If
\|Y\|=0
, the latter is trivial as
\mathbb{I}_{n_{2}-p}\succeq\Sigma^{T}\Sigma
and
D=0
. If
\|Y\|>0
, by Schur complement, the above inequality means

(d\|Y\|^{2}+1)\mathbb{I}_{n_{2}-p}-\Sigma^{T}D-D^{T}\Sigma-\Sigma^{T}\Sigma-\frac{1}{d\|Y\|^{2}}(B^{T}+C^{T}\Sigma)(B+\Sigma^{T}C)\succeq 0.

(4.27)

Note that
\gamma=\|\Sigma\|
and
\max\{\|B\|,\|C\|,\|D\|\}\leq\|Y\|
. As
-\Sigma^{T}D-D^{T}\Sigma
is symmetric, we have

-\Sigma^{T}D-D^{T}\Sigma\succeq-\|\Sigma^{T}D+D^{T}\Sigma\|\mathbb{I}_{n_{2}-p}\succeq-2\|\Sigma^{T}D\|\mathbb{I}_{n_{2}-p}\geq-2\|\Sigma^{T}\|\cdot\|D\|\mathbb{I}_{n_{2}-p}\succeq-2\gamma\|Y\|\mathbb{I}_{n_{2}-p}.

Similarly,

-(B^{T}+C^{T}\Sigma)(B+\Sigma^{T}C)\succeq-\|B^{T}+C^{T}\Sigma\|^{2}\mathbb{I}_{n_{2}-p}\succeq-(\|B\|+\|C\|\cdot\|\Sigma\|)^{2}\mathbb{I}_{n_{2}-p}\succeq-(1+\gamma)^{2}\|Y\|^{2}\mathbb{I}_{n_{2}-p}.

These two inequalities imply that
the left-hand side of (
4.27
) is “
\succeq
” than

\begin{array}[]{ll}\left[(d\|Y\|^{2}+1)-2\gamma\|Y\|-\gamma^{2}-\frac{(1+\gamma)^{2}}{d}\right]\mathbb{I}_{n_{2}-p}&=\left[d\|Y\|^{2}-2\gamma\|Y\|+\left(1-\gamma^{2}-\frac{(1+\gamma)^{2}}{d}\right)\right]\mathbb{I}_{n_{2}-p}\\
&\succeq 2\left[\sqrt{d\left(1-\gamma^{2}-\frac{(1+\gamma)^{2}}{d}\right)}-\gamma)\right]\|Y\|\mathbb{I}_{n_{2}-p}=0,\end{array}

where the last equality holds due to the choice of
d=\dfrac{\gamma^{2}+(1+\gamma)^{2}}{(1-\gamma^{2})}
. This verifies (
4.27
) and thus (
4.26
). It follows from (
4
) and (
4.26
) that

(Y+\overline{\Sigma})^{T}(Y+\overline{\Sigma})\preceq((1+d)\|Y\|^{2}+1)\mathbb{I}_{n_{2}},

which means

\|Y+\overline{\Sigma}\|-1\leq\dfrac{(1+d)\|Y\|^{2}}{\sqrt{(1+d)\|Y\|^{2}+1}+1}\leq\dfrac{(1+d)\|Y\|^{2}}{2}=\dfrac{(1+(1+\gamma)^{2})}{2(1-\gamma^{2})}\|Y\|_{F}^{2}.

This together with (
4
) and (
4
) verifies (
4.13

4.12

4.7
).

To justify (

\frac{1-\gamma^{2}}{2(1+(1+\gamma)^{2})}=\frac{1-\gamma}{2}\cdot\frac{1+\gamma}{1+(1+\gamma)^{2}}\geq\frac{1-\gamma}{2}\cdot\frac{2}{5}=\frac{1-\gamma}{5},

as

. Combining this with (
4.7
) gives us (
4.9
).

Finally, we prove the quadratic growth condition of
\|\cdot\|_{*}
around
(\overline{X},\overline{Y})
for the set
\mathcal{M}_{*}
defined in (
4.10
). Pick any
\varepsilon>0
with
\varepsilon<1-\gamma
and take any
(X,Y)\in\mbox{\rm gph}\,\partial\|\cdot\|_{*}\cap\mathbb{B}_{\varepsilon}(\overline{X},\overline{Y})
with
Y\in\mathcal{M}_{*}
. It is well-known that
\sigma_{p+1}(\cdot)
is Lipschitz continuous with modulus
1
(Weyl’s inequality). We have

\sigma_{p+1}(Y)\leq\sigma_{p+1}(\overline{Y})+\|Y-\overline{Y}\|_{F}\leq\gamma+\varepsilon<1,

which tells us that
p(Y)=p(\overline{Y})
. For any
U\in\mathbb{B}_{\varepsilon}(X)\subset\mathbb{B}_{2\varepsilon}(\overline{X})
, it follows from (
4.9
) that

\begin{array}[]{ll}\|U\|_{*}-\|X\|-\langle Y,U-X\rangle&\displaystyle\geq\frac{1-\gamma(Y)}{5\|U\|_{*}}\left[{\rm dist}\,(U;(\partial\|\cdot\|_{*})^{-1}(Y))\right]^{2}\\
&\displaystyle\geq\frac{1-\gamma-\varepsilon}{5(\|\overline{X}\|_{*}+2\varepsilon)}\left[{\rm dist}\,(U;(\partial\|\cdot\|_{*})^{-1}(Y))\right]^{2}.\end{array}

As
\varepsilon>0
can be chosen sufficiently small,
\|\cdot\|_{*}
satisfies the quadratic growth condition around
(\overline{X},\overline{Y})
for
\mathcal{M}_{*}
with some constant modulus .

Remark 4.3

(Improving the quadratic growth condition (

The subdominant singular value

Proposition 4.4

(Relative approximations of subgradients of the nuclear norm)
.

Let
\overline{Y}\in\partial\|\overline{X}\|_{*}
. Then the subdifferential mapping
\partial\|\cdot\|_{*}
has relative approximations by the set
\mathcal{M}_{*}
defined in (

).

Proof.

Pick any
(X,Y)\in\mbox{\rm gph}\,\partial\|\cdot\|_{*}\cap\mathbb{B}_{\varepsilon}(\overline{X},\overline{Y})
with
\varepsilon>0
. Since
\|Y\|\leq 1
and
\sigma_{k}(Y)
are Lipschitz continuous for any
k
, we have
{\rm rank}\,(\overline{X})\leq{\rm rank}\,(X)\leq p(Y)\leq p(\overline{Y})

when
\varepsilon
is sufficiently small, where the
p(Y)
counting how many singular values of
Y
are equal to
1
; see (
4.5
).
If
p(Y)=p(\overline{Y})
, i.e.,
Y\in\mathcal{M}_{*}
, a relative approximation of
Y
by
\mathcal{M}_{*}
is
Y
. If
q=p(Y)<p(\overline{Y})
, suppose that a SVD of
Y
is

Y=U\begin{pmatrix}\mathbb{I}_{q}&0&0\\
0&\Sigma&0\end{pmatrix}V^{T},

where
\Sigma={\rm diag}(\sigma_{q+1},\ldots,\sigma_{n_{1}})
with
\sigma_{1}\geq\ldots\geq\sigma_{p}\geq\ldots\sigma_{n_{1}}
being all singular values of
Y
and
(U,V)\in\mathcal{O}(X)\cap\mathcal{O}(Y)
. Let us set

\widehat{Y}=U\begin{pmatrix}\mathbb{I}_{p}&0&0\\
0&\widehat{\Lambda}&0\end{pmatrix}V^{T}\qquad\mbox{and}\qquad\widetilde{Y}=U\begin{pmatrix}\mathbb{I}_{q}&0&0\\
0&\widetilde{\Lambda}&0\end{pmatrix}V^{T},

where
\widehat{\Lambda}
and

\widehat{\Lambda}={\rm Diag}\,(\sigma_{p+1},\ldots,\sigma_{n_{1}})\qquad\mbox{and}\qquad\widetilde{\Lambda}=\mbox{\rm Diag}\,\left(\frac{\sigma_{q+1}-\sigma_{p}}{1-\sigma_{p}},\ldots,\frac{\sigma_{p-1}-\sigma_{p}}{1-\sigma_{q}},0,\sigma_{p+1},\ldots,\sigma_{n_{1}}\right).

As
r\leq q
, observe that both

by Lemma

. Moreover,
\widehat{Y}\in\mathcal{M}_{*}
and

.
As

is Lipschitz continuous with modulus

, we have

1-\sigma_{p}=|1-\sigma_{p}|=|\sigma_{p}(\overline{Y})-\sigma_{p}(Y)|\leq\|\overline{Y}-Y\|_{F}\leq\varepsilon,

which tells us that
\sigma_{p}\in(1-\varepsilon,1)
.
Hence
\widehat{Y}
is a relative approximation of

by
\mathcal{M}_{*}
. Thus

has relative approximations by the set
\mathcal{M}_{*}
.
\hfill\Box

where

and

We are ready to establish the main result in this section, which gives a complete characterization of tilt stability of problem (
4.7
).

Theorem 4.6
.

Suppose that
\overline{X}
is an optimal solution of problem (
4.1
), i.e.,
\overline{Y}=-\nabla f(\overline{X})\in\partial\|\overline{X}\|_{*}
. Hence
\overline{X}
is a tilt-stable solution of problem (
4.1
) if and only if the Strong Sufficient Condition holds:

\mbox{\rm Ker}\,\nabla^{2}f(\overline{X})\cap\overline{U}\begin{pmatrix}\mathbb{S}^{p}&0\\
0&0\end{pmatrix}\overline{V}^{T}=\{0\}\qquad\mbox{with}\qquad p=p(\overline{Y}),

(4.29)

where
(\overline{U},\overline{V})\in\mathcal{O}(\overline{X})\cap\mathcal{O}(\overline{Y})
are from the simultaneous ordered SVD of
\overline{X}
and
\overline{Y}
in (
4.6
).

Proof.

According Theorem
4.2
, Proposition
4.4
, and Theorem
3.9
,
\overline{X}
is a tilt-stable solution if and only if condition (
3.29
) is satisfied. We claim that

\mathfrak{T}_{\mathcal{M}_{*}}=\mathop{{\rm Lim}\,{\rm sup}}_{(X,Y)\mathrel{{\mathop{\longrightarrow}\limits}}(\overline{X},\overline{Y})}T_{(\partial\|\cdot\|_{*})^{-1}(Y)}(X)=\overline{U}\begin{pmatrix}\mathbb{S}^{p}&0\\
0&0\end{pmatrix}\overline{V}^{T}.

(4.30)

Let us verify the “
\subset
” part first. Pick any
W
in the left-hand side set. We find sequences
(X_{k},Y_{k})\in\mbox{\rm gph}\,(\partial\|\cdot\|_{*})
and
W_{k}\in T_{(\partial\|\cdot\|_{*})^{-1}(Y_{k})}(X_{k})
such that
Y_{k}\in\mathcal{M}_{*}
,
(X_{k},Y_{k})\to(\overline{X},\overline{Y})
, and
W_{k}\to W
. As
Y_{k}\in\partial\|X_{k}\|_{*}
,
X_{k}
and
Y_{k}
have simultaneous ordered SVD, i.e., there exists
(U_{k},V_{k})\in\mathcal{O}(X_{k})\cap\mathcal{O}(Y_{k})
such that the SVD of
X_{k}
and
Y_{k}
are

X_{k}=U_{k}\begin{pmatrix}\Sigma_{k}&0\\
0&0\end{pmatrix}V_{k}^{T}\quad\mbox{and}\quad Y_{k}=U_{k}\begin{pmatrix}\mathbb{I}_{p}&0&0\\
0&\Lambda_{k}&0\end{pmatrix}V_{k}^{T}.

(4.31)

By passing to subsequences, we may suppose that
(U_{k},V_{k})\to(U,V)\in\mathcal{O}(\overline{X})\cap\mathcal{O}(\overline{Y})
,
\Sigma_{k}\to\Sigma
, and
\Lambda_{k}\to\Lambda
. Hence, we have

\overline{X}=U\begin{pmatrix}\Sigma&0\\
0&0\end{pmatrix}V^{T}\quad\mbox{and}\quad\overline{Y}=U\begin{pmatrix}\mathbb{I}_{p}&0&0\\
0&\Lambda&0\end{pmatrix}V^{T},

(4.32)

which tells us that
(U,V)\in\mathcal{O}(\overline{X})\cap\mathcal{O}(\overline{Y})
.
As
Y_{k}\to\overline{Y}
and
p=p(\overline{Y})
,
\|\Lambda\|<1
. By (
4.28
), we have

W_{k}\in U_{k}\begin{pmatrix}\mathbb{S}^{p}&0\\
0&0\end{pmatrix}V_{k}^{T},

which implies that

W\in U\begin{pmatrix}\mathbb{S}^{p}&0\\
0&0\end{pmatrix}V^{T}.
It follows that

\mathfrak{T}_{\mathcal{M}_{*}}\subset\left\{U\begin{pmatrix}\mathbb{S}^{p}&0\\
0&0\end{pmatrix}V^{T}|\,(U,V)\in\mathcal{O}(\overline{X})\cap\mathcal{O}(\overline{Y})\right\}.

(4.33)

Recall here that the formula of the tangent cone
T_{(\partial\|\cdot\|_{*})^{-1}(\overline{Y})}(\overline{X})
in (
4.28
) does not depend on the choice of any
(\overline{U},\overline{V})\in\mathcal{O}(\overline{X})\cap\mathcal{O}(\overline{Y})
.
As
(U,V)\in\mathcal{O}(\overline{X})\cap\mathcal{O}(\overline{Y})
, we obtain from (
4.28
) that

T_{(\partial\|\cdot\|_{*})^{-1}(\overline{Y})}(\overline{X})=\left\{U\begin{pmatrix}A&B&0\\
B^{T}&C&0\\
0&0&0\end{pmatrix}V^{T}|\;A\in\mathbb{S}^{r},B\in\mathbb{R}^{r\times(p-r)},C\in\mathbb{S}^{(p-r)\times(p-r)}_{+}\right\}.

This gives us that

\mbox{\rm span}\,\left[T_{(\partial\|\cdot\|_{*})^{-1}(\overline{Y})}(\overline{X})\right]=T_{(\partial\|\cdot\|_{*})^{-1}(\overline{Y})}(\overline{X})-T_{(\partial\|\cdot\|_{*})^{-1}(\overline{Y})}(\overline{X})=U\begin{pmatrix}\mathbb{S}^{p}&0\\
0&0\end{pmatrix}V^{T}

due to the fact that

\mbox{\rm span}\,\mathbb{S}^{(p-r)\times(p-r)}_{+}=\mathbb{S}^{(p-r)\times(p-r)}_{+}-\mathbb{S}^{(p-r)\times(p-r)}_{+}=\mathbb{S}^{(p-r)\times(p-r)}.

Similarly, we also have

\mbox{\rm span}\,\left[T_{(\partial\|\cdot\|_{*})^{-1}(\overline{Y})}(\overline{X})\right]=\overline{U}\begin{pmatrix}\mathbb{S}^{p}&0\\
0&0\end{pmatrix}\overline{V}^{T}.

It follows that

U\begin{pmatrix}\mathbb{S}^{p}&0\\
0&0\end{pmatrix}V^{T}=\overline{U}\begin{pmatrix}\mathbb{S}^{p}&0\\
0&0\end{pmatrix}\overline{V}^{T}.

This together with (
4.33
) verifies the inclusion “
\subset
” in (
4.30

To justify the opposite inclusion in (
4.30
), define

\overline{X}_{k}=\overline{U}\begin{pmatrix}\Sigma&0&0\\
0&\frac{1}{k}\mathbb{I}_{p-r}&0\\
0&0&0\end{pmatrix}\overline{V}^{T}\quad\mbox{and}\quad\overline{Y}_{k}=\overline{Y}.

Observe that
(\overline{X}_{k},\overline{Y}_{k})\to(\overline{X},\overline{Y})
and
\overline{Y}_{k}\in\partial\|\overline{X}_{k}\|_{*}\cap\mathcal{M}_{*}
. Note further from (
4.28

T_{\partial g^{*}(\overline{Y}_{k})}(\overline{X}_{k})=\overline{U}\begin{pmatrix}\mathbb{S}^{p}&0\\
0&0\end{pmatrix}\overline{V}^{T}.

It follows that

\overline{U}\begin{pmatrix}\mathbb{S}^{p}&0\\
0&0\end{pmatrix}\overline{V}^{T}\subset\mathop{{\rm Lim}\,{\rm sup}}_{k\to\infty}T_{(\partial\|\cdot\|_{*})^{-1}(\overline{Y}_{k})}(\overline{X}_{k})\subset\mathfrak{T}_{\mathcal{M}_{*}},

which ensures the inclusion “
\supset
” in (
4.30
). Thus equality (
4.30
) holds. By Theorem
3.9
,
\overline{X}
is tilt-stable if and only if Strong Sufficient Condition (
4.29
) is satisfied.

\hfill\Box

Theorem 4.7
.

Consider the following perturbed optimization problem

{\mathcal{P}}(B,\mu)\qquad\min_{X\in\mathbb{R}^{n_{1}\times n_{2}}}\quad\frac{1}{2\mu}\|\Phi X-B\|^{2}+\|X\|_{*},

(4.34)

where
\Phi:\mathbb{R}^{n_{1}\times n_{2}}\to\mathbb{R}^{m}
is a linear operator and the parameter pair
(B,\mu)\in\mathbb{R}^{m}\times\mathbb{R}_{++}
is around a known pair
(\overline{B},\bar{\mu})\in\mathbb{R}^{m}\times\mathbb{R}_{++}
. The following are equivalent:

(i)

Strong Sufficient Condition holds:

\mbox{\rm Ker}\,\Phi\cap\overline{U}\begin{pmatrix}\mathbb{S}^{p}&0\\
0&0\end{pmatrix}\overline{V}^{T}=\{0\}\qquad\mbox{with}\qquad\overline{Y}=-\frac{1}{\bar{\mu}}\Phi^{*}(\Phi\overline{X}-\overline{B})\quad\mbox{and}\quad p=p(\overline{Y}).

(4.35)

(ii)

The corresponding solution mapping
S
in (
3.41
) is single-valued and Lipschitz continuous around
(\overline{B},\bar{\mu})
with
S(\overline{B},\bar{\mu})=\overline{X}
.

(iii)

The solution mapping
S
in (
3.41
) is single-valued around
(\overline{B},\bar{\mu})
with
S(\overline{B},\bar{\mu})=\overline{X}
.

Proof.

By Corollary
3.12
and Theorem
4.6
, Strong Sufficient Condition (
4.35
) implies the Lipschitz continuity of the solution mapping
S
.
This clearly verifies the implication [
(i)
\Longrightarrow
(ii)
]. Moreover, [
(ii)
\Longrightarrow
(iii)
] is trivial.

It remains to prove [
(iii)
\Longrightarrow
(i)
]. Let us suppose that
S
in (
3.41
) is single-valued around
(\overline{B},\bar{\mu})
with
S(\overline{B},\bar{\mu})=\overline{X}
. By the SVD (
4.6
) for
\overline{X}
and
\overline{Y}
, we define

X_{k}=\overline{U}\begin{pmatrix}\Sigma&0&0\\
0&\frac{1}{k}\mathbb{I}_{p-r}&0\\
0&0&0\end{pmatrix}\overline{V}^{T}\qquad\mbox{and}\qquad Y_{k}=\overline{Y}\quad{\mbox{w}ith}\quad(\overline{U},\overline{V})\in\mathcal{O}(\overline{X})\cap\mathcal{O}(\overline{Y}).

It follows from Lemma
4.1
that
Y_{k}\in\partial\|X_{k}\|_{*}
and
(X_{k},Y_{k})\to(\overline{X},\overline{Y})
as
k\to\infty
. Define further

B_{k}=\frac{1}{\bar{\mu}}\Phi(X_{k}-\overline{X})+\overline{B}\to\overline{B}.

Note that

-\frac{1}{\bar{\mu}}\Phi^{*}(\Phi X_{k}-B_{k})=-\frac{1}{\bar{\mu}}\Phi^{*}(\Phi\overline{X}-\overline{B})=\overline{Y}\in\|X_{k}\|_{*}.

Hence
X_{k}
is the optimal solution of the following problem

\min_{X\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times n}}\quad\frac{1}{2\bar{\mu}}\|\Phi X-B_{k}\|^{2}+\|X\|_{*},

(4.36)

which means
X_{k}=S(B_{k},\bar{\mu})
is the unique solution of problem
\mathcal{P}(B_{k},\mu_{k})
in (
4.36
). We claim that

\mbox{\rm Ker}\,\Phi\cap\mathbb{R}_{+}\big[(\partial\|\cdot\|_{*})^{-1}(Y_{k})-X_{k}\big]=\{0\}.

(4.37)

Indeed, pick any
W\in\mbox{\rm Ker}\,\Phi\cap\mathbb{R}_{+}\big[(\partial\|\cdot\|_{*})^{-1}(Y_{k})-X_{k}\big]

\lambda>0
such that

X_{k}+\lambda W\in(\partial\|\cdot\|_{*})^{-1}(Y_{k}),\;\mbox{i.e.},\;Y_{k}\in\partial\|X_{k}+\lambda W\|_{*}.

-\frac{1}{\mu}\Phi^{*}(\Phi(X_{k}+\lambda W)-B_{k})=-\frac{1}{\mu}\Phi^{*}(\Phi(X_{k}-B_{k}))=Y_{k}\in\partial\|X_{k}+\lambda W\|_{*},

which tells us that

is also an optimal solution of problem (
4.36
). As
X_{k}
is the only solution of problem (
4.36
), we have

. This verifies condition (
4.37
).

According to formula (
4.4

\mathbb{R}_{+}\big[(\partial\|\cdot\|_{*})^{-1}(Y_{k})-X_{k}\big]=\mathbb{R}_{+}\left[\overline{U}\begin{pmatrix}\mathbb{S}^{p}_{+}-\Sigma_{k}&0\\
0&0\end{pmatrix}\overline{V}^{T}\right]=\overline{U}\begin{pmatrix}\mathbb{S}^{p}&0\\
0&0\end{pmatrix}\overline{V}^{T}\;\mbox{with}\;\Sigma_{k}=\begin{pmatrix}\Sigma&0\\

where the last equality holds due to

We only need to prove that if the Nondegeneracy Condition (

\overline{X}
is an optimal solution of problem

is single-valued and Lipschitz continuous around

\overline{X}=S(\overline{B},\mu)

\mbox{\rm Ker}\,\Phi\cap\mathbb{R}_{+}\big[(\partial\|\cdot\|_{*})^{-1}(\overline{Y})-\overline{X}\big]=\{0\}.

\mathbb{R}_{+}\big[(\partial\|\cdot\|_{*})^{-1}(\overline{Y})-\overline{X}\big]=\mathbb{R}_{+}\left[\overline{U}\begin{pmatrix}\mathbb{S}^{r}&0\\

5
Conclusion

In this paper we obtain necessary and sufficient conditions for Lipschitz stability of the solution mapping
S
of parametric problem (
1.1
). Along the way, full geometric characterizations of tilt stability are also obtained for more general problem (
1.8
) without computing the generalized Hessian on
g

Covariant Function Algebras of Invariant Characters of Normal Subgroups
Arash Ghaani Farashahi

Abstract
. This paper presents abstract harmonic analysis foundations for structure of covariant function algebras of invariant characters of normal subgroups. Suppose that G G is a locally compact group and N N is a closed normal subgroup of G G . Let ξ : N → 𝕋 \xi:N\to\mathbb{T} be a continuous G G -invariant character, 1 ≤ p < ∞ 1\leq p<\infty , and L ξ p ​ ( G , N ) L_{\xi}^{p}(G,N) be the L p L^{p} -space of all covariant functions of ξ \xi on G G . We study structure of covariant convolution in L ξ p ​ ( G , N ) L^{p}_{\xi}(G,N) . It is proved that L ξ 1 ​ ( G , N ) L^{1}_{\xi}(G,N) is a Banach ∗ * -algebra and L ξ p ​ ( G , N ) L^{p}_{\xi}(G,N) is a Banach L ξ 1 ​ ( G , N ) L^{1}_{\xi}(G,N) -module. We then investigate the theory of covariant convolutions for the case of characters of canonical normal subgroups in semi-direct product groups. The paper is concluded by realization of the theory in the case of different examples.

2.
Preliminaries and Notations

Throughout this section we present basic preliminaries and notations.

Let
\mathcal{A}
be a Banach
*
-algebra. A right approximate identity in

\mathcal{A}
is a net
\mathcal{E}=(f_{\alpha})_{\alpha\in\Lambda}
such that for every element
f
of
\mathcal{A}
, we have
f=\lim_{\alpha}f\ast f_{\alpha}
. Similarly,
\mathcal{E}
is called a left approximate identity for the Banach algebra
\mathcal{A}
, if
f=\lim_{\alpha}f_{\alpha}\ast f
for all
f\in\mathcal{A}
. A two-sided approximate identity for
\mathcal{A}
is a net
\mathcal{E}=(f_{\alpha})_{\alpha\in\Lambda}
which is both a right approximate identity and a left approximate identity for
\mathcal{A}
. If an approximate identity is a sequence, it is called a sequential approximate identity.

Suppose that
X
is a locally compact Hausdorff space. Then
\mathcal{C}(X)
is the space of all continuous complex valued functions on
X
and
\mathcal{C}_{c}(X)
is the space of all continuous complex valued functions on
X
with compact support. Let
\lambda
be a positive Radon measure on
X
, and
1\leq p<\infty
. We then denote the Banach space consists of equivalence classes of
\lambda
-measurable complex valued functions
f:X\to\mathbb{C}
such that

\|f\|_{L^{p}(X,\lambda)}^{p}:=\int_{X}|f(x)|^{p}\mathrm{d}\lambda(x)<\infty,

by
L^{p}(X,\lambda)
which contains
\mathcal{C}_{c}(X)
as a
\|\cdot\|_{L^{p}(X,\lambda)}
-dense subspace.

2.1.
Abstract Harmonic Analysis on Groups

Let
G
be a locally compact group with the modular function
\Delta_{G}
and a left Haar measure
\lambda_{G}
.
For
1\leq p<\infty
, the notation
L^{p}(G)
used for the Banach function space
L^{p}(G,\lambda_{G})
. The convolution of functions
f,g\in\mathcal{C}_{c}(G)
is defined by

(2.1)

f\ast_{G}g(x):=\int_{G}f(y)g(y^{-1}x)\mathrm{d}\lambda_{G}(y)\quad(x\in G).

Then
f\ast_{G}g\in\mathcal{C}_{c}(G)
and the bilinear product
\ast_{G}:\mathcal{C}_{c}(G)\times\mathcal{C}_{c}(G)\to\mathcal{C}_{c}(G)
has a unique extension to the bilinear product
\ast_{G}:L^{1}(G)\times L^{p}(G)\to L^{p}(G)
such that

\|f\ast_{G}g\|_{L^{p}(G)}\leq\|f\|_{L^{1}(G)}\|g\|_{L^{p}(G)}
, if
f\in L^{1}(G)
and
g\in L^{p}(G)
. The Banach function space
L^{1}(G)
is a Banach
*
-algebra with respect to the bilinear product
\ast_{G}:L^{1}(G)\times L^{1}(G)\to L^{1}(G)
and the involution given by
f\mapsto f^{\ast^{G}}
where
f^{*^{G}}(x):=\Delta_{G}(x^{-1})\overline{f(x^{-1})}
for
x\in G
. The Banach
*
-algebra
L^{1}(G)
admits a two-sided approximate identity. If
G
is second-countable then
L^{1}(G)
admits a sequential approximate identity. For
p>1
, the Banach space
L^{p}(G)
is a Banach left
L^{1}(G)
-module equipped with the left module action
\ast_{G}:L^{1}(G)\times L^{p}(G)\to L^{p}(G)
, see
[
10
,
17
,
32
]
and the list of references therein.

Let
N
be a closed normal subgroup of
G
with the left Haar measure
\lambda_{N}
. The coset space
G/N
is a locally compact group called as quotient (factor) group of
N
in
G
. Then
\mathcal{C}_{c}(G/N)
consists of all functions
T_{N}(f)
, where

f\in\mathcal{C}_{c}(G)
and

(2.2)

T_{N}(f)(xN)=\int_{N}f(xs)\mathrm{d}\lambda_{N}(s)\quad(xN\in G/N).

The quotient group
G/N
has a left Haar measure
\lambda_{G/N}
, normalized with respect to the following Weil’s formula

(2.3)

\int_{G/N}T_{N}(f)(xN)\mathrm{d}\lambda_{G/N}(xN)=\int_{G}f(x)\mathrm{d}\lambda_{G}(x),

for
f\in L^{1}(G)
, see
[
5
,
10
]
.
Then the convolution of
\Psi\in L^{1}(G/N)
and
\Phi\in L^{p}(G/N)
has the form

\Psi\ast_{G/N}\Phi(xN)=\int_{G/N}\Psi(yN)\Phi(y^{-1}xN)\mathrm{d}\lambda_{G/N}(yN),

and the involution is given by

\Psi^{\ast^{G/N}}(xN):=\Delta_{G/N}(x^{-1}N)\overline{\Psi(x^{-1}N)}

for
xN\in G/N
. In this case, the linear map
T_{N}:L^{1}(G)\to L^{1}(G/N)
is a contraction (norm-decreasing)
*
-homomorphism of Banach
*
-algebras, see Theorem 3.5.4 of
[
32
]
.

2.2.
Covariant Functions of Characters

Let
G
be a locally compact group and
N
be a closed subgroup of
G
.
A character
\xi
of
N
, is a continuous group homomorphism
\xi:N\to\mathbb{T}
, where
\mathbb{T}:=\{z\in\mathbb{C}:|z|=1\}
is the circle group. In terms of group representation theory, each character of
N
is a 1-dimensional irreducible continuous unitary representation of
N
. We then denote the set of all characters of
N
by
\chi(N)
.
A function
\psi:G\to\mathbb{C}
satisfies covariant property associated to the character
\xi\in\chi(N)
, if
\psi(xs)=\xi(s)\psi(x)
, for every
x\in G
and
s\in N
. In this case,
\psi
is called a covariant function of
\xi
. The covariant functions appear in abstract harmonic analysis in the construction of induced representations, see
[
10
,
19
]
. We here employ some of the classical tools in this direction. Suppose that
\lambda_{N}
is a left Haar measure on
N
. For
\xi\in\chi(N)
and
f\in\mathcal{C}_{c}(G)
, let
T_{\xi}(f):G\to\mathbb{C}
be the function defined by

T_{\xi}(f)(x):=\int_{N}f(xs)\overline{\xi(s)}\mathrm{d}\lambda_{N}(s),

for every
x\in G
. Assume that
\mathcal{C}_{\xi}(G,N)
is the linear subspace of
\mathcal{C}(G)
given by

\mathcal{C}_{\xi}(G,N):=\{\psi\in\mathcal{C}_{c}(G|N):\psi(xk)=\xi(k)\psi(x),\ {\rm for\ all}\ x\in G,\ k\in N\},

where

\mathcal{C}_{c}(G|N):=\{\psi\in\mathcal{C}(G):\mathrm{q}({\rm supp}(\psi))\ {\rm is\ compact\ in}\ G/N\},

and
\mathrm{q}:G\to G/N
is the canonical map given by
\mathrm{q}(x):=xN
for every
x\in G
. The linear operator
T_{\xi}
maps
\mathcal{C}_{c}(G)
onto
\mathcal{C}_{\xi}(G,N)
, see Proposition 6.1 of
[
10
]
.
If
N
is a compact subgroup of
G
then
\mathcal{C}_{\xi}(G,N)\subseteq\mathcal{C}_{c}(G)
, see Proposition 3.1 of
[
14
]
.

2.3.
Covariant Functions of Characters of Normal Subgroups

We then review some aspects of harmonic analysis of covariant functions of characters of normal subgroups on locally compact groups. Throughout, let
G
be a locally compact group and
N
be a closed normal subgroup of
G
.
Then there exists a unique homomorphism
\sigma_{N}:G\to(0,\infty)
such that

(2.4)

\int_{N}v(s)\mathrm{d}\lambda_{N}(x^{-1}sx)=\sigma_{N}(x)\int_{N}v(s)\mathrm{d}\lambda_{N}(s),

and

\int_{N}v(s)\mathrm{d}\mu_{N}(x^{-1}sx)=\sigma_{N}(x)\int_{N}v(s)\mathrm{d}\mu_{N}(s),

for every left Haar measure
\lambda_{N}
of
N
, right Haar measure
\mu_{N}
of
N
,
v\in\mathcal{C}_{c}(N)
, and
x\in G
. It is worthwhile to mention that existence of the homomorphism
\sigma_{N}:G\to(0,\infty)
guaranteed by the uniqueness of left Haar measures on the locally compact group
N
, Theorem 2.20 of
[
10
]
. We then have
\sigma_{N}(t)=\Delta_{N}(t)
for all
t\in N
and hence
\sigma_{G}(x)=\Delta_{G}(x)
for
x\in G
. The modular function of
G/N
satisfies
\Delta_{G}(x)=\sigma_{N}(x)\Delta_{G/N}(xN)
for
x\in G
, see Proposition 11 of
[
2
, Chap. VII, §2]
. In particular, if
N
is central we then have
\sigma_{N}=1
.

Suppose that
\xi\in\chi(N)
is given. Let
\psi:G\to\mathbb{C}
be a covariant function of
\xi
. Then

(2.5)

\psi(sx)=\xi(x^{-1}sx)\psi(x),

for
x\in G
and
s\in N
. Also, according to Proposition 3.3 of
[
16
]
, for
x\in G
, and
f\in\mathcal{C}_{c}(G)
, we have

(2.6)

T_{\xi}(f)(x)=\sigma_{N}(x)\int_{N}f(sx)\overline{\xi(x^{-1}sx)}\mathrm{d}\lambda_{N}(s).

We then review structure of classical
L^{p}
-spaces of covariant functions of characters of normal subgroups. Let
\lambda_{G}
be a left Haar measure on
G
and
\lambda_{N}
be a left Haar measure on
N
.
Suppose that
\lambda_{G/N}
is the left Haar measure on the quotient group
G/N
normalized with respect to Weil’s formula (
2.3
). For every
1\leq p<\infty
and
\psi\in\mathcal{C}_{\xi}(G,N)
, the covariant norm is defined by

\|\psi\|_{(p)}^{p}:=\int_{G/N}|\psi(y)|^{p}\mathrm{d}\lambda_{G/N}(yN).

We then assume that
L^{p}_{\xi}(G,N)
is the Banach completion of the normed linear space
\mathcal{C}_{\xi}(G,N)
with respect to
\|\cdot\|_{(p)}
. The completion norm is also denoted by
\|\cdot\|_{(p)}
. Invoking the structure of
L^{p}_{\xi}(G,N)
, as the Banach completion of a normed space, one can identify the abstract space
L^{p}_{\xi}(G,N)
with a linear space of complex-valued functions on
G
, where two functions are identified when they are equal locally almost everywhere, see Remark 3.6 of
[
15
]
.

It is shown that the linear operator
T_{\xi}:(\mathcal{C}_{c}(G),\|\cdot\|_{L^{1}(G)})\to(\mathcal{C}_{\xi}(G,N),\|\cdot\|_{(1)})

is a contraction, see Theorem 4.4 of
[
16
]
.
Therefore,
T_{\xi}:(\mathcal{C}_{c}(G),\|\cdot\|_{L^{1}(G)})\to(\mathcal{C}_{\xi}(G,N),\|\cdot\|_{(1)})
has a unique extension to a contraction from
L^{1}(G)
onto
L^{1}_{\xi}(G,N)
. The extended map
T_{\xi}:L^{1}(G)\to L^{1}_{\xi}(G,N)
is given by

, where (Theorem 5.4 of
[
16
]
)

T_{\xi}(f)(x)=\int_{N}f(xs)\overline{\xi(s)}\mathrm{d}\lambda_{N}(s)\ \ \ {\rm for\ \ l.a.e.}\ x\in G.

If
\mathcal{N}_{\xi}^{1}(G,N)
is the kernel of the linear operator
T_{\xi}
in
L^{1}(G)
then the Banach space
L_{\xi}^{1}(G,N)
is canonically isometric isomorphic to the quotient Banach space
L^{1}(G)/\mathcal{N}_{\xi}^{1}(G,N)
, see Theorem 5.9 of
[
16
]
.

If
N
is a compact normal subgroup of
G
, each Haar measure of
N
is finite and hence

(2.7)

\|\psi\|_{(p)}=\lambda_{N}(N)^{-1/p}\|\psi\|_{L^{p}(G)},

for every
\psi\in\mathcal{C}_{\xi}(G,N)
, see Proposition 3.2 of
[
15
]
. Therefore,
L^{p}_{\xi}(G,N)\subseteq L^{p}(G)
when
N
is compact.
It is proved that if
N
is a compact subgroup of
G

with the probability Haar measure
\lambda_{N}
on
N
and
p>1
, then the linear operator
T_{\xi}:(\mathcal{C}_{c}(G),\|.\|_{L^{p}(G)})\to(\mathcal{C}_{\xi}(G,N),\|.\|_{L^{p}(G)})
is a contraction, see Theorem 4.1 of
[
14
]
.
We then denote by
T_{\xi}:L^{p}(G)\to L^{p}_{\xi}(G,N)
, the unique extension of
T_{\xi}:(\mathcal{C}_{c}(G),\|.\|_{L^{p}(G)})\to(\mathcal{C}_{\xi}(G,N),\|.\|_{L^{p}(G)})
to a contraction from
L^{p}(G)
onto
L^{p}_{\xi}(G,N)
. In this case, the Banach space
L_{\xi}^{p}(G,N)
is isometrically isomorphic to the quotient Banach
L^{p}(G)/\mathcal{N}_{\xi}^{p}(G,N)
, where
\mathcal{N}^{p}_{\xi}(G,N)
is the kernel of the linear operator
T_{\xi}:L^{p}(G)\to L_{\xi}^{p}(G,N)
in

, see Theorem 4.9 of
[

]
.

Covariant Function Algebras of Invariant Characters

In this section, we study structure of covariant function algebras of invariant characters of normal subgroups. We introduce the abstract notions of convolutions and involutions for covariant functions of invariant characters of normal subgroups. Suppose that
G
is a locally compact group and
N
is a closed normal subgroup of
G
. Let
\lambda_{G}
be a left Haar measure on
G
and
\lambda_{N}
be a left Haar measure on
N
.
Assume that
\lambda_{G/N}
is the left Haar measure on the
quotient group
G/N
normalized with respect to Weil’s formula (
2.3
).

3.1.
Covariant Convolutions

Throughout, we present the abstract notion of covariant convolutions, convolution of covariant functions, for invariant characters of normal subgroups.

A character
\xi\in\chi(N)
is called
G
-invariant
if
\xi_{x}=\xi
for every
x\in G
, where the character
\xi_{x}:N\to\mathbb{T}
is given by

\xi_{x}(s):=\xi(x^{-1}sx)
, for every
s\in N
. We then denote the set of all
G
-invariant characters of
N
by
\Gamma(G,N)
, that is

\Gamma(G,N):=\{\xi\in\chi(N):\xi_{x}=\xi,\ {\rm for\ all}\ x\in G\}.

If
N
is a closed central
subgroup of
G
then
\Gamma(G,N)=\chi(N)
.

Remark 3.1
.

Let
N
be a closed normal subgroup of
G
, and
\xi\in\Gamma(G,N)
. Suppose that
\psi:G\to\mathbb{C}
is a covariant function of
\xi
. Then
\psi(sx)=\xi(s)\psi(x)
for
x\in G
and
s\in N
. Indeed,
using (
2.5
) and since
\xi
is
G
-invariant, we have

\psi(sx)=\xi(x^{-1}sx)\psi(s)=\xi(s)\psi(x).

We then have the following observations concerning invariant characters.

Proposition 3.2
.

Let
G
be a locally compact group and
N
be a closed normal subgroup of
G
. Suppose
\xi\in\Gamma(G,N)
, and
f\in\mathcal{C}_{c}(G)
are arbitrary. Then

(1)

For every
x\in G
, we have

T_{\xi}(f)(x)=\sigma_{N}(x)\int_{N}f(sx)\overline{\xi(s)}\mathrm{d}\lambda_{N}(s).

(2)

For every
x\in G
and
k\in N
, we have
T_{\xi}(L_{k}f)(x)=\overline{\xi(k)}T_{\xi}(f)(x)
.

Proof.

(1) Let
x\in G
be given. Using (
2.6
) and since
\xi
is
G
-invariant, we have

T_{\xi}(f)(x)=\sigma_{N}(x)\int_{N}f(sx)\overline{\xi(x^{-1}sx)}\mathrm{d}\lambda_{N}(s)=\sigma_{N}(x)\int_{N}f(sx)\overline{\xi(s)}\mathrm{d}\lambda_{N}(s).

(2) Invoking Proposition 3.4 of
[
16
]
, we get

T_{\xi}(L_{k}f)(x)=\overline{\xi(x^{-1}kx)}T_{\xi}(f)(x)=\overline{\xi(k)}T_{\xi}(f)(x).

∎

Proposition 3.3
.

Let
G
be a locally compact group and
N
be a closed normal subgroup of
G
. Suppose
\xi\in\Gamma(G,N)
,
x\in G
, and
\psi,\phi\in\mathcal{C}_{\xi}(G,N)
. The mapping
\varphi_{x}:G\to\mathbb{C}
given by
\varphi_{x}(y):=\psi(y)\phi(y^{-1}x)
reduces to constant on
N
and hence it defines a well-defined complex-valued function on
G/N
.

Proof.

Let
y\in G
and
s\in N
. Since
N
is normal and
\xi
is
G
-invariant, using Remark
3.1
, we have

\displaystyle\phi(s^{-1}y^{-1}x)=\xi(s^{-1})\phi(y^{-1}x).

Therefore, we get

\displaystyle\varphi_{x}(ys)

\displaystyle=\psi(ys)\phi((ys)^{-1}x)=\psi(ys)\phi(s^{-1}y^{-1}x)

\displaystyle=\psi(ys)\xi(s^{-1})\phi(y^{-1}x)=\xi(s)\psi(y)\xi(s^{-1})\phi(y^{-1}x)

\displaystyle=\xi(s)\psi(y)\overline{\xi(s)}\phi(y^{-1}x)=|\xi(s)|^{2}\psi(y)\phi(y^{-1}x)=\varphi_{x}(y).

∎

Let
\xi\in\Gamma(G,N)
and
\psi,\phi\in\mathcal{C}_{\xi}(G,N)
. We then define the
covariant convolution

\psi\natural\phi:G\to\mathbb{C}
by

(3.1)

\psi\natural\phi(x):=\int_{G/N}\psi(y)\phi(y^{-1}x)\mathrm{d}\lambda_{G/N}(yN),

for every
x\in G
.

Invoking Proposition
3.3
, for any
x\in G
, the function

\varphi_{x}:G\to\mathbb{C}
given by
\varphi_{x}(y):=\psi(y)\phi(y^{-1}x)
reduces to constant on
N
. Thus it defines a well-defined complex-valued function on
G/N
. Since
\psi,\phi
are continuous on
G
,
{\rm supp}(\psi)N
and
{\rm supp}(\phi)N
are compact in
G/N
, we deduce that
\varphi_{x}
defines a function in
\mathcal{C}_{c}(G/N)
which can be integrated on
G/N
with respect to
\lambda_{G/N}
. Therefore, the right hand side of (
3.1
) defines a well-defined function on
G
.

Let

Proposition 3.6
.

Let

Theorem 3.12

\displaystyle\left(\int_{G/N}|\psi\natural\phi(x)|^{p}\mathrm{d}\lambda_{G/N}(xN)\right)^{1/p}

\displaystyle=\left(\int_{G/N}\left|\int_{G/N}\psi(y)\phi(y^{-1}x)\mathrm{d}\lambda_{G/N}(yN)\right|^{p}\mathrm{d}\lambda_{G/N}(xN)\right)^{1/p}

\displaystyle=\int_{N}\overline{f(s^{-1}x^{-1})}\Delta_{G}(s^{-1}x^{-1})\overline{\xi(s)}\mathrm{d}\lambda_{N}(s)=\Delta_{G}(x^{-1})\int_{N}\overline{f(s^{-1}x^{-1})}\Delta_{G}(s^{-1})\overline{\xi(s)}\mathrm{d}\lambda_{N}(s).

\displaystyle T_{\xi}(f^{\ast^{G}})(x)

\displaystyle=\Delta_{G}(x^{-1})\int_{N}\overline{f(s^{-1}x^{-1})}\Delta_{G}(s^{-1})\overline{\xi(s)}\mathrm{d}\lambda_{N}(s)

\displaystyle=\Delta_{G}(x^{-1})\int_{N}\overline{f(sx^{-1})}\Delta_{G}(s)\xi(s)\mathrm{d}\lambda_{N}(s^{-1})

\displaystyle=\Delta_{G}(x^{-1})\int_{N}\overline{f(sx^{-1})}\Delta_{G}(s)\Delta_{N}(s^{-1})\xi(s)\mathrm{d}\lambda_{N}(s).

Since

is normal in

, see Proposition 3.3.17 of
[

\displaystyle T_{\xi}(f^{\ast^{G}})(x)

\displaystyle=\Delta_{G}(x^{-1})\int_{N}\overline{f(sx^{-1})}\Delta_{G}(s)\Delta_{N}(s^{-1})\xi(s)\mathrm{d}\lambda_{N}(s)

\displaystyle=\Delta_{G}(x^{-1})\int_{N}\overline{f(sx^{-1})}\Delta_{N}(s)\Delta_{N}(s^{-1})\xi(s)\mathrm{d}\lambda_{N}(s)=\Delta_{G}(x^{-1})\int_{N}\overline{f(sx^{-1})}\xi(s)\mathrm{d}\lambda_{N}(s).

Since
\xi
is
G

3.2
(1), we get

\displaystyle T_{\xi}(f^{\ast^{G}})(x)

\displaystyle=\Delta_{G}(x^{-1})\int_{N}\overline{f(sx^{-1})}\xi(s)\mathrm{d}\lambda_{N}(s)

\displaystyle=\Delta_{G}(x^{-1})\sigma_{N}(x)\int_{N}\overline{f(x^{-1}s)}\xi(s)\mathrm{d}\lambda_{N}(s)=\Delta_{G}(x^{-1})\sigma_{N}(x)\overline{T_{\xi}(f)(x^{-1})}=T_{\xi}(f)^{\natural}(x).

∎

Corollary 3.24
.

Let
G
be a locally compact group and
N
be a compact normal subgroup of
G
. Suppose
\xi\in\Gamma(G,N)
and
f\in\mathcal{C}_{c}(G)
. Then

T_{\xi}(f^{\ast^{G}})=T_{\xi}(f)^{\ast^{G}}.

We then have the following observations concerning covariant involutions.

Proposition 3.25
.

Let
G
be a locally compact group,
N
be a closed normal subgroup of
G
, and
\xi\in\Gamma(G,N)
. Suppose
\psi,\phi\in\mathcal{C}_{\xi}(G,N)
are given. Then

(1)

\|\psi^{\natural}\|_{(1)}=\|\psi\|_{(1)}
.

(2)

\psi^{{\natural}^{\natural}}=\psi
.

(3)

(\psi\natural\phi)^{\natural}=\phi^{\natural}\natural\psi^{\natural}
.

Proof.

(1) We have

\displaystyle\|\psi^{\natural}\|_{(1)}

\displaystyle=\int_{G/N}|\psi^{\natural}(x)|\mathrm{d}\lambda_{G/N}(xN)=\int_{G/N}\Delta_{G/N}(x^{-1}N)|\psi(x^{-1})|\mathrm{d}\lambda_{G/N}(xN)

\displaystyle=\int_{G/N}\Delta_{G/N}(xN)|\psi(x)|\mathrm{d}\lambda_{G/N}(x^{-1}N)=\int_{G/N}|\psi(x)|\mathrm{d}\lambda_{G/N}(xN)=\|\psi\|_{(1)}.

(2) It follows from Theorem
3.23
.

(3) Let
f,g\in\mathcal{C}_{c}(G)
with
T_{\xi}(f)=\psi
and
T_{\xi}(g)=\phi
. Using Theorem
3.8
and Theorem
3.23
, we get

\displaystyle(\psi\natural\phi)^{\natural}

\displaystyle=T_{\xi}(f\ast_{G}g)^{\natural}=T_{\xi}((f\ast_{G}g)^{*^{G}})

\displaystyle=T_{\xi}(g^{*^{G}}\ast_{G}f^{\ast^{G}})=T_{\xi}(g^{*^{G}})\natural T_{\xi}(f^{\ast^{G}})=T_{\xi}(g)^{\natural}\natural T_{\xi}(f)^{\natural}=\phi^{\natural}\natural\psi^{\natural}.

∎

Therefore, the mapping
{}^{\natural}:(\mathcal{C}_{\xi}(G,N),\|\cdot\|_{(1)})\to(\mathcal{C}_{\xi}(G,N),\|\cdot\|_{(1)})
given by
\psi\mapsto\psi^{\natural}
is an isometric involution and hence
(\mathcal{C}_{\xi}(G,N),\natural,^{\natural},\|\cdot\|_{(1)})
is a normed
*
-algebra.

We then extend the involution map (
3.6
) into
L^{1}_{\xi}(G,N)
.

Theorem 3.26
.

Let
G
be a locally compact group,
N
be a closed normal subgroup of
G
, and
\xi\in\Gamma(G,N)
. The covariant involution

{}^{\natural}:\mathcal{C}_{\xi}(G,N)\to\mathcal{C}_{\xi}(G,N)
has a unique extension to an isometric involution
{}^{\natural}:L^{1}_{\xi}(G,N)\to L^{1}_{\xi}(G,N)
,
in which the covariant Banach algebra
L^{1}_{\xi}(G,N)
equipped with the extended involution is a Banach
*
-algebra.

Proof.

Let
\psi\in L^{1}_{\xi}(G,N)
. Suppose
(\psi_{n})\subset\mathcal{C}_{\xi}(G,N)
with
\psi=\lim_{n}\psi_{n}
in

. Then
(\psi_{n}^{\natural})
is Cauchy in

. Since

is a Banach space,

has a limit in

. We then define

\psi^{\natural}:=\lim_{n}\psi_{n}^{\natural}
. Then
\psi^{\natural}
is independent of the choice of the sequences
(\psi_{n})
. Also, we have
\psi^{\natural}\in L^{1}_{\xi}(G,N)

, and
\|\psi^{\natural}\|_{(1)}=\|\psi\|_{(1)}
. Further, let

in

(3), we get

Therefore, the covariant Banach algebra
L^{1}_{\xi}(G,N)
equipped with the extended covariant involution
{}^{\natural}:L^{1}_{\xi}(G,N)\to L^{1}_{\xi}(G,N)
is a Banach
*
-algebra.
∎

4.
Covariant Function Algebras on Semi-direct Product Groups

Throughout this section, we investigate the theory of covariant convolutions/involutions for characters of canonical normal subgroups in
semi-direct product of locally compact groups.
We here assume that
H,K
are locally compact groups and
\theta:H\to Aut(K)
is a continuous homomorphism. Suppose
G_{\theta}=H\ltimes_{\theta}K
is the semi-direct product of
H
and
K
with respect to
\theta
.
The semi-direct product
G_{\theta}=H\ltimes_{\theta}K
is the locally compact topological group with the underlying set
H\times K

which is equipped by the product topology and the group operation

(h,k)\ltimes_{\theta}(h^{\prime},k^{\prime})=(hh^{\prime},k\theta_{h}(k^{\prime}))\hskip 14.22636pt{\rm and}\hskip 14.22636pt(h,k)^{-1}=(h^{-1},\theta_{h^{-1}}(k^{-1})).

Let
N
be a closed normal subgroup of
K
such that
\theta_{h}(N)=N
, for every
h\in H
. In this case,
N
is called a
\theta
-invariant subgroup of
K
. For each
h\in H
, assume that
\delta_{H,N}(h)\in(0,\infty)
is given by

(4.1)

\mathrm{d}\lambda_{N}(\theta_{h}(s))=\delta_{H,N}(h)^{-1}\mathrm{d}\lambda_{N}(s),

where
\lambda_{N}
is a left Haar measure on
N
.
Then the map
\delta_{H,N}:H\to(0,\infty)
defined by
h\mapsto\delta_{H,N}(h)
is a continuous homomorphism.

The continuous homomorphism
\delta_{H,K}
characterizes Haar measure and modular function on the semi-direct product
G_{\theta}:=H\ltimes_{\theta}K
.
Let
\Delta_{H}
and
\Delta_{K}
be modular functions of
H
and
K
respectively. The modular function
\Delta_{G_{\theta}}
of
G_{\theta}
is given by
\Delta_{G_{\theta}}(h,k)=\delta_{H,K}(h)\Delta_{H}(h)\Delta_{K}(k)
.
Suppose that
\lambda_{H}
and
\lambda_{K}
are left Haar measures on
H
and
K
respectively. Then

(4.2)

\mathrm{d}\lambda_{G_{\theta}}(h,k):=\delta_{H,K}(h)\mathrm{d}\lambda_{H}(h)\mathrm{d}\lambda_{K}(k),

is a left Haar measure on
G_{\theta}
, see
[
17
, §15.26 and §15.29]
.

Proposition 4.1
.

Let
H,K
be locally compact groups and
\theta:H\to Aut(K)
be a continuous homomorphism. Suppose
G_{\theta}=H\ltimes_{\theta}K
,
N
is a closed normal
\theta
-invariant subgroup of
K
, and
(h,k)\in G_{\theta}
. Then

(1)

\sigma_{N}(h)=\delta_{H,N}(h)
.

(2)

If
k\in N
then
\sigma_{N}(h,k)=\delta_{H,N}(h)\Delta_{N}(k)
.

Proof.

(1) Let
x:=(h,e_{K})\in G_{\theta}
. Then, for each
s\in N
, we have

x^{-1}\ltimes(e_{H},s)\ltimes x=(e_{H},\theta_{h^{-1}}(s)).

Suppose
\lambda_{N}
is a fixed left Haar measure on
N
. Using (
2.4
) and (
4.1
), for each
v\in\mathcal{C}_{c}(N)
, we get

\displaystyle\sigma_{N}(h)\int_{N}v(s)\mathrm{d}\lambda_{N}(s)

\displaystyle=\int_{N}v(s)\mathrm{d}\lambda_{N}(x^{-1}\ltimes(e_{H},s)\ltimes x)

\displaystyle=\int_{N}v(s)\mathrm{d}\lambda_{N}(\theta_{h^{-1}}(s))=\delta_{H,N}(h)\int_{N}v(s)\mathrm{d}\lambda_{N}(s),

which implies that
\sigma_{N}(h)=\delta_{H,N}(h)
.

(2) Suppose
k\in N
. Using (1) and since
\sigma_{N}:G_{\theta}\to(0,\infty)
is a group homomorphism, we obtain

\sigma_{N}(h,k)=\sigma_{N}((e_{H},k)\ltimes(h,e_{K}))=\sigma_{N}(k)\sigma_{N}(h)=\Delta_{N}(k)\delta_{H,N}(h).

∎

Corollary 4.2
.

Let
H,K
be locally compact groups and
\theta:H\to Aut(K)
be a continuous homomorphism. Suppose
G_{\theta}=H\ltimes_{\theta}K
and
(h,k)\in G_{\theta}
. Then

\sigma_{K}(h,k)=\delta_{H,K}(h)\Delta_{K}(k).

The continuous homomorphism
\theta:H\to Aut(K)
induces the continuous homomorphism
\widetilde{\theta}:H\to Aut(K/N)
, given by
h\mapsto\widetilde{\theta}_{h}
, where
\widetilde{\theta}_{h}:K/N\to K/N
is defined by

\widetilde{\theta}_{h}(kN):=\theta_{h}(k)N,

for every
h\in H
and
kN\in K/N
.

Then the quotient group
G_{\theta}/N
is canonically isomorphic as a topological group to the semi-direct product
G_{\widetilde{\theta}}:=H\ltimes_{\widetilde{\theta}}(K/N)
via the topological group isomorphism
(h,k)N\mapsto(h,kN)
. Invoking Proposition 5.1(1) of
[
15
]
, we have

\delta_{H,K}(h)=\delta_{H,N}(h)\delta_{H,K/N}(h)
for every
h\in H
. Therefore, for
(h,k)\in G_{\theta}
, we obtain

(4.3)

\Delta_{G_{\theta}/N}((h,k)N)=\delta_{H,K}(h)\frac{\Delta_{H}(h)\Delta_{K}(k)}{\sigma_{N}(h,k)}=\delta_{H,K/N}(h)\frac{\Delta_{H}(h)\Delta_{K}(k)}{\sigma_{N}(k)}.

Assume that
\lambda_{K/N}
is the left Haar measure on
K/N
normalized with respect to left Haar measures
\lambda_{N}
on
N
and
\lambda_{K}
on
K
. Then

\mathrm{d}\lambda_{G_{\theta}/N}(h,kN):=\delta_{H,K/N}(h)\mathrm{d}\lambda_{H}(h)\mathrm{d}\lambda_{K/N}(kN),

uniquely identifies the left Haar measure on
G_{\theta}/N
normalized with respect to the left Haar measure
\lambda_{G_{\theta}}
on
G_{\theta}
given by (
4.2
) and the left Haar measure
\lambda_{N}
on
N
, see Proposition 5.1(2) of
[
15
]
.

Suppose that
N
is a closed subgroup of
K
such that
\theta_{h}(N)=N
, for every
h\in H
. Assume that
\lambda_{N}
is a fixed left Haar measure on
N
and
\xi\in\chi(N)
. For
f\in\mathcal{C}_{c}(G_{\theta})
, the function
T_{\xi}(f):G_{\theta}\to\mathbb{C}
is given by

T_{\xi}(f)(a,b)=\int_{N}f(a,b\theta_{a}(s))\overline{\xi(s)}\mathrm{d}\lambda_{N}(s),

for all
(a,b)\in G_{\theta}
. Then, using (
4.1
), we have

(4.4)

T_{\xi}(f)(a,b)=\delta_{H,N}(a)\int_{N}f(a,bs)\overline{\xi(\theta_{a^{-1}}(s))}\mathrm{d}\lambda_{N}(s).

In particular, if
b\in N
, we get

T_{\xi}(f)(a,b)=\delta_{H,N}(a)\xi(\theta_{a^{-1}}(b))\int_{N}f(a,s)\overline{\xi(\theta_{a^{-1}}(s))}\mathrm{d}\lambda_{N}(s).

Let
\psi\in\mathcal{C}_{\xi}(G_{\theta},N)
. Then
\psi(h,k\theta_{h}(s))=\xi(s)\psi(h,k)
, for
h\in H
,
k\in K
, and
s\in N
. Therefore,

\psi(h,s)=\xi\circ\theta_{h^{-1}}(s)\psi(h,e_{K})\ \ \ \ \ \ {\rm for}\ \ (h,s)\in H\times N.

In this case, for
1\leq p<\infty
, the norm
\|\psi\|_{(p)}
can be computed by

\|\psi\|_{(p)}^{p}:=\int_{H}\int_{K/N}|\psi(h,k)|^{p}\delta_{H,K/N}(h)\mathrm{d}\lambda_{H}(h)\mathrm{d}\lambda_{K/N}(kN).

We then have the following characterization for invariant characters of normal subgroups of
K
.

Proposition 4.3
.

Let
H,K
be locally compact groups and
\theta:H\to Aut(K)
be a continuous homomorphism. Suppose
G_{\theta}=H\ltimes_{\theta}K
,
N
is a closed normal
\theta
-invariant subgroup of
K
, and
\xi\in\chi(N)
. Then,
\xi\in\Gamma(G_{\theta},N)
if and only if

\xi(s)=\xi\circ\theta_{h}(k^{-1}sk)
, for
h\in H
,
k\in K
, and
s\in N
.

Proof.

Suppose
h\in H
,
k\in K
, and
s\in N
. Let
x:=(h,k)
. We then have

\displaystyle x^{-1}\ltimes(e_{H},s)\ltimes x

\displaystyle=(h,k)^{-1}\ltimes(e_{H},s)\ltimes(h,k)=(h^{-1},\theta_{h^{-1}}(k^{-1}))\ltimes(e_{H},s)\ltimes(h,k)

\displaystyle=(h^{-1},\theta_{h^{-1}}(k^{-1}s))\ltimes(h,k)=(h^{-1}h,\theta_{h^{-1}}(k^{-1}sk))=(e_{H},\theta_{h^{-1}}(k^{-1}sk)).

Therefore, we get

\xi_{x}(s)=\xi(x^{-1}\ltimes(e_{H},s)\ltimes x)=\xi(\theta_{h^{-1}}(k^{-1}sk)),

implying
\xi\in\Gamma(G_{\theta},N)
if and only if

\xi(s)=\xi\circ\theta_{h}(k^{-1}sk)
, for
h\in H
,
k\in K
, and
s\in N
.
∎

.

Let
H,K
be locally compact groups and

be a continuous homomorphism. Suppose

if and only if

, for every

.

Let
N
be a closed
\theta
-invariant normal subgroup of
K
, and
\xi\in\Gamma(G_{\theta},N)
. Suppose that
f\in\mathcal{C}_{c}(G_{\theta})
and

are given. Then, using (
4.4
), we obtain

(4.5)

T_{\xi}(f)(a,b)=\delta_{H,N}(a)\int_{N}f(a,bs)\overline{\xi(s)}\mathrm{d}\lambda_{N}(s).

In particular, if
b\in N
, we get

T_{\xi}(f)(a,b)=\delta_{H,N}(a)\xi(b)\int_{N}f(a,s)\overline{\xi(s)}\mathrm{d}\lambda_{N}(s).

Assume that
\psi,\phi\in\mathcal{C}_{\xi}(G_{\theta},N)
. Then

\psi(h,ks)=\xi(s)\psi(h,k),

for every
h\in H
,
k\in K
,
s\in N
. The covariant convolution

\psi\natural\phi:G_{\theta}\to\mathbb{C}
is given by

(4.6)

\psi\natural\phi(a,b)=\int_{H}\int_{K/N}\psi(h,k)\phi(h^{-1}a,\theta_{h^{-1}}(k^{-1}b))\delta_{H,K/N}(h)\mathrm{d}\lambda_{H}(h)\mathrm{d}\lambda_{K/N}(kN),

and the covariant involution
\psi^{\natural}:G_{\theta}\to\mathbb{C}
is given by

(4.7)

\psi^{\natural}(a,b):=\Delta_{G_{\theta}/N}(a^{-1},\theta_{a^{-1}}(b^{-1})N)\overline{\psi(a^{-1},\theta_{a^{-1}}(b^{-1}))},

for every
(a,b)\in G_{\theta}
.

4.1.
The Case

Let
H,K
be locally compact groups and
\theta:H\to Aut(K)
be a continuous homomorphism. Then
\widetilde{K}=\{(e_{H},k):k\in K\}
is a closed normal subgroup of

.

and

is a left Haar measure on

.
Assume that

and

), we get

Next we present a criterion for
G_{\theta}
-invariant characters of

.

Proposition 4.5
.

Let
H,K
be locally compact groups and
\theta:H\to Aut(K)
be a continuous homomorphism. Suppose
G_{\theta}=H\ltimes_{\theta}K
and
\xi\in\chi(K)
. Then,
\xi\in\Gamma(G_{\theta},K)

if and only if
\xi=\xi\circ\theta_{h}
for every

.

Proof.

Let
\xi\in\Gamma(G_{\theta},K)
be given. Using Proposition
4.3
, we get
\xi(s)=\xi(\theta_{a}(b^{-1}sb))
,
for all

,

then
\xi(s)=\xi(\theta_{a}(s))=\xi\circ\theta_{a}(s)
, for every

and

. So
\xi=\xi\circ\theta_{h}
for all

. Conversely, let
\xi\circ\theta_{a}=\xi
for all

\displaystyle\xi(\theta_{a}(b^{-1}sb))

\displaystyle=\xi\circ\theta_{a}(b^{-1}sb)=\xi\circ\theta_{a}(b^{-1})\xi\circ\theta_{a}(s)\xi\circ\theta_{a}(b)

\displaystyle=\overline{\xi\circ\theta_{a}(b)}\xi\circ\theta_{a}(s)\xi\circ\theta_{a}(b)=\xi\circ\theta_{a}(s)=\xi(s),

for all

,

. Then, applying Proposition

.
∎

For
\xi\in\Gamma(G_{\theta},K)
,

In this case, every

(4.9)

\phi(h,k)=\xi(k)\phi(h,e_{K}),

for all

and

\mathcal{C}_{\xi}(G_{\theta},K)
, if

.

Proposition 4.6
.

Let
H,K
be locally compact groups and
\theta:H\to Aut(K)
be a continuous homomorphism. Suppose
G_{\theta}:=H\ltimes_{\theta}K
,

\xi\in\Gamma(G_{\theta},K)
,
\psi,\phi\in\mathcal{C}_{\xi}(G_{\theta},K)
, and
(a,b)\in G_{\theta}
. Then

(1)

The covariant convolution

\psi\natural\phi:G_{\theta}\to\mathbb{C}
is given by

(4.10)

\psi\natural\phi(a,b)=\xi(b)\int_{H}\psi(h,e_{K})\phi(h^{-1}a,e_{K})\mathrm{d}\lambda_{H}(h).

(2)

The covariant involution
\psi^{\natural}:G_{\theta}\to\mathbb{C}
is given by

(4.11)

\psi^{\natural}(a,b)=\xi(b)\Delta_{H}(a^{-1})\overline{\psi(a^{-1},e_{K})}.

Proof.

(1) Using (
4.6
) and (
4.9
), for
N:=K
, we get

\displaystyle\psi\natural\phi(a,b)

\displaystyle=\int_{H}\int_{K/N}\psi(h,k)\phi(h^{-1}a,\theta_{h^{-1}}(k^{-1}b))\delta_{H,K/N}(h)\mathrm{d}\lambda_{H}(h)\mathrm{d}\lambda_{K/N}(kN)

\displaystyle=\int_{H}\psi(h,e_{K})\phi(h^{-1}a,\theta_{h^{-1}}(b))\mathrm{d}\lambda_{H}(h)

\displaystyle=\int_{H}\psi(h,e_{K})\phi(h^{-1}a,e_{K})\xi(\theta_{h^{-1}}(b))\mathrm{d}\lambda_{H}(h)=\xi(b)\int_{H}\psi(h,e_{K})\phi(h^{-1}a,e_{K})\mathrm{d}\lambda_{H}(h).

(2) Using (
4.3
), (
4.7
) and (
4.9
), we have

\displaystyle\psi^{\natural}(a,b)

\displaystyle=\Delta_{G_{\theta}/K}(a^{-1},\theta_{a^{-1}}(b^{-1})K)\overline{\psi(a^{-1},\theta_{a^{-1}}(b^{-1}))}=\Delta_{H}(a^{-1})\overline{\psi(a^{-1},\theta_{a^{-1}}(b^{-1}))}

\displaystyle=\Delta_{H}(a^{-1})\overline{\xi(\theta_{a^{-1}}(b^{-1}))}\overline{\psi(a^{-1},e_{K})}=\xi(b)\Delta_{H}(a^{-1})\overline{\psi(a^{-1},e_{K})}.

∎

Throughout this section, we discuss the theory of covariant convolutions and involutions in the case of different examples of semi-direct product groups.

5.1.
Abstract Weyl-Heisenberg Groups

Let
L
be a locally compact Abelian group with the dual group
\widehat{L}
.
Suppose
H:=L
and
K:=\widehat{L}\times\mathbb{T}
. Let
\theta:H\to Aut(K)
be the continuous homomorphism given by
x\mapsto\theta_{x}
, where
\theta_{x}:K\to K
is defined by
\theta_{x}(\omega,z):=(\omega,\omega(x)z)
,
for every
(\omega,z)\in K=\widehat{L}\times\mathbb{T}
.
The abstract Weyl-Heisenberg group associated to the LCA group
L
, denoted by
\mathbb{W}(L)
, is the semi-direct product
H\ltimes_{\theta}K
.
The abstract Weyl-Heisenberg group
\mathbb{W}(L)
has the underlying set
L\times\widehat{L}\times\mathbb{T}
equipped with the following group law

(x,\omega,z)\ltimes(x^{\prime},\omega^{\prime},z^{\prime})=(x+x^{\prime},\omega\omega^{\prime},\omega^{\prime}(x)zz^{\prime}),

(x,\omega,z)^{-1}=(-x,\overline{\omega},\omega(x)\overline{z}),

for every
(x,\omega,z),(x^{\prime},\omega^{\prime},z^{\prime})\in\mathbb{W}(L)
.

The Case

The set

\theta_{x}(\mathbf{1},z)=(\mathbf{1},z)

\chi(N)=\{\chi_{n}:n\in\mathbb{Z}\}

for

and

Proposition 5.1
.

Let

be a LCA group and

Suppose

5.2.1.
The Case

\nu\in\mathbb{R}

4.6

\displaystyle\psi\natural\phi(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y},t)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\psi(\mathbf{x}^{\prime},\mathbf{y}^{\prime},0)\phi(\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{x}^{\prime},\mathbf{y}-\mathbf{y}^{\prime},t-\langle\mathbf{x}^{\prime},\mathbf{y}-\mathbf{y}^{\prime}\rangle)\mathrm{d}\mathbf{x}^{\prime}\mathrm{d}\mathbf{y}^{\prime}.

Proposition 5.9
.

Let

\Gamma(\mathbb{H}^{d},\mathbb{R}^{d+1})=\mathbb{R}^{d}.

Proof.

Let

\displaystyle\mathbf{e}_{\mathbf{z},\nu}\circ\theta_{\mathbf{x}}(\mathbf{y},s)

\displaystyle=\mathbf{e}_{\mathbf{z},\nu}(\theta_{\mathbf{x}}(\mathbf{y},s))=\mathbf{e}_{\mathbf{z},\nu}(\mathbf{y},s+\langle\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y}\rangle)=e^{\mathrm{i}\langle\mathbf{z},\mathbf{y}\rangle}e^{\mathrm{i}\nu(s+\langle\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y}\rangle)}

\displaystyle=e^{\mathrm{i}\langle\mathbf{z},\mathbf{y}\rangle}e^{\mathrm{i}\nu s}e^{\mathrm{i}\nu\langle\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y}\rangle}=e^{\mathrm{i}\langle\mathbf{z},\mathbf{y}\rangle}e^{\mathrm{i}\langle\nu\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y}\rangle}e^{\mathrm{i}\nu s}=e^{\mathrm{i}\langle\mathbf{z}+\nu\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y}\rangle}e^{\mathrm{i}\nu s}=\mathbf{e}_{\mathbf{z}+\nu\mathbf{x},\nu}(\mathbf{y},s),

for every

and

.
Therefore, we obtain
\mathbf{e}_{\mathbf{z},\nu}\circ\theta_{\mathbf{x}}=\mathbf{e}_{\mathbf{z}+\nu\mathbf{x},\nu}
, for every
\mathbf{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
.
Using Proposition

, we conclude that
\mathbf{e}_{\mathbf{z},\nu}\in\Gamma(\mathbb{H}^{d},\mathbb{R}^{d+1})

\mathbf{e}_{\mathbf{z},\nu}=\mathbf{e}_{\mathbf{z}+\nu\mathbf{x},\nu}
, for every

. So
\mathbf{e}_{\mathbf{z},\nu}\in\Gamma(\mathbb{H}^{d},\mathbb{R}^{d+1})
if and only if

for every

. This implies that

∎

Let

Let

5.3.
The Subgroups of Jacobi Groups

Let
d\geq 1
and
\mathrm{Sp}(d)
be the real symplectic group.
Throughout this section, we briefly present basics of classical harmonic analysis on the real symplectic group
\mathrm{Sp}(d)
, for more details we referee the readers to
[
4
,
21
]
and the comprehensive list of references therein.

A matrix
\mathbf{S}\in M_{2d\times 2d}(\mathbb{R})
is called symplectic if and only if
\mathbf{S}^{T}\mathbf{J}\mathbf{S}=\mathbf{S}\mathbf{J}\mathbf{S}^{T}=\mathbf{J}
, with

\mathbf{J}=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}\mathbf{0}&{\mathbf{I}_{d}}\\
{-\mathbf{I}_{d}}&\mathbf{0}\\
\end{array}\right),

where

\mathbf{I}_{d}
is
d\times d
identity matrix..
The group consists of all symplectic matrices is called the (real) symplectic group which is denoted by
\mathrm{Sp}(d)
.
It is a simple non-compact finite-dimensional real Lie group.
In block-matrix notation, the symplectic group
\mathrm{Sp}(d)

consists of all real
2d\times 2d
matrices in block form

\mathbf{S}=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}\mathbf{A}&\mathbf{B}\\
\mathbf{C}&\mathbf{D}\\
\end{array}\right),\hskip 28.45274pt\mathbf{A},\mathbf{B},\mathbf{C},\mathbf{D}\in M_{d\times d}(\mathbb{R}),

such that
\mathbf{A}^{T}\mathbf{C}=\mathbf{C}^{T}\mathbf{A}
,
\mathbf{B}^{T}\mathbf{D}=\mathbf{D}^{T}\mathbf{B}
, and
\mathbf{A}^{T}\mathbf{D}-\mathbf{C}^{T}\mathbf{B}=\mathbf{I}_{d}
.

The real symplectic group
\mathrm{Sp}(d)
satisfies the following decomposition, namely Iwasawa (Gram-Schmidt) decomposition,
\mathrm{Sp}(d)=\mathcal{K}\mathcal{A}\mathcal{N}
where
[
4
,
12
]

(5.12)

\mathcal{K}_{d}:=\left\{\Omega(\mathbf{A}+\mathrm{i}\mathbf{B})=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}\mathbf{A}&\mathbf{-B}\\
\mathbf{B}&\mathbf{A}\\
\end{array}\right):\mathbf{A}+\mathrm{i}\mathbf{B}\in\mathrm{U}(d,\mathbb{C})\right\},

(5.13)

\mathcal{A}_{d}:=\left\{{\rm diag}(h_{1},...,h_{d},h_{1}^{-1},...,h_{d}^{-1}):h_{1},...,h_{d}>0\right\},

and

(5.14)

\mathcal{N}_{d}:=\left\{\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}\mathbf{A}&\mathbf{B}\\
\mathbf{0}&\mathbf{A}^{-T}\\
\end{array}\right):\mathbf{A}\ {\rm is\ unit\ upper\ triangular},\ \mathbf{AB}^{T}=\mathbf{BA}^{T}\right\}.

Regarding elements of
\mathrm{Sp}(d)
as linear transformations over the vector space
\mathbb{R}^{2d}={\mathbb{R}^{d}}\times\widehat{{\mathbb{R}^{d}}}
, then the symplectic group

\mathrm{Sp}(d)
is precisely the group of all linear automorphisms of

{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\times\widehat{{\mathbb{R}^{d}}}
which
preserve the canonical (symplectic) form.
Also, it is easy to check that, if
\lambda_{{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\times\widehat{{\mathbb{R}^{d}}}}
is the Lebesgue measure on
{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\times\widehat{{\mathbb{R}^{d}}}
, then

\mathrm{d}\lambda_{{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\times\widehat{{\mathbb{R}^{d}}}}(\mathbf{S}\mbox{\boldmath$\mu$ \unboldmath}\hskip-3.61371pt)=\mathrm{d}\lambda_{{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\times\widehat{{\mathbb{R}^{d}}}}(\mbox{\boldmath$\mu$ \unboldmath}\hskip-3.61371pt)
, for
\mathbf{S}\in\mathrm{Sp}(d)
.

Let
H
be a closed subgroup of the real symplectic group
\mathrm{Sp}(d)
. Assume that
K
is the underlying manifold
\mathbb{R}^{2d}\times\mathbb{R}
equipped with the following group law

(\mbox{\boldmath$\mu$ \unboldmath}\hskip-3.61371pt,t)\ltimes(\mbox{\boldmath$\mu$ \unboldmath}\hskip-3.61371pt^{\prime},t^{\prime}):=(\mbox{\boldmath$\mu$ \unboldmath}\hskip-3.61371pt+\mbox{\boldmath$\mu$ \unboldmath}\hskip-3.61371pt^{\prime},t+t^{\prime}+\frac{1}{2}\mathcal{J}[\mbox{\boldmath$\mu$ \unboldmath}\hskip-3.61371pt,\mbox{\boldmath$\mu$ \unboldmath}\hskip-3.61371pt^{\prime}]),

(\mbox{\boldmath$\mu$ \unboldmath}\hskip-3.61371pt,t)^{-1}:=(-\mbox{\boldmath$\mu$ \unboldmath}\hskip-3.61371pt,-t),

where the symplectic form
\mathcal{J}:\mathbb{R}^{2d}\times\mathbb{R}^{2d}\to\mathbb{R}
is
\mathcal{J}[\mbox{\boldmath$\mu$ \unboldmath}\hskip-3.61371pt,\mbox{\boldmath$\mu$ \unboldmath}\hskip-3.61371pt^{\prime}]:=\mbox{\boldmath$\mu$ \unboldmath}\hskip-3.61371pt^{T}\mathbf{J}\mbox{\boldmath$\mu$ \unboldmath}\hskip-3.61371pt^{\prime}
,
for all
\mbox{\boldmath$\mu$ \unboldmath}\hskip-3.61371pt,\mbox{\boldmath$\mu$ \unboldmath}\hskip-3.61371pt^{\prime}\in\mathbb{R}^{2d}
with

\mathbf{J}:=\left(\begin{matrix}\mathbf{0}&\mathbf{I}_{d}\\
-\mathbf{I}_{d}&\mathbf{0}\\
\end{matrix}\right).

Suppose
\mathbb{G}_{d}(H):=H\ltimes_{\theta}K

(\mathbf{h},\mathbf{p},\mathbf{q},t)\ltimes(\mathbf{h}^{\prime},\mathbf{p}^{\prime},\mathbf{q}^{\prime},t^{\prime})=(\mathbf{h}\odot\mathbf{h}^{\prime},\mathbf{p}+\mathbf{h}\odot\mathbf{p}^{\prime},\mathbf{q}+\mathbf{h}^{-1}\odot\mathbf{q}^{\prime},t+t^{\prime}+\frac{\langle\mathbf{p},\mathbf{h}^{-1}\odot\mathbf{q}^{\prime}\rangle-\langle\mathbf{q},\mathbf{h}\odot\mathbf{p}^{\prime}\rangle}{2}),

where
\odot
is the Hadamard product. Let
\nu\in\mathbb{R}
,
\psi,\phi\in\mathcal{C}_{\nu}(\mathbb{G}_{d}(H),\mathbb{R})
, and
(\mathbf{h},\mbox{\boldmath$\mu$ \unboldmath}\hskip-3.61371pt,t)\in\mathbb{G}_{d}(H)
. Then, using (
5.16
), the covariant convolution is

\psi\natural\phi(\mathbf{h},\mbox{\boldmath$\mu$ \unboldmath}\hskip-3.61371pt,t)=e^{\mathrm{i}\nu t}\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}^{d}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2d}}e^{-\mathrm{i}\nu\frac{1}{2}\mathcal{J}[\mbox{\boldmath$\mu$ \unboldmath}\hskip-3.61371pt^{\prime},\mbox{\boldmath$\mu$ \unboldmath}\hskip-3.61371pt]}\psi(\mathbf{h}^{\prime},\mbox{\boldmath$\mu$ \unboldmath}\hskip-3.61371pt^{\prime},0)\phi(\mathbf{h}^{\prime-1}\odot\mathbf{h},\mathbf{S}(\mathbf{h}^{\prime-1})\mbox{\boldmath$\mu$ \unboldmath}\hskip-3.61371pt-\mathbf{S}(\mathbf{h}^{\prime-1})\mbox{\boldmath$\mu$ \unboldmath}\hskip-3.61371pt^{\prime},0)\mathrm{d}\mathbf{h}^{\prime}\mathrm{d}\mbox{\boldmath$\mu$ \unboldmath}\hskip-3.61371pt^{\prime},

and the covariant involution is

\psi^{\natural}(\mathbf{h},\mbox{\boldmath$\mu$ \unboldmath}\hskip-3.61371pt,t):=e^{\mathrm{i}\nu t}\overline{\psi(\mathbf{h}^{-1},-\mathbf{S}(\mathbf{h})^{-1}\mbox{\boldmath$\mu$ \unboldmath}\hskip-3.61371pt,0)}.

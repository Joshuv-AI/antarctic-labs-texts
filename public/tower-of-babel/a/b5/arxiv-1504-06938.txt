1.
A version of General Neron Desingularization in the idea of Ploski

A ring morphism
u:A\rightarrow A^{\prime}
has
regular fibers
if for all prime ideals
P\in\operatorname{Spec}A
the ring
A^{\prime}/PA^{\prime}
is a regular ring, i.e. its localizations are regular local rings. It has
geometrically regular fibers
if for all prime ideals
P\in\operatorname{Spec}A
and all finite field extensions
K
of the fraction field of
A/P
the ring
K\otimes_{A/P}A^{\prime}/PA^{\prime}
is regular. If
A\supset{\bf Q}
then regular fibers of
u
are geometrically regular. We call
u

regular
if it is flat and its fibers are geometrically regular. A Henselian local ring
(A,m)
is
excellent
if it is Noetherian and the completion map
A\rightarrow{\hat{A}}
is regular. A regular morphism is
smooth
if it is finitely presented and it is
essentially smooth
if it is a localization of a finitely presented morphism.

Theorem 1
.

(Ploski
[
15
]
) Let
{\bf C}\{x\}
,
x=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})
,
f=(f_{1},\ldots,f_{s})
be some convergent power series from
{\bf C}\{x,Y\}
,
Y=(Y_{1},\ldots,Y_{N})
and
\hat{y}\in{\bf C}[[x]]^{N}
with
\hat{y}(0)=0
be a solution of
f=0
. Then the map
v:B={\bf C}\{x,Y\}/(f)\rightarrow{\bf C}[[x]]
given by
Y\rightarrow\hat{y}
factors through an
A
-algebra of type
B^{\prime}={\bf C}\{x,Z\}
for some variables
Z=(Z_{1},\ldots,Z_{s})
, that is
v
is a composite map
B\rightarrow B^{\prime}\rightarrow{\bf C}[[x]]
.

This result shows a particular case of the following theorem in Krull dimension
>1
, the case when

are DVR being given by the so called Néron

[

The following theorem is a consequence of Theorem
2
in the idea of Ploski (perhaps it is already known but we couldn’t find a precise reference).

Theorem 3
.

Let
(A,m)
be an excellent Henselian local ring,
\hat{A}
its completion,
B
a finite type
A
-algebra and
v:B\rightarrow\hat{A}
an
A
-morphism. Then
v
factors through an
A
-algebra of type
A[Z]^{h}
for some variables
Z=(Z_{1},\ldots,Z_{s})
, where
A[Z]^{h}
is the Henselization of
A[Z]_{(m,Z)}
.

Proof.

By Theorem
2
we see that
v
factors through a smooth
A
-algebra
B^{\prime}
, let us say
v
is the composite map
B\rightarrow B^{\prime}\xrightarrow{v^{\prime}}\hat{A}
.
Using the local structure of smooth algebras given by Grothendieck (see
[
24
, Theorem 2.5]
) we may assume that
B^{\prime}_{v^{\prime-1}(m\hat{A})}
is a localization of a smooth
A
-algebra of type
(A[Z,T]/(g))_{g^{\prime}h}
, where
Z=(Z_{1},\ldots,Z_{s})
,
g^{\prime}=\partial g/\partial T
. Then there exists
h\in A[Z,T]
such that
v^{\prime}
factors through
C=(A[Z,T]/(g))_{g^{\prime}h}
let us say
v^{\prime}
is the composite map
B^{\prime}\rightarrow C\xrightarrow{w}\hat{A}
.

Choose
z_{0}\in A^{N}
,
t_{0}\in A
, such that
(z_{0},t_{0})\equiv(\hat{z},\hat{t})
modulo
m\hat{A}
. Changing
(Z,T)
by
(Z-z_{0},T-t_{o})
in
C
we may suppose that
w(Z),w(T)
are in
m\hat{A}
.
Then
w
extends to a map
w^{\prime}:C^{\prime}=(A[Z]^{h}[T]/(g))_{g^{\prime}h}\rightarrow\hat{A}
and we have

since

is an etale neighborhood of
A[Z]^{h}
.

A Noetherian local ring
(A,m)
has the
property of Artin approximation
if the solutions in
A
of every system of polynomial equations
f
over
A
is dense with respect of the
m
-adic topology in the set of solutions of
f
in the completion
\hat{A}
of
A
.
In fact
(A,m)
has the property of Artin approximation if and only if every system of polynomial equations
f
over
A
has a solution in
A
if it has one in
\hat{A}
.

Theorem 4

(Popescu

,

. We will show that

. Set

,

,

,

and let

be the Artin function associated to

Corollary 8
.

Let
(A,m)
be a Noetherian local ring with the completion map
A\rightarrow\hat{A}
regular,
c\in\bf N
,
B
a finite type
A
-algebra and
\lambda
its function defined by Theorem
6
. Let
g:B\rightarrow{\hat{A}}/m^{\lambda(c)}\hat{A}
be an
A
-morphism. Then there exists a
B
-algebra
C
, which is smooth over
A
, and an
A
-morphism
w:C\rightarrow\hat{A}
such that the composite map
B\rightarrow C\xrightarrow{w}\hat{A}
coincides with
g
modulo
m^{c}\hat{A}
. In particular,
\lambda\geq\nu
, the last map being the Artin function over
\hat{A}
associated to the system of polynomials
f
defining

. Moreover, for any
A
-morphism
v:B\rightarrow\hat{A}
congruent with

modulo
m^{\lambda(c)}\hat{A}
, there exists an
A
-morphism
w:C\rightarrow\hat{A}
such that the composite map
B\rightarrow C\xrightarrow{w}\hat{A}
coincides with
v
modulo
m^{c}\hat{A}
.

Proof.

Let
\lambda
be given by Theorem
6
. Then there exists a
B
-algebra
C
which is smooth over
A
and an
A
-morphism
{\tilde{w}}:C\rightarrow A/m^{c}
such that the composite map
B\rightarrow C\xrightarrow{\tilde{w}}{\hat{A}}/m^{c}{\hat{A}}
coincides with

. By the Implicit Function Theorem

can be lifted to an

coincides with the composite map

.

∎

2.
A special case of Corollary
8

Let
A
be a discrete valuation ring,
x
a local parameter of
A
,
A^{\prime}=\hat{A}
its completion and
B=A[Y]/I
,
Y=(Y_{1},\ldots,Y_{n})
a finite type
A
-algebra.
If
f=(f_{1},\ldots,f_{r})
,
r\leq n
is a system of polynomials from
I
then we consider a
r\times r
-minor
M
of the Jacobian matrix
(\partial f_{i}/\partial Y_{j})
. Let
c\in\bf N
. Suppose that there exist an
A
-morphism
v:B\rightarrow A^{\prime}/(x^{2c+1})
and
N\in((f):I)
such that

v(NM)\not\in(x)^{c}/(x^{2c+1})
, where for simplicity we write
v(NM)
instead
v(NM+I)
. We may assume that
M=\det((\partial f_{i}/\partial Y_{j})_{i,j\in[r]})
.

Theorem 10
.

Then there exists a
B
-algebra
C
which is smooth over
A
such that every
A
-morphism
v^{\prime}:B\rightarrow A^{\prime}
with
v^{\prime}\equiv v\ \mbox{modulo}\ x^{2c+1}
(that is
v^{\prime}(Y)\equiv v(Y)\ \mbox{modulo}\\
x^{2c+1}
) factors through
C
.

Proof.

We follow somehow the proof of
[
16
, Theorem 3]
given in a more general assumptions.
Since
A/(x^{2c+1})\cong A^{\prime}/(x^{2c+1})
we may choose
y^{\prime}\in A^{n}
, such that
v(Y)=y^{\prime}+(x^{2c+1})
.
Set
P=NM
and
d=P(y^{\prime})
. Multiplying
N
by a certain power of
x
we may suppose that
dA=x^{c}A
.

Let
H
be the
n\times n
-matrix obtained adding down to
(\partial f/\partial Y)
as a border the block
(0|\mbox{Id}_{n-r})
(we assume as above that
M
is given on the first
r
columns of the Jacobian matrix). Let
G^{\prime}
be the adjoint matrix of
H
and
G=NG^{\prime}
. We have

GH=HG=NM\mbox{Id}_{n}=P\mbox{Id}_{n}

and so

d\mbox{Id}_{n}=P(y^{\prime})\mbox{Id}_{n}=G(y^{\prime})H(y^{\prime}).

Let

h=Y-y^{\prime}-dG(y^{\prime}))T,

where
T=(T_{1},\ldots,T_{n})
are new variables. Since

Y-y^{\prime}\equiv dG(y^{\prime})T\ \mbox{modulo}\ h

and

f(Y)-f(y^{\prime})\equiv\sum_{j}\partial f/\partial Y_{j}(y^{\prime})(Y_{j}-y^{\prime}_{j})

modulo higher order terms in
Y_{j}-y^{\prime}_{j}
by Taylor’s formula we see that we have

f(Y)-f(y^{\prime})\equiv\sum_{j}d\partial f/\partial Y_{j}(y^{\prime})G_{j}(y^{\prime})T_{j}+d^{2}Q=

dP(y^{\prime})T+d^{2}Q=d^{2}(T+Q)\ \mbox{modulo}\ h,

where
Q\in T^{2}A[T]^{r}
. This is because
(\partial f/\partial Y)G=(P\mbox{Id}_{r}|0)
. We have
f(y^{\prime})=d^{2}a
for some
a\in xA^{r}
. Set

g_{i}=a_{i}+T_{i}+Q_{i}
,
i\in[r]
and
E=A[Y,T]/(I,g,h)
. Clearly, it holds
d^{2}g\subset(f,h)
and
(f)\subset(h,g)
.

Note that
U=(A[T]/(g))_{s}
is smooth for some
s\in 1+(T)
because the
r\times r
-minor of the Jacobian matrix
(\partial g/\partial T)
given by the first
r
columns has the form
\det(\mbox{Id}_{r}+(\partial Q_{i}/\partial T_{j})_{i,j\in[r]})
. In particular,
U
is flat over
A
and so
d
is regular in
U
.
We claim that
E_{ss^{\prime}}\cong U_{s^{\prime}}
for some
s^{\prime}\in 1+(x,T)
. It will be enough to show that
I\subset(h,g)A[Y,T]_{ss^{\prime}}
. We have
PI\subset(f)\subset(h,g)
and so
P(y^{\prime}+dL)I\subset(h,g)
where
L=G(y^{\prime})T
. But
P(y^{\prime}+dL)
has the form
ds^{\prime}
for some
s^{\prime}\in 1+(d,T)
. It follows that
s^{\prime}I\subset(h,g)A[Y,T]_{s}
since
d
is regular in
U
and so
I\subset(h,g)A[Y,T]_{ss^{\prime}}
.
Thus
C=E_{ss^{\prime}}
is a
B
-algebra smooth over
A
.

Remains to see that an arbitrary
A
-morphism
v^{\prime}:B\rightarrow A^{\prime}
with
v^{\prime}\equiv v\ \mbox{modulo}\ x^{2c+1}
factors through
C
. We have
v^{\prime}(Y)\equiv v(Y)\equiv y^{\prime}\ \mbox{modulo}\ x^{2c+1}
and so there exists
\varepsilon\in xA^{\prime n}
such that
v^{\prime}(Y)-y^{\prime}=d^{2}\varepsilon
.
Then
t:=H(y^{\prime})\varepsilon\in xA^{\prime n}

satisfies

G(y^{\prime})t=P(y^{\prime})\varepsilon=d\varepsilon

and so

v^{\prime}(Y)-y^{\prime}=dG(y^{\prime})t,

that is
h(v^{\prime}(Y),t)=0
.
Note that
d^{2}g(t)\in(h(v^{\prime}(Y)),t),f(v^{\prime}(Y)))=(0)

and it follows that
g(t)=0
since
A^{\prime}
is a domain. Thus
v^{\prime}
factors through
E
, that is
v^{\prime}
is a composite map
B\rightarrow E\xrightarrow{\alpha}A^{\prime}
, where
\alpha
is a
B
-morphism given by
T\rightarrow t
.
As
\alpha(s)\equiv 1
modulo
x
and
\alpha(s^{\prime})\equiv 1
modulo
(x,t)
,
t\in xA^{\prime}
we see that
\alpha(s),\alpha(s^{\prime})
are invertible because
A^{\prime}
is local and so
\alpha
(thus
v^{\prime}
) factors through the standard smooth
A
-algebra
C
.

∎

Corollary 12
.

In the assumptions and notations of the above theorem, let
\rho:B\rightarrow C
be the structural algebra map. Then
\rho
induces a bijection
\rho^{*}
between
\{w\in\operatorname{Hom}_{A}(C,A^{\prime}):w\rho\equiv v\ \mbox{modulo}\ x^{2c+1}\}
and

\{v^{\prime}\in\operatorname{Hom}_{A}(B,A^{\prime}):v^{\prime}\equiv v\ \mbox{modulo}\ x^{2c+1}\}

\rho^{*}(w)=w\rho
.

Proof.

By the above theorem
\rho^{*}
is surjective. Let

be such that
w\rho=w^{\prime}\rho\equiv v\ \mbox{modulo}\ x^{2c+1}

Since

.

Corollary 13
.

In the assumptions and notations of the above corollary, suppose that there exists an
A
-morphism
{\tilde{v}}:B\rightarrow A^{\prime}

with
{\tilde{v}}\equiv v\ \mbox{modulo}\ x^{2c+1}
. Then
there exists an unique
A
-morphism
{\tilde{w}}:C\rightarrow A^{\prime}
such that
{\tilde{w}}\rho={\tilde{v}}
.

For the proof take

{\tilde{w}}={\rho^{*}}^{-1}(\tilde{v})
, where
\rho^{*}
is defined in the above corollary.

By a Grothendieck’s theorem
[
24
, Theorem 2.5]
,
C
can be chosen of the form
(A[Z,V]/(g))_{g^{\prime}h}
, where
Z=(Z_{1},\ldots,Z_{s})
and
g^{\prime}=\partial g/\partial V
.

Lemma 14
.

There exists a canonical bijection

A^{\prime s}\rightarrow\{w^{\prime}\in\operatorname{Hom}_{A}(C,A^{\prime}):w^{\prime}\equiv{\tilde{w}}\ \mbox{modulo}\ x^{2c+1}\}.

Proof.

Let
w^{\prime}\in\operatorname{Hom}_{A}(C,A^{\prime})
be with
w^{\prime}\equiv{\tilde{w}}\ \mbox{modulo}\ x^{2c+1}
.
We have

g(w^{\prime}(Z),{\tilde{w}}(V))\equiv g({\tilde{w}}(Z),{\tilde{w}}(V))=0\ \mbox{modulo}\ x^{2c+1},

g^{\prime}(w^{\prime}(Z),{\tilde{w}}(V))\equiv g^{\prime}({\tilde{w}}(Z),{\tilde{w}}(V))\not\equiv 0\ \mbox{modulo}\ x,

h(w^{\prime}(Z),{\tilde{w}}(V))\equiv h({\tilde{w}}(Z),{\tilde{w}}(V))\not\equiv 0\ \mbox{modulo}\ x.

Thus
g(w^{\prime}(Z),V)=0
has an unique solution (namely
w^{\prime}(V)
) in
{\tilde{w}}(V)+x^{2c+1}A^{\prime}
by the Implicit Function Theorem.
It follows that the restriction

\{w^{\prime}\in\operatorname{Hom}_{A}(C,A^{\prime}):w^{\prime}\equiv{\tilde{w}}\ \mbox{modulo}\ x^{2c+1}\}\rightarrow

\{w^{\prime\prime}\in\operatorname{Hom}_{A}(A[Z],A^{\prime}):w^{\prime\prime}\equiv{\tilde{w}}|_{A[Z]}\ \mbox{modulo}\ x^{2c+1}\}

is bijective.

On the other hand, the map

\{w^{\prime\prime}\in\operatorname{Hom}_{A}(A[Z],A^{\prime}):w^{\prime\prime}\equiv{\tilde{w}}|_{A[Z]}\ \mbox{modulo}\ x^{2c+1}\}\rightarrow{\tilde{w}}(Z)+x^{2c+1}A^{\prime s}

given by

is a bijection too. It follows that there exists a canonical bijection between

\{w^{\prime}\in\operatorname{Hom}_{A}(C,A^{\prime}):w^{\prime}\equiv{\tilde{w}}\ \mbox{modulo}\ x^{2c+1}\}
and
{\tilde{w}}(Z)+x^{2c+1}A^{\prime s}
, the last one being in bijection with
A^{\prime s}
.

∎

Let

3.
An application of General Neron Desingularization to an extension of Bass-Quillen Conjecture

Let
R[T]
,
T=(T_{1},\ldots,T_{n})
be a polynomial algebra in
T
over a Noetherian ring
R
. A finitely generated projective
R[T]
-module
M
is
extended
from
R
if there exists a finitely generated projective
R
-module
M^{\prime}
such that
M\cong R[T]\otimes_{R}M^{\prime}
.
An extension of Serre’s Problem proved by Quillen and Suslin is the following

Conjecture 17
.

(Bass-Quillen) If
R
is a regular ring then every finitely generated projective module over
R[T]
is extended from
R
.

If
(R,m)
is regular local ring then the above conjecture says that every finitely generated projective module over
R[T]
is free. For a possible proof it is enough to consider only this case using the Quillen’s Patching Theorem
[
22
, Theorem 1]
.
A consequence of Theorem
2
is a partial answer to a question of Swan which was the bases of the following theorem.

Theorem 18
.

(Popescu
[
19
]
) The Bass-Quillen Conjecture holds if
R
is a regular local ring in one of the cases:

(1)

R
contains a field,

(2)

the characteristic
p
of
k
is not in
m^{2}
,

(3)

R
is excellent Henselian.

An interesting problem is to replace in the Bass-Quillen Conjecture the polynomial algebra
R[T]
by other
R
-algebras. A useful result in this idea is the following theorem which we found too late to use it in
[
19
]
.

Theorem 19
.

(Vorst
[
25
]
) Let
A
be a ring,
A[X]
,
X=(X_{1},\ldots,X_{r})
a polynomial algebra,
I\subset A[X]
a monomial ideal and
B=A[X]/I
. Then every finitely generated projective
B
-module
M
is extended from a finitely generated projective
A
-module if for all
n\in\bf N
every finitely generated projective
A[T]
-module,
T=(T_{1},\ldots,T_{n})
is extended from a finitely generated projective
A
-module.

Using the above theorem and Corollary
18
we get the following theorem.

Theorem 20
.

Let
R
be a regular local ring in one of the cases of Theorem
18
,
I\subset R[X]
,
X=(X_{1},\ldots,X_{r})
be a monomial ideal and
B=R[X]/I

B
-module is free.

The Bass-Quillen Conjecture could also hold when
R
is not regular as shows the following theorem.

Theorem 21

Let
R

,
I\subset R[X]
,

-module,

For the proof note that

If

Asymptotic Growth patterns for class field towers
Arindam Bhattacharyya, Vishnu Kadiri, Anwesh Ray

Abstract
. Let p p be an odd prime number. We study growth patterns associated with finitely ramified Galois groups considered over the various number fields varying in a ℤ p \mathbb{Z}_{p} -tower. These Galois groups can be considered as non-commutative analogues of ray class groups. For certain ℤ p \mathbb{Z}_{p} -extensions in which a given prime above p p is completely split, we prove precise asymptotic lower bounds. Our investigations are motivated by the classical results of Iwasawa, who showed that there are growth patterns for p p -primary class numbers of the number fields in a ℤ p \mathbb{Z}_{p} -tower.

1.1.
Motivation from Iwasawa theory

Let
\mathds{L}

p

\bar{\mathds{L}}

\mathds{L}
. Let
H_{p}(\mathds{L})
be the maximal abelian unramified
p
-extension of
\mathds{L}
, and let
\mathcal{A}_{p}(\mathds{L})
denote the Galois group
\operatorname{Gal}(H_{p}(\mathds{L})/\mathds{L})
. By class field theory,
\mathcal{A}_{p}(\mathds{L})
is naturally isomorphic to the
p
-primary part of the class group of
\mathds{L}
. Setting
h_{p}(\mathds{L})\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\#\mathcal{A}_{p}(\mathds{L})
, we refer to
h_{p}(\mathds{L})
as the
p
-class number of
\mathds{L}
. We set
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
to denote the ring of
p
-adic integers. A
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-extension of a number field
\mathds{L}
is an infinite Galois extension
\mathds{L}_{\infty}/\mathds{L}
, such that
\operatorname{Gal}(\mathds{L}_{\infty}/\mathds{L})
is isomorphic to
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
(as a topological group). Given a
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-extension
\mathds{L}_{\infty}/\mathds{L}
, and

, we set

to be the extension such that
\mathds{L}_{n}\subseteq\mathds{L}_{\infty}
and
[\mathds{L}_{n}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\mathds{L}]=p^{n}
. The field
\mathds{L}_{n}
is the
n

, and we obtain a tower of number fields

\mathds{L}=\mathds{L}_{0}\subseteq\mathds{L}_{1}\subseteq\dots\subseteq\mathds{L}_{n}\subseteq\mathds{L}_{n+1}\subseteq\dots.

Let
e_{n}\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
be such that
p^{e_{n}}=h_{p}(\mathds{L}_{n})

Thus, Iwasawa’s results show that there are interesting growth patterns for
p
-class numbers in certain infinite towers of number fields. These results motivate the study of asymptotic growth properties of
p
Hilbert class field towers along
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-extensions. Throughout,
p
will be a fixed odd prime number, and let
\mathds{L}_{\infty}/\mathds{L}
be a
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-extension. Set
\mathcal{F}(\mathds{L}_{n})
to denote the maximal unramified pro-

The field

&H_{p}^{(1)}(\mathds{L}_{n})\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=H_{p}(\mathds{L}_{n}),\\
&H_{p}^{(j)}(\mathds{L}_{n})\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=H_{p}\left(H_{p}^{(j-1)}(\mathds{L}_{n})\right)\text{ for }j\geq 2.\end{split}

Iwasawa studied asymptotic growth patterns for the degrees

p
groups
G_{n}
as

exponential growth number

\rho(G)
of a finitely generated pro-
p
group
G
, which is a natural invariant associated with its Hilbert series. In this context, there is a natural analogy with the Hilbert series of an algebraic variety. Let
\Omega(G)
denote the mod-
p
Iwasawa algebra associated to
G
, defined as the inverse limit
\varprojlim_{U}\mathbb{F}_{p}[G/U]
, where
U
ranges over all finite index normal subgroups of
G
. Let
I_{G}
be the augmentation ideal of
\Omega(G)
, and for
n\geq 0
, set
c_{n}(G)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\operatorname{dim}_{\mathbb{F}_{p}}\left(I_{G}^{n}/I_{G}^{n+1}\right)
, where it is understood that
c_{0}(G)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=1
. Let

H(G;t)=\sum_{n\geq 0}c_{n}(G)t^{n}

denote the Hilbert series associated to
G
, cf. Definition

G
is finite, then

is a

-adic analytic group of dimension

, we find that

(cf.
[
DdSMS99

-adic analytic groups. For many of the Galois groups constructed in this article, the numbers

increase at an exponential rate. The radius of convergence of

is given by
R_{G}=\rho(G)^{-1}
, where
\rho(G)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\limsup_{n\rightarrow\infty}|c_{n}(G)|^{\frac{1}{n}}
. It is understood that

when
\rho(G)=0
, and in this case,
H(G,t)
is an
entire function
. The constant
\rho(G)
measures the exponential growth of subgroups in the Zassenhaus filtration of
G
. It is clear that if
\rho(G)>1
, then
G
is not an analytic pro-
p
group. It is shown, in various contexts that pro-
p
Hilbert class field towers are infinite via an application of the Golod-Shafarevich-Vinberg criterion (cf.
[
HMR20
, Theorem 1.2]
). This strategy is developed and employed in the following works:
[
VK78
,
Sch86
,
KL87
,
JM11
,
HMR20
,
HMR21
]
. The class of pro-
p
groups for which it can be shown that
\rho(G)>1
, via the Golod-Shafarevich-Vinberg criterion have special properties, and are known as Golod-Shafarevich groups (cf. Definition
2.4
). We refer to
[
Ers12
]
for an introduction to the theory of Golod-Shafarevich groups.

Given a number field
L
, its
root discriminant
is defined to be
D_{L}^{1/n}
, where
D_{L}
is its absolute discriminant, and
n=[L\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\mathbb{Q}]
. It is an old question as to whether there exists an infinite tower of number fields, unramified away from a finite set of primes, for which the root discriminant is bounded. Since the root discriminant is constant in unramified extensions, the above question is related to the constants of Martinet
[
Mar78
]
and Odlyzko’s bounds
[
Odl90
]
. In the number field extensions
L/\mathds{L}_{n}
such that

, the root discriminant remains bounded. Hajir and Maire
[
HM01
]
construct tamely ramified Golod-Shafarevich Galois groups and are able to improve upon Martinet’s constants. These root discriminant bounds are further refined by Hajir, Maire and Ramakrishna in
[
HMR21
]
. One is thus interested in constructing infinite unramified Galois pro-
p
groups
G
, such that
\rho(G)
is large. In this paper, we study the following question.

Question 1.1
.

Let
\mathds{L}
be a number field and
p
be a prime number. Given a
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-extension
\mathds{L}_{\infty}/\mathds{L}
, what can one say about the growth of
\rho(G_{n})
, as
n\rightarrow\infty
?

1.2.
Main results

We consider a variant of the above question, for certain natural
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-extensions in which one of the primes above
p
is infinitely split. For
k\geq 0
, let
\mathcal{F}^{[k]}(\mathds{L}_{n})
be the maximal pro-
p
extension of
\mathds{L}_{n}
for which

•

all primes
v\nmid p
are unramified,

•

all decomposition groups at primes
v|p
are abelian,

•

for all primes
v\mid p
, every element of the decomposition group of
v
has order dividing
p^{k}
.

Note that
\mathcal{F}(\mathds{L}_{n})\supseteq\mathcal{F}^{[0]}(\mathds{L}_{n})
. For
k\geq 1
, there is finite ramification in the extension
\mathcal{F}^{[k]}(\mathds{L}_{n})/\mathds{L}_{n}
. As a consequence, there is an intermediate extension
\mathds{L}_{n}\subseteq\mathds{L}_{n}^{\prime}\subseteq\mathcal{F}^{[k]}(\mathds{L}_{n})
, such that
\mathds{L}_{n}^{\prime}/\mathds{L}_{n}
is a finite extension, and
\mathcal{F}^{[k]}(\mathds{L}_{n})/\mathds{L}_{n}^{\prime}
is unramified (cf. the proof of
[
HMR20
, Proposition 1.5]
). In particular, for all number fields contained in
\mathcal{F}^{[k]}(\mathds{L}_{n})
, the root discriminant is bounded. We shall set
\mathcal{G}^{[k]}(\mathds{L}_{n})\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\operatorname{Gal}(\mathcal{F}^{[k]}(\mathds{L}_{n})/\mathds{L}_{n})
, and
\rho^{[k]}(\mathds{L}_{n})\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\rho\left(\mathcal{G}^{[k]}(\mathds{L}_{n})\right)
.

Let
\mathds{L}
be a number field extension of
\mathbb{Q}
and let
p
be an odd prime. Let
r_{1}
(resp.
r_{2}
) be the number of real embeddings (resp. complex embeddings) of
\mathds{L}
. Let
\mathfrak{p}=\mathfrak{p}_{1},\mathfrak{p}_{2},\dots,\mathfrak{p}_{g}
be the primes of
\mathds{L}
that lie above
p
, and assume that
g\geq 2
. Set
e_{i}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=e(\mathfrak{p}_{i}/p)
(resp.
f_{i}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=f(\mathfrak{p}_{i}/p)
) to denote the ramification index (resp. inertial degree) of
\mathfrak{p}_{i}
over
p
. Note that
d_{i}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=[\mathds{L}_{\mathfrak{p}_{i}}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\mathbb{Q}_{p}]=e_{i}f_{i}
. Let
U_{i}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\mathcal{O}_{\mathds{L}_{\mathfrak{p}_{i}}}^{\times}
and
U_{i}^{(1)}
the principal local units in
U_{i}
. We set
\mathfrak{U}
to denote the product
\prod_{i=2}^{g}U_{i}^{(1)}
, and
\bar{E}
to denote the
p
-adic closure of the image of the group of global units
\mathcal{O}_{\mathds{L}}^{\times}
in
\mathfrak{U}
. Setting
\delta\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\operatorname{rank}_{\mathbb{Z}_{p}}(\bar{E})
, we note that
\delta\leq\operatorname{rank}\mathcal{O}_{\mathds{L}}^{\times}=r_{1}+r_{2}-1
. We set

m\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\operatorname{rank}_{\mathbb{Z}_{p}}\left(\mathfrak{U}/\bar{E}\right)-1=[\mathds{L}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\mathbb{Q}]-d_{1}-\delta-1,

and assume that
m\geq 1
. It follows from a standard application of global class field theory that there exists a
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-extension
\mathds{L}_{\infty}/\mathds{L}
in which
\mathfrak{p}
is completely split. Note that when
\mathds{L}
is totally imaginary, the condition

is automatically satisfied when
[\mathds{L}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\mathbb{Q}]\geq 2(d_{1}+1)
.

Theorem 1.2
.

Assume that the following conditions are satisfied

(1)

p
is odd and there are
g>1
primes of
\mathds{L}
that lie above
p
,

(2)

\mathds{L}
contains
\mu_{p}
, the
p
-th roots of unity,

(3)

[\mathds{L}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\mathbb{Q}]\geq 2\left(d_{1}+1\right)
,

(4)

([\mathds{L}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\mathbb{Q}]+2)^{2}>8\left(\sum_{i=2}^{g}d_{i}^{2}\right)
.

Then, there exists a
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-extension
\mathds{L}_{\infty}/\mathds{L}
in which
\mathfrak{p}_{1}
is totally split. Moreover, there exists a constant
C>0
(independent of
n
and
k
) and
n_{0}\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
, such that for all
n\geq n_{0}
and
k\geq 1
, we have that

\rho^{[k]}(\mathds{L}_{n})\geq Cp^{n}.

Let
\mu(\mathds{L}_{\infty}/\mathds{L})
denote the Iwasawa
\mu
-invariant for the
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-extension
\mathds{L}_{\infty}/\mathds{L}
. Let
T_{1}
be the number of primes of
\mathds{L}
that lie above
p
, that are infinitely decomposed in
\mathds{L}_{\infty}
.
The constant
C
can be chosen to be any value such that

0<C<\frac{2\left(\sum_{i=2}^{g}d_{i}^{2}\right)}{\left(2T_{1}+[\mathds{L}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\mathbb{Q}]+2\mu(\mathds{L}_{\infty}/\mathds{L})\right)}.

Remark 1.3
.

•

We observe that the hypotheses imply that

\frac{2\left(\sum_{i=2}^{g}d_{i}^{2}\right)}{\left(2T_{1}+[\mathds{L}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\mathbb{Q}]+2\mu(\mathds{L}_{\infty}/\mathds{L})\right)}<\left(\frac{[\mathds{L}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\mathbb{Q}]+2}{4}\right).

•

Note that the condition (
2
) implies that
\mathds{L}
is totally imaginary and (

) implies that

.

We prove Theorem

by adapting the strategy of Hajir, Maire and Ramakrishna
[

]
. The following result illustrates Theorem

.

Corollary 1.4
.

Let

\mathds{L}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\mathbb{Q}(\mu_{p\ell})

be the number of primes of

We also define another notion measuring the

2.
Pro-
p
groups and their Hilbert series

We review some preliminaries and set up some basic notation in this section. Throughout,
p
shall be an odd prime. Let
G
be a finitely generated pro-
p
group, set
\Omega(G)
to denote the completed group algebra of
G
over
\mathbb{F}_{p}
. More precisely,
\Omega(G)
is defined to be the inverse limit

\Omega(G)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\varprojlim_{U}\mathbb{F}_{p}[G/U],

where
U
runs over all finite index normal subgroups of
G
. We refer to
\Omega(G)
as the
mod-
p
Iwasawa algebra
of
G
. Many properties of the group
G
are captured by algebraic properties of
\Omega(G)
. We consider the
Hilbert series
associated with
\Omega(G)
. Given a normal subgroup
U
of
G
, there is a natural map
\iota_{U}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}G\rightarrow\mathbb{F}_{p}[G/U]
, which sends
g\in G
to the element
\bar{g}\cdot 1
. Here,
\bar{g}
is the congruence class of
g
modulo
U
. The map
\iota\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}G\rightarrow\Omega(G)
is the inverse limit
\iota\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\varprojlim_{U}\iota_{U}
. The augmentation map
\alpha\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\Omega(G)\rightarrow\mathbb{F}_{p}
maps each element of the form
\iota(g)
to
1
. We shall, by abuse of notation, simply let
g\in\Omega(G)
simply denote the element
\iota(g)
.
Set
I_{G}
to denote the augmentation ideal of
\Omega(G)
, i.e., the kernel of the augmentation map. For
n\geq 1
,
\Omega(G)/I_{G}^{n}
is a finite dimensional
\mathbb{F}_{p}
-vector space.

Definition 2.1
.

For
n\geq 1
, let
c_{n}(G)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\operatorname{dim}_{\mathbb{F}_{p}}\left(I_{G}^{n}/I_{G}^{n+1}\right)
, and set
c_{0}(G)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=1
. The Hilbert series
H(G;t)
is a formal power series defined by
H(G;t)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\sum_{n\geq 0}c_{n}(G)t^{n}
. Setting
\rho(G)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\limsup_{n\rightarrow\infty}|c_{n}(G)|^{\frac{1}{n}}
, we note that the radius of convergence of
H(G;t)
is given by

R_{G}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\begin{cases}{\rho(G)}^{-1}&\text{ if }\rho(G)<\infty;\\
0&\text{ if }\rho(G)=\infty.\\
\end{cases}

Given
x\in G
such that
x\neq 1
, the
depth
of
x
is defined as follows

\omega_{G}(x)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\operatorname{max}\{n\mid x-1\in I_{G}^{n}\}.

We set
\omega_{G}(1)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\infty
.

Definition 2.2
.

The
Zassenhaus filtration
is defined as follows

G_{n}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\{g\in G\mid\omega_{G}(g)\geq n\}.

The sequence
G_{n}
is a sequence of open normal subgroups of
G
. The quotient
G_{n}/G_{n+1}
is a finite dimensional
\mathbb{F}_{p}
vector space, set
a_{n}(G)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\operatorname{dim}_{\mathbb{F}_{p}}\left(G_{n}/G_{n+1}\right)
.
The quantity
\rho(G)
measures the order of exponential growth of the quotients
\left(I_{G}^{n}/I_{G}^{n+1}\right)
as
n\rightarrow\infty
. These numbers are related to the growth of groups in the Zassenhaus filtration. There is an explicit relationship between the numbers
\{a_{n}(G)\}
and
\{c_{n}(G)\}
, established by Mináč, Rogelstad and Tân
[
MRT16
]
.

Assume that
G
is a finitely presented pro-
p
group, and let

\begin{split}&d(G)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\operatorname{dim}_{\mathbb{F}_{p}}H^{1}(G,\mathbb{F}_{p}),\\
&r(G)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\operatorname{dim}_{\mathbb{F}_{p}}H^{2}(G,\mathbb{F}_{p}).\end{split}

Remark 2.1
.

We identify
H^{1}(G,\mathbb{F}_{p})
with
\operatorname{Hom}\left(\frac{G}{[G,G]G^{p}},\mathbb{F}_{p}\right)
, and thus,

d(G)=\dim_{\mathbb{F}_{p}}\left(\frac{G}{[G,G]G^{p}}\right).

If
\mathcal{G}
is a quotient of
G
, then, it is clear that
d(\mathcal{G})\leq d(G)
.

Consider a minimal presentation

(2.1)

1\rightarrow R\rightarrow F\xrightarrow{\varphi}G\rightarrow 1

of
G
. Here,

is a free pro-
p
group generated by

elements. The subgroup

of

is the normal subgroup
\langle\rho_{1},\dots,\rho_{r}\rangle^{\operatorname{Norm}}
generated by

elements. Given a choice of minimal presentation of

, we define the associated Golod-Shafarevich polynomials. Let
\Omega(F)
be the mod-
p
Iwasawa algebra associated to
F
, and
I_{F}
be the augmentation ideal of
\Omega(F)
. By a theorem of Lazard
[

]
, the algebra
\Omega(F)
is isomorphic to the algebra of power series
\mathbb{F}_{p}\langle\langle u_{1},\dots,u_{d}\rangle\rangle
. Here

is identified with the element
(\sigma_{i}-1)
. The augmentation ideal

is generated by

. Let
\omega_{F}
be the depth function on
F
, defined by setting

\omega_{F}(x)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\operatorname{max}\{n\mid x-1\in I_{F}^{n}\},

for

Theorem 2.3

(Vinberg’s criterion)

Let

be a pro-

be the Hilbert series associated to

Proof.

This result is well known. We refer to

The next result shows that if

takes a negative value

in the interval

, then,

. Furthermore, it gives us a lower bound for

, and can therefore be viewed as a refinement of the Golod-Shafarevich-Vinberg criterion (cf.
[

, Theorem 1.2]
).

Proposition 2.4
.

Suppose that for some

, the value

is negative. Then, the order of growth satisfies the lower bound
\rho(G)\geq\frac{1}{t_{0}}
.

Proof.

Let
R_{G}
be the radius of convergence of the Hilbert series

and set
R_{G}^{\prime}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\operatorname{min}\{1,R_{G}\}
. By definition, the coefficients of
H(G;t)
are all non-negative. Then, for
t\in(0,R_{G}^{\prime})
, the series

converges absolutely to a positive value. By Vinberg’s criterion,
P(G;t)H(G;t)\geq 1
for all
t\in(0,R_{G}^{\prime})
. Since
P(G;t_{0})<0
, it follows that

does not converge absolutely at

. In other words,

lies outside the domain of convergence. Therefore,

. Since

, it follows that
R_{G}=R_{G}^{\prime}<1
and therefore,
t_{0}\geq R_{G}=\rho(G)^{-1}
. Therefore, we conclude that
\rho(G)\geq\frac{1}{t_{0}}
.
∎

A pro-

of

Let

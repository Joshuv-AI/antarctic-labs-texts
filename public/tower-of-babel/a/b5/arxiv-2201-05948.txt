2.
Four-Coefficient Sturm–Liouville Operators

In this section we summarize the basics of four-coefficient Sturm–Liouville differential expressions and their associated Hilbert space operators to be used in the sequel. In particular, since the main aim of Section
4
is to characterize the lower bounds of the Friedrichs extension of the underlying minimal operator, we recall several results that connect lower semiboundedness of the minimal operator with non-oscillation properties of the underlying differential expression. These results lead to a characterization of the Friedrichs extension in terms of principal/nonprincipal solutions and generalized boundary values entirely analogous to those introduced for three-coefficient expressions in
[
24
]
. With the exception of Theorems
2.18

and
2.20

and part of Corollary
2.24
, all of the results in this section may be found with complete proofs in
[
19
]
. Although Theorems
2.18

and
2.20

are new in the context of four-coefficient Sturm–Liouville expressions, the proof of Theorem
2.18

is identical to the analogous result for three-coefficient expressions obtained in
[
24
, Theorem 4.5]
, and Theorem
2.20

then follows as a restatement of
[
19
, Theorem 6.4]
in terms of generalized boundary values.

We begin by introducing a basic hypothesis which is assumed throughout this paper.

Hypothesis 2.1
.

Let
-\infty\leqslant a<b\leqslant\infty
. Suppose that
p
,

,

,

(a,b)

Assuming Hypothesis
2.1

\mathfrak{D}_{\tau}((a,b))=\big\{g\in AC_{loc}((a,b))\,\big|\,g^{[1]}=p[g^{\prime}+sg]\in AC_{loc}((a,b))\big\}

and the differential expression

\tau f=\frac{1}{r}\Big(-\big(f^{[1]}\big)^{\prime}+sf^{[1]}+qf\Big)\in L_{loc}^{1}((a,b);r\,dx),\quad f\in\mathfrak{D}_{\tau}((a,b)),

of

In particular, if

, then the Wronskian of two solutions

\begin{split}&f(a):=\lim_{x\downarrow a}f(x),\quad f^{[1]}(a):=\lim_{x\downarrow a}f^{[1]}(x),\\

(2.6)

(

\displaystyle\int_{a}dx\,ph_{a}^{2}\bigg|\bigg(\frac{g}{h_{a}}\bigg)^{\prime}\bigg|^{2}

(2.48)

Finally, (

3.
Strict Domain Monotonicity of the Principal Eigenvalue

of the Friedrichs Extension

We shall assume throughout this section that
\tau
is
regular
on
(a,b)
. In this case,
T_{min}
is lower semibounded by Corollary
2.12
, and its Friedrichs extension
T_{F}
is characterized by Dirichlet boundary conditions according to (
2.47
). The sesquilinear form
Q_{F}
uniquely associated to
T_{F}
is given by (cf.
[
19
, Equation (13.6)]
)

\displaystyle Q_{F}(f,g)

\displaystyle=\int_{a}^{b}dx\,\Big[p(x)^{-1}\overline{f^{[1]}(x)}g^{[1]}(x)+q(x)\overline{f(x)}g(x)\Big]

(3.1)

\displaystyle=\big(|T_{F}|^{1/2}f,\sgn(T_{F})|T_{F}|^{1/2}g\big)_{L^{2}((a,b);r\,dx)},

\displaystyle f,g\in\dom(Q_{F})

\displaystyle=\Bigg\{h\in L^{2}((a,b);r\,dx)\,\Bigg|\,\begin{aligned} &h\in AC([a,b]),\,h(a)=h(b)=0,\\
&(rp)^{-1/2}h^{[1]}\in L^{2}((a,b);r\,dx)\end{aligned}\Bigg\}

(3.2)

\displaystyle=\dom\big(|T_{F}|^{1/2}\big).

In the regular case, every self-adjoint extension of
T_{min}
has a discrete spectrum consisting of isolated eigenvalues with finite (geometric) multiplicities, see
[
19
, Corollary 7.2]
. Actually, in the case of separated boundary conditions, all eigenvalues are simple by Theorem
2.20

(i)
(cf.
[
19
, Corollary 7.4]
); that is, all eigenvalues have multiplicity one. In particular, each eigenvalue of the Friedrichs extension
T_{F}
of
T_{min}
is simple. Next, we turn to strict domain monotonicity of the principal (i.e., smallest) eigenvalue of the Friedrichs extension,
T_{F,(a_{1},b_{1})}
,
-\infty<a_{1}<b_{1}<\infty
, where we temporarily indicate the dependence of all relevant objects (
T_{F}
,
Q_{F}(\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,)
, etc.) on the underlying finite interval
(a_{1},b_{1})
by appending the interval as a subscript (
T_{F,(a_{1},b_{1})}
,
Q_{F,(a_{1},b_{1})}(\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,)
, etc.). We shall denote the corresponding principal eigenvalue of
T_{F,(a_{1},b_{1})}
by

\lambda_{1}(T_{F,(a_{1},b_{1})})
.

The proof of strict domain monotonicity of the principal eigenvalue
\lambda_{1}(T_{F,(a,b)})
of the Friedrichs extension
T_{F,(a,b)}
of
T_{min,(a,b)}
in Theorem
3.4
below, relies on the variational characterization of the principal eigenvalue in terms of Rayleigh quotients. As such, it is convenient to introduce the following notation for the Rayleigh quotient of a lower semibounded self-adjoint operator
T
in a Hilbert space
{\mathcal{H}}
:

R_{T}(f;{\mathcal{H}}):=\frac{\big(|T|^{1/2}f,\sgn(T)|T|^{1/2}f\big)_{{\mathcal{H}}}}{\|f\|_{{\mathcal{H}}}^{2}},\quad f\in\dom\big(|T|^{1/2}\big)\big\backslash\{0\}.

(3.3)

We start by recalling Rayleigh’s principle:

Theorem 3.1
.

Suppose
A
is self-adjoint and bounded from below in
{\mathcal{H}}
, that is, for some
c\in{\mathbb{R}}
,
A\geqslant cI_{{\mathcal{H}}}
. In addition, assume that
\lambda_{0,ess}=\inf(\sigma_{ess}(A))>c
and that

\sigma_{d}(A)\cap[c,\lambda_{0,ess})\neq\emptyset
. Denoting the eigenvalues of
A
below
\lambda_{0,ess}

by
\{\lambda_{j}(A)\}_{j\in J}
, and the associated orthogonal eigenfunctions by
u_{j}
,
J\subseteq{\mathbb{N}}
an appropriate
(
finite or infinite
)
index set, using the natural ordering
\lambda_{1}(A)\leqslant\lambda_{2}(A)\leqslant\cdots

(
taking multiplicities into account
)
, one obtains

\displaystyle\lambda_{1}(A)=\underset{0\neq f\in\dom(A)}{\min}\frac{(f,Af)_{{\mathcal{H}}}}{\|f\|^{2}_{{\mathcal{H}}}}=\underset{0\neq f\in\dom(|A|^{1/2})}{\min}R_{A}(f;{\mathcal{H}}),

(3.4)

and, if
|J|\geqslant 2
,

\displaystyle\begin{split}\lambda_{j}(A)=\underset{\begin{subarray}{c}0\neq f\in\dom(A)\\
(f,u_{k})_{{\mathcal{H}}}=0,\,1\leqslant k\leqslant j-1\end{subarray}}{\min}\frac{(f,Af)_{{\mathcal{H}}}}{\|f\|^{2}_{{\mathcal{H}}}}=\underset{\begin{subarray}{c}0\neq f\in\dom(|A|^{1/2})\\
(f,u_{k})_{{\mathcal{H}}}=0,\,1\leqslant k\leqslant j-1\end{subarray}}{\min}R_{A}(f;{\mathcal{H}}),&\\
j\in J,\;j\geqslant 2.&\end{split}

(3.5)

Here, it is of course understood that
\lambda_{0,ess}=+\infty
if
\sigma_{ess}(A)=\emptyset
. The first parts in (
3.4
) and (
3.5
) are proven via the spectral theorem for
A
in
[
93
, Theorem 1.1]
; their quadratic form versions—the second parts in (
3.4

The following related result will be employed in connection with strict domain monotonicity of the lowest eigenvalue of regular Sturm–Liouville operators with Dirichlet boundary conditions in Theorem
3.4

Theorem 3.2
.

Suppose
A
is self-adjoint and bounded from below in
{\mathcal{H}}
, that is, for some

,

. In addition, assume that
\lambda_{0,ess}(A)=\inf(\sigma_{ess}(A))\in(c,\infty)\cup\{\infty\}
and that

\sigma_{d}(A)\cap[c,\lambda_{0,ess}(A))\neq\emptyset
. We denote the eigenvalues of
A
below

\lambda_{0,ess}(A)
by
\{\lambda_{j}(A)\}_{j\in J}
, and the associated orthogonal eigenfunctions by
u_{j}
,

J\subseteq{\mathbb{N}}
an appropriate
(
finite or infinite
)
index set, using the natural ordering
\lambda_{1}(A)\leqslant\lambda_{2}(A)\leqslant\cdots

(
taking multiplicities into account
)
. If there exist vectors
v_{j}\in\dom(A)\backslash\{0\}
, respectively,

v_{j}\in\dom\big(|A|^{1/2}\big)\big\backslash\{0\}
,
j\in J
, such that

\mu_{1}(A)=\frac{(v_{1},Av_{1})_{{\mathcal{H}}}}{\|v_{1}\|^{2}_{{\mathcal{H}}}}=\underset{0\neq f\in\dom(A)}{\min}\frac{(f,Af)_{{\mathcal{H}}}}{\|f\|^{2}_{{\mathcal{H}}}},

(3.6)

and, if
|J|\geqslant 2

\mu_{j}(A)=\frac{(v_{j},Av_{j})_{{\mathcal{H}}}}{\|v_{j}\|^{2}_{{\mathcal{H}}}}=\underset{\begin{subarray}{c}0\neq f\in\dom(A)\\
(f,v_{k})_{{\mathcal{H}}}=0,\,1\leqslant k\leqslant j-1\end{subarray}}{\min}\frac{(f,Af)_{{\mathcal{H}}}}{\|f\|^{2}_{{\mathcal{H}}}},\quad j\in J,\;j\geqslant 2,

(3.7)

respectively,

\displaystyle\mu_{1}(A)=R_{A}(v_{1};{\mathcal{H}})=\underset{0\neq f\in\dom(|A|^{1/2})}{\min}R_{A}(f;{\mathcal{H}}),

(3.8)

and, if
|J|\geqslant 2
,

\displaystyle\mu_{j}(A)=R_{A}(v_{j};{\mathcal{H}})=\underset{\begin{subarray}{c}0\neq f\in\dom(|A|^{1/2})\\
(f,v_{k})_{{\mathcal{H}}}=0,\,1\leqslant k\leqslant j-1\end{subarray}}{\min}R_{A}(f;{\mathcal{H}}),\quad j\in J,\;j\geqslant 2,

(3.9)

then
\mu_{j}(A)=\lambda_{j}(A)
and

is a corresponding eigenvector,

also satisfies
v_{j}\in\dom(A)
,
j\in J
.

Proof.

Since (

(3.10)

and hence, using

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle\leqslant\varepsilon^{2}\big[\big(|A|^{1/2}w,\sgn(A)|A|^{1/2}w\big)_{{\mathcal{H}}}-\mu_{1}(A)\|w\|_{{\mathcal{H}}}^{2}\big]

\displaystyle\quad+2\varepsilon\text{\rm Re}\big(\big(|A|^{1/2}v_{1},\sgn(A)|A|^{1/2}w\big)_{{\mathcal{H}}}-\mu_{1}(A)(v_{1},w)_{{\mathcal{H}}}\big).

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle=\big(|A|^{1/2}v_{1},\sgn(A)(|A|^{1/2}w\big)_{{\mathcal{H}}}-\mu_{1}(A)(v_{1},w)_{{\mathcal{H}}}

Next, suppose

Remark 3.3
.

Compared to the min–max (resp., max–min) theorem, Theorems

and

have the drawback that they directly involve

knowledge of the eigenvectors

, or, of the vectors

,

, rather than

-dimensional) subspaces,

is instrumental in proving strict domain monotonicity for

(as opposed to just domain monotonicity) as will be illustrated in the proof of Theorem

Theorem 3.4
.

Assume Hypothesis
2.1
and suppose that
\tau
is regular on
(a,b)
. Let
T_{F,(c,d)}
denote the Friedrichs extension of
T_{min,(c,d)}
, where
(c,d)\subset(a,b)
and
c<d
; that is, either
a\leqslant c
and
d<b
, or else
a<c
and
d\leqslant b
, so that
(c,d)
is strictly contained in
(a,b)
. Then

\lambda_{1}(T_{F,(a,b)})<\lambda_{1}(T_{F,(c,d)}).

(3.25)

Proof.

For simplicity we shall assume that
a\leqslant c
and
d<b
. The case
a<c
and
d\leqslant b
is analogous. We begin with the following simple observation. If
f\in\dom\big(|T_{F,(c,d)}|^{1/2}\big)
, then the characterization in (
3.2
) implies the function
\widetilde{f}
defined on
(a,b)
by

\widetilde{f}(x)=\begin{cases}f(x),&x\in(c,d),\\
0,&x\in(a,b)\backslash(c,d),\end{cases}

(3.26)

belongs to
\dom\big(|T_{F,(a,b)}|^{1/2}\big)
and (
3.1
) yields

Q_{F,(c,d)}(f,f)=Q_{F,(a,b)}\big(\widetilde{f},\widetilde{f}\big).

(3.27)

Hence, by the variational characterization of the principal eigenvalue (cf. Theorem
3.1
),

\displaystyle\lambda_{1}(T_{F,(a,b)})

\displaystyle=\min_{0\neq f\in\dom(|T_{F,(a,b)}|^{1/2})}R_{T_{F,(a,b)}}\big(f;L^{2}((a,b);r\,dx)\big)

\displaystyle\leqslant\min_{0\neq f\in\dom(|T_{F,(c,d)}|^{1/2})}R_{T_{F,(c,d)}}\big(f;L^{2}((c,d);r\,dx)\big)

\displaystyle=\lambda_{1}(T_{F,(c,d)}).

(3.28)

To prove the inequality in (
3.28
) is strict, we argue by way of contradiction and assume that
\lambda_{1}(T_{F,(a,b)})=\lambda_{1}(T_{F,(c,d)})
. Let
u_{1,F,(c,d)}\in\dom(T_{F,(c,d)})
denote the eigenfunction (unique up to nonzero constant multiples) corresponding to the principal eigenvalue
\lambda_{1}(T_{F,(c,d)})
of

and introduce the test function

by

v_{1,F,(a,b)}(x):=\widetilde{u}_{1,F,(c,d)}(x)=\begin{cases}u_{1,F,(c,d)}(x),&x\in(c,d),\\
0,&x\in(a,b)\backslash(c,d).\end{cases}

(3.29)

By the observation at the beginning of the proof,

v_{1,F,(a,b)}\in\dom(Q_{F,(a,b)})=\dom\big(|T_{F,(a,b)}|^{1/2}\big),

(3.30)

and (
3.27
) yields

\displaystyle\lambda_{1}(T_{F,(a,b)})

\displaystyle=R_{T_{F,(c,d)}}\big(u_{1,F,(c,d)};L^{2}((c,d);r\,dx)\big)

(3.31)

\displaystyle=R_{T_{F,(a,b)}}\big(v_{1,F,(a,b)};L^{2}((a,b);r\,dx)\big)

\displaystyle=\lambda_{1}(T_{F,(c,d)})

\displaystyle=\min_{0\neq f\in\dom(|T_{F,(a,b)}|^{1/2})}R_{T_{F,(a,b)}}\big(f;L^{2}((a,b);r\,dx)\big).

Therefore,
v_{1,F,(a,b)}
is a minimizer of the Rayleigh quotient for
T_{F,(a,b)}
. In consequence, by Theorem
3.2
,

is an eigenfunction of
T_{F,(a,b)}
corresponding to the eigenvalue

\lambda_{1}(T_{F,(a,b)})
. In particular,
(\tau-\lambda_{1}(T_{F,(a,b)}))v_{1,F,(a,b)}=0
on
(a,b)
. However, since
v_{1,F,(a,b)}
vanishes identically on the open interval
(d,b)
, Theorem
2.3

implies

, which is a contradiction.
∎

4.
Characterization of the Lower Bounds of the Friedrichs Extension in Terms of Strictly Positive Solutions

on
(c,d)

u_{1}(\lambda,\,\cdot\,)
and
u_{2}(\lambda,\,\cdot\,)
are linearly independent on
(c,d)

.

Assume Hypothesis
2.1

(iv)

on
(a,b)

(4.2)

(iii)
Every pair of real-valued linearly independent solutions

,
j\in\{1,2\}
, of
(\tau-\lambda)u=0
on

for every pair of points

with
x_{1}\neq x_{2}
.

(iv)
There exists a strictly positive solution
u_{0}(\lambda,\,\cdot\,)>0
of
(\tau-\lambda)u=0
on
(a,b)

Proof.

To prove the equivalence of statements
(i)
–
(iii)
, let
u_{j}(\lambda,\,\cdot\,)
,
j\in\{1,2\}
, denote linearly independent real-valued solutions of
(\tau-\lambda)u=0
on
(a,b)
. Any real-valued solution

of
(\tau-\lambda)u=0
is then of the form

(4.4)

Let
x_{1},x_{2}\in(a,b)
,
x_{1}\neq x_{2}
. The system

\begin{pmatrix}u_{1}(\lambda,x_{1})&u_{2}(\lambda,x_{1})\\
u_{1}(\lambda,x_{2})&u_{2}(\lambda,x_{2})\\
\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}c_{1}\\
c_{2}\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}u_{1}\\
u_{2}\end{pmatrix}

(4.5)

has a unique solution for all values

,
j\in\{1,2\}
, if and only if (
4.3
) holds. This yields the equivalence of statements
(ii)
and
(iii)
. Alternatively, (
4.5
) has a unique solution for all values
u_{j}\in{\mathbb{R}}
,
j\in\{1,2\}
, if and only if

\begin{pmatrix}u_{1}(\lambda,x_{1})&u_{2}(\lambda,x_{1})\\
u_{1}(\lambda,x_{2})&u_{2}(\lambda,x_{2})\\
\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}c_{1}\\
c_{2}\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}0\\
0\end{pmatrix}\,\text{ implies $c_{1}=c_{2}=0$.}

(4.6)

In turn, (
4.6
) holds if and only if the only solution
u_{*}(\lambda,\,\cdot\,)
of
(\tau-\lambda)u=0
on
(a,b)
that has zeros at

and
x_{2}
is
u_{*}(\lambda,\,\cdot\,)\equiv 0
. This yields the equivalence of statements
(i)
and
(ii)
.

The proof can be completed by establishing the equivalence of statements
(i)
and
(iv)
. To prove that
(i)
implies
(iv)
, we argue by way of contradiction. Assume
\tau-\lambda
is disconjugate on
(a,b)
and that every real-valued solution of
(\tau-\lambda)u=0
on
(a,b)
has at least one zero in
(a,b)
. Since
\tau-\lambda
is disconjugate, every nontrivial real-valued solution of
(\tau-\lambda)u=0
has at most one zero in
(a,b)
. Thus, every nontrivial real-valued solution of
(\tau-\lambda)u=0
on
(a,b)
has exactly one zero in
(a,b)
. Choose linearly independent real-valued solutions
u_{j}(\lambda,\,\cdot\,)
,
j\in\{1,2\}
, of
(\tau-\lambda)u=0
on
(a,b)
and distinct points
x_{1},x_{2}\in(a,b)
with

\begin{split}&u_{1}(\lambda,x_{1})=0,\quad u_{1}^{[1]}(\lambda,x_{1})=u_{1}(\lambda,x_{2}),\\
&u_{2}(\lambda,x_{2})=0,\quad u_{2}^{[1]}(\lambda,x_{2})=u_{2}(\lambda,x_{1}).\end{split}

(4.7)

Note that
u_{1}(\lambda,x_{2})\neq 0
and
u_{2}(\lambda,x_{1})\neq 0
by hypothesis. Since the
u_{j}(\lambda,\,\cdot\,)
,
j\in\{1,2\}
, are linearly independent solutions, their Wronskian (cf. (
2.4
)) is a nonzero constant, say
W(u_{1}(\lambda,\,\cdot\,),u_{2}(\lambda,\,\cdot\,))(x)=c\neq 0
,
x\in(a,b)
. Separately evaluating the Wronskian at
x=x_{1}
and
x=x_{2}
, one obtains, by (
4.7
),

\begin{split}&0\neq c=W(u_{1}(\lambda,\,\cdot\,),u_{2}(\lambda,\,\cdot\,))(x_{1})=-u_{1}(\lambda,x_{2})u_{2}(\lambda,x_{1}),\\
&0\neq c=W(u_{1}(\lambda,\,\cdot\,),u_{2}(\lambda,\,\cdot\,))(x_{2})=u_{1}(\lambda,x_{2})u_{2}(\lambda,x_{1}).\end{split}

(4.8)

The identities in (

) immediately imply

0\neq 2c=u_{1}(\lambda,x_{2})u_{2}(\lambda,x_{1})-u_{1}(\lambda,x_{2})u_{2}(\lambda,x_{1})=0,

(4.9)

which is an obvious contradiction.

Finally, to prove that
(iv)
implies
(i)
, suppose that
u_{1}(\lambda,\,\cdot\,)>0
is a strictly positive solution of
(\tau-\lambda)u=0
on
(a,b)
. Fixing a point

applied to

implies that all real-valued solutions of

on

are given by

u(\lambda,x)=u_{1}(\lambda,x)\bigg[c_{1}+c_{2}\int_{x_{0}}^{x}\frac{dt}{p(t)u_{1}(\lambda,t)^{2}}\bigg],\quad c_{j}\in{\mathbb{R}},\,j\in\{1,2\},\,x\in(a,b).

(4.10)

The function
\int_{x_{0}}^{x}dt\,\big[p(t)u_{1}(\lambda,t)^{2}\big]^{-1}
is strictly increasing on
(a,b)
, so the factor in square brackets in (
4.10
) has at most one zero in
(a,b)
. Since
u_{1}(\lambda,\,\cdot\,)
does not vanish in
(a,b)
, it follows from (

) that every real-valued solution of
(\tau-\lambda)u=0
on
(a,b)
has at most one zero in
(a,b)
. Thus,
\tau-\lambda
is disconjugate on
(a,b)
.
∎

In the following theorem, the principal result of this paper, we show that
\lambda_{0}\in{\mathbb{R}}
is a lower bound of
T_{F}
(equivalently, a lower bound of
T_{min}
) if and only if
(\tau-\lambda_{0})u=0
has a strictly positive solution on
(a,b)
. In turn, this is equivalent to
\tau-\lambda_{0}
on
(a,b)
being disconjugate on
(a,b)
.

Theorem 4.3
.

Assume Hypothesis
2.1
and let
\lambda_{0}\in{\mathbb{R}}
. Then the following statements
(i)
–
(iv)
are equivalent.

(i)

T_{min}\geqslant\lambda_{0}I_{L^{2}((a,b);r\,dx)}
.

(ii)

T_{F}\geqslant\lambda_{0}I_{L^{2}((a,b);r\,dx)}
.

(iii)

\tau-\lambda_{0}
is disconjugate on
(a,b)
.

(iv)
There exists a strictly positive solution
u_{0}(\lambda_{0},\,\cdot\,)>0
of
(\tau-\lambda_{0})u=0
on
(a,b)
.

Proof.

The equivalence of statements
(i)
and
(ii)
follows immediately from the abstract fact that a densely defined lower semibounded operator and its Friedrichs extension share the same greatest lower bound (cf., e.g.,
[
88
, Theorem 10.17
(i)
]
). Theorem
4.2

immediately implies the equivalence of statements
(iii)
and
(iv)
. To complete the proof, we show that
(iv)
implies
(i)
and that
(i)
implies
(iii)
.

To prove that
(iv)
implies
(i)
, let
u_{0}(\lambda_{0},\,\cdot\,)>0
be a strictly positive solution of
(\tau-\lambda_{0})u=0
on
(a,b)
. In order to show
(i)
, it suffices to prove
\overset{\textbf{\Large.}}{T}\geqslant\lambda_{0}I_{L^{2}((a,b);r\,dx)}
, since
T_{min}
is the closure of
\overset{\textbf{\Large.}}{T}
. Let
f\in\dom\big(\overset{\textbf{\Large.}}{T}\big)
. Since
(\tau-\lambda_{0})u_{0}(\lambda_{0},\,\cdot\,)=0
a.e. on
(a,b)
and
u_{0}(\lambda_{0},\,\cdot\,)
does not vanish on
(a,b)
,
q
may be recovered pointwise a.e. on
(a,b)
by

q=\lambda_{0}r-s\frac{u_{0}^{[1]}(\lambda,\,\cdot\,)}{u_{0}(\lambda,\,\cdot\,)}+\frac{\big(u_{0}^{[1]}(\lambda,\,\cdot\,)\big)^{\prime}}{u_{0}(\lambda,\,\cdot\,)}\,\text{ a.e.~on $\in(a,b)$}.

(4.11)

In addition, we recall the following version of Jacobi’s factorization identity (cf.
[
19
, Equation (11.66)]
):

\displaystyle-\big(f^{[1]}\big)^{\prime}+sf^{[1]}+\frac{(u_{0}^{[1]}(\lambda_{0},\,\cdot\,))^{\prime}}{u_{0}(\lambda_{0},\,\cdot\,)}f-s\frac{u_{0}^{[1]}(\lambda_{0},\,\cdot\,)}{u_{0}(\lambda_{0},\,\cdot\,)}f

(4.12)

\displaystyle\quad=-\frac{1}{u_{0}(\lambda_{0},\,\cdot\,)}\bigg[pu_{0}(\lambda_{0},\,\cdot\,)^{2}\bigg(\frac{f}{u_{0}(\lambda_{0},\,\cdot\,)}\bigg)^{\prime}\bigg]^{\prime}\,\text{ a.e.~in $(a,b)$},

Applying (
4.12
), (
4.11
), and the fact that
f
has compact support in
(a,b)
to integrate by parts,

\begin{split}&\big(f,\overset{\textbf{\Large.}}{T}f\big)_{L^{2}((a,b);r\,dx)}\\
&\quad=\lambda_{0}\|f\|_{L^{2}((a,b);r\,dx)}^{2}+\int_{a}^{b}dx\,p(x)u_{0}(\lambda_{0},x)^{2}\bigg|\bigg(\frac{f(x)}{u_{0}(\lambda_{0},x)}\bigg)^{\prime}\bigg|^{2}.\end{split}

(4.13)

The integral on the right-hand side in (
4.13
) is nonnegative. Since
f\in\dom\big(\overset{\textbf{\Large.}}{T}\big)
was arbitrary, it follows that
\overset{\textbf{\Large.}}{T}\geqslant\lambda_{0}I_{L^{2}((a,b);r\,dx)}
.

To prove
(i)
implies
(iii)
we argue by way of contradiction. Assume that
(i)
holds and
\tau-\lambda_{0}
is not disconjugate on
(a,b)
. Statement
(i)
implies

T_{F}\geqslant\lambda_{0}I_{L^{2}((a,b);r\,dx)},

(4.14)

and since
\tau-\lambda_{0}
is not disconjugate on
(a,b)
, there exists a nontrivial real-valued solution
u_{1}(\lambda_{0},\,\cdot\,)
of
(\tau-\lambda_{0})u=0
on
(a,b)
with two consecutive zeros
x_{1},x_{2}\in(a,b)
,
x_{1}<x_{2}
. Without loss of generality, we may assume that
u_{1}(\lambda_{0},\,\cdot\,)>0
on
(x_{1},x_{2})
. Let
T_{F,(x_{1},x_{2})}
denote the Friedrichs extension of the minimal operator
T_{min,(x_{1},x_{2})}
corresponding to the restriction of
\tau
to the interval
(x_{1},x_{2})
, that is,
\tau|_{(x_{1},x_{2})}
, and let
\lambda_{1}(T_{F,(x_{1},x_{2})})
denote the principal eigenvalue of
T_{F,(x_{1},x_{2})}
. One notes that the differential expression
\tau|_{(x_{1},x_{2})}
is regular on
(x_{1},x_{2})
. Invoking (
3.1
), a calculation analogous to (
4.13

\displaystyle Q_{F,(x_{1},x_{2})}(f,f)

\displaystyle=\lambda_{0}\|f\|_{L^{2}((x_{1},x_{2});r\,dx)}^{2}+\int_{x_{1}}^{x_{2}}dx\,p(x)u_{0}(\lambda_{0},x)^{2}\bigg|\bigg(\frac{f(x)}{u_{0}(\lambda_{0},x)}\bigg)^{\prime}\bigg|^{2}

\displaystyle\geqslant\lambda_{0}\|f\|_{L^{2}((x_{1},x_{2});r\,dx)}^{2},\quad f\in\dom\big(|T_{F,(x_{1},x_{2})}|^{1/2}\big)\backslash\{0\}.

(4.15)

Note that equality holds in (

f=u_{0}(\lambda_{0},\,\cdot\,)|_{(x_{1},x_{2})}
. Therefore, it follows from Theorem
3.2
and the simplicity of the eigenvalues of

that
\lambda_{0}=\lambda_{1}(T_{F,(x_{1},x_{2})})
and

is a corresponding strictly positive eigenfunction which is unique up to constant multiples. Choose
x_{3}\in(a,x_{1})
and
x_{4}\in(x_{2},b)
such that the following chain of inclusions holds

(x_{1},x_{2})\subset(x_{3},x_{4})\subset(a,b),

(4.16)

and let
T_{F,(x_{3},x_{4})}
in

\displaystyle\lambda_{0}\leqslant\lambda_{1}(T_{F})

We conclude by recalling an intimate connection between nonoscillatory behavior and the limit point case for
\tau
at an endpoint. This result is well-known within the context of traditional three-term Sturm–Liouville differential expressions of the form
\tau u=r^{-1}[-(pu^{\prime})^{\prime}+qu]
. It was first derived by Hartman
[
34
]
in the particular case
r=1
in 1948. Three years later, in 1951, Rellich
[
75
]
extended the result to the general three-term case under some additional smoothness assumptions on
p,q,r
; for additional results in this direction see
[
23
, Lemma C.1]
,
[
59
, Sect. 5]
,
[
73
, Corollary 3.2]
.

Theorem 4.5

(see
[
19
, Theorem 11.7, Corollary 11.8]
)
.

Assume Hypothesis
2.1
, let
c\in(a,b)
, and suppose that
\tau-\lambda
is nonoscillatory at
b
for some

\lambda\in{\mathbb{R}}
. If

\int_{c}^{b}|r(x)/p(x)|^{1/2}dx=\infty,

(4.20)

then
\tau
is in the limit point case at
b
. An analogous result holds at
a
, in particular, if
\tau-\lambda_{a}
is nonoscillatory at
a
for some

\lambda_{a}\in{\mathbb{R}}
and
\tau-\lambda_{b}
is nonoscillatory at
b
for some
\lambda_{b}\in{\mathbb{R}}
, and

\int_{a}^{c}dx\,|r(x)/p(x)|^{1/2}=\infty,\quad\int_{c}^{b}dx\,|r(x)/p(x)|^{1/2}=\infty,

(4.21)

then
\overset{\textbf{\Large.}}{T}_{min}
is essentially self-adjoint and hence
T_{min}=T_{max}
is self-adjoint.

Proof.

For convenience of the reader we present Hartman’s short argument as used in

[
19
, Theorem 11.7]
. It suffices to treat the case where
\tau-\lambda
is nonoscillatory at
b
. Then by Theorem
2.8
there exists a principal solution, say
u_{b}(\lambda,\,\cdot\,)
, of
\tau u=\lambda u
. If
x_{0}
strictly exceeds the largest zero of
u_{b}(\lambda,\,\cdot\,)
in
(c,b)
, then
\widehat{u}_{b}(\lambda,\,\cdot\,)
defined by

\widehat{u}_{b}(\lambda,x)=u_{b}(\lambda,x)\int_{x_{0}}^{x}\frac{dx^{\prime}}{p(x^{\prime})u_{b}(\lambda,x^{\prime})^{2}},\quad x\in(x_{0},b),

(4.22)

is a nonprincipal solution on

by Theorem
2.10
, and consequently,

\int_{x_{0}}^{b}\frac{dx}{|p(x)|\widehat{u}_{b}(\lambda,x)^{2}}<\infty.

(4.23)

Arguing by contradiction, and hence assuming

to be in the limit circle case at

\int_{x_{0}}^{b}r(x)dx\,\widehat{u}_{b}(\lambda,x)^{2}<\infty.

(4.24)

Consequently, Hölder’s inequality yields the contradiction,

\displaystyle\int_{x_{0}}^{b}dx\,|r(x)/p(x)|^{1/2}\leqslant\bigg|\int_{x_{0}}^{b}r(x)dx\,\widehat{u}_{b}(\lambda,x)^{2}\bigg|^{1/2}\bigg|\int_{x_{0}}^{b}\frac{dx}{|p(x)|\widehat{u}_{b}(\lambda,x)^{2}}\bigg|^{1/2}<\infty.

(4.25)

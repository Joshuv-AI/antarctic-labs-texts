The classical Lie-Yamaguti Yang-Baxter equation and Lie-Yamaguti bialgebras Thanks: Mathematics Subject Classification (2020): 17B38, 16T25, 17A30, 17B81
Jia Zhao, Yu Qiao*

Abstract
. In this paper, we develop the bialgebra theory for Lie-Yamaguti algebras. For this purpose, we exploit two types of compatibility conditions: local cocycle condition and double construction. We define the classical Yang-Baxter equation in Lie-Yamaguti algebras and show that a solution to the classical Yang-Baxter equation corresponds to a relative Rota-Baxter operator with respect to the coadjoint representation. Furthermore, we generalize some results by Bai in [ 1 ] and Semonov-Tian-Shansky in [ 19 ] to the context of Lie-Yamaguti algebras. Then we introduce the notion of matched pairs of Lie-Yamaguti algebras, which leads us to the concept of double construction Lie-Yamaguti bialgebras following the Manin triple approach to Lie bialgebras. We prove that matched pairs, Manin triples of Lie-Yamaguti algebras, and double construction Lie-Yamaguti bialgebras are equivalent. Finally, we clarify that a local cocycle condition is a special case of a double construction for Lie-Yamaguti bialgebras.



1.
Introduction

Roughly speaking, a bialgebra structure on a given algebra
\mathfrak{g}
is endowed with a compatible coalgebra structure on
\mathfrak{g}
. For instance, a Lie bialgebra is a Lie algebra
(\mathfrak{g},[\cdot,\cdot])
together with a cobracket
\delta:\mathfrak{g}\longrightarrow\otimes^{2}\mathfrak{g}
such that
\delta^{*}:\otimes^{2}\mathfrak{g}^{*}\longrightarrow\mathfrak{g}^{*}
is also a Lie algebra structure on
\mathfrak{g}^{*}
and a certain compatibility condition is satisfied. As we all know, compatibility conditions for a Lie bialgebra can be expressed as three aspects: derivation condition, cocycle condition, and double construction. A Lie bialgebra enjoys an elegant property that these conditions are equivalent and that every condition has its own advantage. More precisely, since the corresponding exterior algebra
\wedge^{\bullet}\mathfrak{g}
is in fact a graded Lie algebra, the cobracket
\delta
can be seen as a derivation on
\wedge^{\bullet}\mathfrak{g}
. Thus the derivation condition reads that

\delta[x,y]=[\delta(x),y]+[x,\delta(y)],\quad\forall x,y\in\mathfrak{g}.

The notation
[x,\delta(y)]
means that
(\mathrm{ad}_{x}\otimes{{\rm{Id}}}+{{\rm{Id}}}\otimes\mathrm{ad}_{x})\delta(y)
, where
\mathrm{ad}:\mathfrak{g}\longrightarrow\mathfrak{gl}(\mathfrak{g})
is the adjoint representation of
\mathfrak{g}
. The cocycle condition can be read that the cobracket
\delta
is a
1
-cocycle on
\mathfrak{g}
with coefficients in the tensor representation
\mathrm{ad}\otimes{{\rm{Id}}}+{{\rm{Id}}}\otimes\mathrm{ad}
, for we are able to form the tensor representation of two representations. Finally, the double construction is that there is a Lie algebra structure on
\mathfrak{g}\oplus\mathfrak{g}^{*}
together with a nondegenerate and symmetric bilinear form.

Parallel to Lie bialgebras, there exist three types of compatibility conditions for
3
-Lie bialgebras:
derivation condition, cocycle condition, and double construction.

[

The derivation condition was referred in
[

3

, thus Bai, Guo, and Sheng investigated cocycle conditions and double constructions as compatibility conditions in

Since there is no tensor representation on
3
-Lie algebras, cocycle condition does not fit to study
3
-Lie bialgebras. However, for a given

Due to the importance of bialgebras and Lie-Yamaguti algebras, it is natural to develop a bialgebra theory for Lie-Yamaguti algebras. Motivated by Lie bialgebras and
3
-Lie bialgebras, one considers to define a Lie-Yamaguti bialgebra structure on a Lie-Yamaguti algebra
(\mathfrak{g},[\cdot,\cdot],\left\llbracket\cdot,\cdot,\cdot\right\rrbracket)
as a pair of two cobrackets
(\delta,\omega)
, where
\delta:\mathfrak{g}\longrightarrow\otimes^{2}\mathfrak{g}
and
\omega:\mathfrak{g}\longrightarrow\otimes^{3}\mathfrak{g}
, such that one of the following compatibility conditions is satisfied:

•

derivation condition: the cobrackets
\delta
and
\omega
is a derivation on
\wedge^{\bullet}\mathfrak{g}
with respect to the binary and ternary brackets respectively, i.e.,

\displaystyle\delta([x,y])

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle[\delta(x),y]+[x,\delta(y)],

\displaystyle\omega(\left\llbracket x,y,z\right\rrbracket)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left\llbracket\omega(x),y,z\right\rrbracket+\left\llbracket x,\omega(y),z\right\rrbracket+\left\llbracket x,y,\omega(z)\right\rrbracket,\quad\forall x,y,z\in\mathfrak{g};

•

cocycle condition: the cobrackets
\delta
and
\omega
are
1
-cocycles of
\mathfrak{g}
with respect to a certain representation;

•

double construction: there is a Lie-Yamaguti algebra structure on
\mathfrak{g}\oplus\mathfrak{g}^{*}
together with a symmetric, nondegenerate bilinear form.

Since we have not found a suitable Lie-Yamaguti algebra structure on the exterior algebra
\wedge^{\bullet}\mathfrak{g}
so far, the derivation condition is not considered in this paper. Therefore firstly we investigate the cocycle condition in Section 3 after a preparation in Section 2. Since there is
no
natural tensor representation of a Lie-Yamaguti algebra
\mathfrak{g}
, so we decided to use the
local cocycle condition
as the compatibility condition parallel to that of

-Lie bialgebras in
[

(\otimes^{2}\mathfrak{g};{{\rm{Id}}}\otimes\mathrm{ad},{{\rm{Id}}}\otimes\mathcal{R}),~(\otimes^{2}\mathfrak{g};\mathrm{ad}\otimes{{\rm{Id}}},\mathcal{R}\otimes{{\rm{Id}}})
and
(\otimes^{3}\mathfrak{g};\mathrm{ad}\otimes{{\rm{Id}}}\otimes{{\rm{Id}}},\mathcal{R}\otimes{{\rm{Id}}}\otimes{{\rm{Id}}}),~(\otimes^{3}\mathfrak{g};{{\rm{Id}}}\otimes\mathrm{ad}\otimes{{\rm{Id}}},{{\rm{Id}}}\otimes\mathcal{R}\otimes{{\rm{Id}}}),~(\otimes^{3}\mathfrak{g};{{\rm{Id}}}\otimes{{\rm{Id}}}\otimes\mathrm{ad},{{\rm{Id}}}\otimes{{\rm{Id}}}\otimes\mathcal{R})
are representations of a Lie-Yamaguti algebra
\mathfrak{g}
, where
(\mathfrak{g};\mathrm{ad},\mathcal{R})
is the adjoint representation of
\mathfrak{g}
, thus we modify the cocycle condition as follows (Definition
3.18
):

•

\delta_{1}
is a
1
-cocycle with respect to the representation
(\otimes^{2}\mathfrak{g};{{\rm{Id}}}\otimes\mathrm{ad},{{\rm{Id}}}\otimes\mathcal{R})
;

•

\delta_{2}
is a
1
-cocycle with respect to the representation
(\otimes^{2}\mathfrak{g};\mathrm{ad}\otimes{{\rm{Id}}},\mathcal{R}\otimes{{\rm{Id}}})
;

•

\omega_{1}
is a
1
-cocycle with respect to the representation
(\otimes^{3}\mathfrak{g};\mathrm{ad}\otimes{{\rm{Id}}}\otimes{{\rm{Id}}},\mathcal{R}\otimes{{\rm{Id}}}\otimes{{\rm{Id}}})
;

•

\omega_{2}
is a
1
-cocycle with respect to the representation
(\otimes^{3}\mathfrak{g};{{\rm{Id}}}\otimes\mathrm{ad}\otimes{{\rm{Id}}},{{\rm{Id}}}\otimes\mathcal{R}\otimes{{\rm{Id}}})
;

•

\omega_{3}
is a
1
-cocycle with respect to the representation
(\otimes^{3}\mathfrak{g};{{\rm{Id}}}\otimes{{\rm{Id}}}\otimes\mathrm{ad},{{\rm{Id}}}\otimes{{\rm{Id}}}\otimes\mathcal{R})
,

where
\delta=\delta_{1}+\delta_{2}
and
\omega=\omega_{1}+\omega_{2}+\omega_{3}
are cobrackets on
\mathfrak{g}
. Moreover, we define the classical Yang-Baxter equation in Lie-Yamaguti algebras, but its solution
fails
to give rise to a local cocycle Lie-Yamaguti bialgebra structure. However, we find that a solution to the classical Yang-Baxter equation is one-to-one correspondence to a relative Rota-Baxter operator with respect to the coadjoint representation. That is, we have the following theorem.

Theorem 1
.

(Theorem
3.6
)
A skew-symmetric
2
-tensor
r\in\otimes^{2}\mathfrak{g}
is a solution to the classical Lie-Yamaguti Yang-Baxter equation if and only if the induced map
r^{\sharp}:\mathfrak{g}^{*}\longrightarrow\mathfrak{g}
is a relative Rota-Baxter operator with respect to the coadjoint representation, where
\left\langle r^{\sharp}(\xi),\eta\right\rangle=\left\langle r,\xi\otimes\eta\right\rangle
, for all
\xi,\eta\in\mathfrak{g}^{*}
.

Furthermore, we generalize some results in
[
1
]
and in
[
19
]
by Bai and Semonov-Tian-Shansky respectively to the context of Lie-Yamaguti algebras.

In Section 4, motivated by the double of a Lie bialgebra, it is natural to consider the
double construction
as a compatibility condition for a Lie-Yamaguti bialgebra.

In order to extend this approach to the context of Lie-Yamaguti algebras, we introduce the notions of Manin triples and matched pairs of Lie-Yamaguti algebras. Moreover, we prove that matched pairs, Manin triples of Lie-Yamaguti algebras, and double construction Lie-Yamaguti bialgebras are equivalent. That is the following vital theorem.

Theorem 2
.

(Theorem
4.13
)
Let
(\mathfrak{g},[\cdot,\cdot],\left\llbracket\cdot,\cdot,\cdot\right\rrbracket)
be a Lie-Yamaguti algebra, and
\delta:\mathfrak{g}\longrightarrow\otimes^{2}\mathfrak{g}
and
\omega:\mathfrak{g}\longrightarrow\otimes^{3}\mathfrak{g}
linear maps. Suppose that a pair of structure maps
(\delta^{*},\omega^{*})
defines a Lie-Yamaguti algebra structure on
\mathfrak{g}^{*}
. Then the following statements are equivalent:

(1)

(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g}^{*})
is a double construction Lie-Yamaguti bialgebra;

(2)

the quadruple
\Big(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g}^{*};(\mathrm{ad}^{*},-\mathcal{R}^{*}\tau),(\mathfrak{ad}^{*},-\mathfrak{R}^{*}\tau)\Big)
is a matched pair of Lie-Yamaguti algebras, where
(\mathrm{ad}^{*},-\mathcal{R}^{*}\tau)
and
(\mathfrak{ad}^{*},-\mathfrak{R}^{*}\tau)
are the coadjoint representations of
\mathfrak{g}
and
\mathfrak{g}^{*}
on
\mathfrak{g}^{*}
and
\mathfrak{g}
respectively;

(3)

the triple
\Big(\mathfrak{g}\oplus\mathfrak{g}^{*},\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g}^{*}\Big)
is a Manin triple of Lie-Yamaguti algebras.

Similar to the case of
3
-Lie bialgebras, local cocycle condition and double construction for a Lie-Yamaguti bialgebra are
not
equivalent as compatibility conditions. In fact, a local cocycle condition is a special case of double construction, which implies that properties of ternary operations on Lie-Yamaguti algebras or
3
-Lie algebras are quite different from those of binary operations on Lie algebras.

2.
Preliminaries

All vector spaces occurring in the article are assumed to be over a field of characteristic zero and finite-dimensional.
In this section, we briefly recall some basic notions such as Lie-Yamaguti algebras, representations and their cohomology theory. In particular, the coadjoint representation of a Lie-Yamaguti algebra is a vital object in this paper.

Definition 2.1
.

[
15
]

A
Lie-Yamaguti algebra
is a vector space
\mathfrak{g}
together with a bilinear bracket
[\cdot,\cdot]:\wedge^{2}\mathfrak{g}\to\mathfrak{g}
and a trilinear bracket
\left\llbracket\cdot,\cdot,\cdot\right\rrbracket:\wedge^{2}\mathfrak{g}\otimes\mathfrak{g}\to\mathfrak{g}
, such that the following conditions hold

\displaystyle[[x,y],z]+[[y,z],x]+[[z,x],y]+\left\llbracket x,y,z\right\rrbracket+\left\llbracket y,z,x\right\rrbracket+\left\llbracket z,x,y\right\rrbracket=0,

\displaystyle\left\llbracket[x,y],z,w\right\rrbracket+\left\llbracket[y,z],x,w\right\rrbracket+\left\llbracket[z,x],y,w\right\rrbracket=0,

\displaystyle\left\llbracket x,y,[z,w]\right\rrbracket=[\left\llbracket x,y,z\right\rrbracket,w]+[z,\left\llbracket x,y,w\right\rrbracket],

\displaystyle\left\llbracket x,y,\left\llbracket z,w,t\right\rrbracket\right\rrbracket=\left\llbracket\left\llbracket x,y,z\right\rrbracket,w,t\right\rrbracket+\left\llbracket z,\left\llbracket x,y,w\right\rrbracket,t\right\rrbracket+\left\llbracket z,w,\left\llbracket x,y,t\right\rrbracket\right\rrbracket,

for all
x,y,z,w,t\in\mathfrak{g}
. We denote a Lie-Yamaguti algebra by
(\mathfrak{g},[\cdot,\cdot],\left\llbracket\cdot,\cdot,\cdot\right\rrbracket)
.

Note that a Lie-Yamaguti algebra
(\mathfrak{g},[\cdot,\cdot],\left\llbracket\cdot,\cdot,\cdot\right\rrbracket)
with
[x,y]=0
for all
x,y\in\mathfrak{g}
reduces to a Lie triple system, while with
\left\llbracket x,y,z\right\rrbracket=0
for all
x,y,z\in\mathfrak{g}
it reduces to a Lie algebra.

The following example is taken from
[
18
]
.

Let

1

the set of vector fields on

\displaystyle[x,y]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-T(x,y),

\displaystyle\left\llbracket x,y,z\right\rrbracket

\displaystyle=

The notion of representations of Lie-Yamaguti algebras was introduced in
[
26

Definition 2.3
.

Let
(\mathfrak{g},[\cdot,\cdot],\left\llbracket\cdot,\cdot,\cdot\right\rrbracket)
be a Lie-Yamaguti algebra. A
representation
of
\mathfrak{g}
is a vector space
V
endowed with a linear map
\rho:\mathfrak{g}\to\mathfrak{gl}(V)
and a bilinear map
\mu:\otimes^{2}\mathfrak{g}\to\mathfrak{gl}(V)
, which satisfies the following conditions for all

,

\displaystyle\mu([x,y],z)-\mu(x,z)\rho(y)+\mu(y,z)\rho(x)=0,

\displaystyle\mu(x,[y,z])-\rho(y)\mu(x,z)+\rho(z)\mu(x,y)=0,

\displaystyle\rho(\left\llbracket x,y,z\right\rrbracket)=[D_{\rho,\mu}(x,y),\rho(z)],

\displaystyle\mu(z,w)\mu(x,y)-\mu(y,w)\mu(x,z)-\mu(x,\left\llbracket y,z,w\right\rrbracket)+D_{\rho,\mu}(y,z)\mu(x,w)=0,

\displaystyle\mu(\left\llbracket x,y,z\right\rrbracket,w)+\mu(z,\left\llbracket x,y,w\right\rrbracket)=[D_{\rho,\mu}(x,y),\mu(z,w)],

where
D_{\rho,\mu}
is given by

(1)

\displaystyle D_{\rho,\mu}(x,y)=\mu(y,x)-\mu(x,y)+[\rho(x),\rho(y)]-\rho([x,y]),\quad\forall x,y\in\mathfrak{g}.

It is easy to see that
D_{\rho,\mu}
is skew-symmetric. We denote a representation of
\mathfrak{g}
by
(V;\rho,\mu)
. In the sequel, we write
D_{\rho,\mu}
as
D
for short without confusion.

Note that the notion of representations of Lie-Yamaguti algebras is also a generalization of that of Lie algebras or Lie triple systems. By a direct computation, we have the following proposition.

Proposition 2.4
.

If
(V;\rho,\mu)
is a representation of a Lie-Yamaguti algebra
(\mathfrak{g},[\cdot,\cdot],\left\llbracket\cdot,\cdot,\cdot\right\rrbracket)

\displaystyle D([x,y],z)+D([y,z],x)+D([z,x],y)=0;

\displaystyle D(\left\llbracket x,y,z\right\rrbracket,w)+D(z,\left\llbracket x,y,w\right\rrbracket)=[D(x,y),D(z,w)];

\displaystyle\mu(\left\llbracket x,y,z\right\rrbracket,w)=\mu(x,w)\mu(z,y)-\mu(y,w)\mu(z,x)-\mu(z,w)D(x,y),

for all
x,y,z,w\in\mathfrak{g}

Example 2.5
.

Let
(\mathfrak{g},[\cdot,\cdot],\left\llbracket\cdot,\cdot,\cdot\right\rrbracket)

Theorem 3.6
.

\displaystyle(T\circ\mathcal{B}^{\natural})\Big([T\circ\mathcal{B}^{\natural}(x),y]+[x,T\circ\mathcal{B}^{\natural}(y)]\Big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle T\Big(\mathcal{B}^{\natural}(\mathrm{ad}_{T\circ\mathcal{B}^{\natural}(x)}y)-\mathcal{B}^{\natural}(\mathrm{ad}_{T\circ\mathcal{B}^{\natural}(y)}x)\Big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle T\Big(\mathrm{ad}^{*}_{T\circ\mathcal{B}^{\natural}(x)}\mathcal{B}^{\natural}(y)-\mathrm{ad}^{*}_{T\circ\mathcal{B}^{\natural}(y)}\mathcal{B}^{\natural}(x)\Big),

and

\displaystyle(T\circ\mathcal{B}^{\natural})\Big(\left\llbracket T\circ\mathcal{B}^{\natural}(x),T\circ\mathcal{B}^{\natural}(y),z\right\rrbracket+\left\llbracket x,T\circ\mathcal{B}^{\natural}(y),T\circ\mathcal{B}^{\natural}(z)\right\rrbracket-\left\llbracket y,T\circ\mathcal{B}^{\natural}(x),T\circ\mathcal{B}^{\natural}(z)\right\rrbracket\Big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle T\Big(\mathcal{L}^{*}(T\circ\mathcal{B}^{\natural}(x),T\circ\mathcal{B}^{\natural}(y))\mathcal{B}^{\natural}(z)-\mathcal{R}^{*}(T\circ\mathcal{B}^{\natural}(z),T\circ\mathcal{B}^{\natural}(y))\mathcal{B}^{\natural}(x)+\mathcal{R}^{*}(T\circ\mathcal{B}^{\natural}(z),T\circ\mathcal{B}^{\natural}(x))\mathcal{B}^{\natural}(y)\Big).

Thus we obtain that
T
is a relative Rota-Baxter operator on
(\mathfrak{g},[\cdot,\cdot],\left\llbracket\cdot,\cdot,\cdot\right\rrbracket)
with respect to the coadjoint representation
(\mathfrak{g}^{*};\mathrm{ad}^{*},-\mathcal{R}^{*}\tau)
if and only if
T\circ\mathcal{B}^{\natural}
is a relative Rota-Baxter operator on
(\mathfrak{g},[\cdot,\cdot],\left\llbracket\cdot,\cdot,\cdot\right\rrbracket)
with respect to the adjoint representation
(\mathfrak{g};\mathrm{ad},\mathcal{R})
. This finishes the proof.
∎

3.16
is a generalized result of Semonov-Tian-Shansky’s in

to the context of Lie-Yamaguti algebras, whereas the generalized result of Leibniz algebra version was given in

Let

Definition 3.18

A

\omega=\omega_{1}+\omega_{2}+\omega_{3}:\mathfrak{g}\longrightarrow\otimes^{3}\mathfrak{g}
such that

\mathfrak{g}^{*}

•

\delta_{1}
is a
1
-cocycle with respect to the representation
(\otimes^{2}\mathfrak{g};1\otimes\mathrm{ad},1\otimes\mathcal{R})
;

•

\delta_{2}
is a
1
-cocycle with respect to the representation
(\otimes^{2}\mathfrak{g};\mathrm{ad}\otimes 1,\mathcal{R}\otimes 1)
;

•

\omega_{1}
is a
1
-cocycle with respect to the representation
(\otimes^{3}\mathfrak{g};\mathrm{ad}\otimes 1\otimes 1,\mathcal{R}\otimes 1\otimes 1)
;

•

\omega_{2}
is a
1
-cocycle with respect to the representation
(\otimes^{3}\mathfrak{g};1\otimes\mathrm{ad}\otimes 1,1\otimes\mathcal{R}\otimes 1)
;

•

\omega_{3}
is a
1
-cocycle with respect to the representation
(\otimes^{3}\mathfrak{g};1\otimes 1\otimes\mathrm{ad},1\otimes 1\otimes\mathcal{R})

Remark 3.19
.

When a given Lie-Yamaguti algebra

such that the following conditions are satisfied:

•

\omega_{1}
is a
1
-cocycle with respect to the representation
(\otimes^{3}\mathfrak{g};\mathrm{ad}\otimes 1\otimes 1,\mathcal{R}\otimes 1\otimes 1)
;

•

\omega_{2}
is a
1
-cocycle with respect to the representation
(\otimes^{3}\mathfrak{g};1\otimes\mathrm{ad}\otimes 1,1\otimes\mathcal{R}\otimes 1)

•

\omega_{3}
is a
1
-cocycle with respect to the representation
(\otimes^{3}\mathfrak{g};1\otimes 1\otimes\mathrm{ad},1\otimes 1\otimes\mathcal{R})

where

\mathfrak{g}
.

Remark 3.20
.

We would like to point out that
\delta_{i}
and
\omega_{j}

(1\leqslant i\leqslant 2,1\leqslant j\leqslant 3)
as in Eqs. (

) are not

-cocycles of a Lie-Yamaguti algebra

4.
Manin triples, matched pairs, and double construction Lie-Yamaguti bialgebras

In this section, we consider double construction Lie-Yamaguti bialgebras and clarify the relationship between double construction Lie-Yamaguti bialgebras and local cocycle Lie-Yamaguti bialgebras.
First, we introduce the notion of Manin triples.

Definition 4.1
.

Let
\mathfrak{g}_{1}
and
\mathfrak{g}_{2}
be two Lie-Yamaguti algebras. A
Manin triple
of
\mathfrak{g}_{1}
and
\mathfrak{g}_{2}
is a quadratic Lie-Yamaguti algebra
\Big((\mathfrak{g},[\cdot,\cdot],\left\llbracket\cdot,\cdot,\cdot\right\rrbracket),\mathcal{B}\Big)
such that

(i)

\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{g}_{1}\oplus\mathfrak{g}_{2}
as vector spaces;

(ii)

\mathfrak{g}_{1}
and
\mathfrak{g}_{2}
are subalgebras of
\mathfrak{g}
which are isotropic, i.e.,
\mathcal{B}(x_{1},y_{1})=\mathcal{B}(x_{2},y_{2})=0
, for any
x_{1},y_{1}\in\mathfrak{g}_{1}
and
x_{2},y_{2}\in\mathfrak{g}_{2}
;

(iii)

For all
x_{1},y_{1}\in\mathfrak{g}_{1}
and
x_{2},y_{2}\in\mathfrak{g}_{2}
, we have

\displaystyle\mathrm{pr}_{1}\left\llbracket x_{1},y_{1},x_{2}\right\rrbracket=0,\quad\mathrm{pr}_{1}\left\llbracket x_{1},x_{2},y_{1}\right\rrbracket=0,\quad\mathrm{pr}_{2}\left\llbracket x_{2},y_{2},x_{1}\right\rrbracket=0,\quad\mathrm{pr}_{2}\left\llbracket x_{2},x_{1},y_{2}\right\rrbracket=0,

where
\mathrm{pr}_{1}
and
\mathrm{pr}_{2}
are projections from
\mathfrak{g}_{1}\oplus\mathfrak{g}_{2}
to
\mathfrak{g}_{1}
and
\mathfrak{g}_{2}
respectively.

We denote a Manin triple of Lie-Yamaguti algebras by
\Big((\mathfrak{g},\mathcal{B}),\mathfrak{g}_{1},\mathfrak{g}_{2}\Big)
or simply by
(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g}_{1},\mathfrak{g}_{2})
.

Remark 4.2
.

Recall that a product structure on a Lie-Yamaguti algebra
(\mathfrak{g},[\cdot,\cdot],\left\llbracket\cdot,\cdot,\cdot\right\rrbracket)
is a Nijenhuis operator
E:\mathfrak{g}\longrightarrow\mathfrak{g}
satisfying
E^{2}={\rm{Id}}
.
There exists a product structure
E
on
\mathfrak{g}
if and only if
\mathfrak{g}
admits a decomposition into two subalgebras:
\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{g}_{1}\oplus\mathfrak{g}_{2}
. Moreover, Condition (iii) in Definition
4.1
is just the condition that makes the product structure perfect. See
[
21
]
for more details about product structures and complex structures on Lie-Yamaguti algebras. Thus a Manin triple
\Big((\mathfrak{g},\mathcal{B}),\mathfrak{g}_{1},\mathfrak{g}_{2}\Big)
of Lie-Yamaguti algebras is in fact the quadratic Lie-Yamaguti algebra
(\mathfrak{g},\mathcal{B})
such that there is a perfect product structure on
\mathfrak{g}
whose decomposed subalgebras are isotropic.

Let
\Big((\mathfrak{g},\mathcal{B}),\mathfrak{g}_{1},\mathfrak{g}_{2}\Big)
and
\Big((\mathfrak{g}^{\prime},\mathcal{B}^{\prime}),\mathfrak{g}_{1}^{\prime},\mathfrak{g}_{2}^{\prime}\Big)
be two Manin triples of Lie-Yamaguti algebras. An
isomorphism
between
\Big((\mathfrak{g},\mathcal{B}),\mathfrak{g}_{1},\mathfrak{g}_{2}\Big)
and
\Big((\mathfrak{g}^{\prime},\mathcal{B}^{\prime}),\mathfrak{g}_{1}^{\prime},\mathfrak{g}_{2}^{\prime}\Big)
is an isomorphism between Lie-Yamaguti algebras
f:\mathfrak{g}\longrightarrow\mathfrak{g}^{\prime}
such that

f(\mathfrak{g}_{1})\subset\mathfrak{g}_{1}^{\prime},\quad f(\mathfrak{g}_{2})\subset\mathfrak{g}_{2}^{\prime},\quad\mathcal{B}(x,y)=\mathcal{B}^{\prime}(f(x),f(y)),\quad\forall x,y\in\mathfrak{g}

Let
(\mathfrak{g},[\cdot,\cdot],\left\llbracket\cdot,\cdot,\cdot\right\rrbracket)
and
(\mathfrak{g}^{*},[\cdot,\cdot]{{}_{*}},\left\llbracket\cdot,\cdot,\cdot\right\rrbracket_{*})
be a Lie-Yamaguti algebras. There is a natural nondegenerate symmetric bilinear form
\mathcal{B}
on
\mathfrak{g}\oplus\mathfrak{g}^{*}
given by

(22)

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}(x+\xi,y+\eta)=\langle x,\eta\rangle+\langle\xi,y\rangle,\quad\forall x,y\in\mathfrak{g},~\xi,\eta\in\mathfrak{g}^{*}.

Define a pair of operations
([\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathfrak{g}\oplus\mathfrak{g}^{*}},\left\llbracket\cdot,\cdot,\cdot\right\rrbracket_{\mathfrak{g}\oplus\mathfrak{g}^{*}})
to be

(23)

\displaystyle[x+\xi,y+\eta]_{\mathfrak{g}\oplus\mathfrak{g}^{*}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle[x,y]+\mathrm{ad}_{x}^{*}\xi-\mathrm{ad}_{y}^{*}\eta

\displaystyle+[\xi,\eta]_{*}+\mathfrak{ad}_{\xi}^{*}y-\mathfrak{ad}_{\eta}^{*}x,

(24)

\displaystyle\left\llbracket x+\xi,y+\eta,z+\zeta\right\rrbracket_{\mathfrak{g}\oplus\mathfrak{g}^{*}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left\llbracket x,y,z\right\rrbracket+\mathcal{L}^{*}(x,y)\zeta-\mathcal{R}^{*}(z,y)\xi+\mathcal{R}^{*}(z,x)\eta

\displaystyle+\left\llbracket\xi,\eta,\zeta\right\rrbracket_{*}+\mathfrak{L}^{*}(\xi,\eta)z-\mathfrak{R}^{*}(\zeta,\eta)x+\mathfrak{R}^{*}(\zeta,\xi)y,

for all
x,y,z\in\mathfrak{g}
and
\xi,\eta,\zeta\in\mathfrak{g}^{*}
. Here
(\mathrm{ad}^{*},-\mathcal{R}^{*}\tau)
and
(\mathfrak{ad}^{*},-\mathfrak{R}^{*}\tau)
are the coadjoint representations of
\mathfrak{g}
on
\mathfrak{g}^{*}
and
\mathfrak{g}^{*}
on
\mathfrak{g}
respectively, where
\mathcal{L}^{*}=D_{\mathrm{ad}^{*},-\mathcal{R}^{*}\tau}
and
\mathfrak{L}^{*}=D_{\mathfrak{ad}^{*},-\mathfrak{R}^{*}\tau}
.

Note that the bracket
([\cdot,\cdot]_{\bowtie},\left\llbracket\cdot,\cdot,\cdot\right\rrbracket_{\bowtie})
given by (
23
) and (
24
) is invariant with respect to the bilinear form
\mathcal{B}
given by (
22
) and satisfies the Condition (iii) in Definition
4.1
. If
(\mathfrak{g}\oplus\mathfrak{g}^{*},[\cdot,\cdot]{{}_{\mathfrak{g}\oplus\mathfrak{g}^{*}}},\left\llbracket\cdot,\cdot,\cdot\right\rrbracket_{\mathfrak{g}\oplus\mathfrak{g}^{*}})
is a Lie-Yamaguti algebra, then it is easy to see that
\mathfrak{g}
and
\mathfrak{g}^{*}
are isotropic subalgebras with respect to the bilinear form
\mathcal{B}
given by (
22
). Consequently,
\Big((\mathfrak{g}\oplus\mathfrak{g}^{*},\mathcal{B}),\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g}^{*}\Big)
is a Manin triple of
\mathfrak{g}
and
\mathfrak{g}^{*}
, which is called the
standard Manin triple
.

Proposition 4.3
.

Any Manin triple of Lie-Yamaguti algebras is isomorphic to a standard one.

Proof.

Let
\mathfrak{g}_{1}
and
\mathfrak{g}_{2}
be Lie-Yamaguti algebras. If
\Big((\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{g}_{1}\oplus\mathfrak{g}_{2},\mathcal{B}),\mathfrak{g}_{1},\mathfrak{g}_{2}\Big)
is a Manin triple of
\mathfrak{g}_{1}
and
\mathfrak{g}_{2}
, then
\mathfrak{g}_{2}
is isomorphic to
\mathfrak{g}_{1}^{*}
as vector spaces via

\langle\alpha,x\rangle:=\mathcal{B}(\alpha,x),\quad\forall\alpha\in\mathfrak{g}_{2},~x\in\mathfrak{g}_{1}.

Moreover,
\mathfrak{g}_{1}^{*}
is equipped with a Lie-Yamaguti algebra structure from
\mathfrak{g}_{2}
via this isomorphism. Then
\Big((\mathfrak{g}_{1}\oplus\mathfrak{g}_{2},\mathcal{B}),\mathfrak{g}_{1},\mathfrak{g}_{2}\Big)
is isomorphic to the standard Manin triple
\Big((\mathfrak{g}_{1}\oplus\mathfrak{g}_{1}^{*},\mathcal{B}),\mathfrak{g}_{1},\mathfrak{g}_{1}^{*}\Big)
. This completes the proof.
∎

Remark 4.4
.

By the proof of Proposition
4.3
, we obtain that any Manin triple of Lie-Yamaguti algebras
\Big((\mathfrak{g},\mathcal{B}),\mathfrak{g}_{1},\mathfrak{g}_{2}\Big)
is also isomorphic to the standard Manin triple
\Big((\mathfrak{g}_{2}^{*}\oplus\mathfrak{g}_{2},\mathcal{B}),\mathfrak{g}_{2}^{*},\mathfrak{g}_{2}\Big)
of
\mathfrak{g}_{2}
and
\mathfrak{g}_{2}^{*}
. So the statement of the proposition is that any Manin triple of Lie-Yamaguti algebras is isomorphic to “a” standard one, not “the”.

Note that the operation

Definition 4.6
.

Let
(\mathfrak{g}_{1},[\cdot,\cdot]{{}_{1}},\left\llbracket\cdot,\cdot,\cdot\right\rrbracket_{1})
and
(\mathfrak{g}_{2},[\cdot,\cdot]{{}_{2}},\left\llbracket\cdot,\cdot,\cdot\right\rrbracket_{2})
be two Lie-Yamaguti algebras. If

The following proposition reveals the relationship between matched pairs and Manin triples of Lie-Yamaguti algebras.

Proposition 4.9
.

Let
(\mathfrak{g},[\cdot,\cdot],\left\llbracket\cdot,\cdot,\cdot\right\rrbracket)
and
(\mathfrak{g}^{*},[\cdot,\cdot]{{}_{*}},\left\llbracket\cdot,\cdot,\cdot\right\rrbracket_{*})
be Lie-Yamaguti algebras. Then the quadruple
\Big(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g}^{*};(\mathrm{ad}^{*},-\mathcal{R}^{*}\tau),(\mathfrak{ad}^{*},-\mathfrak{R}^{*}\tau)\Big)
is a matched pair of
\mathfrak{g}
and
\mathfrak{g}^{*}
if and only if the triple
\Big((\mathfrak{g}\oplus\mathfrak{g}^{*},\mathcal{B}),\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g}^{*}\Big)
is a Manin triple of
\mathfrak{g}
and
\mathfrak{g}^{*}
, where the invariant bilinear form
\mathcal{B}
is given by Eq. (
22
).

Proof.

Let
\Big(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g}^{*};(\mathrm{ad}^{*},-\mathcal{R}^{*}\tau),(\mathfrak{ad}^{*},-\mathfrak{R}^{*}\tau)\Big)
be a matched pair of Lie-Yamaguti algebras. Then
(\mathfrak{g}\oplus\mathfrak{g}^{*},[\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathfrak{g}\oplus\mathfrak{g}^{*}},\left\llbracket\cdot,\cdot,\cdot\right\rrbracket_{\mathfrak{g}\oplus\mathfrak{g}^{*}})
is a Lie-Yamaguti algebra, where
([\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathfrak{g}\oplus\mathfrak{g}^{*}},\left\llbracket\cdot,\cdot,\cdot\right\rrbracket_{\mathfrak{g}\oplus\mathfrak{g}^{*}})
is given by (
23
) and (
24
). We only need to prove that
\mathcal{B}
satisfies the invariant condition (
20
) and (
21
). Indeed, for all
x,y,z,w\in\mathfrak{g}
and
\xi,\eta,\zeta,\delta\in\mathfrak{g}^{*}
, we have

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}(x+\xi,[y+\eta,z+\zeta]_{\mathfrak{g}\oplus\mathfrak{g}^{*}})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}(x+\xi,[y,z]+\mathrm{ad}_{y}^{*}\zeta-\mathrm{ad}_{z}^{*}\eta+[\eta,\zeta]_{*}+\mathfrak{ad}_{\eta}^{*}z-\mathfrak{ad}_{\zeta}^{*}y)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left\langle x,\mathrm{ad}_{y}^{*}\zeta-\mathrm{ad}_{z}^{*}\eta+[\eta,\zeta]_{*}\right\rangle+\left\langle\xi,[y,z]+\mathfrak{ad}_{\eta}^{*}z-\mathfrak{ad}_{\zeta}^{*}y\right\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\left\langle[y,x],\zeta\right\rangle+\left\langle[z,x],\eta\right\rangle+\left\langle x,[\eta,\zeta]_{*}\right\rangle

\displaystyle+\left\langle\xi,[y,z]\right\rangle-\left\langle[\eta,\xi]_{*},z\right\rangle+\left\langle[\zeta,\xi]_{*},y\right\rangle,

on the other hand, we also have that

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}([x+\xi,y+\eta]_{\mathfrak{g}\oplus\mathfrak{g}^{*}},z+\zeta)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}([x,y]+\mathrm{ad}_{x}^{*}\eta-\mathrm{ad}_{y}^{*}\xi+[\xi,\eta]_{*}+\mathfrak{ad}_{\xi}^{*}y-\mathfrak{ad}_{\eta}^{*}x,z+\zeta)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left\langle\mathrm{ad}_{x}^{*}\eta-\mathrm{ad}_{y}^{*}\xi+[\xi,\eta]_{*},z\right\rangle+\left\langle[x,y]+\mathfrak{ad}_{\xi}^{*}y-\mathfrak{ad}_{\eta}^{*}x,\zeta\right\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\left\langle\eta,[x,z]\right\rangle+\left\langle\xi,[y,z]\right\rangle+\left\langle[\xi,\eta]_{*},z\right\rangle

\displaystyle+\left\langle[x,y],\zeta\right\rangle-\left\langle y,[\xi,\zeta]_{*}\right\rangle+\left\langle x,[\eta,\zeta]_{*}\right\rangle,

which implies that

\mathcal{B}(x+\xi,[y+\eta,z+\zeta]_{\mathfrak{g}\oplus\mathfrak{g}^{*}})=\mathcal{B}([x+\xi,y+\eta]_{\mathfrak{g}\oplus\mathfrak{g}^{*}},z+\zeta).

Moreover, we have

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}(\left\llbracket x+\xi,y+\eta,z+\zeta\right\rrbracket_{\mathfrak{g}\oplus\mathfrak{g}^{*}},w+\delta)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}\Big(\left\llbracket x,y,z\right\rrbracket+\mathcal{L}^{*}(x,y)\zeta-\mathcal{R}^{*}(z,y)\xi+\mathcal{R}^{*}(z,x)\eta

\displaystyle+\left\llbracket\xi,\eta,\zeta\right\rrbracket_{*}+\mathfrak{L}^{*}(\xi,\eta)z-\mathfrak{R}^{*}(\zeta,\eta)x+\mathfrak{R}^{*}(\zeta,\xi)y,w+\delta\Big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left\langle\left\llbracket x,y,z\right\rrbracket+\mathfrak{L}^{*}(\xi,\eta)z-\mathfrak{R}^{*}(\zeta,\eta)x+\mathfrak{R}^{*}(\zeta,\xi)y,\delta\right\rangle

\displaystyle+\left\langle\left\llbracket\xi,\eta,\zeta\right\rrbracket_{*}+\mathcal{L}^{*}(x,y)\zeta-\mathcal{R}^{*}(z,y)\xi+\mathcal{R}^{*}(z,x)\eta,w\right\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left\langle\left\llbracket x,y,z\right\rrbracket,\delta\right\rangle-\left\langle z,\left\llbracket\xi,\eta,\delta\right\rrbracket_{*}\right\rangle+\left\langle x,\left\llbracket\delta,\zeta,\eta\right\rrbracket_{*}\right\rangle-\left\langle y,\left\llbracket\delta,\zeta,\xi\right\rrbracket_{*}\right\rangle

\displaystyle+\left\langle\left\llbracket\xi,\eta,\zeta\right\rrbracket_{*},w\right\rangle-\left\langle\zeta,\left\llbracket x,y,w\right\rrbracket_{*}\right\rangle+\left\langle\xi,\left\llbracket w,z,y\right\rrbracket\right\rangle-\left\langle\eta,\left\llbracket w,z,x\right\rrbracket\right\rangle,

on the other hand, we also have that

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}(x+\xi,\left\llbracket w+\delta,z+\zeta,y+\eta\right\rrbracket_{\mathfrak{g}\oplus\mathfrak{g}^{*}})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}\Big(x+\xi,\left\llbracket w,z,y\right\rrbracket+\mathcal{L}^{*}(w,z)\eta-\mathcal{R}^{*}(y,z)\delta+\mathcal{R}^{*}(y,w)\zeta

\displaystyle+\left\llbracket\delta,\zeta,\eta\right\rrbracket_{*}+\mathfrak{L}^{*}(\delta,\zeta)y-\mathfrak{R}^{*}(\eta,\zeta)w+\mathfrak{R}^{*}(\eta,\delta)z\Big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left\langle x,\left\llbracket\delta,\zeta,\eta\right\rrbracket_{*}+\mathcal{L}^{*}(w,z)\eta-\mathcal{R}^{*}(y,z)\delta+\mathcal{R}^{*}(y,w)\zeta\right\rangle

\displaystyle+\left\langle\xi,\left\llbracket w,z,y\right\rrbracket+\mathfrak{L}^{*}(\delta,\zeta)y-\mathfrak{R}^{*}(\eta,\zeta)w+\mathfrak{R}^{*}(\eta,\delta)z\right\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left\langle x,\left\llbracket\delta,\zeta,\eta\right\rrbracket_{*}\right\rangle-\left\langle\left\llbracket w,z,x\right\rrbracket,\eta\right\rangle+\left\langle\left\llbracket x,y,z\right\rrbracket,\delta\right\rangle-\left\langle\left\llbracket x,y,w\right\rrbracket,\zeta\right\rangle

\displaystyle+\left\langle\xi,\left\llbracket w,z,y\right\rrbracket\right\rangle-\left\langle\left\llbracket\delta,\zeta,\xi\right\rrbracket_{*},y\right\rangle+\left\langle\left\llbracket\xi,\eta,\zeta\right\rrbracket_{*},w\right\rangle-\left\langle\left\llbracket\xi,\eta,\delta\right\rrbracket_{*},z\right\rangle,

which implies that

\mathcal{B}(\left\llbracket x+\xi,y+\eta,z+\zeta\right\rrbracket_{\mathfrak{g}\oplus\mathfrak{g}^{*}},w+\delta)=\mathcal{B}(x+\xi,\left\llbracket w+\delta,z+\zeta,y+\eta\right\rrbracket_{\mathfrak{g}\oplus\mathfrak{g}^{*}}).

Conversely, if
\Big((\mathfrak{g}\oplus\mathfrak{g}^{*},\mathcal{B}),\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g}^{*}\Big)
is a Manin triple, where
\mathcal{B}
is an invariant bilinear form given by (
22
). For all
x\in\mathfrak{g}
and
\xi,\eta,\zeta\in\mathfrak{g}^{*}
, by (
20
), we have

\displaystyle\left\langle\eta,\rho_{2}(\xi)x\right\rangle=\mathcal{B}(\eta,[\xi,x]_{\mathfrak{g}\oplus\mathfrak{g}^{*}})=-\mathcal{B}([\xi,\eta]_{*},x)=-\left\langle[\xi,\eta]_{*},x\right\rangle=\langle\eta,\mathfrak{ad}_{\xi}^{*}x\rangle,

which implies that
\rho_{2}=\mathfrak{ad}^{*}.
Moreover, by (
21
), we also have

\displaystyle\left\langle\zeta,\mu_{2}(\xi,\eta)x\right\rangle=\mathcal{B}(\zeta,\left\llbracket x,\xi,\eta\right\rrbracket_{\mathfrak{g}\oplus\mathfrak{g}^{*}})=\mathcal{B}(\left\llbracket\zeta,\eta,\xi\right\rrbracket_{*},x)=\left\langle\left\llbracket\zeta,\eta,\xi\right\rrbracket_{*},x\right\rangle=-\left\langle\zeta,\mathfrak{R}^{*}(\eta,\xi)x\right\rangle,

which implies that
\mu_{2}=-\mathfrak{R}^{*}\tau.
Similarly, we have that
\rho_{1}=\mathrm{ad}^{*}
and
\mu_{1}=-\mathcal{R}^{*}\tau
. Thus we obtain that
\Big(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g}^{*},(\mathrm{ad}^{*},-\mathcal{R}^{*}\tau),(\mathfrak{ad}^{*},-\mathfrak{R}^{*}\tau)\Big)
is a matched pair of Lie-Yamaguti algebras. This completes the proof.
∎

It is in a position to introduce the notion of double construction Lie-Yamaguti bialgebras. Before this, we show the following proposition.

Proposition 4.10
.

Let
(\mathfrak{g},[\cdot,\cdot],\left\llbracket\cdot,\cdot,\cdot\right\rrbracket)
be a Lie-Yamaguti algebra endowed with two linear maps
\delta:\mathfrak{g}\longrightarrow\otimes^{2}\mathfrak{g}
and
\omega:\mathfrak{g}\longrightarrow\otimes^{3}\mathfrak{g}
. Then
\Big(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g}^{*};(\mathrm{ad}^{*},-\mathcal{R}^{*}\tau),(\mathfrak{ad}^{*},-\mathfrak{R}^{*}\tau)\Big)
is a matched pair of
(\mathfrak{g},[\cdot,\cdot],\left\llbracket\cdot,\cdot,\cdot\right\rrbracket)
and
(\mathfrak{g}^{*},[\cdot,\cdot]{{}_{*}},\left\llbracket\cdot,\cdot,\cdot\right\rrbracket_{*})
if and only if the following conditions are satisfied

(i)

(\mathfrak{g},\delta,\omega)
is a Lie-Yamaguti coalgebra;

(ii)

the following compatibility conditions are satisfied:
\forall x,y,z\in\mathfrak{g},

(45)

\displaystyle~\delta([x,y])

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Big(\mathrm{ad}_{x}\otimes 1+1\otimes\mathrm{ad}_{x}\Big)\delta(y)-\Big(\mathrm{ad}_{y}\otimes 1+1\otimes\mathrm{ad}_{y}\Big)\delta(x),

(46)

\displaystyle~\Big(1\otimes\mathcal{R}(y,z)\Big)\delta(x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Big(1\otimes\mathcal{R}(x,z)\Big)\delta(y),

(47)

\displaystyle~\omega([x,y])

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Big(1\otimes 1\otimes\mathrm{ad}_{x}\Big)\omega(y)-\Big(1\otimes 1\otimes\mathrm{ad}_{y}\Big)\omega(x),

(48)

\displaystyle~\delta(\left\llbracket x,y,z\right\rrbracket)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Big(\mathcal{L}(x,y)\otimes 1+1\otimes\mathcal{L}(x,y)\Big)\omega(z),

(49)

\displaystyle~\Big(\mathcal{R}(y,z)\otimes 1\Big)\delta(x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Big(\mathcal{R}(x,z)\otimes 1\Big)\delta(y),

(50)

\displaystyle~\omega(\left\llbracket x,y,z\right\rrbracket)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Big(\mathcal{L}(x,y)\otimes 1\otimes 1+1\otimes\mathcal{L}(x,y)\otimes 1+1\otimes 1\otimes\mathcal{L}(x,y)\Big)\omega(z),

(51)

\displaystyle~\Big(1\otimes\mathcal{R}(y,x)\otimes 1-R(x,y)\otimes 1\otimes 1\Big)\omega(z)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sigma_{12}\sigma_{23}\Big(1\otimes\mathcal{R}(x,z)\otimes 1\Big)\omega(y)

\displaystyle+\sigma_{23}\Big(1\otimes\mathcal{R}(y,z)\otimes 1\Big)\omega(x).

Proof.

It is sufficient to show that Eqs. (
27
)-(
44
) are equivalent to Condition (ii). Note that when
\rho_{1}=\mathrm{ad}^{*},~\mu_{1}=-\mathcal{R}^{*}\tau
and
\rho_{2}=\mathfrak{ad}^{*},~\mu_{2}=-\mathfrak{R}^{*}\tau
, Eqs. (
27
)-(
30
) and Eqs. (
33
)-(
34
) are equivalent to Conditions (
45
)-(
51
) respectively, Eq. (
31
) is equivalent to that
\omega
is skew-symmetric with respect to the first two variables, and moreover Eq. (
34
) and (
35
) are equivalent.
Indeed, for all
x,y,z\in\mathfrak{g}
and
\xi,\eta\in\mathfrak{g}^{*}
, let us now compute that

\displaystyle\left\langle[\mathfrak{ad}_{\xi}^{*}x,y]-\mathfrak{ad}_{\mathrm{ad}_{x}^{*}\xi}^{*}y-\mathfrak{ad}_{\xi}^{*}[x,y]-[\mathfrak{ad}_{\xi}^{*}y,x]+\mathfrak{ad}_{\mathrm{ad}_{y}^{*}\xi}^{*}x,\eta\right\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\left\langle\delta(x),\xi\otimes\mathrm{ad}_{y}^{*}\eta\right\rangle+\left\langle\delta(y),\mathrm{ad}_{x}^{*}\xi\otimes\eta\right\rangle+\left\langle\delta([x,y]),\xi\otimes\eta\right\rangle+\left\langle\delta(y),\xi\otimes\mathrm{ad}_{x}^{*}\eta\right\rangle-\left\langle\delta(x),\mathrm{ad}_{y}^{*}\xi\otimes\eta\right\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left\langle\delta([x,y])-(\mathrm{ad}_{x}\otimes 1+1\otimes\mathrm{ad}_{x})\delta(y)+(\mathrm{ad}_{y}\otimes 1+1\otimes\mathrm{ad}_{y})\delta(x),\xi\otimes\eta\right\rangle,

which implies that Eq. (
27
) is equivalent to Eq. (
45
). Moreover, we have that

\displaystyle\left\langle\left\llbracket\mathfrak{ad}_{\xi}^{*}x,y,z\right\rrbracket-\left\llbracket\mathfrak{ad}_{\xi}^{*}y,x,z\right\rrbracket,\zeta\right\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\left\langle\mathfrak{ad}_{\xi}^{*}x,\mathcal{R}^{*}(y,z)\eta\right\rangle+\left\langle\mathfrak{ad}_{\xi}^{*}y,\mathcal{R}^{*}(x,z)\eta\right\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left\langle\delta(x),\xi\otimes\mathcal{R}^{*}(y,z)\eta\right\rangle-\left\langle\delta(y),\xi\otimes\mathcal{R}^{*}(x,z)\eta\right\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left\langle-\Big(1\otimes\mathcal{R}(y,z)\Big)\delta(x)+\Big(1\otimes\mathcal{R}(x,z)\Big)\delta(y),\xi\otimes\eta\right\rangle,

which implies that Eq. (
28
) is equivalent to Eq. (
46
). Similarly, we obtain that Eqs. (
29
)-(
30
) and Eqs. (
33
)-(
34
) are equivalent to Eqs. (
47
)-(
51
). What is left is to show that Eqs. (
42
)-(
43
) are equivalent to Eqs. (
27
)-(
34
) respectively. We only prove the equivalence of Eq. (
34
) and Eq. (
43
) since others are similar. Indeed, we have that

\displaystyle\left\langle\mathcal{R}^{*}(\mathfrak{R}^{*}(\eta,\xi)y,x)\zeta+\left\llbracket\mathcal{R}^{*}(y,x)\zeta,\xi,\eta\right\rrbracket_{*}-\mathcal{L}^{*}(y,\mathfrak{R}^{*}(\xi,\zeta)x)\eta-\mathcal{R}^{*}(\mathfrak{R}^{*}(\eta,\zeta)x,y)\xi,z\right\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left\langle\zeta,\left\llbracket\mathfrak{R}^{*}(\eta,\xi)y,z,x\right\rrbracket\right\rangle+\left\langle\zeta,\mathcal{R}(y,x)\mathfrak{R}^{*}(\xi,\eta)z\right\rangle-\left\langle\eta,\mathcal{R}(y,z)\mathfrak{R}^{*}(\xi,\zeta)x\right\rangle-\left\langle\xi,\mathcal{R}(z,y)\mathfrak{R}^{*}(\eta,\zeta)x\right\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\left\langle\left\llbracket\eta,\mathcal{R}^{*}(z,x)\zeta,\xi\right\rrbracket_{*},y\right\rangle-\left\langle\left\llbracket\xi,\mathcal{R}^{*}(y,x)\zeta,\eta\right\rrbracket_{*},z\right\rangle-\left\langle\left\llbracket\mathcal{R}^{*}(y,z)\eta,\xi,\zeta\right\rrbracket_{*},x\right\rangle+\left\langle\left\llbracket\eta,\mathcal{R}^{*}(z,y)\xi,\zeta\right\rrbracket_{*},x\right\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\langle(\mathcal{R}(y,z)\otimes 1\otimes 1-1\otimes\mathcal{R}(z,y)\otimes 1)\omega(x)+\sigma_{12}\sigma_{23}(1\otimes\mathcal{R}(y,x)\otimes 1)\omega(z)+\sigma_{23}(1\otimes\mathcal{R}(z,x)\otimes 1)\omega(y),

\displaystyle\eta\otimes\xi\otimes\zeta\rangle,

which gives the equivalence of Eqs. (
34
) and (
43
). This completes the proof.
∎

Definition 4.11
.

Let
(\mathfrak{g},[\cdot,\cdot],\left\llbracket\cdot,\cdot,\cdot\right\rrbracket)
be a Lie-Yamaguti algebra, and structure maps
\delta:\mathfrak{g}\longrightarrow\otimes^{2}\mathfrak{g}
and
\omega:\mathfrak{g}\longrightarrow\otimes^{3}\mathfrak{g}
linear maps. If Conditions (i) and (ii) in Proposition
4.10
are satisfied, then we say that
\mathfrak{g}
is a
double construction Lie-Yamaguti bialgebra
. We denote a Lie-Yamaguti bialgebra by
(\mathfrak{g},[\cdot,\cdot],\left\llbracket\cdot,\cdot,\cdot\right\rrbracket,\delta,\omega)
, or simply by
(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g}^{*})
.

The following corollary is obvious.

Corollary 4.12
.

If
(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g}^{*})
is a double construction Lie-Yamaguti bialgebra, then so is
(\mathfrak{g}^{*},\mathfrak{g})
.

By Proposition
4.9
and Proposition
4.10
, we obtain the following theorem directly.

Theorem 4.13
.

Let
(\mathfrak{g},[\cdot,\cdot],\left\llbracket\cdot,\cdot,\cdot\right\rrbracket)
be a Lie-Yamaguti algebra and
\delta:\mathfrak{g}\longrightarrow\otimes^{2}\mathfrak{g}
and
\omega:\mathfrak{g}\longrightarrow\otimes^{3}\mathfrak{g}
linear maps. Suppose that the structure map
(\delta^{*},\omega^{*})
defines a Lie-Yamaguti algebra structure on
\mathfrak{g}^{*}
. Then the following statements are equivalent:

(1)

the Lie-Yamaguti algebra
\mathfrak{g}
makes
(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g}^{*})
into a double construction Lie-Yamaguti bialgebra;

(2)

the quadruple
\Big(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g}^{*};(\mathrm{ad}^{*},-\mathcal{R}^{*}\tau),(\mathfrak{ad}^{*},-\mathfrak{R}^{*}\tau)\Big)
is a matched pair of Lie-Yamaguti algebras;

(3)

the triple
\Big((\mathfrak{g}\oplus\mathfrak{g}^{*},\mathcal{B}),\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g}^{*}\Big)
is a standard Manin triple, where the invariant bilinear form
\mathcal{B}
is given by (
22
).

In this case, the Lie-Yamaguti algebra
(\mathfrak{g}\oplus\mathfrak{g}^{*},[\cdot,\cdot]{{}_{\mathfrak{g}\oplus\mathfrak{g}^{*}}},\left\llbracket\cdot,\cdot,\cdot\right\rrbracket_{\mathfrak{g}\oplus\mathfrak{g}^{*}})
is called the
double
of the Lie-Yamaguti bialgebra
(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g}^{*})
, and is denoted by
\mathfrak{g}\bowtie\mathfrak{g}^{*}
.

The following proposition reveals the relationship between local cocycle Lie-Yamaguti bialgebras and double construction Lie-Yamaguti bialgebras.

Proposition 4.14
.

A double construction Lie-Yamaguti bialgebra gives rise to a local cocycle Lie-Yamaguti bialgebra.

Proof.

Let
(\mathfrak{g},\delta,\omega)
be a double construction Lie-Yamaguti bialgebra. Let
k_{1},k_{2},k_{3}
be complex numbers such that
k_{1}=k_{2}
and
k_{1}+k_{2}+k_{3}=1
. Denote by
\delta_{i}=\frac{1}{2}\delta
and
\omega_{j}=k_{j}\omega
, where
i=1,2;~j=1,2,3
. Set
\delta=\delta_{1}+\delta_{2}
and
\omega=\omega_{1}+\omega_{2}+\omega_{3}
. It is obvious that
\delta_{1},\delta_{2}
are
1
-cocycles of
\mathfrak{g}
with respect to the representations
(\otimes^{2}\mathfrak{g};1\otimes\mathrm{ad},1\otimes\mathcal{R}),(\otimes^{2}\mathfrak{g};\mathrm{ad}\otimes 1,\mathcal{R}\otimes 1)
respectively, and
\omega_{1},\omega_{2},\omega_{3}
are
1
-cocycles of
\mathfrak{g}
with respect to
(\otimes^{3}\mathfrak{g};\mathrm{ad}\otimes 1\otimes 1,\mathcal{R}\otimes 1\otimes 1),(\otimes^{3}\mathfrak{g};1\otimes\mathrm{ad}\otimes 1,1\otimes\mathcal{R}\otimes 1),~(\otimes^{3}\mathfrak{g};1\otimes 1\otimes\mathrm{ad},1\otimes 1\otimes\mathcal{R})
respectively. Hence
(\delta,\omega)
defines a local cocycle Lie-Yamaguti bialgebra on
\mathfrak{g}
.
∎

We give some examples of double construction Lie-Yamaguti bialgebras to end up with this section. As a first example, we have the following trivial Lie-Yamaguti bialgebra.

Example 4.15
.

For any Lie-Yamaguti algebra
\mathfrak{g}
, taking
\delta=0
and
\omega=0
, then
(\mathfrak{g},\delta,\omega)
is a Lie-Yamaguti bialgebra. In this case, the corresponding Manin triple gives a quadratic Lie-Yamaguti algebra
(\mathfrak{g}\ltimes_{\mathrm{ad}^{*},-\mathcal{R}^{*}\tau}\mathfrak{g}^{*},\mathcal{B})
. Dually, for any trivial Lie-Yamaguti algebra
\mathfrak{g}
(that is, both binary and trinary brackets are zero), any Lie-Yamaguti algebra structure
(\delta^{*},\omega^{*})
on the dual space
\mathfrak{g}^{*}
makes
(\mathfrak{g},\delta,\omega)
a Lie-Yamaguti bialgebra. Such Lie-Yamaguti bialgebra is called the
trivial Lie-Yamaguti bialgebra
.

Example 4.16
.

Let
\mathfrak{g}
be the
2
-dimensional Lie-Yamaguti algebra given in Example
3.9
.
The nonzero cobrackets
\delta:\mathfrak{g}\longrightarrow\otimes^{2}\mathfrak{g}
and
\omega:\mathfrak{g}\longrightarrow\otimes^{3}\mathfrak{g}
are given by

\delta(e_{1})=e_{1}\otimes e_{2},\quad\omega(e_{1})=e_{1}\otimes e_{2}\otimes e_{2}.

Then
\delta^{*}:\otimes^{2}\mathfrak{g}^{*}\longrightarrow\mathfrak{g}^{*}
and
\omega^{*}:\otimes^{3}\mathfrak{g}^{*}\longrightarrow\mathfrak{g}^{*}
defines a pair of Lie-Yamaguti algebra structure on
\mathfrak{g}^{*}
that is isomorphic to
\mathfrak{g}
. It is direct to see that
(\mathfrak{g},\delta,\omega)
is a Lie-Yamaguti bialgebra.

Example 4.17
.

Let
\mathfrak{g}
be a
4
-dimensional Lie-Yamaguti algebra with a basis
\{e_{1},e_{2},e_{3},e_{4}\}
defined to be

[e_{1},e_{2}]=2e_{4},\quad\left\llbracket e_{1},e_{2},e_{1}\right\rrbracket=e_{4}.

If the dual of linear maps
\delta:\mathfrak{g}\longrightarrow\otimes^{2}\mathfrak{g}
and
\omega:\mathfrak{g}\longrightarrow\otimes^{3}\mathfrak{g}
forms a Lie-Yamaguti algebra structure on
\mathfrak{g}^{*}
such that
(\mathfrak{g}\oplus\mathfrak{g}^{*},\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g}^{*})
is a Manin triple, where the invariant bilinear form is given by (
22
). Then
\delta=0
and
\omega=0
.

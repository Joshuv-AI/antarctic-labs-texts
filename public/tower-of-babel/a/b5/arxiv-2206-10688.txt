Complex-Order Scale-Invariant Operators and Self-Similar Processes Journal: X
Arash Amini, Julien Fageot, Michael Unser

Abstract
Derivatives and integration operators are well-studied examples of linear operators that commute with scaling up to a fixed multiplicative factor; i.e., they are scale-invariant. Fractional order derivatives (integration operators) also belong to this family. In this paper, we extend the fractional operators to complex-order operators by constructing them in the Fourier domain. We analyze these operators in details with a special emphasis on the decay properties of the outputs. We further use these operators to introduce a family of complex-valued stable processes that are self-similar with complex-valued Hurst indices. These processes are expressed via the characteristic functionals over the Schwartz space of functions. Besides the self-similarity and stationarity, we study the regularity (in terms of Sobolev spaces) of the proposed processes.

\displaystyle=\mathcal{F}\big\{f\big\}(w)=\int_{\mathbb{R}}f(x){\rm e}^{-{\rm i}\omega x}{\rm d}x,

3.1
Generalized Random Processes and their Characteristic Functional

The theory of generalized random processes has been formalized by Gelfand and Itô
[
40
,
41
]
.
It is the probabilistic counterpart of the theory of generalized functions of Schwartz
[
52
]
and allows for the construction of broad classes of random processes, including the ones that do not admit pointwise representations such as the stable white noises
[
40
, Chapter 3]
. We briefly recap this formalism, with a special emphasis on the characteristic functional.

A tempered generalized random process
S
(or simply a generalized random process) is a collection of random variables
\langle S,\varphi\rangle
, where
\varphi
is a test function in the Schwartz space
\mathcal{S}
, such that

1.

Linearity:
for any
\varphi_{1},\varphi_{2}\in\mathcal{S}
and
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
,
\langle S,\varphi_{1}+\lambda\varphi_{2}\rangle=\langle S,\varphi_{1}\rangle+\lambda\langle S,\varphi_{2}\rangle
almost surely;

2.

Continuity:
for any converging sequence
\varphi_{n}\rightarrow\varphi
in
\mathcal{S}
, the random variables
\langle S,\varphi_{n}\rangle
converge in probability to
\langle S,\varphi\rangle
.

The space
\mathcal{S}^{\prime}
of tempered generalized functions is endowed with the weak* topology
[
55
]
. This topology defines a Borelian
\sigma
-field on
\mathcal{S}^{\prime}
and a tempered generalized random process
S
can then be seen as a random element of the space of tempered generalized functions. The probability law of a generalized random process
S
is the probability measure
\mathscr{P}_{S}
over
\mathcal{S}^{\prime}
such that, for any Borelian set
B\subset\mathcal{S}^{\prime}
, we have
\mathscr{P}_{S}(B)=\mathscr{P}(S\in B)
.
We refer the interested reader to
[
56
]
for a comprehensive introduction to these notions in the framework of tempered generalized functions and for additional references.

We are interested in complex-valued random processes; hence, we shall adapt the usual concepts to this case. Thereafter,
\mathcal{S}
and
\mathcal{S}^{\prime}
denote the complex-valued Schwartz space and space of tempered generalized functions, respectively. The characteristic function of a complex random variable
Z
is given by
\widehat{\mathscr{P}}_{Z}(\xi)=\mathbb{E}[\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\real(Z\bar{\xi})}]
where
\bar{\xi}
is the complex conjugate of
\xi\in\mathbb{C}
. The extension to complex-valued generalized random processes is as follows.

Definition 3.1

(
Characteristic functional).

The characteristic functional of a complex valued tempered generalized random process
S
is defined as

\widehat{\mathscr{P}}_{S}(\varphi)=\mathbb{E}\left[\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\real(\langle S,\bar{\varphi}\rangle)}\right]=\int_{\mathcal{S}^{\prime}}\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\real(\langle u,\bar{\varphi}\rangle)}\mathrm{d}\mathscr{P}_{S}(u)

(21)

for any
\varphi\in\mathcal{S}
.

The characteristic functional has been introduced by Kolmogorov
[
57
]
and popularized by Gelfand and Vilenkin
[
40
]
.
The probability law of a generalized random process is characterized by its characteristic functional, which is its (infinite-dimensional) Fourier transform.
In particular, two random processes with identical characteristic functionals have identical finite-dimensional marginals.

The Bochner-Minlos theorem ensures that the characteristic functionals of tempered generalized processes are exactly the functionals
\widehat{\mathscr{P}}:\mathcal{S}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
that are continuous, positive-definite, and such that
\widehat{\mathscr{P}}(0)=1

[
40
]
. This provides a way of constructing tempered generalized processes via their characteristic functionals.

For example, for any
\alpha\in]0,2]
, it is known that the functional

\widehat{\mathscr{P}}(\varphi)=\exp\left(-\int_{\mathbb{R}}|\varphi(x)|^{\alpha}\mathrm{d}x\right)=\exp\left(-\lVert\varphi\rVert_{\alpha}^{\alpha}\right)

(22)

is continuous and positive-definite over real-valued Schwartz functions and satisfies
\widehat{\mathscr{P}}(\varphi)=0

[
48
]
. Therefore, there exists a real generalized random process
W_{\alpha}
, called a symmetric-
\alpha
-stable (S
\alpha
S) white noise, such that
\widehat{\mathscr{P}}_{W_{\alpha}}(\varphi)=\widehat{\mathscr{P}}(\varphi)
.
The family of stable white noises
W_{\alpha}
will play an important role in this paper. Note that, seen as a complex-valued generalized random process acting on complex-valued test functions
\varphi\in\mathcal{S}
and using that
W_{\alpha}
itself is real, we have, due to (
21
), that

\widehat{\mathscr{P}}_{W_{\alpha}}(\varphi)=\mathbb{E}\left[\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\real(\langle W_{\alpha},\bar{\varphi}\rangle)}\right]=\exp(-\lVert\real(\varphi)\rVert_{\alpha}^{\alpha}).

(23)

3.3

We first adapt the notion of self-similarity to complex-valued generalized random processes that may have no pointwise interpretation.

Definition 3.4

(

A complex-valued tempered generalized random process
S
is self-similar with Hurst index
H\in\mathbb{C}
if the probability laws of
S
and
T^{-H}S(T\cdot)
are identical for any
T>0
.

When
S
admits a pointwise interpretation, we readily recover the conventional self-similarity defined in (
2
). In the present setting where the random process
S
is complex, the Hurst index is generally complex as well.
However, the driving S
\alpha
S white noise
W_{\alpha}
is self-similar and real-valued with (real) Hurst index
H=\frac{1}{\alpha}-1

[
32
, Proposition 4.2]
.

Proposition 3.5
.

Under the conditions of Proposition
3.2
, the fractional stable process

is self-similar with Hurst exponent

H=\gamma+\frac{1}{\alpha}-1\in\mathbb{C}.

(29)

Proof.

To simplify the notations, we set
S=S_{a,b}^{\gamma,\alpha}
and
{\rm I}={\rm I}^{{\gamma};\scalebox{0.6}{$k$}(\gamma,\alpha)}_{a,b}
.
The self-similarity can be proved using the characteristic functional and the fact that
\widehat{\mathscr{P}}_{S_{1}}=\widehat{\mathscr{P}}_{S_{2}}
if and only if
S_{1}
and
S_{2}
have the same probability law. Indeed, for
T>0
, we have that

\displaystyle\widehat{\mathscr{P}}_{S(T\cdot)}(\varphi)

\displaystyle=\widehat{\mathscr{P}}_{S}\big(T^{-1}\varphi(\cdot/T)\big)=\mathrm{e}^{-\lVert T^{-1}\real({\rm I}^{*}\{\varphi(\cdot/T)\})\rVert_{\alpha}^{\alpha}}

\displaystyle=\mathrm{e}^{-\lVert T^{-1-\gamma}\real({\rm I}^{*}\{\varphi\}(\cdot/T))\rVert_{\alpha}^{\alpha}}=\mathrm{e}^{-\lVert T^{-1-\gamma+\frac{1}{\alpha}}\real({\rm I}^{*}\{\varphi\})\rVert_{\alpha}^{\alpha}}

\displaystyle=\widehat{\mathscr{P}}_{S}(T^{-1-\gamma+\frac{1}{\alpha}}\varphi)=\widehat{\mathscr{P}}_{T^{-1-\gamma+\frac{1}{\alpha}}S}(\varphi),

(32)

where we used in particular the

-homogeneity of

for the third equality.
Hence,
T^{-H}S(T\cdot)=S
for any

where

∎

Remark 3.7

Classically, we say that a random process
S=(S(x))_{x\in\mathbb{R}}
with well-defined sampled values has stationary increments if the law of
s(x_{1})-s(x_{0})
only depends on the difference
(x_{1}-x_{0})
for any
x_{0}<x_{1}
. Definition
3.6
covers and generalizes this notion. Indeed, assume that
s
has stationary increments of order
k=1
in the sense of Definition
3.6
. Then, for any test function
\varphi
,
h_{0}>0
and
t\in\mathbb{R}
, we have that
\langle\Delta_{h_{0}}S,\varphi\rangle
and
\langle\Delta_{h_{0}}S,\varphi(\cdot-t)\rangle
are equal in law. Picking
\varphi=\delta(\cdot-t_{0})
(which is possible for pointwise random processes),
h_{0}=t_{1}-t_{0}
, and
t=-t_{0}
, we deduce that

s(t_{1})-s(t_{0})\overset{(\mathcal{L})}{=}s(t_{1}-t_{0})-s(0).

(33)

The latter only depends on
t_{1}-t_{0}
and
S
has stationary increments in the classical sense.

Proposition 3.8
.

Under the conditions of Proposition
3.2
, the fractional stable process
S_{a,b}^{\gamma,\alpha}

1.

is stationary if
0<\real(\gamma)<1
; and

2.

has stationary increments of order
\lfloor\real(\gamma)\rfloor
if
\real(\gamma)\geq 1

Proof.

We set

and

.
The proof relies on the characteristic functional. For

(see (

the operator

\displaystyle\widehat{\mathscr{P}}_{S(\cdot-x_{0})}(\varphi)

\displaystyle=\widehat{\mathscr{P}}_{S}(\varphi(\cdot+x_{0}))=\exp(-\lVert\real({\rm I}^{*}\{\varphi(\cdot+x_{0}))\}\rVert_{\alpha}^{\alpha})

\displaystyle=\exp(-\lVert\real({\rm I}^{*}\{\varphi\}(\cdot+x_{0}))\rVert_{\alpha}^{\alpha})=\exp(-\lVert\real({\rm I}^{*}\{\varphi\})\rVert_{\alpha}^{\alpha})

(34)

where we used the shift-invariance of

Assume that

3.4
Regularity of Fractional Stable Processes

We characterize the smoothness of fractional stable processes in terms of local Sobolev regularity in the space
W_{p,\mathrm{loc}}^{\tau}
with
\tau\in\mathbb{R}
and
1\leq p\leq\infty
. With
p=2
, we recover the
L_{2}
-Sobolev regularity, while
p=\infty
corresponds to the Hölder regularity
[
58
]
. For fixed
p\geq 1
and
\tau_{1}\leq\tau_{2}
, we have the continuous embedding
W_{p,\mathrm{loc}}^{\tau_{2}}\subseteq W_{p,\mathrm{loc}}^{\tau_{1}}
.

Following
[
59
]
, we characterize the regularity properties of a generalized (random) function

via its critical smoothness function, defined for
1\leq p\leq\infty
by

\tau_{f}(p)=\sup\{\tau\in\mathbb{R},\ f\in W_{p,\mathrm{loc}}^{\tau}\}.

(36)

Then,
f\in W_{p,\mathrm{loc}}^{\tau}
for any
\tau<\tau_{f}(p)
and
f\notin W_{p,\mathrm{loc}}^{\tau}
for
\tau>\tau_{f}(p)
.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
h_{a,b}^{{\gamma}}(\cdot)
be a homogeneous generalized function of degree
{\gamma}\in\mathbb{C}
with
\real({\gamma})>-1
as in (
2.1
), and let
n\in\{1,2,\dots,\lceil\real({\gamma})\rceil+1\}
.
If
\widehat{\phi}:\mathbb{R}\mapsto\mathbb{C}
is
n
-times continuously differentiable such that
|\widehat{\phi}^{(j)}(\omega)|(1+|\omega|^{r+j})
is bounded for all
1\leq j\leq n
and some

, then,

\displaystyle\forall\,1<T,~~~~~~~~\Big\|\frac{{\rm d}^{n}}{{\rm d}\omega^{n}}\Big(\big(\widehat{\phi}(\omega)-\widehat{\phi}(\tfrac{\omega}{T})\big)h_{a,b}^{{\gamma}}(\omega)\Big)\Big\|_{1}\leq c\;T^{\real({\gamma})+2-n},

where

\displaystyle c=\tfrac{8\,(r+1)\,(|\real({\gamma})|+2)\,\max(|a|\,,\,|b|)}{(\real({\gamma})-n+2)\,(r+n-\real({\gamma})-1)}\bigg(\sum_{k=1}^{n}\scalebox{1}{$\binom{n}{k}$}\sup_{\omega}\Big(\big|\widehat{\phi}^{(k)}(\omega)\big|(1+|\omega|^{r+k})\Big)\bigg).

Proof.

Intuitively,
\widehat{\phi}(\omega)-\widehat{\phi}(\tfrac{\omega}{T})
behaves similar to
(1-\tfrac{1}{T})\omega\widehat{\phi}^{(1)}(\omega)
around
\omega=0
(a rigorous statement will be presented soon). Thus,
\big(\widehat{\phi}(\omega)-\widehat{\phi}(\tfrac{\omega}{T})\big)h_{a,b}^{{\gamma}}(\omega)
has a zero of order at least
\real({\gamma})+1
at
\omega=0
. This suggests that
\frac{{\rm d}^{n}}{{\rm d}\omega^{n}}\big(\widehat{\phi}(\omega)-\widehat{\phi}(\tfrac{\omega}{T})\big)
is either bounded at
\omega=0
(if
n\leq\lceil\real({\gamma})\rceil
) or is at least locally integrable around
\omega=0
(if
n=\lceil\real({\gamma})\rceil+1
). We also employ the boundedness property of
\big\{|\widehat{\phi}^{(j)}(\omega)|(1+|\omega|^{\gamma+j})\big\}_{i}
to show that
\frac{{\rm d}^{n}}{{\rm d}\omega^{n}}\big(\widehat{\phi}(\omega)-\widehat{\phi}(\tfrac{\omega}{T})\big)
decays asymptotically no slower than
\tfrac{1}{1+|\omega|^{1+\epsilon}}
for some
\epsilon>0
that is determined by
r
. As a result, we conclude that the
L_{1}
-norm of
\frac{{\rm d}^{n}}{{\rm d}\omega^{n}}\big(\widehat{\phi}(\omega)-\widehat{\phi}(\tfrac{\omega}{T})\big)
is finite (
\widehat{\phi}
is not even required to be asymptotically decaying). However, the main point in Lemma
4.1
is the scaling of the
L_{1}
-norm in terms of
T
.

To start our rigorous arguments, let us define

\displaystyle\mathchoice{\hbox to0.0pt{{$\underline{{\hbox to4.55551pt{}}}$\hss}}}{\hbox to0.0pt{{$\underline{{\hbox to4.55551pt{}}}$\hss}}}{\hbox to0.0pt{{$\underline{{\hbox to4.55551pt{}}}$\hss}}}{\hbox to0.0pt{{$\underline{{\hbox to4.55551pt{}}}$\hss}}}\widehat{\phi}(\omega)=\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}\tau_{\phantom{.}}\\
|\tau|\geq|\omega|\end{subarray}}\big|\tfrac{{\rm d}}{{\rm d}\tau}\widehat{\phi}(\tau)\big|.

(40)

Based on the assumptions,
|\widehat{\phi}^{(1)}(\omega)|(1+|\omega|^{r+1})
is bounded. Moreover,
r+1>\real({\gamma})-n+2>\real({\gamma})-\lceil\real({\gamma})\rceil+1>0
. This shows that
|\widehat{\phi}^{(1)}(\omega)|
is bounded and asymptotically decaying. Hence,
\mathchoice{\hbox to0.0pt{{$\underline{{\hbox to4.55551pt{}}}$\hss}}}{\hbox to0.0pt{{$\underline{{\hbox to4.55551pt{}}}$\hss}}}{\hbox to0.0pt{{$\underline{{\hbox to4.55551pt{}}}$\hss}}}{\hbox to0.0pt{{$\underline{{\hbox to4.55551pt{}}}$\hss}}}\widehat{\phi}(\omega)
is a bounded, even, and non-negative-valued function that is non-increasing in terms of
|\omega|
. Since

, we conclude that
\mathchoice{\hbox to0.0pt{{$\underline{{\hbox to4.55551pt{}}}$\hss}}}{\hbox to0.0pt{{$\underline{{\hbox to4.55551pt{}}}$\hss}}}{\hbox to0.0pt{{$\underline{{\hbox to4.55551pt{}}}$\hss}}}{\hbox to0.0pt{{$\underline{{\hbox to4.55551pt{}}}$\hss}}}\widehat{\phi}(\omega)(1+|\omega|^{r+1})
is also bounded:

\displaystyle\mathchoice{\hbox to0.0pt{{$\underline{{\hbox to4.55551pt{}}}$\hss}}}{\hbox to0.0pt{{$\underline{{\hbox to4.55551pt{}}}$\hss}}}{\hbox to0.0pt{{$\underline{{\hbox to4.55551pt{}}}$\hss}}}{\hbox to0.0pt{{$\underline{{\hbox to4.55551pt{}}}$\hss}}}\widehat{\phi}(\omega)(1+|\omega|^{r+1})=\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}\tau_{\phantom{.}}\\
|\tau|\geq|\omega|\end{subarray}}\big|\widehat{\phi}^{(1)}(\tau)\big|(1+|\omega|^{r+1})

\displaystyle\leq\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}\tau_{\phantom{.}}\\
|\tau|\geq|\omega|\end{subarray}}\big|\widehat{\phi}^{(1)}(\tau)\big|(1+|\tau|^{r+1})

\displaystyle\leq\underbrace{\sup_{\tau}\big|\widehat{\phi}^{(1)}(\tau)\big|(1+|\tau|^{r+1})}_{\text{a finite constant}}.

(41)

Next, we bound the difference
\widehat{\phi}(\omega)-\widehat{\phi}(\tfrac{\omega}{T})
via
\mathchoice{\hbox to0.0pt{{$\underline{{\hbox to4.55551pt{}}}$\hss}}}{\hbox to0.0pt{{$\underline{{\hbox to4.55551pt{}}}$\hss}}}{\hbox to0.0pt{{$\underline{{\hbox to4.55551pt{}}}$\hss}}}{\hbox to0.0pt{{$\underline{{\hbox to4.55551pt{}}}$\hss}}}\widehat{\phi}(\omega)

\displaystyle\real\big(\widehat{\phi}(\omega)\big)-\real\big(\widehat{\phi}(\tfrac{\omega}{T})\big)

\displaystyle=(\omega-\tfrac{\omega}{T})\tfrac{{\rm d}}{{\rm d}\omega}\real\big(\widehat{\phi}(\omega)\big)\Big|_{\omega=\zeta_{r}}=\tfrac{T-1}{T}\omega\,\real\big(\widehat{\phi}^{(1)}(\zeta_{r})\big),

\displaystyle\imag\big(\widehat{\phi}(\omega)\big)-\imag\big(\widehat{\phi}(\tfrac{\omega}{T})\big)

\displaystyle=(\omega-\tfrac{\omega}{T})\tfrac{{\rm d}}{{\rm d}\omega}\imag\big(\widehat{\phi}(\omega)\big)\Big|_{\omega=\zeta_{i}}=\tfrac{T-1}{T}\omega\,\imag\big(\widehat{\phi}^{(1)}(\zeta_{i})\big),

\displaystyle\leq|\omega|\;\mathchoice{\hbox to0.0pt{{$\underline{{\hbox to4.55551pt{}}}$\hss}}}{\hbox to0.0pt{{$\underline{{\hbox to4.55551pt{}}}$\hss}}}{\hbox to0.0pt{{$\underline{{\hbox to4.55551pt{}}}$\hss}}}{\hbox to0.0pt{{$\underline{{\hbox to4.55551pt{}}}$\hss}}}\widehat{\phi}(\tfrac{\omega}{T}).

Similarly, we can show that

\displaystyle\big|\imag\big(\widehat{\phi}(\omega)-\widehat{\phi}(\tfrac{\omega}{T})\big)\big|\leq|\omega|\;\mathchoice{\hbox to0.0pt{{$\underline{{\hbox to4.55551pt{}}}$\hss}}}{\hbox to0.0pt{{$\underline{{\hbox to4.55551pt{}}}$\hss}}}{\hbox to0.0pt{{$\underline{{\hbox to4.55551pt{}}}$\hss}}}{\hbox to0.0pt{{$\underline{{\hbox to4.55551pt{}}}$\hss}}}\widehat{\phi}(\tfrac{\omega}{T}).

6
Conclusion

In this paper, we initially studied complex-order fractional operators. We showed that by applying certain complex-order fractional integration operators to real-valued symmetric
\alpha
-stable white noise processes, we can generate self-similar stable processes with complex-valued Hurst exponent. Some of the introduced processes can be whitened by applying complex-order fractional derivative operators; as such, they can be described as solutions of fractional complex-order stochastic differential equations.
While we proved the existence of the random processes using characteristic functionals, we provided tools for numerically approximating such processes. We further studied the smoothness properties of the processes and showed that they have stationary increments of large enough orders.

G G -torsors on perfectoid spaces
Ben Heuer

Abstract
For any rigid analytic group variety G G over a non-archimedean field K K over ℚ p \mathbb{Q}_{p} , we study G G -torsors on adic spaces over K K in the v v -topology. Our main result is that on perfectoid spaces, G G -torsors in the étale and v v -topology are equivalent. This generalises the known cases of G = 𝔾 a G=\mathbb{G}_{a} and G = GL n G=\mathrm{GL}_{n} due to Scholze and Kedlaya–Liu. On a general adic space X X over K K , where there can be more v v -topological G G -torsors than étale ones, we show that for any open subgroup U ⊆ G U\subseteq G , any G G -torsor on X v X_{v} admits a reduction of structure group to U U étale-locally on X X . This has applications in the context of the p p -adic Simpson correspondence: For example, we use it to show that on any adic space, generalised ℚ p \mathbb{Q}_{p} -representations are equivalent to v v -vector bundles.

Introduction

Let
G
be a smooth group scheme over a field
K
, and let
X
be a
K
-scheme. Then by a well-known Theorem of Grothendieck
[
Gro68
, Théorème 11.7, Remark 11.8]
, the functor

\{\text{$G$-torsors on $X_{{\operatorname{\acute{e}t}}}$}\}\xrightarrow{\raisebox{-2.79033pt}{\smash{$\sim$}}}\{\text{$G$-torsors on $X_{\mathrm{fppf}}$}\}

is an equivalence of categories. This article studies a similar functor in
p
-adic geometry:

Let
K
be a non-archimedean field over
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
and let
X
be an adic space over
K
, considered as a diamond in the sense of Scholze
[
Sch18
, §15]
. Then
X
has an étale topology and a much finer
v
-topology
[
Sch18
, §14]
.
Let
G
be any rigid analytic group variety over
K
, not necessarily commutative, considered as a diamond. In this article, we study the functor

\{\text{$G$-torsors on $X_{{\operatorname{\acute{e}t}}}$}\}\hookrightarrow\{\text{$G$-torsors on $X_{v}$}\},

where a
G
-torsor is a sheaf with a
G
-action that is locally isomorphic to
G
(see
Section

).
This functor is always fully faithful. The question whether it is essentially surjective can be phrased in terms of the morphism of sites

\nu:X_{v}\to X_{{\operatorname{\acute{e}t}}}

by asking whether
R^{1}\nu_{\ast}G
vanishes.

If
X
is a perfectoid space, then torsors under
G=\mathbb{G}_{a}
on
X_{{\operatorname{\acute{e}t}}}
and
X_{v}
agree, namely Scholze proves that
R\nu_{\ast}\mathbb{G}_{a}=\mathbb{G}_{a}

[
Sch18
, Proposition 8.8]
.
By a Theorem of Kedlaya–Liu, also vector bundles on
X_{{\operatorname{\acute{e}t}}}
and

agree
[
KL16
, Theorem 3.5.8]
(cf.
[
SW20
, Lemma 17.1.8]
), which is the case of
G=\mathrm{GL}_{n}
. This implies the case of linear algebraic

by the Tannakian formalism
[
SW20
, §19.5]
.
Our main result is the following generalisation of all of these:

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
X
be a perfectoid space and let
G
be a rigid group over
K
. The functor

\{\text{$G$-torsors on $X_{{\operatorname{\acute{e}t}}}$}\}\xrightarrow{\raisebox{-2.95564pt}{\smash{$\sim$}}}\{\text{$G$-torsors on $X_{v}$}\}

is an equivalence of categories. If
G
is commutative, then we more generally have
R\nu_{\ast}G=G
.

Our method is different to that of the aforementioned works, and also gives a new proof of Kedlaya–Liu’s Theorem.
One interesting new case is
G=\mathrm{GL}_{n}(\mathcal{O}^{+})
, which says that the categories of finite locally free
\mathcal{O}^{+}
-modules on
X_{{\operatorname{\acute{e}t}}}
and
X_{v}
agree. Another consequence is:

Corollary 1.2
.

Let
X
be any adic space over

, then the categories of
G
-torsors on
X_{{\operatorname{qpro\acute{e}t}}}
(the quasi-pro-étale site of
X
) and
X_{v}
are equivalent, and any
G
-torsor on
X_{v}
is a diamond.

1.1
G
-torsors on rigid spaces

Let now
X
be a rigid space, then it is known that there are in general many more
v
-topological

G
-torsors than there are étale ones: For example, if
K
is a complete algebraically closed extension of
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
and
X
is smooth over
K
, then already for
G=\mathbb{G}_{a}
, a key result in Scholze’s approach to the Hodge–Tate spectral sequence is a canonical isomorphism

R^{1}\nu_{\ast}\mathbb{G}_{a}=\Omega_{X}(-1)

on
X_{{\operatorname{\acute{e}t}}}
to a Tate twist of the Kähler differentials on
X

[
Sch13b
, Proposition 3.23]
.

Also for
G=\mathrm{GL}_{n}
, it is known that étale vector bundles and
v
-vector bundles on
X
are not the same, see
[
Heu20
]
for the case of
G=\mathbb{G}_{m}
. The difference is closely related to the
p
-adic Simpson correspondence: As we explain in
Section

2
, there is an equivalence

\{\text{finite locally free $\mathcal{O}^{+}$-modules on $X_{v}$}\}\xrightarrow{\raisebox{-2.79033pt}{\smash{$\sim$}}}\{\text{generalised representations on $X$}\}

where following Faltings
[
Fal05
]
, generalised representations are compatible systems
(V_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
of finite locally free
\mathcal{O}^{+}/p^{n}
-modules
V_{n}
on
X_{{\operatorname{\acute{e}t}}}
. The equivalence is similar in spirit to the equivalence between lisse
\mathbb{Q}_{l}
-sheaves on a scheme with locally free
\mathbb{Q}_{l}
-modules on the pro-étale site in the sense of Bhatt–Scholze
[
BS15
, §1]
. The proof hinges on the following:

Proposition 1.3
.

We have an isomorphism
R\nu_{\ast}(\mathcal{O}^{+}/p^{n})=\mathcal{O}^{+}/p^{n}
(already before passing to the almost category) and
R^{1}\nu_{\ast}\mathrm{GL}_{n}(\mathcal{O}^{+}/p^{n})=1
. In particular, we have an equivalence

\nu^{\ast}:\Big\{\begin{array}[]{c}\text{finite locally free}\\
\text{$\mathcal{O}^{+}/p^{n}$-modules on $X_{\operatorname{\acute{e}t}}$}\end{array}\Big\}\xrightarrow{\raisebox{-2.95564pt}{\smash{$\sim$}}}\Big\{\begin{array}[]{c}\text{finite locally free}\\
\text{$\mathcal{O}^{+}/p^{n}$-modules on $X_{v}$}\end{array}\Big\}.

One reason why we are interested in
v
-topological
G
-torsors under general rigid groups
G
is for generalisations of the
p
-adic Simpson correspondence to more general non-abelian coefficients, as explored in
[
Heu
]
: These relate
v
-topological
G
-bundles to
G
-Higgs bundles. The relevance of
1.1
in this context is that it shows that
v
-topological
G
-bundles are “locally small”, namely they admit reductions of structure groups to small open subgroups:

1.2
Reduction of structure group

The technical heart of this article is the study of sheaves
F
that “commute with tilde-limits”:

Definition 1.4
.

A sheaf
F
on the big étale site of sousperfectoid spaces over
K
is said to satisfy the
approximation property
if for any affinoid perfectoid tilde-limit
X\sim\varprojlim_{i\in I}X_{i}
of affinoid spaces
X_{i}
such that
\varinjlim_{i\in I}\mathcal{O}(X_{i})\to\mathcal{O}(X)
has dense image,
F(X)=\varinjlim_{i\in I}F(X_{i})
.

Examples of such a sheaves include
\mathcal{O}^{+}/p^{n}
and thus also
\mathrm{GL}_{n}(\mathcal{O}^{+}/p^{n})
. We show:

Proposition 1.5
.

Let
F
be a sheaf of groups satisfying the approximation property. Then
F
is already a
v
-sheaf and

R^{1}\nu_{\ast}F=1
. If
F
is a sheaf of abelian groups, then
R\nu_{\ast}F=F
.

The relevance to our study of
G
-torsors is then the following:

Proposition 1.6
.

Let
G
be any rigid group. Let
U\subseteq G
be any rigid open subgroup, not necessarily normal. Then
G/U
satisfies the approximation property.

For example, both
\mathcal{O}^{+}/p^{n}
and
\mathrm{GL}_{n}(\mathcal{O}^{+}/p^{n})
are of this form.
We use the Proposition to show that any
G
-torsor on
X
admits a reduction of structure group to
U
on an étale cover:

Theorem 1.7
.

Let
G
be a rigid group over
K
and let
U\subseteq G
be a rigid open subgroup. Let
X
be a sousperfectoid space over
K
and let
\nu:X_{v}\to X_{{\operatorname{\acute{e}t}}}
be the natural morphism of sites. Then the natural map

R^{1}\nu_{\ast}U\to R^{1}\nu_{\ast}G

is surjective. If
G
is commutative, we more generally have

R^{k}\nu_{\ast}U=R^{k}\nu_{\ast}G

for all
k\geq 1
.

For non-commutative
G
, the map
R^{1}\nu_{\ast}U\to R^{1}\nu_{\ast}G
is not in general an isomorphism.

The idea for the proof of
1.1
is now that by the theory of
p
-adic Lie groups, there is a large supply of open subgroups of
G
. If
G
is commutative, then these are isomorphic to open subgroups of the Lie algebra of
G
via the exponential, and one can deduce the result from the case of
\mathbb{G}_{a}
. In general, the exponential does not respect the group structure, but the relation to the Lie algebra suffices to trivialise
G
-torsors étale-locally by inductive lifting.

We note that in turn,

1.7
can easily be recovered from
1.1
.

Conceptually, the main aim of this work is to launch a systematic study of
G
-torsors on adic spaces with a view towards generalisations and reformulations of the
p
-adic Simpson correspondence. Apart from the Theorems mentioned above, we therefore prove some further foundational results on
G
-torsors. We continue our study in
[
Heu
]
, where based on the results of this article, we give an explicit description of the sheaf
R^{1}\nu_{\ast}G
on smooth rigid spaces, and use this to construct analytic moduli spaces of
G
-torsors on rigid spaces.

Setup and Notation

Throughout let
p
be a prime and
K
a non-archimedean field of residue characteristic
p
. We fix a subring
K^{+}\subseteq K
of integral elements, which we often drop from notation. By an adic space over
K
we mean an adic space over
\operatorname{Spa}(K,K^{+})
in the sense of Huber. For any adic space

, we use the étale site
X_{{\operatorname{\acute{e}t}}}
in the sense of Kedlaya–Liu
[
KL15
, Definition 8.2.16]
.

By a rigid space over

we mean an adic space locally of topologically finite type over
\operatorname{Spa}(K,K^{+})
. One example we use several times is the closed ball
\mathbb{B}^{d}=\operatorname{Spa}(K\langle T_{1},\dots,T_{d}\rangle)
. We use perfectoid spaces in the sense of
[
Sch12
]
, as well as Scholze’s category of diamonds
[
Sch18
]
.
Most adic spaces we consider throughout will be sousperfectoid in the sense of
[
SW20
, §6.3]
[
HK
]
: examples of such are smooth rigid spaces, perfectoid spaces, and products thereof. Moreover, we recall that for any sousperfectoid space
X
over
K
, any object of the étale site
X_{{\operatorname{\acute{e}t}}}
is again sousperfectoid, hence sheafy. We may therefore freely switch back and forth between
X
and its associated locally spatial diamond
X^{\diamondsuit}

[
Sch18
, §15]
. As we have fixed a structure map to
K
, we may regard
X^{\diamondsuit}
as a sheaf on the
v
-site
\operatorname{Perf}_{K,v}
of perfectoid spaces over
K
. Then diamondification identifies the étale sites and the structure sheaves
[
Sch18
, Lemma 15.6]
of
X
and
X^{\diamondsuit}
, where on the diamantine side we always mean the structure sheaf
\mathcal{O}
on
X^{\diamondsuit}
induced from that on
\operatorname{Perf}_{K,v}
.

We denote by
\mathrm{Sous}_{K}
the category of sousperfectoid spaces over
K
. We consider this as a site equipped either with the étale topology
\mathrm{Sous}_{K,{\operatorname{\acute{e}t}}}
or the
v
-topology
\mathrm{Sous}_{K,v}
.

Generalised representations on rigid spaces

Before we discuss
G
-torsors for general rigid groups

, we study in this section the simpler case of
v
-vector
bundles, that is,
\mathrm{GL}_{n}
-torsors on
X_{v}
. While this simplifies the setting, the overall line of argument is already the same as in the general case.
We begin by giving an alternative description of

-vector bundles that is of interest in the

-adic Simpson correspondence: The equivalence between

-vector bundles and Faltings’ generalised representations.

2.1
Generalised representations

We start by adapting generalised representations as defined by Faltings
[
Fal05
, §2 and Theorem 3]
from the algebraic setting of log schemes to an analytic setting: Throughout this section, let
K
be any non-archimedean extension of
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
and let
X
be any adic space over
K
.

Definition 2.1
.

1.

A
generalised representation
on
X
is a system
(M_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
of finite locally free
\mathcal{O}^{+}/p^{k}
modules
M_{k}
on
X_{{\operatorname{\acute{e}t}}}
with isomorphisms
M_{k+1}/p^{k}\xrightarrow{\raisebox{-2.79033pt}{\smash{$\sim$}}}M_{k}
for all
k
.

2.

The isogeny category is the localisation of the category of generalised representations on
X
at multiplication by
p
.
A
generalised
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
-representation
on
X
is a system of generalised representations
(M_{U_{i}})_{U_{i}\in\mathfrak{U}}
on some étale cover
\mathfrak{U}
of
X
with isomorphisms
M_{U_{i}|U_{i}\times_{X}U_{j}}\to M_{U_{j}|U_{j}\times_{X}U_{i}}
in the isogeny category that satisfy the cocycle condition.

Remark 2.2
.

The name “generalised representation” stems from the following observation: Fix any base-point
x\in X(\overline{K})
and consider the étale fundamental group
\pi_{1}(X):=\pi^{{\operatorname{\acute{e}t}}}_{1}(X,x)
, then to any semilinear continuous representation
\rho:\pi_{1}(X)\to\mathrm{GL}(E)
on a finite free
\mathcal{O}_{K}
-module
E
, we can associate a generalised representation: Namely, by continuity, the reduction
\rho_{k}:\pi_{1}(X)\to\mathrm{GL}_{r}(\mathcal{O}_{K}/p^{k})
factors through a finite quotient of
\pi_{1}(X)
, corresponding to a finite étale cover
X^{\prime}\to X
. We can then regard
\rho_{n}
as a descent datum for a finite locally free
\mathcal{O}^{+}/p^{k}
-module
M_{k}
on along
X^{\prime}\to X
, by defining
M_{k}
on any
U\in X_{{\operatorname{\acute{e}t}}}
to be

M_{k}(U):=\{x\in E(U\times_{X}X^{\prime})/p^{k}\mid\gamma^{\ast}x=\rho^{-1}(\gamma)x\text{ for all }\gamma\in\pi_{1}(X)\}.

The system
(M_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
then defines a generalised representation in the above sense.

The main goal of this short section is to prove the following equivalent characterisation:

Proposition 2.3
.

Let
X
be an adic space over a non-archimedean field
K
over
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
such that every object of
X_{{\operatorname{\acute{e}t}}}
is a sheafy adic space (e.g.
X
can be any rigid spaces, or sousperfectoid).

1.

The morphism of sites
\nu:X_{v}\to X_{{\operatorname{\acute{e}t}}}
induces a natural equivalence of categories

\displaystyle\{\text{finite locally free $\mathcal{O}^{+}$-modules on $X_{v}$}\}

\displaystyle\to\{\text{generalised representations on $X$}\}

\displaystyle V

\displaystyle\mapsto(\nu_{\ast}(V/p^{k}))_{k\in\mathbb{N}}.

Moreover, these are equivalent to finite locally free
\mathcal{O}^{+}
-modules on
X_{{\operatorname{qpro\acute{e}t}}}
, and if
X
is locally Noetherian also to finite locally free
\mathcal{O}^{+}
-modules on
X_{{\operatorname{pro\acute{e}t}}}
.

2.

By localising at multiplication by
p
, this defines an equivalence of categories

\displaystyle\{\text{finite locally free $\mathcal{O}$-modules on $X_{v}$}\}

\displaystyle\to\{\text{generalised $\mathbb{Q}_{p}$-representations on $X$}.\}

Moreover, these are equivalent to finite locally free
\mathcal{O}
-modules on
X_{{\operatorname{qpro\acute{e}t}}}
, and if
X
is locally Noetherian also to finite locally free
\mathcal{O}
-modules on
X_{{\operatorname{pro\acute{e}t}}}
.

Definition 2.4
.

We also call a finite locally free
\mathcal{O}
-modules on
X_{v}
a

-vector bundle
. We then call finite locally free
\mathcal{O}
-modules on

From this perspective, one could reverse-engineer Faltings’ definition of generalised

-representations by saying that they describe

-vector bundles purely in terms of

.

Remark 2.5
.

The idea that generalised representations on a rigid space

are vector bundles on

is mentioned or implicit in other works: In the arithmetic setting of smooth rigid spaces over discretely valued fields, it is hinted at by Liu–Zhu
[

]
(see Remark 2.6), and a related result appears in the work of Morrow–Tsuji (cf
[

, Theorem 5.7]
) in a good reduction setting. In more algebraic geometric settings, related statements have been shown by Xu
[

, §3.29]
and Yang–Zhu

, Lemma 2.10]

2.3
now provides a result in the full generality of adic spaces over

, free from any choice of integral model, or additional assumptions on
X
or

. We note that this analytic setting introduces some additional subtleties due to the difference between
\mathcal{O}^{+}/p^{k}
-modules on

and modules on the reduction mod

of some given formal model.

The step to finite locally free
\mathcal{O}^{+}
-modules in the

-topology is new: It is relevant because

is only defined for locally Noetherian

, while the

-topology is available more generally, which is useful even for rigid spaces e.g. in the relative setting studied in

]
.

The proof will take up the entire section as we will also discuss some related topics to prepare the more general version for
G
-torsors.
The basic idea is to compare locally free
\mathcal{O}^{+}/p
-modules for various topologies. For this we can relax the setup of this section:

Let
K
be any non-archimedean field, not necessarily over
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
, and let
\varpi\in\mathcal{O}_{K}
be any pseudo-uniformiser of
K
. The following Proposition is the technical heart of this section:

Proposition 2.6
.

Let
X
be an adic space over
K
such that every object of its étale site
X_{{\operatorname{\acute{e}t}}}
is sheafy. Then for
m=0,1
the map

H^{m}_{{\operatorname{\acute{e}t}}}(X,\mathrm{GL}_{n}(\mathcal{O}^{+}/\varpi))\xrightarrow{\raisebox{-2.95564pt}{\smash{$\sim$}}}H^{m}_{v}(X,\mathrm{GL}_{n}(\mathcal{O}^{+}/\varpi))

is an isomorphism for all
r\in\mathbb{N}
. Similarly for
\mathrm{GL}_{n}(\mathcal{O}^{+}/\varpi\mathfrak{m})
. In particular, the functor

\nu^{\ast}:\Big\{\begin{array}[]{c}\text{finite locally free}\\
\text{$\mathcal{O}^{+}/\varpi$-modules on $X_{\operatorname{\acute{e}t}}$}\end{array}\Big\}\to\Big\{\begin{array}[]{c}\text{finite locally free}\\
\text{$\mathcal{O}^{+}/\varpi$-modules on $X_{v}$}\end{array}\Big\}

is an equivalence of categories, where
\nu:X_{v}\to X_{{\operatorname{\acute{e}t}}}
is the natural morphism.

Proof.

To see that the functor is fully faithful, it suffices to see
\nu_{\ast}(\mathcal{O}^{+}/\varpi)=\mathcal{O}^{+}/\varpi
, i.e.
H^{0}_{{\operatorname{\acute{e}t}}}(X,\mathcal{O}^{+}/\varpi)=H^{0}_{v}(X,\mathcal{O}^{+}/\varpi)

is an (honest, not just almost) isomorphism:
Indeed, for any two finite locally free
\mathcal{O}^{+}/\varpi
-modules
E_{1}
and
E_{2}
on
X_{{\operatorname{\acute{e}t}}}
, we have
\HOM(\nu^{\ast}E_{1},\nu^{\ast}E_{2})=\nu^{\ast}\HOM(E_{1},E_{2})
, and locally where the locally free
\mathcal{O}^{+}/\varpi
-module
\HOM(E_{1},E_{2})
becomes trivial, the statement then follows.

Similarly, to see essential surjectivity, it suffices to see that
R^{1}\nu_{\ast}\mathrm{GL}_{r}(\mathcal{O}^{+}/\varpi)=1
.

These statements, and their analogues for
\mathcal{O}^{+}/\varpi\mathfrak{m}
, all follow by approximation arguments: We now axiomatise these and discuss them in detail in the following subsection.

In the situation of

Lemma 2.10

Suppose that we have cofiltered inverse systems

for all

Consider the Cartesian diagram of sheaves of sets

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces M_{n}(\mathcal{O}^{+}/\varpi)}

\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\det}

Proof of

We start with a few technical Lemmas about the situation:

Lemma 2.16

Lemma 2.19

Let

We can now give the proof of

For strictly totally disconnected
X

F_{v}:=\nu_{\ast}\nu^{\ast}F
.

Step 1:
F\to\nu_{\ast}\nu^{\ast}F
is injective for strictly totally disconnected
X
.

Let
\beta\in F(X)
be in the kernel of
F(X)\to F_{v}(X)
, then it is in the kernel of
F(X)\to F(Y)
for some
v
-cover
Y\to X
. Applying the approximation property to the tilde-limit

from

F(Y)=\varinjlim F(Y_{i})
,
so

is already killed by some cover

. But

also says that the map

has a section, so it follows that

is trivial.

Step 2:
F\to\nu_{\ast}\nu^{\ast}F
is injective for any
X

Let

be the strictly totally disconnected pro-étale cover of

from

is an étale sheaf, then the approximation property, and finally step 1, we find that the following map is injective:

F(X)\hookrightarrow\varinjlim F(X_{i})=F(\widetilde{X})\hookrightarrow F_{v}(\widetilde{X}).

As this factors through
F_{v}(X)
, this shows that

is injective.

Step 3:
F\to\nu_{\ast}\nu^{\ast}F
is surjective for strictly totally disconnected
X
.

Let
\alpha\in F_{v}(X)
, then there is an affinoid perfectoid
v
-cover
Y\to X
such that

appears in

. By step 2 applied to
F(Y\times_{X}Y)\hookrightarrow F_{v}(Y\times_{X}Y)

By the same approximation

as in step

, as well as

and the approximation property, we have
{\check{H}}^{0}(Y\to X,F)=\varinjlim{\check{H}}^{0}(Y_{i}\to X,F)
. It follows that for

already lies in the image of

Step 4:

is an isomorphism for any adic space
X

Using the cover from
2.18
.2, we deduce as in step 2 from the approximation property that

F_{v}(X)={\check{H}}^{0}(\widetilde{X}\to X,F_{v})={\check{H}}^{0}(\widetilde{X}\to X,F)=\varinjlim{\check{H}}^{0}(X_{i}\to X,F)=F(X),

where the second equality uses step 3, as well as step 2 to see that

We now prove the vanishing of

for

for strictly totally disconnected

As

is strictly totally disconnected, it has trivial étale cohomology, so we need to show that

.
Let
\alpha\in H^{m}_{v}(X,F)
. By locality of cohomology, there is an affinoid perfectoid
v
-cover
Y\to X
such that
\alpha
becomes trivial in

.

For the non-abelian case and

For the abelian case and

{\check{H}}^{k}(Y\to X,H^{j}_{v}(-,F))\Rightarrow H^{k+j}_{v}(X,F)

for

2.17
and invoke

and
2.16

{\check{H}}^{k}(Y\to X,H^{j}_{v}(-,F))={\check{H}}^{k}(Y\to X,H^{j}_{{\operatorname{\acute{e}t}}}(-,F))=\varinjlim{\check{H}}^{k}(Y_{i}\to X,H^{j}_{{\operatorname{\acute{e}t}}}(-,F)).

As

is split, the last term vanishes for

,

contributes. This means that

has trivial kernel. Hence

.

Step 6: induction step to prove

for general
X
.

Finally, we assume by induction hypothesis that
R^{j}\nu_{\ast}F=1
for any
1\leq j<m
and any
X
, as well as for

if
X
is totally disconnected, and deduce that

. For this we again use the cover
\widetilde{X}\to X
from
2.18
.2. As in step 5, we consider the short exact sequence of pointed sets, respectively the Čech-to-sheaf spectral sequence for abelian
F

{\check{H}}^{k}(\widetilde{X}\to X,H^{j}_{v}(-,F))\Rightarrow H^{k+j}_{v}(X,F)

for

.
By induction hypothesis, we have

{\check{H}}^{k}(\widetilde{X}\to X,H^{j}_{v}(-,F))=\varinjlim_{i}{\check{H}}^{k}(X_{i}\to X,H^{j}_{{\operatorname{\acute{e}t}}}(-,F))

for all

and

and

. For

we deduce that

It follows that finite locally free

Already for

Lemma 2.24
.

Let
X
be an affinoid perfectoid space. Recall that
\varpi
is any pseudo-uniformiser.

1.

Let
V
be a
\varpi
-torsionfree
\mathcal{O}^{+}
-module on

such that
V=\varprojlim_{k}V/\varpi^{k}

such that
V/\varpi\mathfrak{m}\cong\mathcal{O}^{+r}/\varpi\mathfrak{m}
as
\mathcal{O}^{+}
-modules. Then
V\cong\mathcal{O}^{+r}
.

2.

Let
V
be a finite locally free
\mathcal{O}^{+}/\varpi
-module. If
V/\mathfrak{m}
is free over
\mathcal{O}^{+}/\mathfrak{m}
, then
V
is free.

Proof.

Since
V
is
\varpi
-torsionfree, we have for any
k>0
a short exact sequence on
X_{v}

0\to\mathfrak{m}V/\varpi\mathfrak{m}\xrightarrow{\varpi^{k}}V/\varpi^{k+1}\mathfrak{m}\to V/\varpi^{k}\mathfrak{m}\to 0.

For part 1, assume that
V/\varpi^{k}\mathfrak{m}
is free of rank
r
, and let
v_{1},\dots,v_{r}
be any basis of
V/\varpi^{k}\mathfrak{m}(X)
as a finite free
\mathcal{O}^{+}/\varpi^{k}\mathfrak{m}(X)
-module. Consider the long exact sequence

0\to\mathfrak{m}V/\varpi\mathfrak{m}(X)\to V/\varpi^{k+1}\mathfrak{m}(X)\to V/\varpi^{k}\mathfrak{m}(X)\to H^{1}_{v}(X,\mathfrak{m}V/\varpi\mathfrak{m}).

The last term vanishes since
\mathfrak{m}V/\varpi\mathfrak{m}\cong\mathfrak{m}\mathcal{O}^{+r}/\varpi\mathfrak{m}
by assumption and
H^{1}_{v}(X,\mathcal{O}^{+}/\varpi\mathfrak{m})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle a}}{{=}}0
vanishes after tensoring with
\mathfrak{m}
.
This shows that we can lift the basis vectors to sections
v_{1}^{\prime},\dots,v_{r}^{\prime}\in V/\varpi^{k+1}\mathfrak{m}(X)
. Starting with

, we can thus inductively define a map

\phi_{k+1}:\mathcal{O}^{+r}/\varpi^{k+1}\mathfrak{m}\to V/\varpi^{k+1}\mathfrak{m}

which reduces mod
\varpi^{k}\mathfrak{m}
to

. By the 5-Lemma,

is an isomorphism. In the limit, due to the completeness assumption on

\phi=\varprojlim_{k}\phi_{k}:\mathcal{O}^{+r}\to V

To deduce part 2, observe that we can by the same argument lift any basis of

to sections of

. This defines a map
\phi:\mathcal{O}^{+r}/\varpi\mathfrak{m}\to V/\varpi\mathfrak{m}
that is an isomorphism mod
\mathfrak{m}
. If we know a priori that

is finite locally free, we can consider

which is an invertible section mod

, thus invertible. This shows that

is an isomorphism.

Rigid groups have been studied primarily in the commutative case, e.g. in

Example 3.3
.

1.

For any algebraic group

Assume that

3.1
The correspondence between rigid groups and Lie algebras

For any rigid group
G
over
(K,K^{+})
, we denote by

\mathfrak{g}:=\operatorname{Lie}G:=\ker(G(K[X]/X^{2})\to G(K))

the
Lie algebra
of
G
, defined exactly like for algebraic groups (see also
[
Far19
, §1.2]
). Its underlying
K
-vector space is the tangent space of
G
at the identity, so
\dim\mathfrak{g}=\dim G
. Similarly as for algebraic groups, we can also regard
\mathfrak{g}
as a rigid vector group over
K
, explicitly given by
\operatorname{Lie}G\otimes_{K}\mathbb{G}_{a}
. As usual, we also consider this as a
v
-sheaf on
\operatorname{Perf}_{K}
, explicitly given for any perfectoid
K
-algebra
(R,R^{+})
by
\mathfrak{g}(R)=\ker(G(R[X]/X^{2})\to G(R))
.
We shall therefore from now on write
\mathfrak{g}(K)
when we mean the underlying
K
-vector space.

One application of the
v
-sheaf perspective is that it immediately shows that we have a rigid analytic
adjoint action

\mathrm{ad}:G\to\mathrm{GL}(\mathfrak{g}),

a homomorphism of rigid groups sending
g\in G(R)
to the
R
-linear automorphism of
\mathfrak{g}(R)
induced on tangent spaces by the conjugation map
G\times_{K}R\to G\times_{K}R
that sends
h\mapsto g^{-1}hg
. On tangent spaces, this induces a map
\mathrm{ad}:\mathfrak{g}\to\mathrm{End}(\mathfrak{g})
which defines the Lie bracket on
\mathfrak{g}
.

With these definitions, there is a
p
-adic analogue of the Lie group-Lie algebra correspondence in complex geometry, the main difference to the complex case being that one needs to account for the fact that in the
p
-adic setting there are many more open subgroups. While we do not know of a place in the literature where this is discussed in the present setting of rigid analytic groups, the construction is of course essentially classical as it follows immediately from the theory of
p
-adic Lie groups, as developed in
[
Bou72
]
[
Ser06
]
[
Sch11
]
:

Theorem 3.4

(
[

, II]
)
.

The functor constructed above

\mathrm{Lie}:\{\text{rigid groups over $K$}\}\to\{\text{fin.\ dim.\ Lie algebras over $K$}\}

becomes an equivalence of categories after localising the left hand side at the class of homomorphisms of rigid groups that are open immersions.

Moreover, for any homomorphism
f:G\to H
of rigid groups for which the morphism
\mathrm{Lie}(f):\mathrm{Lie}(G)\to\mathrm{Lie}(H)
is an isomorphism, there are rigid open subgroups
G_{0}\subseteq G
and
H_{0}\subseteq H
such that

restricts to an isomorphism of rigid groups
G_{0}\to H_{0}
.

Proof.

Due to
3.2
, there are open neighbourhoods of
1\in G
and
1\in H
inside of which rigid open subdiscs around
1
with morphisms of rigid spaces between them are equivalent to open subdiscs of
G(K)
and
H(K)
centered at
1
and analytic maps between them in the sense of
p
-adic Lie groups as defined in
[
Ser06
, II, 2]
. In particular, the category on the left becomes after localisation equivalent to Serre’s “analytic group chunks”.

Again by
3.2
, the completion
\widehat{G}
of
G
at
1
is isomorphic as a formal scheme to
\operatorname{Spf}(K[[X_{1},\dots,X_{d}]])
and inherits the structure of a formal group law. This defines a functor

R:\{\text{$d$-dim.\ rigid groups over $K$}\}\to\{\text{$d$-dim.\ formal group laws over $K$}\}.

On the other hand, sending a formal group law

over
K
to the tangent space
\mathfrak{h}
at the origin defines an equivalence of categories
[
Ser06
, II, 5, §6]

S:\{\text{$d$-dim.\ formal group laws over $K$}\}\to\{\text{$d$-dim.\ Lie algebras over $K$}\}.

The composition is easily seen to coincide with
\mathrm{Lie}
.

It thus suffices to prove the results for the functor

is essentially surjective follows from
[

, II, 4, §8, Theorem 1]
(or
[
Sch11
, Proposition 17.6]
). It becomes full after the localisation by
[

, II, 5, §7, Theorem 1]

The last sentence follows from
[
Ser06
, II, 5, §7, Corollary 1.2]
. For an alternative proof of this statement, see also
[
Far19
, §1, Lemme 1,2]
.

Remark 3.15

The functor sending a geometric

Proof.

4
Reduction of structure group to open subgroups

In the past section, we have seen using the exponential that any rigid group
G
has a neighbourhood basis of open subgroups. In this section, we study when
G
-torsors in the
v
-topology admit a reduction of structure group to open subgroups. We then deduce the Main Theorem.

4.1
Approximation for quotient sheaves

The key technical result of this section is a generalisation of the approximation property
2.11
for
\mathrm{GL}_{n}(\mathcal{O}^{+}/\varpi)
to all rigid groups and all rigid open subgroups:

Proposition 4.1
.

Let
G
be a rigid group and let
U\subseteq G
be any rigid open subgroup (not necessarily normal). Then the sheaf of cosets
G/U
on
\mathrm{Sous}_{K,{\operatorname{\acute{e}t}}}
satisfies the approximation property of

: For any affinoid perfectoid tilde limit

, we have

G/U(X)=\varinjlim_{i\in I}G/U(X_{i}).

In particular,
G/U
is already a
v
-sheaf on
\mathrm{Sous}_{K,{\operatorname{\acute{e}t}}}
.

Proof.

We consider the natural map

\varinjlim_{i\in I}G(X_{i})/U(X_{i})\to G(X)/U(X).

Using
2.8
, it makes sense to sheafify this map:

Claim 4.2
.

The map is injective after sheafification on
X_{{\operatorname{\acute{e}t,qcqs}}}=2\text{-}\varinjlim_{i\in I}X_{i,{\operatorname{\acute{e}t,qcqs}}}
.

Proof.

If
g_{1},g_{2}\in G(X_{i})
have the same image, then
\delta=g_{1}^{-1}\cdot g_{2}\in G(X_{i})
lands in
U(X)\subseteq G(X)
. To deduce that
\delta
lands in
U(X_{j})
for some
j\geq i
, we no longer need the group structure on
G
and use the following argument that we learnt from
[
Sch12
,
Lemma 6.13.(iv)]
:

Claim 4.3
.

Let
H
be an affinoid adic space over
K
and
V\subseteq H
a rational subspace. Let
X_{i}\to H
be a morphism such that
X\to X_{i}\to H
factors through
V
. Then
X_{j}\to X_{i}\to H
factors through
V
for
j\gg i
.

Proof.

For
j\geq i
, let
W_{j}
be the pullback of
V
along
X_{j}\to H
, then
W_{j}\subseteq X_{j}
is still rational open, hence affinoid. The assumptions imply that
X\to X_{j}
factors through
W_{j}
and induce a homeomorphism
|X|\to\varprojlim_{j\geq i}|W_{j}|
. We now use that
(W_{j})_{j\geq i}
is an inverse system of affinoid adic spaces, hence
(|W_{j}|)_{j\geq i}
is an inverse system of spectral spaces with spectral transition maps. Since
\varprojlim_{j\geq i}|X_{j}|\backslash|W_{j}|
is empty, it follows from
[
dJ+22
, Tag 0A2W]
that
X_{j}\backslash W_{j}
is empty for some
j\geq i
. Hence
X_{j}=W_{j}
and thus
X_{j}\to H
factors through
V
.
∎

We apply the claim locally on
X
: Let
H
be any affinoid open subspace of
G
and cover
U\cap H
by rational opens
V
. As
X_{i}
is quasi-compact, finitely many such opens cover the image of
X_{i}\to U
. Replacing
X
and the
X_{i}
by the respective pullback of

, we may assume that
\delta
lies in
V(X)
. The claim shows that already
\delta\in U(X_{j})
for some
j\geq i
.
∎

It thus remains to prove that the map is surjective. For this we again localise on
G
: Let
\operatorname{Spa}(A,A^{+})=V\subseteq G
be any affinoid open such that
(A,A^{+})
is of topologically finite type over
(K,K^{+})
. By
[
Hub94
, Lemma 3.3.(ii)]
, this means that there is a subring of definition
A_{0}\subseteq A^{+}
of topologically finite type (hence automatically of topologically finite presentation) over
K^{+}
such that
A^{+}
is the integral closure of
A_{0}
in
A
.
Write
X=\operatorname{Spa}(R,R^{+})
and
X_{i}=\operatorname{Spa}(R_{i},R^{+}_{i})
, then any point in
G(X_{\infty})
that factors through

defines a map

. By
[
Heu21a
, Lemma 3.11]
, the assumption

implies
R^{+}/p^{n}=\varinjlim R^{+}_{i}/p^{n}
for all

. Since

is of finite presentation over
K^{+}/p
, the reduction of
f
mod
p^{n}
factors through a morphism

A_{0}/p^{n}\to R_{i}^{+}/p^{n}.

for some
i\in I
. We now use:

Lemma 4.4
.

Let
A
be a
p
-adically complete flat
K^{+}
-algebra such that

is a smooth affinoid
K
-algebra. Let us assume for simplicity that
\Omega^{1}_{A[\frac{1}{p}]|K}
is finite free. Then there is

such that for any
p
-adically complete
K^{+}
-algebra

and any homomorphism
of
K^{+}
-algebras

for some

, there is a homomorphism of
K^{+}
-algebras

with

Proof.

This is a statement about torsion in the cotangent complex: By
[

, there is for any

in

and any morphism

Claim 4.5
.

There is

independent of

such that
\operatorname{Ext}_{n,s}^{1}
is
p^{t}
-torsion.

Proof.

We first note that the inclusion

has bounded
p
-torsion cokernel. Similarly for
K^{+}\to\mathcal{O}_{K}:=K^{\circ}
. We may therefore without loss of generality replace

by a ring of integral elements that is of topologically finite presentation over

and

by

.
In this setting, we can use the analytic cotangent complex for formal schemes and rigid spaces introduced by Gabber–Ramero
[
GR03
, §7]
: This is a pseudo-coherent complex

of

L^{{\mathrm{an}}}_{A|\mathcal{O}_{K}}\otimes^{\mathbb{L}}_{A}A/p^{k}=L_{A|\mathcal{O}_{K}}\otimes^{\mathbb{L}}_{A}A/p^{k}

for all

. Moreover,

L^{{\mathrm{an}}}_{A|\mathcal{O}_{K}}[\tfrac{1}{p}]=L^{{\mathrm{an}}}_{A[\frac{1}{p}]|K}=\Omega_{A[\frac{1}{p}]|K}

is smooth.

\Omega^{+}
be any finite free

that contains the image of

is killed by

for some

We now first apply
\otimes^{\mathbb{L}}_{A}R/p^{n}
and then
\operatorname{Ext}^{1}_{R/p^{s}}(-,R/p^{s})
to the distinguished triangle

and obtain an exact sequence

\operatorname{Ext}^{1}(C\otimes^{\mathbb{L}}_{A}R/p^{k},R/p^{s})\to\operatorname{Ext}^{1}_{n,s}\to\operatorname{Ext}^{1}(\Omega^{+}\otimes_{A}R/p^{k},R/p^{s}).

The last term vanishes because

is a finite free

The first term is

-torsion because the second argument of

∎

Let now

be such that

. It is clear from short exact sequences that

to

. By the claim, this morphism is zero. It follows that we may lift the reduction

to a morphism

to the complete
\mathcal{O}_{K}
-algebra

.
∎

Since we may after localising assume that
\Omega_{A|K}
is finite free, the Lemma applies and setting
k=n-t
, we see that there is a sequence of indices
(i_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
and morphisms
f_{k}:A_{0}\to R^{+}_{i_{k}}
such that

. Since the ring extension

is integral with bounded cokernel, it is clear that

extends to
f_{k}:A^{+}\to R^{+}_{i_{k}}
, and that after re-indexing we may still assume that

. After inverting

now define a sequence of points in
G(X_{i_{k}})
. Let
x_{k}\in G(X)
be the images of these points under
G(X_{i_{k}})\to G(X)
.

Claim 4.6
.

For
k\gg 0
, the difference
\delta_{k}:=x^{-1}x_{k}
lies in
U(X)
.

This will prove that

\varinjlim_{i\in I}G(X_{i})\to G(X)/U(X)

is surjective, which then finishes the proof of the approximation property. That

is a

-sheaf then follows from

.

Proof.

Let
Z_{k}\subseteq X
be the preimage of
U\subseteq G
under the map

\delta_{k}:X\xrightarrow{\delta_{k}}G
.
It is clear that
Z_{k}
is open. It therefore remains to prove that each

is contained in
Z_{k}
for some
k
. From this the claim follows by compactness of
X
.

Let
z\in X
be any point, and let
\operatorname{Spa}(C,C^{+})\to X
be any morphism where
(C,C^{+})
is a perfectoid field such that
z
is in the image. The points
x
and
x_{k}
both define maps

\operatorname{Spa}(C,C^{+})\to X\to V

that correspond to two homomorphisms of
K^{+}
-algebras

A^{+}\to R^{+}\to C^{+}.

By construction, these agree modulo
p^{k}
.
We now consider the affinoid rigid space
V_{C}:=V\times_{\operatorname{Spa}(K)}\operatorname{Spa}(C)
over
\operatorname{Spa}(C)
and the topological space
V_{C}(C)
. Let
W_{k}\subseteq V_{C}(C)
be the subspace of points whose associated morphisms

agree with

mod
p^{k}
.

Claim 4.7
.

Any open neighbourhood of
x\in V_{C}(C)
contains one of the
W_{k}
.

Proof.

We reduce to the case of

, where the statement is clear: For this reduction, embed

into some

-torsion cokernel, then also the compositions

agree mod

for any two points in

. The claim follows since

∎

For the base-change

of

to

This finishes the proof of

∎

Proposition 4.8
.

Let
G
be a rigid group over
K
and let

be a rigid open subgroup. Let

be a sousperfectoid adic space over

and let
\nu:X_{v}\to X_{{\operatorname{\acute{e}t}}}

is surjective. In other words, any

on

admits a reduction of structure group to

, i.e. there is an étale cover

and a

as

-torsors. If

is commutative, then we more generally have

R^{k}\nu_{\ast}U=R^{k}\nu_{\ast}G

for all

, so in this case

is uniquely determined up to isomorphism.

We first observe that if

is normal, then we can consider the short exact sequence of pointed sets

R^{1}\nu_{\ast}U\to R^{1}\nu_{\ast}G\to R^{1}\nu_{\ast}(G/U)

and the result follows from the fact that by

, we may apply

and conclude that

In general,

is only a sheaf of cosets, but we can still make sense of the change-of-fibre

as a fibre bundle on

with structure group

.

\overline{E}
is étale-locally trivial, i.e.
there is an étale cover

\overline{E}\times_{X}Y\xrightarrow{\raisebox{-2.95564pt}{\smash{$\sim$}}}G/U\times Y

We may without loss of generality replace

We first deal with the case that

such that

can be described on any

as the equaliser

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\overline{E}(Z)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces G/U(Y\times_{X}Z)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces G/U(Y\times_{X}Y\times_{X}Z).}

\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\pi_{2}^{\ast}}

be the quasi-pro-étale cover
Y\to X
by a strictly totally disconnected space from
2.18
, i.e.
Y\approx\varprojlim Y_{i}
is a cofiltered tilde-limit of étale surjective maps
Y_{i}\to Y
such that
Y\to Y_{i}
is a
v
-cover. By the first part, there is an isomorphism
\alpha:G/U\times Y\xrightarrow{\raisebox{-2.79033pt}{\smash{$\sim$}}}\overline{E}\times_{X}Y
. Again by the approximation property, we have

\overline{E}(Y)=\varinjlim\overline{E}(Y_{i})
,
and by the same approximation argument as in the strictly totally disconnected case, we obtain from this a
U
-equivariant map
Y_{i}\to\overline{E}
. By the claim, this gives a trivialisation.
∎

Lemma 4.11
.

Assume that there is a
G
-equivariant isomorphism
\alpha:G/U\times X\to\overline{E}
on
X_{v}
, then
E
admits a reduction of structure group to
U
.

Proof.

This is a standard argument about fibre bundles: Let
s:=\alpha(1,{\operatorname{id}}):X\to\overline{E}
be the associated unit section.
We define
F\subseteq E
to be the subsheaf given by the fibre product

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces F}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces E}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces X}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\overline{E}.}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces s}

Since
s
is fixed by the
U
-action, the
G
-action on
E
restricts to a
U
-action on
F
.
Let now
Y\to X
be a
v
-cover by a strictly totally disconnected space on which
E
becomes trivial.
Then the pullback of the whole diagram along
Y\to X
is isomorphic to

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces F\times_{X}Y}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces G\times Y}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces Y}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces G/U\times Y.}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces s}

Here we use that
Y
is strictly totally disconnected, so
G/U(Y)=G(Y)/U(Y)
, which implies that we may take the right map to be the projection
G\to G/U
. It follows that we have a
U
-equivariant isomorphism

F\times_{X}Y\xrightarrow{\raisebox{-2.79033pt}{\smash{$\sim$}}}U\times Y,

as the right hand side is also the fibre product. Thus
F
is a
v
-topological
U
-torsor on
Y
.
∎

This finishes the proof of
4.8

∎

4.2
p
-adic integral subgroups for rigid groups of good reduction

In this subsection, we take a closer look at the structure of rigid open subgroups in the special case that
G
has good reduction.
Throughout this subsection we fix a rigid group
G
of good reduction as well as a smooth formal model
\mathcal{G}
of
G
over
\operatorname{Spf}(K^{+})
. The Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}^{+}
of
\mathcal{G}
is a finite projective
K^{+}
-module, hence free, and we can consider it as a canonical open
K^{+}
-lattice
\mathfrak{g}^{+}\subseteq\mathfrak{g}
. As before, we also consider these as
v
-sheaves on
K_{v}
and write explicitly
\mathfrak{g}^{+}(K)
and
\mathfrak{g}(K)
for the underlying modules.

Lemma 4.12
.

The
v
-sheaf
G
on
\operatorname{Perf}_{K}
is the analytic sheafification of the functor

(R,R^{+})\mapsto\mathcal{G}(R^{+})

Proof.

For any formal affine open
\operatorname{Spf}A\subseteq\mathcal{G}
the adic generic fibre is by definition given by
(\operatorname{Spf}A)^{\mathrm{ad}}_{\eta}=\operatorname{Spa}(A[\tfrac{1}{p}],A^{+})
where
A^{+}
is the integral closure of
A
in
A[\tfrac{1}{p}]
. Hence the points
G(R,R^{+})
correspond to the homomorphisms of
K^{+}
-algebras
A\to R^{+}
.
∎

We can use this to give a natural generalisation of the sheaves
\mathrm{GL}_{n}(\mathcal{O}^{+}/p^{k}\mathfrak{m})
:

Definition 4.13
.

For any
0\leq k\in\log|K|
, consider the sheaf
\overline{G}_{k}
on the big étale site of sousperfectoid spaces
\mathrm{Sous}_{K,{\operatorname{\acute{e}t}}}
defined by étale sheafification of the presheaf
\mathrm{Sous}_{K,{\operatorname{\acute{e}t}}}

Y\mapsto\mathcal{G}(\mathcal{O}^{+}/p^{k}\mathfrak{m}(Y)).

We denote by
G_{k}\subseteq G
the kernel of the morphism
G\to\overline{G}_{k}
.

For
k=0
, the restriction to
\operatorname{Perf}_{K}
of
\overline{G}_{k}
is the sheaf
\overline{G}^{\diamondsuit}
studied in
[
Heu21b
, §5]
.

Lemma 4.14
.

The following sequence on
\mathrm{Sous}_{K,{\operatorname{\acute{e}t}}}
is short exact:

0\to G_{k}\to G\to\overline{G}_{k}\to 0.

Proof.

Left-exactness is clear by definition. It is right exact because
\mathcal{G}
is formally smooth over
K^{+}
, so for any
k>0
and any affinoid
(S,S^{+})
in
\mathrm{Sous}_{K,{\operatorname{\acute{e}t}}}
, the map
\mathcal{G}(S^{+})\to\mathcal{G}(S^{+}/p^{k}\mathfrak{m})
is surjective. For
k=0
, we observe that after passing to an analytic cover, any
x\in\mathcal{G}(S^{+}/\mathfrak{m})
lifts to
\mathcal{G}(S^{+}/p^{\epsilon})
for some
\epsilon>0
as
\mathcal{G}
is locally of topologically finite presentation.
∎

We can describe
G_{k}
in more classical terms as follows: Let
\widehat{\mathcal{G}}
be the completion of
\mathcal{G}
at the origin. This is a formal Lie group: Any local choice of generators of the sheaf of ideals defining the unit section in
G
induces an isomorphism of formal schemes

\widehat{\mathcal{G}}\cong\operatorname{Spf}(K^{+}[[T_{1},\dots,T_{d}]]),

see
[
Mes72
, p25-26]
. In particular, on global sections,
\widehat{\mathcal{G}}
defines a formal group law over
K^{+}
.

Lemma 4.15
.

1.

G_{0}
is represented by the adic generic fibre
\widehat{\mathcal{G}}^{\mathrm{ad}}_{\eta}
of
\widehat{\mathcal{G}}
.

2.

The natural morphism
G_{k}\to G_{0}
is isomorphic to the adic generic fibre of the morphism of affine formal schemes defined on global sections by

K^{+}[[T_{1},\dots,T_{d}]]\to K^{+}[[T^{\prime}_{1},\dots,T^{\prime}_{d}]],\quad T_{i}\mapsto p^{k}T^{\prime}_{i}.

In particular,
G_{k}\subseteq G
is represented by a normal rigid open subgroup whose underlying rigid space is isomorphic to an open ball.

Proof.

Let
\operatorname{Spf}(A)\subseteq\mathcal{G}
be any open neighbourhood of the unit section, and let
I\subseteq A
be the ideal of the unit section. Then the functor
\widehat{\mathcal{G}}^{\mathrm{ad}}_{\eta}
is the sheafification of the functor that sends
(R,R^{+})
to the continuous homomorphisms
\varphi:A\to\varprojlim A/I^{n}=K^{+}[[T_{1},\dots,T_{d}]]\to R^{+}
. By continuity, the images of the
T_{i}
land in the topologically nilpotent elements
\mathfrak{m}R^{+}
. Thus
\varphi
reduces mod
\mathfrak{m}
to the unit section
A\to K^{+}/\mathfrak{m}\to R^{+}/\mathfrak{m}
. Conversely, if
A\to R^{+}
factors mod
\mathfrak{m}
through the unit section, it already does so mod
p^{\epsilon}
for some
\epsilon>0
since
A
is of topologically finite presentation. Thus for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
, the map
A\to R^{+}\to R^{+}/p^{n}
factors through
A/I^{m}
for some
m
, and in the limit we get the desired map.

For part 2, we similarly observe that if a map
A\to R^{+}
factors through
K^{+}[[T^{\prime}_{1},\dots,T^{\prime}_{d}]]
, then it factors through the unit section after reducing mod
p^{k}\mathfrak{m}
. Conversely, if it factors through the unit section mod
p^{k}\mathfrak{m}
, then the induced morphism
K^{+}[[T_{1},\dots,T_{d}]]\to R^{+}
from the first part must send
T_{i}
into
p^{k}\mathfrak{m}R^{+}
and thus factors through the displayed map.

That
G_{k}
is normal can be proved on the level of sheaves, where it follows from the short exact sequence in
4.14
.
∎

Combined with our work in the previous sections, this shows:

Proposition 4.16
.

The sheaf
\overline{G}_{k}
on
\mathrm{Sous}_{K,{\operatorname{\acute{e}t}}}
is already a
v
-sheaf. Moreover,

R^{1}\nu_{\ast}\overline{G}_{k}=1.

If
G
is commutative, we more generally have
R\nu_{\ast}\overline{G}_{k}=\overline{G}_{k}
.

Proof.

By
4.14
and
4.15
, we know that
\overline{G}_{k}=G/G_{k}
is the quotient of the rigid group
G
by the normal open subgroup
G_{k}
. By
4.1
, it follows that
\overline{G}_{k}
is a
v
-sheaf with the desired properties.
∎

The system
(G_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
forms a neighbourhood basis of open subgroups and gives us a notion of completeness of
G
, generalising the isomorphism
\mathrm{GL}_{n}(\mathcal{O}^{+})=\varprojlim_{k}\mathrm{GL}_{n}(\mathcal{O}^{+}/p^{k}\mathfrak{m})
:

Lemma 4.17
.

The natural map
G\to\varprojlim_{k\in\mathbb{N}}\overline{G}_{k}

is an isomorphism of
v
-sheaves.

Proof.

It is clear from
4.15
that
\varprojlim_{k\in\mathbb{N}}G_{k}=\cap_{k\in\mathbb{N}}G_{k}=1
, so the map is injective. To see that it is surjective, suppose we are given a compatible system of elements
x_{k}\in G/G_{k}(R,R^{+})
for some perfectoid
(R,R^{+})
. Using that the
v
-site is replete, we can inductively find a
v
-cover
(R,R^{+})\to(S,S^{+})
such that the
x_{k}
are in the image of
\mathcal{G}(S^{+}/p^{k}\mathfrak{m})\to G/G_{k}(S,S^{+})
and form a compatible system of elements in
\mathcal{G}(S^{+}/p^{k}\mathfrak{m})
. On the level of formal schemes, this defines an element
x\in\mathcal{G}(S^{+})
whose image in
G(S,S^{+})
is a preimage of
(x_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
.
∎

Remark 4.18
.

Due to the Lemma, one could now generalise Faltings’ notion of generalised representations by defining “generalised
G
-representations” to be compatible systems of
\overline{G}_{k}
-torsors on the étale site. Due to
4.16
and
4.17

-torsors on

We can now identify the subgroups that appeared in the context of the exponential with the integral subgroups

of this section in the context of good reduction:

Definition 4.19
.

For any
0\leq k\in\log|K|
,
we denote by
\mathfrak{g}_{k}^{+}\subseteq\mathfrak{g}
the subsheaf
p^{k}\mathfrak{m}\mathfrak{g}^{+}
, represented by an open rigid subgroup of

\overline{\mathfrak{g}}^{+}_{k}:=\mathfrak{g}^{+}/\mathfrak{g}^{+}_{k}
on

Lemma 4.20
.

There is
\alpha\geq 1/(p-1)
such that for any
\alpha<k\in\log|K|
, the exponential of
G
is defined on
\mathfrak{g}^{+}_{k}
and restricts to an isomorphism of rigid spaces

\exp:\mathfrak{g}^{+}_{k}\xrightarrow{\raisebox{-2.95564pt}{\smash{$\sim$}}}G_{k}.

Proof.

As the exponential
\mathfrak{g}^{\circ}\to G
induces the identity on Lie algebras, the associated morphism of formal Lie groups over

is of the form
F:K[[T_{1},\dots,T_{n}]]\to K[[T_{1},\dots,T_{n}]]
with
F(T_{i})=T_{i}+(\text{terms of higher degree})
. As

converges on some disc centred at

by

on both sides via

4.15
.2 that the left hand side becomes

and the right hand side becomes

.
∎

Definition 4.21
.

Let us denote by
\alpha
the infimum of all

for which
4.20
holds. As before, we denote by
\log

\exp
.

Lemma 4.22
.

For any
k>\alpha

preserves
\mathfrak{g}_{k}^{+}
.

Proof.

This follows from
4.20
and
3.10
.
∎

Lemma 4.23
.

For any
\alpha<r<s\in\mathbb{Q}
with
s\leq 2r-\alpha_{0}\in\mathbb{Q}
, the exponential induces an isomorphism of abelian sheaves on
\mathrm{Sous}_{K,{\operatorname{\acute{e}t}}}

\exp:\mathfrak{g}_{r}^{+}/\mathfrak{g}_{s}^{+}\xrightarrow{\raisebox{-2.95564pt}{\smash{$\sim$}}}G_{r}/G_{s}.

In fact, we already have an isomorphism
\exp:\mathfrak{g}_{r}^{+}(X)/\mathfrak{g}_{s}^{+}(X)\xrightarrow{\raisebox{-2.95564pt}{\smash{$\sim$}}}G_{r}(X)/G_{s}(X)
for any
X\in\mathrm{Sous}_{K}
. We thus get a short exact sequence on
\mathrm{Sous}_{K,{\operatorname{\acute{e}t}}}

0\to\overline{\mathfrak{g}}^{+}_{s-r}\xrightarrow{\exp}\overline{G}_{s}\to\overline{G}_{r}\to 1.

Remark 4.24
.

For
G=\mathrm{GL}_{n}
, this is the natural isomorphism

M_{n}(p^{r}\mathfrak{m}\mathcal{O}^{+}/p^{s}\mathfrak{m})\xrightarrow{\raisebox{-2.79033pt}{\smash{$\sim$}}}1+p^{r}\mathfrak{m}M_{n}(\mathcal{O}^{+})/1+p^{s}\mathfrak{m}M_{n}(\mathcal{O}^{+}),\quad x\mapsto 1+x

which coincides with the exponential because the conditions on

and

ensure that

x^{2}/2!+x^{3}/3!+\dots\in p^{s}\mathcal{O}^{+}\text{ for any }x\in p^{r}\mathcal{O}^{+}

by the usual estimate
v_{p}(x^{n}/n!)>nv_{p}(x)-\tfrac{n-1}{p-1}
. Similarly for linear algebraic groups.

Proof.

By the commutative diagram in
3.5
,
\mathrm{BCH}
defines on
\mathfrak{g}^{+}_{r}
a rigid group structure isomorphic to
G_{r}
. It suffice to prove that this agrees with the additive group structure on the quotient
\mathfrak{g}^{+}_{r}/\mathfrak{g}^{+}_{s}
. This is guaranteed by the following Lemma:∎

Lemma 4.25
.

For any
r>\alpha
, let
x,y\in\mathfrak{g}^{+}_{r}
. Then
\mathrm{BCH}(x,y)
converges in
\mathfrak{g}^{+}_{r}
and

\mathrm{BCH}(x,y)\equiv x+y\bmod\mathfrak{g}^{+}_{s}

for any
r<s<2r-\alpha_{0}
.

Proof.

This follows from the estimates in
[
Sch11
, (26) and Proposition 17.6]
: We have
\mathrm{BCH}=\sum_{n\geq 1}H_{n}
where
H_{n}(X,Y)
is homogeneous of degree

, and

\|H_{n}\|\leq|p|^{-(n-1)\alpha_{0}}
.
It follows that for

, we have

v_{p}(H_{n}(x,y))>nr-(n-1)\alpha_{0}=n(r-\alpha_{0})+\alpha_{0}\geq 2r-\alpha_{0}\geq s

where we define the valuation
v_{p}
on
\mathfrak{g}
with respect to
\mathfrak{g}^{+}
. Thus

\mathrm{BCH}(x,y)=x+y+H_{2}(x,y)+\dots\in x+y+p^{s}\mathfrak{m}\mathfrak{g}^{+}

is of the desired form.
∎

4.3
G
-torsors on perfectoid spaces

Given our technical preparations, we can now generalise the results on
Section

2
. First,
by a generalisation of
2.24
, small
G
-torsors on affinoid perfectoid
Z
are trivial:

Lemma 4.26
.

Let
X
be an affinoid perfectoid space, then a
G
-torsor
V
on
X_{v}
is trivial if and only if the associated
\overline{G}_{k}
-torsor
V_{k}
on
X_{v}
is trivial for some
k>\alpha
. In particular,

H^{1}_{v}(X,G_{k})=1.

Proof.

We apply
H^{1}_{v}(X,-)
to the short exact sequence of
v
-sheaves in Lemma
4.23
. Since
H^{1}_{v}(X,\overline{\mathfrak{g}}_{d-k}^{+})=0
, this shows inductively that if
V_{k}
is trivial then so is
V_{d}
for any
d>k
.

We then use that we have a short exact sequence of pointed sets

0\to R^{1}\varprojlim_{k}\overline{G}_{k}(X)\to H^{1}_{v}(X,G)\to\varprojlim_{k}H^{1}_{v}(X,\overline{G}_{k}).

It thus suffices to prove that
(\overline{G}_{k}(X))_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
is a Mittag-Leffler system. This follows again from Lemma
4.23
and
H^{1}_{v}(X,\overline{\mathfrak{g}}_{s-r})=0
, which shows inductively that any element in
\overline{G}_{k}(X)
can be lifted to
\overline{G}_{d}(X)
for any
d>k
.

In particular, by
4.17
this also shows that the map
G(X)\to\overline{G}_{k}(X)
is surjective.
The last sentence then follows from the long exact sequence of
H^{0}_{v}(X,-)
on
4.14
.
∎

We can now prove the main theorem of this article, generalising Kedlaya–Liu’s
2.20
and

2.21
, which were the cases of
G=\mathrm{GL}_{n}
and
G=\mathrm{GL}_{n}(\mathcal{O}^{+})
:

Theorem 4.27
.

Let
X
be a perfectoid space and let
G
be any rigid group over
K
. Then the categories of
G
-torsors on
X_{{\operatorname{\acute{e}t}}}
and
G
-torsors on
X_{v}
are equivalent.

Proof.

By
3.8
, there exists a rigid open subgroup
U\subseteq G
which has good reduction. By
4.8
, the map

R^{1}\nu_{\ast}U\to R^{1}\nu_{\ast}G

is surjective, so it suffices to prove that
R^{1}\nu_{\ast}U=1
.
We have thus reduced to the case that
G
has good reduction. By the same argument, we may then further replace
G
by the open subgroup
G_{k}
for
k>\alpha
. Then by
4.26
, we have
R^{1}\nu_{\ast}G_{k}=1
.
∎

Corollary 4.28
.

Let
X
be any adic space over
K
. Then the categories of
G
-torsors on
X_{{\operatorname{qpro\acute{e}t}}}
and
X_{v}
are equivalent. Moreover, any geometric
G
-torsor on
X_{v}
is a diamond.

Proof.

The first part follows since by
2.18
, the space
X
admits a quasi-pro-étale cover by a perfectoid space. For the second part,
let
E
be a geometric
G
-torsor on
X_{v}
. The statement is étale-local on
X
, so we may assume that
X
has a pro-finite-étale perfectoid Galois cover

such that
E\times_{X}X^{\prime}\cong G\times X^{\prime}
. Then the projection
G\times X^{\prime}\to E
is a pro-finite-étale cover of

by a sousperfectoid space. Hence

is a diamond.
∎

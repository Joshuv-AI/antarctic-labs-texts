Revisiting Semiring Provenance for Datalog
Camille Bourgaux, Pierre Bourhis, Liat Peterfreund, Michaël Thomazo

Abstract
Data provenance consists in bookkeeping meta information during query evaluation, in order to enrich query results with their trust level, likelihood, evaluation cost, and more. The framework of semiring provenance abstracts from the specific kind of meta information that annotates the data. While the definition of semiring provenance is uncontroversial for unions of conjunctive queries, the picture is less clear for Datalog. Indeed, the original definition might include infinite computations, and is not consistent with other proposals for Datalog semantics over annotated data. In this work, we propose and investigate several provenance semantics, based on different approaches for defining classical Datalog semantics. We study the relationship between these semantics, and introduce properties that allow us to analyze and compare them.



1
Introduction

Datalog is a rule language widely studied both in the database community, where it is seen as a query language, and in the KR community, as an ontology language.

In relational databases, the framework of
semiring provenance
was introduced to generalize computations over annotated databases, e.g., the semantics of probabilistic databases
[
2017
]
, the bag semantics, lineage or why-provenance
[
2009
]
.
In this framework, the semantics of positive relational algebra queries over databases annotated with elements of any commutative semiring is inductively defined on the structure of the query
[
2007
,
2017
]
.

Provenance semirings
are expressions (such as polynomials) built from variables associated to each tuple of the database
[
2009
]
. A provenance expression provides a general representation of how tuples have been used to derive a query result, and can be faithfully evaluated in any semiring in which the considered provenance semiring can be homomorphically embedded.

Semiring provenance has also been studied for Datalog queries, for which it was defined based on the set of all
derivation trees
for the query
[
2007
,
2014
,
2018
]
.
However, this definition
seems less axiomatic than in the case of relational databases.
Indeed, there may be infinitely many derivation trees, leading to infinite provenance expressions, while Datalog programs have finite models that can be computed efficiently
[
1995
]
.
A consequence is that this definition is valid only for a restricted class of semirings, namely
\omega
-continuous.
Recently,
?
(
?
) restrict the semiring even further by considering fully-chain complete semirings in order to extend provenance definition to logical languages featuring negation and fixed-point.
Even if numerous useful semirings are
\omega
-continuous, or can be extended to a such semiring, infinite provenance expressions may be considered unintuitive in some cases. Consider, for example, the counting semiring (i.e., natural numbers with standard operations) for which provenance of positive relational algebra queries corresponds to their bag semantics. This semiring can be extended to an
\omega
-continuous one by adding
\infty
to the natural numbers, hence providing a way to capture the bag semantics for Datalog queries
[
1990
,
2007
]
. However, query answers having infinite multiplicities may not seem very natural or informative.
Moreover, alternative bag semantics for languages close to Datalog have been defined, and would not lead to such infinite multiplicities when applied to Datalog. This is in particular the case of the bag semantics for ontology-based data access
[
2017
,
2019
]
, which corresponds to one of the two semantics proposed for source-to-target tuple generating dependencies in the context of data exchange
[
2017
]
.
Interestingly, these bag semantics are not based on derivation trees but are
model-theoretic
semantics: they define annotated interpretations, and conditions for rules satisfaction over such interpretations.
Such model-theoretic semantics have also been used in other contexts to evaluate Datalog and variants over annotated databases, such as fuzzy Datalog
[
1995
]
or description logic knowledge bases annotated with provenance tokens
[
2019
,
2020
]
. Finally, yet other semantics definitions have been proposed for some use cases. For instance,

?
(
?
) consider
minimal depth proof trees
, which correspond to a Datalog
evaluation algorithm
, with the intended use of understanding the computation of the result, and guiding debugging.

The fact that the above semantics are not encompassed by the definition of semiring provenance for Datalog, along with the need of handling infinite computations which are entailed by this definition, motivate us to investigate alternative natural semantics that
might be a better fit in different contexts.

In this paper we introduce several natural provenance semantics for Datalog over annotated data.
Our definitions are based on different classical approaches: model-theoretic, execution-based and proof tree-based.
They capture the semantics mentioned previously, and
are inspired by practical needs.
For instance, our semantics definition based on minimal depth derivation trees capture the behavior of Datalog engines, such as Soufflé
[
2020
]
, that store only minimal depth derivation trees instead of storing them all (which might be impossible in case there are infinitely many); Our semantics based on non-recursive derivation trees (in which a fact is not derived from itself) resembles the approach taken by some graph query languages, e.g., SPARQL and Cypher, to handle queries with possibly infinite outputs by allowing to explicitly restrict the output to include only simple paths.
In addition, some of the semantics suggested in the paper are closely related to paradigms for weighted reasoning in the context of words and trees
[
2011
,
2008
]
.

After defining these different semantics,
we study under which conditions they coincide and investigate their connections.
We then provide a general framework for defining such provenance semantics, and present several properties relevant for provenance semantics that allow us to compare them.
We briefly discuss some complexity issues in conclusion.
Proofs and additional discussion are available in the appendix.

Annotated Databases

2.3
Provenance Semirings

Provenance semirings have been introduced to abstract from a particular semiring by associating a unique provenance token to each fact of the database, and building expressions that trace their use.
Given a set

that annotate the database, a

Various such semirings were introduced in the context of relational databases

\mathit{PosBool}(X):=(\mathit{PosBool}(X),\vee,\wedge,\mathsf{false},\mathsf{true})

In the Datalog context, it is important to allow for infinite provenance expressions, as there can be infinitely many derivation trees.

. A formal power series

.

3
Alternative Semantics

In this section we propose several natural ways of defining the semantics of Datalog over annotated databases, and investigate their connections. We have seen that the semantics of Datalog can equivalently be defined through models, fixpoints or derivation trees.
The semantics we propose also fall into these three approaches.
For presentation purposes,
we see each semantics as a partial function
\mathcal{P}
that associates to a Datalog program
\Sigma

(D,\mathbb{K},\lambda)
, and fact

\mathcal{P}(\Sigma,D,\mathbb{K},\lambda,\alpha)
.

and
cognizant
semantics.
The difference between them arise from the two different semantics of bag union: the incognizant semantics uses the maximum-based union, while the cognizant semantics uses the sum-based union.

In more details, both semantics are based on the following semantics for source-to-target tuple generating dependencies (s-t tgds): a pair
(I,J)
of source and target instances satisfies an s-t tgd
q_{1}(\vec{x})\rightarrow q_{2}(\vec{x})
if for every answer
\vec{a}
to
q_{1}
over
I
,
\vec{a}
is an answer to
q_{2}
over
J
with at least the same multiplicity.
Given a set of s-t tgds
\Sigma
and a source
I
, a target
J
is an
incognizant solution
for
I
w.r.t.
\Sigma
if
(I,J)
satisfies every s-t tgd in
\Sigma
.
It is a
cognizant solution
if for every
r\in\Sigma
, there is a target instance
J_{r}
such that
(I,J_{r})
satisfies
r
and
\uplus J_{r}\subseteq J
, where
\uplus
denotes the sum-union of bags (i.e., the multiplicity of each element of the sum-union is equal to the sum of its multiplicities).
The incognizant (resp. cognizant)
certain answers
to a query
q
w.r.t.
\Sigma
on
I
are defined using bag intersection of the answers over the incognizant (resp. cognizant) solutions for
I
w.r.t.
\Sigma
, i.e., the multiplicity of an answer is the minimum of its multiplicities over the solutions. Note that for BCQs, the only possible certain answer is the empty tuple.

For example, consider
\Sigma=\{B(x)\rightarrow A(x),C(x)\rightarrow A(x)\}
and
D=\{(B(a),1),(C(a),1)\}
. Under the incognizant semantics, the multiplicity of the certain answer of the Boolean query
A(a)
w.r.t.
\Sigma
and
D
is
1
while under the cognizant semantics it is
2
.
Indeed,
J=\{(A(a),1)\}
is an incognizant solution for
D
w.r.t.
\Sigma
as it satisfies both s-t tgds, but is not a cognizant solution as the sum of multiplicities that arise from the two rules is
2
.

It is easy to show that the cognizant semantics is equivalent to
\mathcal{P}^{\texttt{AT}}
on the counting semiring

\mathbb{N}=(\mathbb{N},+,\times,0,1)
, and thus coincides with the classical bag semantics for Datalog. However, we have seen that the incognizant and cognizant semantics differ.
Moreover, note that in the field of ontology-based data access, the bag semantics defined by
?
for DL-Lite
R
(
?
;
?
) coincides with the
incognizant
semantics, thus disagrees with the classical Datalog bag semantics
[
1990
,
2007
]
.

We hence define a provenance semantics that coincides with these semantics when used with the counting semiring.
Since it is based on greatest lower bounds, it is defined on a restricted class of semirings.

Let
\mathbb{K}=(K,+,\times,0,1)
be a commutative
\omega
-continuous semiring such that for every
K^{\prime}\subseteq K
, the greatest lower bound
\inf(K^{\prime})
of
K^{\prime}
is well defined (i.e., there exists a unique
z\in K
such that
z\sqsubseteq x
for every
x\in K^{\prime}
and every
z^{\prime}
such that
z^{\prime}\sqsubseteq x
for every
x\in K^{\prime}
is such that
z^{\prime}\sqsubseteq z
),
\Sigma
be a Datalog program, and
(D,\mathbb{K},\lambda)
be an annotated database. We define

\mathbb{K}
-annotated interpretations
as pairs
(I,\mu^{I})
where
I
is a set of facts, and
\mu^{I}
is a function from
I
to
K
.
We say that a

\mathbb{K}
-annotated interpretation

(I,\mu^{I})
is a
model
of
\Sigma
and
(D,\mathbb{K},\lambda)
, denoted by
(I,\mu^{I})\models(\Sigma,D,\mathbb{K},\lambda)
, if

1.

D\subseteq I
, and for every
\alpha\in D
,
\lambda(\alpha)\sqsubseteq\mu^{I}(\alpha)
;

2.

for every
\phi(\vec{x},\vec{y})\rightarrow H(\vec{x})
in
\Sigma
, whenever there is a homomorphism
h:\phi(\vec{x},\vec{y})\mapsto I
,
then
h(H(\vec{x}))\in I
and
\displaystyle\sum_{h^{\prime}:\phi(\vec{x},\vec{y})\mapsto I,h^{\prime}(\vec{x})=h(\vec{x})}\prod_{\beta\in h^{\prime}(\phi(\vec{x},\vec{y}))}\mu^{I}(\beta)\sqsubseteq\mu^{I}(h(H(\vec{x})))
.

The
annotated model-based provenance semantics

\mathcal{P}^{\texttt{AM}}
is defined by

\mathcal{P}^{\texttt{AM}}(\Sigma,D,\mathbb{K},\lambda,\alpha):=\inf(\{\mu^{I}(\alpha){\mid}(I,\mu^{I}){\models}(\Sigma,D,\mathbb{K},\lambda)\}).

Proposition 1
.

If the Datalog rules in
\Sigma
are (1) s-t tgds, or (2) formulated in DL-Lite
R
, then for every BCQ
q
,
\mathcal{P}^{\texttt{AM}}(\Sigma\cup\{q\rightarrow\mathsf{goal}\},D,\mathbb{N},\lambda_{\mathbb{N}},\mathsf{goal})
is equal to the multiplicity of the empty tuple in (1) the incognizant certain answers or (2) the bag certain answers to
q
w.r.t.
\Sigma
and
(D,\mathbb{N},\lambda_{\mathbb{N}})
.

Set-Annotated Model-based

We adapt the work on provenance for the description logics DL-Lite
R
and
\mathcal{ELH}^{r}

[
2019
,
2020
]
, where the semiring is assumed to be a
\times
-idempotent provenance semiring
\mathit{Prov}(X)
and rules are also annotated.
Annotated models of annotated knowledge bases are defined as set of facts annotated with sets of monomials from
\mathit{Prov}(X)
.
Given a fact
\alpha
and a monomial
m
over
X
,
(\Sigma,D,\mathit{Prov}(X),\lambda_{X})\models(\alpha,m)
holds when
m
belongs to the annotation set of
\alpha
in every models of
\Sigma
and
(D,\mathit{Prov}(X),\lambda_{X})
.

To obtain an analog provenance semantics for Datalog, we define interpretations which associate facts with (possibly infinite)
sets of annotations
, and formulate the requirements for them to be models of
\Sigma
and
(D,\mathbb{K},\lambda)
.

Let
\mathbb{K}=(K,+,\times,0,1)
be a commutative
\omega
-continuous semiring,
\Sigma
be a Datalog program, and
(D,\mathbb{K},\lambda)
be an annotated database. We define

\mathbb{K}
-set-annotated interpretations
as pairs
(I,\mu^{I})
where
I
is a set of facts, and
\mu^{I}
is a function from
I

to the
power-set of
K
.
We say that a
\mathbb{K}
-set-annotated interpretation

(I,\mu^{I})
is a model of
\Sigma
and
(D,\mathbb{K},\lambda)
, denoted by
(I,\mu^{I})\models(\Sigma,D,\mathbb{K},\lambda)
, if

1.

D\subseteq I
, and for every
\alpha\in D
,
\lambda(\alpha)\in\mu^{I}(\alpha)
;

2.

for every
\phi(\vec{x},\vec{y})\rightarrow H(\vec{x})
in
\Sigma
, whenever there is a homomorphism
h:\phi(\vec{x},\vec{y})\mapsto I
,
then
h(H(\vec{x}))\in I
and if
h(\phi(\vec{x},\vec{y}))=\beta_{1}\wedge\dots\wedge\beta_{n}
,
\{\Pi_{i=1}^{n}k_{i}\mid(k_{1},\dots,k_{n})\in\mu^{I}(\beta_{1})\times\dots\times\mu^{I}(\beta_{n})\}\subseteq\mu^{I}(h(H(\vec{x})))
.

The
set-annotated model-based provenance semantics

\mathcal{P}^{\texttt{SAM}}
is defined by

\mathcal{P}^{\texttt{SAM}}(\Sigma,D,\mathbb{K},\lambda,\alpha):=\sum_{k\in\bigcap_{(I,\mu^{I})\models(\Sigma,D,\mathbb{K},\lambda)}\mu^{I}(\alpha)}k.

Connections between semantics

Let
\sqsubseteq
be the binary relation between provenance semantics such that
\mathcal{P}\sqsubseteq\mathcal{P}^{\prime}
if and only if
\mathcal{P}(\Sigma,D,\mathbb{K},\lambda,\alpha)\sqsubseteq\mathcal{P}^{\prime}(\Sigma,D,\mathbb{K},\lambda,\alpha)
for every
\Sigma
,
(D,\mathbb{K},\lambda)
and
\alpha
on which
\mathcal{P}
and
\mathcal{P}^{\prime}
are well-defined.

Proposition 2
.

The following holds:

\mathcal{P}^{\texttt{AM}}\sqsubseteq\mathcal{P}^{\texttt{AT}}\text{ and }\mathcal{P}^{\texttt{SAM}}\sqsubseteq\mathcal{P}^{\texttt{AT}}.

Next examples show that
\mathcal{P}^{\texttt{AM}}
and
\mathcal{P}^{\texttt{SAM}}
are incomparable.

Example 6
.

Let
\Sigma=\{A(x)\rightarrow\mathsf{goal},B(x)\rightarrow\mathsf{goal}\}
,
D=\{A(a),B(a)\}
,

\lambda_{\mathbb{N}}(A(a))=2
and
\lambda_{\mathbb{N}}(B(a))=3
.

Annotated models of
\Sigma
and
(D,\mathbb{N},\lambda_{\mathbb{N}})
are such that
\mu^{I}(\mathsf{goal})\geq 3
, so
\mathcal{P}^{\texttt{AM}}(\Sigma,D,\mathbb{N},\lambda_{\mathbb{N}},\mathsf{goal})=3
.

Set-annotated models of
\Sigma
and
(D,\mathbb{N},\lambda_{\mathbb{N}})
are such that
\{2,3\}\subseteq\mu^{I}(\mathsf{goal})
, so
\mathcal{P}^{\texttt{SAM}}(\Sigma,D,\mathbb{N},\lambda_{\mathbb{N}},\mathsf{goal})=5
.

Hence
\mathcal{P}^{\texttt{AM}}(\Sigma,D,\mathbb{N},\lambda_{\mathbb{N}},\mathsf{goal}){<}\mathcal{P}^{\texttt{SAM}}(\Sigma,D,\mathbb{N},\lambda_{\mathbb{N}},\mathsf{goal})
.

Example 7
.

Let
\Sigma=\{R(x,y)\rightarrow\mathsf{goal}\}
,
D=\{R(a,b),R(a,c)\}
,

\lambda_{\mathbb{N}}(R(a,b))=2
and
\lambda_{\mathbb{N}}(R(a,c))=2
.

Annotated models of
\Sigma
and
(D,\mathbb{N},\lambda_{\mathbb{N}})
are such that
\mu^{I}(\mathsf{goal})\geq 4
, so
\mathcal{P}^{\texttt{AM}}(\Sigma,D,\mathbb{N},\lambda_{\mathbb{N}},\mathsf{goal})=4
.

Set-annotated models of
\Sigma
and
(D,\mathbb{N},\lambda_{\mathbb{N}})
are such that

\mathcal{P}^{\texttt{SAM}}(\Sigma,D,\mathbb{N},\lambda_{\mathbb{N}},\mathsf{goal})=2
.

Hence
\mathcal{P}^{\texttt{AM}}(\Sigma,D,\mathbb{N},\lambda_{\mathbb{N}},\mathsf{goal}){>}\mathcal{P}^{\texttt{SAM}}(\Sigma,D,\mathbb{N},\lambda_{\mathbb{N}},\mathsf{goal})
.

Despite of their inherently different approaches,

\mathcal{P}^{\texttt{AM}}
,
\mathcal{P}^{\texttt{SAM}}
and
\mathcal{P}^{\texttt{AT}}
coincide on a large class of semirings.

Proposition 3
.

If
\mathbb{K}
is a commutative
+\,
-idempotent
\omega
-continuous semiring, then for every
\Sigma
,
(D,\mathbb{K},\lambda)
, and
\alpha
,

\mathcal{P}^{\texttt{AM}}(\Sigma,D,\mathbb{K},\lambda,\alpha)=\mathcal{P}^{\texttt{SAM}}(\Sigma,D,\mathbb{K},\lambda,\alpha)=\mathcal{P}^{\texttt{AT}}(\Sigma,D,\mathbb{K},\lambda,\alpha).

Additional insights on the connection between definitions can be gained by considering the provenance semiring
\mathbb{N}^{\infty}\llbracket X\rrbracket
: the monomials with non-zero coefficients are the same with all semantics but their coefficients may differ (
\mathcal{P}^{\texttt{AT}}
leading to the highest coefficients by Proposition
2
).

Proposition 4
.

Let
\lambda_{X}
be an injective function from
D
to
X
.

•

A monomial occurs in
\mathcal{P}^{\texttt{AT}}(\Sigma,D,\mathbb{N}^{\infty}\llbracket X\rrbracket,\lambda_{X},\alpha)
if and only if it occurs in
\mathcal{P}^{\texttt{AM}}(\Sigma,D,\mathbb{N}^{\infty}\llbracket X\rrbracket,\lambda_{X},\alpha)
.

•

\mathcal{P}^{\texttt{SAM}}(\Sigma,D,\mathbb{N}^{\infty}\llbracket X\rrbracket,\lambda_{X},\alpha)
is obtained by setting all non-zero coefficients to
1
in
\mathcal{P}^{\texttt{AT}}(\Sigma,D,\mathbb{N}^{\infty}\llbracket X\rrbracket,\lambda_{X},\alpha)
.

An example where
\mathcal{P}^{\texttt{AT}}
and
\mathcal{P}^{\texttt{AM}}
or
\mathcal{P}^{\texttt{SAM}}
differ on

is the following:
Let
\Sigma
contain
A(x)\rightarrow B(x)
,
B(x)\rightarrow A(x)
,
D=\{A(a)\}
and
\lambda_{X}(A(a))=x
.

Since there are infinitely many derivation trees for
A(a)
,
\mathcal{P}^{\texttt{AT}}(\Sigma,D,\mathbb{K},\lambda_{X},A(a))=\infty x
while for
\mathcal{P}\in\{\mathcal{P}^{\texttt{AM}},\mathcal{P}^{\texttt{SAM}}\}
,
\mathcal{P}(\Sigma,D,\mathbb{N}^{\infty}\llbracket X\rrbracket,\lambda_{X},A(a))=x
, as
\{A(a),B(a)\}
with both facts annotated with

(resp.

) is a (resp. set-)annotated model for

and
(D,\mathbb{N}^{\infty}\llbracket X\rrbracket,\lambda_{X})
.

Note that
\mathcal{P}^{\texttt{AM}}
and
\mathcal{P}^{\texttt{SAM}}
can still lead to infinite provenance expressions: Let
\Sigma=\{A(x)\wedge B(x)\rightarrow A(x)\}
,
D=\{A(a),B(a)\}
,

and
\lambda_{X}(B(a))=y

\mathcal{P}\in\{\mathcal{P}^{\texttt{AM}},\mathcal{P}^{\texttt{SAM}}\}

.

3.2
Execution- and Tree-Based Semantics

We saw that when annotations are present there is more than one way to define a model-based semantics for Datalog and that it differs from the all-tree semantics.
We now investigate definitions based on classical Datalog evaluation algorithms.

We extend the notion of
immediate consequence operator

describing the application of rules onto facts, with the computation of annotation. To this end, we introduce the

annotation aware immediate consequence operator

T_{\Sigma}
.
Applying
T_{\Sigma}
on a set of annotated facts
(I,\mathbb{K},\lambda)
results in
(I_{T_{\Sigma}},\mathbb{K},\lambda_{T_{\Sigma}})

where
I_{T_{\Sigma}}
is the result of applying the immediate consequence operator to
\Sigma
and
I
, and
\lambda_{T_{\Sigma}}
annotates facts in
I_{T_{\Sigma}}
with the relational provenance (over
(I,\mathbb{K},\lambda)
) of the UCQ formed by the bodies of the rules that create them.
Formally,

\displaystyle{I_{T_{\Sigma}}}:=\{H(\vec{a})\mid I\models\exists\vec{y}\,\phi(\vec{a},\vec{y})\,,\,\phi(\vec{x},\vec{y})\rightarrow H(\vec{x})\in\Sigma\}

\displaystyle\lambda_{T_{\Sigma}}(H(\vec{a})):=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}h(\vec{x})=\vec{a},\,I\models h(\phi(\vec{x},\vec{y}))\\
\phi(\vec{x},\vec{y})\rightarrow H(\vec{x})\in\Sigma\end{subarray}}\,\,\prod_{\beta\in h(\phi(\vec{x},\vec{y}))}\lambda(\beta)

We define a union operator
for annotated databases (over the same semiring):
(I,\mathbb{K},\lambda)\cup(I^{\prime},\mathbb{K},\lambda^{\prime}):=(I\cup I^{\prime},\mathbb{K},\lambda^{\prime\prime})
where
\lambda^{\prime\prime}(\alpha):=\lambda(\alpha)+\lambda^{\prime}(\alpha)

where we slightly abuse notation by setting
\lambda(\alpha)=0
if
\alpha\notin I
, and
\lambda^{\prime}(\alpha)=0
if
\alpha\notin I^{\prime}
.

Naive Evaluation / All Trees

In the naive evaluation algorithm, all rules are applied in parallel until a fixpoint is reached.
The ‘annotation aware’ version of it is as follows:
We set
I_{\mathsf{n}}^{0}(\Sigma,D,\mathbb{K},\lambda):=(D,\mathbb{K},\lambda)
, and define inductively
I_{\mathsf{n}}^{i+1}(\Sigma,D,\mathbb{K},\lambda):=T_{\Sigma}(I_{\mathsf{n}}^{i}(\Sigma,D,\mathbb{K},\lambda))\cup(D,\mathbb{K},\lambda)
.
Note that the subscript
\mathsf{n}
of
I_{\mathsf{n}}
is an abbreviation for ‘naive’, and the superscript
i
indicates how many times
T_{\Sigma}
was applied.

Let
(I_{\mathsf{n}}^{i},\mathbb{K},\lambda_{\mathsf{n}}^{i})
denote
I_{\mathsf{n}}^{i}(\Sigma,D,\mathbb{K},\lambda)
. We say that
I_{\mathsf{n}}^{i}(\Sigma,D,\mathbb{K},\lambda)

converges
if there is some
k
such that
I_{\mathsf{n}}^{\ell}=I_{\mathsf{n}}^{k}
for every
\ell\geq k
, and
\sup(\lambda_{\mathsf{n}}^{i}(\alpha))
exists for every
\alpha\in I_{\mathsf{n}}^{k}
.

Proposition 5
.

For every
\Sigma,D,\mathbb{K},\lambda
,
if
\mathbb{K}
is
\omega
-continuous then

I_{\mathsf{n}}^{i}(\Sigma,D,\mathbb{K},\lambda)
converges.

In this case, we define
I_{\mathsf{n}}^{\infty}:=I_{\mathsf{n}}^{k}
and
\lambda_{\mathsf{n}}^{\infty}:=\sup_{i\rightarrow\infty}\lambda_{\mathsf{n}}^{i}
.
The
naive execution provenance semantics

\mathcal{P}^{\texttt{NE}}
is defined by

\mathcal{P}^{\texttt{NE}}(\Sigma,D,\mathbb{K},\lambda,\alpha):=\left\{\begin{matrix}\lambda_{\mathsf{n}}^{\infty}(\alpha)&\alpha\in I_{\mathsf{n}}^{\infty}\\
0&\text{otherwise}\end{matrix}\right.

and is equivalent to the all-tree semantics.

Proposition 6
.

It holds that

\mathcal{P}^{\texttt{NE}}=\mathcal{P}^{\texttt{AT}}
.

Optimized Naive Evaluation / Minimal Depth Trees

We consider an optimized version of the naive algorithm
that stops as soon as the desired fact
is derived.
We define the ‘annotation aware’ version of this algorithm by

I_{\mathsf{o,\alpha}}^{0}(\Sigma,D,\mathbb{K},\lambda):=(D,\mathbb{K},\lambda)
, and

I_{\mathsf{o,\alpha}}^{i+1}(\Sigma,D,\mathbb{K},\lambda):=\\
\left\{\begin{matrix}T_{\Sigma}(I_{\mathsf{o,\alpha}}^{i}(\Sigma,D,\mathbb{K},\lambda))\cup(D,\mathbb{K},\lambda)&\alpha\notin I_{\mathsf{o,\alpha}}^{i}\\
I_{\mathsf{o,\alpha}}^{i}(\Sigma,D,\mathbb{K},\lambda)&\text{otherwise}\end{matrix}\right.

where
I_{\mathsf{o,\alpha}}^{i}
is such that
I_{\mathsf{o,\alpha}}^{i}(\Sigma,D,\mathbb{K},\lambda):=(I_{\mathsf{o,\alpha}}^{i},\mathbb{K},\lambda_{\mathsf{o,\alpha}}^{i})
.

Proposition 7
.

For every
\Sigma,D,\mathbb{K},\lambda
,
and
\alpha
such that
\Sigma,D\models\alpha
, there exists
k\geq 0
such that
I_{\mathsf{o,\alpha}}^{k}(\Sigma,D,\mathbb{K},\lambda)=I_{\mathsf{o,\alpha}}^{\ell}(\Sigma,D,\mathbb{K},\lambda)
for every
\ell\geq k
.

With
k
as provided by Proposition
7
, we define the
optimized execution provenance semantics

\mathcal{P}^{\texttt{OE}}
by:

\mathcal{P}^{\texttt{OE}}(\Sigma,D,\mathbb{K},\lambda,\alpha):=\left\{\begin{matrix}\lambda_{\mathsf{o,\alpha}}^{k}(\alpha)&\alpha\in I_{\mathsf{o,\alpha}}^{k}\\
0&\text{otherwise}\end{matrix}\right.

We show that an equivalent tree-based semantics can be obtained
by considering only minimal depth trees for the desired fact.
This approach
has been considered useful, for example to present a ‘small proof’ for debugging
[
2020
]
.
Formally, let
\texttt{depth}(t)
denote the depth of tree
t
. We say that
t\in T^{\Sigma}_{D}(\alpha)
is
of minimal depth
if for every
t^{\prime}\in T^{\Sigma}_{D}(\alpha)
it holds that
\texttt{depth}(t)\leq\texttt{depth}(t^{\prime})
.
The
minimal depth tree provenance semantics

\mathcal{P}^{\texttt{MDT}}
is defined by

\mathcal{P}^{\texttt{MDT}}(\Sigma,D,\mathbb{K},\lambda,\alpha):=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}t\in T^{\Sigma}_{D}(\alpha)\\
\text{ is of minimal depth}\end{subarray}}\Lambda(t)

and is equivalent to the optimized naive execution.

Proposition 8
.

It holds that

\mathcal{P}^{\texttt{OE}}=\mathcal{P}^{\texttt{MDT}}.

Seminaive Evaluation / Hereditary Minimal Depth Trees

In the seminaive evaluation algorithm, facts are derived only once.
We introduce a new consequence operator
\Delta_{\Sigma}
that derives only new facts and is defined as follows:

\Delta_{\Sigma}(I,\mathbb{K},\lambda):=(I_{\Delta_{\Sigma}},\mathbb{K},\lambda_{\Delta_{\Sigma}})
where

T_{\Sigma}(I,\mathbb{K},\lambda):=(I_{T_{\Sigma}},\mathbb{K},\lambda_{T_{\Sigma}})
,

I_{\Delta_{\Sigma}}:=I_{T_{\Sigma}}\setminus I
, and
\lambda_{\Delta_{\Sigma}}
is the restriction of
\lambda_{T_{\Sigma}}
to
I_{\Delta_{\Sigma}}
.
We can now define the annotation aware version of the seminaive evaluation:

I_{\mathsf{sn}}^{0}(\Sigma,D,\mathbb{K},\lambda):=(D,\mathbb{K},\lambda)
and
I_{\mathsf{sn}}^{i+1}(\Sigma,D,\mathbb{K},\lambda):=I_{\mathsf{sn}}^{i}(\Sigma,D,\mathbb{K},\lambda)\cup\Delta_{\Sigma}(I_{\mathsf{sn}}^{i}(\Sigma,D,\mathbb{K},\lambda))
.

Proposition 9
.

For every
\Sigma,D,\mathbb{K},\lambda
, there exists
k\geq 0
such that
I_{\mathsf{sn}}^{k}(\Sigma,D,\mathbb{K},\lambda)=I_{\mathsf{sn}}^{\ell}(\Sigma,D,\mathbb{K},\lambda)
for every
\ell\geq k
.

Note that, unlike in Proposition
5
, we do not require
\mathbb{K}
to be
\omega
-continuous.
With
k
provided by Proposition
9
, the
seminaive execution provenance semantics

\mathcal{P}^{\texttt{SNE}}
is defined by

\mathcal{P}^{\texttt{SNE}}(\Sigma,D,\mathbb{K},\lambda,\alpha):=\left\{\begin{matrix}\lambda_{\mathsf{sn}}^{k}&\alpha\in I_{\mathsf{sn}}^{k}\\
0&\text{otherwise}\end{matrix}\right.

To capture this with the tree-based approach we need to further restrict all subtrees to be of minimal depth.
Formally, a derivation tree
t\in T^{\Sigma}_{D}(\alpha)
is a
hereditary minimal-depth (derivation) tree
if for every node
n
of
t
labeled by
(\beta,r,h)
, the subtree
t_{\beta}
with root
n
is a minimal-depth derivation tree for
\beta
.
The
hereditary minimal depth tree provenance semantics

\mathcal{P}^{\texttt{HMDT}}
is defined by

\mathcal{P}^{\texttt{HMDT}}(\Sigma,D,\mathbb{K},\lambda,\alpha):=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}t\in T^{\Sigma}_{D}(\alpha)\\
\text{ is hereditary minimal-depth}\end{subarray}}\Lambda(t)

and is equivalent to the seminaive execution.

Proposition 10
.

It holds that

\mathcal{P}^{\texttt{SNE}}=\mathcal{P}^{\texttt{HMDT}}.

3.3
Non-Recursive Tree-Based Semantics

Both execution-based semantics
\mathcal{P}^{\texttt{OE}}
and
\mathcal{P}^{\texttt{SNE}}
take into account finite subsets of derivation trees (and hence converge). Is there a more informative tree-based semantics (i.e., one that takes into account a bigger subset of derivation trees) that still converges?
We present such a semantics based on the intuition that deriving a fact from itself is redundant.

Formally, a
non-recursive (derivation) tree
is a derivation tree that does not contain two nodes labeled with the same fact and such that one is the descendant of the other. The
non-recursive tree provenance semantics

\mathcal{P}^{\texttt{NRT}}
is defined by

\mathcal{P}^{\texttt{NRT}}(\Sigma,D,\mathbb{K},\lambda,\alpha):=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}t\in T^{\Sigma}_{D}(\alpha)\\
\text{ is non-recursive}\end{subarray}}\Lambda(t).

Connections between semantics

Next proposition follows from the fact that hereditary minimal-depth trees are of minimal-depth and non recursive. The sets of minimal depth trees and non-recursive trees are incomparable, so that
\mathcal{P}^{\texttt{NRT}}\not\sqsubseteq\mathcal{P}^{\texttt{MDT}}
and
\mathcal{P}^{\texttt{MDT}}\not\sqsubseteq\mathcal{P}^{\texttt{NRT}}
.

Proposition 11
.

The following hold:

\mathcal{P}^{\texttt{HMDT}}\sqsubseteq\mathcal{P}^{\texttt{NRT}}\sqsubseteq\mathcal{P}^{\texttt{AT}}\ \text{ and }\quad\mathcal{P}^{\texttt{HMDT}}\sqsubseteq\mathcal{P}^{\texttt{MDT}}\sqsubseteq\mathcal{P}^{\texttt{AT}}

Moreover
\mathcal{P}^{\texttt{NRT}}
and
\mathcal{P}^{\texttt{AT}}
coincide on specific semirings.

Proposition 12
.

For every
\Sigma,D,\mathbb{K},\lambda
and
\alpha
,
if
\mathbb{K}
is a commutative absorptive
\omega
-continuous semiring, then

\mathcal{P}^{\texttt{NRT}}(\Sigma,D,\mathbb{K},\lambda,\alpha)=\mathcal{P}^{\texttt{AT}}(\Sigma,D,\mathbb{K},\lambda,\alpha)
.

If
\mathbb{K}
is not absorptive, there exists
\Sigma
,
(D,\mathbb{K},\lambda)
and
\alpha
such that
\mathcal{P}^{\texttt{NRT}}(\Sigma,D,\mathbb{K},\lambda,\alpha)\neq\mathcal{P}^{\texttt{AT}}(\Sigma,D,\mathbb{K},\lambda,\alpha)
, even in the case where
\mathbb{K}
is
+\,
-idempotent and
\times
-idempotent: Let
\Sigma
consist of the rule
A(x)\wedge B(x)\rightarrow A(x)
and
D=\{A(a),B(a)\}
. Then
\mathcal{P}^{\texttt{NRT}}(\Sigma,D,\mathbb{K},\lambda,A(a))=\lambda(A(a))
while

4
Basics Properties

In this section, we provide a framework allowing to compare the provenance semantics presented in the previous section. It is clear that they all fulfill the following definition.

Definition 3

(Provenance semantics)
.

A
provenance semantics
is a partial function that assigns to a Datalog program
\Sigma
, annotated database
(D,\mathbb{K},\lambda)

and fact
\alpha
, an element
\mathcal{P}(\Sigma,D,\mathbb{K},\lambda,\alpha)
in
K
such that:

1.

\Sigma,D\not\models\alpha
implies
\mathcal{P}(\Sigma,D,\mathbb{K},\lambda,\alpha)=0_{\mathbb{K}}
.

2.

If
\mathbb{K}
is positive,
\mathcal{P}(\Sigma,D,\mathbb{K},\lambda,\alpha)=0_{\mathbb{K}}
implies
\Sigma,D\not\models\alpha
.

We call the
semiring domain
of
\mathcal{P}
the maximal set
S
of semirings such that
\mathcal{P}(\Sigma,D,\mathbb{K},\lambda,\alpha)
is defined for every
\mathbb{K}\in S
, and every
\Sigma
,
(D,\mathbb{K},\lambda)
and
\alpha
.

Intuitively, Definition
3
means that the semantics reflects fact (non)-entailment.
It is extremely permissive: We could define such a semantics that associates to each
entailed fact a random semiring element different from zero, and
does not bring any information beyond facts entailment.
In the sequel, we state and discuss a number of properties that may be expected to be satisfied by a provenance semantics.

Throughout this section, when not stated otherwise,
\mathcal{P}
,
\Sigma
,
D
,
\mathbb{K}
,
\lambda
and
\alpha
denote respectively an arbitrary provenance semantics, Datalog program, database, commutative semiring
(K,+,\times,0,1)
, function from
D
to
K\setminus\{0\}
, and fact.
We phrase properties as conditions, and say that

\mathcal{P}

satisfies
a property if it satisfies the condition.

We also denote by
\lambda_{X}
an injective function
\lambda_{X}:D\mapsto X
.

4.1
Compatibility with Classical Notions

Property
1
is a sanity check:
if a Datalog program amounts to a UCQ, the provenance should be the same as the one defined for relational databases
[
2007
]
.
A Datalog program
\Sigma
is
UCQ-defined
if its rules are of the form
\phi(\vec{x},\vec{y})\rightarrow H(\vec{x})
where
H
is a predicate
that does not occur in the body of any rule.

In this case,
the equivalent UCQ
Q^{\Sigma}
of
\Sigma
is
\bigcup_{\phi(\vec{x},\vec{y})\rightarrow H(\vec{x})\in\Sigma}\exists\vec{y}\phi(\vec{x},\vec{y})
.

Property 1

(Algebra Consistency)
.

If
\Sigma
is UCQ-defined with rule head
H(\vec{x})

and
H\notin\mathcal{S}(D)
,
then for every tuple
\vec{a}
of same arity as
\vec{x}
, the relational provenance of
Q^{\Sigma}(\vec{a})

is equal to
\mathcal{P}(\Sigma,D,\mathbb{K},\lambda,H(\vec{a}))
.

While Property 1 considers the behavior of a provenance semantics on a restricted class of queries, we can alternatively consider its behavior on a specific semiring.
Boolean provenance has a very natural definition, based on the database subsets that entail the query, and
is widely used, notably for probabilistic databases
[
2017
]
, but also for ontology-mediated query explanation (e.g., in Datalog
+/-

or description logics
[
2019
,
2020
]
).
It is formalized with the semiring
\mathit{PosBool}(X)
.

Property 2

(Boolean Compatibility)
.

\mathcal{P}(\Sigma,D,\mathit{PosBool}(X),\lambda_{X},\alpha)=\bigvee_{\begin{subarray}{c}D^{\prime}\subseteq D\\
\Sigma,D^{\prime}\models\alpha\end{subarray}}\bigwedge_{\beta\in D^{\prime}}\lambda_{X}(\beta)

Property
2
expresses ‘insensibility’ to syntax, that is, every provenance semantics that satisfies Property
2
agrees on equivalent programs (i.e., those that have the same models) for the semiring
\mathit{PosBool}(X)
.
This is related to ideas from
[
2009
]
on the provenance of equivalent UCQs.

4.2
Compatibility with Specialization

Semiring provenance has been introduced to abstract from the particular semiring at hand, and factor the computations in some provenance semiring which specializes correctly to any semiring of interest.
The next property allows one to do so (Appendix
B.1
),
and is thus highly desirable.

Property 3

(Commutation with Homomorphisms)
.

If there is a semiring homomorphism
h
from
\mathbb{K}_{1}
to
\mathbb{K}_{2}
, then
h(\mathcal{P}(\Sigma,D,\mathbb{K}_{1},\lambda,\alpha))=\mathcal{P}(\Sigma,D,\mathbb{K}_{2},h\circ\lambda,\alpha)
.

We call Property
3
restricted to
\omega
-continuous homomorphisms
Commutation with
\omega
-Continuous Homomorphisms
.

Specializing correctly is all the more useful when
\mathcal{P}
is well-defined for a lot of semirings, in particular on all commutative or at least all commutative
\omega
-continuous semirings.

Property 4

(Any (
\omega
-Continuous) Semiring)
.

\mathcal{P}
satisfies the
Any Semiring Property
(resp.
Any
\omega
-Continuous Semiring Property
) if the semiring domain of
\mathcal{P}
contains the set of all commutative (resp. commutative
\omega
-continuous) semirings.

4.3
Joint and Alternative Use of the Data

How is the actual usage of the data reflected in the provenance semantics?
The next property formalizes that multiplication reflects joint use of the data, and addition alternative use. For the rest of this section, we set
\mathsf{goal}
to be a nullary predicate not in
\mathcal{S}(\Sigma)\cup\mathcal{S}(D)
.

Property 5

(Joint and Alternative Use)
.

For all tuples of facts
(\alpha^{1}_{1},\cdots,\alpha^{1}_{n_{1}})
,
\dots
,
(\alpha^{m}_{1},\cdots,\alpha^{m}_{n_{m}})
, it holds that

\mathcal{P}(\Sigma^{\prime},D,\mathbb{K},\lambda,\mathsf{goal})=\Sigma_{i=1}^{m}\Pi_{j=1}^{n_{i}}\mathcal{P}(\Sigma,D,\mathbb{K},\lambda,\alpha^{i}_{j})

where
\Sigma^{\prime}=\Sigma\cup\{\bigwedge_{j=1}^{n_{i}}\alpha^{i}_{j}\rightarrow\mathsf{goal}\mid 1\leq i\leq m\}
.

We weaken the above by
referring to each mode
separately:

Property 6

(Joint Use)
.

For all facts
\alpha_{1},\cdots,\alpha_{n}
,

\mathcal{P}(\Sigma^{\prime},D,\mathbb{K},\lambda,\mathsf{goal})=\Pi_{j=1}^{n}\mathcal{P}(\Sigma,D,\mathbb{K},\lambda,\alpha_{j})

where
\Sigma^{\prime}=\Sigma\cup\{\bigwedge_{j=1}^{n}\alpha_{j}\rightarrow\mathsf{goal}\}
.

Property 7

(Alternative Use)
.

For all facts
\alpha_{1},\cdots,\alpha_{m}
,

\mathcal{P}(\Sigma^{\prime},D,\mathbb{K},\lambda,\mathsf{goal})=\Sigma_{i=1}^{m}\mathcal{P}(\Sigma,D,\mathbb{K},\lambda,\alpha_{i})

where

\Sigma^{\prime}=\Sigma\cup\{\alpha_{i}\rightarrow\mathsf{goal}\mid 1\leq i\leq m\}
.

4.4
Fact Roles in Entailment.

After considering how facts can be combined or used alternatively
to entail a result, we ponder their possible
roles w.r.t. the entailment.
Property
8
asserts that the original annotation of a fact
takes part in
the provenance of its entailment.

Property 8

(Self)
.

If
\alpha\in D
, then
\lambda(\alpha)\sqsubseteq\mathcal{P}(\Sigma,D,\mathbb{K},\lambda,\alpha)
.

Moreover, if a database fact cannot be alternatively derived using the rules, then its provenance should be exactly its original annotation. To phrase this property we use the
grounding

\Sigma_{D}
of
\Sigma
w.r.t.
D
, defined by
\Sigma_{D}=\{h(\phi(\vec{x},\vec{y}))\rightarrow h(H(\vec{x}))\mid\phi(\vec{x},\vec{y})\rightarrow H(\vec{x})\in\Sigma,h:\vec{x}\cup\vec{y}\mapsto\mathcal{D}(D)\}
.
It holds that
\Sigma,D\models\alpha
if and only if
\Sigma_{D},D\models\alpha
.

Property 9

(Parsimony)
.

If
\alpha\in D
does not occur in any rule head in
\Sigma_{D}

then
\mathcal{P}(\Sigma,D,\mathbb{K},\lambda,\alpha)=\lambda(\alpha)
.

Parsimony Property together with other constraints guarantee Algebra Consistency Property.

Proposition 13
.

If
\mathcal{P}
satisfies Properties
5
(Joint and Alternative Use) and
9
(Parsimony), and is such that for every
\Sigma,D,\mathbb{K},\lambda,\alpha
,
\mathcal{P}(\Sigma,D,\mathbb{K},\lambda,\alpha)=\mathcal{P}(\Sigma_{D},D,\mathbb{K},\lambda,\alpha)
,
then it satisfies Property
1
(Algebra Consistency).

Property
10
states that
\mathcal{P}
reflects the necessity of a fact for the entailment.
We say that
\beta\in D
is
necessary
to
\Sigma,D\models\alpha
if
\Sigma,D\setminus\{\beta\}\not\models\alpha
,
and denote by
\mathit{Nec}
the set of such facts.

Property 10

(Necessary Facts)
.

There exists
e\in K
such that

\mathcal{P}(\Sigma,D,\mathbb{K},\lambda,\alpha)=\Pi_{\beta\in\mathit{Nec}}\lambda({\beta})\times e
.

A fact is
usable
to
\Sigma,D\models\alpha
if it occurs in some derivation tree in
T^{\Sigma}_{D}(\alpha)
. Usable facts are related to the notion of
lineage

[
2000
]
and can be defined without resorting to derivation trees (cf. Appendix
B.3
).
Intuitively, if a fact is not usable to derive another fact, it should not have any influence on its provenance.

Property 11

(Non-Usable Facts)
.

For every
\lambda^{\prime}
that differs from
\lambda
only on facts that are not usable to
\Sigma,D\models\alpha
, it holds that

\mathcal{P}(\Sigma,D,\mathbb{K},\lambda,\alpha)=\mathcal{P}(\Sigma,D,\mathbb{K},\lambda^{\prime},\alpha)
.

4.5
Data Modification

The last two properties indicate how provenance is impacted when facts are inserted or deleted.

Property 12

(Insertion)
.

For every
(D^{\prime},\mathbb{K},\lambda^{\prime})
such that
D\cap D^{\prime}=\emptyset
,

\mathcal{P}(\Sigma,D,\mathbb{K},\lambda,\alpha)+\mathcal{P}(\Sigma,D^{\prime},\mathbb{K},\lambda^{\prime},\alpha)
\sqsubseteq\mathcal{P}(\Sigma,D\cup D^{\prime},\mathbb{K},\lambda\cup\lambda^{\prime},\alpha)
.

Maintaining provenance upon fact deletion is very useful in practice. We formalize this using a provenance semiring, which allows us to keep track of the facts.
A partial evaluation of a provenance expression
p(X)
over variables
X
is an expression obtained from
p(X)
by replacing some of the variables by a given value.

Property 13

(Deletion)
.

For every provenance semiring
\mathit{Prov}(X)
and
D^{\prime}\subseteq D
, if
\lambda^{\prime}
is the restriction of
\lambda_{X}
to
D^{\prime}
and
\Delta=D\setminus D^{\prime}
,
then

\mathcal{P}(\Sigma,D^{\prime},\mathit{Prov}(X),\lambda^{\prime},\alpha)
is equal to the partial evaluation of
\mathcal{P}(\Sigma,D,\mathit{Prov}(X),\lambda_{X},\alpha)
obtained by setting the annotations of facts in
\Delta
to
0
:
\mathcal{P}(\Sigma,D,\mathit{Prov}(X),\lambda_{X},\alpha)[\{\lambda_{X}(x)=0\}_{x\in\Delta}]
.

5.1
Tree- and Execution-Based Semantics Cases

We first discuss
\mathcal{P}^{\texttt{MDT}}
and
\mathcal{P}^{\texttt{HMDT}}
, which have not been much investigated and stand out compared to
\mathcal{P}^{\texttt{AT}}
and
\mathcal{P}^{\texttt{NRT}}
.
The next example shows that they do not satisfy the Boolean Compatibility, Joint and Alternative Use, Alternative Use, Insertion and Deletion Properties.

Example 9
.

Consider
\Sigma
and
(D,\mathit{Prov}(X),\lambda_{X})
as follows.

\displaystyle\Sigma=

\displaystyle\{A(x)\rightarrow\mathsf{goal},\ B(x)\rightarrow\mathsf{goal},\ C(x)\rightarrow B(x)\}

\displaystyle D=

\displaystyle\{A(a),\ C(a)\}\text{ with }\lambda_{X}(A(a))=a,\ \lambda_{X}(C(a))=c

It holds that both
\mathcal{P}^{\texttt{MDT}}(\Sigma,D,\mathit{Prov}(X),\lambda_{X},\mathsf{goal})
and
\mathcal{P}^{\texttt{HMDT}}(\Sigma,D,\mathit{Prov}(X),\lambda_{X},\mathsf{goal})
are equal to
a
.
For
\mathcal{P}\in\{\mathcal{P}^{\texttt{MDT}},\mathcal{P}^{\texttt{HMDT}}\}
we then have the following:

(i)
The Boolean provenance of
\mathsf{goal}
is
a\vee c
, hence
\mathcal{P}
does not satisfy the Boolean Compatibility Property.

(ii)
Since
\mathcal{P}(\emptyset,D,\mathit{Prov}(X),\lambda_{X},A(a))=a
and
\mathcal{P}(\emptyset,D,\mathit{Prov}(X),\lambda_{X},C(a))=c
,

\mathcal{P}
does not satisfy the Alternative Use, nor the Joint and Alternative Use Property.

(iii)
Let
D^{\prime}=\{\mathsf{goal}\}
and
\lambda^{\prime}(\mathsf{goal})=g
. It holds that

\mathcal{P}(\Sigma,D\cup D^{\prime},\mathit{Prov}(X),\lambda_{X}\cup\lambda_{X}^{\prime},\mathsf{goal})=g
, which is different from

\mathcal{P}(\Sigma,D,\mathit{Prov}(X),\lambda_{X},\mathsf{goal})+\mathcal{P}(\Sigma,D^{\prime},\mathit{Prov}(X),\lambda_{X}^{\prime},\mathsf{goal})+e
for every
e\in\mathit{Prov}(X)
. Hence
\mathcal{P}
does not satisfy the Insertion Property.

(iv)
Let
D^{\prime}=D\setminus\{A(a)\}=\{C(a)\}
. The partial evaluation of
\mathcal{P}(\Sigma,D,\mathit{Prov}(X),\lambda_{X},\mathsf{goal})
where
a
is set to
0
is equal to
0
while
\mathcal{P}(\Sigma,D^{\prime},\mathit{Prov}(X),\lambda_{X},\mathsf{goal})=c
. Hence
\mathcal{P}
does not satisfy the Deletion Property.

We now illustrate the difference between
\mathcal{P}^{\texttt{HMDT}}
and
\mathcal{P}^{\texttt{MDT}}
:
\mathcal{P}^{\texttt{HMDT}}
satisfies the Joint Use Property while
\mathcal{P}^{\texttt{MDT}}
does not.

Example 10
.

Let
\Sigma=\{C(x)\rightarrow B(x),\ D(x)\rightarrow A(x)\}
,
D=\{B(a),C(a),D(a)\}
and
\lambda(B(a))=b
,
\lambda(C(a))=c
,
\lambda(D(a))=d
, and consider

\Sigma^{\prime}=\Sigma\cup\{A(a)\wedge B(a)\rightarrow\mathsf{goal}\}
.

\mathcal{P}^{\texttt{MDT}}(\Sigma^{\prime},D,\mathbb{K},\lambda,\mathsf{goal})=b\times d+c\times d

while
\mathcal{P}^{\texttt{MDT}}(\Sigma,D,\mathbb{K},\lambda,A(a))=d
and
\mathcal{P}^{\texttt{MDT}}(\Sigma,D,\mathbb{K},\lambda,B(a))=b
.
Hence
\mathcal{P}^{\texttt{MDT}}
does not satisfy the Joint Use Property.

We conclude this discussion with the remark that
\mathcal{P}^{\texttt{AT}}
satisfies the Commutation with
\omega
-Continuous Homomorphisms but not the Commutation with Homomorphisms Property.

Example 11
.

Consider the semiring
\mathbb{N}^{\infty}
with the classical operations, and define
\mathbb{N}^{\infty,\infty^{\prime}}
as its extension by an element
\infty^{\prime}
such that for every
n\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{\infty\}
,
n+\infty^{\prime}=\infty^{\prime}
, and
n\times\infty^{\prime}=\infty^{\prime}
if
n\neq 0
, and
0
otherwise.
Both semirings are
\omega
-continuous and
h:\mathbb{N}^{\infty}\mapsto\mathbb{N}^{\infty,\infty^{\prime}}
defined by
h(n)=n
for every
n\in\mathbb{N}
,
h(\infty)=\infty^{\prime}
is a semiring homomorphism (which is not
\omega
-continuous).
Assume that
\mathcal{P}^{\texttt{AT}}(\Sigma,D,\mathbb{N}^{\infty},\lambda,\mathsf{goal})=\Sigma_{i\in\mathbb{N}}1=\infty
.
Then
h(\mathcal{P}^{\texttt{AT}}(\Sigma,D,\mathbb{N}^{\infty},\lambda,\mathsf{goal}))=\infty^{\prime}
is different from
\mathcal{P}^{\texttt{AT}}(\Sigma,D,\mathbb{N}^{\infty,\infty^{\prime}},h\circ\lambda,\mathsf{goal})=\Sigma_{i\in\mathbb{N}}h(1)=\infty
.

5.2
Model-Based Semantics Cases

On
+\,
-idempotent semirings,

\mathcal{P}^{\texttt{AM}}
and
\mathcal{P}^{\texttt{SAM}}
coincide with
\mathcal{P}^{\texttt{AT}}
so verify the same properties, and the semiring
\mathbb{B}\llbracket X\rrbracket
of formal power series with Boolean coefficients can be used to compute them in any
+\,
-idempotent semiring (Appendix
E.1
).
However, on non-idempotent semirings, they do not satisfy several properties, and in particular the Commutation with (
\omega
-Continuous) Homomorphisms Properties.

Example 12
.

Let
\Sigma=\{A(x)\rightarrow\mathsf{goal},B(x)\rightarrow\mathsf{goal}\}
and
D=\{A(a),B(a)\}
and consider the provenance semiring
\mathbb{N}^{\infty}\llbracket X\rrbracket
with
\lambda_{X}(A(a))=x
and
\lambda_{X}(B(a))=y
.

It holds that both
\mathcal{P}^{\texttt{AM}}(\Sigma,D,\mathbb{N}^{\infty}\llbracket X\rrbracket,\lambda_{X},\mathsf{goal})
and
\mathcal{P}^{\texttt{SAM}}(\Sigma,D,\mathbb{N}^{\infty}\llbracket X\rrbracket,\lambda_{X},\mathsf{goal})

are equal to
x+y
.

Consider now the semiring

\mathbb{N}^{\infty}
,
\lambda_{\mathbb{N}}(A(a))=2
and
\lambda_{\mathbb{N}}(B(a))=2
.
Both
\mathcal{P}^{\texttt{AM}}(\Sigma,D,\mathbb{N}^{\infty},\lambda_{\mathbb{N}},\mathsf{goal})
and
\mathcal{P}^{\texttt{SAM}}(\Sigma,D,\mathbb{N}^{\infty},\lambda_{\mathbb{N}},\mathsf{goal})
are equal to
2
.

For
\mathcal{P}\in\{\mathcal{P}^{\texttt{AM}},\mathcal{P}^{\texttt{SAM}}\}
we then have the following:

(i)
Let
h
be a
\omega
-continuous homomorphism from
\mathbb{N}^{\infty}\llbracket X\rrbracket
to
\mathbb{N}^{\infty}
such that
h(x)=2
and
h(y)=2
.
Since
h(x+y)=h(x)+h(y)=4
,
\mathcal{P}
does not satisfy the Commutation with
\omega
-Continuous Homomorphisms Property.

(ii)
The relational provenance of
Q^{\Sigma}()
w.r.t.
\mathbb{N}^{\infty}
and
\lambda_{\mathbb{N}}
is
4
so
\mathcal{P}
does not satisfy the Algebra Consistency Property.

(iii)

\mathcal{P}(\emptyset,D,\mathbb{N}^{\infty},\lambda_{\mathbb{N}},A(a))+\mathcal{P}(\emptyset,D,\mathbb{N}^{\infty},\lambda_{\mathbb{N}},B(a))=4

so
\mathcal{P}
does not satisfy the Alternative Use nor the Joint and Alternative Use Property.

(iv)
Since
\mathcal{P}(\Sigma,\{A(a)\},\mathbb{N}^{\infty},\lambda_{\mathbb{N}},\mathsf{goal})+\mathcal{P}(\Sigma,\{B(a)\},\mathbb{N}^{\infty},\lambda_{\mathbb{N}},\mathsf{goal})=4
is strictly greater than
\mathcal{P}(\Sigma,D,\mathbb{N}^{\infty},\lambda_{\mathbb{N}},\mathsf{goal})
,
\mathcal{P}
does not satisfy the Insertion Property.

Moreover,
\mathcal{P}^{\texttt{SAM}}
does not satisfy the Joint Use Property.

Example 13
.

Let

\displaystyle\Sigma=\{

\displaystyle A(x)\rightarrow\mathsf{g}_{1},A(x)\rightarrow\mathsf{g}_{2},B(x)\rightarrow\mathsf{g}_{1},B(x)\rightarrow\mathsf{g}_{2}\}

\displaystyle D=\{

\displaystyle A(a),B(a)\}\text{ with }\lambda_{X}(A(a))=x,\lambda_{X}(B(a))=y.

Both
\mathcal{P}^{\texttt{SAM}}(\Sigma,D,\mathbb{N}^{\infty}\llbracket X\rrbracket,\lambda_{X},\mathsf{g}_{1})
and
\mathcal{P}^{\texttt{SAM}}(\Sigma,D,\mathbb{N}^{\infty}\llbracket X\rrbracket,\lambda_{X},\mathsf{g}_{2})
are equal to
x+y

but

\mathcal{P}^{\texttt{SAM}}(\Sigma\cup\{\mathsf{g}_{1}\wedge\mathsf{g}_{2}\rightarrow\mathsf{goal}\},D,\mathbb{N}^{\infty}\llbracket X\rrbracket,\lambda_{X},\mathsf{goal})=x^{2}+y^{2}+xy\neq(x+y)^{2}
.

We show that
\mathcal{P}^{\texttt{AM}}
does not satisfy the Necessary Facts Property in Appendix
D.1

because we needed to craft a specific semiring to get a counter-example.

6
Complexity Considerations and Conclusion

In this paper, we present alternative provenance semantics for Datalog based on models, execution algorithms and derivation trees, and compare them through the lens of different properties.
\mathcal{P}^{\texttt{NRT}}
is the only one that satisfies all the studied properties but does not coincide with an execution based semantics contrary to the other tree-based semantics
\mathcal{P}^{\texttt{AT}}
,
\mathcal{P}^{\texttt{MDT}}
, and
\mathcal{P}^{\texttt{HMDT}}
.
The equivalence between the tree-based
\mathcal{P}^{\texttt{AT}}
,
\mathcal{P}^{\texttt{NRT}}
and model-based
\mathcal{P}^{\texttt{AM}}
and
\mathcal{P}^{\texttt{SAM}}
definitions on absorptive semirings may also indicates a robust provenance on this restricted setting.

One of the main complexity sources of Datalog provenance stems from
its infinite representation.

?
(
?
) studied semirings for which the provenance expressions given by
\mathcal{P}^{\texttt{AT}}
are finite, and showed that they can be represented by polynomial size circuits.
We show (Appendix
F
)
that the annotations produced at each iteration of our execution algorithms can be represented by arithmetic circuits of polynomial size in the data.
Consequently, both
\mathcal{P}^{\texttt{MDT}}
and
\mathcal{P}^{\texttt{HMDT}}
can be represented by polynomial size circuits
regardless of the semiring.
On the contrary, we show
that (assuming
\mathsf{P}\neq\mathsf{NP}
) there is no polynomially computable circuit that computes
\mathcal{P}^{\texttt{NRT}}
on
\mathbb{N}^{\infty}\llbracket X\rrbracket
, by a reduction from a result by
?
(
?
).
Whether it is possible to polynomially compute circuits for

\gamma
that is replaced by the derivation tree of
\gamma^{\alpha}
is a derivation tree for
\beta^{\alpha}
w.r.t.
D^{\alpha}
and
\Sigma\cup\Sigma^{\alpha}
, hence showing that
D^{\alpha},\Sigma\cup\Sigma^{\alpha}\models\beta^{\alpha}
.

We now show that any adornment-usable fact is usable, thanks to the following two observations:

•

removing adornments from facts and rules in a derivation tree of
\beta^{\alpha}
w.r.t.
D^{\alpha}
and
\Sigma\cup\Sigma^{\alpha}
results in a derivation tree of
\beta
w.r.t.
D
and
\Sigma
;

•

any derivation tree of
\beta^{\alpha}
contains
\alpha^{\alpha}
as a leaf: indeed, a node can have an adorned atom only if one of its child has an adorned atom, or if it is a leaf and is adorned. As
\alpha^{\alpha}
is the only adorned atom of
D^{\alpha}
, this concludes the proof.∎

Proposition 35

We can conclude that each Necessary Facts appear in every derivation tree of
\alpha

\mathcal{P}(\Sigma,D,\mathbb{K},\lambda,\alpha)=\Pi_{\beta\in\mathit{Nec}}\lambda({\beta})\times e

\mathcal{P}^{\texttt{AT}}

Let us analyze the derivation trees of

Proposition 45
.

\mathcal{P}^{\texttt{AM}}
satisfies the Non-Usable Facts Property.

Proof.

By Lemma
11
,
the interpretation
(I_{0},\mu^{I_{0}})
defined by
I_{0}=\{\alpha\mid\Sigma,D\models\alpha\}

\mu^{I_{0}}(\alpha)=\mathcal{P}^{\texttt{AM}}(\Sigma,D,\mathbb{K},\lambda,\alpha)

\Sigma

\mu^{I_{0}}(\alpha)
does not depend on non-usable facts, in the sense that none of the constraints of the form ‘

’ that

has to fulfill according to the definition of annotated models involves non-usable facts.

\mathcal{P}^{\texttt{AM}}(\Sigma,D,\mathbb{K},\lambda,\alpha)

Let
\alpha
be a fact such that
\Sigma,D\models\alpha
.
Assume for a contradiction that there exists a constraint
\mathcal{C}
on

1.

\mathcal{C}
cannot be of the form ‘
\lambda(\alpha)\sqsubseteq\mu^{I_{0}}(\alpha)
’, because it would mean that
\alpha\in D
and hence is usable to
\Sigma,D\models\alpha
, so that

does not involved any non-usable fact.

2.

Hence

\Sigma
and

a homomorphism from

to

.

3.

By assumption, there exists

We can choose such

. Otherwise, if the only

\alpha
itself, the constraint

does not add any dependance on non-usable facts to

.

5.

For every

, since

, then
\Sigma,D\models\beta
by construction of
(I_{0},\mu^{I_{0}})
.
Hence there exists a derivation tree
t_{0}
of
\alpha
with root
(\alpha,r,h^{\prime})
having children of the form
(\beta,\rho,g)
with
\beta\in h^{\prime}(\phi(\vec{x},\vec{y}))
(including
\beta_{0}
),
\rho\in\Sigma
, and
g
homomorphism.

6.

By (3), there is a constraint
\mathcal{C}_{0}
on
\mu^{I_{0}}(\beta_{0})
involving non-usable facts.

7.

\mathcal{C}_{0}
cannot be of the form ‘

’ because in this case
\beta_{0}
would be in
D
and hence usable as a leaf of

8.

Thus
\mathcal{C}_{0}

and

a homomorphism from

.

9.

It follows that there exists

Lemma 12

RPQ queries are binary queries over labeled-edge graphs. They are based on a regular language that can be defined by a deterministic automaton.

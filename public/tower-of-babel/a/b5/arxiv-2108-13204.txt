On variants of the Euler sums and symmetric extensions of the Kaneko-Tsumura conjecture
Weiping Wang, Ce Xu [1pt] a . a. School of Science, Zhejiang Sci-Tech University, Hangzhou 310018, P.R. China b . b. School of Mathematics and Statistics, Anhui Normal University, Wuhu 241002, P.R. China

Abstract




Abstract

By using various expansions of the parametric digamma function and the method of residue computations, we study three variants of the linear Euler sums, related Hoffman’s double
t
-values and Kaneko-Tsumura’s double
T
-values, and establish several symmetric extensions of the Kaneko-Tsumura conjecture. Some special cases are discussed in detail to determine the coefficients of involved mathematical constants in the evaluations. In particular, it can be found that several general convolution identities on the classical Bernoulli numbers and Genocchi numbers are required in this study, and they are verified by the derivative polynomials of hyperbolic tangent.

AMS classification :

11M32; 11A07; 40A25; 05A19; 11B68

Keywords :

Multiple zeta values; Multiple
t
-values; Multiple
T
-values; Harmonic numbers; Euler sums; Convolution identities; Bernoulli numbers; Genocchi numbers

1.  Introduction

The
alternating multiple zeta values
(alternating MZVs) are defined by

\zeta(s_{1},s_{2},\ldots,s_{k};\sigma_{1},\sigma_{2},\ldots,\sigma_{k})=\sum_{n_{1}>n_{2}>\cdots>n_{k}\geq 1}\frac{\sigma_{1}^{n_{1}}\sigma_{2}^{n_{2}}\cdots\sigma_{k}^{n_{k}}}{n_{1}^{s_{1}}n_{2}^{s_{2}}\cdots n_{k}^{s_{k}}}\,,

(1.1)

where
s_{j}
are positive integers,
\sigma_{j}=\pm 1
, for
j=1,2,\ldots,k
, with
(s_{1},\sigma_{1})\neq(1,1)
. As usual, we can combine the strings of exponents and signs into a single string, with
s_{j}
in the
j
th position when
\sigma_{j}=+1
, and
\bar{s}_{j}
in the
j
th position when
\sigma_{j}=-1
. In particular, when
s_{1}>1
, setting
\sigma_{j}=1
,
j=1,2,\ldots,k
, in Eq. (
1.1
), we obtain
multiple zeta values
(MZVs), and denote them by
\zeta(s_{1},s_{2},\ldots,s_{k})
.

The study of multiple zeta values began in the early 1990s with the works of Hoffman
[
13
]
and Zagier
[
26
]
, and has attracted a lot of research in the last two decades. For detailed history and applications, the readers may consult in, e.g., the book of Zhao
[
27
]
.

In a recent paper
[
10
]
, Hoffman introduced and studied an “odd” variant of the MZVs:

\displaystyle t(s_{1},s_{2},\ldots,s_{k})

\displaystyle=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n_{1}>n_{2}>\cdots>n_{k}\geq 1\\
n_{i}\ \mathrm{odd}\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{n_{1}^{s_{1}}n_{2}^{s_{2}}\cdots n_{k}^{s_{k}}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n_{1}>n_{2}>\cdots>n_{k}\geq 1}\frac{1}{(2n_{1}-1)^{s_{1}}(2n_{2}-1)^{s_{2}}\cdots(2n_{k}-1)^{s_{k}}}\,,

which are called the
multiple
t
-values
(MtVs). For convenience, let us call the normalized version

\displaystyle\tilde{t}(s_{1},s_{2},\ldots,s_{k})

\displaystyle=\sum_{n_{1}>n_{2}>\cdots>n_{k}\geq 1}\frac{1}{(n_{1}-1/2)^{s_{1}}(n_{2}-1/2)^{s_{2}}\cdots(n_{k}-1/2)^{s_{k}}}

\displaystyle=2^{s_{1}+s_{2}+\cdots+s_{k}}t(s_{1},s_{2},\ldots,s_{k})

the
multiple
\tilde{t}
-values
. According to the definitions,
\tilde{t}(s)=2^{s}t(s)=(2^{s}-1)\zeta(s)
for integer
s\geq 2
, where
\zeta(s)
is the
Riemann zeta function
. As showed in
[
10
, Corollaries 4.1 and 4.2]
, the MtVs are reducible to linear combinations of alternating MZVs. Due to this fact as well as the congruence condition in the summation, the MtVs can be regarded as MZVs of level two. In 2020, Kaneko and Tsumura
[
14
]
introduced another variant of MZVs of level two:

\displaystyle T(s_{1},s_{2},\ldots,s_{k})

\displaystyle=2^{k}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n_{1}>n_{2}>\cdots>n_{k}\geq 1\\
n_{i}\equiv k-i+1{\@displayfalse\pmod{2}}\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{n_{1}^{s_{1}}n_{2}^{s_{2}}\cdots n_{k}^{s_{k}}}

\displaystyle=2^{k}\sum_{n_{1}>n_{2}>\cdots>n_{k}\geq 1}\frac{1}{(2n_{1}-k)^{s_{1}}(2n_{2}-k+1)^{s_{2}}\cdots(2n_{k}-1)^{s_{k}}}\,,

which are called the
multiple
T
-values
(MTVs).

By convention, for the MZVs, MtVs and MTVs, the quantity
k
is called the “
depth
” and the quantity
w:=s_{1}+s_{2}+\cdots+s_{k}
is called the “
weight
”.

Let
\mathcal{Z}
be the space of usual multiple zeta values. In
[
14
, Conjecture 5.3]
, Kaneko and Tsumura observed that the following relation holds:

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}i+j=m\\
i,j\geq 0\end{subarray}}\binom{p+i-1}{i}\binom{q+j-1}{j}T(p+i,q+j)\in\mathcal{Z}\,,

(1.2)

for
m,q\geq 1
and
p\geq 2
, with
m+p+q
even. That is, these sums are expressible in terms of MZVs. In 2021, Murakami
[
18
, Theorem 42]
proved this conjecture by using the motivic method employed in
[
8
]
(see also
[
14
, Remark 5.6]
).

For simplicity, let us define the transformation operator
\lambda_{m}
by

\lambda_{m}(\varOmega_{p,q}):=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}i+j=m-1\\
i,j\geq 0\end{subarray}}\binom{p+i-1}{i}\binom{q+j-1}{j}\varOmega_{p+i,q+j}\,,\quad\text{for }m\geq 1\,,

(1.3)

which maps a sum
\varOmega_{p,q}
of two parameters to another one of three parameters, with
\lambda_{1}(\varOmega_{p,q})=\varOmega_{p,q}
. Then Kaneko and Tsumura’s conjecture (
1.2
) can be rewritten as

\lambda_{m+1}(T(p,q))\in\mathcal{Z}\,,\quad\text{for }m,q\geq 1,\ p\geq 2,\ \text{with }m+p+q\text{ even}.

Let
H_{n}^{(r)}
and
h_{n}^{(r)}
stand for the
generalized harmonic numbers
and the
odd harmonic numbers
of order
r
, respectively, defined by
H_{0}^{(r)}=h_{0}^{(r)}=0
and

H_{n}^{(r)}=\sum_{k=1}^{n}\frac{1}{k^{r}}\,,\quad h_{n}^{(r)}=\sum_{k=1}^{n}\frac{1}{(k-1/2)^{r}}\,,\quad\text{for }n,r=1,2,\ldots,

with
H_{n}\equiv H_{n}^{(1)}
and
h_{n}\equiv h_{n}^{(1)}
. The classical
Euler sums
are infinite series

S_{p_{1}p_{2}\cdots p_{k},q}=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{H_{n}^{(p_{1})}H_{n}^{(p_{2})}\cdots H_{n}^{(p_{k})}}{n^{q}}\,,

where
p_{1}\leq p_{2}\leq\ldots\leq p_{k}
and
q\geq 2
, and the linear sums are of the form
S_{p,q}
. For an early introduction and study on the evaluations of the classical Euler sums, the readers may consult in Flajolet and Salvy’s paper
[
6
]
, and for some recent progress, the readers are referred to
[
20
,
23
,
25
]
and references therein.

In this paper, using various expansions of the parametric digamma function and the method of residue computations, we establish symmetric extensions of the Kaneko-Tsumura conjecture (
1.2
) on three variants of the linear Euler sums, defined by

T_{p,q}:=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{h_{n-1}^{(p)}}{(n-1/2)^{q}}\,,\quad\tilde{S}_{p,q}:=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{h_{n}^{(p)}}{n^{q}}\,,\quad R_{p,q}:=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{H_{n-1}^{(p)}}{(n-1/2)^{q}}\,,\quad\text{for }q\geq 2\,,

respectively, which were introduced and studied in
[
21
,
22
]
. In particular, we show that for
m,p\geq 1
and
q\geq 2
, there hold

\displaystyle(-1)^{m-1}\lambda_{p}(T_{m,q})+(-1)^{p-1}\lambda_{m}(\tilde{S}_{p,q})\in\mathbb{Q}[\ln(2),\text{zeta values}]\,,

(1.4)

\displaystyle(-1)^{m-1}\lambda_{p}(R_{m,q})+(-1)^{p-1}\lambda_{m}(R_{p,q})\in\mathbb{Q}[\ln(2),\text{zeta values}]\,.

(1.5)

In other words, both of the symmetric sums on the left are reducible to
\ln(2)
and zeta values. Explicit expressions of these two sums are presented, and a detailed discussion on some interesting special cases follows.

Moreover, by the derivative polynomials of hyperbolic tangent, we obtain a very general convolution identity for the Bernoulli numbers
B_{n}
and Genocchi numbers
G_{n}
, which is used to produce the following one:

\displaystyle\sum_{i=0}^{q-1}\binom{q-1}{i}\frac{B_{q+i}G_{2n+q-i}}{(q+i)(2n+q-i)}

\displaystyle\quad=-\frac{1}{4}\sum_{i=0}^{2n}(-1)^{i}\binom{2n}{i}\frac{G_{q+i}G_{2n+q-i}}{(q+i)(2n+q-i)}+\frac{(-1)^{q}}{q\binom{2q}{q}}\frac{G_{2n+2q}}{2n+2q}\,,\quad\text{for }n\geq 0\,,\ q\geq 2\,,

(1.6)

so that the coefficients of various constants in the evaluations of the sums in (
1.4
), which correspond to the case of
m=q
and
p
odd, can be finally determined.

On the other hand, by the definitions, the linear
T
-sums and
\tilde{S}
-sums are associated with Hoffman’s double
t
-values and Kaneko-Tsumura’s double
T
-values, respectively:

\displaystyle T_{p,q}

\displaystyle=\tilde{t}(q,p)=2^{p+q}t(q,p)=2^{p+q-2}\{\zeta(q,p)-\zeta(q,\bar{p})-\zeta(\bar{q},p)+\zeta(\bar{q},\bar{p})\}\,,

(1.7)

\displaystyle\tilde{S}_{p,q}

\displaystyle=2^{p+q-2}T(q,p)

\displaystyle=(2^{p+q-1}-1)\{\zeta(q,p)+\zeta(p+q)\}+2^{p+q-1}\{\zeta(\bar{q},p)+\zeta(\overline{p+q})\}\,,

(1.8)

and the linear
R
-sums can be expressed in terms of zeta values,
t
-values and double
T
-values:

R_{p,q}=\zeta(p)\tilde{t}(q)-\tilde{S}_{q,p}=\zeta(p)\tilde{t}(q)-2^{p+q-2}T(p,q)\,,\quad\text{for }p,q\geq 2

(1.9)

(see
[
21
, Eqs. (3.5), (3.7), (3.8) and (3.11)]
). Therefore, we can transform the results on variants of linear Euler sums to those on variants of double zeta values. For example, a symmetric sum on the double
T
-values can be obtained directly from (
1.5
) and (
1.9
):

(-1)^{m}\lambda_{p}(T(m,q))+(-1)^{p}\lambda_{m}(T(p,q))\in\mathbb{Q}[\text{zeta values}]\,,\quad\text{for }m,p,q\geq 2\,,

which further indicates that the sums
\lambda_{p}(T(p,q))
are reducible to zeta values if
p,q\geq 2
. Additionally, we show that for any even weight
w:=m+q
, with
m,q\geq 2
, the double
t
-values
t(q,m)
and linear
T
-sums
T_{m,q}
are expressible in terms of MZVs.

The paper is organized as follows. In Section
2
, we present some expansions of the parametric digamma function and introduce the residue theorem, which are used in the establishment of the symmetric extensions of the Kaneko-Tsumura conjecture (
1.2
). Section
3
is devoted to the symmetric sums on the linear
T
-sums and
\tilde{S}
-sums, and Section
4
is devoted to the symmetric sums on the linear
R
-sums. Finally, in Section
5
, we prove the convolution identity (
1.6
) on the Bernoulli numbers and Genocchi numbers, which is required in a proof in Section
3
.

2.  Expansions and residue theorem

In
[
24
]
, we introduced a
parametric digamma function

\varPsi(-s;a)
by

\varPsi(-s;a)+\gamma=\frac{1}{s-a}+\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\left(\frac{1}{k+a}-\frac{1}{k+a-s}\right)\,,\quad\text{for}\ s\in\mathbb{C}\,,\ a\in\mathbb{C}\setminus\mathbb{Z}^{-}\,,

where
\gamma
is the
Euler–Mascheroni constant
, and
\mathbb{Z}^{-}:=\{-1,-2,\ldots\}
. The function
\varPsi(-s;a)
is meromorphic in the entire complex plane with a simple pole at
s=n+a
for each nonnegative integer
n
. Here, let

\varPsi(-s):=\varPsi(-s;-\tfrac{1}{2})+\gamma=\frac{1}{s+1/2}+\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\left(\frac{1}{k-1/2}-\frac{1}{k-1/2-s}\right)\,.

By
[
24
, Theorems 2.1–2.3 and Corollary 2.4]
, the next two lemmas can be established. Firstly, using the special value of the digamma function
\psi(1/2)=-2\ln(2)-\gamma
, we have Lemma
2.1
.

Lemma 2.1
.

For integers
n\geq 0
and
p\geq 2
, the following expansions hold:

\displaystyle\varPsi(\tfrac{1}{2}-s)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle s\to n}}{{=}}\frac{1}{s-n}+H_{n}+2\ln(2)+\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\{(-1)^{j}H_{n}^{(j+1)}-\zeta(j+1)\}(s-n)^{j}\,,

(2.1)

\displaystyle\frac{\varPsi^{(p-1)}(\tfrac{1}{2}-s)}{(p-1)!}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle s\to n}}{{=}}\frac{1}{(s-n)^{p}}+(-1)^{p}\sum_{j=p}^{\infty}\binom{j-1}{p-1}\{\zeta(j)+(-1)^{j}H_{n}^{(j)}\}(s-n)^{j-p}\,.

(2.2)

Next, according to the definitions of the
Hurwitz zeta function

\zeta(s,a+1)=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{(k+a)^{s}}
and the
parametric harmonic numbers

H_{n}^{(s)}(a)=\sum_{k=1}^{n}\frac{1}{(k+a)^{s}}
, we have

\zeta(s,\tfrac{1}{2})=\tilde{t}(s)\,,\quad H_{n}^{(s)}(\tfrac{1}{2})+2^{s}=h_{n+1}^{(s)}\,,\quad H_{n}^{(s)}(-\tfrac{1}{2})=h_{n}^{(s)}\,,

which yield Lemma
2.2
.

Lemma 2.2
.

For integers
n\geq 1
and
p\geq 2
, the following expansions hold:

\displaystyle\varPsi(\tfrac{1}{2}-s)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle s\to n-1/2}}{{=}}h_{n}+\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\{(-1)^{j}h_{n}^{(j+1)}-\tilde{t}(j+1)\}(s-n+\tfrac{1}{2})^{j}\,,

(2.3)

\displaystyle\frac{\varPsi^{(p-1)}(\tfrac{1}{2}-s)}{(p-1)!}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle s\to n-1/2}}{{=}}(-1)^{p}\sum_{j=p}^{\infty}\binom{j-1}{p-1}\{\tilde{t}(j)+(-1)^{j}h_{n}^{(j)}\}(s-n+\tfrac{1}{2})^{j-p}\,,

(2.4)

\displaystyle\varPsi(\tfrac{1}{2}-s)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle s\to-(n-1/2)}}{{=}}h_{n-1}+\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\{h_{n-1}^{(j+1)}-\tilde{t}(j+1)\}(s+n-\tfrac{1}{2})^{j}\,,

(2.5)

\displaystyle\frac{\varPsi^{(p-1)}(\tfrac{1}{2}-s)}{(p-1)!}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle s\to-(n-1/2)}}{{=}}(-1)^{p}\sum_{j=p}^{\infty}\binom{j-1}{p-1}\{\tilde{t}(j)-h_{n-1}^{(j)}\}(s+n-\tfrac{1}{2})^{j-p}\,.

(2.6)

Besides the above two lemmas, by computation, we obtain the next one.

Lemma 2.3
.

For integers
n\geq 1
and
p\geq 2
, the following expansions hold:

\displaystyle\varPsi(\tfrac{1}{2}-s)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle s\to-n}}{{=}}H_{n-1}+2\ln(2)+\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\{H_{n-1}^{(j+1)}-\zeta(j+1)\}(s+n)^{j}\,,

(2.7)

\displaystyle\frac{\varPsi^{(p-1)}(\tfrac{1}{2}-s)}{(p-1)!}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle s\to-n}}{{=}}(-1)^{p-1}\sum_{j=p}^{\infty}\binom{j-1}{p-1}\{H_{n-1}^{(j)}-\zeta(j)\}(s+n)^{j-p}\,.

(2.8)

Proof..

It can be found that

\varPsi(\tfrac{1}{2}-s)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle s\to-n}}{{=}}2+\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\left(\frac{1}{k-1/2}-\frac{1}{k+n}-\frac{1}{k+n}\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\left(\frac{s+n}{k+n}\right)^{j}\right)\,,

which, together with the infinite series

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\left(\frac{1}{k-1/2}-\frac{1}{k+n}\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\left(\frac{1}{k-1/2}-\frac{1}{k+1}\right)+\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\left(\frac{1}{k+1}-\frac{1}{k+n}\right)

\displaystyle=-2+2\ln(2)+\gamma+\psi(n)=-2+2\ln(2)+H_{n-1}\,,

arising from the properties of the digamma function, gives (
2.7
). Differentiating (
2.7
)
p-1
times with respect to
s
further leads us to the second expansion of this lemma.
∎

In particular, if we interpret
\zeta(1):=-2\ln(2)
and
\tilde{t}(1):=0
wherever they occur, the expansions (
2.2
), (
2.4
), (
2.6
) and (
2.8
) hold for
p=1
. Moreover, it can be found that the expansion (
2.4
) also hold for
n=0
, for it coincides with the
n=1
case of (
2.6
).

Finally, due to Flajolet and Salvy’s
[
6
, Lemma 2.1]
, the following residue theorem holds.

Lemma 2.4
.

Let
\xi(s)
be a kernel function and let
r(s)
be a rational function which is
O(s^{-2})
at infinity. Then

\sum_{\alpha\in O}{\rm Res}(r(s)\xi(s),\alpha)+\sum_{\beta\in S}{\rm Res}(r(s)\xi(s),\beta)=0\,,

where
S
is the set of poles of
r(s)
and
O
is the set of poles of
\xi(s)
that are not poles of
r(s)
. Here
{\rm Res}(h(s),\lambda)
denotes the residue of
h(s)
at
s=\lambda
, and the kernel function
\xi(s)
is meromorphic in the whole complex plane and satisfies
\xi(s)=o(s)
over an infinite collection of circles
|s|=\rho_{k}
with
\rho_{k}\to+\infty
.

3.  Symmetric extension on linear
T
-sums and
\tilde{S}
-sums

3.1.  Main theorem on linear
T
-sums and
\tilde{S}
-sums

Let us consider the symmetric extension of the Kaneko-Tsumura conjecture on the linear
T
-sums and
\tilde{S}
-sums.

Theorem 3.1
.

For integers
m,p\geq 1
and
q\geq 2
, we have

\displaystyle(-1)^{m-1}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}i+j=p-1\\
i,j\geq 0\end{subarray}}\binom{m+i-1}{i}\binom{q+j-1}{j}T_{m+i,q+j}

\displaystyle\quad+(-1)^{p-1}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}i+j=m-1\\
i,j\geq 0\end{subarray}}\binom{p+i-1}{i}\binom{q+j-1}{j}\tilde{S}_{p+i,q+j}\in\mathbb{Q}[\ln(2),\mathrm{zeta\ values}]\,.

(3.1)

In particular, the following expression holds:

\displaystyle(-1)^{m-1}\lambda_{p}(T_{m,q})+(-1)^{p-1}\lambda_{m}(\tilde{S}_{p,q})

\displaystyle\quad=(-1)^{m}\lambda_{p}((-1)^{m}\tilde{t}(m)\tilde{t}(q))+(-1)^{p}\lambda_{m}((-1)^{p}\tilde{t}(p)\zeta(q))-\lambda_{q}(\zeta(m)\tilde{t}(p))\,,

(3.2)

where, by our conventions,
\zeta(1):=-2\ln(2)
and
\tilde{t}(1):=0
wherever they occur.

Proof..

To prove this identity, we consider

\mathcal{G}_{1}(s):=\frac{\varPsi^{(m-1)}(\tfrac{1}{2}-s)\varPsi^{(p-1)}(-s)}{(s+1)^{q}(m-1)!(p-1)!}\,.

The function
\mathcal{G}_{1}(s)
has a pole of order
q
at
s=-1
. By (
2.6
) and (
2.8
), the residue is

{\rm Res}(\mathcal{G}_{1}(s),-1)=(-1)^{m+p}\lambda_{q}(\zeta(m)\tilde{t}(p))\,.

Similarly,
\mathcal{G}_{1}(s)
has poles of order
m
at
s=n
and poles of order
p
at
s=n-1/2
for
n\geq 0
. Then by appealing to the expansions (
2.2
) and (
2.4
), the residues are found to be

{\rm Res}(\mathcal{G}_{1}(s),n)=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}i+j=m-1\\
i,j\geq 0\end{subarray}}(-1)^{p+j}\binom{p+i-1}{i}\binom{q+j-1}{j}\frac{\tilde{t}(p+i)+(-1)^{p+i}h_{n+1}^{(p+i)}}{(n+1)^{q+j}}

and

{\rm Res}(\mathcal{G}_{1}(s),n-\tfrac{1}{2})=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}i+j=p-1\\
i,j\geq 0\end{subarray}}(-1)^{m+j}\binom{m+i-1}{i}\binom{q+j-1}{j}\frac{\tilde{t}(m+i)+(-1)^{m+i}h_{n}^{(m+i)}}{(n+1/2)^{q+j}}\,,

respectively. Hence, combining these three residue results, applying Lemma
2.4
, and using the definitions of
\zeta(s)
,
\tilde{t}(s)
,
T_{p,q}
and
\tilde{S}_{p,q}
, we obtain (
3.2
), which further gives the statement (
3.1
) because
\tilde{t}(s)=(2^{s}-1)\zeta(s)
for integer
s\geq 2
.
∎

In fact, Theorem
3.1
gives an infinite series identity of weight
w=m+p+q-1
. Note that, for odd weights, all the linear
T
-sums
T_{p,q}
and
\tilde{S}
-sums
\tilde{S}_{p,q}
are already reducible to
\ln(2)
and zeta values
[
21
, Corollaries 3.3 and 3.8]
. Therefore, in this case, the statement (
3.1
) is somewhat trivial. However, for even weights, the sums
T_{p,q}
and
\tilde{S}_{p,q}
may be only expressible in terms of (alternating) zeta values and double zeta values (see Eqs. (
1.7
) and (
1.8
)), but Theorem
3.1
asserts that the symmetric sums

(-1)^{m-1}\lambda_{p}(T_{m,q})+(-1)^{p-1}\lambda_{m}(\tilde{S}_{p,q})

are still reducible to
\ln(2)
and zeta values. In Sections
3.2
–
3.5
, we present several special cases and related examples of this theorem.

3.2.  The case of
m=p=1

When
m=p=1
, Theorem
3.1
reduces to the next result.

Corollary 3.2
.

For integer
q\geq 2
, the sums
T_{1,q}+\tilde{S}_{1,q}
are reducible to
\ln(2)
and zeta values:

T_{1,q}+\tilde{S}_{1,q}=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{h_{n-1}}{(n-1/2)^{q}}+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{h_{n}}{n^{q}}=2\ln(2)\tilde{t}(q)-\sum_{j=1}^{q-2}\zeta(q-j)\tilde{t}(j+1)\,.

(3.3)

Example 3.1
.

In Theorem
3.1
, replacing
(m,p,q)
by
(1,1,2)
–
(1,1,5)
yields

\displaystyle T_{1,2}+\tilde{S}_{1,2}=\pi^{2}\ln(2)\,,

\displaystyle T_{1,3}+\tilde{S}_{1,3}=14\ln(2)\zeta(3)-\tfrac{1}{12}\pi^{4}\,,

\displaystyle T_{1,4}+\tilde{S}_{1,4}=-\tfrac{5}{3}\pi^{2}\zeta(3)+\tfrac{1}{3}\pi^{4}\ln(2)\,,

\displaystyle T_{1,5}+\tilde{S}_{1,5}=62\ln(2)\zeta(5)-7\zeta(3)^{2}-\tfrac{1}{30}\pi^{6}\,,

respectively, which correspond to the cases of
q=2,3,4,5
of Corollary
3.2
.
Note that the two ones corresponding to
q=3,5
are of even weights, and the evaluations of the four involved linear
T
-sums and
\tilde{S}
-sums contain the polylogarithm
\,\mathrm{Li}_{4}(\tfrac{1}{2})
and alternating double zeta values
\zeta(\bar{5},1)
:

3.4.  More discussions on the case of
m=q
and
p
odd

It will be interesting to give an explicit characterization for the coefficients of various mathematical constants involved in the reduction of the sums
(-1)^{q-1}\lambda_{p}(T_{q,q})+\lambda_{q}(\tilde{S}_{p,q})
appeared in (
3.4
) in the last section.

To do this, let
B_{n}
be the well-known
Bernoulli numbers
and
G_{n}
be the
Genocchi numbers
, defined by

\frac{t}{\mathrm{e}^{t}-1}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}B_{n}\frac{t^{n}}{n!}\quad\text{and}\quad\frac{2t}{\mathrm{e}^{t}+1}=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}G_{n}\frac{t^{n}}{n!}\,,

respectively (see, for example,
[
4
, Section 1.14]
). Then
G_{n}=2(1-2^{n})B_{n}
for
n\geq 0
, and
B_{2k+1}=G_{2k+1}=0
for
k\geq 1
. As mentioned in Section
1
, the convolution identity (
1.6
) on the Bernoulli numbers and Genocchi numbers can be established, which helps us obtain the next theorem. Note that the proof of identity (
1.6
) will be given in the last section (i.e., Section
5
) of the present paper.

Theorem 3.6
.

For integer
q\geq 2
and odd integer
p\geq 1
, the following explicit expression holds:

\displaystyle(-1)^{q-1}\lambda_{p}(T_{q,q})+\lambda_{q}(\tilde{S}_{p,q})

\displaystyle\quad=(-1)^{q-1}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}i=0\\
q+i\ \mathrm{odd}\end{subarray}}^{p-1}\binom{q+i-1}{i}\binom{p+q-2-i}{p-1-i}\tilde{t}(q+i)\tilde{t}(p+q-1-i)

\displaystyle\quad\quad+\frac{(-1)^{\frac{p-1}{2}}}{8}\binom{p-2+2q}{p-1}\frac{G_{p-1+2q}}{(p-1+2q)!}(2\pi)^{p-1+2q}\,.

(3.5)

Proof..

By appealing to the values of the Riemann zeta function at even positive integers:

\zeta(2k)=\frac{(-1)^{k+1}B_{2k}(2\pi)^{2k}}{2\cdot(2k)!}\,,

the second term on the right of Eq. (
3.4
), abbreviated as
\varOmega_{2}
, can be rewritten as

\displaystyle\varOmega_{2}

\displaystyle=-2\sum_{k=1}^{[\frac{q}{2}]}\binom{p-2+2k}{p-1}\binom{2q-1-2k}{q-2k}(2^{p-1+2k}-1)\zeta(p-1+2k)\zeta(2q-2k)

\displaystyle=\frac{(-1)^{\frac{p-1+2q}{2}}}{2}(2\pi)^{p-1+2q}\sum_{k=1}^{[\frac{q}{2}]}\binom{p-2+2k}{p-1}\binom{2q-1-2k}{q-2k}\frac{(1-2^{p-1+2k})B_{p-1+2k}B_{2q-2k}}{(p-1+2k)!(2q-2k)!}

\displaystyle=\frac{(-1)^{\frac{p-1+2q}{2}}}{4}\frac{(2\pi)^{p-1+2q}}{(p-1)!(2q)!}\binom{2q}{q}q^{2}\sum_{k=1}^{[\frac{q}{2}]}\binom{q-1}{2k-1}\frac{G_{p-1+2k}B_{2q-2k}}{(p-1+2k)(2q-2k)}\,.

(3.6)

By considering the fact
B_{2k+1}=G_{2k+1}=0
for
k\geq 1
, the convolution identity (
1.6
) can be rewritten as

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{[\frac{q}{2}]}\binom{q-1}{2k-1}\frac{G_{p-1+2k}B_{2q-2k}}{(p-1+2k)(2q-2k)}

\displaystyle\quad=\frac{(-1)^{q}}{q\binom{2q}{q}}\frac{G_{p-1+2q}}{p-1+2q}+\frac{1}{4}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}i=0\\
q+i\ \mathrm{even}\end{subarray}}^{p-1}(-1)^{i+1}\binom{p-1}{i}\frac{G_{q+i}G_{p+q-1-i}}{(q+i)(p+q-1-i)}\,,

where
p\geq 1
is odd, and
q\geq 2
. Substituting it into the right of (
3.6
), and using the relation

G_{2k}=2(1-2^{2k})B_{2k}=\frac{(-1)^{k}\cdot 4\tilde{t}(2k)\cdot(2k)!}{(2\pi)^{2k}}\,,

we have

\displaystyle\varOmega_{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{(-1)^{\frac{p-1}{2}}}{8}\binom{p-2+2q}{p-1}\frac{G_{p-1+2q}}{(p-1+2q)!}(2\pi)^{p-1+2q}

\displaystyle\quad\quad-\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}i=0\\
q+i\ \mathrm{even}\end{subarray}}^{p-1}(-1)^{i}\binom{q+i-1}{i}\binom{p+q-2-i}{p-1-i}\tilde{t}(q+i)\tilde{t}(p+q-1-i)\,.

On the other hand, the first term on the right of Eq. (
3.4
) equals

\displaystyle\varOmega_{1}

\displaystyle=(-1)^{q}\lambda_{p}((-1)^{q}\tilde{t}(q)\tilde{t}(q))

\displaystyle=(-1)^{q}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}i+j=p-1\\
i,j\geq 0\end{subarray}}\binom{q+i-1}{i}\binom{q+j-1}{j}(-1)^{q+i}\tilde{t}(q+i)\tilde{t}(q+j)

\displaystyle=\left\{\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}i=0\\
q+i\ \mathrm{odd}\end{subarray}}^{p-1}+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}i=0\\
q+i\ \mathrm{even}\end{subarray}}^{p-1}\right\}(-1)^{i}\binom{q+i-1}{i}\binom{p+q-2-i}{p-1-i}\tilde{t}(q+i)\tilde{t}(p+q-1-i)\,.

Thus, all the summands with even
q+i
will be eliminated in
\varOmega_{1}+\varOmega_{2}
, and we obtain (
3.5
).
∎

By Theorem
3.6
, the coefficients of various mathematical constants in the evaluations of the sums

can be completely determined. For example, setting

-values, linear
T
-sums and linear
\tilde{S}
-sums, from which we can further show a reduction property of the double
t
-values and linear
T
-sums.

Corollary 3.11
.

For any even weight
w:=m+q
, where
m,q\geq 2
, the double
t
-values
t(q,m)
and linear
T
-sums
T_{m,q}
are expressible in terms of MZVs.

Proof..

According to the Kaneko-Tsumura conjecture (
1.2
) (see also
[
18
, Theorem 42]
), for integers
m,q\geq 2
and
p\geq 1
, with
m+p+q-1
even, the sums
\lambda_{m}(T(q,p))\in\mathcal{Z}
, so
\lambda_{m}(\tilde{S}_{p,q})\in\mathcal{Z}
by (
3.9
). On the other hand, if
m,q\geq 2
, we have

(-1)^{m-1}2^{m+p+q-1}\lambda_{p}(t(q,m))+(-1)^{p-1}2^{m+p+q-3}\lambda_{m}(T(q,p))\in\mathbb{Q}[\mathrm{zeta\ values}]\,,

and
\ln(2)
does not appear in the evaluations of the sums on the left. Thus, when
m,q\geq 2
,
p\geq 1
, and
m+p+q-1
is even, we have

\lambda_{p}(t(q,m)),\ \lambda_{p}(T_{m,q})\in\mathcal{Z}\,.

(3.10)

The final assertion of this corollary follows by setting
p=1
.
∎

Remark 3.12
.

Corollary
3.11
can be compared with
[
21
, Corollary 3.3]
, where the latter shows that
T_{p,q}
and
t(q,p)
are reducible to
\ln(2)
and zeta values if
p\geq 1
,
q\geq 2
, and
p+q
is odd. Corollary
3.11
is also a special case of Murakami’s recent result
[
18
, Theorem 1]
, which shows that when all
s_{j}\geq 2
, the MtVs
t(s_{1},\ldots,s_{k})
are expressible in terms of MZVs. ∎

4.  Symmetric extension on linear
R
-sums

4.1.  Main theorem on linear
R
-sums

In this section, we present a symmetric extension of the Kaneko-Tsumura conjecture (
1.2
) on the linear
R
-sums:

R_{p,q}:=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{H_{n-1}^{(p)}}{(n-1/2)^{q}}\,,\quad\text{for }q\geq 2\,.

Theorem 4.1
.

For integers
m,p\geq 1
and
q\geq 2
, we have

\displaystyle(-1)^{m-1}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}i+j=p-1\\
i,j\geq 0\end{subarray}}\binom{m+i-1}{i}\binom{q+j-1}{j}R_{m+i,q+j}

\displaystyle\quad+(-1)^{p-1}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}i+j=m-1\\
i,j\geq 0\end{subarray}}\binom{p+i-1}{i}\binom{q+j-1}{j}R_{p+i,q+j}\in\mathbb{Q}[\ln(2),\mathrm{zeta\ values}]\,.

(4.1)

In particular, the following expression holds:

\displaystyle(-1)^{m-1}\lambda_{p}(R_{m,q})+(-1)^{p-1}\lambda_{m}(R_{p,q})

\displaystyle\quad=\binom{m+p+q-2}{q-1}\tilde{t}(m+p+q-1)+(-1)^{m}\lambda_{p}((-1)^{m}\zeta(m)\tilde{t}(q))

\displaystyle\quad\quad+(-1)^{p}\lambda_{m}((-1)^{p}\zeta(p)\tilde{t}(q))-\lambda_{q}(\tilde{t}(m)\tilde{t}(p))\,,

(4.2)

where, by our conventions,
\zeta(1):=-2\ln(2)
and
\tilde{t}(1):=0
wherever they occur.

Proof..

The proof of this theorem is similar to that of Theorem
3.1
. Now, consider the function

\mathcal{G}_{2}(s):=\frac{\varPsi^{(m-1)}(\tfrac{1}{2}-s)\varPsi^{(p-1)}(\tfrac{1}{2}-s)}{(s+\tfrac{1}{2})^{q}(m-1)!(p-1)!}\,.

It is obvious that the only singularities are poles at
s=-1/2
and
s=n
for
n\geq 0
. By (
2.4
), the pole at
-1/2
has order
q
, and the residue is

{\rm Res}(\mathcal{G}_{2}(s),-\tfrac{1}{2})=(-1)^{m+p}\lambda_{q}(\tilde{t}(m)\tilde{t}(p))\,.

Next, by (
2.2
), the pole at a nonnegative integer
n
has order
m+p
, and the residue is

\displaystyle{\rm Res}(\mathcal{G}_{2}(s),n)

\displaystyle\quad=(-1)^{m+p-1}\binom{m+p+q-2}{q-1}\frac{1}{(n+\tfrac{1}{2})^{m+p+q-1}}

\displaystyle\quad\quad+(-1)^{m+p-1}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}i+j=p-1\\
i,j\geq 0\end{subarray}}\binom{m+i-1}{i}\binom{q+j-1}{j}\frac{(-1)^{i}\zeta(m+i)+(-1)^{m}H_{n}^{(m+i)}}{(n+\tfrac{1}{2})^{q+j}}

\displaystyle\quad\quad+(-1)^{m+p-1}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}i+j=m-1\\
i,j\geq 0\end{subarray}}\binom{p+i-1}{i}\binom{q+j-1}{j}\frac{(-1)^{i}\zeta(p+i)+(-1)^{p}H_{n}^{(p+i)}}{(n+\tfrac{1}{2})^{q+j}}\,.

Hence, summing these two contributions, considering the definitions of
\tilde{t}(s)
and
R_{p,q}
, and doing some transformations, we obtain the desired formula (
4.2
), and therefore the statement.
∎

Now, let us briefly discuss some special cases of this theorem. Setting
m=p=1
in Eq. (
4.2
) yields an expression of the sums
R_{1,q}
, and an alternate way to obtain this expression is to set
a=-1/2
in
[
24
, Theorem 3.2]
.

Corollary 4.2
.

For integer
q\geq 2
, the linear sums
R_{1,q}
are reducible to
\ln(2)
and zeta values:

R_{1,q}=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{H_{n-1}}{(n-1/2)^{q}}=\frac{q}{2}\tilde{t}(q+1)-2\ln(2)\tilde{t}(q)-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{j=1}^{q-2}\tilde{t}(q-j)\tilde{t}(j+1)\,.

(4.3)

Example 4.1
.

The first few sums are

\displaystyle R_{1,2}=7\zeta(3)-\pi^{2}\ln(2)\,,

\displaystyle R_{1,3}=-14\ln(2)\zeta(3)+\tfrac{1}{8}\pi^{4}\,,

\displaystyle R_{1,4}=62\zeta(5)-\tfrac{7}{2}\pi^{2}\zeta(3)-\tfrac{1}{3}\pi^{4}\ln(2)\,,

\displaystyle R_{1,5}=-62\ln(2)\zeta(5)-\tfrac{49}{2}\zeta(3)^{2}+\tfrac{1}{12}\pi^{6}\,,

which can also be computed from Theorem
4.1
by replacing
(m,p,q)
by
(1,1,2)
–
(1,1,5)
. The values of more sums can be obtained by specifying the parameter
q
directly. ∎

More generally, setting
m=p\geq 2
in Eq. (
4.2
), we have

Corollary 4.3
.

For integers
p,q\geq 2
, the sums
\lambda_{p}(R_{p,q})
are reducible to zeta values:

\displaystyle\lambda_{p}(R_{p,q})

\displaystyle=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}i+j=p-1\\
i,j\geq 0\end{subarray}}\binom{p+i-1}{i}\binom{q+j-1}{j}R_{p+i,q+j}

\displaystyle=\frac{(-1)^{p-1}}{2}\left\{\binom{2p+q-2}{q-1}\tilde{t}(2p+q-1)-\lambda_{q}(\tilde{t}(p)\tilde{t}(p))\right\}-\lambda_{p}((-1)^{p}\zeta(p)\tilde{t}(q))\,.

As illustrated in
[
21
, Eq. (3.9)]
and
[

, Corollary 3.2]
, besides the linear sums
R_{1,q}
, the sums

with

odd are also reducible to
\ln(2)
and zeta values. Therefore, we show here some more special cases of Theorem
4.1
and Corollary
4.3
with even weights.

Example 4.2

Setting

by
(2,2,3)
,

in Theorem

or Corollary
4.3

\displaystyle 3R_{2,4}+2R_{3,3}=3\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{H_{n-1}^{(2)}}{(n-1/2)^{4}}+2\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{H_{n-1}^{(3)}}{(n-1/2)^{3}}=112\zeta(3)^{2}-\tfrac{1}{6}\pi^{6}\,,

\displaystyle 5R_{2,6}+2R_{3,5}=1798\zeta(3)\zeta(5)-\tfrac{17}{72}\pi^{8}\,.

However, by (

\displaystyle R_{3,3}=-192\zeta(\bar{5},1)+\tfrac{109}{2}\zeta(3)^{2}-\tfrac{8}{105}\pi^{6}\,,

\displaystyle R_{2,6}=768\zeta(\bar{7},1)+289\zeta(6,2)-864\zeta(3)\zeta(5)+\tfrac{59}{525}\pi^{8}\,,

\displaystyle R_{3,5}=-1920\zeta(\bar{7},1)-\tfrac{1445}{2}\zeta(6,2)+3059\zeta(3)\zeta(5)-\tfrac{2011}{5040}\pi^{8}\,.

.

4.2.  Symmetric sum on double

.

(-1)^{m}\lambda_{p}(T(m,q))+(-1)^{p}\lambda_{m}(T(p,q))\in\mathbb{Q}[\mathrm{zeta\ values}].

In particular, the following expression holds:

\displaystyle 2^{m+p+q-3}\{(-1)^{m}\lambda_{p}(T(m,q))+(-1)^{p}\lambda_{m}(T(p,q))\}

(4.4)

further yields the next corollary.

For integers

\displaystyle\lambda_{p}(T(p,q))

5.  Proof of the required convolution identity

In this section, we give the proof of the convolution identity (
1.6
) on the Bernoulli numbers and Genocchi numbers used in the discussions of Section
3.4
.

Firstly, it can be found that there are polynomials
P_{n}(y)
of degree
n+1
with integer coefficients, so that

P_{n}(\tanh(t))=\mathcal{D}_{t}^{n}\tanh(t)\,,\quad\text{for }n=0,1,2,\ldots,

where
\mathcal{D}_{t}
is the derivative operator defined by
\mathcal{D}_{t}f(t)=f^{\prime}(t)
. In particular,

P_{0}(y)=y\,,\quad P_{1}(y)=1-y^{2}\,,\quad P_{2}(y)=-2y+2y^{3}\,,\quad P_{3}(y)=-2+8y^{2}-6y^{4}\,,

and

P_{n+1}(y)=(1-y^{2})P_{n}^{\prime}(y)\,,\quad\text{for }n\geq 0\,.

These polynomials are called the
derivative polynomials of hyperbolic tangent
. Note that the higher derivatives of
\coth(t)
are formed in the same pattern as those of
\tanh(t)
. Therefore,
P_{n}(y)
are also the derivative polynomials of
\coth(t)
.

The concepts of derivative polynomials for tangent and secant were introduced by Hoffman
[
12
,
11
]
, but the study of these polynomials goes back to Knuth and Buckholtz
[
15
]
, and Krishnamachary and Bhimasena Rao
[
16
]
. More results on these polynomials as well as their hyperbolic analogs can be found in, for example, the papers due to Boyadzhiev
[
2
]
, Chu and Wang
[
3
]
, Cvijović
[
5
]
, Hetyei
[
9
]
, and Ma
[
17
]
.

Next, from the series expansion of the hyperbolic tangent, we have

\tanh(t)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{2^{2n}(2^{2n}-1)B_{2n}t^{2n-1}}{(2n)!}=-\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}G_{2n}\frac{(2t)^{2n-1}}{(2n)!}=1-\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{G_{k+1}}{k+1}\frac{(2t)^{k}}{k!}\,,

which further gives

P_{n}(\tanh(t))=-\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{G_{k+n+1}}{k+n+1}\frac{2^{k+n}t^{k}}{k!}+\delta_{n,0}\,,

(5.1)

where
\delta_{n,k}
is the Kronecker delta. Chu and Wang
[
3
, Section 2.4]
used the method of linearization to establish the expansion

P_{m}(y)P_{n}(y)=-\rho_{m,n}^{(0)}P_{m+n+1}(y)-\sum_{k=1}^{[(m+n)/2]}\rho_{m,n}^{(k)}\frac{2^{2k}B_{2k}}{2k}P_{m+n+1-2k}(y)+\chi(m=n=0)\,,

where
\chi
is used in place of the Iverson bracket, and defined by

\chi(\text{true})=1\quad\text{and}\quad\chi(\text{false})=0\,,

and

\rho_{m,n}^{(k)}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{cc}\displaystyle(-1)^{m}\binom{n}{m+n+1-2k}+(-1)^{n}\binom{m}{m+n+1-2k}\,,&k\geq 1\,,\\[8.61108pt]
\displaystyle\frac{m!n!}{(m+n+1)!}\,,&k=0\,,\end{array}\right.

(5.2)

with
m,n\geq 0
. Then

\displaystyle[t^{l}]P_{m}(\tanh(t))P_{n}(\tanh(t))

\displaystyle\quad=\frac{2^{l+m+n+1}}{l!}\left\{\rho_{m,n}^{(0)}\frac{G_{l+m+n+2}}{l+m+n+2}+\sum_{k=1}^{[(m+n)/2]}\rho_{m,n}^{(k)}\frac{B_{2k}G_{l+m+n-2k+2}}{(2k)(l+m+n-2k+2)}\right\}

\displaystyle\quad\quad+\chi(l=m=n=0)\,,

(5.3)

and the following theorem can be established.

Theorem 5.1
.

For integers
n,\alpha,\gamma\geq 0
and
\delta,\varepsilon=0,1
, there holds the convolution identity

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{n}\binom{2n+2-\delta-\varepsilon}{2k+1-\delta}\frac{G_{2k+2\alpha+2}}{k+\alpha+1}\frac{G_{2n-2k+2\gamma+2}}{n-k+\gamma+1}

\displaystyle\quad=4\rho_{2\alpha+\delta,2\gamma+\varepsilon}^{(0)}\frac{G_{2n+2\alpha+2\gamma+4}}{n+\alpha+\gamma+2}+2\sum_{k=1}^{\alpha+\gamma+[(\delta+\varepsilon)/2]}\rho_{2\alpha+\delta,2\gamma+\varepsilon}^{(k)}\frac{B_{2k}}{k}\frac{G_{2n-2k+2\alpha+2\gamma+4}}{n-k+\alpha+\gamma+2}\,,

where
\rho_{m,n}^{(k)}
is defined in (
5.2
).

Proof..

Using Eq. (
5.1
), and doing some elementary reduction, we have

P_{2\alpha+\delta}(\tanh(t))=-\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{G_{2k+2\alpha+2}}{k+\alpha+1}\frac{2^{2k+2\alpha}t^{2k+1-\delta}}{(2k+1-\delta)!}\,,

for
\alpha\geq 0
and
\delta=0,1
. Applying Eq. (
5.3
) to

[t^{2n+2-\delta-\varepsilon}]P_{2\alpha+\delta}(\tanh(t))P_{2\gamma+\varepsilon}(\tanh(t))
,
and considering the above expansion as well as the fact

\chi(2n+2-\delta-\varepsilon=2\alpha+\delta=2\gamma+\varepsilon=0)=0\,,

we obtain the desired convolution formula.
∎

Finally, the identity (
1.6
) can be verified by combining Theorem
5.1
with Eq. (
5.3
).

Theorem 5.2
.

For integers
n\geq 0
and
q\geq 2
, there holds the convolution identity

\sum_{i=0}^{q-1}\binom{q-1}{i}\frac{B_{q+i}G_{2n+q-i}}{(q+i)(2n+q-i)}+\frac{1}{4}\sum_{i=0}^{2n}(-1)^{i}\binom{2n}{i}\frac{G_{q+i}G_{2n+q-i}}{(q+i)(2n+q-i)}=\frac{(-1)^{q}}{q\binom{2q}{q}}\frac{G_{2n+2q}}{2n+2q}\,.

Proof..

Let us prove Theorem
5.2
according to the parity of the parameter
q
. In Theorem
5.1
, setting
\alpha=\gamma=l
with
l\geq 0
and
\delta=\varepsilon=1
, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{n}\binom{2n}{2k}\frac{G_{2k+2l+2}G_{2n-2k+2l+2}}{(k+l+1)(n-k+l+1)}

\displaystyle\quad=\frac{4}{(4l+3)\binom{4l+2}{2l+1}}\frac{G_{2n+4l+4}}{n+2l+2}-4\sum_{k=1}^{2l+1}\binom{2l+1}{4l+3-2k}\frac{B_{2k}G_{2n-2k+4l+4}}{k(n-k+2l+2)}

\displaystyle\quad=\frac{4}{(l+1)\binom{4l+4}{2l+2}}\frac{G_{2n+4l+4}}{n+2l+2}-4\sum_{k=0}^{l}\binom{2l+1}{2k}\frac{B_{2k+2l+2}G_{2n-2k+2l+2}}{(k+l+1)(n-k+l+1)}\,,

for
n,l\geq 0
, which is just the
q=2l+2
case of Theorem
5.2
. Similarly, in Theorem
5.1
, let
\alpha=\gamma=l+1
with
l\geq 0
and
\delta=\varepsilon=0
, and do some transformation. Then we can show that when
q=2l+3
and
n\geq 1
, Theorem
5.2
is still true. Hence, it suffices to show that when
q=2l+3
and
n=0
, the result holds. In fact, according to (
5.1
), we have

[t^{0}]P_{n}(\tanh(t))=-\frac{2^{n}G_{n+1}}{n+1}+\delta_{n,0}\,.

Then by setting
m=n=2l+2
, for
l\geq 0
, we obtain from (
5.3
) that

[t^{0}]P_{2l+2}(\tanh(t))^{2}=0=2^{4l+5}\left\{\rho_{2l+2,2l+2}^{(0)}\frac{G_{4l+6}}{4l+6}+\sum_{k=1}^{2l+2}\rho_{2l+2,2l+2}^{(k)}\frac{B_{2k}G_{4l-2k+6}}{(2k)(4l-2k+6)}\right\}\,.

This further gives

\sum_{k=0}^{l}\binom{2l+2}{2k+1}\frac{B_{2l+2k+4}G_{2l-2k+2}}{(l+k+2)(l-k+1)}=-\frac{2}{(2l+3)\binom{4l+6}{2l+3}}\frac{G_{4l+6}}{2l+3}\,,

(5.4)

which is just the
q=2l+3
and
n=0
case of the desired result.
∎

Example 5.1
.

In Theorem
5.2
, setting further
n=0,1
gives

\displaystyle\sum_{i=0}^{q-1}\binom{q-1}{i}\frac{B_{q+i}G_{q-i}}{(q+i)(q-i)}=\frac{(-1)^{q}}{q\binom{2q}{q}}\frac{G_{2q}}{2q}-\frac{G_{q}^{2}}{4q^{2}}\,,

\displaystyle\sum_{i=0}^{q-1}\binom{q-1}{i}\frac{B_{q+i}G_{q+2-i}}{(q+i)(q+2-i)}=\frac{(-1)^{q}}{q\binom{2q}{q}}\frac{G_{2q+2}}{2q+2}-\frac{G_{q}G_{q+2}}{2q(q+2)}+\frac{G_{q+1}^{2}}{2(q+1)^{2}}\,,

for
q\geq 2
; while setting
q=3,4
yields

\displaystyle\sum_{i=0}^{2n}(-1)^{i}\binom{2n}{i}\frac{G_{i+3}G_{2n+3-i}}{(i+3)(2n+3-i)}=-\frac{G_{2n+6}}{15(2n+6)}+\frac{G_{2n+2}}{15(2n+2)}\,,

\displaystyle\sum_{i=0}^{2n}(-1)^{i}\binom{2n}{i}\frac{G_{i+4}G_{2n+4-i}}{(i+4)(2n+4-i)}=\frac{G_{2n+8}}{70(2n+8)}+\frac{G_{2n+4}}{30(2n+4)}-\frac{G_{2n+2}}{21(2n+2)}\,,

for
n\geq 0
. More special cases can be obtained from Theorems
5.1
and
5.2
by specifying the parameters. ∎

New monotonicity for p p -capacitary functions in 3 3 -manifolds with nonnegative scalar curvature Thanks: CX is supported by the NSFC (Grant No. 12271449). JY is supported by the NSFC (Grant No. 12201138) and Mathematics Tianyuan fund project (Grant No. 12226350).
Chao Xia, Jiabin Yin, Xingjian Zhou

Abstract
. In this paper, we derive general monotone quantities and geometric inequalities associated with p p -capacitary functions in asymptotically flat 3 3 -manifolds with simple topology and nonnegative scalar curvature. The inequalities become equalities on the spatial Schwarzschild manifolds outside rotationally symmetric spheres. This generalizes Miao’s result [ 27 ] from p = 2 p=2 to p ∈ ( 1 , 3 ) p\in(1,3) . As applications, we recover mass-to- p p -capacity and p p -capacity-to-area inequalities due to Bray-Miao [ 14 ] and Xiao [ 31 ] .



1.
Introduction

For an asymptotically flat
3
-manifold
(M,g)
with outmost minimal boundary and non-negative scalar curvature, the Riemannian-Penrose inequality says that its ADM mass

(1.1)

\displaystyle\mathfrak{m}_{ADM}(M)\geq\sqrt{\frac{|\partial M|}{16\pi}},

with equality holding if and only if
(M,g)
is a spatial Schwarzschild manifold. It was first proved in the case of connected boundary by Huisken-Ilmanen
[
22
]
via establishing a Geroch-type monotonicity formula for a level set formulation of inverse mean curvature flow. The general case and the higher dimensional inequality in dimensions less than eight was proved by Bray
[
10
]
and Bray-Lee
[
13
]
via a conformal flow of metrics. Recently, Agostiniani-Mantegazza-Mazzieri-Oronzio
[
4
]
gave a new proof of the 3-dimensional Riemannian-Penrose inequality in the case of connected boundary via establishing monotonicity formula for
p
-harmonic functions. Before that, Bray-Kazaras-Khuri-Stern
[
12
]
and Agostiniani-Mazzieri-Oronzio
[
5
]
also gave two new proofs of the Riemannian positive mass theorem via establishing various monotonicity formulas for harmonic functions.

On the other hand, Bray
[
10
]
applied the Riemannian positive mass theorem to prove a mass-capacity inequality

\mathfrak{m}_{ADM}\geq{\rm Cap}(\partial M)

for asymptotically flat
3
-manifolds with minimal boundary, where
{\rm Cap}(\partial M)
is the capacity of
\partial M
in
M
. Later, Bray-Miao
[
27
]
extended Bray’s mass-capacity inequality to manifolds with nonnegative Hawking mass and connected boundary that is not necessary minimal,

(1.2)

\displaystyle\frac{\mathfrak{m}_{ADM}(M)}{{\rm Cap}(\partial M)}\geq 1-\left(\frac{1}{16\pi}\int_{\partial M}H^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}},

and Xiao
[
31
]
extended Bray-Miao’s result to the
p
-capacity.

Recently, inequality (
1.2
) was shown by Miao
[
27
]
without the assumption of nonnegative Hawking mass, which offers another proof of 3-dim PMT, see Miao
[
28
]
. To prove that, Miao
[
27
]
found several monotone quantities associated to capacitary functions on
3
-manifolds with simple topology and non-negative scalar curvature, by which he established the following geometric inequalities

(1.3)

\displaystyle 4\pi+\int_{\partial M}H|\nabla u|\geq 3\int_{\partial M}|\nabla u|^{2},

(1.4)

\displaystyle 4\pi-\int_{\partial M}|\nabla u|^{2}\leq 4\pi\frac{\mathfrak{m}_{ADM}(M)}{{\rm Cap}(\partial M)},

where
u
is the capacitary function on
M
and
H
is the mean curvature. Moreover, equality in both (
1.3
) and (
1.4
) holds if and only if
(M,g)
is isometric to
\mathbb{R}^{3}
minus a round ball, see
[
27
, Theorems 3.1 and 3.2]
. Inspired by Bray’s work
[
10
]
, by using a conformal metric which preserves the nonnegativity of scalar curvature and the harmonicity of a function, Miao
[
27
]
promoted the above geometric inequalities to the following ones: for
l>0
,

(1.5)

\displaystyle 4\pi+l\int_{\partial M}H|\nabla u|\geq l(4-l)\int_{\partial M}|\nabla u|^{2},

(1.6)

\displaystyle 4\pi\left(2-\frac{1}{l}\right)-l\int_{\partial M}|\nabla u|^{2}\leq 4\pi\frac{\mathfrak{m}_{ADM}(M)}{{\rm Cap}(\partial M)},

which become equalities in a spatial Schwarzschild manifold outside a rotationally symmetric sphere, see
[
27
, (7.10), (7.27)]
. Similar monotone quantities and geometric inequalities on capacitary functions have been also found by Oronzio
[
29
]
independently. We also remark that various monotone quantities for Green’s functions and
p
-harmonic Green’s
functions on
3
-manifolds with non-negative scalar curvature and their applications were studied by Agostiniani-Mazzieri-Oronzio
[
5
]
, Munteanu-Wang
[
26
]
and Chan-Chu-Lee-Tsang
[
15
]
respectively. These works were inspired and preceded by their harmonic counterparts
[
2
,
17
,
18
]
, starting with Colding’s breakthrough
[
16
]
.

Continuing on Miao’s work
[
27
]
, Hirsch-Miao-Tam
[
24
]
also found several monotone quantities associated to
p
-capacitary functions on
3
-manifolds with simple topology and non-negative scalar curvature, by which they established geometric inequalities associated to
p
-capacitary functions in the same spirit of (
1.3
) and (
1.4
), whose model space is
\mathbb{R}^{3}
minus a round ball. Before that, Agostiniani-Mantegazza-Mazzieri-Oronzio
[
4
]
also derives a similar monotone quantity associated to
p
-capacitary functions, aiming at Riemannian-Penrose inequality as we mentioned at the beginning.
However, there seems no corresponding conformal metric which preserves the
p
-harmonicity and it is not known whether Hirsch-Miao-Tam’s monotone quantities can be promoted to the ones which become equality in a spatial Schwarzschild manifold.

In this paper, we give an affirmative answer to this question, that is, we find general monotone quantities associated to
p
-capacitary functions whose model space is a spatial Schwarzschild manifold outside a rotationally symmetric sphere. In order to state our main result, we first introduce several preliminaries.

A
3
-dimensional Riemannian manifold
(M,g)
is said to be a one-end asymptotically flat if there is a compact subset
K
of
M
and a diffeomorphism
\Phi:M\setminus K\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{3}\setminus\overline{\Omega}
, where
\overline{\Omega}
is a compact subset of
\mathbb{R}^{3}
and with respect to the standard coordinates on
\mathbb{R}^{3}
,
g
satisfies

g_{ij}=\delta_{ij}+\sigma_{ij},

where
\sigma
is a symmetric
(0,2)-
tensor such that
\sigma=O_{2}\left(|x|^{-\tau}\right)
for some
\tau>\frac{1}{2}
, which means that

(1.7)

\partial_{J}\sigma=O\left(|x|^{-\tau-|J|}\right)\ \ \text{as}\ x\rightarrow\infty\ \text{for every multi-index}\ J\ \text{with}\ |J|\leq 2.

The ADM mass of
(M,g)
, which has been introduced by the physicists Arnowitt, Deser and Misner in
[
6
]
, is defined by

(1.8)

\mathfrak{m}_{ADM}:=\frac{1}{16\pi}\lim\limits_{r\rightarrow\infty}\int_{S_{r}}\left(\partial_{j}g_{ij}-\partial_{i}g_{jj}\right)\frac{x^{i}}{|x|}dA_{\bar{g}},

where
S_{r}=\{|x|=r\}
and
dA_{\bar{g}}
is the volume form induced from the Euclidean metric.
The scalar curvature
R
of
(M,g)
is assumed to be integrable so that the ADM mass of
(M,g)
exists and its value does not depend on the asymptotically flat coordinate chart, see
[
7
]
.

Given
m>0
, the spatial Schwarzschild manifold of ADM mass
m
is the
3
-manifold with boundary given by
(\mathcal{M}_{m}^{3},g_{m})
, where

(1.9)

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}_{m}^{3}:=\mathbb{R}^{3}\setminus{B_{\frac{m}{2}}(0)},\quad g_{m}:=\left(1+\frac{m}{2|x|}\right)^{4}\bar{g},

where
\bar{g}
is the Euclidean metric.
Given
r_{0}\geq\frac{m}{2}
, we use
(\mathcal{M}_{m,r_{0}}^{3},g_{m})
to denote the spatial Schwarzschild manifold of mass
m
outside a rotationally symmetric sphere,

(1.10)

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}_{m,r_{0}}^{3}:=\mathbb{R}^{3}\setminus{B_{r_{0}}(0)},\quad g_{m}=\left(1+\frac{m}{2|x|}\right)^{4}\bar{g}.

In the case
r_{0}=\frac{m}{2}
, it reduces to the spatial Schwarzschild manifold with horizon boundary, namely,
\partial\mathcal{M}_{m,\frac{m}{2}}=\partial\mathcal{M}_{m}
is a outmost minimal surface.

Let
(M,g)
be a one-end asymptotically flat
3
-manifold with boundary
\partial M=\Sigma
. For
p\in(1,3)
, let
u\in W^{1,p}_{loc}(M)
be the weak solution to

(1.11)

\left\{\begin{aligned} \Delta_{p}u:={\rm div}(|\nabla u|^{p-2}\nabla u)&=0\ \ {\rm in}\ \ M\\
u&=0\ \ {\rm on}\ \ \partial M\\
u(x)&\rightarrow 1\ \ {\rm as}\ \ |x|\rightarrow\infty.\end{aligned}\right.

The
p-
capacity of
\Sigma
in
(M,g)
is defined by

\displaystyle\operatorname{Cap}_{p}(\partial M):=\inf\left\{\int_{M}|\nabla\phi|^{p}\right\},

where the infimum is taken over all Lipschitz functions
\phi
with compact support such that
\phi=1
at
\Sigma
.
It is clear that
\operatorname{Cap}_{p}(\partial M)
is related to the solution
u
by

\displaystyle\operatorname{Cap}_{p}(\partial M)=\int_{M}|\nabla u|^{p}=\int_{\{u=t\}}|\nabla u|^{p-1},

when
\{u=t\}
is a regular level set of
u
.
We denote

\displaystyle a:=\frac{3-p}{p-1},\quad\hbox{ and }\quad\mathfrak{c}_{p}:=\left(\frac{\operatorname{Cap}_{p}}{4\pi}\right)^{\frac{1}{p-1}}.

It is known that,
u
has an asymptotic expansion (See
[
25
]
,
[
19
, Theorem 4.1]
)

(1.12)

u=1-\frac{\mathfrak{c}_{p}}{a}r^{-a}+O_{2}\left(r^{-a-\tilde{\tau}}\right),\quad\text{as}\ r=|x|\rightarrow\infty,

for any
0<\tilde{\tau}<\min\{\tau,1\}
.

Our main result is as following.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
(M,g)
be a
3
-dimensional, complete, one-end asymptotically flat manifold with boundary
\partial M=\Sigma
. Assume that
\Sigma
is connected and
H_{2}(M,\Sigma)=0
and
(M,g)
has nonnegative scalar curvature. Let
p\in(1,3)
and
u
be the weak solution to (
1.11
).
For any
k\in(-1,1)
,
denote

m:=2\operatorname{sgn}(k)\left(I_{a}(k)\mathfrak{c}_{p}\right)^{\frac{1}{a}},\hbox{ and }r_{0}:=\frac{m}{2k}=|k|^{-1}\left(I_{a}(k)\mathfrak{c}_{p}\right)^{\frac{1}{a}},

where
a:=\frac{3-p}{p-1}
,
\operatorname{sgn}(k)
is the sign function on
k
and

I_{a}(k):=\int_{0}^{|k|}s^{a-1}(1+\operatorname{sgn}(k)s)^{-2a}ds.

Then the following inequalities hold:

(1.13)

\displaystyle 4\pi-\frac{(1+k)^{2}}{(1-k)^{2}}(\eta(r_{0}))^{2}\int_{\Sigma}|\nabla u|^{2}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\frac{(1+k)^{2}r_{0}}{\eta(r_{0})}\frac{(1-a\eta(r_{0}))}{m}\left\{4\pi\frac{(1-k)^{2}}{(1+k)^{2}}-\int_{\Sigma}(\frac{H}{2})^{2}+\int_{\Sigma}\left(\frac{H}{2}-\eta(r_{0})|\nabla u|\right)^{2}\right\},

(1.14)

\displaystyle 4\pi-\frac{(1+k)^{2}}{(1-k)^{2}}(\eta(r_{0}))^{2}\int_{\Sigma}|\nabla u|^{2}\leq 8\pi a\left(\mathfrak{m}_{ADM}-m\right)\frac{(1-a\eta(r_{0}))}{m}.

Here
\eta(t)
is a function defined by (
1.22
) below and

\eta(r_{0})=\mathfrak{c}_{p}^{-1}r_{0}^{a}\left(1+k\right)^{2a-1}\left(1-k\right).

Moreover, equality in each of the above inequalities holds for some
k
if and only if
(M,g)
is isometric to either the spatial Schwarzschild manifold of mass
m
outside a rotationally symmetric ball,
(\mathcal{M}_{m,r_{0}}^{3},g_{m})
with
m=2r_{0}k>0
or the Euclidean space outside a rotationally symmetric ball
(\mathbb{R}^{3}\setminus B_{r_{0}}(0),\delta)
.

Remark 1.1
.

When
k=0
, the quantities are understood in the limit sense, that is,

I_{a}(0)=0,\quad m=0,\quad r_{0}=\left(\frac{\mathfrak{c}_{p}}{a}\right)^{\frac{1}{a}},\quad\eta(r_{0})=\frac{1}{a},\quad\frac{1-a\eta(r_{0})}{m}=\lim_{k\to 0}\frac{1-a\eta(r_{0})}{m}=\frac{a^{2}}{a+1}\left(\frac{\mathfrak{c}_{p}}{a}\right)^{-\frac{1}{a}}.

When
-1<k<0
, equality holds for
(\mathcal{M}_{m,r_{0}}^{3},g_{m})
with
m<0
. This is the so-called Schwarzschild ZAS metric, which has cone-type singularities. See Bray-Jauregui
[
11
]
and Miao
[
28
]
for more discussion on this metric. Hence, for our theorem, equality cannot occur for
-1<k<0
.

When
k=1
, we may regard
r_{0}=\frac{m}{2}
and
\frac{\eta(r_{0})}{1-k}=I_{a}(1)2^{2a-1}
in Theorem
1.1
so that we have the following.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
(M,g)
and
u
be as in Theorem
1.1
. Denote
m=2\left(I_{a}(1)\mathfrak{c}_{p}\right)^{\frac{1}{a}}.

Then we have

(1.15)

\displaystyle 4\pi+2\int_{\Sigma}H|\nabla u|-2^{4a}(I_{a}(1))^{2}\int_{\Sigma}|\nabla u|^{2}\geq 0,

(1.16)

\displaystyle 4\pi(1+2a)-2^{4a}(I_{a}(1))^{2}\int_{\Sigma}|\nabla u|^{2}\leq 8\pi a\frac{\mathfrak{m}_{ADM}}{m}.

Moreover, equality in each of the above inequalities holds if and only if
(M,g)
is isometric to the spatial Schwarzschild manifold of mass

,
(\mathcal{M}_{m}^{3},g_{m})
.

Next we make some comments on our approach to find the monotone quantities. As we have mentioned before, in a series of previous significant works
[
5
,
4
,
27
,
29
,
24
]
, various monotone quantities associated with
p
-capacitary functions have been found in
3
-mainfolds with nonnegative scalar curvature. These works share a common feature that when a first explicit monotone quantity has been found, some other monotone quantities might be found by making use of the first explicit quantity as well as its asymptotic behavior. Our paper gives a new perspective. We consider general quantities with undetermined coefficients associated with Schwarzschild manifold model. The monotonicity of the general quantities can be verified if the undetermined coefficients satisfy a system of ODEs. We solve out the system of ODEs so that we get all the possible monotone quantities and subsequently employ them to deduce new results which generalized Miao
[
27
]
and Oronzio’s
[
29
]
results in the case
p=2
.

The rest of our paper is organized as follows. In Section 2, we study the
p
-capacitary function in a spatial Schwarzschild manifold outside a rotationally symmetric sphere. In Sections 3 and 4, we prove the monotonicity and rigidity part in Theorem
1.5
respectively. In Section 5, we study the asymptotic behavior for the monotone quantities and in Section 6, we given some applications. In the Appendix, we solve out the solutions to a system of ODEs that appears in the proof of monotonicity.

be the spatial Schwarzschild manifold of mass
m
outside a rotationally symmetric sphere of mass

can be taken to be a negative real number, we still call it the Schwarzschild manifold (See Bray-Jauregui
[
11
]
and Miao
[

p
-capacitary function in
(\mathcal{M}_{m,r_{0}}^{3},g_{m})

Proposition 2.1
.

Let
(\mathcal{M}_{m,r_{0}}^{3},g_{m})
be the spatial Schwarzschild manifold of mass
m
outside a rotationally symmetric sphere given by (
1.9
) and
u
be the solution to (
1.11
). Then
u(x)=f_{m,r_{0}}(r),r=|x|
, where
f_{m,r_{0}}
is given by

\displaystyle f_{m,r_{0}}(r)

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle 1-\int_{r}^{\infty}\mathfrak{c}_{p}s^{-a-1}(1+\frac{m}{2s})^{-2a}ds=1-\frac{I_{a}(\frac{m}{2r})}{I_{a}(\frac{m}{2r_{0}})},

where
\mathfrak{c}_{p}
and
m
can be related by

(2.1)

|m|=2\left(\mathfrak{c}_{p}I_{a}(\frac{m}{2r_{0}})\right)^{\frac{1}{a}}.

Proof.

Since
(\mathcal{M}_{m,r_{0}}^{3},g_{m})
is rotationally symmetric and the solution to (
1.11
) is unique,
u
is rotationally symmetric, i.e. there exists some
f(r)
such that
u(x)=f(r)
.
The Euclidean
p
-Lapacian on
u
gives

\bar{\Delta}_{p}u=\left(f^{\prime}(r)\right)^{p-2}\left[(p-1)f^{\prime\prime}(r)+2\frac{1}{r}f^{\prime}(r)\right].

Let
w=1+\frac{m}{2r}
so that
g_{m}=w^{4}\bar{g}
. By using the transformation formula for
p
-Laplacian under conformal change,
we get that

\displaystyle\Delta_{p}u

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle w^{-2p}\left(\bar{\Delta}_{p}u+2(3-p)|\bar{\nabla}u|^{p-2}_{\bar{g}}\langle\bar{\nabla}u,\bar{\nabla}\ln w\rangle\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle w^{-2p}(f^{\prime}(r))^{p-2}\left[(p-1)f^{\prime\prime}(r)+2\frac{1}{r}f^{\prime}(r)+2(3-p)f^{\prime}(r)\partial_{r}(\operatorname{ln}w)\right].

It follows from
\Delta_{p}u=0
that

f^{\prime}(r)=Cr^{-a-1}\left(1+\frac{m}{2r}\right)^{-2a},

where
C
is a positive constant. We claim that
C=\mathfrak{c}_{p}
. Indeed,
since

|\nabla u|_{g}=w^{-2}|\bar{\nabla}u|_{\bar{g}}=w^{-2}f^{\prime}(r),

we see

\operatorname{Area}_{g}(\partial\mathcal{M}_{m,r_{0}})=\int_{\{r=r_{0}\}}d\sigma=\int_{\{r=r_{0}\}}w^{4}d\bar{\sigma}=(w(r_{0}))^{4}4\pi r_{0}^{2}.

It follows that

\displaystyle\mathfrak{c}_{p}=\left(\frac{1}{4\pi}\int_{\partial\mathcal{M}_{m,r_{0}}}|\nabla u|_{g}^{p-1}d\sigma\right)^{\frac{1}{p-1}}=(w(r_{0}))^{2\frac{3-p}{p-1}}r_{0}^{\frac{2}{p-1}}f^{\prime}(r_{0})=C.

Since
u\rightarrow 1
as
r\rightarrow\infty
, we get

f(r)=1-\int_{r}^{+\infty}\mathfrak{c}_{p}t^{-a-1}\left(1+\frac{m}{2t}\right)^{-2a}dt.

By a change of variable
s=\frac{|m|}{2t}
, we see that

f(r)=1-(\frac{|m|}{2})^{-a}\mathfrak{c}_{p}\int_{0}^{\frac{|m|}{2r}}s^{a-1}(1+\operatorname{sgn}(m)s)^{-2a}ds=1-(\frac{|m|}{2})^{-a}\mathfrak{c}_{p}I_{a}(\frac{m}{2r}).

Since
u=0
on boundary
\partial\mathcal{M}_{m,r_{0}}=\{r=r_{0}\}
, we get

(\frac{|m|}{2})^{-a}\mathfrak{c}_{p}I_{a}(\frac{m}{2r_{0}})=1.

∎

Proposition 2.2
.

Let
(\mathcal{M}_{m,r_{0}}^{3},g_{m})
be a spatial Schwarzschild manifold outside a rotationally symmetric sphere of mass
m
given by (
1.9
) and
u
be the solution to (
1.11
). Let
H
be the mean curvature of the level set
S_{r}=\{|x|=r\}
. Then

\displaystyle H=\frac{2}{r}\left(1+\frac{m}{2r}\right)^{-3}\left(1-\frac{m}{2r}\right),

(2.2)

\frac{H}{2|\nabla u|_{g}}=\eta(r)=\mathfrak{c}_{p}^{-1}r^{a}\left(1+\frac{m}{2r}\right)^{2a-1}\left((1-\frac{m}{2r}\right).

Proof.

Denote
\bar{H}
and
\bar{\nu}
be the mean curvature and the unit normal of
S_{r}=\{|x|=r\}
under the Euclidean metric
\bar{g}
, respectively.
By the transformation formula for mean curvature under conformal change, we have

H=w^{-3}\left(\bar{H}w+4\frac{\partial w}{\partial\bar{\nu}}\right)=w^{-3}\left(\frac{2}{r}w+4\frac{\partial w}{\partial r}\right)=\frac{2}{r}\left(1+\frac{m}{2r}\right)^{-3}\left(1-\frac{m}{2r}\right).

Note that

the second assertion follows.
∎

3.
Monotonicity

The aim of this section is to prove the monotonicity of
F(t)
in Theorem
1.5
.

3.1.
Monotonicity of
F(t)
when
|\nabla u|\neq 0

We assume that
|\nabla u|\neq 0
in
M
, then
u\in C^{\infty}(M)
.
Consider the level set
\Sigma_{t}=\{u=f(t)\}
, where
f
is a given one-variable function. One sees readily that
\{\Sigma_{t}\}
satisfies the flow equation

(3.1)

\begin{cases}\Psi:\Sigma\times(r_{0},+\infty)\rightarrow\ M,\\
\partial_{t}\Psi(p,t)=f^{\prime}(t)\frac{\nabla u}{|\nabla u|^{2}}=f^{\prime}(t)\frac{1}{|\nabla u|}\nu.\end{cases}

The following basic facts are well-known, see for example
[
24
]
.

Lemma 3.1
.

The mean curvature of a regular level set
\Sigma_{t}
is given by

(3.2)

\displaystyle H=(1-p)\frac{1}{|\nabla u|}u_{\nu\nu}=\frac{1-p}{2}\frac{1}{|\nabla u|^{2}}\nu(|\nabla u|^{2}),

where
u_{\nu\nu}=\nabla^{2}u\left(\nu,\nu\right)
.

The evolution equation for the mean curvature along the flow (
3.1
) is as following, see for example
[
27
]
.

Lemma 3.2
.

(3.3)

\frac{\partial}{\partial t}H=-f^{\prime}(t)\left(\Delta_{\Sigma_{t}}\left(\frac{1}{|\nabla u|}\right)+\left(|h|^{2}+\operatorname{Ric}_{M}(\nu,\nu)\right)\frac{1}{|\nabla u|}\right).

Next we prove the following variational formula.

Lemma 3.3
.

Along the level set flow, we have

(3.4)

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\int_{\Sigma_{t}}|\nabla u|^{2}=-af^{\prime}(t)\int_{\Sigma_{t}}H|\nabla u|,

(3.5)

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\int_{\Sigma_{t}}H|\nabla u|=-f^{\prime}(t)\left\{\int_{\Sigma_{t}}|\nabla u|^{-2}|\nabla_{\Sigma_{t}}|\nabla u||^{2}+\frac{1}{2}(R_{M}-K_{\Sigma_{t}}+|\overset{\circ}{h}|^{2})+\frac{2a+1}{4}H^{2}\right\},

where
\overset{\circ}{h}
denotes the traceless part of the second fundamental form of the level set.

Proof.

Recall that the variation field of the level set flow is
\partial_{t}\Psi=f^{\prime}(t)\frac{1}{|\nabla u|}\nu
.
Using (
3.2
), one computes

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\int_{\Sigma_{t}}|\nabla u|^{2}=

\displaystyle\int_{\Sigma_{t}}f^{\prime}(t)\frac{1}{|\nabla u|}\nu(|\nabla u|^{2})+|\nabla u|^{2}Hf^{\prime}(t)\frac{1}{|\nabla u|}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\Sigma_{t}}f^{\prime}(t)\frac{2}{1-p}|\nabla u|H+f^{\prime}(t)H|\nabla u|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-af^{\prime}(t)\int_{\Sigma_{t}}H|\nabla u|.

On one hand, by the divergence theorem, we obtain

(3.6)

\displaystyle\int_{\Sigma_{t}}|\nabla u|\Delta_{\Sigma_{t}}\left(\frac{1}{|\nabla u|}\right)=

\displaystyle\int_{\Sigma_{t}}|\nabla u|^{-2}\left|\nabla_{\Sigma_{t}}{|\nabla u|}\right|^{2}.

On the other hand, the Gauss equation tells that

(3.7)

2\operatorname{Ric}_{M}(\nu,\nu)=R_{M}-K_{\Sigma_{t}}+H^{2}-|h|^{2}.

It follows from (
3.2
), (
3.3
), (
3.6
) and (
3.7
) that

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\left(\int_{\Sigma_{t}}H|\nabla u|\right)=

\displaystyle\int_{\Sigma_{t}}-f^{\prime}(t)\left(\Delta_{\Sigma_{t}}\left(\frac{1}{|\nabla u|}\right)+\left(|h|^{2}+\operatorname{Ric}_{M}(\nu,\nu)\right)\frac{1}{|\nabla u|}\right)|\nabla u|

\displaystyle+\int_{\Sigma_{t}}Hf^{\prime}(t)\frac{1}{|\nabla u|}\nu(|\nabla u|)+H|\nabla u|Hf^{\prime}(t)|\nabla u|^{-1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-f^{\prime}(t)\left\{\int_{\Sigma_{t}}|\nabla u|^{-2}|\nabla_{\Sigma_{t}}|\nabla u||^{2}+\frac{1}{2}(R_{M}-K_{\Sigma_{t}}+|\overset{\circ}{h}|^{2})+\frac{2a+1}{4}H^{2}\right\}.

∎

Proposition 3.1
.

Let
(M,g)
,
u
,
F(t)
be as in Theorem
1.5
. Assume in addition that
|\nabla u|\neq 0
in
M
. Then
F^{\prime}(t)\leq 0
.

Proof.

Since
|\nabla u|\neq 0
, it follows from Lemma
3.3
that

\displaystyle F^{\prime}(t)=

\displaystyle 4\pi\gamma^{\prime}(t)+\alpha^{\prime}(t)\int_{\Sigma_{t}}H|\nabla u|+\beta^{\prime}(t)\int_{\Sigma_{t}}|\nabla u|^{2}

\displaystyle-\alpha(t)f^{\prime}(t)\left\{\int_{\Sigma_{t}}|\nabla u|^{-2}|\nabla_{\Sigma_{t}}|\nabla u||^{2}+\frac{1}{2}(R_{M}-K_{\Sigma_{t}}+|\overset{\circ}{h}|^{2})+\frac{2a+1}{4}H^{2}\right\}

\displaystyle-a\beta(t)f^{\prime}(t)\int_{\Sigma_{t}}H|\nabla u|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 4\pi\gamma^{\prime}(t)-\alpha(t)f^{\prime}(t)\left\{\int_{\Sigma_{t}}|\nabla u|^{-2}|\nabla_{\Sigma_{t}}|\nabla u||^{2}+\frac{1}{2}(R_{M}-K_{\Sigma_{t}}+|\overset{\circ}{h}|^{2})\right\}

\displaystyle-(2a+1)\alpha(t)f^{\prime}(t)\int_{\Sigma_{t}}\left(\frac{H}{2}-\eta(u)|\nabla u|\right)^{2}

\displaystyle+\left[\alpha^{\prime}(t)-(2a+1)\eta(t)f^{\prime}(t)\alpha(t)-af^{\prime}(t)\beta(t)\right]\int_{\Sigma_{t}}H|\nabla u|

\displaystyle+\left[\beta^{\prime}(t)+(2a+1)(\eta(t))^{2}f^{\prime}(t)\alpha(t)\right]\int_{\Sigma_{t}}|\nabla u|^{2}.

Using the system of ODEs (
1.23
), we get

\displaystyle F^{\prime}(t)=

\displaystyle\gamma^{\prime}(t)\left\{\int_{\Sigma_{t}}|\nabla u|^{-2}|\nabla_{\Sigma_{t}}|\nabla u||^{2}+\frac{1}{2}R_{M}+\frac{1}{2}|\overset{\circ}{h}|^{2}+(2a+1)\left(\frac{H}{2}-\eta(u)|\nabla u|\right)^{2}\right\}

\displaystyle+\gamma^{\prime}(t)\left(4\pi-\int_{\Sigma_{t}}K_{\Sigma_{t}}\right).

By the assumption
H_{2}(M,\Sigma)=0
and
\Sigma
is connected, we known that
\Sigma_{t}
is connected, see for example
[
27
]
. It follows from the Gauss-Bonnet formula that
\int_{\Sigma_{t}}K_{\Sigma_{t}}\leq 4\pi
. In view of Proposition
A.2
and the assumption (
1.25
), we see that
\alpha(t)\geq 0
. The assertion follows since
\gamma^{\prime}(t)=-f^{\prime}(t)\alpha(t)\leq 0
,
R_{M}\geq 0
and
2a+1=\frac{5-p}{p-1}>0
. ∎

\displaystyle F_{\varepsilon}(t_{2})-F_{\varepsilon}(t_{1})=

Next we compute the intergrand in the right hand side of(

Lemma 3.5

(i)

{\rm div}U_{\varepsilon}=4\pi\gamma^{\prime}_{\varepsilon}(v){\rm Cap}_{p,\varepsilon}^{-1}|\nabla v|_{\varepsilon}^{p-2}|\nabla v|^{2}

(ii)

\displaystyle\alpha_{\varepsilon}(v)|\nabla v|^{-1}_{\delta}\Bigg\{(2-p)^{2}\frac{|\nabla v|^{4}}{|\nabla v|_{\varepsilon}^{4}}v_{\nu\nu}^{2}-(2-p)\frac{|\nabla v|^{4}}{|\nabla v|_{\varepsilon}^{2}|\nabla v|_{\delta}^{2}}v_{\nu\nu}^{2}-|\nabla v|^{2}{\rm Ric}(\nu,\nu)

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2}\frac{|\nabla v|^{2}}{|\nabla v|_{\varepsilon}^{2}}\left((p-2)^{2}\frac{|\nabla v|^{2}}{|\nabla v|_{\varepsilon}^{2}}+2p-3\right)v_{\nu\nu}^{2}\Bigg\}

\displaystyle+\alpha^{\prime}_{\varepsilon}(v)\left((2-p)\frac{|\nabla v|^{4}}{|\nabla v|^{2}_{\varepsilon}|\nabla v|_{\delta}}-\frac{|\nabla v|^{2}}{|\nabla v|_{\delta}}\right)v_{\nu\nu}.

Proof.

(i)(ii) By simple computation, using (

), we get (i)(ii). Next we prove (iii).

From the proof of Lemma 3.3 (iii) in

\displaystyle{\rm div}\left(\frac{\Delta v}{|\nabla v|_{\delta}}\nabla v\right)=

\displaystyle(2-p)^{2}\frac{|\nabla v|^{4}}{|\nabla v|^{4}_{\varepsilon}|\nabla v|_{\delta}}v_{\nu\nu}^{2}-(2-p)\frac{|\nabla v|^{4}}{|\nabla v|^{2}_{\varepsilon}|\nabla v|_{\delta}^{3}}v_{\nu\nu}^{2}+\frac{\langle\nabla\Delta v,\,\nabla v\rangle}{|\nabla v|_{\delta}},

and

It follows that

4.
Rigidity

In this section, we will prove the rigidity part in Theorem
1.5
.

First, we verify
F(t)\equiv 0
if
(M,g)=(\mathcal{M}_{m,r_{0}}^{3},g_{m})
is a spatial Schwarzschild manifold outside a rotationally symmetric sphere of mass
m
, given by (
1.9

\displaystyle u(x)=f_{m,r_{0}}(r)=1-\int_{r}^{\infty}\mathfrak{c}_{p}s^{-a-1}(1+\frac{m}{2s})^{-2a}ds=1-\frac{I_{a}(\frac{m}{2r})}{I_{a}(\frac{m}{2r_{0}})},

By the reformation of

.

Second, we show the converse. Assume that

is a constant on
\mathcal{T}

Also, we see from (

\displaystyle\nabla|\nabla u|=-\frac{1}{p-1}H\nabla u=-(a+1)\eta(u)|\nabla u|\nabla u.

\displaystyle t\left(1+\frac{m}{2t}\right)^{2}\left\{\left(C_{2}\mathfrak{c}_{p}+C_{1}\frac{a}{m}\frac{I_{a}(\frac{m}{2t})}{I_{a}(\frac{m}{2r_{0}})}\right)\eta(t)-C_{1}\frac{1}{m}\right\}.

In the second equality, we have used the definition of
\eta(t)
.

To calculate
\beta
, we need the derivative of
\alpha
. By computation, we get

\displaystyle\alpha^{\prime}(t)=

\displaystyle(a+1)(1+\frac{m}{2t})(1-\frac{m}{2t})\left\{\left(C_{2}\mathfrak{c}_{p}+C_{1}\frac{a}{m}\frac{I_{a}(\frac{m}{2t})}{I_{a}(\frac{m}{2r_{0}}}\right)\eta(t)-C_{1}\frac{1}{m}\right\}

\displaystyle+m\left(C_{2}\mathfrak{c}_{p}+C_{1}\frac{a}{m}\frac{I_{a}(\frac{m}{2t})}{I_{a}(\frac{m}{2r_{0}})}\right)\mathfrak{c}_{p}^{-1}t^{a-1}(1+\frac{m}{2t})^{2a}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(a+1)\eta(t)f^{\prime}(t)\alpha(t)+\left(C_{2}\mathfrak{c}_{p}+C_{1}\frac{a}{m}\frac{I_{a}(\frac{m}{2t})}{I_{a}(\frac{m}{2r_{0}})}\right)m\mathfrak{c}_{p}^{-2}t^{2a}(1+\frac{m}{2t})^{4a}f^{\prime}(t)

It follows from the ODE (
1.23
) that

\displaystyle\beta(t)=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{af^{\prime}(t)}\alpha^{\prime}(t)-\frac{2a+1}{a}\eta(t)\alpha(t)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\eta(t)\alpha(t)+\left(C_{2}\mathfrak{c}_{p}\frac{m}{a}+C_{1}\frac{I_{a}(\frac{m}{2t})}{I_{a}(\frac{m}{2r_{0}})}\right)\mathfrak{c}_{p}^{-2}t^{2a}\left(1+\frac{m}{2t}\right)^{4a}.

Finally, using the ODE (
1.23
) again, we notice that

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\left(-\mathfrak{c}_{p}^{2}t^{-2a}\left(1+\frac{m}{2t}\right)^{-4a}(\beta(t)+2\eta(t)\alpha(t))\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\mathfrak{c}_{p}^{2}t^{-2a}\left(1+\frac{m}{2t}\right)^{-4a}\left\{\beta^{\prime}(t)+2\eta^{\prime}(t)\alpha(t)+2\eta(t)\alpha^{\prime}(t)-2a\eta(t)f^{\prime}(t)(\beta(t)+2\eta(t)\alpha(t))\right\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-f^{\prime}(t)\alpha(t).

It follows that

\displaystyle\gamma(t)=

\displaystyle-\mathfrak{c}_{p}^{2}t^{-2a}\left(1+\frac{m}{2t}\right)^{-4a}(\beta(t)+2\eta(t)\alpha(t))+C_{3}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\mathfrak{c}_{p}^{2}t^{-2a}\left(1+\frac{m}{2t}\right)^{-4a}\eta(t)\alpha(t)-\left(C_{2}\mathfrak{c}_{p}\frac{m}{a}+C_{1}\frac{I_{a}(\frac{m}{2t})}{I_{a}(\frac{m}{2r_{0}})}\right)+C_{3},

where
C_{3}\in\mathbb{R}
is a constant.
∎

Remark A.2
.

The functions
\alpha(t)
,
\beta(t)
,
\gamma(t)
are still the solutions of the ordinary differential equations when
m<0
.

Proposition A.2
.

\alpha(t)\geq 0
on
[r_{0},+\infty)
if and only if

C_{2}\geq 0,\hbox{ and }\left(C_{2}\mathfrak{c}_{p}+C_{1}\frac{a}{m}\right)\eta(r_{0})\geq C_{1}\frac{1}{m}.

In particular, if
m>0
,
r_{0}=\frac{m}{2}
, then

\alpha(t)\geq 0
on
[r_{0},+\infty)
if and only if
C_{2}\geq 0
and
C_{1}\leq 0
.

Proof.

First we consider the necessary conditions of
\alpha(t)\geq 0,\forall t\in[r_{0},+\infty)
.

(i).

\alpha(r_{0})\geq 0
, i.e.
\left(C_{2}\mathfrak{c}_{p}+C_{1}\frac{a}{m}\right)\eta(r_{0})\geq C_{1}\frac{1}{m}
.

(ii).

\lim\limits_{t\to\infty}\alpha(t)\geq 0
. Notice that
t(1+\frac{m}{2t})^{2}\eta(t)>0
as if
t>r_{0}
.
Then
\alpha(t)
and
\frac{\alpha(t)}{t(1+\frac{m}{2t})^{2}\eta(t)}=\left(C_{2}\mathfrak{c}_{p}-C_{1}\xi(t)\right)
has the same sign on
(r_{0},+\infty)
. Here

\xi(t)=\mathfrak{c}_{p}\int_{t}^{+\infty}s^{-a-2}\left(1+\frac{m}{2s}\right)^{-2a}\left(1-\frac{m}{2s}\right)^{-2}ds.

Recall (
5.5
),

\lim\limits_{t\to\infty}\left(C_{2}\mathfrak{c}_{p}-C_{1}\xi(t)\right)=C_{2}\mathfrak{c}_{p}
.
i.e.
\lim\limits_{t\to\infty}\alpha(t)\geq 0
is equivalent to
C_{2}\geq 0
.

Now we prove that the conditions are sufficient.

(1).
When
r_{0}>\frac{|m|}{2}
,
\eta(t)>0
on
[r_{0},+\infty)
,

\xi(t)=\frac{C_{1}\frac{a}{m}\frac{I_{a}(\frac{m}{2t})}{I_{a}(\frac{m}{2r_{0}})}\eta(t)-C_{1}\frac{1}{m}}{\eta(t)}

It’s easy to see that
C_{2}\mathfrak{c}_{p}-C_{1}\xi(t)
is monotone on
[r_{0},+\infty)
, and by the conditions,
C_{2}\mathfrak{c}_{p}-C_{1}\xi(t)\geq 0
when
t=r_{0}
and when
t\rightarrow+\infty
. Thus when the conditions hold, we have that
\alpha(t)\geq 0
.

(2).
When
r_{0}=\frac{m}{2}
, the conditions become
C_{2}\geq 0,C_{1}\leq 0
.
Notice that

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\frac{\alpha(t)}{t^{a+1}(1+\frac{m}{2t})^{2a+1}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\left(C_{2}(1-\frac{m}{2t})-C_{1}\frac{1}{m}t^{-a}(1+\frac{m}{2t})^{-2a+1}+C_{1}\frac{a}{m}(1-\frac{m}{2t})\int_{t}^{+\infty}s^{-a-1}(1+\frac{m}{2t})^{-2a}ds\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{m}{2t^{2}}\mathfrak{c}_{p}^{-1}\left(C_{2}\mathfrak{c}_{p}+C_{1}\frac{a}{m}\frac{I_{a}(\frac{m}{2t})}{I_{a}(\frac{m}{2r_{0}})}+C_{1}\frac{1}{m}t^{-a-2}(1+\frac{m}{2t})^{-2a}\right).

And since that

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\left(C_{2}\mathfrak{c}_{p}+C_{1}\frac{a}{m}\frac{I_{a}(\frac{m}{2t})}{I_{a}(\frac{m}{2r_{0}})}+C_{1}\frac{1}{m}\mathfrak{c}_{p}t^{-a}(1+\frac{m}{2t})^{-2a}\right)=-2C_{1}\frac{a}{m}\mathfrak{c}_{p}t^{-a-1}(1+\frac{m}{2t})^{-2a-1}\geq 0,

then when
t
increases from
r_{0}
to
+\infty
, the value of

\left(C_{2}\mathfrak{c}_{p}+C_{1}\frac{a}{m}\frac{I_{a}(\frac{m}{2t})}{I_{a}(\frac{m}{2r_{0}})}+C_{1}\frac{1}{m}\mathfrak{c}_{p}t^{-a}(1+\frac{m}{2t})^{-2a}\right)

changes from
\left(C_{2}\mathfrak{c}_{p}+C_{1}\frac{a}{m}+C_{1}\frac{1}{m}(I_{a}(1))^{-1}2^{-2a}\right)
to
C_{2}\mathfrak{c}_{p}
.
Recall that

\alpha^{\prime}(t)=(a+1)\eta(t)f^{\prime}(t)\alpha(t)+\left(C_{2}\mathfrak{c}_{p}+C_{1}\frac{a}{m}\frac{I_{a}(\frac{m}{2t})}{I_{a}(\frac{m}{2r_{0}})}\right)m\mathfrak{c}_{p}^{-2}t^{2a}(1+\frac{m}{2t})^{4a}f^{\prime}(t),

now we talk about the sign of
\alpha
in two cases.

Case 1.
If
C_{2}\mathfrak{c}_{p}+C_{1}\frac{a}{m}\geq 0
, then
\alpha^{\prime}(t)\geq 0
,
\alpha(t)\geq\alpha(r_{0})=0
.

Case 2.
If
C_{2}\mathfrak{c}_{p}+C_{1}\frac{a}{m}<0
, then the derivative of

\frac{\alpha(t)}{t^{a+1}(1+\frac{m}{2t})^{2a+1}}
is increasing from a negative number to a non-negative number when t changes from
r_{0}
to
+\infty
. Thus the value of
\frac{\alpha(t)}{t^{a+1}(1+\frac{m}{2t})^{2a+1}}
is monotone non-increasing from
r_{0}
to
r_{1}
and is monotone non-decreasing from
r_{1}
to
+\infty
, where
r_{1}
is the number such that

\left(C_{2}\mathfrak{c}_{p}+C_{1}\frac{a}{m}\frac{I_{a}(\frac{m}{2r_{1}})}{I_{a}(\frac{m}{2r_{0}})}+C_{1}\frac{1}{m}\mathfrak{c}_{p}r_{1}^{-a}(1+\frac{m}{2r_{1}})^{-2a}\right)=0.

While
t=r_{1}
,

\displaystyle\alpha(r_{1})=

\displaystyle r_{1}\left(1+\frac{m}{2r_{1}}\right)^{2}\left\{\left(C_{2}\mathfrak{c}_{p}+C_{1}\frac{a}{m}\frac{I_{a}(\frac{m}{2r_{1}})}{I_{a}(\frac{m}{2r_{0}})}\right)\eta(r_{1})-C_{1}\frac{1}{m}\right\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle r_{1}\left(1+\frac{m}{2r_{1}}\right)^{2}\left\{-C_{1}\frac{1}{m}\mathfrak{c}_{p}r_{1}^{-a}(1+\frac{m}{2r_{1}})^{-2a}\eta(r_{1})-C_{1}\frac{1}{m}\right\}\geq 0.

Thus

\frac{\alpha(t)}{t^{a+1}(1+\frac{m}{2t})^{2a+1}}\geq 0

holds on
[r_{0},+\infty)
, which implies that
\alpha(t)\geq 0
on
[r_{0},+\infty)
.
∎

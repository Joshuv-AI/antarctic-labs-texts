1.
Introduction

In this paper, a meromorphic function will always be non-constant and meromorphic in the complex plane C, unless specifically stated otherwise. In what follows, we assume that the reader is familiar with the elementary Nevanlinna theory,(see
[
16
,
19
,
30

f

\mathcal{S}(f)
denotes the family of all meromorphic function
\zeta

T(r,\zeta)=S(r,f)=o(T(r,f))

r\rightarrow\infty
outside of a possible set of finite logarithmic measure.For convenience, we agree that
\mathcal{S}(f)
includes all constant functions and
\overline{\mathcal{S}}(f):=\mathcal{S}(f)\cup\{\infty\}.

A set
\mathcal{S}
is called a unique range set (URSE) for a certain class of entire functions if each inverse image of the set uniquely determines a function from the given class. Let
\mathcal{S}
be a finite set of some entire functions and
f
an entire function. Then, a set
E_{f}(\mathcal{S})
is defined as

\displaystyle E_{f}(\mathcal{S})=\{(z,m)\in\mathbb{C}\times\mathbb{Z}:f(z)-a(z)=0\text{\;with multiplicity}\;m,a\in\mathcal{S}\}.

If we do not the count multiplicities, then we denote the set as
\overline{E}_{f}(\mathcal{S})
.Assume that
g
is another entire funct ion. We say that
f
and
g
share
\mathcal{S}

CM
if
E_{f}(\mathcal{S})=E_{g}(\mathcal{S})
, and we say
f
and
g
share
\mathcal{S}

IM
if
\overline{E}_{f}(\mathcal{S})=\overline{E}_{g}(\mathcal{S})
. Thus, a set
\mathcal{S}
is called
URSE
if
E_{f}(\mathcal{S})=E_{g}(\mathcal{S}),
where
f
and
g
are two entire functions, then
f\equiv g.

Gross
and

[
13

[
11

12

23

. One can verify that the form of the function will be
f(z)=ce^{z}.
where

[

A special topic widely studied in the uniqueness theory is the case when

shares value(s) or set(s) with its derivatives or differential polynomials. We recall a result of this type from the preceding literature:

2.
Main result

In this article, we dealt with the above questions and answered them all affirmatively. Following is the main result of this paper.

Theorem 2.1
.

Suppose that
\alpha
,
\beta
are two distinct entire functions, and
f
is a non-constant entire function with
\lambda(f)<\rho(f)<\infty
such that
\max\{\rho(\alpha),\rho(\beta)\}<\rho(f)
and
\mathcal{L}^{n}_{c}(f)(\not\equiv 0)
. If
E_{f}(\{\alpha,\beta\})=E_{\mathcal{L}^{n}_{c}(f)}(\{\alpha,\beta\})
, then

\displaystyle f(z)=\mathcal{A}_{1}\lambda_{1}^{z/c}+\mathcal{A}_{2}\lambda_{2}^{z/c}+\ldots+\mathcal{A}_{n}\lambda_{n}^{z/c},

where
\mathcal{A}_{i},\lambda_{i}\in\mathbb{C}\smallsetminus\{0\}
for
i=1,2,\ldots,n
, and
\lambda_{i}
are the roots of the equation

(2.1)

\displaystyle a_{n}w^{n}+a_{n-1}w^{(n-1)c}+\ldots+a_{1}w+a_{0}-1=0.

Furthermore
\mathcal{L}^{n}_{c}(f(z))\equiv f(z)
for all
z\in\mathbb{C}
.

Remark 2.1
.

Entire functions satisfying
Theorem
2.1

Let

[

Lemma 3.4

\displaystyle\frac{(\mathcal{L}^{n}_{c}(f)-\alpha)((\mathcal{L}^{n}_{c}(f)-\beta))}{(f-\alpha)(f-\beta)}=e^{\mathcal{Q}},

∎

Lemma 3.5
.

Let
f
and
\mathcal{G}
be two entire functions and
P(z)
is a polynomial in

, and

,

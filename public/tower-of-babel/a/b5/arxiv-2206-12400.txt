The projective Fraïssé limit of the class of all connected finite graphs with confluent epimorphisms Thanks: Funded by the Deutsche Forschungsgemeinschaft (DFG, German Research Foundation) under Germany’s Excellence Strategy EXC 2044–390685587, Mathematics Münster: Dynamics–Geometry–Structure and by CRC 1442 Geometry: Deformations and Rigidity.
Włodzimierz J. Charatonik, Aleksandra Kwiatkowska, Robert P. Roe

Abstract
. We show that the class of finite connected graphs with confluent epimorphism is a projective Fraïssé class and we investigate the continuum obtained as the topological realization of the projective Fraïssé limit. This continuum was unknown before. We show that it is indecomposable, but not hereditarily indecomposable, one-dimensional, Kelley, pointwise self-homeomorphic, but not homogeneous. It is hereditarily unicoherent and each point is the top of the Cantor fan. Moreover, the universal solenoid, the universal pseudo-solenoid, and the pseudo-arc may be embedded in it.

1.
Introduction

In 2006, Irwin and Solecki
[
11
]
initiated the study of projective Fraïssé limits creating a bridge between logic and continuum theory.
In their article they constructed the pseudo-arc as the topological realization of the projective Fraïssé limit of the Fraïssé class consisting of all finite linear (combinatorial) graphs and all epimorphisms, i.e., surjective mappings between the finite graphs that preserve the edge relation.

The study of projective Fraïssé limits has in recent years caught the attention of many researchers. They used properties of the mappings between finite structures, in particular finite graphs, to construct and investigate various compact one-dimensional metric spaces, such as the Lelek fan, generalized Ważewski dendrites or Knaster continua, see for example
[
3
]
,
[
2
]
,
[
7
]
,
[
12
]
,
[
20
]
.
Compact spaces were also studied in a framework of an approximate Fraïssé theory, see for example

[
1
]
or
[
14
]
, where in particular, the Poulsen simplex or pseudo-solenoids were realized as Fraïssé limits.

Finite graphs are discrete as topological spaces. Therefore they are topologically totally disconnected. Nevertheless, they can be connected as graphs.
In
[
17
]
, Panagiotopoulos and Solecki introduced appropriate definitions for connectedness and for maps between topological graphs preserving connectedness,
which take into account both the topology and the edge relation. They showed that the class of finite connected graphs with monotone epimorphisms is a Fraïssé class whose topological realization is the Menger curve.
In
[
6
]
, Charatonik and Roe considered finite trees with epimorphisms, which are monotone, confluent, order-preserving, retractions, light, etc. As topological realizations of the various Fraïssé limits they obtained known continua, such as the Cantor fan and the generalized Ważewski dendrite
D_{3}
, as well as previously unknown continua.

In the present
article, we study topological properties of the
continuum obtained as the topological realization of the Fraïssé limit of the class of finite
connected graphs with confluent epimorphisms.
Confluent maps between continua were introduced by Charatonik
[
4
]
as a generalization of monotone maps and have been very well studied. Confluent maps between finite connected graphs are discretizations of such maps.
We summarize our results in the following theorems.

Theorem 1.1
.

The class
{\mathcal{G}}
of finite connected graphs with confluent epimorphisms is a projective Fraïssé class. Its projective Fraïssé limit

\mathbb{G}
has a transitive edge relation and its topological realization
|\mathbb{G}|
is a continuum, which has the following properties:

(1)

it is a Kelley continuum;

(2)

it is one-dimensional;

(3)

it is an indecomposable continuum;

(4)

all arc components are dense;

(5)

each point is the top of the Cantor fan;

(6)

it is pointwise self-homeomorphic;

(7)

it is hereditarily unicoherent, in particular, the circle
S^{1}
does not embed in it;

(8)

the pseudo-arc, the universal pseudo-solenoid, and the universal solenoid embed in it.

In Section
9
, we will show that topological realizations of Fraïssé limits of many Fraïssé classes of cycles with confluent epimorphisms exist and are solenoids, see Theorem
9.6
.

Understanding solenoids that can be embedded in the Fraïssé limit leads to a major and somewhat surprising result of the article, that the continuum
|\mathbb{G}|
is not homogeneous, which we accomplish in Section
9
.
Rogers
[
19
]
proved that
each homogeneous, hereditarily indecomposable curve (i.e. continuum of dimension 1) is
tree-like. Note that we cannot apply his theorem since
|\mathbb{G}|

is not hereditarily indecomposable.
To accomplish the non-homogeneity, we show that some points of
|\mathbb{G}|
belong to a solenoid and other do not. In particular, we show:

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
{\mathcal{G}}
,
\mathbb{G}
, and
|\mathbb{G}|
be as in Theorem
1.1
. Then the following hold.

(1)

Let
S_{univ}
be the universal solenoid. The set

\{{\bf x}\in|\mathbb{G}|\colon\text{ there is an embedding }\tau\colon S_{univ}\to|\mathbb{G}|\text{ such that }{\bf x}\in\tau(S_{univ})\}

is dense in
|\mathbb{G}|
.

(2)

The set

\{{\bf y}\in|\mathbb{G}|\colon\text{for every solenoid }S\text{ and every embedding }\tau\colon S\to|\mathbb{G}|\text{ we have }{\bf y}\notin\tau(S)\}

is dense in
|\mathbb{G}|
.

(3)

The universal solenoid is the only solenoid which embeds into
|\mathbb{G}|
.

To prove Theorem
1.2
(2) in Section
9
we analyze topological subgraphs of
\mathbb{G}
whose topological realizations are solenoids. These turn out to be not just inverse limits of finite cycles with confluent bonding maps. In fact, we have to deal with more general topological graphs, which we call almost graph-solenoids.

Theorem
1.2
immediately yields the following.

Corollary 1.3
.

The continuum
|\mathbb{G}|
is not homogeneous.

2.
Definitions and background results

A
graph
is an ordered pair
A=(V(A),E(A))
, where
E(A)\subseteq V(A)^{2}
is a reflexive and symmetric relation on
V(A)
. The elements of
V(A)
are called
vertices
of graph
A
and elements of
E(A)
are called
edges
.
For the sake of simplicity of notation we will assume that
\langle x,y\rangle
represents both the edge
\langle x,y\rangle
and the edge
\langle y,x\rangle
. If
A
and
B
are graphs then
A\setminus B
denotes the graph with
V(A\setminus B)=V(A)\setminus V(B)
and
\langle x,y\rangle\in E(A\setminus B)
if and only if
x,y\in V(A\setminus B)
and
\langle x,y\rangle\in E(A)
.
We say that a graph
A
is a
subgraph
of a graph
B
, and denote it by
A\subseteq B
, if
V(A)\subseteq V(B)
and

\langle x,y\rangle\in E(A)
if and only if
\langle x,y\rangle\in E(B)
.

A
topological graph

K
is a graph
(V(K),E(K))
, whose domain
V(K)
is a 0-dimensional, compact, second-countable (thus has a metric) space and
E(K)
is a closed, reflexive and symmetric subset of
V(K)^{2}
. A topological graph is an example of a topological
\mathcal{L}
-structure, in the sense of Irwin-Solecki

[
11
]
.

Given two graphs
A
and
B
, a function
f\colon A\to B
is a
homomorphism
if it maps edges to edges, i.e.
\langle a,b\rangle\in E(A)
implies
\langle f(a),f(b)\rangle\in E(B)
.
A homomorphism
f
is an
epimorphism
if it is moreover surjective
on both vertices and edges. An
isomorphism
is an injective epimorphism.

The theory of projective Fraïssé limits was developed in
[
11
]
and further refined in
[
17
]
. We literally recall their definitions here.

Definition 2.1
.

Let

Notation 2.4
.

Given a sequences

of finite graphs and epimorphisms

We define the edge relation on

.

3.
Confluent epimorphisms

Let us start with recalling the following definition introduced in
[
6
]
.

Definition 3.1
.

Given two topological graphs
G
and
H
, an epimorphism
f\colon G\to H
is called
confluent
if for every closed connected subset
Q
of
V(H)
and every component
C
of
f^{-1}(Q)
we have
f(C)=Q
. Equivalently, if for every closed connected subset
Q
of
V(H)
and every vertex
a\in V(G)
such that
f(a)\in Q
, there is a connected set
C
of
V(G)
such that
a\in C
and
f(C)=Q
.
It is called
monotone
if preimages of closed connected sets are connected.
Clearly, every monotone epimorphism is confluent.

Observation 3.2
.

If
f\colon X\to Y
and
g\colon Y\to Z
are confluent epimorphisms between topological graphs, then
g\circ f\colon X\to Z
is a confluent epimorphism.

Let us recall some results shown in
[
6
]

Theorem 3.3
.

Given two finite graphs
G
and
H
, the following conditions are equivalent for an epimorphism
f\colon G\to H
:

(1)

f
is confluent;

(2)

for every edge
P\in E(H)

We call the inverse limit of an inverse sequence of cycles

Let

Our goal now is to show that the topological realization of the

Example 9.4
.

Let

be prime numbers and let

be the class of cycles having an even number of vertices and with confluent epimorphisms whose winding numbers are of the form

is a projective Fraïssé class.

A necessary and sufficient condition for

to have a topological realization (transitive set of edges) is the following:

(
\ddagger
)

for every

there is
f\colon D\to C
in

such that for every

The sufficiency follows from Theorem 2.18 in

Let

be the set of winding numbers of epimorphisms in

. Therefore, by the amalgamation property, if

, then there is

, which is divisible by both

Now let

be a multiplicative semigroup of positive integers such that

, and if

, then there is

, which is divisible by both

and

and

Let

be the class that consists of all cycles and all confluent epimorphisms such that winding numbers are in

is the maximal (with respect to containment) class of confluent epimorphisms such that

The proof is analogous to the proof of Lemma
9.2
. Furthermore we have the following.

Lemma 9.5
.

Let
\mathcal{D}
be an infinite projective Fraïssé class of cycles with confluent epimorphisms which satisfies

.
Then the projective Fraïssé limit of

Let

and let

. We have to show that there are subsequences
(n_{k})
and
(m_{k})
and confluent epimorphisms

Theorem 9.6
.

Let
\mathcal{D}
be a projective Fraïssé class of cycles with confluent epimorphisms, which satisfies

and

is infinite. Then the topological realization

Note that in case

We first need a lemma.

Now we prove (2).
Now let

9.2.
Points in
|\mathbb{G}|
that belong to a universal solenoid.

In this subsection we show the following theorem.

Theorem 9.8

There is a dense set of points in
|\mathbb{G}|
that belong to a universal solenoid.

We start with some definitions and several lemmas.

We say that
{\bf x}\in\mathbb{G}

belongs to a graph-solenoid
if there is a graph-solenoid
\mathbb{S}
and an embedding
h\colon{\mathbb{S}}\to{\mathbb{G}}

Similarly,

and an embedding

with

. A solenoid

is said to be

if for any solenoid

there is a continuous mapping from
S
onto

.

Lemma 9.9

Let
A,B\in\mathcal{G}

Then there is a cycle

in

, and

Note first that by the confluence of

, for every

f(x)=c_{i}
then there is a path

in

, such that for every

and
f(z)=c_{i+1}
. Using this simple observation, and that

is a finite set, we obtain that for any

there is a path

’s, then a sequence of
c_{1}
’s, etc.,

The graph induced on the vertices

is a wrapping map onto

. To see this, start with a graph

.
Suppose there are vertices
y_{i},y_{j}
in

and
\langle y_{i},y_{j}\rangle\in E(B)
.
Let

be the graph such that

Let

and

Since

is an epimorphism and

is a cycle,

In the case that

9.3.
Almost graph-solenoids – definition and properties

Solenoids may be obtained as topological realizations of topological graphs other than graph-solenoids, see
Theorems
9.27
and
9.22
.
To prove Theorem
9.1
, we also have to understand
certain more general topological graphs whose topological realization is a solenoid.
In this subsection we define and study almost wrapping maps between cycles and correspondingly define almost graph-solenoids.

A surjective homomorphism
g\colon D\to C
, where
C,D
are cycles with
C=(c_{0},c_{1},c_{2},\ldots,c_{k}=c_{0})
is an
almost wrapping map
if there is a confluent epimorphism
f\colon D\to C
such that for every
y\in D
and
x=c_{i}\in C
,
if
f(y)=c_{i}
then
g(y)\in\{c_{i},c_{i+1}\}
. We call
f
a
confluent witness
for
g
. Clearly a confluent witness is not unique. Note that almost wrapping maps are epimorphisms. If for every
x\in V(C)
every component of
f^{-1}(x)
contains at least
2
vertices, then we call
f
a
proper confluent witness
for
g
. A
winding number
of
g
is the winding number of
f
.
We call the inverse limit of an inverse sequence of cycles
\{C_{n},p_{n}\}
, where
p_{n}
are almost wrapping maps, an
almost graph-solenoid
(
almost graph-circle
) if for infinitely many
n
the winding number of
p_{n}
is greater than 1 (all winding numbers are equal to 1) and every
p_{n}
has a proper confluent witness.

Whenever we consider almost wrapping maps between cycles, we will assume that these cycles are oriented and that almost wrapping map are orientation preserving. If
C=(c_{0},c_{1},c_{2},\ldots,c_{k}=c_{0})
, then we let the
orientation

<_{C}
on
C
to be the binary relation
<_{C}=\{(c_{i},c_{i+1})\colon i=0,\ldots,k-1\}
, and we write
c_{i}<_{C}c_{i+1}
.
An almost wrapping map
g\colon D\to C
, where
C,D
are cycles, is
orientation preserving
if for some (equivalently any) confluent witness
f\colon D\to C
of
g
we have: for every
x,y\in D
, if
x<_{D}y
, then
f(x)<_{C}f(y)
or
f(x)=f(y)
.
For a cycle
C
and
a,b\in C
,
an oriented arc
, which we simply denote by
[a,b]
,
is the proper subset of
C
,
\{a=x_{0},x_{1},\ldots,x_{m}=b\}
such that for each
j=0,1,\ldots,m-1
, it holds
x_{j}<_{C}x_{j+1}
. We allow that
a=b
and in that case

. Note that unless
a=b
, then
[a,b]\neq[b,a]
. We also let
(a,b)=[a,b]\setminus\{a,b\}
, and similarly define
[a,b)
and
(a,b]
.

The following lemma is an immediate consequence of the definition of an almost wrapping map.

Lemma 9.17
.

Let

,

,

, and

.
The following observation follows immediately from Lemma

.

are cycles and

Generalizing Remark
9.19
we have the following description of almost wrapping maps. It characterizes almost wrapping maps as those surjective homomorphisms, which do not reverse the order of triples.

Lemma 9.20
.

Let

be a surjective homomorphism, where

are cycles.
Then

is an almost wrapping map if and only if there are no non-adjacent

and no

such that
w(c)=x
,
w(a)=z
,
and
w([a,c])=[x,z]
.

Proof.

Suppose first that
w\colon D\to C
is an almost wrapping map. Write
C=(c_{0},c_{1},\ldots,c_{n}=c_{0})
and let
f\colon D\to C
be a confluent epimorphism such that for every
y\in D
and

,
if

then
w(y)\in\{c_{l},c_{l+1}\}
, i.e. if
w(y)=c_{l}
, then
f(y)\in\{c_{l-1},c_{l}\}
. Suppose there are non-adjacent

and

such that
w(c)=x
,
w(a)=z
,
and
w([a,c])=[x,z]
. Then we have

,

f(a)\in\{c_{j-1},c_{j}\}
,
and
f([a,c])
is one of
[c_{i-1},c_{j-1}]
,
[c_{i-1},c_{j}]
,
[c_{i},c_{j-1}]
,
[c_{i},c_{j}]
. As

is order preserving,
f(a)\in\{c_{i-1},c_{i}\}

and
f(c)\in\{c_{j-1},c_{j}\}
. However, this is impossible since

and

are non-adjacent.

To prove the converse, suppose that there are no non-adjacent
x\neq z\in C
and no
a,c\in D
such that
w(c)=x
,
w(a)=z
, and
w([a,c])=[x,z]
,
and write
C=(c_{1},\ldots,c_{n}=c_{1})
.
Then there are no

with

and

, and

with

,

for

. This implies that we can partition

(in a possibly non-unique way) into arcs

, for some

, and

adjacent to

, such that

.
Therefore

is a confluent witness for

.

.

. Therefore we have to modify

to get a proper confluent witness for

.

of

,

denote the component of

. If there is

such that

be the first such vertex, that is,

. Analogously, if there is

Define a map from

assumes value

on

. To see that

, since then

.

9.4.
Almost graph-solenoids as preimages of solenoids

In this subsection we show that each solenoid in
|\mathbb{G}|
comes from an almost graph-solenoid in
\mathbb{G}
via the quotient map.

Theorem 9.22
.

Let
S
be a solenoid embedded in
|\mathbb{G}|
.
Then there is an almost graph-solenoid
\mathbb{S}\subseteq\mathbb{G}
such that
\pi(\mathbb{S})=S
, where
\pi\colon\mathbb{G}\to|\mathbb{G}|
is the quotient map.

Let
\mathbb{F}
be a topological graph such that for every
{\bf x}\in\mathbb{F}
there is at most one
{\bf y}\neq{\bf x}
,
{\bf y}\in\mathbb{F}
with
\langle{\bf x},{\bf y}\rangle\in E(\mathbb{F})
, and let
\delta\colon\mathbb{F}\to|\mathbb{F}|
be the quotient map. Let

O_{\mathbb{F}}=\{{\bf x}\in\mathbb{F}\colon\text{ there is no }{\bf y}\neq{\bf x}\text{ with }\langle{\bf x},{\bf y}\rangle\in E(\mathbb{F})\}

be the set of
singletons
of
\mathbb{F}
and let

T_{\mathbb{F}}=\{{\bf x}\in\mathbb{F}\colon\text{ there is exactly one }{\bf y}\neq{\bf x}\text{ with }\langle{\bf x},{\bf y}\rangle\in E(\mathbb{F})\}

be the set of
doubletons
of
\mathbb{F}
. We have
\mathbb{F}=O_{\mathbb{F}}\cup T_{\mathbb{F}}
.

The set
O_{\mathbb{F}}
is
G_{\delta}
, i.e. it is the intersection of countably many open sets. Indeed, it is the intersection of

A_{n}=\{{\bf x}\in\mathbb{F}\colon d({\bf x},{\bf y})<\frac{1}{n}\text{ for every }{\bf y}\text{ such that }\langle{\bf x},{\bf y}\rangle\in E(\mathbb{F})\},

where
d
is some fixed metric on
\mathbb{F}
. Each
\mathbb{F}\setminus A_{n}
is closed since the edge relation
E(\mathbb{F})
is closed in
\mathbb{F}^{2}
and
\mathbb{F}
is compact.

Note that if
\mathbb{F}_{1}\subseteq\mathbb{F}_{2}
are topological graphs then
\mathbb{F}_{1}\cap O_{\mathbb{F}_{2}}\subseteq O_{\mathbb{F}_{1}}
, but the equality may not hold.

Remark 9.23
.

The set
O_{\mathbb{G}}
, where
\mathbb{G}
is the Fraïssé limit of graphs with confluent epimorphisms,
is a dense
G_{\delta}
in
\mathbb{G}
.

Lemma 9.24
.

Let
\mathbb{F}_{0}
be a topological graph such that
\mathbb{F}_{0}=O_{\mathbb{F}_{0}}\cup T_{\mathbb{F}_{0}}
and let
\delta\colon\mathbb{F}_{0}\to|\mathbb{F}_{0}|
be the quotient map. Then there is a nonempty open set
U\subseteq\mathbb{F}_{0}
such that if
\mathbb{F}=\mathbb{F}_{0}\setminus U
then
O_{\mathbb{F}}
is dense in
\mathbb{F}
and
\delta(\mathbb{F})=|\mathbb{F}_{0}|
.

Proof.

Let
U_{1},U_{2},U_{3},\ldots
be a countable open base for
\mathbb{F}_{0}
with
U_{i}\not=\emptyset
. We will proceed by the following recursive procedure. At step
n-1
we have constructed a closed subset
\mathbb{F}_{n-1}\subseteq\mathbb{F}_{0}
.
If for every
{\bf x}\in\mathbb{F}_{n-1}\cap U_{n}
there is
{\bf y}\in\mathbb{F}_{n-1}\setminus U_{n}
such that
\langle{\bf x},{\bf y}\rangle\in E(\mathbb{F}_{n-1})
, then take
\mathbb{F}_{n}=\mathbb{F}_{n-1}\setminus U_{n}
. Otherwise, let
\mathbb{F}_{n}=\mathbb{F}_{n-1}
.
Clearly
\delta(\mathbb{F}_{n})=|\mathbb{F}_{0}|
.

We claim that the set
\mathbb{F}=\bigcap_{n}\mathbb{F}_{n}
is as required. Clearly
\delta(\mathbb{F})=|\mathbb{F}_{0}|
.
Suppose towards a contradiction that
O_{\mathbb{F}}
is not dense in
\mathbb{F}
.
Let
U
be an open nonempty set in
\mathbb{F}
such that
for every
{\bf x}\in U
there is

{\bf y}\in\mathbb{F}
,
{\bf y}\neq{\bf x}
, so that
\langle{\bf x},{\bf y}\rangle\in E(\mathbb{F})
.
Fix a metric
d
on
\mathbb{F}
and
let

D_{n}=\{{\bf x}\in U\colon\text{ there is }{\bf y}\in\mathbb{F}\text{ with }\langle{\bf x},{\bf y}\rangle\in E(\mathbb{F})\text{ and }d({\bf x},{\bf y})\geq\frac{1}{n}\}.

Each
D_{n}
is closed in
U
and we have
\bigcup_{n}D_{n}=U
.
By the Baire category theorem for the open set
U
there is
n_{0}
such that
D_{n_{0}}
has a nonempty interior.
Take
V\subseteq{\rm int}(D_{n_{0}})
open nonempty of diameter
<\frac{1}{n_{0}}
.
Therefore, for every
{\bf x}\in V
, there is
{\bf y}\in\mathbb{F}\setminus V
with
\langle{\bf x},{\bf y}\rangle\in E(\mathbb{F})
.
Let
n
be such that
U_{n}\cap\mathbb{F}\subseteq V
, and without loss of generality by shrinking
V
, we have
U_{n}\cap\mathbb{F}=V
.
This means that
U_{n}
was subtracted in the construction of
\mathbb{F}
, which gives a contradiction. Indeed, if
U_{n}
was not subtracted at stage

, then one of the following would have to happen. The first possibility is that there is
{\bf x}\in U_{n}\cap O_{\mathbb{F}_{n-1}}
. However, then
{\bf x}\in\mathbb{F}\setminus V
,
which is impossible. The second possibility is that
there are
{\bf x}\neq{\bf y}\in U_{n}
with
\langle{\bf x},{\bf y}\rangle\in E(\mathbb{F}_{n-1})
. However, then
{\bf x},{\bf y}\notin V
, hence also
{\bf x},{\bf y}\notin\mathbb{F}
, which cannot be the case as
\delta(\mathbb{F})=|\mathbb{F}_{0}|
.
∎

Example 9.25
.

Let
\mathbb{F}

be its topological realization. Suppose that

is uncountable and dense in

Lemma 9.32
.

&w_{2}(k_{m_{0}-1})=w_{2}(l_{m_{0}-1}),w_{2}(J_{m_{0}-1}),w_{2}(k_{m_{0}})=w_{2}(l_{m_{0}}),w_{2}(u_{m_{0}}).\end{split}

By (3), any two consecutive elements in this sequence are adjacent, therefore it is a path joining
w_{2}(v_{m_{0}})
and
w_{2}(u_{m_{0}})
; we denote it by
P
. The
w_{2}(J_{m})
contains at least two vertices and
P
does not contain edges from
w_{2}(J_{m})
. Therefore
C_{2}=w_{2}(J_{m})\cup P
, which implies that
w_{2}(E)=A
and
C_{2}\setminus A

is the union of singletons
\{w_{2}(k_{i})=w_{2}(l_{i})\}
. But this is a contradiction since there are arcs in the complement of
A
, which are not singletons.

∎

Proof of Theorem
9.30
.

Let
{\bf x}\in\mathbb{G}
be the vertex constructed in Proposition
9.33
, along with the Fraïssé sequence
\{K_{n},\beta_{n}\}
.
We claim that
z=\pi({\bf x})
, where
\pi\colon\mathbb{G}\to|\mathbb{G}|
is the projection map, does not belong to a solenoid. Suppose towards a contradiction that there is a solenoid
S
such that
z\in S\subseteq|\mathbb{G}|
. By Lemma
9.24
there is a topological graph
\mathbb{S}\subseteq\pi^{-1}(S)
such that
O_{\mathbb{S}}
is dense in
\mathbb{S}

\pi(\mathbb{S})=S
.
Since
{\bf x}\in O_{\pi^{-1}(S)}
, we have
{\bf x}\in\mathbb{S}
.
By Theorem
9.27
,
\mathbb{S}
is an almost graph-solenoid.
On the other hand, by Theorem
9.34
,
{\bf x}

10.
Open problems

As we proved in Section 9, we can distinguish points in
|\mathbb{G}|
: some of them belong to the universal solenoid and some do not. Moreover,
every solenoid, which embeds into
|\mathbb{G}|
, is the universal solenoid.

We do not know if we can further distinguish points by the the number of solenoids, which contain them. For
x\in|\mathbb{G}|
let
n_{S}(x)
denote the cardinality of the set

\{S\subseteq|\mathbb{G}|\colon S\text{ is homeomorphic to a solenoid and }x\in S\}.

Question 10.1
.

What are the possible values of
n_{S}(x)
for
x\in|\mathbb{G}|
?

Furthermore, we do not know whether there are continua, other than solenoids, which we can use to distinguish points in
|\mathbb{G}|
. In particular, can we distinguish points by belonging or not belonging to the pseudo-arc. In Section 6, we showed that the pseudo-arc can be embedded into
|\mathbb{G}|
.

Question 10.2
.

Are there
x\in|\mathbb{G}|
such that for any embedding
\tau\colon P\to|\mathbb{G}|
, where
P
is the pseudo-arc, we have
x\notin\tau(P)
?

The homeomorphism group
H(|\mathbb{G}|)
acts on
|\mathbb{G}|
via
(h,x)\mapsto h(x)
.

Question 10.3
.

What is the cardinality of orbits under this action?

Question 10.4
.

Are all orbits dense?

We know that the pseudo-arc, the universal pseudo-solenoid, the universal solenoid, and the Cantor fan, embed into

|\mathbb{G}|
. On the other hand, every continuum which embeds into
|\mathbb{G}|
is one-dimensional and hereditary unicoherent. Not all continua with those two properties embed into
|\mathbb{G}|
since the universal solenoid is the only solenoid, which embeds into
|\mathbb{G}|
.

Problem 10.5
.

Which one-dimensional hereditary unicoherent continua can be embedded into
|\mathbb{G}|
?
In particular, can we embed the pseudo-circle and the solenoid of pseudo-arcs into
|\mathbb{G}|
?

We have shown many properties of the continuum
|\mathbb{G}|
. We would like to have a complete list of properties that characterize
|\mathbb{G}|
up to homeomorphism.

Problem 10.6
.

Find a topological characterization of
|\mathbb{G}|
.

The homeomorphism group
H(|\mathbb{G}|)
becomes a separable topological group when equipped with the supremum metric.

Problem 10.7
.

Investigate properties of
H(|\mathbb{G}|)
, the homeomorphism group of
|\mathbb{G}|
.

Finally, in investigating the homogeneity of
|\mathbb{G}|
we spent time thinking about the following combinatorial question, which we believe is of independent interest.

Question 10.8
.

Given a finite connected graph
H
does there exist a homogeneous finite connected graph
G
and a confluent epimorphism
f\colon G\to H
?

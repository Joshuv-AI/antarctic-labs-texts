Design and Reprogrammability of Zero Modes in 2D Materials from a Single Element
Daniel Revier, Molly Carton, Jeffrey I. Lipton*

Abstract
Mechanical extremal materials, a class of metamaterials that exist at the bounds of elastic theory, possess the extraordinary capability to engineer any desired elastic behavior by harnessing mechanical zero modes — deformation modes that demand minimal or no elastic energy. However, the potential for arbitrary construction and reprogramming of metamaterials remains largely unrealized, primarily due to significant challenges in qualitatively transforming zero modes within the confines of existing metamaterial design frameworks. In this work, we show a method for explicitly defining and in situ reprogramming zero modes of two-dimensional extremal materials by employing straight-line mechanisms (SLMs) and planar symmetry, which prescribe and coordinate the zero modes, respectively. We design, test, and reprogram centimeter-scale isotropic, orthotropic, and chiral extremal materials by reorienting the SLMs in place, enabling these materials to smoothly and reversibly interpolate between extremal modalities (e.g., unimode to bimode) and material properties (e.g., negative to positive Poisson’s ratios) without changing the metamaterial’s global structure. Our methodology provides a straightforward and explicit strategy for the design and tuning of all varieties of two-dimensional extremal materials, enabling arbitrary and dynamic mechanical metamaterial construction to completely cover the gamut of elastic properties.

Straight-line mechanisms (SLMs) form the foundational element of our extremal materials.
Among the various SLM designs

SLMs are therefore inherently extremal (unimodal).
They possess one intrinsic zero mode and form the basis for constructing other extremal materials

A key innovation of our approach is the SLM’s rotationally symmetric design, which allows in situ reorientation of the compliant direction (see

We express the idealized SLM compliance as the deformation gradient

The SLM zero mode deformation can also be expressed as a strain under the assumption of linearity (
\varepsilon=\frac{1}{2}(F+F^{T})-I

Thus, our SLM can adapt its extremal material response — from shear to normal deformation and back — through simple rotational adjustments, enabling in situ tuning of the mechanical behavior without requiring global structural change or lengthy reprogramming processes.

Naively tiling unimodal SLMs will only result in unimodal materials described by

Introducing more independent SLMs is necessary to achieve a broader range of behaviors; however, each SLM introduces rigidity (i.e., constraints) along with its DOF.
These must be coordinated to ensure that they do not interfere with each other and restrict deformation, which requires simultaneously satisfying each individual SLM’s deformation gradient (see SI).

We use planar symmetries, termed the
wallpaper groups
, to introduce multiple SLMs and coordinate their zero modes.
Wallpaper groups ensure complete tiling of the plane for metamaterial construction

; they also effectively arrange each individual SLM zero mode to avoid the combinatorial complexity seen in other efforts to design zero mode materials

Our reprogrammable SLM design fits seamlessly into regular square and hexagonal lattices because of its uniform spacing.
In contrast to other reprogrammable zero mode materials

This allows the lattice to act as a scaffold, where the nodes serve as connection points for the SLMs positioned along the edges.
The loading of the structure is transferred through the lattice connections, but deformations are allowed only in line with the SLM orientations.
This results in various emergent behaviors of a single lattice, which are coordinated by the orientation of zero modes relative to the lattice lines.
This setup creates a two-dimensional material where the number and characteristics of zero modes are determined by the SLM orientation and symmetry.

5
Conclusion

In this study, we introduced a novel, reprogrammable approach using straight-line mechanisms (SLMs) and symmetry to engineer two-dimensional extremal materials.
SLMs provide a basis for explicitly programming the zero modes of extremal materials, constructing nullmode through trimode materials depending on the SLM orientation, the lattice type (square vs. hexagonal), and SLM orientation symmetry (rotational vs. mirror).
We use the orientation of the SLM as a control field to spatially vary the properties on a given lattice.
This allowed us select which elastic properties are extremized, creating a range of Poisson’s ratios, high bulk-shear modulus ratios, and selective chirality all from a single framework.
These properties can be continuously varied to form extremal state trajectories, demonstrating how extremal materials can smoothly vary between types, constrained by the normalized eigenvalue gamut.
This new framework for designing mechanical metamaterials is not limited to 2D SLMs but is generalizable to any metamaterial design system that can be expressed in DOFs.
Our work paves the way for the expanded use of extremal materials in practical settings, particularly in sectors that benefit from materials capable of dynamic responses to environmental changes such as soft robotics, underwater acoustics, or wearable technology.

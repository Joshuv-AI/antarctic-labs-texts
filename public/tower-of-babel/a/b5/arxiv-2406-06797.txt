Combinatorial identities with multiple harmonic-like numbers


Abstract
Multiple harmonic-like numbers are studied using the generating function approach. A closed form is stated for binomial sums involving these numbers and two additional parameters. Several corollaries and examples are presented which are immediate consequences of the main result. Finally, combinatorial identities involving harmonic-like numbers and other prominent sequences like hyperharmonic numbers and odd harmonic numbers are offered.

Preliminaries

Cheon and El-Mikkawy
[
8
,
9
]
defined multiple harmonic-like numbers by

H_{n}(m)=\sum_{1\leq k_{1}+k_{2}+\cdots+k_{m}\leq n}\frac{1}{k_{1}k_{2}\cdots k_{m}}

(1)

with
H_{n}(0)=1
for
n\geq 0
and
H_{0}(m)=0
for
m\geq 1
. They showed that the generating function of
H_{n}(m)
equals

H(z)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}H_{n}(m)z^{n}=\frac{(-\ln(1-z))^{m}}{1-z}.

(2)

For
m=1
these numbers reduce to ordinary harmonic numbers
H_{n}
as

H_{n}(1)=\sum_{1\leq k_{1}\leq n}\frac{1}{k_{1}}=H_{n},\qquad H_{0}=0.

(3)

For
m=2
we see that

\displaystyle H_{n}(2)

\displaystyle=\sum_{1\leq k_{1}+k_{2}\leq n}\frac{1}{k_{1}k_{2}}=\sum_{j=1}^{n}\sum_{k_{1}+k_{2}=j}\frac{1}{k_{1}k_{2}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{n}\sum_{k_{1}=1}^{j-1}\frac{1}{k_{1}(j-k_{1})}=\sum_{j=1}^{n}\frac{2}{j}H_{j-1}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{n}\frac{H_{n-j}}{j}=H_{n}^{2}-H_{n}^{(2)},

where in the last line a result of Kargin and Can
[
13
]
was used and where
H_{n}^{(2)}
are the second-order harmonic numbers, i.e.,

H_{n}^{(2)}=\sum_{j=1}^{n}\frac{1}{j^{2}}.

The brute force computation of
H_{n}(3)
is tedious. The result is

H_{n}(3)=\sum_{j=1}^{n}\frac{1}{j}\sum_{l=1}^{n-j}\frac{H_{n-j-l}}{l}.

The next lemma is helpful.

Lemma 1
.

For all
n\geq 1
and
m\geq 0
we have the identity

H_{n}(m+1)=\sum_{j=1}^{n}\frac{H_{n-j}(m)}{j}.

(4)

Proof.

Using (
2
) we have

\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}H_{n}(m+1)z^{n}

\displaystyle=(1-z)\frac{-\ln(1-z)}{1-z}\frac{(-\ln(1-z))^{m}}{1-z}

\displaystyle=(1-z)\left(\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}H_{n}z^{n}\right)\left(\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}H_{n}(m)z^{n}\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\sum_{j=0}^{n}H_{j}H_{n-j}(m)z^{n}-\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}H_{j}H_{n-1-j}(m)z^{n}.

Extracting and comparing the coefficients of
z^{n}
we obtain for all
n\geq 1

\displaystyle H_{n}(m+1)

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}H_{j}\left(H_{n-j}(m)-H_{n-1-j}(m)\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{n}H_{n-j}(m)(H_{j}-H_{j-1})

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{n}\frac{H_{n-j}(m)}{j}

as claimed.
∎

In what follows we will need the definition of the Stirling numbers of the first kind,
s(n,k)
.
These numbers is defined by the generating function

\sum_{n=k}^{\infty}s(n,k)\frac{z^{n}}{n!}=\frac{\ln^{k}(1+z)}{k!}.

(5)

Some particular values are

\displaystyle s(n,k)

\displaystyle=0\qquad\mbox{for}\,\,n<k,

\displaystyle s(n,0)

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}1,&n=0;\\
0,&n\geq 1;\\
\end{cases}

\displaystyle s(n,1)

\displaystyle=(-1)^{n-1}(n-1)!

\displaystyle s(n,2)

\displaystyle=(-1)^{n}(n-1)!H_{n-1}.

2
Binomial sums involving
H_{n}(m)

For
a,b\in\mathbb{C}
let
S_{n}(a,b,m)
be defined by

S_{n}(a,b,m)=\sum_{k=0}^{n}\binom{n}{k}a^{k}b^{n-k}H_{k}(m).

(6)

Then we have the following result.

Theorem 1
.

For all
n\geq 0
we have

S_{n}(a,b,m)=\sum_{j=0}^{m}\binom{m}{j}\sum_{k=0}^{n}H_{k}(j)(a+b)^{k}\frac{(m-j)!}{(n-k)!}(-1)^{n-k}b^{n-k}s(n-k,m-j),

(7)

where
s(n,k)
are the Stirling numbers of the first kind.

Proof.

Let
S(z)
denote the generating function of
S_{n}(a,b,m)
. Then (see
[
3
,
16
]
)

\displaystyle S(z)

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}S_{n}(a,b,m)z^{m}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{1-bz}H\left(\frac{az}{1-bz}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{1-(a+b)z}\left(-\ln\left(\frac{1-(a+b)z}{1-bz}\right)\right)^{m}

\displaystyle=(-1)^{m}\frac{(\ln(1-(a+b)z)-\ln(1-bz))^{m}}{1-(a+b)z}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=0}^{m}\binom{m}{j}\frac{(-\ln(1-(a+b)z))^{j}}{1-(a+b)z}\ln^{m-j}(1-bz).

The result follows from (
2
) and (
5
) in combination with the Cauchy product.
∎

Remark
.

When
m=0
then using
H_{n}(0)=1,n\geq 0
,

S_{n}(a,b,0)=\sum_{k=0}^{n}\binom{n}{k}a^{k}b^{n-k}=(a+b)^{n}.

When
m=1
then we get

\displaystyle S_{n}(a,b,1)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{n}\binom{n}{k}a^{k}b^{n-k}H_{k}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{n}\frac{(-1)^{n-k}}{(n-k)!}(a+b)^{k}b^{n-k}s(n-k,1)+\sum_{k=0}^{n}\frac{(-1)^{n-k}}{(n-k)!}H_{k}(a+b)^{k}b^{n-k}s(n-k,0)

\displaystyle=H_{n}(a+b)^{n}-\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}(a+b)^{k}b^{n-k}\frac{1}{n-k},

(8)

which reproduces Boyadzhiev’s main result (Proposition 6) from
[
2
]
.

Corollary 2
.

For
n,m\geq 0
we have

\sum_{k=0}^{n}\binom{n}{k}(-1)^{k}H_{k}(m)=(-1)^{n}\frac{m!}{n!}s(n,m)

(9)

and

\sum_{k=m}^{n}\binom{n}{k}\frac{s(k,m)}{k!}=\frac{1}{m!}H_{n}(m).

(10)

Proof.

The first result follows immediately by setting
a=-1
and
b=1
in (
7
) and simplifying.
The second identity is the inverse binomial transform of the first (for information on the binomial transform consult
[
3
]
).
∎

Remark
.

The identity (
9
) was discovered first by Chen and Guo (see
[
7
, Corollary 8]
).
The second identity also appears in that paper (with a typo, see the proof of Proposition 13).

Corollary 3
.

For
n,m\geq 0
we have

\sum_{k=0}^{n}\binom{n}{k}H_{k}(m)=\sum_{j=0}^{m}\binom{m}{j}\sum_{k=0}^{n}H_{k}(j)(-1)^{n-k}2^{k}\frac{(m-j)!}{(n-k)!}s(n-k,m-j).

(11)

In particular, we recover the classical identity
[
2
]

\sum_{k=0}^{n}\binom{n}{k}H_{k}=2^{n}\left(H_{n}-\sum_{k=1}^{n}\frac{1}{2^{k}k}\right).

(12)

Proof.

Set
a=b
in (
7
) and simplify.
∎

Corollary 4
.

For
n\geq 0
we have

S_{n}(a,b,2)=H_{n}(2)(a+b)^{n}+2\sum_{k=1}^{n}(a+b)^{n-k}b^{k}\frac{H_{k-1}-H_{n-k}}{k}.

(13)

Proof.

Set
m=2
in (
7
) and simplify.
∎

Some consequences of Corollary
4
will be stated as examples.

Example 1
.

We have

\sum_{k=0}^{n}\binom{n}{k}H_{k}(2)=2^{n}\left(H_{n}(2)+2\sum_{k=1}^{n}\frac{H_{k-1}-H_{n-k}}{2^{k}k}\right).

(14)

Example 2
.

From

\sum_{k=0}^{n}\binom{n}{k}(-1)^{k}H_{k}(2)=\frac{2}{n}H_{n-1}

(15)

we deduce that

\sum_{k=0}^{n}\binom{n}{k}(-1)^{k}H_{k}^{(2)}=-\frac{H_{n}}{n},

(16)

where we used that (see for instance
[
1
]
)

\sum_{k=0}^{n}\binom{n}{k}(-1)^{k}H_{k}^{2}=\frac{H_{n}}{n}-\frac{2}{n^{2}}.

The inverse binomial relation also yields

\sum_{k=1}^{n}\binom{n}{k}(-1)^{k+1}\frac{H_{k}}{k}=H_{n}^{(2)}.

(17)

Example 3
.

We have

\sum_{k=0}^{n}\binom{n}{k}2^{k}(-1)^{n-k}H_{k}(2)=H_{n}(2)+2\sum_{k=1}^{n}(-1)^{k}\frac{H_{k-1}-H_{n-k}}{k}.

(18)

Example 4
.

We have

\sum_{k=0}^{n}\binom{n}{k}2^{k}H_{k}(2)=3^{n}\left(H_{n}(2)+2\sum_{k=1}^{n}\frac{H_{k-1}-H_{n-k}}{3^{k}k}\right).

(19)

Example 5
.

Let
F_{n}
and
L_{n}
be the Fibonacci and Lucas numbers, respectively (see Koshy
[
15
]
or Vajda
[
17
]
). Then we have

\sum_{k=0}^{n}\binom{n}{k}F_{k}H_{k}(2)=H_{n}(2)F_{2n}+2\sum_{k=1}^{n}F_{2(n-k)}\frac{H_{k-1}-H_{n-k}}{k},

(20)

\sum_{k=0}^{n}\binom{n}{k}L_{k}H_{k}(2)=H_{n}(2)L_{2n}+2\sum_{k=1}^{n}L_{2(n-k)}\frac{H_{k-1}-H_{n-k}}{k},

(21)

and

\sum_{k=0}^{n}\binom{n}{k}(-1)^{k+1}F_{k}H_{k}(2)=H_{n}(2)F_{n}+2\sum_{k=1}^{n}F_{n-k}\frac{H_{k-1}-H_{n-k}}{k},

(22)

\sum_{k=0}^{n}\binom{n}{k}(-1)^{k}L_{k}H_{k}(2)=H_{n}(2)L_{n}+2\sum_{k=1}^{n}L_{n-k}\frac{H_{k-1}-H_{n-k}}{k}.

(23)

Corollary 5
.

For
n\geq 0
we have

\displaystyle S_{n}(a,b,3)

\displaystyle=H_{n}(3)(a+b)^{n}

\displaystyle\quad-3\sum_{k=1}^{n}(a+b)^{n-k}b^{k}\frac{H_{k-1}^{2}-H_{k-1}^{(2)}-2H_{k-1}H_{n-k}+H_{n-k}^{2}-H_{n-k}^{(2)}}{k}.

(24)

Proof.

Set
m=3
in (
7
) and simplify using

s(n,3)=\frac{1}{2}(-1)^{n-1}(n-1)!\left(H_{n-1}^{2}-H_{n-1}^{(2)}\right).

∎

3
Combinatorial identities from clever telescoping

Lemma 2
.

If
(a_{n})_{n\geq 1}
is a sequence, then

\sum_{k=1}^{n}H_{k}\left({a_{k+1}-a_{k}}\right)=H_{n}a_{n+1}-\sum_{k=1}^{n}\frac{a_{k}}{k}.

Proof.

This is an immediate consequence of the recurrence relation of the harmonic numbers.
∎

Lemma
2
is broadly applicable and allows to instantly state a myriad of interesting results.
We give three examples as a warmup. Setting
a_{n}=H_{n-1}
gives the known result

\sum_{k=1}^{n}\frac{{H_{k-1}}}{k}=\frac{1}{2}\left({H_{n}^{2}-H_{n}^{(2)}}\right).

The choice
a_{n}=n
yields the well-known harmonic number summation formula

\sum_{k=1}^{n}H_{k}=(n+1)H_{n}-n.

Let
a_{n}=F_{n+1}
be the sequence of (shifted) Fibonacci numbers. Then we get the following Fibonacci-harmonic number identity:

\sum_{k=1}^{n}H_{k}F_{k}=H_{n}F_{n+2}-\sum_{k=1}^{n}\frac{{F_{k+1}}}{k}.

Theorem 6
.

If
m
and
n
are non-negative integers, then

\sum_{k=1}^{n}H_{k}\sum_{j=m}^{k}{\binom{{k-1}}{{j-1}}\frac{{s(j,m)}}{{j!}}}=\frac{1}{{m!}}H_{n}(m)H_{n}-\frac{1}{{m!}}\sum_{k=1}^{n}\frac{{H_{k-1}(m)}}{k}.

(25)

Proof.

Use Lemma
2
with
a_{k}=H_{k-1}\left(m\right)
while noting from (
10
) that

H_{k}(m)-H_{k-1}(m)=m!\sum_{j=m}^{k}\binom{{k-1}}{{j-1}}\frac{s(j,m)}{j!}.

(26)

∎

Setting
m=1
in (
25
) gives the known result

\sum_{k=1}^{n}\frac{{H_{k}}}{k}=\frac{1}{2}\left(H_{n}^{2}+H_{n}^{(2)}\right),

while
m=2
gives

2\sum_{k=1}^{n}H_{k}\sum_{j=1}^{k}{(-1)^{j}\binom{{k-1}}{{j-1}}\frac{{H_{j-1}}}{j}}=H_{n}^{3}-H_{n}^{(2)}H_{n}-\sum_{k=1}^{n}\frac{{H_{k-1}^{2}-H_{k-1}^{(2)}}}{k}.

Theorem 7
.

If
m
and
n
are positive integers, then

\sum_{k=1}^{n}H_{k}\sum_{j=m}^{n-k+1}\binom{{n-k}}{{j-1}}\frac{s(j,m)}{j!}=\frac{1}{m!}H_{n+1}(m+1).

(27)

In particular,

\sum_{k=1}^{n}\frac{{H_{k}}}{{n-k+1}}=H_{n+1}^{2}-H_{n+1}^{(2)}

(28)

and

\sum_{k=1}^{n}H_{k}\sum_{j=2}^{n-k+1}\binom{{n-k}}{{j-1}}\frac{{\left({-1}\right)^{j}}}{j}H_{j-1}=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{k=1}^{n+1}\frac{1}{k}\sum_{j=1}^{n+1-k}\frac{{H_{n-k-j+1}}}{j}.

(29)

Proof.

Set
a_{k}=H_{n-k+1}\left(m\right)
in Lemma
2
and use (
4
) and (
26
).
∎

Lemma 3
.

If
(a_{n})_{n\geq 0}
is a sequence, then

\sum_{k=1}^{n}\frac{a_{k}-a_{k-1}}{k}=\sum_{k=1}^{n}\frac{{a_{k}}}{{k(k+1)}}-a_{0}+\frac{a_{n}}{n+1}.

(30)

Proof.

This is a consequence of

\frac{1}{k}-\frac{1}{k+1}=\frac{1}{k(k+1)}.

∎

Again, many results can be derived from Lemma
3
. For instance, setting
a_{k}=H_{k}+p
(
p
a non-negative integer) gives

\sum_{k=1}^{n}\frac{{H_{k+p}}}{k(k+1)}=\begin{cases}H_{n}^{(2)}-\frac{H_{n}}{n+1},&p=0;\\
\\
\frac{H_{n}+H_{p}-H_{n+p}}{p}+H_{p}-\frac{H_{n+p}}{n+1},&p\geq 1;\end{cases}

(31)

where we used the result

\sum_{k=1}^{n}\frac{1}{k(k+p)}=\begin{cases}H_{n}^{(2)},&p=0;\\
\\
\frac{H_{n}+H_{p}-H_{n+p}}{p},&p\geq 1.\end{cases}

The result (
31
) is most likely not new.

Theorem 8
.

If
m
is a non-negative integer and
n
is a positive integer, then

\sum_{k=1}^{n}{\frac{{H_{n-k}\left(m\right)}}{{k\left({k+1}\right)}}}=H_{n}\left(m\right)+H_{n}\left({m+1}\right)-H_{n+1}\left({m+1}\right).

(32)

In particular,

\sum_{k=1}^{n}{\frac{{H_{n-k}}}{{k\left({k+1}\right)}}}=H_{n}+H_{n}^{2}-H_{n}^{(2)}-H_{n+1}^{2}+H_{n+1}^{(2)}

(33)

and

\sum_{k=1}^{n}{\frac{{H_{n-k}^{2}-H_{n-k}^{(2)}}}{{k(k+1)}}}=H_{n}^{2}-H_{n}^{(2)}+\sum_{k=1}^{n}{\frac{1}{k}\sum_{j=1}^{n-k}{\frac{{H_{n-k-j}}}{j}}}-\sum_{k=1}^{n+1}{\frac{1}{k}\sum_{j=1}^{n+1-k}{\frac{{H_{n+1-k-j}}}{j}}}.

(34)

Proof.

Use
a_{k}=H_{n-k}(m)
in Lemma
3
and invoke Lemma
1
.
∎

Lemma 4
.

Let
(a_{n})_{n\geq 0}
be a sequence. If
r
is a complex number and
n
is a non-negative number, then

\sum_{k=0}^{n}(-1)^{k}\binom{{r-1}}{k}(a_{k+1}-a_{k})=(-1)^{n}\binom{{r-1}}{n}a_{n+1}-\sum_{k=0}^{n}(-1)^{k}\binom{r}{k}a_{k}.

(35)

Proof.

This is a variation on an identity of Kollár
[
14
, Lemma 1]
.
∎

Theorem 9
.

If
r
is a complex number and
m
and
n
are positive integers, then

\begin{split}\sum_{k=0}^{n}{(-1)^{k}\binom{{r-1}}{k}\sum_{j=m}^{k+1}{\binom{{k}}{{j-1}}\frac{{s(j,m)}}{{j!}}}}&=(-1)^{n}\binom{{r-1}}{n}\frac{1}{{m!}}H_{n+1}\left(m\right)\\
&\qquad-\frac{1}{{m!}}\sum_{k=0}^{n}{(-1)^{k}\binom{{r}}{k}H_{k}\left(m\right)}.\end{split}

(36)

In particular,

\sum_{k=0}^{n}{\frac{{\left({-1}\right)^{k}}}{{k+1}}\binom{{r-1}}{k}}=\left({-1}\right)^{n}\binom{{r-1}}{n}H_{n+1}-\sum_{k=0}^{n}{\left({-1}\right)^{k}\binom{{r}}{k}H_{k}}

(37)

and

\begin{split}\sum_{k=0}^{n}{\left({-1}\right)^{k}\binom{{r-1}}{k}\sum_{j=2}^{k+1}{\left({-1}\right)^{j}\binom{{k}}{{j-1}}\frac{{H_{j-1}}}{j}}}&=\left({-1}\right)^{n}\binom{{r-1}}{n}\frac{1}{2}\left({H_{n+1}^{2}-H_{n}^{(2)}}\right)\\
&\qquad-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{k=0}^{n}{\left({-1}\right)^{k}\binom{{r}}{k}\left({H_{k}^{2}-H_{k}^{(2)}}\right)}.\end{split}

(38)

Proof.

Use
a_{k}=H_{k}\left(m\right)
in Lemma
4
.
∎

4
More identities involving
H_{n}(m)
and other sequences

Hyperharmonic numbers
H_{n,p}
(or
h_{n}^{(p)}
),
p\geq 1,
are another generalization of harmonic numbers (
[
4
,
10
,
11
]
).
They are defined by

H_{n,p}=\sum_{i=1}^{n}H_{i,p-1}\qquad\mbox{with}\qquad H_{n,0}=\frac{1}{n},\,H_{0,p}=0,\,H_{n,1}=H_{n}.

(39)

They can be written in the compact form

H_{n,p+1}=\binom{n+p}{n}(H_{n+p}-H_{p}),\qquad p=0,1,2,\ldots,

(40)

Theorem 10
.

For all
n,p,m\geq 0
we have

\sum_{k=0}^{n}\binom{k+p}{k}H_{n-k}(m)(H_{k+p}-H_{p})=\sum_{k=0}^{n}\binom{k+p}{k}H_{n-k}(m+1).

(41)

In particular,

\sum_{k=0}^{n}\binom{k+p}{k}(H_{k+p}-H_{p})=\sum_{k=0}^{n}\binom{k+p}{k}H_{n-k}

(42)

and

\sum_{k=0}^{n}\binom{k+p}{k}H_{n-k}(H_{k+p}-H_{p})=\sum_{k=0}^{n}\binom{k+p}{k}\left(H_{n-k}^{2}-H_{n-k}^{(2)}\right).

(43)

Proof.

Let
B(z)
be the generating function of
H_{n,p+1}
. We can calculate

\displaystyle H(z)\cdot B(z)

\displaystyle=\left(\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}H_{n}(m)z^{n}\right)\left(\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}H_{n,p+1}z^{n}\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\sum_{k=0}^{n}H_{k}(m)H_{n-k,p+1}z^{n}

\displaystyle=\frac{(-\ln(1-z))^{m}}{1-z}\frac{-\ln(1-z)}{(1-z)^{p+1}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{(1-z)^{p+1}}\frac{(-\ln(1-z))^{m+1}}{1-z}

\displaystyle=\left(\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\binom{n+p}{n}z^{n}\right)\left(\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}H_{n}(m+1)z^{n}\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\sum_{k=0}^{n}\binom{k+p}{k}H_{n-k}(m+1)z^{n}

and the statement follows.
∎

The next theorem contains an identity involving
H_{n}(m)
and odd harmonic numbers
O_{n}
, the latter being defined by

O_{n}=\sum_{k=1}^{n}\frac{1}{2k-1},\quad O_{0}=0.

Obvious relations between harmonic numbers
H_{n}
and odd harmonic numbers
O_{n}
are given by

H_{2n}=\frac{1}{2}H_{n}+O_{n}\qquad\text{and}\qquad H_{2n-1}=\frac{1}{2}H_{n-1}+O_{n}.

Additional relations are contained in the next lemma.

Lemma 5
.

If
n
is an integer, then

\displaystyle H_{n-1/2}-H_{-1/2}=2O_{n},

(44)

\displaystyle H_{n-1/2}-H_{1/2}=2\left({O_{n}-1}\right),

(45)

\displaystyle H_{n+1/2}-H_{-1/2}=2O_{n+1},

(46)

\displaystyle H_{n+1/2}-H_{1/2}=2\left({O_{n+1}-1}\right),

(47)

\displaystyle H_{n+1/2}-H_{n-1/2}=\frac{2}{{2n+1}},

(48)

\displaystyle H_{n-1/2}-H_{-3/2}=2\left({O_{n}-1}\right),

(49)

\displaystyle H_{n+1/2}-H_{-3/2}=2\left({O_{n+1}-1}\right).

(50)

Lemma 6
.

If
p
and
r
are non-negative integers, then

H_{r,p+1/2}=\frac{1}{{2^{2r-1}}}\binom{{2p}}{p}^{-1}\binom{{2(r+p)}}{{r+p}}\binom{{r+p}}{r}\left({O_{r+p}-O_{p}}\right).

(51)

Proof.

Identity (
45
) gives

H_{r+p-1/2}-H_{r-1/2}=2\left({O_{r+p}-O_{p}}\right).

(52)

Using this and

\binom{{r+p-1/2}}{r}=\frac{1}{{2^{2r}}}\binom{{2p}}{p}^{-1}\binom{{2(r+p)}}{{r+p}}\binom{{r+p}}{r}

(53)

in (
40
) gives (
51
).
∎

Theorem 11
.

If
n
and

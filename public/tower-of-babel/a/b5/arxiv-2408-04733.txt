An Inductive Approach to Basepoint-Freeness of Adjoint Series on Irregular Varieties
Houari Benammar Ammar

Abstract
In this paper, we show how to prove the basepoint-freeness for linear systems on irregular varieties inductively. For instance, we prove that Fujita’s conjecture holds for irregular varieties of dimension n \mathnormal{n} with a nef anticanonical bundle assuming it holds for lower-dimensional varieties and under mild conditions. 2020 Mathematics Subject Classification: Primary 14C20, Secondary 14F06, 14F17. Introduction

Let
D
be a positive divisor on a smooth complex projective irregular variety
X
of dimension
n
. In this paper, we shall use the Albanese map to prove that the basepoint-freeness problem for the adjoint linear system
|K_{X}+cD|
, where
c
is a positive integer, can be reduced to a lower-dimensional cases.

To place matters into context, let us recall that one celebrated problem for the basepoint-freeness of adjoint linear systems is Fujita’s conjecture
Fujita (1987)
.

Conjecture 1.1

(
(
Fujita, 1987
, Page 167)
)
.

Let
X
be a complex smooth projective variety of dimension
n
, and let
D
be an ample divisor on
X
. Then, the linear system
|K_{X}+mD|
is basepoint-free for all
m\geq n+1
.

This conjecture is trivial in dimension
1
and was proved by Reider
(
Reider, 1988
, Theorem 1)
in dimension
2
. For
\dim X=3
or
4
, it was proved by Ein-Lazarsfeld
(
Ein and Lazarsfeld, 1993
, Corollary 2*)
and Kawamata
(
Kawamata, 1997
, Theorem 4.1)
, respectively. Recently, the conjecture was settled in dimension
5
by Ye and Zhu, see
(
Ye and Zhu, 2020
, Page 3)
. In higher dimensions, there exist some partial results. For instance, Demailly in
(
Demailly, 1993
, Page 324)
and
(
Demailly, 1996
, Theorem 0.2)
established certain results using analytic techniques and Monge–Ampère equations, he showed that
2K_{X}+mD
is very ample if
m\geq 2+\binom{3n+1}{n}
for every ample divisor
D
on
X
. In
(
Angehrn and Siu, 1995
, Corollary 0.2)
, Angehrn and Siu achieved an important result by utilizing multiplier ideal sheaves, Nadel’s vanishing theorem, and the Ohsawa–Takegoshi extension theorem. Specifically, they proved that Conjecture
1.1
holds if
m\geq\frac{n(n+1)}{2}
. Later, Heier
(
Heier, 2002
, Theorem 1.4)
improved this bound to
O(n^{\frac{4}{3}})
. Some logarithmic bounds were obtained by Ghidelli and Lacini
(
Ghidelli and Lacini, 2024
, Theorem 1.1)
. Notably, Helmke
Helmke (1997)
and
(
Helmke, 1999
, Page 3)
proved Conjecture
1.1
under stronger numerical conditions on
D
.

Recall that by the classical Castelnuovo-Mumford regularity theorem, if
D
is ample and globally generated, then Conjecture
1.1
holds (
(
Lazarsfeld, 2004
, Example 1.8.23)
).

In this article, we mainly study Fujita’s Conjecture for irregular varieties. We first mention that, in
(
Pareschi and Popa, 2003
, Section 5)
, Pareschi and Popa, for instance, obtained some basepoint-freeness results on varieties whose Albanese morphism is finite. In Section 3, we present new inductive results on Fujita’s Conjecture for irregular varieties. The main results are as follows:

Theorem

(Theorem
3.5
)
.

Let
X
be an irregular variety of dimension
n\geq 2
with Albanese dimension
1\leq\alpha(X)<n
. Let
X\xrightarrow{f}Z\xrightarrow{u}\operatorname{alb}(X)\subseteq\operatorname{Alb}(X)
be the Stein factorization of the Albanese morphism
\operatorname{alb}
, and let
F
be a general fiber of the morphism
f
. Let
D
be an ample divisor on
X
.

If Conjecture
1.1
holds in dimension
<n
, then
|K_{X}+mD+\operatorname{alb}^{*}p|
has no basepoint supported on
F
for all
m\geq n-\alpha(X)+1
and for general
p\in\operatorname{Pic}^{0}(\operatorname{Alb}(X))
.

Additionally, if the following condition is satisfied:

(*)

There exists an integer
r
with
1\leq r\leq\alpha(X)
such that
|rD_{|_{F}}|
is basepoint-free and
rD-K_{X}
is nef and big,

then
|K_{X}+mD|
has no basepoint supported on
F
for all
m\geq n+1
.

We can include the case of
X
of maximal Albanese dimension in Theorem
3.5
. However, we decided to separate this case from the inductive case
(in other words, from the positive-dimensional fiber). Theorem
5.2
covers the case of maximal Albanese dimension.

In some special situations, such as in the case of varieties with
-K_{X}
nef, we can prove Conjecture
1.1
by induction under mild conditions. Recall that projective varieties with
-K_{X}
nef are a larger class than Fano varieties. One of the methods to study these varieties is to analyze their Albanese maps if they are irregular. In
(
Cao, 2019
, Theorem 1.2)
, Cao proved that for an irregular variety
X
with
-K_{X}
nef, the Albanese map
\operatorname{alb}:X\to\operatorname{Alb}(X)
is a locally trivial fibration. This result was conjectured in
(
Demailly et al., 1996
, Page 221)
, many authors contributed
to solving the problem and proved some partial results, for instance, in
(
Lu et al., 2010
, Page 532)
and
(
Zhang, 1996
, Theorem 1)
.

Further, Cao and Höring
(
Cao and Höring, 2019
, Theorem 1.4)
proved a decomposition structure theorem for varieties with
-K_{X}
nef. They showed that the universal cover
\widetilde{X}
of
X
decomposes as the following product:

\widetilde{X}\simeq\mathbb{C}^{d}\times\prod Y_{j}\times\prod S_{k}\times Z,

where
Y_{j}
are irreducible projective Calabi-Yau varieties,
S_{k}
are irreducible projective hyperkähler varieties, and
Z
is a projective rationally connected variety with
-K_{Z}
nef. Now, we state the following theorem for these type of varieties.

Theorem

(Theorem
3.10
)
.

Let
X
be an irregular variety of dimension
n\geq 2
with
-K_{X}
nef. Let
\operatorname{alb}:X\to\operatorname{Alb}(X)
be the Albanese map, and let
D
be an ample divisor on
X
. If Conjecture
1.1
holds in dimension
<n
and there exists an integer
r
with
1\leq r\leq\alpha(X)
such that
|rD_{|_{F}}|
is basepoint-free for every fiber
F
of
\operatorname{alb}
, then Conjecture
1.1
holds for
X
.

Organization of the paper.

In Section 2, after recalling preliminaries, we provide comparisons between the notion of a sheaf being
continuously globally generated
(Definition
2.2
) and the notion of a sheaf having no
essential basepoints
(Definition
2.8
). While these results are not necessarily needed in the proof of Theorem
3.5
and Theorem
3.10
, they are of some independent interest and are given in Proposition
2.10
and Proposition
2.12
.

In Section 3, we study Fujita’s Conjecture
1.1
on irregular varieties. We obtain some interesting results using the Albanese map, and by assuming the conjecture holds in lower dimensions. In particular, we provide the proofs of Theorem
3.5
and Theorem
3.10
.

In Section 4, we study the basepoint-freeness of adjoint series for varieties
fibered over abelian varieties, where we state Proposition
4.1
and Theorem
4.2
.

In Section 5, we explore the basepoint-freeness of adjoint series for varieties
of maximal Albanese dimension. In this case, the Albanese map
\operatorname{alb}
is generically finite. As a consequence, we obtain Theorem
5.2
.

Definition 2.1

(
(
Pareschi and Popa, 2008
, Definition 2.3)
)
.

A coherent sheaf
\mathcal{F}
on an abelian variety
Y
with
\dim Y=g
satisfies
IT with index
0
if

H^{i}(Y,\mathcal{F}\otimes p)=0\hskip 5.69046pt\text{for all }p\in\operatorname{Pic}^{0}(Y)\hskip 5.69046pt\text{and for all }i>0.

Definition 2.2

(
(
Pareschi and Popa, 2003
, Definition 2.10)
)
.

Let
X
be an irregular variety, and let
\mathcal{F}
be a coherent sheaf on
X
. We say that
\mathcal{F}
is
continuously globally generated
if for any nonempty open subset
U\subseteq\operatorname{Pic}^{0}(X)
, the sum of
evaluation maps

\bigoplus_{p\in U}H^{0}(X,\mathcal{F}\otimes p)\otimes p^{\vee}\to\mathcal{F}

is surjective.

We recall the Fourier-Mukai setting. We refer to Mukai
Mukai (1981)
for more details. We denote by
\mathcal{P}
the Poincaré line bundle on
Y\times\operatorname{Pic}^{0}(Y)
, and
Y
is an abelian variety as before. For any coherent sheaf
\mathcal{F}
on
Y
, we can associate the sheaf
{p_{2}}_{*}(p_{1}^{*}\mathcal{F}\otimes\mathcal{P})
on
\operatorname{Pic}^{0}(Y)
where

p_{1}
and
p_{2}
are the natural projections on
Y
and
\operatorname{Pic}^{0}(Y)
, respectively. This correspondence gives a
functor

\hat{S}:\operatorname{Coh}(Y)\to\operatorname{Coh}(\operatorname{Pic}^{0}(Y)).

If we denote by
\operatorname{D}(Y)
and
\operatorname{D}(\operatorname{Pic}^{0}(Y))
the bounded derived categories of
\operatorname{Coh}(Y)
and

\operatorname{Coh}(\operatorname{Pic}^{0}(Y))
, then the derived functor

\mathcal{R}\hat{S}:\operatorname{D}(Y)\to\operatorname{D}(\operatorname{Pic}^{0}(Y))

is defined and called the
Fourier-Mukai functor. Similarly, we consider

\mathcal{R}S:\operatorname{D}(\operatorname{Pic}^{0}(Y))\to\operatorname{D}(Y)

in a similar
way. According to the celebrated result of Mukai
(
Mukai, 1981
, Theorem 2.2)
, the Fourier-Mukai functor induces an equivalence of categories between the two derived categories
\operatorname{D}(Y)
and
\operatorname{D}(\operatorname{Pic}^{0}(Y))
. More precisely, we have

\mathcal{R}S\circ\mathcal{R}\hat{S}\simeq(-1_{Y})^{*}[-g]

and

\mathcal{R}\hat{S}\circ\mathcal{R}S\simeq(-1_{\operatorname{Pic}^{0}(Y)})^{*}[-g]

where
[-g]
is an operation that shifts the complex
g
places
to the right.

Now, we describe the cohomological support locus
Green and Lazarsfeld (1987)

Definition 2.3

(
(
Green and Lazarsfeld, 1987
, Page 389)
)
.

Let
\mathcal{F}
be a coherent sheaf on an abelian variety
Y
of dimension
g
. The
i

V^{i}(\mathcal{F})
of
\mathcal{F}
is defined to be:

V^{i}(\mathcal{F}):=\{p\in\operatorname{Pic}^{0}(Y)|H^{i}(Y,\mathcal{F}\otimes p)\neq 0\},\hskip 5.69046pt\text{for all }0\leq i\leq g.

The cohomological support locus are studied very carefully in
Green and Lazarsfeld (1987)
and
Simpson (1993)

Remark 2.4
.

By base change, the following inclusion always holds:

\operatorname{Supp}(\mathcal{R}^{i}\hat{S}(\mathcal{F}))\subseteq V^{i}(\mathcal{F}).

Definition 2.5

(
(
Pareschi and Popa, 2008
, Definition 3.1)
)
.

A coherent sheaf
\mathcal{F}
on
Y
is called
M-regular
if

\text{codim}_{\operatorname{Pic}^{0}(Y)}\text{(Supp}(\mathcal{R}^{i}\hat{S}(\mathcal{F})))>i\hskip 5.69046pt\text{for all }i\geq 1.

A coherent sheaf
\mathcal{F}
on
Y
is called a
generic vanishing sheaf
or a

if its cohomological support locus
V^{i}(\mathcal{F})
satisfies the following inequality:

\text{codim}_{\operatorname{Pic}^{0}(Y)}(V^{i}(\mathcal{F}))\geq i\hskip 5.69046pt\text{for all }i\geq 1.

We remark that
M
-regularity is achieved, in particular, if

\text{codim}_{\operatorname{Pic}^{0}(Y)}(V^{i}(\mathcal{F}))>i\hskip 5.69046pt\text{for all }i\geq 1.

Remark 2.6
.

If
\mathcal{F}
is an
M
-regular sheaf on an abelian variety
Y
, then
\mathcal{F}
is a
GV
-sheaf. Furthermore, if
\mathcal{F}
satisfies
IT
with index
0
, then it is clearly

-regular. In the following proposition, we recall that the

-regular property is stronger than the notion of being continuously globally generated.

Proposition 2.7

(
(
Pareschi and Popa, 2003
, Proposition 2.13)
)
.

Every
M
-regular coherent sheaf
\mathcal{F}
on an abelian variety
Y
is continuously globally generated.

Definition 2.8

(
(
Chen and Hacon, 2002

)
.

A coherent sheaf
\mathcal{F}
on an irregular variety
X
is said to have an

\mathcal{F}\to\mathbb{C}(x)
such that for all

, the induced map
H^{0}(X,\mathcal{F}\otimes p)\to H^{0}(X,\mathbb{C}(x))
is zero.

In
Chen and Hacon (2002)
, the authors proved the next proposition.

Proposition 2.9

(
(
Chen and Hacon, 2002
, Proposition 2.3)
)
.

If
\mathcal{F}
is a coherent sheaf on an abelian variety
Y
that satisfies
IT
with index
0
, then
\mathcal{F}
has no essential basepoints.

The proof of the main theorems does not rely on the following propositions, but we are interested in comparing Definition

Proposition 2.10
.

Let
\mathcal{F}
be a coherent sheaf on an irregular variety
X
. If
\mathcal{F}
is continuously globally generated, then
\mathcal{F}
has no essential basepoints.

Proof.

If
\mathcal{F}
is continuously globally generated, then for any nonempty open subset
U\subseteq\operatorname{Pic}^{0}(X)
, the sum of evaluation maps

\bigoplus_{p\in U}H^{0}(X,\mathcal{F}\otimes p)\otimes p^{\vee}\to\mathcal{F}

is surjective. Now, fix a point
x\in X
and suppose we are given a surjective map
\mathcal{F}\to\mathbb{C}(x)
. Thus, we have the following commutative diagram:

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\displaystyle\bigoplus_{p\in U}{H^{0}(X,\mathcal{F}\otimes p)\otimes p^{\vee}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{F}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbb{C}(x)}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\phi}

However, the induced map
\phi
defined by composition is surjective and hence nonzero. In particular, there exists
p\in U
such that the map

H^{0}(X,\mathcal{F}\otimes p)\otimes p^{\vee}\to\mathbb{C}(x)

is nonzero, and hence the map

H^{0}(X,\mathcal{F}\otimes p)\to\mathbb{C}(x)

is nonzero. Thus
\mathcal{F}
has no essential basepoints as claimed.
∎

Example 2.11
.

It is clear that every ample line bundle
L
on an abelian variety
Y
satisfies
IT
with index
0
, and thus is continuously globally generated. Furthermore, by Proposition
2.9
, we also see that
L
has no essential basepoints. On the other hand, the converse of Proposition

is not true. Indeed, for an irregular variety
X
,
\mathcal{O}_{X}\in\operatorname{Pic}^{0}(X)
has no essential basepoints, however, it is not continuously globally generated.

In the next proposition, we provide a sufficient condition for Definition
2.2
and Definition
2.8
to be equivalent in the case of line bundles.

Proposition 2.12
.

Let
X
be an irregular variety, and let
D
be a divisor on
X
such that
h^{0}(X,\mathcal{O}_{X}(D)\otimes p)
is constant for all
p\in\operatorname{Pic}^{0}(X)
. Then,
\mathcal{O}_{X}(D)
has no essential basepoints if and only if
\mathcal{O}_{X}(D)
is continuously globally generated.

Proof.

Assume that
\mathcal{O}_{X}(D)
has no essential basepoints, then for all
x\in X
and for any surjective map

\psi:\mathcal{O}_{X}(D)\to\mathbb{C}(x),

we can find
p_{x}\in\operatorname{Pic}^{0}(X)
(Definition
2.8
) such that the induced map

H^{0}(X,\mathcal{O}_{X}(D)\otimes p_{x})\to H^{0}(X,\mathbb{C}(x))

is surjective. Thus

h^{0}(X,\operatorname{Ker}\psi\otimes p_{x})=h^{0}(X,\mathcal{O}_{X}(D)\otimes p_{x})-1.

By the upper semi-continuity of
h^{0}(X,\operatorname{Ker}\psi\otimes p)
as
p
varies in
\operatorname{Pic}^{0}(X)
, we deduce that

h^{0}(X,\operatorname{Ker}\psi\otimes p_{x})=h^{0}(X,\operatorname{Ker}\psi\otimes p)

for general
p\in\operatorname{Pic}^{0}(X)
. By assumption,
h^{0}(X,\mathcal{O}_{X}(D)\otimes p)
is constant for all
p\in\operatorname{Pic}^{0}(X)
. Therefore, we conclude that the map

H^{0}(X,\mathcal{O}_{X}(D)\otimes p)\to H^{0}(X,\mathbb{C}(x))\simeq\mathbb{C}(x)

(2.1)

is surjective for general
p\in\operatorname{Pic}^{0}(X)
.

Now, we shall establish that
\mathcal{O}_{X}(D)
is continuously globally generated. In other words, we will prove for all
x\in X
and for all
U\subseteq\operatorname{Pic}^{0}(X)
nonempty open subset, the sum of evaluation maps

\bigoplus_{p\in U}H^{0}(X,\mathcal{O}_{X}(D)\otimes p)\otimes p^{\vee}\to\mathcal{O}_{X}(D)_{|_{x}}\simeq\mathbb{C}(x)

(2.2)

is surjective. Indeed, the map
(\ref{surforgen})
is surjective for general
p\in\operatorname{Pic}^{0}(X)
, in particular for some
p_{U}\in U
. Thus, twisting the map (
2.1
) by
p_{U}^{\vee}
and then the following map

H^{0}(X,\mathcal{O}_{X}(D)\otimes p_{U})\otimes p_{U}^{\vee}\to\mathbb{C}(x)

is surjective. Finally, we conclude that the sum of
evaluation maps (
2.2
) is surjective as desired. The converse is proved in Proposition
2.10
.
∎

3
Fujita’s Freeness Conjecture on Irregular Varieties

Before presenting the main results of this article, we first establish some auxiliary lemmas that will be used in the subsequent proofs. These preliminary results serve as key technical ingredients and help clarify the arguments that follow.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
X
be an irregular variety of dimension
n\geq 2
with
h:X\to Y
be a morphism to an Abelian variety
Y
. Let
X\xrightarrow{f}Z\xrightarrow{u}Y
be the Stein factorization of
h
, and let
F
be a general fiber of
f
. Let
N
be a divisor on
X
. If
|N_{|_{F}}|
is basepoint-free and
f_{*}\mathcal{O}_{X}(N)
is continuously globally generated, then
|N+h^{*}p|
has no basepoints supported on
F
for general
p\in\operatorname{Pic}^{0}(Y)
.

Proof.

Indeed, take a point
x\in F
such that
z=f(x)
. By assumption,
f_{*}\mathcal{O}_{X}(N)
is continuously globally generated, that is
for every nonempty open subset
U\subset\operatorname{Pic}^{0}(Z)
, the following direct sum of evaluation maps

\bigoplus_{p\in U}H^{0}(Z,f_{*}\mathcal{O}_{X}(N)\otimes p)\otimes p^{\vee}\to f_{*}\mathcal{O}_{X}(N)_{|_{z}}

(3.1)

is surjective. Since
\operatorname{Pic}^{0}(Y)\hookrightarrow\operatorname{Pic}^{0}(Z)
, we have in particular that for every nonempty open subset
U\subset\operatorname{Pic}^{0}(Y)
, the direct sum of evaluation maps

\bigoplus_{p\in U}H^{0}(Z,f_{*}\mathcal{O}_{X}(N)\otimes u^{*}p)\otimes u^{*}p^{\vee}\to f_{*}\mathcal{O}_{X}(N)_{|_{z}}

(3.2)

is surjective. Furthermore,

f_{*}\mathcal{O}_{X}(N)_{|_{z}}=H^{0}(F,\mathcal{O}_{F}(N))

(3.3)

and

H^{0}(Z,f_{*}\mathcal{O}_{X}(N)\otimes u^{*}p)\simeq H^{0}(X,\mathcal{O}_{X}(N)\otimes h^{*}p).

(3.4)

Combining (
3.4
) with (
3.3
) and (
3.2
), we obtain that the following sum of maps

\bigoplus_{p\in U}H^{0}(X,\mathcal{O}_{X}(N)\otimes h^{*}p)\otimes h^{*}p^{\vee}\to H^{0}(F,\mathcal{O}_{F}(N))

(3.5)

is surjective. By hypothesis,
|N_{|_{F}}|
is basepoint-free. Together with (
3.5
), this implies that for every nonempty open subset
U\subset\operatorname{Pic}^{0}(Y)
, the following direct sum of evaluation maps

\bigoplus_{p\in U}H^{0}(X,\mathcal{O}_{X}(N)\otimes h^{*}p)\otimes h^{*}p^{\vee}\to\mathcal{O}_{X}(N)_{|_{x}}

(3.6)

is surjective. In particular, for some
p_{U}\in U
, the map

H^{0}(X,\mathcal{O}_{X}(N)\otimes h^{*}p_{U})\otimes h^{*}p_{U}^{\vee}\to\mathcal{O}_{X}(N)_{|_{x}}

(3.7)

is surjective. Thus, twisting
(\ref{se5})
by
h^{*}p_{U}
, we deduce that

H^{0}(X,\mathcal{O}_{X}(N)\otimes h^{*}p_{U})\to\mathcal{O}_{X}(N)_{|_{x}}

(3.8)

is surjective. Hence, the linear system

|N+h^{*}p_{U}|

has no basepoints supported on
F
. Now, we define the following subset
S\subseteq\operatorname{Pic}^{0}(Y)
by

S:=\left\{p\in\operatorname{Pic}^{0}(Y)\;\middle|\;|N+h^{*}p|\text{ has no basepoints supported on }F\right\}.

We claim that
S
is dense. Indeed, for any nonempty open subset
U\subseteq\operatorname{Pic}^{0}(Y)
, one can find an element
p_{U}\in U
such that
|N+h^{*}p_{U}|
has no basepoints supported on
F
, as proved. Therefore,
S\cap U\neq\emptyset
for every nonempty open subset

U\subseteq\operatorname{Pic}^{0}(Y)
. Hence,
S
is dense.
Otherwise,
\left(\operatorname{Pic}^{0}(Y)\setminus\overline{S}\right)\cap S\neq\emptyset
,
a contradiction. It follows that

|N+h^{*}p|

has no basepoints supported on
F
for general

p\in\operatorname{Pic}^{0}(Y)
.
∎

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
Y
and
Z
be irregular varieties and
u:Z\to Y
be a finite morphism. Let
\mathcal{F}
be a coherent sheaf on
Z
.

1)

If
H^{1}(Y,u_{*}\mathcal{F}\otimes p)=0
for all
p\in\operatorname{Pic}^{0}(Y)
and
u_{*}\mathcal{F}
has no essential basepoints, then
\mathcal{F}
has no essential basepoints.

2)

If
u_{*}\mathcal{F}
is continuously globally generated, then
\mathcal{F}
is continuously globally generated.

Proof.

Assuming that
H^{1}(Y,u_{*}\mathcal{F}\otimes p)=0
for all
p\in\operatorname{Pic}^{0}(Y)
and that
u_{*}\mathcal{F}
has no essential basepoints, we will prove that
\mathcal{F}
has no essential basepoints. Indeed,
we fix
z\in Z
,
y=u(z)\in Y
, and assume that there exists the following exact sequence:

0\to\mathcal{K}\to\mathcal{F}\to\mathbb{C}(z)\to 0\text{.}

(3.9)

By applying
u_{*}
to this exact sequence, we obtain:

0\to u_{*}\mathcal{K}\to u_{*}\mathcal{F}\to u_{*}\mathbb{C}(z)\to\mathcal{R}^{1}u_{*}\mathcal{K}\to\cdots

(3.10)

Since the map
u
is finite, we have
\mathcal{R}^{1}u_{*}\mathcal{K}=0
. Moreover, it is clear that
u_{*}\mathbb{C}(z)=\mathbb{C}(y)
, so the previous sequence reduces to a short exact sequence of sheaves:

0\to u_{*}\mathcal{K}\to u_{*}\mathcal{F}\to\mathbb{C}(y)\to 0\text{.}

(3.11)

Since
u_{*}\mathcal{F}
has no essential basepoints, there exists
p_{y}\in\operatorname{Pic}^{0}(Y)
such that the map

H^{0}(Y,u_{*}\mathcal{F}\otimes p_{y})\to\mathbb{C}(y)

(3.12)

is surjective. Twisting the sequence (
3.11
) by
p_{y}
and passing to the associated long exact sequence in cohomology, we obtain:

0\to H^{0}(Y,u_{*}\mathcal{K}\otimes p_{y})\to H^{0}(Y,u_{*}\mathcal{F}\otimes p_{y})\to\mathbb{C}(y)

\to H^{1}(Y,u_{*}\mathcal{K}\otimes p_{y})\to H^{1}(Y,u_{*}\mathcal{F}\otimes p_{y})\to 0\text{.}

From the assumption that

H^{1}(Y,u_{*}\mathcal{F}\otimes p_{y})=0

and the surjectivity of the map in (
3.12
), it follows that

H^{1}(Y,u_{*}\mathcal{K}\otimes p_{y})=0\text{,}

which in turn implies

H^{1}(Z,\mathcal{K}\otimes u^{*}p_{y})=0\text{.}

We now twist the sequence (
3.9
) by
u^{*}p_{y}
and pass to the associated long exact sequence in cohomology, obtaining:

0\to H^{0}(Z,\mathcal{K}\otimes u^{*}p_{y})\to H^{0}(Z,\mathcal{F}\otimes u^{*}p_{y})\to\mathbb{C}(z)\to 0\text{.}

It follows that the map

H^{0}(Z,\mathcal{F}\otimes u^{*}p_{y})\to\mathbb{C}(z)

is surjective. We conclude that
\mathcal{F}
has no essential basepoints.

Now, assume that
u_{*}\mathcal{F}
is continuously globally generated. That is, for every
y\in Y
and for every nonempty open subset
U\subset\operatorname{Pic}^{0}(Y)
, the following direct sum of evaluation maps

\bigoplus_{p\in U}H^{0}(Y,u_{*}\mathcal{F}\otimes p)\otimes p^{\vee}\to u_{*}\mathcal{F}_{|_{y}}

(3.13)

is surjective. Our goal is to prove that
\mathcal{F}
is continuously globally generated. Indeed, consider a nonzero element
v\in\mathcal{F}_{|_{z}}\simeq u_{*}(\mathcal{F}_{|_{z}})_{|_{y}}
where the isomorphism holds because
u
is finite. This implies that
v\in u_{*}\mathcal{F}_{|_{y}}
. Then, by the surjectivity of the map in (
3.13
), we can find elements
(p_{i})_{1\leq i\leq t}
in
\operatorname{Pic}^{0}(Y)
and section
(s_{i})_{1\leq i\leq t}
in
H^{0}(Y,u_{*}\mathcal{F}\otimes p_{i})
such that
s_{i}(y)\neq 0
for all
i
with
1\leq i\leq t
. By the isomorphism

H^{0}(Y,u_{*}\mathcal{F}\otimes p_{i})\simeq H^{0}(Z,\mathcal{F}\otimes u^{*}p_{i})

which holds for all
i
with
1\leq i\leq t
, we can find nonzero sections
(u_{i})_{1\leq i\leq t}
of
H^{0}(Z,\mathcal{F}\otimes u^{*}p_{i})

such that
v
has a preimage under the following sum of evaluation map defined by
(u_{i})_{1\leq i\leq t}
:

\bigoplus_{p\in U}H^{0}(Z,\mathcal{F}\otimes u^{*}p)\otimes u^{*}p^{\vee}\to\mathcal{F}_{|_{z}}\text{.}

(3.14)

Thus, we conclude that
\mathcal{F}
is continuously globally generated.
∎

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
X
be an irregular variety, and
h:X\to Y
be a morphism to an Abelian variety
Y
. Let
D
be a nef and big divisor on

. If
h_{*}\mathcal{O}_{X}(K_{X}+D)\neq 0
, then

satisfies

with index
0
.

Proof.

Applying Kawamata-Viehweg’s vanishing theorem (
(
Kawamata, 1982
, Theorem 1)
,
(
Viehweg, 1982
, Corollary 0.3)
), we have

H^{i}(X,\mathcal{O}_{X}(K_{X}+D)\otimes h^{*}p)=0\hskip 5.69046pt\text{for all }i\geq 1\hskip 5.69046pt\text{and for all }p\in\operatorname{Pic}^{0}(Y)\text{.}

(

, Theorem 2.2.1)

with index
0
.
∎

Proposition 3.4
.

Let
X
be an irregular variety, and
h:X\to Y
be a morphism to an Abelian variety
Y
. Let
N
and
M
be divisors on

. If a point

is not a basepoint of
|N+h^{*}p|
for general

,
and
x
is not a basepoint of
|M+h^{*}p|

is not a base point of

Theorem 3.5
.

Let
X
be an irregular variety of dimension
n\geq 2
with Albanese dimension
1\leq\alpha(X)<n
. Let
X\xrightarrow{f}Z\xrightarrow{u}\operatorname{alb}(X)\subseteq\operatorname{Alb}(X)

\operatorname{alb}
, and let
F
be a general fiber of the morphism
f
. Let
D
be an ample divisor on
X
.

1.1
holds in dimension
<n
, then
|K_{X}+mD+\operatorname{alb}^{*}p|
has no basepoint supported on
F
for all

and for general

Additionally, if the following condition is satisfied:

(*)

There exists an integer
r
with
1\leq r\leq\alpha(X)
such that
|rD_{|_{F}}|
is basepoint-free and
rD-K_{X}
is nef and big,

has no basepoint supported on

for all
m\geq n+1

Proof.

From the hypothesis, Conjecture
1.1
holds for lower-dimensional varieties, meaning that
|K_{F}+mD_{|_{F}}|
is basepoint-free for all

. In particular,

h^{0}(F,\omega_{F}\otimes\mathcal{O}_{F}(mD))\neq 0\hskip 5.69046pt\text{for all }m\geq n-\alpha(X)+1\text{,}

since
(K_{X}+mD)_{|_{F}}=K_{F}+mD_{|_{F}}
.
Thus,
f_{*}\mathcal{O}_{X}(K_{X}+mD)
is a nonzero coherent sheaf on
Z
for all
m\geq n-\alpha(X)+1
. Therefore,
\operatorname{alb}_{*}\mathcal{O}_{X}(K_{X}+mD)
is a nonzero coherent sheaf for all
m\geq n-\alpha(X)+1
, as the map
u
is finite.

Applying Lemma
3.3
, it follows that
\operatorname{alb}_{*}\mathcal{O}_{X}(K_{X}+mD)
satisfies
IT
with index
0
. By Remark
2.6
and Proposition
2.7
, we deduce that
\operatorname{alb}_{*}\mathcal{O}_{X}(K_{X}+mD)
is continuously globally generated.

Applying Lemma
3.2
, we conclude that

is continuously globally generated. Again, by assumption, we know that
|K_{F}+mD_{|_{F}}|
is basepoint-free for all
m\geq n-\alpha(X)+1
. Then, applying Lemma
3.1
, we deduce that
|K_{X}+mD+\operatorname{alb}^{*}p|
has no basepoint supported on
F
for all
m\geq n-\alpha(X)+1
and for general
p\in\operatorname{Pic}^{0}(\operatorname{Alb}(X))
. This proves the first statement.

Now, suppose that condition
(*)
is satisfied. In particular,
|rD_{|_{F}}|
is basepoint-free, and

h^{0}(F,\mathcal{O}_{F}(rD))\neq 0.

Therefore,
f_{*}\mathcal{O}_{X}(rD)
is a nonzero sheaf on
Z
, and so is
\operatorname{alb}_{*}\mathcal{O}_{X}(rD)
. We observe that

rD=K_{X}+rD-K_{X}.

Thus, applying Lemma
3.3
, we obtain that
\operatorname{alb}_{*}\mathcal{O}_{X}(rD)
satisfies
IT
with index
0
, and therefore,
\operatorname{alb}_{*}\mathcal{O}_{X}(rD)
is continuously globally generated. Applying Lemma
3.2
, we deduce that
f_{*}\mathcal{O}_{X}(D)
is continuously globally generated.

Again, by assumption, we know that
|rD_{|_{F}}|
is basepoint-free. Then, applying Lemma
3.1
, we conclude that

|rD+\operatorname{alb}^{*}p|

has no basepoint supported on a general fiber
F
for general
p\in\operatorname{Pic}^{0}(\operatorname{Alb}(X))
.

Finally, applying Proposition
3.4
to
|K_{X}+mD+\operatorname{alb}^{*}p|
and
|rD+\operatorname{alb}^{*}p|

|K_{X}+m^{{}^{\prime}}D|

has no basepoints supported on
F
for all
m^{{}^{\prime}}\geq n+r-\alpha(X)+1

.
∎

Remark 3.6

In Theorem

Example 3.7
.

Let
\mathcal{E}
be a rank
2
-vector bundle on elliptic curve

given by the following non-split short exact sequence:

0\to\mathcal{O}_{C}\to\mathcal{E}\to\mathcal{O}_{C}\to 0\text{.}

(3.15)

We take the ruled surface

is a fiber. We know that the canonical line bundle

But since

. Now, let

0\to H^{0}(X,\mathcal{O}_{X}(F))\to H^{0}(X,\mathcal{O}_{X}(D))\to H^{0}(B,\mathcal{O}_{B}(D))\to 0

is not basepoint-free.

4
Basepoint-Freeness of
Adjoint Series for Varieties Fibered over Abelian Varieties

In the following proposition, we consider the base to be an abelian variety, not necessarily the Albanese variety. We are interested in studying the linear system defined by the canonical sheaf twisted by an ample line bundle from the base, under the assumption that the morphism is an algebraic fiber space and that a general fiber has a basepoint-free canonical bundle.

Proposition 4.1
.

Let
h:X\to Y
be a surjective morphism with connected fibers onto an abelian variety
Y
of dimension
g
, and let
F
be a general fiber of
h
. If
|K_{F}|
is basepoint-free and
\Theta
is an ample divisor on
Y
, then
|K_{X}+2h^{*}\Theta|
has no basepoints supported on
F
.

Proof.

From the hypothesis,
|K_{F}|
is basepoint-free. In particular,

h^{0}(F,\omega_{F})\neq 0\text{.}

Thus,
h_{*}\mathcal{O}_{X}(K_{X})
is a nonzero coherent sheaf.

By Kollár’s vanishing theorem (
(
Kollár, 1986
, Theorem 2.1)
), we have

H^{i}(Y,h_{*}\mathcal{O}_{X}(K_{X})\otimes\mathcal{O}_{Y}(\Theta)\otimes p)=0\hskip 5.69046pt\text{for all }i\geq 1\hskip 5.69046pt\text{and for all }p\in\operatorname{Pic}^{0}(Y).

Thus,
h_{*}\mathcal{O}_{X}(K_{X})\otimes\mathcal{O}_{Y}(\Theta)
is a nonzero coherent sheaf satisfies
IT
with index
0
. Therefore, it is continuously globally generated. By assumption,
|K_{F}|
is basepoint-free. Thus, applying Lemma
3.1
, we deduce that

|K_{X}+h^{*}\Theta+h^{*}p|

has no basepoint supported on
F
for general
p\in\operatorname{Pic}^{0}(Y)
.

Moreover, since
\Theta
is an ample divisor on
Y
, it satisfies
IT
with index
0
. Furthermore,
h^{*}\Theta
is trivial when restricted to any fiber. Thus, applying Lemma
3.1
again, we deduce that

|h^{*}(\Theta+p)|

is basepoint-free for general
p\in\operatorname{Pic}^{0}(Y)
.

Finally, applying Proposition
3.4
to
|K_{X}+h^{*}\Theta+h^{*}p|
and
|h^{*}(\Theta+p)|
, we conclude that

|K_{X}+2h^{*}\Theta|

has no basepoints supported on
F
.
∎

In the next theorem, we consider the Albanese map and remove condition
(*)
from Theorem
3.5
. We observe that if we assume the adjoint linear system of a general fiber is basepoint-free, then the linear system defined by the adjoint canonical bundle twisted with an ample line bundle from the base has no basepoints supported on a general fiber.

Theorem 4.2
.

Assume that the Albanese map
\operatorname{alb}:X\to\operatorname{Alb}(X)
is a surjective morphism with connected fibers, and let
F
be a general fiber. Let
D
be a nef and big divisor on
X
, and
\Theta
an ample divisor on
\operatorname{Alb}(X)
. If there exists an integer
c>0
such that
|K_{F}+mD_{|_{F}}|
is basepoint-free on
F
for all
m\geq c
, then
|K_{X}+mD+\operatorname{alb}^{*}\Theta|
has no basepoints supported on a general fiber
F
for all
m\geq c
.

Proof.

From the hypothesis,
|K_{F}+mD_{|_{F}}|
is basepoint-free for all
m\geq c
. In particular,

h^{0}(F,\omega_{F}\otimes\mathcal{O}_{F}(mD))\neq 0\hskip 5.69046pt\text{for all }m\geq c\text{.}

Thus,
\operatorname{alb}_{*}\mathcal{O}_{X}(K_{X}+mD)
is a nonzero coherent sheaf for all
m\geq c
.

Applying Lemma
3.3
, it follows that
\operatorname{alb}_{*}\mathcal{O}_{X}(K_{X}+mD)
satisfies
IT
with index
0
. By Remark
2.6
and Proposition
2.7
, we deduce that
\operatorname{alb}_{*}\mathcal{O}_{X}(K_{X}+mD)
is continuously globally generated. Thus, applying Lemma
3.1
, we conclude that

|K_{X}+mD+\operatorname{alb}^{*}p|

has no basepoint supported on
F
for all
m\geq c
and for general
p\in\operatorname{Pic}^{0}(\operatorname{Alb}(X))
.

Applying Lemma
3.1
to
\operatorname{alb}^{*}(\Theta)
, we obtain that

|\operatorname{alb}^{*}(\Theta+p)|

is basepoint-free for general
p\in\operatorname{Pic}^{0}(\operatorname{Alb}(X))
.

Finally, applying Proposition
3.4
, we conclude that

|K_{X}+mD+\operatorname{alb}^{*}\Theta|

has no basepoints supported on
F
for all
m\geq c
.
∎

5
Basepoint-Freeness of Adjoint Series for Varieties of Maximal Albanese Dimension

In what follows, we present some results on the basepoint-freeness of linear series, assuming that
X
is an irregular variety of dimension
n
with maximal Albanese dimension, that is,
\alpha(X)=n
. Related results on basepoint-freeness, in the setting of varieties whose Albanese morphism is finite, were obtained in
(
Pareschi and Popa, 2003
, Theorem 5.1)
. First, we recall the definition of the exceptional set of
\operatorname{alb}
.

Definition 5.1
.

Let
X
be an irregular variety of maximal Albanese dimension. Let
X\xrightarrow{f}Z\xrightarrow{u}\operatorname{alb}(X)\subseteq\operatorname{Alb}(X)
be the Stein factorization of
\operatorname{alb}
. The exceptional set of
\operatorname{alb}
and
f
is the subset of
X
where the morphism
\operatorname{alb}
is not finite.

Theorem 5.2
.

Let
X
be an irregular variety of maximal Albanese dimension, that is
\alpha(X)=n
, and let
D
be a nef and big divisor on
X
such that
nD-K_{X}
is nef and big, or
nD
is continuously globally generated. Then
|K_{X}+mD|
is basepoint-free outside the exceptional set of
\operatorname{alb}
for all
m\geq n+1
.

Proof.

Since
X
is a variety of maximal Albanese dimension, the Albanese map is generically finite. We take the Stein factorization
X\xrightarrow{f}Z\xrightarrow{u}\operatorname{alb}(X)\subseteq\operatorname{Alb}(X)
of
\operatorname{alb}
, where
f
is a birational map. Thus,
f_{*}\mathcal{O}_{X}(K_{X}+D)
is nonzero, and consequently, so is
\operatorname{alb}_{*}\mathcal{O}_{X}(K_{X}+D)
. Thus,
\operatorname{alb}_{*}\mathcal{O}_{X}(K_{X}+D)
is a nonzero coherent sheaf that satisfies
IT
with index
0
, by Lemma
3.3
. Therefore, it is continuously globally generated. By Lemma
3.2
, this implies that

|K_{X}+D+\operatorname{alb}^{*}p|

has no basepoints outside the exceptional set of
\operatorname{alb}
for general
p\in\operatorname{Pic}^{0}(\operatorname{Alb}(X))
.

By assumption
nD-K_{X}
is nef and big. Then, applying Lemma
3.3
, we deduce that
\operatorname{alb}_{*}\mathcal{O}_{X}(mD)
is a nonzero coherent sheaf that satisfies
IT
with index
0
for all
m\geq n
. Hence, it is continuously globally generated. Thus,

|mD+\operatorname{alb}^{*}p|

is basepoint-free outside the exceptional set of
\operatorname{alb}
for all
p\in\operatorname{Pic}^{0}(\operatorname{Alb}(X))
and for all
m\geq n
, by Lemma
3.2
.

Finally, applying Proposition
3.4
, we conclude that
|K_{X}+m^{{}^{\prime}}D|
is basepoint-free outside the exceptional set of
\operatorname{alb}
for all
m^{{}^{\prime}}\geq n+1
.
∎

Remark 5.3
.

If we assume that the Albanese map is finite in Theorem
5.2
, under the same conditions on
D
and
nD-K_{X}
, then
|K_{X}+mD|
is basepoint-free on
X
for all
m\geq n+1
.

Corollary 5.4
.

Let
X
be a minimal variety of general type with maximal Albanese dimension,
\alpha(X)=n
. Then,
|4K_{X}|
is basepoint-free outside the exceptional set of
\operatorname{alb}
.

Proof.

We take the Stein factorization
X\xrightarrow{f}Z\xrightarrow{u}\operatorname{alb}(X)\subseteq\operatorname{Alb}(X)
of
\operatorname{alb}
, where
f
is a birational map. Thus,
f_{*}\mathcal{O}_{X}(2K_{X})
is nonzero, and consequently, so is
\operatorname{alb}_{*}\mathcal{O}_{X}(2K_{X})
.

Since
K_{X}
is nef and big, we apply Lemma
3.3
to obtain that

\operatorname{alb}_{*}\mathcal{O}_{X}(2K_{X})
is a nonzero coherent sheaf that satisfies
IT
with index
0
. Consequently, it is continuously globally generated. Thus,

|2K_{X}+\operatorname{alb}^{*}p|

is basepoint-free outside the exceptional set of
\operatorname{alb}
for all
p\in\operatorname{Pic}^{0}(\operatorname{Alb}(X))
. Using this fact twice, we conclude that
|4K_{X}|
is basepoint-free outside the exceptional set of
\operatorname{alb}
by Proposition
3.4
.
∎

Remark 5.5
.

This last corollary is not sharp. Indeed, in
Jiang et al. (2013)
, the authors proved that
|3K_{X}|
is birational under the assumption that
X
is an irregular variety of general type and of maximal Albanese dimension.

The next proposition is an analogue of Proposition
4.1
for varieties admitting a finite morphism to an abelian variety.

Proposition 5.6
.

Let
h:X\to Y
be a surjective finite morphism from
X
to an abelian variety
Y
. Let
\Theta
be an ample divisor on
Y
. Then
|K_{X}+2h^{*}(\Theta)|
is basepoint-free on
X
.

Proof.

By Kollár’s vanishing theorem (
(
Kollár, 1986
, Theorem 2.1)
), we have

H^{i}(Y,h_{*}\mathcal{O}_{X}(K_{X})\otimes\mathcal{O}_{Y}(\Theta)\otimes p)=0\hskip 5.69046pt\text{for all }i\geq 1\hskip 5.69046pt\text{and for all }p\in\operatorname{Pic}^{0}(Y).

Thus,
h_{*}\mathcal{O}_{X}(K_{X})\otimes\mathcal{O}_{Y}(\Theta)
is a nonzero coherent sheaf that satisfies
IT
with index
0
. Therefore, it is continuously globally generated. By Lemma
3.2
, it follows that

|K_{X}+h^{*}\Theta+h^{*}p|

is basepoint-free for general
p\in\operatorname{Pic}^{0}(Y)
.

Since
\Theta
is an ample divisor on
Y
, it satisfies
IT
with index
0
. Thus,

|h^{*}(\Theta+p)|

is basepoint-free for general
p\in\operatorname{Pic}^{0}(Y)
, by Lemma
3.2
. Applying Proposition
3.4
, we conclude that

|K_{X}+2h^{*}\Theta|

is basepoint-free.
∎

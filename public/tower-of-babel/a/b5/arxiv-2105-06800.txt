A new approach to space-time boundary integral equations for the wave equation
Olaf Steinbach, Carolina Urzúa-Torres

Abstract
We present a new approach for boundary integral equations for the wave equation with zero initial conditions. Unlike previous attempts, our mathematical formulation allows us to prove that the associated boundary integral operators are continuous and satisfy inf-sup conditions in trace spaces of the same regularity, which are closely related to standard energy spaces with the expected regularity in space and time. This feature is crucial from a numerical perspective, as it provides the foundations to derive sharper error estimates and paves the way to devise efficient adaptive space-time boundary element methods, which will be tackled in future work. On the other hand, the proposed approach is compatible with current time dependent boundary element method’s implementations and we predict that it explains many of the behaviours observed in practice but that were not understood with the existing theory.

Introduction

Different strategies have been used to derive variational methods for time
domain boundary integral equations for the wave equation.
The more established and successful ones include weak formulations derived
via the Laplace transform, and also space-time energetic variational
formulations, often referred as
energetic BEM
in the literature.
These approaches started with the groundbreaking works of Bamberger and
Ha Duong
[
4
]
, and Aimi et al.
[
3
]
, respectively.
In spite of their extensive use
[
1
,
2
,
5
,
12
,
13
,
14
,
15
,
16
,
17
,
18
,
23
,
24
,
25
]

at the time of writing this article,
the numerical analysis corresponding to these formulations was still incomplete
and presents difficulties that are hard to overcome, if possible at all.

One of these difficulties is the fact that current approaches provide continuity
and coercivity estimates which are not in the same space-time (Sobolev) norms.
Indeed, there is a so-called
norm gap
arising from a loss of regularity
in time of the related boundary integral operators. Yet, recent work by Joly
and Rodríguez shows that these norm gaps are not present in 1D
[
18
]
.
Moreover, to the best of the authors’ knowledge, there is no proof nor numerical
evidence that such loss of time regularity should hold for higher dimensions
either. These two observations encouraged us to believe that one may be able to
prove sharper results using different mathematical tools.
Another disadvantage of current strategies is that they do not provide the
foundations for space-time boundary element methods, which are basically
boundary element discretizations where the time variable is treated simply as
another space variable, in contrast to techniques such as time-stepping methods
and convolution quadrature methods
[
25
]
.

Space-time discretization methods offer an increasingly popular alternative,
since they allow the treatment of moving boundaries, adaptivity in space and
time simultaneously, and space-time parallelization

[
11
,
26
,
27
,
29
]
.
However, in order to exploit these advantages, one needs to have a complete numerical
analysis of the corresponding Galerkin methods.

We construct a new approach to boundary integral equations for the wave
equation by working directly in the time domain. Furthermore, we develop a
mathematical framework that not only overcomes the aforementioned
difficulties, but also paves the way to stable space-time FEM/BEM coupling.
We present these new results following the standard pieces and arguments
from classical boundary integral equations. We hope this highlights some
mathematical intuitions behind the obtained results and makes the article
easier to read for those familiarized with the boundary integral equation
literature.
In addition to a new boundary integral equation formulation, we provide novel
existence and uniqueness results for the Dirichlet and Neumann wave equation
initial boundary value problems, when initial conditions are zero.

The structure of this article is as follows.
Section
2
introduces notation and summarizes results from
the literature that will be needed later in the paper. We begin by using some
key ideas of recent work on the wave equation in
H^{1}(Q)

[
30
,
34
]
.
Then, in Section
3
, we introduce trace spaces, trace operators
and their corresponding properties for three different families of spaces. With
this we aim, on the one hand, to emphasize the link between the existing
space-time (volumetric) variational formulations and our
new results. On the other hand, we
prove that the related trace spaces are indeed connected, which provides a new
and deeper understanding of the different existing boundary integral formulations
and their relation. Section
4
presents some required results on
initial boundary value problems for the wave equation, while all the remaining
building blocks of the new boundary integral equation formulation are presented
in Section
5
.
This final section concludes with the existence and uniqueness
results for solutions of related boundary integral equations.

Notation and mathematical framework

Let
{\mathcal{O}}\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{m}
,

We stick to the usual notation for the space

\displaystyle H^{1}_{0,}(0,T;L^{2}(\Omega))

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle\Big\{v\in L^{2}(Q):\partial_{t}v\in L^{2}(Q),\;v(x,0)=0\quad\mbox{for}\;x\in\Omega\Big\},

\displaystyle H^{1}_{,0}(0,T;L^{2}(\Omega))

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle\Big\{v\in L^{2}(Q):\partial_{t}v\in L^{2}(Q),\;v(x,T)=0\quad\mbox{for}\;x\in\Omega\Big\}.

\displaystyle H^{1,1}_{;0,}(Q)

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle L^{2}(0,T;H^{1}(\Omega))\cap H^{1}_{0,}(0,T;L^{2}(\Omega)),

\displaystyle H^{1,1}_{;,0}(Q)

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle L^{2}(0,T;H^{1}(\Omega))\cap H^{1}_{,0}(0,T;L^{2}(\Omega)),

\displaystyle\|u\|_{H^{1,1}_{;0,}(Q)}:=\sqrt{\|\partial_{t}u\|_{L^{2}(Q)}^{2}+\|\nabla_{x}u\|^{2}_{L^{2}(Q)}}\,,

\displaystyle\|v\|_{H^{1,1}_{;,0}(Q)}:=\sqrt{\|\partial_{t}v\|_{L^{2}(Q)}^{2}+\|\nabla_{x}v\|^{2}_{L^{2}(Q)}}\,.

Note that the space

corresponds to

\vphantom{,}{}_{0}H^{1}(Q)
as used in
[

,
20
,
21
]
.
In the case of zero Dirichlet boundary data along the lateral boundary
\Sigma

we define the subspaces

\displaystyle H^{1,1}_{0;0,}(Q)

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle L^{2}(0,T;H^{1}_{0}(\Omega))\cap H^{1}_{0,}(0,T;L^{2}(\Omega)),

\displaystyle H^{1,1}_{0;,0}(Q)

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle L^{2}(0,T;H^{1}_{0}(\Omega))\cap H^{1}_{,0}(0,T;L^{2}(\Omega)).

We remark that
H^{1,1}_{;0,}(Q)
and
H^{1,1}_{0;0,}(Q)
prescribe zero initial

, while
H^{1,1}_{;,0}(Q)

In this paper we will consider, as in

[
31
]
, a generalized variational formulation to describe
solutions of the wave equation (

) also for

, we define

\widetilde{u}\in L^{2}(Q_{-})

\widetilde{u}(x,t):=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ccl}u(x,t)&&\mbox{for}\;(x,t)\in Q,\\
0&&\mbox{for}\;(x,t)\in Q_{-}\backslash Q.\end{array}\right.

The application of the wave operator
\Box
to
\widetilde{u}\in L^{2}(Q_{-})

is defined as a distribution on
Q_{-}
, i.e., for all test functions

\varphi\in C^{\infty}_{0}(Q_{-})
, we define

\langle\Box\widetilde{u},\varphi\rangle_{Q_{-}}:=\int_{-T}^{T}\int_{\Omega}\widetilde{u}(x,t)\,\Box\varphi(x,t)\,dx\,dt=\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}u(x,t)\,\Box\varphi(x,t)\,dx\,dt\,.

This motivates to consider the Sobolev space
H^{1}_{0}(Q_{-})
with the
norm

\|\phi\|_{H^{1}_{0}(Q_{-})}=\sqrt{\|\partial_{t}\phi\|^{2}_{L^{2}(Q_{-})}+\|\nabla_{x}\phi\|_{L^{2}(Q_{-})}^{2}}\quad\mbox{for}\;\phi\in H^{1}_{0}(Q_{-}),

[H^{1}_{0}(Q_{-})]^{\prime}
, and the duality pairing

\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{Q_{-}}

{\mathcal{E}}:H^{1,1}_{0;,0}(Q)\to H^{1}_{0}(Q_{-})

\|{\mathcal{E}}\|_{H^{1,1}_{0;,0}(Q),H^{1}_{0}(Q_{-})}:=\sup\limits_{0\neq v\in H^{1,1}_{0;,0}(Q)}\frac{\|{\mathcal{E}}v\|_{H^{1}_{0}(Q_{-})}}{\|v\|_{H^{1,1}_{0;,0}(Q)}}\,,

and its adjoint

{\mathcal{E}}^{\prime}:[H^{1}_{0}(Q_{-})]^{\prime}\to[H^{1,1}_{0;,0}(Q)]^{\prime}
, satisfying

{\mathcal{R}}{\mathcal{E}}\phi=\phi

\phi\in H^{1,1}_{0;,0}(Q)
.

As in
[
31
]
we introduce the Banach space

2.3
Transformation operator
\mathcal{H}_{T}

For
u\in L^{2}(0,T)
we consider the Fourier series

\displaystyle u(t)=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}u_{k}\sin\left(\left(\frac{\pi}{2}+k\pi\right)\frac{t}{T}\right),\quad u_{k}=\frac{2}{T}\int_{0}^{T}u(t)\sin\left(\left(\frac{\pi}{2}+k\pi\right)\frac{t}{T}\right)\,dt.

In the following, we summarize some additional properties fulfilled by the

and
\mathcal{H}_{T}^{-1}

[

]

Proposition 2.1
.

1.

For any

\displaystyle\langle\mathcal{H}_{T}u,v\rangle_{L^{2}(0,T)}=\langle u,\mathcal{H}_{T}^{-1}v\rangle_{L^{2}(0,T)}.

2.

For all

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\mathcal{H}_{T}u=-\mathcal{H}_{T}^{-1}\partial_{t}u.

3.

\mathcal{H}_{T}
and

are norm preserving, i.e.,

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{H}_{T}w\|_{L^{2}(0,T)}=\|w\|_{L^{2}(0,T)},\quad\|\mathcal{H}_{T}^{-1}w\|_{L^{2}(0,T)}=\|w\|_{L^{2}(0,T)}\quad\forall w\in L^{2}(0,T).

\displaystyle\langle w,\mathcal{H}_{T}w\rangle_{L^{2}(0,T)}\geq 0.

We conclude this subsection by extending the modified Hilbert transformation

2.4
Fundamental solution and retarded potentials

Let us briefly present the
boundary layer potentials
for the wave
equation, often called
retarded potentials
. We refer the reader to

[
9
]
and
[
16
]
for further details.
First, we introduce the fundamental solution of the wave equation,

\displaystyle G(x,t)=\begin{cases}\dfrac{1}{2}\,\mathsf{H}(t-\left\lvert x\right\rvert),&\quad n=1,\\[14.45377pt]
\dfrac{1}{2\pi}\,\dfrac{\mathsf{H}(t-\left\lvert x\right\rvert)}{\sqrt{t^{2}-\left\lvert x\right\rvert^{2}}},&\quad n=2,\\[21.68121pt]
\dfrac{1}{4\pi}\,\dfrac{\delta(t-\left\lvert x\right\rvert)}{\left\lvert x\right\rvert},&\quad n=3,\end{cases}

(2.3)

with
\delta
the Dirac distribution, and
\mathsf{H}

the Heaviside step function.
Let
\mathscr{S}
be the
single layer potential
and
\mathscr{D}
the

double layer potential
, i.e., for
(x,t)\in Q
and
regular enough densities
w
and
z
, respectively,

\displaystyle(\mathscr{S}w)(x,t)

\displaystyle:=\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\Gamma}G(x-y,t-\tau)\,w(y,\tau)\,ds_{y}\,d\tau,

(2.4)

\displaystyle(\mathscr{D}z)(x,t)

\displaystyle:=\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\Gamma}\partial_{n_{y}}G(x-y,t-\tau)\,z(y,\tau)\,ds_{y}\,d\tau.

(2.5)

Concretely, for
n=3
, these are

\displaystyle(\mathscr{S}w)(x,t)

\displaystyle:=\frac{1}{4\pi}\int_{\Gamma}\frac{w(y,t-\left\lvert x-y\right\rvert)}{\left\lvert x-y\right\rvert}ds_{y},

(2.6)

\displaystyle(\mathscr{D}z)(x,t)

\displaystyle:=\frac{1}{4\pi}\int_{\Gamma}\left[\partial_{n_{y}}\frac{z(y,t-\left\lvert x-y\right\rvert)}{\left\lvert x-y\right\rvert}-\frac{\partial_{n_{y}}\left\lvert x-y\right\rvert}{\left\lvert x-y\right\rvert}\partial_{t}z(y,t-\left\lvert x-y\right\rvert)\right]ds_{y}.

(2.7)

The fact that the time argument is the retarded time
\tau=t-\left\lvert x-y\right\rvert

motivates that
\mathscr{S}
and
\mathscr{D}
are usually called retarded
potentials.

\displaystyle[X,Y]_{1/2}

\displaystyle=[L^{2}(0,T;H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n-1}_{x^{\prime}}))\cap H^{1}_{0,}(0,T;L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n-1}_{x^{\prime}})),Y]_{1/2}

\displaystyle=[L^{2}(0,T;H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n-1}_{x^{\prime}})),Y]_{1/2}\cap[H^{1}_{0,}(0,T;L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n-1}_{x^{\prime}})),Y]_{1/2}.

Consequently, by interpolation we get

\displaystyle[X,Y]_{1/2}=L^{2}(0,T;H^{1/2}(\mathbb{R}^{n-1}_{x^{\prime}}))\cap H^{1/2}_{0,}(0,T;L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n-1}_{x^{\prime}})),

which corresponds to
H^{1/2}_{0,}(\mathbb{R}^{n-1}_{x^{\prime}}\times[0,T])
.
Hence, we conclude that
\gamma_{\Sigma}^{i}u\in H^{1/2}_{0,}(\Sigma)
.
Surjectivity also follows from Theorem 4.2 in
[
20
, Chapt. 1]
.
∎

.

The interior trace map
\gamma_{\Sigma}^{i}
is continuous and surjective from

to

.

Finally, we define the lateral trace space

{\mathcal{H}}_{0,}(\Sigma):=\Big\{v=\gamma_{\Sigma}^{i}V\quad\mbox{for all}\;V\in{\mathcal{H}}_{;0,}(Q)\Big\}

with the norm

Remark 3.3
.

By the definition of
\mathcal{H}_{0,}(\Sigma)
and using the

,
we have that for any

there exists a sequence

.

Remark 3.4
.

The trace spaces investigated in this paper are closely related to the
spaces used in the classical time dependent BEM approach for the wave

[

]
.
Indeed, as pointed out in
[

, Remark 2]
,

agrees with

\displaystyle H^{1/2,1/2}_{\sigma,\Gamma}:=\Big\{u\in LT(\sigma,H^{1/2}(\Gamma))\,;\,\int_{\mathbb{R}+i\sigma}\left\lvert\hat{u}\right\rvert_{1/2,\omega,\Gamma}d\omega<\infty\Big\}

when

. Additionally,

for the detailed

4
Initial boundary value problems

4.1
Homogeneous Dirichlet data

Instead of (
2.1
), let us first consider the Dirichlet initial
boundary value problem with zero boundary conditions,

\begin{array}[]{rclcl}\Box u(x,t)&=&f(x,t)&&\text{for}\;(x,t)\in Q,\\
u(x,t)&=&0&&\text{for}\;(x,t)\in\Sigma,\\
u(x,0)=\partial_{t}u(x,t)_{|t=0}&=&0&&\text{for}\;x\in\Omega.\end{array}

(4.1)

A possible variational formulation of (
4.1
) is to find

u\in H^{1,1}_{0;0,}(Q)
such that

-\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}\partial_{t}u\,\partial_{t}v\,dx\,dt+\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}\nabla_{x}u\cdot\nabla_{x}v\,dx\,dt=\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}f\,v\,dx\,dt

(4.2)

is satisfied for all
v\in H^{1,1}_{0;,0}(Q)
. When assuming
f\in L^{2}(Q)

we are able to construct a unique solution
u\in H^{1,1}_{0;0,}(Q)
of the
variational formulation (
4.1
), satisfying the stability estimate

[
30
, Theorem 5.1]
, see also
[
19
, Chapt. IV, Theorem 3.1]
,

\|u\|_{H^{1,1}_{0;0,}(Q)}\leq\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\,T\,\|f\|_{L^{2}(Q)}.

While the variational formulation (
4.2
) is well posed also
for
f\in[H^{1,1}_{0;,0}(Q)]^{\prime}
, it is not possible to prove a related
inf-sup condition to ensure the existence of a unique solution

u\in H^{1,1}_{0;0,}(Q)
, see
[
34
, Theorem 4.2.24]
.
However, by definition we have the inf-sup condition

\|\Box\widetilde{u}\|_{[H^{1}_{0}(Q_{-})]^{\prime}}=\sup\limits_{0\neq v\in H^{1,1}_{0;,0}(Q)}\frac{|\langle\Box\widetilde{u},{\mathcal{E}}v\rangle_{Q_{-}}|}{\|v\|_{H^{1,1}_{;,0}(Q)}}\quad\mbox{for all}\;u\in{\mathcal{H}}_{0;0,}(Q),

(4.3)

and therefore we conclude unique solvability of the variational formulation
to find
u\in{\mathcal{H}}_{0;0,}(Q)
such that

\langle\Box\widetilde{u},{\mathcal{E}}v\rangle_{Q_{-}}=\langle f,v\rangle_{Q}

(4.4)

is satisfied for all
v\in H^{1,1}_{0;,0}(Q)
, see
[
31
, Theorem 3.9]
.
Moreover, for the solution
u
it holds

\|\Box\widetilde{u}\|_{[H^{1}_{0}(Q_{-})]^{\prime}}=\sup\limits_{0\neq v\in H^{1,1}_{0;,0}(Q)}\frac{|\langle\Box\widetilde{u},{\mathcal{E}}v\rangle_{Q_{-}}|}{\|v\|_{H^{1,1}_{0;,0}(Q)}}=\sup\limits_{0\neq v\in H^{1,1}_{0;,0}(Q)}\frac{|\langle f,v\rangle_{Q}|}{\|v\|_{H^{1,1}_{0;,0}(Q)}}\leq\|f\|_{[H^{1,1}_{0;,0}(Q)]^{\prime}}.

In fact, (
4.4
) is the variational formulation of the operator
equation
{\mathcal{E}}^{\prime}\Box\widetilde{u}=f
in

[H^{1,1}_{0;,0}(Q)]^{\prime}
, i.e.,

f_{u}(v):=\langle{\mathcal{E}}^{\prime}\Box\widetilde{u},v\rangle_{Q}=\langle\Box\widetilde{u},{\mathcal{E}}v\rangle_{Q_{-}}\quad\mbox{for}\;v\in H^{1,1}_{0;,0}(Q)\subset H^{1,1}_{;,0}(Q)

is a continuous linear functional with norm

\|f_{u}\|_{[H^{1,1}_{;,0}(Q)]^{\prime}}=\sup\limits_{0\neq v\in H^{1,1}_{0;,0}(Q)}\frac{|f_{u}(v)|}{\|v\|_{H^{1,1}_{;,0}(Q)}}=\sup\limits_{0\neq v\in H^{1,1}_{0;,0}(Q)}\frac{|\langle\Box\widetilde{u},{\mathcal{E}}v\rangle_{Q_{-}}}{\|v\|_{H^{1,1}_{;,0}(Q)}}=\|\Box\widetilde{u}\|_{[H^{1}_{0}(Q_{-})]^{\prime}}.

Recall that for
u\in H^{1,1}_{0;0,}(Q)\subset{\mathcal{H}}_{0;0,}(Q)

we have

f_{u}(v)=\langle\Box\widetilde{u},{\mathcal{E}}v\rangle_{Q_{-}}=-\langle\partial_{t}u,\partial_{t}v\rangle_{L^{2}(Q)}+\langle\nabla_{x}u,\nabla_{x}v\rangle_{L^{2}(Q)}\quad\mbox{for all}\;v\in H^{1,1}_{0;,0}(Q).

Using the Hahn–Banach theorem, e.g.,
[
33
, Chapt. IV., Sect. 5]
,

[
6
, Theorem 5.9-1]
,
there exists a linear continuous functional

\widetilde{f}_{u}:H^{1,1,}_{;,0}(Q)\to{\mathbb{R}}
satisfying

\displaystyle\widetilde{f}_{u}(v)=f_{u}(v)\quad\mbox{for all}\;v\in H^{1,1}_{0;,0}(Q),

(4.5)

\displaystyle\|\widetilde{f}_{u}\|_{[H^{1,1}_{;,0}(Q)]^{\prime}}=\|f_{u}\|_{[H^{1,1}_{;,0}(Q)]^{\prime}}=\|\Box\widetilde{u}\|_{[H^{1}_{0}(Q_{-})]^{\prime}}.

Indeed, for
u\in H^{1,1}_{0;0,}(Q)
, we have the explicit representation

\widetilde{f}_{u}(v):=\langle\Box\widetilde{u},{\mathcal{E}}v\rangle_{Q_{-}}=-\langle\partial_{t}u,\partial_{t}v\rangle_{L^{2}(Q)}+\langle\nabla_{x}u,\nabla_{x}v\rangle_{L^{2}(Q)}\quad\forall\;v\in H^{1,1}_{;,0}(Q).

(4.6)

In the following we assume
f\in[H^{1,1}_{;,0}(Q)]^{\prime}
, and we consider
the variational formulation to find

\lambda_{i}\in[H^{1/2}_{,0}(\Sigma)]^{\prime}
such that

\langle(\gamma_{\Sigma}^{i})^{\prime}\lambda_{i},v\rangle_{Q}=\langle\lambda_{i},\gamma_{\Sigma}^{i}v\rangle_{\Sigma}=\widetilde{f}_{u}(v)-\langle f,v\rangle_{Q}\quad\mbox{for all}\;v\in H^{1,1}_{;,0}(Q).

(4.7)

For
v\in H^{1,1}_{0;,0}(Q)\subset H^{1,1}_{;,0}(Q)
, it holds

\widetilde{f}_{u}(v)-\langle f,v\rangle_{Q}=f_{u}(v)-\langle f,v\rangle_{Q}=\langle\Box\widetilde{u},{\mathcal{E}}v\rangle_{Q_{-}}-\langle f,v\rangle_{Q}=0,

i.e.,

\widetilde{f}_{u}-f\in(\mbox{ker}\,\gamma_{\Sigma}^{i})^{0}=(H^{1,1}_{0;,0}(Q))^{0}:=\Big\{g\in[H^{1,1}_{;,0}(Q)]^{\prime}:\langle g,v\rangle_{Q}=0\quad\forall\;v\in H^{1,1}_{0;,0}(Q)\Big\}\,.

By the closed range theorem, we obtain

\widetilde{f}_{u}-f\in\mbox{Im}_{[H^{1/2}_{,0}(\Sigma)]^{\prime}}(\gamma_{\Sigma}^{i})^{\prime},

which ensures existence of a solution

\lambda_{i}\in[H^{1/2}_{,0}(\Sigma)]^{\prime}

of the variational formulation (
4.7
).
Since the norm in
[H^{1/2}_{,0}(\Sigma)]^{\prime}
is defined by duality,
this immediately implies the inf-sup condition

\|\lambda\|_{[H^{1/2}_{,0}(\Sigma)]^{\prime}}=\sup\limits_{0\neq v\in H^{1,1}_{;,0}(Q)}\frac{|\langle\lambda,\gamma_{\Sigma}^{i}v\rangle_{\Sigma}|}{\|v\|_{H^{1,1}_{;,0}(Q)}}\quad\mbox{for all}\;\lambda\in[H^{1/2}_{,0}(\Sigma)]^{\prime},

and therefore uniqueness of
\lambda_{i}\in[H^{1/2}_{,0}(\Sigma)]^{\prime}
.
Moreover, this also gives

\displaystyle\|\lambda_{i}\|_{[H^{1/2}_{,0}(\Sigma)]^{\prime}}

\displaystyle=\sup\limits_{0\neq v\in H^{1,1}_{;,0}(Q)}\frac{|\langle\lambda_{i},\gamma_{\Sigma}v\rangle_{\Sigma}|}{\|v\|_{H^{1,1}_{;,0}(Q)}}

\displaystyle=\sup\limits_{0\neq v\in H^{1,1}_{;,0}(Q)}\frac{|\widetilde{f}_{u}(v)-\langle f,v\rangle_{Q}|}{\|v\|_{H^{1,1}_{;,0}(Q)}}\leq 2\,\|f\|_{[H^{1,1}_{;,0}(Q)]^{\prime}},

where we used

\displaystyle\sup\limits_{0\neq v\in H^{1,1}_{;,0}(Q)}\frac{|\widetilde{f}_{u}(v)|}{\|v\|_{H^{1,1}_{;,0}(Q)}}

\displaystyle=\|\widetilde{f}_{u}\|_{[H^{1,1}_{;,0}(Q)]^{\prime}}

\displaystyle=\|f_{u}\|_{[H^{1,1}_{;,0}(Q)]^{\prime}}=\|\Box\widetilde{u}\|_{[H^{1}_{0}(Q_{-})]^{\prime}}\leq\|f\|_{[H^{1,1}_{;,0}(Q)]^{\prime}}.

We now rewrite the variational formulation (
4.7
) as

\widetilde{f}_{u}(v)=\langle f,v\rangle_{Q}+\langle\lambda_{i},\gamma_{\Sigma}^{i}v\rangle_{\Sigma},\quad v\in H^{1,1}_{;,0}(Q).

In particular, for
u\in H^{1,1}_{0;0,}(Q)
, and using
(
4.6
), this gives

-\langle\partial_{t}u,\partial_{t}v\rangle_{L^{2}(Q)}+\langle\nabla_{x}u,\nabla_{x}v\rangle_{L^{2}(Q)}=\langle f,v\rangle_{Q}+\langle\lambda_{i},\gamma_{\Sigma}^{i}v\rangle_{\Sigma},\quad v\in H^{1,1}_{;,0}(Q).

i.e.,

\Phi(u,v)=\langle f,v\rangle_{Q}+\langle\lambda_{i},\gamma_{\Sigma}^{i}v\rangle_{\Sigma}.

When comparing this with Green’s first formula (
3.2
)
for suitable chosen functions, we observe that
\lambda_{i}
corresponds
to the spatial normal derivative of
u
.
Hence, also in the general case we shall write

\gamma_{N}^{i}u:=\partial_{n_{x}}u=\lambda_{i}

and call this distribution the interior spatial normal derivative of

u\in{\mathcal{H}}_{0;0,}(Q)
, i.e.,

\gamma_{N}^{i}:{\mathcal{H}}_{0;0,}(Q)\to[H^{1/2}_{,0}(\Sigma)]^{\prime}.

In a similar way, we also define

\gamma_{N}^{i}:{\mathcal{H}}_{0;,0}(Q)\to[H^{1/2}_{0,}(\Sigma)]^{\prime}.

For a related approach in the case of an elliptic
equation, see also
[
22
, pp. 116–117]
.

4.2
Inhomogeneous Dirichlet data

Next we consider the Dirichlet boundary value problem (
2.1
).
For
g\in{\mathcal{H}}_{0,}(\Sigma)
there exists, by definition, an
extension
u_{g}={\mathcal{E}}_{\Sigma}g\in{\mathcal{H}}_{;0,}(Q)
, and
the zero extension
\widetilde{u}_{g}\in L^{2}(Q_{-})
.
Thus, it remains to find

u_{0}:=u-u_{g}\in{\mathcal{H}}_{0;0,}(Q)
satisfying

\langle\Box\widetilde{u}_{0},{\mathcal{E}}v\rangle_{Q_{-}}=\langle f,v\rangle_{Q}-\langle\Box\widetilde{u}_{g},{\mathcal{E}}v\rangle_{Q_{-}}\quad\mbox{for all}\;v\in H^{1,1}_{0;,0}(Q).

Note that
u_{g}\in{\mathcal{H}}_{;0,}(Q)\subset{\mathcal{H}}(Q)

involves
\Box\widetilde{u}_{g}\in[H^{1}_{0}(Q_{-})]^{\prime}
.
For the solution
u_{0}
we obtain

\displaystyle\|\Box\widetilde{u}_{0}\|_{[H^{1}_{0}(Q_{-})]^{\prime}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sup\limits_{0\neq v\in H^{1,1}_{0;,0}(Q)}\frac{|\langle\Box\widetilde{u}_{0},{\mathcal{E}}v\rangle_{Q_{-}}|}{\|v\|_{H^{1,1}_{;,0}(Q)}}\,=\,\sup\limits_{0\neq v\in H^{1,1}_{0;,0}(Q)}\frac{|\langle f,v\rangle_{Q}-\langle\Box\widetilde{u}_{g},{\mathcal{E}}v\rangle_{Q_{-}}|}{\|v\|_{H^{1,1}_{;,0}(Q)}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\|f\|_{[H^{1,1}_{0;,0}(Q)]^{\prime}}+\|{\mathcal{E}}\|_{H^{1,1}_{0;,0}(Q),H^{1}_{0}(Q_{-})}\|g\|_{{\mathcal{H}}_{0,}(\Sigma)},

where we have used

\|\Box\widetilde{u}_{g}\|_{[H^{1}_{0}(Q_{-})]^{\prime}}\leq\sqrt{\|u_{g}\|_{L^{2}(Q)}^{2}+\|\Box\widetilde{u}_{g}\|_{[H^{1}_{0}(Q_{-})]^{\prime}}^{2}}=\|u_{g}\|_{{\mathcal{H}}(Q)}=\|g\|_{{\mathcal{H}}_{0,}(\Sigma)}.

As before, we can determine
\lambda_{i}\in[H^{1/2}_{,0}(\Sigma)]^{\prime}
as unique
solution of the variational formulation (
4.7
),
and where
\gamma_{N}^{i}u:=\lambda_{i}

is again the spatial normal derivative of the solution
u
of the
Dirichlet boundary value problem (
2.1
), satisfying

\|\lambda_{i}\|_{[H^{1/2}_{,0}(\Sigma)]^{\prime}}\leq 2\,\|f\|_{[H^{1,1}_{0;,0}(Q)]^{\prime}}+\|{\mathcal{E}}\|_{H^{1,1}_{0;,0}(Q),H^{1}_{0}(Q_{-})}\|g\|_{{\mathcal{H}}_{0,}(\Sigma)}\,.

(4.8)

Specially, for
f\equiv 0
, this describes the interior
Dirichlet to Neumann map
g\mapsto\lambda_{i}=\gamma_{N}^{i}u
, where
u
is the
solution of the homogeneous wave equation with zero initial data.
This can be written as
\lambda_{i}=\operatorname{\mathsf{S}}_{i}g
, where

\operatorname{\mathsf{S}}_{i}:{\mathcal{H}}_{0,}(\Sigma)\to[H^{1/2}_{,0}(\Sigma)]^{\prime}
is the
so-called Steklov-Poincaré operator, and from (
4.8
)
we immediately conclude

\|\operatorname{\mathsf{S}}_{i}g\|_{[H^{1/2}_{,0}(\Sigma)]^{\prime}}\leq c_{2}^{S_{i}}\,\|g\|_{{\mathcal{H}}_{0,}(\Sigma)}\quad\mbox{for all}\;g\in{\mathcal{H}}_{0,}(\Sigma),

(4.9)

with
c_{2}^{S_{i}}:=\|{\mathcal{E}}\|_{H^{1,1}_{0;,0}(Q),H^{1}_{0}(Q_{-})}
.

As before, we can write the variational formulation (
4.7
) as

\widetilde{f}_{u}(v)=\langle f,v\rangle_{Q}+\langle\lambda_{i},\gamma_{\Sigma}^{i}v\rangle_{\Sigma},\quad v\in H^{1,1}_{;,0}(Q).

Now, for
u\in{\mathcal{H}}_{;0,}(Q)
there exists a sequence

\{u_{n}\}_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}\subset H^{1,1}_{;,0}(Q)
with

\lim\limits_{n\to\infty}\|u-u_{n}\|_{{\mathcal{H}}(Q)}=0.

Hence we can write

\widetilde{f}_{u}(v)=\lim\limits_{n\to\infty}\widetilde{f}_{u_{n}}(v)=\lim\limits_{n\to\infty}\Big[-\langle\partial_{t}u_{n},\partial_{t}v\rangle_{L^{2}(Q)}+\langle\nabla_{x}u_{n},\nabla_{x}v\rangle_{L^{2}(Q)}\Big]\,.

In particular, for
v\in H^{1,1}_{;,0}(Q)\cap H^{2}(Q)
, we can apply integration
by parts to obtain

\displaystyle\widetilde{f}_{u}(v)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lim\limits_{n\to\infty}\Big[\langle u_{n},\Box v\rangle_{L^{2}(Q)}+\langle\gamma_{\Sigma}^{i}u_{n},\gamma_{N}^{i}v\rangle_{\Sigma}-\langle u_{n}(T),\partial_{t}v(T)\rangle_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\Big]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\langle u,\Box v\rangle_{L^{2}(Q)}+\langle\gamma_{\Sigma}^{i}u,\gamma_{N}^{i}v\rangle_{\Sigma}-\langle u(T),\partial_{t}v(T)\rangle_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\,.

With this we finally obtain Green’s second formula for the solution

u\in{\mathcal{H}}_{;0,}(Q)
of (
2.1
) and

v\in H^{1,1}_{;,0}(Q)\cap H^{2}(Q)
,

\langle u,\Box v\rangle_{L^{2}(Q)}+\langle\gamma_{\Sigma}^{i}u,\gamma_{N}^{i}v\rangle_{\Sigma}-\langle u(T),\partial_{t}v(T)\rangle_{L^{2}(\Omega)}=\langle f,v\rangle_{Q}+\langle\gamma_{N}^{i}u,\gamma_{\Sigma}^{i}v\rangle_{\Sigma}.

(4.10)

4.3
The Neumann boundary value problem

We now consider (as in (
4.7
)) the variational problem to find

u\in{\mathcal{H}}_{;0,}(Q)
such that

\widetilde{f}_{u}(v)=\langle f,v\rangle_{Q}+\langle\lambda,\gamma_{\Sigma}^{i}v\rangle_{\Sigma}\quad\mbox{for all}\;v\in H^{1,1}_{;,0}(Q),

(4.11)

when
\lambda\in[H^{1/2}_{,0}(\Sigma)]^{\prime}
is given.
This is the generalized variational formulation of the
Neumann boundary value problem

\Box u=f\quad\mbox{in}\;Q,\quad\gamma_{N}^{i}u=\lambda\quad\mbox{on}\;\Sigma,\quad u=\partial_{t}u=0\quad\mbox{on}\;\Sigma_{0}.

(4.12)

Lemma 4.1
.

For all
u\in{\mathcal{H}}_{;0,}(Q)
there holds the inf-sup
stability condition

\frac{\sqrt{2}}{\sqrt{2+T^{2}}}\,\|u\|_{{\mathcal{H}}(Q)}\leq\sup\limits_{0\neq v\in H^{1,1}_{;,0}(Q)}\frac{|\widetilde{f}_{u}(v)|}{\|v\|_{H^{1,1}_{;,0}(Q)}}\,.

(4.13)

Proof.

Using (
4.5
) and the norm definition by duality, we first have

\|\Box\widetilde{u}\|_{[H^{1}_{0}(Q_{-})]^{\prime}}=\|\widetilde{f}_{u}\|_{[H^{1,1}_{;,0}(Q)]^{\prime}}=\sup\limits_{0\neq v\in H^{1,1}_{;,0}(Q)}\frac{|\widetilde{f}_{u}(v)|}{\|v\|_{H^{1,1}_{;,0}(Q)}}\,.

Now, for
0\neq u\in{\mathcal{H}}_{;0,}(Q)
, there exists a
non-trivial sequence
(u_{n})_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}\subset H^{1,1}_{;0,}(Q)
,

u_{n}\not\equiv 0
, with

\lim\limits_{n\to\infty}\|u-u_{n}\|_{{\mathcal{H}}(Q)}=0.

For each
u_{n}\in H^{1,1}_{;0,}(Q)
we can write, as in
(
4.6
),

\widetilde{f}_{u_{n}}(v)=-\langle\partial_{t}u_{n},\partial_{t}v\rangle_{L^{2}(Q)}+\langle\nabla_{x}u_{n},\nabla_{x}v\rangle_{L^{2}(Q)}\quad\mbox{for all}\;v\in H^{1,1}_{;,0}(Q),

and we define
w_{n}\in H^{1,1}_{;,0}(Q)
as the unique solution of the
variational formulation

-\langle\partial_{t}v,\partial_{t}w_{n}\rangle_{L^{2}(Q)}+\langle\nabla_{x}v,\nabla_{x}w_{n}\rangle_{L^{2}(Q)}=\langle u_{n},v\rangle_{L^{2}(Q)}\quad\mbox{for all}\;v\in H^{1,1}_{;0,}(Q).

This variational formulation corresponds to a Neumann boundary value
problem for the wave equation with a volume source
u_{n}\in L^{2}(Q)
,
and zero conditions at the terminal time
t=T
.
As for the related Dirichlet problem we conclude the bound

\|w_{n}\|_{H^{1,1}_{;,0}(Q)}\leq\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\,T\,\|u_{n}\|_{L^{2}(Q)}.

In particular, for the test function
v=u_{n}
, the variational formulation
gives

-\langle\partial_{t}u_{n},\partial_{t}w_{n}\rangle_{L^{2}(Q)}+\langle\nabla_{x}u_{n},\nabla_{x}w_{n}\rangle_{L^{2}(Q)}=\|u_{n}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(Q)}.

With this, we now conclude

\displaystyle\|\widetilde{f}_{u_{n}}\|_{[H^{1,1}_{;,0}(Q)]^{\prime}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sup\limits_{0\neq v\in H^{1,1}_{;,0}(Q)}\frac{|\widetilde{f}_{u_{n}}(v)|}{\|v\|_{H^{1,1}_{;,0}(Q)}}\geq\frac{|\widetilde{f}_{u_{n}}(w_{n})|}{\|w_{n}\|_{H^{1,1}_{;,0}(Q)}}

\displaystyle\hskip-71.13188pt=\frac{|-\langle\partial_{t}u_{n},\partial_{t}w_{n}\rangle_{L^{2}(Q)}+\langle\nabla_{x}u_{n},\nabla_{x}w_{n}\rangle_{L^{2}(Q)}|}{\|w_{n}\|_{H^{1,1}_{;,0}(Q)}}=\frac{\|u_{n}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(Q)}}{\|w_{n}\|_{H^{1,1}_{;,0}(Q)}}\geq\frac{\sqrt{2}}{T}\,\|u_{n}\|_{L^{2}(Q)}\,.

Completion for
n\to\infty
now gives

\|\widetilde{f}_{u}\|_{[H^{1,1}_{;,0}(Q)]^{\prime}}\geq\frac{\sqrt{2}}{T}\,\|u\|_{L^{2}(Q)}\,.

Hence, we can write, for some
\alpha\in(0,1)
,

\displaystyle\|\widetilde{f}_{u}\|^{2}_{[H^{1,1}_{;,0}(Q)]^{\prime}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\alpha\,\|\widetilde{f}_{u}\|^{2}_{[H^{1,1}_{;,0}(Q)]^{\prime}}+(1-\alpha)\,\|\widetilde{f}_{u}\|^{2}_{[H^{1,1}_{;,0}(Q)]^{\prime}}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\alpha\,\frac{2}{T^{2}}\,\|u\|_{L^{2}(Q)}^{2}+(1-\alpha)\,\|\Box\widetilde{u}\|^{2}_{[H^{1}_{0}(Q_{-})]^{\prime}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(1-\overline{\alpha})\Big(\|u\|_{L^{2}(Q)}^{2}+\|\Box\widetilde{u}\|^{2}_{[H^{1}_{0}(Q_{-})]^{\prime}}\Big)=(1-\overline{\alpha})\,\|u\|^{2}_{{\mathcal{H}}(Q)},

when

1-\overline{\alpha}=\overline{\alpha}\,\frac{2}{T^{2}}

is satisfied, i.e.,

\overline{\alpha}=\frac{T^{2}}{2+T^{2}},\quad 1-\overline{\alpha}=\frac{2}{2+T^{2}}.

This concludes the proof.
∎

Lemma 4.2
.

For all
0\neq v\in H^{1,1}_{;,0}(Q)
, there exists a function

u_{v}\in{\mathcal{H}}_{;0,}(Q)
such that

\widetilde{f}_{u_{v}}(v)>0.

(4.14)

Proof.

For
0\neq v\in H^{1,1}_{;,0}(Q)
, there exists a unique solution

u_{v}\in H^{1,1}_{;0,}(Q)\subset{\mathcal{H}}_{;0,}(Q)
, satisfying

-\langle\partial_{t}u_{v},\partial_{t}w\rangle_{L^{2}(Q)}+\langle\nabla_{x}u_{v},\nabla_{x}w\rangle_{L^{2}(Q)}=\langle v,w\rangle_{L^{2}(Q)}\quad\mbox{for all}\;w\in H^{1,1}_{;,0}(Q),

and, for
w=v
, we obtain

\widetilde{f}_{u_{v}}(v)=-\langle\partial_{t}u_{v},\partial_{t}v\rangle_{L^{2}(Q)}+\langle\nabla_{x}u_{v},\nabla_{x}v\rangle_{L^{2}(Q)}=\|v\|^{2}_{L^{2}(Q)}>0.

∎

The inf-sup condition (
4.13
) and the
surjectivity condition (
4.14
) ensure unique
solvability of the variational formulation (
4.11
), i.e., for
the unique solution
u\in{\mathcal{H}}_{;0,}(Q)
we obtain

\displaystyle\frac{\sqrt{2}}{\sqrt{2+T^{2}}}\,\|u\|_{{\mathcal{H}}(Q)}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sup\limits_{0\neq v\in H^{1,1}_{;,0}(Q)}\frac{|\widetilde{f}_{u}(v)|}{\|v\|_{H^{1,1}_{;,0}(Q)}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sup\limits_{0\neq v\in H^{1,1}_{;,0}(Q)}\frac{|\langle f,v\rangle_{Q}+\langle\lambda,\gamma_{\Sigma}^{i}v\rangle_{\Sigma}|}{\|v\|_{H^{1,1}_{;,0}(Q)}}\,\leq\,\|f\|_{[H^{1,1}_{;,0}(Q)]^{\prime}}+\|\lambda\|_{[H^{1/2}_{,0}(\Sigma)]^{\prime}},

and when taking the lateral trace this gives

\|\gamma_{\Sigma}^{i}u\|_{{\mathcal{H}}_{0,}(\Sigma)}\leq\|u\|_{{\mathcal{H}}(Q)}\leq\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\sqrt{2+T^{2}}\,\Big[\|f\|_{[H^{1,1}_{;,0}(Q)]^{\prime}}+\|\lambda\|_{[H^{1/2}_{,0}(\Sigma)]^{\prime}}\Big].

(4.15)

In particular, for
f\equiv 0
, this defines the interior Neumann to Dirichlet
map
\lambda\mapsto\gamma_{\Sigma}^{i}u
which can be written as

\gamma_{\Sigma}^{i}u=\operatorname{\mathsf{S}}_{i}^{-1}\lambda
when using the inverse of the
Steklov–Poincaré operator
\operatorname{\mathsf{S}}_{i}
. From (
4.15
)
we then conclude

\|\operatorname{\mathsf{S}}_{i}^{-1}\lambda\|_{{\mathcal{H}}_{0,}(\Sigma)}\leq c_{2}^{S_{i}^{-1}}\,\|\lambda\|_{[H^{1/2}_{,0}(\Sigma)]^{\prime}}\quad\mbox{for all}\;\lambda\in[H^{1/2}_{,0}(\Sigma)]^{\prime},\quad c_{2}^{S_{i}^{-1}}:=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\sqrt{2+T^{2}}\,.

(4.16)

Now, using (
4.9
)
and duality this gives

\|\lambda\|_{[H^{1/2}_{,0}(\Sigma)]^{\prime}}=\|\operatorname{\mathsf{S}}_{i}\gamma_{\Sigma}^{i}u\|_{[H^{1/2}_{,0}(\Sigma)]^{\prime}}\leq c_{2}^{S_{i}}\,\|\gamma_{\Sigma}^{i}u\|_{{\mathcal{H}}_{0,}(\Sigma)}=c_{2}^{S_{i}}\sup\limits_{0\neq\mu\in[{\mathcal{H}}_{0,}(\Sigma)]^{\prime}}\frac{|\langle\gamma_{\Sigma}^{i}u,\mu\rangle_{\Sigma}|}{\|\mu\|_{[{\mathcal{H}}_{0,}(\Sigma)]^{\prime}}},

i.e., the inf-sup stability condition

\frac{1}{c_{2}^{S_{i}}}\,\|\lambda\|_{[H^{1/2}_{,0}(\Sigma)]^{\prime}}\leq\sup\limits_{0\neq\mu\in[{\mathcal{H}}_{0,}(\Sigma)]^{\prime}}\frac{|\langle\operatorname{\mathsf{S}}_{i}^{-1}\lambda,\mu\rangle_{\Sigma}|}{\|\mu\|_{[{\mathcal{H}}_{0,}(\Sigma)]^{\prime}}}\quad\mbox{for all}\;\lambda\in[H^{1/2}_{,0}(\Sigma)]^{\prime}.

(4.17)

Furthermore, using (
4.16
) for
g_{i}:=\gamma_{\Sigma}^{i}u

and duality we obtain

\displaystyle\|g_{i}\|_{{\mathcal{H}}_{0,}(\Sigma)}

\displaystyle=\|\gamma_{\Sigma}^{i}u\|_{{\mathcal{H}}_{0,}(\Sigma)}=\|\operatorname{\mathsf{S}}_{i}^{-1}\lambda\|_{{\mathcal{H}}_{0,}(\Sigma)}

\displaystyle\leq c_{2}^{S_{i}^{-1}}\,\|\lambda\|_{[H^{1/2}_{,0}(\Sigma)]^{\prime}}=c_{2}^{S_{i}^{-1}}\,\sup\limits_{0\neq v\in H^{1/2}_{,0}(\Sigma)}\frac{|\langle\lambda,v\rangle_{\Sigma}|}{\|v\|_{H^{1/2}_{,0}(\Sigma)}},

i.e., the inf-sup condition

\frac{1}{c_{2}^{S_{i}^{-1}}}\,\|g_{i}\|_{{\mathcal{H}}_{0,}(\Sigma)}\leq\sup\limits_{0\neq v\in H^{1/2}_{,0}(\Sigma)}\frac{|\langle\operatorname{\mathsf{S}}_{i}g_{i},v\rangle_{\Sigma}|}{\|v\|_{H^{1/2}_{,0}(\Sigma)}}\quad\mbox{for all}\;g_{i}\in{\mathcal{H}}_{0,}(\Sigma).

(4.18)

4.4
Adjoint problems

Related to the variational problem (
4.11
) we now
consider the adjoint problem to find

w\in H^{1,1}_{;,0}(Q)
such that

\widetilde{f}_{u}(w)=\langle f,u\rangle_{Q}+\langle g,\gamma_{\Sigma}^{i}u\rangle_{\Sigma}

(4.19)

is satisfied for all
u\in{\mathcal{H}}_{;0,}(Q)
.
For
w\in H^{1,1}_{;,0}(Q)
, let
u_{w}\in{\mathcal{H}}_{;0,}(Q)

be the unique solution of the variational problem

\widetilde{f}_{u_{w}}(v)=\langle\partial_{t}w,\partial_{t}v\rangle_{L^{2}(Q)}+\langle\nabla_{x}w,\nabla_{x}v\rangle_{L^{2}(Q)}\quad\mbox{for all}\;v\in H^{1,1}_{;,0}(Q).

For
v=w
, this gives

\widetilde{f}_{u_{w}}(w)=\|w\|^{2}_{H^{1,1}_{;,0}(Q)}\,.

Moreover, using the inf-sup stability condition
(
4.13
), we obtain

\displaystyle\frac{\sqrt{2}}{\sqrt{2+T^{2}}}\,\|u_{w}\|_{{\mathcal{H}}(Q)}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sup\limits_{0\neq v\in H^{1,1}_{;,0}(Q)}\frac{|\widetilde{f}_{u_{w}}(v)|}{\|v\|_{H^{1,1}_{;,0}(Q)}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sup\limits_{0\neq v\in H^{1,1}_{;,0}(Q)}\frac{|\langle\partial_{t}w,\partial_{t}v\rangle_{L^{2}(Q)}+\langle\nabla_{x}w,\nabla_{x}v\rangle_{L^{2}(Q)}|}{\|v\|_{H^{1,1}_{;,0}(Q)}}\,\leq\,\|w\|_{H^{1,1}_{;,0}(Q)},

and thus it follows that

\widetilde{f}_{u_{w}}(w)=\|w\|^{2}_{H^{1,1}_{;,0}(Q)}\geq\frac{\sqrt{2}}{\sqrt{2+T^{2}}}\,\|u_{w}\|_{{\mathcal{H}}(Q)}\|w\|_{H^{1,1}_{;,0}(Q)}.

In other words, we have

\frac{\sqrt{2}}{\sqrt{2+T^{2}}}\,\|w\|_{H^{1,1}_{;,0}(Q)}\leq\sup\limits_{0\neq u\in{\mathcal{H}}_{;0,}(Q)}\frac{|\widetilde{f}_{u}(w)|}{\|u\|_{{\mathcal{H}}(Q)}}\quad\mbox{for all}\;w\in H^{1,1}_{;,0}(Q).

Since the inf-sup condition (
4.13
) also
implies surjectivity, unique solvability of the variational formulation
(
4.19
) follows. In fact, for
f\in[{\mathcal{H}}_{;0,}(Q)]^{\prime}

and
g\in[{\mathcal{H}}_{0,}(\Sigma)]^{\prime}
we have
w\in H^{1,1}_{;,0}(Q)
as the
weak solution of the adjoint Neumann problem for the wave equation

\Box w=f\quad\mbox{in}\;Q,\quad\gamma_{N}^{i}w=g\quad\mbox{on}\;\Sigma,\quad w=\partial_{t}w=0\quad\mbox{on}\;\Sigma_{T}.

(4.20)

5
Boundary Integral Equations

5.1
Representation formula

Let
u\in{\mathcal{H}}_{;0,}(Q)
be a solution of the generalized
wave equation
{\mathcal{E}}^{\prime}\Box u=f
in
[H^{1,1}_{;,0}(Q)]^{\prime}
.
For
(x,t)\in Q
and

v(y,\tau)=\kappa_{t}G(x-y,\tau)
, with
G(\cdot,\cdot)
being the fundamental
solution introduced in (
2.3
) and
\kappa_{t}
the
time-reversal map from (
2.2
), formula (
4.10
)
becomes the representation formula

\displaystyle u(x,t)=\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\Omega}f(y,\tau)\,G(x-y,t-\tau)\,dy\,d\tau+\left\langle\gamma_{N}^{i}u\,,\,\gamma_{\Sigma}^{i}v\right\rangle_{\Sigma}-\left\langle\gamma_{\Sigma}^{i}u\,,\,\gamma_{N}^{i}v\right\rangle_{\Sigma}.

In particular, for
f\equiv 0
, we conclude the following
representation formula

\displaystyle u(x,t)=(\mathscr{S}\gamma_{N}^{i}u)(x,t)-(\mathscr{D}\gamma_{\Sigma}^{i}u)(x,t),\quad(x,t)\in Q,

(5.1)

with the single and double layer potentials
\mathscr{S}
and

\mathscr{D}
, defined as in (
2.4
) and (
2.5
), respectively.

5.2
Single layer potential

We first recall the definition (
2.4
) of the single layer potential

u_{w}(x,t)=({\mathscr{S}}w)(x,t)=\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\Gamma}G(x-y,t-\tau)\,w(y,\tau)\,ds_{y}\,d\tau,\quad(x,t)\in Q.

Proposition 5.1
.

For the single layer potential we have

{\mathscr{S}}:[H^{1/2}_{,0}(\Sigma)]^{\prime}\to{\mathcal{H}}_{;0,}(Q).

Proof.

For
u_{w}={\mathscr{S}}w
and a suitable
\psi
, we can write the duality
pairing as extension of the inner product in
L^{2}(Q)
as

\displaystyle\langle u_{w},\psi\rangle_{Q}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}u_{w}(x,t)\,\psi(x,t)\,dx\,dt

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\Gamma}G(x-y,t-\tau)\,w(y,\tau)\,ds_{y}\,d\tau\,\psi(x,t)\,dx\,dt

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Gamma}w(y,\tau)\int_{\tau}^{T}G(x-y,t-\tau)\,\psi(x,t)\,dx\,dt\,ds_{y}\,d\tau

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Gamma}w(y,\tau)\,\varphi_{\psi}(y,\tau)\,ds_{y}\,d\tau

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\langle w,\gamma_{\Sigma}^{i}\varphi_{\psi}\rangle_{\Sigma},

where

\varphi_{\psi}(y,\tau)=\int_{\tau}^{T}G(x-y,t-\tau)\,\psi(x,t)\,dx\,dt,\quad(y,\tau)\in Q,

is a solution of the adjoint problem
(
4.20
). Hence, for

\psi\in[{\mathcal{H}}_{;0,}(Q)]^{\prime}
, we obtain

\varphi_{\psi}\in H^{1,1}_{;,0}(Q)
, and therefore

\gamma_{\Sigma}^{i}\varphi_{\psi}\in H^{1/2}_{,0}(\Sigma)
.
From this, we conclude that

u_{w}\in{\mathcal{H}}_{;0,}(Q)
when

w\in[H^{1/2}_{,0}(\Sigma)]^{\prime}
is given.
∎

As a corollary of the previous result, we can define the single layer
boundary integral operator

\operatorname{\mathsf{V}}:=\gamma_{\Sigma}^{i}{\mathscr{S}}:[H^{1/2}_{,0}(\Sigma)]^{\prime}\to{\mathcal{H}}_{0,}(\Sigma),

(5.2)

and the normal derivative of the single layer potential,

\gamma_{N}^{i}{\mathscr{S}}:[H^{1/2}_{,0}(\Sigma)]^{\prime}\to[H^{1/2}_{,0}(\Sigma)]^{\prime}.

(5.3)

5.3
Double layer potential

We first recall the definition
(
2.5
) of the double layer potential

u_{z}(x,t)=({\mathscr{D}}z)(x,t)=\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\Gamma}\partial_{n_{y}}G(x-y,t-\tau)\,z(y,\tau)\,ds_{y}\,d\tau,\quad(x,t)\in Q.

Proposition 5.2
.

For the double layer potential we have

{\mathscr{D}}:{\mathcal{H}}_{0,}(\Sigma)\to{\mathcal{H}}_{;0,}(Q).

Proof.

For
u_{z}={\mathscr{D}}z
and a suitable
\psi
we can write the duality
pairing as extension of the inner product in
L^{2}(Q)
as

\displaystyle\langle u_{z},\psi\rangle_{Q}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}u_{z}(x,t)\,\psi(x,t)\,dx\,dt

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\Gamma}\partial_{n_{y}}G(x-y,t-\tau)\,z(y,\tau)\,ds_{y}\,d\tau\,\psi(x,t)\,dx\,dt

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Gamma}z(y,\tau)\;\partial_{n_{y}}\int_{\tau}^{T}G(x-y,t-\tau)\,\psi(x,t)\,dx\,dt\,ds_{y}\,d\tau

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Gamma}z(y,\tau)\,\partial_{n_{y}}\varphi_{\psi}(y,\tau)\,ds_{y}\,d\tau

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\langle z,\gamma_{N}^{i}\varphi_{\psi}\rangle_{\Sigma},

where

\varphi_{\psi}(y,\tau)=\int_{\tau}^{T}\int_{\Gamma}G(x-y,t-\tau)\,\psi(x,t)\,dx\,dt,\quad(y,\tau)\in Q,

is a solution of the adjoint problem
(
4.20
). Hence, for

\psi\in[{\mathcal{H}}_{;0,}(Q)]^{\prime}
we obtain

\varphi_{\psi}\in H^{1,1}_{;,0}(Q)
, and

\gamma_{N}^{i}\varphi_{\psi}\in[{\mathcal{H}}_{0,}(\Sigma)]^{\prime}
.
From this we conclude

u_{z}\in{\mathcal{H}}_{;0,}(Q)
when

z\in{\mathcal{H}}_{0,}(\Sigma)
is given.
∎

With the previous result we are in a position to consider the lateral trace
of the double layer potential

\gamma_{\Sigma}^{i}{\mathscr{D}}:{\mathcal{H}}_{0,}(\Sigma)\to{\mathcal{H}}_{0,}(\Sigma),

(5.4)

and the so-called hypersingular boundary integral operator as normal derivative
of the double layer potential,

\operatorname{\mathsf{W}}:=-\gamma_{N}^{i}{\mathscr{D}}:{\mathcal{H}}_{0,}(\Sigma)\to[H^{1/2}_{,0}(\Sigma)]^{\prime}.

(5.5)

5.4
Boundary integral operators and Calderón identities

Without loss of generality, let us consider the complementary domains

\Omega^{c}:=B_{R}\setminus\overline{\Omega}\quad\text{ and }\quad Q^{c}:=\Omega^{c}\times(0,T),

with
B_{R}:=\{\mathbf{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\,:\,\left\lvert\mathbf{x}\right\rvert<R\}
is a sufficiently
large ball containing
\Gamma
.
With this, we define the exterior traces
\gamma_{\Sigma}^{e}
and
\gamma_{N}^{e}

following the same ideas from Subsection
3.1
, but using
Q^{c}

instead of
Q
.

Remark 5.3
.

Clearly, the mappings

\displaystyle\gamma_{\Sigma}^{e}\,

\displaystyle:H^{1,1}_{;0,}(Q^{c})\to H^{1/2}_{0,}(\Sigma),\qquad\qquad\gamma_{\Sigma}^{e}\,:H^{1,1}_{;,0}(Q^{c})\to H^{1/2}_{,0}(\Sigma),

\displaystyle\gamma_{\Sigma}^{e}\,

\displaystyle:\mathcal{H}_{;0,}(Q^{c})\to\mathcal{H}_{0,}(\Sigma),\qquad\>\,\qquad\gamma_{\Sigma}^{e}\,:\mathcal{H}_{;,0}(Q^{c})\to\mathcal{H}_{,0}(\Sigma),

are continuous and surjective, while

\displaystyle\gamma_{N}^{e}\,

\displaystyle:H^{1,1}_{;0,}(Q^{c})\to[H^{1/2}_{,0}(\Sigma)]^{\prime},\qquad\qquad\gamma_{N}^{e}\,:H^{1,1}_{;,0}(Q^{c})\to[H^{1/2}_{0,}(\Sigma)]^{\prime},

\displaystyle\gamma_{N}^{e}\,

\displaystyle:\mathcal{H}_{;0,}(Q^{c})\to[H^{1/2}_{,0}(\Sigma)]^{\prime},\qquad\>\qquad\gamma_{N}^{e}\,:\mathcal{H}_{;,0}(Q^{c})\to[H^{1/2}_{0,}(\Sigma)]^{\prime},

are continuous. Moreover, Green’s formulae and other properties of
the interior trace operators
\gamma_{\Sigma}^{i}
and
\gamma_{N}^{i}

also apply to these exterior traces in their corresponding spaces.
Indeed, following Propositions
5.1
and

5.2
, we have the continuity of the mappings

\mathscr{S}:[H^{1/2}_{,0}(\Sigma)]^{\prime}\to\mathcal{H}_{;0,}(Q^{c}),\qquad\mathscr{D}:\mathcal{H}_{0,}(\Sigma)\quad\to\mathcal{H}_{;0,}(Q^{c}).

We define the jumps across
\Sigma
by

\left[\gamma_{\Sigma}u\right]:=\gamma_{\Sigma}^{e}u-\gamma_{\Sigma}^{i}u,\qquad\left[\gamma_{N}u\right]:=\gamma_{N}^{e}u-\gamma_{N}^{i}u,

which clearly do not depend on the choice of
B_{R}
.
Now we can state the following result:

Proposition 5.4
.

The following jump relations hold for all

w\in[H^{1/2}_{,0}(\Sigma)]^{\prime}
and
z\in\mathcal{H}_{0,}(\Sigma)
,

[\gamma_{\Sigma}\mathscr{S}w]=0,\qquad[\gamma_{N}\mathscr{S}w]=-w,\qquad[\gamma_{\Sigma}\mathscr{D}z]=z,\qquad[\gamma_{N}\mathscr{D}z]=0.

Proof.

The jump relations are known to hold when
w
and
z
are smooth, e.g.,

[
10
, Sect. 2.2.1]
, and
[
25
, Sect. 1.3]
. We extend them to

(w,z)\in[H^{1/2}_{,0}(\Sigma)]^{\prime}\times\mathcal{H}_{0,}(\Sigma)
by using
that the combined trace map

(\gamma_{\Sigma},\gamma_{N}):u\mapsto(\gamma_{\Sigma}u,\gamma_{N}u)
maps

\left.C^{\infty}_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}_{+})\right|_{\overline{Q}}
onto a dense
subspace of
[H^{1/2}_{,0}(\Sigma)]^{\prime}\times H^{1/2}_{0,}(\Sigma)

(cf.
[
7
, Lemma 3.5]
), and that
H^{1/2}_{0,}(\Sigma)
is
dense in
\mathcal{H}_{0,}(\Sigma)
.
∎

We can now define the boundary integral operators as follows:

Definition 5.5
.

\displaystyle\operatorname{\mathsf{V}}w

\displaystyle:=\gamma_{\Sigma}^{i}\mathscr{S}w=\gamma_{\Sigma}^{e}\mathscr{S}w,

\displaystyle\operatorname{\mathsf{K}}z

\displaystyle:=\frac{1}{2}\left(\gamma_{\Sigma}^{i}\mathscr{D}z+\gamma_{\Sigma}^{e}\mathscr{D}z\right),

\displaystyle\operatorname{\mathsf{K}}^{\prime}w

\displaystyle:=\frac{1}{2}\left(\gamma_{N}^{i}\mathscr{S}w+\gamma_{N}^{e}\mathscr{S}w\right),

\displaystyle\operatorname{\mathsf{W}}z

\displaystyle:=-\gamma_{N}^{i}\mathscr{D}z=-\gamma_{N}^{e}\mathscr{D}z\,.

From this definition and (
5.2
), (
5.3
),
(
5.4
), (
5.5
), we obtain:

Theorem 5.6
.

The boundary integral operators introduced in Definition
5.5
are
continuous in the following spaces:

\displaystyle\operatorname{\mathsf{V}}

\displaystyle:[H^{1/2}_{,0}(\Sigma)]^{\prime}\to\>\,\mathcal{H}_{0,}(\Sigma),

\displaystyle\operatorname{\mathsf{K}}

\displaystyle:\>\,\mathcal{H}_{0,}(\Sigma)\quad\to\>\,\mathcal{H}_{0,}(\Sigma),

\displaystyle\operatorname{\mathsf{K}}^{\prime}

\displaystyle:[H^{1/2}_{,0}(\Sigma)]^{\prime}\to[H^{1/2}_{,0}(\Sigma)]^{\prime},

\displaystyle\operatorname{\mathsf{W}}

\displaystyle:\>\,\mathcal{H}_{0,}(\Sigma)\quad\to[H^{1/2}_{,0}(\Sigma)]^{\prime}.

Next, we take traces on the representation formula (
5.1
) and
get

\displaystyle\gamma_{\Sigma}^{i}u

\displaystyle=(\frac{1}{2}\operatorname{\mathsf{I}}-\operatorname{\mathsf{K}})\gamma_{\Sigma}^{i}u+\operatorname{\mathsf{V}}\gamma_{N}^{i}u,

\displaystyle\gamma_{N}^{i}u

\displaystyle=\operatorname{\mathsf{W}}\gamma_{\Sigma}^{i}u+(\frac{1}{2}\operatorname{\mathsf{I}}+\operatorname{\mathsf{K}}^{\prime})\gamma_{N}^{i}u.

As usual, we can rewrite this as

\displaystyle\left(\begin{array}[]{c}\gamma_{\Sigma}^{i}u\\
\gamma_{N}^{i}u\end{array}\right)=\underbrace{\left(\begin{array}[]{c c }(\frac{1}{2}\operatorname{\mathsf{I}}-\operatorname{\mathsf{K}})&\operatorname{\mathsf{V}}\\
\operatorname{\mathsf{W}}&(\frac{1}{2}\operatorname{\mathsf{I}}+\operatorname{\mathsf{K}}^{\prime})\end{array}\right)}_{=:\operatorname{\mathsf{C}}_{Q}^{i}}\left(\begin{array}[]{c}\gamma_{\Sigma}^{i}u\\
\gamma_{N}^{i}u\end{array}\right)

with the interior Calderon projection
\operatorname{\mathsf{C}}_{Q}^{i}
.

Using standard arguments (see for example
[
25
, Sect. 1.4]
), we
can now prove

\displaystyle\left(\begin{array}[]{c}z\\
w\end{array}\right)=\operatorname{\mathsf{C}}_{Q}^{i}\left(\begin{array}[]{c}z\\
w\end{array}\right),\quad\forall w\in[H^{1/2}_{,0}(\Sigma)]^{\prime},\,z\in\mathcal{H}_{0,}(\Sigma).

Furthermore, this gives
(\operatorname{\mathsf{C}}_{Q}^{i})^{2}=\operatorname{\mathsf{C}}_{Q}^{i}
, from which we get

\operatorname{\mathsf{V}}\operatorname{\mathsf{W}}=(\frac{1}{2}\operatorname{\mathsf{I}}-\operatorname{\mathsf{K}})(\frac{1}{2}\operatorname{\mathsf{I}}+\operatorname{\mathsf{K}}),\quad\operatorname{\mathsf{W}}\operatorname{\mathsf{V}}=(\frac{1}{2}\operatorname{\mathsf{I}}-\operatorname{\mathsf{K}}^{\prime})(\frac{1}{2}\operatorname{\mathsf{I}}+\operatorname{\mathsf{K}}^{\prime}),\quad\operatorname{\mathsf{V}}\operatorname{\mathsf{K}}^{\prime}=\operatorname{\mathsf{K}}\operatorname{\mathsf{V}},\quad\operatorname{\mathsf{K}}^{\prime}\operatorname{\mathsf{W}}=\operatorname{\mathsf{W}}\operatorname{\mathsf{K}}.

5.5
Coercivity of boundary integral operators

In this subsection, we are going to prove coercivity properties of boundary
integral
operators, i.e., of the single layer boundary integral operator
\operatorname{\mathsf{V}}
and
the hypersingular boundary integral operator
\operatorname{\mathsf{W}}
, which ensure unique
solvability of related boundary integral equations.

Theorem 5.7
.

The single layer boundary integral operator

\operatorname{\mathsf{V}}:[H^{1/2}_{,0}(\Sigma)]^{\prime}\to{\mathcal{H}}_{0,}(\Sigma)

satisfies the inf-sup stability condition

c_{1}^{V}\,\|w\|_{[H^{1/2}_{,0}(\Sigma)]^{\prime}}\leq\sup\limits_{0\neq\mu\in[{\mathcal{H}}_{0,}(\Sigma)]^{\prime}}\frac{|\langle\operatorname{\mathsf{V}}w,\mu\rangle_{\Sigma}|}{\|\mu\|_{[{\mathcal{H}}_{0,}(\Sigma)]^{\prime}}}\quad\mbox{for all}\;w\in[H^{1/2}_{,0}(\Sigma)]^{\prime}.

(5.12)

Proof.

For
w\in[H^{1/2}_{,0}(\Sigma)]^{\prime}
we consider the single layer potential

u={\mathscr{S}}w
which defines a solution
u\in{\mathcal{H}}_{0,}(Q)

of the homogeneous wave equation. When taking the lateral trace of
u
this
gives
g=\gamma_{\Sigma}^{i}u=\operatorname{\mathsf{V}}w\in{\mathcal{H}}_{0,}(\Sigma)
.
In fact,
u
is the unique solution of the Dirichlet boundary value
problem

\Box u=0\quad\mbox{in}\;Q,\quad\gamma_{\Sigma}^{i}u=g\quad\mbox{on}\;\Sigma,\quad u=\partial_{t}u=0\quad\mbox{on}\;\Sigma_{0}.

When using the interior Steklov–Poincaré operator
\operatorname{\mathsf{S}}_{i}
we can
determine the related interior Neumann trace

\lambda_{i}=\gamma_{N}^{i}u=\operatorname{\mathsf{S}}_{i}g\in[H^{1/2}_{,0}(\Sigma)]^{\prime}.

Since the Steklov–Poincaré operator
\operatorname{\mathsf{S}}_{i}
is invertible, this gives

g=\operatorname{\mathsf{S}}_{i}^{-1}\lambda_{i}
, i.e.,
g=\gamma_{\Sigma}^{i}u
is the lateral trace
of the solution of the Neumann boundary value problem

\Box u=0\quad\mbox{in}\;Q,\quad\gamma_{N}^{i}u=\lambda_{i}\quad\mbox{on}\;\Sigma,\quad u=\partial_{t}u=0\quad\mbox{on}\;\Sigma_{0}.

From the inf-sup stability condition (
4.17
)
of the inverse interior Steklov-Poincaré operator
\operatorname{\mathsf{S}}_{i}^{-1}

we now conclude

\frac{1}{c_{2}^{S_{i}}}\,\|\lambda_{i}\|_{[H^{1/2}_{,0}(\Sigma)]^{\prime}}\leq\sup\limits_{0\neq\mu\in[{\mathcal{H}}_{0,}(\Sigma)]^{\prime}}\frac{|\langle\operatorname{\mathsf{S}}_{i}^{-1}\lambda_{i},\mu\rangle_{\Sigma}|}{\|\mu\|_{[{\mathcal{H}}_{0,}(\Sigma)]^{\prime}}}=\sup\limits_{0\neq\mu\in[{\mathcal{H}}_{0,}(\Sigma)]^{\prime}}\frac{|\langle\operatorname{\mathsf{V}}w,\mu\rangle_{\Sigma}|}{\|\mu\|_{[{\mathcal{H}}_{0,}(\Sigma)]^{\prime}}}

For the exterior problem we can derive a related estimate, i.e.,

\frac{1}{c_{2}^{S_{e}}}\,\|\lambda_{e}\|_{[H^{1/2}_{,0}(\Sigma)]^{\prime}}\leq\sup\limits_{0\neq\mu\in[{\mathcal{H}}_{0,}(\Sigma)]^{\prime}}\frac{|\langle\operatorname{\mathsf{V}}w,\mu\rangle_{\Sigma}|}{\|\mu\|_{[{\mathcal{H}}_{0,}(\Sigma)]^{\prime}}}\,,

where
\lambda_{e}
is the exterior Neumann trace of the single layer
potential
u={\mathscr{S}}w
. Now, and using the jump relation of the
adjoint double layer potential, this gives

\displaystyle\|w\|_{[H^{1/2}_{,0}(\Sigma)]^{\prime}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\|\lambda_{i}-\lambda_{e}\|_{[H^{1/2}_{,0}(\Sigma)]^{\prime}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\|\lambda_{i}\|_{[H^{1/2}_{,0}(\Sigma)]^{\prime}}+\|\lambda_{e}\|_{[H^{1/2}_{,0}(\Sigma)]^{\prime}}\leq(c_{2}^{S_{i}}+c_{2}^{S_{e}})\sup\limits_{0\neq\mu\in[{\mathcal{H}}_{0,}(\Sigma)]^{\prime}}\frac{|\langle\operatorname{\mathsf{V}}w,\mu\rangle_{\Sigma}|}{\|\mu\|_{[{\mathcal{H}}_{0,}(\Sigma)]^{\prime}}}\,,

which implies the desired inf-sup condition.
∎

While the inf-sup stability condition (
5.12
) ensures uniqueness of
a solution of a related boundary integral equation, the following
result will provide solvability.

Lemma 5.8
.

For any
0\neq\mu\in[{\mathcal{H}}_{0,}(\Sigma)]^{\prime}
there exists a

w_{\mu}\in[H^{1/2}_{,0}(\Sigma)]^{\prime}
such that

\langle\operatorname{\mathsf{V}}w_{\mu},\mu\rangle_{\Sigma}>0

is satisfied.

Proof.

For given
0\neq\mu\in[{\mathcal{H}}_{0,}(\Sigma)]^{\prime}
we define the
adjoint single layer potential
u_{\mu}\in H^{1,1}_{;,0}(Q)
by

u_{\mu}(y,\tau)=\int_{\tau}^{T}\int_{\Gamma}G(x-y,t-\tau)\,\mu(x,t)\,ds_{x}\,dt\quad\mbox{for}\;(y,\tau)\in Q.

For the lateral trace
\gamma_{\Sigma}^{i}u_{\mu}\in H^{1/2}_{,0}(\Sigma)
and
arbitrary
w\in[H^{1/2}_{,0}(\Sigma)]^{\prime}
we then have

\displaystyle\langle w,\gamma_{\Sigma}^{i}u_{\mu}\rangle_{\Sigma}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Gamma}w(y,\tau)\int_{\tau}^{T}\int_{\Gamma}G(x-y,t-\tau)\,\mu(x,t)\,ds_{x}\,dt\,ds_{y}\,d\tau

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Gamma}\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\Gamma}G(x-y,t-\tau)\,w(y,\tau)\,ds_{y}\,d\tau\,\mu(x,t)\,ds_{x}\,dt\,=\,\langle\operatorname{\mathsf{V}}w,\mu\rangle_{\Sigma}\,.

Moreover, we compute

U_{\mu}(x,t):=\int_{0}^{t}u_{\mu}(x,s)\,ds\quad\mbox{for}\;(x,t)\in Q,

with the lateral trace

g_{\mu}:=\gamma_{\Sigma}^{i}U_{\mu}\in H^{1/2}_{0,}(\Sigma)\subset{\mathcal{H}}_{0,}(\Sigma)
. Hence, there exists a unique solution

v_{\mu}\in{\mathcal{H}}_{;0,}(Q)
of the Dirichlet problem for
the wave equation,

\Box v_{\mu}=0\quad\mbox{in}\;Q,\quad v_{\mu}=g_{\mu}\quad\mbox{on}\;\Sigma,\quad v_{\mu}=\partial_{t}v_{\mu}=0\quad\mbox{on}\;\Sigma_{0}.

We then conclude

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\Gamma}\frac{\partial}{\partial n_{x}}v_{\mu}(x,s)\,\partial_{s}v_{\mu}(x,s)\,ds_{x}\,ds

\displaystyle\hskip 28.45274pt=\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\Omega}\Big[\partial_{ss}v_{\mu}(x,s)\,\partial_{s}v_{\mu}(x,s)+\nabla_{x}v_{\mu}(x,s)\cdot\nabla_{x}\partial_{s}v_{\mu}(x,s)\Big]\,dx\,ds

\displaystyle\hskip 28.45274pt=\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{t}\frac{d}{ds}\int_{\Omega}\Big[[\partial_{s}v_{\mu}(x,s)]^{2}+[\nabla_{x}v_{\mu}(x,s)]^{2}\Big]\,dx\,ds

\displaystyle\hskip 28.45274pt=\frac{1}{2}\,\|\partial_{t}v_{\mu}(t)\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+\frac{1}{2}\,\|\nabla_{x}v_{\mu}(t)\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\geq 0\quad\mbox{for all}\;t\in(0,T].

In the case

\frac{1}{2}\,\|\partial_{t}v_{\mu}(t)\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+\frac{1}{2}\,\|\nabla_{x}v_{\mu}(t)\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega)}=0\quad\mbox{for all}\;t\in(0,T],

and together with the zero initial conditions, we would conclude

v_{\mu}\equiv 0
in
Q
, which then implies
g_{\mu}\equiv 0
on

\Sigma
, and thus

u_{\mu}\equiv 0
. But this contradicts
\mu\not\equiv 0
. Therefore
we have

\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Gamma}\frac{\partial}{\partial n_{x}}v_{\mu}(x,t)\,\partial_{t}v_{\mu}(x,t)\,ds_{x}\,dt=\frac{1}{2}\,\|\partial_{t}v_{\mu}(T)\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+\frac{1}{2}\,\|\nabla_{x}v_{\mu}(T)\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega)}>0,

and with

\partial_{t}U_{\mu}=u_{\mu}\quad\mbox{in}\;Q,\quad\partial_{t}v_{\mu}=\partial_{t}g_{\mu}\quad\mbox{on}\;\Sigma,\quad g_{\mu}=\gamma_{\Sigma}^{i}U_{\mu},\quad w_{\mu}:=\gamma_{N}^{i}v_{\mu}\in[H^{1/2}_{,0}(\Sigma)]^{\prime}

we finally conclude

\langle\operatorname{\mathsf{V}}w_{\mu},\mu\rangle_{\Sigma}=\langle w_{\mu},\gamma_{\Sigma}^{i}u_{\mu}\rangle_{\Sigma}=\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Gamma}\frac{\partial}{\partial n_{x}}v_{\mu}(x,t)\,\partial_{t}v_{\mu}(x,t)\,ds_{x}dt>0.

∎

The solution of the Dirichlet boundary value problem

\Box u=0\quad\mbox{in}\;Q,\quad u=g\quad\mbox{on}\;\Sigma,\quad u=\partial_{t}u=0\quad\mbox{on}\;\Sigma_{0}

is given by the representation formula

u(x,t)=({\mathscr{S}}\gamma_{N}^{i}u)(x,t)-({\mathscr{D}}g)(x,t)\quad\mbox{for}\;(x,t)\in Q,

where we can determine the yet unknown Neumann datum

w=\gamma_{N}^{i}u\in[H^{1/2}_{,0}(\Sigma)]^{\prime}
as the unique solution
of the first kind boundary integral equation

\operatorname{\mathsf{V}}w=(\frac{1}{2}\operatorname{\mathsf{I}}+\operatorname{\mathsf{K}})g\quad\mbox{on}\;\Sigma,

(5.13)

i.e., of the variational formulation

\langle\operatorname{\mathsf{V}}w,\mu\rangle_{\Sigma}=\langle(\frac{1}{2}\operatorname{\mathsf{I}}+\operatorname{\mathsf{K}})g,\mu\rangle_{\Sigma}\quad\mbox{for all}\;\mu\in[{\mathcal{H}}_{0,}(\Sigma)]^{\prime}.

(5.14)

Solvability of the variational formulation (
5.14
) follows from
Lemma
5.8
, while uniqueness of the solution is a consequence
of Theorem
5.7
. Instead of the variational formulation
(
5.14
), we may use the modified Hilbert transformation

{\mathcal{H}}_{T}
as defined in subsection
2.3

to end up with an equivalent variational problem to find

w\in[H^{1/2}_{,0}(\Sigma)]^{\prime}
such that

\langle\mathcal{H}_{T}\operatorname{\mathsf{V}}w,\mu\rangle_{\Sigma}=\langle\mathcal{H}_{T}(\frac{1}{2}\operatorname{\mathsf{I}}+\operatorname{\mathsf{K}})g,\mu\rangle_{\Sigma}\quad\mbox{for all}\;\mu\in[{\mathcal{H}}_{,0}(\Sigma)]^{\prime}.

(5.15)

Due to the inclusion
H^{1/2}_{,0}(\Sigma)\subset{\mathcal{H}}_{,0}(\Sigma)
,
we obviously have

[{\mathcal{H}}_{,0}(\Sigma)]^{\prime}\subset[H^{1/2}_{,0}(\Sigma)]^{\prime}
which will
allow for a Galerkin–Bubnov space-time boundary element discretization
of (
5.15
).

Remark 5.9
.

For a solution
u
of the homogeneous wave equation with
zero initial data but inhomogeneous Dirichlet boundary conditions and
a suitable test function
v
we can write Green’s first formula as

\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}\partial_{n_{x}}u\,v\,dx\,dt=\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}\Big[\partial_{tt}u\,v+\nabla_{x}u\cdot\nabla_{x}v\Big]\,dx\,dt\,.

In particular, for
v=\partial_{t}u
, this results in the energy representation

\displaystyle E(u):

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}\Big[\partial_{tt}u\,\partial_{t}u+\nabla_{x}u\cdot\nabla_{x}\partial_{t}u\Big]\,dx\,dt

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\,\|\partial_{t}u(T)\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+\frac{1}{2}\,\|\nabla_{x}u(T)\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega)}>0\,.

Note that this representation is the basis of the energetic BEM,
see, e.g.,
[
3
]
.
Instead, when using the particular test function

v={\mathcal{H}}_{T}u
and Proposition
2.1

this gives

\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Gamma}\frac{\partial}{\partial n_{x}}u\,{\mathcal{H}}_{T}u\,ds_{x}dt=\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}\Big[{\mathcal{H}}_{T}\partial_{t}u\,\partial_{t}u+\nabla_{x}u\cdot{\mathcal{H}}_{T}\nabla_{x}u\Big]\,dx\,dt\geq 0\,.

Specifically, for the single layer potential
u={\mathscr{S}}w
in

{\mathbb{R}}^{n+1}\backslash\Sigma
we then conclude

\langle w,{\mathcal{H}}_{T}\operatorname{\mathsf{V}}w\rangle_{\Sigma}=\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}\Big[{\mathcal{H}}_{T}\partial_{t}u\,\partial_{t}u+\nabla_{x}u\cdot{\mathcal{H}}_{T}\nabla_{x}u\Big]\,dx\,dt\geq 0\,.

In fact, when considering the spatially one-dimensional case
n=1
we can
prove the following ellipticity estimate

[
28
,
32
]

\langle w,{\mathcal{H}}_{T}\operatorname{\mathsf{V}}w\rangle_{\Sigma}\geq c_{1}^{V}\,\|w\|^{2}_{[H^{1/2}_{,0}(\Sigma)]^{\prime}}\quad\mbox{for all}\;w\in[H^{1/2}_{,0}(\Sigma)]^{\prime}.

Since the single layer boundary integral operator

\operatorname{\mathsf{V}}:[H^{1/2}_{,0}(\Sigma)]^{\prime}\to{\mathcal{H}}_{0,}(\Sigma)
is invertible,
we can write the solution of the boundary integral equation
(
5.13
) as

w=\gamma_{N}^{i}u=\operatorname{\mathsf{V}}^{-1}(\frac{1}{2}\operatorname{\mathsf{I}}+\operatorname{\mathsf{K}})g=\operatorname{\mathsf{S}}_{i}g,

representing the Dirichlet to Neumann map with the interior
Steklov–Poincaré operator

\operatorname{\mathsf{S}}_{i}=\operatorname{\mathsf{V}}^{-1}(\frac{1}{2}\operatorname{\mathsf{I}}+\operatorname{\mathsf{K}}):{\mathcal{H}}_{0,}(\Sigma)\to[H^{1/2}_{,0}(\Sigma)]^{\prime}\,.

Hence we find that

\operatorname{\mathsf{V}}\operatorname{\mathsf{S}}_{i}=\frac{1}{2}\operatorname{\mathsf{I}}+\operatorname{\mathsf{K}}:{\mathcal{H}}_{0,}(\Sigma)\to{\mathcal{H}}_{0,}(\Sigma)

is invertible. As we can formulate a related boundary integral
equation also for the exterior Dirichlet boundary value problem,

\operatorname{\mathsf{V}}\gamma_{N}^{e}u=(-\frac{1}{2}\operatorname{\mathsf{I}}+\operatorname{\mathsf{K}})g\quad\mbox{on}\;\Sigma,

this gives that the exterior Steklov–Poincaré operator

\operatorname{\mathsf{S}}_{e}=-\operatorname{\mathsf{V}}^{-1}(\frac{1}{2}\operatorname{\mathsf{I}}-\operatorname{\mathsf{K}}):{\mathcal{H}}_{0,}(\Sigma)\to[H^{1/2}_{,0}(\Sigma)]^{\prime}

is invertible, and so is

\frac{1}{2}\operatorname{\mathsf{I}}-\operatorname{\mathsf{K}}=-\operatorname{\mathsf{V}}\operatorname{\mathsf{S}}_{e}:{\mathcal{H}}_{0,}(\Sigma)\to{\mathcal{H}}_{0,}(\Sigma)\,.

Consequently

\operatorname{\mathsf{V}}\operatorname{\mathsf{W}}=(\frac{1}{2}\operatorname{\mathsf{I}}-\operatorname{\mathsf{K}})(\frac{1}{2}\operatorname{\mathsf{I}}+\operatorname{\mathsf{K}}):{\mathcal{H}}_{0,}(\Sigma)\to{\mathcal{H}}_{0,}(\Sigma),

and thus

\operatorname{\mathsf{W}}=\operatorname{\mathsf{V}}^{-1}(\frac{1}{2}\operatorname{\mathsf{I}}-\operatorname{\mathsf{K}})(\frac{1}{2}\operatorname{\mathsf{I}}+\operatorname{\mathsf{K}}):{\mathcal{H}}_{0,}(\Sigma)\to[H^{1/2}_{,0}(\Sigma)]^{\prime}\,.

This finally implies that the hypersingular boundary integral operator

\operatorname{\mathsf{W}}:{\mathcal{H}}_{0,}(\Sigma)\to[H^{1/2}_{,0}(\Sigma)]^{\prime}

satisfies the inf-sup stability condition

c_{1}^{\operatorname{\mathsf{W}}}\,\|v\|_{{\mathcal{H}}_{0,}(\Sigma)}\leq\sup\limits_{0\neq\eta\in H^{1/2}_{,0}(\Sigma)}\frac{|\langle\operatorname{\mathsf{W}}v,\eta\rangle_{\Sigma}|}{\|\eta\|_{H^{1/2}_{,0}(\Sigma)}}\quad\mbox{for all}\;v\in{\mathcal{H}}_{0,}(\Sigma).

(5.16)

The solution of the Neumann boundary value problem

\Box u=0\quad\mbox{in}\;Q,\quad\partial_{n_{x}}u=\lambda\quad\mbox{on}\;\Sigma,\quad u=\partial_{t}u=0\quad\mbox{on}\;\Sigma_{0}

is given by the representation formula

u(x,t)=(\mathscr{S}\lambda)(x,t)-(\mathscr{D}z)(x,t)\quad\mbox{for}\;(x,t)\in Q,

where we can determine the yet unknown Dirichlet datum

z=\gamma_{\Sigma}^{i}u\in{\mathcal{H}}_{0,}(\Sigma)
as the unique
solution of the first kind boundary integral equation

\operatorname{\mathsf{W}}z=(\frac{1}{2}\operatorname{\mathsf{I}}-\operatorname{\mathsf{K}})\lambda\quad\mbox{on}\;\Sigma.

Unique solvability follows as described above.

6
Conclusions

In this paper, we presented a new framework to describe the
mapping properties of boundary integral operators for the wave equation.
The results are similar as known for the boundary integral operators
for elliptic partial differential equations, i.e., providing ellipticity
and boundedness with respect to function spaces of the same Sobolev spaces.
This will be the starting point to derive quasi-optimal error estimates
for related boundary element methods which are not available so far, and
which will be reported in forthcoming work. Other topics of interest
include efficient implementations of the proposed scheme using
the modified Hilbert transformation, a posteriori error estimates and
adaptivity, an efficient solution of the resulting linear systems of algebraic
equations, and the coupling with space-time finite element methods.

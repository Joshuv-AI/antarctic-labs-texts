Eight classes of new Hopf algebras of dimension 128 128 without the Chevalley property
Naihong Hu, Rongchuan Xiong

Abstract
Classifying Hopf algebras of a given dimension is a hard and open question. Using the generalized lifting method, we determine all finite-dimensional Hopf algebras over an algebraically closed field of characteristic zero whose coradical generates a Hopf algebra H H of dimension 16 16 without the Chevalley property and the corresponding infinitesimal braidings are simple objects in 𝒴 H H ​ 𝒟 {}^{H}_{H}\mathcal{YD} . In particular, we figure out 8 8 classes of new Hopf algebras of dimension 128 128 without the Chevalley property. Keywords: Nichols algebra; Hopf algebra; generalized lifting method.

Introduction

Let
\mathds{k}
be an algebraically closed field of characteristic zero. The question of classification of all Hopf algebras over
\mathds{k}
of a given dimension up to isomorphism was posed by I. Kaplansky in 1975. He conjectured that every Hopf algebra over
\mathds{k}
of prime dimension must be isomorphic to a group algebra which was proved by Y. Zhu
[
Z94
]
in 1994. Since then, more and more mathematicians have been trying to classify finite-dimensional Hopf algebras of a given dimension and have made some progress. As the aforementioned, the classification of Hopf algebras of prime dimension
p
has been completed by Y. Zhu
[
Z94
]
and all of them are isomorphic to the cyclic group algebra of dimension
p
. Further results have completed the classification of Hopf algebras of dimension
p^{2}
for
p
a prime (see

[
Ma96
,
AS
,
Ng02
]
), of dimension
2p
for
p
an odd prime (see
[
Ma95
,
Ng05
]
), and of
dimension
2p^{2}
for
p
an odd prime (see
[
HN09
]
). For survey of the classification for dimensions up to
100
, we refer to
[
BG13
]
and the references therein. Even though the classification of finite-dimensional pointed Hopf algebras as a special class (with abelian groups as the coradicals) has made some astonishing breakthrough under the assumption that the order of the finite abelian groups are prime to
210

(
[
AS10
]
, etc.) in terms of the lifting method (only as one kind approach of the so-called principle realization) introduced by Andruskiewitsch-Schneider in
[
AS98
]
, there are few unequivocal results and solving methods in the classification of Hopf algebras of a given dimension. This constitutes actually a great challenge after Heckenberger et al’s studies on the level of Nichols algebras (of group type).

Comparison with other newly designed approaches, we would prefer to the lifting method since any new method inevitably encounters the common hard point question: how to derive the explicit relations involving generators. Let us briefly recall it. Let
A
be a finite dimensional Hopf algebra such that the coradical
A_{0}
is a Hopf subalgebra, which implies that the coradical filtration
\{A_{n}\}_{n=0}^{\infty}
is a Hopf algebra filtration where
A_{n}=A_{0}\bigwedge A_{n-1}
. Let
G=\text{gr}\,A
be the associated graded Hopf algebra, that is,

G=\oplus_{n=0}^{\infty}G(n)
with
G(0)=A_{0}
and
G(n)=A_{n}/A_{n-1}
. Denote by
\pi:G\rightarrow A_{0}
the Hopf
algebra projection of
G
onto
A_{0}=G(0)
, then
\pi
splits the inclusion
i:A_{0}\hookrightarrow G
and thus by a theorem of Radford
[
R85
]
,
G\cong R\sharp A_{0}
, where
R=G^{co\pi}=\{h\in H\mid(id\otimes\pi)\Delta_{G}(h)=h\otimes 1\}
is a braided Hopf algebra in
{}^{A_{0}}_{A_{0}}\mathcal{YD}
. Moreover,
R=\oplus_{n=0}^{\infty}R(n)=R\cap G(n)
with
R(0)=\mathds{k}
and
R(1)=\mathcal{P}(R)
, the space of primitive elements of

, which is a braided vector space called the infinitesimal braiding. In particular, the subalgebra generated as a braided Hopf algebra by

is so-called Nichols algebra over

, which plays a key role in the classification of pointed Hopf algebra under the following

(

[

]
)

Any finite-dimensional braided Hopf algebra

in
{}^{A_{0}}_{A_{0}}\mathcal{YD}

is generated by

Usually, if we fix a Hopf algebra

, then the main steps of the lifting method for classification of all finite-dimensional Hopf algebras

such that

are:

•

Determine all objects

in
{}^{H}_{H}\mathcal{YD}
such that Nichols algebras

)
over

•

Prove that any finite-dimensional Hopf algebra over

So far, the lifting method has produced many striking results of the classification of pointed or copointed Hopf algebras. For more details about the results, we refer to

]

If

is a Hopf algebra without the Chevalley property, then the coradical filtration

•

•

Especially, if

and standard filtration is just the coradical filtration. Under the assumption that

Any Hopf algebra with injective antipode is a deformation of the bosonization of a Hopf algebra generated by a

2.1
Yetter-Drinfeld module and Nichols algebra

Definition 2.1
.

Let
H
be a Hopf algebra with bijective antipode. A Yetter-Drinfeld module over
H
is a left
H
-module and a left
H
-comodule with comodule structure denoted by
\delta:V\mapsto H\otimes V,v\mapsto v_{(-1)}\otimes v_{(0)}
, such that

\displaystyle\delta(h\cdot v)=h_{(1)}v_{(-1)}S(h_{(3)})\otimes h_{(2)}v_{(0)},

for all
v\in V,h\in H
. Let
{}^{H}_{H}\mathcal{YD}
be the
category of Yetter-Drinfeld modules over
H
with
H
-linear and

H
-colinear maps as morphisms.

The category
{}^{H}_{H}\mathcal{YD}
is monoidal, braided. Indeed, if
V,W\in{}^{H}_{H}\mathcal{YD}
,
V\otimes W
is the
tensor product over
\mathbb{C}
with the diagonal action and coaction of
H
and braiding

\displaystyle c_{V,W}:V\otimes W\mapsto W\otimes V,v\otimes w\mapsto v_{(-1)}\cdot w\otimes v_{(0)},\forall\,v\in V,w\in W.

(1)

Moreover,
{}^{H}_{H}\mathcal{YD}
is rigid. That is, it has left dual and right dual, if we take
V^{\ast}
and
{}^{\ast}V
to the dual of

V
as vector space, then the left dual and the right dual are defined by

\displaystyle\langle h\cdot f,v\rangle=\langle f,S(h)v\rangle,\quad f_{(-1)}\langle f_{(0)},v\rangle=S^{-1}(v_{(-1)})\langle f,v_{(0)}\rangle,

\displaystyle\langle h\cdot f,v\rangle=\langle f,S^{-1}(h)v\rangle,\quad f_{(-1)}\langle f_{(0)},v\rangle=S(v_{(-1)})\langle f,v_{(0)}\rangle.

We consider Hopf algebra in
{}^{H}_{H}\mathcal{YD}
. If
R
is a Hopf algebra in
{}^{H}_{H}\mathcal{YD}
,
the space of primitive elements
P(R)=\{x\in R|\delta(x)=x\otimes 1+1\otimes x\}
is a Yetter-Drinfeld
submodule of
R
. Moreover, for any finite-dimensional graded Hopf algebra in
{}^{H}_{H}\mathcal{YD}
, it satisfies the Poincaré duality:

Proposition 2.2
.

[
AG99
, Proposition 3.2.2]

Let
R=\oplus_{n=0}^{N}R(i)
be a finite-dimensional graded Hopf algebra in
{}^{H}_{H}\mathcal{YD}
, and suppose that

. Then
\dim R(i)=\dim R(N-i)
for any
0\leq i<N
.

Definition 2.3
.

Let

and

be the largest
\mathds{N}
-graded ideal and coideal
such that

. We call

. Then
\mathcal{B}(V)=\oplus_{n\geq 0}\mathcal{(}B)^{n}(V)

-graded Hopf algebra in

.

.

[
AS02
]

The Nichols algebra of an object

is the
(up to isomorphism) unique

-graded Hopf algebra

2.2
Radford biproduct construction

Let
R
be a bialgebra (resp. Hopf algebra) in
{}^{H}_{H}\mathcal{YD}
and denote the coproduct
by
\Delta_{R}(r)=r^{(1)}\otimes r^{(2)}
. We define the Radford biproduct

. As a vector space,

and the multiplication and comultiplication are given by the smash product and smash-coproduct, respectively:

\displaystyle(r\#g)(s\#h)

\displaystyle=r(g_{(1)}\cdot s)\#g_{(2)}h,

(2)

\displaystyle\Delta(r\#g)

\displaystyle=r^{(1)}\#(r^{(2)})_{(-1)}g_{(1)}\otimes(r^{(2)})_{(0)}\#g_{(2)}.

(3)

Clearly, the map
\iota:H\rightarrow R\#H,h\mapsto 1\#h,\ \forall h\in H

. Moreover,

and

describes a

\displaystyle h\cdot r

\displaystyle=h_{(1)}rS_{A}(h_{(2)}),\quad\delta(r)=(\pi\otimes id)\Delta_{A}(r),

Remark 3.2

\displaystyle\Delta(g)=g\otimes g,\quad\Delta(h)=h\otimes h,\quad\Delta(x)=x\otimes 1+gh\otimes x.

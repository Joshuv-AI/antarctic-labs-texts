A Tamed Euler Scheme for SDEs with Non-Locally Integrable Drift Coefficient
Tim Johnston, Sotirios Sabanis

Abstract
In this article we show that for SDEs with a drift coefficient that is non-locally integrable, one may define a tamed Euler scheme that converges in L p L^{p} at rate 1 / 2 1/2 to the true solution. The taming is required in this case since one cannot expect the regular Euler scheme to have finite moments in L p L^{p} . Our novel proof strategy involves controlling the inverse moments of the distance of scheme and the true solution to the singularity set. We additionally show that our setting applies to the case of two scalar valued particles with singular interaction kernel. To the best of the author’s knowledge, this is the first work we are aware of to prove strong convergence of an Euler-type scheme in the case of non-locally integrable drift.



1
Introduction

In this article we consider SDEs taking values in an open set
D\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
with additive noise, and the performance of the associated Euler scheme. That is, for drift coefficient
b:D\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
we consider

dX_{t}=b(X_{t})dt+dW_{t},\;\;\;t\geq 0,\;\;\;X_{0}=x_{0}\in D,

(1)

and the Euler scheme approximation

dX^{n}_{t}=b(X_{\kappa_{n}(t)})dt+dW_{t},\;\;\;t\geq 0,\;\;\;X^{n}_{0}=x_{0}\in D,

(2)

where
\kappa_{n}(t):=\lfloor nt\rfloor/n
is the projection backwards onto the grid
\{0,1/n,2/n,...\}
. The performance of the Euler scheme has been extensively studied under a wide variety of assumptions on the coefficients. It is well known that the Euler scheme converges in
L^{p}
at rate
1/2
given Lipschitz drift and diffusion coefficient, see
[
21
]
, and in an almost-sure sense under significantly weaker assumptions, see
[
11
]
. However the Euler scheme fails to converge at all in
L^{p}
(and in fact diverges) when the coefficients are superlinear, see
[
17
]
, and as a result in order to simulate such an SDE explicitly the notion of ‘tamed’ schemes was first introduced in
[
18
]
and developed independently in
[
32
]
. For these schemes, one replaces the drift coefficient
b
in the Euler scheme with functions
b_{n}
that depend on the stepsize. For more information on the topic of tamed schemes see
[
31
,
15
,
22
,
16
]
and references therein. Furthermore, much research in recent years has focused on the perfomance of the Euler scheme for discontinuous coefficients. In this case, the Euler scheme generally converges well. Indeed, for bounded and measurable drift coefficient and uniformly elliptic diffusion coefficient the Euler scheme converges in
L^{p}
at rate
1/2-
, see
[
5
]
. Similar results have been shown in
[
27
,
25
,
26
,
4
]
. For a survey of this fast moving area of research one could consult
[
28
]
.

In this article we consider the performance of the Euler scheme for another class of non-Lipschitz drift coefficients, namely coefficients that are locally unbounded, in particular non-locally integrable. In
[
29
,
34
,
6
]
strong convergence of the Euler scheme in
L^{p}
at rate
1/2
is proven for drifts with logarithmic, and therefore integrable, poles. These results therefore match the convergence results achieved in this article for drifts with more severe poles. The weak convergence of a similar scheme to the one considered in this article was considered in the case where the drift coefficient obeyed a certain intergrability property in
[
19
]
, however the necessity of such a property ruled out applicability to many SDEs of interest. Additionally, in
[
20
]
, a similar scheme was considered for the underdamped Langevin dynamics for Lennard-Jones interactions, that is, for a second order SDE whose drift coefficient is the gradient of a very singular scalar potential, and weak convergence was proven. Furthermore, in
[
13
]
a tamed Euler scheme similar to those discussed previously is shown to converge in probability under a setting similar to that considered in this article, however no rate is presented. In this article we present another tamed scheme, and show that one can obtain strong convergence at rate
1/2
even when the drift coefficient
b
is not-locally integrable, given certain conditions that ensure the true solution is ‘pushed’ away from points where the drift is singular. Key to our proofs is a certain stopping time argument which means we do not have to consider what happens when either the scheme or the true solution get too close to ‘bad’ points. A stopped scheme is used under different conditions to approximate the exit time of a stochastic process in
[
9
]
, and the related idea of ‘discarding’ bad trajectories is shown to ensure weak convergence up to some
\epsilon>0
in a variety of non-Lipschitz settings in
[
24
]
. However, this is the first paper we are aware of to study strong convergence of an Euler-type scheme for non-locally integrable coefficients. Strong convergence of numerical methods is important for obtaining bounds for sampling algorithms based on discretisations of SDEs in Wasserstein distance, see
[
3
,
1
]
and many others, as well as for multi-level Monte Carlo methods, see
[
8
]
.

For the study of SDEs with singular drift, it is important to show that the original continuous time dynamics are well-posed, that is, the SDE has a unique strong solution. If this is not the case then one cannot expect to construct a solution to the dynamics from the driving noise alone, and as a result one cannot expect any numerical method to converge strongly. In
[
13
]
strong solutions are constructed given more general conditions than those presented in this article, using directly an Euler-type scheme as pioneered in the classic articles
[
12
,
11
]
. The literature on existence and uniqueness of strong solutions for SDEs with singular drift is vast, see for instance
[
33
,
14
,
30
]
, as well as the book-length study
[
2
]
. In this article we choose to prove existence and uniqueness directly, since our argument relates fundamentally to the key strategy of the paper, which is to control the inverse moments of the distance of the scheme and the true solution to the singular points of the drift. We observe that the results of
[
13
]
could be applied to this end by choosing an appropriate Lyapunov function
V
, for instance
V=\rho^{-2}
, for
\rho
as given in the assumptions below.

The primary motivation for the study of such singular drift coefficients comes from particle dynamics, where one considers particle systems with strong repulsive forces at small distance, for instance the famous Lennard-Jones dynamics, wherein particles at distance of
r
away from one another repulse each other with force
\sim r^{-12}-r^{-6}
. In Corollary
1
we show that the scheme presented is this article converges strongly to the true solution of this dynamics in the ‘overdamped case’ where friction does not play a role, for two scalar valued particles. In future we would like to refine this restriction to more general classes of interacting particle systems with singular interaction.

One cannot expect the Euler scheme to converge for coefficients that are not locally integrable. Indeed, if
b
is not in
L^{1}_{loc}(D)
then there exists an open set
D^{\prime}\subset D
with compact closure such that
\int_{D^{\prime}}\lvert b\rvert=\infty
. Therefore for any Gaussian random variable
Z
that is absolutely continuous with respect to the Lebesgue density one has
E\lvert b(Z)\rvert=\infty
, due to the lower bounded on the density of a Gaussian on any compact set. It can easily be deduced from this that (
2
) satisfies

E\lvert X^{n}_{t}\rvert=\infty,

(3)

for every
t>0
. Therefore, we consider a tamed Euler scheme whose drift coefficient is bounded for every
n\geq 1
. This, in combination with a certain stopping time argument, allows us to demonstrate strong convergence to the true solution. In particular, our argument involves controlling both the regular moments, as well as the inverse moments to the singularity set, of the scheme and the true solution. Furthermore, in Corollary
1
we demonstrate that the conditions of Theorem
1
are general enough to contain a large class of singular potentials for interacting particle systems, including the famous Lennard-Jones interaction in the case of two scalar particles.

2
Assumptions and Main Theorem

Our assumptions and main result are given below. We consider the case where
b
and other key functions given by the singularity set obey an ‘inverse polynomial Lipschitz condition’, meaning the Lipschitz constant can blow up as a negative power of the distance to the singularity set. We additionally assume that the drift coefficient ‘pushes the solution away’ from the points where
b
blows up. In the case of two interacting particle systems with singular interactions
S
is the set of points for which two particles meet, as demonstrated in Corollary
1
.

Assumptions
.

There exists an open set
D\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
and a measurable set
S\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
such that the initial condition
x_{0}
of (
1
) satisfies
x_{0}\in D
, and additionally
D\cap S=\emptyset
and
\partial D\subset S
. We assume the function
\rho:D\to[0,\infty)
, given by

\rho(x):=dist(x,S),

(4)

is twice continuously differentiable on
D
, that is,
\rho\in C^{2}(D)
. Finally
b:\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
is a measurable function for which there exists
c,\alpha,l,h_{1},h_{2},h_{3},h_{4}>0
and
1<\beta<\alpha
such that for every
x,y\in D

\langle b(x)-b(y),x-y\rangle\leq c\lvert x-y\rvert^{2},

(5)

\langle\nabla\rho(x),b(x)\rangle\geq h_{1}\rho(x)^{-\alpha}-h_{2}\rho(x)^{-\beta}-h_{3}-h_{4}\rho(x),

(6)

\lvert\nabla^{2}\rho(x)\rvert\leq c\biggr(1+\rho(x)^{-\beta}\biggr),

(7)

\lvert b(x)-b(y)\rvert\leq c\biggr(1+\rho(x)^{-l}+\rho(y)^{-l}\biggr)\lvert x-y\rvert.

(8)

The key assumption is (
6
), which demonstrates that the dynamics push the true solution away from the singular points of the drift, which lie on
S
. Actually (
6
) furthermore implies that for every sequence
x_{n}\in D
such that
x_{n}\to s\in S
, one has
b(x_{n})\to\infty
.

Remark 1
.

For a simple case obeying the above assumptions, for any
\alpha>1
one could take
d=1
,
D=(0,\infty)
,
b(x)=x^{-\alpha}
,
S=\{0\}
,
\rho(x)=x
,
\beta=0
and
l=\alpha+1
. In particular, since the derivatives of
\rho
are constant, this setting simplifies certain calculations. One observes that (
5
) follows since
b
is decreasing, and (
8
) follows by the mean value theorem.

The monotonicity assumption (
5
) as well as assumption (
6
) mean that our assumptions do not cover interacting particles with singular interaction kernels in higher dimensions, nor with more than two particles. In Corollary
1
we show that our assumptions do indeed cover Lennard-Jones interactions for two particles in the scalar valued case where
d=1
. In future work we would like to lessen these assumptions so as to allow for more general examples.

Theorem 1
.

Let the assumptions presented in this section hold for (
1
). Let us fix
\delta>0
and
w\in[0,\frac{1}{3l}]
. For
\rho
as given in (
4
) and for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
, let us define the truncated coefficients
b_{n}:\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
as

b_{n}(x)=b(x)1_{\{x\in D\}\cap\{\rho(x)\geq\delta n^{-w}\}}.

(9)

Then if one defines
\kappa_{n}(t):=\lfloor nt\rfloor/n
to be the projection backwards onto
\{0,1/n,2/n,...\}
, one has that (
1
) has a unique strong solution
(X_{t})_{t\geq 0}
and

dX^{n}_{t}=b_{n}(X^{n}_{\kappa_{n}(t)})+dW_{t}

(10)

is uniquely well defined for all
t\geq 0
. Furthermore, for any
T>0
and
p>0
there exists
c>0
independent of
n\geq 1
such that

E\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\lvert X^{n}_{t}-X_{t}\rvert^{p}\leq cn^{-p/2}.

(11)

3
Continuous Time Solution

In this section we prove that, given the assumptions in Section
2
, there exists a unique strong solution of (
1
) that stays in
D
almost surely. We begin by showing (
1
) has a unique strong solution up to some stopping time, and in Lemma
3
refine this to show (
1
) has a unique strong solution for all time. For our calculations we use a local version of Itô’s formula presented in the appendix. Henceforth, we assume that the assumptions given in Section
2
hold.

Note 1
.

The constant
c>0
appearing in the following Lemmas may depend on
T>0
but never on
n\geq 1
,
\epsilon>0
or any time parameters

Lemma 1
.

For any
\epsilon>0
there exists a stopping time
\tau_{\epsilon}
such that (
1
) has a unique strong

solution on
[0,\tau_{\epsilon}]
, and furthermore

\tau_{\epsilon}=\inf\{t\geq 0|\;\rho(X_{t})\leq\epsilon\}.

(12)

Proof.

Fix
y_{0}\in D
. Since
D\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
is an open set, there exists an
a>0
for which

, and so by the definition of

in (

\rho(y_{0})>a>0
. Therefore by (

\displaystyle\lvert b(x)\rvert

\displaystyle\leq\lvert b(y_{0})\rvert+\lvert b(x)-b(y_{0})\rvert

\displaystyle\leq c(1+\rho(x)^{-l})(1+\lvert x\rvert),

(13)

where the constant

is independent of

. Now let us define

\displaystyle K_{\epsilon}

\displaystyle:=\{x\in D|\;\rho(x)>\epsilon\},

so that we may define the truncated coefficients

In particular, it follows that by setting

. Here

is the same initial condition as (

). Let us furthermore define the stopping time
\tau_{\epsilon}
by

\tau_{\epsilon}:=\inf\{t\geq 0|\;\bar{X}^{\epsilon}_{t}\not\in K_{\epsilon}\}.

(17)

One observes then that

and therefore

is a strong solution of (

) on

. Furthermore uniqueness follows since if this solution were non-unique on

. Finally (

.

Lemma 2
.

Let
\tau_{\epsilon}
be as in Lemma
1
. Then for every
p>0
and
T>0
one has

\displaystyle\sup_{\epsilon>0}E\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\rho(X_{t\wedge\tau_{\epsilon}})^{-p}<\infty.

(19)

Proof.

Let
X_{t}
be the solution to (
1
) on
[0,\tau_{\epsilon}]
which is given in Lemma
1
. Then by Proposition
1
(see Appendix) one may write

\displaystyle\rho(X_{t\wedge\tau_{\epsilon}})^{-p}

\displaystyle=\rho(x_{0})^{-p}-p\int^{t\wedge\tau_{\epsilon}}_{0}\rho(X_{s})^{-p-1}\langle\nabla\rho(X_{s}),b(X_{s})\rangle ds

\displaystyle-\frac{p}{2}\int^{t\wedge\tau_{\epsilon}}_{0}\rho(X_{s})^{-p-1}tr(\nabla^{2}\rho(X_{s}))ds

\displaystyle+\frac{p(p+1)}{2}\int^{t\wedge\tau_{\epsilon}}_{0}\rho(X_{s})^{-p-2}tr(\nabla\rho(X_{s})\otimes\nabla\rho(X_{s}))ds

\displaystyle-p\int^{t\wedge\tau_{\epsilon}}_{0}\rho(X_{s})^{-p-1}\langle\nabla\rho(X_{s}),dW_{s}\rangle.

(20)

Now observe that for every
x,y\in D
one has

\rho(x)=\inf\{\lvert x-z\rvert|z\in S\}\leq\inf\{\lvert x-y\rvert+\lvert y-z\rvert|z\in S\}=\lvert x-y\rvert+\rho(y),

(21)

so
\rho
is Lipschitz with constant
1
. As a result, since by assumption
\rho\in C^{2}(D)
, one has
\lvert\nabla\rho\rvert\leq 1
, so by this and (
6
) and (
7
) one therefore sees that there exists
c_{1},c_{2},c_{3},c_{4},c_{5}>0
not depending on
\epsilon>0
or
t>0
for which

\displaystyle\rho(X_{t\wedge\tau_{\epsilon}})^{-p}

\displaystyle\leq\rho(x_{0})^{-p}+\int^{t\wedge\tau_{\epsilon}}_{0}(-c_{1}\rho(X_{s})^{-p-1-\alpha}+c_{2}\rho(X_{s})^{-p-1-\beta})ds

\displaystyle+\int^{t\wedge\tau_{\epsilon}}_{0}(c_{3}\rho(X_{s})^{-p-1}+c_{4}\rho(X_{s})^{-p-2})ds

\displaystyle+\int^{t\wedge\tau_{\epsilon}}_{0}c_{5}\rho(X_{s})^{-p}ds-p\int^{t\wedge\tau_{\epsilon}}_{0}\rho(X_{s})^{-p-1}\langle\nabla\rho(X_{s}),dW_{s}\rangle.

(22)

Now observe that since
1<\beta<\alpha
one has

\sup_{x\geq 0}(-c_{1}x^{-p-1-\alpha}+c_{2}x^{-p-1-\beta}+c_{3}x^{-p-1}+c_{4}x^{-p-2})<\infty.

(23)

since the first term dominates in the limit as
x\to 0
. Therefore there exists a constant
c>0
independent of
\epsilon>0
or
t>0
such that

\displaystyle\rho(X_{t\wedge\tau_{\epsilon}})^{-p}

\displaystyle\leq\rho(x_{0})^{-p}+ct+\int^{t\wedge\tau_{\epsilon}}_{0}c_{5}\rho(X_{s\wedge\tau_{\epsilon}})^{-p}ds

\displaystyle-p\int^{t\wedge\tau_{\epsilon}}_{0}\rho(X_{s})^{-p-1}\langle\nabla\rho(X_{s}),dW_{s}\rangle.

(24)

Furthermore, since the integrand of the stochastic integral in (
24
) is bounded by the definition of
\tau_{\epsilon}
, it is a true martingale. As a result, by Grönwall’s inequality one has for every
p>0
that

\displaystyle\sup_{\epsilon>0}\sup_{t\in[0,T]}E\rho(X_{t\wedge\tau_{\epsilon}})^{-p}<\infty.

(25)

Now to prove strong bounds on
[0,T]
, one may proceed by squaring (
24
), taking supremum, and then expectation and the Burkholder-Davis-Gundy inequality, to obtain

\displaystyle E\sup_{u\in[0,t]}

\displaystyle\rho(X_{u\wedge\tau_{\epsilon}})^{-2p}\leq c(1+t^{2})+c\int^{t\wedge\tau_{\epsilon}}_{0}E\rho(X_{s})^{-2p}ds

\displaystyle+cE\sup_{s\in[0,t]}\biggr(\int^{s\wedge\tau_{\epsilon}}_{0}\rho(X_{s})^{-p-1}\langle\nabla\rho(X_{s}),dW_{s}\rangle\biggr)^{2}

\displaystyle\leq c\biggr(1+t^{2}+c\int^{t}_{0}E\rho(X_{s\wedge\tau_{\epsilon}})^{-2p-2}ds\biggr).

(26)

Since (
25
) holds for all
p>0
, the result follows.
∎

Lemma 5
.

For every
p>0
and
T>0
one has

E\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\lvert X_{t}\rvert^{p}<\infty.

(34)

Proof.

Let us define

\tilde{\tau}_{R}:=\inf\{t\geq 0|\;\lvert X_{t}\rvert\geq R\}

(35)

By Itô’s formula there exists a constant
c>0
independent of
R>0
and
t>0
such that

\displaystyle\lvert X_{t\wedge\tilde{\tau}_{R}}\rvert^{p}

\displaystyle\leq\lvert x_{0}\rvert^{p}+\int^{t\wedge\tilde{\tau}_{R}}_{0}(p\lvert X_{s}\rvert^{p-2}\langle X_{s},b(X_{s})\rangle+c\lvert X_{s}\rvert^{p-2})ds

\displaystyle+\int^{t\wedge\tilde{\tau}_{R}}_{0}p\lvert X_{s}\rvert^{p-2}\langle X_{s},dW_{s}\rangle.

(36)

Now observe that, fixing arbitrary
y_{0}\in D
, one has by (
5
) and Young’s inequality that there exists a constant

such that for every

\displaystyle\langle x,b(x)\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle x-y_{0},b(x)-b(y_{0})\rangle+\langle y_{0},b(x)-b(y_{0})\rangle+\langle x,b(y_{0})\rangle

\displaystyle\leq c(1+\lvert b(x)\rvert+\lvert x\rvert^{2}).

(37)

Applying this bound and squaring, one obtains for every

that

\displaystyle\lvert X_{t\wedge\tilde{\tau}_{R}}\rvert^{2p}\leq c\lvert

\displaystyle x_{0}\rvert^{2p}+c\int^{t\wedge\tilde{\tau}_{R}}_{0}\lvert X_{s}\rvert^{2p-4}(1+\lvert b(X_{s})\rvert^{2}+\lvert X_{s}\rvert^{4})ds

\displaystyle+c\biggr(\int^{t\wedge\tilde{\tau}_{R}}_{0}p\lvert X_{s}\rvert^{p-2}\langle X_{s},dW_{s}\rangle\biggr)^{2}.

(38)

Furthermore, using (
13
) there exists another constant

such that for all
x\in D

\lvert b(x)\rvert\leq c(1+\rho(x)^{-2l}+\lvert x\rvert^{2}),

(39)

and therefore

Appendix: Local Itô’s Formula

In this section we prove a local Itô’s formula, which shall be crucial for the proofs of Lemmas
2
and
7
. A related Itô’s formula was proven in
[
9
]
for functions
f\in C^{2}(\bar{D})\cap C^{0}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
of processes that can escape the region
D
, which therefore necessitated extra terms to account for the behaviour on the boundary. In our case we consider a case in which the ‘regular’ Itô’s formula is recovered.

Proposition 1
.

Let the assumptions given in Section
2
hold, and let
(Y_{t})_{t\geq 0}
be a process satisfying

Y_{t}=\int^{t}_{0}g_{s}ds+W_{t},\;\;\;Y_{0}=y_{0}\in D,

(97)

where
(W_{t})_{t\geq 0}
is a Wiener martingale defined on a filtered probability space
(\Omega,P,\mathcal{F},(\mathcal{F}_{t})_{t\geq 0})
, and where
g_{t}
is an
\mathcal{F}_{t}
-measurable processes satisfying

\int^{t}_{0}g_{s}ds<\infty,

(98)

almost surely. Recall the definition of
K_{\epsilon}
in (
14
), and suppose
\tau
is a stopping time such that
Y_{s}\in K_{\epsilon}
almost surely for
s\geq 0
such that
0\leq s\leq\tau
holds. Suppose
f\in C^{2}(D)
. Then one has

f(Y_{\tau})=f(y_{0})+\int^{\tau}_{0}(\langle\nabla f(Y_{s}),Y_{s}\rangle+\frac{1}{2}\Delta f(Y_{s}))ds+\int^{\tau}_{0}\langle\nabla f(Y_{s}),dW_{s}\rangle.

(99)

Proof.

We claim there exists a function
\phi\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
such that
\phi(x)=1
for
x\in K_{3\epsilon/4}
and
\phi(x)=0
for
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus K_{\epsilon/4}
. First let us show that

K_{3\epsilon/4}+B_{\epsilon/16}\subset K_{\epsilon/2}.

(100)

To this end, let
x\in K_{3\epsilon/4}
,
y\in B_{\epsilon/16}
. Then for every
z\in S
one has

\lvert x+y-z\rvert\geq\lvert x-z\rvert-\epsilon/16\geq 3\epsilon/4-\epsilon/16>\epsilon/2.

(101)

Similarly one may show

(\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus K_{\epsilon/4}+B_{\epsilon/16})\cap K_{\epsilon/2}=\emptyset,

(102)

since for every
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus K_{\epsilon/4}
,
y\in B_{\epsilon/16}
and
z\in S
one has

\lvert x+y-z\rvert\leq\lvert x-z\rvert+\epsilon/16<\epsilon/2.

(103)

Now note furthermore that exists a function
\psi\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
such that
\int\psi(x)dx=1
and
supp\;\psi\subset B_{\epsilon/16}
, see Section C.4 in
[
7
]
. Furthermore, by Theorem 6, C.4 in
[
7
]
one has that
\psi*1_{K_{\epsilon/2}}\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. Combining this with (
100
) and (
102
), and noting that every
y\in K_{3\epsilon/4}+B_{\epsilon/16}
satisfies
y\in B_{\epsilon/16}(x)
for some
x\in K_{3\epsilon/4}
, one has for every
x\in K_{3\epsilon/4}
that

\psi*1_{K_{\epsilon/2}}(x)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}1_{K_{\epsilon/2}}(y)\psi(x-y)dy=\int_{B_{\epsilon/16}(x)}1_{K_{\epsilon/2}}(y)\psi(x-y)dy=1.

(104)

Similarly, for every
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus K_{\epsilon/4}
one has
\psi*1_{K_{\epsilon/2}}(x)=0
. Therefore one may set
\phi=\psi*1_{K_{\epsilon/2}}
, and a result one has that
f\phi\in C^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. Furthermore, since for every
x\in K_{\epsilon}
one has that
\phi=1
on a neighbourhood of
x
, it holds that for all multi-indices
\alpha
of order
2
one has
\partial^{\alpha}(f\phi)=\partial^{\alpha}f
on
K_{\epsilon}
. Therefore, one may apply the classical Itô’s formula to obtain an expression for
f(Y_{\tau})\phi(Y_{\tau})
, at which point the result follows.
∎

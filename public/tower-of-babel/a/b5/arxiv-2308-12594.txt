Characterizing existence of certain ultrafilters
Rafał Filipów, Krzysztof Kowitz, Adam Kwela

Abstract
. Following Baumgartner [J. Symb. Log. 60 (1995), no. 2], for an ideal ℐ \mathcal{I} on ω \omega , we say that an ultrafilter 𝒰 \mathcal{U} on ω \omega is an ℐ \mathcal{I} -ultrafilter if for every function f : ω → ω f:\omega\to\omega there is A ∈ 𝒰 A\in\mathcal{U} with f ⁡ [ A ] ∈ ℐ f[A]\in\mathcal{I} . If there is an ℐ \mathcal{I} -ultrafilter which is not a 𝒥 \mathcal{J} -ultrafilter, then ℐ \mathcal{I} is not below 𝒥 \mathcal{J} in the Katětov order ≤ K \leq_{\Kat} (i.e. for every function f : ω → ω f:\omega\to\omega there is A ∈ ℐ A\in\mathcal{I} with f − 1 ​ [ A ] ∉ 𝒥 f^{-1}[A]\notin\mathcal{J} ). On the other hand, in general ℐ ≰ K 𝒥 \mathcal{I}\not\leq_{\Kat}\mathcal{J} does not imply that existence of an ℐ \mathcal{I} -ultrafilter which is not a 𝒥 \mathcal{J} -ultrafilter is consistent. We provide some sufficient conditions on ideals to obtain the equivalence: ℐ ≰ K 𝒥 \mathcal{I}\not\leq_{\Kat}\mathcal{J} if and only if it is consistent that there exists an ℐ \mathcal{I} -ultrafilter which is not a 𝒥 \mathcal{J} -ultrafilter. In some cases when the Katětov order is not enough for the above equivalence, we provide other conditions for which a similar equivalence holds. We are mainly interested in the cases when the family of all ℐ \mathcal{I} -ultrafilters or 𝒥 \mathcal{J} -ultrafilters coincides with some known family of ultrafilters: P-points, Q-points or selective ultrafilters (a.k.a. Ramsey ultrafilters). In particular, our results provide a characterization of Borel ideals ℐ \mathcal{I} which can be used to characterize P-points as ℐ \mathcal{I} -ultrafilters. Moreover, we introduce a cardinal invariant which is used to obtain a sufficient condition for the existence of an ℐ \mathcal{I} -ultrafilter which is not a 𝒥 \mathcal{J} -ultrafilter. Finally, we prove some new results concerning existence of certain ultrafilters under various set-theoretic assumptions.

By

2.1.
Ideals

A family
\mathcal{I}\subseteq\mathcal{P}(X)
is called an
ideal on
X
if it satisfies the following conditions:

(1)

if
A,B\in\mathcal{I}
, then
A\cup B\in\mathcal{I}
,

(2)

if
A\subseteq B
and
B\in\mathcal{I}
, then
A\in\mathcal{I}
,

(3)

\mathcal{I}
contains all finite subsets of
X
,

(4)

X\notin\mathcal{I}
.

A family
\mathcal{F}\subseteq\mathcal{P}(X)
is called a
filter on
X
if it satisfies the following conditions:

(1)

if
A,B\in\mathcal{F}
, then
A\cap B\in\mathcal{F}
,

(2)

if
A\subseteq B
and
A\in\mathcal{F}
, then
B\in\mathcal{F}
,

(3)

\mathcal{F}
contains all cofinite subsets of
X
,

(4)

\emptyset\notin\mathcal{F}
.

If
\mathcal{A}\subseteq\mathcal{P}(X)
then we write
\mathcal{A}^{*}=\{B\subseteq X:X\setminus B\in\mathcal{A}\}
. It is easy to see that if
\mathcal{I}
is an ideal then
\mathcal{I}^{*}
is a filter (called the
dual filter of
\mathcal{I}
). Conversely, if
\mathcal{F}
is a filter then
\mathcal{F}^{*}
is an ideal (called the
dual ideal of
\mathcal{F}
).

We say that an ideal
\mathcal{I}
on
X
is generated by a family
\mathcal{A}\subseteq\mathcal{P}(X)
whenever
\mathcal{I}=\{A\subseteq X:\exists F\in[\mathcal{A}]^{<\omega}(A\subseteq\bigcup F)\}
. For an ideal
\mathcal{I}
on
X
,
we write
\mathcal{I}^{+}=\{A\subseteq X:A\notin\mathcal{I}\}
and call it the
coideal of
\mathcal{I}
.
The ideal of all finite subsets of an infinite set
X

is denoted by
\mathrm{Fin}(X)
(we write
\mathrm{Fin}
instead of
\mathrm{Fin}(\omega)
for short).
For an ideal
\mathcal{I}
on
X
and
A\subseteq X
we define
\mathcal{I}\restriction A=\{B\cap A:B\in\mathcal{I}\}
. It is easy to see that
\mathcal{I}\restriction A
is an ideal on
A
if and only if
A\notin\mathcal{I}
.
An ideal
\mathcal{I}
on
X
is

tall
if for every infinite
A\subseteq X
there is an infinite
B\in\mathcal{I}
such that
B\subseteq A
(some authors use the name
dense ideal
in this case).
An ideal
\mathcal{I}
on
X
is a
P-ideal
(
weak P-ideal
, resp.) if for any countable family
\mathcal{A}\subseteq\mathcal{I}
there is
B\in\mathcal{I}^{*}
(
B\in\mathcal{I}^{+}
, resp.) such that
A\cap B
is finite for every
A\in\mathcal{A}
.
An ideal
\mathcal{I}
on
X
is a
P^{+}
-ideal
(
selective ideal
, resp.) if for any decreasing sequence
\{A_{n}:n\in\omega\}\subseteq\mathcal{I}^{+}
there is
A\in\mathcal{I}^{+}
,
A\subseteq A_{0}
such that

A\cap(A_{n}\setminus A_{n+1})
is finite
(
|A\cap(A_{n}\setminus A_{n+1})|\leq 1
, resp.)
for every
n
.

For
A\subseteq\omega\times\omega
and
n\in\omega
,
we write

A_{(n)}=\{k\in\omega:(n,k)\in A\}

i.e.
A_{(n)}
is
the vertical
section of
A
at the point
n
.

In the sequel, we will use the following ideals.

•

\mathrm{Fin}^{2}=\{A\subseteq\omega\times\omega:\forall^{\infty}n\,(|A_{(n)}|<\omega)\}
.

•

\mathcal{ED}=\{A\subseteq\omega\times\omega:\exists m\,\forall^{\infty}n\,(|A_{(n)}|<m)\}
.

•

\mathcal{ED}_{\mathrm{Fin}}=\{A\subseteq\Delta:\exists m\,\forall n\,(|A_{(n)}|<m)\}
, where
\Delta=\{(n,k)\in\omega^{2}:n\geq k\}
.

•

The
summable ideals
:

\mathcal{I}_{g}=\{A\subseteq\omega:\sum_{n\in A}g(n)<\infty\}
, where
g:\omega\to[0,\infty)
satisfies
\sum_{n\in\omega}g(n)=\infty
.

•

\mathrm{nwd}=\{A\subseteq\mathbb{Q}\cap[0,1]:\text{$A$ is nowhere dense}\}
.

•

\mathrm{conv}=\{A\subseteq\mathbb{Q}\cap[0,1]:\text{$A$ has at most finitely many limit points}\}
.

•

\mathcal{R}
is the ideal generated by homogeneous sets in some fixed instance of the random graph on
\omega
(see e.g.
[
33
, p.29]
for details).

•

\mathcal{W}
is the ideal of all subsets of
\omega
which does not contain arbitrary long finite arithmetic progression.

Let
\mathcal{I},\mathcal{J}
be ideals on
X
and
Y
respectively.
We write
\mathcal{J}\approx\mathcal{I}
if there is a bijection
f:X\to Y
such that
f^{-1}[B]\in\mathcal{I}\iff B\in\mathcal{J}
, and say that
\mathcal{I}
and
\mathcal{J}
are
isomorphic
.

One can think about all of the above ideals as ideals on
\omega
by identifying the domain set of the ideal (for instance,
\omega\times\omega
or
\mathbb{Q}\cap[0,1]
) with
\omega
via a fixed bijection.

If
\mathcal{I}\not\approx\mathrm{Fin}
and
\mathcal{I}\not\approx\mathrm{Fin}\oplus\mathcal{P}(\omega)=\{A\subseteq\omega\times 2:(\omega\times\{0\})\cap A\text{ is finite}\}
, then we define

\adds(\mathcal{I})=\min\{|\mathcal{A}|:\mathcal{A}\subseteq\mathcal{I}\land\neg(\exists B\in\mathcal{I}\,\forall A\in\mathcal{A}\,(|A\setminus B|<\omega)\}
.
It is easy to see that
\adds(\mathcal{I})=\omega
for non P-ideals and
\adds(\mathcal{I})\geq\omega_{1}
for P-ideals.

2.2.
Borel ideals

An ideal on
\omega
is called
F_{\sigma}
(
\mathbf{\Sigma}^{0}_{\alpha}
, Borel, analytic and so on, resp.) if it is an
F_{\sigma}
(
\mathbf{\Sigma}^{0}_{\alpha}
, Borel, analytic and so on, resp.) subset of
\mathcal{P}(\omega)
with a topology induced from the Cantor space
\{0,1\}^{\omega}
by identifying subsets of
\omega
with their characteristic functions.

A map
\phi:\mathcal{P}(\omega)\to[0,+\infty]
is a
submeasure on
\omega
if for every
A,B\subseteq\omega
we have:
\phi(\emptyset)=0
and
\phi(A)\leq\phi(A\cup B)\leq\phi(A)+\phi(B)
. A submeasure
\phi
is
lower semicontinuous
(in short: lsc) if
\phi(A)=\lim_{n\to\infty}\phi(A\cap n)
for all
A\subseteq\omega
. For any lsc submeasure
\phi
, we define the following families:
\mathrm{Fin}(\phi)=\{A\subseteq\omega:\phi(A)<\infty\}
and
\mathrm{Exh}(\phi)=\{A\subseteq\omega:\lim_{n\to\infty}\phi(A\setminus n)=0\}
.

Theorem 2.1

(
[
32
]
and
[
34
]
)
.

(1)

If
\mathcal{I}
is an
F_{\sigma}
-ideal, then there is a lsc submeasure
\phi
such that
\mathcal{I}=\mathrm{Fin}(\phi)
.

(2)

If
\mathcal{I}
is an analytic P-ideal, then there is a lsc submeasure
\phi
such that
\mathcal{I}=\mathrm{Exh}(\phi)
.

(3)

If
\mathcal{I}
is an
F_{\sigma}
P-ideal, then there is a lsc submeasure
\phi
such that
\mathcal{I}=\mathrm{Exh}(\phi)=\mathrm{Fin}(\phi)
.

It is not difficult to see that each summable ideal
\mathcal{I}_{g}
is an
F_{\sigma}
P-ideal, and it is tall iff
\lim_{n\to\infty}g(n)=0
.

Proposition 2.2

(
[
25
, Proposition 4.5]
)
.

Each tall analytic P-ideal contains a tall summable ideal. In particular, an ideal contains a tall summable ideal if and only if it contains a tall analytic P-ideal.

2.3.
Katětov order

By
Y^{X}
we denote the family of all functions from
X
into
Y
.
A function
f\in Y^{X}
is
finite-to-one
if the inverse image
f^{-1}[\{y\}]
is finite for every
y\in Y
.

Let
\mathcal{I},\mathcal{J}
be ideals on
X
and
Y
respectively.
For
\mathcal{F}\subseteq Y^{X}
, we write

\mathcal{J}\leq_{\mathcal{F}}\mathcal{I}
if there is a function
f\in\mathcal{F}
such that
f^{-1}[B]\in\mathcal{I}
for each
B\in\mathcal{J}
. In particular, we write

(1)

\mathcal{J}\leq_{\Kat}\mathcal{I}
if
\mathcal{F}=Y^{X}
(
\leq_{\Kat}
is called the
Katětov order
),

(2)

\mathcal{J}\leq_{\KB}\mathcal{I}
if
\mathcal{F}
is the family of all finite-to-one functions from
X
to
Y
(
\leq_{\KB}
is called the
Katětov-Blass order
),

(3)

\mathcal{J}\leq_{\point}\mathcal{I}
if
\mathcal{F}
is the family of all one-to-one functions from
X
to
Y
,

(4)

\mathcal{J}\sqsubseteq\mathcal{I}
if
\mathcal{F}
is the family of all bijections from
X
to
Y
(in this case we say that
\mathcal{I}
contains an isomorphic copy of
\mathcal{J}
).

In the sequel, we will be particularly interested in three families of functions:

•

\omega^{\omega}
– the family of all functions
f:\omega\to\omega

•

\mathrm{Fin}-1
– the family of all finite-to-one functions
f:\omega\to\omega
,

•

1-1
– the family of all one-to-one functions
f:\omega\to\omega
.

Moreover, as we can think about ideals on countable sets as ideals on
\omega
, we will sometimes write
\mathcal{I}\leq_{\omega^{\omega}}\mathcal{J}
,
\mathcal{I}\leq_{\mathrm{Fin}-1}\mathcal{J}
or
\mathcal{I}\leq_{1-1}\mathcal{J}
even if
\mathcal{I}
and
\mathcal{J}
are defined on other countable sets than
\omega
.

exists by the observation from the second paragraph of this proof) and pick an infinite
E^{\prime}_{n+1}\subseteq E_{n}
such that
E^{\prime}_{n+1}\times\{k_{n+1}\}\subseteq\Delta
and
f\restriction E^{\prime}_{n+1}\times\{k_{n+1}\}
is one-to-one. Again, using tallness of
\mathcal{I}
find
E_{n+1}\subseteq E^{\prime}_{n+1}
such that
f[E_{n+1}\times\{k_{n+1}\}]\in\mathcal{I}
.

Once the induction is completed, as
\mathcal{I}
is a P-ideal, there is
B\in\mathcal{I}
such that
f[E_{n}\times\{k_{n}\}]\setminus B
is finite for all
n\in\omega
. Then
F_{n}=E_{n}\setminus\{i\in E_{n}:\ f(i,k_{n})\in B\}\in\mathrm{Fin}
as
f\restriction E_{n}\times\{k_{n}\}
is one-to-one. Thus

f^{-1}[B]\supseteq\bigcup_{n\in\omega}\left((E_{n}\setminus F_{n})\times\{k_{n}\}\right)\notin\mathcal{ED}_{\mathrm{Fin}}

as for each
m\in\omega
we have

\left|(\{l\}\times\omega)\cap\bigcup_{n\in\omega}\left((E_{n}\setminus F_{n})\times\{k_{n}\}\right)\right|\geq m,

where
l=\min(E_{m}\setminus\bigcup_{j\leq m}F_{j})
.
∎

2.4.
Ultrafilters and
\mathcal{I}
-ultrafilters

Recall that an ultrafilter
\mathcal{U}
on
\omega
is

(1)

a
P-point
if
\mathcal{U}^{*}
is a P-ideal (equivalently, if
\mathcal{U}^{*}
is a
P^{+}
-ideal),

(2)

a
selective ultrafilter
(a.k.a.
Ramsey ultrafilter
) if
\mathcal{U}^{*}
is a selective ideal,

(3)

a
Q-point
if for each partition
\{A_{n}:n\in\omega\}
of
\omega
into finite sets there is
A\in\mathcal{U}
such that
|A_{n}\cap A|=1
for each
n
.

It is known that an ultrafilter is selective if and only if it is both a P-point and a Q-point (see e.g.
[
6
]
).

Following Baumgartner, Brendle and Flašková
[
4
,
11
]
, for an ideal
\mathcal{I}
on
\omega
, we say that an ultrafilter
\mathcal{U}
on
\omega
is

(1)

an
\mathcal{I}
-ultrafilter
if
\mathcal{I}\not\leq_{\Kat}\mathcal{U}^{*}
i.e. there is no function
f:\omega\to\omega
such that
f^{-1}[B]\in\mathcal{U}^{*}
for each
B\in\mathcal{I}
,

(2)

a
weak
\mathcal{I}
-ultrafilter
if
\mathcal{I}\not\leq_{\KB}\mathcal{U}^{*}
i.e. there is no finite-to-one function
f:\omega\to\omega
such that
f^{-1}[B]\in\mathcal{U}^{*}
for each
B\in\mathcal{I}
,

(3)

an
\mathcal{I}
-point
if
\mathcal{I}\not\leq_{\point}\mathcal{U}^{*}
i.e. there is no one-to-one function
f:\omega\to\omega
such that
f^{-1}[A]\in\mathcal{U}^{\star}
for all
A\in\mathcal{I}
.
If
\mathcal{I}
is tall, then, using
[
2
, Lemma 3.3]
, one can see that in the definition of
\mathcal{I}
-point one can consider only bijections instead of one-to-one functions functions i.e.
\mathcal{I}\not\leq_{\point}\mathcal{U}^{*}\iff\mathcal{I}\not\sqsubseteq\mathcal{U}^{*}
.

It was already mentioned in the Introduction that if there is an
\mathcal{I}
-ultrafilter which is not a
\mathcal{J}
-ultrafilter, then necessary
\mathcal{I}\not\leq_{K}\mathcal{J}
. Similarly, for instance, if there is an
\mathcal{I}
-ultrafilter (weak
\mathcal{I}
-ultrafilter or
\mathcal{I}
-point, resp.) which is not a
\mathcal{J}
-point, then
\mathcal{I}\not\leq_{K}\mathcal{J}
(
\mathcal{I}\not\leq_{KB}\mathcal{J}
or
\mathcal{I}\not\leq_{P}\mathcal{J}
, resp.).

Theorem 2.4

(see e.g.

2.5.
Homogeneous ideals

An ideal
\mathcal{I}
is called
homogeneous
if
\mathcal{I}\restriction A\approx\mathcal{I}
for each
A\in\mathcal{I}^{+}
.
Similarly, an ideal
\mathcal{I}
is called
\leq_{\mathcal{F}}
-homogeneous
if
\mathcal{I}\restriction A\approx_{\mathcal{F}}\mathcal{I}
(i.e.
\mathcal{I}\restriction A\leq_{\mathcal{F}}\mathcal{I}
and
\mathcal{I}\leq_{\mathcal{F}}\mathcal{I}\restriction A

Homogeneous ideals were defined and examined in
[
29
]

\Kat

\mathcal{I}
is
\leq_{\mathcal{F}}
-homogeneous if and only if

,

, Example 2.6]
, respectively.

2.6.
P^{+}(\mathcal{I})
-ideal

Let
\mathcal{I}
be an ideal on
X
.
We write
B\subseteq^{\mathcal{I}}A
when
B\setminus A\in\mathcal{I}
(if
\mathcal{I}=\mathrm{Fin}
, we write
B\subseteq^{*}A
).
A set
B\in\mathcal{I}^{+}
is an
\mathcal{I}^{+}
-pseudointersection
of a family
\mathcal{A}
if
B\subseteq^{\mathcal{I}}A
for each
A\in\mathcal{A}
(if
\mathcal{I}=\mathrm{Fin}
, we just say
pseudointersection
).
An ideal
\mathcal{I}
is a
P^{+}(\mathcal{I})
-ideal
if for each decreasing sequence
A_{0}\supseteq A_{1}\supseteq\ldots
of sets from
\mathcal{I}^{+}
there is an
\mathcal{I}^{+}
-pseudointersection of the family
\{A_{n}:n\in\omega\}
.
Obviously, if
\mathcal{I}
is a
P^{+}
-ideal, then it is
P^{+}(\mathcal{I})
-ideal as well.

Proposition 2.6
.

(1)

Every
F_{\sigma}
ideal is a
P^{+}
-ideal.

(2)

\mathrm{Fin}^{2}
is a
P^{+}(\mathrm{Fin}^{2})
-ideal, but it is not a
P^{+}
-ideal.

Proof.

(
1
) See
[
33
, Lemma 3.2.4]
.

(
2
)

\mathrm{Fin}^{2}
is not a

-ideal, because the sequence
A_{n}=(\omega\setminus n)\times\omega
does not have a
(\mathrm{Fin})^{+}
-pseudointersection.
Below, we show that
\mathrm{Fin}^{2}
is a

-ideal.

Let
A_{n}\in(\mathrm{Fin}^{2})^{+}
be a decreasing sequence.
Then we can pick

such that

is infinite for each

.
Then

.

2.7.
Pseudointersection numbers

A
nonempty
family
\mathcal{A}\subseteq\mathcal{P}(\omega)
has
\mathcal{I}^{+}
-FIP
if
\bigcap F\in\mathcal{I}^{+}
for each finite
F\subseteq\mathcal{A}
(if
\mathcal{I}=\mathrm{Fin}
, we say
SFIP
instead of
\mathrm{Fin}^{+}
-FIP).
The
pseudointersection number
of an ideal
\mathcal{I}
on
\omega
is defined by:

\begin{split}\mathfrak{p}(\mathcal{I})=\min(\{|\mathcal{A}|:\text{ $\mathcal{A}$ has $\mathcal{I}^{+}$-FIP,}&\text{ but $\mathcal{A}$ does not have }\\
&\text{an $\mathcal{I}^{+}$-pseudointersection}\}\cup\{\mathfrak{c}^{+}\}).\end{split}

The following easy proposition summarizes a few basic properties of
\mathfrak{p}(\mathcal{I})
.

Proposition 2.7
.

(1)

\mathfrak{p}(\mathrm{Fin})=\mathfrak{p}
.

(2)

\mathfrak{p}(\mathcal{I})=\mathfrak{c}^{+}
for each maximal ideal
\mathcal{I}
.

(3)

\mathfrak{p}(\mathcal{I})\geq\omega_{1}

\iff

\mathcal{I}
is a
P^{+}(\mathcal{I})
-ideal.

Theorem 2.8

(
[
35
]
, see also
[
23
, Proposition 8.19]
or
[
8
, p. 897]
)
.

\mathfrak{p}(\mathrm{Fin}^{2})=\omega_{1}
.

The following theorem seems to be known (see
[
9
]
or
[
16
]
), however, we were unable to find a written proof of it anywhere, so we decided to include it for completeness.

Theorem 2.9
.

\mathfrak{p}\leq\mathfrak{p}(\mathcal{I})
for each
F_{\sigma}
ideal
\mathcal{I}
.

Proof.

Let
\phi
be a lsc submeasure such that
\mathcal{I}=\mathrm{Fin}(\phi)
(see Theorem
2.1
).
Fix
\kappa<\mathfrak{p}
and any
\mathcal{A}\in[\mathcal{I}^{+}]^{\kappa}
having
\mathcal{I}^{+}
-FIP. We will find a set
B\in\mathcal{I}^{+}
such that
B\setminus A\in\mathcal{I}
for each
A\in\mathcal{A}
.

We define a poset
(\mathbb{P},\leq)
as follows:

\mathbb{P}=[\omega]^{<\omega}\times[\mathcal{A}]^{<\omega}
and
(s,F)\leq(t,G)\iff

s\supseteq t
,

F\supseteq G

and

s\setminus t\subseteq\bigcap G
.
Now we define dense subsets of the poset:

(1)

D_{A}=\{(s,F)\in\mathbb{P}:A\in F\}
for
A\in\mathcal{A}
,

(2)

E_{n}=\{(s,F)\in\mathbb{P}:\phi(s)>n\}
for every
n\in\omega
.

Let
\mathcal{D}=\{D_{A}:A\in\mathcal{A}\}\cup\{E_{n}:n\in\omega\}
.
Since

\mathbb{P}
is
\sigma
-centered and

|\mathcal{D}|<\mathfrak{p}
, using Bell’s Theorem (
[
5
]
, see also
[
6
, Theorem 7.12]
or
[
3
, Theorem 1.4.22]
), there exists a filter
\mathcal{G}\subseteq\mathbb{P}
such that
\mathcal{G}\cap D\neq\emptyset
for each
D\in\mathcal{D}
.

We define
B=\bigcup\{s\in[\omega]^{<\omega}:\text{$(s,F)\in\mathcal{G}$ for some finite $F\subseteq\mathcal{A}$}\}
. Then
B\in\mathcal{I}^{+}
, because for each
n
there is
(s,F)\in\mathcal{G}\cap E_{n}
, so
\phi(B)\geq\phi(s)>n
.

Finally,
B\setminus A
is finite for each
A\in\mathcal{A}
. Indeed, if
A\in\mathcal{A}
, then there is
(s,F)\in\mathcal{G}\cap D_{A}
and we will show that
B\setminus A\subseteq s
.

For
k\in B\setminus A
, we find
(s^{\prime},F^{\prime})\in\mathcal{G}
such that
k\in s^{\prime}
. Since
\mathcal{G}
is a filter, there exists
(s^{\prime\prime},F^{\prime\prime})\in\mathcal{G}
such that
(s^{\prime\prime},F^{\prime\prime})\leq(s,F)
and
(s^{\prime\prime},F^{\prime\prime})\leq(s^{\prime},F^{\prime})
.
Then
s^{\prime\prime}\setminus s\subseteq\bigcap F\subseteq A
and
k\notin A
, so
k\notin s^{\prime\prime}\setminus s
.
Taking into account that
k\in s^{\prime\prime}
, we obtain
k\in s
.
∎

3.
The cardinal characteristics and the order associated with
\mathcal{I}
-ultrafilters

In this section we develop tools which will be used in our further considerations – we introduce two cardinal invariants associated to pairs of ideals and families of functions and show how they can be used in our studies.

3.1.
The cardinal
\wsp

Definition 3.1
.

For ideals
\mathcal{I},\mathcal{J}\subseteq\mathcal{P}(\omega)
and a family
\mathcal{F}\subseteq\omega^{\omega}
we define

\begin{split}\wsp(\mathcal{I},\mathcal{J},\mathcal{F})=&\min(\{|\mathcal{A}|:\text{$\mathcal{A}$ has $\mathcal{J}^{+}$-FIP and }\\
&\neg\left(\forall f\in\mathcal{F}\,\exists B\in\mathcal{I}\,(\mathcal{A}\cup\{f^{-1}[B]\}\text{ has $\mathcal{J}^{+}$-FIP})\right)\}\cup\{\mathfrak{c}^{+}\}).\end{split}

The following theorem provides us a basic tool for constructing
\mathcal{I}
-ultrafilters which are not
\mathcal{J}
-ultrafilters.

Theorem 3.2
.

If
\wsp(\mathcal{I},\mathcal{J},\mathcal{F})\geq|\mathcal{F}|
, then there exists an ultrafilter
\mathcal{U}
such that
\mathcal{I}\not\leq_{\mathcal{F}}\mathcal{U}^{*}
and
\mathcal{J}\subseteq\mathcal{U}^{*}
.

In particular, if
\mathcal{F}=\omega^{\omega}
(
\mathcal{F}
is the family of all finite-to-one functions or
\mathcal{F}
is the family of all one-to-one functions, resp.), we obtain an
\mathcal{I}
-ultrafilter (weak
\mathcal{I}
-ultrafilter or
\mathcal{I}
-point, resp.) which is not a
\mathcal{J}
-point.

Proof.

Let
\mathcal{F}=\{f_{\alpha}:\alpha<|\mathcal{F}|\}
.
We will construct a sequence
\{A_{\alpha}:\alpha<|\mathcal{F}|\}\subseteq\mathcal{P}(\omega)
such that for each
\alpha
we have:

(1)

\{A_{\beta}:\beta<\alpha\}
has
\mathcal{J}^{+}
-FIP,

(2)

f_{\alpha}[A_{\alpha}]\in\mathcal{I}
.

Suppose
A_{\beta}
has been constructed for
\beta<\alpha
.
We have 2 cases.

Case 1. If there is
F\in[\alpha]^{<\omega}
with
f_{\alpha}[\bigcap\{A_{\beta}:\beta\in F\}]\in\mathcal{I}
, we put
A_{\alpha}=\bigcap\{A_{\beta}:\beta\in F\}
.

Case 2. Suppose that
f_{\alpha}[\bigcap\{A_{\beta}:\beta\in F\}]\notin\mathcal{I}
for each
F\in[\alpha]^{<\omega}
.
Since
|\alpha|<|\mathcal{F}|\leq\wsp(\mathcal{I},\mathcal{J},\mathcal{F})
and
\mathcal{A}=\{A_{\beta}:\beta<\alpha\}
has
\mathcal{J}^{+}
-FIP, there is
B\in\mathcal{I}
such that
\mathcal{A}\cup\{f_{\alpha}^{-1}[B]\}
has
\mathcal{J}^{+}
-FIP.
Then we put
A_{\alpha}=f_{\alpha}^{-1}[B]
.

The construction of
\langle A_{\alpha}:\alpha<|\mathcal{F}|\rangle
is finished.

Since
\{A_{\alpha}:\alpha<|\mathcal{F}|\}
has
\mathcal{J}^{+}
-FIP, the family

\mathcal{J}^{*}\cup\{A_{\alpha}:\alpha<|\mathcal{F}|\}
has
\mathcal{J}^{+}
-FIP as well (in particular it has SFIP).
Thus, there is an ultrafilter
\mathcal{U}
such that
\mathcal{J}^{*}\cup\{A_{\alpha}:\alpha<|\mathcal{F}|\}\subseteq\mathcal{U}
.
It is easy to see that
\mathcal{U}
is the required ultrafilter.
∎

3.2.
The new order and the cardinal
\mathfrak{u}

For an ideal
\mathcal{I}
on
\omega

and
\mathcal{A}\subseteq\mathcal{P}(\omega)
we write

\mathcal{I}(\mathcal{A})=\left\{B\subseteq\omega:\exists F\in[\mathcal{A}]^{<\omega}(B\setminus\bigcup F\in\mathcal{I})\right\}.

Notice that either
\mathcal{I}(\mathcal{A})=\mathcal{P}(\omega)
or
\mathcal{I}(\mathcal{A})
is the ideal generated by
\mathcal{I}\cup\mathcal{A}
. The latter case holds exactly when
\omega\notin\mathcal{I}(\mathcal{A})
(or, equivalently, when the family
\{\omega\setminus A:A\in\mathcal{A}\}
has
\mathcal{I}^{+}
-FIP).

Definition 3.3
.

Let
\mathcal{I}
and
\mathcal{J}
be ideals on
\omega
,
\mathcal{F},\mathcal{G}\subseteq\omega^{\omega}
and
\lambda\leq\mathfrak{c}
.

(1)

We write
\mathcal{I}\preceq^{\lambda}_{\mathcal{F},\mathcal{G}}\mathcal{J}
if for each ideal
\mathcal{J}^{\prime}
with
\mathcal{J}\leq_{\mathcal{G}}\mathcal{J}^{\prime}
there exists
\mathcal{A}\in[\mathcal{P}(\omega)]^{<\lambda}

such that
\omega\notin\mathcal{J}^{\prime}(\mathcal{A})
and
\mathcal{I}\leq_{\mathcal{F}}\mathcal{J}^{\prime}(\mathcal{A})
.

(2)

We define

\mathfrak{u}(\mathcal{I},\mathcal{F},\mathcal{J},\mathcal{G})=\min\left(\left\{\lambda\leq\mathfrak{c}:\ \mathcal{I}\preceq^{\lambda}_{\mathcal{F},\mathcal{G}}\mathcal{J}\right\}\cup\left\{\mathfrak{c}^{+}\right\}\right).

The following theorem provides us a basic tool for proving when the existence of
\mathcal{I}
-ultrafilters which are not
\mathcal{J}
-ultrafilters is equivalent to
\mathcal{I}\nleq_{\Kat}\mathcal{J}
.

Theorem 3.4
.

If
\mathcal{G}
contains the identity function, then

\begin{split}\mathfrak{u}(\mathcal{I},\mathcal{F},\mathcal{J},\mathcal{G})>|\mathcal{F}|\iff&\text{ there exists an ultrafilter $\mathcal{U}$ such that}\\
&\text{ $\mathcal{I}\not\leq_{\mathcal{F}}\mathcal{U}^{*}$ and $\mathcal{J}\leq_{\mathcal{G}}\mathcal{U}^{*}$.}\end{split}

Proof.

(\Leftarrow)

If
\mathcal{U}
is an ultrafilter
such that
\mathcal{I}\not\leq_{\mathcal{F}}\mathcal{U}^{*}
and
\mathcal{J}\leq_{\mathcal{G}}\mathcal{U}^{*}
, then

\mathcal{J}^{\prime}=\mathcal{U}^{*}
is a maximal ideal
\leq_{\mathcal{G}}
-above
\mathcal{J}
which witnesses
\mathcal{I}\not\preceq^{\lambda}_{\mathcal{F},\mathcal{G}}\mathcal{J}
for every
\lambda\leq\mathfrak{c}
. Hence
\mathfrak{u}(\mathcal{I},\mathcal{F},\mathcal{J},\mathcal{G})=\mathfrak{c}^{+}>|\mathcal{F}|
.

(\Rightarrow)

If
\mathfrak{u}(\mathcal{I},\mathcal{F},\mathcal{J},\mathcal{G})>|\mathcal{F}|
, then
we have
\mathcal{I}\not\preceq^{|\mathcal{F}|}_{\mathcal{F},\mathcal{G}}\mathcal{J}
. Thus, there is an ideal
\mathcal{J}^{\prime}
such that
\mathcal{J}\leq_{\mathcal{G}}\mathcal{J}^{\prime}

and

\mathcal{I}\not\leq_{\mathcal{F}}\mathcal{J}^{\prime}(\mathcal{A})

for every
\mathcal{A}\in[\mathcal{P}(\omega)]^{<|\mathcal{F}|}

with
\omega\notin\mathcal{J}^{\prime}(\mathcal{A})
.

Let
\mathcal{F}=\{f_{\alpha}:\alpha<|\mathcal{F}|\}
.
We will construct a sequence
\langle A_{\alpha}:\alpha<|\mathcal{F}|\rangle
of subsets of
\omega
such that for each
\alpha
we have:

(1)

\{\omega\setminus A_{\beta}:\beta<\alpha\}
has
(\mathcal{J}^{\prime})^{+}
-FIP,

(2)

f_{\alpha}[\omega\setminus A_{\alpha}]\in\mathcal{I}
.

Suppose
A_{\beta}
has been constructed for
\beta<\alpha
.
Let
\mathcal{A}=\{A_{\beta}:\beta<\alpha\}
.
Since
\{\omega\setminus A_{\beta}:\beta<\alpha\}
has
(\mathcal{J}^{\prime})^{+}
-FIP,

\omega\notin\mathcal{J}^{\prime}(\mathcal{A})
.
Since
|\mathcal{A}|\leq|\alpha|<|\mathcal{F}|
,

\mathcal{I}\not\leq_{\mathcal{F}}\mathcal{J}^{\prime}(\mathcal{A})
.
Thus, there is
B\in(\mathcal{J}^{\prime}(\mathcal{A}))^{+}
such that
f_{\alpha}[B]\in\mathcal{I}
.
Then we put
A_{\alpha}=\omega\setminus B
, and the construction of
\langle A_{\alpha}:\alpha<|\mathcal{F}|\rangle
is finished.

Since
\{\omega\setminus A_{\alpha}:\alpha<|\mathcal{F}|\}
has
(\mathcal{J}^{\prime})^{+}
-FIP, the family

(\mathcal{J}^{\prime})^{*}\cup\{\omega\setminus A_{\alpha}:\alpha<|\mathcal{F}|\}
has
(\mathcal{J}^{\prime})^{+}
-FIP as well (in particular it has SFIP).
Thus, there is an ultrafilter
\mathcal{U}
such that
(\mathcal{J}^{\prime})^{*}\cup\{\omega\setminus A_{\alpha}:\alpha<|\mathcal{F}|\}\subseteq\mathcal{U}
.
It is easy to see that
\mathcal{U}
is the required ultrafilter.
∎

Corollary 3.5
.

Let
\mathcal{I}
and
\mathcal{J}
be ideals on
\omega
.

(1)

\mathcal{I}\not\preceq_{\omega^{\omega},\omega^{\omega}}^{\mathfrak{c}}\mathcal{J}

(
\mathcal{I}\not\preceq_{\omega^{\omega},\mathrm{Fin}-1}^{\mathfrak{c}}\mathcal{J}
or
\mathcal{I}\not\preceq^{\mathfrak{c}}_{\omega^{\omega},1-1}\mathcal{J}
, resp.)

\iff
there exists an
\mathcal{I}
-ultrafilter which is not a
\mathcal{J}
-ultrafilter (weak
\mathcal{J}
-ultrafilter or
\mathcal{J}
-point, resp.).

(2)

\mathcal{I}\not\preceq_{\mathrm{Fin}-1,\mathrm{Fin}-1}^{\mathfrak{c}}\mathcal{J}
(
\mathcal{I}\not\preceq_{\mathrm{Fin}-1,1-1}^{\mathfrak{c}}\mathcal{J}
, resp.)

\iff

there exists a weak
\mathcal{I}
-ultrafilter which is not a weak
\mathcal{J}
-ultrafilter (
\mathcal{J}
-point, resp.).

(3)

\mathcal{I}\not\preceq_{1-1,1-1}^{\mathfrak{c}}\mathcal{J}

\iff
there exists an
\mathcal{I}
-point which is not a
\mathcal{J}
-point.

Remark
.

It is easy to see that
\mathcal{I}\leq_{\Kat}\mathcal{J}
implies
\mathcal{I}\preceq_{\omega^{\omega},\omega^{\omega}}^{\mathfrak{c}}\mathcal{J}
. However, the inverse implication is not true in general. Indeed, it is known that
\mathrm{Fin}^{2}\not\leq_{\Kat}\mathrm{conv}

[
24
]
, but
\mathrm{Fin}^{2}\preceq_{\omega^{\omega},\omega^{\omega}}^{\mathfrak{c}}\mathrm{conv}
holds as
\mathrm{Fin}^{2}
-ultrafilters and
\mathrm{conv}
-ultrafilters and P-points all are the same notion (see Theorem
2.4
(
1
)).

We end this section with a result connecting the two cardinal invariants introduced above.

Theorem 3.6
.

If
\mathcal{G}
contains the identity function, then
either

\wsp(\mathcal{I},\mathcal{J},\mathcal{F})=\mathfrak{u}(\mathcal{I},\mathcal{F},\mathcal{J},\mathcal{G})=\mathfrak{c}^{+}

or

\wsp(\mathcal{I},\mathcal{J},\mathcal{F})<\mathfrak{u}(\mathcal{I},\mathcal{F},\mathcal{J},\mathcal{G}).

Proof.

Assume first that
\wsp(\mathcal{I},\mathcal{J},\mathcal{F})<\mathfrak{c}^{+}
.
We will show that
\mathcal{I}\not\preceq_{\mathcal{F},\mathcal{G}}^{\wsp(\mathcal{I},\mathcal{J},\mathcal{F})}\mathcal{J}
.
Let
\mathcal{J}^{\prime}=\mathcal{J}
and take any
\mathcal{A}\subseteq\mathcal{P}(\omega)
such that
|\mathcal{A}|<\wsp(\mathcal{I},\mathcal{J},\mathcal{F})
and
\omega\notin\mathcal{J}^{\prime}(\mathcal{A})
.
Let
\mathcal{B}=\{\omega\setminus A:A\in\mathcal{A}\}

Then
\mathcal{B}
has
\mathcal{J}^{+}
-FIP and
|\mathcal{B}|<\wsp(\mathcal{I},\mathcal{J},\mathcal{F})
,
so for each

there is

such that

\mathcal{J}^{+}
-FIP. In particular,

\mathcal{I}\not\leq_{\mathcal{F}}\mathcal{J}^{\prime}(\mathcal{A})

If

∎

Theorem 4.4
.

Let
\mathcal{I}
be a maximal ideal on
\omega
.
If
\mathcal{F}\subseteq\omega^{\omega}
contains the identity function, then

\wsp(\mathcal{I},\mathrm{Fin},\mathcal{F})=\chi(\mathcal{I}^{*}),

where

\chi(\mathcal{I}^{*})=\min\{|\mathcal{B}|:\mathcal{B}\subseteq\mathcal{I}^{*}\land\forall A\in\mathcal{I}^{*}\,\exists B\in\mathcal{B}\,(B\subseteq^{*}A)\}
.

Proof.

First we show
\wsp(\mathcal{I},\mathrm{Fin},\mathcal{F})\geq\chi(\mathcal{I}^{*})
.
Take a family
\mathcal{A}
with SFIP and such that
|\mathcal{A}|<\chi(\mathcal{I}^{*})
.
Take any
f\in\mathcal{F}
.
We have 2 cases.

Case 1. There is
F\in[\mathcal{A}]^{<\omega}
with
f[\bigcap F]\in\mathcal{I}
. Then we put
B=f[\bigcap F]
and observe that

\bigcap G\cap f^{-1}[B]\supseteq\bigcap G\cap\bigcap F\notin\mathrm{Fin}
for each
G\in[\mathcal{A}]^{<\omega}
.

Case 2. For each
F\in[\mathcal{A}]^{<\omega}
,
f[\bigcap F]\notin\mathcal{I}
.
Since
\mathcal{I}
is maximal,

f[\bigcap F]\in\mathcal{I}^{*}
for each
F\in[\mathcal{A}]^{<\omega}
.
Since
|[\mathcal{A}]^{<\omega}|=|\mathcal{A}|<\chi(\mathcal{I}^{*})
, there is
C\in\mathcal{I}^{*}
such that
f[\bigcap F]\not\subseteq^{*}C
for each
F\in[\mathcal{A}]^{<\omega}
.
Then we put
B=\omega\setminus C
.
Since
\bigcap F\cap f^{-1}[B]=\bigcap F\setminus f^{-1}[C]

for each
F\in[\mathcal{A}]^{<\omega}
,

|\bigcap F\cap f^{-1}[B]|\geq|f[\bigcap F]\setminus C|=\omega
.

We conclude that
\wsp(\mathcal{I},\mathrm{Fin},\mathcal{F})>|\mathcal{A}|
and consequently
\wsp(\mathcal{I},\mathrm{Fin},\mathcal{F})\geq\chi(\mathcal{I}^{*})
.

Now we show
\wsp(\mathcal{I},\mathrm{Fin},\mathcal{F})\leq\chi(\mathcal{I}^{*})
.
Fix
\mathcal{G}\subseteq\mathcal{I}^{*}
with
|\mathcal{G}|<\wsp(\mathcal{I},\mathrm{Fin},\mathcal{F})
.
Since
\mathcal{G}
has SFIP and
|\mathcal{G}|<\wsp(\mathcal{I},\mathrm{Fin},\mathcal{F})
, for the identity function
f\in\mathcal{F}
, we can find
B\in\mathcal{I}
such that
\mathcal{G}\cup\{B\}=\mathcal{G}\cup\{f^{-1}[B]\}
has SFIP.
Let
C=\omega\setminus B
.
Then
C\in\mathcal{I}^{*}
and for each
G\in\mathcal{G}
we have

|G\setminus C|=|G\cap B|=\omega
, so
G\not\subseteq^{*}C
.
Thus
\chi(\mathcal{I}^{*})>|\mathcal{G}|
, and consequently,
\wsp(\mathcal{I},\mathrm{Fin},\mathcal{F})\leq\chi(\mathcal{I}^{*})
.

5.
Let’s play a game

In this section we develop an infinite game which will enable us to replace CH or
\mathfrak{p}=\mathfrak{c}
with
\cov(\mathcal{M})=\mathfrak{c}
in some of our results.

Definition 5.1
.

Consider the following game
G(\mathcal{J},A,f)
associated to an ideal
\mathcal{J}
on
\omega
,
A\notin\mathcal{J}
and a function
f:A\to\omega
which is
\mathcal{J}
-to-one (i.e.
f^{-1}[\{n\}]\in\mathcal{J}
for every
n
). In the first move Player I plays
k_{0}\in\omega
and then in the
n
th move Player II plays
G_{n}\in\mathrm{Fin}
and Player I responses in the
(n+1)
st move with a pair
(F_{n},k_{n+1})\in\mathrm{Fin}\times\omega
such that
F_{n}\cap G_{n}=\emptyset
and
|F_{n}|\leq k_{n}
. At the end Player I is declared the winner if
f^{-1}[\bigcup_{n\in\omega}F_{n}]\notin\mathcal{J}\restriction A
. Otherwise Player II is the winner.

Next result characterizes winning strategies in
G(\mathcal{J},A,f)
and connects the considered game with the property of containing a tall summable ideal.

Lemma 5.2
.

(1)

Player I has a winning strategy in
G(\mathcal{J},A,f)
if and only if
there are families
\mathcal{F}_{0},\mathcal{F}_{1},\dots\subseteq\mathrm{Fin}
such that:

(a)

\sup\{|F|:F\in\mathcal{F}_{n}\}<\infty
for each
n
,

(b)

\sup\{\min(F):F\in\mathcal{F}_{n}\}=\infty
for each
n
,

(c)

if
F_{n}\in\mathcal{F}_{n}
for each
n
, then
f^{-1}[\bigcup_{n\in\omega}F_{n}]\notin\mathcal{J}\restriction A
.

(2)

Player II has a winning strategy in
G(\mathcal{J},A,f)
if and only if there is a tall summable ideal
\mathcal{I}_{g}
such that
f
witnesses
\mathcal{I}_{g}\leq_{\Kat}\mathcal{J}\restriction A
.

(3)

If
\mathcal{J}\restriction A
is a Borel ideal, then the game
G(\mathcal{J},A,f)
is determined for all
f
.

(4)

If
\mathcal{J}
is Borel such that for every
A\notin\mathcal{J}
the ideal
\mathcal{J}\restriction A
is not
\leq_{\Kat}
-above any tall summable ideal, then Player I has a winning strategy in
G(\mathcal{J},A,f)
, for all
A
and
f
.

Proof.

(
1
) Assume that there are families
\mathcal{F}_{n}
satisfying (a)-(c). Then Player I has a winning strategy by playing in his initial move
k_{0}=\sup\{|F|:F\in\mathcal{F}_{0}\}
and then in each next move (in response to some
G_{n}\in\mathrm{Fin}
) any
F_{n}\in\mathcal{F}_{n}
such that
F_{n}\cap G_{n}=\emptyset
(such
F_{n}
exists from the property (b)) and
k_{n+1}=\sup\{|F|:F\in\mathcal{F}_{n+1}\}
.

Now, assume that Player I has a winning strategy
\$:\mathrm{Fin}^{<\omega}\to\mathrm{Fin}\times\omega
.
Let
\pi_{\mathrm{Fin}}:\mathrm{Fin}\times\omega\to\mathrm{Fin}
and
\pi_{\omega}:\mathrm{Fin}\times\omega\to\omega
be the projections onto the first and the second coordinate, respectively (so
\pi_{\mathrm{Fin}}(F,n)=F
and
\pi_{\omega}(F,n)=n
for all
(F,n)\in\mathrm{Fin}\times\omega
). Let
\mathcal{F}(\emptyset)=\{\pi_{\mathrm{Fin}}(\$(G)):G\in\mathrm{Fin}\}
and

\mathcal{F}(G_{0},\ldots,G_{n})=\{\pi_{\mathrm{Fin}}(\$(G_{0},\ldots,G_{n},G)):G\in\mathrm{Fin}\}

for all
(G_{0},\ldots,G_{n})\in\mathrm{Fin}^{<\omega}
. Observe that for each
F\in\mathcal{F}(G_{0},\ldots,G_{n})
we have
|F|\leq\pi_{\omega}(\$(G_{0},\ldots,G_{n}))
, and
\sup\{\min(F):F\in\mathcal{F}(G_{0},\ldots,G_{n})\}=\infty
as for each
m\in\omega
minimum of the set
\pi_{\mathrm{Fin}}(\$(G_{0},\ldots,G_{n},[0,m]))\in\mathcal{F}(G_{0},\ldots,G_{n})
is greater than
m
. Thus, the families
\mathcal{F}(G_{0},\ldots,G_{n})
satisfy (a) and (b). As
\mathrm{Fin}^{<\omega}
is countable, it suffices to check (c). For each
(G_{0},\ldots,G_{n})\in\mathrm{Fin}^{<\omega}
, take
F_{(G_{0},\ldots,G_{n})}\in\mathcal{F}(G_{0},\ldots,G_{n})
.
Let
B=\bigcup\{F_{(G_{0},\ldots,G_{n})}:(G_{0},\ldots,G_{n})\in\mathrm{Fin}^{<\omega}\}
.
To finish the proof, we have to show that
f^{-1}[B]\notin\mathcal{J}\restriction A
.

Since
F_{\emptyset}\in\mathcal{F}(\emptyset)
, there is
H_{0}\in\mathrm{Fin}
such that
F_{\emptyset}=\pi_{\mathrm{Fin}}(\$(H_{0}))
.
Since
F_{(H_{0})}\in\mathcal{F}(H_{0})
, there is
H_{1}\in\mathrm{Fin}
such that
F_{(H_{0})}=\pi_{\mathrm{Fin}}(\$(H_{0},H_{1}))
.
Continuing in this way we can inductively pick an infinite sequence
H_{0},H_{1},\ldots\in\mathrm{Fin}
such that
F_{(H_{0},\dots,H_{n})}=\pi_{\mathrm{Fin}}(\$(H_{0},\ldots,H_{n},H_{n+1}))\in\mathcal{F}(H_{0},\dots,H_{n})
for each
n\in\omega
.
Then

f^{-1}[B]\supseteq f^{-1}\left[\bigcup_{n\in\omega}F_{(H_{0},\dots,H_{n})}\right]\notin\mathcal{J}\restriction A

as
\bigcup_{n\in\omega}F_{(H_{0},\dots,H_{n})}
constitutes an outcome of a play of the game along the winning strategy for Player I.

(
2
)
Suppose that
f:A\to\omega
witnesses
\mathcal{I}_{g}\leq_{\Kat}\mathcal{J}\restriction A
. Then Player II has a winning strategy by playing in each move a set
[0,m_{n}]\in\mathrm{Fin}
such that
k_{n}g(i)<1/2^{n}
for all
i>m_{n}
as in this case

\sum_{i\in\bigcup_{n\in\omega}F_{n}}g(i)\leq\sum_{n\in\omega}\frac{1}{2^{n}}<+\infty,

hence
\bigcup_{n\in\omega}F_{n}\in\mathcal{I}_{g}
and consequently
f^{-1}[\bigcup_{n\in\omega}F_{n}]\in\mathcal{J}\restriction A
.

Now, assume that Player II has a winning strategy
\$:\omega\times(\mathrm{Fin}\times\omega)^{<\omega}\to\mathrm{Fin}
.

Put
M_{0}=\$(1)
,
M_{1}=\$(2)
and define inductively:

\begin{split}M_{n+1}=&\bigcup\{\$(k_{0},F_{0},k_{1},F_{1}\ldots,k_{l},F_{l},k_{l+1}):l\leq n,\forall i\leq l\left(|F_{i}|\leq k_{i}\right),\\
&\forall i\leq l\left(F_{i}\cap\$(k_{0},F_{0},\ldots,k_{i})=\emptyset\right),\forall i\leq l+1\left(k_{i}\leq n+2\right),\bigcup_{i\leq n}F_{i}\subseteq M_{n}\}\end{split}

for all
n>0
. Without loss of generality we may assume that
(M_{n})_{n\in\omega}
is a strictly increasing sequence of finite sets and
\bigcup_{n\in\omega}M_{n}=\omega
(as playing larger sets by Player II only increases chances of winning).

Define
g:\omega\to[0,+\infty)
by
g(i)=\frac{1}{n+1}
whenever
i\in M_{n+1}\setminus M_{n}
and
g(i)=2
for all
i\in M_{0}
. Observe that
\mathcal{I}_{g}
is tall as
\lim_{i\to\infty}g(i)=0
. We claim that
f
witnesses
\mathcal{I}_{g}\leq_{\Kat}\mathcal{J}\restriction A
.

For every
B\in\mathcal{I}_{g}
there is
k\in\omega
such that

\frac{|B\cap M_{n}|}{n}\leq\sum_{i\in B\cap M_{n}}g(i)\leq k,

for all
n>0
. Hence,
B
can be partitioned into
M_{0}\cap B
(for which we have
f^{-1}[M_{0}\cap B]\subseteq f^{-1}[M_{0}]\in\mathcal{J}\restriction A
as
M_{0}\in\mathrm{Fin}
and
f^{-1}[\{i\}]\in\mathcal{J}\restriction A
for all
i\in\omega
) and
k
many sets
C
satisfying the property
|C\cap M_{n}|\leq n
for all
n>0
. Thus, to finish the proof we need to show that for each set
C\subseteq\omega
such that
|C\cap M_{n}|\leq n
for all
n
we have
f^{-1}[C]\in\mathcal{J}\restriction A
.

Fix
C\subseteq\omega
as above and let
C_{n}=C\cap(M_{n+1}\setminus M_{n})
for all
n\in\omega
. Then for each
n\in\omega
we have
|C_{2n+2}|\leq 2n+3
and

C_{2n+2}\cap\$(1,C_{0},3,C_{2},\ldots,2n+1,C_{2n},2n+3)\subseteq C_{2n+2}\cap M_{2n+2}=\emptyset.

Thus,
C^{\prime}=\bigcup_{n\in\omega}C_{2n}
is an outcome of a play of the game along the winning strategy for Player II. Hence,
f^{-1}[C^{\prime}]\in\mathcal{J}\restriction A
. Similarly one can show that
f^{-1}[C^{\prime\prime}]\in\mathcal{J}\restriction A
where
C^{\prime\prime}=\bigcup_{n\in\omega}C_{2n+1}=C\setminus C^{\prime}
.

(
3
) We will use Martin’s theorem on Borel determinacy. Indeed, using the notation from
[
27
, Section 20A]
,
G(\mathcal{J},A,f)
is equivalent to the game
G(T,\phi^{-1}[\mathcal{J}\restriction A])
where

T=\left\{(F_{0},\ldots,F_{n})\in\mathrm{Fin}^{<\omega}:\forall{i\in\omega}\left(|F_{3i}|=1\text{ and }F_{3i+1}\cap F_{3i+2}=\emptyset\text{ and }\right.\right.

\left.\left.\left(|F_{3i+2}|\leq x\text{, where }\{x\}=F_{3i}\right)\right)\right\}

and
\phi:[T]\to\mathcal{P}(A)
given by
\phi((F_{0},F_{1},\ldots))=f^{-1}[\bigcup_{i\in\omega}F_{3i+2}]
is a function of Baire class
1
(

denotes the set of all infinite branches of the tree
T
).

(
4
) Let
A\notin\mathcal{J}
and
f:A\to\omega
be such that
f^{-1}[\{n\}]\in\mathcal{J}
for all

. Since
\mathcal{J}\restriction A
is not
\leq_{\Kat}
-above any tall summable ideal, Player II cannot have a winning strategy in

by item (

is determined by item (

) as

being Borel implies that
\mathcal{J}\restriction A
is Borel as well. Thus, Player I has a winning strategy in

.
∎

Proposition 5.5
.

Suppose that
\mathcal{J}=\mathrm{Fin}(\phi)
is a
\bf{\Sigma^{0}_{2}}
ideal such that:

(\star)\ \forall_{n\in\omega}\ \exists_{k\in\omega}\ \forall_{A\notin\mathrm{Fin}(\phi)}\ \exists_{F\in\mathrm{Fin}}\ (|F|\leq k\land\phi(F\cap A)\geq n).

Then for each
A\notin\mathcal{J}
the ideal
\mathcal{J}\restriction A
is not
\leq_{\Kat}
-above any tall summable ideal (in particular, Player I has a winning strategy in
G(\mathcal{J},A,f)
for all
A
and
f
).

Proof.

The ”in particular” part follows from Lemma
5.2
(
4
).

Let
A\notin\mathcal{J}
,
f:A\to\omega
and
\mathcal{I}_{g}
be a tall summable ideal. If
f^{-1}[\{n\}]\notin\mathcal{J}
for some
n\in\omega
then we are done, so suppose that
f^{-1}[\{n\}]\in\mathcal{J}
for all
n\in\omega
.

We will inductively pick finite sets
B_{n}
, for
n\in\omega
, such that:

•

\phi(f^{-1}[B_{n}])\geq n
;

•

\sum_{i\in B_{n}}g(i)\leq\frac{1}{2^{n}}
.

In the
n
th step, using condition
(\star)
find
k\in\omega
such that:

\forall_{C\notin\mathrm{Fin}(\phi)}\ \exists_{F\in\mathrm{Fin}}\ (|F|\leq k\land\phi(F\cap C)\geq n).

Since
\mathcal{I}_{g}
is tall,
\lim_{i\to\infty}g(i)=0
. Hence, there is
G\in\mathrm{Fin}
such that
g(i)\leq\frac{1}{k\cdot 2^{n}}
for all

. Then
A\setminus f^{-1}[G]\notin\mathcal{J}
(as
A\notin\mathcal{J}
and
f^{-1}[G]\in\mathcal{J}
), so there is
F_{n}\in\mathrm{Fin}
such that
|F_{n}|\leq k
and
\phi(F_{n}\cap(A\setminus f^{-1}[G]))\geq n
. Put
B_{n}=f[F_{n}]
and observe that
\sum_{i\in B_{n}}g(i)\leq k\cdot\frac{1}{k\cdot 2^{n}}=\frac{1}{2^{n}}
.

Define
B=\bigcup_{n\in\omega}B_{n}
. Then
\phi(f^{-1}[B])\geq\phi(f^{-1}[B_{n}])\geq n
for all
n\in\omega
, so
f^{-1}[B]\notin\mathcal{J}\restriction A
. On the other hand,
\sum_{i\in B}g(i)\leq\sum_{n\in\omega}\sum_{i\in B_{n}}g(i)\leq\sum_{n\in\omega}\frac{1}{2^{n}}<\infty
, so
B\in\mathcal{I}_{g}
. ∎

Proposition 5.6
.

\mathrm{Fin}
,

,

and

all are examples of
\bf{\Sigma^{0}_{2}}

from Proposition

.

Proof.

We will show that
\mathcal{W}
and
\mathcal{ED}
satisfy condition
(\star)
from Proposition
5.5
. The cases of
\mathrm{Fin}
and
\mathcal{ED}_{\mathrm{Fin}}
are similar.

Observe that
\mathcal{W}=\mathrm{Fin}(\phi_{\mathcal{W}})
where

\phi_{\mathcal{W}}(A)=\sup\{n\in\omega:A\text{ contains an arithmetic progression of length }n\}

for all
A\subseteq\omega
. We claim that
k=n
works for all
n
. Fix
n\in\omega
and
A\notin\mathcal{W}
. Then
A
contains arbitrary long finite arithmetic progressions. In particular, there is an arithmetic progression
F\subseteq A
of length
n
. Clearly,
|F|=n
and
\phi_{\mathcal{W}}(F\cap A)=\phi_{\mathcal{W}}(F)=n
.

\mathcal{ED}=\mathrm{Fin}(\phi_{\mathcal{ED}})

]

works for all

\phi_{\mathcal{ED}}(A)>n

Theorem 5.7
.

If
\mathcal{I}
contains a tall summable ideal and Player I has a winning strategy in
G(\mathcal{J},A,f)
for all
A
and
f
(in particular, if
\mathcal{J}
is a Borel ideal such that
\mathcal{J}\restriction A
is not
\leq_{\Kat}
-above any tall summable ideal for each
A\notin\mathcal{J}
),
then

\cov(\mathcal{M})\leq\wsp(\mathcal{I},\mathcal{J},\omega^{\omega}).

Proof.

The “in particular” part follows from Lemma
5.2
(
4
).

Since
\mathcal{I}
contains a tall summable ideal, there is

g:\omega\to[0,+\infty)
such that

\lim_{i\to\infty}g(i)=0

and

\mathcal{I}_{g}=\{A\subseteq\omega:\sum_{a\in A}g(a)<\infty\}\subseteq\mathcal{I}

Fix a family
\mathcal{A}
having
\mathcal{J}^{+}
-FIP and such that
|\mathcal{A}|<\cov(\mathcal{M})
.
Let
f\in\omega^{\omega}
. We need to show that
\{f^{-1}[B]\}\cup\mathcal{A}
has
\mathcal{J}^{+}
-FIP for some
B\in\mathcal{I}
.

If there is
n\in\omega
such that
\{f^{-1}[\{n\}]\}\cup\mathcal{A}
has
\mathcal{J}^{+}
-FIP, then the proof is finished in this case.

Now, suppose that

\{f^{-1}[\{n\}]\}\cup\mathcal{A}
does not have
\mathcal{J}^{+}
-FIP for any
n\in\omega
.

We define a poset
(\mathbb{P},\leq)
as follows:

\mathbb{P}=\left\{p\in\mathrm{Fin}\setminus\{\emptyset\}:\sum_{i\in p}g(i)\leq\left(2-\frac{1}{2^{|p|}}\right)\cdot\frac{1}{1+\min p}\right\}

and

q\leq p

\iff

(1)

q\supseteq p
,

(2)

\min(q\setminus p)>\max p
[here
\min\emptyset=\omega
by convention].

Let

T=\left\{H\in[\mathcal{A}]^{<\omega}:f^{-1}[\{n\}]\cap\bigcap H\notin\mathcal{J}\text{ for infinitely many $n$}\right\}.

For each
H\in[\mathcal{A}]^{<\omega}\setminus T
, there is
k_{H}\in\omega
such that
f^{-1}[\{n\}]\cap\bigcap H\in\mathcal{J}
for each
n\geq k_{H}
. Then,
\bigcap H\setminus f^{-1}[\{i:i<k_{H}\}]\notin\mathcal{J}
(otherwise,
\{f^{-1}[\{n\}]\}\cup\mathcal{A}
would have
\mathcal{J}^{+}
-FIP for some
n<k_{H}
) and
f\restriction(\bigcap H\setminus f^{-1}[\{i:i<k_{H}\}])
is
\mathcal{J}
-to-1. Since Player I has a winning strategy in
G(\mathcal{J},\bigcap H\setminus f^{-1}[\{i:i<k_{H}\}]),f\restriction(\bigcap H\setminus f^{-1}[\{i:i<k_{H}\}]))
, we can use Lemma
5.2
(
1
) to obtain
families
\mathcal{F}_{0}^{H},\mathcal{F}_{1}^{H},\dots\subseteq\mathrm{Fin}
such that:

(1)

\sup\{|F|:F\in\mathcal{F}_{n}^{H}\}<\infty
for each
n
,

(2)

\sup\{\min(F):F\in\mathcal{F}_{n}^{H}\}=\infty
for each
n
,

(3)

if
F_{n}\in\mathcal{F}_{n}^{H}
for each
n
, then
f^{-1}[\bigcup_{n\in\omega}F_{n}]\notin\mathcal{J}\restriction(\bigcap H\setminus f^{-1}[\{i:i<k_{H}\}]))
.

Let us define the following sets:

(1)

D_{H}=\{p\in\mathbb{P}:f^{-1}[p]\cap\bigcap H\notin\mathcal{J}\}
for each
H\in T
,

(2)

E^{n}_{H}=\{p\in\mathbb{P}:F\subseteq p\text{ for some }F\in\mathcal{F}_{n}^{H}\}

for each
n\in\omega
and
H\in[\mathcal{A}]^{<\omega}\setminus T
.

Below, we show that these sets are dense in
(\mathbb{P},\leq)
.

(1)
Let
H\in T
and
p\in\mathbb{P}
. Since
\lim_{i\to\infty}g(i)=0
, there is
t\in\omega
such that

g(i)<\left(\frac{1}{2^{|p|}}-\frac{1}{2^{|p|+1}}\right)\cdot\frac{1}{1+\min p}

for all
i>t
.
Since
H\in T
, there is
n>\max(t,\max(p))
with
f^{-1}[\{n\}]\cap\bigcap H\notin\mathcal{J}
.
Let
q=p\cup\{n\}
.
Then
q\in\mathbb{P}
as

\begin{split}\sum_{i\in q}g(i)&<\sum_{i\in p}g(i)+\left(\frac{1}{2^{|p|}}-\frac{1}{2^{|p|+1}}\right)\cdot\frac{1}{1+\min p}\leq\\
&\leq\left(2-\frac{1}{2^{|p|}}\right)\cdot\frac{1}{1+\min p}+\left(\frac{1}{2^{|p|}}-\frac{1}{2^{|p|+1}}\right)\cdot\frac{1}{1+\min p}\\
&=\left(2-\frac{1}{2^{|p|+1}}\right)\cdot\frac{1}{1+\min p}=\left(2-\frac{1}{2^{|q|}}\right)\cdot\frac{1}{1+\min q}.\end{split}

Since
q\in D_{H}
and
q\leq p
,
D_{H}
is dense in
(\mathbb{P},\leq)
.

(2) Let
n\in\omega
,
H\in[\mathcal{A}]^{<\omega}\setminus T
and
p\in\mathbb{P}
.
Put
k_{n}=\sup\{|F|:F\in\mathcal{F}_{n}^{H}\}
.
Since
\lim_{i\to\infty}g(i)=0
, there is
t\in\omega
such that

g(i)<\frac{1}{k_{n}}\cdot\left(\frac{1}{2^{|p|}}-\frac{1}{2^{|p|+k_{n}}}\right)\cdot\frac{1}{1+\min p}

for all
i>t
.
Since
\sup\{\min(F):F\in\mathcal{F}_{n}^{H}\}=\infty
,
there is
F\in\mathcal{F}_{n}^{H}
with
\min(F)>\max(t,\max(p))
.
Let
q=p\cup F
. Then
q\in\mathbb{P}
as

\begin{split}\sum_{i\in q}g(i)&<\sum_{i\in p}g(i)+\sum_{i\in F}\frac{1}{k_{n}}\cdot\left(\frac{1}{2^{|p|}}-\frac{1}{2^{|p|+k_{n}}}\right)\cdot\frac{1}{1+\min p}\leq\\
&\leq\left(2-\frac{1}{2^{|p|}}\right)\cdot\frac{1}{1+\min p}+\left(\frac{1}{2^{|p|}}-\frac{1}{2^{|p|+k_{n}}}\right)\cdot\frac{1}{1+\min p}\\
&=\left(2-\frac{1}{2^{|p|+k_{n}}}\right)\cdot\frac{1}{1+\min p}\leq\left(2-\frac{1}{2^{|q|}}\right)\cdot\frac{1}{1+\min q}.\end{split}

Since
q\in E^{n}_{H}
and
q\leq p
,
E_{H}^{n}
is dense in
(\mathbb{P},\leq)
.

Let
\mathcal{D}
be the family of all the sets of the form
D_{H}
and
E_{H}^{n}
.

Since

\mathbb{P}
is countable and

|\mathcal{D}|<\cov(\mathcal{M})
, there is a filter
\mathcal{G}\subseteq\mathbb{P}
such that
\mathcal{G}\cap D\neq\emptyset
for each
D\in\mathcal{D}
(see e.g.
[
6
, Theorem 7.13]
)).

We define
B=\bigcup\mathcal{G}
.
If we show that
B\in\mathcal{I}
and
\{f^{-1}[B]\}\cup\mathcal{A}
has
\mathcal{J}^{+}
-FIP, then the proof will be finished in this case.

Let
B=\{b_{i}:\ i\in\omega\}
be the increasing enumeration of
B
. For each
i\in\omega
, there is
p_{i}\in\mathcal{G}
with
b_{i}\in p_{i}
. Since
\mathcal{G}
is a filter, without loss of generality we may assume that
p_{0}\subseteq p_{1}\subseteq\dots
.
Then

\sum_{i\in\omega}g(b_{i})=\lim_{i\to\infty}\sum_{j\in p_{i}}g(j)\leq\lim_{i\to\infty}\left(2-\frac{1}{2^{|p_{i}|}}\right)\cdot\frac{1}{1+\min p_{i}}=\frac{2}{1+\min B}<\infty,

so
B\in\mathcal{I}_{g}\subseteq\mathcal{I}
.

Finally, we show that
\{f^{-1}[B]\}\cup\mathcal{A}
has
\mathcal{J}^{+}
-FIP.
Let
H\in[\mathcal{A}]^{<\omega}
.

If
H\in T
, take
p\in\mathcal{G}\cap D_{H}
.
Then
f^{-1}[B]\cap\bigcap H\supseteq f^{-1}[p]\cap\bigcap H\notin\mathcal{J}
.

If
H\in[\mathcal{A}]^{<\omega}\setminus T
, take

p_{n}\in\mathcal{G}\cap E_{H}^{n}
for each
n\in\omega
, and pick
F_{n}\in\mathcal{F}_{n}^{H}
such that
F_{n}\subseteq p_{n}
.
Then

f^{-1}[B]\cap\bigcap H\supseteq f^{-1}\left[\bigcup_{n\in\omega}p_{n}\right]\cap\bigcap H\supseteq f^{-1}\left[\bigcup_{n\in\omega}F_{n}\right]\cap\bigcap H\notin\mathcal{J}.

∎

∎

6.
P-points which are not
\mathcal{J}
-ultrafilters

Now we turn our attention to ideals
\mathcal{J}
such that it is consistent that there is a P-point which is not a
\mathcal{J}
-ultrafilter. We start with an immediate consequence of our previous results.

Proposition 6.1
.

Let
\mathcal{J}
be a
\text{P}^{+}(\mathcal{J})
-ideal which is
\leq_{K}
-homogeneous.

(1)

Assume CH. The following conditions are equivalent.

(a)

\mathrm{Fin}^{2}\not\leq_{\Kat}\mathcal{J}
.

(b)

There is a P-point which is not a
\mathcal{J}
-point.

(c)

There is a P-point which is not a weak
\mathcal{J}
-ultrafilter.

(d)

There is a P-point which is not a
\mathcal{J}
-ultrafilter.

(2)

If
\mathcal{J}
is
F_{\sigma}
, then CH can be relaxed to the assumption
\mathfrak{p}=\mathfrak{c}
.

Proof.

It follows from Theorems
4.3
(
1
)
and
2.4
(
1
). In the case of
F_{\sigma}
-ideals additionally we need to use Theorems
4.3
(4).
∎

Although the assumptions on
\mathcal{J}
in the above proposition seem to be strong, it can be applied for instance for
\mathcal{J}=\mathcal{ED}_{\mathrm{Fin}}
and
\mathcal{J}=\mathcal{W}
(by Propositions
2.6
(
1
),
2.5
and
2.3
(
3
)).
However, we cannot drop the assumption on the ideal
\mathcal{J}
in general.
Indeed, it is known that
\mathrm{Fin}^{2}\not\leq_{\Kat}\mathrm{conv}
(
[
24
]
), but
all P-points (i.e.
\mathrm{Fin}^{2}
-ultrafilters)
are
\mathrm{conv}
-ultrafilters by Theorem
2.4
(
1
).
Nevertheless, in Theorem
6.3
we show that
the assumption on the ideal
\mathcal{J}
can be removed if we replace the Katětov order
\leq_{\Kat}
by the property that
\mathcal{J}
can be extended to a
P^{+}
-ideal. In order to show it, we will need the following rather easy result about
P^{+}
-ideals.

Lemma 6.2
.

Let
\mathcal{I}
and
\mathcal{J}
be ideals on
\omega
.

(1)

If
\mathcal{I}\leq_{\Kat}\mathcal{J}
and
\mathcal{J}
is a
P^{+}
-ideal, then
\mathcal{I}
can be extended to a
P^{+}
-ideal.

(2)

If
\mathcal{J}
is a
P^{+}
-ideal and
\mathcal{A}\subseteq\mathcal{P}(\omega)
is a countable family such that
\omega\notin\mathcal{J}(\mathcal{A})
, then
\mathcal{J}(\mathcal{A})
is a
P^{+}
-ideal.

(3)

If
\mathcal{J}
is not a
P^{+}
-ideal, then there is a countable family
\mathcal{A}\subseteq\mathcal{P}(\omega)
such that
\omega\notin\mathcal{J}(\mathcal{A})
and
\mathrm{Fin}^{2}\leq_{\Kat}\mathcal{J}(\mathcal{A})
.

Proof.

(
1
)
Let
f:\omega\to\omega
be such that
f^{-1}[B]\in\mathcal{J}
for each
B\in\mathcal{I}
.
Let
\mathcal{K}=\{B:f^{-1}[B]\in\mathcal{J}\}
.
It is easy to see that
\mathcal{K}
is an ideal and
\mathcal{I}\subseteq\mathcal{K}
.
We show that
\mathcal{K}
is a
P^{+}
-ideal.
Let
(B_{n})\subseteq\mathcal{K}^{+}
and
B_{n}\supseteq B_{n+1}
for each
n
.
Then
f^{-1}[B_{n}]\in\mathcal{J}^{+}
and

f^{-1}[B_{n}]\supseteq f^{-1}[B_{n+1}]
for each
n
.
Since
\mathcal{J}
is a
P^{+}
-ideal, there is
A\notin\mathcal{J}
such that
A\setminus f^{-1}[B_{n}]
is finite for each
n
.
Then
B=f[A]\notin\mathcal{K}
and

B\setminus B_{n}
is finite for each
n
.

(
2
)
Let
\mathcal{A}=\{A_{n}:n\in\omega\}
be such that
\omega\notin\mathcal{J}(\mathcal{A})
.
Take any decreasing sequence
\{B_{n}:n\in\omega\}\subseteq\mathcal{J}(\mathcal{A})^{+}
and define
C_{0}=\omega
and
C_{n+1}=B_{n}\setminus\bigcup_{i<n}A_{i}
. Then
C_{n}\notin\mathcal{J}(\mathcal{A})
and
C_{n}\supseteq C_{n+1}
for each
n
.
Since
\mathcal{J}
is a
P^{+}
-ideal, there is
C^{\prime}\notin\mathcal{J}
such that
C^{\prime}\setminus C_{n}
is finite for each
n
. Define
C=C^{\prime}\cap C_{1}
and note that
C\notin\mathcal{J}
as
C^{\prime}\setminus C=C^{\prime}\setminus C_{1}
is finite.
Once we show that
C\notin\mathcal{J}(\mathcal{A})
, the proof will be finished.
Suppose that
C\in\mathcal{J}(\mathcal{A})
. Then there is
n
such that
C\setminus\bigcup_{i<n}A_{i}\in\mathcal{J}
.
Moreover,
C\cap\bigcup_{i<n}A_{i}
is finite since
C\cap\bigcup_{i<n}A_{i}\subseteq C\setminus C_{n+1}
.
Thus,
C\in\mathcal{J}
, a contradiction.

(
3
)
Since
\mathcal{J}
is not a
P^{+}
-ideal, there is a decreasing sequence
(B_{n})
of sets not belonging to
\mathcal{J}
such that if
X\setminus B_{n}\in\mathrm{Fin}
for all
n
, then
X\in\mathcal{J}
.
Let
A_{0}=\omega\setminus B_{0}
and
A_{n+1}=B_{n}\setminus B_{n+1}
for all
n
. Observe that
\bigcup_{i\leq n}A_{i}=\omega\setminus B_{n}\notin\mathcal{J}^{*}
, so
\omega\notin\mathcal{J}(\{A_{n}:n\in\omega\})
. What is more, any one-to-one function
f:\omega\to\omega^{2}
such that
f[A_{n}]\subseteq\{n\}\times\omega
witnesses
\mathrm{Fin}^{2}\leq_{\Kat}\mathcal{J}(\{A_{n}:n\in\omega\})
.
∎

Theorem 6.3
.

Let
\mathcal{J}
be an ideal on
\omega
.

(1)

The following conditions are equivalent.

(a)

\mathcal{J}
is extendable to a
P^{+}
-ideal.

(b)

\mathrm{Fin}^{2}\not\preceq_{\omega^{\omega},1-1}^{\omega_{1}}\mathcal{J}
.

(c)

\mathrm{Fin}^{2}\not\preceq_{\omega^{\omega},\mathrm{Fin}-1}^{\omega_{1}}\mathcal{J}
.

(d)

\mathrm{Fin}^{2}\not\preceq_{\omega^{\omega},\omega^{\omega}}^{\omega_{1}}\mathcal{J}
.

(2)

Under CH, the above conditions are equivalent to the following conditions.

(e)

There is a P-point which is not a
\mathcal{J}
-point.

(f)

There is a P-point which is not a weak
\mathcal{J}
-ultrafilter.

(g)

There is a P-point which is not a
\mathcal{J}
-ultrafilter.

(3)

If
\mathcal{J}
is Borel, then CH can be relaxed to the assumption
\mathfrak{p}=\mathfrak{c}
in (
2
).

(4)

If
\mathcal{J}
is Borel, then the following conditions are equivalent.

(a)

It is consistent that there exists a P-point which is not a
\mathcal{J}
-point (weak
\mathcal{J}
-ultrafilter or
\mathcal{J}
-ultrafilter, resp.).

(b)

Under CH, there exists a P-point which is not a
\mathcal{J}
-point (weak
\mathcal{J}
-ultrafilter or
\mathcal{J}
-ultrafilter, resp.).

In particular, if one can find a P-point which is not a
\mathcal{J}
-point (weak
\mathcal{J}
-ultrafilter or
\mathcal{J}
-ultrafilter, resp.) in some sophisticated model of ZFC, then one could do it already under CH.

Proof.

(
1a
)
\implies
(
1b
)
Let
\mathcal{J}^{\prime}\supseteq\mathcal{J}
and
\mathcal{J}^{\prime}
be a
P^{+}
-ideal.
By Lemma
6.2
(
2
),
\mathcal{J}^{\prime}(\mathcal{A})
is a
P^{+}
-ideal for each countable family
\mathcal{A}\subseteq\mathcal{P}(\omega)
such that
\omega\notin\mathcal{J}^{\prime}(\mathcal{A})
.
So
\mathrm{Fin}^{2}\not\leq_{\Kat}\mathcal{J}^{\prime}(\mathcal{A})
, by Proposition
2.3
(
3
).
Thus,
\mathrm{Fin}^{2}\not\preceq_{\omega^{\omega},1-1}^{\omega_{1}}\mathcal{J}
.

The implications (
1b
)
\implies
(
1c
)
and
(
1c
)
\implies
(
1d
)
are obvious.

(
1d
)
\implies
(
1a
)
Since
\mathrm{Fin}^{2}\not\preceq_{\omega^{\omega},\omega^{\omega}}^{\omega_{1}}\mathcal{J}
, there is an ideal
\mathcal{J}^{\prime}\geq_{\Kat}\mathcal{J}
such that
\mathrm{Fin}^{2}\not\leq_{\Kat}\mathcal{J}^{\prime}(\mathcal{A})
for each countable
\mathcal{A}\subseteq\mathcal{P}(\omega)
such that
\omega\notin\mathcal{J}^{\prime}(\mathcal{A})
.
By Lemma
6.2
(
3
),
\mathcal{J}^{\prime}
is a
P^{+}
-ideal, hence by Lemma
6.2
(
1
),
\mathcal{J}
can be extended to a
P^{+}
-ideal.

(
1b
)
\implies
(
2e
)
Under CH, it follows from Corollary
3.5
(
1
) and Theorem
2.4
.

The implications
(
2e
)
\implies
(
2f
)
and
(
2f
)
\implies
(
2g
)
are obvious (and hold in ZFC).

(
2g
)
\implies
(
1a
)
Take a P-point
\mathcal{U}
which is not a
\mathcal{J}
-ultrafilter (i.e.
\mathcal{J}\leq_{\Kat}\mathcal{U}^{*}
).
Then
\mathcal{U}^{*}
is a
P^{+}
-ideal, so
\mathcal{J}
can be extended to a
P^{+}
-ideal by Lemma
6.2
(
1
).
(Note that this argument works in ZFC.)

(
3
)
Taking into account that only the proof of the implication
“(
1b
)
\implies
(
2e
)”
was not done in ZFC, and (
1a
)
\iff
(
1b
) holds in ZFC,
we only need to show
the implication
“(
1a
)
\implies
(
2e
)”
under the assumption
\mathfrak{p}=\mathfrak{c}
with
\mathcal{J}
being Borel.

Assume that
\mathfrak{p}=\mathfrak{c}
and
\mathcal{J}
is a Borel ideal that can be extended to a
P^{+}
-ideal.
It is known that if a Borel ideal can be extended to a
P^{+}
-ideal, then it can be extended to an
F_{\sigma}
-ideal (see e.g.
[
33
, Theorem 3.2.7]
or
[
22
, Corollary 3.7]
).
Let
\widehat{\mathcal{J}}
be an
F_{\sigma}
ideal such that
\mathcal{J}\subseteq\widehat{\mathcal{J}}
.
Then
\widehat{\mathcal{J}}\restriction A
is also an
F_{\sigma}
ideal for each
A\notin\widehat{\mathcal{J}}
.
Thus,
\mathrm{Fin}^{2}\not\leq_{\Kat}\widehat{\mathcal{J}}\restriction A
for each
A\notin\widehat{\mathcal{J}}
by Proposition
2.3
(
3
).
By Theorems
4.1
,
3.6
and
2.9
,
\mathfrak{u}(\mathrm{Fin}^{2},\omega^{\omega},\widehat{\mathcal{J}},1-1)>\mathfrak{c}=|\omega^{\omega}|
.
By Theorem
3.4
, we obtain a
\mathrm{Fin}^{2}
-ultrafilter (hence a P-point by Theorem
2.4
(
1
)) which is not a
\widehat{\mathcal{J}}
-point (so it is not a
\mathcal{J}
-point as well).

(
4
)
The implication “(
4b
)
\implies
(
4a
)” is obvious, so below we show only the implication “(
4a
)
\implies
(
4b
)”.
Assume that it is consistent that there exists a P-point that is not a
\mathcal{J}
-point (weak
\mathcal{J}
-ultrafilter or
\mathcal{J}
-ultrafilter, resp.).
Then consistently
\mathfrak{u}(\mathrm{Fin}^{2},\omega^{\omega},\mathcal{J},1-1)=\mathfrak{c}^{+}
(
\mathfrak{u}(\mathrm{Fin}^{2},\omega^{\omega},\mathcal{J},Fin-1)=\mathfrak{c}^{+}
or
\mathfrak{u}(\mathrm{Fin}^{2},\omega^{\omega},\mathcal{J},\omega^{\omega})=\mathfrak{c}^{+}
, resp.) by Theorem
3.4
, so in particular it is consistent that
\mathrm{Fin}^{2}\not\preceq_{\omega^{\omega},1-1}^{\omega_{1}}\mathcal{J}
(
\mathrm{Fin}^{2}\not\preceq_{\omega^{\omega},Fin-1}^{\omega_{1}}\mathcal{J}
or
\mathrm{Fin}^{2}\not\preceq_{\omega^{\omega},\omega^{\omega}}^{\omega_{1}}\mathcal{J}
, resp.).
Then, by item (
1
), it is consistent that
\mathcal{J}
can be extended to a
P^{+}
-ideal, and since
\mathcal{J}
is Borel,
\mathcal{J}
can be extended to an
F_{\sigma}
-ideal (by
[
33
, Theorem 3.2.7]
or
[
22
, Corollary 3.7]
).
However, if
\mathcal{J}
is a Borel ideal, then the sentence “
\mathcal{J}
can be extended to an
F_{\sigma}
-ideal” is absolute, so
\mathcal{J}
can be extended to an
F_{\sigma}
ideal in ZFC.
Then, using (
2
) we obtain, under CH,
that there exists a P-point which is not a
\mathcal{J}
-point (weak
\mathcal{J}
-ultrafilter or
\mathcal{J}
-ultrafilter, resp.).
∎

An ideal
\mathcal{I}
has the
property
\FinBW
(in short:
\mathcal{I}\in\FinBW
) if for each bounded sequence
(x_{n})_{n\in\omega}
of reals there is
A\notin\mathcal{I}
such that the subsequence
(x_{n})_{n\in A}
is convergent (
[
14
]
).

The ideal

\mathcal{I}_{d}=\{A\subseteq\omega:\lim_{n\to\infty}|A\cap n|/n=0\}
of asymptotic density zero sets,
the ideal

\mathcal{I}_{u}=\{A\subseteq\omega:\lim_{n\to\infty}\max\{|A\cap\{m,m+1,\ldots,m+n-1\}|:m\in\omega\}/n=0\}
of Banach (a.k.a. uniform) density zero sets, the ideal
\mathrm{nwd}
, the ideal
\mathrm{conv}
and the ideal
\mathcal{L}

generated by lacunary sets (recall: a set
A\subseteq\omega
is
lacunary
(
[
12
]
) if
\lim_{n\to\infty}(a_{n+1}-a_{n})=\infty
, where
(a_{n})_{n\in\omega}
is the increasing enumeration of all elements of
A
)
do not have the
\FinBW
property (see
[
21
, Example 3]
,
[
1
, Corollary 1]
,
[
14
, Example 3.1]
,
[
2
, Proposition 6.4]
and
[
28
, Lemma 3.2]
, respectively).

Proposition 6.4
.

If
\mathcal{J}\notin\FinBW
, then every P-point is a
\mathcal{J}
-ultrafilter.
In particular, every P-point is an
\mathcal{I}_{d}
-ultrafilter,
\mathcal{I}_{u}
-ultrafilter,
\mathrm{nwd}
-ultrafilter,
\mathrm{conv}
-ultrafilter and
\mathcal{L}
-ultrafilter (note that the cases of the ideals
\mathcal{I}_{d}
and
\mathcal{L}
were earlier done in a different manner in
[
18
, Proposition 2.3.3]
).

Proof.

Let
\mathcal{U}
be a P-point and suppose that
\mathcal{U}
is not a
\mathcal{J}
-ultrafilter i.e.
\mathcal{J}\leq_{\Kat}\mathcal{U}^{*}
.
Since
\mathcal{J}\notin\FinBW
, then
\mathrm{conv}\leq_{\Kat}\mathcal{J}
(
[
33
, Section 2.7]
, see also
[
2
, Proposition 6.4]
).
Consequently,
\mathrm{conv}\leq_{\Kat}\mathcal{U}^{*}
, so
\mathcal{U}
is not a P-point by Theorem
2.4
(
1
), a contradiction. ∎

It is known (
[
14
, Proposition 4.1 and 3.4]
) that
\mathcal{J}\in\FinBW
for every ideal
\mathcal{J}
which can be extended to an
F_{\sigma}
ideal. In the realm of analytic P-ideals,
\mathcal{J}\in\FinBW
is even equivalent to extendability of
\mathcal{J}
to an
F_{\sigma}
ideal (
[
2
, Proposition 6.5]
).
It was a question of Hrušak (
[
23
, Question 5.16]
) whether the same holds in the realm of all Borel ideals, but the answer to his question is negative as was recently showed by Kwela (
[
30
]
).

Theorem 6.5
.

If
\mathcal{J}
is an
F_{\sigma}
ideal and
\mathcal{I}
is not a weak P-ideal, then

\cov(\mathcal{M})\leq\wsp(\mathcal{I},\mathcal{J},\mathcal{F})

for every
\mathcal{F}\subseteq\omega^{\omega}
.

Proof.

Let
\phi
be a lsc submeasure such that
\mathcal{J}=\mathrm{Fin}(\phi)
.
Let
\mathcal{A}
be a family having
\mathcal{J}^{+}
-FIP and such that
|\mathcal{A}|<\cov(\mathcal{M})
.
Fix
f\in\mathcal{F}
.

Since
\mathcal{I}
is not a weak P-ideal, there is a partition
\{B_{n}:n\in\omega\}\subseteq\mathcal{I}
of
\omega
such that for each
B\notin\mathcal{I}
there is
n
with
|B_{n}\cap B|=\omega
.
For each
n\in\omega
, we define
C_{n}=f^{-1}[B_{n}]
.
We have 2 cases.

Case 1.
There is
n\in\omega
such that
\bigcap F\cap C_{n}\notin\mathcal{J}
for each finite
F\subseteq\mathcal{A}
.
Then, we put
C=B_{n}
.
Since
C\in\mathcal{I}
and
\mathcal{A}\cup\{f^{-1}[C]\}
has
\mathcal{J}^{+}
-FIP, the proof is finished in this case.

Case 2.
For each
n\in\omega

there exists a finite
F\subseteq\mathcal{A}
with

\bigcap F\cap C_{n}\in\mathcal{J}
.

We define a poset
(\mathbb{P},\leq)
as follows:

\mathbb{P}=\left\{(H,n)\in[\omega]^{<\omega}\times\omega:H\subseteq\bigcup_{i<n}C_{i}\right\}

and
(H_{1},n_{1})\leq(H_{2},n_{2})\iff

(1)

H_{1}\supseteq H_{2}
,

(2)

n_{1}\geq n_{2}
,

(3)

\max H_{2}<\min(H_{1}\setminus H_{2})
[here
\max\emptyset=-1
and
\min\emptyset=\omega
by convention],

(4)

(H_{1}\setminus H_{2})\cap\bigcup_{i<n_{2}}C_{i}=\emptyset
.

Now we define sets

(1)

D_{F,N}=\{(H,n)\in\mathbb{P}:\phi(\bigcap F\cap H)>N\}
for each finite
F\subseteq\mathcal{A}
and
N\in\omega
,

(2)

E_{k}=\{(H,n)\in\mathbb{P}:n>k\}
for each
k\in\omega

and below show that these sets are dense in
(\mathbb{P},\leq)
.

First we show that sets
D_{F,N}
are dense.
Take any
(H,n)\in\mathbb{P}
.
In the present case, we know that for each
i<n
there is a finite
F_{i}\subseteq\mathcal{A}
with
\bigcap F_{i}\cap C_{i}\in\mathcal{J}
.
Since
\mathcal{A}
has
\mathcal{J}^{+}
-FIP,
X=\bigcap_{i<n}\bigcap F_{i}\notin\mathcal{J}
.
Consequently, there is a finite set
H^{\prime}\subseteq X\setminus(\bigcup_{i<n}C_{i}\cup\max H)

with
\phi(H^{\prime})>N
.
We put
G=H\cup H^{\prime}

and
m=\max(\{i\in\omega:G\cap C_{i}\neq\emptyset\}\cup\{n\})
.
Since
(G,m)\in D_{F,N}
and
(G,m)\leq(H,n)
, we obtain denseness of
D_{F,N}
.

Now we show that
E_{k}
are dense.
Take any
(H,n)\in\mathbb{P}
.
Let
G=H
and
m=\max(n,k+1)
.
Since
(G,m)\in E_{k}
and
(G,m)\leq(H,n)
, we obtain denseness of
E_{k}
.

Let
\mathcal{D}
be the family of all the sets of the form
D_{F,N}
and
E_{k}
.

Since
\mathbb{P}
is countable and
|\mathcal{D}|<\cov(\mathcal{M})
, there is a filter

\mathcal{G}\subseteq\mathbb{P}
such that
\mathcal{G}\cap D\neq\emptyset
for each
D\in\mathcal{D}
(see e.g.
[
6
, Theorem 7.13]
)).

We define

B=f[A],\text{\ \ where\ \ }A=\bigcup\{H:(H,n)\in\mathcal{G}\text{ for some $n\in\omega$}\}.

If we show that
B\in\mathcal{I}
and
\mathcal{A}\cup\{f^{-1}[B]\}
has
\mathcal{J}^{+}
-FIP, the proof will be finished.

First we show that
\mathcal{A}\cup\{f^{-1}[B]\}
has
\mathcal{J}^{+}
-FIP.
Take any finite set
F\subseteq\mathcal{A}
.
Let
N\in\omega
.
Since
\mathcal{G}
is
\mathcal{D}
-generic, there is
(H,n)\in\mathcal{G}\cap D_{F,N}
.
In particular,
H\subseteq A
and
\phi(\bigcap F\cap H)>N
.
Since
A\subseteq f^{-1}[B]
, we obtain
\phi(\bigcap F\cap f^{-1}[B])>N
.
Consequently,
\phi(\bigcap F\cap f^{-1}[B])=\infty
, so
\bigcap F\cap f^{-1}[B]\notin\mathcal{J}
.

Finally, we show that
B\in\mathcal{I}
.
If we show that
A\cap C_{k}
is finite for each
k
, then
B\cap B_{k}
will be finite for each
k
, and consequently
B\in\mathcal{I}
.

Take any
k\in\omega
.
Since
\mathcal{G}
is
\mathcal{D}
-generic, there is
(H,n)\in\mathcal{G}\cap E_{k}
.
We claim that
A\cap C_{k}\subseteq H
(in particular, it means that
A\cap C_{k}
is finite).

Take any
a\in A\cap C_{k}
.
Then there is
(G,m)\in\mathcal{G}
such that
a\in G
.
Since
\mathcal{G}
is a filter, there is
(K,l)\in\mathcal{G}
such that
(K,l)\leq(H,n)
and
(K,l)\leq(G,m)
.
We have 2 cases.

Case 1.
Assume that
m\leq k
.
Since
(G,m)\in\mathbb{P}
,
G\subseteq\bigcup_{i<m}C_{i}
, and consequently,
a\notin C_{k}
, a contradiction, so this case is not possible.

Case 2.
Assume that
m>k
.
Since
(K,l)\leq(H,n)
,
(K\setminus H)\cap\bigcup_{i<n}C_{i}=\emptyset
.
Since
(H,n)\in E_{k}
,
n>k
. Consequently,
(K\setminus H)\cap C_{k}=\emptyset
.
Suppose, in the sake of contradiction, that
a\notin H
.
Since
a\in G\subseteq K
,
a\in K\setminus H
.
Since
a\in C_{k}
,

Lemma 9.2
.

Let
\mathcal{I}
and
\mathcal{J}
be ideals on
\omega
.

(1)

If
\mathcal{I}\leq_{\Kat}\mathcal{J}
and
\mathcal{J}
is a selective ideal, then
\mathcal{I}
can be extended to a selective ideal.

(2)

If
\mathcal{J}
is a selective ideal and
\mathcal{A}\subseteq\mathcal{P}(\omega)
is a countable family such that
\omega\notin\mathcal{J}(\mathcal{A})
, then
\mathcal{J}(\mathcal{A})
is a selective ideal.

(3)

If
\mathcal{J}
is not a selective ideal, then there is a countable family
\mathcal{A}\subseteq\mathcal{P}(\omega)
such that
\omega\notin\mathcal{J}(\mathcal{A})
and
\mathcal{ED}\leq_{\Kat}\mathcal{J}(\mathcal{A})
.

Proof.

(
1
)
Let
f:\omega\to\omega
be such that
f^{-1}[B]\in\mathcal{J}
for each
B\in\mathcal{I}
.
Let
\mathcal{K}=\{B:f^{-1}[B]\in\mathcal{J}\}
.
It is easy to see that
\mathcal{K}
is an ideal and
\mathcal{I}\subseteq\mathcal{K}
.
We show that
\mathcal{K}
is a selective ideal.
Let
(B_{n})\subseteq\mathcal{K}^{+}
be a decreasing sequence.
Then
f^{-1}[B_{n}]\in\mathcal{J}^{+}
and
f^{-1}[B_{n}]\supseteq f^{-1}[B_{n+1}]
for each
n
.
Since
\mathcal{J}
is a selective ideal, there is
A\in\mathcal{J}^{+}
such that
|A\cap(f^{-1}[B_{n}]\setminus f^{-1}[B_{n+1}])|\leq 1
for each
n
.
Then
B=f[A]\in\mathcal{K}^{+}
and

|B\cap(B_{n}\setminus B_{n+1})|\leq 1
for each
n
.

(
2
)
Let
\mathcal{A}=\{A_{n}:n\in\omega\}
be such that
\omega\notin\mathcal{J}(\mathcal{A})
.
Take any decreasing sequence
B_{n}\in\mathcal{J}(\mathcal{A})^{+}
.
Let
C_{0}=\omega
and
C_{n+1}=B_{n}\setminus\bigcup_{i<n}A_{i}
.
Then
C_{n}\in\mathcal{J}(\mathcal{A})^{+}
and
C_{n}\supseteq C_{n+1}
for each
n
.
Since
C_{n}\in\mathcal{J}^{+}
and
\mathcal{J}
is a selective ideal, there is
C^{\prime}\in\mathcal{J}^{+}
such that

|C^{\prime}\cap(C_{n}\setminus C_{n+1})|\leq 1

for each
n
.
Define
C=C^{\prime}\cap C_{1}
. Then
C\in\mathcal{J}^{+}
as
|C^{\prime}\setminus C|=|C^{\prime}\cap(C_{0}\setminus C_{1})|\leq 1
.
Once we show that
C\in\mathcal{J}(\mathcal{A})^{+}
, the proof will be finished.
Suppose that
C\in\mathcal{J}(\mathcal{A})
. Then there is
n
such that
C\setminus\bigcup_{i<n}A_{i}\in\mathcal{J}
.
But
C\cap\bigcup_{i<n}A_{i}
is finite (it has at most
n
elements), so
C\in\mathcal{J}
, a contradiction.

(
3
)
Since
\mathcal{J}
is not a selective ideal, there is a decreasing sequence
B_{n}\in\mathcal{J}^{+}
such that if
|X\cap(B_{n}\setminus B_{n+1})|\leq 1
for all
n
then
X\in\mathcal{J}
.
Let
A_{0}=\omega\setminus B_{0}
and
A_{n+1}=B_{n}\setminus B_{n+1}
for all
n
. Observe that
\bigcup_{i\leq n}A_{i}=\omega\setminus B_{n}\notin\mathcal{J}^{*}
, so
\omega\notin\mathcal{J}(\{A_{n}:n\in\omega\})
. What is more, any one-to-one function
f:\omega\to\omega^{2}
such that
f[A_{n}]\subseteq\{n\}\times\omega
witnesses
\mathcal{ED}\leq_{\Kat}\mathcal{J}(\{A_{n}:n\in\omega\})
.
∎

Theorem 9.3
.

Let
\mathcal{J}
be an ideal on
\omega
.

(1)

The following conditions are equivalent.

(a)

\mathcal{J}
is extendable to a selective ideal.

(b)

\mathcal{ED}\not\preceq_{\omega^{\omega},1-1}^{\omega_{1}}\mathcal{J}
.

(c)

\mathcal{ED}\not\preceq_{\omega^{\omega},\mathrm{Fin}-1}^{\omega_{1}}\mathcal{J}

Proposition 10.1
.

Any
G_{\delta\sigma}
-ideal is extendable to a
F_{\sigma}
-ideal.

Proof.

Let
\mathcal{J}
be a
G_{\delta\sigma}
-ideal. We will show that
\mathrm{Fin}^{2}\not\preceq^{\omega_{1}}_{\omega^{\omega},1-1}\mathcal{J}
. By Theorem
6.3
, this will finish the proof, because for Borel ideals extendability to
P^{+}
-ideals is equivalent to extendability to
F_{\sigma}
-ideals (see
[
33
, Theorem 3.2.7]
). We claim that
\mathcal{J}^{\prime}=\mathcal{J}
witnesses
\mathrm{Fin}^{2}\not\preceq^{\omega_{1}}_{\omega^{\omega},1-1}\mathcal{J}
. Let
\mathcal{A}=\{A_{i}:\ i\in\omega\}\subseteq\mathcal{P}(\omega)
be such that
|\mathcal{A}|=\omega
and
\omega\notin\mathcal{J}(\mathcal{A})
. Observe that

\mathcal{J}(\mathcal{A})=\left\{B\subseteq\omega:\exists n\in\omega(B\setminus(A_{0}\cup\ldots\cup A_{n})\in\mathcal{J})\right\}=\bigcup_{n\in\omega}\phi_{n}^{-1}[\mathcal{J}],

where
\phi_{n}:\mathcal{P}(\omega)\to\mathcal{P}(\omega)
is given by
\phi_{n}(B)=B\setminus(A_{0}\cup\ldots\cup A_{n})
. It is easy to check that each
\phi_{n}
is continuous. Thus,
\mathcal{J}(\mathcal{A})
is
G_{\delta\sigma}
. Since the ideal
\mathcal{J}(\mathcal{A})
is
\Pi_{4}^{0}
, its rank is less than or equal to
1
(by
[
13
, Theorem 9.1]
; see
[
13
, Definition 3.1]
for the definition of rank of an analytic ideal). Hence, using
[
13
, Theorem 7.5]
we get that
\mathrm{Fin}^{2}\not\sqsubseteq\mathcal{J}(\mathcal{A})
. Thus,
\mathrm{Fin}^{2}\not\leq_{\Kat}\mathcal{J}(\mathcal{A})
by
[
2
, Example 4.1]
.
∎

A set
A\subseteq\omega
is
lacunary
(
[
12
]
) if
\lim_{n\to\infty}(a_{n+1}-a_{n})=\infty
, where
(a_{n})_{n\in\omega}
is the increasing enumeration of all elements of
A
. The ideal
\mathcal{L}
generated by lacunary sets was examined for instance in

]

Proposition 10.2
.

The ideal
\mathcal{L}
is a
P^{+}(\mathcal{L})
-ideal.

Proof.

We will use the following characterization (
[
18
, Lemma 1.2.7]
):

A\notin\mathcal{L}\iff\forall_{n\in\omega}\exists_{d\in\omega}\forall_{K\in\omega}\exists_{k\geq K}\,(|A\cap[k,k+d]|\geq n).

Let
A_{n}\notin\mathcal{L}
and
A_{n}\supseteq A_{n+1}
for each
n\in\omega
.
Using the above characterization for each set
A_{n}
separately, we find
d_{n}\in\omega
such that for each
K\in\omega
there is
k\geqslant K
with
|A_{n}\cap[k,k+d_{n}]|\geq n
.
Then, for each
n
, we choose an increasing sequence
k^{n}_{0}<k^{n}_{1}<\dots
such that

\{k^{n}_{i}:i\in\omega\}\in\mathcal{L}

and

|A_{n}\cap[k^{n}_{i},k^{n}_{i}+d_{n}]|\geq n
for each
i
.
Finally, we define

A=\bigcup_{n\in\omega}\left(\bigcup_{i\in\omega}A_{n}\cap[k^{n}_{i},k^{n}_{i}+d_{n}]\right).

Using the above mentioned characterization, it is easy to see that
A\notin\mathcal{L}
.

Below, we show that
A\setminus A_{n}\in\mathcal{L}
for each
n
.

First, we observe that for each
n
the set

B_{n}=\bigcup_{i\in\omega}A_{n}\cap[k^{n}_{i},k^{n}_{i}+d_{n}]

is a union of
d_{n}+1
lacunary sets, so
B_{n}\in\mathcal{L}
.
Then, taking into account that
A_{0}\supseteq A_{1}\supseteq\dots
, we notice that

A\setminus A_{n}\subseteq\bigcup_{i<n}B_{i}\in\mathcal{L}
.
∎

1.
Introduction

The moduli space

{\cal M}_{0,n}=[(\mathbb{P}^{1})^{n}-\Delta]/\mathrm{Aut}(\mathbb{P}^{1}),\quad\Delta=\bigcup_{i\neq j}\{p_{i}=p_{j}\}

of
n
distinct points
p_{1},\cdots,p_{n}
on projective line
\mathbb{P}^{1}
up to projective equivalence is one of the most studied objects in algebraic geometry since the nineteenth century and its compactification
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{0,n}
by Deligne-Mumford
[
7
]
has played a major role in enumerative algebraic geometry and related areas.
The symmetric group acts on
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{0,n}
by permuting the marked points and hence on the cohomology
H^{*}(\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{0,n})
.
Thanks to
[
1
,
12
,
15
,
17
,
18
,
19
,
25
,
29
]
to name a few, a lot is known about
H^{*}(\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{0,n})
but we still cannot answer simple questions like

Question 1.1
.

(1)

Can we give a closed formula for the character of the
S_{n}
-representation on
H^{2k}(\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{0,n})
for each
k
?

(2)

For each
k
, is
H^{2k}(\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{0,n})
a permutation representation of
S_{n}
? (
[
29
, Question 1]
.)

Here a representation
\phi:S_{n}\to\mathrm{GL}_{N}(\mathbb{C})
is called a permutation representation if
\phi
factors through the subgroup
S_{N}\subset\mathrm{GL}_{N}(\mathbb{C})
of permutation matrices.

The purpose of this paper is to provide answers to the above questions through a new inductive construction of the moduli space
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{0,n}
motivated by our investigation
[
6
]
on the Landau-Ginzburg/Calabi-Yau correspondence.
More precisely,

•

(Theorem
6.1
) we provide a closed formula for the
S_{n}
-character on
H^{*}(\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{0,n})
;

•

(Corollary
6.2
) we prove that
H^{2k}(\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{0,n})
is a permutation representation for
k\leq 3
or
k\geq n-6
;

•

(Theorem
7.1
) we also prove that
H^{2k}(\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{0,n})\oplus H^{2k-2}(\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{0,n})
is a permutation representation for each
k
.

In principle, our closed formula should enable us to answer Question
1.1
(2) in full generality but the combinatorics seems too complicated to work out for the moment.
Note that the
S_{n}
-representation on
H^{2k}(\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{0,n})
was proved to be a permutation representation for
k=1
or
k=n-4
by Farkas-Gibney
[

]
and for
k=2
or
k=n-5
by Ramadas-Silversmith
[
29
]
through completely different methods.

We remark that our method works for other moduli spaces including the Fulton-MacPherson compactification (cf.
[
11
]
)

\mathbb{P}^{1}[n]=\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{0,n}(\mathbb{P}^{1},1)

and enables us to provide a closed formula (Theorem

) for the character of the
S_{n}
-representation on the cohomology of
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{0,n}(\mathbb{P}^{m-1},1)
for any
m\geq 2
.

In this paper, all varieties and schemes are defined over the complex number field
\mathbb{C}
and we will use rational coefficients

for cohomology groups.

1.1.
Cohomology of
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{0,n}

Let us recall previously known results about the cohomology of
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{0,n}
.

An
n
-pointed stable curve of genus 0 is a connected projective curve
C
of arithmetic genus 0 with at worst nodal singularities together with
n
distinct smooth points
p_{1},\cdots,p_{n}
such that there is no nontrivial automorphism of
C
preserving the marked points
p_{i}
(cf.
[
7
]
).
For convenience, we will often write
C
instead of
(C,p_{1},\cdots,p_{n})
when the meaning is clear from the context.
The moduli functor of
n
-pointed stable curves of genus
0
is represented by a smooth projective variety of dimension
n-3
and an explicit inductive construction was first provided by Knudsen
in
[
22
]
in 1983.

Motivated by enumerative geometry and string theory, the cohomology ring of
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{0,n}
was investigated in 1990s by several authors. In
[
25
]
, Manin used the virtual Poincaré polynomial coming from Deligne’s mixed Hodge structure to give an inductive formula for the Betti numbers of the cohomology
H^{*}(\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{0,n})
.
In
[
18
]
, Keel discovered an alternative inductive construction of
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{0,n+1}
by applying a sequence of blowups to
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{0,n}\times\mathbb{P}^{1}
along smooth subvarieties.
By the blowup formulas
[
14
, p.605]
and
[
10
, Theorem 6.7]
, he proved that
the cycle class map

A^{*}(\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{0,n})\longrightarrow H^{*}(\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{0,n})

from the Chow group to the cohomology is an isomorphism and
H^{*}(\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{0,n})
is generated by boundary divisors with only some evident relations like WDVV.
In particular, there is no odd degree cohomology part and the cohomology group
H^{2k}(\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{0,n})
coincides with the Chow group
A^{k}(\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{0,n})
.

As mentioned above, the symmetric group
S_{n}
acts on
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{0,n}
by permuting the marked points
p_{1},\cdots,p_{n}
. In fact, there are no other automorphisms of
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{0,n}
for
n\geq 5
by
[
2
]
.
Thus it is natural to think of the cohomology of
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{0,n}
as an
S_{n}
-representation. Unfortunately, Keel’s construction consists of morphisms and blowups which are not
S_{n}
-equivariant, and hence it cannot be used to compute the representations on the cohomology of
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{0,n}
.

In
[
5
]
, Chen, Gibney and Krashen studied a different but similar construction of
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{0,n+1}
by applying a sequence of blowups to a projective bundle over
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{0,n}
.

[
5
]
contains a similar construction.
See
[
5
, Theorem 3.3.1 and Proposition 3.4.3]
. However, their construction is not
S_{n}
-equivariant although obviously
S_{n-1}
-equivariant. This is basically because their interpretation detects only an
S_{n-1}
-action on
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{0,n}
which fixes one marked point, called the
root
in their terminology (
[
5
, Definition 2.0.1]
). See Appendix
A
for details.

In
[
12
]
, Getzler gave an algorithm for the characters of
H^{*}(\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{0,n})
using the language of operads, and managed to compute them for
n\leq 6
.
In 1994, Kapranov discovered a new construction of
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{0,n}
by a sequence of blowups starting with
\mathbb{P}^{n-3}
. In fact, after fixing
n-1
general points
q_{1},\cdots,q_{n-1}
in
\mathbb{P}^{n-3}
, we blow up
\mathbb{P}^{n-3}
along the
n-1
points
\{q_{i}\}_{1\leq i<n}
, along the proper transforms of the
\binom{n-1}{2}
lines
\{\overline{q_{i}q_{j}}\}
, along the proper transforms of
\binom{n-1}{3}
planes
\{\overline{q_{i}q_{j}q_{k}}\}
, and so on. After these
n-2
blowups, we end up with
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{0,n}
. For each permutation of the points
q_{1},\cdots,q_{n-1}
, there is a unique projective transformation of
\mathbb{P}^{n-3}
which extends the permutation and hence we can think of
S_{n-1}
as a subgroup of
\mathrm{PGL}_{n-2}(\mathbb{C})
. Obviously, the blowup centers are invariant with respect to this subgroup
S_{n-1}
and the induced action of
S_{n-1}
on
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{0,n}
coincides with the action of the subgroup
S_{n-1}
of
S_{n}
permuting
\{2,\cdots,n\}
with
1
fixed.
By applying the blowup formula, we can calculate the cohomology
H^{*}(\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{0,n})
as an
S_{n-1}
-representation.
This calculation will be explicitly worked out in §
5
.

In 2003, Hassett introduced in
[
15
]
the notion of weighted pointed stable curves and showed that Kapranov’s blowups are actually the natural morphisms among a special type of moduli spaces of weighted pointed stable curves of genus 0. Moreover the blowup centers are also the moduli spaces of suitable weighted pointed stable curves of genus 0.
Using Hassett’s moduli spaces and morphisms, Bergström and Minabe in
[
1
]

gave a recursive algorithm to compute the characters of the
S_{n}
-representations
H^{*}(\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{0,n})
and managed to compute them for
n\leq 20
(cf.
[
29
]
).
The algorithm starts with a result of the second named author and Moon in
[
19
]
that factorizes the natural morphism

\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{0,n}\longrightarrow(\mathbb{P}^{1})^{n}/\!/\mathrm{PGL}_{2}(\mathbb{C})

to the geometric invariant theory (GIT) quotient into a sequence of explicit blowups.
The algorithm uses the blowup formula and pushes the computation little by little to
\overline{{\cal M}}_{0,k}
with
k<n
.
However, no algorithms known so far have provided us with a closed formula for the characters of
the
S_{n}
-representation
H^{*}(\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{0,n})
.
The first goal of this paper is to provide a closed formula for these characters
(Theorem
6.1
). Our result answers Question
1.1
(1) positively.

Recall that an
S_{n}
-representation
V
is called a
permutation representation
if it has a basis
B
(called a
permutation basis
) such that an
S_{n}
-action on
B
induces the
S_{n}
-action on
V
.
An interesting question related to
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{0,n}
is whether the cohomology group
A^{k}(\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{0,n})=H^{2k}(\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{0,n})
is a permutation representation or not for each
k
(Question
1.1
(2)).
Here is some evidence on why the answer to this question may be yes.

(1)

A recent work
[
4
]
of Castravet and Tevelev which is again based on
[
19
]
tells us that the derived category of
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{0,n}
admits a full exceptional collection which is invariant under the action of
S_{n}
.
Hence the Grothendieck group of coherent sheaves on
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{0,n}
is a permutation representation of
S_{n}
and so is the total cohomology

\oplus_{k=0}^{n-3}A^{k}(\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{0,n})
by Grothendieck-Riemann-Roch (GRR).
However, as GRR mixes the degrees, it does not imply that
A^{k}(\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{0,n})
itself is a permutation representation for each
k
.

(2)

For
k=1
(and hence for
k=n-4
by Poincaré duality), Farkas and Gibney
[
9
, Lemma 2]
found a permutation basis for the Picard group of
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{0,n}
. Ramadas
[
28
]
found another permutation basis for
A^{1}(\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{0,n})
which is not isomorphic to that in
[
9
]
.

(3)

Ramadas and Silversmith in
[
29
]
extended the technique of
[
28
]
to give a permutation basis for
A^{2}(\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{0,n})
.

(4)

Ramadas and Silversmith checked that
A^{k}(\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{0,n})
is a permutation representation for
n\leq 10
and for all
k
by using (unpublished) computational data of Bergström-Minabe.

The second goal of this paper is to answer Question
1.1
(2) as much as we can.
Using the closed formula (Theorem
6.1
), we prove (Corollary
6.2
) that

(1.1)

A^{k}(\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{0,n})\text{ is a permutation representation of }S_{n}\text{ for }k\leq 3\text{ or }k\geq n-6.

Our method is completely different from known approaches even for
k=1,2
. Furthermore, we prove (Theorem
7.1
) that

(1.2)

A^{k}(\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{0,n})\oplus A^{k-1}(\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{0,n})\text{ is a permutation representation of }S_{n}\text{ for all }k\text{ and }n.

We prove the above by a new inductive construction of
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{0,n}
which arises from
the wall crossings of the moduli spaces of
\delta
-stable quasimaps developed in
[
6
]
.
Our method works well with other interesting moduli spaces like the stable map space
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{0,n}(\mathbb{P}^{m-1},1)
by simply considering more sections for quasimaps (Theorem
6.7
).

1.2.
Strategy of the proofs

Recall that Kapranov’s construction of
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{0,n+1}
by applying a sequence of blowups to
\mathbb{P}^{n-2}
is
S_{n}
-equivariant. The blowup centers are also obtained by applying sequences of blowups to projective spaces of lower dimensions. By the blowup formula (Lemma
5.1
), we can calculate the
S_{n}
-representation on
H^{*}(\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{0,n+1})=A^{*}(\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{0,n+1})
.
The combinatorics of the blowups is handled by
weighted rooted trees
(Definition
5.9
) which are trees with certain decorations. Letting

(1.3)

Q_{n}=\sum_{k}\mathrm{ch}_{n}(A^{k}(\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{0,n+1}))t^{k}

where
\mathrm{ch}_{n}
denotes the character of
S_{n}
, we will show (Proposition
5.12
) that

(1.4)

Q_{n}=\sum_{k\geq 0}\sum_{T\in\mathscr{T}_{n,k}/S_{n}}\mathrm{ch}_{n}(U_{T})t^{k}

where
\mathscr{T}_{n,k}
denotes the set of weighted rooted trees with
n
inputs and weight
k
while

U_{T}=\mathrm{Ind}^{S_{n}}_{\mathrm{Stab}(T)}e

is the induced
S_{n}
-representation from the trivial representation of
\mathrm{Stab}(T)
.

Now our goal is to find

(1.5)

P_{n}=\sum_{k}\mathrm{ch}_{n}(A^{k}(\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{0,n}))t^{k}

from (
1.4
). So we want to compare
A^{*}(\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{0,n})
with
A^{*}(\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{0,n+1})
as
S_{n}
-representation spaces.
For this, we use a new inductive construction of
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{0,n}
which arises from the wall crossings of moduli spaces of
\delta
-stable quasimaps in
[
6
]

To build
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{0,n+1}
from
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{0,n}
, we should add one extra point to a stable curve
(C,p_{1},\cdots,p_{n})
with
n
marked points. If we think of a point on
C
as a pair
(L,s)
of a line bundle
L
of degree
1
on
C
and a nonzero section
s
of
L
, we gain freedom to choose a stability condition for the pair
(L,s)
. Le Potier in
[
23
]
discovered that there is a sequence of stability conditions for such a pair over a smooth projective curve and Huybrechts-Lehn in
[
16
]
generalized Le Potier’s stability conditions and moduli spaces to arbitrary projective schemes.
In
[
6
]
, the construction was further extended to the moduli spaces of triples
(C,L,s)
where
C
is now allowed to vary. See §
2
for details.

As a consequence, we have a sequence of
S_{n}
-equivariant blowups (Theorem
2.8
)

(1.6)

\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{0,n+1}\cong\mathfrak{Q}^{\delta=\infty}\xrightarrow{~\mathfrak{q}_{2}~}\cdots\xrightarrow{~\mathfrak{q}_{\ell}~}\mathfrak{Q}^{\delta=0^{+}}

whose blowup centers are disjoint unions of
\binom{n}{h}
copies of

\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{0,h+1}\times\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{0,n-h+1},\quad 2\leq h\leq\ell=\lfloor\frac{n-1}{2}\rfloor.

Using this, by the blowup formula, we will show (Proposition
4.1
) that

(1.7)

Q_{n}=P_{\mathfrak{Q}^{\delta=0^{+}}}+t\sum^{\ell}_{h=2}Q_{h}Q_{n-h}

where
P_{\mathfrak{Q}^{\delta=0^{+}}}
denotes the character polynomial of the moduli space
\mathfrak{Q}^{\delta=0^{+}}
of
\delta=0^{+}
-stable quasimaps.
Together with (
1.4
), (
1.7
) gives us a closed formula for
P_{\mathfrak{Q}^{\delta=0^{+}}}
.

The last moduli space
\mathfrak{Q}^{\delta=0^{+}}
in (
1.6
) is a
\mathbb{P}^{1}
-bundle over
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{0,n}
when
n
is odd.
When
n
is even,
\mathfrak{Q}^{\delta=0^{+}}
is the blowup of a
\mathbb{P}^{1}
-bundle over
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{0,n}
along
\frac{1}{2}\binom{n}{\ell+1}
disjoint copies of
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{0,\ell+2}\times\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{0,\ell+2}
.
We will show (Corollary
4.7
) that

(1.8)

P_{\mathfrak{Q}^{\delta=0^{+}}}=(1+t)P_{n}+ts_{(1,1)}\circ Q_{\frac{n}{2}}

where
Q_{\frac{n}{2}}
is defined to be zero if
n
is odd.
Here
s_{(1,1)}\circ Q_{\frac{n}{2}}
denotes the antisymmetric part in
Q_{\frac{n}{2}}.Q_{\frac{n}{2}}
. See §
3
for the notations in the
S_{n}
-representation theory.

From (
1.4
), (
1.7
) and (
1.8
), we obtain a closed formula for
P_{n}
(Theorem
6.1
).
Then a direct computation using our closed formula for
P_{n}
proves (
1.1
) (cf. Corollary
6.2
).
By showing that all the blowup contributions in (
1.7
) cancel with factors of
Q_{n}
, we obtain (
1.2
). Moreover, by taking the invariant parts, we can calculate the cohomology of the moduli space
\overline{{\cal M}}_{0,n}/S_{n}
of stable curves of genus 0 with unordered marked points (Corollary
6.6
).

Although it is ideal to find a permutation basis for
A^{k}(\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{0,n})
, it is not easy from our computation to find an explicit permutation basis even for
A^{k}(\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{0,n})\oplus A^{k-1}(\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{0,n})
. The main reason is that the cancellation of the blowup contributions in (
1.7
) is proved in a purely combinatorial manner. Nevertheless, when
k
is small, the combinatorial complexity is manageable and the computation for
A^{k}(\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{0,n})
can be done by hand for all
n
. See Corollary
6.2
.

The notion of a quasimap is quite flexible and it can be adapted for many other problems.
By simply increasing the number
m
of sections of a line bundle
L
of degree 1, our method applies to the computation of the
S_{n}
-representation on the cohomology of the moduli space

\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{0,n}(\mathbb{P}^{m-1},1)

of stable maps of degree 1 to

and gives a closed formula (Theorem

). In particular,
we can calculate the
S_{n}
-representation on the cohomology of the Fulton-MacPherson space

constructed in
[
11
]
.

The

-wall crossing was originally developed to provide a moduli theoretic framework for the Landau-Ginzburg/Calabi-Yau correspondence (cf.

]
). The wall crossings in (

) are for the Calabi-Yau side of the correspondence. On the Landau-Ginzburg side, we have the moduli spaces of quasimaps on twisted curves. An analogous investigation of the representation theory on the latter side will be also an interesting line of research for the future.

2.
Wall crossings of the moduli spaces of quasimaps

In this section, we review necessary facts about stable quasimaps and their moduli defined and studied in
[
6
]
. The study of wall crossing behavior of these moduli spaces as the stability condition varies results in a new factorization of the morphism forgetting the first marked point

\overline{{\cal M}}_{0,n+1}\longrightarrow\overline{{\cal M}}_{0,n}

into the sequence (
1.6
) of smooth blowups and a projection
\mathfrak{Q}^{\delta=0^{+}}\to\overline{{\cal M}}_{0,n}
which are
S_{n}
-equivariant.
Moreover, we will see that the wall crossings of quasimap moduli spaces give us a factorization of the forgetful morphism

\overline{{\cal M}}_{0,n}(\mathbb{P}^{m-1},1)\longrightarrow\overline{{\cal M}}_{0,n}

from the moduli space of stable maps of degree 1 to
\overline{{\cal M}}_{0,n}
for
m\geq 2
.

2.1.
\delta
-stability of pairs

In this subsection, we review the notion of
\delta
-stability for a
pair
(E,\alpha)
of a coherent sheaf
E
and a multi-section
\alpha
from
[
23
,
16
]
.

Let
m\in\mathbb{N}
. Let
C
be a connected projective curve with at worst nodal singularities. Let us call such a curve
prestable
.
Fix an ample line bundle
{\cal O}_{C}(1)
.
Let
E
be a coherent sheaf on
C
. A
multi-section
of
E
refers to a homomorphism

\alpha:{\cal O}_{C}^{\oplus m}\longrightarrow E

of
{\cal O}_{C}
-modules.
When
E
is a line bundle and
\alpha
is surjective, the pair
(E,\alpha)
defines a morphism
|\alpha|:C\to\mathbb{P}^{m-1}
.
For
\alpha\neq 0
, we call a pair
(E,\alpha)
a
quasimap
from
C
to
\mathbb{P}^{m-1}
when
E
is a line bundle.

For a subsheaf
E^{\prime}\subset E
, we let
\theta(E^{\prime},\alpha)=1
if
\alpha
factors through
E^{\prime}
, and
\theta(E^{\prime},\alpha)=0
otherwise. For
\delta\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}
, we write

P^{\delta}_{E^{\prime},\alpha}(t):=P_{E^{\prime}}(t)+\theta(E^{\prime},\alpha)\delta,

where
P_{E^{\prime}}(t)=r(E^{\prime})t+\chi(E^{\prime})
is the Hilbert polynomial of
E^{\prime}
with respect to
{\cal O}_{C}(1)
. If
r(E^{\prime})\neq 0
, we define the
reduced Hilbert polynomial
of
(E^{\prime},\alpha)
by

p^{\delta}_{E^{\prime},\alpha}(t):=\frac{P^{\delta}_{E^{\prime},\alpha}(t)}{r(E^{\prime})}=t+\frac{\chi(E^{\prime})}{r(E^{\prime})}+\theta(E^{\prime},\alpha)\frac{\delta}{r(E^{\prime})}.

Recall that a coherent sheaf
E
is said to be
pure
if for every nonzero subsheaf
E^{\prime}
of
E
, the support of
E^{\prime}
has the same dimension as the support of
E
. Let
E
be a 1-dimensional sheaf on
C
and let
\alpha
be a nonzero multi-section of
E
.

Definition 2.1
.

For
\delta>0
, a pair
(E,\alpha)
on
C
is called
\delta
-(semi)stable
with respect to
{\cal O}_{C}(1)
if
E
is pure and for every nonzero subsheaf
E^{\prime}\neq E
,

(2.1)

p^{\delta}_{E^{\prime},\alpha}<p^{\delta}_{E,\alpha}\quad\text{ (resp. }p^{\delta}_{E^{\prime},\alpha}\leq p^{\delta}_{E,\alpha}).

Note that when
m=1
, the notion of
\delta
-stability of pairs agrees with the stability defined by Le Potier in
[
23
]
.

A morphism
\varphi:(E_{1},\alpha_{1})\xrightarrow{}(E_{2},\alpha_{2})
of pairs refers to a homomorphism
\varphi:E_{1}\xrightarrow{}E_{2}
of
{\cal O}_{C}
-modules satisfying
\varphi\circ\alpha_{1}=\alpha_{2}
. We say
\varphi
is an
isomorphism
if it is an isomorphism of
{\cal O}_{C}
-modules.

Nowadays it is standard to construct the moduli space of
\delta
-stable pairs by GIT.

Theorem 2.2
.

[
16
]

For
\delta>0
, there is a projective coarse moduli scheme of (S-equivalence classes of)
\delta
-semistable pairs on
C
if we fix the Hilbert polynomial of
E
with respect to
{\cal O}_{C}(1)
.

So far, we considered sheaves and sections on a fixed curve
C
.
In the next subsection, we will also vary the curve
C
.

2.2.
\delta
-stable quasimaps

Let
d,m\in\mathbb{N}
and
g,n\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
. Recall that the degree of a line bundle on a prestable curve is defined to be the sum of the degrees of its restrictions to the irreducible components.

For a prestable curve
C
with
n
smooth points
p_{1},\cdots,p_{n}\in C
,
the log canonical line bundle is defined as

\omega_{C}^{\mathrm{log}}:=\omega_{C}(\sum_{i=1}^{n}p_{i}).

A prestable curve
C
with distinct smooth marked points
p_{1},\cdots,p_{n}
is
stable
if and only if
\omega_{C}^{\mathrm{log}}
is ample.

In this paper, a
quasimap
to
\mathbb{P}^{m-1}
of degree
d
and genus
g
with
n
marked points refers to a triple
(C,L,s)
of an
n
-pointed prestable curve
C
of arithmetic genus
g
, a line bundle
L\in\mathrm{Pic}^{d}(C)
of degree
d
on
C
and a nonzero multi-section
s\in H^{0}(C,L)^{\oplus m}
.
We recall the following definition from
[
6
]
.

Definition 2.3
.

[
6
, Definition 5.2]

For
\delta{>0}
, a quasimap
(C,L,s)
is called
\delta
-(semi)stable
if

(i)

\omega_{C}^{\mathrm{log}}\otimes L^{a}
is ample for any
a\in\mathbb{Q}_{>0}
, and

(ii)

for every line bundle
A\in\mathrm{Pic}(C)
such that
\omega_{C}^{\mathrm{log}}\otimes A^{\epsilon}
is ample for any
\epsilon>0
, a pair
(L,s)
is
\delta
-(semi)stable with respect to
\omega_{C}^{\mathrm{log}}\otimes A^{\epsilon}
in the sense of Definition
2.1
for arbitrarily small
\epsilon>0
.

We say that a quasimap is
\emph{strictly$\delta$-semistable}
if it is
\delta
-semistable but not
\delta
-stable.

A pointed prestable curve is called
semistable
if every rational irreducible component has at least two special points (i.e. nodes or marked points). A rational component of a semistable curve is called a
rational bridge
if it has precisely two nodes and no marked points, or is called a
rational tail
if it has only one node and one marked point. A pointed semistable curve is called
quasi-stable
if it has no rational tails and any chain of rational bridges is of length at most 1. We remark the following two simple facts (see
[
6
, p.176]
).

1.

If a quasimap
(C,L,s)
is
\delta
-semistable for some
\delta>0
, then
C
is quasi-stable. If
E\subset C
is a rational bridge, then
L|_{E}\simeq{\cal O}_{E}(1)
.

2.

The condition (ii) in Definition
2.3
can be replaced by

(ii
′
)

the pair
(\rho_{*}L,\rho_{*}s)
is
\delta
-(semi)stable on
\bar{C}
with respect to
\omega_{\bar{C}}^{\mathrm{log}}
,

where
\rho:C\xrightarrow{}\bar{C}
is the stabilization morphism and
\omega_{\bar{C}}^{\mathrm{log}}:=\omega_{\bar{C}}(\sum\rho(p_{i}))
for the marked points
p_{i}
of
C
.

The stabilization morphism
\rho
contracts rational bridges and for all
t
, we have
\chi(L\otimes(\omega_{C}^{\mathrm{log}})^{t})=\chi(\bar{L}\otimes(\omega_{\bar{C}}^{\mathrm{log}})^{t})
where
\bar{L}=\rho_{*}L
.

2.3.
Moduli of
\delta
-stable quasimaps

In this subsection, we define the moduli stack
\mathfrak{Q}^{\delta}
of
\delta
-semistable quasimaps for each
\delta>0
and define the notion of a wall. When
\delta
is not a wall,
\mathfrak{Q}^{\delta}
is a proper Deligne-Mumford stack of finite type over
\mathbb{C}
.

Let
\mathfrak{Q}=\mathfrak{Q}_{g,n,d,m}
denote the stack of quasimaps to
\mathbb{P}^{m-1}
of degree
d
and genus
g
with
n
marked points, whose section over a scheme
S
consists of

(1)

a flat proper morphism
{\cal C}\xrightarrow{}S
of finite type with
n
disjoint smooth sections whose geometric fibers are
n
-pointed prestable curves of genus
g
,

(2)

a line bundle
{\cal L}
on
{\cal C}
whose restriction to each fiber over
S
is of degree
d
,

(3)

a homomorphism
{\cal O}_{{\cal C}}^{\oplus m}\xrightarrow{}{\cal L}
which is nonzero on each fiber over
S
.

By
[
6
, Theorem 2.5]
,
\mathfrak{Q}
is an algebraic stack.
In fact
\mathfrak{Q}
is a huge stack and by fixing a stability condition, one can cut out
many interesting open substacks.
For instance, the moduli stack

\overline{{\cal M}}_{g,n}(\mathbb{P}^{m-1},d)
of stable maps is an open substack of
\mathfrak{Q}
consisting of quasimaps
(C,L,s)
such that
\omega_{C}^{\mathrm{log}}\otimes L^{3}
is ample and
s:{\cal O}_{C}^{\oplus m}\to L
is surjective, by
[
6
, Lemma 2.4]
.

For each
\delta>0
,
\mathfrak{Q}^{\delta}=\mathfrak{Q}^{\delta}_{g,n,d,m}
is now defined as the open substack consisting of
\delta
-semistable quasimaps
[
6
, Remark 5.7]
.
For our purpose, it is important to see how
\mathfrak{Q}^{\delta}
varies as
\delta
changes.

Definition 2.4
.

[
6
, Definition 5.8]
We say that
\delta
is
general
with respect to a polynomial
P(t)=rt+\chi\in\mathbb{Z}[t]
if there are no strictly
\delta
-semistable quasimaps
(C,L,s)
such that
P_{\bar{L}}(t)=P(t)
where
\rho:C\xrightarrow{}\bar{C}
is the stabilization morphism and
\bar{L}=\rho_{*}L
. We say
\delta
is a
wall
if it is not general.

By
[
6
, Lemma 5.9]
, for a fixed quadruple
(g,n,d,m)
, there are only finitely many walls and the
\delta
-stability of quasimaps remains constant between two consecutive walls.

For every quasimap
(C,L,s)\in\mathfrak{Q}^{\delta}_{g,n,d,m}
, the Hilbert polynomial of
\bar{L}
is

P_{g,n,d}:=P_{\bar{L}}(t)=(2g-2+n)t+d+1-g.

Theorem 2.5
.

[
6
, Theorem 5.10]

Suppose that
2g-2+n>0
and
d+\delta\geq g-1
. For
\delta>0
general with respect to
P_{g,n,d}
,
\mathfrak{Q}^{\delta}=\mathfrak{Q}^{\delta}_{g,n,d,m}
is a proper Deligne-Mumford stack of finite type over
\mathbb{C}
.

In this paper, we are only interested in the case of
g=0
.

Lemma 2.6
.

Let
g=0
and
\delta
be general with respect to
P_{0,n,d}
. Then the moduli stack
\mathfrak{Q}^{\delta}=\mathfrak{Q}^{\delta}_{0,n,d,m}
is a smooth projective variety of dimension
n+m(d+1)-4
.

Proof.

As
\delta
is general, there are no strictly
\delta
-semistable quasimaps and hence no nontrivial automorphisms on
\mathfrak{Q}^{\delta}
.

By
[
6
, Theorem 2.5]
, the obstruction sheaf for
\mathfrak{Q}^{\delta}
is

R^{1}\pi_{*}{\cal L}^{\oplus m}
where
{\cal L}
is the universal line bundle over the universal curve
\pi:{\cal C}\to\mathfrak{Q}^{\delta}
.
Since
H^{1}(L)^{\oplus m}
is zero whenever
g=0
and
d>0
,
R^{1}\pi_{*}{\cal L}^{\oplus m}=0
by the base change property. Hence
\mathfrak{Q}^{\delta}
is smooth.

The dimension count comes from
[
6
, Corollary 2.6]
.
∎

2.4.
Wall crossings for
m=d=1

Our computation of
A^{k}(\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{0,n})
utilizes the wall crossings of the moduli spaces of
\delta
-stable quasimaps when
g=0
and
m=d=1
.

We write
\delta=\infty
when
\delta
is sufficiently large so that there are no walls larger than
\delta
and
\delta=0^{+}
when
\delta
is a sufficiently small positive number.

Lemma 2.7
.

When
m=d=1
,
\mathfrak{Q}^{\delta=\infty}_{g,n,1,1}\simeq\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n+1}
.

Proof.

Let
(C,L,s)\in\mathfrak{Q}^{\delta=\infty}
. If
C
is stable, then
(L,s)
can be an arbitrary pair except that
s
does not vanish at the nodes of
C
. If
C
is not stable, then
C
is quasi-stable with a unique rational bridge
E\simeq\mathbb{P}^{1}
, and
s
is uniquely determined by a section of
L|_{E}\simeq{\cal O}_{E}(1)
which does not vanish at the nodes on
E
.

Relativizing this, to each family
({\cal C},{\cal L},s)
of
\delta=\infty
-stable quasimaps parametrized by a scheme
S
, one can associate an extra section of
{\cal C}\xrightarrow{}S
, which parametrizes the vanishing locus of the section
s
. Indeed, the vanishing locus is flat over
S
by
[
30
, Lemma 31.18.9]
and fiberwisely a smooth point, so the structure morphism to
S
is an isomorphism.

As the universal curve over
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}
is
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n+1}
, we
thus have a morphism

(2.2)

\mathfrak{Q}^{\delta=\infty}\longrightarrow\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n+1}.

To get the inverse, consider a family
{\cal C}\xrightarrow{}S
of stable curves of genus
g
with
n+1
sections. Then the image of the first section is a relative Cartier divisor of degree 1, so corresponds to a pair
({\cal L},s)
of a line bundle
{\cal L}\in\mathrm{Pic}({\cal C})
of fiberwise degree 1 and a nonzero section
s
. This gives us a family
({\cal C},{\cal L},s)
of
\delta=\infty
-stable quasimaps and hence an inverse to (
2.2
). ∎

If furthermore
g=0
, the wall crossings of the moduli spaces of
\delta
-stable quasimaps, which we call
\delta
-wall crossings
, result in a new construction of
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{0,n+1}
from

.
Let

By
[

, Lemma 7.9]
, there exist

\delta_{h}=\frac{n-2}{h-1}-2,\quad\text{for }2\leq h\leq\ell.

Let
\delta_{h}^{+}\in(\delta_{h-1},\delta_{h})
,
\delta_{2}^{+}:=\infty
and
\delta_{\ell+1}^{+}:=0^{+}
. Let

denote the forgetful morphism which forgets the first marking.

Theorem 2.8
.

Suppose
g=0
and
m=d=1
. Let
\mathfrak{Q}^{\delta}=\mathfrak{Q}^{\delta}_{0,n,1,1}
and
\ell=\lfloor\frac{n-1}{2}\rfloor
. Then the
\delta
-wall crossings factorize the morphism

as

(2.3)

\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{0,n+1}\cong\mathfrak{Q}^{\delta=\infty}\xrightarrow{~\mathfrak{q}_{2}~}\mathfrak{Q}^{\delta_{3}^{+}}\xrightarrow{~\mathfrak{q}_{3}~}\cdots\xrightarrow{~\mathfrak{q}_{\ell-1}~}\mathfrak{Q}^{\delta_{\ell}^{+}}\xrightarrow{~\mathfrak{q}_{\ell}~}\mathfrak{Q}^{\delta=0^{+}}\xrightarrow{~\mathfrak{p}~}\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{0,n}~

(1)

at the wall

(b)

-equivariant.

Proof.

The identification of the forgetful morphism

.

For each

3.
Preliminaries on representations of the symmetric groups

In this section, we collect necessary facts on the representation theory of
S_{n}
.
The main reference is
[
24
]
.

3.1.
Characteristic map

Let
R
be the ring of representations of the symmetric groups and let
\Lambda:=\varprojlim\mathbb{Z}[x_{1},\cdots,x_{n}]^{S_{n}}
be the ring of symmetric functions. It is well known that
\Lambda\otimes\mathbb{Q}=\mathbb{Q}[p_{1},p_{2},\cdots]
, where
p_{n}=\sum x_{i}^{n}
is the
n
-th power sum. For a representation
V
of
S_{n}
, we define its character by

\mathrm{ch}_{n}(V)=\frac{1}{n!}\sum_{\sigma\in S_{n}}\mathrm{Tr}_{V}(\sigma)p_{\sigma},

where
\mathrm{Tr}_{V}(\sigma)
is the trace of the action of
\sigma
on
V
and
p_{\sigma}=p_{\lambda_{1}}\cdots p_{\lambda_{r}}
if
\sigma
has the cycle type
\lambda=(\lambda_{1},\cdots,\lambda_{r})
. For example if
\sigma=(1,3,5)(2,4)
, then
p_{\sigma}=p_{3}p_{2}
.

Recall that irreducible representations of
S_{n}
bijectively correspond to partitions of
n
. Let
V_{\lambda}
be the irreducible representation corresponding to a partition
\lambda
. Then we have
\mathrm{ch}_{n}(V_{\lambda})=s_{\lambda}\in\Lambda
, the Schur function.

The characteristic map
\mathrm{ch}:R\xrightarrow{}\Lambda
is a ring isomorphism, that is,

(3.1)

\mathrm{ch}_{i}(V)\mathrm{ch}_{j}(W)=\mathrm{ch}_{i+j}\big(\mathrm{Ind}^{S_{i+j}}_{S_{i}\times S_{j}}V\otimes W\big),

where
\mathrm{Ind}
denotes the induced representation. Hence, we sometimes do not distinguish a representation
V
of
S_{n}
and its character
\mathrm{ch}_{n}(V)
. For example,
V.W
denotes
\mathrm{Ind}^{S_{i+j}}_{S_{i}\times S_{j}}V\otimes W
for
S_{i}
-representation
V
and
S_{j}
-representation
W
.

3.2.
Plethysm

In
\Lambda
, there is another multiplication, called
plethysm
. For two symmetric functions
f
and
g
, the plethysm is denoted by
f\circ g
and it is uniquely defined by the following properties:

(1)

f\circ p_{n}=p_{n}\circ f=f(x_{1}^{n},x_{2}^{n},\cdots)
,

(2)

(f+g)\circ h=f\circ h+g\circ h
,

(3)

(fg)\circ h=(f\circ h)(g\circ h)

for symmetric functions
f,g
and
h
. Note that
p_{1}=s_{(1)}
acts as a two sided identity. The plethysm is not commutative in general, but it is associative.
For more details, see
[
24
, I. 8]
.

On the representation ring
R
, we can define the corresponding product. Namely, given an
S_{n}
-representation
V
and
S_{m}
-representation
W
, the plethysm is defined by

W\circ V=\mathrm{Ind}^{S_{mn}}_{(S_{n})^{m}\rtimes S_{m}}\big(W\otimes T^{m}(V)\big),

where
T^{m}(V)
is the
m
-th tensor power of
V
and
S_{m}
acts on
T^{m}(V)
by permuting its factors. Then it is well known that

(3.2)

\mathrm{ch}(W\circ V)=\mathrm{ch}(W)\circ\mathrm{ch}(V).

In this paper, we will use plethysm when
W
is the trivial representation (
\mathrm{ch}(W)=s_{(m)}
) or the alternating representation (
\mathrm{ch}(W)=s_{(1^{m})}
). We also do not distinguish a representation and its character when we use plethysm.

Example 3.1
.

Let
V
be a representation of
S_{n}
. Consider a natural action of
S_{2}
on
V.V
switching two copies of
V
. Then
s_{(2)}\circ V
and
s_{(1,1)}\circ V
are the symmetric and the antisymmetric parts of
V.V
respectively. From the defining properties of plethysm, one can easily check the following:

(1)

s_{(2)}\circ V=\mathrm{Ind}^{S_{2n}}_{(S_{n}\times S_{n})\rtimes S_{2}}V\otimes V=(V.V)^{S_{2}}
and

(2)

s_{(1,1)}\circ V=V.V-s_{(2)}\circ V
.

Furthermore, we have an identity of symmetric functions (
[

)

s_{(1,1)}\circ(f+g)=fg+s_{(1,1)}\circ f+s_{(1,1)}\circ g.

Hence, if

into

-representations

(3.3)

s_{(1,1)}\circ\left(\bigoplus_{i=1}^{r}V_{i}\right)=\bigoplus_{i<j}V_{i}.V_{j}\oplus\bigoplus_{i=1}^{r}\left(s_{(1,1)}\circ V_{i}\right).

3.3.
Permutation representation

Definition 3.2
.

A representation
V
of
S_{n}
is a

if there exists an
S_{n}
-invariant basis of
V
. If further such a basis is one orbit of
S_{n}
, then

is said to be
transitive
.

Every permutation representation decomposes into a direct sum of transitive ones. While such a decomposition is not necessarily unique, every decomposition of a given permutation representation has the same number of transitive components because it is equal to the dimension of the invariant subspace.

Every transitive permutation representation of
S_{n}
is isomorphic to the induced representation of the trivial representation of some subgroup
H\subset S_{n}
, which we denote by

U_{H}:=\mathrm{Ind}^{S_{n}}_{H}e

where
e
is the trivial
H
-representation. Equivalently,
U_{H}
is isomorphic to the permutation representation of
S_{n}
generated by the cosets in
S_{n}/H
with the
S_{n}
-action given by left multiplication. This is also isomorphic to the
H
-invariant subspace of the regular representation of
S_{n}
with respect to the action of
H
induced by the right multiplication.

We introduce the following notations.

Definition 3.3
.

(1)

When
n=\sum a_{i}
, from
\prod S_{a_{i}}\subset S_{n}
, we write

U_{a_{1},\cdots,a_{k}}:=U_{\prod S_{a_{i}}}.

(2)

Let

. Consider
((S_{a})^{m}\rtimes S_{m})\times\prod S_{a_{i}}\subset S_{n}
, where
S_{m}
permutes factors of
(S_{a})^{m}
. We write

U_{a^{m},a_{1},\cdots,a_{k}}^{S_{m}}:=U_{((S_{a})^{m}\rtimes S_{m})\times\prod S_{a_{i}}},

where
a^{m}
denotes the sequence of
m
copies of
a
.

The representation
U_{a^{m},a_{1},\cdots,a_{k}}^{S_{m}}
is the invariant subspace of
U_{a^{m},a_{1},\cdots,a_{k}}
under an action of
S_{m}
, which commutes with the action of
S_{n}
. Note that by (

) and (

), we have

\mathrm{ch}(U_{a_{1},\cdots,a_{k}})=\prod_{i=1}^{k}s_{(a_{i})}

and

\mathrm{ch}(U_{a^{m},a_{1},\cdots,a_{k}}^{S_{m}})=(s_{(m)}\circ s_{(a)}).\prod_{i=1}^{k}s_{(a_{i})}.

5.1.
Blowup formula for transversal subvarieties

Note that the blowup centers for Kapranov’s blowups are usually not smooth and hence we cannot directly apply the usual blowup formula
[
14
, p.605]
or
[
10
, Theorem 6.7]
.
In this section, we recall the blowup formula for transversal subvarieties.

Let
X
be a smooth variety and

Y=\bigcup_{a\in A}Y_{a}

be a transversal union of smooth irreducible subvarieties
Y_{a}
of
X
, that is, every nonempty intersection

Y_{A^{\prime}}:=\bigcap_{a\in A^{\prime}}Y_{a},\quad\text{for }A^{\prime}\subset A

is transversal. For each subset
A^{\prime}\subset A
, we let

V_{A^{\prime}}=\bigotimes_{a\in A^{\prime}}V_{a}

where
V_{a}:=A^{+}(\mathbb{P}^{\mathrm{codim}(Y_{a})-1})
is the cohomology of
\mathbb{P}^{\mathrm{codim}(Y_{a})-1}
of positive degrees.

Lemma 5.1
.

[
1
, Proposition 6.1]
Let
{\widetilde{X}}
be the blowup of
X
along
Y=\bigcup_{a\in A}Y_{a}
. Then,

(5.1)

A^{*}({\widetilde{X}})=A^{*}(X)\oplus\bigoplus_{\emptyset\neq A^{\prime}\subset A}A^{*}(Y_{A^{\prime}})\otimes V_{A^{\prime}},

where
A^{\prime}
runs over the nonempty subsets of
A
with
Y_{A^{\prime}}\neq\emptyset
.

Proof.

{\widetilde{X}}
is isomorphic to the iterated smooth blowup of
X
along (the proper transforms of)
Y_{a}
. The assertion follows from a repeated application of the blowup formula for smooth irreducible centers.
∎

Note that if a finite group
G
acts on
X
and
Y
is
G
-invariant, then there are natural actions of
G
on
{\widetilde{X}}
and
A
as
G
permutes
Y_{a}
. In this case, (
5.1
) is
G
-equivariant.

The Kapranov map
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{0,n+1}\xrightarrow{}\mathbb{P}^{n-2}
breaks into a sequence of blowup morphisms. The intermediate spaces and blowup centers are suitable Hassett moduli spaces of weighted pointed stable curves
[
15
]
. The weight of the first marking is fixed to be one. We call this marking the
0-th marking
. The remaining
n
markings are indexed by the set
[n]=\{1,2,\cdots,n\}
and the symmetric group
S_{n}
acts on the moduli spaces by permuting them. All the blowup morphisms are
S_{n}
-equivariant by construction, and so we can apply the blowup formula to compute the
S_{n}
-equivariant Poincaré polynomial. We will introduce the notion of weighted rooted trees to encode the combinatorial data of the blowup centers.

5.2.
Hassett moduli of weighted stable curves

In
[
15
]
, Hassett introduced the notion of
weighted
stable curves which generalizes that of stable curves.
Recall that for each weight

\mathcal{A}=(\mathcal{A}(1),\cdots,\mathcal{A}(n))\in(\mathbb{Q}\cap(0,1])^{\oplus n},

a pair
(C,p_{1},\cdots,p_{n})
of a prestable curve
C
with (not necessarily distinct) smooth marked points
p_{i}\in C
is called
{\cal A}
-stable if

(1)

\omega_{C}(\sum_{i=1}^{n}{\cal A}(i)p_{i})
is ample, and

(2)

for any
p\in C
,
\sum_{p_{i}=p}{\cal A}(i)\leq 1
.

Note that when
{\cal A}(i)=1
for all
i
, we recover the notion of the usual stable curve. Hassett proved that for each weight
\mathcal{A}\in(\mathbb{Q}\cap(0,1])^{\oplus n}
with
\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mathcal{A}(i)+2g-2>0
there exists a smooth projective Deligne-Mumford stack
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,\mathcal{A}}
parametrizing
\mathcal{A}
-weighted stable curves of genus
g
. If
g=0
, every such Hassett moduli space is a smooth projective variety.

When
\mathcal{A},\mathcal{B}\in(\mathbb{Q}\cap(0,1])^{\oplus n}
are weights with
\mathcal{A}(i)\geq\mathcal{B}(i)
for all
i
, we write
\mathcal{A}\geq\mathcal{B}
. For weights
\mathcal{A}\geq\mathcal{B}
, there exists the
reduction morphism

\rho_{\mathcal{A},\mathcal{B}}:\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,\mathcal{A}}\xrightarrow{}\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,\mathcal{B}}
which stabilizes
\mathcal{A}
-stable curves with respect to the weight
\mathcal{B}
.

Proposition 5.2
.

[
15
]

Reduction morphisms have the following properties.

(1)

For weights
\mathcal{A}\geq\mathcal{B}\geq\mathcal{C}
, we have
\rho_{\mathcal{A},\mathcal{C}}=\rho_{\mathcal{B},\mathcal{C}}\circ\rho_{\mathcal{A},\mathcal{B}}
.

(2)

For weights
\mathcal{A}\geq\mathcal{B}
, there are only finitely many
t\in(0,1]
such that

(5.2)

\sum_{i\in I}\mathcal{A}(i)>\sum_{i\in I}(1-t)\mathcal{A}(i)+t\mathcal{B}(i)=1\geq\sum_{i\in I}\mathcal{B}(i)

for some
I\subset[n]
with
|I|\geq 3
.

(3)

If there exists only one such
t
in (2), then
\rho_{\mathcal{A},\mathcal{B}}
is the blowup along the transversal union of the loci
Z_{I}
along which the
I
-markings collide, where
I
runs over the subsets of
[n]
satisfying (
5.2
) and
|I|\geq 3
. Moreover,
Z_{I}\simeq\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,\mathcal{B}_{I}}
where
\mathcal{B}_{I}\in(\mathbb{Q}\cap(0,1])^{\oplus(n-|I|+1)}
is the weight obtained from
\mathcal{B}
by adding up
\{\mathcal{B}(i)\}_{i\in I}
as one entry.

(4)

The intersection of some of the loci
Z_{I_{1}},\cdots,Z_{I_{r}}
in (3) is nonempty if and only if
I_{j}
are mutually disjoint. Furthermore, in this case
\bigcap_{j}Z_{I_{j}}\simeq\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,\mathcal{B}_{I_{1},\cdots,I_{r}}}
where

Remark 6.4
.

One may want to refine Question
1.1
(2) by asking whether the primitive cohomology
H^{2k}_{\mathrm{prim}}(\overline{{\cal M}}_{0,n})
is a permutation representation or not.
The answer is no. The primitive cohomology
H^{2k}_{\mathrm{prim}}(\overline{{\cal M}}_{0,n})
as a virtual representation is equal to
A^{k}(\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{0,n})-A^{k-1}(\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{0,n})
which is not a permutation representation in general. Indeed, the character of
A^{2}(\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{0,7})-A^{1}(\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{0,7})
is (cf. Appendix)

2s_{(7)}+s_{(4,3)}+2s_{(5,2)}+s_{(6,1)}+s_{(4,2,1)}.

If this is a permutation representation, it should be a direct sum of two transitive permutation representations. By examining all possible combinations and using the fact that a transitive representation
V
of a group
G
satisfies the properties that

(1)

\mathrm{Tr}_{V}(1)
divides
|G|
, and

(2)

\mathrm{Tr}_{V}(\sigma)\leq\mathrm{Tr}_{V}(\sigma^{2})
for
\sigma\in G
,

one can check that
A^{2}(\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{0,7})-A^{1}(\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{0,7})
is not a permutation representation.

Although a primitive cohomology is not a permutation representation in general, we
do not know whether the sum of two consecutive primitive cohomology groups is a permutation representation or not.

If we plug (

-equivariant Poincaré polynomial

P_{\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{0,n}(\mathbb{P}^{m-1},1)}
of the moduli space
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{0,n}(\mathbb{P}^{m-1},1)

of stable maps to
\mathbb{P}^{m-1}
of degree 1 for
m\geq 2
.

The
S_{n}
-equivariant Poincaré polynomial
P_{\overline{{\cal M}}_{0,n}(\mathbb{P}^{m-1},1)}
can be computed by torus localization as well. Consider the action of
\mathbb{C}^{*}
on
\mathbb{P}^{m-1}
given by

t\cdot[x_{0},x_{1},\cdots,x_{m-1}]=[x_{0},tx_{1},\cdots,tx_{m-1}]\hskip 20.00003pt\text{for }t\in\mathbb{C}^{*}.

There are two fixed sets: the isolated point
p=[1:0:\cdots:0]
and the hyperplane
H=\{x_{0}=0\}
. In
[
27
]
, Oprea showed that the moduli space
\overline{{\cal M}}_{0,n}(\mathbb{P}^{m-1},1)
admits a Białynicki-Birula decomposition for the torus action on
\overline{{\cal M}}_{0,n}(\mathbb{P}^{m-1},1)
induced by this
\mathbb{C}^{*}
-action on
\mathbb{P}^{m-1}
. The decomposition is compatible with
S_{n}
-action and this leads to the computation of
S_{n}
-equivariant Poincaré polynomial
P_{\overline{{\cal M}}_{0,n}(\mathbb{P}^{m-1},1)}
.

For a
\mathbb{C}^{*}
-fixed stable map
f
in
\overline{{\cal M}}_{0,n}(\mathbb{P}^{m-1},1)
, the domain curve has a unique non-contracted component which is mapped isomorphically by
f
to a curve in
H
or an invariant curve joining
p
and a point in
H
. The contracted components and the special points are mapped by
f
to
p
or to
H
. For a general classification of fixed stable maps indexed by decorated graphs, see
[
27
]
.

Let
\mathcal{F}_{i}
be a connected component of the fixed point locus. Then
\overline{{\cal M}}_{0,n}(\mathbb{P}^{m-1},1)
is covered by locally closed subvarieties
\mathcal{F}_{i}^{+}
, called the
Białynicki-Birula (plus) cells
, which are affine fibrations over
\mathcal{F}_{i}
. The Poincaré polynomial of
\overline{{\cal M}}_{0,n}(\mathbb{P}^{m-1},1)
is then given by (cf.
[
27
, Lemma 6]
)

P_{\overline{{\cal M}}_{0,n}(\mathbb{P}^{m-1},1)}(t)=\sum P_{\mathcal{F}_{i}}(t)t^{n_{i}^{-}},

where
n_{i}^{-}
is the codimension of
\mathcal{F}_{i}^{+}
, which is equal to the number of negative weights on the tangent bundle of
\overline{{\cal M}}_{0,n}(\mathbb{P}^{m-1},1)
at a fixed point in
\mathcal{F}_{i}
.

Theorem 6.8
.

For
m\geq 2
,

(6.4)

P_{\overline{{\cal M}}_{0,n}(\mathbb{P}^{m-1},1)}=t^{2}P_{\overline{{\cal M}}_{0,n}(\mathbb{P}^{m-2},1)}+\sum_{a=0}^{n}t^{\min\{n-a,2\}}\frac{1-t^{m-2}}{1-t}Q_{a}Q_{n-a},

where we set
P_{\overline{{\cal M}}_{0,n}(\mathbb{P}^{m-2},1)}=0
for
m=2
.

Proof.

The locus of
\mathbb{C}^{*}
-fixed stable maps whose image lies in
H
is nonempty if and only if
m>2
and is isomorphic to
\overline{{\cal M}}_{0,n}(\mathbb{P}^{m-2},1)
. By
[
27
, Theorem 2]
, the corresponding Białynicki-Birula cell has codimension 2. Hence we have the first term on the right side.

The locus of
\mathbb{C}^{*}
-fixed stable maps whose image is an invariant curve joining
p
and a point in

We will define the K-theoretic cuspidal block
K_{0}(\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{0,n})_{cusp}
of
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{0,n}
, which is the K-theoretic analogue of the cuspidal block defined on the derived category by Castravet and Tevelev
[
3
]
. We will see that
K_{0}(\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{0,n})
admits an
S_{n}
-equivariant decomposition by
K_{0}(\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{0,k})_{cusp}
with
k\leq n
. We will give a closed formula for the
S_{n}
-character of
K_{0}(\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{0,n})_{cusp}
and check that it is a permutation representation for

.

In this section, we only consider Grothendieck groups with rational coefficients.

8.1.
K-theoretic cuspidal block of
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{0,n}

In
[
3
]
, Castravet and Tevelev defined the
cuspidal block

\mathcal{D}^{b}_{cusp}(\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{0,n})\subset\mathcal{D}^{b}(\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{0,n})

of
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{0,n}
as the full triangulated subcategory of the bounded derived category
\mathcal{D}^{b}(\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{0,n})
of coherent sheaves on
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{0,n}
consisting of
cuspidal
objects. By definition, a cuspidal object is the object
E
satisfying

R\pi_{i*}E=0\quad\text{~for~}i=1,\cdots,n

where
\pi_{i}:\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{0,n}\xrightarrow{}\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{0,n-1}
is the morphism which forgets the
i
-th marking.

They showed that
\mathcal{D}^{b}(\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{0,n})
admits a semi-orthogonal decomposition by the pullbacks of
\mathcal{D}^{b}_{cusp}(\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{0,k})
with
k\leq n
in an
S_{n}
-equivariant way
[
3
, Proposition 1.6]
, and raised a question whether there exists an
S_{n}
-invariant full exceptional collection in
\mathcal{D}^{b}_{cusp}(\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{0,n})

[
3
, Question 1.5]
. An affirmative answer for this question will give a new
S_{n}
-equivariant decomposition of
\mathcal{D}^{b}(\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{0,n})
.

Let us consider K-theoretic analogues.

Definition 8.1
.

The
K-theoretic cuspidal block
of
\overline{{\cal M}}_{0,n}
is defined as

K_{0}(\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{0,n})_{cusp}:=\{E\in K_{0}(\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{0,n})~|~(\pi_{i})_{!}E=0\text{ all }i\}.

For a finite set
S
, we denote by
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{0,S}
the moduli space of stable rational curves with
|S|
points marked by
S
. We identify
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{0,n}
with
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{0,[n]}
. For each
J\subset[n]
with
|J|\leq n-4
, we denote by
\pi_{J}:\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{0,n}\xrightarrow{}\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{0,[n]\setminus J}
the forgetful morphism which forgets the markings by
J
. We let
\pi_{\emptyset}=\mathrm{id}
for convenience. The following is essentially proved in
[
3
, §3]
.

Proposition 8.2
.

There is an
S_{n}
-equivariant isomorphism

(8.1)

K_{0}(\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{0,n})=\mathbb{Q}\oplus\bigoplus_{J\subset[n],|J|\leq n-4}\pi_{J}^{*}K_{0}(\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{0,[n]\setminus J})_{cusp}.

where
\mathbb{Q}
is generated by the structure sheaf
\mathcal{O}_{\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{0,n}}
.

Proof.

First note that
(\pi_{i})_{!}
is a left inverse of
\pi_{i}^{*}
for each
i
. Hence we have the decomposition

(8.2)

K_{0}(\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{0,n})=\pi_{i}^{*}K_{0}(\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{0,n-1})\oplus\{E\in K_{0}(\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{0,n})~|~(\pi_{i})_{!}E=0\}.

Now the assertion follows by taking intersections of such decompositions for
i=1,\cdots,n
, which make sense
because

(8.3)

\pi_{j}^{*}(\pi_{j})_{!}\pi_{i}^{*}K_{0}(\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{0,n-1})\subset\pi_{i}^{*}K_{0}(\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{0,n-1}),\quad\text{for all }1\leq i,j\leq n.

Note that (
8.3
) follows from the equality
(\pi_{j})_{!}\pi_{i}^{*}=\pi_{i}^{*}(\pi_{j})_{!}
which holds by
the flat base change theorem and the fact that
(\pi_{i})_{!}\mathcal{O}_{\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{0,n}}=\mathcal{O}_{\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{0,n-1}}
.
∎

The following transitive permutation representations will be useful.

Let

Definition 8.3
.

For

Appendix A

In this section, we compare (

from
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{0,n}
in
[

]
. We explain in detail that the construction in
[

]
is not
S_{n}
-equivariant and the blowup is different from (

). See Example
A.3
for a case of small

In
[
5
]
, Chen, Gibney and Krashen constructed the moduli space
T_{n}:=T_{1,n}
of
n
-pointed rooted trees of projective lines, and proved the following theorems.

Theorem A.1
.

[
5
, Proposition 3.4.3]

T_{n}\cong\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{0,n+1}
for
n\geq 2
.

By definition,
T_{n}
is the sublocus of the Fulton-MacPherson compactification
\mathbb{P}^{1}[n]
of the configuration space of
n
marked points in
\mathbb{P}^{1}
where the
n
marked points collide to a given point
x
in
\mathbb{P}^{1}
(
[
5
, Definition 3.3.2]
). Equivalently, under the identification
\mathbb{P}^{1}[n]\cong\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{0,n}(\mathbb{P}^{1},1)
,

(A.1)

T_{n}=\bigcap_{i=1}^{n}ev_{i}^{-1}(x)

where
ev_{i}:\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{0,n}(\mathbb{P}^{1},1)\to\mathbb{P}^{1}
is the evaluation map at the
i
-th marking.

Let
f:(C,p_{1},\ldots,p_{n})\to\mathbb{P}^{1}
be a stable map in
T_{n}
under the identification (
A.1
). Suppose that
C
has irreducible components
C^{\prime},D_{1},\ldots,D_{r}
with
f_{*}([C^{\prime}])=1
and
f_{*}([D_{i}])=0
. Note that none of the marked points
p_{i}
may lie on
C^{\prime}
and
C^{\prime}
has a unique node attached to
\cup_{i}D_{i}
. By forgetting
C^{\prime}
, we get an
(n+1)
-pointed stable curve where the
(n+1)
st marking is given by the attaching node of
C^{\prime}
. This gives the isomorphism in Theorem
A.1
. The
(n+1)
st marking corresponds to the
root
on an
n
-pointed rooted tree of projective lines (
[
5
, Definition 2.0.1]
).

Fix
n\geq 2
. Let
N=\{1,\ldots,n\}
and
N^{+}=N\cup\{n+1\}
. For a subset
S\subset N
, let
S^{+}=S\cup\{n+1\}
. Denote by
\bullet
the root on each
n
-pointed rooted tree of projective lines.
We identify

\begin{split}T_{n}&\cong\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{0,N\cup\{\bullet\}}~(\cong\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{0,n+1})\ \ \text{ and}\\
T_{n+1}&\cong\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{0,N^{+}\cup\{\bullet\}}~(\cong\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{0,n+2}).\end{split}

Theorem A.2
.

[
5
, Theorem 3.3.1]

The forgetful morphism

(A.2)

\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{0,N^{+}\cup\{\bullet\}}\longrightarrow\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{0,N\cup\{\bullet\}}

which forgets the
(n+1)
st marking, admits the factorization

(A.3)

T_{n+1}=F^{n}_{n}\xrightarrow{b_{n-1}}F^{n-1}_{n}\xrightarrow{b_{n-2}}\cdots\xrightarrow{~b_{1}~}F^{1}_{n}\xrightarrow{~b_{0}~}F^{0}_{n}=T_{n},

where

(1)

for
0\leq i\leq n
,
F_{n}^{i}
is smooth and it has subvarieties
F_{n}^{i}(S)
indexed by
S\subset N^{+}
with
\lvert S\rvert\geq 2
,

(2)

the morphism
b_{0}
is a
\mathbb{P}^{1}
-bundle,

(3)

for
1\leq i\leq n-1
, the morphism
b_{i}
is the blowup along the disjoint union of
F_{n}^{i}(S^{+})
for
S\subset N
with
\lvert S\rvert=n-i
,

(4)

F_{n}^{i}(S^{+})\cong\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{0,S\cup\{\star\}}\times\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{0,S^{c}\cup\{\star,\bullet\}}
if
\lvert S\rvert=n-i\neq 1
,

(5)

F_{n}^{n-1}(S^{+})\cong\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{0,N\cup\{\bullet\}}
if
\lvert S\rvert=1
.

The factorization (
A.3
) is obtained as the restriction to
T_{n+1}
of the construction of the Fulton-MacPherson configuration space in
[

]

\mathbb{P}^{1}[n+1]=:\mathbb{P}^{1}[n,n]\xrightarrow{~\rho_{n}~}\mathbb{P}^{1}[n,n-1]\xrightarrow{\rho_{n-1}}\cdots\xrightarrow{\rho_{1}}\mathbb{P}^{1}[n,0]:=\mathbb{P}^{1}[n]\times\mathbb{P}^{1},

where the composition
\mathbb{P}^{1}[n+1]\to\mathbb{P}^{1}[n]\times\mathbb{P}^{1}
is given by the forgetful map of the
(n+1)
st marking and the evaluation map
ev_{n+1}
. Here, the image
T_{n}
of
T_{n+1}
sits inside
\mathbb{P}^{1}[n]\times\{x\}
for

. See
[
5
, §3.1]
.

The above map
\mathbb{P}^{1}[n+1]\to\mathbb{P}^{1}[n]\times\mathbb{P}^{1}
can be viewed as the contraction of the universal curve over

to the degree 1 component. Furthermore, for each
k
, the composition
\rho_{n-k+1}\circ\cdots\circ\rho_{n}
contracts all the connected degree 0 subcurves

of the universal curve

such that

has precisely

.

Claim.
(

) is not equivariant under the action of

Proof.

By the above description, the composition

-equivariant.
∎

) is different from the factorization (

Bohr topology and difference sets for some abelian groups
John T. Griesmer

Abstract
. For a fixed prime p p , 𝔽 p \mathbb{F}_{p} denotes the field with p p elements, and 𝔽 p ω \mathbb{F}_{p}^{\omega} denotes the countable direct sum ⨁ n = 1 ∞ 𝔽 p \bigoplus_{n=1}^{\infty}\mathbb{F}_{p} . Viewing 𝔽 p ω \mathbb{F}_{p}^{\omega} as a countable abelian group, we construct a set A ⊆ 𝔽 p ω A\subseteq\mathbb{F}_{p}^{\omega} having positive upper Banach density while the difference set A − A := { a − b : a , b ∈ A } A-A:=\{a-b:a,b\in A\} does not contain a Bohr neighborhood of any c ∈ 𝔽 p ω c\in\mathbb{F}_{p}^{\omega} . For p = 2 p=2 we obtain a stronger conclusion: A − A A-A does not contain a set of the form g + ( B − B ) g+(B-B) , where B B is piecewise syndetic. This construction answers negatively a variant of the following question asked by several authors: if A ⊆ ℤ A\subseteq\mathbb{Z} has positive upper Banach density, must A − A A-A contain a Bohr neighborhood of some n ∈ ℤ n\in\mathbb{Z} ? We also construct sets S , A ⊆ 𝔽 p ω S,A\subseteq\mathbb{F}_{p}^{\omega} such that S S is dense in the Bohr topology of 𝔽 p ω \mathbb{F}_{p}^{\omega} , A A has positive upper Banach density, and A + S A+S is not piecewise Bohr. For p = 2 p=2 we show that every translate of S S is a set of topological recurrence and A + S A+S is not piecewise syndetic. These constructions answer a variant of a question asked by the author.

1.4.
Upper Banach density

A

for an abelian group
G
is a sequence of finite subsets
\Phi_{n}\subseteq G
such that
\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{|(\Phi_{n}+g)\cap\Phi_{n}|}{|\Phi_{n}|}=1
for every
g\in G
.

If

is a Følner sequence for
G
and

, the
upper density of
A
with respect to
\mathbf{\Phi}
is
\bar{d}_{\mathbf{\Phi}}(A):=\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{|A\cap\Phi_{n}|}{|\Phi_{n}|}
; we write

if the limit exists. The
upper Banach density
of
A
is
d^{*}(A):=\sup\{\bar{d}_{\mathbf{\Phi}}(A):\mathbf{\Phi}\text{ is a F{\o}lner sequence}\}
. Note that for every
A\subseteq G
, there is a Følner sequence
\mathbf{\Phi}
such that
d^{*}(A)=d_{\mathbf{\Phi}}(A)
.

If
(H_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is an increasing sequence of finite subgroups of
G
such that
G=\bigcup_{n=1}^{\infty}H_{n}
, then
(H_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is a Følner sequence for
G
.

While upper Banach density is not finitely additive, it enjoys the following weaker property.

We omit the proof, which is a straightforward application of the relevant definitions.

3.3.
Hamming Balls

Let

.

Let

denote the constant function where
\mathbf{1}(t)=1\in\mathbb{F}_{2}
for all

. Let

\mathbf{0}(t)=0
for all

.

Note that for

\|g\|_{0}=2^{-n}\cdot|\{\tau\in\Omega_{n}:g_{\tau}=\mathbf{0}\}|

For

, let

This is the

around

\mathbf{0}
. In other words,

is the set of

which are constant on intervals of the form

, and

for at least
2^{n}-k
such intervals
\tau
. Let

, so that

We call

the
Hamming ball of scale
n
and radius
k
around

\mathbf{1}
. Note that
\mathbf{1}\in V(n,k)
for all

.

Lemma 3.2
.

If
m\leq n
and
k\in\mathbb{N}
,

(i)

for all

and
\tau\in\Omega_{m}
,

. Similarly if
v\in V(n,k)
, then
v_{\tau}\in V(n-m,k)
.

(ii)

for all

, all

, and at least
n-k
intervals
\tau\in\Omega_{n}
,

.

Proof.

(i) If

, then

is constant on all intervals
\tau\in\Omega_{n}
, and

for at most
k-1
such intervals. For an interval

, the definition of

then guarantees that

for at most

Part (ii) follows directly from the definition of

.
∎

Remark 3.3

We call the sets

Let

Lemma 4.2

If

Lemma 4.3

Now assume

,

Let

We now return to the Hamming balls defined in §

Proof of (ii).
For

Definition 4.6
.

For
n,k\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}
let

C(0,n;k):=\{g\in G_{n}:\|g\|_{0}\geq\tfrac{1}{2}+k2^{-n}\}.

So

is the set of functions

which are constant on the intervals in
\Omega_{n}
, and the number of such intervals

is at least
\tfrac{1}{2}|\Omega_{n}|+k

Lemma 4.7
.

Let
n,k,k^{\prime}\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}
,
k^{\prime}\leq k
.

(i)

U(n,k^{\prime})+C(0,n,k)\subseteq C(0,n,k-k^{\prime})
.

(ii)

C(0,n,k)\subseteq C(0,n,k^{\prime})
.

Proof.

Part (i) follows from the fact that if
u\in U(n,k)
and
g\in C(0,n,k)
, then
(u+g)(t)=g(t)
for all
t\in u^{-1}(0)
. In other words,
u+g
and
g
differ on a set of measure at most
k^{\prime}2^{-n}
. Part (ii) follows immediately from the definition.
∎

Lemma 4.8
.

If
k,k^{\prime}\geq 0
and
k+k^{\prime}>0
, then

(C(0,n;k)+\mathbf{1})\cap C(0,n;k^{\prime})=\varnothing.

Proof.

Observe that
(g+\mathbf{1})^{-1}(0)=g^{-1}(1)
. Thus every
g
in the intersection satisfies
\|g\|_{0}+\|g\|_{1}\geq\frac{1}{2}+k2^{-n}+\frac{1}{2}+k^{\prime}2^{-n}>1
, which is impossible.
∎

Lemma 4.9
.

Fix
k\in\mathbb{N}
. Then
\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{|C(0,n;k)|}{|G_{n}|}=\frac{1}{2}
.

The conclusion of the lemma can be written as

(4.1)

|C(0,n;k)|=\bigl(\tfrac{1}{2}+o(1)\bigr)|G_{n}|,

where
o(1)
is a quantity tending to
0
as

(with

Proof.

Identifying an element

with the collection of intervals
\{\tau\in\Omega_{n}:g_{\tau}=\mathbf{1}\}
, we see that

is equal to the number of subsets of

having cardinality at most

. Thus

|C(0,n;k)|=\tfrac{1}{2}\Bigl(2^{|\Omega_{n}|}-\sum_{j=-k+1}^{k-1}\binom{|\Omega_{n}|}{\tfrac{1}{2}|\Omega_{n}|-j}\Bigr)\geq\tfrac{1}{2}2^{|\Omega_{n}|}-2k\binom{|\Omega_{n}|}{|\Omega_{n}|/2},

as
\binom{|\Omega_{n}|}{|\Omega_{n}|/2}\geq\binom{|\Omega_{n}|}{t}
for all

. The standard binomial estimate
\binom{|\Omega_{n}|}{|\Omega_{n}|/2}=o(2^{|\Omega_{n}|})
yields

. Since

5.1.
Presentation of
\mathbb{F}_{p}^{\omega}

Fix a prime number
p
. Write the elements of
\mathbb{F}_{p}
as
0,1,\dots,p-1
. We continue to write
I
for the half-open unit interval
[0,1)
,
\mu
for Lebesgue measure on
I
, and
\Omega_{n}
for the collection of intervals
\{[\frac{j}{2^{n}},\frac{j+1}{2^{n}}):0\leq j\leq 2^{n}-1\}
. Let
G_{n}
be the group of functions
f:I\to\mathbb{F}_{p}
which are constant on every interval
\tau\in\Omega_{n}
, with the group operation of pointwise addition. Then
G_{n}
is isomorphic to
\mathbb{F}_{p}^{2^{n}}
, and has cardinality
p^{2^{n}}
. Let
G=\bigcup_{n=1}^{\infty}G_{n}
. Then
G
is a countably infinite vector space over
\mathbb{F}_{p}
, so

is isomorphic to
\mathbb{F}_{p}^{\omega}
.

Let
\mathbf{0}
denote the identity element of

,
\mathbf{1}
denote the constant function
\mathbf{1}(t)=1\in\mathbb{F}_{p}
for all
t\in I
, and for
y\in\mathbb{F}_{p}
, let
y\mathbf{1}
denote the constant function so that
y\mathbf{1}(t)=y
for all
t\in I
.

For
g\in G
and
x\in\mathbb{F}_{p}
, let
\|g\|_{x}:=\mu(g^{-1}(x))
. The following identity is crucial.

(5.1)

\|g+y\mathbf{1}\|_{x}=\|g\|_{x-y}\text{ for all }g\in G,x,y\in\mathbb{F}_{p}.

The identity follows from the equation
(g+y\mathbf{1})^{-1}(x)=g^{-1}(x-y)
.

For
k,n\in\mathbb{N}
, let
V(n,k):=\{g\in G_{n}:\|x\|_{1}\geq 1-k2^{-n}\}
.

Remark 5.1
.

We do not assume
p
is odd here, and in fact when
p=2
the construction of the sets
A
and
S
in this section will repeat the construction of
A
and
S
in §§
3
and
4
.

Lemma 5.2
.

Let
G
be a countable abelian group,
g\in G
,
S\subset G
dense in the Bohr topology, and
B\subset G
a piecewise Bohr set. Then
g+(B-B)\cap S\neq\varnothing
.

Proof.

There is a Bohr set
B^{\prime}
such that
B
contains a translate of every finite subset of
B^{\prime}
. Hence
B^{\prime}-B^{\prime}\subset B-B
. Since
B^{\prime}
is a Bohr set,
g+(B^{\prime}-B^{\prime})
is a Bohr neighborhood, so it has nonempty intersection with
S
.
∎

5.2.
Bohr topology of
\mathbb{F}_{p}^{\omega}
and Hamming balls

Since every nonzero element of
\mathbb{F}_{p}^{\omega}
has order
p
, every Bohr set is a finite intersection of kernels of homomorphisms
\rho:\mathbb{F}_{p}^{\omega}\to Z_{p}
, where
Z_{p}\subseteq\mathbb{C}
is the group of
p
th roots of unity. It follows that the Bohr topology on
\mathbb{F}_{p}^{\omega}
is the topology whose open sets are unions of cosets of finite index subgroups. This leads to the following observation.

Observation 5.3
.

A set
S\subseteq\mathbb{F}_{p}^{\omega}
is dense in the Bohr topology if and only if
\rho(S)=\rho(G)
for every surjective homomorphism
\rho
from
G
to a finite group.

Observation 5.4
.

If
E\subseteq\mathbb{F}_{p}^{k}
generates
\mathbb{F}_{p}^{k}
as an abelian group, then every element of
\mathbb{F}_{p}^{k}
is a sum of at most
pk
elements of
E
, since some subset of
E
must form a basis of
\mathbb{F}_{p}^{k}
as a vector space over
\mathbb{F}_{p}
.

In order to prove that unions of the
V(n,k)
are dense in the Bohr topology, we first consider Hamming balls in
\mathbb{F}_{p}^{n}
. For
x\in\mathbb{F}_{p}^{n}
, let
\operatorname{supp}(x):=\{i\leq n:x_{i}\neq 0\}
, and let
|x|
be the cardinality of
\operatorname{supp}(x)
. Let
H_{n,k}:=\{x\in\mathbb{F}_{p}^{n}:|x|\leq k\}
. We say that
S\subseteq\mathbb{F}_{p}^{n}
is
k
-Bohr dense
if for every surjective homomorphism
\rho:\mathbb{F}_{p}^{n}\to\mathbb{F}_{p}^{k}
,
\rho(S)=\mathbb{F}_{p}^{k}
.

Lemma 5.5
.

H_{n,pk}
is
k
-Bohr dense in
\mathbb{F}_{p}^{n}
.

Proof.

Let
\rho:\mathbb{F}_{p}^{n}\to\mathbb{F}_{p}^{k}
be a surjective homomorphism. Now
H_{n,1}
generates
\mathbb{F}_{p}^{n}
, so
\rho(H_{n,1})
generates
\mathbb{F}_{p}^{k}
, and Observation
5.4
guarantees that every element of
\mathbb{F}_{p}^{k}
is a sum of at most
pk
elements of
\rho(H_{n,1})
. In other words,
\mathbb{F}_{p}^{k}=\sum_{j=1}^{pk}\rho(H_{n,1})
. Since
\rho
is a homomorphism, the last set is equal to
\rho(H_{n,pk})
.
∎

Lemma 5.6
.

If
n_{j}
,
k_{j}\to\infty
, then

Proof.

By Observation

it suffices to show that

for every surjective homomorphism

be such a homomorphism. Then every nonzero element of

, so

for some

. Choose

and

∎

5.3.
Bias patterns

Let
\mathcal{S}:=\{S\subseteq\mathbb{F}_{p}:S\notin\{\varnothing,\mathbb{F}_{p}\}\}
. Then
\mathbb{F}_{p}
acts on
\mathcal{S}
by addition:
S+x:=\{s+x:s\in S\}
. The orbit of every
S
has cardinality
p
, since
p
is prime and
S+1\neq S
for all
S\in\mathcal{S}
. Fix, once and for all, a partition of
\mathcal{S}
into sets
\mathcal{S}_{x}
,
x\in\mathbb{F}_{p}
, such that
\mathcal{S}_{x+y}=\{S+y:S\in\mathcal{S}_{x}\}
for all
x,y\in\mathbb{F}_{p}
. We also insist that
\{0\}\in\mathcal{S}_{0}
. For example, with

, we can take

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}_{0}=\{\{0\},\{0,1\}\},\ \mathcal{S}_{1}=\{\{1\},\{1,2\}\},\ \mathcal{S}_{2}=\{\{2\},\{2,0\}\}.

For
S\in\mathcal{S}
and

let
\Bias_{n}(S,k)
be the set of
g\in G_{n}
such that

(5.2)

\displaystyle\|g\|_{x}

\displaystyle\geq\tfrac{1}{p}+k2^{-n}

\displaystyle\text{for all }x\in S

(5.3)

\displaystyle\|g\|_{x}

\displaystyle\leq\tfrac{1}{p}-k2^{-n}

\displaystyle\text{for all }x\in\mathbb{F}_{p}\setminus S.

Lemma 5.7
.

Let
n,k\in\mathbb{N}
,
y\in\mathbb{F}_{p}
.

(i)

\Bias_{n}(S,k)+y\mathbf{1}=\Bias_{n}(S+y,k)
.

(ii)

If

,
S\neq S^{\prime}\in\mathcal{S}
, then
\Bias_{n}(S,k)\cap\Bias_{n}(S^{\prime},k^{\prime})=\varnothing
.

Proof.

(i) We will show that
\Bias_{n}(S,k)+y\mathbf{1}\subseteq\Bias_{n}(S+y,k)
; the reverse containment follows by symmetry. Suppose
g\in\Bias_{n}(S,k)
and
y\in\mathbb{F}_{p}
. If
x\in S+y
, then
x-y\in S
, and Identity (
5.1
) implies
\|g+y\mathbf{1}\|_{x}=\|g\|_{x-y}\geq\frac{1}{p}+k2^{-n}
. Similarly, if
x\in\mathbb{F}_{p}\setminus(S+y)
, then
\|g+y\mathbf{1}\|_{x}\leq\frac{1}{p}-k2^{-n}
. Thus

.

To prove Part (ii), let
g
be in the intersection. If
S\neq S^{\prime}
, there is an
x
in the symmetric difference
S\triangle S^{\prime}
. For such

, Inequalities (
5.2
) and (

) must both be satisfied, which is impossible.
∎

, let

Note that our insistence that

Lemma 5.9
.

If

and

, then

Proof.

It suffices to show that
(\Bias_{n}(S,k)+x\mathbf{1})\cap\Bias_{n}(S^{\prime},k^{\prime})=\varnothing
for every

. Fix such

,
S^{\prime}
. Part (i) of Lemma

implies

Lemma 5.10
.

Fix
k\in\mathbb{N}
. Then
\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{|C(0,n;k)|}{|G_{n}|}=\frac{1}{p}
.

Proof.

For
x\in\mathbb{F}_{p}
, let
E_{n,x}:=C(0,n;k)+x\mathbf{1}
, and let
Z_{n}:=G_{n}\setminus\bigcup_{x\in\mathbb{F}_{p}}E_{n,x}
. By Lemma
5.9
, the sets
E_{n,x}
,
x\in\mathbb{F}_{p}
are mutually disjoint, and
|E_{n,x}|=|E_{n,y}|
for all
x,y\in\mathbb{F}_{p}
. It therefore suffices to show that
|Z_{n}|=o(|G_{n}|)
. Note that
Z_{n}
is the set of
g\in G_{n}
such that
\frac{1}{p}-k2^{-n}<\|g\|_{x}<\frac{1}{p}+k2^{-n}
for every

.

Let
M_{n,k}
be the largest value of
\binom{|\Omega_{n}|}{t}
where
\frac{1}{p}|\Omega_{n}|-k\leq t\leq\frac{1}{p}|\Omega_{n}|+k
. For such
t
and
y\in\mathbb{F}_{p}
, the number of
g\in G_{n}
satisfying
\|g\|_{y}=t2^{-n}
is at most

(p-1)^{|\Omega_{n}|-\lfloor\frac{1}{p}|\Omega_{n}|-k\rfloor}M_{n,k}.

Summing over all possible values of
t
and

, we get

|Z_{n}|\leq p(2k+1)(p-1)^{|\Omega_{n}|-\lfloor\frac{1}{p}|\Omega_{n}|-k\rfloor}M_{n,k}.

Estimating the binomial coefficients in the definition of

with Stirling’s formula, we find
|Z_{n}|=o(p^{|\Omega_{n}|})=o(|G_{n}|)
. ∎

For

Lemma 5.15

Let
0=n_{0}<n_{1}<n_{2}<\cdots<n_{l}
be an increasing sequence of integers and
k_{i},k_{i}^{\prime}>0
for each
i
. If
A:=\sum_{i=1}^{l}C(n_{i-1},n_{i};k_{i})
,
A^{\prime}:=\sum_{i=1}^{l}C(n_{i-1},n_{i};k_{i}^{\prime})
and
x\in\mathbb{F}_{p}\setminus\{0\}
, then
(A+x\mathbf{1})\cap A^{\prime}=\varnothing
.

Proof.

Induction on
l
. The base case
l=1
is Lemma
5.9
. Assume the lemma holds for the sequence
0=n_{0}<n_{1}<\cdots<n_{l-1}
and
k_{i}
,
k_{i}^{\prime}>0
. Assume, to get a contradiction, that
(A+x\mathbf{1})\cap A\neq\varnothing
. Then there are
c_{i}\in C(n_{i-1},c_{i};k_{i})
,
c_{i}^{\prime}\in C(n_{i-1},n_{i};k_{i}^{\prime})
such that

(5.6)

x\mathbf{1}+c_{1}+\cdots+c_{l}=c_{1}^{\prime}+\cdots+c_{l}^{\prime}

The left-hand side of Equation (
5.6
) is an sum of the form
g+h
, where
g=x\mathbf{1}+c_{1}+\cdots+c_{l-1}\in G_{n_{l-1}}
and
h=c_{l}\in C(n_{l-1},n_{l};k)
, and the right hand side has a similar form, as
g^{\prime}+h^{\prime}
, where
g^{\prime}=c_{1}^{\prime}+\cdots+c_{l-1}^{\prime}\in G_{n_{l-1}}
and
h^{\prime}=c_{l}^{\prime}\in C(n_{l-1},n_{l};k^{\prime})
. Both
c_{l}
and
c_{l}^{\prime}
are in
C(n_{l-1},n_{l};k^{\prime\prime})
, where
k^{\prime\prime}=\min(k,k^{\prime})
. Lemma
5.12
then implies
g=g^{\prime}
, so
x\mathbf{1}+c_{1}+\cdots+c_{l-1}=c_{1}^{\prime}+\cdots+c_{l-1}^{\prime}
. The last equation is impossible, by the induction hypothesis, and this is the desired contradiction. ∎

5.4.
Proof of Theorems
1.4
and
1.8

Fix
\varepsilon>0
. Apply Lemma
5.10
to choose
n_{1}
large enough that
|C(0,n_{1},3)|>\frac{1-\varepsilon}{p}|G_{n_{1}}|
. Apply Lemma
5.14
to choose a sequence of integers
n_{2}<n_{3}<\cdots
so that

(5.7)

|C(0,n_{1},3)+\cdots+C(n_{l-1},n_{l},3l)|>\frac{1-\varepsilon}{p}|G_{n_{l}}|

for each
l
. Set
B_{l}:=\sum_{j=1}^{l}C(n_{j-1},n_{j};3j)
. Let
R:=\{0,2,\dots,p-3\}\subseteq\mathbb{F}_{p}
, so that
|R|=\frac{p-1}{2}
. Let

A_{l}:=\bigcup_{x\in R}B_{l}+x\mathbf{1}.

Finally, let
A:=\bigcup_{l=1}^{\infty}A_{l}
. The sets
B_{l}+x\mathbf{1}
,
x\in\mathbb{F}_{p}
are mutually disjoint, by Lemma
5.15
, so
d^{*}(A)\geq|R|\frac{1-\varepsilon}{p}=\frac{p-1}{2p}(1-\varepsilon)
. Thus
d^{*}(A)\geq\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{2p}-\varepsilon
.

Let
S:=\bigcup_{j=1}^{\infty}V(n_{j},j)
. Lemma
5.6
implies
S
is dense in the Bohr topology of
G
.

Lemma
5.15
implies that
(A+S)\cap A=\varnothing
, via an argument similar to the proof of (
4.9
), so
(A-A)\cap S=\varnothing
, proving Theorem
1.4
. Likewise we have that
A^{\prime}:=A+S
satisfies
(A^{\prime}-A^{\prime})\cap S=\varnothing
. Thus
A^{\prime}
cannot be piecewise Bohr, as Lemma
5.2
guarantees
(B-B)\cap S\neq\varnothing
for every piecewise Bohr set
B
. This completes the proof of Theorem
1.8
.

Remark 5.18
.

We have two reasons for using the presentation of
\mathbb{F}_{2}^{\omega}
introduced in §
3
. The first is that we do not know how to prove Theorem
1.2
using the standard presentation. To elaborate: write an element of
\mathbb{F}_{2}^{\omega}
as
x=(x_{1},x_{2},\dots,)
, and let
|x|=|\{i:x_{i}=1\}|
. Let
F_{n}:=\{x\in\mathbb{F}_{2}^{\omega}:x_{i}=0\text{ for all }i>n\}
. The natural approach to proving Theorem
1.2
would be: let
(n_{j})_{j\in\mathbb{N}}
be a rapidly increasing sequence of integers, let
S_{j}\subseteq\mathbb{F}_{2}^{\omega}
,
S_{j}:=\{x\in F_{n_{j}}:|x|>n_{j}-j\}
. Then Lemma
4.3
implies every translate of

is chromatically intersective. One would then construct sets

having
|A_{l}|\approx\frac{1}{2}|F_{n_{l}}|
and
(A_{l}-A_{l})\cap S=\varnothing
for each
l
. We do not see how to construct such
A_{l}
, so we resorted to the presentation of
\mathbb{F}_{2}^{\omega}
given in §
3
.

Our second reason for using our preferred presentation is that it arises naturally in certain approaches to answer Question
5.17
for

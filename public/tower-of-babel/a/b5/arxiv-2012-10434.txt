Classification of Planar Graphs Associated to the Ideal of the Numerical Semigroup
Muhammad Ahsan Binyamin, Wajid Ali, Adnan Aslam, Hasan Mahmood

Abstract
. Let Λ \Lambda be a numerical semigroup and I ⊂ Λ I\subset\Lambda be an ideal of Λ \Lambda . The graph G I ​ ( Λ ) G_{I}(\Lambda) assigned to an ideal I I of Λ \Lambda is a graph with elements of ( Λ ∖ I ) ∗ (\Lambda\setminus I)^{*} as vertices and any two vertices x , y x,y are adjacent if and only if x + y ∈ I x+y\in I . A graph

is an ordered pair with the vertex set

is connected by a path. A graph

. The graph
G
is bipartite if its vertex set

and

or vice versa. If
|V_{1}(G)|=|V_{2}(G)|=m

is called a complete bipartite graph and is denoted by
K_{m,m}
. A planar graph is a graph that can be drawn in the plane without crossings that is, no two edges intersect geometrically except at a vertex to which both are incident. Two graphs
G
and

are said to be homeomorphic if both
G
and
H
can be obtained by a same graph by inserting vertices of degree
2
into its edges. It is well known that

and
K_{5}
are non-planar. In order to show that a graph is planar one can use the famous Kuratowski’s theorem which states that: A graph is planar if and only if it contains no subgraph homeomorphic to
K_{5}
or
K_{3,3}
. For more undefined terminologies related to graph theory see

17

2.
Planar and Non-planar Graphs
G_{I}(\Lambda)

Let
\Lambda=<A>
, where
A=\{a_{1},a_{2},\dots,a_{n}\}
is the minimal system of generators. Let
x\in\Lambda
, then

x=u_{1}a_{1}+u_{2}a_{2}+\dots+u_{n}a_{n},

where
u_{1},u_{2},\ldots,u_{n}
are non negative integers.
Note that
\Lambda
can be written as

\Lambda=\bigcup_{p=1}^{n}L^{(p)},

where
L^{(p)}
denote the collection of all those elements of
\Lambda
, which can be written as a linear combination of exactly
p
elements of
A
that is if
x\in L^{(p)}
then
x=u_{1}a_{i_{1}}+u_{2}a_{i_{2}}+\ldots+u_{p}a_{i_{p}},
where
a_{i_{1}},a_{i_{2}},\ldots,a_{i_{p}}\in A
and
u_{1},u_{2},\ldots,u_{p}
are positive integers. By using the above notation, for each
x\in\Lambda
, we define

L_{x}^{(p)}=\{\Sigma\in L^{(p)}:\Sigma=x\}.

Lemma 2.1
.

With the notations defined above, we have

B(x)\supseteq\{v_{1}a_{i_{1}}+v_{2}a_{i_{2}}+\dots+v_{p}a_{i_{p}}:0\leq v_{i}\leq u_{i},\quad a_{i_{1}},a_{i_{2}},\ldots,a_{i_{p}}\in A\}

and

|B(x)|\geq\sum\limits_{i=1}^{p}u_{i}+\sum\limits_{1\leq i_{1}<i_{2}\leq p}u_{i_{1}}u_{i_{2}}+\sum\limits_{1\leq i_{1}<i_{2}<i_{3}\leq p}u_{i_{1}}u_{i_{2}}u_{i_{3}}+\ldots+u_{1}u_{2}\cdots u_{p}.

Proof.

The proof of this Lemma follows from the definition of
B(x)
.
∎

The following Propositions provide us the bounds in term of

to compute the graph

of order 6 and 7.

Proposition 2.2
.

Let
\Lambda=<A>
be a numerical semigroup of embedding dimension
n\geq 2
. Then
\mid G_{I}(\Lambda)\mid\neq 6
, if one of the following holds:

(1)

L^{(p)}_{x}\neq\emptyset
for
p\geq 3

(2)

\mid L^{(1)}_{x}\mid\geq 3
.

(3)

L^{(2)}_{x}\neq\emptyset
and
L^{(1)}_{x}=\emptyset
.

(4)

\mid L^{(1)}_{x}\mid\geq 2
and
\mid L^{(2)}_{x}\mid\geq 2
.

Proposition 2.3
.

Let
\Lambda=<A>
be a numerical semigroup of embedding dimension
n\geq 2
. Then
\mid G_{I}(\Lambda)\mid\neq 7
, if one of the following hold:

(1)

L^{(p)}_{x}\neq\emptyset
for
p\geq 4
.

(2)

\mid L^{(3)}_{x}\mid\geq 2
.

(3)

\mid L^{(3)}_{x}\mid=1
and
L^{(1)}_{x}\neq\emptyset
or
L^{(2)}_{x}\neq\emptyset
.

(4)

\mid L^{(2)}_{x}\mid\geq 4
.

(5)

\mid L^{(2)}_{x}\mid=3
and
L^{(1)}_{x}\neq\emptyset
.

(6)

\mid L^{(2)}_{x}\mid=2
and
\mid L^{(1)}_{x}\mid\geq 2
.

(7)

\mid L^{(2)}_{x}\mid=1
and
\mid L^{(1)}_{x}\mid\geq 2
.

(8)

\mid L^{(1)}_{x}\mid\geq 3
.

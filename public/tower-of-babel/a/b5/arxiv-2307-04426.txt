1.
Introduction

The problem

(1.1)

-\Delta u=|u|^{4\over n-2}u+\lambda Vu\ \hbox{in}\ \Omega,\ u=0\ \hbox{on}\ \partial\Omega

where
\Omega
is a bounded regular domain in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
and
V\in C^{0}(\overline{\Omega})

was introduced by Brezis and Nirenberg in the pivotal paper
[
4
]
, where they address the existence of positive solutions
in the autonomous case, i.e. the potential
V
is constant. Since then, a huge aumont of work has been done.
In the following we will make a brief history highlighting the results which are much closer to the problem we wish to study in the present paper.

It is well known that existence and multiplicity of positive and sign-changing solutions to (
1.1
) is strictly affected by the geometry of the domain, the dimension of the euclidean space where the domain lies and the values of the parameter
\lambda
.
For example, if
\lambda\leq 0,

V
is constant and
\Omega
is a starshaped domain, problem (
1.1
) does not have any solutions.

A particular feature of problem (
1.1
), due to the critical behaviour of the non-linearity which appears on the R.H.S., is the possible existence of solutions which blow-up at one or more points in the domain as the parameter
\lambda
approaches
0
in dimensione
n\geq 4
or a positive number
\lambda_{*}
in dimension
n=3.
The description of the profile of the positive blowing-up solutions has been the subject of a wide literature.
Starting from the pioneering paper by Brezis and Peletier
[
5
]
where the authors consider the radial case, a lot of results have been obtained concerning the asymptotic profiles of solutions to (
1.1
). When
n\geq 4
, the first result is due to Han
[
9
]
and Rey
[
19
]
who study the profile of one-peak solutions in a general domain when the potential V is constant, while
the most recent one has been obtained by Konig and Laurin
[
10
]
who give an accurate description of the blow-up profile of a solution with multiple blow-up points in the non-autonomous case.
The 3-dimensional case has been firstly faced by Druet
[
7
]
in the case of one blow-up point and very recently by Konig and Laurain
[
11
]
in the presence of multiple blow-up points.

In particular, the asymptotic analysys of blowing-up solutions ensures that the blow-up points are nothing but the critical points of a suitable function which involves the function V, the Green’s function of
-\Delta
in
\Omega
with Dirichlet boundary condition and the Robin’s function.

While the behaviour of blowing-up positive solutions is by now clear, the profile of the sign-changing solutions is far being completely understood. As far as we know the complete scenario of sign-changing solutions has been obtained only in the radial case by Esposito, Ghossoub, Pistoia and Vaira in
[
8
]
in dimensions
n\geq 7
and by Amadori, Gladiali, Grossi, Pistoia and Varia
[
1
]

in lower dimensions.

A parallel and prolific field of research consists in finding blowing-up solutions whose profile is predicted by the asymptotic analysis developed in the previous papers. The first result is due to Rey
[
19
]

who build solutions blowing-up at a non-degenerate critical point of the Robin function when
V
is constant and
n\geq 5.
Successively, positive solutions blowing-up at multiple points have been constructed by Musso and Pistoia
[
15
]
. If
n\geq 5
in the non-autonomous case, solutions with one blow-up point have been found by Pistoia and Molle
[
13
]
, while Micheletti and Pistoia
[
12
]
build positive and sign-changing solutions with multiple blow-up points.

While all the blow-up points of positive solutions are always isolated and simple, in high dimensions
n\geq 7
sign-changing solutions can have
towering blow-up points
(see Premoselli
[
18
]
and Morabito-Pistoia-Vaira
[
14
]
) and clustering blow-up points (see Pistoia Vaira
[
17
]
).

In the 3-dimensional case, when
V
is constant, single and multiple blowing-up solutions have been built by del Pino, Dolbeault and Musso
[
6
]
and Musso and Salazar

[
16
]
, respectively.

Even if the problem has been widely studied in the last decades and a huge number of results has been produced, many problems are still open.

Here we focus on the problem (
1.1
) in dimension
n=4
:

(1.2)

\begin{cases}-\Delta u=u^{3}+\varepsilon Vu&\mbox{in }\Omega\\
u=0&\mbox{on }\partial\Omega\end{cases}

when
\Omega
is a bounded regular domain in
{\mathbb{R}^{4}}
,
V\in C^{1}(\Omega)\cap C^{0}(\overline{\Omega})

and we prove the existence of solutions which blows-up at a single point as the parameter
\epsilon
approaches zero.

More precisely, if
\tau
denotes the Robin function, our main result reads as follows.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
{\xi}_{0}\in\Omega
be a non-degenerate critical point of

f(\xi):={\tau(\xi)\over V(\xi)}

with
V(\xi_{0})>0.

If
\epsilon
is small enough there exists a solution of problem
(
1.2
) which blows-up at the point
\xi_{0}
as
\varepsilon\to 0
.

The proof relies on a classical Ljapunov-Schmidt procedure. The setting of the problem (see Section
2
) and the reduction process
(see Section
3
) can be carried out as usual. However, the last step in the procedure needs new ideas.
In fact, the rate of the error term is not small enough to argue as in the higher dimensional case and
the reduced problem is solved using some
local Pohozaev identities (see Section
4
).

Remarkably, differently from solutions with one blow-up point, in the case of multiple blow-up points it is harder to derive the concentration speed.
Indeed, it seems that they appear at the second order expansion and so to catch them a more accurate description of the ansatz is needed.
This will be the topic of a forthcoming paper in collaboration with Monica Musso.

2.
Setting of the problem

2.1.
The bubbles

All the positive solutions to the limit problem

-\Delta U=U^{3}\mbox{ in }{\mathbb{R}^{4}}

are the so called
bubbles
(see
[
2
,
20
]
)

U_{\delta,\xi}(x)=\frac{1}{\delta}U\left(x-\xi\over\delta\right),\ x,\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{4},\ \delta>0

where

U(x):=\mathfrak{c}\frac{1}{1+|x|^{2}},\ \mathfrak{c}:=2\sqrt{2}.

It is useful to introduce the projection of the bubble
U_{\delta,\xi}
onto
H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
, namely the solution of the problem

-\Delta PU_{\delta,\xi}=-\Delta U_{\delta,\xi}\ \mbox{ in }\Omega,\ PU_{\delta,\xi}=0\ \mbox{ on }\partial\Omega.

Let
G
be the Green function of
-\Delta
on
H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)
and
H
be its regular part, i.e.

G(x,y)=\frac{1}{2\omega}\frac{1}{|x-y|^{2}}-H(x,y)

where
\omega
denotes the measure of the unit sphere
S^{3}\subset{\mathbb{R}^{4}}
.
The Robin function is defined as
\tau(x)=H(x,x).

It is well known that

PU_{\delta,\xi}(x)=U_{\delta,\xi}(x)-\mathfrak{C}\delta H(x,\xi)+\mathcal{O}(\delta^{3}),\ \mathfrak{C}:=2\mathfrak{c}\omega

uniformly with respect to
x\in\Omega
and
\xi
in compact sets of
\Omega

and

PU_{\delta,\xi}(x)=\mathfrak{C}\delta G(x,\xi)+\mathcal{O}(\delta^{3})

uniformly with respect to
x
in compact sets of
\Omega\setminus\{\xi\}
and
\xi
in compact sets of
\Omega

2.2.
Some background material

Let
H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
be the Hilbert space equipped with the usual inner product and the usual norm

\langle u,v\rangle=\int_{\Omega}\nabla u\cdot\nabla v\ \hbox{and}\ \|u\|:=\|u\|_{H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)}=\left(\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u|^{2}\right)^{1/2}.

For
r\in[1,+\infty)
the space
L^{r}(\Omega)
is also equipped with the standard norm

\|u\|_{r}=\left(\int_{\Omega}|u|^{r}\right)^{\frac{1}{r}}.

Now, let us introduce
{\mathtt{i}}^{*}:L^{4/3}(\Omega)\to H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
as the adjoint operator of the embedding
\mathtt{i}:H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)\hookrightarrow L^{4}(\Omega)
, i.e.

u={\mathtt{i}}^{*}(f)
if and only if

\langle u,\varphi\rangle=\int_{\Omega}f(x)\varphi(x)dx\mbox{ for all }\varphi\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)

or equivalently

-\Delta u=f\mbox{ in }\Omega,\ u=0\mbox{ on }\partial\Omega

The operator
{\mathtt{i}}^{*}:L^{4/3}(\Omega)\to H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
is continuous as

\|{\mathtt{i}}^{*}(f)\|_{H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)}\leqslant S^{-1}\|f\|_{4/3}

where
S
is the best constant for the Sobolev embedding.

Therefore,
the problem (
1.2
) can be rewritten as

(2.1)

u={\mathtt{i}}^{*}(u^{3}+\varepsilon Vu),\quad u\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega).

2.3.
The ansatz

Let
k\geq 1
be a fixed integer. We look for a solution to (
1.2
) of the form

(2.2)

u=U_{\delta,\xi}+\phi,

where
U_{\delta,\xi}=PU_{\delta,\xi}
whose
blow-up rate is
\delta=\delta(\varepsilon)\to 0
and blow-up point is
\xi=\xi(\varepsilon)\in\Omega
.
Moreover, the lower order term

\phi
satisfies a set of orthogonal conditions. More precisely, let us consider the linear problem

-\Delta\psi=3U^{2}\psi\mbox{ in }{\mathbb{R}^{4}}.

It is well known
[
3
]
that the set of solutions is a
5-
dimensional linear space spanned by

\psi^{0}(x):=U(x)+\frac{1}{2}\nabla U(x)\cdot x=-\mathfrak{c}\frac{1-|x|^{2}}{(1+|x|^{2})^{2}}

and

\psi^{j}(x):=\frac{\partial U}{\partial x_{j}}(x)=-2\mathfrak{c}\frac{x_{j}}{(1+|x|^{2})^{2}},\ j=1,\dots,4.

For
j=0,1,2,3,4
we set

\psi_{\delta,\xi}^{j}(x)=\frac{1}{\delta}\psi^{j}\left(x-\xi\over\delta\right).

We introduce their projections
P\psi_{\delta,\xi}^{j}={\mathtt{i}}^{*}(3U_{\delta,\xi}^{2}\psi_{\delta,\xi}^{j})
onto
H^{1}_{0}(\Omega),
namely the solutions to the problem

-\Delta P\psi_{\delta,\xi}^{j}=-\Delta\psi_{\delta,\xi}^{j}=3U_{\delta,\xi}^{2}\psi_{\delta,\xi}^{j}\ \hbox{in}\ \Omega,\ P\psi_{\delta,\xi}^{j}=0\ \hbox{in}\ \partial\Omega.

Finally, we introduce the linear space

K_{{\delta},{\xi}}=\vspan\{P\psi_{\delta,\xi}^{j}\ |\ j=0,\cdots,4\}

and its orthogonal space

K_{\delta,\xi}^{\perp}=\{\phi\in H_{0}^{1}(\Omega):\langle\phi,P\psi_{\delta,\xi}^{j}\rangle=0,\mbox{ for all }j=0,\cdots,4\}.

The function
\phi
belongs to the space
K_{\delta,\xi}^{\perp}.

It is useful to remind the well known properties

P\psi^{j}_{\delta,\xi}=\psi^{j}_{\delta,\xi}-\delta^{2}\mathfrak{C}\partial_{\xi_{j}}H(x,\xi)+\mathcal{O}(\delta^{3}),\quad j=1,\ldots,4

uniformly for
x\in\Omega
and
\xi
in compact sets of
\Omega

and

P\psi^{0}_{\delta,\xi}=\psi^{0}_{\delta,\xi}-\delta\mathfrak{C}H(x,\xi)+\mathcal{O}(\delta^{2})

uniformly for
x
in compact sets of
\Omega\setminus\{\xi\}
and
\xi
in compact sets of
\Omega.

2.4.
An equivalent system

Let us introduce the linear projection

\Pi_{{\delta},{\xi}}:K\to K
and
\Pi_{{\delta},{\xi}}^{\perp}:K^{\perp}\to K^{\perp}
which are defined by

\Pi_{{\delta},{\xi}}(\phi)=\sum_{j=0,\cdots,4}\langle\phi,P\psi_{\delta,\xi}^{j}\rangle P\psi_{\delta,\xi}^{j}\qquad\hbox{and}\qquad\Pi_{{\delta},{\xi}}^{\perp}(\phi)=\phi-\Pi_{{\delta},{\xi}}(\phi).

Equation (
2.1
) can be rewritten as the following system of two equations

(2.3)

\Pi_{{\delta},{\xi}}^{\perp}\big[\mathcal{L}_{\delta,\xi}(\phi)-\mathcal{E}_{\delta,\xi}-\mathcal{N}_{\delta,\xi}(\phi)\big]=0

and

(2.4)

\Pi_{{\delta},{\xi}}\big[\mathcal{L}_{\delta,\xi}(\phi)-\mathcal{E}_{\delta,\xi}-\mathcal{N}_{\delta,\xi}(\phi)\big]=0,

where the linear operator
\mathcal{L}_{\delta,\xi}
is

\mathcal{L}_{\delta,\xi}(\phi)=\phi-{\mathtt{i}}^{*}\left(3\phi W_{{\delta},{\xi}}^{2}+\varepsilon\phi\right),

the error term
\mathcal{E}_{\delta,\xi}
is

\mathcal{E}_{\delta,\xi}={\mathtt{i}}^{*}\left(W_{{\delta},{\xi}}^{3}+\varepsilon W_{{\delta},{\xi}}\right)-W_{{\delta},{\xi}}

and the nonlinear term
\mathcal{N}_{\delta,\xi}
is

\mathcal{N}_{\delta,\xi}(\phi)={\mathtt{i}}^{*}\left(\phi^{3}+3\phi^{2}W_{{\delta},{\xi}}\right).

3.
Solving equation (
2.3
)

Given
\rho>0
small, let

\mathcal{O}_{\rho}=\left\{{\xi}\in\Omega\ |\ \dist(\xi,\partial\Omega)\geq\rho\right\}.

First of all, we estimate the error term
\mathcal{E}_{\delta,\xi}.

Lemma 3.1
.

For any
\rho>0
small enough there exist
\varepsilon_{0}>0
and
c>0
such that for any
\xi\in\mathcal{O}_{\rho}
and for any
\varepsilon,\delta\in(0,\varepsilon_{0})
it holds

\|\mathcal{E}_{\delta,\xi}\|\lesssim|{\delta}|^{2}+\varepsilon|{\delta}|.

Proof.

First of all, we remark that

W_{{\delta},{\xi}}={\mathtt{i}}^{*}\left(U_{\delta,\xi}^{3}\right).
Then by a straightforward computation

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{E}_{\delta,\xi}\|

\displaystyle\lesssim\|(PU_{\delta,\xi})^{3}-U_{\delta,\xi}^{3}\|_{\frac{4}{3}}+\varepsilon\|V\|_{\infty}\|PU_{\delta,\xi}\|_{\frac{4}{3}}

where

\displaystyle\|PU_{\delta,\xi}^{3}-U_{\delta,\xi}^{3}\|

\displaystyle\lesssim\delta\|U_{\delta,\xi}^{2}\|_{\frac{4}{3}}+\delta^{2}\|U_{\delta,\xi}\|_{\frac{4}{3}}+\delta^{3}\lesssim\delta^{2}.

and
\|PU_{\delta,\xi}\|_{\frac{4}{3}}=\mathcal{O}(\delta)
. ∎

Next, we state the invertibility of the linear operator
\mathcal{L}_{\delta,\xi}.

Lemma 3.2
.

For any
\rho>0
small enough there exist
\varepsilon_{0}>0
and
c>0
such that for any
\varepsilon,\delta\in(0,\varepsilon_{0}),

i=1,\cdots,k
, and for any
{\xi}\in\mathcal{O}_{\rho}

\|\left(\Pi^{\perp}_{\delta,\xi}\circ\mathcal{L}_{\delta,\xi}\right)(\phi)\|\geq c\|\phi\|\mbox{ for all }\phi\in K_{\delta,\xi}^{\perp}.

Furthermore, the operator
\Pi^{\perp}_{\delta,\xi}\circ\mathcal{L}_{\delta,\xi}
is invertible and its inverse is continuous.

Proof.

We omit the proof because it is enough to apply the arguments used in the proof of Lemma 1.7 in
[
15
]
to the
4-
dimensional case.
∎

Finally, using a classical fixed point argument, we can solve equation (
2.3
).

Proposition 3.3
.

For any
\rho>0
small enough there exists
\varepsilon_{0}>0
such that
for any
\varepsilon,\delta\in(0,\varepsilon_{0})
and
\xi\in\mathcal{O}_{\rho}
, there exists a unique
\phi=\phi_{\delta,\xi}\in K_{\delta,\xi}^{\perp}
solving
(
2.3
), which also satisfies

(3.1)

\|\phi\|\lesssim{\delta}^{2}+\varepsilon{\delta}.

4.
Solving equation (
2.4
)

Let
u=U_{\delta,\xi}+\phi
(see (
2.2
)). By (
2.3
) we deduce that

(4.1)

-\Delta u-u^{3}-\epsilon Vu=\sum\limits_{j=0,\dots,4}c^{j}U_{\delta,\xi}^{2}\psi_{\delta,\xi}^{j}

for some real numbers
c^{j}=c^{j}(\delta,\xi)
. Then
solving equation (
2.4
) is equivalent to find
(\delta,\xi)
such that the
c^{j}
’s are zero.

First of all, we prove a sufficient condition which ensures that all the
c^{j}
’s in (
4.1
) are zero.
Set
\partial_{j}u:={\partial u\over\partial x_{j}}.

Lemma 4.1
.

If

(4.2)

\int\limits_{\Omega}(-\Delta u-u^{3}-\epsilon Vu)\psi_{\delta,\xi}^{0}=0

and for some
\eta>0

(4.3)

\int\limits_{B(\xi,\eta)}(-\Delta u-u^{3}-\epsilon Vu)\partial_{j}u=0,\quad\mbox{ for all }j=1,2,3,4

then
c^{\ell}=0
for any
\ell=0,1,\dots,4.

Proof.

By (
4.2
) and (
4.3
)

\sum\limits_{\ell=0}^{4}c^{\ell}\int\limits_{\Omega}U_{\delta,\xi}^{2}\psi_{\delta,\xi}^{\ell}\psi_{\delta,\xi}^{0}=\sum\limits_{\ell=0}^{4}c^{\ell}\int\limits_{B(\xi,\eta)}U_{\delta,\xi}^{2}\psi_{\delta,\xi}^{\ell}\partial_{j}u=0\quad\mbox{ for all }j=0,\dots,4

and the linear system in the
c^{\ell}
’s is diagonally dominant. Indeed

\int\limits_{\Omega}U_{\delta,\xi}^{2}\psi_{\delta,\xi}^{\ell}\psi_{\delta,\xi}^{0}=\begin{cases}a+\mathcal{O}(\delta^{4})&\mbox{if }\ell=0\\
\mathcal{O}(\delta^{5})&\mbox{if }\ell=1,\cdots,4\\
\end{cases}

for some constant
a\not=0.

Moreover,

\int\limits_{B(\xi,\eta)}U_{\delta,\xi}^{2}\psi_{\delta,\xi}^{\ell}\partial_{j}u=\begin{cases}\frac{1}{\delta}b+\mathcal{O}(\varepsilon)&\mbox{if }\ell=j\\
\mathcal{O}(\varepsilon)&\mbox{if }\ell\neq j\\
\end{cases}

for some constant
b\not=0,
because by (
2.2
) and (
3.1
),

\int\limits_{B(\xi_{i},\eta_{i})}U_{\delta,\xi}^{2}\psi_{\delta,\xi}^{\ell}\partial_{j}\phi=\mathcal{O}(\|\phi\|)\left(\int_{B(\xi,\eta)}|U_{\delta,\xi}\psi_{\delta,\xi}^{\ell}|^{2}\right)^{1/2}=\mathcal{O}(\varepsilon)\quad\mbox{for all }\ell=0,\cdots,4

and

\int\limits_{B(\xi,\eta)}U_{\delta,\xi}^{2}\psi_{\delta,\xi}^{\ell}\partial_{j}PU_{\delta,\xi}=\begin{cases}\frac{1}{\delta}b+\mathcal{O}(\delta^{2})&\mbox{if }\ell=j\\
\mathcal{O}(\delta^{2})&\mbox{if }\ell\neq j\\
\end{cases}

∎

Next, we write the first order term of (
4.2
).

Lemma 4.2
.

For any
\rho>0
small enough

(4.4)

\int\limits_{\Omega}(-\Delta u-u^{3}-\epsilon V(x)u)\psi_{\delta,\xi}^{0}=\mathfrak{C}^{2}\delta^{2}\tau(\xi)+\varepsilon\delta^{2}\ln\delta\mathfrak{c}^{2}\omega V(\xi)+o(\delta^{2}),

as
\varepsilon,\delta\to 0
, uniformly respect to
\xi\in\mathcal{O}_{\rho}
.

Proof.

We point out that

\displaystyle\int\limits_{\Omega}(-\Delta u-u^{3}-\epsilon Vu)\psi_{\delta,\xi}^{0}

\displaystyle=\underbrace{\int\limits_{\Omega}\left(-\Delta U_{\delta,\xi}-U_{\delta,\xi}^{3}-\epsilon U_{\delta,\xi}\right)\psi_{\delta,\xi}^{0}}_{=:I_{1}}

\displaystyle+\underbrace{\int\limits_{\Omega}\left(-\Delta\phi-3U_{\delta,\xi}^{2}\phi\right)\psi_{\delta,\xi}^{0}}_{=:I_{2}}

\displaystyle+\underbrace{\int\limits_{\Omega}\left(\phi^{3}+3U_{\delta,\xi}\phi^{2}\right)\psi_{\delta,\xi}^{0}}_{=:I_{3}}\

First of all, let us prove that

(4.5)

I_{1}=\delta^{2}\mathfrak{C}^{2}H(\xi,\xi)+\varepsilon\delta^{2}\ln\delta\mathfrak{c}^{2}\omega V(\xi)+o(\delta^{2}).

We observe that

\displaystyle I_{1}

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}\left[U_{\delta,\xi}^{3}-PU_{\delta,\xi}^{3}-\varepsilon V(x)PU_{\delta,\xi}\right]\psi_{\delta,\xi}^{0}

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}(U_{\delta,\xi}^{3}-PU_{\delta,\xi}^{3})\psi_{\delta,\xi}^{0}-\varepsilon\int_{\Omega}V(x)PU_{\delta,\xi}\psi_{\delta,\xi}^{0}.

Now

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}(U_{\delta,\xi}^{3}-PU_{\delta,\xi}^{3})

\displaystyle\psi_{\delta,\xi}^{0}=3\int_{B(\xi,\rho)}U_{\delta,\xi}^{2}\underbrace{(U_{\delta,\xi}-PU_{\delta,\xi})}_{\delta\mathfrak{C}H(x,\xi)+\mathcal{O}(\delta^{3})}\psi_{\delta,\xi}^{0}+\mathcal{O}(\delta^{3})

\displaystyle=3\delta\mathfrak{C}\int_{\Omega}H(x,\xi)U_{\delta,\xi}^{2}\psi_{\delta,\xi}^{0}+\mathcal{O}(\delta^{3})

\displaystyle=3\delta^{4}\mathfrak{C}\mathfrak{c}^{3}\int_{B(\xi,\rho)}H(x,\xi)\frac{|x-\xi|^{2}-\delta^{2}}{\left(\delta^{2}+|x-\xi|^{2}\right)^{4}}+o(\delta^{2})

\displaystyle=3\delta^{2}\mathfrak{C}\mathfrak{c}^{3}\int_{B(0,\rho/\delta)}\underbrace{H(\xi+\delta t,\xi)}_{H(\xi,\xi)+\mathcal{O}(\delta)}\frac{|t|^{2}-1}{(1+|t|^{2})^{4}}+o(\delta^{2})

\displaystyle=\delta^{2}\mathfrak{C}^{2}H(\xi,\xi)+o(\delta^{2})

because

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{4}}\frac{|t|^{2}-1}{(1+|t|^{2})^{4}}=\frac{\omega}{12},

and

\displaystyle\varepsilon\int_{\Omega}V(x)PU_{\delta,\xi}\psi_{\delta,\xi}^{0}

\displaystyle=\varepsilon\delta^{2}\mathfrak{c}^{2}\int_{B(\xi,\rho)}V(x)\frac{|x-\xi|^{2}-\delta^{2}}{(\delta^{2}+|x-\xi|^{2})^{3}}+\mathcal{O}(\delta^{3})

\displaystyle=\varepsilon\delta^{2}\mathfrak{c}^{2}\int_{B(0,\rho/\delta)}V(\delta t+\xi)\frac{|y|^{2}-1}{(1+|y|^{2})^{3}}+\mathcal{O}(\delta^{3})

\displaystyle=-\varepsilon\delta^{2}\ln\delta\mathfrak{c}^{2}\omega V(\xi)+o(\delta^{2}\varepsilon\ln\delta).

Let us estimate the other terms.
It is important to point out the estimate

\int_{\partial\Omega}|\partial_{\nu}\phi|^{2}=o(\delta^{2})

proved in
[
19
]
.
Then, recalling that
-\Delta\psi_{\delta,\xi}^{0}=3U_{i}^{2}\psi_{\delta,\xi}^{0}
, for all
i=1\cdots,k
, we have

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}(-\Delta\phi)\psi_{\delta,\xi}^{0}

\displaystyle=\underbrace{\int_{\Omega}\phi(-\Delta\psi_{\delta,\xi}^{0})}_{=3\int_{\Omega}\phi U_{i}^{2}\psi_{\delta,\xi}^{0}}+\int_{\partial\Omega}\underbrace{\phi}_{=0}\nabla\psi_{\delta,\xi}^{0}\cdot\nu-\int_{\partial\Omega}\underbrace{\psi_{\delta,\xi}^{0}}_{=\mathcal{O}(\delta)}\nabla\phi\cdot\nu

\displaystyle=3\int_{\Omega}\phi U_{i}^{2}\psi_{\delta,\xi}^{0}+o(|\delta|^{2}).

Therefore,

(4.6)

\displaystyle|I_{2}|

\displaystyle\lesssim 3\int_{\Omega}\phi|PU_{\delta,\xi}^{2}-U_{\delta,\xi}^{2}||\psi_{\delta,\xi}^{0}|+\varepsilon\int_{\Omega}|\phi||\psi_{\delta,\xi}^{0}|+o(|\delta|^{2})

\displaystyle\lesssim\|\phi\|_{4}\|(PU_{\delta,\xi}^{2}-U_{i}^{2})\psi_{\delta,\xi}^{0}\|_{4/3}+\varepsilon\|\phi\|_{4}\|\psi_{\delta,\xi}^{0}\|_{4/3}=o\left(\delta^{2}\right).

and

(4.7)

\displaystyle|I_{3}|

\displaystyle\leqslant\int_{\Omega}\left[|\phi|^{3}+3|\phi|^{2}|PU_{\delta,\xi}|\right]|\psi_{\delta,\xi}^{0}|

\displaystyle\lesssim\|\phi\|^{3}\|\psi_{\delta,\xi}^{0}\|_{4}+\|\phi\|^{2}\|PU_{\delta,\xi}\psi_{\delta,\xi}^{0}\|_{2}=o(\delta^{2}).

Finally, (
4.4
) follows by (
4.5
), (
4.6
) and (
4.7
).
∎

Finally, we write the first order term of (
4.3
).

Lemma 4.3
.

For any
\rho>0,
there exists
\eta_{i}>0
such that

\int\limits_{B(\xi_{i},\eta_{i})}(-\Delta u-u^{3}-\epsilon Vu)\partial_{j}u=-\frac{1}{2}\delta^{2}\left[\mathfrak{C}^{2}{\partial_{j}}\tau(\xi)+(\varepsilon\ln\delta)\omega\mathfrak{c}^{2}{\partial_{j}}V(\xi)\right]+o\left(\delta^{2}\right),

as
\varepsilon,\delta\to 0
, uniformly respect to
\xi\in\mathcal{O}_{\rho}
.

Proof.

First of all, we point out that

(4.8)

\int\limits_{B(\xi_{i},\eta_{i})}(-\Delta u-u^{3})\partial_{j}u=\int\limits_{\partial B(\xi_{i},\eta_{i})}\left(-\partial_{\nu}u\partial_{j}u+\frac{1}{2}|\nabla u|^{2}\nu_{j}-\frac{1}{4}u^{4}\nu_{j}\right)

By Lemma
4.4
and (
3.1
), we choose
\eta_{i}
such that

(4.9)

\int_{\partial B(\xi_{i},\eta_{i})}\left(|\nabla\phi|^{2}+|\phi|^{4}\right)\lesssim\delta^{4}.

Now, by (
2.2
)

(4.10)

U_{\delta,\xi}(x):={\delta\mathfrak{c}\over|x-\xi|^{2}}-\mathfrak{C}\delta H(x,\xi)+\mathcal{O}\left(|\delta|^{2}\right)

C^{1}-
uniformly on
\partial B(\xi,\eta).

It is crucial to point out that the function
H(x,\xi)
is harmonic.
Therefore, by (
4.8
), (
4.9
) and (
4.10
)

\displaystyle\int\limits_{B(\xi,\eta)}(-\Delta u-u^{3})\partial_{j}u

\displaystyle=

Lemma 4.4
.

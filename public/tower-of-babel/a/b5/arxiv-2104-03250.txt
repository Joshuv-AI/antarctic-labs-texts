Kato’s irrreducibility criterion for Kac-Moody groups over local fields
Auguste Hébert

Abstract
In 2014, Braverman, Kazhdan, Patnaik and Bardy-Panse, Gaussent and Rousseau associated Iwahori-Hecke algebras to Kac-Moody groups over non-Archimedean local fields. In a previous paper, we defined and studied their principal series representations. In 1982, Kato provided an irreducibility criterion for these representations, in the reductive case. We had obtained partially this criterion in the Kac-Moody case. In this paper, we prove this criterion in the Kac-Moody case.



1
Introduction

1.1
The reductive case

Let
G
be a split reductive group over a non-Archimedean local field
\mathcal{K}
.
To each open compact subgroup
K
of
G
is associated the Hecke algebra
\mathcal{H}_{K}
. This is the algebra of functions from
G
to
\mathbb{C}
which have compact support and are
K
-bi-invariant. There exists a strong link between the smooth representations of
G
and the representations of the Hecke algebras of
G
. Let
K_{I}
be the Iwahori subgroup of
G
. Then the Hecke algebra
\mathcal{H}_{\mathbb{C}}
associated with
K_{I}
is called the Iwahori-Hecke algebra of
G
and plays an important role in the representation theory of
G
.

Let
T
be a maximal split torus of
G
and
Y
be the cocharacter lattice of
(G,T)
. Let
B
be a Borel subgroup of
G
containing
T
. Let
T_{\mathbb{C}}=\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathrm{Gr}}(Y,\mathbb{C}^{*})
and
\tau\in T_{\mathbb{C}}
. Then
\tau
can be extended to a character
\tau:B\rightarrow\mathbb{C}^{*}
. If
\tau\in T_{\mathbb{C}}
, the principal series representation
I(\tau)
of
G
is the induction of
\tau\delta^{1/2}
from
B
to
G
, where
\delta:B\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{*}_{+}
is the modulus character of
B
. More explicitly, this is the space of locally constant functions
f:G\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
such that
f(bg)=\tau\delta^{1/2}(b)f(g)
for every
g\in G
and
b\in B
. Then
G
acts on
I(\tau)
by right translation.

Let
W^{v}
be the vectorial Weyl group of
(G,T)
. By the Bernstein-Lusztig relations,
\mathcal{H}_{\mathbb{C}}
admits a basis
(Z^{\lambda}*T_{w})_{\lambda\in Y,w\in W^{v}}
such that
\bigoplus_{\lambda\in Y}\mathbb{C}Z^{\lambda}
is a subalgebra of
\mathcal{H}_{\mathbb{C}}
isomorphic to the group algebra
\mathbb{C}[Y]
of
Y
. We identify
\bigoplus_{\lambda\in Y}\mathbb{C}Z^{\lambda}
and
\mathbb{C}[Y]
. We regard
\tau
as an algebra morphism
\tau:\mathbb{C}[Y]\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
. Then the algebra
\mathcal{H}_{\mathbb{C}}
acts on
I_{\tau,G}:=I(\tau)^{K_{I}}
,
I(\tau)
is irreducible as a representation of
G
if and only
I_{\tau,G}
is irreducible as a representation of
\mathcal{H}_{\mathbb{C}}
and
I_{\tau,G}
is isomorphic to the induced representation
I_{\tau}=\mathrm{Ind}_{\mathbb{C}[Y]}^{\mathcal{H}_{\mathbb{C}}}(\tau)
.

Matsumoto and Kato gave criteria for the irreducibility of
I_{\tau}
. The group
W^{v}
acts on
Y
and thus it acts on
T_{\mathbb{C}}
. If
\tau\in T_{\mathbb{C}}
, we denote by
W_{\tau}
the stabilizer of
\tau
in
W^{v}
. Denote by
q
be the residue cardinal of
\mathcal{K}
. Let
W_{(\tau)}
be the subgroup of
W_{\tau}
generated by the reflections
r_{\alpha^{\vee}}
, for
\alpha^{\vee}\in\Phi^{\vee}
such that
\tau(\alpha^{\vee})=1
, where
\Phi^{\vee}
stands for the coroot lattice of
G
. Then Kato proved the following theorem (see
[
Kat81
, Theorem 2.4]
):

Theorem 1
.

Let
\tau\in T_{\mathbb{C}}
. Then
I_{\tau}
is irreducible if and only if it satisfies the following conditions:

1.

W_{\tau}=W_{(\tau)}
,

2.

for all
\alpha^{\vee}\in\Phi^{\vee}
,
\tau(\alpha^{\vee})\neq q
.

When
\tau
is
regular
, that is when
W_{\tau}=\{1\}
, condition (
1
) is satisfied and this is a result by Matsumoto (see
[
Mat77
, Théorème 4.3.5]
).

1.2
The Kac-Moody case

Let
G
be a split Kac-Moody group over a non-Archimedean local field
\mathcal{K}
. We do not know which topology on
G
could replace the usual topology on reductive groups over
\mathcal{K}
. There is up to now no definition of smoothness for the representations of
G
. However one can define certain Hecke algebras in this framework. In
[
BK11
]
and
[
BKP16
]
, Braverman, Kazhdan and Patnaik defined the spherical Hecke algebra and the Iwahori-Hecke
\mathcal{H}_{\mathbb{C}}
of
G
when
G
is affine. In
[
GR14
]
and
[
BPGR16
]
, Bardy-Panse, Gaussent and Rousseau generalized these constructions to the case where
G
is a general Kac-Moody group. They achieved this construction by using masures (also known as hovels), which are analogous to Bruhat-Tits buildings (see
[
GR08
]
).

Let
B
be a positive Borel subgroup of
G
and
T
be a maximal split torus of
G
contained in
B
. Let
Y
be the cocharacter lattice of
G
,
W^{v}
be the Weyl group of
G
and
Y^{++}
be the set of dominant cocharacters of
Y
. The Bruhat decomposition does not hold on
G
: if
G
is not reductive,

G^{+}:=\bigsqcup_{\lambda\in Y^{++}}K_{I}\lambda K_{I}\subsetneq G.

The set
G^{+}
is a sub-semi-group of
G
. Then
\mathcal{H}_{\mathbb{C}}
is defined to be the set of functions from
K_{I}\backslash G^{+}/K_{I}
to
\mathbb{C}
which have finite support. The Iwahori-Hecke algebra
\mathcal{H}_{\mathbb{C}}
of
G
admits a Bernstein-Lusztig presentation but it is no longer indexed by
Y
. Let
Y^{+}=\bigcup_{w\in W^{v}}w.Y^{++}\subset Y
. Then
Y^{+}
is the
integral Tits cone
and we have
Y^{+}=Y
if and only
G
is reductive. The
Bernstein-Lusztig-Hecke algebra of
G
is the space
{{}^{\mathrm{BL}}\mathcal{H}}_{\mathbb{C}}=\bigoplus_{w\in W^{v}}\mathbb{C}[Y]*T_{w}
subject to to some relations (see subsection
2.2
). Then
\mathcal{H}_{\mathbb{C}}
is isomorphic to
\bigoplus_{w\in W^{v}}\mathbb{C}[Y^{+}]*T_{w}
.

Let
\tau\in T_{\mathbb{C}}=\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathrm{Gr}}(Y,\mathbb{C}^{*})
. In
[
Héb21b
, 6]
we defined the space
\widehat{I(\tau)}
of functions
f
from
G
to
\mathbb{C}
such that for all
g\in G
,
b\in B
, we have
f(bg)=\tau\delta^{1/2}(b)f(g)
. As we do not know which condition could replace
“locally constant”, we do not impose any regularity condition on the functions of
\widehat{I(\tau)}
. Then
G
acts by right translation on
\widehat{I(\tau)}
.
Let
I_{\tau,G}
be the subspace of functions
f\in\widehat{I(\tau)}
which are invariant under the action of
K_{I}
and whose support satisfy some finiteness conditions. We defined an action of
\mathcal{H}_{\mathbb{C}}
on
I_{\tau,G}
. This actions extends to an action of
{{}^{\mathrm{BL}}\mathcal{H}}_{\mathbb{C}}
on
I_{\tau,G}
and is isomorphic to the induced representation
I_{\tau}=\mathrm{Ind}_{\mathbb{C}[Y]}^{{{}^{\mathrm{BL}}\mathcal{H}}_{\mathbb{C}}}(\tau)
. Moreover the
{{}^{\mathrm{BL}}\mathcal{H}}_{\mathbb{C}}
-submodules of
I_{\tau}
are exactly the
\mathcal{H}_{\mathbb{C}}
-submodules of
I_{\tau}
(see
[
Héb21a
, Proposition 3.1]
) and thus we regard
I_{\tau}
as a
{{}^{\mathrm{BL}}\mathcal{H}}_{\mathbb{C}}
-module. We then obtained a weak version of Theorem
1
: we obtained one implication and we proved the equivalence only under the assumption that the Kac-Moody matrix defining
G
has size
2
(see
[
Héb21b
, Theorem 3 and 4]
). In this paper, we prove Theorem
1
in a full generality (see Corollary
4.8
).

Basic ideas of the proof

Let us explain the basic ideas of our proof. We have
I_{\tau}=\bigoplus_{w\in W^{v}}\mathbb{C}T_{w}.\mathbf{v}_{\tau}
, where
\mathbf{v}_{\tau}\in I_{\tau}
is such that
\theta.\mathbf{v}_{\tau}=\tau(\theta).\mathbf{v}_{\tau}
for
\theta\in\mathbb{C}[Y]
. For
w\in W^{v}
, one sets

I_{\tau}(w.\tau)=\{x\in I_{\tau}|\theta.x=w.\tau(\theta).x,\ \forall\theta\in\mathbb{C}[Y]\}.

By the Frobenius reciprocity,
\mathrm{Hom}_{{{}^{\mathrm{BL}}\mathcal{H}}_{\mathbb{C}}-\mathrm{mod}}(I_{\tau},I_{w.\tau})
is isomorphic to
I_{\tau}(w.\tau)
as a vector space. Let
\mathcal{U}_{\mathbb{C}}
be the set of
\tau\in T_{\mathbb{C}}
such that
\tau(\alpha^{\vee})\neq q
for all
\alpha^{\vee}\in\Phi^{\vee}
. Let
\tau\in\mathcal{U}_{\mathbb{C}}
. In
[
Héb21b
, Theorem 4.8]
we proved that
I_{\tau}
is irreducible if and only if
\mathrm{End}_{{{}^{\mathrm{BL}}\mathcal{H}}_{\mathbb{C}}-\mathrm{mod}}(I_{\tau})=\mathbb{C}.\mathrm{Id}
, if and only if
I_{\tau}(\tau)=\mathbb{C}\mathbf{v}_{\tau}
.

In order to study
I_{\tau}(w.\tau)
, for
w\in W^{v}
, it is convenient to introduce
{{}^{\mathrm{BL}}\mathcal{H}}(T_{\mathbb{C}})=\bigoplus_{w\in W^{v}}T_{w}*\mathbb{C}(Y)
. The elements of
{{}^{\mathrm{BL}}\mathcal{H}}(T_{\mathbb{C}})
can be regarded as rational functions from an open subset of
T_{\mathbb{C}}
to
\mathcal{H}_{W^{v},\mathbb{C}}=\bigoplus_{w\in W^{v}}\mathbb{C}T_{w}
. Following Reeder, we introduced elements
F_{w}\in{{}^{\mathrm{BL}}\mathcal{H}}(T_{\mathbb{C}})
,
w\in W^{v}
, such that for all
\chi\in T_{\mathbb{C}}
for which
F_{w}(\chi)
is well defined,
F_{w}(\chi).\mathbf{v}_{\chi}\in I_{\chi}(w.\chi)
. The group
W_{\tau}
decomposes as
W_{\tau}=R_{\tau}\ltimes W_{(\tau)}
, where
R_{\tau}
is some subgroup of
W_{\tau}
called the
R
-group. If
w_{R}\in R_{\tau}
, then
F_{w_{R}}
has no pole at
\tau
and thus
F_{w_{R}}(\tau).\mathbf{v}_{\tau}
corresponds to an element
\psi_{w_{r}}
of
\mathrm{End}(I_{\tau})
. For
w\in W_{(\tau)}
however,
F_{w}
has poles at
\tau
and thus describing
I_{\tau}(\tau)
requires some works. Inspired by works of Reeder and Keys in the reductive case, (
[
Ree97
]
and
[
Key82
]
), we determined

I_{\tau}(\tau,\mathrm{gen})=\{x\in I_{\tau}|\forall\theta\in\mathbb{C}[Y],\forall n\gg 0,\big(\theta-\tau(\theta)\big)^{n}.x=0\}.

We proved (see
[
Héb21a
, Proposition 5.13]
) that

I_{\tau}(\tau,\mathrm{gen})=\bigoplus_{w_{R}\in R_{\tau}}\psi_{w_{R}}\left(I_{\tau}(\tau,\mathrm{gen},W_{(\tau)})\right),

where
I_{\tau}(\tau,\mathrm{gen},W_{(\tau)}):=\left({{}^{\mathrm{BL}}\mathcal{H}}_{\mathbb{C}}\cap\bigoplus_{w\in W_{(\tau)}}F_{w}*\mathbb{C}(Y)\right).\mathbf{v}_{\tau}
corresponds to the “
W_{(\tau)}
-part” of
I_{\tau}(\tau,\mathrm{gen})
. We deduced that

I_{\tau}(\tau)=\bigoplus_{w_{R}\in R_{\tau}}\psi_{w_{R}}\left(I_{\tau}(\tau)\cap I_{\tau}(\tau,\mathrm{gen},W_{(\tau)})\right).

It then remained to prove that
I_{\tau}(\tau,\mathrm{gen},W_{(\tau)})\cap I_{\tau}(\tau)=\mathbb{C}\mathbf{v}_{\tau}
, which we achieve in this paper (see Theorem
4.6
). Note that by our description of
I_{\tau}(\tau,\mathrm{gen},W_{(\tau)})
, proving that
I_{\tau}(\tau)\cap I_{\tau}(\tau,\mathrm{gen},W_{(\tau)})=\mathbb{C}\mathbf{v}_{\tau}
can more or less be reduced to proving that
I_{\mathds{1}}(\mathds{1})=\mathbb{C}\mathbf{v}_{\mathds{1}}
, where
I_{\mathds{1}}=\mathrm{Ind}_{\mathbb{C}(Y)_{\tau}}^{\mathscr{K}_{\tau}}(\mathds{1})
,
\mathbb{C}(Y)_{\tau}
is the subset of
\mathbb{C}(Y)
consisting of the elements which have no pole at
\tau
,
\mathds{1}:Y\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
is the constant function equal to
1
and
\mathscr{K}_{\tau}\subset\bigoplus_{w\in W^{v}}T_{w}*\mathbb{C}(Y)_{\tau}
is some kind of Bernstein-Lusztig-Hecke algebra associated with
\tau
(see subsection
3.1
for the definition of
\mathscr{K}_{\tau}
).

We then deduce:

Corollary 2
.

(see Corollary
4.7
and Corollary
4.8
)
Let
\tau\in\mathcal{U}_{\mathbb{C}}
. Then
\mathrm{End}(I_{\tau})\simeq\mathbb{C}[R_{\tau}]
, where
R_{\tau}=W_{\tau}/W_{(\tau)}
. In particular,
\mathrm{End}(I_{\tau})=\mathbb{C}\mathrm{Id}
if and only
R_{\tau}=\{1\}
.

In
[
Héb21a
]
, we studied the submodules and the quotients of
I_{\tau}
, for
\tau\in\mathcal{U}_{\mathbb{C}}
. Many results were proved only when the Kac-Moody matrix defining
G
has size
2
or under some conjecture (
[
Héb21a
, Conjecture 5.16]
). As we prove this conjecture, we can drop the assumption on the size of the matrix. In particular,
[
Héb21a
, Theorem 5.34 and Theorem 5.38]
yield links between the submodules and the quotients of
I_{\tau}
and the right submodules and quotients of
\mathrm{End}(I_{\tau})
respectively.

Frameworks

Actually, following
[
BPGR16
]
we study Iwahori-Hecke algebras associated with abstract masures. In particular our results also apply when
G
is an almost-split Kac-Moody group over a non-Archimedean local field. In this case, most of the results of this introduction are true but the formulas are more complicated (they are given in the paper). Corollary
2
is not necessarily true for almost-split groups, even in the reductive case.

Organization of the paper

This paper is organized as follows. In section
2
, we recall the definitions of the Iwahori–Hecke algebras and of the principal series representations, and we introduce tools to study these representations.

In section
3
, we study the algebra
\mathscr{K}_{\tau}
mentioned above and describe
I_{\tau}(\tau,\mathrm{gen},W_{(\tau)})
.

In section
4
, we prove Kato’s irreducibility criterion.

2
Iwahori-Hecke algebras

Let
G
be a Kac-Moody group over a non-archimedean local field. Then Gaussent and Rousseau constructed a space
\mathcal{I}
, called a masure on which
G
acts, generalizing the construction of the Bruhat-Tits buildings (see
[
GR08
]
,
[
Rou16
]
and
[
Rou17
]
). In
[
BPGR16
]
Bardy-Panse, Gaussent and Rousseau attached an Iwahori-Hecke algebra
\mathcal{H}_{\mathcal{R}}
to each masure satisfying certain conditions and to each ring
\mathcal{R}
. They in particular attach an Iwahori-Hecke algebra to each almost-split Kac-Moody group over a local field. The algebra
\mathcal{H}_{\mathcal{R}}
is an algebra of functions defined on some pairs of chambers of the masure, equipped with a convolution product. Then they prove that under some additional hypothesis on the ring
\mathcal{R}
(which are satisfied by
\mathbb{C}
),
\mathcal{H}_{\mathcal{R}}
admits a Bernstein-Lusztig presentation. We restrict our study to the case where
\mathcal{R}=\mathbb{C}
. In this paper, we will only use the Bernstein-Lusztig presentation of
\mathcal{H}_{\mathbb{C}}
. More precisely, we introduce an algebra
{{}^{\mathrm{BL}}\mathcal{H}}(T_{\mathbb{C}})=\bigoplus_{w\in W^{v}}T_{w}*\mathbb{C}(Y)
, which contains both
{{}^{\mathrm{BL}}\mathcal{H}}_{\mathbb{C}}
and
\mathcal{H}_{\mathbb{C}}
. We mainly study
{{}^{\mathrm{BL}}\mathcal{H}}(T_{\mathbb{C}})
and
{{}^{\mathrm{BL}}\mathcal{H}}_{\mathbb{C}}
. We do not introduce masures nor Kac-Moody groups. We however introduce the standard apartment of a masure.

2.1
Standard apartment of a masure

A (finite)
Kac-Moody matrix
(or generalized Cartan matrix) is a square matrix
A=(a_{i,j})_{i,j\in I}
indexed by a finite set
I
, with integral coefficients, and such that :

(i)

\forall\ i\in I,\ a_{i,i}=2
;

(ii)

\forall\ (i,j)\in I^{2},(i\neq j)\Rightarrow(a_{i,j}\leq 0)
;

(iii)

\forall\ (i,j)\in I^{2},\ (a_{i,j}=0)\Leftrightarrow(a_{j,i}=0
).

In the paper, we will also consider infinite Kac-Moody matrices. The definition is the same except that
I
is infinite. However we will only consider root generating systems associated with finite Kac-Moody matrices.

A
root generating system
is a
5
-tuple
\mathcal{S}=(A,X,Y,(\alpha_{i})_{i\in I},(\alpha_{i}^{\vee})_{i\in I})
made of a finite Kac-Moody matrix
A
indexed by the finite set
I
, of two dual free
\mathbb{Z}
-modules
X
and
Y
of finite rank, and of a free family
(\alpha_{i})_{i\in I}
(respectively
(\alpha_{i}^{\vee})_{i\in I}
) of elements in
X
(resp.
Y
) called
simple roots
(resp.
simple coroots
) that satisfy
a_{i,j}=\alpha_{j}(\alpha_{i}^{\vee})
for all
i,j
in
I
. Elements of
X
(respectively of
Y
) are called
characters
(resp.
cocharacters
).

Fix such a root generating system
\mathcal{S}=(A,X,Y,(\alpha_{i})_{i\in I},(\alpha_{i}^{\vee})_{i\in I})
and set
\mathbb{A}:=Y\otimes\mathbb{R}
. Each element of
X
induces a linear form on
\mathbb{A}
, hence
X
can be seen as a subset of the dual
\mathbb{A}^{*}
. In particular, the
\alpha_{i}
’s (with
i\in I
) will be seen as linear forms on
\mathbb{A}
. This allows us to define, for any
i\in I
, an involution
r_{i}
of
\mathbb{A}
by setting
r_{i}(v):=v-\alpha_{i}(v)\alpha_{i}^{\vee}
for any
v\in\mathbb{A}
. Let
\mathscr{S}=\{r_{i}|i\in I\}
be the (finite) set of
simple reflections
. One defines the
Weyl group of
\mathcal{S}
as the subgroup
W^{v}
of
\mathrm{GL}(\mathbb{A})
generated by
\mathscr{S}
. The pair
(W^{v},\mathscr{S})
is a Coxeter system, hence we can consider the length
\ell(w)
with respect to
\mathscr{S}
of any element
w
of
W^{v}
. If
s\in\mathscr{S}
,
s=r_{i}
for some unique
i\in I
. We set
\alpha_{s}=\alpha_{i}
and
\alpha_{s}^{\vee}=\alpha_{i}^{\vee}
.

The following formula defines an action of the Weyl group
W^{v}
on
\mathbb{A}^{*}
:

\displaystyle\forall\ x\in\mathbb{A},w\in W^{v},\alpha\in\mathbb{A}^{*},\ (w.\alpha)(x):=\alpha(w^{-1}.x).

Let
\Phi:=\{w.\alpha_{i}|(w,i)\in W^{v}\times I\}
(resp.
\Phi^{\vee}=\{w.\alpha_{i}^{\vee}|(w,i)\in W^{v}\times I\}
) be the set of
real roots
(resp.
real coroots
): then
\Phi
(resp.
\Phi^{\vee}
) is a subset of the
root lattice

Q_{\mathbb{Z}}:=\displaystyle\bigoplus_{i\in I}\mathbb{Z}\alpha_{i}
(resp.
coroot lattice

Q^{\vee}_{\mathbb{Z}}=\bigoplus_{i\in I}\mathbb{Z}\alpha_{i}^{\vee}
). By
[
Kum02
, 1.2.2 (2)]
, we have
\mathbb{R}\alpha^{\vee}\cap\Phi^{\vee}=\{\pm\alpha^{\vee}\}
and
\mathbb{R}\alpha\cap\Phi=\{\pm\alpha\}
for all
\alpha^{\vee}\in\Phi^{\vee}
and
\alpha\in\Phi
.

Reflections and roots

We equip
(W^{v},\mathscr{S})
with the Bruhat order
\leq
(see
[
BB05
, Definition 2.1.1]
).

Let
\mathscr{R}=\{wsw^{-1}|w\in W^{v},s\in\mathscr{S}\}
be the set of
reflections
of
W^{v}
. Let
r\in\mathscr{R}
. Write
r=wsw^{-1}
, where
w\in W^{v}
,
s\in\mathscr{S}
and
ws>w
(which is possible because if
ws<w
, then
r=(ws)s(ws)^{-1}
). Then one sets
\alpha_{r}=w.\alpha_{s}\in\Phi_{+}
(resp.
\alpha_{r}^{\vee}=w.\alpha_{s}^{\vee}\in\Phi^{\vee}_{+}
). Conversely, if
\alpha\in\Phi
or
\alpha^{\vee}\in\Phi^{\vee}
,
\alpha=w.\alpha_{s}
or
\alpha^{\vee}=w.\alpha_{s}^{\vee}
, where
w\in W^{v}
and
s\in\mathscr{S}
, one sets
r_{\alpha}=wsw^{-1}
or
r_{\alpha^{\vee}}=wsw^{-1}
. This is independant of the choices of
w
and
s
by
[
Kum02
, 1.3.11 Theorem (b5)]
.

2.2
Iwahori-Hecke algebras

In this subsection, we give the definition of the Iwahori-Hecke algebra via its Bernstein-Lusztig presentation.

2.2.1
The algebra
{{}^{\mathrm{BL}}\mathcal{H}}(T_{\mathbb{C}})

Let
(\sigma_{s})_{s\in\mathscr{S}},(\sigma^{\prime}_{s})_{s\in\mathscr{S}}\in\mathbb{C}^{\mathscr{S}}
satisfying the following relations:

—

if
\alpha_{s}(Y)=\mathbb{Z}
, then
\sigma_{s}=\sigma^{\prime}_{s}
;

—

if
s,t\in\mathscr{S}
are conjugate (i.e if there exists a sequence
s_{1},\ldots,s_{n}\in\mathscr{S}
such that
s_{1}=s
,
s_{n}=t
and
\alpha_{s_{i}}(\alpha_{s_{i+1}}^{\vee})=\alpha_{s_{i+1}}(\alpha_{s_{i}}^{\vee})=-1
, for
i\in\llbracket 1,n-1\rrbracket
), then
\sigma_{s}=\sigma_{t}=\sigma^{\prime}_{s}=\sigma^{\prime}_{t}
.

As in
[
Héb21b
]
, we moreover assume that
|\sigma_{s}|,|\sigma_{s}^{\prime}|>1
, for all
s\in\mathscr{S}
.

Definition 2.1
.

Let
\mathcal{H}_{W^{v},\mathbb{C}}
be the
Hecke algebra of the Coxeter group
W^{v}
over
\mathbb{C}
, that is:

—

as a vector space,
\mathcal{H}_{W^{v},\mathbb{C}}=\bigoplus_{w\in W^{v}}\mathbb{C}T_{w}
, where the
T_{w}
,
w\in W^{v}
are symbols,

—

\forall\ s\in\mathscr{S},\forall\ w\in W^{v}
,
T_{s}*T_{w}=\left\{\begin{aligned} &T_{sw}&\mathrm{\ if\ }\ell(sw)=\ell(w)+1\\
&(\sigma_{s}^{2}-1)T_{w}+\sigma_{s}^{2}T_{sw}&\mathrm{\ if\ }\ell(sw)=\ell(w)-1.\end{aligned}\right.\

Let
\mathbb{C}[Y]
be the group algebra of
Y
over
\mathbb{C}
, that is:

—

as a vector space,
\mathbb{C}[Y]=\bigoplus_{\lambda\in Y}\mathbb{C}Z^{\lambda}
, where the
Z^{\lambda}
,
\lambda\in Y
are symbols,

—

for all
\lambda,\mu\in Y
,
Z^{\lambda}*Z^{\mu}=Z^{\lambda+\mu}
.

We denote by
\mathbb{C}(Y)
its field of fractions. For
\theta=\frac{\sum_{\lambda\in Y}{a_{\lambda}Z^{\lambda}}}{\sum_{\lambda\in Y}b_{\lambda}Z^{\lambda}}\in\mathbb{C}(Y)
and
w\in W^{v}
, set
{{}^{w}}\theta:=\frac{\sum_{\lambda\in Y}a_{\lambda}Z^{w.\lambda}}{\sum_{\lambda\in Y}b_{\lambda}Z^{w.\lambda}}
.

\ell_{\tau}({{}^{s}\theta}.x^{\prime})\leq\ell_{\tau}(x^{\prime})-1
. For all
v\in\mathrm{supp}({{}^{s}\theta}.x^{\prime})
, if
sv<_{\tau}v
, then
\ell_{\tau}(\tilde{\mathcal{K}}_{s}*\tilde{\mathcal{K}}_{v}.\mathbf{v}_{\tau})\leq\ell_{\tau}(v)\leq\ell_{\tau}({{}^{s}\theta}.x^{\prime})\leq\ell_{\tau}(x^{\prime})-1
by Proposition
3.1
(
2
). Therefore for all
v\in\mathrm{supp}({\tilde{\mathcal{K}}_{s}*^{s}\theta}.x)
such that
\ell_{\tau}(v)=\ell_{\tau}(x^{\prime})
, we have
sv<_{\tau}v
. Therefore

\mathrm{supp}\big(\mathrm{LT}(\tilde{\mathcal{K}}_{s}*{{}^{s}\theta}*x^{\prime})\big)\cap\mathrm{supp}\big(\mathrm{LT}(x^{\prime})\big)=\emptyset.

(4.7)

Take
\lambda\in Y
such that
\alpha_{s}(\lambda)=1
and set
\theta=Z^{\lambda}-\tau(\lambda)\in\mathcal{J}_{\tau}
. Then by Lemma
4.1
,
\tau\big(\tilde{\Omega_{s}}(\theta)\big)=\tau(\lambda)\sigma_{s,\tau}^{\prime\prime}\alpha_{s}(\lambda)=\tau(\lambda)\sigma_{s,\tau}^{\prime\prime}
and thus by combining (
4.6
) and (
4.7
), we obtain that
\ell_{\tau}(\theta.\tilde{\mathcal{K}}_{s}.x)=\ell_{\tau}(x^{\prime})
. By assumption we deduce that
\mathrm{ord}_{\tau}(\theta.\tilde{\mathcal{K}}_{s}.x)=\ell_{\tau}(x^{\prime})+1
. Consequently
\mathrm{ord}_{\tau}(\tilde{\mathcal{K}}_{s}.x)\geq\ell_{\tau}(x)+1
and by Lemma
4.3
we deduce that
\mathrm{ord}_{\tau}(\tilde{\mathcal{K}}_{s}.x)=\ell_{\tau}(x)+1
.

We now assume that we are in the case (2). Let
w\in\mathrm{supp}\big((\mathrm{LT}(x)\big)
and
s\in\mathscr{S}
be such that
sw<_{\tau}w
. Set
x^{\prime}=\tilde{\mathcal{K}}_{s}.x
. Then by assumption we have
\ell_{\tau}(x^{\prime})=\ell_{\tau}(x)+1
and thus

\mathrm{supp}\big(\mathrm{LT}(x^{\prime})\big)\subset s.\mathrm{supp}\left(\mathrm{LT}(x)\right)\setminus\{sw\}.

In particular
\mathcal{N}_{\tau}(x^{\prime})<\mathcal{N}_{\tau}(x)
. By our induction assumption we deduce that
\mathrm{ord}_{\tau}(x^{\prime})=\ell_{\tau}(x^{\prime})+1=\ell_{\tau}(x)+2
. By Lemma
4.5
we have that
\mathrm{ord}_{\tau}(x)\geq\mathrm{ord}_{\tau}(x^{\prime})-1\geq\ell_{\tau}(x)+1
. By Lemma
4.3
we deduce that
\ell_{\tau}(x)+1=\mathrm{ord}_{\tau}(x)
. This completes the proof of the theorem.

∎

Note that the above proof can be simplified in the case where
W_{(\tau)}
is finite. In this case, denote by
w_{0}
, the maximal element of
W_{(\tau)}
(for the Bruhat order
\leq_{\tau}
). Let
x\in I_{\tau}(\tau,\mathrm{gen},W_{(\tau)})\setminus\{0\}
. Let
w\in\mathrm{supp}\big(\mathrm{LT}(x)\big)
. Set
y=\tilde{\mathcal{K}}_{w_{0}w^{-1}}.x
. Then
\mathrm{supp}(\tilde{\mathcal{K}}_{w_{0}w^{-1}}.x)\ni w_{0}
. By Lemma
4.4
,
\mathrm{ord}_{\tau}(\tilde{\mathcal{K}}_{w_{0}w^{-1}}.x)=\ell_{\tau}(w_{0})+1
. By Lemma
4.5
, we deduce that

\mathrm{ord}_{\tau}(x)\geq\mathrm{ord}_{\tau}(y)-\ell(w_{0}w^{-1})=\ell_{\tau}(w_{0})+1-\big(\ell(w_{0})-\ell(w^{-1})\big)=\ell(w)+1.

By Lemma
4.3
, we deduce that
\mathrm{ord}_{\tau}(x)=\ell_{\tau}(x)+1
.

We obtain a version of the Knapp-Stein dimension theorem in our frameworks:

Corollary 4.7
.

(see
[
Sil78
]
)
Let
\tau\in\mathcal{U}_{\mathbb{C}}
. Then

I_{\tau}(\tau)=\bigoplus_{w_{R}\in R_{\tau}}\mathbb{C}F_{w_{R}}.\mathbf{v}_{\tau}.

If moreover
\sigma_{s}=\sigma_{s}^{\prime}
for all
s\in\mathscr{S}
, then
\mathrm{End}_{\mathcal{H}-\mathrm{mod}}(I_{\tau})\simeq\mathbb{C}[R_{\tau}]
.

Proof.

This follows from
[
Héb21a
, Proposition 5.13 (2) and Proposition 5.27]
.
∎

We also obtain Kato’s irreducibility criterion:

Corollary 4.8
.

(see
[
Kat81
, Theorem 2.4]
)

Let
\tau\in T_{\mathbb{C}}
. Then
I_{\tau}
is irreducible if and only if:

1.

\tau\in\mathcal{U}_{\mathbb{C}}
,

2.

W_{(\tau)}=\{1\}
.

Proof.

By
[
Héb21b
, Proposition 4.17 and Theorem 4.8]
, if

1.
Introduction

In this paper, as usual
L^{p,\sigma}(\mathbb{R}^{+}=(0,\infty))
denotes the weighted
L^{p}-
space with norm

\displaystyle\|f\|_{L^{p,\sigma}}=\|f\|_{p,\sigma}=\left(\int_{0}^{\infty}|f(x)|^{p}d\sigma(x)\right)^{\frac{1}{p}},(1\leq p<\infty),

(1.1)

\displaystyle\|f\|_{\infty,\sigma}=\text{ess sup}_{0<x<\infty}|f(x)|<\infty.

(1.2)

The Hankel transformation of the function
f\in L^{1,\sigma}(\mathbb{R}^{+})
is defined by

\displaystyle\hat{f}(x)=\int\limits_{0}^{\infty}j(xt)f(t)d\sigma(t),\,\,\,\,0\leq x<\infty,

(1.3)

where
\sigma(t)=\frac{t^{2\nu+1}}{2^{\nu+\frac{1}{2}}\varGamma(\nu+\frac{3}{2})}
,
j(.)=C_{\nu}x^{\frac{1}{2}-\nu}J_{\nu-\frac{1}{2}}
,
C_{\nu}=2^{\nu+\frac{1}{2}}\varGamma(\nu+\frac{1}{2})
and
J_{\nu-\frac{1}{2}}
denote the Bessel function of first kind of order
\nu-\frac{1}{2}
.

If
\hat{f}\in L^{1,\sigma}(\mathbb{R}^{+})
, then the inverse of Hankel transformations is given by

\displaystyle f(x)=\int\limits_{0}^{\infty}j(xt)\hat{f}(t)d\sigma(t),\,\,\,\,0<x<\infty.

(1.4)

Also, Parseval’s formula of the Hankel transformation for
f,g\in L^{1,\sigma}\bigcap L^{2,\sigma}
is given by

\displaystyle\int\limits_{0}^{\infty}\hat{f}(x)\hat{g}(x)d\sigma(x)=\int\limits_{0}^{\infty}f(u)g(u)d\sigma(u).

(1.5)

By denseness and continuity the Parseval’s formula can be extended to all
f,g\in L^{2,\sigma}(\mathbb{R}^{+})
. Hence Hankel transform is isometry on
L^{2,\sigma}(\mathbb{R}^{+})
.

If
f,g\in L^{1,\sigma}(\mathbb{R}^{+})
, then the convolution associated with the Hankel is defined as (see
[
10
]
)

\displaystyle(f\#g)(x)=\int\limits_{0}^{\infty}f(x,y)g(y)d\sigma(y),

(1.6)

where the Hankel translation is given by

\displaystyle f(x,y)=\tau_{y}f(x)=:\int\limits_{0}^{\infty}f(z)D(x,y,z)d\sigma(z),\,\,\,0<x,y<\infty,

(1.7)

and

\displaystyle D(x,y,z)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int\limits_{0}^{\infty}j(xu)j(yu)j(zu)d\sigma(u)

(1.8)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2^{3\nu-\frac{5}{2}}[\varGamma(\nu+\frac{1}{2})]^{2}\left(\varGamma(\nu)\pi^{\frac{1}{2}}\right)^{-1}(xyz)^{-2\nu-1}[\Delta(xyz)]^{2\nu-2}

where
\Delta
denotes the area of a triangle.
D(x,y,z)
is symmetric in
x,y,z
.

From (1.4) and (
1.8
), we have

\displaystyle\int\limits_{0}^{\infty}j(zu)D(x,y,z)d\sigma(z)=j(xu)j(yu),\,\,0<x,y<\infty,\,\,0\leq u<\infty,

(1.9)

and for
u=0
, we get

\displaystyle\int\limits_{0}^{\infty}D(x,y,z)d\sigma(z)=1,

(1.10)

and

\displaystyle\hat{(f\#g)}(x)=\hat{f}(x)\hat{g}(x),\,\,0\leq x<\infty.

(1.11)

,

,

Lemma 1.1
.

Let
f\in L^{p,\sigma}(\mathbb{R}^{+}),\,\,1\leq p<\infty
. Then we have

\displaystyle||\tau_{y}f(x)||_{p,\sigma}\leq||f||_{p,\sigma}.

(1.12)

Lemma 1.2
.

Let
f\in L^{p,\sigma}(\mathbb{R}^{+})
and
g\in L^{q,\sigma}(\mathbb{R}^{+}),\,\,\frac{1}{p}+\frac{1}{q}=1+\frac{1}{r}
. Then we have

\displaystyle||f\#g||_{r,\sigma}\leq||f||_{p,\sigma}||g||_{p,\sigma}.

(1.13)

.

(Besov-Hankel Space):

Let measurable function
\phi

(0,\infty)
belongs to

if
\phi\in L^{p,\sigma}(\mathbb{R}^{+})
and

\displaystyle\int\limits_{0}^{\infty}\left(h^{-\alpha}w_{p}(\phi)(h)\right)^{q}\frac{dh}{h}<\infty\,\,\,\,\text{for}\,\,\alpha>0,1\leq p,q<\infty,

(1.14)

1.1.
Bessel Wavelet

Using the properites of Hankel transform Pathak and Dixit (see
[
2
]
) define the continuous Bessel wavelet for
\psi\in L^{p,\sigma}(\mathbb{R}^{+})
,
1\leq p<\infty
,
b\geq 0
and
a>0
as

\displaystyle\psi_{b,a}(x):

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle D_{a}\tau_{b}\psi(x)

(1.15)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle a^{-2\nu-1}\int\limits_{0}^{\infty}\psi(z)D\left(\frac{b}{a},\frac{x}{a},z\right)d\sigma(z)

where
D_{a}
denote the dilation operator.

The continuous Bessel wavelet transform of
f\in L^{2,\sigma}(\mathbb{R}^{+})
with respect to a wavelet
\psi\in L^{2,\sigma}(\mathbb{R}^{+})
is defined as

\displaystyle(B_{\psi}f)(b,a)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int\limits_{0}^{\infty}f(x)\overline{\psi_{b,a}}(x)d\sigma(x)

(1.16)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle a^{-2\nu-1}\int\limits_{0}^{\infty}\int\limits_{0}^{\infty}f(x)\overline{\psi(z)}D\left(\frac{b}{a},\frac{x}{a},z\right)d\sigma(z)d\sigma(x).

Moreover , using (
1.6
), we have

\displaystyle(B_{\psi}f)(b,a)=(f\#\psi_{a})(b),

(1.17)

where
\psi_{a}(t)=a^{-2\nu-1}\overline{\psi(t/a)}
.

Betancor and Rodriguez-Mesa
[
9
]
first time introduce Besov-Hankel spaces and characterized by mean of the Bochner-Riesz mean and the partial Hankel integrals. Perrier and Basdevant
[
5
]
established the characterization of Besov spaces by means of continuous wavelet transform. Motivated by these two works, we characterized Besov-Hankel spaces by the continuous Bessel wavelet transform.

Present paper is organized in following manner: section 1 is introductory, in which we recall some properties of Hankel transform , Besov-Hankel space and continuous Bessel wavelet transform. Section 2 is related to continuous the continuous Besse wavelet transform in

2.
The Continuous Bessel Wavelet Transform in
L^{p,\sigma}(\mathbb{R}^{+})

In this section we extend the concept of Bessel wavelet transform on
L^{p,\sigma}(\mathbb{R}^{+})
.

Theorem 2.1
.

Suppose that the Bessel wavelet
\psi\in L^{p,\sigma}(\mathbb{R}^{+})
satisfies the admissibility condition

\displaystyle A_{\psi}=\int^{\infty}_{0}\omega^{-2\nu-1}|\hat{\psi}(\omega)|^{2}d\omega>0,

where
\hat{\psi}
denote the Hankel transform of
\psi
then continuous Bessel wavelet transform is a bounded linear operator

\displaystyle L^{p,\sigma}(\mathbb{R}^{+})\rightarrow L^{2,\sigma}(\mathbb{R}^{+},\frac{d\sigma(a)}{a^{2\nu+1}})\times L^{p,\sigma}(\mathbb{R}^{+}),

moreover, for any
f\in L^{p,\sigma}(\mathbb{R}^{+})
,
1<p<\infty

\displaystyle\|f\|_{L^{p,\sigma}(\mathbb{R}^{+})}=\left(\int^{\infty}_{0}\left(\int^{\infty}_{0}|B_{\psi}f(b,a)|^{2}\frac{d\sigma(a)}{a^{2\nu+1}}\right)^{\frac{p}{2}}d\sigma(b)\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}.

(2.1)

Proof.

Let
S_{p}
denote the space
L^{2,\sigma}(\mathbb{R}^{+},\frac{d\sigma(a)}{a^{2\nu+1}})\times L^{p,\sigma}(\mathbb{R}^{+})
associated to the norm

\|f\|_{S_{p}}=\left\{\int^{\infty}_{0}\left(\int^{\infty}_{0}|f(b,a)|^{2}\frac{d\sigma(a)}{a^{2\nu+1}}\right)^{\frac{p}{2}}d\sigma(b)\right\}^{\frac{1}{p}}
.

If we take
p=2
, then from Plancherel’s theorem:

\displaystyle\|B_{\psi}f\|_{S_{2}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left\{\int^{\infty}_{0}\left(\int^{\infty}_{0}|B_{\psi}f(b,a)|^{2}\frac{d\sigma(a)}{a^{2\nu+1}}\right)d\sigma(b)\right\}^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\|B_{\psi}f\|_{S_{2}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sqrt{A_{\psi}}\|f\|_{L_{2,\sigma}},

where
A_{\psi}=\int^{\infty}_{0}\omega^{-2\nu-1}|\hat{\psi}(\omega)|^{2}d\omega>0
, if
\psi
is real. From singular integral theorem, the operators on
L^{2,\sigma}(\mathbb{R}^{+},\frac{d\sigma(a)}{a^{2\nu+1}})
holds inequality:

\|B_{\psi}f\|_{S_{p}}\leq C_{p}\|f\|_{L^{p,\sigma}(\mathbb{R}^{+})}

for

1<p\leq 2
,

where the constant
C_{p}
depends only on
p
and
\psi
(see
[
12
]
). Due to duality the inequality is also valid for
1<p<\infty
. It follows that

\displaystyle\left\{\int^{\infty}_{0}\left(\int^{\infty}_{0}|B_{\psi}f(b,a)|^{2}\frac{d\sigma(a)}{a^{2\nu+1}}\right)^{\frac{p}{2}}d\sigma(b)\right\}^{\frac{1}{p}}\leq C_{p}\|f\|_{L^{p,\sigma}(\mathbb{R}^{+})}.

(2.2)

Conversely suppose that
f\in L^{2,\sigma}(\mathbb{R}^{+})\cap L^{p,\sigma}(\mathbb{R}^{+})
. Since continuous Bessel wavelet transform is isomerty for every
g\in L^{2,\sigma}(\mathbb{R}^{+})\cap L^{q,\sigma}(\mathbb{R}^{+})
, we can write

\displaystyle\int^{\infty}_{0}\int^{\infty}_{0}B_{\psi}f(b,a)\overline{B_{\psi}g(b,a)}a^{-2\nu-1}d\sigma(a)d\sigma(b)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle A_{\psi}\langle f,g\rangle

\displaystyle\frac{1}{A_{\psi}}\int^{\infty}_{0}\int^{\infty}_{0}B_{\psi}f(b,a)\overline{B_{\psi}g(b,a)}a^{-2\nu-1}d\sigma(a)d\sigma(b)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int^{\infty}_{0}f(x)\overline{g(x)}d\sigma(x).

(2.3)

Now,

\displaystyle|\int^{\infty}_{0}f(x)g(x)d\sigma(x)|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{A_{\psi}}|\int^{\infty}_{0}\int^{\infty}_{0}B_{\psi}f(b,a)\overline{B_{\psi}g(b,a)}a^{-2\nu-1}d\sigma(a)d\sigma(b)|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{A_{\psi}}\int^{\infty}_{0}\int^{\infty}_{0}|B_{\psi}f(b,a)\overline{B_{\psi}g(b,a)}|a^{-2\nu-1}d\sigma(a)d\sigma(b),

using Schwarz inequality and then Holder’s inequality, we have

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{A_{\psi}}\left(\int^{\infty}_{0}\left(\int^{\infty}_{0}|B_{\psi}f(b,a)|^{2}a^{-2\nu-1}d\sigma(a)\right)^{\frac{p}{2}}d\sigma(b)\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}

\displaystyle\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\times\left(\int^{\infty}_{0}\left(\int^{\infty}_{0}|B_{\psi}g(b,a)|^{2}a^{-2\nu-1}d\sigma(a)\right)^{\frac{q}{2}}d\sigma(b)\right)^{\frac{1}{q}},

where
\frac{1}{p}+\frac{1}{q}=1
.

From equation (
2.2
), we get

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{A_{q}}{A_{\psi}}\left(\int^{\infty}_{0}\left(\int^{\infty}_{0}|B_{\psi}f(b,a)|^{2}a^{-2\nu-1}d\sigma(a)\right)^{\frac{p}{2}}d\sigma(b)\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}\|g\|_{L^{q,\sigma}(\mathbb{R}^{+})}.

By Density theorem

\displaystyle\|f\|_{L^{p,\sigma}(\mathbb{R}^{+})}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle A\left(\int^{\infty}_{0}\left(\int^{\infty}_{0}|B_{\psi}f(b,a)|^{2}a^{-2\nu-1}d\sigma(a)\right)^{\frac{p}{2}}d\sigma(b)\right)^{\frac{1}{p}},

where
A=\frac{A_{q}}{A_{\psi}}
.
∎

Theorem 2.2
.

(
Parseval’s formula
)

Let us assume
\phi_{1}\in L^{p,\sigma}(\mathbb{R^{+}})
,
\phi_{2}\in L^{q,\sigma}(\mathbb{R^{+}})
with
1\leq p,q<\infty
and
\frac{1}{p}+\frac{1}{q}=1
. If
\psi
is a real wavelet then

\displaystyle\frac{1}{A_{\psi}}\int^{\infty}_{0}\int^{\infty}_{0}B_{\psi}\phi_{1}(b,a)\overline{B_{\psi}\phi_{2}(b,a)}a^{-2\nu-1}d\sigma(a)d\sigma(b)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int^{\infty}_{0}\phi_{1}(x)\overline{\phi_{2}(x)}d\sigma(x),

where
A_{\psi}=\int^{\infty}_{0}\omega^{-2\nu-1}|\hat{\psi}(\omega)|^{2}d\omega>0
and
\hat{\psi}
denote the Hankel transform.

Proof.

Let us define bilinear transform
T:L^{p,\sigma}(\mathbb{R}^{+})\times L^{q,\sigma}(\mathbb{R}^{+})\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{+}
by

T(\phi_{1},\phi_{2})=\langle B_{\psi}\phi_{1}(b,a),B_{\psi}\phi_{2}(b,a)\rangle_{(\frac{d\sigma(a)}{a^{2\nu+1}}d\sigma(b))}
.

Now, applying Holder’s inequality two times we obtain

\displaystyle\lvert T(\phi_{1},\phi_{2})\rvert

\displaystyle=\lvert\langle B_{\psi}\phi_{1}(b,a),B_{\psi}\phi_{2}(b,a)\rangle_{\frac{d\sigma(a)}{a^{2\nu+1}},d\sigma(b)}\rvert

\displaystyle\leq\int^{\infty}_{0}\left(\int^{\infty}_{0}\lvert B_{\psi}\phi_{1}(b,a)\rvert^{2}\frac{d\sigma(a)}{a^{2\nu+1}}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\left(\int^{\infty}_{0}\lvert B_{\psi}\phi_{2}(b,a)\rvert^{2}d\sigma(b)\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\leq\left(\int^{\infty}_{0}\left(\int^{\infty}_{0}\lvert B_{\psi}\phi_{1}(b,a)\rvert^{2}\frac{d\sigma(a)}{a^{2\nu+1}}\right)^{\frac{p}{2}}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}\times\left(\int^{\infty}_{0}\left(\int^{\infty}_{0}\lvert B_{\psi}\phi_{2}(b,a)\rvert^{2}d\sigma(b)\right)^{\frac{q}{2}}\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}

using Theorem 2.1. we have

\displaystyle\lvert T(\phi_{1},\phi_{2})\rvert\leq C\lVert\phi_{1}\rVert_{L^{p,\sigma}(\mathbb{R_{+}})}\lVert\phi_{2}\rVert_{L^{q,\sigma}(\mathbb{R_{+}})}.

(2.5)

Moreover for all
\phi_{1}\in L^{2,\sigma}(\mathbb{R}^{+})\cap L^{p,\sigma}(\mathbb{R}^{+})
and
\phi_{2}\in L^{2,\sigma}(\mathbb{R}^{+})\cap L^{q,\sigma}(\mathbb{R}^{+})
we get

\displaystyle T(\phi_{1},\phi_{2})=\langle B_{\psi}\phi_{1}(b,a),B_{\psi}\phi_{2}(b,a)\rangle_{\frac{d\sigma(a)}{a^{2\nu+1}},d\sigma(b)}=A_{\psi}\langle\phi_{1},\phi_{2}\rangle.

(2.6)

From equations (3.5 ), (3.6) and density of spaces
L^{2,\sigma}(\mathbb{R}^{+})\cap L^{p,\sigma}(\mathbb{R}^{+})
in
L^{p,\sigma}(\mathbb{R}^{+})
gives the result.
∎

3.
Characterization of Besov-Hankel Norms

In present section, By using the above results, we characterize the Besov-Hankel norms associated the Bessel wavelet transform.

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
f\in B^{p,q}_{\alpha,\sigma}(\mathbb{R}^{+})\left(p,q>1,\alpha\neq\mathbb{Z}\right)
and analysing wavelet
\psi
has
[\alpha]+1
cancellations and
(z^{\alpha-[\alpha]}\psi)\in L^{1,\sigma}(\mathbb{R}^{+})
, then the wavelet coefficient of function
f
holds following conditions:

if
q<\infty
,
\int^{\infty}_{0}\left[a^{-\alpha}\|B_{\psi}(.,a)\|_{L^{p,\sigma}}\right]^{q}\frac{da}{a}<\infty

if
q=\infty
,
a\rightarrow a^{-\alpha}\|B_{\psi}(.,a)\|_{L^{p,\sigma}}\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{+})
.

Moreover the function
a\rightarrow a^{-\alpha}\|B_{\psi}(.,a)\|_{L^{p,\sigma}}\in L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{+},\frac{da}{a^{2\nu+1}})
and we have:

\displaystyle\Arrowvert a^{-\alpha}\|B_{\psi}(.,a)\|_{L^{p,\sigma}}\Arrowvert_{L^{q,\sigma}(\frac{da}{a})}\leq\|z^{\alpha-[\alpha]}\psi\|^{L_{1,\sigma}}\times\|h^{\alpha-[\alpha]}\omega_{p}(f,h)\|_{L^{q,\sigma}(\frac{dh}{h})}

(3.1)

Proof.

By the definition of continuous Bessel wavelet transform, we have

\displaystyle B_{\psi}(b,a)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int^{\infty}_{0}f(x)\overline{\psi_{b,a}(x)}d\sigma(x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int^{\infty}_{0}f(x)\left(\int^{\infty}_{0}a^{-2\nu-1}D\left(\frac{b}{a},\frac{x}{a},z\right)\overline{\psi(z)}d\sigma(z)\right)d\sigma(x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int^{\infty}_{0}\overline{\psi(z)}\left(\int^{\infty}_{0}a^{-2\nu-1}D\left(\frac{b}{a},\frac{x}{a},z\right)f(x)d\sigma(x)\right)d\sigma(z)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int^{\infty}_{0}\overline{\psi(z)}\left(\int^{\infty}_{0}D\left(b,x,az\right)f(x)d\sigma(x)\right)d\sigma(z)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left\{\int^{\infty}_{0}(\tau_{az}f)(b)\overline{\psi(z)}d\sigma(z)-\int^{\infty}_{0}f(b)\overline{\psi(z)}d\sigma(z)\right\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int^{\infty}_{0}\overline{\psi(z)}\left((\tau_{az}f)(b)-f(b)\right)d\sigma(z).

Taking
L^{p,\sigma}-
norm of the wavelet coefficient

\displaystyle\|B_{\psi}(b,a)\|_{L^{p,\sigma}}=\int^{\infty}_{0}\left\{|\int^{\infty}_{0}\overline{\psi(z)}\left((\tau_{az}f)(b)-f(b)\right)d\sigma(z)|^{p}\right\}^{\frac{1}{p}}d\sigma(b).

Using Minkowski inequality of integrability for
p\neq\infty

\displaystyle\|B_{\psi}(b,a)\|_{L^{p,\sigma}}\leq\int^{\infty}_{0}\left\{\int^{\infty}_{0}|(\tau_{az}f)(b)-f(b)|d\sigma(b)|^{p}\right\}^{\frac{1}{p}}|\psi(z)|d\sigma(z).

(3.2)

Suppose that
q<\infty
and integrating w.r.t.
a
, we get

\displaystyle\int^{\infty}_{0}\left[a^{-\alpha}\|B_{\psi}(b,a)\|_{L^{p,\sigma}}\right]^{q}\frac{da}{a}\leq\int^{\infty}_{0}\left[a^{-\alpha}\int^{\infty}_{0}|\psi(z)|\omega_{p}(f,az)d\sigma(z)\right]^{q}\frac{da}{a}.

Again using Minkowski integrabilty inequality

\displaystyle\int^{\infty}_{0}\left[a^{-\alpha}\|B_{\psi}(b,a)\|_{L^{p,\sigma}}\right]^{q}\frac{da}{a}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left[\int^{\infty}_{0}|\psi(z)|d\sigma(z)\left\{\int^{\infty}_{0}\left(a^{-\alpha}\omega_{p}(f,az)\right)^{q}\frac{da}{a}\right\}^{\frac{1}{q}}\right]^{q}.

Applying change of variable
h=az

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left[\int^{\infty}_{0}z^{-\alpha}|\psi(z)|d\sigma(z)\left\{\int^{\infty}_{0}\left(h^{-\alpha}\omega_{p,\sigma}(f,h)\right)^{q}\frac{dh}{h}\right\}^{\frac{1}{q}}\right]^{q}

(3.3)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left\{\int^{\infty}_{0}z^{-\alpha}|\psi(z)|d\sigma(z)\right\}^{q}\times\left\{\int^{\infty}_{0}\left(h^{-\alpha}\omega_{p,\sigma}(f,h)\right)^{q}\frac{dh}{h}\right\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left\{\int^{\infty}_{0}|z^{-\alpha}\psi(z)|d\sigma(z)\right\}^{q}\times\left\{\int^{\infty}_{0}\left(h^{-\alpha}\omega_{p,\sigma}(f,h)\right)^{q}\frac{dh}{h}\right\}

\displaystyle<

\displaystyle\infty.

If
q=\infty
the hypothesis on
f
says that
h^{-\alpha}\omega_{p,\sigma}(f,h)\in{L_{\infty,\sigma}(\mathbb{R}^{+})}
, so

\displaystyle\|B_{\psi}(b,a)\|_{L_{p,\sigma}}\leq a^{\alpha}\|h^{-\alpha}\omega_{p,\sigma}(f,h)\|_{L_{\infty,\sigma}(\mathbb{R}^{+})}\int^{\infty}_{0}|z^{-\alpha}\psi(z)|d\sigma(z).

(3.4)

The theorem has been proved for

\displaystyle\times a^{-\alpha}\|(B_{\psi}f)(a,\cdot)\|_{L^{p,\sigma}}

\displaystyle f(x)=\frac{1}{A_{\psi}}\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{d\sigma(a)}{a^{2\nu+1}}\int_{0}^{\infty}(B_{\psi}f)(a,b)\psi_{a,b}(x)d\sigma(b)

(3.6)

and

\displaystyle\tau_{h}f(x)=\frac{1}{A_{\psi}}\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{d\sigma(a)}{a^{2\nu+1}}\int_{0}^{\infty}(B_{\psi}f)(a,b)\tau_{h}\psi_{a,b}(x)d\sigma(b).

(3.7)

Then

\displaystyle\tau_{h}f(x)-f(x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{A_{\psi}}\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{d\sigma(a)}{a^{2\nu+1}}\int_{0}^{\infty}(B_{\psi}f)(a,b)\left\{\tau_{h}\psi_{a,b}(x)-\psi_{a,b}(x)\right\}d\sigma(b)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{A_{\psi}}\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{d\sigma(a)}{a^{2\nu+1}}\int_{0}^{\infty}(B_{\psi}f)(a,b)\left\{\tau_{\frac{h}{a}}\tau_{\frac{b}{a}}\psi(\frac{x}{a})-\tau_{\frac{b}{a}}\psi(\frac{x}{a})\right\}d\sigma(b)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{A_{\psi}}\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{d\sigma(a)}{a^{2\nu+1}}\int_{0}^{\infty}(B_{\psi}f)(a,b)a^{-2\nu-1}D(\frac{b}{a},\frac{x}{a},y)d\sigma(b)

\displaystyle\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\times\int_{0}^{\infty}\left\{\tau_{\frac{h}{a}}\psi(y)-\psi(y)\right\}d\sigma(y)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{A_{\psi}}\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{d\sigma(a)}{a^{2\nu+1}}\tau_{\frac{y}{a}}(B_{\psi}f)(a,\frac{x}{a})\int_{0}^{\infty}\left\{\tau_{\frac{h}{a}}\psi(y)-\psi(y)\right\}d\sigma(y)

Taking
L^{p,\sigma}
- norm on both side, we have

\displaystyle w_{p,\sigma}(f,h)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{A_{\psi}}\left\{\int_{0}^{\infty}\arrowvert\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{d\sigma(a)}{a^{2\nu+1}}\tau_{\frac{y}{a}}(B_{\psi}f)(a,\frac{x}{a})\int_{0}^{\infty}\left\{\tau_{\frac{h}{a}}\psi(y)-\psi(y)\right\}d\sigma(y)\arrowvert^{p}d\sigma(x)\right\}^{\frac{1}{p}}

applying Minkowski’s inequality

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{A_{\psi}}\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{d\sigma(a)}{a^{2\nu+1}}\int_{0}^{\infty}\arrowvert\tau_{\frac{h}{a}}\psi(y)-\psi(y)\arrowvert d\sigma(y)\left\{\int_{0}^{\infty}\lvert\tau_{\frac{y}{a}}(B_{\psi}f)(a,\frac{x}{a})\rvert^{p}d\sigma(x)\right\}^{\frac{1}{p}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{A_{\psi}}\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{d\sigma(t)}{t^{2\nu}}\|(B_{\psi}f)(\frac{h}{t},\cdot)\|_{L^{p,\sigma}}\int_{0}^{\infty}\arrowvert\tau_{\frac{h}{a}}\psi(y)-\psi(y)\arrowvert d\sigma(y)

Now,consider
0<\alpha<1
and using Minkowski’s inequality

\displaystyle\left\{\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{dh}{h}h^{-\alpha q}w_{p,\sigma}(f,h)^{q}\right\}^{\frac{1}{q}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{A_{\psi}}\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{d\sigma(t)}{t^{2\nu}}\int_{0}^{\infty}\arrowvert\tau_{\frac{h}{a}}\psi(y)-\psi(y)\arrowvert d\sigma(y)

\displaystyle\times\left\{\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{dh}{h}h^{-\alpha q}\|(B_{\psi}f)(\frac{h}{t},\cdot)\|_{L^{p,\sigma}}^{q}\right\}^{\frac{1}{q}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{A_{\psi}}\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{d\sigma(t)}{t^{2\nu+\alpha}}\int_{0}^{\infty}\arrowvert\tau_{\frac{h}{a}}\psi(y)-\psi(y)\arrowvert d\sigma(y)

\displaystyle\times\left\{\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{da}{a}a^{-\alpha q}\|(B_{\psi}f)(a,\cdot)\|_{L^{p,\sigma}}^{q}\right\}^{\frac{1}{q}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{C}{A_{\psi}}\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{dt}{t^{1+\alpha}}\int_{0}^{\infty}\arrowvert\tau_{\frac{h}{a}}\psi(y)-\psi(y)\arrowvert d\sigma(y)

\displaystyle\times\left\{\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{da}{a}a^{-\alpha q}\|(B_{\psi}f)(a,\cdot)\|_{L^{p,\sigma}}^{q}\right\}^{\frac{1}{q}}.

Using Lemma
1.1
, we obtain

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\arrowvert\tau_{\frac{h}{a}}\psi(y)-\psi(y)\arrowvert d\sigma(y)\leq 2\Arrowvert\psi\Arrowvert_{L^{1,\sigma}}

(3.8)

and

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\arrowvert\tau_{\frac{h}{a}}\psi(y)-\psi(y)\arrowvert d\sigma(y)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\arrowvert\int_{0}^{t}\left(\tau_{z}\psi(y)\right)^{\prime}dz\arrowvert d\sigma(y)

(3.9)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}\int_{0}^{\infty}\arrowvert\left(\tau_{z}\psi(y)\right)^{\prime}dz\arrowvert d\sigma(y)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\lVert\psi^{\prime}\rVert_{L^{1,\sigma}}t.

Here, we observe that

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{dt}{t^{1+\alpha}}\int_{0}^{\infty}\arrowvert\tau_{\frac{h}{a}}\psi(y)-\psi(y)\arrowvert d\sigma(y)

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{1}\frac{dt}{t^{1+\alpha}}\int_{0}^{\infty}\arrowvert\tau_{\frac{h}{a}}\psi(y)-\psi(y)\arrowvert d\sigma(y)

\displaystyle+\int_{1}^{\infty}\frac{dt}{t^{1+\alpha}}\int_{0}^{\infty}\arrowvert\tau_{\frac{h}{a}}\psi(y)-\psi(y)\arrowvert d\sigma(y)

\displaystyle\leq 2\Arrowvert\psi\Arrowvert_{L^{1,\sigma}}\int_{1}^{\infty}\frac{dt}{t^{1+\alpha}}+2\lVert\psi^{\prime}\rVert_{L^{1,\sigma}}\int_{0}^{1}\frac{dt}{t^{\alpha}}

\displaystyle=\frac{2}{\alpha}\Arrowvert\psi\Arrowvert_{L^{1,\sigma}}+\frac{1}{1-\alpha}\Arrowvert\psi^{\prime}\Arrowvert_{L^{1,\sigma}},

which proved the result for
0<\alpha<1
.

If

, by the hypothesis

and
\psi

Calculate in sinmilar manner as above for

Corollary 3.3
.

Let
f\in B^{p,q}_{\alpha,\sigma}(\mathbb{R}^{+})\left(p,q>1,\alpha\neq\mathbb{Z}\right)
, then

\displaystyle||f||_{B^{p,q}_{\alpha,\sigma}}=||f||_{L^{p,\sigma}(\mathbb{R}^{+})}+|f|_{{B^{p,q}_{\alpha,\sigma}}}

where
|f|_{{B^{p,q}_{\alpha,\sigma}}}
is equal to

\displaystyle|f|^{q}_{B^{p,q}_{\alpha,\sigma}}=\int\limits_{0}^{\infty}\left(h^{-\alpha}w_{p}(\phi)(h)\right)^{q}\frac{dh}{h}\approx\int^{\infty}_{0}\left[a^{-\alpha}\|B_{\psi}(.,a)\|_{L^{p,\sigma}}\right]^{q}\frac{da}{a}.

Remark 3.4
.

Considerable work has already been done on the characterization of Besov-k-Hankel norms by means of k-Hankel wavelet transform and Mehler-Besov-Fock spaces by using Mehler-Fock wavelet transform etc..

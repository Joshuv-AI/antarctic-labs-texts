Minimal modal logics, constructive modal logics and their relations
Tiziano Dalmonte

Abstract
We present a family of minimal modal logics (namely, modal logics based on minimal propositional logic) corresponding each to a different classical modal logic. The minimal modal logics are defined based on their classical counterparts in two distinct ways: (1) via embedding into fusions of classical modal logics through a natural extension of the Gödel-Johansson translation of minimal logic into modal logic S4; (2) via extension to modal logics of the multi- vs. single-succedent correspondence of sequent calculi for classical and minimal logic. We show that, despite being mutually independent, the two methods turn out to be equivalent for a wide class of modal systems. Moreover, we compare the resulting minimal version of K with the constructive modal logic CK studied in the literature, displaying tight relations among the two systems. Based on these relations, we also define a constructive correspondent for each minimal system, thus obtaining a family of constructive modal logics which includes CK as well as other constructive modal logics studied in the literature. Keywords— Minimal modal logic, constructive modal logic, modal companion, sequent calculus, neighbourhood semantics

By extending these relations to the other systems, we then define a constructive correspondent for each minimal modal logic,

We consider to this purpose the following notions

Definition 2.1

(Fusion)

Let
\mathsf{L}_{1}
and

be

The

\mathsf{L}_{1}\oplus\mathsf{L}_{2}

and

Definition 2.6

(Minimal birelational semantics)
.

A
minimal birelational model
is a tuple

\mathcal{M}=\langle\mathcal{W},\leq,\mathbb{F},\mathcal{R},\mathcal{V}\rangle
,
where
\langle\mathcal{W},\leq,\mathbb{F},\mathcal{V}\rangle
is a minimal relational model,
and
\mathcal{R}
is a binary relation on
\mathcal{W}
.
The forcing relation
\mathcal{M},w\Vdash A
is
inductively defined extending the clauses
for
p,\bot,\land,\lor,\supset

in Section
1.2

with the following
clauses for the modalities:

\mathcal{M},w\Vdash\Box B

iff

for all
v\geq w
, for all
u
, if
v\mathcal{R}u
, then
\mathcal{M},u\Vdash B
;

\mathcal{M},w\Vdash\Diamond B

iff

for all
v\geq w
, there is
u
such that
v\mathcal{R}u
and
\mathcal{M},u\Vdash B
.

The semantics in Definition
2.6
essentially coincides with
Wijesekera’s
[
43
]
semantics for
(the propositional fragment of)
Constructive Concurrent Dynamic Logic
with the only difference of the addition of the fallible worlds,
which is due to the fact that the base
models are minimal rather than intuitionistic.

The generalisation of the standard clauses for
\Box,\Diamond
in the relational semantics
to all
\leq
-successors
is the simplest way to preserve the hereditary property of minimal relational models.

Proposition 2.1

(Hereditary property)
.

Given a minimal birelational model
\mathcal{M}
and a formula
A
of
\mathcal{L}
, for every worlds
w
and
v
of
\mathcal{M}
it holds:
If
w\Vdash A
and
w\leq v
, then
v\Vdash A
.

Proof.

Immediate by induction on the construction of
A
.
∎

Theorem 2.2

(Soundness)
.

For all
A\in\mathcal{L}
,
if
A
is derivable in
\mathsf{M.K}
, then
A
is valid in every minimal birelational model.

Proof.

By showing that all the axioms and rules of
\mathsf{M.K}
are valid, respectively validity preserving, in every model for
\mathsf{M.K}
.
We consider the modal principles.

(
{K_{\Box}}
)

Suppose that
w\Vdash\Box(A\supset B)
and
w\Vdash\Box A
. Then for all
v,u
, if
w\leq v
and
v\mathcal{R}u
, then

u\Vdash A\supset B
and
u\Vdash A
, hence
u\Vdash B
.
Thus,
w\Vdash\Box B
.
Therefore
\mathcal{M}\models\Box(A\supset B)\supset(\Box A\supset\Box B)
.

(
{K_{\Diamond}}
)

Suppose that
w\Vdash\Box(A\supset B)
and
w\Vdash\Diamond A
. Then for all
v
, if
w\leq v
, then
there is
u
such that
v\mathcal{R}u
and
u\Vdash A
. Moreover,
u\Vdash A\supset B
.
Thus
u\Vdash B
, hence
w\Vdash\Diamond B
.
Therefore
\mathcal{M}\models\Box(A\supset B)\supset(\Diamond A\supset\Diamond B)
.

(
{nec}
)

Suppose that
\mathcal{M}\models A
. Then for all
w,v,u
, if
w\leq v
and
v\mathcal{R}u
, then
u\Vdash A
, hence
w\Vdash\Box A
. Therefore
\mathcal{M}\models\Box A
.∎

We now present a completeness proof for
\mathsf{M.K}
with respect to the minimal birelational semantics
by the canonical model technique.
The proof adapts the completeness proof for
\mathsf{W.K}
by Wijesekera
[
43
]
with the addition of the impossible worlds.
For every logic
\mathsf{L}
in
\mathcal{L}
, we call
\mathsf{L}
-
full
any set
\Phi
of formulas of
\mathcal{L}

such that
if
\Phi\vdash_{\mathsf{L}}A
, then
A\in\Phi
(closure under derivation), and
if
A\lor B\in\Phi
, then
A\in\Phi
or
B\in\Phi
(disjunction property).
Moreover, for every set of formulas
\Phi
,
we denote
\Box^{-}\Phi
the set
\{A\mid\Box A\in\Phi\}
.
One can prove in a standard way the following lemma.

Lemma 2.3

(Lindenbaum)
.

For every set
\Phi
of formulas of
\mathcal{L}
, there is a
\mathsf{M.K}
-full set
\Psi
such that
\Phi\subseteq\Psi
.
Moreover, if
\Phi\not\vdash_{\mathsf{M.K}}A
, then there is a
\mathsf{M.K}
-full set
\Psi
such that
\Phi\subseteq\Psi
and
A\notin\Psi
.

Definition 2.7
.

For every logic
\mathsf{L}
in
\mathcal{L}
,
an
\mathsf{L}
-
relational segment
, or just
segment
, is a pair
(\Phi,\mathscr{U})
,
where
\Phi
is an
\mathsf{L}
-full set, and
\mathscr{U}
is a set of
\mathsf{L}
-full sets such that:

•

if
\Box A\in\Phi
, then for all
\Psi\in\mathscr{U}
,
A\in\Psi
; and

•

if
\Diamond A\in\Phi
, then there is
\Psi\in\mathscr{U}
such that
A\in\Psi
.

The following holds.

Lemma 2.4
.

For every
\mathsf{M.K}
-full set
\Phi
, there exists an
\mathsf{M.K}
-relational segment
(\Phi,\mathscr{U})
.

Proof.

Given a
\mathsf{M.K}
-full set
\Phi
, we define

\mathscr{U}=\{\Psi\ \mathsf{M.K}\textup{-full}\mid\Box^{-}\Phi\subseteq\Psi\textup{ and }B\in\Psi\textup{ for some }\Diamond B\in\Phi\}
.
Then by definition, for all
\Box A\in\Phi
and all
\Psi\in\mathscr{U}
,
A\in\Psi
.
Moreover, suppose that
\Diamond A\in\Phi
.
By Lemma
2.3
, there is an
\mathsf{M.K}
-full set
\Psi
such that
\Box^{-}\Phi\cup\{A\}\subseteq\Psi
,
then
A\in\Psi
and
\Psi\in\mathscr{U}
.
Hence
(\Phi,\mathscr{U})
is an
\mathsf{M.K}
-segment.
∎

Definition 2.8
.

For every logic
\mathsf{L}
in
\mathcal{L}
,
the
canonical birelational model
for
\mathsf{L}
is
the tuple
\mathcal{M}=\langle\mathcal{W},\leq,\mathbb{F},\mathcal{R},\mathcal{V}\rangle
,
where:

•

\mathcal{W}
is the class of all
\mathsf{L}
-relational segments;

•

for all
(\Phi,\mathscr{U}),(\Psi,\mathscr{V})\in\mathcal{W}
,
(\Phi,\mathscr{U})\leq(\Psi,\mathscr{V})
if and only if
\Phi\subseteq\Psi
;

•

for all
(\Phi,\mathscr{U})\in\mathcal{W}
,
(\Phi,\mathscr{U})\in\mathbb{F}
if and only if
\bot\in\Phi
;

•

for all
(\Phi,\mathscr{U}),(\Psi,\mathscr{V})\in\mathcal{W}
,
(\Phi,\mathscr{U})\mathcal{R}(\Psi,\mathscr{V})
if and only if
\Psi\in\mathscr{U}
;

•

for all
(\Phi,\mathscr{U})\in\mathcal{W}
,
(\Phi,\mathscr{U})\in\mathcal{V}(p)
if and only if
p\in\Phi
.

It is easy to see that the canonical birelational model for
\mathsf{M.K}
is a minimal birelational model
(Definition
2.6
).
We prove the following lemma.

Lemma 2.5
.

Let
\mathcal{M}=\langle\mathcal{W},\leq,\mathbb{F},\mathcal{R},\mathcal{V}\rangle
be the canonical birelational model for
\mathsf{M.K}
. Then for all
(\Phi,\mathscr{U})\in\mathcal{W}
and all
A\in\mathcal{L}
,

(\Phi,\mathscr{U})\Vdash A
if and only if
A\in\Phi
.

Proof.

By induction on the construction of
A
.
For the base case
A=p
and the inductive cases
A=B\land C,B\lor C
the proof is immediate.
We show the other cases, writing
\vdash
for
\vdash_{\mathsf{M.K}}
.

(
A=\bot
)

(\Phi,\mathscr{U})\Vdash\bot
iff
(\Phi,\mathscr{U})\in\mathbb{F}
iff, by definition,
\bot\in\Phi
.

(
A=B\supset C
)

Suppose that
B\supset C\in\Phi
, and assume
(\Phi,\mathscr{U})\leq(\Psi,\mathscr{V})
and
(\Psi,\mathscr{V})\Vdash B
.
By definition,
\Phi\subseteq\Psi
, thus
B\supset C\in\Psi
.
Moreover by i.h.,
B\in\Psi
, hence
C\in\Psi
, thus by i.h.,
(\Psi,\mathscr{V})\Vdash C
.
Therefore
(\Phi,\mathscr{U})\Vdash B\supset C
.
Now suppose that
B\supset C\notin\Phi
.
Then
\Phi\not\vdash B\supset C
, thus
\Phi\cup\{B\}\not\vdash C
.
By Lemma
2.3
, there is
\Psi

\mathsf{M.K}
-full such that
\Phi\cup\{B\}\subseteq\Psi
and
C\notin\Psi
.
Then by Lemma
2.4
and Definition
2.8
, there is an
\mathsf{M.K}
-segment

(\Psi,\mathscr{V})
, and
(\Psi,\mathscr{V})\in\mathcal{W}
.
Thus by definition,
(\Phi,\mathscr{U})\leq(\Psi,\mathscr{V})
, and by i.h.,
(\Psi,\mathscr{V})\Vdash B
and
(\Psi,\mathscr{V})\not\Vdash C
.
Therefore
(\Phi,\mathscr{U})\not\Vdash B\supset C
.

(
A=\Box B
)

Suppose that
\Box B\in\Phi
.
Then for all
(\Psi,\mathscr{V})\geq(\Phi,\mathscr{U})
,
\Box B\in\Psi
.
Moreover by definition, if
(\Psi,\mathscr{V})\mathcal{R}(\Theta,\mathscr{Z})
,
then
\Theta\in\mathscr{V}
,
hence by definition of segment,
B\in\Theta
.
Then by i.h.,
(\Theta,\mathscr{Z})\Vdash B
, therefore
(\Phi,\mathscr{U})\Vdash\Box B
.
Now suppose that
\Box B\notin\Phi
.
Then
\Box^{-}\Phi\not\vdash B

(indeed, if
\Box^{-}\Phi\vdash B
,
then
\vdash C_{1}\land...\land C_{n}\supset B
for some
\Box C_{1},...,\Box C_{n}\in\Phi
,
then by
{nec}
,
\vdash\Box(C_{1}\land...\land C_{n}\supset B)
,
and by
{K_{\Box}}
,
\vdash\Box(C_{1}\land...\land C_{n})\supset\Box B
;
since
\vdash\Box C_{1}\land...\land\Box C_{n}\supset\Box(C_{1}\land...\land C_{n})
,
we have
\vdash\Box C_{1}\land...\land\Box C_{n}\supset\Box B
,
hence
\Phi\vdash\Box B
, therefore
\Box B\in\Phi
,
against the assumption).
By Lemma
2.3
, there is
\Psi

\mathsf{M.K}
-full such that
\Box^{-}\Phi\subseteq\Psi
and
B\notin\Psi
.
We define
\mathscr{V}=\{\Psi\}\cup\{\Theta\ \mathsf{M.K}\textup{-full}\mid\Box^{-}\Phi\subseteq\Theta\textup{ and }C\in\Theta\textup{ for some }\Diamond C\in\Phi\}
.
Given that, by Lemma
2.3
, such a set
\Theta
exists for every
\Diamond C\in\Phi
,
we have that
(\Phi,\mathscr{V})
is an
\mathsf{M.K}
-segment.
Furthermore, by Lemma
2.4
, there exists an
\mathsf{M.K}
-segment
(\Psi,\mathscr{Z})
,
hence since
\Psi\in\mathscr{V}
, by definition,
(\Phi,\mathscr{V})\mathcal{R}(\Psi,\mathscr{Z})
.
Moreover, since
B\notin\Psi
, by i.h.,
(\Psi,\mathscr{Z})\not\Vdash B
, then since
(\Phi,\mathscr{U})\leq(\Phi,\mathscr{V})
,
we have

(\Phi,\mathscr{U})\not\Vdash\Box B
.

(
A=\Diamond B
)

Suppose that
\Diamond B\in\Phi
. Then for all
(\Psi,\mathscr{V})\geq(\Phi,\mathscr{U})
,
\Diamond B\in\Psi
.
Thus by Definition
2.7
, there is
\Theta\in\mathscr{V})
such that
B\in\Theta
,
and by Lemma
2.4
, there is a segment
(\Theta,\mathscr{Z})\in\mathcal{M}
.
Moreover, by definition,
(\Psi,\mathscr{V})\mathcal{R}(\Theta,\mathscr{Z})
, and by i.h.,
(\Theta,\mathscr{Z})\Vdash B
.
It follows that
(\Phi,\mathscr{U})\Vdash\Diamond B
.
Now suppose that
\Diamond B\notin\Phi
.
Then for every
\Diamond C\in\Phi
,

\Box^{-}\Phi\cup\{C\}\not\vdash B

(indeed, if
\Box^{-}\Phi\cup\{C\}\vdash B
,
then
\vdash D_{1}\land...\land D_{n}\land C\supset B
for some
\Box D_{1},...,\Box D_{n}\in\Phi
,
thus
\vdash D_{1}\land...\land D_{n}\supset(C\supset B)
,
hence
\vdash\Box(D_{1}\land...\land D_{n})\supset\Box(C\supset B)
,
then by
{K_{\Diamond}}
and valid principles,

\vdash\Box D_{1}\land...\land\Box D_{n}\supset(\Diamond C\supset\Diamond B)
,
so

\vdash\Box D_{1}\land...\land\Box D_{n}\land\Diamond C\supset\Diamond B
,
which implies

\Phi\vdash\Diamond B
,
hence, finally,
\Diamond B\in\Phi
,
against the assumption).
We define
\mathscr{V}=\{\Psi\ \mathsf{M.K}\textup{-full}\mid\Box^{-}\Phi\subseteq\Psi,B\notin\Psi\textup{ and }C\in\Psi\textup{ for some }\Diamond C\in\Phi\}
.
By Lemma
2.3
,
such a set
\Psi
exists for every
\Diamond C\in\Psi
.
It is easy to see that
(\Phi,\mathscr{V})
is a
\mathsf{M.K}
-segment,
hence
(\Phi,\mathscr{V})\in\mathcal{W}
.
Moreover, by definition, for all
(\Psi,\mathscr{Z})
such that

(\Phi,\mathscr{V})\mathcal{R}(\Psi,\mathscr{Z})
,

B\notin\Psi
, thus by i.h.,
(\Psi,\mathscr{Z})\not\Vdash B
.
Given that
(\Phi,\mathscr{U})\leq(\Phi,\mathscr{V})
,
we obtain
(\Phi,\mathscr{U})\not\Vdash\Diamond B
.∎

Theorem 2.6

(Completeness)
.

For all
A\in\mathcal{L}
,
if
A
is valid in every minimal birelational model, then
A
is derivable in
\mathsf{M.K}
.

Proof.

Suppose that

\mathsf{M.K}\not\vdash A
.
Then by Lemma
2.3
, there is an
\mathsf{M.K}
-full set
\Psi
such that
A\notin\Psi
,
hence by Lemma
2.4
,
there exists an
\mathsf{M.K}
-segment
(\Psi,\mathscr{U})
. By Definition
2.8
,
(\Psi,\mathscr{U})
belongs to the canonical model
\mathcal{M}
for
\mathsf{M.K}
,
then by Lemma
2.5
,
(\Psi,\mathscr{U})\not\Vdash A
.
Since
\mathcal{M}
is a minimal birelational model,
we conclude that it is not the case that

A
is valid in all models for
\mathsf{M.K}
.
∎

Based on this semantic characterisation,
we now show that
\mathsf{M.K}
is the solution to the equation (
\ast
) for
\mathsf{L}
replaced with
\mathsf{K}
,
hence, according to our criterion, it is the minimal counterpart of classical
\mathsf{K}
.

Theorem 2.7
.

For all
A\in\mathcal{L}
,

\mathsf{M.K}

\mathsf{S4}\oplus\mathsf{K}

\mathsf{S4}\oplus\mathsf{K}

\mathcal{R}_{2}

\mathcal{W}

\mathcal{R}_{1}

(

)

\mathsf{S4}\oplus\mathsf{K}\not\vdash A^{t}

\mathcal{W}
of
\mathcal{M}
,

Relating minimal K and constructive K

The minimal modal logic
\mathsf{M.K}
just defined is strictly related to the constructive modal logic
\mathsf{C.K}

studied in the literature.
In particular, from an axiomatical perspective,
\mathsf{C.K}
coincides with the extension of
\mathsf{M.K}
with ex falso quodlibet
\bot\supset A
, exactly as
\mathsf{IPL}
amounts to
\mathsf{MPL}
+
\bot\supset A
.
Except for the different propositional base,
\mathsf{M.K}
and
\mathsf{C.K}
share the same modal principles.
In this section, we show that
\mathsf{C.K}
is also strictly related to
\mathsf{M.K}

both from a semantical and from a proof theoretical point of view.

4.1
Semantics

As recalled in Section
1.2
,
disregarding the modalities, there are two
ways to transform relational models for
\mathsf{MPL}
into relational models for
\mathsf{IPL}
:
(1)
assuming
\mathbb{F}=\emptyset
,
thus obtaining Kripke’s intuitionistic relational models,
or
(2) preserving the fallible worlds but
ensuring the validity of ex falso quodlibet
by
assuming
\mathbb{F}\subseteq\mathcal{V}(p)
for all
p\in\mathit{Atm}
.
Interestingly, the two ways are equivalent for propositional logic,
as they both provide a semantics for
\mathsf{IPL}
,
but they are not equivalent in presence of the modalities.
In particular,
if applied to minimal birelational models,
the restriction (1) gives relational models for
\mathsf{W.K}
as defined in
[
43
]
.
By contrast, a suitable adaptation of (2) which esures the validity of
\bot\supset A
also
in presence of the modalities
gives birelational models for
\mathsf{C.K}
.

Definition 4.1

(Constructive birelational semantics)

(ii)

;

(iii)

there is
v
such that
w\mathcal{R}v
.

Analogous constructive birelational models for
\mathsf{C.K}
were defined in
[

]
.
The models in Definition

are slightly different because of the latter condition (iii)
which is not considered in
[
31
]
.
We observe however that this (or a similar) condition
is necessary in order to ensure the validity of ex falso quodlibet over the whole language
\mathcal{L}
.
To see this, consider a model

satisfying (i) and (ii) but not (iii), where

Lemma 5.4
.

For every minimal modal logic
\mathsf{M.L}
, the canonical neighbourhood model
\mathcal{M}
for
\mathsf{M.L}
is a minimal neighbourhood model for
\mathsf{M.L}

Proof.

It is easy to very that
\mathcal{M}
is a minimal neighbourhood model.
We show that
\mathcal{M}
satisfies the conditions among
(C), (N), (P), (D), (T)
associated to the axioms of
\mathsf{M.L}
.

(C)

Suppose that
\alpha,\beta\in\mathcal{N}((\Phi,\mathscr{C}))
. Then, by definition,

\alpha=\alpha_{\mathscr{U}}

\beta=\alpha_{\mathscr{V}}
for some
\mathscr{U},\mathscr{V}\in\mathscr{C}

By the property (C-s) of
\mathsf{M.L}
-segments,
\mathscr{U}\cap\mathscr{V}\in\mathscr{C}

\alpha_{\mathscr{U}\cap\mathscr{V}}=\{(\Phi,\mathscr{C})\mid\Phi\in\mathscr{U}\cap\mathscr{V}\}=\{(\Phi,\mathscr{C})\mid\Phi\in\mathscr{U}\}\cap\{(\Phi,\mathscr{C})\mid\Phi\in\mathscr{V}\}=\alpha_{\mathscr{U}}\cap\alpha_{\mathscr{V}}=\alpha\cap\beta
.

(N)

For all
\mathsf{M.L}
-full sets
\Phi
,
\Box\top\in\Phi
,
then for all
\mathsf{M.L}
-segments
(\Phi,\mathscr{C})
,
\mathscr{C}\not=\emptyset
,
thus
\mathcal{N}((\Phi,\mathscr{C}))\not=\emptyset
.

(P)

For all
\mathsf{M.L}
-full sets
\Phi
,
\Diamond\top\in\Phi
,
then for all
\mathsf{M.L}
-segments
(\Phi,\mathscr{C})
and all
\mathscr{U}\in\mathscr{C}

\mathscr{U}\not=\emptyset

thus for all
\alpha_{\mathscr{U}}\in\mathcal{N}((\Phi,\mathscr{C}))

\alpha_{\mathscr{U}}\not=\emptyset

that is,

(D)

Suppose that
\alpha,\beta\in\mathcal{N}((\Phi,\mathscr{C}))
. Then

\alpha=\alpha_{\mathscr{U}}
and
\beta=\alpha_{\mathscr{V}}
for some
\mathscr{U},\mathscr{V}\in\mathscr{C}
.
By (D-s),
\mathscr{U}\cap\mathscr{V}\not=\emptyset

(T)

Suppose that
\alpha\in\mathcal{N}((\Phi,\mathscr{C}))
. Then

\alpha=\alpha_{\mathscr{U}}
for an
\mathscr{U}\in\mathscr{C}

By (T-s),
\Phi\in\mathscr{U}

(\Phi,\mathscr{C})\in\alpha_{\mathscr{U}}=\alpha

6
Discussion

A framework of minimal and constructive modal logics.

We have defined a family of minimal modal logics and a related family of constructive modal logics
corresponding each to a different classical modal logic.
The minimal modal logics have been defined
by
means of (1) a reduction to fusions of classical modal logics via the extended Gödel-Johansson translation,
as well as (2) the restriction of sequent calculi for classical modal logics to single-succedent sequents.
We have seen that the resulting minimal counterpart of
\mathsf{K}
is strictly connected with the constructive modal logic
\mathsf{C.K}
,
as the two systems essentially validate the same modal principles.
In particular,
\mathsf{C.K}
can be obtained from
\mathsf{M.K}

(1) semantically, by forcing the validity of ex falso quodlibet in minimal birelational models;
(2) based on the sequent calculi, by adding the minimal modal rules to an intuitionistic sequent calculus;
(3) axiomatically, by extending
\mathsf{M.K}
with ex falso quodlibet
\bot\supset A
.
Based on this
relation between
\mathsf{M.K}
and
\mathsf{C.K}
, we have defined a constructive correspondent for each minimal system.
Among the resulting constructive logics, the systems

\mathsf{C.K}
,
\mathsf{C.KD}
and
\mathsf{C.KT}
coincides with the constructive counterparts of
\mathsf{K}
,
\mathsf{KD}
and
\mathsf{KT}
studied in

[
2
,
32
]

(
\mathsf{C.KT}
also coincides with the propositional fragment of Fitch’s first-order intuitionistic modal logic
[
14
]
).
All in all, this work
organises some constructive modal logics already studied in the literature into a uniform framework
and also extends this family with constructive counterparts of some non-normal modal logics.
As a consequence of the applied methodology, all minimal and constructive modal logics are
endowed with cut-free sequent calculi and a modular semantic characterisation.

Simpson’s requirements.
Simpson
[
41
]
listed some requirements to evaluate whether an intuitionistic modal logic
\mathsf{IL}
can be understood as an intuitionistic counterpart of a classical modal logic
\mathsf{L}
:

\mathsf{IL}
should be a conservative extension of
\mathsf{IPL}
,
it should contain all axioms of
\mathsf{IPL}
(over the whole language
\mathcal{L}
) and be closed under modus ponens,
it should satisfy the disjunction property
(if
A\lor B
is derivable, then
A
is derivable or
B
is derivable),
the modalities in
\mathsf{IL}
should be independent,
the extension of
\mathsf{IL}
with
A\lor\neg A
should coincide with
\mathsf{L}
.
It looks natural to adapt these requirements to pairs of minimal and constructive/intuitionistic modal logics.
It is easy to verify that each logic
\mathsf{M.L}

is a conservative extension of
\mathsf{MPL}
,
contains all axioms of
\mathsf{MPL}
and modus ponens,
satisfies the disjunction property
and has independent modalities.
Moreover, the extension of
\mathsf{M.L}
with
\bot\supset A
coincides with the corresponding logic
\mathsf{C.L}
.
In this sense, each pair of corresponding logics
\mathsf{M.L}
and
\mathsf{C.L}
is a Simpsonian pair of modal logics.

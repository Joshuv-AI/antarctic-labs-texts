On the Uniqueness and Orbital Stability of Slow and Fast Solitary Wave Solutions of the Benjamin Equation
May ABDALLAH, Mohamad DARWICH, Luc MOLINET

Abstract
This paper is devoted to the study of existence and properties of solitary waves of the Benjamin equation. The studied equation includes a parameter γ \gamma in front of the Benjamin-Ono term. We show the existence, uniqueness, decay and orbital stability of solitary wave solutions obtained as a solution to a certain minimization problem, associated either with high speeds without a sign condition on the parameter γ \gamma or with low speeds for the appropriate sign.



Keywords
: Solitary waves - Benjamin equation - Korteweg-de Vries equation

-Constraint Minimization Problem - Orbital Stability

1
Introduction

We consider the Benjamin equation

\partial_{t}u+u_{xxx}+\gamma\,\mathcal{H}u_{xx}+u\partial_{x}u=0,\quad(t,x)\in{I\!\!R}^{2},

(1.1)

where
u
is a real-valued function,
\mathcal{H}
denotes the Hilbert transform and
\gamma\in{I\!\!R}^{*}
.
Here, the Hilbert transform
\mathcal{H}
is defined by

\mathcal{H}v\,(x)=p.v.\frac{1}{\pi}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\frac{v(y)}{y-x}\ dy=\frac{1}{\pi}\lim_{\varepsilon\rightarrow 0}\int_{|y-x|>\varepsilon}\frac{v(y)}{y-x}\ dy.

(1.2)

With this notation, we note that
\displaystyle\int u_{x}\mathcal{H}u=\int|D_{x}^{\frac{1}{2}}u|^{2}=\|u\|_{\dot{H}^{\frac{1}{2}}}^{2}
.
Moreover,
\mathcal{H}
can be written as a Fourier multiplier operator given by

\widehat{\mathcal{H}(u)}(\xi)=i\ \text{sgn}(\xi)\ \hat{u}(\xi)\;.

In the case
\gamma<0
, this equation has been derived in
[
7
]
(see also
[
8
]
and
[
2
]
) as an asymptotic model for the propagation of long interfacial waves when the surface tension cannot be safely ignored. In this context,
u
represents the vertical displacement of the interface between the two fluids. Actually, physically, it could be reduced to the Korteweg-de Vries (KdV) equation

u_{t}+u_{xxx}+uu_{x}=0

when the dispersive effects are dominated by the surface tension, and to the Benjamin-Ono (BO) equation

u_{t}-\mathcal{H}u_{xx}+uu_{x}=0

when the surface tension is negligible.

Equation (
1.1
) enjoys the following conservation laws

\text{Energy:}\quad E(u(t))=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\bigg(\frac{1}{2}(\partial_{x}u)^{2}+\frac{\gamma}{2}(D_{x}^{\frac{1}{2}}u)^{2}-\frac{1}{6}u^{3}\bigg)dx\quad=E(u(0)),

(1.3)

and

\text{Mass:}\quad M(u(t))=\frac{1}{2}\ \int_{\mathbb{R}}u^{2}dx\quad=M(u(0)),

and reduces to KdV when
\gamma=0
. It is worth noticing that the physical case, associated with the condition
\gamma<0
, is where the KdV operator
\partial_{x}^{3}
and the Benjamin-Ono (BO) operator
\mathcal{H}\partial_{x}^{2}
compete together. In particular, the quadratic part of the energy is then not positively defined. Note also that, whereas the KdV and BO equations are completely integrable systems, the Benjamin equation is not known to be integrable.

It is well known that the Cauchy problem associated with (
1.1
) is globally well-posed in the energy space
H^{1}(\mathbb{R})
(see
[
20
]
) and even in
H^{s}(\mathbb{R})
for
s\geq 0
(see
[
23
]
). In this work, we are mainly interested in the existence and properties of traveling wave solutions of (
1.1
), that is, solutions of the form

u(x,t)=\varphi(x-ct)

(1.4)

with
c>0
and
\varphi(x)\to 0
as
|x|\to+\infty
. Substituting (
1.4
) into equation (
1.1
), followed by integration on
\mathbb{R}
, we infer that such profiles
\varphi
satisfy

c\ \varphi-\ \varphi^{\prime\prime}+\gamma\mathcal{H}\varphi^{\prime}=\frac{1}{2}\varphi^{2}.

(1.5)

The existence, spatial asymptotics and orbital stability of solitary waves to (
1.1
) have already been studied in several papers. In
[
7
]
and
[
8
]
, a Leray-Schauder degree theory approach is used to show the existence of solitary wave profiles to (
1.1
) with
\gamma<0
for a speed
c>\gamma^{2}/4
. In
[
2
]
, the implicit function theorem together with the existence and stability result in the case
\gamma=0
(KdV equation) is used to prove the existence and uniqueness of even solitary wave profiles to (
1.1
) for
|\gamma|
small enough. This argument together with classical perturbation theory and the criterion for orbital stability established in the seminal paper
[
13
]
enables then to obtain the orbital stability of these solitary waves for
|\gamma|
small enough. Finally, variational approaches are used in
[
11
]
and
[
4
]
to prove the existence of solitary waves for

c>\gamma^{2}/4
whenever
\gamma<0
. In
[
11
]
, an optimal decay of the solitary waves is also provided whereas in
[
4
]
the stability of the set of ground states is established for the same range of speed.

Our goal in this work is to complete these results in some directions and even give another proof for some of them. We first prove the existence of solitary wave profiles to (
1.1
) for
c>\gamma^{2}/4
in the case
\gamma<0
and for
c>0
in the case
\gamma>0
. To do this, we use a variational approach as in
[
4
]
but instead of minimizing the Energy with the Mass as a constraint, following
[
11
]
(see also
[
22
]
), we minimize the quadratic part of the action functional
E+cM
under the constraint of its cubic part. This has the advantage of being really simpler but has the disadvantage of not directly leading to the orbital stability of the set of ground states. Actually in this work, we consider only the uniqueness and orbital stability for fast or slow solitary waves. First, by a dilation argument, we notice that this is equivalent to looking at solutions of (
1.7
) below with either
\alpha=1
and small
|\beta|
, or
\beta=1
and small
\alpha>0
. Then, we follow the ideas of
[
19
]
. We prove the convergence of our ground state solutions of (
1.11
) to the KdV soliton profile of speed
1
in case
\alpha=1
and
\beta\to 0
, and to the BO soliton profile of speed
1
in case
\beta=1
and
\alpha\searrow 0
. Later, we establish the uniqueness and orbital stability in
H^{1}(\mathbb{R})
of our ground state solutions in both cases when either
\beta
or
\alpha>0
are close enough to
0
by a perturbation argument making use of known results on the coercivity of the second derivative of the action functional associated respectively with the KdV and the BO equation. Note that this type of approaches can also be found in
[
18
]
and
[
16
]
. Finally, we follow arguments developed in
[
12
]
to prove decay estimates for even
H^{1}
-solutions to (
1.5
) as well as their first derivatives. Briefly, compared to existing results, our main contributions are essentially the uniqueness and orbital stability of the slow ground state solutions in the case
\gamma>0
. Note also that, in contrary to the arguments in
[
2
]
, our arguments have the advantage of being applicable for both slow and fast solitary waves.

To end this introduction, we would like to emphasize that, as noticed in
[
21
]
, classical Pohozaev identities lead to the non existence of solitary wave with speed
c\leq-\gamma^{2}/5
in the case
\gamma<0
(and
c\leq 0
in the case
\gamma>0
). Therefore, the question of existence of solitary waves to (
1.1
) with
\gamma<0
and speed
c\in]-\gamma^{2}/5,\gamma^{2}/4]
is still open.

1.1
Main Results

By using dilation symmetries, we will connect the study of (
1.1
) to the study of the following equation

\partial_{t}u+\alpha\partial_{x}^{3}u+\beta\mathcal{H}\partial_{x}^{2}u+u\partial_{x}u=0

(1.6)

with either
\alpha=1
and
|\beta|\ll 1
or
\beta=1
and
0<\alpha\ll 1
. Considering traveling wave solutions with speed
c
,
\varphi_{c}(x-c\,t)
of (
1.6
), we observe that they solve

c\varphi-\alpha\varphi^{\prime\prime}-\beta\mathcal{H}\varphi^{\prime}=\frac{1}{2}\varphi^{2}.

(1.7)

Note that any solution
\varphi_{c,\alpha,\beta}
to (
1.7
) will have the index
\{c,\alpha,\beta\}
referring to different values of
c,\ \alpha,
and
\beta
, with
\varphi_{\alpha,\beta}
the particular solution of (
1.11
) corresponding to
c=1
. Following
[
22
]
, we set

I(\varphi)=I_{c,\alpha,\beta}(\varphi)=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\bigg(\alpha(\varphi_{x})^{2}+\beta(D_{x}^{\frac{1}{2}}\varphi)^{2}+c\varphi^{2}\bigg)dx\;,

(1.8)

K(\varphi)=\frac{1}{6}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\varphi^{3}dx\ ,

(1.9)

and consider the constraint-minimization problem

M^{\lambda}=M^{\lambda}_{c,\alpha,\beta}=\inf\{I_{c,\alpha,\beta}(\varphi);\ \varphi\in H^{1}_{e}\ \text{such \ that}\ K(\varphi)=\lambda\}\;,

(1.10)

where
\lambda>0
and
H_{e}^{s}(\mathbb{R})
is the closed subspace of
H^{s}({I\!\!R})
defined for
s\geq 0
by

H_{e}^{s}(\mathbb{R})=\{u\in H^{s}(\mathbb{R})\,/\ u(-y)=u(y)\ \forall\ y\in\mathbb{R}\}\;.

For our purpose, we will mainly work with traveling wave solutions with speed
c=1
that satisfy

\varphi-\alpha\varphi^{\prime\prime}-\beta\mathcal{H}\varphi^{\prime}=\frac{1}{2}\varphi^{2}.

(1.11)

Note that upon integrating this equation against
\varphi
, it can be checked that any
H^{1}(\mathbb{R})
-solution to (
1.11
) satisfies

2\ I_{1,\alpha,\beta}(\varphi)=3\ K(\varphi).

(1.12)

Before stating our main result, let’s define some of the notions to be used throughout this work.

Definition 1.1
.

(Ground state solution)
An even ground state solution of (
1.5
) is an
H^{1}({I\!\!R})
-solution to (
1.5
) that solves the constraint minimization problem
M^{\lambda}_{c,1,\gamma}
for some
\lambda>0
.

Definition 1.2
.

(Orbital stability)
Let
v\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R})
be a solution to (
1.5
). We say that
v
is orbitally stable in
H^{1}(\mathbb{R})
if for any
\delta>0
, there exists
\eta>0
such that for all initial data
u_{0}\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R})
satisfying

\|u_{0}-v\|_{H^{1}}\leq\eta,

the solution
u\in C(\mathbb{R}_{+},H^{1})
associated to the initial data
u_{0}
in turn satisfies

\sup_{t\in\mathbb{R}_{+}}\inf_{z\in\mathbb{R}}\|u(t,.+z)-v\|_{H^{1}}\leq\delta.

We are now in position to state our main results.

Theorem 1.3
.

[Existence, uniqueness, asymptotic behavior and orbital stability of traveling waves]

(
1.1
) possesses traveling waves solutions with any speed
c>0
whenever
\gamma>0
and with any speed
c>\gamma^{2}/4
whenever
\gamma<0
. The profile
\varphi
of this traveling wave is an even function belonging to
H^{\infty}({I\!\!R})
and satisfying

x^{2}|\varphi|+|x^{3}|(|\varphi^{\prime}|+|\varphi^{\prime\prime}|)\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})\;.

Moreover, there exists
r_{2}>r_{1}>0
such that :

•

If
\gamma>0
, then for
c\in]0,r_{1}\gamma^{2}[\cup]r_{2}\gamma^{2},+\infty[
this profile is the unique even ground state solution to (
1.1
) and is orbitally stable.

•

If
\gamma<0
, then for
c\in]r_{2}\gamma^{2},+\infty[
this profile is the unique even ground state solution to (
1.1
) and is orbitally stable.

Remark 1.4
.

In the case
\gamma>0
, it follows from
[
26
]
that any solution of the constraint minimization problem

\widetilde{M}^{\lambda}_{c,\alpha,\beta}=\inf\{I_{c,\alpha,\beta}(\varphi);\ \varphi\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R})\ \text{such that}\ K(\varphi)=\lambda\}\

has to be an even function. Therefore, the uniqueness result still holds when replacing
M^{\lambda}_{c,1,\gamma}
by
\widetilde{M}^{\lambda}_{c,1,\gamma}
. In other words, we do not need to minimize in the set of even functions to get the uniqueness.

-solution to (

) as well as for its first derivatives.

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
c=1
,
\alpha\in]0,1]
and
\beta\in\displaystyle]-2\sqrt{\alpha},+\infty[
, then
\sqrt{I_{c,\alpha,\beta}}
is a norm issued from a scalar product on
H^{1}(\mathbb{R})
, and is equivalent to the classical norm of
H^{1}(\mathbb{R})
.

Proof.

First, it is clear that
I(u)
is associated to a bilinear form on
H^{1}(\mathbb{R})
. Let
u\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R})
.
Taking the Fourier Transform (Plancherel Identity), it holds

2I_{1,\alpha,\beta}(u)=\bigg[1+\beta|\xi|+\alpha|\xi|^{2}\bigg]|\hat{u}|^{2}\ .

In the case
\beta\geq 0
, we trivially have

1+\beta|\xi|+\alpha|\xi|^{2}\geq 1+\alpha|\xi|^{2}\geq\frac{1}{2}(1+|\xi|+\alpha|\xi|^{2})\geq\frac{1}{2}\min(\alpha,1)(1+|\xi|+|\xi|^{2}).

Now for
-2\sqrt{\alpha}<\beta<0
, we may rewrite
\beta
as
\beta=-2\sqrt{\alpha-\varepsilon}(1-\varepsilon)
with
0<\varepsilon<\alpha
.
Noticing that

\min_{\xi\in{I\!\!R}}\Bigl[(1-\varepsilon)-2\sqrt{\alpha-\varepsilon}(1-\varepsilon)|\xi|+(\alpha-\varepsilon)|\xi|^{2}\Bigr]=0,

this leads to

1+\beta|\xi|+\alpha|\xi|^{2}\geq\varepsilon(1+|\xi|^{2})\geq\frac{\varepsilon}{2}(1+|\xi|+|\xi|^{2}).

Hence, it holds

I_{1,\alpha,\beta}(u)\geq C_{\alpha,\beta}\|u\|^{2}_{H^{1}}\;.

(2.5)

for some
C_{\alpha,\beta}>0
.

So,
I
is coercive in
H^{1}(\mathbb{R})
. Besides, for both cases of
\alpha
and
\beta
, one can see that

I(u)\leq\frac{3}{4}\max(1,|\beta|)\|u\|^{2}_{H^{1}}\ .

Eventually,
\sqrt{I_{1,\alpha,\beta}}
is equivalent to the
H^{1}
-norm.
∎

Lemma 2.3
.

M^{\lambda}>0
for any
\lambda>0
. Moreover, for any
(\lambda,\lambda_{0})\in\mathbb{R}^{*}_{+}\times\mathbb{R}^{*}_{+}
it holds

M^{\lambda}=\bigg(\frac{\lambda}{\lambda_{0}}\bigg)^{\frac{2}{3}}M^{\lambda_{0}}\quad\text{and}\quad M^{\lambda_{0}}<M^{\lambda}+M^{\lambda_{0}-\lambda}\;.

(2.6)

Proof.

Let
\lambda>0
. Suppose to the contrary that there exists
(u_{n})
, a minimizing sequence of
M^{\lambda}
, such that
I(u_{n})\longrightarrow_{n\rightarrow+\infty}0
. But
I
is equivalent to
H^{1}
-norm, then
\|u_{n}\|_{H^{1}}\longrightarrow 0
too. Yet, the constraint
K
is controlled by the
H^{1}
-norm as
H^{1}(\mathbb{R})\hookrightarrow L^{3}(\mathbb{R})
. This leads to a contradiction with the fact that the constraint
K(u_{n})=\lambda>0
, and thus
M^{\lambda}>0
.

Now, by a dilation argument, one can check that
(u_{n})
is a minimizing sequence of
M^{\lambda}
if and only if
(v_{n})
, defined by
\displaystyle v_{n}=\bigg(\frac{\lambda_{0}}{\lambda}\bigg)^{\frac{1}{3}}u_{n}
, is a minimizing sequence of
M^{\lambda_{0}}
.

For the second statement in (
2.6
), let
\lambda_{0}>\lambda>0
. We recall that
f:x\mapsto x^{2/3}
is a strictly concave function, thus is also strictly subadditive, which principally means that
f(x+y)<f(x)+f(y)
for
x,\ y>0
.
This finishes the proof of (
2.6
).
∎

2.2
Existence of minimizers

Proposition 2.4
.

Let
\lambda>0
. For any
\alpha\in[0,1]
and
\beta>-2\sqrt{\alpha}
, there exists a solution to the constrained minimizing problem
M^{\lambda}_{1,\alpha,\beta}
.

More precisely, set
\theta=1
for
\alpha>0
and
\theta=1/2
for
\alpha=0
.
Given a minimizing sequence
(\psi_{n})
of
M^{\delta}_{1,\alpha,\beta}
, there exists a subsequence
(\psi_{n_{k}})
converging to
\psi
in
H^{\theta}_{e}(\mathbb{R})
that is a solution to the constrained minimization problem
M^{\delta}_{1,\alpha,\beta}
.

Finally,
Q_{KdV}
and
Q_{BO}
defined in (
2.1
) and (
2.3
) are respectively the unique solutions to
M_{1,1,0}^{K(Q_{KdV})}
and
M_{1,0,1}^{K(Q_{BO})}
.

Proof.

We shall treat first the case
\alpha\neq 0
. Let us solve the constraint minimization problem
M^{\delta}
. We start by taking
(\psi_{n})_{n\geq 1}
a minimizing sequence of the problem
M^{\delta}
, that is,

I(\psi_{n})\longrightarrow_{n\rightarrow+\infty}M^{\delta}\quad\text{and}\quad K(\psi_{n})=\delta>0.

(2.7)

We claim that there exists a subsequence
(\psi_{k})_{k\geq 1}
, and
\psi\in H^{1}_{e}
such that
\psi_{k}\longrightarrow\psi
in
H^{1}_{e}
and
\psi
is a minimizer of
M^{\delta}
. As
I
is equivalent to
H^{1}
-norm according to Lemma
2.2
, then by (
2.7
) too,
(\psi_{n})
is bounded in
H^{1}(\mathbb{R})
which is a Hilbert space. Thus, there exists a subsequence
(\psi_{k})
and a function
\psi
such that

\psi_{k}\rightharpoonup\psi\quad\text{in}\ H^{1}(\mathbb{R})

(2.8)

and

\|\psi\|^{2}_{H_{1}}\leq\liminf_{k\rightarrow+\infty}\|\psi_{k}\|^{2}_{H^{1}}.

(2.9)

Besides, thanks to the compact embedding
H^{1}\hookrightarrow L^{3}_{loc}(\mathbb{R})
, we get

\psi_{k}\longrightarrow\psi\quad\text{in}\ L^{3}_{loc}(\mathbb{R})

(2.10)

and

\psi_{k}\longrightarrow\psi\quad\text{a.e \ in}\ \mathbb{R}.

(2.11)

Now, define the sequence of positive and even functions

\rho_{n}=|\psi_{n}|^{2}+|\partial_{x}\psi_{n}|^{2};\quad\quad n\in\mathbb{N}^{*}.

As
(\rho_{n})
is bounded in
L^{1}(\mathbb{R})
, we can extract a subsequence
(\rho_{k})
assuming that

\lim_{k\rightarrow+\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\rho_{k}=L<+\infty.

By normalizing, we may also assume that

\int_{\mathbb{R}}\rho_{k}=L\quad\forall\ k\in\mathbb{N}^{*}.

Based on the above analysis, Lemma
2.2
and Lemma
2.3
, we are now eligible to employ the Concentration Compactness Lemma developed by Lions
[
24
]
,
[
25
]
, and we end up with three possibilities:

(P1)

Concentration: There exists
(y_{k})\subset\mathbb{R}
such that for all
\varepsilon>0,\ \exists\ R_{\varepsilon}>0
;
\forall\ k\in\mathbb{N}^{*}
we have

\int_{|x-y_{k}|\leq R_{\varepsilon}}\rho_{k}\ dx\ \geq\ \int_{\mathbb{R}}\rho_{k}\ dx\quad-\varepsilon,

(P2)

Vanishing: For every
R>0
, we have

\lim_{k\rightarrow+\infty}\ \sup_{y\in\mathbb{R}}\int_{|x-y|\leq R}\rho_{k}\ dx=0,

or

(P3)

Dichotomy: There exists
l\in(0,L)
such that for all
\varepsilon>0,\ \exists\ R>0
,
(R_{k})_{k}
with
R_{k}\rightarrow+\infty
,
(y_{k})_{k\geq 1}
, and
k_{0}
such that
\forall\ k\geq k_{0}
, we have

\bigg|\int_{|x-y_{k}|\leq R}\rho_{k}\ dx\quad-l\bigg|<\varepsilon^{2},\quad\text{and}\quad\bigg|\int_{R<|x-y_{k}|<R_{k}}\rho_{k}\ dx\bigg|<\varepsilon^{2}.

Our plan is to rule out possibilities (P2) and (P3) and so (P1) holds, thus proving that the limit of the minimizing sequence is itself a minimizer of
M^{\delta}
.

To begin with, suppose (P2) is true and take
R=1
. By Sobolev embedding, we have

\displaystyle\displaystyle\int_{|x-y|\leq 1}|\psi_{k}|^{3}\ dx

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\bigg(\int_{|x-y|\leq 1}|\psi_{k}|^{2}+|\partial_{x}\psi_{k}|^{2}\ dx\bigg)^{\frac{3}{2}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\bigg(\int_{|x-y|\leq 1}\rho_{k}\bigg)^{\frac{3}{2}}.

Yet, one can assert from assumption (P2) that for any
\varepsilon>0
there exists
k(\varepsilon)>0
such that for all
k\geq k(\varepsilon)
and all
y\in\mathbb{R}
, it holds

\int_{|x-y|\leq 1}\rho_{k}\ dx\leq\varepsilon.

So, for every
y\in\mathbb{R}
,

\int_{|x-y|\leq 1}|\psi_{k}|^{3}\ dx\lesssim\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{2}}\bigg(\int_{|x-y|\leq 1}\rho_{k}\ dx\bigg).

Re-summing over intervals centered at even integers, then taking
\varepsilon
small enough, we get a contradiction to the fact that
K(\psi_{k})=\lambda>0
for all
k\in\mathbb{N}^{*}
.

Now, suppose (P3) is true. We start by fixing
\varepsilon>0
. The idea is to decompose
\psi_{k}
into two parts in order to construct two minimizing sequences that are going infinitely away from each other. This will lead to a contradiction due to the sub-additivity condition following from (
2.6

Define two smooth cutoff functions

\displaystyle I_{1,1,0}(\psi_{1,1,\beta_{n}})=I_{1,1,\beta_{n}}(\psi_{1,1,\beta_{n}})-\frac{\beta_{n}}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}}(D_{x}^{\frac{1}{2}}(\psi_{1,1,\beta_{n}}))^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle M^{K(Q_{KdV})}_{1,1,\beta_{n}}-\frac{\beta_{n}}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}}(D_{x}^{\frac{1}{2}}(\psi_{1,1,\beta_{n}}))^{2}.

Taking
\beta_{n}\rightarrow 0
, we get

M^{K(Q_{KdV})}_{1,1,\beta_{n}}\longrightarrow M^{K(Q_{KdV})}_{1,1,0}\ .

(2.25)

Hence,
\psi_{1,1,\beta_{n}}
is a minimizing sequence of
M_{1,1,0}^{K(Q_{KdV})}
. By Proposition
2.4
, there exists a subsequence
(\psi_{1,1,\beta_{n_{k}}})
that converges in
H^{1}(\mathbb{R})
to a solution
\psi
of
M_{1,1,0}^{K(Q_{KdV})}
. Recalling that according to Proposition
2.4
,
Q_{KdV}
is the unique solution to

, we deduce that
(\psi_{1,1,\beta_{n_{k}}})
converges to
Q_{KdV}
in
H^{1}(\mathbb{R})
.
Finally, by the uniqueness of the possible limits in
H^{1}(\mathbb{R})
of its subsequences, we deduce that
\psi_{1,1,\beta_{n}}\rightarrow Q_{KdV}
in
H^{1}(\mathbb{R})
.
∎

2.3.2
Convergence Case 2:
\{\alpha\ll 1,\ \beta=1\}

Proposition 2.8
.

Let
\alpha_{n}\rightarrow 0
, and
(\psi_{1,\alpha_{n},1})
be a sequence of minimizers to the constraint problem
M^{K(Q_{BO})}_{1,\alpha_{n},1}
. Then,
(\psi_{1,\alpha_{n},1})_{n\geq 1}
converges to the BO soliton profile
Q_{BO}
in
H^{1}(\mathbb{R})
.

Proof.

Let
\beta=1
and
(\alpha_{n})_{n\geq 1}\subset\mathbb{R}^{\ast}
be any sequence such that
\displaystyle\lim_{n\rightarrow+\infty}\alpha_{n}=0
, and let
(\psi_{\alpha_{n}})
be an associated sequence of minimizers of
M^{K(Q_{BO})}_{1,\alpha_{n},1}
with
(\mu_{n})
the associated sequence of Lagrange multiplier in (
2.22
). Contrarily to the preceding case where
\alpha
was converging to zero, we are only left with boundedness of
(\psi_{1,\alpha_{n},1})
in
H^{1/2}_{e}(\mathbb{R})
since
I_{1,\alpha_{n},1}(\psi_{1,\alpha_{n},1})\geq\|\psi_{1,\alpha_{n},1}\|_{H^{1/2}}
. To get the boundedness in
H^{1}(\mathbb{R})
, we have to work a little more.
First, we notice that
\mu_{n}
is bounded since

0\leq\mu_{n}=\frac{2}{3}\ \frac{I_{1,\alpha_{n},1}(\psi_{1,\alpha_{n},1})}{K(Q_{BO})}=\frac{2}{3}\ \frac{M^{K(Q_{BO})}_{1,\alpha_{n},1}}{K(Q_{BO})}\ \leq\ \frac{2}{3}\ \frac{I_{1,\alpha_{n},1}(Q_{BO})}{K(Q_{BO})}\;.

(2.26)

Second, applying the Fourier Transform to (
2.22
), we get

\bigg[1+|\xi|+\alpha_{n}|\xi|^{2}\bigg]\widehat{\psi_{1,\alpha_{n},1}}(\xi)=\mu_{n}\,\widehat{\psi^{2}_{1,\alpha_{n},1}}\quad\text{for \ any}\ \xi\in\mathbb{R}.

(2.27)

By Plancherel equality and Sobolev inequalities, we have

\|\widehat{\psi^{2}_{1,\alpha_{n},1}}\|_{L^{2}}=\|\psi_{1,\alpha_{n},1}\|^{2}_{L^{4}}\lesssim\|\psi_{1,\alpha_{n},1}\|^{2}_{H^{\frac{1}{2}}}\ .

So, taking the
L^{2}
-norm of both sides of (
2.27
) we obtain that
(\psi_{1,\alpha_{n},1})
is actually bounded in
H^{1}(\mathbb{R})
. Note that, multiplying (

) by
\sqrt{1+\xi^{2}}
, taking again the

-norm of both sides of (

) and using that
H^{1}({I\!\!R})
is an algebra, we even obtain that
(\psi_{1,\alpha_{n},1})
is bounded in
H^{2}(\mathbb{R})
.

Now, we notice that

\displaystyle\displaystyle M^{K(Q_{BO)}}_{1,0,1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle I_{1,\alpha_{n},1}(Q_{BO})-\frac{\alpha_{n}}{2}\int(\partial_{x}Q_{BO})^{2}\geq M^{K(Q_{BO})}_{1,\alpha_{n},1}-\frac{\alpha_{n}}{2}\int(\partial_{x}Q_{BO})^{2}

while, on the other hand,

\displaystyle\displaystyle M^{K(Q_{BO})}_{1,0,1}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle I_{1,0,1}(\psi_{1,\alpha_{n},1})=I_{1,\alpha_{n},1}(\psi_{1,\alpha_{n},1})-\frac{\alpha_{n}}{2}\int(\partial_{x}\psi_{1,\alpha_{n},1})^{2}

(2.28)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle M^{K(Q_{BO})}_{1,\alpha_{n},1}-\frac{\alpha_{n}}{2}\int(\partial_{x}\psi_{1,\alpha_{n},1})^{2}.

Thanks to the boundedness of
(\psi_{1,\alpha_{n},1})
in

, we may pass to the limit on the above two inequalities and obtain the convergence

M^{K(Q_{BO})}_{1,\alpha_{n},1}\longrightarrow M^{K(Q_{BO})}_{1,0,1}\quad\text{as}\ \alpha_{n}\rightarrow 0\ .

(2.29)

Hence,
(\psi_{1,\alpha_{n},1})
is a minimizing sequence for
M^{K(Q_{BO})}_{1,0,1}
. Proposition
2.4
with
\alpha=0
then ensures that there exists a subsequence
(\psi_{1,\alpha_{n_{k}},1})
which converges in
H^{1/2}_{e}(\mathbb{R})
to a minimizer
\psi
of
M^{K(Q_{BO})}_{1,0,1}
and the uniqueness of the solution to
M^{K(Q_{BO})}_{1,0,1}
ensures, as in the proof of Proposition
2.7
, that
(\psi_{1,\alpha_{n},1})
converges to
Q_{BO}
in
H^{1/2}(\mathbb{R})
. Finally, the boundedness of
(\psi_{1,\alpha_{n},1})
in

forces this convergence to hold also in

.
∎

3
Uniqueness and Orbital Stability of the ground state solutions

In this section, we will first prove the uniqueness of the even ground state solutions of (
1.11
) and then their orbital stability over the two cases of study.

Proposition 3.1
.

There exists
\tilde{\beta}_{0}>0
(respectively
\tilde{\alpha}_{0}>0
) such that
\forall\ |\beta|<\tilde{\beta}_{0}
(respectively
\forall\ 0\leq\alpha<\tilde{\alpha}_{0}
) there exists a unique even ground state solution
Q_{1,1,\beta}
to (
1.11
) with
\alpha=1
(respectively
Q_{1,\alpha,1}
to (
1.11
) with
\beta=1
). Besides, the maps

]-\tilde{\beta}_{0},\tilde{\beta}_{0}[\ni\beta\longmapsto Q_{1,1,\beta}\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R})

and

[0,\tilde{\alpha}_{0}[\ni\alpha\longmapsto Q_{1,\alpha,1}\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R})

are continuous.

Proof.

Case 1:

\{\alpha=1,\ |\beta|\ll 1\}
:

We start by proving that
\forall\ \varepsilon>0,\ \exists\ \beta_{\varepsilon}>0
such that any
\varphi_{1,1,\beta}
even ground state solution to (
1.11
) with
\alpha=1
satisfies

\|\varphi_{1,1,\beta}-Q_{KdV}\|_{H^{1}}<\frac{\varepsilon}{2}\quad\quad\forall\ 0<|\beta|<\beta_{\varepsilon}\ .

(3.1)

This is clearly equivalent to proving that for any sequence
(\beta_{n})_{n\geq 1}\subset]0,1]
that tends to zero as
n\rightarrow+\infty
, and any sequence of associated even ground state solutions
(\varphi_{\beta_{n}})_{n\geq 1}
to (
1.11
) with
\alpha=1
, it holds

\varphi_{\beta_{n}}\to Q_{KdV}\quad\text{in}\quad H^{1}(\mathbb{R})\;.

So, let be given a sequence
(\beta_{n})_{n\geq 1}\subset\mathbb{R}^{\ast}_{+}
that tends to zero as
n\rightarrow+\infty
, and a sequence of even ground state solutions
(\varphi_{\beta_{n}})_{n\geq 1}
to (
1.11
) with
(\alpha,\beta)=(1,\beta_{n})
.
Defining
\Phi_{\beta_{n}}
in the following way

\Phi_{\beta_{n}}=\bigg(\frac{K(Q_{KdV})}{K(\varphi_{\beta_{n}})}\bigg)^{\frac{1}{3}}\varphi_{\beta_{n}}\ ,

(3.2)

we get that
K(\Phi_{\beta_{n}})=K(Q_{KdV})>0
and thus
\Phi_{\beta_{n}}
is a solution to
M^{K(Q_{KdV})}_{1,1,\beta_{n}}
. According to Proposition
2.7
, it follows that
\Phi_{\beta_{n}}\rightarrow Q_{KdV}
in
H^{1}(\mathbb{R})
as
\beta_{n}
tends to zero.
Now, as (
3.2
) leads to

I_{1,1,\beta_{n}}(\varphi_{\beta_{n}})=\bigg(\frac{K(\varphi_{\beta_{n}})}{K(Q_{KdV})}\bigg)^{\frac{2}{3}}I_{1,1,\beta_{n}}(\Phi_{\beta_{n}}),

(
1.12
) on the other hand shows that

I_{1,1,\beta_{n}}(\varphi_{\beta_{n}})=\frac{3}{2}\ K(\varphi_{\beta_{n}}).

Combining the last two identities, we get the following

\displaystyle\displaystyle\frac{3}{2}\frac{K({\varphi_{\beta_{n}}})}{K(Q_{KdV})}=\bigg(\frac{K(\varphi_{\beta_{n}})}{K(Q_{KdV})}\bigg)^{\frac{2}{3}}\ \frac{I_{1,1,\beta_{n}}(\Phi_{\beta_{n}})}{K(Q_{KdV})},

or equivalently,

\bigg(\frac{K({\varphi_{\beta_{n}}})}{K(Q_{KdV})}\bigg)^{\frac{1}{3}}=\frac{2}{3}\ \frac{I_{1,1,\beta_{n}}(\Phi_{\beta_{n}})}{K(Q_{KdV})}\ .

Substituting the above identity in what follows, we obtain

\displaystyle\varphi_{\beta_{n}}=\bigg(\frac{K(\varphi_{\beta_{n}})}{K(Q_{KdV})}\bigg)^{\frac{1}{3}}\Phi_{\beta_{n}}=\frac{2}{3}\ \frac{I_{1,1,\beta_{n}}(\Phi_{\beta_{n}})}{K(Q_{KdV})}\ \Phi_{\beta_{n}}\ .

(3.3)

Yet,
\Phi_{\beta_{n}}
converges to
Q_{KdV}
in
H^{1}(\mathbb{R})
and so
I_{1,1,\beta_{n}}(\Phi_{\beta_{n}})\longrightarrow I_{1,1,0}(Q_{KdV})
, and
Q_{KdV}
satisfies (
1.12
), thus we reach

\varphi_{\beta_{n}}\longrightarrow Q_{KdV}\quad\text{in}\ H^{1}

which proves (
3.1
). Now, we follow the argument of [
[
19
]
, Proposition 3] to prove the uniqueness for
|\beta|
small enough. Let
\varphi^{1}_{\beta}
and
\varphi^{2}_{\beta}
be two ground state solutions to (
1.11
) with
\alpha=1
and set
\tilde{w}=\varphi^{1}_{\beta}-\varphi^{2}_{\beta}
. We claim that
\tilde{w}=0
as soon as
|\beta|
is small enough. Thanks to (
3.1
), we can see that

\|\tilde{w}\|_{H^{1}}\leq\|\varphi^{1}_{\beta}-Q_{KdV}\|_{H^{1}}+\|Q_{KdV}-\varphi^{2}_{\beta}\|_{H^{1}}\leq\varepsilon(\beta),

with
\varepsilon(y)\to 0
as
y\to 0
.
Moreover,
\tilde{w}
satisfies

\tilde{w}-\beta\ \mathcal{H}\partial_{x}\tilde{w}-\ \partial^{2}_{x}\tilde{w}=\frac{1}{2}(\varphi^{1}_{\beta})^{2}-\frac{1}{2}(\varphi^{2}_{\beta})^{2}.

(3.4)

We also define the operator
\Upsilon_{1,1,\beta}
as

\Upsilon_{1,1,\beta}=1-\beta\ \mathcal{H}\partial_{x}-\ \partial_{x}^{2}-\varphi^{1}_{\beta}.

It was noticed in
[
19
]
that the following crucial cancellation holds

\Upsilon_{1,1,\beta}\tilde{w}=\frac{1}{2}(\varphi^{1}_{\beta})^{2}-\frac{1}{2}(\varphi^{2}_{\beta})^{2}-\varphi^{1}_{\beta}\tilde{w}=\frac{1}{2}\tilde{w}^{2}\;.

(3.5)

Let us now assume that
\tilde{w}\neq 0
and prove that it leads to a contradiction. Setting
w=\displaystyle\frac{\tilde{w}}{\|\tilde{w}\|_{H^{1}}}
so that
\|w\|_{H^{1}}=1
, it follows from (
3.5
) that

\|\Upsilon_{1,1,\beta}w\|_{L^{2}}\leq\frac{1}{\|\tilde{w}\|_{H^{1}}}\ \|\tilde{w}\|^{2}_{L^{4}}\leq\frac{1}{\|\tilde{w}\|_{H^{1}}}\ \|\tilde{w}\|^{2}_{H^{1}}=\|w\|_{H^{1}}\ \|\tilde{w}\|_{H^{1}}\leq\varepsilon(\beta).

(3.6)

Hence by (
3.6
) and (
3.1
), we get

\displaystyle\displaystyle|\langle\Upsilon_{1,1,0}w,w\rangle_{L^{2}}|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\bigg|\langle w-\partial_{x}^{2}w-Q_{KdV}\ w,w\rangle_{L^{2}}\bigg|

(3.7)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\bigg|\langle\Upsilon_{1,1,\beta}w,w\rangle_{L^{2}}+\langle\varphi^{1}_{\beta}\ w-Q_{KdV}\ w,w\rangle_{L^{2}}+\beta\langle\mathcal{H}\partial_{x}w,w\rangle_{L^{2}}\bigg|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\bigg|\langle\Upsilon_{1,1,\beta}w,w\rangle_{L^{2}}+\langle\varphi^{1}_{\beta}-Q_{KdV},w^{2}\rangle_{L^{2}}\bigg|+|\beta|\ \|D_{x}^{\frac{1}{2}}w\|^{2}_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 2\;\varepsilon(\beta)+|\beta|.

Now, according to
[
13

, for any
u\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R})
satisfying the orthogonality conditions
\langle u,Q_{KdV}\rangle_{L^{2}}=\langle u,Q^{\prime}_{KdV}\rangle_{L^{2}}=0

\gamma_{0}>0

\langle\Upsilon_{1,1,0}u,u\rangle\geq\gamma_{0}\|u\|^{2}_{H^{1}}.

(3.8)

Moreover, the coercivity of the operator

[
15

replaced by
\gamma_{0}/2

Before all else, the first orthogonality condition

\langle w,Q^{\prime}_{KdV}\rangle=0

(3.9)

is straightforward as

is even and

is odd. The first almost orthogonality condition is thus a direct result.
For the second condition, we recall that differentiating (

\Upsilon_{1,1,0}\ \partial_{c|{{}_{c=1}}}Q_{c,KdV}=Q_{1,KdV}=Q_{KdV}\;.

According to (

\partial_{c|{{}_{c=1}}}Q_{c,KdV}=-\frac{3}{2}\tanh(x/2)\,{\rm sech}^{2}(x/2)=:{\mathcal{V}}\in H^{\infty}({I\!\!R})\;.

Therefore, by (
3.6
) and Proposition
2.7
, we obtain

\displaystyle\displaystyle|\langle w,Q_{KdV|_{c=1}}\rangle_{L^{2}}|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\big|\langle w,\Upsilon_{1,1,0}\,{\mathcal{V}}\rangle_{L^{2}}\big|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\big|\langle\Upsilon_{1,1,0}w,{\mathcal{V}}\rangle_{L^{2}}\big|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\big|\langle\Upsilon_{1,1,\beta}w,{\mathcal{V}}\rangle_{L^{2}}\big|+\big|\big\langle w{\mathcal{V}}\ ,\varphi^{1}_{\beta}-Q_{KdV}\big\rangle_{L^{2}}\big|+\beta\ \big|\langle\mathcal{H}\partial_{x}w,{\mathcal{V}}\rangle_{L^{2}}\big|

\displaystyle\big(\varepsilon(\beta)+\beta\big)\|w\|_{H^{1}}\ .

Thus, for
|\beta|
small enough,

is almost orthogonal to

, and so we deduce that

which contradicts (

). This asserts that

and proves the uniqueness of the ground state to (

\tilde{\beta}\in]-\tilde{\beta}_{0},\tilde{\beta}_{0}[

\displaystyle Q_{1,1,\beta_{n}}=\bigg(\frac{K(Q_{1,1,\beta_{n}})}{K(Q_{1,1,\tilde{\beta}})}\bigg)^{\frac{1}{3}}\Phi_{\beta_{n}}=\frac{2}{3}\ \frac{I_{1,1,\beta_{n}}(\Phi_{\beta_{n}})}{K(Q_{1,1,\tilde{\beta}})}\ \Phi_{\beta_{n}}\

Q_{BO}
, and so we conclude that

\langle\Gamma_{1,0,1}w,w\rangle_{L^{2}}\geq\frac{\eta_{0}}{2}\|w\|^{2}_{H^{\frac{1}{2}}}=\frac{\eta_{0}}{2},

which contradicts (
3.15
). This hence asserts that
\tilde{w}=0
and uniqueness is proved. In the sequel, we let
Q_{1,\alpha,1}
denote this unique even ground state.

Finally, the continuity of
\alpha\mapsto Q_{1,\alpha,1}
follows exactly the same lines as in
Case 1
and will thus be disregarded.
∎

3.0.1
Orbital stability

To prove the orbital stability of an even ground state solution in
H^{1}(\mathbb{R})
, we recall the following proposition; check
[
14
]
.

Proposition 3.2
.

Set the operator
\Upsilon_{c,\alpha,\beta}
associated to the second derivative of the action functional
E+cV
and linearized at
\varphi_{c,\alpha,\beta}\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R})
solution to (
1.11
); where
\Upsilon_{c,\alpha,\beta}
is defined by

\Upsilon_{c,\alpha,\beta}:=c-\alpha\partial_{x}^{2}-\beta\mathcal{H}\partial_{x}-\varphi_{c,\alpha,\beta}.

Assume that there exists
\gamma>0
such that

\langle\Upsilon_{c,\alpha,\beta}u,u\rangle_{L^{2}}\geq\gamma\|u\|^{2}_{H^{1}}

for any
u\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R})
satisfying the orthogonality conditions

\langle u,\varphi_{c,\alpha,\beta}\rangle_{L^{2}}=\langle u,\varphi^{\prime}_{c,\alpha,\beta}\rangle=0

then
\varphi_{c,\alpha,\beta}
is orbitally stable in
H^{1}(\mathbb{R})
. More precisely, there exists
K>0
and
\varepsilon_{0}>0
such that if
u_{0}\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R})
satisfies

\|u_{0}-\varphi_{c,\alpha,\beta}\|_{H^{1}}<\varepsilon

for some
0<\varepsilon<\varepsilon_{0}
then

\sup_{t\in{I\!\!R}}\inf_{y\in{I\!\!R}}\|u(t)-\varphi_{c,\alpha,\beta}(\cdot-y)\|_{H^{1}}<K\varepsilon\;.

We are now in position to prove the orbital stability of the even ground state solutions
\varphi_{1,1,\beta}
of (
1.11
) with
\alpha=1
, then that of
\varphi_{1,\alpha,1}
for
\beta=1
in (
1.11
).

Case 1:
Orbital stability of
\varphi_{1,1,\beta}

To begin with, we note that thanks to Proposition
2.7
, any
u\in H^{1}
satisfying
\langle u,Q_{1,1,\beta}\rangle_{L^{2}}=\langle u,Q^{\prime}_{1,1,\beta}\rangle_{L^{2}}=0
is almost orthogonal in
L^{2}
to
Q_{KdV}
and
Q^{\prime}_{KdV}
for
|\beta|
small enough and thus (
3.8
) holds with
\gamma_{0}
replaced by
\gamma_{0}/2
. Hence,

\displaystyle\displaystyle\langle\Upsilon_{1,1,\beta}u,u\rangle_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\langle\Upsilon_{1,1,0}u,u\rangle_{L^{2}}-\langle Q_{1,1,\beta}-Q_{KdV},u^{2}\rangle_{L^{2}}-\beta\langle\mathcal{H}\partial_{x}u,u\rangle_{L^{2}}

(3.18)

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\frac{\gamma_{0}}{2}\|u\|^{2}_{H^{1}}-\varepsilon(\beta)\|u\|^{2}_{H^{1}}-|\beta|\ \|D_{x}^{\frac{1}{2}}u\|^{2}_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\bigg(\frac{\gamma_{0}}{2}-\varepsilon(\beta)-\varepsilon(|\beta|)\bigg)\|u\|^{2}_{H^{1}}

where
\varepsilon(\beta),\ \varepsilon(|\beta|)\longrightarrow 0
as
\beta\rightarrow 0
. Eventually, thanks to Proposition
3.2
, we deduce that there exists
r_{0}>0
such that
\varphi_{1,1,\beta}
is orbitally stable in
H^{1}(\mathbb{R})
for any
|\beta|<r_{0}
.

Lastly, since
u
is a solution of (
1.6
) with
\alpha=1
if and only if
v(t,x)=\lambda^{2}\ u(\lambda^{3}\ t,\lambda\ x)
is a solution to (
1.1
) with
\gamma=|\lambda|\beta
, there is an equivalence between the uniqueness and orbital stability of
Q_{1,1,\beta}
and the ones of
Q_{\lambda^{2},1,|\lambda|\beta}
. This leads to the uniqueness and orbital stability of
Q_{c,1,\gamma}
with
\gamma\in\mathbb{R}^{*}
as soon as

c>r_{0}^{-2}\gamma^{2}
.

Case 2:
Orbital stability of
\varphi_{1,\alpha,1}

Again, we first note that thanks to Proposition
2.8
, any
u\in H^{\frac{1}{2}}(\mathbb{R})
satisfying
\langle u,Q_{1,\alpha,1}\rangle=\langle u,Q^{\prime}_{1,\alpha,1}\rangle=0
is almost orthogonal in
L^{2}
to
Q_{BO}
and
Q^{\prime}_{BO}
for
\alpha>0
small enough and thus (
3.16
) holds with
\eta_{0}
replaced by
\eta_{0}/2
.
Hence

\displaystyle\displaystyle\langle\Upsilon_{1,\alpha,1}u,u\rangle_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\langle\Upsilon_{1,0,1}u,u\rangle_{L^{2}}-\langle Q_{1,\alpha,1}-Q_{BO},u^{2}\rangle_{L^{2}}+\alpha\|\partial_{x}u\|^{2}_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\frac{\eta_{0}}{2}\|u\|^{2}_{H^{\frac{1}{2}}}-\varepsilon(\alpha)\|u\|^{2}_{L^{2}}+\alpha\|\partial_{x}u\|^{2}_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\min\bigg\{\frac{\eta_{0}}{2}-\varepsilon(\alpha),\alpha\bigg\}\|u\|^{2}_{H^{1}}

where
\varepsilon(\alpha)\longrightarrow 0
as
\alpha\rightarrow 0
. Eventually, thanks to Proposition
3.2
, we deduce that there exists
\tilde{r}_{0}>0
such that
Q_{1,\alpha,1}
is orbitally stable in
H^{1}(\mathbb{R})
for any
\alpha\in]0,\tilde{r}_{0}[
.

Now, we return to the uniqueness and orbital stability of solution to (
1.1
). Let us fix
\gamma>0
. Since
u
is a solution to (
1.6
) with
\beta=1
if and only if
v(t,x)=\gamma^{2}\alpha u(\gamma^{3}\alpha^{2}t,\gamma\alpha x)
satisfies (
1.1
), then the uniqueness and orbital stability of
Q_{1,\alpha,1}
is equivalent to the uniqueness and orbital stability of
Q_{\gamma^{2}\alpha,1,\gamma}
. This leads to the uniqueness and orbital stability of
Q_{c,1,\gamma}
with
\gamma>0
as soon as

0<c<\tilde{r}_{0}\gamma^{2}
, and eventually completes the proof of Theorem
1.3
.

4
Decay of the ground state solutions

In this last section, we prove some decay estimates for any even
H^{1}
-solution to (
1.5
) as well as its two first derivatives. We follow the arguments in
[
12
]
. Note that such decay estimates were already proved in
[
11
]
, except for the derivatives. It is also worth noting that these decay estimates are an important requirement to derive sharp properties of the flow of the equation in the neighborhood of the solitary waves.

Proposition 4.1
.

Let
\gamma\in{I\!\!R}
. Given that
c>0
in the case
\gamma>0
and
c>\gamma^{2}/4
in the case
\gamma<0
, the even solutions to (
1.5
) satisfy

|x^{2}\,\varphi_{c,1,\gamma}|+|x^{3}\,\varphi_{c,1,\gamma}^{\prime}(x)|+|x^{3}\,\varphi_{c,1,\gamma}^{\prime\prime}|\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})\;.

Proof.

We consider only the case
c=1
and
\gamma>-2
. Indeed, as already mentioned in the beginning of Section
2
,
\varphi
is a solution to (
1.5
) with
c=1
and
\gamma=\beta
if and only if
\phi(\cdot)=\lambda^{2}\varphi(\lambda\cdot)
is a solution to (
1.5
) with
c=\lambda^{2}
and
\gamma=|\lambda|\beta
. Therefore, the decay estimate for
c=1
and
\gamma>-2
ensures the same decay estimate for any speed
c>0
in the case
\gamma>0
and for any speed
c>\gamma^{2}/4
in the case
\gamma<0
.
Now, let us denote by
\varphi_{\gamma}
an even
H^{1}
-solution to (
1.5
) with
c=1
and
\gamma>-2
. We know that
\varphi_{\gamma}\in H^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})
and according to (
1.5
) it holds

\widehat{\varphi_{\gamma}}(\xi)=\frac{1}{2(1+\xi^{2}+\gamma|\xi|)}\ \widehat{\varphi_{\gamma}^{2}}(\xi).

(4.1)

Therefore, for
k=0,1
,
\varphi_{\gamma}^{(k)}(x)=G^{(k)}\ast\varphi_{\gamma}^{2}(x)
with
G^{(k)}
given by

G^{(k)}(x)=\frac{1}{2\sqrt{2\pi}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\frac{(i\xi)^{k}\ e^{ix\xi}}{1+\alpha\xi^{2}+\gamma|\xi|}d\xi\ .

Since
\displaystyle\frac{1}{2(1+\xi^{2}+\gamma|\xi|)}
belongs to
H^{1}({I\!\!R})
, the hypotheses of (cf.
[
12
]
, Theorem 3.1.2, Page 384) are clearly fulfilled. Thus according to this theorem, there exists
0<l\leq 1
such that
|x|^{l}\varphi_{\gamma}\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})
. Since
\varphi_{\gamma}
belongs to
L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
, it follows that
\varphi_{\gamma}^{2}
belongs to
L^{1}(\mathbb{R})
and decays at least as
|x|^{-2l}
. We are going to prove that
G
decays at least as
|x|^{-2}
and thus also belongs to
L^{1}(\mathbb{R})
. Assuming this decay for a while, the identity
\varphi_{\gamma}=G\ast\varphi_{\gamma}^{2}
ensures that
\varphi_{\gamma}
decays at least as
|x|^{-2l}
; reiterating this argument for a finite number of times, we obtain that
\varphi_{\gamma}
decays at least as
|x|^{-2}
.
Therefore, it remains to prove the decay of
G
. We start by noticing that since
\displaystyle\frac{1}{1+\alpha\xi^{2}+\beta|\xi|}
is a real and even function, then so is its inverse Fourier. Hence,

\displaystyle G(x)=\frac{1}{2\sqrt{2\pi}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\frac{\cos(x\xi)}{1+\alpha\xi^{2}+\gamma|\xi|}\ d\xi=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{+}}\frac{\cos(x\xi)}{1+\alpha\xi^{2}+\gamma\xi}\ d\xi.

Fix
x
, and set
g(z)=g(x,z)=\displaystyle\frac{e^{i|x|z}}{1+z^{2}+\gamma z}
. We notice that the roots of
p(z)=1+z^{2}+\gamma z
are given when
\gamma\geq 2
by
\frac{-\gamma\mp\sqrt{\gamma^{2}-4}}{2}
and when
|\gamma|<2
by
\frac{-\gamma\mp i\sqrt{4-\gamma^{2}}}{2}
. Therefore,
g
is analytic on the open convex set

\Lambda_{\theta}:=\{z\in{\mathbb{C}}\,/\,\Re(z)>\theta_{1}\text{ and }|\Im z|<\theta_{2}\}

with
(\theta_{1},\theta_{2})=(\frac{-\gamma+\sqrt{\gamma^{2}-4}}{2},1)
when
\gamma\geq 2
,
(\theta_{1},\theta_{2})=(-\gamma/2,1)
when
0<\gamma<2
and
(\theta_{1},\theta_{2})=(-1,\frac{\sqrt{4-\gamma^{2}}}{2})
when
-2<\gamma\leq 0
.
We can thus use Cauchy’s Integral Theorem along the closed path
\delta_{R}:=\delta_{1,R}+\delta_{2,R}+\delta_{3,R}+\delta_{4,R}
, where
\delta_{1,R}=[0,R]
,
\delta_{2,R}=\{R+i\ t;\ t\in[0,\frac{\theta}{2}]
},
\delta_{3,R}=\{t+i\frac{\theta}{2};\ t\in[R,0]\}
, and
\delta_{4,R}=\{i\ t;\ t\in[\frac{\theta}{2},0]\}
, to get

\displaystyle\displaystyle\oint_{\delta_{R}}g(z)\ dz

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\delta_{1,R}}g(z)\ dz+\int_{\delta_{2,R}}g(z)\ dz+\int_{\delta_{3,R}}g(z)\ dz+\int_{\delta_{4,R}}g(z)\ dz

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle I_{1,R}+I_{2,R}+I_{3,R}+I_{4,R}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 0.

Since
\displaystyle\frac{\cos(x\xi)}{1+\alpha\xi^{2}+\gamma\xi}
is integrable on
\mathbb{R}^{*}_{+}
, we have

\sqrt{2\pi}\,G(x)=\lim_{R\rightarrow+\infty}\Re(I_{1,R})=\lim_{R\rightarrow+\infty}\Re\Bigl(-I_{2,R}-I_{3,R}-I_{4,R}\Bigr).

We recall that for a piecewise
C^{1}
-path

\gamma:[t_{1},t_{2}]\ni t\mapsto\gamma(t)\in\mathbb{C},

we have

\int_{\gamma}g(z)\ dz=\int_{t_{1}}^{t_{2}}g(\gamma(t))\ \gamma^{\prime}(t)\ dt.

Starting with
I_{2}
,

\displaystyle\displaystyle I_{2,R}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle i\int_{0}^{\frac{\theta}{2}}\frac{e^{i|x|(R+it)}}{1+(R+it)^{2}+\gamma(R+it)}\ dt=ie^{-|x|t}\int_{0}^{\frac{\theta}{2}}\frac{e^{i|x|R}}{1+(R+it)^{2}+\gamma(R+it)}\ dt.

Using the fact that
|e^{i|x|R}|\leq 1
, integrating over
[0,\frac{\theta}{2}]
, then taking the limit
R\rightarrow+\infty
, we end up with

\lim_{R\rightarrow+\infty}|I_{2,R}|=0.

(4.2)

As for
I_{3}
,

I_{3,R}=\int_{R}^{0}\frac{e^{i|x|(t+i\frac{\theta}{2})}}{1+(t+i\frac{\theta}{2})^{2}+\gamma(t+i\frac{\theta}{2})}\ dt=-e^{-\theta|x|/2}\int_{0}^{R}\frac{e^{i|x|t}}{1+(t+i\frac{\theta}{2})^{2}+\gamma(t+i\frac{\theta}{2})}\ dt,

so there exists
C=C(\gamma)>0
independent of
R
such that

|I_{3,R}|\leq C\ e^{-\frac{\theta|x|}{2}}.

(4.3)

Lastly for
I_{4,R}
, we get

\displaystyle\displaystyle I_{4,R}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle i\int_{\frac{\theta}{2}}^{0}\frac{e^{i|x|(it)}}{1+(it)^{2}+\gamma it}\ dt=-i\int_{0}^{\frac{\theta}{2}}\frac{e^{-|x|t}}{(1-t^{2})+\gamma it}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-i\int_{0}^{\frac{\theta}{2}}\frac{(1-t^{2})-i\gamma t}{(1-t^{2})^{2}+\gamma^{2}t^{2}}e^{-|x|t}\,dt

and so

\Re(I_{4,R})=-\int_{0}^{\frac{\theta}{2}}\frac{\gamma t\ e^{-|x|t}}{(1-t^{2})^{2}+\gamma^{2}t^{2}}\ dt.

Performing the change of variable
y=|x|t
, we get

\Re(I_{4,R})=-\frac{\gamma}{|x|^{2}}\int_{0}^{\frac{\theta|x|}{2}}\frac{ye^{-y}}{(1-\frac{y^{2}}{|x|^{2}})^{2}+\gamma^{2}\frac{y^{2}}{|x|^{2}}}\ dy.

But, since
\displaystyle 0\leq y\leq\frac{\theta|x|}{2}
then
\displaystyle\frac{1}{(1-\frac{y^{2}}{|x|^{2}})^{2}+\gamma^{2}\frac{y^{2}}{|x|^{2}}}\leq\frac{1}{(1-\frac{y^{2}}{|x|^{2}})^{2}}\leq\frac{1}{1-\frac{\theta^{2}}{4}}\leq\frac{4}{3}
. Besides, the fact that
\displaystyle\int ye^{-y}\ dy=e^{-y}(-y-1)
ensures that

|\Re(I_{4,R})|\leq C\,\frac{\gamma}{|x|^{2}}\;.

(4.4)

Finally, we get by (
4.2
), (
4.3
), and (
4.4
) that

|G(x)|\lesssim\frac{|\gamma|}{|x|^{2}}+C(\gamma)e^{-\frac{\theta|x|}{2}}

(4.5)

and so the decay of the even ground state
\varphi_{\gamma}
is given at least by
C_{\gamma}|x|^{-2}
, and that of
Q^{\prime}_{\gamma}
is given by
C_{\gamma}|x|^{-3}
.

In fact, to obtain the decay on
\varphi_{\gamma}^{\prime}
, we are going to prove that
G^{\prime}
decays at least as
|x|^{-3}
. For this, we proceed exactly in the same way. We first notice that

\displaystyle G^{\prime}(x)=\frac{-1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\frac{\xi\sin(x\xi)}{1+\alpha\xi^{2}+\gamma|\xi|}\ d\xi

and for
x
fixed, we set
\tilde{g}(z)=\tilde{g}(x,z)=\displaystyle\frac{-ze^{i|x|z}}{1+z^{2}+\gamma z}
and we integrate the latter along
\delta_{R}
. Of course,
\displaystyle\frac{\xi\sin(x\xi)}{1+\alpha\xi^{2}+\gamma|\xi|}
does not belong to
L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{*}_{+})
. As the latter is no longer integrable on
\mathbb{R}
, we resort to oscillatory integrals where thanks to an integration by parts, it is direct to check that its generalized integral converges on
\mathbb{R}_{+}
. Therefore as above, it holds

\sqrt{2\pi}\,G^{\prime}(x)=\lim_{R\rightarrow+\infty}\Im\Bigl(\int_{\delta_{1,R}}\tilde{g}(z)\ dz\Bigr)=\lim_{R\rightarrow+\infty}\Im\Bigl(-\int_{\delta_{2,R}}\tilde{g}(z)\ dz-\int_{\delta_{3,R}}\tilde{g}(z)\ dz-\int_{\delta_{4,R}}\tilde{g}(z)\ dz\Bigr)\;.

We set
\displaystyle\tilde{I}_{k,R}=\int_{\delta_{k,R}}\tilde{g}(z)\ dz
for
k=2,3,4
. We notice that
\tilde{I}_{2,R}
can be treated exactly as
I_{2,R}
to obtain
\displaystyle\lim_{R\to+\infty}\tilde{I}_{2,R}=0
and that

\tilde{I}_{3,R}=-e^{-\theta|x|/2}\int_{0}^{R}\frac{(t+i\frac{\theta}{2})e^{i|x|t}}{1+(t+i\frac{\theta}{2})^{2}+\gamma(t+i\frac{\theta}{2})}\ dt.

Integrating by parts, we get that there exists
C=C(\gamma)>0
independent of
R
such that for
|x|\geq 1
,

|\tilde{I}_{3,R}|\leq C\,e^{-\frac{\theta|x|}{2}}.

(4.6)

As for
\tilde{I}_{4,R}
, we write

\displaystyle\tilde{I}_{4,R}=i\int_{0}^{\frac{\theta}{2}}\frac{(it)e^{-|x|t}}{(1-t^{2})+\gamma it}\\
\displaystyle=-\int_{0}^{\frac{\theta}{2}}\frac{t((1-t^{2})-i\gamma t)}{(1-t^{2})^{2}+\gamma^{2}t^{2}}e^{-|x|t}\,dt

so that

\Im(\tilde{I}_{4,R})=\int_{0}^{\frac{\theta}{2}}\frac{\gamma t^{2}\ e^{-|x|t}}{(1-t^{2})^{2}+\gamma^{2}t^{2}}\ dt.

Performing the change of variable
y=|x|t
, we get that

|\Im(\tilde{I}_{4,R})|\lesssim\gamma|x|^{-3}\ .

Gathering the above estimates, we obtain the desired decay for
G^{\prime}
, and the identity
\varphi_{\gamma}^{\prime}=G^{\prime}\ast\varphi_{\gamma}^{2}
together with the decay of
\varphi_{\gamma}
ensures that
\varphi_{\gamma}^{\prime}
decays at least as
|x|^{-3}
. Consequently, the decay of
\varphi_{\gamma}^{\prime\prime}
follows by noticing that

\varphi_{\gamma}^{\prime\prime}=2G^{\prime}\ast(\varphi_{\gamma}\varphi_{\gamma}^{\prime})
.
∎

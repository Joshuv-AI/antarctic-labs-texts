Poisson gauge theory
Vladislav G. Kupriyanov

Abstract
The Poisson gauge algebra is a semi-classical limit of complete non-commutative gauge algebra. In the present work we formulate the Poisson gauge theory which is a dynamical field theoretical model having the Poisson gauge algebra as a corresponding algebra of gauge symmetries. The proposed model is designed to investigate the semi-classical features of the full non-commutative gauge theory with coordinate dependent non-commutativity Θ a ​ b ​ ( x ) \Theta^{ab}(x) , especially whose with a non-constant rank. We derive the expression for the covariant derivative of matter field. The commutator relation for the covariant derivatives defines the Poisson field strength which is covariant under the Poisson gauge transformations and reproduces the standard U ⁡ ( 1 ) U(1) field strength in the commutative limit. We derive the corresponding Bianchi identities. The field equations for the gauge and the matter fields are obtained from the gauge invariant action. We consider different examples of linear in coordinates Poisson structures Θ a ​ b ​ ( x ) \Theta^{ab}(x) , as well as non-linear ones, and obtain explicit expressions for all proposed constructions. Our model is unique up to invertible field redefinitions and coordinate transformations.

(3.35)

Indeed, the expression
\{f(x),p_{a}\}_{p_{a}-A_{a}(x)=0}
means that first we calculate the Poisson bracket
\{f(x),p_{a}\}
according to (
1.5
) and then substitute all
p
with
A
, so,

\{f(x),p_{a}\}_{\Phi=0}=\gamma^{l}_{a}(A)\,\partial_{l}f(x)\,.

The main property of the transformation (
3.34
) if formulated by the following proposition.

Proposition 3.36
.

The Poisson gauge transformations (
3.34
) close the algebra (
1.4
).

Proof.

We start with some preliminary definitions,

\delta_{f}F(A):=F\left(A+\delta_{f}A\right)-F(A)\,.

(3.37)

In particular,

\delta_{g}\left(\{A_{a},f\}\right)=\{\delta_{g}A_{a},f\}\,,

(3.38)

and if
F(A)
is a smooth function in
A
, then,

\delta_{f}F(A)=\left(\partial_{A}^{b}F\right)\,\delta_{f}A_{b}\,,

(3.39)

where as before we use the notation,
\partial_{A}^{b}=\partial/\partial A_{b}
. The composition of two gauge transformations can be written,

\displaystyle\delta_{f}\left(\delta_{g}A_{a}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\{g(x),\delta_{f}A_{a}\}_{\Phi=0}+\left(\partial^{b}_{p}\{g(x),\Phi_{a}\}\right)_{\Phi=0}\delta_{f}A_{b}

(3.40)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\{g(x),\{f(x),\Phi_{a}\}_{\Phi=0}\}_{\Phi=0}+\left(\partial^{b}_{p}\{g(x),\Phi_{a}\}\right)_{\Phi=0}\{f(x),\Phi_{b}\}_{\Phi=0}\,,

since,

\partial_{A}^{b}\left(\{g(x),\Phi_{a}\}_{\Phi=0}\right)=\left(\partial_{p}^{b}\{g(x),\Phi_{a}\}\right)_{\Phi=0}\,.

(3.41)

And thus we find,

\displaystyle\delta_{f}\left(\delta_{g}A_{a}\right)-\delta_{g}\left(\delta_{f}A_{a}\right)=

(3.42)

\displaystyle-\{g(x),\{f(x),\Phi_{a}\}_{\Phi=0}\}_{\Phi=0}+\left(\partial^{b}_{p}\{g(x),\Phi_{a}\}\right)_{\Phi=0}\{f(x),\Phi_{b}\}_{\Phi=0}

\displaystyle+\{f(x),\{g(x),\Phi_{a}\}_{\Phi=0}\}_{\Phi=0}-\left(\partial^{b}_{p}\{f(x),\Phi_{a}\}\right)_{\Phi=0}\{g(x),\Phi_{b}\}_{\Phi=0}\,.

Using the relation (
7.135
) from the appendix in the right hand side of (
3.42
) we represent it as,

-\{g(x),\{f(x),\Phi_{a}\}\}_{\Phi=0}+\{f(x),\{g(x),\Phi_{a}\}\}_{\Phi=0}\,.

(3.43)

Finally applying now the Jacobi identity we end up with,

\delta_{f}\left(\delta_{g}A_{a}\right)-\delta_{g}\left(\delta_{f}A_{a}\right)=\{\{f(x),g(x)\},\Phi_{a}\}_{\Phi=0}=\delta_{\{f,g\}}A_{a}\\
.

(3.44)

The latter means that the gauge variations (
3.34
) close the Lie algebra (
1.4
).
\Box

3.1
Field redefinition

The form of the Poisson gauge transformation (
3.34
) relies on the precise symplectic embedding
\gamma^{i}_{a}(x,p)
of given Poisson structure (
1.2
).
However, as it was discussed in Sec. 2.1 the symplectic embedding of (
1.2
) is not unique. Different embeddings are related by the invertible change of
p
-variables,
\phi:\,p\to\tilde{p}
, with
\phi(p)|_{\alpha=0}=p
. Having the Poisson gauge transformation (
3.34
) which satisfy the relation (
1.4
), one may construct another gauge transformation corresponding to the new symplectic embedding
\tilde{\gamma}^{i}_{a}(x,\tilde{p})
,

\tilde{\delta}_{f}\tilde{A}_{a}=\tilde{\gamma}^{i}_{a}(\tilde{A})\,\partial_{i}f+\{f,\tilde{A}_{a}\}\,,

(3.45)

which will close the same gauge algebra (
1.4
).

From the physical perspective one may expect that the invertible field redefinitions defined by,
\phi:A\to\tilde{A}
, and
\tilde{f}=f
,
will leave our construction invariant. We define the new gauge transformations
\tilde{\delta}_{f}
by setting,

\tilde{\delta}_{f}:=\phi\circ\delta_{f}\circ\phi^{-1}\,,

(3.46)

or in the other words,

\tilde{\delta}_{f}\tilde{A}_{a}:=\left.\delta_{f}\tilde{A}_{a}(A)\right|_{A(\tilde{A})}=\left.\partial^{k}_{A}\tilde{A}_{a}(A)\,\delta_{f}A_{k}\right|_{A(\tilde{A})}\,.

(3.47)

Taking into account (
2.11
) we see that it is exactly the expression (
3.45
), meaning that the arbitrariness in our construction corresponds to the invertible field redefinition.

Since the field redefinition is invertible the gauge orbits are mapped onto the gauge orbits and the Seiberg-Witten condition,

\tilde{A}\left(A+\delta_{f}A\right)=\tilde{A}(A)+\tilde{\delta}_{f}\tilde{A}(A)\,,

(3.48)

is trivially satisfied up to the linear order in
f
. Moreover,

\left[\tilde{\delta}_{f},\tilde{\delta}_{g}\right]=\tilde{\delta}_{f}\circ\tilde{\delta}_{g}-\tilde{\delta}_{g}\circ\tilde{\delta}_{f}=\phi\circ\left(\delta_{f}\circ\delta_{g}-\delta_{g}\circ\delta_{f}\right)\circ\phi^{-1}=\tilde{\delta}_{\{f,g\}}\,,

(3.49)

meaning that the gauge algebra remains the same, as expected. In
[
36
]
it was shown that the Seiberg-Witten maps correspond to L
∞
-quasi-isomorphisms which describe the arbitrariness in the definition of the related L
∞
algebra in the L
∞
-bootstrap approach
[
10
]
.

4
Covariant derivative

We start with the introduction of the matter fields
\psi
and the definition of the non-commutative
U(1)
gauge transformations
\delta^{NC}_{f}\psi
which should close the same gauge algebra (
1.3
) as the corresponding gauge fields
A_{a}
. In case pf the associative star products there are three possibilities to define such a gauge transformation. It can be taken to be left or right star multiplication,
\delta^{NC}_{f}\psi=if\star\psi
or
\delta^{NC}_{f}\psi=i\psi\star f
, or even as the star commutator,
\delta^{NC}_{f}\psi=-i[\psi,f]_{\star}
. Only the third possibility is compatible with the semi-classical limit which is the subject of this research. That is why we define the Poisson gauge transformation of the matter field as,

\delta_{f}\psi=\{\psi,f\}\,.

(4.50)

The Jacobi identity implies that such a determined gauge transformations close the same algebra as (
1.4
), i.e.,

[\delta_{f},\delta_{g}]\psi=\delta_{\{f,g\}}\psi\,.

(4.51)

We note that in the commutative limit the Poisson gauge variation of the mater field vanishes,
\lim_{\alpha\to 0}\delta_{f}\psi=0
, meaning that in this limit the matter field
\psi
is ’electrically neutral’ and does not interact with the gauge field
A_{a}
. The interaction between the gauge field and such a defined matter field is caused by the non-commutativity. At the moment we do not see a clear physical meaning of such an interaction. The field
\psi
appears in our construction more like an auxiliary object needed for the consistent definition of the covariant derivative, which in turn is a central object of this work. As we will see in the next Section, the commutator of the covariant derivatives will produce the gauge covariant field strength for the gauge field. Also this object is essential for the derivation of the corresponding Bianchi identity and the equations of motion.

Our aim now is to construct the covariant derivative
{\cal D}_{a}(\psi)
, the object satisfying two main requirements.

•

It should transform covariantly under the Poisson gauge transformation
\delta_{f}
defined in (
3.34
) and (
4.50
), i.e.,

\delta_{f}\left({\cal D}_{a}(\psi)\right)=\{{\cal D}_{a}(\psi),f\}\,.

(4.52)

This property will be essential for the construction of the gauge covariant Lagrangian and the gauge invariant action for the Poisson gauge theory.

•

In the commutative limit it should reproduce the standard partial derivative,

\lim_{\alpha\to 0}{\cal D}_{a}(\psi)=\partial_{a}\psi\,,

(4.53)

since the interaction between the matter field and the gauge field disappears when
\alpha\to 0
.

The answer is given by the following proposition.

Proposition 4.54
.

The operator,

{\cal D}_{a}(\psi)=\rho_{a}^{i}(A)\,\{\psi,\Phi_{i}\}_{\Phi=0}\,,

(4.55)

satisfies the above two conditions if the matrix,
\rho_{a}^{i}(A):=\rho^{i}_{a}(x,p)_{\Phi=0}
, and
\rho^{i}_{a}(x,p)
obeys the equation,

\{f(x),\rho_{a}^{i}(x,p)\}+\rho_{a}^{b}(x,p)\,\partial_{p}^{i}\{f(x),p_{b}\}=0\,,\qquad\forall f(x)\,.

(4.56)

Proof.

Let us calculate,

\displaystyle\delta_{f}\left(\rho_{a}^{i}(A)\,\{\psi,\Phi_{i}\}_{\Phi=0}\right)=

(4.57)

\displaystyle\partial_{A}^{b}\rho_{a}^{i}(A)\,\{f,\Phi_{b}\}_{\Phi=0}\,\{\psi,\Phi_{i}\}_{\Phi=0}+\rho_{a}^{i}(A)\,\{\{\psi,f\},\Phi_{i}\}_{\Phi=0}+

\displaystyle\rho_{a}^{i}(A)\,(\partial_{p}^{b}\{\psi,\Phi_{i}\})_{\Phi=0}\,\{f,\Phi_{b}\}_{\Phi=0}-\rho_{a}^{i}(A)\,\{\psi,\{f,\Phi_{i}\}_{\Phi=0}\}_{\Phi=0}\,.

Observe that,

\partial_{A}^{b}\rho_{a}^{i}(A)\,\{f,\Phi_{b}\}_{\Phi=0}=\{f,\rho_{a}^{i}(p)-\rho_{a}^{i}(A)\}_{\Phi=0}\,,

(4.58)

and by (
7.135
) from the appendix,

\{\psi,\{f,\Phi_{i}\}_{\Phi=0}\}_{\Phi=0}=\{\psi,\{f,\Phi_{i}\}\}_{\Phi=0}-(\partial_{p}^{b}\{f,\Phi_{i}\})_{\Phi=0}\,\{\psi,\Phi_{b}\}_{\Phi=0}\,.

(4.59)

So the right hand side of (
4.57
) becomes,

\displaystyle\{f,\rho_{a}^{i}(p)\}_{\Phi=0}\,\{\psi,\Phi_{i}\}_{\Phi=0}+\{\rho_{a}^{i}(A),f\}_{\Phi=0}\,\{\psi,\Phi_{i}\}_{\Phi=0}+

(4.60)

\displaystyle\rho_{a}^{i}(A)\,\{\{\psi,f\},\Phi_{i}\}_{\Phi=0}+\rho_{a}^{i}(A)\,(\partial_{p}^{b}\{\psi,\Phi_{i}\})_{\Phi=0}\,\{f,\Phi_{b}\}_{\Phi=0}+

\displaystyle\rho_{a}^{i}(A)\,(\partial_{p}^{b}\{f,\Phi_{i}\})_{\Phi=0}\,\{\psi,\Phi_{b}\}_{\Phi=0}-\rho_{a}^{i}(A)\,\{\psi,\{f,\Phi_{i}\}\}_{\Phi=0}\,.

Using the Jacobi identity we rewrite it as,

\displaystyle\left[\{f,\rho_{a}^{i}(p)\}_{\Phi=0}+\rho_{a}^{b}(A)\,(\partial_{p}^{i}\{f,\Phi_{b}\})_{\Phi=0}\right]\{\psi,\Phi_{i}\}_{\Phi=0}+

(4.61)

\displaystyle\{\rho_{a}^{i}(A),f\}_{\Phi=0}\,\{\psi,\Phi_{i}\}_{\Phi=0}+\rho_{a}^{i}(A)\left[(\partial_{p}^{b}\{\psi,\Phi_{i}\})_{\Phi=0}\,\{f,\Phi_{b}\}_{\Phi=0}+\{\{\varphi,\Phi_{i}\},f\}_{\Phi=0}\right]\,.

Taking into account that by (
7.135
),

(\partial_{p}^{b}\{\varphi,\Phi_{i}\})_{\Phi=0}\,\{f,\Phi_{b}\}_{\Phi=0}+\{\{\varphi,\Phi_{i}\},f\}_{\Phi=0}=\{\{\varphi,\Phi_{i}\}_{\Phi=0},f\}_{\Phi=0}\,,

(4.62)

the second line in (
4.61
) becomes,

\{\rho_{a}^{i}(A)\,\{\varphi,\Phi_{i}\}_{\Phi=0},f\}_{\Phi=0}=\{{\cal D}_{a}(\varphi),f\}\,.

(4.63)

Therefore the relation (
4.52
) holds true if the first line of (
4.61
) vanishes, i.e.,

\{f(x),\rho_{a}^{i}(x,p)\}+\rho_{a}^{b}(x,p)\,\partial_{p}^{i}\{f(x),\Phi_{b}\}=0\,,\qquad\forall f(x)\,.

(4.64)

Since
\{A_{b}(x),f(x)\}
does not depend on
p
one arrives at (
4.56
). The statement regarding the commutative limit,
\alpha\to 0
, follows from the fact that in this limit,
\{\psi,\Phi_{i}\}\to\partial_{i}\psi
, and
\rho_{a}^{i}(x,p)\to\delta_{a}^{i}
.
\Box

In local coordinates the equation (
4.56

can be written as,

\gamma^{j}_{b}\,\partial^{b}_{p}\,\rho_{a}^{i}+\rho_{a}^{b}\,\partial^{i}_{p}\gamma^{j}_{b}+\alpha\,\Theta^{jb}\,\partial_{b}\rho_{a}^{i}=0\,,

(4.65)

where

For the arbitrary non-commutativity parameter

\displaystyle\delta^{i}_{a}-\frac{\alpha}{2}\,\partial_{a}\Theta^{ib}p_{b}+

(4.66)

6
Action principle and field equations

Having in hand the covariant derivative (
4.55
) and the Poisson field strength (
5.89
) now we are in the position to construct the gauge covariant Lagrangian,
\delta_{f}{\cal L}=\{{\cal L},f\}
, and gauge invariant action,
\delta_{f}S=0
. To do so, first one ought to introduce an appropriate integration mesure
\mu(x)
, which for any two Schwartz functions
f
and
g
should satisfy the condition,

\int\!d^{N}x\,\mu(x)\,\{f,g\}=0\,,\qquad\Leftrightarrow\qquad\partial_{l}\left(\mu(x)\,\Theta^{lk}(x)\right)=0\,.

(6.120)

For the
su(2)
-like Poisson structure (
2.17
) the integration measure can be taken to be a constant
\mu(x)=1
. For
\kappa
-Minkowski Lie-Poisson bi-vector (
2.23
) the measure is not constant. An explicit expression for
\mu(x)
in this case was found in
[
35
]
.

Let us discuss first the pure gauge sector without matter fields
\psi
. The gauge invariant action in this case reads
[
37
]
,

S_{g}=\int\!d^{N}x\,\mu(x)\,{\cal L}_{g}\,,\qquad{\cal L}_{g}=-\frac{1}{4}\,{\cal F}_{ab}\,{\cal F}^{ab}\,.

(6.121)

To derive the corresponding Euler-Lagrange equations we will need the following relation.

Proposition 6.122
.

Provided that the matrices
\gamma^{i}_{l}(A)
and
\rho_{a}^{l}(A)
are defined by the propositions
3.36
and
4.54
correspondingly, and the measure
\mu(x)
satisfies (
6.120
), then the following relation holds true,

\displaystyle\partial_{i}\left(\mu(x)\,\rho_{a}^{l}(A)\,\gamma^{i}_{l}(A)\right)+\mu(x)\left\{A_{l},\rho_{a}^{l}(A)\right\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\mu(x)\,\Lambda_{a}{}^{cd}(A)\,{\cal F}_{cd}

(6.123)

\displaystyle+\left[\partial_{i}\left(\mu(x)\,\rho_{a}^{l}(p)\,\gamma^{i}_{l}(p)\right)\right]_{\Phi=0}\,,

where the quantities
\Lambda_{a}{}^{cd}
and
{\cal F}_{cd}
were defined in the proposition
5.87
.

Proof.

Using the equation (
4.56
) we represent the left hand side as,

\displaystyle\left[\partial_{i}\left(\mu(x)\,\rho_{a}^{l}(p)\,\gamma^{i}_{l}(p)\right)\right]_{\Phi=0}+\partial^{m}_{A}\rho^{l}_{a}(A)\,\{\Phi_{l},\Phi_{m}\}_{\Phi=0}\,,

and use the definitions of
\Lambda_{a}{}^{cd}(A)
and
{\cal F}_{cd}
to complete the proof.

\Box

Using the relation (
6.123
) one finds the Euler-Lagrange equations for (
6.121
),

\displaystyle\mu\,\rho_{b}^{k}\,{\cal D}_{a}\left({\cal F}^{ab}\right)-\mu\,{\cal F}^{ab}\left(\rho_{a}^{k}\,\partial^{m}_{A}\rho_{b}^{l}+\rho_{a}^{m}\,\partial^{l}_{A}\rho_{b}^{k}+\rho_{a}^{l}\,\partial^{k}_{A}\rho_{b}^{m}\right)\{\Phi_{l},\Phi_{m}\}_{\Phi=0}

(6.124)

\displaystyle+{\cal F}^{ab}\left[\partial_{i}\left(\mu\,\rho_{a}^{l}(p)\,\rho_{b}^{k}(p)\,\gamma^{i}_{l}(p)\right)\right]_{\Phi=0}=0\,.

Multiplying by
\left(\rho^{-1}\right)^{c}_{k}
we end up with Poisson gauge equations,

\displaystyle\mu\,{\cal D}_{a}\left({\cal F}^{ac}\right)+\frac{\mu}{2}\,{\cal F}^{cb}\,\Lambda_{b}{}^{de}\,{\cal F}_{de}-\mu\,{\cal F}^{db}\,\Lambda_{b}{}^{ce}\,{\cal F}_{de}+\left(\rho^{-1}\right)^{c}_{k}{\cal F}^{ab}\partial_{i}\left(\mu\,\rho_{a}^{l}\,\rho_{b}^{k}\,\gamma^{i}_{l}\right)=0\,.

(6.125)

These equations transform covariantly under the Poisson gauge transformation (
3.34
) and reproduce the first pare of the Maxwell equations in the commutative limit
\alpha\to 0
, if the measure
\mu(x)
is constant.

As it was already mentioned, for the
su(2)
-like Poisson structure (
2.17
) the integration measure is constant
\mu(x)=1
. Since, for any linear Poisson structures the matrix
\rho_{a}^{l}(p)
does not depend on coordinates, the last term on the left in (
6.125
) disappears. Moreover, one may check that in this case,

{\cal F}^{ab}\left(\rho_{a}^{k}\,\partial^{m}_{A}\rho_{b}^{l}+\rho_{a}^{m}\,\partial^{l}_{A}\rho_{b}^{k}+\rho_{a}^{l}\,\partial^{k}_{A}\rho_{b}^{m}\right)\{\Phi_{l},\Phi_{m}\}_{\Phi=0}\equiv 0\,.

(6.126)

Therefore the equations of motion of the
su(2)
-like Poisson gauge theory become,

\displaystyle{\cal D}^{\varepsilon}_{a}\left({\cal F}^{ac}_{\varepsilon}\right)=0\,,

(6.127)

where the superscript
\varepsilon
in
{\cal D}^{\varepsilon}_{a}
and the subscript
\varepsilon
in
{\cal F}^{ac}_{\varepsilon}
just indicate that these objects correspond to the
su(2)
-structure.
For the
\kappa
-Minkowski case the measure is not constant, so the equations of motion will contain additional terms.

Now let us discuss the contribution from the matter fields. First of all let us observe that due proposition
6.122
one has,

\displaystyle\int\!d^{N}x\,\mu\,\psi\,{\cal D}_{a}(\psi)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\int\!d^{N}x\,\mu\,{\cal D}_{a}(\psi)\,\psi+\int\!d^{N}x\,\mu\,\psi\,\Lambda_{a}{}^{cd}\,{\cal F}_{cd}\,\psi

(6.128)

\displaystyle+\int\!d^{N}x\left[\partial_{i}\left(\mu(x)\,\rho_{a}^{l}(p)\,\gamma^{i}_{l}(p)\right)\right]_{\Phi=0}\psi\,\psi\,.

The latter means that the operator
{\cal D}_{a}
is not self-adjoint. Therefore to construct real gauge invariant action for the Fermionic matter fields
\psi
we write,

S_{int}=\int\!d^{N}x\,\mu(x)\,{{\cal L}_{int}\,,\qquad\cal L}_{int}=\frac{i}{2}\left[\bar{\psi}\,\Gamma^{a}{\cal D}_{a}\psi-\left({\cal D}_{a}\bar{\psi}\right)\Gamma^{a}\,\psi\right]-m\,\bar{\psi}\,\psi\,,

(6.129)

where
\Gamma^{a}
are the gamma matrices. The corresponding Dirac equation reads,

\left[i\,\mu\,\Gamma^{a}{\cal D}_{a}-\frac{i\mu}{2}\,\Gamma^{a}\,\Lambda_{a}{}^{cd}\,{\cal F}_{cd}-\frac{i}{2}\,\Gamma^{a}\,\partial_{b}\left(\mu\,\rho_{a}^{l}\,\gamma^{b}_{l}\right)-\mu\,m\right]\psi=0\,.

(6.130)

To find the contribution of the matter fields to the Poisson Gauge equations one calculates,

\displaystyle\frac{\delta S_{int}}{\delta A_{k}}=i\,\mu\,\rho_{c}^{k}\,\{\bar{\psi}\,\Gamma^{c},\psi\}-\frac{i\mu}{2}\left(\partial^{k}_{A}\rho_{c}^{l}-\partial^{l}_{A}\rho_{c}^{k}\right)\left[\{\bar{\psi},\Phi_{l}\}_{\Phi=0}\,\Gamma^{c}\,\psi-\bar{\psi}\,\Gamma^{c}\,\{\psi,\Phi_{l}\}_{\Phi=0}\right]\,

(6.131)

Multiplying it by
\left(\rho^{-1}\right)^{a}_{k}
we obtain the right hand side (the current) of the eq. (
6.125
),

J^{a}=-i\,\mu\,\{\bar{\psi}\,\Gamma^{a},\psi\}+\frac{i\mu}{2}\,\Lambda_{c}{}^{ab}\left[\left({\cal D}_{b}\bar{\psi}\right)\Gamma^{c}\,\psi-\bar{\psi}\,\Gamma^{c}\,{\cal D}_{b}\psi\right]\,.

(6.132)

In the commutative limit the interaction disappears, the field become electrically neutral as it was already discussed in the Sec. 4.

7

Conclusions and discussion

Working in the framework of the symplectic embeddings we introduce the Poisson gauge transformations (
3.34
) which close the algebra (
1.4
) obtained as the semi-classical limit of the full non-commutative gauge algebra (
1.3
). The proposition
4.54
determines the gauge covariant derivative of the matter field
{\cal D}_{a}(\psi)
. The commutator relation for the covariant derivatives (
5.88
) defines the Poisson field strength (
5.89
) which transforms covariantly under the gauge transformations (
3.34
) and tends to the standard
U(1)
field strength in the commutative limit. The corresponding Bianchi identity is set by the proposition
5.107
. The gauge invariant action, given by (
6.121
) for the pure gauge fields and by (
6.129
) for the interaction part with the matter fields, is constructed in the Sec. 6 from the gauge covariant objects
{\cal D}_{a}(\psi)
and
{\cal F}_{ab}
. From this action we derive the field equations (
6.125
) for the gauge fields without interaction with the matter field, the Dirac equation for the matter field (
6.130
), and the current term (
6.132
) describing the interaction between the gauge and the matter fields in the equations of motion.

The symplectic embedding of given Poisson structure is not unique, the corresponding arbitrariness is described in the Sec. 2.1. So, for the same Poisson structure
\Theta^{ab}(x)
one may obtain different Poisson gauge transformations and other subsequent structures. However the corresponding gauge systems are related by the Seiberg-Witten map described in the Sec. 3.1. If two different Poisson structures
\Theta^{ij}(x)
and
\tilde{\Theta}^{ij}(\tilde{x})
are related by the invertible coordinate transformation,
\upsilon:\,x\to\tilde{x}
, then the corresponding Poisson gauge theories constructed following the proposed here scheme are related by the same coordinate transformation.

For arbitrary given Poisson structure
\Theta^{ab}(x)

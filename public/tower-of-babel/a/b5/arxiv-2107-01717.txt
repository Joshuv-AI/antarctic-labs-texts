The b b -weight distribution for MDS codes
Canze Zhu, Qunying Liao

Abstract




Abstract.

For a positive integer
b\geq 2
, the
b
-symbol code is a new coding framework proposed to combat
b
-errors in
b
-symbol
read channels. Especially, the
2
-symbol code is called a symbol-pair code. Remarkably, a classical maximum distance separable (MDS) code is
also an MDS
b
-symbol code. Recently, for any MDS code
\mathcal{C}
, Ma and Luo determined the symbol-pair weight distribution of
\mathcal{C}
. In this paper, by calculating the number of solutions for some equations and utilizing some shortened codes of
\mathcal{C}
, we give the connection between the
b
-weight distribution and the number of codewords in shortened codes of
\mathcal{C}
with special shape. Furthermore, note that shortened codes of
\mathcal{C}
are also MDS codes, the number of these codewords with special shape are also determined by the shorten method. From the above calculation, the
b
-weight distribution of
\mathcal{C}
is determined. Our result generalies the corresonding result of Ma and Luo
[
15
]
.

Keywords.
MDS code; MDS
b
-symbol code;
b
-weight distribution

94A24, 94B05

Introduction

In 2011, Cassuto and Blaum
[
2
]
first proposed a new coding framework for symbol-pair read channels. The outputs of the read process in the channels are overlapping pairs of
symbols. After that, Chee et al.
[
3
,
4
]
established a Singleton-type bound for symbol-pair codes and considered the constructions for symbol-pair codes meeting the bound.

In 2016, Yaakobi et al.
[
18
]
generalized the coding framework for symbol-pair read channels to that for
b
-symbol read channels, where the read operation is performed as a
consecutive sequence of
b
-symbols
(b>2)
. They also generalized some known results for symbol-pair read channels to those for
b
-symbol read channels, and showed that a
code
\mathcal{C}
with minimum
b
-distance
d_{b}
can correct at most
\lfloor\frac{d_{b}-2}{2}\rfloor

b
-symbol errors. Furthermore, they gave a decoding algorithm based on a bounded distance decoder for the cyclic code. Later, Ding et al.
[
7
]
extended the Singleton-type bound to be the
b
-symbol case and constructed MDS
b
-symbol codes basing on projective geometry, and then, they showed that an MDS
b
-symbol code with
b
-distance
d_{b}<n
is also an MDS
(b+1)
-symbol code. Moreover, relative results about constructions for MDS symbol-pair codes can be seen in
[
5
,
6
,
8
,
9
,
10
,
12
,
13
,
14
]
.

It is well-known that the Hamming weight distribution for an MDS code can be uniquely determined
[
11
]
. However, there are few works on the
b
-weight distribution for MDS
b
-symbol codes. In 2021, Ma and Luo gave the symbol-pair weight distribution of MDS codes by employing some shortened MDS codes
[
15
]
. Furthermore, there are some results about the
b
-weight distribution for cyclic code. In 2018, Sun et al. gave the symbol-pair weight distribution of a class of repeated-root cyclic codes
[
17
]
. In 2021, Shi et al. presented the geometric approach to
b
-weights of cyclic codes, and constructed a class of irreducible cyclic codes with constant
b
-weight
[
16
]
. Recently, Zhu et al. gave a complete
b
-weight distribution of a class of irreducible cyclic codes with two nonzero
b
-weight
[
19
]
.

In this paper, for any MDS code
\mathcal{C}
, the connection between the
b
-weight distribution and the number of codewords in shortened codes of
\mathcal{C}
with special shape is presented by calculating the number of solutions for some equations and utilizing some shortened codes of
\mathcal{C}
. Furthermore, by the shorten method, the number of these codewords with special shape is given. And then the
b
-weight distribution is completely determined. Especially, for
b=2
, we obtain Theorem
1
in
[
15
]
.

The paper is organized as follows. In section 2, some related basic notations and
results are given. In section 3, the main results are presented. In section 4, the proofs
for the main results are given. In section 5, we conclude the whole paper.

2
Preliminaries

Throughout this paper, let
\mathbb{F}_{q}
be the finite field with
q
elements, where
q
is a prime power. An element in
\mathbb{F}_{q}
is also called a symbol. Let
b
be a positive integer. For a vector
\mathbf{x}=(x_{1},x_{2},\ldots,x_{n})\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{n}
, we define the
b
-symbol read vector
of
\mathbf{x}
as

\displaystyle\pi_{b}(\mathbf{x})=((x_{1},x_{2},\ldots,x_{b}),(x_{2},x_{3},\ldots,x_{b+1}),\ldots,(x_{n-1},x_{n},\ldots,x_{b-2}),(x_{n},x_{1},\ldots,x_{b-1})).

For any two vectors
\mathbf{x}
and
\mathbf{y}
in
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{n}
, one has

\displaystyle\pi_{b}(\mathbf{x}+\mathbf{y})=\pi_{b}(\mathbf{x})+\pi_{b}(\mathbf{y}),

and the
b
-distance between
\mathbf{x}
and
\mathbf{y}
is defined as

\displaystyle D_{b}(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y}):=\#\{1\leq i\leq n~|~(x_{i},\ldots,x_{i+b-1})\neq(y_{i},\ldots,y_{i+b-1})\},

where
x_{m}=x_{m-n}
if
m>n
. Accordingly, the
b
-weight of
\mathbf{x}
is defined as

\displaystyle wt_{b}(\mathbf{x}):=\#\{1\leq i\leq n~|~(x_{i},\ldots,x_{i+b-1})\neq\mathbf{0}\},

where
\mathbf{0}
denotes the all-zeros vector in
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{n}
, and
x_{m}=x_{m-n}
if
m>n
. Especially, for
b=1
,
D_{b}(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y})
and
wt_{b}(\mathbf{x})
are called Hamming distance and Hamming weight, respectively.

An linear
b
-symbol code
\mathcal{C}
over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
is a
k
-dimensional subspace of
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{n}
with minimum
b
-distance

\displaystyle d_{b}:=\min\#\{D_{b}(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y})~|~\mathbf{x},~\mathbf{y}\in\mathcal{C},~\mathbf{x}\neq\mathbf{y}\}.

Especially, if
b=1
, let
d=d_{1}
, then
\mathcal{C}
is called an
[n,k,d]
linear code.

The size of a
b
-symbol code satisfies the following Singleton bound.

Lemma 2.1

[
7
]

Let
b\leq d_{b}\leq n
. If
\mathcal{C}
is a
b
-symbol code over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
with length
n
and
minimum
b
-distance
d_{b}
, then

\displaystyle\big|\mathcal{C}\big|\leq q^{n-d_{b}+b}.

An
b
-symbol code with parameters achieving the Singleton bound is called a maximum distance separable (in short, MDS)
b
-symbol code. For
b=1
, the MDS
b
-symbol code is also called the MDS code. It is easy to see that an MDS code is also an MDS
b
-symbol code by the following lemma.

Lemma 2.2

[
7
]

An MDS
b
-symbol code with
d_{b}<n
is an MDS
(b+1)
-symbol code.

Next, we give some notations on shortened codes and the Hamming weight distribution for MDS codes.

Suppose
\mathcal{C}
is a code of length
n
over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
, we denote
[1,n]=\{1,2,\ldots,n\}
, and for
S\subseteq[1,n]
, denote
\overline{S}=[1,n]\backslash S
. Then the code
\mathcal{C}_{S}
shortened on
S
from
\mathcal{C}
is defined by

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}_{S}=\bigg\{(c_{j})_{j\in\overline{S}}~\bigg|~\text{there exists some ~}(c_{1},c_{1},\ldots,c_{n})\in\mathcal{C},\text{such that}~c_{i}=0~\text{for all}~i\in S\bigg\}.

For more details, see Section 1.5 in
[
11
]
.

The Hamming weight distribution for the MDS code is determined by the following lemma.

Lemma 2.3

[
11
]

Let
\mathcal{C}
be an
[n,k,d]
MDS code and
A_{i}

(i=0,1,\ldots,n)
the number of codewords in
\mathcal{C}

with weight
i
. Then the weight distribution for
\mathcal{C}
is given by
A_{0}=1
,
A_{i}=0~(1\leq i\leq d-1)

and

\displaystyle A_{i}=\binom{n}{i}\sum\limits_{j=0}^{i-d}(-1)^{j}\binom{i}{j}\big(q^{i+1-d-j}-1\big)\hskip 9.24994pt(d\leq i\leq n).

The following lemma characterizes that a code shortened on
S
from an MDS code is also an MDS code.

Lemma 2.4

[
11
]

Let
\mathcal{C}
be an
[n,k,n-k+1]_{q}
MDS code and
\mathcal{C}_{S}
be the code shortened on
S
from
\mathcal{C}
, where
S
is a set with
|S|=s<k
. Then
\mathcal{C}_{S}
is an
[n-s,k-s,n-k+1]_{q}
MDS code.

The following notations
N_{\infty}(r,L)
and
N_{b}(r,L)
are useful to calculate the number of codewords in an MDS code with special shape. For
b
,
r
,
L

\in\mathbb{N}^{*}
with
b\geq 3
and
r\leq L
, let
N_{\infty}(r,L)
be the number of solutions for the equation

\displaystyle\begin{cases}x_{1}+x_{2}+\cdots+x_{r}=L;\\
x_{i}\in\mathbb{N}^{*}.\end{cases}

Let
N_{b}(e,L)
be the number of solutions for equation

\displaystyle\begin{cases}x_{1}+x_{2}+\cdots+x_{r}=L;\\
x_{i}\in\mathbb{N}^{*},~1\leq x_{i}\leq b-2~(i=1,\ldots,r),\end{cases}

furthermore, we set
N_{b}(0,0)=1
for convience. In fact,
N_{\infty}(e,L)
and
N_{b}(e,L)
are correspond to the number of
r
-combinations of a multiset problem.
N_{\infty}(e,L)
is given in the following

Lemma 2.5
(Chapter
6.2

[
1
]
)

N_{\infty}(e,L)=\binom{L-1}{e-1}
.

In addition,
N_{b}(e,L)
can be calculated by
N_{\infty}(e,L)
and the inclusion-exclusion principle, for more details, see Chapter
2.5
and Chapter
6.2

[
1
]
.

3
The
b
-weight distribution for MDS codes

In this section, the
b
-weight distribution for MDS codes is determined. We begin with the following notation.

Notation:
For any positive integers
d,I,L
and
L_{i}~(i=1,\ldots,I)
with
\sum\limits_{i=1}^{I}L_{i}=L>d
, let
\mathcal{C}
be an MDS code
[L,L-d+1,d]
. Let
F_{b,d}(L_{1},\ldots,L_{I})
be the number of codewords
\mathbf{c}\in\mathcal{C}
satisfying the following two conditions.

(\mathrm{F}1)

\mathbf{c}=(\underbrace{c_{1},\ldots,c_{L_{1}}}_{1},\underbrace{c_{L_{1}+1},\ldots,c_{L_{1}+L_{2}}}_{2},\ldots,\underbrace{c_{L_{1}+\cdots+L_{I-1}+1},\ldots,c_{L_{1}+\cdots+L_{I}}}_{I})
with

\displaystyle c_{L_{1}+\cdots+L_{i-1}+1}\neq 0\hskip 9.24994pt\text{and}\hskip 9.24994ptc_{{L_{1}+\cdots+L_{i}}}\neq 0~~(1\leq i\leq I);

(\mathrm{F}2)

wt_{b-1}(\mathbf{c})=L
.

For completeness, if
I=1
, we set
L_{1}+\cdots+L_{I-1}=0
in
(\mathrm{F}1)
.

In the following theorems, we set
\binom{m_{1}}{0}=1

(m_{1}\in\mathbb{N})
and
\binom{0}{m_{2}}=0

(m_{2}\in\mathbb{N}^{*})
for completeness. Now the connnection between the
b
-weight distribution for an MDS code and
F_{b,d}(L_{1},\ldots,L_{I})
is presented.

Theorem 3.1

For
b\geq 2
and an
[n,k,d]
MDS code
\mathcal{C}
, let
A^{b}(w)
be the number of codewords in
\mathcal{C}
with
b
-weight
w
. If
d+b-1<n
, then

\displaystyle A^{b}(w)=\begin{cases}1,\hskip 9.24994pt&w=0;\\
0,\hskip 9.24994pt&1\leq w\leq d+b-2;\end{cases}

and for
d+b-1\leq w\leq n
,

\displaystyle A^{b}(w)=

\displaystyle\delta_{n}(w)\sum\limits_{t=0}^{b-2}(t+1)F_{b,d}(n-t)+(n-w+b)F_{b,d}(w-b+1)

\displaystyle+\sum\limits_{t=0}^{b-2}(t+1)\sum\limits_{i=1}^{\lfloor M_{1}(w,b,t)\rfloor}\binom{n-w+i-1}{i-1}\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}a_{1},\ldots,a_{i+1}\in\mathbb{N}^{*}\\
a_{1}+\cdots+a_{i+1}=w-t-i(b-1)\end{subarray}}F_{b,d}(a_{1},\ldots,a_{i+1})

\displaystyle+\sum\limits_{t=b-1}^{n-w+b-1}(t+1)\sum\limits_{i=1}^{\lfloor M_{2}(w,b)\rfloor}\binom{n-w-t+b+i-2}{i-1}\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}a_{1},\ldots,a_{i+1}\in\mathbb{N}^{*}\\
a_{1}+\cdots+a_{i+1}=w-(i+1)(b-1)\end{subarray}}\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!F_{b,d}(a_{1},\ldots,a_{i+1}),

where

~~~\delta_{n}(w)=\begin{cases}1,\!&w=n;\\
0,\!&\text{otherwise},\end{cases}

M_{1}(w,b,t)=\min\big\{\frac{w-t-1}{b},\frac{w-t-d}{b-1}\big\},

M_{2}(w,b)=\min\big\{\frac{w-b}{b},\frac{w-d}{b-1}-1\big\}.

In the following theorem, we show that the value of
F_{b,d}(L_{1},\ldots,L_{I})
can be determined by
N_{b}(r,L)
.

Theorem 3.2

\displaystyle F_{b,d}(L_{1},\ldots,L_{I})=\begin{cases}A\Big(\sum\limits_{i=1}^{I}L_{i},d\Big),\hskip 9.24994pt&\text{if}~b=2;\\
f_{b}(L_{1},m_{1})\cdots f_{b}(L_{I},m_{I})A\Big(\sum\limits_{i=1}^{I}m_{i},d\Big),\hskip 9.24994pt&\text{if}~b\geq 3.\\
\end{cases}

where

\displaystyle f_{b}(L_{i},m_{i})=\sum\limits_{m_{i}=\big\lceil\frac{L_{i}+(b-2)}{b-1}\big\rceil}^{L_{i}}\sum\limits_{e_{i}=\big\lceil\frac{L_{i}-m_{i}}{b-2}\big\rceil}^{\min\{m_{i}-1,L_{i}-m_{i}\}}\binom{m_{i}-1}{e_{i}}N_{b}(e_{i},L_{i}-m_{i}),

and

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} A\Big(\sum\limits_{i=1}^{I}m_{i},d\Big)=\begin{cases}0,&\sum\limits_{i=1}^{I}m_{i}<d;\\
\sum\limits_{j=0}^{\sum\limits_{i=1}^{I}m_{i}-d}(-1)^{j}\binom{\sum\limits_{i=1}^{I}m_{i}}{j}\bigg(q^{\sum\limits_{i=1}^{I}m_{i}+1-d-j}-1\bigg),&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}\end{aligned}

Remark 3.1

By Theorems
3.1
-
3.2
, the
b
-weight distribution for an MDS code can be determined.

Remark 3.2

In general, the expression of
A^{b}(w)

(d+b-1\leq w\leq n)
is long and complex, the main reason is that the value of the term
\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}a_{1},\ldots,a_{i+1}\in\mathbb{N}^{*}\\
a_{1}+\cdots+a_{i+1}=w-t-i(b-1)\end{subarray}}F_{b,d}(a_{1},\ldots,a_{i+1})
is associated with
N_{b}(e,L)
, the value of which is not explicted. However, not only for
b=2~\text{or}~3
, but also for some special cases of general
b
,
N_{b}(e,L)
can be given explictly, thus
A^{b}(w)

(d+b-1\leq w\leq n)
can be given conciesly, see corollaries
3.1
-
3.4
.

Corollary 3.1
(Theorem
1
,
[
15
]
)

Let
\mathcal{C}
be an
[n,k,d]
MDS code, then

\displaystyle A^{2}(w)=\begin{cases}1,\hskip 9.24994pt&w=0;\\
0,\hskip 9.24994pt&1\leq w\leq d;\end{cases}

and for
d+1\leq w\leq n
,

\displaystyle A^{2}(w)=

\displaystyle\delta_{n}(w)A(n,d)+\sum\limits_{i=1}^{\lfloor M_{1}(w,0,2)\rfloor}\binom{n-w+i-1}{i-1}\binom{w-i-1}{i}A(w-i,d)

\displaystyle+\sum\limits_{i=1}^{\lfloor M_{2}(w,2)\rfloor+1}\bigg(2\binom{n-w+i-1}{i-1}+\binom{n-w+i}{i}\bigg)\binom{w-i-1}{i-1}A(w-i,d).

Proof
. By

\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}a_{1},\ldots,a_{i+1}\in\mathbb{N}^{*}\\
a_{1}+\cdots+a_{i+1}=N\end{subarray}}1=\binom{N-1}{i}~~\big(N=w-i~\text{or}~w-(i+1)\big)

and

\sum\limits_{t=1}^{n-w+1}(t+1)\binom{n-w-t+i}{i-1}=2\binom{n-w+i}{i}+\binom{n-w+i}{i+1},

let
b=2
in Theorems
3.1
-
3.2
, then we get the corollary.
\hfill\Box

Corollary 3.2

For an
[n,k,d]
MDS code
\mathcal{C}
with
d+2=n
, we have
A^{3}(w)=\begin{cases}1,\hskip 9.24994pt&w=0;\\
0,\hskip 9.24994pt&1\leq w\leq d+1,\end{cases}

and for
d+2\leq w\leq n
,

\displaystyle A^{3}(w)=

\displaystyle\delta_{n}(w)\sum\limits_{t=0}^{1}(t+1)F_{b,d}(n-t)+(n+3-w)F_{3,d}(w-2)

\displaystyle+\sum\limits_{t=0}^{1}(t+1)\sum\limits_{i=1}^{\lfloor M_{1}(w,3,t)\rfloor}\binom{n-w+i-1}{i-1}\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}a_{1},\ldots,a_{i+1}\in\mathbb{N}^{*}\\
a_{1}+\cdots+a_{i+1}=w-t-2i\end{subarray}}F_{3,d}(a_{1},\ldots,a_{i+1})

\displaystyle+\sum\limits_{t=2}^{n-w+2}(t+1)\sum\limits_{i=1}^{\lfloor M_{2}(w,3)\rfloor}\binom{n-w-t+i-1}{i-1}\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}a_{1},\ldots,a_{i+1}\in\mathbb{N}^{*}\\
a_{1}+\cdots+a_{i+1}=w-2(i+1)\end{subarray}}\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!F_{3,d}(a_{1},\ldots,a_{i+1}),

where

\displaystyle F_{b,d}(L_{1},\ldots,L_{I})=\sum\limits_{m_{1}=\big\lceil\frac{L_{1}+1}{2}\big\rceil}^{L_{1}}\binom{m_{1}-1}{L_{1}-m_{1}}\cdots\sum\limits_{m_{I}=\big\lceil\frac{L_{I}+1}{2}\big\rceil}^{L_{I}}\binom{m_{I}-1}{L_{I}-m_{I}}A\Big(\sum\limits_{i=1}^{I}m_{i},d\Big)

Proof
. Let
b=3
in Theorems
3.1
-
3.2
, by the definition of
N_{3}(e_{i},L_{i}-m_{i})
, we have

N_{3}(e_{i},L_{i}-m_{i})=\begin{cases}1,&\text{~if~}e_{i}=L_{i}-m_{i};\\
0,&\text{~if~}e_{i}\neq L_{i}-m_{i},\end{cases}

and then the
3
-weight distribution for the MDS code is given.
\hfill\Box

Corollary 3.3

Let
\mathcal{C}
be an
[n,k,d]
MDS code, if
w=d+b-1<n
, then

A^{b}(w)=n(q-1).

Proof
. For
w=d+b-1<n
, note that

\displaystyle\delta_{n}(w)=0,~~\lfloor M_{1}(w,b,0)\rfloor=1,~~\lfloor M_{1}(w,b,t)\rfloor\leq 0~(t\geq 1),~~M_{2}(w,b)=0，

\displaystyle A^{b}(d+b-1)=(n-d+1)F_{b,d}(d)+\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}a_{1},a_{2}\in\mathbb{N}^{*}\\

\displaystyle~~~~F_{b,d}(d)=A(d,d)=q-1\text{~~~and~~~}F_{b,d}(a_{1},a_{2})=A(d,d)=q-1~(a_{1}+a_{2}=d).

Thus

\displaystyle A^{b}(d+b-1)=(n-d+1)(q-1)+(d-1)(q-1)=n(q-1).

\hfill\Box

({c}_{L_{1}+\cdots+L_{i-1}+1},\ldots,{c}_{L_{1}+\cdots+L_{i}})\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{L_{i}}\backslash\{\mathbf{0}\}
, we decompose the
L_{i}
coordinates of
\mathbf{c}
into
j_{i}
blocks, i,e,

\displaystyle[L_{1}+\cdots+L_{i-1}+1,L_{1}+\cdots+L_{i}]=N_{i,1}\cup N_{i,2}\cup\cdots\cup N_{i,j_{i}},

where

\displaystyle N_{i,y}=[L_{1}+\cdots+L_{i-1}+n_{i,1}+\cdots+n_{i,y-1}+1,~L_{1}+\cdots+L_{i-1}+n_{i,1}+\cdots+n_{i,y}]~~(y=1,\ldots,j_{i}),

and
|N_{i,y}|=n_{i,y}\geq 1
. Obviously,
L_{i}=\sum\limits_{y=1}^{j_{i}}n_{i,y}
. we call
({c}_{L_{1}+\cdots+L_{i-1}+1},\ldots,{c}_{L_{1}+\cdots+L_{i}})\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{L_{i}}\backslash\{\mathbf{0}\}
possessing shape
(N_{i,1},N_{i,2},\ldots,N_{i,j_{i}})
if it satisfies the following two conditions.

(\mathrm{B}1)
For every block
N_{i,y}
, either each coordinate in
N_{i,y}
is zero or nonzero;

(\mathrm{B}2)
For any two adjacent blocks
N_{i,y}
and
N_{i,y+1}
, either each coordinate in

N_{i,y}
is zero and each coordinate in
N_{i,y+1}
is nonzero, or vice versa.

\bullet
Let
\mathbb{V}_{i}=\Big\{(c_{1},\ldots,c_{L_{i}})\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{L_{i}}~\big|~c_{1}\neq 0,~c_{L_{i}}\neq 0\Big\}.
Note that for a vector
(c_{1},\ldots,c_{L_{i}})\in\mathbb{V}_{i}
has shape
(N_{i,1},N_{i,2},\ldots,N_{i,j_{i}})
, then
j_{i}
is odd, thus the following set

\displaystyle P_{i}\big(j_{i},m_{i}\big)=\Big\{(N_{i,1},N_{i,2},\ldots,N_{i,j_{i}})~|

\displaystyle~|N_{i,1}|+|N_{i,3}|+\cdots+|N_{i,j_{i}}|=m_{i}~\text{and}~

\displaystyle\text{If~$\mathbf{c}\in\mathbb{V}_{i}$~has~shape~$(N_{i,1},N_{i,2},\ldots,N_{i,j_{i}})$},\text{then~$wt_{b-1}(\mathbf{c})=L_{i}$}\Big\}.

is well-defined.

\bullet
Let
Q(m_{1},\ldots,m_{I},N_{1,1},\ldots,N_{1,j_{1}},\ldots,N_{I,1},\ldots,N_{I,j_{I}})
be the number of codewords

\mathbf{c}=(\underbrace{c_{1},\ldots,c_{L_{1}}}_{1},\underbrace{c_{L_{1}+1},\ldots,c_{L_{1}+L_{2}}}_{2},\ldots,\underbrace{c_{L_{1}+\cdots+L_{I-1}+1},\ldots,c_{L_{1}+\cdots+L_{I}}}_{I})\in\mathcal{C}

satisfies
(\mathrm{F}1)
-
(\mathrm{F}2)
and every

\big(c_{L_{1}+\cdots+L_{i-1}+1},\ldots,c_{L_{1}+\cdots+L_{i}}\big)
has the Hamming weigh
m_{i}
and shape
\big(N_{i,1},\ldots,N_{1,j_{i}}\big)
.

Now by above notations and conditions
(\mathrm{F}1)
-
(\mathrm{F}2)
, we have

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} &F_{b,d}(L_{1},\ldots,L_{I})\\
=&\prod_{i=1}^{I}\sum\limits_{j_{i}}\sum\limits_{m_{i}}\sum\limits_{(N_{i,1},N_{i,2},\ldots,N_{i,j_{i}})\in P_{i}(j_{i},m_{i})}Q(m_{1},\ldots,m_{I},N_{1,1},\ldots,N_{1,j_{1}},\ldots,N_{I,1},\ldots,N_{I,j_{I}}).\end{aligned}

(4.15)

In the following,
Q(m_{1},\ldots,m_{I},N_{1,1},\ldots,N_{1,j_{1}},\ldots,N_{I,1},\ldots,N_{I,j_{I}})
is given by shorting
\mathcal{C}
. Let

\displaystyle T=\underbrace{N_{1,2}\cup\cdots\cup N_{1,j_{1}-1}}_{1}\cup\underbrace{N_{2,2}\cup\cdots\cup N_{2,j_{2}-1}}_{2}\cup\cdots\cup\underbrace{N_{I,2}\cup\cdots\cup N_{I,j_{I}-1}}_{I},

then

\displaystyle\big|[1,n]\backslash T\big|=m_{1}+m_{2}+\cdots+m_{I}.

If
m_{1}+m_{2}+\cdots+m_{I}<d
, then

\displaystyle Q(m_{1},\ldots,m_{I},N_{1,1},\ldots,N_{1,j_{1}},\ldots,N_{I,1},\ldots,N_{I,j_{I}})=0.

(4.16)

Otherwise, let
\mathcal{C}_{T}
be the code shortened on
T
from
\mathcal{C}
. It follows from Lemma
2.4
that

\mathcal{C}_{T}
is an MDS code with parameters

\displaystyle\bigg[\sum\limits_{i=1}^{I}m_{i},~\sum\limits_{i=1}^{I}m_{i}-d+1,~d~\bigg].

Ovbiously,
Q(m_{1},\ldots,m_{I},N_{1,1},\ldots,N_{1,j_{1}},\ldots,N_{I,1},\ldots,N_{I,j_{I}})
is equal to the number of codewords in
\mathcal{C}_{T}
with Hamming weight
\sum\limits_{i=1}^{I}m_{i}
,
By Lemma
2.3
, that is

\displaystyle Q(m_{1},\ldots,m_{I},N_{1,1},\ldots,N_{1,j_{1}},\ldots,N_{I,1},\ldots,N_{I,j_{I}})=\sum\limits_{j=0}^{\sum\limits_{i=1}^{I}m_{i}-d}(-1)^{j}\binom{\sum\limits_{i=1}^{I}m_{i}}{j}\bigg(q^{\sum\limits_{i=1}^{I}m_{i}-d+1-j}-1\bigg).

(4.17)

If
b=2
, then
\mathbf{c}\in\mathcal{C}
which satisfy conditions
(\mathrm{F}1)
-
(\mathrm{F}2)
if and only if all the coordinates of
\mathbf{c}
are nonzero, i.e.,
L_{i}=m_{i}
and
j_{i}=1
. Thus one has

\displaystyle F_{b,d}(L_{1},\ldots,L_{I})=\sum\limits_{j=0}^{L-d}(-1)^{j}\binom{L}{j}\Big(q^{L+1-d-j}-1\Big).

(4.18)

If
b=3
, then the calculation of
\sum\limits_{(N_{i,1},N_{i,2},\ldots,N_{i,j_{i}})\in P_{i}(j_{i},m_{i})}~1
is given by calculating the number of solutions for the corresponding equations.

For
m_{i}=L_{i}
, it is easy to see that
j_{i}=1
and

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{(N_{i,1},N_{i,2},\ldots,N_{i,j_{i}})\in P_{i}(j_{i},m_{i})}1=1.

(4.19)

For
m_{i}<L_{i}
, we have
j_{i}\geq 3
, and then by the definition of
P_{i}\big(j_{i},m_{i}\big)
, we know that
(N_{i,1},N_{i,2},\ldots,N_{i,j_{i}})\in P_{i}\big(j_{i},m_{i}\big)
if and only if

\displaystyle\begin{cases}n_{i,1}+n_{i,2}+\cdots+n_{i,j_{i}}=L_{i};\\
n_{i,1}+n_{i,3}+\cdots+n_{i,j_{i}}=m_{i};\\
n_{i,y}\in\mathbb{N}^{*},~~n_{i,y}\leq b-2~(y~\text{is even});\\
m_{i}<L_{i},~~j_{i}\geq 3\text{~is~odd},\end{cases}

where
n_{i,y}=|N_{i,y}|
. Above equations is equivalent to

\displaystyle\begin{cases}n_{i,2}+n_{i,4}+\cdots+n_{i,j_{i}-1}=L_{i}-m_{i};\\
n_{i,1}+n_{i,3}+\cdots+n_{i,j_{i}}=m_{i};\\
n_{i,y}\in\mathbb{N}^{*},~~n_{i,y}\leq b-2~(y~\text{is even});\\
m_{i}<L_{i},~~j_{i}\geq 3\text{~is~odd}.\end{cases}

(4.20)

Now we give the bound for
m_{i}
and
\frac{j_{i}-1}{2}
. By
(\ref{njzs})
, we have

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\frac{j_{i}-1}{2}\leq n_{i,2}+n_{i,4}+\cdots+n_{i,j_{i}-1}=L_{i}-m_{i}\leq\frac{j_{i}-1}{2}(b-2);\\
\frac{j_{i}+1}{2}\leq n_{i,1}+n_{i,3}+\cdots+n_{i,j_{i}}=m_{i};\\
n_{i,y}\in\mathbb{N}^{*},~~\leq n_{i,y}\leq b-2~(y~\text{is even});\\
m_{i}<L_{i},~~j_{i}\geq 3\text{~is~odd}.\end{cases}

Thus,

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\frac{j_{i}-1}{2}\leq\min\{L_{i}-m_{i},~m_{i}-1\},\\
L_{i}-m_{i}\leq\frac{j_{i}-1}{2}(b-2)\leq(m_{i}-1)(b-2)=(b-2)m_{i}-(b-2),\\
\end{cases}

it leads

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \begin{cases}\big\lceil\frac{L_{i}-m_{i}}{b-2}\big\rceil\leq\frac{j_{i}-1}{2}\leq\min\{L_{i}-m_{i},~m_{i}-1\},\\
\big\lceil\frac{L_{i}+(b-2)}{b-1}\big\rceil\leq m_{i}\leq L_{i}-1.\end{cases}\end{aligned}

(4.21)

Now by
(\ref{mm3})
, Lemma
2.5
and the definition of
N_{b}(\frac{j_{i}-1}{2},L_{i}-m_{i})
, we have

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} &\sum\limits_{(N_{i,1},N_{i,2},\ldots,N_{i,j_{i}})\in P_{i}(j_{i},m_{i})}1\\
=&\begin{cases}\binom{m_{i}-1}{\frac{j_{i}-1}{2}}N_{b}(\frac{j_{i}-1}{2},L_{i}-m_{i}),&\text{~if~}(\ref{mm3})~\text{holds};\\
0,&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}\end{aligned}

(4.22)

So far, by
(\ref{FFF})
-
(\ref{F1})
,
(\ref{FFP})
, we have

\displaystyle F_{b,d}(L_{1},\ldots,L_{I})=\begin{cases}A\Big(\sum\limits_{i=1}^{I}L_{i},d\Big),\hskip 9.24994pt&\text{if}~b=2;\\
f_{b}(L_{1},m_{1})\cdots f_{b}(L_{I},m_{I})A\Big(\sum\limits_{i=1}^{I}m_{i},d\Big),\hskip 9.24994pt&\text{if}~b\geq 3.\\
\end{cases}

where

\displaystyle f_{b}(L_{i},m_{i})=\sum\limits_{m_{i}=\big\lceil\frac{L_{i}+(b-2)}{b-1}\big\rceil}^{L_{I}}\sum\limits_{e_{i}=\big\lceil\frac{L_{i}-m_{i}}{b-2}\big\rceil}^{\min\{m_{i}-1,L_{i}-m_{i}\}}\binom{m_{i}-1}{e_{i}}N_{b}(e_{i},L_{i}-m_{i}),

and

\displaystyle A\Big(\sum\limits_{i=1}^{I}m_{i},d\Big)=\begin{cases}0,&\sum\limits_{i=1}^{I}m_{i}<d;\\
\sum\limits_{j=0}^{\sum\limits_{i=1}^{I}m_{i}-d}(-1)^{j}\binom{\sum\limits_{i=1}^{I}m_{i}}{j}\bigg(q^{\sum\limits_{i=1}^{I}m_{i}+1-d-j}-1\bigg),&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

\hfill\Box

Remark 4.1

In the proof of Theorem
3.2
, For the cases
b=2
and
b\geq 3
, we can see that the method for giving the value of
F_{b,d}(L_{1},\ldots,L_{I})
is different. In fact,
for
b=2
, the codeword
\mathbf{c}\in\mathcal{C}
which satisfy conditions
(\mathrm{F}1)
-
(\mathrm{F}2)
is equivalent to all the coordinates of
\mathbf{c}
are nonzero, i.e., the Hamming weight of
\mathbf{c}
is equal to the length, and then
F_{b,d}(L_{1},\ldots,L_{I})
can be obtained from the weight distribution of MDS code explictly. However, for the general case
b\geq 3
, it might have zero in the coordinates of codeword
\mathbf{c}\in\mathcal{C}
which satisfy conditions
(\mathrm{F}1)
-
(\mathrm{F}2)
. In order to give the value of
F_{b,d}(L_{1},\ldots,L_{I})
, we need to establish the connection between
F_{b,d}(L_{1},\ldots,L_{I})
and
N_{b}(e_{i},L_{i}-m_{i})
by using the shorten method, which leads the form of the value
F_{b,d}(L_{1},\ldots,L_{I})
is more complicated than that of the case
b=2
.

5
Conclusions and Further Study

In this paper, we obtain the
b
-weight distribution for MDS codes, which is a class
of MDS
b
-symbol codes. For an MDS code
\mathcal{C}
, by calculating the number of solutions for some equations and utilizing its shortened codes, we give the connection between the
b
-weight distribution and the number of codewords in shortened codes of
\mathcal{C}
with special shape. Furthermore, note thet shortened codes of
\mathcal{C}
are MDS codes, and then the number of these codewords with special shape are obtained by the shorten method. Our result generalizes Theorem
1
in
[
15
]
.

The key points of our method is that the Hamming weight distribution for an MDS code are uniquely determined by its parameters, and a shorten code of an MDS code is also an MDS code. However, these points are not always true for an MDS
b
-symbol code, but not an MDS code. Thus for an general MDS
b
-symbol code, we can not determine its
b
-weight distribution by using the method in this paper.

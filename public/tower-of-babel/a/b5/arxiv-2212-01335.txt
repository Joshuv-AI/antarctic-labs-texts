Graphs with equal Grundy domination and independence number
Gábor Bacsó, Boštjan Brešar, Kirsti Kuenzel, Douglas F. Rall

Abstract
The Grundy domination number, γ gr ​ ( G ) {\gamma_{\rm gr}}(G) , of a graph G G is the maximum length of a sequence ( v 1 , v 2 , … , v k ) (v_{1},v_{2},\ldots,v_{k}) of vertices in G G such that for every i ∈ { 2 , … , k } i\in\{2,\ldots,k\} , the closed neighborhood N ⁡ [ v i ] N[v_{i}] contains a vertex that does not belong to any closed neighborhood N ⁡ [ v j ] N[v_{j}] , where j < i j<i . It is well known that the Grundy domination number of any graph G G is greater than or equal to the upper domination number Γ ⁡ ( G ) \Gamma(G) , which is in turn greater than or equal to the independence number α ⁡ ( G ) \alpha(G) . In this paper, we initiate the study of the class of graphs G G with Γ ​ ( G ) = γ gr ​ ( G ) \Gamma(G)={\gamma_{\rm gr}}(G) and its subclass consisting of graphs G G with α ​ ( G ) = γ gr ​ ( G ) \alpha(G)={\gamma_{\rm gr}}(G) . We characterize the latter class of graphs among all twin-free connected graphs, provide a number of properties of these graphs, and prove that the hypercubes are members of this class. In addition, we give several necessary conditions for graphs G G with Γ ​ ( G ) = γ gr ​ ( G ) \Gamma(G)={\gamma_{\rm gr}}(G) and present large families of such graphs.

Introduction

Given a graph
G
, a set
D
is a
dominating set
if every vertex in
V(G)-D
has a neighbor in
D
. The
domination number
of
G
is defined as
\gamma(G)=\min\{|D|:\,D\textrm{ is a dominating set of }G\}
.
A vertex
x

dominates
a vertex
y
if
y
is a neighbor of
x
or
y=x
. Building a dominating set in
G
can be viewed as a process of adding vertices from
G
to
D
one by one so that each time a vertex
x
is added to
D
it dominates a vertex that was not dominated by vertices added to
D
before
x
. The size of a largest dominating set obtained by such a process is the
Grundy domination number
,
\gamma_{\rm gr}(G)
, of
G
. Grundy domination was introduced in
[
7
]

and studied by a number of authors, see
[
3
,
4
,
5
,
10
,
11
,
17
,
18
]
for a selection of papers on this parameter.

It follows from the definitions that
\gamma(G)\leq\gamma_{\rm gr}(G)
in any graph
G
, and often the Grundy domination number is much larger than the domination number of
G
. In the seminal paper from 2014
[
7
]
, the question of which graphs
G
enjoy
\gamma(G)=\gamma_{\rm gr}(G)
was considered. It was proved that
\gamma(G)=\gamma_{\rm gr}(G)=1
only in complete graphs and
\gamma(G)=\gamma_{\rm gr}(G)=2
precisely in graphs
G
whose complement
\overline{G}
is the disjoint union of one or more complete bipartite graphs. A few years later, Erey proved that the mentioned classes of graphs are the only connected graphs in which equality
\gamma(G)=\gamma_{\rm gr}(G)=2
holds
[
16
]
. We mention that an analogous question for two related parameters, the total domination number and the Grundy total domination number, was intensively studied
[
2
,
9
,
13
]
, yet a complete characterization seems to be elusive.

Since the complete characterization of the graphs
G
with
\gamma(G)=\gamma_{\rm gr}(G)
has been found, natural questions appear by involving graph parameters that lie between
\gamma
and
\gamma_{\rm gr}
. Two such important parameters (namely, the independence number
\alpha(G)
and the upper domination number
\Gamma(G)
) will be considered in this paper, and graphs
G
in which
\gamma_{\rm gr}(G)
is equal to one of these parameters will be studied.
In the next two subsections, we (1) give some necessary definitions and present basic observations that arise, and (2) formulate the main results of the paper and its organization.

1.1
Definitions and preliminaries

Let
G
be a finite, simple graph with vertex set
V(G)
and edge set
E(G)
. (When there is no chance of confusion we will
shorten this notation by setting

and

.) The order of

. For a vertex

open neighborhood of

is the set

defined by

. The
closed neighborhood

is

N(x)\cup\{x\}
. The open neighborhood of a set

is
N(A)=\cup_{a\in A}N(a)
and its

N[A]=N(A)\cup A
. Two vertices

and

of

are

if
N[u]=N[v]
, and we say

is
twin-free

if it has no twins. For

, the
private neighborhood
of

(with respect to
A
) is denoted by pn
[a,A]
and is defined
by pn
[a,A]=\{w\in V:\,N[w]\cap A=\{a\}\}
. Any vertex in pn
[a,A]
is called a
private neighbor
of

with respect to
A
.
The subgraph of

induced by
A
is denoted by
G\langle A\rangle
, and for a positive integer

, we will use

to
denote the set of positive integers not larger than
n
.

A set
A
of vertices is a
dominating
set of
G
if
N[A]=V
. A dominating set

is a minimal dominating set if
A-\{a\}
does not dominate

G
for every
a\in A
. (Equivalently, pn
[a,A]\neq\emptyset
for every
a\in A
.) Imposing this minimality condition while not requiring the set
to be dominating leads to the concept of irredundance. The set
A
is
irredundant
in
G
if pn
[a,A]\neq\emptyset
for every
a\in A
.
Note that a dominating set of
G
is a minimal dominating set only if it is a maximal irredundant set in
G
. The
domination number
of
G
, denoted
\gamma(G)
,
is the minimum cardinality of a dominating set of
G
. The
upper domination number
of
G
is the cardinality of a largest minimal dominating set
of
G
and is denoted
\Gamma(G)
. The minimum cardinality of a maximal irredundant set in

is the
irredundance number
of
G
and is denoted by

while the
upper irredundance number
,

, is the maximum cardinality of an irredundant set in
G
. The
independence number
of
G

is denoted
\alpha(G)
and is the maximum cardinality of a subset of vertices in
G
that are pairwise nonadjacent;
i(G)
denotes the
independent domination number

of
G
, which is the minimum cardinality of a dominating set that is also independent. Equivalently,
i(G)
is the minimum cardinality of a maximal
independent set. If
A
is a minimal dominating set of cardinality
\gamma(G)
(respectively,
\Gamma(G)
), then

will be called a
\gamma(G)
-set (respectively, a
\Gamma(G)
-set). Similar language will be used for each of these other four graphical invariants

ir,i,\alpha
and
IR
. If
I
is an independent set in
G
, then
x\in{\rm pn}[x,I]
for every
vertex

in
I
, which implies that

is irredundant. For any graph

, the following well known and much studied string of inequalities

ir(G)\leq\gamma(G)\leq i(G)\leq\alpha(G)\leq\Gamma(G)\leq IR(G)

(1)

follows from these definitions.

In what follows we will need the following result of Cockayne et al.

Theorem 1
.

(
[
12
, Theorem 5]
)

If
G
is a bipartite graph, then
\alpha(G)=\Gamma(G)=IR(G)
.

Let
S=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})
be a sequence of distinct vertices in
G
. We denote the length of
S
by
|S|
. The set
\{x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}\}
whose elements are the vertices in

is denoted by
\widehat{S}
. The sequence
S
is called a
closed neighborhood sequence
(or a
legal sequence
) if

N[x_{i+1}]-\bigcup_{j=1}^{i}N[x_{j}]\not=\emptyset

(2)

for each
i\in[n-1]
. That is,
(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})
is a closed neighborhood sequence if
x_{i+1}
has a private neighbor with respect to
\{x_{1},\ldots,x_{i+1}\}
for each
i\in[n-1]
. We will also say that
x_{i+1}

footprints
the vertices from
N[x_{i+1}]-\bigcup_{j=1}^{i}N[x_{j}]
with respect to

, and that
x_{i+1}
is the

of any

. If

is a legal sequence and
\widehat{S}
is a dominating set of

, then

is called a
dominating sequence
in
G
. It is clear that for a dominating sequence

each vertex in

has a unique footprinter in
\widehat{S}
. Hence, the function
f_{S}:V\to\widehat{S}
that maps each vertex to its footprinter is well defined.
Clearly, a shortest possible dominating sequence has length
\gamma(G)
. A longest possible dominating sequence in
G
is called a
Grundy dominating sequence
, and its length is the
Grundy domination number
of
G
, denoted
\gamma_{\rm gr}(G)
. Legal sequences were introduced in
[
7
]
as sequences of legal moves in the domination game played on a graph. If Staller is the only player making moves, then the length of the resulting dominating sequence is the Grundy domination number of the graph. See the book
[
8
]
for more on domination game and its relations with dominating sequences.

Note that any legal sequence in
G
can always be extended (if it is not already) to a dominating sequence in
G
. Thus, any longest legal sequence in
G
is a Grundy dominating sequence. A legal sequence that remains legal under any permutation of its vertices is said to be
commutative
.

The next observation follows immediately from the definitions.

Observation 1
.

If
S=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{k})
is a legal sequence in
G
such that
\widehat{S}
is irredundant, then
S
is commutative.

If
A
is an irredundant set in
G
, then any permutation of the vertices in
A
forms a legal sequence. If
A
is also a dominating set, then
this sequence is a dominating sequence. On the other hand, if

does not dominate

, then, as noted above, the sequence can be extended to a dominating sequence in
G
. This immediately implies that
IR(G)\leq\gamma_{\rm gr}(G)
, and thus for any
G
, we have the following extension of (
1
)

ir(G)\leq\gamma(G)\leq i(G)\leq\alpha(G)\leq\Gamma(G)\leq IR(G)\leq\gamma_{\rm gr}(G).

(3)

The join
G\oplus H
of graphs
G
and
H
is the graph obtained from the disjoint union of
G
and
H
by adding the edges from the set
\{gh:\,g\in V(G)\text{ and }h\in V(H)\}
.
Given two graphs

and

, the
Cartesian product

G\,\square\,H
of

and

is the graph with
V(G\,\square\,H)=V(G)\times V(H)
and
(g,h)(g^{\prime},h^{\prime})\in E(G\,\square\,H)
whenever (

and
hh^{\prime}\in E(H)
) or (

and

). Cartesian product is associative and commutative. The

-cube

, or the

.

1.2
Goal and brief outline of the paper

In this paper, we initiate the study of two natural classes of graphs that arise from involving three invariants in the above inequality chain (
3
). Notably, we consider the graphs
G
with
\Gamma(G)=\gamma_{\rm gr}(G)
, and the graphs
G
with
\alpha(G)=\gamma_{\rm gr}(G)
.

It is easy to see that if
u
is a twin in the graph
G
, then
\Gamma(G)=\Gamma(G-u)

and
\gamma_{\rm gr}(G)=\gamma_{\rm gr}(G-u)
. Therefore, we will assume that all the graphs under investigation are twin-free. Furthermore,
\Gamma(G)=\gamma_{\rm gr}(G)
if and only if
\Gamma(C)=\gamma_{\rm gr}(C)
, for each component
C
of
G
. With this in mind we let
{\cal F}
denote the class of twin-free, connected graphs
G
for which
\Gamma(G)=\gamma_{\rm gr}(G)
. In addition we
let
{\cal F_{\alpha}}
be the subclass of
{\cal F}
consisting of those
G\in{\cal F}
such that
\alpha(G)=\gamma_{\rm gr}(G)
. Note that
{\cal F_{\alpha}}
is a proper subclass of
{\cal F}
as can be seen by

\Gamma(K_{n}\,\square\,K_{2})=n=\gamma_{\rm gr}(K_{n}\,\square\,K_{2})
and
\alpha(K_{n}\,\square\,K_{2})=2
.

Graphs in
{\cal F}
and
{\cal F_{\alpha}}
are in some sense very special, since
\gamma_{gr}(G)-\Gamma(G)
and
\gamma_{gr}(G)-\alpha(G)
can be arbitrarily large. Indeed, consider the following family of graphs.
For each positive integer
n
let
V(G_{n})=\{x_{1},\ldots,x_{n},y_{1},\ldots,y_{n},z_{1},\ldots,z_{n}\}
. Both of the sets
\{y_{1},\ldots,y_{n}\}
and

\{z_{1},\ldots,z_{n}\}
induce a complete subgraph in
G_{n}
and
\{x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}\}
is independent. The remaining edges of
G_{n}

are
x_{i}y_{i}
and
y_{i}z_{i}
for each
i\in[n]
.
Now,
\gamma_{gr}(G_{n})=2n
, since
S=(x_{1},x_{2},\ldots,x_{n},y_{1},y_{2},\ldots,y_{n})
is a Grundy dominating set. On the other hand,
\Gamma(G_{n})=n+1
and
\alpha(G_{n})=n+1
.

In Section
2
we consider the class
{\cal F}
of graphs
G
with
\Gamma(G)=\gamma_{\rm gr}(G)
. Note that any minimal dominating set
D
of size
\Gamma(G)
gives rise to the partition of
G
into vertices in
D
, the private neighbor sets for all vertices in
D
, and the remaining vertices (which are not in
D
and have at least two neighbors in
D
). We present several necessary conditions that a graph in
{\cal F}
must possess, which are expressed in terms of the private neighborhoods of vertices in a minimal dominating set of size
\Gamma(G)
. While these conditions do not necessarily give rise to a characterization of graphs in
{\cal F}
, in Section
3
we present several families of graphs that belong to
{\cal F}
or even to
{\cal F_{\alpha}}
. We prove that the operation of join preserves the property of a graph being in
{\cal F}
, and provide necessary conditions on graphs
G
and
H
whose Cartesian product
G\,\square\,H
belongs to
{\cal F_{\alpha}}
. In addition, we use some connections with linear algebra to prove that all hypercubes belong to
{\cal F_{\alpha}}
. Section
4
is about graphs in
{\cal F_{\alpha}}
and is the most extensive one. We prove several necessary and sufficient conditions that a triangle-free graph in
{\cal F_{\alpha}}
must possess. Most of these conditions are of structural nature and can be expressed as properties that are related to a maximum independent set of a graph. In particular, they lead to a characterization of bipartite graphs in
{\cal F_{\alpha}}
whose girth is at least
6
. Finally, in Theorem
20
we give our main result, which is a characterization of graphs in
{\cal F_{\alpha}}
among all graphs. The characterization is not structural, since it relies on specific properties that any legal closed neighborhood sequence must possess. Nevertheless, it implies a characterization of
n
-crossed prisms that belong to
{\cal F_{\alpha}}
. We conclude the paper with several remarks and open problems.

2
The Class
{\cal F}

In this section, we derive a number of properties that hold for any
\Gamma(G)
-set if
G\in{\cal F}
. First we see that the class
{\cal F}

coincides with the class of twin-free, connected graphs whose upper irredundance number equals its Grundy domination number.

Lemma 2
.

If
G
is any twin-free, connected graph and
IR(G)=\gamma_{\rm gr}(G)
, then
G\in{\cal F}
.

Proof.

Suppose
IR(G)=n
and let
S=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})
be a legal sequence formed from a
IR(G)
-set
\widehat{S}
. Since
|S|=IR(G)=\gamma_{\rm gr}(G)
by assumption,
it follows that
\widehat{S}
is a dominating set (for otherwise
S
could be extended to a dominating sequence). Also,
\widehat{S}
is a minimal dominating set since
\widehat{S}
is irredundant. Now we get

\Gamma(G)\geq|\widehat{S}|=\gamma_{\rm gr}(G)=IR(G)\geq\Gamma(G)\,,

and thus
\Gamma(G)=\gamma_{\rm gr}(G)
.
∎

Lemma 3
.

Let
G\in{\cal F}
. If
D
is any
\Gamma(G)
-set, then
pn
[u,D]
induces a complete subgraph of

for every
u\in D
.

Proof.

Let
D=\{x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}\}
be a minimal dominating set of cardinality
\Gamma(G)
and suppose that
v_{1}
and
v_{2}
are distinct vertices in
pn
[x_{1},D]
. The sequence
(x_{1},x_{2},\ldots,x_{n})
is a Grundy dominating sequence. If
v_{1}v_{2}\notin E
, then
(x_{2},x_{3},\ldots,x_{n},v_{1},v_{2})
is
a legal sequence of length

since
v_{1}
footprints itself and
v_{2}
footprints itself. This contradiction implies that
G\langle{\rm pn}[x_{1},D]\rangle
is
a complete subgraph. Since any permutation of

is a legal sequence, the lemma follows.
∎

Corollary 4

If
G
is a triangle-free graph in
{\cal F}
and

is a
\Gamma(G)
-set, then

In particular, this corollary holds for bipartite graphs in

. Note that if

If

,
x_{k}
footprints the vertices in
P_{k}
, while
v
footprints vertices in
P_{j}
. Finally,
x_{j}
footprints itself, since it is not adjacent to
x_{k}
, for any
k\in I_{v}
. In the end, one can add the remaining vertices of
D
to the sequence, each of which footprints its private neighborhood. The resulting sequence is legal of length
n+1
, a contradiction to
G\in{\cal F}
.

For the proof of (iii) let
w\in X
and
i\in[n]
such that
x_{i}x_{j}\notin E
for all
j\in I_{w}
, and assume that
wx_{i}\notin E
while
i\in I_{w}
.
Consider the sequence, which starts with the vertices from
\{x_{j}:\,j\in I_{w}\}-\{x_{i}\}
in any order, is followed by
(w,x_{i})
, and completed by the remaining vertices in
D
. This sequence is legal because each vertex of
D-\{x_{i}\}
footprints a vertex in
P
,
w
footprints vertices in
P_{i}
, and
x_{i}
footprints itself.
Statement (iv) is clear.
∎

3
Examples and constructions of graphs in
{\cal F}

In this section, we present some families of graphs that belong to
{\cal F}
or
{\cal F_{\alpha}}
as well as give some constructions by which the class
{\cal F}
is preserved.
The following classes of graphs belong to
{\cal F}
, most of which also belong to
{\cal F_{\alpha}}
. (Note that some of the graphs in the following classes of graphs have twins, yet the equality
\Gamma(G)=\gamma_{\rm gr}(G)
holds for all graphs
G
in the mentioned classes.)

1.

Complete multipartite graphs,
G=K_{n_{1},\ldots,n_{k}}
, such that
n_{1}\geq\cdots\geq n_{k}
,
k\geq 2
, and
n_{k-1}\geq 2
. Note that
\alpha(G)=\Gamma(G)=\gamma_{\rm gr}(G)=n_{1}
. The special case where
n_{i}=2
for all
i\in[k]
are the so-called cocktail-party graphs, for which the authors of
[
7
]
proved that the Grundy domination number equals the domination number.

2.

Prisms over complete graphs,
G=K_{n}\,\square\,K_{2}
, for
n\geq 2
. Note that
\Gamma(G)=\gamma_{\rm gr}(G)=n
. Also,
2=\alpha(G)
, which is less than
\gamma_{\rm gr}(G)
unless
n=2
.

3.

Certain subclasses of Kneser graphs, as noted by Brešar, Kos and Torres in
[
10
]
. Given positive integers
n
and
r
such that
n\geq 2r
, the Kneser graph
K(n,r)
has as its vertex set the set of all
r
-subsets of
[n]
. Two vertices are adjacent in
K(n,r)
if and only if they are disjoint. A famous result by Erdös, Ko and Rado
[
14
]
is that
\alpha(K(n,r))=\binom{n-1}{r-1}
. Brešar et al. proved that
\gamma_{\rm gr}(K(n,2))=\alpha(K(n,2))

for
n\geq 6
and that for any
r\geq 3
there exists a positive integer
n_{r}
such that
\gamma_{\rm gr}(K(n,r))=\alpha(K(n,r))=\binom{n-1}{r-1}
, for
n\geq n_{r}
. Therefore,

K(n,2)\in{\cal F_{\alpha}}
if
n\geq 6
, and for
n\geq 3
,
K(n,r)\in{\cal F_{\alpha}}
for each
n
larger than some threshold value that depends on
r
.

4.

The class of (twin-free, connected) cographs. Recall that the class of
P_{4}
-free graphs (also known as
cographs
) are those graphs that can be constructed from
K_{1}
by repeatedly applying the graph operations of
taking disjoint unions or joins. It was proved in
[
7
]
that
\gamma_{\rm gr}(G)=\alpha(G)
, for any cograph
G
, implying that the class
{\cal F_{\alpha}}
contains the class of twin-free connected cographs.
Alternatively, one can prove this by using Lemma
9
below together with the obvious fact that the disjoint union of graphs from
{\cal F}
have equal independence and Grundy domination numbers.

In the following result we consider the join
G\oplus H
of graphs
G
and
H
. This is obtained from the disjoint union of
G
and
H
by adding the
edges from the set
\{gh:\,g\in V(G)\text{ and }h\in V(H)\}
.

Lemma 9
.

If
G_{1}
and
G_{2}
are graphs in
{\cal F}
, then
G_{1}\oplus G_{2}\in{\cal F}
.

Proof.

Let
G_{1}
and
G_{2}
be graphs in
{\cal F}
and assume without loss of generality that
\Gamma(G_{1})\geq\Gamma(G_{2})
. We claim that
\Gamma(G_{1}\oplus G_{2})=\gamma_{\rm gr}(G_{1}\oplus G_{2})=\Gamma(G_{1})
. Note that a minimal dominating set of either
G_{1}
or
G_{2}
is a minimal dominating set of their join. Furthermore, if
A
is any subset of
V(G_{1}\oplus G_{2})
and
A
contains a vertex from each of
G_{1}
and
G_{2}
, then
A
dominates
G_{1}\oplus G_{2}
. We infer that
\Gamma(G_{1}\oplus G_{2})\geq\Gamma(G_{1})
.
If
\Gamma(G_{1})=1
, then the join is a complete graph and the claim holds. Suppose now that
\Gamma(G_{1})\geq 2
. By the above we can select a largest minimal dominating set
D
of
G_{1}\oplus G_{2}
to be a
\Gamma(G_{1})
-set. Any permutation of the vertices of
D
is a dominating sequence of
G_{1}\oplus G_{2}
. Any sequence of vertices from

V(G_{1}\oplus G_{2})
of length more than
|D|
is not a legal sequence since
\gamma_{\rm gr}(G_{1})=|D|
and since a sequence that contains at least one vertex from each of
G_{1}
and
G_{2}

cannot be extended to a legal sequence.
∎

It seems natural to investigate whether there exist nontrivial Cartesian products in
{\cal F}
or in
{\cal F_{\alpha}}
. We will make use of the following result from
[
4
]
.

Proposition 10
.

(
[
4
, Proposition 3]
)

For any two graphs
G
and
H
,

\gamma_{\rm gr}(G\,\square\,H)\geq\max\{\gamma_{\rm gr}(G)n(H),\gamma_{\rm gr}(H)n(G)\}\,.

We now prove a necessary condition for a Cartesian product to belong to
{\cal F_{\alpha}}
.

Lemma 11
.

If
G
and
H
are two graphs such that
G\,\square\,H\in{\cal F_{\alpha}}
, then both
G
and
H
are in
{\cal F_{\alpha}}
and
\frac{\alpha(G)}{n(G)}=\frac{\alpha(H)}{n(H)}
.

Proof.

Let
G
and
H
be graphs such that
G\,\square\,H\in{\cal F_{\alpha}}
. It is well-known that
\alpha(G\,\square\,H)\leq\min\{\alpha(G)n(H),\alpha(H)n(G)\}
.
By Proposition
10
we have
\gamma_{\rm gr}(G\,\square\,H)\geq\max\{\gamma_{\rm gr}(G)n(H),\gamma_{\rm gr}(H)n(G)\}
. Since
\gamma_{\rm gr}(G\,\square\,H)=\alpha(G\,\square\,H)
, it follows that

\max\{\gamma_{\rm gr}(G)n(H),\gamma_{\rm gr}(H)n(G)\}\leq\min\{\alpha(G)n(H),\alpha(H)n(G)\}\,.

We
infer the following.

\displaystyle\gamma_{\rm gr}(G)n(H)

\displaystyle\leq\alpha(G)n(H)

(4)

\displaystyle\gamma_{\rm gr}(G)n(H)

\displaystyle\leq\alpha(H)n(G)

(5)

\displaystyle\gamma_{\rm gr}(H)n(G)

\displaystyle\leq\alpha(G)n(H)

(6)

\displaystyle\gamma_{\rm gr}(H)n(G)

\displaystyle\leq\alpha(H)n(G)

(7)

The first of these inequalities together with
\alpha(G)\leq\gamma_{\rm gr}(G)
implies that
\gamma_{\rm gr}(G)=\alpha(G)
. Similarly, using the last
of these four inequalities we get
\gamma_{\rm gr}(H)=\alpha(H)
. Therefore,
\{G,H\}\subseteq{\cal F_{\alpha}}
. Finally, we use the second and the third of these inequalities
together with
\gamma_{\rm gr}(G)=\alpha(G)
and
\gamma_{\rm gr}(H)=\alpha(H)
to conclude
that
\frac{\alpha(G)}{n(G)}=\frac{\alpha(H)}{n(H)}
.
∎

By Lemma
11
, all but one book graph, or graphs of the form
K_{1,m}\,\square\,K_{2}
, are not in
{\cal F_{\alpha}}
, since
\frac{\alpha(K_{2})}{n(K_{2})}=\frac{1}{2}<\frac{\alpha(K_{1,m})}{n(K_{1,m})}
when
m>1
. Since
P_{n}\in{\cal F_{\alpha}}
if and only if
n\in[3]
, one can use Lemma
11
together with the fact
that
\alpha(P_{3}\,\square\,P_{3})=5
and
\gamma_{\rm gr}(P_{3}\,\square\,P_{3})=6
to see that
P_{2}\,\square\,P_{2}
is the only grid graph with two nontrivial factors that is in
{\cal F_{\alpha}}
.
The
3
-dimensional hypercube
Q_{3}=C_{4}\,\square\,K_{2}
is an example of a (nontrivial) Cartesian product that belongs to
{\cal F_{\alpha}}
. In addition, all hypercubes belong to
{\cal F_{\alpha}}
, which we will prove by using some connections with linear algebra.

Let
G
be a graph of order
n
, and without loss of generality denote its vertex set by
[n]
. Let
{\cal S}(G)
be the family of all
n\times n
real symmetric matrices whose
i,j
-entry, where
i\neq j
, is non-zero if and only if
ij\in E(G)
. Note that there are no restrictions on the diagonal entries.
Minimum rank
of
G
is defined as
{\rm mr}(G)=\min\{{\rm rank}(A):\,A\in{\cal S}(G)\}
.

In
[
5
]
, a close connection was established between a variation of the Grundy domination number, called the Z-Grundy domination number, and the zero forcing number, the concept introduced in
[
1
]
and studied in a number of papers both by graph theorists and linear algebraists. Lin continued the investigation from
[
5
]
, and among other results found a similar relation between the Grundy domination number of a graph and the so-called loop zero forcing number. The latter concept is in turn related to a version of a minimum rank of a graph, which is defined as follows.

Let
{\cal S}_{\dot{\ell}}(G)
denote the set of all matrices in
{\cal S}(G)
whose all diagonal entries are non-zero. Then,
{\rm mr}_{\dot{\ell}}(G)=\min\{{\rm rank}(A):\,A\in{\cal S}_{\dot{\ell}}(G)\}
. Lin proved that
\gamma_{\rm gr}(G)\leq{\rm mr}_{\dot{\ell}}(G)
holds for every graph
G
, which together with (
3
) yields

\alpha(G)\leq\gamma_{\rm gr}(G)\leq{\rm mr}_{\dot{\ell}}(G),

for any graph
G
. Now, let
G
be the hypercube
Q_{d}
, where
d
is a positive integer. Clearly,
\alpha(Q_{d})=2^{d-1}
, which gives
2^{d-1}\leq\gamma_{\rm gr}(Q_{d})
. For the reversed inequality we invoke a result of Huang, Chang and Yeh from
[
15
, Theorem 10]
, where in the proof a matrix
B_{d}
appears, which belongs to
{\cal S}(Q_{d})
. In addition, it is easy to see that diagonal entries of
B_{d}
are non-zero, which implies
B_{d}\in{\cal S}_{\dot{\ell}}(Q_{d})
. It is proved in
[
15
]
that
{\rm rank}(B_{d})=2^{d-1}
, which yields
{\rm mr}_{\dot{\ell}}(Q_{d})\leq 2^{d-1}
, hence
\gamma_{\rm gr}(Q_{d})\leq 2^{d-1}
. We thus infer the following result.

Proposition 12
.

Hypercubes belong to
{\cal F_{\alpha}}
. More precisely,
\gamma_{\rm gr}(Q_{d})=\alpha(Q_{d})=2^{d-1}
for all positive integers
d
.

This result is an improvement of the result from
[
1
]
that the zero-forcing number in hypercubes
Q_{d}
equals
2^{d-1}
.

4
The Class
{\cal F_{\alpha}}

In this section, we prove our main result, a characterization of the graphs that are in
{\cal F_{\alpha}}
. In the beginning of the section we focus on triangle-free graphs that are in
{\cal F_{\alpha}}
. Our first result shows that classifying triangle-free graphs in
{\cal F_{\alpha}}
reduces to classifying all bipartite graphs in
{\cal F_{\alpha}}
.

Proposition 13
.

If
G
is a triangle-free graph of order at least
3
and
G\in{\cal F_{\alpha}}
, then
G
is bipartite with
\alpha(G)=n(G)/2
or
G
is bipartite and has a unique
\alpha(G)
-set. In particular, if
A
is any
\alpha(G)
-set, then
V-A
is independent.

Proof.

Let
A=\{x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}\}
be an
\alpha(G)
-set. We first show that if
x,y\in V-A
such that
N(x)\cap N(y)\cap A=\emptyset
, then
xy\not\in E
. Suppose to the contrary that
\{x,y\}\subseteq V-A
such that
xy\in E
and
N(x)\cap N(y)\cap A=\emptyset
. Assume without loss of generality that
N(x)\cap A=\{x_{1},\ldots,x_{r}\}
and
N(y)\cap A=\{x_{r+1},\ldots,x_{t}\}
. The sequence
S=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})
is a Grundy dominating sequence. But now
S^{\prime}=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{r},x,x_{r+1},\ldots,x_{n})
is a legal sequence. (In
S^{\prime}
,
x
footprints
y
and
x_{i}
footprints
x_{i}
for every
i\in[n]
.) This is a contradiction and therefore no such pair
x,y\in V-A
exists.

Next, we show that
V-A
is indeed independent. Let
\{x,y\}\subseteq V-A
. If
N(x)\cap N(y)\cap A=\emptyset
, then
xy\notin E
from the above argument. On the other hand, if
N(x)\cap N(y)\cap A\neq\emptyset
, then
xy\notin E
since
G
is triangle-free.
In all of these cases
V-A
is an independent set. It follows that
G
is bipartite. Thus,
\alpha(G)\geq n(G)/2
.

Now, if
\alpha(G)=n(G)/2
, we are done, so let us assume that
\alpha(G)>n(G)/2
. We claim that there is only one
\alpha(G)
-set, and suppose to the contrary that
A
and
B
are distinct
\alpha(G)
-sets. Let
C=A\cap B
and let
D=V-(A\cup B)
. In addition, let
A_{1}=A-C
and
B_{1}=B-C
. Since
A\neq B
and

|A|=|B|=\alpha(G)>n(G)/2
, we infer that
C\neq\emptyset
and that neither of
A_{1}
nor
B_{1}
is empty. From the above argument, both of
A_{1}\cup D

and
B_{1}\cup D
are independent. Let
u
be any vertex in
C
and let
v
be any vertex in
A_{1}
. Since
N(C)\subseteq D
and
N(D)\subseteq C
there is no

u,v
-path in
G
, which contradicts the fact that
G
is connected. Therefore, if
\alpha(G)>n(G)/2
, then
G
has a unique
\alpha(G)
-set.
∎

Based on the above result, we spend the remainder of this section focusing on bipartite graphs. We next give two properties that help us determine when a bipartite graph is in
{\cal F_{\alpha}}
.

Property H:
If
A
is any
\alpha(G)
-set, then for every
W\subseteq V-A
with
|W|<|A|
, we have
|N(W)|\geq|W|+1
.

Proposition 14
.

If
G
is a bipartite graph of order at least
3
and
G\in{\cal F_{\alpha}}
, then
G
satisfies Property H.

Proof.

Assuming that the statement is false, let
A
be an
\alpha(G)
-set, and

W=\{w_{1},\ldots,w_{k}\}
be a subset of
V-A
with
|W|<|A|
and
|N(W)|\leq|W|
. Since
G
is connected, there exists
x\in N(W)
having a neighbor
y\notin W
. Letting
A-N(W)=\{x_{1},\ldots,x_{\ell}\}
, note that
S=(w_{1},\ldots,w_{k},x,x_{1},\ldots,x_{\ell})
is a legal sequence, since
x
footprints
y
while every other vertex in
S
footprints itself (note that
W
is independent by Proposition
13
). Since
|\widehat{S}|\geq|A|+1
, this contradicts the assumption that
G\in{\cal F_{\alpha}}
.
∎

Property T:
If
A
is any
\alpha(G)
-set and
w\in V-A
, then for each
u\in N(w)\cap A
, we have
N(u)\subseteq N((N(w)\cap A)-\{u\})
.

Proposition 15
.

If
G
is a triangle-free graph of order at least
3
and
G\in{\cal F_{\alpha}}
, then
G
satisfies Property T.

Proof.

Let
A=\{x_{1},\dots,x_{n}\}
be an
\alpha(G)
-set and let
w\in V-A
. By Proposition
13
,
G
is bipartite and
V-A
is independent.
Reindexing if necessary, we may assume
N(w)=N(w)\cap A=\{x_{1},\dots,x_{k}\}
. Suppose for some
i\in[k]
that

N(x_{i})\not\subseteq N(N(w)-\{x_{i}\}).

That is, there exists
y\in N(x_{i})
such that
\displaystyle y\not\in\bigcup_{j\neq i,j\in[k]}N(x_{j})
. Note that

S=(x_{1},\dots,x_{i-1},x_{i+1},\dots,x_{k},w,x_{i},x_{k+1},\dots,x_{n})

is a legal sequence since
x_{j}
footprints itself for
j\in[n]-\{i\}
,
w
footprints
x_{i}
, and
x_{i}
footprints
y
. However, this is a contradiction.
Therefore no such
x_{i}
exists, and
G
satisfies Property T.
∎

Lemma 16
.

If
G
is a bipartite graph of order at least
3
in
{\cal F_{\alpha}}
, and
A
is any
\alpha(G)
-set, then
|N(x)\cap N(y)\cap A|\neq 1
for every pair
x,y\in V-A
.

Proof.

By Proposition
15
,
G
satisfies Property T. By Corollary
7
,
|N(u)\cap A|\geq 2
for every
u\in V-A
. Let
\{x,y\}\subseteq V-A
and suppose to the contrary that
N(x)\cap N(y)\cap A=\{z\}
. It follows that
y\in N(z)
and
y\not\in N((N(x)\cap A)-\{z\})
, which violates Property T.
∎

By Corollary
7
and Lemma
16
, we can classify all bipartite graphs in
{\cal F}
of girth
6
or more.

Theorem 17
.

A bipartite graph
G
with girth at least
6
is in
{\cal F}
if and only if
G=K_{1}
or
G
is a star
K_{1,r}
, where
r\geq 1
.

Proof.

It is easy to see that
K_{1}
and all stars belong to
{\cal F_{\alpha}}
. For the converse let
G
be a bipartite graph in the class
{\cal F}
having order
at least
3
and girth at least
6
. Since
G
is bipartite, it follows from Theorem
1
that
\alpha(G)=\Gamma(G)
. Thus,

G\in{\cal F}
if and only if
G\in{\cal F_{\alpha}}
.
Let
A
be any
\alpha(G)
-set. Suppose that
|V-A|\geq 2
. By Proposition
13
,
V-A
is independent.
Since
G
is connected, there exists a pair of vertices
x
and
y
that belong to
V-A
such that
N(x)\cap N(y)\cap A=N(x)\cap N(y)\neq\emptyset
. By
Lemma

, we infer that
|N(x)\cap N(y)\cap A|\geq 2
, which implies that
G
contains a
4
-cycle. This is a contradiction, and thus

|A|=n-1
, which means that

∎

Proposition 18
.

Let
G
be a connected bipartite graph of order
5
or more, and let
A
be an
\alpha(G)
-set. If there exist distinct vertices
x
and
y
in
V-A

and distinct vertices

and
v
in
A
such that
N(x)\cap A=\{u,v\}=N(y)\cap A
, then
G\not\in{\cal F_{\alpha}}
.

Proof.

Suppose to the contrary that
G\in{\cal F_{\alpha}}
. Note that
S=(x_{1},x_{2},\dots,x_{k})
is a legal sequence where
\widehat{S}=A
. Moreover, reindexing if necessary, we may assume
N(x)\cap A=\{x_{1},x_{2}\}=N(y)\cap A
. Note that if
N(\{x_{1},x_{2}\})=\{x,y\}
, then
G=C_{4}
. Therefore, we may assume there exists
z\in N(x_{1})-\{x,y\}
.

Suppose first that
N(x_{2})\subseteq N(x_{1})
. If there exists
w\in N(x_{1})-N(x_{2})
, then
S^{\prime}=(x_{2},x,x_{1},x_{3},x_{4},\dots,x_{k})
is a legal sequence as
x_{i}
footprints itself for
2\leq i\leq k
,
x_{1}
footprints
w
, and
x
footprints
x_{1}
. Thus, this case cannot occur and we may assume
N(x_{1})=N(x_{2})
. However, now
S^{\prime\prime}=(x,y,x_{1},x_{3},x_{4},\dots,x_{k})
is a legal sequence since each vertex of
S^{\prime\prime}
other than
x_{1}
footprints itself and
x_{1}
footprints
z
. Therefore, we may assume
N(x_{2})\not\subseteq N(x_{1})
. Let
t\in N(x_{2})-N(x_{1})
and consider
T=(x_{1},x,x_{2},x_{3},\dots,x_{k})
. Note that
T
is a legal sequence as each vertex of
T
other than
x
and
x_{2}
footprint themselves,
x
footprints
x_{2}
, and
x_{2}
footprints
t
.

In each case, we have a contradiction. Thus,
G\not\in\mathcal{F}_{\alpha}
.
∎

We next present a family of bipartite graphs that shows Property H and T alone are not sufficient to guarantee that a bipartite graph is in
{\cal F_{\alpha}}
. For each positive
integer
n
at least
3
, we construct a bipartite graph
G_{n}
. The set of vertices of

consists of two partite sets

and

. The vertices
x
and
y
are adjacent to every vertex of

, and for every

, the vertex

is adjacent to

and
w_{i}
, while
a_{i,2}
and
a_{i,3}
are adjacent only to

. Now,

is the unique
\alpha
-set of

with

, and

satisfies Properties H and T. On the other hand, the sequence
S=(a_{1,3},a_{1,2},u_{1},a_{1,1},a_{2,3},a_{2,2},u_{2},a_{2,1},\ldots,a_{n,3},a_{n,2},u_{n},a_{n,1})
is a closed neighborhood sequence, since

and
a_{i,2}
footprint themselves,

footprints
a_{i,1}
, and
a_{i,1}
footprints

, for all

. Since

, this implies that

.

We next consider the following additional property, which can be viewed as a generalization of Property T.

Property
\rm{T}^{*}
:
If
A
is any
\alpha(G)
-set and
W\subseteq V-A
, then for every
U\subseteq N(W)\cap A
with
|U|=|W|
we have
N(U)\subseteq N((N(W)\cap A)-U)
.

To see that Property
\rm{T}^{*}
is a generalization of Property T, note that the latter is obtained from Property
\rm{T}^{*}
by letting
W=\{w\}
and
U=\{u\}
.

Proposition 19
.

Let
G
be a connected bipartite graph such that for any
\alpha(G)
-set
A
, the set
V-A
is independent. If
G
satisfies Properties H and
\rm{T}^{*}
, then
G\in{\cal F_{\alpha}}
.

Proof.

Let
G
be a bipartite graph in which Properties H and
\rm{T}^{*}
hold (for every
\alpha
-set of
G
). Let
A
be an
\alpha(G)
-set. Suppose that
S
is a closed neighborhood sequence of
G
that involves some vertices in
V-A
, and let
w_{1},\ldots,w_{k}
be these vertices in the order that they appear in
S
. We may set

S=(a_{1},\ldots,a_{i_{1}},w_{1},a_{i_{1}+1},\ldots,a_{i_{2}},w_{2},\ldots,a_{i_{k}},w_{k},a_{i_{k}+1},\ldots,a_{m}),

where each of
i_{1}=0
and
i_{k}=m
is also possible (in the first case
w_{1}
is the first vertex of
S
, and in the second case
w_{k}
ends
S
), and
a_{r}\in A

.

We claim that for each

,

|(N(w_{1})\cup\cdots\cup N(w_{p}))-\{a_{1},\ldots,a_{i_{p}}\}|\geq p.

(8)

First, we prove this for
p\in\{1,2\}
and then use induction. For
p=1
, suppose to the contrary, that
|N(w_{1})-\{a_{1},\ldots,a_{i_{1}}\}|=0
. This implies that every vertex in
N[w_{1}]
is dominated by
\{a_{1},\ldots,a_{i_{1}}\}
. Hence,
w_{1}
footprints no vertex, a contradiction. For
p=2
, suppose to the contrary that
|(N(w_{1})\cup N(w_{2}))-\{a_{1},\dots,a_{i_{2}}\}|\leq 1
. Note that we may assume
|(N(w_{1})\cup N(w_{2}))-\{a_{1},\dots,a_{i_{2}}\}|=1
for otherwise
w_{2}
does not footprint a vertex. Let
(N(w_{1})\cup N(w_{2}))-\{a_{1},\dots,a_{i_{2}}\}=\{a\}
. By Property H,
|N(w_{2})|\geq 2
and therefore some neighbor of
w_{2}
is in
\{a_{1},\dots,a_{i_{2}}\}
. Thus,
w_{2}
footprints only
a

aw_{1}\not\in E
. It follows that
N(w_{1})\subseteq\{a_{1},\dots,a_{i_{2}}\}
. Let
x

N[w_{1}]
to appear in
(a_{1},\dots,a_{i_{1}},w_{1},a_{i_{1}+1},\dots,a_{i_{2}})
. It is clear that
x\neq w_{1}
, for otherwise
w_{1}
does not footprint a vertex. Hence,
x\in A
, and so by Property
\rm{T}^{*}
,
N(x)\subseteq N(N(w_{1})-\{x\})
. We derive that

does not footprint a vertex, a contradiction.

Now, assume that for some

|(N(w_{1})\cup\cdots\cup N(w_{j-1}))-\{a_{1},\ldots,a_{i_{j-1}}\}|\geq j-1\,.

We claim that also

|(N(w_{1})\cup\cdots\cup N(w_{j-1}))-\{a_{1},\ldots,a_{i_{j}}\}|\geq j-1.

(9)

Suppose this is not the case, and let
t\in\{i_{j-1}+1,\ldots,i_{j}\}
be the smallest index such that

|(N(w_{1})\cup\cdots\cup N(w_{j-1}))-\{a_{1},\ldots,a_{t}\}|=j-2\,.

Let
w\in V-A
be a vertex footprinted by
a_{t}
. Let
W=\{w_{1},\ldots,w_{j-1}\}
and let

U=(N(w_{1})\cup\cdots\cup N(w_{j-1}))-\{a_{1},\ldots,a_{t-1}\}
. Then,
|U|=j-1=|W|
,
w\in N(U)
, while
w\notin N(N(W)-U)
, which is a contradiction to Property
\rm{T}^{*}
, and (
9
) is proved.

Since
S
is a closed neighborhood sequence,
w_{j}
footprints at least one vertex. We claim that
w_{j}
footprints a vertex other than itself. If this were not
the case, then
N[w_{j}]-N[\widehat{S^{\prime}}]=\{w_{j}\}
, where
S^{\prime}
is the (leading) subsequence of

given by

S^{\prime}=(a_{1},\ldots,a_{i_{1}},w_{1},a_{i_{1}+1},\ldots,a_{i_{2}},w_{2},\ldots,a_{i_{j}})
.
However, this is not possible since
\widehat{S^{\prime}}

. Thus,
w_{j}
footprints some vertex

It is clear that

, which implies, combined with (

) that

|(N(w_{1})\cup\cdots\cup N(w_{j}))-\{a_{1},\ldots,a_{i_{j}}\}|\geq j
. By induction we now have that

|(N(w_{1})\cup\cdots\cup N(w_{p}))-\{a_{1},\ldots,a_{i_{p}}\}|\geq p,\text{ for every }p\in[k]\,,

and so (
8
) is proved.
In particular,
|(N(w_{1})\cup\cdots\cup N(w_{k}))-\{a_{1},\ldots,a_{i_{k}}\}|\geq k.

We claim that

|(N(w_{1})\cup\cdots\cup N(w_{k}))-\{a_{1},\ldots,a_{m}\}|\geq k.

(10)

Note that whenever a vertex in
(N(w_{1})\cup\cdots\cup N(w_{k}))\cap\{a_{i_{k}+1},\ldots,a_{m}\}
is added to
S
, it does not footprint itself.
Suppose that
|(N(w_{1})\cup\cdots\cup N(w_{k}))-\{a_{1},\ldots,a_{m}\}|<k
, and let
a_{t}\in(N(w_{1})\cup\cdots\cup N(w_{k}))\cap\{a_{i_{k}+1},\ldots,a_{m}\}
be the vertex with the smallest index

such that

|(N(w_{1})\cup\cdots\cup N(w_{k}))-\{a_{1},\ldots,a_{t}\}|=k-1
. Let
w\in V-A
be a vertex footprinted by

. Now, setting
W=\{w_{1},\ldots,w_{k}\}
, and

, and

, while

. This is a contradiction with Property

, hence (

Thus

and so

.

We point out that Property H and Property
\rm{T}^{*}
are structural properties. Although we were not able to find structural properties that are necessary and sufficient to guarantee that a graph is in
{\cal F_{\alpha}}
, we are able to show that the following property based on checking all legal sequences of
G
is necessary and sufficient to guarantee that a graph is in
{\cal F_{\alpha}}
.

Property U:
Let
A
be any
\alpha(G)
-set. If
S
is a legal sequence and
W=\widehat{S}\cap(V-A)
, then
|(N(W)\cap A)-\widehat{S}|\geq|W|
.

Theorem 20
.

If
G
is a connected graph, then
G\in{\cal F_{\alpha}}
if and only if
G
satisfies Property U.

Proof.

Let
S=(x_{1},\dots,x_{m})
be a Grundy sequence of
G
, let
A
be any
\alpha(G)
-set, and let
B=V-A
.
We first show that if
G
satisfies Property U, then
G\in{\cal F_{\alpha}}
. We let
W=B\cap\widehat{S}
and
(N(W)\cap A)\cap\widehat{S}=X
. Let
A^{\prime}=A-N(W)
. Suppose some
a^{\prime}\in A^{\prime}
is not in
\widehat{S}
. Then
S^{\prime}=(x_{1},\dots,x_{m},a^{\prime})
is a longer legal sequence, which is a contradiction. Hence,
A^{\prime}\subseteq\widehat{S}
. By Property U,
|(N(W)\cap A)-\widehat{S}|\geq|W|
and therefore

|\widehat{S}|=|B\cap\widehat{S}|+|A\cap\widehat{S}|=|W|+|X|+|A^{\prime}|\leq|(N(W)\cap A)-\widehat{S}|+|X|+|A^{\prime}|=|A|.

On the other hand,
|\widehat{S}|\geq|A|
and so it must be that
|\widehat{S}|=|A|
and
G\in{\cal F_{\alpha}}
.

For the converse, suppose
G
does not satisfy Property U. Thus, there exists a legal sequence
S=(x_{1},\dots,x_{m})
where
W=B\cap\widehat{S}
and
|(N(W)\cap A)-\widehat{S}|<|W|
. Let
A^{\prime}=A-N(W)
. Let
A^{\prime\prime}=\{a\in A^{\prime}\,:\,a\notin\widehat{S}\}
. Write
A^{\prime\prime}=\{y_{1},\dots,y_{\ell}\}
. Then we can extend
S
to the legal sequence
S^{\prime}=(x_{1},\dots,x_{m},y_{1},\dots,y_{\ell})
. Therefore,

\displaystyle\gamma_{\rm gr}(G)\geq|\widehat{S^{\prime}}|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle|W|+|(N(W)\cap A)\cap\widehat{S}|+|A^{\prime}|

\displaystyle>

\displaystyle|(N(W)\cap A)-\widehat{S}|+|(N(W)\cap A)\cap\widehat{S}|+|A-N(W)|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle|A|,

and we conclude that
G\notin{\cal F_{\alpha}}
.
∎

By Theorem

5
Concluding remarks

In this paper, we initiated the study of graphs
G
in which
\gamma_{\rm gr}(G)=\Gamma(G)
, or
\gamma_{\rm gr}(G)=\alpha(G)
, respectively. Since the graphs
G
in which
\gamma_{\rm gr}(G)=\gamma(G)
have been completely characterized
[
7
,
16
]
, studying the mentioned two classes of graphs is the natural step forward.

We found several properties of graphs
G
in family
\cal F
of connected twin-free graphs with
\gamma_{\rm gr}(G)=\Gamma(G)
; the properties are related to the partition of a graph
G
derived from a
\Gamma(G)
-set
D
, which is formed by
D
, private neighborhoods of vertices in
D
, and the remainder of the graph.
It would be interesting to know if these properties together imply that the graph belongs to
\cal F
, which we formulate as the following problem.

Problem 1
.

Is the reverse direction in Proposition
8
also true? That is, do the properties (i)-(iv) together imply that a twin-free, connected graph is in
\cal F
?

Besides the class
\cal F
we also consider the class
{\cal F_{\alpha}}
consisting of connected, twin-free graphs with
\alpha(G)=\gamma_{\rm gr}(G)
.
The two classes of graphs are rather rich, which is reflected in a number of families that belong to one or both of the classes (for instance, we proved that a large family of Kneser graphs as well as all cographs and hypercubes satisfy
\alpha=\gamma_{\rm gr}
). In addition, several graph operations preserve the property of being in one of the two classes.

The most thorough investigation was given to the class
{\cal F_{\alpha}}
. We proved that triangle-free graphs in
{\cal F_{\alpha}}
are always bipartite graphs in which an
\alpha
-set is either unique or of the size half the order. We found two structural properties (called Property H and Property T) of bipartite graphs in
{\cal F_{\alpha}}
, but they turned out not to be sufficient for a graph to be in
{\cal F_{\alpha}}
. It would be interesting to investigate whether Property T could be strengthened in such a way that together with Property H it would yield a characterization of bipartite graphs in
{\cal F_{\alpha}}
. In particular, Propositions
14
,
15
and
19
lead us to the following question.

Problem 2
.

Is there a condition stronger than Property T but weaker than Property
\rm{T}^{*}
such that the connected bipartite graphs in
{\cal F_{\alpha}}
would be characterized by this condition and Property H?

Finally, a different kind of condition (called Property U) was established, which characterizes all graphs in
{\cal F_{\alpha}}
. The condition relies on certain connections between legal sequences and
\alpha
-sets in
G
, and as a result we could determine which of the graphs are in
{\cal F_{\alpha}}
within the class of
n
-crossed prisms. It will be interesting if one can use Property U to determine the graphs in
{\cal F_{\alpha}}
within some other natural class of graphs.

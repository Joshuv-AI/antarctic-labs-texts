Around a Farkas type Lemma
N. Dinh, M. A. Goberna, M. Volle

Abstract
The first two authors of this paper asserted in Lemma 4 of ”New Farkas-type constraint qualifications in convex infinite programming” (DOI: 10.1051/cocv:2007027) that a given reverse convex inequality is consequence of a given convex system satisfying the Farkas-Minkowski constraint qualification if and only if certain set depending on the data contains a particular point of the vertical axis. This paper identifies a hidden assumption in this reverse Farkas lemma which always holds in its applications to nontrivial optimization problems. Moreover, it shows that the statement remains valid when the Farkas-Minkowski constraint qualification fails by replacing the mentioned set by its closure. This hidden assumption is also characterized in terms of the data. Finally, the paper provides some applications to convex infinite systems and to convex infinite optimization problems.



Key words
Convex infinite systems; Farkas Lemma; Convex infinite
optimization

\partial f(\overline{x})+\sum_{j\in J}{{}\mathchoice{\mbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle\mathchar 277$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\textstyle\mathchar 277$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptstyle\mathchar 277$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptscriptstyle\mathchar 277$}}}_{j}\partial f_{j}(\overline{x})+N_{C}(\overline{x})\subset\partial\Big(f+\sum_{j\in J}{{}\mathchoice{\mbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle\mathchar 277$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\textstyle\mathchar 277$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptstyle\mathchar 277$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptscriptstyle\mathchar 277$}}}_{j}f_{j}+{{}\mathchoice{\mbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle\mathchar 270$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\textstyle\mathchar 270$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptstyle\mathchar 270$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptscriptstyle\mathchar 270$}}}_{C}\Big)(\overline{x}).

[\mathrm{(iii)}\Longrightarrow\mathrm{(i)}]
If
\mathrm{(iii)}
holds
then

\Big(f+\sum_{j\in J}{{}\mathchoice{\mbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle\mathchar 277$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\textstyle\mathchar 277$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptstyle\mathchar 277$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptscriptstyle\mathchar 277$}}}_{j}f_{j}+{{}\mathchoice{\mbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle\mathchar 270$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\textstyle\mathchar 270$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptstyle\mathchar 270$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptscriptstyle\mathchar 270$}}}_{C}\Big)(x)-\Big(f+\sum_{j\in J}{{}\mathchoice{\mbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle\mathchar 277$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\textstyle\mathchar 277$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptstyle\mathchar 277$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptscriptstyle\mathchar 277$}}}_{j}f_{j}+{{}\mathchoice{\mbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle\mathchar 270$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\textstyle\mathchar 270$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptstyle\mathchar 270$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptscriptstyle\mathchar 270$}}}_{C}\Big)(\overline{x})\geq\langle x^{\ast},x-\overline{x}\rangle,

or, equivalently (as
\sum_{j\in J}{{}\mathchoice{\mbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle\mathchar 277$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\textstyle\mathchar 277$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptstyle\mathchar 277$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptscriptstyle\mathchar 277$}}}_{j}f_{j}(\overline{x})=0
),

f(x)-\langle x^{\ast},x\rangle+\sum_{j\in J}{{}\mathchoice{\mbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle\mathchar 277$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\textstyle\mathchar 277$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptstyle\mathchar 277$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptscriptstyle\mathchar 277$}}}_{j}f_{j}(x)\geq f(\overline{x})-\langle x^{\ast},\overline{x}\rangle+\sum_{j\in J}{{}\mathchoice{\mbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle\mathchar 277$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\textstyle\mathchar 277$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptstyle\mathchar 277$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptscriptstyle\mathchar 277$}}}_{j}f_{j}(\overline{x})=f(\overline{x})-\langle x^{\ast},\overline{x}\rangle,\forall x\in C.

Thus, when
x\in A
we get

f(x)-\langle x^{\ast},x\rangle\geq f(\overline{x})-\langle x^{\ast},\overline{x}\rangle,

which means that
\overline{x}
is an optimal solution of
(P_{x^{\ast}})

and
\mathrm{(i)}
has been proved. The proof is complete.
\square

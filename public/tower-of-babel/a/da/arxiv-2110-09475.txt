Level of noises and long time behavior of the solution for space-time fractional SPDE in bounded domains
Jebessa B. Mijena, Erkan Nane, Alemayehu G. Negash

Abstract
In this paper we study the long time behavior of the solution to a certain class of space-time fractional stochastic equations with respect to the level λ \lambda of a noise and show how the choice of the order β ∈ ( 0 , 1 ) \beta\in(0,\,1) of the fractional time derivative affects the growth and decay behavior of their solution. We consider both the cases of white noise and colored noise. Our results extend the main results in Foondun [ 16 ] to fractional Laplacian as well as higher dimensional cases.



Keywords:
Space-time fractional SPDE, space-time white noise, space colored noise, moment bounds in bounded domains.

Introduction and Statement of the Main results

In 1855, Adolf Fick
[
11
]
developed his now-famous principle governing the transport of mass through diffusive media. In his second law, Fick showed that
u(t,\,\text{x})
obeys the classical diffusion equation
\partial_{t}u=D\partial_{xx}u
in the spatial dimension, which estimates how the concentration
u(t,\,\text{x})
of a diffusive substance varies with space and time, where
D
is the diffusion coefficient.
The space-time fractional diffusion equation which is obtained when integer-order derivative operators in space and time are replaced by fractional counterparts (in Caputo or Riemann-Liouville sense) has
been recently treated by several authors (see, for example, Mijena and Nane
[
20
]
, Saichev and Zaslavsky
[
28
]
, and Mainardi, Luchko, and Pagnini
[
19
]
). The typical form of the space-time fractional equation is
\partial_{t}^{\beta}u=-(-\Delta)^{\alpha/2}u
, where
\partial_{t}^{\beta}
is the Caputo fractional derivative with
\beta\in(0,\,1)
,
\alpha\in(0,\,2)
and
\Delta=\sum_{i=1}^{d}\partial_{x_{i}}^{2}
is the Laplacian. These equations can be used to model anomalous diffusion processes or diffusion processes in non-homogeneous media with random fractal structures (see, for instance, Meerschaert
et al.

[
22
]
, Meerschaert, Nane, and Vellaisamy
[
24
]
, Baeumer, Luks, and Meerschaert
[
4
]
, Chen, Kim and Kim
[
7
]
and the references therein). Since these space-time fractional equations depend on the fractional parameters
\beta
and
\alpha

[
9
]
the importance of the continuity of their solutions with respect to these parameters is discussed. For example, if the partial derivative in time
\partial_{t}
in the classical heat equation

is substituted with fractional derivatives

for

[

In
[
20

In this section we give some preliminary results, which are needed for the proofs of the main theorems.

Let
X_{t}
denote a symmetric stable process of index
\alpha\in(0,2)
in
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
and
B
be a regular bounded open subset of
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
. Let
X_{t}^{B}
denote the symmetric stable process killed upon exiting
B
. The following Hilbert-Schmidt expansion (see Davies
[
10
]
) holds for the probability density function
p_{B}
of
X_{t}^{B}
,

p_{B}(t,\,\text{x},\,\text{y}):=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}e^{-\mu_{n}t}\varphi_{n}(\text{x})\varphi_{n}(\text{y}),

(2.1)

for all
\text{x},\,\text{y}\in B,\,t>0,
where
\{\varphi_{n}\}_{n\geqslant 0}
is an orthonormal basis of
L^{2}(B)
, and
0<\mu_{1}<\mu_{2}\leqslant\mu_{3}\leqslant\cdots
is a sequence of positive real numbers such that, for every
n\geqslant 1
,
P_{t}^{B}\varphi_{n}=e^{-\mu_{n}t}\varphi_{n}
.

From Theorem 2.3 of Blumenthal and Getoor
[
5
]
and the proof of Theorem 5.1 of Chen, Meerschaert, and Nane
[
6
]
, we infer the following lemma:

Lemma 2.1
.

[
5
,
6
]

For any bounded open subset
B
of
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
, the system of eigenfunctions
\{\varphi_{n}\}_{n\geqslant 1}
and the corresponding eigenvalues
\{\mu_{n}\}_{n\geqslant 1}
satisfy the following:

a)

C_{1}n^{\frac{\alpha}{d}}\leqslant\mu_{n}\leqslant C_{2}n^{\frac{\alpha}{d}}
for every
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
,

b)

|\varphi_{n}(\mathrm{x})|\leqslant C\mu_{n}^{\frac{d}{2\alpha}}
for every
\mathrm{x}\in B
,

where
C
,
C_{1}
, and
C_{2}
are positive real numbers.

It is also known that

\displaystyle u_{t}(\mathrm{x})

\displaystyle:={\mathbb{E}}^{x}[u_{0}(X_{t}^{B})]

\displaystyle=\int_{B}p_{B}(t,\,\text{x},\,\text{y})\,u_{0}(y)\,{\rm d}y,

solves the heat equation
\partial_{t}u_{t}(\mathrm{x})=-(-\Delta)^{\alpha/2}u_{t}(\text{x})
defined on
B
with Dirichlet boundary condition and initial condition
u_{0}
.

Let
D=\{D_{r},r\geqslant 0\}
denote a
\beta
-stable subordinator and
E_{t}
to be the inverse of a stable subordinator of index
\beta\in(0,\,1)
. The process
X_{E_{t}}^{B}
is just a time-changed of the killed
\alpha
-symmetric stable process
X^{B}_{t}
and since
\beta\in(0,\,1)
,
X_{E_{t}}^{B}
moves more slowly than
X_{t}^{B}.
It is known that the density of the time changed process
X_{E_{t}}^{B}
is given by the
G^{(\beta)}(t,x)
. By conditioning, we have

\displaystyle G^{(\beta)}(t,x)=\int_{0}^{\infty}p_{B}(s,x)f_{t}(s)ds,

(2.2)

where

\displaystyle f_{t}(s)=t\beta^{-1}s^{-1-1/\beta}g_{\beta}(ts^{-1/\beta}),

(2.3)

where
g_{\beta}(.)
(Cf. Meerschaert and Straka
[
23
]

D_{1}
and is infinitely differentiable on the entire real line, with
g_{\beta}(u)=0
for
u\leqslant 0
; see Meerschaert and Scheffler
[
25
]
for more information about the inverse stable subordinator
E_{t}
.

The function
v_{t}(\text{x}):={\mathbb{E}}^{x}[u_{0}(X_{E_{t}}^{B})]
solves the space-time fractional equation

\left\{\begin{aligned} &\partial^{\beta}_{t}v_{t}(\text{x})=-(-\Delta)^{\alpha/2}v_{t}(\text{x})\;\;\text{for}\;\;\text{x}\in B\;\;\text{and}\;\;t>0\\
&v_{t}(\text{x})=0\quad\text{for}\;\;\text{x}\notin B,\end{aligned}\right.

(2.4)

with initial condition
u_{0}
(Chen, Meerschaert, and Nane
[
6
]
). Thus, we get the following representation of
v_{t}(x)

\displaystyle v_{t}(\text{x})

\displaystyle:={\mathbb{E}}^{\text{x}}[u_{0}(X_{E_{t}}^{B})]

\displaystyle=\int_{B}\int_{0}^{\infty}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}e^{-\mu_{n}s}\varphi_{n}(\text{x})\varphi_{n}(\text{y})f_{t}(s)\,u_{0}(\text{y})\,{\rm d}s{\rm d}\text{y}

\displaystyle=\int_{B}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}E_{\beta}(-\mu_{n}t^{\beta})\varphi_{n}(\text{x})\varphi_{n}(\text{y})u_{0}(\text{y}){\rm d}\text{y}

\displaystyle=\int_{B}G_{B}^{(\beta)}(t,\,\text{x},\,\text{y})u_{0}(\text{y})\,{\rm d}\text{y},

(2.5)

this follows since the Laplace transform of

is
\hat{f}_{t}(\lambda)=E_{\beta}(-\lambda t^{\beta})
where
E_{\beta}(x)=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{x^{k}}{\Gamma(1+\beta k)}
is the
Mittag-Leffler function and have the following property,

\frac{1}{1+\Gamma(1-\beta)x}\leqslant E_{\beta}(-x)\leqslant\frac{1}{1+\Gamma(1+\beta)^{-1}x}\ \ \ \text{for}\ x>0,

(2.6)

where
\Gamma(\cdot)
is the gamma function. Thus, we get the following expansion,

G_{B}^{(\beta)}(t,\,\text{x},\,\text{y}):=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}E_{\beta}(-\mu_{n}t^{\beta})\varphi_{n}(\text{x})\varphi_{n}(\text{y}).

(2.7)

Using (
2.3
) and a change of variable from (

) we obtain

\displaystyle G_{B}^{(\beta)}(t,\,\text{x},\,\text{y})

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}e^{-\mu_{n}(t/u)^{\beta}}\varphi_{n}(\text{x})\varphi_{n}(\text{y})g_{\beta}(u){\rm d}u

(2.8)

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}p_{B}(\left(\frac{t}{u}\right)^{\beta},\,\text{x},\,\text{y})g_{\beta}(u)\,{\rm d}u.

(2.9)

The following Lemma from Mijena and Nane
[

about the

-norm of the heat kernel which is a crucial ingredient for the existence and uniqueness of solution of equation (

Lemma 2.2

(Lemma 1 in
[

\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}[G_{t}^{(\beta)}(x)]^{2}dx=C^{*}t^{\frac{-\beta d}{\alpha}},

(2.10)

C^{*}=\frac{(\nu)^{-\frac{d}{\alpha}}2\pi^{\frac{d}{2}}}{\alpha\Gamma(\frac{d}{2})}\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{d}}\int_{0}^{\infty}z^{\frac{d}{\alpha-1}}({\mathbb{E}}_{\beta}(-z))^{2}dz.

Lemma 2.3
.

For
p\geqslant 2
and
\beta\in\left(\frac{1}{p},\,1\right)
, we have

\int_{0}^{t}E_{\beta}(-\mu_{n}(t-s)^{\beta})^{p}{\rm d}s\lesssim\mu_{n}^{-\frac{1}{\beta}},

(2.11)

for every
t>0
.

Proof.

Using the Mittag-Leffler function bounds given in (
2.6
), we get

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}E_{\beta}(-\mu_{n}(t-s)^{\beta})^{p}\,{\rm d}s

\displaystyle\leqslant C\,\int_{0}^{t}\left(1+\Gamma(1+\beta)^{-1}\mu_{n}(t-s)^{\beta}\right)^{-p}\,{\rm d}s

\displaystyle\leqslant C\,\int_{0}^{t}\left(1\wedge\left(\mu_{n}(t-s)^{\beta}\right)^{-p}\right)\,{\rm d}s,

(2.12)

where

1\wedge\left(\mu_{n}(t-s)^{\beta}\right)^{-p}=\begin{cases}1&\text{if }\left(\mu_{n}(t-s)^{\beta}\right)^{-p}>1,\\
\left(\mu_{n}(t-s)^{\beta}\right)^{-p}&\text{if }\left(\mu_{n}(t-s)^{\beta}\right)^{-p}\leqslant 1.\end{cases}

(2.13)

Now

\displaystyle\left(\mu_{n}(t-s)^{\beta}\right)^{-p}\leqslant 1

\displaystyle\implies\mu_{n}(t-s)^{\beta}\geqslant 1

\displaystyle\implies(t-s)^{\beta}\geqslant\frac{1}{\mu_{n}}

\displaystyle\implies t-s\geqslant\left(\frac{1}{\mu_{n}}\right)^{\frac{1}{\beta}}

\displaystyle\implies t-\mu_{n}^{-\frac{1}{\beta}}\geqslant s.

(2.14)

So, from (
2.13
) using (
2
) we obtain

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}E_{\beta}(-\mu_{n}(t-s)^{\beta})^{p}\,{\rm d}s

\displaystyle\leqslant C\,\int_{0}^{t}\left(1\wedge\left(\mu_{n}(t-s)^{\beta}\right)^{-p}\right)\,{\rm d}s

(2.15)

\displaystyle=C\,\left(\int_{0}^{t-\mu_{n}^{-\frac{1}{\beta}}}\left(\mu_{n}(t-s)^{\beta}\right)^{-p}{\rm d}s+\int_{t-\mu_{n}^{-\frac{1}{\beta}}}^{t}{\rm d}s\right)

\displaystyle=C\,\left(\frac{1}{\mu_{n}^{p}}\int_{0}^{t-\mu_{n}^{-\frac{1}{\beta}}}(t-s)^{-p\beta}{\rm d}s+\mu_{n}^{-\frac{1}{\beta}}\right)

\displaystyle=C\,\left(\frac{1}{\mu_{n}^{p}(1-p\beta)}\left(t^{-p\beta+1}-\left(\frac{1}{\mu_{n}}\right)^{-p+\frac{1}{\beta}}\right)+\mu_{n}^{-\frac{1}{\beta}}\right)

\displaystyle\leqslant C\,\left(\frac{\mu_{n}^{p-\frac{1}{\beta}}}{\mu_{n}^{p}(p\beta-1)}+\mu_{n}^{-\frac{1}{\beta}}\right)

\displaystyle\leqslant C\,\left(\frac{1}{p\beta-1}+1\right)\mu_{n}^{-\frac{1}{\beta}}\,

\displaystyle\lesssim\mu_{n}^{-\frac{1}{\beta}},

(2.16)

since
\frac{t^{-p\beta+1}}{1-p\beta}<0
.

∎

Lemma 2.4
.

For
p\geqslant 2
,

Lemma 2.5

For

,

The following continuity Lemma is crucial to justify the conclusion of Theorem

Lemma 2.6
.

Assume that
\{\varphi_{n}\}_{n\geqslant 1}
are uniformly bounded by a constant
C(B)
, depending on the geometrical characteristics of the domain
B
, i.e.,

C(B)=\sup_{n\geqslant 1,\text{x}\in B}\varphi_{n}(\text{x}).

Fix
t>0
, then for
d<\alpha
we have

\lim_{\gamma\rightarrow\beta}\sup_{x\in B}\left|(\mathcal{G}_{B}^{(\gamma)}u_{0})_{t}(x)-(\mathcal{G}_{B}^{(\beta)}u_{0})_{t}(x)\right|=0,

where

(\mathcal{G}_{B}^{(\gamma)}u_{0})_{t}(x):=\int_{B}G_{B}^{(\gamma)}(t,\,\text{x},\,\text{y})u_{0}(\text{y})\,{\rm d}\text{y}.

Proof.

Since the initial datum
u_{0}
and
G_{B}^{(\gamma)}(t,\,x,\,y)
are given to be bounded from above, it suffices to show that

\lim_{\gamma\rightarrow\beta}\sup_{x\in B}\left|G_{B}^{(\gamma)}(t,\,x,\,y)-G_{B}^{(\beta)}(t,\,x,\,y)\right|=0.

Now using the Laplace transform of the Mittag-Leffler function, for any
\theta>0
we have

\displaystyle\lim_{\gamma\rightarrow\beta}\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-\theta t}E_{\gamma}(-\mu_{n}t^{\gamma})\,{\rm d}t

\displaystyle=\lim_{\gamma\rightarrow\beta}\frac{\theta^{\gamma-1}}{\theta^{\gamma}+\mu_{n}}=\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-\theta t}E_{\beta}(-\mu_{n}t^{\beta})\,{\rm d}t.

This implies that

\displaystyle\lim_{\gamma\rightarrow\beta}E_{\gamma}(-\mu_{n}t^{\gamma})=E_{\beta}(-\mu_{n}t^{\beta}).

(2.21)

Now using the expansions for the heat kernel, we have

\displaystyle|G_{B}^{(\beta)}(t,\,x,\,y)-G_{B}^{(\gamma)}(t,\,x,\,y)|

\displaystyle\leqslant\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\left[|E_{\beta}(-\mu_{n}t^{\beta})-E_{\gamma}(-\mu_{n}t^{\gamma})||\varphi_{n}(\text{x})||\varphi_{n}(\text{y})|\right].

(2.22)

Taking limit as
\gamma\rightarrow\beta
on both sides of (
2.22
) we obtain the required result, since each terms in the summation can be bounded by a quantity independent of
\gamma
and
\beta
. This is due to the uniform boundedness of the eigenfunctions
\{\varphi_{n}\}_{n\geqslant 1}
and the bounds on the Mittag-Leffler function together with Lemma
2.1
(a), and summable as shown below.

For any
t>0
, then we obtain

\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\bigg[|E_{\beta}(-\mu_{n}t^{\beta})-E_{\gamma}(-\mu_{n}t^{\gamma})|

\displaystyle|\varphi_{n}(\text{x})||\varphi_{n}(\text{y})|\bigg]\leqslant[C(B)]^{2}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\left|E_{\beta}(-\mu_{n}t^{\beta})-E_{\gamma}(-\mu_{n}t^{\gamma})\right|

\displaystyle\leqslant[C(B)]^{2}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\left[\frac{1}{1+\Gamma(1+\beta)^{-1}\mu_{n}t^{\beta}}+\frac{1}{1+\Gamma(1+\gamma)^{-1}\mu_{n}t^{\gamma}}\right]

\displaystyle\leqslant[C(B)]^{2}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{\mu_{n}}\left(\frac{1}{t^{\beta}}+\frac{1}{t^{\gamma}}\right)

\displaystyle\leqslant 2[C(B)]^{2}\text{max}(1,1/t)\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}n^{-\frac{\alpha}{d}}<\infty,

(2.23)

where
C
is a constant positive real number.
∎

Lemma 2.7
.

If
\beta\in(\beta_{0},\beta_{1})
for some

and

\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-2\theta s/p}\frac{1}{s^{2\lambda\beta}}{\rm d}s\lesssim p^{1-2\lambda\beta_{0}}\left[\frac{\mathscr{C}_{\mu_{0}}^{2}\theta^{-2\mu_{0}}}{1-2\lambda-2\mu_{0}}+\frac{\mathscr{C}_{\mu_{1}}^{2}\theta^{-2\mu_{1}}}{2\lambda\beta_{0}+2\mu_{1}-1}\right],

where

(2.25)

where

for some

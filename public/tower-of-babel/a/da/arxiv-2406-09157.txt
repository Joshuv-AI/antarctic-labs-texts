Cong Xu 1, Qing-Hua Zhang 2, Shao-Ming Fei 1,3

Abstract
We present the uncertainty relations in terms of the symmetrized ρ \rho -absolute variance, which generalizes the uncertainty relations for arbitrary operator (not necessarily Hermitian) to quantum channels. By recalling the quantity | U ρ | ​ ( Φ ) |U_{\rho}|(\Phi) proposed by Zhang e ​ t ​ a ​ l . et\ al. (Quantum Inf. Process. 22 456, 2023), which involves terms of more quantum mechanical nature. 1. Introduction

As one of the cornerstones of quantum mechanics, uncertainty principle has been widespreadly concerned since proposed by Heisenberg
[
1
]
in
1927
.
The standard form of Heisenberg uncertainty relation was given by two arbitrary observables
A
and
B
with respect to a quantum state
\rho

[
2
]
,

\displaystyle V_{\rho}(A)V_{\rho}(B)\geq\frac{1}{4}|\mathrm{Tr}(\rho[A,B])|^{2},

(1)

where
V_{\rho}(A)=\mathrm{Tr}(\rho A^{2})-(\mathrm{Tr}\rho A)^{2}
is the conventional variance and
[A,B]=AB-BA
denotes the commutator. Later (
1
) was refined by Schrödinger
[
3
,
4
]
,

\displaystyle V_{\rho}(A)V_{\rho}(B)\geq\frac{1}{4}|\mathrm{Tr}(\rho[A,B])|^{2}+\frac{1}{4}|\mathrm{Tr}(\rho\{A_{0},B_{0}\})|^{2},

(2)

where
A_{0}=A-\mathrm{Tr}(\rho A)
,
B_{0}=B-\mathrm{Tr}(\rho B)
and
\{A_{0},B_{0}\}=A_{0}B_{0}+A_{0}B_{0}
denotes the anti-commutator.

The quantities
V_{\rho}(A)
,
V_{\rho}(B)
and
\mathrm{Tr}(\rho[A,B])
are all purely quantum terms when
\rho
is a pure state. If
\rho
is a mixed state, inequality (
1

V_{\rho}(A)
is a hybrid of both classical mixing and quantum uncertainty
[
5
]
. Luo
[
6
]
striped certain classical mixing uncertainty off the variance and introduced the quantity
U_{\rho}(A)=\sqrt{V^{2}_{\rho}(A)-[V_{\rho}(A)-I_{\rho}(A)]^{2}}
, where
I_{\rho}(A)=-\frac{1}{2}\mathrm{Tr}\left(\left[\sqrt{\rho},A\right]^{2}\right)
is the Wigner-Yanase (WY) skew information
[
7
]
.
U_{\rho}(A)
satisfies the following uncertainty relation
[
6
]
,

\displaystyle U_{\rho}(A)U_{\rho}(B)\geq\frac{1}{4}|\mathrm{Tr}(\rho[A,B])|^{2}.

(3)

As the most general description of quantum measurement, quantum channels play an essential role in quantum theory
[
8
]
. For a quantum channel

2. Preliminaries

Let
\mathcal{H}
be an
n
-dimensional Hilbert space. Denote
\mathcal{B(H)}
and
\mathcal{S(H)}
the sets of
all bounded linear operators and Hermitian operators on
\mathcal{H}
, respectively. For
K
,
L\in\mathcal{B(H)}
,
\langle K,L\rangle=\mathrm{Tr}(K^{\dagger}L)
is the inner product. The norm of
K\in\mathcal{B(H)}
is defined by
\|K\|=\sqrt{\mathrm{Tr}(K^{{\dagger}}K)}
.

Definition 1
For a quantum state
\rho\in\mathcal{D(H)}
and an operator
K\in\mathcal{B(H)}
, the
\rho
-absolute variance of
K
is defined by
[
34
]

\displaystyle|V_{\rho}|(K):=\mathrm{Tr}({\rho}|K_{0}|^{2})=\|K_{0}\sqrt{\rho}\|^{2}=\mathrm{Tr}({\rho}|K|^{2})-|\mathrm{Tr}({\rho}K)|^{2},

(4)

where
K_{0}=K-\mathrm{Tr}(\rho K)
and
|K|=(K^{{\dagger}}K)^{\frac{1}{2}}\in\mathcal{S(H)}
. The symmetrized
\rho
-absolute variance is defined by
[
10
]

\displaystyle|V^{\circ}_{\rho}|(K)=\frac{1}{2}[|V_{\rho}|(K)+|V_{\rho}|(K^{{\dagger}})],

(5)

where
|V_{\rho}|(K)
is the
\rho
-absolute variance. Note that both
|V_{\rho}|(K)
and
|V^{\circ}_{\rho}|(K)
reduce to the conventional variance
V_{\rho}(K)=\mathrm{Tr}(\rho K^{2})-(\mathrm{Tr}\rho K)^{2}
when
K
is a Hermitian operator.

Definition 2
The symmetrized commutator
[X,Y]^{\circ}
of
X
and
Y\in\mathcal{B(H)}
is defined by
[
10
]

\displaystyle[X,Y]^{\circ}=\frac{1}{2}([X,Y]+[X^{{\dagger}},Y^{{\dagger}}]),

(6)

and the symmetrized anti-commutator
\{X,Y\}^{\circ}
of
X
and
Y
is defined by
[
10
]

\displaystyle\{X,Y\}^{\circ}=\frac{1}{2}(\{X,Y\}+\{X^{{\dagger}},Y^{{\dagger}}\}).

(7)

Lemma 1
For any
K\in\mathcal{B(H)}
and state
\rho
, if
K=A+\mathrm{i}B
is the Cartesian decomposition of
K
, i.e.,
A,B\in\mathcal{S(H)}
, then
[
10
]

\displaystyle|\mathrm{Tr}(\rho K)|^{2}=|\mathrm{Tr}(\rho A)|^{2}+|\mathrm{Tr}(\rho B)|^{2}.

(8)

Based on Definition 1 and Definition 2,
Dou
et\ al.

[
10
]
generalized the inequalitie (
1
-
3
) to the case arbitrary operators
K
and
L\in\mathcal{B(H)}
,

\displaystyle|V^{\circ}_{\rho}|(K)|V^{\circ}_{\rho}(L)|\geq\frac{1}{4}|\mathrm{Tr}(\rho[K,L])|^{2},

(9)

\displaystyle|V^{\circ}_{\rho}|(K)|V^{\circ}_{\rho}(L)|\geq\frac{1}{4}|\mathrm{Tr}(\rho[K,L]^{\circ})|^{2}+\frac{1}{4}|\mathrm{Tr}(\rho\{K,L\}^{\circ})|^{2}

(10)

and

\displaystyle|U_{\rho}|(K)|U_{\rho}(L)|\geq\frac{1}{4}|\mathrm{Tr}(\rho[K,L]^{\circ})|^{2},

(11)

where
|U_{\rho}|(K)=\sqrt{|V^{\circ}_{\rho}|^{2}(K)-(|V^{\circ}_{\rho}|(K)-|I_{\rho}|(K))^{2}}
,
[K,L]^{\circ}
and
\{K,L\}^{\circ}
are the symmetrized commutator and the symmetrized anti-commutator, respectively
[
10
]
. The inequalities (
9
)-(
11
) give rise to the inequalities (
1
)-(
3
) when
K,L\in\mathcal{S(H)}
.

Following the idea in Ref.
[
9
]
, Sun
et\ al.
defined the quantity
|V^{\circ}_{\rho}|(\Phi)=\sum_{i}|V^{\circ}_{\rho}|(E_{i})
, the sum of the uncertainties of the
Kraus operators
\{E_{i}\}

[
27
]
. The quantities
|V^{\circ}_{\rho}|(\Phi)
and
|I_{\rho}|(\Phi)
characterize the total uncertainty and the quantum uncertainty of the quantum channel
\Phi
, respectively
[
28
,
27
]
. Zhang
et\ al.

[
28
]
further extended the quantity
|U_{\rho}|(K)
to

\displaystyle|U_{\rho}|(\Phi)=

\displaystyle\sqrt{|V^{\circ}_{\rho}|^{2}(\Phi)-[|V^{\circ}_{\rho}|(\Phi)-|I_{\rho}|(\Phi)]^{2}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sqrt{|V^{\circ}_{\rho}|^{2}(\Phi)-|C_{\rho}|^{2}(\Phi)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sqrt{[|V^{\circ}_{\rho}|(\Phi)+|C_{\rho}|(\Phi)][|V^{\circ}_{\rho}|(\Phi)-\Phi|C_{\rho}|(\Phi)]}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sqrt{|\mathaccent 869{I}|_{\rho}(\Phi)|\mathaccent 869{J}|_{\rho}(\Phi)}.

(12)

By direct verification we have

|\mathaccent 869{I}|_{\rho}(\Phi)=\sum_{i}|I_{\rho}|(E_{i0})=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i}\mathrm{Tr}\left([\sqrt{\rho},E_{i0}]^{{\dagger}}[\sqrt{\rho},E_{i0}]\right)
and

|\mathaccent 869{J}|_{\rho}(\Phi)=\sum_{i}|J_{\rho}|(E_{i0})=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i}\mathrm{Tr}\left(\{\sqrt{\rho},E_{i0}\}^{{\dagger}}\{\sqrt{\rho},E_{i0}\}\right)
where
E_{i0}=E_{i}-\mathrm{Tr}(\rho E_{i})
.

For two arbitrary quantum channels
\Phi
and

[

\displaystyle|U_{\rho}|(\Phi)|U_{\rho}|(\Psi)\geq\frac{1}{4}\sum_{ij}|\mathrm{Tr}([F_{j},{E^{{\dagger}}_{i}}]\rho)|^{2}

\displaystyle|U_{\rho}|^{2}(\Psi)+|U_{\rho}|^{2}(\Phi)\geq\frac{1}{2}\sum_{ij}\left|\langle[\sqrt{\rho},F_{i}],[\sqrt{\rho},E_{i}]\rangle\left(\langle\{\sqrt{\rho},F_{j}\},\{\sqrt{\rho},E_{j}\}\rangle-4\langle F_{j}^{{\dagger}}\rangle\langle E_{j}\rangle\right)\right|,

(14)

where
\Phi(\rho)=\sum_{i}E_{i}\rho E_{i}^{\dagger}
and
\Psi(\rho)=\sum_{j}F_{j}\rho F_{j}^{\dagger}
.

3. Uncertainty relations of quantum channels in terms of the symmetrized
\rho
-absolute variance and MWY skew information

In this section, we generalize the uncertainty relations (
9
)-(
11
) of arbitrary operators to quantum channels.

Theorem 1
For two arbitrary quantum channels with Kraus representations
\Phi(\rho)=\sum_{i=1}^{N}E_{i}\rho E_{i}^{\dagger}
and
\Psi(\rho)=\sum_{j=1}^{N}F_{j}\rho F_{j}^{\dagger}
on an
n
-dimensional Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}
, the following uncertainty relations hold,

\displaystyle|V^{\circ}_{\rho}|(\Phi)|V^{\circ}_{\rho}|(\Psi)\geq\mathrm{max}\left\{\frac{1}{4N^{2}}\left|\sum_{ij}\mathrm{Tr}(\rho[E_{i},F_{j}])\right|^{2},\frac{1}{4N^{2}}\left|\sum_{ij}\mathrm{Tr}(\rho\{E_{i0},F_{j0}\})\right|^{2}\right\}.

(15)

Proof

Define
2\times 2
matrices
\mathcal{A}_{ij}
and
\mathcal{B}_{ij}
by

\mathcal{A}_{ij}=\begin{pmatrix}|V_{\rho}|(E^{{\dagger}}_{i0})&\langle\sqrt{\rho},E_{i0}F_{j0}\sqrt{\rho}\rangle&\\
\langle\sqrt{\rho},F^{{\dagger}}_{j0}E^{{\dagger}}_{i0}\sqrt{\rho}\rangle&|V_{\rho}|(F_{j0})&\\
\end{pmatrix}

and

\mathcal{B}_{ij}=\begin{pmatrix}|V_{\rho}|(E_{i0})&\langle\sqrt{\rho},-F_{j0}E_{i0}\sqrt{\rho}\rangle&\\
\langle\sqrt{\rho},-E^{{\dagger}}_{i0}F^{{\dagger}}_{j0}\sqrt{\rho}\rangle&|V_{\rho}|(F^{{\dagger}}_{j0})&\\
\end{pmatrix},

respectively. It is easily seen that
\overline{\langle\sqrt{\rho},F^{{\dagger}}_{j0}E^{{\dagger}}_{i0}\sqrt{\rho}\rangle}=\langle\sqrt{\rho},E_{i0}F_{j0}\sqrt{\rho}\rangle=\langle E^{{\dagger}}_{i0}\sqrt{\rho},F_{j0}\sqrt{\rho}\rangle
and
|V_{\rho}|(E^{{\dagger}}_{i0})|V_{\rho}|(F_{j0})\geq|\langle E^{{\dagger}}_{i0}\sqrt{\rho},F_{j0}\sqrt{\rho}\rangle|^{2}=|\langle\sqrt{\rho},E_{i0}F_{j0}\sqrt{\rho}\rangle|^{2}
by Cauchy-Schwarz inequality.
Hence,
\mathcal{A}_{ij}
and
\sum_{ij}\mathcal{A}_{ij}
are positive semi-definite. In a similar way, we can verify that the matrices
\mathcal{B}_{ij}
,
\sum_{ij}\mathcal{B}_{ij}
and
\sum_{ij}(\mathcal{A}_{ij}+\mathcal{B}_{ij})
are all positive semi-definite. Moreover, by using the fact that

|V^{\circ}_{\rho}|(E_{i0})=|V^{\circ}_{\rho}|(E_{i})
,

[E_{i0},F_{j0}]=[E_{i},F_{j}]
and (
5
),
\sum_{ij}(\mathcal{A}_{ij}+\mathcal{B}_{ij})
can be rewritten as

\sum_{ij}(\mathcal{A}_{ij}+\mathcal{B}_{ij})=\begin{pmatrix}N\sum_{i}[|V_{\rho}|(E^{{\dagger}}_{i0})+|V_{\rho}|(E_{i0})]&\sum_{ij}\langle\sqrt{\rho},(E_{i0}F_{j0}-F_{j0}E_{i0})\sqrt{\rho}\rangle&\\
\sum_{ij}\langle\sqrt{\rho},(F^{{\dagger}}_{j0}E^{{\dagger}}_{i0}-E^{{\dagger}}_{i0}F^{{\dagger}}_{j0})\sqrt{\rho}\rangle&N\sum_{j}[|V_{\rho}|(F_{j0})+|V_{\rho}|(F^{{\dagger}}_{j0})]&\\
\end{pmatrix}

=\begin{pmatrix}2N|V^{\circ}_{\rho}|(\Phi)&\sum_{ij}\langle\sqrt{\rho},[E_{i},F_{j}]\sqrt{\rho}\rangle&\\
\sum_{ij}\langle\sqrt{\rho},[E_{i},F_{j}]^{{\dagger}}\sqrt{\rho}\rangle&2N|V^{\circ}_{\rho}|(\Psi)&\\
\end{pmatrix}.

Therefore, we have

\displaystyle|V^{\circ}_{\rho}|(\Phi)|V^{\circ}_{\rho}|(\Psi)\geq\frac{1}{4N^{2}}\left|\sum_{ij}\mathrm{Tr}(\rho[E_{i},F_{j}])\right|^{2}.

In a similar way, we prove that the following
2\times 2
matrix
\mathcal{C}_{ij}
,

\mathcal{C}_{ij}=\begin{pmatrix}|V_{\rho}|(E_{i0})&\langle\sqrt{\rho},F_{j0}E_{i0}\sqrt{\rho}\rangle&\\
\langle\sqrt{\rho},E^{{\dagger}}_{i0}F^{{\dagger}}_{j0}\sqrt{\rho}\rangle&|V_{\rho}|(F^{{\dagger}}_{j0})&\\
\end{pmatrix}

is positive semi-definite too. It is easily seen
\sum_{ij}(\mathcal{A}_{ij}+\mathcal{C}_{ij})\geq 0
. Therefore,

\displaystyle|V^{\circ}_{\rho}|(\Phi)|V^{\circ}_{\rho}|(\Psi)\geq\frac{1}{4N^{2}}\left|\sum_{ij}\mathrm{Tr}(\rho\{E_{i0},F_{j0}\})\right|^{2}.

\Box

Theorem 2
For two arbitrary quantum channels given by
\Phi(\rho)=\sum_{i=1}^{N}E_{i}\rho E_{i}^{\dagger}
and
\Psi(\rho)=\sum_{j=1}^{N}F_{j}\rho F_{j}^{\dagger}
, we have

\displaystyle|V^{\circ}_{\rho}|(\Phi)|V^{\circ}_{\rho}|(\Psi)\geq\frac{1}{4N^{2}}\left(\left|\sum_{ij}\mathrm{Tr}(\rho\{E_{i0},F_{j0}\}^{\circ})\right|^{2}+\left|\sum_{ij}\mathrm{Tr}(\rho[E_{i0},F_{j0}]^{\circ})\right|^{2}\right).

(16)

Proof

Define
2\times 2
matrices
\mathcal{A}_{ij}
and
\mathcal{D}_{ij}
by

\mathcal{A}_{ij}=\begin{pmatrix}|V_{\rho}|(E^{{\dagger}}_{i0})&\langle\sqrt{\rho},E_{i0}F_{j0}\sqrt{\rho}\rangle&\\
\langle\sqrt{\rho},F^{{\dagger}}_{j0}E^{{\dagger}}_{i0}\sqrt{\rho}\rangle&|V_{\rho}|(F_{j0})&\\
\end{pmatrix}

and

\mathcal{D}_{ij}=\begin{pmatrix}|V_{\rho}|(E_{i0})&\langle\sqrt{\rho},E^{{\dagger}}_{i0}F^{{\dagger}}_{j0}\sqrt{\rho}\rangle&\\
\langle\sqrt{\rho},F_{j0}E_{i0}\sqrt{\rho}\rangle&|V_{\rho}|(F^{{\dagger}}_{j0})&\\
\end{pmatrix},

respectively. Similar to the proof of Theorem 1, it can be verified that
\sum_{ij}\mathcal{A}_{ij}
,
\sum_{ij}\mathcal{D}_{ij}
and
\sum_{ij}(\mathcal{A}_{ij}+\mathcal{D}_{ij})
are positive semi-definite. Moreover,

\sum_{ij}(\mathcal{A}_{ij}+\mathcal{D}_{ij})=\begin{pmatrix}N\sum_{i}(|V_{\rho}|(E^{{\dagger}}_{i0})+|V_{\rho}|(E_{i0}))&\sum_{ij}\langle\sqrt{\rho},(E_{i0}F_{j0}\sqrt{\rho}+E^{{\dagger}}_{i0}F^{{\dagger}}_{j0})\sqrt{\rho}\rangle&\\
\sum_{ij}\langle\sqrt{\rho},(F^{{\dagger}}_{j0}E^{{\dagger}}_{i0}\sqrt{\rho}+F^{{\dagger}}_{j0}E^{{\dagger}}_{i0})\sqrt{\rho}\rangle&N\sum_{j}(|V_{\rho}|(F_{j0})+|V_{\rho}|(F^{{\dagger}}_{j0}))&\\
\end{pmatrix}

=\begin{pmatrix}2N|V^{\circ}_{\rho}|(\Phi)&\sum_{ij}\langle\sqrt{\rho},(E_{i0}F_{j0}+E^{{\dagger}}_{i0}F^{{\dagger}}_{j0})\sqrt{\rho}\rangle&\\
\sum_{ij}\langle\sqrt{\rho},(F^{{\dagger}}_{j0}E^{{\dagger}}_{i0}+F_{j0}E_{i0})\sqrt{\rho}\rangle&2N|V^{\circ}_{\rho}|(\Psi)&\\
\end{pmatrix}.

In this case,
E_{i0}F_{j0}+E^{{\dagger}}_{i0}F^{{\dagger}}_{j0}
can be rewritten as

\displaystyle E_{i0}F_{j0}+E^{{\dagger}}_{i0}F^{{\dagger}}_{j0}=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}([E_{i0},F_{j0}]+[E^{{\dagger}}_{i0},F^{{\dagger}}_{j0}])+\frac{1}{2}(\{E_{i0},F_{j0}\}+\{E^{{\dagger}}_{i0},F^{{\dagger}}_{j0}\})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathrm{i}\frac{1}{2}(-\mathrm{i}([E_{i0},F_{j0}]+[E^{{\dagger}}_{i0},F^{{\dagger}}_{j0}]))+\frac{1}{2}(\{E_{i0},F_{j0}\}+\{E^{{\dagger}}_{i0},F^{{\dagger}}_{j0}\})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathrm{i}(-\mathrm{i}[E_{i0},F_{j0}]^{\circ})+\{E_{i0},F_{j0}\}^{\circ}.

Hence,

\displaystyle|V^{\circ}_{\rho}|(\Phi)|V^{\circ}_{\rho}|(\Psi)\geq\frac{1}{4N^{2}}

\displaystyle\left|\sum_{ij}\langle\sqrt{\rho},[\mathrm{i}(-\mathrm{i}[E_{i0},F_{j0}]^{\circ})+\{E_{i0},F_{j0}\}^{\circ}]\sqrt{\rho}\rangle\right|^{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{4N^{2}}

\displaystyle\left|\sum_{ij}\mathrm{Tr}(\rho\{E_{i0},F_{j0}\}^{\circ})+\mathrm{i}\sum_{ij}\mathrm{Tr}\rho(-\mathrm{i}[E_{i0},F_{j0}]^{\circ})\right|^{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{4N^{2}}

\displaystyle\left(\left|\sum_{ij}\mathrm{Tr}(\rho\{E_{i0},F_{j0}\}^{\circ})\right|^{2}+\left|\sum_{ij}\mathrm{Tr}\rho([E_{i0},F_{j0}]^{\circ})\right|^{2}\right),

where the last equality follows from Lemma 1.

The lower bounds given in Theorem 1 and 2 are generally different for different channels
\Phi
and
\Psi
and states
\rho
. They are complementary in lower bounding the product
|V^{\circ}_{\rho}|(\Phi)|V^{\circ}_{\rho}|(\Psi)
.

Next we present tighter uncertainty relations of (
13
) and (
14
) for quantum channels based on MWY skew information. Let
\Phi
and
\Psi
be two arbitrary quantum channels on
n
-dimensional Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}
with Kraus representations
\Phi(\rho)=\sum_{i}E_{i}\rho E_{i}^{\dagger}
and
\Psi(\rho)=\sum_{j}F_{j}\rho F_{j}^{\dagger}
. Denote

|\rho^{E_{i0}}_{k}\rangle=\rho^{E_{i0}}|k\rangle=[\sqrt{\rho},E_{i0}]|k\rangle
,
|\rho^{F_{j0}}_{l}\rangle=\rho^{F_{j0}}|l\rangle=\{\sqrt{\rho},F_{j0}\}|l\rangle
,
|\rho^{\mathaccent 869{E}_{i0}}_{k}\rangle=\rho^{\mathaccent 869{E}_{i0}}|k\rangle=\{\sqrt{\rho},E_{i0}\}|k\rangle
and
|\rho^{\mathaccent 869{F}_{j0}}_{l}\rangle=\rho^{\mathaccent 869{F}_{j0}}|l\rangle=[\sqrt{\rho},F_{j0}]|l\rangle
, where
\{|t\rangle\}^{n}_{t=1}
is an orthonormal basis in
\mathcal{H}
. Denote

\vec{e}_{n}^{i}=(|\rho_{1}^{E_{i0}}\rangle,|\rho_{2}^{E_{i0}}\rangle\cdots,|\rho_{n}^{E_{i0}}\rangle)^{T}
,

\vec{f}_{n}^{j}=(|\rho_{1}^{{F}_{j0}}\rangle,|\rho_{2}^{{F}_{j0}}\rangle\cdots,|\rho_{n}^{{F}_{j0}}\rangle)^{T}

\vec{g}_{n}^{i}=(|\rho_{1}^{\mathaccent 869{E}_{i0}}\rangle,|\rho_{2}^{\mathaccent 869{E}_{i0}}\rangle\cdots,|\rho_{n}^{\mathaccent 869{E}_{i0}}\rangle)^{T}
,

\vec{h}_{n}^{j}=(|\rho_{1}^{\mathaccent 869{F}_{j0}}\rangle,|\rho_{2}^{\mathaccent 869{F}_{j0}}\rangle\cdots,|\rho_{n}^{\mathaccent 869{F}_{j0}}\rangle)^{T}
,
and
\vec{e}_{1}^{i}=(|\rho_{1}^{E_{i0}}\rangle,\cdots,\cdots,\vec{0}_{n\times 1})^{T}
,

\vec{f}_{1}^{j}=(|\rho_{1}^{F_{j0}}\rangle,\cdots,\vec{0}_{n\times 1})^{T}
,

\vec{g}_{1}^{i}=(|\rho_{1}^{\mathaccent 869{E}_{i0}}\rangle,\cdots,\vec{0}_{n\times 1})^{T}
,

\vec{h}_{1}^{j}=(|\rho_{1}^{\mathaccent 869{F}_{j0}}\rangle,\cdots,\vec{0}_{n\times 1})^{T}
.
In particular, we have
\vec{e}_{0}^{i}=\vec{f}_{0}^{j}=\vec{g}_{0}^{i}=\vec{h}_{0}^{j}=\vec{0}^{T}_{n^{2}\times 1}
.
\vec{e}_{1,c}
,
\vec{f}_{1,c}
,
\vec{g}_{1,c}
and
\vec{h}_{1,c}
are the complementary vectors of
\vec{e}_{1}^{i}
,
\vec{f}_{1}^{j}
,
\vec{g}_{1}^{i}
and
\vec{h}_{1}^{j}
, respectively. With these notions
|I_{\rho}|(E_{i0})
and

can be written as

\displaystyle|I_{\rho}|(E_{i0})=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\mathrm{Tr}\left([\sqrt{\rho},E_{i0}]^{{\dagger}}[\sqrt{\rho},E_{i0}]\right)=\frac{1}{2}\left(\sum_{k=1}^{n}\langle k|(\rho^{E_{i0}})^{{\dagger}}\rho^{E_{i0}}|k\rangle\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\left(\sum_{k=1}^{n}\||\rho^{E_{i0}}_{k}\rangle\|^{2}\right)=\frac{1}{2}\|\vec{e}_{n}^{i}\|^{2}

and

\displaystyle|J_{\rho}|(F_{j0})=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\mathrm{Tr}\left(\{\sqrt{\rho},F_{j0}\}^{{\dagger}}\{\sqrt{\rho},F_{j0}\}\right)=\frac{1}{2}\left(\sum_{l=1}^{n}\langle l|(\rho^{F_{j0}})^{{\dagger}}\rho^{F_{j0}}|l\rangle\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\left(\sum_{l=1}^{n}\||\rho^{F_{j0}}_{l}\rangle\|^{2}\right)=\frac{1}{2}\|\vec{f}_{n}^{j}\|^{2}.

The quantities
|I_{\rho}|(F_{j0})=\frac{1}{2}\|\vec{h}_{n}^{j}\|^{2}
and
|J_{\rho}|(E_{i0})=\frac{1}{2}\|\vec{g}_{n}^{i}\|^{2}
are defined similarly. We have the following theorems.

Theorem 3
For two arbitrary quantum channels with Kraus representations
\Phi(\rho)=\sum_{i}^{N}E_{i}\rho E_{i}^{\dagger}
and
\Psi(\rho)=\sum_{j}^{N}F_{j}\rho F_{j}^{\dagger}
on an
n
-dimensional Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}
, the following uncertainty relation holds,

\displaystyle|U_{\rho}|(\Phi)|U_{\rho}|(\Psi)\geq\sqrt{I_{1}\mathaccent 869{I}_{1}},

(17)

where
I_{1}=\sum_{ij}I_{1}^{i,j}
and
\mathaccent 869{I}_{1}=\sum_{ij}\mathaccent 869{I}_{1}^{i,j}
.

Proof
By using the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality, we have

\displaystyle|I_{\rho}|({E_{i0}})|J_{\rho}|({F_{j0}})=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{4}\|\vec{e}_{n}^{i}\|^{2}\|\vec{f}_{n}^{j}\|^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{4}(\|\vec{e}_{1}^{i}\|^{2}+\|\vec{e}_{1,c}^{i}\|^{2})(\|\vec{f}_{1}^{j}\|^{2}+\|\vec{f}_{1,c}^{j}\|^{2})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{4}\left(\|\vec{e}_{1}^{i}\|^{2}\|\vec{f}_{1}^{j}\|^{2}+\|\vec{e}_{1}^{i}\|^{2}\|\vec{f}_{1,c}^{j}\|^{2}+\|\vec{e}_{1,c}^{i}\|^{2}(\|\vec{f}_{1}^{j}\|^{2}+\|\vec{f}_{1,c}^{j}\|^{2})\right)

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{4}\left(|\langle\vec{e}_{1}^{i},\vec{f}_{1}^{j}\rangle|^{2}+\|\vec{e}_{1}^{i}\|^{2}\|\vec{f}_{1,c}^{j}\|^{2}+\|\vec{e}_{1,c}^{i}\|^{2}(\|\vec{f}_{1}^{j}\|^{2}+\|\vec{f}_{1,c}^{j}\|^{2})\right).

(18)

Denote
I_{0}^{i,j}=|I_{\rho}|({E_{i0}})|J_{\rho}|({F_{j0}})
and

I_{1}^{i,j}=\frac{1}{4}\left(|\langle\vec{e}_{1}^{i},\vec{f}_{1}^{j}\rangle|^{2}+\|\vec{e}_{1}^{i}\|^{2}\|\vec{f}_{1,c}^{j}\|^{2}+\|\vec{e}_{1,c}^{i}\|^{2}(\|\vec{f}_{1}^{j}\|^{2}\right.\\
\left.+\|\vec{f}_{1,c}^{j}\|^{2})\right)
.
We obtain

\displaystyle I_{1}^{i,j}=

\displaystyle I_{0}^{i,j}-\frac{1}{4}(\|\vec{e}_{1}^{i}\|^{2}\|\vec{f}_{1}^{j}\|^{2}-|\langle\vec{e}_{1}^{i},\vec{f}_{1}^{j}\rangle|^{2})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle|I_{\rho}|({E_{i0}})|J_{\rho}|({F_{j0}})-\frac{1}{4}\left(\langle\langle\rho_{1}^{E_{i0}}|,|\rho_{1}^{E_{i0}}\rangle\rangle\langle\langle\rho_{1}^{F_{j0}}|,|\rho_{1}^{F_{j0}}\rangle\rangle-|\langle\langle\rho_{1}^{E_{i0}}|,|\rho_{1}^{F_{j0}}\rangle\rangle|^{2}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle|I_{\rho}|({E_{i0}})|J_{\rho}|({F_{j0}})-\frac{1}{4}(\langle 1|[\sqrt{\rho},E_{i0}]^{{\dagger}}[\sqrt{\rho},E_{i0}]|1\rangle\langle 1|\{\sqrt{\rho},F_{j0}\}^{{\dagger}}\{\sqrt{\rho},F_{j0}\}|1\rangle)

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\frac{1}{4}(|\langle 1|[\sqrt{\rho},E_{i0}]^{{\dagger}}\{\sqrt{\rho},F_{j0}\}|1\rangle|^{2}).

(19)

Summing over the indices
i
and
j
on both sides of the inequality (
18
), we have

\mathaccent 869{I}_{\rho}(\Phi)\mathaccent 869{J}_{\rho}(\Psi)\geq I_{1}
, which
is saturated if and only if
|\rho_{1}^{E_{i0}}\rangle
and
|\rho_{1}^{F_{j0}}\rangle
are linear dependent for arbitrary
i
and
j
.

Similarly, for
|I_{\rho}|({F_{j0}})|J_{\rho}|({E_{i0}})
, we have
\mathaccent 869{I}_{1}^{i,j}=\frac{1}{4}(|\langle\vec{h}_{1}^{j},\vec{g}_{1}^{i}\rangle|^{2}+\|\vec{h}_{1}^{j}\|^{2}\|\vec{g}_{1,c}^{i}\|^{2}+\|\vec{h}_{1,c}^{j}\|^{2}(\|\vec{g}_{1}^{i}\|^{2}+\|\vec{g}_{1,c}^{i}\|^{2}))
and

\mathaccent 869{I}_{0}^{i,j}=|I_{\rho}|({F_{j0}})|J_{\rho}|({E_{i0}})
. We obtain

\displaystyle\mathaccent 869{I}_{1}^{i,j}=

\displaystyle\mathaccent 869{I}_{0}^{i,j}-\frac{1}{4}(\|\vec{h}_{1}^{j}\|^{2}\|\vec{g}_{1}^{i}\|^{2}-|\langle\vec{h}_{1}^{j},\vec{g}_{1}^{i}\rangle|^{2})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle|I_{\rho}|({F_{j0}})|J_{\rho}|({E_{i0}})-\frac{1}{4}\left(\langle\langle\rho_{1}^{\mathaccent 869{F}_{j0}}|,|\rho_{1}^{\mathaccent 869{F}_{j0}}\rangle\rangle\langle\langle\rho_{1}^{\mathaccent 869{E}_{i0}}|,|\rho_{1}^{\mathaccent 869{E}_{i0}}\rangle\rangle-|\langle\langle\rho_{1}^{\mathaccent 869{F}_{j0}}|,|\rho_{1}^{\mathaccent 869{E}_{i0}}\rangle\rangle|^{2}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle|I_{\rho}|({F_{j0}})|J_{\rho}|({E_{i0}})-\frac{1}{4}\langle 1|[\sqrt{\rho},F_{j0}]^{{\dagger}}[\sqrt{\rho},F_{j0}]|1\rangle\langle 1|\{\sqrt{\rho},E_{i0}\}^{{\dagger}}\{\sqrt{\rho},E_{i0}\}|1\rangle

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\frac{1}{4}|\langle 1|[\sqrt{\rho},F_{j0}]^{{\dagger}}\{\sqrt{\rho},E_{i0}\}|1\rangle|^{2}.

(20)

Thus we have
\mathaccent 869{I}_{\rho}(\Psi)\mathaccent 869{J}_{\rho}(\Phi)\geq\mathaccent 869{I}_{1}
,
which is saturated if and only if
|\rho_{1}^{\mathaccent 869{F}_{j0}}\rangle
and
|\rho_{1}^{\mathaccent 869{E}_{i0}}\rangle
are linear dependent for arbitrary
i
and
j
. Therefore, we have

|U_{\rho}|(\Phi)|U_{\rho}|(\Psi)=\sqrt{\mathaccent 869{I}_{\rho}(\Phi)\mathaccent 869{J}_{\rho}(\Psi)\mathaccent 869{I}_{\rho}(\Psi)\mathaccent 869{J}_{\rho}(\Phi)}\\
\geq\sqrt{I_{1}\mathaccent 869{I}_{1}}
.
\Box

Theorem 4
For two arbitrary quantum channels with Kraus representations
\Phi(\rho)=\sum_{i}^{N}E_{i}\rho E_{i}^{\dagger}
and
\Psi(\rho)=\sum_{j}^{N}F_{j}\rho F_{j}^{\dagger}
on an
n
-dimensional Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}
, the following uncertainty relation holds,

\displaystyle|U_{\rho}|^{2}(\Psi)+|U_{\rho}|^{2}(\Phi)\geq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{4}\sum_{ij}^{N}(|\langle[\sqrt{\rho},F_{i}],\{\sqrt{\rho},F_{j}\}\rangle|^{2}

(21)

\displaystyle+\langle[\sqrt{\rho},E_{i}],[\sqrt{\rho},E_{i}]\rangle(\langle\{\sqrt{\rho},E_{j}\},\{\sqrt{\rho},E_{j}\}\rangle-4|\mathrm{Tr}(\rho E_{j})|^{2})).

Proof
By using the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality, we have

\displaystyle|U_{\rho}|^{2}(\Psi)+|U_{\rho}|^{2}(\Phi)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathaccent 869{I}_{\rho}(\Psi)\mathaccent 869{J}_{\rho}(\Psi)+\mathaccent 869{I}_{\rho}(\Phi)\mathaccent 869{J}_{\rho}(\Phi)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{ij}^{N}\left[I_{\rho}(F_{i0})J_{\rho}(F_{j0})+I_{\rho}(E_{i0})J_{\rho}(E_{j0})\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{4}\sum_{ij}^{N}[\langle[\sqrt{\rho},F_{i0}],[\sqrt{\rho},F_{i0}]\rangle\langle\{\sqrt{\rho},F_{j0}\},\{\sqrt{\rho},F_{j0}\}\rangle+\langle[\sqrt{\rho},E_{i0}],[\sqrt{\rho},E_{i0}]\rangle\langle\{\sqrt{\rho},E_{j0}\},\{\sqrt{\rho},E_{j0}\}\rangle]

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{4}\sum_{ij}^{N}[|\langle[\sqrt{\rho},F_{i0}],\{\sqrt{\rho},F_{j0}\}\rangle|^{2}+\langle[\sqrt{\rho},E_{i0}],[\sqrt{\rho},E_{i0}]\rangle\langle\{\sqrt{\rho},E_{j0}\},\{\sqrt{\rho},E_{j0}\}\rangle].

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{4}\sum_{ij}^{N}[|\langle[\sqrt{\rho},F_{i}],\{\sqrt{\rho},F_{j}\}\rangle|^{2}+\langle[\sqrt{\rho},E_{i}],[\sqrt{\rho},E_{i}]\rangle(\langle\{\sqrt{\rho},E_{j}\},\{\sqrt{\rho},E_{j}\}\rangle-4|\mathrm{Tr}(\rho E_{j})|^{2})].

\Box

4. Conclusions

We have explored the product form uncertainty relations of quantum channels in terms of the symmetrized
\rho
-absolute variance, which generalizes the results in Ref.
[
10
]
from operators to quantum channels. By using Cauchy-Schwarz inequality, we have also presented the product and summation form uncertainty relations of quantum channels in terms of the quantity
|U_{\rho}|(\Phi)
. By explicit examples, we have shown that our lower bounds are tighter than the existing ones. Our results may highlight further researches on uncertainty relations of quantum channels characterized in other ways.

Self-Decomposable Laws Associated with General Tempered Stable (GTS) Distribution and their Simulation Applications
Aubain Nzokem

Abstract
The paper describes the self-decomposable distribution and the background driving Lévy process (BDLP) associated with the Generalized Tempered Stable (GTS) distribution. Two distributions are provided: the background driving Lévy process (BDLP) of the GTS distribution and the self-decomposable distribution generated by the GTS distribution as BDLP. The derived self-decomposable distribution and the GTS distribution are used as stationary distribution in the Ornstein-Uhlenbeck type process. A simulation method, based on sampling the random integral representation, is applied to mimic the S&P 500 Index and Bitcoin daily cumulative return process.



keywords
Self-Decomposable, Background Driving Lévy Process (BDLP), Ornstein–Uhlenbeck Process, Generalized Tempered Stable Distribution

Decomposable Laws: Overview

Consider a sequence
(X_{k}:k=1,2,...)

is said to belong to Class L. In the classical Central Limit Theorem (CLT), the asymptotically standard normal is of Class L. Likewise, the class of stable laws is of Class L. It can be described by four parameters: a location parameter (
\mu
), a scale parameter (
\sigma>0
), a stability index (
0\leq\alpha\leq 2
) and a skewness parameter (
-1\leq\beta\leq 1
). The distribution of any random variable in the class L is infinitely divisible.

In addition, it is shown

that a Class L is also a class of self-decomposable probability distributions. The distribution of a random variable

(

\displaystyle X\overset{d}{=}cX+X_{c}\hskip 28.45274pt \hbox{with $X$ and $X_{c}$ are independent variable}

means equality in distribution.

Analytically, a random variable

\displaystyle\forall(0<c<1)\,\exists\rho_{c}\,\forall(t\in\mathbb{R})\quad\quad\phi(t)=\phi(ct)\rho_{c}(t)

where
\rho_{c}
is also a characteristic function.

Class L distributions is also described

as distributions of random integrals. Class L is represented as follows

\displaystyle L=\left\{\int_{0}^{+\infty}e^{-t}dY(s),\mbox{with $Y$ is a L\'{e}vy Process and $E\left[\log(1+|Y(1)|)\right]\leq\infty$ }\right\}

From a probability density function

with parameter

of size

and the sample data

of size

, we definite the likelihood function and its derivatives as follows:

\displaystyle L(x,V)

\displaystyle=\prod_{j=1}^{m}f(x_{j},V)\quad

\displaystyle l(x,V)

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{m}log(f(x_{j},V))

(48)

\displaystyle \frac{dl(x,V)}{dV_{j}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{m}\frac{\frac{df(x_{i},V)}{dV_{j}}}{f(x_{i},V)}\quad\quad \frac{d^{2}l(x,V)}{dV_{k}dV_{j}}

(49)

48
), the quantities

and

) .

5.1
Stationary process Algorithm of Ornstein-Uhlenbeck type

We recall the stationary process of Ornstein-Uhlenbeck type
\left\{X(t)\right\}_{t>0}
and a background driving Lévy process (BDLP)
\left\{L(t)\right\}_{t>0}
independent of
X_{0}
, such that
X(t)\overset{d}{=}X
as described in Theorem (
5
)

\displaystyle X_{t}=e^{-\lambda t}X_{0}+\int_{0}^{t}e^{-\lambda(t-s)}dL_{\lambda s}=e^{-\lambda t}X_{0}+\int_{0}^{\lambda t}e^{-\lambda t+s}dL_{s} \quad\lambda>0.

(53)

Theorem 8
.

Let
\phi
be the characteristic exponent of a random variable X and
\Psi(x)
be the characteristic exponent of a random variable
L_{1}
.

\left\{X(t)\right\}_{t>0}
is the stationary process of Ornstein-Uhlenbeck type, such that
X(t)\overset{d}{=}X
.

\displaystyle\lim_{t\to+\infty}Log(E\left[e^{i\xi X_{t}}\right])=\phi(\xi)=\int_{0}^{\xi}\frac{\Psi(u)}{u}du

(54)

Proof.

X_{0}
is an initial value, not a variable.

\displaystyle Log(E\left[e^{i\xi X_{t}}\right])

\displaystyle=e^{-\lambda t}X_{0}\xi i+\int_{0}^{\lambda t}Log(E\left[e^{i\xi e^{-\lambda t+s}L_{1}}\right])ds=e^{-\lambda t}X_{0}\xi i+\int_{0}^{\lambda t}\Psi(\xi e^{-\lambda t+s})ds

\displaystyle=e^{-\lambda t}X_{0}\xi i+\int_{\xi e^{-\lambda t+s}}^{\xi}\frac{\Psi(u)}{u}du\quad\quad u=\xi e^{-\lambda t+s}

We take the limit on both side

\displaystyle\lim_{t\to+\infty}Log(E\left[e^{i\xi X_{t}}\right])=\phi(\xi)=\int_{0}^{\xi}\frac{\Psi(u)}{u}du

∎

Theorem 9
.

Let us consider the stationary process of Ornstein-Uhlenbeck type
\left\{X(t)\right\}_{t>0}
with background driving Lévy process (BDLP)
\left\{L(t)\right\}_{t>0}
, such that
X(t)\overset{d}{=}X
. The discrete-time horizon is
t_{0}<t_{1}<t_{2}<t_{3},...,<t_{n-1}<t_{n}
with

\Delta t=t_{i}-t_{i-1}
. The discrete version of the stationary process of Ornstein-Uhlenbeck type becomes

\displaystyle X_{k\Delta t}=e^{-\lambda\Delta t}X_{(k-1)\Delta t}+\int_{0}^{\lambda\Delta t}e^{-\lambda\Delta t+s}dL_{s}

(55)

Proof.

For
k=1
, replace
t
by
\Delta t
in the Equation (
5.1
) and we have :

\displaystyle X_{\Delta t}=e^{-\lambda\Delta t}X_{0}+\int_{0}^{\lambda\Delta t}e^{-\lambda\Delta t+s}dL_{s}

We assume the equation (
55
) holds for k. We have the following relation for
k+1
.

\displaystyle X_{(k+1)\Delta t}

\displaystyle=e^{-\lambda(k+1)\Delta t}X_{0}+\int_{0}^{\lambda(k+1)\Delta t}e^{-\lambda(k+1)\Delta t+s}dL_{s}

\displaystyle=e^{-\lambda\Delta t}\left(e^{-\lambda k\Delta t}X_{0}+\int_{0}^{\lambda k\Delta t}e^{-\lambda k\Delta t+s}dL_{s}\right)+\int_{\lambda k\Delta t}^{\lambda(k+1)\Delta t}e^{-\lambda(k+1)\Delta t+s}dL_{s}

\displaystyle=e^{-\lambda\Delta t}X_{k\Delta t}+\int_{0}^{\lambda\Delta t}e^{-\lambda\Delta t+u}dL_{u+\lambda k\Delta t}\quad\quad u=s-\lambda k\Delta t\quad dL_{u+\lambda k\Delta t}=dL_{u}

\displaystyle=e^{-\lambda\Delta t}X_{k\Delta t}+\int_{0}^{\lambda\Delta t}e^{-\lambda\Delta t+u}dL_{u}

∎

The relation (
5.1
) can be used to simulate
Y=\int_{0}^{\lambda\Delta t}e^{-\lambda\delta t+s}dL_{s}

X_{k\Delta t}\overset{d}{=}X

\phi

5.1

\displaystyle Log(E\left[e^{i\xi X_{\Delta t}}\right])=Log(E\left[e^{i\xi{e^{-\lambda\Delta t}X_{(k-1)\Delta t}}}\right])+Log(E\left[e^{i\xi Y}\right])

(56)

It is

\displaystyle\phi(\xi)=\phi(e^{-\lambda\Delta t}\xi)+Log(E\left[e^{i\xi Y}\right])\quad\quad Log(E\left[e^{i\xi Y}\right])=\phi(\xi)-\phi({e^{-\lambda\Delta t}\xi})

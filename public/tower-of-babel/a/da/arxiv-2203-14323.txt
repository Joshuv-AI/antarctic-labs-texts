On quantum free-electron laser: Superradience
Alexander Iomin

Abstract
A quantum model of a free-electron laser is considered for the many electron system. An exact expression for the evolution of the laser amplitude is obtained in the framework of the coherent state consideration. Reliable conditions for the superradiance of the high-gained laser is discussed for the short time limit of the exact solution.

Introduction

Experimental implementation and theoretical description of
free-electron lasers is a long-lasting problem that started

;

In this notes we consider a quantum model of a free-electron laser (FEL),
which has been obtained from the consideration of a
non-relativistic electron in an electromagnetic field

Becker and McIver 1983
;
Becker and McIver 1987
in a so-called Bambini–Renieri frame

Bambini and Renieri 1978
;
Bambini et al. 1979
, see also App.
A

N_{e}=N
electrons,
the quantum Hamiltonian reads
Becker and McIver 1983
;
Becker and McIver 1987
;
Kling et al. 2015
;
Kling et al. 2019

\hat{H}=\sum_{j=1}^{N}\frac{\hat{p}_{j}^{2}}{2m}+\hbar g\left(\hat{a}\sum_{j=1}^{N}e^{2ik\hat{z}_{j}}+\hat{a}^{\dagger}\sum_{j=1}^{N}e^{-2ik\hat{z}_{j}}\right),

(1)

where summation relates to the positions
\hat{z}_{j}
and momenta

\hat{p}_{j}
of electrons with the mass
m
and the wave number
k
.
The position-momentum commutation rule is

[\hat{z}_{k},\hat{p}_{j}]=i\hbar\delta_{k,j}
.
The laser mode is described by the photon annihilation and
creation operators
\hat{a}
and
\hat{a}^{\dagger}
, respectively
with the commutation rule

[\hat{a},\hat{a}^{\dagger}]=1
. The interaction parameter
g
couples
the electron dynamics to the photon laser field.

Various approaches have been considered and discussed in the
literature
Bonifacio et al. 2005
;
Kling et al. 2015
;
Kling et al. 2019
,
see an extended discussion in recent results
Kling et al. 2019
;
Kling et al. 2021
.
The problem is well posed and discussed in the literature,
and our main concern in this paper relates to the technicality
of the exact solution and its short time approximation.

Our main question in task is the intensity of the laser
field amplitude

\mathcal{A}(t)=\langle\hat{a}^{\dagger}(t)\hat{a}(t)\rangle=\langle\Psi_{0}|\hat{U}^{\dagger}(t)\hat{a}^{\dagger}\hat{a}\hat{U}(t)|\Psi_{0}\rangle,

(2)

where
\hat{U}(t)
is the unitary evolution operator. The initial wave
function is the direct product of the photon and electron wave functions

|\Psi_{0}\rangle=|\alpha\rangle\otimes|\mathcal{P}\rangle,

(3)

where the coherent states
Glauber 1963

|\alpha\rangle
are chosen for the photon wave function
Becker et al. 1982
,
while the electron wave function can be different realization of
the electron initial conditions. In particular it can be either an
orthogonal basis

|\mathcal{P}\rangle=|\mathbf{p}\rangle=\prod_{j=1}^{N}|p_{j}\rangle\equiv|p_{1},\dots,p_{N}\rangle
, where

\langle p_{k}|{p^{\prime}}_{j}\rangle=\delta_{k,j}
, or the electron coherent
states
|\mathcal{P}\rangle=|\vec{\beta}\rangle
. We consider the both.

It is worth be stressing that discussing the dynamics of the
system we also set the notation for the operators considered here.
Namely, for any time dependent operator

\hat{x}(t)\equiv\hat{x}_{t}
its initial value is denoted by

\hat{x}=\hat{x}(t=0)
. The same is concerned with its average
values denoting
x(t)\equiv x_{t}
and
x=x(t=0)
, where

x=(a^{*},a,\{p_{j},z_{j}\})
.
The only exclusion is for the Hamiltonian, since it is
the integral of motion and correspondingly
\hat{\cal H}(t)=\hat{\cal H}
.

We start from the Heisenberg equation of motion for the dynamics of the laser field intensity in the operator form

\hat{\mathcal{A}}(t)=\hat{a}^{\dagger}(t)\hat{a}(t)
,

\dot{\hat{\mathcal{A}}}(t)=\frac{1}{i\hbar}[\hat{\mathcal{A}}(t),\hat{\cal H}].

(4)

In this approach, the evolution of the
averaged value of the laser field intensity is defined as follows

\langle\hat{\mathcal{A}}(t)\rangle=e^{{\cal K}t}\langle\hat{\mathcal{A}}(t=0)\rangle,

where
{\cal K}
is a so-called Koopman operator
Koopman 1931
,
see also
e.g.
,
Gaspard 1988
;
Cvitanović et al. 2022
.

Another approach, developed in the paper is investigation
of the evolution of the initial wave function,
which relates to the construction of the evolution
operator by means of the path integrals
Feynman and Hibbs 1965
;
Schulman 1981
.
For the latter example, in the initial wave functions
(
3
), the electron wave function is the
direct product of electron wave functions

|\mathcal{P}\rangle=|\mathbf{z}\rangle=\prod_{j=1}^{N}|z_{j}\rangle\equiv|z_{1},\dots,z_{N}\rangle

(5)

where

\langle z_{k}|{z^{\prime}}_{j}\rangle=\delta_{k,j}\delta(z-z^{\prime})
.

Another possibility is the momentum representation of the electron
wave function, when
\hat{p}_{j}|\mathbf{p}\rangle=p_{j}|\mathbf{p}\rangle
and

\hat{z}_{j}=i\hbar\frac{d}{dp_{j}}
,
the operator
e^{-2ik\hat{z}_{j}}
acts as a shift operator

e^{-2ik\hat{z}_{j}}|p_{j}\rangle=e^{2k\hbar\frac{d}{dp_{j}}}|p_{j}\rangle=|p_{j}+2k\hbar\rangle.

(6)

Analogously,
\langle p_{j}+2k\hbar|=\langle p_{j}|e^{2ik\hat{z}_{j}}=\left(e^{-2ik\hat{z}_{j}}|p_{j}\rangle\right)^{\dagger}
.

II
Koopman operator

For the quantum mechanical analysis of the Heisenberg equations
of motion
we use a technique of mapping the Heisenberg equations on a basis
of the coherent states
Sinitsyn and Tsukernik 1982
;
Berman et al. 1981
;
Berman et al. 1986
;
Iomin 2016
;
Iomin 2017
.
Since the Hamiltonian is the integral of motion
then it is time independent,
\hat{\cal H}(t)=\hat{\cal H}(t=0)
.
Therefore, the Hamiltonian in the Heisenberg equations
can be mapped on a basis of the coherent states

|\alpha\rangle
and
|\vec{\beta}\rangle
, constructed
at the initial moment
t=0
..
First, we construct the basis of the photon coherent states.
That is, at the initial moment
t=0
, one introduces
the coherent states vector
{|\alpha\rangle}
as the eigenfunction
of the annihilation operators
\hat{a}=\hat{a}(t=0)
, such that

\hat{a}(t=0)|\alpha\rangle\equiv\hat{a}|\alpha\rangle=\alpha|\alpha\rangle

and correspondingly

\langle\alpha|\hat{a}^{\dagger}=[\hat{a}|\alpha\rangle]^{{\dagger}}=\alpha^{*}\langle\alpha|
.
The coherent state can be also constructed from a vacuum state

|0\rangle
as follows
Perelomov 1986

{|\alpha\rangle}=\exp[\alpha\hat{a}^{\dagger}-\alpha^{*}\hat{a}]|0\rangle,\quad\hat{a}|0\rangle=0.

(7)

Then averaging the photon field operator over the photon coherent states
{\langle\alpha|}\hat{\mathcal{A}}(t){|\alpha\rangle}
,
one maps the Heisenberg equation of motion (
4
)
on the basis of the coherent states
{|\alpha\rangle}

as follows

i\hbar\frac{d}{dt}{\langle\alpha|}\hat{\mathcal{A}}(t){|\alpha\rangle}=\langle\alpha|\left[\hat{\mathcal{A}}\hat{{\cal H}}-\hat{{\cal H}}\hat{\mathcal{A}}(t)\right]|\alpha\rangle.

(8)

Taking into account Eq. (
7
), one obtains
the mapping rules
Klauder and Sudarshan 1968

\displaystyle{\langle\alpha|}\hat{\mathcal{A}}(t)\hat{a}^{\dagger}{|\alpha\rangle}=e^{-|\alpha|^{2}}\frac{\partial}{\partial\alpha}e^{|\alpha|^{2}}\mathcal{A}(t),

(9a)

\displaystyle{\langle\alpha|}\hat{a}\hat{\mathcal{A}}(t){|\alpha\rangle}=e^{-|\alpha|^{2}}\frac{\partial}{\partial\alpha^{*}}e^{|\alpha|^{2}}\mathcal{A}(t).

(9b)

The next step is the averaging procedure for the
electron part of the Hilbert space.
To this end we construct a basis of the electron coherent
states at the initial moment
t=0
.
We admit that the electron system in the Hamiltonian (
1
)
is considered as a system of free spinless
particles and their commutation
rules corresponds to a so called Heisenberg - Weyl group

Perelomov 1986
. Therefore introducing creation

\hat{b}^{\dagger}
and annihilation
\hat{b}
operators:

\displaystyle\hat{b}_{j}=\frac{z_{j}+i\hat{p}_{j}}{\sqrt{2\hbar}},\quad\quad\hat{b}^{\dagger}=\frac{z_{j}-i\hat{p}_{j}}{\sqrt{2\hbar}},

(10a)

\displaystyle z_{j}=\sqrt{\frac{\hbar}{2}}(\hat{b}_{j}+\hat{b}^{\dagger}_{j}),\quad\hat{p}_{j}=i\sqrt{\frac{\hbar}{2}}(\hat{b}^{\dagger}_{j}-\hat{b}_{j}),

(10b)

where
[\hat{b}_{j},\hat{b}^{\dagger}_{j^{\prime}}]=\delta_{j,j^{\prime}}
,
we introduce the coherent state basis

|\vec{\beta}\rangle=\prod_{j=1}^{N}|\beta_{j}\rangle=\prod_{j=1}^{N}\exp[\beta_{j}\hat{b}^{\dagger}_{j}-\beta_{j}^{*}\hat{b}_{j}]|0\rangle,\quad\hat{b}_{j}|0\rangle=0.

(11)

which belongs to the electron Hilbert space.

Introducing the double average

\mathcal{A}(t)\equiv\langle\hat{\mathcal{A}}(t)\rangle=\langle\langle\hat{\mathcal{A}}(t){\rangle}_{\alpha}{\rangle}_{\beta}

(12)

and using properties (
9
) for both

\hat{a},\hat{a}^{\dagger}
and
\hat{b},\hat{b}^{\dagger}
operators and taking into account
the commutator
[\hat{\mathcal{A}},\mathbf{p}^{2}]
, we obtain the “Koopman equation”

\frac{d}{dt}\mathcal{A}(t)={\cal K}\mathcal{A}(t),

(13)

where the Hermitian Koopman operator reads

\displaystyle{\cal K}=

\displaystyle\frac{i}{4m}\sum_{j=1}^{N}\left(\partial_{\beta^{*}_{j}}^{2}-\partial_{\beta_{j}}^{2}+2\beta_{j}\partial_{\beta^{*}_{j}}\right.

\displaystyle-

\displaystyle\left.2\beta_{j}^{*}\partial_{\beta_{j}}+2\beta_{j}\partial_{\beta_{j}}-2\beta_{j}^{*}\partial_{\beta^{*}_{j}}\right)

(14a)

\displaystyle+ig\sum_{j=1}^{N}\left[\partial_{\alpha^{*}}e^{2ik(\beta_{j}^{*}+\beta_{j})}e^{2ik\partial_{\beta_{j}^{*}}}\right.

\displaystyle-

\displaystyle\left.\partial_{\alpha}e^{-2ik(\beta_{j}^{*}+\beta_{j})}e^{-2ik\partial_{\beta_{j}}}\right].

(14b)

The Koopman operator consists of two parts:

{\cal K}={\cal K}_{e}+{\cal K}_{e-ph}
. The first part
{\cal K}_{e}
, described
by Eq. (
14a
) corresponds to the kinetic part of
free electrons, while the second part describes
the electron-photon interaction, Eq. (
14b
).
The second part is more sophisticated operator and its inferring
needs some care see App.
B
.

II.1
Exact solutions

An exact expression can be obtained from the formal solution
(
18
). Let us consider the Koopman equation
(
13
),

\frac{d}{dt}\mathcal{A}(t)={\cal K}\mathcal{A}(t)

(15)

with the solution

\mathcal{A}(t)=e^{t{\cal K}}\alpha^{*}\alpha,

(16)

and let the Koopman operator is such that

{\cal K}\mathcal{A}(t=0)={\cal K}\alpha^{*}\alpha=e\alpha^{*}\alpha,

(17)

where is a constant value. This stationary equation leads to the exponential gain solution

\mathcal{A}(t)=e^{et}|\alpha|^{2}

with
e>0
.

Another important and exact result in the electron coherent states
is the constant values of the averaged energy and the moment of electrons. This conservation law immediately follows from
the specific form of the Koopman operator (
14
) and the transformations
(
10
).
Namely

{\cal K}\langle p_{j}^{2}\rangle=0~~\text{and}~~{\cal K}\langle p_{j}\rangle=0,

then

\langle p_{j}^{2}(t)\rangle=\langle p_{j}^{2}\rangle
and

\langle p_{j}(t)\rangle=\langle p_{j}\rangle
. This situation
is a pure quantum effect. It
can be explained by coherent Cherenkov radiation, which is described just by the same Hamiltonian as in Eq. (
1
)
Becker and McIver 1987
,
see also Appendix
A
.

II.2
Short time approximation I

The solution of the Koopman equation (
13
)
for the initial condition
\langle\mathcal{A}(t=0)\rangle=\alpha^{*}\alpha

is presented in the exponential form
Iomin 2022

\mathcal{A}(t)=e^{t{\cal K}}\alpha^{*}\alpha=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{t^{n}}{n!}{\cal K}^{n}\alpha^{*}\alpha.

(18)

We take into account that electrons spend a very short time
inside the laser size, namely
t\ll 1
,
which results from the fact that the laser size is
finite and the electron velocity is very large (even large than the speed of light). Then we should
take into account only few first terms in the expansion
(
18
), and we restrict ourselves by the second
order of
t^{2}
.
Obviously, for
t^{0}
, the zero order term corresponds
to the initial condition:
{\langle\mathcal{A}\rangle}^{(0)}=|\alpha|^{2}
.
Correspondingly, the first order term for
t^{1}
reads

{\langle\mathcal{A}\rangle}^{(1)}=t{\cal K}\alpha^{*}\alpha=igt\sum_{j=1}^{N}\left[\partial_{\alpha^{*}}e^{2ik(\beta_{j}^{*}+\beta_{j})}e^{2ik\partial_{\beta_{j}^{*}}}-\right.\\
\left.\partial_{\alpha}e^{-2ik(\beta_{j}^{*}+\beta_{j})}e^{-2ik\partial_{\beta_{j}}}\right]\alpha^{*}\alpha\\
=igt\sum_{j=1}^{N}\left[\alpha e^{2ik(\beta_{j}^{*}+\beta_{j})}-\alpha^{*}e^{-2ik(\beta_{j}^{*}+\beta_{j})}\right].

(19)

At this step, the action of the Koopman operator reduces
to differentiation
with respect to
\alpha
and
\alpha^{*}
only. The derivatives

\partial_{\beta_{j}}
and
\partial_{\beta_{j}^{*}}
do not “work”
here. The situation changes for the second order term
for which we obtain

{\langle\mathcal{A}\rangle}^{(2)}=\frac{t}{2}{\cal K}{\langle\mathcal{A}\rangle}^{(1)}={\cal K}_{e}{\langle\mathcal{A}\rangle}^{(1)}-\frac{g^{2}t^{2}}{2}\\
\times\sum_{j=1}^{N}\left[\partial_{\alpha^{*}}e^{2ik(\beta_{j}^{*}+\beta_{j})}e^{2ik\partial_{\beta_{j}^{*}}}-\partial_{\alpha}e^{-2ik(\beta_{j}^{*}+\beta_{j})}e^{-2ik\partial_{\beta_{j}}}\right]\\
\times\sum_{j=1}^{N}\left[\alpha e^{2ik(\beta_{j}^{*}+\beta_{j})}-\alpha^{*}e^{-2ik(\beta_{j}^{*}+\beta_{j})}\right]\\
=\frac{t}{2}{\cal K}_{e}{\langle\mathcal{A}\rangle}^{(1)}+e^{4k^{2}}\frac{g^{2}t^{2}}{2}\sum_{j^{\prime},j=1}^{N}e^{2ik(\beta_{j}^{*}+\beta_{j}-\beta_{j^{\prime}}^{*}+\beta_{j^{\prime}})}.

(20)

We restrict the expansion by the second order in Eq.
(
20
). The last term is the main
contribution
to laser amplitude

\mathcal{A}(t)\approx e^{4k^{2}}\frac{g^{2}t^{2}}{2}\sum_{j^{\prime},j=1}^{N}e^{2ik(\beta_{j}^{*}+\beta_{j}-\beta_{j^{\prime}}^{*}+\beta_{j^{\prime}})},

(21)

and it is the first main result, which will be discussed
in Sec.
IV

IV
Discussion: Superradiance

We collect some physical consequences of the calculations
of the laser intensity and shall discuss them in this section.
The main contribution to the intensity of the laser photon field
is due to the interaction term in the Hamiltonian
(
1
), that is

\mathcal{A}(t)\sim g^{2}\left\langle\left[\hat{a}\sum_{j=1}^{N}e^{2ikz_{j}}+\hat{a}^{\dagger}\sum_{j=1}^{N}e^{-2ikz_{j}}\right]^{2}\right\rangle.

(37)

Two different approaches for the evaluation of the laser intensity
are suggested in the framework of the Heisenberg and
Schrödinger representations of quantum mechanics.

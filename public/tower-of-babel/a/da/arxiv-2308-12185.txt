Profinite Subgroup Accessibility and Recognition of Amalgamated Factors
Julian Wykowski

Abstract
. We investigate accessible subgroups of a profinite group G G , i.e. subgroups H H appearing as vertex groups in a graph of profinite groups decomposition of G G with finite edge groups. We prove that any accessible subgroup H ≤ G H\leq G arises as the kernel of a continuous derivation of G G in a free module over its completed group algebra. This allows us to deduce splittings of an abstract group from splittings of its profinite completion. We prove that any finitely generated subgroup Δ \Delta of a finitely generated virtually free group Γ \Gamma whose closure is a factor in a profinite amalgamated product Γ ^ = Δ ¯ ∐ K L \widehat{\Gamma}=\overline{\Delta}\amalg_{K}L along a finite K K must be a factor in an amalgamated product Γ = Δ ∗ χ Λ \Gamma=\Delta\ast_{\upchi}\Lambda along some χ ≅ K \raisebox{1.75003pt}{$\upchi$}\cong K . This extends previous results of Parzanchevski–Puder, Wilton and Garrido–Jaikin-Zapirain on free factors.



1.
Introduction

The structure of profinite groups is as intriguing as it is elusive. As Galois groups, profinite groups appear throughout mathematics, and it is of great interest in fields such as number theory or algebraic geometry to shed light on the peculiar structural properties of these objects. Within group theory, particular focus lies on the study of
profinite rigidity
, that is, the question to what extent the structure of an abstract group
\Gamma
is visible in its finite quotients. More concisely, this problem can be rephrased as the question of how much the structure of an abstract group
\Gamma
is visible in its profinite completion
\widehat{\Gamma}
, the latter being a profinite group comprised of the inverse system of the finite quotients of
\Gamma
and their epimorphisms.

A natural question regarding the structure of a mathematical object is whether it can be decomposed via the canonical constructions in the category it inhabits, that is, limits and colimits. While limits commute with the forgetful functor from the profinite to the abstract category, colimits do not: already the coproduct of two profinite groups, their profinite free product, can significantly outgrow their coproduct as abstract groups, the free product. This makes the study of profinite coproduct decompositions both harder and more interesting. A larger class of colimit decompositions is given by
graph of groups splittings
, which are related via Bass–Serre theory to a group’s ability to act on trees. In recent years, a beautiful profinite analogue of this geometric theory—profinite groups acting on profinite trees—has come to fruition, albeit in a far more obscure form than its abstract sibling. This development has brought about answers as well as questions.

It is natural then to ask whether profinite decompositions of the profinite completion
\widehat{\Gamma}
of a residually finite group
\Gamma
must arise from abstract decompositions of
\Gamma
. In other words: can one recognise decompositions of
\Gamma
in its finite quotients? The restriction to the class of residually finite groups is necessary here to ensure that
\Gamma
embeds into its profinite completion
\widehat{\Gamma}
as a subgroup. A celebrated instance of this general question, attributed to Remeslennikov (cf.
[
NRR82
, Question 15]
), asks whether there exists a finitely generated residually finite group which is not free but whose profinite completion is free profinite. The solution remains out of sight at the time being. However, in recent years, the related question of whether a subgroup
\Delta\leq\Gamma
whose closure
\overline{\Delta}
in
\widehat{\Gamma}
is a factor in a profinite decomposition must itself arise as a factor in an abstract decomposition of
\Gamma
has gained considerable attention. In the case of free factors of finitely generated free groups, this was answered positively by Parzanchevski and Puder in
[
PP15
]
and by Wilton in
[
Wil18a
]
. Last year, Garrido and Jaikin-Zapirain generalised in
[
GJ22
]
this result to free factors of finitely generated virtually free groups, providing a Bass–Serre theoretic proof.

Thus, one is tempted to extend the overarching question to a more general class of decompositions. The natural next step would be to consider free products with amalgamation, which arise for instance as the fundamental groups of unions of path-connected spaces with path-connected intersection. Given a residually finite group
\Gamma
which splits as a free product
\Gamma=\Delta\ast_{\upchi}\Lambda
with amalgamation along a finite
\upchi
, it follows from the definition of pushouts (cf.
[
RZ10
, Excercise 9.2.7(3)]
) that
\widehat{\Gamma}=\overline{\Delta}\amalg_{\upchi}\overline{\Lambda}
, where
\amalg
denotes the profinite amalgamated product, and
\overline{\Delta}
and
\overline{\Lambda}
denote the closures of
\Delta
and
\Lambda
in
\widehat{\Gamma}
, respectively. We ask here the converse direction of this implication, which is considerably more involved and vastly unanswered. We remark the particular significance of this question in light of the recent discovery (cf.
[
Che+24
, Corollary 1.2]
) that splitting as an amalgam is not a profinite invariant among finitely presented residually finite groups.

Question 1
.

Let
\Gamma
be a finitely generated residually finite group and
\Delta\leq\Gamma
a finitely generated subgroup. Assume that
\widehat{\Gamma}=\overline{\Delta}\amalg_{K}L

\raisebox{1.78891pt}{$\upchi$},\Lambda
such that
\Gamma=\Delta\ast_{\upchi}\Lambda
and
\raisebox{1.78891pt}{$\upchi$}\cong K

The theory of Bass and Serre is not unique among those which infer the structure of a group from its ability to act on other mathematical objects. The topologically inspired—yet entirely algebraic—theory of groups acting on modules, group cohomology, has proven instrumental in the study of groups in the last century. In 1979, Dunwoody related these two theories in an elegant result, which states that a subgroup
\Delta
of a group
\Gamma
is contained in the kernel of a non-trivial derivation of
\Gamma
in its group ring
R[\Gamma]
if and only if
\Delta
is
accessible within

\Gamma
, i.e.
\Delta
is contained in the stabiliser of a vertex under an action of
\Gamma
on a tree with finite edge stabilisers. We introduce the following analogous definition for profinite groups.

Definition 1
.

Let
G
be a profinite group. A closed subgroup
H\leq G
is
accessible within
G
if
G=\Pi_{1}{(\mathcal{G},\Xi,\Theta)}
for some graph of profinite groups
(\mathcal{G},\Xi,\Theta)
over a finite graph
\Xi
such that all edge groups
\mathcal{G}(e)
for
e\in E(\Xi)
are finite and there exists a vertex
v\in\mathcal{G}(v)
with
H=\mathcal{G}(v)
.

When necessary to specify the graph of groups, we shall say that

is
accessible within
G
via

(\mathcal{G},\Xi,\Theta)
. We refer the reader to Section
2.1
for details on graphs of profinite groups. The cohomology theory of profinite groups emerges analogously to that of abstract groups, and in the case of a profinite completion
\iota\colon\Gamma\to\widehat{\Gamma}

\Gamma
and
\widehat{\Gamma}
in a variety of ways. This was exploited partially by Garrido–Jaikin-Zapirain in
[

, who used Dunwoody’s theorem to construct an abstract graph of groups splitting using a continuous derivation obtained from a profinite free product. In this paper, we address the question of obtaining these continuous derivations for general graphs of profinite groups. We prove the following profinite analogue of the “if” direction in Dunwoody’s result.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
G
be a profinite group and
H\leq G
a closed subgroup accessible within
G
via a graph of profinite groups
(\mathcal{G},\Xi,\Theta)
with
n

The difficulty in erecting such a derivation in the profinite setting is that, first, the minimal subtree of a profinite tree containing two vertices may be infinite, so Dunwoody’s construction using almost invariant sets does not carry over, and second, an arbitrary element of a graph of profinite groups does not have a normal form, so precise control over the derivation’s kernel becomes a question of asymptotics within a profinite group. We settle the first issue in the proof of Proposition
3.2
, and dedicate the proof of Theorem
1.1
to the resolution of the second issue.

Having established Theorem
1.1
, we aim to utilise it together with the abstract opposite direction to deduce splittings of an abstract group from splittings of its profinite completion. In
[

]
, Garrido and Jaikin-Zapirain follow this strategy in the case of free products. They start with a finitely generated virtually free group
\Gamma

\Delta
whose closure in
\widehat{\Gamma}
is a factor in a profinite free splitting
\widehat{\Gamma}=\overline{\Delta}\amalg L
. Using a direct sum decomposition of augmentation ideals inherent to free products, they obtain a continuous derivation

of
\widehat{\Gamma}
in its completed group algebra
R[[\widehat{\Gamma}]]
for a finite ring

. Restricting

to
\Gamma

f(\Gamma)
lies in a projective
R[\Gamma]
-module. Dunwoody’s theorem then yields a graph of groups decomposition of
\Gamma
, which they mould to the form
\Gamma=\Delta\ast\Lambda
for some
\Lambda\leq\Gamma
.

In this paper, we employ a similar idea to attack Question
1

\widehat{\Gamma}
must be obtained by alternative means. We do this in Theorem
1.1
for all graphs of groups. The second difficulty lies in the increased complexity of Bass–Serre theoretic manipulations necessary to retrieve the desired decomposition. This we accomplish in Section

The article is structured as follows. In Section

In Section

). However, in the case where the quotient

is finite, it forms a bona fide graph of groups, so it is possible to construct a quotient graph of groups, and one obtains a profinite analogue of the Bass–Serre structure theorem. We refer the curious reader to Section 6 in
[
Rib17
]
for details and to
[
Rib17
, Theorem 6.6.1]
for the statement of the structure theorem, which we omit here in the interest of brevity. Recently, a general structure theorem has also been established in the pro-
p
category: see
[
WZ17
, Theorem 1.1]
.

The other direction associates to a graph of profinite groups
(\mathcal{G},X)
a profinite tree
T
referred to as the
profinite structure tree
whereon the fundamental group
G=\Pi_{1}{(\mathcal{G},X)}
acts naturally and whose quotient by this action retrieves the initial graph. Although this construction can be carried out for any graph of profinite groups (cf.
[
Rib17
, Section 6.3]
), we shall only presently describe it in the case where
X
is a finite graph, as we will not require the complexity of greater generality. In that case, choose a spanning subtree
\Theta\subseteq X
and write
G(x)\leq G
for the image of
\mathcal{G}(x)
in
G
under the inclusion specified by
\Theta
. Define the profinite tree
T
as

V(T)=\bigsqcup_{v\in V(X)}G/G(v)\quad\text{and}\quad E(T)=\bigsqcup_{e\in E(X)}G/G(e)

where
G/G(x)
denotes the space of left cosets of
G(x)\leq G
endowed with the (profinite) quotient topology, and

d_{0}(gG(e))=gG(d_{0}(e))\quad\text{and}\quad d_{1}(gG(e))=gt_{e}G(d_{1}(e))

whenever
e\in E(X)
is an edge and
t_{e}
the associated stable letter. One verifies that
T
forms a profinite graph, and in fact a profinite tree: see
[
Rib17
, Corollary 6.3.6]
. Moreover, as each
G/G(e)
is closed in
T
and
X
is finite, we obtain:

Remark 2.6
.

The structure tree of a graph of profinite groups over a finite graph has closed edge set.

The profinite structure tree construction is especially useful in that it allows one to study profinite groups which decompose as graphs of profinite groups via their actions on the structure tree. This was first exploited by Zalesskii and Melnikov in
[
ZM89
]
, where certain structural properties of closed subgroups of such profinite groups were deduced from their action on the profinite structure tree. For instance, they prove the following result, which we shall utilise throughout the present work.

Proposition 2.7

(Theorem 2.10 in
[
ZM89
]
)
.

A finite group acting on a profinite tree must fix a vertex.

As a consequence, one finds a variety of structural results regarding subgroups of such profinite groups: see
[
ZM89
, Section 3]
. We refer the curious reader also to
[
BCR16
]
for a recent application of this result in the proof of an elegant theorem on the profinite rigidity of Fuchsian groups.

2.2.
Homological Algebra of Profinite Groups

Let
G
be a profinite group and
R
a profinite ring. A left/right
G
-module
is an abelian topological group
M
equipped with a left/right continuous
G
-action. The completed group algebra of
G
over
R
is the profinite ring

R[[G]]=\lim_{\longleftarrow U}\left(R\left[G/U\right]\right)

where
U\trianglelefteq_{f}G
runs over all open normal subgroups. The abstract group algebra
R[G]
may be endowed with the natural topology generated by the cosets of the ideals which arise as kernels of the quotient epimorphisms

R[G]\longrightarrow(R/I)[G/U]

for
I\trianglelefteq_{o}R
an open ideal and
U\trianglelefteq_{o}G
an open subgroup. By
[
RZ10
, Lemma 5.3.5]
,
R[G]
embeds in
R[[G]]
and the latter is its completion with respect to the above topology. Given a
G
-module and a continuous action
R\curvearrowright M
, one obtains a canonical
R[[G]]
-module structure on
M
, where the action of
G
extends
R
-linearly to the image of
R[G]
as a dense subset of
R[[G]]
, and then extends continuously to all of
R[[G]]
. We define the cohomology groups of a
G
-module
M
via

H^{\bullet}(G,M)=\lim_{U\longrightarrow}H^{\bullet}(G/U,M^{U})

where
U\trianglelefteq_{o}G
runs over all open normal subgroups of
G
. Note that given an abstract group
\Gamma
with profinite completion map
\iota\colon\Gamma\to\widehat{\Gamma}
, any
\widehat{\Gamma}
-module
M
forms a
\Gamma
-module with action via
\iota
. Conversely, if
M
is a
finite

\Gamma
-module, the action
\Gamma\to\operatorname{Aut}(M)
factors through a map
\widehat{\Gamma}\to\operatorname{Aut}(M)
, making
M
a discrete
\widehat{\Gamma}
-module. We say that
\Gamma
is
cohomologically good
if the profinite completion map
\iota\colon\Gamma\to\widehat{\Gamma}
induces an isomorphism of cohomology groups

\iota^{*}\colon H^{\bullet}(\widehat{\Gamma},M)\longrightarrow H^{\bullet}(\Gamma,M)

for any finite
\Gamma
-module
M
. The following result yields an equivalent characterisation of cohomological goodness for
\mathrm{FP}_{\infty}
-groups. It was proven first by Wilkes in
[
Wil16
, Proposition 3.14]
and subsequently, in the below formulation, by Jaikin-Zapirain in
[
Jai20
, Proposition 3.1]
.

Lemma 2.8

(Proposition 3.1 in
[
Jai20
]
)
.

Let
\Gamma
be a group of type
\mathrm{FP}_{\infty}
and

(2.7)

\cdots\rightarrow\mathbb{Z}[\Gamma]^{n_{i}}\xrightarrow{\phi_{i}}\mathbb{Z}[\Gamma]^{n_{i}-1}\rightarrow\ldots\rightarrow\mathbb{Z}[\Gamma]^{n_{1}}\xrightarrow{\phi_{1}}\mathbb{Z}[\Gamma]\rightarrow\mathbb{Z}\rightarrow 0

be a resolution of the trivial
\Gamma
-module
\mathbb{Z}
by finitely generated free
\mathbb{Z}[\Gamma]
-modules. Then
\Gamma
is cohomologically good iff the induced sequence

(2.8)

\cdots\rightarrow\widehat{\mathbb{Z}}[[\widehat{\Gamma}]]^{n_{i}}\xrightarrow{\widehat{\phi_{i}}}\widehat{\mathbb{Z}}[[\widehat{\Gamma}]]^{n_{i}-1}\rightarrow\ldots\rightarrow\widehat{\mathbb{Z}}[[\widehat{\Gamma}]]^{n_{1}}\xrightarrow{\widehat{\phi_{1}}}\widehat{\mathbb{Z}}[[\widehat{\Gamma}]]\rightarrow\widehat{\mathbb{Z}}\rightarrow 0

is exact.

We conclude this subsection with a result of Garrido and Jaikin-Zapirain on the homological algebra of the completed group algebra of the profinite completion
\widehat{\Gamma}
of a virtually free group
\Gamma
, as an abstract
\Gamma
-module. We shall use it as a portal from the profinite to the abstract world, which allows us to pass derivations of
\widehat{\Gamma}
in its completed group algebra to derivations of
\Gamma
in a meaningful
\Gamma
-module.

Theorem 2.9

(Corollary 5.6 in
[
GJ22
]
)
.

Let
\Gamma
be a finitely generated virtually free group. For any prime
p
, the left
\mathbb{F}_{p}[\Gamma]
-module
\mathbb{F}_{p}[[\widehat{\Gamma}]]
is isomorphic to a direct union of free left
\mathbb{F}_{p}[\Gamma]
-modules.

We note that
R[[\widehat{\Gamma}]]
continues to be isomorphic to a direct limit of projective
R[\Gamma]
-modules for certain other finite rings, such as
\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z}
for
n
a product of distinct primes: cf.
[
GJ22
, Corollary 5.7]
. However, this shall not be necessary for our purposes, as we substitute their argument on supernatural numbers by a Bass–Serre theoretic argument (cf. Lemma
4.1
).

2.3.
A juxtaposition

Pursuant of a Bass–Serre theoretic description of groups of cohomological dimension one, Dunwoody proved in 1979 a remarkable result juxtaposing the worlds of Bass–Serre theory and group cohomology. Recall that a
left derivation
or
crossed homomorphism
of a group
\Gamma
in a left
\Gamma
-module
M
is a map
f\colon\Gamma\to M
satisfying
f(gh)=f(g)+gf(h)
for all
g,h\in\Gamma
. Similarly, a
right derivation
in a right
\Gamma
-module
M
is a map
f\colon\Gamma\to M
satisfying
f(gh)=f(g)h+f(h)
for all
g,h\in\Gamma
. Note that right derivations of
\Gamma
in a right
\Gamma
-module
M
are equivalently left derivations of the (isomorphic) opposite group
\Gamma^{\mathrm{op}}
in
M
endowed with its natural left
\Gamma^{\mathrm{op}}
-module structure. For this reason, it is inconsequential whether one works with left or right derivations; we choose right derivations out of convenience and agreement with the convention of Dicks and Dunwoody. Hence, unless stated otherwise, all derivations appearing in this article shall be assumed
right
derivations unless explicitly mentioned otherwise.

Given a right
\Gamma
-module
M
, one may form the right semi-direct product
\Gamma\ltimes M
, which is a group with underlying set
\Gamma\times M
and group operation
(g_{1},m_{1})\cdot(g_{2},m_{2})=(g_{1}g_{2},m_{1}g_{2}+m_{2})
. It comes equipped with natural projections of sets

\operatorname{proj_{1}}\colon\Gamma\ltimes M\to\Gamma\quad\text{and}\quad\operatorname{proj_{2}}\colon\Gamma\ltimes M\to M

where we note that
\operatorname{proj_{1}}
forms a homomorphism of groups but
\operatorname{proj_{2}}
might not. A
homomorphic section
of
\operatorname{proj_{1}}
is then a homomorphism of groups
F\colon\Gamma\to\Gamma\ltimes M
which satisfies
\operatorname{proj}_{1}F=\operatorname{id}_{\Gamma}
. We obtain the following alternative description of right derivations, which shall be useful in the proofs of Section
3
.

Remark 2.10
.

Let
\Gamma
be a group and
M
a right
\Gamma
-module. There exists a bijective correspondence between right derivations
f\colon\Gamma\to M
and homomorphic sections
F\colon\Gamma\to\Gamma\ltimes M
of the projection
\operatorname{proj}_{1}\colon\Gamma\ltimes M\to\Gamma
, given by
f\mapsto(\operatorname{id}_{\Gamma},f)
on derivations and
F\mapsto\operatorname{proj}_{2}F
on sections.

The analogous statement for left derivations is explained in
[
Bro94
, Section IV.2]
; we invite the reader to verify that the present form applies to right derivations. Naturally, both the equivalence of left and right derivations, as well as their equivalence to homomorphic sections of the semi-direct product
\Gamma\ltimes M
, continue to hold in the profinite category (see
[
RZ10
, Section 6.8]
, where derivations and homomorphisms are continuous and
\Gamma\ltimes M
carries the product topology. We shall make ample use of this fact in Section
3
.

In the cohomology of abstract and profinite groups, derivations and continuous derivations appear as 1-coboundaries. The first cohomology group of an abstract or profinite group is thus given by the set of its (continuous) derivations modulo its (continuous)
inner
derivations, that is, functions
\Gamma\to M
of the form
g\mapsto m(g-1)
for some
m\in M
. We refer the reader to
[
Bro94
, Section IV.2]
and
[
RZ10
, Section 6.8]
for details on the abstract and profinite versions of the theory, respectively. It was precisely this characterisation which Dunwoody used to interlink Bass–Serre theory and group cohomology.

Theorem 2.11

(Theorem 5.2 in
[
Dun79
]
)
.

Let
\Gamma
be a group and
\Delta\leq\Gamma
a subgroup such that
\Gamma
is generated by
\Delta
together with a finite set of elements. For any ring
R\neq 0
, the following are equivalent:

(1)

there exists a graph of groups
(\mathcal{G},\Xi,\Theta)
over a finite graph
\Xi
with finite edge groups satisfying
\Gamma=\Pi_{1}^{\mathrm{abs}}(\mathcal{G},\Xi,\Theta)
and
\Delta\leq\mathcal{G}(v)
for some
v\in V(\Xi)
;

(2)

there exists a non-zero derivation
f\colon\Gamma\to R[\Gamma]
such that
\Delta\leq\operatorname{Ker}(f)
.

We shall construct a profinite analogue of the direction
(a)\Rightarrow(b)
in Proposition
3.2
, which we then refine to yield Theorem
1.1
. Together with the abstract direction
(b)\Rightarrow(a)
in Theorem
2.11
, this will enable us to prove in Theorem
1.2
that certain amalgamated factors can be recognised in the profinite completions of virtually free groups. Specifically, we shall use the following refinement of the abstract direction
(b)\Rightarrow(a)
which was proven by Dicks in
[
Dic80
]
.

Theorem 2.12

(Theorem III.4.6 in
[
Dic80
]
)
.

Let
\Gamma
be a group,
R\neq 0
a ring,
P
a projective
R[\Gamma]
-module and
f\colon\Gamma\to P
a derivation. If
\Gamma
is generated by
\operatorname{Ker}(f)
together with a finite set of elements, then there exists a graph of groups
(\mathcal{G},\Xi)
with finite edge groups such that
\Gamma=\Pi_{1}^{\mathrm{abs}}(\mathcal{G},\Xi)
and
\operatorname{Ker}(f)=\mathcal{G}(v)
for some vertex
v\in V(\Xi)
.

3.
Profinite Subgroup Accessibility and Continuous Derivations

In this section, we prove the first of our two main results, Theorem
1.1
, which associates to any accessible subgroup
H
of a profinite group
G
a continuous derivation of
G
in a free module over its completed group algebra with kernel
H
. We commence with a general statement about the structure of continuous derivations on a graph of profinite groups over a finite graph.

Proposition 3.1
.

Let
(\mathcal{G},\Xi,\Theta)
be an injective graph of profinite groups over a finite graph
\Xi

G=\Pi_{1}{(\mathcal{G},\Xi,\Theta)}
and

, and let

be a profinite right
G

\{f_{v}\colon G(v)\to M\}_{v\in V(\Xi)}

(3.1)

\left(f_{d_{1}(e)}(\partial_{1}(g))-f_{T}(t_{e})\right)t_{e}^{-1}\partial_{0}(g)^{-1}t_{e}+f_{d_{0}(g)}(\partial_{0}(g)^{-1})t_{e}+f_{T}(t_{e})=0

and

which extends

Having fixed the spanning tree
\Theta

\mathcal{G}(x)
with its image
\mathcal{G}(x)\leq G=\Pi_{1}{(\mathcal{G},\Xi,\Theta)}

G=W/N
, where

W=\coprod_{v\in V(\Xi)}\mathcal{G}(v)\amalg\mathscr{F}(T)

and

, which forms a continuous homomorphism into the right semi-direct product
G\ltimes M
(cf. Remark
2.10
). On the other hand, given an edge

, define the continuous homomorphism
F_{e}\colon\overline{\langle t_{e}\rangle}\to G\ltimes M
as follows. If
e\in E(\Theta)
, then
t_{e}=1
and we set
F_{e}=0
. Otherwise, assume that

, and observe

and
\overline{\langle t_{e}\rangle}\cong\widehat{\mathbb{Z}}
as

has been constructed to include the full profinite completion
\widehat{\mathbb{Z}}\cdot t_{e}
of
\langle t_{e}\rangle
. Now
\langle t_{e}\rangle\cong\mathbb{Z}
is cohomologically good and the trivial
\langle t_{e}\rangle
-module
\mathbb{Z}
has free resolution given by

(3.2)

0\rightarrow\mathbb{Z}[\langle t_{e}\rangle]\xrightarrow{\mu}\mathbb{Z}[\langle t_{e}\rangle]\xrightarrow{\varepsilon}\mathbb{Z}\rightarrow 0

where
\mu
is multiplication by
(t_{e}-1)
. Thus, Lemma
2.8
gives a short exact sequence of abelian profinite groups

(3.3)

0\rightarrow\widehat{\mathbb{Z}}[[\overline{\langle t_{e}\rangle}]]\xrightarrow{\mu}\widehat{\mathbb{Z}}[[\overline{\langle t_{e}\rangle}]]\xrightarrow{\varepsilon}\widehat{\mathbb{Z}}\rightarrow 0

where
\mu(x)=x(t_{e}-1)
and which is split as
\widehat{\mathbb{Z}}
is free profinite. Hence, there exists a retraction
\rho
of
\mu
, and we set

F_{e}(g)=(g,\rho(g-1)\cdot f_{T}(t_{e}))

where the action of
\widehat{\mathbb{Z}}[[\overline{\langle t_{e}\rangle}]]
on
M
is induced by the action of
\widehat{\mathbb{Z}}[[G]]
. Having defined
F_{v}
and
F_{e}
, the universal property of the coproduct yields a homomorphism
F\colon W\to G\ltimes M
which extends
F_{v}
and
F_{e}
for all
v\in V(\Xi)
and
e\in E(\Xi)
. We claim that
F|_{N}=0
; for this it will suffice to show that
F
is zero on all generators of
N
as a closed normal subgroup. If
e\in E(\Theta)
then

by definition. On the other hand, if
e\in E(\Xi)-E(\Theta)
and
g\in\mathcal{G}(e)
, then
F(\partial_{1}(g)t_{e}^{-1}\partial_{0}(g)^{-1}t_{e})
evaluates as the expression in (
3.1
), which is zero by assumption. It follows that
F
factors through a continuous homomorphism
\widetilde{F}\colon G\to G\ltimes M
such that the diagrams

(3.4)

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{G}(v)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces G}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\overline{\langle t_{e}\rangle}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces G}

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces G\ltimes M}

and

With this tool at hand, we proceed to prove the profinite analogue of direction
(a)\Rightarrow(b)
in Theorem
2.11
. The proof is a transfiguration of Dunwoody’s argument, adjusted substantially to accommodate the eccentricities of the profinite world.

Proposition 3.2
.

Let
G
be a profinite group,
H\leq G
be a closed subgroup accessible within
G
via some graph of profinite groups
(\mathcal{G},\Xi,\Theta)
, and
R
be a profinite ring. For any vertex
w\in V(\Xi)
, there exists a continuous derivation
f\colon G\to R[[G]]
satisfying
\mathcal{G}(w)\cap\operatorname{Ker}(f)=\mathcal{G}(w)\cap H
.

Proof.

Let
G
be a profinite group which decomposes as a graph of groups
\Pi_{1}{(\mathcal{G},\Xi,\Theta)}
, where the spanning tree
\Theta\subseteq\Xi
is fixed and we shall write
\partial_{x}\colon\mathcal{G}(x)\to G=\Pi_{1}{(\mathcal{G},\Xi,\Theta)}
to denote the associated inclusion of profinite groups over a given point
x\in\Xi
. We note that
\partial_{x}
is indeed a monomorphism by Proposition
2.1
. Similarly, write

\widehat{\partial_{x}}\colon R[\mathcal{G}(x)]\xhookrightarrow{\iota}R[[\mathcal{G}(x)]]\xhookrightarrow{\partial^{*}_{x}}R[[G]]

where
\iota
is the profinite completion of rings and
\partial_{x}^{*}
is the map induced by
\partial_{x}
on completed group algebras.

Assume that the closed subgroup
H\leq G
is accessible, so that there is a vertex
v\in V(\Xi)
with
H=\mathcal{G}(v)
. If
v=w
then the zero derivation yields the result; assume thus
v\neq w
. Write
[v,w]
to denote the minimal subtree of
\Theta
which contains both
v
and
w
, and define
e
to be the unique edge in
[v,w]
which is incident at
v
. Given any other vertex
\tau\in V(\Xi)
, we shall say that
\tau
is positive (
\tau>v
) if
[v,\tau]
contains
e
, neutral if
\tau=v
, and negative (
\tau<v
) if it is neither positive nor neutral. Similarly, an edge
\eta
shall be positive (
\eta>e
) if both its initial and terminal vertex are positive, neutral (
\eta\approx e
) if exactly one of its initial and terminal vertices is positive, and negative (
\eta<e
) if it is neither positive nor neutral. Note that the only neutral vertex is
v
but there might be neutral edges other than
e
if
\Xi
is not a tree.

We shall define the continuous derivation
f
separately on all vertex groups and stable letters, and then demonstrate that these definitions glue together appropriately. Given a vertex
\tau\in V(\Xi)
, define the continuous map

f_{\tau}\colon\mathcal{G}(\tau)\xrightarrow{\hskip 8.19447pt}R[\mathcal{G}(\tau)]\xhookrightarrow{\widehat{\partial}_{\tau}}R[[G]]

as

f_{\tau}(g)=\sum_{k\in\mathcal{G}(e)}k(g-1)

if
\tau>v
, and

f_{\tau}=0

otherwise. We note that, depending on whether
\tau>v
or
\tau\leq v
, these maps are precisely the inner derivations associated to the elements
\sum_{k\in\mathcal{G}(e)}k\in R[[G]]
and
0\in R[[G]]
, respectively, so in particular they form continuous right derivations of
G
in
R[[G]]
(cf.
[
RZ10
, Section 6.8]
. On the other hand, let
\eta\in E(\Xi)
be an edge and
t_{\eta}
the corresponding stable letter. If
\eta\in\Theta
then
t_{\eta}=1
, and we set
f(t_{\eta})=0
. Otherwise, assume
\eta\in\Xi-\Theta
and proceed by cases depending on the sign of
\eta
. Set

f(t_{\eta})=\sum_{k\in\mathcal{G}(e)}k(t_{\eta}-1)

if
\eta>e
,

f(t_{\eta})=-\sum_{k\in\mathcal{G}(e)}k

if
\eta\approx e
, and

f(t_{\eta})=0

if
\eta<0
. In this way, we have defined continuous derivations
f_{\tau}
in
R[[G]]
on all vertex groups of
(\mathcal{G},\Xi)
, and an assignment of stable letters to values in
R[[G]]
. By Proposition
3.1
, this data induces a continuous derivation
f\colon G\to R[[G]]
, provided we show that

(3.5)

\left(f_{d_{1}(\eta)}(\partial_{1}(g))-f(t_{\eta})\right)t_{\eta}^{-1}\partial_{0}(g)^{-1}t_{\eta}+f_{d_{0}(\eta)}(\partial_{0}(g)^{-1})t_{\eta}+f(t_{\eta})=0

whenever
\eta\in E(\Xi)
and
g\in\mathcal{G}(\eta)
. Indeed, choose
\eta\in E(\Xi)
and write
\alpha=d_{0}(\eta)
and
\beta=d_{1}(\eta)
. We proceed again by cases on
\eta
. If
\eta<e
then
f(t_{\eta})=f_{\alpha}(g)=f_{\beta}(g)=0
for all
g\in\mathcal{G}(\eta)
, so there is nothing to prove. If
\eta>e
then (
3.5
) evaluates as

(3.6)

\begin{split}\sum_{k\in\mathcal{G}(e)}k(\partial_{1}(g)-1)t_{\eta}^{-1}\partial_{0}(g)^{-1}t_{\eta}-\sum_{k\in\mathcal{G}(e)}k(t_{\eta}-1)t_{\eta}^{-1}\partial_{0}(g)^{-1}t_{\eta}\\
+\sum_{k\in\mathcal{G}(e)}k(\partial_{0}(g)^{-1}-1)t_{\eta}+\sum_{k\in\mathcal{G}(e)}k(t_{\eta}-1)\end{split}

which we can re-write using the identity
\partial_{1}(g)=t_{\eta}^{-1}\partial_{0}(g)t_{\eta}
in
G
, yielding

(3.7)

\begin{split}\sum_{k\in\mathcal{G}(e)}k\Big(1-t_{\eta}^{-1}\partial_{0}(g)^{-1}t_{\eta}-\partial_{0}(g)^{-1}t_{\eta}+t_{\eta}^{-1}\partial_{0}(g)^{-1}t_{\eta}\\
+\partial_{0}(g)^{-1}t_{\eta}-t_{\eta}+t_{\eta}-1\Big)\end{split}

which reduces to zero. Finally, suppose that
\eta\approx e
and distinguish further between the cases
\eta=e
and
\eta\notin\Theta
. In the former case,
t_{\eta}=1
in
G
and
\partial_{0}=\partial_{1}
on

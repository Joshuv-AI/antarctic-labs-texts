Abstract
. We prove a lower bound estimate for capacities in Hajłasz-Besov, Hajłasz-Triebel-Lizorkin and Hajłasz-Sobolev spaces with generalized smoothness defined on metric spaces in terms of Netrusov-Hausdorff content or Hausdorff content. These results are improvements of the results obtained in [ 18 ] .



Keywords: Hajłasz-Besov space, Hajłasz-Triebel-Lizorkin space, capacity, Hausdorff content, Netrusov-Hausdorff content.

2010 Mathematics Subject Classification: 31E05, 31B15.

1.
Introduction

Function spaces with generalized smoothness have received a lot of attention in recent years due to its applications in various fields. In
[
7
]
, the authors have studied the generalized Besov spaces
B^{(\sigma,N)}_{p,q}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
and Triebel-Lizorkin spaces
F^{(\sigma,N)}_{p,q}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
for the full range of parameters, where
\sigma
is a weight sequence of positive numbers and
N
is a sequence of strictly increasing positive numbers connected a suitable partition of unity, and they are used to replace the classical smoothness
\{2^{js}\}.
Generalized Besov and Triebel-Lizorkin spaces also have been studied where smoothness is given by a parameter function, see
[
1
,
5
,
6
,
2
,
4
,
3
]
. Recently Li, Yang and Yuan have introduced Hajłasz-Sobolev spaces
M^{\phi,p}(X)
, Hajłasz-Besov spaces
N^{\phi}_{p,q}(X)
and Hajłasz-Triebel-Lizorkin spaces
M^{\phi}_{p,q}(X)
with generalized smoothness on metric spaces,
[
19
]
. These spaces have been defined using Hajłasz gradients which were first introduced by Hajłasz
[
8
]
and later defined for fractional scales independently by Hu
[
11
]
and Yang
[
27
]
and then generalized to sequence of gradients to define Besov and Triebel-Lizorkin spaces
[
17
]
. Please see Section 2 for the definition of these spaces.

In this paper we study the relation between the capacity defined on Hajłasz-Sobolev spaces
M^{\phi,p}(X)
, Hajłasz-Besov spaces
N^{\phi}_{p,q}(X)
or Hajłasz-Triebel-Lizorkin spaces
M^{\phi}_{p,q}(X)
with generalized smoothness and Netrusov-Hausdorff content (or Hausdorff content). Hajłasz-Sobolev capacity was first studied in
[
16
]
and later it was used in
[
15
]
to prove the existence of Lebesgue points of functions in Hajłasz-Sobolev space
M^{1,p}(X),

p\in(1,Q],
outside a set of Hajłasz-Sobolev capacity zero, where
X
is a doubling metric measure space and
Q
is the doubling dimension of
X.
This result leads to a series of related work on other function spaces such as fractional Hajłasz-Sobolev spaces
[
24
]
, Orlicz-Sobolev spaces
[
20
]
as well as Hajłasz-Besov and Hajłasz-Triebel-Lizorkin spaces
[
10
]
. Let us recall here the definition of the capacities on these spaces from
[
18
]
.

Definition 1.1
.

Let
0<s<\infty,

0<p,q<\infty,

\phi\in\mathcal{A}
and
\mathcal{F}\in\{M^{\phi,p},M^{\phi}_{p,q},N^{\phi}_{p,q}\}.
The
\mathcal{F}
-capacity of a set
E\subset X
is

\capacity_{\mathcal{F}}(E)=\inf\{\|u\|_{\mathcal{F}(X)}^{p}:u\in\mathcal{G_{F}}(E)\},

where

\mathcal{G_{F}}(E)=\{u\in\mathcal{F}(X):u\geq 1\,\,\text{on a neighbourhood of}\,\,E\}.

Lower bound and upper bound estimates for Hajłasz-Besov capacity (
N^{s}_{p,q}
-capacity) were found in
[
23
]
in terms of generalized Netrusov-Hausdorff content, whereas the estimates for Hajłasz-Triebel-Lizorkin capacity (
M^{s}_{p,q}
-capacity) were found in
[
13
]
in terms of generalized Hausdorff measure. These results were also extended in
[
18
]
for spaces with generalized smoothness and recently some of them have been improved in
[
14
]
. In this paper, we improve the results of
[
18
]
on the relation between the capacities and the Netrusov-Hausdorff content (or Hausdorff content) by relaxing the assumptions on the metric space. Here is our first result:

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
\phi\in\mathcal{A}_{0}
,
0<p<\infty
,
0<q<\infty
and
\omega
be any given function of admissible growth such that, for any
L\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
,

\sum_{k\geq L}\frac{1}{\omega(2^{-k})}<\infty.

Let
x_{0}\in X,

0<R<\infty.
Then there are constants
C>0
and
c>0
such that, for any compact set
E\subset B(x_{0},R),

(1.1)

\mathcal{H}^{h_{\omega},q/p}_{cR}(E)\leq C\capacity_{N^{\phi}_{p,q}}(E)

where, for any
r\in(0,R],

h_{\omega}(r)=[\phi(r)\omega(r)]^{p}.

Note that, this result is an improvement of
[
18
, Theorem 7]
in the sense that here we do not require any additional assumption of the existence of some non-empty annulus on the underlying metric space which was required for Theorem 7 of
[
18
]
. Also for Triebel-Lizorkin space, the lower bound is obtained, without the additional assumption on the underlying metric space, in terms of Hausdorff measure and the result turns out to be true as well for Sobolev spaces with generalized smoothness
M^{\phi,p}=M^{\phi}_{p,\infty}.

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
\phi\in\mathcal{A}_{0}
,
0<p<\infty
,
0<q<\infty
and
\omega
be any given function of admissible growth such that, for any
L\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
,

\sum_{k\geq L}\frac{1}{\omega(2^{-k})}<\infty.

Let
x_{0}\in X,

0<R<\infty.
Then there exist constants
c
and
C
such that, for any compact set
E\subset B(x_{0},R),

H^{h_{\omega}}_{cR}(E)\leq C\capacity_{M^{\phi}_{p,q}}(E)

where, for any
r\in(0,R],

h_{\omega}(r)=[\phi(r)\omega(r)]^{p}.

Theorem 1.4
.

Let
\phi\in\mathcal{A}_{0}
,
0<p<\infty
, and
\omega
be any given function of admissible growth such that, for any
L\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
,

\sum_{k\geq L}\frac{1}{\omega(2^{-k})}<\infty.

Let
x_{0}\in X,

0<R<\infty.
Then there exist constants
c
and
C
such that, for any compact set
E\subset B(x_{0},R),

H^{h_{\omega}}_{cR}(E)\leq C\capacity_{M^{\phi,p}}(E)

where, for any
r\in(0,R],

h_{\omega}(r)=[\phi(r)\omega(r)]^{p}.

2.
Notations and Preliminaries

We assume throughout the article that
X=(X,d,\mu)
is a doubling metric measure space equipped with a metric
d
and a doubling Borel regular measure
\mu.
Note that a Borel regular measure
\mu
on a metric space
(X,d)
is said to be a
doubling measure
if every ball in
X
has positive and finite measure and there exist a constant
c_{d}\geq 1
such that

\mu(B(x,2r))\leq c_{d}\,\mu(B(x,r))

holds for each
x\in X
and
r>0.

2.1.
Smoothness weight sequences and functions

We first recall some tools used to describe the generalized smoothness. Smoothness in terms of sequences can be found in
[
7
,
12
]
and in terms of sequences can be found in
[
18
,
19
]
.

Definition 2.1
.

Let
E\in\{\mathbb{Z},\mathbb{Z_{+}}\}.
A sequence of positive numbers
\{\sigma_{j}\}_{j\in E}
is said to be admissible if there exist two positive constants
d_{0}
and
d_{1}
such that for any
j\in E
,
d_{0}\sigma_{j}\leq\sigma_{j+1}\leq d_{1}\sigma_{j}.

Definition 2.2
.

A continuous function
\phi:[0,\infty)\rightarrow[0,\infty)
is said to be of admissible growth if
\{\phi(2^{j})\}_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}
is an admissible sequence and
\phi(t)\sim\phi(2^{k})
for any
k\in\mathbb{Z}
and
t\in[2^{k},2^{k+1})
with the positive equivalence constants independent of both
t
and
k
.

Here we recall some indices from
[
18
]
. For any given sequence
\sigma:=\{\sigma_{k}\}_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}
of positive numbers and any given function
\phi:[0,\infty)\rightarrow[0,\infty)
, let

\alpha_{\phi}:=\max\{\alpha^{-}_{\phi},\alpha^{+}_{\phi}\}:=\max\left\{\limsup_{k\rightarrow-\infty}\frac{\phi(2^{k})}{\phi(2^{k+1})},\limsup_{k\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\phi(2^{k})}{\phi(2^{k+1})}\right\}

and

\beta_{\phi}:=\max\{\beta^{-}_{\phi},\beta^{+}_{\phi}\}:=\max\left\{\limsup_{k\rightarrow-\infty}\frac{\phi(2^{k+1})}{\phi(2^{k})},\limsup_{k\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\phi(2^{k+1})}{\phi(2^{k})}\right\}.

Throughout this article, we denote by
\mathcal{A}
the class of all continuous and almost increasing functions
\phi:[0,\infty)\rightarrow[0,\infty)
such that
\phi(0)=0,\phi(1)=1,
and
\{\phi(2^{k})\}_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}
is admissible. We also denote by
\mathcal{A}_{\infty}
the class of all functions
\phi\in\mathcal{A}
so that
\frac{\phi(t)}{t}
almost decreases on
[0,\infty).
Recall that a function
f:[0,\infty)\rightarrow[0,\infty)
is said to be almost increasing (resp., almost decreasing) if there exists a positive constant
C_{I}\in[1,\infty)
such that for any
t_{1},t_{2}\in[0,\infty)
with
t_{1}\leq t_{2}
(resp.,
t_{1}\geq t_{2}
),
f(t_{1})\leq C_{I}f(t_{2}).
For any
r\in(0,\infty)
, let
\mathcal{A}_{r}
be the set of all functions
\phi\in\mathcal{A}_{\infty}
so that
\phi
is of admissible growth and there exist an integer
i
and two positive constants
X_{i}
and
Y_{i}
depending on
i
and
r,
such that

(2.1)

\left\{\sum_{j\geq i}[\phi(2^{j})]^{-r}\right\}^{\frac{1}{r}}\leq X_{i}\hskip 10.00002pt\text{and}\hskip 10.00002pt\left\{\sum_{j\geq i}2^{-jr}[\phi(2^{-j})]^{-r}\right\}^{\frac{1}{r}}\leq Y_{i}.

Also, let
\mathcal{A}_{0}
be the class of all functions
\phi
satisfying that
\alpha_{\phi}\in(0,1)
,
\beta^{-}_{\phi}\in(0,2)
and
\phi
is of admissible growth. Note that, by using the elementary inequality

(2.2)

\sum_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}a_{i}\leq\Big(\sum_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}a_{i}^{\beta}\Big)^{\frac{1}{\beta}}

which holds whenever
a_{i}\geq 0
for all
i\in\mathbb{Z}
and
0<\beta\leq 1,
we have
\mathcal{A}_{r_{1}}\subset\mathcal{A}_{r_{2}}\subset\mathcal{A}_{\infty}
for any
r_{1},r_{2}\in(0,\infty)
with
r_{1}\leq r_{2}.
Also, note that if
\phi\in\mathcal{A}_{0},
then
\phi\in\mathcal{A}_{r},
for any
r\in(0,\infty].

2.2.
Hajłasz-Sobolev, Hajłasz-Besov and Hajłasz-Triebel-Lizorkin spaces with generalized smoothness

Here we recall the definitions of
\phi
-Hajłasz gradients and the spaces with generalized smoothness from
[
19
]
.

Definition 2.3
.

A non-negative measurable function
g
is called a
\phi
-Hajłasz gradient of
u
if there exists a set
E\subset X
with
\mu(E)=0
such that, for any
x,y\in X\setminus E,

|u(x)-u(y)|\leq\phi(d(x,y))[g(x)+g(y)].

We denote the collection of all
\phi
-Hajłasz gradients of
u
by
\mathcal{D}^{\phi}(u).

Definition 2.4
.

Let
\phi\in\mathcal{A}
and
0<p\leq\infty
. The homogeneous
\phi
-Hajłasz-Sobolev space
\dot{M}^{\phi,p}(S)
consists of all measurable, almost everywhere finite function
u:S\rightarrow\bar{\mathbb{R}}
for which the semi norm

(2.3)

\|u\|_{\dot{M}^{\phi,p}(S)}=\inf_{g\in\mathcal{D}^{\phi}(u)}\|g\|_{L^{p}(S)}

is finite.
The non-homogeneous
\phi
-Hajłasz-Sobolev space
M^{\phi,p}(S)
is
\dot{M}^{\phi,p}(S)\cap L^{p}(S)
equipped with the norm

(2.4)

\|u\|_{M^{\phi,p}(S)}=\|u\|_{L^{p}(S)}+\|u\|_{\dot{M}^{\phi,p}(S)}.

Let
S\subset X
be a measurable set. For
0<p,q\leq\infty
and a sequence
\vec{f}=(f_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}
of measurable functions, we define

\|(f_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\|_{L^{p}(S,l^{q})}=\big\|\|(f_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\|_{l^{q}}\big\|_{L^{p}(S)}

and

\|(f_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\|_{l^{q}(L^{p}(S))}=\big\|(\|f_{k}\|_{L^{p}(S)})_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\big\|_{l^{q}}

where

\|(f_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\|_{l^{q}}=\begin{cases}(\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}|f_{k}|^{q})^{1/q},&~\text{when}~0<q<\infty,\\
\sup_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}|f_{k}|,&~\text{when}~q=\infty.\end{cases}

Definition 2.5
.

A sequence of non negative measurable functions,
\vec{g}:=\{g_{k}\}_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}
is called a
\phi
-Hajłasz gradient sequence of
u
if, for any
k\in\mathbb{Z},
there exists a set
E_{k}\subset X
with
\mu(E_{k})=0
such that , for any
x,y\in X\setminus E_{k},
with
2^{-k-1}\leq d(x,y)<2^{-k},

|u(x)-u(y)|\leq\phi(d(x,y))[g_{k}(x)+g_{k}(y)].

We denote the collection of all
\phi
-Hajłasz gradient sequences of
u
by
\mathbb{D}^{\phi}(u).

Definition 2.6
.

Let
\phi\in\mathcal{A}
and
0<p,q\leq\infty
. The homogeneous
\phi
-Hajłasz-Besov space
\dot{N}^{\phi}_{p,q}(S)
consists of all measurable, almost everywhere finite function
u:S\rightarrow\bar{\mathbb{R}}
for which the semi norm

(2.5)

\|u\|_{\dot{N}^{\phi}_{p,q}(S)}=\inf_{(g_{k})\in\mathbb{D}^{\phi}(u)}\|(g_{k})\|_{l^{q}(L^{p}(S))}

is finite.
The non-homogeneous
\phi
-Hajłasz-Besov space
N^{\phi}_{p,q}(S)
is
\dot{N}^{\phi}_{p,q}(S)\cap L^{p}(S)
equipped with the norm

(2.6)

\|u\|_{N^{\phi}_{p,q}(S)}=\|u\|_{L^{p}(S)}+\|u\|_{\dot{N}^{\phi}_{p,q}(S)}.

Definition 2.7
.

Let
\phi\in\mathcal{A}
and
0<p,q\leq\infty
. The homogeneous
\phi
-Hajłasz-Triebel-Lizorkin space
\dot{M}^{\phi}_{p,q}(S)
consists of all measurable, almost everywhere finite function
u:S\rightarrow\bar{\mathbb{R}}
for which the semi norm

(2.7)

\|u\|_{\dot{M}^{\phi}_{p,q}(S)}=\inf_{(g_{k})\in\mathbb{D}^{\phi}(u)}\|(g_{k})\|_{L^{p}(S,l^{q})}

is finite.
The non-homogeneous
\phi
-Hajłasz-Triebel-Lizorkin space
M^{\phi}_{p,q}(S)
is
\dot{M}^{\phi}_{p,q}(S)\cap L^{p}(S)
equipped with the norm

(2.8)

\|u\|_{M^{\phi}_{p,q}(S)}=\|u\|_{L^{p}(S)}+\|u\|_{\dot{M}^{\phi}_{p,q}(S)}.

2.3.
\gamma
-median

As our functions may fail to be locally integrable, we will use the concept of
\gamma
-median instead of integral averages. We recall here the definition and some of its properties which from
[
10
,
25
,
23
]
.

Definition 2.8
.

Let
0<\gamma\leq 1/2.
The
\gamma
-median
m_{u}^{\gamma}(A)
of a measurable, almost everywhere finite function
u
over a set
A\subset X
of finite measure is

m_{u}^{\gamma}(A)=\sup\{M\in\mathbb{R}:\mu(\{x\in A:u(x)<M\})\leq\gamma\mu(A)\}.

Lemma 2.9
.

Let
0<\gamma\leq 1/2
and
A\subset X.
Then for two measurable functions
u,v:A\rightarrow\mathbb{R},
the
\gamma
-median has the following properties:

(a)
If
\gamma\leq\gamma^{\prime},
then
m_{u}^{\gamma^{\prime}}(A)\leq m_{u}^{\gamma}(A).

(b)
If
u\leq v
almost everywhere, then
m_{u}^{\gamma}(A)\leq m_{v}^{\gamma}(A).

(c)
If
A\subset B
and
\mu(B)\leq C\mu(A),
then
m_{u}^{\gamma}(A)\leq m_{u}^{\gamma/C}(B).

(d)
If
c\in\mathbb{R},
then
m_{u}^{\gamma}(A)+c=m_{u+c}^{\gamma}(A).

(e)
If
c\in\mathbb{R},
then
m_{cu}^{\gamma}(A)=cm_{u}^{\gamma}(A).

(f)

|m_{u}^{\gamma}(A)|\leq m_{|u|}^{\gamma}(A).

(g)
If
u\in L^{p}(A),

p>0,
then

m_{|u|}^{\gamma}(A)\leq\left(\gamma^{-1}\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$}}\kern-4.86108pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$}}\kern-3.43057pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.908pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.76045pt}}\!\int_{A}|u|^{p}\,d\mu\right)^{1/p}.

(h)
If
u
is continuous, then for every
x\in X,

\lim_{r\rightarrow 0}m_{u}^{\gamma}(B(x,r))=u(x).

We also recall the notion of discrete
\gamma
-median convolution introduced in
[
10
]
.

Definition 2.10

(
[
18
]
)
.

Let
\gamma\in(0,1/2]
and
u\in L^{0}(X).
The discrete
\gamma
- median convolution
u^{\gamma}_{r}
of
u
at scale
r\in(0,\infty)
is defined by setting, for any
x\in X

u_{r}^{\gamma}(x):=\sum_{j\in\mathcal{J}}m^{\gamma}_{u}(B_{j})\varphi_{j}(x),

where
\mathcal{J}
is an index set,
\{B_{j}\}_{j\in\mathcal{J}}
is a cover of
X
with the radius
r
such that
\sum_{j\in\mathcal{J}}\chi_{2B_{j}}
is finite and
\{\varphi_{j}\}_{j\in\mathcal{J}}
is a partition of unity with respect to
\{B_{j}\}_{j\in\mathcal{J}}.

2.4.
Hausdorff content

Netrusov-Hausdorff content was first introduced by Netrusov
[
21
,
22
]
and later it was generalized using an increasing function
h
in
[
23
]
. For the generalized Hausdorff content (and measure), we refer to
[
26
]
.

Definition 2.11
.

Let
0<\theta<\infty,

0<R<\infty
and
h:(0,\infty)\rightarrow(0,\infty)
be an increasing function. The generalized Netrusov-Hausdorff content of a set
E\subset X
is

\mathcal{H}^{h,\theta}_{R}(E)=\inf\Big[\sum_{i:2^{-i}<R}\Big(\sum_{j\in I_{i}}\frac{\mu(B(x_{j},r_{j}))}{h(r_{j})}\Big)^{\theta}\Big]^{\frac{1}{\theta}},

where
I_{i}=\{j\in\mathbb{N}:2^{-i}\leq r_{j}<2^{-i+1}\}
and the infimum is taken over all coverings
\{B(x_{j},r_{j})\}
of
E
with
0<r_{j}\leq R.
If
R=\infty,
then we take the infimum over all coverings of
E
and the first sum is over all
i\in\mathbb{Z}.

Definition 2.12
.

Let
0<R\leq\infty
and
h:(0,\infty)\rightarrow(0,\infty)
be an increasing function. The generalized Hausdorff content of a set
E\subset X
is

H^{h}_{R}(E)=\inf\Big\{\sum_{j\in\mathbb{N}}\frac{\mu(B(x_{j},r_{j}))}{h(r_{j})}:E\subset\bigcup B(x_{j},r_{j}),r_{j}\leq R\Big\}

and the generalized Hausdorff measure is
H^{h}(E)=\limsup_{R\rightarrow 0}H^{h}_{R}(E).

Note that
\mathcal{H}^{h,1}_{R}(E)=H^{h}_{R}.
Also by using the elementary inequality (
2.2
)
we have
\mathcal{H}^{h,\theta}_{R}(E)\leq H^{h}_{R}(E)
if
\theta>1
and
\mathcal{H}^{h,\theta}_{R}(E)\geq H^{h}_{R}(E)
if
\theta<1.

3.
Proofs

We will first prove two lemmas (Lemma
3.1
and Lemma
3.4
) to restrict the set of admissible functions for the capacities to locally Lipschitz functions. The proof of Lemma
3.1
follows by extending the proof of
[
23
, Theorem 4.8]
and using the convergence of approximations by discrete
\gamma
-median convolutions,
[
18
, Theorem 4]
. We give the details here for the sake of completeness.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
\phi\in\mathcal{A}_{0}
,
0<p,q<\infty,\hskip 10.00002pt\mathcal{F}\in\{M^{\phi}_{p,q}(X),N^{\phi}_{p,q}(X)\}
and
E\subseteq X
be a compact set. Then

(3.1)

\capacity_{\mathcal{F}}(E)\approx\inf\{\|u\|^{p}_{\mathcal{F}}:u\in\mathcal{\tilde{G}}_{\mathcal{F}}(E)\},

where
\mathcal{\tilde{G}}_{\mathcal{F}}(E)=\{u\in\mathcal{G}_{\mathcal{F}}(E):u
is locally Lipschitz
\}.

Proof.

Since
\mathcal{\tilde{G}_{F}}(E)\subset\mathcal{G_{F}}(E)
, it is enough to prove the
``\gtrsim"
part. Let
u\in\mathcal{G}_{\mathcal{F}}(E).
Then there is an open set
U\supset E
such that
u\geq 1
in
U.
Let
V=\{x:d(x,E)<d(E,X\setminus U)/2\}.
If
x\in V
and
r<d(E,X\setminus U)/8,
then
B(y,2r)\subset U
whenever
x\in B(y,2r).
So,
u_{r}^{\gamma}\geq 1
in
V
when
r<d(E,X\setminus U)/8.
So,
u_{r}^{\gamma}\in\mathcal{\tilde{G}}_{\mathcal{F}}(E)
for small
r
and hence by the convergence of approximations by discrete
\gamma
-median convolutions
[
18
, Theorem 4]
,

\displaystyle\inf\{\|v\|^{p}_{\mathcal{F}}:v\in\mathcal{\tilde{G}_{F}}(E)\}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\lim\inf_{i\rightarrow\infty}\|u^{\gamma}_{2^{-i}}\|^{p}_{\mathcal{F}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\lim\inf_{i\rightarrow\infty}C(\|u\|^{p}_{\mathcal{F}}+\|u^{\gamma}_{2^{-i}}-u\|^{p}_{\mathcal{F}})

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\|u\|^{p}_{\mathcal{F}}.

The claim follows by taking the infimum over
u\in\mathcal{G}_{\mathcal{F}}(E).

∎

Note that, when
q=\infty,
the approximation by the
\gamma
-median convolutions is not always true, even for the function
\phi(t)=t;
see
[
10
, Example 3.5]
for a counterexample. In this case, we use the denseness of the
\phi
-Lipschitz class of functions, proved in
[
18
, Theorem 2]
. Recall that for given
\phi\in\mathcal{A},
a function
u
on
X
is said to be in the
\phi
-Lipschitz class
\Lip_{\phi}(X)
if there exists a constant
C>0
such that for any two points
x
and
y
in
X,
there holds

|u(x)-u(y)|\leq C\phi(d(x,y)).

Theorem 3.2
.

[
18
, Theorem 2]

Let
\phi
be a modulus of continuity and
p\in(0,\infty).
Then
\Lip_{\phi}(X)\cap M^{\phi,p}(X)
is a dense subset of
M^{\phi,p}(X).

We also need the following result to prove Lemma
3.4
.

Lemma 3.3
.

[
18

On The Weak Harnack Estimate For Nonlocal Equations
Harsh Prasad

Abstract
We prove a weak Harnack estimate for a class of doubly nonlinear nonlocal equations modelled on the nonlocal Trudinger equation ∂ t ( | u | p − 2 ​ u ) + ( − Δ p ) s ​ u = 0 \displaystyle\displaystyle\partial_{t}(|u|^{p-2}u)+(-\Delta_{p})^{s}u=0 for p ∈ ( 1 , ∞ ) \displaystyle p\in(1,\infty) and s ∈ ( 0 , 1 ) \displaystyle s\in(0,1) . Our proof relies on expansion of positivity arguments developed by DiBenedetto, Gianazza and Vespri adapted to the nonlocal setup. Even in the linear case of the nonlocal heat equation and in the time-independent case of fractional p − \displaystyle p- Laplace equation, our approach provides an alternate route to Harnack estimates without using Moser iteration, log estimates or Krylov-Safanov covering arguments.

Introduction

1.1
The Problem

We prove weak Harnack estimates for positive super-solutions to the nonlocal Trudinger equation

\partial_{t}(|u|^{p-2}u)-Lu=0\text{ weakly in }E_{T}

(1.1)

where
\displaystyle E_{T}=E\times(-T,T]
for some open set
\displaystyle E\subset\mathbb{R}^{N}
and some
\displaystyle T>0
;
\displaystyle L
is a nonlocal operator modelled on the fractional
\displaystyle p-
Laplacian given by

Lu(x,t)=\text{P.V.}\int\limits\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}K(x,y,t)|u(x,t)-u(y,t)|^{p-2}(u(x,t)-u(y,t))\,dy

(1.2)

where the kernel
\displaystyle K:(-T,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{N}\times\mathbb{R}^{N}\rightarrow[0,\infty)
is a measurable function satisfying

K(x,y,t)=K(y,x,t)

(1.3)

and

\lambda|x-y|^{-N-sp}\leq K(x,y,t)\leq\Lambda|x-y|^{-N-sp}

(1.4)

for some
\displaystyle 0<\lambda\leq\Lambda
,
\displaystyle p\in(1,\infty)
and
\displaystyle s\in(0,1)
. The general equation is modelled after the nonlocal Trudinger equation

\partial_{t}(|u|^{p-2}u)+(-\Delta_{p})^{s}u=0.

(1.5)

In particular for the case
\displaystyle p=2
, we get the nonlocal heat equation and in the time-independent case we get the fractional
\displaystyle p-
Laplacian.

2.2
Shrinking Lemma

As we are working with nonlocal equations, we do not have any bounds on the gradient of the solution and so we cannot apply the usual De Giorgi isoperimetric inequality to control jumps for the solutions. In fact, such an inequality cannot be true due to the presence of a jump term (see
[
3
, Theorem 4]
.) On the other hand, it was observed in
[
2
]
that the isoperimetric inequality for nonlocal equations is a property of the solution itself and can be obtained by a more careful energy estimate. The following can be found in
[
2
, Lemma 3.4]

Lemma 2.5
.

Suppose that for some level
\displaystyle m
, some fraction
\displaystyle f\in(0,1)
and all time levels
\displaystyle\tau
in some interval
\displaystyle J
we have

|[u(\cdot,\tau)>m]\cap K_{\rho}|\geq f|K_{\rho}|

and we can arrange that

\displaystyle\displaystyle\int\limits_{J}\int\limits_{K_{\rho}}(u-l)_{-}(x,t)\int\limits_{K_{A\rho}}\frac{(u-l)_{+}^{p-1}(y,t)}{|x-y|^{N+sp}}\,dy\,dx\,dt\leq C_{1}\frac{l^{p}}{\rho^{sp}}|Q|

(2.1)

where

l=\frac{m}{2^{j}}\qquad j\geq 1\qquad\text{ and }\qquad Q=K_{\rho}\times J

then we have

\left|\left[u<\frac{m}{2^{j+1}}\right]\cap Q\right|\leq\frac{1}{f}\left(\frac{C}{2^{j}-1}\right)^{p-1}|Q|.

where
\displaystyle C>0
depends only on
\displaystyle C_{1},A
and
\displaystyle N
.

4.2
Expansion of positivity in time

Lemma 4.4
.

Let
\displaystyle M>0
and
\displaystyle\alpha\in(0,1)
. Then, there exist
\displaystyle\delta
and
\displaystyle\epsilon
in
\displaystyle(0,1)
,
depending only on the data and
\displaystyle\alpha
, such that whenever
\displaystyle u
is a globally non-negative weak supersolution to
Eq.

1.1
in
\displaystyle E_{T}
satisfying

\Big|\Big\{u(\cdot,t_{o})\geq M\Big\}\cap K_{\varrho}(x_{o})\Big|\geq\alpha\big|K_{\varrho}\big|,

\Big|\Big\{u(\cdot,t)\geq\epsilon M\Big\}\cap K_{\varrho}(x_{o})\Big|\geq\frac{\alpha}{2}|K_{\varrho}|\quad\mbox{ for all $\displaystyle t\in(t_{o},t_{o}+\delta\varrho^{sp}]$.}

(4.8)

Further the expansion parameter
\displaystyle\delta
and the reduction parameter
\displaystyle\epsilon
depends on the scaling parameter
\displaystyle\alpha
as

\delta=B_{2}^{-1}\alpha^{p+1}\quad\text{and}\quad\epsilon=B_{3}^{-1}\alpha

(4.9)

for a constants
\displaystyle B_{2},B_{3}>1
depending only on the data.

Proof.

Without loss of generality assume that
\displaystyle(x_{o},t_{o})=(0,0)
. For
\displaystyle k>0
and
\displaystyle t>0
set

A_{k,\rho}(t)=[u(\cdot,t)<k]\cap K_{\rho}.

By hypothesis,

|A_{M,\rho}(0)|\leq(1-\alpha)|K_{\rho}|.

We consider the energy estimate for
\displaystyle(u-k)_{-}
with
\displaystyle k=M
over the cylinder
\displaystyle K_{\rho}\times(0,\delta\rho^{sp}]
where
\displaystyle\delta>0
will be chosen later. Note that
\displaystyle(u-k)_{-}\leq M
globally because of the non-negativity of
\displaystyle u
. For
\displaystyle\sigma\in(0,1/8]
to be chosen later, we take a cutoff function
\displaystyle\zeta=\zeta(x)
, nonnegative such that it is supported in
\displaystyle K_{(1-\sigma/2)\rho}
,
\displaystyle\zeta=1
on
\displaystyle K_{(1-\sigma)\rho}
and
\displaystyle|\partial\zeta|\leq 2(\sigma\rho)^{-1}
to get

\displaystyle\displaystyle\int\limits_{K_{\varrho}\times\{t\}}\int\limits_{u}^{k}|s|^{p-2}(s-k)_{-}\,ds\zeta^{p}

\displaystyle\displaystyle\,dx

\displaystyle\displaystyle\leq\frac{\gamma M^{p}}{(\sigma\rho)^{sp}}\delta\rho^{sp}|K_{\rho}|

\displaystyle\displaystyle+\int\limits_{K_{\varrho}\times\{0\}}\int\limits_{u}^{k}|s|^{p-2}(s-k)_{-}\,ds\zeta^{p}\,dx

\displaystyle\displaystyle+C\underset{\stackrel{{\scriptstyle t\in(0,\delta\rho^{sp}]}}{{x\in\text{supp }\zeta}}}{\esssup}\int\limits_{K_{\rho}^{c}}\frac{(u-k)_{-}^{p-1}(y,t)}{|x-y|^{N+sp}}\,dy\int\limits_{0}^{\delta\rho^{sp}}\int\limits_{K_{\rho}}(u-k)_{-}(x,t)\zeta(x,t)\,dx\,dt

Using
\displaystyle u\geq 0
we get

\displaystyle\displaystyle\int\limits_{K_{\varrho}\times\{0\}}\int\limits_{u}^{k}|s|^{p-2}(s-k)_{-}\,ds\zeta^{p}\,dx\leq|A_{M,\rho}(0)|\int\limits_{0}^{k}|s|^{p-2}(s-k)_{-}\,ds\leq(1-\alpha)|K_{\varrho}|\int\limits_{0}^{k}|s|^{p-2}(s-k)_{-}\,ds.

For the Tail term, we follow what was described in
Section

3.2
to get

\displaystyle\displaystyle\underset{\stackrel{{\scriptstyle t\in(0,\delta\rho^{sp}]}}{{x\in\text{supp }\zeta}}}{\esssup}\int\limits_{K_{\rho}^{c}}\frac{(u-k)_{-}^{p-1}(y,t)}{|x-y|^{N+sp}}\,dy\leq c\sigma^{-(N+sp)}\frac{M^{p-1}}{\varrho^{sp}}

for a universal constant
\displaystyle c>0
.
We estimate from below

\displaystyle\displaystyle\int\limits_{K_{\varrho}\times\{t\}}\int\limits_{u}^{k}|s|^{p-2}(s-k)_{-}\,ds\zeta^{p}\,dx\geq\big|A_{k_{\epsilon},(1-\sigma)\varrho}(t)\big|\int\limits_{k_{\epsilon}}^{k}|s|^{p-2}(s-k)_{-}\,ds

where

k_{\epsilon}=\epsilon M

and
\displaystyle\epsilon\in(0,\frac{1}{2})
will be chosen later. We now use

\frac{1}{2}M\leq(1-\epsilon)M=k-k_{\epsilon}\leq|k_{\epsilon}|+|k|\leq 2M

to estimate from below again

\displaystyle\displaystyle\int\limits_{k_{\epsilon}}^{k}|s|^{p-2}(s-k)_{-}\,ds=\tfrac{1}{p-1}\,\mathfrak{g}_{-}(k_{\epsilon},k)\geq\tfrac{1}{\boldsymbol{\gamma}(p)}\big(|k_{\epsilon}|+|k|\big)^{p-2}(k-k_{\epsilon})^{2}\geq\tfrac{1}{\boldsymbol{\gamma}(p)}M^{p}.

(4.10)

Next, we note that

\displaystyle\displaystyle\big|A_{k_{\epsilon},\varrho}(t)\big|

\displaystyle\displaystyle=\big|A_{k_{\epsilon},(1-\sigma)\varrho}(t)\cup(A_{k_{\epsilon},\varrho}(t)\setminus A_{k_{\epsilon},(1-\sigma)\varrho}(t))\big|

\displaystyle\displaystyle\leq\big|A_{k_{\epsilon},(1-\sigma)\varrho}(t)\big|+|K_{\varrho}\setminus K_{(1-\sigma)\varrho}|

\displaystyle\displaystyle\leq\big|A_{k_{\epsilon},(1-\sigma)\varrho}(t)\big|+N\sigma|K_{\varrho}|.

Putting together the above estimates gives

\displaystyle\displaystyle|A_{k_{\epsilon},\varrho}(t)|\leq\frac{\int\limits_{0}^{k}|s|^{p-2}(s-k)_{-}\,ds}{\int\limits_{k_{\epsilon}}^{k}|s|^{p-2}(s-k)_{-}\,ds}(1-\alpha)|K_{\varrho}|+\frac{\boldsymbol{\gamma}\delta}{\sigma^{p}}|K_{\varrho}|+N\sigma|K_{\varrho}|

for a universal constant
\displaystyle\boldsymbol{\gamma}>0
. We write

\frac{\int\limits_{0}^{k}|s|^{p-2}(s-k)_{-}\,ds}{\int\limits_{k_{\epsilon}}^{k}|s|^{p-2}(s-k)_{-}\,ds}=1+\frac{\int\limits_{0}^{k_{\epsilon}}|s|^{p-2}(s-k)_{-}\,ds}{\int\limits_{k_{\epsilon}}^{k}|s|^{p-2}(s-k)_{-}\,ds}=1+I_{\epsilon}.

Using
\displaystyle|k_{\epsilon}|\leq M
and applying
Lemma

2.1
we get

\int\limits_{0}^{k_{\epsilon}}|\tau|^{p-2}(\tau-k)_{-}\,d\tau\leq M\int\limits_{0}^{k_{\epsilon}}|\tau|^{p-2}\,d\tau=M|s|^{p-2}s\Big|_{0}^{k_{\epsilon}}\leq\boldsymbol{\gamma}(p)M^{p}\epsilon.

And so using
Eq.

4.10

\displaystyle\displaystyle I_{\epsilon}\leq\boldsymbol{\gamma}(p)\epsilon.

We choose
\displaystyle\epsilon\in(0,1)
small enough such that

(1-\alpha)(1+\boldsymbol{\gamma}\epsilon)\leq 1-\tfrac{3}{4}\alpha.

and define
\displaystyle\sigma:=\frac{\alpha}{8N}
.
Finally, we choose
\displaystyle\delta\in(0,1)
small enough so that

\frac{\boldsymbol{\gamma}\delta}{\sigma^{p}}\leq\frac{\alpha}{8}.

The conclusion follows.
∎

n=1+\ln\sigma^{-\frac{1}{\ln 2}}=1+\log_{\frac{1}{\eta^{\prime}}}\sigma^{\frac{\ln\eta^{\prime}}{\ln 2}}.

Thus recalling the polynomial dependence of
\displaystyle\epsilon
and
\displaystyle\sigma
on the parameter
\displaystyle\alpha
we obtain the existence of
\displaystyle\eta_{0}\in(0,1)
and
\displaystyle d>1
depending only on the data such the the conclusion holds.
∎

5.2
Proof of Weak Harnack

Without loss of generality assume that
\displaystyle(x_{0},t_{0})=(0,0)
. We fix the constants
\displaystyle d>1
and
\displaystyle\eta_{0},\delta_{0}\in(0,1)

Proposition

5.1
. Recall that they depend only on the data. Let

\lambda_{0}\quad=\underset{K_{2\rho}\times(\delta_{0}(\rho/2)^{sp},\delta_{0}\rho^{sp}]}{\essinf}u.

\int\limits_{K_{\rho}}u^{q}(\cdot,0)\,dx\leq\lambda_{0}^{q}|K_{\rho}|+q\int\limits_{\lambda_{0}}^{\infty}M^{q-1}|\{u(\cdot,0)>M\}\cap K_{\rho}|\,dM.

\lambda_{0}\geq\eta_{0}M\left(\frac{|\{u(\cdot,0)>M\}\cap K_{\rho}|}{|K_{\rho}|}\right)^{d}.

q\int\limits_{\lambda_{0}}^{\infty}M^{q-1}|\{u(\cdot,0)>M\}\cap K_{\rho}|\,dM\leq\frac{q\lambda_{0}^{\frac{1}{d}}}{\eta_{0}^{\frac{1}{d}}}|K_{\rho}|\int\limits_{\lambda_{0}}^{\infty}M^{q-\frac{1}{d}-1}\,dM.

Thus

\fint\limits_{K_{\rho}}u^{q}(\cdot,0)\,dx\leq\lambda_{0}^{q}\left(1+\frac{q}{\eta_{0}^{\frac{1}{d}}(\frac{1}{d}-q)}\right).

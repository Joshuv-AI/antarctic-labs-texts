Two-flavor color superconducting quark stars may not exist
Wen-Li Yuan, Ang Li

Abstract
Large uncertainties in the determinations of the equation of state of dense stellar matter allow the intriguing possibility that the bulk quark matter in beta equilibrium might be the true ground state of the matter at zero pressure. And quarks will form Cooper pairs very readily since the dominant interaction between quarks is attractive in some channels. As a result, quark matter will generically exhibit color superconductivity, with the favored pairing pattern at intermediately high densities being two-flavor pairing. In the light of several possible candidates for such self-bound quark stars, including the very low-mass central compact object in supernova remnant HESS J1731-347 reported recently, we carry out one field-theoretic model, the Nambu–Jona-Lasinio model, of investigation on the stability of beta-stable two-flavor color superconducting (2SC) phase of quark matter, nevertheless find no physically-allowed parameter space for the existence of 2SC quark stars.



I
Introduction

It is generally believed that the degree of freedom of dense matter is hadronic around the nuclear saturation density,
\rho_{0}\sim 0.16\;\text{fm}^{-3}
, which undergoes a chiral phase transition or deconfinement phase transition at supranuclear densities to become quark matter.
A recent analysis of the supernova remnant HESS J1731-347 suggested that the mass and radius of the central compact object within it might be
M=0.77^{+0.20}_{-0.17}\,M_{\odot}
and
R=10.4^{+0.86}_{-0.78}\hbox{$\,{\rm km}$}
, respectively
(
Doroshenko et al. 2022
)
.
The possible existence of ultra-low-mass compact stars has sparked interest in speculating about self-bound quark matter as the potential physical nature of pulsar-like objects
(
Bodmer 1971
;
Witten 1984
)
.
For example, based on the mass-radius relations obtained from the MIT bag model
and Nambu–Jona-Lasinio (NJL) model, as well as the constant speed of sound parametrization,

Di Clemente et al. 2022
demonstrated that this compact star might be a strange star,
while further indicating an existing astrophysical path that could lead to the formation of such an object.
Nonstrange quark stars compatible with the compact object HESS J1731-347 were studied in
(
Restrepo et al. 2023
)
.
The study employed a developed resummation technique known as renormalization group optimized perturbation theory (RGOPT) to determine the equation of state (EOS) describing nonstrange cold quark matter at next-to-leading order.

Nevertheless, as quantum chromodynamics (QCD) predicts an attractive force between quarks in the color-antitriplet channel, it is expected that quarks near Fermi surfaces will form Cooper pairs, resulting in color superconductivity.
And the color-flavor locked (CFL) phase is anticipated as the favored pairing pattern at asymptotic densities
(
Alford et al. 1998
;
Alford et al. 1999
;
Alford et al. 2008
)
.
Along this line, the CFL quark stars have been studied within MIT bag models in light of the compact object in the HESS J1731-347.
Horvath et al. 2023
showed that candidate XMMU J173203.3-344518 might fit into a strange star scenario that could be
also consistent with heavier compact stars using the same quark EOS in the MIT bag model, and discussed a possible scenario where a small gap
(\Delta\leq 1\;\text{MeV})
in the CFL phase would explain the high surface temperature of XMMU J173203.3-344518.

Oikonomou & Moustakidis 2023
indicated that the parameter space could be further confined to the additional requirement for a high maximum mass
(M_{\rm{TOV}}\geq 2.6\,M_{\odot})

(
Abbott et al. 2020
)
, without violating the conformal bound
(
Fujimoto et al. 2022
)
.

In this study, using the NJL-type model that incorporates quark-antiquark vector interaction and scalar diquark interaction at the microcosmic level, we aim to explore the stability of two-flavor color superconducting (2SC) quark matter, which is suggested to exist at intermediate densities associated with compact stars
(
Alford 2001
;
Alford et al. 2008
)
.
We take into account the charge neutrality conditions that must be imposed for a stable bulk phase.
Previous studies have investigated the consequences of maintaining electric and color neutrality in the color superconducting phase.
Existing research has primarily focused on fundamental aspects like the size of the pairing gap or the free energy of the system
(
Rajagopal & Wilczek 2001
;
Steiner et al. 2002
;
Huang et al. 2003
;
Mishra & Mishra 2004
;
Steiner 2005
;
Blaschke et al. 2005
;
Rüster et al. 2005
)
.
They, however, have not questioned the absolute stability of bulk 2SC quark matter, where the energy per baryon should be lower than that of a
{}^{56}\rm Fe
nucleus
E/A(p=0)=(56m_{N}-56\times 8.8\;\text{MeV})/56=930\;\text{MeV}

(
Bodmer 1971
;
Witten 1984
)

II
The formalism

II.1
The two-flavor NJL-type model

The SU(2) NJL-type model Lagrangian density, with the inclusion of quark-antiquark scalar and vector interactions and scalar diquark interaction, has the form as
(
Klevansky 1992
;
Buballa 2005
)
:

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}=

\displaystyle\bar{q}\left(i\gamma^{\mu}\partial_{\mu}-m_{0}+\mu\gamma^{0}\right)q+G_{S}\left[(\bar{q}q)^{2}+\left(\bar{q}i\gamma_{5}\vec{\tau}q\right)^{2}\right]

(1)

\displaystyle-

\displaystyle G_{V}\left(\bar{q}\gamma^{\mu}q\right)^{2}+G_{D}\left[\left(\bar{q}i\gamma_{5}\tau_{2}\lambda_{A}q_{c}\right)\left(\bar{q}_{c}i\gamma_{5}\tau_{2}\lambda_{A}q\right)\right]\ .

Here,
q_{c}=C\bar{q}^{T},\ \bar{q}_{c}=q^{T}C
are charge-conjugate spinors,
C=i\gamma^{2}\gamma^{0}
is the charge conjugation matrix (the superscript
T
denotes the transposition operation), the quark field
q\equiv q_{i\alpha}
with
i=1,2
and
\alpha=1,2,3
is a flavor doublet and color triplet, as well as a four-component Dirac spinor,
\lambda_{A}
is asymmetric matrix in the color space.
The effective coupling constants
G_{S}
,
G_{V}
, and
G_{D}
are independent parameters that convey information about the strength of the scalar, vector interactions, and the diquark interaction near the Fermi surface, respectively.

\mu
is the quark chemical potential. When electric and color charge neutrality is considered, the chemical potential
\mu
is a diagonal
6\times 6
matrix in flavor and color space, which can be expressed as
\mu=\operatorname{diag}\left(\mu_{ur},\mu_{ug},\mu_{ub},\mu_{dr},\mu_{dg},\mu_{db}\right)
.
m_{0}
is the diagonal mass matrix for quarks in flavor and color space, which contains
the small current quark masses and introduces a small explicit chiral symmetry breaking.

Following the Pauli principle, the diquark condensate demands the operator between two fermion fields in the diquark condensate to be asymmetric in Dirac, flavor, and color space. As a result, the diquark condensate can be represented as
\Delta\propto\varepsilon_{ij}\varepsilon^{\alpha\beta 3}
, where the Latin indices denote the flavors and the Greek indices represent the colors. The number “3" signifies the chosen direction in color space.
We opt for blue as the preferred direction, thereby only the red and green colors participate in the condensate, while the blue one does not. Thus, the specific asymmetric operator is denoted as
\lambda_{A}=\lambda_{2}
.

Under the mean-field approximation, the Lagrangian can be written as:

\displaystyle\widetilde{\mathcal{L}}

\displaystyle=\bar{q}\left(i\gamma^{\mu}\partial_{\mu}-M+\tilde{\mu}\gamma^{0}\right)q

(2)

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2}\Delta^{*}\left(i\bar{q}_{c}\gamma_{5}\tau_{2}\lambda_{2}q\right)-\frac{1}{2}\Delta\left(i\bar{q}\gamma_{5}\tau_{2}\lambda_{2}q_{c}\right)

\displaystyle-\frac{(m_{0}-M)^{2}}{4G_{S}}+\frac{(\mu-\tilde{\mu})^{2}}{4G_{V}}-\frac{\Delta^{*}\Delta}{4G_{D}}\ .

Here, we introduce the constituent quark mass
M
and effective quark chemical potential
\tilde{\mu}
as follows:

\displaystyle M

\displaystyle=m_{0}-2G_{S}\sigma,\quad\sigma=\langle\bar{q}q\rangle\ ,

(3)

\displaystyle\tilde{\mu}

\displaystyle=\mu-2G_{V}n,\quad n=\left\langle\bar{q}\gamma^{0}q\right\rangle\ ,

\displaystyle\Delta

\displaystyle=-2G_{D}\left\langle\bar{q}_{c}i\gamma_{5}\tau_{2}\lambda_{2}q\right\rangle\ ,

\displaystyle\Delta^{*}

\displaystyle=-2G_{D}\left\langle\bar{q}i\gamma_{5}\tau_{2}\lambda_{2}q_{c}\right\rangle\ .

II.2
The thermodynamic potential

In a star containing quark matter, it is necessary to ensure both beta equilibrium and charge neutrality.
The stellar matter is composed of quarks with the charge neutrality maintained by the inclusion of electrons.
Various quark chemical potentials are determined by their electric and color charges, given by
\mu_{i}=\mu-Q_{e}\mu_{e}+T_{3}\mu_{3c}+T_{8}\mu_{8c}
, with
Q_{e}
,
T_{3}
, and
T_{8}
representing the generators of
U(1)_{Q}
,
U(1)_{3}
, and
U(1)_{8}
, respectively.
Since the diquark condenses in the blue color direction and the other two colored quarks are degenerate, assuming
\mu_{3c}=0
is safe
(
Huang et al. 2003
;
Ebert et al. 2007
)
.
The explicit expressions for the quark chemical potentials for each color and flavor are:

\displaystyle\mu_{ur}=\mu_{ug}=\frac{1}{3}\mu_{B}-\frac{2}{3}\mu_{e}+\frac{1}{3}\mu_{8c}\ ,

(4)

\displaystyle\mu_{dr}=\mu_{dg}=\frac{1}{3}\mu_{B}+\frac{1}{3}\mu_{e}+\frac{1}{3}\mu_{8c}\ ,

\displaystyle\mu_{ub}=\frac{1}{3}\mu_{B}-\frac{2}{3}\mu_{e}-\frac{2}{3}\mu_{8c}\ ,

\displaystyle\mu_{db}=\frac{1}{3}\mu_{B}+\frac{1}{3}\mu_{e}-\frac{2}{3}\mu_{8c}\ .

For quarks of the same flavor, the difference in chemical potentials between the red and green-colored quarks and the blue-colored quark is induced by
\mu_{8c}
, while the difference in chemical potentials between
u
and
d
quarks, for the same color, is induced by
\mu_{e}
.
Hereafter for convenience, we define the mean chemical potential
\bar{\mu}
for the pairing quarks, i.e.,
q_{ur},\ q_{dg}
, and
q_{ug},\ q_{dr}
:

\displaystyle\bar{\mu}=\frac{\mu_{ur}+\mu_{dg}}{2}=\frac{\mu_{ug}+\mu_{dr}}{2}=\frac{1}{3}\mu_{B}-\frac{1}{6}\mu_{e}+\frac{1}{3}\mu_{8c}\ ,

(5)

and the difference of the chemical potential
\delta\mu
as:

\displaystyle\delta\mu=\frac{\mu_{dg}-\mu_{ur}}{2}=\frac{\mu_{dr}-\mu_{ug}}{2}=\frac{\mu_{e}}{2}\ .

(6)

With the help of the energy projectors for massive particles
(
Huang et al. 2002
;
Huang et al. 2003
)
, the thermodynamic potential of the 2SC quark-matter system can be derived as (see details in Appendix):

\displaystyle\Omega_{q}

\displaystyle=\frac{(m_{0}-M)^{2}}{4G_{S}}-\frac{\left(\mu-\tilde{\mu}\right)^{2}}{4G_{V}}+\frac{\Delta^{2}}{4G_{D}}

(7)

\displaystyle-2\int\frac{d^{3}\boldsymbol{p}}{(2\pi)^{3}}\left\{2E_{\boldsymbol{p}}+2E_{\Delta}^{+}+2E_{\Delta}^{-}\right.

\displaystyle+T\ln\left[1+\exp\left(-\beta E_{ub}^{+}\right)\right]+T\ln\left[1+\exp\left(-\beta E_{ub}^{-}\right)\right]

\displaystyle+T\ln\left[1+\exp\left(-\beta E_{db}^{+}\right)\right]+T\ln\left[1+\exp\left(-\beta E_{db}^{-}\right)\right]

\displaystyle+2T\ln\left[1+\exp\left(-\beta E_{\Delta^{+}}^{+}\right)\right]+2T\ln\left[1+\exp\left(-\beta E_{\Delta^{-}}^{+}\right)\right]

\displaystyle\left.+2T\ln\left[1+\exp\left(-\beta E_{\Delta^{+}}^{-}\right)\right]+2T\ln\left[1+\exp\left(-\beta E_{\Delta^{-}}^{-}\right)\right]\right\}

with
E_{\Delta^{\pm}}^{\pm}=E_{\Delta}^{\pm}\pm\delta\mu
, where
E_{\Delta}^{\pm}=\sqrt{(E_{\boldsymbol{p}}\pm\bar{\mu})^{2}+\Delta^{2}}
,
E_{b}^{\pm}=E_{\boldsymbol{p}}\pm\tilde{\mu}_{b}
and
E_{\boldsymbol{p}}=\sqrt{\boldsymbol{p}^{2}+M^{2}}
.

Assuming the electron mass is zero, one has the contribution from the electron gas
\Omega_{e}
as:

\displaystyle\Omega_{e}=-\frac{\mu_{e}^{4}}{12\pi^{2}}\ .

(8)

Then the total thermodynamic potential of the system is:

\Omega=\Omega_{q}+\Omega_{e}\ .

(9)

II.3
Gap equations and charge-neutrality conditions

The self-consistent solutions of quark-antiquark condensate
\sigma
and diquark condensate
\Delta
, as well as the quark number density
n
, correspond to the stationary points of the total thermodynamic potential:

\displaystyle\frac{\delta\Omega}{\delta M}=\frac{\delta\Omega}{\delta\Delta}=\frac{\delta\Omega}{\delta\tilde{\mu}}=\frac{\delta\Omega}{\delta\mu_{8c}}=\frac{\delta\Omega}{\delta\mu_{e}}=0\ .

(10)

The gap equation for the constituent quark mass is obtained as:

\displaystyle M=

\displaystyle m_{0}+4G_{s}M\int\frac{d^{3}\boldsymbol{p}}{(2\pi)^{3}}\frac{1}{E_{\boldsymbol{p}}}\left\{\left[1-f\left(E_{ub}^{+}\right)-f\left(E_{ub}^{-}\right)\right]\right.

(11)

\displaystyle\left.+\left[1-f\left(E_{db}^{+}\right)-f\left(E_{db}^{-}\right)\right]\right.

\displaystyle\left.+2\frac{E_{\boldsymbol{p}}^{+}}{E_{\Delta}^{+}}\left[1-f\left(E_{\Delta^{+}}^{+}\right)-f\left(E_{\Delta^{-}}^{+}\right)\right]\right.

\displaystyle\left.+2\frac{E_{\boldsymbol{p}}^{-}}{E_{\Delta}^{-}}\left[1-f\left(E_{\Delta^{+}}^{-}\right)-f\left(E_{\Delta^{-}}^{-}\right)\right]\right\}\ .

where
E_{\boldsymbol{p}}^{\pm}=E_{\boldsymbol{p}}\pm\bar{\mu}
and
\tilde{f}(x)=1/\left(e^{\beta x}+1\right)
is the Fermi-Dirac distribution function with
\beta=1/T
.

The obtained gap equation for diquark condensate is:

\displaystyle\Delta=

\displaystyle 4G_{D}\Delta\int\frac{d^{3}\boldsymbol{p}}{(2\pi)^{3}}\left\{\frac{2}{E_{\Delta}^{-}}\left[1-f\left(E_{\Delta^{+}}^{-}\right)-f\left(E_{\Delta^{-}}^{-}\right)\right]\right.

(12)

\displaystyle\left.+\frac{2}{E_{\Delta}^{+}}\left[1-f\left(E_{\Delta^{+}}^{+}\right)-f\left(E_{\Delta^{-}}^{+}\right)\right]\right\}\ .

By utilizing the thermodynamic potential in terms of
\tilde{\mu}
in Eq. (
10
), we can obtain the number density of the two colored up quarks,

\displaystyle n_{ur}=n_{ug}=

\displaystyle\int\frac{d^{3}\boldsymbol{p}}{(2\pi)^{3}}\left\{\frac{E^{+}}{E_{\Delta}^{+}}\left[1-f\left(E_{\Delta^{+}}^{+}\right)-f\left(E_{\Delta^{-}}^{+}\right)\right]\right.

(13)

\displaystyle-

\displaystyle\left.\frac{E^{-}}{E_{\Delta}^{-}}\left[1-f\left(E_{\Delta^{+}}^{-}\right)-f\left(E_{\Delta^{-}}^{-}\right)\right]\right.

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\left.f\left(E_{\Delta^{+}}^{-}\right)+f\left(E_{\Delta^{+}}^{+}\right)-f\left(E_{\Delta^{-}}^{+}\right)-f\left(E_{\Delta^{-}}^{-}\right)\right\}\ ,

and the two colored down quarks,

\displaystyle n_{dr}=n_{dg}=

\displaystyle\int\frac{d^{3}\boldsymbol{p}}{(2\pi)^{3}}\left\{\frac{E^{+}}{E_{\Delta}^{+}}\left[1-f\left(E_{\Delta^{+}}^{+}\right)-f\left(E_{\Delta^{-}}^{+}\right)\right]\right.

(14)

\displaystyle-

\displaystyle\left.\frac{E^{-}}{E_{\Delta}^{-}}\left[1-f\left(E_{\Delta^{+}}^{-}\right)-f\left(E_{\Delta^{-}}^{-}\right)\right]\right.

\displaystyle-

\displaystyle\left.f\left(E_{\Delta^{+}}^{-}\right)-f\left(E_{\Delta^{+}}^{+}\right)+f\left(E_{\Delta^{-}}^{+}\right)+f\left(E_{\Delta^{-}}^{-}\right)\right\}\ .

For the unpaired blue quarks, we have:

\displaystyle n_{ub}=2\int\frac{d^{3}\boldsymbol{p}}{(2\pi)^{3}}\left[f\left(E_{ub}^{-}\right)-f\left(E_{ub}^{+}\right)\right]\ ,

(15)

\displaystyle n_{db}=2\int\frac{d^{3}\boldsymbol{p}}{(2\pi)^{3}}\left[f\left(E_{db}^{-}\right)-f\left(E_{db}^{+}\right)\right]\ .

The color charge-neutrality condition requires the system to have zero net charge
T_{8}
.
This requirement is met by evaluating the equation
n_{8}=-\frac{\delta\Omega}{\partial\mu_{8c}}=0
, resulting in the color charge-neutrality condition:

\displaystyle\int\frac{d^{3}\boldsymbol{p}}{(2\pi)^{3}}\left\{\frac{E^{+}}{E_{\Delta}^{+}}\left[1-f\left(E_{\Delta^{+}}^{+}\right)-f\left(E_{\Delta^{-}}^{+}\right)\right]\right.

(16)

\displaystyle-\frac{E^{-}}{E_{\Delta}^{-}}\left[1-f\left(E_{\Delta^{+}}^{-}\right)-f\left(E_{\Delta^{-}}^{-}\right)\right]

\displaystyle\left.+f\left(E_{ub}^{+}\right)-f\left(E_{ub}^{-}\right)+f\left(E_{db}^{+}\right)-f\left(E_{db}^{-}\right)\right\}=0\ .

The electric chemical potential
\mu_{e}
should be chosen to ensure the condition of electric charge neutrality. From
n_{Q}=-\frac{\delta\Omega}{\delta\mu_{e}}=0
, one has:

\displaystyle\int\frac{d^{3}\boldsymbol{p}}{(2\pi)^{3}}\left\{2\frac{E^{+}}{E_{\Delta}^{+}}\left[1-f\left(E_{\Delta^{+}}^{+}\right)-f\left(E_{\Delta^{-}}^{+}\right)\right]\right.

(17)

\displaystyle\left.-2\frac{E^{-}}{E_{\Delta}^{-}}\left[1-f\left(E_{\Delta^{+}}^{-}\right)-f\left(E_{\Delta^{-}}^{-}\right)\right]\right.

\displaystyle\left.+6\left[f\left(E_{\Delta^{+}}^{-}\right)+f\left(E_{\Delta^{+}}^{+}\right)-f\left(E_{\Delta^{-}}^{+}\right)-f\left(E_{\Delta^{-}}^{-}\right)\right]\right.

\displaystyle\left.+4\left[f\left(E_{ub}^{-}\right)-f\left(E_{ub}^{+}\right)\right]-2\left[f\left(E_{db}^{-}\right)-f\left(E_{db}^{+}\right)\right]\right\}-\frac{\mu_{e}^{3}}{\pi^{2}}=0\ .

II.4
Parameter fixing in NJL model

Before proceeding with calculations, parameter fitting is necessary.
Since the NJL model is not renormalizable, a momentum cutoff
\Lambda
needs to be introduced. In the chiral limit (
m_{0}=0
), the fermion momentum cutoff
\Lambda=0.6533\;\text{GeV}
and the scalar quark-antiquark coupling constant
G_{S}=5.0163\;\text{GeV}^{-2}
in the color singlet channel are determined by fitting experimental data on the pion decay constant and pion mass.
The coupling strengths of
G_{V}
and
G_{D}
cannot currently be fixed and involve many uncertainties.
The value of
G_{V}/G_{S}
ranges from
0.25
to
0.5
, derived by a Fierz transformation of the effective one-gluon exchange interaction, where
G_{V}
depends on the strength of the
U_{A}(1)
anomaly in the two-flavor model
(
Klevansky 1992
;
Kashiwa et al. 2011
)
.
Some studies suggest that
G_{V}
is similar in magnitude to
G_{D}
to explain lattice results on the curvature of the linear chiral restoration at zero density
(
Buballa 2005
;
Bratovic et al. 2013
;
Masuda et al. 2013
)
.
The strength of
G_{D}
is even less clear.
Typically, one might assume
G_{D}/G_{S}=3/4
for equal contributions from the quark-antiquark interaction channels and the Fierz-transformed diquark interaction channels
(
Steiner 2005
;
Anglani et al. 2014
;
Cao and Zhuang 2015
)
.
In the present study, we treat both
G_{V}
and
G_{D}
as free parameters, for our purpose of exploring whether a parameter space exists for absolutely stable 2SC quark matter.
For later convenience, we define
G_{V}/G_{S}\equiv R_{V}
and
G_{D}/G_{S}\equiv R_{D}
.

III
Results and discussion

III.1
Dynamically generated mass and pairing gaps

In Fig.
1
, we present the constituent quark mass and pairing gap
for 2SC quark matter under electric and color charge neutrality conditions, considering a typical case with
R_{V}=0
,
R_{D}=0.75
.
It is shown that when the quark chemical potential exceeds the vacuum constituent quark mass (
M=313\;\text{MeV}
), chiral symmetry begins to restore.
The 2SC phase emerges around
\mu=313\;\text{MeV}
, in which diquark condensate
\Delta

subsequently decreases after reaching its maximum.
This cessation in the increasing trend is related to the momentum cutoff.
In this charged 2SC phase, where the electrons have a large Fermi surface extending to
\sim 150\;\text{MeV}
, the magnitude of
\Delta
is around
50\;\text{MeV}
, significantly smaller than the uncharged case (
\sim 100\;\text{MeV}
)
(
Huang et al. 2002
;
Huang et al. 2003
;
Yuan et al. 2023
)
.
The distinct Fermi surfaces of the two pairing quarks contribute to the reduction in the magnitude of the diquark condensate.

In Fig.
2
, we specifically examine the influence of the strength of the attractive diquark interaction.
It is seen that, when the vector interaction is absent (
R_{V}=0
), an increase in the attractive diquark interaction strength leads to a larger maximum value of the diquark gap (
\Delta
), while the critical chemical potential, marking the onset of the 2SC phase, decreases.
The nature of the chiral phase transition remains first order, and its transition hardly changes.
However, for extremely large
R_{D}
coupling (as large as e.g.,
R_{D}=1.3
),
the 2SC phase emerges at significantly lower quark chemical potentials, approaching zero; Simultaneously, the maximum
\Delta
value exceeds the constituent quark mass (
M
), rendering it physically unrealistic.

III.2
The EOS

At finite chemical potential and zero temperature, the pressure-chemical potential relation for quark matter can be rigorously derived using the functional path integrals of QCD
(
Zong & Sun 2008
)
:

P(\mu;M)=P(\mu=0;M)+\int_{0}^{\mu}d\mu^{\prime}\rho\left(\mu^{\prime}\right)\ .

(18)

While the negative vacuum pressure
P(\mu=0;M)
can be consistently calculated in NJL-type models
(
Buballa 2005
)
, the results are unsatisfactory due to the lack of confinement at vanishing density, as discussed in detail in
Yuan et al. 2022
.
Therefore, in this study, we treat
P(\mu=0;M)
as a phenomenological parameter corresponding to
-B
(with
B
being the vacuum bag constant), which preserves quark confinement.

The thermodynamic relation between energy density and pressure is the EOS of the system

\varepsilon=-P(\mu;M)+\sum_{i=u,d,s,e}\mu_{i}\rho_{i}\left(\mu_{i}\right)\ .

(19)

The pressure of normal quark matter and 2SC quark matter within NJL-type models are shown together in Fig.
3
.
In the same plot, we include the corresponding results from MIT bag model calculations
(
Alford & Rajagopal 2002
;
Zhang & Mann 2021
)
, where we have purposely chosen a similar diquark gap (
\Delta=50\;\text{MeV}
; see discussions above accompanying Figs.
2
and
1
).
Additionally, we include a representative result for neutron star matter within the quark mean-field model
(
Li et al. 2020
)
.

It is seen that in both MIT and NJL-type models,
with increasing chemical potential, the pressure of 2SC quark matter becomes higher than that of normal quark matter. This indicates the lower energy of 2SC matter, making it energetically favored.
Specially in NJL-type model calculations, the inclusion of vector interaction pushes the baryon chemical potential of quark matter at zero pressure to high baryon densities, resembling (although not as significant as) an increase in
-B
(a decrease in vacuum bag constant).
Conversely, the inclusion of diquark interaction has the opposite effect in both MIT and NJL-type models, due to its attractive nature.
The results from MIT model calculations generally appear energetically unfavorable, staying below those of NJL-type model calculations and even below those of neutron star matter. In contrast, for the NJL-type model, starting from a certain value of baryon chemical potential (
\mu_{B}\sim 1.1\;\text{GeV}
), the pressure of neutron star matter is lower than that of quark matter.
This lends initial support to the possibility of the existence of self-bound compact stars composed of deconfined quarks, as mentioned in our introduction.

IV
Summary

In this study, we investigated the possibility of absolutely stable 2SC quark matter within NJL-type models, adopting the three-momentum cutoff regularization scheme while incorporating vector and scalar diquark interaction.
The study shed light on the substantial impact of repulsive vector interactions and attractive diquark interactions near the Fermi surface on the stability of 2SC quark matter within the NJL-type model.

The results indicated that the introduction of charge neutrality in the 2SC phase suppresses the size of the superconducting energy gap
\Delta
compared to the phase without charge neutrality.
As the 2SC phase emerges at lower chemical potentials, the maximum value of the color superconducting gap increases.
While an increase in diquark interaction minimally affects the chiral phase transition, it significantly influences the critical quark chemical potential at which the 2SC phase initiates.
Despite the lowering of vacuum pressure
B^{1/4}
and the diquark interaction
R_{D}
resulting in a decreased
E/A
, no viable parameter space exists for absolutely stable 2SC quark matter, specifically where
E/A~(\rm{2SC})<930\;\text{MeV}~(^{56}\rm Fe)
.
Consequently, the presence of 2SC quark matter in the compact object associated with HESS J1731-347 is not supported by the present stability study within NJL-type models.

Appendix A
Partition function and the thermodynamic potential

The partition function of the grand canonical ensemble can be evaluated by using the standard method:

\displaystyle\mathcal{Z}=N^{\prime}\int[d\bar{q}][dq]\exp\left\{\int_{0}^{\beta}d\tau\int d^{3}\boldsymbol{x}\left(\mathcal{L}+\mu\bar{q}\gamma_{0}q\right)\right\}\ .

(A1)

Under mean-field approximation, the Lagrangian in Eq. (

2

After introducing the bispinor field
\Psi(x)

\displaystyle\widetilde{\mathcal{L}}=\bar{\Psi}G^{-1}\Psi+V\ .

(A2)

Here, the inverse of the propagator matrix is:

\displaystyle G^{-1}=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}\gamma^{\mu}p_{\mu}+\tilde{\mu}\gamma^{0}-M&\Delta^{-}\\
\Delta^{+}&\gamma^{\mu}p_{\mu}-\tilde{\mu}\gamma^{0}-M\end{array}\right)

(A3)

with
\Delta^{-}=-i\Delta\gamma_{5}\tau_{2}\lambda_{2}
and
\Delta^{+}=-i\Delta^{*}\gamma_{5}\tau_{2}\lambda_{2}

The quark propagator in the normal phase is:

\displaystyle\mathrm{G}_{0}^{-1}=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}{\left[G_{0}^{+}\right]^{-1}}&0\\
0&{\left[G_{0}^{-}\right]^{-1}}\end{array}\right)\ ,

(A4)

with

and
p_{0}=i\omega_{n},\ \omega_{n}=(2n+1)\pi T
.
The quark propagator in the color-breaking phase is:

\displaystyle\mathrm{G}^{-1}=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}{\left[G_{0}^{+}\right]^{-1}}&\Delta^{-}\\
\Delta^{+}&{\left[G_{0}^{-}\right]^{-1}}\end{array}\right)\ .

(A5)

By solving
\mathrm{G}^{-1}\mathrm{G}=1
, the Nambu-Gorkov propagator
\mathrm{G}(p)
can be determined, resulting in

\displaystyle\mathrm{G}=\left(\begin{array}[]{ll}G^{+}&\Xi^{-}\\
\Xi^{+}&G^{-}\end{array}\right)\ ,

(A6)

with the components

\displaystyle G^{\pm}\equiv\left\{\left[G_{0}^{\pm}\right]^{-1}-\Sigma^{\pm}\right\}^{-1},\quad\Sigma^{\pm}\equiv\Delta^{\mp}G_{0}^{\mp}\Delta^{\pm},\quad\Xi^{\pm}\equiv-G^{\mp}\Delta^{\pm}G_{0}^{\pm}=-G_{0}^{\mp}\Delta^{\pm}G^{\pm}\ ,

As we mentioned in Sec.

\displaystyle\mathcal{Z}=\mathcal{Z}_{\text{const }}\mathcal{Z}_{b}\mathcal{Z}_{r,g}\ ,

\displaystyle\mathcal{Z}_{\text{const }}=N^{\prime}\exp\left\{-\int_{0}^{\beta}d\tau\int d^{3}\boldsymbol{x}\left[\frac{\sigma^{2}}{4G_{S}}-\frac{(\mu-\tilde{\mu})^{2}}{4G_{V}}+\frac{\Delta^{*}\Delta}{4G_{D}}\right]\right\}\ .

A.1
The energy projectors for massive particles and dispersion relations

To handle the intricate massive quark propagator, we can employ energy projectors for massive particles to evaluate it.
The energy projectors onto positive- and negative-energy states for free massive particles are defined as follows:

\displaystyle\Lambda_{\pm}(\boldsymbol{p})=\frac{1}{2}\left(1\pm\frac{\gamma_{0}(\boldsymbol{\gamma}\cdot\boldsymbol{p}+M)}{E_{\boldsymbol{p}}}\right)\ ,

\displaystyle\gamma_{0}\Lambda_{\pm}\gamma_{0}=\tilde{\Lambda}_{\mp},\quad\gamma_{5}\Lambda_{\pm}\gamma_{5}=\tilde{\Lambda}_{\pm}\ .

Using energy projectors, the quark propagator for the normal phase [Eq. (

Appendix B
Evaluating the thermodynamic potential

After deriving the
\mathcal{Z}_{b}
unpairing part and
\mathcal{Z}_{r,g}
pairing part in Eq. (
A8
), we can finally evaluate the thermodynamic potential
(
Kapusta and Gale 2011
)
. The frequency summation of the free energy

\displaystyle\ln\mathcal{Z}_{f}=\sum_{n}\ln\left[\beta^{2}\left(p_{0}^{2}-E_{\boldsymbol{p}}^{2}\right)\right]

(B1)

can always be obtained by performing the frequency summation of the propagator
1/\left(p_{0}^{2}-E_{\boldsymbol{p}}^{2}\right)
.
By differentiating Eq. (
B1
) with respect to
E_{\boldsymbol{p}}
:

\displaystyle\frac{\partial\ln\mathcal{Z}_{f}}{\partial E_{p}}=-2E_{\boldsymbol{p}}\sum_{n}\frac{1}{p_{0}^{2}-E_{p}^{2}}=\beta\left[1-2\tilde{f}\left(E_{\boldsymbol{p}}\right)\right]\ ,

(B2)

where
\tilde{f}(x)=1/\left(e^{\beta x}+1\right)
is the usual Fermi-Dirac distribution function,
then integrating with respect to
E_{\boldsymbol{p}}
, one can get the free energy

\displaystyle\ln\mathcal{Z}_{f}=\beta\left[E_{\boldsymbol{p}}+2T\ln\left(1+e^{-\beta E_{\boldsymbol{p}}}\right)\right]\ .

(B3)

With the help of the above expression, and by replacing

\displaystyle\sum_{p}\rightarrow V\int\frac{d^{3}\boldsymbol{p}}{(2\pi)^{3}}\ ,

(B4)

we can obtain the thermodynamic potential of the quark-matter system as written in Eq. (
7
):

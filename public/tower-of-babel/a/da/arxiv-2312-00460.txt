A large family of strongly regular graphs with small Weisfeiler-Leman dimension
Jinzhuan Cai, Jin Guo, Alexander L. Gavrilyuk, Ilia Ponomarenko

Abstract
. In 2002, D. Fon-Der-Flaass constructed a prolific family of strongly regular graphs. In this paper, we prove that for infinitely many natural numbers n n , this family contains n Ω ⁡ ( n 2 / 3 ) n^{\mathsf{\Omega}(n^{2/3})} strongly regular n n -vertex graphs X X with the same parameters, which satisfy the following condition: an isomorphism between X X and any other graph can be verified by the 4 4 -dimensional Weisfeiler-Leman algorithm.



1.
Introduction

An undirected graph
X
is said to be
strongly regular
if the number
k
(respectively,
\lambda
,
\mu
) of common neighbors of any two of its vertices depends only on whether they are equal (respectively, adjacent, nonadjacent). Together with the order of
X
, these numbers form the set of
parameters
of
X
and are intersection numbers of the coherent configuration associated with
X
(the exact definitions are given in Section
2
). To date, the monograph
[
3
]
can serve as
the most complete (though not exhaustive) reference for
the theory of strongly regular graphs.

Informally speaking, “strongly regular graphs lie on the cusp
between highly structured and unstructured”
[
4
]
.
Given the parameters
(n,k,\lambda,\mu)
,
there may be very few (or none at all) pairwise nonisomorphic
strongly regular graphs having these parameters or, conversely,
an abundance of those, say, the number of them is bounded from
below by an exponential function in
n
.

In the former case, a strongly regular graph
X
can typically be
nicely characterized in terms of its local structure, which gives
rise to polynomial-time algorithms to test isomorphism between
X

and any other graph (examples here are the Johnson, Hamming, and
Grassman graphs of diameter two). Moreover, the role
of such an algorithm is often played by a low-dimensional
Weisfeiler-Leman algorithm
[
7
,
18
]
;
in other words, the Weisfeiler-Leman dimension
(see Section
3
) of the graph
X
is usually bounded
from above by a small constant.

In the latter case, there are numerous examples, such as
the graphs of Latin squares, Steiner systems, and the graphs
obtained by various prolific constructions
[
8
,
14
,
17
,
15
,
2
]
.
However, the authors were not aware of any abundant family
of strongly regular graphs with small Weisfeiler-Leman dimensions.
This was a primary motivation for the paper and our main result is to present such a family.

Theorem 1.1
.

For any large enough power of
2
, say
q
,
there is a family

of pairwise nonisomorphic
strongly regular graphs with the same parameters,

(1)

\dim_{\scriptscriptstyle\WL}({\mathfrak{F}})\leq 4\quad\text{and}\quad|{\mathfrak{F}}|>q^{\mathsf{\Omega}(q^{2})},

where

is the maximum Weisfeiler-Leman dimension over the graphs

{\mathfrak{F}}
.

The parameters of all

-vertex strongly regular graphs in the family

are equal to

(n,k,\lambda,\mu)=(q^{2}(q+2),q(q+1),q,q).

In fact,

For the family of graphs constructed in Theorem

Second, the coloring of the arcs of the complete graph

Our result leaves open several natural questions.
Denote by
{\mathfrak{F}}’
the family of all Fon-Der-Flaass graphs constructed from a fixed affine plane

and a fixed arc coloring of the graph

. Then the second part of Eq. (

show that the quotient

We wonder if it is possible to show, perhaps using a more general type of switching, that the Weisfeiler-Leman dimension of almost all graphs of the family
{\mathfrak{F}}^{\prime}
is bounded from above by some absolute constant.

In connection with this question, it is worth noting that the Fon-Der-Flaass graphs constructed with

being the same affine design with even number of points and the high-dimensional graphs
of Cai-Fürer-Immerman type

9

,
whose Weisfeiler-Leman dimension is unbounded,
are special cases of the same general construction of graphs

It would also be interesting to see whether there exists a larger family of strongly regular Fon-Der-Flaass graphs under some milder
restrictions than the ones we have chosen, e.g., if the affine designs are not necessarily affine planes, or if they are planes but not necessarily Desarguesian. Note also the Fon-Der-Flaass construction
was generalized in several ways, see
[
17

15

.

The most challenging step in the proof of Theorem

is to verify the first inequality in Eq. (

Throughout this section,

3.1.
Multidimensional Weisfeiler-Leman algorithm

The Weisfeiler-Leman dimension of a graph is defined with the help of the
m
-dimensional Weisfeiler-Leman algorithm. For a given graph
X=(\Omega,E)
, this algorithm constructs a certain coloring
c(m,X)
of
\Omega^{m}
; here, a
coloring
is understood
as a function
c
from
\Omega^{m}
to some linearly ordered
set
\im(c)
whose elements are called
colors
.

At the first stage, an initial coloring
c_{0}=c_{0}(m,X)
is constructed from an auxiliary coloring
c^{\prime}
defined as follows: given
x,y\in\Omega^{m}
, we set
c^{\prime}(x)=c^{\prime}(y)
if and only if for all
i,j\in M
,

(4)

x_{i}=x_{j}\ \Leftrightarrow\ y_{i}=y_{j}\quad\text{and}\quad(x_{i},x_{j})\in E\ \Leftrightarrow\ (y_{i},y_{j})\in E.

Now the color
c_{0}(x)
of an
m
-tuple
x
is set to be the tuple
(c^{\prime}(x^{\sigma}))_{\sigma\in\mon(M)}
, where
\mon(M)
is the monoid
of all mappings
\sigma\colon M\to M
and
x^{\sigma}=(x_{1^{\sigma}},\ldots,x_{m^{\sigma}})
. A linear ordering of
the colors is inherited in a natural way from
the lexicographic ordering of the tuples
(1^{\sigma},\ldots,m^{\sigma})
,
\sigma\in\mon(M)
.

At the second stage, the initial coloring is refined step by step. Namely, if
c_{i}
is the coloring constructed at the
i
th step (
i\geq 0
), then the color of an
m
-tuple
x
in the coloring
c_{i+1}
is defined to be

c_{i+1}(x)=(c_{i}(x),\{\hskip-3.0pt\{(c_{i}(x_{1\leftarrow\alpha}),\ldots,c_{i}(x_{m\leftarrow\alpha}))\colon\alpha\in\Omega\}\hskip-3.0pt\}),

where
\{\hskip-3.0pt\{\cdot\}\hskip-3.0pt\}
denotes a multiset and, for all
i\in M
, we denote by

x_{i\leftarrow\alpha}
the
m
-tuple
(x_{1},\ldots,x_{i-1},\alpha,x_{i+1},\ldots,x_{m})
. The algorithm stops when
|\im(c_{i})|=|\im(c_{i+1})|
and the final coloring
c(m,X)
is set to be
c_{i}
.

Two graphs

and

are said to be
\WL_{m}
-equivalent

\im(c_{X})=\im(c_{X^{\prime}})

of a graph

such that every graph
\WL_{m}
-equivalent to

is isomorphic to

.

Lemma 3.3

For an integer

5.
Elementary and path switchings

5.1.
Elementary switchings

Throughout this section
A
is an affine plane of order
q
.

Let
X\in{\mathfrak{F}}_{A}({\cal L})
, and let
\Delta=V\times\{i\}
,
\Gamma=V\times\{j\}
be fibers of
X
,
i\neq j
. Let
{\cal L}_{ij}={\cal L}_{ji}=\{L_{1},\ldots,L_{q}\}
be a (uniquely determined) parallel class of the plane
A
. Given a permutation
f\in\sym(q)
, put

E^{\prime}_{\Delta,\Gamma}:=\bigcup_{k=1}^{q}L_{k}\times L_{k^{f}}.

and
E^{\prime}=(E\setminus E_{\Delta,\Gamma})\cup E^{\prime}_{\Delta,\Gamma}
. We say that the graph
X^{\prime}=(\Omega,E^{\prime})
is obtained from the graph
X
by

elementary switching
(with respect to
\Delta,\Gamma
)
if the relation
E_{\Delta,\Gamma}\cup E^{\prime}_{\Delta,\Gamma}
is connected, or, equivalently, if the permutation
f
is a full cycle.

Lemma 5.1
.

Let a graph
X^{\prime}
be obtained from a graph
X\in{\mathfrak{F}}_{A}({\cal L})
by an elementary switching with respect to
\Delta
and
\Gamma
. Then
X^{\prime}\in{\mathfrak{F}}_{A}({\cal L})
and, given distinct vertices
\alpha,\beta\in\Omega
, one of the following statements holds:

(1)

if
\alpha\neq\beta
and
\alpha,\beta\not\in\Delta\cup\Gamma
, then
(\alpha,\beta)\in E\ \Leftrightarrow\ (\alpha,\beta)\in E^{\prime}
, and moreover

N_{X}(\alpha,\beta)=N_{X^{\prime}}(\alpha,\beta)\quad\text{and}\quad|e_{X}(\alpha,\beta)-e_{X^{\prime}}(\alpha,\beta)|\leq 1;

(2)

if
\alpha\in\Delta
and
\beta\not\in\Delta\cup\Gamma
, then
(\alpha,\beta)\in E\ \Leftrightarrow\ (\alpha,\beta)\in E^{\prime}
, and moreover

|N_{X}(\alpha,\beta)\setminus N_{X^{\prime}}(\alpha,\beta)|\leq 1\quad\text{and}\quad|e_{X}(\alpha,\beta)-e_{X^{\prime}}(\alpha,\beta)|\leq q-1;

(3)

if
\alpha\in\Delta
and
\beta\in\Gamma
, then
(\alpha,\beta)\in E\ \Rightarrow\ (\alpha,\beta)\not\in E^{\prime}
, and moreover

N_{X}(\alpha,\beta)=N_{X^{\prime}}(\alpha,\beta)\quad\text{and}\quad e_{X}(\alpha,\beta)=e_{X^{\prime}}(\alpha,\beta);

(4)

if
\alpha,\beta\in\Delta
or
\alpha,\beta\in\Gamma
, then
(\alpha,\beta)\not\in E\cup E^{\prime}
, and moreover,

N_{X}(\alpha,\beta)=N_{X^{\prime}}(\alpha,\beta)\quad\text{or}\quad N_{X}(\alpha,\beta)\cap N_{X^{\prime}}(\alpha,\beta)=\varnothing,

and
e_{X}(\alpha,\beta)=e_{X^{\prime}}(\alpha,\beta)=0

in either case.

Proof.

Obviously,
X^{\prime}\in{\mathfrak{F}}_{A}({\cal L})
.

(1)
Let
\alpha\neq\beta
and
\alpha,\beta\not\in\Delta\cup\Gamma
. Since the elementary switching affects the edges between
\Delta
and
\Gamma
only, we have
(\alpha,\beta)\in E\ \Leftrightarrow\ (\alpha,\beta)\in E^{\prime}
and
N_{X}(\alpha,\beta)=N_{X^{\prime}}(\alpha,\beta)
. Furthermore, if
\alpha,\beta
are in the same fiber of
X
and hence that of
X^{\prime}
, then

by Lemma
4.1
(2). If

are in different fibers, then

and
N_{X}(\alpha,\beta)\cap\Gamma
. Hence,
|e_{X}(\alpha,\beta)-e_{X^{\prime}}(\alpha,\beta)|\leq 1
.

(2)
Let
\alpha\in\Delta
and
\beta\not\in\Delta\cup\Gamma

\Delta
and
\Gamma

(\alpha,\beta)\in E\ \Leftrightarrow\ (\alpha,\beta)\in E^{\prime}

(1),

N_{X}(\alpha,\beta)\cap\Gamma=\{\gamma\}\quad\text{and}\quad N_{X^{\prime}}(\alpha,\beta)\cap\Gamma=\{\gamma^{\prime}\}

for some vertices

. In particular, the graphs induced by

. It follows that
|e_{X}(\alpha,\beta)-e_{X^{\prime}}(\alpha,\beta)|\leq q-1
.

(3)
If
\alpha\in\Delta
and
\beta\in\Gamma
, then the required statements easily follow from Lemma
4.1
(1) and the definition of elementary switching.

(4)
Let
\alpha,\beta\in\Delta

6.
Proof of Theorems
1.1

The construction of the graph
X^{*}
below and Lemma
6.2
was proposed by M. Muzychuk (private communication), and, in fact, is a special case of
[
17
, Proposition 5.1]
applied to a hyperoval in a Desarguesian affine plane of even order.

Let
A
be a Desarguesian affine plane of even order
q
. Following
the notation of Subsection
4.1
, assume that we are given
a hyperoval in
A
, say

H=\{h_{i}\in V\colon i\in I\}.

Choose a coordinatization of
A
so that
H
does not contains the zero point. For any two distinct indices
i,j\in I
, we put

(12)

{\cal L}_{ij}=\{[h_{i}-h_{j}]+v\colon v\in V\},

where
[h_{i}-h_{j}]
is the line of
A
, which is incident to zero point and the point
h_{i}-h_{j}
.

Lemma 6.1
.

{\cal L}_{ij}={\cal L}_{ji}
and
{\cal L}_{ij}\neq{\cal L}_{ik}
for all pairwise distinct indices
i,j,k\in I
.

Proof.

The first statement is obvious. To prove the second statement, we assume that
{\cal L}_{ij}={\cal L}_{ik}
for some pairwise distinct indices
i,j,k\in I
. Then
{\cal L}_{ij}
and
{\cal L}_{ik}
have the same line
[u]
containing zero point; in particular,
[h_{i}-h_{j}]=[h_{i}-h_{k}]
. Now, the point
h_{i}
belongs to the lines
[u]+h_{j}
and
[u]+h_{k}
lying in the same parallel class. It follows that they are equal to the same line, and this line contains
h_{i}
,
h_{j}
, and
h_{k}
, which contradicts the definition of hyperoval.
∎

By Lemma
6.1
,
the parallel classes
{\cal L}_{ij}
and
the identical bijections
\sigma_{ij}\colon{\cal L}_{ij}\to{\cal L}_{ji}

satisfy Eq. (
8
).
Hence the array
{\cal L}:=({\cal L}_{ij})

and the set of bijections
\Sigma:=(\sigma_{ij})

define a strongly regular graph

X^{*}=X(A,H)=X_{A}({\cal L},\Sigma)

with vertex set
\Omega=V\times I
and the parameters as in Eq. (
2
), see Subsection
4.1
.

Lemma 6.2
.

The graph
X^{*}
belongs to the class
{\mathfrak{F}}_{q}({\cal L})
, and satisfies the
4
-condition.

Proof.

The first statement is obvious. To prove the second one, take arbitrary
a\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
and
v\in V
, and put

K(a,v)=\{(ah_{i}+v,i)\in\Omega\colon i\in I\}.

We claim that
K(a,v)
is a clique (of size
q+2
) of
X^{*}
.
Indeed, let
(ah_{i}+v,i)
and
(ah_{j}+v,j)
be distinct vertices of
K(a,v)
; in particular,
i\neq j
. The difference
(ah_{i}+v)-(ah_{j}+v)=a(h_{i}-h_{j})
belongs to the line
[h_{i}-h_{j}]
.
Consequently, the points
ah_{i}+v
and
ah_{j}+v
belong to the same line of the parallel class
{\cal L}_{ij}
, see Eq. (
12
). But this exactly means that the vertices
(ah_{i}+v,i)
and
(ah_{j}+v,j)
are adjacent in
X^{*}
.

Next, any two adjacent vertices
\alpha=(v_{i},i)
and
\beta=(v_{j},j)
of the graph
X^{*}
are containd in a common clique
K(a,v)
, where the element
a
and point
v
form a solution of the linear system

\left\{\begin{aligned} v_{i}=ah_{i}+v,\\
v_{j}=ah_{j}+v.\\
\end{aligned}\right.

Hence,
K(a,v)\subseteq N(\alpha,\beta)\cup\{\alpha,\beta\}
, where
N(\alpha,\beta)=N_{X^{*}}(\alpha,\beta)
. Since the graph
X^{*}
is strongly regular with parameters (
2
), we have
|N(\alpha,\beta)|=q=|K(a,v)|-2
. Thus,

N(\alpha,\beta)\cup\{\alpha,\beta\}=K(a,v),

and hence the subgraph

is complete for all adjacent vertices
\alpha
and
\beta
. By Corollary
4.4
, this proves that the graph
X^{*}
satisfies the
4
-condition.
∎

Denote by

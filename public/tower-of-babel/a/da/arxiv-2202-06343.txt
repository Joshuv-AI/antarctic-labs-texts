A characterization of Gabor Riesz bases with separable time-frequency shifts
Christina Frederick, Azita Mayeli

Abstract
A Gabor system generated by a window function g ∈ L 2 ​ ( ℝ d ) g\in L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d}) and a separable set Λ × Γ ⊂ ℝ 2 ​ d \Lambda\times\Gamma\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{2d} is the collection of time-frequency shifts of g g given by 𝒢 ⁡ ( g , Λ × Γ ) = { e 2 ​ π ​ i ​ ξ ⋅ t ​ g ​ ( t − x ) } ( x , ξ ) ∈ Λ × Γ \mathcal{G}(g,\Lambda\times\Gamma)=\left\{e^{2\pi i\xi\cdot t}g(t-x)\right\}_{(x,\xi)\in\Lambda\times\Gamma} . One of the fundamental problems in Gabor analysis is to characterize all windows and time-frequency sets that generate a Gabor frame or Gabor orthonormal basis. The case of Gabor orthonormal bases generated by characteristic functions g = χ Ω g=\chi_{\Omega} has been solved by Han and Wang [ 12 , 23 ] . In this paper, we build on these results and obtain a full characterization of Riesz Gabor systems of the form 𝒢 ⁡ ( χ Ω , Λ × Γ ) \mathcal{G}(\chi_{\Omega},\Lambda\times\Gamma) when Ω \Omega is a tiling of ℝ d {\mathbb{R}}^{d} with respect to Λ \Lambda . Furthermore, for a certain class of lattices Λ × Γ \Lambda\times\Gamma , we prove that a necessary condition for the characteristic function of a multi-tiling set to serve as a window function for a Riesz Gabor basis is that the set must be a tiling set. To prove this, we develop new results on the zeros of the Zak transform and connect these results to Gabor frames.



Keywords:
Gabor Riesz bases, multi-tiling sets, Zak transforms.

1
Introduction

We will assume that the sets
\Lambda
and
\Gamma
are countable subsets of
\mathbb{R}^{d}
and define the
Gabor system

\mathcal{G}(g,\Lambda\times\Gamma)
with respect to the window function
g\in L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
and the set
\Lambda\times\Gamma
to be the collection of functions

\displaystyle\mathcal{G}(g,\Lambda\times\Gamma)=\left\{e^{2\pi i\xi\cdot t}g(t-x):\ (x,\xi)\in\Lambda\times\Gamma\right\}.

(1)

This system is also known as a
Weyl-Heisenberg system
. The set
\Lambda\times\Gamma
is called the set of
time-frequency shifts
or the
Gabor spectrum
. If the Gabor system is a frame, Riesz basis or orthogonal basis, we call it a
Gabor frame
,
Gabor Riesz basis
, or
Gabor orthogonal basis
, respectively, for
L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
.

Gabor systems, first introduced by Gabor in 1946
[
9
]
, now play a central role in applied and computational harmonic analysis, engineering (signal and image processing, communication theory), and physics (the phase-space and coherent space representation). The Gabor analysis permits a time-frequency representation of a signal
f(t)
in the time domain, hence making it possible to view the frequency spectrum locally in time. For more on the history of the Gabor bases, we refer to
[
11
]
.

Motivation:
This study is inspired by the well-known characterization of Gabor orthogonal bases of the form
\mathcal{G}(\chi_{\Omega},\Lambda\times\Gamma)
by Han and Wang in
[
12
]
, where
\chi_{\Omega}
is the characteristic function of a measurable set
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
.

Han-Wang’s Theorem
.

The system
\mathcal{G}(\chi_{\Omega},\Lambda\times\Gamma
)
is a Gabor orthogonal basis for
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
if and only if the following two conditions hold:

1.

\Omega
tiles
\mathbb{R}^{d}
by
\Lambda
, i.e.,
\sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda}\chi_{\Omega}(x-\lambda)=1
, for almost every
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, and,

2.

The family of exponentials
\mathcal{E}(\Gamma)=\{e^{2\pi ix\cdot\gamma}:\ \gamma\in\Gamma\}
is an orthogonal basis for
L^{2}(\Omega).

We aim to generalize these results to Gabor Riesz bases and Gabor frames for two principal reasons. First, orthogonality is a strong condition that is often not satisfied in practical applications. Moreover, the orthogonality of
\mathcal{G}(\chi_{\Omega},\Lambda\times\Gamma)
is unstable with respect to small perturbations either in translation or modulation. This motivates the need for the analysis of Gabor frames and Gabor Riesz bases, in which orthogonality is relaxed and stability is improved.

One question that we ask ourselves here is: What are sufficient conditions on
\Omega
,
\Lambda
, and
\Gamma
that produce a Gabor Riesz basis?
A natural approach is to modify the two conditions in
Han-Wang’s Theorem
, and assume that:

1.

\Omega

multi-tiles

{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
by
\Lambda
at level
k
, i.e.,

\sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda}\chi_{\Omega}(x-\lambda)=k
for almost every
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, and,

2.

\mathcal{E}(\Gamma)
is a Riesz basis for
L^{2}(\Omega)
.

However, these modifications are not sufficient when
k>1
. This is demonstrated in the next two examples that fail the necessary condition, given in
[
25
]
, that:

\text{dens}(\Lambda\times\Gamma)=1\text{ when }\mathcal{G}(g,\Lambda\times\Gamma)\text{ is a Gabor Riesz basis.}

Here, dens
(\Lambda\times\Gamma)
denotes the Beurling density of a set
S
.

Example 1.1
.

If
\Omega
multi-tiles
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
by a lattice
\Lambda
at level
k>1
, it is known that there exist distinct vectors
\{a_{i}\}_{i=1}^{k}
in
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
such
\mathcal{E}(\Gamma)
is a Riesz basis for
L^{2}(\Omega)
, where
\Gamma=\cup_{i=1}^{k}(\Lambda^{\perp}+a_{i})

[
10
,
20
]
. However, dens
(\Lambda\times\Gamma)=k>1
.

Example 1.2
.

Let
\Omega=[0,1]
,
\Lambda=2^{-1}\mathbb{Z}
and
\Gamma=2\mathbb{Z}\cup 2\mathbb{Z}+a
for
0<a<1
. Then,
\mathcal{E}(\Gamma)
is Riesz basis for
L^{2}(\Omega)
, however, dens
(\Lambda\times\Gamma)\neq 1
.

The goal of this paper is to obtain an analog of
Han-Wang’s Theorem

for Gabor Riesz bases
\mathcal{G}(\chi_{\Omega},\Lambda\times\Gamma)
. More precisely, we address the following question:

Question A
.

Given a Gabor Riesz basis of the form
\mathcal{G}(\chi_{\Omega},\Lambda\times\Gamma)
, what can be inferred about the relationship between the spectral and the geometry of
\Omega
, e.g.:
Is
\mathcal{G}(\chi_{\Omega},\Lambda\times\Gamma)
a Gabor Riesz basis if and only if
\mathcal{E}(\Gamma)
is a Riesz basis for
L^{2}(\Omega)
and
\Omega
is a tiling of
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
by
\Lambda
?

In this paper, we answer
Question A
for special cases of
\Omega
(tilings and multi-tilings).
Our main results are collected in Theorem
1.2
, Theorem
1.3
and Theorem
1.4
, as we shall explain in the following section. Some stability results are also obtained as an outcome of Theorem
1.2
.

Establishing a complete characterization (even in lattice case) remains a challenge, and we end with a discussion of open problems generated by this work.

1.1
Main results

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
be a measurable set, and let
\Gamma
and
\Lambda
be countable subsets of
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
.
Assume that
(\Omega,\Lambda)
is a multi-tiling pair. Then,

(i)

If
(\Omega,\Gamma)
is a frame spectral pair, then
\mathcal{G}(\chi_{\Omega},\Lambda\times\Gamma)
is a frame for
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
.

(ii)

The converse of (
i
) holds when
(\Omega,\Lambda)
is a tiling pair.

Notice that
in the present of conditions (i) and (ii) in the previous theorem, one can explicitly determine the dual Gabor frame. Indeed, assume
\{g_{\gamma}\}_{\gamma\in\Gamma}
is a dual frame for the frame
\{e_{\gamma}\}_{\gamma\in\Gamma}
in
L^{2}(\Omega)
. Then the system
\{g_{\gamma}(\cdot-\lambda)\}_{(\lambda,\gamma)}
forms a dual frame for the Gabor frame
\mathcal{G}(\Omega,\Lambda\times\Gamma)
.

The following theorem establishes the equivalence between the Riesz spectral pair
(\Omega,\Gamma)
and the Gabor Riesz basis
\mathcal{G}(\chi_{\Omega},\Lambda\times\Gamma)
, provided that
\Omega
is a tiling set with respect to
\Lambda
.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
be a measurable set with positive and finite measure, and let
\Gamma
and
\Lambda
be countable subsets of
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
.
If
(\Omega,\Lambda)
is a tiling pair, then the following are equivalent:

(i)

(\Omega,\Gamma)
is a Riesz spectral pair.

(ii)

\mathcal{G}(\chi_{\Omega},\Lambda\times\Gamma)
is a Riesz basis for
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
.

Moreover, if
e_{\lambda}(\gamma)=1
for all
\lambda,\gamma
, then the dual Riesz basis can be expressed explicitly as the collection of all dual Riesz bases on each
\Omega+\lambda
,
\lambda\in\Lambda
.

Remark 1.1
.

The conclusion of Theorem
1.2
also holds when the Riesz condition is replaced by completeness or frame condition.

If the tiling condition does not hold in Theorem
1.2
and
\mathcal{G}(\chi_{\Omega},\Lambda\times\Gamma)
is a Gabor Riesz basis, then
\mathcal{E}(\Gamma)
is a Bessel sequence for
L^{2}(\Omega)
with the unified upper Riesz bound for the Gabor frame
\mathcal{G}(\Omega,\Lambda\times\Gamma)
. By the completeness of the Gabor system,
\Omega+\Lambda
covers
\mathbb{R}^{d}
.

The condition that
\Omega
tiles
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
by
\Lambda
is a sufficient condition in Theorem
1.2
. The next result states that the tiling condition is also a necessary condition
for
\mathcal{G}(\Omega,\Lambda\times\Gamma)
to be a Gabor frame when
\Lambda

and
\Gamma
satisfy additional lattice assumptions.

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
M
and
N
be full rank matrices such that
\det(MN)=1
and
N^{T}M
is an integer matrix.
Assume that
\Omega
multi-tiles
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
by
M({\mathbb{Z}}^{d})
. If the system
\mathcal{G}(\chi_{\Omega},M({\mathbb{Z}}^{d})\times N({\mathbb{Z}}^{d}))
is a Gabor frame, then 1)
\Omega
tiles
\mathbb{R}^{d}
by
M({\mathbb{Z}}^{d})
, and 2)
\mathcal{E}(N({\mathbb{Z}}^{d}))
is an orthogonal basis for
L^{2}(\Omega)
.

It is known that for
\alpha\beta=1
, any Gabor frame with lattice spectrum of the form
\mathcal{G}(g,\alpha\mathbb{Z}^{n}\times\beta\mathbb{Z}^{n})
is also a Gabor Riesz basis
[
13
,
11
]
. Our next result gives conditions for which
not only
is any Gabor frame
G(\chi_{\Omega},M({\mathbb{Z}}^{d})\times N({\mathbb{Z}}^{d}))
a Gabor Riesz basis but it is, surprisingly,
also
a Gabor orthogonal basis.

Theorem 1.4
.

Let
M
and
N
be full rank matrices such that
\det(MN)=1
and
N^{T}M
is an integer matrix. Assume that
\Omega
multi-tiles
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
by
M({\mathbb{Z}}^{d})
. Then, the following are equivalent:

(i)

\mathcal{G}(\chi_{\Omega},M({\mathbb{Z}}^{d})\times N({\mathbb{Z}}^{d}))
is a frame for
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
.

(ii)

\mathcal{G}(\chi_{\Omega},M({\mathbb{Z}}^{d})\times N({\mathbb{Z}}^{d}))
is a Riesz basis for
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
.

(iii)

\mathcal{G}(\chi_{\Omega},M({\mathbb{Z}}^{d})\times N({\mathbb{Z}}^{d}))
is an orthogonal basis for
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
.

(iv)

\Omega
tiles
\mathbb{R}^{d}
by
M({\mathbb{Z}}^{d})
, and

\mathcal{E}(N({\mathbb{Z}}^{d}))
is an orthogonal basis for
L^{2}(\Omega)
.

1.2
Relevant work, comments, and remarks

The motivation of our study has grown from the results of three papers:
[
12
]
,
[
15
]

and

[
5
]
. In their paper in
[
12
]
, Han and Wang proved that the necessary and sufficient conditions for a Gabor systems
\mathcal{G}(\Omega,\Lambda\times\Gamma)
to be an orthogonal basis is that
\Omega
tiles
\mathbb{R}^{d}
by translations by
\Lambda
and
\mathcal{E}(\Gamma)
is an orthogonal basis for
L^{2}(\Omega)
. Han and Wang’s result rules out the possibility that the characteristic function of a unit ball in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
can serve as a window function for a Gabor basis with separable time-frequency shifts domain, as the unit ball neither tiles nor it admits an exponential orthogonal basis
[
7
]
. In
[
15
]
, the second author and Iosevich proved that the characteristic function of the unit ball can never serve as a window function for a Gabor orthogonal basis with respect to any time-frequency shifts domain when
d\neq 1
(mod 4). The results for
d=1
(mod 4) is still an open question. As the Riesz bases are the next best bases after the orthogonal bases, an immediate question that one can ask here is whether or not the unit ball can serve as generator of a Gabor Riesz basis. The answer to this question is still unknown. This is yet another motivating fact for our research in this paper.

In
[
23
]
, the second author and Lai have extended the Han and Wang’s characterization to non-separable time-frequency shifts domain, i.e., the Gabor orthogonal bases
\mathcal{G}(\Omega,S)
where
S\subset\mathbb{R}^{2d}
is a lattice but not in form of
\Lambda\times\Gamma\subset\mathbb{R}^{2d}
. The authors have characterized the Gabor orthogonal bases for a large class of lattices. The characterization in full generality is still open.

In the case where
\Omega
multi-tiles
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
, an analysis of the connections between frame spectral pairs, Riesz spectral pairs, and spectral pairs was done in
[
5
]
. When
\Omega
multi-tiles by a lattice (Definition
2.4
), it is well-known that the set admits an exponential Riesz basis
[
10
,
20
]
.
In the latter case, the first author and Okoudjou have computed the explicit form of the dual Riesz basis

[
6
]
. In this context, one might believe that if
\Omega
is a multi-tiling (but not tiling) with
\Lambda
and
\mathcal{E}(\Gamma)
is a Riesz basis for
L^{2}(\Omega)
, then an analog comparable to Han-Wang’s Theorem may hold and
\mathcal{G}(\Omega,\Lambda\times\Gamma)
is a Riesz basis. Unfortunately, as we observed in Example
1.1
, the necessary density condition may fail, thus the Gabor system
\mathcal{G}(\Omega,\Lambda\times\Gamma)
is not a Riesz basis.

In the following remarks, we comment on the relations between our results and the existing results for orthogonal bases.

Remark 1.2
.

If
\Omega
does not tile, then there is no countable set
\Lambda
such that the Gabor system (
1
) is an orthogonal basis
[
12
,
23
]
.

If
\Omega
tiles by
\Lambda
but
L^{2}(\Omega)
does not admit any exponential Riesz basis, in Theorem
1.2
we prove that for such set there is no countable set
\Gamma\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
such that the system
\mathcal{G}(\chi_{\Omega},\Lambda\times\Gamma)
is a Gabor Riesz basis for
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. Notice that the existence of a domain with no exponential Riesz basis has been recently proved in
[
21
]
.

Remark 1.3
.

While the tiling and spectral property of the set
\Omega
are necessary for a Gabor system (
1
) to be an orthogonal basis, the answer to the question of whether or not the tiling and Riesz spectral criteria are also necessary for a Gabor system to be a Riesz basis is yet unknown for general sets
\Omega
.
However, with prior knowledge on the geometry of
\Omega
(i.e. multi-tiling by a lattice), in Theorem
1.3
we prove the necessity of the criteria.

Remark 1.4
.

Theorem
1.3
is an extension of Han-Wang’s Theorem to Gabor frames for a special class of time-frequency shifts domains. As a result of the theorem,

\Omega
tiles by the lattice
M(\mathbb{Z}^{d})
. Thus, by the Fuglede Conjecture,
\mathcal{E}(M^{-T}(\mathbb{Z}^{d})
is an orthogonal basis for
L^{2}(\Omega)
. However, in Theorem

1.3

the
lattice
N({\mathbb{Z}}^{d})
doesn’t have to be the dual lattice of
M({\mathbb{Z}}^{d})
. This result links Fuglede’s conjecture to the study of Gabor bases as we have demonstrated in Theorem
5.2
. (For the statement of the Fuglede’s conjecture see Sec.
1.4
in this paper. For the history of the conjecture we refer to
[
17
,
3
]
and the reference therein.)

Remark 1.5
.

While the necessity of tiling of the set
\Omega
by
\Lambda
for a Gabor Riesz basis
\mathcal{G}(\Omega,\Lambda\times\Gamma)
is not known yet, for the non-separable case the answer is in general negative.
For example, in

[
8
]
, the authors construct a non-separable Gabor spectrum
S=\cup_{t\in J}\{t\}\times S_{t}
such that
\chi_{[0,1]^{2}}
serves as a Gabor window function for an orthogonal basis, however, the set
[0,1]^{2}
does not tile
\mathbb{R}^{2}
by
J
.

2
Notations and Preliminaries

This section contains basic definitions and notations used in the proofs of the main results.

Definition 2.1
.

Given a measurable set
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
with positive and finite measure, and a discrete and countable set
\Gamma\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
, we say that the collection of functions
\{f_{\gamma}\}_{\gamma\in\Gamma}\subset L^{2}(\Omega)
constitutes a frame for
L^{2}(\Omega)
if there exist constants
0<\ell\leq u<\infty
such that for any
f\in L^{2}(\Omega)
the following estimation holds:

\displaystyle\ell\|f\|^{2}\leq\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma}|\langle f,f_{\gamma}\rangle|^{2}\leq u\|f\|^{2}.

(2)

When the left-hand inequality in (
2
) holds, we say that the system has a lower frame bound. When the right-hand inequality in (
2
) holds, but not necessarily the left-hand inequality, we say that the system has an upper frame bound and the sequence
\{f_{\gamma}\}_{\gamma\in\Gamma}
is called a Bessel sequence.

Definition 2.2
.

We say
\{f_{\gamma}\}_{\gamma\in\Gamma}\subset L^{2}(\Omega)
is a Riesz basis for
L^{2}(\Omega)
if it is the image of an orthonormal basis under an invertible linear map. This definition is equivalent to say that the sequence
\{f_{\gamma}\}_{\gamma\in\Gamma}\subset L^{2}(\Omega)
is a frame for
L^{2}(\Omega)
and for any sequence
\{c_{\gamma}\}\in\ell^{2}(\Gamma)
with finite man non-zero elements, the following estimation holds:

\displaystyle\ell\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma}|c_{\gamma}|^{2}\leq\left\|\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma}c_{\gamma}f_{\gamma}\right\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}\leq u\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma}|c_{\gamma}|^{2},

(3)

where
l
and
u
are the frame bounds in (
2
).
The sequence
\{f_{\gamma}\}_{\gamma\in\Gamma}
is called a Riesz sequence in
L^{2}(\Omega)
if it satisfies the estimation (
3
) but the sequence
\{f_{\gamma}\}_{\gamma\in\Gamma}
is not complete in
L^{2}(\Omega)
.

Definition 2.3

(Analytical pairs)
.

We say
(\Omega,\Gamma)
is a

frame spectral pair
if the collection of exponential functions
\mathcal{E}(\Gamma)=\{e^{2\pi ix\cdot\gamma}:\ \gamma\in\Gamma\}
forms a frame for
L^{2}(\Omega)
, a

Riesz spectral pair
, if
\mathcal{E}(\Gamma)
forms
a Riesz basis for
L^{2}(\Omega)
, and a
spectral pair
if
\mathcal{E}(\Gamma)
is an orthogonal basis for
L^{2}(\Omega)
.

For the analysis of analytical pairs related to multi-tiling domains see the author’s previous paper
[
5
]
.

Definition 2.4

(Multi-tiling sets)
.

A set
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
with positive and finite measure
is called a
multi-tiling set
with respect to a countable set
\Lambda\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
at level
k\in\mathbb{N}
if almost every point
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
can be covered exactly by
k
translations of
\Omega
by elements of
\Lambda
. That is, for a.e.
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, there are exactly
k
vectors
\lambda_{1}(x),\cdots,\lambda_{k}(x)\in\Lambda
such that
x\in\cap_{i=1}^{k}\Omega+\lambda_{i}(x)
. The set is a
tiling
set when
k=1
. That is, almost every point
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
is covered exactly by one
\Lambda
translation of
\Omega

Definition 2.5

(Full rank lattices)
.

A
full rank lattice

\Lambda
in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
is given by
M(\mathbb{Z}^{d})
where
M
is an
d\times d
non-singular matrix. We denote the

volume
of the lattice by
\text{Vol}(\Lambda)
that is defined by
\text{Vol}(\Lambda)=|\det(M)|
. The density of
\Lambda
is given by
\text{dens}(\Lambda)=|\det(M)|^{-1}
.
The
dual lattice
of
\Lambda
is defined as

\Lambda^{\perp}:=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}:\ \lambda\cdot x\in\mathbb{Z},\ \forall\lambda\in\Lambda\}.

Here,
\lambda\cdot x
denotes the inner product of two vectors in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
.
A direct calculation shows that when
\Lambda=M(\mathbb{Z}^{d})
,
\Lambda^{\perp}=M^{-T}(\mathbb{Z}^{d})
, and
M^{-T}
the inverse transpose of
M
.

In this paper, we assume all lattices are full rank lattices.

Definition 2.6

(Fundamental domain of a lattice)
.

Let
\Lambda=M(\mathbb{Z}^{d})
be a full rank lattice in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
.
The fundamental domain of
\Lambda
is a measurable set in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, denote it by
Q_{M}
, which contains distinct representatives (mod
\Lambda
) in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, so that any intersection of
Q_{M}
with any coset
x+\Lambda
has only one element.

Equivalently,
Q_{M}
is a fundamental domain of
\Lambda
if it is a tiling set with respect to
\Lambda
.
The
existence of fundamental domains is proved in Theorem 1 in
[
4
]
. Any translation of a fundamental domain is also a fundamental domain, and
\text{Vol}(\Lambda)=|Q_{M}|
.

Notation:
For positive quantities
A
and
B
, by
A\asymp B
we mean that there are constants
0<c_{1}\leq c_{2}<\infty
such that
c_{1}B\leq A\leq c_{2}B
.

3
Proof of Theorem
1.1
and Theorem
1.2

In this section, we connect properties of Gabor systems
\mathcal{G}(\Omega,\Lambda\times\Gamma)
with frame spectral pairs and Riesz spectral pairs in the case where
\Omega
multi-tiles
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
by
\Lambda
. Here, the sets
\Lambda
and
\Gamma
are countable sets that are not necessarily lattices.

3.1
Multi-tiling sets and Gabor frames - Proof of Theorem
1.1

The proof of Theorem
1.2
(
i
) relies on the following result that describes how multi-tiling sets can be used to express the norm of a function.

Lemma 3.1
.

Assume that
(\Omega,\Lambda)
is a
multi-tiling pair of level
k\in\mathbb{N}
. Then for any
f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
,

\displaystyle\sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda}\|f\|_{L^{2}(\Omega+\lambda)}^{2}=k\|f\|^{2}_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})}.

(4)

Proof.

We rewrite the left-hand side of (
4
) as

\displaystyle\sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda}\|f\|_{L^{2}(\Omega+\lambda)^{2}}=\sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|f(x)\chi_{\Omega+\lambda}(x)|^{2}dx=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda}|f(x)\chi_{\Omega+\lambda}(x)|^{2}dx.

(5)

The exchange of the integral and series is justified by Fubini’s theorem. For a.e.
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
we formally define

g(x):=\sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda}|f(x)\chi_{\Omega+\lambda}(x)|^{2}.

Since
\Omega
is a multi-tiling of
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
with respect to
\Lambda
,

for a.e.
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
there are lattice points
\{\lambda_{i}(x)\in\Lambda\}_{1\leq i\leq k}
such that
x\in\cap_{i=1}^{k}\Omega+\lambda_{i}(x)
.
Therefore,
g(x)
is a finite sum for almost every
x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
and

g(x)=\sum_{i=1}^{k}|f(x)\chi_{\Omega+\lambda_{i}(x)}(x)|^{2}=\sum_{i=1}^{k}|f(x)|^{2}=k|f(x)|^{2}.

Substituting this expression into (
5
), we obtain

\displaystyle\sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda}\|f\|_{L^{2}(\Omega+\lambda)}=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}g(x)dx=k\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|f(x)|^{2}dx=k\|f\|^{2}.

(6)

This completes the proof.
∎

Remark 3.1
.

With the additional assumption that
\Lambda
is a lattice, Lemma
3.1
can also be proved by expressing
\Omega
as the finite union
\Omega=\cup_{i=1}^{k}E_{i}\cup F

where
|F|=0
and
E_{i}
,
1\leq i\leq k
are fundamental domains of
\Lambda
(see
[
20
]
). Then,

\displaystyle\sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda}\|f\|_{L^{2}(\Omega+\lambda)}^{2}=\sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda}\sum_{i=1}^{k}\|f\|_{L^{2}(E_{i}+\lambda)}^{2}=\sum_{i=1}^{k}\left(\sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda}\|f\|_{L^{2}(E_{i}+\lambda)}^{2}\right)=\sum_{i=1}^{k}\|f\|^{2}=k\|f\|^{2}.

We are ready to prove Theorem
1.1
.

Proof of Theorem
1.1
.

(
i
)
Let
(\Omega,\Lambda)
be a multi-tiling pair and
(\Omega,\Gamma)
be a frame spectral pair. We show that
\mathcal{G}(\chi_{\Omega},\Lambda\times\Gamma)
is a frame for
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
.
Let
f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
.
For
\lambda\in\Lambda
we define
f_{\lambda}:=f\chi_{\Omega+\lambda}\in L^{2}(\Omega+\lambda)
.
By the frame spectral property of
(\Omega+\lambda,\Gamma)
we have

\ell\|f\|_{L^{2}(\Omega+\lambda)}^{2}\leq\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma}|\langle f_{\lambda},e_{\gamma}\rangle|_{L^{2}(\Omega+\lambda)}^{2}\leq u\|f\|_{L^{2}(\Omega+\lambda)}^{2},

where
\ell
and
u
denote the frame constants for the exponential frame
\{e^{2\pi ix\cdot\gamma}\}_{\gamma\in\Gamma}\subset L^{2}(\Omega)
. Summing over
\Lambda
, we have

\ell\sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda}\|f\|_{L^{2}(\Omega+\lambda)}^{2}\leq\sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda}\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma}|\langle f_{\lambda},e_{\gamma}\rangle_{L^{2}(\Omega+\lambda)}|^{2}\leq u\sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda}\|f\|_{L^{2}(\Omega+\lambda)}^{2}.

The frame bounds are obtained using Lemma
3.1
,

k\ell\|f\|^{2}\leq\sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda}\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma}|\langle f,e_{\gamma}\chi_{\Omega+\lambda}\rangle_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}|^{2}\leq ku\|f\|^{2}.

(\ref{thm1.1.ii})

Assume that
\Omega
is a tiling set and the Gabor system
\mathcal{G}(\chi_{\Omega},\Lambda\times\Gamma)
is a frame.
For a fixed
\lambda_{0}\in\Lambda
and
f\in L^{2}(\Omega+\lambda_{0})
, we have

\displaystyle\ell\|f\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{2}\leq\sum_{(\lambda,\gamma)\in\Lambda\times\Gamma}\left|\langle f,\chi_{\Omega+\lambda}e_{\gamma}\rangle_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\right|^{2}\leq u\|f\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{2},

(7)

where
\ell
and
u
are the lower and upper frame constants, respectively, for the Gabor frame
\mathcal{G}(\chi_{\Omega},\Lambda\times\Gamma)
.
Since
\Omega
tiles
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
by
\Lambda
, we have that
\langle f,\chi_{\Omega+\lambda}e_{\gamma}\rangle=0
for all
\lambda\neq\lambda_{0}
, and therefore
(\Omega+\lambda_{0},\Gamma)
is a frame spectral pair, i.e.,

\displaystyle\ell\|f\|_{L^{2}(\Omega+\lambda_{0})}^{2}\leq\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma}\left|\langle f,e_{\gamma}\rangle_{L^{2}(\Omega+\lambda_{0})}\right|^{2}\leq u\|f\|_{L^{2}(\Omega+\lambda_{0})}^{2}.

(8)

Now, for any
g\in L^{2}(\Omega)
take
f(x):=g(x-\lambda_{0})\in L^{2}(\Omega+\lambda_{0})
and apply (
8
),
to prove that
(\Omega,\Gamma)
is a frame spectral pair.
∎

The converse of Theorem
1.1
does not hold in general. That is,
when

is a frame and

is a frame spectral pair,

does not necessarily tile
\mathbb{R}^{d}
with

. This is illustrated in the next example.

Example 3.1
.

Take
\Omega=(0,1)
,
\Lambda=2^{-1}\mathbb{Z}
, and
\Gamma=\mathbb{Z}
. The set

\Omega
multi-tiles
\mathbb{R}

at

. Also,
(\Omega,\mathbb{Z})
is a spectral pair. By Theorem
1.1
(
i
),
the system
\mathcal{G}(\chi_{\Omega},2^{-1}\mathbb{Z}\times\mathbb{Z})
is a frame,
and
\Omega
does not tile
\mathbb{R}
by
\Lambda
.

3.2
Tiling sets and Gabor Riesz bases - Proof of Theorem
1.2

Assume that
(\Omega,\Gamma)
is a Riesz spectral pair and that
(\Omega,\Lambda)
is a multi-tiling pair. We have seen in Example
1.2
that these assumptions do not guarantee that
\mathcal{G}(\chi_{\Omega},\Lambda\times\Gamma)
is a Gabor Riesz basis for
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. However, by Theorem
1.1
(
i
), the system is a Gabor frame, and, the Ron-Shen Duality Principle can be applied.

Example 3.2
.

Consider the setting from Example
1.2
:
\Omega=(0,1),\Lambda=2^{-1}\mathbb{Z},\Gamma=\mathbb{Z}
.
The system
\mathcal{G}(\chi_{\Omega},2^{-1}\mathbb{Z}\times\mathbb{Z})
is a frame by Theorem
1.1
(i). By the Ron-Shen Duality Principle,
the system
\mathcal{G}(\chi_{\Omega},\mathbb{Z}\times 2\mathbb{Z})
is a Riesz sequence in
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. From the other hand,
\mathcal{G}(\chi_{\Omega},\mathbb{Z}\times 2\mathbb{Z})\subset\mathcal{G}(\chi_{\Omega},\mathbb{Z}\times\mathbb{Z}).
This implies that

\mathcal{G}(\chi_{\Omega},\mathbb{Z}\times 2\mathbb{Z})
is a Bessel sequence.
However, the system
\mathcal{G}(\chi_{\Omega},\mathbb{Z}\times 2\mathbb{Z})
is not complete.

Theorem
1.2
states that to establish the equivalence of Riesz spectral pairs and Gabor Riesz bases, a stronger condition is needed.

Proof of Theorem
1.2
.

(\ref{thm1.1.i})\Rightarrow(\ref{thm1.1.ii}):

Assume that

(\Omega,\Gamma)
is a Riesz spectral pair and
(\Omega,\Lambda)
is a tiling pair. We show that the Gabor system (
1
) is a Riesz basis.
Assume that
\{a_{\lambda,\gamma}\}_{(\lambda,\gamma)\in\Lambda\times\Gamma}
is a collection of scalar coefficients with finite many non-zero elements. Then, by the tiling property of
\Omega
by
\Lambda
, we have

\displaystyle\left\|\sum_{(\lambda,\gamma)\in\Lambda\times\Gamma}a_{\lambda,\gamma}e_{\gamma}\chi_{\Omega+\lambda}\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\lambda^{\prime}\in\Lambda}\left\|\sum_{(\lambda,\gamma)\in\Lambda\times\Gamma}a_{\lambda,\gamma}e_{\gamma}\chi_{\Omega+\lambda}\right\|_{L^{2}(\Omega+\lambda^{\prime})}^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda}\left\|\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma}a_{\lambda,\gamma}e_{\gamma}\chi_{(\Omega+\lambda)}\right\|_{L^{2}(\Omega+\lambda)}^{2}

(9)

By applying the Riesz inequalities for the inner summation we obtain (
9
)

\eqref{eq:RZ}\asymp\sum_{\lambda}\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma}|a_{\lambda,\gamma}|^{2}

The calculations above
prove that the Gabor system in (
1
) is a Riesz sequence in
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. The completeness holds by Proposition
1.1
.
Moreover, the Riesz frame constants coincide with the frame constants for the Riesz spectral pair
(\Omega,\Gamma)
.

(\ref{thm1.1.ii})\Rightarrow(\ref{thm1.1.i}):
Assume that that
\mathcal{G}(\chi_{\Omega},\Lambda\times\Gamma)
is a Riesz basis and
(\Omega,\Lambda)
is a tiling pair. We will prove that
(\Omega,\Gamma)
is a Riesz spectral pair.
For this, fix
\lambda_{0}\in\Lambda
. By Proposition
1.1
, the pair
(\Omega+\lambda_{0},\Gamma)
is a frame pair. So, we only need to prove that the set of exponential functions
\mathcal{E}(\Gamma)
form a Riesz sequence in
L^{2}(\Omega+\lambda_{0})
.
Let
\{a_{\gamma}\}_{\gamma\in\Gamma}
be a sequence of scalars where only finite many are none-zero. For any
(\lambda,\gamma)\in\Lambda\times\Gamma
, define
b_{\lambda,\gamma}:=a_{\gamma}
if
\lambda=\lambda_{0}
, and
0
otherwise. Therefore
\{b_{\lambda,\gamma}\}_{\Lambda\times\Gamma}
is a sequence with finitely many non-zero elements. By the Riesz inequality for
\mathcal{G}(\chi_{\Omega},\Lambda\times\Gamma)
with the frame constants
0<\ell\leq u<\infty
, we then have

\displaystyle\ell\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma}|b_{\lambda_{0},\gamma}|^{2}\leq\left\|\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma}b_{\lambda_{0},\gamma}\chi_{\Omega+\lambda_{0}}e_{\gamma}\right\|^{2}\leq u\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma}|b_{\lambda_{0},\gamma}|^{2},

(10)

or,

\displaystyle\ell\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma}|a_{\gamma}|^{2}\leq\left\|\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma}a_{\gamma}e_{\gamma}\right\|_{L^{2}(\Omega+\lambda_{0})}^{2}\leq u\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma}|a_{\gamma}|^{2}.

(11)

This proves that
\mathcal{E}(\Gamma)
is a Riesz sequence for
L^{2}(\Omega+\lambda_{0})
with the same Riesz constants as the Gabor Riesz basis. The completeness of the exponentials
\mathcal{E}(\Gamma)
is due to Proposition
1.1
. Thus,
(\Omega+\lambda_{0},\Gamma)
is a Riesz spectral pair. Since the Riesz basis property of
\mathcal{E}(\Gamma)
is invariant under the translation of the domain, we conclude that
(\Omega,\Gamma)
is also a Riesz spectral pair, and that completes the proof of the first part of the theorem.

Next we compute the biorthogonal basis for the Gabor Riesz basis. The biorthogonal basis for a Gabor Riesz basis
\mathcal{G}(\Omega,\Lambda\times\Gamma)
is a unique family of functions
\{g_{\lambda,\gamma}\}_{\Lambda\times\Gamma}
such that for any pairs
(\lambda,\gamma)
and
(\lambda^{\prime},\gamma^{\prime})

\langle e_{\gamma}\chi_{\Omega+\lambda},g_{\lambda,\gamma}\rangle=\delta_{\lambda,\lambda^{\prime}}\delta_{\gamma,\gamma^{\prime}}

Let
(\Omega,\Gamma)
be a Riesz spectral pair, and let
\{g_{\gamma}\}_{\gamma\in\Gamma}
denote the dual Riesz basis in
L^{2}(\Omega)
.
The dual Riesz basis for the
Riesz spectral pair
(\Omega+\lambda,\Gamma)
is given
as

\displaystyle\{e_{\gamma}(\lambda)g_{\gamma}(\cdot-\lambda)\chi_{\Omega+\lambda}\}_{\gamma\in\Gamma}.

(12)

We prove that, under the assumption
e_{\gamma}(\lambda)=1
for all
\gamma,\lambda
, the system

\displaystyle\{g_{\gamma}(\cdot-\lambda)\chi_{\Omega+\lambda}\}_{\gamma\in\Gamma,\lambda\in\Lambda}

(13)

is biorthogonal to
\mathcal{G}(\chi_{\Omega},\Lambda\times\Gamma)
, thus it is the dual basis.
Let
(\lambda,\gamma)\neq(\lambda^{\prime},\gamma^{\prime})
.
If
\lambda\neq\lambda^{\prime}
, due to the disjointess of the sets
\Omega+\lambda
and
\Omega+\lambda^{\prime}
, the functions

e_{\gamma}\chi_{\Omega+\lambda}
and
g_{\gamma^{\prime}}(\cdot-{\lambda^{\prime}})\chi_{\Omega+\lambda^{\prime}}
are orthogonal. Now assume that
\lambda=\lambda^{\prime}
, then we must have
\gamma\neq\gamma^{\prime}
. Orthogonality also holds due the following equality:

\langle e_{\gamma},g_{\gamma^{\prime}}(\cdot-\lambda)\rangle_{L^{2}(\Omega+\lambda)}=\langle e_{\gamma}(\lambda)e_{\gamma},g_{\gamma^{\prime}}\rangle_{L^{2}(\Omega)}=\langle e_{\gamma},g_{\gamma^{\prime}}\rangle_{L^{2}(\Omega)}=\delta_{\gamma,\gamma^{\prime}}.

This completes the proof.
∎

3.3
Stability of Gabor Riesz bases

In this section, we show the stability results with respect to the time-frequency shifts set
\Lambda\times\Gamma
.

Definition 3.1
.

Given a countable set
\Gamma\subset{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}
, we say the sequence
{\Gamma}_{p}=\{\kappa_{\gamma}\}_{\gamma\in\Gamma}\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
is a perturbation of
\Gamma
with constant
c>0
if
|\gamma-\kappa_{\gamma}|<c
for all
\gamma\in\Gamma
.

We recall the classical Paley-Wiener theorem (see e.g.
[
2
]
).

Theorem 3.1

(Paley-Wiener Theorem)
.

Let
\{f_{i}\}_{i\in I}
be basis for the Banach space
X
, let
\{g_{i}\}_{i\in I}
be a family of vectors in
X
. Assume that there exists a constant
\lambda\in[0,1)
such that for any sequence
\{c_{i}\}_{i\in I}
with finite many non-zero elements,

\left\|\sum_{i}c_{i}(f_{i}-g_{i})\right\|\leq\lambda\left\|\sum_{i}c_{i}f_{i}\right\|.

Then
\{g_{i}\}
is a basis for
X
.

Applying the Paley-Wiener theorem, we obtain the following stability result in terms of Kadec’s 1/4 theorem for exponential Riesz bases.

Proposition 3.1

(Kadec’s
1/4
Theorem for exponential Riesz bases)
.

Assume that
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
has finite and positive measure.
Let the system
\mathcal{E}({\Gamma})
be a Riesz basis for

L^{2}(\Omega)
. Then there is a positive constant
0<L<1
such that for any
\Gamma_{p}
, permutation of
\Gamma
, with
0<c\leq L
, the system of exponential functions
\mathcal{E}(\Gamma_{p})
is also a Riesz basis for
L^{2}(\Omega)
.

The set
\mathcal{E}(\Lambda)
is called an unconditional basis for
L^{2}(\Omega)
if it is complete and
each element in
f\in L^{2}(\Omega)
can be represented uniquely as
f=\sum_{\lambda]in\Lambda}c_{\lambda}e^{2\pi i\lambda\cdot x}
where the series converges in norm and unconditionally.

Proposition
3.1
is well-known in dimension
d=1
for unconditional bases (see e.g.
[
26
]
). The result extends to Riesz bases of exponentials in higher dimensions as a consequence of the classical Paley-Wiener Theorem
3.1
as follows:

Proof of Proposition
3.1
.

Any Riesz basis is an unconditional basis, and by the assumptions of Proposition
3.1
and the Paley-Wiener theorem, for any small perturbation
\Gamma_{p}
with
c
sufficiently small (as in Definition
3.1
),
\mathcal{E}(\Gamma_{p})
also is an unconditional basis for
L^{2}(\Omega)
. To prove that
\mathcal{E}(\Gamma_{p})
is a Riesz sequence, notice that
any mapping on
L^{2}(\Omega)
defined by
e^{2\pi i\lambda}\to e^{2\pi i\kappa_{\lambda}}
can be extended to an isomorphism on
L^{2}(\Omega)
. This proves that the Riesz inequalities hold for
\mathcal{E}(\Gamma_{p})
, whence the claim.
∎

We conclude this section with a Paley-Wiener theorem for Gabor orthogonal bases and prove the stability of Gabor Riesz bases with separable time-frequency shifts domain.

Corollary 3.1

(Paley-Wiener theorem for Gabor orthogonal bases)
.

Assume that
\mathcal{G}(\chi_{\Omega},\Lambda\times\Gamma)
is an orthogonal basis for
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. Assume that
\Gamma_{p}=\{\kappa_{\gamma}\}_{\gamma\in\Gamma}\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
is a countable set such that for some constant
0\leq L<1
we have

\displaystyle|\gamma-\kappa_{\gamma}|<L,\quad\forall\gamma\in\Gamma.

(14)

If
L
is small enough, then

\mathcal{G}(\chi_{\Omega},(\Lambda+\alpha)\times\Gamma_{p})
is a Gabor Riesz basis for
L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
,
\alpha>0
.

Proof.

By the assumption that
\mathcal{G}(\chi_{\Omega},\Lambda\times\Gamma)
is an orthogonal basis, the pair
(\Omega,\Lambda)
is a tiling pair and
(\Omega,\Gamma)
is a spectral pair
[
23
]
. By Proposition
3.1
and the condition (
14
),
\mathcal{E}(\Gamma_{p})
is a Riesz basis for
L^{2}(\Omega)
. By applying Theorem
1.2
to the tiling pair
(\Omega,\Lambda+\alpha)
and the Riesz spectral pair
(\Omega,\Gamma_{c})
, the assertion of the corollary holds, i.e.
\mathcal{G}(\chi_{\Omega},(\Lambda+\alpha)\times\Gamma_{c})
is a Gabor Riesz basis for
L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
.
∎

4
Lattice Zak transform

The classical Zak transform is associated with lattices of the form
\alpha{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}
. The case
d=1
was introduced in
[
19
]
. For the definition of the classical Zak transform in higher dimensions see e.g.
[
11
]
. Here, we introduce an extension to the lattice
M(\mathbb{Z}^{d})
.

Definition 4.1

(Lattice Zak transform)
.

For a full rank lattice
M(\mathbb{Z}^{d})
, we formally define the lattice Zak transform of a function
f
by

\displaystyle Z_{M}f(x,\xi):=|Q_{M}|^{1/2}\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}f(x+M(n))e^{2\pi i\xi\cdot M(n)}\quad(x,\xi)\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}.

(15)

When
M=I
, where
I
is the
d\times d
identity matrix, we omit the subscript and denote the Zak transform of
f
by
Zf
.

Let
M
and
N
be any
d\times d
full rank matrix such that
N^{T}M
is a matrix with integer entries. The Zak transform
Z_{M}
clearly possesses
quasi-periodicity
relations with respect to the lattices
M(\mathbb{Z}^{d})
and
N(\mathbb{Z}^{d})
:

\displaystyle Z_{M}f(x+M(m),\xi)

\displaystyle=e^{-2\pi i\xi\cdot M(m)}Z_{M}f(x,\xi)

(16)

\displaystyle Z_{M}f(x,\xi+N(m))

\displaystyle=Z_{M}f(x,\xi).

(17)

The quasi-periodicity of the transform shows that the values of the transform on
\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}
is completely determined by its values on
Q_{M}\times Q_{N}
.

Remark 4.1
.

The definition of the lattice Zak transform in Definition
4.1
coincides with the classical definition of the Zak transform when
M=\text{diag}(\alpha)
is a diagonal
d\times d
matrix with
\alpha
in diagonal entries. The quasi-periodicity is an extension in the classical case when
N=\text{diag}(\beta)
with
\alpha\beta=1
. For some properties of classical Zak transforms see e.g.
[
18
]
and
[
13
]
.

A generalized Zak transform in the setting of unitary group representation theory was defined for arbitrary Hilbert spaces by Hernández and co-authors in
[
14
]
. The definition relies on a unitary map that the authors termed
the Bracket map
. The classical Zak transform is a
restriction of the generalized Zak transform to the case when the Hilbert transform is
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
and the unitary representation (Gabor) is defined on
\alpha\mathbb{Z}^{d}\times\beta\mathbb{Z}^{d}
. For non-commutative cases, e.g. the Heisenberg group, see
[
1
]
.

4.1
Common fundamental domains for lattices

Recall that two full rank lattices in
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
with the same volume have a common fundamental domain that is a tiling with respect to both lattices.

Theorem 4.1

(Theorem 1.1 in
[
12
]
)
.

Let
M(\mathbb{Z}^{d})
and
N(\mathbb{Z}^{d})
be two full rank lattices in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
such that
\text{Vol}(M(\mathbb{Z}^{d}))=\text{Vol}(N(\mathbb{Z}^{d}))
. Then
M(\mathbb{Z}^{d})
and
N(\mathbb{Z}^{d})
have a common fundamental domain. That is, there exists a measurable set
Q\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
such that
Q
is a tiling set with respect to both
M(\mathbb{Z}^{d})
and
N(\mathbb{Z}^{d})
.

An immediate corollary of this theorem is our next result that the same result holds for lattices with the same density.

Corollary 4.1
.

Let
M(\mathbb{Z}^{d})
and
N(\mathbb{Z}^{d})
be two full rank lattices in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
such that
den(M(\mathbb{Z}^{d})\times N(\mathbb{Z}^{d}))=1
.
Then
M(\mathbb{Z}^{d})
and
(N(\mathbb{Z}^{d}))^{\perp}
have common fundamental domains.

Proof.

Note that
den(M(\mathbb{Z}^{d})\times N(\mathbb{Z}^{d}))=1
implies
\det(MN)=1
, or
\det(M)=\det(N^{-1})=\det(N^{-T})
. Since
\text{Vol}(\Lambda)=|\det(M)|=|\det(N^{-T})|=\text{Vol}((N(\mathbb{Z}^{d}))^{\perp})
, the claim follows by Theorem
4.1
.
∎

Another consequence of Theorem
4.1
is our next result which states that orthogonal Gabor bases can be constructed using two full-rank lattices that have a common fundamental domain:

Corollary 4.2
.

Let
M
and
N
be full rank
d\times d
matrices such that
\det(MN)=1
and
N^{T}M
is an integer matrix. Then
\mathcal{E}(N(\mathbb{Z}^{d}))
is an orthogonal basis for
L^{2}(Q)
, where
Q
is the common fundamental domain for the lattices
M(\mathbb{Z}^{d})
and
N^{-T}(\mathbb{Z}^{d})
, and the Gabor system
\mathcal{G}(\chi_{Q},M(\mathbb{Z}^{d})\times N(\mathbb{Z}^{d}))
is an orthogonal basis for
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
.

The assumptions of Corollary
4.2
always hold for the choice
N:=M^{-T}
. In one dimension, this is the only choice for
N
, but in higher dimensions, there are examples of
N
for which
N\neq M^{-T}
and
M^{T}N
is an integer matrix. The next example illustrates this.

Example 4.1
.

Figure 1
:
The fundamental domains for lattices
M({\mathbb{Z}}^{d})
and
{{N}^{-T}}({\mathbb{Z}}^{d})
coincide and are different than the fundamental domain for the lattice
{M^{-T}}({\mathbb{Z}}^{d})
.

Let
M=\begin{bmatrix}\sqrt{2}&0\\
1&1\end{bmatrix}
and

N=\begin{bmatrix}\sqrt{2}^{-1}&-\sqrt{2}\\
0&1\end{bmatrix}
. Then
N\neq M^{-T}
, however
N^{T}M

is an integer matrix. The common fundamental domain for the lattices
\Lambda=M(\mathbb{Z}^{d})
and
\Gamma^{\perp}=N^{-T}(\mathbb{Z}^{d})
is given by the parallelogram with vertices in the set
\{(0,0),(0,1),(\sqrt{2},1),(\sqrt{2},2)\}
. The fundamental domains
Q=Q_{M}=Q_{N^{-T}}
and
Q_{M^{-T}}
are shown in Figure
1
.

More generally, let
a
and
d
be any numbers such that
ad\neq 0
, and let
k,\ell,m,n
be integers such that
\ell\neq 0
,
n\neq m
. Take
M,N
to be the matrices

M=\begin{bmatrix}a&0\\

Then,
\det(MN)=1
,
N^{T}M
is an integer matrix, and

.

4.2
The lattice Zak transform is isometric

Proposition
4.1
justifies the definition and isometry property of the lattice Zak transform.

Proposition 4.1
.

Let
M
and
N
be full rank
d\times d
matrices such that
\det(MN)=1
and
MN^{T}
is an integer matrix. Then the Zak transform is a unitary map from
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
onto
L^{2}(Q_{M}\times Q_{N})
, and for any
f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
,

\displaystyle\|Z_{M}f\|_{L^{2}(Q_{M}\times Q_{N})}=\|f\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}.

(18)

Proof.

We give a sketch of the proof here.
First, we write the left side of (
18
)
in terms of the inner product. From Corollary
4.2
, the exponentials
\mathcal{E}(M({\mathbb{Z}}^{d}))
are orthogonal on
Q_{N}
and
|Q_{M}|=|Q_{N}|^{-1}
. This proves the isometry, thus the injectivity of the map. The proof of subjectivity can be obtained with a similar argument used in Theorem 4.3.2 of
[
13
]
. Indeed, the image of the Gabor orthogonal basis
\mathcal{G}(\chi_{Q_{M}},M(\mathbb{Z}^{d})\times N(\mathbb{Z}^{d}))
for
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
under the map
Z_{M}
is the orthogonal exponential basis
\mathcal{E}(M^{-T}(\mathbb{Z}^{d})\times N^{-T}(\mathbb{Z}^{d}))
for
L^{2}(Q_{M}\times Q_{N})
.
This shows that
Z_{M}
is surjective, completing the proof.
∎

4.3
Zeros of the lattice Zak transforms

In one dimension, the continuity of the Zak transform implies the existence of a zero on the domain (see e.g.
[
19
]
or Theorem 4.3.4 in
[
13
]
and the references therein).
In the
following lemma we extend this result to lattice Zak transforms. The proof follows from a modification of the argument used in dimension
d=1
.

Lemma 4.1
.

For
f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, if the Zak transform
Z_{M}f
is continuous on
\mathbb{R}^{d}\times{\mathbb{R}^{d}}
, then it must have a zero.

Proof.

Assume, for contradiction, that
Z_{M}f
has no zeros. That is,
Z_{M}f(x,\xi)\neq 0
for all
(x,\xi)\in Q_{M}\times Q_{N}
. Consider the mapping from
\mathbb{R}^{d}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\to{\mathbb{R}}
given by

(x,\xi)\mapsto\cfrac{Z_{M}f(x,\xi)}{|Z_{M}f(x,\xi)|}.

The map is well-defined and continuous. Since
\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}
is simply connected, there is a continuous real-valued function
\varphi:\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}
, also known as an
argument function
, such that

\displaystyle e^{2\pi i\varphi(x,\xi)}=\cfrac{Z_{M}f(x,\xi)}{|Z_{M}f(x,\xi)|}\quad\quad\forall~(x,\xi)\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{d},

(19)

due to Lemma 7, Sec. VI.1 in
[
24
]
.

The quasi-periodic properties (
16
) and (
17
) of the Zak transform then imply that for all
(x,\xi)\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
n\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}
,

\displaystyle e^{2\pi i\varphi(x+M(n),\xi)}

\displaystyle=e^{-2\pi i\xi\cdot M(n)}e^{2\pi i\varphi(x,\xi)}

\displaystyle e^{2\pi i\varphi(x,\xi+N(n))}

\displaystyle=e^{2\pi i\varphi(x,\xi)}.

Since
N^{T}M
is an integer matrix, there are integers
k_{(x,\xi,n)}
and
\ell_{(x,\xi,n)}
such that

\displaystyle\varphi(x+M(n),\xi)-\varphi(x,\xi)+\xi\cdot M(n)

\displaystyle=k_{(x,\xi,n)}

(20)

\displaystyle\varphi(x,\xi+N(n))-\varphi(x,\xi)

\displaystyle=\ell_{(x,\xi,n)}.

(21)

Both (
20
) and (
21
) equate a continuous function of
x
and
\xi
with a discrete function of
x
and
\xi
.
This implies that for any
n\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}
,
k_{n}:=k_{(x,\xi,n)}
and
\ell_{n}:=\ell_{(x,\xi,n)}
must be constant for all
(x,\xi)
.
Let
{\bf 0}
denote the zero vector, and
e_{1}
denote
the first vector in standard basis for
\mathbb{R}^{d}
(or
e_{k}
, where
a_{kk}
is the first nonzero diagonal entry of the matrix
N^{T}M
).
We can calculate
\varphi(M(e_{1}),N(e_{1}))
in two different ways using equations (
20
) and (
21
):
First, for
x=0
,
n=e_{1}
and
\xi=N(e_{1})
,

\displaystyle\varphi(M(e_{1}),N(e_{1}))=\varphi({\bf 0}+M(e_{1}),N(e_{1}))

\displaystyle\overbrace{=}^{by~\eqref{eq:varphi1}}\varphi({\bf 0},N(e_{1}))+1+k_{e_{1}}\overbrace{=}^{by~\eqref{eq:varphi2}}\varphi({\bf 0},{\bf 0})+\ell_{e_{1}}+1+k_{e_{1}}.

Secondly,
x=M(e_{1})
,
n=e_{1}
and
\xi=0
, and therefore

\displaystyle\varphi(M(e_{1}),N(e_{1}))=\varphi(M(e_{1}),{\bf 0}+N(e_{1}))

\displaystyle\overbrace{=}^{by~\eqref{eq:varphi2}}\varphi(M(e_{1}),{\bf 0})+\ell_{e_{1}}\overbrace{=}^{by~\eqref{eq:varphi1}}\varphi({\bf 0},{\bf 0})+k_{e_{1}}+\ell_{e_{1}}.

This is a contradiction because the right-hand sides of the last two equations can never be equal. Therefore, the Zak transform must have a zero.
∎

5
Characterization of Gabor systems with lattice time-frequency shifts - Proofs of Theorem
1.3
and Theorem
1.4

This section aims to prove that for given pair of full rank matrices
M
and
N
with
\det(MN)=1
and
N^{T}M
an integer matrix, for a given multi-tiling set
\Omega
with respect to lattice
M(\mathbb{Z}^{d})
, the necessary condition of the Gabor system

\mathcal{G}(\chi_{\Omega},M(\mathbb{Z}^{d})\times N(\mathbb{Z}^{d}))
to be a frame for
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
is that
\Omega
is a tiling. We then conclude that under the assumptions for
M
and
N
, any Riesz Gabor basis
\mathcal{G}(\chi_{\Omega},M(\mathbb{Z}^{d})\times N(\mathbb{Z}^{d}))
is an orthogonal basis.

5.1
Gabor systems for any window and lattice Zak transform

We prove the following result which is similar to the result when the time-frequency shift domain is in the form of
\alpha\mathbb{Z}^{d}\times\beta\mathbb{Z}^{d}
,
\alpha\beta\neq 0
.

Proposition 5.1
.

Assume that
M
and
N
are full rank
d\times d
matrices such that
\det(MN)=1
and
N^{T}M
is an integer matrix.
Let

Z_{M}
be the Zak transform given by (
15
) with respect to

M(\mathbb{Z}^{d})
.
Then for any
g\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
the following holds:

(i)

\mathcal{G}(g,M(\mathbb{Z}^{d})\times N(\mathbb{Z}^{d}))
is complete if and only if
Z_{M}g(x,\xi)\neq 0
for a.e.
x\in Q_{M}
and
\xi\in Q_{N}
.

(ii)

The following statements are equivalent:

(a)

There are constants
0<\ell\leq u<\infty
such that
\ell\leq|Z_{M}g(x,\xi)|\leq u
for. a.e.
x\in Q_{M}
and
\xi\in Q_{N}
.

(b)

\mathcal{G}(g,M(\mathbb{Z}^{d})\times N(\mathbb{Z}^{d}))
is a frame.

(c)

\mathcal{G}(g,M(\mathbb{Z}^{d})\times N(\mathbb{Z}^{d}))
is a Riesz basis.

(iii)

\mathcal{G}(g,M(\mathbb{Z}^{d})\times N(\mathbb{Z}^{d}))
is an orthogonal basis if and only if
|Z_{M}g(x,\xi)|=1
for a.e.
x\in Q_{M}
and
\xi\in Q_{N}
.

Proof.

Here we only prove (i) as the rest can be obtained similarly. As before, take
\Lambda=M(\mathbb{Z}^{d})
and
\Gamma=N(\mathbb{Z}^{d})
. Assume that
Z_{M}g\neq 0
almost everywhere. We prove that the Gabor system is complete. For this, assume that there is a function
h\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
such that
\langle h,g(\cdot-\lambda)e^{2\pi i\gamma\cdot(\cdot)}\rangle=0
for all
\lambda\in\Lambda
and
\gamma\in\Gamma
. Since the Zak transform is a unitary map,

\langle Z_{M}h,e_{\lambda}e_{\gamma}Z_{M}g\rangle=\langle(Z_{M}h)(Z_{M}g),e_{\lambda}e_{\gamma}\rangle=0.

Since
\{e_{\lambda}e_{\gamma}\}_{(\lambda,\gamma)\in\Lambda\times\Gamma}
is a complete system in
L^{2}(Q_{M}\times Q_{N})
, then we must have
(Z_{M}g)(Z_{M}h)=0
a.e. Since
Z_{M}g\neq 0
a.e., by the unitary property of
Z_{M}
, the latter implies that

h=0
a.e. Thus
\mathcal{G}(g,M({\mathbb{Z}}^{d})\times N({\mathbb{Z}}^{d}))
is complete.
The other direction holds because
g\neq 0
and
Z_{M}
is a unitary map.
∎

Our next result applies Proposition
5.1
to characteristic functions of multi-tiling sets.

Corollary 5.1
.

Let
M(\mathbb{Z}^{d})
and
N(\mathbb{Z}^{d})
be full rank lattices such that
\det(MN)=1
and
N^{T}M
is an integer matrix.
Assume that

\Omega\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
is a multi-tiling set with respect to
M(\mathbb{Z}^{d})
at level
k
. Then
\mathcal{G}(\chi_{\Omega},M(\mathbb{Z}^{d})\times N(\mathbb{Z}^{d}))
is complete and has a positive upper Riesz bound.

Proof.

To prove our claim, it is sufficient to show that for
g=\chi_{\Omega}
we have

0<|Z_{M}g(x,\xi)|\leq k\quad\text{for a.e. }(x,\xi)\in Q_{M}\times Q_{N}.

By the multi-tiling property of
\Omega
by lattice
M(\mathbb{Z}^{d})
, for a.e.
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, there are exactly
k
multi-integers
n_{1}(x),\cdots,n_{k}(x)\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}
such that

x\in\cap_{i=1}^{k}\Omega+M(n_{i}(x)),

and there is no other
n
such that
x\in\Omega+M(n)
.
This implies that for almost every
(x,\xi)\in Q_{M}\times Q_{N}
we have

\displaystyle Z_{M}g(x,\xi)=\sum_{i=1}^{k}\chi_{\Omega+M(n_{i}(x))}(x)e^{2\pi i\xi\cdot M(n_{i}(x))}=\sum_{i=1}^{k}e^{2\pi i\xi\cdot M(n_{i}(x))}.

(22)

Now the final result follows by taking the modulus of
Z_{M}g
, using the triangle inequality, and applying Proposition
5.1
(i).
∎

Proposition
5.1
can be used more generally to characterize window functions for Gabor frames, Gabor Riesz bases, and complete Gabor systems with spectrum
M({\mathbb{Z}}^{d})\times N({\mathbb{Z}}^{d})
(specific examples are given later in Section
5.4
).

5.2
Multi-tiling and lattice Zak transform

Suppose that
\Omega
multi-tiles
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
by a lattice
\Lambda
at level
k
.

The following key lemma gives the existence of an open set
D\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
in the intersection of translates of
\Omega
by
k
distinct points in
\Lambda
.

Lemma 5.1
.

Let
\Omega
be a multi-tiling set at level
k\geq 1
with respect to a lattice
M({\mathbb{Z}}^{d})
. Then, for each
x\in Q_{M}
there is an open set
D
containing
x
and lattice points
\lambda_{i}=\lambda_{i}(x)\in M({\mathbb{Z}}^{d}),1\leq i\leq k
, for which
|D|>0
and

D\subset\cap_{i=1}^{k}\left(\Omega+\lambda_{i}\right).

Proof.

Without loss of generality, assume that
\Omega
is an open set. Let
Q_{M}
denote a fundamental domain of the lattice, and
let
x\in Q_{M}
. By the
k-
tiling property there are exactly
k
lattice points
\lambda_{i}(x)\in M({\mathbb{Z}}^{d})
such that
x\in\cap_{i=1}^{k}(\Omega+\lambda_{i}(x))
.
Since the intersection of finitely many open sets is an open set, there exists an open neighborhood
N_{x}\subset Q_{M}
of
x
such that
N_{x}\subset\cap_{i=1}^{k}\left(\Omega+\lambda_{i}(x)\right)
.

Now, assume for contradiction that there is a
\lambda\in M({\mathbb{Z}}^{d})\backslash\{\lambda_{i}(x)\}_{i=1}^{k}
such that
N_{x}\cap(\Omega+\lambda)\neq\emptyset
. Let
t\in N_{x}\cap(\Omega+\lambda)
. Since
N_{x}
is open it contains a neighborhood
N_{x,t}\subset N_{x}
of
t
such that
N_{x,t}\subset\Omega+\lambda
.
This implies that almost every point of
N_{x,t}
is covered by at least
k+1

translations of
\Omega
by the set
M({\mathbb{Z}}^{d})
, contradicting the
k
-tiling property of
\Omega
.
Taking
D:=N_{x}
and
\lambda_{i}=\lambda_{i}(x)
, we have that
D\subset Q_{M}
and
D\subset\cap_{i=1}^{k}(\Omega+\lambda_{i})
. This completes the proof.
∎

Theorem 5.1
.

Assume that
M
and
N
are full rank
d\times d
matrices such that
\det(MN)=1
and
N^{T}M
is an integer matrix.
Let
g=\chi_{\Omega}
, where
\Omega
is a multi-tiling set at level
k\geq 1
with respect to the lattice
\Lambda=M(\mathbb{Z}^{d})
. If
k>1
, then
Z_{M}g
given as in (
15
) has a zero in
Q_{M}\times Q_{N}
.

Proof.

As before,
Q_{M}
denote a fundamental domain of the lattice
\Lambda=M(\mathbb{Z}^{d})
.
By Lemma
5.1
, for any
x\in Q_{M}
there exists an open set
x\in D\subset Q_{M}
and lattice points
\{\lambda_{i}\}_{i=1}^{k}\subset\Lambda
such that
D\subset\cap_{i=1}^{k}(\Omega+\lambda_{i})
, and
k
is the maximum number of lattice points
\lambda_{i}
such that
D
intersects
\Omega+\lambda
.
Thus for a.e.
\xi\in Q_{N}

we have

\displaystyle Z_{M}g(x,\xi)=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\chi_{\Omega+M(n)}(x)e^{2\pi iM(n)\cdot\xi}=\sum_{i=1}^{k}\chi_{\Omega+M(n_{i})}(x)e^{2\pi iM(n_{i})\cdot\xi}=\sum_{i=1}^{k}e^{2\pi iM(n_{i})\cdot\xi}.

(23)

Therefore, the function
Zg
is continuous in
Q_{M}\times Q_{N}
,
and by Lemma
4.1
, its zero set is not empty.
∎

Remark:
Notice that the continuity of the Zak transform in Theorem
5.1
is directly obtained from the fact that for any point
(x_{0},\xi_{0})
and small neighborhood of it, the lattice points
M(n_{i})
are same. Indeed, let
(x_{n},\xi_{n})\to(x_{0},\xi_{0})
. Then for any
\epsilon>0
there is an
N_{0}>0
such that for any
n>N_{0}
,
|(x_{n},\xi_{n})-(x_{0},\xi_{0})|<\epsilon
, by Lemma
5.1
the set of finite lattice points are the same: For all
n>N_{0}
,
\{M(n_{i}(x_{n}))\}_{i=1}^{k}=\{M(n_{i}(x_{0}))\}_{i=1}^{k}
.

5.3
Proofs of Theorem
1.3
and Theorem
1.4

The Fuglede Conjecture asserts that any tiling set in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
admits an orthogonal basis of exponential forms and vice versa. In 1974, Fuglede proved the assertion under the lattice assumption. Indeed, he proves that if (T)
\Omega
tiles
\mathbb{R}^{d}
by translation of a lattice
\Lambda
, then (S)
\mathcal{E}(\Lambda^{\perp})
is an orthogonal exponential basis for
L^{2}(\Omega)
and vice versa. Below, in Theorem
5.2
we prove that if the set tiles by a lattice, then it has exponential basis with respect to a class of certain lattices. This can be considered as a slight
extension
of Fuglede’s result for lattices in direction (T)
\Rightarrow
(S).

Theorem 5.2

(
T\Rightarrow S
direction of Fuglede Conjecture holds for two lattices)
.

Let
M(\mathbb{Z}^{d})
and
N(\mathbb{Z}^{d})
be full rank lattices such that
\det(MN)=1
and
N^{T}M
is an integer matrix.
Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
be a domain with finite and positive measure. If
\Omega
tiles
\mathbb{R}^{d}
by
M(\mathbb{Z}^{d})
, then
\mathcal{E}(N(\mathbb{Z}^{d}))
is an orthogonal exponential basis for
L^{2}(\Omega)
.

Proof.

Let
g=\chi_{\Omega}
. If
\Omega
is a tiling set, by a similar calculation as in the proof of Corollary
5.1
we can show that
|Z_{M}g|=1
a.e. on
Q_{M}\times Q_{N}
. Thus by Proposition
5.1
the system
\mathcal{G}(\chi_{\Omega},M(\mathbb{Z}^{d})\times N(\mathbb{Z}^{d}))
is an orthogonal basis. The Gabor basis is separable, thus by a result in
[
23
]

\mathcal{E}(N(\mathbb{Z}^{d}))
is an orthogonal basis for
L^{2}(\Omega)
.
∎

Now we are ready to prove our two main results here.

Proof of Theorem
1.3
.

If
\Omega
multi-tiles
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
by
M(\mathbb{Z}^{d})
at level
k>1
, then by Theorem
5.1
the Zak transform has a zero, therefore it can not be bounded from below. This contradicts the frame assumption of the Gabor system. Therefore it has to tile. Since
\Omega
tiles with the lattice
\Lambda
, then by Theorem
5.2
the exponential set
\mathcal{E}(N(\mathbb{Z}^{d}))
form an orthogonal basis for
L^{2}(\Omega)
. This completes the proof.
∎

Proof of Theorem
1.4
.

We only prove that if

\mathcal{G}(\chi_{\Omega},M(\mathbb{Z}^{d})\times N(\mathbb{Z}^{d}))
is frame, then the orthogonality holds. Indeed, by the lower frame bound and
Theorem
1.3
, the set
\Omega
must tile
\mathbb{R}^{d}
by
M(\mathbb{Z}^{d})
, and by Theorem
5.2
the exponentials set
\mathcal{E}(N(\mathbb{Z}^{d}))
is an orthogonal basis for
L^{2}(\Omega)
. Therefore,
\mathcal{G}(\chi_{\Omega},M(\mathbb{Z}^{d})\times N(\mathbb{Z}^{d}))
is an orthogonal basis
[
23
]
.
∎

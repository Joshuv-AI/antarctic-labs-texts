Essential spectrum and Feller type properties
Ali BenAmor, Batu Güneysu, Peter Stollmann

Abstract
We give necessary and sufficient conditions for a regular semi-Dirichlet form to enjoy a new Feller type property, which we call weak Feller property . Our characterization involves potential theoretic as well as probabilistic aspects and seems to be new even in the symmetric case. As a consequence, in the symmetric case, we obtain a new variant of a decomposition principle of the essential spectrum for (the self-adjoint operators induced by) regular symmetric Dirichlet forms and a Persson type theorem, which applies e.g. to Cheeger forms on 𝖱𝖢𝖣 ∗ \mathsf{RCD^{*}} spaces.

Introduction

Let
X
be a locally compact separable metrizable space equipped with a positive Radon measure
m
with full support. Let
\mathscr{E}
be a regular semi-Dirichlet form on
L^{2}(X)
with
H
the associated sectorial operator and
T_{t}:=e^{-tH}
the associated semigroup. Given a subset
A\subset X
we denote by
\mathrm{cap}(A)
the induced capacity, and given an open subset
U\subset X
we denote by
H_{U}
the restriction of
H
to
U
, which is a sectorial operator in
L^{2}(U)\subset L^{2}(X)
. We refer the reader to Section
2
for detailed definitions. The starting point for our investigations is the following result from
[
LS19
]
:

Theorem 1.1
.

Assume
\mathscr{E}
is symmetric and spatially locally compact, in the sense that
\mathbf{1}_{A}T_{t}
is compact for all
t>0
and all Borel sets
A\subset X
with
m(A)<\infty
. Then for all open
U\subset X
with
\mathrm{cap}(X\setminus U)<\infty
one has
\sigma_{\mathrm{ess}}(H_{U})=\sigma_{\mathrm{ess}}(H)
.

Although the class of
U
’s considered in this result is very large (in fact, somewhat optimal), the restriction on the underlying geometry through the spatial local compactness assumption is rather strong: for example, there exist
\mathsf{RCD}^{*}
spaces of finite measure such that the (operator induced by the) Cheeger form (cf. Example
4.3
for the definitions) does not have a purely discrete spectrum and thus is not spatially locally compact. Nontrivial examples of such spaces are provided by the natural Dirichlet forms (cf. Example
4.2
) of certain complete Riemannian manifolds with Ricci curvature bounded from below: indeed, noncompact hyperbolic manifolds with finite volume are never spatially locally compact (as these always have an nonempty essential spectrum
[
MP90
]
). The main goal of this paper is to obtain a variant of Theorem
1.1
, which allows to treat geometries such as arbitrary
\mathsf{RCD}^{*}
spaces. To this end, we found a surprising connection between such results and probability theory. Namely, we say that
\mathscr{E}
has the
weak Feller property
, if the following two properties are satisfied:

(
\alpha)

with
L^{\infty}_{0}(X)
the space of all
u\in L^{\infty}(X)
such that for all
\epsilon>0
there exists
K\subset X
compact such that
\|1_{X\setminus K}u\|_{\infty}<\epsilon
, one has

T_{t}(L^{\infty}_{0}(X))\subset L^{\infty}_{0}(X)\quad\text{for all $t>0$.}

(\beta)

For any compact
K\subset X
there exists a function
0\leq w\in L^{2}(X)\cap L^{\infty}_{0}(X)
with
(H+1)^{-1}w\geq\mathbf{1}_{K}
.

Recall that
\mathscr{E}
is said to
induce a Feller semigroup
, if

\displaystyle T_{t}(C_{0}(X))\subset C_{0}(X)\quad\text{for all $t>0$},

(1.1)

\displaystyle\|T_{t}\phi-\phi\|_{\infty}\to 0\quad\text{ as $t\to 0+$ for all $\phi\in C_{0}(X)$},

(1.2)

with
C_{0}(X)
the space functions on
X
that vanish at
\infty
. In general, this property implies the weak Feller property and these notions are equivalent on Riemannian manifolds, but there exist
\mathscr{E}
’s which have the weak Feller property but not the Feller property (cf. Section
3
and Section
4
). We provide a list of equivalent characterizations of the weak Feller property under the above condition (
\alpha
), one of which is the traditional probabilistic condition that compact sets are hard to hit from close to infinity by the underlying diffusion: namely, whenever
K
is compact and
\sigma_{K}
denotes the induced first hitting time, then for all
t\geq 0
one has

\mathds{P}^{\bullet}\{\sigma_{K}\leq t\}\in L^{\infty}_{0}(X),

(1.3)

keeping in mind that by the seminal work of Azencott
[
Aze74
]
it is well-known that
in the Riemannian case
, (
1.3
) is
equivalent
to the Feller property.

With this definition, one of our main results is:

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
\mathscr{E}
be symmetric and satisfy the weak Feller property, and let
\mathscr{E}
be
weakly
spatially local compact, in the sense that
\mathbf{1}_{K}T_{t}
is compact for all compact
K\subset X
and all
t>0
. Then for every open
U\subset X
with
X\setminus U
compact one has
\sigma_{\mathrm{ess}}(H_{U})=\sigma_{\mathrm{ess}}(H)
.

The weak Feller property enters the proof of Theorem
1.2
precisely in the form (
1.3
), through a method from
[
LS19
]
.

Concerning the assumptions of Theorem
1.2
we remark that the natural Dirichlet form on a connected Riemannian manifold is automatically weakly spatially locally compact and satisfies the condition (
\beta
), and has the (weak) Feller property, e.g., if the manifold is complete and its Ricci curvature does not decay to fast to
-\infty
; likewise, the Cheeger energy of an arbitrary
\mathsf{RCD}^{*}
space as well as many jump diffusion Dirichlet forms satisfy all assumptions of the above theorem (cf. Example
4.2
, Example
4.3
, Example
4.4
and Section
5
).

Finally, we obtain a variant of Persson’s theorem, which states that under the same assumptions on
\mathscr{E}
as in Theorem
1.2
one has

\inf\sigma_{\mathrm{ess}}(H)=\lim_{K\to X}\inf\sigma(H_{X\setminus K}),

and we show that these assumptions on
\mathscr{E}
are satisfied, if
\mathscr{E}
has the doubly Feller property in the sense of
[
KKT17
]
, that is, if
\mathscr{E}
has the Feller property in addition to
T_{t}(L^{\infty}(X))\subset C_{0}(X)
for all
t>0
.

For completeness, we have also included an appendix, where we show that every semi-Dirichlet form whose semigroup satisfies (

) is automatically regular, a result that comes in handy e.g. for possibly nonsymmetric jump diffusions.

3
The weak Feller property

Recall that by definition
\mathscr{E}
induces a Feller semigroup, if one has (
1.1
). The generalization of this property that we seek for relies on the space

L^{\infty}_{0}(X):=\bigl\{u\in L^{\infty}(X)\bigm|\text{for all}\ \epsilon>0,\,\exists\ K\subset X\ \text{compact s.t.}\ \|u1_{X\setminus K}\|_{\infty}<\epsilon\bigr\}

of bounded functions that vanish at
\infty
in the measure theoretic sense.

Definition 3.1
.

(
\alpha
)
We say that
\mathscr{E}
satisfies the
L^{\infty}_{0}
–
diffusion property
, provided

T_{t}(L^{\infty}_{0}(X))\subset L^{\infty}_{0}(X).

(3.1)

(
\beta
) We say that
\mathscr{E}
satisfies property
(P)
, if for any compact
K\subset X
there is
w\in L^{2}(X)\cap L^{\infty}_{0}(X),w\geq 0
such that
G_{1}w\geq\mathbf{1}_{K}
.

(
\gamma
) We say that
\mathscr{E}
satisfies the
weak Feller property
, if it has the
L^{\infty}_{0}
–diffusion property and if
(P)
holds.

We discuss a number of sufficient conditions:

Remark 3.2
.

(1)

The following property implies
(P)
:

(P”)
: for any compact
K\subset X
there exists
w\in L^{2}(X)\cap L^{\infty}_{0}(X),w\geq 0
and
t^{\prime}>0
such that for all
0<t<t^{\prime}
one has
T_{t}w\geq\mathbf{1}_{K}
, which follows from

G_{1}=\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-t}T_{t}dt,

where the improper Riemannian integral is well-defined in the norm topology of
L^{p}(X)
if
p\in[1,\infty)
, and in the weak-*-topology of
L^{\infty}(X)
.

(2)

If
\mathscr{E}
induces a Feller semigroup, then it also has the weak Feller property: indeed, given a compact
K\subset X
pick
0\leq w\in C_{c}(X)
with
w\geq c
in
K
for some
c>0
. Then, since
t\mapsto T_{t}
is strongly continuous at
t=0
in
C_{0}(X)
, we can pick a
c^{\prime}>0
and
t^{\prime}>0
such that
T_{t}w\geq c^{\prime}
in
K
for all
0<t<t^{\prime}
, so that the claim follows from the previous item.

(3)

Property
(P)
is equivalent to:

(P’)
: there is
w\in L^{2}(X)\cap L^{\infty}_{0}(X),w\geq 0
such that for any compact
K\subset X
one has

G_{1}w\geq\mathbf{1}_{K}.

(3.2)

(4)

By local compactness and the semigroup property, the
L^{\infty}_{0}
–diffusion property is equivalent to the following property:

T_{t}\mathbf{1}_{K}\in L^{\infty}_{0}(X)\quad\text{ for all compact $K\subset X$, $0<t<1$.}

We are now in position to give a characterization of the weak Feller property:

Theorem 3.3
.

Under assumption
(P)
, the following assertions are equivalent:

(1)

\mathscr{E}
satisfies the
L^{\infty}_{0}
–diffusion property.

(2)

For any compact
K\subset X
,
t\geq 0
one has
\mathds{P}^{\bullet}\{\sigma_{K}\leq t\}\in L^{\infty}_{0}(X).

(3)

For any compact
K\subset X
one has
e_{K}\in L^{\infty}_{0}(X).

(4)

For any compact
K\subset X
there exists an
1
-excessive function
\phi\in\mathscr{F}
with
\mathbf{1}_{K}\leq\phi\in L^{\infty}_{0}(X).

Proof.

(4)
\Rightarrow
(3): Let
K
as in (3) and pick a relatively compact,
open
B\supset K
. By (4) there exists an
1
-excessive
\phi\in L^{\infty}_{0}(X)\cap\mathscr{F}
such that

\mathbf{1}_{B}\leq\phi
. From Remark
2.1
,(3) above, we get that
e_{B}\leq\phi
and so

e_{K}\leq e_{B}\in L^{\infty}_{0}(X).

(3)
\Rightarrow
(2): follows from

\mathds{P}^{\bullet}\{\sigma_{K}\leq t\}\leq e^{t}e_{K}.

(2)
\Rightarrow
(1): follows from Remark
3.2
, (4) and

T_{t}\mathbf{1}_{K}=\mathds{E}^{\bullet}[\mathbf{1}_{K}\circ X_{t}]\leq\mathds{P}^{\bullet}\{\sigma_{K}\leq t\}.

(1)
\Rightarrow
(4): Pick

as in
(P)
and set

\phi:=G_{1}w=\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-t}T_{t}wdt.

(3.3)

Condition

and evidently,

-excessive and belongs to

. Finally, by the

Examples of weakly Feller

Example 4.1

(Multiplication operators)

\displaystyle\mathscr{F}=\bigl\{f\in L^{2}(X)\bigm|h^{\frac{1}{2}}f\in L^{2}(X)\bigr\}

5
The decomposition principle

Here, we state some spectral theoretic consequences of Feller type properties, mainly addressing the issue of stability of the essential spectrum. Since functional calculus will be involved,

we fix a regular symmetric Dirichlet form
\mathscr{E}
throughout this section,

with
H
,
T_{t}
denoting, as usual, the associated self adjoint operator and semigroup and
\mathscr{E}_{U}
,
H_{U}
,
T^{U}_{t}
the corresponding restriction to an open
U\subset X
, as discussed in Section
2
. We denote by
\left\|\cdot\right\|
the operator norm in
L^{2}(X)
.

Our main aim is to show that

\sigma_{\mathrm{ess}}(H)=\sigma_{\mathrm{ess}}(H_{X\setminus K})

provided
K
is compact and
H
is suitable. The essential spectrum of a self-adjoint operator
S
can be characterized as

\sigma_{\mathrm{ess}}(S)=\bigl\{\lambda\in\mathbb{C}\bigm|S-\lambda\mbox{ is not a Fredholm operator}\bigr\},

see
[
RS78
, Chapter XIII.14]
. It was introduced by H. Weyl in a slightly different form in
[
Wey10
]
, who also proved its essential property, namely that it is invariant under compact perturbations, see
[
VW09
]
.

Definition 5.1
.

We say that
\mathscr{E}
satisfies the
weak spatial local compactness property
, if

\mathbf{1}_{K}T_{t}
is a compact operator for every compact
K\subset X
and every
t>0
.

Clearly, this property is weaker than spatial local compactness, introduced in
[
LS19
, Definition 2.1]
which requires that
\mathbf{1}_{A}T_{t}
is a compact operator for every Borel set
A\subset X
with
m(A)<\infty
.

The main point is the following variant of the corresponding result in
[
LS19
]
:

Lemma 5.2
.

Let
\mathscr{E}
satisfy the weak Feller property.
Let
K\subset X
be compact. Then there is a
sequence
(K_{n})_{n\in\mathds{N}}
of compact sets such that for every
t>0

\lim_{n\to\infty}\left\|\mathbf{1}_{K_{n}}\left(T_{t}-T_{t}^{X\setminus K}\right)-\left(T_{t}-T_{t}^{X\setminus K}\right)\right\|=0.

(5.1)

This result is proved exactly as
[
LS19
, Corollary 2.5.]
, where

K_{n}\supset\{e_{K}>1/n\}

can be chosen compact in view of the
L^{\infty}_{0}
–diffusion property of
\mathscr{E}
, for
n
large enough. With exacly the same argument as in
[
LS19
]
, one can now prove:

Theorem 5.3

(Decomposition principle)
.

Let
\mathscr{E}
satisfy the weak Feller property and
H
the weak spatial local compactness property.
Let
K\subset X
be compact. Then the operator

\varphi(H_{X\setminus K})-\varphi(H)
is compact for every
\varphi\in C_{0}(\mathds{R})
. In
particular,

\sigma_{\mathrm{ess}}(H_{X\setminus K})=\sigma_{\mathrm{ess}}(H).

Remark 5.4
.

The
\mathscr{E}
’s from Example
4.2
, Example
4.3
and Example
4.4
(assuming symmetry in the last case) are weakly locally compact: indeed, in each case
T_{t}
has a jointly continuous integral kernel, which shows that the Hilbert-Schmidt norm of the integral operator
\mathbf{1}_{K}T_{t}
is finite for all compact
K\subset X
,
t>0
. Thus, for
\mathsf{RCD}^{*}
-spaces and symmetric jump diffusions Theorem
5.3
applies directly. In the Riemannian case, one has to guarantee the Feller property, which holds, as noted above, if the manifold is complete with a Ricci curvature that does not decay to fast to
\infty
. In fact, as shown in
[
DL79
]
with completely different methods, the decomposition principle holds in the Riemannian case without any assumptions on the geometry. From this point of view, the main strenght of Theorem
5.3
stems from the fact that it can deal with many local and nonlocal situations
simultaniously
.

We get the following variant of the Persson type theorem from
[
LS19
]
,
where
\lim_{K\to X}
stands for the limit along the net of
compact subsets of
X
:

Theorem 5.5

(Persson’s theorem)
.

Let
\mathscr{E}
satisfy the weak Feller property and
H
the weak spatial local compactness property. Then,

\inf\sigma_{\mathrm{ess}}(H)=\lim_{K\to X}\inf\sigma(H_{X\setminus K}).

Finally, we present further results that connect Feller type properties with the stability of the essential spectrum. The first one shows that the compactness of
\varphi(H_{X\setminus K})-\varphi(H)
deduced in Theorem
5.3
actually implies the corresponding spatial local compactness property:

Proposition 5.6
.

Let
B\subset X
be closed,
U:=X\setminus B
. If the operator

\varphi(H_{U})-\varphi(H)
is compact for every
\varphi\in C_{0}(\mathds{R})
then
\mathbf{1}_{B}T_{t}
is a compact operator as well.

We have chosen the assumption and the conclusion so as to fit the above results, many different equivalent formulations are at hand:

Remark 5.7
.

Let
U\subset X
be open and
B\subset X
be closed.

1. The following properties are equivalent:

•

\varphi(H_{U})-\varphi(H)
is compact for every
\varphi\in C_{0}(\mathds{R})
,

•

T_{t}^{U}-T_{t}
is compact for one (every)
t>0
,

•

D_{\lambda}:=G_{\lambda}-(H_{U}+\lambda)^{-1}
is compact for one (every)
\lambda>0
.

2. The following properties are equivalent:

•

\mathbf{1}_{B}\varphi(H)
is compact for every
\varphi\in C_{0}(\mathds{R})
,

•

\mathbf{1}_{B}T_{t}
is compact for one (every)
t>0
,

•

\mathbf{1}_{B}G_{\lambda}
is compact for one (every)
\lambda>0
.

For the proof of the second statement, see
[
LSW10
, Thm 1.3]
; the first statement can be deduced with the help of the Stone-Weierstrass theorem, see proof of Thm. 2.3 in
[

]
.

Proof of Prop.
5.6
.

By
[

, Lemma 3]

D_{1}:=G_{1}-(H_{U}+1)^{-1}=(\check{H}^{1/2}JG_{1})^{*}\check{H}^{1/2}JG_{1},

where

and
\check{H}
is the selfadjoint operator of the trace of

w.r.t. the map

. Accordingly,

is compact if and only if
\check{H}^{1/2}JG_{1}
is compact as well. Since

, we get that

is compact, whence the assertion in view of the preceding remark, part (2).
∎

Proposition 5.8
.

Let
\mathscr{E}
satisfy the Feller property. If

is compact for some relatively compact open

, then

is compact for every compact subset

Let

With an argument just as in

(5.2)

Since

Our goal here is to prove the following result, which should be known to experts:

Theorem 6.1
.

Assume one has

\displaystyle T_{t}(C_{0}(X))\subset C_{0}(X)\quad\text{for all $t>0$},

(6.1)

\displaystyle\|T_{t}\phi-\phi\|_{\infty}\to 0\quad\text{as $t\to 0+$ for all $\phi\in C_{0}(X)$}.

(6.2)

Then
\mathscr{E}
is regular.

The proof relies on the following auxiliary results:

Proposition 6.2
.

The following statements are equivalent:

•

\mathscr{E}
is regular.

•

\mathscr{F}\cap C_{0}(X)
is dense in
(\mathscr{F},\left\|\bullet\right\|_{\mathscr{E}})
and in
(C_{0}(X),\left\|\bullet\right\|_{\infty})
.

Proof.

It suffices to show that every
\phi\in\mathscr{F}\cap C_{0}(X)
can be approximated by a sequence in
\mathscr{F}\cap C_{c}(X)
with respect to
\left\|\bullet\right\|_{\mathscr{E}}
. To this end, by the Markovian property of
\mathscr{E}
, we can assume
\phi\geq 0
. Then one has
\phi_{n}:=(\phi-1/n)_{+}\in\mathscr{F}\cap C_{c}(X)
by the Markovian property,
\phi_{n}\to\phi
in
L^{2}(X)
, and moreover
\sup_{n}\mathscr{E}(\phi_{n},\phi_{n})<\infty
. Thus, a subsequence of
\phi_{n}
does the job.
∎

Proposition 6.3
.

Under (
6.1
) and (
6.2
), the space
D_{0}:=\dom(H)\cap C_{0}(X)
is dense in
(\dom(H),\left\|\bullet\right\|_{H})
and in
(C_{0}(X),\left\|\bullet\right\|_{\infty})
.

Proof.

Note first that in this situation
(T_{t};t\geq 0)
becomes a strongly continuous contraction semigroup in
(C_{0}(X),\left\|\bullet\right\|_{\infty})
. Thus, one has

\displaystyle G_{\alpha}\phi=\int^{\infty}_{0}e^{-\alpha t}T_{t}\phi\ dt\quad\text{for every $\alpha>0$, $\phi\in C_{0}(X)$,}

(6.3)

where the integral converges in the uniform norm.

1. With

D_{1}:=\bigl\{G_{\alpha}\phi\bigm|\phi\in C_{c}(X),\alpha>0\bigr\},

in view of (
6.3
), one has
D_{1}\subset D_{0}
. Given
g\in\dom(H)
we have
f:=G_{1}g\in L^{2}(X)
, so there exists a sequence
f_{n}
in
C_{c}(X)
with
f_{n}\to f
in
L^{2}(X)
. It follows that with
g_{n}:=G_{1}f_{n}\in D_{1}
we have

g_{n}\to g\quad\text{in $(\dom(H),\left\|\bullet\right\|_{H})$,}

showing that
D_{1}
is dense in
(\dom(H),\left\|\bullet\right\|_{H})
.

2. Given
\phi\in C_{c}(X)
we have
\alpha G_{\alpha}\phi\in D_{1}
for all
\alpha>0
and

\alpha G_{\alpha}\phi\to\phi\quad\text{in $(C_{0}(X),\left\|\bullet\right\|_{\infty})$, as $\alpha\to\infty$,}

in view of formula (
6.3
): indeed, given
\epsilon>0
we can pick
\delta>0
such that for all
t\in[0,\delta]
one has
\left\|T_{t}\phi-\phi\right\|_{\infty}<\epsilon/2
. It then follows easily from decomposing the integral in (
6.3
) as
\int^{\infty}_{0}\cdots=\int^{\delta}_{0}\dots+\int^{\infty}_{\delta}\cdots
that for all
\alpha>0
one has

\left\|\alpha G_{\alpha}\phi-\phi\right\|_{\infty}\leq\epsilon/2+2\left\|\phi\right\|_{\infty}\alpha e^{-\alpha\delta}.

∎

Proof of Theorem
6.1
.

As the embedding

(\dom(H),\left\|\bullet\right\|_{H})\hookrightarrow(\mathscr{F},\left\|\bullet\right\|_{\mathscr{E}})

has a dense image, the second proposition gives that
\mathscr{F}\cap C_{0}(X)
is dense in both,
(\dom(H),\left\|\bullet\right\|_{H})
and
(C_{0}(X),\left\|\bullet\right\|_{\infty})
, so that the claim follows from the first proposition.
∎

Abstract
. The main goal of this paper is to determine for which odd prime numbers r r can an elliptic curve E E defined over ℚ \mathbb{Q} have an r r -isogeny over ℚ ⁡ ( ζ r ) \mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{r}) . We study this question under various assumptions on the 2-torsion of E E . Apart from being a natural question itself, the mod r r representations attached to such E E arise in the Darmon program for the generalized Fermat equation of signature ( r , r , p ) (r,r,p) , playing a key role in the proof of modularity of certain Frey varieties in the recent work of Billerey, Chen, Dieulefait and Freitas.

11G05

1.
Introduction

In their recent work
[
4
]
, Billerey, Chen, Dieulefait and Freitas develop an approach to the generalized Fermat equation of signature
(r,r,p)
based on ideas from Darmon’s program
[
7
]
and a construction of Frey hyperelliptic curves due to Kraus. The results of the present paper play a key role in their proof of the modularity of the Jacobians of these Frey hyperelliptic curves and other abelian varieties considered by Darmon.

Let
r\geq 3
be a prime number. For an elliptic curve
E
defined over a number field
K
, we denote by
{\overline{\rho}}_{E,r}:G_{K}\to\operatorname{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{r})
its mod
r
Galois representation (after fixing a basis for the
r
-torsion module
E[r]
). The present paper is concerned with the (ir)reducibility of the representation
{\overline{\rho}}_{E,r}
where
K=\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{r})
and
E
is a base change to
K
of an elliptic curve defined over
\mathbb{Q}
.

In some cases our results will also hold for certain elliptic curves
E/\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{r})
with
j(E)\in\mathbb{Q}
, not just those which are base changes of elliptic curves defined over
\mathbb{Q}
; in those instances we will state our results in this greater generality.

The reducibility of
{\overline{\rho}}_{E,r}:G_{\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{r})}\to\operatorname{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{r})
is equivalent to
E/\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{r})
having an isogeny of degree
r
over
\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{r})
. We study this question under various assumptions on the
2
-torsion of
E
.

All code for Magma computations used to verify the claims in the paper can be found at

https://github.com/F-Najman/r-isogenies
.

2.
Elliptic curves with
r
-isogenies over
\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{r})

In this subsection we determine when do elliptic curves
E
defined over
\mathbb{Q}
, or in some instances with
j(E)\in\mathbb{Q}
, have an
r
-isogeny over
\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{r})
. Having an
r
-isogeny over
\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{r})
is equivalent to
{\overline{\rho}}_{E,r}(G_{\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{r})})
being conjugate in
\operatorname{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{r})
to a subgroup of a Borel subgroup.

For a subgroup
G\leq\operatorname{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{r})
, define
S(G):=G\cap\operatorname{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{r})
. Denote by
C_{s}(r)
the subgroup of diagonal matrices in
\operatorname{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{r})
and by
C_{s}^{+}(r)
its normalizer in
\operatorname{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{r})
. Let
\epsilon=-1
if
r\equiv 3\pmod{4}
and otherwise let
\epsilon\geq 2
be the smallest integer which is not a quadratic residue modulo
r
. Denote by
C_{ns}(r)
the group

C_{ns}(r):=\left\{\begin{pmatrix}a&\epsilon b\\
b&a\end{pmatrix},(a,b)\in\mathbb{F}_{r}^{2}\backslash\{(0,0)\}\right\},

whose conjugates in
\operatorname{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{r})
are the non-split Cartan subgroups and let

(2.1)

C_{ns}^{+}(r):=\left\{\begin{pmatrix}a&\epsilon b\\
b&a\end{pmatrix},(a,b)\in\mathbb{F}_{r}^{2}\backslash\{(0,0)\}\right\}\cup\left\{\begin{pmatrix}c&\epsilon d\\
-d&-c\end{pmatrix},(c,d)\in\mathbb{F}_{r}^{2}\backslash\{(0,0)\}\right\}

be the normalizer of
C_{ns}(r)
in
\operatorname{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{r})
.

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
r
be an odd prime. The group
S(C_{s}^{+}(r))
is conjugate in
\operatorname{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{r})
to

\left\{\begin{pmatrix}a&0\\
0&a^{-1}\end{pmatrix},\begin{pmatrix}0&a\\
-a^{-1}&0\par\end{pmatrix},a\in\mathbb{F}_{r}^{\times}\right\}.

The group
S(C_{ns}^{+}(r))
is the subset of matrices in (
2.1
) with determinant
1
. The groups
S(C_{s}^{+}(r))
and
S(C_{ns}^{+}(r))
are not conjugate in
\operatorname{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{r})
to a subgroup of a Borel subgroup.

Proof.

It is straightforward to check that
S(C_{s}^{+}(r))
and
S(C_{ns}^{+}(r))
are of the claimed form and that
S(C_{s}^{+}(r))
acts freely on
\mathbb{F}_{r}^{2}\backslash\{(0,0)\}
, from which it follows that the length of each orbit is
2(r-1)
. As a fixed
1
-dimensional subspace would give an orbit of length
\leq r-1
, it follows that there are no fixed
1
-dimensional subspaces of
\mathbb{F}_{r}^{2}
.

The group
S(C_{ns}^{+}(r))
has order
2(r+1)
since
\det:C_{ns}^{+}(r)\rightarrow\mathbb{F}_{r}^{\times}
is onto. Examine the action of
S(C_{ns}^{+}(r))
on
\mathbb{F}_{r}^{2}\backslash\{(0,0)\}
. The characteristic polynomial of a matrix in
S(C_{ns}^{+}(r))
is either
x^{2}-2ax+1
or
x^{2}+1
and hence
1
is a root of it only in the first case when
a=1
, which happens only for the identity matrix. We conclude that
S(C_{ns}^{+}(r))
acts freely on
\mathbb{F}_{r}^{2}\backslash\{(0,0)\}
and hence the orbit of any
(x,y)
has cardinality
2(r+1)
and again we can conclude that there are no fixed
1
-dimensional subspaces of
\mathbb{F}_{r}^{2}
.

∎

We will need the following result of Zywina (
[
17
, Proposition 1.13.]
); see
[
9
, Appendix B]
for a published proof.

Proposition 2.3

(Zywina)
.

Suppose that
E/\mathbb{Q}
does not have CM,
r\geq 17
,
(r,j(E))\notin\{(17,-17\cdot 373^{3}/2^{17}),(17,-17^{2}\cdot 101^{3}/2),(37,-7\cdot 11^{3}),(37,-7\cdot 137^{3}\cdot 2083^{3})\}
and
{\overline{\rho}}_{E,r}
is not surjective. Then

(1)

If
r\equiv 1\pmod{3}
, then
{\overline{\rho}}_{E,r}(G_{\mathbb{Q}})
is conjugate in
\operatorname{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{r})
to
C_{ns}^{+}(r)
.

(2)

If
r\equiv 2\pmod{3}
, then
{\overline{\rho}}_{E,r}(G_{\mathbb{Q}})
is conjugate in
\operatorname{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{r})
to either
C_{ns}^{+}(r)
or

G_{3}(r):=\left\{a^{3},\ a\in C_{ns}(r)\right\}\cup\left\{\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\
0&-1\end{pmatrix}a^{3},\ a\in C_{ns}(r)\right\}.

Remark 2.4
.

The recent results of Furio and Lombardo
[
8
]
prove that if
r\geq 37
, then the case (2) in
Proposition

2.3
is not possible.

Given an elliptic curve
E
defined over
\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{r})
with
j(E)\neq 0,1728
, having an
r
-isogeny and a certain amount of
2
-torsion is a property depending only on the
j
-invariant, so we can (and will in the proofs of the propositions below) suppose that
E
is defined over
\mathbb{Q}
. This is justifiable because if
E
is not defined over
\mathbb{Q}
and
j(E)\in\mathbb{Q}\backslash\{0,1728\}
, then
E
has a quadratic twist
E^{\prime}
defined over
\mathbb{Q}
and
E
and
E^{\prime}
will have the same
2
-torsion structure and isogenies of the same degrees over
\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{r})
, and so we can replace
E
by
E^{\prime}
in the arguments.

A subgroup
G
of
\operatorname{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{r})
is called
applicable
(cf.
[
17
, Definition 2.1]
) if
\det G=\mathbb{F}_{r}^{\times}
and
G
contains an element of trace
0
and determinant
-1
(which is the image of complex conjugation). Note that
{\overline{\rho}}_{E,r}(G_{\mathbb{Q}})
is always an applicable subgroup
[
17
, Proposition 2.2]
.

Proposition 2.5
.

Let
r
be an odd prime,
E/\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{r})
with
j(E)\in\mathbb{Q}
be an elliptic curve without complex multiplication (CM), such that
E
has an
r
-isogeny over
\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{r})
and

(2.6)

(r,j(E))\notin\left\{(7,\frac{3^{3}\cdot 5\cdot 7^{5}}{2^{7}})\right\}\\
\cup\left\{\left(5,\frac{5^{4}t^{3}(t^{2}+5t+10)^{3}(2t^{2}+5t+5)^{3}(4t^{4}+30t^{3}+95t^{2}+150t+100)^{3}}{(t^{2}+5t+5)^{5}(t^{4}+5t^{3}+15t^{2}+25t+25)^{5}}\right),t\in\mathbb{Q}\right\}.

Then
E
has an
r
-isogeny over
\mathbb{Q}
.

Proof.

As explained above we can assume that
E
is defined over
\mathbb{Q}
. Suppose first
r\geq 17
. We have shown in Lemma
2.2
that
S(C_{ns}^{+}(r))
does not fix any
1
-dimensional subspace of
\mathbb{F}_{r}^{2}
. Assume now
r\equiv 2\pmod{3}
. The group
\{a^{3},a\in C_{ns}(r)\}
is cyclic of order
\frac{r^{2}-1}{3}
, and the map
t\mapsto t^{3}
is an automorphism of
\mathbb{F}_{r}^{\times}
, so we conclude that
\#S(G_{3}(r))=\frac{2(r+1)}{3}
.

Now we study the action of
S(G_{3}(r))
on
\mathbb{F}_{r}^{2}\backslash\{(0,0)\}
. Since
S(G_{3}(r))\leq S(C_{ns}^{+}(r))
this action is free. Hence the orbit of any
(x,y)
has cardinality
2(r+1)/3
. It is again easy to see that there cannot be a fixed
1
-dimensional subspace of
\mathbb{F}_{r}^{2}
, as otherwise there would be orbits
S_{1},\ldots,S_{k}
in
\mathbb{F}_{r}^{2}\backslash\{(0,0)\}
such that the sum of their lengths is
r-1
. This is clearly impossible.
Now applying Proposition
2.3
completes the proof for
r\geq 17
.

To deal with
r<17
we note that
E
has an
r
-isogeny over
\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{r})
but no
r
-isogeny over
\mathbb{Q}
if and only if
{\overline{\rho}}_{E,r}(G_{\mathbb{Q}})
acts irreducibly on
\mathbb{F}_{r}^{2}
, but
{\overline{\rho}}_{E,r}(G_{\mathbb{Q}})\cap\operatorname{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{r})
acts reducibly on
\mathbb{F}_{r}^{2}
. A brute force search among all proper applicable subgroups of
\operatorname{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{r})
yields, up to conjugacy, one possible subgroup for
r=5
, 2 groups for
r=7,11
and
13
each.

For
r=13
the candidate groups are of index 182 and 546, which are not possible by
[
17
, Theorem 1.8 and Remark 1.9]
and
[
2
, Theorem 1.1.]
. For
r=11
the candidate groups are of index 132 and 264, which are not possible by
[
17
, Theorem 1.6]
.

For
r=7
, the two candidate groups are of index 56 and 112, and both are possible by
[
17
, Theorem 1.5]
; they are conjugate in
\operatorname{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{7})
to the groups denoted
G_{1}
and
H_{1,1}
in
[
17
, Section 1.4]
. As
G_{1}=\pm H_{1,1}
and our conditions are quadratic-twist invariant, by
[
17
, Theorem 1.5]
, it follows that
E
has a
7
-isogeny over
\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{7})
, but no
7
-isogeny over
\mathbb{Q}
, if and only if
j(E)=\frac{3^{3}\cdot 5\cdot 7^{5}}{2^{7}}
.

For
r=5
the only candidate group we find is conjugate in
\operatorname{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{5})
to the group denoted
G_{3}
in
[
17
, Section 1.3]
. By
[
17
, Theorem 1.4. (ii)]
,
{\overline{\rho}}_{E,3}\subseteq G_{3}
if and only if
j(E)
is of the form given in (
2.6
).

∎

Proposition 2.8
.

Suppose that
r\geq 5
, that
E/\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{r})
has
CM
and either
j(E)\in\mathbb{Q}\backslash\{0,1728\}
or if
j(E)\in\{0,1728\}
that
E
is a base change of an elliptic curve defined over
\mathbb{Q}
. The following are equivalent:

(a)

The curve
E
has an
r
-isogeny over
\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{r})
.

(b)

Every elliptic curve
E^{\prime}/\mathbb{Q}
with
j(E^{\prime})=j(E)
has an
r
-isogeny over
\mathbb{Q}
.

(c)

r
divides
D
, where
-D
is the discriminant of the imaginary quadratic number field containing the endomorphism ring of
E
.

Proof.

We can assume that
E=E^{\prime}
, i.e.,
E
is a base change of an elliptic curve defined over
\mathbb{Q}
, using for
j(E)\notin\{0,1728\}
the same argumentation as before
Proposition

2.5
.

For
j(E)\neq 0
, this follows directly from
[
17
, Proposition 1.14]
and Lemma
2.2
.

Suppose
j(E)=0
. By
[
17
, Proposition 1.16]
if
r\equiv 2,5,8\pmod{9}
then
\overline{\rho}_{E,r}(G_{\mathbb{Q}})
contains the subgroup
G_{3}(r)
defined in
Proposition

2.3
. Using the same argumentation as in the proof of Proposition
2.5
we conclude that
E
has no
r
-isogeny over
\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{r})
.

Suppose
r\equiv 1,4,7\pmod{9}
. By
[
17
, Proposition 1.16]
it follows that
\overline{\rho}_{E,r}(G_{\mathbb{Q}})
contains the subgroup
G
of
C_{s}^{+}(r)
consisting of matrices of the form
\begin{pmatrix}a&0\\
0&b\end{pmatrix}

and
\begin{pmatrix}0&a\\
b&0\end{pmatrix}

with
a/b\in(\mathbb{F}_{r}^{\times})^{3}
. Suppose that
\overline{\rho}_{E,r}(G_{\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{r})})
is reducible, so
S(G)
fixes a 1-dimensional subspace of
\mathbb{F}_{r}^{2}
generated by
(x,y)
. Let
t\in\mathbb{F}_{r}
such that
t^{3}\neq t^{-3}
; such a
t
exists for all
r\neq 7
. Then for

B:=\begin{pmatrix}t^{3}&0\\
0&t^{-3}\end{pmatrix}\in S(G)
, since we have
B(x,y)=(t^{3}x,t^{-3}y)=\beta(x,y)
for some
\beta\in\mathbb{F}_{r}^{\times}
we conclude that either
x=0
or
y=0
.

Since
A=\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\
-1&0\end{pmatrix}\in S(G)
we have
A(x,y)=(y,-x)=\alpha(x,y)
for some
\alpha\in\mathbb{F}_{r}^{\times}
, so
y=\alpha x=-\alpha^{2}y
, which implies that if one of
x
or
y
is equal to 0, then so is the other. The equality
y=\alpha x=-\alpha^{2}y
also eliminates the case
r=7
as
-1
is not a square in
\mathbb{F}_{7}^{\times}
, so again we get
y=x=0
. This gives a contradiction and the result.
∎

3.
Elliptic curves with a
2
-torsion point and reducible mod
r
Galois representations

In this section we prove results about surjectivity of mod
r
Galois representations of elliptic curves
E/\mathbb{Q}
with a point of order
2
over
\mathbb{Q}
.

Proposition 3.1
.

Let
E/\mathbb{Q}
be a non-CM elliptic curve such that
E
has a
\mathbb{Q}
-rational
2
-torsion point and let
r\geq 11
be a prime number. Then, we have
{\overline{\rho}}_{E,r}(G_{\mathbb{Q}})=\operatorname{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{r})
. In particular,
E
has no isogeny of degree
r
over
\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{r})
.

Proof.

Denote by
j(E)
the
j
-invariant of
E
and assume for a contradiction that the representation
{\overline{\rho}}_{E,r}:G_{\mathbb{Q}}\rightarrow\operatorname{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{r})
is not surjective. We first deal with the case
r=13
. Recall that we are in one of the following situations:

(1)

{\overline{\rho}}_{E,13}(G_{\mathbb{Q}})
is included in a Borel subgroup of
\operatorname{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{13})
;

(2)

{\overline{\rho}}_{E,13}(G_{\mathbb{Q}})
is included in the normalizer of a Cartan subgroup of
\operatorname{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{13})
;

(3)

the projective image
H_{E,13}
of
{\overline{\rho}}_{E,13}(G_{\mathbb{Q}})
is isomorphic to
A_{4}
,
S_{4}
, or
A_{5}
.

We eliminate (
1
) with a result of Mazur-Vélu
[
14
]
: If
E
had a
13
-isogeny, then it would have a
26
-isogeny over
\mathbb{Q}
, which is impossible. The main result of
[
1
]
states that case (
2
) does not occur. Finally,
H_{E,13}
is not isomorphic to
A_{5}
as the order of
\operatorname{PGL}_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{13})
is not divisible by
5
. Therefore
H_{E,13}\subset S_{4}
and according to
[
2
, Theorem 1.1 and Section 5.1]
(see also
[
3
, Corollary 1.9]
), we have that

j(E)\in\left\{2^{4}\cdot 5\cdot 13^{4}\cdot 17^{3}/3^{13},-2^{12}\cdot 5^{3}\cdot 11\cdot 13^{4}/3^{13},\right.\\
\left.2^{18}\cdot 3^{3}\cdot 13^{4}\cdot 127^{3}\cdot 139^{3}\cdot 157^{3}\cdot 283^{3}\cdot 929/(5^{13}\cdot 61^{13})\right\}.

For each of these three values, we check that any elliptic curve with that
j
-invariant has trivial
2
-torsion over
\mathbb{Q}
, which shows that the case (
3
) is not possible.

From now on, assume
r\geq 11
and
r\neq 13
. We first show that
{\overline{\rho}}_{E,r}(G_{\mathbb{Q}})
is conjugate to a subgroup of
C_{ns}^{+}(r)
. For
r=11
, this follows from
[
17
, Theorem 1.6]
after checking that elliptic curves with
j
-invariant
-11^{2}
and
-11\cdot 131^{3}
have no non-trivial
2
-torsion over
\mathbb{Q}
. If
r\geq 17
, this follows from Proposition
2.3
(see also
[
17
, Theorem 1.11]
), unless we have
j(E)\in\{-17\cdot 373^{3}/2^{17},-17^{2}\cdot 101^{3}/2,-7\cdot 11^{3},-7\cdot 137^{3}\cdot 2083^{3}\}
. However, we check as before that these
j
-invariants correspond to elliptic curves with trivial rational
2
-torsion. Hence we have proved that
{\overline{\rho}}_{E,r}(G_{\mathbb{Q}})
is conjugate to a subgroup of
C_{ns}^{+}(r)
.

According to
[
10
, Proposition 2.1]
, we therefore have
j(E)\in\mathbb{Z}
. Since moreover
E
has a rational point of order
2
, its
j
-invariant
j(E)
is of the shape

j(E)=\frac{(t+16)^{3}}{t}

for some
t\in\mathbb{Q}
(
[
5
, p. 179]
) and hence one can see from
[
10
, p. 142]
(eliminating the CM
j
-invariants) that

j(E)\in\{-2^{2}\cdot 7^{3},-2^{4}\cdot 3^{3},-2^{6},2^{7},2^{4}\cdot 5^{3},2^{11},2^{2}\cdot 3^{6},2^{7}\cdot 3^{3},17^{3},2^{5}\cdot 7^{3},2^{5}\cdot 3^{6},2^{4}\cdot 17^{3},\\
2^{3}\cdot 31^{3},2^{2}\cdot 3^{6}\cdot 7^{3},2^{2}\cdot 5^{3}\cdot 13^{3},2\cdot 127^{3},2\cdot 3^{3}\cdot 43^{3},257^{3}\}.

For each element in this list, we find an elliptic curve over
\mathbb{Q}
with the corresponding
j
-invariant such that all of its mod
p
representations are surjective for
p>3
prime. We check this in LMFDB
[
13
]
. In particular, its mod
r
representation is surjective. Since the mod
r
representation of a single quadratic twist is surjective if and only if it is surjective for all quadratic twists, the same conclusion holds for
E
. This gives the desired contradiction.

The last statement follows from the fact that
{\overline{\rho}}_{E,r}(G_{\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{r})})={\overline{\rho}}_{E,r}(G_{\mathbb{Q}})\cap\operatorname{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{r})=\operatorname{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{r})
has order greater than the order of a Borel subgroup of
\operatorname{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{r})
.
∎

Proposition 3.2
.

The only elliptic curves
E/\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{7})
with
j(E)\in\mathbb{Q}
, a point of order
2
and an isogeny of degree
7
over
\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{7})
are those with
j(E)\in\{-3^{3}\cdot 5^{3},3^{3}\cdot 5^{3}\cdot 17^{3}\}
. Furthermore,
E/\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{7})
with
j(E)\in\mathbb{Q}
has full
2
-torsion over
\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{7})
and a
7
-isogeny if and only if we have
j(E)=-3^{3}\cdot 5^{3}.

Proof.

For
r=7
we compute
X_{0}(14)(\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{7}))\simeq\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}\oplus\mathbb{Z}/6\mathbb{Z}
(
X_{0}(14)
is an elliptic curve). Of these
12
points, 6 are rational;
4
are cusps and
2
correspond to elliptic curves over
\mathbb{Q}
with
j
-invariants
3^{3}\cdot 5^{3}\cdot 17^{3}
and
-3^{3}\cdot 5^{3}
. Elliptic curves with either of these
j
-invariants have a single
2
-torsion point over
\mathbb{Q}
, multiplication by an order of
\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-7})
, and hence a
7
-isogeny over
\mathbb{Q}
. Recall that an elliptic curve with at least one
2
-torsion point has full
2
-torsion over a number field
k
if and only if its discriminant is a square in
k
and that taking a different model of the curve changes the discriminant by a 12th power, so having
2
-torsion is model-invariant.
Hence, to check whether they acquire full
2
-torsion over
\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{7})
, it remains to check whether their discriminant is a square over
\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{7})
. For
j(E)=-3^{3}\cdot 5^{3}
, we have
\Delta(E)\in-7\cdot(\mathbb{Q}^{\times})^{2}
and for
j(E)=3^{3}\cdot 5^{3}\cdot 17^{3}
we have
\Delta(E)\in 7\cdot(\mathbb{Q}^{\times})^{2}
. As
-7
is a square in
\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{7})
and
7
is not, we conclude that only elliptic curves with
j(E)=-3^{3}\cdot 5^{3}
have full
2
-torsion and a
7
-isogeny over
\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{7})
.

Of the remaining
6
points which are not rational,
4
correspond to
j
-invariants which are not
\mathbb{Q}
-rational, and
2
more points correspond to the
j
-invariant
-3^{3}\cdot 5^{3}
. The fact that there are 3 points in
X_{0}(14)(\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{7}))
corresponding to the
j
-invariant
-3^{3}\cdot 5^{3}
is explained by the fact that an elliptic curve with
j
-invariant
-3^{3}\cdot 5^{3}
has full
2
-torsion over
\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{7})
and hence 3
G_{\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{7})}
-invariant subgroups of order
14
.

∎

Remark 3.3
.

For
r=3
and
5
there exist infinitely many rational elliptic curves with a point of order
2
and an isogeny of degree
r
, as the modular curves
X_{0}(6)
and
X_{0}(10)
have genus 0 and have rational cusps. Also, there are infinitely many elliptic curves with full
2
-torsion and reducible mod
3
Galois representations; any elliptic curve with
\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}\times\mathbb{Z}/6\mathbb{Z}
torsion over
\mathbb{Q}
is such an example.

4.
Elliptic curves with full
2
-torsion and reducible mod
r
Galois representations

In the next two propositions we determine the elliptic curves with
j(E)\in\mathbb{Q}
with both full
2
-torsion and an
r
-isogeny over
\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{r})
.

Proposition 4.1
.

Suppose that
r\geq 5
, that
E/\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{r})
has CM and either
j(E)\in\mathbb{Q}\backslash\{0,1728\}
or if
j(E)\in\{0,1728\}
that
E
is a base change of an elliptic curve defined over
\mathbb{Q}
. Suppose that
r
divides the discriminant of the imaginary quadratic field containing the endomorphism ring of
E
over
\overline{\mathbb{Q}}
. Then
E
has full
2
-torsion and an
r
-isogeny over
\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{r})
if and only if
r=7
and
j(E)=-3^{3}\cdot 5^{3}
.

Proof.

We can assume that
E
is defined over
\mathbb{Q}
, using for
j(E)\notin\{0,1728\}
the same argumentation as before
Proposition

2.5
.

By
Proposition

2.8
it follows that if
E
has an
r
-isogeny over
\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{r})
, then
E
has an
r
-isogeny over
\mathbb{Q}
. If
{\overline{\rho}}_{E,2}(G_{\mathbb{Q}})=\operatorname{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{2})
, then obviously
E
does not have full
2
-torsion over
\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{r})
(which is abelian over
\mathbb{Q}
). Hence by
[
17
, Proposition 1.15]
we have

j(E)\in\left\{2^{4}\cdot 3^{3}\cdot 5^{3},2^{3}\cdot 3^{3}\cdot 11^{3},-3^{3}\cdot 5^{3},3^{3}\cdot 5^{3}\cdot 17^{3},2^{6}\cdot 5^{3}\right\}.

By
Proposition

2.8
,
E
has an
r
-isogeny over
\mathbb{Q}
if and only if
r
divides
D
, where
-D
is the discriminant of the imaginary quadratic number field containing the endomorphism ring of
E
. For
j(E)\in\left\{2^{4}\cdot 3^{3}\cdot 5^{3},2^{3}\cdot 3^{3}\cdot 11^{3},2^{6}\cdot 5^{3}\right\},

D
will be divisible by only
2
or
3
(see e.g.
[
17
, Table 1]
), so we need only consider elliptic curves with
j(E)=-3^{3}\cdot 5^{3}
and
3^{3}\cdot 5^{3}\cdot 17^{3}
. Now the result follows from
Proposition

3.2
.

∎

Proposition 4.2
.

Suppose that
r\geq 5
, that
E/\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{r})
and either
j(E)\in\mathbb{Q}\backslash\{0,1728\}
or if
j(E)\in\{0,1728\}
that
E
is a base change of an elliptic curve defined over
\mathbb{Q}
. Then if
E
has an
r
-isogeny and full
2
-torsion over
\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{r})
, then
j(E)=-3^{3}\cdot 5^{3}
and
r=7
.

Proof.

As before we can assume that
E
is a defined over
\mathbb{Q}
, using for
j(E)\notin\{0,1728\}
the same argumentation as before
Proposition

2.5
.

As we have already dealt with elliptic curves with CM in
Proposition

4.1
, it remains to deal with elliptic curves without CM.

For
r\geq 17
, we check that for all elliptic curves with
j(E)\in\{-17\cdot 373^{3}/2^{17},-17^{2}\cdot 101^{3}/2,-7\cdot 11^{3},-7\cdot 137^{3}\cdot 2083^{3}\}
, we have
\Delta(E)\in-10\cdot(\mathbb{Q}^{\times})^{2}
for the first 2
j
-invariants, and
\Delta(E)\in-5\cdot(\mathbb{Q}^{\times})^{2}
for the last two
j
-invariants. Neither
\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-5})
nor
\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-10})
are contained in
\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{17})
or
\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{37})
and now the claim follows from
Proposition

2.3
and Lemma
2.2
.

For
r=5
we compute that
X_{0}(20)(\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{5}))=X_{0}(20)(\mathbb{Q})
(
X_{0}(20)
is an elliptic curve). As an elliptic curve with full
2
-torsion and an
r
-isogeny would be
2
-isogenous to an elliptic curve with a
4r
-isogeny (see
[
16
, Lemma 7]
), we are done with this case as
X_{0}(20)(\mathbb{Q})
consists of cusps
[
11
]
.

The case
r=7
has already been dealt with in
Proposition

3.2
.

For
r=11
, we compute
X_{0}(11)(\mathbb{Q})=X_{0}(11)(\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{11}))
, so the only elliptic curves with
11
-isogenies over
\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{11})
are those with
j(E)\in\{-11^{2},-2^{15},-11\cdot 131^{3}\}
. Elliptic curves with these
j
-invariants have trivial torsion over
\mathbb{Q}
, so cannot gain any
2
-torsion over a number field of degree not divisible by
3
.

Finally, for
r=13
, by
[
17
, Theorem 1.8.]
, the discussion after it and
[
1
, Theorem 1.1 and Theorem 1.3]
, we know that
{\overline{\rho}}_{E,13}(G_{\mathbb{Q}})
for a non-CM elliptic curve has to be
\operatorname{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{13})
, contained in a conjugate of a Borel subgroup or conjugate to the group
G_{7}
(using Zywina’s notation)

G_{7}:=\left\langle\begin{pmatrix}2&0\\
0&2\end{pmatrix},\begin{pmatrix}2&0\\
0&3\end{pmatrix},\begin{pmatrix}0&-1\\
1&0\end{pmatrix},\begin{pmatrix}1&1\\
-1&1\end{pmatrix}\right\rangle.

We compute in Magma that the orbits of
S(G_{7})
acting on
\mathbb{F}_{13}^{2}\backslash\{(0,0)\}
are all of length 24, so elliptic curves with such image do not have
13
-isogenies over
\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{13})
. We conclude that if a non-CM elliptic curve has a
13
-isogeny over
\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{13})
, it has one already over
\mathbb{Q}
. As there are no
26
-isogenies over
\mathbb{Q}

[
14
]
, an elliptic curve with a
13
-isogeny over
\mathbb{Q}
cannot have a 2-torsion point over
\mathbb{Q}
. Since
E
gains full
2
-torsion over
\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{13})
, it follows that
\mathbb{Q}(E[2])
is a subfield of
\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{13})
and hence
{\overline{\rho}}_{E,2}(G_{\mathbb{Q}})
is cyclic of order 3. But there are no such curves, which can be seen from
[
6
, Table A8]
in the line [2Cn,13B]; this shows that the modular curve whose rational points parametrize elliptic curves
E/\mathbb{Q}
having simultaneously
{\overline{\rho}}_{E,2}(G_{\mathbb{Q}})
cyclic of order 3 (denoted 2Cn) and a
13
-isogeny (denoted 13B) has
2
rational points, both of which can easily be seen to be cusps.

∎

For
r=5
and
7
, if we assume that
E
is an elliptic curve over
\mathbb{Q}
with full
2
-torsion over
\mathbb{Q}
, we can prove a stronger result, similar to the one in
Proposition

3.1
.

Proposition 4.3
.

Let
E/\mathbb{Q}
be an elliptic curve without CM with full
2
-torsion over
\mathbb{Q}
. Then
{\overline{\rho}}_{E,r}(G_{\mathbb{Q}})=\operatorname{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{r})
for
r=5,7
.

Proof.

For
r=7
, by
[
15
, Theorem C]
, if
{\overline{\rho}}_{E,7}(G_{\mathbb{Q}})\neq\operatorname{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{7})
, then
{\overline{\rho}}_{E,7}(G_{\mathbb{Q}})
is contained in a Borel subgroup, which would imply that
E
has a
14
-isogeny over
\mathbb{Q}
, which is impossible since it does not have CM.

For
r=5
, by
[
15
, Theorem C]
, if
{\overline{\rho}}_{E,5}(G_{\mathbb{Q}})\neq\operatorname{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{5})
, then
{\overline{\rho}}_{E,5}(G_{\mathbb{Q}})\leq G_{9,5}
, where (using the notation of
[
15
]
)

G_{9,5}:=\left\langle\begin{pmatrix}2&0\\
0&1\end{pmatrix},\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\
0&2\end{pmatrix},\begin{pmatrix}0&-1\\
1&0\end{pmatrix},\begin{pmatrix}1&1\\
1&-1\end{pmatrix}\right\rangle\leq\operatorname{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{5}).

The projective image of the group
G_{9,5}
is isomorphic to
S_{4}
, and is denoted by
5S4
in
[
6
]
.

In
[
6
, Table A13]
the line
[2Cs,5S4]
shows that the modular curve whose rational points parametrize elliptic curves
E/\mathbb{Q}
having simultaneously
{\overline{\rho}}_{E,2}(G_{\mathbb{Q}})\{I\}
(denoted
2Cs
) and having
{\overline{\rho}}_{E,5}(G_{\mathbb{Q}})\leq G_{9,5}
(denoted
S_{4}
as mentioned above) has 3 non-cuspidal rational points, all corresponding to
j(E)=2^{6}\cdot 3^{3}
, and such curves have CM.
∎

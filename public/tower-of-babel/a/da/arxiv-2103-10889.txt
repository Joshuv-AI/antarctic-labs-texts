p p -adic Directions of Primitive Vectors
Antonin Guilloux, Tal Horesh

Abstract
Linnik type problems concern the distribution of projections of integral points on the unit sphere as their norm increases, and different generalizations of this phenomenon. Our work addresses a question of this type: we prove the uniform distribution of the projections of primitive ℤ 2 \mathbb{Z}^{2} points in the p p -adic unit sphere, as their (real) norm tends to infinity. The proof is via counting lattice points in semi-simple S S -arithmetic groups.



A primitive vector is an
n
-tuple
\left(a_{1},\ldots,a_{n}\right)

of co-prime integers, and we let
\mathbb{Z}_{\operatorname{prim}}^{n}
denote the set
of primitive vectors in
\mathbb{Z}^{n}
. Since every integral vector is
an integer multiple of a unique primitive vector, it is very natural
to restrict questions about equidistribution of integer vectors to
the set of primitive vectors. For example, one question about an equidistribution
property for integer vectors that has been studied in the past (e.g. in
[
Sch98
]
) is whether the directions of integral vectors,
i.e. their projections to the unit sphere in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, distribute
uniformly in the unit sphere as their norm tends to
\infty
. This
question belongs to the well known family of
Linnik type problems

(e.g.
[
Lin68
,
Duk03
,
Duk07
]
), and the answer is positive:
for every “reasonable” subset
\Theta
of the sphere, it holds
that

\frac{\#\left\{\frac{v}{\left\|v\right\|}\in\Theta:v\in\mathbb{Z}^{n},\left\|v\right\|\leq R\right\}}{\#\left\{\frac{v}{\left\|v\right\|}\in\mathbb{S}^{n-1}:v\in\mathbb{Z}^{n},\left\|v\right\|\leq R\right\}}\underset{R\to\infty}{\longrightarrow}\operatorname{Leb}(\Theta),

(1)

where
\operatorname{Leb}
is the Lebesgue measure on the sphere. While in the
above quotient every “integral direction” on the unit sphere is
hit several times (the first time for a primitive vector, and then
another time for each one of its integer multiples), restricting to

v\in\mathbb{Z}_{\operatorname{prim}}^{n}
allows every integral direction to be considered
exactly once.

Questions about equidistribution of directions, as well as of other
parameters of primitive vectors, have been studied recently using
dynamical methods in
[
Mar10
,
AES16b
,
AES16a
,
EMSS16
,
ERW17
]
.
In the present paper we restrict to dimension
n=2
, and study the
equidistribution of
p
-adic directions
of primitive vectors.
Indeed, since primitive vectors have integer coordinates, they can
be seen as vectors over any field that contains the rationals, and
in particular over the field of
p
-adic numbers
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
for a
positive prime number
p
. There, just like the direction of a real
vector is its projection to the (real) unit sphere through multiplication
by inverse of the norm, the
p
-adic direction of a vector is its
projection to the
p
-adic unit sphere. However, the primitive vectors
have
p
-adic norm one, so in fact they are already contained in
the
p
-adic unit sphere
\mathbb{S}^{1}_{p}
(we will observe this below,
where we recall some basic definitions in the
p
-adic setting).
So,
\mathbb{Z}_{\operatorname{prim}}^{2}
is a countable subset of
\mathbb{S}^{1}_{p}
which
is equipped with a natural height function: the real norm. One is
then led to ask whether the set
\mathbb{Z}_{\operatorname{prim}}^{2}
equidistributes
in
\mathbb{S}^{1}_{p}
, i.e., if an analog to (
1
)
holds when
\mathbb{Z}^{n}
is replaced by
\mathbb{Z}_{\operatorname{prim}}^{2}
, and
\mathbb{S}^{1}

is replaced by
\mathbb{S}^{1}_{p}
. To formulate such an analog, we need
to declare what are the analogous objects in
\mathbb{S}^{1}_{p}
for
an arc on the (real) unit circle, and for the Lebesgue measure on
it. Below, we will define the concept of a
p
-adic arc,
\Theta_{p}\subset\mathbb{S}^{1}_{p}
,
and recall a Haar measure
\mu_{p}
on
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
. Since
\mathbb{S}^{1}_{p}

is an open and compact subset of the
p
-adic plane, then the restriction
of the Haar measure
\mu_{p}^{2}
on the plane
\mathbb{Q}_{p}^{2}
to

\mathbb{S}^{1}_{p}
is a finite non-zero measure on
\mathbb{S}^{1}_{p}
.
As we shall see below, it corresponds to the Lebesgue measure on the
real unit circle.

The theorem below establishes the uniform distribution of the primitive
vectors in
\mathbb{S}^{1}_{p}
as their real norm tends to infinity;
even more, it establishes
joint equidistribution
of their real
and
p
-adic directions in the product of unit circles
\mathbb{S}^{1}\times\mathbb{S}^{1}_{p}
.

Theorem 1
.

For
v\in\mathbb{Z}_{\operatorname{prim}}^{2}
, the pairs
of real and
p
-adic directions

\left(\frac{v}{\left\|v\right\|},v\right)\in\mathbb{S}^{1}\times\mathbb{S}^{1}_{p}

become uniformly distributed in
\mathbb{S}^{1}\times\mathbb{S}^{1}_{p}
w.r.t.
\operatorname{Leb}\times\mu_{p}^{2}|_{\mathbb{S}^{1}_{p}}
as
\left\|v\right\|\to\infty
, meaning
that for every product of arcs
\Theta\times\Theta_{p}\subset\mathbb{S}^{1}\times\mathbb{S}^{1}_{p}

it holds that

\frac{\#\left\{v\in\mathbb{Z}_{\operatorname{prim}}^{2}:\,\left(\frac{v}{\left\|v\right\|},v\right)\in\Theta\times\Theta_{p},\quad\left\|v\right\|\leq R\right\}}{\#\left\{v\in\mathbb{Z}_{\operatorname{prim}}^{2}:\quad\left\|v\right\|\leq R\right\}}\underset{R\to\infty}{\longrightarrow}\operatorname{Leb}(\Theta)\cdot\mu_{p}^{2}(\Theta_{p}).

The convergence is at rate at most
O\left(R^{-2\tau_{p}+\delta}\right)

for every
\delta>0
, where
\tau_{p}=\frac{1}{28}
.

Remark
.

The parameter
\tau_{p}
in error term exponent depends on the
rate of decay of the matrix coefficients of automorphic representations
of
\operatorname{SL}_{2}\left(\mathbb{R}\right)\times\operatorname{SL}_{2}\left(\mathbb{Q}_{p}\right)
, and can
be improved to
\frac{1}{14}
when assuming the Ramanujan conjecture
(cf. Remark
18
).

A similar theorem holds when considering a finite set of primes instead
of a single
p
. Theorem
21

in Section
4
states a related
equidistribution result in this
S
-arithmetic setting, that translates
into a counting statement as above, see Remark
23
.
However, we have chosen to deal with only one prime here in order
to ease the exposition.

Organization of the paper.

The first section of the paper is a collection of general facts on

p
-adic numbers and arithmetic lattices. Sections
2

and
3
are devoted to the proof
of Theorem
1
, along the lines of the
proof given in
[
HN16
,
HK19
]
for the uniform distribution
of the real directions of primitive vectors in the unit sphere. It
consists of two stages: the first is a translation of the theorem
to a statement about counting lattice points in the group
\operatorname{SL}_{2}\left(\mathbb{R}\right)\times\operatorname{SL}_{2}\left(\mathbb{Q}_{p}\right)

(Section
2
), and the second is proving
the counting statement via a method developed in
[
GN12
]
(Section

3
). In Section
4
,
we show that the same method gives a stronger statement than the one
in Theorem
1
, namely the joint equidistribution
of real and
p
-adic directions for any finite number of primes
p

— cf. Theorem
21
(and
Remark
23
). Section
5

is devoted to proving a technical result on well-roundedness in the

p
-adic setting that is used in Section
4
.

1
p
-adic numbers and arithmetic lattices

Let us now recall some basic facts on the
p
-adic numbers and arithmetic
lattices.

1.1
p
-adic numbers and vector spaces

Definition 2

(
p
-adic valuation and absolute value
)
.

For a non-zero
p
-adic number
a
, the
p
-adic valuation

of
a
is defined to be the biggest integer
\nu\left(a\right)

such that

a=\sum_{i=\nu\left(a\right)}^{\infty}\alpha_{i}p^{i},\quad\alpha_{i}\in\left\{0,1,\ldots,p-1\right\},

where for
a=0
one defines
\nu\left(0\right)=\infty
. The
p
-adic
absolute value
is given by

\left|a\right|_{p}=p^{-\nu\left(a\right)}.

The ring of
p
-adic integers

\mathbb{Z}_{p}<\mathbb{Q}_{p}
is the
p
-adic
unit ball, namely the set of
p
-adic numbers with absolute value
at most
1
(equivalently, of non-negative valuation). Inside
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
,
the set of
invertible

p
-adic integers
\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{*}\subset\mathbb{Z}_{p}

is the set of
p
-adic numbers with absolute value
1
(equivalently,
of valuation
0
).

Note 3
.

It is easy to see that
every
a\in\mathbb{Q}_{p}
can be written (uniquely) as

a=u_{a}p^{\nu\left(a\right)}=u_{a}\left|a\right|_{p}^{-1}

where
u_{a}\in\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{\times}
.

A norm on
\mathbb{Q}_{p}^{2}
(and therefore a unit circle) is then defined
as follows.

Definition 4

(
norm and unit circle in
\mathbb{Q}_{p}^{2}
)
.

The
p
-adic norm (or just “norm”)
of a vector
(a,b)\in\mathbb{Q}_{p}^{2}
is defined to be

\left\|(a,b)\right\|_{p}=\max\left\{\left|a\right|_{p},\left|b\right|_{p}\right\}=p^{-\min\left\{\nu\left(a\right),\nu\left(b\right)\right\}}.

Accordingly, the
p
-adic unit circle
in
\mathbb{Q}_{p}^{2}
is
the set of vectors of norm one:

\mathbb{S}^{1}_{p}:=\left\{v\in\mathbb{Q}_{p}^{2}:\left\|v\right\|_{p}=1\right\}.

Note that
(a,b)
is in
\mathbb{S}^{1}_{p}
if and only if both
a,b

are in
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
, and at least one of them is in
\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{*}
.
In particular, since the “usual” integers are also
p
-adic integers,
and since an integer lies in
\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{*}
if and only if it is
not divisible by
p
, we have that
\mathbb{Z}_{\operatorname{prim}}^{2}\subset\mathbb{S}^{1}_{p}
.

Since
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
is the unit ball in
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
, then a ball of radius

p^{-N}
around
\alpha\in\mathbb{Q}_{p}
is
\alpha+p^{N}\mathbb{Z}_{p}
. Similarly,
a ball of radius
p^{-N}
around
(\alpha,\beta)\in\mathbb{Q}_{p}^{2}
is
(\alpha,\beta)+p^{N}\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{2}
.
The analog in
\mathbb{Q}_{p}^{2}
for an arc
\Theta
in the unit circle

\mathbb{S}^{1}
is a ball that is contained in the
p
-adic circle:

Definition 5

(
p
-adic arc
)
.

Let
N>0
be an integer. A
p
-adic arc
of radius
p^{-N}

is a ball
\theta+p^{N}\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{2}
, where
\theta\in\mathbb{S}^{1}_{p}
.
It will be denoted by
\Theta_{p}=\Theta_{p}\left(\theta,p^{-N}\right)
.

Note that it is sufficient that
\theta\in\mathbb{S}^{1}_{p}
in order to
have
\Theta_{p}\subset\mathbb{S}^{1}_{p}
!

A Haar measure
\mu_{p}
is defined on
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
(see, e.g.
[
BS05
]
)
by assigning to a ball of radius
p^{-N}
the volume
p^{-N}
. We
let
\mu_{p}^{2}:=\mu_{p}\times\mu_{p}
denote the resulting Haar measure
on
\mathbb{Q}_{p}^{2}
, which then assigns to a ball of radius
p^{-N}

the volume
p^{-2N}
. Being a Haar measure on
\mathbb{Q}_{p}^{2}
,
\mu_{p}^{2}

is invariant under the group
\operatorname{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})
of
p
-adic
2
by

2
matrices with determinant one. We note that unlike the real
case, in the
p
-adic plane the unit circle has positive Haar measure.
Indeed, it contains the subset
\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{\times}\times\mathbb{Z}_{p}
, which
has measure

\mu_{p}(\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{\times})\cdot\mu_{p}(\mathbb{Z}_{p})=\mu_{p}(\mathbb{Z}_{p}-p\mathbb{Z}_{p})\cdot\mu_{p}(\mathbb{Z}_{p})=(1-\frac{1}{p})\cdot 1=1-\frac{1}{p}.

Hence, it is possible to restrict
\mu_{p}^{2}
to
\mathbb{S}^{1}_{p}
.
This measure is the analog of the Lebesgue measure on the real unit
circle, in the sense that it is invariant under the group of norm
preserving linear transformations of
\mathbb{Q}_{p}^{2}
. Indeed, it is
known (e.g.
[
GI63
, Cor. 3.3]
) that the group
\operatorname{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z}_{p})

is the stabilizer of the norm
\left\|\cdot\right\|_{p}
on
\mathbb{Q}_{p}^{2}
,
so in particular it preserves and acts transitively on
\mathbb{S}^{1}_{p}
.

1.2
S
-arithmetic lattices

The field
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
does not contain a lattice; but, inside the ring

\mathbb{F}:=\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{Q}_{p},

the subring
\mathbb{Z}\left[\frac{1}{p}\right]
of polynomials in
\frac{1}{p}

with integer coefficients embeds diagonally as a co-compact lattice.
It is an integral domain, and as such it plays the role of the integral
lattice inside
\mathbb{F}
. According to the Borel Harish–Chandra Theorem

[
BHC62
]
, the diagonal embedding of the subgroup

\operatorname{SL}_{2}\left(\mathbb{Z}\left[\frac{1}{p}\right]\right)
, which by abuse of
notation we denote by
\operatorname{SL}_{2}\left(\mathbb{Z}\left[\frac{1}{p}\right]\right)
,
is a lattice inside

\operatorname{SL}_{2}\left(\mathbb{F}\right):=\operatorname{SL}_{2}\left(\mathbb{R}\right)\times\operatorname{SL}_{2}\left(\mathbb{Q}_{p}\right).

Both these lattices,
\mathbb{Z}\left[\frac{1}{p}\right]<\mathbb{F}
and
\operatorname{SL}_{2}\left(\mathbb{Z}\left[\frac{1}{p}\right]\right)<\operatorname{SL}_{2}\left(\mathbb{F}\right)
,
are a special case of
S
-arithmetic lattices

[
PR92
]
.
To familiarize the reader with the
S
-arithmetic framework, we include
a proof that these two discrete subgroups are indeed lattices, by
establishing the existence of finite-volume fundamental domains. Here
a fundamental domain means a full set of representatives.

Fact 6
.

Let
\mathcal{D}_{\infty}

denote a fundamental domain for
\operatorname{SL}_{2}\left(\mathbb{Z}\right)
in
\operatorname{SL}_{2}\left(\mathbb{R}\right)
.
Then

1.

\left[\frac{1}{2},\frac{1}{2}\right)\times\mathbb{Z}_{p}
is a fundamental domain for

\mathbb{Z}\left[\frac{1}{p}\right]
in
\mathbb{F}
.

2.

\mathcal{D}_{\infty}\times\operatorname{SL}_{2}\left(\mathbb{Z}_{p}\right)
is a fundamental
domain for
\operatorname{SL}_{2}\left(\mathbb{Z}\left[\frac{1}{p}\right]\right)
in
\operatorname{SL}_{2}\left(\mathbb{F}\right)
.

Proof.

1. Given
\left(x,\alpha\right)\in\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{Q}_{p}
, with
\alpha=\sum_{i=\nu\left(\alpha\right)}^{\infty}\alpha_{i}p^{i}
,
write

\left\{\alpha\right\}=\sum_{i=\nu\left(\alpha\right)}^{-1}\alpha_{i}p^{i}\in\mathbb{Z}\left[\frac{1}{p}\right].

Then clearly
\alpha-\left\{\alpha\right\}=\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}\alpha_{i}p^{i}\in\mathbb{Z}_{p}
,
and so

\left(x,\alpha\right)-\left(\left\{\alpha\right\},\left\{\alpha\right\}\right)\in\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{Z}_{p}.

Now let
m\in\mathbb{Z}
be an integer such that
x-\left\{\alpha\right\}-m\in\left[\frac{1}{2},\frac{1}{2}\right)
.
Since
\mathbb{Z}\subset\mathbb{Z}_{p}
, then

\left(x,\alpha\right)-\left(\left\{\alpha\right\}+m,\left\{\alpha\right\}+m\right)\in\left[\frac{1}{2},\frac{1}{2}\right)\times\mathbb{Z}_{p}.

For uniqueness, assume
\left(x,\alpha\right)
and
\left(y,\beta\right)

are both in
\left[\frac{1}{2},\frac{1}{2}\right)\times\mathbb{Z}_{p}
, with
f\in\mathbb{Z}\left[\frac{1}{p}\right]

such that

\left(x,\alpha\right)+\left(f,f\right)=\left(y,\beta\right).

Then
f=\beta-\alpha\in\mathbb{Z}_{p}
must be an integer since
\mathbb{Z}\left[\frac{1}{p}\right]\cap\mathbb{Z}_{p}=\mathbb{Z}
,
and the real coordinate forces that
f=0
.

2. For the second part, let
g=\left(g_{\infty},g_{p}\right)\in\operatorname{SL}_{2}\left(\mathbb{R}\right)\times\operatorname{SL}_{2}\left(\mathbb{Q}_{p}\right)
;
we show that there exists a unique
\left(\gamma,\gamma\right)\in\operatorname{SL}_{2}\left(\mathbb{Z}\left[\frac{1}{p}\right]\right)

such that

\left(g_{\infty},g_{p}\right)\cdot\left(\gamma,\gamma\right)\in\mathcal{D}_{\infty}\times\operatorname{SL}_{2}\left(\mathbb{Z}_{p}\right).

It is a consequence of row reduction that any
g_{p}\in\operatorname{SL}_{2}\left(\mathbb{Q}_{p}\right)

can be written (non-uniquely) as

g_{p}=k_{p}\gamma_{p}

with
k_{p}\in\operatorname{SL}_{2}\left(\mathbb{Z}_{p}\right)
and
\gamma_{p}\in\operatorname{SL}_{2}\left(\mathbb{Z}\left[\frac{1}{p}\right]\right)
.
Then
\left(g_{\infty},g_{p}\right)=\left(g_{\infty},k_{p}\gamma_{p}\right)

meaning that

\left(g_{\infty},g_{p}\right)\left(\gamma_{p}^{-1},\gamma_{p}^{-1}\right)=\left(g_{\infty}\gamma_{p}^{-1},k_{p}\right).

Write

\operatorname{SL}_{2}\left(\mathbb{R}\right)\ni g_{\infty}\gamma_{p}^{-1}=x_{\infty}\gamma_{\infty}

where
x_{\infty}\in\mathcal{D}_{\infty}
and
\gamma_{\infty}\in\operatorname{SL}_{2}\left(\mathbb{Z}\right)
.
Then

\left(g_{\infty},g_{p}\right)\left(\gamma_{p}^{-1}\gamma_{\infty}^{-1},\gamma_{p}^{-1}\gamma_{\infty}^{-1}\right)=(x_{\infty},k_{p}\gamma_{\infty}^{-1})\in\mathcal{D}_{\infty}\times\operatorname{SL}_{2}\left(\mathbb{Z}_{p}\right),

which establishes existence. For uniqueness of
\left(\gamma,\gamma\right)=(\gamma_{p}^{-1}\gamma_{\infty}^{-1},\gamma_{p}^{-1}\gamma_{\infty}^{-1})
,
assume that
\left(g_{\infty},g_{p}\right)\cdot\left(\gamma,\gamma\right)=\left(x_{p},x_{\infty}\right)

and
\left(g_{\infty},g_{p}\right)\cdot\left(\gamma^{\prime},\gamma^{\prime}\right)=\left(x_{\infty}^{\prime},x_{p}^{\prime}\right)
,
where both
\left(x_{\infty},x_{p}\right)
and
\left(x_{\infty}^{\prime},x_{p}^{\prime}\right)

lie in
\mathcal{D}_{\infty}\times\operatorname{SL}_{2}\left(\mathbb{Z}_{p}\right)
. Then

\left(x_{\infty},x_{p}\right)\cdot\left(\gamma^{-1}\gamma^{\prime},\gamma^{-1}\gamma^{\prime}\right)=\left(x_{\infty}^{\prime},x_{p}^{\prime}\right)\in\mathcal{D}_{\infty}\times\operatorname{SL}_{2}\left(\mathbb{Z}_{p}\right)

where
\gamma^{-1}\gamma^{\prime}\in\operatorname{SL}_{2}\left(\mathbb{Z}\left[\frac{1}{p}\right]\right)
.
From the
p
-adic component of the equation we have that
\gamma^{-1}\gamma^{\prime}\in\operatorname{SL}_{2}\left(\mathbb{Z}_{p}\right)
.
Then
\gamma^{-1}\gamma^{\prime}\in\operatorname{SL}_{2}\left(\mathbb{Z}\left[\frac{1}{p}\right]\right)\cap\operatorname{SL}_{2}\left(\mathbb{Z}_{p}\right)=\operatorname{SL}_{2}\left(\mathbb{Z}\right)
.
On the other hand, from the real component of the equation, we have
that
\gamma^{-1}\gamma^{\prime}
cannot lie in
\operatorname{SL}_{2}\left(\mathbb{Z}\right)
,
unless
\gamma^{-1}\gamma^{\prime}=\operatorname{id}
. We conclude that
\gamma=\gamma^{\prime}
.
∎

2
From primitive vectors to lattice points in the group
\operatorname{SL}_{2}\left(\mathbb{R}\right)\times\operatorname{SL}_{2}\left(\mathbb{Q}_{p}\right)

It is well known that there exists a connection between primitive
vectors in
\mathbb{Z}^{2}
, and integral matrices inside
\operatorname{SL}_{2}\left(\mathbb{R}\right)
;
the goal of this section is to establish an analogous connection in
the setting of
\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{Q}_{p}
, hence reducing the proof of Theorem

1
to counting lattice points in the group

\operatorname{SL}_{2}\left(\mathbb{R}\right)\times\operatorname{SL}_{2}\left(\mathbb{Q}_{p}\right)
. To exhibit
such a connection, we first extend the notion of primitive vectors
(subsection
2.1
), then find suitable
coordinates on
\operatorname{SL}_{2}\left(\mathbb{F}\right)
through a Bruhat-Iwasawa decomposition
(Definition
12
). Proposition
13

states the precise connection.

2.1
Primitive vectors over
\mathbb{Z}\left[\frac{1}{p}\right]

We aim to formulate a connection between primitive vectors in
\mathbb{Z}\left[\frac{1}{p}\right]^{2}

and matrices inside
\operatorname{SL}_{2}\left(\mathbb{Z}\left[\frac{1}{p}\right]\right)
.
First, let us extend the definition of a primitive vector from
\mathbb{Z}

to a general integral domain:

Definition 7
.

Let
\mathcal{O}
be an integral domain. A vector
v=\left(a,b\right)\in\mathcal{O}^{2}

is called
primitive
if the prime ideals
\left\langle a\right\rangle

and
\left\langle b\right\rangle
satisfy that
\left\langle a\right\rangle+\left\langle b\right\rangle=\mathcal{O}
.
In other words, if there exists a solution
\left(x,y\right)\in\mathcal{O}^{2}

to the
\mathcal{O}
–diophantine equation

ax+by=1.

We refer to this equation as the
gcd equation
of
v
, and
denote the set of primitive elements in
\mathcal{O}^{2}
by
\mathcal{O}_{\operatorname{prim}}^{2}
.

Clearly, the set of primitive
\mathbb{Z}\left[\frac{1}{p}\right]^{2}
vectors
contains the set of primitive
\mathbb{Z}^{2}
vectors, but is not equal
to it; e.g. the vector
(p,0)
is primitive in
\mathbb{Z}\left[\frac{1}{p}\right]^{2}

but not in
\mathbb{Z}^{2}
. However, these sets are very much related to
each other: every element in
\mathbb{Z}\left[\frac{1}{p}\right]_{\operatorname{prim}}^{2}

is a multiplication by a power of
p
of an element in
\mathbb{Z}_{\operatorname{prim}}^{2}
.

Lemma 8
.

The primitive
vectors in
\mathbb{Z}\left[\frac{1}{p}\right]^{2}
are
\left\{p^{\alpha}v:\alpha\in\mathbb{Z},\,v\in\mathbb{Z}_{\operatorname{prim}}^{2}\right\}
.

For the proof, we observe that every element
f
of
\mathbb{Z}\left[\frac{1}{p}\right]

can be written as
f=\frac{m}{p^{n}}
where
m,n\in\mathbb{Z}
and
m

is coprime to
p
. If
\deg(f)=0
(as a polynomial in
\frac{1}{p}
),
then
f
is an integer and this fact is clear. Otherwise, let
n>0

and write

f=\frac{a_{n}}{p^{n}}+\frac{a_{n-1}}{p^{n-1}}+\cdots+\frac{a_{1}}{p}+a_{0}

where
a_{0},\ldots,a_{n}
are integers that are (except maybe
a_{0}
)
coprime to
p
, and
a_{n}\neq 0
. Then

f=\frac{a_{n}+a_{n-1}p+\cdots+a_{1}p^{n-1}+a_{0}p^{n}}{p^{n}},

and the denominator is coprime to
p
when
n>0
.

Proof of Lemma
8
.

Let
\left(a,b\right)\in\mathbb{Z}\left[\frac{1}{p}\right]_{\operatorname{prim}}^{2}

and write
a=a^{\prime}p^{\alpha}
and
b=b^{\prime}p^{\beta}
where
a^{\prime},b^{\prime}\in\mathbb{Z}

are co-prime to
p
. We claim that
\gcd(a^{\prime},b^{\prime})=1
.
To see this, substitute
a
and
b
into the
\gcd
equation
ax+by=1

(
x,y
variables in
\mathbb{Z}\left[\frac{1}{p}\right]
) to obtain
a^{\prime}p^{\alpha}x+b^{\prime}p^{\beta}y=1
.
Multiply both sides by a non-negative power of
p
to obtain an equation
in integers:
a^{\prime}x^{\prime}+b^{\prime}y^{\prime}=p^{m}
where

x^{\prime},y^{\prime}\in\mathbb{Z}
. It then follows that
\gcd\left(a^{\prime},b^{\prime}\right)

is a power of
p
, but since both
a^{\prime},b^{\prime}
are co-prime
to
p
, then
\gcd\left(a^{\prime},b^{\prime}\right)=1
. Without
loss of generality, assume
\beta\geq\alpha
. Then
\left(a,b\right)=p^{\alpha}\left(a^{\prime},b^{\prime}p^{\beta-\alpha}\right)=p^{\alpha}v

where
v=\left(a^{\prime},b^{\prime}p^{\beta-\alpha}\right)\in\mathbb{Z}_{\operatorname{prim}}^{2}
.
∎

To formulate the connection between primitive
\mathbb{Z}\left[\frac{1}{p}\right]^{2}

vectors to matrices in
\operatorname{SL}_{2}\left(\mathbb{Z}\left[\frac{1}{p}\right]\right)
,
we introduce the Bruhat-Iwasawa decomposition of
\operatorname{SL}_{2}\left(\mathbb{F}\right)
.

2.2
Iwasawa decomposition of
\operatorname{SL}_{2}\left(\mathbb{R}\right)

Let us first recall the
KAN
decomposition of
\operatorname{SL}_{2}\left(\mathbb{R}\right)
.
Here
\operatorname{SL}_{2}\left(\mathbb{R}\right)=K_{\infty}A_{\infty}N_{\infty}
where

K_{\infty}=\operatorname{SO}_{2}\left(\mathbb{R}\right)
is maximal compact,
A_{\infty}=\left\{\left(\begin{smallmatrix}\alpha&0\\
0&\alpha^{-1}\end{smallmatrix}\right)\right\}
is the diagonal subgroup and
N_{\infty}=\left\{\left(\begin{smallmatrix}1&\mathbb{R}\\
0&1\end{smallmatrix}\right)\right\}
is the subgroup of upper unipotent matrices. Then, for
\left(\begin{smallmatrix}a&c\\
b&d\end{smallmatrix}\right)\in\operatorname{SL}_{2}\left(\mathbb{R}\right)
,

\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}a&c\\
b&d\end{array}\right)=\underset{\in K_{\infty}}{\underbrace{\frac{1}{\sqrt{a^{2}+b^{2}}}\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}a&-b\\
b&a\end{array}\right)}}\underset{\in A_{\infty}}{\underbrace{\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}\sqrt{a^{2}+b^{2}}&0\\
0&\frac{1}{\sqrt{a^{2}+b^{2}}}\end{array}\right)}}\underset{\in N_{\infty}}{\underbrace{\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}1&\frac{ac+bd}{a^{2}+b^{2}}\\
0&1\end{array}\right)}}.

By letting
v:=\left(a,b\right)^{\mbox{t}}
and
w:=\left(c,d\right)^{\mbox{t}}
,
we obtain

\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}v&w\end{array}\right)=\underset{\in K_{\infty}}{\underbrace{\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}\hat{v}&\hat{v}^{\perp}\end{array}\right)}}\underset{\in A_{\infty}}{\underbrace{\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}\left\|v\right\|&0\\
0&\left\|v\right\|^{-1}\end{array}\right)}}\underset{\in N_{\infty}}{\underbrace{\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}1&\frac{\left\langle w,\hat{v}\right\rangle}{\left\|v\right\|}\\
0&1\end{array}\right)}},

(2)

where

\hat{v}:=v/\left\|v\right\|

is the unit vector pointing in the direction of
v
,

v^{\perp}:=\left(-b,a\right)

is the vector pointing in the orthogonal direction to
v
, and
\left\langle\cdot,\cdot\right\rangle

is the standard dot product in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
.

We note that any pair of the three subgroups
K_{\infty}
,
A_{\infty}

and
N_{\infty}
intersect trivially, and therefore the decomposition

\operatorname{SL}_{2}\left(\mathbb{R}\right)=K_{\infty}A_{\infty}N_{\infty}
induces coordinates
on
\operatorname{SL}_{2}\left(\mathbb{R}\right)
: every element
g\in\operatorname{SL}_{2}\left(\mathbb{R}\right)

has a unique presentation as
g=kan
. In addition, a Haar measure
on
\operatorname{SL}_{2}\left(\mathbb{R}\right)
can be decomposed in the Iwasawa coordinates
as

d\mu_{\operatorname{SL}_{2}\left(\mathbb{R}\right)}(g)=d\mu_{\operatorname{SL}_{2}\left(\mathbb{R}\right)}(kan)=\frac{d\mu_{K_{\infty}}(k)d\mu_{A_{\infty}}(a)d\mu_{N_{\infty}}(n)}{\alpha},

where:
\mu_{N_{\infty}}
is the Haar measure on
N_{\infty}
corresponding
to the Lebesgue measure on
\mathbb{R}
under the isomorphism
\left(\begin{smallmatrix}1&x\\
0&1\end{smallmatrix}\right)\leftrightarrow x
;
\mu_{K_{\infty}}
is the Haar measure on
K_{\infty}
corresponding
to the Lebesgue measure on
\mathbb{S}^{1}
under the isomorphism
\left(\begin{smallmatrix}\cos\theta&\sin\theta\\
-\sin\theta&\cos\theta\end{smallmatrix}\right)\leftrightarrow\theta
; and
\mu_{A_{\infty}}
is a Haar measure on
A_{\infty}
corresponding
to the Lebesgue measure on the multiplicative group of
\mathbb{R}_{>0}

under the isomorphism
\left(\begin{smallmatrix}\alpha&0\\
0&\alpha^{-1}\end{smallmatrix}\right)\leftrightarrow\alpha
. With these isomorphisms in mind, and recalling that the Haar measure
on
\left(\mathbb{R}_{>0},\cdot\right)
is
\frac{d\alpha}{\alpha}
, we have

d\mu_{\operatorname{SL}_{2}\left(\mathbb{R}\right)}(g)=d\mu_{\operatorname{SL}_{2}\left(\mathbb{R}\right)}\left(\left(\begin{smallmatrix}\cos\theta&\sin\theta\\
-\sin\theta&\cos\theta\end{smallmatrix}\right)\left(\begin{smallmatrix}\alpha&0\\
0&\alpha^{-1}\end{smallmatrix}\right)\left(\begin{smallmatrix}1&x\\
0&1\end{smallmatrix}\right)\right)=\frac{d\theta d\alpha dx}{\alpha^{2}},

(3)

where
dy
stands for integration w.r.t. the Lebesgue measure
on
\mathbb{R}
.

2.3
Bruhat decomposition of
\operatorname{SL}_{2}\left(\mathbb{Q}_{p}\right)

Proceeding to the
p
-adic case, the group
\operatorname{SL}_{2}\left(\mathbb{Q}_{p}\right)

can also be written as
K_{p}A_{p}N_{p}
, but these are not coordinates.
Indeed, set

\begin{array}[]{cclcc}K_{p}&:=&\left\{\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}a&c\\
b&d\end{array}\right):a,b,c,d\in\mathbb{Z}_{p},ad-bc=1\right\}&=&\operatorname{SL}_{2}\left(\mathbb{Z}_{p}\right)\\
A_{p}&:=&\left\{\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}p^{-t}&0\\
0&p^{t}\end{array}\right):t\in\mathbb{Z}\right\}\\
N_{p}&:=&\left\{\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}1&\alpha\\
0&1\end{array}\right):\alpha\in\mathbb{Q}_{p}\right\}.\end{array}

(4)

Since
K_{p}
and
N_{p}
intersect non-trivially, the Iwasawa
decomposition on
\operatorname{SL}_{2}\left(\mathbb{Q}_{p}\right)
is not unique. To remedy
this, we use the Bruhat decomposition instead. Let

M_{p}:=\left\{\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}u&0\\
0&u^{-1}\end{array}\right):u\in\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{*}\right\}

be the centralizer of
A_{p}
in
K_{p}
,
D_{p}:=\left\{\left(\begin{smallmatrix}\alpha&0\\
0&\alpha^{-1}\end{smallmatrix}\right):\alpha\in\mathbb{Q}_{p}\right\}
be the diagonal subgroup (satisfying
D_{p}=M_{p}A_{p}
), and

N_{p}^{-}:=\left\{\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}1&0\\
\alpha&1\end{array}\right):\alpha\in\mathbb{Q}_{p}\right\}

be the lower unipotent subgroup. Each two of the subgroups
N_{p}^{-}
,

M_{p}
,
A_{p}
and
N_{p}
intersect trivially, which means that
they induce coordinates — the Bruhat coordinates — on the subset

N_{p}^{-}M_{p}A_{p}N_{p}\subset\operatorname{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{Q}_{p}).

This set is not the whole of
\operatorname{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})
, but its complement
in
\operatorname{SL}_{2}\left(\mathbb{Q}_{p}\right)
is of Haar measure zero.

We choose the following Haar measures on the above subgroups. The
natural isomorphisms between
N_{p}
and
N_{p}^{-}
to
\mathbb{Q}_{p}

equip
N_{p}
and
N_{p}^{-}
with Haar measures that correspond
to the Haar measure
\mu_{p}
on
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
:

d\mu_{N_{p}}\left(\begin{smallmatrix}1&\beta\\
0&1\end{smallmatrix}\right)=d\mu_{p}(\beta),\qquad d\mu_{N_{p}^{-}}\left(\begin{smallmatrix}1&0\\
\alpha&1\end{smallmatrix}\right)=d\mu_{p}(\alpha).

Similarly, the natural isomorphism between
A_{p}
to
\mathbb{Z}
induces

A_{p}
with the Haar measure on
\mathbb{Z}
that is the counting measure:

\mu_{A_{p}}=\sum_{a\in A_{p}}\Delta_{a}.

Finally,
D_{p}
is naturally isomorphic with the multiplicative
group
\mathbb{Q}_{p}^{\times}
of the field
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
, from which it inherits
the Haar measure:

d\mu_{D_{p}}\left(\begin{smallmatrix}\alpha&0\\
0&\alpha^{-1}\end{smallmatrix}\right)=d\mu_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}^{\times}}(\alpha)=\frac{d\mu_{p}(\alpha)}{\left|\alpha\right|_{p}}.

Since the measure on
\mathbb{Q}_{p}^{\times}
restricts to the measure
on
\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{\times}
, and the latter is isomorphic to
M_{p}
in
the same way that
\mathbb{Q}_{p}^{\times}
is isomorphic to
D_{p}
, we
obtain a Haar measure on
M_{p}
:

d\mu_{M_{p}}\left(\begin{smallmatrix}u&0\\
0&u^{-1}\end{smallmatrix}\right)=d\mu_{\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{\times}}(u)=\frac{d\mu_{p}(u)}{\left|u\right|_{p}}=d\mu_{p}(u).

A Haar measure on
\operatorname{SL}_{2}\left(\mathbb{Q}_{p}\right)
can be expressed in
the Bruhat coordinates as follows.

Lemma 9
.

The Haar measure on
G_{p}=\operatorname{SL}_{2}\left(\mathbb{Q}_{p}\right)

w.r.t. the Bruhat coordinates is

d\mu_{G_{p}}(g)=d\mu_{G_{p}}\left(\left(\begin{smallmatrix}1&0\\
\alpha&1\end{smallmatrix}\right)\left(\begin{smallmatrix}u&0\\
0&u^{-1}\end{smallmatrix}\right)\left(\begin{smallmatrix}p^{-t}&0\\
0&p^{t}\end{smallmatrix}\right)\left(\begin{smallmatrix}1&\beta\\
0&1\end{smallmatrix}\right)\right)=p^{2t}d\mu_{p}(\alpha)d\mu_{p}(u)\Delta_{t}d\mu_{p}(\beta)

Proof.

Let
P_{p}^{-}
denote the subgroup of lower triangular matrices
in
\operatorname{SL}_{2}\left(\mathbb{Q}_{p}\right)
, meaning that
P_{p}^{-}=N_{p}^{-}D_{p}=N_{p}^{-}M_{p}A_{p}
.
By
[
EW13
, Lemma 11.31]
, since
G_{p}
is unimodular, a Haar
measure
\mu_{G_{p}}=\mu_{\operatorname{SL}_{2}\left(\mathbb{Q}_{p}\right)}
is given by

\mu_{G_{p}}=\mu_{P_{p}^{-}}^{L}\times\mu_{N_{p}}^{R}
, where
\mu_{P_{p}^{-}}^{L}

is a left Haar measure on
P_{p}^{-}
and
\mu_{N_{p}}^{R}
is a
right Haar measure on
N_{p}
, which is simply
\mu_{N_{p}}
. It
is left to compute a left Haar measure on
P_{p}^{-}
. Note that

P_{p}^{-}
can be introduced in two ways as
N_{p}^{-}D_{p}=D_{p}N_{p}^{-}
,
but the expression for the Haar measure will correspond to the choice
of coordinates. It is easy to show that
\mu_{D_{p}}\times\mu_{N_{p}^{-}}

is a left Haar measure on
P_{p}^{-}=D_{p}N_{p}^{-}
; to obtain this
left Haar measure in the coordinates
N_{p}^{-}D_{p}
we will perform
a change of variables:

\displaystyle\int\int f\left(\left[\begin{smallmatrix}\alpha^{-1}&0\\
o&\alpha\end{smallmatrix}\right]\left[\begin{smallmatrix}1&0\\
y&1\end{smallmatrix}\right]\right)d\mu_{D_{p}}\left(\left[\begin{smallmatrix}\alpha^{-1}&0\\
o&\alpha\end{smallmatrix}\right]\right)d\mu_{N_{p}^{-}}\left(\left[\begin{smallmatrix}1&0\\
y&1\end{smallmatrix}\right]\right)

\displaystyle=\int\int f\left(\left[\begin{smallmatrix}1&0\\
y\alpha^{-2}&1\end{smallmatrix}\right]\left[\begin{smallmatrix}\alpha&0\\
o&\alpha^{-1}\end{smallmatrix}\right]\right)d\mu_{D_{p}}\left(\left[\begin{smallmatrix}\alpha^{-1}&0\\
o&\alpha\end{smallmatrix}\right]\right)d\mu_{N_{p}^{-}}\left(\left[\begin{smallmatrix}1&0\\
y&1\end{smallmatrix}\right]\right)

\displaystyle=\int\int f\left(\left[\begin{smallmatrix}1&0\\
x&1\end{smallmatrix}\right]\left[\begin{smallmatrix}\alpha&0\\
o&\alpha^{-1}\end{smallmatrix}\right]\right)\left|\alpha\right|_{p}^{2}d\mu_{D_{p}}\left(\left[\begin{smallmatrix}\alpha^{-1}&0\\
o&\alpha\end{smallmatrix}\right]\right)d\mu_{N_{p}^{-}}\left(\left[\begin{smallmatrix}1&0\\
x&1\end{smallmatrix}\right]\right)

Since the integral on the left-hand side is invariant under replacing

f(g)
by
f(hg)
for any
h\in P_{p}^{-}
, then so is the integral
on the right-hand side. Hence
\mu_{P_{p}^{-}}^{L}=\left|\alpha\right|_{p}^{2}\left(\mu_{D_{p}}\times\mu_{N_{p}^{-}}\right)
.

Now, since
D_{p}=M_{p}A_{p}
where
M_{p}
and
A_{p}
commute
and are abelian, we have that
\mu_{D_{p}}=\mu_{M_{p}}\times\mu_{A_{p}}
.
We conclude that a left Haar measure on
G_{p}
is given in the Bruhat
coordinates as

\mu_{G_{p}}=\left|\alpha\right|_{p}^{2}\left(\mu_{N_{p}^{-}}\times\mu_{M_{p}}\times\mu_{A_{p}}\times\mu_{N_{p}}\right).\qed

Remark 10
.

Under this choice of Haar measure, the (compact and open) subgroup

\operatorname{SL}_{2}\left(\mathbb{Z}_{p}\right)
has mass
1-\frac{1}{p}
. Indeed, we
have

\operatorname{SL}_{2}\left(\mathbb{Z}_{p}\right)\simeq N_{p}^{-}\left(\mathbb{Z}_{p}\right)\times M_{p}\times N_{p}\left(\mathbb{Z}_{p}\right)\simeq\mathbb{Z}_{p}\times\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{\times}\times\mathbb{Z}_{p}

and therefore

\mu_{G_{p}}\left(\operatorname{SL}_{2}\left(\mathbb{Z}_{p}\right)\right)=\mu_{p}\left(\mathbb{Z}_{p}\right)\mu_{p}\left(\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{\times}\right)\mu_{p}\left(\mathbb{Z}_{p}\right)=1\cdot\left(1-\frac{1}{p}\right)\cdot 1=1-\frac{1}{p}.

2.4
The Bruhat-Iwasawa coordinates

While the Bruhat decomposition of
\operatorname{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})
provides uniqueness,
the Iwasawa decomposition provides an arithmetic interpretation of
the different components, as suggested in (
2
)
for the
\operatorname{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{R})
case. Luckily, these two decompositions coincide
on a “large” subset of
\operatorname{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})
, on which we are going
to focus from now on.

Consider
g=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}a&c\\
b&d\end{smallmatrix}\right)\in\operatorname{SL}_{2}\left(\mathbb{Q}_{p}\right)
; assuming that
a\neq 0
we have that

\displaystyle\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}a&c\\
b&d\end{array}\right)

\displaystyle\overset{{}_{a\neq 0}}{=}\underset{\in N_{p}^{-}}{\underbrace{\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}1&0\\
\frac{b}{a}&1\end{array}\right)}}\underset{\in D_{p}}{\underbrace{\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}a&0\\
0&a^{-1}\end{array}\right)}}\underset{\in N_{p}}{\underbrace{\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}1&\frac{c}{a}\\
0&1\end{array}\right)}}

\displaystyle\overset{{}_{a=u_{a}\left|a\right|_{p}^{-1}}}{=}\underset{\in N_{p}^{-}}{\underbrace{\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}1&0\\
\frac{b}{a}&1\end{array}\right)}}\underset{\in M_{p}}{\underbrace{\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}u_{a}&0\\
0&u_{a}^{-1}\end{array}\right)}}\underset{\in A_{p}}{\underbrace{\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}\left|a\right|_{p}^{-1}&0\\
0&\left|a\right|_{p}\end{array}\right)}}\underset{\in N_{p}}{\underbrace{\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}1&\frac{c}{a}\\
0&1\end{array}\right)}}.

Indeed, the set of
g\in\operatorname{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})
with
a\neq 0
is exactly

N_{p}^{-}M_{p}A_{p}N_{p}
. If we assume further that
\left|a\right|_{p}\geq\left|b\right|_{p}
,
then
\frac{b}{a}\in\mathbb{Z}_{p}
and therefore the
N_{p}^{-}
component
lies also in
K_{p}
; letting

G_{p}^{+}=\operatorname{SL}_{2}\left(\mathbb{Q}_{p}\right)^{+}:=\left\{\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}a&c\\
b&d\end{array}\right)\in\operatorname{SL}_{2}\left(\mathbb{Q}_{p}\right):\left|a\right|_{p}\geq\left|b\right|_{p}\right\},

in which necessarily
a\neq 0
, we conclude that the Bruhat decomposition
of
G_{p}^{+}
, which is unique, coincides with the Iwasawa decomposition.
This is due to the fact that the
N_{p}^{-}M_{p}
component in the
Bruhat decomposition coincides with the
K_{p}
component in the
Iwasawa decomposition. Denoting this component by

Q_{p}:=\left\{\begin{subarray}{c}\text{lower triangular}\\
\text{matrices in $K_{p}$}\end{subarray}\right\}=\left\{\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}u&0\\
m&u^{-1}\end{array}\right):m\in\mathbb{Z}_{p},u\in\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{\times}\right\}<K_{p}

(note that it indeed lies in
G_{p}^{+}
), we have that
G_{p}^{+}=Q_{p}A_{p}N_{p}
,
and these are coordinates on
G_{p}^{+}
.

Moving forward to the arithmetic interpretation of these coordinates,
it is clear that the first columns of the elements in
G_{p}^{+}

lie in the
p
-adic “right half plane”:

\mathbb{Q}_{p}^{2\,+}:=\left\{(a,b)\in\mathbb{Q}_{p}^{2}:\left|a\right|_{p}\geq\left|b\right|_{p}\right\}.

Accordingly, the right half of the
p
-adic unit sphere is denoted

\mathbb{S}^{1,+}_{p}:=\mathbb{S}^{1}_{p}\cap\mathbb{Q}_{p}^{2\,+}=\left\{(u,a):u\in\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{\times},a\in\mathbb{Z}_{p}\right\}.

Fact 11
.

The half-sphere
\mathbb{S}^{1,+}_{p}

is homeomorphic to
Q_{p}
and to
\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{\times}\times\mathbb{Z}_{p}
.
Its Haar measure
\mu_{p}^{2}\left(\mathbb{S}^{1,+}_{p}\right)
equals

\mu_{p}(\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{\times})\mu_{p}(\mathbb{Z}_{p})=1-\frac{1}{p}=\mu_{G_{p}}\left(\operatorname{SL}_{2}\left(\mathbb{Z}_{p}\right)\right)
.

To conclude, we have that for
\left(\begin{smallmatrix}a&c\\
b&d\end{smallmatrix}\right)\in G_{p}^{+}
(i.e.
v=(a,b)^{\mbox{t}}\in\mathbb{Q}_{p}^{2\,+}
),

\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}a&c\\
b&d\end{array}\right)\overset{a=u_{a}\left|a\right|_{p}^{-1}}{=}\underset{\in Q_{p}}{\underbrace{\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}u_{a}&0\\
\frac{b}{a}u_{a}&u_{a}^{-1}\end{array}\right)}}\underset{\in A_{p}}{\underbrace{\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}\left|a\right|_{p}^{-1}&0\\
0&\left|a\right|_{p}\end{array}\right)}}\underset{\in N_{p}}{\underbrace{\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}1&\frac{c}{a}\\
0&1\end{array}\right)}},

which means that the
p
-adic analog to (
2
)
is the following. For
g=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}v&w\end{array}\right)\in G_{p}^{+}
, it holds that

\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}v&w\end{array}\right)=\underset{\in Q_{p}}{\underbrace{\left(\begin{matrix}\check{v}&*\end{matrix}\right)}}\underset{\in A_{p}}{\underbrace{\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}\left\|v\right\|_{p}^{-1}&0\\
0&\left\|v\right\|_{p}\end{array}\right)}}\underset{\in N_{p}}{\underbrace{\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}1&\frac{y\left(w\right)}{y\left(v\right)}\\
0&1\end{array}\right)}},

(5)

where
v=(x(v),y(v))^{\mbox{t}}
,
w=(x(w),y(w))^{\mbox{t}}
,
and

\check{v}:=\left\|v\right\|_{p}v

is the unit vector pointing in the
p
-adic direction of
v
(the
projection of
v
to the
p
-adic unit sphere).

Definition 12
.

The Iwasawa-Bruhat decomposition of

\operatorname{SL}_{2}\left(\mathbb{F}\right)^{+}:=\operatorname{SL}_{2}\left(\mathbb{R}\right)\times\operatorname{SL}_{2}\left(\mathbb{Q}_{p}\right)^{+}

is

\operatorname{SL}_{2}\left(\mathbb{F}\right)^{+}=K_{\infty}A_{\infty}N_{\infty}\times Q_{p}A_{p}N_{p}=\underset{:=Q}{\underbrace{\left(K_{\infty}\times Q_{p}\right)}}\underset{:=A}{\underbrace{\left(A_{\infty}\times A_{p}\right)}}\underset{:=N}{\underbrace{\left(N_{\infty}\times N_{p}\right)}}.

2.5
Correspondence between primitive vectors in
\mathbb{Z}\left[\frac{1}{p}\right]^{2}

and matrices in
\operatorname{SL}_{2}\left(\mathbb{Z}\left[\frac{1}{p}\right]\right)

We now define natural subsets in the Bruhat-Iwasawa components.

•

For
\mathcal{D}\subset\mathbb{F}
, we consider

N_{\mathcal{D}}=\left\{\left(\left(\begin{smallmatrix}1&\alpha\\

for

,

for a real arc

Proof of Proposition
13
.

If
a,b\in\mathbb{Z}\left[\frac{1}{p}\right]
are such that

is primitive, then there exist (infinitely many) solutions

over
\mathbb{Z}\left[\frac{1}{p}\right]
,

ax+by=1
. If
\left(x_{0},y_{0}\right)
is such a solution, then
the set of all solutions is

\left\{\left(x_{0},y_{0}\right)+m\left(-b,a\right):m\in\mathbb{Z}\left[\frac{1}{p}\right]\right\}.

A choice of
m\in\mathbb{Z}\left[\frac{1}{p}\right]
sets a unique solution
to this equation. The
\gcd
equation of
v
can also be written
in the form

\det\left(\left[\begin{smallmatrix}b&x\\
-a&y\end{smallmatrix}\right]\right)=1,

which is equivalent to setting

\displaystyle v^{\perp}

\displaystyle=\left(b,-a\right)^{\mbox{t}}

\displaystyle w

\displaystyle=\left(x,y\right)^{\mbox{t}}

and requiring that

\left[\begin{array}[]{cc}v^{\perp}&w\end{array}\right]\in\operatorname{SL}_{2}\left(\mathbb{Z}\left[\frac{1}{p}\right]\right).

The possibilities for the second column
w
are all the solutions
to the
\gcd
equation of
v
; the matrices obtained from the different
possibilities are

\left\{\left[\begin{array}[]{cc}v^{\perp}&w_{0}+mv^{\perp}\end{array}\right]:m\in\mathbb{Z}\left[\frac{1}{p}\right]\right\}

where
w_{0}=\left(x_{0},y_{0}\right)^{\mbox{t}}
is some solution.
This set of matrices is an orbit for the group
\left\{\left[\begin{smallmatrix}1&\mathbb{Z}\left[\frac{1}{p}\right]\\
0&1\end{smallmatrix}\right]\right\}=N_{p}\cap\operatorname{SL}_{2}\left(\mathbb{Z}\left[\frac{1}{p}\right]\right)
acting by right multiplication:

\left\{\left[\begin{array}[]{cc}v^{\perp}&w_{0}\end{array}\right]\left[\begin{array}[]{cc}1&m\\
0&1\end{array}\right]:m\in\mathbb{Z}\left[\frac{1}{p}\right]\right\}.

According to (
2
) and (
5
),
when viewing this set of matrices (via the diagonal embedding) in

\operatorname{SL}_{2}\left(\mathbb{F}\right)
, it equals

\left\{\left(k_{\infty}a_{\infty}\left[\begin{array}[]{cc}1&\frac{\left\langle w,\hat{v}\right\rangle}{\left\|v\right\|}+m\\
0&1\end{array}\right],k_{p}a_{p}\left[\begin{array}[]{cc}1&\frac{y\left(w\right)}{y\left(v\right)}+m\\
0&1\end{array}\right]\right):m\in\mathbb{Z}\left[\frac{1}{p}\right]\right\}.

Since
\mathcal{D}
is a fundamental domain for
\mathbb{Z}\left[\frac{1}{p}\right]^{\operatorname{diag}}

in
\mathbb{F}
, there exists a unique
m
for which
\left(\frac{\left\langle w,\hat{v}\right\rangle}{\left\|v\right\|}+m,\frac{y\left(w\right)}{y\left(v\right)}+m\right)

lies in
\mathcal{D}
. This
m=m\left(\mathcal{D}\right)
determines
a unique solution
w_{v,\mathcal{D}}
which in turn determines a
unique matrix

\gamma_{v,D}:=\left[\begin{array}[]{cc}v^{\perp}&w_{v,\mathcal{D}}\end{array}\right]\in\operatorname{SL}_{2}\left(\mathbb{Z}\left[\frac{1}{p}\right]\right).

This establishes a one to one correspondence
v\leftrightarrow\gamma_{v,\mathcal{D}}

between
\mathbb{Z}\left[\frac{1}{p}\right]^{2}
primitive vectors and matrices
in
\operatorname{SL}_{2}\left(\mathbb{Z}\left[\frac{1}{p}\right]\right)\cap QAN_{\mathcal{D}}
,
and proves part
1
of the proposition. As for
part
2
: the fact that
\left(\hat{v},\check{v}\right)\in\Theta\times\Theta_{p}

if and only if
\pi_{K}\left(\gamma_{v}\right)\in Q_{\Theta,\Theta_{p}}

follows from (
2
) and the definition of
Q_{\Theta,\Theta_{p}}
,
and the fact that
\left\|v\right\|\leq R
and
p^{t_{1}}\leq\left\|v\right\|_{p}\leq p^{t_{2}}

if and only if
\pi_{A}\left(\gamma_{v}\right)\in A_{R,t_{1},t_{2}}

follows from (
5
) and the definition
of
A_{R,t_{1},t_{2}}
.
∎

Counting lattice points inside well-rounded sets

Proposition

gives a reformulation of Theorem

in

inside the

-arithmetic group

]
,
and it relies on ergodic theory. The corpus of work on equidistribution

3.1
Well-roundedness and counting lattice points

Proposition
13

allows us to translate the question on the number of primitive
\mathbb{Z}\left[\frac{1}{p}\right]^{2,+}

vectors of real norm
\left\|v\right\|\leq R
,
p
-adic norm

and directions

to the problem of counting
\operatorname{SL}_{2}\left(\mathbb{Z}\left[\frac{1}{p}\right]\right)^{+}

From now on we fix the fundamental domain from Fact

,

\mathcal{D}:=\left(-\frac{1}{2},\frac{1}{2}\right]\times\mathbb{Z}_{p}.

We now describe a method to approach this counting problem.

Notation 14
.

In what follows,
\mathbf{G}
will denote the product

\prod_{v\in S}\mathbf{G}_{v}\left(\mathbb{K}_{v}\right)
where
\mathbb{K}
is a
number field,
\mathbb{K}_{v}

\mathbb{K}
over a place

is a simple algebraic group defined over

and

is a finite set of places that contains

Definition 15
.

Let
\mathbf{G}
be as in Notation
14
,

\mu
a Borel measure on
\mathbf{G}
, and
\left\{\mathcal{O}_{\epsilon}\right\}_{\epsilon>0}

1.

For a measurable subset
\mathcal{B}\subset\mathbf{G}

\mathcal{B}^{+}\left(\epsilon\right):=\mathcal{O}_{\epsilon}\mathcal{B}\mathcal{O}_{\epsilon}=\bigcup_{u,v\in\mathcal{O}_{\epsilon}}u\,\mathcal{B}\,v,

The set
\mathcal{B}
is

with (positive)

\mu\left(\mathcal{B}^{+}\left(\epsilon\right)\right)\leq\left(1+\mathcal{C}\epsilon\right)\>\mu\left(\mathcal{B}^{-}\left(\epsilon\right)\right).

(6)

3.

A family
\left\{\mathcal{B}_{R}\right\}_{R>0}\subset\mathbf{G}

0<\epsilon<\epsilon_{0}
and

\mathcal{B}_{R}

\left(\mathcal{C},\epsilon_{0}\right)

Remark 16
.

Indeed the definition of LWR depends on the choice of a family
\left\{\mathcal{O}_{\epsilon}\right\}_{\epsilon>0}

of identity neighborhoods; however, we disregard this fact since we
will only work with a specific family, cf. Definition
19
.

The notion of well-roundedness of a family is by now standard (see
e.g.
[
EM93
]
), but it is less common to define a well-rounded

set
; however, this notion will be useful for us, as the sets
under our consideration will project to a well-rounded family in the

-adic one.

Theorem 17

(
[
GN12
, Theorems 1.9, 4.5, and Remark 1.10]

.

Let
\mathbf{G}
as in Notation
14

and with Haar measure

be a lattice.
Assume that
\left\{\mathcal{B}_{R}\right\}\subset\mathbf{G}

\mu\left(\mathcal{B}_{R}\right)\to\infty

as
T\to\infty
. If the family
\left\{\mathcal{B}_{R}\right\}

\tau\left(\Gamma\right)\in\left(0,\frac{1}{2\left(1+\dim\mathbf{G}\right)}\right)

such that for
R

\delta>0

\left|\#\left(\mathcal{B}_{R}\cap\Gamma\right)-\frac{\mu\left(\mathcal{B}_{R}\right)}{\mu\left(\mathbf{G}/\Gamma\right)}\right|\underset{\mathbf{G},\Gamma,\delta}{\ll}\text{const}\cdot\mu\left(\mathcal{B}_{R}\right)^{1-\tau\left(\Gamma\right)+\delta}

of a fundamental domain of

in
\mathbf{G}
.

Remark 18

(
The error exponent
)
.

The parameter
\tau\left(\Gamma\right)

depends on estimates on the rate of decay of matrix coefficients of
automorphic representations of
\mathbf{G}
. For
\mathbf{G}=\operatorname{SL}_{2}\left(\mathbb{R}\right)\times\operatorname{SL}_{2}\left(\mathbb{Q}_{p}\right)

any bound toward the generalized Ramanujan conjecture, from Gelbart-Jacquet

[
GJ78
]
to Kim-Sarnak
[
Kim03
]
, implies that these

(see e.g.
[

These bounds give

,
and only the full Ramanujan conjecture would give a better exponent,

[

]
.

According to Theorem

5
Proof of Well-roundedness in
\operatorname{SL}_{2}\left(\mathbb{Q}_{p}\right)

The goal of this final subsection is to prove Proposition
20
,
i.e. the well-roundedness of the sets
\left(Q_{p}\right)_{\Theta_{p}}\left(A_{p}\right)_{t_{1},t_{2}}\left(N_{p}\right)_{\alpha+p^{\psi}\mathbb{Z}_{p}}

in
\operatorname{SL}_{2}\left(\mathbb{Q}_{p}\right)
. The main step is a measurement of
how the Bruhat components are modified by a small left or right perturbation.
To state this proposition, we need an additional notation: for any

g\in\operatorname{SL}_{2}\left(\mathbb{Q}_{p}\right)
, we denote by
\|\operatorname{Ad}_{g}\|_{op}
the
operator norm of
\operatorname{Ad}_{g}
acting on
Mat_{2}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})
. Note that
this operator norm takes values in
p^{\mathbb{Z}}
, as the max norm on

\mathbb{Q}_{p}^{2}
.

Proposition 25

(
Effective Bruhat-Iwasawa decomposition
)
.

Let
g=qan\in\operatorname{SL}_{2}^{+}\left(\mathbb{Q}_{p}\right)

with
a=\left[\begin{smallmatrix}p^{-t}&0\\
o&p^{t}\end{smallmatrix}\right]
. Let
c\left(a,n\right)=\left\|\operatorname{Ad}_{n}\right\|_{\text{op}}\max(p^{-t},1)\in p^{\mathbb{Z}}
.
The function
c
is bounded when
n
is restricted to a bounded
set and
t
is bounded from below Moreover, we have

\mathcal{O}_{\epsilon}^{G_{p}}qan\mathcal{O}_{\epsilon}^{G_{p}}\in q\mathcal{O}_{c\left(a,n\right)\cdot\epsilon}^{Q_{p}}a\mathcal{O}_{c\left(a,n\right)\cdot\epsilon}^{N_{p}}n

when
\epsilon\in p^{-\mathbb{N}}
is small enough.

The proof of Proposition
25

requires the following three Lemmas:

Lemma 26
.

For
g\in\operatorname{SL}_{2}\left(\mathbb{Q}_{p}\right)
,

g\mathcal{O}_{p^{-N}}g^{-1}\subseteq\mathcal{O}_{\left\|\operatorname{Ad}_{g}\right\|_{\text{op}}p^{-N}}

where
\left\|\operatorname{Ad}_{g}\right\|_{\text{op}}\in p^{\mathbb{Z}}
is the
operator norm of conjugation by
g
.

Proof.

Indeed,

\displaystyle g\mathcal{O}_{p^{-N}}g^{-1}

\displaystyle\subseteq g\left(\operatorname{I}_{2}+p^{N}\text{Mat}_{2}\left(\mathbb{Z}_{p}\right)\right)g^{-1}

\displaystyle=\left(\operatorname{I}_{2}+g\cdot p^{N}\text{Mat}_{2}\left(\mathbb{Z}_{p}\right)\cdot g^{-1}\right)

\displaystyle\subseteq\left(\operatorname{I}_{2}+\left\|\operatorname{Ad}_{g}\right\|_{\text{op}}p^{N}\text{Mat}_{2}\left(\mathbb{Z}_{p}\right)\right)=\mathcal{O}_{\left\|\operatorname{Ad}_{g}\right\|_{\text{op}}p^{-N}}.

It is clear that
\left\|\operatorname{Ad}_{g}\right\|_{\text{op}}
is a
power of
p
, since, as an operator norm, it is the maximum of norms
of (
p
-adic) vectors.
∎

Lemma 27
.

For every
k_{p}\in K_{p}
,
\left\|\operatorname{Ad}_{k_{p}}\right\|_{\text{op}}=1
.

Proof.

We know that
K_{p}\cdot\mathbb{S}^{1}_{p}=\mathbb{S}^{1}_{p}
, and that this
action preserves the
p
-adic norm. For
T\in\text{Mat}_{2}\left(\mathbb{Q}_{p}\right)
,
we need to show that
\left\|\operatorname{Ad}_{k_{p}}\left(T\right)\right\|=\left\|T\right\|
, where
the norm on
\text{Mat}_{2}\left(\mathbb{Q}_{p}\right)
is the operator norm. Indeed

\left\|\operatorname{Ad}_{k_{p}}\left(T\right)\right\|=\sup_{x\in\mathbb{S}^{1}_{p}}\left\|\operatorname{Ad}_{k_{p}}\left(T\right)\cdot x\right\|_{p}=\sup_{x\in\mathbb{S}^{1}_{p}}\left\|k_{p}^{-1}Tk_{p}\cdot x\right\|_{p}=\sup_{y\in\mathbb{S}^{1}_{p}}\left\|Ty\right\|_{p}=\left\|T\right\|.

Then
\operatorname{Ad}_{k_{p}}
is norm preserving, and therefore has operator
norm
1
.
∎

Lemma 28
.

For any
N\geq 1
,

\mathcal{O}_{p^{-N}}^{G_{p}}=\mathcal{O}_{p^{-N}}^{Q_{p}}\mathcal{O}_{p^{-N}}^{N_{p}},

\mathcal{O}_{p^{-N}}^{Q_{p}}=\mathcal{O}_{p^{-N}}^{N_{p}^{-}}\mathcal{O}_{p^{-N}}^{M_{p}}.

Proof.

We note that when
N\geq 1

\mathcal{O}_{p^{-N}}^{Q_{p}}=\left\{\left(\begin{matrix}1+p^{N}a&0\\
p^{N}b&\left(1+p^{N}a\right)^{-1}\end{matrix}\right):a,b\in\mathbb{Z}_{p}\right\},

\mathcal{O}_{p^{-N}}^{N_{p}}=\left\{\left(\begin{matrix}1&p^{N}\mathbb{Z}_{p}\\
0&1\end{matrix}\right)\right\}

and

\mathcal{O}_{p^{-N}}^{M_{p}}=\left\{\left(\begin{matrix}1+p^{N}\alpha&0\\
0&\left(1+p^{N}\alpha\right)^{-1}\end{matrix}\right):\alpha\in\mathbb{Z}_{p}\right\}.

The inclusions
\supseteq
in the statement of the lemma are trivial.
For the opposite direction, observe that

\mathcal{O}_{p^{-N}}^{G_{p}}\ni\left(\begin{matrix}1+p^{N}a&p^{N}c\\
p^{N}d&1+p^{N}b\end{matrix}\right)=\left(\begin{matrix}1+p^{N}x&0\\
p^{N}y&\left(1+p^{N}x\right)^{-1}\end{matrix}\right)\left(\begin{matrix}1&p^{N}z\\
0&1\end{matrix}\right)

for

\begin{matrix}x=a,&y=d,&z=\frac{c}{1+p^{N}a}\end{matrix},

as
\left(1+p^{N}x\right)^{-1}=1-\frac{P^{N}x}{1+p^{N}x}
and the
determinant of the left-hand matrix is
1
. Similarly,

\mathcal{O}_{p^{-N}}^{Q_{p}}\ni\left(\begin{matrix}1+p^{N}a&0\\
p^{N}b&\left(1+p^{N}a\right)^{-1}\end{matrix}\right)=\left(\begin{matrix}1&0\\
p^{N}z&1\end{matrix}\right)\left(\begin{matrix}1+p^{N}x&0\\
0&\left(1+p^{N}x\right)^{-1}\end{matrix}\right)

for

\begin{matrix}x=a,&z=\frac{b}{1+p^{N}a}\end{matrix}.\qed

We now turn to prove Proposition
25
.

Proof of Proposition
25
.

Let
g=qan
with
q\in Q
,
a=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}p^{-t}&0\\
o&p^{t}\end{smallmatrix}\right)\in A_{p}
and
n=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}1&x\\
0&1\end{smallmatrix}\right)\in N_{p}
. We will use the fact that

\begin{array}[]{ccccccc}a^{-1}n_{x}a&=&\left(\begin{smallmatrix}p^{t}&0\\
o&p^{-t}\end{smallmatrix}\right)\left(\begin{smallmatrix}1&x\\
0&1\end{smallmatrix}\right)\left(\begin{smallmatrix}p^{-t}&0\\
o&p^{t}\end{smallmatrix}\right)&=&\left(\begin{smallmatrix}1&xp^{2t}\\
0&1\end{smallmatrix}\right)&=&n_{xp^{2t}}\\
\\
an_{y}^{-}a^{-1}&=&\left(\begin{smallmatrix}p^{-t}&0\\
o&p^{t}\end{smallmatrix}\right)\left(\begin{smallmatrix}1&0\\
y&1\end{smallmatrix}\right)\left(\begin{smallmatrix}p^{t}&0\\
o&p^{-t}\end{smallmatrix}\right)&=&\left(\begin{smallmatrix}1&0\\
yp^{2t}&1\end{smallmatrix}\right)&=&n_{yp^{2t}}^{-}\end{array}

(7)

where
n_{x}=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}1&x\\
0&1\end{smallmatrix}\right)\in N_{p}
,
n_{y}^{-}=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}1&0\\
y&1\end{smallmatrix}\right)\in N_{p}^{-}
and
a=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}p^{-t}&0\\
o&p^{t}\end{smallmatrix}\right)
.

Step 1: Left perturbations.

Set
\epsilon=p^{-N}
. Since
\left\|\operatorname{Ad}_{q}\right\|_{\text{op}}=1

(Lemma
27
), then by Lemmas
26

and
28
we have that,

\displaystyle\mathcal{O}_{\epsilon}^{G_{p}}qan

\displaystyle=q\left(q^{-1}\mathcal{O}_{\epsilon}^{G_{p}}q\right)an\subseteq q\mathcal{O}_{\epsilon}^{G_{p}}an=q\mathcal{O}_{\epsilon}^{Q_{p}}\mathcal{O}_{\epsilon}^{N_{p}}an.

According to (
7
),

=q\mathcal{O}_{\epsilon}^{Q_{p}}a\cdot a^{-1}\mathcal{O}_{\epsilon}^{N_{p}}an=q\mathcal{O}_{\epsilon}^{Q_{p}}\cdot a\cdot\mathcal{O}_{p^{-2t}\epsilon}^{N_{p}}n.

Step 2: Right perturbations.

By letting
C\left(n\right)=\left\|\operatorname{Ad}_{n}\right\|_{\text{op}}\in p^{\mathbb{Z}}
,
we have according to Lemma
26

that

qan\mathcal{O}_{\epsilon}^{G_{p}}=qa\left(n\mathcal{O}_{\epsilon}^{G_{p}}n^{-1}\right)n\subseteq qa\mathcal{O}_{C\left(n\right)\epsilon}^{G_{p}}n;

By Lemma
28
and to (
7
),

\displaystyle\subseteq qa\cdot\mathcal{O}_{C\left(n\right)\epsilon}^{N_{p}^{-}}\mathcal{O}_{C\left(n\right)\epsilon}^{M_{p}}\mathcal{O}_{C\left(n\right)\epsilon}^{N_{p}}\cdot n

\displaystyle=qa\cdot\mathcal{O}_{C\left(n\right)\epsilon}^{N_{p}^{-}}a^{-1}\cdot a\mathcal{O}_{C\left(n\right)\epsilon}^{M_{p}}\mathcal{O}_{C\left(n\right)\epsilon}^{N_{p}}\cdot n

\displaystyle=q\mathcal{O}_{p^{-2t}C\left(n\right)\epsilon}^{N_{p}^{-}}a\cdot\mathcal{O}_{C\left(n\right)\epsilon}^{M_{p}}\mathcal{O}_{C\left(n\right)\epsilon}^{N_{p}}\cdot n.

Since
A
and
M
commute,

=q\mathcal{O}_{p^{-2t}C\left(n\right)\epsilon}^{N_{p}^{-}}\mathcal{O}_{C\left(n\right)\epsilon}^{M_{p}}\cdot a\mathcal{O}_{C\left(n\right)\epsilon}^{N_{p}}n.

By letting
C\left(a\right)=\max\left\{p^{-2t},1\right\}
we have

\subseteq q\mathcal{O}_{C\left(a\right)C\left(n\right)\epsilon}^{N_{p}^{-}}\mathcal{O}_{C\left(a\right)C\left(n\right)\epsilon}^{M_{p}}\cdot a\mathcal{O}_{C\left(n\right)\epsilon}^{N_{p}}n.

and then by Lemma
28

\subseteq q\mathcal{O}_{C\left(a\right)C\left(n\right)\epsilon}^{Q_{p}}\mathcal{O}_{C\left(a\right)C\left(n\right)\epsilon}^{N_{p}}a\mathcal{O}_{C\left(n\right)\epsilon}^{N_{p}}n.

Finally, by (
7
)

\displaystyle=q\mathcal{O}_{C\left(a\right)C\left(n\right)\epsilon}^{Q_{p}}a\cdot a^{-1}\mathcal{O}_{C\left(a\right)C\left(n\right)\epsilon}^{N_{p}}a\mathcal{O}_{C\left(n\right)\epsilon}^{N_{p}}n

\displaystyle=q\mathcal{O}_{C\left(a\right)C\left(n\right)\epsilon}^{Q_{p}}a\mathcal{O}_{p^{-2k}C\left(a\right)C\left(n\right)\epsilon}^{N_{p}}\mathcal{O}_{C\left(n\right)\epsilon}^{N_{p}}n

\displaystyle\subseteq q\mathcal{O}_{C\left(a\right)^{2}C\left(n\right)\epsilon}^{Q_{p}}a\mathcal{O}_{C\left(a\right)^{2}C\left(n\right)\epsilon}^{N_{p}}n.

Combining the effect of both left and right perturbations, we obtain
that

\mathcal{O}_{\epsilon}^{G_{p}}qan\mathcal{O}_{\epsilon}^{G_{p}}\in q\mathcal{O}_{c\left(a,n\right)\cdot\epsilon}^{Q_{p}}a\mathcal{O}_{c\left(a,n\right)\cdot\epsilon}^{N_{p}}n

where
c\left(a,n\right)=C\left(a\right)^{2}C\left(n\right)
is a
power of
p
(since
C\left(a\right)
and
C\left(n\right)
are)
that is bounded when
t
is bounded from below and
n
is restricted
to a bounded set. Require that
\epsilon<c\left(a,n\right)^{-1}
to obtain
that
c\left(a,n\right)\epsilon\in p^{-\mathbb{N}}
.
∎
We can now prove Proposition
20
.
The proof essentially relies on the ultrametric nature of
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
:
a small enough perturbation of a ball is the ball itself. The first
claim (
8
) is the translation of this
phenomenon in our setting.

Proof of Proposition
20
.

We first claim that for
N\geq 0
large enough and
c\epsilon\leq p^{-N}

we have:

\begin{cases}\mathcal{O}_{c\epsilon}^{N_{p}}\cdot\left(N_{p}\right)_{\alpha+p^{\psi}\mathbb{Z}_{p}}\subseteq\left(N_{p}\right)_{\alpha+p^{\psi}\mathbb{Z}_{p}}&\text{ and }\left(N_{p}\right)_{\alpha+p^{\psi}\mathbb{Z}_{p}}\cdot\mathcal{O}_{c\epsilon}^{N_{p}}\subseteq\left(N_{p}\right)_{\alpha+p^{\psi}\mathbb{Z}_{p}}\\
\mathcal{O}_{c\epsilon}^{Q_{p}}\left(Q_{p}\right)_{\Theta_{p}}\subseteq\left(Q_{p}\right)_{\Theta_{p}}&\text{ and }\left(Q_{p}\right)_{\Theta_{p}}\cdot\mathcal{O}_{c\epsilon}^{Q_{p}}\subseteq\left(Q_{p}\right)_{\Theta_{p}}.\end{cases}.

(8)

The inclusions in the first row are a trivial computation. For the
inclusions in the second row, write
\Theta_{p}=\Theta_{p}\left(\check{v},p^{k}\right)

where
k\geq 0
and
\check{v}\in\mathbb{S}^{1,+}_{p}
. Let
N\geq 0
such
that
\mathcal{O}_{c\epsilon}=\mathcal{O}_{p^{N}}
, and assume that
N\geq k
. Observe
that

\mathcal{O}_{c\epsilon}^{Q_{p}}=\mathcal{O}_{p^{N}}^{Q_{p}}=\left\{\left(\begin{matrix}1+p^{N}\mathbb{Z}_{p}&0\\
p^{N}\mathbb{Z}_{p}&*\end{matrix}\right)\right\}.

By letting
\check{v}=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}u_{1}\\
p^{\ell}u_{2}\end{smallmatrix}\right)\in\mathbb{S}^{1,+}_{p}
where
u_{1},u_{2}\in\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{\times}
and
\ell\geq 0
, then

\displaystyle\left(Q_{p}\right)_{\Theta_{p}\left(\check{v},p^{k}\right)}=\left\{\left(\begin{matrix}\check{v}+p^{k}\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{2}&\begin{smallmatrix}0\\
\end{smallmatrix}\end{matrix}\right)\right\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left\{\left(\begin{matrix}u_{1}+p^{k}\mathbb{Z}_{p}&0\\
p^{\ell}u_{2}+p^{k}\mathbb{Z}_{p}&*\end{matrix}\right)\right\}.

Take
\left(\begin{smallmatrix}u_{1}+p^{k}\alpha&0\\
p^{\ell}u_{2}+p^{k}\beta&\left(u_{1}+p^{k}\alpha\right)^{-1}\end{smallmatrix}\right)\in\left(Q_{p}\right)_{\Theta_{p}\left(\check{v},p^{k}\right)}
and
\left(\begin{smallmatrix}1+p^{N}\gamma&0\\
p^{N}\delta&\left(1+p^{N}\gamma\right)^{-1}\end{smallmatrix}\right)\in\mathcal{O}_{p^{-N}}^{Q_{p}}
(here
\alpha,\beta,\gamma\in\mathbb{Z}_{p}
). Now,

\displaystyle\left(\begin{smallmatrix}1+p^{N}\gamma&0\\
p^{N}\delta&\left(1+p^{N}\gamma\right)^{-1}\end{smallmatrix}\right)\cdot\left(\begin{smallmatrix}u_{1}+p^{k}\alpha&0\\
p^{\ell}u_{2}+p^{k}\beta&\left(u_{1}+p^{k}\alpha\right)^{-1}\end{smallmatrix}\right)

\displaystyle\overset{N\geq k}{\in}\left\{\left(\begin{smallmatrix}u_{1}+p^{k}\mathbb{Z}_{p}&0\\
p^{\ell}u_{2}+p^{k}\mathbb{Z}_{p}&*\end{smallmatrix}\right)\right\}

\displaystyle=\left(Q_{p}\right)_{\Theta_{p}\left(\check{v},p^{k}\right)},

\displaystyle\left(\begin{smallmatrix}u_{1}+p^{k}\alpha&0\\
p^{\ell}u_{2}+p^{k}\beta&\left(u_{1}+p^{k}\alpha\right)^{-1}\end{smallmatrix}\right)\cdot\left(\begin{smallmatrix}1+p^{N}\gamma&0\\
p^{N}\delta&\left(1+p^{N}\gamma\right)^{-1}\end{smallmatrix}\right)

\displaystyle\overset{N\geq k}{\in}\left(\begin{smallmatrix}u_{1}+p^{k}\mathbb{Z}_{p}&0\\
p^{\ell}u_{2}+p^{k}\mathbb{Z}_{p}&*\end{smallmatrix}\right)

\displaystyle=\left(Q_{p}\right)_{\Theta_{p}\left(\check{v},p^{k}\right)}.

Having proved the inclusions in (
8
),
the statement of Proposition
20

follows: according to Proposition
25
,
when
g_{p}=qan\in G_{p}^{+}
lies in
Q_{\Theta_{p}^{+}}A_{t_{1},t_{2}}N_{\alpha+p^{\psi}\mathbb{Z}_{p}}
,
then

\mathcal{O}_{\epsilon}^{G_{p}}qan\mathcal{O}_{\epsilon}^{G_{p}}\subseteq q\mathcal{O}_{c\epsilon}^{Q_{p}}a\mathcal{O}_{c\epsilon}^{N_{p}}n

(where
c=c\left(a,n\right)
); but then according to (
8
),
this is contained in
Q_{\Theta_{p}^{+}}A_{t_{1},t_{2}}N_{\alpha+p^{\psi}\mathbb{Z}_{p}}
.
Thus

\left(Q_{\Theta_{p}^{+}}A_{t_{1},t_{2}}N_{\alpha+p^{\psi}\mathbb{Z}_{p}}\right)^{+\epsilon}\subseteq Q_{\Theta_{p}^{+}}A_{t_{1},t_{2}}N_{\alpha+p^{\psi}\mathbb{Z}_{p}},

and the opposite inclusion is obvious. Similarly,

Q_{\Theta_{p}^{+}}A_{t_{1},t_{2}}N_{\alpha+p^{\psi}\mathbb{Z}_{p}}\subseteq\left(Q_{\Theta_{p}^{+}}A_{t_{1},t_{2}}N_{\alpha+p^{\psi}\mathbb{Z}_{p}}\right)^{-\epsilon}

and the opposite inclusion is obvious. Then

\left(Q_{\Theta_{p}^{+}}A_{t_{1},t_{2}}N_{\alpha+p^{\psi}\mathbb{Z}_{p}}\right)^{-\epsilon}=Q_{\Theta_{p}^{+}}A_{t_{1},t_{2}}N_{\alpha+p^{\psi}\mathbb{Z}_{p}}=\left(Q_{\Theta_{p}^{+}}A_{t_{1},t_{2}}N_{\alpha+p^{\psi}\mathbb{Z}_{p}}\right)^{+\epsilon},

meaning that the well-roundedness condition holds trivially.
∎

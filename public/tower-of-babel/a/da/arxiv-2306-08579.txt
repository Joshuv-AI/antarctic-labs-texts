Fibrations by plane quartic curves with a canonical moving singularity
Cesar Hilario, Karl-Otto Stöhr

Abstract
. We classify fibrations by integral plane projective rational quartic curves whose generic fibre is regular but admits a non-smooth point that is a canonical divisor. These fibrations can only exist in characteristic two. The geometric generic fibre, which determines the generic behaviour of the special fibres, is an integral plane projective rational quartic curve over the algebraic closure of the function field of the base. It has the remarkable property that the tangent lines at the non-singular points are either all bitangents or all non-ordinary inflection tangents; moreover it is strange, that is, all the tangent lines meet in a common point. We construct two fibrations that are universal in the sense that any other fibration with the aforementioned properties can be obtained from one of them by a base extension. Furthermore, among these fibrations we choose a pencil of plane quartic curves and study in detail its geometry. We determine the corresponding minimal regular model and we describe it as a purely inseparable double covering of a quasi-elliptic fibration.

1.
Introduction

By Bertini’s theorem on variable singular points, in characteristic zero the general fibre of a fibration
f:T\to B
of smooth algebraic varieties over an algebraically closed field
k
is smooth.
This no longer holds in positive characteristic.
Classical examples include the quasi-elliptic fibrations that appeared in the classification of algebraic surfaces in characteristics 2 and 3 by Bombieri and Mumford
[
3
]
.

In this paper we classify a certain class of fibrations with non-smooth general fibre, namely fibrations by plane projective rational quartic curves with a canonical moving singularity.
The generic fibre
C=f^{-1}(\eta)
of such a fibration
f:T\to B
is a regular non-hyperelliptic geometrically integral curve over the function field
K=k(B)
of the base, which admits a non-smooth point
\mathfrak{p}
that is a canonical divisor on
C
.
Moreover it is geometrically rational, i.e., the geometric generic fibre
C\otimes_{K}\overline{K}
is a rational curve over the algebraic closure
\overline{K}
, and the arithmetic genus is equal to
h^{1}(C,\mathcal{O}_{C})=3
.

It turns out that a curve
C
satisfying the above properties can only exist in characteristic
p=2
.
Our main result gives a precise description

Theorem 1.1

(see Theorem
4.3
)
.

Let
C
be a regular proper non-hyperelliptic geometrically integral and geometrically rational curve of arithmetic genus
h^{1}(C,\mathcal{O}_{C})=3
over a field
K
of characteristic
p
.
Assume in addition that
C
admits a non-smooth point
\mathfrak{p}
that is a canonical divisor on
C
.
Then
p=2
and
C
is isomorphic to one of the following plane projective quartics.

(1)

y^{4}+az^{4}+xz^{3}+bx^{2}z^{2}+cx^{4}=0
,

where
a,b,c\in K
are constants satisfying
c\notin K^{2}
.

(2)

y^{4}+az^{4}+bx^{2}y^{2}+cx^{2}z^{2}+bx^{3}z+dx^{4}=0
,

where
a,b,c,d\in K
are constants satisfying
a\notin K^{2}
and
b\neq 0
.
In this case the polynomial expression
\iota=ab^{2}+c^{2}+1
is an invariant of the curve
C
.

Conversely, each of these equations defines a curve of the above type.

In addition, we determine the isomorphism classes of these curves (see Propositions
4.5
and
3.5
),
thus completing their classification.
The point
\mathfrak{p}
is the only non-smooth point on the regular curve
C
.
Its images
\mathfrak{p}_{n}\in C_{n}
in the sequence of normalized Frobenius pullbacks

C_{0}=C\to C_{1}\to C_{2}\to C_{3}\to\cdots,

where
C_{n}
denotes the normalization of the
n
th iterated Frobenius pullback
C^{(p^{n})}
,
have geometric
\delta
-invariants

\delta(\mathfrak{p})=3
,
\delta(\mathfrak{p}_{1})=1
,
\delta(\mathfrak{p}_{2})=\delta(\mathfrak{p}_{3})=\dots=0
.
For
n\geq 3
the smooth point
\mathfrak{p}_{n}
is actually rational (see
[
11
, Theorem 2.24]
), whereas for
n=2
both possibilities can occur:

\mathfrak{p}_{2}
is rational for item
1
, and non-rational for item
2
.

If the curve
C|K
is the generic fibre of a fibration
f:T\to B
, then the properties of almost all special fibres are governed by the properties of the geometric generic fibre
C\otimes_{K}\overline{K}
,
which is an integral plane projective rational quartic curve over the algebraic closure
\overline{K}=\overline{k(B)}
.
Its only singularity is a unibranch point, whose only tangent line does not intersect the curve at any other point.
The quartic curve
C\otimes_{K}\overline{K}
is
strange
, i.e., all its tangent lines meet in a common point,
and it has the remarkable property that the tangent lines at its non-singular points are
either all bitangents (in case
2
) or all non-ordinary inflection tangents (in case
1
).

We construct two fibrations by plane projective rational quartics with a canonical moving singularity, that are universal in the sense that any other fibration whose generic fibre satisfies the hypotheses in Theorem
1.1
can be obtained from one of them by a base extension (see Theorems
5.1
and
5.2
).
We prove that their total spaces are uniruled, and in fact that the total space of every closed subfibration obtained from them is uniruled.

We show that the only singular point on
C\otimes_{K}\overline{K}
has multiplicity two or three.
The case of multiplicity three occurs exactly in item
1
, when
b=0
;
the corresponding pencil of quartics is studied in detail in Section
6
,
where we describe explicitly its minimal regular model
(see Theorem
6.2
).
The determination of the minimal model is inspired by work of Kodaira and Néron
[
13
,
16
]

on the classification of special fibres of minimal fibrations by elliptic curves.
Furthermore, we describe the pencil as a purely inseparable double cover of a
quasi-elliptic fibration
.
The existence of such a cover is due to a remarkable fact: the normalized Frobenius pullbacks
C_{1}
of the curves
C
in Theorem
1.1
are
quasi-elliptic
.

Quasi-elliptic fibrations
were introduced by Bombieri and Mumford in their extension of the Enriques-Kodaira classification of complex algebraic surfaces to arbitrary characteristics
[
3
,
4
]
.
The generic fibre of a quasi-elliptic fibration is a
quasi-elliptic curve
, which is a regular geometrically integral curve of arithmetic genus
1
that is non-smooth.
Since
C_{1}
is quasi-elliptic, the curve
C
is a purely inseparable cover of degree
p=2
of a quasi-elliptic curve, via the Frobenius map
C\to C_{1}
.
This is a unique feature of geometry in characteristic
p=2
,
for in characteristic
p>2
the normalized Frobenius pullback
X_{1}
of any regular geometrically integral curve
X
of arithmetic genus
h^{1}(X,\mathcal{O}_{X})=3
is smooth (see
[
11
, Corollary 2.7]
).

The investigation of varieties over imperfect fields has attracted substantial attention in recent years due to their connection with the Minimal Model Program in positive characteristic, where Del Pezzo surfaces play a prominent role (see
[
7
,
23
,
2
]
).
The case of interest to us, namely the case of regular but non-smooth curves, was analysed quite early by Tate
[
24
]
, who established restrictions on the drop in genus, by Queen
[
17
]
, who studied quasi-elliptic function fields, and quite recently by Tanaka
[
23
, Sections 9 and 10]
, Fanelli and Schröer
[
7
, Section 12]
, and Hilario and Schröer
[
9
]
.

The present article is the first in a sequence of two papers on the classification of fibrations by plane projective rational quartic curves in characteristic two (see
[
10
]
).
Our approach relies on the correspondence between function fields and regular curves
[
8
, II.7.4]
, a procedure we have also exploited in our previous article
[
11
]
.
The starting point is the main theorem in
[
11
]
, which guarantees that the function field of the regular curve
C_{3}|K
is rational.
Then the Riemann–Roch theorem coupled with the Bedoya–Stöhr algorithm
[
1
]
provide us with suitably chosen algebraic functions that we can adjoin to the function field of
C_{3}|K
, to successively construct explicit presentations for the function fields of
C_{2}|K
,
C_{1}|K
and
C|K
.
This allows us to realize
C|K
as a plane projective quartic curve via a canonical embedding, given by the canonical linear system
|\mathfrak{p}|
.
In addition, since the regular curve
C_{1}|K
is quasi-elliptic, on our way we are able to recover Queen’s characterization of quasi-elliptic function fields
[
17
]
(see Section
2
).

The paper is organized as follows.
In Section
2
we obtain a description of quasi-elliptic function fields that is suited to our needs in Section
3
, where we carry out a detailed study of the function fields
F|K
of genus
g=3
which are geometrically rational and have a unique singular prime
\mathfrak{p}
that is a canonical divisor.
These results are employed in Section
4
to construct their canonical models in the non-hyperelliptic case,
which in turn yield the desired embeddings of our curves as plane projective quartics.
In the last two sections we discuss the two aforementioned universal fibrations and the pencil of quartics that can be viewed as a cover of a quasi-elliptic pencil.

2.
Quasi-elliptic function fields

Given a regular proper geometrically integral curve
C
over a field
K
, the properties of
C|K
are encoded in the function field
F|K=K(C)|K
, which is a

one-dimensional separable function field
, i.e.,
F|K
is a separable finitely generated field extension of transcendence degree
1
, such that
K
is algebraically closed in
F
.
Conversely, every one-dimensional separable function field
F|K
is attached to a curve
C|K
of the above type.

A one-dimensional separable function field
F|K
is called
quasi-elliptic
if it has genus
g=1
and if it is geometrically rational, that is, the extended function field
\overline{K}F|\overline{K}=\overline{K}\otimes_{K}F|\overline{K}
has genus
\overline{g}=0
.
These function fields can only occur in characteristic
p\in\{2,3\}
, and they indeed play an important role in the arithmetic of Enriques surfaces in characteristic two (see for example
[
3
,
12
]
).

Quasi-elliptic function fields were investigated in detail by Queen
[
17
,
18
]
.
Our motivation for revising Queen’s results in this section is to provide a description that is suitable for our purposes in Section
3
, where we will encounter function fields
F|K
whose first Frobenius pullbacks
F_{1}|K=F^{p}{\cdot}K|K
are quasi-elliptic.
Our approach, based on the main theorem in
[
11
]
,
provides certain Riemann-Roch spaces that are not given in
[
17
]
,
but which will be crucial to derive a normal form of
F|K
out of a normal form of
F_{1}|K
in Section
3
.
The method also illustrates a general procedure to construct a normal form of a function field
F|K
as soon as a normal form of some Frobenius pullback
F_{n}|K=F^{p^{n}}{\cdot}K|K
is known (see
[
11
, p. 281]
).
Recall that each Frobenius pullback
F_{n}|K
is characterized by the property that
F_{n}
is the only intermediate field of
F|K
such that the extension
F|F_{n}
is purely inseparable of degree
p^{n}
.

Let
F|K
be a quasi-elliptic function field.
By Rosenlicht’s genus drop formula (see
[
19
, Corollary of Theorem 11]
or
[
11
, Formula 2.3]
) the number of the singular primes of
F|K
counted with their geometric singularity degrees is equal to
g-\overline{g}=1
,
hence there is a unique singular prime
\mathfrak{p}
, whose geometric singularity degree
\delta(\mathfrak{p})
is equal to
1
.
As singular primes can only occur in characteristic
p>0
,
in which case their singularity degrees are multiples of
(p-1)/2

[
11
, Proposition 2.5]
, we conclude that
p=2
or
p=3
.

To study the extension of
\mathfrak{p}
to the purely inseparable base field extension
FK^{1/p}|K^{1/p}
, one can apply the absolute Frobenius map
z\mapsto z^{p}
and consider instead the restriction
\mathfrak{p}_{1}
of
\mathfrak{p}
to the first Frobenius pullback
F_{1}|K=F^{p}K|K
of
F|K
.
The restricted prime
\mathfrak{p}_{1}
is non-singular by
[
11
, Corollary 2.6]
and so by the genus drop formula the function field
F_{1}|K
has genus
g_{1}=\overline{g}=0
.
According to
[
11
, Corollary 2.19]
the prime
\mathfrak{p}
is non-decomposed (in the base field extension
\overline{K}F|\overline{K}
), i.e., there is a unique prime of
\overline{K}F|\overline{K}
lying over
\mathfrak{p}
,
i.e.,
\mathfrak{p}
is purely inseparable
[
11
, Corollary 2.17]
.
By
[
11
, Theorem 2.24]
the restriction
\mathfrak{p}_{2}
of
\mathfrak{p}
to the second Frobenius pullback
F_{2}|K
is rational.
In characteristic
p=3
the non-singular prime
\mathfrak{p}_{1}
is also rational, but in characteristic
p=2
it may not be, as illustrated in the following theorem.

Theorem 2.1

(
[
17
, Theorem 2]
)
.

A one-dimensional separable function field
F|K
of characteristic
p=2
is quasi-elliptic, i.e.,
it is geometrically rational and admits a unique singular prime
\mathfrak{p}
, whose geometric singularity degree
\delta(\mathfrak{p})
is equal to 1,
if and only if
F|K
can be put into one of the normal forms below.

(1)

F|K=K(x,z)|K
, where

z^{2}=a_{0}+x+a_{2}x^{2}+a_{4}x^{4}

and
a_{0},a_{2}\in K
,
a_{4}\in K\setminus K^{2}
.
Here the singular prime
\mathfrak{p}
has degrees
\deg(\mathfrak{p})=2
,
\deg(\mathfrak{p}_{1})=1
, and residue fields
\kappa(\mathfrak{p})=K(a_{4}^{1/2})
,
\kappa(\mathfrak{p}_{1})=K
.

(2)

F|K=K(x,z)|K
, where

z^{4}=a_{0}+x+a_{2}x^{2}+b_{2}^{2}x^{4},

and
a_{0}\in K
,
a_{2}\in K\setminus K^{2}
,
b_{2}\in K\setminus K^{2}(a_{2})
.
Here
\deg(\mathfrak{p})=4
,
\deg(\mathfrak{p}_{1})=2
,
\deg(\mathfrak{p}_{2})=1
, and
\kappa(\mathfrak{p})=K(a_{2}^{1/2},b_{2}^{1/2})
,
\kappa(\mathfrak{p}_{1})=K(a_{2}^{1/2})
,
\kappa(\mathfrak{p}_{2})=K
.

(3)

F|K=K(x,z)|K
, where

z^{4}=a_{0}+x+a_{2}x^{2},

and
a_{0}\in K
,
a_{2}\in K\setminus K^{2}
.
Here
\deg(\mathfrak{p})=\deg(\mathfrak{p}_{1})=2
,
\deg(\mathfrak{p}_{2})=1
, and
\kappa(\mathfrak{p})=\kappa(\mathfrak{p}_{1})=K(a_{2}^{1/2})
,
\kappa(\mathfrak{p}_{2})=K
.

In each case the singular prime
\mathfrak{p}
is the only pole of the function
x
.

Before getting to the proof,
let us note that although the standing assumption in
[
1
]
is that the base field
K
of
F|K
is separably closed,
all we need in order to run the Bedoya-Stöhr algorithm is that the restriction
\mathfrak{p}_{n}
of the prime
\mathfrak{p}
in question is rational for some
n
.
This means that the prime
\mathfrak{p}
is non-decomposed, a condition that is automatic if
K
is separably closed (see
[
11
, Corollary 2.17]
).

Proof.

Let
F|K
be a quasi-elliptic function field and let
\mathfrak{p}
be its only singular prime.
By the preceding discussion, the first Frobenius pullback
F_{1}|K
has genus
g_{1}=0
and the restricted primes
\mathfrak{p}_{1}
and
\mathfrak{p}_{2}
are non-singular and rational respectively.

Assume first that
\mathfrak{p}_{1}
is rational.
Then the prime
\mathfrak{p}
is unramified over
F_{1}
, that is,
\deg(\mathfrak{p})=p\,\deg(\mathfrak{p}_{1})=2
.
Since the Frobenius pullback
F_{1}|K
has genus
g_{1}=0
and its prime
\mathfrak{p}_{1}
is rational, it is a rational function field, say
F_{1}|K=K(x)|K
with
v_{\mathfrak{p}_{1}}(x)=-1
.
By Riemann’s theorem, the space of global sections
H^{0}(\mathfrak{p}_{1}^{n})
of the divisor
\mathfrak{p}_{1}^{n}
has dimension
n+1
, and so

H^{0}(\mathfrak{p}_{1}^{n})=K\oplus Kx\oplus\dots\oplus Kx^{n}\ \text{ for all $n\geq 0$.}

Similarly, since the function field
F|K
has genus
g=1
and
\mathfrak{p}
has degree
2
we have

\dim H^{0}(\mathfrak{p}^{n})=2n\ \text{ for all $n\geq 1$.}

As
\dim H^{0}(\mathfrak{p}^{2})=4>\dim H^{0}(\mathfrak{p}_{1}^{2})=3
, there is a function
z\in F
such that

H^{0}(\mathfrak{p}^{2})=H^{0}(\mathfrak{p}_{1}^{2})\oplus Kz=K\oplus Kx\oplus Kx^{2}\oplus Kz.

This function does not belong to
F_{1}=K(x)
because
H^{0}(\mathfrak{p}^{2})\cap F_{1}=H^{0}(\mathfrak{p}_{1}^{2})
.
Equivalently
F=F_{1}(z)=K(x,z)
, which means that
z
is a separating variable of
F|K
,
i.e., the finite extension
F|K(z)
is separable.
Since the square
z^{2}
lies in
H^{0}(\mathfrak{p}^{4})\cap F_{1}=H^{0}(\mathfrak{p}_{1}^{4})
, there exist constants
a_{i}\in K
such that

z^{2}=a_{0}+a_{1}x+a_{2}x^{2}+a_{3}x^{3}+a_{4}x^{4}.

Note that one of the constants
a_{1},a_{3}
must be non-zero, for
x
is separable over
K(z)
.
Looking at

as a Laurent series in the local parameter

at
\mathfrak{p}_{1}
, we deduce from

[
1
, Proposition 4.1]
that the non-rationality of
\mathfrak{p}
together with

mean that

a_{3}=0
,
a_{1}\neq 0
and
a_{4}\notin K^{2}
,
in which case we may normalize
a_{1}=1
by substituting
x
with
a_{1}x
and
z
with
a_{1}z
, respectively. This yields the normal form in
1
.

Conversely, since a singular prime satisfies the Jacobian criterion
[
20
, Corollaries 4.5 and 4.6]
, the normal form in question guarantees that a function field
F|K=K(x,z)|K
given as in
1
has a unique singular prime,
namely the pole
\mathfrak{p}
of
x
, which is inertial over
F_{1}=K(x)
and has geometric singularity degree
\delta(\mathfrak{p})=1
,
i.e.,
F|K
is quasi-elliptic.

We assume now that the non-singular prime
\mathfrak{p}_{1}
is non-rational.
Since
\mathfrak{p}_{2}
is rational the prime
\mathfrak{p}_{1}
is unramified over
F_{2}
and has
\deg(\mathfrak{p}_{1})=2
.
Let
e
denote the ramification index of
\mathfrak{p}
over
F_{1}
.
As the function field
F_{2}|K
has genus
g_{2}=0
and its prime
\mathfrak{p}_{2}
is rational, we infer as before that
F_{2}|K
takes the form
F_{2}|K=K(x)|K
with
v_{\mathfrak{p}_{2}}(x)=-1
, and that

H^{0}(\mathfrak{p}_{2}^{n})=K\oplus Kx\oplus\dots\oplus Kx^{n}\quad\text{for all $n\geq 0$.}

By Riemann’s theorem, since the function fields
F_{1}|K
and
F|K
have genera
g_{1}=0
and
g=1
, and the divisors
\mathfrak{p}_{1}
and
\mathfrak{p}^{e}
have degrees
2
and
4
, respectively, we deduce that

\displaystyle\dim H^{0}(\mathfrak{p}_{1}^{n})

\displaystyle=2n+1\quad\text{for all $n\geq 0$,}

\displaystyle\dim H^{0}(\mathfrak{p}^{ne})

\displaystyle=4n\quad\text{for all $n\geq 1$.}

As
\dim H^{0}(\mathfrak{p}_{1})=3>\dim H^{0}(\mathfrak{p}_{2})=2
, we can find a function
w\in F_{1}
such that

H^{0}(\mathfrak{p}_{1})=H^{0}(\mathfrak{p}_{2})\oplus Kw=K\oplus Kx\oplus Kw,

which does not belong to
F_{2}=K(x)
because
H^{0}(\mathfrak{p}_{1})\cap F_{2}=H^{0}(\mathfrak{p}_{2})
. Thus
w
is a separating variable of
F_{1}|K
, or equivalently
F_{1}=F_{2}(w)=K(x,w)
. Since
w^{2}
lies in
H^{0}(\mathfrak{p}_{1}^{2})\cap F_{2}=H^{0}(\mathfrak{p}_{2}^{2})
, there exist constants
a_{i}\in K
such that

w^{2}=a_{0}+a_{1}x+a_{2}x^{2},

with
a_{1}\neq 0
because
x
is separable over
K(w)
.
Hence we can normalize

by replacing

with
a_{1}^{-1}x
.
By

, the fact that

is

a_{2}\notin K^{2}
,
thus yielding the following normal form of

F_{1}|K=K(x,w)|K,\quad\text{where}\quad w^{2}=a_{0}+x+a_{2}x^{2},\quad\text{ $a_{0}\in K,a_{2}\in K\setminus K^{2}$.}

Conversely, we observe that the quadratic equation already ensures that the function field

, and also that the pole

of

.

.
As
\dim H^{0}(\mathfrak{p}^{e})=4>\dim H^{0}(\mathfrak{p}_{1})=3

H^{0}(\mathfrak{p}^{e})=H^{0}(\mathfrak{p}_{1})\oplus Kz=K\oplus Kx\oplus Kw\oplus Kz,

which lies outside

.
In particular,

is a separating variable of

, i.e.,

.
Because

belongs to
H^{0}(\mathfrak{p}^{2e})\cap F_{1}=H^{0}(\mathfrak{p}_{1}^{2})=K\oplus Kx\oplus Kx^{2}\oplus Kw\oplus Kxw
,

One of the constants

We rephrase the fact that

has geometric singularity degree

in terms of equations on the constants

.
To do this we introduce the functions
{\breve{x}}:=x^{-1}\in F_{2}

In particular, for the residue classes

\breve{w}(\mathfrak{p})^{2}=a_{2}\notin K^{2},\qquad\breve{z}(\mathfrak{p})^{2}=b_{2}+b_{4}\breve{w}(\mathfrak{p}).

We claim that the condition

and

. Indeed, when
\breve{z}(\mathfrak{p})\notin\kappa({\mathfrak{p}_{1}})=K(\breve{w}(\mathfrak{p}))

is inertial over

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
F|K
be a one-dimensional separable function field of characteristic
p>0
.
Let
\mathfrak{p}
be a singular prime that is ramified over
F_{1}
, i.e.,
\kappa(\mathfrak{p})=\kappa(\mathfrak{p}_{1})
.
Then

\delta(\mathfrak{p})\geq p\,\delta(\mathfrak{p}_{1})+\tfrac{p-1}{2}\cdot\deg(\mathfrak{p}).

Note that as
\kappa(\mathfrak{p})=\kappa(\mathfrak{p}_{1})
we can replace
\deg(\mathfrak{p})
with
\deg(\mathfrak{p}_{1})
in the above inequality.

Proof.

Assume first that
\mathfrak{p}
is non-decomposed, i.e., the extension
\kappa(\mathfrak{p})|K
is purely inseparable, i.e., some restriction
\mathfrak{p}_{n}
is rational
[
11
, Corollary 2.17]
.
Then
\mathfrak{p}
is non-rational and
\deg(\mathfrak{p})>1
is a
p
-power.
Choose an integer
n>1
such that
\mathfrak{p}_{n}
is rational and let
z\in F
be a local parameter at
\mathfrak{p}
.
Since the function
z^{p^{n}}\in F_{n}
has order
p^{n}/e_{0}\cdots e_{n-1}=\deg(\mathfrak{p})
at
\mathfrak{p}_{n}
, where
e_{i}
denotes the ramification index of the extension
\mathfrak{p}_{i}|\mathfrak{p}_{i+1}
,
the order of the differential
dz^{p^{n}}
of
F_{n}|K
at
\mathfrak{p}_{n}
is at least
\deg(\mathfrak{p})
. Thus the claim follows from
[
1
, Theorem 2.3]
.

Now we drop the non-decomposedness assumption on
\mathfrak{p}
.
Let
\mathfrak{q}
be a (non-decomposed) prime of the extended function field
K^{sep}F|K^{sep}
that lies over
\mathfrak{p}
, where
K^{sep}
denotes the separable closure of
K
.
By
[
11
, Proposition 2.12]
we have
\delta(\mathfrak{p})=r\delta(\mathfrak{q})
,
\delta(\mathfrak{p}_{1})=r\delta(\mathfrak{q}_{1})
and
\deg(\mathfrak{p})=r\deg(\mathfrak{q})
, where

is the separability degree of the extension
\kappa(\mathfrak{p})|K
.
∎

4.
Non-hyperelliptic regular non-smooth curves

Our next objective is to obtain the projective regular models of the function fields in the preceding section.
We are particularly interested in those function fields which are non-hyperelliptic.
Recall that the
canonical field
of a one-dimensional function field
F|K
is the subfield generated by the quotients of the non-zero holomorphic differentials of
F|K
.
Equivalently, the canonical field of
F|K
is the subfield generated over
K
by the global sections of any canonical effective divisor.
A function field
F|K
of genus
g\geq 2
is called
hyperelliptic
if it admits a quadratic subfield of genus zero;
this subfield is equal to the canonical field and so it is uniquely determined.

Proposition 4.1
.

Every function field

in Theorem

1
is non-hyperelliptic.

Proof.

The canonical field is generated over
K
by
H^{0}(\mathfrak{p})=K\oplus Kx\oplus Ky
, as follows from Remark
3.4
.
∎

Proposition 4.2
.

Let

5.
Universal fibrations by rational quartics in characteristic two

The function fields in Theorem
4.3
give rise to fibrations by rational plane projective quartic curves in characteristic
2
, which we now investigate.
We start with the second item in the theorem.
Let
k
be an algebraically closed ground field of characteristic
2
.
Consider the smooth integral fivefold

\displaystyle Z_{2}

\displaystyle=\{\,((x:y:z),(a,b,c,d))\in\mathbb{P}^{2}\times\mathbb{A}^{4}\mid y^{4}+az^{4}+bx^{2}y^{2}+cx^{2}z^{2}+bx^{3}z+dx^{4}=0\,\}

inside
\mathbb{P}^{2}\times\mathbb{A}^{4}
.
The projection morphism

\pi_{2}:Z_{2}\longrightarrow\mathbb{A}^{4}

is proper and flat,
and it provides a
4
-dimensional family of plane projective quartic curves.

Let
B
be a closed subvariety of
\mathbb{A}^{4}
, and let

denote its affine coordinate functions.
By restricting the base of the fibration
\pi_{2}:Z_{2}\to\mathbb{A}^{4}

Clearly

satisfies the two conditions

and

2
if and only if

is not contained in the hyperplane

and the tangent spaces of

are not all contained in the hyperplanes

Note that the first condition

Assuming both conditions are satisfied, if we dehomogenize

then the field of rational functions on the total space

becomes

k(T)=k(B)(z,y),\,\,\,\text{where}\,\,\,y^{4}+az^{4}+by^{2}+cz^{2}+bz+d=0,

and

,

and

If we replace the ground field

of the base, then the higher dimensional function field

of the total space becomes a one-dimensional function field

and has been characterized in Theorem
4.3

2
.
When the base

is the line defined by the equations
b=a
,

and

[

By Corollary
4.4

2

(\bar{x},(a,b,c,d))\mapsto((1:\bar{y}:\bar{z}),(a,b,c,d)).

where
\bar{y}
and
\bar{z}
stand for the corresponding polynomial expressions in

whose coefficients are rational expressions in

,
b^{1/4}
,

and

.
However

is not a rational map.
To repair this we consider the third

of the variety

, and take the dominant rational map

\displaystyle\mathbb{A}^{1}\times B^{\prime}

\displaystyle\dashrightarrow T

\displaystyle(\bar{x},(a,b,c,d))

\displaystyle\mapsto\psi(\bar{x}^{8},(a^{8},b^{8},c^{8},d^{8})).

is uniruled (see
[

However, as the dominant rational map is inseparable, this does not assure that the Kodaira dimension of

is

(see
[

Let

We describe the fibres of

If

Assume next that

and furthermore it has no inflection points, since every tangent line at a non-singular point is a bitangent.

If

then the fibre
\pi_{2}^{-1}(P)
has the same properties as the fibres in the generic case, the only difference being that the multiplicity of the singular point is equal to

.

Now we build a fibration out of the function fields in Theorem

1
.
The variety

\displaystyle Z_{1}

\displaystyle=\{\,((x:y:z),(a,b,c))\in\mathbb{P}^{2}\times\mathbb{A}^{3}\mid y^{4}+az^{4}+xz^{3}+bx^{2}z^{2}+cx^{4}=0\,\}

then the fibre over the point

has the same properties as the geometric generic fibre described in the previous section.
More precisely, in this case the fibre is an integral plane projective rational quartic curve with a unique singular point of multiplicity

If

6.

Let

The only singular point on

The case of multiplicity three occurs if and only if

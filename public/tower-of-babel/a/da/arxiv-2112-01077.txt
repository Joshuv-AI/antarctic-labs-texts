Blind Super-Resolution of Point Sources via Projected Gradient Descent
Sihan Mao and Jinchi Chen

Abstract
Blind super-resolution can be cast as a low rank matrix recovery problem by exploiting the inherent simplicity of the signal and the low dimensional structure of point spread functions. In this paper, we develop a simple yet efficient non-convex projected gradient descent method for this problem based on the low rank structure of the vectorized Hankel matrix associated with the target matrix. Theoretical analysis indicates that the proposed method exactly converges to the target matrix with a linear convergence rate under the similar conditions as convex approaches. Numerical results show that our approach is competitive with existing convex approaches in terms of recovery ability and efficiency.



1
Introduction

Blind super-resolution is the problem of estimating high-resolution information of a signal from its low-resolution measurements when the point spread functions (PSFs) are unknown. Such problem arises in a wide variety of applications, including seismic data analysis
[
25
]
, nuclear magnetic resonance spectroscopy
[
27
]
, multi-user communication system
[
23
]
, and 3D single-molecule microscopy
[
28
]
. In particular, when the knowledge of PSFs is available, blind super-resolution reduces to the super-resolution problem
[
7
,
8
]
.

Without any additional assumptions, blind super-resolution of point sources is an ill-posed problem. To alleviate this issue, it is common to assume that the PSFs belong to a known low-dimensional subspace. Under this assumption and utilizing the lift technique, blind super-resolution of point sources can be formulated as a matrix recovery problem. By exploiting low dimensional structures of the target matrix, a series of works
[
15
,
37
,
21
,
12
,
31
]
theoretically studied under which conditions the target matrix can be recovered. The author in
[
15
]
considered the setting where the PSF is shared among all point sources, and established the recovery guarantees for the atomic norm minimization (ANM) method. Yang et al.
[
37
]
further studied the same method, but with multiple unknown PSFs. Li et al.
[
21
]
provided robust analysis of blind 1D super-resolution and later the work in
[
31
]
generalized
[
21
]
to 2D case. Recently, Chen et al.
[
12
]
proposed a nuclear norm minimization method based on the vectorized Hankel lift framework, which also appears in
[
38
,
40
]
but for matrix completion. Moreover,
[
12
]
established the corresponding exact recovery guarantees. While strong theoretical guarantees have been built for blind super-resolution based on convex methods, these approaches are computational inefficient for the high dimensional setting. Therefore it is necessary to design efficient and provable algorithms to deal with the large-scale regime.

In the past few years, substantial progress has been made on designing and analyzing provable fast algorithms for applications from science and engineering via non-convex optimization
[
20
,
13
,
16
]
, including matrix completion
[
14
,
42
]
, phase retrieval
[
29
]
, blind deconvolution
[
22
]
, spectrally sparse signal recovery
[
5
,
6
]
, to name just a few. The goal of this work is to develop an efficient non-convex algorithm for blind super-resolution problem.

1.1
Comparisons with Related Work and Main Contributions

Our work is closely related to
[
12
,
5
,
43
]
.
As already mentioned,

[
12
]
proposed a convex approach called Vectorized Hankel Lift (VHL) for blind super-resolution.
Based on this framework, we develop an efficient and provable non-convex algorithm for blind super-resolution of point sources.
More precisely, we parameter the vectorized Hankel matrix corresponding to a candidate solution in terms of the Burer–Monteiro factorization and develop a projected gradient descent method to directly recover the low-rank factors.

Our algorithm is inspired by the method in
[
5
]
, where the projected gradient descent was developed for spectrally sparse signal recovery problem based on the low rank structure of the Hankel matrix corresponding to the target signal. Despite this,
both the structures of sensing operator and target matrix in this paper are substantially different from that in
[
5
]
. Therefore, the convergence analysis in
[
5
]
can not be easily extended to our model.

Recently,
[
43
]
follows our work and develops an iterative hard thresholding method based on the framework of vectorzied Hankel lift to solve blind super-resolution problem. It is worth pointing out that
they directly apply our result to bound the initialization error of their method.
Furthermore, their proof idea is inspired by the guarantee analysis of low rank matrix recovery over Riemannian manifold
[
6
,
36
]
. Therefore the proof techniques are totally different with ours.

The main contributions of this work are summerized as follows. Firstly, we present a new non-convex algorithm called projected gradient descent via vectorized Hankel lift (PGD–VHL) for blind super-resolution. Numerical experiments show that PGD–VHL is competitive with convex recovery methods such as ANM and VHL in terms of recovery ability, but is much more efficient. Secondly, we establish the recovery performance of PGD–VHL. Our results show that PGD–VHL started from a spectral initialization converges linearly to the target matrix under the similar sample complexity as convex approaches. Lastly, it is worth mentioning that the theoretical guarantee of PGD–VHL requires a slightly milder assumption on the low-dimensional subspace than that for VHL in
[
12
]
.

\displaystyle{\cal D}^{2}(\bm{X})=\begin{bmatrix}w_{0}\bm{x}_{0}&\cdots&w_{n-1}\bm{x}_{n-1}\end{bmatrix},

\displaystyle w_{i}=\#\{(j,k)|j+k=i,0\leq j\leq n_{1}-1,0\leq k\leq n_{2}-1\}.

{\cal G}^{\ast}={\cal D}^{-1}{\cal H}^{\ast}
. Additionally,
{\cal G}

\displaystyle\bm{G}_{i}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{w_{i}}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}j+k=i\\

\displaystyle{\cal G}(\bm{X})=\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}{\cal G}\left(\bm{x}_{i}\bm{e}_{i}^{\mathsf{T}}\right)=\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}\bm{G}_{i}\otimes\bm{x}_{i},

(1.4)

where
\bm{G}_{i}\otimes\bm{x}_{i}

\bm{G}_{i}

\bm{x}_{i}
.

2
Problem formulation

The point source signal model can be represented as a superposition of
r
spikes

\displaystyle x(t)=\sum_{k=1}^{r}d_{k}\delta(t-\tau_{k}),

(2.1)

where
\delta(\cdot)
is the Dirac function,
d_{k}\in\mathbb{C}
and
\tau_{k}\in[0,1)
are the amplitude and location of the
k
-th point source, respectively. Let
\{g_{k}(t)\}_{k=1}^{r}
be the unknown point spread functions depending on the locations of point sources. The observation is a convolution between
x(t)
and
\{g_{k}(t)\}_{k=1}^{r}
, that is,

\displaystyle y(t)=\sum_{k=1}^{r}d_{k}\delta(t-\tau_{k})*g_{k}(t)=\sum_{k=1}^{r}d_{k}\cdot g_{k}(t-\tau_{k}).

(2.2)

After taking the Fourier transform and sampling, we obtain the measurements as

\displaystyle\bm{y}[j]=\sum_{k=1}^{r}d_{k}e^{-2\pi\imath\tau_{k}\cdot j}\hat{g}_{k}[j],\quad j=0,\cdots n-1.

(2.3)

Let
\bm{g}_{k}=\begin{bmatrix}\hat{g}_{k}[0]&\cdots&\hat{g}_{k}[n-1]\end{bmatrix}^{\mathsf{T}}
be a vector corresponding to the
k
-th unknown point spread function. The goal is to estimate
\{d_{k},\tau_{k}\}_{k=1}^{r}
as well as
\{\bm{g}_{k}\}_{k=1}^{r}
from (
2.3
).

Obviously, the problem of blind super-resolution is ill-posed without any additional assumptions, because the number of unknowns in (
2.3
) is
{\cal O}(nr)
, which is larger than the number of samples
n
. To tackle this issue, we follow the same route as that in
[
1
,
15
,
37
,
12
]
and assume that all the Fourier samples of the unknown PSFs
\{\bm{g}_{k}\}_{k=1}^{r}
belong to a known low-dimensional subspace spanned by the columns of
\bm{B}\in\mathbb{C}^{n\times s}
with
s<n
, i.e.,

\displaystyle\bm{g}_{k}=\bm{B}\bm{h}_{k},

(2.4)

where
\bm{h}_{k}\in\mathbb{C}^{s}
denotes the unknown directional vector of
\bm{g}_{k}
in the subspace. According to the subspace assumption (
2.4
) and using the lift trick
[
1
]
, we can easily rewrite (
2.3
) as a set of linear measurements with respect to the target matrix
\bm{X}^{\natural}=\sum_{k=1}^{r}d_{k}\bm{h}_{k}\bm{a}_{\tau_{k}}^{\mathsf{T}}
,

\displaystyle\bm{y}[j]=\left\langle\bm{b}_{j}\bm{e}_{j}^{\mathsf{T}},\bm{X}^{\natural}\right\rangle,\quad j=0,\cdots,n-1,

(2.5)

where
\bm{a}_{\tau_{k}}=\left[1,e^{-2\pi\iota\tau_{k}},\cdots,e^{-2\pi\iota\tau_{k}\cdot(n-1)}\right]^{\mathsf{T}}
,
\bm{b}_{j}\in\mathbb{C}^{s}
is the
j
th column vector of
\bm{B}^{\mathsf{H}}
,
\bm{e}_{j}
is the
j
-th standard basis of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
.
The measurement model (
2.5
) can be rewritten succinctly as

\displaystyle\bm{y}={\cal A}(\bm{X}^{\natural}),

(2.6)

where
{\cal A}:\mathbb{C}^{s\times n}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}^{n}
is the linear operator. Let
{\cal A}^{\ast}
be the adjoint operator of
{\cal A}
which is given by
{\cal A}^{\ast}(\bm{y})=\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}\bm{y}[j]\bm{b}_{j}\bm{e}_{j}^{\mathsf{T}}
. Furthermore, define
\bm{D}=\diag\left(\sqrt{w_{0}},\cdots,\sqrt{w_{n-1}}\right)
. We have
\bm{D}{\cal A}(\bm{X})={\cal A}{\cal D}(\bm{X})
for any
\bm{X}
. The measurements can be reformulated as

\displaystyle\bm{D}\bm{y}={\cal A}{\cal D}(\bm{X}^{\natural}).

(2.7)

Note that once the data matrix
\bm{X}^{\natural}
is reconstructed, the frequencies
\{\tau_{k}\}_{k=1}^{r}
can be retrieved through spatial smoothing MUSIC
[
18
,
17
,
39
,
12
]
, and the amplitudes
\{d_{k}\}_{k=1}^{r}
and coefficients
\{\bm{h}_{k}\}_{k=1}^{r}
can be estimated by solving an over-determined linear system. Therefore in this work we focus on the problem of recovering
\bm{X}^{\natural}
from its linear measurements (
2.6
).

It has been shown that
{\cal H}(\bm{X}^{\natural})
is a rank-
r
matrix
[
12
]
and thus the matrix
{\cal H}(\bm{X}^{\natural})
admits low rank structure when
r\ll\min(sn_{1},n_{2})
. Equipped with the low rank structure of
{\cal H}(\bm{X}^{\natural})
, it is natural to recover
\bm{X}^{\natural}
by solving the constrained least squares problem

\displaystyle\min_{\bm{X}}~\frac{1}{2}\left\|\bm{D}\bm{y}-{\cal A}{\cal D}(\bm{X})\right\|_{{\footnotesize{\mbox{2}}}}^{2}~\text{ s.t. }\rank({\cal H}(\bm{X}))=r.

(2.8)

Letting
\bm{Z}={\cal H}(\bm{X})={\cal G}{\cal D}(\bm{X})
for any
\bm{X}
, it can be verified that
({\cal I}-{\cal G}{\cal G}^{\ast})(\bm{Z})=\bm{0}
. To eliminate the rank constrain in (
2.8
), we apply the Burer–Monteiro factorization
[
4
]
to parameterize
\bm{Z}
as
\bm{Z}=\bm{L}\bm{R}^{\mathsf{H}}
, where
\bm{L}\in\mathbb{C}^{sn_{1}\times r}
and
\bm{R}\in\mathbb{C}^{n_{2}\times r}
are two rank-
r
matrices. Therefore, the optimization problem (
2.8
) can be rewritten as

\displaystyle\min_{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}{\bm{L},\bm{R}}}}~\frac{1}{2}\left\|\bm{D}\bm{y}-{\cal A}{\cal G}^{\ast}(\bm{L}\bm{R}^{\mathsf{H}})\right\|_{{\footnotesize{\mbox{2}}}}^{2}\text{ s.t. }({\cal I}-{\cal G}{\cal G}^{\ast})(\bm{L}\bm{R}^{\mathsf{H}})=\bm{0}.

(2.9)

Before introducing our algorithm, we make an assumption that
\bm{Z}^{\natural}={\cal H}(\bm{X}^{\natural})
is
\mu_{1}
-incoherent which is defined below.

Assumption 2.1
.

Let
\bm{Z}^{\natural}=\bm{U}\bm{\Sigma}\bm{V}^{\mathsf{H}}
be the singular value decomposition of
\bm{Z}^{\natural}
, where
\bm{U}\in\mathbb{C}^{sn_{1}\times r},\bm{\Sigma}\in\mathbb{R}^{r\times r}
and
\bm{V}\in\mathbb{C}^{n_{2}\times r}
. Denote
\bm{U}^{\mathsf{H}}=\begin{bmatrix}\bm{U}_{0}^{\mathsf{H}}&\cdots&\bm{U}_{n_{1}-1}^{\mathsf{H}}\end{bmatrix}^{\mathsf{H}}
, where
\bm{U}_{j}=\bm{U}[js:(j+1)s-1,:]
is the
j
-th block of
\bm{U}
for
j=0,\cdots n_{1}-1
. The matrix
\bm{Z}^{\natural}
is
\mu_{1}
-incoherent if
\bm{U}
and
\bm{V}
obey that

\displaystyle\max_{0\leq j\leq n_{1}-1}\left\|\bm{U}_{j}\right\|_{{\footnotesize{\mathsf{F}}}}^{2}\leq\frac{\mu_{1}r}{n}\text{ and }\max_{0\leq k\leq n_{2}-1}\left\|\bm{e}_{k}^{\mathsf{T}}\bm{V}\right\|_{2}^{2}\leq\frac{\mu_{1}r}{n}

for some positive constant
\mu_{1}
.

Remark 2.1
.

Assumption
2.1
is the same as the one made in
[
11
,
40
]
for low rank matrix recovery and is used in
[
12
]
for blind super-resolution. It has been established that Assumption
2.1
is obeyed when the minimum wrap-up distance between the locations of point sources is greater than about
2/n
.

Let
\mu
and
\sigma
be two numerical constants such
\mu_{1}\leq\mu
and
\sigma_{1}\leq\sigma
, and
{\cal M}
be a convex set defined as follows

\displaystyle{\cal M}=\bigg\{\begin{bmatrix}\bm{L}\\
\bm{R}\\
\end{bmatrix}~:~\max_{0\leq j\leq n_{1}-1}\left\|\bm{L}_{j}\right\|_{{\footnotesize{\mathsf{F}}}}\leq\sqrt{\frac{\mu r\sigma}{n}},\left\|\bm{R}\right\|_{{\footnotesize{\mbox{2,$\infty$}}}}\leq\sqrt{\frac{\mu r\sigma}{n}}\bigg\},

(2.10)

where
\bm{L}_{j}
is the
j
-th block of
\bm{L}
. Define

\displaystyle\bm{M}^{\natural}=\begin{bmatrix}\bm{L}^{\natural}\\
\bm{R}^{\natural}\\
\end{bmatrix}=\begin{bmatrix}\bm{U}\bm{\Sigma}^{1/2}\\
\bm{V}\bm{\Sigma}^{1/2}\end{bmatrix}.

Since
\bm{Z}^{\natural}
is
\mu_{1}
-incoherent, we have
\bm{M}^{\natural}\in{\cal M}
. Therefore, we consider a penalized version of (
2.9
) for recovering the factorized matrices:

\displaystyle\min_{\bm{M}\in{\cal M}}~\bigg\{f(\bm{M}):=\frac{1}{2}\left\|\bm{D}\bm{y}-{\cal A}{\cal G}^{\ast}(\bm{L}\bm{R}^{\mathsf{H}})\right\|_{2}^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\left\|\left({\cal I}-{\cal G}{\cal G}^{\ast}\right)(\bm{L}\bm{R}^{\mathsf{H}})\right\|_{{\footnotesize{\mathsf{F}}}}^{2}+\frac{1}{16}\left\|\bm{L}^{\mathsf{H}}\bm{L}-\bm{R}^{\mathsf{H}}\bm{R}\right\|_{{\footnotesize{\mathsf{F}}}}^{2}\bigg\},

(2.11)

where
\bm{M}=\begin{bmatrix}\bm{L}^{\mathsf{H}}&\bm{R}^{\mathsf{H}}\end{bmatrix}^{\mathsf{H}}\in\mathbb{C}^{(sn_{1}+n_{2})\times r}
, and the last term penalizes the mismatch between
\bm{L}
and
\bm{R}
, which is widely used in rectangular low rank matrix recovery
[
34
,
42
,
16
]
.

3
Algorithm: projected gradient descent

Inspired by
[
5
]
, we design a projected gradient descent method for the problem (
2.11
), which is summarized in Algorithm
1
.

Algorithm 1

PGD–VHL

Input:
{\cal A},\bm{y},n,s,r

Initialization:

~\quad\widehat{\bm{Z}}_{0}=\widehat{\bm{U}}_{0}\widehat{\bm{\Sigma}}_{0}{\widehat{\bm{V}}_{0}}^{\mathsf{H}}={\cal P}_{r}{\cal H}{\cal A}^{\ast}(\bm{y})

~\quad\widehat{\bm{L}}_{0}=\widehat{\bm{U}}_{0}{\widehat{\bm{\Sigma}}_{0}}^{1/2},\quad\widehat{\bm{R}}_{0}=\widehat{\bm{V}}_{0}{\widehat{\bm{\Sigma}}_{0}}^{1/2}

~\quad\widehat{\bm{M}}_{0}=\begin{bmatrix}\widehat{\bm{L}}_{0}^{\mathsf{H}}&\widehat{\bm{R}}^{\mathsf{H}}_{0}\end{bmatrix}^{\mathsf{H}}

~\quad(\bm{L}_{0},\bm{R}_{0})={\cal P}_{{\cal M}}((\widehat{\bm{L}}_{0},\widehat{\bm{R}}_{0}))

~\quad\bm{M}_{0}=\begin{bmatrix}\bm{L}_{0}^{\mathsf{H}}&\bm{R}^{\mathsf{H}}_{0}\end{bmatrix}^{\mathsf{H}}

while
not convergence
do

\bm{M}_{t+1}={\cal P}_{{\cal M}}\left(\bm{M}_{t}-\eta\nabla f(\bm{M}_{t})\right)
.

end

while

The initialization involves two steps: (1) computes the best rank
r
approximation of
{\cal H}{\cal A}^{\ast}(\bm{y})
via one step hard thresholding
{\cal P}_{r}(\cdot)
, where
{\cal A}^{\ast}
is the adjoint of
{\cal A}
and
{\cal P}_{r}(\bm{Z})
is the best rank
r
approximation of
\bm{Z}
; (2) projects the low-rank factors of best rank-
r
approximated matrix onto the convex feasible set
{\cal M}
.
Given a matrix
\bm{M}=\begin{bmatrix}\bm{L}^{\mathsf{H}}&\bm{R}^{\mathsf{H}}\end{bmatrix}^{\mathsf{H}}
, the projection onto
{\cal M}
, denoted by
\begin{bmatrix}\widehat{\bm{L}}^{\mathsf{H}}&\widehat{\bm{R}}^{\mathsf{H}}\end{bmatrix}^{\mathsf{H}}
, has a closed form solution:

\displaystyle\widehat{\bm{L}}_{j}=\begin{cases}\bm{L}_{j}&\text{ if }\left\|\bm{L}_{j}\right\|_{{\footnotesize{\mathsf{F}}}}\leq\sqrt{\frac{\mu r\sigma}{n}}\\
\frac{1}{\left\|\bm{L}_{j}\right\|_{{\footnotesize{\mathsf{F}}}}}\bm{L}_{j}\cdot\sqrt{\frac{\mu r\sigma}{n}}&\text{ otherwise}\end{cases}

for
0\leq j\leq n_{1}-1
and

\displaystyle\bm{e}_{k}^{\mathsf{T}}\widehat{\bm{R}}=\begin{cases}\bm{e}_{k}^{\mathsf{T}}\bm{R}&\text{ if }\left\|\bm{e}_{k}^{\mathsf{T}}\bm{R}\right\|_{{\footnotesize{\mbox{2}}}}\leq\sqrt{\frac{\mu r\sigma}{n}}\\
\frac{\bm{e}_{k}^{\mathsf{T}}\bm{R}}{\left\|\bm{e}_{k}^{\mathsf{T}}\bm{R}\right\|_{{\footnotesize{\mbox{2}}}}}\cdot\sqrt{\frac{\mu r\sigma}{n}}&\text{ otherwise}\end{cases}

for
0\leq k\leq n_{2}-1
.
Let
\bm{M}_{t}
be the current estimator. The algorithm updates
\bm{M}_{t}
along gradient descent direction
-\nabla f(\bm{M}_{t})
with step size
\eta
, followed by projection onto the set
{\cal M}
.
The gradient of
f(\bm{M})
is computed with respect to Wirtinger calculus given by
\nabla f=\begin{bmatrix}\nabla^{\mathsf{H}}_{\bm{L}}f&\nabla^{\mathsf{H}}_{\bm{R}}f\end{bmatrix}^{\mathsf{H}}

where

\displaystyle\nabla_{\bm{L}}f=

\displaystyle\left({\cal G}{\cal A}^{\ast}\left({\cal A}{\cal G}^{\ast}(\bm{L}\bm{R}^{\mathsf{H}})-\bm{D}\bm{y}\right)\right)\bm{R}+\left(({\cal I}-{\cal G}{\cal G}^{\ast})(\bm{L}\bm{R}^{\mathsf{H}})\right)\bm{R}+\frac{1}{4}\bm{L}(\bm{L}^{\mathsf{H}}\bm{L}-\bm{R}^{\mathsf{H}}\bm{R}),

\displaystyle\nabla_{\bm{R}}f=

\displaystyle\left({\cal G}{\cal A}^{\ast}\left({\cal A}{\cal G}^{\ast}(\bm{L}\bm{R}^{\mathsf{H}})-\bm{D}\bm{y}\right)\right)^{\mathsf{H}}\bm{L}+\left(({\cal I}-{\cal G}{\cal G}^{\ast})(\bm{L}\bm{R}^{\mathsf{H}})\right)^{\mathsf{H}}\bm{L}+\frac{1}{4}\bm{R}(\bm{R}^{\mathsf{H}}\bm{R}-\bm{L}^{\mathsf{H}}\bm{L}).

Indeed PGD–VHL algorithm can be efficiently implemented. To obtain the computational cost of
\nabla f
, we first introduce some notations. Let
{\cal H}_{v}
be the Hankel operator which maps a vector
\bm{x}\in\mathbb{C}^{n}
into an
n_{1}\times n_{2}
matrix,

\displaystyle{\cal H}_{v}(\bm{x})=\begin{bmatrix}x_{0}&\cdots&x_{n_{2}-1}\\
\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
x_{n_{1}-1}&\cdots&x_{n-1}\\
\end{bmatrix},

where
x_{i}
is the
i
-th entry of
\bm{x}
. The adjoint of
{\cal H}_{v}
, denoted by
{\cal H}_{v}^{\ast}
, is a linear mapping from
n_{1}\times n_{2}
to
n
. It can be seen that
{\cal H}^{\ast}_{v}\left(\bm{L}_{v}\bm{R}_{v}^{\mathsf{H}}\right)
can be computed via
r
fast convolutions by noting that

\displaystyle\left[{\cal H}^{\ast}_{v}\left(\bm{L}_{v}\bm{R}_{v}^{\mathsf{H}}\right)\right]_{i}

\displaystyle=\left[{\cal H}^{\ast}_{v}\left(\sum_{s=1}^{r}\bm{L}_{v}[:,s]\overline{\bm{R}_{v}}[:,s]^{\mathsf{T}}\right)\right]_{i}

\displaystyle=\sum_{s=1}^{r}\left[{\cal H}^{\ast}_{v}\left(\bm{L}_{j}[:,s]\bar{\bm{R}}[:,s]^{\mathsf{T}}\right)\right]_{i}

\displaystyle=\sum_{s=1}^{r}\left(\bm{L}_{v}[:,s]\ast\overline{\bm{R}_{v}}[:,s]\right)[i],

where
\bm{L}_{v}\in\mathbb{C}^{n_{1}\times r}
and
\bm{R}_{v}\in\mathbb{C}^{n_{2}\times r}
.
In addition, we can compute
\left({\cal H}_{v}(\bm{x})\right)\bm{R}_{v}
by
r
fast Hankel matrix–vector multiplications, that is,

\displaystyle\left({\cal H}_{v}(\bm{x})\bm{R}_{v}\right)[j,s]

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{n_{2}-1}\bm{x}[j+k]\bm{R}_{v}[k,s]

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{n_{2}-1}\tilde{\bm{x}}[n-1-j-k]\bm{R}_{v}[k,s]

\displaystyle=(\tilde{\bm{x}}\ast\bm{R}_{v}[:,s])[n-1-j],

where
\tilde{\bm{x}}
is a vector reversing the order of
\bm{x}
.
Therefore the computational complexity of both
{\cal H}_{v}^{\ast}(\bm{L}_{v}\bm{R}_{v}^{\mathsf{H}})
and
\left({\cal H}_{v}(\bm{x})\right)\bm{R}_{v}
is
{\cal O}(rn\log n)
flops. Moreover, the authors in
[
12
]
show that
{\cal H}(\bm{X})=\bm{P}\widetilde{{\cal H}}(\bm{X})
, where
\widetilde{{\cal H}}(\bm{X})
is a matrix constructed by stacking all
\{{\cal H}_{v}(\bm{e}_{\ell}^{\mathsf{T}}\bm{X})\}_{\ell=1}^{s}
on top of one another, and
\bm{P}
is a permutation matrix. Therefore we can compute
{\cal G}^{\ast}(\bm{L}\bm{R}^{\mathsf{H}})
and
{\cal G}{\cal D}(\bm{X})\bm{R}
by using
{\cal O}(srn\log n)
flops. Thus the implementation of our algorithm is very efficient and the main computational complexity in each step is
{\cal O}(sr^{2}n+srn\log n)
.

In this section, we provide a theoretical analysis of PGD–VHL under a random subspace model.

Assumption 4.1
.

The column vectors

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\bm{b}_{i}\bm{b}_{i}^{\mathsf{H}}\rule{0.0pt}{8.53581pt}\right]

\displaystyle=\bm{I}_{s},\quad i=0,\cdots,n-1,

(4.1)

\displaystyle\max_{0\leq\ell\leq s-1}|\bm{b}_{i}[\ell]|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\mu_{0},\quad i=0,\cdots,n-1.

(4.2)

Remark 4.1

Now we present the main result, whose proofs are deferred to Section

.

Compared with the sample complexity established in

Remark 4.4
.

Theorem

implies that PGD–VHL converges to

\bm{y}_{m}[n]

\bm{y}_{m}\in\mathbb{C}^{N}

\displaystyle\bm{y}[j]=\left\langle\bm{b}_{j}\bm{e}_{j}^{\mathsf{T}},\sum_{k=1}^{r}d_{k}\bm{h}(\bm{a}_{\psi_{k}}\otimes\bm{a}_{\phi_{k}})^{\mathsf{T}}\right\rangle

Proof of Theorem
4.1

The proof follows a well established route that has been widely used in non-convex optimization for low rank matrix recovery
[
9
,
42
,
5
]
. In a nutshell, the initialization provided in Algorithm
1
will be shown to lie in a basin of attraction where the sequence returned by Algorithm
1
converges linearly to the true solution. Despite this, the proof details are quite involved and substantially different.
We first list two useful lemmas, whose proofs are deferred to Section
6.1
and
6.2
.

Lemma 6.1
.

Suppose
\bm{Z}^{\natural}
is
\mu_{1}
-incoherent and
n\geq c_{0}\varepsilon^{-2}\kappa^{2}\mu_{0}^{2}\mu s^{2}r^{2}\log^{2}(sn)
. Then one has

\displaystyle\dist^{2}(\bm{M}_{0},\bm{M}^{\natural})\leq\frac{\varepsilon^{2}\sigma_{r}}{\mu_{0}s}

with probability at least
1-(sn)^{-c_{1}}
.

Lemma 6.2
.

Let
\{\bm{M}_{t}\}
be the sequence returned by Algorithm
1
. Denote
\bm{\Delta}_{t}:=\bm{M}_{t}-\bm{M}^{\natural}\bm{Q}_{t}
, where

\bm{Q}_{t}=\arg\min\limits_{\bm{Q}\bm{Q}^{\mathsf{H}}=\bm{Q}^{\mathsf{H}}\bm{Q}=\bm{I}_{r}}\left\|\bm{M}_{t}-\bm{M}^{\natural}\bm{Q}\right\|_{{\footnotesize{\mathsf{F}}}}.

Let
\eta\leq\frac{\sigma_{r}}{9000(\mu_{0}\mu sr\sigma_{1})^{2}}
,
\beta=\frac{\sigma_{r}}{72}
. Then with probability
1-(sn)^{-c}
, one has

\displaystyle\dist^{2}(\bm{M}_{t},\bm{M}^{\natural})\leq(1-\eta\beta)^{t}\dist^{2}(\bm{M}_{0},\bm{M}^{\natural})

for
t=1,2,\cdots
.

Combining Lemma
6.1
and Lemma
6.2
together, we complete the proof.

6.1
Proof of Lemma
6.1

We begin our presentation of the proof with a useful lemma whose proof is provided in Section
6.3
.

Lemma 6.3
.

Suppose that
\bm{Z}^{\natural}
is
\mu_{1}
-incoherent. Then with probability at least
1-(sn)^{-c_{1}}
, the matrix
\widehat{\bm{Z}}_{0}={\cal P}_{r}({\cal G}{\cal D}{\cal A}^{\ast}(\bm{y}))
obeys

\displaystyle\left\|\widehat{\bm{Z}}_{0}-\bm{Z}^{\natural}\right\|\leq c_{0}\sigma_{1}\sqrt{\frac{\mu_{0}\mu_{1}sr\log^{2}(sn)}{n}},

(6.1)

where
c_{0}
and
c_{1}
are absolute constants.

By Lemma
6.3
and the assumption that
n
should be larger than
c_{0}\varepsilon^{-2}\kappa^{2}\mu_{0}^{2}\mu s^{2}r^{2}\log^{2}(sn)
, the event

\displaystyle\left\|\widehat{\bm{Z}}_{0}-\bm{Z}^{\natural}\right\|\leq c_{1}\sqrt{\frac{\mu_{0}\mu_{1}sr\log^{2}(sn)}{n}}\sigma_{1}(\bm{Z}^{\natural})\leq\varepsilon\sigma_{1}(\bm{Z}^{\natural})

occurs with probability at least

\displaystyle\left\|{\cal A}{\cal G}^{\ast}\left(\bm{\Phi}\right)\right\|_{{\footnotesize{\mbox{2}}}}^{2}+2\left\|{\cal A}{\cal G}^{\ast}\left(\bm{\Psi}\right)\right\|_{{\footnotesize{\mbox{2}}}}^{2}-3\left|\left\langle{\cal A}{\cal G}^{\ast}\left(\bm{\Phi}\right),{\cal A}{\cal G}^{\ast}\left(\bm{\Psi}\right)\right\rangle\right|

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\left\|{\cal A}{\cal G}^{\ast}\left(\bm{\Phi}\right)\right\|_{{\footnotesize{\mbox{2}}}}^{2}+2\left\|{\cal A}{\cal G}^{\ast}\left(\bm{\Psi}\right)\right\|_{{\footnotesize{\mbox{2}}}}^{2}-3\left\|{\cal A}{\cal G}^{\ast}\left(\bm{\Phi}\right)\right\|_{{\footnotesize{\mbox{2}}}}\cdot\left\|{\cal A}{\cal G}^{\ast}\left(\bm{\Psi}\right)\right\|_{{\footnotesize{\mbox{2}}}}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle~\frac{3}{4}\left\|{\cal A}{\cal G}^{\ast}\left(\bm{\Phi}\right)\right\|_{{\footnotesize{\mbox{2}}}}^{2}-7\left\|{\cal A}{\cal G}^{\ast}\left(\bm{\Psi}\right)\right\|_{{\footnotesize{\mbox{2}}}}^{2}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle~\frac{3}{4}\left\|{\cal A}{\cal G}^{\ast}\left(\bm{\Phi}\right)\right\|_{{\footnotesize{\mbox{2}}}}^{2}-\frac{7\varepsilon^{2}}{4}\sigma_{r}\left\|\bm{\Delta}\right\|_{{\footnotesize{\mathsf{F}}}}^{2},

(6.10)

where the second line is due to
\left\langle\bm{x},\bm{x}\right\rangle=\left\|\bm{x}\right\|_{{\footnotesize{\mbox{2}}}}^{2}
and
\Re\left(\left\langle\bm{x},\bm{y}\right\rangle\right)=\Re\left(\left\langle\bm{y},\bm{x}\right\rangle\right)
for any vectors
\bm{x},\bm{y}\in\mathbb{C}^{n}
, the third line is due to
\Re\left(\left\langle\bm{x},\bm{y}\right\rangle\right)\leq|\left\langle\bm{x},\bm{y}\right\rangle|
, and the last second line is due to
a+2b-3\sqrt{ab}\geq\frac{3}{4}a-7b
for any
a,b\geq 0
and the last line is due to the fact
\left\|{\cal A}{\cal G}^{\ast}\left(\bm{\Psi}\right)\right\|_{{\footnotesize{\mbox{2}}}}^{2}\leq\frac{\varepsilon^{2}}{4}\sigma_{r}\left\|\bm{\Delta}\right\|_{{\footnotesize{\mathsf{F}}}}^{2}
. Moreover the fact used in the last line can be proved as follows:

\displaystyle\left\|{\cal A}{\cal G}^{\ast}\left(\bm{\Psi}\right)\right\|_{{\footnotesize{\mbox{2}}}}^{2}

\displaystyle=\left\|{\cal A}{\cal G}^{\ast}\left(\bm{\Delta}_{\bm{L}}\bm{\Delta}_{\bm{R}}^{\mathsf{H}}\right)\right\|_{{\footnotesize{\mbox{2}}}}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\left(\left\|{\cal A}\right\|\left\|{\cal G}^{\ast}\right\|\cdot\left\|\bm{\Delta}_{\bm{L}}\bm{\Delta}_{\bm{R}}^{\mathsf{H}}\right\|_{{\footnotesize{\mathsf{F}}}}\right)^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\mu_{0}s\cdot\frac{1}{4}\left(\left\|\bm{\Delta}_{\bm{L}}\right\|_{{\footnotesize{\mathsf{F}}}}^{2}+\left\|\bm{\Delta}_{\bm{R}}\right\|_{{\footnotesize{\mathsf{F}}}}^{2}\right)^{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{\mu_{0}s}{4}\left\|\bm{\Delta}\right\|_{{\footnotesize{\mathsf{F}}}}^{4}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\varepsilon^{2}}{4}\sigma_{r}\left\|\bm{\Delta}\right\|_{{\footnotesize{\mathsf{F}}}}^{2},

(6.11)

where the second line is due to
\left\|{\cal A}\right\|\leq\sqrt{\mu_{0}s}
,
\left\|{\cal G}^{\ast}\right\|\leq 1
and the last line is due to
\left\|\bm{\Delta}\right\|_{{\footnotesize{\mathsf{F}}}}^{2}\leq\frac{\varepsilon^{2}\sigma_{r}}{\mu_{0}s}
. Plugging (
6.10
) into (
6.7
) reveals that

\displaystyle\Re\left(\left\langle\nabla f_{1},\bm{\Delta}\right\rangle\right)\geq\frac{3}{4}\left\|{\cal A}{\cal G}^{\ast}\left(\bm{\Phi}\right)\right\|_{{\footnotesize{\mbox{2}}}}^{2}-\frac{7\varepsilon^{2}}{4}\sigma_{r}\left\|\bm{\Delta}\right\|_{{\footnotesize{\mathsf{F}}}}^{2}.

(6.12)

Bounding
\Re\left(\left\langle\nabla f_{2},\bm{\Delta}\right\rangle\right)

This term can be bounded as follows:

\displaystyle\Re\left(\left\langle\nabla f_{2},\bm{\Delta}\right\rangle\right)=

\displaystyle\Re\left(\left\langle({\cal I}-{\cal G}{\cal G}^{\ast})(\bm{L}\bm{R}^{\mathsf{H}}),\bm{\Delta}_{\bm{L}}\bm{R}^{\mathsf{H}}+\bm{L}\bm{\Delta}_{\bm{R}}^{\mathsf{H}}\right\rangle\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Re\left(\left\langle({\cal I}-{\cal G}{\cal G}^{\ast})(\bm{L}\bm{R}^{\mathsf{H}}-\bm{L}^{\natural}{\bm{R}^{\natural}}^{\mathsf{H}}),\bm{\Delta}_{\bm{L}}\bm{R}^{\mathsf{H}}+\bm{L}\bm{\Delta}_{\bm{R}}^{\mathsf{H}}\right\rangle\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Re\left(\left\langle({\cal I}-{\cal G}{\cal G}^{\ast})(\bm{\Phi}+\bm{\Psi}),(\bm{\Phi}+2\bm{\Psi})\right\rangle\right)

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\left\|\left({\cal I}-{\cal G}{\cal G}^{\ast}\right)\left(\bm{\Phi}\right)\right\|_{{\footnotesize{\mathsf{F}}}}^{2}+2\left\|\left({\cal I}-{\cal G}{\cal G}^{\ast}\right)(\bm{\Psi})\right\|_{{\footnotesize{\mathsf{F}}}}^{2}

\displaystyle\quad-3\left\|\left({\cal I}-{\cal G}{\cal G}^{\ast}\right)\left(\bm{\Phi}\right)\right\|_{{\footnotesize{\mathsf{F}}}}\cdot\left\|\left({\cal I}-{\cal G}{\cal G}^{\ast}\right)(\bm{\Psi})\right\|_{{\footnotesize{\mathsf{F}}}}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\frac{3}{4}\left\|\left({\cal I}-{\cal G}{\cal G}^{\ast}\right)\left(\bm{\Phi}\right)\right\|_{{\footnotesize{\mathsf{F}}}}^{2}-7\left\|\left({\cal I}-{\cal G}{\cal G}^{\ast}\right)\left(\bm{\Psi}\right)\right\|_{{\footnotesize{\mathsf{F}}}}^{2},

(6.13)

where the second line is due to
({\cal I}-{\cal G}{\cal G}^{\ast})(\bm{L}^{\natural}{\bm{R}^{\natural}}^{\mathsf{H}})=\bm{0}
and the last line follows from the fact that
a+2b-3\sqrt{ab}\geq\frac{3}{4}a-7b
for any
a,b\geq 0
. Combining (
6.12
) and (
6.13
) together, one has

\displaystyle\Re\left(\left\langle\nabla f_{1},\bm{\Delta}\right\rangle\right)+\Re\left(\left\langle\nabla f_{2},\bm{\Delta}\right\rangle\right)\geq

\displaystyle\frac{3}{4}\left\|{\cal A}{\cal G}^{\ast}\left(\bm{\Phi}\right)\right\|_{{\footnotesize{\mbox{2}}}}^{2}-\frac{7\varepsilon^{2}}{4}\sigma_{r}\left\|\bm{\Delta}\right\|_{{\footnotesize{\mathsf{F}}}}^{2}+\frac{3}{4}\left\|\left({\cal I}-{\cal G}{\cal G}^{\ast}\right)\left(\bm{\Phi}\right)\right\|_{{\footnotesize{\mathsf{F}}}}^{2}-7\left\|\left({\cal I}-{\cal G}{\cal G}^{\ast}\right)(\bm{\Psi})\right\|_{{\footnotesize{\mathsf{F}}}}^{2}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\frac{3}{4}\left(\left\|{\cal A}{\cal G}^{\ast}\left(\bm{\Phi}\right)\right\|_{{\footnotesize{\mbox{2}}}}^{2}+\left\|\left({\cal I}-{\cal G}{\cal G}^{\ast}\right)\left(\bm{\Phi}\right)\right\|_{{\footnotesize{\mathsf{F}}}}^{2}\right)-\frac{7\varepsilon^{2}}{4}\sigma_{r}\left\|\bm{\Delta}\right\|_{{\footnotesize{\mathsf{F}}}}^{2}-7\left\|\bm{\Psi}\right\|_{{\footnotesize{\mathsf{F}}}}^{2},

(6.14)

where the last line follows from the fact that
{\cal I}-{\cal G}{\cal G}^{\ast}
is a projection operator. Let
T
be the tangent space at
\bm{Z}^{\natural}
defined as follows

\displaystyle T:=\{\bm{U}\bm{J}^{\mathsf{H}}+\bm{K}\bm{V}^{\mathsf{H}}:\bm{J}\in\mathbb{C}^{n_{2}\times r},\bm{K}\in\mathbb{C}^{sn_{1}\times r}\}.

It can be seen that
\bm{\Phi}\in T
. Therefore a simple calculation yields that

\displaystyle\left\|{\cal A}{\cal G}^{\ast}\left(\bm{\Phi}\right)\right\|_{{\footnotesize{\mbox{2}}}}^{2}+\left\|\left({\cal I}-{\cal G}{\cal G}^{\ast}\right)\left(\bm{\Phi}\right)\right\|_{{\footnotesize{\mathsf{F}}}}^{2}=

\displaystyle\left\langle{\cal G}{\cal A}^{\ast}{\cal A}{\cal G}^{\ast}(\bm{\Phi}),\bm{\Phi}\right\rangle+\left\langle\bm{\Phi},\bm{\Phi}\right\rangle-\left\langle{\cal G}{\cal G}^{\ast}(\bm{\Phi}),\bm{\Phi}\right\rangle

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\left\|\bm{\Phi}\right\|_{{\footnotesize{\mathsf{F}}}}^{2}-\left|\left\langle{\cal G}\left({\cal I}-{\cal A}^{\ast}{\cal A}\right){\cal G}^{\ast}(\bm{\Phi}),\bm{\Phi}\right\rangle\right|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left\|\bm{\Phi}\right\|_{{\footnotesize{\mathsf{F}}}}^{2}-\left|\left\langle{\cal P}_{T}{\cal G}\left({\cal I}-{\cal A}^{\ast}{\cal A}\right){\cal G}^{\ast}{\cal P}_{T}(\bm{\Phi}),\bm{\Phi}\right\rangle\right|

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle(1-\varepsilon)\left\|\bm{\Phi}\right\|_{{\footnotesize{\mathsf{F}}}}^{2},

(6.15)

where the last line follows from Lemma
6.7
and the assumption on
n
. In addition, we have

\displaystyle\left\|\bm{\Psi}\right\|_{{\footnotesize{\mathsf{F}}}}^{2}\leq

\displaystyle\left(\left\|\bm{\Delta}_{\bm{L}}\right\|_{{\footnotesize{\mathsf{F}}}}\left\|\bm{\Delta}_{\bm{R}}\right\|_{{\footnotesize{\mathsf{F}}}}\right)^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{4}\left\|\bm{\Delta}\right\|_{{\footnotesize{\mathsf{F}}}}^{4}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{\varepsilon^{2}\sigma_{r}}{4}\left\|\bm{\Delta}\right\|_{{\footnotesize{\mathsf{F}}}}^{2},

(6.16)

where we use
\left\|\bm{\Delta}\right\|_{{\footnotesize{\mathsf{F}}}}^{2}\leq\frac{\varepsilon^{2}\sigma_{r}(\bm{Z}^{\natural})}{s\mu_{0}}\leq\varepsilon^{2}\sigma_{r}(\bm{Z}^{\natural})
.
Hence plugging (
6.15
) and (
6.16
) into (
6.14
) yields that

\displaystyle\Re\left(\left\langle\nabla f_{1},\bm{\Delta}\right\rangle\right)+\Re\left(\left\langle\nabla f_{2},\bm{\Delta}\right\rangle\right)\geq

\displaystyle\frac{3}{4}(1-\varepsilon)\left\|\bm{\Phi}\right\|_{{\footnotesize{\mathsf{F}}}}^{2}-\frac{7\varepsilon^{2}}{2}\sigma_{r}\left\|\bm{\Delta}\right\|_{{\footnotesize{\mathsf{F}}}}^{2}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\left\|\bm{\Phi}\right\|_{{\footnotesize{\mathsf{F}}}}^{2}-\frac{7\varepsilon^{2}}{2}\sigma_{r}\left\|\bm{\Delta}\right\|_{{\footnotesize{\mathsf{F}}}}^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\left(\left\|\bm{\Delta}_{\bm{L}}(\bm{R}^{\natural}\bm{Q})^{\mathsf{H}}\right\|_{{\footnotesize{\mathsf{F}}}}^{2}+\left\|\bm{L}^{\natural}\bm{Q}\bm{\Delta}_{\bm{R}}^{\mathsf{H}}\right\|_{{\footnotesize{\mathsf{F}}}}^{2}\right)-\frac{7\varepsilon^{2}}{2}\sigma_{r}\left\|\bm{\Delta}\right\|_{{\footnotesize{\mathsf{F}}}}^{2}

\displaystyle\quad+\Re\left(\left\langle\bm{\Delta}_{\bm{L}}(\bm{R}^{\natural}\bm{Q})^{\mathsf{H}},(\bm{L}^{\natural}\bm{Q})\bm{\Delta}_{\bm{R}}^{\mathsf{H}}\right\rangle\right)

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\left(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{7\varepsilon^{2}}{2}\right)\sigma_{r}\left\|\bm{\Delta}\right\|_{{\footnotesize{\mathsf{F}}}}^{2}+\Re\left(\left\langle\bm{\Delta}_{\bm{L}}^{\mathsf{H}}(\bm{L}^{\natural}\bm{Q}),(\bm{R}^{\natural}\bm{Q})^{\mathsf{H}}\bm{\Delta}_{\bm{R}}\right\rangle\right)

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{9}\sigma_{r}\left\|\bm{\Delta}\right\|_{{\footnotesize{\mathsf{F}}}}^{2}+\Re\left(\left\langle\bm{\Delta}_{\bm{L}}^{\mathsf{H}}(\bm{L}^{\natural}\bm{Q}),(\bm{R}^{\natural}\bm{Q})^{\mathsf{H}}\bm{\Delta}_{\bm{R}}\right\rangle\right),

(6.17)

where the last line is due to
\varepsilon\leq\frac{1}{3}
.

Bounding
\Re\left(\left\langle\nabla f_{3},\bm{\Delta}\right\rangle\right)

Denote

\bm{S}=\begin{bmatrix}\bm{I}_{sn_{1}}&\\
&-\bm{I}_{n_{2}}\\
\end{bmatrix}.

We can bound
\Re\left(\left\langle\nabla f_{3},\bm{\Delta}\right\rangle\right)
as follows:

\displaystyle 4\Re\left(\left\langle\nabla f_{3},\bm{\Delta}\right\rangle\right)=

\displaystyle\Re\left(\left\langle\bm{S}\bm{M}\bm{M}^{\mathsf{H}}\bm{S}\bm{M},\bm{\Delta}\right\rangle\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Re\left(\left\langle\bm{M}^{\mathsf{H}}\bm{S}\bm{M},\bm{M}^{\mathsf{H}}\bm{S}\bm{\Delta}\right\rangle\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Re\left(\left\langle(\bm{\Delta}+\bm{M}^{\natural}\bm{Q})^{\mathsf{H}}\bm{S}(\bm{\Delta}+\bm{M}^{\natural}\bm{Q}),(\bm{\Delta}+\bm{M}^{\natural}\bm{Q})^{\mathsf{H}}\bm{S}\bm{\Delta}\right\rangle\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left\|\bm{\Delta}^{\mathsf{H}}\bm{S}\bm{\Delta}\right\|_{{\footnotesize{\mathsf{F}}}}^{2}+\left\|(\bm{M}^{\natural}\bm{Q})^{\mathsf{H}}\bm{S}\bm{\Delta}\right\|_{{\footnotesize{\mathsf{F}}}}^{2}+3\Re\left(\left\langle(\bm{M}^{\natural}\bm{Q})^{\mathsf{H}}\bm{S}\bm{\Delta},\bm{\Delta}^{\mathsf{H}}\bm{S}\bm{\Delta}\right\rangle\right)+\Re\left(\left\langle(\bm{M}^{\natural}\bm{Q})^{\mathsf{H}}\bm{S}\bm{\Delta},\bm{\Delta}^{\mathsf{H}}\bm{S}(\bm{M}^{\natural}\bm{Q})\right\rangle\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\left\|(\bm{M}^{\natural}\bm{Q})^{\mathsf{H}}\bm{S}\bm{\Delta}\right\|_{{\footnotesize{\mathsf{F}}}}^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\left\|(\bm{M}^{\natural}\bm{Q})^{\mathsf{H}}\bm{S}\bm{\Delta}+3\bm{\Delta}^{\mathsf{H}}\bm{S}\bm{\Delta}\right\|_{{\footnotesize{\mathsf{F}}}}^{2}-\frac{7}{2}\left\|\bm{\Delta}^{\mathsf{H}}\bm{S}\bm{\Delta}\right\|_{{\footnotesize{\mathsf{F}}}}^{2}+\Re\left\langle(\bm{M}^{\natural}\bm{Q})^{\mathsf{H}}\bm{S}\bm{\Delta},\bm{\Delta}^{\mathsf{H}}\bm{S}(\bm{M}^{\natural}\bm{Q})\right\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\left\|(\bm{M}^{\natural}\bm{Q})^{\mathsf{H}}\bm{S}\bm{\Delta}\right\|_{{\footnotesize{\mathsf{F}}}}^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\left\|(\bm{M}^{\natural}\bm{Q})^{\mathsf{H}}\bm{S}\bm{\Delta}+3\bm{\Delta}^{\mathsf{H}}\bm{S}\bm{\Delta}\right\|_{{\footnotesize{\mathsf{F}}}}^{2}-\frac{7}{2}\left\|\bm{\Delta}^{\mathsf{H}}\bm{S}\bm{\Delta}\right\|_{{\footnotesize{\mathsf{F}}}}^{2}

\displaystyle\quad+\Re\left(\left\langle(\bm{M}^{\natural}\bm{Q})^{\mathsf{H}}\bm{\Delta},\bm{\Delta}^{\mathsf{H}}(\bm{M}^{\natural}\bm{Q})\right\rangle\right)-4\Re\left(\left\langle\bm{\Delta}_{\bm{L}}^{\mathsf{H}}(\bm{L}^{\natural}\bm{Q}),(\bm{R}^{\natural}\bm{Q})^{\mathsf{H}}\bm{\Delta}_{\bm{R}}\right\rangle\right)

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\left\|(\bm{M}^{\natural}\bm{Q})^{\mathsf{H}}\bm{S}\bm{\Delta}\right\|_{{\footnotesize{\mathsf{F}}}}^{2}-\frac{7}{2}\left\|\bm{\Delta}^{\mathsf{H}}\bm{S}\bm{\Delta}\right\|_{{\footnotesize{\mathsf{F}}}}^{2}-4\Re\left(\left\langle\bm{\Delta}_{\bm{L}}^{\mathsf{H}}(\bm{L}^{\natural}\bm{Q}),(\bm{R}^{\natural}\bm{Q})^{\mathsf{H}}\bm{\Delta}_{\bm{R}}\right\rangle\right).

(6.18)

The fourth equality is due to
(\bm{M}^{\natural}\bm{Q})^{\mathsf{H}}\bm{S}(\bm{M}^{\natural}\bm{Q})=\bm{0}
. The last equality follows from

\displaystyle\Re\left(\left\langle\bm{\Delta}_{\bm{L}}^{\mathsf{H}}(\bm{L}^{\natural}\bm{Q}),(\bm{R}^{\natural}\bm{Q})^{\mathsf{H}}\bm{\Delta}_{\bm{R}}\right\rangle\right)=

\displaystyle\Re\left(\left\langle(\bm{R}^{\natural}\bm{Q})^{\mathsf{H}}\bm{\Delta}_{\bm{R}},\bm{\Delta}_{\bm{L}}^{\mathsf{H}}(\bm{L}^{\natural}\bm{Q})\right\rangle\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Re\left(\left\langle(\bm{L}^{\natural}\bm{Q})^{\mathsf{H}}\bm{\Delta}_{\bm{L}},\bm{\Delta}_{\bm{R}}^{\mathsf{H}}(\bm{R}^{\natural}\bm{Q})\right\rangle\right).

Finally, combining (
6.18
) with (
6.17
) yields that

\displaystyle\Re\left(\left\langle\nabla f(\bm{M}),\bm{\Delta}\right\rangle\right)\geq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{9}\sigma_{r}\left\|\bm{\Delta}\right\|_{{\footnotesize{\mathsf{F}}}}^{2}+\frac{1}{8}\left\|{\bm{M}^{\natural}}^{\mathsf{H}}\bm{S}\bm{\Delta}\right\|_{{\footnotesize{\mathsf{F}}}}^{2}-\frac{7}{8}\left\|\bm{\Delta}^{\mathsf{H}}\bm{S}\bm{\Delta}\right\|_{{\footnotesize{\mathsf{F}}}}^{2}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{72}\sigma_{r}\left\|\bm{\Delta}\right\|_{{\footnotesize{\mathsf{F}}}}^{2}+\frac{1}{8}\left\|{\bm{M}^{\natural}}^{\mathsf{H}}\bm{S}\bm{\Delta}\right\|_{{\footnotesize{\mathsf{F}}}}^{2},

(6.19)

where the last line follows from that
\left\|\bm{\Delta}^{\mathsf{H}}\bm{S}\bm{\Delta}\right\|_{{\footnotesize{\mathsf{F}}}}^{2}\leq\left\|\bm{\Delta}\right\|_{{\footnotesize{\mathsf{F}}}}^{4}\leq\frac{\varepsilon^{2}\sigma_{r}}{\mu_{0}s}\left\|\bm{\Delta}\right\|_{{\footnotesize{\mathsf{F}}}}^{2}
and
\varepsilon\leq\frac{1}{3}
.

6.4.2
Proof of (
6.6
)

Applying simple triangular inequality yields that

\displaystyle\left\|\nabla f(\bm{M})\right\|_{{\footnotesize{\mathsf{F}}}}^{2}

\displaystyle=\left\|\nabla f_{1}+\nabla f_{2}+\nabla f_{3}\right\|_{{\footnotesize{\mathsf{F}}}}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq 2\left\|\nabla f_{1}+\nabla f_{2}\right\|_{{\footnotesize{\mathsf{F}}}}^{2}+2\left\|\nabla f_{3}\right\|_{{\footnotesize{\mathsf{F}}}}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq 4\left\|\nabla f_{1}\right\|_{{\footnotesize{\mathsf{F}}}}^{2}+4\left\|\nabla f_{2}\right\|_{{\footnotesize{\mathsf{F}}}}^{2}+2\left\|\nabla f_{3}\right\|_{{\footnotesize{\mathsf{F}}}}^{2}.

(6.20)

We will bound these three terms separately.

Bounding
\left\|\nabla f_{1}\right\|_{{\footnotesize{\mathsf{F}}}}^{2}

For any

\bm{X}=\begin{bmatrix}\bm{X}_{\bm{L}}^{\mathsf{H}}&\bm{X}_{\bm{R}}^{\mathsf{H}}\end{bmatrix}^{\mathsf{H}}\in\mathbb{C}^{(sn_{1}+n_{2})\times r}

such that
\left\|\bm{X}\right\|_{{\footnotesize{\mathsf{F}}}}=1
, we have

\displaystyle\left|\left\langle\nabla f_{1},\bm{X}\right\rangle\right|^{2}=

\displaystyle\left|\left\langle{\cal G}{\cal A}^{\ast}\left({\cal A}{\cal G}^{\ast}(\bm{L}\bm{R}^{\mathsf{H}})-\bm{D}\bm{y}\right),\bm{X}_{\bm{L}}\bm{R}^{\mathsf{H}}+\bm{L}\bm{X}_{\bm{R}}^{\mathsf{H}}\right\rangle\right|^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left|\left\langle{\cal G}{\cal A}^{\ast}{\cal A}{\cal G}^{\ast}(\bm{L}\bm{R}^{\mathsf{H}}-\bm{L}^{\natural}{\bm{R}^{\natural}}^{\mathsf{H}}),\bm{X}_{L}\bm{R}^{\mathsf{H}}+\bm{L}\bm{X}_{R}^{\mathsf{H}}\right\rangle\right|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\big(\left|\left\langle{\cal G}{\cal A}^{\ast}{\cal A}{\cal G}^{\ast}(\bm{L}\bm{\Delta}_{\bm{R}}^{\mathsf{H}}),\bm{X}_{\bm{L}}\bm{R}^{\mathsf{H}}\right\rangle\right|

\displaystyle\quad+\left|\left\langle{\cal G}{\cal A}^{\ast}{\cal A}{\cal G}^{\ast}(\bm{\Delta}_{\bm{L}}(\bm{R}^{\natural}\bm{Q})^{\mathsf{H}}),\bm{X}_{\bm{L}}\bm{R}^{\mathsf{H}}\right\rangle\right|

\displaystyle\quad+\left|\left\langle{\cal G}{\cal A}^{\ast}{\cal A}{\cal G}^{\ast}(\bm{L}\bm{\Delta}_{\bm{R}}^{\mathsf{H}}),\bm{L}\bm{X}_{\bm{L}}^{\mathsf{H}}\right\rangle\right|

\displaystyle\quad+\left|\left\langle{\cal G}{\cal A}^{\ast}{\cal A}{\cal G}^{\ast}(\bm{\Delta}_{\bm{L}}(\bm{R}^{\natural}\bm{Q})^{\mathsf{H}}),\bm{L}\bm{X}_{\bm{R}}^{\mathsf{H}}\right\rangle\right|\big)^{2}.

(6.21)

where we have used the fact
\bm{L}\bm{R}^{\mathsf{H}}-\bm{L}^{\natural}{\bm{R}^{\natural}}^{\mathsf{H}}=\bm{L}\bm{\Delta}_{\bm{R}}^{\mathsf{H}}+\bm{\Delta}_{\bm{L}}(\bm{R}^{\natural}\bm{Q})^{\mathsf{H}}
.
Apparently, the upper bounds of the above four terms can be established similarly. We focus on the first term
\left|\left\langle{\cal G}{\cal A}^{\ast}{\cal A}{\cal G}^{\ast}(\bm{L}\bm{\Delta}_{R}^{\mathsf{H}}),\bm{X}_{L}\bm{R}^{\mathsf{H}}\right\rangle\right|
and its upper bound can be obtained as follows:

\displaystyle\left|\left\langle{\cal G}{\cal A}^{\ast}{\cal A}{\cal G}^{\ast}(\bm{L}\bm{\Delta}_{R}^{\mathsf{H}}),\bm{X}_{L}\bm{R}^{\mathsf{H}}\right\rangle\right|=

\displaystyle\left|\left\langle{\cal A}{\cal G}^{\ast}(\bm{L}\bm{\Delta}_{R}^{\mathsf{H}}),{\cal A}{\cal G}^{\ast}(\bm{X}_{L}\bm{R}^{\mathsf{H}})\right\rangle\right|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}\left|\left\langle\bm{b}_{i}\bm{e}_{i}^{\mathsf{T}},{\cal G}^{\ast}(\bm{L}\bm{\Delta}_{R}^{\mathsf{H}})\right\rangle\right|\cdot\left|\left\langle\bm{b}_{i}\bm{e}_{i}^{\mathsf{T}},{\cal G}^{\ast}(\bm{X}_{L}\bm{R}^{\mathsf{H}})\right\rangle\right|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}\left|\left\langle{\cal G}(\bm{b}_{i}\bm{e}_{i}^{\mathsf{T}}),\bm{L}\bm{\Delta}_{R}^{\mathsf{H}}\right\rangle\right|\cdot\left|\left\langle{\cal G}(\bm{b}_{i}\bm{e}_{i}^{\mathsf{T}}),\bm{X}_{L}\bm{R}^{\mathsf{H}}\right\rangle\right|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}\left|\left\langle\bm{G}_{i}\otimes\bm{b}_{i},\bm{L}\bm{\Delta}_{R}^{\mathsf{H}}\right\rangle\right|\cdot\left|\left\langle\bm{G}_{i}\otimes\bm{b}_{i},\bm{X}_{L}\bm{R}^{\mathsf{H}}\right\rangle\right|.

(6.22)

To complete the proof, it suffers to control
\left|\left\langle\bm{G}_{i}\otimes\bm{b}_{i},\bm{L}\bm{\Delta}_{R}^{\mathsf{H}}\right\rangle\right|
and
\left|\left\langle\bm{G}_{i}\otimes\bm{b}_{i},\bm{X}_{L}\bm{R}^{\mathsf{H}}\right\rangle\right|
. Notice that

\displaystyle\left|\left\langle\bm{G}_{i}\otimes\bm{b}_{i},\bm{L}\bm{\Delta}_{R}^{\mathsf{H}}\right\rangle\right|=

\displaystyle\left|\frac{1}{\sqrt{w_{i}}}\sum_{j+k=i}\left\langle(\bm{e}_{j}\otimes\bm{b}_{i})\bm{e}_{k}^{\mathsf{T}},\bm{L}\bm{\Delta}_{R}^{\mathsf{H}}\right\rangle\right|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\sqrt{w_{i}}}\sum_{j+k=i}\left\|(\bm{e}_{j}^{\mathsf{T}}\otimes\bm{b}_{i}^{\mathsf{H}})\bm{L}\right\|_{{\footnotesize{\mbox{2}}}}\left\|\bm{e}_{k}^{\mathsf{T}}\bm{\Delta}_{R}\right\|_{{\footnotesize{\mbox{2}}}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sqrt{\sum_{j+k=i}\left\|(\bm{e}_{j}^{\mathsf{T}}\otimes\bm{b}_{i}^{\mathsf{H}})\bm{L}\right\|_{{\footnotesize{\mbox{2}}}}^{2}\left\|\bm{e}_{k}^{\mathsf{T}}\bm{\Delta}_{R}\right\|_{{\footnotesize{\mbox{2}}}}^{2}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sqrt{\sum_{j+k=i}\left\|\bm{b}_{i}^{\mathsf{H}}\bm{L}_{j}\right\|_{{\footnotesize{\mbox{2}}}}^{2}\left\|\bm{e}_{k}^{\mathsf{T}}\bm{\Delta}_{R}\right\|_{{\footnotesize{\mbox{2}}}}^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sqrt{\sum_{j+k=i}\left\|\bm{b}_{i}\right\|_{{\footnotesize{\mbox{2}}}}^{2}\left\|\bm{L}_{j}\right\|_{{\footnotesize{\mathsf{F}}}}^{2}\left\|\bm{e}_{k}^{\mathsf{T}}\bm{\Delta}_{R}\right\|_{{\footnotesize{\mbox{2}}}}^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sqrt{\mu_{0}s}\cdot\sqrt{\sum_{j+k=i}\left\|\bm{L}_{j}\right\|_{{\footnotesize{\mathsf{F}}}}^{2}\left\|\bm{e}_{k}^{\mathsf{T}}\bm{\Delta}_{R}\right\|_{{\footnotesize{\mbox{2}}}}^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sqrt{\mu_{0}s}\cdot\sqrt{\frac{\mu r\sigma}{n}}\cdot\sqrt{\sum_{j+k=i}\left\|\bm{e}_{k}^{\mathsf{T}}\bm{\Delta}_{R}\right\|_{{\footnotesize{\mbox{2}}}}^{2}}.

(6.23)

Similarly one has

\displaystyle\left|\left\langle\bm{G}_{i}\otimes\bm{b}_{i},\bm{X}_{L}\bm{R}^{\mathsf{H}}\right\rangle\right|\leq

\displaystyle\sqrt{\mu_{0}s}\cdot\sqrt{\sum_{j+k=i}\left\|[\bm{X}_{\bm{L}}]_{j}\right\|_{{\footnotesize{\mathsf{F}}}}^{2}\left\|\bm{e}_{k}^{\mathsf{T}}\bm{R}\right\|_{{\footnotesize{\mbox{2}}}}^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq

7
Conclusion

In this paper, we propose a non-convex method called PGD–VHL for low rank vectorized Hankel matrix recovery problem in blind super-resolution of point sources. Our theoretical analysis shows that PGD–VHL converges to the target matrix linearly when the number of samples is larger than
{\cal O}(s^{2}r^{2}\log^{2}(sn))
.
The performance of PGD–VHL has also been demonstrated by our numerical simulations.
For future work, it is interesting to study the recovery performance of PGD–VHL in the presence of noise and the behavior of vanilla gradient descent method for blind super-resolution.

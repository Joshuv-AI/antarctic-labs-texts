Fractional Leibniz rule on the torus
Árpád Bényi, Tadahiro Oh, and Tengfei Zhao

Abstract
. We discuss the fractional Leibniz rule for periodic functions on the d d -dimensional torus, including the endpoint cases. As an application, we present a product estimate, involving distributions of negative regularities.

1.
Introduction

In their seminal work
[
23
]

on the well-posedness theory of the Navier-Stokes and Euler equations, Kato and Ponce proved the following commutator estimate:

(1.1)

\displaystyle\|J^{s}(fg)-fJ^{s}g\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|J^{s}f\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\|g\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}+\|\nabla f\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\|J^{s-1}g\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}

for
s>0
,
1<p<\infty
, and
f,g\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
.
Here,
J^{s}=J^{s}_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}=(1-\Delta)^{\frac{s}{2}}
denotes
the Fourier multiplier operator on
\mathbb{R}^{d}

with multiplier
(1+4\pi^{2}|\xi|^{2})^{\frac{s}{2}}
.

1

1

In the following,
when there is no confusion, we simply write

Theorem A

In this section, we state several preliminary lemmas.

We first establish decay estimates for the kernels
of
D^{s}=D^{s}_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}
and
J^{s}=J^{s}_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}
on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
.
Given
s>0
, let
\mathcal{K}_{s}
and
\mathcal{G}_{s}

be
the kernels of
D^{s}
and
J^{s}
on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, respectively, given by

(2.1)

\displaystyle D^{s}f=\mathcal{K}_{s}*f\qquad\text{and}\qquad J^{s}f=\mathcal{G}_{s}*f.

The following lemma establishes a (well-known) decay
of these kernels away from the origin.
We present its proof for readers’ convenience.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
s>0
. Then, given
c_{0}>0
,
there exists
C_{1},C_{2}>0
such that

(2.2)

\displaystyle|\mathcal{K}_{s}(x)|

\displaystyle\leq C_{1}|x|^{-d-s},

(2.3)

\displaystyle|\mathcal{G}_{s}(x)|

\displaystyle\leq C_{2}|x|^{-d-s}

for any
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
with
|x|\geq c_{0}
,
where
C_{1}
is independent of
c_{0}
.

Proof.

We first consider the case
0<s<2
.
By writing

D^{s}=(-\Delta)D^{-2+s}
,
we have
\mathcal{K}_{s}=(-\Delta)K_{2-s}
,
where
K_{\sigma}
,
\sigma>0
,
denotes
the kernel of the Riesz potential
D^{-\sigma}
of order
\sigma>0
, given by

\displaystyle K_{\sigma}(x)=C_{d,\sigma}|x|^{-d+\sigma},\quad x\neq 0.

By applying
-\Delta
to
K_{2-s}
, we then have

\displaystyle|\mathcal{K}_{s}(x)|=C_{d,2-s}\big|(-\Delta)|x|^{-d+2-s}\big|\lesssim|x|^{-d-s},

yielding (
2.2
).

Similarly, by writing
J^{s}=(1-\Delta)J^{-2+s}
,
we have

(2.4)

\displaystyle\mathcal{G}_{s}=(1-\Delta)G_{2-s},

where
G_{\sigma}
,
\sigma>0
,
denotes the kernel of the Bessel potential
(1-\Delta)^{-\sigma}

of order
\sigma>0
, given by

(2.5)

\displaystyle G_{\sigma}(x)=C_{d,\sigma}\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-t}e^{-\frac{|x|^{2}}{4t}}t^{\frac{\sigma-d}{2}}\frac{dt}{t}.

See
[
15
, p. 14]
.
Note that
G_{\sigma}(x)
is smooth away from the origin
x=0
.
For
|x|\geq c_{0}
, we have

(2.6)

\displaystyle t+\frac{|x|^{2}}{4t}\geq t+\frac{c_{0}^{2}}{4t}\quad\text{and}\quad t+\frac{|x|^{2}}{4t}\geq|x|,

where the latter follows from completing a square.
Thus, from (
2.5
) and (
2.6
), we have

(2.7)

\displaystyle|G_{2-s}(x)|\lesssim e^{-\frac{|x|}{2}}\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-\frac{t}{2}}e^{-\frac{c_{0}^{2}}{8t}}t^{\frac{2-s-d}{2}}\frac{dt}{t}\lesssim e^{-\frac{|x|}{2}}

for
|x|\geq c_{0}
.
Similarly, by applying
-\Delta
to
G_{2-s}
in (
2.5
) with (
2.6
), we have

(2.8)

\displaystyle|(-\Delta)G_{2-s}(x)|\lesssim e^{-\frac{|x|}{2}}\int_{0}^{\infty}\Big(\frac{1}{t}+\frac{|x|^{2}}{t^{2}}\Big)e^{-\frac{t}{2}}e^{-\frac{c_{0}^{2}}{8t}}t^{\frac{2-s-d}{2}}\frac{dt}{t}\lesssim e^{-\frac{|x|}{3}}

for
|x|\geq c_{0}
.
Hence, (
2.3
) follows from (
2.4
), (
2.7
),
and (
2.8
).

When
s\geq 2
, by writing

\displaystyle D^{s}=(-\Delta)^{k+1}D^{-2(k+1)+s}\quad\text{and}\quad J^{s}=(1-\Delta)^{k+1}J^{-2(k+1)+s}

with
k=\big[\frac{s}{2}\big]
, where
[\,\cdot\,]
denotes the integer part,
we can repeat computations analogous to those presented above
and obtain (
2.2
) and (
2.3
).
We omit the details.

Lemma 2.2
.

Let

Lastly, let us introduce the Littlewood-Paley projector
\mathbf{Q}_{j}
and

).

\phi:\mathbb{R}\to[0,1]

For

\Phi_{0}(\xi)=\phi(|\xi|)

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}(\mathbf{Q}_{j}f)(n)=\Phi_{j}(n)\mathcal{F}_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}(f)(n),

.

\displaystyle[2^{-js}D^{s}_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\mathbf{Q}_{j},f](g)=2^{-js}D^{s}_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\mathbf{Q}_{j}(fg)-f\cdot 2^{-js}D^{s}_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\mathbf{Q}_{j}g.

Zygmund regularity of even singular integral operators on domains
Andrei V. Vasin

Abstract
. Given a bounded Lipschitz domain D ⊂ ℝ d , D\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}, a convolution Calderón-Zygmund operator T T and a growth function ω ⁡ ( x ) \omega(x) of type n n , we study what conditions on the boundary of the domain are sufficient for boundedness of the restricted even operator T D T_{D} on the generalized Zygmund space C ∗ ω ​ ( D ) C^{\omega}_{*}(D) . Based on a recent T(P) theorem, we prove that this holds if the smoothness of the boundary of a domain D D is by one point, in a sense, greater than the smoothness of the corresponding Zygmund space C ∗ ω ​ ( D ) C^{\omega}_{*}(D) . The main argument of the proof are the higher order gradient estimates of the transform T D ​ χ D T_{D}\chi_{D} of the characteristic function of a domain with the polynomial boundary.

1.
Introduction

1.1.
Backround

A
C^{k}
smooth homogeneous Calderón-Zygmund
operator is a principal
value convolution operator

Tf(y)=PV\int f(x)K(y-x)\,dx,

where
dx
denotes Lebesgue measure in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
and

K(x)=\frac{\Omega(x)}{|x|^{d}},\quad x\neq 0,

\Omega(x)
is a homogeneous function of degree 0 and
\Omega(x)
is
C^{k}
-differentiable on

\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus\{0\}
with zero integral on the unit sphere. The function
K(x)
is called a Calderón-Zygmund kernel.
Given a domain
D\subset\mathbb{R}^{d},
we consider a modification of
T
. The operator defined by the formula

T_{D}f=(Tf)\chi_{D},\quad\mathrm{supp}f\subset\mathrm{clos}(D),

is called a
restricted
Calderón-Zygmund operator.

The results of the present paper are motivated by the
the next theorem of Mateu, Orobitg and Verdera
[
6
]
(see also Anikonov
[
1
]
), who studied Lipschitz regularity
of quasiconformal mappings.

Theorem 1.1

(
[
6
, Main Lemma]
)
.

Let
D
be a bounded domain with the
C^{1+\alpha}
-smooth boundary,
0<\alpha<1
. Then the restricted Calderón-Zygmund operator
T_{D}
with an even kernel maps the Lipschitz space
Lip_{\alpha}(D)
into itself.

Observe that the restricted Calderón-Zygmund operators are not bounded in the Lipschitz spaces
Lip_{\alpha}(D)
for domains of general kind. A computation shows this for the Hilbert transform on an interval or for the Beurling transform on a square (see
[
6
]
).
Also, one can see that Theorem
1.1
holds when the
C^{1+\alpha}
smoothness of the boundary of a domain
D
is exactly by one point greater than the smoothness of the corresponding Lipschitz space
Lip_{\alpha}(D)
.

Theorem
1.1
is extended in
[
11
]
to certain spaces of zero smoothness between
Lip_{\alpha}(D)
and
BMO(D)
. Here we extend Theorem
1.1
for the higher smoothness general Zygmund spaces.

1.2.
Growth functions and smooth domains

To define smoothness we
follow Jansson (see
[
4
]
), and consider general growth functions.

Definition 1
.

A continuous increasing function
\omega:[0,\infty)\rightarrow[0,\infty),\,\omega(0)=0

is called a growth function of type
n
, if
n
is the positive integer such that the following two regularity properties are satisfied:

(1)

For some
q
,
n<q<n+1
the function
\frac{\omega(t)}{t^{q}}
is
almost decreasing
, that is there exists a positive constant
C=C(q)
such that

(1.1)

\omega(st)<Cs^{q}\omega(t),\;s>1.

(2)

For each
r
,
r<n
, the function
\frac{\omega(t)}{t^{r}}
is
almost increasing
, that is there exists a positive constant
C=C(r)
such that

(1.2)

\omega(st)<Cs^{r}\omega(t),\;s<1.

Definition 2
.

A bounded domain
D\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
is called a
(\delta,R)
-Lipschitz domain if, for each point
a\in\partial D
, there exists a function
A:\mathbb{R}^{d-1}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
with
\|\nabla A\|_{\infty}\leq\delta
and a cube
Q
with side length
R
and centre
a
such that, after a suitable shift and rotation, that sends
a
to origin, one has

D\cap Q=\{(x^{\prime},x_{d})\in(\mathbb{R}^{d-1},\mathbb{R})\cap Q:x_{d}>A(x^{\prime})\}.

The cube
Q
is called an
R
-window of the domain. Without risk of confusion, we omit parameters
\delta
and
R
, and consider Lipschitz domains in general.

Given a growth function
\omega
of type
n
, a bounded Lipschitz domain

D
is called a
C^{\omega}
domain if the function
A
from the definition satisfies the following condition:
there exists a polynomial
P(x^{\prime})
of order
n
such that

|A(x^{\prime})-P(x^{\prime})|\lesssim_{D}\omega(|x^{\prime}|),\>x=(x^{\prime},x_{d})\in Q.

Remark 1
.

For a growth function such that
\omega(t)=o(t^{n})
the parametrization
A
is of class
C^{n}
and
P
is the corresponding Taylor polynomial. In general case of a type
n
growth function (e.g.
\omega(t)=t^{n}
), the parametrization is of class
C^{n-1}
only.

1.3.
Regularity of the transform of the characteristic function

In our first theorem we relate the smoothness of the boundary
\partial D
with the gradient estimates of
T_{D}\chi_{D}
near the boundary.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
\omega
be a growth function of type
n>1
, let
D\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
be a
C^{\omega}
domain and let
T
be a
C^{2n-1}
-smooth homogeneous Calderón-Zygmund operator with an even kernel. Then

(1.3)

|\nabla^{n}T_{D}\chi_{D}(x)|\lesssim_{D,K}\frac{\omega(\rho(x))}{\rho(x)^{n+1}},

where
\rho(x)
is distance from
x\in D
to the boundary
\partial D
.

For
n=1
, the result is obtained in
[
11
, Proposition 1.10]
.
The known argument
[
1
,
6
]
to estimate
T_{D}\chi_{D}
is to apply an extra cancellation property of even convolution Calderón-Zygmund operators. This extra cancellation property means particularly that a derivative of the transform of the characteristic
function of a half-space is zero out of its boundary.
For higher order of smoothness,
n>1
, we need
to approximate the boundary of the domain by polynomial graphs instead of
hyperplanes. Since a derivative of the transform of the characteristic function of
a domain bounded by a polynomial graph of degree greater than one is not
zero anymore in general, the proof is more complicated. In Section 3 we obtain the higher order gradient estimates
|\nabla^{n}T_{D}\chi_{D}(x)|
for the domains bounded by polynomial graphs.
To do this job we will find a new form of extra cancellation property in Lemma
2.2
, which is suitable for the polynomial domains in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
.

The results proved are related not only to cited
[
6
]
, but also to Crus and Tolsa
[
2
,
10
]
, where the authors research Sobolev regularity of the Beurling
transform
B
of the characteristic function
\chi_{D}
of a Lipschitz domain
D\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
. They obtained that the outward unit normal
N
to the boundary, and the trace of
B\chi_{D}
belong to the same Besov space
B^{\alpha-1/p}_{p,p}(\partial D)
.
The results on Sobolev regularity in
[
2
,
10
]
of the Beurling
transform
B
of the characteristic function
\chi_{D}
were extended on higher orders of smoothness by Prats
[
8
]
. The author used there one complex variable approach.

Note that the dependence on the smoothness of the boundary for estimate (
1.3
) is sharp. It is proved in
[
11
]
in a case of the Beurling transform on plane domains and the Dini regular growth function for
n=1
. For the case of Sobolev regularity it is proved in
[
10
]

In the present paper we do not consider the sharpness (
1.3
). Our approach to obtain the gradient estimates (
1.3
) assumes
C^{2n-1}
smoothness of the kernel
K
. In any case, one may consider the
restricted Riecz multipliers as the operator
T_{D}
.

1.4.
Zygmund spaces

Let
dx
denote Lebesgue measure in
\mathbb{R}^{d}.
Let
Q
be a cube in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
with edges parallel to coordinate axes, let
|Q|
denote the volume of
Q
and let
\ell=\ell(Q)
be its side length. Also, let
\mathcal{P}_{n}
be the space of polynomials of degree at most
n
.

Definition 3
.

Given a growth function
\omega
of type
n>0
, the homogeneous generalized Zygmund space
C_{*}^{\omega}(D)
in a domain
D\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
consists of those
f\in L^{1}_{loc}(D,dx)
for which the seminorm

(1.4)

\|f\|_{\omega,D}=\sup_{Q\subset D}\inf_{P\in\mathcal{P}_{n}}\frac{1}{\omega(\ell)}\|f-P\|_{L^{\infty}(Q,dx/|Q|)}.

is finite.

Remark 2
.

It clearly holds
C^{\omega}(D)\subseteq C_{*}^{\omega}(D)
. Particularly,
for a type
n\geq 1
growth function
\omega(t)=t^{s}
with real
s
,
n<s<n+1
, both the spaces coincide, and are called the Hölder space
C^{s}(D)
of order
s
.
On the other hand,
for
\omega(t)=t^{n}
with positive integer
n
, one has the strict embedding, where
C_{*}^{\omega}(D)
is the classical Zygmund space
C^{n}_{*}(D)
, while
C^{\omega}(D)
is the Lipschitz of order
n
space
C^{n-1,1}(D)
.

Recently (
[
12
]
), it was proved a criteria of the boundedness of the Calderón-Zygmund operator on the Zygmund space.
To formulate the result, define the associated growth function as following

(1.5)

\widetilde{\omega}(x)=\frac{\omega(x)}{\max\{1,\int_{x}^{1}\omega(t)t^{-n-1}dt\}}.

Theorem 1.3

(
[
12
, Theorem 1.3]
)
.

Let
\omega
be a growth function of type
n\in\mathbb{N}
and let
D\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
be a bounded Lipschitz domain. Let
T
be a
C^{n+1}
-smooth homogeneous Calderón-Zygmund operator. Then the restricted operator
T_{D}
is bounded on the space
C_{*}^{\omega}(D)
if and only if two following properties have place:

(1)

T_{D}P\in C_{*}^{\omega}(D)
for any polynomial
P\in\mathcal{P}_{n}(D)
.

(2)

For any cube
Q\in D
with the centre
x_{0}
and for any homogeneous polynomial
P_{x_{0}}(x)=P(x-x_{0})
of degree
n
, there exists a polynomial
S_{Q}\in\mathcal{P}_{n}(D)
such that

\|T_{D}P_{x_{0}}-S_{Q}\|_{L^{1}(Q,dx/|Q|)}\leq C\|P\|\widetilde{\omega}(\ell)

with a constant
C
independent of
Q
.

Remark 3
.

These type of arguments were refered by Prats and Tolsa
[
9
]
as T(P) theorem
to indicate explicitly that the corresponding characterization uses values
of the operator
T
on the polynomials of appropriate degree.

Remark 4
.

If a growth function
\omega
of type
n
is Dini regular, that is, the integral

\int_{0}\omega(t)t^{-n-1}dt

converges, then
\widetilde{\omega}(x)
is equivalent to
\omega(x)
. In this case the second condition in the theorem follows from the first one and may be omitted.

Checking the conditions of Theorem
1.3
, we obtain the main result, where we extend Theorem
1.1
to the higher smoothness Zygmund spaces. Given a type
n
growth function
\omega(t)
, we denote by
C^{\omega,1}
domain
D
for the type
n+1
growth function
t\omega(t)
.

Theorem 1.4
.

Let
\omega
be a growth function of type
n
,
n\geq 1
, and let
D\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
be a bounded
C^{\omega,1}
domain. Let
T
be a
C^{2n+1}
-smooth homogeneous Calderón-Zygmund operator with an even kernel. Then
T_{D}
is bounded on the space
C_{*}^{\omega}(D)
.

For the classical Zygmund and the Hölder spaces Theorem
1.4
states the next smoothness drop by one point.

Corollary 1.5
.

For a positive integer
n
, let
T
be a
C^{2n+1}
-smooth homogeneous Calderón-Zygmund operator with an even kernel.
Then

(1)

T_{D}
is bounded on the Hölder space
C^{s}(D)
, provided
D
is
C^{s+1}
domain,
n<s<n+1
;

(2)

T_{D}
is bounded on the original Zygmund space
C^{n}_{*}(D)
, if the relevant domain
D
has the
C^{n,1}
boundary.

1.5.
Organization and notation

In Section 2 we prove auxiliary facts about differentiation of PV integrals concerning the extra cancellation property.
Theorems
1.2
and
1.4
are obtained in Section 3 and 4, respectively.

As usual, the letter
C
will denote a constant, which may be different at each

Notation
A\lesssim B
means that there is a fixed positive constant
C
such that
A<CB.
If
A\lesssim B\lesssim A,

A\approx B.
We write
A\lesssim_{a,b}B,
if a corresponding constant depends on
a,b.

2.
Cancellation property and differentiation of PV integrals

2.1.
Cancellation property of even kernels.

The following proposition
plays a crucial role in the study of a smooth convolution Calderón-Zygmund operator with an even kernel.

Lemma 2.1

(
[
3
,
6
]
)
.

Let
B
be an arbitrary Euclidean ball in
\mathbb{R}^{d},
let
T
be a smooth homogeneous Calderón-Zygmund operator with an even kernel. Then
(T\chi_{B})\chi_{B}\equiv 0
.

In fact, the authors proved in
[
6
]
that

(2.1)

\int_{B\setminus\{x:|x-y|<\varepsilon\}}K(x-y)dx=0,

where
y\in B
and
\varepsilon<dist(y,\partial B)
.

In what follows, we use a reformulation of the cancellation property of the even functions with zero integral on the unit sphere.

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
K
be an even homogeneous function of degree
-d
in
\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus\{0\}
with zero integral on the unit sphere. Then

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d-1}}K(x^{\prime},1)dx^{\prime}=0,

provided
dx^{\prime}
is Lebesgue measure on
\mathbb{R}^{d-1}
.

Proof.

Put
x^{\prime}=ur
,
u\in\mathbb{S}^{d-2}
,
r=|x^{\prime}|
and integrate in spherical coordinates in
\mathbb{R}^{d-1}
. We have

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d-1}}K(x^{\prime},1)dx^{\prime}=\int_{S^{d-2}}dS_{d-2}(u)\int_{0}^{\infty}K(ur,1)r^{d-2}dr,

where
dS_{d-2}(u)
is the induced surface measure on the unit sphere
S^{d-2}
.
Using the new variable
r=\tan\theta
,
\theta\in(0,\pi/2)
, in the inner integral, we obtain by homogeneity

=\int_{S^{d-2}}dS_{d-2}(u)\int_{0}^{\pi/2}K(u\tan\theta,1)\tan^{d-2}\theta\cos^{-2}\theta d\theta

=\int_{S^{d-2}}dS_{d-2}(u)\int_{0}^{\pi/2}K(u\sin\theta,\cos\theta)\sin^{d-2}\theta d\theta

=\int_{U}K(x)dS_{d-1}(x),

where the set
U
is the half of the unit sphere
S^{d-1}
above the hyperplane
x_{d}=0
and
dS_{d-1}(x)
is the induced surface measure on the sphere
S^{d-1}
. Since
K
is even, the last integral is zero and the proof of the lemma is completed.
∎

From the Lemma
2.2
one easily has

Corollary 2.3
.

Let
K
be an even homogeneous function of degree
-d
in
\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus\{0\}
with zero integral on the unit sphere. Let
\mathbb{H}^{d-1}

be an affine hyperplane that does not pass through the origin, and denote by
dx
Lebesgue measure in
\mathbb{H}^{d-1}
. Then

\int_{\mathbb{H}^{d-1}}K(x)dx=0.

2.2.
Differentiation of
PV
integrals with even kernels.

We prove the differentiation formula for
PV
integrals for even Calderón-Zygmund kernels.

Lemma 2.4
.

Let
K(x)

be an even
C^{1}
smooth Calderón-Zygmund kernel in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
. Let
y
be in a
C^{1}
domain
D
,

D_{\varepsilon}=D\setminus\{x:|x-y|<\varepsilon\}
,
\varepsilon<\textrm{dist}(y,\partial D)
. Then,

(2.2)

\frac{\partial}{\partial y_{i}}\int_{D_{\varepsilon}}K(x-y)dx=\int_{D_{\varepsilon}}\frac{\partial}{\partial y_{i}}K(x-y)dx,

and tending
\varepsilon
to
0
,

(2.3)

\frac{\partial}{\partial y_{i}}PV\int_{D}K(x-y)dx=PV\int_{D}\frac{\partial}{\partial y_{i}}K(x-y)dx.

Proof.

We start with the formula of differentiation under the integral sign (see
[
7
, Section 8(3)]
). Notice that the restriction of having an even function is not there anymore.

(2.4)

\frac{\partial}{\partial y_{i}}\int_{D_{\varepsilon}}K(x-y)dx=\int_{D_{\varepsilon}}\frac{\partial}{\partial y_{i}}K(x-y)dx+\int_{S_{\varepsilon}}K(x-y)\cos(\nu,x_{i})dS(x),

where
S_{\varepsilon}=\{x:|x-y|=\varepsilon\}
,
\nu
is the outer normal to
S_{\varepsilon}
, and
dS(x)
is the induced surface measure on
S_{\varepsilon}
.

Green’s formula, applied to the first integral on the right hand side of (
2.4
), provides

\int_{D_{\varepsilon}}\frac{\partial}{\partial y_{i}}K(x-y)dx=-\int_{D_{\varepsilon}}\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{i}}K(x-y)dx

=-\int_{\partial D\cup S_{\varepsilon}}K(x-y)\cos(\nu,x_{i})dS(x).

Therefore,

(2.5)

\frac{\partial}{\partial y_{i}}\int_{D_{\varepsilon}}K(x-y)dx=-\int_{\partial D}K(x-y)\cos(\nu,x_{i})dS(x).

Replace the domain
D
in (
2.5
) by an arbitrary ball
B
. By (
2.1
), for
y\in B
we have

0=\frac{\partial}{\partial y_{i}}\int_{B_{\varepsilon}}K(x-y)dx=-\int_{\partial B}K(x-y)\cos(\nu,x_{i})dS(x),

where
B_{\varepsilon}=B\setminus\{x:|x-y|<\varepsilon\}
and
\partial B
is a boundary of
B
.
Therefore, the second integral on the right hand side of (
2.4
) is equal to zero for any
\varepsilon
, and (
2.2
) holds.

On the other hand, the surface integral on right hand side of (
2.5
) is independent of
\varepsilon
, so, by Differentiable Limit Theorem,
we obtain (
2.3
).
Lemma
2.4
is proved.
∎

Iterating the formula obtained and using (
2.1
) we have

Corollary 2.5
.

Let
K(x)

be an even
C^{n}
smooth Calderón-Zygmund kernel in

\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
D_{\varepsilon}=D\setminus\{x:|x-y|<\varepsilon\}
,
\varepsilon<\textrm{dist}(y,\partial D)
and let
\partial^{k}_{y}
be the derivative of order
k
,
k=1,\dots,n
. Then

(2.6)

\partial^{k}_{y}\int_{D_{\varepsilon}}K(x-y)dx=\int_{D_{\varepsilon}}\partial^{k}_{y}K(x-y)dx

and respectively

(2.7)

\partial^{k}_{y}PV\int_{D}K(x-y)dx=PV\int_{D}\partial^{k}_{y}K(x-y)dx

for each
y
in a
C^{1}
domain
D
.

By Green’s formula, one has

Lemma 2.6
.

Let
K
be a
C^{n}
-smooth Calderón-Zygmund kernel in

\mathbb{R}^{d}
. Let
P_{k}(x)
be a homogeneous polynomial of order
k
and let
\partial^{k}_{x}
be a partial derivative of order
k
. Then the functions

N_{1}(x)=\partial^{k}_{x}(P_{k}K)(x)
and
N_{2}(x)=P_{k}(x)\partial^{k}_{x}K(x)
,
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus 0
, are
C^{n-k}
-smooth Calderón-Zygmund kernels.

Proof.

It is easy to see that
N_{1}
and
N_{2}
are homogeneous of order
-d
functions,
C^{n-k}
-smooth on
\mathbb{R}^{d}\backslash 0
.
That a degeneration can occur, for example, for the generalized Riesz potential, doesn’t bother us. We need to check that

\int_{r<|x|<R}N_{i}(x)dx=0,\>i=1,2,

for arbitrary
0<r<R<\infty
.

We argue by induction on order of polynomial
P_{k}(x)
. So, we start with
the function
N_{1}=\partial_{j}(x_{k}K(x))
. Let
\nu
be the outer normal to the spheres
S_{r}
and
S_{R}
, and let
dS(x)
be the induced surface measure. By Green’s formula, we have

\int_{r<|x|<R}N_{1}(x)dx=\int_{r<|x|<R}\partial_{j}(x_{k}K(x))dx

=\int_{S_{R}\cup S_{r}}x_{k}K(x)\cos(\nu,x_{j})dS(x)

=\int_{S_{R}}x_{k}K(x)\cos(\nu,x_{j})dS(x)-\int_{S_{r}}x_{k}K(x)\cos(\nu,x_{j})dS(x).

By homogeneity, the integral
\int_{S_{R}}x_{k}K(x)\cos(\nu,x_{j})dS(x)
does not depend on
R
, therefore

\int_{r<|x|<R}N_{1}(x)dx=0
.

For
N_{2}=x_{k}\partial_{j}K(x)
, we have

\int_{r<|x|<R}N_{2}(x)dx=\int_{r<|x|<R}x_{k}\partial_{j}K(x)dx

=\int_{r<|x|<R}N_{1}(x)dx-\delta_{k,j}\int_{r<|x|<R}K(x)dx=0,

since both integrals are equal to zero.

For
N_{1}
defined by an arbitrary polynomial
P_{k}
, we repeat the argument from the first step. So, by Green’s formula for
-d
-homogenous functions, we obtain

\int_{r<|x|<R}N_{1}(x)dx=\int_{r<|x|<R}\partial^{k}_{x}(P_{k}(x)K(x))dx

=\int_{r<|x|<R}\partial_{j}\partial^{k-1}_{x}(P_{k}K)(x)dx

=\int_{S_{R}\cup S_{r}}\partial^{k-1}_{x}(P_{k}K)(x)\cos(\nu,x_{j})dS(x)=0

by homogeneity.

For
N_{2}
with an arbitrary polynomial
P_{k}
, we have

N_{2}(x)=P_{k}(x)\partial^{k}_{x}K(x)=P_{k}(x)\partial_{j}\partial^{k-1}_{x}K(x)

=\partial_{j}(P_{k}\partial^{k-1}_{x}K)(x)-\partial_{j}P_{k}(x)\partial^{k-1}_{x}K(x).

Then

\int_{r<|x|<R}N_{2}(x)dx

=\int_{r<|x|<R}\partial_{j}(P_{k}\partial^{k-1}_{x}K)(x)dx-\int_{r<|x|<R}\partial_{j}P_{k}(x)\partial^{k-1}_{x}K(x)dx.

Apply Green’s formula to the first summand, then

\int_{r<|x|<R}\partial_{j}(P_{k}\partial^{k-1}_{x}K)(x)dx=\int_{S_{R}\cup S_{r}}P_{k}\partial^{k-1}_{x}K(x)\cos(\nu,x_{j})dS(x)=0

by homogenuity.
Since
\textrm{deg}(\partial_{j}P_{k})=k-1
, the induction assumption is applicable for the second summand.
The proof of Lemma
2.6
is completed.
∎

By Differentiable Limit Theorem, Lemma
2.6
and Lemma
2.4
imply a corollary.

Corollary 2.7
.

Let
K(x)

be an even homogeneous of degree
-d
function,
C^{n}
-smooth on

\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus 0
with zero integral on the unit sphere. Let
P
be a homogeneous polynomial of degree
k\geq 0
. Then, for the derivative of order
l
,
k<l\leq n
, we have

(2.8)

\partial^{l}_{y}PV\int_{D}K(x-y)P(x-y)dx=PV\int_{D}\partial^{l}_{y}(K(x-y)P(x-y))dx.

Proof.

Replacing
K
by
KP
in (
2.4
) and using Green’s formula, we have

\partial_{y_{i}}\int_{D_{\varepsilon}}K(x-y)P(x-y)dx

=\int_{D_{\varepsilon}}\partial_{y_{i}}(K(x-y)P(x-y))dx+\int_{S_{\varepsilon}}K(x-y)P(x-y)\cos(\nu,x_{i})dS(x)

=-\int_{\partial D}K(x-y)P(x-y)\cos(\nu,x_{i})dS(x),

where
D_{\varepsilon}=D\setminus\{x:|x-y|<\varepsilon\}
,
S_{\varepsilon}=\{x:|x-y|=\varepsilon\}
,
\varepsilon<\textrm{dist}(y,\partial D)
,
\nu
is the outer normal to
S_{\varepsilon}
, and
dS(x)
is the induced surface measure on
S_{\varepsilon}
.

Therefore, for any derivative
\partial^{k}_{y}
of order
k
,

\partial^{k}_{y}\partial_{y_{i}}\int_{D_{\varepsilon}}K(x-y)P(x-y)dx=-\int_{\partial D}\partial^{k}_{y}(K(x-y)P(x-y))\cos(\nu,x_{i})dS(x).

By Lemma
2.6
,
\partial^{k}_{x}(KP)(x)
is a smooth homogeneous Calderón-Zygmund kernel; it is even, since
K
is even.
Replace
K(x-y)
by
\partial^{k}_{y}(K(x-y)P(x-y))
in (
2.5
). We have

\partial_{y_{i}}\int_{D_{\varepsilon}}\partial^{k}_{y}(K(x-y)P(x-y))dx=-\int_{\partial D}\partial^{k}_{y}(K(x-y)P(x-y))\cos(\nu,x_{i})dS(x).

Comparing the last two formulas, one has

\partial^{k}_{y}\partial_{y_{i}}\int_{D_{\varepsilon}}K(x-y)P(x-y)dx=\partial_{y_{i}}\int_{D_{\varepsilon}}\partial^{k}_{y}(K(x-y)P(x-y))dx.

Iterating, we have

\partial^{l}_{y}\int_{D_{\varepsilon}}K(x-y)P(x-y)dx=\partial^{l-k}_{y}\int_{D_{\varepsilon}}\partial^{k}_{y}(K(x-y)P(x-y))dx.

with
k<l\leq n
.

By Corollary
2.5
applied to the even kernel
\partial^{k}_{x}(KP)(x)
, we obtain

\partial^{l}_{y}\int_{D_{\varepsilon}}K(x-y)P(x-y)dx=\int_{D_{\varepsilon}}\partial^{l}_{y}(K(x-y)P(x-y))dx,

with
k<l\leq n
. Finally, by Differentiable Limit Theorem, we obtain (
2.8
).
∎

We finish the section by the following lemma.

Lemma 2.8
.

Let
\Pi
be the boundary of a halfspace
\Pi_{+}
, let
K(x)

be an even homogeneous of degree
-d
function,
C^{n}
-smooth on

\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus 0
with zero integral on the unit sphere. Then for any derivative of order
0<k\leq n
and any
y\in\Pi_{+}
, one has

PV\int_{\Pi_{+}}\partial^{k}K(x-y)dx=0.

Proof.

After a shift and a rotation, we may assume that
\Pi
is defined by the equation
x_{d}=0
, and
\Pi_{+}
is the upper halfspace defined by
x_{d}>0
, where
x=(x^{\prime},x_{d})\in\mathbb{R}^{d-1}\times\mathbb{R}
.
Consider a sequence of imbedded balls
B_{r}
with centres
(0^{\prime},r)
and radii
r>0
.
By (
2.1
) and (
2.6
), we have for
y\in B_{r}

\int_{B_{r}\setminus\{x:|x-y|<\varepsilon\}}\partial^{k}K(x-y)dx=0.

Since
\partial^{k}K(x-y)\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus\{x:|x-y|<\varepsilon\},dx)
, hence tending
r
to
\infty
,
by the Dominated Convergence Theorem, it holds

\int_{B_{r}\setminus\{x:|x-y|<\varepsilon\}}\partial^{k}K(x-y)dx\longrightarrow\int_{\Pi_{+}\setminus\{x:|x-y|<\varepsilon\}}\partial^{k}K(x-y)dx

for arbitrary

and
\varepsilon<dist(y,\Pi)
. That is required, by Differentiable Limit Theorem.
∎

3.
Proof of Theorem

3.1.
C^{\omega}
parametrization.

Fix a constant
r_{0}
small enough so that series of properties
that will be needed along the proof are satisfied.
Choose
y\in D
and put
\delta=\textrm{dist}(y,\partial D)\leq r_{0}
. Assume that this distance is attained at a point
a\in\partial D
, which we can assume to be
a=0
. We assume that
y=(0^{\prime},\delta)
and

(x^{\prime},x_{d})\in D
for
0<t<r_{0}
.

By Definition
2
, we have a function
A
, an
R
-window
Q
with centre in origin such that, after suitable shift and rotation

D_{Q}=D\cap Q=\{(x^{\prime},x_{d})\in(\mathbb{R}^{d-1},\mathbb{R})\cap Q:x_{d}>A(x^{\prime})\}.

It is more convenient for us to take a cylinder
Q=\{(x^{\prime},x_{d}),|x^{\prime}|<r_{0},|x_{d}|<r_{0}\}
as a
R
-window. Also, assume that the tangent hyperplane to
\partial D
in origin is
x_{d}=0
,
A(0^{\prime})=0
and
\nabla A(0^{\prime})=0
.
For the relevant polynomial
P(x^{\prime})\in\mathcal{P}_{n}(\mathbb{R}^{d-1})
one has

|A(x^{\prime})-P(x^{\prime})|\lesssim_{D}\omega(|x^{\prime}|),\>|x^{\prime}|<r_{0}.

Observe that the polynomial
P
inherits certain properties of the function
A
:
P(0^{\prime})=0
and
\nabla P(0^{\prime})=0
.

3.2.
Start of proof.

In order to prove Theorem
1.3
we will estimate for each derivative of order
n
the following integral

I=\partial_{y}^{n}PV\int_{D}K(x-y)dx=PV\int_{D}\partial_{y}^{n}K(x-y)dx,

where equality is granted by Corollary
2.5
.

Split
I
at the level
r_{0}
:

I=PV\int_{D_{Q}}\partial_{x}^{n}K(x-y)dx+\int_{D\setminus Q}\partial_{x}^{n}K(x-y)dx

=II+III.

For term
III
, we clearly have

|III|\lesssim\frac{1}{r_{0}^{n}}.

To estimate term
II
, consider the domain
W=\{x=(x^{\prime},x_{d}):x_{d}>P(x^{\prime})\}
and denote
W_{Q}=W\cap Q
. We represent
II
as

II=\int_{D_{Q}\backslash W_{Q}}\partial_{x}^{n}K(x-y)dx-\int_{W_{Q}\backslash D_{Q}}\partial_{x}^{n}K(x-y)dx+

+PV\int_{W_{Q}}\partial_{x}^{n}K(x-y)dx=IV_{1}+IV_{2}+V=IV+V.

The domain
S=D_{Q}\backslash W_{Q}\cup W_{Q}\backslash D_{Q}
and the boundary
\partial D
are tangent in origin; hence,
S
is small. An absolute value estimate of IV follows
[
11
]
and is given in the next section.

The estimates of the principle value term
V
in a polynomial domain
W_{Q}
are postponed to Sections 3.4-3.6.

3.3.
Estimate of integral over a small sector (term IV)

Observe that the domain
S
is contained in the domain
\{x=(x^{\prime},x_{d}):|x^{\prime}|<r_{0},\>|x_{d}|\lesssim\omega(|x^{\prime}|)\}
. Therefore, we get

|IV|\leq\int_{S}|\partial_{x}^{n}K(x-y)dx|

=\int_{|x^{\prime}|<r_{0}}dx^{\prime}\left|\int_{P(x^{\prime})}^{A(x^{\prime})}\frac{dx_{d}}{(|x^{\prime}|^{2}+(x_{d}-\delta)^{2})^{(d+n)/2}}\right|

=\int_{|x^{\prime}|<r_{0}}dx^{\prime}\int_{|x_{d}|\leq|A(x^{\prime})-P(x^{\prime})|}\frac{dx_{d}}{(|x^{\prime}|^{2}+(x_{d}+P(x^{\prime})-\delta)^{2})^{(d+n)/2}}

\leq\int_{|x^{\prime}|<r_{0}}dx^{\prime}\int_{|x_{d}|\lesssim\omega(|x^{\prime}|)}\frac{dx_{d}}{(|x^{\prime}|^{2}+(x_{d}+P(x^{\prime})-\delta)^{2})^{(d+n)/2}}.

Split the outer integral at the level
\delta
:

\int_{|x^{\prime}|<r_{0}}=\int_{|x^{\prime}|<\delta}+\int_{\delta<|x^{\prime}|<r_{0}}.

For the first integral above, observe that the estimates
|x_{d}|\lesssim\omega(|x^{\prime}|)=o(|x^{\prime}|)
and
|P(x^{\prime})|\lesssim|x^{\prime}|^{2}
imply that

|x^{\prime}|^{2}+(x_{d}+P(x^{\prime})-\delta)^{2}\gtrsim\delta^{2}

for
\delta
small enough.
Hence, we have

\int_{|x^{\prime}|<\delta}dx^{\prime}\int_{|x_{d}|\lesssim\omega(|x^{\prime}|)}\frac{dx_{d}}{(|x^{\prime}|^{2}+(x_{d}+P(x^{\prime})-\delta)^{2})^{(d+n)/2}}

\lesssim\frac{1}{\delta^{d+n}}\int_{|x^{\prime}|<\delta}dx^{\prime}\int_{|x_{d}|\lesssim\omega(|x^{\prime}|)}dx_{d}

\lesssim\frac{1}{\delta^{d+n}}\int_{|x^{\prime}|<\delta}\omega(|x^{\prime}|)dx^{\prime}

\lesssim\frac{\omega(\delta)}{\delta^{n+1}}.

For the second integral, we use the estimate
|x^{\prime}|^{2}+(x_{d}+P(x^{\prime})-\delta)^{2}\geq|x^{\prime}|^{2}
, and
by almost decreasing property (
1.1
), we obtain

\int_{\delta<|x^{\prime}|<r_{0}}dx^{\prime}\int_{|x_{d}|\lesssim\omega(|x^{\prime}|)}\frac{dx_{d}}{(|x^{\prime}|^{2}+(x_{d}+P(x^{\prime})-\delta)^{2})^{(d+n)/2}}

\lesssim\int_{\delta<|x^{\prime}|<r_{0}}\frac{dx^{\prime}}{|x^{\prime}|^{d+n}}\int_{|x_{d}|\lesssim\omega(|x^{\prime}|)}dx_{d}

\lesssim\int_{\delta<|x^{\prime}|<r_{0}}\frac{\omega(|x^{\prime}|)dx^{\prime}}{|x^{\prime}|^{d+n}}

\lesssim\frac{\omega(\delta)}{\delta^{n+1}}.

So, integral
IV
is estimated.

3.4.
Estimate of integral over a polynomial domain (term V)

Consider the upper half-space

\Pi_{+}=\{x=(x^{\prime},x_{d})\in\mathbb{R}^{d}:\>x_{d}>0
}. By Lemma
2.8
, we have

\int_{W_{Q}}\partial_{x}^{n}K(x-y)dx

=\int_{W_{Q}}\partial_{x}^{n}K(x-y)dx-\int_{\Pi_{+}}\partial_{x}^{n}K(x-y)dx

=\int_{W_{Q}}\partial_{x}^{n}K(x-y)dx-\int_{\Pi_{+}\cap Q}\partial_{x}^{n}K(x-y)dx+\int_{\Pi_{+}\setminus Q}\partial_{x}^{n}K(x-y)dx.

For the third integral above we have

\int_{\Pi_{+}\setminus Q}|\partial_{x}^{n}K(x-y)|dx

\lesssim\int_{|x|>r_{0}}|\partial_{x}^{n}K(x-y)|dx\lesssim\frac{1}{r_{0}^{n}}.

It remains to estimate the following quantity

J=\int_{W_{Q}}\partial_{x}^{n}K(x-y)dx-\int_{\Pi_{+}\cap Q}\partial_{x}^{n}K(x-y)dx

=-\int_{|x^{\prime}|<r_{0}}dx^{\prime}\int_{0}^{P(x^{\prime})}\partial_{x}^{n}K(x-y)dx_{d}.

The domain of integration is tangent to the boundary in origin, but it is not small enough. Indeed, the inequality
0<x_{d}<P(x^{\prime})
implies only that
|x_{d}|<|x^{\prime}|^{2}
, and we can not follow the proof of term IV in previous Section 3.3, where the estimate
|x_{d}|<\omega(|x^{\prime}|)
is crucial. Our approach is to expand the integral
J
into a sum of terms of two types. Using polynomial restrictions on the integration domain we will evaluate the first ones explicitly, while the latter are evaluated absolutely.

For this consider the Taylor expansion of the inner integral up to the order
n
with respect to the variable
x_{d}
around
0
. We have

\int_{0}^{P(x^{\prime})}\partial_{x}^{n}K(x^{\prime},x_{d}-\delta)dx_{d}

=\sum_{k=1}^{n-1}\frac{1}{k!}\partial_{d}^{k-1}\partial_{x}^{n}K(x^{\prime},-\delta)P(x^{\prime})^{k}+

+\frac{1}{(n)!}\partial_{d}^{n}\partial_{x}^{n}K(x^{\prime},\eta-\delta)P(x^{\prime})^{n}

=\sum_{k=1}^{n}I_{k},

where
\eta\in(0,P(x^{\prime}))
is an appropriate point and
\partial_{d}
is the derivative with respect to
x_{d}
.

We represent each polynomial
P(x^{\prime})^{k}
as the sum:

(3.1)

P(x^{\prime})^{k}=Q_{n+k}(x^{\prime})+T_{n+k}(x^{\prime}),k=1,\dots,n

where
Q_{n+k}
is a polynomial of order less than
n+k
and
T_{n+k}
has no terms of order less then
n+k
.

Since the polynomial
P(x^{\prime})
has no terms of order less than 2,
P(x^{\prime})^{k}
and hence
Q_{n+k}
have no terms of order less than
2k
. In particular,
Q_{2n}
has no terms of order less than
2n
, hence
Q_{2n}
is zero.
So, we get

I_{k}=\partial_{d}^{k-1}\partial_{x}^{n}K(x^{\prime},-\delta)Q_{n+k}(x^{\prime})+\partial_{d}^{k-1}\partial_{x}^{n}K(x^{\prime},-\delta)T_{n+k}(x^{\prime})

=I^{\prime}_{k}+I^{\prime\prime}_{k},

for
k=1,\dots,n-1
and respectively

I_{n}=\partial_{d}^{n-1}\partial^{n}K(x^{\prime},\eta-\delta)T_{2n}(x^{\prime})=I^{\prime\prime}_{n}.

3.5.
Estimates of integrals with
I^{\prime}_{k}
-terms

To estimate the integrals with
I^{\prime}_{k}
-terms, we write
Q_{n+k}=\sum_{i=2k}^{n+k-1}q_{i}
,
k=1,\dots,n-1
, where
q_{i}
is a homogeneous polynomials of order
i
. In what follows, for simplicity, we write
\partial^{n+k-1}
instead of
\partial_{d}^{k-1}\partial_{x}^{n}
. Then, one has

\int_{|x^{\prime}|<r_{0}}I^{\prime}_{k}(x^{\prime})dx^{\prime}

=\int_{|x^{\prime}|<r_{0}}\partial^{n+k-1}K(x^{\prime},-\delta)Q_{n+k}(x^{\prime})dx^{\prime}

=\sum_{i=2k}^{n+k}\int_{|x^{\prime}|<r_{0}}\partial^{n+k-1}K(x^{\prime},-\delta)q_{i}(x^{\prime})dx^{\prime}.

Consider the summand

J_{i}=\int_{|x^{\prime}|<r_{0}}\partial^{n+k-1}K(x^{\prime},-\delta)q_{i}(x^{\prime})dx^{\prime}

=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d-1}}\partial^{n+k-1}K(x^{\prime},-\delta)q_{i}(x^{\prime})dx^{\prime}-\int_{|x^{\prime}|>r_{0}}\partial^{n+k-1}K(x^{\prime},-\delta)q_{i}(x^{\prime})dx^{\prime}.

The second term is bounded by a constant
C(r_{0})
. Indeed, we have

\int_{|x^{\prime}|>r_{0}}|\partial^{n+k-1}K(x^{\prime},-\delta)q_{i}(x^{\prime})|dx^{\prime}

\lesssim\int_{|x^{\prime}|>r_{0}}|\partial^{n+k-1}K(x^{\prime},-\delta)||x^{\prime}|^{i}dx^{\prime}

\lesssim\int_{|x^{\prime}|>r_{0}}|x^{\prime}|^{-n-k+1-d+i}dx^{\prime}

\lesssim\int_{r_{0}}^{\infty}\frac{dr}{r^{n+k+1-i}}

\lesssim\frac{{r_{0}}^{i}}{{r_{0}}^{n+k}}<\infty.

For the first term, we have

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d-1}}\partial^{n+k-1}K(x^{\prime},-\delta)q_{i}(x^{\prime})dx^{\prime}=

\delta^{-n-k+1+i}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d-1}}\partial^{n+k-1}K(x^{\prime},-\delta)q_{i}(x^{\prime})\delta^{n+k-1-i}dx^{\prime}.

By Lemma
2.6
, the function
N(x^{\prime},\delta)=\partial^{n+k-1}K(x^{\prime},-\delta)q_{i}(x^{\prime})\delta^{n+k-1-i}
is an even smooth homogeneous Calderón-Zygmund kernel. By homogeneity and by Lemma
2.2
, we have

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d-1}}\partial^{n+k-1}K(x^{\prime},-\delta)q_{i}(x^{\prime})dx^{\prime}=0

and hence,
|J_{i}|\leq C(r_{0})
for every
i=2k,\dots,n+k-1
. This clearly implies

\left|\int_{|x^{\prime}|<r_{0}}I^{\prime}_{k}(x^{\prime})dx^{\prime}\right|<C(r_{0})<\infty

for
k=1,\dots,n-1
. Recall that
I^{\prime}_{n}=0
; thus, all terms
I^{\prime}_{k}
are uniformly bounded by a constant independent of
\delta
.

3.6.
Estimates of integrals with
I^{\prime\prime}_{k}
-terms

The integrals with terms
I^{\prime\prime}_{k}
will be estimated absolutely. Recall that
T_{k}
does not contain terms of order less than
\leq n+k
; hence, we get

|T_{k}(x^{\prime})|\lesssim|x^{\prime}|^{n+k}

for
|x^{\prime}|<r_{0}
and
k=1,\dots,n
. Also, observe that

|\partial^{n+k-1}K(x^{\prime},-\delta)|\lesssim\frac{1}{(|x^{\prime}|^{2}+\delta^{2})^{(n+k-1+d)/2}}

for
k=1,\dots,n-1
uniformly with respect to
x\in D
.

For
k=n
, observe that
\eta\in(0,P(x^{\prime}))
implies
|\eta|=o(|x^{\prime}|)
;
hence
|x^{\prime}|^{2}+(\eta-\delta)^{2}\gtrsim|x^{\prime}|^{2}+\delta^{2}
. So, we obtain

|\partial^{2n-1}K(x^{\prime},\eta-\delta)|\lesssim\frac{1}{(|x^{\prime}|^{2}+\delta^{2})^{(2n-1+d)/2}}

uniformly with respect to
x\in\partial D
. Therefore, for
k=1,\dots,n
,

\int_{|x^{\prime}|<r_{0}}|I^{\prime\prime}_{k}(x^{\prime})|dx^{\prime}\lesssim\int_{|x^{\prime}|<r_{0}}\frac{|x^{\prime}|^{n+k}dx^{\prime}}{(|x^{\prime}|^{2}+\delta^{2})^{(n+k-1+d)/2}}.

Next, split the integral under consideration at the level
\delta
as

\int_{|x^{\prime}|<r_{0}}=\int_{|x^{\prime}|<\delta}+\int_{\delta<|x^{\prime}|<r_{0}}.

For the first integral on the right hand side, we have
|x^{\prime}|^{2}+\delta^{2}\geq\delta^{2}
; hence,

\int_{|x^{\prime}|<\delta}\frac{|x^{\prime}|^{n+k}dx^{\prime}}{(|x^{\prime}|^{2}+\delta^{2})^{(n+k-1+d)/2}}\leq\int_{|x^{\prime}|<\delta}\frac{|x^{\prime}|^{n+k}dx^{\prime}}{\delta^{n+k-1+d}}\lesssim C

with
C
independent of
\delta
.

For the second integral on the right hand side, we have
|x^{\prime}|^{2}+\delta^{2}\geq|x^{\prime}|^{2}
; hence,

\int_{\delta<|x^{\prime}|<r_{0}}\frac{|x^{\prime}|^{n+k}dx^{\prime}}{(|x^{\prime}|^{2}+\delta^{2})^{(n+k-1+d)/2}}\lesssim\int_{\delta<|x^{\prime}|<r_{0}}\frac{|x^{\prime}|^{n+k}dx^{\prime}}{|x^{\prime}|^{d+n+k-1}}

\lesssim\log\frac{1}{\delta}.

Therefore,

\int_{|x^{\prime}|<r_{0}}|I^{\prime\prime}_{k}(x^{\prime})|dx^{\prime}\lesssim 1+\log\frac{1}{\delta}\lesssim\frac{\omega(\delta)}{\delta^{n+1}}

by (
1.1
).

With estimates of terms IV and V in hand, the proof of Theorem
1.2
is completed.

as a finite sum of next summands

\partial_{x}^{i}S^{\prime}(x-x_{0})\partial_{x}^{n-i}\int_{D}K(x-y)S^{\prime\prime}(y-x)dy

=\partial_{x}^{i}S^{\prime}(x-x_{0})\partial_{x}^{1+k-i}(-1)^{n-1-k}\int_{D}\partial_{y}^{n-1-k}(K(x-y)S^{\prime\prime}(y-x))dy

with certain derivatives
\partial^{i}_{x}
of order
i=0,\dots,k
.
Clearly, we have

(4.3)

|\partial_{x}^{i}S^{\prime}(x-x_{0})|\lesssim|x-x_{0}|^{k-i}.

To estimate a quantity

|\partial_{x}^{1+k-i}\int_{D}\partial_{y}^{n-1-k}(K(x-y)S^{\prime\prime}(y-x))dy|

we follow Lemma
4.1
. So,
denote
l=1+k-i
,
l=1,\dots,n
, define the kernel
N=\partial^{k}(KS^{\prime\prime})
, and take a type
l
growth function
\omega_{l}(t)=t^{l+q-n}
.
We obtain

|\partial_{x}^{l}\int_{D}N(x-y)dy|\lesssim\frac{\rho(x)^{l+q-n}}{\rho(x)^{l+1}}\lesssim\rho(x)^{q-n-1},

when
l=1,\dots,n-1
.

If
l=n
, then
i=k=0
; so exactly by Theorem
1.2
one gets

|\partial_{x}^{n}\int_{D}K(x-y)dy|\lesssim\frac{\omega(\rho(x))}{\rho(x)^{n+1}}.

Combining this with the estimate (
4.3
) one has

|\partial_{x}^{n}T_{D}S_{x_{0}}(x)|\lesssim\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}|x-x_{0}|^{i}\rho(x)^{q-n-1}+|x-x_{0}|^{n}\frac{\omega(\rho(x))}{\rho(x)^{n+1}},

and we obtain (
4.2
) for
x\in Q
.
∎

We need two lemmas, which are valid in the general Lipschitz domains. Define the function that is the the denominator of
\widetilde{\omega}
in (
1.5
):

(4.4)

\xi(x)=\max\{1,\int_{x}^{1}\omega(t)t^{-n-1}dt\}.

Lemma 4.3

(
[
12
, Lemma 2.8]
)
.

Given a growth function
\omega
of type
n
, a bounded Lipschitz domain
D\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
, a function
f\in C_{*}^{\omega}(D)
, a cube
Q
with size length
\ell
and a polynomial
P_{f,Q}\in\mathcal{P}_{n}
such that
\|f-P_{f,Q}\|_{L^{\infty}(Q)}\lesssim\|f\|_{\omega,D}\omega(\ell).

Then the estimate

\|P_{f,Q}\|_{L^{\infty}(D)}\lesssim\|f\|_{\omega,D}\xi(\ell)

holds with a constant independent of
f
.

The second one is a variant of Marchaud argument (see for powered growth functions, e.g.,
[
5
, Proposition 4.5]
).

Lemma 4.4
.

Given growth function
\omega
of type
n
, a bounded domain
D\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
with the Lipschitz boundary, a function
f\in C_{*}^{\omega}(D)
and a polynomial
S
, the estimate

(4.5)

\|fS\|_{\omega,D}\lesssim\|S\|_{L^{\infty}(D)}\|f\|_{\omega,D}

holds with a constant independent of
f
.

Proof.

Let
Q
be a cube such that
2Q\subset D
.
Since
f\in C_{\omega}(D)
, by Definition
3
one has
\|f-P_{f,Q}\|_{L^{\infty}(Q)}\lesssim\|f\|_{\omega,D}\omega(\ell)
,
with a certain polynomial
P_{f,Q}\in\mathcal{P}_{n}
. Hence,

(4.6)

\|Sf-SP_{f,Q}\|_{L^{\infty}(Q)}\lesssim\|S\|_{L^{\infty}(D)}\|f\|_{\omega,D}\omega(\ell).

The conclusion of the lemma holds if we may replace the polynomial
SP_{f,Q}
by a polynomial of degree
n
in inequality (
4.6
). To do this, take for
SP_{f,Q}
the Taylor polynomial
T_{x_{0}}
of degree
n
around the centre
x_{0}
. We have

I=\|SP_{f,Q}-T_{x_{0}}\|_{L^{\infty}(Q)}\lesssim_{n,d}\|\nabla^{n+1}(SP_{f,Q})\|_{L^{\infty}(Q)}\ell^{n+1}

\lesssim_{n,d}\|\nabla^{n+1}(SP_{f,Q})\|_{L^{\infty}(D)}\ell^{n+1}

\lesssim_{n,d}\|S\|_{L^{\infty}(D)}\|P_{f,Q}\|_{L^{\infty}(D)}\ell^{n+1}

\lesssim_{n,d}\|S\|_{L^{\infty}(D)}\|f\|_{\omega,D}\xi(\ell)\ell^{n+1},

by Bernstein inequality on
D
and by Lemma
4.3
.
Since almost decreasing property (
1.1
), we have
\xi(\ell)\ell^{n+1}\lesssim\omega(\ell)
, and therefore,

I\lesssim\|S\|_{L^{\infty}(D)}\|f\|_{\omega,D}\omega(\ell),

as required.
∎

4.2.
Checking of point (1) of Theorem
1.3
for constants.

Proof.

By Theorem
1.2
applied for the following parameters: the type
n+1
,
n\geq 1
, growth function
t\omega(t)
,
C^{\omega,1}
domain
D
and
C^{2n+1}
-smooth homogeneous Calderón-Zygmund operator
T_{D}
with an even kernel, one has the gradient estimate

|\nabla^{n+1}T_{D}\chi_{D}(x)|\lesssim\frac{\rho(x)\omega(\rho(x))}{\rho^{n+2}(x)}=\frac{\omega(\rho(x))}{\rho^{n+1}(x)},\;x\in D.

By Taylor’s formula for
T_{D}\chi_{D}
with the Taylor polynomial
P_{n,x_{0}}
of degree
n
around the centre
x_{0}
of a cube
Q
such that
2Q\subset D
, we get

\displaystyle|T_{D}\chi_{D}(x)-P_{n,x_{0}}(x)|

\displaystyle\lesssim\sup_{Q}|\nabla^{n+1}T_{D}\chi_{D}(x)||x-x_{0}|^{n+1}

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{\omega(\rho(x))}{\rho^{n+1}(x)}|x-x_{0}|^{n+1}\lesssim\omega(|x-x_{0}|)

uniformly with respect on
x\in Q
. By Proposition 2.2
[
12
]
,

T_{D}\chi_{D}\in C^{\omega}(D)
, which clearly follows
T_{D}\chi_{D}\in C^{\omega}_{*}(D)
, and item (1) of Theorem
1.3
for constants is completed.
∎

4.3.
Checking of point (1) of Theorem
1.3
for polynomials.

Proof.

We repeat the estimates of the reminder in the Taylor’s formula. For this put the type
n+1
growth function
t\omega(t)
,
C^{\omega,1}
domain
D
and
C^{2n+1}
-smooth
homogeneous Calderón-Zygmund operator
T_{D}
with an even kernel. Take a polynomial

S\in\mathcal{P}_{n}
and a cube
Q
such that
2Q\subset D
.
We have

T_{D}S=\int_{D}K(x-y)S(x)dx

=\int_{D}K(x-y)(S(x)-S(y))dx+S(y)\int_{D}K(x-y)dx

=\int_{D}K(x-y)(S(x)-S(y))dx+S(y)T_{D}\chi_{D}(y)=A+B.

For term A we will apply the Taylor’s formula. So, consider the homogeneous expansion

S(x)-S(y)=\sum_{k=1}^{n}S_{k}(y-x)

where
S_{k}
are homogeneous polynomials of degree
k,\;k=1,\dots,n
. Define
S_{k,x}(\cdot)=S_{k}(\cdot-x)
and put
Q
be a cube with centre
x_{0}
and
2Q\subset D
. For each
T_{D}S_{k,x}\chi_{D}
the relevant reminder in the Taylor’s formula of
n
order by Lemma
4.1
for the growth function
t\omega(t)
of type
n+1
is estimated as following

\sup_{Q}|\nabla^{n+1}T_{D}S_{k,x}(x)\chi_{D}(x)||x-x_{0}|^{n+1}

\lesssim\rho(x_{0})^{q+1-n-2}|x-x_{0}|^{n+1}

\lesssim|x-x_{0}|^{q}\lesssim\omega(\ell).

For term B we apply Lemma
4.4
with
f=T_{D}\chi_{D}
and easily obtain (
4.5
). This completes point (1) of Theorem
1.3
.
∎

4.4.
Proof of point (2) of Theorem
1.3
.

To finish the proof of Theorem
1.4
it remains to check point (2) of Theorem
1.3
.
Take a cube
Q
,
2Q\in D
, with the centre
x_{0}
and a homogeneous polynomial
P_{x_{0}}(x)=P(x-x_{0})
of order
n>0
. We again estimate the reminder of the Taylor’s formula for
T_{D}\chi_{D}
and its Taylor polynomial
P_{n,x_{0}}
of degree
n
around the centre
x_{0}
of a cube
Q
with size length
\ell
.

For any parameter
q
such that the almost decreasing condition (
1.1
) holds, we may replace it by any other
q^{\prime}
,
q<q^{\prime}<n+1
, so that
\frac{\omega(t)}{t^{q^{\prime}}}
is almost decreasing as well, and therefore,
\omega(t)\gtrsim t^{q^{\prime}}
. Given
q
, we choose this
q^{\prime}
in the form

q^{\prime}=q+n-r
, where
r
is a parameter from the almost increasing condition (
1.2
) such that
r+1>q
. Then (
4.2
) is replaced by

(4.7)

|\partial_{x}^{n+1}T_{D}P_{x_{0}}(x)|\lesssim\rho(x_{0})^{q^{\prime}-n-1}+\frac{\omega(\rho(x_{0}))}{\rho(x_{0})}.

We have a simple estimate on
\widetilde{\omega}(t)
from below.

Lemma 4.5
.

\widetilde{\omega}(t)\gtrsim t^{q^{\prime}}
.

Proof.

Consider the function
\xi(x)
from (
4.4
). By almost increasing property (
1.2
), one has

\xi(x)\approx\int_{x}^{1}\omega(t)t^{-n-1}dt=\int_{x}^{1}\frac{\omega(t)}{t^{r}}t^{r-n-1}dt\lesssim x^{r-n}.

Therefore, by almost decreasing property (
1.1
),

\widetilde{\omega}(x)=\frac{\omega(x)}{\xi(x)}\gtrsim\omega(x)x^{n-r}\gtrsim x^{q+n-r},

that is required.
∎

By Lemma
4.5
, for the first summand in (
4.7
) we have

\rho(x_{0})^{q^{\prime}-n-1}\lesssim\frac{\widetilde{\omega}(\rho(x_{0}))}{\rho(x_{0})^{n+1}}

and for the second one it simply holds

\frac{\omega(\rho(x_{0}))}{\rho(x_{0})}\lesssim\frac{\widetilde{\omega}(\rho(x_{0}))}{\rho(x_{0})^{n-r+1}}

\lesssim\frac{\widetilde{\omega}(\rho(x_{0}))}{\rho(x_{0})^{n+1}}.

Combining, by the Taylor’s formula we obtain point (2) of Theorem
1.3
. The proof of Theorem
1.4
is completed.

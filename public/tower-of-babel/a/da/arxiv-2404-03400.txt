Abstract
. The eigenvalue probability density function of the Gaussian unitary ensemble permits a q q -extension related to the discrete q q -Hermite weight and corresponding q q -orthogonal polynomials. A combinatorial counting method is used to specify a positive sum formula for the spectral moments of this model. The leading two terms of the scaled 1 / N 2 1/N^{2} genus-type expansion of the moments are evaluated explicitly in terms of the incomplete beta function. Knowledge of these functional forms allows for the smoothed leading eigenvalue density and its first correction to be determined analytically.

1.
Introduction

1.1.
Unitary invariant ensembles and spectral moments

For a given weight
\omega,
we consider the eigenvalues
\boldsymbol{x}=\{x_{j}\}_{j=1}^{N}
of an
N\times N
unitary invariant random matrix
X
whose joint eigenvalue probability distribution follows

(1.1)

d\mathbf{P}(\boldsymbol{x})\propto\prod_{j<k}(x_{j}-x_{k})^{2}\prod_{j=1}^{N}\omega(x_{j})\,dx_{j}.

Such a random matrix is constructed according to its eigenvalue/eigenvector decomposition
X=UDU^{\dagger}
, where the diagonal matrix of eigenvalues is sampled according to (
1.1
), and
U
is a Haar distributed random unitary matrix corresponding to the eigenvectors.
The
1
-point function of
\boldsymbol{x}
is defined by

(1.2)

\rho_{N}(x)\equiv\rho_{N,\omega}(x):=N\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N-1}}\mathbf{P}(x,x_{2},\dots,x_{N})\,dx_{2}\,dx_{3}\dots dx_{N}.

This can be expressed as

(1.3)

\rho_{N}(x)=\sum_{j=0}^{N-1}\frac{P_{j}(x)^{2}}{h_{j}}\omega(x)=\frac{P_{N}^{\prime}(x)P_{N-1}(x)-P_{N-1}^{\prime}(x)P_{N}(x)}{h_{N-1}}\omega(x),

where
\{P_{j}\}_{j\geq 0}
is the sequence of
monic
orthogonal polynomials satisfying the orthogonality condition

(1.4)

\int_{\mathbb{R}}P_{j}(x)P_{k}(x)\,\omega(x)\,dx=h_{j}\,\delta_{jk}.

Here, the second expression of (
1.3
) follows from the Christoffel-Darboux formula; see e.g.
[
25
, Ch. 5]
.

The
p
-th spectral moment is defined by

(1.5)

m_{N,p}:=\mathbb{E}\Big[\,\textup{Tr}X^{p}\Big]=\mathbb{E}\Big[\sum_{j=1}^{N}x_{j}^{p}\Big]=\int_{\mathbb{R}}x^{p}\,\rho_{N}(x)\,dx,

where the expectation is taken with respect to the measure (
1.1
).
We also denote

(1.6)

\mathfrak{m}_{p,j}:=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\frac{x^{p}P_{j}(x)^{2}}{h_{j}}\omega(x)\,dx.

Then by (
1.3
), it follows that

(1.7)

m_{N,p}=\sum_{j=0}^{N-1}\mathfrak{m}_{p,j},\qquad\mathfrak{m}_{p,j}=m_{j+1,p}-m_{j,p}.

1.2.
Spectral moments of the GUE

As a prominent example, we discuss the spectral statistics of the Gaussian unitary ensemble (GUE).
Let us write

(1.8)

\omega^{\rm(H)}(x)=\frac{e^{-x^{2}/2}}{\sqrt{2\pi}}

for the standard Gaussian weight.
With this weight, the measure (
1.1
) coincides up to a scaling factor with the eigenvalue distribution of the GUE; see e.g.
[
25
, Definition 1.3.1]
for the element-wise construction of such Hermitian random matrices.
The corresponding orthogonal polynomial is given by the Hermite polynomial

(1.9)

He_{n}(x)=(-1)^{n}e^{x^{2}/2}\frac{d^{n}}{dx^{n}}e^{-x^{2}/2},

which satisfies

(1.10)

\int_{\mathbb{R}}He_{n}(x)He_{m}(x)\,\omega^{\rm(H)}(x)\,dx=n!\,\delta_{nm}.

It satisfies the three-term recurrence relation

(1.11)

He_{n+1}(x)=x\,He_{n}(x)-n\,He_{n-1}(x).

By (
1.3
), the
1
-point function
\rho_{N}^{\rm(H)}
of the GUE is then given by

(1.12)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\rho_{N}^{\rm(H)}(x)&=\frac{e^{-x^{2}/2}}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\sum_{j=0}^{N-1}\frac{He_{j}(x)^{2}}{j!}=\frac{e^{-x^{2}/2}}{\sqrt{2\pi}(N-1)!}\Big(NHe_{N-1}(x)^{2}-(N-1)He_{N}(x)He_{N-2}(x)\Big).\end{split}

We also write

(1.13)

\mathfrak{m}_{2p,j}^{\rm(H)}=\int_{\mathbb{R}}x^{2p}\frac{He_{j}(x)^{2}}{j!}\omega^{\rm(H)}(x)\,dx,\qquad m_{N,2p}^{\rm(H)}=\sum_{j=0}^{N-1}\mathfrak{m}_{2p,j}^{\rm(H)}

for the spectral moments of the GUE.

Proposition 1.1

(
Spectral moments of the GUE
, cf.
[
35
,
36
,
42
,
30
]
)
.

The following evaluation formulas hold true:

(i)

(Positive sum formula)
We have

(1.14)

\displaystyle\mathfrak{m}_{2p,j}^{\rm(H)}

\displaystyle=(2p-1)!!\sum_{l=0}^{p}\binom{j}{l}\binom{p}{l}2^{l},

(1.15)

\displaystyle m_{N,2p}^{\rm(H)}

\displaystyle=(2p-1)!!\sum_{l=0}^{p}\binom{N}{l+1}\binom{p}{l}2^{l}.

(ii)

(Alternating sum formula)
We have

(1.16)

\displaystyle\mathfrak{m}_{2p,j}^{\rm(H)}

\displaystyle=(2p-1)!!\sum_{r=0}^{p}(-1)^{r}\binom{j+2p-2r}{2p}\binom{p}{r},

(1.17)

\displaystyle m_{N,2p}^{\rm(H)}

\displaystyle=(2p-1)!!\,\sum_{r=0}^{p-1}(-1)^{r}\bigg[\binom{N+2p-2r-1}{2p}+\binom{N+2p-2r-2}{2p}\bigg]\binom{p-1}{r}.

Remark 1.2
.

The formula (
1.15
) was shown in
[
35
]
, where the three-term recurrence relation

(1.18)

(p+1)m_{N,2p}^{\rm(H)}=(4p-2)N\,m_{N,2p-2}^{\rm(H)}+(p-1)(2p-1)(2p-3)\,m_{N,2p-4}^{\rm(H)}

was also established.
One notes too a combinatorial proof of (
1.15
) due to Kerov, see
[
42
, Theorems 1 and 2]
.
In particular, in
[
42
, Lemma 1]
, the quantity
\mathfrak{m}_{2p,j}^{\rm(H)}
was identified with the number of rook configurations on Ferrer boards; see also
[
34
]
.
We also mention that the spectral moment can be expressed as

(1.19)

\displaystyle\begin{split}m_{N,2p}^{\rm(H)}=(2p-1)!!\,N\,{}_{2}F_{1}(-p,1-N\,;\,2\,;\,2);\end{split}

see e.g.
[
22
, Eqs.(4.2) and (4.3)]
and
[
36
,
68
]
, where

(1.20)

{}_{2}F_{1}(a,b;c;z):=\frac{\Gamma(c)}{\Gamma(a)\Gamma(b)}\sum_{s=0}^{\infty}\frac{\Gamma(a+s)\Gamma(b+s)}{\Gamma(c+s)\ s!}z^{s}

is the Gauss hypergeometric function.

Remark 1.3
.

Notice that two different expressions (
1.14
) and (
1.16
) indicate the identity

(1.21)

\sum_{l=0}^{p}\binom{j}{l}\binom{p}{l}2^{l}=\sum_{r=0}^{p}(-1)^{r}\binom{j+2p-2r}{2p}\binom{p}{r}.

This identity seems to most naturally follow from a double counting; see Subsection
3.2
.

Remark 1.4
.

The first few values of the spectral moments are given by

(1.22)

\displaystyle\begin{split}&m_{N,0}^{\rm(H)}=N,\qquad m_{N,2}^{\rm(H)}=N^{2},\qquad m_{N,4}^{\rm(H)}=2N^{3}+N,\\
&m_{N,6}^{\rm(H)}=5N^{4}+10N,\qquad m_{N,8}^{\rm(H)}=14N^{5}+70N^{3}+21N.\end{split}

On the other hand, it follows from the three-term recurrence (
1.18
) that

(1.23)

\lim_{N\to\infty}\frac{1}{N^{p+1}}m_{N,2p}^{\rm(H)}=C_{p}:=\frac{1}{p+1}\binom{2p}{p},

where
C_{p}
is the
p
-th Catalan number. These limiting moments can be used to reconstruct the limiting scaled spectral density (see the proof of Proposition
4.4
below) and give rise to Wigner’s semi-circle law; see e.g.
[
3
]
.

Remark 1.5

(Genus expansion)
.

The Stirling numbers of the first kind
[
58
, Section 26.8]
are given by

(1.24)

s(n,k)=(-1)^{n-k}\sum_{1\leq b_{1}<\dots<b_{n-k}\leq n-1}b_{1}b_{2}\dots b_{n-k},\qquad(n>k\geq 1).

Their generating function is given by

(1.25)

\sum_{k=0}^{n}s(n,k)x^{k}=\frac{\Gamma(x+1)}{\Gamma(x-n+1)};

see e.g.
[
58
, Eq.(26.8.7)]
.
By using this and rearranging the terms in the expression (
1.15
) of
m_{N,2p}^{\rm(H)}
, we have

\displaystyle m_{N,2p}^{\rm(H)}

\displaystyle=(2p-1)!!\sum_{l=0}^{p}\bigg(\sum_{k=0}^{l+1}\frac{s(l+1,k)}{(l+1)!}N^{k}\bigg)\binom{p}{l}2^{l}

\displaystyle=(2p-1)!!\sum_{r=0}^{p+1}\bigg(\sum_{m=0}^{r}\frac{s(p+1-m,p+1-r)}{(p+1-m)!}\binom{p}{m}2^{p-m}\bigg)N^{p+1-r}.

Furthermore, it follows from the identity

(1.26)

\sum_{m=0}^{r}\frac{s(p+1-m,p+1-r)}{(p+1-m)!}\binom{p}{m}2^{p-m}=0,\qquad\textup{if }r\textup{ is odd}

that we have the
1/N^{2}
expansion

(1.27)

m_{N,2p}^{\rm(H)}=\sum_{g=0}^{\lfloor(p+1)/2\rfloor}\mathcal{E}_{g}(p)N^{p+1-2g},

where

(1.28)

\mathcal{E}_{g}(p)=(2p-1)!!\sum_{m=0}^{2g}\frac{s(p+1-m,p+1-2g)}{(p+1-m)!}\binom{p}{m}2^{p-m}.

The coefficient
\mathcal{E}_{g}(p)
corresponds to the count of pairings among the edges of a
2p
-gon, which is dual to a map on a compact Riemann surface of genus
g
; see
[
35
]
.
Therefore, the expansion (
1.26
) is commonly referred to as the genus expansion.
In particular, the first few values of
\mathcal{E}_{g}
are given as

(1.29)

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}_{0}(p)

\displaystyle=C_{p},\qquad\mathcal{E}_{1}(p)=C_{p}\,\frac{(p+1)!}{(p-2)!}\frac{1}{12},\qquad\mathcal{E}_{2}(p)=C_{p}\,\frac{(p+1)!}{(p-4)!}\frac{5p-2}{1440},

(1.30)

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}_{3}(p)

\displaystyle=C_{p}\,\frac{(p+1)!}{(p-6)!}\frac{35p^{2}-77p+12}{362880},\qquad\mathcal{E}_{4}(p)=C_{p}\,\frac{(p+1)!}{(p-8)!}\frac{175p^{3}-945p^{2}+1094p-72}{87091200}.

The small order cases also follow from
[
68
, Theorem 7]
, which were based on a large
N
analysis of (
1.18
). The form of
\mathcal{E}_{g}(p)
given by (
1.28
) appears to be new.
We also mention that
\mathcal{E}_{g}(p)
satisfies the two parameter recurrence relation
[
35
]

(1.31)

(p+2)\mathcal{E}_{g}(p+1)=2(2p+1)\mathcal{E}_{g}(p)+p(2p+1)(2p-1)\mathcal{E}_{g-1}(p-1),

which is also known as the topological recursion.

1.3.
Discrete
q
-deformed GUE

We now discuss the discrete
q
-formed version of the GUE.
For this purpose, we shall frequently use the notations

(1.32)

\displaystyle(a;q)_{n}:=(1-a)(1-aq)\dots(1-aq^{n-1}),\qquad(a_{1},\dots,a_{k};q)_{\infty}:=\prod_{l=0}^{\infty}(1-a_{1}q^{l})\dots(1-a_{k}q^{l}).

The
q
-hypergeometric series
{}_{r}\phi_{s}
is defined by

(1.33)

{}_{r}\phi_{s}\left(\begin{matrix}a_{1},\dots,a_{r}\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\\
b_{1},\dots,b_{s}\end{matrix}\,;\,q,z\right)=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{(a_{1};q)_{k}\dots(a_{r};q)_{k}}{(b_{1};q)_{k}\dots(b_{s};q)_{k}}\frac{z^{k}}{(q;q)_{k}}\Big((-1)^{k}q^{k(k-1)/2}\Big)^{1+s-r};

see e.g.
[
58
, Chapter 17]
.
Note that if one of the parameters
a_{1},\dots,a_{r}
is a non-positive power of
q
, then the sum terminates.
We also mention that as
q\to 1
, it recovers the more familiar
{}_{r}F_{s}
hypergeometric function:

(1.34)

{}_{r}\phi_{s}\left(\begin{matrix}q^{c_{1}},\dots,q^{c_{r}}\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\\
q^{d_{1}},\dots,q^{d_{s}}\end{matrix}\,;\,q,(q-1)^{1+s-r}\,z\right)\to{}_{r}F_{s}\left(\genfrac{.}{.}{0.0pt}{}{\__hypergeometric_process:n{c_1,\dots,c_r}}{\__hypergeometric_process:n{d_1, \dots,d_s}}\;\middle|\;z\right):=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{(c_{1})_{k}\dots(c_{r})_{k}}{(d_{1})_{k}\dots(d_{s})_{k}}\frac{z^{k}}{k!}.

Let us recall that the
q
-derivative
D_{q}
is defined by

(1.35)

D_{q}f(x)=\frac{f(x)-f(qx)}{(1-q)x}.

Recall also that its anti-derivative, the Jackson
q
-integral, is defined by

(1.36)

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}f(x)\,d_{q}x=(1-q)\sum_{k=-\infty}^{\infty}q^{k}\,f(q^{k}),\qquad\int_{0}^{\alpha}f(x)\,d_{q}x=(1-q)\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\alpha q^{k}\,f(\alpha q^{k}),

and

(1.37)

\int_{a}^{b}f(x)\,d_{q}x=\int_{0}^{b}f(x)\,d_{q}x-\int_{0}^{a}f(x)\,d_{q}x.

Note too that

(1.38)

\int_{-1}^{1}f(x)\,d_{q}x=\frac{1}{a}\int_{-a}^{a}f\Big(\frac{x}{a}\Big)\,d_{q}x.

The discrete
q
-Hermite weight and its scaled version are defined by

(1.39)

\omega^{\rm(dH)}(x):=\frac{(qx,-qx;q)_{\infty}}{(q,-1,-q;q)_{\infty}},\qquad\widehat{\omega}^{\rm(dH)}(x):=\sqrt{1-q}\,\omega^{{\rm(dH)}}(\sqrt{1-q}\,x).

The support of
\omega^{\rm(dH)}(x)
is taken as the
q
-lattice
\pm q^{k}
,
k=0,1,\dots
(note that for these values of
x
,
\omega^{\rm(dH)}(x)>0
), which in the continuum limit
q\to 1^{-}
is the interval
[-1,1]
.
In this same limit,
\widehat{\omega}^{\rm(dH)}(x)
has support of all the real line, and moreover it recovers the Gaussian weight

(1.40)

\lim_{q\to 1^{-}}\widehat{\omega}^{\rm(dH)}(x)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}e^{-x^{2}/2};

see e.g.
[
1
]
.
For
\omega^{\rm(dH)}(x)
, one notes
that the even moments are given by

(1.41)

\int_{-1}^{1}x^{2p}\,\omega^{\rm(dH)}(x)\,d_{q}x=(1-q)(q;q^{2})_{p};

see e.g.
[
55
]
,
whereas the odd moments vanish. The moments of
\widehat{\omega}^{\rm(dH)}(x)
follow from (
1.41
) by applying (
1.38
).

We shall use several different conventions for the
q
-Hermite polynomials.

•

The
q
-Hermite polynomial
H_{n}(x;q)
is defined by

(1.42)

\displaystyle\begin{split}H_{n}(x;q)&:=x^{n}\,{}_{2}\phi_{0}\left(\begin{matrix}q^{-n},q^{-n+1}\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\\
-\end{matrix}\,;\,q^{2},\frac{q^{2n-1}}{x^{2}}\right)\\
&=\sum_{k=0}^{n/2}(-1)^{k}\,\frac{(q^{-n};q^{2})_{k}(q^{-n+1};q^{2})_{k}q^{k(2n-k)}}{(q^{2};q^{2})_{k}}x^{n-2k};\end{split}

see
[
43
, Section 14.28]
.
It satisfies the orthogonality

(1.43)

\int_{-1}^{1}H_{m}(x;q)H_{n}(x;q)\omega^{\rm(dH)}(x)\,d_{q}x=(1-q)(q;q)_{n}q^{n(n-1)/2}\delta_{n,m}

and the three-term recurrence relation

(1.44)

x\,H_{n}(x;q)=H_{n+1}(x;q)+q^{n-1}(1-q^{n})H_{n-1}(x;q).

•

We also set

(1.45)

\begin{split}\widehat{H}_{n}(x;q)&:=\frac{1}{(1-q)^{n/2}}H_{n}\Big(\sqrt{1-q}\,x;q\Big)\\
&=\sum_{k=0}^{n/2}(-1)^{k}\,\frac{(q^{-n};q^{2})_{k}(q^{-n+1};q^{2})_{k}q^{k(2n-k)}}{(q^{2};q^{2})_{k}}\frac{1}{(1-q)^{k}}x^{n-2k}.\end{split}

Then it follows that

(1.46)

x\,\widehat{H}_{n}(x;q)=\widehat{H}_{n+1}(x;q)+q^{n-1}[n]_{q}\,\widehat{H}_{n-1}(x;q),

where we used the convention that the
q
-analogue of an integer
n
given by

[n]_{q}:=\dfrac{1-q^{n}}{1-q}.

Note that in the continuum limit, it recovers the classical Hermite polynomial

(1.47)

\lim_{q\to 1^{-}}\widehat{H}_{n}(x;q)=He_{n}(x).

Remark 1.6
.

In
[
38
]
, the
q
-Hermite polynomial
\widetilde{H}_{n}(x;q)
that satisfies the recurrence relation

(1.48)

x\,\widetilde{H}_{n}(x;q)=\widetilde{H}_{n+1}(x;q)+[n]_{q}\,\widetilde{H}_{n-1}(x;q)

has been studied.
We emphasize that while
H_{n}
and
\widehat{H}_{n}
are related by simple scaling,
\widetilde{H}_{n}
is not.

According to (
1.3
) the density
\rho_{N}^{\rm(dH)}
of the discrete
q
-GUE is given by

(1.49)

\rho_{N}^{\rm(dH)}(x)=\frac{1}{1-q}\sum_{j=0}^{N-1}\frac{H_{j}(x,q)^{2}}{(q;q)_{j}q^{j(j-1)/2}}\omega^{\rm(dH)}(x).

Similarly, we define

(1.50)

\widehat{\rho}_{N}^{\rm(dH)}(x):=\sqrt{1-q}\,\rho_{N}^{\rm(dH)}(\sqrt{1-q}\,x).

Let us define the corresponding spectral moments

(1.51)

\displaystyle m_{N,2p}^{\rm(dH)}

\displaystyle:=\int_{-1}^{1}|x|^{2p}\rho_{N}^{\rm(dH)}(x)\,d_{q}x,

(1.52)

\displaystyle\widehat{m}_{N,2p}^{\rm(dH)}

\displaystyle:=\int_{-1/\sqrt{1-q}}^{1/\sqrt{1-q}}|x|^{2p}\,\widehat{\rho}_{N}^{\rm(dH)}(x)\,d_{q}x=\frac{1}{(1-q)^{p}}\,m_{N,2p}^{\rm(dH)}.

We also denote

(1.53)

\mathfrak{m}_{2p,j}^{\rm(H)}=\int_{-1}^{1}\frac{|x|^{2p}H_{j}(x,q)^{2}\omega^{\rm(dH)}(x)}{(1-q)\,(q;q)_{j}q^{j(j-1)/2}}\,d_{q}x,\qquad\widehat{\mathfrak{m}}_{2p,j}^{\rm(H)}=\frac{1}{(1-q)^{p}}\mathfrak{m}_{2p,j}^{\rm(H)}

so that

(1.54)

m_{N,2p}^{\rm(dH)}=\sum_{j=0}^{N-1}\mathfrak{m}_{2p,j}^{\rm(H)},\qquad\widehat{m}_{N,2p}^{\rm(dH)}=\sum_{j=0}^{N-1}\widehat{\mathfrak{m}}_{2p,j}^{\rm(H)}.

1.4.
Objectives and previous literature

A celebrated result in the application of random matrices is that the
1/N^{2}
expansion of the GUE moments (
1.27
), albeit with the coefficients defined implicitly via the two-variable recurrence relation (
1.31
), counts the number of pairings of the sides of a
2p
-gon which are dual to a map on a compact orientable Riemann surface of genus
g
(equivalently, a surface of genus
g
results when the sides of the polygon are identified in pairs). This line of work was initiated by Brézin et al.
[
11
]
, with the recursive specification of the coefficients obtained in
[
35
]
.
As noted below (
1.23
), the leading term in the expansion (
1.27
) was isolated much earlier in the development of random matrix theory, specifically as a way of computing the limiting spectral density
[
67
]
.

In the ensuing years eigenvalue probability distributions of the general form (
1.1
) have occurred in various applications, notably in matrix models of low-dimensional quantum field theories
[
49
]
, fermionic systems
[
23
]
, quantum transport
[
7
]
, number theory
[
41
]
, wireless communication
[
63
]
and tiling models
[
8
]
. Consideration of observables in these settings has motivated a number of studies into the corresponding spectral moments; references include
[
59
,
20
,
37
,
50
,
66
,
53
,
32
,
61
,
12
,
40
,
4
,
56
,
6
,
28
,
51
,
52
]
.

An explanation of the three-term recurrence (
1.18
) in terms of differential properties of the Laplace transform of
\rho_{N}^{\rm(H)}
, first uncovered in
[
44
]
(see also
[
36
]
), has given motivation to the study of spectral moments in other random matrix ensembles exhibiting this and related mathematical structures
[
46
,
68
,
22
,
5
,
60
,
31
,
13
,
14
,
21
,
33
]
. Considerations along these lines relate to integrable properties of the corresponding ensembles. Specifically, in
[
54
]
the ensemble (
1.1
) with discrete
q
-Hermite weight (
1.39
) was isolated as possessing distinguished integrable properties,
and which furthermore has the status of being
a generalisation of the GUE natural from the viewpoint of
q
-orthogonal polynomial theory. The subsequent works
[
30
]
and
[
16
]
furthered the study of the integrable properties of this model, building on
[
17
,

]
.
We also refer to
[
9
,
10
,
29
,
39
,

,

]
for related studies on discrete orthogonal polynomial ensembles.

The aim of the present work is to study integrable properties of spectral moments and the eigenvalue density of the discrete
q
-Hermite unitary invariant random matrix ensemble beyond the results of the earlier works
[

,

,

]
. The details of our findings are specified in the next section. They include a positive sum formula for the spectral moments (
2.1
), the leading two terms of the scaled
1/N^{2}
expansion of this formula (Theorem
2.3
), and the form of the smoothed leading eigenvalue density and its leading correction (Theorem

).

2.
Main results

2.1.
Spectral moments

As a
q
-analogue of Proposition
1.1
, we have the following.

Theorem 2.1

(
Spectral moments of the
q
-deformed GUE
)
.

The following hold true:

(i)

(Positive sum formula)
We have

(2.1)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\widehat{\mathfrak{m}}_{2p,j}^{\rm(H)}&=\sum_{l=0}^{p}q^{(j-l)(2p-l)+l(l-1)/2}\genfrac{[}{]}{0.0pt}{}{j}{l}_{q}\dfrac{[2p]_{q}!}{[2p-2l]_{q}!!\,[l]_{q}!},\end{split}

(2.2)

\displaystyle\widehat{m}_{N,2p}^{\rm(dH)}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=0}^{N-1}\sum_{l=0}^{p}q^{(j-l)(2p-l)+l(l-1)/2}\genfrac{[}{]}{0.0pt}{}{j}{l}_{q}\dfrac{[2p]_{q}!}{[2p-2l]_{q}!!\,[l]_{q}!}.

Taking
q\to 1
, we reclaim (
1.14
) and (
1.15
).

(ii)

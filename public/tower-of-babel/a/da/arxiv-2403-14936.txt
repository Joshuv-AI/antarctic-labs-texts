Some evaluations of interpolated multiple zeta values and interpolated multiple t t -values
Zhonghua Li and Zhenlu Wang

Abstract
In this paper, we study the evaluation formulas of the interpolated multiple zeta values and the interpolated multiple t t -values with indices involving 1 , 2 , 3 1,2,3 . To get these evaluations, we derive the corresponding algebraic relations in the harmonic algebra.



Keywords
interpolated multiple zeta values; interpolated multiple
t
-values; harmonic algebra.

2020 Mathematics Subject Classification
11M32, 16W99.

1
Introduction

A finite sequence of positive integers is called an index. An index
\mathbf{k}=(k_{1},\ldots,k_{n})
is admissible if
k_{1}\geq 2
. The depth of
\mathbf{k}
is defined by
\operatorname{dep}(\mathbf{k})=n
.

To study the multiple zeta values and the multiple zeta-star values simultaneously, S. Yamamoto introuced the interpolated multiple zeta values in
[
11
]
, which are interpolation polynomials of these two types of values. Let
r
be a variable. For an admissible index
\mathbf{k}=(k_{1},\ldots,k_{n})
, the interpolated multiple zeta value (
r
-MZV)
\zeta^{r}(\mathbf{k})
is defined by

\displaystyle\zeta^{r}(\mathbf{k})=\zeta^{r}(k_{1},\ldots,k_{n})=\sum\limits_{\mathbf{p}=(k_{1}\Box k_{2}\Box\cdots\Box k_{n})\atop\Box=``,"\text{or}``+"}r^{n-\operatorname{dep}(\mathbf{p})}\zeta(\mathbf{p}),

where for an admissible index
\mathbf{k}=(k_{1},\ldots,k_{n})
, the multiple zeta value (MZV)
\zeta(\mathbf{k})
is defined by

\zeta(\mathbf{k})=\zeta(k_{1},\ldots,k_{n})=\sum\limits_{m_{1}>\cdots>m_{n}>0}\frac{1}{m_{1}^{k_{1}}\cdots m_{n}^{k_{n}}}.

It is obvious that
\zeta^{0}(\mathbf{k})=\zeta(\mathbf{k})
. And one can find that

\zeta^{1}(\mathbf{k})=\zeta^{\star}(\mathbf{k})=\zeta^{\star}(k_{1},\ldots,k_{n})=\sum\limits_{m_{1}\geq\cdots\geq m_{n}>0}\frac{1}{m_{1}^{k_{1}}\cdots m_{n}^{k_{n}}},

which is called the multiple zeta-star value (MZSV). See
[
5
,
11
,
13
,
7
]
for more details.

In recent years, an odd variant of multiple zeta(-star) values, known as the multiple
t
-(star) values, has been widely studied, see
[
1
,
9
,
10
]
. For an admissible index
\mathbf{k}=(k_{1},\ldots,k_{n})
, the multiple
t
-value (M
t
V) and the multiple
t
-star value (M
t
SV) are defined respectively as

t(\mathbf{k})=t(k_{1},\ldots,k_{n})=\sum\limits_{m_{1}>\cdots>m_{n}>0\atop m_{i}:\text{odd}}\frac{1}{m_{1}^{k_{1}}\cdots m_{n}^{k_{n}}}

and

t^{\star}(\mathbf{k})=t(k_{1},\ldots,k_{n})=\sum\limits_{m_{1}\geq\cdots\geq m_{n}>0\atop m_{i}:\text{odd}}\frac{1}{m_{1}^{k_{1}}\cdots m_{n}^{k_{n}}}.

Similarly, for an admissible index
\mathbf{k}=(k_{1},\ldots,k_{n})
, the interpolated multiple
t
-value (
r
-M
t
V) is defined in
[
2
]
by

\displaystyle t^{r}(\mathbf{k})=t^{r}(k_{1},\ldots,k_{n})=\sum\limits_{\mathbf{p}=(k_{1}\Box k_{2}\Box\cdots\Box k_{n})\atop\Box=``,"\text{or}``+"}r^{n-\operatorname{dep}(\mathbf{p})}t(\mathbf{p}).

It is easy to see that
t^{0}(\mathbf{k})=t(\mathbf{k})
and
t^{1}(\mathbf{k})=t^{\star}(\mathbf{k})
.

Multiple zeta(-star) values and multiple
t
-(star) values with indices involving
1,2,3
are widely studied. D. Zagier gave evaluation formulas for
\zeta(2,\ldots,2,3,2,\ldots,2)
and
\zeta^{\star}(2,\ldots,2,3,2,\ldots,2)
in
[
13
]
. The analogue evaluation formulas for
t(2,\ldots,2,3,2,\ldots,2)
and
t^{\star}(2,\ldots,2,3,2,\ldots,2)
were showed in
[
10
]
and
[
9
]
respectively. S. Charlton gave an evaluation formula for regularized multiple
t
-values
t^{V}_{\ast}(2,\ldots,2,1,2,\ldots,2)
in
[
1
]
and the corresponding formula for regularized multiple
t
-star values were showed in
[
9
]
.

In this paper, we discuss evaluation formulas of
r
-MZVs and
r
-M
t
Vs. In Section
2
, we use the harmonic algebra to derive some algebraic relations. As applications, we show some evaluation formulas of
r
-MZVs and
r
-M
t
Vs with indices involving
1,2,3
in Section
3
.

2
Harmonic algebra

2.1
Algebraic setup

Here we recall the harmonic algebra introduced in
[
5
,
11
]
.

Let
\mathfrak{A}
be a commutative
\mathbb{Q}
-algebra and
A
be an alphabet with non-commutative letters. Denote by
\mathfrak{h}^{1}
the non-commutative polynomial algebra generated by the set
A
over the algebra
\mathfrak{A}
, and
\mathfrak{z}
the
\mathfrak{A}
-submodule of
\mathfrak{h}^{1}
generated by
A
.

Assume the
\mathfrak{z}
admits a commutative
\mathfrak{A}
-algebra structure (not necessary unitary) given by a product
\circ
, which is called the circle product. Define an action of
\mathfrak{z}
on
\mathfrak{h}^{1}
by
\mathfrak{A}
-linearity and the rules:

\displaystyle a\circ 1_{w}=0\quad\text{and}\quad a\circ(bw)=(a\circ b)w,

where
1_{w}
is the empty word,
a,b\in A
and
w\in\mathfrak{h}^{1}
is a word. We denote
\overbrace{a\circ a\circ\cdots\circ a}^{n}
by
a^{\circ n}
for convenience.

The harmonic product
\ast
on
\mathfrak{h}^{1}
is defined by
\mathfrak{A}
-bilinearity and the rules:

\displaystyle 1_{w}\ast w=w\ast 1_{w}=w,

\displaystyle aw_{1}\ast bw_{2}=a(w_{1}\ast bw_{2})+b(aw_{1}\ast w_{2})+(a\circ b)(w_{1}\ast w_{2}),

for all
a,b\in A
and all words
w,w_{1},w_{2}\in\mathfrak{h}^{1}
. Under the harmonic product,
\mathfrak{h}^{1}
becomes a unitary commutative
\mathfrak{A}
-algebra which is called the harmonic algebra and is denoted by
\mathfrak{h}^{1}_{\ast}
.

Define an
\mathfrak{A}[r]
-linear map
\mathrm{S}^{r}
on
\mathfrak{h}^{1}[r]
by
\mathrm{S}^{r}(1_{w})=1
and

\displaystyle\mathrm{\mathrm{S}}^{r}(aw)=a\mathrm{S}^{r}(w)+ra\circ\mathrm{S}^{r}(w)

for all
a\in A
and any word
w\in\mathfrak{h}^{1}
.

Remark 2.1
.

Obviously,
\mathrm{S}^{0}
is the identity map on
\mathfrak{h}^{1}
, and
\mathrm{S}^{1}=\mathrm{S}
is the map in
[
5
, Definition 1]
. Also it is easy to see that

\displaystyle\mathrm{S}^{r}(a\circ w)=a\circ\mathrm{S}^{r}(w)

for all
a\in\mathfrak{z}
and
w\in\mathfrak{h}^{1}[r]
.

In the next subsection, we will deduce some formulas in the harmonic algebra
\mathfrak{h}^{1}_{\ast}
. For applications, we recall the special cases concerned with multiple zeta values and multiple
t
-values. Let
\mathfrak{A}=\mathbb{Q}
,
A=\{z_{k}\}_{k=1}^{\infty}
and

\mathfrak{h}^{0}=\mathbb{Q}\oplus\bigoplus\limits_{k=2}^{\infty}z_{k}\mathfrak{h}^{1}\subset\mathfrak{h}^{1}.

The circle product on
\mathfrak{z}
is defined by

\displaystyle z_{k}\circ z_{l}=z_{k+l},\quad\text{for any}\quad k,l\geq 1.

(2.1)

Then
\mathfrak{h}^{0}
is a subalgebra of
\mathfrak{h}^{1}_{\ast}
, which is denoted by
\mathfrak{h}^{0}_{\ast}
.

There are homomorphisms of
\mathbb{Q}
-algebras

\displaystyle\mathrm{\zeta}:\mathfrak{h}_{\ast}^{0}\to\mathbb{R},\quad\mathrm{t}:\mathfrak{h}_{\ast}^{0}\to\mathbb{R},

which are determined by
\zeta(1_{w})=1=\mathrm{t}(1_{w})
, and

\displaystyle\zeta(z_{k_{1}}\cdots z_{k_{n}})=\zeta(k_{1},\ldots,k_{n}),\quad\mathrm{t}(z_{k_{1}}\cdots z_{k_{n}})=t(k_{1},\ldots,k_{n}),

where
z_{k_{1}}\cdots z_{k_{n}}\in\mathfrak{h}^{0}
is a word. Similarly, we have homomorphisms of
\mathbb{Q}[r]
-algebras

\displaystyle\zeta^{r}:\mathfrak{h}_{\ast}^{0}\to\mathbb{R}[r],\quad\mathrm{t}^{r}:\mathfrak{h}_{\ast}^{0}\to\mathbb{R}[r],

such that
\zeta^{r}(1_{w})=1=\mathrm{t}^{r}(1_{w})
, and for any
z_{k_{1}}\cdots z_{k_{n}}\in\mathfrak{h}^{0}
,

\displaystyle\zeta^{r}(z_{k_{1}}\cdots z_{k_{n}})=\zeta^{r}(k_{1},\ldots,k_{n}),\quad\mathrm{t}^{r}(z_{k_{1}}\cdots z_{k_{n}})=t^{r}(k_{1},\ldots,k_{n}).

Then one can easily find that for any
w\in\mathfrak{h}^{0}
,

\displaystyle\zeta^{r}(w)=\zeta(\mathrm{S}^{r}(w))

and

\displaystyle\mathrm{t}^{r}(w)=\mathrm{t}(\mathrm{S}^{r}(w)).

The maps
\zeta
and
\mathrm{t}
can be extended to be homomorphisms of
\mathbb{Q}
-algebras

\displaystyle\mathrm{\zeta}^{T}_{\ast}:\mathfrak{h}_{\ast}^{1}\to\mathbb{R}[T],\quad\mathrm{t}^{V}_{\ast}:\mathfrak{h}_{\ast}^{1}\to\mathbb{R}[V],

such that
\mathrm{\zeta}^{T}_{\ast}(1_{w})=1=\mathrm{t}^{V}_{\ast}(1_{w})
,
\mathrm{\zeta}^{T}_{\ast}(z_{1})=T,\mathrm{t}^{V}_{\ast}(z_{1})=V
, and for any
z_{k_{1}}\cdots z_{k_{n}}\in\mathfrak{h}^{0}
,

\displaystyle\mathrm{\zeta}^{T}_{\ast}(z_{k_{1}}\cdots z_{k_{n}})=\zeta(k_{1},\ldots,k_{n}),\quad\mathrm{t}^{V}_{\ast}(z_{k_{1}}\cdots z_{k_{n}})=t(k_{1},\ldots,k_{n}).

Then for any index
(k_{1},\ldots,k_{n})
, one can define

\displaystyle\zeta^{T}_{\ast}(k_{1},\ldots,k_{n})=\mathrm{\zeta}^{T}_{\ast}(z_{k_{1}}\cdots z_{k_{n}}),\quad t^{V}_{\ast}(k_{1},\ldots,k_{n})=\mathrm{t}^{V}_{\ast}(z_{k_{1}}\cdots z_{k_{n}}),

which are called the harmonic regularized multiple zeta values and the harmonic regularized multiple
t
-values respectively. Hence we have
\zeta^{T}_{\ast}(1)=\mathrm{\zeta}_{\ast}^{T}(z_{1})=T
and
t^{V}_{\ast}(1)=\mathrm{t}_{\ast}^{V}(z_{1})=V
. For more details, see
[
1
,
8
,
6
]
.

Furthermore, there are homomorphisms of
\mathbb{Q}[r]
-algebras

\mathrm{\zeta}^{T,r}_{\ast}:\mathfrak{h}^{1}_{\ast}\to\mathbb{R}[T,r]
and
\mathrm{t}^{V,r}_{\ast}:\mathfrak{h}^{1}_{\ast}\to\mathbb{R}[V,r]
, such that for any
w\in\mathfrak{h}^{1}
,

\displaystyle\mathrm{\zeta}^{T,r}_{\ast}(w)=\mathrm{\zeta}^{T}_{\ast}(\mathrm{S}^{r}(w)),\quad\mathrm{t}^{V,r}_{\ast}(w)=\mathrm{t}^{V}_{\ast}(\mathrm{S}^{r}(w)).

Similarly, for any index
(k_{1},\ldots,k_{n})
, define

\displaystyle\zeta^{T,r}_{\ast}(k_{1},\ldots,k_{n})=\mathrm{\zeta}^{T,r}_{\ast}(z_{k_{1}}\cdots z_{k_{n}}),\quad t^{V,r}_{\ast}(k_{1},\ldots,k_{n})=\mathrm{t}^{V,r}_{\ast}(z_{k_{1}}\cdots z_{k_{n}}),

which are called the harmonic regularized interpolated multiple zeta values and the harmonic regularized interpolated multiple
t
-values respectively.

Moreover, we denote
\zeta^{T,1}_{\ast}(k_{1},\ldots,k_{n})
by
\zeta^{T,\star}_{\ast}(k_{1},\ldots,k_{n})
, and call them the harmonic regularized multiple zeta-star values. In a similar way, we call
t^{V,1}_{\ast}(k_{1},\ldots,k_{n})=t^{V,\star}_{\ast}(k_{1},\ldots,k_{n})
the harmonic regularized multiple
t
-star values.

2.2
Algebraic relations

We give the following lemma which generalizes
[
8
, Lemma 4.5]
.

Lemma 2.2
.

For any
a_{1},a_{2},\ldots,a_{n}\in\mathfrak{z}
, we have

\displaystyle\mathrm{S}^{r}(a_{1}a_{2}\cdots a_{n})=\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}r^{i-1}(a_{1}\circ a_{2}\circ\cdots\circ a_{i})\mathrm{S}^{r}(a_{i+1}\cdots a_{n}).

(2.2)

In particular, for any positive integer
n
and any
a\in\mathfrak{z}
, we have

\displaystyle\mathrm{S}^{r}(a^{n})=\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}r^{i-1}a^{\circ i}\mathrm{S}^{r}(a^{n-i}).

(2.3)

Proof.
We prove (
2.2
) by induction on
n
. The case of
n=1
is trivial. Assume that
n\geq 2
, since

\displaystyle\mathrm{S}^{r}(a_{1}a_{2}\cdots a_{n})

\displaystyle=a_{1}\mathrm{S}^{r}(a_{2}\cdots a_{n})+ra_{1}\circ\mathrm{S}^{r}(a_{2}\cdots a_{n})

\displaystyle=a_{1}\mathrm{S}^{r}(a_{2}\cdots a_{n})+ra_{1}\circ\sum\limits_{i=2}^{n}r^{i-2}(a_{2}\circ\cdots\circ a_{i})\mathrm{S}^{r}(a_{i+1}\cdots a_{n})

\displaystyle=a_{1}\mathrm{S}^{r}(a_{2}\cdots a_{n})+\sum\limits_{i=2}^{n}r^{i-1}(a_{1}\circ a_{2}\circ\cdots\circ a_{i})\mathrm{S}^{r}(a_{i+1}\cdots a_{n}),

we finish the proof.
∎

The following proposition generalizes
[
4
, Corollary 5.5]
.

Proposition 2.3
.

Let
u
be a formal variable. For any
a\in\mathfrak{z}
, we have

\displaystyle\mathrm{S}^{r}\left(\frac{1}{1-au}\right)=\frac{1}{1-a(1-r)u}\ast\mathrm{S}\left(\frac{1}{1-aru}\right),

(2.4)

or equivalently, for any positive integer
n
, we have

\displaystyle\mathrm{S}^{r}(a^{n})=\sum\limits_{i=0}^{n}r^{n-i}(1-r)^{i}a^{i}\ast\mathrm{S}(a^{n-i}).

(2.5)

Proof.
We prove (
2.5
) by induction on
n
. It is obvious that (
2.5
) holds for
n=1
. Then assume that
n\geq 2
. Using (
2.3
) with
r=1
, the right hand side of (
2.5
) is

\displaystyle(1-r)^{n}a^{n}+r^{n}\mathrm{S}(a^{n})+\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n-1}\sum\limits_{j=1}^{i}r^{i}(1-r)^{n-i}a^{n-i}\ast a^{\circ j}\mathrm{S}(a^{i-j}),

which is
r^{n}\mathrm{S}(a^{n})+R_{1}+R_{2}+R_{3}
, where

\displaystyle R_{1}

\displaystyle=(1-r)^{n}a^{n}+a\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n-1}\sum\limits_{j=1}^{i}r^{i}(1-r)^{n-i}a^{n-i-1}\ast a^{\circ j}\mathrm{S}(a^{i-j}),

\displaystyle R_{2}

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n-1}\sum\limits_{j=1}^{i}r^{i}(1-r)^{n-i}a^{\circ j}(a^{n-i}\ast\mathrm{S}(a^{i-j})),

\displaystyle R_{3}

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n-1}\sum\limits_{j=1}^{i}r^{i}(1-r)^{n-i}a^{\circ(j+1)}(a^{n-i-1}\ast\mathrm{S}(a^{i-j})).

For
R_{1}
, we have

\displaystyle R_{1}=(1-r)^{n}a^{n}+a\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n-1}r^{i}(1-r)^{n-i}a^{n-i-1}\ast\mathrm{S}(a^{i}),

which is
(1-r)a\mathrm{S}^{r}(a^{n-1})
by the inductive hypothesis.
For
R_{2}
, by changing the order of summation, we have

\displaystyle R_{2}

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{j=1}^{n-1}r^{j}a^{\circ j}\sum\limits_{i=j}^{n-1}r^{i-j}(1-r)^{n-i}a^{n-i}\ast\mathrm{S}(a^{i-j})

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{j=1}^{n-1}r^{j}a^{\circ j}\sum\limits_{i=0}^{n-j-1}r^{i}(1-r)^{n-i-j}a^{n-i-j}\ast\mathrm{S}(a^{i}).

Using the inductive hypothesis, we have

\displaystyle R_{2}

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{j=1}^{n-1}r^{j}a^{\circ j}(\mathrm{S}^{r}(a^{n-j})-r^{n-j}\mathrm{S}(a^{n-j}))

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{j=1}^{n-1}r^{j}a^{\circ j}\mathrm{S}^{r}(a^{n-j})-\sum\limits_{j=1}^{n-1}r^{n}a^{\circ j}\mathrm{S}(a^{n-j}).

Similarly, for
R_{3}
, we get

\displaystyle R_{3}

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{j=1}^{n-1}r^{j}a^{\circ(j+1)}\sum\limits_{i=j}^{n-1}r^{i-j}(1-r)^{n-i}a^{n-i-1}\ast\mathrm{S}(a^{i-j})

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{j=1}^{n-1}(1-r)r^{j}a^{\circ(j+1)}\mathrm{S}^{r}(a^{n-j-1})

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{j=2}^{n}r^{j-1}a^{\circ j}\mathrm{S}^{r}(a^{n-j})-\sum\limits_{j=2}^{n}r^{j}a^{\circ j}\mathrm{S}^{r}(a^{n-j}).

Therefore, the right hand side of (
2.5
) is

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{j=1}^{n}r^{j-1}a^{\circ j}\mathrm{S}^{r}(a^{n-j}),

which is
\mathrm{S}^{r}(a^{n})
by (
2.3
).
∎

We may regard (
2.4
) as a generalization of

\displaystyle\mathrm{S}\left(\frac{1}{1-au}\right)\ast\frac{1}{1+au}=1,

(2.6)

which was proved in
[
5
, Corollary 1]
. For a positive integer
k\geq 2
, let
a=z_{k}
and the circle product follows (
2.1
). By applying the evaluation maps
\zeta
and
\mathrm{t}
to (
2.6
), we get

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{i=0}^{n}(-1)^{i}\zeta(\{k\}^{i})\zeta^{\star}(\{k\}^{n-i})=\sum\limits_{i=0}^{n}(-1)^{i}t(\{k\}^{i})t^{\star}(\{k\}^{n-i})=\begin{cases}1&n=0,\\
0&n\geq 1.\end{cases}

(2.7)

Also, by applying the evaluation maps
\zeta
and
\mathrm{t}
to (
2.4
), we have

\displaystyle\zeta^{r}(\{k\}^{n})

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{i=0}^{n}r^{n-i}(1-r)^{i}\zeta(\{k\}^{i})\zeta^{\star}(\{k\}^{n-i}),

(2.8)

\displaystyle t^{r}(\{k\}^{n})

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{i=0}^{n}r^{n-i}(1-r)^{i}t(\{k\}^{i})t^{\star}(\{k\}^{n-i}).

(2.9)

The following two lemmas can be deduced from
[
7
, Theorem 2.2]
.

Lemma 2.4
.

Let
u
be a formal variable. For any
a_{1},a_{2}\in\mathfrak{z}
, we have

\displaystyle\mathrm{S}\left(\frac{1}{1-a_{1}u}a_{2}\right)

\displaystyle=a_{2}\frac{1}{1+a_{1}u}\ast\mathrm{S}\left(\frac{1}{1-a_{1}u}\right),

or equivalently, for any positive integer
p
, we have

\displaystyle\mathrm{S}(a_{1}^{p}a_{2})

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{i=0}^{p}(-1)^{i}a_{2}a_{1}^{i}\ast\mathrm{S}(a_{1}^{p-i}).

(2.10)

Lemma 2.5
.

Let
u
be a formal variable. For any
a_{1},a_{2}\in\mathfrak{z}
, we have

\displaystyle\mathrm{S}\left(a_{1}\frac{1}{1-a_{2}u}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{1+a_{2}u}a_{1}\ast\mathrm{S}\left(\frac{1}{1-a_{2}u}\right),

or equivalently, for any positive integer
q
, we have

\displaystyle\mathrm{S}(a_{1}a_{2}^{q})

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{i=0}^{q}(-1)^{i}a_{2}^{i}a_{1}\ast\mathrm{S}(a_{2}^{q-i}).

We have the following generalized results.

Proposition 2.6
.

Let
u
be a formal variable. For any
a_{1},a_{2}\in\mathfrak{z}
, we have

\displaystyle\mathrm{S}^{r}\left(\frac{1}{1-a_{1}u}a_{2}\right)=\frac{1}{1-a_{1}(1-r)u}a_{2}\frac{1}{1+a_{1}ru}\ast\mathrm{S}\left(\frac{1}{1-a_{1}ru}\right),

or equivalently, for any positive integer
p
, we have

\displaystyle\mathrm{S}^{r}(a_{1}^{p}a_{2})=\sum\limits_{i+j=0\atop i,j\geq 0}^{p}(-1)^{j}r^{p-i}(1-r)^{i}a_{1}^{i}a_{2}a_{1}^{j}\ast\mathrm{S}(a_{1}^{p-i-j}).

(2.11)

Proof.
We prove (
2.11
) by induction on
p
. By direct calculation, it is obvious that (
2.11
) holds when
p=1
. Assume that
p\geq 2
. The right hand side of (
2.11
) is equal to

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{i=0}^{p}(-1)^{p-i}r^{p-i}(1-r)^{i}a_{1}^{i}a_{2}a_{1}^{p-i}+\sum\limits_{j=0}^{p-1}(-1)^{j}r^{p}a_{2}a_{1}^{j}\ast\mathrm{S}(a_{1}^{p-j})

\displaystyle+\sum\limits_{i=1}^{p-1}\sum\limits_{j=0}^{p-i-1}(-1)^{j}r^{p-i}(1-r)^{i}a_{1}^{i}a_{2}a_{1}^{j}\ast\mathrm{S}(a_{1}^{p-i-j}).

Using (
2.3
) with
r=1
and (
2.10
), the right hand side of (
2.11
) becomes

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{i=0}^{p}(-1)^{p-i}r^{p-i}(1-r)^{i}a_{1}^{i}a_{2}a_{1}^{p-i}+r^{p}\mathrm{S}(a_{1}^{p}a_{2})+(-1)^{p-1}r^{p}a_{2}a_{1}^{p}+Q_{1}+Q_{2}+Q_{3},

where

\displaystyle Q_{1}

\displaystyle=a_{1}\sum\limits_{i=1}^{p-1}\sum\limits_{j=0}^{p-i-1}\sum\limits_{n=1}^{p-i-j}(-1)^{j}r^{p-i}(1-r)^{i}a_{1}^{i-1}a_{2}a_{1}^{j}\ast a_{1}^{\circ n}\mathrm{S}(a_{1}^{p-i-j-n}),

\displaystyle Q_{2}

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{i=1}^{p-1}\sum\limits_{j=0}^{p-i-1}\sum\limits_{n=1}^{p-i-j}(-1)^{j}r^{p-i}(1-r)^{i}a_{1}^{\circ n}(a_{1}^{i}a_{2}a_{1}^{j}\ast\mathrm{S}(a_{1}^{p-i-j-n})),

\displaystyle Q_{3}

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{i=1}^{p-1}\sum\limits_{j=0}^{p-i-1}\sum\limits_{n=1}^{p-i-j}(-1)^{j}r^{p-i}(1-r)^{i}a_{1}^{\circ(n+1)}(a_{1}^{i-1}a_{2}a_{1}^{j}\ast\mathrm{S}(a_{1}^{p-i-j-n})).

For
Q_{1}
, we have

\displaystyle Q_{1}

\displaystyle=a_{1}\sum\limits_{i=1}^{p-1}\sum\limits_{j=0}^{p-i-1}(-1)^{j}r^{p-i}(1-r)^{i}a_{1}^{i-1}a_{2}a_{1}^{j}\ast\mathrm{S}(a_{1}^{p-i-j})

\displaystyle=a_{1}\sum\limits_{i+j=0\atop i,j\geq 0}^{p-2}(-1)^{j}r^{p-i-1}(1-r)^{i+1}a_{1}^{i}a_{2}a_{1}^{j}\ast\mathrm{S}(a_{1}^{p-i-j-1}).

According to the inductive hypothesis, we get

\displaystyle Q_{1}

\displaystyle=(1-r)a_{1}\mathrm{S}^{r}(a_{1}^{p-1}a_{2})-\sum\limits_{i=1}^{p}(-1)^{p-i}r^{p-i}(1-r)^{i}a_{1}^{i}a_{2}a_{1}^{p-i}.

For
Q_{2}
, changing the order of summation, we have

\displaystyle Q_{2}

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{n=1}^{p-1}r^{n}a_{1}^{\circ n}\sum\limits_{i=1}^{p-n}\sum\limits_{j=0}^{p-n-i}(-1)^{j}r^{p-n-i}(1-r)^{i}a_{1}^{i}a_{2}a_{1}^{j}\ast\mathrm{S}(a_{1}^{p-i-j-n}).

Using the inductive hypothesis and (
2.10
), we get

\displaystyle Q_{2}

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{n=1}^{p-1}r^{n}a_{1}^{\circ n}\left(\mathrm{S}^{r}(a_{1}^{p-n}a_{2})-\sum\limits_{j=0}^{p-n}(-1)^{j}r^{p-n}a_{2}a_{1}^{j}\ast\mathrm{S}(a_{1}^{p-n-j})\right)

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{n=1}^{p-1}r^{n}a_{1}^{\circ n}\mathrm{S}^{r}(a_{1}^{p-n}a_{2})-\sum\limits_{n=1}^{p-1}r^{p}a_{1}^{\circ n}\sum\limits_{j=0}^{p-n}(-1)^{j}a_{2}a_{1}^{j}\ast\mathrm{S}(a_{1}^{p-n-j})

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{n=1}^{p-1}r^{n}a_{1}^{\circ n}\mathrm{S}^{r}(a_{1}^{p-n}a_{2})-\sum\limits_{n=1}^{p-1}r^{p}a_{1}^{\circ n}\mathrm{S}(a_{1}^{p-n}a_{2}).

Similarly, for
Q_{3}
, we have

\displaystyle Q_{3}

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{n=1}^{p-1}r^{n}a_{1}^{\circ(n+1)}\sum\limits_{i=1}^{p-n}\sum\limits_{j=0}^{p-n-i}(-1)^{j}r^{p-n-i}(1-r)^{i}a_{1}^{i-1}a_{2}a_{1}^{j}\ast\mathrm{S}(a_{1}^{p-i-j-n})

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{n=1}^{p-1}(1-r)r^{n}a_{1}^{\circ(n+1)}\mathrm{S}^{r}(a_{1}^{p-n-1}a_{2})

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{n=2}^{p}r^{n-1}a_{1}^{\circ n}\mathrm{S}^{r}(a_{1}^{p-n}a_{2})-\sum\limits_{n=2}^{p}r^{n}a_{1}^{\circ n}\mathrm{S}^{r}(a_{1}^{p-n}a_{2}).

Hence, the right hand side of (
2.11
) is

\displaystyle r^{p}\mathrm{S}(a_{1}^{p}a_{2})-r^{p}\sum\limits_{n=1}^{p}a_{1}^{\circ n}\mathrm{S}(a_{1}^{p-n}a_{2})+\sum\limits_{n=1}^{p}r^{n-1}a_{1}^{\circ n}\mathrm{S}^{r}(a_{1}^{p-n}a_{2}),

which is
\mathrm{S}^{r}(a_{1}^{p}a_{2})
by (
2.2
).
∎

Proposition 2.7
.

Let
u
be a formal variable. For any
a_{1},a_{2}\in\mathfrak{z}
, we have

\displaystyle\mathrm{S}^{r}\left(a_{1}\frac{1}{1-a_{2}u}\right)=\frac{1}{1+a_{2}ru}a_{1}\frac{1}{1-a_{2}(1-r)u}\ast\mathrm{S}\left(\frac{1}{1-a_{2}ru}\right),

or equivalently, for any positive integer
q
, we have

\displaystyle\mathrm{S}^{r}(a_{1}a_{2}^{q})=\sum\limits_{i+j=0\atop i,j\geq 0}^{q}(-1)^{i}r^{q-j}(1-r)^{j}a_{2}^{i}a_{1}a_{2}^{j}\ast\mathrm{S}(a_{2}^{q-i-j}).

(2.12)

Proof.
We prove (
2.12
) by induction on
q
. The case of
q=1
holds by direct calculation. Assume that
q\geq 2
. By using (
2.3
) with
r=1
, the right hand side of (
2.12
) is

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{i=0}^{q}(-1)^{i}r^{i}(1-r)^{q-i}a_{2}^{i}a_{1}a_{2}^{q-i}+\sum\limits_{j=0}^{q-1}r^{q-j}(1-r)^{j}a_{1}a_{2}^{j}\ast\mathrm{S}(a_{2}^{q-j})+T_{1}+T_{2}+T_{3},

where

\displaystyle T_{1}

\displaystyle=a_{2}\sum\limits_{i=1}^{q-1}\sum\limits_{j=0}^{q-i-1}\sum\limits_{n=1}^{q-i-j}(-1)^{i}r^{q-j}(1-r)^{j}a_{2}^{i-1}a_{1}a_{2}^{j}\ast a_{2}^{\circ n}\mathrm{S}(a_{2}^{q-i-j-n}),

\displaystyle T_{2}

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{i=1}^{q-1}\sum\limits_{j=0}^{q-i-1}\sum\limits_{n=1}^{q-i-j}(-1)^{i}r^{q-j}(1-r)^{j}a_{2}^{\circ n}(a_{2}^{i}a_{1}a_{2}^{j}\ast\mathrm{S}(a_{2}^{q-i-j-n})),

\displaystyle T_{3}

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{i=1}^{q-1}\sum\limits_{j=0}^{q-i-1}\sum\limits_{n=1}^{q-i-j}(-1)^{i}r^{q-j}(1-r)^{j}a_{2}^{\circ(n+1)}(a_{2}^{i-1}a_{1}a_{2}^{j}\ast\mathrm{S}(a_{2}^{q-i-j-n})).

For
T_{1}
, we have

\displaystyle T_{1}

\displaystyle=a_{2}\sum\limits_{i=1}^{q-1}\sum\limits_{j=0}^{q-i-1}(-1)^{i}r^{q-j}(1-r)^{j}a_{2}^{i-1}a_{1}a_{2}^{j}\ast\mathrm{S}(a_{2}^{q-i-j})

\displaystyle=-a_{2}\sum\limits_{i+j=0\atop i,j\geq 0}^{q-2}(-1)^{i}r^{q-j}(1-r)^{j}a_{2}^{i}a_{1}a_{2}^{j}\ast\mathrm{S}(a_{2}^{q-i-j-1}).

Then by the inductive hypothesis, we get

\displaystyle T_{1}

\displaystyle=-ra_{2}\mathrm{S}^{r}(a_{1}a_{2}^{q-1})-\sum\limits_{i=1}^{q}(-1)^{i}r^{i}(1-r)^{q-i}a_{2}^{i}a_{1}a_{2}^{q-i}.

For
T_{2}
, changing the order of the summation, we have

\displaystyle T_{2}

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{n=1}^{q-1}r^{n}a_{2}^{\circ n}\sum\limits_{i=1}^{q-n}\sum\limits_{j=0}^{q-i-n}(-1)^{i}r^{q-n-j}(1-r)^{j}a_{2}^{i}a_{1}a_{2}^{j}\ast\mathrm{S}(a_{2}^{q-n-i-j})

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{n=1}^{q-1}r^{n}a_{2}^{\circ n}\mathrm{S}^{r}(a_{1}a_{2}^{q-n})-\sum\limits_{n=1}^{q-1}r^{n}a_{2}^{\circ n}\sum\limits_{j=0}^{q-n}r^{q-n-j}(1-r)^{j}a_{1}a_{2}^{j}\ast\mathrm{S}(a_{2}^{q-n-j}).

Similarly, for
T_{3}
, we get

\displaystyle T_{3}

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{n=1}^{q-1}r^{n+1}a_{2}^{\circ(n+1)}\sum\limits_{i=1}^{q-n}\sum\limits_{j=0}^{q-n-i}(-1)^{i}r^{q-n-j-1}(1-r)^{j}a_{2}^{i-1}a_{1}a_{2}^{j}\ast\mathrm{S}(a_{2}^{q-n-i-j})

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{n=1}^{q-1}r^{n+1}a_{2}^{\circ(n+1)}\sum\limits_{i=0}^{q-n-1}\sum\limits_{j=0}^{q-n-i-1}(-1)^{i-1}r^{q-n-j-1}(1-r)^{j}a_{2}^{i}a_{1}a_{2}^{j}\ast\mathrm{S}(a_{2}^{q-n-i-j-1})

\displaystyle=-\sum\limits_{n=2}^{q}r^{n}a_{2}^{\circ n}\mathrm{S}^{r}(a_{1}a_{2}^{q-n}).

Therefore, the right hand side of (
2.12
) becomes

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{j=0}^{q}r^{q-j}(1-r)^{j}a_{1}a_{2}^{j}\ast\mathrm{S}(a_{2}^{q-j})-\sum\limits_{n=1}^{q}\sum\limits_{j=0}^{q-n}r^{q-j}(1-r)^{j}a_{2}^{\circ n}(a_{1}a_{2}^{j}\ast\mathrm{S}(a_{2}^{q-n-j}))

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{j=0}^{q}\sum\limits_{n=1}^{q-j}r^{q-j}(1-r)^{j}\left\{a_{1}(a_{2}^{j}\ast a_{2}^{\circ n}\mathrm{S}(a_{2}^{q-n-j}))+(a_{1}\circ a_{2}^{\circ n})(a_{2}^{j}\ast\mathrm{S}(a_{2}^{q-n-j})\right\}.

Then by (
2.3
), (
2.5
) and the inductive hypothesis, we find that the right hand side of (
2.12
) is equal to

\displaystyle a_{1}\mathrm{S}^{r}({a_{2}^{q}})+\sum\limits_{n=1}^{q}r^{n}(a_{1}\circ a_{2}^{\circ n})\mathrm{S}(a_{2}^{q-n}),

which is
\mathrm{S}^{r}(a_{1}a_{2}^{q})
.
∎

For any positive integer
n
, we give the following notations

\displaystyle\Phi_{r}^{n}

\displaystyle=\frac{2(r-1)^{n}-(\sqrt{r-1}+\sqrt{r})^{2n}-(\sqrt{r-1}-\sqrt{r})^{2n}}{2r},

\displaystyle\Psi_{r}^{n}

\displaystyle=\frac{2r^{n}-(\sqrt{r-1}+\sqrt{r})^{2n}-(\sqrt{r-1}-\sqrt{r})^{2n}}{2(r-1)},

\displaystyle\Theta_{r}^{n}

\displaystyle=\frac{(\sqrt{r-1}+\sqrt{r})^{2n}-(\sqrt{r-1}-\sqrt{r})^{2n}}{2\sqrt{r(r-1)}},

\displaystyle\Omega_{r}^{n}

\displaystyle=\frac{(\sqrt{r-1}+\sqrt{r})^{2n}+(\sqrt{r-1}-\sqrt{r})^{2n}}{2}.

Considering the interpolated multiple zeta values with indices involving 2 and 3, we get the following theorem.

Theorem 3.1
.

For any nonnegative integers
p
and
q
, we have

\displaystyle\zeta^{r}(\{2\}^{p},3)

\displaystyle=2\sum\limits_{n=0}^{p}\Phi_{r}^{n+1}\zeta^{r}(\{2\}^{p-n})\zeta(2n+3)+2(1-2^{-2p-2})\Theta_{r}^{p+1}\zeta(2p+3),

(3.1)

\displaystyle\zeta^{r}(3,\{2\}^{q})

\displaystyle=2\sum\limits_{n=0}^{q}(1-2^{-2n-2})\Theta_{r}^{n+1}\zeta^{r}(\{2\}^{q-n})\zeta(2n+3)+2\Psi_{r}^{q+1}\zeta(2q+3).

(3.2)

Proof.
We prove (
3.1
). It is obvious that (
3.1
) holds when
p=0
. For
p\geq 1
, setting
a_{1}=z_{2}
and
a_{2}=z_{3}
in (
2.11
), and applying the map
\zeta

\displaystyle\zeta^{r}(\{2\}^{p},3)=\sum\limits_{i+j=0\atop i,j\geq 0}^{p}(-1)^{j}r^{p-i}(1-r)^{i}\zeta(\{2\}^{i},3,\{2\}^{j})\zeta^{\star}(\{2\}^{p-i-j}).

The following evaluation formula was showed in
[
13

\displaystyle\zeta(\{2\}^{i},3,\{2\}^{j})

\displaystyle=2\sum\limits_{n=1}^{i+j+1}(-1)^{n}\left\{\binom{2n}{2j+2}-(1-2^{-2n})\binom{2n}{2i+1}\right\}\zeta(\{2\}^{i+j-n+1})\zeta(2n+1).

(3.3)

Then we have

\displaystyle\zeta^{r}(\{2\}^{p},3)=2\sum\limits_{n=1}^{p+1}(-1)^{n}\zeta(2n+1)B_{1}(n,p)+2\sum\limits_{n=1}^{p+1}(-1)^{n}(1-2^{-2n})\zeta(2n+1)B_{2}(n,p),

where

\displaystyle B_{1}(n,p)

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{i+j=n-1\atop i,j\geq 0}^{p}(-1)^{j}r^{p-i}(1-r)^{i}\binom{2n}{2j+2}\zeta(\{2\}^{i+j-n+1})\zeta^{\star}(\{2\}^{p-i-j}),

\displaystyle B_{2}(n,p)

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{i+j=n-1\atop i,j\geq 0}^{p}(-1)^{j-1}r^{p-i}(1-r)^{i}\binom{2n}{2i+1}\zeta(\{2\}^{i+j-n+1})\zeta^{\star}(\{2\}^{p-i-j}).

For
B_{1}(n,p)
, we have

\displaystyle B_{1}(n,p)

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{j=0}^{n-1}\sum\limits_{i=n-1-j}^{p-j}(-1)^{j}r^{p-i}(1-r)^{i}\binom{2n}{2j+2}\zeta(\{2\}^{i+j-n+1})\zeta^{\star}(\{2\}^{p-i-j})

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{j=0}^{n-1}(-1)^{j}r^{j}(1-r)^{n-j-1}\binom{2n}{2j+2}\sum\limits_{i=0}^{p-n+1}r^{p-n+1-i}(1-r)^{i}\zeta(\{2\}^{i})\zeta^{\star}(\{2\}^{p-n+1-i}).

Using (
2.8
), we have

\displaystyle B_{1}(n,p)=\sum\limits_{j=0}^{n-1}(-1)^{j}r^{j}(1-r)^{n-1-j}\binom{2n}{2j+2}\zeta^{r}(\{2\}^{p-n+1}).

Using the combinatorial identity
[
3
, (1.87)]

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{k=0}^{n}\binom{2n}{2k}x^{k}=\frac{(1+\sqrt{x})^{2n}+(1-\sqrt{x})^{2n}}{2}\quad\text{for}\quad n\geq 0,

(3.4)

we get

\displaystyle B_{1}(n,p)=(-1)^{n}\Phi_{r}^{n}\zeta^{r}(\{2\}^{p-n+1}).

For
B_{2}(n,p)
, we have

\displaystyle B_{2}(n,p)

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{i=0}^{n-1}\sum\limits_{j=n-1-i}^{p-i}(-1)^{j-1}r^{p-i}(1-r)^{i}\binom{2n}{2i+1}\zeta(\{2\}^{i+j-n+1})\zeta^{\star}(\{2\}^{p-i-j})

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{i=0}^{n-1}(-1)^{n-i}r^{p-i}(1-r)^{i}\binom{2n}{2i+1}\sum\limits_{j=0}^{p-n+1}(-1)^{j}\zeta(\{2\}^{j})\zeta^{\star}(\{2\}^{p-n+1-j}).

Using (
2.7
) and the combinatorial identity
[
3

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{k=0}^{n-1}\binom{2n}{2k+1}x^{k}=\frac{(1+\sqrt{x})^{2n}-(1-\sqrt{x})^{2n}}{2\sqrt{x}}\quad\text{for}\quad n\geq 1,

(3.5)

we get

\displaystyle B_{2}(n,p)=\begin{cases}(-1)^{p-1}\Theta_{r}^{p+1}&n=p+1,\\
0&1\leq n\leq p.\end{cases}

Therefore, we have

\displaystyle\zeta^{r}(\{2\}^{p},3)=2\sum\limits_{n=1}^{p+1}\zeta(2n+1)\Phi_{r}^{n}\zeta^{r}(\{2\}^{p-n+1})+2(1-2^{-2p-2})\Theta_{r}^{p+1}\zeta(2p+3),

which completes the proof.
∎

3

Corollary 3.2
.

For any nonnegative integers

\displaystyle\zeta^{\frac{1}{2}}(\{2\}^{p},3)

\displaystyle\quad+4(1-2^{-2p-2})\cos\frac{p\pi}{2}\zeta(2p+3),

\displaystyle\zeta^{\frac{1}{2}}(3,\{2\}^{q})

\displaystyle=4\sum\limits_{n=0}^{q}(1-2^{-2n-2})\cos\frac{n\pi}{2}\zeta^{\frac{1}{2}}(\{2\}^{q-n})\zeta(2n+3)

\displaystyle\quad-4\left(2^{-q-1}+\sin\frac{q\pi}{2}\right)\zeta(2q+3).

Proof.

In the following, we consider the harmonic regularized multiple zeta values with indices involving 1 and 2. We denote
\delta_{a,b}
the Kronecker symbol. First, we give the following lemma.

Lemma 3.3
.

For any nonnegative integers
p
and

\displaystyle\zeta_{\ast}^{T}(\{2\}^{p},1,\{2\}^{q})

\displaystyle=2\sum\limits_{n=1}^{p+q}(-1)^{n}\left\{\binom{2n}{2p}-(1-2^{-2n})\binom{2n}{2q+1}\right\}\zeta(\{2\}^{p+q-n})\zeta(2n+1)+\delta_{p,0}T\zeta(\{2\}^{q}).

(3.7)

Proof.
If

, by the duality formula (see
[
12
]

\displaystyle\zeta(\{2\}^{p},1,\{2\}^{q})=\zeta(\{2\}^{q},3,\{2\}^{q-1}),

we get (
3.6
) from (
3

\displaystyle\zeta^{T}_{\ast}(1,\{2\}^{q})

\displaystyle=T\zeta(\{2\}^{q})-\sum\limits_{i=1}^{q}\zeta(\{2\}^{i},1,\{2\}^{q-i})-\sum\limits_{i=0}^{q-1}\zeta(\{2\}^{i},3,\{2\}^{q-1-i})

\displaystyle=T\zeta(\{2\}^{q})-4\sum\limits_{n=1}^{q}(-1)^{n}\sum\limits_{i=1}^{q}\left(\binom{2n}{2i}-(1-2^{-2n})\binom{2n}{2q-2i+1}\right)\zeta(\{2\}^{q-n})\zeta(2n+1).

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{i=1}^{q}\left\{\binom{2n}{2i}-(1-2^{-2n})\binom{2n}{2q-2i+1}\right\}=2^{2n-1}-1-(1-2^{-2n})2^{2n-1}=-\frac{1}{2},

\displaystyle\zeta^{T}_{\ast}(1,\{2\}^{q})

Then we get evaluations for the harmonic regularized interpolated multiple zeta values.

Theorem 3.4

Corollary 3.5

For any nonnegative integers

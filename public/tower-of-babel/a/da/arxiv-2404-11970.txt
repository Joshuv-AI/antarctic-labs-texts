More on the (Uniform) Mazur Intersection Property
Deepak Gothwal

Abstract
. In this paper, we introduce two moduli of w*-semidenting points and characterise the Mazur Intersection Property (MIP) and the Uniform MIP (UMIP) in terms of these moduli. We show that a property slightly stronger than UMIP already implies uniform convexity of the dual. This may lead to a possible approach towards answering the long standing open question whether the UMIP implies the existence of an equivalent uniformly convex renorming. We also obtain the condition for stability of the UMIP under ℓ p \ell_{p} -sums.

46B20

1.
Introduction

The Mazur Intersection Property (MIP) has been an extensive area of study. Defined as:

A Banach space
X
is said to have the MIP if every closed bounded convex set in
X
is the intersection of the closed balls containing it,

the property brings more features to the structure of the Banach space. For instance, Giles, Gregory and Sims in
[
7
]
showed that
X
has the MIP if and only if the set of the w*-denting points of the unit ball
B(X^{*})
of
X^{*}
is dense in the unit sphere
S(X^{*})
. This initiated a great lead to a lot of analysis on the behaviour of a Banach space with the MIP.

But the uniform version of the MIP (UMIP) didn’t get much attention since its introduction by Whitfield and Zizler in
[
9
]
. It was defined as:

X
has the UMIP if for every
\varepsilon>0
, there exists
K>0
such that for every closed bounded and convex set
C\subseteq X
and
p\in X
with
diam(C)<1/\varepsilon
and
d(p,C)\geq\varepsilon
, there is a closed ball
B[x_{0},r_{0}]
in
X
such that
C\subseteq B[x_{0},r_{0}]
,
d(p,B[x_{0},r_{0}])\geq\varepsilon/2
and
r_{0}\leq K
.

In our earlier paper
[
1
]
, we characterised the UMIP in terms of uniform w*-semidenting points, thus filling a long felt gap. In this paper, we continue our investigation of Banach spaces with the UMIP. We introduce two moduli of w*-semidenting points and characterise the MIP and UMIP in terms of these moduli.

One of the moduli is motivated by the modulus of denting points discussed by Dutta and Lin in
[
6
]
. They have already observed that their notion of uniform denting implies the existence of an equivalent uniformly convex renorming. We improve this result and show that
X^{*}
is uniformly w*-denting actually implies
X^{*}
is uniformly convex. Using this, we introduce a property slightly stronger than the UMIP (uniform version of the property “
\mathcal{P}=\mathcal{H}
” discussed in
[
8
]
) which characterises the uniform convexity of
X^{*}
. This may lead to a possible approach towards answering the long standing open question whether the UMIP implies the existence of an equivalent uniformly convex renorming of
X^{*}
.

Using the second modulus, we obtain a condition for stability of the UMIP under
\ell_{p}
-sums.

2.
Notations and Preliminaries

Throughout this article,
X
is a real Banach space. For
x\in X
and
r>0
, we denote by
B(x,r)

the open ball

\{y\in X:\|x-y\|<r\}
and by
B[x,r]

the closed ball

\{y\in X:\|x-y\|\leq r\}
. We denote by
B(X)
the
closed unit ball

\{x\in X:\|x\|\leq 1\}
and by
S(X)
the
unit sphere

\{x\in X:\|x\|=1\}
.

For
x\in S(X)
, we denote by
D(x)
the set
\{f\in S(X^{*}):f(x)=1\}
. Any selection of
D
is called a support mapping.

Definition 2.1
.

A
slice
of
B(X)
determined by
f\in S(X^{*})
is a set of the form

S(B(X),f,\alpha):=\{x\in B(X):f(x)>\alpha\}

for some
0<\alpha<1
.

For
x\in S(X)
,
S(B(X^{*}),x,\alpha)
is called a w*-slice of
B(X^{*})
.

We say that
x\in S(X)
is a
denting point
of
B(X)
if
for every
\varepsilon>0
,
x
is contained in a slice of
B(X)

of diameter less than
\varepsilon
.

A w*-denting point of
B(X^{*})
is defined similarly.

Remark 2.2
.

Note that a more common notation for a slice is

S(B(X),f,\alpha):=\{x\in B(X):f(x)>1-\alpha\}.

However, the above is more convenient in our context.

Definition 2.3
.

[
1
]

f\in S(X^{*})
is said to be a
w*-semidenting point
of
B(X^{*})
if for every
\varepsilon>0
, there exist
0<\alpha<1
and
x\in S(X)
such that
S(B(X^{*}),x,\alpha)\subseteq B(f,\varepsilon)
.

A semidenting point of
B(X)
can be defined similarly.

Theorem 2.4
.

(
a
)

[
2
]
A Banach space
X
has the MIP if and only if every
f\in S(X^{*})
is w*-semidenting.

(
b
)

[
1
]
A Banach space
X
has the UMIP if and only if every
f\in S(X^{*})
is uniformly w*-semidenting, i.e., given
\varepsilon>0
, there exists
0<\alpha<1
such that for any
f\in S(X^{*})
, there exists
x\in S(X)
such that

S(B(X^{*}),x,\alpha)\subseteq B(f,\varepsilon).

We now recall the notions of uniform convexity.

Definition 2.5
.

For
t>0
, let us consider the following quantity:

\delta_{X}(t):=\inf\left\{1-\frac{\|x+y\|}{2}:x,y\in S(X),\|x-y\|\geq t\right\}.

This quantity is called the modulus of convexity of
X
. A Banach space
X
is uniformly convex if
\delta_{X}(t)>0
for all
t>0
.

We will write
\delta(t)
in place of
\delta_{X}(t)
if there is no scope of confusion.

2.1.
Modulus of Denting Point

We now look at the modulus of denting and uniform denting points discussed by Dutta and Lin
[
6
]
. This serves as a background for the moduli we define later. For the sake of completeness, we include the proofs, filling up some of the details missing in
[
6
]
.

Definition 2.6
.

For
0<t<2
,
f\in S(X^{*})
and
x\in S(X)
, define

s(x,f,t):=\inf\{\|x+y\|-1:y\in\ker f,\|y\|\geq t/4\}.

For
0<t<2
and
x\in S(X)
, define

\displaystyle d(x,t)

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle\sup_{f\in S(X^{*})}s(x,f,t)\quad\mbox{ and}

\displaystyle d(t)

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle\inf_{x\in S(X)}d(x,t).

Note that for
0<t<2
,
x\in S(X)
,
f\in D(x)
and
y\in\ker f
,

1=f(x)=f(x+y)\leq\|x+y\|.

It follows that
s(x,f,t)\geq 0
and hence,
d(x,t)\geq 0
.

Lemma 2.7
.

[
6
, Lemma 3.1]

Let
x\in S(X)
and
f\in S(X^{*})
be such that
f(x)>0
and for some
0<t<2
,
s(x,f,t)>0
, then

diam(S(B(X),f,f(x)(1-s(x,f,t))))<2t.

Proof.

Suppose
s=s(x,f,t)>0
. Let
v\in\ker f
with
\|v\|=t/4
. Clearly,
s\leq\|x\|+\|v\|-1\leq t/4<1
.

Claim :

f(x)\geq 1-t/4
.

Let, if possible,
f(x)<1-t/4
. Let
0<\varepsilon<\min\{s/(1+s),1-t/4-f(x)\}
.

Let
v\in S(X)
be such that
f(v)>1-\varepsilon
. Put
z=x-\frac{f(x)}{f(v)}v
. Then,
z\in\ker f
and

\|z\|\geq 1-\frac{f(x)}{f(v)}\geq 1-\frac{1-t/4-\varepsilon}{1-\varepsilon}=\frac{t/4}{1-\varepsilon}>t/4.

It follows that

1+s\leq\|x-z\|\leq\frac{f(x)}{f(v)}\leq\frac{1}{1-\varepsilon}<1+s.

This is a contradiction. Hence, the claim.

Let
y\in S=S(B(X),f,f(x)(1-s))
. Then,
f(y)>f(x)(1-s)
. Consider
z=y-\frac{f(y)}{f(x)}x
. Then
z\in\ker f
. Also,

\|x+z\|-1=\left\|x+y-\frac{f(y)}{f(x)}x\right\|-1\leq\left|1-\frac{f(y)}{f(x)}\right|.

Case 1 :
Suppose
f(y)\leq f(x)
. Then,
|1-f(y)/f(x)|=1-f(y)/f(x)<s
. So,
\|z\|<t/4
. Hence,

\|y-x\|=\left\|z+\frac{f(y)}{f(x)}x-x\right\|\leq\|z\|+\left|1-\frac{f(y)}{f(x)}\right|<t/4+s\leq t/4+t/4=t/2.

Case 2 :
If
1\geq f(y)>f(x)
, put
z_{1}=f(x)z=f(x)y-f(y)x
. Then
z_{1}\in\ker f
. Also,

\|x+z_{1}\|-1=\|x+f(x)y-f(y)x\|-1\leq f(x)+1-f(y)-1\leq 0<s.

Then,
\|f(x)z\|<t/4
, i.e.,
\|z\|<(t/4)/f(x)
. Therefore, by the claim above,

\|y-x\|\leq\|z\|+\left|1-\frac{f(y)}{f(x)}\right|<\frac{t/4}{f(x)}+\frac{1}{f(x)}-1\leq\frac{t/4+1}{1-t/4}-1<t.

Since
x\in S
,
diam(S)<2t
.
∎

Lemma 2.8
.

Given
x\in S(X)
and
0<t<2
, suppose there exists
f\in S(X^{*})
and
\alpha\in(0,1)
such that
x\in S:=S(B(X),f,\alpha)
and
diam(S)<t/5
. Then

s(x,f,t)\geq\beta:=\min\left\{\frac{f(x)-\alpha}{\alpha},\frac{t}{20}\right\}>0.

Proof.

Let
y\in\ker f
such that
z=x+y\in B(X)
. Then
z\in S
. Thus,
\|y\|=\|z-x\|<t/5
. This implies
s(x,f,t)\geq 0
.

Suppose
0\leq s(x,f,t)<\beta
. Choose
\varepsilon>0
such that
s(x,f,t)<\varepsilon<\beta
.

Then
1\geq f(x)>\alpha(1+\varepsilon)>\alpha
.

Clearly,
x\in S_{1}:=\{y\in B(X):f(y)>\alpha(1+\varepsilon)\}\subseteq S
and so,
diamS_{1}<t/5
.

Now, since
0\leq s(x,f,t)<\varepsilon
, there exists
y_{0}\in\ker f
such that
\|y_{0}\|\geq t/4
and
1\leq\|x+y_{0}\|<1+\varepsilon
.

Put
z_{0}=x+y_{0}
. Then,
f(z_{0})>\alpha(1+\varepsilon)
. Therefore,
f(z_{0}/\|z_{0}\|)>\alpha
.

This implies,
z_{0}/\|z_{0}\|\in S
. So,
\|x-z_{0}/\|z_{0}\|\|<t/5
. Therefore,

\|y_{0}\|=\|x-z_{0}\|\leq\left\|x-\frac{z_{0}}{\|z_{0}\|}\right\|+\left\|z_{0}-\frac{z_{0}}{\|z_{0}\|}\right\|<\frac{t}{5}+(\|z_{0}\|-1)<\frac{t}{5}+\varepsilon<\frac{t}{4}.

This is a contradiction, proving
s(x,f,t)\geq\beta
.
∎

Theorem 2.9
.

[
6
, Proposition 3.3
(a)
]

x\in S(X)
is a denting point of
B(X)
if and only if
d(x,t)>0
for all
0<t<2
.

Proof.

Let
0<t<2
be given. Suppose
d(x,t)>0
. Then there exists
f\in S(X^{*})
such that
s=s(x,f,t)>0
.

If
f(x)=0
, then
(t/4)(-x)\in\ker f
. So,
s\leq\|x-(t/4)x\|-1\leq-t/4<0
, a contradiction. Hence,
f(x)\neq 0
. Note that
s(x,f,t)=s(x,-f,t)
. Therefore, without loss of generality, we may assume
f(x)>0
. Hence, by Lemma
2.7
,
x\in S=S(B(X),f,f(x)(1-s))
and
diam(S)<2t
. Since
0<t<2
is arbitrary,
x
is a denting point of
B(X)
.

Conversely, let
0<t<2
be given. Since,
x
is denting point, there exists
f\in S(X^{*})
and
\alpha\in(0,1)
such that
x\in S:=S(B(X),f,\alpha)
and
diam(S)<t/5
.

By Lemma
2.8
,
s(x,f,t)\geq\min\{(f(x)-\alpha)/\alpha,t/20\}>0
which implies that
d(x,t)>0
.
∎

Definition 2.10
.

A Banach space
X
is uniformly denting if
d(t)>0
for all
0<t<2
.

Dutta and Lin
[
6
]
sketch an argument to show that if
X
is uniformly denting then
X
has an equivalent uniformly convex norm. We will elaborate and improve on this in Section
4
.

We end this section with another observation regarding the above modulus.

Proposition 2.11
.

If
D
is a dense set in
S(X)
, then

d(t)=\inf_{x\in D}d(x,t)\mbox{ for all }0<t<2.

Proof.

Let
d_{D}(t):=\inf_{x\in D}d(x,t)
. Clearly,
d_{D}(t)\geq d(t)
for all
0<t<2
.

Let
\varepsilon>0
and
x\in S(X)
. Since
D
is dense in
S(X)
, there exists
z\in D
such that
\|x-z\|<\varepsilon
. Then for any
f\in S(X^{*})
and
y\in\ker f
with
\|y\|\geq t/4
, we have
\|z+y\|\leq\|x+y\|+\varepsilon
. It follows that
s(z,f,t)\leq s(x,f,t)+\varepsilon
and hence,
d_{D}(t)\leq d(z,t)\leq d(x,t)+\varepsilon
. Therefore,
d_{D}(t)\leq d(t)+\varepsilon
.
∎

3.
Modulus of w*-Semidenting Points

In this section, we are going to study the MIP and the UMIP with the help of some moduli motivated by the above moduli.

We begin with the analogues of the moduli defined above for dual spaces.

Definition 3.1
.

For
0<t<2
,
f\in S(X^{*})
and
x\in S(X)
, define

s^{*}(f,x,t):=\inf\{\|f+h\|-1:\|h\|\geq t/4,h(x)=0\}.

For
0<t<2
and
f\in S(X^{*})
, define

\displaystyle d^{*}(f,t)

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle\sup_{x\in S(X)}s^{*}(f,x,t)\quad\mbox{ and}

\displaystyle d^{*}(t)

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle\inf_{f\in S(X^{*})}d^{*}(f,t).

The following lemma is the w*-analogue of Lemma
2.7
and Theorem
2.9
.

Lemma 3.2
.

(
a
)

Let
x\in S(X)
and
f\in S(X^{*})
be such that
f(x)>0
and for some
0<t<2
,
s^{*}(f,x,t)>0
, then

diam(S(B(X^{*}),x,f(x)(1-s^{*}(f,x,t))))<2t.

(
b
)

f\in S(X^{*})
is a w*-denting point of
B(X^{*})
if and only if
d^{*}(f,t)>0
for all
0<t<2
.

Definition 3.3
.

X^{*}
is uniformly w*-denting if
d^{*}(t)>0
for all
0<t<2
.

The proof of
(b)
below is essentially contained in that of
[
5
, Fact 2.2]
. We include the proof for completeness.

Lemma 3.4
.

Let
\varepsilon>0
,
0<\beta<\alpha<1
and
x\in S(X)
.

(
a
)

If
diam(S(B(X^{*}),x,\alpha))<\varepsilon
, then
\alpha\geq 1-\varepsilon
.

(
b
)

\displaystyle diam(S(B(X^{*}),x,\beta))\leq\left(\frac{1-\beta}{1-\alpha}\right)diam(S(B(X^{*}),x,\alpha))
.

Proof.

(a)
. Let
h\in D(x)
. If possible, let
\alpha<1-\varepsilon
. Choose
g\in B(X^{*})
such that
\alpha<g(x)<1-\varepsilon
. Then
h,g\in S(B(X^{*}),x,\alpha)
but
\|h-g\|\geq h(x)-g(x)>\varepsilon
.

(b)
. Let
g,h\in S(B(X^{*}),x,\beta)
,
f\in D(x)
and
\lambda=\frac{\alpha-\beta}{1-\beta}
.
Let
g_{\lambda}:=\lambda f+(1-\lambda)g
and
h_{\lambda}:=\lambda f+(1-\lambda)h
.
We have,

g_{\lambda}(x)>\lambda+(1-\lambda)\beta=\alpha.

So,
g_{\lambda}\in S(B(X^{*}),x,\alpha)
. Similarly,
h_{\lambda}\in S(B(X^{*}),x,\alpha)
. Therefore,

\displaystyle\|h-g\|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\|g_{\lambda}-h_{\lambda}\|}{1-\lambda}\leq\frac{diam(S(B(X^{*}),x,\alpha))}{1-\lambda}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\frac{1-\beta}{1-\alpha}\right)diam(S(B(X^{*}),x,\alpha)).

Hence,

diam(S(B(X^{*}),x,\beta))\leq\left(\frac{1-\beta}{1-\alpha}\right)diam(S(B(X^{*}),x,\alpha)).

∎

Corollary 3.5
.

Let
\gamma>0
and
k\in\mathbb{N}
be such that
1-2k(1-\gamma)>0
, then

diam(S(B(X^{*}),x,1-2k(1-\gamma)))\leq 2k(diam(S(B(X^{*}),x,\gamma))).

Theorem 3.6
.

For a Banach space
X
, the following are equivalent :

(
a
)

For every
t>0
, there exists
\delta>0
such that for every
f\in S(X^{*})
, there exists
x\in S(X)
satisfying

diam(S(B(X^{*}),x,f(x)-\delta))<t.

(
b
)

For every
t>0
, there exists
\delta>0
such that for every
f\in S(X^{*})
, there exists
x\in S(X)
satisfying

diam(S(B(X^{*}),x,f(x)(1-\delta)))<t.

(
c
)

X^{*}
is uniformly w*-denting.

Proof.

(a)\implies(b)
.
Let
t>0
be given. So, there exists
\delta>0
such that for every
f\in S(X^{*})
, there exists
x\in S(X)
satisfying

diam(S(B(X^{*}),x,f(x)-\delta))<t.

Since,
f(x)(1-\delta)\geq f(x)-\delta
,

diam(S(B^{*},x,f(x)(1-\delta)))<t.

(b)\implies(c)
.
Let
t>0
be given. By
(b)
, there exists
\delta>0
that works for
t/5
. Let
f\in S(X^{*})
. Then, there exists
x\in S(X)
satisfying

diam(S(B(X^{*}),x,f(x)(1-\delta)))<t/5.

By Lemma
2.8
,
s^{*}(f,x,t)\geq\beta(t):=\min\{\delta/(1-\delta),t/20\}>0
, which implies that

. Hence,

is uniformly w*-denting.

(c)\implies(a)
.
Let
t>0
be given. Then,
\delta:=d^{*}(t/4)/2>0
. So, by definition, for every

, there exists

such that

. Therefore, by Lemma

diam(S(B(X^{*}),x,f(x)(1-\delta)))<t/2.

By Corollary

.
So, there exists
x\in S(X)
and
g\in S(X^{*})
such that
\|f-g\|<t/4
and
\displaystyle s^{*}=s^{*}(g,x,t/4)>\gamma(t)
. As before, changing
x
to
-x
if necessary, we may assume
g(x)>0
.
Let
S:=S(B(X^{*}),x,g(x)(1-s^{*}))
. Then
g\in S
and by Lemma
2.7
, we have
diam(S)<t/2
. Since
\|f-g\|<t/4
, we have
S\subseteq B(f,t)
.
Also,
0<g(x)\leq 1
and
\displaystyle s^{*}>\gamma(t)
imply that

S(B(X^{*}),x,1-\gamma(t))\subseteq S(B(X^{*}),x,1-s^{*})\subseteq S\subseteq B(f,t).

It follows that
\alpha(t)=1-\gamma(t)
works.
∎

Remark 3.9
.

Note that if
X
has the UMIP, it is uniformly w*-semidenting, also since it has the MIP, the set
D
of w*-denting points of
B(X^{*})
is norm dense in
S(X^{*})
. However, it does not follow that these w*-denting points are uniform over
D
, that is,
\inf_{f\in D}d^{*}(f,t)>0
, as this, by Proposition
2.11
, would imply that
X^{*}
is uniformly w*-denting, which, in general, is a much stronger condition, as finite dimensional examples can show.

4.
Uniform w*-Denting and Hyperplane UMIP

Now we will discuss a geometric property which is a stronger form of the UMIP, a uniform version of
``\mathcal{P}=\mathcal{H}"
discussed in
[
8
]
. We will call it Hyperplane UMIP (H-UMIP). We will show that this property is equivalent to
X^{*}
being uniformly convex.

First, let us recall the definition of the condition “
\mathcal{P}=\mathcal{H}
”.

Definition 4.1
.

We say that a Banach space
X
has the Hyperplane MIP (H-MIP) if for every closed convex bounded set
C\subseteq X
and
f\in S(X^{*})
such that
\inf f(C)>0
, there exists a closed ball
B[x_{0},r_{0}]
in
X
such that
C\subseteq B[x_{0},r_{0}]
and
\inf f(B[x_{0},r_{0}])>0
.

The following characterisation of the above property is already known:

Theorem 4.2
.

[
3
, Proposition 5.3, p. 196]

X
has the H-MIP if and only if every
f\in S(X^{*})
is a w*-denting point of
B(X^{*})
.

Definition 4.3
.

We say that
X
has the Hyperplane UMIP (H-UMIP) if for every
\varepsilon>0
and
M(\varepsilon)\geq 1
, there is
K(\varepsilon)>0
such that whenever a closed convex set
C\subseteq X
and
f\in S(X^{*})
are such that
\sup\{\|x\|:x\in C\}\leq M(\varepsilon)
and
\inf f(C)\geq\varepsilon
, there is a closed ball
B[x_{0},r_{0}]
in
X
such that
C\subseteq B[x_{0},r_{0}]
,
\inf f(B[x_{0},r_{0}])\geq\varepsilon/2
and
r_{0}\leq K
.

Remark 4.4
.

Note that unlike the UMIP, in this case, the uniform bound is not on the diameter of
C
, but its farthest distance from the point being separated, which is an apparently stronger assumption.

Definition 4.5
.

X^{*}
is said to be uniformly w*-denting (I) if for every
\varepsilon>0
, there exists
0<\alpha<1
such that for every
f\in S(X^{*})
, there is
x\in S(X)
such that

f(x)>\alpha\mbox{ and }diam(S(B(X^{*}),x,\alpha))<\varepsilon.

Lemma 4.6
.

If
X^{*}
is uniformly w*-denting (I), then for every
\varepsilon>0
, there exist
0<\beta<\alpha<1
such that for every
f\in S(X^{*})
, there exists
x\in S(X)
satisfying

diam(S(B(X^{*}),x,\beta))<\varepsilon\mbox{ and }f(x)>\alpha.

Proof.

Let
X^{*}
be uniformly w*-denting (I). Let
\varepsilon>0
be given. There exists
\alpha>0
such that given
f\in S(X^{*})
, there exists
x\in S(X)
such that

diam(S(B(X^{*}),x,\alpha))<\varepsilon/2\mbox{ and }f(x)>\alpha.

Let
\beta>0
be such that
\alpha>\beta>2\alpha-1
. By Lemma
3.4

(b)
, we have that

diam(S(B(X^{*}),x,\beta))\leq\left(\frac{1-\beta}{1-\alpha}\right)diam(S(B(X^{*}),x,\alpha))<\varepsilon.

∎

For
\varepsilon>0
, let

F_{\varepsilon}:=B(X^{*})\setminus\bigcup\{S:S\mbox{ is a w*-slice of }B(X^{*})\mbox{ with }diam(S)<\varepsilon\}.

Theorem 4.7
.

The following statements are equivalent :

(
a
)

X^{*}
is uniformly w*-denting.

(
b
)

For every
\varepsilon>0
, there exists
\delta(\varepsilon)>0
such that

F_{\varepsilon}\subseteq(1-\delta(\varepsilon))B(X^{*}).

(
c
)

X^{*}
is uniformly convex.

(
d
)

X^{*}
is uniformly w*-denting (I).

(
e
)

X
has the H-UMIP.

Proof.

(a)\implies(b)
.
Let
X^{*}
be uniformly w*-denting and
\varepsilon>0
. By Theorem
3.6
, there exists
\delta>0
such that for every
f\in S(X^{*})
, there is
x\in S(X)
such that

diam(S(B(X^{*}),x,f(x)(1-\delta)))<\varepsilon.

Let
f\in F_{\varepsilon}
. Now, for
h=f/\|f\|
, there exists
x\in S(X)
such that

diam(S(B(X^{*}),x,h(x)(1-\delta)))<\varepsilon.

Since,
f\notin S(B(X^{*}),x,h(x)(1-\delta))
,
f(x)\leq h(x)(1-\delta)
. Then,
\|f\|\leq 1-\delta
. Therefore,

F_{\varepsilon}\subseteq(1-\delta)B(X^{*}).

(b)\implies(c)
. Let
\varepsilon>0
be given. Let
\delta>0
be such that

F_{\varepsilon/2}\subseteq(1-\delta)B(X^{*}).

Claim :
If
f,g\in S(X^{*})
and
\|f-g\|\geq\varepsilon
, then
\displaystyle\left\|\frac{f+g}{2}\right\|\leq 1-\delta
.

Suppose not. Then,
\displaystyle\left\|\frac{f+g}{2}\right\|>1-\delta
and hence,
\displaystyle\frac{f+g}{2}\notin F_{\varepsilon/2}
.

Thus, there exists
S=S(B(X^{*}),x,\alpha)
such that
\displaystyle\frac{f+g}{2}\in S
and
diam(S)<\varepsilon/2
.

Now,
\displaystyle\frac{f(x)+g(x)}{2}>\alpha
implies either
f(x)>\alpha
or
g(x)>\alpha
, that is, at least one of
f
or
g\in S
.

Since
\displaystyle\frac{f+g}{2}\in S
and
diam(S)<\varepsilon/2
,
\displaystyle\left\|\frac{f-g}{2}\right\|<\varepsilon/2
, or
\displaystyle\left\|f-g\right\|<\varepsilon
. Contradiction.
So,
X^{*}
is uniformly convex.

(c)\implies(d)
.
Let
\varepsilon>0
be given. So, there exists
0<\delta\leq\varepsilon/4
such that for any
f,g\in S(X^{*})
with

\frac{\|f+g\|}{2}>1-\delta,

we have
\|f-g\|\leq\varepsilon/4
.

Let
f\in S(X^{*})
. Choose
x\in S(X)
such that
f(x)=1
(this is possible since, because of the uniform convexity of
X^{*}
,
X
is reflexive). Let
g\in B(X^{*})
be such that
g(x)>1-\delta
. We have
\|g\|>1-\delta\geq 1-\varepsilon/4
and therefore,
1-\|g\|<\varepsilon/4
and
\frac{g}{\|g\|}(x)>\frac{1}{\|g\|}(1-\delta)>1-\delta
.

Now,

\displaystyle\frac{\|f+(g/\|g\|)\|}{2}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\frac{(f+(g/\|g\|))(x)}{2}=\frac{1}{2}(1+(g/\|g\|)(x))

\displaystyle>

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}(1+1-\delta)=1-\delta/2>1-\delta.

Then,
\|f-\frac{g}{\|g\|}\|<\varepsilon/4
.

\|f-g\|=\|f-\frac{g}{\|g\|}\|+1-\|g\|<\varepsilon/2.

Hence,
diam(S(B(X^{*}),x,1-\delta))<\varepsilon
and
f\in S(B(X^{*}),x,1-\delta)
. So,
X^{*}
is uniformly w*-denting (I).

(d)\implies(e)
.
Let
\varepsilon>0
and
M\geq 2
be given. Let
M_{1}:=M+3\varepsilon/4
.
Since
X^{*}
is uniformly w*-denting (I), by Lemma
4.6
, there exist
0<\alpha<1
and
\delta>0
such that for every
f\in S(X^{*})
, there exists
x\in S(X)
satisfying

diam(S(B(X^{*}),x,\alpha-\delta))<\varepsilon/4M_{1}\mbox{ and }f(x)>\alpha.

Choose
k\in\mathbb{N}
such that
1/2k<\varepsilon/4M_{1}
. Again, there exists
0<\gamma<1
such that for every
f\in S(X^{*})
, there is
x\in S(X)
such that

f(x)>\gamma\mbox{ and }diam(S(B(X^{*}),x,\gamma))<\delta/4k.

Then, by Lemma
3.4

(a)
,
(1-\gamma)\leq\delta/4k<1/2k<\varepsilon/4M_{1}
. So,
1-2k(1-\gamma)>0
.

It suffices to show that for
K=\frac{M_{1}}{2k(1-\gamma)}
, whenever a closed convex set
C
and
f\in S(X^{*})
are such that
\sup\{\|x\|:x\in C\}\leq M
and
\inf f(C)\geq\varepsilon
, there is a closed ball
B[x_{0},r_{0}]
in
X
such that
C\subseteq B[x_{0},r_{0}]
,
\inf f(B[x_{0},r_{0}])\geq\varepsilon/2
and
r_{0}\leq K
.

Let
C
and
f\in S(X^{*})
be as above. For
f
, there exists
x_{1}\in S(X)
such that

diam(S(B(X^{*}),x_{1},\alpha-\delta))<\varepsilon/4M_{1}\mbox{ and }f(x_{1})>\alpha.

Again, there exists
x_{2}\in S(X)
such that

diam(S(B(X^{*}),x_{2},\gamma))<\delta/4k\mbox{ and }f(x_{2})>\gamma.

If
g\notin S(B(X^{*}),x_{1},\alpha-\delta/2)
, then
\|f-g\|\geq f(x_{1})-g(x_{1})>\delta/2
.

Let
\eta=1-2k(1-\gamma)>0
. By Corollary
3.5
,

diam(S(B(X^{*}),x_{2},\eta))\leq 2k(diam(S(B(X^{*}),x_{2},\gamma)))<\delta/2.

Since
f\in S(B(X^{*}),x_{2},\eta)
, it follows that
g\notin S(B(X^{*}),x_{2},\eta)
. Thus, we obtain that

\displaystyle f\in S(B(X^{*}),x_{2},\gamma)

\displaystyle\subseteq

\displaystyle S_{1}=S(B(X^{*}),x_{2},\eta)

\displaystyle\subseteq

\displaystyle S(B(X^{*}),x_{1},\alpha-\delta/2)\subseteq S(B(X^{*}),x_{1},\alpha-\delta).

So,
diam(S_{1})<\varepsilon/4M_{1}
.

Define
D=\overline{C+3\varepsilon/4B(X)}
. Then
D\subseteq B[0,M_{1}]
and
\inf f(D)\geq\varepsilon/4
. Hence, for
z\in D
and
g\in B(X^{*})\cap B(f,\varepsilon/4M_{1})
,

g(z)\geq f(z)-\|f-g\|\|z\|>\varepsilon/4-\frac{\varepsilon M_{1}}{4M_{1}}=0.

Therefore,

S_{1}\subseteq B(X^{*})\cap B(f,\varepsilon/4M_{1})\subseteq\{g\in B(X^{*}):g(z)>0\mbox{ for all }z\in D\}.

Let

d=\frac{d(0,D)(1-\eta)}{1+\eta}>0.

Then as in
[
1
, Theorem 2.6]
, for
\lambda=M_{1}/(1-\eta)=M_{1}/2k(1-\gamma)
,

D\subseteq B\left[\lambda x_{2},\lambda-d\right].

i.e.

C\subseteq B\left[\lambda x_{2},\lambda-d-\frac{3\varepsilon}{4}\right]=B_{1}.

Since,
1/2k<\varepsilon/4M_{1}
, i.e.,
M_{1}/2k<\varepsilon/4
, we have

\displaystyle\inf f(B_{1})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle f(\lambda x_{2})-\lambda+d+\frac{3\varepsilon}{4}>\lambda(\gamma-1)+d+\frac{3\varepsilon}{4}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{M_{1}}{2k}+d+\frac{3\varepsilon}{4}\geq d+\frac{3\varepsilon}{4}-\varepsilon/4>\varepsilon/2.

Also,

\frac{M_{1}}{2k(1-\gamma)}-d-\frac{3\varepsilon}{4}\leq\frac{M_{1}}{2k(1-\gamma)}=K.

Hence,
X
has the H-UMIP.

(e)\implies(a)
.
Let
0<\varepsilon<1
and
M(\varepsilon)\geq 1
be given. Then, there is
K=K(\varepsilon)>0
such that whenever a closed convex set
C\subseteq X
and
f\in S(X^{*})
are such that
\sup\{\|x\|:x\in C\}\leq M(\varepsilon)
and
\inf f(C)\geq\varepsilon/4
, there is a closed ball
B[x_{0},r_{0}]
in

such that
C\subseteq B[x_{0},r_{0}]
,
\inf f(B[x_{0},r_{0}])\geq\varepsilon/8
and

. We may assume w.l.o.g. that

.

Let
f\in S(X^{*})
. Consider

. Then,
\inf f(A)=\varepsilon/4
. Also,
\sup\{\|x\|:x\in C\}\leq 1\leq M(\varepsilon)
. So, there exist
B=B[x_{0},r]
containing
A
such that
r\leq K
and
\inf f(B)\geq\varepsilon/8
. Put
\alpha=K/(K+\varepsilon/9)
. We claim that
\delta=1-\alpha
works.
Note that
r/(r+\varepsilon/9)\leq K/(K+\varepsilon/9)=\alpha
.

Since
\inf f(B)\geq\varepsilon/8
we have
f(x_{0})\geq r+\varepsilon/8>r+\varepsilon/9
.

Put
z_{0}=x_{0}/\|x_{0}\|
. Let
S=S(B(X^{*}),z_{0},f(z_{0})(1-\delta))
.

Let
g\in S
. Then,
g(z_{0})>f(z_{0})\alpha
, which implies,
g(x_{0})>f(x_{0})\alpha>f(x_{0})(r/(r+\varepsilon/9))\geq r
. That is,
\inf g(B)>0
. So,

\{x\in B(X):f(x)>\varepsilon/3\}\subseteq A\subseteq B\subseteq\{x:g(x)>0\}.

So, by
[
1
, Lemma 2.5]
,
\|f-g\|\leq 2\varepsilon/3
.
But

.
Therefore,

diam(S)<4\varepsilon/3.

So,
X^{*}
is uniformly w*-denting.
∎

5.
Modulus of the UMIP by Slices

In this section, we define another modulus of w*-semidenting points and characterise MIP and UMIP in its terms.

Definition 5.1
.

For
f\in S(X^{*})
,
x\in S(X)
and
0<t<1
, define

\displaystyle\beta(f,x,t)

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle\inf\{1-g(x):g\in B(X^{*}),\|f-g\|\geq t\},

\displaystyle\beta(f,t)

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle\sup_{x\in S(X)}\beta(f,x,t)\mbox{ and, }

\displaystyle\beta(t)

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle\inf_{f\in S(X^{*})}\beta(f,t).

Lemma 5.2
.

Let
x\in S(X)
,
f\in S(X^{*})
and
0<t<1
be such that
\beta(f,x,t)>0
, then

S(B(X^{*}),x,1-\beta(f,x,t))\subseteq B(f,t)

Proof.

Let
g\in S(B(X^{*}),x,1-\beta(f,x,t))
. Then
1-g(x)<\beta(f,x,t)
. So, by definition of
\beta(f,x,t)
,

\|f-g\|<t
.
∎

Theorem 5.3
.

X
has MIP if and only if for every
f\in S(X^{*})
and
0<t<1
,
\beta(f,t)>0
.

Proof.

Let
f\in S(X^{*})
. If for every
0<t<1
,
\beta(f,t)>0
, then there exists
x\in S(X)
such that
\beta(f,x,t)>0
.
So, by the above lemma,
f\in S(X^{*})
is a w*-semidenting point of
B(X^{*})
. Thus,
X
has MIP.

Conversely, let
X
has MIP. Then, every
f\in S(X^{*})
is a w*-semidenting point of
B(X^{*})
. Hence, for every
f\in S(X^{*})
and
t>0
, there exist
1>\alpha>0
and
x\in S(X)
such that
S(B(X),x,\alpha)\subseteq B(f,t)
. So, if
g\in B(X^{*})
and
\|g-f\|\geq t
, then
g(x)\leq\alpha
, i.e.,
0<1-\alpha\leq 1-g(x)
.

So,
\beta(f,x,t)=\inf\{1-g(x):g\in B(X^{*}),\|f-g\|\geq t\}\geq 1-\alpha>0
. Thus,
\beta(f,t)>0
.
∎

Theorem 5.4
.

X
has UMIP if and only if for every
0<t<1
,
\beta(t)>0
.

Proof.

Let
X
has UMIP. Let
t>0
. There exists
0<\alpha<1
such that for any
f\in S(X^{*})
, there exists
x\in S(X)
such that if
g\in B(X^{*})
and
g(x)>\alpha
, then
\|f-g\|<t
. Hence, for every
f\in S(X^{*})
, there exists
x\in S(X)
such that
\beta(f,x,t)\geq 1-\alpha
. Then,
\beta(t)\geq 1-\alpha>0
.

Conversely, let
\beta(t)>0
for every
0<t<1
. So, for every
f\in S(X^{*})
, there exists
x\in S(X)
such that
\beta(f,x,t)\geq\beta(t)/2>0
. Hence,
g\in S(B(X^{*}),x,1-\beta(t)/2)
implies
1-g(x)<\beta(f,x,t)
. So,

S(B(X^{*}),x,1-\beta(t)/2)\subseteq B(f,t).

Thus
X
has the UMIP.
∎

Remark 5.5
.

It is to be noted that for
0<\varepsilon_{1}<\varepsilon_{2}
,
\beta(\varepsilon_{1})\leq\beta(\varepsilon_{2})
.

It is proved in
[
9
]
that if
X^{*}
is uniformly convex, then
X
has the UMIP. In the next theorem, we obtain a relationship between the modulus of uniform convexity and the modulus of UMIP when
X^{*}
is uniformly convex.

Following observation will be used in the proof in the next section.

Observation 5.6
.

If
X^{*}
is uniformly convex, then given any
f\in S(X^{*})
and
x\in D^{-1}(f)
,

diam(S(B(X^{*}),x,1-2\delta_{X^{*}}(t/2)))<t.

Proof.

Since,
X^{*}
is uniformly convex,
X
is reflexive. Let
t>0
, then
\delta_{X^{*}}(t)>0
. Let
f\in S(X^{*})
and
x\in S(X)
be such that

.

Let

be such that

. Then

.

diamS(B(X^{*}),x,1-2\delta_{X^{*}}(t/2))<t

∎

6.
Stability of the UMIP under
\ell_{p}
sum

Let
1<p,q<\infty
be such that
1/p+1/q=1
. Let
\{X_{i}\}
is a sequence of Banach spaces, and

X_{p}=\left(\bigoplus_{i=1}^{\infty}X_{i}\right)_{p}:=\left\{(x_{i}):x_{i}\in X_{i}\mbox{ such that }\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\|x_{i}\|^{p}<\infty\right\}

with
\displaystyle\|x\|_{p}=\left(\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\|x_{i}\|^{p}\right)^{1/p}
as the norm on
X_{p}
.

If
X_{i}=X
for all
i
, we write
\ell_{p}(X)
instead of
(\bigoplus_{i=1}^{\infty}X_{i})_{p}
.

It is well known that

(X_{p})^{*}=X^{*}_{q}=\left(\bigoplus_{i=1}^{\infty}X^{*}_{i}\right)_{q},

with the action
\displaystyle f(x)=\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}f_{i}(x_{i})
.

In this section, we are going to analyse the stability of the UMIP under
\ell_{p}
-sum for
1<p<\infty
.
[
9
, Proposition 2]
shows that the
\ell_{2}
-sum of spaces with UMIP may fail UMIP. Here we prove a positive result.

Let
\beta_{i}(t)
be the modulus of the UMIP for
X_{i}
for all
i\in\mathbb{N}
, as defined in the previous section.

Theorem 6.1
.

Let
1<p<\infty
.
X_{p}
has the UMIP if and only if for all
i\in\mathbb{N}
,
X_{i}
has the UMIP and
\inf_{i\in\mathbb{N}}\beta_{i}(t)>0
for every
0<t<1
.

Proof.

Let
X_{p}
has the UMIP. Fix
k\in\mathbb{N}
. We show that
X_{k}
has the UMIP.

We use the uniformly quasicontinuous characterisation of UMIP
[
1
, Theorem 2.11]
.

Let
\varepsilon>0
. Since
X_{p}
has the UMIP, there exists
\delta>0
such that for any
f\in S(X_{q}^{*})
, there is
x\in S(X_{p})
such that if
y\in S(X_{p})
and
\|x-y\|_{p}<\delta
, then
\|f-f_{y}\|_{q}<\varepsilon/2
for all
f_{y}\in D(y)
.

Let
f_{k}\in S(X_{k}^{*})
. Define
f=(f_{i})\in S(X_{q}^{*})
by putting

f_{i}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}f_{k}&\mbox{if \, \, }i=k\\
0&\mbox{otherwise}\end{array}\right.

By the above, there is
x\in S(X_{p})
such that if
y\in S(X_{p})
and
\|x-y\|_{p}<\delta
, then
\|f-f_{y}\|_{q}<\varepsilon/2
for all
f_{y}\in D(y)
. In particular, if
g\in D(x)
then
\|f-g\|_{q}<\varepsilon/2
.

Let
z_{i}=x_{i}/\|x_{i}\|
for all
i
such that
x_{i}\neq 0
and
g=(g_{i})\in D(x)
.
Then,

\displaystyle 1

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{i}^{\infty}g_{i}(x_{i})

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sum_{i}^{\infty}\|g_{i}\|\|x_{i}\|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left(\sum_{i}^{\infty}\|g_{i}\|^{q}\right)^{1/q}\left(\sum_{i}^{\infty}\|x_{i}\|^{p}\right)^{1/p}\mbox{ (using Holder's inequality)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 1.

So, for each
i\in\mathbb{N}
,
g_{i}(x_{i})=\|g_{i}\|\|x_{i}\|
. Also, the equality case of Holder’s inequality gives us that
\|x_{i}\|^{p}=\|g_{i}\|^{q}
.
Combining the two, we obtain that
\left(\frac{g_{i}}{\|x\|^{p/q}}\right)\left(\frac{x_{i}}{\|x_{i}\|}\right)=1
.
Thus,
g
has the form

g_{i}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\|x_{i}\|^{p/q}h_{i}&\mbox{if \, \, }x_{i}\neq 0\\
0&\mbox{otherwise}\end{array}\right.

where
h_{i}\in D(z_{i})
. Let
\displaystyle\gamma_{k}=\sum_{i\neq k}\|x_{i}\|^{p}
. It follows that
1=\|x\|_{p}^{p}=\|x_{k}\|^{p}+\gamma_{k}
. And

(\varepsilon/2)^{q}>\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\|f_{i}-g_{i}\|^{q}=\left\|f_{k}-\|x_{k}\|^{p/q}h_{k}\right\|^{q}+\gamma_{k}\geq\left(1-\|x_{k}\|^{p/q}\right)^{q}+\gamma_{k}.

It follows that
\gamma_{k}<(\varepsilon/2)^{q}
. Therefore,
\|x_{k}\|^{p}>1-(\varepsilon/2)^{q}
.

Let
y_{k}\in S(X_{k})
be such that
\|y_{k}-z_{k}\|<\delta
and
h_{k}\in D(y_{k})
. Let
u\in S(X_{p})
be defined as

u_{i}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\|x_{k}\|y_{k}&\mbox{if \, \, }i=k\\
x_{i}&\mbox{otherwise}\end{array}\right.

We have
u\in S(X_{p})
and
\|x-u\|_{p}=\|x_{k}\|\|y_{k}-z_{k}\|<\delta
. Consider
g=(g_{i})
where

g_{i}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\|x_{k}\|^{p/q}h_{k}&\mbox{if \, \, }i=k\\
\|x_{i}\|^{p/q}f_{z_{i}}&\mbox{otherwise}\end{array}\right.

Here
f_{z_{i}}\in D(z_{i})
. Then
g\in D(u)
. So, as before

(\varepsilon/2)^{q}>\|f-g\|^{q}_{q}=\left\|f_{k}-\|x_{k}\|^{p/q}h_{k}\right\|^{q}+\gamma_{k}.

Now,

\displaystyle\|f_{k}-h_{k}\|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\|f_{k}-\|x_{k}\|^{p/q}h_{k}\|+\left\|\|x_{k}\|^{p/q}h_{k}-h_{k}\right\|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\varepsilon/2+(1-\|x_{k}\|^{p/q})

\displaystyle<

\displaystyle\varepsilon/2+(1-(1-(\varepsilon/2)^{q})^{1/q})\leq\varepsilon/2+\varepsilon/2=\varepsilon.

Hence,
X_{k}
has the UMIP and the
\delta
that works for
\varepsilon/2
for
X_{p}
works for
\varepsilon
for
X_{k}
. By
[
1
, Theorem 2.11]
, this implies
\inf_{i\in\mathbb{N}}\beta_{i}(t)>0
.

Conversely, for any
t>0
, let
\beta_{0}(t)=\inf_{i}\beta_{i}(t)
. Clearly,
\beta_{0}(t)>0
for any
0<t<1
and for
0<t_{1}<t_{2}
,
\beta_{0}(t_{1})\leq\beta_{0}(t_{2})
(using Remark
5.5
).

Let
f=(f_{i})\in S(X^{*}_{q})
. If
f_{i}\neq 0
then
f_{i}/\|f_{i}\|\in S(X_{i}^{*})
. So, there exists
x_{i}\in S(X_{i})
such that

h_{i}\in\{g_{i}\in B(X_{i}^{*}):g_{i}(x_{i})>1-\beta_{0}(t)\}\implies\left\|h_{i}-\frac{f_{i}}{\|f_{i}\|}\right\|<t.

Define
z=(z_{i})\in S(X_{p})
by

z_{i}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\|f_{i}\|^{q/p}x_{i}&\mbox{if \, \, }f_{i}\neq 0\\
0&\mbox{if \, \, }f_{i}=0\end{array}\right.

Let
g=(g_{i})\in B(X^{*}_{q})
be such that
\|g-f\|_{q}\geq t
.
First, we deal with the case
\|g_{i}\|=\|f_{i}\|
for all
i\in\mathbb{N}
.
Let
a_{i}=\|f_{i}-g_{i}\|
and
b_{i}=\|f_{i}\|=\|g_{i}\|
. We follow the proof in
[
4
, Theorem 3]
. Clearly,
\|f_{i}-g_{i}\|\leq\|f_{i}\|+\|g_{i}\|
, i.e.,
a_{i}\leq 2b_{i}
.
Now, Let
A:=\{i\in\mathbb{N}:a_{i}/b_{i}>t/4\}
and
B:=\mathbb{N}\setminus A
. Then,

1=\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}b_{i}^{q}\geq\sum_{i\in B}b_{i}^{q}\geq(4/t)^{q}\sum_{i\in B}a_{i}^{q}.

That is,

\sum_{i\in B}a_{i}^{q}\leq\left(t/4\right)^{q}.

Thus,

\sum_{i\in A}a_{i}^{q}=\sum_{i\in\mathbb{N}}a_{i}^{q}-\sum_{i\in B}a_{i}^{q}\geq t^{q}-(t/4)^{q}\geq\left(3t/4\right)^{q}.

Hence,

\alpha_{0}=\sum_{i\in A}b_{i}^{q}\geq(3t/8)^{q}.

Since
\|f_{i}-g_{i}\|=a_{i}
and
\|f_{i}\|=b_{i}
, we have
g_{i}(x_{i})\leq(1-\beta_{0}(a_{i}/b_{i}))b_{i}
. Therefore,

\displaystyle g(z)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\|f_{i}\|^{q/p}g_{i}(x_{i})\leq\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}b_{i}^{q}(1-\beta_{0}(a_{i}/b_{i}))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{i\in A}b_{i}^{q}(1-\beta_{0}(a_{i}/b_{i})))+\sum_{i\in B}b_{i}^{q}(1-\beta_{0}(a_{i}/b_{i})))

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle(1-\beta_{0}(t/4))\sum_{i\in A}b_{i}^{q}+\sum_{i\in B}b_{i}^{q}\mbox{ (follows from Remark ~\ref{rmk1})}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(1-\beta_{0}(t/4))\alpha_{0}+(1-\alpha_{0})=1-\alpha_{0}\beta_{0}(t/4)\leq 1-(3t/8)^{q}\beta_{0}(t/4).

It follows that for any
f\in S(X^{*}_{q})
, there is
z\in S(X_{p})
with the property that, if
g\in B(X^{*}_{q})
is such that
\|g_{i}\|=\|f_{i}\|
for all
i\in\mathbb{N}
and
g(z)>1-(3t/8)\beta_{0}(t/4)
, then
\|f-g\|_{q}<t
.

Now, we move to the general case.
We know that
\ell_{q}
is uniformly convex. Let
\delta_{q}(t)
for
0<t\leq 2
be the modulus of convexity for
\ell_{q}
.

Choose
\alpha
such that
0<\alpha<t/2
and
\delta_{q}(\alpha/2)+\alpha<(3t/16)\beta_{0}(t/8)
.

Let
g\in B(X^{*}_{q})
be such that
g(z)>1-\delta_{q}(\alpha/2)
.
So,

\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\|f_{i}\|^{q/p}\|g_{i}\|\geq\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\|f_{i}\|^{q/p}|g_{i}(x_{i})|>1-\delta_{q}(\alpha/2).

Let
F:=(\|f_{i}\|)\in S(\ell_{q})
,
G:=(\|g_{i}\|)\in B(\ell_{q})
and
u:=(\|f_{i}\|^{q/p})\in S(\ell_{p})
. Then,

F(u)=\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\|f_{i}\|\|f_{i}\|^{q/p}=1\quad\mbox{and}\quad G(u)>1-\delta_{q}(\alpha/2).

Now, by Observation
5.6
,

\|F-G\|_{q}=\left(\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\bigg|\|g_{i}\|-\|f_{i}\|\bigg|^{q}\right)^{1/q}<\alpha.

Let
h=(h_{i})
, where,
h_{i}=\frac{g_{i}\|f_{i}\|}{\|g_{i}\|}
. Clearly,
\|h_{i}\|=\|f_{i}\|
for each
i
. And

\|g-h\|_{q}=\left(\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\bigg|\|g_{i}\|-\|f_{i}\|\bigg|^{q}\right)^{1/q}<\alpha.

Since
g(z)>1-\delta_{q}(\alpha/2)
, we have
h(z)\geq g(z)-\|g-h\|_{q}>1-\delta_{q}(\alpha/2)-\alpha>1-(3t/16)\beta_{0}(t/8)
, by the choice of
\alpha
. By the first case,

\|f-h\|_{q}<t/2.

Hence,

\|f-g\|_{q}\leq\|f-h\|_{q}+\|h-g\|_{q}<t.

∎

Corollary 6.2
.

Let
1<p<\infty
. Then
\ell_{p}(X)
has the UMIP if and only if
X
has the UMIP.

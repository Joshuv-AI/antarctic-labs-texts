Matthew D. Blair, Xiaoqi Huang, Christopher D. Sogge

Abstract
. We obtain new improved spectral projection estimates on manifolds of non-positive curvature, including sharp ones for relatively large spectral windows for general tori. Our results are stronger than those in an earlier work of the first and third authors [ 6 ] , and the arguments have been greatly simplified. We more directly make use of pointwise estimates that are implicit in the work of Bérard [ 2 ] and avoid the use of weak-type spaces that were used in the previous works [ 6 ] and [ 22 ] . We also simplify and strengthen the bilinear arguments by exploiting the use of microlocal L 2 → L q c L^{2}\to L^{q_{c}} Kakeya-Nikodym estimates and avoiding the of L 2 → L 2 L^{2}\to L^{2} ones as in earlier results. This allows us to prove new results for manifolds of negative curvature and some new sharp estimates for tori. We also have new and improved techniques in two dimensions for general manifolds of non-positive curvature.

Let us now state our main results. We shall consider smoothed out spectral projection
operators of the form

(1.2)

\rho\bigl(T(\lambda-P)\bigr),\quad T=T(\lambda),

with
P=\sqrt{-\Delta_{g}}

where
\rho
is a real-valued function satisfying

(1.3)

\rho\in{\mathcal{S}}({\mathbb{R}}),\quad\rho(0)=1,\quad\text{and }\text{supp }\hat{\rho}\subset\{|t|\in(\delta/2,\delta)\},

where
0<\delta<1

will be specified later. For the most part, we shall
take

(1.4)

T=c_{0}\log\lambda,

with
c_{0}>0
ultimately chosen sufficiently small depending on
(M,g)
. For tori, though, we shall take
T
to be much larger.

Our main result which improves on estimates
in
[
6
]
then is the following

Theorem 1.1
.

Assume that
(M,g)
is an
n
-dimensional compact manifold with non-positive sectional curvatures. Then, for
T
as above,

(1.5)

\|\rho(T(\lambda-P))f\|_{L^{q_{c}}(M)}\lesssim\lambda^{\frac{1}{q_{c}}}\,\bigl(\log\lambda\bigr)^{-\sigma_{n}}\|f\|_{L^{2}(M)},

where
\sigma_{n}=\tfrac{2}{(n+1)q_{c}}
.

This result improves the earlier results of two of the authors in
[
6
]
by obtaining a larger power of
\sigma_{n}
in all dimensions.
For instance, in
[
6
]
, this exponent was
\tfrac{4}{3(n+1)^{3}}\ll\tfrac{2}{(n+1)q_{c}}=\tfrac{n-1}{(n+1)^{2}}
. The proof of
Theorem
1.1
is also much simpler than earlier proofs, and, as we shall see leads to further improvements in certain geometries.

Let us note that if
\varepsilon(\lambda)=(\log\lambda)^{-1}
then
(
1.5
) immediately implies bounds for the
spectral projection operators

(1.6)

\chi_{[\lambda-\varepsilon(\lambda),\lambda+\varepsilon(\lambda)]}={\bf 1}_{[\lambda-\varepsilon(\lambda),\,\lambda+\varepsilon(\lambda)]}(P),

with
{\bf 1}_{I}
denoting the indicator function of the
interval
I
. Indeed since
\rho(0)=1
, by a simple
orthogonality argument, Theorem
1.1
yields
the following.

Corollary 1.2
.

If
\chi_{[\lambda-\varepsilon(\lambda),\lambda+\varepsilon(\lambda)]}
is as in
(
1.6
) and
(M,g)
as above has non-positive
curvatures then for
\lambda\geq 1
and

\sigma_{n}=2/(n+1)q_{c}

(1.7)

\bigl\|\chi_{[\lambda-(\log\lambda)^{-1},\,\lambda+(\log\lambda)^{-1}]}f\bigr\|_{L^{q_{c}}(M)}\leq C\lambda^{\frac{1}{q_{c}}}(\log\lambda)^{-\sigma_{n}}\|f\|_{L^{2}(M)}.

Recall that the universal bounds of one of
us
[
18
]
say that

(1.8)

\|\chi_{[\lambda-1,\,\lambda+1]}\|_{L^{2}(M)\to L^{q}(M)}\lesssim\lambda^{\mu(q)},\quad 2<q\leq\infty,

with

(1.9)

\mu(q)=\max\Bigl(n(\tfrac{1}{2}-\tfrac{1}{q})-\tfrac{1}{2},\,\tfrac{n-1}{2}(\tfrac{1}{2}-\tfrac{1}{q})\Bigr).

Since
\mu(q_{c})=1/q_{c}
, (
1.7
) is a
log-power improvement of the universal bounds on
manifolds of non-positive curvature. We need to use
shrinking spectral windows in order to improve
the bounds (
1.8
) which are saturated on
any manifold (see
[

]
); however, for

there is no improvement of (

) even
if one uses
(\log\lambda)^{-1}
–spectral windows as in

1.7
). So the assumption of non-positive
curvature is needed to obtain the improved spectral
projection estimates in (

).

We also note that, by interpolating with the trivial

L^{2}\to L^{2}
estimates for the spectral projection
operators, (
1.7
) yields

(1.10)

\bigl\|\chi_{[\lambda-(\log\lambda)^{-1},\,\lambda+(\log\lambda)^{-1}]}f\bigr\|_{L^{q}(M)}\leq\Bigl(\,(\log\lambda)^{-\frac{2}{n+1}}\lambda\Bigr)^{\mu(q)}\,\|f\|_{L^{2}(M)},\quad 2<q\leq q_{c},

with

\mu(q)=\tfrac{n-1}{2}(\tfrac{1}{2}-\tfrac{1}{q})
as
in (
1.9
). By using dyadic Sobolev estimates
one can also obtain log-power improvements for

q>q_{c}
; however, these would not be as strong
as the
(\log\lambda)^{-1/2}
improvements over the universal
bounds for supercritical exponents of
Bérard
[
2
]
and Hassell and Tacy
[
14
]
.

After we prove Theorem
1.1
, we shall see that
stronger estimates hold if one assumes all of the

,

Next, to motivate the two main steps in our proofs, let us recall the eigenfunctions on
S^{n}
that saturate
the universal estimates (
1.7
). If we wish to
obtain improved bounds, such as those in (
1.5
)
or (
1.7
), we need to develop techniques that will
be able to show that the types of extreme eigenfunctions
that exist on standard spheres cannot exist on manifolds
of non-positive curvature.

The eigenfunctions that saturate (
1.8
) on
S^{n}

with eigenvalue
\lambda=\sqrt{k(k+n-1)}
are the
zonal spherical harmonics of degree
k
,
Z_{k}
. If
they are
L^{2}
-normalized and if
B_{\pm}(c\lambda^{-1})

are geodesic balls of radius
c\lambda^{-1}
about the
two poles on
S^{n}
then

(1.11)

|Z_{k}(x)|\approx\lambda^{\frac{n-1}{2}},\quad x\in B_{\pm}(c\lambda^{-1}),

for some sufficiently small uniform constant
c>0
.
(See, e.g.,
[
17
]
). As a result, an
easy calculation shows that for some
c_{0}>0

we have the lower bounds

\|Z_{k}\|_{L^{q}(S^{n})}\geq c_{0}\lambda^{n(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{q})-\frac{1}{2}},\quad\text{if }\,\,\lambda=\sqrt{k(k+n-1)}.

This implies that (
1.8
) cannot be improved
when
M=S^{n}
for the range of exponents for which

\mu(q)=n(\tfrac{1}{2}-\tfrac{1}{q})-\tfrac{1}{2}
, which
is
q\geq q_{c}
, where
q_{c}
is as in (
1.1
).

The other extreme eigenfunctions for the above
values of
\lambda
are the highest weight spherical
harmonics of degree
k
,
Q_{k}
. If

\gamma\subset S^{n}
is the equator, and if
{\mathcal{T}}_{\lambda^{-1/2}}(\gamma)
is a
\lambda^{-1/2}
-tubular neighborhood
of
\gamma
, then

\|Q_{k}\|_{L^{2}({\mathcal{T}}_{\lambda^{-1/2}}\,(\gamma))}\approx 1\quad\text{if }\,\lambda=\sqrt{k(k+n-1)}\quad\text{and }\,\,\|Q_{k}\|_{L^{2}(S^{n})}=1.

(See, e.g.
[
17
]
.) Therefore, as we shall
show in §
7
, it is a simple exercise
using Hölder’s inequality to verify that for
some uniform
c_{0}>0

(1.12)

\|Q_{k}\|_{L^{q}(S^{n})}\geq c_{0}\,\lambda^{\frac{n-1}{2}(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{q})},\quad 2<q\leq\infty.

As a result, (
1.8
) cannot be improved for

2<q\leq q_{c}
since
\mu(q)=\tfrac{n-1}{2}(\tfrac{1}{2}-\tfrac{1}{q})
for such exponents. To further motivate
the splitting that we shall use in order to prove
Theorem
1.1
, we recall (see
[
17
]
)
that the lower bounds in (
1.12
) are actually
upper bounds, and, in particular

(1.13)

\|Q_{k}\|_{L^{\infty}(S^{n})}\approx\lambda^{\frac{n-1}{4}},\quad\text{if }\,\,\lambda=\sqrt{k(k+n-1)}.

We note that both the zonal spherical harmonics,
Z_{k}
,
and the highest weight spherical harmonics,
Q_{k}
, saturate the
universal bounds (
1.8
) when
q
equals the critical exponent
q_{c}
in (
1.1
). So estimates
involving this exponent are sensitive to both “point
concentration” and “geodesic concentration”. The
two main steps in the proof of Theorem
1.1
will
rule out these types of extreme concentration under
our curvature assumptions, and, based on the arguments
from §
7
and (
1.13
), the “height” at which
the transition between these types of concentration
can occur should be
\lambda^{\frac{n-1}{4}}
.

2.
The height splitting

As in some of our earlier works, we shall employ
bilinear techniques that require us to compose
the “global operators”

(2.1)

\rho_{\lambda}=\rho(T(\lambda-P))

in Theorem
1.1
with the “local operators”

(2.2)

\sigma_{\lambda}=\rho(\lambda-P).

Also, as in earlier works, it will be convenient to localize a bit more. To this end, let us write

I=\sum_{j=1}^{N}B_{j}(x,D),

The size of the support will be described in §4; however, we note now that

. Also note that if

B=B_{j,\lambda}=B_{j}\circ\beta(P/\lambda)

(2.5)

\|B\|_{L^{p}(M)\to L^{p}(M)}=O(1)\quad\text{for }\,\,1\leq p\leq\infty.

See e.g.,
[
26

for definitions of the above symbol classes, and the bounds in (

the results in
[

and the proof of this result

We then shall further localize

by setting

\tilde{\sigma}_{\lambda}=B\circ\sigma_{\lambda},

where

is one of the

operators in (

We also define

\tilde{\rho}_{\lambda}=\tilde{\sigma}_{\lambda}\circ\rho_{\lambda}.

We note that the

can be thought of as
“smoothed out” versions of the operators in (

Similarly, it is an easy exercise using orthogonality

\|(I-\sigma_{\lambda})\circ\rho_{\lambda}\|_{L^{2}(M)\to L^{q_{c}}(M)}\leq CT^{-1}\lambda^{\frac{1}{q_{c}}},\,\,\text{if }\,T\geq 1,\\
\text{and }\,\,\,\|\sigma_{\lambda}-\beta(P/\lambda)\circ\sigma_{\lambda}\|_{L^{2}(M)\to L^{q_{c}}(M)}=O(\lambda^{-N}),\,\,\,\forall\,N.

Therefore by (

) and (

), in order to prove (

if

that the function

in (

-normalized,

We shall then split our task (

-norm of

(2.11)

\displaystyle\|B\rho_{\lambda}f\|^{2}_{L^{q_{c}}(A_{+})}

\displaystyle=\Bigl(\,\int f\cdot\bigl(\rho_{\lambda}B^{*}\bigr)({\bf 1}_{A_{+}}\cdot g)(x)\,dx\,\Bigr)^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\int\bigl|\,\rho_{\lambda}B^{*}\bigl({\bf 1}_{A_{+}}\cdot g\bigr)(x)\,\bigr|^{2}\,dx

\displaystyle=\int\bigl(B\circ\Psi(T(\lambda-P))\circ B^{*}\bigr)({\bf 1}_{A_{+}}\cdot g)(x)\,\cdot\,\overline{{\bf 1}_{A_{+}}(x)\,g(x)}\,dx

\displaystyle=\int\bigl(B\circ L_{\lambda}\circ B^{*}\bigr)({\bf 1}_{A_{+}}\cdot g)(x)\cdot\overline{{\bf 1}_{A_{+}}(x)\,g(x)}\,dx

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad+\int\bigl(B\circ G_{\lambda}\circ B^{*}\bigr)({\bf 1}_{A_{+}}\cdot g)(x)\cdot\overline{{\bf 1}_{A_{+}}(x)\,g(x)}\,dx

\displaystyle=I+II,

with here, and in what follows
\|h\|_{p}=\|h\|_{L^{p}(M)}
.
Here,
G_{\lambda}
is the operator whose kernel is as in (
3.1
), while
L_{\lambda}
is the “local” operator

L_{\lambda}=(2\pi T)^{-1}\int a(t)\hat{\Psi}(t/T)e^{it\lambda}e^{-itP}\,dt.

Thus,
L_{\lambda}h=T^{-1}\sum_{j}m(\lambda;\lambda_{j})E_{j}h
, where
E_{j}
denotes the projection onto the
j
th eigenspace of
P
and
the spectral multiplier
m(\lambda;\lambda_{j})=(\check{a}*\Psi_{T})(\lambda-\lambda_{j})
,
\Psi_{T}(\tau)=T\Psi(T\tau)
,
satisfies

m(\lambda;\lambda_{j})=O((1+|\lambda-\lambda_{j}|)^{-N}),\quad T\geq 1,

for any
N
. Consequently, by the universal bounds in
[
18
]
, we have

\|L_{\lambda}\|_{L^{q_{c}^{\prime}}(M)\to L^{q_{c}}(M)}\lesssim T^{-1}\lambda^{\frac{2}{q_{c}}}.

Since
T=c_{0}\log\lambda
, if we use Hölder’s inequality and (
2.5
) we find that

(3.5)

\displaystyle|I|

\displaystyle\leq\bigl\|BL_{\lambda}B^{*}\bigr({\bf 1}_{A_{+}}\cdot g\bigr)\bigr\|_{q_{c}}\cdot\|{\bf 1}_{A_{+}}\cdot g\|_{q_{c}^{\prime}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\bigl\|L_{\lambda}B^{*}\bigr({\bf 1}_{A_{+}}\cdot g\bigr)\bigr\|_{q_{c}}\cdot\|{\bf 1}_{A_{+}}\cdot g\|_{q_{c}^{\prime}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\lambda^{\frac{2}{q_{c}}}(\log\lambda)^{-1}\bigl\|B^{*}\bigr({\bf 1}_{A_{+}}\cdot g\bigr)\bigr\|_{q_{c}^{\prime}}\cdot\|{\bf 1}_{A_{+}}\cdot g\|_{q_{c}^{\prime}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\lambda^{\frac{2}{q_{c}}}(\log\lambda)^{-1}\|g\|^{2}_{L^{q_{c}^{\prime}}(A_{+})}

\displaystyle=\lambda^{\frac{2}{q_{c}}}(\log\lambda)^{-1}.

To estimate
II
, we choose
c_{0}>0
small enough
so that if
C_{0}
is the constant in (
3.1
) then

\exp(C_{0}T)\leq\lambda^{\frac{1}{8}}\quad\text{if }\,\,T=c_{0}\log\lambda\quad\text{and }\,\,\lambda\gg 1.

Consequently, (
3.1
) yields

\|G_{\lambda}\|_{L^{1}(M)\to L^{\infty}(M)}\leq\lambda^{\frac{n-1}{2}+\frac{1}{8}}.

As a result, since the dyadic operators
B
are uniformly bounded on
L^{1}
and
L^{\infty}
, we can
repeat the argument that we used to estimate
I
to see that

|II|\leq C\lambda^{\frac{n-1}{2}}\lambda^{\frac{1}{8}}\,\bigl\|{\bf 1}_{A_{+}}\cdot g\bigr\|_{1}^{2}\leq C\lambda^{\frac{n-1}{2}}\lambda^{\frac{1}{8}}\,\|g\|^{2}_{L^{q_{c}^{\prime}}(A_{+})}\,\|{\bf 1}_{A_{+}}\|_{q_{c}}^{2}=C\lambda^{\frac{n-1}{2}}\lambda^{\frac{1}{8}}\,\|{\bf 1}_{A_{+}}\|_{q_{c}}^{2}.

If we recall the definition (
2.11
) of
A_{+}
, we can estimate the last factor:

\|{\bf 1}_{A_{+}}\|_{q_{c}}^{2}\leq\bigl(\lambda^{\frac{n-1}{4}+\frac{1}{8}}\bigr)^{-2}\|\tilde{\rho}_{\lambda}f\|^{2}_{L^{q_{c}}(A_{+})}.

Therefore,

|II|\lesssim\lambda^{-1/8}\|\tilde{\rho}_{\lambda}f\|^{2}_{L^{q_{c}}(A_{+})}\leq\bigl(\tfrac{1}{2}\|\tilde{\rho}_{\lambda}f\|_{L^{q_{c}}(A_{+})}\bigr)^{2},

assuming, as we may, that
\lambda
is large enough.

If we combine this bound with the earlier one, (
3.5
), for
I
we conclude that
(
3.3
) is valid
which of course yields (
3.2
) and completes the proof of Proposition
3.1
.
∎

4.
Controlling small heights using microlocal Kakeya-Nikodym estimates

Note that, under our curvature assumption, Proposition
3.1
rules out the existence of eigenfunctions
like the zonal spherical harmonics
Z_{k}
on
S^{n}
that
maximally concentrate near points. On the other hand,
given (
1.13
) it does not rule out the existence
of eigenfunctions like the highest weight spherical harmonics since the
Q_{k}
vanish on
A_{+}
.
To complete the proof of Theorem
1.1
we also need the following result for the complementary set,
A_{-}
,
which does rule out the existence of these types of
eigenfunctions with maximal concentration near geodesics.

Proposition 4.1
.

Let
M
have nonpositive curvature. Then

(4.1)

\|\tilde{\rho}_{\lambda}f\|_{L^{q_{c}}(A_{-})}\leq C\lambda^{\frac{1}{q_{c}}}\,(\log\lambda)^{-\sigma_{n}}\,\|f\|_{L^{2}(M)},

where
\sigma_{n}
is as in Theorem
1.1
.

Let us collect the tools from
[
6
]
that we shall need to prove (
4.1
). We first
recall that the symbol
B(x,\xi)
of
B
in (
2.4
)
is supported in a conic
neighborhood of some
(x_{0},\xi_{0})\in S^{*}M
. We may
assume that its symbol has small enough support so that
we may work in a coordinate chart
\Omega
so that

x_{0}=0
,
\xi_{0}=(0,0,\dots,0,1)

and
g_{jk}(0)=\delta^{j}_{k}
in the local coordinates.
So we shall assume that the
x
-support of
B(x,\xi)

is contained in
\Omega
. We also may assume that

B(x,\xi)
vanishes when
\xi
is outside of a small
neighborhood of
(0,\dots,0,1)
. These reductions and those that
follow will contribute to the number of summands in (
2.3
); however, it will be clear that the

N
there will be independent of
\lambda\gg 1
.

The bilinear arguments we shall need to use will involve
microlocal cutoffs corresponding to angular sectors of
aperture
\approx\lambda^{-1/8}
. To construct them
write
x=(x^{\prime},x_{n})
so that
x^{\prime}=(x_{1},\dots,x_{n-1})

denotes coordinates on the hypersurface where

x_{n}=0
in our coordinates. We shall want these
cutoffs to commute with the unit-speed geodesic flow

\chi_{t}:\,S^{*}M\to S^{*}M
on the support of
B(x,\xi)

if
|t|\leq\delta
, where
\delta
is as in
(
1.3
). Recall that
\chi_{t}
is the flow
generated by the Hamilton vector field associated with
the principal symbol

p(x,\xi)=\bigl(\,\sum_{j,k}g^{jk}(x)\xi_{j}\xi_{k}\,\bigr)^{1/2}

of
P=\sqrt{-\Delta_{g}}
. Here
g^{jk}(x)=\bigl(g_{jk}(x)\bigr)^{-1}
is the cometric and so

S^{*}M=\{(x,\xi):\,p(x,\xi)=1\}
.

If
|t|\leq 2\delta
with
\delta>0
small enough then the
map

(4.2)

(t,x^{\prime},\eta)\to\chi_{t}(x^{\prime},0,\eta)\in S^{*}M,\quad(x^{\prime},0,\eta)\in S^{*}M

is a diffeomorphism from a neighborhood of
x^{\prime}=0
,
t=0

and
\eta=(0,\dots,0,1)
to a neighborhood of

(x_{0},\xi_{0})=(0,(0,\dots,0,1))\in S^{*}M
. Indeed, since we are
assuming that
g_{jk}(0)=\delta^{j}_{k}
, the Jacobian of the
map is the identity at
(x_{0},\xi_{0})
. After possibly
shrinking the support, we may assume that these
properties are valid on a neighborhood of
\text{supp }B(x,\xi)
.

Write the inverse of (
4.2
) as

(4.3)

S^{*}M\ni(x,\omega)\to\bigl(\tau(x,\omega),\Phi(x,\omega),\Theta(x,\omega)\bigr)\in(-\delta,\delta)\times\{y^{\prime}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n-1}\}\times S^{*}_{(\Phi(x,\omega),0)}M.

Thus, the unit speed geodesic passing through

(x,\omega)\in S^{*}\Omega
arrives at the plane in our local coordinates where

y_{n}=0
at
\Phi(x,\omega)
, has covector

\Theta(x,\omega)\in S^{*}_{(\Phi(x,\omega),0)}M
there, and
\tau(x,\omega)=d_{g}(x,(\Phi(x,\omega),0))
is the geodesic
distance between
x
and the point
(\Phi(x,\omega),0)
on this plane.

We can now define the microlocal cutoffs that we shall
use. First, we let
\nu
range over a

\lambda^{-\frac{1}{8}}
–separated
set in
S^{n-1}
lying in our conic neighborhood
of
\xi_{0}=(0,\dots,0,1)
. We then choose a
partition of unity
\sum\beta_{\nu}(\xi)=1
on this set
which includes a neighborhood of the
\xi
-support of
B(x,\xi)

so that each
\beta_{\nu}
is supported in a
2\lambda^{-\frac{1}{8}}
cap about
\nu\in S^{n-1}
, and so that if

\beta_{\nu}(\xi)
is the homogeneous of degree zero
extension of
\beta_{\nu}
to
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}\backslash 0
we have

|D^{\alpha}\beta_{\nu}(\xi)|\leq C_{\alpha}\lambda^{|\alpha|/8}\quad\text{if }\,\,|\xi|=1.

Finally, if
\psi\in C^{\infty}_{0}(\Omega)
equals
one in a neighborhood of the
x
-support of
B(x,\xi)
,
and if
\tilde{\beta}\in C^{\infty}_{0}((0,\infty))
equals one in a neighborhood of the support of the Littlewood-Paley bump function
in (
2.4
) we define

(4.4)

q_{\nu}(x,\xi)=\psi(x)\,\beta_{\nu}\big(\Theta(x,\xi/p(x,\xi)\bigr)\,\tilde{\beta}\bigl(p(x,\xi)/\lambda\bigr).

It then follows that the pseudo-differential operators

Q_{\nu}
with these symbols belong to a bounded subset
of
S^{0}_{7/8,1/8}(M)
. We have constructed these operators so that

(4.5)

q_{\nu}(x,\xi)=q_{\nu}\bigl(\chi_{t}(x,\xi)\bigr)\quad\text{on \, supp }B(x,\xi)\,\,\text{if }\,\,|t|\leq 2\delta.

Since

\sigma_{\lambda}=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int e^{it\lambda}e^{-itP}\,\hat{\rho}(t)\,dt

and

\hat{\rho}(t)=0
,
|t|\geq\delta
, as noted in

[
6
, (3.24)]
one can use Egorov’s theorem and the universal estimates in
[
18
]

to see that

(4.6)

\bigl\|\tilde{\sigma}_{\lambda}Q_{\nu}-Q_{\nu}\tilde{\sigma}_{\lambda}\bigr\|_{L^{2}(M)\to L^{q_{c}}(M)}\lesssim\lambda^{-\frac{1}{4}}.

In the proof of Theorem
1.1
we only need weaker
O(\lambda^{\frac{1}{q_{c}}-\frac{1}{4}})
bounds, which, by a simple exercise,
follow from (
4.5
), Sobolev estimates and the bounds in
[
18
]
.
If
\delta>0
in (
1.3
) is small enough
we also have

(4.7)

\tilde{\sigma}_{\lambda}-\sum_{\nu}\tilde{\sigma}_{\lambda}Q_{\nu}=R_{\lambda}\quad\text{where }\,\|R_{\lambda}\|_{L^{p}\to L^{p}}=O(\lambda^{-N})\quad\forall\,N\,\,\text{if }\,\,1\leq p\leq\infty.

Finally, the support properties of the symbols imply that
we have the uniform bounds

(4.8)

\sum_{\nu}\|Q_{\nu}h\|^{2}_{L^{2}(M)}\leq C\|h\|^{2}_{L^{2}(M)}.

Next, the global estimates that we shall use to prove the small heights bounds in Proposition
4.1

are the following
“global”
“
L^{q_{c}}
-microlocal Kakeya-Nikodym estimates”:

(4.9)

\sup_{\nu}\|Q_{\nu}\rho_{\lambda}\|_{L^{2}(M)\to L^{q_{c}}(M)}\lesssim\lambda^{\frac{1}{q_{c}}}\cdot\bigl(\log\lambda\bigr)^{-\frac{1}{q_{c}}}.

This, like (
3.1
), is valid when
(M,g)
has non-positive sectional curvatures, although in the next section we shall see that stronger bounds hold if the sectional curvatures all assumed to be negative.
The inequality (
4.9
) is a slight improvement
over the corresponding estimate (2.18) in
[
6
]
.

One obtains (
4.9
) via a simple interpolation argument and the following pointwise microlocalized estimates

(4.10)

K_{\lambda,\mu}(x,y)=T^{-1}\int\hat{\Psi}(t/T)\beta(t/\mu)\,e^{it\lambda}\Bigl(Q_{\nu}\circ\cos(tP)\circ Q_{\nu}^{*}\Bigr)(x,y)\,dt\\
=O\bigl(T^{-1}\,\mu^{1-\frac{n-1}{2}}\lambda^{\frac{n-1}{2}}\bigr),\,\,\Psi=(\rho)^{2},\,\,\quad 1\leq\mu\leq T=4c_{0}\log\lambda,

if
\beta\in C^{\infty}_{0}((1/2,2))
is a Littlewood-Paley bump function
satisfying
1=\sum_{-\infty}^{\infty}\beta(t/2^{j})
for
t>0
,
and
c_{0}>0
is sufficiently small. To prove this
one uses the Hadamard parametrix after lifting
the calculation up to the universal cover.
The argument is almost identical to the proof of
(3.8) in
[
5
]
or
the proof of Theorem 2.1 in
[
6
]
. The
pointwise bounds in (
4.10
) are more
favorable than those in (
3.1
) since the result of the
microlocalization in (
4.10
) is that
number of significant terms in the calculation only grows linearly in
T
, unlike in the
proof of (
3.1
) where there is exponential growth.
For more details, see the remark at the
end of §
5
.

By (
4.10
), the integral operator with kernel

K_{\lambda,\mu}
maps
L^{1}(M)\to L^{\infty}(M)
with norm

O\bigl(T^{-1}\,\mu^{1-\frac{n-1}{2}}\lambda^{\frac{n-1}{2}}\bigr)
. By orthogonality it also maps
L^{2}\to L^{2}
with norm

O(T^{-1}\mu)
. So by interpolation, it maps
L^{q_{c}^{\prime}}\to L^{q_{c}}
with norm
O(T^{-1}\mu^{\frac{2}{n+1}}\lambda^{\frac{n-1}{n+1}})
.
By taking
\mu=2^{j}\lesssim T
and adding up these
dyadic estimates we find that if
a
is as in (
3.1
) we have that the operator with kernel

T^{-1}\int\hat{\Psi}(t/T)(1-a(t))\,e^{it\lambda}\Bigl(Q_{\nu}\circ\cos(tP)\circ Q_{\nu}^{*}\Bigr)(x,y)\,dt

maps
L^{q_{c}^{\prime}}\to L^{q_{c}}
with norm
O((\lambda/T)^{\frac{2}{q_{c}}})
. Finally, since the local estimates from
[
18
]
show that when we
replace
(1-a(t))
above by
a(t)
the resulting integral operator maps
L^{q_{c}^{\prime}}\to L^{q_{c}}
with a better norm norm
O(T^{-1}\lambda^{\frac{2}{q_{c}}})
, we must have

(4.11)

\sup_{\nu}\|Q_{\nu}\Psi(T(\lambda-P))Q_{\nu}^{*}\|_{L^{q_{c}^{\prime}}(M)\to L^{q_{c}}(M)}\lesssim\lambda^{\frac{2}{q_{c}}}\cdot\bigl(\log\lambda\bigr)^{-\frac{2}{q_{c}}},

which implies (
4.9
) by a simple “
TT^{*}
” argument.

Bilinear decomposition

We also require the (local) bilinear harmonic arguments from
[
6
]
that take advantage of the
fact that the norm in (
4.1
) is taken over
A_{-}
. These techniques go back to the bilinear Fourier restriction arguments from
Tao
[
24
]
, Tao, Vargas and Vega
[
25
]
, Lee
[
16
]
, Wolff
[
27
]
and others.

We shall write using (
2.10
) and (
4.7
)

(4.12)

\bigl(\tilde{\rho}_{\lambda}f\bigr)^{2}=\sum_{\nu,\tilde{\nu}}(\tilde{\sigma}_{\lambda}Q_{\nu}h)\cdot(\tilde{\sigma}_{\lambda}Q_{\tilde{\nu}}h)+O(\lambda^{-N}),\quad h=\rho_{\lambda}f.

We shall organize the pairs of directions

(\nu,\tilde{\nu})\in S^{n-1}\times S^{n-1}
just as
in
[
25
]
. So, let us write

\nu=(\nu^{\prime},\nu_{n})
where
\nu^{\prime}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n-1}

is near the origin since
\nu
is close
to
(0,\dots,0,1)
.

To this end, consider the collection of
dyadic cubes
\{\tau^{j}_{\mu^{\prime}}\}
in
{\mathbb{R}}^{n-1}

of side length
2^{j}
, where
\tau^{j}_{\mu^{\prime}}
denotes
the translation of
[0,2^{j})^{n-1}
by

\mu^{\prime}\in 2^{j}{\mathbb{Z}}^{n-1}
. Two such cubes
are said to be “close” if they are not adjacent
but have adjacent parents of sidelength
2^{j+1}
.
If this is the case, we write
\tau^{j}_{\mu^{\prime}}\sim\tau^{j}_{\tilde{\mu}^{\prime}}
. Close cubes are separated
by a distance which is comparable to
2^{j}
. As noted
in
[
25
]
, any distinct
\nu^{\prime},\tilde{\nu}^{\prime}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n-1}
lie in a unique pair of
close cubes. Thus, there is a unique triple

j,\mu^{\prime}\tilde{\mu}^{\prime}
such that

(\nu^{\prime},\tilde{\nu}^{\prime})\in\tau^{j}_{\mu^{\prime}}\times\tau^{j}_{\tilde{\mu}^{\prime}}
and
\tau^{j}_{\mu^{\prime}}\sim\tau^{j}_{\tilde{\mu}^{\prime}}
. Since

\nu^{\prime}
is close to the origin for our
\nu\in S^{n-1}
,
we need only to consider integers
j\leq 0
.

If we then let
J\ll 0
be the integer satisfying

2^{J-1}<8\lambda^{-1/8}\leq 2^{J}
, it follows that the
sum in (
4.12
) can be organized as

(4.13)

\left(\sum_{J+1\leq j\leq 0}\,\,\sum_{(\nu^{\prime},\tilde{\nu}^{\prime})\in\{\tau^{j}_{\mu^{\prime}}\times\tau^{j}_{\tilde{\mu}^{\prime}}:\,\tau^{j}_{\mu^{\prime}}\sim\tau^{j}_{\tilde{\mu}^{\prime}}\}}+\sum_{(\nu^{\prime},\tilde{\nu}^{\prime})\in\Xi_{J}}\right)\bigl(\tilde{\sigma}_{\lambda}Q_{\nu}h\bigr)\cdot\bigl(\tilde{\sigma}_{\lambda}Q_{\tilde{\nu}}h\bigr),

where
\Xi_{J}
denotes the remaining pairs not included
in the first sum. These include diagonal pairs where

\nu^{\prime}=\tilde{\nu}^{\prime}
, and all
(\nu^{\prime},\tilde{\nu}^{\prime})\in\Xi_{J}
satisfy
|\nu^{\prime}-\tilde{\nu}^{\prime}|\lesssim\lambda^{-1/8}
.
Thus, for each fixed
\nu^{\prime}
, we have

\#\{\tilde{\nu}^{\prime}:\,(\nu^{\prime},\tilde{\nu}^{\prime})\in\Xi_{J}\}=O(1)
.

As in
[
6
]
, write

(4.14)

\Upsilon^{\text{diag}}(h)=\sum_{(\nu^{\prime},\tilde{\nu}^{\prime})\in\Xi_{J}}\bigl(\tilde{\sigma}_{\lambda}Q_{\nu}h\bigr)\cdot\bigl(\tilde{\sigma}_{\lambda}Q_{\tilde{\nu}}h\bigr),\\
\text{if }\,\,\lambda^{-1/8}\in(2^{J-4},2^{J-3}],\,\,\text{and }\quad h=\rho_{\lambda}f.

We shall estimate the
L^{q_{c}/2}
-norm of this “diagonal” term using results from
[
6
]
.

Next, if we consider the first sum in the left of (
4.13
) over
(\nu^{\prime},\tilde{\nu}^{\prime})\notin\Xi_{J}
along with the error term in (
4.12
),

(4.15)

\Upsilon^{\text{far}}(h)=\sum_{J+1\leq j\leq 0}\sum_{(\nu^{\prime},\tilde{\nu}^{\prime})\in\{\tau^{j}_{\mu^{\prime}}\times\tau^{j}_{\tilde{\mu}^{\prime}}:\,\tau^{j}_{\mu^{\prime}}\sim\tau^{j}_{\tilde{\mu}^{\prime}}\}}\bigl(\tilde{\sigma}_{\lambda}Q_{\nu}h\bigr)\cdot\bigl(\tilde{\sigma}_{\lambda}Q_{\tilde{\nu}}h\bigr)+O(\lambda^{-N}),

we have
the favorable bilinear estimates

(4.16)

\int|\Upsilon^{\text{far}}(h)|^{q/2}\,dx\leq C\lambda\,\bigl(\lambda^{\frac{7}{8}}\bigr)^{\frac{n-1}{2}(q-q_{c})}\|h\|_{L^{2}(M)}^{q},\,\,\text{if }\,\,q\in(\tfrac{2(n+2)}{n},q_{c}).

For, in the notation of
[
6
]
,

\Upsilon^{\text{far}}(h)
here equals

\Upsilon^{\text{off}}(h)+\Upsilon^{\text{smooth}}(h)
together with
the error term in (
4.12
), and so
(
4.16
) follows from
summing over
j\in[J+1,0]
in inequality
(4.7) in
[
6
]
and the fact that, like the
error term in (
4.12
),

\|\Upsilon^{\text{smooth}}(h)\|_{q_{c}/2}\lesssim\lambda^{-N}\|h\|^{2}_{2}
for all
N
, due to
(4.5) in
[
6
]
. Based on this, one obtains
(
4.16
). We note that the estimates
in
[
6
]
for the non-trivial part,

\Upsilon^{\text{off}}
of
\Upsilon^{\text{far}}
are based
on the bilinear oscillatory integral estimates
of Lee
[
16
]
. The terms
\Upsilon^{\text{smooth}}
in

[
6
]
were trivial microlocal error terms to allow us to use
those estimates via parabolic scaling. We have opted to lump together these
two terms into (
4.15
) to simplify the notation and argument a tiny bit in what follows.

The significance of (
4.16
) is that the
second power of
\lambda
in the right is negative
as
q<q_{c}
there. On the other hand, this makes it somewhat
awkward to take advantage of this power-improvement
to prove the
L^{q_{c}}
bound in our remaining
inequality (
4.1
). However, as in
[
6
]
, we shall
be able to use (
4.16
) since the norm in
the left hand side of (
4.1
) is taken
over
A_{-}
.
We also note that, we have rewritten the left side of (
4.12
) as follows

(4.17)

(\tilde{\rho}_{\lambda}f)^{2}=(\tilde{\sigma}_{\lambda}h)^{2}=\Upsilon^{\text{diag}}(h)+\Upsilon^{\text{far}}(h),\quad h=\rho_{\lambda}f.

Next, let us use the fact that we can use (
4.16
) and (
4.17
) to prove a slightly
stronger version of estimates in
[
6
]
that will allow us to prove (
4.1
).
Specifically, as we shall show in the next subsection, we can use (
4.16
) to obtain the following

(4.18)

\|\tilde{\sigma}_{\lambda}h\|_{L^{q_{c}}(A_{-})}\leq C\lambda^{\frac{1}{q_{c}}}\lambda^{-\delta_{n}}\|h\|_{L^{2}(M)}+C\Bigl(\sum_{\nu}\|\tilde{\sigma}_{\lambda}Q_{\nu}h\|_{L^{q_{c}}(M)}^{q_{c}}\Bigr)^{1/q_{c}},\,\,h=\rho_{\lambda}f,

for certain
\delta_{n}>0
.

Let us now see how we can use (
4.18
) along with the microlocal Kakeya-Nikodym estimate (
4.9
) to prove Proposition
4.1
.

Proof of Proposition
4.1
.

In this proof, let us express the microlocal log-power gain in (
4.9
) as

(4.19)

\varepsilon(\lambda)=(\log\lambda)^{-\frac{1}{q_{c}}}.

With
h=\rho_{\lambda}f
, we have from (
4.18
) that for
n\geq 2

(4.20)

\|\tilde{\rho}_{\lambda}f||_{L^{q_{c}}(A_{-})}\leq C\lambda^{\frac{1}{q_{c}}}(\log\lambda)^{-1}\|f\|_{L^{2}(M)}+C\Bigl(\sum_{\nu}\|\tilde{\sigma}_{\lambda}Q_{\nu}\rho_{\lambda}f\|_{L^{q_{c}}(M)}^{q_{c}}\Bigr)^{1/q_{c}}.

Note that the universal (local) spectral projection bounds from
[
18
]
and (
2.5
) yield

(4.21)

\|\tilde{\sigma}_{\lambda}\|_{L^{2}\to L^{q_{c}}}=O(\lambda^{\frac{1}{q_{c}}}).

Using this and the fact that the
Q_{\nu}
are almost orthogonal, we find that

(4.22)

\sum_{\nu}\|\tilde{\sigma}_{\lambda}Q_{\nu}\rho_{\lambda}f\|_{L^{q_{c}}(M)}^{2}\lesssim\lambda^{\frac{2}{q_{c}}}\sum_{\nu}\|Q_{\nu}\rho_{\lambda}f\|_{2}^{2}\lesssim\lambda^{\frac{2}{q_{c}}}\|\rho_{\lambda}f\|_{2}^{2}\lesssim\lambda^{\frac{2}{q_{c}}}\|f\|_{2}^{2}.

Next, note that by (
4.6
) and the fact that
\|\rho_{\lambda}\|_{L^{2}\to L^{2}}=O(1)
, we have

(4.23)

\|\tilde{\sigma}_{\lambda}Q_{\nu}\rho_{\lambda}f\|_{L^{q_{c}}(M)}\lesssim\|Q_{\nu}\tilde{\sigma}_{\lambda}\rho_{\lambda}f\|_{L^{q_{c}}(M)}+\lambda^{-\frac{1}{4}}\|f\|_{L^{2}(M)}.

Additionally,

is in

and band limited (see

)
we have
\|[Q_{\nu},B]\|_{L^{q_{c}}\to L^{q_{c}}}=O(\lambda^{-\frac{3}{4}})
. So, by (

) and (

)

(4.24)

\displaystyle\|Q_{\nu}\tilde{\sigma}_{\lambda}\rho_{\lambda}f\|_{L^{q_{c}}(M)}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|BQ_{\nu}\sigma_{\lambda}\rho_{\lambda}f\|_{L^{q_{c}}(M)}+\lambda^{\frac{1}{q_{c}}-\frac{3}{4}}\|f\|_{L^{2}(M)}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|Q_{\nu}\sigma_{\lambda}\rho_{\lambda}f\|_{L^{q_{c}}(M)}+\lambda^{\frac{1}{q_{c}}-\frac{3}{4}}\|f\|_{L^{2}(M)}.

If we combine (
4.23
) and (
4.24
), since the microlocal cutoffs
Q_{\nu}
are uniformly
bounded on all

spaces, we conclude that

\displaystyle\|\tilde{\sigma}_{\lambda}Q_{\nu}\rho_{\lambda}f\|_{L^{q_{c}}(M)}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|Q_{\nu}\sigma_{\lambda}\rho_{\lambda}f\|_{L^{q_{c}}(M)}+\lambda^{\frac{1}{q_{c}}-\frac{1}{4}}\|f\|_{L^{2}(M)}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|Q_{\nu}\rho_{\lambda}f\|_{L^{q_{c}}(M)}+\|Q_{\nu}\circ(I-\sigma_{\lambda})\rho_{\lambda}f\|_{L^{q_{c}}(M)}+\lambda^{\frac{1}{q_{c}}-\frac{1}{4}}\|f\|_{L^{2}(M)}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|Q_{\nu}\rho_{\lambda}f\|_{L^{q_{c}}(M)}+\|(I-\sigma_{\lambda})\rho_{\lambda}f\|_{L^{q_{c}}(M)}+\lambda^{\frac{1}{q_{c}}-\frac{1}{4}}\|f\|_{L^{2}(M)}.

If we use the first part of (
2.8
) and our Kakeya-Nikodym bounds (
4.9
) to estimate the first two terms in the right
we conclude that

(4.25)

\|\tilde{\sigma}_{\lambda}Q_{\nu}\rho_{\lambda}f\|_{L^{q_{c}}(M)}\lesssim\lambda^{\frac{1}{q_{c}}}\,\varepsilon(\lambda)\,\|f\|_{L^{2}(M)},

with

as in (

If we combine (
4.20

4.22
) and (

\displaystyle\|\tilde{\rho}_{\lambda}f\|_{L^{q_{c}}(A_{-})}

\displaystyle\lesssim\sup_{\nu}\|\tilde{\sigma}_{\lambda}Q_{\nu}\rho_{\lambda}f\|_{L^{q_{c}}(M)}^{\frac{q_{c}-2}{q_{c}}}\cdot\bigl(\,\sum_{\nu}\|\tilde{\sigma}_{\lambda}Q_{\nu}\rho_{\lambda}f\|_{L^{q_{c}}(M)}^{2}\,\bigr)^{\frac{1}{q_{c}}}+\lambda^{\frac{1}{q_{c}}}(\log\lambda)^{-1}\|f\|_{L^{2}(M)}

\displaystyle\lesssim\bigl(\lambda^{\frac{1}{q_{c}}}\varepsilon(\lambda))^{\frac{q_{c}-2}{q_{c}}}\,(\lambda^{\frac{2}{q_{c}}})^{\frac{1}{q_{c}}}\|f\|_{L^{2}(M)}+\lambda^{\frac{1}{q_{c}}}(\log\lambda)^{-1}\|f\|_{L^{2}(M)}

\displaystyle\lesssim\lambda^{\frac{1}{q_{c}}}\,\bigl(\varepsilon(\lambda)\bigr)^{\frac{q_{c}-2}{q_{c}}}\|f\|_{2}

\displaystyle=\lambda^{\frac{1}{q_{c}}}\bigl(\varepsilon(\lambda)\bigr)^{\frac{2}{n+1}}\,\|f\|_{2}.

This combined with (

\|\tilde{\rho}_{\lambda}f\|_{L^{q_{c}}(M)}\lesssim\lambda^{\frac{1}{q_{c}}}(\varepsilon(\lambda))^{\frac{2}{n+1}}\,\|f\|_{L^{2}(M)},

if one recalls (

\|\rho_{\lambda}f\|_{L^{6}(M)}\lesssim\lambda^{\frac{1}{6}}\,(\log\lambda)^{-\frac{1}{3}}\,\|f\|_{L^{2}(M)},\quad n=2,

\|\rho_{\lambda}f\|_{L^{4}(M)}\lesssim\lambda^{\frac{1}{4}}(\log\lambda)^{-\frac{1}{4}}\,\|f\|_{L^{2}(M)},\quad n=3.

Estimates (

[

[

]
, since we were interested in results for general manifolds of non-positive
curvature and the techniques in these papers or
[
6
]
only provided somewhat modest improvements for spectral projection estimates.

Turning to the proof of (
5.1
), just as in
[
5
]
, we shall want to make use of the fact that if
Q_{\nu}(x,y)
denotes the
kernels of our microlocal cutoffs there, then we have the uniform bounds

(5.5)

\sup_{x}\int|Q_{\nu}(x,y)|\,dy\leq C.

This implies that
Q_{\nu}:\,L^{\infty}(M)\to L^{\infty}(M)
uniformly, and, since they are also uniformly bounded on
L^{2}(M)
, by interpolation and duality,
we conclude that

(5.6)

\|Q_{\nu}\|_{L^{p}(M)\to L^{p}(M)}=O(1),\quad 2\leq p\leq\infty\\
\text{and }\,\,\|Q^{*}_{\nu}\|_{L^{p}(M)\to L^{p}(M)}=O(1),\quad 1\leq p\leq 2.

Also, of course if, as in (
4.10
),
\Psi=\rho^{2}
, then (
5.1
) is equivalent to the estimate

(5.7)

\bigl\|\,Q_{\nu}\Psi(T(\lambda-P))Q^{*}_{\nu}\,\bigr\|_{L^{q_{c}^{\prime}}(M)\to L^{q_{c}}(M)}\lesssim\lambda^{2/q_{c}}\,(\log\lambda)^{-1},

if, as before,

(5.8)

T=c_{0}\log\lambda,

with
c_{0}>0
sufficiently small.

By (
5.6
), this would be a consequence of

(5.9)

\bigl\|\,Q_{\nu}\Psi(T(\lambda-P))\,\bigr\|_{L^{q_{c}^{\prime}}(M)\to L^{q_{c}}(M)}\lesssim\lambda^{2/q_{c}}\,(\log\lambda)^{-1}.

The proof of this will closely follow the related
L^{2}\to L^{2}
Kakeya-Nikodym estimate (3.6) in
[
5
]
.

First, as was done in the proof of Proposition
3.1
, let us split our smoothed out spectral projection operators

\Psi(T(\lambda-P))
into the sum of a “local” and “global” part. This time, since we shall want to dyadically decompose
the “global” piece, let us assume, as before, that our Littlewood-Paley function
\beta\in C^{\infty}_{0}((1/2,2))
satisfies

\sum_{j=-\infty}^{\infty}\beta(t/2^{j})=1
,
t>0
. We then let

\beta_{0}(t)=1-\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\beta(j/2^{j})\in C^{\infty}_{0}({\mathbb{R}}_{+}),

and note that
\beta_{0}(t)\equiv 1
for
t>0
near the origin. The “local” operator then is

L_{\lambda}=\frac{1}{2\pi T}\int e^{i\lambda t}e^{-itP}\beta_{0}(|t|)\,\hat{\Psi}(t/T)\,dt,

and we have

\|Q_{\nu}L_{\lambda}\|_{L^{q^{\prime}_{c}}(M)\to L^{q_{c}}(M)}=O(\lambda^{2/q_{c}}\,(\log\lambda)^{-1})

using (
5.6
), (
5.7
) and the universal local spectral projection estimates in
[
18
]
just as we did
in the proof of Proposition
3.1
.

As a result of these local bounds, we would have (
5.9
) and be done if we could show that

(5.10)

\|Q_{\nu}G_{\lambda}\|_{L^{q^{\prime}_{c}}(M)\to L^{q_{c}}(M)}=O(\lambda^{2/q_{c}}\,(\log\lambda)^{-1}),

if

(5.11)

G_{\lambda}=\frac{1}{2\pi T}\int e^{i\lambda t}e^{-itP}\,\bigl(1-\beta_{0}(|t|)\bigr)\,\hat{\Psi}(t/T)\,dt.

Note that if we replace
e^{-itP}
by
e^{itP}
here, then the resulting operator has a kernel which
is
O(\lambda^{-N})
and so trivially enjoys the bounds in (
5.10
).

Consequently, by Euler’s formula, it suffices to show that if

(5.12)

\tilde{G}_{\lambda}=\frac{1}{\pi T}\int e^{i\lambda t}\cos(t\sqrt{-\Delta_{g}})\,\bigl(1-\beta_{0}(|t|)\bigr)\,\hat{\Psi}(t/T)\,dt,

then

(5.13)

\|Q_{\nu}\tilde{G}_{\lambda}\|_{L^{q^{\prime}_{c}}(M)\to L^{q_{c}}(M)}=O(\lambda^{2/q_{c}}\,(\log\lambda)^{-1}).

If we let for
\mu=2^{j}\geq 1

(5.14)

\tilde{G}_{\lambda,\mu}=\frac{1}{\pi T}\int e^{i\lambda t}\cos(t\sqrt{-\Delta_{g}})\,\beta(|t|/\mu)\,\hat{\Psi}(t/T)\,dt,

then since

\tilde{G}_{\lambda}=\sum_{\{\mu=2^{j}:\,j\geq 0\}}\tilde{G}_{\lambda,\mu},

in order to prove (
5.13
), it suffices to show that

(5.15)

\|Q_{\nu}\tilde{G}_{\lambda,\mu}\|_{L^{q^{\prime}_{c}}(M)\to L^{q_{c}}(M)}\leq C\lambda^{2/q_{c}}\,(\log\lambda)^{-1}\,\mu^{-1}.

We note that since
\hat{\Psi}
is compactly supported, by Huygens’ principle,
\tilde{G}_{\lambda,\mu}=0
if
\mu
is larger than
a fixed multiple of
T
as in (
5.8
).

As before, we shall prove (
5.15
) using
interpolation.
To this end, we first note that since the Fourier transform of

t\to T^{-1}\beta(|t|/\mu)\hat{\Psi}(t/T)

is
O(\mu/T)
, by (
5.6
) and orthogonality, we have

(5.16)

\|Q_{\nu}\tilde{G}_{\lambda}\|_{L^{2}(M)\to L^{2}(M)}=O(\mu/T).

The other estimate that we shall use, which requires our curvature assumption, is that

(5.17)

\|Q_{\nu}\tilde{G}_{\lambda}\|_{L^{1}(M)\to L^{\infty}(M)}\leq C_{K,N}T^{-1}\lambda^{\frac{n-1}{2}}\mu^{-N},\quad N=1,2,\dots,

if all of the sectional curvatures of
(M,g)
are assumed to be
\leq-K^{2}<0
. This is analogous to
(3.8
′
) in
[
5
]
which did not include the rapid decay
\mu^{-N}
since the
curvatures there were merely assumed to be non-positive. Also, there, we did not break things up dyadically as we have done so here.

By a simple interpolation argument (as in the proof of (
4.9
) above), (
5.16
) and (
5.17
) imply (
5.15
).
So, we have reduced matters to establishing (
5.17
).

Just as in
[
5
]
and other works, we have switched from the half-wave operator in (
5.11
) to
the use of
\cos t\sqrt{-\Delta_{g}}
so that we can use the Hadamard parametrix and the Cartan-Hadamard theorem to lift the
calculations that will be needed for (
5.17
) up to the universal cover
({\mathbb{R}}^{n},\tilde{g})
of
(M,g)
. This approach was used
earlier in
[
5
]
and
[
23
]
.

To this end, let
\{\alpha\}=\Gamma
denote the group of deck transformation preserving the associated
covering map
\kappa:\,{\mathbb{R}}^{n}\to M
coming from the exponential map at the point in
M
with coordinates
0
in
\Omega
above.
The metric
\tilde{g}
on
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
that we mentioned above is the pullback of
g
via
\kappa
. Choose a Dirichlet domain
D\simeq M
for
M
centered at the lift of the point with coordinates
0
.

Following
[
2
]
,
[
23
]
and others, we recall that if
\tilde{x}
denotes the lift of
x\in M
to
D
, then we have the following formula

\bigl(\cos t\sqrt{-\Delta_{g}}\bigr)(x,y)=\sum_{\alpha\in\Gamma}\bigl(\cos t\sqrt{-\Delta_{\tilde{g}}}\bigr)(\tilde{x},\alpha(\tilde{y})),

which is a cousin of the classical Poisson summation formula. As a result, for later use,
if
K_{\lambda,\mu}(x,y)
denotes the kernel of
\tilde{G}_{\lambda,\mu}
then we have the formula

(5.18)

K_{\lambda,\mu}(x,y)=\sum_{\alpha\in\Gamma}K_{\lambda,\mu}(\tilde{x},\alpha(\tilde{y})),\\
\text{if }\,\,K_{\lambda,\mu}(\tilde{x},\tilde{y})=\frac{1}{\pi T}\int e^{i\lambda t}\,\bigl(\cos t\sqrt{-\Delta_{\tilde{g}}}\bigr)(\tilde{x},\tilde{y})\,\beta(|t|/\mu)\,\hat{\Psi}(t/T)\,dt.

The key estimate for us, which is an improvement of (3.8) in
[
5
]
and requires our
curvature assumption is that

(5.19)

|K_{\lambda,\mu}(\tilde{x},\tilde{y})|\leq C_{K,N}T^{-1}\,\lambda^{\frac{n-1}{2}}\,\bigl(d_{\tilde{g}}(\tilde{x},\tilde{y})\bigr)^{-\frac{n-1}{2}}\mu^{-N},\quad N=1,2,\dots.

To prove this, we note that this kernel vanishes if
d_{\tilde{g}}(\tilde{x},\tilde{y})
is larger than
2\mu
by the Huygens principle
since
\beta(|t|/\mu)=0
for
|t|\geq 2\mu
. Also, since this function also vanishes for
|t|\leq\mu/2
, it is straightforward to use
the Hadamard parametrix to see that
K_{\lambda,\mu}(\tilde{x},\tilde{y})=O(\lambda^{-N})
for every
N
if

d_{\tilde{g}}(\tilde{x},\tilde{y})\leq\mu/4
, which is better than the bounds in (
5.19
) as

\mu\lesssim\log\lambda\ll\lambda
.

Due to these simple facts, to prove (
5.19
), we may assume that
d_{\tilde{g}}(\tilde{x},\tilde{y})\approx\mu
. We shall
make use of the Hadamard parametrix for
\cos t\sqrt{-\Delta_{\tilde{g}}}
. As is well known and described, for instance, in

[
2
]
,
[
20
]
and
[
23
]
, the leading term in this parametrix is of the form

(5.20)

w_{0}(\tilde{x},\tilde{y})\cdot(2\pi)^{-n}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}e^{id_{\tilde{g}}(\tilde{x},\tilde{y})\xi_{1}}\,\cos(t|\xi|)\,d\xi,

where in geodesic normal coordinates about
\tilde{x}
this leading coefficient is given by

(5.21)

w_{0}(\tilde{x},\tilde{y})=\bigl(\,\text{det }\tilde{g}_{{}_{ij}}(\tilde{y})\,\bigr)^{-1/4}.

Thus, if in geodesic polar coordinates the volume element is given by

dV_{\tilde{g}}(\tilde{y})=\bigl(\,{\cal A}(t,\omega)\,\bigr)^{n-1}\,dtd\omega,\,\,\,t=d_{\tilde{g}}(\tilde{x},\tilde{y}),

then

w_{0}(\tilde{x},\tilde{y})=\bigl(\,t/{\mathcal{A}}(t,\omega)\,\bigr)^{\frac{n-1}{2}}.

By the classical Günther comparison theorem from Riemannian geometry (see
[
11
, §III.4]
)

(5.22)

{\mathcal{A}}(t,\omega)\geq\frac{1}{K}\,\sinh(Kt),

if, as we are assuming all the sectional curvatures are
\leq-K^{2}<0
.
Thus,

(5.23)

w_{0}(\tilde{x},\tilde{y})\leq C_{K,N}\mu^{-N}\quad\text{if }\,\,\,d_{\tilde{g}}(\tilde{x},\tilde{y})\approx\mu.

Also, a routine stationary phase calculation yields

(5.24)

T^{-1}\iint e^{i\lambda t}e^{id_{\tilde{g}}(\tilde{x},\tilde{y})\xi_{1}}\,\cos(t|\xi|)\,\beta(|t|/\mu)\,\hat{\Psi}(t/T)\,dtd\xi\\
=O(T^{-1}\lambda^{\frac{n-1}{2}}\,(d_{\tilde{g}}(\tilde{x},\tilde{y}))^{-\frac{n-1}{2}}),\quad\text{if }\,\,d_{\tilde{g}}(\tilde{x},\tilde{y})\approx\mu\geq 1.

Due to (
5.23
) and (
5.24
), if we replace
(\cos t\sqrt{-\Delta_{\tilde{g}}})(\tilde{x},\tilde{y})
in
(
5.18
) with the leading term (
5.20
) in its Hadamard parametrix, the resulting expression will
satisfy the bounds in (
5.19
). Since one can also use stationary phase to see that the lower order terms in the
Hadamard parametrix (see, e.g.,
[
20
, Theorem 2.4.1]
) will each make contributions
which are
O(\lambda^{\frac{n-1}{2}-1})
which is better than the bounds posited in (
5.19
), we obtain this bound.

Unfortunately, although (
5.19
) is sufficient for obtaining bounds like (
3.1
), it, by itself, can not be sufficient for
proving (
5.17
) since there are
\exp(c\mu)
terms in the sum in (
5.18
) with
d_{\tilde{g}}(\tilde{x},\tilde{y})\approx\mu
.
To prove (
5.17
), as in
[
5
]
, we need to use the fact that (
5.17
) includes the microlocal
cutoff which means that the kernel of
Q_{\nu}\tilde{G}_{\lambda,\mu}
,

(5.25)

\sum_{\alpha\in\Gamma}\frac{1}{\pi T}\int e^{i\lambda t}\,\bigl(Q_{\nu}\cos t\sqrt{-\Delta_{\tilde{g}}}\bigr)(\tilde{x},\alpha(\tilde{y}))\,\beta(|t|/\mu)\,\hat{\Psi}(t/T)\,dt,

only involves
O(\mu)
non-trivial terms. In the above
Q_{\nu}
denotes the pullback of the operator
Q_{\nu}
to the fundamental domain
D
.

To see this we shall argue exactly as in
[
5
]
. We first recall that through the point
x\in\Omega
there
is a unique geodesic
\gamma_{x,\nu}
which passes through the plane
z_{n}=0
with covector
\xi_{\nu}
at this point in the plane.
After modifying the coordinates in
\Omega
, we may assume that
z^{\prime}=0
at the intersection point in the plane.
Then, as in
[
5
]
, we let
\tilde{\gamma}(t)
,
t\in{\mathbb{R}}
denote the lift of the projection of the geodesic

\gamma
in
\Omega
to the universal cover and

{\mathcal{T}}_{R}(\tilde{\gamma})=\{\tilde{z}:\,d_{\tilde{g}}(\tilde{\gamma},\tilde{z})\leq R\}

denote an
R
-tube about
\tilde{\gamma}
. Then, just as in
[
5
]
, if
R
is fixed sufficiently large and

\alpha(D)\cap{\mathcal{T}}_{R}(\tilde{\gamma})=\emptyset
, the corresponding summand in (
5.25
) is
O(\lambda^{-1})
by Toponogov’s theorem
and microlocal arguments. Indeed, this is exactly how (3.9) in
[
5
]
was proved and one can
simply repeat the arguments there. Since there are
O(\lambda^{1/2})
non-zero terms in (
5.25
) if
c_{0}
in (
5.8
) is fixed small enough, we obtain in this case

\sum_{\{\alpha:\,\alpha(D)\cap{\mathcal{T}}_{R}(\tilde{\gamma})=\emptyset\}}\frac{1}{\pi T}\int e^{i\lambda t}\,\bigl(Q_{\nu}\cos t\sqrt{-\Delta_{\tilde{g}}}\bigr)(\tilde{x},\alpha(\tilde{y}))\,\beta(|t|/\mu)\,\hat{\Psi}(t/T)\,dt=O(\lambda^{-1/2}).

Therefore, the operator with this kernel maps
L^{1}(M)
to
L^{\infty}(M)
with norm
O(\lambda^{-1/2})
, which is much better than
the bounds in (
5.17
) as
\mu\leq\log\lambda
.

Since (
5.17
) is equivalent to the statement that the kernel of
Q_{\nu}\tilde{G}_{\lambda,\mu}
has sup-norm bounded by the
right side of (
5.17
), it suffices to show that the remaining part of the sum in (
5.25
) where
\alpha(D)\cap{\mathcal{T}}_{R}(\tilde{\gamma})\neq\emptyset
can be bounded by the right side of (
5.17
). As above, the terms where

d_{\tilde{g}}(\tilde{x},\alpha(\tilde{y}))
is not comparable to
\mu
yield
O(\lambda^{-N})
contributions and so can
be ignored. Thus, since, by (
5.6
),
Q_{\nu}:\,L^{\infty}\to L^{\infty}
, our task now would be complete if we could show that

(5.26)

\sum_{\{\alpha:\,\alpha(D)\cap{\mathcal{T}}_{R}(\tilde{\gamma})\neq\emptyset,\,\text{and }\,d_{\tilde{g}}(\tilde{x},\alpha(\tilde{y}))\approx\mu\}}\frac{1}{T}\Bigl|\,\int e^{i\lambda t}\,\bigl(\cos t\sqrt{-\Delta_{\tilde{g}}}\bigr)(\tilde{x},\alpha(\tilde{y}))\,\beta(|t|/\mu)\,\hat{\Psi}(t/T)\,dt\,\Bigr|\\
\leq C_{K,N}T^{-1}\,\lambda^{\frac{n-1}{2}}\,\mu^{-N},\quad N=1,2,\dots.

It is easy to see directly or by arguments in
[
5
]
, this sum only involves
O(\mu)
terms. By
(
5.19
), each is of these is
O(T^{-1}\lambda^{\frac{n-1}{2}}\mu^{-N})
for any
N
, which yields (
5.26
) and completes
the proof.

Remark:
This argument also yields the
microlocal Kakeya-Nikodym estimate (
4.9
) in which
we were merely assuming that
(M,g)
has non-positive
curvature. In this case, the leading coefficient of
the Hadamard parametrix is no longer rapidly decreasing. So,
we cannot use (
5.23
). However, we do have that

w_{0}
is bounded also by the Günther comparison
theorem. If we repeat the above arguments, we would
see that, up to an
O(T^{-1}\lambda^{\frac{n-1}{2}}\mu^{-N})
error, the kernels
K_{\lambda,\mu}
in
(
4.10
) would be dominated by the left side of
(
5.26
). Since
w_{0}
is bounded, by (
5.24
),
each summand is
O(T^{-1}\lambda^{\frac{n-1}{2}}\mu^{-\frac{n-1}{2}})
since
d_{\tilde{g}}(\tilde{x},\alpha(\tilde{y}))\approx\mu
in the sum. Since there are
O(\mu)

terms, we conclude that the left side of (
5.26
)
under the assumption of non-positive curvature is

O(T^{-1}\lambda^{\frac{n-1}{2}}\mu^{1-\frac{n-1}{2}})
, and
so we obtain (
4.10
).

6.
Some sharp estimates on tori

In this section, we shall see how we can modify the previous arguments to get the following sharp improved spectral
projection bounds on tori.

Theorem 6.1
.

Let
n\geq 2
,
{\mathbb{T}}^{n}={\mathbb{R}}/(\ell_{1}\mathbb{Z})\times\dots\times{\mathbb{R}}/(\ell_{n}\mathbb{Z})
denote the rectangular torus with
\ell_{i}\geq 1
.
Then,

(6.1)

\|\rho(T(\lambda-P))f\|_{L^{q_{c}}(\mathbb{T}^{n})}\lesssim\lambda^{1/q_{c}}\,T^{-1/q_{c}}\|f\|_{2},\,\,\,\,\,\forall\,\,1\leq T\leq\lambda^{\frac{1}{n+3}-\varepsilon}.

Here
\varepsilon
is an arbitrary small constant, and for convenience we are assuming
\ell_{i}\geq 1
, which implies that the injectivity radius of the torus is bounded below by
\frac{1}{2}
. The implicit constants in (
6.1
) may depend on
\ell_{i}
and
\varepsilon
, but do not depend on
T
and
\lambda
. As we shall see later in Proposition
7.1
,
the
T^{-1/q_{c}}
gain in the above estimate is sharp.

To prove (
6.1
), if we define

\tilde{\rho}_{\lambda}
as in (
2.7
), by arguing as before, it suffices to show that

(6.2)

\|\tilde{\rho}_{\lambda}f\|_{L^{q_{c}}({\mathbb{T}}^{n})}\leq\lambda^{1/q_{c}}T^{-1/q_{c}}\|f\|_{2}.

Proposition 6.2
.

Let

A_{+}=\{x\in{\mathbb{T}}^{n}:\,|\tilde{\rho}_{\lambda}f(x)|\geq C(T\lambda)^{\frac{n-1}{4}}\,\}\subset{\mathbb{T}}^{n},

where
C
is a large enough constant which may depend on
\ell_{i}
. Then,

(6.3)

\|\tilde{\rho}_{\lambda}f\|_{L^{q_{c}}(A_{+})}\leq C\lambda^{\frac{1}{q_{c}}}\,T^{-1/2}\,\|f\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{T}}^{n})}.

Proof.

We first note that, by (
2.5
) and (
2.8
), we have

\|\tilde{\rho}_{\lambda}f\|_{L^{q_{c}}(A_{+})}\leq\|B\rho_{\lambda}f\|_{L^{q_{c}}(A_{+})}+C\lambda^{\frac{1}{q_{c}}}/T,

since we are assuming that
f
is
L^{2}
-normalized. Thus, we would have (
6.3
) if we could show that

(6.4)

\|B\rho_{\lambda}f\|_{L^{q_{c}}(A_{+})}\leq C\lambda^{\frac{1}{q_{c}}}T^{-1/2}+\tfrac{1}{2}\|\tilde{\rho}_{\lambda}f\|_{L^{q_{c}}(A_{+})}.

If we repeat the argument in (
3.4
), by duality,

\displaystyle\|B\rho_{\lambda}f\|^{2}_{L^{q_{c}}(A_{+})}

\displaystyle\leq\int\big(B\circ L_{\lambda}\circ B^{*}\big)\bigl({\bf 1}_{A_{+}}\cdot g\bigr)(x)\cdot\overline{{\bf 1}_{A_{+}}(x)\,g(x)}\,dx

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad+\int\big(B\circ G_{\lambda}\circ B^{*}\big)\bigl({\bf 1}_{A_{+}}\cdot g\bigr)(x)\cdot\overline{{\bf 1}_{A_{+}}(x)\,g(x)}\,dx

\displaystyle=I+II.

Here,
G_{\lambda}
is the operator whose kernel is in (
3.1
), while
L_{\lambda}
is the “local” operator

L_{\lambda}=(2\pi T)^{-1}\int a(t)\hat{\Psi}(t/T)e^{it\lambda}e^{-itP}\,dt.

As before the local operator
L_{\lambda}
satisfies

\|L_{\lambda}\|_{L^{q_{c}^{\prime}}({\mathbb{T}}^{n})\to L^{q_{c}}({\mathbb{T}}^{n})}\lesssim T^{-1}\lambda^{2/q_{c}}.

By Hölder’s inequality, we have

(6.5)

\displaystyle|I|

\displaystyle\leq\bigl\|BL_{\lambda}B^{*}\bigr({\bf 1}_{A_{+}}\cdot g\bigr)\bigr\|_{q_{c}}\cdot\|{\bf 1}_{A_{+}}\cdot g\|_{q_{c}^{\prime}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\bigl\|L_{\lambda}B^{*}\bigr({\bf 1}_{A_{+}}\cdot g\bigr)\bigr\|_{q_{c}}\cdot\|{\bf 1}_{A_{+}}\cdot g\|_{q_{c}^{\prime}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\lambda^{2/q_{c}}T^{-1}\bigl\|B^{*}\bigr({\bf 1}_{A_{+}}\cdot g\bigr)\bigr\|_{q_{c}^{\prime}}\cdot\|{\bf 1}_{A_{+}}\cdot g\|_{q_{c}^{\prime}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\lambda^{2/q_{c}}T^{-1}\|g\|^{2}_{L^{q_{c}^{\prime}}(A_{+})}

\displaystyle=\lambda^{2/q_{c}}T^{-1}.

To handle the second term, we shall use the fact that if
\beta\in C^{\infty}_{0}((1/2,2))
and

(6.6)

G^{j}_{\lambda}(x,y)=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\bigl(1-a(t)\bigr)\beta(t/2^{j})\,T^{-1}\hat{\Psi}(t/T)e^{it\lambda}\,\bigl(e^{-itP})(x,y)\,dt,

then the integral operator with this kernel maps
L^{1}\to L^{\infty}
with norm

O\bigl(T^{-1}\,2^{\frac{n+1}{2}j}\lambda^{\frac{n-1}{2}}\bigr)
.
To see this, note that if we replace
e^{-itP}
above by
e^{itP}
, the kernel
is
O(\lambda^{-N})
for all
N>0
. Thus by Euler’s formula, it suffices to show that for any fixed
x,y

\big|\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\bigl(1-a(t)\bigr)\beta(t/2^{j})\,T^{-1}\hat{\Psi}(t/T)e^{it\lambda}\,\bigl(\cos(tP))(x,y)\,dt\big|=O\bigl(T^{-1}\,2^{\frac{n+1}{2}j}\lambda^{\frac{n-1}{2}}\bigr).

We postpone the proof of this to (
6.26
)-(
6.29
) below.

As a result, since the dyadic operators
B
are uniformly bounded on
L^{1}
and
L^{\infty}
, we can
repeat the argument that we used to estimate
I
to see that

(6.7)

\|G_{\lambda}\|_{L^{1}({\mathbb{T}}^{n})\to L^{\infty}({\mathbb{T}}^{n})}\leq\sum_{1\leq 2^{j}\leq T}\|G^{j}_{\lambda}\|_{L^{1}({\mathbb{T}}^{n})\to L^{\infty}({\mathbb{T}}^{n})}\lesssim(T\lambda)^{\frac{n-1}{2}}.

Then (
6.7
) yields

|II|\leq C_{0}(T\lambda)^{\frac{n-1}{2}}\,\bigl\|{\bf 1}_{A_{+}}\cdot g\bigr\|_{1}^{2}\leq C_{0}(T\lambda)^{\frac{n-1}{2}}\,\|g\|^{2}_{q_{c}^{\prime}}\,\|{\bf 1}_{A_{+}}\|_{q_{c}}^{2}=C_{0}(T\lambda)^{\frac{n-1}{2}}\,\|{\bf 1}_{A_{+}}\|_{q_{c}}^{2}.

If we recall the definition of
A_{+}
and use Chebyshev’s inequality we can estimate the last factor:

\|{\bf 1}_{A_{+}}\|_{q_{c}}^{2}\leq\bigl(C(T\lambda)^{\frac{n-1}{4}}\bigr)^{-2}\|\tilde{\rho}_{\lambda}f\|^{2}_{L^{q_{c}}(A_{+})}.

By choosing the constant
C
large enough such that
C^{-2}C_{0}\leq\frac{1}{4}
, we have,

|II|\leq\tfrac{1}{4}\|\tilde{\rho}_{\lambda}f\|^{2}_{L^{q_{c}}(A_{+})}.

If we combine this bound with the earlier one, (
6.5
), for
I
we conclude that
(
6.4
) is valid.
∎

Proposition 6.3
.

Let

(6.8)

A_{-}=\{x\in{\mathbb{T}}^{n}:\,|\tilde{\rho}_{\lambda}f(x)|\leq C(T\lambda)^{\frac{n-1}{4}}\,\}\subset{\mathbb{T}}^{n}.

Then, for any fixed
\varepsilon>0
we have

(6.9)

\|\tilde{\rho}_{\lambda}f\|_{L^{q_{c}}(A_{-})}\leq C\lambda^{\frac{1}{q_{c}}}\,T^{-1/q_{c}}\,\|f\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{T}}^{n})},\,\,\,\,\,\forall\,\,1\leq T\leq\lambda^{\frac{1}{n+3}-\varepsilon}.

In order to make use of the bilinear estimates, we shall
work in the microlocalized modes.
First, let
\nu
range over a

T^{-1}
–separated
set in
S^{n-1}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|Q_{\nu}\sigma_{\lambda}\rho_{\lambda}\tilde{Q}_{\nu}f\|_{L^{q_{c}}(M)}+\lambda^{\frac{1}{q_{c}}-1}T^{2}\|\tilde{Q}_{\nu}f\|_{L^{2}(M)}.

If we combine (
6.53
) and (
6.54
), by
(
2.8
), (
6.14
) along with the Kakeya-Nikodym bounds (
6.18
), we have

(6.55)

\displaystyle\|

\displaystyle\tilde{\sigma}_{\lambda}Q_{\nu}\rho_{\lambda}\tilde{Q}_{\nu}f\|_{L^{q_{c}}({\mathbb{T}}^{n})}

\displaystyle\leq\|Q_{\nu}\sigma_{\lambda}\rho_{\lambda}\tilde{Q}_{\nu}f\|_{L^{q_{c}}({\mathbb{T}}^{n})}+C\lambda^{\frac{1}{q_{c}}-\frac{1}{2}}T^{2}\|\tilde{Q}_{\nu}f\|_{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|Q_{\nu}\rho_{\lambda}\tilde{Q}_{\nu}f\|_{L^{q_{c}}({\mathbb{T}}^{n})}+\|Q_{\nu}\circ(I-\sigma_{\lambda})\rho_{\lambda}\tilde{Q}_{\nu}f\|_{L^{q_{c}}({\mathbb{T}}^{n})}+C\lambda^{\frac{1}{q_{c}}-\frac{1}{2}}T^{2}\|\tilde{Q}_{\nu}f\|_{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|Q_{\nu}\rho_{\lambda}\tilde{Q}_{\nu}f\|_{L^{q_{c}}({\mathbb{T}}^{n})}+\lambda^{\frac{1}{q_{c}}}T^{-1}\|\tilde{Q}_{\nu}f\|_{2}+\lambda^{\frac{1}{q_{c}}}\,T^{-\frac{1}{q_{c}}}\,\|\tilde{Q}_{\nu}f\|_{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim\lambda^{\frac{1}{q_{c}}}\,T^{-\frac{1}{q_{c}}}\,\|\tilde{Q}_{\nu}f\|_{2},

Here
we used the fact that
\lambda^{\frac{1}{q_{c}}-\frac{1}{2}}T^{2}\leq\lambda^{\frac{1}{q_{c}}}T^{-\frac{1}{q_{c}}}
for
1\leq T\leq\lambda^{\frac{1}{n+3}-\varepsilon}
.

If we combine (
6.41
) and (
6.55
), we have

(6.56)

\Bigl(\sum_{\nu}\|\tilde{\sigma}_{\lambda}Q_{\nu}\rho_{\lambda}\tilde{Q}_{\nu}f\|_{L^{q_{c}}({\mathbb{T}}^{n})}^{q_{c}}\Bigr)^{1/q_{c}}\leq C\lambda^{\frac{1}{q_{c}}}T^{-\frac{1}{q_{c}}}(\sum_{\nu}\|\tilde{Q}_{\nu}f\|^{2}_{2})^{\frac{1}{2}}\leq C\lambda^{\frac{1}{q_{c}}}T^{-\frac{1}{q_{c}}}\|f\|_{2},

which implies (
6.52
). The proof for the case
n=2
is similar. If we combine Proposition
6.2
and Proposition
6.3
, we get (
6.2
).
∎

In the next section, we shall show that the results in Theorem
6.1
are optimal in terms of how the
bounds depend on
T
.
It would be interesting to see whether the optimal results in Theorem

are valid for

.

7.
Geodesic concentration of quasimodes and lower bounds of
L^{q}(M)
-norms

In this section we shall show that the bounds in Theorem
6.1
are sharp in terms of their dependence on
T
even though we expect the bounds to hold for a larger range of
T
, at least
T\in[1,\lambda^{1/2-}]
. Bourgain and Demeter
[
9
]
and Hickman
[
15
]
(see also Germain and Myerson
[
13
]
) obtained near optimal bounds when
T
is larger than a fixed power of
\lambda
; however, since their arguments used decoupling they involved

\lambda^{\varepsilon}
-losses for arbitrary
\varepsilon>0
.

We shall consider here
relationships between the problem of obtaining lower bounds for the
L^{q}(M)
norms of quasimodes
\Psi_{\lambda}
of frequency
\lambda\gg 1
on

M
and the problem of establishing
their
nontrivial
L^{2}
-mass concentration near a fixed
closed geodesic
\gamma\in M
. Here, we are assuming that the spectrum associated with
P=\sqrt{-\Delta_{g}}
of
the quasimodes
\Psi_{\lambda}
belongs to intervals of the form
[\lambda-\varepsilon(\lambda),\lambda+\varepsilon(\lambda)]
, where, as
\lambda
goes to infinity through a sequence

\lambda_{j}\to+\infty
we have

\varepsilon(\lambda)\searrow 0
. We shall also assume that these quasimodes are
L^{2}
-normalized, i.e.,

(7.1)

\|\Psi_{\lambda}\|_{L^{2}(M)}=1.

Additionally, to get lower bounds for
L^{q}
-norms with
q>2
, if

(7.2)

{\mathcal{T}}_{\lambda,N}={\mathcal{T}}_{N(\lambda)\cdot\lambda^{-1/2}}(\gamma)=\{y\in M:\,d_{g}(y,\gamma)\leq N(\lambda)\cdot\lambda^{-1/2}\},\,\,\,N=N(\lambda),

is a tube of width

N(\lambda)\cdot\lambda^{-1/2}
about our closed geodesic
\gamma
, we shall assume that we have the following uniform lower bounds for the

L^{2}({\mathcal{T}}_{\lambda,N})
mass of our quasimodes

(7.3)

\|\Psi_{\lambda}\|_{L^{2}({\mathcal{T}}_{\lambda,N})}\geq\delta_{0}>0.

Typically, we shall have
N(\lambda)\to+\infty
as
\lambda\to+\infty
, and (
7.3
) with
N(\lambda)\equiv 1
corresponds to the concentration of
Gaussian beam quasimodes, which is expected to be the tightest possible concentration about geodesics.
It is also not natural to consider tubes which are wider than the injectivity radius of

M
. So, by (
7.2
), it is natural to assume, as we shall,
that

(7.4)

1\leq N(\lambda)=O(\lambda^{1/2-}).

Note that if we assume (
7.3
), then by Hölder’s inequality we have for
q>2

\delta_{0}\leq\|\Psi_{\lambda}\|_{L^{2}({\mathcal{T}}_{\lambda,N})}\leq|{\mathcal{T}}_{\lambda,N}|^{\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{q}}\,\|\Psi_{\lambda}\|_{L^{q}(M)}.

Since the Riemannian volume of the tubes satisfies
|{\mathcal{T}}_{\lambda,N}|\approx\bigl(N(\lambda)\cdot\lambda^{-1/2}\bigr)^{(n-1)}
, this gives the lower bounds

(7.5)

\bigl(N(\lambda)\bigr)^{-(n-1)(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{q})}\,\lambda^{\frac{n-1}{2}(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{q})}\lesssim\|\Psi_{\lambda}\|_{L^{q}(M)}

for the lower bounds of our quasimodes satisfying (
7.3
). Tighter concentration of
L^{2}
-mass near the geodesic (i.e. smaller
N(\lambda)
) implies stronger lower bounds.

Note that for the standard round sphere,
S^{n}
, the
L^{2}
-normalized highest weight spherical harmonics
Q_{\lambda}
are
eigenfunctions

satisfying (
7.3
) with
N(\lambda)\equiv 1
, and the lower bounds in (
7.5
) with
\Psi_{\lambda}=Q_{\lambda}
are sharp since

\|Q_{\lambda}\|_{L^{q}(S^{n})}\approx\lambda^{\frac{n-1}{2}(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{q})}
if
q>2
, and this fact shows that the universal bounds in
[
18
]

are best possible for
2<q\leq q_{c}
. (See
[
17
]
.)

Let us also see here that we can also obtain natural lower bounds for
L^{q}
estimates for this range of
q
if
M
is a product manifold
with
S^{1}
as one of its factors. So let us assume for now that

(7.6)

M=S^{1}\times X^{n-1}

is a Cartesian product of
S^{1}
with an
(n-1)
-dimensional compact manifold
X^{n-1}
equipped with the product metric.
If
x_{0}\in X^{n-1}
is fixed, let us consider the geodesic
\gamma=S^{1}\times\{x_{0}\}
in
M
. Also, for
\lambda=1,2,3,\dots
consider the
function on
S^{1}\times X^{n-1}
defined by

(7.7)

\Psi_{\lambda}(\theta,x)=(\lambda^{1/2}/N)^{-(n-1)/2}e^{i\lambda\theta}\,\beta\bigl(P_{X}/(\lambda^{1/2}/N)\bigr)(x_{0},x),\quad N=N(\lambda),

where
0\leq\beta\in C^{\infty}_{0}((1/2,2))
is a Littlewood-Paley bump function and
P_{X}=\sqrt{-\Delta_{X^{n-1}}}
is the first order operator on
X^{n-1}
coming from its metric Laplacian.
Here
\beta(P_{X}/\mu)(x,y)
is the kernel of the operator
\beta(P_{X}/\mu)
on
X=X^{n-1}
defined by the spectral theorem.

Let us now prove the following simple result on such product manifolds.

Proposition 7.1
.

Let
M=S^{1}\times X^{n-1}
be as in (
7.6
) with
n\geq 2
.
Then if
\varepsilon(\lambda)\searrow 0
and (
7.4
) is valid with
N(\lambda)=(\varepsilon(\lambda))^{-1/2}
, there is a geodesic
\gamma\in M
so that
we have (
7.3
).
Consequently, we have

(7.8)

\|\chi_{[\lambda-\varepsilon(\lambda),\lambda+\varepsilon(\lambda)]}\|_{L^{2}(S^{1}\times X^{n-1})\to L^{q}(S^{1}\times X^{n-1})}\geq c_{0}\,\bigl(\varepsilon(\lambda)\cdot\lambda)^{\frac{n-1}{2}(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{q})},\\
q\in(2,q_{c}],\quad q_{c}=\tfrac{2(n+1)}{n-1}.

Remark 7.2
.

Note that the universal bounds in
[
18
]
imply that the operators in (
7.8
) map
L^{2}\to L^{q}
with norm

O\bigl(\lambda^{\frac{n-1}{2}(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{q})}\bigr)
, and so (
7.8
) says that for manifolds as in (
7.6
) one can, at best, improve these bounds by
a factor of
\varepsilon(\lambda))^{\frac{n-1}{2}(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{q})}
. Consequently,
since tori are product manifolds involving
S^{1}
(perhaps scaled) as a factor,
the bounds in Theorem
6.1
are sharp for the
range
\varepsilon(\lambda)\in[\lambda^{-\frac{1}{n+3}+},1]
there.

As we pointed out before, Hickman
[
15
]
showed that for tori
if one includes additional arbitrary
\lambda^{\varepsilon}
factors in the right side of (
7.8
) then this
controls the bounds of the projection operators there. Further results were obtained by Germain and Myerson
[
13
]

as well as the lower bound (
7.8
) for tori using a slightly different and more direct method.

Proof.

We shall take
\gamma=S^{1}\times\{x_{0}\}
as mentioned above.
We then notice that, if
\{\mu_{k}\}
are the eigenvalues of
P_{X}
and
\{e_{k}\}
is the associated orthonormal basis, then

\displaystyle\bigl\|\beta\bigl(P_{X}/(\lambda^{1/2}/N)\bigr)(x_{0},\,\cdot\,)\|_{L^{2}(X^{n-1})}^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k}\beta^{2}(\mu_{k}/(\lambda^{1/2}/N))\,|e_{k}(x_{0})|^{2}

\displaystyle\approx\#\{\mu_{k}:\,\mu_{k}\approx(\lambda^{1/2}/N)\}\approx(\lambda^{1/2}/N)^{n-1}.

Therefore, the functions in (
7.7
) have
L^{2}(S^{1}\times X^{n-1})
norms which are comparable to one.

Consequently, by (
7.5
) with
N(\lambda)=(\varepsilon(\lambda))^{-1/2}
we would have the lower-bound (
7.8
) if we could show that
for large
\lambda\in{\mathbb{N}}

(7.9)

\text{Spec }\Psi_{\lambda}\subset[\lambda-C_{0}\varepsilon(\lambda),\lambda+C_{0}\varepsilon(\lambda)],

for some fixed constant, as well as

(7.10)

\|\Psi_{\lambda}\|_{L^{2}(\{y\in S^{1}\times X^{n-1}:\,d_{g}(y,\gamma)\leq(\varepsilon(\lambda)\lambda)^{-1/2}\})}\geq\delta_{0},\quad\gamma=S^{1}\times\{x_{0}\},\,\,x_{0}\in X^{n-1},

for some fixed
\delta_{0}>0
. Here
\text{Spec }\Psi_{\lambda}
of course denotes the
P
-spectrum of
\Psi_{\lambda}
.

To prove (
7.9
) we note that
\Psi_{\lambda}(\theta,x)
is a linear combination of eigenfunctions on
S^{1}\times X^{n-1}
of the form

e^{i\lambda\theta}e_{k}(x)
where
\lambda\in{\mathbb{N}}
is fixed and
\mu_{k}\approx\lambda^{1/2}/N(\lambda)=\sqrt{\varepsilon(\lambda)\lambda}
. Since

\sqrt{-\bigl(\tfrac{\partial^{2}}{\partial\theta^{2}}+\Delta_{X}\bigr)}\bigl(e^{i\lambda\theta}e_{k}(x)\bigr)=\sqrt{\lambda^{2}+\mu_{k}^{2}}\,\bigl(e^{i\lambda\theta}e_{k}(x)\bigr)

and

\sqrt{\lambda^{2}+\mu_{k}^{2}}-\lambda=O(\varepsilon(\lambda))\quad\text{if }\,\,\mu_{k}\leq C\sqrt{\varepsilon(\lambda)\lambda},

we conclude that (
7.9
) is valid.

To prove (
7.10
) it suffices to see that if
B(x_{0},r)
is the geodesic ball of radius
r\ll 1
about
x_{0}
in

X^{n-1}
then we have the lower bound

(7.11)

\bigl(\varepsilon(\lambda)\lambda\bigr)^{-\frac{n-1}{4}}\,\bigl\|\beta\bigl(P_{X}/(\varepsilon(\lambda)\lambda)^{1/2}\bigr)(x_{0},\,\cdot\,)\|_{L^{2}(B(x_{0},\,(\varepsilon(\lambda)\lambda)^{-1/2}))}\geq\delta_{0}>0.

This is standard calculation. Since
\varepsilon(\lambda)\lambda\geq\lambda^{\sigma}
for some
\sigma>0
because of our assumption
(
7.4
) one can argue as in

[
21
, §4.3]

to see that for large
\lambda
we have

\beta\bigl(P_{X}/(\varepsilon(\lambda)\lambda)^{1/2}\bigr)(x_{0},y)\approx(\sqrt{\varepsilon(\lambda)\lambda})^{n-1}\quad\text{if }\,\,\,y\in B(x_{0},\,c_{0}(\varepsilon(\lambda)\lambda)^{-1/2})

for some fixed
c_{0}>0
, which yields (
7.11
) and
completes the proof.
∎

Concentration problems for
manifolds of negative curvature

As we pointed out before the analog of (
7.8
) with
\varepsilon(\lambda)\equiv 1
is valid
on
S^{n}
since there are
L^{2}
-normalized eigenfunctions on
S^{n}
for which (
7.3
) is valid with

N(\lambda)\equiv 1
, which means they have a fixed fraction
of their
L^{2}
-mass in
\lambda^{-1/2}
tubes about a geodesic. The proof of Proposition
7.1
showed that
on tori one can find
\varepsilon(\lambda)
-quasimodes with fixed

L^{2}
-mass in
(\varepsilon(\lambda))^{-1/2}\lambda^{-1/2}
tubes around
a closed geodesic whenever
\varepsilon(\lambda)\in[\lambda^{-1+},1]
.
So we have natural concentration results for quasimodes
near geodesics in manifolds of positive and zero curvature.

We wonder if there are any analogous results for manifolds of
negative
curvature. Recently there
has be somewhat related work by Brooks
[
10
]
,
Eswarathasan and Nonnenmacher
[
12
]
and others
who showed that there is strong scarring of logarithmic quasimodes on compact
quotients of
{\mathbb{H}}^{n}
. These say that, on such
manifolds, if
\gamma
is a closed geodesic then there
is a sequence of
L^{2}
-normalized quasimodes
\Psi_{\lambda}

satisfying (
7.9
) (or a related
L^{2}
-quasimode
condition) so that the quantum probability measures

|\Psi_{\lambda}|^{2}\,dVol_{g}
exhibit strong scarring
along
\gamma
. By this we mean that weak limits of
these measures must include a positive multiple of
the Dirac mass on
\gamma
, and so the
\Psi
are
tightly concentrated on this geodesic.

It would be interesting if one could find more
quantitative versions of these results that might
lead to nontrivial lower bounds for the
L^{2}\to L^{q_{c}}

operator norms of the operators in (
7.8
) with
\varepsilon(\lambda)=(\log\lambda)^{-1}
(or perhaps larger).
So it would be interesting to see if for certain

N(\lambda)\nearrow
we could find logarithmic quasimodes

\Psi_{\lambda}
(so
\varepsilon(\lambda)\approx(\log\lambda)^{-1}
)
on hyperbolic quotients for which we
have (
7.2
). It is routine to see that on

any
manifold one cannot have for
\varepsilon(\lambda)\searrow 0

(7.12)

\bigl\|\chi_{[\lambda-\varepsilon(\lambda),\lambda+\varepsilon(\lambda)]}\bigr\|_{L^{2}\to L^{q_{c}}}=o(\sqrt{\varepsilon(\lambda)}\lambda^{\frac{1}{q_{c}}}),

since these bounds would imply that the operators map

L^{2}\to L^{\infty}
with norm
o(\sqrt{\varepsilon(\lambda)}\lambda^{\frac{n-1}{2}})
, and this would violate the
Weyl counting formula.

Even though Hassell and Tacy
[
14
]
showed
that there is
(\log\lambda)^{-1/2}
improvements over
the universal bounds in
[
18
]
on the

L^{2}\to L^{q}
norms of these operators for

q>q_{c}
when
M
has negative curvature and

\varepsilon(\lambda)=(\log\lambda)^{-1}
, given
Proposition
7.1
one might expect
such an improvement to not hold when
q
is the critical
exponent
q=q_{c}
. This would follow from showing
that there is a closed geodesic
\gamma
and

L^{2}
-normalized logarithmic quasimodes as described
above so that one has the non-trivial
lower bounds (
7.3
) with

N(\lambda)=o((\log\lambda)^{q_{c}/4})=o((\log\lambda)^{\frac{n+1}{2(n-1)}})
. So, for instance, when
n=2
, if one
could find
\Psi_{\lambda}
as above with fixed
lower bounds of
L^{2}
-mass in

o((\log\lambda)^{3/2}\lambda^{-\frac{1}{2}})
neighborhoods
of a closed geodesic
\gamma
, then the

\sqrt{\varepsilon(\lambda)}=(\log\lambda)^{-1/2}
improvement
of Hassell and Tacy
[
14
]
for
q>q_{c}

could not hold for
q=q_{c}
.

Also, it would be interesting to know whether our estimates in (
5.3
) for log-quasimodes on surfaces of negative curvature
are sharp. If one could construct a sequence of such quasimodes for which
\|\Psi_{\lambda}\|_{L^{2}({\mathcal{T}})}\geq\delta_{0}
for tubes
{\mathcal{T}}={\mathcal{T}}_{\lambda}(\gamma)
of width

\log\lambda\cdot\lambda^{-1/2}
about a periodic geodesic
\gamma
, then (
5.3
) would be saturated. Note that these are much wider tubes
than the ones we used for
S^{1}\times X^{n-1}
, which is to be expected due to the much faster divergence of
geodesics on manifolds of negative curvature.

As was kindly pointed out to us by Eswarathasan, due to
the role of the Ehrenfest time in the constructions,
it does not seem likely that the techniques in

[
10
]
or
[
12
]
could be used to prove
this. It would also be interesting to see whether
such a result might hold for log-log quasimodes, in which case one would replace the above
\log\lambda
terms
with
\log\log\lambda
. So
\varepsilon(\lambda)=(\log\log\lambda)^{-1}

and
N(\lambda)=o((\log\log\lambda)^{\frac{n+1}{2(n-1)}})
.
If we then had (
7.3
) this would imply that the

\sqrt{\varepsilon(\lambda)}
improvements for the

L^{2}\to L^{q}
norms of the operators in (
7.8
) of Hassell and Tacy for
q>q_{c}

could not hold for
q=q_{c}
with
\varepsilon(\lambda)=(\log\log\lambda)^{-1}
.

Breakup corrections to spin asymmetries in the 3 He beam polarization measurement with the Polarized Atomic Hydrogen Gas Jet Target
A. A. Poblaguev

Abstract
The requirements for hadron polarimetry at the future Electron-Ion Collider (EIC) include measurements of the absolute helion ( 3 He, h h ) beam polarization with systematic uncertainties better than σ P syst / P ≤ 1 % \sigma^{\text{syst}}_{P}/P\leq 1\% . Recently, it was proposed that the Polarized Atomic Hydrogen Gas Jet Target (HJET) be utilized for the precision measurement of the polarization of the ≈ \approx 100 GeV/n helion beam. At the Relativistic Heavy Ion Collider, HJET serves to determine the absolute proton beam polarization with low systematic uncertainties of about δ syst ​ P / P ≲ 0.5 % \delta^{\text{syst}}P/P\lesssim 0.5\% . To adapt the HJET method for the EIC helion beam, the experimentally determined ratio of the beam and target (jet) spin-correlated asymmetries should be adjusted by the ratio of p ↑ ​ h p^{\uparrow}{h} and h ↑ ​ p h^{\uparrow}{p} analyzing powers. A potential problem with the suggested method is that the breakup of 3 He in polarization measurements could drastically affect the analyzing power ratio. However, an analysis of the breakup corrections, presented in this paper, reveals that while these corrections can be as substantial as ≈ 4 % \approx\!4\% , the effect cancels out to a negligible level in the measured beam polarization.

II.1
A Model Used to Isolate the Breakup Events

Considering the breakup
A(p,p)X
reaction as elastic scattering of a nucleon cluster in the nucleus off the jet proton, one can relate
Poblaguev 2022b
the missing mass excess
\Delta=m_{X}-m_{A}
to the recoil proton energy:

\Delta=\left(1-\frac{m^{*}}{m_{A}}\right)T_{R}+p_{x}\sqrt{\frac{2T_{R}}{m_{p}}},

(11)

where
m^{*}
is the nucleon cluster mass and
p_{x}
is the internal motion’s transverse momentum of the cluster in the direction of the detector. Since only low-energy recoil protons (
T_{R}\lesssim 10\,\text{MeV}
) can be detected at HJET,
\Delta
is much less than
m_{A}
. This indicates that the breakup event rate for this process would be strongly suppressed by the phase-space factor.

Obviously, for the deuteron beam,
m^{*}\!=\!m_{p}
. Assuming that the
p_{x}
distribution in a deuteron is given by a unity integral normalized Breit ​– ​Wigner function

f_{\text{BW}}(p_{x},\sigma_{p})=\frac{\pi^{-1}\,\sqrt{2}\sigma_{p}}{p_{x}^{2}+2\sigma_{p}^{2}},

(12)

the
d\to pn
breakup fraction
\omega(T_{R},\Delta)
can be found
Poblaguev 2022b
as a convolution of the
\Delta
distribution, calculated in accordance with Eq. (
11
), and the phase-space integral (calculated as a function of
\Delta
),

\displaystyle\omega(T_{R},\Delta)=\frac{d^{2}\sigma_{\text{brk}}(T_{R},\Delta)}{d\sigma_{\text{el}}(T_{R})\,d\Delta}=\frac{\sqrt{2m_{p}m_{n}}}{4\pi m_{d}}

\displaystyle\qquad\times|\bar{\psi}(T_{R},\Delta)|^{2}f_{\text{BW}}(\Delta\!-\!\Delta_{0},\sigma_{\Delta})\sqrt{\frac{\Delta\!-\!\Delta^{d}_{\text{thr}}}{m_{d}}},

(13)

\displaystyle\Delta_{0}=(1-m_{p}/m_{d})T_{R},\qquad\sigma^{2}_{\Delta}=2\sigma_{p}^{2}T_{R}/m_{p}.

(14)

Since the key dependence of the breakup amplitude on
T_{R}
and
\Delta
is allocated in the function
f_{\text{BW}}
,
\bar{\psi}(T_{R},\Delta)
should be interpreted as a reduced ratio of the breakup to elastic amplitudes. Therefore, for HJET measurements, i.e., for low
T_{R}
and
\Delta
,
\bar{\psi}(T_{R},\Delta)
can be substituted by a constant
\bar{\psi}\!\equiv\!\bar{\psi}(0,0)

d\!\to\!pn
breakup, the threshold is
\Delta^{d}_{\text{thr}}\!=\!m_{p}\!+\!m_{n}\!-\!m_{d}\!=\!2.2\,\text{MeV}

Integrating over
\Delta

\omega(T_{R})
, as a function of the recoil proton energy, in the elastic data.

III
Hadronic Spin-Flip Amplitudes in High-Energy Proton-Nucleus Scattering

In Ref.
Kopeliovich and Trueman 2001
, it was shown that, at high energy, to a very good approximation, the ratio of the spin-flip to the nonflip parts of the elastic proton-nucleus amplitude is the same as for proton-nucleon scattering. In terms of the hadronic spin-flip amplitude parameter
r_{5}
, the result can be written as

r_{5}^{pA}=\frac{i+\rho^{pA}}{i+\rho^{pp}}r_{5}\approx r_{5},

(25)

where
\rho^{pA}
and
\rho^{pp}
are the real-to-imaginary ratio for the elastic
pA
and
\mathit{pp}
scattering, respectively. This result can be readily derived considering the polarized proton scattering off an unpolarized nucleus in Glauber (diffraction) approximation
Glauber 1955
;
*Glauber:1959
.

In this approach, the elastic (
f\!=\!i
) and/or breakup (
f\!\neq\!i
) proton-nucleus amplitude can be presented
Glauber and Matthiae 1970
using the following integral over the impact vector
\bm{b}
:

\displaystyle F_{fi}(\bm{q})

\displaystyle=\frac{ik}{2\pi}\int{d^{2}\bm{b}\,e^{i\bm{qb}}}\,\prod_{j=1}^{A}{d^{3}\bm{r}_{j}}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\Psi_{f}^{*}(\{r_{j}\})\Gamma(\bm{b},\bm{s}_{1}\ldots\bm{s}_{A})\Psi_{i}(\{r_{j}\})

(26)

where
\bm{k}
is the momentum of the incident proton,
\Psi_{i}
and
\Psi_{f}
are the nucleus’s initial and final state wave functions, and
\Gamma(\bm{b},\bm{s}_{1}\ldots\bm{s}_{A})
is the profile function. The positions of the
A
nucleons in the nucleus were defined by the vectors
\bm{r}_{j},~j\!=\!1,\ldots,A
, and
\bm{s}_{j}
are the projections of these vectors on the plane perpendicular to
\bm{k}
.

For a proton-deuteron small angle scattering, the elastic
pd
amplitude
F_{ii}
can be approximated
Franco and Glauber 1966
by combining the proton-proton
f_{p}
and proton-neutron
f_{n}
ones as

\displaystyle F_{ii}(\bm{q})

\displaystyle=S(\bm{q}/2)f_{n}(\bm{q})+S(\bm{q}/2)f_{p}(\bm{q})

\displaystyle+\frac{i}{2\pi k}\int{S(\bm{q}^{\prime})f_{n}(\bm{q}/2\!+\!\bm{q}^{\prime})f_{p}(\bm{q}/2\!-\!\bm{q}^{\prime})d^{2}\bf{q}^{\prime}},

(27)

where
S(\bm{q})
can be interpreted as a deuteron form factor.

To calculate the
p^{\uparrow}d
spin-flip amplitude
F_{ii}^{\text{sf}}
, one can utilize the proton-nucleon one,
f_{N}^{\text{sf}}(\bm{q})
, which, according to the definition of
r_{5}

Buttimore et al. 1999
, is

f_{N}^{\text{sf}}(\bm{q})=\frac{\bm{qn}}{m_{p}}\,\frac{r_{5}^{pp}}{i+\rho^{pp}}\,f_{N}(\bm{q})=\bm{qn}\,\hat{r}_{5}^{pp}f(\bm{q}).

(28)

Since
|r_{5}^{pp}|\,q/m_{p}\lesssim 0.003
in the HJET measurements, only one nonflip amplitude
f_{N}
in each term of Eq.
27
should be replaced by its spin-flip counterpart
f_{N}^{\text{sf}}
. In particular,

\displaystyle f_{p}f_{n}

\displaystyle\to\left[(\bm{q}/2\!+\!\bm{q}^{\prime})\bm{n}f_{p}\,f_{n}+f_{p}\,(\bm{q}/2\!-\!\bm{q}^{\prime})\bm{n}f_{n}\right]\,\hat{r}_{5}^{pp}

\displaystyle=\bm{qn}\,\hat{r}_{5}^{pp}\,f_{p}f_{n}.

(29)

Because each term on the right side of Eq. (
27
) acquired a factor
\bm{qn}\,\hat{r}_{5}
, one can readily reach Eq. (
25
).

Generally, elastic
pA
amplitude can be displayed as

\displaystyle F_{ii}(\bm{q})

\displaystyle=\sum_{i}{\{{\cal S}_{i}f_{i}\}}+\sum_{i,j}{\{{\cal S}_{ij}f_{i}f_{j}\}}

\displaystyle+\sum_{i,j,k}{\{{\cal S}_{ijk}f_{i}f_{j}f_{k}\}}+\ldots,

(30)

where, e.g., for the 3-amplitude terms,

\displaystyle\{{\cal S}_{ijk}f_{i}f_{j}f_{k}\}

IV
Evaluation of Breakup Corrections in Helion Beam Polarization Measurements

A basic assumption of the Glauber theory approach that a high energy beam proton crosses a target nucleus before any changes in the nucleus structure caused by the proton occur, suggests that an electromagnetic interaction is the same for the elastic and breakup scattering. However, to compare the electromagnetic amplitudes for the elastic and breakup scattering, the interaction, in accordance with Eq. (
26

\Psi_{h}
and
\Psi_{pd}

13

\frac{\langle\bar{\Psi}_{pd}|{{}^{3}\text{He}}^{*}(\bm{q})\rangle_{\text{em}}}{\langle{\Psi}_{h}|{{}^{3}\text{He}}^{*}(\bm{q})\rangle_{\text{em}}}\approx\frac{\langle\bar{\Psi}_{pd}|{{}^{3}\text{He}}\rangle_{\text{em}}}{\langle{\Psi}_{h}|{{}^{3}\text{He}}\rangle_{\text{em}}}=\frac{\langle\bar{\Psi}_{pd}|{{}^{3}\text{He}}\rangle_{\text{had}}}{\langle{\Psi}_{h}|{{}^{3}\text{He}}\rangle_{\text{had}}}=\bar{\psi}.

(36)

Summary

In this paper, possible

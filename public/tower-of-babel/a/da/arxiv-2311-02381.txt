Entire monogenic functions of given proximate order and continuous homomorphisms
Fabrizio Colombo, R.S. Krausshar, Stefano Pinton, Irene Sabadini

Abstract
. Infinite order differential operators appear in different fields of mathematics and physics. In the past decade they turned out to play a crucial role in the theory of superoscillations and provided new insight in the study of the evolution as initial data for the Schrödinger equation. Inspired by the infinite order differential operators arising in quantum mechanics, in this paper we investigate the continuity of a class of infinite order differential operators acting on spaces of entire hyperholomorphic functions. Precisely, we consider homomorphisms acting on functions in the kernel of the Dirac operator. For this class of functions, often called monogenic functions, we introduce the proximate order and prove some fundamental properties. As important application we are able to characterize infinite order differential operators that act continuously on spaces of monogenic entire functions.

Key words
: Infinite order differential operators,
Dirac operator, proximate order of monogenic functions.

1.
Introduction

Infinite order differential operators have been studied already since a long time.
In the recent years they turned out to be of fundamental importance in the study of the evolution of superoscillations as initial data for the Schrödinger equation.
Superoscillatory functions arise in several areas of science and technology,
for example in quantum mechanics they are the outcome
of Aharonov’s weak values, see
[
1
,
8
]
.
To study their time evolution as initial data of quantum field equations represents an important problem in quantum mechanics.

The study of the evolution of superoscillatory functions under the Schrödinger equation
is highly non-trivial. A natural functional analytic setting is
the space of entire functions with certain growth conditions.
In fact, the Cauchy problem for the Schrödinger equation with superoscillatory initial data leads
to infinite order differential operators of the type

\mathcal{U}(t,z;\partial_{z})=\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}u_{m}(t,z)\partial_{z}^{m},

where the coefficients
u_{m}(t,z)
depend on the Green’s function
of the time dependent Schrödinger equation for a given potential
V
,

t
is the time variable and
z
is the complexification of the space variable.
For more details, see for example the monograph
[
6
]
and
[
2
,
3
,
4
,
7
,
14
,
16
,
17
,
30
]
.

Another application of infinite order differential operators
within the scope of the theory of superoscillatory functions
is the extension of this theory to several superoscillating variables, see
[
5
]
.

For
p\geq 1
the natural spaces on which operators such as

\mathcal{U}(t,z;\partial_{z})
act
are the spaces of entire functions with growth order either order lower than
p
or equal to
p
and finite type. In other words, they consist of entire functions
f
for which there exist constants
B,C>0
such that

|f(z)|\leq Ce^{B|z|^{p}}.

The problem to extend infinite order differential operators
to the hypercomplex setting is treated
in the recent paper
[
9
]
where the authors investigate the continuity of a class of infinite order differential operators
acting on spaces of entire hyperholomorphic functions
that include monogenic functions.
In
[
9
]
we find the hypercomplex version of some results obtained in
[
10
,
13
]
; the class of monogenic functions is the most delicate case to investigate.
Even though the classical exponential function is not in the kernel of the Dirac operator, monogenic functions with exponential bounds play a crucial role in the study of continuity of a class of infinite order differential operators in the hypercomplex settings.
The hypercomplex setting is non-trivial and requires
some efforts because of the structure of monogenic functions
and of the fact that they admit series expansions in terms of
the so called Fueter polynomials.
Precisely, the Fueter’s polynomials
V_{k}(x)
, see for instance formula
(4)
of
[
21
]
page
794
or elsewhere, are
defined by

V_{k}(x):=\frac{k!}{|k|!}\sum_{\sigma\in perm(|k|)}z_{j_{\sigma(1)}}z_{j_{\sigma(2)}}\ldots z_{j_{\sigma(|k|)}},

where
k
represents a multi-index. They
play the role of the sequence of the complex polynomials
(z^{n})_{n}
when
z
is a complex variable.
In order to preserve the monogenicity we need a special product, called Cauchy-Kowalewski product, for short CK-product,
that does not coincide with the ordinary pointwise product.
The CK-product of two left entire monogenic
f
and
g
is denoted by

f\odot_{L}g
is given in Definition
2.4
in terms of the Fueter polynomials. A similar definition is given for right monogenic functions.

In the paper
[
9
]
we obtained the following result
regarding monogenic functions.
Let
p\geq 1
and set
\mathbb{N}_{0}=\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}
.
Let
(u_{m})_{m\in(\mathbb{N}_{0})^{n}}:\mathbb{R}^{n+1}\to\mathbb{R}_{n}
be left entire monogenic functions
such that for every
\varepsilon>0
there exist
B_{\varepsilon}>0
,
C_{\varepsilon}>0
for which

(1.1)

|u_{m}(x)|\leq C_{\varepsilon}\frac{\varepsilon^{|m|}}{(|m|!)^{1/q}}\exp(B_{\varepsilon}|x|^{p}),\ \ \ {\rm for\ all}\ \ \ m\in(\mathbb{N}_{0})^{n},

where
1/p+1/q=1
and where we set
1/q:=0
when
p=1
, and
m
is a multi-index.
We considered the formal infinite order differential operator

(1.2)

U_{L}(x,\partial_{x})f(x):=\sum_{|m|=0}^{\infty}u_{m}(x)\odot_{L}\partial_{x}^{m}f(x),

for left entire monogenic functions
f

where
\partial_{x}^{m}:=\partial_{x_{1}}^{m_{1}}\dots\partial_{x_{n}}^{m_{n}}
and
\odot_{L}
denotes the CK-product.
Then for
p\geq 1
, we proved that the operator

U_{L}(x,\partial_{x})

acts continuously on the space of left monogenic functions with the condition

|f(x)|\leq Ce^{B|x|^{p}}.

In this paper we
characterize the continuous homomorphisms of type (
1.2
) acting on monogenic functions.
In order to do this we introduce proximate orders
for monogenic functions and we study some fundamental properties. After that we investigate the monogenic counterpart
of the differential operator representation of continuous homomorphisms
between the spaces of entire functions of given proximate order proved
by T. Aoki and co-authors in

Preliminary results on monogenic functions

In this section we recall some results on monogenic functions, whose proofs can be found in

[
15
]

We recall that
\mathbb{R}_{n}
is the real Clifford algebra over
n
imaginary units
e_{1},\ldots,e_{n}
.
The element

(x_{0},x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})\in\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
will be identified with the paravector

\mathbf{x}=x_{0}+\underline{x}=x_{0}+\sum_{\ell=1}^{n}x_{\ell}e_{\ell}

and the real part
x_{0}
of
\mathbf{x}
will also be denoted by
{\rm Re}(\mathbf{x})
.
An element
y
in
\mathbb{R}_{n}
, is called a
Clifford number
.
If
A
is an element in the power set
P(1,\ldots,n)
,
then the element
e_{i_{1}}\ldots e_{i_{r}}
can be written as
e_{i_{1}...i_{r}}
or, in short,
e_{A}
.
Thus, we can write a Clifford number as

y=\sum_{A}y_{A}e_{A}.

Possibly using the defining relations
e_{i}^{2}=-1
,
e_{i}e_{j}+e_{j}e_{i}=0
,
i,j\in\{1,\ldots,n\}
,
i\not=j
, we will order the indices in
A
as
i_{1}<\ldots<i_{r}
. When
A=\emptyset
we set
e_{\emptyset}=1
.
The Euclidean norm of an element
y\in\mathbb{R}_{n}
is
given by
|y|^{2}=\sum_{A}|y_{A}|^{2}
,
in particular the norm of the paravector
\mathbf{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
is
|\mathbf{x}|^{2}=x_{0}^{2}+x_{1}^{2}+\ldots+x_{n}^{2}
.

Definition 2.1
.

We call
f:\mathbb{R}^{n+1}\to\mathbb{R}_{n}
an entire left (right) monogenic function if
f\in\mathcal{C}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n+1},\mathbb{R}_{n})
and
\mathcal{D}f\equiv 0
(resp.
f\mathcal{D}\equiv 0
) where

\mathcal{D}:=\partial_{x_{0}}+\sum_{i=1}^{n}e_{i}\partial_{x_{i}}.

We will restrict ourselves to consider only the set of the left monogenic functions since for right monogenic functions analogous computations hold.

We will denote the set of left entire monogenic function by
\mathcal{M}_{L}(\mathbb{R}^{n+1})
.
The Cauchy formula
for monogenic functions and their derivatives, is computed on the boundary
of
U\subset\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
where
\overline{U}
is contained in the set of monogenicity of
f
.

Theorem 2.2
.

If
f
is a left monogenic function in a neighborhood of
\overline{U}
then for any
\mathbf{x}\in U

f(\mathbf{x})=\frac{1}{A_{n+1}}\int_{\partial U}q_{0}(\mathbf{x}-\xi)d\tau(\xi)f(\xi)

where
A_{n+1}
is the
n
-dimensional surface area of the
n+1
-dimensional unit ball,

d\tau(\xi)=\sum_{j=0}^{n}(-1)^{j}e_{j}\widehat{d\xi_{j}}\quad\textrm{and}\quad q_{0}(\mathbf{x})=\frac{\bar{\mathbf{x}}}{\|\mathbf{x}\|^{n+1}}

with

\widehat{d\xi_{j}}=d\xi_{0}\wedge\dots\wedge d\xi_{j-1}\wedge d\xi_{j+1}\wedge\dots\wedge d\xi_{n}.

Theorem 2.3
.

Let
\mathbf{m}:=(m_{1},\dots,m_{n})\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{n}
be a multi-index. We denote by
|\mathbf{m}|=m_{1}+\dots+m_{n}
. If
f
is a left monogenic function in a ball
\|\mathbf{x}\|<R
, then for all
\|\mathbf{x}\|<r
with
0<r<R
, its Taylor series expansion is given by

f(\mathbf{x})=\sum_{|\mathbf{m}|=0}^{\infty}V_{\mathbf{m}}(\mathbf{x})a_{\mathbf{m}}

where
V_{\mathbf{m}}
are the Fueter polynomials and

a_{\mathbf{m}}=\frac{1}{\mathbf{m}!A_{n+1}}\int_{\|\xi\|\leq r}q_{\mathbf{m}}(\xi)d\tau(\xi)f(\xi)

where
q_{\mathbf{m}}(\mathbf{x})=\partial_{x_{1}}^{m_{1}}\dots\partial_{x_{n}}^{m_{n}}q_{0}(\mathbf{x})
. We have the following Cauchy inequality

\|a_{\mathbf{m}}\|\leq c(n,\mathbf{m})\frac{M(r,f)}{r^{|\mathbf{m}|}},

where

c(n,\mathbf{m}):=\frac{n(n+1)\cdot\dots\cdot(n+|\mathbf{m}|-1)}{\mathbf{m}!}

and
M(r,f)=\sup_{\|\mathbf{x}\|=r}\|f(\mathbf{x})\|
. In particular, setting
\mathbf{m}:=(m_{0},\dots,m_{n})\in\mathbb{N}^{n+1}_{0}
, we have

(2.1)

\|\partial_{x_{0}}^{m_{0}}\dots\partial_{x_{n}}^{m_{n}}q_{0}(\mathbf{x})\|\leq\frac{n(n+1)\cdots(n+|\mathbf{m}|-1)}{\|\mathbf{x}\|^{n+|\mathbf{m}|}}.

Definition 2.4
.

Let
f,\,g\in\mathcal{M}_{L}(\mathbb{R}^{n+1})
(resp.
f,\,g\in\mathcal{M}_{R}(\mathbb{R}^{n+1})
). Using their Taylor series representation

f(\mathbf{x})=\sum_{|\mathbf{m}|=0}^{\infty}V_{\mathbf{m}}(\mathbf{x})a_{\mathbf{m}},\quad\textrm{(resp. $f(\mathbf{x})=\sum_{|\mathbf{m}|=0}^{\infty}a_{\mathbf{m}}V_{\mathbf{m}}(\mathbf{x})$)}

and

g(\mathbf{x})=\sum_{|\mathbf{m}|=0}^{\infty}V_{\mathbf{m}}(\mathbf{x})b_{\mathbf{m}},\quad\textrm{(resp. $g(\mathbf{x})=\sum_{|\mathbf{m}|=0}^{\infty}b_{\mathbf{m}}V_{\mathbf{m}}(\mathbf{x})$)}

we define

f\odot_{L}g:=\sum_{|\mathbf{m}|=0}^{\infty}\sum_{|\mathbf{k}|=0}^{\infty}V_{\mathbf{m}+\mathbf{k}}(\mathbf{x})a_{\mathbf{m}}b_{\mathbf{k}}\quad\textrm{$\left(\textrm{resp. }f\odot_{R}g:=\sum_{|\mathbf{m}|=0}^{\infty}\sum_{|\mathbf{k}|=0}^{\infty}a_{\mathbf{m}}b_{\mathbf{k}}V_{\mathbf{m}+\mathbf{k}}(\mathbf{x})\right)$}.

Next, following
[

, we recall the definition of the standard growth order of an entire monogenic function:

Definition 2.5
.

Let
f:\mathbb{R}^{n+1}\to\mathbb{R}_{n}
be an entire left monogenic function. Then its growth order is said to be

\rho=\rho(f)=\limsup\limits_{r\to+\infty}\frac{\log^{+}\log^{+}(M(r,f))}{\log r},

where
\log^{+}(r)=\max\{0,\log(r)\}
.

It may occur that
0\leq\rho\leq+\infty
. For the case where
0<\rho<+\infty
Constales et al. defined in
[
19
]
the growth type of an entire monogenic function of growth order
\rho
by

\sigma=\sigma(f)=\limsup\limits_{r\to\infty+}\frac{\log^{+}M(r,f)}{r^{\rho}}.

As shown in
[
18
]
the growth order of a monogenic function can be directly computed by its Taylor coefficients, namely by

\rho(f)=\limsup\limits_{|{\bf m}|\to+\infty}\frac{|{\bf m}|\log|{\bf m}|}{-\log\Big|\frac{1}{c(n,{\bf m})}a_{\bf m}\Big|}.

Similarly, as shown in
[
19
]
the growth type can be expressed by

\sigma(f)=\frac{1}{e\rho}\limsup\limits_{r\to+\infty}|{\bf m}|\Big(|a_{\bf m}|\Big)^{\frac{\rho}{|{\bf m}|}}.

Remark 2.6
.

To also get a finer classification of functions with slow growth
\rho=0
and fast growth
\rho=\infty
Seremeta
[
31
]
and Shah
[

introduced the notion of generalized growth in the complex analysis setting which has been generalized to the monogenic setting in

,

More precisely the authors considered functions

\beta(\cdot)

and introduced the notions of the generalized growth and type in the way

\rho_{\alpha,\beta}(f)=\limsup\limits_{r\to+\infty}\frac{\alpha(\log^{+}M(r,f))}{\beta(\log(r))}

and

\sigma_{\alpha,\beta,\gamma}(f)=\limsup\limits_{r\to+\infty}\frac{\alpha(\log^{+}M(r,f))}{\beta((\gamma(r))^{\rho_{\alpha,\beta}})},

(see for instance Definition 2 of

In the particular case where
\alpha(r)=\log(r)
and where

is the identity function one re-obtains the classical definition of the growth order and growth type. Another generalization of the classical growth order that includes the classical growth order as a special case, and even the generalized growth orders under particular conditions, is the definition of the proximate growth order.

We introduce:

Definition 2.7
.

A differentiable function
\rho(r)\geq 0
defined for
r\geq 0
is said to be a proximate order for the order
\rho\geq 0
if it satisfies

(1)

\lim_{r\to+\infty}\rho(r)=\rho
,

(2)

\lim_{r\to+\infty}\rho^{\prime}(r)r\ln(r)=0.

We observe that for any proximate order function
\rho(r)
there exists a positive constant
r_{0}>0
such that for any
r>r_{0}
the function
r^{\rho(r)}
is strictly increasing and tending to
+\infty
.

Definition 2.8
.

For any proximate order function
\rho(r)
we can always take another proximate order function, called the normalization of the proximate order function
\rho(r)
,
\hat{\rho}(r)
such that there exists a constant
r_{1}>0
for which
\hat{\rho}(r)=\rho(r)
for any
r\geq r_{1}
and
r^{\hat{\rho}(r)}
is strictly increasing on
r>0
and maps the interval
(0,+\infty)
to
(0,+\infty)
.

We denote by
\varphi:(0,+\infty)\to(0+\infty)
the inverse function of the function
t=r^{\hat{\rho}(r)}
. Moreover, we set

(2.2)

G_{q}=G_{\hat{\rho},q}:=\frac{\varphi(q)^{q}}{(e\rho)^{q/\rho}},\quad\textrm{for $q\in\mathbb{N}$.}

Let
\rho(r)
be a proximate order for a positive order
\rho>0
. For any
\sigma>0
, we consider the Banach space

A_{\rho,\sigma}:=\{f\in\mathcal{M}_{L}(\mathbb{R}^{n+1}):\,\|f\|_{\rho,\sigma}:=\sup_{\mathbf{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{n+1}}\|f(\mathbf{x})\|\exp(-\sigma\|\mathbf{x}\|^{\rho(\|\mathbf{x}\|)})<+\infty\}

with the norm
\|\cdot\|_{\rho,\sigma}
. Moreover, we use the following notation: for any
\mathbf{m}=(m_{1},\dots,m_{n})\in\mathbb{N}^{n}
we write

\partial^{\mathbf{m}}_{\mathbf{x}}f(\mathbf{x}):=\partial_{x_{1}}^{m_{1}}\dots\partial_{x_{n}}^{m_{n}}f(\mathbf{x}).

We recall some basic properties of proximate orders that will be used in the following.
The proofs of these results can be found in Section 2 of

and in the references therein.

Lemma 2.9
.

There exist constants
k>0
and
B>0
depending only on

such that

\forall r>0,\,\forall s>0,\quad(r+s)^{\hat{\rho}(r+s)}\leq k(r^{\hat{\rho}(r)}+s^{\hat{\rho}(s)})+B.

Precisely speaking, we can choose

depending only on the order
\rho=\lim_{r\to+\infty}\hat{\rho}(r)

Lemma 2.10
.

The sequence
\{G_{p}\}_{p}
is supermultiplicative, that is,

G_{p}G_{q}\leq G_{p+q},\quad\textrm{for any $p,\,q\in\mathbb{N}$.}

Lemma 2.11
.

For every
\delta>0
with
\delta<\frac{1}{\rho}
, there exists

such that if

\left(\frac{1}{\rho}-\delta\right)\frac{d}{dt}\ln(t)<\frac{d}{dt}\ln\varphi(t)<\left(\frac{1}{\rho}+\delta\right)\frac{d}{dt}\ln(t).

Lemma 2.12
.

For

Lemma 3.1
.

If
\sigma_{2}>\sigma_{1}>0
, then the inclusion map
A_{\rho,\sigma_{1}}\xhookrightarrow{}A_{\rho,\sigma_{2}}
is compact.

Proof.

We will show that
B:=\{f\in A_{\rho,\sigma_{1}}:\,\|f\|_{\rho,\sigma_{1}}\leq 1\}
is relatively compact in
A_{\rho,\sigma_{2}}
, i.e., any sequence
\{f_{j}\}_{j\in\mathbb{N}}\subset B
has an accumulation point with respect to the norm of
A_{\rho,\sigma_{2}}
.

First we will prove that any sequence
\{f_{j}\}_{j\in\mathbb{N}}\subset B
admits a convergent subsequence in the uniform convergence topology to an entire monogenic function. By the Arzelá-Ascoli Theorem it is sufficient to prove that
\{f_{j}\}_{j\in\mathbb{N}}
is equicontinuous and uniformly bounded in any compact convex set
K\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
. We fix a compact convex subset
K
of
\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
. Since
\{f_{j}\}_{j\in\mathbb{N}}\subset B
, the sequence is uniformly bounded int
K
. Moreover, we have

\|f_{j}(\mathbf{x})-f_{j}(\mathbf{y})\|\leq C_{j}\|\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{y}\|,

where
\nabla
is the usual gradient,
C_{j}=\sup_{\mathbf{x}\in K}\|\nabla f_{j}(\mathbf{x})\|
and
\mathbf{x},\,\mathbf{y}\in K
. We choose
r
large enough in a such way that
K\subset B(0,r)
. Thus, there exists a positive constant
C_{K}
which only depends on
K
such that for any
\mathbf{x}\in K
and for any
j\in\mathbb{N}
, we have

\begin{split}\|\nabla f_{j}(\mathbf{x})\|&=\|\frac{1}{A_{n+1}}\int_{\partial B(0,r)}\nabla q_{0}(\mathbf{x}-\xi)d\tau(\xi)f_{j}(\xi)\|\\
&\leq C_{1}\int_{\partial B(0,r)}\frac{1}{\|\mathbf{x}-\xi\|^{1+n}}|d\tau(\xi)|M(r,f_{j})\leq C_{2}\frac{M(r,f_{j})}{\operatorname{dist}(\mathbf{x},\partial B(0,r))^{1+n}}\leq C_{K}.\end{split}

where
C_{1}
and
C_{2}
are suitable positive constants while in the first inequality we have used (
2.1
).
In particular, for any
\mathbf{x},\,\mathbf{y}\in K
and for any
j\in\mathbb{N}
we have

\|f_{j}(\mathbf{x})-f_{j}(\mathbf{y})\|\leq C_{K}\|\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{y}\|,

i.e.,
\{f_{j}\}_{j\in\mathbb{N}}
is equicontinuous. After taking a subsequence if necessary, we can suppose that the sequence
\{f_{j}\}_{j\in\mathbb{N}}
converges to
f\in\mathcal{M}_{L}(\mathbb{R}^{n+1})
in the topology of the uniform convergence. Now we will prove that the sequence
\{f_{j}\}_{j\in\mathbb{N}}
is a Cauchy sequence in
A_{\rho,\sigma_{2}}
. We fix
\delta>0
and we observe that for any
R>0
we have

\begin{split}&\sup_{\mathbf{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{n+1}}\|f_{j}(\mathbf{x})-f_{\ell}(\mathbf{x})\|\exp(-\sigma_{2}\|\mathbf{x}\|^{\rho(\|\mathbf{x}\|)})\\
&=\max\left\{\sup_{\|\mathbf{x}\|\leq R}\|f_{j}(\mathbf{x})-f_{\ell}(\mathbf{x})\|\exp(-\sigma_{2}\|\mathbf{x}\|^{\rho(\|\mathbf{x}\|)}),\sup_{\|\mathbf{x}\|\geq R}\|f_{j}(\mathbf{x})-f_{\ell}(\mathbf{x})\|\exp(-\sigma_{2}\|\mathbf{x}\|^{\rho(\|\mathbf{x}\|)})\right\}.\end{split}

With respect to the second supremum, we can choose
R>0
large enough such that:

\exp((\sigma_{1}-\sigma_{2})\|\mathbf{x}\|^{\rho(\|\mathbf{x}\|)})\leq\frac{\delta}{2},\ \ \ {\rm for\ any}\ \|\mathbf{x}\|\geq R.

Thus, since
f_{j},\,f_{\ell}\in B
, we have that

\begin{split}&\sup_{\|\mathbf{x}\|\geq R}\|f_{j}(\mathbf{x})-f_{\ell}(\mathbf{x})\|\exp(-\sigma_{2}\|\mathbf{x}\|^{\rho(\|\mathbf{x}\|)})\\
&=\sup_{\|\mathbf{x}\|\geq R}\|f_{j}(\mathbf{x})-f_{\ell}(\mathbf{x})\|\exp(-\sigma_{1}\|\mathbf{x}\|^{\rho(\|\mathbf{x}\|)})\exp((\sigma_{1}-\sigma_{2})\|\mathbf{x}\|^{\rho(\|\mathbf{x}\|)})\leq 2\cdot\frac{\delta}{2}=\delta.\end{split}

Moreover, with respect to the first supremum, by the uniform convergence of the sequence
\{f_{j}\}_{j\in\mathbb{N}}
on the compact subset of
\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
, there exists a positive integer
N
such that for any
j,\,\ell\geq N
we have

\sup_{\|\mathbf{x}\|\leq R}\|f_{j}(\mathbf{x})-f_{\ell}(\mathbf{x})\|\exp(-\sigma_{2}\|\mathbf{x}\|^{\rho(\|\mathbf{x}\|)})\leq\sup_{\|\mathbf{x}\|\leq R}\|f_{j}(\mathbf{x})-f_{\ell}(\mathbf{x})\|\leq\delta.

Thus we have proved that the sequence
\{f_{j}\}_{j\in\mathbb{N}}
is a Cauchy sequence.

Definition 3.2

(The spaces
A_{\rho}
and
A_{\rho,\sigma+0}
)
.

We define the space

A_{\rho}:=\lim_{\underset{\sigma>0}{\rightarrow}}A_{\rho,\sigma}

We also define the space

A_{\rho,\sigma+0}:=\lim_{\underset{\epsilon>0}{\leftarrow}}A_{\rho,\sigma+\epsilon}

i.e.,
A_{\rho,\sigma+0}:=\cap_{\epsilon>0}A_{\rho,\sigma+\epsilon}
and we say that a sequence
\{f_{j}\}_{j\in\mathbb{N}}\subseteq A_{\rho,\sigma+0}
converges to
f\in A_{\rho,\sigma+0}
if for any
\epsilon>0
we have
\{f_{j}\}_{j\in\mathbb{N}}\subseteq A_{\rho,\sigma+\epsilon}
,
f\in A_{\rho,\sigma+\epsilon}
and
\lim_{j\to+\infty}\|f_{j}-f\|_{\rho,\sigma+\epsilon}=0
.

Remark 3.3
.

The spaces
A_{\rho}
and
A_{\hat{\rho}}
coincide with each other and they share the same locally convex topologies as well, and the same holds for
A_{\rho,\sigma+0}
and
A_{\hat{\rho},\sigma+0}
.

For any fixed
f(\mathbf{x})=\sum_{|\mathbf{m}|=0}^{\infty}V_{\mathbf{m}}(\mathbf{x})a_{\mathbf{m}}\in\mathcal{M}_{L}(\mathbb{R}^{n+1})
we define

K_{q}:=\sup_{\|\mathbf{x}\|\leq 1}\|\sum_{|\mathbf{m}|=q}V_{\mathbf{m}}(\mathbf{x})a_{\mathbf{m}}\|

and

P_{q}({\mathbf{x}}):=\sum_{|{\mathbf{m}}|=q}V_{\mathbf{m}}({\mathbf{x}})a_{\mathbf{m}}.

Theorem 3.4
.

Let
f(\mathbf{x})=\sum_{|\mathbf{m}|=0}^{\infty}V_{\mathbf{m}}(\mathbf{x})a_{\mathbf{m}}\in\mathcal{M}_{L}(\mathbb{R}^{n+1})
be a left monogenic entire function of finite order
\rho>0
and of proximate order
\rho(r)
. Then its type
\sigma
with respect to
\rho(r)
is given by

(3.1)

\frac{1}{\rho}\ln(\sigma)=\limsup_{q\to\infty}\left(\frac{1}{q}\ln K_{q}+\ln\varphi(q)\right)-\frac{1}{\rho}-\frac{\ln(\rho)}{\rho}.

Proof.

First we prove that

\frac{1}{\rho}\ln(\sigma)\geq\limsup_{q\to\infty}\left(\frac{1}{q}\ln K_{q}+\ln\varphi(q)\right)-\frac{1}{\rho}-\frac{\ln(\rho)}{\rho}.

Let

P_{q}(\mathbf{x})=\sum_{|\mathbf{m}|=q}V_{\mathbf{m}}(\mathbf{x})a_{\mathbf{m}}

and suppose that
\mathbf{w}_{q}\in\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
be such that
\|P_{q}(\mathbf{w}_{q})\|=K_{q}
and
\|\mathbf{w}_{q}\|=1
. There exists
\mathbf{b}\in\mathbb{R}_{n}
(see
[
25
, Theorem
3.20
]
) such that

•

\mathbf{b}=\mathbf{z}_{1}\cdots\mathbf{z}_{r}
where
\mathbf{z}_{i}\in\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
and
\|\mathbf{z}_{i}\|=1
for any
i=1,\dots r
,

•

\mathbf{w}_{q}:=\mathbf{b}(x\mathbf{e}_{1})\bar{\mathbf{b}}
for some
x\in\mathbb{R}
with
|x|\leq 1
where
\bar{\mathbf{b}}:=\bar{\mathbf{z}}_{r}\cdots\bar{\mathbf{z}}_{1}
.

The function
g(\mathbf{x}):=\mathbf{b}f(\mathbf{b}\mathbf{x}\bar{\mathbf{b}})
is a left monogenic entire function (see
[
29
]
) whose Taylor series is

g(\mathbf{x})=\sum_{|\mathbf{m}|=0}^{\infty}V_{\mathbf{m}}(\mathbf{x})a_{\mathbf{m}}^{\prime}.

We define

\tilde{P}_{q}(\mathbf{x}):=\sum_{|\mathbf{m}|=q}V_{\mathbf{m}}(\mathbf{x})a_{\mathbf{m}}^{\prime}.

Thus we have

K_{q}=\|P_{q}(\mathbf{w}_{q})\|=\|\tilde{P}_{q}(x\mathbf{e}_{1})\|=\|x^{q}a_{\mathbf{m}_{q}}^{\prime}\|\leq\|a_{\mathbf{m}_{q}}^{\prime}\|\leq\frac{c(n,\mathbf{m}_{q})M(r,g)}{r^{q}}=\frac{c(n,\mathbf{m}_{q})M(r,f)}{r^{q}},

where
\mathbf{m}_{q}=(q,0,\dots,0)
. If
\tilde{\sigma}>\sigma
then for
r
large we have:

M(r,f)\leq\exp\left(\tilde{\sigma}r^{\rho(r)}\right),

and

(3.2)

\ln(K_{q})\leq\ln(c(n,\mathbf{m}_{q}))+\tilde{\sigma}r^{\rho(r)}-q\ln(r).

If
q
is large enough, then we define
r_{q}
to be the real number such that
q=\tilde{\sigma}\cdot\rho\cdot(r_{q})^{\rho(r_{q})}
and we have
\varphi\left(\frac{q}{\tilde{\sigma}\rho}\right)=r_{q}
. Thus, for
q
large enough, dividing by
q
and summing
\log(\varphi(q))
to both side of inequality (
3.2
), we have

(3.3)

\ln\left(\varphi(q)K_{q}^{\frac{1}{q}}\right)<\ln\left(c(n,\mathbf{m}_{q})^{\frac{1}{q}}\right)+\frac{1}{\rho}+\ln\left(\frac{\varphi(q)}{\varphi\left(\frac{q}{\tilde{\sigma}\rho}\right)}\right).

By
[
28
, (1) Theorem 1.23]
we have

\lim_{q\to+\infty}\frac{\varphi(q)}{\varphi\left(\frac{q}{\tilde{\sigma}\rho}\right)}=(\tilde{\sigma}\rho)^{\frac{1}{\rho}}.

Moreover, since
\lim_{q\to+\infty}\left(c(n,\mathbf{m}_{q})\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}=1
, taking the
\limsup
to both side of (
3.3
), we have

\limsup_{q\to+\infty}\ln\left(\varphi(q)K_{q}^{\frac{1}{q}}\right)\leq\ln\left((e\tilde{\sigma}\rho)^{\frac{1}{\rho}}\right)

for any
\tilde{\sigma}>\sigma
.
Thus we have proved that

\limsup_{q\to+\infty}\ln\left(\varphi(q)K_{q}^{\frac{1}{q}}\right)\leq\ln\left((e\sigma\rho)^{\frac{1}{\rho}}\right).

The other side of the inequality follows as in
[
28
, (3) Theorem 1.23]
by the properties of
\varphi
.
∎

Lemma 3.5
.

A left monogenic entire function
f(\mathbf{x})=\sum_{|\mathbf{m}|=0}^{+\infty}V_{\mathbf{m}}(\mathbf{x})a_{\mathbf{m}}\in\mathcal{M}_{L}(\mathbb{R}^{n+1})
belongs to
A_{\rho,\sigma+0}
if and only if we have

\limsup_{q\to\infty}(K_{q}G_{\hat{\rho},q})^{\frac{\rho}{q}}\leq\sigma.

Proof.

We have that
f\in A_{\rho,\sigma+0}
if and only if for any
\epsilon>0
there exists a
D_{\epsilon}>0
such that

\|f({\mathbf{x}})\|\leq D_{\epsilon}\exp((\sigma+\epsilon)\|{\mathbf{x}}\|^{\rho({\mathbf{x}})})\quad\textrm{for all ${\mathbf{x}}\in\mathbb{R}^{n+1}$}.

This is equivalent to

(3.4)

\limsup_{r\to+\infty}\frac{\sup_{\|{\mathbf{x}}\|\leq r}\ln(\|f({\mathbf{x}})\|)}{r^{\rho(r)}}\leq\sigma.

Since

\limsup_{r\to+\infty}\frac{\sup_{\|{\mathbf{x}}\|\leq r}\ln(\|f({\mathbf{x}})\|)}{r^{\rho(r)}}

is the type of
f
with respect to
\rho(r)
, by Theorem
3.4
and inequality (
3.4
) we have

\limsup_{q\to+\infty}\left(\frac{\rho}{q}\ln(K_{q})+\rho\ln(\varphi(q))\right)-\ln(e\rho)\leq\ln(\sigma).

Finally, we can conclude that

\limsup_{q\to+\infty}\,\ln\left(K_{q}^{\rho/q}\frac{\varphi(q)^{\rho}}{e\rho}\right)\leq\ln(\sigma)

which gives

\limsup_{q\to+\infty}\left(K_{q}\frac{\varphi(q)^{q}}{(e\rho)^{q/\rho}}\right)^{\rho/q}=\limsup_{q\to+\infty}\left(K_{q}G_{\hat{\rho},q}\right)^{\rho/q}\leq\sigma.

∎

Corollary 3.6
.

If
f(\mathbf{x})=\sum_{|\mathbf{m}|=q}V_{\mathbf{m}}(\mathbf{x})a_{\mathbf{m}}\in\mathcal{M}_{L}(\mathbb{R}^{n+1})
belongs to
f\in A_{\rho,\sigma+0}
, then we have

(3.5)

\limsup_{q\to\infty}\left(\max_{|\mathbf{m}|=q}\|a_{\mathbf{m}}\|G_{\hat{\rho},q}\right)^{\rho/q}\leq n^{\rho}\sigma.

Conversely, if
\{a_{\mathbf{m}}\}
satisfies (
3.5
), then we have
f(\mathbf{x})\in A_{\rho,n^{\rho}\sigma+0}
.

Proof.

We have

\|a_{\mathbf{m}}\|=\frac{1}{{\mathbf{m}}!}\|\partial_{\mathbf{x}}^{\mathbf{m}}P_{q}(0)\|\leq\frac{c(n,{\mathbf{m}})\sup_{\|x\|\leq r}\|P_{q}({\mathbf{x}})\|}{r^{q}}=c(n,{\mathbf{m}})\sup_{\|x\|\leq 1}\|P_{q}({\mathbf{x}})\|=c(n,{\mathbf{m}})K_{q}.

Combining the previous estimate with Lemma
3.5
we get that for any
\epsilon>0
there exists
q_{0}>0
such that for any
q>q_{0}
we have

\begin{split}\left(\max_{|{\mathbf{m}}|=q}\|a_{\mathbf{m}}\|G_{\rho,q}\right)^{\rho/q}=\left(\max_{|{\mathbf{m}}|=q}c(n,{\mathbf{m}})K_{q}G_{\rho,q}\right)^{\rho/q}&\leq\left(\left(\sum_{|{\mathbf{m}}|=q}c(n,{\mathbf{m}})\right)^{1/q}\right)^{\rho}(K_{q}G_{\rho,q})^{\rho/q}\\
&\leq(n+\epsilon)^{\rho}(\sigma+\epsilon),\end{split}

which implies

\limsup_{q\to+\infty}\,\left(\max_{|{\mathbf{m}}|=q}\|a_{\mathbf{m}}\|G_{\rho,q}\right)^{\rho/q}\leq n^{\rho}\sigma.

Conversely, if we have (
3.5
), then for any
\epsilon>0
there exists
C>0
and
q_{0}>0
such that for any
q>q_{0}

we have

\begin{split}K_{q}=\sup_{\|\mathbf{x}\|\leq 1}\|P_{q}({\mathbf{x}})\|\leq\sup_{\|\mathbf{x}\|\leq 1}\sum_{\|{\mathbf{m}}\|=q}\|a_{\mathbf{m}}\|\|{\mathbf{x}}\|^{q}\leq\max_{|{\mathbf{m}}|=q}\|a_{\mathbf{m}}\|G_{\rho,q}\frac{(q+1)^{n-1}}{G_{\rho,q}}\leq(n^{\rho}\sigma+\epsilon)^{q/\rho}\frac{(q+1)^{n-1}}{G_{\rho,q}}.\end{split}

Thus, we have

\limsup_{q\to+\infty}(K_{q}G_{\rho,q})^{\rho/q}\leq n^{\rho}\sigma

and
f\in A_{\rho,n^{\rho}\sigma+0}
.
∎

The following proposition is a direct consequence of the previous corollary.

Proposition 3.7
.

A left monogenic entire function
f(\mathbf{x})=\sum_{|\mathbf{m}|=q}V_{\mathbf{m}}(\mathbf{x})a_{\mathbf{m}}\in\mathcal{M}_{L}(\mathbb{R}^{n+1})
belongs to
A_{\rho}
if and only if we have

\limsup_{q\to\infty}\left(\max_{|\mathbf{m}|=q}\|a_{\mathbf{m}}\|G_{\hat{\rho},q}\right)^{\rho/q}<\infty.

We now need some estimates of norms of Fueter polynomials.

Lemma 3.8
.

Suppose
\rho>0
. For any
\sigma
and
\sigma^{\prime}
with
0<\sigma^{\prime}<\sigma
, there exists
C>0
such that for any
\mathbf{m}\in\mathbb{N}^{n}_{0}
, we have

\|V_{\mathbf{m}}(\mathbf{x})\|_{\rho,\sigma}\leq C\sigma^{\prime-|\mathbf{m}|/\rho}G_{\hat{\rho},|\mathbf{m}|}.

Proof.

Since the norms
\|\cdot\|_{\rho,\sigma}
and
\|\cdot\|_{\hat{\rho},\sigma}
are equivalent, we may assume from the beginning that
\hat{\rho}(r)
is as in Definition
2.8
and
\hat{\rho}(r)=\rho(r)
.

First we prove, for sufficiently large
r=\|{\mathbf{x}}\|
and
q=|{\mathbf{m}}|
, that

\|V_{\mathbf{m}}({\mathbf{x}})e^{-\sigma r^{\rho(r)}}\|\leq(\sigma^{\prime})^{-|{\mathbf{m}}|/\rho}G_{\hat{\rho},|{\mathbf{m}}|}.

Let
\varphi
be the inverse function of
t=r^{\rho(r)}
. We define
r=\varphi(t)=\|{\mathbf{x}}\|
. Thus we have

\begin{split}\|V_{\mathbf{m}}({\mathbf{x}})e^{-\sigma r^{\rho(r)}}\|G^{-1}_{\hat{\rho},q}&\leq r^{q}e^{-\sigma r^{\rho(r)}}G^{-1}_{\hat{\rho},q}\\
&=\exp\left(q\left(\ln(\varphi(t))-\sigma\frac{t}{q}-\frac{1}{q}\ln(G_{\hat{\rho},q})\right)\right)\\
&=\exp\left(q\left(\ln(\varphi(t))-\sigma\frac{t}{q}-\ln(\varphi(q))+\frac{1}{\rho}\ln(e\rho)\right)\right)\\
&=\exp\left(q\left(\ln\left(\frac{\varphi(t)}{\varphi(q)}\right)-\sigma\frac{t}{q}+\frac{1}{\rho}\ln(e\rho)\right)\right)\\
\end{split}

By Lemma
2.12
, for any
0<\sigma^{\prime}<\sigma
there exists
T_{1}\geq 0
such that

\ln\left(\frac{\varphi(t)}{\varphi(q)}\right)-\sigma\frac{t}{q}+\frac{1}{\rho}\ln(e\rho)\leq-\frac{1}{\rho}\ln(\sigma^{\prime})\quad\textrm{for any $q,t\geq T_{1}$}.

This implies

(3.6)

\|V_{\mathbf{m}}({\mathbf{x}})e^{-\sigma r^{\rho(r)}}\|G^{-1}_{\hat{\rho},q}\leq\exp\left(-\frac{q}{\rho}\ln\sigma^{\prime}\right)=(\sigma^{\prime})^{-q/\rho}

for
|{\mathbf{m}}|=q\geq T_{1}
and
t\geq T_{1}
(i.e.
\|{\mathbf{x}}\|\geq\varphi(T_{1})
). Next we consider the case
\|{\mathbf{x}}\|\leq\varphi(T_{1})
(i.e.
t\leq T_{1}
) and
q=|{\mathbf{m}}|>>1
. We have

\begin{split}\|V_{\mathbf{m}}({\mathbf{x}})e^{-\sigma r^{\rho(r)}}\|G^{-1}_{\hat{\rho},q}&\leq r^{q}e^{-\sigma r^{\rho(r)}}G^{-1}_{\hat{\rho},q}\\
&=\exp\left(q\left(\ln\left(\frac{\varphi(t)}{\varphi(q)}\right)-\sigma\frac{t}{q}+\frac{1}{\rho}\ln(e\rho)\right)\right)\\
&\leq\exp\left(q\left(\ln\left(\frac{\varphi(T_{1})}{\varphi(q)}\right)+\frac{1}{\rho}\ln(e\rho)\right)\right).\end{split}

Note that for any given
\sigma^{\prime}>0
, we can take
T_{2}\geq T_{1}
:

\ln\left(\frac{\varphi(T_{1})}{\varphi(T_{2})}\right)+\frac{1}{\rho}\ln(e\rho)\leq-\frac{1}{\rho}\ln(\sigma^{\prime}).

Thus we have

(3.7)

\|V_{\mathbf{m}}({\mathbf{x}})e^{-\sigma r^{\rho(r)}}\|G^{-1}_{\hat{\rho},q}\leq(\sigma^{\prime})^{-q/\rho}

for
|{\mathbf{m}}|=q\geq T_{2}
and
\|{\mathbf{x}}\|\leq\varphi(T_{1})
. By (
3.6
) and (
3.7
) for any
{\mathbf{x}}\in\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
and for any
|{\mathbf{m}}|=q\geq T_{2}
, we have

\|V_{\mathbf{m}}({\mathbf{x}})e^{-\sigma r^{\rho(r)}}\|G^{-1}_{\hat{\rho},q}\leq(\sigma^{\prime})^{-q/\rho}.

We know that there exists
C>0
such that
\|V_{\mathbf{m}}({\mathbf{x}})e^{-\sigma r^{\rho(r)}}\|\leq C
for any
|{\mathbf{m}}|<T_{2}
and for any
{\mathbf{x}}\in\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
. Thus

\|V_{\mathbf{m}}({\mathbf{x}})e^{-\sigma r^{\rho(r)}}\|G^{-1}_{\hat{\rho},q}\leq C^{\prime}(\sigma^{\prime})^{-q/\rho}

where

C^{\prime}=\sup_{|{\mathbf{m}}|\leq T_{2}}\left(1,\frac{C}{(\sigma^{\prime})^{-q/\rho}G_{\hat{\rho},q}}\right).

∎

Lemma 3.9
.

There exists a constant
k
depending only on
\rho
for which the following statement holds: For any
\sigma>0
, we can take
C(\sigma)
such that for any
f\in A_{\hat{\rho},\sigma}
, and any
\mathbf{m}\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{n}
, the inequality

\frac{1}{\mathbf{m}!}\|\partial^{\mathbf{m}}_{\mathbf{x}}f(\mathbf{x})\|_{\hat{\rho},k\sigma}\leq C(\sigma)\|f\|_{\hat{\rho},\sigma}\frac{(2k(c(n,{\mathbf{m}}))^{\rho/q}\sigma)^{q/\rho}}{G_{\hat{\rho},q}}

holds. Here we write
q=|\mathbf{m}|
.

Proof.

There exists a constant
k>0
which depends on
\rho
and there exists a constant
B>0
which depends on
\hat{\rho}(r)
such that

(r+s)^{\hat{\rho}(r+s)}\leq k(r^{\hat{\rho}(r)}+s^{\hat{\rho}(s)})+B\quad\textrm{for all $r,s>0.$}

The Cauchy estimates give us, for
\|{\mathbf{x}}\|\leq r
and
|{\mathbf{m}}|=q
the chain of inequalities

\begin{split}\frac{\|\partial^{\mathbf{m}}_{\mathbf{x}}f({\mathbf{x}})\|}{{\mathbf{m}}!}&\leq\inf_{s>0}\frac{c(n,{\mathbf{m}})\max_{|\xi|=s}\|f({\mathbf{x}}+\xi)\|}{s^{|{\mathbf{m}}|}}\\
&\leq\|f\|_{\hat{\rho},\sigma}\inf_{s>0}\frac{c(n,{\mathbf{m}})}{s^{q}}\exp\left(\sigma(r+s)^{\hat{\rho}(r+s)}\right)\\
&\leq\|f\|_{\hat{\rho},\sigma}\inf_{s>0}\frac{c(n,{\mathbf{m}})}{s^{q}}\exp\left(k\sigma(r^{\hat{\rho}(r)}+s^{\hat{\rho}(s)})+B\sigma\right)\\
&=e^{B\sigma}\|f\|_{\hat{\rho},\sigma}c(n,{\mathbf{m}})\exp\left(k\sigma r^{\hat{\rho}(r)}\right)\inf_{s>0}\frac{\exp\left(k\sigma s^{\hat{\rho}(s)}\right)}{s^{q}}\\
&\leq e^{B\sigma}\|f\|_{\hat{\rho},\sigma}\exp\left(k\sigma r^{\hat{\rho}(r)}\right)\frac{\left(c(n,{\mathbf{m}})^{\rho/q}e\right)^{q/\rho}}{\varphi(q)^{q}}\left(\frac{\varphi(q)}{\varphi(q/(k\sigma\rho))}\right)^{q}\\
&\leq C(\sigma)\|f\|_{\hat{\rho},\sigma}\exp\left(k\sigma r^{\hat{\rho}(r)}\right)\frac{\left(2kc(n,{\mathbf{m}})^{\rho/q}\sigma\right)^{q/\rho}}{G_{\hat{\rho},q}},\end{split}

where the last two inequalities are obtained as in
[
11
, Lemma 3.8]
. In particular we have

\frac{\|\partial_{\mathbf{x}}^{\mathbf{m}}f(\mathbf{x})\|}{\mathbf{m}!}\exp\left(k\sigma\|\mathbf{x}\|^{\hat{\rho}(\|\mathbf{x}\|)}\right)\leq C(\sigma)\|f\|_{\hat{\rho},\sigma}\frac{\left(2kc(n,{\mathbf{m}})^{\rho/q}\sigma\right)^{q/\rho}}{G_{\hat{\rho},q}},

which implies the inequality in the statement.
∎

Remark 3.10
.

Observe that since

\limsup_{q\to+\infty}\left(\sum_{|{\mathbf{m}}|=q}c(n,{\mathbf{m}})\right)^{1/q}=n

there exists a constant
C(n)
which depends only on
n
such that for any
{\mathbf{m}}\in\mathbb{N}^{n}
we have

(c(n,{\mathbf{m}}))^{1/|{\mathbf{m}}|}\leq C(n).

Thus, in Lemma
3.9
the estimate can be rewritten in the following way

\frac{1}{\mathbf{m}!}\|\partial^{\mathbf{m}}_{\mathbf{x}}f(\mathbf{x})\|_{\hat{\rho},k\sigma}\leq C(\sigma)\|f\|_{\hat{\rho},\sigma}\frac{(2k(C(n))^{\rho}\sigma)^{q/\rho}}{G_{\hat{\rho},q}}.

In view of the above stated properties, we can now prove the following crucial results.

Proposition 3.11
.

For an entire left monogenic function
f(\mathbf{x})
belonging to
A_{\rho,\sigma+0}
, its Taylor expansion
\sum_{\mathbf{m}\in\mathbb{N}^{n}}V_{\mathbf{m}}(\mathbf{x})a_{\mathbf{m}}
converges to
f(\mathbf{x})
in the space
A_{\rho,n^{\rho}\sigma+0}
.
In particular, the set of Fueter polynomials is dense in
A_{\rho,+0}
and also dense in
A_{\rho}
.

Proof.

For the former statement, it suffices to show that

\sum_{{\mathbf{m}}\in\mathbb{N}^{n}}\|V_{\mathbf{m}}({\mathbf{x}})a_{\mathbf{m}}\|_{\rho,n^{\rho}(\sigma+\epsilon)}

is finite for any
\epsilon>0
. By Lemma
3.8
we have

\|V_{\mathbf{m}}({\mathbf{x}})\|_{\rho,n^{\rho}(\sigma+\epsilon)}\leq C_{0}(n^{\rho}(\sigma+\epsilon/2))^{-|{\mathbf{m}}|/\rho}G_{\hat{\rho},|{\mathbf{m}}|}.

On the other hand, by Corollary
3.6
, we have

\max_{|{\mathbf{m}}|=q}\|a_{\mathbf{m}}\|G_{\hat{\rho},q}\leq C_{1}(n^{\rho}(\sigma+\epsilon/4))^{q/\rho}.

Therefore, we have

\begin{split}\sum_{{\mathbf{m}}\in\mathbb{N}^{n}}\|V_{\mathbf{m}}({\mathbf{x}})a_{\mathbf{m}}\|_{\rho,n^{\rho}(\sigma+\epsilon)}&=\sum_{q\in\mathbb{N}}\sum_{|{\mathbf{m}}|=q}\|a_{\mathbf{m}}\|G_{\hat{\rho},q}\|V_{\mathbf{m}}({\mathbf{x}})\|_{\rho,n^{\rho}(\sigma+\epsilon)}G^{-1}_{\hat{\rho},q}\\
&\leq\sum_{q\in\mathbb{N}}\sum_{|{\mathbf{m}}|=q}C_{0}(n^{\rho}(\sigma+\epsilon/2))^{-q/\rho}C_{1}(n^{\rho}(\sigma+\epsilon/4))^{q/\rho}\\
&\leq C_{0}C_{1}\sum_{q\in\mathbb{N}}(q+1)^{n-1}\left(\frac{\sigma+\epsilon/4}{\sigma+\epsilon/2}\right)^{q/\rho},\end{split}

where the last series is convergent. For the latter statement in the case
f\in A_{\rho,+0}
, it follows from the former one with
\sigma=0
that

\lim_{q\to+\infty}\sum_{|{\mathbf{m}}|\leq q}V_{\mathbf{m}}({\mathbf{x}})a_{\mathbf{m}}=f({\mathbf{x}})

in the space
A_{\rho,+0}
. In the case
f\in A_{\rho}
, there exists
\sigma>0
such that
f\in A_{\rho,\sigma+0}
. Then the same convergence holds in the space
A_{\rho,n^{\rho}\sigma+0}
and therefore also in the space
A_{\rho}
.
∎

Lemma 3.12
.

Let
f\in A_{\rho,\sigma}
and
f=\sum_{|\bf m|=0}^{\infty}V_{{\bf m}}({\bf x})f_{{\bf m}}
. Let
s
be a real positive number. Then, for any
\eta>0
there exists
C_{\eta}>0
such that for any
{\bf m}\in\mathbb{N}^{n}_{0}
, we have

\|f_{{\bf m}}\|\exp(-\sigma(1+\eta)(s+1)^{\rho}r^{\rho(r)})\leq\exp(\sigma C_{\eta})\frac{\|f\|_{\rho,\sigma}c(n,{\bf m})}{(sr)^{|{\bf m}|}}.

Proof.

We have that

\begin{split}\|f_{{\bf m}}\|&=\frac{\|\partial^{\bf m}f(0)\|}{{\bf m}!}\leq\sup_{\|{\bf x}\|=r}\frac{\|\partial^{\bf m}f({\bf x})\|}{{\bf m}!}\leq\sup_{\|{\bf x}\|=r}\frac{(\sup_{\|\zeta-{\bf x}\|=s\|{\bf x}\|}\|f(\zeta)\|)c(n,{\bf m})}{(sr)^{|{\bf m}|}}\\
&\leq\exp(\sigma((s+1)r)^{\rho((s+1)r)})\frac{\left(\sup_{\|{\bf x}\|=(s+1)r}\|f({\bf x})\|\exp(-\sigma((s+1)r)^{\rho((s+1)r)})\right)c(n,{\bf m})}{(sr)^{|{\bf m}|}}\\
&\leq\exp(\sigma(1+\eta)(s+1)^{\rho}r^{\rho(r)}+\sigma C_{\eta})\frac{\|f\|_{\rho,\sigma}c(n,{\bf m})}{(sr)^{|{\bf m}|}},\end{split}

where in the first inequality we have used the maximum modulus principle, in the second inequality we have used the Cauchy inequalities in the ball centered at
{\bf x}
with radius
s\|{\bf x}\|
, in the third inequality we have used the fact that all the balls centered at
{\bf x}
with
\|{\bf x}\|=r
of radius
s\|{\bf x}\|
are contained in the ball centered at
0
with radius
(s+1)r
and in the last inequality we have used the fact that for any
\eta>0
there exists a positive constant
C_{\eta}
such that

(kr)^{\rho(kr)}\leq(1+\eta)k^{\rho}r^{\rho(x)}+C_{\eta},

see
[
28
, p.
16
, Proposition
1.20
]
.
∎

Lemma 3.13
.

Let
g_{1}({\bf x})=\sum_{|{\bf m}|=0}^{\infty}V_{\bf m}({\bf x})a_{{\bf m}}\in A_{\rho,\tau_{1}}
and
g_{2}({\bf x})=\sum_{|{\bf m}|=0}^{\infty}V_{\bf m}({\bf x})b_{{\bf m}}\in A_{\rho,\tau_{2}}
. Let
\delta
be a positive constant then for any
\eta>0
there exists
C(n,\eta,\tau_{1},\tau_{2})>0
such that

\|g_{1}\odot_{CK}g_{2}({\bf x})\|_{A_{\rho,(1+\eta)(n+\delta+1)^{\rho}(\tau_{1}+\tau_{2})}}\leq C(n,\eta,\tau_{1},\tau_{2})\|g_{1}\|_{\rho,\tau_{1}}\|g_{2}\|_{\rho,\tau_{2}}.

Proof.

Choosing
s=(n+\delta)
in Lemma
3.12
, we have

\begin{split}&\|g_{1}\odot_{CK}g_{2}({\bf x})\|_{\rho,(1+\eta)(n+\delta+1)^{\rho}(\tau_{1}+\tau_{2})}\\
&\leq 2^{n}\sup_{{\bf x}\in\mathbb{R}^{n+1}}\left(\sum_{|{\bf m}|=0}^{\infty}\sum_{|{\bf\ell}|=0}^{\infty}\|V_{{\bf m}+{\bf\ell}}({\bf x})\|\|a_{m}\|\|b_{\ell}\|\exp(-(1+\eta)(n+\delta+1)^{\rho}(\tau_{1}+\tau_{2})\|{\bf x}\|^{\rho({\bf x})})\right)\\
&\leq C^{\prime}(n,\eta,\tau_{1},\tau_{2})\|g_{1}\|_{\rho,\tau_{1}}\|g_{2}\|_{\rho,\tau_{2}}\sum_{|{\bf m}|=0}^{\infty}\sum_{|\ell|=0}^{\infty}\|x\|^{|{\bf m}|+|\ell|}\frac{c(n,{\bf m})}{(n+\delta)^{|{\bf m}|}\|{\bf x}\|^{|{\bf m}|}}\frac{c(n,{\bf\ell})}{(n+\delta)^{|{\bf\ell}|}\|{\bf x}\|^{|\ell|}}\\
&\leq C(n,\eta,\tau_{1},\tau_{2})\|g_{1}\|_{\rho,\tau_{1}}\|g_{2}\|_{\rho,\tau_{2}},\end{split}

where in the last inequality we used the fact that

\sum_{|{\bf m}|=0}^{\infty}\frac{c(n,{\bf m})}{(n+\delta)^{|{\bf m}|}}<\infty

since

\limsup_{r\to\infty}\left(\frac{1}{(n+\delta)^{r}}\sum_{|m|=r}c(n,{\bf m})\right)^{\frac{1}{r}}=\frac{n}{(n+\delta)}<1.

∎

4.
Differential operators, representations of continuous homomorphisms

Before studying continuous homomorphisms between
A_{\rho_{i}}

(i=1,\,2)
and those between
A_{\rho_{i},+0}

(i=1,\,2)

\mathbb{R}_{n}[{\mathbf{x}}]:=\left\{\sum_{|{\mathbf{m}}|=0}^{q}V_{{\mathbf{m}}}({\mathbf{x}})a_{{\mathbf{m}}}:\,a_{{\mathbf{m}}}\in\mathbb{R}_{n}\quad\textrm{and}\quad q\in\mathbb{N}_{0}\right\}

to

\mathbb{R}_{n}[[{\mathbf{x}}]]:=\left\{\sum_{|{\mathbf{m}}|=0}^{\infty}V_{{\mathbf{m}}}({\mathbf{x}})a_{{\mathbf{m}}}:\,a_{{\mathbf{m}}}\in\mathbb{R}_{n}\right\}.

We define the space of formal right linear differential operators of infinite order with coefficients in
\mathbb{R}_{n}[[{\mathbf{x}}]]
by

\hat{D}:=\left\{P=\sum_{{\mathbf{m}}\in\mathbb{N}^{n}_{0}}u_{{\mathbf{m}}}({\mathbf{x}})\odot_{L}\partial^{\mathbf{m}}_{\mathbf{x}}:\,u_{\mathbf{m}}({\mathbf{x}})\in\mathbb{R}_{n}[[{\mathbf{x}}]]\right\}.

Note that
\hat{D}
is linearly isomorphic to
\prod_{{\mathbf{m}}\in\mathbb{N}^{n}}\mathbb{R}_{n}[[{\mathbf{x}}]]
, by the correspondence

\sum_{{\mathbf{m}}\in\mathbb{N}^{n}_{0}}u_{{\mathbf{m}}}({\mathbf{x}})\odot_{L}\partial^{\mathbf{m}}_{\mathbf{x}}\mapsto(u_{{\mathbf{m}}}({\mathbf{x}}))_{{\mathbf{m}}\in\mathbb{N}^{n}}.

Proposition 4.1
.

There are two left linear isomorphisms:

\hat{D}\leftrightarrow\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbb{R}_{n}}(\mathbb{R}_{n}[{\mathbf{x}}],\,\mathbb{R}_{n}[[{\mathbf{x}}]])\leftrightarrow\prod_{{\mathbf{m}}\in\mathbb{N}^{n}}\mathbb{R}_{n}[[{\mathbf{x}}]],

where the first and second mappings are given by

\begin{split}&\sum_{{\mathbf{m}}\in\mathbb{N}^{n}_{0}}u_{{\mathbf{m}}}({\mathbf{x}})\odot_{L}\partial^{\mathbf{m}}_{\mathbf{x}}\mapsto\left(\mathbb{R}_{n}[{\mathbf{x}}]\ni\sum_{{\mathbf{m}}}V_{{\mathbf{p}}}({\mathbf{x}})f_{{\mathbf{p}}}\mapsto\sum_{{\mathbf{m}}\leq{\mathbf{p}}}\frac{{\mathbf{p}}!}{({\mathbf{p}}-{\mathbf{m}})!}u_{{\mathbf{m}}}({\mathbf{x}})\odot_{L}V_{{\mathbf{p}}-{\mathbf{m}}}({\mathbf{x}})f_{{\mathbf{p}}}\right),\\
&F\mapsto(F(V_{{\mathbf{m}}}({\mathbf{x}}))/{\mathbf{m}}!)_{{\mathbf{m}}\in\mathbb{N}^{n}_{0}},\end{split}

respectively.

Proof.

We can easily see that both mappings are injective left linear mappings between vector spaces and that their composition is given by

\sum_{{\mathbf{m}}}u_{{\mathbf{m}}}({\mathbf{x}})\odot_{L}\partial^{\mathbf{m}}_{\mathbf{x}}\mapsto\left(\sum_{{\mathbf{m}}\leq{\mathbf{p}}}u_{{\mathbf{m}}}({\mathbf{x}})\odot_{L}\frac{V_{{\mathbf{p}}-{\mathbf{m}}}({\mathbf{x}})}{({\mathbf{p}}-{\mathbf{m}})!}\right)_{{\mathbf{p}}\in\mathbb{N}^{n}_{0}}.

Therefore, it suffices to show that the relations

(4.1)

b_{\mathbf{p}}({\mathbf{x}})=\sum_{{\mathbf{m}}\leq{\mathbf{p}}}u_{{\mathbf{m}}}({\mathbf{x}})\odot_{L}\frac{V_{{\mathbf{p}}-{\mathbf{m}}}({\mathbf{x}})}{({\mathbf{p}}-{\mathbf{m}})!},\quad{\mathbf{p}}\in\mathbb{N}^{n}_{0},

induce a bijection

\prod_{{\mathbf{m}}\in\mathbb{N}^{n}_{0}}\mathbb{R}_{n}[[{\mathbf{x}}]]\ni(u_{{\mathbf{m}}}({\mathbf{x}}))_{{\mathbf{m}}\in\mathbb{N}^{n}}\mapsto(b_{\mathbf{p}}({\mathbf{x}}))_{{\mathbf{p}}\in\mathbb{N}^{n}}\in\prod_{{\mathbf{p}}\in\mathbb{N}^{n}_{0}}\mathbb{R}_{n}[[{\mathbf{x}}]].

This follows from the fact that the relation (
4.1
) can be inverted as

(4.2)

u_{{\mathbf{m}}}({\mathbf{x}})=\sum_{{\mathbf{p}}\leq{\mathbf{m}}}b_{{\mathbf{p}}}({\mathbf{x}})\odot_{L}\frac{V_{{\mathbf{m}}-{\mathbf{p}}}(-{\mathbf{x}})}{({\mathbf{m}}-{\mathbf{p}})!}.

In fact, we can calculate the
{\mathbf{p}}
-element of the image of
(u_{{\mathbf{m}}}({\mathbf{x}}))_{{\mathbf{m}}\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{n}}
by composition of (
4.2
) and (
4.1
) as

\begin{split}\sum_{{\mathbf{s}}\leq{\mathbf{p}}}\sum_{{\mathbf{m}}\leq{\mathbf{s}}}\left(u_{{\mathbf{m}}}({\mathbf{x}})\odot_{L}\frac{V_{{\mathbf{s}}-{\mathbf{m}}}({\mathbf{x}})}{({\mathbf{s}}-{\mathbf{m}})!}\right)&\odot_{L}\frac{V_{{\mathbf{p}}-{\mathbf{s}}}(-{\mathbf{x}})}{({\mathbf{p}}-{\mathbf{s}})!}=\sum_{{\mathbf{s}}\leq{\mathbf{p}}}\sum_{{\mathbf{m}}\leq{\mathbf{s}}}u_{{\mathbf{m}}}({\mathbf{x}})\odot_{L}\left(\frac{V_{{\mathbf{s}}-{\mathbf{m}}}({\mathbf{x}})}{({\mathbf{s}}-{\mathbf{m}})!}\odot_{L}\frac{V_{{\mathbf{p}}-{\mathbf{s}}}(-{\mathbf{x}})}{({\mathbf{p}}-{\mathbf{s}})!}\right)\\
&=\sum_{{\mathbf{m}}\leq{\mathbf{p}}}u_{{\mathbf{m}}}({\mathbf{x}})\odot_{L}\left(\sum_{{\mathbf{m}}\leq{\mathbf{s}}\leq{\mathbf{p}}}\frac{V_{{\mathbf{s}}-{\mathbf{m}}}({\mathbf{x}})\odot_{L}V_{{\mathbf{p}}-{\mathbf{s}}}(-{\mathbf{x}})}{({\mathbf{s}}-{\mathbf{m}})!({\mathbf{p}}-{\mathbf{s}})!}\right)\\
&=\sum_{{\mathbf{m}}\leq{\mathbf{p}}}u_{{\mathbf{m}}}({\mathbf{x}})\odot_{L}\left(\sum_{{\mathbf{m}}\leq{\mathbf{s}}\leq{\mathbf{p}}}\frac{({\mathbf{p}}-{\mathbf{m}})!(-1)^{|{\mathbf{p}}-{\mathbf{s}}|}V_{{\mathbf{p}}-{\mathbf{m}}}({\mathbf{x}})}{({\mathbf{s}}-{\mathbf{m}})!({\mathbf{p}}-{\mathbf{s}})!({\mathbf{p}}-{\mathbf{m}})!}\right)\\
&=\sum_{{\mathbf{m}}\leq{\mathbf{p}}}u_{{\mathbf{m}}}({\mathbf{x}})\odot_{L}\left(\frac{(1-1)^{|{\mathbf{p}}-{\mathbf{m}}|}V_{{\mathbf{p}}-{\mathbf{m}}}({\mathbf{x}})}{({\mathbf{p}}-{\mathbf{m}})!}\right)=u_{{\mathbf{p}}}({\mathbf{x}}),\\
\end{split}

which implies that the composition is the identity. We can similarly show that the composition of (
4.1
) and (
4.2
) is the identity.
∎

Proof.

Again we can make a substitution of proximate orders as in Remark

(1)

\sigma
. Similar computations as in (
4.7
) yield

(4.12)

\begin{split}&\sum_{\mathbf{m}\in\mathbb{N}^{n}_{0}}\|u_{\mathbf{m}}\odot_{L}\partial^{\mathbf{m}}_{\mathbf{x}}f\|_{\rho_{2},(1+\eta)(n+\delta+1)^{\rho_{2}}(\sigma+k\tau)}\\
&\leq C(n,\eta,\sigma,k\tau)\sum_{\mathbf{m}\in\mathbb{N}^{n}_{0}}\|u_{\mathbf{m}}\|_{\rho_{2},\sigma}\|\partial^{\mathbf{m}}_{\mathbf{x}}f\|_{\rho_{2},k\tau}\\
&\leq C(n,\eta,\sigma,k\tau)\sum_{\mathbf{m}\in\mathbb{N}^{n}_{0}}\|u_{\mathbf{m}}\|_{\rho_{2},\sigma}\|\partial^{\mathbf{m}}_{\mathbf{x}}f\|_{\rho_{1},k\tau}\\
&\leq C^{\prime}(n,\eta,\sigma,k\tau)\sum_{\mathbf{m}\in\mathbb{N}^{n}_{0}}\frac{G_{\rho_{1},|\mathbf{m}|}}{\mathbf{m}!}\lambda_{\sigma}^{|\mathbf{m}|}\|f\|_{\rho_{1},\tau}\frac{\mathbf{m}!}{G_{\rho_{1},|\mathbf{m}|}}(2k(c(n,{\mathbf{m}}))^{\rho_{1}/|{\mathbf{m}}|}\tau)^{|\mathbf{m}|/\rho_{1}}\\
&=C^{\prime}(n,\eta,\sigma,k\tau)\|f\|_{\rho,\tau}\sum_{q\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}\sum_{|\mathbf{m}|=q}\lambda_{\sigma}^{q}(2k(c(n,{\mathbf{m}}))^{\rho_{1}/q}\tau)^{q/\rho_{1}}\\
&\leq C^{\prime}(n,\eta,\sigma,k\tau)\|f\|_{\rho,\tau}\sum_{q=0}^{+\infty}(q+1)^{n-1}(C(n)(2k\tau)^{1/\rho_{1}}\lambda_{\sigma})^{q},\end{split}

for any
\sigma,\,\tau>0,

f\in A_{\rho_{1},+0}
. Here
k=k(\rho_{1})
depends only on
\rho_{1}
, and
C^{\prime}(n,\eta,\sigma,k\tau)
is independent of
f
. Note that the last sum is finite if
C(n)(2k\tau)^{1/\rho_{1}}\lambda_{\sigma}<1
. In fact, we may first choose
\sigma
as

0<\sigma<\frac{\epsilon}{2(1+\eta)(n+\delta+1)^{\rho_{2}}},

which determines
\lambda_{\sigma}
, and then secondly choose
\tau>0
as

k\tau<\frac{\epsilon}{2(1+\eta)(n+\delta+1)^{\rho_{2}}},\quad C(n)(2k\tau)^{1/\rho_{1}}\lambda_{\sigma}<1.

Therefore, for any
\epsilon>0
, there exist
C^{\prime\prime}(\epsilon)>0
and
\tau(\epsilon)>0
such that

\|P(f)\|_{\rho_{2},\epsilon}\leq C^{\prime\prime}(\epsilon)\|f\|_{\rho_{1},\tau(\epsilon)},\quad\textrm{for $f\in A_{\rho,+0}$},

which implies that
P:A_{\rho_{1},+0}\to A_{\rho_{2},+0}
is continuous.

(2)
For a given continuous homomorphism
F:A_{\rho_{1},+0}\to A_{\rho_{2},+0}
, we construct

P=\sum_{{\mathbf{m}}}u_{\mathbf{m}}({\mathbf{x}})\odot_{L}\partial_{\mathbf{x}}^{\mathbf{m}}

via (
4.9
) in the same way as in the proof of Theorem
4.4

(2)
. Let us show the convergence of
P
in
A_{\rho_{2},+0}
together with the estimates. For any
\sigma^{\prime}>0
, by continuity of
F
there exists a
\tau_{1}>0
and a
C(\tau_{1})>0
such that

(4.13)

\|F(f)\|_{\rho_{2},\sigma^{\prime}/2}\leq C(\tau_{1})\|f\|_{\rho_{1},\tau_{1}},\quad\textrm{for $f\in A_{\rho_{1},+0}$.}

We fix
\sigma>0
. Take
\tau_{0}
with
0<\tau_{0}<\min\{\tau_{1},\sigma^{\prime}/2\}
where
\sigma^{\prime}=\frac{\sigma}{(1+\eta)(n+\delta+1)^{\rho_{1}}}
. Similarly to the proof of (
4.12
), we have

(4.14)

\begin{split}\|u_{{\mathbf{m}}}\|_{\rho_{2},\sigma}&\leq\|u_{{\mathbf{m}}}\|_{\rho_{2},(1+\eta)(n+\delta+1)^{\rho_{1}}\sigma^{\prime}}\\
&\leq C(\eta,n,\tau_{1},\tau^{\prime}(\tau_{1}))\sum_{{\mathbf{s}}\leq{\mathbf{m}}}\frac{\|V_{{\mathbf{m}}-{\mathbf{s}}}({\mathbf{x}})\|_{\rho_{2},\sigma^{\prime}/2}\|F(V_{{\mathbf{s}}}({\mathbf{x}}))\|_{\rho_{2},\sigma^{\prime}/2}}{({\mathbf{m}}-{\mathbf{s}})!{\mathbf{s}}!}\\
&\leq C(\eta,n,\tau_{1},\tau^{\prime}(\tau_{1}))\sum_{{\mathbf{s}}\leq{\mathbf{m}}}\frac{\|V_{{\mathbf{m}}-{\mathbf{s}}}({\mathbf{x}})\|_{\rho_{1},\sigma^{\prime}/2}C(\tau_{1})\|V_{{\mathbf{s}}}({\mathbf{x}})\|_{\rho_{1},\tau_{1}}}{({\mathbf{m}}-{\mathbf{s}})!{\mathbf{s}}!}\\
&\leq C(\eta,n,\tau_{1},\tau^{\prime}(\tau_{1}))\sum_{{\mathbf{s}}\leq{\mathbf{m}}}\frac{C(\tau_{0},\sigma^{\prime}/2)\tau_{0}^{-|{\mathbf{m}}-{\mathbf{s}}|/\rho_{1}}G_{\rho_{1},|{\mathbf{m}}-{\mathbf{s}}|}C(\tau_{1})C(\tau_{0},\tau_{1})\tau_{0}^{-|{\mathbf{s}}|/\rho_{1}}G_{\rho_{1},|{\mathbf{s}}|}}{({\mathbf{m}}-{\mathbf{s}})!{\mathbf{s}}!}\\
&\leq C(\eta,n,\tau_{1},\tau^{\prime}(\tau_{1}))C(\tau_{1})C(\tau_{0},\sigma^{\prime}/2)C(\tau_{0},\tau_{1})\sum_{{\mathbf{s}}\leq{\mathbf{m}}}\binom{{\mathbf{m}}}{{\mathbf{s}}}\frac{G_{\rho_{1},|{\mathbf{m}}|}}{{\mathbf{m}}!}\tau_{0}^{-|{\mathbf{m}}|/\rho_{1}}\\
&=C(\eta,n,\tau_{1},\tau^{\prime}(\tau_{1}))C(\tau_{1})C(\tau_{0},\sigma^{\prime}/2)C(\tau_{0},\tau_{1})\frac{G_{\rho_{1},|{\mathbf{m}}|}}{{\mathbf{m}}!}2^{|{\mathbf{m}}|}\tau_{0}^{-|{\mathbf{m}}|/\rho_{1}}.\end{split}

Here we used Lemma
3.13
at the first inequality, (
4.6
) and (

3.8
twice with

Therefore, by defining

we have

\|u_{\mathbf{m}}({\mathbf{x}})\|_{\rho_{2},\sigma}\leq C^{\prime}\frac{G_{\rho_{1},|{\mathbf{m}}|}}{{\mathbf{m}}!}\lambda^{|{\mathbf{m}}|}

for any
{\mathbf{m}}
. Since

The remaining thing is to show

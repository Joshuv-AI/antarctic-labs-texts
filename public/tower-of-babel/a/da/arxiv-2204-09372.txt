Constructions of Polyphase Golay Complementary Arrays
Cheng Du, Student Member, IEEE, Yi Jiang, Member, IEEE,

Abstract
Golay complementary matrices (GCM) have recently drawn considerable attentions owing to its potential applications in omnidirectional precoding. In this paper we generalize the GCM to multi-dimensional Golay complementary arrays (GCA) and propose new constructions of GCA pairs and GCA quads. These constructions are facilitated by introducing a set of identities over a commutative ring. We prove that a quaternary GCA pair is feasible if the product of the array sizes in all dimensions is a quaternary Golay number with an additional constraint on the factorization of the product. For the binary GCM quads, we conjecture that the feasible sizes are arbitrary, and verify for sizes within 78 × 78 78\times 78 and other less densely distributed sizes. For the quaternary GCM quads, all the positive integers within 1000 can be covered for the size in one dimension.

Preliminaries: Polynomials of Golay Complementary Arrays and Commutative Ring

The Golay complementary array (GCA) set has three types of equivalent definitions

For an

is defined as an array of size
(2s_{1}-1)\times\cdots\times(2s_{r}-1)

\displaystyle

\displaystyle\bf{\displaystyle R}_{A}\left[\delta_{1},\cdots,\delta_{r}\right]

(1)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{i_{1}}\cdots\sum_{i_{r}}{\bf A}\left[i_{1},\cdots,i_{r}\right]\overline{{\bf A}\left[i_{1}-\delta_{1},\cdots,i_{r}-\delta_{r}\right]},

where
i_{1},\cdots,i_{r}
and
\delta_{1},\cdots,\delta_{r}
are array indices,
{\bf A}\left[i_{1},\cdots,i_{r}\right]=0
if
i_{k}<0
or
i_{k}\geq s_{k}
for any

, and the overbar represents the complex conjugation. The array
{\bf A}
is indexed from
\left[0,\cdots,0\right]
, while the index
\left[0,\cdots,0\right]
corresponds to the center of the array
{\bf R}_{A}
.

The weight of an array
{\bf A}
is a nonnegative real number defined as
[
9
]

w({\bf A})=\sum_{i_{1}}\cdots\sum_{i_{r}}{\lvert{\bf A}\left[i_{1},\cdots,i_{r}\right]\rvert}^{2}.

(2)

Definition II.1
.

A set of arrays
\{{\bf A}_{1},{\bf A}_{2},\cdots,{\bf A}_{L}\}
with unimodular or zero entries is called a Golay complementary array (GCA) set if

\sum_{i=1}^{L}{\bf R}_{A_{i}}=\sum_{i=1}^{L}w({\bf A}_{i})\cdot\bf{\Delta},

(3)

where
{\bf{\Delta}}\in{\mathbb{C}}^{(2s_{1}-1)\times\cdots\times(2s_{r}-1)}
is an
r
-dimensional unit pulse function, i.e, the entries of
\bf{\Delta}
are zeros that the center
{\bf{\Delta}}[0,\cdots,0]=1
.

When the entries are unimodular, the weight is exactly
\prod_{i=1}^{r}s_{i}
. When zero occurs in an array, we say the array is weight-deficient. When the entries are the
N
-th unit roots, we refer it as a polyphase array. Specifically, a binary array is one with entries
\{1,-1\}
and a quaternary array is one with entries
\{1,-1,j,-j\}
where
j=\sqrt{-1}
. An array is trivial in the
i
-th dimension if
s_{i}=1
, and when it’s trivial in all dimensions, we say the array is trivial.

A GCA set degenerates into a Golay sequence set
[
5
]
or a Golay complementary matrix (GCM) set
[

when

{\bf A}_{1}=\begin{bmatrix}1&1&-1\\
-1&-j&-1\end{bmatrix},\quad{\bf A}_{2}=\begin{bmatrix}-1&-1&1\\
-1&-j&-1\end{bmatrix}.

(4)

And their autocorrelation functions are

\displaystyle{\bf R}_{A_{1}}

\displaystyle=\begin{bmatrix}-1&-1+j&j&-1-j&1\\
0&0&6&0&0\\
1&-1+j&-j&-1-j&-1\end{bmatrix},

(5)

\displaystyle{\bf R}_{A_{2}}

\displaystyle=\begin{bmatrix}1&1-j&-j&1+j&-1\\
0&0&6&0&0\\
-1&1-j&j&1+j&1\end{bmatrix},

whose summation is a two-dimensional delta-function.

The multi-variable polynomial
A({\bf z})
of an array

\displaystyle

\displaystyle A({\bf z}):=A\left(z_{1},\cdots,z_{r}\right)

\displaystyle\sum_{i_{1}}\cdots\sum_{i_{r}}{\bf A}\left[i_{1},\cdots,i_{r}\right]z_{1}^{i_{1}}\cdots z_{r}^{i_{r}},

In the context of signal processing, the polynomial of an array is the Z-transform of the signal. Then by the Wiener–Khinchin theorem, the Z-transform of the autocorrelation function of an array is equal to the power spectrum of the array, i.e.,

R_{A}({\bf z})=A({\bf z})\overline{A({\bf z}^{-1})},

(7)

where

\overline{A({\bf z}^{-1})}=\sum_{i_{1}}\cdots\sum_{i_{r}}\overline{{\bf A}\left[i_{1},\cdots,i_{r}\right]}z_{1}^{-i_{1}}\cdots z_{r}^{-i_{r}}.

(8)

Since the Z-transform of the

-function equals

]

Definition II.2
.

A set of arrays
\{{\bf A}_{1},{\bf A}_{2},\cdots,{\bf A}_{L}\}
with unimodular or zero entries is called a GCA set if

\sum_{i=1}^{L}A_{i}({\bf z})\overline{A_{i}({\bf z}^{-1})}=\sum_{i=1}^{L}w({\bf A}_{i}).

Nevertheless, the negative power of

in the expansion of

is defined as

\displaystyle

\displaystyle A^{*}({\bf z}):=A^{*}\left(z_{1},\cdots,z_{r}\right)=\sum_{i_{1}}\cdots\sum_{i_{r}}

\displaystyle\overline{{\bf A}\left[s_{1}-1-i_{1},\cdots,s_{r}-1-i_{r}\right]}z_{1}^{i_{1}}\cdots z_{r}^{i_{r}}.

where
*
denotes rotating an array

A^{*}({\bf z})=z_{1}^{s_{1}-1}\cdots z_{r}^{s_{r}-1}\overline{A({\bf z}^{-1})}.

Then by (

Definition II.3

A set of arrays
\{{\bf A}_{1},{\bf A}_{2},\cdots,{\bf A}_{L}\}

\sum_{i=1}^{L}A_{i}({\bf z})A_{i}^{*}({\bf z})=\sum_{i=1}^{L}w({\bf A}_{i})z_{1}^{s_{1}-1}\cdots z_{r}^{s_{r}-1}.

(12)

A more basic interpretation of Definition

Definition II.4
.

A commutative ring is a triple
({\mathbb{R}},+,\cdot)
, where
{\mathbb{R}}
is a non-vacuous set(do not confuse it with the field of real number),
+
and
\cdot
are two compositions for elements in
{\mathbb{R}}
, satisfying the following conditions:

1.

Closeness. The set
{\mathbb{R}}
is closed under the compositions
+
and
\cdot
, i.e.,
a+b\in{\mathbb{R}}
and
a\cdot b\in{\mathbb{R}}
hold for any
a,b\in{\mathbb{R}}
.

2.

Associativity. The compositions
+
and
\cdot
satisfy the associative law, i.e., for any
a,b,c\in{\mathbb{R}}
,
(a+b)+c=a+(b+c)
and
(a\cdot b)\cdot c=a\cdot(b\cdot c)
.

3.

Commutativity. The compositions
+
and
\cdot
satisfy the commutative law, i.e., for any
a,b\in{\mathbb{R}}
,
a+b=b+a
and
a\cdot b=b\cdot a
.

4.

Distributivity. The compositions
+
and
\cdot
satisfy the distributive law, i.e., for any
a,b,c\in{\mathbb{R}}
,
a\cdot(b+c)=a\cdot b+a\cdot c
and
(b+c)\cdot a=b\cdot a+c\cdot a
.

5.

Identity elements. There exist an element
0\in{\mathbb{R}}
and an element
1\in{\mathbb{R}}
, such that for any
a\in{\mathbb{R}}
,
a+0=0+a=a
and
a\cdot 1=1\cdot a=a
.

6.

Invertibility. Any element in
{\mathbb{R}}
is invertible in the sense of the composition
+
, i.e., for any
a\in{\mathbb{R}}
, there exists an element in
{\mathbb{R}}
denoted by
a^{-}
, such that
a+a^{-}=a^{-}+a=0
.

For ease of notation,
b+a^{-}
is written as
b-a
and
a\cdot b
is written as
ab
.

Property II.5
.

For any
a,b\in{\mathbb{R}}
, it holds that

ab^{-}=(ab)^{-}=a^{-}b.

(13)

Proof.

Noting that
a0=a(0+0)=a0+a0
, addition of
(a0)^{-}
gives
a0=0
. Then
ab^{-}+ab=a(b+b^{-})=a0=0
; similarly,
ab+ab^{-}=0
. Thus
ab^{-}=(ab)^{-}
. Similarly,
a^{-}b=(ab)^{-}
.
∎

Polynomial ring is an example of commutative ring. Let
{\mathbb{R}}=\{A({\bf z})\}
be the set of the multi-variable polynomials defined in (

and

are defined as the conventional polynomial summation and multiplication respectively. The identity elements

It is well-known that the multiplication of two polynomials amounts to the convolution of two arrays. Relating the compositions

and

of a commutative ring of complex arrays to the element-wise summation and the convolution of two arrays respectively, we see that the array ring and the polynomial ring are isomorphic

. Roughly speaking, they have the same structure or they are identical. This explains the equivalence of Definition

and Definition

An involutive automorphism of a commutative ring

\displaystyle=a^{*}+b^{*},\quad(a\cdot b)^{*}=a^{*}\cdot b^{*},

\displaystyle 1^{*}

This section presents four lemmas on the identities over a commutative ring, which will be the cornerstones of the construction of the polyphase GCA set developed later in the paper.

The following Lemma

Lemma 1
.

Given a commutative ring
{\mathbb{R}}
with an involutive automorphism
*
, and

e=ac+bd,\quad f=b^{*}c-a^{*}d,

ee^{*}+ff^{*}=\left(aa^{*}+bb^{*}\right)\left(cc^{*}+dd^{*}\right).

(18)

By the property of a commutative ring with an involutive automorphism, we have

\displaystyle ee^{*}+ff^{*}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(ac+bd)(ac+bd)^{*}+(b^{*}c-a^{*}d)(b^{*}c-a^{*}d)^{*}

\displaystyle\overset{({\cal A})}{=}

\displaystyle(ac+bd)(a^{*}c^{*}+b^{*}d^{*})+(b^{*}c-a^{*}d)(bc^{*}-ad^{*})

\displaystyle\overset{({\cal B})}{=}

\displaystyle ac(a^{*}c^{*})+ac(b^{*}d^{*})+bd(a^{*}c^{*})+bd(b^{*}d^{*})+

\displaystyle b^{*}c(bc^{*})+(b^{*}c)(ad^{*})^{-}+(a^{*}d)^{-}(bc^{*})+(a^{*}d)^{-}(ad^{*})^{-}

\displaystyle\overset{({\cal C})}{=}

\displaystyle aa^{*}cc^{*}+bb^{*}cc^{*}+aa^{*}dd^{*}+bb^{*}dd^{*}+

\displaystyle ab^{*}cd^{*}+a^{*}bc^{*}d-ab^{*}cd^{*}-a^{*}bc^{*}d

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(aa^{*}+bb^{*}\right)\left(cc^{*}+dd^{*}\right)

where
\overset{({\cal A})}{=}
follows from Definition

,
\overset{({\cal B})}{=}
follows from the distributivity,
\overset{({\cal C})}{=}

Corollary V.6
.

There exist quaternary GCM quads whose sizes in one dimension can cover all the positive integers within 1000.

VI
Conclusions

In the sight of four identities over a commutative ring, we propose some construction methods of the polyphase GCA pairs and the polyphase GCA quads.

The feasible sizes of the quaternary GCA pairs are
s_{1}\times\cdots\times s_{r}
, where
s_{1}\cdots s_{r}
is of form
2^{a+u}3^{b}5^{c}11^{d}13^{e}
,
a,b,c,d,e,u\geq 0
,
b+c+d+e\leq a+2u+1
,
u\leq c+e
, and each of
u
factors of
10,26
should not be factorized into different dimensions.

The feasible sizes of the binary GCM quads are of form
g_{b}^{(1)}m\times g_{b}^{(2)}n
or
g_{b}^{(1)}\times g_{b}^{(2)}mn
where
m,n\in\{2s+1|0\leq s\leq 38,or\ s=g_{b}^{(3)}\}\cup\{2(g_{b}^{(4)}+g_{b}^{(5)})\}
and
g_{b}^{(1)},\cdots,g_{b}^{(5)}
are binary Golay numbers. Specifically, all the sizes within
78\times 78
can be covered. We conjecture that the binary GCM quads exist for arbitrary sizes.

For the quaternary GCM quad of size
s_{1}\times s_{2}
, all the positive integers within 1000 can be covered for
s_{1}
or
s_{2}
. We also obtain the quaternary GCM quads of size
2s_{1}(t_{2}+t_{3})\times 2t_{1}(s_{2}+s_{3})
where
s_{1}\times s_{2}
,
s_{1}\times s_{3}
,
t_{2}\times t_{1}
and
t_{3}\times t_{1}
are the feasible sizes of quaternary GCM pairs.

Uniform C 1 , α C^{1,\alpha} -regularity for almost-minimizers of some nonlocal perturbations of the perimeter
M. Goldman, B. Merlet, M. Pegon

Abstract
. In this paper, we establish a C 1 , α C^{1,\alpha} -regularity theorem for almost-minimizers of the functional ℱ ε , γ = P − γ ​ P ε \mathcal{F}_{\varepsilon,\gamma}=P-\gamma P_{\varepsilon} , where γ ∈ ( 0 , 1 ) \gamma\in(0,1) and P ε P_{\varepsilon} is a nonlocal energy converging to the perimeter as ε \varepsilon vanishes. Our theorem provides a criterion for C 1 , α C^{1,\alpha} -regularity at a point of the boundary which is uniform as the parameter ε \varepsilon goes to 0 0 . Since the two terms in the energy are of the same order when ε \varepsilon is small, we are considering here much stronger nonlocal interactions than those considered in most related works. As a consequence of our regularity result, we obtain that, for ε \varepsilon small enough, volume-constrained minimizers of ℱ ε , γ \mathcal{F}_{\varepsilon,\gamma} are balls. For small ε \varepsilon , this minimization problem corresponds to the large mass regime for a Gamow-type problem where the nonlocal repulsive term is given by an integrable kernel G G with sufficiently fast decay at infinity.

Introduction

The aim of this paper is to complete the program started in

We are interested in the minimization problem

(1)

To state our

-regularity theorem and sketch its proof, we need to introduce the

For a set of finite perimeter

Definition 1.2

(Spherical excess)

For any set of finite perimeter

When

(which we will usually assume by translation invariance of the statements). We can now state our main

Theorem B

Assume that

1

5

Remark 1.3
.

We could generalize the definition

-minimizers to an open subset

We would obtain a uniform regularity result for

We now give the main steps of the proof of
B
or more precisely of the excess decay stated in
5.5
. While the overall strategy follows the classical regularity theory for minimizers of the perimeter
as pioneered by De Giorgi, Federer, Almgren and Allard (to name a few), the presence of a

competing nonlocal term
makes it quite delicate to adapt. In particular, the arguments
described below in Steps 2 & 4 are new. Indeed, as we go from large scales to
smaller scales the nonlocal nature of

is increasingly important. Even at scales
r\gg\varepsilon

the nonlocal contributions at distances

has to be precisely estimated. Besides, for the
Caccioppoli inequality we introduce a new strategy based on the slicing techniques of
[
28
,
26
]

and on the study of the new
shadow
functional
\Sha
.

Here we follow the order of the presentation of the classical theory of
[
27
]
and we use the same notation.

We first establish density upper and lower bounds, both for the volume and the perimeter.
This is a direct consequence of a weak quasi-minimality property for
(\Lambda,r_{0})
-minimizers

of

(see
[

or

]
). Indeed, we prove

that
E
satisfies

P(E;B_{r}(x))\leq CP(F;B_{r}(x))

for every
F
such that
E\triangle F\subset\subset B_{r}(x)
with

,

0<\Lambda r\leq 1-\gamma
and
C
depends only on
n
,

and
\gamma
.

We then prove the excess decay itself. We argue differently for scales
r\geq r_{+}\gg\varepsilon
and
r\leq r_{-}\ll\varepsilon
.

The decay of the excess in the situation

is done in

The proof goes through a Campanato iteration scheme which relies on the improvement of the excess by tilting proven in
5.3

the excess is small at some scale

then up to tilting and an error of the order of

for some

.
Let us stress again that as opposed to the usual applications of this idea, here the
error term gets
larger as

decreases
.

Roughly speaking, the idea of the proof of the tilt Lemma is that for
r\gg\varepsilon
,
P_{\varepsilon}

is close to the perimeter functional (at least for sufficiently smooth sets). Writing
{\mathcal{F}}_{\varepsilon,\gamma}=(1-\gamma)P+\gamma(P-P_{\varepsilon})
we may hope to reproduce the classical strategy for the excess decay by treating

as a negligible term.
This is actually quite delicate. Indeed, formal computations show that on smooth sets the energy
P-P_{\varepsilon}
penalizes curvature rather than volume (or even perimeter). The remainder
P-P_{\varepsilon}
is therefore a singular perturbation of the main term
(1-\gamma)P
.

We now sketch the four main steps of this strategy. We first need to introduce the cylindrical
excess and some more notation.
Let

(1.4)

\mathbf{C}(x,r,\nu)=x+\Big\{y+t\nu\penalty\ :\penalty\ y\in\nu^{\perp}\text{ with }\lvert y\rvert<r\text{ and }-r<t<r\Big\}

and

We now comment on the shadow functional

(1)

The obstruction condition reads

B^{\prime}\times(-\infty,-1/4)\ \subset\ F\ \subset\ B^{\prime}\times(-\infty,1/4).

(2)

For a Borel set
\Sigma\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
,
\Sha(\Sigma)
is the average over all lighting directions of the area of the shadow of
\Sigma
, namely,

\Sha(\Sigma):=\dfrac{1}{2\omega_{n-1}}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}{\mathcal{H}}^{n-1}\big(\pi_{\sigma^{\perp}}\Sigma\big)\mathop{}\mathopen{}\mathrm{d}\mathcal{H}^{n-1}_{\sigma},

where
\pi_{\sigma^{\perp}}
denotes the orthogonal projection on the hyperplane
\sigma^{\perp}
. By Fubini, we may equivalently integrate over all possible lines
L
and count one if
L
intersects
\Sigma
and 0 in the other cases:

\Sha(\Sigma)=\dfrac{1}{2\omega_{n-1}}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}\int_{\sigma^{\perp}}\chi\left((x+{\mathbb{R}}\sigma)\cap\Sigma\right)\,\mathrm{d}{\mathcal{H}}^{n-1}_{x}\,\mathop{}\mathopen{}\mathrm{d}\mathcal{H}^{n-1}_{\sigma},

where
\chi(\emptyset)=0
and
\chi(A)=1
for nonempty subsets
A\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
.

The prefactor is chosen so that
\Sha(P)={\mathcal{H}}^{n-1}(P)
for Borel sets
P
lying in a

(n-1)
-affine subspace of
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
. Notice that substituting the counting measure
{\mathcal{H}}^{0}
for

\chi
, we obtain the integral geometric measure
\mathcal{I}^{n-1}
, hence,
\Sha(\Sigma)
is not
larger (and in general smaller) than
\mathcal{I}^{n-1}(\Sigma)
.

We prove in
4.3
that
B^{\prime}\times(-\infty,0)
minimizes
\Sha(\partial^{*}F\cap[B^{\prime}\times{\mathbb{R}}])
among obstructing sets
F
. However, substituting
\Sha
for
\mathcal{H}^{n-1}
in the Plateau problem, the solutions (if any) are in general not minimal surfaces anymore. The obvious reason is that, denoting

\widetilde{\Sigma}:=\{x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}:(x+{\mathbb{R}}\sigma)\cap\Sigma\neq\emptyset\text{ for every }\sigma\in\mathbb{S}^{n-1}\},

there holds
\Sha(\widetilde{\Sigma})=\Sha(\Sigma)
but for nonplanar hypersurfaces,
{\mathcal{H}}^{n}(\widetilde{\Sigma})>0
. Considering for instance the Steiner problem associated with the set of vertices
V
of some convex polygon
P\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
, the minimizers of
\Sha
among closed connected sets containing
V
are exactly the closed connected sets
\Sigma
such that

. This includes sets with Hausdorff dimensions ranging from 1 to 2. The situation may be more subtle in higher dimension, as minimizers of
\Sha
among sets spanning a given boundary

may not contain a solution of the corresponding Plateau problem. For instance, for
n=3
, if
\Gamma=\mathbb{S}^{1}\times\{-t,t\}
for some

then for

small enough the minimizer of

is the (empty) cylinder
\mathbb{S}^{1}\times(-t,t)
.

Motivation and related results

As already alluded to, under the additional hypothesis that
G\in L^{1}
and after rescaling,

{\mathcal{F}}_{\varepsilon,\gamma}
is equivalent to the generalized Gamow functionals (see
[
5
]
),

(1.6)

\min\penalty\ \Big\{P(E)+\gamma\int_{E\times E}G(x-y)\mathop{}\mathopen{}\mathrm{d}x\mathop{}\mathopen{}\mathrm{d}y\penalty\ :\penalty\ \lvert E\rvert=m\Big\}.

Besides the case of compactly supported kernels studied in
[
31
]
and for which existence of minimizers holds for any
m
, the main example studied in the literature is the case of Riesz
interaction energies,
G(z)=|z|^{-(n-\alpha)}
with
\alpha\in(0,n)
. The case
n=3
and
\alpha=2
corresponding to Gamow’s
liquid drop model for the atomic nucleus, see
[
7
]
for a short overview on this problem.
In this problem it has been shown in
[
22
,
14
]
that, for small
m
, minimizers
are balls while for large
m
there is non-existence of minimizers under the assumption that
\alpha\in[n-2,n)
, see
[
22
,
25
,
15
]
. The question of the existence of minimizers in the case
\alpha\in(0,n-2)
is still open.
The proof of the rigidity of balls for small
m
in
[
22
,
14
]
(see also

[
5
]
for the case of quite general kernels
G
) is of the same spirit as for

A
and goes through a Selection Principle along the lines of
[
8
]
.
Notice however that in that case one can directly rely on the classical regularity theory for quasi-minimizers of the perimeter, see
Equation

2.4
.
Let us point out that in a related direction, it has been shown in
[
1
]
that if we
replace the Euclidean perimeter in
Equation

1.6
by a weighted perimeter
P_{a}(E)\coloneqq\int_{\partial^{*}E}a(x)\mathop{}\mathopen{}\mathrm{d}\mathcal{H}_{x}^{n-1}
, with a weight
a
growing fast enough at infinity, then balls
are the unique minimizers for large
m
.

Recently, there has been a growing interest for related nonlocal isoperimetric problems which do not fall within the standard regularity theory for perimeter almost-minimizers.
A first example comes from a variational model for charged liquid drops where the kernel is still given by the Riesz interaction kernel but
now the charge is not assumed to be uniformly distributed on
E
. This leads in general to much more singular interactions, see
[
19
]
.
However, introducing either an additional penalization of the charge as in
[
12
]

or restricting to
\alpha\leq 1
as in
[
21
]
, it is possible to obtain an epsilon-regularity theorem in the same spirit as the one for minimal surfaces.
A major difference between our setting and
[
12
,
21
]
is that in the charged liquid drop model, it is possible to show that for smooth
sets the nonlocal term is actually a volume term (while for us it penalizes curvature). Another
example studied in
[
29
]
and which is strongly related to
Equation

P

,
is formally
Equation

1.6
with the Riesz kernel but for
n=2
and
\alpha=-1
. This is
motivated by dipolar repulsion. In order to make the model meaningful, a small-scale cut-off has to be introduced (otherwise the energy is always infinite).
This cut-off plays a similar role in that model as our parameter
\varepsilon
. In particular, just like in our case, in the limit of vanishing cut-off length and after proper renormalization,
the nonlocal term converges to the perimeter. Among many other things, it is shown in
[
29
]
that as in
A
, in the sub-critical regime (in our language
\gamma<1
)
minimizers are disks for small but finite cut-off lengths. Just like in our problem, the main issue
in
[
29
]
is to obtain regularity estimates which hold at a macroscopic scale.
However, our strategy to obtain these estimates is very different from
[
29
]
. Indeed, while we propagate regularity from the macroscopic scale down to the microscopic scale (in the form of excess decay),

[
29
]
relies on the Euler–Lagrange equation to bootstrap the regularity from the microscopic scale up to the macroscopic scale.
Let us point out that on the one hand, the proof in
[
29
]
does not seem to be easily adapted to dimensions higher than two and
that on the other hand the logarithmic scaling in
[
29
]
allows to directly pass (in our notation) from a scale
r\gg\varepsilon
to a scale
r\ll\varepsilon
.
We refer to
[
6
,
24
,
11
]
for other related models where however rigidity of the ball has not been investigated.

In conclusion, besides
[
29
]
which concerns a two dimensional problem,
B
is the first uniform regularity theorem for quasi-minimizers of a functional built with two competing local/nonlocal perimeters which remain asymptotically of the same order.

One may compare our regularity result with the one of
[
4
]
. Therein, the authors establish a uniform
C^{1,\alpha}
-regularity result for local
minimizers of the
s
-perimeter which is uniform in
s
as
s\to 1^{-}
.
However, due to the lack of a competing term, the problem and its analysis are rather different from
the ones of the present work.

The following proposition is a consequence of

\mathit{P}_{K}(E)\coloneqq\int_{{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}\times{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}}\lvert{\mathbf{1}}_{E}(x)-{\mathbf{1}}_{E}(y)\rvert K(x-y)\mathop{}\mathopen{}\mathrm{d}x\mathop{}\mathopen{}\mathrm{d}y.

Proposition 2.1

(Upper bound)
.

Assume that

Then, for every set of finite perimeter

\mathit{P}_{K}(E)\leq\mathbb{K}_{1,n}I_{K}^{1}P(E).

Lemma 2.2

(Continuity)

Assume that

and

Let

Setting for

t\mapsto{\mathcal{F}}_{\varepsilon,\gamma}(f_{t}(E))
is differentiable at
t=0
with
\delta{\mathcal{F}}_{\varepsilon,\gamma}(E)[T]\coloneqq\left[\mathop{}\mathopen{}\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}{\mathcal{F}}_{\varepsilon,\gamma}(f_{t}(E))\right]_{|t=0}
given by

\displaystyle\delta{\mathcal{F}}_{\varepsilon,\gamma}(E)[T]=\int_{\partial^{*}E}\dvg_{E}T\mathop{}\mathopen{}\mathrm{d}\mathcal{H}^{n-1}

\displaystyle-2\gamma\left(\int_{E\times E^{\mathrm{c}}}\dvg T(x)G_{\varepsilon}(x-y)\mathop{}\mathopen{}\mathrm{d}x\mathop{}\mathopen{}\mathrm{d}y+\int_{\partial^{*}E}\int_{E}G_{\varepsilon}(x-y)\left(T(x)-T(y)\right)\cdot\nu_{E}(y)\mathop{}\mathopen{}\mathrm{d}x\mathop{}\mathopen{}\mathrm{d}\mathcal{H}^{n-1}_{y}\right)

where
\dvg_{E}T
is the boundary divergence of
T
on
E
, defined by

\dvg_{E}T(x)\coloneqq\dvg T(x)-\nu_{E}(x)\cdot\nabla T(x)\nu_{E}(x),\quad\forall x\in\partial^{*}E.

Proof.

Since the computation of the first variation of the perimeter is standard, see
e.g.
[
27
, Theorem 17.5]
, we only compute the first variation of
P_{\varepsilon}
. We show that
(recall the notation
T_{x}=T(x)
),

\left[\mathop{}\mathopen{}\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\mathit{P}_{\varepsilon}(f_{t}(E))\right]_{|t=0}=2\int_{E\times E^{\mathrm{c}}}\dvg T(x)G_{\varepsilon}(x-y)\mathop{}\mathopen{}\mathrm{d}x\mathop{}\mathopen{}\mathrm{d}y\\
\qquad+2\int_{\partial^{*}E}\int_{E}G_{\varepsilon}(x-y)\left(T_{x}-T_{y}\right)\cdot\nu_{E}(y)\mathop{}\mathopen{}\mathrm{d}x\mathop{}\mathopen{}\mathrm{d}\mathcal{H}^{n-1}_{y}.

Notice that using
Equation

2.2
and the fact that
T
is Lipschitz continuous,
Items

1
and
2
imply that both terms on the right-hand side are well-defined. Since
\varepsilon
does not play any role we may assume without loss of generality that
\varepsilon=1
. We set
F_{G}(t)\coloneqq\frac{1}{2}P_{1}(f_{t}(E))
. Note that by choosing
t_{0}\leq 1/\lVert\nabla T\rVert_{L^{\infty}}
,
f_{t}
is a diffeomorphism of
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
for
every
t
such that
\lvert t\rvert<t_{0}
.
In particular
f_{t}(E)
is a set of finite perimeter (see e.g.
[
27
, Proposition 17.1]
).
Thus,
F_{G}(t)
is well-defined for every
t\in(-t_{0},t_{0})
. We then set (for the moment this is just a notation)

F^{\prime}_{G}(0)\coloneqq\int_{E\times E^{\mathrm{c}}}\dvg T(x)G(x-y)\mathop{}\mathopen{}\mathrm{d}x\mathop{}\mathopen{}\mathrm{d}y+\int_{\partial^{*}E}\int_{E}G(x-y)\left(T_{x}-T_{y}\right)\cdot\nu_{E}(y)\mathop{}\mathopen{}\mathrm{d}x\mathop{}\mathopen{}\mathrm{d}\mathcal{H}^{n-1}_{y}.

We claim that as
t\to 0
,

(2.9)

F_{G}(t)-F_{G}(0)-tF_{G}^{\prime}(0)=o(t).

This would show that
F_{G}
is differentiable in
0
with derivative
F^{\prime}_{G}(0)
, concluding the proof.
Changing variables, for any
t
small enough we have

F_{G}(t)=\int_{E\times E^{c}}G(f_{t}(x)-f_{t}(y))\det Df_{t}(x)\,\det Df_{t}(y)\mathop{}\mathopen{}\mathrm{d}x\mathop{}\mathopen{}\mathrm{d}y.

Since
\det Df_{t}(x)=1+t\dvg T(x)+O(t^{2})
, we find

F_{G}(t)=\int_{E\times E^{c}}G(f_{t}(x)-f_{t}(y))(1+t\dvg T(x)+t\dvg T(y)+O(t^{2}))\mathop{}\mathopen{}\mathrm{d}x\mathop{}\mathopen{}\mathrm{d}y.

Notice that by the reverse change of variables and
Equation

2.2
,

\int_{E\times E^{c}}G(f_{t}(x)-f_{t}(y))\mathop{}\mathopen{}\mathrm{d}x\mathop{}\mathopen{}\mathrm{d}y\leq C\int_{f_{t}(E)\times f_{t}(E)^{c}}G(x-y)\mathop{}\mathopen{}\mathrm{d}x\mathop{}\mathopen{}\mathrm{d}y\leq CP(f_{t}(E))\leq CP(E).

Therefore

F_{G}(t)=\int_{E\times E^{c}}G(f_{t}(x)-f_{t}(y))(1+t\dvg T(x)+t\dvg T(y))\mathop{}\mathopen{}\mathrm{d}x\mathop{}\mathopen{}\mathrm{d}y+O(t^{2}).

Now, using that

G(f_{t}(x)-f_{t}(y))-G(x-y)=t\int_{0}^{1}\nabla G(f_{st}(x)-f_{st}(y))\cdot(T_{x}-T_{y})\mathop{}\mathopen{}\mathrm{d}s

and the Lipschitz continuity of
T
, we have

\displaystyle\left|\int_{E\times E^{c}}G(f_{t}(x)-f_{t}(y))\dvg T(x)\mathop{}\mathopen{}\mathrm{d}x\mathop{}\mathopen{}\mathrm{d}y-\int_{E\times E^{c}}G(x-y)\dvg T(x)\mathop{}\mathopen{}\mathrm{d}x\mathop{}\mathopen{}\mathrm{d}y\right|

\displaystyle\leq C\lvert t\rvert\int_{0}^{1}\int_{E\times E^{c}}|\nabla G(f_{st}(x)-f_{st}(y))||x-y|\mathop{}\mathopen{}\mathrm{d}x\mathop{}\mathopen{}\mathrm{d}y\mathop{}\mathopen{}\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle\leq C\lvert t\rvert\int_{0}^{1}\int_{E\times E^{c}}|\nabla G(f_{st}(x)-f_{st}(y))||f_{st}(x)-f_{st}(y)|\mathop{}\mathopen{}\mathrm{d}x\mathop{}\mathopen{}\mathrm{d}y\mathop{}\mathopen{}\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle\leq C\lvert t\rvert\int_{0}^{1}\int_{f_{st}(E)\times(f_{st}(E))^{c}}|\nabla G(x-y)||x-y|\mathop{}\mathopen{}\mathrm{d}x\mathop{}\mathopen{}\mathrm{d}y\mathop{}\mathopen{}\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle\leq CI_{|\nabla G|}^{2}\lvert t\rvert\int_{0}^{1}P(f_{st}(E))\mathop{}\mathopen{}\mathrm{d}s\leq CI_{|\nabla G|}^{2}\lvert t\rvert,

where we used again
Equation

2.1
but for the kernel
K=|\cdot||\nabla G|
. Since the same holds with
\dvg T(x)
replaced by
\dvg T(y)
, in order to prove

Equation

2.9
it is thus enough to show

(2.10)

\int_{E\times E^{c}}G(f_{t}(x)-f_{t}(y))\mathop{}\mathopen{}\mathrm{d}x\mathop{}\mathopen{}\mathrm{d}y-\int_{E\times E^{c}}G(x-y)\mathop{}\mathopen{}\mathrm{d}x\mathop{}\mathopen{}\mathrm{d}y\\
+t\left(\int_{E\times E^{c}}G(x-y)\dvg T(y)\mathop{}\mathopen{}\mathrm{d}x\mathop{}\mathopen{}\mathrm{d}y-\int_{\partial^{*}E}\int_{E}G(x-y)\left(T_{x}-T_{y}\right)\cdot\nu_{E}(y)\mathop{}\mathopen{}\mathrm{d}x\mathop{}\mathopen{}\mathrm{d}\mathcal{H}^{n-1}_{y}\right)=o(t).

Writing as above that

\int_{E\times E^{c}}G(f_{t}(x)-f_{t}(y))\mathop{}\mathopen{}\mathrm{d}x\mathop{}\mathopen{}\mathrm{d}y-\int_{E\times E^{c}}G(x-y)\mathop{}\mathopen{}\mathrm{d}x\mathop{}\mathopen{}\mathrm{d}y\\
=t\int_{0}^{1}\int_{E\times E^{c}}\nabla G(f_{st}(x)-f_{st}(y))\cdot(T_{x}-T_{y})\mathop{}\mathopen{}\mathrm{d}x\mathop{}\mathopen{}\mathrm{d}y\mathop{}\mathopen{}\mathrm{d}s

we reduce it further to the proof of

(2.11)

\lim_{t\to 0}\int_{0}^{1}\int_{E\times E^{c}}\nabla G(f_{st}(x)-f_{st}(y))\cdot(T_{x}-T_{y})\mathop{}\mathopen{}\mathrm{d}x\mathop{}\mathopen{}\mathrm{d}y\mathop{}\mathopen{}\mathrm{d}s=\int_{E\times E^{c}}\nabla G(x-y)\cdot(T_{x}-T_{y})\mathop{}\mathopen{}\mathrm{d}x\mathop{}\mathopen{}\mathrm{d}y

together with the integration by parts formula

(2.12)

\int_{E\times E^{c}}\nabla G(x-y)\cdot(T_{x}-T_{y})+G(x-y)\dvg T(y)\mathop{}\mathopen{}\mathrm{d}x\mathop{}\mathopen{}\mathrm{d}y\\
=\int_{\partial^{*}E}\int_{E}G(x-y)\left(T_{x}-T_{y}\right)\cdot\nu_{E}(y)\mathop{}\mathopen{}\mathrm{d}x\mathop{}\mathopen{}\mathrm{d}\mathcal{H}^{n-1}_{y}.

Assuming
Equations

2.11
and
2.12
, we see that
Equation

2.10
holds true,
which proves the lemma.
Notice that these identities would be easy to prove if
G
were a smooth kernel with compact support. However,
since our assumptions on
G
seem too weak to prove these directly we argue by approximation. Let
G_{k}
be a sequence of smooth compactly supported radial kernels with

\lim_{k\to\infty}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}|z||(G-G_{k})(z)|\mathop{}\mathopen{}\mathrm{d}z=0\qquad\qquad\textrm{and}\qquad\qquad\lim_{k\to\infty}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}|z|^{2}|\nabla(G-G_{k})(z)|\mathop{}\mathopen{}\mathrm{d}z=0.

Since we assumed that
I^{1}_{G}+I^{2}_{|\nabla G|}<\infty
it is not difficult to construct such a sequence. We start with
Equation

2.11
. For every fixed
s\in[0,1]
, we have

\displaystyle\left|\int_{E\times E^{c}}\nabla G(f_{st}(x)-f_{st}(y))\cdot(T_{x}-T_{y})\mathop{}\mathopen{}\mathrm{d}x\mathop{}\mathopen{}\mathrm{d}y-\int_{E\times E^{c}}\nabla G_{k}(f_{st}(x)-f_{st}(y))\cdot(T_{x}-T_{y})\mathop{}\mathopen{}\mathrm{d}x\mathop{}\mathopen{}\mathrm{d}y\right|

\displaystyle\leq C\int_{E\times E^{c}}|(\nabla G-\nabla G_{k})(f_{st}(x)-f_{st}(y))||x-y|\mathop{}\mathopen{}\mathrm{d}x\mathop{}\mathopen{}\mathrm{d}y

\displaystyle\leq C\int_{f_{st}(E)\times f_{st}(E)^{c}}|\nabla(G-G_{k})(x-y)||x-y|\mathop{}\mathopen{}\mathrm{d}x\mathop{}\mathopen{}\mathrm{d}y

\displaystyle\leq C\left(\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}|z|^{2}|\nabla(G-G_{k})(z)|\mathop{}\mathopen{}\mathrm{d}z\right)P(f_{st}(E))

\displaystyle\leq C\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}|z|^{2}|\nabla(G-G_{k})(z)|\mathop{}\mathopen{}\mathrm{d}z,

where we used
Equation

2.1
with
K=|\cdot||\nabla(G-G_{k})|
(which is radially symmetric). Integrating in
s
and using a simple diagonal argument, this proves
Equation

2.11
. We now turn to
Equation

2.12
. Since

-\dvg_{y}(G(x-y)(T_{x}-T_{y}))=\nabla G(x-y)\cdot(T_{x}-T_{y})+G(x-y)\dvg T(y),

the integration by parts formula
Equation

2.12
holds with
G
replaced by
G_{k}
. By the previous computations it is therefore enough to observe that on the one hand

\left|\int_{E\times E^{c}}G(x-y)\dvg T(y)\mathop{}\mathopen{}\mathrm{d}x\mathop{}\mathopen{}\mathrm{d}y-\int_{E\times E^{c}}G_{k}(x-y)\dvg T(y)\mathop{}\mathopen{}\mathrm{d}x\mathop{}\mathopen{}\mathrm{d}y\right|\leq CP(E)\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}|z||(G-G_{k})(z)|\mathop{}\mathopen{}\mathrm{d}z

and on the other hand,

\left|\int_{\partial^{*}E}\int_{E}G(x-y)\left(T_{x}-T_{y}\right)\cdot\nu_{E}(y)\mathop{}\mathopen{}\mathrm{d}x\mathop{}\mathopen{}\mathrm{d}\mathcal{H}^{n-1}_{y}-\int_{\partial^{*}E}\int_{E}G_{k}(x-y)\left(T_{x}-T_{y}\right)\cdot\nu_{E}(y)\mathop{}\mathopen{}\mathrm{d}x\mathop{}\mathopen{}\mathrm{d}\mathcal{H}^{n-1}_{y}\right|\\
\leq C\int_{\partial^{*}E}\int_{E}|x-y||(G-G_{k})(x-y)|\mathop{}\mathopen{}\mathrm{d}x\mathop{}\mathopen{}\mathrm{d}\mathcal{H}^{n-1}_{y}\leq CP(E)\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}|z||(G-G_{k})(z)|\mathop{}\mathopen{}\mathrm{d}z.

This proves the lemma.
∎

2.2.
Perimeter quasi-minimizing properties of minimizers

We recall from
[
30
, (4.2)]
that using
Equation

2.2
it follows that if
E

satisfies
{\mathcal{F}}_{\varepsilon,\gamma}(E)\leq{\mathcal{F}}_{\varepsilon,\gamma}(B_{1})
, then

(2.13)

P(E)\leq P(B_{1})+\frac{\gamma}{1-\gamma}\left(P(B_{1})-P_{\varepsilon}(B_{1})\right)\leq\frac{1}{1-\gamma}P(B_{1}).

We now use the scaling properties given in
2.4
to prove the equivalence between

Equation

P

and the unconstrained minimization problem

(
\mathcal{P}^{\prime}
)

\min\penalty\ \Big\{{\mathcal{F}}_{\varepsilon,\gamma}(E)+\Lambda\big|\lvert E\rvert-\lvert B_{1}\rvert\big|\penalty\ :\penalty\ E\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{n}\text{
measurable}\Big\}

if
\Lambda
is large enough, not depending on
\varepsilon
. As a consequence, minimizers of

Equation

P

are
(\Lambda,r_{0})
-minimizers of
{\mathcal{F}}_{\varepsilon,\gamma}
.

Proposition 2.6
.

Assume that
G
satisfies
Items

1
and
2
and let
\gamma\in(0,1)
. There exists
C=C(n)>0
such that for every

\gamma\in(0,1)
,
\varepsilon>0
and
\Lambda\geq C/(1-\gamma)
, problems
Equation

P

and

Equation

P
′

are equivalent, in the sense that
Equation

P
′

admits a minimizer if and only if
Equation

P

does, and their minimizers coincide. In particular, any minimizer of
Equation

P

is a

(\Lambda,r_{0})
-minimizer of
{\mathcal{F}}_{\varepsilon,\gamma}
for any
\Lambda\geq C/(1-\gamma)
and any

r_{0}>0
.

Proof.

Let us set

\Lambda_{0}\coloneqq\frac{2}{1-\gamma}\left(1+\Big(n+\frac{2}{\mathbb{K}_{1,n}}\Big)\right)\frac{P(B_{1})}{\lvert B_{1}\rvert}.

Since

\inf_{|E|=|B_{1}|}{\mathcal{F}}_{\varepsilon,\gamma}(E)\geq\inf_{E}{\mathcal{F}}_{\varepsilon,\gamma,\Lambda}(E),

it is enough to prove that for
\Lambda\geq\Lambda_{0}
, the converse inequality holds and that any
set minimizing
{\mathcal{F}}_{\varepsilon,\gamma,\Lambda}
has measure
\omega_{n}
.
This in turn is equivalent to the claim that if
E
is such that

\lvert E\rvert\neq\omega_{n}\qquad\qquad\text{ and }\qquad\qquad{\mathcal{F}}_{\varepsilon,\gamma,\Lambda}(E)\leq\inf_{|E|=|B_{1}|}{\mathcal{F}}_{\varepsilon,\gamma}(E)

then
\Lambda<\Lambda_{0}
. Let
E
be such a set. Recall that
E
satisfies
Equation

2.13
.
In the case
\lvert E\rvert\leqslant\lvert B_{1}\rvert/2
, testing the minimality of
E
against
B_{1}
, we readily obtain

\frac{\Lambda\omega_{n}}{2}\leqslant\frac{P(B_{1})}{1-\gamma},

so that
\Lambda<\Lambda_{0}
.

Now, in the case
\lvert E\rvert>\lvert B_{1}\rvert/2
, let
\lambda>0
be such that
|\lambda E|=|B_{1}|
. In particular,
\lambda^{n}>1/2
.
We then have

P(E)-\gamma P_{\varepsilon}(E)+\Lambda||E|-|B_{1}||={\mathcal{F}}_{\varepsilon,\gamma,\Lambda}(E)\leq{\mathcal{F}}_{\varepsilon,\gamma}(\lambda E)=\lambda^{n-1}P(E)-\gamma P_{\varepsilon}(\lambda E).

Reorganizing terms we find

(2.14)

\Lambda\omega_{n}\lambda^{-n}|1-\lambda^{n}|\leq(\lambda^{n-1}-1)P(E)+\gamma|P_{\varepsilon}(E)-P_{\varepsilon}(\lambda E)|.

By
2.4
and
Equation

2.2
, for any

t>0
we have

\left\lvert\mathop{}\mathopen{}\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\left[P_{\varepsilon}(tE)\right]\right\rvert\leq\frac{1}{t}\left(nP_{\varepsilon}(tE)+\lvert\widetilde{P}_{G_{\varepsilon}}(tE)\rvert\right)\leq\frac{1}{t}\left(nP(tE)+2P(tE)I_{G}^{1}\right)\leq t^{n-2}\left(n+\frac{2}{\mathbb{K}_{1,n}}\right),

thus

\lvert P_{\varepsilon}(E)-P_{\varepsilon}(\lambda E)\rvert\leq\left\lvert\int_{\lambda}^{1}\mathop{}\mathopen{}\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\left[P_{\varepsilon}(tE)\right]\mathop{}\mathopen{}\mathrm{d}t\right\rvert\leq C_{1}\lvert\lambda^{n-1}-1\rvert P(E),

where
C_{1}\coloneqq\left(n+\frac{2}{\mathbb{K}_{1,n}}\right)
. Inserting this into
Equation

2.14
and using
Equation

2.13
, this leads to

\Lambda\omega_{n}\lambda^{-n}|1-\lambda^{n}|\leq\frac{1}{1-\gamma}\left(1+C_{1}\gamma\right)P(B_{1})|\lambda^{n-1}-1|.

Since
1/2<\lambda^{-n}
and
|\lambda^{n-1}-1|<|\lambda^{n}-1|
, we conclude that
\Lambda<\Lambda_{0}
.
∎

We recall the following elementary scaling properties of the energy which we will heavily use in the paper.

Proposition 2.7
.

For any set of finite perimeter
E
, any
\varepsilon>0
and any
r>0
we have

{\mathcal{F}}_{\varepsilon,\gamma}(E)=r^{n-1}{\mathcal{F}}_{\varepsilon/r,\gamma}(E/r).

In particular
E
is a
(\Lambda,r_{0})
-minimizer of
{\mathcal{F}}_{\varepsilon,\gamma}
if and only if
E/r
is a

(\Lambda r,\frac{r_{0}}{r})
-minimizer of
{\mathcal{F}}_{\varepsilon/r,\gamma}
.

We now prove that
(\Lambda,r_{0})
-minimizers of
{\mathcal{F}}_{\varepsilon,\gamma}
are quasi-minimizers of the
perimeter and thus have density bounds which are uniform in
\varepsilon
.

Proposition 2.8

(Weak quasi-minimality)
.

Assume that
G
satisfies
Items

1
and
2
and let
\gamma\in(0,1)
,
\varepsilon>0
,
\Lambda>0

and
r_{0}>0
with
\Lambda r_{0}\leq 1-\gamma
.
Then, for any
(\Lambda,r_{0})
-minimizer
E
of
{\mathcal{F}}_{\varepsilon,\gamma}
and every set
F
with

E\triangle F\subset\subset B_{r}(x)
with
0<r\leq r_{0}
we have

(2.15)

P(E;B_{r}(x))\leq\frac{4}{1-\gamma}P(F;B_{r}(x)).

As a consequence, there exists
C=C(n)>0
such that for every
x\in\partial E
and every
0<r\leq r_{0}
,

(2.16)

\left(\frac{1-\gamma}{4}\right)^{n}\leq\frac{\lvert E\cap B_{r}(x)\rvert}{r^{n}}\leq 1-\left(\frac{1-\gamma}{4}\right)^{n}\quad\text{ and }\quad\frac{(1-\gamma)^{n-1}}{C}\leq\frac{P(E;B_{r}(x))}{r^{n-1}}\leq\frac{C}{1-\gamma}.

In particular, we have

(2.17)

{\mathcal{H}}^{n-1}(\partial E\setminus\partial^{*}E)=0.

Proof.

We only prove
Equation

2.15
since it is standard that weak quasi-minimality implies density
upper and lower bounds (see
[
16
, Theorem 5.6]
), which then imply
Equation

2.17
. To obtain the correct scaling in
\gamma
, one can repeat the proof in

[
27
, Theorem 21.11]
. By scaling and translation, we may assume that
r=1
and
x=0
.
Testing the
(\Lambda,r_{0})
-minimality of
E
against
F
, we have

\displaystyle P(E;B_{1})

\displaystyle\penalty\ \leq\penalty\ \,P(F;B_{1})+\gamma\left(P_{\varepsilon}(E)-P_{\varepsilon}(F)\right)+\Lambda\lvert E\triangle F\rvert

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathclap{\lx@cref{creftype\lx@tilde refnum}{eq:difPe}\&\lx@cref{creftype\lx@tilde refnum}{boundpg}}\vphantom{\big|}}}{{\penalty\ \leq\penalty\ }}\,P(F;B_{1})+\gamma P(E\triangle F)+\Lambda\lvert E\triangle F\rvert.

We now argue as in
[
27
, Remark 21.7]
and use the isoperimetric
inequality to infer

\lvert E\triangle F\rvert=\lvert E\triangle F\rvert^{\frac{1}{n}}\lvert E\triangle F\rvert^{1-\frac{1}{n}}\leq\frac{1}{n}P(E\triangle F).

We thus find

P(E;B_{1})\leq P(F;B_{1})+\left(\gamma+\frac{\Lambda}{n}\right)P(E\triangle F)\leq P(F;B_{1})+\left(\gamma+\frac{\Lambda}{n}\right)(P(E;B_{1})+P(F;B_{1})).

Rearranging terms and using that
\Lambda/n\leq(1-\gamma)/2
yields
Equation

2.15
.
∎

Remark 2.9
.

Thanks to
Equation

2.17
when

Proposition 2.10
.

Assume that

satisfies

1
,
2
and
4
. Then there exists
C=C(n,G,\gamma)>0
such
that for every

,

every

-minimizer

of
{\mathcal{F}}_{\varepsilon,\gamma}

and every set

P(E;B_{r}(x))\leq P(F;B_{r}(x))+\left(\frac{C}{\varepsilon^{1-s_{0}}}\right)r^{n-s_{0}}+\Lambda|E\triangle F|.

2.3.
Basic properties of the excess

The cylindrical excess and spherical excess are respectively defined in
1.2

and
1.4
.
We refer to
[
27

Proposition 2.12

(Scaling properties)
.

Let
E\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
be a set of finite perimeter,

,
\nu\in\mathbb{S}^{n-1}

{\mathbf{e}}(E,x,r,\nu)\leq\left(\frac{R}{r}\right)^{n-1}{\mathbf{e}}(E,x,R,\nu)\qquad\text{ and }

In addition, setting

{\mathbf{e}}(E_{x,r},0,1,\nu)={\mathbf{e}}(E,x,R,\nu).

Note that this property holds for the spherical excess as well.

Proposition 2.13

(Changes of direction)
.

Let
\gamma\in(0,1)
and
\varepsilon>0
. There exists
C=C(n,\gamma)>0
such that for every

(\Lambda,r_{0})
-minimizer
E
of
{\mathcal{F}}_{\varepsilon,\gamma}
with
\Lambda r_{0}\leq 1-\gamma

Proof.

2.4.
The height bound

Thanks to the density estimates of

-minimizers of

Proposition 2.14

(The height bound)
.

Let
\varepsilon>0
,
\gamma\in(0,1)
,
\Lambda>0
and
r_{0}>0
with
\Lambda r_{0}\leq 1-\gamma
.

-minimizer

As recalled by

Mag2012

is to obtain the

∎

3.
Lipschitz approximation theorem

This section is devoted to the proof of the Lipschitz approximation theorem for

(\Lambda,r_{0})
-minimizers of
{\mathcal{F}}_{\varepsilon,\gamma}
, which is divided into two parts.
We first state that a small excess of an almost-minimizer

in that cylinder is almost entirely covered by the graph of a Lipschitz
function

function as long as the scale is much larger than
\varepsilon

\int_{D_{2}\times D_{2}}|u_{x^{\prime}}-u_{y^{\prime}}|^{2}\widetilde{G}_{\varepsilon}(\lvert\Phi_{stu}(x^{\prime},y^{\prime})\rvert)\mathop{}\mathopen{}\mathrm{d}x^{\prime}\mathop{}\mathopen{}\mathrm{d}y^{\prime}\leq CI^{2}_{|\nabla G|}\int_{D_{2}}|\nabla u|^{2}\leq CI^{2}_{|\nabla G|}{\mathbf{e}}_{n}(14),

In the proof of

Lemma 3.6
.

Let

be a radial kernel such that (recall definition

For

We start by estimating

\displaystyle\int_{D_{2}\times D_{2}}(u(x^{\prime})-u(y^{\prime}))^{2}G(\Phi_{u}(x^{\prime},y^{\prime}))\mathop{}\mathopen{}\mathrm{d}x^{\prime}\mathop{}\mathopen{}\mathrm{d}y^{\prime}

\displaystyle\leq\int_{0}^{1}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n-1}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{n-1}}{\mathbf{1}}_{D_{2}}(x^{\prime}){\mathbf{1}}_{D_{2}}(y^{\prime})\lvert\nabla u(x^{\prime}+t(y^{\prime}-x^{\prime}))\rvert^{2}\lvert x^{\prime}-y^{\prime}\rvert^{2}G(\Phi_{u}(x^{\prime},y^{\prime}))\mathop{}\mathopen{}\mathrm{d}x^{\prime}\mathop{}\mathopen{}\mathrm{d}y^{\prime}\mathop{}\mathopen{}\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{1}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n-1}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{n-1}}{\mathbf{1}}_{D_{2}}(\hat{x}^{\prime}-tz^{\prime}){\mathbf{1}}_{D_{2}}(\hat{x}^{\prime}+(1-t)z^{\prime})\lvert\nabla u(\hat{x})\rvert^{2}\lvert z^{\prime}\rvert^{2}G(\Phi_{u}(\hat{x}^{\prime}-tz^{\prime},\hat{x}^{\prime}+(1-t)z^{\prime}))

,

. We finally claim that

(3.23)

and write using polar coordinates

\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n-1}}\lvert z^{\prime}\rvert^{2}G(\Phi_{u}(x^{\prime}-tz^{\prime},x^{\prime}+(1-t)z^{\prime}))\mathop{}\mathopen{}\mathrm{d}z^{\prime}\\

4.
Caccioppoli inequality

First, we introduce the notion of
flatness
for sets of finite perimeter.

Definition 4.1

(Flatness)
.

For any set of finite perimeter
E\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
we define the flatness of

E
in
x\in\partial E
at scale
r>0
with respect to the direction
\nu\in\mathbb{S}^{n-1}
by

\mathbf{f}(E,x,r,\nu)\coloneqq\inf_{c\in{\mathbb{R}}}\penalty\ \frac{1}{r^{n-1}}\int_{\partial^{*}E\cap\mathbf{C}(x,r,\nu)}\frac{\lvert(y-x)\cdot\nu-c\rvert^{2}}{r^{2}}\mathop{}\mathopen{}\mathrm{d}\mathcal{H}^{n-1}_{y}.

When
\nu=e_{n}
, we write
\mathbf{f}_{n}(E,x,r)
for
\mathbf{f}(E,x,r,e_{n})
and we write
\mathbf{f}_{n}(E,r)
for
\mathbf{f}_{n}(E,0,r)
.

Using the harmonic approximation result given by
3.3
, we will be able to show in
5.3
that there exists a direction
\nu
such that

\mathbf{f}(E,\lambda r,\nu)\lesssim\lambda^{2}{\mathbf{e}}_{n}(E,r)
for
(\Lambda,r_{0})
-minimizers of
{\mathcal{F}}_{\varepsilon,\gamma}
, as long as
r
is much larger than
\varepsilon
.
To pass this estimate to the excess at scale
\lambda r/2
, we establish in this section a
Caccioppoli-type (or Reverse Poincaré) inequality.
The key argument is to prove first that for sets which are sufficiently flat, the quasi-minimality
condition
Equation

2.15
can be upgraded.

Let us introduce some additional notation. For the convenience of the reader, these also appear in
the
Notation.
section at the beginning of the paper.

For any
t>0
and
z\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n-1}
, we define
\mathbf{K}_{t}(z)\coloneqq D_{t}(z)\times\left(-1,1\right)
, and we
simply write
\mathbf{K}_{t}
when
z=0
.
For any cylinder
\mathbf{K}_{t}(z)
, any set of locally finite perimeter
E
, and any constant
c\in{\mathbb{R}}
, we define
the quantities

{\mathscr{F}}(E,\mathbf{K}_{t}(z),c)\coloneqq\int_{\mathbf{K}_{t}(z)\cap\partial^{*}E}\frac{(x_{n}-c)^{2}}{t^{2}}\mathop{}\mathopen{}\mathrm{d}\mathcal{H}^{n-1}

and

{\mathscr{E}}(E,\mathbf{K}_{t}(z))\coloneqq P(E;\mathbf{K}_{t}(z))-{\mathcal{H}}^{n-1}(D_{t}(z)).

When
z=0
, we make the abuse of notation
{\mathscr{E}}(E,t)={\mathscr{E}}(E,\mathbf{K}_{t}(0))
and

{\mathscr{F}}(E,t,c)={\mathscr{E}}(E,\mathbf{K}_{t}(0),c)
.
Let us point out that these two quantities are respectively linked with the (non-scale-invariant)
flatness and excess of
E
at scale
t
in the direction
e_{n}
.
Indeed, if
0\in\partial E
and if for some
h\in(0,t)

\left\{(x^{\prime},x_{n})\in\mathbf{K}_{t}\penalty\ :\penalty\ x_{n}<-h\right\}\subset E\cap\mathbf{C}_{t}\subset\left\{(x^{\prime},x_{n})\in\mathbf{K}_{t}\penalty\ :\penalty\ x_{n}<h\right\},

then

\mathbf{f}_{n}(E,t)=\inf_{c\in{\mathbb{R}}}\penalty\ \frac{1}{t^{n-1}}{\mathscr{F}}(E,t,c),

and

{\mathcal{H}}^{n-1}(D_{t})=\int_{\partial^{*}E\cap\mathbf{C}_{t}}\nu_{E}\cdot e_{n}

(see
[
27
, Lemma 22.11]
), thus, for any
t\in(0,1)
,

(4.1)

{\mathscr{E}}(E,t)=\int_{\partial^{*}E\cap\mathbf{C}_{t}}(1-\nu_{E}\cdot e_{n})\mathop{}\mathopen{}\mathrm{d}\mathcal{H}^{n-1}=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\partial^{*}E\cap\mathbf{C}_{t}}\lvert\nu_{E}-e_{n}\rvert^{2}\mathop{}\mathopen{}\mathrm{d}\mathcal{H}^{n-1}=\left(\frac{t^{n-1}}{2}\right){\mathbf{e}}_{n}(E,t).

Notice in particular that
{\mathscr{E}}(E,\cdot)
is nondecreasing in
(0,1)
.
Eventually, recalling the definition of the function
Q
in
Equation

1.3
, for any
\theta\in[0,1]
we define the function
Q_{1-\theta}
by

(4.2)

Q_{1-\theta}(t)\coloneqq Q(t^{1-\theta}),\qquad\forall t>0.

4.1.
A refined quasi-minimality condition

We improve the quasi-minimality condition

2.15
for sets which are sufficiently
flat. For any
\varepsilon>0
, let us define the
“critical”
energy functional

{\mathcal{F}}_{\varepsilon}(E)\coloneqq{\mathcal{F}}_{1,\varepsilon}(E)=(P-P_{\varepsilon})(E).

Proposition 4.2
.

Assume that
G
satisfies
Items

1
and
2
, and let
\varepsilon\in(0,1)
,
\gamma\in(0,1)
,

\theta\in(0,1]
and
\Lambda>0
with
4\Lambda\leq 1-\gamma
.
There exists
C=C(n)>0
such that if
E
is a
(\Lambda,4)
-minimizer of

{\mathcal{F}}_{\varepsilon,\gamma}
with

\left\{x_{n}<-\frac{1}{4}\right\}\cap\mathbf{K}_{3}\subset E\cap\mathbf{K}_{3}\subset\left\{x_{n}<\frac{1}{4}\right\}\cap\mathbf{K}_{3},

then the following holds.
If
t\in(0,2)
is such that
{\mathcal{H}}^{n-1}(\partial\mathbf{K}_{t}\cap\partial E)=0
then for any set
F
of finite perimeter such that

and

\left\{x_{n}<-\frac{1}{4}\right\}\cap\mathbf{K}_{t}\subset F\cap\mathbf{K}_{t}\subset\left\{x_{n}<\frac{1}{4}\right\}\cap\mathbf{K}_{t},

we have

(4.3)

{\mathscr{E}}(E,t)\leq\left(\frac{1+\gamma}{1-\gamma}\right){\mathscr{E}}(F,t)+\frac{2\gamma}{(1-\gamma)}\big[{\mathscr{E}}(E,t+\varepsilon^{\theta})-{\mathscr{E}}(E,t)\big]\\
+\frac{C}{(1-\gamma)}Q_{1-\theta}\left(\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\right)+\frac{\Lambda}{(1-\gamma)}\lvert E\triangle F\rvert+\frac{1+3\gamma}{(1-\gamma)}{{\mathcal{H}}^{n-1}}(\partial^{*}F\cap\partial\mathbf{K}_{t}).

Proof.

To simplify notation a bit set
\eta\coloneqq{{\mathcal{H}}^{n-1}}(\partial^{*}F\cap\partial\mathbf{K}_{t})
. Since

{\mathcal{H}}^{n-1}(\partial\mathbf{K}_{t}\cap\partial E)=0
and
E\triangle F\subset\mathbf{K}_{t}
we have

(4.4)

P(E)-P(F)=P(E;\mathbf{K}_{t})-P(F;\mathbf{K}_{t})-\eta.

By
(\Lambda,4)
-minimality of
E
we find

(4.5)

(1-\gamma)P(E;\mathbf{K}_{t})\leq(1-\gamma)P(F;\mathbf{K}_{t})+\gamma\big[{\mathcal{F}}_{\varepsilon}(F)-{\mathcal{F}}_{\varepsilon}(E)\big]+\Lambda\lvert E\triangle F\rvert+(1-\gamma)\eta.

In the next two steps we prove that

(4.6)

{\mathcal{F}}_{\varepsilon}(F)-{\mathcal{F}}_{\varepsilon}(E)\leq 2{\mathscr{E}}(F,t+\varepsilon^{\theta})+CQ_{1-\theta}\left(\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\right).

Step 1.
In this first step we localize the estimate. Setting for simplicity

\widetilde{D}_{t}\coloneqq D_{t+\varepsilon^{\theta}},\qquad\widetilde{\mathbf{K}}_{t}\coloneqq\mathbf{K}_{t+\varepsilon^{\theta}}

and defining the

{\mathcal{F}}_{\varepsilon}^{{\mathrm{loc}}}(E)\coloneqq P(E;\widetilde{\mathbf{K}}_{t})-\int_{(E\cap\widetilde{\mathbf{K}}_{t})\times(E^{\mathrm{c}}\cap\widetilde{\mathbf{K}}_{t})}G_{\varepsilon}(x-y)\mathop{}\mathopen{}\mathrm{d}x\mathop{}\mathopen{}\mathrm{d}y,

we claim that

(4.7)

{\mathcal{F}}_{\varepsilon}(F)-{\mathcal{F}}_{\varepsilon}(E)\leq{\mathcal{F}}_{\varepsilon}^{\mathrm{loc}}(F)-{\mathcal{F}}_{\varepsilon}^{\mathrm{loc}}(E)+CQ_{1-\theta}(\varepsilon^{-1}).

Since
E\triangle F\subset\mathbf{K}_{t}\subset\subset\widetilde{\mathbf{K}}_{t}
,

P(E)-P(F)=P(E;\widetilde{\mathbf{K}}_{t})-P(F;\widetilde{\mathbf{K}}_{t})
and thus in order to prove
Equation

4.7
, we just
need to consider the nonlocal part. Setting
\Phi(A,B)=\int_{A\times B}G_{\varepsilon}(x-y)\mathop{}\mathopen{}\mathrm{d}x\mathop{}\mathopen{}\mathrm{d}y
and
using that
E\triangle F\subset\mathbf{K}_{t}
and
\Phi(A,B)\geqslant 0
, we have

\displaystyle P_{\varepsilon}(E)-P_{\varepsilon}(F)=\Phi(E,E^{c})-\Phi(F,F^{c})

\displaystyle=\left[\Phi(E\cap\widetilde{\mathbf{K}}_{t},E^{c}\cap\widetilde{\mathbf{K}}_{t})-\Phi(F\cap\widetilde{\mathbf{K}}_{t},F^{c}\cap\widetilde{\mathbf{K}}_{t})\right]+\Phi(E\cap\mathbf{K}_{t},E^{c}\backslash\widetilde{\mathbf{K}}_{t})-\Phi(F\cap\mathbf{K}_{t},E^{c}\backslash\widetilde{\mathbf{K}}_{t})

\displaystyle+\Phi(E\backslash\widetilde{\mathbf{K}}_{t},E^{c}\cap\mathbf{K}_{t})-\Phi(E\backslash\widetilde{\mathbf{K}}_{t},F^{c}\cap\mathbf{K}_{t})

\displaystyle\leq\left[\Phi(E\cap\widetilde{\mathbf{K}}_{t},E^{c}\cap\widetilde{\mathbf{K}}_{t})-\Phi(F\cap\widetilde{\mathbf{K}}_{t},F^{c}\cap\widetilde{\mathbf{K}}_{t})\right]+\Phi(E\cap\mathbf{K}_{t},E^{c}\backslash\widetilde{\mathbf{K}}_{t})+\Phi(E\backslash\widetilde{\mathbf{K}}_{t},E^{c}\cap\mathbf{K}_{t})

\displaystyle\leq\left[\Phi(E\cap\widetilde{\mathbf{K}}_{t},E^{c}\cap\widetilde{\mathbf{K}}_{t})-\Phi(F\cap\widetilde{\mathbf{K}}_{t},F^{c}\cap\widetilde{\mathbf{K}}_{t})\right]+2\Phi(\mathbf{K}_{t},(\widetilde{\mathbf{K}}_{t})^{c}).

In order to prove
Equation

4.7

\Phi(\mathbf{K}_{t},(\widetilde{\mathbf{K}}_{t})^{c})
. For this we write that

\displaystyle\Phi(\mathbf{K}_{t},(\widetilde{\mathbf{K}}_{t})^{c})

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbf{K}_{t}\times(\widetilde{\mathbf{K}}_{t})^{c}}G_{\varepsilon}(x-y)\mathop{}\mathopen{}\mathrm{d}x\mathop{}\mathopen{}\mathrm{d}y

\displaystyle=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}{\mathbf{1}}_{\mathbf{K}_{t}}(x){\mathbf{1}}_{\widetilde{\mathbf{K}}_{t}^{c}}(x+z)G_{\varepsilon}(z)\mathop{}\mathopen{}\mathrm{d}x\mathop{}\mathopen{}\mathrm{d}z

\displaystyle=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}{\mathbf{1}}_{\mathbf{K}_{t}}(x){\mathbf{1}}_{\widetilde{\mathbf{K}}_{t}^{c}}(x+z){\mathbf{1}}_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}\backslash B_{\varepsilon^{\theta}}}(z)G_{\varepsilon}(z)\mathop{}\mathopen{}\mathrm{d}x\mathop{}\mathopen{}\mathrm{d}z

\displaystyle\leq\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}{\mathbf{1}}_{\mathbf{K}_{t}}(x){\mathbf{1}}_{\mathbf{K}_{t}^{c}}(x+z){\mathbf{1}}_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}\backslash B_{\varepsilon^{\theta}}}(z)G_{\varepsilon}(z)\mathop{}\mathopen{}\mathrm{d}x\mathop{}\mathopen{}\mathrm{d}z

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2}\left(\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}\backslash B_{\varepsilon^{\theta}}}|z|G_{\varepsilon}(z)\mathop{}\mathopen{}\mathrm{d}z\right)P(\mathbf{K}_{t})

\displaystyle\leq CQ_{1-\theta}(\varepsilon^{-1}),

where we used
Equation

2.1
with
K={\mathbf{1}}_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}\backslash B_{\varepsilon^{\theta}}}G_{\varepsilon}
.

Step 2.
In this step we show

(4.8)

{\mathcal{F}}_{\varepsilon}^{\mathrm{loc}}(F)-{\mathcal{F}}_{\varepsilon}^{\mathrm{loc}}(E)\leq 2{\mathscr{E}}(F,t+\varepsilon^{\theta})+CQ_{1-\theta}(\varepsilon^{-1}).

Together with
Equation

4.7
this would conclude the proof of
Equation

4.6
. To this aim,
we use a slicing argument (see
[
28

[

as an average over

Let us set

for

. For every line segment

Proceeding as in

Step 2.1.

We claim that for every

and

-a.e

\leq\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}0&\qquad\text{ it }\partial^{*}F\cap\widetilde{L}_{\sigma,x}=\emptyset\text{,}\\
2\left({\mathcal{H}}^{0}(\partial^{*}F\cap\widetilde{L}_{\sigma,x})-1\right)+CQ_{1-\theta}(\varepsilon^{-1})&\qquad\text{ otherwise.}\end{array}\right.

By the standard properties of one-dimensional restrictions of sets of finite perimeter (see e.g.
[

),
it is enough to prove

for

and

By

\displaystyle=1-2\int_{0}^{t_{F}}\int_{t_{F}}^{a}\rho_{\varepsilon}(s-t)\mathop{}\mathopen{}\mathrm{d}s\mathop{}\mathopen{}\mathrm{d}t

Case 2:

.
Either

Notice that

\int_{\frac{1}{2}}^{\frac{3}{4}}[{\mathscr{E}}(E,st+\varepsilon)-{\mathscr{E}}(E,st)]\mathop{}\mathopen{}\mathrm{d}s=\frac{1}{t}\int_{\frac{t}{2}}^{\frac{3t}{4}}[{\mathscr{E}}(E,a+\varepsilon^{\theta})-{\mathscr{E}}(E,a)]\mathop{}\mathopen{}\mathrm{d}a\\
=\frac{1}{t}\left(\int_{\frac{t}{2}+\varepsilon^{\theta}}^{\frac{3t}{4}+\varepsilon^{\theta}}{\mathscr{E}}(E,a)\mathop{}\mathopen{}\mathrm{d}a-\int_{\frac{t}{2}}^{\frac{3t}{4}}{\mathscr{E}}(E,a)\mathop{}\mathopen{}\mathrm{d}a\right)\leq\frac{1}{t}\int_{\frac{3t}{4}}^{\frac{3t}{4}+\varepsilon^{\theta}}{\mathscr{E}}(E,a)\mathop{}\mathopen{}\mathrm{d}a\leq\left(\frac{\varepsilon^{\theta}}{t}\right){\mathscr{E}}(E,t),

where for the last inequality we used again that
{\mathscr{E}}(E,\cdot)
is
nondecreasing.
Third,

(4.32)

\int_{\frac{1}{2}}^{\frac{3}{4}}stV_{{\mathscr{E}}}(st)\mathop{}\mathopen{}\mathrm{d}s\leq\frac{3}{4}\int_{\frac{t}{2}}^{\frac{3t}{4}}V_{{\mathscr{E}}}(a)\mathop{}\mathopen{}\mathrm{d}a=\frac{3}{4}\left({\mathscr{E}}\left(E_{k},\frac{3t}{4}\right)-{\mathscr{E}}\left(E_{k},\frac{t}{2}\right)\right)\leq\frac{3}{4}{\mathscr{E}}(E_{k},t).

Finally, with a similar argument using that

is nondecreasing, we have

(4.33)

\int_{\frac{1}{2}}^{\frac{3}{4}}\frac{1}{st}V_{{\mathscr{F}}}(st)\mathop{}\mathopen{}\mathrm{d}s\leq 2{\mathscr{F}}(E_{k},t,c).

Inserting
Equation

4.30
,
Equation

4.31
,
Equations

4.32
and
4.33

into
Equation

4.29
yields

{\mathscr{E}}(E,t/2)\leq C\left[\lambda{\mathscr{E}}(E_{k},t)+\frac{1}{\lambda}{\mathscr{F}}(E_{k},t,c)+\left(\frac{\varepsilon^{\theta}}{t}\right){\mathscr{E}}(E,t)+\Lambda t^{n-1}+Q_{1-\theta}(\varepsilon^{-1})+|E\triangle E_{k}|\right].

By
Equation

4.25
we can send

to obtain

{\mathscr{E}}(E,t/2)\leq C\left[\lambda{\mathscr{E}}(E,t)+\frac{1}{\lambda}{\mathscr{F}}(E,t,c)+\left(\frac{\varepsilon^{\theta}}{t}\right){\mathscr{E}}(E,t)+\Lambda t^{n-1}+Q_{1-\theta}(\varepsilon^{-1})\right].

Recalling that

concludes the proof of

4.24
for a.e.

. By the left-continuity of

and

.
∎

Proposition 4.6

(Caccioppoli inequality)
.

and
2

\gamma\in(0,1)

Proof.

Up to a rotation and rescaling, we may assume that

small enough,

4.35
recalling that

4.1

or

for

and

If

,

Proposition 5.1
.

Let
\omega>0
,
\alpha\in(0,1)
and
r_{0}>0
be fixed. Then, there exists
\tau=\tau(n,\alpha)>0
and
C=C(n,\alpha)
such that the following holds. If

E
is such that for every

and every

P(E;B_{r})\leq P(F;B_{r})+\omega r^{n-1+\alpha},

assuming that

and

{\mathbf{e}}(E,R)+\omega R^{\alpha}\leq\tau

for some
R\leq r_{0}
, then

{\mathbf{e}}(E,r)\leq C\left(R^{-\alpha}{\mathbf{e}}(E,R)+\omega\right)r^{\alpha}\qquad\forall r\in(0,R).

Proof.

Although this result is standard and can be reconstructed from e.g.
[
32
]
,
we provide a short proof since this precise statement is not easily accessible in the literature. By scaling we may assume without loss of generality that
R=1
(replacing
\omega
by
\omega R^{\alpha}
).
We thus want to prove that there exist
\tau=\tau(n)>0
and
C=C(n,\alpha)>0
such that provided

(5.1)

{\mathbf{e}}(E,1)+\omega\leq\tau

then

(5.2)

{\mathbf{e}}(E,r)\leq C\left({\mathbf{e}}(E,1)+\omega\right)r^{\alpha}\qquad\forall r\in(0,1).

We recall that by the tilt Lemma (see for instance
[
20
, Lemma 4.6]
applied to
\Lambda=\omega r^{\alpha}
and
\ell=0
combined with
the beginning of the proof of
[
20
, Proposition 4.1]

\lambda
small enough,
there exists
\tau_{\rm tilt}=\tau_{\rm tilt}(n,\lambda)>0
,

and

such that provided

{\mathbf{e}}(E,r)+\omega r^{\alpha}\leq\tau_{\rm tilt},

we have

(5.4)

{\mathbf{e}}(E,\lambda r)\leq C_{\rm tilt}\lambda^{2}{\mathbf{e}}(E,r)+C_{\lambda}\omega r^{\alpha}.

We now choose
\lambda
such that
C_{\rm tilt}\lambda^{2}\leq\lambda^{\alpha}/2
and then set
C=2C_{\lambda}/\lambda^{\alpha}
. Letting for
k\geq 0
,
r_{k}=\lambda^{k}
, we claim that for every
k\geq 0
,

{\mathbf{e}}(E,r_{k})\leq C({\mathbf{e}}(E,1)+\omega)r_{k}^{\alpha}.

By the scaling properties of the excess, see

, this would conclude the proof of
Equation

5.2
.

We start by noting that the statement holds for

. Now if it holds for

\tau
small enough,

5.3
holds for

{\mathbf{e}}(E,r_{k})\leq C_{\rm tilt}\lambda^{2}{\mathbf{e}}(E,r_{k-1})+C_{\lambda}\omega r_{k-1}^{\alpha}\leq\frac{C}{2}({\mathbf{e}}(E,1)+\omega)r_{k}^{\alpha}+\frac{C_{\lambda}}{\lambda^{\alpha}}\omega r_{k}^{\alpha}\leq C({\mathbf{e}}(E,1)+\omega)r_{k}^{\alpha}.

This proves

5.5
.
∎

Proposition 5.2

(Excess decay at small scales)

Assume that
G
satisfies

,
2
and
4
, and let

(\Lambda,r_{0})

By scaling (recall

\lambda
, we have

\left(\frac{\varepsilon}{\lambda}\right)^{\theta}{\mathbf{e}}(\lambda,\nu)\leq C\left(\frac{\varepsilon}{\lambda}\right)^{\theta}{\mathbf{e}}(M_{\mathrm{cac}}\lambda,\nu)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\lx@cref{creftype\lx@tilde refnum}{excess_improv2:eq3}}}{{\leq}}\frac{L\tau_{\mathrm{tilt}}}{\lambda^{n-1+\theta}}\leq\lambda^{2}L.

Thus, for
\lambda_{\mathrm{tilt}}
small enough,
Equations

5.11
and
5.15

give

{\mathbf{e}}(\lambda/2,\nu)\leq C\left(\lambda^{2}L+\lambda\Lambda+Q_{1-\theta}\left(\frac{\lambda}{\varepsilon}\right)\right).

Since
Q
is nonincreasing, this gives
Equation

5.9
with
r=4
and
\lambda/2
in place
of
\lambda
, which concludes the proof.
∎

As a corollary, iterating properly
5.3
, we get the following power decay of the
excess down to scales which are large compared to
\varepsilon
(the constant
\eta
below is typically large).

Proposition 5.4
.

Assume that
G
satisfies
Items

1
,
2
and
3
. Let
\gamma\in(0,1)
,
\varepsilon>0

\Lambda>0
,
r_{0}>0
and
\eta\geq 1
with
\Lambda r_{0}\leq 1-\gamma
.
Given any
\theta\in(0,1)
, there exists a positive
constant

\tau_{\mathrm{dec}}^{\mathbf{\ell}}=\tau_{\mathrm{dec}}^{\mathbf{\ell}}(n,G,\gamma,\theta)
such that the following holds. If
E
is a

(\Lambda,r_{0})
-minimizer of
{\mathcal{F}}_{\varepsilon,\gamma}
with
0\in\partial E
satisfying, for some

\eta\varepsilon\leq R\leq r_{0}
,

(5.16)

{\mathbf{e}}(E,R)+\Lambda R+\eta^{-1}\leq\tau_{\mathrm{dec}}^{\mathbf{\ell}}

then, for all
r\in[\eta\varepsilon,R]
, we have

(5.17)

\displaystyle{\mathbf{e}}(E,r)

\displaystyle\leq C\left[\frac{r}{R}\big({\mathbf{e}}(E,R)+\Lambda R\big)+Q_{1-\theta}\left(\frac{r}{\lambda\varepsilon}\right)\right]

where
\lambda
and
C
depend only on
n,G,\gamma
.

Proof.

With
5.3
at hand, the proof of
Equation

5.17
is very similar to the proof of
5.2
.
By scaling (recall
2.7
) we may assume that
R=1
. Arguing as in
[
20
, Proposition
4.1]
we may use the scaling properties of the excess (see
2.12
)
to post-process
5.3
and replace the cylindrical excess by the spherical excess both in the hypothesis
Equation

5.8
and the conclusion
Equation

5.9
.

Let
\lambda=\lambda(n,G,\gamma)\leq 1
to be chosen later and set for
k\geq 0
,
r_{k}=\lambda^{k}
. By the scaling properties of the excess (see
2.12
) and the monotonicity of
Q_{1-\theta}
, in order to prove
Equation

5.17
, it is enough to prove that there exists
C>0
such that if
r_{k}\geq\eta\varepsilon
then

(5.18)

{\mathbf{e}}(E,r_{k})\leq C\left(r_{k}({\mathbf{e}}(E,1)+\Lambda)+Q_{1-\theta}\left(\frac{r_{k}}{\varepsilon}\right)\right).

Let
C_{\rm tilt}>0
be the constant given in
Equation

5.9
. We then choose
\lambda
such that
C_{\rm tilt}\lambda^{2}\leq\lambda/2
and set
C=2C_{\rm tilt}
. Since
Equation

5.18
holds for
k=0

it is enough to show that provided it holds for
k-1
then it also holds for
k
.

By
Equation

5.16
, the induction hypothesis and the fact that
Q
vanishes at infinity, up to choosing
\tau_{\mathrm{dec}}^{\mathbf{\ell}}
small enough,
Equation

5.8
is satisfied for
r=r_{k-1}

(notice that
(\varepsilon/r_{k-1})^{\theta}\leq\eta^{-\theta}
). Therefore, by
Equation

5.9
and the monotonicity of
Q_{1-\theta}
,

{\mathbf{e}}(E,r_{k})\leq C_{\rm tilt}\left(\lambda^{2}{\mathbf{e}}(E,r_{k-1})+\Lambda r_{k}+Q_{1-\theta}\left(\frac{r_{k}}{\varepsilon}\right)\right)\\
\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\lx@cref{creftype\lx@tilde refnum}{claimlargescales}}}{{\leq}}\frac{C}{2}\left(r_{k}({\mathbf{e}}(E,1)+\Lambda)+Q_{1-\theta}\left(\frac{r_{k}}{\varepsilon}\right)\right)+C_{\rm tilt}\left(\Lambda r_{k}+Q_{1-\theta}\left(\frac{r_{k}}{\varepsilon}\right)\right)\\
\leq C\left(r_{k}({\mathbf{e}}(E,1)+\Lambda)+Q_{1-\theta}\left(\frac{r_{k}}{\varepsilon}\right)\right).

This concludes the proof.
∎

Combining
5.2
and
5.4
we obtain that the excess is controlled by a power of the radius down to arbitrarily small scales.

Theorem 5.5
.

Assume that
G
satisfies
Items

1
to
5
, and let
\gamma\in(0,1)
,

\Lambda>0
and
r_{0}>0
with
\Lambda r_{0}\leq 1-\gamma
.
Then, for every
\alpha\in(0,\alpha_{*})
with

\alpha_{*}=\frac{1}{2}\frac{p_{0}}{(n-s_{0})(n+p_{0})+p_{0}}(1-s_{0}),

there exist

\beta=\beta(n,G,\alpha)
,
\tau_{\mathrm{reg}}=\tau_{\mathrm{reg}}(n,G,\gamma,\alpha)
and

\varepsilon_{\rm reg}=\varepsilon_{\rm reg}(n,G,\gamma,\alpha,\Lambda)
such that the following holds.
If
E
is a
(\Lambda,r_{0})
-minimizer of
{\mathcal{F}}_{\varepsilon,\gamma}
with
\varepsilon\in(0,\varepsilon_{\rm reg})
and
0\in\partial E
satisfying, for
some
\varepsilon^{1-\beta}\leq R\leq r_{0}
,

{\mathbf{e}}(E,R)+\Lambda R\leq\tau_{\mathrm{dec}}

then

(5.19)

\displaystyle{\mathbf{e}}(E,r)

\displaystyle\leq C\left(\frac{r}{R}\left({\mathbf{e}}(E,R)+\Lambda R\right)+r^{2\alpha}\right)\qquad\textrm{for }0<r\leq R,

where
C=C(n,G,\gamma,\alpha)
.

Before giving the proof, let us explain how we deduce
A
and

B
.

Proof of
B
.

The proof is a standard consequence of the excess decay proven in
5.5
and Campanato’s criterion for Hölder-continuous functions. We refer to
[
27
]
for more details.
∎

Proof of
A
.

By
[
30
, Theorem B]
(see also
[
28
, proof of Theorem 2.7]
about the hypothesis

G\in L^{1}
), if
\varepsilon
is small enough we have existence of minimizers
E_{\varepsilon}
for
Equation

P

.
Moreover, still by
[
30
, Theorem B]
, they converge up to translation to
B_{1}
as
\varepsilon\to 0
.
The convergence is meant here both in
L^{1}
for the sets and in the Hausdorff distance for the
boundaries. In addition, we have convergence of the perimeters. This yields continuity of the excess
(see e.g.
[
27
]
for this fact).
Since
B_{1}
is smooth, this implies that
B
may be applied at every point of the boundary of
E_{\varepsilon}
at a scale
R
which is uniform in
\varepsilon
(and the point).
By a standard covering argument, see e.g.
[
8
]
this upgrades the Hausdorff convergence of the boundaries to
C^{1,\alpha}
. In particular, for
\varepsilon
small enough,
\partial E_{\varepsilon}
are small
C^{1,\alpha}
graphs over
\partial B_{1}
.
Then,
A
is an immediate consequence of
[
28
, Theorem 3]
.
∎

Eventually, we prove the power decay of the excess.

Proof of
5.5
.

Starting from a scale
R
, the idea of the proof is to use
Equation

5.17
to obtain the decay of the excess up to a scale
r_{+}
.
Then, in order to use
Equation

5.7
we use the scaling properties of the excess (see
2.7
) to jump to a scale
r_{-}
.
Setting
L={\mathbf{e}}(E,R)+\Lambda R
, since we want that in particular
{\mathbf{e}}(E,r_{+})\leq C((r_{+}/R)L+r_{+}^{2\alpha})
, in light of
Equation

5.17
we need to take
r_{+}=\varepsilon^{1-\beta^{\prime}}
for some
\beta^{\prime}\in(0,1)
.
Similarly, since we want
{\mathbf{e}}(E,r_{-})\leq Cr_{-}^{2\alpha}
,
Equation

5.7
imposes
r_{-}=\varepsilon^{1+\beta^{\prime\prime}}
for some
\beta^{\prime\prime}>0
. Therefore, when relying on
2.7
to pass from
r_{+}
to
r_{-}

we will lose a factor in the estimates. To compensate that we need to assume that the starting scale
R
is much larger than
r_{+}
i.e.
R=\varepsilon^{1-\beta}
for some
1>\beta>\beta^{\prime}
.

We start by choosing
\beta
arbitrarily close to
1
,
p\in(0,p_{0})
arbitrarily close to
p_{0}
, and
\theta\in(0,1)
such that

(1-\theta)(n-1+p_{0})=(n-1+p).

For
\beta^{\prime}
to be chosen later, if
r_{+}=\varepsilon^{1-\beta^{\prime}}
we have that for
\varepsilon
small enough depending on
\beta^{\prime}
that
Equation

5.16
is satisfied (with
\eta=\varepsilon^{-\beta^{\prime}}
) so that
Equation

5.17
together with
Item

5
yield

{\mathbf{e}}(E,r)\leq C\left(\left(\frac{r}{R}\right)L+\left(\frac{\varepsilon}{r}\right)^{n-1+p}\right)\qquad\textrm{for }R\geq r\geq r_{+}.

This gives
Equation

5.19
provided

\left(\frac{\varepsilon}{r}\right)^{n-1+p}\leq r^{2\alpha}\qquad\textrm{for }R\geq r\geq r_{+}

which is equivalent to

\left(\frac{\varepsilon}{r_{+}}\right)^{n-1+p}\leq r_{+}^{2\alpha}.

Since
r_{+}=\varepsilon^{1-\beta^{\prime}}
, this gives the condition

(5.20)

2\alpha\leq\frac{\beta^{\prime}}{1-\beta^{\prime}}(n-1+p).

Thanks to
2.7
and
L\leq 1
, we then have

(5.21)

{\mathbf{e}}(E,r)\leq\left(\frac{r_{+}}{r}\right)^{n-1}{\mathbf{e}}(E,r_{+})\leq C\left(\frac{r_{+}}{r}\right)^{n-1}\left(\frac{r_{+}}{R}+\left(\frac{\varepsilon}{r_{+}}\right)^{n-1+p}\right)\qquad\textrm{for }r_{+}\geq r\geq r_{-}.

In order to have
{\mathbf{e}}(E,r)\leq Cr^{2\alpha}
in this range it is enough to have this estimate for
r=r_{-}
which means

\varepsilon^{-(\beta^{\prime}+\beta^{\prime\prime})(n-1)}(\varepsilon^{\beta-\beta^{\prime}}+\varepsilon^{\beta^{\prime}(n-1+p)})\leq\varepsilon^{2\alpha(1+\beta^{\prime\prime})}.

In particular, the optimal choice is

(5.22)

\beta^{\prime}=\frac{\beta}{n+p},

for which the condition becomes

(5.23)

2\alpha\leq\frac{\beta^{\prime}p-(n-1)\beta^{\prime\prime}}{1+\beta^{\prime\prime}}.

Let us point out that
Equation

5.23
is stronger than
Equation

5.20
. Notice that under
Equation

5.23
, we have in particular that
Equation

5.6
is satisfied at
R=r_{-}
provided
\varepsilon
is small enough. We may thus use
Equation

5.7
to obtain

{\mathbf{e}}(E,r)\leq C\left(\frac{r}{r_{-}}\right)^{1-s_{0}}\left({\mathbf{e}}(E,r_{-})+\left(\frac{r_{-}}{\varepsilon}\right)^{1-s_{0}}\right)\qquad\textrm{for }r_{-}\geq r>0.

By
Equation

5.21
(with the choice
Equation

5.22
) this reduces to

{\mathbf{e}}(E,r)\leq C\left(\frac{r}{r_{-}}\right)^{1-s_{0}}\left(\varepsilon^{\beta^{\prime}p-(n-1)\beta^{\prime\prime}}+\varepsilon^{(1-s_{0})\beta^{\prime\prime}}\right)\qquad\textrm{for }r_{-}\geq r>0.

In particular
{\mathbf{e}}(E,r)\leq Cr^{2\alpha}
for
r\leq r_{-}
provided it holds for
r=r_{-}
i.e.

\varepsilon^{\beta^{\prime}p-(n-1)\beta^{\prime\prime}}+\varepsilon^{\beta^{\prime\prime}(1-s_{0})}\leq\varepsilon^{2\alpha(1+\beta^{\prime\prime})}

which gives the condition

2\alpha\leq\min\left(\frac{\beta^{\prime}p-(n-1)\beta^{\prime\prime}}{1+\beta^{\prime\prime}},\frac{\beta^{\prime\prime}}{1+\beta^{\prime\prime}}(1-s_{0})\right).

This constraint implies
Equation

5.23
(which itself implies
Equation

5.20
). Since the first term is decreasing in
\beta^{\prime\prime}
and the second one is increasing, we see that the optimal choice of
\beta^{\prime\prime}
is obtained when both terms are equal, that is

\beta^{\prime\prime}=\frac{\beta p}{(n-s_{0})(n+p)}

which gives

2\alpha\leq\frac{\beta p}{(n-s_{0})(n+p)+\beta p}(1-s_{0}).

Since
p
can be taken arbitrarily close to
p_{0}
and
\beta
arbitrarily close to
1
, this concludes the proof.
∎

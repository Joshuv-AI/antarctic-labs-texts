Modeling Sparse Graph Sequences and Signals Using Generalized Graphons
Feng Ji, Xingchao Jian, Wee Peng Tay

Abstract
Graphons are limit objects of sequences of graphs and used to analyze the behavior of large graphs. Recently, graphon signal processing has been developed to study signal processing on large graphs. A major limitation of this approach is that any sparse sequence of graphs inevitably converges to the zero graphon, rendering the resulting signal processing theory trivial and inadequate for sparse graph sequences. To overcome this limitation, we propose a new signal processing framework that leverages the concept of generalized graphons and introduces the stretched cut distance as a measure to compare these graphons. Our framework focuses on the sampling of graph sequences from generalized graphons and explores the convergence properties of associated operators, spectra, and signals. Our signal processing framework provides a comprehensive approach to analyzing and processing signals on graph sequences, even if they are sparse. Finally, we discuss the practical implications of our theory for real-world large networks through numerical experiments.

Graphons are limit objects of sequences of finite graphs. More precisely, as the number of nodes tends to infinity, many graph sequences converge to a graphon in the cut distance. This has important signal processing implications such as the convergence of graph frequencies.

•

A graphon may be interpreted as a probabilistic model from where useful finite graphs are sampled. This consideration is used to study the transferability of graph filters on different graphs sampled from the same graphon.

II
Generalized graphons and the stretched cut distance

We first review the concept of generalized graphons. The discussion is largely based on
[
26
,
27
]
. A classical graphon
[
21
]
is defined on the compact domain
[0,1]^{2}=[0,1]\times[0,1]
. However, in the context of signal processing, it is more practical to define a graphon on a non-compact domain
\Omega^{2}=\Omega\times\Omega
. For our purposes, we focus on
\Omega=\mathbb{R}_{+}
, which is a concrete choice. This allows us to consider a generalized graphon as a non-negative, bounded, and symmetric function on
\mathbb{R}_{+}^{2}
that belongs to
L^{1}(\mathbb{R}_{+}^{2})
.

Definition 1
.

Consider the Lebesgue measure
\mu
on
\mathbb{R}_{+}
. A
generalized graphon

W
is a non-negative, bounded, and symmetric function on
\mathbb{R}_{+}^{2}
. Moreover, we require
W\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R}_{+}^{2})
. We assume that
\lVert W\rVert_{1}>0
, unless otherwise stated.

As
W
is assumed to be bounded, it belongs to
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+}^{2})
as well
[
28
, p.126 Remarks (iii)]
. In many applications, the upper bound of
W
can be chosen to be
1
, as values of
W(\cdot,\cdot)
are used as the probability of the existence of edges.

We denote the
1
-norm and
2
-norm of a generalized graphon
W
as
\lVert W\rVert_{1}
and
\lVert W\rVert_{2}
respectively. To handle sparse graph sequences, we introduce the concept of a
stretched graphon

W^{\mathfrak{s}}
(cf.
[
26
]
), obtained by stretching
W
with a factor of
\lVert W\rVert_{1}^{1/2}
.
The stretched graphon
W^{\mathfrak{s}}
is defined as

\displaystyle W^{\mathfrak{s}}(x,y)=W(\lVert W\rVert_{1}^{1/2}x,\lVert W\rVert_{1}^{1/2}y).

(1)

This transformation allows us to convert a sparse graphon into a denser one with a fixed
1
-norm of
1
. For convenience, for the rest of the paper, a graphon defined on
[0,1]^{2}
is called a
classical graphon
.
In some places, we adopt the following convention if we want to emphasize the stretch factor.

Notations
. Given
r>0
and a function
f
, we use
f^{r}
to denote
f
stretched
with respect to (w.r.t.)

r
, i.e.,
f^{r}(\mathbf{x})=f(r\mathbf{x})
.

An illustration is given in
Fig.

1
. The motivation for introducing a stretch is that if
W
is sparse in the sense that
\lVert W\rVert_{1}
is very small, then
W^{\mathfrak{s}}
is a dense variant whose
1
-norm is always
1
. It is important to note that the addition of two stretched graphons does not yield the same result as stretching the sum of the original graphons, i.e., in general for

, we have
(W_{1}+W_{2})^{\mathfrak{s}}\neq W_{1}^{\mathfrak{s}}+W_{2}^{\mathfrak{s}}
.

This exemplifies a subtlety involved in generalizing signal processing methods under the classical graphon framework to the generalized graphon framework. We need to carefully handle stretch factors associated with different graphons and signals, and the results in
[
22
]
no longer apply directly. In subsequent sections, we develop additional regularity conditions on graphons and signals. Moreover, on
\mathbb{R}_{+}
or
\mathbb{R}_{+}^{2}
, we need to (cf.
[
26
]
) circumvent the non-applicability of certain classical results on compact domains
[
21
]
.

To compare graphons, the cut distance derived from the cut norm is usually used. As we need to consider functions on
\mathbb{R}_{+}^{2}
of the form
W_{1}-W_{2}
, we do not require
W
to be non-negative in the following definition.

Definition 2
.

The
cut norm
of
W
is defined as

\displaystyle\lVert W\rVert_{\square}=\sup_{U,V\subset\mathbb{R}_{+}}\left\lvert\int_{U\times V}W(x,y)\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}y\right\rvert,

where
\sup
is taken over measurable subsets
U,V
of
\mathbb{R}_{+}
.

Key examples of graphons are constructed from finite graphs.

Definition 3
.

Let
G=(V,E)
be a finite graph of size

with

. The
canonical graphon

W_{G}
associated with

is the step function supported on
[0,1]^{2}
as follows. Divide
[0,1]
into
n
equal subintervals
(I_{l})_{1\leq l\leq n}
of the same length
1/n
. For
x\in I_{i},y\in I_{j}
, let
W_{G}(x,y)=1
if
(v_{i},v_{j})\in E
, and
W_{G}(x,y)=0

In this paper, we extend its domain to
\mathbb{R}_{+}^{2}
by defining
W_{G}(x,y)=0
for
(x,y)\notin[0,1]^{2}
.

For the canonical graphon associated with
G
, its
1
-norm is

\displaystyle\lVert W_{G}\rVert_{1}=2|E|\cdot\frac{1}{n^{2}}=2\mathcal{E}(G),

(2)

as each small square in

and

Apparently,

depends on the ordering of the nodes, and in many applications, we do not want to distinguish

27

Definition 4
.

We define

\displaystyle\delta_{\square}(W_{1},W_{2})=\inf_{\phi_{1},\phi_{2}}d_{\square}(W_{1}^{\phi_{1}},W_{2}^{\phi_{2}}),

\displaystyle d_{\square,\mathfrak{s}}(W_{1},W_{2})=d_{\square}(W_{1}^{\mathfrak{s}},W_{2}^{\mathfrak{s}}),\text{ and }

\displaystyle\delta_{\square,\mathfrak{s}}(W_{1},W_{2})=\delta_{\square}(W_{1}^{\mathfrak{s}},W_{2}^{\mathfrak{s}}).

The latter is called the

Notice that converging in

implies that in
\delta_{\square}
. Similarly, converging in
d_{\square,\mathfrak{s}}
implies that in
\delta_{\square,\mathfrak{s}}
. To illustrate, we continue from

For convenience, let

The stretched version

III
Operator norm convergence

In this section, we study the convergence of operators on graphon signals. We first introduce the operator norm and then discuss the convergence of operators. To start, we have the following simple observation.

Lemma 1
.

For the operator
T_{W^{\mathfrak{s}}}
, we have

\displaystyle\lVert T_{W^{\mathfrak{s}}}f\rVert_{2}\leq\frac{\lVert W\rVert_{2}}{\lVert W\rVert_{1}}\lVert f\rVert_{2}.

As a consequence,
W
induces a
bounded linear operator

T_{W^{\mathfrak{s}}}:L^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+})\to L^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+})
.
To analyze
T_{W^{\mathfrak{s}}}
, it is natural to consider the operator norm.

Definition 5
.

Let
\lVert W\rVert_{2,2,\mathfrak{s}}
denote the operator norm of
T_{W^{\mathfrak{s}}}:L^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+})\to L^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+})
.
Similarly, we define
\lVert W\rVert_{2,2}
to be the operator norm of
T_{W}
.

From the lemma above, we see
\lVert W\rVert_{2,2,\mathfrak{s}}\leq\lVert W\rVert_{2}/\lVert W\rVert_{1}.

We have the following regarding operator norm convergence.

Theorem 1
.

If
(W_{n})_{n>0}
is a sequence of uniformly bounded generalized graphons converging to
W
in
d_{\square,\mathfrak{s}}
, then they also converge to
W
in
\lVert\cdot\rVert_{2,2,\mathfrak{s}}
. Moreover, if
(W_{n})_{n>0}
converges to
W
in
\delta_{\square,\mathfrak{s}}
, then there is a sequence of measure preserving
\phi_{n}
such that
{W_{n}^{\mathfrak{s}}}^{\phi_{n}}\to W^{\mathfrak{s}}
in
\lVert\cdot\rVert_{2,2}
.

In GSP and GNNs, polynomial filters are commonly used. In the context of graphons, we can adopt polynomial filters by considering a polynomial
P
, and obtaining
P(T_{W^{\mathfrak{s}}})
by plugging
T_{W^{\mathfrak{s}}}
into
P
, where the (operator) composition serves as the multiplication operation.

Corollary 1
.

Let
P
be a polynomial. If
(W_{n})_{n>0}
is a sequence of uniformly bounded generalized graphons converging to
W
in
d_{\square,\mathfrak{s}}
, then
\big(P(T_{W_{n}^{\mathfrak{s}}})\big)_{n>0}
converges to
P(T_{W^{\mathfrak{s}}})
in the operator norm
\lVert\cdot\rVert_{2,2}
as operators
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+})\to L^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+})
.

In practice, we also need to deal with the situation when the signal
f
is approximated by a sequence
(f_{n})_{n>0}
.

Corollary 2
.

Suppose
(W_{n})_{n>0}
is a sequence of uniformly bounded generalized graphons converging to

in

. If
(f_{n})_{n>0}
is a sequence in

that converges to

, then
(T_{W_{n}^{\mathfrak{s}}}f_{n})_{n>0}
converges to

in
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+})
.

We aim to obtain samples of finite graphs with associated graph signals from a given generalized graphon and a signal.

(or
\delta_{\square,\mathfrak{s}}
) of generalized graphons and the

-norm convergence of signals, respectively. We explore these directions in detail in the following two sections.

Recall

We denote the eigenvalues and orthonormal eigenvectors of

\Lambda_{W}=\{\lambda_{t}:t\neq 0\}
and
\Phi_{W}=\{\varphi_{t}(x):t\neq 0\}

The eigenvalues

\displaystyle\lambda_{1}(W)\geq\lambda_{2}(W)\geq\dots 0,\text{ and }\lambda_{-1}(W)\leq\lambda_{-2}(W)\leq\dots 0.

It can be shown by

that

can be decomposed as

\displaystyle W(x,x^{\prime})=\sum_{t\in\mathbb{Z}\backslash\{0\}}\lambda_{t}\varphi_{t}(x)\varphi_{t}(x^{\prime}).

(6)

Consider the sampled graph
G_{m,n}
from

1

m,n

A_{m,n}
of
G_{m,n}
. More specifically, we label the eigenvalues of the symmetric matrix

by

\displaystyle\lambda_{-1,m,n}\leq\lambda_{-2,m,n}\leq\ldots\leq 0\leq\ldots\leq\lambda_{2,m,n}\leq\lambda_{1,m,n}.

We have the following spectral convergence result (cf.
[
31
]
).

Corollary 4
.

Under the same assumptions as
2

\displaystyle\lim_{m\to\infty}\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{\lambda_{t,m,n}}{\sqrt{2|E_{m,n}|}}=\frac{\lambda_{t}}{\sqrt{\lVert W\rVert_{1}}}.

(7)

We end this section by generalizing
1

\displaystyle h(T_{W^{\mathfrak{s}}})f=\sum_{t\in\mathbb{Z}\backslash\{0\}}h(\overline{\lambda_{t}})\langle f,\overline{\varphi_{t}}\rangle\overline{\varphi_{t}}.

Notice
\overline{\varphi_{t}}(x)=\varphi_{t}(\sqrt{\lVert W\rVert_{1}}x)

Theorem 4
.

Suppose

of a bounded open interval

\displaystyle\lim_{m\to\infty}\lim_{n\to\infty}h(T_{W_{m,n}^{\mathfrak{s}}})=h(T_{W^{\mathfrak{s}}}).

VIII
Conclusion

We have presented a signal processing framework based on the theory of generalized graphons, which include limit objects of sparse sequences of graphs with respect to the stretched cut distance. This framework is suitable for various types of large real-world networks. We have obtained operator convergence results for sequences of graphons that converge in the stretched cut distance. Additionally, we have proposed a sampling scheme for generalized graphons and demonstrated convergence results for sampled graphs, their spectra, and their signals.

An interesting future work is to extend this signal processing framework to incorporate the graphex model
[
26
,
27
]
. The graphex model includes not only the "graphon component" discussed in this paper but also the "star component" and the "dust component," which represent star subgraphs and isolated edges, respectively. These components collectively describe the "non-core parts" of graphs. By incorporating the graphex model, we aim to further enhance our signal processing framework for a broader range of graph structures.

It converges to

and

On each interval

, let

\displaystyle|g_{m,n,k}(x)-f_{m}^{\prime}(x)|\leq\sup_{l}-\inf_{l}.

Moreover, by (b) above, the number of points in

deviates from the average by at most

\displaystyle\int_{I_{l}}|g_{m,n,k}(x)-f_{m}^{\prime}(x)|\,\mathrm{d}x\leq\frac{1}{k}(\sup_{l}-\inf_{l})+2\cdot\frac{2b_{f}}{n}.

\displaystyle\lVert g_{m,n,k}-f_{m}^{\prime}\rVert_{1}\leq\sum_{1\leq l\leq k}|I_{l}|(\sup_{l}-\inf_{l})+\frac{4kb_{f}}{n}.

Therefore, as

is
a.e. cts

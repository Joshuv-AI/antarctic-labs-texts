Exploring quantum criticality and ergodicity-breaking dynamics in spin-1 Kitaev chains via single-ion anisotropies
Wen-Yi Zhang, Qing-Min Hu, Jie Ren, Liangsheng Li, Wen-Long You

Abstract
We investigate topological gauge-theory terms and quantum criticality in a spin-1 Kitaev chain with general single-ion anisotropies (SIAs). The ground-state phase diagram, including the Kitaev spin liquid (KSL) and gapless dimer phases, is determined by the infinite time evolving block decimation (iTEBD) method. A quantum phase transition between the KSL and dimer phases occurs by varying uniaxial SIA, analogous to the confinement-deconfinement transition in the lattice Schwinger model with a topological θ \theta angle of π \pi . Introducing rhombic SIA shifts this angle from π \pi , resulting in y y - and x x -ferroquadrupole phases. The transition between these phases can occur through a crossover in the KSL phase or a genuine phase transition along a deconfined line. We map the spin-1 Hamiltonian to an effective spin-1/2 PXP Hamiltonian, with uniaxial SIA corresponding to uniform detuning and rhombic SIA to staggered detuning. We explore the hierarchical fragmentation of the Hilbert space, revealing that quantum many-body scars (QMBSs) emerge under weak uniform detuning, while slow dynamics under large staggered detuning is accurately captured by a second-order effective Hamiltonian via the Schrieffer-Wolff transformation. Our work establishes a framework for simulating topological θ \theta angles and ergodicity-breaking dynamics, bridging higher-spin generalizations of scarred models with lattice gauge theories, potentially realizable using state-of-the-art cold-atom quantum simulators.

This mapping establishes a clear connection between the quantum critical points of the Kitaev model and the non-ergodic dynamics in the PXP model. The results not only clarify the interplay between quantum criticality and non-ergodicity but also provide experimentally testable insights for realizing topological gauge-theory terms in platforms such as Rydberg atom arrays.

Effect of tunable single-ion anisotropy for
\phi=3\pi/4

\displaystyle\hat{H}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{N/2}(S_{2j-1}^{x}S_{2j}^{x}+S_{2j}^{y}S_{2j+1}^{y})

\displaystyle\frac{A_{r}\sin\theta}{\sqrt{2}}\sum_{j=1}^{N}\left[(S_{j}^{x})^{2}-(S_{j}^{y})^{2}\right]+A_{r}\cos\theta\sum_{j=1}^{N}(S_{j}^{z})^{2},\quad\;

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}^{xx}_{1,2}=\mathcal{C}^{yy}_{2,3},\mathcal{C}^{yy}_{1,2}=\mathcal{C}^{xx}_{2,3},\mathcal{C}^{zz}_{1,2}=\mathcal{C}^{zz}_{2,3}.

\phi=3\pi/4

\displaystyle U

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\prod_{j}\exp(i\pi S_{2j}^{x})\exp(i\frac{\pi}{2}S_{2j}^{z}).

\displaystyle K\sum_{j=1}^{N}S_{j}^{x}S_{j+1}^{y}+\frac{A_{r}\sin\theta}{\sqrt{2}}\sum_{j=1}^{N}(-1)^{j}\left[(S_{j}^{x})^{2}-(S_{j}^{y})^{2}\right]

\displaystyle A_{r}\cos\theta\sum_{j=1}^{N}(S_{j}^{z})^{2}.

\displaystyle\tilde{W}_{j}=\Sigma_{j}^{y}\Sigma_{j+1}^{x},

(13)

The Hilbert space

component of the dimer order parameter progressively rises from zero to a non-zero value, signaling a second-order transition at
\theta=\pi,A_{rc}=0.655
. It is observed that the screw symmetry (
6
) is broken after
A_{r}>A_{rc}
due to the imbalance between
\mathcal{C}^{yy}_{2,3}
and
\mathcal{C}^{xx}_{1,2}
in the dimer phase. The other components of the two-point spin-spin correlations remain zero and thus result in the
y
and
z
components of the dimer order parameter staying zero. The two-spin correlation functions shown in Fig.
2
(c) demonstrate that the local gauge symmetries of the model strictly determine which expectation values can be zero or non-zero, as explained in Appendix
A
.
The detection of non-zero dimer correlations prior to reaching the critical point within the dimer phase can be attributed to the limitations imposed by the finite bond dimension. In the thermodynamic limit, the ground state for the dimer phase exhibits a two-fold degeneracy, contrasting with the gapped ground state observed in the KSL phase.

IV.2
The east-west direction

Next, we consider another limit
A_{r}\to\infty
(equivalently
K\to 0
), focusing on the scenario where
\theta=3\pi/2
. In this case, the Hamiltonian (
12
) simplifies to a form due to the dominance of the staggered rhombic SIA (
A_{x}=-A_{y}\gg K
and
A_{z}=0
):

\displaystyle\tilde{H}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{N}S_{j}^{x}S_{j+1}^{y}+\frac{A_{r}}{\sqrt{2}}\sum_{j=1}^{N}(-1)^{j+1}\left[(S_{j}^{x})^{2}-(S_{j}^{y})^{2}\right].\quad\quad

(15)

Notably, the condition
\theta=\pi/2
can similarly be obtained by a direct spatial translation from
\theta=3\pi/2
.
In the limit
A_{r}\to\infty
, the system can be analyzed perturbatively using an effective Hamiltonian approach based on the Schrieffer-Wolff transformation
[
66
]
. Considering
(S_{j}^{a})^{2}=(1-\Sigma_{j}^{a})/2

(a=x,y,z)
,
we rewrite Eq. (
15
) as
\tilde{H}=\tilde{H}_{0}+\tilde{V}
,
where

\displaystyle\tilde{H}_{0}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{A_{r}}{2\sqrt{2}}\sum_{j=1}^{N}(-1)^{j+1}(\Sigma_{j}^{y}-\Sigma_{j}^{x}),

(16)

and

\displaystyle\tilde{V}=\sum_{j=1}^{N}S_{j}^{x}S_{j+1}^{y}.

(17)

To obtain the effective Hamiltonian, we apply a unitary transformation generated by an anti-Hermitian operator
S
:

\displaystyle\tilde{H}_{\rm{eff}}=e^{S}\tilde{H}e^{-S}\equiv\tilde{H}_{0}+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\tilde{H}_{\rm{eff}}^{(n)},

(18)

where each term
\tilde{H}_{\rm{eff}}^{(n)}
is of order
(1/A_{r})^{n}
.
The Schrieffer-Wolff generator
S
is formulated to ensure

[\tilde{H}_{\rm{eff}}^{(n)},\tilde{H}_{0}]=0
for any
n
.
This is achieved by expanding
S=\sum_{m=1}^{{\infty}}S^{(m)}
where each
S^{(m)}
takes the order
(1/A_{r})^{m}
.
Typically, selecting
S\equiv S^{(1)}
simplifies the first order term

\tilde{H}_{\rm{eff}}^{(1)}=0
, and allows the computation of higher-order terms based on
S^{(1)}
and
\tilde{H}_{0}
.
In this case, we can obtain

\displaystyle\tilde{H}_{\rm{eff}}\!

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\tilde{H}_{0}+\mathcal{P}([S^{(1)},\tilde{H}_{0}]+\tilde{V})\mathcal{P}

(19)

\displaystyle+\!\mathcal{P}([S^{(1)},\!\tilde{V}]\!+\!\frac{1}{2!}[S^{(1)},\![S^{(1)},\!\tilde{H}_{0}]])\mathcal{P}\!+\!\cdots\!,

where
\mathcal{P}
is the projection operator that maps each term in the expression above onto
the eigenbasis of
\tilde{H}_{0}
,
specifically projecting onto the subspace where all
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
gauge charges are positive, corresponding to the diagonal entries in the site parity matrices defined in Eq. (
14
).
The requirement for
\tilde{H}_{\rm{eff}}^{(1)}=0
implies that
S^{(1)}
must satisfy

\displaystyle[S^{(1)},\tilde{H}_{0}]+\tilde{V}=0.

(20)

This is satisfied by choosing:

\displaystyle S^{(1)}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}A_{r}}\sum_{j=1}^{N}(-1)^{j+1}S_{j}^{x}\Sigma_{j}^{z}S_{j+1}^{y}.

(21)

It is straightforward to verify that
[S^{(1)},\tilde{W}_{j}]=0
.
The leading-order effective Hamiltonian, up to second order, then becomes:

\displaystyle\tilde{H}_{\rm{eff}}^{(2)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{4\sqrt{2}A_{r}}\sum_{j=1}^{N}(-1)^{j}(\Sigma_{j}^{z}-\Sigma_{j}^{y})(1-\Sigma_{j+1}^{y}).

(22)

See Appendix
B
for details. When expanded up to the second order, the effective Hamiltonian (
22
) exhibits a diagonal structure in the specified bases (
2
), representing an emergent conserved quantity enforced by the Schrieffer-Wolff transformation. The underlying reasoning for this will be further elucidated in the spin-1/2 representation discussed in Sec.
V.2
.

V
Mapping to spin-1/2 extended PXP model

In the context of a spin-1 Kitaev chain, it was established that the ground state resides in the sector where all
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
gauge charges are positive, represented by
\vec{w}=\{1,1,\cdots,1\}
. Interestingly, this fractal dimension-based subspace is the largest among all possible sectors. In this constrained Hilbert space, there is a one-to-one mapping between the permissible two-site configurations
\langle j,j+1\rangle
on the primary lattice and the corresponding dual lattice
[
45
]
, where a spin-
1/2
degree of freedom is located at the bond center, denoted as
i\equiv j+1/2
. This site-to-bond mapping rule schematically illustrated in Fig.
3
(a). Within the sector with all positive gauge charges, the Hamiltonian in Eq. (
12
) can be exactly mapped to a compact form in the spin-1/2 representation:

\displaystyle\hat{H}_{\rm ePXP}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{N}P_{i-1}X_{i}P_{i+1}+2A_{r}\cos\theta\sum_{i=1}^{N}P_{i-1}n_{i}P_{i+1}

(23)

\displaystyle-

\displaystyle\sqrt{2}A_{r}\sin\theta\sum_{i=1}^{N}(-1)^{i}P_{i-1}n_{i}P_{i+1},

where
X=|\downarrow\rangle\langle\uparrow|+|\uparrow\rangle\langle\downarrow|
and
P=|\downarrow\rangle\langle\downarrow|
acts as a projector that ensures neighboring sites are not both in the excited state, and
n=1-P=|\uparrow\rangle\langle\uparrow|
. Here, the uniform detuning in Eq. (
23
) is equivalent to the uniaxial SIA in the spin-1 Kitaev model, while the staggered detuning corresponds to the rhombic SIA.

V.1
Effect of uniform detuning

Parallel to the discussion in Sec.
IV.1
, tuning
A_{r}
along the axis where
\theta=\pi
in Hamiltonian Eq. (
23
) reveals two distinct phases, which become apparent when considering two limiting cases. In the limit of large uniform detuning
A_{r}
, the system energetically favors atoms in the excited state.
However, the Rydberg blockade prevents neighboring atoms from simultaneously occupying the excited state. As a result, the most energetically favorable configuration is for half of the atoms to be in the excited state, with the Rydberg atoms occupying every other site in an alternating sequence. This configuration leads to an antiferromagnetic spin configuration in the ground state
|\mathbb{Z}_{2}\rangle=|\uparrow\downarrow\uparrow\downarrow\cdots\rangle
or
|\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{\prime}\rangle=|\downarrow\uparrow\downarrow\uparrow\cdots\rangle
, which spontaneously breaks the global
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
symmetry, resulting in double degeneracy.

In the opposite limit, where
A_{r}=0
, the ground state of the pure PXP model is gapped and exhibits exponentially decaying correlations in local observables. The Hamiltonian for the pure PXP model is given by

\displaystyle\hat{H}_{\rm{PXP}}=\sum_{i=1}^{N}P_{i-1}X_{i}P_{i+1}.

(24)

In this limit, the Néel state shows significant overlap with the high-energy scarred eigenstates. The Rydberg blockade imposes kinetic constraints crucial for the atypical dynamics observed in QMBS states. At
t=0
, the system is initialized in product states
|\psi(0)\rangle
and evolves as
|\psi(t)\rangle=\exp(-i\hat{H}_{\rm PXP}t)|\psi(0)\rangle
. Quantum quenches from
|\mathbb{Z}_{2}\rangle
or
|\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{\prime}\rangle
reveal periodic revivals in quantum fidelity:

\displaystyle F(t)=|\langle\psi(0)|\psi(t)\rangle|^{2}.

(25)

These oscillations and non-ergodic behavior stem from the equal spacing of QMBS eigenstates in Eq. (
24
). A uniform detuning field transitions the system from a QMBS regime to a quantum critical regime as its strength increases. The initially non-thermal scar states can be tracked into the quantum critical regime by tuning the field. As the system approaches the critical point, it shows increased thermalization tendencies, with scar states evolving into low-energy critical states with low entanglement entropy
[
67
,
68
]
. The von Neumann entanglement entropy for a subsystem consisting of
\ell
sites is defined as

\displaystyle S_{\ell}=-{\rm tr}(\rho_{\ell}^{(n)}\log_{2}\rho_{\ell}^{(n)}),

(26)

where
\rho_{\ell}^{(n)}
is the reduced density matrix obtained by tracing out the
N-\ell
sites of the complement system from the density matrix
\rho^{(n)}=|\chi_{n}\rangle\langle\chi_{n}|
of each
n
th eigenstate
|\chi_{n}\rangle
.

When
\theta
in Eq. (
23
) deviates from
\pi
, the staggered potential, which differentiates on-site energies between even and odd sites, selectively stabilizes one of the degenerate states. This asymmetry inhibits the occurrence of a quantum phase transition. The phase structure of the spin-1/2 extended PXP model is in perfect correspondence with the phase diagram of the spin-1 Kitaev model, as depicted in Fig.
1
(c).
Thus, the Kitaev model is an ideal candidate for simulating the lattice Schwinger model, where the parameter
\theta
acts like the topological angle. The exact site-to-bond mapping between the Kitaev and PXP models within the ground-state manifold, where the
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
gauge charge
w_{j}=1
for all bonds, ensures that the
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
gauge condition in LGT is automatically met, thus establishing equivalence between the lattice Schwinger model and the Kitaev model.
According to Eq. (
23
), both the mass of the matter field and the topological angle can be experimentally controlled in the Kitaev model, enabling exploration of the phase diagram from multiple perspectives.

V.2
Effect of staggered detuning

Next, we focus on the effect of the staggered detuning, specifically at
\theta=3\pi/2
, as discussed in Sec.
IV.2
. In this case, the Hamiltonian for the staggered detuned PXP model is given by

\displaystyle\hat{H}_{\rm{sdPXP}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{N}\!P_{i-1}X_{i}P_{i+1}\!+\!\sqrt{2}A_{r}\!\sum_{i=1}^{N}\!(-1)^{i}\!P_{i-1}n_{i}P_{i+1}.\quad\quad

(27)

Figure
4
shows the half-chain entanglement entropy
S_{N/2}
as a function of eigenenergy
E
for different values of the staggered detuning
A_{r}
. At low
A_{r}
[Fig.
4
(a)], the spectrum is populated by a large number of non-degenerate states. As
A_{r}
increases, the energy levels become more uniformly spaced
[
69
]
. Figure
4
(b) shows a mix of states, including anomalous states with low
S_{N/2}
and typical thermal state with high
S_{N/2}
, within each primary cluster.
At sufficiently large
A_{r}
, the eigenstates of the effective Hamiltonian in Eq. (
27
) organize into nearly equidistant clusters, as depicted in Fig.
4
(c).
Figure
5
shows the energy level distribution for
\hat{H}_{\rm{sdPXP}}
at a moderate
A_{r}
, which clearly identifies several primary sets of energy levels, each separated by
\sqrt{2}A_{r}
.
The energy levels are organized into
N+1
distinct sectors. This classification hinges on the staggered occupancy number defined by:

\displaystyle n_{\rm s}=\sum_{j=1}^{N}(-1)^{j}n_{j}.

(28)

The staggered occupancy number acts as a metric analogous to the Hamming distance, differentiating states across these sectors, as illustrated in Fig.
3
(b). It effectively defines physical boundaries between
|\mathbb{Z}_{2}\rangle
and
|\mathbb{Z}^{\prime}_{2}\rangle
states.
Recently the non-Hermitian scar has been proposed
[
70
]
, where the Hamming distance serves as a proxy for spatial coordinates, and these states function as physical boundaries.
Within primary clusters, secondary clustering occurs with states spaced at intervals of
1/\sqrt{2}A_{r}
. As
A_{r}
increases towards infinity, the degeneracy of the system markedly increases.

Eigenstate clustering in the nonintegrable model of Eq. (
27
) under large staggered detuning
A_{r}
, which leads to violation of ETH at intermediate frequencies, can be understood through the Schrieffer-Wolff Hamiltonian.
According to the site-to-bond mapping rule, Eq. (
16
) can be rewritten as

\displaystyle\hat{H}_{\rm{eff}}^{(0)}\!

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\!\sqrt{2}A_{r}\sum_{i=1}^{N}(-1)^{i}P_{i-1}n_{i}P_{i+1}\equiv\sqrt{2}A_{r}\hat{H}_{\rm{sPnP}},\quad

(29)

and Eq. (
22
) is recast into

\displaystyle\hat{H}_{\rm{eff}}^{(2)}\!=\!\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}A_{r}}\!\sum_{i=1}^{N}(-1)^{i}\!P_{i-1}\!Z_{i}P_{i+1}\!\equiv\!\frac{1}{2\sqrt{2}A_{r}}\!\hat{H}_{\rm{sPZP}}.

(30)

Meanwhile, the Schrieffer-Wolff generator
S^{(1)}
to the spin-1/2 system can also be mapped as

\displaystyle S^{(1)}\!=\!\frac{i}{\sqrt{2}A_{r}}\!\sum_{i=1}^{N}(-1)^{i}P_{i-1}Y_{i}P_{i+1}\!\equiv\!\frac{i}{\sqrt{2}A_{r}}\hat{H}_{\rm{sPYP}}.

(31)

The formation of primary clusters is attributed to the nearly integrable nature of the zero-order effective Hamiltonian
\hat{H}_{\rm{eff}}^{(0)}
in Eq. (
29
) under large staggered detuning. Secondary clusters, appearing at a much smaller quasi-energy scale, are generated by the second-order effective Hamiltonian
\hat{H}_{\rm{eff}}^{(2)}
in Eq. (
30
) at a large
A_{r}
. In fact, the diagonal structure can be interpreted as resulting from the roles of Eqs. (
24
), (
29
), and (
31
) as the generators ”X”, ”Z”, and ”Y” of a nearly SU(2) algebra. Specifically, the commutation relations are given by:

\displaystyle\left[\hat{H}_{\rm{sPnP}},\hat{H}_{\rm{PXP}}\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle i\hat{H}_{\rm{sPYP}},

(32a)

\displaystyle\left[\hat{H}_{\rm{sPnP}},\hat{H}_{\rm{sPYP}}\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-i\hat{H}_{\rm{PXP}},

(32b)

\displaystyle\left[\hat{H}_{\rm{PXP}},\hat{H}_{\rm{sPYP}}\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle i\hat{H}_{\rm{sPZP}}.

(32c)

It is interesting to note that the right-hand side of Eq. (
32c
) does not adhere strictly to the SU(2) algebra. This deviation is encapsulated by the expression:

\displaystyle\hat{H}_{\rm{sPZP}}=(\hat{H}_{\rm{sPnP}}+O_{zzz}),

(33)

where
O_{zzz}=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i=1}^{N}(-1)^{i}Z_{i-1}Z_{i}Z_{i+1}
represents a residual contribution. Such
nearly SU(2) algebra was studied in the context of QMBS in the PXP model
in Refs.
[
37
,
71
,
38
]
. The term ”nearly” SU(2) refers to the fact that while certain aspects of the system exhibit similarities to an SU(2) algebra, deviations occur due to the presence of additional generators. The algebraic counterpart for Eq. (
15
) is detailed in Appendix
B
.

VI
Summary

In summary, we study a one-dimensional spin-1 Kitaev model with three-component single-ion anisotropies (SIAs). To effectively describe the effects of SIAs on ground-state properties and far-from-equilibrium dynamics, we use a three-dimensional coordinate, i.e.,
A_{x}\!=\!A_{r}\sin\theta\cos\phi
,
A_{y}\!=\!A_{r}\sin\theta\sin\phi
and
A_{z}\!=\!A_{r}\cos\theta
. At zero SIA field (
A_{r}=0
), the local bond operators defined in (
13
) of the pure Kitaev chain are conserved with eigenvalues
w_{j}=\pm 1
. Even in the presence of SIAs, these local bond operators retain their conservation, playing a role of the gauge charge. In terms of the infinite time evolving block decimation (iTEBD) method, the comprehensive phase diagram is given. In the zero SIA field limit, the Kitaev chain hosts the Kitaev spin liquid (KSL) ground state, characterized by extremely short-ranged spin-spin correlations. At
\theta=\pi
, where the
\phi
is irrelevant, a phase transition is triggered by increasing
A_{r}
from the KSL phase to the dimer phase. Due to the spontaneous breaking of global
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
symmetry, the two-fold degenerate dimer phase is analogous to the deconfined phase in the lattice Schwinger model, which incorporates a celebrated topological
\theta
angle of
\pi
. To this end, the KSL-dimer transition is equivalent to the confinement-deconfinement transition. When a translation-symmetry-breaking term is added, the topological angle
\theta
starts to deviate from
\pi
. In this case, such Coleman’s transition no longer exists and the deconfined line disappears as a consequence of charge-parity symmetry breaking. We here find that the polar angle
\theta
can act as the topological angle in the U(1) lattice gauge theory. When
\phi=\pi/4
, the deconfined line expands into a semi-infinite region, while for other
\phi
values, it remains a simple deconfined line.

We focus on the scenario where

It has been recognized the spin-1/2 PXP model is embedded in the ground-state manifold of the spin-1 Kitaev model, where the topological charges are
+1

The quantum quench from either

For the PXP model with spatially uniform detuning, the mapping to U(1) lattice gauge theory is that the system hosts a regime exhibiting quark-antiquark confinement, which provides a route to investigate confinement in continuous gauge theories with quantum simulators. Intriguingly, for the case of the PXP model with staggered detuning, a Schrieffer-Wolff transformation adopted in the large staggered detuning enforces an additional conservation law for the staggered occupation number

Returning to the spin-1 Kitaev chain, due to the existence of the conserved gauge charges
\hat{W}_{j}
(

Our work not only introduces a theoretical perspective in simulating the topological

We here show the ground-state expectation values of certain operators can be analysed through symmetry considerations. Under the unitary transformation (
11
), the three components of the dimer order parameter in Eq.(

) can be expressed by

\displaystyle\tilde{O}_{D}^{x}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\langle{S}_{2}^{x}{S}_{3}^{y}\rangle-\langle{S}_{1}^{x}{S}_{2}^{y}\rangle,

\displaystyle\tilde{O}_{D}^{y}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\langle{S}_{2}^{y}{S}_{3}^{x}\rangle-\langle{S}_{1}^{y}{S}_{2}^{x}\rangle,

\displaystyle\tilde{O}_{D}^{z}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\langle{S}_{2}^{z}{S}_{3}^{z}\rangle-\langle{S}_{1}^{z}{S}_{2}^{z}\rangle.

(35)

Recalling that
\tilde{W}_{j}=e^{i\pi S_{j}^{y}}e^{i\pi S_{j+1}^{x}}
commutes components in the dimer order parameter, such as

,
S_{j}^{y}S_{j+1}^{x}
and
S_{j}^{z}S_{j+1}^{z}
. To this end, we can apply the action of

\displaystyle\tilde{W}_{j+1}=e^{i\pi S_{j+1}^{y}}e^{i\pi S_{j+2}^{x}}

on the components in the dimer order parameter, which arrives

\displaystyle\tilde{W}_{j+1}S_{j}^{x}S_{j+1}^{y}\tilde{W}_{j+1}^{-1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle S_{j}^{x}S_{j+1}^{y},

(36)

\displaystyle\tilde{W}_{j+1}S_{j}^{y}S_{j+1}^{x}\tilde{W}_{j+1}^{-1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-S_{j}^{y}S_{j+1}^{x},

(37)

\displaystyle\tilde{W}_{j+1}S_{j}^{z}S_{j+1}^{z}\tilde{W}_{j+1}^{-1}

\displaystyle-S_{j}^{z}S_{j+1}^{z}.

(38)

commutes with the Hamiltonian Eq. (

Schrieffer-Wolff transformation and nearly SU(2) algebra in spin-1 systems

The derivation process is as follows. First, we define the projection operator that projects the Hamiltonian to the flux-free sector
\vec{w}{=}\{1,1,\cdots,1\}

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}=\prod_{j}(\frac{\Sigma_{j}^{y}\Sigma_{j+1}^{x}+\mathbbm{1}}{2}).

(41)

The requirement for
\tilde{H}_{\rm{eff}}^{(1)}=0
implies that
S^{(1)}
must satisfy
[S^{(1)},\tilde{H}_{0}]+\tilde{V}=0.
Next, we calculate the commutation relations in the constrained Hilbert space

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}[S^{(1)},H_{0}]\mathcal{P}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\sum_{j=1}^{N}\left(\mathcal{P}\left[(-1)^{j+1}S_{j}^{x}\Sigma_{j}^{z}S_{j+1}^{y},(-1)^{j+1}\left((S_{j}^{x})^{2}-(S_{j}^{y})^{2}\right)\right]\right.\mathcal{P}

\displaystyle+\left.\mathcal{P}\left[(-1)^{j+1}S_{j}^{x}\Sigma_{j}^{z}S_{j+1}^{y},(-1)^{j+2}\left((S_{j+1}^{x})^{2}-(S_{j+1}^{y})^{2}\right)\right]\mathcal{P}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\left(-S_{j}^{x}S_{j+1}^{y}-S_{j}^{x}S_{j+1}^{y}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\tilde{V}.

(42)

Therefore, the selected
S^{(1)}
satisfies
[S^{(1)},H_{0}]+V=0
. The second-order effective Hamiltonian can be calculated as

\displaystyle\tilde{H}_{\rm{eff}}^{(2)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\mathcal{P}[S^{(1)},V]\mathcal{P}

(43)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\mathcal{P}\left[\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}A_{r}}\sum_{j=1}^{N}(-1)^{j+1}S_{j}^{x}\Sigma_{j}^{z}S_{j+1}^{y},\sum_{j=1}^{N}S_{j}^{x}S_{j+1}^{y}\right]\mathcal{P}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}A_{r}}\sum_{j=1}^{N}(-1)^{j}(S_{j}^{x})^{2}\Sigma_{j}^{z}(S_{j+1}^{y})^{2}

\displaystyle\frac{1}{4\sqrt{2}A_{r}}\sum_{j=1}^{N}(-1)^{j}(\Sigma_{j}^{z}-\Sigma_{j}^{y})(1-\Sigma_{j+1}^{y}).

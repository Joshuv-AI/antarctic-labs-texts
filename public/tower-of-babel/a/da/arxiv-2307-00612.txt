Reynolds number scaling and inner-outer overlap of stream-wise Reynolds stress in wall turbulence
Peter A. Monkewitz 1

Abstract
The scaling of Reynolds stresses in turbulent wall-bounded flows is the subject of a long running debate. In the near-wall “inner” region, a sizeable group, inspired by the “attached eddy model”, has advocated the unlimited growth of ⟨ u ​ u ⟩ + \langle uu\rangle^{+} and in particular of its inner peak at y + ≊ 15 y^{+}\approxeq 15 , with ln ⁡ Re τ \ln\mathrm{Re}_{\tau} (see e.g. Smits et al. 2021 , and references therein) . Only recently, Chen & Sreenivasan 2021 ; Chen & Sreenivasan 2022 have argued on the basis of bounded dissipation, that ⟨ u ​ u ⟩ + \langle uu\rangle^{+} remains finite in the inner near-wall region for Re τ → ∞ \mathrm{Re}_{\tau}\rightarrow\infty , with finite Reynolds number corrections of order Re τ − 1 / 4 \mathrm{Re}_{\tau}^{-1/4} . In this paper, the overlap between the two-term inner expansion f 0 ​ ( y + ) + f 1 ​ ( y + ) / Re τ 1 / 4 f_{0}(y^{+})+f_{1}(y^{+})/\mathrm{Re}_{\tau}^{1/4} of Monkewitz 2022 and the leading order outer expansion for ⟨ u ​ u ⟩ + \langle uu\rangle^{+} is shown to be of the form C 0 + C 1 ​ ( y + / Re τ ) 1 / 4 C_{0}+C_{1}\,(y^{+}/\mathrm{Re}_{\tau})^{1/4} . With a new indicator function, overlaps of this form are reliably identified in ⟨ u ​ u ⟩ + \langle uu\rangle^{+} profiles for channels and pipes, while the situation in boundary layers requires further clarification. On the other hand, the standard logarithmic indicator function, evaluated for the same data, shows no sign of a logarithmic law to connect an inner expansion of ⟨ u ​ u ⟩ + \langle uu\rangle^{+} growing as ln ⁡ Re τ \ln{\mathrm{Re}_{\tau}} to an outer expansion of order unity. 1
Introduction and outline of the problem

Dividing wall-normal profiles of turbulence statistics in wall bounded flows into inner and outer parts connected through an overlap, is intrinsically a concept of matched asymptotic expansions
(
Kevorkian & Cole 1981
;
Wilcox 1995
, abbreviated MAE, see e.g.)
. Its application to mean flow profiles can be traced back to the celebrated work of
von Kármán 1934
and
Millikan 1938
, who introduced the logarithmic overlap law for the mean velocity profile.

Here and in the following, the classical non-dimensionalization is adopted with the “inner” or viscous length scale
\widehat{\ell}\equiv(\widehat{\nu}/\widehat{u}_{\tau})
, and
\widehat{u}_{\tau}\equiv(\widehat{\tau}_{w}/\widehat{\rho})^{1/2}
,
\widehat{\rho}
and
\widehat{\nu}
the friction velocity, density and dynamic viscosity, respectively, with hats identifying dimensional quantities. The resulting non-dimensional inner and outer wall-normal coordinates are
y^{+}=\widehat{y}/\widehat{\ell}
and
Y=y^{+}/\mathrm{Re}_{\tau}
, respectively, with
\mathrm{Re}_{\tau}\equiv\widehat{L}/\widehat{\ell}
the friction Reynolds number and
\widehat{L}
the outer length scale, i.e. the channel half height, pipe radius or boundary layer thickness.

Relative to the mean velocity, the situation for the Reynolds stresses is reversed, as the
inner
parts vary significantly with
\mathrm{Re}_{\tau}
, while the outer parts quickly approach asymptotic profiles, with small finite Reynolds number corrections of order
\mathcal{O}(\mathrm{Re}_{\tau}^{-1})
or less. In the present paper, the discussion focusses on the
stream-wise Reynolds stress

\langle uu\rangle^{+}
, as it is the component with the most data available.

For this stream-wise component, the scaling of its inner part, and in particular of its inner peak height, is a subject of controversy. The two opposing views are summarized as follows:

•

The Reynolds stresses scale according to the “attached eddy” model, in the following abbreviated “AE” model. Its main characteristic is the unbounded increase, proportional to
\ln{\mathrm{Re}_{\tau}}
, of Reynolds stresses in the inner near-wall region. This model has been
first proposed by
Townsend 1976
, has recently been reviewed by
Marusic & Monty 2019
and has been extensively covered in the literature.

•

The Reynolds stresses remain finite in the limit of
\mathrm{Re}_{\tau}\!\to\!\infty
everywhere in the flow. For the zero-pressure-gradient turbulent boundary layer, in the following abbreviated ZPG TBL, this view has been advanced by
Monkewitz & Nagib 2015
. More recently, it has been further developed by
Chen & Sreenivasan 2021
;
Chen & Sreenivasan 2022
, who have argued, on the basis of the “law of bounded dissipation”, that in the inner, near-wall region, the finite Reynolds number corrections are of order
\mathcal{O}(\mathrm{Re}_{\tau}^{-1/4})
. This new scaling, in the following abbreviated as “BD” scaling for “bounded dissipation”, has been taken up by
Monkewitz 2022
, who developed a composite asymptotic expansion for
\langle uu\rangle^{+}
, that compares well with a number of DNS and experimental data, but used an ad-hoc fit for the overlap and outer parts.

The above alternative scalings correspond to the inner and outer asymptotic sequences

\displaystyle\mathrm{BD\,scaling:}\ \{\Phi^{\mathrm{(in)}}_{\mathrm{BD}}\}=

\displaystyle\{1,\mathrm{Re}_{\tau}^{-1/4},\mathrm{Re}_{\tau}^{-1},...\}\,;\ \{\Phi^{\mathrm{(out)}}_{\mathrm{BD}}\}=\{1,\mathrm{Re}_{\tau}^{-1},...\}

(1)

\displaystyle\mathrm{AE\,scaling:}\ \{\Phi^{\mathrm{(in)}}_{\mathrm{AE}}\}=

\displaystyle\{\ln{\mathrm{Re}_{\tau}}\,\&\,1,\mathrm{Re}_{\tau}^{-1},...\}\,;\ \{\Phi^{\mathrm{(out)}}_{\mathrm{AE}}\}=\{1,\mathrm{Re}_{\tau}^{-1},...\}

(2)

where for both BD and AE scaling, the dependence of outer Reynolds stresses on
\mathrm{Re}_{\tau}
is weak and finite Reynolds number corrections are thought to be of order
\mathcal{O}(\mathrm{Re}_{\tau}^{-1})
at most. It is also noted, that the terms of order
\mathcal{O}(\ln{\mathrm{Re}_{\tau}})
and of
\mathcal{O}(1)
in equation (
2
) must, for the matching to the outer expansion, be treated together as a “block”
(
Crighton & Leppington 1973
)
, in the same way as for the matching of inner and outer mean velocity across the log law (see for instance
Monkewitz & Nagib 2023
).

Discriminating between the two inner scalings on the basis of the
\mathrm{Re}_{\tau}
-dependence of the inner peak height
\langle uu\rangle^{+}_{\mathrm{IP}}
at
y^{+}_{\mathrm{IP}}\approxeq 15
, has so far been inconclusive because of the limited Reynolds number range of reliable data. Both fits,
[a_{1}\ln{\mathrm{Re}_{\tau}}+a_{2}]
and
[b_{1}+b_{2}\,\mathrm{Re}_{\tau}^{-1/4}]
are defendable, as seen in
Marusic et al. 2010
and
Monkewitz 2022
, for instance. Determining the scaling of coefficients in the Taylor expansion of different stresses about the wall, as in
Smits et al. 2021
, is equally inconclusive for the same reasons. These authors have also challenged the use by
Chen & Sreenivasan 2021
of the Taylor expansion of
\langle uu\rangle^{+}
about the wall to infer the scaling of the inner peak. The argument is valid insofar as using the Taylor expansion of
\langle uu\rangle^{+}
across the region with the sharpest variation of the different terms in the transport equation for
\langle uu\rangle^{+}
to estimate the
magnitude
of its inner peak is questionable. However, this Taylor series argument is not relevant for the
scaling
of the inner
\langle uu\rangle^{+}
or any other quantity, as the scaling
can only change across an overlap
. Clearly, no such overlap exists between the wall and the inner peak of
\langle uu\rangle^{+}
at
y^{+}_{\mathrm{IP}}\approxeq 15
!

The above synopsis of this scaling problem suggests, that the overlap between the inner and outer asymptotic expansions of
\langle uu\rangle^{+}
has received insufficient attention. The overlap, also called common part, is a key element of MAE, which provides the smooth transition between inner and outer expansions based on different asymptotic sequences, in particular the sequences (
1
) and (
2
). This is the subject of the next section
2
, where the overlap is analyzed by determining, from channel DNS and experiments, the indicator function for the new BD scaling and comparing it to the standard log-indicator function for AE scaling.

In section
3
, the two competing indicator functions are evaluated from experimental and DNS data for pipe flow, with results closely matching those for the channel. Section
4
is then devoted to the ZPG TBL and reveals that the indicator functions are significantly different from channel and pipe, indicating a much faster drop of
\langle uu\rangle^{+}
towards zero in the outer part of the boundary layer, presumably because of entrained free stream fluid. Before this drop, most ZPG TBL data appear slightly better fitted by the BD overlap, but the question remains open.

The conclusions in section
5
are unequivocal in support of BD scaling for channel and pipe flow, while better data will be required to definitively settle the issue for the ZPG TBL.

2
The inner-outer overlap of
\langle uu\rangle^{+}
for channel flow

The inner-outer overlap plays a key role in MAE, as it smoothly connects inner and outer expansions. The choice tool to detect overlaps are indicator functions, well established for logarithmic overlaps.

Logarithmic or AE overlap

Assuming that the inner part of
\langle uu\rangle^{+}
scales according to the AE model (equation
2
), the functional form of the overlap is just the reverse of the mean flow overlap, where the
\ln{\mathrm{Re}_{\tau}}
term is part of the outer expansion. Hence, the AE overlap of
\langle uu\rangle^{+}

must
contain a term proportional to
\ln{(\mathrm{Re}_{\tau}/y^{+})}\equiv-\ln{Y}
in order to avoid a
\ln{\mathrm{Re}_{\tau}}
term in the outer expansion. Indeed, in
Marusic et al. 2013
, authored by the principal advocates of the attached eddy model, the overlap law for
\langle uu\rangle^{+}
is given as
-C\ln Y+D
and the name of “Townsend-Perry” constant with a value of 1.26 was proposed for
C
.
However, this author is not aware of any published strong evidence for such a logarithmic law, such as a region of constant log-indicator function

\Xi_{\mathrm{AE}}=Y\left(\mathrm{d}\langle uu\rangle^{+}/\mathrm{d}Y\right)\equiv y^{+}\left(\mathrm{d}\langle uu\rangle^{+}/\mathrm{d}y^{+}\right)\quad.

(3)

There may have been the expectation that a region of constant
\Xi_{\mathrm{AE}}
would eventually develop at higher Reynolds numbers, but this is no longer tenable in view of the successful identification of the BD overlap.

BD overlap

A composite expansion of
\langle uu\rangle^{+}
, based on the asymptotic sequence (
1
) has been constructed in
Monkewitz 2022
, in the following referred to as “M22”, but the overlap and outer profiles were a bit of a “bricolage”, which may be loosely translated from French as “slapped together”. The construction consisted essentially of connecting the inner expansion at a fixed
y^{+}_{\times}=470
(equ. 2.7 of M22) to a fit of
\langle uu\rangle^{+}
on the centerline.
The resulting logarithmic slope of the overlap (equ. 2.9 and section 3 of M22) was Reynolds number dependent, unlike the universal slope proposed by
Marusic et al. 2013
and others, but the choice of a logarithm in M22 may have been another manifestation of the long shadow of von Kármán.

As it turns out, the overlap construction in M22 is just an awkward approximation of the new inner-outer overlap or common part (subscript “cp”) of
\langle uu\rangle^{+}
for BD scaling

\langle uu\rangle^{+}_{\mathrm{BDcp}}(Y)=10.74-10.2\,Y^{1/4}\equiv 10.74-10.2\,(y^{+})^{1/4}\,\mathrm{Re}_{\tau}^{-1/4}

(4)

The complete leading order of the outer expansion is easily obtained by adding a “wake” to the overlap (
4
)

\langle uu\rangle^{+}_{\mathrm{BDout}}(Y)=\langle uu\rangle^{+}_{\mathrm{BDcp}}+0.26\,\exp\left(\frac{-10.2\,(1-Y)}{4\times 0.26}\right)

(5)

Here, the coefficients in (
4
)and (
5
) have been determined from channel DNS, but will not be significantly different for the pipe.
Equations (
4
) and (
5
) are seen in figure

(a) to provide an excellent description of
the overlap of
\langle uu\rangle^{+}
in channels and of the wake which becomes noticeable beyond
Y\approx 0.75
, marked by the red vertical arrow.

When looking at the “band” of data in figure

major differences to the channel and pipe indicator functions are evident:

•

Both
\Xi

0.2\lessapprox Y\lessapprox 0.8
, indicating a much steeper decrease of
\langle uu\rangle^{+}
in this region. A likely reason for this negative bulge is intermittency, as discussed below.

•

In the near-wall interval
0.1\lessapprox Y\lessapprox 0.25

one may see a short region of constant
\Xi_{\mathrm{BD}}\approx-9.5
in figure

\Xi_{\mathrm{AE}}\approx-1.5

Y\approx 0.25
do not allow to discriminate between BD and AE scaling in the ZPG TBL, and the
\mathrm{Re}_{\tau}
’s of DNS are too low to help. However, it would be rather surprising, if the inner asymptotic sequence for
\langle uu\rangle^{+}
in the ZPG TBL was different from channel and pipe!

To test the hypothesis that the large negative bulge of
\Xi
beyond
Y\approxeq 0.25
is due to intermittency, i.e. to the entrainment of free stream fluid, a rough model is developed, based on the location of the “turbulent non-turbulent interface” (TNTI). The PDF of its location has been studied by
Chauhan et al. 2014
, who has determined its mean location
\bar{Y}_{\mathrm{TNTI}}=0.69
and its standard deviation
\sigma=0.11
. With the cumulative distribution function
\mathcal{C}(Y)
of the TNTI location , the measured
\langle uu\rangle^{+}
may be expressed in terms of a hypothetical, fully turbulent stress as

\langle uu\rangle^{+}=\langle uu\rangle^{+}_{\textrm{turb}}\,[1-\mathcal{C}(Y)]\,,

(7)

where the drastic simplification has been made, that
\langle uu\rangle^{+}\equiv 0
during the incursions of free-stream fluid into the boundary layer. With this,
\Xi_{\mathrm{BD}}
can be decomposed as

\Xi_{\mathrm{BD}}=4Y^{3/4}\,\Big(\mathrm{d}\langle uu\rangle^{+}_{\textrm{turb}}\Big/\mathrm{d}Y\Big)-4Y^{3/4}\,\Big(\mathrm{d}[\mathcal{C}(Y)\,\langle uu\rangle^{+}_{\textrm{turb}}]\Big/\mathrm{d}Y\Big)

(8)

Assuming that the first term in equation (
8
) corresponds to the hypothetical, non-intermittent ZPG TBL, with an overlap value close to the one for channel and pipe, the second term represents the intermittency correction. Concentrating on the overlap, the intermittency correction is evaluated with the channel overlap of equation (
4
) and the
\mathcal{C}(Y)
of
Chauhan et al. 2014
. As shown in figure
5
(a), this model captures the essence of the deviation of
\Xi_{\mathrm{BD}}
from the constant value
\approxeq-10
. It is furthermore noted, that the largest deviation of
\Xi_{\mathrm{BD}}
from the channel and pipe indicator functions occurs at
Y\approxeq 0.65
, essentially at the mean location of the TNTI. This strongly supports the notion, that the difference between the ZPG TBL and the channel and pipe overlaps is principally due to the entrainment of free stream fluid.

5
Conclusions

The clear conclusion from the present overlap analysis of the stream-wise Reynolds stress
\langle uu\rangle^{+}
for channel and pipe flow is that
\langle uu\rangle^{+}
remains finite everywhere in the limit of infinite Reynolds number and, in the inner region, decreases from there as
\mathrm{Re}_{\tau}^{-1/4}
. This has been demonstrated by analyzing the inner-outer overlap with the indicator functions
\Xi_{\mathrm{BD}}
for the “bounded dissipation” scaling of
Chen & Sreenivasan 2021
;
Chen & Sreenivasan 2022
, and comparing to
\Xi_{\mathrm{AE}}
for the “attached eddy” or logarithmic scaling
(
Marusic & Monty 2019
, see for instance)
.
In other words, the unlimited growth of near-wall stream-wise Reynolds stress with
\ln{\mathrm{Re}_{\tau}}
in channel and pipe flow is a feature of the attached eddy model and not of physical reality. One possible explanation for this result is the essentially inviscid nature of the attached eddy model. As
\ln\mathrm{Re}_{\tau}
represents a weak divergence for
\mathrm{Re}_{\tau}\to\infty
, it may well be that it could be eliminated by introducing some viscous “damping” in the model, without compromising its physical attractiveness.

Indications have also been presented for BD scaling of
\langle uu\rangle^{+}
in the ZPG TBL, but no definitive conclusion can be drawn on the basis of the available data. The main reasons are the short extent of the
\langle uu\rangle^{+}
overlap, ending already at
Y\approxeq 0.25
, as opposed to
Y\approxeq 0.75
in channels and pipes, combined with the relatively large scatter of the
\Xi
’s. As argued in section
4
,
the major difference beyond
Y\approxeq 0.25
between the
\Xi
’s in the ZPG TBL and in channels and pipes is most likely due to the intermittent intrusion of free stream fluid, and sets the ZPG TBL clearly apart from channel and pipe.

In all likelihood, the present conclusions also apply to the other components of the Reynolds stress tensor, which will be the subject of a future full-length paper.
To conclude, a finite limit of Reynolds stresses for
\mathrm{Re}_{\tau}\!\to\!\infty
is not only of theoretical interest, but has important technological implications, such as in ship hydrodynamics and hydraulic engineering.

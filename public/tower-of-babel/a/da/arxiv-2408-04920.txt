Tight bounds on the number of non-equivalent parameterized squares in a word
Rikuya Hamai, Kazushi Taketsugu, Yuto Nakashima, Shunsuke Inenaga, Hideo Bannai, Jakub Radoszewski

Abstract
Two words x , y x,y of the same length are said to be parameterized equivalent if there exists a character bijection that transforms x x into y y . A word w w is called a parameterized square if w w is a concatenation of two parameterized equivalent words. Kociumaka et al. [TCS 2016] showed that in a word of length n n that contains σ \sigma distinct characters, the number of parameterized squares that are non-equivalent with respect to parameterized equivalence is at most 2 ​ σ ! ​ n 2\sigma!n . In this paper, we show that the maximum number of non-equivalent parameterized squares is less than σ ​ n \sigma n , which significantly improves the best-known upper bound by Kociumaka et al. Moreover, we construct a family of words containing Ω ⁡ ( σ ​ n ) \Omega(\sigma n) non-equivalent parameterized squares, which demonstrates that the upper bound is asymptotically tight.

In combinatorics on words, repetitive structures (e.g., maximal repetitions, squares) and avoidability are well-studied topics.
Studies on such topics were initiated more than a hundred years ago by Thue

Words

.

and

of

, respectively.
Let
\mathsf{Substr}(w)
denote the set of subwords of
w
.
The
i
-th symbol of a word
w
is denoted by
w[i]
, where
0\leq i<|w|
.
For a word
w
and two integers
0\leq i\leq j<|w|
,
let
w[i..j]
denote the subword of
w
that begins at position
i
and ends at

. For convenience, let

when

.
Also,
let

,

, and

w[i..j]=w(i-1..j]=w[i..j+1)
.
For any word

, let
w^{1}=w
and let
w^{k}=ww^{k-1}
for any integer

.
A word

is said to be

if

.

2.2
Parameterized equivalence

Two words
x
and
y
of length
k
each are said to be
parameterized equivalent

iff there is a bijection
f
on
\Sigma
such that
f(x[i])=y[i]
for all
0\leq i<k
.
We write
x\approx y
iff two words
x
and
y
are parameterized equivalent.
A bijection can be seen as a permutation of
\Sigma
.
A transposition is a permutation that exchanges two elements and leaves every other element fixed.

Fact 1
.

Any permutation can be represented by a product of transpositions.
The parity (odd or even) of the number of transpositions is uniquely determined for any given permutation.

2.3
Parameterized squares

A word
w
is called a
parameterized square
when
w=xy
for words
x
,
y
such that
x
and
y
are parameterized equivalent (i.e.,
x\approx y
).
We say that two words
x
and
y
of equal length are
non-equivalent
(under the parameterized equivalence) iff
x\not\approx y
.
Let
\mathsf{PS}(w)
denote the set of all parameterized squares occurring in a word
w
.
Let
\mathsf{PSeq}(w)
denote the number of equivalence classes on
\mathsf{PS}(w)

with respect to
\approx
.
We call
\mathsf{PSeq}(w)
the number of non-equivalent parameterized squares in
w
.

Example 1
.

Let
w=\mathtt{aabbac}
.
For example,
w
contains

\mathtt{aa},\mathtt{bb}

as standard squares,

\mathtt{aa},\mathtt{ab},\mathtt{ac},\mathtt{ba},\mathtt{bb},\mathtt{aabb},\mathtt{abba}

as parameterized squares, and

\mathtt{aa}\approx\mathtt{bb},\mathtt{ab}\approx\mathtt{ac}\approx\mathtt{ba},\mathtt{aabb},\mathtt{abba}

as non-equivalent parameterized squares,
and
\mathsf{PSeq}(w)=4
.

2.4
Parameterized periods

An integer
p
is said to be a
parameterized period
(
p-period
) of word
w

if
w[0..|w|-p)\approx w[p..|w|)
holds.
For any p-period
p
of
w
,
we write
p\parallel_{f}w
if
w[0..|w|-p)\approx w[p..|w|)
holds by a bijection
f
.
We sometimes drop the subscript
f
(i.e., we just write
p\parallel_{\!}w
) when no confusions occur.
The smallest p-period of
w
is denoted by
\mathrm{p}(w)
.
By the definition of p-periods, we obtain the following fact which we will use in our proof.

Fact 2

(cf.
[
21
]
)
.

Let
w
be a word that satisfies
p\parallel_{f}w
.
For any position
i
satisfying
0\leq i<|w|-p
,
f(w[i])=w[i+p]
.

Apostolico and Giancarlo
[
2
]
showed a variant of the periodicity lemma in the parameterized equivalence model.

Lemma 3

(Lemma 3 of
[
2
]
)
.

Let
w
be a word that satisfies
p\parallel_{f}w
and
q\parallel_{g}w
.
If
p+q\leq|w|
and
f\circ g=g\circ f
, then
\gcd(p,q)\parallel_{\!}w
.

The above lemma uses the commutativity of the two bijections.
Recently, Ideguchi et al.
[
13
]
proposed a variant of the parameterized periodicity lemma which does not use the commutativity of the bijections as follows:

Lemma 4

(Lemma 5 of
[
13
]
)
.

Let
w
be a word that satisfies
w\in\Sigma^{*}
,
p\parallel_{\!}w,\,q\parallel_{\!}w
.
If
p+q+\min(p,q)\cdot(|\Sigma_{w}|-1)\leq|w|
, then
\gcd(p,q)\parallel_{\!}w
.

The number of distinct characters in a word may play an important role in bounding the parameterized periodicity, as can be seen in the previous lemma.
In our result here we will extensively show more of such relations.
The next lemma, shown by Ideguchi et al.
[
13
]
, also gives a useful relation between them.

Lemma 5

(Lemma 4 of
[
13
]
)
.

Let
w
be a word.
For any subword
w^{\prime}
of
w
,
If
|w^{\prime}|\geq\mathrm{p}(w)\cdot(|\Sigma_{w}|-1)
,
then
|\Sigma_{w^{\prime}}|\geq|\Sigma_{w}|-1
holds.

In the next section, we present a tighter version for each of Lemmas
4
and
5
.

Upper bound for non-equivalent parameterized squares

We show the following upper bound for the maximum number
\mathsf{PSeq}(w)
of non-equivalent parameterized squares in a word
w
.
The case
\sigma=1
is trivial, since a unary word of length
n
contains at most
\lfloor n/2\rfloor
parameterized-square equivalence classes. Hence, in what follows, we assume
\sigma\geq 2
.

See
1

To prove Theorem
1
, we use a charging argument based on the following theorem:

Theorem 6
.

For any word
w
that contains
\sigma
distinct characters,
there can be at most
\sigma
prefixes of
w
that are parameterized squares and have no other parameterized occurrence in
w
.

For each equivalence class
C
of parameterized squares occurring in a string
w
of length
n
, choose an occurrence of a member of
C
whose starting position is the largest (i.e. rightmost), and charge
C
to this starting position.
If
C
is charged to position
i
, then the chosen occurrence is a prefix of the suffix
w[i..]
.
Moreover, by the maximality of
i
, this prefix has no other parameterized occurrence in
w[i..]
;
otherwise,
C
must have an occurrence starting at a position larger than
i
, a contradiction.
Thus, by Theorem
6
, at most
|\Sigma_{w[i..]}|\leq\sigma
classes are charged to each position
i
.
Since no parameterized square begins at the last position, summing over all
n-1
positions gives
\mathsf{PSeq}(w)\leq\sigma(n-1)<\sigma n
.
It now remains to prove Theorem
6
.

In order to prove Theorem
6
, we first prove Lemma
7
.
This lemma states that any sufficiently long subword with respect to a p-period of the whole word contains many distinct characters.
Lemma
7
is a generalized property of a tighter version of the previous statement (Lemma
5
).

Lemma 7
.

Let
w
be a word that satisfies
p\parallel_{\!}w
.
For any subword
w^{\prime}
of
w
and any integer
k
satisfying
2\leq k\leq|\Sigma_{w}|+1
,
if
|w^{\prime}|\geq p\cdot(k-2)+1
,
then
|\Sigma_{w^{\prime}}|\geq k-1
holds.

Proof.

We prove this lemma by induction on
k
.
It is clear that the statement holds for
k=2
.
Suppose that the statement holds for
k=m
for some integer
m
satisfying
2\leq m\leq|\Sigma_{w}|
.
Namely, if
|w^{\prime}|\geq p\cdot(m-2)+1
, then
|\Sigma_{w^{\prime}}|\geq m-1
holds.
We show that the statement holds for
k=m+1
.
Since
|w^{\prime}|\geq p\cdot(m-1)+1\geq p\cdot(m-2)+1
,

|\Sigma_{w^{\prime}}|\geq m-1
holds by the induction hypothesis.
Assume on the contrary that
|\Sigma_{w^{\prime}}|<m
.
This implies that
|\Sigma_{w^{\prime}}|=m-1
.
Let us consider the prefix
w^{\prime}[0..i)
of length
i=p\cdot(m-2)+1
of
w^{\prime}
.
By the induction hypothesis,
|\Sigma_{w^{\prime}[0..i)}|\geq m-1
.
Moreover, since
w^{\prime}[0..i)
is a subword of
w^{\prime}
,
|\Sigma_{w^{\prime}{[0..i)}}|=m-1
holds.
Due to the p-period
p\parallel_{f}w
,
f(w^{\prime}[0..i))=w^{\prime}[p..i+p)
holds.
This implies that
\Sigma_{w^{\prime}[0..i)}=\Sigma_{w^{\prime}[p..i+p)}

(if not, there exists a character
c\in\Sigma_{w^{\prime}[p..i+p)}\setminus\Sigma_{w^{\prime}[0..i)}

and then it contradicts
|\Sigma_{w^{\prime}}|=m-1
).
Let
A=\Sigma_{w^{\prime}[0..i)}
.
Since
p\parallel_{f}w
and
\Sigma_{w^{\prime}[p..i+p)}=A
, we have
f(A)\subseteq A
.
As
f
is a bijection and
|f(A)|=|A|
, it follows that
f(A)=A
.
Consequently,
f^{\ell}(A)=A
for every integer
\ell
.
We now prove that every character of
w
belongs to
A
.
Since
|w^{\prime}|\geq p(m-1)+1\geq p+1
, the occurrence of
w^{\prime}
in
w

contains at least one position from each residue class modulo
p
.
For any position of
w
, choose a position in this occurrence of
w^{\prime}

with the same residue modulo
p
.
By repeatedly applying the
p
-period relation forward or backward,
the corresponding character is obtained by applying some power of
f

to a character in
A
.
Since
f^{\ell}(A)=A
for every integer
\ell
, this character also belongs to
A
.
Thus
\Sigma_{w}\subseteq A
, which contradicts
|A|=m-1<m\leq|\Sigma_{w}|
.
∎

To prove it, we introduce a relation between the number of distinct characters of a subword and the commutativity of any two permutations of the alphabet.

Lemma 8
.

Assume that
|\Sigma|\geq 2
.
Let
\Sigma^{\prime}
be a subset of
\Sigma
satisfying
|\Sigma^{\prime}|=|\Sigma|-2
.
For any permutations
f
and
g
of
\Sigma
,
if
(f\circ g)(a)=(g\circ f)(a)
for all
a\in\Sigma^{\prime}
,
then

and

Proof.

Let
f
and
g
be permutations of
\Sigma=\{c_{1},\dots,c_{\sigma}\}
.
Also, let
\Sigma^{\prime}=\{c_{1},\dots,c_{\sigma-2}\}
be a subset of
\Sigma

such that
(f\circ g)(a)=(g\circ f)(a)
holds for every
a\in\Sigma^{\prime}
.
Assume on the contrary that
(f\circ g)(c_{\sigma-1})\neq(g\circ f)(c_{\sigma-1})
.
Let
(f\circ g)(c_{\sigma-1})=c_{i}
and
(g\circ f)(c_{\sigma-1})=c_{j}
for some
i\neq j
.
Then,
(f\circ g)(c_{\sigma})=c_{j}
and
(g\circ f)(c_{\sigma})=c_{i}
.
Thus,
g\circ f=(c_{i},c_{j})\circ(f\circ g)
where
(c_{i},c_{j})
is a transposition of elements
c_{i}
and
c_{j}
.
This implies that
g\circ f
and
(c_{i},c_{j})\circ(f\circ g)
are products of transposition with different parity.
This fact contradicts Fact

.

Lemma 9
.

Let
w
be a word that satisfies
p\parallel_{\!}w
,

, and
|\Sigma_{w}|\geq 2
.
If
p+q+\min(p,q)\cdot(|\Sigma_{w}|-2)\leq|w|

\gcd(p,q)\parallel_{\!}w

Proof.

Let
w
be a word that satisfies
p\parallel_{f}w
,
q\parallel_{g}w
, and
|\Sigma_{w}|\geq 2
.
Also, let
w^{\prime}=w[0..\min(p,q)\cdot(|\Sigma_{w}|-2))
.
Then,
(g\circ f)(w^{\prime})=w[p+q..\min(p,q)\cdot(|\Sigma_{w}|-2)+p+q)

.
Therefore, for any

In the proof of Theorem
6
, we need to discuss structures of overlapping parameterized squares.
If there exist two words
xy
and
yz
that are overlapping each other and have the same standard period (in the exact matching model) that does not exceed
|y|
,
we can see that the whole word
xyz
also has the same period.
This property does not always hold for the parameterized equivalence,
since p-periods in the two overlapping words may come from different bijections.
In the next lemma, we show that a similar property holds if the overlapping part is sufficiently long (i.e., the overlapping has many distinct characters due to the above lemmas).

Lemma 10
.

For any non-empty words
x,y
, and
z
,
if
p\parallel_{\!}xy
,
p\parallel_{\!}yz
, and
|y|\geq p\cdot(|\Sigma_{xyz}|-1)+1
,
then
p\parallel_{\!}xyz
.

Proof.

If
|\Sigma_{xyz}|=1
, then
xyz
is unary and the claim is immediate.
Hence assume
|\Sigma_{xyz}|\geq 2
.
Let
f
(resp.
g
) be a bijection of
p\parallel_{\!}xy
(resp.
p\parallel_{\!}yz
), and

\sigma=|\Sigma_{xyz}|
.
Since
p\parallel_{f}xy
and
p\parallel_{g}yz
and
|y|\geq p+1
, we have
p\parallel_{f}y
and
p\parallel_{g}y
.
This implies that
f(y[0..(\sigma-2)p])=y[p..(\sigma-1)p]
, and

g(y[0..(\sigma-2)p])=y[p..(\sigma-1)p]
.
Thus,
f(c)=g(c)
for any character
c\in\Sigma_{y[0..(\sigma-2)p]}
.
By Lemma
7
,
|\Sigma_{y[0..(\sigma-2)p]}|\geq\sigma-1
holds.
These facts imply that
f=g
.
Thus,
f((xyz)[0..|xyz|-p))=(xyz)[p..|xyz|)
holds and the lemma holds.
∎

The next lemma explains that a p-period of a prefix which is sufficiently long can extend to the whole word,
if the whole word has a p-period that is a multiple of the p-period of the prefix.

Lemma 11
.

Let
s
be a word that satisfies
q\parallel_{g}s
, and

w
be a prefix of
s
that satisfies
p\parallel_{f}w
.
If
|w|\geq p(|\Sigma_{s}|-2)+q+1
and
q=kp
for some integer
k
, then
p\parallel_{f}s
.

Proof.

If
|\Sigma_{s}|=1
, then
s
is unary and the claim is immediate.
Hence assume
|\Sigma_{s}|\geq 2
.
Let
w^{\prime}=w[0..p(|\Sigma_{s}|-2)]
.
Since
|w^{\prime}|+q\leq|w|
and
q=kp
,

g(w^{\prime})=w[q..p(|\Sigma_{s}|-2)+q]=w[kp..p(|\Sigma_{s}|-2)+kp]=f^{k}(w^{\prime})
,
and hence
g(a)=f^{k}(a)
for every
a\in\Sigma_{w^{\prime}}
.

Assume on the contrary that
|\Sigma_{w^{\prime}}|\leq|\Sigma_{s}|-2

This implies that

4
Lower bound for non-equivalent parameterized squares

In this section, we show a lower bound on non-equivalent parameterized squares.
See
2

Let
\alpha\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
and
\lambda=\alpha(\alpha+1)
.
We construct a word
x[0..\lambda)
over alphabet
\{0,\ldots,\lambda-1\}
as follows:

x[i]=\left.\begin{cases}\binom{p+1}{2}-1&\text{if }i=\frac{\lambda}{2}+\binom{p}{2},\ p\in\{1,\ldots,\alpha\},\\
i&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}\right.

Let
\phi
be a morphism such that
\phi(c)=c^{k}
for every
c\in\{0,\ldots,\lambda-1\}
. We define a word
w:=\phi(x)
of length
\lambda k
. Thus for any
i\in\{0,\ldots,\lambda k-1\}
, we have

w[i]=\left.\begin{cases}\binom{p+1}{2}-1&\text{if }\left\lfloor\frac{i}{k}\right\rfloor=\frac{\lambda}{2}+\binom{p}{2},\ p\in\{1,\ldots,\alpha\},\\
\left\lfloor\frac{i}{k}\right\rfloor&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}\right.

We prove this word
w
gives our lower bound.
We start with simple observations.

Observation 1
.

\alpha\in\Theta(\sqrt{\lambda})
.

Observation 2
.

|\Sigma_{w}|=|\Sigma_{x}|=\lambda-\alpha=\alpha^{2}
.

Observation 3
.

For every
i\in\{1,\ldots,\lambda-1\}
, we have
x[i]\neq x[i-1]
. Hence, for every
i\in\{1,\ldots,\lambda k-1\}
, if
i\bmod k=0
, then
w[i]\neq w[i-1]
.

Proof.

It suffices to prove the first part of the observation. If
x[i]=i
and
x[i-1]=i-1
, the conclusion obviously holds. If
x[i]\neq i
, then we consider three cases:

1.

If
i=\frac{\lambda}{2}
, then
x[i]=0
but
x[i-1]=\frac{\lambda}{2}-1
.

2.

If
i=\frac{\lambda}{2}+1
, then
x[i]=2
and
x[i-1]=0
.

3.

If
i>\frac{\lambda}{2}+1
, then
x[i]\leq\frac{\lambda}{2}-1
and
x[i-1]=i-1>\frac{\lambda}{2}
since two numbers of the form
\binom{p}{2}
and
\binom{p+1}{2}
for positive integer
p
can differ by 1 only if
p=1
.

In either case, we have
x[i]\neq x[i-1]
.

If
x[i-1]\neq i-1
, the proof is analogous (we only have cases similar to cases 2 and 3 above).
∎

The key idea is as follows.
Every length-
2m
subword whose length is slightly greater than half the length of
w
is non-equivalent (the precise threshold will be defined in Lemma
12
).
This is because, by the definitions of
x
and
w
, each such subword contains the same character at a special position.
Since the distances between occurrences of this character basically differ for each character,
all these subwords form parameterized squares for an appropriate
m
.
Figure
2
gives an example of a word which we defined and several p-squares in the word.
We show these properties in Lemmas
12
and
13
.

Lemma 12
.

If
2m\geq\frac{\lambda k}{2}+k
,
0\leq i,j\leq\lambda k-2m
, and
i\neq j
, then
w[i..i+2m)\not\approx w[j..j+2m)
.

Proof.

We will find indices
a,b\in\{0,\ldots,2m-1\}
such that

(w[i+a]=w[i+b]\,\land\,w[j+a]\neq w[j+b])\,\lor\,(w[i+a]\neq w[i+b]\,\land\,w[j+a]=w[j+b]).

We consider several cases.

Case 1:
i\not\equiv j\pmod{k}
.

Assume w.l.o.g. that
i\bmod k<j\bmod k
. By Observation
3
, we have

w[j+(k-j\bmod k)]\neq w[j]\text{ but }w[i+(k-j\bmod k)]=w[i]

since
\left\lfloor(i+(k-j\bmod k))/k\right\rfloor=\left\lfloor i/k\right\rfloor
.

Case 2:
i\equiv j\pmod{k}
.

For any index
r
, let

p_{r}=\min\left\{p\in\{1,\ldots,\alpha\}\,:\,k\binom{p+1}{2}-1\geq r\right\}.

Note that
p_{i}
and
p_{j}
are well-defined as

i,j<\lambda k-2m<\frac{\lambda k}{2}
.

We have by definition

w\left[k\binom{p_{i}+1}{2}-1\right]=\binom{p_{i}+1}{2}-1=w\left[\frac{\lambda k}{2}+k\binom{p_{i}}{2}+k-1\right].

(1)

Moreover,

i\leq k\binom{p_{i}+1}{2}-1<\frac{\lambda k}{2}\leq\frac{\lambda k}{2}+k\binom{p_{i}}{2}+k-1\leq\frac{\lambda k}{2}+i+k-1<i+2m.

The penultimate inequality follows by the definition of
p_{i}
, as
k\binom{p_{i}}{2}-1<i
. Hence, (
1
) can be seen as
w[i+a]=w[i+b]
for integers
a,b
such that
0\leq a<b<2m
and

b-a=\frac{\lambda k}{2}+k\binom{p_{i}}{2}-k\binom{p_{i}+1}{2}+k=\frac{\lambda k}{2}-kp_{i}+k.

(2)

Subcase 2a:
p_{i}\neq p_{j}
.

Assume w.l.o.g. that
p_{i}<p_{j}
.
For any integers
a,b
that satisfy (
2
), we have
w[j+a]\neq w[j+b]
. Indeed, by definition of
w
, if
w[j+a^{\prime}]=w[j+b^{\prime}]
for any integers
a^{\prime},b^{\prime}\geq 0
, then

b^{\prime}-a^{\prime}\leq\frac{\lambda k}{2}+k\binom{p_{j}}{2}+k-1-k\binom{p_{j}+1}{2}+k=\\
\frac{\lambda k}{2}-kp_{j}+2k-1\leq\frac{\lambda k}{2}-kp_{i}-k+2k-1<\frac{\lambda k}{2}-kp_{i}+k=b-a.

Subcase 2b:
p_{i}=p_{j}
.

Assume w.l.o.g. that
i<j
. In particular,
j\geq i+k
as
i\equiv j\pmod{k}
. Then
w[j+a]\neq w[i+a]=\binom{p_{i}+1}{2}-1
. Similarly as in subcase 2a, if
w[j+a+a^{\prime}]=w[j+a+b^{\prime}]
for any integers
a^{\prime},b^{\prime}\geq 0
, then
b^{\prime}-a^{\prime}<b-a
. In particular,
w[j+a]\neq w[j+b]
.
∎

Lemma 13
.

If
k\mid m
,
i\in\{0,\ldots,\lambda k-2m\}
, and
m\leq k(\frac{\lambda}{2}-\alpha)
, then
w[i..i+2m)
is a parameterized square.

Proof.

Let
a=k-i\bmod k
and
t=\frac{m}{k}
. We will show that for each
j\in\{i,i+m\}
,

w[j..j+m)\approx 0^{a}1^{k}2^{k}\dots(t-1)^{k}t^{k-a}.

(3)

This will imply that
w[i..i+m)\approx w[i+m..i+2m)
and indeed
w[i..i+2m)
is a parameterized square.

We proceed to a proof of (
3
). By the structure of
w
, it is clear that
w[j..j+m)\approx c_{0}^{a}c_{1}^{k}c_{2}^{k}\dots c_{t-1}^{k}c_{t}^{k-a}
for some characters
c_{0},c_{1},\ldots,c_{t}
. We only need to show that all of them are different. Assume to the contrary that
c_{u}=c_{v}
for some
0\leq u<v\leq t
. By Observation
3
,
u+1\neq v
. This would imply that
w[x]=w[y]
for some
x<y
such that
x+k\leq y
and
y-x<m
. However, if
w[x]=w[y]
for some
x<y
such that
x+k\leq y
, then

y-x\geq\min_{p\in\{1,\ldots,\alpha\}}\left\{\frac{\lambda k}{2}+k\binom{p}{2}-k\binom{p+1}{2}+1\right\}=\frac{\lambda k}{2}+1-\alpha k>m.

Thus indeed all characters
c_{0},c_{1},\ldots,c_{t}
are different, which implies (
3
).
∎

Theorem 14
.

We have
|w|=\alpha(\alpha+1)k
,
|\Sigma_{w}|=\alpha^{2}
, and
the number of non-equivalent parameterized squares in
w
is at least
\frac{1}{16}\alpha^{4}k-O(\alpha^{3}k)
.

Proof.

By Lemmas
12
and
13
, the number of non-equivalent parameterized squares in
w
is at least

\displaystyle\mathsf{PSeq}(w)

\displaystyle\geq\left|\left\{(m,i)\,:\,i\in\left\{0,\ldots,\lambda k-2m\right\},\,k\mid m,\,\frac{\lambda+2}{4}\leq\frac{m}{k}\leq\frac{\lambda}{2}-\alpha\right\}\right|

\displaystyle\geq\sum_{t=\left\lceil\frac{\lambda+2}{4}\right\rceil}^{\frac{\lambda}{2}-\alpha}(\lambda k-2tk+1)

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{\lambda}{2}-\alpha-\left\lceil\frac{\lambda+2}{4}\right\rceil+1\right)(\lambda k+1)-2k\sum_{t=\left\lceil\frac{\lambda+2}{4}\right\rceil}^{\frac{\lambda}{2}-\alpha}t

\displaystyle>\left(\frac{\lambda}{2}-\alpha\right)(\lambda k+1)-\left(\frac{\lambda+6}{4}\right)(\lambda k+1)-2k\left(\binom{\frac{\lambda}{2}-\alpha+1}{2}-\binom{\left\lceil\frac{\lambda+2}{4}\right\rceil}{2}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{\lambda^{2}k}{4}-2k\left(\frac{\lambda^{2}}{8}-\frac{\lambda^{2}}{32}\right)-O(\lambda^{1.5}k)

\displaystyle=\frac{\lambda^{2}k}{16}-O(\lambda^{1.5}k).

Here we used Observation
1
.
∎

To obtain a word over an alphabet of an arbitrary size
\sigma
that contains
\Theta(\sigma n)
non-equivalent parameterized squares, where
n
is the length of the word, it suffices to construct the word
w
from Theorem
14
for
\alpha^{2}
being the largest integer square not exceeding
\sigma
and append
w
with
\sigma-\alpha^{2}\leq 2\alpha
distinct characters.

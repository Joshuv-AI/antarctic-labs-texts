Fermiology and Origin of T c \textit{T}_{c} Enhancement in a Kagome Superconductor OPEN Cs(V 1 − x ​ Nb x ) 3 ​ Sb 5 \textrm{Cs(V}_{1-x}\textrm{Nb}_{x})_{3}\textrm{Sb}_{5}
Takemi Kato, Yongkai Li, Kosuke Nakayama, Zhiwei Wang, Seigo Souma, Fumihiko Matsui, Miho Kitamura, Koji Horiba, Hiroshi Kumigashira, Takashi Takahashi, Yugui Yao, Takafumi Sato

Abstract
Kagome metals AV 3 ​ Sb 5 \textrm{AV}_{3}\textrm{Sb}_{5} (A = K, Rb, and Cs) exhibit a characteristic superconducting ground state coexisting with charge-density wave (CDW), whereas the mechanisms of the superconductivity and CDW have yet to be clarified. Here we report a systematic ARPES study of OPEN Cs(V 1 − x ​ Nb x ) 3 ​ Sb 5 \textrm{Cs(V}_{1-x}\textrm{Nb}_{x})_{3}\textrm{Sb}_{5} as a function of Nb content x x , where isovalent Nb substitution causes an enhancement of superconducting transition temperature ( T c T_{c} ) and the reduction of CDW temperature ( T CDW T_{\textrm{CDW}} ). We found that the Nb substitution shifts the Sb-derived electron band at the Γ \Gamma point downward and simultaneously moves the V-derived band around the M point upward to lift up the saddle point (SP) away from the Fermi level, leading to the reduction of CDW-gap magnitude and T CDW T_{\textrm{CDW}} . This indicates a primary role of the SP density of states to stabilize CDW. The present result also suggests that the enhancement of superconductivity by Nb substitution is caused by the cooperation between the expansion of the Sb-derived electron pocket and the recovery of the V-derived density of states at the Fermi level.



The kagome lattice offers a fertile ground to explore exotic quantum phenomena originating from electron correlation and non-trivial band topology.

In this paper, we report ARPES study of Nb-substituted CVS,
\textrm{Cs(V}_{1-x}\textrm{Nb}_{x})_{3}\textrm{Sb}_{5}
(CVNS;
x

We have revealed that Nb-substitution induces the shift of bands characterized by an expansion of the Sb-derived electron pocket and a shrinkage of the V-derived electron pocket forming the SP, the latter of which is well correlated with the CDW suppression.
We discuss implications of the present results in relation to the mechanism of CDW and superconductivity.

Taking into account the essential role of SP to the CDW, the mechanism of intriguing
T_{c}
enhancement in CVNS is understood as following.
Since the CDW is observed even in the superconducting state, the superconductivity would occur in the metallic
k
region where the CDW gap is absent
Nakayama et al. 2021
.
It is thus suggested that the suppression of CDW with Nb substitution increases the V-derived DOS around
E_{\textrm{F}}
and contributes to the
T_{c}
enhancement.
Moreover, since the Sb band maintains the gapless feature even in the CDW phase [Fig. 3(f)], it would also play an important role to the superconductivity.
In fact, observed expansion of the
\alpha
pocket upon Nb substitution (expanded by 16 % from
x=0
to 0.07) would lead to the increase in DOS at
E_{\textrm{F}}
and provide more electrons to the superconducting pairing.
Thus, the observed opposite band shift gives rise to better conditions for both the Sb- and V-derived electrons, leading to the cooperative promotion of the superconductivity with higher
T_{c}
.

Finally, it is remarked that the observed opposite band shift in Nb-substituted CVS is different from the band shift reported recently in Ti-substituted CVS
Liu et al.
.
The Ti substitution acts as a hole doping to the crystal, moving the SP away from
E_{\textrm{F}}
as in the case of Nb-substituted CVS.
Thus, the mechanism of CDW suppression is understood with the same framework for both Nb and Ti substitutions.
On the other hand, the
\alpha
pocket shrinks with Ti substitution in contrast to the case of Nb substitution.
This shrinkage produces lower DOS at
E_{\textrm{F}}
and would be responsible for the
T_{c}
reduction at higher Ti concentrations
Liu et al.
;
Yang et al.
, distinct from the Nb case where the
T_{c}
monotonically increases with Nb substitution.
Such a critical difference in the behavior of
T_{c}
is well understood in terms of the difference in the electron filling of the
\alpha
pocket.

In conclusion, we reported an ARPES study of
\textrm{Cs(V}_{1-x}\textrm{Nb}_{x})_{3}\textrm{Sb}_{5}
as a function of
x
.
We uncovered an intriguing change in the band structure upon Nb substitution characterized by the downward shift of the Sb-derived electron band in contrast to the upward shift of the V-derived SP band.
We also found that
T_{\textrm{CDW}}
becomes higher when the SP band is located at closer to
E_{\textrm{F}}
, pointing to a close connection between the SP and CDW.
We have concluded that the enhancement of
T_{c}
with Nb substitution is caused by the cooperative expansion of Sb-derived electron pocket and recovery of V-derived density of states at
E_{\textrm{F}}
.

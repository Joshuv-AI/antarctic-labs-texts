Hausdorff dimension of unique beta expansions
Derong Kong, Wenxia Li

Abstract
. Given an integer N ≥ 2 N\geq 2 and a real number β > 1 {\beta}>1 , let Γ β , N \Gamma_{{\beta},N} be the set of all x = ∑ i = 1 ∞ d i / β i x=\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}{d_{i}}/{{\beta}^{i}} with d i ∈ { 0 , 1 , ⋯ , N − 1 } d_{i}\in\{0,1,\cdots,N-1\} for all i ≥ 1 i\geq 1 . The infinite sequence ( d i ) (d_{i}) is called a β {\beta} -expansion of x x . Let 𝐔 β , N \mathbf{U}_{{\beta},N} be the set of all x x ’s in Γ β , N \Gamma_{{\beta},N} which have unique β {\beta} -expansions. We give explicit formula of the Hausdorff dimension of 𝐔 β , N \mathbf{U}_{{\beta},N} for β {\beta} in any admissible interval [ β L , β U ] [{{\beta}}_{L},{{\beta}}_{U}] , where β L {{\beta}_{L}} is a purely Parry number while β U {{\beta}_{U}} is a transcendental number whose quasi-greedy expansion of 1 1 is related to the classical Thue-Morse sequence. This allows us to calculate the Hausdorff dimension of 𝐔 β , N \mathbf{U}_{{\beta},N} for almost every β > 1 \beta>1 . In particular, this improves the main results of Gábor Kallós (1999, 2001). Moreover, we find that the dimension function f ⁡ ( β ) = dim H 𝐔 β , N f({\beta})=\dim_{H}\mathbf{U}_{{\beta},N} fluctuates frequently for β ∈ ( 1 , N ) {\beta}\in(1,N) . Keywords : unique beta expansion, Hausdorff dimension, generalized Thue-Morse sequence, admissible block, admissible interval, transcendental number. MSC: 37B10, 11A67, 28A80

Introduction

Given an integer
N\geq 2
and a real number
{\beta}>1
, we call the infinite
sequence
(d_{i})
a
{\beta}
-expansion
of

if we can write

x=\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\frac{d_{i}}{{\beta}^{i}}

with
d_{i}\in\{0,1,\cdots,N-1\}
for all
i\geq 1.

Let
\Gamma_{{\beta},N}
be the set of all such
x
’s, i.e.,

\Gamma_{{\beta},N}=\Big\{\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\frac{d_{i}}{{\beta}^{i}}:d_{i}\in\{0,1,\cdots,N-1\},i\geq 1\Big\}.

Then
\Gamma_{{\beta},N}
is a self-similar set generated by
the
iterated function systems
(IFS)
\{f_{d}(x)=(x+d)/{\beta}:d\in\{0,1,\cdots,N-1\}\}
(cf.
[
16
]
).
Let
\{0,1,\cdots,N-1\}^{\infty}
be the set of all expansions
(d_{i})
with each digit
d_{i}\in\{0,1,\cdots,N-1\}
.
We define the projection map
\Pi_{\beta}
from
\{0,1,\cdots,N-1\}^{\infty}
to
\Gamma_{{\beta},N}
by

(1)

\Pi_{\beta}((d_{i}))=\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\frac{d_{i}}{{\beta}^{i}}.

When

{\beta}>N
, the IFS
\{f_{d}(\cdot):d\in\{0,1,\cdots,N-1\}\}

satisfies the
strong separation condition
(SSC), and then the map
\Pi_{\beta}
is bijective which implies that
every point in
\Gamma_{{\beta},N}
has a unique
{\beta}
-expansion. When

{\beta}=N
, the IFS
\{f_{d}(\cdot):d\in\{0,1,\cdots,N-1\}\}
fails
the SSC but satisfies the
open set condition
(OSC). Then all
except for countably many points in
\Gamma_{{\beta},N}
have unique

{\beta}
-expansions.

However, when
{\beta}<N
, the IFS
\{f_{d}(\cdot):d\in\{0,1,\cdots,N-1\}\}
fails the OSC. In this case,
\Gamma_{{\beta},N}=[0,(N-1)/({\beta}-1)]
and almost every point in
\Gamma_{{\beta},N}
have continuum of
{\beta}
-expansions (cf.
[
33
,
9
,
35
]
).
This has close connections to representations of real numbers in non-integer bases. After the seminal works of Rényi
[
31
]
and Parry
[
29
]

-expansions were widely considered from many aspects of mathematics, such as dynamical systems, measure theory, probability, number theory and so on (cf.
[
32
,
17
,
15
,
10
,
30
,
34
,
13
,
36
]
).

In 1990 Erdös, Joó and Komornik
[
15
]
showed for

that for
{\beta}\in(1,G)
any internal point of
\Gamma_{{\beta},N}
has continuum of
{\beta}
-expansions, and for
{\beta}\in(G,2)
there exist infinitely many points of
\Gamma_{{\beta},N}
having unique
{\beta}
-expansions (cf.
[

]
), where

is the golden ratio.
Recently, Baker
[

]
generalized their result and showed for

that there exists

defined by

(2)

G_{N}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}k+1&\textrm{if}~N=2k+1,\\
\frac{k+\sqrt{k^{2}+4k}}{2}&\textrm{if}~N=2k,\end{array}\right.

such that for each
{\beta}\in(1,G_{N})
any internal point of
\Gamma_{{\beta},N}
has continuum of
{\beta}
-expansions, and for

there exist infinitely many points in
\Gamma_{{\beta},N}
having unique
{\beta}
-expansions (cf.
[

]
).

Let

which have unique

-expansions, i.e.,

When

,

,

,

Recently, De Vries and Komornik

,

,

Here

The purpose of this paper is to investigate the Hausdorff dimension of

Later in

In this paper we improve the main results of Kallós

2.
Preliminary and main results

Given an integer
N\geq 2
, for any
{\beta}\in(1,N)
the set
\Gamma_{{\beta},N}
is a closed interval, i.e.,
\Gamma_{{\beta},N}=[0,(N-1)/({\beta}-1)]
. Then any real number in this interval
\Gamma_{{\beta},N}
has a
{\beta}
-expansion, some of them may have multiple
{\beta}
-expansions. Among these expansions
we define the so-called
greedy
{\beta}
-expansion

(b_{i}(x))=(b_{i})
of
x\in\Gamma_{{\beta},N}
recursively as follows (cf.
[
29
]
). For
x\in\Gamma_{{\beta},N}
, if
b_{i}
has already been defined for
1\leq i<n
(no condition if
n=1
), then
b_{n}
is the largest element in
\{0,1,\cdots,N-1\}
satisfying

\frac{b_{1}}{{\beta}}+\frac{b_{2}}{{\beta}^{2}}+\cdots+\frac{b_{n}}{{\beta}^{n}}\leq x.

One can verify that
(b_{i})
is indeed a
{\beta}
-expansion of

x
. Moreover,
(b_{i})
is the largest
{\beta}
-expansion of
x
in the sense of lexicographical order among all
{\beta}
-expansions of
x
.

Accordingly, we define the so-called
quasi-greedy
{\beta}
-expansion

(a_{i}(x))=(a_{i})
of
x\in\Gamma_{{\beta},N}
recursively as follows (cf.
[
13
]
). For
x=0
we set
(a_{i})=0^{\infty}.
For
x\in\Gamma_{{\beta},N}\setminus\{0\}
, if
a_{i}
has already been defined for
1\leq i<n
(no condition if
n=1
), then
a_{n}
is the largest element in
\{0,1,\cdots,N-1\}
satisfying

\frac{a_{1}}{{\beta}}+\frac{a_{2}}{{\beta}^{2}}+\cdots+\frac{a_{n}}{{\beta}^{n}}<x.

One can also verify that
(a_{i})
is indeed a
{\beta}
-expansion of

x
. Clearly, the quasi-greedy
{\beta}
-expansion of
x
is the
largest infinite
{\beta}
-expansion of
x
in the sense of lexicographical
order among all
{\beta}
-expansions of
x
. Here we call a

{\beta}
-expansion
infinite
if the expansion has infinitely many
non-zero elements.

For a positive integer
p
let
\{0,1,\cdots,N-1\}^{p}
be the set of blocks
c_{1}\cdots c_{p}
of length
p
with each element
c_{i}\in\{0,1,\cdots,N-1\}
. For two blocks
c_{1}\cdots c_{p}
and
d_{1}\cdots d_{q}
let
c_{1}\cdots c_{p}d_{1}\cdots d_{q}\in\{0,1,\cdots,N-1\}^{p+q}
denote their concatenation.
In particular, let
(c_{1}\cdots c_{p})^{k}
denote the
k
times concatenations of
c_{1}\cdots c_{p}
to itself, and let
(c_{1}\cdots c_{p})^{\infty}
denote the infinite concatenations of
c_{1}\cdots c_{p}
to itself.
For a digit
c\in\{0,1,\cdots,N-1\}
its

reflection

\overline{c}
is defined by

\overline{c}:=N-1-c.

Accordingly, if
c_{i}\in\{0,1,\cdots,N-1\}
for

i\geq 1
, we shall also write
\overline{c_{1}\cdots c_{n}}
instead of

\overline{c_{1}}\cdots\overline{c_{n}}
, and
\overline{c_{1}c_{2}\cdots}

instead of
\overline{c_{1}}\,\overline{c_{2}}\cdots
.
Finally, for a block
c_{1}\cdots c_{p}\in\{0,1,\cdots,N-1\}^{p}
with
c_{p}>0

we set

c_{1}\cdots c_{p}^{-}:=c_{1}\cdots c_{p-1}(c_{p}-1).

Similarly, for a block
c_{1}\cdots c_{p}\in\{0,1,\cdots,N-1\}^{p}
with
c_{p}<N-1
we set

c_{1}\cdots c_{p}^{+}:=c_{1}\cdots c_{p-1}(c_{p}+1).

In particular, when
p=1
we set
c_{1}\cdots c_{p}^{-}=c_{1}^{-}=c_{1}-1
and
c_{1}\cdots c_{p}^{+}=c_{1}^{+}=c_{1}+1
.

In the sequel we will use
lexicographical order between blocks and sequences.

Definition 2.1
.

A block
t_{1}\cdots t_{p}\in\{0,1,\cdots,N-1\}^{p}
is called an
admissible block
if
t_{p}<N-1
and for any
1\leq i\leq p
we have

\overline{t_{1}\cdots t_{p}}\leq t_{i}\cdots t_{p}t_{1}\cdots t_{i-1}\quad\textrm{and}\quad t_{i}\cdots t_{p}^{+}\,\overline{t_{1}\cdots t_{i-1}}\leq{t_{1}\cdots t_{p}^{+}}.

Clearly, there exist infinitely many admissible blocks.
In the following we introduce a generalized Thue-Morse sequence which plays an essential role in this paper.

Definition 2.2
.

For a block
t_{1}\cdots t_{p}\in\{0,1,\cdots,N-1\}^{p}
with
t_{p}<N-1
, we call the sequence
(\theta_{i})=(\theta_{i}(t_{1}\cdots t_{p}^{+}))
a
generalized Thue-Morse sequence
generated by the block
t_{1}\cdots t_{p}^{+}
if
(\theta_{i})
can be defined by induction as follows. First, we set

\theta_{1}\cdots\theta_{p}=t_{1}\cdots t_{p}^{+}.

Then, if
\theta_{1}\cdots\theta_{2^{m}p}
is already defined for some nonnegative integer
m
, we set

\theta_{2^{m}p+1}\cdots\theta_{2^{m+1}p}=\overline{\theta_{1}\cdots\theta_{2^{m}p}}\,^{+}.

We first discovered the generalized Thue-Morse sequences from the work of De Vries and Komornik
[
13
]
. Later, we found that these sequences were previously studied by Allouche and Cosnard
[
1
]
, Komornik and Loreti
[
24
]
, et al.

If
N=2k+1
, then the sequence
(\lambda_{i}(N))
defined in Equation (
3
) is exactly the generalized Thue-Morse sequence
(\theta_{i}(k+1))
. If
N=2k
, by using Lemma

one can also show that

Thus, for any

we have

(\lambda_{i}(N))=(\theta_{i}(\lceil\frac{N}{2}\rceil)),

where

denotes the least integer larger than or equal to
x
.

In the remainder of the paper we will reserve the notation

(\alpha_{i}({\beta}))
especially for the quasi-greedy
{\beta}
-expansion of

1\in\Gamma_{{\beta},N}=[0,(N-1)/({\beta}-1)]
(since

).

Theorem 2.3
.

For
N\geq 2
, let
t_{1}\cdots t_{p}\in\{0,1,\cdots,N-1\}^{p}
. Then
t_{1}\cdots t_{p}
is an admissible block if and only if
(\alpha_{i}({{\beta}_{L}}))=(t_{1}\cdots t_{p})^{\infty}
and
(\alpha_{i}({{\beta}_{U}}))=(\theta_{i}(t_{1}\cdots t_{p}^{+}))
for some bases

, where

is the critical base defined in
(
2
)
.
Moreover,
{{\beta}_{L}}<{{\beta}_{U}}
, and
{{\beta}_{L}}
is algebraic while
{{\beta}_{U}}
is transcendental.

We point out that Theorem
2.3
generalizes some results in
[
2
]
and
[
23
]
.
Here we call the transcendental numbers
{\beta}_{U}

De Vries-Komornik constants
since these numbers were first studied by De Vries and Komornik in
[
13
]
.
Later in Proposition
4.3
and Theorem
4.4
we will show that
t_{1}\cdots t_{p}
is an admissible block if and only if
(\alpha_{i}({\beta}_{U}))=(\theta_{i}(t_{1}\cdots t_{p}^{+}))
, if and only if
(\theta_{i}(t_{1}\cdots t_{p}^{+}))
is the unique
{\beta}_{U}
-expansion of
1
.

Definition 2.4
.

The closed interval
[{{\beta}_{L}},{{\beta}_{U}}]
given in Theorem
2.3
is called an
admissible interval
generated by
t_{1}\cdots t_{p}

(
simply called,
admissible interval
)
if

(\alpha_{i}({{\beta}_{L}}))=(t_{1}\cdots t_{p})^{\infty}\quad\textrm{and}\quad(\alpha_{i}({{\beta}_{U}}))=(\theta_{i}(t_{1}\cdots t_{p}^{+})).

Since we have infinitely many admissible intervals, it is worthwhile to investigate the size of union of these admissible intervals and the relationship between them as well.

Theorem 2.5
.

The union of all admissible intervals covers almost every point of
(\beta_{c}(N),N)
, where
\beta_{c}(N)
is the Komornik-Loreti constant. Moreover, for any two admissible intervals
[{\alpha_{L}},{\alpha_{U}}]
and
[{{\beta}_{L}},{{\beta}_{U}}]
, either
[\alpha_{L},\alpha_{U}]\cap[{\beta}_{L},{\beta}_{U}]=\emptyset
or
\alpha_{U}={\beta}_{U}
.

Theorem
2.5
says that for any two admissible intervals, either they are separated from each other or they have the same right endpoint. Now we state our main result on the Hausdorff dimension of
\mathbf{U}_{{\beta},N}
.

Theorem 2.6
.

For
N\geq 2
, let
[{{\beta}_{L}},{{\beta}_{U}}]
be an admissible interval generated by
t_{1}\cdots t_{p}
.
Then for any
{\beta}\in[{{\beta}_{L}},{{\beta}_{U}}]
the Hausdorff dimension of
\mathbf{U}_{{\beta},N}
is given by

\dim_{H}\mathbf{U}_{{\beta},N}=\frac{h(Z_{t_{1}\cdots t_{p}})}{\log{\beta}},

where
h(Z_{t_{1}\cdots t_{p}})
is the topological entropy of the subshift of finite type

Z_{t_{1}\cdots t_{p}}:=\big\{(d_{i}):\overline{t_{1}\cdots t_{p}}\leq d_{n}\cdots d_{n+p-1}\leq t_{1}\cdots t_{p},n\geq 1\big\}.

We point out that when
N=2
Barrera
[
8
]
investigated the topological entropy of
\mathbf{U}_{{\beta},N}
. We also point out that Theorem
2.6
generalizes some results in
[
12
,
20
,
21
,
5
]
. This will be explained in Section

via some examples for which the Hausdorff dimension of
\mathbf{U}_{{\beta},N}
can be calculated explicitly.

3.
Properties of unique expansions

Recall that
(\alpha_{i}({\beta}))
is the quasi-greedy

{\beta}
-expansion of
1
. The following characterization of
(\alpha_{i}({\beta}))
can be proved by a slight modification of the proof of
[
13
, Proposition 2.3]
(see also,
[
6
, Theorem 2.2]

Proposition 3.1
.

Let
N\geq 2
and
(\alpha_{i}({\beta}))
be the quasi-greedy
{\beta}
-expansion of
1
w.r.t. the digit set
\{0,1,\cdots,N-1\}
. Then the map
{\beta}\rightarrow(\alpha_{i}({\beta}))
is a strictly increasing bijection from the interval
(1,N]
onto the set of all infinite sequences
(\gamma_{i})\in\{0,1,\cdots,N-1\}^{\infty}
satisfying

\gamma_{k+1}\gamma_{k+2}\cdots\leq\gamma_{1}\gamma_{2}\cdots\quad\textrm{for all}~k\geq 0.

Moreover, the map
{\beta}\rightarrow(\alpha_{i}({\beta}))
is continuous w.r.t. the topology in
\{0,1,\cdots,N-1\}^{\infty}
induced by the
metric defined by
d\big((c_{i}),(d_{i})\big)=2^{-\min\{j:c_{j}\neq d_{j}\}}
.

In the sequel we will write
(\alpha_{i})
instead of
(\alpha_{i}({\beta}))
for the quasi-greedy
{\beta}
-expansion of
1
if no confusion arises for
{\beta}
.
The following proposition for the characterization of greedy expansions can be proved in a similar way as in
[
15
]
(see also,
[
6
, Theorem 3.2]
).

Proposition 3.2
.

For
N\geq 2
and
{\beta}\in(1,N]
,
(b_{i}(x))=(b_{i})
is the greedy
{\beta}
-expansion of some
x\in[0,(N-1)/({\beta}-1)]
if and only if

b_{n+1}b_{n+2}\cdots<\alpha_{1}\alpha_{2}\cdots

whenever
b_{n}<N-1
.

By Proposition
3.2
we have an equivalent characterization for the greedy expansions (see also
[
13
,
6
]
).

Proposition 3.3
.

For
N\geq 2
and
{\beta}\in(1,N]
,
(b_{i})=(b_{i}(x))
is the greedy
{\beta}
-expansion of some
x\in[0,(N-1)/({\beta}-1)]
if and only if

(4)

b_{n+k+1}b_{n+k+2}\cdots<\alpha_{1}\alpha_{2}\cdots

for all
k\geq 0
whenever
b_{n}<N-1
.

Proof.

The sufficiency follows directly by taking
k=0
in Equation (
4
) and then using Proposition
3.2
. For the necessity, suppose
(b_{i})
is the greedy expansion of some
x
, and suppose
b_{n}<N-1
for some
n\geq 1
. By Proposition
3.2
we have

(5)

{{\beta}_{L}}<{{\beta}_{U}}

Lemma 5.5

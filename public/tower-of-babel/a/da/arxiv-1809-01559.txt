Conformal scattering of the Maxwell-scalar field system on de Sitter space
Grigalius Taujanskas

Abstract
We prove small data energy estimates of all orders of differentiability between past null infinity and future null infinity of de Sitter space for the conformally invariant Maxwell-scalar field system. This allows us to construct bounded and invertible, but nonlinear, scattering operators taking past asymptotic data to future asymptotic data. We also deduce exponential decay rates for solutions with data having at least two derivatives, and for more regular solutions discover an asymptotic decoupling of the scalar field from the charge. The construction involves a carefully chosen complete gauge fixing condition which allows us to control all components of the Maxwell potential, and a nonlinear Grönwall inequality for higher order estimates.



Studies of scattering go back to the beginnings of physics. Perhaps the most famous modern mathematical treatment was developed in the 1960s by Lax and Phillips
[
33
,
34
]
, who used spectral techniques to study the scattering of a wave by an obstacle in flat space. In general relativity it is of interest to study metric scattering, that is the effects of curved space on the asymptotic behaviour of fields. Around the same time as Lax and Phillips were developing their framework, Roger Penrose discovered a way to compactify certain spacetimes by conformally rescaling the metric and attaching a boundary,
\mathscr{I}

[
43
,
44
]
. He called the class of spacetimes admitting such a compactification
asymptotically simple
and the boundary so attached
null infinity
, for this was where all null geodesics ended up ‘at infinity’. This led to a brand new way of viewing the asymptotics of massless fields in general relativity: one works in Penrose’s conformally compactified spacetime and studies the regularity of fields on
\mathscr{I}
, and then translates the regularity in the conformally rescaled spacetime to fall-off conditions in the physical spacetime.

It was not until the work of Friedlander
[
19
,
20
]
in 1980, however, that it was understood that the approaches of Lax and Phillips on the one hand and Penrose on the other could be combined into a robust
geometric
formulation of scattering theory. Friedlander showed that, although one cannot perform the same analytically explicit constructions in curved space, one can make sense of the Lax–Phillips asymptotic profiles of fields by identifying them with suitably rescaled limits of fields going to infinity along null directions. These became known as Friedlander’s radiation fields. The ideas of such
conformal scattering
were taken up by Baez, Segal and Zhou
[
6
,
7
,
8
,
9
]
to study a nonlinear wave equation and to some extent Yang–Mills equations on flat space, and later by Mason and Nicolas
[
35
,
36
]
to study linear equations on a large class of asymptotically simple spacetimes constructed by Corvino, Schoen, Chruściel, Delay, Klainerman, Nicolò, Friedrich and others
[
12
,
13
,
15
,
16
,
30
,
31
]
.
This spurred a programme of constructing conformal scattering theories for various fields on a variety of backgrounds and since then a number of works have appeared, many focussing on conformal scattering on black hole spacetimes

The above programmes were concerned mainly with asymptotically flat spacetimes. However, astronomical observations have by now shown that the cosmological constant
\Lambda
in our universe, though tiny, is positive
[
46
,
47
,
49
,
52
]
. It is thus of interest to study scattering, especially of nonlinear fields, on de Sitter space. De Sitter space is the Lorentzian analogue of the sphere in Euclidean geometry and one of the three maximally symmetric solutions to the vacuum Einstein equations as classified by the sign of the cosmological constant, with flat Euclidean space corresponding to Minkowski space (
\Lambda=0
) and hyperbolic space corresponding to anti-de Sitter space (
\Lambda<0
). As such, de Sitter space differs from Minkowski space in several crucial aspects. Firstly, it is not asymptotically flat. Nonetheless, it is asymptotically simple in the sense of Penrose
[
44
]
and so admits a conformal compactification. Secondly, the positive cosmological constant, no matter how small, renders null infinity
spacelike
in de Sitter space, which has implications for conformal scattering. In the asymptotically flat case the constructions of Mason and Nicolas required the resolution of a global linear Goursat problem, which had been shown by Hörmander
[
25
]
to be solvable in some generality. In de Sitter space, however, a spacelike
\mathscr{I}
means that the construction of a scattering theory instead requires the resolution of a regular Cauchy problem. Thirdly, while obtaining flat space scattering and peeling results through conformal techniques is fine for linear fields, nonlinear fields generically possess so-called charges at spacelike infinity
[
48
,
1
,
14
]
. This is a major obstruction to constructing conformal scattering theories for nonlinear fields in asymptotically flat spacetimes and is related to infrared divergences in quantum field theory
[
32
,
40
]
. The problem is entirely absent in de Sitter space as it is spatially compact.

From an analytic point of view, it has been known since the work of Friedrich
[
21
,
22
]
that de Sitter space is a stable solution of Einstein’s equations with a positive cosmological constant. Moreover, a recent and much celebrated result of Hintz and Vasy has shown that Kerr-de Sitter black holes are stable
[
24
]
. One therefore expects scattering results on de Sitter space to fit into a larger host of stories on asymptotically de Sitter spacetimes. Other results in this vein have been obtained by, for example, Vasy, Melrose and Sá Barreto,
[
56
,
38
]
. From a more physical perspective, de Sitter space has the peculiar feature that no single observer can ever observe the entire spacetime, in contrast to the Minkowski case where an observer’s past lightcone eventually contains the whole history of the universe. This is related to the existence of cosmological horizons, null hypersurfaces criss-crossing the Penrose diagram of de Sitter space. Their existence has implications for the definition of a classical scattering matrix: the construction of one requires a timelike Killing or conformally Killing vector field, and here one has a choice in de Sitter space. One might wish to use the Killing field provided by the standard static coordinates, i.e. the coordinates an observer at the south pole in de Sitter space might use for themselves, but this is problematic as it fails to be timelike and future pointing beyond the cosmological horizons. Another approach is to conformally compactify de Sitter space and embed it in the Einstein cylinder, where one has a natural globally timelike Killing field which becomes conformally Killing in physical de Sitter space. This can then be used to define an observer-oblivious classical scattering matrix in de Sitter space. We adopt the latter approach here. The importance of the construction of such scattering matrices for quantum gravity in de Sitter is explained well in
[
53
]
and the references therein.

1
Results

We prove small data energy estimates of all orders of differentiability
m

\mathscr{I}^{-}

\mathscr{I}^{+}

We find that, using a careful choice of gauge, it is possible to control all components of the Maxwell potential and the scalar field, and close the estimates using a nonlinear Grönwall inequality. .

The scalar field and the components of the Maxwell potential decay exponentially in proper time along timelike geodesics approaching

Since the nonlinearities are of the same order, in principle there is no obstruction to extending our estimates to the Yang–Mills–Higgs system on de Sitter space. As a result, the same scattering and decay results should apply there.

3
The Conformally Invariant Maxwell-Scalar Field System

Let
(\mathcalboondox{M},g)
be a
4
-dimensional Lorentzian manifold and consider the Lagrangian density

\mathcal{L}=-\frac{1}{4}F_{ab}F^{ab}+\frac{1}{2}\mathrm{D}_{a}\phi\overline{\mathrm{D}^{a}\phi}-\frac{1}{12}R|\phi|^{2},

(3.1)

where
F_{ab}=2\nabla_{[a}A_{b]}
is a real
2
-form called the Maxwell field,
A_{a}
is a real
1
-form called the Maxwell potential,
\phi
is a complex scalar field on
\mathcalboondox{M}
,
R
is the scalar curvature of
g_{ab}
, and
\mathrm{D}_{a}\phi=\nabla_{a}\phi+iA_{a}\phi
, where
\nabla_{a}
is the Levi–Civita connection of
g_{ab}
. The differential operator
\mathrm{D}_{a}
is called the gauge covariant derivative. The Euler–Lagrange equations associated to (
3.1
) are

\nabla^{b}F_{ab}=\operatorname{Im}\left(\bar{\phi}\mathrm{D}_{a}\phi\right)\quad\text{and}\quad\mathrm{D}^{a}\mathrm{D}_{a}\phi+\frac{1}{6}R\phi=0.

(3.2)

The Maxwell-scalar field system (
3.1
) is the simplest classical field theory exhibiting a non-trivial gauge dependence. Indeed, the
1
-form
A_{a}
is not uniquely determined by the
2
-form
F_{ab}
, and any transformation of the form

A_{a}\longmapsto A_{a}+\nabla_{a}\chi

leaves
F_{ab}
unchanged. This transforms

\mathrm{D}_{a}\phi=\nabla_{a}\phi+iA_{a}\phi\longmapsto\nabla_{a}\phi+i(A_{a}+\nabla_{a}\chi)\phi=\mathrm{e}^{-i\chi}\mathrm{D}_{a}(\mathrm{e}^{i\chi}\phi),

so that if one makes the corresponding transformation

\phi\longmapsto\mathrm{e}^{-i\chi}\phi,

the Lagrangian (
3.1
), and thus also the field equations (
3.2
), remain unchanged.

Remark 3.1
.

The gauge covariant derivative
\mathrm{D}_{a}
acting on
\phi
is a connection on a principal bundle
P
over
\mathcalboondox{M}
with fibre
\mathrm{U}(1)
. This connection is represented by the real
1
-form
A_{a}
on
\mathcalboondox{M}
in any trivialisation of
P
, where the factor of
i
in
\mathrm{D}_{a}
comes from
\mathfrak{u}(1)=i\mathbb{R}
. The scalar field
\phi
is a section of a complex line bundle over
\mathcalboondox{M}
associated to
P
by the representation
\mathrm{e}^{i\chi}
of
\mathrm{U}(1)
.

Consider a conformal rescaling of
(\mathcalboondox{M},g)
,

\hat{g}_{ab}=\Omega^{2}g_{ab}.

(3.3)

It turns out that in many cases it is possible to fully or partially compactify
\mathcalboondox{M}
by choosing the conformal factor
\Omega
so that it compensates for the divergence of distances with respect to the physical metric
g
and attach the boundary
\mathscr{I}\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=\{\Omega=0\}
to
\mathcalboondox{M}
; this is Roger Penrose’s notion of asymptotically simple spacetimes first described around 1963 in
[
43
]
and
[
44
]
. For our purposes it will be sufficient to assume that the spacetime
\mathcalboondox{M}
is regular enough so that it may be compactified in this way to make a smooth compact manifold with boundary,
\widehat{\mathcalboondox{M}}\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=\mathcalboondox{M}\cup\mathscr{I}
, although weaker, partial compactifications leaving singularities at a finite number of points in the boundary are widely used to study, for example, black hole spacetimes
[
26
,
35
,
36
,
39
,
41
,
42
]
. We equip
\widehat{\mathcalboondox{M}}
with the rescaled (also called unphysical) metric
\hat{g}_{ab}
and call the spacetime
(\widehat{\mathcalboondox{M}},\hat{g})
the rescaled spacetime.

It is possible to transport the fields
(A_{a},\phi)
into the rescaled spacetime
\widehat{\mathcalboondox{M}}
by weighting them appropriately by the conformal factor
\Omega
so that the field equations (
3.2
) are preserved in
\widehat{\mathcalboondox{M}}
. The correct choice of conformal weights for
(A_{a},\phi)
are
(0,-1)
,

\hat{A}_{a}\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=A_{a},\qquad\quad\hat{\phi}\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=\Omega^{-1}\phi,

and we show below that this implies the conformal invariance of the Maxwell-scalar field system (
3.2
). Under the rescaling (
3.3
) the Christoffel symbols
\Gamma^{a}_{bc}
of
g_{ab}
transform as

\hat{\Gamma}^{a}_{bc}=\Gamma^{a}_{bc}+\Upsilon_{c}\delta^{a}_{b}+\Upsilon_{b}\delta^{a}_{c}-\Upsilon_{d}g^{ad}g_{bc},

where
\Upsilon_{a}\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=\Omega^{-1}\partial_{a}\Omega=\partial_{a}\log\Omega
, and using this one calculates that

-\frac{1}{4}F_{ab}F^{ab}=-\frac{1}{4}\Omega^{4}\hat{F}_{ab}\hat{F}^{ab}

and

\frac{1}{2}\mathrm{D}_{a}\phi\overline{\mathrm{D}^{a}\phi}=\frac{1}{2}\Omega^{4}\hat{\mathrm{D}}_{a}\hat{\phi}\overline{\hat{\mathrm{D}}^{a}\hat{\phi}}+\frac{1}{2}\Omega^{4}\left(2\Upsilon_{a}\operatorname{Re}(\hat{\phi}\overline{\hat{\mathrm{D}}^{a}\hat{\phi}})+\hat{g}^{ab}\Upsilon_{a}\Upsilon_{b}|\hat{\phi}|^{2}\right).

Moreover, because in
4

transforms as (see

45
]
, eq. (6.8.25))

\frac{1}{12}R=\Omega^{2}\left(\frac{1}{12}\hat{R}-\frac{1}{2}\hat{\nabla}^{a}\Upsilon_{a}+\frac{1}{2}\hat{g}^{ab}\Upsilon_{a}\Upsilon_{b}\right),

one has

-\frac{1}{12}R|\phi|^{2}=-\frac{1}{12}\Omega^{4}\hat{R}|\hat{\phi}|^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\Omega^{4}\left(\hat{\nabla}^{a}\Upsilon_{a}-\hat{g}^{ab}\Upsilon_{a}\Upsilon_{b}\right)|\hat{\phi}|^{2}.

Adding these together one sees that the Lagrangian transforms as

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}

\displaystyle=\Omega^{4}\hat{\mathcal{L}}+\frac{1}{2}\Omega^{4}\left(2\Upsilon_{a}\operatorname{Re}(\hat{\phi}\overline{\hat{\mathrm{D}}^{a}\hat{\phi}})+(\hat{\nabla}^{a}\Upsilon_{a})|\hat{\phi}|^{2}\right)

\displaystyle=\Omega^{4}\hat{\mathcal{L}}+\frac{1}{2}\Omega^{4}\left(\Upsilon_{a}\hat{\nabla}^{a}(|\hat{\phi}|^{2})+(\hat{\nabla}^{a}\Upsilon_{a})|\hat{\phi}|^{2}\right)

\displaystyle=\Omega^{4}\hat{\mathcal{L}}+\frac{1}{2}\Omega^{4}\hat{\nabla}^{a}(|\hat{\phi}|^{2}\Upsilon_{a}).

Now the volume form
\widehat{\dvol}
of
\widehat{\mathcalboondox{M}}
is related to the volume form
\dvol
of
\mathcalboondox{M}
by
\dvol=\Omega^{-4}\widehat{\dvol}
, so the action

S=\int_{\mathcalboondox{M}}\mathcal{L}\dvol

transforms as

S=\hat{S}+\frac{1}{2}\int_{\widehat{\mathcalboondox{M}}}\hat{\nabla}^{a}(|\hat{\phi}|^{2}\Upsilon_{a})\,\widehat{\dvol}=\hat{S}+\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathscr{I}}|\hat{\phi}|^{2}\Upsilon_{a}\hat{g}^{ab}\partial_{b}\intprod\widehat{\dvol}.

(3.4)

In other words,

is conformally invariant up to a boundary term. Since the Euler-Lagrange equations arise from a

variation of the action, this implies the conformal invariance of the field equations (
3.2
).

4.3
Choice of Energy-Momentum Tensor on
\mathfrak{E}

From now on we denote by
\phi
and
A_{a}
the scalar field and Maxwell potential on the Einstein cylinder
\mathfrak{E}
, and by
\tilde{\phi}
and
\tilde{A}_{a}
the conformally related physical fields on de Sitter space
\mathrm{dS}_{4}
,

\phi=\Omega^{-1}\tilde{\phi},\qquad\quad A_{a}=\tilde{A}_{a},

(4.5)

where
\Omega=H\cos\tau
.

We define the energy-momentum tensor for the system (
3.2
) on
\mathfrak{E}
to be

\displaystyle\begin{split}\mathbf{T}_{ab}[\phi,A]&\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=-F_{ac}F_{b}^{\phantom{b}c}+\frac{1}{4}\mathfrak{e}_{ab}F_{cd}F^{cd}+\overline{\mathrm{D}_{(a}\phi}\mathrm{D}_{b)}\phi-\frac{1}{2}\mathfrak{e}_{ab}\overline{\mathrm{D}_{c}\phi}\mathrm{D}^{c}\phi+\frac{1}{2}\mathfrak{e}_{ab}|\phi|^{2}\\
&\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=\mathbf{T}_{ab}[A]+\mathbf{T}_{ab}[\phi].\end{split}

(4.6)

One can check by direct calculation that, as a consequence of the field equations (
3.2
),
\mathbf{T}_{ab}
is conserved,

\nabla^{a}\mathbf{T}_{ab}=0,

so
\mathbf{T}_{ab}
is suitable for defining a conserved energy for the system (
3.2
),

\mathcal{E}_{\tau}[\phi,A]\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=\int_{\mathbb{S}^{3}_{\tau}}\mathbf{T}_{00}[\phi,A]\dvol_{\mathfrak{s}_{3}}=\int_{\mathbb{S}^{3}_{\tau}}\mathbf{T}_{ab}[\phi,A]T^{a}T^{b}\dvol_{\mathfrak{s}_{3}}.

(4.7)

Since
T^{a}
is Killing on
\mathfrak{E}
, this clearly satisfies

\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}\tau}\mathcal{E}_{\tau}[\phi,A]=0

if the field equations (
3.2
) are satisfied. We call (
4.7
) the
geometric
energy for the system (
3.2
). We also define the geometric energies for the individual sectors of the scalar field
\phi
and the Maxwell potential
A_{a}
,

\mathcal{E}_{\tau}[\phi]\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=\int_{\mathbb{S}^{3}_{\tau}}\mathbf{T}_{00}[\phi]\dvol_{\mathfrak{s}_{3}},\qquad\quad\mathcal{E}_{\tau}[A]\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=\int_{\mathbb{S}^{3}_{\tau}}\mathbf{T}_{00}[A]\dvol_{\mathfrak{s}_{3}}.

The sectorial geometric energies
\mathcal{E}_{\tau}[\phi]
and
\mathcal{E}_{\tau}[A]
are not conserved individually and can exchange energy throughout the evolution, but of course the total geometric energy
\mathcal{E}_{\tau}[\phi,A]=\mathcal{E}_{\tau}[\phi]+\mathcal{E}_{\tau}[A]
is. For
m\geqslant 1
we also define the Sobolev-type approximate energies

\displaystyle\mathrm{S}_{m}[\phi]\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=\|\dot{\phi}\|^{2}_{H^{m-1}}+\|\phi\|^{2}_{H^{m}},

\displaystyle\mathrm{S}_{m}[A]\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=\mathrm{S}_{m}[\mathbf{A}]+\mathrm{S}_{m}[A_{0}],

\displaystyle\mathrm{S}_{m}[\mathbf{A}]\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=\|\dot{\mathbf{A}}\|^{2}_{H^{m-1}}+\|\mathbf{A}\|^{2}_{H^{m}},

\displaystyle\mathrm{S}_{m}[\phi,\mathbf{A}]\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=\mathrm{S}_{m}[\phi]+\mathrm{S}_{m}[\mathbf{A}],

\displaystyle\mathrm{S}_{m}[A_{0}]\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=\|A_{0}\|^{2}_{H^{m}},

\displaystyle\mathrm{S}_{m}[\phi,A]\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=\mathrm{S}_{m}[\phi,\mathbf{A}]+\mathrm{S}_{m}[A_{0}],

where
H^{0}=L^{2}
. Furthermore, for brevity we will often simply write
\mathrm{S}_{m}
to mean
\mathrm{S}_{m}[\phi,A]
.

4.4
Scaling of Initial Energies

We will consider initial data on the hypersurface
\{\tau=0\}=\{\eta=0\}
and use the coordinate
\tau
and the metric
\mathfrak{e}
on the rescaled spacetime, and the coordinate
\eta
and the metric (
4.1
) on the physical spacetime. By differentiating the relationship
\tan(\tau/2)=\tanh(H\eta/2)
we find

\mathrm{d}\tau=\frac{H}{\cosh(H\eta)}\mathrm{d}\eta,

so raising indices with
\mathfrak{e}^{-1}=\Omega^{-2}g^{-1}
, where
g
is the metric (
4.1
), we find that
\partial_{\tau}
and
\partial_{\eta}
are related by

\partial_{\tau}=\frac{\cosh(H\eta)}{H}\partial_{\eta}.

Furthermore, the conformal factor
\Omega
in the global coordinates (
4.1
) is given by

\Omega=H\cos\tau=\frac{H}{\cosh(H\eta)}.

Consider the rescaled energies

\mathrm{S}_{m}[\phi,A](\tau)=\|\dot{\phi}\|^{2}_{H^{m-1}}(\tau)+\|\phi\|^{2}_{H^{m}}(\tau)+\|\dot{\mathbf{A}}\|^{2}_{H^{m-1}}(\tau)+\|\mathbf{A}\|^{2}_{H^{m}}(\tau)+\|A_{0}\|^{2}_{H^{m}}(\tau).

On the initial surface
\{\tau=0\}=\{\eta=0\}
the conformal factor is a constant and has vanishing derivative,
\partial_{\tau}\Omega|_{\tau=0}=0
, so the rescaled scalar field
\phi
is related to the physical scalar field
\tilde{\phi}
by

\phi|_{\tau=0}=(\Omega^{-1}\tilde{\phi})|_{\tau=0}=\frac{1}{H}\tilde{\phi}|_{\eta=0},

while their time derivatives are related by

\dot{\phi}|_{\tau=0}=(\Omega^{-1}\partial_{\tau}\tilde{\phi}-(\partial_{\tau}\Omega)\Omega^{-2}\tilde{\phi})|_{\tau=0}=\frac{1}{H^{2}}\partial_{\eta}\tilde{\phi}|_{\eta=0}.

Since the conformal factor is independent of the
\mathbb{S}^{3}
coordinates,
\not{\nabla}\Omega=0
, and the metric induced on
\{\eta=0\}
by (
4.1
) is equivalent to
\mathfrak{s}_{3}
, the rescaled and physical norms of the scalar field are equivalent,

\|\dot{\phi}\|^{2}_{H^{m-1}}(\tau=0)+\|\phi\|^{2}_{H^{m}}(\tau=0)\simeq\|\partial_{\eta}\tilde{\phi}\|^{2}_{H^{m-1}}(\eta=0)+\|\tilde{\phi}\|^{2}_{H^{m}}(\eta=0),

where there is equality if
H=1
. One similarly checks that

\displaystyle\|\dot{\mathbf{A}}\|^{2}_{H^{m-1}}(\tau=0)+\|\mathbf{A}\|^{2}_{H^{m}}(\tau=0)\simeq\|\partial_{\eta}\tilde{\mathbf{A}}\|^{2}_{H^{m-1}}(\eta=0)+\|\tilde{\mathbf{A}}\|^{2}_{H^{m}}(\eta=0)

and

\|A_{0}\|^{2}_{H^{m}}(\tau=0)\simeq\|\tilde{A}_{\eta}\|^{2}_{H^{m}}(\eta=0),

where
A_{0}\mathrm{d}\tau+\mathbf{A}_{\mu}\mathrm{d}x^{\mu}=A=\tilde{A}=\tilde{A}_{\eta}\mathrm{d}\eta+\tilde{\mathbf{A}}_{\mu}\mathrm{d}x^{\mu}
, and
x^{\mu}
are coordinates on
\mathbb{S}^{3}
. Thus

\mathrm{S}_{m}[\phi,\mathbf{A}](\tau=0)\simeq\mathrm{S}_{m}[\tilde{\phi},\tilde{\mathbf{A}}](\eta=0),

(4.8)

and also
\mathrm{S}_{m}[A_{0}](\tau=0)\simeq\mathrm{S}_{m}[\tilde{A}_{\eta}](\eta=0)
.

6
Field Equations and Gauge Fixing

The field equations (
3.2
) can be written out in terms of the Maxwell potential
A_{a}
,

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\Box A_{a}-\nabla_{a}(\nabla^{b}A_{b})+R_{ab}A^{b}=-\operatorname{Im}\left(\bar{\phi}\mathrm{D}_{a}\phi\right),\\
&\Box\phi+2iA_{a}\nabla^{a}\phi+\left(\frac{1}{6}R-A_{a}A^{a}+i\nabla^{a}A_{a}\right)\phi=0.\end{split}

(6.1)

We shall be commuting differential operators into these equations, so it will be convenient to introduce the operators representing their left-hand sides. For any
1
-form
\omega
and any scalar field
\psi
we set

\mathfrak{M}(\omega)_{a}\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=\Box\omega_{a}-\nabla_{a}(\nabla_{b}\omega^{b})+R_{ab}\omega^{b}\quad\text{and}\quad\mathfrak{S}(\psi)\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=\mathrm{D}^{a}\mathrm{D}_{a}\psi+\frac{1}{6}R\psi.

The system (
6.1
) is then equivalent to

\mathfrak{M}(A)_{a}=-\operatorname{Im}\left(\bar{\phi}\mathrm{D}_{a}\phi\right)\quad\text{and}\quad\mathfrak{S}(\phi)=0.

(6.2)

In the following sections we specialise to the case of the Einstein cylinder
(\mathfrak{E},\mathfrak{e})
. As noted earlier, for ease of notation we will not hat any rescaled quantities on
\mathfrak{E}
and instead denote the corresponding physical quantities on
\mathrm{dS}_{4}
with a tilde, as in
\tilde{\phi}
or
\tilde{A}_{a}
. For the metric
\mathfrak{e}
we compute

R=6\qquad\text{and}\qquad R_{ab}\mathrm{d}x^{a}\mathrm{d}x^{b}=-2\mathfrak{s}_{3}.

6.1
Strong Coulomb Gauge

We will work in the Coulomb gauge adapted to the foliation
\mathcal{F}
,

\not{\nabla}\cdot\mathbf{A}=0,

(6.3)

but will also need to use the residual gauge freedom to fix the gauge fully. More precisely, given a solution
(A,\phi)
to the Maxwell-scalar field system (
6.1
), a general gauge transformation sends
\phi\mapsto\mathrm{e}^{-i\chi}\phi
and
A_{a}\mapsto A_{a}+\nabla_{a}\chi
, and
eq.

6.3
is imposed by solving the elliptic equation

\not{\Delta}\chi=-\not{\nabla}\cdot\mathbf{A}

on
\mathbb{S}^{3}_{\tau}
for every fixed
\tau
. This does not determine
\chi
uniquely: there is still the residual gauge freedom of
\chi\mapsto\chi+\chi_{\text{res.}}
, where
\chi_{\text{res.}}
solves

\not{\Delta}\chi_{\text{res.}}=0

on each
\mathbb{S}^{3}_{\tau}
. Because
\mathbb{S}^{3}
is compact, the kernel of the Laplacian
\not{\Delta}
is just the vector space of constant functions, i.e. those
\chi_{\text{res.}}
which satisfy
\not{\nabla}\chi_{\text{res.}}=0
, but the
\tau
dependence in the
\chi_{\text{res.}}
is still arbitrary. Thus in the Coulomb gauge we have the residual gauge freedom

\displaystyle\phi\longmapsto\mathrm{e}^{-i\chi_{\text{res.}}(\tau)}\phi,

\displaystyle A_{0}\longmapsto A_{0}+\dot{\chi}_{\text{res.}}(\tau),

\displaystyle\mathbf{A}\longmapsto\mathbf{A},

which allows one to choose

\dot{\chi}_{\text{res.}}(\tau)=-\frac{1}{|\mathbb{S}^{3}|}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{3}}A_{0}(\tau)\dvol_{\mathfrak{s}_{3}}=\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}-\bar{A}_{0}(\tau)

and so impose the additional gauge condition

\bar{A}_{0}(\tau)=0.

This determines
\chi_{\text{res.}}
up to the addition of a global constant, so there is very little remaining gauge freedom. Indeed, constants are irrelevant for the gauge transformation of
A_{a}
and only impart a constant phase change in
\phi
, so we have now fixed the gauge as completely as possible. We call this stronger gauge fixing condition

\not{\nabla}\cdot\mathbf{A}=0,\qquad\bar{A}_{0}=0

(6.4)

strong Coulomb gauge
. For us, the most useful feature of the strong Coulomb gauge will be the fact that in this gauge
A_{0}
will obey the Poincaré inequality on each leaf
\mathbb{S}^{3}_{\tau}
of
\mathcal{F}
,

\|A_{0}\|_{L^{2}}(\tau)\leqslant C\|\not{\nabla}A_{0}\|_{L^{2}}(\tau).

In strong Coulomb gauge the field equations (
6.1
) are equivalent to the system

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\Box\phi+2iA_{0}\dot{\phi}-2i\mathbf{A}\cdot\not{\nabla}\phi+(1-A^{2}_{0}+|\mathbf{A}|^{2}+i\dot{A}_{0})\phi=0,\\
&\Box\mathbf{A}+(2+|\phi|^{2})\mathbf{A}=-\operatorname{Im}(\bar{\phi}\not{\nabla}\phi)+\not{\nabla}\dot{A}_{0},\\
-&\not{\Delta}A_{0}+|\phi|^{2}A_{0}=-\operatorname{Im}(\bar{\phi}\dot{\phi}),\\
&\not{\nabla}\cdot\mathbf{A}=0,\\
&\bar{A}_{0}(\tau)=0.\end{split}

(6.5)

We do not prescribe initial data on
A_{0}
since it is non-dynamical: it is completely determined by
\phi
and
\dot{\phi}
via the elliptic equation on each slice of constant
\tau
. It is convenient to incorporate the constraint
\not{\nabla}\cdot\mathbf{A}=0
into the equations by projecting the equation for
\mathbf{A}
onto divergence free
1
-forms on
\mathbb{S}^{3}
. Let
\mathcal{P}
be this projection (see
Section

A.1
); then since

\not{\nabla}\cdot\Box\mathbf{A}=\Box(\not{\nabla}\cdot\mathbf{A})-2\not{\nabla}\cdot\mathbf{A}=0

and

\operatorname{curl}\not{\nabla}\dot{A}_{0}=0,

applying
\mathcal{P}
to the equation for
\mathbf{A}
gives

\Box\mathbf{A}+2\mathbf{A}+\mathcal{P}\left(|\phi|^{2}\mathbf{A}\right)=-\mathcal{P}\left(\operatorname{Im}(\bar{\phi}\not{\nabla}\phi)\right).

Thus the system (
6.5
) is equivalent to

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\Box\phi+2iA_{0}\dot{\phi}-2i\mathbf{A}\cdot\not{\nabla}\phi+(1-A_{0}^{2}+|\mathbf{A}|^{2}+i\dot{A}_{0})\phi=0,\\
&\Box\mathbf{A}+2\mathbf{A}+\mathcal{P}\left(|\phi|^{2}\mathbf{A}\right)=-\mathcal{P}\left(\operatorname{Im}(\bar{\phi}\not{\nabla}\phi)\right),\\
-&\not{\Delta}A_{0}+|\phi|^{2}A_{0}=-\operatorname{Im}(\bar{\phi}\dot{\phi}),\\
&\bar{A}_{0}(\tau)=0,\end{split}

(6.6)

provided one considers divergence-free initial data for
\mathbf{A}
and
\dot{\mathbf{A}}
. Indeed, it is easily seen that
v=\not{\nabla}\cdot\mathbf{A}
satisfies

\Box v=0,

so
v\equiv 0
whenever
v=0
and
\dot{v}=0
initially.

In addition to the restriction
\not{\nabla}\cdot\mathbf{A}_{0}=0=\not{\nabla}\cdot\mathbf{A}_{1}
on the initial data, the extra gauge condition
\bar{A}_{0}=0
restricts the set of initial data further. Suppose we prescribe initial data
\phi(\tau=0)=\phi_{0}
and
\dot{\phi}(\tau=0)=\phi_{1}
. We must then solve for
A_{0}(\tau=0)=a_{0}
by solving

8
Energies

8.1
The Maxwell Sector

For ease of presentation we treat the Maxwell and the scalar field sectors of the energy-momentum tensor
\mathbf{T}_{ab}
separately. The energy-momentum tensor for the Maxwell sector in terms of the Maxwell field
F_{ab}
on
\mathfrak{E}
is

\mathbf{T}_{ab}[F]=-F_{a}^{\phantom{a}c}F_{bc}+\frac{1}{4}\mathfrak{e}_{ab}F_{cd}F^{cd},

or in terms of the potential
A_{a}

\displaystyle\mathbf{T}_{ab}[A]=

\displaystyle-\nabla_{a}A^{c}\nabla_{b}A_{c}+\nabla^{c}A_{a}\nabla_{b}A_{c}+\nabla_{a}A^{c}\nabla_{c}A_{b}-\nabla^{c}A_{a}\nabla_{c}A_{b}

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{2}\mathfrak{e}_{ab}\left(\nabla_{c}A_{d}\nabla^{c}A^{d}-\nabla_{c}A_{d}\nabla^{d}A^{c}\right).

The Maxwell sector energy density with respect to the foliation
\mathcal{F}
is given by the component

\displaystyle\mathbf{T}_{00}[A]

\displaystyle=\mathbf{T}_{ab}T^{a}T^{b}

\displaystyle=-\dot{A}^{c}\dot{A}_{c}+2\dot{A}_{c}\nabla^{c}A_{0}-\nabla^{c}A_{0}\nabla_{c}A_{0}+\frac{1}{2}\left(\nabla_{c}A_{d}\nabla^{c}A^{d}-\nabla_{c}A_{d}\nabla^{d}A^{c}\right),

where in the above we have denoted by
A_{0}\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=T^{a}A_{a}
and
\dot{A}_{a}\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=T^{b}\nabla_{b}A_{a}
. Note that the metric
\mathfrak{e}
splits as the direct sum
\mathfrak{e}=g_{\mathbb{R}}\oplus(-\mathfrak{s}_{3})
, so in particular the full connection
\nabla
also splits as
\nabla=\nabla^{\mathbb{R}}\oplus\nabla^{\mathfrak{s}_{3}}=\partial_{\tau}\oplus\not{\nabla}
. This can also be seen at the level of the Christoffel symbols on
\mathfrak{E}
in
Proposition

A.3
. Furthermore, there is no curvature in the
\tau
direction (see
Proposition

A.5
), so in particular
\partial_{\tau}
commutes with the
3
-sphere derivatives,
[\partial_{\tau},\,\not{\nabla}]=0
. We have

\mathbf{T}_{00}[A]=\frac{1}{2}|\dot{\mathbf{A}}|^{2}+\frac{1}{2}|\not{\nabla}A_{0}|^{2}+\frac{1}{2}|\not{\nabla}\mathbf{A}|^{2}-\dot{\mathbf{A}}\cdot\not{\nabla}A_{0}-\frac{1}{2}(\not{\nabla}_{\mu}\mathbf{A}_{\nu})(\not{\nabla}^{\nu}\mathbf{A}^{\mu}).

(8.1)

We impose the Coulomb gauge

\not{\nabla}\cdot\mathbf{A}=0

on each
\mathbb{S}^{3}_{\tau}\simeq\mathbb{S}^{3}
so that the last two terms become non-negative-definite upon integration by parts:

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{S}^{3}}-\dot{\mathbf{A}}\cdot\not{\nabla}A_{0}\,\dvol_{\mathfrak{s}_{3}}=\int_{\mathbb{S}^{3}}A_{0}\not{\nabla}\cdot\dot{\mathbf{A}}\,\dvol_{\mathfrak{s}_{3}}=0,

and

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{S}^{3}}-\frac{1}{2}(\not{\nabla}_{\mu}\mathbf{A}_{\nu})(\not{\nabla}^{\nu}\mathbf{A}^{\mu})\,\dvol_{\mathfrak{s}_{3}}

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{S}^{3}}\left(\frac{1}{2}\mathbf{A}^{\mu}\not{\nabla}_{\mu}\not{\nabla}_{\nu}\mathbf{A}^{\nu}-\frac{1}{2}R({\mathfrak{s}_{3})}_{\mu\nu}\mathbf{A}^{\mu}\mathbf{A}^{\nu}\right)\dvol_{\mathfrak{s}_{3}}

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{S}^{3}}|\mathbf{A}|^{2}\,\dvol_{\mathfrak{s}_{3}}.

Thus the Maxwell energy on surfaces of constant
\tau
is

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}_{\tau}[A]

\displaystyle\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=\int_{\mathbb{S}^{3}}\mathbf{T}_{00}[A]\,\dvol_{\mathfrak{s}_{3}}(\tau)

\displaystyle\simeq\|\mathbf{A}\|^{2}_{H^{1}}(\tau)+\|\dot{\mathbf{A}}\|^{2}_{L^{2}}(\tau)+\|\not{\nabla}A_{0}\|^{2}_{L^{2}}(\tau)

\displaystyle=\mathrm{S}_{1}[\mathbf{A}](\tau)+\|\not{\nabla}A_{0}\|^{2}_{L^{2}}(\tau).

Imposing the additional condition
\bar{A}_{0}(\tau)=0
, one has that
\|A_{0}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{3})}^{2}\lesssim\|\not{\nabla}A_{0}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{3})}
, so

\mathcal{E}_{\tau}[A]\simeq\mathrm{S}_{1}[A](\tau)

(8.2)

for all
\tau\in\mathbb{R}
.

8.1.1
Higher Order Energies

More generally, for a
1

\alpha

\displaystyle\mathbf{T}_{ab}[\alpha]

\displaystyle\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=-\nabla_{a}\alpha^{c}\nabla_{b}\alpha_{c}+\nabla^{c}\alpha_{a}\nabla_{b}\alpha_{c}+\nabla_{a}\alpha^{c}\nabla_{c}\alpha_{b}-\nabla^{c}\alpha_{a}\nabla_{c}\alpha_{b}

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{2}\mathfrak{e}_{ab}\left(\nabla_{c}\alpha_{d}\nabla^{c}\alpha^{d}-\nabla_{c}\alpha_{d}\nabla^{d}\alpha^{c}\right).

When
\alpha_{a}=A_{a}
, this is, of course, just the Maxwell energy-momentum tensor written out in terms of the potential. As in

\mathbf{T}_{00}[\alpha]=\frac{1}{2}|\dot{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}|^{2}+\frac{1}{2}|\not{\nabla}\alpha_{0}|^{2}+\frac{1}{2}|\not{\nabla}\boldsymbol{\alpha}|^{2}-\dot{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}_{\mu}\not{\nabla}^{\mu}\alpha_{0}-\frac{1}{2}(\not{\nabla}_{\mu}\boldsymbol{\alpha}_{\nu})(\not{\nabla}^{\nu}\boldsymbol{\alpha}^{\mu}).

Integrating by parts as before we obtain

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}_{\tau}[\alpha]\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{S}^{3}}\mathbf{T}_{00}[\alpha]\,\dvol_{\mathfrak{s}_{3}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{3}}|\dot{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}|^{2}\,\dvol_{\mathfrak{s}_{3}}+\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{3}}|\not{\nabla}\alpha_{0}|^{2}\,\dvol_{\mathfrak{s}_{3}}+\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{3}}|\not{\nabla}\boldsymbol{\alpha}|^{2}\,\dvol_{\mathfrak{s}_{3}}

\displaystyle+\int_{\mathbb{S}^{3}}\alpha_{0}\not{\nabla}_{\mu}\dot{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}^{\mu}\,\dvol_{\mathfrak{s}_{3}}-\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{3}}|\not{\nabla}\cdot\boldsymbol{\alpha}|^{2}\,\dvol_{\mathfrak{s}_{3}}+\int_{\mathbb{S}^{3}}|\boldsymbol{\alpha}|^{2}\,\dvol_{\mathfrak{s}_{3}}.

For our second order estimates we will want to set
\alpha_{a}=X_{i}^{\mu}\not{\nabla}_{\mu}A_{a}\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=\not{\nabla}_{i}A_{a}
and sum over

the second term becomes

\sum_{i}|\not{\nabla}\alpha_{0}|^{2}=\sum_{i}\not{\nabla}_{\mu}\not{\nabla}_{i}A_{0}\not{\nabla}^{\mu}\not{\nabla}_{i}A_{0}=|\not{\nabla}^{2}A_{0}|^{2}+\text{l.o.t.s},

the third term becomes

\sum_{i}|\not{\nabla}\boldsymbol{\alpha}|^{2}=\sum_{i}\not{\nabla}_{\mu}\not{\nabla}_{i}\mathbf{A}_{\nu}\not{\nabla}^{\mu}\not{\nabla}_{i}\mathbf{A}^{\nu}=|\not{\nabla}^{2}\mathbf{A}|^{2}+\text{l.o.t.s},

\sum_{i}\alpha_{0}\not{\nabla}_{\mu}\dot{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}^{\mu}=\sum_{i}\not{\nabla}_{i}A_{0}\not{\nabla}_{\mu}\not{\nabla}_{i}\dot{\mathbf{A}}^{\mu}=\text{l.o.t.s},

\sum_{i}|\not{\nabla}\cdot\boldsymbol{\alpha}|^{2}=\sum_{i}\not{\nabla}_{\mu}\not{\nabla}_{i}\mathbf{A}^{\mu}\not{\nabla}_{\nu}\not{\nabla}_{i}\not{\mathbf{A}}^{\nu}=\text{l.o.t.s},

\not{\nabla}_{j}\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=X_{j}^{\mu}\not{\nabla}_{\mu}
, and the lower order terms are at most quadratic and of order zero and one in derivatives of

. The sixth and final term is

\sum_{i}|\boldsymbol{\alpha}|^{2}=\sum_{i}|\not{\nabla}_{i}\mathbf{A}|^{2}=\text{l.o.t.s}.

The lower order terms can be controlled by
\mathcal{E}_{\tau}[A]\simeq\mathrm{S}_{1}[A](\tau)
, so we can find a constant

large enough such that

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}_{\tau}[A]+\sum_{i}\mathcal{E}_{\tau}[\not{\nabla}_{i}A]

\mathcal{E}_{\tau}[A]+\sum_{i}\mathcal{E}_{\tau}[\not{\nabla}_{i}A]\simeq\mathrm{S}_{2}[A](\tau).

(8.3)

8.2.1

Proposition 8.1
.

For any fixed
\tau\in\mathbb{R}
and any sufficiently smooth complex scalar field

\mathfrak{E}

small enough such that if

\|\not{\nabla}\psi\|^{2}_{L^{2}}(\tau)\lesssim\mathcal{E}_{\tau}[\psi].

Proof.

We suppress the

\displaystyle\|\not{\nabla}\psi\|^{2}_{L^{2}}\lesssim\|\not{\mathrm{D}}\psi\|^{2}_{L^{2}}+\|\mathbf{A}\psi\|^{2}_{L^{2}}\lesssim\mathcal{E}[\psi]+\|\mathbf{A}\|^{2}_{L^{6}}\|\psi\|^{2}_{L^{3}}.

Now since

\|\psi\|_{L^{3}}\lesssim\|\psi\|_{L^{6}}

\|\mathbf{A}\|^{2}_{L^{6}}\lesssim\|\not{\nabla}\mathbf{A}\|^{2}_{L^{2}}+\|\mathbf{A}\|^{2}_{L^{2}}\lesssim\mathrm{S}_{1}[\mathbf{A}]

\|\not{\nabla}\psi\|^{2}_{L^{2}}\leqslant C(1+\mathrm{S}_{1}[\mathbf{A}])\mathcal{E}[\psi]+C\mathrm{S}_{1}[\mathbf{A}]\|\not{\nabla}\psi\|^{2}_{L^{2}}\leqslant C\varepsilon\|\not{\nabla}\psi\|^{2}_{L^{2}}+C(1+\varepsilon)\mathcal{E}[\psi],

so

\|\not{\nabla}\psi\|^{2}_{L^{2}}\leqslant C\left(\frac{1+\varepsilon}{1-\varepsilon C}\right)\mathcal{E}[\psi]\lesssim\mathcal{E}[\psi]

for
\varepsilon>0
small enough.
∎

Proposition 8.2
.

For any fixed
\tau\in\mathbb{R}
and any sufficiently smooth complex scalar field
\psi
on
\mathfrak{E}
there exists
\varepsilon>0
such that if
\mathrm{S}_{1}[\mathbf{A}](\tau)\leqslant\varepsilon
, then

\|\dot{\psi}\|^{2}_{L^{2}}(\tau)\lesssim(1+\mathrm{S}_{1}[A_{0}](\tau))\,\mathcal{E}_{\tau}[\psi].

Proof.

Working similarly to the previous proposition,

\displaystyle\|\dot{\psi}\|^{2}_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|\mathrm{D}_{0}\psi\|^{2}_{L^{2}}+\|A_{0}\psi\|^{2}_{L^{2}}\lesssim\mathcal{E}[\psi]+\|A_{0}\|^{2}_{L^{6}}\|\psi\|^{2}_{L^{3}}\lesssim\mathcal{E}[\psi]+\|A_{0}\|^{2}_{H^{1}}\|\psi\|^{2}_{L^{6}}.

Also
\|\psi\|^{2}_{L^{6}}\lesssim\|\not{\nabla}\psi\|^{2}_{L^{2}}+\|\psi\|^{2}_{L^{2}}
, so

\displaystyle\|\dot{\psi}\|^{2}_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\mathcal{E}[\psi]+\mathrm{S}_{1}[A_{0}]\left(\|\not{\nabla}\psi\|^{2}_{L^{2}}+\|\psi\|^{2}_{L^{2}}\right)\lesssim\left(1+\mathrm{S}_{1}[A_{0}]\right)\mathcal{E}[\psi]+\mathrm{S}_{1}[A_{0}]\|\not{\nabla}\psi\|^{2}_{L^{2}}.

Proposition

8.1
now gives the result for small
\mathrm{S}_{1}[\mathbf{A}]
.
∎

Proposition 8.3
.

For any fixed
\tau\in\mathbb{R}
and any sufficiently smooth complex scalar field
\psi
on
\mathfrak{E}
one has

\|\not{\mathrm{D}}\psi\|^{2}_{L^{2}}(\tau)\lesssim\mathrm{S}_{1}[\psi](\tau)(1+\mathrm{S}_{1}[\mathbf{A}](\tau)).

Proof.

This is a simple consequence of the compactness of
\mathbb{S}^{3}
and the Sobolev Embedding Theorem as above,

\displaystyle\|\not{\mathrm{D}}\psi\|^{2}_{L^{2}}\lesssim\|\not{\nabla}\psi\|^{2}_{L^{2}}+\|\mathbf{A}\psi\|^{2}_{L^{2}}\lesssim\|\not{\nabla}\psi\|^{2}_{L^{2}}+\|\mathbf{A}\|^{2}_{L^{6}}\|\psi\|^{2}_{L^{6}}\lesssim\mathrm{S}_{1}[\psi]+\mathrm{S}_{1}[\mathbf{A}]\mathrm{S}_{1}[\psi].

∎

Proposition 8.4
.

For any fixed
\tau\in\mathbb{R}
and any sufficiently smooth complex scalar field
\psi
on
\mathfrak{E}
one has

\|\mathrm{D}_{0}\psi\|^{2}_{L^{2}}(\tau)\lesssim(1+\mathrm{S}_{1}[A_{0}](\tau))\mathrm{S}_{1}[\psi](\tau).

Proof.

This follows from the same splitting and embedding as the previous propositions,

\|\mathrm{D}_{0}\psi\|^{2}_{L^{2}}\lesssim\|\dot{\psi}\|^{2}_{L^{2}}+\|A_{0}\psi\|^{2}_{L^{2}}\lesssim\mathrm{S}_{1}[\psi](1+\|A_{0}\|^{2}_{H^{1}})\lesssim(1+\mathrm{S}_{1}[A_{0}])\mathrm{S}_{1}[\psi].

∎

Theorem 8.5
.

For any fixed
\tau\in\mathbb{R}
and any sufficiently smooth complex scalar field
\psi
on
\mathfrak{E}
there exists
\varepsilon>0
such that if
\mathrm{S}_{1}[A]\leqslant\varepsilon
, then

\mathrm{S}_{1}[\psi](\tau)\simeq\mathcal{E}[\psi](\tau).

Proof.

Suppose
\mathrm{S}_{1}[A]
is small. Then in particular both
\mathrm{S}_{1}[\mathbf{A}]
and
\mathrm{S}_{1}[A_{0}]
are small, so by
Proposition

8.1

\|\not{\nabla}\psi\|^{2}_{L^{2}}\lesssim\mathcal{E}[\psi]
. By
Proposition

8.2
,
\|\dot{\psi}\|^{2}_{L^{2}}\lesssim\mathcal{E}[\psi]
, so

\mathrm{S}_{1}[\psi]\lesssim\mathcal{E}[\psi].

Conversely, by
Propositions

8.3
and
8.4
,
\|\not{\mathrm{D}}\psi\|^{2}_{L^{2}}\lesssim\mathrm{S}_{1}[\psi]
and
\|\mathrm{D}_{0}\psi\|^{2}_{L^{2}}\lesssim\mathrm{S}_{1}[\psi]
, so

\mathcal{E}[\psi]\lesssim\mathrm{S}_{1}[\psi].

∎

In particular,
\mathcal{E}[\phi]\simeq\mathrm{S}_{1}[\phi]
and
\mathcal{E}[\not{\nabla}\phi]\simeq\mathrm{S}_{1}[\not{\nabla}\phi]
. Since
\mathrm{S}_{1}[\phi]+\mathrm{S}_{1}[\not{\nabla}\phi]\simeq\mathrm{S}_{2}[\phi]
, one then has

\mathcal{E}_{\tau}[\phi]+\mathcal{E}_{\tau}[\not{\nabla}\phi]\simeq\mathrm{S}_{2}[\phi](\tau)

(8.4)

if
\mathrm{S}_{1}[A](\tau)
is sufficiently small. Similarly,

\sum_{k=0}^{m-1}\mathcal{E}_{\tau}[\not{\nabla}^{k}\phi]\simeq\mathrm{S}_{m}[\phi](\tau)

if
\mathrm{S}_{1}[A](\tau)
is sufficiently small.

8.3
Elliptic Estimates

As we have already seen, one useful feature of the Coulomb gauge is that the field equation for
A_{0}
becomes elliptic,

-\not{\Delta}A_{0}+|\phi|^{2}A_{0}=-\operatorname{Im}(\bar{\phi}\dot{\phi}).

(8.5)

Even though the component
A_{0}
is non-dynamical, it still carries energy. This energy is controlled by
\dot{\phi}
as follows.

Proposition 8.6
.

The non-dynamical component
A_{0}
satisfies the estimates

\|\not{\nabla}A_{0}\|^{2}_{L^{2}}(\tau)+\|\phi A_{0}\|^{2}_{L^{2}}(\tau)+\|A_{0}\|^{2}_{L^{2}}(\tau)\lesssim\|\dot{\phi}\|^{2}_{L^{2}}(\tau)

for every fixed
\tau\in\mathbb{R}
.

Proof.

Multiplying equation (
8.5
) by
A_{0}
and integrating, we have

\|\not{\nabla}A_{0}\|^{2}_{L^{2}}+\|\phi A_{0}\|^{2}_{L^{2}}=-\int_{\mathbb{S}^{3}}\operatorname{Im}(\bar{\phi}\dot{\phi})A_{0}\dvol_{\mathfrak{s}_{3}}\leqslant\|\phi A_{0}\|_{L^{2}}\|\dot{\phi}\|_{L^{2}}\leqslant\frac{1}{2}\|\phi A_{0}\|^{2}_{L^{2}}+\frac{1}{2}\|\dot{\phi}\|^{2}_{L^{2}},

which gives the first two estimates. The third estimate follows from the Poincaré inequality for
A_{0}
.
∎

We will need these estimates to extend energy smallness assumptions on
\mathbf{A}
and
\phi
to
A_{0}
.

9
Energy Estimates

9.1
Conservation of Energy

For general
\alpha
,
\psi
one finds that

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\nabla^{a}\mathbf{T}_{ab}[\alpha]=\mathfrak{M}(\alpha)^{a}\left(\nabla_{a}\alpha_{b}-\nabla_{b}\alpha_{a}\right),\\
&\nabla^{a}\mathbf{T}_{ab}[\psi]=\frac{1}{2}\overline{\mathfrak{S}(\psi)}\mathrm{D}_{b}\psi+\frac{1}{2}\mathfrak{S}(\psi)\overline{\mathrm{D}_{b}\psi}+F_{ab}\operatorname{Im}\left(\bar{\psi}\mathrm{D}^{a}\psi\right).\end{split}

(9.1)

When
\alpha_{a}=A_{a}
and
\psi=\phi
, the field equations
\mathfrak{M}(A)_{a}=-\operatorname{Im}\left(\bar{\phi}\mathrm{D}_{a}\phi\right)
and
\mathfrak{S}(\phi)=0
imply that

\nabla^{a}\mathbf{T}_{ab}[\phi,A]=\nabla^{a}(\mathbf{T}_{ab}[A]+\mathbf{T}_{ab}[\phi])=F_{ab}\left(\operatorname{Im}\left(\bar{\phi}\mathrm{D}^{a}\phi\right)-\operatorname{Im}\left(\bar{\phi}\mathrm{D}^{a}\phi\right)\right)=0.

9.2
H^{1}
estimates

Consider admissible initial data for the system (
6.6
). We can make no a priori assumptions about the smallness of the non-dynamical component
A_{0}
, but we will of course be able to extract all the required information about
A_{0}
using the elliptic equation (
8.5
).

Theorem 9.1
.

There exists an
\varepsilon>0
such that if
\mathrm{S}_{1}[\phi,\mathbf{A}](0)\leqslant\varepsilon
, then

\mathrm{S}_{1}[\phi,A](\tau)\simeq\mathrm{S}_{1}[\phi,A](0)

for all
\tau\in\mathbb{R}
.

Proof.

Since
\nabla^{a}\mathbf{T}_{ab}[\phi,A]=0
and
T^{b}=\partial_{\tau}
is Killing on
\mathfrak{E}
, integrating
\mathsf{e}_{1}\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=\nabla^{a}(T^{b}\mathbf{T}_{ab}[\phi,A])=0
over the region
\mathbb{S}^{3}\times[0,\tau]
for any
\tau>0
immediately gives

0=\int_{\mathbb{S}^{3}\times[0,\tau]}\mathsf{e}_{1}\dvol=\int_{\mathbb{S}^{3}_{\tau}}\mathbf{T}_{00}[\phi,A]\dvol_{\mathfrak{s}_{3}}-\int_{\mathbb{S}^{3}_{0}}\mathbf{T}_{00}[\phi,A]\dvol_{\mathfrak{s}_{3}},

i.e.

\mathcal{E}_{\tau}[\phi]+\mathcal{E}_{\tau}[A]=\mathcal{E}_{\tau}[\phi,A]=\mathcal{E}_{0}[\phi,A]=\mathcal{E}_{0}[\phi]+\mathcal{E}_{0}[A].

(9.2)

Now the smallness assumption
\mathrm{S}_{1}[\phi,\mathbf{A}](0)\leqslant\varepsilon
implies that
\mathrm{S}_{1}[\mathbf{A}](0)\leqslant\varepsilon
and
\mathrm{S}_{1}[\phi](0)\leqslant\varepsilon
, so by
Proposition

8.6

\|\not{\nabla}A_{0}\|^{2}_{L^{2}}(0)\lesssim\mathrm{S}_{1}[\phi](0)\leqslant\varepsilon,

and so
\mathrm{S}_{1}[A](0)\lesssim\varepsilon
. Then by
Theorem

8.5
,
\mathcal{E}_{0}[\phi]\simeq\mathrm{S}_{1}[\phi](0)
. Now equation (
8.2
) reads
\mathcal{E}_{\tau}[A]\simeq\mathrm{S}_{1}[A](\tau),
which in particular holds at
\tau=0
, so we have
\mathcal{E}_{0}[\phi]+\mathcal{E}_{0}[A]\simeq\mathrm{S}_{1}[\phi](0)+\mathrm{S}_{1}[A](0)
, and so by
eq.

9.2

\mathcal{E}_{\tau}[\phi]+\mathcal{E}_{\tau}[A]\simeq\mathrm{S}_{1}[\phi](0)+\mathrm{S}_{1}[A](0).

This means that
\mathcal{E}_{\tau}[\phi]+\mathcal{E}_{\tau}[A]
is small too,
\mathcal{E}_{\tau}[\phi,A]\lesssim\varepsilon
. In particular,
\mathcal{E}_{\tau}[A]\simeq\mathrm{S}_{1}[A](\tau)
is small, so again by
Theorem

8.5
,
\mathcal{E}_{\tau}[\phi]\simeq\mathrm{S}_{1}[\phi](\tau)
. We deduce that

\mathrm{S}_{1}[\phi](\tau)+\mathrm{S}_{1}[A](\tau)\simeq\mathrm{S}_{1}[\phi](0)+\mathrm{S}_{1}[A](0)

(9.3)

for all
\tau>0
. The same argument works for
\tau<0
.

∎

9.3
H^{2}
estimates

9.3.1
A Nonlinear Grönwall Inequality

Some useful small data nonlinear Grönwall inequalities may be proved by reduction to the standard Grönwall inequality using a careful change of variables. More precisely, suppose
g(\tau)
satisfies a nonlinear differential inequality, say

g^{\prime}(\tau)\leqslant F\left(g(\tau)\right).

If we can find a function
G
such that

G(g(\tau))^{\prime}=G^{\prime}(g(\tau))g^{\prime}(\tau)\overset{!}{\leqslant}G(g(\tau)),

then we can apply the standard Grönwall inequality to
G(\tau)\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=G(g(\tau))
and possibly invert
G(g)
to recover an inequality for
g
. This will not in general produce an immediately useful statement due to the nonlinear nature of
F
(and hence
G
), but with a smallness assumption on
g(0)
the offending terms can frequently be dealt with. Clearly finding such a
G
amounts to solving the differential inequality

G^{\prime}(g)F(g)\leqslant G(g).

Lemma 9.2
.

Let
\tau\in[0,1]
and
f:[0,1]\to\mathbb{R}
be a continuous non-negative function. Suppose
f
satisfies the inequality

f(\tau)\leqslant f(0)+\int_{0}^{\tau}f(\sigma)P(f(\sigma)^{1/2})\,\mathrm{d}\sigma

for some polynomial
P
with positive coefficients. Then there exists
\varepsilon>0
small enough such that if
f(0)\leqslant\varepsilon
, then

f(\tau)\leqslant Cf(0)

for some
C>1
and all
\tau\in[0,1]
.

Proof.

The case when
P
has order zero is trivial, so assume that
P(x)=\sum_{k=0}^{d}P_{k}x^{k}
for some
d>0
and some non-negative real numbers
\{P_{k}\}_{k}
. We may reduce the inequality as follows,

\displaystyle f(\tau)

\displaystyle\leqslant f(0)+\int_{0}^{\tau}f(\sigma)P(f(\sigma)^{1/2})\,\mathrm{d}\sigma

\displaystyle\leqslant f(0)+\int_{0}^{\tau}\sum_{k=0}^{d}P_{k}f(\sigma)^{k/2+1}\,\mathrm{d}\sigma

\displaystyle\leqslant f(0)+\int_{\{0<\sigma<\tau\,:\,f(\sigma)<1\}}\sum_{k=0}^{d}P_{k}f(\sigma)^{k/2+1}\,\mathrm{d}\sigma

\displaystyle+\int_{\{0<\sigma<\tau\,:\,f(\sigma)>1\}}\sum_{k=0}^{d}P_{k}f(\sigma)^{k/2+1}\,\mathrm{d}\sigma

\displaystyle\leqslant f(0)+\int_{0}^{\tau}\sum_{k=0}^{d}P_{k}f(\sigma)\,\mathrm{d}\sigma+\int_{0}^{\tau}\sum_{k=0}^{d}P_{k}f(\sigma)^{d/2+1}\,\mathrm{d}\sigma

\displaystyle\leqslant f(0)+\int_{0}^{\tau}Df(\sigma)\,\mathrm{d}\sigma+\int_{0}^{\tau}Df(\sigma)^{d/2+1}\,\mathrm{d}\sigma,

where
D=(d+1)\max_{k}P_{k}
. Now set

g(\tau)\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=f(0)+\int_{0}^{\tau}Df(\sigma)\,\mathrm{d}\sigma+\int_{0}^{\tau}Df(\sigma)^{d/2+1}\,\mathrm{d}\sigma.

Then
f(\tau)\leqslant g(\tau)
,
f(0)=g(0)
, and
g^{\prime}(\tau)\leqslant Df(\tau)+Df(\tau)^{d/2+1}\leqslant Dg(\tau)\left(1+g(\tau)^{d/2}\right)
. Defining

G(\tau)\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=g(\tau)^{1/D}D^{-2/(Dd)}\left(1+g(\tau)^{d/2}\right)^{-2/(Dd)}

and differentiating, one obtains

\displaystyle G^{\prime}(\tau)

\displaystyle=g^{\prime}(\tau)g(\tau)^{1/D-1}D^{-2/(Dd)-1}\left(1+g(\tau)^{d/2}\right)^{-2/(Dd)-1}

\displaystyle\leqslant g(\tau)^{1/D}D^{-2(Dd)}\left(1+g(\tau)^{d/2}\right)^{-2/(Dd)},

so that
G^{\prime}(\tau)\leqslant G(\tau)
. Since
\tau
is contained in a compact interval, this gives
G(\tau)\lesssim G(0)
, or equivalently

\displaystyle g(\tau)^{1/D}\left(1+g(\tau)^{d/2}\right)^{-2/(Dd)}

\displaystyle\lesssim g(0)^{1/D}\left(1+g(0)^{d/2}\right)^{-2/(Dd)}

\displaystyle\lesssim g(0)^{1/D}.

Rearranging gives

g(\tau)^{d/2}\lesssim g(0)^{d/2}\left(1+g(\tau)^{d/2}\right),

so if
g(0)=f(0)
is small enough one has
g(\tau)^{d/2}\lesssim g(0)^{d/2}
and so

f(\tau)\leqslant g(\tau)\leqslant Cg(0)\leqslant Cf(0).

∎

Remark 9.3
.

Clearly the above proof goes through exactly the same with
[0,1]
replaced with any interval
[0,r]
,
r\in\mathbb{R}
.

9.3.2
Commutators

Proposition 9.4
.

One has the following bounds on the commutators of
\not{\nabla}
with the field equation operators
\mathfrak{M}
and
\mathfrak{S}
:

\left|[\not{\nabla},\,\mathfrak{M}]A\right|_{\mathbb{S}^{3}}\lesssim|\not{\nabla}^{2}\mathbf{A}|+|\not{\nabla}\mathbf{A}|+|\not{\nabla}\dot{A}_{0}|,

and

\displaystyle\left|[\not{\nabla},\,\mathfrak{S}](\phi)\right|

\displaystyle\lesssim|\dot{\phi}\not{\nabla}A_{0}|+|\phi\not{\nabla}\dot{A}_{0}|+|\phi A_{0}\not{\nabla}A_{0}|+|\not{\nabla}^{2}\phi|+|\phi\not{\nabla}^{2}\mathbf{A}|

\displaystyle+|\mathbf{A}\not{\nabla}\phi|+|\not{\nabla}\phi|+|\phi\not{\nabla}\mathbf{A}|+|\mathbf{A}\phi|+|\not{\nabla}\phi\not{\nabla}\mathbf{A}|+|\phi\mathbf{A}\not{\nabla}\mathbf{A}|.

Proof.

Note that in the following the index
i
always refers to a contraction with a basis vector field
X_{i}
. Recall that the operator
\mathfrak{M}_{\mu}
on
A
is given by
\mathfrak{M}(A)_{\mu}=\Box\mathbf{A}_{\mu}-\not{\nabla}_{\mu}\dot{A}_{0}-2\mathbf{A}_{\mu}
, so for any
i

\displaystyle|[\not{\nabla}_{i},\,\mathfrak{M}](A)|_{\mathbb{S}^{3}}

\displaystyle=|\not{\nabla}_{i}\mathfrak{M}(A)_{\mu}-\mathfrak{M}(\not{\nabla}_{i}A)_{\mu}|

\displaystyle=\left|\not{\nabla}_{i}\left(\Box\mathbf{A}_{\mu}-\not{\nabla}_{\mu}\dot{A}_{0}-2\mathbf{A}_{\mu}\right)-\Box(\not{\nabla}_{i}\mathbf{A}_{\mu})+\not{\nabla}_{\mu}\not{\nabla}_{i}\dot{A}_{0}+2\not{\nabla}_{i}\mathbf{A}_{\mu}\right|

\displaystyle=\left|\not{\nabla}_{i}\not{\nabla}^{\nu}\not{\nabla}_{\nu}\mathbf{A}_{\mu}-\not{\nabla}^{\nu}\not{\nabla}_{\nu}(X_{i}^{\lambda}\not{\nabla}_{\lambda}\mathbf{A}_{\mu})+\not{\nabla}_{\mu}X_{i}^{\nu}\not{\nabla}_{\nu}\dot{A}_{0}\right|

\displaystyle\leqslant C\left[|\not{\nabla}^{2}\mathbf{A}|+|\not{\nabla}\mathbf{A}|+|\not{\nabla}\dot{A}_{0}|\right],

where the constant
C
depends on the geometry of
\mathbb{S}^{3}
. To calculate the other commutator we need a couple of preliminary formulae. Let
\psi
be any sufficiently regular complex scalar field. Then

[\not{\nabla}_{i},\,\mathrm{D}_{0}](\psi)=\not{\nabla}_{i}(\dot{\psi}+iA_{0}\psi)-\mathrm{D}_{0}\not{\nabla}_{i}\psi=i\psi\not{\nabla}_{i}A_{0},

and similarly

[\not{\nabla}_{i},\,\not{\mathrm{D}}_{\mu}](\psi)=-(\not{\nabla}_{\mu}X_{i}^{\nu})\not{\nabla}_{\nu}\psi+i\psi\not{\nabla}_{i}\mathbf{A}_{\mu},

so

\displaystyle[\not{\nabla}_{i},\,\mathrm{D}_{0}\mathrm{D}_{0}](\phi)

\displaystyle=\mathrm{D}_{0}[\not{\nabla}_{i},\,\mathrm{D}_{0}](\phi)+[\not{\nabla}_{i},\,\mathrm{D}_{0}](\mathrm{D}_{0}\phi)

\displaystyle=\mathrm{D}_{0}(i\phi\not{\nabla}_{i}A_{0})+i\mathrm{D}_{0}\phi\not{\nabla}_{i}A_{0}

\displaystyle=i\phi\not{\nabla}_{i}\dot{A}_{0}+2i\dot{\phi}\not{\nabla}_{i}A_{0}-2\phi A_{0}\not{\nabla}_{i}A_{0}.

Further, for any vector field
\mathbf{V}
on
\mathbb{S}^{3}

\displaystyle[\not{\nabla}_{i},\,\not{\mathrm{D}}_{\mu}]\mathbf{V}^{\mu}

\displaystyle=\not{\nabla}_{i}(\not{\nabla}_{\mu}\mathbf{V}^{\mu}+i\mathbf{A}_{\mu}\mathbf{V}^{\mu})-(\not{\nabla}_{\mu}+i\mathbf{A}_{\mu})(\not{\nabla}_{i}\mathbf{V}^{\mu})

\displaystyle=\not{\nabla}_{i}\not{\nabla}_{\mu}\mathbf{V}^{\mu}-\not{\nabla}_{\mu}\not{\nabla}_{i}\mathbf{V}^{\mu}+i(\not{\nabla}_{i}\mathbf{A}_{\mu})\mathbf{V}^{\mu}

\displaystyle\leqslant C\left[|\not{\nabla}\mathbf{V}|+|\mathbf{V}|+|\mathbf{V}\not{\nabla}\mathbf{A}|\right],

where, as before,
C
depends on the geometry of
\mathbb{S}^{3}
. Then

\displaystyle[\not{\nabla}_{i},\,\not{\mathrm{D}}_{\mu}\not{\mathrm{D}}^{\mu}]\phi

\displaystyle=\not{\mathrm{D}}^{\mu}[\not{\nabla}_{i},\,\not{\mathrm{D}}_{\mu}]\phi+[\not{\nabla}_{i},\,\not{\mathrm{D}}_{\mu}]\not{\mathrm{D}}^{\mu}\phi

\displaystyle\leqslant\not{\mathrm{D}}^{\mu}\left(-\not{\nabla}_{\mu}X_{i}^{\nu}\not{\nabla}_{\nu}\phi+i\phi\not{\nabla}_{i}\mathbf{A}_{\mu}\right)+C\left[|\not{\nabla}\not{\mathrm{D}}\phi|+|\not{\mathrm{D}}\phi|+|\not{\mathrm{D}}\phi\not{\nabla}\mathbf{A}|\right]

\displaystyle\leqslant-\not{\Delta}X_{i}^{\nu}\not{\nabla}_{\nu}\phi-\not{\nabla}_{\mu}X_{i}^{\nu}\not{\nabla}^{\mu}\not{\nabla}_{\nu}\phi+i\not{\nabla}^{\mu}\phi\not{\nabla}_{i}\mathbf{A}_{\mu}+i\phi\not{\nabla}_{\mu}\not{\nabla}_{i}\mathbf{A}_{\mu}

\displaystyle-i\mathbf{A}^{\mu}\not{\nabla}_{\mu}X_{i}^{\nu}\not{\nabla}_{\nu}\phi-\phi\mathbf{A}^{\mu}\not{\nabla}_{i}\mathbf{A}_{\mu}

\displaystyle+C\left[|\not{\nabla}^{2}\phi|+|\not{\nabla}(\mathbf{A}\phi)|+|\not{\nabla}\phi|+|\mathbf{A}\phi|+|\not{\nabla}\phi\not{\nabla}\mathbf{A}|+|\mathbf{A}\phi\not{\nabla}\mathbf{A}|\right]

\displaystyle\lesssim|\not{\nabla}\phi|+|\not{\nabla}^{2}\phi|+|\not{\nabla}\phi\not{\nabla}\mathbf{A}|+|\phi\not{\nabla}^{2}\mathbf{A}|+|\phi\not{\nabla}\mathbf{A}|+|\mathbf{A}\not{\nabla}\phi|

\displaystyle+|\phi\mathbf{A}\not{\nabla}\mathbf{A}|+|\mathbf{A}\phi|.

Putting these together, we have

\displaystyle[\not{\nabla}_{i},\,\mathfrak{S}]\phi

\displaystyle=[\not{\nabla}_{i},\,\mathrm{D}^{a}\mathrm{D}_{a}+1]\phi

\displaystyle=[\not{\nabla}_{i},\,\mathrm{D}_{0}\mathrm{D}_{0}]\phi-[\not{\nabla}_{i},\,\not{\mathrm{D}}^{\mu}\not{\mathrm{D}}_{\mu}]\phi

\displaystyle\lesssim|\phi\not{\nabla}\dot{A}_{0}|+|\dot{\phi}\not{\nabla}A_{0}|+|\phi A_{0}\not{\nabla}A_{0}|+|\not{\nabla}\phi|+|\not{\nabla}^{2}\phi|+|\not{\nabla}\phi\not{\nabla}\mathbf{A}|

\displaystyle+|\phi\not{\nabla}^{2}\mathbf{A}|+|\phi\not{\nabla}\mathbf{A}|+|\mathbf{A}\not{\nabla}\phi|+|\phi\mathbf{A}\not{\nabla}\mathbf{A}|+|\mathbf{A}\phi|.

∎

Most of the terms in the above estimates we can control by the energy directly, with the exception of time derivatives of
A_{0}
. These terms we shall control using the elliptic equation for
A_{0}
and the evolution equation for
\phi
.

Proposition 9.5
.

For any fixed
\tau\in\mathbb{R}
there exists
\varepsilon>0
such that if
\mathrm{S}_{1}[\phi]<\varepsilon
and
A_{a}
satisfies the strong Coulomb gauge, then

\|\dot{A}_{0}\|^{2}_{H^{1}}(\tau)\lesssim\mathrm{S}_{2}[\phi](\tau)(1+\mathrm{S}_{1}[A](\tau))^{2}.

Proof.

First note that in the strong Coulomb gauge
\bar{A}_{0}(\tau)=0
for all
\tau
, and so
\dot{\bar{A}}_{0}(\tau)=0
for all
\tau
as well. Thus
\|\dot{A}_{0}\|_{L^{2}}\lesssim\|\not{\nabla}\dot{A}_{0}\|_{L^{2}}
, and we only need to estimate
\|\not{\nabla}\dot{A}_{0}\|_{L^{2}}
. Differentiating
eq.

8.5
in
\tau
, we have

-\not{\Delta}\dot{A}_{0}+|\phi|^{2}\dot{A}_{0}=-\operatorname{Im}(\bar{\phi}\ddot{\phi})-\bar{\phi}\dot{\phi}A_{0}-\dot{\bar{\phi}}\phi A_{0}.

Multiplying through by
\dot{A}_{0}
and integrating we have

\|\not{\nabla}\dot{A}_{0}\|^{2}_{L^{2}}+\|\phi\dot{A}_{0}\|^{2}_{L^{2}}\leqslant\|\phi\dot{A}_{0}\|_{L^{2}}\|\ddot{\phi}\|_{L^{2}}+2\|\phi\dot{A}_{0}\|_{L^{2}}\|\dot{\phi}A_{0}\|_{L^{2}}

which gives

\|\not{\nabla}\dot{A}_{0}\|^{2}_{L^{2}}+\delta\|\phi\dot{A}_{0}\|^{2}_{L^{2}}\lesssim\|\ddot{\phi}\|^{2}_{L^{2}}+\|\dot{\phi}A_{0}\|^{2}_{L^{2}}

(9.4)

for some
0<\delta<1
. We thus need to estimate
\|\ddot{\phi}\|_{L^{2}}
, for which we shall use the field equation for
\phi
,

\Box\phi+2iA_{0}\dot{\phi}-2i\mathbf{A}\cdot\not{\nabla}\phi+(1-A_{0}^{2}+|\mathbf{A}|^{2}+i\dot{A}_{0})\phi=0.

We estimate

|\ddot{\phi}|^{2}\lesssim|\not{\Delta}\phi|^{2}+|A_{0}\dot{\phi}|^{2}+|\mathbf{A}\not{\nabla}\phi|^{2}+|\phi|^{2}+|A_{0}^{2}\phi|^{2}+|\mathbf{A}^{2}\phi|^{2}+|\dot{A}_{0}\phi|^{2}.

(9.5)

With the exception of the term
|\dot{A}_{0}\phi|^{2}
, the right-hand side of
eq.

9.5
will be easily controlled as we will see shortly. To deal with the problematic term we will use smallness of the data. Integrating
eq.

9.5
over the
3
-sphere we have

\displaystyle\|\ddot{\phi}\|^{2}_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|\not{\Delta}\phi\|^{2}_{L^{2}}+\|A_{0}\dot{\phi}\|^{2}_{L^{2}}+\|\mathbf{A}\not{\nabla}\phi\|^{2}_{L^{2}}+\|\phi\|^{2}_{L^{2}}+\|A_{0}^{2}\phi\|^{2}_{L^{2}}+\|\mathbf{A}^{2}\phi\|^{2}_{L^{2}}+\|\dot{A}_{0}\phi\|^{2}_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|\phi\|^{2}_{H^{2}}+\|A_{0}\|^{2}_{L^{3}}\|\dot{\phi}\|^{2}_{L^{6}}+\|\mathbf{A}\|^{2}_{L^{3}}\|\not{\nabla}\phi\|^{2}_{L^{6}}

\displaystyle+\|A_{0}\|_{L^{6}}^{4}\|\phi\|^{2}_{L^{6}}+\|\mathbf{A}\|^{4}_{L^{6}}\|\phi\|^{2}_{L^{6}}+\|\dot{A}_{0}\|^{2}_{L^{3}}\|\phi\|^{2}_{L^{6}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|\phi\|^{2}_{H^{2}}+\|A_{0}\|^{2}_{H^{1}}\|\dot{\phi}\|^{2}_{H^{1}}+\|\mathbf{A}\|^{2}_{H^{1}}\|\phi\|^{2}_{H^{2}}

\displaystyle+\|A_{0}\|^{4}_{H^{1}}\|\phi\|^{2}_{H^{1}}+\|\mathbf{A}\|^{4}_{H^{1}}\|\phi\|^{2}_{H^{1}}+\|\dot{A}_{0}\|^{2}_{H^{1}}\|\phi\|^{2}_{H^{1}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\mathrm{S}_{2}[\phi]+\mathrm{S}_{1}[A]\mathrm{S}_{2}[\phi]+\mathrm{S}_{1}[\mathbf{A}]\mathrm{S}_{2}[\phi]+\mathrm{S}_{1}[A]^{2}\mathrm{S}_{1}[\phi]+\mathrm{S}_{1}[\mathbf{A}]^{2}\mathrm{S}_{1}[\phi]+\|\dot{A}_{0}\|^{2}_{H^{1}}\mathrm{S}_{1}[\phi]

\displaystyle\lesssim\mathrm{S}_{2}[\phi](1+\mathrm{S}_{1}[A])^{2}+\|\dot{A}_{0}\|^{2}_{H^{1}}\mathrm{S}_{1}[\phi].

Putting this into
eq.

9.4
gives

\|\not{\nabla}\dot{A}_{0}\|^{2}_{L^{2}}\lesssim\mathrm{S}_{2}[\phi](1+\mathrm{S}_{1}[A])^{2}+\|\dot{A}_{0}\|^{2}_{H^{1}}\mathrm{S}_{1}[\phi],

so provided
\mathrm{S}_{1}[\phi]
is sufficiently small the Poincaré inequality gives

\|\not{\nabla}\dot{A}_{0}\|^{2}_{L^{2}}\lesssim\mathrm{S}_{2}[\phi](1+\mathrm{S}_{1}[A])^{2}.

∎

9.3.3
Estimate Algebra

For ease of presentation we outline a schematic procedure to track how we bound the various terms arising in our
H^{2}
estimates. The idea is simply to track the number of derivatives and their Sobolev exponents of the error terms and check that they do not exceed certain critical values. Let
f
denote either
A
or
\phi
, let
\partial
denote either the
\mathbb{S}^{3}
-derivatives
\not{\nabla}
or the
\tau
-derivative
\partial_{\tau}
, and let
\partial^{2}
denote either
\not{\nabla}^{2}
or
\partial_{\tau}\not{\nabla}
(that is, not
\partial_{\tau}^{2}
). Then all the error terms that we encounter will in fact be of the form

\||\partial^{2}f|^{m}|\partial f|^{k}|f|^{l}\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{S}^{3})},

where
m
,
k
, and
l
are non-negative integers and in particular
m=0
,
1
, or
2
.

If
m=0
, we have

\||\partial f|^{k}|f|^{l}\|_{L^{1}}\leqslant\|f\|_{L^{\infty}}^{l}\|\partial f\|^{k}_{L^{k}}.

Now since
\mathbb{S}^{3}
is compact, the Lebesgue spaces
L^{p}(\mathbb{S}^{3})
form a decreasing sequence in
p
,

L^{\infty}(\mathbb{S}^{3})\hookrightarrow\dots\hookrightarrow L^{p}(\mathbb{S}^{3})\hookrightarrow\dots\hookrightarrow L^{q}(\mathbb{S}^{3})\hookrightarrow\dots\hookrightarrow L^{1}(\mathbb{S}^{3}),

p>q
, where
\hookrightarrow
denotes continuous inclusion. As
\mathbb{S}^{3}
has dimension
3
, by Sobolev Embedding we also have

H^{1}(\mathbb{S}^{3})\hookrightarrow L^{6}(\mathbb{S}^{3})\quad\text{ and }\quad H^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{3})\hookrightarrow C^{0,\frac{1}{2}}(\mathbb{S}^{3})\hookrightarrow L^{\infty}(\mathbb{S}^{3}),

so provided
k\leqslant 6
we have

\||\partial f|^{k}|f|^{l}\|_{L^{1}}\lesssim\|f\|^{l}_{2}\|f\|^{k}_{2}=\|f\|_{2}^{k+l},

where

\|f\|_{2}\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=\|f\|_{H^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{3})}+\|\dot{f}\|_{H^{1}(\mathbb{S}^{3})}

(notice that the norm
\|\cdot\|^{2}_{2}
is the familiar Sobolev-type energy
\mathrm{S}_{2}
).

If
m=1
, we perform the splitting

\displaystyle\||\partial^{2}f||\partial f|^{k}|f|^{l}\|_{L^{1}}

\displaystyle=\int|\partial^{2}f||\partial f|^{k}|f|^{l}

\displaystyle\leqslant\int|\partial^{2}f|^{2}+\int|\partial f|^{2k}|f|^{2l}\leqslant\|f\|^{2}_{2}+\||\partial f|^{2k}|f|^{2l}\|_{L^{1}}.

Now provided
2k\leqslant 6
, the second term in the above may be dealt with as in the case
m=0
, so we have

\||\partial^{2}f||\partial f|^{k}|f|^{l}\|_{L^{1}}\lesssim\|f\|^{2}_{2}+\|f\|_{2}^{2(k+l)}.

Finally, when
m=2
it will in fact turn out that
k
is necessarily zero, so we will have

\||\partial^{2}f|^{2}|f|^{l}\|_{L^{1}}\leqslant\|f\|^{l}_{L^{\infty}}\|f\|^{2}_{2}\lesssim\|f\|^{l+2}_{2}.

It will thus be sufficient to use the following prescription. For terms involving no
|\partial^{2}f|
(i.e.
m=0
) we shall check if
k\leqslant 6
, and if so, conclude that the term is bounded by
\|f\|_{2}^{k+l}
; for terms involving
|\partial^{2}f|
(i.e.
m=1
), we shall check if
k\leqslant 3
, and if so, conclude that the term is bounded by
\|f\|^{2}_{2}+\|f\|^{2(k+l)}_{2}
; finally, for terms with
m=2
we shall check that
k=0
, and if so, conclude that these are bounded by
\|f\|^{l+2}_{2}
. In the estimates that follow we will write down a term to be estimated,

|\partial^{2}f|^{m}|\partial f|^{k}|f|^{l},

and underneath note down its ‘signature’
(m,k,l)
, as in

\underset{{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}(m,k,l)}}{|\partial^{2}f|^{m}|\partial f|^{k}|f|^{l}}.

If the criteria outlined above are met (that is,
k\leqslant 6
for
m=0
,
k\leqslant 3
for
m=1
, and
k=0
for
m=2
), we shall tick the triplet,

\underset{{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}(m,k,l)\checkmark}}{|\partial^{2}f|^{m}|\partial f|^{k}|f|^{l}}.

Altogether this notation will thus mean that

\||\partial^{2}f|^{m}|\partial f|^{k}|f|^{l}\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{S}^{3})}\lesssim Q(\|f\|_{2})

for some polynomial
Q
with positive coefficients.

for some polynomial

.

Theorem 9.6
.

Let

be a fixed compact interval in
\mathbb{R}
containing zero. There exists
\varepsilon>0
such that if
\mathrm{S}_{2}[\phi,\mathbf{A}](0)\leqslant\varepsilon

\mathrm{S}_{2}[\phi,A](\tau)\simeq\mathrm{S}_{2}[\phi,A](0)

.

\mathsf{e}_{2}

,
\tau>0

(9.7)

From

, and also that

. Furthermore, we have that

8.4

8.3

so adding

9.7

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}_{\tau}[\phi,A]+\sum_{i}\left(\mathcal{E}_{\tau}[\not{\nabla}_{i}\phi]+\mathcal{E}_{\tau}[\not{\nabla}_{i}A]\right)=\mathcal{E}_{0}[\phi,A]

\displaystyle+\sum_{i}\left(\mathcal{E}_{0}[\not{\nabla}_{i}\phi]+\mathcal{E}_{0}[\not{\nabla}_{i}A]\right)

\displaystyle+\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{3}}\mathsf{e}_{2}(\sigma)\dvol_{\mathfrak{s}_{3}}\mathrm{d}\sigma,

(9.8)

Now

9.6

\displaystyle\mathrm{S}_{2}[\phi,A](\tau)

\displaystyle\lesssim\mathrm{S}_{2}[\phi,A](0)+\int_{0}^{\tau}\|\mathsf{e}_{2}\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{S}^{3})}(\sigma)\,\mathrm{d}\sigma

\displaystyle\lesssim\mathrm{S}_{2}[\phi,A](0)+\int_{0}^{\tau}\mathrm{S}_{2}[\phi,A](\sigma)P\left(\mathrm{S}_{2}[\phi,A](\sigma)^{1/2}\right)\,\mathrm{d}\sigma.

,

\mathrm{S}_{2}[\phi,A](\tau)\lesssim\mathrm{S}_{2}[\phi,A](0)

for
\tau\in I
.

\mathrm{S}_{2}[\phi,A](0)\lesssim\mathrm{S}_{2}[\phi,A](\tau)
, and so

\mathrm{S}_{2}[\phi,A](\tau)\simeq\mathrm{S}_{2}[\phi,A](0).

for all

. In particular, picking

10
Higher Order Estimates

From here it is straightforward to play the same game for higher order estimates. It is clear that if for a given

A.1

Let

\displaystyle-\not{\Delta}^{(1)}\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=\delta

Theorem A.1

Proposition A.2
.

Since

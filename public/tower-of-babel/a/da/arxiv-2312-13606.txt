Scattering for 2d semi-relativistic Hartree equations with short range potential
Changhun Yang

Abstract
. We study the long time behavior of small solutions to the semi-relativistic Hartree equations in two dimension. The nonlinear term is convolved with the singular potential | x | − γ |x|^{-\gamma} for 1 < γ < 2 1<\gamma<2 , which is referred to as short-range interaction potential in the sense of scattering phenomenon. We establish the scattering results for small solutions in a weighted space, in other words, we prove that the nonlinear solutions exist globally and behave asymptotically like a linear solution whenever the initial data is sufficiently small. To achieve this, we should obtain time decay estimates for the nonlinear term which is integrable. A key observation is that the loss in time in the course of weighted energy estimates can be recovered by the space resonance method with the help of null structure .



1.
Introduction

We consider the semi-relativistic Hartree equations

(1.1)

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{aligned} -i\partial_{t}u+\sqrt{m^{2}-\Delta}u&={\lambda}\left(|x|^{-\gamma}*|u|^{2}\right)u&&\mathrm{in}\;\;\mathbf{\mathbb{R}}\times\mathbb{R}^{d},\\
u(0,\cdot)&=u_{0}&&\mathrm{in}\;\;\mathbb{R}^{d},\end{aligned}\right.

where the unknown
u:\mathbb{R}^{1+d}\to\mathbb{C}
, some fixed
constant
{\lambda}\in\mathbf{\mathbb{R}}
, and
0<\gamma<d
. The nonlocal differential operator
\sqrt{m^{2}-\Delta}

is defined as a Fourier multiplier operator associated to the symbol

\sqrt{m^{2}+|\xi|^{2}}
and
\ast
denotes the convolution in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
.

m>0
represents the mass which will be normalized to
1
by scaling throughout the paper.
For three dimensional case
d=3
, (
1.1
) with Coulomb potential (
\gamma=1
) is often referred to as
Boson star equation
which describes the dynamics and collapse of relativistic Boson stars. It was rigorously derived as the mean-field limit of large systems of bosons. See
[
17
,
7
,
15
]
and references therein. (
1.1
) with generalized potential in all dimension has been also widely studied by many researchers in the mathematical viewpoint.
The singular potential
|x|^{-\gamma}
is called
the
long range
(or,
short range
) interaction when
0<\gamma\leq 1
(or,
1<\gamma<d
, respectively).
The mass and energy of solution to (
1.1
) are defined by

(1.2)

\displaystyle M(u)(t)

\displaystyle=\|u(t)\|_{L^{2}(\mathbf{\mathbb{R}}^{d})},

\displaystyle E(u)(t)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbf{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\overline{u}\sqrt{m^{2}-\Delta}udx+\frac{\lambda}{4}\int_{\mathbf{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\left(|x|^{-\gamma}\ast|u|^{2}\right)|u|^{2}dx,

respectively and conserved as time evolves. From the conservations laws, one sees that
H^{\frac{1}{2}}
is the energy space. Furthermore,
in massless case
m=0
, we have the scaling symmetry. If
u
is solution to (
1.1
),
u_{\alpha}
defined by

u_{\alpha}(t,x)=\alpha^{\frac{1-\gamma+d}{2}}u(\alpha t,\alpha x)
is also solution, so the scaling invariant space is
H^{\frac{\gamma-1}{2}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. Thus (
1.1
) is often called
H^{\frac{\gamma-1}{2}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
-critical.

There have been numerous results on global solutions to semi-relativistic
Hartree equations (
1.1
). Lenzmann
[
14
]
proved that the Boson star equation (
\gamma=1
and
d=3
) was globally well-posed in the energy space
H^{\frac{1}{2}}(\mathbf{\mathbb{R}}^{3})
.
Cho and Ozawa
[
3
]
extended this result to (
1.1
) under the suitable assumption on
\gamma
and
d
. Furthermore, they verified the asymptotic behavior of solutions when
d\geq 3
and
2<\gamma<d
by showing the small data scattering in
H^{s}(\mathbf{\mathbb{R}}^{d})
for
s>\frac{\gamma}{2}-\frac{d-2}{2d}
. By a
scattering
in
H^{s}(\mathbf{\mathbb{R}}^{d})
, we mean that solutions to nonlinear PDEs converge to solutions to the linear equation in
H^{s}(\mathbf{\mathbb{R}}^{d})
as time goes to infinity. On the other hand, they computed the time decay of
L^{2}
norm of the nonlinearity, computed on a solution of the linear equation, as follows:

(1.3)

\displaystyle\left\|\Big(|x|^{-\gamma}*\big|e^{-it\sqrt{m^{2}-\Delta}}u_{0}\big|^{2}\Big)e^{-it\sqrt{m^{2}-\Delta}}u_{0}\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathbf{\mathbb{R}}^{d})}\sim|t|^{-\gamma}\quad\text{ for }|t|\gg 1.

The computation is based on the well-known dispersive property of the linear solutions (see
[
16
, Lemma 3]
)

\displaystyle\|e^{-it\sqrt{m^{2}-\Delta}}u_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbf{\mathbb{R}}^{d})}\lesssim(1+|t|)^{-\frac{d}{2}}\quad\text{for}\quad u_{0}\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\mathbf{\mathbb{R}}^{d}).

Based on (
1.3
), they further proved the nonexistence of scattering state when the
long range
interaction
(0<\gamma\leq 1\text{ for }d\geq 3\text{ and }0<\gamma<1

for

d=2)
was concerned where the time decay of nonlinear term fails to be integrable. In contrast, the global solutions to (

, Pusateri
[
18

confirmed this anticipation for sufficiently small initial data in a weighted spaces by closing the gap corresponding to
1<\gamma\leq 2
by proving the scattering result of (
1.1
). Inspired by the work
[
18
]
, we focus on the two dimensional case with full

interaction,
1<\gamma<2
.

Besides, there have been also a lot of results on local well-posedness of (
1.1
) given rough initial data and global well-posedness under the various assumptions. We refer to
[
4

5
,
6
,
11

and references therein for interested readers.

We now state our main theorem for the two dimensional semi-relativistic Hartree equations (

Theorem 1.3

Let
s\geq 30
and

. There exists
\epsilon_{0}>0
satisfying the following:

If the initial data

and

(1.5)

\displaystyle\|u(t)\|_{W^{7,\infty}(\mathbf{\mathbb{R}}^{2})}\lesssim(1+|t|)^{-1}\varepsilon.

Furthermore, there exist
\delta>0
and asymptotic state
v_{+}

with

\displaystyle\|v_{+}\|_{H^{s}(\mathbf{\mathbb{R}}^{2})}+\|\langle x\rangle^{2}v_{+}\|_{H^{10}(\mathbf{\mathbb{R}}^{2})}\lesssim\varepsilon,

satisfying

\displaystyle\Big\|e^{it\sqrt{m^{2}-\Delta}}u(t,x)-v_{+}\Big\|_{H^{s}(\mathbf{\mathbb{R}}^{2})}+\Big\|\langle x\rangle^{2}\Big(e^{it\sqrt{m^{2}-\Delta}}u(t,x)-v_{+}\Big)\Big\|_{H^{10}(\mathbf{\mathbb{R}}^{2})}\lesssim(1+t)^{-\delta}\varepsilon.

Remark 1.4
.

The required regularity
s
depends on
\gamma
but we are not interested in the dependence as well as their optimality in this paper.

In Theorem
1.3
, we prove the global existence of solutions to (
1.1
) when the initial data is sufficiently small in a weighted space. Also, we show that the small nonlinear solutions decays as time evolves with the optimal decay rate in the sense that they decay with the same rate as the linear solution. Furthermore, the solution scatters, i.e., behaves like a linear solution in the weighted spaces as time goes to infinity with the convergence rate
t^{-\delta}
.

Our proof is based on the standard bootstrap argument in a weighted Sobolev spaces. We adapt the weighted space so that we obtain and utilize the optimal time decay of solutions from the well-known dispersive estimates (see (
2.4
)). Then, we
perform the weighted energy estimates to prove the bounds of the energy norms under the a priori assumption. The strategy is based on the method of
space-time resonance
which was developed by Germain-Masmoudi-Shata
[
8
,
9
,
10
]
.

Let us explain in detail the ideas of proof in the weighted energy estimates. We introduce the interaction representation of solutions
u(t)
so as to track the scattering states

\displaystyle f(t,x):=e^{it\sqrt{m^{2}-\Delta}}u(t,x).

Then we can express
f
via Duhamel’s representation

(1.6)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \widehat{f}(t,\xi)&=\widehat{u_{0}}(\xi)+i{\lambda}\mathcal{I}(t,\xi),\\
\mathcal{I}(t,\xi)&=\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}e^{it^{\prime}\phi(\xi,\eta)}|\eta|^{-2+\gamma}\widehat{f}(t^{\prime},\xi-\eta)\widehat{|u|^{2}}(t^{\prime},\eta)d\eta dt^{\prime}\end{aligned}

with the resonance function

\displaystyle\phi(\xi,\eta)=\langle\xi\rangle-\langle\xi-\eta\rangle.

In the course of weighted energy estimates, we have to bound
x^{2}f
in
H^{10}(\mathbf{\mathbb{R}}^{2})
which is converted to
\nabla^{2}\widehat{f}
in the fourier space. The most delicate term in
\nabla^{2}\widehat{f}
occurs when two derivatives both fall on
e^{it^{\prime}\phi(\xi,\eta)}
to give us

(1.7)

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}|t^{\prime}|^{2}\big(\nabla_{\xi}\phi(\xi,\eta)\big)^{2}e^{is\phi(\xi,\eta)}|\eta|^{-2+\gamma}\widehat{f}(t^{\prime},\xi-\eta)\widehat{|u|^{2}}(t^{\prime},\eta)d\eta dt^{\prime},

where we have to compensate the loss in time,
|t^{\prime}|^{2}
.
Here, we encounter the main difficulty from two dimensional nature, i.e., the weaker time decay
|t^{\prime}|^{-1}
of solutions (
1.5
) compared to three or higher dimensional problem because if we just take
L^{2}
-norm of cubic nonlinearity in (
1.7
), we only manage to obtain time decay
|t^{\prime}|^{-2}
at best which is not sufficient to not only recover the time loss but also for the nonlinear term to be integrable in time.
A key observation to overcome this is to exploit the structure in the quadratic term
|u|^{2}
, in other words, to exploit the
space resonance
. Writing it it in terms of
f
as

(1.8)

\displaystyle\widehat{|u|^{2}}(t^{\prime},\eta)=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{2}}\int_{\mathbf{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}e^{-it^{\prime}\phi(\sigma,\eta)}\widehat{f}(t^{\prime},\sigma-\eta)\overline{\widehat{f}(t^{\prime},\sigma})d\sigma,

we find that whenever we preform an integration by parts by using the following relation

\displaystyle e^{it^{\prime}\phi}=-\frac{i}{t^{\prime}}\,\frac{\nabla_{\sigma}\phi\cdot\nabla_{\sigma}e^{it^{\prime}\phi}}{|\nabla_{\sigma}\phi|^{2}},

we can obtain a time decay
|t^{\prime}|^{-1}
.
At the same time, however, it yields a singularity in
\eta
variable because

\displaystyle\nabla_{\sigma}\phi(\sigma,\eta)|_{\eta=0}=\nabla_{\sigma}\Big(\langle\sigma\rangle-\langle\sigma-\eta\rangle\Big)\Big|_{\eta=0}=0.

Nevertheless, in order to recover the time loss,
|t^{\prime}|^{2}
, in (
1.7
), we indeed integrate by parts (
1.8
) twice, which will be possible because we a priori assume
x^{2}f\in H^{10}(\mathbf{\mathbb{R}}^{2})
.

The resulting multiplier which is singular near the origin, or more specifically comparable to
|\eta|^{-2}
, can be bounded with the help of
null structure
in (
1.7
). Indeed, as already observed above, we have

\displaystyle\nabla_{\xi}\phi(\xi,\eta)|_{\eta=0}=0,

so we can think that the following multiplier behaves like the constant

|\nabla_{\xi}\phi(\xi,\eta)|^{2}|\nabla_{\sigma}\phi(\sigma,\eta)|^{-2}\approx 1,

as far as estimates are concerned
1
1

1

See (
3.9
) for precise inequality.
.
Thus, we can finally estimate (
1.7
) similarly to (
1.6
) under the assumption
x^{2}f\in H^{10}(\mathbf{\mathbb{R}}^{2})
. We remark that the regularity of weighted function is required when we apply the multiplier inequality in this procedure.

Organization

In Section 2, we provide the proof of main theorem relying on the standard bootstrapping argument. The rest sections are devoted to proving the weighted energy estimates which played a key role in Section 2. In section 3, we discuss some auxiliary estimates and establish the time decay estimates for the frequently localized quadratic term by exploiting the space resonance. In section 4, we finally prove the weighted energy estimates with the help of null structures.

3.
Time decay estimates

In this section, we establish the time decay estimates for the nonlinear terms and frequency localized solutions under the a priori assumption.

3.1.
Preliminaries

We begin with estimating quadratic terms convolved with the short range potential.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
1<\gamma<2
. For any
\mathbb{C}
-valued functions
u\in L^{2}(\mathbf{\mathbb{R}}^{2})\cap L^{\infty}(\mathbf{\mathbb{R}}^{2})
,
we get

(3.1)

\displaystyle\left\||x|^{-\gamma}*|u|^{2}\right\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|u\|_{L^{2}}^{2-\gamma}\|u\|_{L^{\infty}}^{\gamma}.

Proof.

The proof is quite standard, but we provide it for completeness. We estimate

\displaystyle\||x|^{-\gamma}\ast|u|^{2}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbf{\mathbb{R}}^{2})}

\displaystyle=\sup\int_{\mathbf{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}|y|^{-\gamma}|u|^{2}(x-y)dy

\displaystyle\leq\sup\int_{|y|<R}|y|^{-\gamma}|u|^{2}(x-y)dy+\sup\int_{|y|\geq R}|y|^{-\gamma}|u|^{2}(x-y)dy

\displaystyle\leq\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbf{\mathbb{R}}^{2})}^{2}R^{2-\gamma}+R^{-\gamma}\|u\|_{L^{2}(\mathbf{\mathbb{R}}^{2})}^{2}.

Then taking
R=\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbf{\mathbb{R}}^{2})}^{-1}\|u\|_{L^{2}(\mathbf{\mathbb{R}}^{2})}
gives (
3.1
).
∎

The next lemma is useful when we handle the Fourier multiplier operator.

Lemma 3.2

(Coifman-Meyer operator estimates)
.

Assume that a multiplier
\textbf{m}(\xi,\eta)
satisfies that

(3.2)

\displaystyle C(\textbf{m}):=\left\|\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{2}\times\mathbf{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}\textbf{m}(\xi,\eta)e^{ix\cdot\xi}e^{iy\cdot\eta}\,d\eta d\xi\right\|_{L_{x,y}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{2}\times\mathbf{\mathbb{R}}^{2})}<\infty.

Then, for
\frac{1}{p}+\frac{1}{q}=\frac{1}{2}
,

(3.3)

\displaystyle\left\|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\textbf{m}(\xi,\eta)\widehat{u}(\xi\pm\eta)\widehat{v}(\eta)\,d\eta\right\|_{L_{\xi}^{2}(\mathbf{\mathbb{R}}^{2})}\lesssim C(\textbf{m})\|u\|_{L^{p}(\mathbf{\mathbb{R}}^{2})}\|v\|_{L^{q}(\mathbf{\mathbb{R}}^{2})}.

Proof.

See, for example,
[
18
, Lemma B.1]
.
∎

3.2.
Time decay estimates

We find time decay of the frequency localized quadratic terms under the a priori assumption.

Lemma 3.3
.

Let

u
satisfy the a priori assumption (
2.2
). For a dyadic number
N\in 2^{\mathbf{\mathbb{N}}\cup\{0\}}
, we have

(3.4)

\displaystyle\left\|S_{N}\big(|u(t)|^{2}\big)\right\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbf{\mathbb{R}}^{2})}

\displaystyle\lesssim\min\big(N^{-7}\langle t\rangle^{-2},N^{2-s}\big)\varepsilon_{1}^{2},

Proof.

We write

\displaystyle S_{N}\big(|u(t)|^{2}\big)(x)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{2}}\int_{\mathbf{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}e^{ix\cdot\xi}\rho_{N}(\xi)\langle\xi\rangle^{-7}\mathcal{F}\big\{\langle D\rangle^{7}(|u(t)|^{2})\big\}(\xi)d\xi

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{2}}\mathcal{F}^{-1}\big(\rho_{N}(\xi)\langle\xi\rangle^{-7}\big)\ast\langle D\rangle^{7}(|u(t)|^{2})(x).

By Young’s inequality, we have

\displaystyle\left\|S_{N}\big(|u(t)|^{2}\big)\right\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbf{\mathbb{R}}^{2})}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|\mathcal{F}^{-1}(\rho_{N}(\xi)\langle\xi\rangle^{-7})\|_{L^{1}(\mathbf{\mathbb{R}}^{2})}\|\langle D\rangle^{7}(|u(t)|^{2})\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbf{\mathbb{R}}^{2})}

\displaystyle\lesssim N^{-7}\|u(t)\|_{W^{7,\infty}(\mathbf{\mathbb{R}}^{2})}^{2},

which completes the proof of the first bound in (
3.4
) by (
2.5
).

We consider the second bound. Using the Sobolev embedding, we immediately obtain that

\displaystyle\left\|S_{N}\big(|u(t)|^{2}\big)\right\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbf{\mathbb{R}}^{2})}

\displaystyle\lesssim N\left\|S_{N}\big(|u(t)|^{2}\big)\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathbf{\mathbb{R}}^{2})}.

The Plancherel’s identity yields that

\displaystyle\|S_{N}\big(|u(t)|^{2}\big)\|_{L^{2}(\mathbf{\mathbb{R}}^{2})}=\frac{1}{2\pi}\|\rho_{N}\mathcal{F}\big(|u(t)|^{2}\big)\|_{L^{2}(\mathbf{\mathbb{R}}^{2})},

where

\displaystyle\rho_{N}(\eta)\mathcal{F}\big(|u(t)|^{2}\big)(\eta)

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbf{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}\rho_{N}(\eta)\widehat{u}(\sigma)\overline{\widehat{u}(\sigma+\eta)}d\sigma

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbf{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}\frac{\rho_{N}(\eta)}{\langle\sigma\rangle^{s}\langle\sigma+\eta\rangle^{s}}\widehat{\langle D\rangle^{s}u}(\sigma)\overline{\widehat{\langle D\rangle^{s}u}(\sigma+\eta)}d\sigma,

which implies that

(3.5)

\displaystyle\|S_{N}|u(t)|^{2}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbf{\mathbb{R}}^{2})}\lesssim N\langle N\rangle^{-s}\|u\|_{H^{s}(\mathbf{\mathbb{R}}^{2})}^{2}.

∎

In view of (
2.5
) and (
3.4
) in the previous lemma, it seems that time decay
\langle t\rangle^{-2}
for
L^{\infty}(\mathbf{\mathbb{R}}^{2})
norm of quadratic term is optimal. However, by exploiting structure from bilinear interaction, we can obtain an extra time decay under the a priori assumption at the cost of a derivative for the low frequency part.
In other words, we employ the space resonance method.

Lemma 3.4
.

Let
u
satisfy the a priori assumption (
2.2
). For a dyadic number
L\in 2^{\mathbf{\mathbb{Z}}}

(3.6)

\displaystyle\left\|P_{L}\big(|u(t)|^{2}\big)\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathbf{\mathbb{R}}^{2})}

\displaystyle\lesssim\min\big(L^{-2}{\langle t\rangle}^{-3},L\langle L\rangle^{-s}\big)\varepsilon_{1}^{2}.

Remark 3.5
.

By the Sobolev embedding, we immediately have that

(3.7)

\displaystyle\left\|P_{L}\big(|u(t)|^{2}\big)\right\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbf{\mathbb{R}}^{2})}

\displaystyle\lesssim\min\big(L^{-1}{\langle t\rangle}^{-3},L^{2}\langle L\rangle^{-s}\big)\varepsilon_{1}^{2}.

Proof.

The second bound in (
3.6
) can be proved similarly to the proof of (
3.5
).

We consider the first bound in (
3.6
). We write

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}\big(|u(t)|^{2}\big)(\eta)=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{2}}\int_{\mathbf{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}e^{it({\langle\sigma+\eta\rangle}-{\langle\sigma\rangle})}\widehat{f}(t,\sigma)\overline{\widehat{f}(t,\eta+\sigma})d\sigma,

where
f(t,x)=e^{it\sqrt{1-\Delta}}u(t,x)

Using the relation

(3.8)

\displaystyle e^{it({\langle\sigma+\eta\rangle}-{\langle\sigma\rangle})}=-\frac{i}{t}\,\frac{\nabla_{\sigma}({\langle\sigma+\eta\rangle}-{\langle\sigma\rangle})\cdot\nabla_{\sigma}e^{it({\langle\sigma+\eta\rangle}-{\langle\sigma\rangle})}}{|{\langle\sigma+\eta\rangle}-{\langle\sigma\rangle}|^{2}},

we perform an integration by parts twice to write

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}\big(|u(t)|^{2}\big)(\eta)=J_{1}+J_{2}+J_{3}+J_{4},

where

\displaystyle=\frac{2}{t^{2}}\int_{\mathbf{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}e^{it(\langle\sigma+\eta\rangle-\langle\sigma\rangle)}\nabla_{\sigma}\cdot\mathbf{m}(\eta,\sigma)\,\mathbf{m}(\eta,\sigma)\cdot\nabla_{\sigma}\Big(\widehat{f}(t,\sigma)\overline{\widehat{f}(t,\eta+\sigma})\Big)d\sigma,

\displaystyle J_{3}(t,\eta)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{t^{2}}\int_{\mathbf{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}e^{it(\langle\sigma+\eta\rangle-\langle\sigma\rangle)}\nabla_{\sigma}\otimes\mathbf{m}(\eta,\sigma)\cdot\mathbf{m}(\eta,\sigma)\otimes\nabla_{\sigma}\Big(\widehat{f}(t,\sigma)\overline{\widehat{f}(t,\eta+\sigma})\Big)d\sigma,

\displaystyle J_{4}(t,\eta)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{t^{2}}\int_{\mathbf{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}e^{it(\langle\sigma+\eta\rangle-\langle\sigma\rangle)}\mathbf{m}\otimes\mathbf{m}(\eta,\sigma)\cdot\nabla_{\sigma}\otimes\nabla_{\sigma}\Big(\widehat{f}(t,\sigma)\overline{\widehat{f}(t,\eta+\sigma})\Big)d\sigma,

\displaystyle\frac{|\eta|}{\max(\langle\eta+\sigma\rangle,\langle\sigma\rangle)\min(\langle\eta+\sigma\rangle,\langle\sigma\rangle)^{2}}\lesssim\left|\frac{\eta+\sigma}{{\langle\eta+\sigma\rangle}}-\frac{\sigma}{{\langle\sigma\rangle}}\right|\lesssim\frac{|\eta|}{\max(\langle\eta+\sigma\rangle,\langle\sigma\rangle)},

one can easily verify that for multi index

\displaystyle\left|\partial_{\sigma}^{\alpha}\mathbf{m}(\eta,\sigma)\right|\lesssim|\eta|^{-1}\max(\langle\eta+\sigma\rangle,\langle\sigma\rangle)\min(\langle\eta+\sigma\rangle,\langle\sigma\rangle)^{2+|\alpha|}.

First, we consider

Proof of (

)
.

Recall that under the a priori assumption (

Then, by using (

\displaystyle\|N_{\gamma}(u,u,u)(t)\|_{H^{s}(\mathbf{\mathbb{R}}^{2})}

∎

.

\mathcal{I}_{1}
.
We write

\displaystyle\left\|\mathcal{I}_{1}(t^{\prime},\xi)\right\|_{L_{\xi}^{2}(\mathbf{\mathbb{R}}^{2})}\leq\left\|\mathcal{I}_{1,1}(t^{\prime},\xi)\right\|_{L_{\xi}^{2}(\mathbf{\mathbb{R}}^{2})}+\left\|\mathcal{I}_{1,2}(t^{\prime},\xi)\right\|_{L_{\xi}^{2}(\mathbf{\mathbb{R}}^{2})},

where

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}_{1,1}(t^{\prime},\xi)

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}e^{it^{\prime}{\phi}(\xi,\eta)}|\eta|^{-2+\gamma}\langle\xi-\eta\rangle^{10}\widehat{x^{2}f}(t^{\prime},\xi-\eta)\mathcal{F}(|u(t^{\prime})|^{2})(\eta)\,d\eta,

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}_{1,2}(t^{\prime},\xi)

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}e^{it^{\prime}{\phi}(\xi,\eta)}\big(\langle\xi\rangle^{10}-\langle\xi-\eta\rangle^{10}\big)|\eta|^{-2+\gamma}\widehat{x^{2}f}(t^{\prime},\xi-\eta)\mathcal{F}(|u(t^{\prime})|^{2})(\eta)\,d\eta.

Using (
3.1
) and time decay estimates (
4.1
), the first term is bounded by

\displaystyle\left\|\mathcal{I}_{1,1}(t^{\prime},\xi)\right\|_{L_{\xi}^{2}(\mathbf{\mathbb{R}}^{2})}

\displaystyle\lesssim\Big\|\big(|x|^{-\gamma}\ast|u(t^{\prime})|^{2}\big)\langle D\rangle^{10}(x^{2}f)\left(t^{\prime}\right)\Big\|_{L^{2}(\mathbf{\mathbb{R}}^{2})}

\displaystyle\lesssim\left\||x|^{-\gamma}\ast|u(t^{\prime})|^{2}\right\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbf{\mathbb{R}}^{2})}\|x^{2}f(t^{\prime})\|_{H^{10}(\mathbf{\mathbb{R}}^{2})}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|u(t^{\prime})\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbf{\mathbb{R}}^{2})}^{\gamma}\|u(t^{\prime})\|_{L^{2}(\mathbf{\mathbb{R}}^{2})}^{2-\gamma}\|x^{2}f(t^{\prime})\|_{H^{10}(\mathbf{\mathbb{R}}^{2})}\lesssim\langle t^{\prime}\rangle^{-\gamma}\varepsilon_{1}^{3}.

Next, for
I_{1,2}
, we observe that the singularity near the origin,
|\eta|^{-2+\gamma}
, is removed by the multiplier as follows:

(4.3)

\displaystyle\big(\langle\xi\rangle^{10}-\langle\xi-\eta\rangle^{10}\big)|\eta|^{-2+\gamma}\lesssim|\eta|^{\gamma-1}\max(\langle\xi\rangle^{9},\langle\xi-\eta\rangle^{9}).

We perform dyadic decomposition

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}_{1,2}(t^{\prime},\xi)

\displaystyle=\sum_{N_{1},N_{2}\in 2^{\mathbf{\mathbb{N}}\cup\{0\}}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}e^{it^{\prime}{\phi}(\xi,\eta)}\big(\langle\xi\rangle^{10}-\langle\xi-\eta\rangle^{10}\big)|\eta|^{-2+\gamma}\widehat{S_{N_{1}}x^{2}f}(t^{\prime},\xi-\eta)\widehat{S_{N_{2}}|u(t^{\prime})|^{2}}(\eta)\,d\eta,

and obtain that

\displaystyle\left\|\mathcal{I}_{1,2}(t^{\prime},\xi)\right\|_{L_{\xi}^{2}(\mathbf{\mathbb{R}}^{2})}\lesssim

\displaystyle\qquad\sum_{N_{0},N_{1},N_{2}\in 2^{\mathbf{\mathbb{N}}\cup\{0\}}}\left\|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}e^{it^{\prime}{\phi}(\xi,\eta)}m_{1}^{(N_{0},N_{1},N_{2})}(\xi,\eta)\widehat{S_{N_{1}}x^{2}f}(t^{\prime},\xi-\eta)\widehat{S_{N_{2}}|u(t^{\prime})|^{2}}(\eta)\,d\eta\right\|_{L_{\xi}^{2}(\mathbf{\mathbb{R}}^{2})},

where

\displaystyle m_{1}^{(N_{0},N_{1},N_{2})}(\xi,\eta):=\big(\langle\xi\rangle^{10}-\langle\xi-\eta\rangle^{10}\big)|\eta|^{-2+\gamma}\rho_{N_{0}}(\xi)\rho_{N_{1}}(\xi-\eta)\rho_{N_{2}}(\eta).

Here, we observe that the summation actually runs over those indices
(N_{0},N_{1},N_{2})
in the following set

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}:=\left\{(N_{0},N_{1},N_{2})\in(2^{\mathbf{\mathbb{N}}\cup\{0\}})^{3}\;|\;N_{0}\lesssim N_{1}\sim N_{2}\text{ or }N_{0}\sim\max(N_{1},N_{2})\right\}.

A direct computation with (
4.3
) yields that

\displaystyle C\big(m_{1}^{(N_{0},N_{1},N_{2})}\big)\lesssim\max(N_{0},N_{1})^{9}N_{2}^{\,-1+\gamma},

thus, by using the operator inequality (
3.3
) with the above bound, we have

\displaystyle\left\|\mathcal{I}_{1,2}(t^{\prime},\xi)\right\|_{{}_{L_{\xi}^{2}(\mathbf{\mathbb{R}}^{2})}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{(N_{0},N_{1},N_{2})\in\mathcal{A}}\max(N_{0},N_{1})^{9}N_{2}^{\,-1+\gamma}\|S_{N_{1}}x^{2}f(t^{\prime})\|_{L^{2}(\mathbf{\mathbb{R}}^{2})}\|S_{N_{2}}|u(t^{\prime})|^{2}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbf{\mathbb{R}}^{2})}.

Then, by applying (
3.4
) to the quadratic term, we bound the sum over
N_{2}\leq\langle t^{\prime}\rangle^{\frac{2}{s-9}}
by

\displaystyle\langle t^{\prime}\rangle^{-2}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}(N_{0},N_{1},N_{2})\in\mathcal{A}\\
N_{2}\leq\langle t^{\prime}\rangle^{\frac{2}{s-9}}\end{subarray}}\max(N_{0},N_{1})^{9}N_{2}^{-1+\gamma}N_{1}^{-10}N_{2}^{-7}\epsilon_{1}^{3}

\displaystyle\lesssim\langle t^{\prime}\rangle^{-2+\frac{2}{s-9}(\gamma+1)}\varepsilon_{1}^{3},

which is bounded by
\langle t^{\prime}\rangle^{-\frac{\gamma-1}{3}}\varepsilon_{1}^{3}
, since
s\geq 30
.
On the other hand, we use (
3.7
) to bound the remaining contribution by

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}(N_{0},N_{1},N_{2})\in\mathcal{A}\\
N_{2}\geq\langle t^{\prime}\rangle^{\frac{2}{s-9}}\end{subarray}}\max(N_{0},N_{1})^{9}N_{1}^{-10}N_{2}^{1+\gamma-s}\varepsilon_{1}^{3}\lesssim\langle t^{\prime}\rangle^{-2+\frac{2}{s-9}(\gamma+1)}\varepsilon_{1}^{3}.

Estimates for
\mathcal{I}_{2}
. We decompose the frequency variables
\xi,\xi-\eta
into the dyadic pieces
|\xi|\sim N_{0}
and
|\xi-\eta|\sim N_{1}
for
N_{0},N_{1}\in 2^{\mathbf{\mathbb{N}}\cup\{0\}}
, respectively.
On the other hand, for
\eta
variable associated to the potential, we decompose not only high frequency but also the low frequency part near the origin, i.e.,
|\eta|\sim L
for
L\in 2^{\mathbf{\mathbb{Z}}}
. Then, we write

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}_{2}(t^{\prime},\xi)=\sum_{N_{0},N_{1}\in 2^{\mathbf{\mathbb{N}}\cup\{0\}},\,L\in 2^{\mathbf{\mathbb{Z}}}}\mathcal{I}_{2}^{(N_{0},N_{1},L)}(t^{\prime},\xi),

where

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}_{2}^{(N_{0},N_{1},L)}(t^{\prime},\xi)=it^{\prime}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}e^{it^{\prime}\phi(\xi,\eta)}\mathbf{m}_{2}^{(N_{0},N_{1},L)}(\xi,\eta)\cdot\widehat{S_{N_{1}}xf}(t^{\prime},\xi-\eta)\widehat{P_{L}(|u(t^{\prime})|^{2})}(\eta)\,d\eta,

where

\displaystyle\mathbf{m}_{2}^{(N_{0},N_{1},L)}(\xi,\eta)=\langle\xi\rangle^{10}|\eta|^{-2+\gamma}\,\nabla_{\xi}\phi(\xi,\eta)\rho_{N}(\xi)\rho_{N_{1}}(\xi-\eta)\chi_{L}(\eta).

Then,

\displaystyle\left\|\mathcal{I}_{2}(t^{\prime},\xi)\right\|_{L_{\xi}^{2}(\mathbf{\mathbb{R}}^{2})}\lesssim\sum_{(N_{0},N_{1},L)\in\widetilde{\mathcal{A}}}\left\|\mathcal{I}^{(N_{0},N_{1},L)}_{2}(t^{\prime},\xi)\right\|_{L_{\xi}^{2}(\mathbf{\mathbb{R}}^{2})},

where

(4.4)

\displaystyle\widetilde{\mathcal{A}}=\left\{(N_{0},N_{1},L)\in(2^{\mathbf{\mathbb{N}}\cup\{0\}})^{2}\times 2^{\mathbf{\mathbb{Z}}}\;|\;L\lesssim N_{1}\sim N_{2}\text{ or }L\sim\max(N_{0},N_{1})\right\}.

From (
3.9
), one can readily verify that

(4.5)

\displaystyle C\big(\mathbf{m}_{2}^{(N_{0},N_{1},L)}\big)\lesssim N_{0}^{10}L^{-1+\gamma}\max(N_{0},N_{1})^{-1}.

Then, by the operator inequality (
3.3
) with the above bound, we obtain

(4.6)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} &\left\|\mathcal{I}_{2}^{(N_{0},N_{1},L)}(t^{\prime},\xi)\right\|_{L_{\xi}^{2}(\mathbf{\mathbb{R}}^{2})}\lesssim\\
&\qquad|t^{\prime}|N_{0}^{10}L^{-1+\gamma}\max(N_{0},N_{1})^{-1}\left\|S_{N_{1}}{xf}(t^{\prime})\,\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathbf{\mathbb{R}}^{2})}\left\|P_{L}|u(t^{\prime})|^{2}\right\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbf{\mathbb{R}}^{2})}.\end{aligned}

We observe from (
3.13
) that under the a priori assumption

\left\|S_{N_{1}}{xf}(t^{\prime})\,\right\|_{L^{2}}\lesssim N_{1}^{-10}\epsilon_{1}.

Using this and (
3.7
), we bound the summation over
L\leq\langle t^{\prime}\rangle^{-1}
by

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}(N_{0},N_{1},L)\in\widetilde{\mathcal{A}}\\
L\,\lesssim\,\langle t^{\prime}\rangle^{-1}\end{subarray}}\left\|\mathcal{I}_{2}^{(N_{0},N_{1},L)}(t^{\prime},\xi)\right\|_{L_{\xi}^{2}(\mathbf{\mathbb{R}}^{2})}

\displaystyle\lesssim|t^{\prime}|\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}(N_{0},N_{1},L)\in\widetilde{\mathcal{A}}\\
L\,\lesssim\,\langle t^{\prime}\rangle^{-1}\end{subarray}}N_{0}^{10}L^{1+\gamma}\max(N_{0},N_{1})^{-1}N_{1}^{-10}\varepsilon_{1}^{3}

\displaystyle\lesssim\langle t^{\prime}\rangle^{-\gamma}\varepsilon_{1}^{3},

the summation over
\langle t^{\prime}\rangle^{-1}\leq L\leq\langle t^{\prime}\rangle^{\frac{2}{s-3}}
by

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}(N_{0},N_{1},L)\in\widetilde{\mathcal{A}}\\
\langle t^{\prime}\rangle^{-1}\,\lesssim L\,\lesssim\,\langle t^{\prime}\rangle^{\frac{3}{s-3}}\end{subarray}}\left\|\mathcal{I}_{2}^{(N_{0},N_{1},L)}(t^{\prime},\xi)\right\|_{L_{\xi}^{2}(\mathbf{\mathbb{R}}^{2})}

\displaystyle\quad\lesssim\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}(N_{0},N_{1},L)\in\widetilde{\mathcal{A}}\\
\langle t^{\prime}\rangle^{-1}\,\lesssim L\,\lesssim\,\langle t^{\prime}\rangle^{\frac{2}{s-3}}\end{subarray}}|t^{\prime}|^{-2}N_{0}^{10}L^{-2+\gamma}\max(N_{0},N_{1})^{-1}N_{1}^{-10}\varepsilon_{1}^{3}

\displaystyle\quad\lesssim\langle t^{\prime}\rangle^{-\gamma}\varepsilon_{1}^{3}+\langle t^{\prime}\rangle^{-2+\frac{2}{s-3}(7+\gamma)}\varepsilon_{1}^{3},

and finally the summation over
L\geq\langle t^{\prime}\rangle^{\frac{2}{s-3}}
by

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}(N_{0},N_{1},L)\in\widetilde{\mathcal{A}}\\
L\,\geq\,\langle t^{\prime}\rangle^{\frac{2}{s-3}}\end{subarray}}\left\|\mathcal{I}_{2}^{(N_{0},N_{1},L)}(t^{\prime},\xi)\right\|_{L_{\xi}^{2}(\mathbf{\mathbb{R}}^{2})}

\displaystyle\lesssim|t^{\prime}|\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}(N_{0},N_{1},L)\in\widetilde{\mathcal{A}}\\
L\,\geq\,\langle t^{\prime}\rangle^{\frac{2}{s-3}}\end{subarray}}N_{0}^{10}L^{1+\gamma-s}\max(N_{0},N_{1})^{-1}N_{1}^{-10}

\displaystyle\lesssim\langle t^{\prime}\rangle^{-2+\frac{2}{s-3}(7+\gamma)}\varepsilon_{1}^{3},

which is bounded by
\langle t^{\prime}\rangle^{-\frac{\gamma-1}{3}}\varepsilon_{1}^{3}
, since
s\geq 30
.

Estimates for
\mathcal{I}_{3}
.

\mathcal{I}_{3}
can be handled in an anolugous manner to
\mathcal{I}_{2}
.
Indeed, one finds that the multiplier
\nabla_{\xi}^{2}\phi(\xi,\eta)
in
\mathcal{I}_{3}
verifies the smaller bound than the one given in (
3.9
) satisfied by the multiplier
\nabla_{\xi}\phi(\xi,\eta)
in
\mathcal{I}_{2}

More precisely, the following bound holds

\displaystyle\left|\nabla_{\xi}^{2}\phi(\xi,\eta)\right|\lesssim\frac{|\eta|}{\max(\langle\xi\rangle,\langle\xi-\eta\rangle)^{2}}.

Thus, if we let

\displaystyle\mathbf{m}_{3}^{(N_{0},N_{1},L)}(\xi,\eta)=\langle\xi\rangle^{10}|\eta|^{-2+\gamma}\nabla_{\xi}^{2}\phi(\xi,\eta)\rho_{N_{0}}(\xi)\chi_{L}(\eta)\rho_{N_{1}}(\xi-\eta),

one can verify that

\displaystyle C(\mathbf{m}_{3}^{(N_{0},N_{1},L)})

\displaystyle\lesssim N_{0}^{10}\max(N_{0},N_{1})^{-2}L^{-1+\gamma}.

Applying these bounds into (
4.6
) instead of (
4.5
), one can obtain the desired bounds.

Estimates for

It remains to estimate the main case
\mathcal{I}_{4}

As before, we decompose

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}_{4}(t^{\prime},\xi)=\sum_{N_{0},N_{1}\in(2^{\mathbf{\mathbb{N}}})^{2},\,L\in 2^{\mathbf{\mathbb{Z}}}}\mathcal{I}_{4}^{(N_{0},N_{1},L)}(t^{\prime},\xi),

where

\displaystyle\mathbf{m}_{4}^{(N_{0},N_{1},L)}(\xi,\eta)=\langle\xi\rangle^{10}|\eta|^{-2+\gamma}\big(\nabla_{\xi}\phi(\xi,\eta)\big)^{2}\rho_{N_{0}}(\xi)\rho_{N_{1}}(\xi-\eta)\chi_{L}(\eta).

Then,

\displaystyle\left\|\mathcal{I}_{4}(t^{\prime},\xi)\right\|_{L_{\xi}^{2}(\mathbf{\mathbb{R}}^{2})}\lesssim\sum_{(N_{0},N_{1},L)\in\widetilde{\mathcal{A}}}\left\|\mathcal{I}^{(N_{0},N_{1},L)}_{4}(t^{\prime},\xi)\right\|_{L_{\xi}^{2}(\mathbf{\mathbb{R}}^{2})},

where
\widetilde{\mathcal{A}}
is given in (

).
From (
3.9
), one can readily verify that

\displaystyle C\big(\mathbf{m}_{4}^{(N_{0},N_{1},L)}\big)\lesssim L^{\gamma}N_{0}^{10}\max(N_{0},N_{1})^{-2},

which gives that

\displaystyle\left\|\mathcal{I}_{4}^{(N_{0},N_{1},L)}(t^{\prime},\xi)\right\|_{L_{\xi}^{2}(\mathbf{\mathbb{R}}^{2})}\lesssim|t^{\prime}|^{2}L^{\gamma}N_{0}^{10}\max(N_{0},N_{1})^{-2}\|S_{N_{1}}u(t^{\prime})\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbf{\mathbb{R}}^{2})}\|P_{L}(|u(t^{\prime})|^{2})\|_{L^{2}(\mathbf{\mathbb{R}}^{2})}.

Using (
3.6
), we estimate the summation over
L\leq\langle t^{\prime}\rangle^{-1}

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}(N_{0},N_{1},L)\in\widetilde{\mathcal{A}}\\

the summation over
\langle t^{\prime}\rangle^{-1}\leq L\leq\langle t^{\prime}\rangle^{\frac{3}{s-3}}

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}(N_{0},N_{1},L)\in\widetilde{\mathcal{A}}\\
\langle t^{\prime}\rangle^{-1}\leq L\leq\langle t^{\prime}\rangle^{\frac{3}{s-3}}\end{subarray}}\left\|\mathcal{I}^{(N_{0},N_{1},L)}_{4}(t^{\prime},\xi)\right\|_{L_{\xi}^{2}(\mathbf{\mathbb{R}}^{2})}

\displaystyle\quad\lesssim\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}(N_{0},N_{1},L)\in\widetilde{\mathcal{A}}\\
\langle t^{\prime}\rangle^{-1}\leq L\leq\langle t^{\prime}\rangle^{\frac{3}{s-3}}\end{subarray}}\langle t^{\prime}\rangle^{-2}L^{\gamma-2}N_{0}^{10}\max(N_{0},N_{1})^{-2}N_{1}^{-7}\varepsilon_{1}^{3}

\displaystyle\quad\lesssim\langle t^{\prime}\rangle^{-2}\varepsilon_{1}^{3}+\langle t^{\prime}\rangle^{-2+\frac{3(6+\gamma)}{s-3}}\varepsilon_{1}^{3},

and finally the summation over

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}(N_{0},N_{1},L)\in\widetilde{\mathcal{A}}\\

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}(N_{0},N_{1},L)\in\widetilde{\mathcal{A}}\\
L\geq\langle t^{\prime}\rangle^{\frac{3}{s-3}}\end{subarray}}|t^{\prime}|L^{\gamma+1-s}\langle N_{0}\rangle^{10}\max(N_{0},N_{1})^{-2}N_{1}^{-7}\varepsilon_{1}^{3}

\displaystyle\lesssim\langle t^{\prime}\rangle^{-2+\frac{3}{s-3}(\gamma+6)}\varepsilon_{1}^{3},

which is bounded by
\langle t^{\prime}\rangle^{-\frac{\gamma-1}{3}}\varepsilon_{1}^{3}
, since
s\geq 30
.

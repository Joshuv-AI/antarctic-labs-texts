1.
Introduction and Background

Poincaré and Poincaré-Sobolev inequalities have been studied extensively in a wide variety of scenarios, including the general theory for convex sets. The particular case of cubes is specially interesting, once we have a good result on cubes there are mechanisms (sometimes chaining methods) to pass to more general domains, see for instance Chapter 9 from the very recent manuscript
[
KLV
]
(also
[
HKST15
]
). See also
the last part of Section 5 in
[
FPW98
]
to see the connection with weights.
The classical technique for getting this kind of inequalities is through the use of a
representation formula in terms of a fractional integral. An approach which avoids the use of any representation formula to obtain Poincaré-Sobolev inequalities on cubes (or balls) was introduced in
[
FPW98
]
and then sharpened in

[
MP98
]
. This approach merges with the John-Nirenberg theory of the
\operatorname{BMO}
spaces and others under the phenomenon of the so called self-imroving property.
However this “abstract” general theory has not been developed in the context of rectangles or, more generally, product spaces as was considered for first time in
[
ST93
]
and later in
[
LW98
]
, which will be the main purpose of this article. In this different scenario it is reasonable to expect some sensitivity with respect to very thin rectangles which is reflected in the dependence on the notion of
eccentricity
: roughly, a quantity that indicates how far the given object is from being a cube. This notion will be essential to our results and we will properly define it later.

We will study generalized weighted Poincaré and Poincaré-Sobolev type inequalities related to two product space settings. The most general possible situation is the case of the family of rectangles in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
seen as
n
-fold product of intervals on
\mathbb{R}
, denoted by
\mathcal{R}
.
We will study a variant of this case by considering rectangles
R
of the form
R=I_{1}\times I_{2}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
where
I_{1}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n_{1}}
and
I_{2}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n_{2}}
are cubes with sides parallel to the coordinate axes, denoted by
\mathfrak{R}
. We will deal with these two situations separately to simplify the presentation. Our approach consists in starting with some generalized unweighted Poincaré inequality with respect to an
L^{1}
mean oscillation and from there we will improve it in order to gain higher integrability with the minimum loss in the involved constants.

The motivation to study this situation comes from the following very interesting
(1,1)
-Poincaré inequality valid for any
bounded convex domain
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
,

(1.1)

\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$}}\kern-4.86108pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$}}\kern-3.43057pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.908pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.76045pt}}\!\int_{\Omega}|f-f_{\Omega}|dx\leq\frac{1}{2}\,d(\Omega)\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$}}\kern-4.86108pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$}}\kern-3.43057pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.908pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.76045pt}}\!\int_{\Omega}|\nabla f|dx,

where we used
f_{\Omega}
and
\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$}}\kern-4.86108pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$}}\kern-3.43057pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.908pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.76045pt}}\!\int_{\Omega}f\ dx
to denote the average of the function
f
over the set
\Omega
and
d(\Omega)
stands for the diameter of the set
\Omega
. This result is due to G. Acosta and R. Durán in
[
AD04
]
where the constant
\frac{1}{2}
in front is best possible. Of course, it is a classical well-known fact that this estimate holds for any convex set
\Omega
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
with a larger constant in front.
In particular, (
1.1
) holds in the case of
\Omega
being a rectangle of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
with sides parallel to the axes.
The key idea here is that inequality (
1.1
) enjoys a sort of self-improving property, meaning that we can reach higher integrability and therefore obtain
(p,p)
-Poincaré and
(p^{*},p)
-Poincaré-Sobolev type inequalities, with
p^{*}>p
, both in the unweighted and the weighted settings. This self-improving phenomena is not new and can be traced back to
[
SC92
,
HaK95
]
in a more classical setting, but in a more general abstract way in
[
FPW98
]
improved in
[
MP98
]
. A strengthened version of the results from these papers
can be found in
[
PR19
]
where much more precise
L^{q}-L^{p}
weighted Poincaré-Sobolev inequalities over cubes in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
were obtained. To be more precise
it is remarkable that the self-improving property is not so much related to the presence of a gradient on the right hand side of (
1.1
), but to a discrete summation condition associated to the functional that it defines. In fact, we will consider as a starting point estimates of the form

(1.2)

\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$}}\kern-4.86108pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$}}\kern-3.43057pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.908pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.76045pt}}\!\int_{R}|f-f_{R}|dx\leq a(R)\qquad R\in\mathcal{F}

where
\mathcal{F}
is a given family of sets and
a:\mathcal{F}\to(0,\infty)
is some general functional with no restriction. Typical examples of
\mathcal{F}
are the family of cubes
\mathcal{Q}
, the families of rectangles from
\mathcal{R}
or
\mathfrak{R}
, or any other collection of sets. The key difference when moving from the basis
\mathcal{R}
to the basis
\mathfrak{R}
is not on the selfimproving phenomena, as it will be clear from the proofs. The issue will be to find the appropriate analogue for (
1.2
), where the functional
a(R)
will be replaced by a
sum
of two functionals
a_{1}(I)+a_{2}(J)
, with
R=I\times J
. Part of the effort will be devoted to prove these starting points where the oscillation is controlled by a sum of functionals.

This kind of generalized Poincaré inequality with a general abstract functional acting on balls (or cubes)
over a metric (or quasi-metric) space with a doubling measure (spaces of homogeneous type) was considered for first time in
[
FPW98
]
.
A special geometric condition, denoted by
D_{p}
, was introduced with the idea of deriving self-improving properties of the given mean oscillation of the function on the left hand side. This condition has been recently refined in
[
PR19
]
, denoted
SD_{p}
, which produces much more accurate results.
We introduce both conditions
D_{p}
and
SD_{p}
for the families
\mathcal{R}
and
\mathfrak{R}
in Definition
1.3
and Definition
1.1
below
in the context of rectangles. We will make special emphasis on the
SD_{p}
type condition since it is highly efficient allowing to improve the estimates obtained in
[
FPW98
]
in a much more precise way.

To present here a preview of the main contributions of this paper, consider the particular case of
a(R)
being the average of the
L^{1}
norm of the gradient of
f
, namely
a(R)=d(R)\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$}}\kern-4.86108pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$}}\kern-3.43057pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.908pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.76045pt}}\!\int_{R}|\nabla f|
. The main goal here would be, starting from an inequality of the form (
1.2
) and using certain discrete summability conditions satisfied by the functional
a
, to obtain an inequality involving
L^{q}-L^{p}
norms of the form

\left(\frac{1}{w(R)}\int_{R}|f-f_{R}|^{q}w\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}\leq C_{w}\,d(R)\left(\frac{1}{w(R)}\int_{R}|\nabla f|^{p}w\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}

where
w
is a certain weight. We will try to reach the best possible
q
but keeping controlled the constant
C_{w}

extending and improving the results in
[
FPW98
]
and
[
PR19
]
.

The precise definition that we need is the following. As usual, we will say that a
weight
is a non negative locally integrable function defined on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Also, the Lebesgue measure for a measurable set
\Omega
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
is denoted by
|\Omega|
.

Definition 1.1
.

For a given weight
w
, and
s>0
, we say that a functional
a\in SD_{p,\mathcal{R}}^{s}(w)
for
0<p<+\infty
if there is a constant
c
such that for any family
of disjoint
dyadic
subrectangles
\left\{R_{i}\right\}_{i}
of
R
the following inequality holds

(1.3)

\left(\sum_{i}a(R_{i})^{p}\frac{w(R_{i})}{w(R)}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}\leq c\left(\frac{|\bigcup_{i}R_{i}|}{|R|}\right)^{\frac{1}{s}}a(R).

The best possible constant
c
(the infimum of the constants in last inequality) is denoted by

\|a\|_{SD_{p,\mathcal{R}}^{s}}
. Usually, if there is no chance of confusion, we will drop the subscript for the sake of clarity in the exposition.

Sometimes we will refer to this condition as the smallness preservation condition. The idea comes from the main application, namely if
L>1
and if
R
is a rectangle, and a family of dyadic rectangles is
L
-small, namely that

(1.4)

\frac{|\bigcup_{i}R_{i}|}{|R|}\leq\frac{1}{L}.

then the functional
a
preserves the smallness by applying (
1.3
).

Remark 1.2
.

As it can be easily verified, the exact same definition applies to the family
\mathfrak{R}
using the appropriate notation
SD_{p,\mathfrak{R}}^{s}(w)
and
\|a\|_{SD_{p,\mathfrak{R}}^{s}}.

We also recall the following geometric type condition which is somehow a “rough” version of (
1.3
). Again, the same definition applies to
\mathfrak{R}
with the obvious notational modifications.

Definition 1.3
.

Let
w
be any weight. We say that the functional
a
satisfies the weighted
D_{p,\mathcal{R}}(w)
condition for
0<p<\infty
if there is a constant
c
such that for any
R\in\mathcal{R}
and any family of disjoint
dyadic
subrectangles
\left\{R_{i}\right\}_{i}
of
R
the following inequality holds:

(1.5)

\left(\sum_{i}a(R_{i})^{p}\frac{w(R_{i})}{w(R)}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}\leq c\,a(R).

The best possible constant
c
above is denoted by
\|a\|_{D_{p,\mathcal{R}}(w)}
. We will write in this case that
a\in D_{p,\mathcal{R}}(w)
. Observe that
\|a\|_{D_{p,\mathcal{R}}(w)}\geq 1
.

Condition (
1.5
) in the context of cubes or balls in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, or more generally in the context of Spaces of Homogeneous type was introduced in
[
FPW98
]
. It was also considered in
[
OP02
]
in the “non-homogeneous” context where an abstract self-improving property was obtained.

We conclude this introduction by remarking how condition (
1.5
) can be used, in the much easier context of cubes
\mathcal{Q}
with no weight, to derive very sharp proofs in the theory of fractional Sobolev spaces. Indeed, in this paper, Corollary
2.11

and many others, are extensions of the fractional Poincaré-Sobolev inequality

(1.6)

\left(\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$}}\kern-4.86108pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$}}\kern-3.43057pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.908pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.76045pt}}\!\int_{Q}|u-u_{Q}|^{p^{*}_{\delta}}dx\right)^{\frac{1}{p^{*}_{\delta}}}\leq c_{n,p^{*}_{\delta}}\,[u]_{W^{\delta,p}}(Q),\qquad Q\in\mathcal{Q}.

We are using here the notation
p_{\delta}^{*}
for the fractional Sobolev exponent defined by
\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{p_{\delta}^{*}}=\frac{\delta}{n}
and also the notation

[u]_{W^{\delta,p}(Q)}:=\ell(Q)^{\delta}\left(\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$}}\kern-4.86108pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$}}\kern-3.43057pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.908pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.76045pt}}\!\int_{Q}\int_{Q}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{p}}{|x-y|^{n+\delta p}}\,dy\,dx\right)^{1/p}.

To illustrate the self-improving philosophy in the case of cubes, we will discuss the main steps of the proof of (
1.6
) and a further improvement. This approach avoids completely the use of classical potential theory (see
[
KLV
]
in the classical case and
[
NPV12
]
in the fractional case) and this is the reason why we can go beyond and consider other geometries and degeneracies. Moreover, due to the presence of a more complicated geometry and to the presence of degeneracies produced by weights, the proofs of the results in the present paper require substantial work and involve serious technical difficulties.

We begin with the following easy to get
inequality

(1.7)

\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$}}\kern-4.86108pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$}}\kern-3.43057pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.908pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.76045pt}}\!\int_{Q}\lvert u(x)-u_{Q}\rvert\,dx\leq c_{n}\,[u]_{W^{\delta,p}(Q)}\qquad Q\in\mathcal{Q},\quad\delta>0

which we claim encodes a lot of information, at least in the case
\delta\in(0,1)
. Indeed, it can be shown using the ideas in
[
FPW98
]
, with the recently obtained improvements from
[
CP21
]
, the following weak type version of (
1.6
)

(1.8)

\|u-u_{Q}\|_{L^{p^{*}_{\delta},\infty}\big(Q,\frac{dx}{|Q|}\big)}\leq c_{n}\,p^{*}_{\delta}\,\,[u]_{W^{\delta,p}(Q)},

which holds whenever
p\in[1,\infty)
and for
\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{p_{\delta}^{*}}=\frac{\delta}{n}
. Inequality (
1.8
) really follows from

\left(\sum_{i}a(Q_{i})^{p^{*}_{\delta}}\frac{|Q_{i}|}{|Q|}\right)^{\frac{1}{p^{*}_{\delta}}}\leq a(Q),

where
a(Q)=[u]_{W^{\delta,p}(Q)}
for any
\delta\in(0,1)
and any
p\in[1,\infty)
(observe that the constant in front is equal to 1). Now, since the truncation argument works for the functional
[u]_{W^{\delta,p}(Q)}
as shown in
[
DIV16
]
, we can replace the weak norm (
1.8
) by the strong norm

\|u-u_{Q}\|_{L^{p^{*}_{\delta}}\big(Q,\frac{dx}{|Q|}\big)}\leq c_{n}\,p^{*}_{\delta}\,\,[u]_{W^{\delta,p}(Q)}

(actually we can put the Lorentz norm
\|\|_{L^{p^{*}_{\delta},p}}
as well). On the other hand, it turns out that this result is far from being optimal. Indeed, motivated by the works
[
BBM02
,
MS02
]
, M. Milman obtained in
[
M05
]
, using ideas from interpolation theory, a very general way of proving the following self-improvement of (
1.7
),

\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$}}\kern-4.86108pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$}}\kern-3.43057pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.908pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.76045pt}}\!\int_{Q}|u-u_{Q}|\,dx\leq c_{n}\,(1-\delta)^{\frac{1}{p}}\,\,[u]_{W^{\delta,p}(Q)},

where the highly interesting extra gain
(1-\delta)^{\frac{1}{p}}
appears in front.
Then, exactly as above, the results in
[
CP21
]
combined with the truncation from
[
DIV16
]
yield the following highly interesting improvement of (
1.6
).

Theorem 1.4
.

Let
0<\delta<1
and
1\leq p<\infty
. Then,

\left(\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$}}\kern-4.86108pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$}}\kern-3.43057pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.908pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.76045pt}}\!\int_{Q}|u(x)-u_{Q}|^{p^{*}_{\delta}}dx\right)^{\frac{1}{p^{*}_{\delta}}}\leq c_{n}\,p^{*}_{\delta}\,(1-\delta)^{\frac{1}{p}}\,[u]_{W^{\delta,p}(Q)}\qquad Q\in\mathcal{Q}

Remark 1.5
.

Standard arguments can be used to obtain the strong global estimate in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
with the correction factor in front
(1-\delta)^{\frac{1}{p}}
.

Some extensions of (
1.6
) to the case of domains can be found in
[
HV13
]
.
We also refer to
[
CDM19
]
for extensions to the doubling metric case.

We plan to extend this result into the context of the product space
\mathfrak{R}
from which we will derive the corresponding version
of (
1.6
) and then derive its corresponding Poincaré-Sobolev extension from our general method (see Theorem
2.20
).

2.
Main results

For the sake of clarity on the presentation, we will present our results for the basis
\mathcal{R}
and
\mathfrak{R}
separately.

2.1.
Analysis in
\mathcal{R}

In this section we will study the self-improving phenomena in the context of
\mathcal{R}
wich will be served as a model for the case
\mathfrak{R}
that will be considered in Section
2.5
. First we will obtain Poincaré-Sobolev type inequalities involving higher order derivatives for rectangles in
\mathcal{R}
(some references about higher order Poincaré-Sobolev type inequalities can be found in
[
AH96
,
T00
]
). To see where is the difficulty here, note in Definition
1.1
that the condition on the family of rectangles is related to their measure. The classical one parameter theory for cubes works fine here, since the
n
-th power of the diameter (or the sidelength) of a cube is comparable to its measure. However, in the multiparametric setting of rectangles, this is no longer true. That is why the notion of eccentricity comes into play.

Our first main theorem on self-improving functionals is the following. We use here an oscillation with respect to the polynomial
P_{R}
defined in Section
3.2
, equation (
3.4
). We anticipate here that this object
P_{R}
is a projection to a space of polynomials of certain degree, and is the natural substitute for the average
f_{R}
when dealing with functionals involving higher derivatives. The weighted estimate will be valid for any weight in the Muckenhoupt class
w\in A_{\infty}:=\bigcup_{p>1}A_{p}
associated to the basis
\mathcal{R}
(this can be defined for any basis, see more details about this class in Section
3.3
).

Theorem 2.1
.

Let
w
be any
A_{\infty,\mathcal{R}}
weight in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
.
Consider also the functional
a
such that for some
p\geq 1
it satisfies the weighted condition
SD^{s}_{p,\mathcal{R}}(w)
from (
1.3
) with
s>0
and constant
\|a\|
.
Let
f
be a locally integrable function such that

(2.1)

\frac{1}{|R|}\int_{R}|f-P_{R}f|\leq a(R)\qquad R\in\mathcal{R}.

Then, there exists a dimensional constant
c_{n}
such that for any
R\in\mathcal{R}
,

(2.2)

\left(\frac{1}{w(R)}\int_{R}|f-P_{R}f|^{p}\,wdx\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}\leq c_{n}(1+s)\max\{\|a\|^{s},1\}\,a(R).

This result generalizes and improves on the corresponding result from
[
PR19
]
Theorem 1.24 where the linear constant
s
is of exponential type.

As consequences of this sort of general template for a self-improving theorem, we will obtain Poincaré-Sobolev inequalities for higher order derivatives with a rather precise control on the
A_{p,\mathcal{R}}
-like quantities involved.

Remark 2.2
.

We will often (but not always) use a compact notation for the weighted local
L^{q}
average over a rectangle
R
defined as follows:

\left\|f\right\|_{L^{q}\left(\frac{w\,dx}{w(R)}\right)}:=\left(\frac{1}{w(R)}\int_{R}|f|^{q}wdx\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}.

Similarly, we will use the standard notation for the normalized weak
(r,\infty)
(quasi)norm: for any
0<r<\infty
, measurable
E
and weight
w
, we define

\|f\|_{L^{r,\infty}\big(\frac{w\,dx}{w(E)}\big)}:=\sup_{t>0}t\,\left(\frac{1}{w(E)}w(\{x\in E:|f(x)|>t\})\right)^{1/r}.

The general Theorem
2.1
will be used to derive several corollaries. One of the most important consequences is that we will get Poincaré-Sobolev type inequalities involving higher derivatives instead of the gradient.

We also present here the following result, which can be seen as a weak norm version of the main theorem, since we are asking the functional to only satisfy the “rough”
D_{p}
condition (
1.5
) but obtaining a self-improving for the weak norm. It is a self-improving result as in
[
FPW98
]
but combined with the recent improvement obtained in
[
CP21
]
where linear bounds in both
[w]_{A_{\infty},\mathcal{R}}
and
p
are obtained instead of exponential.

Theorem 2.3
.

Let
w
be any
A_{\infty,\mathcal{R}}
weight in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
a\in D_{p,\mathcal{R}}(w)
. Let
f
be a locally integrable function such that,

\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$}}\kern-4.86108pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$}}\kern-3.43057pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.908pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.76045pt}}\!\int_{R}|f-P_{R}f|\leq a(R)\qquad R\in\mathcal{R}.

Then there exists a dimensional constant
c>0
such that for every
R\in\mathcal{R}
,

\big\|f-P_{R}f\big\|_{L^{p,\infty}\big(R,\frac{w\,dx}{w(R)}\big)}\leq c\,p\,[w]_{A_{\infty,\mathcal{R}}}\,\|a\|_{D_{p,\mathcal{R}}(w)}\,a(R).

This result is completely new and has a lot of potential applications. We will show some of them in next sections.

Recall that

\big\|g\big\|_{L^{q}(X)}\leq\left(\frac{p}{p-q}\right)^{\frac{{1}}{q}}\,\big\|g\big\|_{L^{p,\infty}(X)}

whenever
(X,\mu)
is a probability space, and
0<q<p<\infty
(sometimes called Kolmogorov’s inequality.) Then, under the assumption
of Theorem
2.3
, if
q<p
,

\big\|f-P_{R}f\big\|_{L^{q}\big(R,\frac{w\,dx}{w(R)}\big)}\leq c_{p,q}\,[w]_{A_{\infty,\mathcal{R}}}\,\|a\|_{D_{p,\mathcal{R}}(w)}\,a(R).

2.2.
Higher order Poincaré

As a consequence of our general result we will prove, using condition (
1.3
) as the key point, inequalities
for
R\in\mathcal{R}
of the form

\left(\frac{1}{w(R)}\int_{R}|f-P_{R}f|^{q}wdx\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}\leq C_{w}\,d(R)^{m}\left(\frac{1}{w(R)}\int_{R}|\nabla^{m}f|^{p}wdx\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}

where
C_{w}
is a constant and
P_{R}f
is an appropriate optimal polynomial of degree less than
m
, defined in Section
3.2
. The goal is to find the best possible improvement in the exponent
q
on the left hand side and precise estimates on
C_{w}
. We will refer to the above inequality as a higher order
(q,p)
-Poincaré-Sobolev weighted inequality.

A
(p,p)
version of the above inequality in the case of cubes with
p\geq 1
, was proved in
[
PR19
]
. Here we improve on that result achieving a higher Sobolev-type exponent on the left hand side and also valid for the family of rectangles
\mathcal{R}
. As usual, when proving weighted inequalities related to rectangles, the appropriate class of weights is the
strong
class defined analogously to the standard
A_{p}
class as follows. We will say that
w\in A_{p,\mathcal{R}}
if

[w]_{A_{p,\mathcal{R}}}:=\sup_{R\in\mathcal{R}}\left(\frac{1}{|R|}\int_{R}w(x)\,dx\right)\left(\frac{1}{|R|}\int_{R}w(x)^{-\frac{1}{p-1}}\,dx\right)^{p-1}<\infty.

The strong
A_{\infty,\mathcal{R}}
class is defined in the same way as in the cubic case and it enjoys the same geometric conditions (see Section
3.3
for the details) and the corresponding constant
[w]_{A_{\infty,\mathcal{R}}}
is defined in (
3.6
).

The following corollary of a
(p,p)
-Poincaré inequality (for cubes) was already obtained as a corollary of
[
PR19
, Theorem 1.24]
which is a weaker version of our Theorem
2.1
for the case of cubes.

Corollary 2.4

(
[
PR19
]
)
.

Let
p\geq 1
and let
w\in A_{p}
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Then the following
(p,p)
-Poincaré inequality holds

\left(\frac{1}{w(Q)}\int_{Q}|f-P_{Q}f|^{p}\,w\ dx\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}\leq C\,[w]^{\frac{1}{p}}_{A_{p}}\ell(Q)^{m}\left(\frac{1}{w(Q)}\int_{Q}|\nabla^{m}f|^{p}\,w\ dx\right)^{\frac{1}{p}},

where
C=C_{n,m}
is a structural constant and
\ell(Q)
is the sidelength of the cube
Q\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
.

The method of proof goes beyond Poincaré to reach Poincaré-Sobolev type inequalities with a higher exponent on the left hand side. In the following theorem the method seems to work best when the weight adds some degeneracy, which is a bit surprising. More precisely, we will say that a weight
w\in A_{\infty,\mathcal{R}}
is
nontrivial
whenever
[w]_{A_{\infty,\mathcal{R}}}>1
. The same notion for other bases of rectangles or cubes will be used as well.

Theorem 2.5
.

Let
1\leq p<n
and let
w\in A_{q,\mathcal{R}}
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a
nontrivial weight
with
1\leq q\leq p
. Let also
p_{w}^{*}
be defined by

(2.3)

\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{p_{w}^{*}}=\frac{\delta}{n}\frac{1}{q+\log[w]_{A_{q,\mathcal{R}}}}.

For a fixed
\delta>0
, let
a
be the functional defined by

(2.4)

a(R)=d(R)^{\delta}\left(\frac{1}{w(R)}\mu(R)\right)^{1/p},

where
\mu
is any Radon measure in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Suppose that
f
satisfies

(2.5)

\frac{1}{|R|}\int_{R}|f-P_{R}f|\leq a(R)

for every rectangle
R\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Then, there exists a dimensional constant
C_{n}
such that for any rectangle
R\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}

\left(\frac{1}{w(R)}\int_{R}|f-P_{R}f|^{p_{w}^{*}}\,wdx\right)^{\frac{1}{p_{w}^{*}}}\leq B_{w,q}\,C_{n}\frac{1}{\delta}a(R),

where
B_{w,q}=\frac{1+\log[w]_{A_{q,\mathcal{R}}}^{\frac{1}{q}}}{\log[w]_{A_{q,\mathcal{R}}}^{\frac{1}{q}}}
.

Note that the dependence on the weight can be disregarded if we are looking for the asymptotic behavior when
[w]_{{A_{q,\mathcal{R}}}}\to\infty
(we can bound that expression by 2 by considering weights such that
[w]_{{A_{q,\mathcal{R}}}}\geq e^{q}
). The real problem is with
flat weights
. We borrow that terminology from
[
PR18
]
to refer to weights with small
A_{\infty}
constant, close to 1. Those weights are, in some sense, getting closer to being just a multiple of the Lebesgue measure, and that is why this situation is counter intuitive, since one would expect an easier problem for this latter case. Also note that there is some sort of balance between the exponent
p^{*}_{w}
and the constant in the inequality. The closer the
A_{q}
constant gets to 1, the closer the value of
p^{*}_{w}
gets to the optimal Sobolev exponent. But it should also be observed that the constant in the inequality blows up when
[w]_{A_{q,\mathcal{R}}}\to 1
. And that is precisely the reason why this result can not be specialized on the limit case of the constant weight, but we can give a reasonable substitute by using weak norms.

Theorem 2.6
.

Let
1\leq p<n
and let
w\in A_{q,\mathcal{R}}
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
with
1\leq q\leq p
. If we define the exponent
p^{*}_{w}
as in (
2.3
), the functional
a
as in (
2.4
) and consider a function satisfying (
2.5
), there exists a dimensional constant
c_{n}
such that for any rectangle
R\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}

(2.6)

\left\|f-P_{R}f\right\|_{L^{p_{w}^{*},\infty}(R,\frac{wdx}{w(R)})}\leq\,c_{n}\,\frac{1}{\delta}a(R).

The idea is to use a specific choice of the functional
a
as the starting point. Since a
(1,1)
-Poincaré inequality involving higher derivatives is known to hold on rectangles, we obtain the following result.

Corollary 2.7
.

Let
1\leq p<n
and let
w\in A_{q,\mathcal{R}}
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
with
1\leq q\leq p
. Let
p_{w}^{*}
as in the previous theorem. Then the following inequality holds,

\left\|f-P_{R}f\right\|_{L^{p_{w}^{*}}\left(R,\frac{w\,dx}{w(R)}\right)}\leq B_{w,q}C_{n}\frac{1}{m}[w]_{A_{q,\mathcal{R}}}^{\frac{1}{p}}\,d(R)^{m}\left\|\nabla^{m}f\right\|_{L^{p}\left(R,\frac{w\,dx}{w(R)}\right)}

for every rectangle
R\in\mathcal{R}
, where as before,
B_{w,q}=\frac{1+\log[w]_{A_{q,\mathcal{R}}}^{\frac{1}{q}}}{\log[w]_{A_{q,\mathcal{R}}}^{\frac{1}{q}}}
.

We also have the estimate for the weak norm

(2.7)

\left\|f-P_{R}f\right\|_{L^{p_{w}^{*},\infty}(R,\frac{wdx}{w(R)})}\leq\,C_{n,p}\frac{1}{m}[w]_{A_{q,\mathcal{R}}}^{\frac{1}{p}}\,d(R)^{m}\left\|\nabla^{m}f\right\|_{L^{p}\left(R,\frac{w\,dx}{w(R)}\right)}.

The proof of this result can be found in Section
4
.

Remark 2.8
.

We remark
that for any nontrivial weight separated from the constant weight, say for instance

[w]_{{A_{q,\mathcal{R}}}}\geq e^{q}
, we can apply Theorem
2.5
to an appropriate starting point. Without this non-degeneracy condition, and in particular in the case of flat weights, we avoid the logarithmic blowup but at the cost of obtaining only a weak norm estimate. Of course, when
m=1
the truncation argument can be carried out to reach the strong norm from (
2.7
), to wit

\left\|f-P_{R}f\right\|_{L^{p_{w}^{*}}(R,\frac{wdx}{w(R)})}\leq\,c_{n}[w]_{A_{q,\mathcal{R}}}^{\frac{1}{p}}\,d(R)\left\|\nabla f\right\|_{L^{p}\left(R,\frac{w\,dx}{w(R)}\right)}.

2.3.
Fractional Poincaré-Sobolev and eccentricity.

This section is motivated by the fractional Poincaré-Sobolev inequalities (
1.6
). First we will introduce a fractional type Poincaré estimate adapted to the product space
\mathcal{R}
from which we will derive the corresponding Poincaré-Sobolev type result.

As before, we will introduce functionals satisfying the smallness preservation condition
SD_{p,\mathcal{R}}^{s}
for some choice of parameters
where the concept of eccentricity will play a central role in the proofs.
It comes from the natural interplay between the notion of diameter and measure for a given rectangle.

Definition 2.9
.

Let
R\in\mathcal{R}
be any rectangle in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. We define the eccentricity as the number

(2.8)

e(R):=\frac{|R|^{\frac{1}{n}}}{d(R)}.

The main property we use is the following observation: all the involved families of rectangles will be obtained from dyadic partitions of a fixed initial rectangle. Therefore, for each instance of this initial choice of a rectangle, all of them are dyadic children of a given rectangle. The main feature here is that all of them have the same eccentricity.

Lemma 2.10
.

Let
\tilde{R}
be any dyadic descendant of
R\in\mathcal{R}
. Then

e(\tilde{R})=e(R).

Indeed, if
j
be the dyadic level to which
\tilde{R}
belongs. Then,

(2.9)

e(\tilde{R})=\frac{|\tilde{R}|^{1/n}}{d(\tilde{R})}=\frac{(|R|2^{-jn})^{1/n}}{d(R)2^{-j}}=e(R).

By introducing this notion of eccentricity, we now are able to formulate other corollaries from our general self-improving Theorem
2.5
. We have the following intermediate estimate.

Corollary 2.11
.

Let
w\in A_{q,\mathcal{R}}
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
with
1\leq q\leq p<n
. Let
p_{w}^{*}
as in (
2.3
), namely

\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{p_{w}^{*}}=\frac{\delta}{n}\frac{1}{q+\log[w]_{A_{q,\mathcal{R}}}}.

Let
R\in\mathcal{R}
and

\delta>0
. Consider the function

(2.10)

A(R,x)=\int_{R}\frac{|f(x)-f(y)|^{p}}{|x-y|^{n+\delta p}}\,dy,\qquad\qquad x\in R.

Then the following inequality holds,

(2.11)

\left\|f-f_{R}\right\|_{L^{p_{w}^{*}}(R,\frac{wdx}{w(R)})}\leq C_{n,p,q}\frac{1}{\delta}\,[w]^{\frac{1}{p}}_{A_{p,\mathcal{R}}}\,\frac{d(R)^{\delta}}{e(R)^{\frac{n}{p}}}\left(\frac{1}{w(R)}\int_{R}A(R,\cdot)w\,dx\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}.

Remark 2.12
.

We will prove this result from an initial inequality (
1.2
) with a non standard functional
a(R)
involving the quantity
A(R,x)
, namely

\frac{1}{|R|}\int_{R}|f-f_{R}|\leq[w]^{\frac{1}{p}}_{A_{p,\mathcal{R}}}\,\frac{d(R)^{\delta}}{e(R)^{\frac{n}{p}}}\left(\frac{1}{w(R)}\int_{R}A(R,x)\,w(x)dx\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}.

Remark 2.13
.

There should be here a result as in Theorem
2.19
with the extra factor
(1-\delta)^{\frac{1}{p}}
at least in the case
w\in A_{1,\mathcal{R}}
. We don’t know how to do it. However, we proved the corresponding variation of this result in the context of
\mathfrak{R}
in Theorem
2.20
.

Note that, on the one hand the above starting point does not involve any derivative, but on the other hand it is imposing a somewhat stronger control over the
L^{1}
oscillation, since it is known that for any
0<\delta<1
, and any
p\geq 1
one has

\ell(Q)^{\delta}\left(\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$}}\kern-4.86108pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$}}\kern-3.43057pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.908pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.76045pt}}\!\int_{Q}\int_{Q}\frac{|f(x)-f(y)|^{p}}{|x-y|^{n+\delta p}}dydx\right)^{1/p}\lesssim C_{\delta,p,n}\ell(Q)\left(\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$}}\kern-4.86108pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$}}\kern-3.43057pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.908pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.76045pt}}\!\int_{Q}|\nabla f|^{p}\right)^{1/p}.

We will in fact provide the proof of an improved version of this inequality for the case
p=1
in Lemma
5.3
, inequality (
5.4
). The proof is included in
the Appendix
B
.

2.4.
A weaker starting point

The aim of this section is to show that we can replace the
L^{1}
mean oscillation in (
2.1
) as initial hypothesis by a weaker condition to derive the same results. Indeed, as shown in Theorems
2.1
and Theorem
2.5
, the natural starting point is given by the expression

(2.12)

\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$}}\kern-4.86108pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$}}\kern-3.43057pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.908pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.76045pt}}\!\int_{R}|f(x)-f_{R}|\,dx\leq a(R)

is a functional satisfying some sort of discrete summation condition. We show in next theorem that we can replace the

mean oscillation in (

)
by
L^{\delta}
,
0<\delta<1
weakening the initial assumption. This idea was already considered in

[
LP02
]
and
[
LP05
]
but the results and the methods are not so precise as the ones we obtain here within a different context.

Theorem 2.14
.

Let
f
be a measurable function and let
\delta\in(0,1)
. Suppose that there is a functional
a
such that,

(2.13)

\inf_{c\in\mathbb{R}}\left(\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$}}\kern-4.86108pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$}}\kern-3.43057pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.908pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.76045pt}}\!\int_{R}|f(x)-c|^{\delta}\right)^{\frac{1}{\delta}}dx\leq a(R)\qquad R\in\mathcal{R}.

satisfies the

condition for some

or the

condition for some

, then there is a self-improving to

(2.14)

\inf_{c\in\mathbb{R}}\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$}}\kern-4.86108pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$}}\kern-3.43057pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.908pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.76045pt}}\!\int_{R}|f(x)-c|dx\leq C\,a(R),\qquad R\in\mathcal{R},

where

depends on

.

Remark 2.15
.

This is also very interesting even in the case of
BMO
. In that case
a(R)=1
and the question can be phrased in terms of minimal conditions on the starting estimate (
2.13
) to conclude the membership to
BMO
. An approach to this problem (in the context of cubes) using Bellman functions can be found in

]
. However, this result has been improved in several ways in the more recent work
[

. It may be possible to adapt these ideas to the context of the current paper.

2.5.
Analysis in
\mathfrak{R}

We will show that we can apply our techniques to obtain other type of results for the class
\mathfrak{R}
which denotes the family formed by rectangles
R
of the form
R=I_{1}\times I_{2}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
where
I_{1}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n_{1}}
and
I_{2}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n_{2}}
are cubes with sides parallel to the coordinate axes. We will discuss different possibilities for the starting inequality (
1.2
)
which will lead to different results on Poincaré-Sobolev type inequalities more in the spirit of

[
ST93
,
LW98
]
.

The first results in the product case context were obtained by
X. L. Shi and A. Torchinsky in
[
ST93
]
. They obtained
(q,p)
-Poincaré-Sobolev inequalities with weights satisfying certain strong conditions. Later on, Seng-Kee Chua
[
Chu95
]
obtained some results assuming mixed norm assumptions on the gradient which we will not consider. Later on, G. Lu and R. Wheeden also derived in
[
LW98
]
Poincaré-Sobolev
inequalities in the context of vector fields. We improve these results in the classical context. This case could be certainly treated as a particular case of the general theory developed for the family
\mathcal{R}
, since the selfimproving method works in the same way. But here we will consider different starting points, better adapted to this particular geometry.

We will use the following notation: for a given function
f:U\to\mathbb{R}
defined on the open set
U\subset\mathbb{R}^{n_{1}}\times\mathbb{R}^{n_{2}}
, we will write
f(x)=f(x_{1},x_{2})
where
x_{1}
stands for the first
n_{1}
variables and
x_{2}
stands for the remaining
n_{2}
variables.

\nabla_{1}f
will denote the partial gradient of
f
containing the
x_{1}
-derivatives and
similarly
\nabla_{2}f
will denote the partial gradient of
f
containing the
x_{2}
-derivatives.
Recall that the family
\mathfrak{R}
is defined by rectangles of the form
R=I_{1}\times I_{2}
where in
I_{1}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n_{1}}
and
I_{2}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n_{2}}
are cubes with sides parallel to the coordinate axes and
n:=n_{1}+n_{2}
. As already mentioned, the classical method to derive these type of results is based on finding a representation formula and this is the way was done in the first paper in this context
[
ST93
]
. We follow a completely different path using some of the key ideas from
[
FPW98
]
.

Theorem 2.16
.

Let
w\in A_{q,\mathfrak{R}}
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
1\leq q\leq p.

Let also

\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{p^{*}}=\frac{1}{n}\frac{1}{(q+\log[w]_{A_{q,\mathfrak{R}}})}.

Then, there exists a constant
c=c(n,p,q)>0
such that for every Lipschitz function
f
and any
R=I_{1}\times I_{2}\in\mathfrak{R}

\left\|f-f_{R}\right\|_{L^{p^{*}}(R,\frac{wdx}{w(R)})}\leq c\,[w]_{A_{p,\mathfrak{R}}}^{\frac{1}{p}}\left(a_{1}(R)+a_{2}(R)\right),

where

a_{1}(R)=\ell(I_{1})\left\|\nabla_{1}f\right\|_{L^{p}(R,\frac{wdx}{w(R)})}\quad\text{ and }\quad a_{2}(R)=\ell(I_{2})\left\|\nabla_{2}f\right\|_{L^{p}(R,\frac{wdx}{w(R)})}.

This result will be proved in Section
6
.
As far as we know these results are not known in the literature (for instance
[
Chu95
,
LW98
]
) even for the case
p>1
which is usually simpler.
We will show that this theorem
will follow essentially from the following
(1,1)
-Poincaré inequality in product spaces
which seems to be unknown.

Lemma 2.17
.

There exists a dimensional constant
c>0
such that for every Lipschitz function

\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$}}\kern-4.86108pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$}}\kern-3.43057pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.908pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.76045pt}}\!\int_{R}|f-f_{R}|\leq c\,\ell(I_{1})\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$}}\kern-4.86108pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$}}\kern-3.43057pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.908pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.76045pt}}\!\int_{R}|\nabla_{1}f|+c\,\ell(I_{2})\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$}}\kern-4.86108pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$}}\kern-3.43057pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.908pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.76045pt}}\!\int_{R}|\nabla_{2}f|.

Inequality (

\left(\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$}}\kern-4.86108pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$}}\kern-3.43057pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.908pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.76045pt}}\!\int_{R}|f-f_{R}|^{p_{\delta,w}^{*}}\,wdx\right)^{\frac{1}{p_{\delta,w}^{*}}}\leq c\,[w]_{A_{1,\mathfrak{R}}}^{\frac{1}{p}}\,(1-\delta)^{\frac{1}{p}}\Big(a_{1}(R)+a_{2}(R)\Big)

a_{1}(R)=\left(\frac{\ell(I_{1})^{p\delta}}{w(R)}\int_{R}\int_{I_{1}}\frac{|f(x_{1},x_{2})-f(y_{1},x_{2})|^{p}}{|x_{1}-y_{1}|^{n_{1}+p\delta}}\,w(x_{1},x_{2})dx_{1}dx_{2}\ dy_{1}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}

and

a_{2}(R)=\left(\frac{\ell(I_{2})^{p\delta}}{w(R)}\int_{R}\int_{I_{2}}\frac{|f(y_{1},x_{2})-f(y_{1},y_{2})|^{p}}{|x_{2}-y_{2}|^{n_{2}+p\delta}}\,w(y_{1},y_{2})\,dy_{1}dy_{2}\ dx_{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}.

This result is completely new and the proof can be found in Section
7
.
An interesting question here is to extend this result beyond
A_{1,\mathfrak{R}}
, for instance to the
A_{p,\mathfrak{R}}
case which is not known even in the one parameter situation, Theorem
2.20

We include here some well known preliminaries and some needed lemmas and further properties.
Throughout this section, the ambient space will be

3.1.
Smallness preserving functionals

We start with some model examples of functionals mentioned in the statements of our results. As we will see, the constant eccentricity is crucial.

Example 3.1
.

Consider the functional defined by

(3.1)

a(R)=d(R)^{\delta}\left(\frac{1}{w(R)}\int_{R}A(R,x)\,dx\right)^{1/p}

where
A(R,x)
is nonnegative and increasing in
R
. That is,
R_{1}\subset R_{2}
implies that
A(R_{1},x)\leq A(R_{2},x)
. We will verify that this functional satisfies Definition
1.1
, more precisely we will check that

a\in SD^{n/\delta}_{p,\mathcal{R}}(w)
for a weight
w
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. More importantly, that
\|a\|_{SD^{n/\delta}_{p,\mathcal{R}}(w)}=1
. Consider
q>1
such that
\frac{\delta pq}{n}>1
. Then by the key eccentricity property in Lemma
2.10

\displaystyle\sum_{i}a(R_{i})^{p}w(R_{i})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{i}d(R_{i})^{\delta p}\int_{R_{i}}A(R_{i},x)\,dx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{e(R)^{\delta p}}\sum_{i}|R_{i}|^{p\delta/n}\int_{R_{i}}A(R_{i},x)\,dx

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{e(R)^{\delta p}}\left(\sum_{i}|R_{i}|^{\frac{\delta pq}{n}}\right)^{1/q}\left(\sum_{i}\left[\int_{R_{i}}A(R_{i},x)\,dx\right]^{q^{\prime}}\right)^{\frac{1}{q^{\prime}}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{e(R)^{\delta p}}\left(\sum_{i}|R_{i}|\right)^{\frac{\delta p}{n}}\int_{R}A(R,x)\,dx.

Now, the eccentricity definition gives the desired bound,

\displaystyle\sum_{i}a(R_{i})^{p}w(R_{i})

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{|R|^{\frac{\delta p}{n}}}{e(R)^{\delta p}}\left(\frac{|\bigcup_{i}R_{i}|}{|R|}\right)^{\frac{\delta p}{n}}\,\int_{R}A(R,x)\,dx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle d(R)^{\delta p}\,\left(\frac{|\bigcup_{i}R_{i}|}{|R|}\right)^{\frac{\delta p}{n}}\int_{R}A(R,x)\,dx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\frac{|\bigcup_{i}R_{i}|}{|R|}\right)^{\frac{\delta p}{n}}\,a(R)^{p}w(R).

Example 3.2
.

The example above belongs to a rather wide family of functionals given by averages of general measures:
a(R)=d(R)^{\delta}\left(\frac{\mu(R)}{w(R)}\right)^{1/p}
,
\delta>0
. The previous computation shows that
a\in SD_{p,\mathcal{R}}^{n/\delta}(w)
for any weight
w
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}.

The above estimates are sufficient to obtain
(p,p)
-Poincaré inequalities since we have that
\|a\|_{SD^{n/\delta}_{p,\mathcal{R}}(w)}\leq 1
. In order to reach higher exponents of integrability on the left hand side and get a Poincaré-Sobolev type inequality, we need to pay a price allowing
\|a\|_{SD_{p,\mathcal{R}}^{s}(w)}
to be larger than 1. A sort of natural way to do this is to consider the involved weight to be in a “better”
A_{q,\mathcal{R}}
class. We follow the line of ideas from
[
PR19
]
adapted to our setting of rectangles.

Lemma 3.3
.

Consider two indices
p,q
such that
1\leq q\leq p<n
and let
w\in A_{q,\mathcal{R}}
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. For
\delta>0
, consider the functional

a(R)=d(R)^{\delta}\left(\frac{1}{w(R)}\mu(R)\right)^{1/p}\qquad R\in\mathcal{R}.

For
M>1
we define
p_{M}^{*}:=p(n,q,M)
by the condition

\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{p_{M}^{*}}=\frac{\delta}{nqM}.

Then
a
satisfies Definition
1.1
with parameters
p=p^{*}_{M}
and
s=\frac{nM^{\prime}}{\delta}
, namely if
R\in\mathcal{R}
and
\{R_{i}\}_{i}
is any family of pairwise disjoint dyadic subrectangles of
R
the following inequality holds:

(3.2)

\left(\sum_{i}a(R_{i})^{p^{*}_{M}}\,\frac{w(R_{i})}{w(R)}\right)^{\frac{1}{p^{*}_{M}}}\leq[w]_{A_{q,\mathcal{R}}}^{\frac{\delta}{nqM}}\,\left(\frac{|\bigcup_{i}R_{i}|}{|R|}\right)^{\frac{\delta}{nM^{\prime}}}a(R).

Note that
p_{M}^{*}
is smaller than the classical Sobolev exponent, namely the sharp one corresponding to the Lebsegue measure case:

p<p_{M}^{*}<p^{*}=\frac{pn}{n-p}\qquad 1\leq p<n.

This condition says that the functional
a
“preserves smallness” for the exponent
p_{M}^{*}
defined in (
2.3
) with index
s=\frac{nM^{\prime}}{\delta}
and constant

\|a\|_{SD_{p^{*}_{M},\mathcal{R}}^{\frac{nM^{\prime}}{\delta}}(w)}\leq[w]_{A_{q,\mathcal{R}}}^{\frac{\delta}{nqM}}.

That is,
a\in SD_{p^{*}_{M},\mathcal{R}}^{\frac{nM^{\prime}}{\delta}}(w)
. The notation here is somewhat involved, but we will use it in a more simplified manner in our applications.
Recall that, as in the case of standard cubic
A_{q}
weights, the strong
A_{q,\mathcal{R}}
weights satisfy the geometric estimate

\left(\frac{|E|}{|R|}\right)^{q}\leq[w]_{A_{q,\mathcal{R}}}\frac{w(E)}{w(R)},

valid for any subset
E\subset R
and any
R\in\mathcal{R}
.
Now we can proceed to the proof of the main estimate for the functional
a
.

Proof of Lemma
3.3
.

Let
M>1
. For simplicity in the exposition, we will omit the subindex
M
and just use
p^{*}
instead of
p^{*}_{M}
. To verify the smallness preservation for the functional
a
, we compute, using again the key eccentricity property in Lemma
2.10
,

\displaystyle\sum_{i}a(R_{i})^{p^{*}}w(R_{i})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{i}\mu(R_{i})^{\frac{p^{*}}{p}}\left(\frac{d(R_{i})^{\delta}}{w(R_{i})^{\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{p^{*}}}}\right)^{p^{*}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{e(R)^{\delta p^{*}}}\sum_{i}\mu(R_{i})^{\frac{p^{*}}{p}}\left(\frac{|R_{i}|^{\frac{\delta}{n}}}{w(R_{i})^{\frac{\delta}{qnM}}}\right)^{p^{*}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{e(R)^{\delta p^{*}}}\sum_{i}\mu(R_{i})^{\frac{p^{*}}{p}}\left(\frac{|R_{i}|}{w(R_{i})^{\frac{1}{qM}}}\right)^{\frac{\delta p^{*}}{n}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{[w]_{A_{q,\mathcal{R}}}^{\frac{\delta p^{*}}{nqM}}}{e(R)^{\delta p^{*}}}\left(\frac{|R|^{q}}{w(R)}\right)^{\frac{\delta p^{*}}{nqM}}\ \sum_{i}\mu(R_{i})^{\frac{p^{*}}{p}}|R_{i}|^{\frac{\delta p^{*}}{nM^{\prime}}}.

Now consider any
t>1
such that
t\frac{\delta p^{*}}{nM^{\prime}}\geq 1
and apply Hölder’s inequality to the sum above to obtain

\displaystyle\sum_{i}\mu(R_{i})^{\frac{p^{*}}{p}}|R_{i}|^{\frac{\delta p^{*}}{nM^{\prime}}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left(\sum_{i}\mu(R_{i})^{\frac{t^{\prime}p^{*}}{p}}\right)^{\frac{1}{t^{\prime}}}\left(\sum_{i}|R_{i}|\right)^{\frac{\delta p^{*}}{nM^{\prime}}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left(\sum_{i}\mu(R_{i})\right)^{\frac{p^{*}}{p}}\,\left(\frac{|\bigcup_{i}R_{i}|}{|R|}\right)^{\frac{\delta p^{*}}{nM^{\prime}}}|R|^{\frac{\delta p^{*}}{nM^{\prime}}}.

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\mu(R)^{\frac{p^{*}}{p}}\,\left(\frac{|\bigcup_{i}R_{i}|}{|R|}\right)^{\frac{\delta p^{*}}{nM^{\prime}}}|R|^{\frac{\delta p^{*}}{nM^{\prime}}}.

Therefore,

\displaystyle\sum_{i}a(R_{i})^{p^{*}}w(R_{i})

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left(\frac{[w]_{A_{q,\mathcal{R}}}|R|^{q}}{w(R)}\right)^{\frac{\delta p^{*}}{nqM}}\mu(R)^{\frac{p^{*}}{p}}\,\frac{|R|^{\frac{\delta p^{*}}{nM^{\prime}}}}{e(R)^{\delta p^{*}}}\,\left(\frac{|\bigcup_{i}R_{i}|}{|R|}\right)^{\frac{\delta p^{*}}{nM^{\prime}}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle[w]_{A_{q,\mathcal{R}}}^{\frac{\delta p^{*}}{nqM}}\frac{|R|^{\frac{\delta p^{*}}{n}}}{w(R)^{\frac{\delta p^{*}}{nqM}}}\mu(R)^{\frac{p^{*}}{p}}\frac{1}{e(R)^{\delta p^{*}}}\,\,\left(\frac{|\bigcup_{i}R_{i}|}{|R|}\right)^{\frac{\delta p^{*}}{nM^{\prime}}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle[w]_{A_{q,\mathcal{R}}}^{\frac{\delta p^{*}}{nqM}}a(R)^{p^{*}}w(R)\,\left(\frac{|\bigcup_{i}R_{i}|}{|R|}\right)^{\frac{\delta p^{*}}{nM^{\prime}}}.

∎

3.2.
Optimal polynomials

We also include here some preliminaries on optimal polynomials.
Given a rectangle
R\in\mathcal{R}
and an integer
m\geq 0
, we consider the space
\mathcal{P}_{m}
of polynomials of degree at most
m
in
n
variables endowed with the inner product given by

<f,g>_{R}:=\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$}}\kern-4.86108pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$}}\kern-3.43057pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.908pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.76045pt}}\!\int_{R}fgdx.

There is an orthonormal basis with respect to this inner product that we will denote by
\{\phi_{\alpha}\}
, being
\alpha=(\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{n})
a multiindex of non negative integers such that
|\alpha|=\alpha_{1}+\cdots+\alpha_{n}\leq m
. An important feature is that

(3.3)

\|\phi_{r}\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq C\left(\frac{1}{|R|}\int_{R}|\phi_{r}|^{2}dx\right)^{1/2}=C,

since the space
\mathcal{P}_{m}
is finite dimensional and therefore all norms are equivalent. Let
P_{R}
the projection defined by the formula

(3.4)

P_{R}(f)=\sum_{r}\left(\frac{1}{|R|}\int_{R}f\phi_{r}dx\right)\phi_{r}.

We clearly have from (
3.3
) that

(3.5)

\|P_{R}f\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq NC^{2}\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$}}\kern-4.86108pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$}}\kern-3.43057pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.908pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.76045pt}}\!\int_{R}|f|,

where
N
depends on
m
. Moreover, as it is the case when
m=0
and the projection is over the constants, we have the following optimality property:

\inf_{\pi\in\mathcal{P}_{m}}\left(\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$}}\kern-4.86108pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$}}\kern-3.43057pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.908pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.76045pt}}\!\int_{R}|f-\pi|^{p}\right)^{1/p}\approx\left(\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$}}\kern-4.86108pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$}}\kern-3.43057pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.908pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.76045pt}}\!\int_{R}|f-P_{R}f|^{p}\right)^{1/p}.

3.3.
A_{\infty,\mathcal{R}}
weights

We recall that the “strong”
A_{\infty,\mathcal{R}}
class of weights is usually defined as

A_{\infty,\mathcal{R}}=\bigcup_{p>1}A_{p,\mathcal{R}}.

There are several different characterizations of the belonging of a weight to this class. We choose here to work with the so called Fujii-Wilson constant defined as

(3.6)

[w]_{A_{\infty,\mathcal{R}}}:=\sup_{R\in\mathcal{R}}\frac{1}{w(R)}\int_{R}M_{s}(w\chi_{R})\ dx<\infty.

Although implicit in
[
Fuj78
]
, this definition was initially considered in
[
HP13
]
and used to derive a quite sharp Reverse Hölder Inequality (RHI) estimate.
We cite here an improved version from
[
HPR12
]
, which is similar to the one obtained
[
LPR17
]
in the context of rectangles. The proof is an easy adaptation from cubes to rectangles and therefore will be omitted.

Theorem 3.4
.

Let
w\in A_{\infty,\mathcal{R}}
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Then for any rectangle
R\in\mathcal{R}
,

\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$}}\kern-4.86108pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$}}\kern-3.43057pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.908pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.76045pt}}\!\int_{R}w^{1+\varepsilon}\ dx\leq 2\left(\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$}}\kern-4.86108pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$}}\kern-3.43057pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.908pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.76045pt}}\!\int_{R}w\ dx\right)^{1+\varepsilon},

for any
\varepsilon>0
such that
0<\varepsilon\leq\frac{1}{2^{n+1}[w]_{A_{\infty,\mathcal{R}}}-1}
.

In addition, we will introduce the following notation for

We will provide here the proof of our main general result Theorem

. Before to proceed with the proof we recall that what we need to prove is that the quantity

X=\sup_{R\in\mathcal{R}}\left(\frac{1}{w(R)}\int_{R}\left|\frac{f-P_{R}f}{a(R)}\right|^{p}\,wdx\right)^{1/p}

is bounded with a precise control on the bound. A brief comment on a technical step in the proof is necessary here. Since the argument involves the trick of bounding

by some variation of itself, the partial step of proving that it is finite is crucial. In many standard situations, this can be achieved by assuming that the function
f
is bounded and also by perturbing the functional
a
to avoid any possible degeneracy considering
a_{\varepsilon}(R):=a(R)+\varepsilon
,

. Once a uniform bound involving

is obtained, a limiting argument proves that the original

is also finite. The problem is that the hypothesis on

satisfying a

condition is not shared by the perturbed new functional
a_{\varepsilon}
. However, some weaker variant is indeed true and it is sufficient for the proof. Here (and only here!) is where the

condition pops in, remarkably absent in the quantitative estimate. We will omit the technical details, we refer the reader to
[

]
for a detailed discussion.

Proof of Theorem
2.1
.

For the proof we follow the ideas from
[
PR19
]
, starting with a standard local C–Z decomposition
in the context of
\mathcal{R}
.

We consider the local C-Z decomposition of
\frac{|f-P_{R}f|}{a(R)}
relative to
R
at level
L
for a large universal constant
L>1
to be chosen. Let
\mathcal{D}(R)
be the family of dyadic subrectangles of
R
which also belong to
\mathcal{R}
. The C–Z decomposition yields a collection
\{R_{j}\}_{j}
of rectangles such that
R_{j}\in\mathcal{D}(R)
, maximal with respect to inclusion, satisfying

(4.3)

L<\frac{1}{|R_{j}|}\int_{R_{j}}\frac{|f-P_{R}f|}{{a(R)}}\,dy.

Then, if
R

R\supset R_{j}

(4.4)

\frac{1}{|R|}\int_{R}\frac{|f-P_{R}f|}{{a(R)}}\,dy\leq L

and hence

(4.5)

L<\frac{1}{|R_{j}|}\int_{R_{j}}\frac{|f-P_{R}f|}{{a(R)}}\,dy\leq L\,2^{n}

for each integer

. Also note that

\left\{x\in R:M_{R}^{d}\left(\frac{|f-P_{R}f|}{{a(R)}}\chi_{R}\right)(x)>L\right\}=\bigcup_{j}R_{j}=:\Omega_{L}

where

stands for the dyadic maximal function adapted to the rectangle
R
. That is,

M^{d}_{R}(f)(x):=\sup_{J\ni x}\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$}}\kern-4.86108pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$}}\kern-3.43057pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.908pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.76045pt}}\!\int_{J}|f(y)|\ dy\qquad x\in J,J\in\mathcal{D}(R).

Then, by the Lebesgue differentiation theorem it follows that

(4.6)

\frac{|f(x)-P_{R}f(x)|}{{a(R)}}\leq L\qquad a.e.\ x\notin\Omega_{L}.

Also, observe that by (
4.3
) (or the weak type
(1,1)
property of
M^{d}_{R}
) and recalling our starting assumption (
2.1
), we have that
\{R_{j}\}_{j}\in S(L)
, namely

|\Omega_{L}|=|\bigcup_{j}R_{j}|<\frac{|R|}{L}.

Now, given the C-Z decomposition of the cube
R
, we decompose the function
\dfrac{f-P_{R}f}{{a(R)}}
as

\displaystyle f-P_{R}f

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\hskip-2.84544pt\left(f-P_{R}f\right)\chi_{\Omega_{L}^{c}}+\sum_{j}\left(P_{R_{j}}f-P_{R}f\right)\chi_{R_{j}}+\sum_{j}\left(f-P_{R_{j}}f\right)\chi_{R_{j}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle A_{1}+A_{2}+A_{3}.

For
A_{1}
and
A_{2}
we have a pointwise estimate. The bound for
A_{1}
follows from (
4.6
).
Also note that
P_{R_{j}}P_{R}=P_{R}
and recall the bound (
3.5
). Then we can control

as

\displaystyle|A_{2}(x)|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sum_{j}\left|(P_{R_{j}}f(x)-P_{R}f(x))\right|\chi_{R_{j}}(x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{j}\left|P_{R_{j}}(f-P_{R}f)\right(x)|\chi_{R_{j}}(x)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sum_{j}NC^{2}\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$}}\kern-4.86108pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$}}\kern-3.43057pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.908pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.76045pt}}\!\int_{R_{j}}|f-P_{R}f|\chi_{R_{j}}(x)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle NC^{2}2^{n}La(R)

since for any given
x\in\Omega
there is only one
j
such that
x\in R_{j}
. So far we have pointwise bounds for
A_{1}
and
A_{2}
. Now we move on to the desired
L^{p}
norm estimate. Consider the local weighted
L^{p}
space on
R
with the measure
\mu:=\frac{w(x)dx}{w(R)}
. By the triangle inequality, we have that

\displaystyle\left\|\frac{f-P_{R}f}{a(R)}\right\|_{L^{p}_{\mu}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left\|\frac{A_{1}}{a(R)}\right\|_{L^{p}_{\mu}}+\left\|\frac{A_{2}}{a(R)}\right\|_{L^{p}_{\mu}}+\left\|\sum_{j}\frac{f-P_{R_{j}}f}{a(R)}\chi_{R_{j}}\right\|_{L^{p}_{\mu}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\tilde{c}L+\left(\frac{1}{w(R)}\int_{R}\sum_{j}\frac{|f-P_{R_{j}}f|^{p}}{a(R)^{p}}\chi_{R_{j}}wdx\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}.

Since the
R_{j}
’s are disjoint, we can compute

\displaystyle\int_{R}\sum_{j}\frac{|f-P_{R_{j}}f|^{p}}{a(R)^{p}}\chi_{R_{j}}wdx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{j}\frac{a(R_{j})^{p}w(R_{j})}{a(R)^{p}w(R_{j})}\int_{R_{j}}\frac{|f-P_{R_{j}}f|^{p}}{a(R_{j})^{p}}wdx

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle X^{p}\sum_{j}\frac{a(R_{j})^{p}w(R_{j})}{a(R)^{p}},

where
X
is the quantity defined as

X=\sup_{R\in\mathcal{R}}\left(\frac{1}{w(R)}\int_{R}\frac{|f-P_{R}f|^{p}}{a(R)^{p}}wdx\right)^{1/p}.

Averaging over
w(R)
and raising to the
1/p
power, we obtain

\displaystyle\left(\frac{1}{w(R)}\int_{R}\left|\frac{f-P_{R}f}{a(R)}\right|^{p}\,wdx\right)^{1/p}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\tilde{c}L+X\left(\sum_{j}\frac{a(R_{j})^{p}w(R_{j})}{a(R)^{p}w(R)}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\tilde{c}L+X\frac{\|a\|}{L^{1/s}}.

Taking the supremum over all
R\in\mathcal{R}
, we finally obtain

(4.7)

X\leq\tilde{c}L+X\frac{\|a\|}{L^{1/s}},

for any
L>1
.
Choosing here an appropriate
L
as
L=e\max\{\|a\|^{s},1\}
and using the elementary fact that
\left(e^{1/s}\right)^{\prime}\leq 1+s
, we finally get that, for any
R\in\mathcal{R}
,

\left(\frac{1}{w(R)}\int_{R}\left|f-P_{R}f\right|^{p}\,wdx\right)^{1/p}\leq c_{n}(1+s)\max\{\|a\|^{s},1\}\,a(R),

which is the desired estimate.
∎

Now we are ready to prove the consequences that can be derived from our general theorem. We start by proving Theorem
2.5
, where we exploit the membership of the weight
w
to a better
A_{q,\mathcal{R}}
class to reach a higher Poincaré-Sobolev exponent.

Proof of Theorem
2.5
.

Recall that the functional from (
2.4
) satisfies a
SD_{p,\mathcal{R}}^{n/\delta}
condition with norm 1 (see Example
3.2
). This is sufficient to prove
(p,p)
-Poincaré inequalities by using Theorem
2.1
. Here, in order to achieve a higher integrability,
the problem reduces to prove a better smallness preservation condition that comes with a price: the norm with respect to the better parameters is no longer uniformly bounded on the weights. Hence, a careful optimization has to be done.
We will use the main estimate from Lemma
3.3
, choosing
M>1
with
s=\frac{nM^{\prime}}{\delta}
, namely

(4.8)

\|a\|:=\|a\|_{SD_{p^{*}_{M},\mathcal{R}}^{\frac{nM^{\prime}}{\delta}}(w)}\leq[w]_{A_{q,\mathcal{R}}}^{\frac{\delta}{nqM}}

where
\displaystyle\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{p_{M}^{*}}=\frac{\delta}{nqM}.
The choice will depend on
[w]_{A_{q,\mathcal{R}}}
so that the expression
s\|a\|^{s}
remains bounded. We choose

(4.9)

M=1+\log[w]_{A_{q,\mathcal{R}}}^{\frac{1}{q}}.

Observe that both
M
and
M^{\prime}=\frac{1+\log[w]_{A_{q,\mathcal{R}}}^{\frac{1}{q}}}{\log[w]_{A_{q,\mathcal{R}}}^{\frac{1}{q}}}>1
and also that this choice of
M
implies that the exponent
p^{*}_{M}
is exactly the value
p^{*}_{w}
from (
2.3
).
Hence applying Theorem
2.1
with
p
replaced by
p^{*}_{w}
and using estimate (
4.8
) we obtain

\displaystyle\left(\frac{1}{w(R)}\int_{R}|f-P_{R}f|^{p_{w}^{*}}\,wdx\right)^{\frac{1}{p_{w}^{*}}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C_{n}s\,\|a\|^{s}\,a(R)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C_{n}\frac{M^{\prime}}{\delta}[w]_{A_{q,\mathcal{R}}}^{\frac{\delta}{nqM}\frac{nM^{\prime}}{\delta}}\,a(R)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C_{n}\frac{M^{\prime}}{\delta}[w]_{A_{q,\mathcal{R}}}^{\frac{1}{\log[w]_{A_{q,\mathcal{R}}}}}\,a(R)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle c_{n}\frac{1+\log[w]_{A_{q,\mathcal{R}}}^{\frac{1}{q}}}{\log[w]_{A_{q,\mathcal{R}}}^{\frac{1}{q}}}\frac{1}{\delta}\,a(R).

This yields the proof of Theorem
2.5
providing a good estimate for weights with large nontrivial
A_{q,\mathcal{R}}
constants. A simple computation shows that for any weight such that
[w]_{A_{q,\mathcal{R}}}>1
, if we choose
K
large such that
[w]_{A_{q,\mathcal{R}}}=e^{\frac{q}{K}}
, then we obtain a cleaner inequality

\left(\frac{1}{w(R)}\int_{R}|f-f_{R}|^{p_{w}^{*}}\,wdx\right)^{\frac{1}{p_{w}^{*}}}\leq c_{n}(K+1)\frac{1}{\delta}\,a(R).

∎

Now we present the proof of Theorem
2.6
covering, in particular, the case of flat weights.

Proof of Theorem
2.6
.

We can assume that the weight
w
satisfies
[w]_{A_{q},\mathcal{R}}\leq e^{q}
, since in the other case we simply use the previous Theorem
2.5
.
We claim that for any family
\{R_{i}\}_{i}
of pairwise disjoint dyadic subrectangles of
R
, the following inequality holds:

(4.10)

\left(\sum_{i}a(R_{i})^{p^{*}_{1}}\,\frac{w(R_{i})}{w(R)}\right)^{\frac{1}{p^{*}_{1}}}\leq e^{\frac{\delta}{n}}\,a(R),

recalling the notation
\displaystyle\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{p_{1}^{*}}=\frac{\delta}{n}\frac{1}{q}
. This means that
a\in D_{p^{*}_{1},\mathcal{R}}(w)

with
\|a\|_{D_{p^{*}_{1},\mathcal{R}}(w)}\leq e^{\frac{\delta}{n}}
according to Definition
1.3
. Let’s postpone the proof of the claim for the time being. Now, applying Theorem
2.3
, we get

\displaystyle\|f-f_{R}\|_{L^{p^{*}_{1},\infty}\big(R,\frac{w\,dx}{w(R)}\big)}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle c\,p^{*}_{1}\,[w]_{A_{\infty,\mathcal{R}}}\,\|a\|_{D_{p^{*}_{1},\mathcal{R}}(w)}\,a(R)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle c\,p^{*}_{1}\,[w]_{A_{q,\mathcal{R}}}\,\|a\|_{D_{p^{*}_{1},\mathcal{R}}(w)}\,a(R)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle c\,p^{*}_{1}\,e^{q}\,e^{\frac{\delta}{n}}\,a(R).

Consider here the same choice of
M=1+\log[w]^{\frac{1}{q}}_{A_{q,\mathcal{R}}}
as in the proof of Theorem
2.5
above. Since
p^{*}_{1}>p^{*}_{M}
(just note that
p^{*}_{M}
is a decreasing function on
M
), by Jensen’s inequality which holds
at weak level (simply use that the inner part of what is inside
()^{\frac{1}{p}}
is less or equal than one) we have,

\displaystyle\|f-f_{R}\|_{L^{p^{*}_{M},\infty}\big(R,\frac{w\,dx}{w(R)}\big)}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\|f-f_{R}\|_{L^{p^{*}_{1},\infty}\big(R,\frac{w\,dx}{w(R)}\big)}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle c\,p^{*}_{1}\,e^{q}\,e^{\frac{\delta}{n}}\,a(R).

This is the desired weak type estimate. To conclude the proof of the theorem we need to check claim (
4.10
). But this is a variant of the proof of Lemma
3.3
, indeed by the key eccentricity property in Lemma
2.10
,

\displaystyle\sum_{i}a(R_{i})^{p^{*}_{1}}w(R_{i})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{i}\mu(R_{i})^{\frac{p^{*}_{1}}{p}}\left(\frac{d(R_{i})^{\delta}}{w(R_{i})^{\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{p^{*}_{1}}}}\right)^{p^{*}_{1}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{e(R)^{\delta p^{*}_{1}}}\sum_{i}\mu(R_{i})^{\frac{p^{*}_{1}}{p}}\left(\frac{|R_{i}|^{\frac{\delta}{n}}}{w(R_{i})^{\frac{\delta}{qn}}}\right)^{p^{*}_{1}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{e(R)^{\delta p^{*}_{1}}}\sum_{i}\mu(R_{i})^{\frac{p^{*}_{1}}{p}}\left(\frac{|R_{i}|}{w(R_{i})^{\frac{1}{q}}}\right)^{\frac{\delta p^{*}_{1}}{n}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{e(R)^{\delta p^{*}_{1}}}\sum_{i}\mu(R_{i})^{\frac{p^{*}_{1}}{p}}\left([w]_{A_{q,\mathcal{R}}}^{\frac{1}{q}}\frac{|R|}{w(R)^{\frac{1}{q}}}\right)^{\frac{\delta p^{*}_{1}}{n}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{e^{\frac{\delta p^{*}_{1}}{n}}}{e(R)^{\delta p^{*}_{1}}}\left(\frac{|R|^{q}}{w(R)}\right)^{\frac{\delta p^{*}_{1}}{nq}}\ \sum_{i}\mu(R_{i})^{\frac{p^{*}_{1}}{p}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{e^{\frac{\delta p^{*}_{1}}{n}}}{e(R)^{\delta p^{*}_{1}}}\left(\frac{|R|^{q}}{w(R)}\right)^{\frac{\delta p^{*}_{1}}{nq}}\ \mu(R)^{\frac{p^{*}_{1}}{p}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle e^{\frac{\delta p^{*}_{1}}{n}}a(R)^{p_{1}^{*}}w(R)

since we assumed that
[w]_{A_{q},\mathcal{R}}\leq e^{q}
. This gives the claim (
4.10
) finishing the proof of the theorem.
∎

Proof of Corollary
2.7
.

In order to apply our self improving method from Theorem
2.5
or Theorem
2.6
, we need a starting point as (
2.5
) where the functional
a
is of the form (
2.4
). Regarding the starting point, we know from the work of Chua in
[

, Lemma 2.5]
that the following unweighted inequality holds
1
1

1

The results in
[

]

\frac{1}{|R|}\int_{R}\mbox{$|f-P_{R}f|$}\,dx\leq C\,\frac{d(R)^{m}}{|R|}\int_{R}\mbox{$|\nabla^{m}f|$}\,dx,

(4.11)

\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$}}\kern-4.86108pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$}}\kern-3.43057pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.908pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.76045pt}}\!\int_{R}\mbox{$|f-P_{R}f|$}\,dx\leq C\,[w]^{1/p}_{A_{p,\mathcal{R}}}d(R)^{m}\left(\frac{1}{w(R)}\int_{R}\mbox{$|\nabla^{m}f|$}^{p}w\,dx\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}.

∎

Now we move on to prove Corollary

.

Proof of Corollary
2.11
.

Again, the key resides in proving a suitable starting point.
Consider, for
\delta>0
, the functional defined by the expression

a(R):=[w]^{\frac{1}{p}}_{A_{p,\mathcal{R}}}\frac{d(R)^{\delta}}{e(R)^{\frac{n}{p}}}\left(\frac{1}{w(R)}\int_{R}A(R,x)\,w(x)dx\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}

where

(4.12)

A(R,x)=\int_{R}\frac{|f(x)-f(y)|^{p}}{|x-y|^{\delta p+n}}\,w(y)dy.

Note that by Hölder’s inequality combined with the definition, again, of the

condition together with the concept of eccentricity we obtain

\displaystyle\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$}}\kern-4.86108pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$}}\kern-3.43057pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.908pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.76045pt}}\!\int_{R}|f-f_{R}|

\displaystyle\frac{1}{|R|}\int_{R}\frac{1}{|R|}\int_{R}|f(x)-f(y)|\,dy\,dx

\displaystyle[w]^{\frac{1}{p}}_{A_{p,\mathcal{R}}}\left(\frac{1}{w(R)|R|}\int_{R}\int_{R}|f(x)-f(y)|^{p}\,dy\,w(x)dx\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}

\displaystyle[w]^{\frac{1}{p}}_{A_{p,\mathcal{R}}}\,\left(\frac{d(R)^{\delta p}}{e(R)^{n}w(R)}\int_{R}\int_{R}\frac{|f(x)-f(y)|^{p}}{|x-y|^{\delta p+n}}\,dy\,w(x)dx\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle[w]^{\frac{1}{p}}_{A_{p,\mathcal{R}}}\,\frac{d(R)^{\delta}}{e(R)^{\frac{n}{p}}}\left(\frac{1}{w(R)}\int_{R}\int_{R}\frac{|f(x)-f(y)|^{p}}{|x-y|^{\delta p+n}}\,dy\,w(x)dx\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle a(R).

Note that the functional
a
here is not
exactly
the same as in the model example (

) due to the presence of the eccentricity in front. But since we are testing the smallness preservation on families of dyadic rectangles on a fixed rectangle

∎

Next we present here the proof of Theorem
2.14
. Recall that the idea is that even only being able to start from a weaker starting point (
2.13
), in the end we arrive at the same place, namely to the usual
L^{1}
oscillation initial point.

Proof of Theorem
2.14
.

Note that proving (
2.14
) is the same as proving that the following quantity

(4.13)

X:=\sup_{R\in\mathcal{R}}\inf_{c\in\mathbb{R}}\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$}}\kern-4.86108pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$}}\kern-3.43057pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.908pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.76045pt}}\!\int_{R}\frac{|f(x)-c|}{a(R)}dx

is finite. There are two reductions that make this trivial: the function
f
being bounded and the functional
a
being uniformly away from zero. Both can be formalized in a standard way. For the functional
a
it suffices to consider the perturbed functional
a_{\varepsilon}:=a+\varepsilon
for
\varepsilon>0
since this does not affect the properties assumed on
a
. This observation may (and should) be compared to the remark made prior to the proof of Theorem
2.1
. The absence of weights makes these reductions much easier.

The argument below shows how to truncate the function
f
. Therefore, we can always assume that the supremum defining
X
is finite. Let us start then by observing that we may assume that the function
f
is bounded. More precisely, suppose that (
2.13
) holds and consider
m\in\mathbb{N}
. Then the truncation
f_{m}:=\min\{|f|;m\}
also verifies (
2.13
). To see why, consider
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
and also truncate it by introducing
\lambda_{m}:=\min\{|\lambda|;m\}
. We can compute directly both minima and obtain:

\displaystyle f_{m}-\lambda_{m}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\min\{|f|;m\}-\min\{|\lambda|;m\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\left(|f|+m-||f|-m|-\left(|\lambda|+m-||\lambda|-m|\right)\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\left(|f|-|\lambda|-||\lambda|-m|-||f|-m|\right).

Hence, taking absolute values,

\displaystyle|f_{m}-\lambda_{m}|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\left(||f|-|\lambda||+|||\lambda|-m|-||f|-m||\right)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\left(|f-\lambda|+||\lambda|-|f||\right)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\left(|f-\lambda|+|f-\lambda|\right)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle|f-\lambda|

for all
m\in\mathbb{N}
and for all
\lambda>0
. This pointwise estimate yields the following

\inf_{c\in\mathbb{R}}\left(\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$}}\kern-4.86108pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$}}\kern-3.43057pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.908pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.76045pt}}\!\int_{R}|f_{m}(x)-c|^{\delta}\right)^{\frac{1}{\delta}}\leq\left(\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$}}\kern-4.86108pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$}}\kern-3.43057pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.908pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.76045pt}}\!\int_{R}|f_{m}(x)-\lambda_{m}|^{\delta}\right)^{\frac{1}{\delta}}\leq\left(\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$}}\kern-4.86108pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$}}\kern-3.43057pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.908pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.76045pt}}\!\int_{R}|f(x)-\lambda|^{\delta}\right)^{\frac{1}{\delta}},

also for any
\lambda>0
. Therefore we obtain that

\inf_{c\in\mathbb{R}}\left(\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$}}\kern-4.86108pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$}}\kern-3.43057pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.908pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.76045pt}}\!\int_{R}|f_{m}(x)-c|^{\delta}\right)^{\frac{1}{\delta}}\leq\inf_{\lambda\in\mathbb{R}}\left(\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$}}\kern-4.86108pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$}}\kern-3.43057pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.908pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.76045pt}}\!\int_{R}|f(x)-\lambda|^{\delta}\right)^{\frac{1}{\delta}}\leq a(R).

This means that the same initial hypothesis also holds for any truncation of
f
at height
m
, independently of
m
.

Now we start with the proof of inequality (
2.12
) assuming that the function
f
is bounded. Given a rectangle
R\in\mathcal{R}
, we have by hypothesis that, for some
c_{R}\in\mathbb{R}
,

\left(\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$}}\kern-4.86108pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$}}\kern-3.43057pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.908pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.76045pt}}\!\int_{R}\left|\frac{f(x)-c_{R}}{a(R)}\right|^{\delta}dx\right)^{\frac{1}{\delta}}\leq 2.

We perform the standard dyadic C-Z decomposition of the function
\left|\frac{f-c_{R}}{a(R)}\right|^{\delta}
adapted to
R
at level
L>2
to be chosen later.
This means that we will have a collection
\{R_{i}\}_{i}
of disjoint maximal dyadic rectangles satisfying

(4.14)

L<\left(\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$}}\kern-4.86108pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$}}\kern-3.43057pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.908pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.76045pt}}\!\int_{R_{i}}\left|\frac{f(x)-c_{R}}{a(R)}\right|^{\delta}dx\right)^{\frac{1}{\delta}}\leq 2^{\frac{n}{\delta}}L.

Lets denote by
\Omega_{L}
the union of such rectangles. As usual, this set is the level set of the dyadic maximal function relative to
R
:

(4.15)

\Omega_{L}=\left\{x\in R:M^{d}_{R}\left(\left|\frac{f-c_{R}}{a(R)}\right|^{\delta}\right)(x)>L^{\delta}\right\},

where

(4.16)

M^{d}_{R}(g)(x)=\sup_{x\in P\in\mathcal{D}(R)}\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$}}\kern-4.86108pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$}}\kern-3.43057pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.908pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.76045pt}}\!\int_{P}|g(y)|dy.

Now, consider the following pointwise decomposition.

\displaystyle\left|\frac{f(x)-c_{R}}{a(R)}\right|^{\delta}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left|\frac{f(x)-c_{R}}{a(R)}\right|^{\delta}\chi_{\Omega}(x)+\left|\frac{f(x)-c_{R}}{a(R)}\right|^{\delta}\chi_{\Omega^{c}}(x)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sum_{i}\left|\frac{f(x)-c_{R}}{a(R)}\right|^{\delta}\chi_{R_{i}}(x)+L^{\delta}

since the Lebesgue differentiation theorem implies that outside
\Omega
the function
\frac{f-c_{R}}{a(R)}
is pointwise bounded by
L
. The usual argument at this stage would be to find a way to intercalate some average-like quantity related to
R_{i}
instead of the original object
R
. We proceed as follows in the sum above. Denote
g_{R}=\frac{f-c_{R}}{a(R)}
, then

\displaystyle\sum_{i}|g_{R}(x)|^{\delta}\chi_{R_{i}}(x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{i}\left(|g_{R}(x)|^{\delta}-\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$}}\kern-4.86108pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$}}\kern-3.43057pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.908pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.76045pt}}\!\int_{R_{i}}|g_{R}(y)|^{\delta}dy\right)\chi_{R_{i}}(x)

\displaystyle+\sum_{i}\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$}}\kern-4.86108pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$}}\kern-3.43057pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.908pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.76045pt}}\!\int_{R_{i}}|g_{R}(y)|^{\delta}\chi_{R_{i}}(x)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sum_{i}\left(\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$}}\kern-4.86108pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$}}\kern-3.43057pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.908pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.76045pt}}\!\int_{R_{i}}\left|\frac{f(x)-f(y)}{a(R)}\right|^{\delta}dy\right)\chi_{R_{i}}(x)+2^{n}L^{\delta},

by using the elementary bound
||a|^{\delta}-|b|^{\delta}|\leq|a-b|^{\delta}
for any
a,b\in\mathbb{R}
and
0<\delta<1
. The second term is obtained using the maximality from (
4.14
). Now, by convexity the
\delta
power can be moved out from the integral and using that the rectangles are disjoint we obtain

\sum_{i}|g_{R}(x)|^{\delta}\chi_{R_{i}}(x)\leq\left(\sum_{i}\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$}}\kern-4.86108pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$}}\kern-3.43057pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.908pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.76045pt}}\!\int_{R_{i}}\left|\frac{f(x)-f(y)}{a(R)}\right|dy\chi_{R_{i}}(x)\right)^{\delta}+2^{n}L^{\delta}.

Therefore, collecting all estimates, we get

\left|\frac{f(x)-c_{R}}{a(R)}\right|^{\delta}\leq\left(\sum_{i}\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$}}\kern-4.86108pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$}}\kern-3.43057pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.908pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.76045pt}}\!\int_{R_{i}}\left|\frac{f(x)-f(y)}{a(R)}\right|dy\chi_{R_{i}}(x)\right)^{\delta}+2^{n}L^{\delta}+L^{\delta}.

Now we can compute the desired
L^{1}
norm.

\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{p_{w}^{*}}=\frac{\delta}{n}\frac{1}{M}.

\left(\sum_{i}a_{j}(R_{i})^{p^{*}_{w}}\,\frac{w(R_{i})}{w(R)}\right)^{\frac{1}{p^{*}_{w}}}\leq[w]_{A_{1,\mathfrak{R}}}^{\frac{\delta}{nM}}\,\left(\frac{|\bigcup_{i}R_{i}|}{|R|}\right)^{\frac{\delta}{nM^{\prime}}}a(R)\qquad j=1,2

and hence the corresponding result holds for

a(R):=a_{1}(R)+a_{2}(R),

more precisily

\left(\sum_{i}(a(R_{i}))^{p^{*}_{w}}\,\frac{w(R_{i})}{w(R)}\right)^{\frac{1}{p^{*}_{w}}}\leq 2[w]_{A_{1,\mathfrak{R}}}^{\frac{\delta}{nM}}\,\left(\frac{|\bigcup_{i}R_{i}|}{|R|}\right)^{\frac{\delta}{nM^{\prime}}}a(R)\qquad j=1,2

Now we are in a position to finish the proof exactly as of Theorem
2.16
. We omit the details.

∎

Appendix A
The truncation method

We include here for completeness the different truncation arguments that we used in our results.

A.1.
The classical weak implies strong for the gradient

We include here the classical truncation argument for Lipschitz functions related to the functional defined by the integral of the gradient.
The general idea of truncation arguments in the context of Poincaré-Sobolev inequalities
is well known, classical references are
[
M85
]
and
[
BCLSC95
]
.

Lemma A.1
.

Let
g:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to{\mathbb{R}}
be any nonnegative Lipschitz function. Suppose that for the pair
1\leq q<p
there is a weak
type estimate for a pair of measures
\nu,\mu
of the form:

\|g\|_{L^{p,\infty}_{\mu}}\lesssim\left(\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}|\nabla g|^{q}\,d\nu\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}.

Then the strong estimate also holds, namely

\|g\|_{L^{p}_{\mu}}\lesssim\left(\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}|\nabla g|^{q}\,d\nu\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}.

Proof.

Consider the usual truncation of a non negative function
g
at level
2^{k}
given by
T_{k}(g)
defined by:

T_{k}g(x):=\left\{\begin{array}[]{cc}0&\text{ if }g(x)\leq 2^{k}\\
g(x)-2^{k}&\text{ if }2^{k}<g(x)<2^{k+1}\\
2^{k}&\text{ if }g(x)\geq 2^{k+1}.\\
\end{array}\right.

Also define for each
k\in\mathbb{Z}
the set
G_{k}:=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:2^{k}<g(x)\leq 2^{k+1}\}
.
We have that, for all
x\in G_{k+1}
,
T_{k}g(x)=2^{k}
and
\text{sop}\nabla(T_{k}g)\subset G_{k}
. We proceed as follows:

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}g^{p}(x)d\mu

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\sum_{k=-\infty}^{k=\infty}2^{kp}\mu(G_{k+1})

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\sum_{k=-\infty}^{k=\infty}2^{(k-1)p}\mu(T_{k}g(x)>2^{k-1})

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\sum_{k=-\infty}^{k=\infty}\left(2^{k-1}\mu(T_{k}g(x)>2^{k-1})^{\frac{1}{p}}\right)^{p}.

Since
T_{k}g
is still a Lipschitz function, we can use the hypothesis and get

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}g^{p}(x)d\mu

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\sum_{k=-\infty}^{k=\infty}\left(\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}|\nabla T_{k}g|^{q}\,d\nu\right)^{\frac{p}{q}}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\sum_{k=-\infty}^{k=\infty}\left(\int_{G_{k}}|\nabla T_{k}g|^{q}\,d\nu\right)^{\frac{p}{q}}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\sum_{k=-\infty}^{k=\infty}\left(\int_{G_{k}}|\nabla g|^{q}\,d\nu\right)^{\frac{p}{q}}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|\nabla g|^{q}\,d\nu\right)^{\frac{p}{q}}

using that
\frac{p}{q}>1
and the disjointness of the family
\{G_{k}\}
.
∎

A.2.
Truncation in the bi-parameter setting

Lemma A.2
.

Let
g:\mathbb{R}^{n_{1}}\times\mathbb{R}^{n_{2}}\to{\mathbb{R}}
be any nonnegative Lipschitz function. Suppose that for
p\geq 1
there is a weak

(q,p)
-type estimate for a pair of measures
\nu,\mu
of the form:

\|g\|_{L^{p,\infty}_{\mu}}\lesssim\left(\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n_{1}}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{n_{2}}}(|\nabla_{1}g|^{q}d\nu\right)^{1/q}+\left(\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n_{1}}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{n_{2}}}(|\nabla_{2}g|^{q}d\nu\right)^{1/q}.

Then the strong estimate also holds, namely

\|g\|_{L^{p}_{\mu}}\lesssim\left(\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n_{1}}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{n_{2}}}(|\nabla_{1}g|^{q}d\nu\right)^{1/q}+\left(\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n_{1}}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{n_{2}}}(|\nabla_{2}g|^{q}d\nu\right)^{1/q}.

Proof.

This case is as easy as Lemma
A.1
, since the class of Lipschitz functions enjoys the truncation property. The same proof works here as well.
∎

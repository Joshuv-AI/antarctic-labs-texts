Luigi De Masi, Guido De Philippis

Abstract
. We prove the existence of minimal surfaces in a bounded convex subset of ℝ 3 \mathbb{R}^{3} ℳ \mathcal{M} intersecting the boundary of ℳ \mathcal{M} with a fixed contact angle. The proof is based on a min-max construction in the spirit of Almgren-Pitts for the capillarity functional.



1.
Introduction

1.1.
Background and main result

Minimal surfaces, i.e. critical points of the area functional, are a fundamental object in Geometric Analysis and the problem of finding a minimal surface (possibly with given side constraints) in a given Riemannian manifold
\mathcal{M}
has been a fundamental topic of research in the last 50 years.

When the topology of the ambient manifold
\mathcal{M}
is non-trivial, existence result can be often obtained via minimization of the area functional in suitable homology classes. On the other hand, when the topology is trivial, it is easy to see that non-trivial minimizers do not exist and one has to rely on different methods.

Since the seminal work of Birkhoff about the existence of closed geodesic in Riemannian manifolds, the use of min-max techniques has been a successful tool to show existence of critical points for a variety of functionals.

The implementation of min-max techniques for the construction of minimal surfaces has been started in the seminal work of Almgren and Pitts and deeply relies on techniques of Geometric Measure Theory. One of the fundamental outcomes of the theory has been to show the existence of a
d
dimensional minimal surface in every
(d+1)
-dimensional manifold,
[
22
,
3
,
5
]
. (To be precise the minimal surface is allowed to have a “small” singular set when
d\geq 7
) . Since their seminal work, the strategy has been suitably modified to show the existence of surfaces with prescribed curvature
[
27
]
, given boundary
[
4
]
as well as free boundary,
[
4
]
. Furthermore these techniques have been as well used to show the existence of
infinitely many
minimal surfaces in a given Riemannian manifold
[
19
,
14
,
26
]

The main result of this paper is to show how Almgren-Pitts techniques (and more precisely their revised version by Colding-De Lellis,
[
3
]
) allows the existence of minimal surfaces with
prescribed contact angle at the boundary
in convex subsets of
\mathbb{R}^{3}
.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
\mathcal{M}\subset\mathbb{R}^{3}
be a convex bounded open set with
C^{2,\alpha}
boundary. Then, for all
\theta\in(0,\pi/2)
there exists a minimal surface
\Sigma\subset\mathcal{M}
with
\partial\Sigma\subset\partial\mathcal{M}
and

\eta_{\Sigma}\cdot N=\sin\theta\qquad\text{on $\partial\Sigma$}

where
\eta_{\Sigma}
is the exterior co-normal field to
\Sigma
at
\partial\Sigma
and
N
is the exterior unit normal to
\partial\mathcal{M}
.

1.2.
Sketch of the argument and structure of the paper

The main idea of the proof, which has already appeared in the Master Thesis of the first author
[
6
]
, where some preliminary steps of this program were carried out, is to apply Almgren-Pitts/Colding-De Lellis techniques to the
capillarity energy
:

(1.1)

F_{a}(\Omega)=\mathcal{H}^{2}(\partial_{i}{\Omega})+\cos\theta\mathcal{H}^{2}(\partial_{b}\Omega),

where
\Omega\subset\mathcal{M}
and
\partial_{i}{\Omega}=\partial\Omega\cap{\kern 0.0pt\mathcal{M}}^{\mathrm{o}}
,
\partial_{b}\Omega=\partial\Omega\cap\partial\mathcal{M}
. Note indeed that a (smooth) stationary point of (
1.1
) shall satisfy

H_{\Sigma}=0\text{ in ${\kern 0.0pt\mathcal{M}}^{\mathrm{o}}$,}\qquad\eta_{\Sigma}\cdot N=\sin\theta\quad\text{on $\partial\Sigma\cap\partial\mathcal{M}$}

where
\Sigma=\partial_{i}{\Omega}
. With respect to the case of the pure area functional, or to the free boundary one (when
\theta=\pi/2
) two main new difficulties arise. First, a priori, the capillarity functional is only defined for surfaces which are boundary of a set and it is not clear how to extend it to class of rectifiable varifold, a technical step which is instrumental to the proof of Theorem
1.1
. The key idea here is to “decouple” the functional and initially look at the interior and boundary part as independent. Once this has been done, the pull-tight procedure can be carried on and one can show the existence of a stationary varifold which satisfies a suitable notion of “angle condition” which a slight modification on the on proposed by Kagaya and Tonegawa in
[
15
]
. This will be done in Section
3
and
4
.

The second key step is to show regularity of the obtained varifold; here we start by following the stategy of
[
22
,
3
,
5
]
, and showing that the stationary varifold found in the previous step is
\varepsilon
-minimizing in annuli and thus it admits replacements there, see Section
5
and
7
. Existence of replacements then follows by the the regularity theory of
[
8
,
9
]
together with the curvature estimate proved for
2
-dimensional stable critical point of the capillarity functional. These estimates, recalled in Section
6
can be easily obtained if one assumes an a priori area growth on the surface (a condition which is met on our construction) and have been recently established in full generality in
[
13
]
. Existence of replacements implies that the obtained varifold is induced by a smooth surface outside a finite set of points and the proof follows the one in
[
4
]
. In order to remove the final singular points we instead rely on a new argument which strongly uses that
\theta<\pi/2
. Under this assumption we can show that stable surface is indeed graphical and satisfies a suitable free boundary problem, from which we can deduce the desired regularity. This is done in Section
8
.

We conclude the introduction by commenting the restriction to open convex subsets of
\mathbb{R}^{3}
. First of all, it will be clear from the proof that we can endow
\mathcal{M}
with any (smooth) Riemaniann metric for which the boundary is still convex and obtain the same result. Convexity on the other hand is used in showing that the “interior” part of
\Sigma
does not touch the boundary of
\mathcal{M}
and it looks plausible that by following the argument of
[
17
]
, the assumption can be dispensed. The restriction to dimension
3
appears instead only in one (crucial) point of our proof, namely in showing curvature estimates for stable capillarity surfaces in Section
6
. We conjecture that similar estimates should hold true in higher dimension as well and if this is the case, the same arguments of the paper will show the existence of
d
-dimensional surfaces into convex sets of
(d+1)
-dimensional manifolds. We refer the reader to the forthcoming PhD thesis of the first author for some of the mentioned generalizations,
[
7
]
.

While completing this paper, we learned that Chao Li, Xin Zhou and Jonathan Zu
[
16
]
had just obtained a similar result with related techniques.

3.
Contact angle condition for varifolds

We now discuss a notion of contact angle condition for varifolds. Our condition follows the one given by Kagaya and Tonegawa in
[
15
]
, but it is weaker since it allows the “boundary part” to be just a
2
-varifold on
\partial\mathcal{M}
(that is a positive Radon measure on
\partial\mathcal{M}
), whereas the one in
[
15
]
is more restrictive, since it fixes the boundary part to be a subset of
\partial\mathcal{M}
. We state our condition for pairs
(V,W)
where
V\in\mathcal{V}_{2}(\mathcal{M})
and
W\in\mathcal{V}_{2}(\partial\mathcal{M})
.

Definition 3.1

(Contact angle condition)
.

Given
\theta\in[0,\frac{\pi}{2})
we say that the pair
(V,W)\in\mathcal{V}_{2}(\mathcal{M})\times\mathcal{V}_{2}(\partial\mathcal{M})
satisfies the contact angle condition
\theta
if there exists a
\lVert V\rVert
-measurable vector field
H\in L^{1}(\mathcal{M},\lVert V\rVert)
such that, for every
X\in\mathfrak{X}_{t}(\mathcal{M})
, it holds

(3.1)

\begin{split}\delta_{F_{a}}(V,W)[X]&=\int_{G_{2}(\mathcal{M})}\dive_{S}X(x)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}V(x,S)+a\int_{G_{2}(\partial\mathcal{M})}\dive_{\partial\mathcal{M}}X(x)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}W(x,T_{x}\partial\mathcal{M})\\
&=-\int_{\mathcal{M}}{X(x)}\cdot{H(x)}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\lVert V\rVert(x),\end{split}

where
a=\cos\theta
. Since we test only with vector fields in
\mathfrak{X}_{t}(\mathcal{M})
, we assume that
H(x)\in T_{x}\partial\mathcal{M}
for
\lVert V\rVert
-a.e.
x\in\partial\mathcal{M}
.

Definition 3.2

(Stationary pairs)
.

If
\theta\in[0,\frac{\pi}{2})
and
a=\cos\theta
, we say that the pair
(V,W)\in\mathcal{V}_{2}(\mathcal{M})\times\mathcal{V}_{2}(\partial\mathcal{M})
is
stationary
for
F_{a}
if it satisfies the contact angle condition (
3.1
) with
H=0
. If

We also give the definition of stability for pairs with respect to

Definition 3.3

(Stable pairs)
.

If

Remark 3.1
.

To understand the intuition behind the previous definitions, we recall that that a
C^{1}
-surface
\Sigma\subset\mathcal{M}
with
C^{1}
-boundary
\partial\Sigma\subset\partial\mathcal{M}
has fixed contact angle
\theta
at
\partial\mathcal{M}
if its unit exterior conormal
\eta_{\Sigma}
satisfies
{\eta(x)}\cdot{N(x)}\equiv\sin\theta
for every
x\in\partial\Sigma
(where
N
is the exterior unit normal vector to
\partial\mathcal{M}
).

If
\Omega\in\mathscr{O}(\mathcal{M})
, then it easily seen that the surface
\partial_{i}\Omega\subset\mathcal{M}
has fixed contact angle
\theta
if and only if either
\eta+a\zeta\perp\partial\mathcal{M}
or
\eta-a\zeta\perp\partial\mathcal{M}
, where

, and
\eta
and
\zeta
are the unit conormal respectively of

and
\partial_{b}\Omega
; thus

has fixed contact angle

if and only if

(3.3)

\int_{\partial_{i}\Omega}\dive_{\partial_{i}\Omega}X\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\mathcal{H}^{2}\pm a\int_{\partial_{b}\Omega}\dive_{\partial\mathcal{M}}X\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\mathcal{H}^{2}=-\int_{\partial_{i}\Omega}{X}\cdot{H}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\mathcal{H}^{2},

where
\pm
must be constantly
+
or
-
and
H
is the mean curvature of
\partial_{i}\Omega
. Moreover, if
H\equiv 0
, then
\partial_{i}\Omega
is a minimal surface which meets
\partial\mathcal{M}
with angle
\theta
.

All the results of this section are proved in Appendix

4.
Existence of non-trivial stationary pairs

4.1.
Sweepouts

In this section we define the basic elements of the min-max procedure.

Definition 4.1
.

A family
\{\Sigma_{t}\}_{t\in[0,1]}
is a
parametrized smooth family of surfaces
if it satisfies the following properties:

(1)

For every
t\in[0,1]

\Sigma_{t}
is a closed subset of
\mathcal{M}
of finite
\mathcal{H}^{2}
-measure;

(2)

For every
t\in[0,1]
there exists a finite set
S_{t}
such that
\Sigma_{t}\setminus S_{t}
is a smooth surface with boundary
\partial\Sigma_{t}\subset\partial\mathcal{M}
which is smooth outside of
S_{t}

(3)

if
t\to s
, then

\sup_{x\in\Sigma_{t}}\dist(x,\Sigma_{s})\to 0
;

(4)

if
U\subset\joinrel\subset\mathcal{M}\setminus S_{s}
, then
\Sigma_{t}
converges in the smooth sense to
\Sigma_{s}
in
U
as
t\to s
;

(5)

the function
t\mapsto\mathcal{H}^{2}(\Sigma_{t})
is continuous.

A first elementary consequence of the definition of parametrized family is that the area of the surfaces converge also locally. This is useful in the following, then we record it in the following lemma.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
\{\Sigma_{t}\}_{t\in[0,1]}
be a smooth parametrized family of surfaces. Then, for every open set
U\subset\mathcal{M}
, it holds

(4.1)

\lim_{t\to s}\mathcal{H}^{2}(\Sigma_{t}\cap U)=\mathcal{H}^{2}(\Sigma_{s}\cap U).

Proof.

For every
\varepsilon>0
, there exists
W\subset\joinrel\subset\mathcal{M}\setminus S_{s}
such that
\mathcal{H}^{2}(\Sigma_{s}\cap W)>\mathcal{H}^{2}(\Sigma_{s})-\varepsilon
. By smooth convergence in
W
, we have that

(4.2)

\lim_{t\to s}\mathcal{H}^{2}(\Sigma_{t}\cap U\cap W)=\mathcal{H}^{2}(\Sigma_{s}\cap U\cap W),\qquad\lim_{t\to s}\mathcal{H}^{2}(\Sigma_{t}\cap(W\setminus U))=\mathcal{H}^{2}(\Sigma_{s}\cap(W\setminus U))

Since
\mathcal{H}^{2}(\Sigma_{t})\to\mathcal{H}^{2}(\Sigma_{s})
by the choice of
W
we have also
|\mathcal{H}^{2}(\Sigma_{t}\cap U\setminus W)-\mathcal{H}^{2}(\Sigma_{s}\cap U\setminus W)|<\varepsilon
for
|t-s|
sufficiently small. This implies that
|\mathcal{H}^{2}(\Sigma_{t}\cap U)-\mathcal{H}^{2}(\Sigma_{s}\cap U)|<\varepsilon
for
|t-s|
sufficiently small.
∎

We often refer to a parametrized smooth family of surfaces simply as a family of surfaces.

Definition 4.2

(Sweepout)
.

A pair

(2)

(3)

for every

\partial_{i}\Omega_{t}\setminus\Sigma_{t}\subset S_{t}

(4)

(5)

\Omega_{0}=\emptyset
and
\Omega_{1}=\mathcal{M}

(6)

\vol(\Omega_{t}\setminus\Omega_{s})+\vol(\Omega_{s}\setminus\Omega_{t})\to 0

As it is stated in

4.2.
Pull-tight procedure

We now want to prove that there exists a minimizing sequence of sweepouts such that every min-max sequence converges to a pair which is stationary for the capillarity functional. From now on, we consider fixed the homotopically closed set of sweepouts
\mathscr{S}
and we call
{m_{0}}={m_{0}}(\mathscr{S})
.

Theorem 4.2

(Pull-tight procedure)
.

There exists a minimizing sequence of sweepouts
\{(\Sigma_{t}^{k},\Gamma_{t}^{k})\}^{k\in\mathbb{N}}
such that every min-max sequence converges, up to subsequences, to a pair
(V,W)\in\mathcal{V}_{2}(\mathcal{M})\times\mathcal{V}_{2}(\partial\mathcal{M})
which is stationary for the capillarity functional.

Proof.

We follow the construction in
[
3
]
.
Let us fix a minimizing sequence of sweepouts
\{(\Sigma_{t}^{k},\Gamma_{t}^{k})\}^{k\in\mathbb{N}}
.
The proof is based on the construction of a deformation of the minimizing sequence; this deformation reduce the mass of the surfaces of a quantity that depends on how much the pair is “far” from being stationary. This deformed minimizing sequence has the desired property.
We call

(4.6)

A^{2{m_{0}}}=\{(V,W)\in\mathcal{V}_{2}(\mathcal{M})\times\mathcal{V}_{2}(\partial\mathcal{M})\mid\lVert V\rVert(\mathcal{M})\leq 2{m_{0}},\lVert W\rVert(\partial\mathcal{M})\leq\mathcal{H}^{2}(\partial\mathcal{M})\}

and

(4.7)

A_{\infty}=\{(V,W)\in A^{2{m_{0}}}\mid(V,W)\text{ is stationary for }F_{a}\}.

Since the weak*-topology is metrizable on bounded sets, we choose a distance which induces it on
\{V\in\mathcal{V}_{2}(\mathcal{M})\mid\lVert V\rVert(\mathcal{M})\leq 2{m_{0}}\}
and
\{W\in\mathcal{V}_{2}(\partial\mathcal{M})\mid\lVert W\rVert(\partial\mathcal{M})\leq\mathcal{H}^{2}(\partial\mathcal{M})\}
and we call
\mathcal{D}
the distance on the product space
A^{2{m_{0}}}
which induces the product topology. Up to scaling
\mathcal{D}
, we can assume that
\diam A^{2{m_{0}}}=1
. Moreover
A^{2{m_{0}}}
is compact since it is a product of compact spaces. Since the property of being stationary is preserved under weak*-convergence of varifolds, it follows that
A_{\infty}
is also a compact subset of
A^{2{m_{0}}}
. We call
\mathcal{B}_{r}(V,W)
the ball of radius
r
and center
(V,W)
with respect the distance
\mathcal{D}
.

For every
i\in\mathbb{N}
, we call

(4.8)

A_{i}=\Big\{(V,W)\in A^{2{m_{0}}}\mid\frac{1}{2^{i+1}}\leq\mathcal{D}\big((V,W),A_{\infty}\big)\leq\frac{1}{2^{i}}\Big\}.

Since every
A_{i}
is closed, it is compact.

Step 1:

We claim that for every
i\in\mathbb{N}
there exists a constant
c_{i}>0
such that, for every
(V,W)\in A_{i}
, there exists
X_{(V,W)}\in\mathfrak{X}_{t}(\mathcal{M})
such that
|X_{(V,W)}(x)|\leq 1
for every
x\in\mathcal{M}
and

(4.9)

\begin{split}\delta_{F_{a}}(V,W)[X_{(V,W)}]=&\int_{G_{2}(\mathcal{M})}\dive_{S}X_{(V,W)}(x)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}V(x,S)+a\int_{G_{2}(\partial\mathcal{M})}\dive_{\partial\mathcal{M}}X_{(V,W)}(x)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}W(x,T_{x}\partial\mathcal{M})\\
\leq&-c_{i}.\end{split}

Indeed, suppose by contradiction that the claim is false for some
i\in\mathbb{N}
; thus there exists a sequence of pairs
(V_{j},W_{j})_{j}
in
A_{i}
such that for every
j
and for every
X\in\mathfrak{X}_{t}(\mathcal{M})
such that
|X|\leq 1
we have

(4.10)

\Big|\int_{G_{2}(\mathcal{M})}\dive_{S}X(x)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}V_{j}(x,S)+a\int_{G_{2}(\partial\mathcal{M})}\dive_{\partial\mathcal{M}}X(x)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}W_{j}(x,T_{x}\partial\mathcal{M})\Big|\leq\frac{1}{j}.

By compactness of
A_{i}
,
(V_{j},W_{j})
converges, up to subsequences, to
(V,W)\in A_{i}
. By the previous equation, it follows that
(V,W)
is stationary for
F_{a}
, which contradicts
(V,W)\in A_{i}
. This proves the claim.

Step 2:

The map
(V,W)\in A^{2{m_{0}}}\setminus A_{\infty}\mapsto X_{(V,W)}\in\mathfrak{X}_{t}(\mathcal{M})
is not necessarily continuous. We want now to use it to construct a continuous one with respect the
C^{1}
-topology in
\mathfrak{X}_{t}(\mathcal{M})
that satisfies an inequality on the first variation of
F_{a}
similar to (
4.9
) in a neighborhood of
(V,W)
.

We first observe that, by continuity of the first variation under varifold convergence, for every
i\in\mathbb{N}
and every
(V,W)\in A_{i}
there exists
r_{(V,W)}\in(0,\frac{1}{2^{i+2}})
such that

(4.11)

\int_{G_{2}(\mathcal{M})}\dive_{S}X_{(V,W)}(x)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\tilde{V}(x,S)+a\int_{G_{2}(\partial\mathcal{M})}\dive_{\partial\mathcal{M}}X_{(V,W)}(x)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\tilde{W}(x,T_{x}\partial\mathcal{M})\leq-\frac{c_{i}}{2}

whenever
\mathcal{D}\big((V,W),(\tilde{V},\tilde{W})\big)\leq r_{(V,W)}
.
Thus the family

(4.12)

\mathcal{F}_{i}=\{\mathcal{B}_{r_{(V,W)}}(V,W)\mid(V,W)\in A_{i}\}

(we recall that
\mathcal{B}_{r}(V,W)
is the ball of center
(V,W)
and radius
r
with respect the distance
\mathcal{D}
)
is an open cover of
A_{i}
and by compactness there exists a finite number
J_{i}
of pairs
\{(V_{i}^{j},W_{i}^{j})\}^{j=1,\dots,J_{i}}
such that

(4.13)

A_{i}\subset\bigcup_{j=1}^{J_{i}}\mathcal{B}_{\frac{r_{i,j}}{2}}(V_{i}^{j},W_{i}^{j})

(where we call
r_{i,j}:=r_{(V_{i}^{j},W_{i}^{j})}
). For simplicity of notation, we call
\mathcal{B}_{i,j}=\mathcal{B}_{r_{i,j}}(V_{i}^{j},W_{i}^{j})
and
\tilde{\mathcal{B}}_{i,j}=\mathcal{B}_{\frac{r_{i,j}}{2}}(V_{i}^{j},W_{i}^{j})
.
We underline that by the choice of
r_{(V,W)}
it follows that

(4.14)

A_{h}\cap\mathcal{B}_{i,j}=\emptyset\qquad\text{whenever }|i-h|\geq 2.

By the assumptions, the family

(4.15)

\mathcal{F}=\{\tilde{\mathcal{B}}_{i,j}\mid i\in\mathbb{N},j=1,\dots,J_{i}\}

is a locally finite covering of
A^{2{m_{0}}}\setminus A_{\infty}
. We can construct a partition of the unity
\psi_{i,j}
with respect to this covering and define

(4.16)

Y_{(V,W)}=\mathcal{D}\big((V,W),A_{\infty}\big)\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}i\in\mathbb{N}\\
j=1,\dots,J_{i}\end{subarray}}\psi_{i,j}(V,W)X_{(V_{i}^{j},W_{i}^{j})}\qquad\forall(V,W)\in A^{2{m_{0}}}.

The map
(V,W)\mapsto Y_{(V,W)}
is continuous with respect the
C^{1}
topology of
\mathfrak{X}_{t}(\mathcal{M})
and
Y_{(V,W)}=0
if
(V,W)\in A_{\infty}
.
If we define for every
(V,W)\in A^{2{m_{0}}}

(4.17)

\rho(V,W)=\min\Big\{\frac{r_{i,j}}{2}\mid(V,W)\in\tilde{\mathcal{B}}_{i,j}\Big\},

by definition we have
\rho(V,W)\leq\frac{1}{2^{i+3}}
whenever
(V,W)\in A_{i}
. Hence, if
(V,W)\in A_{h}
, for every
(\tilde{V},\tilde{W})\in\mathcal{B}_{\rho(V,W)}(V,W)
we have that

(4.18)

\begin{split}\delta_{F_{a}}(\tilde{V},\tilde{W})(Y_{(V,W)})&=\mathcal{D}\big((\tilde{V},\tilde{W}),A_{\infty}\big)\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}|i-h|\leq 1\\
j=1,\dots,J_{i}\end{subarray}}\psi_{i,j}(\tilde{V},\tilde{W})\delta_{F_{a}}(\tilde{V},\tilde{W})(X_{(V_{i}^{j},W_{i}^{j})})\\
&\leq-\frac{1}{2^{h+2}}\min\{c_{h-1},c_{h},c_{h+1}\},\end{split}

by (
4.11
), since
\mathcal{B}_{\rho(V,W)}(V,W)\subset\mathcal{B}_{i,j}
for every
i,j
such that
(V,W)\in\tilde{\mathcal{B}}_{i,j}
.

By compactness again, there exist two continuous functions
d\colon[0,1]\to(0,1]
and

such that

(4.19)

\displaystyle d(s)\leq 2^{-(i+3)}\qquad\forall s\in[2^{-(i+1)},2^{-i}],

(4.20)

\displaystyle\delta_{F_{a}}(\tilde{V},\tilde{W})(Y_{(V,W)})\leq-f\big(\mathcal{D}((V,W),A_{\infty})\big)\quad\text{whenever}\quad\mathcal{D}\big((V,W),(\tilde{V},\tilde{W})\big)<d\big(\mathcal{D}((V,W),A_{\infty})\big).

Step 3:

We now want to use the vector field-valued map constructed to deform the minimizing sequence of sweepouts
(\Sigma_{t}^{k},\Gamma_{t}^{k})^{k\in\mathbb{N}}
in a new minimizing sequence

in

.

If
(V,W)\in A^{2{m_{0}}}

(V_{t},W_{t})=\big((\psi_{(V,W),t})_{\sharp}V,(\psi_{(V,W),t})_{\sharp}W\big)

at the time

(4.21)

\delta_{F_{a}}(V_{t},W_{t})(Y_{(V,W)})\leq-f\big(\mathcal{D}((V,W),A_{\infty})\big)\quad\text{and}\quad\mathcal{D}((V_{t},W_{t}),(V,W))\leq d\big(\mathcal{D}((V,W),A_{\infty})\big).

(4.22)

\displaystyle\delta_{F_{a}}(V_{t},W_{t})(Y_{(V,W)})\leq-f\big(\mathcal{D}((V,W),A_{\infty})\big),

(4.23)

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}((V_{t},W_{t}),(V,W))\leq d\big(\mathcal{D}((V,W),A_{\infty})\big)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{eq:definition_distance_deformation_pulltight}}}{{<}}\frac{\mathcal{D}((V,W),A_{\infty})}{4}.

Hence, setting
\beta=\mathcal{D}((V,W),A_{\infty})

(4.24)

F_{a}(V_{T(\beta)},W_{T(\beta)})-F_{a}(V,W)\leq\int_{0}^{T(\beta)}\delta_{F_{a}}(V_{t},W_{t})(Y_{(V,W)})\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t\leq-T(\beta)f\big(\mathcal{D}((V,W),A_{\infty})\big).

We can re-normalize the flows
\psi_{(V,W),t}
by defining
\Psi_{(V,W),t}(x)=\psi_{(V,W),T(\beta)t}
, where again
\beta=\mathcal{D}((V,W),A_{\infty})
. Thus, re-defining
(V_{t},W_{t})=\big((\Psi_{(V,W),t})_{\sharp}V,\Psi_{(V,W),t})_{\sharp}W\big)
we have that there exists
g\colon(0,1]\to(0,+\infty)
such that
\lim_{s\to 0}g(s)=0
and

(4.25)

F_{a}(V_{1},W_{1})-F_{a}(V,W)\leq-g\big(\mathcal{D}((V,W),A_{\infty})\big),\qquad\mathcal{D}\big((V_{1},W_{1}),(V,W)\big)<\frac{\mathcal{D}((V,W),A_{\infty})}{4}.

Now we choose a minimizing sequence of sweepouts
\{(\Sigma_{t}^{k},\Gamma_{t}^{k})\}^{k\in\mathbb{N}}
in
\mathscr{S}
such that

(4.26)

\max_{t\in[0,1]}F_{a}(\Sigma_{t}^{k},\Gamma_{t}^{k})\leq{m_{0}}(\mathscr{S})+\frac{1}{k}\qquad\forall k\in\mathbb{N}.

Let us fix
k\in\mathbb{N}
. We would like to deform every
(\Sigma_{t}^{k},\Gamma_{t}^{k})
by the flow
\Psi_{(\Sigma_{t}^{k},\Gamma_{t}^{k}),1}
, but since the map

(4.27)

Y_{k}\colon t\in[0,1]\mapsto Y_{(\Sigma_{t}^{k},\Gamma_{t}^{k})}\in\mathfrak{X}_{t}(\mathcal{M})

is only continuous, the family of pairs

t\mapsto(\Psi_{(\Sigma_{t}^{k},\Gamma_{t}^{k}),1})_{\sharp}(\Sigma_{t}^{k},\Gamma_{t}^{k})
may not belong to
\mathscr{S}
.
Therefore we have to smooth the map
Y_{k}
. To this aim let us consider a smooth map
Z_{k}\colon[0,1]\to\mathfrak{X}_{t}(\mathcal{M})
. If
\sup_{t\in[0,1]}\lVert Y_{k}-Z_{k}\rVert_{C^{1}}
is sufficiently small and if
Z_{k}(t)=0
when
Y_{k}(t)=0
, then for every
t\in[0,1]
we have

(4.28)

F_{a}(\tilde{\Sigma}_{t}^{k},\tilde{\Gamma}_{t}^{k})-F_{a}(\Sigma_{t}^{k},\Gamma_{t}^{k})\leq-\frac{g\big(\mathcal{D}((\Sigma_{t}^{k},\Gamma_{t}^{k}),A_{\infty})\big)}{2},\qquad\mathcal{D}\big((\tilde{\Sigma}_{t}^{k},\tilde{\Gamma}_{t}^{k}),(\Sigma_{t}^{k},\Gamma_{t}^{k})\big)\leq\frac{\mathcal{D}\big((\Sigma_{t}^{k},\Gamma_{t}^{k}),A_{\infty}\big)}{4}.

where
(\tilde{\Sigma}_{t}^{k},\tilde{\Gamma}_{t}^{k})=\Phi_{1}(t)_{\sharp}(\Sigma_{t}^{k},\Gamma_{t}^{k})
and
\Phi_{1}(t)
is the flow map of
Z_{k}(t)
evaluated at the time
1
. Hence
(\tilde{\Sigma}_{t}^{k},\tilde{\Gamma}_{t}^{k})_{t\in I}\in\mathscr{S}
.
By repeating the same procedure for every
k\in\mathbb{N}
we obtain a new sequence of sweepouts
(\tilde{\Sigma}_{t}^{k},\tilde{\Gamma}_{t}^{k})^{k\in\mathbb{N}}
. By (
4.28
) we have that this sequence is again a minimizing sequence.

Step 4:

We claim that
(\tilde{\Sigma}_{t}^{k},\tilde{\Gamma}_{t}^{k})^{k\in\mathbb{N}}
has the desired property, that is, every min-max sequence converges to a stationary pair for
F_{a}
. To prove this, suppose by contradiction that the claim is false and let
(\tilde{\Sigma}_{t_{k}}^{k},\tilde{\Gamma}_{t_{k}}^{k})^{k\in\mathbb{N}}
a min-max sequence such that

(4.29)

\limsup_{k\to\infty}\mathcal{D}((\tilde{\Sigma}_{t_{k}}^{k},\tilde{\Gamma}_{t_{k}}^{k}),A_{\infty})=\ell>0.

Up to passing to a subsequence we can assume that the above upper limit is in fact a limit. By the second relation in (
4.28
), we have that, for
k
sufficiently large,

(4.30)

\mathcal{D}\big((\Sigma_{t_{k}}^{k},\Gamma_{t_{k}}^{k}),A_{\infty}\big)\geq\frac{\ell}{2}.

By the first inequality in (
4.28
) we get

(4.31)

F_{a}(\Sigma_{t_{k}}^{k},\Gamma_{t_{k}}^{k})\geq F_{a}(\tilde{\Sigma}_{t_{k}}^{k},\tilde{\Gamma}_{t_{k}}^{k})+\frac{1}{2}g\Big(\frac{\ell}{2}\Big)\xrightarrow{k\to\infty}{m_{0}}+\frac{1}{2}g\Big(\frac{\ell}{2}\Big)>{m_{0}}.

Thus

(4.32)

\liminf_{k\to\infty}\max_{t\in[0,1]}F_{a}(\Sigma_{t}^{k},\Gamma_{t}^{k})\geq\liminf_{k\to\infty}F_{a}(\Sigma_{t_{k}}^{k},\Gamma_{t_{k}}^{k})\geq{m_{0}}+\frac{1}{2}g\Big(\frac{\ell}{2}\Big)

which contradicts the fact that the sequence
(\Sigma_{t}^{k},\Gamma_{t}^{k})
was minimizing in
\mathscr{S}
.

∎

In order to prove that the limit pair of every min-max sequence is non-trivial we need the following proposition.

Proposition 4.3
.

If
\mathscr{S}
is an homotopically closed set of sweepouts, then
a\mathcal{H}^{2}(\partial\mathcal{M})<{m_{0}}(\mathscr{S})
.

Proof.

We want to show that there exists
\eta>0
such that, for every sweepout
(\Sigma_{t},\Gamma_{t})_{t\in[0,1]}\in\mathscr{S}
, it holds
\max_{t\in[0,1]}F_{a}(\Sigma_{t},\Gamma_{t})\geq a\mathcal{H}^{2}(\partial\mathcal{M})+\eta
.

To do so, we are going to prove that there exists
\varepsilon>0
and a differentiable function
f\colon[0,\varepsilon)\to\mathbb{R}^{+}
such that, for every sweepout
(\Sigma_{t},\Gamma_{t})
,
f(0)=0
,
f^{\prime}(s)>0
for
s\in(0,\varepsilon)
and

(4.33)

F_{a}(\Sigma_{t},\Gamma_{t})\geq a\mathcal{H}^{2}(\partial\mathcal{M})+f(|\mathcal{M}\setminus\Omega_{t}|)\qquad\forall t\in[0,1]\text{ s.t. }|\mathcal{M}\setminus\Omega_{t}|\leq\varepsilon.

This clearly implies the result. To prove the existence of such
f
, we write
F_{a}
in a different way, and next we use the relative isoperimetric inequality to obtain a lower bound on
F_{a}(\Sigma_{t},\Gamma_{t})
.

We fix a vector field
X\in\mathfrak{X}(\mathcal{M})
such that
|X(x)|\leq 1
for all
x\in\mathcal{M}
and
X(x)=N(x)
(the exterior unit normal to
\mathcal{M}
) for all
x\in\partial\mathcal{M}
; the
C^{1}
-norm of
X
depends only on
\mathcal{M}
. Using
X
we can write
F_{a}(\Sigma_{t},\Gamma_{t})
as

(4.34)

\begin{split}F_{a}(\Sigma_{t},\Gamma_{t})=&\mathcal{H}^{2}(\Sigma_{t})+a\mathcal{H}^{2}(\Gamma_{t})\\
=&\mathcal{H}^{2}(\Sigma_{t})+a\int_{\Sigma_{t}\cup\Gamma_{t}}{X}\cdot{\nu_{\Sigma}}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\mathcal{H}^{2}-a\int_{\Sigma_{t}}{X}\cdot{\nu_{\Sigma}}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\mathcal{H}^{2}\\
=&\int_{\Sigma_{t}}(1-a{X}\cdot{\nu_{\Sigma}})\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\mathcal{H}^{2}+a\int_{\Omega_{t}}\dive X(x)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x.\end{split}

Since up to sets of
\mathcal{H}^{2}
-measure
0
it holds
\Sigma_{t}\supset\partial\Omega_{t}\cap{\kern 0.0pt\mathcal{M}}^{\mathrm{o}}
, by the relative isoperimetric inequality there exists a constant
c(\mathcal{M})>0
such that

(4.35)

\mathcal{H}^{2}(\Sigma_{t})\geq\mathcal{H}^{2}(\partial\Omega_{t}\cap{\kern 0.0pt\mathcal{M}}^{\mathrm{o}})\geq c(\mathcal{M})\min\{|\Omega_{t}|,|\mathcal{M}\setminus\Omega_{t}|\}^{\frac{2}{3}}.

Thus, by (
4.34
) and
1-aX\cdot\nu_{\Sigma}\geq 1-a
, for every
t\in[0,1]
we have

(4.36)

\begin{split}F_{a}(\Sigma_{t},\Gamma_{t})\geq&c(\mathcal{M})(1-a)\min\{|\Omega_{t}|,|\mathcal{M}\setminus\Omega_{t}|\}^{\frac{2}{3}}+a\int_{\mathcal{M}}\dive X(x)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x-a\int_{\mathcal{M}\setminus\Omega_{t}}\dive X(x)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x\\
=&c(\mathcal{M})(1-a)\min\{|\Omega_{t}|,|\mathcal{M}\setminus\Omega_{t}|\}^{\frac{2}{3}}+a\mathcal{H}^{2}(\partial\mathcal{M})-a\int_{\mathcal{M}\setminus\Omega_{t}}\dive X(x)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x.\end{split}

If
\varepsilon>0
is sufficently small, for all
t\in[0,1]
such that
|\mathcal{M}\setminus\Omega_{t}|\leq\varepsilon
, the previous inequality becomes

(4.37)

\begin{split}F_{a}(\Sigma_{t},\Gamma_{t})\geq&a\mathcal{H}^{2}(\partial\mathcal{M})+c_{1}|\mathcal{M}\setminus\Omega_{t}|^{\frac{2}{3}}-c_{2}|\mathcal{M}\setminus\Omega_{t}|\\
=&a\mathcal{H}^{2}(\partial\mathcal{M})+f(|\mathcal{M}\setminus\Omega_{t}|),\end{split}

where
c_{1},c_{2}
are positive constants depending on
\mathcal{M},a
. Clearly
f\in C^{1}\big((0,\varepsilon)\big)
,
f(0)=0
and, up to choosing
\varepsilon>0
even smaller, we have and
f^{\prime}(s)>0
for all
s\in(0,\varepsilon)
.
∎

5.
Existence of almost minimizing min-max sequences

We now introduce the notion of
almost minimizing
pair
(\Sigma,\Gamma)
.

Definition 5.1
.

Let
\varepsilon>0
,
U\subset\mathcal{M}
be open and let
(\Sigma,\Gamma)
be a pair of surfaces that is compatible with an open set
\Omega\subset\mathcal{M}
(in the sense of Definition
4.3
). We say that
(\Sigma,\Gamma)
is
\varepsilon
-almost minimizing
in
U
if there exists no parametrized pair of surfaces
(\Phi_{t},\Psi_{t})_{t\in[0,1]}
and no family of open sets
\{\Omega_{t}\}_{t\in[0,1]}
such that:

(1)

(\Phi_{t},\Psi_{t})_{t\in[0,1]}
and
\{\Omega_{t}\}_{t\in[0,1]}

satisfy properties
1
,
2
,
3
,
4
and
6
of Definition
4.2
;

(2)

\Omega_{0}=\Omega
and
\Omega_{t}\setminus U=\Omega\setminus U
for every
t\in[0,1]
;

(3)

\Phi_{0}=\Sigma
and
\Phi_{t}\setminus U=\Sigma\setminus U
for every
t\in[0,1]
;

(4)

\Psi_{0}=\Gamma
and
\Psi_{t}\setminus U=\Gamma\setminus U
for every
t\in[0,1]
;

(5)

F_{a}(\Phi_{t},\Psi_{t})\leq F_{a}(\Sigma,\Gamma)+\frac{\varepsilon}{8}
for every
t\in[0,1]
;

(6)

F_{a}(\Phi_{1},\Psi_{1})\leq F_{a}(\Sigma,\Gamma)-\varepsilon
.

A sequence of pairs
\{(\Sigma^{k},\Gamma^{k})\}^{k}
is
almost minimizing
in
U
if there exists a sequence
\varepsilon_{k}\downarrow 0
such that each
(\Sigma^{k},\Gamma^{k})
is
\varepsilon_{k}
-almost minimizing in
U
.

Roughly speaking, a pair is almost minimizing in
U
if it cannot be continuously deformed in
U
into one other pair with
F_{a}
smaller than
\varepsilon
without passing through a pair with
F_{a}
larger than
\varepsilon/8
.
The main result of this section is the following.

Proposition 5.1
.

Let
\mathscr{S}
a homotopically closed set of sweepouts; then there exist a min-max sequence
(\Sigma^{k},\Gamma^{k})^{k\in\mathbb{N}}=(\Sigma_{t_{k}}^{k},\Gamma_{t_{k}}^{k})^{k\in\mathbb{N}}
and a function
r\colon\mathcal{M}\to(0,+\infty)
that satisfy:

(1)

(\Sigma^{k},\Gamma^{k})
converges, up to subsequences, to a pair
(V,W)\in\mathcal{V}_{2}(\mathcal{M})\times\mathcal{V}_{2}(\partial\mathcal{M})
which is stationary for
F_{a}
;

(2)

There exists
\varepsilon_{k}\downarrow 0
such that for every
x\in\mathcal{M}
and for every annulus
\an\in\mathcal{AN}_{r(x)}({x})
, there exists
N\in\mathbb{N}
such that the sequence
(\Sigma^{k},\Gamma^{k})^{k>N}
is
\varepsilon_{k}
-almost minimizing in
\an
.

The strategy of the proof is as follows: we have an homotopically closed set of sweepouts and a minimizing sequence
\{(\Sigma_{t}^{k},\Gamma_{t}^{k})\}^{k\in\mathbb{N}}
such that any min-max sequence converges to a stationary varifold, which is given by Theorem
4.2
. We prove that, if there were no almost minimizing min-max sequence, then the minimizing sequence can be deformed in another one
(\tilde{\Sigma}_{t}^{k},\tilde{\Gamma}_{t}^{k})^{k\in\mathbb{N}}
such that
\lim_{k}\max_{t\in[0,1]}F_{a}(\tilde{\Sigma}_{t}^{k},\tilde{\Gamma}_{t}^{k})<{m_{0}}
and this would be a contradiction.

5.1.
Deforming a sweepout near a non-almost minimizing slice

If a slice
(\Sigma_{t_{0}},\Gamma_{t_{0}})
of a sweepout is not
\varepsilon
-almost minimizing in some open set
U
, it can be deformed in another pair
(\tilde{\Sigma},\tilde{\Gamma})
, with
F_{a}(\tilde{\Sigma},\tilde{\Gamma})\leq F_{a}(\Sigma_{t_{0}},\Gamma_{t_{0}})-\varepsilon
by a parametrized family of pairs
(\Phi_{t},\Psi_{t})_{t\in[0,1]}
such that
(\Phi_{0},\Psi_{0})=(\Sigma_{t_{0}},\Gamma_{t_{0}})
and
(\Phi_{1},\Psi_{1})=(\tilde{\Sigma},\tilde{\Gamma})
.

We cannot simply replace
(\Sigma_{t_{0}},\Gamma_{t_{0}})
with
(\tilde{\Sigma},\tilde{\Gamma})
, first of all because in this way we no longer have a smooth parametrized family of pairs and because we need to deform also the slices for times near
t_{0}
to build the contradiction argument stated above. Hence we need the following lemma, which is similar to
[
5
, Lemma 3.1]
and
[
4
, Lemma 5.1]
.

Lemma 5.2
.

Let
(\Sigma_{t},\Gamma_{t})_{t\in[0,1]}
be a sweepout and suppose
t_{0}\in(0,1)
is such that
(\Sigma_{t_{0}},\Gamma_{t_{0}})
is not
\varepsilon
-almost minimizing in
U\subset\mathcal{M}
for some
\varepsilon>0
and some open set
U
. Then for every open set
V\subset\mathcal{M}
with
U\subset\joinrel\subset V
, there exists
\bar{\eta}>0
such that, whenever we choose
\eta\leq\bar{\eta}
and
0\leq t_{0}-\eta<a<a^{\prime\prime}<b^{\prime\prime}<b<t_{0}+\eta\leq 1
, there exists a sweepout
(\tilde{\Sigma}_{t},\tilde{\Gamma}_{t})_{t\in[0,1]}
that is homotopic to
(\Sigma_{t},\Gamma_{t})_{t\in[0,1]}
and satisfies the following properties:

(1)

(\tilde{\Sigma}_{t},\tilde{\Gamma}_{t})=(\Sigma_{t},\Gamma_{t})
for every
t\in[0,a]\cup[b,1]
;

(2)

(\tilde{\Sigma}_{t},\tilde{\Gamma}_{t})\setminus V=(\Sigma_{t},\Gamma_{t})\setminus V
for every
t\in[0,1]
;

(3)

F_{a}(\tilde{\Sigma}_{t},\tilde{\Gamma}_{t})<F_{a}(\Sigma_{t},\Gamma_{t})+\frac{\varepsilon}{4}
for every
t\in[0,1]
;

(4)

F_{a}(\tilde{\Sigma}_{t},\tilde{\Gamma}_{t})<F_{a}(\Sigma_{t},\Gamma_{t})-\frac{\varepsilon}{2}
for every
t\in[a^{\prime\prime},b^{\prime\prime}]
.

Proof.

The number
\bar{\eta}
will be chosen at the end of the proof.

Step 1:

The first step in the proof is to build the new sweepout
(\tilde{\Sigma}_{t},\tilde{\Gamma}_{t})
; this is achieved first of all by “deforming the time inside
U
”, to have that the slice is frozen on
(\Sigma_{t_{0}},\Gamma_{t_{0}})
in an open neighborhood of
t_{0}
, whereas in
\mathcal{M}\setminus V
nothing changes. While
(\Sigma_{t_{0}},\Gamma_{t_{0}})
remains frozen in
U
, we deform it therein by the family of
(\Phi_{t},\Psi_{t})
given by Definition
5.1
which contradicts the
\varepsilon
-almost minimality of
(\Sigma_{t_{0}},\Gamma_{t_{0}})
. Of course, this requires some technicalities.

Let
\{\Omega_{t}\}_{t\in[0,1]}
be the family of open sets relative to
(\Sigma_{t},\Gamma_{t})
.
Let us fix
V
and choose two open sets
A,B
such that

(5.1)

U\subset\joinrel\subset A\subset\joinrel\subset B\subset\joinrel\subset V

and such that both
\Sigma_{t_{0}}
and
\Gamma_{t_{0}}
are smooth surfaces (with eventually non-empty smooth boundary) in
C:=B\setminus A
(this is possibile since the sets of singularities
S_{t_{0}},G_{t_{0}}
of
\Sigma_{t_{0}}
and
\Gamma_{t_{0}}
are finite).
Let us fix two non-negative smooth functions
\chi_{A},\chi_{B}
such that

•

\chi_{A}\in C_{c}^{\infty}(B)
,
\chi_{B}\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathcal{M}\setminus\mkern 1.0mu\overline{\mkern-1.0muA\mkern-1.0mu}\mkern 1.0mu)
;

•

\chi_{A}(x)+\chi_{B}(x)\equiv 1
for all
x\in\mathcal{M}
.

Since
\partial\mathcal{M}
is
C^{2,\alpha}
, there exists
r>0
such that the distance function
\dist(x,\partial\mathcal{M})
is
C^{2,\alpha}
if
\dist(x,\partial\mathcal{M})\leq 2r
. Let
d(\cdot)
a smooth approximation of
\dist(\cdot,\partial\mathcal{M})
that coincides with
\dist(\cdot,\partial\mathcal{M})
whenever
\dist(x,\partial\mathcal{M})\leq r
. By the regularity of
\partial\mathcal{M}
and
d
, there exists a smooth function
F\colon x\in\mathcal{M}\mapsto\varphi_{x}\in C^{2,\alpha}(B_{r},B_{r})
such that each
\varphi_{x}
is a diffeomorphism and

(5.2)

\varphi_{x}\big(B_{r}\cap D_{{x,1}}(\mathcal{M})\big)=B_{r}\cap\{x_{1}\geq-d(x)\}\qquad\forall x\in\mathcal{M},

where
D_{{x,\lambda}}
is the dilation map defined in (
2.1
). Essentially,
\varphi_{x}
is a local chart that depends smoothly on
x
and maps
\partial\mathcal{M}
in
\{x_{1}=-d(x)\}
.

By a suitable choice of
A,B,\bar{\eta}
and by smooth convergence of
\Sigma_{t}\to\Sigma_{t_{0}}
in
C
, for every
t\in(t_{0}-\bar{\eta},t_{0}+\bar{\eta})

\Sigma_{t}\cap C
can be written as
\psi_{t}(\Sigma_{t_{0}})
for a smooth 1-parameter family of diffeomorphisms such that
\psi_{t_{0}}=\Id\llcorner_{\Sigma_{t_{0}}}
.
Moreover, if
\bar{\eta}
is sufficiently small, then
\psi_{t}(x)\in B_{r}(x)
for all
t\in(t_{0}-\bar{\eta},t_{0}+\bar{\eta})
and for all
x\in\mathcal{M}
.

All the previous arguments show that, for all
s,t\in(t_{0}-\bar{\eta},t_{0}+\bar{\eta})
and all
\lambda\in[0,1]
, it is well defined

(5.3)

f_{t,s,\lambda}(x)=\varphi_{x}^{-1}\Big(\chi_{B}(x)\varphi_{x}(\psi_{t}(x))+\chi_{A}(x)\big[(1-\lambda)\varphi_{x}(\psi_{t}(x))+\lambda\varphi_{x}(\psi_{s}(x))\big]\Big)\quad\forall x\in\mathcal{M}.

In other words,
f_{t,s,\lambda}(\Sigma_{t_{0}}\cap C)
coincides with
\Sigma_{t}
in a neighborhood of
B
and changes smoothly in a convex combination of
\Sigma_{t}
and
\Sigma_{s}
in a neighborhood

.
This definition ensures that
\partial[f_{t,s,\lambda}(\Sigma_{t_{0}}\cap C)]\subset\partial\mathcal{M}
for all
\Sigma,t\in(t_{0}-\bar{\eta},t_{0}+\bar{\eta})
and all
\lambda\in[0,1]
. Moreover, since
f_{t,s,\lambda}(\Sigma_{t_{0}})
is essentially a convex combination of
\Sigma_{t}
and
\Sigma_{s}
and by smooth convergence of the
\Sigma_{t}
in
C
,
\bar{\eta}
can be chosen so that every
f_{t,s,\lambda}(\Sigma_{t_{0}}\cap C)
is diffeomorphic to
\Sigma_{t_{0}}\cap C
and

(5.4)

\mathcal{H}^{2}(f_{t,s,\lambda}(\Sigma_{t_{0}}\cap C))<\mathcal{H}^{2}(\Sigma_{t}\cap C)+\frac{\varepsilon}{32}\qquad\forall t,s,\in(t_{0}-\bar{\eta},t_{0}+\bar{\eta}),\,\forall\lambda\in[0,1].

In a similar way we can define
g_{t,s,\lambda}
which satisfies the same properties of
f_{t,s,\lambda}
with
\Gamma_{t}
in place of
\Sigma_{t}
, in particular

(5.5)

\mathcal{H}^{2}(g_{t,s,\lambda}(\Gamma_{t_{0}}\cap C))<\mathcal{H}^{2}(\Gamma_{t}\cap C)+\frac{\varepsilon}{32}\qquad\forall t,s,\in(t_{0}-\bar{\eta},t_{0}+\bar{\eta}),\,\forall\lambda\in[0,1].

Moreover we have that
g_{t,s,\lambda}(\Gamma_{t_{0}}\cap C)\subset\partial\mathcal{M}
for all
s,t\in(t_{0}-\bar{\eta},t_{0}+\bar{\eta})
and all
\lambda\in[0,1]
. We underline that by the construction of
f_{t,s,\lambda}(\Sigma_{t_{0}}\cap C)
and
g_{t,s,\lambda}(\Gamma_{t_{0}}\cap C)
, it follows that their boundaries coincide and are contained in
\partial\mathcal{M}
.
Again in a similar way, we can define the family of open sets
\Xi_{t,s,\lambda}
relative to
f_{t,s,\lambda}(\Sigma_{t_{0}})
and
g_{t,s,\lambda}(\Sigma_{t_{0}})
.

We now fix
a,a^{\prime},a^{\prime\prime},b^{\prime\prime},b,b^{\prime}\in(0,1)
such that
t_{0}-\bar{\eta}<a<a^{\prime}<a^{\prime\prime}<b^{\prime\prime}<b^{\prime}<b<t_{0}+\bar{\eta}
and two smooth functions
\beta,\gamma\colon[0,1]\to[0,1]
such that

•

\beta(t)=0
for every
t\in[0,a]\cup[b,1]
and
\beta(t)\equiv 1
for every
t\in[a^{\prime},b^{\prime}]
;

•

\gamma(t)=t
for every
t\in[0,a]\cup[b,1]
,
\gamma(t)=t_{0}
for every
t\in[a^{\prime},b^{\prime}]
and
\gamma
is monotone non-decreasing.

We now use the hypotheses that
(\Sigma_{t_{0}},\Gamma_{t_{0}})
is not
\varepsilon
-almost minimizing in
U
, that is there exists a parametrized pair of surfaces
(\Phi_{t},\Psi_{t})_{t\in[0,1]}
which satisfies the properties of Definition
5.1
.
Let us consider another smooth function
\omega\colon[0,1]\to[0,1]
such that
\omega(t)=0
for every

and
\omega(t)=1
for every
t\in[a^{\prime\prime},b^{\prime\prime}]
. We now define the new sweepout
(\tilde{\Sigma}_{t},\tilde{\Gamma}_{t})_{t\in[0,1]}
as follows:

•

(\tilde{\Sigma}_{t},\tilde{\Gamma}_{t})=(\Sigma_{t},\Gamma_{t})
for every

;

•

(\tilde{\Sigma}_{t},\tilde{\Gamma}_{t})\setminus B=(\Sigma_{t},\Gamma_{t})\setminus B
for every

;

•

(\tilde{\Sigma}_{t},\tilde{\Gamma}_{t})\cap C=(f_{t,\gamma(t),\beta(t)}(\Sigma_{t_{0}}\cap C),g_{t,\gamma(t),\beta(t)}(\Gamma_{t_{0}}\cap C))

•

5.2.
Almgren-Pitts combinatorial argument

The aim of this section is to show that there exists a min-max sequence of pairs which is almost minimizing in at least one of any
admissible pair
of open sets, that is a pair of open sets which are at a sufficiently large distance compared to their diameter. This is used in
5.3
to complete the proof of Proposition
5.1
.

Definition 5.2

(Admissible pair of sets)
.

If
U_{1},U_{2}\subset\mathcal{M}
are relatively open and non-empty, we say that
(U_{1},U_{2})
is an
admissible pair of open sets
if

(5.15)

\dist(U_{1},U_{2})\geq 4\min\{\diam U_{1},\diam U_{2}\}.

We call
\mathcal{C}
the family of admissible pairs of open sets.

Definition 5.3
.

For any
\varepsilon>0
and
(U_{1},U_{2})\in\mathcal{C}
, we say that
(\Sigma,\Gamma)
is
\varepsilon
-almost minimizing in
(U_{1},U_{2})
if it is
\varepsilon
-a.m. in at least one of
U_{1},U_{2}
.

We can now state the fundamental result of this subsection.

Lemma 5.3

(Almgren-Pitts combinatorial lemma)
.

Let
\mathscr{S}
be a homotopically closed family of sweepouts; there exists a minimizing sequence of sweepouts
(\Sigma_{t}^{k},\Gamma_{t}^{k})_{t\in[0,1]}^{k\in\mathbb{N}}
and a min-max sequence
(\Sigma^{k},\Gamma^{k})^{k\in\mathbb{N}}:=(\Sigma_{t_{k}}^{k},\Gamma_{t_{k}}^{k})^{k\in\mathbb{N}}
that satisfies, for any
k\in\mathbb{N}
, the following properties:

(1)

F_{a}(\Sigma^{k},\Gamma^{k})\geq{m_{0}}-\frac{1}{k}
;

(2)

F_{a}(\Sigma^{k},\Gamma^{k})
is
\frac{1}{k}
-a.m. in every
(U_{1},U_{2})\in\mathcal{C}
;

(3)

(\Sigma^{k},\Gamma^{k})
converges to a pair in
\mathcal{V}_{2}(\mathcal{M})\times\mathcal{V}_{2}(\partial\mathcal{M})
which is stationary for
F_{a}
.

Before the proof of Lemma
5.3
we state the following simple and useful property.

Lemma 5.4
.

If
(U_{1},U_{2}),(V_{1},V_{2})
are pairs of non-empty open sets such that

(5.16)

\displaystyle\dist(U_{1},U_{2})\geq 2\min\{\diam U_{1},\diam U_{2}\}

(5.17)

\displaystyle\dist(V_{1},V_{2})\geq 2\min\{\diam V_{1},\diam V_{2}\},

then there exist two indices
i,j\in\{1,2\}
such that
U_{i}\cap V_{j}=\emptyset

Proof.

Without loss of generality we can suppose that
\diam U_{1}\leq\diam U_{2}
and
\diam V_{1}\leq\diam V_{2}
. Moreover we can suppose
\diam U_{1}\leq\diam V_{1}
.

If
U_{1}\cap V_{1}=\emptyset
there is nothing to prove. Otherwise, there exists
\xi\in U_{1}\cap V_{1}
. Then, for every
x\in U_{1}
and
y\in V_{2}

(5.18)

\begin{split}|x-y|&\geq|y-\xi|-|\xi-x|\\
&\geq 2\diam V_{1}-\diam U_{1}\\
&\geq\diam V_{1}\\
&\geq\diam U_{1}.\end{split}

Since
U_{1}
is relatively open and
\partial\mathcal{M}
is of class
C^{2,\alpha}
, if
x,y
both belonged to
U_{1}
, then one would have
|x-y|<\diam U_{1}
.
This shows that
U_{1}\cap V_{2}=\emptyset
.
∎

6.
Bernstein’s Theorem, curvature estimates and compactness

In this section we report some properties of (smooth) stable pairs for the capillarity functional.
The second variation of the capillarity functional
F_{a}
on a pair of surfaces
(\Sigma,\Gamma)
in
\mathcal{M}
induces the so called stability operator
Q
, which is a quadratic form on the set of smooth functions with compact support.

Proposition 6.1

(Stability operator)
.

Let
(\Sigma,\Gamma)
be a pair of surfaces in
\mathcal{M}
. Then for any
X\in\mathfrak{X}_{t}(\mathcal{M})
we have

(6.1)

\delta^{2}_{F_{a}}(V,W)[X]=Q(u),

where
u(x)=X(x)\cdot\nu_{\Sigma}(x)
for every
x\in\Sigma
, and

(6.2)

Q(u)=\int_{\Sigma}|\nabla u|^{2}-|A_{\Sigma}|^{2}u^{2}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\mathcal{H}^{2}-\int_{\partial\Sigma}qu^{2}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\mathcal{H}^{1},

where

(6.3)

q=\frac{H_{\partial\mathcal{M}}}{\sin\theta}+\cot\theta H_{\Sigma}-\kappa_{\partial\Sigma}.

Here
\kappa_{\partial\Sigma}={D_{\tau}\eta_{\Sigma}}\cdot{\tau}
, where
\tau
is a unit tangent vector field on
\partial\Sigma
.
In particular,
(\Sigma,\Gamma)
is stable for
F_{a}
if and only if the operator
Q
is non-negative definite on
C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathcal{M})
.

For a proof we refer to
[
23
, Appendix]
and to
[
13
, section 2.1]
.
If
(\Sigma,\Gamma)
is stationary for
F_{a}
in a half space, then
H_{\partial\mathcal{M}}=0
and
H_{\Sigma}=0
. Moreover, if the half space is defined by
\{x_{1}\geq 0\}
, by testing the stability inequality with the function

u=\zeta\Bigl(\frac{1-\cos\theta{\nu_{\Sigma}}\cdot{e_{1}}}{\sin\theta}\Bigr)

we obtain the following proposition, compare with the proof of
[
13
, Theorem 1.5]
.

Proposition 6.2

(Stability inequality for Capillarity functional)
.

Assume that
\mathcal{N}
is a half-space. Then there exists an universal constant
c(a)>0
such that, for every pair
(\Sigma,\Gamma)
which is stationary and stable for
F_{a}
and for every
\zeta\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
we have

(6.4)

\int_{\Sigma}\zeta^{2}|A_{\Sigma}|^{2}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\mathcal{H}^{2}\leq c\int_{\Sigma}|\nabla\zeta|^{2}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\mathcal{H}^{2},

where
|A_{\Sigma}|^{2}
is the norm squared of the second fundamental form of
\Sigma
.

Proof.

Without loss of generality, we can assume that
\mathcal{N}=\{x_{1}\geq 0\}
. Let us define

(6.5)

\varphi(x)=\frac{1-\cos\theta{\nu_{\Sigma}(x)}\cdot{e_{1}}}{\sin\theta}

and let
\zeta\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
be any test function.
The computations in the first part of the proof of
[
13
, Theorem 1.5]
yield (see
[
13
, (4.26)]
)

(6.6)

Q(\zeta\varphi)=\int_{\Sigma}\varphi^{2}|\nabla\zeta|^{2}-\zeta^{2}\varphi\frac{|A_{\Sigma}|^{2}}{\sin\theta}.

Since

(6.7)

0<\frac{1-\cos\theta}{\sin\theta}\leq\varphi\leq\frac{1+\cos\theta}{\sin\theta},

the result easily follows.
∎

The stability inequality is used to obtain a Bernstein-type theorem for stationary and stable surfaces with respect to the capillarity energy in a three-dimensional half space.

Theorem 6.3

(Bernstein Theorem)
.

Let us assume that
(\Sigma,\Gamma)
is stationary and stable for
F_{a}
in
\mathcal{N}\setminus\{0\}
, where
\mathcal{N}:=\{x_{1}\geq 0\}\subset\mathbb{R}^{3}
, and that there exists a constant
M>0
such that

(6.8)

\sup_{R\in(0,\infty)}\frac{\mathcal{H}^{2}(\Sigma\cap B_{R}(0))}{R^{2}}\leq M;

Then
\Sigma
and
\Gamma
are half planes and
\Sigma
forms an angle
\theta
with
\partial\mathcal{N}
such that
a=\cos\theta
.

Proof.

The proof is classical and we just point out that it is enough to test (
6.4
) with the following logarithmic cut-off function, for
N\in\mathbb{N}
:

(6.9)

\zeta_{N}(x)=\begin{cases}0&\text{if }|x|\leq e^{-2N}\\
2+\frac{\log(|x|)}{N}&\text{if }e^{-2N}\leq|x|\leq e^{-N}\\
1&\text{if }e^{-N}\leq|x|\leq e^{N}\\
2-\frac{\log(|x|)}{N}&\text{if }e^{N}\leq|x|\leq e^{2N}\\
0&\text{if }|x|\geq e^{2N}.\end{cases}

This, together with the area bound (
6.8
), gives

(6.10)

\int_{\Sigma\cap\big(B_{e^{N}}\setminus B_{e^{-N}}\big)}|A_{\Sigma}|^{2}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\mathcal{H}^{2}\leq\frac{2cMe^{2}}{N}.

By sending

, we obtain

, hence the conclusion.
∎

As it is well known, Bernstein theorem as above implies local curvature estimates, see

, Theorem 1.6]

Theorem 6.4

(Curvature estimates for stable surfaces)

stationary stable pair for

in
\mathcal{M}

|A_{\Sigma}|(x)\dist\nolimits_{\Sigma}(x,\partial\Sigma\setminus\partial\mathcal{M})\leq\Lambda\qquad\forall x\in\Sigma.

(A.11)

\begin{split}\frac{1}{\rho}\int\gamma\Big(\frac{|x|}{\rho}\Big)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\sigma_{V}\leq&-\frac{2}{\rho}\int_{\mathcal{M}}\gamma^{\prime}\Big(\frac{|x|}{\rho}\Big)\frac{|x|}{\rho^{2}}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}(\lVert V\rVert+a\lVert W\rVert)+\frac{c}{\rho}\int_{\mathcal{M}}\gamma\Big(\frac{|x|}{\rho}\Big)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}(\lVert V\rVert+a\lVert W\rVert)\\
&+\rho^{1-\frac{2}{p}}\left(\int_{\mathcal{M}}\gamma\Big(\frac{|x|}{\rho}\Big)|H+\tilde{H}|^{p}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\lVert V\rVert\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}\left(\frac{1}{\rho^{2}}\int_{\mathcal{M}}\gamma\Big(\frac{|x|}{\rho}\Big)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}(\lVert V\rVert+a\lVert W\rVert)\right)^{1-\frac{1}{p}}\\
=&\frac{2}{\rho}\frac{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}}{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\rho}\left(\int_{\mathcal{M}}\gamma\Big(\frac{|x|}{\rho}\Big)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}(\lVert V\rVert+a\lVert W\rVert)\right)-\frac{2}{\rho^{2}}\int_{\mathcal{M}}\gamma\Big(\frac{|x|}{\rho}\Big)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}(\lVert V\rVert+a\lVert W\rVert)\\
&+\frac{2+c\rho}{\rho^{2}}\int_{\mathcal{M}}\gamma\Big(\frac{|x|}{\rho}\Big)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}(\lVert V\rVert+a\lVert W\rVert)\\
&+\rho^{1-\frac{2}{p}}\left(\int_{\mathcal{M}}\gamma\Big(\frac{|x|}{\rho}\Big)|H+\tilde{H}|^{p}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\lVert V\rVert\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}\left(\frac{1}{\rho^{2}}\int_{\mathcal{M}}\gamma\Big(\frac{|x|}{\rho}\Big)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}(\lVert V\rVert+a\lVert W\rVert)\right)^{1-\frac{1}{p}}.\end{split}

Concerning the last member, we now observe that

\begin{split}\frac{2}{\rho}\frac{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}}{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\rho}\left(\int_{\mathcal{M}}\gamma\Big(\frac{|x|}{\rho}\Big)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}(\lVert V\rVert+a\lVert W\rVert)\right)-&\frac{2}{\rho^{2}}\int_{\mathcal{M}}\gamma\Big(\frac{|x|}{\rho}\Big)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}(\lVert V\rVert+a\lVert W\rVert)\\
=&2\frac{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}}{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\rho}\left(\frac{1}{\rho}\int_{\mathcal{M}}\gamma\Big(\frac{|x|}{\rho}\Big)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}(\lVert V\rVert+a\lVert W\rVert)\right).\end{split}

Substituting in (
A.11
) we get

\begin{split}\frac{1}{\rho}\int\gamma\Big(\frac{|x|}{\rho}\Big)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\sigma_{V}\leq&\,\,2\frac{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}}{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\rho}\left(\frac{1}{\rho}\int_{\mathcal{M}}\gamma\Big(\frac{|x|}{\rho}\Big)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}(\lVert V\rVert+a\lVert W\rVert)\right)\\
&+\frac{2+c\rho}{\rho^{2}}\int_{\mathcal{M}}\gamma\Big(\frac{|x|}{\rho}\Big)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}(\lVert V\rVert+a\lVert W\rVert)\\
&+\rho^{1-\frac{2}{p}}\left(\int_{\mathcal{M}}\gamma\Big(\frac{|x|}{\rho}\Big)|H+\tilde{H}|^{p}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\lVert V\rVert\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}\left(\frac{1}{\rho^{2}}\int_{\mathcal{M}}\gamma\Big(\frac{|x|}{\rho}\Big)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}(\lVert V\rVert+a\lVert W\rVert)\right)^{1-\frac{1}{p}}.\end{split}

Taking into account that

\begin{split}\frac{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}}{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\rho}&\left(\frac{1}{\rho}\int_{\mathcal{M}}\gamma\Big(\frac{|x|}{\rho}\Big)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}(\lVert V\rVert+a\lVert W\rVert)\right)\\
&\qquad=\frac{1}{\rho^{2}}\int_{\mathcal{M}}\gamma\Big(\frac{|x|}{\rho}\Big)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}(\lVert V\rVert+a\lVert W\rVert)+\rho\frac{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}}{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\rho}\left(\frac{1}{\rho^{2}}\int_{\mathcal{M}}\gamma\Big(\frac{|x|}{\rho}\Big)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}(\lVert V\rVert+a\lVert W\rVert)\right),\end{split}

we obtain

(A.12)

\begin{split}\frac{1}{\rho}\int\gamma\Big(\frac{|x|}{\rho}\Big)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\sigma_{V}\leq&\,\frac{4+c\rho}{\rho^{2}}\int_{\mathcal{M}}\gamma\Big(\frac{|x|}{\rho}\Big)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}(\lVert V\rVert+a\lVert W\rVert)+2\rho\frac{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}}{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\rho}\left(\frac{1}{\rho^{2}}\int_{\mathcal{M}}\gamma\Big(\frac{|x|}{\rho}\Big)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}(\lVert V\rVert+a\lVert W\rVert)\right)\\
&+\rho^{1-\frac{2}{p}}\left(\int_{\mathcal{M}}\gamma\Big(\frac{|x|}{\rho}\Big)|H+\tilde{H}|^{p}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\lVert V\rVert\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}\left(\frac{1}{\rho^{2}}\int_{\mathcal{M}}\gamma\Big(\frac{|x|}{\rho}\Big)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}(\lVert V\rVert+a\lVert W\rVert)\right)^{1-\frac{1}{p}}.\end{split}

This complete the estimate of the third term in the right-hand side of (
A.6
).

Gathering (
A.7
), (
A.8
), (
A.10
) and (
A.12
) in (
A.6
), we can estimate (
A.4
) as follows:

\begin{split}\frac{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}}{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\rho}\bigg(\frac{1}{\rho^{2}}&\int_{\mathcal{M}}\gamma\Big(\frac{|x|}{\rho}\Big)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}(\lVert V\rVert+a\lVert W\rVert)\bigg)^{\frac{1}{p}}\\
=&\frac{1}{p}\frac{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}}{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\rho}\left(\frac{1}{\rho^{2}}\int_{\mathcal{M}}\gamma\Big(\frac{|x|}{\rho}\Big)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}(\lVert V\rVert+a\lVert W\rVert)\right)\left(\frac{1}{\rho^{2}}\int_{\mathcal{M}}\gamma\Big(\frac{|x|}{\rho}\Big)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}(\lVert V\rVert+a\lVert W\rVert)\right)^{\frac{1-p}{p}}\\
\geq&-\frac{1+\rho}{p}\rho^{-\frac{2}{p}}\left(\int_{\mathcal{M}}\gamma\Big(\frac{|x|}{\rho}\Big)|H+\tilde{H}|^{p}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\lVert V\rVert\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}-\frac{a\rho^{-\frac{2}{p}}}{p}\left(\int_{\mathcal{M}}\gamma\Big(\frac{|x|}{\rho}\Big)|\tilde{H}|^{p}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\lVert W\rVert\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}\\
&-c(1+\rho)\left(\frac{1}{\rho^{2}}\int_{\mathcal{M}}\gamma\Big(\frac{|x|}{\rho}\Big)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}(\lVert V\rVert+a\lVert W\rVert)\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}\\
&-c\rho\frac{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}}{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\rho}\left(\frac{1}{\rho^{2}}\int_{\mathcal{M}}\gamma\Big(\frac{|x|}{\rho}\Big)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}(\lVert V\rVert+a\lVert W\rVert)\right)^{\frac{1}{p}},\end{split}

which is the desired inequality.
∎

Proof of Proposition
3.2
.

Without loss of generality we can assume
x_{0}=0\in\partial\mathcal{M}
. We first notice that we have to prove the result only for small
\rho
, since it is clearly true for large value of
\rho
.

If we call

(A.13)

f(\rho):=\bigg(\frac{1}{\rho^{2}}\int_{\mathcal{M}}\gamma\Big(\frac{|x|}{\rho}\Big)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}(\lVert V\rVert+a\lVert W\rVert)\bigg)^{\frac{1}{p}},

by Lemma
A.1
, there exists a constant
\Lambda=\Lambda\big(c,\lVert H\rVert_{L^{p}(\mathcal{M})},\mathcal{M},(\lVert V\rVert+a\lVert W\rVert)(\mathcal{M})\big)>0
(where
c
is the constant given by Lemma
A.1
) such that

(A.14)

f^{\prime}(\rho)+\Lambda f(\rho)+\Lambda\rho^{-\frac{2}{p}}\geq 0.

Hence, by
p>2
,

(A.15)

f^{\prime}(\rho)+\Lambda f(\rho)+\Lambda\rho^{-\frac{2}{p}}+\frac{\Lambda^{2}}{1-\frac{2}{p}}\rho^{1-\frac{2}{p}}\geq 0.

Multiplying the above inequality by
e^{\Lambda\rho}
, we get

(A.16)

\frac{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}}{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\rho}e^{\Lambda\rho}\left[\left(\frac{1}{\rho^{2}}\int_{\mathcal{M}}\gamma\Big(\frac{|x|}{\rho}\Big)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}(\lVert V\rVert+a\lVert W\rVert)\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}+\Lambda\rho^{1-\frac{2}{p}}\right]\geq 0.

Since the constant
c
of Lemma
A.1
does not depend on the choice of
\gamma
(the estimates are independent on the choice of
\gamma
unless that
\gamma^{\prime}(s)=0
for
s\in(0,1/2)
), letting
\gamma
increase to
\mathbf{1}_{[0,1)}
we have that the function

\rho\longmapsto e^{\Lambda\rho}\left[\left(\frac{1}{\rho^{2}}\int_{\mathcal{M}}\gamma\Big(\frac{|x|}{\rho}\Big)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}(\lVert V\rVert+a\lVert W\rVert)\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}+\Lambda\rho^{1-\frac{2}{p}}\right]

is monotone increasing.
∎

Proof of Proposition
3.3
.

Since
H\in L^{\infty}(\mathcal{M},\lVert V\rVert)
, in (
A.7
) and (
A.8
) we simply estimate

(A.17)

\begin{gathered}\frac{1}{\rho^{2}}\int_{\mathcal{M}}\gamma\Big(\frac{|x|}{\rho}\Big)|H+\tilde{H}|\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\lVert V\rVert\leq\frac{\lVert H+\tilde{H}\rVert_{\infty}}{\rho^{2}}\int_{\mathcal{M}}\gamma\Big(\frac{|x|}{\rho}\Big)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\lVert V\rVert,\\
\frac{1}{\rho^{2}}\int_{\mathcal{M}}\gamma\Big(\frac{|x|}{\rho}\Big)|\tilde{H}|\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\lVert W\rVert\leq\frac{\lVert\tilde{H}\rVert_{\infty}}{\rho^{2}}\int_{\mathcal{M}}\gamma\Big(\frac{|x|}{\rho}\Big)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\lVert W\rVert.\end{gathered}

For
\rho^{-1}\int\gamma\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\sigma_{V}
, we have

(A.18)

\begin{split}\frac{1}{\rho}\int\gamma\Big(\frac{|x|}{\rho}\Big)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\sigma_{V}\leq&\,\frac{4+c\rho}{\rho^{2}}\int_{\mathcal{M}}\gamma\Big(\frac{|x|}{\rho}\Big)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}(\lVert V\rVert+a\lVert W\rVert)+2\rho\frac{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}}{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\rho}\left(\frac{1}{\rho^{2}}\int_{\mathcal{M}}\gamma\Big(\frac{|x|}{\rho}\Big)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}(\lVert V\rVert+a\lVert W\rVert)\right)\\
&+\frac{\lVert H+\tilde{H}\rVert_{\infty}}{\rho}\int_{\mathcal{M}}\gamma\Big(\frac{|x|}{\rho}\Big)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\lVert V\rVert.\end{split}

Hence, computations similar to the proof of Lemma
A.1
and of Proposition
3.2
yield

(A.19)

\frac{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}}{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\rho}\bigg(\frac{1}{\rho^{2}}\int_{\mathcal{M}}\gamma\Big(\frac{|x|}{\rho}\Big)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}(\lVert V\rVert+a\lVert W\rVert)\bigg)\geq-\frac{\Lambda}{\rho^{2}}\int_{\mathcal{M}}\gamma\Big(\frac{|x|}{\rho}\Big)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}(\lVert V\rVert+a\lVert W\rVert)

for some
\Lambda>0
which depends only on
\mathcal{M},\lVert H\rVert_{\infty},\lVert\tilde{H}\rVert_{\infty}
(which depends on the second fundamental form of
\partial\mathcal{M}
). By multiplying by
e^{\Lambda\rho}
and arguing as in the proof of Proposition
3.2
, we obtain the conclusion.
∎

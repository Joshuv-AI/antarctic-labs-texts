Abstract
. In this paper, we continue investigating the second variation of Perelman’s ν \nu -entropy for compact shrinking Ricci solitons. In particular, we improve some of our previous work in [ 12 ] , as well as the more recent work in [ 34 ] , and obtain a necessary and sufficient condition for a compact shrinking Ricci soliton to be linearly stable. Our work also extends similar results of Hamilton, Ilmanen and the first author in [ 9 ] (see also [ 10 ] ) for positive Einstein manifolds to the compact shrinking Ricci soliton case.

Introduction

This is a sequel to our previous paper
[
12

\nu

Recall that a complete Riemannian manifold
(M^{n},g)
is called a
shrinking Ricci soliton
if there exists a smooth vector field
V
on
M^{n}
such that the Ricci tensor
Rc

of the metric
g
satisfies the equation

Rc+\frac{1}{2}\mathscr{L}_{V}g=\frac{1}{2\tau}g,

where
\tau>0
is a constant and
\mathscr{L}_{V}g
denotes the Lie derivative of
g
in the direction of
V
. If
V
is the gradient vector field
\nabla f
of a smooth function
f
, then we have a
gradient shrinking Ricci soliton
given by

(1.1)

Rc+\nabla^{2}f=\frac{1}{2\tau}g,

for some constant
\tau>0
. Here,
\nabla^{2}f
denotes the Hessian of
f
, and
f
is called a
potential function
of the Ricci soliton. Clearly, when
f
is a constant
we have an Einstein metric of positive scalar curvature. Thus,
gradient shrinking Ricci solitons include positive Einstein manifolds as a special case. In the following, we use
(M^{n},g,f)
to denote a gradient shrinking Ricci soliton.

Gradient shrinking Ricci solitons are self-similar solutions to Hamilton’s Ricci flow, and often arise as Type I singularity models in the Ricci flow as shown by Naber
[
37
]
, Enders-Müller-Topping
[
21
]
and Cao-Zhang
[
13
]
; see also Zhang
[
44
]
. As such, they play a significant role in the study of the formation of singularities in the Ricci flow and its applications. Therefore, it is very important to either classify, if possible, or understand the geometry of gradient shrinking Ricci solitons.

Hamilton
[
28
]
showed that any 2-dimensional complete gradient shrinking Ricci soliton is isometric to either
\mathbb{S}^{2}
, or
\mathbb{RP}^{2}
, or the Gaussian shrinking soliton on
{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
. In dimension
n=3

[
29

proved that a compact shrinking soliton must be a spherical space form
\mathbb{S}^{3}/\Gamma

[

[
37

[

[

that any three-dimensional complete gradient shrinking Ricci soliton is either isometric to the Gaussian soliton
{\mathbb{R}}^{3}
or a finite quotient of either
\mathbb{S}^{3}
or
\mathbb{S}^{2}\times{\mathbb{R}}
.

However, in dimension
n\geq 4

dimension

at the origin. Very recently, a noncompact toric gradient shrinking Kähler-Ricci soliton on the blowup of

Ricci solitons can be viewed as fixed points of the Ricci flow, as
a dynamical system on the space of Riemannian metrics modulo
diffeomorphisms and scalings.
In
[
40
]
, Perelman introduced the
\mathcal{W}
-functional

\mathcal{W}(\hat{g},\hat{f},\hat{\tau})=\int_{M}[\hat{\tau}(\hat{R}+|\nabla\hat{f}|^{2})+\hat{f}-n](4\pi\hat{\tau})^{-\frac{n}{2}}e^{-\hat{f}}d\hat{V},

on any compact manifold
M^{n}
, where
\hat{g}
is a Riemannian
metric on
M
,
\hat{R}
is its scalar curvature,
\hat{f}
is any smooth function on
M^{n}
,
and
{\hat{\tau}}>0
is a positive parameter. The associated

\nu
-entropy is defined by

\nu(\hat{g})=\inf\{\mathcal{W}(\hat{g},\hat{f},\hat{\tau}):\hat{f}\in C^{\infty}(M),\hat{\tau}>0,(4\pi\hat{\tau})^{-\frac{n}{2}}\int_{M}e^{-\hat{f}}d\hat{V}=1\},

which is always attained by some
\hat{f}
and
\hat{\tau}
.
Furthermore, Perelman showed that the
\nu
-entropy is monotone increasing under
the Ricci flow, and its critical points are precisely given by
gradient shrinking Ricci solitons
(M^{n},g,f)
satisfying (1.1). In particular, it follows that all compact shrinking Ricci solitons are necessarily gradient ones.

By definition, a compact shrinking Ricci soliton
(M^{n},g,f)

their linear stability. Among other results, they showed that, while the round sphere

\mathbb{S}^{n}
is linearly stable and the complex projective space
\mathbb{CP}^{n}
is neutrally linearly stable

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
(M^{n},g,f)
be a compact shrinking Ricci soliton satisfying equation (1.1). Then,

(i)

the decomposition of
\mathrm{Sym}^{2}(T^{*}M)

(ii)

are strictly less than
\frac{1}{4\tau}

Theorem 1.2
.

A compact shrinking Ricci soliton
(M^{n},g,f)
is linearly stable if and only if
{\cal L}_{f}\leq 0
on
\Ker(\operatorname{div}_{f})_{0}
.

Preliminaries

In this section, we fix our notation and recall some useful facts that will be used in the proof of Theorem
1.1
.
First of all, by scaling the metric
g

\tau=1
in equation (1.1) so that

(2.1)

Rc+\nabla^{2}f=\frac{1}{2}g.

We also normalize
f

From now on, we shall assume that
(M^{n},g,f)

As in
[
12
]
, for any symmetric 2-tensor

and 1-form

Moreover, as done in

Proof.

The above identities follow from direct computations given below.

\bullet
For (
2.15

\displaystyle\nabla_{i}\Delta_{f}u=

\displaystyle\nabla_{i}\nabla_{j}\nabla_{j}u-\nabla_{i}\nabla_{j}f\nabla_{j}u-\nabla_{j}f\nabla_{i}\nabla_{j}u

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Delta\nabla_{i}u+R_{ijjk}\nabla_{k}u-\frac{1}{2}\nabla_{i}u+R_{ij}\nabla_{j}u-\nabla_{j}f\nabla_{j}\nabla_{i}u

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Delta_{f}\nabla_{i}u-\frac{1}{2}\nabla_{i}u.

\bullet
For (
2.16

2.15
) that

\displaystyle\int_{M}u\operatorname{div}_{f}(\Delta_{f}\omega)\,e^{-f}dV=

\displaystyle\int_{M}-<\Delta_{f}\nabla u,\omega>e^{-f}\,dV

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{M}-<\nabla(\Delta_{f}u)+\frac{1}{2}\nabla u,\omega>\,e^{-f}dV

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{M}u(\Delta_{f}\operatorname{div}_{f}\omega+\frac{1}{2}\operatorname{div}_{f}\omega)\,e^{-f}dV.

\bullet
For (
2.17

2{\cal L}_{f}\operatorname{div}_{f}^{\dagger}\omega=-\frac{1}{2}\Delta_{f}(\nabla_{i}\omega_{j}+\nabla_{j}\omega_{i})-R_{ikjl}(\nabla_{k}\omega_{l}+\nabla_{l}\omega_{k}).

Notice that

\displaystyle\Delta_{f}\nabla_{i}\omega_{j}=

\displaystyle\nabla_{k}\nabla_{k}\nabla_{i}\omega_{j}-\nabla_{k}f\nabla_{k}\nabla_{i}\omega_{j}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\nabla_{k}(\nabla_{i}\nabla_{k}\omega_{j}+R_{kijl}\omega_{l})-\nabla_{k}f(\nabla_{i}\nabla_{k}\omega_{j}+R_{kijl}\omega_{l})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\nabla_{i}\Delta\omega_{j}+R_{il}\nabla_{l}\omega_{j}+R_{kijl}\nabla_{k}\omega_{l}+\nabla_{k}R_{kijl}\omega_{l}+R_{kijl}\nabla_{k}\omega_{l}

\displaystyle-\nabla_{i}(\nabla_{k}f\nabla_{k}\omega_{j})+\nabla_{i}\nabla_{k}f\nabla_{k}\omega_{j}+R_{kijl}\nabla_{k}f\omega_{l}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\nabla_{i}\Delta_{f}\omega_{j}-2R_{ikjl}\nabla_{k}\omega_{l}+\frac{1}{2}\nabla_{i}\omega_{j}.

\bullet
For (
2.18
): According to (
2.5
), (
2.18
) is equivalent to (
2.17
).

\bullet
For (
2.19
): Similar to the proof of (
2.16
), (
2.19
) is the adjoint of (
2.17
) with respect to the inner product (
2.7
).

\bullet
For (
2.20
):

\displaystyle\operatorname{div}_{f}(\mathscr{L}_{\omega^{\sharp}}g)_{j}=

\displaystyle\nabla_{i}(\nabla_{i}\omega_{j}+\nabla_{j}\omega_{i})-\nabla_{i}f(\nabla_{i}\omega_{j}+\nabla_{j}\omega_{i})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Delta_{f}\omega_{j}+\nabla_{j}\nabla_{i}\omega_{i}+R_{jk}\omega_{k}-\nabla_{j}(\nabla_{i}f\omega_{i})+\nabla_{j}\nabla_{i}f\omega_{i}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Delta_{f}\omega_{j}+\nabla_{j}\operatorname{div}_{f}\omega+\frac{1}{2}\omega_{j}.

∎

3.
Proof of the Main Theorems

In this section, we prove Theorem
1.1
and Theorem
1.2
stated in the introduction.
Once again, by scaling the metric
g
, we normalize
\tau=1
and assume that
(M^{n},g,f)
is a compact shrinking Ricci soliton satisfying

(3.1)

Rc+\nabla^{2}f=\frac{1}{2}g.

First of all, recall that we have the following direct sum decomposition

(3.2)

\displaystyle\mathrm{Sym}^{2}(T^{*}M)=

\displaystyle\ \mathrm{Im}(\operatorname{div}_{f}^{\dagger})\oplus{\mathbb{R}}\cdot\mathrm{Rc}\oplus\Ker(\operatorname{div}_{f})_{0},

where
{\mathbb{R}}\cdot\mathrm{Rc}
is the one dimensional subspace generated by the Ricci tensor
Rc
and
\Ker(\operatorname{div}_{f})_{0}
, as defined in (
1.8
), denotes the orthogonal complement of
{\mathbb{R}}\cdot\mathrm{Rc}
in
\Ker(\operatorname{div}_{f})
with respect to the weighted inner product
\int_{M}<\cdot,\cdot>e^{-f}\,dV
.

We divide the proof of Theorem
1.1
into two propositions.

Proposition 3.1
.

The subspaces
\mathrm{Im}(\operatorname{div}_{f}^{\dagger})
,
{\mathbb{R}}\cdot\mathrm{Rc}
,
\Ker(\operatorname{div}_{f})_{0}
are invariant subspaces of the linear operator
{\cal L}_{f}
. Moreover, (3.2) is an orthogonal decomposition with respect to the quadratic form
\delta^{2}_{g}\nu(h,h)
of the second variation in Theorem
2.1
.

Proof.

Firstly, by (
2.17
),

{\cal L}_{f}(\operatorname{div}_{f}^{\dagger}\omega)=\frac{1}{2}\operatorname{div}_{f}^{\dagger}(\Delta_{f}\omega+\frac{1}{2}\omega)\in\mathrm{Im}(\operatorname{div}_{f}^{\dagger}).

This shows that
\mathrm{Im}(\operatorname{div}_{f}^{\dagger})
is invariant under
{\cal L}_{f}
.

Next, from Lemma
2.1
(ii), we have

{\cal L}_{f}Rc=\frac{1}{2}Rc.

Hence,
{\mathbb{R}}\cdot{Rc}
is an invariant subspace of
{\cal L}_{f}
.

Finally, for any
h\in\Ker(\operatorname{div}_{f})_{0}
, it follows from (
2.19
) that

\operatorname{div}_{f}({\cal L}_{f}h)=\frac{1}{2}\left(\Delta_{f}\operatorname{div}_{f}h+\frac{1}{2}\operatorname{div}_{f}h\right)=0.

Moreover, since
{\cal L}_{f}Rc=\frac{1}{2}Rc
, it follows that

\displaystyle\int_{M}<{\cal L}_{f}h,Rc>\,e^{-f}dV=

\displaystyle\int_{M}<h,{\cal L}_{f}Rc>\,e^{-f}dV

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\int_{M}<h,Rc>\,e^{-f}dV=0,

i.e.,
{\cal L}_{f}h\in\Ker(\operatorname{div}_{f})_{0}
. Therefore,
\Ker(\operatorname{div}_{f})_{0}
is also invariant under
{\cal L}_{f}
.

Furthermore, the invariant subspace property just demonstrated together with the fact that
\mathrm{Im}(\operatorname{div}_{f}^{\dagger})
,
{\mathbb{R}}\cdot\mathrm{Rc}
, and
\Ker(\operatorname{div}_{f})_{0}
are mutually orthogonal to each other (with respect to the weighted inner product) immediately imply that
the decomposition (
1.9
) of
\mathrm{Sym}^{2}(T^{*}M)
is also orthogonal with respect to the second variation
\delta^{2}_{g}\nu(h,h)
of the
\nu
-entropy. ∎

Proposition 3.2
.

Let
(M^{n},g,f)
be a compact shrinking Ricci soliton satisfying (3.1). Then, the eigenvalues of
{\cal L}_{f}
on
\mathrm{Im}(\operatorname{div}_{f}^{\dagger})
are strictly less than
\frac{1}{4}.

Proof.

Suppose that
\lambda
is an eigenvalue of
{\cal L}_{f}
on
\mathrm{Im}(\operatorname{div}_{f}^{\dagger})
, and

{\cal L}_{f}(\mathscr{L}_{\omega^{\sharp}}g)=\lambda\mathscr{L}_{\omega^{\sharp}}g

for some
\mathscr{L}_{\omega^{\sharp}}g\equiv-2\operatorname{div}_{f}^{\dagger}\omega\in\mathrm{Im}(\operatorname{div}_{f}^{\dagger})
with
\mathscr{L}_{\omega^{\sharp}}g\neq 0
. We need to show
\lambda<\frac{1}{4}.

Since
N_{f}=0
on
\mathrm{Im}(\operatorname{div}_{f}^{\dagger})
by Lemma
2.3
, from (
2.23
), we have

(3.3)

\displaystyle 0=

\displaystyle N_{f}(\mathscr{L}_{\omega^{\sharp}}g)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\cal{\displaystyle L}_{f}(\mathscr{L}_{\omega^{\sharp}}g)+\operatorname{div}_{f}^{\dagger}\operatorname{div}_{f}\mathscr{L}_{\omega^{\sharp}}g+\nabla^{2}\operatorname{div}_{f}w

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lambda\mathscr{L}_{\omega^{\sharp}}g+\operatorname{div}_{f}^{\dagger}\operatorname{div}_{f}\mathscr{L}_{\omega^{\sharp}}g+\nabla^{2}\operatorname{div}_{f}\omega

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-2\lambda\operatorname{div}_{f}^{\dagger}\omega-2\operatorname{div}_{f}^{\dagger}\operatorname{div}_{f}\operatorname{div}_{f}^{\dagger}\omega-\operatorname{div}_{f}^{\dagger}\nabla\operatorname{div}_{f}\omega

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\operatorname{div}_{f}^{\dagger}(2\lambda\omega+2\operatorname{div}_{f}\operatorname{div}_{f}^{\dagger}\omega+\nabla\operatorname{div}_{f}\omega).

Claim.
The following identity holds,

(3.4)

\Delta_{f}\operatorname{div}_{f}\omega=(2\lambda-1)\operatorname{div}_{f}\omega.

Indeed, it follows from (
2.18
) that

2{\cal L}_{f}(\mathscr{L}_{\omega^{\sharp}}g)=\mathscr{L}_{(\Delta_{f}\omega+\frac{1}{2}\omega)^{\sharp}}g.

From (
2.21
), we know that

\hat{v}_{\mathscr{L}_{\omega^{\sharp}}g}=2\operatorname{div}_{f}\omega.

Here, for any symmetric 2-tensor
h
,
\hat{v}_{h}
is given by (
2.14
).
Hence,

\displaystyle 2\hat{v}_{{\cal L}_{f}(\mathscr{L}_{\omega^{\sharp}}g)}=

\displaystyle\hat{v}_{\mathscr{L}_{(\Delta_{f}\omega+\frac{1}{2}w)^{\sharp}}g}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2\operatorname{div}_{f}(\Delta_{f}\omega+\frac{1}{2}\omega)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2(\Delta_{f}\operatorname{div}_{f}\omega+\operatorname{div}_{f}\omega),

where, in the last step above, we have used (
2.16
).

Since

{\cal L}_{f}(\mathscr{L}_{\omega^{\sharp}}g)=\lambda\mathscr{L}_{\omega^{\sharp}}g
, we get

\displaystyle\Delta_{f}\operatorname{div}_{f}\omega+\operatorname{div}_{f}\omega=

\displaystyle\hat{v}_{{\cal L}_{f}(\mathscr{L}_{\omega^{\sharp}}g)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lambda\hat{v}_{\mathscr{L}_{\omega^{\sharp}}g}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2\lambda\operatorname{div}_{f}\omega,

i.e.,

\Delta_{f}\operatorname{div}_{f}\omega=(2\lambda-1)\operatorname{div}_{f}\omega.

This proves the Claim.

Now, we divide the rest of our argument into two cases.

Case 1:

\operatorname{div}_{f}\omega
is not a constant.

In this case, by the Claim,
\operatorname{div}_{f}\omega
is an eigenfunction of
\Delta_{f}
with eigenvalue
1-2\lambda
. On the other hand, from
[
12
]
, we know that the first eigenvalue of
\Delta_{f}
is greater than
1/2
. Thus,
1-2\lambda>\frac{1}{2}
; hence
\lambda<\frac{1}{4}
.

Case 2:

\operatorname{div}_{f}\omega
is a constant.

In this case, we have

\displaystyle\int_{M}|\operatorname{div}_{f}\omega|^{2}e^{-f}dV

\displaystyle=\int_{M}<\omega,\operatorname{div}_{f}^{\dagger}\operatorname{div}_{f}\omega>e^{-f}dV

\displaystyle=-\int_{M}<\omega,\nabla(\operatorname{div}_{f}\omega)>e^{-f}dV

\displaystyle=0.

It follows that
\operatorname{div}_{f}\omega=0
. So (
3.3
) becomes

\operatorname{div}_{f}^{\dagger}(\lambda\omega+\operatorname{div}_{f}\operatorname{div}_{f}^{\dagger}\omega)=0.

Multiplying both sides of the above identity by
\operatorname{div}_{f}^{\dagger}\omega
and integrating yields

\int_{M}\left(\lambda|\operatorname{div}_{f}^{\dagger}\omega|^{2}+|\operatorname{div}_{f}\operatorname{div}_{f}^{\dagger}\omega|^{2}\right)e^{-f}dV=0.

Since
\operatorname{div}_{f}^{\dagger}\omega=-\frac{1}{2}\mathscr{L}_{\omega^{\sharp}}g\neq 0
by assumption, we have
\lambda\leq 0<1/4
.

Therefore, we have shown that
\lambda<\frac{1}{4}
. This concludes the proof of Proposition
3.2
and Theorem 1.1.

∎

Finally, we are ready to prove Theorem
1.2
.

Proof.

By Theorem
2.1
, a compact shrinking Ricci soliton
(M^{n},g,f)
is linearly stable if and only if

\delta^{2}_{g}\nu(h,h):=\frac{1}{(4\pi)^{n/2}}\int_{M}<N_{f}h,h>e^{-f}dV\leq 0

for every
h\in\mathrm{Sym}^{2}(T^{*}M)=\ \mathrm{Im}(\operatorname{div}_{f}^{\dagger})\oplus{\mathbb{R}}\cdot\mathrm{Rc}\oplus\Ker(\operatorname{div}_{f})_{0}.

However, by Theorem
1.1
(i) (i.e., Proposition
3.1
), we have

\displaystyle\int_{M}<N_{f}h,h>e^{-f}dV=

\displaystyle\int_{M}<N_{f}h_{1},h_{1}>e^{-f}dV+\int_{M}<N_{f}h_{2},h_{2}>e^{-f}dV

\displaystyle+\int_{M}<N_{f}h_{0},h_{0}>e^{-f}dV

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{M}<N_{f}h_{2},h_{2}>e^{-f}dV+\int_{M}<N_{f}h_{0},h_{0}>e^{-f}dV,

where

h=h_{1}+h_{2}+h_{0},\quad{\mbox{with}}\ h_{1}\in\mathrm{Im}(\operatorname{div}_{f}^{\dagger}),\ h_{2}\in{\mathbb{R}}\cdot\mathrm{Rc},\ h_{0}\in\Ker(\operatorname{div}_{f})_{0},

and, in the last equality, we have used the fact that
\delta^{2}_{g}\nu(h_{1},h_{1})=0
for
h_{1}\in\mathrm{Im}(\operatorname{div}_{f}^{\dagger})
due to the diffeomorphism invariance of the
\nu
-entropy.

On the other hand,
since
\operatorname{div}_{f}Rc=0
and
{\cal L}_{f}Rc=\frac{1}{2}Rc
by Lemma
2.1
, we obtain

\displaystyle N_{f}(Rc)=

\displaystyle\cal{\displaystyle L}_{f}Rc-\frac{\int_{M}|Rc|^{2}e^{-f}dV}{\int_{M}R\,e^{-f}dV}Rc

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\cal{\displaystyle L}_{f}Rc-\frac{1}{2}Rc=0,

where we have used the fact that

\int_{M}|Rc|^{2}e^{-f}dV=\frac{1}{2}\int_{M}R\,e^{-f}dV,

because the scalar curvature
R
satisfies the well-known equation
\Delta_{f}R=R-2|Rc|^{2}
.
Hence,
N_{f}=0
on
{\mathbb{R}}\cdot\mathrm{Rc}
, and it follows that

\int_{M}<N_{f}h_{2},h_{2}>e^{-f}dV=0.

Also, as
N_{f}={\cal L}_{f}
on
\Ker(\operatorname{div}_{f})_{0}
, we immediately conclude that

\displaystyle\int_{M}<N_{f}h,h>e^{-f}dV=\int_{M}<N_{f}h_{0},h_{0}>e^{-f}dV

\displaystyle=\int_{M}<{\cal L}_{f}h_{0},h_{0}>e^{-f}dV.

Therefore,
\delta^{2}_{g}\nu(h,h)\leq 0
if and only if

\int_{M}<{\cal L}_{f}h_{0},h_{0}>e^{-f}dV\leq 0.

This finishes the proof of Theorem
1.2
. ∎

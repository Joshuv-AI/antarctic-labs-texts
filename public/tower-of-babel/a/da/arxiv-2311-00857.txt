Asymmetric Ramsey properties of randomly perturbed graphs
Emily Heath, Daniel McGinnis

Abstract
In this note, we investigate for various pairs of graphs ( H , G ) (H,G) the question of how many random edges must be added to a dense graph to guarantee that any red-blue coloring of the edges contains a red copy of H H or a blue copy of G G . We determine this perturbed Ramsey threshold for many new pairs of graphs and various ranges of densities, obtaining several generalizations of results obtained by Das and Treglown. In particular, we resolve the remaining cases toward determining the perturbed Ramsey threshold for pairs ( K t , K s ) (K_{t},K_{s}) where t ≥ s ≥ 5 t\geq s\geq 5 .

In this note, we determine the perturbed Ramsey threshold for several additional classes of graphs and densities. To state our results, we will need the following definition.

Definition 1.3
.

Given a density
0<d<1
, a number of colors
r\in\mathbb{N}
, and a sequence of graphs
(H_{1},\ldots,H_{r})
, the
perturbed Ramsey threshold probability

p(n;H_{1},\ldots,H_{r},d)
satisfies the following:

1.

If
p=p(n)=\omega(p(n;H_{1},\ldots,H_{r},d))
, then for any sequence
(G_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
of
n
-vertex graphs with density at least
d
, the graph
G_{n}\cup G(n,p)
is
(H_{1},\ldots,H_{r})
-Ramsey with high probability.

2.

If
p=p(n)=o(p(n;H_{1},\ldots,H_{r},d))
, then for some sequence
(G_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
of
n
-vertex graphs with density at least
d
, the graph
G_{n}\cup G(n,p)
is with high probability not
(H_{1},\ldots,H_{r})
-Ramsey.

If it is the case that every sufficiently large graph of density at least
d
is
(H_{1},\ldots,H_{r})
-Ramsey, then we define
p(n;H_{1},\ldots,H_{r},d)=0
. In the symmetric case where
H_{1}=\cdots=H_{r}=H
, we denote the threshold by
p(n;r,H,d)
.

Definition 2.1

Given

We will make use of the following well-known properties of

Lemma 2.2

Let

\varepsilon
-regular pair in a graph

1.

If

\left|\left\{(x_{1},x_{2},\dots,x_{\ell})\in A^{\ell}:|\cap_{i}N(x_{i})\cap B|\leq(d-\varepsilon)^{\ell}|B|\right\}\right|\leq\ell\varepsilon|A|^{\ell}.

2.

If

and

and
|B^{\prime}|\geq\alpha|B|

-regular pair of density

\varepsilon^{\prime}=\textrm{max}\{\varepsilon/\alpha,2\varepsilon\}

Main Theorems

In this section, we prove our two main results. Both of these theorems are stated somewhat generally, and we apply them to specific pairs of graphs in Section

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
k\geq 2
and
K
,
G
, and
H
be graphs satisfying the following properties.

1.

For every partition
V_{1},\dots,V_{k}
of

is a subgraph of some

2.

The graph obtained by taking

3.

m_{2}(K)\geq m_{2}(G)\geq 1
,
m_{2}(K)\geq m_{2}(H)\geq 1

is strictly 2-balanced with respect to
m_{2}(\cdot,G)
(respectively
m_{2}(\cdot,H)
) or
m_{2}(K)=m_{2}(G)
(respectively
m_{2}(K)=m_{2}(H)
).

4.

There is a graph

obtained by deleting 1 vertex from

m_{2}(K,H)\geq m_{2}(K^{\prime},G)
and
m_{2}(K^{\prime})\geq m_{2}(G)

m_{2}(K^{\prime})=m_{2}(G)

5.

.

Then for
\frac{k-2}{k-1}<d\leq\frac{k-1}{k}

p\left(n;K,G,d\right)=n^{-1/m_{2}\left(K,H\right)}.

Proof.

First we consider the lower bound. Let
p=o\left(n^{-1/m_{2}\left(K,H\right)}\right)
and
\frac{k-2}{k-1}<d\leq\frac{k-1}{k}

\Gamma_{n}
be the balanced complete

\Gamma_{n}
blue. Since the pair of graphs
(K,H)
satisfies the
0

1.2
, there exists a 2-coloring of
G(n,p)
that does not have a red copy of
K
or a blue copy of
H
. Color the remaining edges of
\Gamma_{n}\cup G(n,p)
according to this coloring. Then
\Gamma_{n}\cup G(n,p)
certainly contains no red
K
, and since any
k
-partition of
G
contains
H
in one of its partite sets,
\Gamma_{n}\cup G(n,p)
also contains no blue
G
.

For the upper bound, let
p=\omega\left(n^{-1/m_{2}\left(K,H\right)}\right)
and
\frac{k-2}{k-1}<d\leq\frac{k-1}{k}
. Let
t=|V(K)|
and
h=|V(H)|
. By Theorem
2.3

\delta=d-\frac{k-2}{k-1}
, and
\varepsilon>0

n

\Gamma

n
sufficiently large, there exist
k
disjoint vertex sets
V_{1},\dots,V_{k}
such that
|V_{1}|=\cdots=|V_{k}|=\eta n
for some
\eta>0
, and for
1\leq i<j\leq k
the pair
(V_{i},V_{j})
is
\varepsilon
-regular of density at least
\delta/2
. Let
\gamma=\gamma(K,H)
be as in Theorem
2.6
.

We now prove by induction that with high probability, in any red-blue coloring of
\Gamma\cup G(n,p)
, either there is a red copy of
K
, or we may a find a blue copy of
H
within each vertex set

1.

Then for

For the lower bound, let

[

and

, there are vertex sets

for all

4
Applications of Theorems
3.1
and
3.2

In this section, we show that Theorem
3.1
can be applied when
K
is a complete graph and
G
is a complete graph minus a perfect matching. Denoting by
K_{s}^{\prime}
the complete graph on
s
vertices minus a maximum matching, we determine the threshold function
p(n;K_{t},K_{s}^{\prime},d)
for the range of densities
\frac{k-2}{k-1}\leq d\leq\frac{k-1}{k}
for each integer
k
such that
2\leq k<\frac{s}{3}
.

Corollary 4.1
.

Let
K_{s}^{\prime}
be the graph obtained by deleting a maximum matching from the complete graph
K_{s}
. For an integer
2\leq k<\frac{s}{3}
and in the range of densities
\frac{k-2}{k-1}\leq d\leq\frac{k-1}{k}
, we have that for
t\geq s\geq 7
,

p(n;K_{t},K_{s}^{\prime},d)=n^{-1/m_{2}(K_{t},K_{\lceil s/k\rceil}^{\prime})}.

Proof.

It suffices to show that the hypothesis of Theorem
3.1
is satisfied when we take
K=K_{t}
,
G=K_{s}^{\prime}
and
H=K_{\lceil s/k\rceil}^{\prime}
. It is clear that in every partition of
V(K_{s}^{\prime})
into
k
sets
V_{1},\dots,V_{k}
, some part contains
K_{\lceil s/k\rceil}^{\prime}
, and that taking
k
copies of
K_{\lceil s/k\rceil}^{\prime}
and adding all possible edges between each pair of copies contains
K_{s}^{\prime}
.

Routine calculation shows that
m_{2}(K_{t})\geq m_{2}(K_{s}^{\prime}),m_{2}(K_{\lceil s/k\rceil}^{\prime})
and that
K_{t}
is strictly 2-balanced with respect to
m_{2}(\cdot,K_{s}^{\prime})
and
m_{2}(\cdot,K_{\lceil s/k\rceil}^{\prime})
.

Similarly,
m_{2}(K_{t-1})\geq m_{2}(K_{s}^{\prime})
, and
K_{t-1}
is strictly 2-balanced with respect to
K_{s}^{\prime}
. It is also routine to check that
m_{2}(K_{t},K_{\lceil s/k\rceil}^{\prime})\geq m_{2}(K_{t-1},K_{s}^{\prime})
.

Finally, by Theorem 1.5 in
[
14
]
, the 0-statement for Conjecture
1.2
holds for the pair
(K_{t},K_{\lceil s/k\rceil}^{\prime})
.

Therefore, we may apply Theorem
3.1
, and this completes the proof.
∎

Remark
.

We require that
k<\frac{s}{3}
in Theorem
3.1
since if
k\geq\lceil\frac{s}{3}\rceil
. then
K_{\lceil\frac{s}{3}\rceil}^{\prime}
is a subgraph of the path on 3 vertices. Hence,
m_{2}(K_{\lceil\frac{s}{3}\rceil}^{\prime})\leq 1
, so the statement of Conjecture
1.2
does not apply to the pair
(K_{t},K_{\lceil\frac{s}{3}\rceil}^{\prime})
when
k\geq\frac{s}{3}
.

Additionally, in
[
18
]
Das and Treglown proved that for an integer
2\leq k<\frac{s}{2}
and for the range of densities
\frac{k-2}{k-1}\leq d\leq\frac{k-1}{k}
, if
k=2
or
s\equiv 1
(mod
k
), then

p(n;K_{t},K_{s},d)=n^{-1/m_{2}(K_{t},K_{\lceil s/k\rceil})}.

However, if
k\geq 3
and
s\not\equiv 1
(mod
k
), they only show that

p(n;K_{t},K_{s},d)=n^{-(1-o(1))/m_{2}(K_{t},K_{\lceil s/k\rceil})}.

We are able to use Theorem
3.1
to remove the

term in the expression, and thus obtain an exact threshold function when

and

(mod
k
).

Corollary 4.2
.

Let
s,t,k
be integers with

and

, and let

p(n;K_{t},K_{s},d)=n^{-1/m_{2}(K_{t},K_{\lceil s/k\rceil})}.

The proof is similar to the proof of Corollary

.
∎

Das and Treglown also determined the threshold function for pairs of the form

[

Corollary 4.3

Given a tree

This follows from applying Theorem

when

and

is obtained by deleting

Here,

Note that

Notice that

for

where the vertices

all have the same color, say red. Let

be the set of red vertices and

be the set of blue vertices, and add all possible edges in between

and
V_{2}
. Then add

, add an edge between

∎

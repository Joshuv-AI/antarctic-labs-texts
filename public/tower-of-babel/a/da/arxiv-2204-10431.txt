Abstract
. Given a finite cocommutative Hopf algebra A A over a commutative regular ring R R , the lattice of localising tensor ideals of the stable category of Gorenstein projective A A -modules is described in terms of the corresponding lattices for the fibres of A A over the spectrum of R R . Under certain natural conditions on the cohomology of A A over R R , this yields a stratification of the stable category. These results apply when A A is the group algebra over R R of a finite group, and also when A A is the exterior algebra on a finite free R R -module.

1.
Introduction

Following the seminal work of Hopkins
[
22
]
and Neeman
[
32
]
in stable homotopy theory and commutative algebra, much attention has been paid in the past few decades to the problem of classifying the thick subcategories of finite dimensional representations over various families of algebras, and also of the localising subcategories of all representations. In terms of the language and machinery developed in
[
9
,
10
]
, the goal is to prove stratification theorems. For example, in the case of modular representations of a finite group, the thick tensor ideal subcategories of the small stable module category were classified in
[
7
]
, while the tensor ideal localising subcategories of the large stable module category were classified in
[
11
]
. These results were generalised to cover all finite group schemes over fields in
[
14
]
.

In this paper we address the problem of change of coefficients, with a
focus on representations of group algebras of finite group schemes,
and in particular, of finite groups. Let
A
be the group algebra of
a finite group scheme over a commutative noetherian ring
R
; in other
words,
A
is a finite cocommutative Hopf algebra over
R
. For
example,
A
could be the group algebra
RG
of a finite group

G
. The appropriate analogue of the stable category of finite
dimensional representations over a finite group, or group scheme, is
the singularity category of
A
, in the sense of
Buchweitz
[
16
]
and Orlov
[
35
]
. By a
result of Buchweitz, this is equivalent to the stable category of
Gorenstein projective
A
-modules:

\operatorname{\underline{Gproj}}A\xrightarrow{\ \raisebox{-1.20552pt}[0.0pt][0.0pt]{$\scriptstyle{\sim}$}\ }\mathbf{D}_{\mathrm{sg}}(A)\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\mathbf{D}^{\mathrm{b}}(\operatorname{mod}A)/\mathbf{D}^{\mathrm{perf}}(A)\,.

An
A
-module is said to be Gorenstein projective if it occurs as a syzygy in an acyclic complex of projective
A
-modules. The Gorenstein projective modules in
\operatorname{mod}A
form a Frobenius category, with projective-injective objects the projective
A
-modules;
\operatorname{\underline{Gproj}}A
is the corresponding stable category. We have also to consider all Gorenstein projectives, not only the finitely generated ones, and the corresponding stable category
\operatorname{\underline{GProj}}A
. The category
\operatorname{\underline{GProj}}A
is triangulated and compactly generated; the subcategory of compact objects is equivalent to
\operatorname{\underline{Gproj}}A
.

From now on assume that
R
is regular, for example,
R=\mathbb{Z}
. In this
case, a finitely generated
A
-module is Gorenstein projective
precisely when it is projective when viewed as an
R
-module. The same
holds also for infinitely generated
A
-modules when in addition

\dim R
is finite, that is to say, when
R
has finite global
dimension; see Lemma
3.10
. Since
A
is a Hopf
algebra over
R
, the tensor product over
R
induces on
\operatorname{\underline{Gproj}}A

the structure of a tensor-triangulated category and also on

\operatorname{\underline{GProj}}A
. Moreover
\operatorname{\underline{GProj}}A
is rigidly compactly generated. Our goal is to classify the thick tensor ideals of

\operatorname{\underline{Gproj}}A
and the localising tensor ideals of
\operatorname{\underline{GProj}}A
.

One approach would be to extend methods developed in the case where
R
is a field to cover more general coefficient rings. In this work,
we take a different tack, by viewing
A
as a family of Hopf algebras parameterised by
\operatorname{Spec}R
, the Zariski spectrum of
R
. The fiber
over each point
\mathfrak{p}
in
\operatorname{Spec}R
is the finite dimensional Hopf algebra
{A}_{k(\mathfrak{p})}\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}A\otimes_{R}k(\mathfrak{p})
, where
k(\mathfrak{p})

is the residue field at
\mathfrak{p}
. The results of
[
14
]
apply to yield a stratification of
\operatorname{\underline{GProj}}{A}_{k(\mathfrak{p})}
in terms of the projective spectrum of the cohomology ring of
{A}_{k(\mathfrak{p})}
. Then the task becomes one of ‘patching’ these local stratifications to obtain a global stratification of
\operatorname{\underline{GProj}}A
in terms of the projective spectrum of the cohomology ring of
A
.

There are two aspects to this task: one representation theoretic and
the other purely cohomological. The former is completely solved by the
result below that can be viewed as a fibrewise criterion for detecting
membership in localising tensor ideals. We deduce it from Theorem
4.6
that deals with the full homotopy category of projective
A
-modules.

1.1 Theorem
.

Let
R
be a regular ring,
A
a finite cocommutative Hopf
R
-algebra, and
M,N
Gorenstein projective
A
-modules. The conditions below are equivalent.

(1)

M\in\operatorname{Loc}^{\otimes}(N)
in
\operatorname{\underline{GProj}}A
;

(2)

{M}_{k(\mathfrak{p})}\in\operatorname{Loc}^{\otimes}({N}_{k(\mathfrak{p})})
in
\operatorname{\underline{GProj}}{A}_{k(\mathfrak{p})}
for each
\mathfrak{p}\in\operatorname{Spec}R
.

The cohomological aspect concerns the relationship between the fibres of the cohomology algebra
S\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\operatorname{Ext}^{*}_{A}(R,R)
of
A
and the cohomology algebra of the fibres of the
R
-algebra
A
. Namely, for each
\mathfrak{p}
in
\operatorname{Spec}R
there is natural map

\kappa_{\mathfrak{p}}\colon S\otimes_{R}k(\mathfrak{p})\longrightarrow\operatorname{Ext}_{{A}_{k(\mathfrak{p})}}^{*}(k(\mathfrak{p}),k(\mathfrak{p}))

of
k(\mathfrak{p})
-algebras. The question is when this map induces a bijection on spectra. Its import is clear from the next result.

1.2 Theorem
.

Let
R
be a regular ring and
A
a finite cocommutative Hopf algebra over
R
such that the
R
-algebra
S
is finitely generated. If the map
\kappa_{\mathfrak{p}}
induces a bijection on spectra for each
\mathfrak{p}
in
\operatorname{Spec}R
, then the tensor-triangulated category

\operatorname{\underline{GProj}}A
is stratified by the action of
S
, and the support of

\operatorname{\underline{GProj}}A
is
\operatorname{Proj}S
.

This result is proved at the end of Section
4
,
where we also recall what it means for a tensor-triangulated to be
stratified by an action of a ring. For now it suffices to record that
the result above, when it applies, yields the sought after
classification of localising tensor ideals of
\operatorname{\underline{GProj}}A
and of the
thick tensor ideals of
\operatorname{\underline{Gproj}}A
.

As to the hypothesis on
A
in the theorem above: We expect that
finite generation holds for all finite cocommutative Hopf algebras. We
do not know if the hypothesis on
\kappa^{a}_{\mathfrak{p}}
does as well; we
rather suspect that it does not. It does hold for the group algebra

A=RG
over a finite group. This is a result of Benson and Habegger
[
8
]
. The proof there is lacking detail, so we provide a full proof here in greater generality; see Theorem
5.5
, and also the recent work of Lau
[
31
, Section 7]
. Putting this together with Theorem
1.2
yields the following stratification result.

1.3 Theorem
.

With
G
a finite group and
R
a regular ring, the
tensor-triangulated category
\operatorname{\underline{GProj}}RG
is stratified by the action of the group cohomology ring
H^{*}(G,R)
.

In fact we prove this stratification result for the slightly bigger
compactly generated tensor-triangulated category
\mathbf{K}(\operatorname{Proj}RG)

consisting of complexes of projective
RG
-modules up to homotopy. The
subcategory of compact objects identifies with the bounded derived
category
\mathbf{D}^{\mathrm{b}}(\operatorname{mod}RG)
. The resulting classification of thick tensor ideals of
\mathbf{D}^{\mathrm{b}}(\operatorname{mod}RG)

has been obtained by Lau
[
31
]
using different
methods. Building on his work, and again using very different methods,
Barthel
[
3
,
4
]
established a classification of
localising tensor ideals of
RG
-modules that are projective as
R
-modules, which is closely related to Theorem
1.3
.

Another case where Theorem
1.2
applies is
when
A
is an exterior algebra, over a finite free
R
-module,
regarded as a
\mathbb{Z}/2
-graded Hopf algebra; see
Example
4.14
.

So far we have focussed on Hopf algebras, but in fact an appropriate
version of Theorem
1.1
holds for any arbitrary
finite projective
R
-algebras
A
; see Theorem
2.1
. In
such contexts the natural cohomology ring to consider, vis-à-vis
stratification, is the Hochschild cohomology of
A
over
R
. It is
plausible that the analogue of the map
\kappa^{a}_{\mathfrak{p}}
in that context
is the key to patching fibrewise stratification, when available, to
get a global stratification result for
A
.

2.
A fibrewise criterion for localising subcategories

Throughout
R
is a commutative noetherian ring and
A
a
finite projective
R
-algebra
; that is to say
A
is an
R
-algebra that is finitely generated and projective as an
R
-module. In particular, as a ring
A
is noetherian on the left and on the right. Given an
A
-complex
X
and a point
\mathfrak{p}
in
\operatorname{Spec}R
, the Zariski spectrum of
R
, set

{X}_{k(\mathfrak{p})}\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}X\otimes_{R}k(\mathfrak{p})

viewed as a complex of
{A}_{k(\mathfrak{p})}
-modules. The assignment
X\mapsto{X}_{k(\mathfrak{p})}
is exact on the homotopy category of
A
-complexes, and hence also on any of its subcategories, in particular, on
\mathbf{K}(\operatorname{Proj}A)
, the homotopy category of projective
A
-modules. The latter has a natural structure of a triangulated category, with arbitrary coproducts. Given an
A
-complex
Y
in
\mathbf{K}(\operatorname{Proj}A)
we write
\operatorname{Loc}(Y)
for the localising subcategory of the homotopy category generated by
Y
. The main result in this section is the following
fibrewise criterion
for detecting objects in
\operatorname{Loc}(Y)
.

2.1 Theorem
.

Suppose that
R
is regular. Let
A
be a finite projective
R
-algebra, and let
X,Y
be objects in
\mathbf{K}(\operatorname{Proj}A)
. The following conditions are equivalent.

(1)

X\in\operatorname{Loc}(Y)
in
\mathbf{K}(\operatorname{Proj}A)
;

(2)

{X}_{k(\mathfrak{p})}\in\operatorname{Loc}({Y}_{k(\mathfrak{p})})
in
\mathbf{K}(\operatorname{Proj}{A}_{k(\mathfrak{p})})
for each
\mathfrak{p}\in\operatorname{Spec}R
.

The ring
R
is by definition
regular
if every finitely
generated
R
-module has finite projective dimension. An equivalent
condition is that the ring
R_{\mathfrak{p}}
has finite global dimension for
each
\mathfrak{p}
in
\operatorname{Spec}R
. The global dimension of
R
is then equal to

\dim R
, its Krull dimension.

The statement above is inspired by,
and extends, an analogous statement for the derived category of
A
,
established in
[
21
]
. It yields also a
statement about the stable category of Gorenstein projective

A
-modules; see Theorem
3.7
.

The proof of the theorem above takes some preparation and is given towards the end of this section. We start by recalling some properties of the homotopy category of projective modules.

The homotopy category of projectives

For the moment,
A
can be any ring that is noetherian on both sides;
that is to say,
A
is noetherian as a left and as a right
A
-module.
For us,
A
-modules mean left
A
-modules, and
{A}^{\mathrm{op}}
-modules are
identified with right
A
-modules. When
A
is an
R
-algebra for some
commutative ring
R
, then it is convenient
to write for any
A
-module the
R
-action on the opposite side. We
denote by
\operatorname{Mod}A
the (abelian) category of
A
-modules and by

\operatorname{mod}A
its full subcategory consisting of finitely generated
modules. The full subcategory of
\operatorname{Mod}A
consisting of projective
modules is denoted
\operatorname{Proj}A
.

For any additive category
\mathcal{A}\subseteq\operatorname{Mod}A
, like the ones in the last paragraph,
\mathbf{K}(\mathcal{A})
will denote the associated homotopy category, with its natural structure as a triangulated category. Morphisms in this category are denoted
\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbf{K}(A)}(-,-)
. An object
X
in
\mathbf{K}(\mathcal{A})
is
acyclic
if
H^{*}(X)=0
, and the full subcategory of acyclic objects in
\mathbf{K}(\mathcal{A})
is denoted
\mathbf{K}_{\mathrm{ac}}(\mathcal{A})
.

Let
\mathbf{D}(\operatorname{Mod}A)
denote the derived category of
A
-modules and
\mathbf{D}^{\mathrm{b}}(\operatorname{mod}A)
the bounded derived category of
\operatorname{mod}A
. Let
\mathbf{q}\colon\mathbf{K}(\operatorname{Mod}A)\to\mathbf{D}(\operatorname{Mod}A)
be the localisation functor; its kernel is
\mathbf{K}_{\mathrm{ac}}(\operatorname{Mod}A)
. We write
\mathbf{q}
also for its restriction to the homotopy category of projective modules. This functor has an adjoint:

(2.2)

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbf{K}(\operatorname{Proj}A)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbf{D}(\operatorname{Mod}A)\,.}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbf{q}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbf{p}}

Our convention is to write the left adjoint above the corresponding right one. In what follows it is convenient to conflate
\mathbf{p}
with
\mathbf{p}\circ\mathbf{q}
.
The image of
\mathbf{p}
, denoted
\mathbf{K}_{\mathrm{proj}}(A)
, consists precisely of the K-projective complexes, namely, those complexes
P
such that
\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbf{K}(A)}(P,-)=0
on acyclic complexes in
\mathbf{K}(\operatorname{Mod}A)
.

Compact objects

The category
\mathbf{K}(\operatorname{Proj}A)
is triangulated, admits arbitrary direct sums, and is compactly generated. As in any triangulated category with arbitrary direct sums, an object
X
in
\mathbf{K}(\operatorname{Proj}A)
is
compact
if
\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbf{K}(A)}(X,-)
commutes with direct sums. The compact objects in
\mathbf{K}(\operatorname{Proj}A)
form a thick subcategory,
denoted
\mathbf{K}^{\mathrm{c}}(\operatorname{Proj}A)
. The assignment
X\mapsto\operatorname{Hom}_{{A}^{\mathrm{op}}}(\mathbf{p}M,A)
induces an equivalence

(2.3)

\iota_{A}\colon{\mathbf{D}^{\mathrm{b}}(\operatorname{mod}{A}^{\mathrm{op}})}^{\mathrm{op}}\xrightarrow{\ \raisebox{-1.20552pt}[0.0pt][0.0pt]{$\scriptstyle{\sim}$}\ }\mathbf{K}^{\mathrm{c}}(\operatorname{Proj}A).

This result is due to
Jørgensen
[
28
, Theorem 3.2]
; see also

[
25
]
.

Regular rings

Recall that the ring
R
is regular if every finitely generated

R
-module has finite projective dimension, that is, every complex in

\mathbf{D}^{\mathrm{b}}(\operatorname{mod}R)
is perfect. We record a couple of basic facts for later
use.

2.4 Lemma
.

Let
R
be a commutative noetherian ring.

(1)

The ring
R
is regular if and only if every
acyclic complex in
\mathbf{K}(\operatorname{Proj}R)
is null-homotopic.

(2)

Suppose that
R
is regular and local of Krull dimension
d
with residue field

k
. Then
\operatorname{RHom}_{R}(k,R)\xrightarrow{\raisebox{-1.20552pt}[0.0pt][0.0pt]{$\scriptstyle{\sim}$}}\Sigma^{d}k
.

Proof.

(1) The functor
\mathbf{p}
in (
2.2
) restricted to compact objects embeds the perfect complexes over
R
into
\mathbf{D}^{\mathrm{b}}(\operatorname{mod}R)
, via (
2.3
). This embedding is an equivalence if and only
\mathbf{p}
is an equivalence. Clearly,
\mathbf{p}
is an
equivalence if and only if
\mathbf{q}
is an equivalence. See also
[
24
]
.

(2) The Koszul complex
K
on a minimal generating set for the maximal
ideal of
R
provides a projective resolution
K\to k
. Since
\operatorname{Hom}_{R}(K,R)\cong\Sigma^{-d}K
, by

[
15
, Proposition 1.6.10]
, the stated assertion follows.
∎

Fibres and the Koszul complex

We return to the context of finite projective algebras over a
commutative ring
R
and wish to describe the functor

-\otimes_{R}k(\mathfrak{p})
for each prime
\mathfrak{p}
in
\operatorname{Spec}R
via the Koszul
complex for
\mathfrak{p}
. We reduce to the local case.

Let
(R,\mathfrak{m},k)
be a local ring, with maximal ideal
\mathfrak{m}
and residue
field
k
. In this paragraph, we write
A_{k}
instead of

A_{k(\mathfrak{m})}
. Let
\pi
be the functor from
\mathbf{K}(\operatorname{Proj}A)
to

\mathbf{K}(\operatorname{Proj}A_{k})
given by the assignment

X\mapsto X_{k}\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}X\otimes_{R}k
. It is clear that
\pi

preserves coproducts and so has a right adjoint by Brown
representability, say
\pi_{r}
. Thus there is
the adjoint pair

(2.5)

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbf{K}(\operatorname{Proj}A)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbf{K}(\operatorname{Proj}A_{k})\,.}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\pi}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\pi_{r}}

The following observation is useful in the sequel.

2.6 Lemma
.

Consider an adjoint pair of functors

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{T}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{U}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\pi}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\pi_{r}}
between compactly generated triangulated categories. Then
\pi

preserves all coproducts if and only if
\pi

preserves compact objects. In that case the restriction

\pi^{c}\colon{\mathcal{T}}^{c}\to{\mathcal{U}}^{c}
admits a right adjoint if
and only if
\pi_{r}
preserves compact objects.

Proof.

The first assertion is well-known; see
[
34
, Theorem 5.1]
. Now assume that
\pi

preserves compact objects and that
\pi^{c}\colon{\mathcal{T}}^{c}\to{\mathcal{U}}^{c}
admits a right adjoint, say
\rho\colon{\mathcal{U}}^{c}\to{\mathcal{T}}^{c}
. Then for any
X\in\mathcal{T}^{c}
and
Y\in\mathcal{U}^{c}
the natural
bijection

\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal{T}}(X,\rho(Y))\cong\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal{U}}(\pi(X),Y)\cong\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal{T}}(X,\pi_{r}(Y))

maps the identity
\operatorname{id}_{\rho(Y)}
to a morphism
\phi\colon\rho(Y)\to\pi_{r}(Y)
. The map
\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal{T}}(X,\phi)
is a
bijection for all
X
. Since
\mathcal{T}
is compactly generated,
\phi
is an isomorphism.

The other implication is clear.
∎

We need to understand the unit
\operatorname{id}\to\pi_{r}\pi
and counit

\pi\pi_{r}\to\operatorname{id}
of the adjunction (
2.5
). To that
end, consider the Koszul complex,
K
, on a minimal generating set for
the ideal
\mathfrak{m}
. The map
R\to K
of
R
-complexes induces a natural
map

(2.7)

X\cong X\otimes_{R}R\longrightarrow X\otimes_{R}K\qquad\text{for $X$ in $\mathbf{K}(\operatorname{Proj}A)$.}

On the other hand, the augmentation
R\to k
factors through
R\to K

via a map
K\to k
, which induces

V\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}K\otimes_{R}k\to k\otimes_{R}k\xrightarrow{\raisebox{-1.20552pt}[0.0pt][0.0pt]{$\scriptstyle{\sim}$}}k
and therefore a
natural map

(2.8)

V\otimes_{k}Y\longrightarrow k\otimes_{k}Y\cong Y\qquad\text{for $Y$ in $\mathbf{K}(\operatorname{Proj}A_{k})$.}

The result below is the key to the proof of Theorem
2.1
and also in other computations that follow.

2.9 Lemma
.

When
R
is regular, the following statements hold.

(1)

Both
\pi
and
\pi_{r}
preserve compact objects and arbitrary direct sums.

(2)

Restricting the adjunction (
2.5
) to compact objects gives the adjunction

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbf{D}^{\mathrm{b}}(\operatorname{mod}{A}^{\mathrm{op}})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbf{D}^{\mathrm{b}}(\operatorname{mod}({A}^{\mathrm{op}}_{k}))}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces-\otimes^{\mathrm{L}}_{R}k}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Sigma^{-d}\rho}

where
\rho
is induced by restriction along the map
A\to A_{k}
and
d\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\dim R
.

(3)

The maps (
2.7
) and (
2.8
) are the unit and the counit, respectively, of the adjunction (
2.5
).

(4)

For
X\in\mathbf{K}(\operatorname{Proj}A)
and
Y\in\mathbf{K}(\operatorname{Proj}A_{k})
, there are natural isomorphisms

\pi_{r}\pi(X)\cong X\otimes_{R}K\qquad\text{and}\qquad\pi\pi_{r}(Y)\cong V\otimes_{k}Y\,.

In particular,
\pi\pi_{r}(Y)
is isomorphic to a finite direct sum of shifts of
Y
.

Proof.

(1) and (2) We have already observed that
\pi
preserves arbitrary direct sums. We verify that
\pi
preserves compacts, equivalently that its right adjoint,
\pi_{r}
, preserves arbitrary direct sums. Any compact object in
\mathbf{K}(\operatorname{Proj}A)
is of the form
\iota_{A}M=\operatorname{Hom}_{A}(\mathbf{p}_{{A}^{\mathrm{op}}}M,A)
for some
M\in\mathbf{D}^{\mathrm{b}}(\operatorname{mod}{A}^{\mathrm{op}})
. A direct computation gives

\displaystyle\pi\operatorname{Hom}_{A}(\mathbf{p}_{{A}^{\mathrm{op}}}M,A)

\displaystyle=\operatorname{Hom}_{A}(\mathbf{p}_{{A}^{\mathrm{op}}}M,A)\otimes_{R}k

\displaystyle\cong\operatorname{Hom}_{A_{k}}((\mathbf{p}_{{A}^{\mathrm{op}}}M)\otimes_{R}k,A_{k})

\displaystyle\cong\operatorname{Hom}_{A_{k}}(\mathbf{p}_{{A}^{\mathrm{op}}}(k\otimes^{\mathrm{L}}_{R}M),A_{k})\,.

Since
R
is regular,
k
is in
\operatorname{Thick}(R)
in
\mathbf{D}(\operatorname{Mod}R)
and hence
k\otimes^{\mathrm{L}}_{R}M
is in
\operatorname{Thick}(M)
in
\mathbf{D}^{\mathrm{b}}(\operatorname{mod}{A}^{\mathrm{op}})
. Therefore the
A_{k}
-module
H^{*}(k\otimes^{\mathrm{L}}_{R}M)
is finitely generated, that is to say,
k\otimes^{\mathrm{L}}_{R}M
is in
\mathbf{D}^{\mathrm{b}}(\operatorname{mod}{A}^{\mathrm{op}}_{k})
. Thus the complex

\operatorname{Hom}_{A_{k}}(\mathbf{p}_{{A}^{\mathrm{op}}}(k\otimes^{\mathrm{L}}_{R}M),A_{k})=\iota_{A_{k}}(k\otimes^{\mathrm{L}}_{R}M)

in
\mathbf{K}(\operatorname{Proj}A_{k})
is compact. This justifies the claim that
\pi
preserves compacts. Along the way we have established that

\pi\iota_{A}(M)\cong\iota_{A_{k}}(k\otimes^{\mathrm{L}}_{R}M)

for
M
in
\mathbf{D}^{\mathrm{b}}(\operatorname{mod}{A}^{\mathrm{op}})
. In other words, restricted to compacts
\pi
is the functor

k\otimes^{\mathrm{L}}_{R}-\colon\mathbf{D}^{\mathrm{b}}(\operatorname{mod}{A}^{\mathrm{op}})\longrightarrow\mathbf{D}^{\mathrm{b}}(\operatorname{mod}{A}^{\mathrm{op}}_{k})

via the identification in (
2.3
). The functor
\Sigma^{-d}\rho
is left adjoint to the functor above:

\displaystyle\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbf{D}(A)}(\Sigma^{-d}\rho N,M)

\displaystyle\cong\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbf{D}(A_{k})}(\Sigma^{-d}N,\operatorname{RHom}_{R}(k,M))

\displaystyle\cong\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbf{D}(A_{k})}(\Sigma^{-d}N,\operatorname{RHom}_{R}(k,R)\otimes^{\mathrm{L}}_{R}M)

At this point we can apply Lemma

(3) The canonical map

3.
Gorenstein algebras

Let

be a commutative noetherian ring. Following

Proof.

By

, Theorem 4.6]
, the

is Gorenstein if and only if the

-bimodule
\operatorname{Hom}_{R}(A,R)
is perfect on both sides. Since the

. Our
primary example is the group algebra of a finite group scheme over

R
.

For a module
M
we denote by
\operatorname{add}(M)
the full subcategory of finite
direct sums of copies of
M
plus their direct summands.

3.8 Lemma
.

Let
R
be a Gorenstein ring and
A
a finite projective
R
-algebra. The conditions below are equivalent.

(1)

The
R
-algebra
A
is fibrewise self-injective;

(2)

\mathrm{add}(\omega_{A/R})=\operatorname{add}(A)
and
\mathrm{add}(\omega_{{A}^{\mathrm{op}}/R})=\operatorname{add}({A}^{\mathrm{op}})
;

(3)

The dualising bimodule
\omega_{A/R}
is projective on the left and on the right.

If they hold the
R
-algebra
A
is Gorenstein, and one has an equivalences of categories

\omega_{A/R}\otimes_{A}(-)\colon\operatorname{Proj}A\xrightarrow{\ \raisebox{-1.20552pt}[0.0pt][0.0pt]{$\scriptstyle{\sim}$}\ }\operatorname{Proj}A

with inverse
\operatorname{Hom}_{A}(\omega_{A/R},-)
, and similarly for
\operatorname{Proj}{A}^{\mathrm{op}}
.

Proof.

In what follows we use the observation that for each
\mathfrak{p}
in

\operatorname{Spec}R
one has an isomorphism of
{A}_{k(\mathfrak{p})}
-bimodules:

\omega_{A/R}\otimes_{R}k(\mathfrak{p})\cong\omega_{{{A}_{k(\mathfrak{p})}}/k(\mathfrak{p})}\,.

Hence the
A
-bimodule
\omega_{A/R}
is projective on either side if
and only if the
{A}_{k(\mathfrak{p})}
-bimodule

\omega_{{{A}_{k(\mathfrak{p})}}/k(\mathfrak{p})}
is projective on either side for
each
\mathfrak{p}
in
\operatorname{Spec}R
; see Lemma
2.19
.

(1)
\Rightarrow
(2) It suffices to prove that
A
is in

\operatorname{add}(\omega_{A/R})
, equivalently that the map

\omega_{A/R}\otimes_{A}\operatorname{Hom}_{A}(\omega_{A/R},A)\longrightarrow A

given by evaluation, is surjective. Since an
R
-module
M
is zero if and only if
{M}_{k(\mathfrak{p})}
is zero for each
\mathfrak{p}
in
\operatorname{Spec}R
, it suffices to check the surjectivity of the map on the fibres, that is to say, the map

\omega_{{A}_{k(\mathfrak{p})}/k(\mathfrak{p})}\otimes_{{A}_{k(\mathfrak{p})}}\operatorname{Hom}_{{A}_{k(\mathfrak{p})}}(\omega_{{A}_{k(\mathfrak{p})}/k(\mathfrak{p})},{{A}_{k(\mathfrak{p})}})\longrightarrow{{A}_{k(\mathfrak{p})}}

is surjective. This holds as the
k(\mathfrak{p})
-algebra
{{A}_{k(\mathfrak{p})}}
is self-injective.

(2)
\Rightarrow
(3) is clear.

(3)
\Rightarrow
(1) Given the isomorphism above, condition (3) yields
that the dualising bimodule of
{A}_{k(\mathfrak{p})}
over
k(\mathfrak{p})
is
projective, that is to say,
{A}_{k(\mathfrak{p})}
is self-injective.

It remains to verify the last part of the statement. The Gorenstein
property follows from Proposition
3.1
. The equivalence
follows from the fact that
\omega_{A/R}
is projective on both sides
and the isomorphism

A\xrightarrow{\ \raisebox{-1.20552pt}[0.0pt][0.0pt]{$\scriptstyle{\sim}$}\ }\operatorname{Hom}_{A}(\omega_{A/R},\omega_{A/R})\,.

See also
[
26
, Theorem 4.5]
.
∎

3.9 Proposition
.

Let
R
be a Gorenstein ring and
A
a fibrewise self-injective
R
-algebra. Then

\operatorname{inj{.}dim}_{A}A=\dim_{R}A=\operatorname{inj{.}dim}_{{A}^{\mathrm{op}}}{{A}^{\mathrm{op}}}\,.

In particular,
A
is Iwanaga–Gorenstein if and only if
\dim_{R}A
is finite.

Proof.

It suffices to verify the equality for
A
; the one for
A^{\mathrm{op}}
holds, by symmetry. Since the injective dimension of
A
is detected by the vanishing of
\operatorname{Ext}_{A}^{i}(-,A)
on finitely generated
A
-modules, it suffices to verify that

\operatorname{inj{.}dim}_{A_{\mathfrak{p}}}A_{\mathfrak{p}}=\dim_{R_{\mathfrak{p}}}A_{\mathfrak{p}}\quad\text{for each $\mathfrak{p}$ in $\operatorname{supp}_{R}A$.}

We can replace
R
and
A
by their localisations at
\mathfrak{p}
so that
R
is local, and hence
A
is semi-local, and
\dim_{R}A=\dim R
. For any simple
A
-module
L
one has

\operatorname{Ext}_{A}^{i}(L,\omega_{A/R})=\operatorname{Ext}_{A}^{i}(L,\operatorname{Hom}_{R}(A,R))\cong\operatorname{Ext}_{R}^{i}(A\otimes_{R}L,R)\,.

Since the ring
R
is Gorenstein, hence of injective dimension
\dim R
, we deduce that
\operatorname{Ext}_{A}^{i}(L,\omega_{A/R})=0
for
i>\dim R
. Thus Lemma
3.8
(3) yields

\operatorname{Ext}_{A}^{i}(L,\omega_{A/R})=0\quad\text{for $i>\dim R$.}

Hence
\operatorname{inj{.}dim}_{A}A\leq\dim R
by
[
16
, Lemma B.3.1]
. For the converse equality, with
k
the residue field of
R
, one has
\operatorname{Ext}_{R}^{d}(k,R)\cong k
; see Lemma
2.4
. Since
A
is a non-zero finite free
R
-module, one gets

\operatorname{Ext}^{d}_{A}(A\otimes_{R}k,A)\cong\operatorname{Ext}_{R}^{d}(k,A)\neq 0\,.

Thus
\operatorname{inj{.}dim}_{A}A\geq\dim R
. This justifies the stated equalities.
∎

In the result below, the converse statement need not hold for general Gorenstein algebras, as can be seen by contemplating the case when
R
is a field.

3.10 Lemma
.

Let
A
be a Gorenstein
R
-algebra and
M
an
A
-module. When
M
is Gorenstein projective, it is Gorenstein projective also as an
R
-module. The converse holds when
A
is fibrewise self-injective and either
M
is finitely generated or
\dim_{R}A
is finite.

Proof.

Since
A
is finite projective as an
R
-module, any projective
A
-module is also projective as an
R
-module, and hence any acyclic complex of projective
A
-modules is an acyclic complex of projective
R
-modules. It follows that any Gorenstein projective
A
-module is Gorenstein projective also as an
R
-module.

Suppose that
A
is fibrewise self-injective and that
M
is Gorenstein projective as an
R
-module. We verify that
\operatorname{Ext}_{A}^{i}(M,-)=0
for
i\geq 1
and on
\operatorname{Proj}A
. Given this, if
M
is finitely generated one can apply
[
26
, Lemma 6.3]
to conclude that it is Gorenstein projective also as an
A
-module. We can draw the same conclusion from
[
17
, Corollary 11.5.3]
for a general
M
when we also know
\dim_{R}A
is finite, for then
A
is Iwanaga–Gorenstein, by Proposition
3.9
.

As to the vanishing of Ext, any projective
A
-module is a direct summand of a free
A
-module, and any free
A
-module is of the form
A\otimes_{R}F
for some free
R
-module
F
. Therefore it suffices to verify that

\operatorname{Ext}^{i}_{A}(M,A\otimes_{R}F)=0\quad\text{for $i\geq 1$.}

Since
A
is in
\operatorname{add}(\omega_{A/R})
, by Lemma
3.8
, the
A
-module
A\otimes_{R}F
is in additive subcategory generated by

\omega_{A/R}\otimes_{R}F=\operatorname{Hom}_{R}(A,R)\otimes_{R}F\cong\operatorname{Hom}_{R}(A,F)\,.

Thus it suffices to verify that
\operatorname{Ext}_{A}^{i}(M,\operatorname{Hom}_{R}(A,F))=0
for
i\geq 1
. This follows from the adjunction isomorphism

\operatorname{Ext}_{A}^{i}(M,\operatorname{Hom}_{R}(A,F))\cong\operatorname{Ext}_{R}^{i}(M,F)

and the hypothesis that
M
is Gorenstein projective as an
R
-module.
∎

4.
Cocommutative Hopf algebras

Throughout this section
R
will be a regular commutative noetherian
ring and
A
a finite cocommutative Hopf algebra over
R
; this
includes the condition that
A
is projective as an
R
-module. Then

\mathbf{K}(\operatorname{Proj}A)
has a natural structure of a tensor-triangulated category,
and one has an analogue of the fibrewise criterion from
Section
2
that takes into account this additional
structure. With some further assumption on the cohomology of
A
we
are then able to stratify the tensor-triangulated category
\mathbf{K}(\operatorname{Proj}A)

via the action of the cohomology ring of
A
.

Tensor structure

Given
A
-modules
X
and
Y
, there is a natural diagonal
A
-module structure on
X\otimes_{R}Y
, obtained by restricting its
A\otimes_{R}A
-module structure along the coalgebra map
\Delta\colon A\to A\otimes_{R}A
.

4.1 Lemma
.

Let
P,Q
be
A
-modules. If
P
is projective over
A
and
Q
is projective over
R
, then the
A
-module
P\otimes_{R}Q
, with the usual diagonal action, is projective.

Proof.

This follows from the standard adjunction isomorphism

\operatorname{Hom}_{A}(P\otimes_{R}Q,-)\cong\operatorname{Hom}_{A}(P,\operatorname{Hom}_{R}(Q,-))\,.\qed

The preceding result implies that
-\otimes_{R}-
induces a tensor
product on
\mathbf{K}(\operatorname{Proj}A)
.

4.2 Lemma
.

The triangulated category
\mathbf{K}(\operatorname{Proj}A)
is tensor-triangulated, with product
-\otimes_{R}-
and unit the

A
-complex

\mathds{1}\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\operatorname{Hom}_{R}(\mathbf{p}_{{A}^{\mathrm{op}}}R,R)\,.

Proof.

We have already seen that
-\otimes_{R}-
provides a tensor product on

\mathbf{K}(\operatorname{Proj}A)
, and it remains to verify the assertion about the unit. Since
{A}^{\mathrm{op}}
is noetherian, one can assume that for any
M
in
\mathbf{D}^{\mathrm{b}}(\operatorname{mod}{A}^{\mathrm{op}})
, its projective resolution
\mathbf{p}_{{A}^{\mathrm{op}}}M
is finitely generated in each degree and that
(\mathbf{p}_{{M}^{\mathrm{op}}})_{i}=0
for
i\ll 0
. This fact will be used multiple times in what follows.

The augmentation
\varepsilon\colon\mathbf{p}_{{A}^{\mathrm{op}}}R\to R
induces the
A
-linear map

\varepsilon^{*}\colon R\longrightarrow\operatorname{Hom}_{R}(\mathbf{p}_{{A}^{\mathrm{op}}}R,R)\,.

For each
X
in
\mathbf{K}(\operatorname{Proj}A)
this induces the map

X\cong X\otimes_{R}R\xrightarrow{\ X\otimes\varepsilon^{*}\ }X\otimes_{R}\operatorname{Hom}_{R}(\mathbf{p}_{{A}^{\mathrm{op}}}R,R)

and the desired result is that this map is an isomorphism.

Since
\mathbf{K}(\operatorname{Proj}A)
is compactly generated, and the functors involved
preserves coproducts, it suffices to verify the claim when
X
is
compact, that is to say, of the form
\operatorname{Hom}_{A}(\mathbf{p}_{{A}^{\mathrm{op}}}M,A)
for some
M
in
\mathrm{mod}\,{A}^{\mathrm{op}}
; see (
2.3
). Consider the diagram

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\operatorname{Hom}_{A}(\mathbf{p}_{{A}^{\mathrm{op}}}M,A)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\operatorname{Hom}_{A}(\mathbf{p}_{{A}^{\mathrm{op}}}M,A)\otimes_{R}\operatorname{Hom}_{R}(\mathbf{p}_{{A}^{\mathrm{op}}}R,R)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\operatorname{Hom}_{A}(\mathbf{p}_{{A}^{\mathrm{op}}}M\otimes_{R}\mathbf{p}_{{A}^{\mathrm{op}}}R,A)}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\alpha}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\operatorname{Hom}_{A}(\mathbf{p}_{{A}^{\mathrm{op}}}M,A)\otimes\varepsilon^{*}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\cong}

where the vertical map is the natural one; it is an isomorphism because
\mathbf{p}_{{A}^{\mathrm{op}}}A
and
\mathbf{p}_{{A}^{\mathrm{op}}}M
are degreewise finitely generated. The map
\alpha
is the obvious composition. It suffices to check that
\alpha
is an isomorphism.

One can verify that the map
\alpha
is obtained from the map

\mathbf{p}_{{A}^{\mathrm{op}}}M\otimes_{R}\mathbf{p}_{{A}^{\mathrm{op}}}R\xrightarrow{\mathbf{p}_{{A}^{\mathrm{op}}}M\otimes\varepsilon}\mathbf{p}_{{A}^{\mathrm{op}}}M\otimes_{R}R\cong\mathbf{p}_{{A}^{\mathrm{op}}}M

by applying
\operatorname{Hom}_{A}(-,A)
. Since
\varepsilon
is a quasi-isomorphism so is
the map above. Since the source and target consist of projective

{A}^{\mathrm{op}}
-modules, and are bounded to the right, they are
semi-projective complexes over
{A}^{\mathrm{op}}
. Thus the map above is a
homotopy equivalence in
\mathbf{K}(\operatorname{Proj}{A}^{\mathrm{op}})
. Therefore applying

\operatorname{Hom}_{A}(-,A)
induces an isomorphism in
\mathbf{K}(\operatorname{Proj}A)
. This is the
desired result.
∎

Rigidity

The tensor-triangulated category
\mathbf{K}(\operatorname{Proj}A)
, which is always compactly
generated, is also rigid when
R
is regular. We recall briefly the
notion of rigidity in tensor-triangulated categories and refer to

[
23
, A.2]
for details.

Let
\mathcal{T}
be a compactly generated tensor-triangulated category,
with product
\otimes
and unit
\mathds{1}
. We assume that
\mathds{1}
is
compact. Being a compactly generated tensor-triangulated category,

\mathcal{T}
has an
internal function object
,
\operatorname{\mathcal{H}\!\!\;\mathit{om}}(-,-)
, defined
by the property that

\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal{T}}(X\otimes Y,Z)\cong\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal{T}}(X,\operatorname{\mathcal{H}\!\!\;\mathit{om}}(Y,Z))

for
X,Y
and
Z
in
\mathcal{T}
. There is a natural map

(4.3)

\operatorname{\mathcal{H}\!\!\;\mathit{om}}(X,\mathds{1})\otimes Y\longrightarrow\operatorname{\mathcal{H}\!\!\;\mathit{om}}(X,Y)

and
X
is
rigid
if this map is an isomorphism for all

Y
. Since
\mathds{1}
is compact, every rigid object is compact, and one
says that
\mathcal{T}
is
rigid
when the converse holds: compact
objects and rigid objects coincide. It is straightforward to verify that for this property to hold, the following conditions are necessary and sufficient:

(1)

the subcategory of compact objects is closed under
\otimes
;

(2)

(
4.3
) is an isomorphism when
X,Y
are compact.

Back to the tensor-triangulated category
\mathbf{K}(\operatorname{Proj}A)
with product

\otimes_{R}
, where
A
is a cocommutative Hopf algebra over
R
. In
this case, the internal function object can be described quite
concretely as

\operatorname{\mathcal{H}\!\!\;\mathit{om}}(Y,Z)=\mathbf{j}\operatorname{Hom}_{R}(Y,Z)

where
\mathbf{j}
is the right adjoint to the inclusion of
\mathbf{K}(\operatorname{Proj}A)
into the homotopy category of flat
A
-modules; see
[
25
, Proposition 2.4]
. Here is the pertinent result; one can also prove that
\mathbf{K}(\operatorname{Proj}A)
is not rigid when
R
is not regular.

4.4 Lemma
.

If
R
is regular, the tensor-triangulated category
\mathbf{K}(\operatorname{Proj}A)
is rigid.

Proof.

As noted above, it suffices to verify that for any
X,Y
in

\mathbf{K}^{\mathrm{c}}(\operatorname{Proj}A)
, the complex
X\otimes_{R}Y
is also in
\mathbf{K}^{\mathrm{c}}(\operatorname{Proj}A)
and
that (
4.3
) is an isomorphism. Given the identification
(
3.4
) of compact objects in
\mathbf{K}(\operatorname{Proj}A)
, this is
tantamount to verifying that for
M,N
in
\mathbf{D}^{\mathrm{b}}(\operatorname{mod}A)
, the complex

M\otimes^{\mathrm{L}}_{R}N
is in
\mathbf{D}^{\mathrm{b}}(\operatorname{mod}A)
and the map

\operatorname{RHom}_{R}(M,R)\otimes^{\mathrm{L}}_{R}N\longrightarrow\operatorname{RHom}_{R}(M,N)

of
A
-complexes is an isomorphism. Both properties are clear, since
R
is regular.
∎

4.5 Remark
.

The functor
\pi\colon\mathbf{K}(\operatorname{Proj}A)\to\mathbf{K}(\operatorname{Proj}A_{k})
from
(
2.5
) is a tensor functor which fits – together with its
adjoints – into the framework discussed in
[
2
]
.

Fibrewise criterion

Here is the analogue of the fibrewise criterion for detecting membership in localising subcategories, Theorem
2.1
, in the presence of the tensor product. In the statement
\operatorname{Loc}^{\otimes}(Y)
denotes the tensor ideal localising subcategory generated by
Y
.

4.6 Theorem
.

Let
R
be a regular ring and
A
a finite cocommutative Hopf
R
-algebra. Let
X,Y
be objects in
\mathbf{K}(\operatorname{Proj}A)
. The following conditions are equivalent.

(1)

X\in\operatorname{Loc}^{\otimes}(Y)
in
\mathbf{K}(\operatorname{Proj}A)
;

(2)

{X}_{k(\mathfrak{p})}\in\operatorname{Loc}^{\otimes}({Y}_{k(\mathfrak{p})})
in
\mathbf{K}(\operatorname{Proj}{A}_{k(\mathfrak{p})})
for each
\mathfrak{p}\in\operatorname{Spec}R
.

Proof.

The argument follows the same lines as that for
Theorem
2.1
, using in addition that
\mathbf{K}(\operatorname{Proj}A)
is a
rigidly generated tensor-triangulated category by
Lemma
4.4
. Again it is straightforward to verify
(1)
\Rightarrow
(2), once one observes that the functor
\pi
from
(
2.5
) respects the tensor products: For
X,Y
in

\mathbf{K}(\operatorname{Proj}A)
, there is a natural isomorphism

\pi(X\otimes_{R}Y)\cong\pi(X)\otimes_{k(\mathfrak{p})}\pi(Y)\quad\text{in $\mathbf{K}(\operatorname{Proj}{A}_{k(\mathfrak{p})})$. }

For the implication (2)
\Rightarrow
(1) we use the version of the
local-to-global theorem (
2.16
) for
tensor-triangulated categories in

[
10
, Theorem 7.2]
. Then the task reduces
to proving for
X,Y
in
\mathbf{K}(\operatorname{Proj}A)
that when
(R,\mathfrak{m},k)
is a local
ring and
X_{k}
is in
\operatorname{Loc}^{\otimes}(Y_{k})
, the complex
\pi_{r}\pi(X)

is in
\operatorname{Loc}^{\otimes}(\pi_{r}\pi(Y))
. When reducing to the local case,
one uses that for each
\mathfrak{p}
in
\operatorname{Spec}R
the category
\mathbf{K}(\operatorname{Proj}A_{\mathfrak{p}})

identifies with a localising tensor ideal of
\mathbf{K}(\operatorname{Proj}A)
via
(
2.13
).

Whilst the functor
\pi_{r}
need not respect tensor products, the
following
projection formula
holds. For
U
in
\mathbf{K}(\operatorname{Proj}A)
and

V
in
\mathbf{K}(\operatorname{Proj}A_{k})
, there is a natural isomorphism

U\otimes_{R}\pi_{r}V\cong\pi_{r}(\pi(U)\otimes_{k}V)\,.

One can verify this directly, but this is a general fact about tensor
functors and their right adjoints between rigidly generated
tensor-triangulated categories; see

[
2
, Theorem 1.3]
. This formula
and the fact that, up to direct summands,
\pi
is surjective on
objects – see Lemma
2.9
– yield the desired result.
∎

Finite generation

Let
R
be a commutative noetherian ring and
A
a finite cocommutative Hopf algebra over
R
. Set

S\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\operatorname{Ext}_{A}^{*}(R,R)\,.

This is a graded-commutative
R
-algebra. The ring
S
can be realised as the graded-ring of morphisms

S\cong\operatorname{Hom}^{*}_{\mathbf{K}(A)}(\mathds{1},\mathds{1})\,.

Since
\mathds{1}
is the unit of the tensor product on
\mathbf{K}(\operatorname{Proj}A)
, it has a natural
S
-linear action on it. For each
\mathfrak{p}
in
\operatorname{Spec}R
one has that
{A}_{k(\mathfrak{p})}
is a finite dimensional cocommutative Hopf algebra over
k(\mathfrak{p})
. Set

(4.7)

S(\mathfrak{p})\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\operatorname{Ext}_{{A}_{k(\mathfrak{p})}}^{*}(k(\mathfrak{p}),k(\mathfrak{p}))\,.

A result of Friedlander and Suslin
[
20
]
yields that the graded-commutative
k(\mathfrak{p})
-algebra
S(\mathfrak{p})
is finitely generated; equivalently, that it is noetherian. Throughout we assume that the
R
-algebra
S
is itself finitely generated.

Cohomological support

Let
R
be a commutative noetherian ring,
A
a finite cocommutative
Hopf algebra over
R
, and
S
the cohomology ring introduced
above. We write
\operatorname{Spec}S
for the homogenous prime ideals in

S
. Following
[
9
, §5]
, the action of
S

on
\mathbf{K}(\operatorname{Proj}A)
gives rise to a notion of
support
for objects in

\mathbf{K}(\operatorname{Proj}A)
. Namely for each
\mathfrak{q}
in
\operatorname{Spec}S
there is a
local
cohomology functor
functor

\varGamma_{\mathfrak{q}}
, defined akin to
(
2.15
). The support of an object
X\in\mathbf{K}(\operatorname{Proj}A)
is by
definition the set

\operatorname{supp}_{S}X\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\{\mathfrak{q}\in\operatorname{Spec}S\mid\varGamma_{\mathfrak{q}}X\neq 0\}\,,

and for any class of objects
\mathcal{X}
we set

\operatorname{supp}_{S}\mathcal{X}\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\bigcup_{X\in\mathcal{X}}\operatorname{supp}_{S}X\,.

Support and fibres

Theorem
4.6
yields a stratification – see the
discussion further below – of
\mathbf{K}(\operatorname{Proj}A)
in terms of subsets of the space

\bigsqcup_{\mathfrak{p}\in\operatorname{Spec}R}\operatorname{Spec}S(\mathfrak{p})\,,

where to each
X
in
\mathbf{K}(\operatorname{Proj}A)
we associate the subset

\bigsqcup_{\mathfrak{p}\in\operatorname{Spec}R}\operatorname{supp}_{S(\mathfrak{p})}{X}_{k(\mathfrak{p})}\,.

The task is to relate this to
\operatorname{supp}_{S}X
, viewed as a subset of
\operatorname{Spec}S
.

To that end consider the structure map
\eta\colon R\to S
, which induces a map

\eta^{a}\colon\operatorname{Spec}S\longrightarrow\operatorname{Spec}R\,.

The fibre of this map over
\mathfrak{p}
is

(\eta^{a})^{-1}(\mathfrak{p})=\operatorname{Spec}(S\otimes_{R}k(\mathfrak{p}))\,,

which we identify with a subset of
\operatorname{Spec}S
in the usual way.

The functor
-\otimes^{\mathrm{L}}_{R}k(\mathfrak{p})
induces a map of
R
-algebras

S=\operatorname{Ext}^{*}_{A}(R,R)\longrightarrow\operatorname{Ext}_{{A}_{k(\mathfrak{p})}}^{*}(k(\mathfrak{p}),k(\mathfrak{p}))=S(\mathfrak{p})\,.

This induces the map of graded
k(\mathfrak{p})
-algebras

\kappa_{\mathfrak{p}}\colon S\otimes_{R}k(\mathfrak{p})\longrightarrow S(\mathfrak{p})\,.

Even in the best of cases, one does not expect this to be an isomorphism. Consider the induced map on spectra:

(4.8)

\kappa^{a}_{\mathfrak{p}}\colon\operatorname{Spec}S(\mathfrak{p})\longrightarrow\operatorname{Spec}(S\otimes_{R}k(\mathfrak{p}))\subseteq\operatorname{Spec}S\,.

The result below tracks the behavior of supports as we pass to the fibres.

4.9 Lemma
.

Let
R
be a regular ring and
A
a finite cocommutative Hopf
R
-algebra. Fix
\mathfrak{p}
in
\operatorname{Spec}R
and let
\kappa^{a}_{\mathfrak{p}}
be the map in (
4.8
). For each
X
in
\mathbf{K}(\operatorname{Proj}A)
there is an equality

\kappa^{a}_{\mathfrak{p}}(\operatorname{supp}_{S(\mathfrak{p})}{X}_{k(\mathfrak{p})})=\operatorname{supp}_{S}X\cap(\eta^{a})^{-1}(\mathfrak{p})\,.

Proof.

We can reduce to the case where
(R,\mathfrak{m},k)
is a local ring and

\mathfrak{p}=\mathfrak{m}
, the maximal ideal of
R
. Set

S_{k}\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}S\otimes_{R}k=S/\mathfrak{m}S
. Via the map
\kappa_{\mathfrak{m}}\colon S_{k}\to S(\mathfrak{m})

the
S(\mathfrak{m})
-action on
\mathbf{K}(\operatorname{Proj}A_{k})

It thus suffices to work with the subset on the right.

Consider the adjunction (

). For any compact object

in

one has isomorphisms of graded

\displaystyle\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbf{K}(A_{k})}^{*}(\pi C,X_{k})

\displaystyle=\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbf{K}(A_{k})}^{*}(\pi C,\pi X)

\displaystyle\cong\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbf{K}(A)}^{*}(C,\pi_{r}\pi X)

\displaystyle\cong\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbf{K}(A)}^{*}(C,X\otimes_{R}K)

where the last isomorphism is by Lemma

. Any compact

is a direct summand of
\pi C
for some compact object

in
\mathbf{K}(\operatorname{Proj}A)

by Lemma

. By

-modules

\displaystyle=\operatorname{supp}_{S}X\cap(\eta^{a})^{-1}(\mathfrak{m})\,.

The second one holds because

∎

Stratification

Let
S
be a graded commutative noetherian ring and
\mathcal{T}
a rigidly
compactly generated tensor-triangulated category. We denote by

\mathcal{T}^{\mathrm{c}}
the full subcategory of compact objects. We say
that
\mathcal{T}
is
S
-linear
to mean that
S
acts on
\mathcal{T}

via a map of graded rings

S\longrightarrow\mathrm{End}^{*}_{\mathcal{T}}(\mathds{1})\,;

see
[
10
, §7]
. We are mainly interested
in the case

\mathcal{T}=\mathbf{K}(\operatorname{Proj}A)\qquad\text{with}\qquad\mathcal{T}^{\mathrm{c}}\cong\mathbf{D}^{\mathrm{b}}(\operatorname{mod}A)

for a cocommutative Hopf algebra
A
over
R
, and
S
its cohomology algebra as above, but it is convenient to make a few observations in greater generality.

In the context above, one says that the tensor-triangulated category

\mathcal{T}
is
stratified
by
S
if for each
\mathfrak{q}
in
\operatorname{Spec}S

the category
\varGamma_{\mathfrak{q}}\mathcal{T}
, consisting of the
\mathfrak{q}
-local and

\mathfrak{q}
-torsion objects in
\mathcal{T}
, is either zero or minimal, in that,
it has no proper localising tensor ideals; see

[
10
, §7.2]
. When this holds one has
a bijection

(4.10)

\left\{\begin{gathered}\text{Localising tensor ideals of $\mathcal{T}$}\end{gathered}\right\}\xrightarrow{\ \operatorname{supp}_{S}(-)\ }\{\begin{gathered}\text{Subsets of $\operatorname{supp}_{S}\mathds{1}$}\end{gathered}\}\,.

When in addition the graded
S
-module
\operatorname{Ext}^{*}_{A}(M,M)
is finitely generated for each
M\in\operatorname{mod}A
, the subset
\operatorname{supp}_{S}M
of
\operatorname{Spec}S
is closed and, by
[
10
, Theorem 6.1]
, the bijection above restricts to a bijection

(4.11)

\left\{\begin{gathered}\text{Thick tensor ideals of ${\mathcal{T}}^{\mathrm{c}}$}\end{gathered}\right\}\xrightarrow{\ \operatorname{supp}_{S}(-)\ }\left\{\begin{gathered}\text{Specialisation closed}\\
\text{subsets of $\operatorname{supp}_{S}\mathds{1}$}\end{gathered}\right\}\,.

Here is a slightly different perspective on the stratification property.

4.12 Lemma
.

Let
\mathcal{T}
be an
S
-linear tensor-triangulated category as above. Then the tensor-triangulated category
\mathcal{T}
is stratified by
S
if and only if for any
X,Y
in
\mathcal{T}
there are equivalences

\displaystyle\operatorname{supp}_{S}X\subseteq\operatorname{supp}_{S}Y

\displaystyle\iff\operatorname{Loc}^{\otimes}(X)\subseteq\operatorname{Loc}^{\otimes}(Y)

\displaystyle\iff X\in\operatorname{Loc}^{\otimes}(Y)\,.

Proof.

We use the fact that for each
\mathfrak{q}
in
\operatorname{Spec}S
we have

\varGamma_{\mathfrak{q}}\mathcal{T}=\{X\in\mathcal{T}\mid\operatorname{supp}_{S}X\subseteq\{\mathfrak{q}\}\}

by
[
9
, Corollary 5.9]
.
Evidently when the stated property holds the localising tensor ideal
\varGamma_{\mathfrak{q}}\mathcal{T}
of
\mathcal{T}
is minimal for each
\mathfrak{q}
in
\operatorname{Spec}S
, so
\mathcal{T}
is stratified by
S
as a tensor-triangulated category. The converse is equally clear.
∎

Here is one of the main results of our work. When it applies, one gets
a classification of the localising tensor ideals of
\mathbf{K}(\operatorname{Proj}A)
and
also the thick tensor ideals of its subcategory of compact objects,
which identifies with
\mathbf{D}^{\mathrm{b}}(\operatorname{mod}A)
.

4.13 Theorem
.

Let
R
be a regular ring and
A
a finite cocommutative Hopf algebra
over
R
such that the
R
-algebra
S\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\operatorname{Ext}^{*}_{A}(R,R)

\operatorname{supp}_{S}\mathbf{K}(\operatorname{Proj}A)=\operatorname{Spec}S

Proof.

The main task is to verify that when

are objects in
\mathbf{K}(\operatorname{Proj}A)

with
\operatorname{supp}_{S}X\subseteq\operatorname{supp}_{S}Y

is in

\operatorname{Loc}^{\otimes}(Y)

\operatorname{supp}_{S(\mathfrak{p})}{X}_{k(\mathfrak{p})}\subseteq\operatorname{supp}_{S(\mathfrak{p})}{Y}_{k(\mathfrak{p})}\,.

Since

It remains to observe that

∎

Gorenstein projective modules

Let

4.15 Lemma
.

Let
R
be a regular ring and

a finite cocommutative Hopf algebra. The tensor product
-\otimes_{R}-
with diagonal

-action endows
\operatorname{\underline{GProj}}A
with a structure of a rigidly compactly generated tensor-triangulated category. This structure is compatible with the equivalence in Theorem
3.5
.

Proof.

If
X
is an acyclic complex of projective
A
-modules and

is a
Gorenstein projective
A
-module, then the complex

of
projective modules is also acyclic, for

. Thus the category of Gorenstein

∎

Let

4.16 Lemma
.

With the assumptions from Theorem

, the

5.
Finite groups

Let
G
be a finite group. The main result of this section is that for
A=RG
, the group algebra of
G
over any commutative noetherian ring
R
, the map (
4.8
) is a homeomorphism for all primes in the spectrum of
R
. As a consequence we get a stratification theorem when
R
is regular; see Theorem
5.7
. In this case the cohomology algebra
\operatorname{Ext}^{*}_{RG}(R,R)
is the group cohomology algebra. This is usually denoted
H^{*}(G,R)
, and we follow suit.

Cup products

Let
R
be a commutative ring, not necessarily noetherian, and
M
an
R
-module, both viewed as
G
-modules with trivial action. The cup product makes
H^{*}(G,R)
an
R
-algebra and
H^{*}(G,M)
a module over
H^{*}(G,R)
. These are defined as follows: Let
P
be a projective resolution of the trivial
\mathbb{Z}G
-module
\mathbb{Z}
and
\Delta\colon P\to P\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}P
a diagonal approximation. Given classes
x\in H^{*}(G,R)
and
y\in H^{*}(G,M)
, represented by cocycles
\tilde{x}\in\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbb{Z}G}(P,R)
and
\tilde{y}\in\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbb{Z}G}(P,M)
the cup product
x\cup y
is represented by the class of the composition of maps

P\xrightarrow{\ \Delta\ }P\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}P\xrightarrow{\ \tilde{x}\otimes\tilde{y}\ }R\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}M\longrightarrow M\,.

If
I\subseteq R
is an ideal,
-\cup-
defines a product on
H^{*}(G,I)
. It is clear from the definition that if
I
is nilpotent of order
n
, then so is
H^{*}(G,I)
.

Infinitesimal deformations of coefficients

Let
R\to R^{\prime}
be a surjective map of commutative rings whose kernel, say
I
, satisfies
I^{2}=0
; thus
I
is an
R^{\prime}
-module. One thinks of
R
as an infinitesimal deformation of
R^{\prime}
. The exact sequence

(5.1)

0\longrightarrow I\longrightarrow R\longrightarrow R^{\prime}\longrightarrow 0

induces a connecting homomorphism

\delta\colon H^{*}(G,R^{\prime})\to H^{*}(G,I)\,.

Since
I
is an
R^{\prime}
-module
H^{*}(G,I)
is a module over
H^{*}(G,R^{\prime})
, via the cup product.

The statement of the result below, and its proof, are a variation on
[
6
, Lemma 4.3.3]
.

5.2 Lemma
.

In the context above, for
x,y\in H^{*}(G,R^{\prime})
one has

\delta(x\cup y)=\delta(x)\cup y+(-1)^{|x|}x\cup\delta(y)\,.

Proof.

As in the proof of
[
6
, Lemma 4.3.3]
, let
P
be the projective resolution of the trivial
\mathbb{Z}G
-module
\mathbb{Z}
, and

\Delta\colon P\to P\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}P
a diagonal approximation. The exact sequence (
5.1
) induces the exact sequence of complexes

0\longrightarrow\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbb{Z}G}(P,I)\longrightarrow\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbb{Z}G}(P,R)\longrightarrow\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbb{Z}G}(P,R^{\prime})\longrightarrow 0\,.

Represent
x
and
y
by cocycles
\tilde{x}
and
\tilde{y}
on the right hand side of this sequence. Then
x\cup y
is represented by
the composite

\tilde{x}\cup\tilde{y}\colon P\xrightarrow{\ \Delta\ }P\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}P\xrightarrow{\ \tilde{x}\otimes\tilde{y}\ }R^{\prime}\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}R^{\prime}\xrightarrow{\ \mu\ }R^{\prime}

where
\mu
is the multiplication map. To compute the effect of the connecting homomorphism, we first lift
\tilde{x}
and
\tilde{y}
to
cochains
\hat{x}
and
\hat{y}
in
\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbb{Z}G}(P,R)
. Since
d\tilde{x}=0=d\tilde{y}
, the elements
d\hat{x}
and
d\hat{y}
lie in
I
.
The element
\tilde{x}\cup\tilde{y}
lifts to
\hat{x}\cup\hat{y}
, and

\displaystyle d(\hat{x}\cup\hat{y})

\displaystyle=d\hat{x}\cup\hat{y}+(-1)^{|x|}\hat{x}\cup d\hat{y}

\displaystyle=d\hat{x}\cup\tilde{y}+(-1)^{|x|}\tilde{x}\cup d\hat{y}

The second equality holds as
d\hat{x}
and
d\hat{y}
lie in
I
. This gives the stated equality.
∎

In what follows, we say that an abelian group
M
is
p
-local
, for a prime number
p
, if the natural map
M\to M_{(p)}
is an isomorphism.

5.3 Lemma
.

Let
p
be a prime dividing
|G|
and
\pi\colon R\to R^{\prime}
a map of
p
-local rings, with
\operatorname{Ker}(\pi)
nilpotent. Then the map
H^{*}(G,\pi)
has nilpotent kernel, and there exists an integer
n
such that for any element
x\in H^{\geqslant 1}(G,R^{\prime})
, the element
x^{p^{n}}
is in the image of the map
H^{*}(G,\pi)
.

Proof.

Set
I\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\operatorname{Ker}(\pi)
. Since this ideal is nilpotent, so is
H^{*}(G,I)
, under cup products. The claim about nilpotence is clear because, by the exact sequence in cohomology arising from (
5.1
), the kernel of
H^{*}(G,\pi)
is the image of the map

H^{*}(G,I)\longrightarrow H^{*}(G,R)

which respects cup products.

As to the second part of the statement, it suffices to consider the case where
I^{2}=0
. Let
n
be the largest integer such that
p^{n}
divides
|G|
. Since
|G|
annihilates
H^{\geqslant 1}(G,R^{\prime})
, and the ring
R^{\prime}
and hence also
H^{*}(G,R^{\prime})
is
p
-local, one gets that

p^{n}\cdot H^{\geqslant 1}(G,R^{\prime})=0\,.

If
|x|
is odd, then
2\cdot x^{2}=0
, since
H^{*}(G,R^{\prime})
is graded-commutative. Thus if also
p
is odd, then
x^{2}=0
, since we are in the
p
-local situation. Thus we can suppose either
|x|
is even or
p=2
. In either case, a repeated application of Lemma
5.2
yields
\delta(x^{i})=ix^{i-1}\delta(x)
for each
i\geq 1
. In particular
\delta(x^{p^{n}})=0
. It then follows from the exact sequence in group cohomology arising from (
5.1
) that
x^{p^{n}}
is in the image of the map
H^{*}(G,R)\to H^{*}(G,R^{\prime})
.
∎

Modules with bounded torsion

Let
M
is an abelian group such that its torsion-subgroup, denoted
\operatorname{tors}(M)
is bounded; that is to say, there exists an integer
n
such that
n\cdot\operatorname{tors}(M)=0
. Fomin
[
19
]
proved that inclusion
\operatorname{tors}(M)\subseteq M
splits; see also
[
29
, Corollary pp. 134]
. This result will be used below.

5.4 Lemma
.

Let
p
be a prime dividing
|G|
and
M
a
p
-local abelian group such that
\operatorname{tors}(M)
is bounded. For all integers
s\gg 0
the map

H^{\geqslant 1}(G,M)\longrightarrow H^{\geqslant 1}(G,M/p^{s}M)

induced by the surjection
M\to M/p^{s}M
, is one-to-one.

Proof.

Since
M
is
p
-local, the only torsion is
p
-torsion. Choose
s\gg 0
such that

p^{s}\operatorname{tors}(M)=0=p^{s}H^{\geqslant 1}(G,M)\,.

The equality on the left means that the sequence below, where the map
M\to p^{s}M
is given by
m\mapsto p^{s}m
, is exact:

0\longrightarrow\operatorname{tors}(M)\longrightarrow M\longrightarrow p^{s}M\longrightarrow 0\,.

This is split-exact, by Fomin’s result recalled above, so the induced map

H^{*}(G,M)\longrightarrow H^{*}(G,p^{s}M)

is surjective. The map
M\xrightarrow{p^{s}}M
factors as
M\to p^{s}M\to M
where the one on the right is inclusion. By the choice of
p^{s}
, the composition of the induced maps

H^{*}(G,M)\longrightarrow H^{*}(G,p^{s}M)\longrightarrow H^{*}(G,M)

is zero in degrees
\geq 1
. Since the map on the left is surjective, it follows that the one on the right is zero in degrees
\geq 1
. Then the cohomology exact sequence arising from the exact sequence

0\longrightarrow p^{s}M\longrightarrow M\longrightarrow M/p^{s}M\longrightarrow 0

yields the desired statement.
∎

Noetherian ring of coefficients

The result below was proved by Benson and
Habegger
[
8
]
when
R=\mathbb{Z}
; the argument
given here is modeled on their proof. A general result, allowing non-trivial

-action on

, was proved by Lau
[
31
, Section 7]
.

Recall that a map of rings
f\colon S\to T
containing a field of positive characteristic

is an

if
\ker(f)
is nilpotent, and for each
t\in T
there exists an
n
such that

is the image of

.

5.5 Theorem
.

Let
G
be a finite group and

a commutative noetherian ring. For each prime number

, the map

H^{*}(G,R)\otimes_{R}R/pR\longrightarrow H^{*}(G,R/pR)

is an F-isomorphism.

Proof.

Set
R_{(p)}\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\mathbb{Z}_{(p)}\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}R
. As

is
p
-local, the map
R\to R/pR
factors through

R_{(p)}
. As localisation is an exact functor, there are natural isomorphisms

\displaystyle H^{*}(G,R)\otimes_{R}R/pR\cong H^{*}(G,R_{(p)})\otimes_{R_{(p)}}R/pR

\displaystyle H^{*}(G,R/pR)\cong H^{*}(G,R_{(p)}/pR_{(p)})\,.

Thus replacing
R
by
R_{(p)}
we can assume
R
is
p
-local.

For any finitely generated
R
-module
M
, the (additive) torsion submodule
\operatorname{tors}(M)
is an
R
-submodule of
M
, and hence finitely generated as an
R
-module, as
R
is noetherian. It follows that
\operatorname{tors}(M)
is bounded, so Lemma
5.4
applies.

Choose an integer
s
large enough that the conclusion of
op. cit.
applies to the
R
-modules
R

5.6 Corollary
.

Let
G
be a finite group and
R
a commutative noetherian ring. For any map of rings
R\to k
with
k
a field of positive characteristic, the natural map

H^{*}(G,R)\otimes_{R}k\longrightarrow H^{*}(G,k)

is an
F
-isomorphism, and hence the induced map on spectra

\operatorname{Spec}H^{*}(G,k)\longrightarrow\operatorname{Spec}(H^{*}(G,R)\otimes_{R}k)

is a homeomorphism.

Proof.

Let
p
be the characteristic of
k
. The map
R\to k
factors through
R/pR
. Applying
-\otimes_{R/pR}k
to the F-isomorphism in Theorem
5.5
yields the F-isomorphism

H^{*}(G,R)\otimes_{R}k\longrightarrow H^{*}(G,R/pR)\otimes_{R/pR}k\,.

It remains to observe that the right hand side is isomorphic to
H^{*}(G,k)
.
∎

5.7 Theorem
.

With
G
a finite group and
R
a regular ring, the tensor-triangulated category
\mathbf{K}(\operatorname{Proj}RG)
is stratified by the action of
H^{*}(G,R)
.

Proof.

The
R
-algebra
H^{*}(G,R)
is finitely generated by a result of
Evens
[
18
]
and Venkov
[
37
]
. Thus the
result follows from
Theorem
4.13
and Corollary
5.6
.
∎

Chien-Chung Huang, François Sellier

Abstract
We consider the problem of maximizing a non-negative submodular function under the b b -matching constraint, in the semi-streaming model. When the function is linear, monotone, and non-monotone, we obtain the approximation ratios of 2 + ε 2+\varepsilon , 3 + 2 ​ 2 ≈ 5.828 3+2\sqrt{2}\approx 5.828 , and 4 + 2 ​ 3 ≈ 7.464 4+2\sqrt{3}\approx 7.464 , respectively. We also consider a generalized problem, where a k k -uniform hypergraph is given, along with an extra matroid or a k ′ k^{\prime} -matchoid constraint imposed on the edges, with the same goal of finding a b b -matching that maximizes a submodular function. When the extra constraint is a matroid, we obtain the approximation ratios of k + 1 + ε k+1+\varepsilon , k + 2 ​ k + 1 + 2 k+2\sqrt{k+1}+2 , and k + 2 ​ k + 2 + 3 k+2\sqrt{k+2}+3 for linear, monotone and non-monotone submodular functions, respectively. Introduction

Let
G=(V,E)
be a multi-graph (without self-loops) where each vertex
v\in V
is associated
with a
capacity

b_{v}\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
. A
b
-matching

is a subset of edges
M\subseteq E
where each vertex
v
has at most

b_{v}
incident edges contained in
M
.

In the maximum weight
b
-matching problem, edges are given weights

w:E\rightarrow\mathbb{R}_{+}
and we need to compute a
b
-matching

M
so that
w(M)=\sum_{e\in M}w(e)
is maximized.
A generalization of the problem is that of
maximizing a non-negative
submodular function

f:2^{E}\rightarrow\mathbb{R_{+}}

under the
b
-matching constraint, namely, we look for a
b
-matching

M
so that
f(M)
is maximized. Here we recall the definition of a submodular function:

\forall X\subseteq Y\subsetneq E,\forall e\in E\backslash Y,f(X\cup\{e\})-f(X)\geq f(Y\cup\{e\})-f(Y).

Additionally
f
is called
monotone
if
\forall X\subseteq Y\subseteq E,f(X)\leq f(Y)
, otherwise it is
non-monotone
. Observe that
the maximum weight matching is the special case where
f
is a linear sum of the weights associated with the edges. Note that
throughout this paper, we implicitly assume that the submodular function
f
is always non-negative.

In the traditional offline setting,
both problems are extensively studied.
The maximum weight
b
-matching can be solved in polynomial time
[
16
]
;
maximizing a non-negative monotone submodular function
under
b
-matching constraint
is NP-hard and the best approximation ratios so far are
2+\varepsilon
and
4+\varepsilon
,
for the monotone and non-monotone case, respectively
[
8
]
.

In this work, we consider the problem in
the semi-streaming model
[
14
]
.
Here the edges in
E
arrive over time
but we have only limited space (ideally
proportional to the output size) and cannot
afford to store all edges in
E
—this rules out the possibility of applying known offline algorithms.

We also consider a generalized problem, where a matroid or a matchoid
is imposed on the edges of an hypergraph. Specifically, here
G=(V,E)
is a
k
-uniform hypergraph, where each edge
e\in E
contains
k
vertices in
V
. In addition to the capacities
b_{v}
, a matroid or a matchoid
\mathcal{M}
(see the next section for the definitions) is imposed on the edges of
E
. A
b
-matching
M
is feasible only if
M
is independent in
\mathcal{M}
. The objective here is to find a feasible
b
-matching
M
that maximizes
f(M)
for
f
a submodular function.

Extension:
k
-uniform hypergraph with a matroid or a matchoid constraint

Before we explain our results, let us recall some definitions.

A pair
\mathcal{M}=(E,\mathcal{I})
is a matroid if the following three conditions hold: (1)
\emptyset\in\mathcal{I}
, (2) if
X\subseteq Y\in\mathcal{I}
, then

X\in\mathcal{I}
, and (3) if
X,Y\in\mathcal{I},|Y|>|X|
,
there exists an element
e\in Y\backslash X
so that

X\cup\{e\}\in\mathcal{I}
. The sets in
\mathcal{I}
are the
independent sets
and the
rank

r_{\mathcal{M}}
of the matroid
\mathcal{M}
is defined as
\max_{X\in\mathcal{I}}|X|
. A minimum (inclusion-wise) non-independent subset is called a
circuit
. For any subset
S\subseteq E
, its
rank

r_{\mathcal{M}}(S)
is defined as the maximum size of an independent set in
S
. We will also use the notion of
span
, which is defined for a subset
S
of
E
as
\text{span}(S)=\{e\in E:r_{\mathcal{M}}(S)=r_{\mathcal{M}}(S\cup\{e\})
. These definitions are close to the ones for independent families in vector spaces, and their nomenclature is similar.

In the following, we will sometimes refer to particular types of matroids: uniform and partition matroids. A
uniform matroid

\mathcal{M}=(E,\mathcal{I})
of rank
r_{\mathcal{M}}
is a matroid such that the independent sets are the sets of cardinality at most
r_{\mathcal{M}}
. A
partition matroid

\mathcal{M}=(E,\mathcal{I})
is defined by a partition
E_{1},\dots,E_{l}
of
E
and bounds
k_{1},\dots,k_{l}
: a set
M\subseteq E
is independent in
\mathcal{M}
if for all
1\leq i\leq l,|M\cap E_{i}|\leq k_{i}
.

A matchoid
\mathcal{M}=(\mathcal{M}_{i}=(E_{i},\mathcal{I}_{i}))_{i=1}^{s}
is a collection of matroids
\mathcal{M}_{i}=(E_{i},\mathcal{I}_{i})
. Specifically, a
k^{\prime}
-matchoid is a matchoid with the additional condition that each element
e\in E=\cup_{i=1}^{s}E_{i}
appears in at most
k^{\prime}
of
E_{i}
s. A set
S\subseteq\cup_{i=1}^{s}E_{i}
is independent in the matchoid
\mathcal{M}
only if
S\cap E_{i}
is independent for all
1\leq i\leq s
. For instance, a partition matroid can be seen as
1
-matchoid made of uniform matroids.

In our generalization, a
k
-uniform hypergraph is given
with an additional matroid or a
k^{\prime}
-matchoid
\mathcal{M}
imposed.
The goal is to find a
b
-matching
M
, which is also independent
in
\mathcal{M}
, so as to maximize

.

is a matroid, our problem is a special case of the

-matchoid submodular function maximization, and if the constraint

1.2
Our Technique

We use a local-ratio technique to decide
to retain or to discard a newly arrived edge during the streaming
phase. After this phase, a greedy algorithm, according to
the reverse edge arrival order, is then applied to add edges
one by one into the solution while guaranteeing feasibility.

This is in fact the same framework used in
[
12
,
15
]
. Our main technical innovation is to introduce a data structure, which takes the form of
a set of queues. Such a set of queues is associated with each vertex (and with the imposed matroid). Every edge, if retained, will appear as an element
2
2

2

In this article, we will often use “edge” and “element” interchangeably.
in one of these queues for each of its endpoints (and for the imposed matroid). These queues will guide the greedy algorithm to make better choices and are critical in our improvement over previous results.
Here we give some intuition behind these queues. Consider the maximum weight
b
-matching problem. Similar to
[
9
]
, we compute, for every edge, a
gain
. The sum of the gains of the retained edges can be shown to be at least half of the real weight of the unknown optimal matching. The question then boils down to how to “extract” a matching whose real weight is large compared to these gains of the retained edges. In our queues, the elements are stacked in such a way that the weight of an element
e
is the sum of the gains of all the elements preceding
e
in any queue containing
e
. This suggests that if
e
is taken by the greedy algorithm, we can as well ignore all elements that are underneath
e
in the queues, as their gains are already “paid for” by
e
.

w_{v}(e)=w_{v}^{*}(e)+g(e)=\sum_{e^{\prime}\in Q_{v,q_{v}(e)}^{(t)}}g(e^{\prime})\,\text{ and }\,w(e)=g(e)+\sum_{u\in e}\sum_{e^{\prime}\in Q_{u,q_{u}(e)}^{(t-1)}}g(e^{\prime}).

(1)

Moreover, observe that

.
One can also observe that Algorithm

. In fact, an element can be added only if no element above it in any of the queues where it appears has already been added into

Claim 9

Given an edge

, or there exists another edge

Observe that if the claim holds, by (

To prove the claim, let

. Consider the two queues

and

∎

3.2
Analysis for Monotone Submodular Function Maximization

Let
\alpha=1+\varepsilon
. In this section,
p=1
(so we have actually a deterministic
algorithm for the monotone case). The following two lemmas relate the total gain

g(S)
with the marginal values
f(S\,|\,\emptyset)
and
f(M^{opt}\,|\,S)
.

Lemma 12
.

It holds that
g(S)\geq\frac{\varepsilon}{1+\varepsilon}f(S\,|\,\emptyset)
.

Proof.

For an element
e=e_{t}\in S
we have
f\left(e\,|\,S^{(t-1)}\right)\geq(1+\varepsilon)\sum_{u\in e}w_{u}^{*}(e)
so

g(e)=f\left(e\,|\,S^{(t-1)}\right)-\sum_{u\in e}w_{u}^{*}(e)\geq f\left(e\,|\,S^{(t-1)}\right)\left(1-\frac{1}{1+\varepsilon}\right),

implying that

g(S)=\sum_{e\in S}g(e)\geq\sum_{e=e_{t}\in S}f\left(e\,|\,S^{(t-1)}\right)\left(1-\frac{1}{1+\varepsilon}\right)=\frac{\varepsilon}{1+\varepsilon}f(S\,|\,\emptyset).

∎

As in the previous section, if an edge
e
is discarded, we assume that
w^{*}_{v}(e)=w_{v}(e)

for each
v\in e
.

Lemma 13
.

It holds that
2(1+\varepsilon)g(S)\geq f(M^{opt}\,|\,S)
.

Proof.

The only elements
e=e_{t}
missing in
S
are the ones satisfying the inequality
f(e\,|\,S^{(t-1)})\leq(1+\varepsilon)\sum_{u\in e}w_{u}^{*}(e)
. So by submodularity,

\displaystyle f(M^{opt}\,|\,S)

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{e\in M^{opt}\backslash S}f(e\,|\,S)\leq\sum_{e=e_{t}\in M^{opt}\backslash S}f(e\,|\,S^{(t-1)})

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{e\in M^{opt}\backslash S}(1+\varepsilon)\sum_{u\in e}w_{u}^{*}(e)=(1+\varepsilon)\sum_{e\in M^{opt}\backslash S}\sum_{u\in e}w_{u}(e)

\displaystyle=(1+\varepsilon)\sum_{u\in V}w_{u}((M^{opt}\backslash S)\cap\delta(u))\leq(1+\varepsilon)\sum_{u\in V}w_{u}(Q_{u})

\displaystyle\leq 2(1+\varepsilon)g(S),

similar to the proof of Lemma
7
.
∎

Lemma 14
.

Algorithm
2
outputs a feasible
b
-matching with
f(M)\geq g(S)+f(\emptyset)
.

Proof.

As argued in the proof of
Lemma
8
,
M
respects the capacities and so is feasible. Now, suppose that
M=\{e_{t_{1}},\cdots,e_{t_{|M|}}\}
,
t_{1}<\cdots<t_{|M|}
. Then

\displaystyle f(M)=f(\emptyset)+\sum_{i=1}^{|M|}f(e_{t_{i}}\,|\,\{e_{t_{1}},\cdots,e_{t_{i-1}}\})

\displaystyle\geq f(\emptyset)+\sum_{i=1}^{|M|}f(e_{t_{i}}\,|\,S^{(t_{i}-1)})\geq f(\emptyset)+g(S),

as the values
f(e_{t_{i}}\,|\,S^{(t_{i-1})})
play the same role as the weights in Lemma
8
.
∎

Theorem 15
.

Algorithms
3
and
2
provide a

approximation if we set
\varepsilon=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}
.

Proof.

By Lemmas

and

,
we derive
\left(2+2\varepsilon+\frac{1+\varepsilon}{\varepsilon}\right)g(S)\geq f(M^{opt}\,|\,S)+f(S\,|\,\emptyset)=f(M^{opt}\cup S\,|\,\emptyset)\geq f(M^{opt}\,|\,\emptyset)

.
By Lemma
14
, the
output
b

\left(3+2\varepsilon+\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\right)f(M)\geq f(M^{opt}\,|\,\emptyset)+f(\emptyset)=f(M^{opt})

∎

Remark 16

When

3.3
Analysis for Non-Monotone Submodular Function Maximization

In this section, we suppose that
\frac{1}{3+2\varepsilon}\leq p\leq\frac{1}{2}
.

Lemma 17
.

It holds that

\left(2(1+\varepsilon)+\frac{1+\varepsilon}{\varepsilon}\right)\mathbb{E}[g(S)]\geq\mathbb{E}[f(S\cup M^{opt}\,|\,\emptyset)].

Proof.

From Lemma
12
we have that for any execution of the algorithm (a realization of randomness), the inequality
\frac{1+\varepsilon}{\varepsilon}g(S)\geq f(S\,|\,\emptyset)
holds, so it is also true in expectation. We will try to prove in the following that
2(1+\varepsilon)\mathbb{E}[g(S)]\geq\mathbb{E}[f(M^{opt}\,|\,S)]
, which is the counterpart of Lemma
13
.

First, we show that for any
e\in M^{opt}
:

(1+\varepsilon)\mathbb{E}\left[\sum_{u\in e}w_{u}(e)\right]\geq\mathbb{E}[f(e\,|\,S)]

(2)

We will use a conditioning similar to the one used in
[
12
]
. Let
e=e_{t}\in M^{opt}
. We consider the event
A_{e}=[f(e\,|\,S^{(t-1)})\leq(1+\varepsilon)\sum_{u\in e}w_{u}^{*}(e)]
. Notice that if
A_{e}
holds,
e
is not part of
S
and
w^{*}_{v}(e)=w_{v}(e)
for each
v\in e
. Now by submodularity,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[f(e\,|\,S)\,|\,A_{e}]\leq\mathbb{E}[f(e\,|\,S^{(t-1)})\,|\,A_{e}]

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{E}\left[(1+\varepsilon)\sum_{u\in e}w_{u}^{*}(e)\,|\,A_{e}\right]

\displaystyle=(1+\varepsilon)\mathbb{E}\left[\sum_{u\in e}w_{u}(e)\,|\,A_{e}\right]

Next we consider the condition
\overline{A_{e}}
(where the edge
e
should be added into
S
with probability
p
). As
p\leq\frac{1}{2}
, and for
e=e_{t}=\{u,v\}
we have the inequality
w_{u}(e)+w_{v}(e)=2f(e\,|\,S^{(t-1)})-w_{u}^{*}(e)-w_{v}^{*}(e)
when
e
is added to
S
, we get

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\sum_{u\in e}w_{u}(e)\,|\,\overline{A_{e}}\right]

\displaystyle=p\cdot\mathbb{E}\left[2f(e\,|\,S^{(t-1)})-\sum_{u\in e}w_{u}^{*}(e)\,|\,\overline{A_{e}}\right]+(1-p)\cdot\mathbb{E}\left[\sum_{u\in e}w_{u}^{*}(e)\,|\,\overline{A_{e}}\right]

\displaystyle=2p\cdot\mathbb{E}\left[f(e\,|\,S^{(t-1)})\,|\,\overline{A_{e}}\right]+(1-2p)\cdot\mathbb{E}\left[\sum_{u\in e}w_{u}^{*}(e)\,|\,\overline{A_{e}}\right]

\displaystyle\geq 2p\cdot\mathbb{E}\left[f(e\,|\,S^{(t-1)})\,|\,\overline{A_{e}}\right].

As a result, for
p\geq\frac{1}{3+2\varepsilon}
,

\displaystyle(1+\varepsilon)\mathbb{E}\left[\sum_{u\in e}w_{u}(e)\,|\,\overline{A_{e}}\right]

\displaystyle\geq 2p(1+\varepsilon)\cdot\mathbb{E}\left[f(e\,|\,S^{(t-1)})\,|\,\overline{A_{e}}\right]

\displaystyle\geq(1-p)\cdot\mathbb{E}\left[f(e\,|\,S^{(t-1)})\,|\,\overline{A_{e}}\right]

\displaystyle\geq\mathbb{E}\left[f(e\,|\,S)\,|\,\overline{A_{e}}\right],

where the last inequality holds
because with probability
p
we have
f(e\,|\,S)=0

(as
e\in S
)
and with probability
1-p
,
f(e\,|\,S)\leq f(e\,|\,S^{(t-1)})
(by submodularity).

So we have proven inequality (
2
) and it follows that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[f(M^{opt}\,|\,S)\right]

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{e\in M^{opt}}\mathbb{E}\left[f(e\,|\,S)\right]\leq(1+\varepsilon)\sum_{e\in M^{opt}}\mathbb{E}\left[\sum_{u\in e}w_{u}(e)\right]

\displaystyle=(1+\varepsilon)\sum_{u\in V}\sum_{e\in M^{opt}\cap\delta(u)}\mathbb{E}\left[w_{u}(e)\right]\leq(1+\varepsilon)\sum_{u\in V}\mathbb{E}\left[w_{u}(Q_{u})\right]

\displaystyle=2(1+\varepsilon)\mathbb{E}[g(S)],

where in the last inequality we use the fact that Lemma
6
holds for every realization of randomness.

Now the bounds on
\mathbb{E}\left[f(M^{opt}\,|\,S)\right]
and the bound on
\mathbb{E}\left[f(S\,|\,\emptyset)\right]
argued in the beginning give the proof
of the lemma.
∎

For the streaming phase, our algorithm, formally described in Algorithm

(so if an edge

Differently from the previous two sections
where we have applied the greedy algorithm

to build a feasible

We now give a more detailed description.

. Then, at each step, if the independent family

is not a

must be dominated by some other edge in

\left(\frac{(\gamma\cdot(1+\varepsilon))^{2}}{\gamma\cdot(1+\varepsilon)-1}k^{\prime}-\frac{\gamma\cdot(1+\varepsilon)}{\gamma\cdot(1+\varepsilon)-1}\right)w_{\mathcal{M}}(S_{f})\geq\gamma\cdot(1+\varepsilon)\cdot w_{\mathcal{M}}^{*}(M^{opt}\backslash S).

As a result, we obtain

\displaystyle f(M^{opt}\,|\,S)

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{e\in M^{opt}\backslash S}f(e\,|\,S)\leq\sum_{e=e_{t}\in M^{opt}\backslash S}f(e\,|\,S^{(t-1)})

\displaystyle\leq(1+\varepsilon)\left(\sum_{e\in M^{opt}\backslash S}\sum_{u\in e}w_{u}^{*}(e)+\gamma\cdot w_{\mathcal{M}}^{*}(e)\right)

\displaystyle\leq\left((1+\varepsilon)k+\frac{(\gamma\cdot(1+\varepsilon))^{2}}{\gamma\cdot(1+\varepsilon)-1}k^{\prime}-\frac{\gamma\cdot(1+\varepsilon)}{\gamma\cdot(1+\varepsilon)-1}\right)g(S).

∎

The following lemma states that
S_{f}
retains a reasonably
large fraction of the gains compared to
S
. For this lemma, we use the notion of

. The erasing tree

of an element

is defined recursively: it is made of the elements

of

that were evicted from

when

associated with these evicted elements.

Lemma 42
.

It holds that
\left(1+\frac{1}{\gamma\cdot(1+\varepsilon)-1}\right)g(S_{f})\geq g(S)
.

Proof.

We have, for all element

,

g(e)\geq(\gamma\cdot(1+\varepsilon)-1)w_{\mathcal{M}}^{*}(e)=(\gamma\cdot(1+\varepsilon)-1)\sum_{e^{\prime}\in T_{e}}g(e^{\prime}).

where

and the proof follows.
∎

Lemma 43
.

It holds that

\left(1+\frac{1}{\gamma\cdot(1+\varepsilon)-1}\right)\cdot\left((1+\varepsilon)k+\frac{(\gamma\cdot(1+\varepsilon))^{2}}{\gamma\cdot(1+\varepsilon)-1}k^{\prime}-\frac{\gamma\cdot(1+\varepsilon)}{\gamma\cdot(1+\varepsilon)-1}+\frac{1+\varepsilon}{\varepsilon}\right)g(S_{f})\\
\geq f(M^{opt}\,|\,\emptyset).

Proof.

By Lemmas
40
and
41
, we have that

\left(1+\frac{1}{\gamma\cdot(1+\varepsilon)-1}\right)\cdot\left((1+\varepsilon)k+\frac{(\gamma\cdot(1+\varepsilon))^{2}}{\gamma\cdot(1+\varepsilon)-1}k^{\prime}-\frac{\gamma\cdot(1+\varepsilon)}{\gamma\cdot(1+\varepsilon)-1}+1+\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\right)g(S_{f})\\
\geq f(M^{opt}\,|\,S)+f(S\,|\,\emptyset)=f(M^{opt}\cup S\,|\,\emptyset)\geq f(M^{opt}\,|\,\emptyset)

because

is monotone. Then we use Lemma

.
∎

Lemma 44
.

With
S_{f}
as input, Algorithm

returns a feasible
b
-matching

with

.

As argued in Lemma

In this section, we assume

The following lemma is the counterpart of Lemma

Lemma 46
.

We have

\left((1+\varepsilon)k+\frac{(\gamma\cdot(1+\varepsilon))^{2}}{\gamma\cdot(1+\varepsilon)-1}k^{\prime}+\frac{1+\varepsilon}{\varepsilon}\right)\mathbb{E}[g(S)]\geq\mathbb{E}[f(S\cup M^{opt}\,|\,\emptyset)]
.

Proof.

By Lemma
40
, for any realization on randomness, we have
\frac{1+\varepsilon}{\varepsilon}g(S)\geq f(S\,|\,\emptyset)
, so the inequality also holds in expectation.

We next show that, for any

we have

\mathbb{E}[f(e\,|\,S)]\leq(1+\varepsilon)\mathbb{E}\left[\sum_{u\in e}w_{u}(e)+\frac{w_{\mathcal{M}}^{\prime}(e)}{1+\varepsilon}\right].

(4)

Let
e\in M^{opt}
. Conditioning on the event
A_{e}=[f(e\,|\,S^{(t-1)})\leq(1+\varepsilon)(\sum_{u\in e}w_{u}^{*}(e)+\gamma\cdot w_{\mathcal{M}}^{*}(e))]
(
i.e.

e
cannot be part of
S
), we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[f(e\,|\,S)\,|\,A_{e}]

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{E}[f(e\,|\,S^{(t-1)})\,|\,A_{e}]

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{E}\left[(1+\varepsilon)(\sum_{u\in e}w_{u}^{*}(e)+\gamma\cdot w_{\mathcal{M}}^{*}(e))\,|\,A_{e}\right]

\displaystyle=(1+\varepsilon)\mathbb{E}\left[\sum_{u\in e}w_{u}(e)+\frac{1}{1+\varepsilon}\cdot w_{\mathcal{M}}^{\prime}(e)\,|\,A_{e}\right].

For the condition
\overline{A_{e}}
, recall that it means
that with probability
p
, the edge is added into
S

and with probability
1-p
, it is not. So

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\sum_{u\in e}w_{u}(e)+\frac{1}{1+\varepsilon}\cdot w_{\mathcal{M}}^{\prime}(e)\,|\,\overline{A_{e}}\right]

\displaystyle=\,

\displaystyle p\cdot\mathbb{E}\left[\left(k+\frac{1}{1+\varepsilon}\right)f(e\,|\,S^{(t-1)})-\left(k+\frac{1}{1+\varepsilon}-1\right)\sum_{u\in e}w_{u}^{*}(e)+kw_{\mathcal{M}}^{*}(e)\,|\,\overline{A_{e}}\right]

\displaystyle\quad+(1-p)\cdot\mathbb{E}\left[\sum_{u\in e}w_{u}^{*}(e)+\gamma\cdot w_{\mathcal{M}}^{*}(e)\,|\,\overline{A_{e}}\right]

\displaystyle\geq\,

\displaystyle p\cdot\mathbb{E}\left[\left(k+\frac{1}{1+\varepsilon}\right)f(e\,|\,S^{(t-1)})-k\sum_{u\in e}w_{u}^{*}(e)-k\cdot\gamma\cdot w_{\mathcal{M}}^{*}(e)\,|\,\overline{A_{e}}\right]

\displaystyle\quad+(1-p)\cdot\mathbb{E}\left[\sum_{u\in e}w_{u}^{*}(e)+\gamma\cdot w_{\mathcal{M}}^{*}(e)\,|\,\overline{A_{e}}\right]

\displaystyle=\,

\displaystyle\left(k+\frac{1}{1+\varepsilon}\right)\cdot p\cdot\mathbb{E}\left[f(e\,|\,S^{(t-1)})\,|\,\overline{A_{e}}\right]

\displaystyle\quad+(1-(k+1)\cdot p)\cdot\mathbb{E}\left[\sum_{u\in e}w_{u}^{*}(e)+\gamma\cdot w_{\mathcal{M}}^{*}(e)\,|\,\overline{A_{e}}\right]

\displaystyle\geq\,

\displaystyle\left(k+\frac{1}{1+\varepsilon}\right)\cdot p\cdot\mathbb{E}\left[f(e\,|\,S^{(t-1)})\,|\,\overline{A_{e}}\right],

where in the second step we use the fact that
\gamma>1
and in the last inequality that

.

Now as

, we have

\displaystyle(1+\varepsilon)\mathbb{E}\left[\sum_{u\in e}w_{u}(e)+\frac{w_{\mathcal{M}}^{\prime}(e)}{1+\varepsilon}\,|\,\overline{A_{e}}\right]

\displaystyle\geq(1+\varepsilon)\left(k+\frac{1}{1+\varepsilon}\right)

\displaystyle\quad\cdot p\cdot\mathbb{E}\left[f(e\,|\,S^{(t-1)})\,|\,\overline{A_{e}}\right]

\displaystyle\geq(1-p)\cdot\mathbb{E}\left[f(e\,|\,S^{(t-1)})\,|\,\overline{A_{e}}\right]

\displaystyle\geq\mathbb{E}\left[f(e\,|\,S)\,|\,\overline{A_{e}}\right],

and we have established (
4
).

Similar to the proof of Lemma
17
we get

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[f(M^{opt}\,|\,S)\right]

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{e\in M^{opt}}\mathbb{E}\left[f(e\,|\,S)\right]

\displaystyle\leq(1+\varepsilon)\sum_{e\in M^{opt}}\mathbb{E}\left[\sum_{u\in e}w_{u}(e)\right]+\mathbb{E}[w_{\mathcal{M}}^{\prime}(M^{opt})]

\displaystyle\leq k(1+\varepsilon)\mathbb{E}[g(S)]+\mathbb{E}[w_{\mathcal{M}}^{\prime}(M^{opt})].

By Proposition
39
we know that for any realization of randomness,

w_{\mathcal{M}}^{\prime}(M^{opt})\leq\frac{(\gamma\cdot(1+\varepsilon))^{2}}{\gamma\cdot(1+\varepsilon)-1}k^{\prime}w_{\mathcal{M}}^{\prime}(S_{f})=\frac{(\gamma\cdot(1+\varepsilon))^{2}}{\gamma\cdot(1+\varepsilon)-1}k^{\prime}\cdot g(S).

Thus we get
\mathbb{E}\left[f(M^{opt}\,|\,S)\right]\leq\left((1+\varepsilon)k+\frac{(\gamma\cdot(1+\varepsilon))^{2}}{\gamma\cdot(1+\varepsilon)-1}k^{\prime}\right)\mathbb{E}[g(S)]
.

Now the bound on
\mathbb{E}\left[f(M^{opt}\,|\,S)\right]

and the bound on
\mathbb{E}\left[f(S\,|\,\emptyset)\right]
(argued in the beginning of the proof) give us the lemma.
∎

Finally, using Lemma
18
we obtain:

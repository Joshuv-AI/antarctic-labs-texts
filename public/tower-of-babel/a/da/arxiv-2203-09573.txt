Two-sided Poisson control of linear diffusions
Harto Saarinen

Abstract
. We study a class of two-sided optimal control problems of general linear diffusions under a so-called Poisson constraint: the controlling is only allowed at the arrival times of an independent Poisson signal processes. We give a weak and easily verifiable set of sufficient conditions under which we derive a quasi-explicit unique solution to the problem in terms of the minimal r r -excessive mappings of the diffusion. We also investigate limiting properties of the solutions with respect to the signal intensity of the Poisson process. Lastly, we illustrate our results with an explicit example. Keywords. Two-sided control, Linear diffusion, Resolvent operator, Poisson process 2010 Mathematics Subject Classification : 93E20, 60J60

Introduction

In a classical stochastic singular control problems the objective is to maximise the expected discounted cumulative yield, given by a function of a stochastic process, and the maximisation takes place over controls that can be continuously applied. In these problems, the controlling can be allowed only downwards or upwards (one-sided problems) or both (two-sided problems) depending on the application. Both one-sided and two-sided singular problems have been studied extensively due to their mathematical attractiveness and applicability in various fields. These include, for example, a reversible or irreversible investment problems (cf.
[
29
,
7
,
10
]
), where the controls can be interpreted as investor purchasing capital and possibly selling it, and rational harvesting (cf.
[
23
,
5
]
), where the controls can be seen as harvesting and replanting.

In Poisson optimal control problems, also called constrained control problems (these terminologies are from
[
15
]
and
[

[

]
and
[
24
]
due to liquidity effects when trading financial assets and in
[
18
]
the possibility of modelling imperfect flow of information is considered. Nowadays the literature on Poisson type control problems is quite extensive. Some examples include optimal stopping
[

,
17
]
, stopping games
[
21
,

]
, ergodic control
[

,

[

In the classical singular control problem when the controlling is costless the optimal policy is often a local time type reflecting barrier policy. The effect of introducing the Poisson restriction to control opportunities has usually similar effect on the optimal strategy, although for different reasons as introducing a constant transaction cost to the model. Indeed, when the controlling is costly the optimal strategy is a sequential impulse control, where the decision maker chooses a sequence of stopping times
\{\tau_{1},\tau_{2},\ldots\}

We extend the Poisson control framework in the following way. Our problem setting is closely related to

The remainder of the study is organized as follows. In Section 2 we set up the underlying dynamics and formulate the two-sided Poisson control problem. Main assumptions and auxiliary calculations are done in Section 3, whereas in Section 4 we derive a candidate solution for the problem and verify its optimality. Asymptotic results connecting the problem to the singular problem are proved in Section 5. The study is concluded by considering explicit example in Section 6.

2.
Underlying dynamics and problem setting

Let
(\Omega,\mathcal{F},\{\mathcal{F}_{t}\}_{t\geq 0},\mathbb{P})
be a filtered probability space that satisfies the usual conditions. We consider an uncontrolled process
X_{t}
defined on
(\Omega,\mathcal{F},\{\mathcal{F}_{t}\}_{t\geq 0},\mathbb{P})
, which lives in
\mathbb{R}_{+}
, and is given as a strong solution to a regular Itô diffusion

dX_{t}=\mu(X_{t})dt+\sigma(X_{t})dW_{t},\quad\quad X_{0}=x,

where
W_{t}
is the Wiener process and the functions
\mu:\mathbb{R}_{+}\to\mathbb{R}
and
\sigma:\mathbb{R}_{+}\to\mathbb{R}_{+}
are sufficiently smooth (see e.g.
[
14
]
chapter 5). The boundaries of the state space
\mathbb{R}_{+}
are assumed to be natural. Even though we consider the case where the process evolves in
\mathbb{R}_{+}
, we remark that the results would remain the same with obvious changes even if the state space would be replaced with any interval.

As usual, we define the second-order linear differential operator
\mathcal{A}
which represents the infinitesimal generator of the diffusion
X
as

\mathcal{A}=\mu(x)\frac{d}{dx}+\frac{1}{2}\sigma^{2}(x)\frac{d^{2}}{dx^{2}},

and for a given
r>0
we respectively denote the increasing and decreasing solutions to the differential equation
(\mathcal{A}-r)f=0
by
\psi_{r}>0
and
\varphi_{r}>0
. These solutions are called
fundamental solutions
or
minimal excessive mappings
(
[
6
]
p.19, p.33).

For
r>0
, we denote by
\mathcal{L}_{1}^{r}
the set of functions
f
on
\mathbb{R}_{+}
, which satisfy the integrability condition

\mathbb{E}_{x}\bigg[\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-rs}|f(X_{s})|ds\bigg]<\infty.

For any
f\in\mathcal{L}_{1}^{r}
, we define the functional
(R_{r}f):\mathbb{R}_{+}\to\mathbb{R}
by

(R_{r}f)(x)=\mathbb{E}_{x}\bigg[\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-rs}f(X_{s})ds\bigg].

This functional, called the
resolvent
, is the inverse of the differential operator
r-\mathcal{A}
.

Also define the scale density of the diffusion by

S^{\prime}(x)=\exp\bigg(-\int^{x}\frac{2\mu(z)}{\sigma^{2}(z)}dz\bigg),

which is the derivative of the monotonic (and non-constant) solution to the differential equation
\mathcal{A}S=0
. Given the scale density and the fundamental solutions, the resolvent
(R_{r}f)(x)
can be re-expressed as (
[
6
]
p.19)

\displaystyle(R_{r}f)(x)=B_{r}^{-1}\varphi_{r}(x)\int_{0}^{x}\psi_{r}(y)f(y)m^{\prime}(y)dy+B_{r}^{-1}\psi_{r}(x)\int_{x}^{\infty}\varphi_{r}(y)f(y)m^{\prime}(y)dy,

where

B_{r}=\frac{\psi_{r}^{\prime}(x)}{S^{\prime}(x)}\varphi_{r}(x)-\frac{\varphi_{r}^{\prime}(x)}{S^{\prime}(x)}\psi_{r}(x)

is the constant Wronskian (does not depend on
x
) and

m^{\prime}(x)=\frac{2}{\sigma^{2}(x)S^{\prime}(x)}

is the density of the speed measure. We also recall the resolvent equation (
[
6
]
p. 4)

(1)

R_{q}R_{r}=\frac{R_{r}-R_{q}}{q-r},

where
q>r>0
.

Having setup the underlying dynamics we next describe the control problem. We study a maximization problem of the expected value of the cumulative payoff when the controlling of
X
is allowed only at the jump times of a signal process
N
.

Assumption 1
.

The process
N
is assumed to be Poisson process with parameter
\lambda
, that is independent of
X
. Further, the filtration
\{\mathcal{F}_{t}\}_{t\geq 0}
is augmented such that it is rich enough to carry the Poisson process.

We call a control policy
(\zeta^{d}_{t},\zeta^{u}_{t})
admissible if both processes are non-negative, non-decreasing, right-continuous and can be represented as

\zeta^{d}_{t}=\int_{0}^{t-}\eta^{d}_{s}dN_{s},\,\,\quad\zeta^{u}_{t}=\int_{0}^{t-}\eta^{u}_{s}dN_{s},

where
\eta^{d}
and
\eta^{u}
are
\{F_{t}\}
-predictable processes. Thus, the controlled process
X^{\zeta}_{t}
is given by

X^{\zeta}_{t}=x+\int_{0}^{t}\mu(X_{s})ds+\int_{0}^{t}\sigma(X_{s})dW_{s}-\zeta^{d}_{t}+\zeta^{u}_{t}.

Denote

J(x,\zeta)=\mathbb{E}_{x}\bigg[\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-rs}\big(\pi(X^{\zeta}_{s})ds+\gamma_{d}d\zeta_{d}-\gamma_{u}d\zeta_{u}\big)\bigg].

Then the optimal control problem is to find the optimal value function

(2)

V(x)=\sup_{(\zeta_{d},\zeta_{u})}J(x,\zeta),

where the supremum is taken over all admissible controls and
\gamma_{d}
and
-\gamma_{u}
are constants, called the unit price and unit cost, respectively. The aim is also to characterize semi-explicitly the optimal control policy
(\zeta_{d}^{*},\zeta_{u}^{*})
that realizes the supremum in (
2
).

We end this section by stating useful bounds on the value function that hold in given regions of the state space. The lemma follows directly from corollary 2.4 of
[
18
]
.

Lemma 1
.

(i)

If there exists an interval
(b,\infty)
of the state space
\mathbb{R}_{+}
such that it is always sub-optimal to use the control
\zeta^{u}_{t}
, the value function satisfies

V(x)\leq(R_{r+\lambda}\pi_{\gamma_{d}})(x)+\frac{\lambda}{r}\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}_{+}}(R_{r+\lambda}\theta_{d})(x)

when
x>b
.

(ii)

If there exists an interval
(0,a)
of the state space
\mathbb{R}_{+}
, such that it is always sub-optimal to use the control
\zeta^{d}_{t}
, the value satisfies

V(x)\geq(R_{r+\lambda}\pi_{\gamma_{u}})(x)+\frac{\lambda}{r}\inf_{x\in\mathbb{R}_{+}}(R_{r+\lambda}\theta_{u})(x)

when
x<a
.

4.
The solution

4.1.
The associated free boundary problem

Similar to
[
9
,
18
,
19
]
, we can use straightforward heuristic arguments to formulate a free boundary problem for the candidate value function of the control problem. Denoting the positive twice continuously differentiable candidate value function by

and two constant boundaries by

, the heuristics give us the free boundary problem

(7)

\displaystyle(\mathcal{A}-r)F(x)=-\pi(x),

\displaystyle a<x<b

(8)

\displaystyle(\mathcal{A}-(r+\lambda))F(x)=-\pi(x)-\lambda(\gamma_{d}(x-b)+F(b)),

\displaystyle x\geq b

(9)

\displaystyle(\mathcal{A}-(r+\lambda))F(x)=-\pi(x)-\lambda(\gamma_{u}(x-a)+F(a)),

\displaystyle x\leq a

(10)

\displaystyle F^{\prime}(b)=\gamma_{d},

(11)

\displaystyle F^{\prime}(a)=\gamma_{u}.

We first consider the equation (
7
). This equation gives

F(x)=B_{1}\varphi_{r}(x)+B_{2}\psi_{r}(x)+(R_{r}\pi)(x),\quad a<x<b,

where

and

are constants. Using the first order conditions (

) and (

B_{1}=\frac{\gamma_{d}\psi_{r}^{\prime}(a)-\gamma_{u}\psi_{r}^{\prime}(b)+\psi_{r}^{\prime}(b)(R_{r}\pi)^{\prime}(a)-\psi_{r}^{\prime}(a)(R_{r}\pi)^{\prime}(b)}{\varphi_{r}^{\prime}(b)\psi_{r}^{\prime}(a)-\varphi_{r}^{\prime}(a)\psi_{r}^{\prime}(b)}

B_{2}=\frac{-\gamma_{d}\varphi_{r}^{\prime}(a)+\gamma_{u}\varphi_{r}^{\prime}(b)-\varphi_{r}^{\prime}(b)(R_{r}\pi)^{\prime}(a)+\varphi_{r}^{\prime}(a)(R_{r}\pi)^{\prime}(b)}{\varphi_{r}^{\prime}(b)\psi_{r}^{\prime}(a)-\varphi_{r}^{\prime}(a)\psi_{r}^{\prime}(b)}.

\displaystyle F(x)

\displaystyle=C\varphi_{r+\lambda}(x)+(R_{r+\lambda}\pi_{\gamma_{d}})(x)

\displaystyle F(x)

\displaystyle=D\psi_{r+\lambda}(x)+(R_{r+\lambda}\pi_{\gamma_{u}})(x)

\displaystyle+\frac{\lambda}{r}\bigg[D\psi_{r+\lambda}(a)+(R_{r+\lambda}\pi_{\gamma_{u}})(a)-\gamma_{u}a\bigg],\quad\,x<a,

where the functions

To solve the boundary points

\displaystyle\frac{\gamma_{d}-(R_{r+\lambda}\pi_{\gamma_{d}})^{\prime}(b)}{\varphi_{r+\lambda}^{\prime}(b)}\varphi_{r+\lambda}^{\prime\prime}(b)+(R_{r+\lambda}\pi_{\gamma_{d}})^{\prime\prime}(b)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{g^{\prime}_{d}(b)\varphi^{\prime\prime}_{r+\lambda}(b)-\lambda(R_{r+\lambda}g_{d})^{\prime}(b)\varphi^{\prime\prime}_{r+\lambda}(b)}{\varphi_{r+\lambda}^{\prime}(b)}+\lambda(R_{r+\lambda}g_{d})^{\prime\prime}(b)+(R_{r}\pi)^{\prime\prime}(b)

Here, by lemma

To deal with the left-hand side of (

\displaystyle+

-2rS^{\prime}(b)\sigma^{-2}(b)B_{r}=\psi^{\prime\prime}_{r}(b)\varphi_{r}^{\prime}(b)-\varphi_{r}^{\prime\prime}(b)\psi_{r}^{\prime}(b)

\displaystyle\frac{g_{d}^{\prime}(b)(\varphi^{\prime\prime}_{r}(b)\psi_{r}^{\prime}(a)-\varphi_{r}^{\prime}(a)\psi^{\prime\prime}_{r}(b))}{\varphi_{r}^{\prime}(b)\psi_{r}^{\prime}(a)-\varphi_{r}^{\prime}(a)\psi_{r}^{\prime}(b)}-g^{\prime}_{d}(b)\frac{\varphi^{\prime\prime}_{r+\lambda}(b)}{\varphi_{r+\lambda}^{\prime}(b)}

\displaystyle\frac{g_{b}^{\prime}(b)\big(\psi_{r}^{\prime}(a)(\varphi_{r+\lambda}^{\prime}(b)\varphi^{\prime\prime}_{r}(b)-\varphi^{\prime\prime}_{r+\lambda}(b)\varphi_{r}^{\prime}(b))}{\varphi_{r+\lambda}^{\prime}(b)(\varphi_{r}^{\prime}(b)\psi_{r}^{\prime}(a)-\varphi_{r}^{\prime}(a)\psi_{r}^{\prime}(b))}

\varphi_{r+\lambda}^{\prime}(b)\varphi^{\prime\prime}_{r}(b)-\varphi^{\prime\prime}_{r+\lambda}(b)\varphi_{r}^{\prime}(b)=-2\lambda S^{\prime}(b)\sigma^{-2}(b)L_{\varphi}(b)

and

\varphi^{\prime\prime}_{r+\lambda}(b)\psi_{r}^{\prime}(b)-\varphi^{\prime}_{r+\lambda}(b)\psi_{r}^{\prime\prime}(b)=2\lambda S^{\prime}(b)\sigma^{-2}(b)L_{\psi}(b).

Finally, using the above calculations the

\frac{g_{d}^{\prime}(b)\big(\varphi_{r}^{\prime}(a)L_{\psi}(b)-\psi_{r}^{\prime}(a)L_{\varphi}(b)\big)-\frac{r}{\lambda}g_{u}^{\prime}(a)\varphi_{r+\lambda}^{\prime}(b)B_{r}}{\varphi_{r}^{\prime}(b)\psi_{r}^{\prime}(a)-\varphi_{r}^{\prime}(a)\psi_{r}^{\prime}(b)}+L_{g_{d}}(b)=0.

Reorganizing the above we have

\scalebox{0.96}{$\psi_{r}^{\prime}(a)\bigg(\frac{\varphi_{r}^{\prime}(b)L_{g_{d}}(b)-g_{d}^{\prime}(b)L_{\varphi}(b)}{\varphi_{r+\lambda}^{\prime}(b)}\bigg)+\varphi_{r}^{\prime}(a)\bigg(\frac{g_{d}^{\prime}(b)L_{\psi}(b)-\psi_{r}^{\prime}(b)L_{g_{d}}(b)}{\varphi_{r+\lambda}^{\prime}(b)}\bigg)=\frac{r}{\lambda}B_{r}g_{u}^{\prime}(a)$}.

By entirely similar arguments we arrive at the lower boundary to the equation

\scalebox{0.96}{$\psi_{r}^{\prime}(b)\bigg(\frac{g_{u}^{\prime}(a)K_{\varphi}(a)-\varphi_{r}^{\prime}(a)K_{g_{u}}(a)}{\psi_{r+\lambda}^{\prime}(a)}\bigg)+\varphi_{r}^{\prime}(b)\bigg(\frac{\psi_{r}^{\prime}(a)K_{g_{u}}(a)-g_{u}^{\prime}(a)K_{\psi}(a)}{\psi_{r+\lambda}^{\prime}(a)}\bigg)=\frac{r}{\lambda}B_{r}g_{d}^{\prime}(b)$}.

Since the function
F
is

(a,b)
, we find by uniqueness, that the optimal thresholds should satisfy the pair of equations

\displaystyle\frac{\psi_{r}^{\prime}(a)K_{g_{u}}(a)-g_{u}^{\prime}(a)K_{\psi}(a)}{\psi^{\prime}_{r+\lambda}(a)}=\frac{g_{d}^{\prime}(b)L_{\psi}(b)-\psi_{r}^{\prime}(b)L_{g_{d}}(b)}{\varphi^{\prime}_{r+\lambda}(b)},

\displaystyle\frac{\varphi_{r}^{\prime}(a)K_{g_{u}}(a)-g_{u}^{\prime}(a)K_{\varphi}(a)}{\psi^{\prime}_{r+\lambda}(a)}=\frac{g_{d}^{\prime}(b)L_{\varphi}(b)-\varphi_{r}^{\prime}(b)L_{g_{d}}(b)}{\varphi^{\prime}_{r+\lambda}(b)}.

Using the notation defined in lemma
5
the pair can be written as

(13)

\begin{cases}H(\psi_{r},g_{u};a)=Q(g_{d},\psi_{r};b),\\
H(\varphi_{r},g_{u};a)=Q(g_{d},\varphi_{r};b).\end{cases}

4.2.
Uniqueness and existence

In this section we prove that an unique solution
(a^{*},b^{*})
to the pair of equations (
13
) exists. To this end, we use a slight generalisation of the method that was first used in
[
16
]
and
[
4
]
in a analogous setting and notice that in the solution
(a^{*},b^{*})
the point
a^{*}
must necessarily be a fixed point of the function

K(x)=H_{u,\varphi}^{-1}(Q_{d,\varphi}(Q_{d,\psi}^{-1}(H_{u,\psi}(x)))),

where slightly shorter notation

\displaystyle H_{u,\varphi}(x)=H(\varphi_{r},g_{u};x),\quad H_{u,\psi}(x)=H(\psi_{r},g_{u};x),

\displaystyle Q_{d,\psi}(x)=Q(g_{d},\psi_{r};x),\quad Q_{d,\varphi}(x)=Q(g_{d},\varphi_{r};x),

is introduced. Thus, in order to prove that the solution exists we must first ensure that
K
is well-defined and then study its fixed points.

The following is the main result on the uniqueness of the solution.

Proposition 1
.

Let the assumptions 1 and 2 hold and assume that the pair of equations

\begin{cases}H(\psi_{r},g_{u};a)=Q(g_{d},\psi_{r};b),\\
H(\varphi_{r},g_{u};a)=Q(g_{d},\varphi_{r};b).\end{cases}

has a solution
(a^{*},b^{*})
. Then the solution is unique.

Proof.

Let
K:(0,\tilde{x}]\to(0,\tilde{x}]
be defined as

(14)

K(x)=\check{H}_{u,\varphi}^{-1}(\hat{Q}_{d,\varphi}(\hat{Q}_{d,\psi}^{-1}(\check{H}_{u,\psi}(x)))),

where
\hat{\cdot}
and
\check{\cdot}
are restrictions to domains
[\hat{x},\infty)
and
(0,\tilde{x}]
respectively. We notice that if a solution
(a^{*},b^{*})
to the pair exists, then
a^{*}
must be fixed point of
K
. Because the functions
H
and
Q
are monotonic in their domains we get

(15)

\displaystyle K^{\prime}(x)=

\displaystyle\check{H}_{u,\varphi}^{-1^{\prime}}(\hat{Q}_{d,\varphi}(\hat{Q}_{d,\psi}^{-1}(\check{H}_{u,\psi}(x))))\cdot\hat{Q}_{d,\varphi}^{\prime}(\hat{Q}_{d,\psi}^{-1}(\check{H}_{u,\psi}(x)))

\displaystyle\cdot

\displaystyle\hat{Q}_{d,\psi}^{-1^{\prime}}(\check{H}_{u,\psi}(x))\cdot\check{H}_{u,\psi}^{\prime}(x)>0,

and hence
K
is increasing in its domain
(0,\tilde{x}_{i}]
. We observe using the fixed point property that

K^{\prime}(a^{*})=\frac{\hat{Q}_{d,\varphi}^{\prime}(b^{*})}{\hat{Q}_{d,\psi}^{\prime}(b^{*})}\frac{\check{H}_{u,\psi}^{\prime}(a^{*})}{\check{H}_{u,\varphi}^{\prime}(a^{*})}=\frac{L_{\varphi}^{r+\lambda}(b^{*})}{L_{\psi}^{r+\lambda}(b^{*})}\frac{K_{\varphi}^{r+\lambda}(a^{*})}{K_{\psi}^{r+\lambda}(a^{*})}<\frac{\varphi_{r}(b^{*})}{\varphi_{r}(a^{*})}\frac{\psi_{r}(a^{*})}{\psi_{r}(b^{*})}<1.

Consequently, whenever
K
intersects the diagonal of
\mathbb{R}_{+}
, the intersection must be from above.
∎

To prove the existence of the solution we need to study the function
K
in more detail. First, we analyze the limiting properties of the functions
Q
and
H
. We notice that by lemma
2
and
3
we have

\displaystyle\varphi_{r+\lambda}^{\prime}(x)Q(g_{d},\varphi_{r};x)

\displaystyle=g_{d}^{\prime}(x)L_{\varphi}^{r+\lambda}(x)-\varphi_{r}^{\prime}(x)L_{g_{d}}^{r+\lambda}(x)

\displaystyle=-\frac{\sigma(x)^{2}}{2\lambda S^{\prime}(x)}\big[\varphi_{r}(x)(\varphi_{r+\lambda}^{\prime\prime}(x)(R_{r+\lambda}\theta_{d})^{\prime}(x)-\varphi_{r+\lambda}^{\prime}(x)(R_{r+\lambda}\theta_{d})^{\prime\prime}(x))

\displaystyle+\varphi_{r+\lambda}^{\prime}(x)(\varphi_{r}^{\prime\prime}(x)g_{d}^{\prime}-\varphi_{r}^{\prime}(x)g_{d}^{\prime\prime}(x))\big]

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\lambda}\big[\varphi_{r+\lambda}^{\prime}(x)L_{\theta_{d}}^{r}(x)-\varphi_{r}^{\prime}(x)L_{\theta_{d}}^{r+\lambda}(x)\big].

Carrying out similar calculations for
H(\psi_{r},g_{u};a),

Q(g_{d},\psi_{r};b)
and
H(\varphi_{r},g_{u};a)
the pair of equations reads as

(16)

\displaystyle\frac{\psi_{r}^{\prime}(a)}{\psi_{r+\lambda}^{\prime}(a)}K_{\theta_{u}}^{r+\lambda}(a)-K_{\theta_{u}}^{r}(a)=-\frac{\psi_{r}^{\prime}(b)}{\varphi_{r+\lambda}^{\prime}(b)}L_{\theta_{d}}^{r+\lambda}(b)-K_{\theta_{d}}^{r}(b),

\displaystyle\frac{\varphi_{r}^{\prime}(a)}{\psi_{r+\lambda}^{\prime}(a)}K_{\theta_{u}}^{r+\lambda}(a)+L_{\theta_{u}}^{r}(a)=-\frac{\varphi_{r}^{\prime}(b)}{\varphi_{r+\lambda}^{\prime}(b)}L_{\theta_{d}}^{r+\lambda}(b)+L_{\theta_{d}}^{r}(b).

Assuming that
x>\max(\tilde{x},x_{d}^{0})
(here
x_{d}^{0}
is the unique root of
\theta_{d}(x)
) we find using mean value theorem that

\displaystyle Q(g_{d},\psi_{r};x)=-\frac{\psi_{r}^{\prime}(x)}{\varphi_{r+\lambda}^{\prime}(x)}L_{\theta_{d}}^{r+\lambda}(x)-K_{\theta_{d}}^{r}(x)

\displaystyle=-(r+\lambda)\frac{\psi_{r}^{\prime}(x)}{\varphi_{r+\lambda}^{\prime}(x)}\int_{x}^{\infty}\theta_{d}(y)\varphi_{r+\lambda}(y)m^{\prime}(y)dy-r\int_{0}^{x}\theta_{d}(y)\psi_{r}(y)m^{\prime}(y)dy

\displaystyle=\frac{\psi_{r}^{\prime}(x)}{S^{\prime}(x)}\theta_{d}(\xi_{x})-r\int_{0}^{x}\theta_{d}(y)\psi_{r}(y)m^{\prime}(y)dy

\displaystyle<\frac{\psi_{r}^{\prime}(x)}{S^{\prime}(x)}(\theta_{d}(\xi_{x})-\theta_{d}(x))<0,

where
\xi_{x}\in(x,\infty)
. Hence,
\lim_{x\to\infty}Q(g_{d},\psi_{r};x)<0
. Similarly, we find that
\lim_{x\to 0}H(\varphi_{r},g_{u};x)>0
.

Moreover, if
x<\min(\hat{x},x_{u}^{0})
(here
x_{u}^{0}
is the unique root of
\theta_{u}(x)
) we get by mean value theorem and lemma
6
that

\displaystyle H(\psi_{r},g_{u};x)

\displaystyle=\frac{\psi_{r}^{\prime}(x)}{\psi_{r+\lambda}^{\prime}(x)}K_{\theta_{u}}^{r+\lambda}(x)-K_{\theta_{u}}^{r}(x)

\displaystyle=(r+\lambda)\frac{\psi_{r}^{\prime}(x)}{\psi_{r+\lambda}^{\prime}(x)}\int_{0}^{x}\theta_{u}(y)\frac{\psi_{r+\lambda}(y)}{\psi_{r}(y)}\psi_{r}(y)m^{\prime}(y)dy-r\int_{0}^{x}\theta_{u}(y)\psi_{r}(y)m^{\prime}(y)dy

\displaystyle=(r+\lambda)\underbrace{\frac{\frac{\psi_{r}^{\prime}(x)}{\psi_{r+\lambda}^{\prime}(x)}}{\frac{\psi_{r}(\xi_{x})}{\psi_{r+\lambda}(\xi_{x})}}}_{<1}\int_{0}^{x}\theta_{u}(y)\psi_{r}(y)m^{\prime}(y)dy-r\int_{0}^{x}\theta_{u}(y)\psi_{r}(y)m^{\prime}(y)dy,

and thus by continuity
\lim_{x\to 0+}H(\psi_{r},g_{u};x)=0
. Similarly, we find that
\lim_{x\to\infty}Q(g_{d},\varphi_{r};x)=0
. Also, because
\hat{x}
is a root of
L_{\theta_{d}}^{r+\lambda}
and
\hat{x}<x^{*}_{d}
(consequently
\theta
is increasing on
(0,\hat{x})
) we have that

\displaystyle Q(g_{d},\psi_{r};\hat{x})=-r\int_{0}^{\hat{x}}(\theta_{d}(y)-\theta_{d}(\hat{x}))\psi_{r}(y)m^{\prime}(y)dy>0.

And again, similarly
H(\varphi_{r},g_{u};\tilde{x})<0
. We summarize these findings:

(17)

\begin{cases}\,\,Q(g_{d},\varphi_{r};\infty)=0,\\
\,\,Q(g_{d},\psi_{r};\infty)<0,\\
\,\,Q(g_{d},\psi_{r};\hat{x})>0,\end{cases}\begin{cases}\,\,H(\psi_{r},g_{u};0+)=0\\
\,\,H(\varphi_{r},g_{u};0+)>0,\\
\,\,H(\varphi_{r},g_{u};\tilde{x})<0,\end{cases}

Unfortunately, the analysis so far is not enough for the existence of the solution. To guarantee that the fixed point of the function
K
exists, the inequalities

\displaystyle H(\psi_{r},g_{u};\tilde{x})\leq Q(g_{d},\psi_{r};\hat{x}),

\displaystyle H(\varphi_{r},g_{u};\tilde{x})\leq Q(g_{d},\varphi_{r};\hat{x})

have to hold. Our assumptions are not enough for these inequalities to hold in general, but the next lemma gives an easily verifiable sufficient condition.

Lemma 7
.

Assume that
\tilde{x}<\hat{x}
. Then

\displaystyle H(\psi_{r},g_{u};\tilde{x})\leq Q(g_{d},\psi_{r};\hat{x}),

\displaystyle H(\varphi_{r},g_{u};\tilde{x})\leq Q(g_{d},\varphi_{r};\hat{x})

Proof.

We prove the first inequality as the second is shown similarly. Because
\tilde{x}
and
\hat{x}
are zeros of
K_{\theta_{u}}^{r+\lambda}
and
L_{\theta_{d}}^{r+\lambda}
respectively, we note by (
16
) that the first inequality is equivalent to

K_{\theta_{d}}^{r}(\hat{x})-K_{\theta_{u}}^{r}(\tilde{x})\leq 0.

By lemma
4
we have that
\hat{x}<x^{*}_{d}
and
\tilde{x}>x_{u}^{*}
. Thus, by our assumption
\tilde{x}<\hat{x}
we have that
x_{u}^{*}<\tilde{x}<\hat{x}<x_{d}^{*}
. Hence, we find that

\displaystyle K_{\theta_{d}}^{r}(\hat{x})-K_{\theta_{u}}^{r}(\tilde{x})

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\hat{x}}\theta_{d}(z)\psi_{r}(z)m^{\prime}(z)dz-\theta_{d}(\hat{x})\int_{0}^{\hat{x}}\psi_{r}(z)m^{\prime}(z)dz

\displaystyle-\int_{0}^{\tilde{x}}\theta_{u}(z)\psi_{r}(z)m^{\prime}(z)dz+\theta_{u}(\tilde{x})\int_{0}^{\tilde{x}}\psi_{r}(z)m^{\prime}(z)dz

\displaystyle\leq\int_{0}^{\tilde{x}}(\theta_{d}(z)-\theta_{d}(\tilde{x}))\psi_{r}(z)m^{\prime}(z)dz+\int_{\tilde{x}}^{\hat{x}}(\theta_{d}(z)-\theta_{d}(\hat{x}))\psi_{r}(z)m^{\prime}(z)dz

\displaystyle-\int_{0}^{\tilde{x}}(\theta_{u}(z)-\theta_{u}(\tilde{x}))\psi_{r}(z)m^{\prime}(z)dz,

\displaystyle\leq\int_{0}^{\tilde{x}}(\theta_{d}(z)-\theta_{d}(\tilde{x})-\theta_{u}(z)+\theta_{u}(\tilde{x}))\psi_{r}(z)m^{\prime}(z)dz\leq 0,

where the first two inequalities follow from part
(v)
of assumptions
2
and last from parts
(i)
and
(iv)
of assumptions
2
.
∎

The assumption
\tilde{x}<\hat{x}
may seem restricting, but based on numerical calculations it cannot easily be relaxed, because there exists cases under the main assumptions
2
, where
\tilde{x}>\hat{x}
and the solution does not exist. Furthermore, because the points
\tilde{x}
and
\hat{x}
are known to be the unique roots of the functionals
K_{\theta_{u}}^{r+\lambda}
and
L_{\theta_{d}}^{r+\lambda}
, it is straightforward to calculate them, at least numerically, and verify the assumption.

Proposition 2
.

Let the assumptions 1 and 2 hold. Assume further that
\tilde{x}<\hat{x}
, then the pair of equations

\begin{cases}H(\psi_{r},g_{u};a)=Q(g_{d},\psi_{r};b),\\
H(\varphi_{r},g_{u};a)=Q(g_{d},\varphi_{r};b).\end{cases}

has a unique solution
(a^{*},b^{*})
.

Proof.

Define the function
K:(0,\tilde{x}]\to(0,\tilde{x}]
as in (
14
). Then by lemma
7
, remark
2
and limiting properties (
17
) the function
K
is well-defined. Further, by (
15
)
K
is monotonic mapping. Thus,
K
is a monotonic mapping from a set to its open subset, and hence it must have at least one fixed point, which we denote by
a^{*}
. Then the pair
(a^{*},b^{*})
, where
b^{*}=Q_{d,\psi}^{-1}(H_{u,\psi}(a^{*}))
, is a solution to the equations (
13
). The uniqueness follow from proposition
1
.
∎

4.3.
Verification

We begin by stating the verification theorem.

Proposition 3
.

Let assumptions
2
hold and denote by
(a^{*},b^{*})
the unique solution to the necessary conditions

\begin{cases}H(\psi_{r},g_{u};a)=Q(g_{d},\psi_{r};b),\\
H(\varphi_{r},g_{u};a)=Q(g_{d},\varphi_{r};b).\end{cases}

Then the optimal policy is as follows. If the controlled process
X^{\zeta}
is not inside the interval
(a^{*},b^{*})
at a jump time
T_{i}
of
N
, i.e.
X^{\zeta}_{T_{i-}}\not\in(a^{*},b^{*})
for any
i
, the optimal policy is to take the controlled process
X^{\zeta}
to the closest boundary of the interval
(a^{*},b^{*})
. Moreover, the optimal value function
V(x)
reads as

(18)

V(x)=\begin{cases}\frac{\gamma_{d}-(R_{r+\lambda}\pi_{\gamma_{d}})^{\prime}(b)}{\varphi_{r+\lambda}^{\prime}(b)}\varphi_{r+\lambda}(x)+(R_{r+\lambda}\pi_{\gamma_{d}})(x)+A_{d}(b),\\
B_{1}\varphi_{r}(x)+B_{2}\psi_{r}(x)+(R_{r}\pi)(x),\\
\frac{\gamma_{u}-(R_{r+\lambda}\pi_{\gamma_{u}})^{\prime}(a)}{\psi_{r+\lambda}^{\prime}(a)}\psi_{r+\lambda}(x)+(R_{r+\lambda}\pi_{\gamma_{u}})(x)+A_{u}(a),\end{cases}

where

\displaystyle B_{1}=\frac{\gamma_{d}\psi_{r}^{\prime}(a)-\gamma_{u}\psi_{r}^{\prime}(b)+\psi_{r}^{\prime}(b)(R_{r}\pi)^{\prime}(a)-\psi_{r}^{\prime}(a)(R_{r}\pi)^{\prime}(b)}{\varphi_{r}^{\prime}(b)\psi_{r}^{\prime}(a)-\varphi_{r}^{\prime}(a)\psi_{r}^{\prime}(b)},

\displaystyle B_{2}=\frac{-\gamma_{d}\varphi_{r}^{\prime}(a)+\gamma_{u}\varphi_{r}^{\prime}(b)-\varphi_{r}^{\prime}(b)(R_{r}\pi)^{\prime}(a)+\varphi_{r}^{\prime}(a)(R_{r}\pi)^{\prime}(b)}{\varphi_{r}^{\prime}(b)\psi_{r}^{\prime}(a)-\varphi_{r}^{\prime}(a)\psi_{r}^{\prime}(b)},

\displaystyle A_{d}(b)=\frac{\lambda}{r}\bigg[C\varphi_{r+\lambda}(b)+(R_{r+\lambda}\pi_{\gamma_{d}})(b)-\gamma_{d}b\bigg],

\displaystyle A_{u}(a)=\frac{\lambda}{r}\bigg[D\psi_{r+\lambda}(a)+(R_{r+\lambda}\pi_{\gamma_{u}})(a)-\gamma_{u}a\bigg].

Before proving the proposition, we show few properties of the candidate value function
F
.

Lemma 8
.

(
i
)

F^{\prime\prime}(x)\leq 0
for all
x\in(a,b)

(
ii
)

The function
x\mapsto F(x)-\gamma_{d}x
has an unique global maximum at
b
. Similarly, the function
x\mapsto F(x)-\gamma_{u}x
has an unique global maximum at
a
.

Proof.

The item
(i)
is same as part (A) of lemma 4.3 in
[
25
]
.

To prove the item
(ii)
, we find by straight differentiation in (
18
) that when
x>b

F^{\prime}(x)-\gamma_{d}=\varphi_{r+\lambda}^{\prime}(x)\bigg[\frac{(R_{r+\lambda}\pi_{\gamma_{d}})^{\prime}(x)-\gamma_{d}}{\varphi_{r+\lambda}^{\prime}(x)}-\frac{(R_{r+\lambda}\pi_{\gamma_{d}})^{\prime}(b)-\gamma_{d}}{\varphi_{r+\lambda}^{\prime}(b)}\bigg]<0,

where we have used that
b>\hat{x}
and lemma
4
. Similarly, when
x<a
we find that
F^{\prime}(x)-\gamma_{u}>0
. Furthermore, as
F^{\prime}(a+)=\gamma_{u}>\gamma_{d}=F^{\prime}(b-)
we find by item
(i)
that we must have
\gamma_{u}\leq F^{\prime}(x)\leq\gamma_{d}
, when
x\in(a,b)
. Hence, the item
(i)
follows by
F^{\prime}(x)>\gamma_{u}>\gamma_{d}
when
x<a
and
F^{\prime}(x)<\gamma_{d}<\gamma_{d}
when
x>b
.
∎

Proof of proposition
3
.

The proof is a slight modification of the proof of theorem 3.6 in
[
18
]
. Define the almost surely finite stopping times
\tau:=\rho\wedge\tau_{\rho},
where
\tau_{\rho}=\inf\{t\geq 0:X_{t}^{\zeta}\geq\rho\}
and let
x\in\mathbb{R}_{+}
. Applying generalised Ito’s lemma to the stopped process
e^{-r(t\wedge\tau)}F(X_{t\wedge\tau}^{\zeta})
we get

(19)

\displaystyle e^{-r(t\wedge\tau)}F(X_{t\wedge\tau}^{\zeta})

\displaystyle=F(x)+\int_{0}^{t\wedge\tau}e^{-rs}(\mathcal{A}-r)F(X_{s}^{\zeta})ds

\displaystyle+\int_{0}^{t\wedge\tau}e^{-rs}\sigma(X^{\zeta}_{s})F^{\prime}(X_{s}^{\zeta})dW_{s}

\displaystyle+\sum_{s\leq t\wedge\tau}e^{-rs}[F(X^{\zeta}_{s})-F(X^{\zeta}_{s-})].

Define the functions

\displaystyle\Phi_{b}(x)=\mathbbm{1}_{\{x>b\}}((F(x)-\gamma_{d}x)-(F(b)-\gamma_{d}b)),

\displaystyle\Phi_{a}(x)=\mathbbm{1}_{\{x<a\}}((F(x)-\gamma_{u}x)-(F(a)-\gamma_{u}a)).

Because the control can jump only if the Poisson process jumps we have by lemma
8
that

\displaystyle F(X^{\zeta}_{s})-F(X^{\zeta}_{s-})+\gamma_{d}\Delta\zeta^{d}_{s}\leq\lambda\Phi_{b}(X^{\zeta}_{s-}).

if the jump is down. And similarly if the jump is up we have that

\displaystyle F(X^{\zeta}_{s})-F(X^{\zeta}_{s-})-\gamma_{u}\Delta\zeta^{u}_{s}\leq\lambda\Phi_{a}(X^{\zeta}_{s-}).

Combining this with (
19
) we find

(20)

\displaystyle e^{-r(t\wedge\tau)}F(X_{t\wedge\tau}^{\zeta})+\int_{0}^{t\wedge\tau}e^{-rs}(\pi(X_{s}^{\zeta})ds+\gamma_{d}d\zeta^{d}_{s}-\gamma_{u}d\zeta^{u}_{s})

\displaystyle\leq F(x)+\int_{0}^{t\wedge\tau}e^{-rs}\sigma(X^{\zeta}_{s})F^{\prime}(X_{s}^{\zeta})dW_{s}

\displaystyle+\lambda\int_{0}^{t\wedge\tau}e^{-rs}\Phi_{b}(X_{s}^{\zeta})ds+\lambda\int_{0}^{t\wedge\tau}e^{-rs}\Phi_{a}(X_{s}^{\zeta})ds

\displaystyle+\int_{0}^{t\wedge\tau}e^{-rs}\Phi_{b}(X_{s}^{\zeta})dN_{s}+\int_{0}^{t\wedge\tau}e^{-rs}\Phi_{a}(X_{s}^{\zeta})dN_{s}

\displaystyle=F(x)+M_{t\wedge\tau}+Z_{t\wedge\tau},

where

\displaystyle M_{t}=\int_{0}^{t\wedge\tau}e^{-rs}\sigma(X^{\zeta}_{s})F^{\prime}(X_{s}^{\zeta})dW_{s},

\displaystyle Z_{t}=\int_{0}^{t\wedge\tau}e^{-rs}\Phi_{b}(X_{s}^{\zeta})d\tilde{N}_{s}+\int_{0}^{t\wedge\tau}e^{-rs}\Phi_{a}(X_{s}^{\zeta})d\tilde{N}_{s}

are local martingales and the process
\tilde{N}_{t}=N_{t}-\lambda t
is a compensated Poisson process. We notice from (
20
) that the local martingale part
M_{t\wedge\tau}+Z_{t\wedge\tau}
is bounded uniformly from below by
-F(x)
and is thus a supermartingale. Taking the expectation in (
20
) and letting
t,\rho\to\infty
we find

\displaystyle\lim_{t,\rho\to\infty}\mathbb{E}_{x}[e^{-r(t\wedge\tau)}F(X_{t\wedge\tau}^{\zeta})]+

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{x}\bigg[\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-rs}(\pi(X_{s}^{\zeta})ds+\gamma_{d}d\zeta^{d}_{s}-\gamma_{u}d\zeta^{u}_{s})ds\bigg]\leq F(x)

Hence, we conclude that

\displaystyle F(x)\geq J(x,\zeta).

To prove that the candidate value is attainable with admissible policy we show that
F(x)\leq J(x,\zeta^{*})
. We first note that as the integrand in
M_{t\wedge\tau}
is continuous and the stopped process
X^{\zeta^{*}}_{t\wedge\tau}
is bounded and thus
M_{t\wedge\tau}
is a martingale. Furthermore,

(21)

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t\wedge\tau}e^{-rs}\Phi_{b}(X^{\zeta^{*}}_{s-})d\tilde{N}_{s}\leq\int_{0}^{t\wedge\tau}e^{-rs}F(X^{\zeta^{*}}_{s-})\mathbbm{1}_{[b^{*},\infty)}ds,

because
\Phi_{b}(x)\geq-F(x)
. Since
\pi_{\gamma_{d}}(x)
and
\varphi_{r+\lambda}(x)
are in
\mathcal{L}_{1}^{r}
we observe by resolvent equation (
1
) that (
21
) is bounded uniformly from above by integrable random variable and consequently is a submartingale. Treating the other integral term in
Z_{t\wedge\tau}
similarly, we see that
Z_{t\wedge\tau}
is a submartingale.
As the inequality (
20
) is equality for the proposed optimal control, we get by taking expectations that

\displaystyle F(x)\leq\liminf_{\tau,\rho\to\infty}\mathbb{E}_{x}[e^{-r(t\wedge\tau)}F(X^{\zeta^{*}}_{t\wedge\tau})]+J(x,\zeta^{*}).

By lemma
8
we find that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{x}[e^{-r(t\wedge\tau(\rho))}F(X^{\zeta^{*}}_{t\wedge\tau(\rho)})]\leq\mathbb{E}_{x}[e^{-r(t\wedge\tau(\rho))}(F(b^{*})+\gamma(X^{\zeta^{*}}_{t\wedge\tau(\rho)}-b^{*}))].

Hence, we must have
\liminf_{\tau,\rho\to\infty}\mathbb{E}_{x}[e^{-r(t\wedge\tau)}F(X^{\zeta^{*}}_{t\wedge\tau})]=0
, as otherwise we would contradict the assumption that
\text{id}\in\mathcal{L}_{1}^{r}
. Consequently,
V(x)=J(x,\zeta^{*})
.
∎

Remark 3
.

We note that the above verification theorem and uniqueness of the optimal pair in proposition
1
, do not require most of our assumptions, and these are only needed to prove the existence of the solution. Thus, other type of assumptions could be allowed as long as one can be sure that the solution exists. Unfortunately, it seems very hard to find general conditions that cover interesting cases. A hint to this direction is given in p. 252 of
[
25
]
, where the shape of the functions
\theta_{n}
are somewhat relaxed, but then more restrictive assumptions about the boundary behaviour of the diffusion are needed.

5.
Note on Asymptotics

A similar control problem, where controlling is not restricted by a signal process is studied in
[
25
]
. In this singular control case, we know that under the assumptions
2
, the optimal control thresholds
(a_{s}^{*},b_{s}^{*})
are the unique solution to the pair of equations

(22)

\begin{split}&L^{r}_{\theta_{u}}(a^{*}_{s})=L^{r}_{\theta_{d}}(b^{*}_{s}),\\
&K^{r}_{\theta_{u}}(a^{*}_{s})=K^{r}_{\theta_{d}}(b^{*}_{s}).\end{split}

Intuitively, this solution should coincide with ours when the signal rate
\lambda
tend to infinity, because then the controlling opportunities are more and more frequent. The next proposition verifies this intuition.

Proposition 4
.

Let
K_{\lambda}(x)
be as in (
14
) and define a function
k:(0,a^{*}_{s}]\to(0,a^{*}_{s}]
as (see
[
25
]
p. 248)

k(x)=\check{L}^{r\,\,-1}_{\theta_{u}}(\hat{L}^{r}_{\theta_{d}}(\hat{K}^{r\,\,-1}_{\theta_{d}}(\hat{K}^{r}_{\theta_{u}}(x)))),

where
\hat{\cdot}
and
\check{\cdot}
are restrictions to domains
[x^{*}_{d},\infty)
and
(0,x^{*}_{u}]
. Then the unique fixed point
a^{*}
of
K_{\lambda}
converges to the unique fixed point
a^{*}_{s}
of
k
as
\lambda
tends to infinity.

Proof.

For all
s>0
, we have

(23)

\mathbb{E}_{x}[e^{-s\tau_{z}}\mid\tau_{z}<\infty]=\dfrac{\psi_{s}(z)}{\psi_{s}(x)},\quad\,z\leq x

where
\tau_{z}=\inf\{t\geq 0\mid X_{t}=z\}
. Further, by lemma
6
the function
\lambda\mapsto\frac{\psi_{r+\lambda}(x)}{\psi_{r+\lambda}^{\prime}(x)}
is decreasing. Thus,

\frac{\psi_{r+\lambda}(z)}{\psi_{r+\lambda}^{\prime}(x)}=\frac{\psi_{r+\lambda}(x)}{\psi_{r+\lambda}^{\prime}(x)}\mathbb{E}_{x}[e^{-(r+\lambda)\tau_{z}}\mid\tau_{z}<\infty]\to 0,\quad\text{when }\lambda\to\infty.

Consequently, by monotone convergence we find that

(24)

\frac{K_{\theta_{u}}^{r+\lambda}(x)}{\psi_{r+\lambda}^{\prime}(x)}\to 0,\quad\text{when }\lambda\to\infty.

Similarly we can show that

(25)

\frac{L_{\theta_{d}}^{r+\lambda}(x)}{\varphi_{r+\lambda}^{\prime}(x)}\to 0,\quad\text{when }\lambda\to\infty.

Hence, we observe from the representation of the pair of equations (
16
) and monotonicity that

\lim_{\lambda\to\infty}K_{\lambda}(x)\xrightarrow{\lambda\to\infty}k(x).

∎

Also, a rather straightforward calculation of the limit
\lambda\to 0
in
(\ref{Eq:CandidateValue})
yields
V(x)=(R_{r}\pi)(x)
for all
x\in\mathbb{R}_{+}
. This result corresponds to the case where the signal process does not jump at all and thus there is no opportunities to control the underlying process. Hence, the reward that the controller gets is the resolvent
(R_{r}\pi)(x)
, i.e. the expected cumulative present value of the instantaneous payoff
\pi
.

6.
Illustration: geometric Brownian motion

We assume that the underlying diffusion is a standard geometric Brownian motion and thus the infinitesimal generator reads as

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}=\frac{1}{2}\sigma^{2}x^{2}\frac{d^{2}}{dx^{2}}+\mu x\frac{d}{dx},

where
\mu\in\mathbb{R}_{+}
and
\sigma\in\mathbb{R}_{+}
are given constants. Furthermore, the scale density and the density of the speed measure read as

S^{\prime}(x)=x^{-\frac{2\mu}{\sigma^{2}}},\quad m^{\prime}(x)=\frac{2}{\sigma^{2}}x^{\frac{2\mu}{\sigma^{2}}-2}.

Assume that
\mu<r
and denote

\displaystyle\beta_{\lambda}=\frac{1}{2}-\frac{\mu}{\sigma^{2}}+\sqrt{\bigg(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{\mu}{\sigma^{2}}\bigg)^{2}+\frac{2(r+\lambda)}{\sigma^{2}}}>1,

\displaystyle\alpha_{\lambda}=\frac{1}{2}-\frac{\mu}{\sigma^{2}}-\sqrt{\bigg(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{\mu}{\sigma^{2}}\bigg)^{2}+\frac{2(r+\lambda)}{\sigma^{2}}}<0.

Then the minimal
r
-excessive functions for
X
read as

\psi_{r}(x)=x^{\beta_{0}},\varphi_{r}(x)=x^{\alpha_{0}},\psi_{r+\lambda}(x)=x^{\beta_{\lambda}},\varphi_{r+\lambda}(x)=x^{\alpha_{\lambda}}.

Define the instantaneous payoff by
\pi(x)=x^{\delta},\,\,0<\delta<1
. Then the net convenience yield is given by
\theta_{n}(x)=x^{\delta}-\gamma_{n}(r-\mu)x
, where
n\in\{u,d\}
and
\gamma_{d}<\gamma_{u}
. We readily verify that our assumptions
2
hold in this case. The resolvent reads as

(R_{r}\pi)(x)=\frac{x^{\delta}}{r-\delta\mu-\frac{1}{2}\sigma^{2}\delta(\delta-1)}.

Noting that
(\beta_{0}-\delta)(\delta-\alpha_{0})=2(r-\delta\mu-\frac{1}{2}\sigma^{2}\delta(\delta-1))/\sigma^{2}
, we find the alternative representation

(R_{r}\pi)(x)=\frac{2}{\sigma^{2}}\frac{x^{\delta}}{(\beta_{0}-\delta)(\delta-\alpha_{0})}.

To write down the pair of equations we first calculate the auxiliary functionals

\displaystyle K_{\theta_{u}}^{r+\lambda}(x)

\displaystyle=\frac{2(r+\lambda)}{\sigma^{2}}\bigg(\bigg[\frac{1}{\delta-\alpha_{\lambda}}+\frac{1}{\alpha_{\lambda}}\bigg]x^{\delta-\alpha_{\lambda}}+\bigg[\frac{\gamma_{u}(\mu-r)}{1-\alpha_{\lambda}}+\frac{\gamma_{u}(\mu-r)}{\alpha_{\lambda}}\bigg]x^{1-\alpha_{\lambda}}\bigg),

\displaystyle L_{\theta_{d}}^{r+\lambda}(x)

\displaystyle=-\frac{2(r+\lambda)}{\sigma^{2}}\bigg(\bigg[\frac{1}{\delta-\beta_{\lambda}}+\frac{1}{\beta_{\lambda}}\bigg]x^{\delta-\beta_{\lambda}}-\bigg[\frac{\gamma_{d}(\mu-r)}{1-\beta_{\lambda}}+\frac{\gamma_{d}(\mu-r)}{\beta_{\lambda}}\bigg]x^{1-\beta_{\lambda}}\bigg).

Then noting that
\beta_{0}-\beta_{\lambda}=\alpha_{\lambda}-\alpha_{0}
and that
(r+\lambda)\beta_{0}\alpha_{0}=r\beta_{\lambda}\alpha_{\lambda}
, we find that the pair of equations read as

(26)

\displaystyle\bigg[\frac{\alpha_{\lambda}}{\alpha_{0}(\delta-\alpha_{\lambda})}-\frac{1}{\delta-\alpha_{0}}\bigg]a^{\delta-\alpha_{0}}+\bigg[\frac{\gamma_{u}(\mu-r)\alpha_{\lambda}}{\alpha_{0}(1-\alpha_{\lambda})}-\frac{\gamma_{u}(\mu-r)}{1-\alpha_{0}}\bigg]a^{1-\alpha_{0}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\bigg[\frac{\beta_{\lambda}}{\alpha_{0}(\delta-\beta_{\lambda})}-\frac{1}{\delta-\alpha_{0}}\bigg]b^{\delta-\alpha_{0}}+\bigg[\frac{\gamma_{d}(\mu-r)\beta_{\lambda}}{\alpha_{0}(1-\beta_{\lambda})}-\frac{\gamma_{u}(\mu-r)}{1-\alpha_{0}}\bigg]b^{1-\alpha_{0}},

\displaystyle\bigg[\frac{\alpha_{\lambda}}{\beta_{0}(\delta-\alpha_{\lambda})}-\frac{1}{\delta-\beta_{0}}\bigg]a^{\delta-\beta_{0}}+\bigg[\frac{\gamma_{u}(\mu-r)\alpha_{\lambda}}{\beta_{0}(1-\alpha_{\lambda})}-\frac{\gamma_{u}(\mu-r)}{1-\beta_{0}}\bigg]a^{1-\beta_{0}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\bigg[\frac{\beta_{\lambda}}{\beta_{0}(\delta-\beta_{\lambda})}-\frac{1}{\delta-\beta_{0}}\bigg]b^{\delta-\beta_{0}}+\bigg[\frac{\gamma_{d}(\mu-r)\beta_{\lambda}}{\beta_{0}(1-\beta_{\lambda})}-\frac{\gamma_{u}(\mu-r)}{1-\beta_{0}}\bigg]b^{1-\beta_{0}}.

However, the assumption
\tilde{x}<\hat{x}
in proposition
2
has to be analyzed separately. We find that
\tilde{x}
and
\hat{x}
read as

\tilde{x}=\gamma_{u}(r-\mu)\frac{\delta-\alpha_{0}}{\delta(1-\alpha_{0})},\quad\,\,\hat{x}=\gamma_{d}(r-\mu)\frac{\delta-\beta_{0}}{\delta(1-\beta_{0})}.

Hence, the condition
\tilde{x}<\hat{x}
is equivalent to

\gamma_{u}\frac{\delta-\alpha_{0}}{1-\alpha_{0}}<\gamma_{d}\frac{\delta-\beta_{0}}{1-\beta_{0}}.

Unfortunately, it seems impossible to solve the pair (
26

Then, as expected, the optimal thresholds converge to the ones in the singular case as
\lambda\to\infty

Simulating Floquet scrambling circuits on trapped-ion quantum computers
Kazuhiro Seki, Yuta Kikuchi, Tomoya Hayata, Seiji Yunoki

Abstract
Complex quantum many-body dynamics spread initially localized quantum information across the entire system. Information scrambling refers to such a process, whose simulation is one of the promising applications of quantum computing. We demonstrate the Hayden-Preskill recovery protocol and the interferometric protocol for calculating out-of-time-ordered correlators to study the scrambling property of a one-dimensional kicked-Ising model on 20-qubit trapped-ion quantum processors. The simulated quantum circuits have a geometrically local structure that exhibits the ballistic growth of entanglement, resulting in the circuit depth being linear in the number of qubits for the entire state to be scrambled. We experimentally confirm the growth of signals in the Hayden-Preskill recovery protocol and the decay of out-of-time-ordered correlators at late times. As an application of the created scrambling circuits, we also experimentally demonstrate the calculation of the microcanonical expectation values of local operators adopting the idea of thermal pure quantum states. Our experiments are made possible by extensively utilizing one of the highest-fidelity quantum processors currently available and, thus, should be considered as a benchmark for the current status of the most advanced quantum computers.

Introduction

The spreading or scrambling of quantum information is a characteristic feature of complex quantum many-body dynamics.
Understanding the properties of information scrambling is crucial for addressing fundamental problems in quantum mechanics, including thermalization and chaos.
As a quantum state evolves under unitary dynamics, it gradually loses microscopic details, ultimately retaining only macroscopic information, a process known as thermalization.
As the system further evolves, quantum information is scrambled in the sense that initially localized information spreads, making it increasingly harder
to recover from local measurements of the evolved state.
The difficulty of information retrieval is closely tied to the complexity of quantum dynamics.
For instance, it is intuitive to understand that
information thrown into a fast scrambler, such as a black hole, would be highly challenging to recover.

Along with advances in analytical and numerical techniques for studying scrambling quantum dynamics, quantum computing has emerged as a promising tool for efficiently simulating such systems.
Despite such anticipation and rapid developments of quantum processors, the full potential of quantum computing remains unrealized largely because of errors caused by interaction with environments and imperfect control of quantum operations.
Extracting signals from currently available quantum hardware requires precise error characterization for each hardware. Furthermore, the way that errors affect the final outcome depends not only on the hardware but also heavily on the type of algorithm or quantum circuits employed, even when the same device is used.
To realize large-scale quantum simulation of complex scrambling dynamics, which is one of the promising applications of quantum computing, it is crucial to assess the feasibility of proposed quantum algorithms on current hardware. Such assessments are essential for accurately estimating the computational resources required to scale up the system size.

and

Provided that the subsystems

and

leads to the decay of the OTOC as it starts to overlap with

For scrambling circuits, the OTOC decays exponentially in

\displaystyle\begin{split}P_{\mathrm{EPR}}&=\mathbb{E}_{O_{A}\sim U(d_{A}),O_{D}\sim U(d_{D})}\big[{\rm OTOC}_{I/d}\big]\\

where the OTOCs are evaluated with respect to the maximally mixed

The Haar averages of

On the right-hand side, Pauli

and Pauli

denote the sets of

A
and
D

In the second equality, we used the fact that the Haar average of
the first moment of a unitary operator and its Hermitian conjugate

Secondly, the recovery fidelity

is expressed
as
[
29
]

\displaystyle F_{\mathrm{EPR}}=\frac{1}{d_{A}^{2}P_{\mathrm{EPR}}}.

(10)

In particular, when the operator

is a Haar random unitary operator,

takes the value

, resulting in the recovery fidelity being

4^{-\delta}
-close to unity for

.
More generally, the recovery fidelity

increases significantly at late times when
U
scrambles the input
state, thereby serving as an indicator of scrambling similar to
OTOCs.

Thirdly, the recovery fidelity
F_{\mathrm{EPR}}
carries equivalent information to the operator-averaged OTOC in the absence of errors.
However, the HPR protocol offers an advantage in the presence of hardware noise. In the HPR protocol, scrambling induces the growth
of

, while decoherence causes the fidelity to decay
[
30
]
. This contrasts with OTOCs, which decay
due to both scrambling and decoherence. Consequently, distinguishing between them becomes challenging in noisy simulations.
In extreme cases, OTOCs may decay even in the absence of scrambling
under strong decoherence.

Finally, the advantage of the interferometric protocol for OTOCs
over the HPR protocol lies in its capability to handle larger
system sizes. This becomes particularly crucial when the number
of available qubits is limited.

from a state 2-design is considered a microcanonical TPQ state.

In the last equality, we used that the purity
{\rm Tr}[\rho_{\sigma}(E)^{2}]
decays exponentially, that is, the thermal entropy of
\rho_{\sigma}(E)
follows a volume law, which holds for a typical state
[
49
]
(see Appendix
D
for details).
Therefore, the inequality states that

\langle O\rangle^{\mathrm{(est)}}
provides an
\epsilon
-precise
estimator with a probability exponentially close to 1.
We prove this statement in Appendix
D
.
Consequently, a single instance of a random state suffices when the associated purity of a targeted microcanonical state rapidly decays.

On the other hand, the quantum states generated after the scrambling time evolution approximate a state
k
-design
[
58
,
59
,
60
,
61
]
.
Considering that the Floquet operator
U_{\mathrm{F}}
exhibits
scrambling properties, we expect that
(U_{\mathrm{F}})^{m}\left|\psi_{0}\right\rangle

behaves as a state sampled from a state 2-design.
In other words, the ensemble

\displaystyle\Psi=\{(U_{\mathrm{F}})^{m}\left|\psi_{0}\right\rangle\mid m\in\mathbb{N}\}

(15)

approximates a state 2-design if the evolution time
mT

exceeds the scrambling time. Supplementary numerical analyses
supporting this assertion can be found in Appendix
C
.

Combining these two observations, we attempt to compute the microcanonical expectation value of
O
with the single state
\left|\psi\right\rangle=(U_{\mathrm{F}})^{m}\left|\psi_{0}\right\rangle

[
71
,
72
,
73
,
74
,
55
,
75
,
76
]
.
To implement
{\rm e}^{-(E-\mathcal{H})^{2}/2\sigma^{2}}
, we rewrite the operator
as follows:

\displaystyle\begin{split}{\rm e}^{-(E-\mathcal{H})^{2}/2\sigma^{2}}&=\frac{\sigma}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\mathrm{d}t\,{\rm e}^{-\sigma^{2}t^{2}/2}{\rm e}^{\mathrm{i}Et}{\rm e}^{-\mathrm{i}\mathcal{H}t}\\
&\approx\frac{\sigma}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\sum_{s=-S}^{S}\Delta t\,{\rm e}^{-\sigma^{2}(s\Delta t)^{2}/2}{\rm e}^{\mathrm{i}Es\Delta t}{\rm e}^{-\mathrm{i}\mathcal{H}s\Delta t},\end{split}

(16)

where the integral is discretized and the cutoff
S\in\mathbb{N}

is introduced to truncate the infinite sum.
Therefore, the task on the quantum processor boils down to
evaluating the following amplitudes:

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{O}(s\Delta t):=\left\langle\psi_{0}\right|(U_{\mathrm{F}})^{{\dagger}m}O{\rm e}^{-\mathrm{i}\mathcal{H}s\Delta t}(U_{\mathrm{F}})^{m}\left|\psi_{0}\right\rangle,

(17)

and

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}(s\Delta t):=\left\langle\psi_{0}\right|(U_{\mathrm{F}})^{{\dagger}m}{\rm e}^{-\mathrm{i}\mathcal{H}s\Delta t}(U_{\mathrm{F}})^{m}\left|\psi_{0}\right\rangle,

(18)

for
s=-S,-S+1,\dots,S
.
The quantity in Eq. (
18
) is known as
Loschmidt amplitude
[
14
,
15
,
16
]
.
We then perform classical post-processing of the outcomes of
quantum computations using Eq. (
16
), i.e.,

\displaystyle D_{O}(E):=\frac{\sigma}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\sum_{s=-S}^{S}\Delta t\,{\rm e}^{-\sigma^{2}(s\Delta t)^{2}/2}{\rm e}^{\mathrm{i}Es\Delta t}\mathcal{L}_{O}(s\Delta t),

(19)

and

\displaystyle D(E):=\frac{\sigma}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\sum_{s=-S}^{S}\Delta t\,{\rm e}^{-\sigma^{2}(s\Delta t)^{2}/2}{\rm e}^{\mathrm{i}Es\Delta t}\mathcal{L}(s\Delta t).

(20)

Notice that
D(E)
is the density of states of
\mathcal{H}
with respect
to the energy
E
.
Using these quantities, the microcanonical expectation value of
O

is estimated as

\displaystyle\langle O\rangle^{\mathrm{(est)}}=\frac{D_{O}(E)}{D(E)}.

(21)

Three remarks are in order.
First, evaluating
\mathcal{L}_{O}(s\Delta t)
and
\mathcal{L}(s\Delta t)
to estimate the normalized traces
{\rm Tr}[O{\rm e}^{-\mathrm{i}\mathcal{H}s\Delta t}]/2^{N}
and
{\rm Tr}[{\rm e}^{-\mathrm{i}\mathcal{H}s\Delta t}]/2^{N}
, respectively, with an additive error of
1/\mathrm{poly}(N)
can be hard tasks for classical computation.
The potential hardness is based on the fact that the normalized trace estimation is a DQC1 complete problem, for which no polynomial-time classical algorithm is likely to exist
[
77
,
78
,
79
,
80
]
. See also Refs.
[
81
,
82
]
for the normalized partition function problem with one-clean qubit.
Second, the protocol presented here, nevertheless, requires a non-scalable shot overhead to evaluate the expectation value in Eq. (
13
) within a given uncertainty.
This is because a scrambled state typically has a small overlap with the state
\rho_{\sigma}(E)
in Eq. (
11
), particularly for small
E
(see Appendix
E
for details).
We note that efforts have been made to reduce the overhead by classically sampling the input states to address the low-energy microcanonical states as well as the low-temperature canonical states
[
73
,
74
,
75
,
76
]
.
Third, quadratic improvement of asymptotic runtime for calculating the expectation value in Eq. (
13
) can be achieved, for instance, by adopting the algorithm proposed in Ref.
[
72
]
.
However, we employ the present protocol to experimentally demonstrate the property of scrambling circuits as an approximate state 2-design on a currently available noisy quantum device (see Appendix
C
for supplementary analyses).

V
Discussion

We performed three experiments on trapped-ion quantum computers: the
HPR protocol, the interferometric protocol, and the calculation of
the thermal expectation value of a local operator.
In the first two experiments, we characterized the information
scrambling of the kicked-Ising model at and around
the self-dual point.
The HPR protocol involved manipulating two copies of the 9-qubit
spin chains augmented by a two-qubit ancillary register,
resulting in a total of 20 qubits. We verified the high-fidelity
teleportation of quantum information, as expected from the chaotic
unitary dynamics. Additionally, We tested the incoherent error
mitigation assuming the depolarizing noise model. The improved
performance with the mitigation suggests that the depolarizing noise
model effectively captures the influence of the noise on the HPR
protocol.

In the second experiment, we scaled up the system size to 19 qubits
and calculated the OTOCs using the interferometric protocol.
Successful execution of the circuits confirmed the sharp decay of
OTOCs at late times, consistent with theoretical predictions.
Despite the maximum two-qubit gate count being as large as 367
inside the causal cone of the measured qubits, we observed
agreements between the experimental data and the exact numerical
values.
Lastly, having confirmed the scrambling property of the unitary
circuits, we employed them to calculate the thermal expectation
values of local operators, borrowing the idea of microcanonical
TPQ states.

While the present studies nicely demonstrate the capability of
currently available quantum devices and the proposed quantum
algorithms, there is still the question of how far these protocols
can be scaled up to potentially reach the realm of classically
intractable system sizes and evolution times.
Foreseeing the interferometric experiments on larger systems,
let us make a rough estimate of the required resources.
Recall that our 20-qubit interferometric circuit for the kicked Ising
model under open boundary conditions contains at most 367 two-qubit
gates inside the causal cone.
Assuming the dominant source of error is two-qubit gate operations,
with their average gate infidelity of
0.1\%
, we now discuss the
fidelity of a 60-qubit interferometric circuit.
For the same one-dimensional geometrically local model under
periodic boundary conditions to exhibit scrambling,
the Floquet system
needs to evolve for
3/2
times more cycles than the 20-qubit
system with open boundary conditions. This leads to a two-qubit
gate counts of
367\times 3\times 3/2\approx 1652
.
Consequently, the circuit fidelity is roughly estimated as

f\sim 0.999^{1652}\approx 0.2
. Evaluating the normalized OTOC
with a statistical uncertainty
\epsilon
requires a shot overhead of

\mathcal{O}((f\epsilon)^{-2})
.
Alternatively, one can introduce long-range interactions to reduce
the scrambling time, which is suitable for the hardware equipped
with long-range connectivity. For instance, fast scrambling systems
like the Sachdev–Ye–Kitaev
model
[
91
,
92
,
93
]
have a scrambling
time
~\mathcal{O}(\log N)
, contrasting with
N
in geometrically local
systems.
We leave more comprehensive studies for larger-scale experiments
in the future.

To analyze the behavior of the normalized OTOC in the presence of

31
),

replaced by

\mathcal{D}_{U}
in the circuit shown in Fig.
3

yielding respectively

\displaystyle\begin{split}\langle 0^{N}|UX_{n}U^{\dagger}Z_{1}UX_{n}U^{\dagger}|0^{N}\rangle^{\mathcal{D}}&=f^{2}\langle 0^{N}|UX_{n}U^{\dagger}Z_{1}UX_{n}U^{\dagger}|0^{N}\rangle,\\

Thus, their ratio provides the OTOC without the depolarizing

C.3
Influence of shot noise on the normalized trace estimation

In the previous subsections, we assumed that the shot noise was absent,
which is relevant for classical simulations or quantum simulations in
the limit of the infinite number of shots.
Now, we discuss the statistical errors of the Loschmidt amplitude
in the presence of the shot noise, assuming that the number
of shots
N_{\rm shots}
is finite.

C.3.1
Sampling over shots

First, let us consider the shot noise when evaluating the Loschmidt
amplitude,
{\cal L}(t)=\left\langle\psi\right|{\cal U}(t)\left|\psi\right\rangle
,
for a fixed pure state
\left|\psi\right\rangle
.
Using the Hadamard test, the Loschmidt amplitude is estimated as

\mathbb{E}^{\prime}_{\mathrm{shots}}[x_{\psi}]+{\rm i}\mathbb{E}^{\prime}_{\mathrm{shots}}[y_{\psi}]
, where
\mathbb{E}^{\prime}_{\mathrm{shots}}[x_{\psi}]
and

\mathbb{E}^{\prime}_{\mathrm{shots}}[y_{\psi}]
represent the sample means of
its real and imaginary parts, respectively, obtained from the
measurement outcomes on the ancillary qubit, i.e.,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}^{\prime}_{\mathrm{shots}}[x_{\psi}]:=\frac{1}{N_{\rm shots}}\sum_{c=1}^{N_{\rm shots}}x_{\psi,c},\qquad\mathbb{E}^{\prime}_{\mathrm{shots}}[y_{\psi}]:=\frac{1}{N_{\rm shots}}\sum_{c=1}^{N_{\rm shots}}y_{\psi,c}.

(49)

Here,
x_{\psi,c}\in\{+1,-1\}
denotes the
X
-measurement outcome of
the ancillary qubit for the
c
-th shot, and similarly

y_{\psi,c}\in\{+1,-1\}
is the
Y
-measurement outcome.
The sample variances of the real and imaginary parts due to the finite
number of shots are given respectively by

\displaystyle{\rm Var}^{\prime}_{\rm shots}[x_{\psi}]=1-\big(\mathbb{E}^{\prime}_{\rm shots}[{x_{\psi}}]\big)^{2},\qquad{\rm Var}^{\prime}_{\rm shots}[y_{\psi}]=1-\big(\mathbb{E}^{\prime}_{\rm shots}[{y_{\psi}}]\big)^{2}.

(50)

The standard deviations of the means are then calculated as

\displaystyle\sigma_{\rm shots}^{\rm mean}[x_{\psi}]:=\sqrt{\frac{{\rm Var}^{\prime}_{\rm shots}[x_{\psi}]}{N_{\rm shots}}},\qquad\sigma_{\rm shots}^{\rm mean}[y_{\psi}]:=\sqrt{\frac{{\rm Var}^{\prime}_{\rm shots}[y_{\psi}]}{N_{\rm shots}}}.

(51)

C.3.2
Sampling over states

The above result is for a single pure state
\left|\psi\right\rangle
. Now, we
extend this analysis to the average over
R
independent realizations
of states
\{\left|\psi_{r}\right\rangle\}_{r=1}^{R}
.
The corresponding estimator of the normalized trace is given by
\mathbb{E}^{\prime}_{\psi}[\mathbb{E}^{\prime}_{\rm shots}[x_{\psi}]]+\mathrm{i}\,\mathbb{E}^{\prime}_{\psi}[\mathbb{E}^{\prime}_{\rm shots}[y_{\psi}]]
, where

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}^{\prime}_{\psi}[\mathbb{E}^{\prime}_{\rm shots}[x_{\psi}]]:=\frac{1}{R}\sum_{r=1}^{R}\mathbb{E}^{\prime}_{\rm shots}[x_{\psi_{r}}]\qquad\mathbb{E}^{\prime}_{\psi}[\mathbb{E}^{\prime}_{\rm shots}[y_{\psi}]]:=\frac{1}{R}\sum_{r=1}^{R}\mathbb{E}^{\prime}_{\rm shots}[y_{\psi_{r}}].

(52)

The sample variances of the real and imaginary parts due to the
sampling over states
\left|\psi_{r}\right\rangle
are given respectively by

\displaystyle{\rm Var}^{\prime}_{\psi}[\mathbb{E}^{\prime}_{\rm shots}[x_{\psi}]]

\displaystyle:=\mathbb{E}^{\prime}_{\psi}[\mathbb{E}^{\prime}_{\rm shots}[x_{\psi}]^{2}]-(\mathbb{E}^{\prime}_{\psi}[\mathbb{E}^{\prime}_{\rm shots}[x_{\psi}]])^{2}

(53)

and

\displaystyle{\rm Var}^{\prime}_{\psi}[\mathbb{E}^{\prime}_{\rm shots}[y_{\psi}]]

\displaystyle:=\mathbb{E}^{\prime}_{\psi}[\mathbb{E}^{\prime}_{\rm shots}[y_{\psi}]^{2}]-(\mathbb{E}^{\prime}_{\psi}[\mathbb{E}^{\prime}_{\rm shots}[y_{\psi}]])^{2}.

(54)

The corresponding standard deviations of the mean due to the sampling
over states are then calculated as

\displaystyle\sigma_{\psi}^{\rm mean}\left[\mathbb{E}^{\prime}_{\rm shots}[x_{\psi}]\right]:=\sqrt{\frac{{\rm Var}^{\prime}_{\psi}[\mathbb{E}^{\prime}_{\rm shots}[x_{\psi}]]}{R}},\qquad\sigma_{\psi}^{\rm mean}\left[\mathbb{E}^{\prime}_{\rm shots}[y_{\psi}]\right]:=\sqrt{\frac{{\rm Var}^{\prime}_{\psi}[\mathbb{E}^{\prime}_{\rm shots}[y_{\psi}]]}{R}}.

(55)

Here, we first average over shots and then states,
to associate the result in the previous subsection with
the present analysis.
Alternatively, one could average over shots and states simultaneously:

{\rm Var}^{\prime}[x]:=\mathbb{E}^{\prime}_{\psi}[\mathbb{E}^{\prime}_{\rm shots}[x_{\psi}^{2}]]-(\mathbb{E}^{\prime}_{\psi}[\mathbb{E}^{\prime}_{\rm shots}[x_{\psi}]])^{2}=1-(\mathbb{E}^{\prime}_{\psi}[\mathbb{E}^{\prime}_{\rm shots}[x_{\psi}]])^{2}

with a similar expression for
{\rm Var}^{\prime}[y]
.
Then, as discussed in the next subsection,
one could readily find that the standard deviation
of the mean
is given by the right-hand side of Eq. (
62
)
assuming that the states are sampled from a state 2-design.

C.3.3
Sampling over shots and states

Recall that each sample mean
\mathbb{E}^{\prime}_{\rm shots}[x_{\psi}]
for a given
\left|\psi\right\rangle
has the standard deviation
\sigma_{\rm shots}^{\rm mean}[x_{\psi}](\sim 1/\sqrt{N_{\rm shots}})
around the population mean.
Therefore, the standard deviation of

\mathbb{E}^{\prime}_{\psi}[\mathbb{E}^{\prime}_{\rm shots}[x_{\psi}]]
should be described
by both
\sigma_{\psi}^{\rm mean}
and
\sigma_{\rm shots}^{\rm mean}
(the same applies to
\mathbb{E}^{\prime}_{\psi}[\mathbb{E}^{\prime}_{\rm shots}[y_{\psi}]]
).
From the error-propagation formula, the standard deviations of
the means

\mathbb{E}^{\prime}_{\psi}[\mathbb{E}^{\prime}_{\rm shots}[x_{\psi}]]
and

\mathbb{E}^{\prime}_{\psi}[\mathbb{E}^{\prime}_{\rm shots}[y_{\psi}]]

are expressed respectively as

\displaystyle\sigma^{\rm mean}[x]

\displaystyle:=\sqrt{\left(\sigma_{\psi}^{\rm mean}[\mathbb{E}^{\prime}_{\rm shots}[x_{\psi}]]\right)^{2}+\frac{1}{R^{2}}\sum_{r=1}^{R}\left(\sigma_{\rm shots}^{\rm mean}[x_{\psi_{r}}]\right)^{2}}

(56)

and

\displaystyle\sigma^{\rm mean}[y]

\displaystyle:=\sqrt{\left(\sigma_{\psi}^{\rm mean}[\mathbb{E}^{\prime}_{\rm shots}[y_{\psi}]]\right)^{2}+\frac{1}{R^{2}}\sum_{r=1}^{R}\left(\sigma_{\rm shots}^{\rm mean}[y_{\psi_{r}}]\right)^{2}}.

(57)

C.3.4
Standard deviation of the mean in the presence of the
shot noise assuming a state 2-design

To proceed further, we consider
\sigma^{\rm mean}[x+{\rm i}y]=\sqrt{(\sigma^{\rm mean}[x])^{2}+(\sigma^{\rm mean}[y])^{2}}
for simplicity.
Additionally, we make the following replacements:

\mathbb{E}^{\prime}_{\rm shots}[{x}_{\psi}]\to\mathbb{E}_{\rm shots}[x_{\psi}]={\rm Re}\left\langle\psi\right|U(t)\left|\psi\right\rangle
,

\mathbb{E}^{\prime}_{\rm shots}[{y}_{\psi}]\to\mathbb{E}_{\rm shots}[y_{\psi}]={\rm Im}\left\langle\psi\right|U(t)\left|\psi\right\rangle
,
and
\mathbb{E}^{\prime}_{\psi}[\cdots]\to\mathbb{E}_{\psi\sim\text{2-design}}[\cdots]
.
Then, the variance due to the shots averaged over states is replaced with

\frac{1}{R^{2}}\sum_{r=1}^{R}\left({\sigma}_{\rm shots}^{\rm mean}[x_{\psi_{r}}+{\rm i}y_{\psi_{r}}]\right)^{2}\to\frac{1}{RN_{\rm shots}}\left(2-\frac{1}{d+1}-\frac{d{\rm SFF}(t)}{d+1}\right),

(58)

and the variance due to the sampling of states is replaced with

\left({\sigma}_{\psi_{r}}^{\rm mean}[\mathbb{E}^{\prime}_{\rm shots}[x_{\psi_{r}}+{\rm i}y_{\psi_{r}}]]\right)^{2}\to\frac{1-{\rm SFF}(t)}{R(d+1)}.

(59)

The standard deviation of the mean is then replaced with

\sigma^{\rm mean}[x+{\rm i}y]\to\sqrt{\frac{1-{\rm SFF}(t)}{R(d+1)}+\frac{1}{RN_{\rm shots}}\left(2-\frac{1}{d+1}-\frac{d{\rm SFF}(t)}{d+1}\right)}.

(60)

Let us first consider a situation relevant to classical state-vector simulations where the output of the Loschmidt amplitude is given by a double-precision (single-precision) floating-point number with a relative error due to round-off of at most
2^{-53}
(
2^{-24}
).
Assuming
R=O(1)
and requiring the shot noise to be no larger than this round-off error, the corresponding number of shots would be around

N_{\rm shots}\sim 2^{106}
(
2^{48}
).
On the other hand, the classically tractable dimension of the Hilbert space is at most
d\sim 2^{48}

[
98
]
.
Therefore, in most cases, classical state-vector simulations with double-precision arithmetic satisfy the relation
R\ll d\ll N_{\rm shots}
.
By taking the limit of
N_{\rm shots}\to\infty
with finite
d
and
R
, we obtain the result consistent with Eq. (
44
) in the previous subsection:

\sigma^{\rm mean}[x+{\rm i}y]\overset{N_{\rm shots}\to\infty}{\to}\sqrt{\frac{1-{\rm SFF}(t)}{R(d+1)}}.

(61)

Next, we consider a situation relevant to quantum computation, where
the relation
R\ll N_{\rm shots}\ll d
holds. In this case,
by taking the limit of
d\to\infty
with finite
N_{\rm shots}

and
R
,
the standard deviation of the mean simplifies to

\sigma^{\rm mean}[x+{\rm i}y]\overset{d\to\infty}{\to}\sqrt{\frac{2-{\rm SFF}(t)}{RN_{\rm shots}}}.

(62)

Thus, unless
N_{\rm shots}
is as large as
O(d)
, the shot noise
dominates
\sigma^{\rm mean}
.

Appendix D
Error in microcanonical expectation value due to a state 2-design

In this Appendix, we shall prove the inequality stated
in Eq. (
14
).
We introduce a filter operator defined as

\displaystyle G_{\sigma}(E):={\rm e}^{-(E-\mathcal{H})^{2}/2\sigma^{2}},

(63)

and examine a thermal state
\rho_{\sigma}=G_{\sigma}/{\rm Tr}[G_{\sigma}]
representing the microcanonical ensemble parametrized by the internal energy
E
and width
\sigma
.
The thermal entropy is expressed as

\displaystyle S_{\sigma}(E)=\log{\rm Tr}[G_{\sigma}(E)],

(64)

while the microcanonical expectation value of a Hermitian operator
O
is calculated by

\displaystyle\langle O\rangle={\rm Tr}[O\rho_{\sigma}].

(65)

Here, we estimate the expectation value
\langle O\rangle
using a state 2-design.
Letting
\left|\psi\right\rangle
be a state sampled from a state 2-design, we compute

\displaystyle\langle O\rangle^{\text{(est)}}=\frac{\left\langle\psi\right|OG_{\sigma}\left|\psi\right\rangle}{\left\langle\psi\right|G_{\sigma}\left|\psi\right\rangle}.

(66)

We first prove that this gives an accurate estimator of
\langle O\rangle
in Eq. (
65
) with high probability.
To this end, we show the mean-square error

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{\psi}\big[|\langle O\rangle^{\text{(est)}}-\langle O\rangle|^{2}\big]=\big|\mathbb{E}_{\psi}[\langle O\rangle^{\text{(est)}}]-\langle O\rangle\big|^{2}+\mathrm{Var}_{\psi}[\langle O\rangle^{\text{(est)}}],

(67)

is exponentially small in the system size
N

and then we employ the Chebyshev inequality, as shown in Eq. (
88
).
Here, The first and second terms on the right-hand side in Eq. (
67
) represent the squared bias and the statistical uncertainty, respectively.

Following Refs.
[
53
,
57
,
99
]
, we introduce the quantities

\displaystyle f:=\left\langle\psi\right|OG_{\sigma}(E)\left|\psi\right\rangle,\qquad g:=\left\langle\psi\right|G_{\sigma}(E)\left|\psi\right\rangle.

(68)

Upon averaging
\left|\psi\right\rangle
over the state 2-design, we obtain

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{\psi}[f]=\frac{{\rm Tr}[OG_{\sigma}]}{d},\qquad\mathbb{E}_{\psi}[g]=\frac{{\rm Tr}[G_{\sigma}]}{d},

(69)

along with the variances

\displaystyle\mathrm{Var}_{\psi}[f]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}_{\psi}[|\left\langle\psi\right|OG_{\sigma}\left|\psi\right\rangle|^{2}]-\big|\mathbb{E}_{\psi}[\left\langle\psi\right|OG_{\sigma}\left|\psi\right\rangle]\big|^{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{{\rm Tr}[O^{\dagger}OG_{\sigma}^{2}]+\big|{\rm Tr}[OG_{\sigma}]\big|^{2}}{d(d+1)}-\frac{\big|{\rm Tr}[OG_{\sigma}]\big|^{2}}{d^{2}}\leq\|O\|^{2}({\rm Tr}[G_{\sigma}])^{2}\frac{{\rm Tr}[\rho_{\sigma}^{2}]}{d^{2}}

(70)

and

\displaystyle\mathrm{Var}_{\psi}[g]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}_{\psi}[\left\langle\psi\right|G_{\sigma}\left|\psi\right\rangle^{2}]-\left(\mathbb{E}_{\psi}[\left\langle\psi\right|G_{\sigma}\left|\psi\right\rangle]\right)^{2}\leq({\rm Tr}[G_{\sigma}])^{2}\frac{{\rm Tr}[\rho_{\sigma}^{2}]}{d^{2}},

(71)

respectively. Here,

\|O\|:=\sqrt{\max_{\left|v\right\rangle}\frac{\left\langle v\right|O^{\dagger}O\left|v\right\rangle}{\left\langle v\middle|v\right\rangle}}
is the spectral norm with
\left|v\right\rangle
being the eigenstate of
O^{\dagger}O
.

Our objective now is to assess
\mathbb{E}_{\psi}[\langle O\rangle^{\mathrm{(est)}}]=\mathbb{E}_{\psi}[f/g]
and its variance.
One might initially consider the expansion

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{\psi}\left[\frac{f}{g}\right]=\mathbb{E}_{\psi}\left[\left(\frac{\mathbb{E}_{\psi}[f]}{\mathbb{E}_{\psi}[g]}+\Delta f\right)\frac{1}{1+\Delta g}\right]=\frac{\mathbb{E}_{\psi}[f]}{\mathbb{E}_{\psi}[g]}\mathbb{E}_{\psi}\left[\left(\frac{\mathbb{E}_{\psi}[f]}{\mathbb{E}_{\psi}[g]}+\Delta f\right)(1+\Delta g+\mathcal{O}(\Delta g^{2}))\right],

(72)

assuming that
\Delta f:=(f-\mathbb{E}_{\psi}[f])/\mathbb{E}_{\psi}[g]
and
\Delta g:=(g-\mathbb{E}_{\psi}[g])/\mathbb{E}_{\psi}[g]
are small.
However, this expansion is not convergent because
|\Delta g|
can be larger than 1.
To circumvent the issue, we partition the ensemble of
\left|\psi\right\rangle
(2-design) into a subset
\Delta
with a small positive number
\delta
, where
|\Delta g|
is small, and its complement
\bar{\Delta}
:

\displaystyle\Delta:=\left\{\left|\psi\right\rangle\big|\,|\Delta f|\leq\delta\text{ and }|\Delta g|\leq\delta\right\},\qquad\bar{\Delta}:=\left\{\left|\psi\right\rangle\big|\,|\Delta f|>\delta\text{ or }|\Delta g|>\delta\right\}.

(73)

Two important observations are that one can safely use expansions in terms of
\Delta g
in the subset
\Delta
, and that the probability of
\left|\psi\right\rangle
falling into
\bar{\Delta}
is very small. The latter observation is supported by the Chebyshev inequalities

\displaystyle\Pr\big[|\Delta f|>\delta\big]<\frac{\mathrm{Var}_{\psi}[f]}{\delta^{2}(\mathbb{E}_{\psi}[g])^{2}}\leq\|O\|^{2}\frac{{\rm Tr}[\rho_{\sigma}^{2}]}{\delta^{2}},\qquad\Pr\big[|\Delta g|>\delta\big]<\frac{\mathrm{Var}_{\psi}[g]}{\delta^{2}(\mathbb{E}_{\psi}[g])^{2}}\leq\frac{{\rm Tr}[\rho_{\sigma}^{2}]}{\delta^{2}},

(74)

leading to

\displaystyle\Pr[\Delta]\geq 1-(1+\|O\|^{2})\frac{{\rm Tr}[\rho_{\sigma}^{2}]}{\delta^{2}},\qquad\Pr[\bar{\Delta}]<(1+\|O\|^{2})\frac{{\rm Tr}[\rho_{\sigma}^{2}]}{\delta^{2}},

(75)

where
\Pr[\Delta]+\Pr[\bar{\Delta}]=1
.
We ascertain that
\Pr[\bar{\Delta}]
is indeed small because the purity
{\rm Tr}[\rho_{\sigma}^{2}]
diminishes exponentially with the system size
N
,
as elaborated later in Eq. (
85
).
The inequalities in Eq. (
75
) will be
used repeatedly to bound the bias and variance in the subsequent analysis.

Bounding bias

Noticing that
\langle O\rangle=\mathbb{E}_{\psi}[f]/\mathbb{E}_{\psi}[g]
, we first establish the upper bound for the bias:

\displaystyle\begin{split}\big|\mathbb{E}_{\psi}\big[\langle O\rangle^{\mathrm{(est)}}\big]-\langle O\rangle\big|&=\Pr[\Delta]\left|\mathbb{E}_{\psi_{\Delta}}\left[\frac{f}{g}-\frac{\mathbb{E}_{\psi}[f]}{\mathbb{E}_{\psi}[g]}\right]\right|+\Pr[\bar{\Delta}]\left|\mathbb{E}_{\psi_{\bar{\Delta}}}\left[\frac{f}{g}-\frac{\mathbb{E}_{\psi}[f]}{\mathbb{E}_{\psi}[g]}\right]\right|\\
&\leq\left|\mathbb{E}_{\psi_{\Delta}}\left[\frac{f}{g}-\frac{\mathbb{E}_{\psi}[f]}{\mathbb{E}_{\psi}[g]}\right]\right|+\frac{(1+\|O\|^{2}){\rm Tr}[\rho_{\sigma}^{2}]}{\delta^{2}}2\|O\|,\end{split}

(76)

where
\mathbb{E}_{\psi_{\Delta}}[\cdot]
represents the average of
\left|\psi\right\rangle
over the subset of a state 2-design in Eq. (
73
) and
\mathbb{E}_{\psi_{\bar{\Delta}}}[\cdot]
is defined analogously.
In deriving the inequality, we used the bound
\big|f/g-\mathbb{E}_{\psi}[f]/\mathbb{E}_{\psi}[g]\big|\leq 2\|O\|
.
The first term is further bounded as

\displaystyle\left|\mathbb{E}_{\psi_{\Delta}}\left[\frac{f}{g}-\frac{\mathbb{E}_{\psi}[f]}{\mathbb{E}_{\psi}[g]}\right]\right|=\left|\mathbb{E}_{\psi_{\Delta}}\left[\left(\frac{\mathbb{E}_{\psi}[f]}{\mathbb{E}_{\psi}[g]}+\Delta f\right)\left(1-\frac{\Delta g}{1+\Delta g}\right)-\frac{\mathbb{E}_{\psi}[f]}{\mathbb{E}_{\psi}[g]}\right]\right|\leq\delta\left(1+\frac{\|O\|+\delta}{1-\delta}\right),

(77)

where the following was applied:

\displaystyle\left|\frac{\Delta g}{1+\Delta g}\right|\leq\frac{\delta}{1-\delta}

(78)

for
|\Delta g|\leq\delta\leq 1
.
By substituting the bound from Eq. (
77
) into the bias given in Eq. (
76
), we derive

\displaystyle\begin{split}\big|\mathbb{E}_{\psi}\big[\langle O\rangle^{\mathrm{(est)}}\big]-\langle O\rangle\big|=\left|\mathbb{E}_{\psi}\left[\frac{f}{g}\right]-\frac{\mathbb{E}_{\psi}[f]}{\mathbb{E}_{\psi}[g]}\right|\leq\delta\left(1+\frac{\|O\|+\delta}{1-\delta}\right)+\frac{2\|O\|(1+\|O\|^{2}){\rm Tr}[\rho_{\sigma}^{2}]}{\delta^{2}}.\end{split}

(79)

Setting
\delta=({\rm Tr}[\rho_{\sigma}^{2}])^{1/3}/2
, we finally establish the following bound on the bias:

\displaystyle\begin{split}\big|\mathbb{E}_{\psi}\big[\langle O\rangle^{\mathrm{(est)}}\big]-\langle O\rangle\big|\leq({\rm Tr}[\rho_{\sigma}^{2}])^{1/3}\big(1+9\|O\|+8\|O\|^{3}\big),\end{split}

(80)

where we used the condition that
0<\delta\leq 1/2
.
As we will show later in Eq. (
85
), the purity
{\rm Tr}[\rho_{\sigma}^{2}]
is exponentially small in the system size
N
.

Bounding variance

Using the same strategy, we bound the variance, which is the second term on the right-hand side of Eq. (
67
), as follows:

\displaystyle\begin{split}\mathrm{Var}_{\psi}\big[\langle O\rangle^{\mathrm{(est)}}\big]&=\mathbb{E}_{\psi}\left[\left(\frac{f}{g}-\mathbb{E}_{\psi}\left[\frac{f}{g}\right]\right)^{2}\right]\\
&=\Pr[\Delta]\mathbb{E}_{\psi_{\Delta}}\left[\frac{f^{2}}{g^{2}}\right]+\Pr[\bar{\Delta}]\mathbb{E}_{\psi_{\bar{\Delta}}}\left[\frac{f^{2}}{g^{2}}\right]-\left(\Pr[\Delta]\mathbb{E}_{\psi_{\Delta}}\left[\frac{f}{g}\right]+\Pr[\bar{\Delta}]\mathbb{E}_{\psi_{\bar{\Delta}}}\left[\frac{f}{g}\right]\right)^{2}\\
&\leq\Pr[\Delta]\mathbb{E}_{\psi_{\Delta}}\left[\frac{f^{2}}{g^{2}}\right]-\left(\Pr[\Delta]\mathbb{E}_{\psi_{\Delta}}\left[\frac{f}{g}\right]\right)^{2}+\Pr[\bar{\Delta}]\cdot 3\|O\|^{2}\\
&\leq\left(\mathbb{E}_{\psi_{\Delta}}\left[\frac{f^{2}}{g^{2}}\right]-\left(\mathbb{E}_{\psi_{\Delta}}\left[\frac{f}{g}\right]\right)^{2}\right)+\Pr[\bar{\Delta}]\cdot 4\|O\|^{2}.\end{split}

(81)

The first term in the last equation is expanded as

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\mathbb{E}_{\psi_{\Delta}}\left[\frac{f^{2}}{g^{2}}\right]-\left(\mathbb{E}_{\psi_{\Delta}}\left[\frac{f}{g}\right]\right)^{2}\\
&=\mathbb{E}_{\psi_{\Delta}}\left[\left(\frac{\mathbb{E}_{\psi}[f]}{\mathbb{E}_{\psi}[g]}+\Delta f-\frac{\mathbb{E}_{\psi}[f]}{\mathbb{E}_{\psi}[g]}\frac{\Delta g}{1+\Delta g}-\frac{\Delta f\Delta g}{1+\Delta g}\right)^{2}\right]-\left(\mathbb{E}_{\psi_{\Delta}}\left[\frac{\mathbb{E}_{\psi}[f]}{\mathbb{E}_{\psi}[g]}+\Delta f-\frac{\mathbb{E}_{\psi}[f]}{\mathbb{E}_{\psi}[g]}\frac{\Delta g}{1+\Delta g}-\frac{\Delta f\Delta g}{1+\Delta g}\right]\right)^{2}\\
&=\mathbb{E}_{\psi_{\Delta}}\left[\Delta f^{2}+\frac{\mathbb{E}_{\psi}[f]^{2}}{\mathbb{E}_{\psi}[g]^{2}}\Big(\frac{\Delta g}{1+\Delta g}\Big)^{2}-2\frac{\mathbb{E}_{\psi}[f]}{\mathbb{E}_{\psi}[g]}\frac{\Delta f\Delta g}{1+\Delta g}\right]\\
&\quad-\left(\mathbb{E}_{\psi_{\Delta}}[\Delta f]^{2}+\frac{\mathbb{E}_{\psi}[f]^{2}}{\mathbb{E}_{\psi}[g]^{2}}\mathbb{E}_{\psi_{\Delta}}\left[\frac{\Delta g}{1+\Delta g}\right]^{2}-2\frac{\mathbb{E}_{\psi}[f]}{\mathbb{E}_{\psi}[g]}\mathbb{E}_{\psi_{\Delta}}[\Delta f]\mathbb{E}_{\psi_{\Delta}}\left[\frac{\Delta g}{1+\Delta g}\right]\right)+\mathcal{O}(\delta^{3})\\
&\leq\delta^{2}+\|O\|^{2}\frac{\delta^{2}}{(1-\delta)^{2}}+4\|O\|\frac{\delta^{2}}{1-\delta}+\mathcal{O}(\delta^{3}).\end{split}

(82)

Thus, the upper bound of the variance in Eq. (
81
) is obtained as

\displaystyle\mathrm{Var}_{\psi}\big[\langle O\rangle^{\mathrm{(est)}}\big]\leq\delta^{2}\left(1+\frac{\|O\|^{2}}{(1-\delta)^{2}}+\frac{4\|O\|}{1-\delta}\right)+\frac{4(1+\|O^{2}\|)\|O\|^{2}{\rm Tr}[\rho_{\sigma}^{2}]}{\delta^{2}}+\mathcal{O}(\delta^{3}).

(83)

Setting
\delta=({\rm Tr}[\rho_{\sigma}^{2}])^{1/4}/2
, we find the bound

\displaystyle\mathrm{Var}_{\psi}\big[\langle O\rangle^{\mathrm{(est)}}\big]\leq({\rm Tr}[\rho_{\sigma}^{2}])^{1/2}\left(\frac{1}{4}+2\|O\|+17\|O\|^{2}+4\|O\|^{4}\right)+\mathcal{O}\big(({\rm Tr}[\rho_{\sigma}^{2}])^{3/4}\big).

(84)

Scaling of purity

Let us evaluate the scaling of the purity
{\rm Tr}[\rho_{\sigma}^{2}]
as follows:

\displaystyle{\rm Tr}[\rho_{\sigma}^{2}]=\frac{{\rm Tr}[G_{\sigma}(E)^{2}]}{({\rm Tr}[G_{\sigma}(E)])^{2}}=\frac{{\rm Tr}[G_{\sigma/\sqrt{2}}(E)]}{({\rm Tr}[G_{\sigma}(E)])^{2}}={\rm e}^{S_{\sigma/\sqrt{2}}-2S_{\sigma}}=\mathcal{O}({\rm e}^{-\alpha N}),

(85)

where
\alpha
is a positive constant independent of
N
. Here, we used that the entropy
S_{\sigma}
defined in Eq. (
64
) is an extensive quantity,
S_{\sigma}=\Theta(N)
, and monotonically increases with
\sigma
, i.e.,

\displaystyle\frac{\partial S_{\sigma}}{\partial\sigma}=\frac{1}{\sigma^{3}}\frac{{\rm Tr}[(E-\mathcal{H})^{2}G_{\sigma}(E)]}{{\rm Tr}[G_{\sigma}(E)]}\geq 0,

(86)

as shown in Ref.
[
55
]
.

Success probability

Substituting the bounds from Eqs. (
80
) and (
84
) into the mean-square error in Eq. (
67
), we obtain

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{\psi}\big[|\langle O\rangle^{\text{(est)}}-\langle O\rangle|^{2}\big]\leq({\rm Tr}[\rho_{\sigma}^{2}])^{1/2}\left(\frac{1}{4}+2\|O\|+17\|O\|^{2}+4\|O\|^{4}\right)+\mathcal{O}\big(({\rm Tr}[\rho_{\sigma}^{2}])^{2/3}\big)

(87)

Note that the bound on the squared bias in Eq. (
80
) is subleading relative to that on the variance in Eq. (
84
).

Applying the Chebyshev inequality, we can bound the success probability as follows:

\displaystyle\mathrm{Pr}_{\left|\psi\right\rangle}\left[\left|\langle O\rangle^{\mathrm{(est)}}-\langle O\rangle\right|\leq\epsilon\right]\geq 1-\frac{\mathbb{E}_{\psi}\big[|\langle O\rangle^{\text{(est)}}-\langle O\rangle|^{2}\big]}{\epsilon^{2}}\geq 1-\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{{\rm e}^{-\alpha N/2}}{\epsilon^{2}}\right),

(88)

as stated in Eq. (
14
).
Thus, we conclude that
\langle O\rangle^{\text{(est)}}
provides an
\epsilon
-precise estimator of the microcanonical expectation value of
O
with high probability.

Appendix E
Resources for imaginary time evolution

We approximate the integral form of
G_{\sigma}(E)

[
72
,
55
]

\displaystyle G_{\sigma}(E)=\frac{\sigma}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\mathrm{d}t\,{\rm e}^{-\frac{\sigma^{2}t^{2}}{2}}{\rm e}^{-\mathrm{i}(E-\mathcal{H})t}

(89)

by truncating and discretizing the integral as

\displaystyle\tilde{G}^{\prime}_{\sigma}(E)=\frac{\sigma}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\sum_{s=-S}^{S}\Delta t\,{\rm e}^{-\frac{\sigma^{2}(s\Delta t)^{2}}{2}}{\rm e}^{-\mathrm{i}(E-\mathcal{H})s\Delta t}.

(90)

We restate two useful results from Ref
[
72
]
for our analysis.
Lemma 1 of Ref.
[
72
]
states that there exist parameters

\displaystyle S=\Theta\left(\frac{\|E-\mathcal{H}\|}{\sigma}\log\frac{1}{\epsilon^{\prime}}\right),\qquad\Delta t=\Theta\left(\frac{1}{\|E-\mathcal{H}\|\sqrt{\log\frac{1}{\epsilon^{\prime}}}}\right),

(91)

such that

\displaystyle\|\tilde{G}^{\prime}_{\sigma}(E)-G_{\sigma}(E)\|\leq\frac{\epsilon^{\prime}}{2}.

(92)

The filter
\tilde{G}^{\prime}_{\sigma}(E)
is an approximation to
G_{\sigma}(E)
provided that
{\rm e}^{-\mathrm{i}Hs\Delta t}
is treated exactly.
Furthermore, Corollary 1 of Ref.
[
72
]
states that under an approximate implementation

of
{\rm e}^{-\mathrm{i}Hs\Delta t}

\displaystyle\|U_{s}(\mathcal{H})-{\rm e}^{-\mathrm{i}\mathcal{H}s\Delta t}\|\leq\frac{\epsilon^{\prime}}{4}

(93)

for all
s\in[-S,S]
. Consequently, the approximate filter operator

\displaystyle\tilde{G}_{\sigma}(E)=\frac{\sigma}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\sum_{s=-S}^{S}\Delta t\,{\rm e}^{-\frac{\sigma^{2}(s\Delta t)^{2}}{2}}U_{s}(\mathcal{H})

(94)

has an error upper-bounded by

\displaystyle\|\tilde{G}_{\sigma}(E)-G_{\sigma}(E)\|\leq\epsilon^{\prime}.

(95)

From Eq. (
95
), one can show

\displaystyle|\left\langle\psi_{0}\right|O\tilde{G}_{\sigma}\left|\psi_{0}\right\rangle-\left\langle\psi_{0}\right|OG_{\sigma}\left|\psi_{0}\right\rangle|=|\left\langle\psi_{0}\right|O(\tilde{G}_{\sigma}-G_{\sigma})\left|\psi_{0}\right\rangle|\leq\|O\|\,\|\tilde{G}_{\sigma}-G_{\sigma}\|\leq\epsilon^{\prime}\|O\|,

(96)

where
\left|\psi_{0}\right\rangle
is an arbitrary quantum state.
Recall that for positive real values
a,\tilde{a},b,\tilde{b}
satisfying

and

, the following inequality holds:

\displaystyle\left|\frac{\tilde{a}}{\tilde{b}}-\frac{a}{b}\right|\leq\frac{|a-\tilde{a}|\tilde{b}}{\tilde{b}b}+\frac{\tilde{a}|b-\tilde{b}|}{\tilde{b}b}\leq\frac{(\tilde{a}+\|O\|\tilde{b})\epsilon}{\tilde{b}b}.

(97)

Setting

\displaystyle a=\left\langle\psi_{0}\right|OG_{\sigma}\left|\psi_{0}\right\rangle,\qquad\tilde{a}=\left\langle\psi_{0}\right|O\tilde{G}_{\sigma}\left|\psi_{0}\right\rangle,\qquad b=\left\langle\psi_{0}\right|G_{\sigma}\left|\psi_{0}\right\rangle,\qquad\tilde{b}=\left\langle\psi_{0}\right|\tilde{G}_{\sigma}\left|\psi_{0}\right\rangle,

(98)

we find

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\left|\frac{\left\langle\psi_{0}\right|O\tilde{G}_{\sigma}\left|\psi_{0}\right\rangle}{\left\langle\psi_{0}\right|\tilde{G}_{\sigma}\left|\psi_{0}\right\rangle}-\frac{\left\langle\psi_{0}\right|OG_{\sigma}\left|\psi_{0}\right\rangle}{\left\langle\psi_{0}\right|G_{\sigma}\left|\psi_{0}\right\rangle}\right|\leq\frac{\epsilon^{\prime}\|O\|(\left\langle\psi_{0}\right|\tilde{G}_{\sigma}\left|\psi_{0}\right\rangle+\left\langle\psi_{0}\right|\tilde{G}_{\sigma}\left|\psi_{0}\right\rangle)}{\left\langle\psi_{0}\right|G_{\sigma}\left|\psi_{0}\right\rangle\left\langle\psi_{0}\right|\tilde{G}_{\sigma}\left|\psi_{0}\right\rangle}\underset{\text{w.h.p}}{\leq}\frac{\epsilon^{\prime}\|O\|}{{\rm Tr}[G_{\sigma}]/2^{N}}=:\epsilon,\end{split}

(99)

where the second inequality holds with high probability. Here, we have defined
\epsilon=\epsilon^{\prime}2^{N}\|O\|/{\rm Tr}[G_{\sigma}]=\epsilon^{\prime}2^{N}\|O\|/{\rm e}^{S_{\sigma}}
using Eq. (
64
).

Finally, we estimate the number of shots required to estimate
\left\langle\psi_{0}\right|O\tilde{G}_{\sigma}\left|\psi_{0}\right\rangle
with the statistical uncertainty
\epsilon^{\prime}
.
The variance of

\displaystyle\begin{split}\mathrm{Var_{shots}}\left[\left\langle\psi_{0}\right|OU_{s}\left|\psi_{0}\right\rangle\right]&:=\left\langle\psi_{0}\right|(OU_{s})^{\dagger}(OU_{s})\left|\psi_{0}\right\rangle-|\left\langle\psi_{0}\right|OU_{s}\left|\psi_{0}\right\rangle|^{2}\\
&\leq\left\langle\psi_{0}\right|U_{s}^{\dagger}O^{\dagger}OU_{s}\left|\psi_{0}\right\rangle\leq\|O\|^{2}.\end{split}

(100)

Then, the variance of

, where

\tilde{G}_{\sigma}
is given in Eq. (
94
), is calculated as

\displaystyle\frac{\sigma^{2}}{2\pi}\sum_{s=-S}^{S}(\Delta t)^{2}\,{\rm e}^{-\sigma^{2}(s\Delta t)^{2}}\mathrm{Var_{shots}}\left[\left\langle\psi_{0}\right|OU_{s}\left|\psi_{0}\right\rangle\right]\leq\mathcal{O}\big(\sigma\Delta t\|O\|^{2}\big).

(101)

Refer to Eq. (
91
) for
\Delta t
.
To achieve a standard deviation of

, the number of shots required is

\displaystyle N_{\mathrm{shots}}=\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{\sigma\Delta t\|O\|^{2}}{\epsilon^{\prime 2}}\right)\approx\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{\|O\|^{4}2^{2N}}{\epsilon^{2}{\rm e}^{2S_{\sigma}}}\right),

(102)

where polynomial dependence on

is ignored in the approximate equality.

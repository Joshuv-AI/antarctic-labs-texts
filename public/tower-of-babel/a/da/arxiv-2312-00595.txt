Monotone duality of interacting particle systems
Jan Niklas Latz, Jan M. Swart

Abstract
The duality theory for monotone interacting particle systems was initiated by Gray (1986) and further developed by Sturm and Swart (2018). It contains the better known additive duality as a special case but differs in the sense that the dual process contains not only single particles but also pairs, triples, and general n n -tuples of particles, which correspond to the fact that in the forward process sometimes several particles are needed to create one particle at a later time. In earlier work, the dual process was constructed for finite initial states only, but, assuming that the empty state is a trap for the forward process, we show that the dual process can be started in infinite initial states and has an upper invariant law. It can therefore be viewed as some sort of interacting particle system in its own right. For the monotone dual of a cooperative contact process, we show that the upper invariant law is the long-time limit started from any nontrivial homogeneous invariant law. We use this to prove continuity of the survival probability of the forward process as a function of its parameters.

{\mathbf{Y}}_{t,s}(Y)\leq{\mathbf{Y}}_{t,s}(Z)
for all
t\geq s
and
Y,Z\in{\cal H}(\Lambda)
such that
Y\leq Z
.

For
a\in S
and
i\in\Lambda
, we define
e^{a}_{i}\in S^{\Lambda}
by

e^{a}_{i}(j):=\begin{cases}a&\text{if}\ j=i,\\
0&\text{else},\end{cases}\qquad(j\in\Lambda).

(1.34)

In particular, if
S=\{0,\ldots,n\}
, then we write
e_{i}:=e^{1}_{i}
. We define

by

Y_{\rm top}:=\big\{e^{a}_{i}:i\in\Lambda,\ a\in S_{\rm sec}\big\}\quad\mbox{with}\quad S_{\rm sec}:=(S\backslash\{0\})^{\circ}.

(1.35)

In particular, if
S=\{0,\ldots,n\}
, then
S_{\rm sec}=\{1\}
and
Y_{\rm top}=\{e_{i}:i\in\Lambda\}
. Elements of

are “second from below” in the order on

is the “top” element of

.

Proposition 9 (Order on the dual state space)

The partial order defined in (

) is compatible with the topology on
{\cal H}(\Lambda)
in the sense that the set

\big\{(Y,Z)\in{\cal H}(\Lambda)^{2}:Y\leq Z\big\}

(1.36)

is closed with respect to the product topology on
{\cal H}(\Lambda)^{2}
. The partially ordered set
{\cal H}(\Lambda)
has a least element, which is
\emptyset

.

As a result of the compatibility condition (

in the usual way, see Appendix

. Together with Lemma

Theorem 10 (Upper invariant law)

Assume (
1.6
), and assume that the rates
(r_{m})_{m\in{\cal G}}
satisfy (
1.10
) and (
1.15
). Then the Feller process

with semigroup

with the property that if

is another invariant law, then

in the stochastic order. Moreover, the process started in

satisfies

{\mathbb{P}}^{Y_{\rm top}}\big[Y_{t}\in\,\cdot\,\big]\underset{{t}\to\infty}{\Longrightarrow}\overline{\nu}_{\rm Y},

(1.37)

where
\Rightarrow

{\cal H}(\Lambda)

\emptyset

1.5
Survival and stability

Throughout this subsection we assume (
1.6
), (
1.10
), and (
1.15
). We also assume that
S
has a greatest element
\top
so that the upper invariant law
\overline{\nu}_{\rm X}
of the forward process is well-defined. We say that the interacting particle system
(X_{t})_{t\geq 0}
is
stable
if
\overline{\nu}_{\rm X}\neq\delta_{\underline{0}}
, the delta measure on the all zero configuration. It is easy to see that this implies that
\overline{\nu}_{\rm X}(\{\underline{0}\})=0
. Indeed, if this would not be the case then we could write
\overline{\nu}_{\rm X}=p\nu^{\prime}+(1-p)\delta_{\underline{0}}
for some
0<p<1
, where
\nu^{\prime}
would be an invariant law that is larger than
\overline{\nu}_{\rm X}
in the stochastic order, contradicting the maximality of the latter. We say that the dual process
(Y_{t})_{t\geq 0}
is
stable
if
\overline{\nu}_{\rm Y}\neq\delta_{\emptyset}
, the delta measure on
\emptyset
, which is the least element of
{\cal H}(\Lambda)
. By the same argument as before, this implies that
\overline{\nu}_{\rm Y}(\{\emptyset\})=0
.

We say that the interacting particle system
(X_{t})_{t\geq 0}
, respectively its monotone dual
(Y_{t})_{t\geq 0}

dies out
if

\begin{array}[]{ll}\displaystyle{\mathbb{P}}\big[\exists t\geq 0\mbox{ s.t.\ }{\mathbf{X}}_{0,t}(x)=\underline{0}\big]=1&\displaystyle\quad(x\in S^{\Lambda}_{\rm fin}),\\[5.0pt]
\displaystyle{\mathbb{P}}\big[\exists t\geq 0\mbox{ s.t.\ }{\mathbf{Y}}_{0,-t}(Y)=\emptyset\big]=1&\displaystyle\quad\big(Y\in{\cal H}_{\rm fin}(\Lambda)\big).\end{array}

(1.39)

If these probabilities are less than one for some

or

or

Lemma 11 (Survival and stability)

Assume (
1.6
), (
1.10
), and (
1.15
), and assume that
S
has a greatest element
\top
. Let
(X_{t})_{t\geq 0}
be the interacting particle system with generator (

) and let
(Y_{t})_{t\geq 0}
be its monotone dual. Then:

(i)

(X_{t})_{t\geq 0}
is stable if and only if
(Y_{t})_{t\geq 0}
survives.

(ii)

(X_{t})_{t\geq 0}
survives if and only if
(Y_{t})_{t\geq 0}
is stable.

We note that part (i) of the lemma (specialised to spin systems) already occurs as formula (30) in
[
Gra86
]
. Part (ii) had to wait to the present paper, since

has (to the best of our knowledge) not previously been constructed for infinite initial states.

(1.42)

for all

\begin{array}[]{c}\displaystyle{\mathbf{Y}}_{t,s}(Y)\mbox{ is the set of minimal elements of }\big\{x\in S^{\Lambda}_{\rm fin}:{\mathbf{X}}_{s,t}(x)\in A\big\}\\[5.0pt]

In particular, setting

and
{\mathbf{Y}}_{t,s}(Y)
is the set of minimal configurations required at time

to create a particle at
i
a time

. Letting

{\mathbb{P}}^{\underline{1}}\big[X_{t}(i)=1\big]={\mathbb{E}}\big[\bm{\psi}_{\rm mon}\big({\mathbf{X}}_{0,t}(\underline{1}),e_{i}\big)\big]={\mathbb{E}}\big[\bm{\psi}_{\rm mon}\big(\underline{1},{\mathbf{Y}}_{t,0}(e_{i})\big)\big]={\mathbb{P}}^{e_{i}}\big[Y_{t}\neq\emptyset\big],

\theta(\alpha,\delta)={\mathbb{P}}^{e_{i}}\big[X_{t}\neq\underline{0}\ \forall t\geq 0\big]=\int_{{\cal H}(\Lambda)}\overline{\nu}_{\rm Y}(\mathrm{d}Y)1_{\{e_{i}\in Y\}}.

(1.47)

We can give a more explicit description of the evolution of the process
(Y_{t})_{t\geq 0}
as follows. In general, if
S
is a partially ordered set containing a least element

(1.48)

The formal generator of
(Y_{t})_{t\geq 0}
is then given by

\hat{G}f(Y):=\sum_{m\in{\cal G}}r_{m}\big\{f\big(\hat{m}(Y)\big)-f\big(Y\big)\big\}\qquad\big(Y\in{\cal H}(\Lambda)\big).

(1.49)

In the special case of the cooperative contact process, one can check that

\begin{array}[]{c}\displaystyle\widehat{\tt dth}_{j}(Y)=\{y\in Y:y(j)=0\},\quad\widehat{\tt bra}_{ij}(Y)=\big(Y\cup\{(y-e_{j})\vee e_{i}:y\in Y,\ y(j)=1\}\big)^{\circ}\\[5.0pt]
\widehat{\tt coop}_{ii^{\prime}j}(Y)=\big(Y\cup\{(y-e_{j})\vee e_{i}\vee e_{i^{\prime}}:y\in Y,\ y(j)=1\}\big)^{\circ}.\end{array}

(1.50)

We observe that the maps
\widehat{\tt dth}_{j}
and
\widehat{\tt bra}_{ij}
have the property that they map the space

{\cal H}_{\rm add}(\Lambda):=\big\{Y\in{\cal H}(\Lambda):|y|=1\ \forall y\in Y\big\}

(1.51)

into itself. As a consequence, for
\alpha=0
the monotone dual
(Y_{t})_{t\geq 0}
of the cooperative contact process has the property that
Y_{0}\in{\cal H}_{\rm add}(\Lambda)
implies
Y_{t}\in{\cal H}_{\rm add}(\Lambda)
for all
t\geq 0
. We claim that this is a consequence of the fact that the process with
\alpha=0
is additive. Indeed, we can naturally identify
\{0,1\}^{\Lambda}
with
{\cal H}_{\rm add}(\Lambda)
via the bijection

\{0,1\}^{{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}}\ni z\mapsto\big\{e_{i}:z(i)=1\big\}\in{\cal H}_{\rm add}(\Lambda).

(1.52)

Identifying
Y_{t}
with an element of
\{0,1\}^{\Lambda}
in this way, one can check that
(Y_{t})_{t\geq 0}
is the additive dual of
(X_{t})_{t\geq 0}
in the sense of additive systems duality. In particular, because of the well-known self-duality of the contact process,
(Y_{t})_{t\geq 0}
is in fact a contact process. Thus, one can view the monotone duals considered in this article as an extension of the classical additive duals of additive interacting particle systems.

1.6
Ergodicity of the dual process

We continue to look at the cooperative contact process on
{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}
with nearest neighbour edges. We define translation operators
S_{i}:\{0,1\}^{{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}}\to\{0,1\}^{{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}}
and
S_{i}:{\cal H}({\mathbb{Z}}^{d})\to{\cal H}({\mathbb{Z}}^{d})
by

(S_{i}x)(j):=x(j-i)\quad(j\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{d})\quad\mbox{and}\quad(S_{i}Y):=\big\{S_{i}y:y\in Y\big\}.

(1.53)

We say that a probability law
\mu
on
{\cal H}({\mathbb{Z}}^{d})
is
homogeneous
if it is invariant under translations, i.e.,
\mu\circ S^{-1}_{i}=\mu
for all
i\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}
. We will prove the following result.

Theorem 12 (Homogeneous initial laws)

Assume that
(\alpha,\delta)\neq(1,0)
. Let
(Y_{t})_{t\geq 0}
be the monotone dual of the cooperative contact process on
{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}
started in an initial law that is homogeneous and satisfies
Y_{0}\neq\emptyset
a.s. Then

{\mathbb{P}}\big[Y_{t}\in\,\cdot\,\big]\underset{{t}\to\infty}{\Longrightarrow}\overline{\nu}_{\rm Y},

(1.54)

where
\Rightarrow
denotes weak convergence of probability measures on
{\cal H}({\mathbb{Z}}^{d})
.

As a consequence of Theorem
12
, by a well-known argument based on the relation (
1.47
), we can prove the following fact about the process we were originally interested in.

Theorem 13 (Continuity of the survival probability)

The function
(\alpha,\delta)\mapsto\theta(\alpha,\delta)
is upper semi-continuous on
[0,1]\times{[0,\infty)}
, and continuous on the set

A:=\big\{(\alpha,\delta)\in[0,1)\times{[0,\infty)}:\exists\varepsilon>0\mbox{ s.t.\ }\theta(\alpha+\varepsilon,\delta+\varepsilon)>0\big\}.

(1.55)

1.7
Discussion and open problems

We have extended the duality theory for monotone interacting particle systems to allow the dual process to be started in infinite initial states, and used this to prove a property of one particular monotone interacting particle system, the cooperative contact process. Apart from monotonicity, we also assumed that the all-zero configuration
\underline{0}
is a trap for the interacting particle system. As long as one is only interested in the dual process started in finite initial states, this condition can be dropped, as already shown by Gray
[
Gra86
]
. In this case, the state space
{\cal H}_{\rm fin}(\Lambda)
needs to be extended so that it also contains
\{\underline{0}\}
(compare (
1.25
)), which now becomes a trap for the dual process. In principle, this construction also works for infinite initial states but it seems that in most cases of interest, the dual process started in an infinite initial state now jumps to the trap
\{\underline{0}\}
immediately. Only when
\underline{0}
is a trap for the interacting particle system, the trap
\{\underline{0}\}
for the dual process becomes inaccessible, which is why we have made this assumption throughout.

There are plenty of open problems for monotone interacting particle systems that are not additive, and for the cooperative contact process in particular. Here we only briefly discuss the latter. For
\alpha=0
the process reduces to a standard contact process, which is self-dual. As a consequence,
\rho(0,\delta)=\theta(0,\delta)
for all
\delta\geq 0
and therefore
\delta^{\prime}_{\rm c}(0)=\delta_{\rm c}(0)
. It is well-known that
0<\delta_{\rm c}(0)<\infty

[
Lig99
]
. Since
\rho
and
\theta
are nonincreasing in
\alpha
it follows that
\delta_{\rm c}(\alpha)
and
\delta^{\prime}_{\rm c}(\alpha)
are finite for all
\alpha\in[0,1]
. A simple monotone coupling with a process without cooperative branching, together with a rescaling of time, tells us that
\delta_{\rm c}(\alpha)\geq(1-\alpha)\delta_{\rm c}(0)
and likewise for
\delta^{\prime}_{\rm c}(\alpha)
, so both functions are
>0
for all
\alpha<1
. It is easy to see that
\delta^{\prime}_{\rm c}(1)=0
, since the process with
\alpha=1
, started in a finite interval, rectangle, or (hyper)cube cannot escape from such a set and hence a.s. dies out for each
\delta>0
. In dimensions
d\geq 2
it is known that
\delta_{\rm c}(1)>0
. This follows from
[
Gra99
, Thm 18.3.1]
. The proof is quite hard and does not yield a lower bound on
\delta_{\rm c}(1)
that is anywhere near the numerically observed value.

Well-known results for the contact process
[
Lig99
]
tell us that the function
\delta\mapsto\theta(0,\delta)
is continuous, with the most difficult statement, continuity at
\delta_{\rm c}(0)
, having been proved in
[
BG90
]
. The methods of the latter paper were generalised in
[
BG94
]
. Applying
[
BG94
, Thm 2.4]
to the cooperative contact process one finds that:

\mbox{The set }\big\{(\alpha,\delta)\in[0,1]\times(0,\infty):\theta(\alpha,\delta)>0\big\}\mbox{ is an open subset of }[0,1]\times(0,\infty),

(1.56)

which combined with our Theorem
13
shows that
\theta
is continuous everywhere except in the point
(\alpha,\delta)=(1,0)
. The methods of
[
BG94
]
can also be used to show that
\theta(\alpha,\delta)>0
implies
\rho(\alpha,\delta)>0
and hence
\delta^{\prime}_{\rm c}(\alpha)\leq\delta_{\rm c}(\alpha)
for all
\alpha\in[0,1]
. This is folklore; the details have unfortunately never been written down.

Apart from the facts we have just mentioned, little seems to be known. It is not known if
\delta^{\prime}_{\rm c}(\alpha)=\delta_{\rm c}(\alpha)
for
\alpha
sufficiently small, as suggested by numerical simulations of the process on
{\mathbb{Z}}^{2}
, nor do there seem to be known results that would allow us to conclude that
\delta^{\prime}_{\rm c}(\alpha)<\delta_{\rm c}(\alpha)
for
\alpha
sufficiently close to one. The continuity of
\rho
on the set
\{(\alpha,\delta):\rho(\alpha,\delta)>0\}
also seems to be an open problem, except on the strip
\alpha=0
. This continuity would follow if one could prove that all homogeneous invariant laws are convex combinations of
\overline{\nu}_{\rm X}
and the delta measure on
\underline{0}
. To prove this, it would suffice to prove in analogy with Theorem
12
that the process
(X_{t})_{t\geq 0}
started in arbitrary nontrivial homogeneous initial law
\mu
converges to
\overline{\nu}_{\rm X}
. The latter statement is well-known for the contact process
[
Har76
]
but it seems plausible it is in general
not true
for the cooperative contact process. Let
{\mathbb{P}}^{\pi_{p}}
denote the law of the process started in product law
\pi_{p}
with intensity
p
, and let

\eta(\alpha,\delta):=\inf\big\{p\in[0,1]:{\mathbb{P}}^{\pi_{p}}\big[X_{t}\in\,\cdot\,\big]\underset{{t}\to\infty}{\Longrightarrow}\overline{\nu}_{\rm X}\big\}

(1.57)

denote the minimal initial density for which the process started in product law converges to the upper invariant law. Then we conjecture that
\eta(\alpha,\delta)>0
in the regime
\delta^{\prime}_{\rm c}(\alpha)<\delta\leq\delta_{\rm c}(\alpha)
. Proving this is an open problem. Finally, numerical simulations suggest that
\delta\mapsto\rho(\alpha,\delta)
is continuous at
\delta_{\rm c}(\alpha)
if
\delta^{\prime}_{\rm c}(\alpha)=\delta_{\rm c}(\alpha)
, but not if
\delta^{\prime}_{\rm c}(\alpha)<\delta_{\rm c}(\alpha)
. Proving this, except at
\alpha=0
, is an open problem too. As a last remark, it is worth mentioning that
[
BG94
]
and
[
Gra99
]
use the structure of
{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}
in an essential way and cannot easily be generalised to different grids, so that on more general grids even less is known.

At this moment, it is too early to tell if monotone duality can help resolve some of these open problems. An interesting line of thought seems to be to try and prove that for
\alpha
sufficiently small, the dual process behaves almost as in the additive case
\alpha=0
, for example in the sense that if
Y
is distributed according to the upper invariant law, then elements
y\in Y
with
|y|\geq 2
have a low density. Another question is under what assumptions the homogeneity assumption in Theorem
12
can be dropped, i.e., if one can prove some form of
complete convergence
for the dual process. (See
[
Lig99
]
for this terminology in the context of the contact process.)

Proof of Proposition
5

We claim that for any
A\subset S^{\Lambda}
,

{\rm(i)}\ (A^{\circ})^{\circ}=A^{\circ},\quad{\rm(ii)}\ (A^{\uparrow})^{\uparrow}=A^{\uparrow},\quad{\rm(iii)}\ (A^{\uparrow})^{\circ}=A^{\circ}.

(2.1)

Indeed, the first two properties are trivial while (iii) follows from (i) and the observations that
A^{\circ}\subset(A^{\uparrow})^{\circ}\subset A
. We next claim that for
A\subset S^{\Lambda}

A^{\uparrow}=A,\ A\mbox{ open}\quad\Rightarrow\quad A^{\circ}\subset S^{\Lambda}_{\rm fin}\mbox{ and }(A^{\circ})^{\uparrow}=A.

(2.2)

To see this, we first note that trivially
(A^{\circ})^{\uparrow}\subset A^{\uparrow}=A
. To prove the converse inclusion, assume that
x\in A
. Then we can find
x_{n}\in S^{\Lambda}_{\rm fin}
such that
x_{n}\to x
. Since
A
is open there exists an
n
such that
x_{n}\in A
. Since
x_{n}\in S^{\Lambda}_{\rm fin}

Since this holds for arbitrary

and the definition of the topology on
{\cal H}(\Lambda)

).

To show uniqueness, assume that

and

and define

(2.23)

We claim that the function

[s,u]\ni t\mapsto\bm{\psi}_{\rm mon}(X_{t},Y_{t})

(2.24)

is constant.

We start by showing that it is cadlag. By Lemma
3
, the function
(X_{t})_{t\geq s}

. Combining this with Theorem

, and
x_{k}:=X_{s_{k}}

(k\geq 0)

We can now use the assumption that

\bm{\psi}_{\rm mon}\big(X_{t-},Y_{t-}\big)=\bm{\psi}_{\rm mon}\big(X_{t-},\hat{m}(Y_{t})\big)=\bm{\psi}_{\rm mon}\big(m(X_{t-}),Y_{t}\big)=\bm{\psi}_{\rm mon}(X_{t},Y_{t})

at times

The fact that the function in (

\bm{\psi}_{\rm mon}(x,Y_{s})=\bm{\psi}_{\rm mon}\big({\mathbf{X}}_{s,u}(x),Y\big)=\bm{\psi}_{\rm mon}\big(x,{\mathbf{Y}}_{t,s}(Y)\big).

Proof of Theorem

Using the fact that the backward stochastic flow

with semigroup

defined in (

denote the space of probability measures on
{\cal H}(\Lambda)

and

(Y_{n},t_{n})\underset{{n}\to\infty}{\longrightarrow}(Y,t)\quad\mbox{implies}\quad{\mathbf{Y}}_{t_{n},0}(Y_{n})\underset{{n}\to\infty}{\longrightarrow}{\mathbf{Y}}_{t,0}(Y)\quad{\rm a.s.}

Proof of Lemma

2.4
Homogeneous invariant laws

In this subsection we prepare for the proof of Theorem

by proving a more general statement that, specialised to the cooperative contact process, will yield Theorem
12
. We first need a characterisation of the upper invariant law of the dual process
(Y_{t})_{t\geq 0}
in terms of the forward process

. We work for the moment in the general set-up of Theorem

Lemma 25 (Distribution determining functions)

Let

be
{\cal H}(\Lambda)

{\mathbb{E}}\big[\prod_{k=1}^{n}\bm{\psi}_{\rm mon}(x_{k},Y)\big]={\mathbb{E}}\big[\prod_{k=1}^{n}\bm{\psi}_{\rm mon}(x_{k},Z)\big]\qquad\forall n\geq 1,\ x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}\in S^{\Lambda}_{\rm fin}.

(2.36)

Then
Y
and
Z
are equal in law.

Proof
Let
{\cal F}
be the class of functions
f:{\cal H}(\Lambda)\to{\mathbb{R}}
of the form
f(Y)=\prod_{k=1}^{n}\bm{\psi}_{\rm mon}(x_{k},Y)
with

and
x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}\in S^{\Lambda}_{\rm fin}
. By
[
Swa22
, Lemma 4.37]
it suffices to show that each

is continuous, and that
{\cal F}
is closed under products and separates points in the sense that for each
Y,Z\in{\cal H}(\Lambda)
, there exists an
f\in{\cal F}
such that

. Functions
f\in{\cal F}
are continuous by Lemma
18
, the class
{\cal F}
is closed under products by construction, and
{\cal F}
separates points by Lemma
22
.

Lemma 26 (Characterisation of the upper invariant law)

Assume (
1.6
), and assume that the rates
(r_{m})_{m\in{\cal G}}
satisfy (
1.10
) and (

). Then the upper invariant law

of the dual process, defined in (

\int\overline{\nu}_{\rm Y}(\mathrm{d}Y)\prod_{k=1}^{n}\bm{\psi}_{\rm mon}(x_{k},Y)={\mathbb{P}}\big[{\mathbf{X}}_{0,t}(x_{k})\neq\underline{0}\ \forall t\geq 0,\ 1\leq k\leq n\big]\qquad(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}\in S^{\Lambda}_{\rm fin}).

(2.37)

Proof
For
n=1

) has already been proved as formula (
2.35

We now set out to prove a general result in the spirit of Theorem
12
. We assume from now on that the grid
\Lambda
is a (not necessarily abelian) group with product
(i,j)\mapsto ij
. A bit unusually, to stay closer to the notation for

\Lambda
by

. For each

(S_{i}x)(j):=x(i^{-1}j)\quad(j\in\Lambda)\quad\mbox{and}\quad(T_{i}m)(x)(j):=m(S_{i^{-1}}x)(i^{-1}j)\quad(j\in\Lambda).

(2.38)

We say that a probability measure
\mu
on

is

if
\mu=\mu\circ S_{i}^{-1}
for all

, and we say that the rates

(2.39)

As we will show, Theorem

is a simple consequence of the following more general theorem. Recall from (

) that
|x|:=\sum_{i\in\Lambda}1_{\{x(i)\neq 0\}}
. The assumptions
S=\{0,\ldots,n\}

(m\in{\cal G})

Theorem 27 (Homogeneous initial laws)

Assume (
1.6
) and that the grid
\Lambda
is a group. Assume that
S=\{0,\ldots,n\}
and
|{\cal D}(m)|=1
for all
m\in{\cal G}

satisfy (

) and (
1.15

(i)

\displaystyle\forall\varepsilon>0\ \exists N<\infty

.

(ii)

for each

and

.

Then the monotone dual

started in an initial law that is homogeneous with

{\mathbb{P}}\big[Y_{t}\in\,\cdot\,\big]\underset{{t}\to\infty}{\Longrightarrow}\overline{\nu}_{\rm Y},

The basic idea behind the proof of Theorem

[

Lemma 28 (Extinction versus unbounded growth)

Assume (
1.6
), (
1.10
), (
1.15
) and condition (i) of Theorem

. Then

{\mathbb{P}}^{x}\big[0<|X_{t}|<N\big]\underset{{t}\to\infty}{\longrightarrow}0\qquad(x\in S^{\Lambda}_{\rm fin},\ N<\infty).

Proof
Condition (i) of Theorem

says that each time the process returns to a state

with
|x|\leq N
, there is a probability of at least
\varepsilon
that the process gets extinct. By a standard argument, this implies that almost surely either
X_{t}=\underline{0}
for some

or

, see
[
Swa22

.

The next lemma is the key step in the proof of Theorem

Lemma 29 (Large is good)

Assume (
1.6
) and that the grid
\Lambda

(r_{m})_{m\in{\cal G}}

1.10
) and (
1.15

We construct
(Y_{t})_{t\geq 0}
as
Y_{t}:={\mathbf{Y}}_{s,s-t}(Y_{0})

(t\geq 0)

Y_{0}
is independent of the graphical representation
\omega

(2.43)

Fix
s,\varepsilon>0

with

as

) as

\begin{array}[]{l}\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}\big[\prod_{k=1}^{n}\bm{\psi}_{\rm mon}\big({\mathbf{X}}_{0,t}(x_{k}),Y_{s}\big)\,\big|\,A\big]\cdot{\mathbb{P}}[A]+{\mathbb{E}}\big[\prod_{k=1}^{n}\bm{\psi}_{\rm mon}\big({\mathbf{X}}_{0,t}(x_{k}),Y_{s}\big)\,\big|\,B\big]\cdot{\mathbb{P}}[B]\\[5.0pt]
\displaystyle+{\mathbb{E}}\big[\prod_{k=1}^{n}\bm{\psi}_{\rm mon}\big({\mathbf{X}}_{0,t}(x_{k}),Y_{s}\big)\,\big|\,C\big]\cdot{\mathbb{P}}[C].\end{array}

(2.54)

Here the first term is zero since
\bm{\psi}_{\rm mon}(x,Y)=0
if
x=\underline{0}
and the second tends to zero by Lemma
28
. By Lemma
29
and a simple union bound,

{\mathbb{P}}\big[\bm{\psi}_{\rm mon}\big({\mathbf{X}}_{0,t}(x_{k}),Y_{s}\big)=0\mbox{ for some }1\leq k\leq n\,\big|\,C\big]\leq n\varepsilon,

(2.55)

while by Lemma
28
the probability of the event
C
tends to
\rho(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})
. It follows that

\begin{array}[]{r@{\,}l}\displaystyle(1-n\varepsilon)\rho(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})&\displaystyle\leq\liminf_{t\to\infty}{\mathbb{E}}\big[\prod_{k=1}^{n}\bm{\psi}_{\rm mon}\big({\mathbf{X}}_{0,t}(x_{k}),Y_{s}\big)\big]\\[5.0pt]
&\displaystyle\leq\limsup_{t\to\infty}{\mathbb{E}}\big[\prod_{k=1}^{n}\bm{\psi}_{\rm mon}\big({\mathbf{X}}_{0,t}(x_{k}),Y_{s}\big)\big]\leq\rho(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}).\end{array}

(2.56)

Since
\varepsilon>0
is arbitrary, this proves the theorem.

We let

Lemma 39 (Evolving set process)

Assume (
1.10

1.15

, there exists a unique cadlag function
\xi^{s,A}:[s,\infty)\to{\cal P}_{\rm fin}(\Lambda)
such that
\xi^{s,A}_{s}=A
and

\xi^{s,A}_{t}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\big\{j\in\Lambda:\exists i\in\xi^{s,A}_{t-}\mbox{ s.t.\ }(i,j)\in{\cal R}(m)\big\}&\mbox{if }(m,t)\in\omega,\\[5.0pt]
\xi^{s,A}_{t-}&\mbox{otherwise}\end{array}\right.\qquad(t>s).

(A.3)

Proof
If
s
and
A
are deterministic, then
(\xi^{s,A}_{s+t})_{t\geq 0}
is a continuous-time Markov chain with countable state space
{\cal P}_{\rm fin}(\Lambda)
. Using (
1.10
) (i), one can show that this process is well-defined in the sense that all its jump rates are finite, and using moreover (
1.15
) one can show that this process is nonexplosive. This is extremely similar to
[

, Lemma 4.21]
, except that the process

considered there “runs backwards in time” and (
1.15
) is replaced by (
1.10
) (ii). Since the proof is essentially the same, we omit the details. Using an argument as in
[
Swa22
, Lemma 4.22]
, one can remove the assumption that
s
and
A
are deterministic, i.e., one can show that the statement holds a.s. for all
s\in{\mathbb{R}}
and

simultaneously.

Approximate option pricing formula for Barndorff-Nielsen and Shephard model
Takuji Arai

Abstract
For the Barndorff-Nielsen and Shephard model, we present approximate expressions of call option prices based on the decomposition formula developed by Arai [ 3 ] . Besides, some numerical experiments are also implemented to make sure how effective our approximations are.



1
Introduction

The Barndorff-Nielsen and Shephard (BNS) model is a representative jump-type stochastic volatility model,
which has been actively researched since it was undertaken by Barndorff-Nielsen and Shephard
[
6
]
,
[
7
]
, but it is by no means tractable mathematically.
Indeed, there are no closed-form expressions of option prices for the BNS model, although option prices themselves are numerically computable by using e.g. the fast Fourier transform.
Then we aim to derive approximations of call option prices based on the decomposition formula developed by Arai
[
3
]
.
Approximations obtained in this paper will be useful to analyze the structure of implied volatilities and to develop calibration procedures for model parameters.

As a similar expression to the so-called Hull-White formula, Alòs
[
1
]
has derived a new decomposition formula of option prices for the Heston model
by making use of Ito calculus.
As its extension to the BNS model,
[
3
]
presented a decomposition expression, in which option prices for the BNS model are decomposed into 7 terms:
a primal term, an additional term, and 5 residual terms.
The primal term is given by substituting the present volatility value for the Black-Scholes formula,
and the additional term is given by integration with respect to the Lévy measure of the jump component in the BNS model,
of which integrand is the sum of a difference and a partial derivative of the Black-Scholes formula.
Since it is very complicated to develop a computational method for the additional term, we shall suggest in this paper two types of correction terms.
One is available for around at the money (ATM) and in the money (ITM) options, and converges to the additional term as the time to maturity tends to 0
with order
1
for around ATM options and with order
\frac{3}{2}
for ITM options that are not around ATM.
On the other hand, the other is available for ITM options alone, and its rate of convergence to the additional term is of order
1
.
Moreover, we shall show that all the 5 residual terms converge to 0 as the time to maturity tends to 0 with order
\frac{3}{2}
.
Hence, adding a correction term to the primal term, we obtain an approximate option pricing formula for the BNS model.
Thus, we shall produce three approximation formulas including one hybrid version of the two correction terms.
Remark that we do not assume the finite activity of the jump component in this paper.
Besides, we shall implement some numerical experiments to examine the effectiveness of our approximations.
The results of our experiments indicate that our approximations are effective particularly when the time to maturity is short.

For various stochastic volatility models, some authors have studied approximate option pricing expressions based on Alòs type decomposition,
which has been undertaken by
[
1
]
as mentioned above for the Heston model: a representative continuous stochastic volatility model.
Further, she also in
[
1
]
developed two types of approximate option pricing formulas with order
\frac{3}{2}
.
A similar approximation with order
1
has been presented by Alòs et al.
[
2
]
.
As an extension of
[
1
]
, Merino and Vives
[
10
]
gave a decomposition and an approximation for spot-dependent volatility models including the CEV model.
As for jump type models, Merino et al.
[
9
]
treated jump type stochastic volatility models with finite activity and obtained an approximation formula with order
1
.
To our best knowledge, there is no result on this topic for jump models with infinite activity.

This paper is organized as follows: Some mathematical preliminaries and notations are given in the following section.
Approximation formulas and numerical results are introduced in Section 3.
Section 4 is devoted to proofs of our main results, and Section 5 concludes this paper.

2
Preliminaries

2.1
Model description

Throughout this paper, we consider a financial market model being composed of one risky asset and one riskless asset with interest rate
r\geq 0
and finite time horizon
T>0
.
In the BNS model, the risky asset price at time
t\in[0,T]
is described by

S_{t}:=S_{0}\exp\left\{\int_{0}^{t}\left(r+\mu-\frac{1}{2}\Sigma_{u}^{2}\right)du+\int_{0}^{t}\Sigma_{u}dW_{u}+\rho H_{\lambda t}\right\},\ \ \ t\in[0,T],

where
S_{0}>0
,
\rho<0
,
\mu\in{\mathbb{R}}
,
\lambda>0
,
H
is a subordinator without drift, and
W
is a
1
-dimensional standard Brownian motion.
Remark that we exclude the case of
\rho=0
, that is, the asset price process
S
is continuous, since such a case is not interesting for our purposes.
Here
\Sigma
is the volatility process defined via its squared process
\Sigma^{2}
, which is the solution to the stochastic differential equation

d\Sigma_{t}^{2}=-\lambda\Sigma_{t}^{2}dt+dH_{\lambda t},\ \ \ t\in[0,T]

with
\Sigma_{0}^{2}>0
, that is,
\Sigma^{2}
is an Ornstein-Uhlenbeck process driven by the subordinator
H_{\lambda}
.
Note that
S
is defined on some filtered probability space
(\Omega,{\mathcal{F}},({\mathcal{F}}_{t})_{0\leq t\leq T},{\mathbb{P}})
with the usual condition,
where
({\mathcal{F}}_{t})_{0\leq t\leq T}
is the filtration generated by
W
and
H_{\lambda}
.
Moreover,
X
denotes the log price process
\log S
, that is,
X_{t}:=\log S_{t}
for
t\in[0,T]
.
For more details on the BNS model, see also Nicolato and Venardos
[
11
]
and Schoutens
[
12
]
.

For later use, we introduce some properties of
\Sigma
: Firstly, we have

\Sigma_{t}^{2}=e^{-\lambda t}\Sigma^{2}_{0}+\int_{0}^{t}e^{-\lambda(t-u)}dH_{\lambda u}\geq e^{-\lambda T}\Sigma^{2}_{0}

(2.1)

for any
t\in[0,T]
, that is,
\Sigma
is bounded from below.
Next, the integrated squared volatility is represented as

\int_{t}^{T}\Sigma_{u}^{2}du=\epsilon(T-t)\Sigma^{2}_{t}+\int_{t}^{T}\epsilon(T-u)dH_{\lambda u}

(2.2)

for any
t\in[0,T]
, where

\epsilon(t):=\frac{1-e^{-\lambda t}}{\lambda}.

Further, we denote by
N
the Poisson random measure of
H_{\lambda}
.
Hence, we have

H_{\lambda t}=\int_{0}^{\infty}zN([0,t],dz),\ \ \ t\in[0,T].

Letting
\nu
be the Lévy measure of
H_{\lambda}
, we find that

\widetilde{N}(dt,dz):=N(dt,dz)-\nu(dz)dt

is the compensated Poisson random measure.
Note that
\nu
is a
\sigma
-finite measure on
(0,\infty)
satisfying

\int_{0}^{\infty}(z\wedge 1)\nu(dz)<\infty

(2.3)

by Proposition 3.10 of Cont and Tankov
[
8
]
.

Now, we introduce our standing assumptions as follows:

Assumption 2.1
.

1.

\displaystyle{\mu=\int_{0}^{\infty}(1-e^{\rho z})\nu(dz)}
.

2.

\displaystyle{\int_{1}^{\infty}e^{2\epsilon(T)z}\nu(dz)<\infty}
.

Note that the finiteness of the right hand side of the condition 1 is ensured by (
2.3
).
The above condition 1 implies that the discounted asset price process
\widehat{S}_{t}:=e^{-rt}S_{t}
becomes a local martingale.
On the other hand, the condition 2 ensures that

\int_{0}^{\infty}z^{2}\nu(dz)<\infty,

which yields
{\mathbb{E}}[H_{\lambda T}^{2}]<\infty
by Proposition 3.13 of
[
8
]
.
In addition,

{\mathbb{E}}\left[\sup_{t\in[0,T]}S^{2}_{t}\right]<\infty

(2.4)

holds under the condition 2 from the view of Subsection 2.3 of Arai and Suzuki
[
5
]
.
Thus,
\widehat{S}
is a square-integrable martingale under Assumption
2.1
.
Moreover, the condition 2, together with (
2.3
), implies

\int_{0}^{\infty}z\nu(dz)<\infty.

(2.5)

Example 2.2
.

We introduce two important examples of the squared volatility process
\Sigma^{2}
.

1.

The first one is the case where
\Sigma^{2}
follows an IG-OU process. The corresponding Lévy measure
\nu
is given by

\nu(dz)=\frac{\lambda a}{2\sqrt{2\pi}}z^{-\frac{3}{2}}(1+b^{2}z)\exp\left\{-\frac{1}{2}b^{2}z\right\}dz,\ \ \ z\in(0,\infty),

where
a>0
and
b>0
.
Note that this is a representative example of the BNS model with infinite active jumps, that is,
\nu((0,\infty))=\infty
.
In this case, the invariant distribution of
\Sigma^{2}
follows an inverse-Gaussian distribution with parameters
a>0
and
b>0
.
Note that the condition 2 of Assumption
2.1
is satisfied whenever
\frac{b^{2}}{2}>2\epsilon(T)

2.

The second example is the gamma-OU case. In this case,
\nu
is described as

\nu(dz)=\lambda abe^{-bz}dz,\ \ \ z\in(0,\infty),

and the invariant distribution of
\Sigma^{2}
is given by a gamma distribution with parameters
a>0
and
b>0
.
If
b>2\epsilon(T)
, then the condition 2 of Assumption
2.1
is satisfied.

Lastly, but not least, we introduce additional assumptions on the Lévy measure
\nu
as follows:

Assumption 2.3
.

The Lévy measure
\nu
is of the form
\nu(dz)=f(z)dz
for
z>0
,
where
f(z)
is a decreasing function on
(0,\infty)
, and there exists constants
C^{\nu}_{0}>0
and
C^{\nu}_{1}>0
such that

f(z)\leq C^{\nu}_{0}\gamma(z)e^{-C^{\nu}_{1}z}

(2.6)

for any
z>0
, where
\gamma(z):=z^{-\frac{3}{2}}\vee z^{-\frac{1}{2}}
.

Remark 2.4
.

Both examples introduced in Example
2.2
: the IG-OU and the gamma-OU cases satisfy Assumption
2.3
.

2.2
Black-Scholes formula

We consider the Black-Scholes model in this subsection.
Let
\sigma>0
be the volatility. For the call option with strike price
K>0
and maturity
T>0
,
its price at time
t\in[0,T)
with the log asset price
x\in{\mathbb{R}}
is well-known as the Black-Scholes formula.
We treat it as a function on
\sigma^{2}
as well as
t
and
x
, and denote it by
BS(t,x,\sigma^{2})
.
Recall that the function
BS
is described as

BS(t,x,\sigma^{2}):=e^{x}\Phi(d^{+})-Ke^{-r\tau_{t}}\Phi(d^{-}),\ \ \ t\in[0,T),x\in{\mathbb{R}},\sigma>0.

(2.7)

Here
\tau_{t}:=T-t
,
\Phi
is the cumulative distribution function of the standard normal distribution, and

d^{\pm}:=\frac{x-\log K+r\tau_{t}}{\sigma\sqrt{\tau_{t}}}\pm\frac{\sigma\sqrt{\tau_{t}}}{2},

where double sign corresponds.
For later use, we define additionally

d^{\pm}_{\rho z}:=d^{\pm}+\frac{\rho z}{\sigma\sqrt{\tau_{t}}}.

for
z>0
.
Remark that the time parameter
t
included in
d^{\pm}
and
d^{\pm}_{\rho z}
might be replaced with
u
according to the situation.
In addition, since we have

\lim_{t\to T}BS(t,x,\sigma^{2})=(e^{x}-K)^{+},

the domain of the function
BS
can be extended to
[0,T]\times{\mathbb{R}}\times(0,\infty)
, and we may define

BS(T,x,\sigma^{2}):=(e^{x}-K)^{+}.

Lastly, we define the following operators for
{\mathbb{R}}
-valued functions
f(t,x,\sigma^{2})
,
t\in[0,T)
,
x\in{\mathbb{R}}
,
\sigma>0
:

\Delta^{a,b}f(t,x,\sigma^{2}):=f(t,x+a,\sigma^{2}+b)-f(t,x,\sigma^{2}),\ \ \ a,b\in{\mathbb{R}},

{\mathcal{L}}^{z}f(t,x,\sigma^{2}):=\Delta^{\rho z,0}f(t,x,\sigma^{2})+\partial_{x}f(t,x,\sigma^{2})(1-e^{\rho z}),\ \ \ z>0,

and

{\overline{{\mathcal{L}}}}f(t,x,\sigma^{2}):=\int_{0}^{\infty}{\mathcal{L}}^{z}f(t,x,\sigma^{2})\nu(dz).

2.3
Decomposition formula

For the BNS model introduced in Subsection 2.1, the price of the call option with strike price
K>0
and maturity
T>0
at time
t\in[0,T]
is given as

V_{t}:=e^{-r\tau_{t}}{\mathbb{E}}[BS(T,X_{T},\Sigma^{2}_{T})|X_{t},\Sigma^{2}_{t}],

(2.8)

where
X_{t}=\log S_{t}
for
t\in[0,T]
, since the discounted asset price process
\widehat{S}
is a square-integrable martingale under Assumption
2.1
.
The following theorem gives a decomposition expression of
V_{t}
, which will play important role for the development of approximations for (
2.8
).

Theorem 2.5

(Theorem 3.1 of Arai
[
3
]
)
.

Under Assumption
2.1
, we have, for
t\in[0,T]
,

V_{t}=BS(t,X_{t},\Sigma^{2}_{t})+\tau_{t}{\overline{{\mathcal{L}}}}BS(t,X_{t},\Sigma^{2}_{t})+I_{1}+I_{2}+I_{3}+I_{4}+I_{5}.

(2.9)

Here,
I_{1},\dots,I_{5}
are defined as follows:

\displaystyle I_{1}

\displaystyle:={\mathbb{E}}\left[\int_{t}^{T}e^{-r(u-t)}\partial_{\sigma^{2}}BS(u,X_{u},\Sigma^{2}_{u})(-\lambda\Sigma^{2}_{u})du\Big|X_{t},\Sigma^{2}_{t}\right],\hskip 256.0748pt

\displaystyle I_{2}

\displaystyle:={\mathbb{E}}\left[\int_{t}^{T}e^{-r(u-t)}\int_{0}^{\infty}\left(\Delta^{\rho z,z}-\Delta^{\rho z,0}\right)BS(u,X_{u},\Sigma^{2}_{u})\nu(dz)du\Big|X_{t},\Sigma^{2}_{t}\right],

\displaystyle I_{3}

\displaystyle:={\mathbb{E}}\left[\int_{t}^{T}e^{-r(u-t)}\tau_{u}\partial_{x}{\overline{{\mathcal{L}}}}BS(u,X_{u},\Sigma^{2}_{u})\mu du\Big|X_{t},\Sigma^{2}_{t}\right],

\displaystyle I_{4}

\displaystyle:={\mathbb{E}}\left[\int_{t}^{T}e^{-r(u-t)}\tau_{u}\partial_{\sigma^{2}}{\overline{{\mathcal{L}}}}BS(u,X_{u},\Sigma^{2}_{u})(-\lambda\Sigma^{2}_{u})du\Big|X_{t},\Sigma^{2}_{t}\right],

and

\displaystyle I_{5}

\displaystyle:={\mathbb{E}}\Bigg[\int_{t}^{T}e^{-r(u-t)}\tau_{u}\int_{0}^{\infty}\Delta^{\rho z,z}{\overline{{\mathcal{L}}}}BS(u,X_{u},\Sigma^{2}_{u})\nu(dz)du\Big|X_{t},\Sigma^{2}_{t}\Bigg],

where
\tau_{u}:=T-u
.

3
Main results

In this section, we present approximate option pricing formulas, and illustrate some numerical results.
Henceforth, we assume Assumptions
2.1
and
2.3
.

3.1
Approximation formulas

For the call option price at time
t\in[0,T]
with strike price
K>0
for the BNS model,
we derive numerically tractable approximations from the decomposition formula (
2.9
).
First of all, the first term
BS(t,X_{t},\Sigma^{2}_{t})
in (
2.9
) is treated as the primal term in our approximations, since it is easily computable.
On the other hand, all the remaining terms are not numerically tractable.
Thus, we need to exclude or replace them from our approximations.
In fact, we shall show that
I_{1},\dots,I_{5}
converge to 0 as the time to maturity goes to 0 with order
\frac{3}{2}
in Proposition
4.1
below.
This fact allows us to exclude
I_{1},\dots,I_{5}
from our approximations.
Hence, all what we have to do is to approximate the second term
\tau_{t}{\overline{{\mathcal{L}}}}BS(t,X_{t},\Sigma^{2}_{t})
.
To this end, we suggest two correction terms to
\tau_{t}{\overline{{\mathcal{L}}}}BS(t,X_{t},\Sigma^{2}_{t})
, both of which are computable.
One is givens as

\tau_{t}\int_{Z^{0}\vee\overline{Z}}^{\infty}\left(Ke^{-r\tau_{t}}\Phi(D^{-})-e^{X_{t}+\rho z}\Phi(D^{+})\right)\nu(dz),

(3.1)

where
\Phi
is the cumulative distribution function of the standard normal distribution,

Z^{0}:=\frac{X_{t}-\log K+r\tau_{t}}{|\rho|},\ \ \ \overline{Z}:=\frac{2\Sigma_{t}^{2}}{|\rho|}\ \ \ \mbox{ and }\ \ \ D^{\pm}:=\frac{X_{t}-\log K+r\tau_{t}}{\Sigma_{t}\sqrt{\tau_{t}}}\pm\frac{\Sigma_{t}\sqrt{\tau_{t}}}{2}.

(
3.1
) is available for ITM and around ATM options, more precisely the case where
X_{t}-\log K>-2\Sigma^{2}_{t}
.
Another correction term is given as follows:

\tau_{t}\int_{Z_{0}}^{\infty}\left(Ke^{-r\tau_{t}}-e^{X_{t}+\rho z}\right)\nu(dz),

(3.2)

which is defined for ITM options alone.

Remark 3.1
.

We can give a financial interpretation for the correction term (
3.2
).
Consider an ITM option at time
t
.
Even if
\tau_{t}
is small, the ITM option may change in a moment into an out-of-the-money (OTM) one due to a big jump in the asset price.
Thus, we can interpret that the integrand in the correction term (
3.2
) eliminates the payoff of the ITM option when a big jump occurs, roughly speaking.
In addition, since the probability that such a big jump occurs is nearly equal to
\tau_{t}\nu([Z^{0},\infty))
, the correction term is multiplied by
\tau_{t}
.
In contrast, since positive jumps never occur in the asset price process, we do not need to take into account the reverse changes, that is,
the changes of an OTM option into an ITM one. Hence, the correction term is available for ITM options alone.

On the other hand, (
3.1
) is available for not only ITM but also around ATM options.
The integrand in (
3.1
) is close to that in (
3.2
) when
X_{t}-\log K>0
since
D^{\pm}
tends to
\infty
as
\tau_{t}\to 0
,
while
D^{\pm}
tends to
-\infty
as
\tau_{t}\to 0
when
X_{t}-\log K<0
, that is, (
3.1
) is close to 0 for OTM options.

Theorems
3.2
and
3.3
provide approximation formulas using the above correction terms (
3.1
) and (
3.2
), respectively, and
a hybrid version is introduced in Corollary
3.4
.

Theorem 3.2
.

Let

\widetilde{V}^{1}_{t}:=BS(t,X_{t},\Sigma^{2}_{t})+\tau_{t}\int_{Z^{0}\vee\overline{Z}}^{\infty}\left(Ke^{-r\tau_{t}}\Phi(D^{-})-e^{X_{t}+\rho z}\Phi(D^{+})\right)\nu(dz).

When
X_{t}-\log K>-2\Sigma_{t}^{2}
, there is a constant
C>0
such that

\left|V_{t}-\widetilde{V}^{1}_{t}\right|\leq C\left\{\tau_{t}^{\frac{3}{2}}{\bf 1}_{\{X_{t}-\log K\geq 2\Sigma_{t}^{2}\}}+\tau_{t}{\bf 1}_{\{|X_{t}-\log K|<2\Sigma_{t}^{2}\}}\right\}

for any
t\in[0,T]
, where
C>0
is depending on
X_{t}
and
\Sigma_{t}
, and a nondecreasing function of
T
.

Theorem 3.3
.

Suppose
X_{t}-\log K\geq 2\Sigma_{t}^{2}
, and denote

\widetilde{V}^{2}_{t}:=BS(t,X_{t},\Sigma^{2}_{t})+\tau_{t}\int_{Z^{0}}^{\infty}\left(Ke^{-r\tau_{t}}-e^{X_{t}+\rho z}\right)\nu(dz).

There is a constant
C>0
such that

\left|V_{t}-\widetilde{V}^{2}_{t}\right|\leq C\tau_{t}^{\frac{3}{2}}

for any
t\in[0,T]
, where
C>0
is depending on
X_{t}
and
\Sigma_{t}
, and a nondecreasing function of
T
.

Corollary 3.4
.

Suppose
X_{t}-\log K>-2\Sigma_{t}^{2}
, and denote

\displaystyle\widetilde{V}^{3}_{t}

\displaystyle:=BS(t,X_{t},\Sigma^{2}_{t})+\tau_{t}\int_{Z^{0}}^{\infty}\left(Ke^{-r\tau_{t}}-e^{X_{t}+\rho z}\right)\nu(dz){\bf 1}_{\{X_{t}-\log K\geq 2\Sigma_{t}^{2}\}}

\displaystyle\hskip 14.22636pt+\tau_{t}\int_{Z^{0}\vee\overline{Z}}^{\infty}\left(Ke^{-r\tau_{t}}\Phi(D^{-})-e^{X_{t}+\rho z}\Phi(D^{+})\right)\nu(dz){\bf 1}_{\{|X_{t}-\log K|<2\Sigma_{t}^{2}\}}.

There is a constant
C>0
such that

\left|V_{t}-\widetilde{V}^{3}_{t}\right|\leq C\tau_{t}

for any
t\in[0,T]
, where
C>0
is depending on
X_{t}
and
\Sigma_{t}
, and a nondecreasing function of
T
.

Remark 3.5
.

Any approximation introduced in this section is not available when the option is deep OTM, that is, the case of
X_{t}-\log K\leq-2\Sigma_{t}^{2}
,
but this exclusion is not restrictive, since deep OTM option prices with short maturity are sufficiently near to zero.

Moreover, in spite that the correction term (

.

.
Therefore, we restrict the value of

to be greater than
2\Sigma_{t}^{2}
in Theorem
3.3
.
On the other hand, Theorem

is useful for around ATM options, too, but the rate of convergence for around ATM options is changed from
\tau_{t}^{\frac{3}{2}}
into
\tau_{t}
.

3.2
Numerical experiments

We implement some numerical experiments in order to examine the effectiveness of approximation formulas
\widetilde{V}^{1}
and
\widetilde{V}^{3}
introduced in Theorem
3.2

and Corollary
3.4
, respectively.
Note that
\widetilde{V}^{2}
in Theorem
3.3
is excluded from our numerical experiments, since it is a part of
\widetilde{V}^{3}
.
More specifically, we treat the IG-OU case introduced in Example
2.2
,
and compute the relative errors of
\widetilde{V}^{1}_{t}
and
\widetilde{V}^{3}_{t}
to the true option price
V_{t}
defined in (
2.8
), where the relative error of
U_{t}
to
V_{t}
is defined as

\frac{|U_{t}-V_{t}|}{V_{t}}.

For comparison, we shall compute simultaneously the relative errors of
BS(t,X_{t},\Sigma^{2}_{t})
.

Now, we explain how to compute the correction terms (
3.1
) and (
3.2
).
Recall that the Lévy measure of the IG-OU case is given as

\nu(dz)=\frac{\lambda a}{2\sqrt{2\pi}}z^{-\frac{3}{2}}(1+b^{2}z)\exp\left\{-\frac{1}{2}b^{2}z\right\}{\bf 1}_{(0,\infty)}(z)dz,

where
a>0
and
b>0
.
To simplify the notation, we denote

c_{0}:=\frac{\lambda a}{2\sqrt{2\pi}},\ \ \ c_{1}:=\frac{\lambda ab^{2}}{2\sqrt{2\pi}}\ \ \ \mbox{and}\ \ \ c_{2}:=\frac{b^{2}}{2}.

For
\alpha
,
\beta>0
, we define a function
\Gamma(\alpha,\beta)
as

\Gamma(\alpha,\beta):=\int_{\beta}^{\infty}e^{-z}z^{\alpha-1}dz,

which is called the upper incomplete gamma function.
Denoting

\Gamma^{1}(c,\beta):=\int_{\beta}^{\infty}e^{-cz}z^{-\frac{1}{2}}dz=\frac{1}{\sqrt{c}}\Gamma\left(\frac{1}{2},\beta c\right)

and

\Gamma^{3}(c,\beta):=\int_{\beta}^{\infty}e^{-cz}z^{-\frac{3}{2}}dz=\frac{2e^{-\beta c}}{\sqrt{\beta}}-2\sqrt{c}\Gamma\left(\frac{1}{2},\beta c\right)

for
c>0
and
\beta>0
, we have

\nu([u,\infty))=c_{0}\Gamma^{3}(c_{2},u)+c_{1}\Gamma^{1}(c_{2},u)

for any
u>0
. Thus, denoting

z^{0}:=\frac{x-\log K+r\tau_{t}}{|\rho|}\ \ \ \mbox{ and }\ \ \ \overline{z}:=\frac{2\sigma^{2}}{|\rho|},

we can compute (
3.1
) and (
3.2
) as follows:

Step 2.
We show (
4.1
) for the case where
|x-\log K|<2\sigma^{2}
.
To this end, we prove that there is a
C>0
such that

\Bigg|\int_{0}^{z^{0}\vee\overline{z}}{\mathcal{L}}^{z}(t,x,\sigma^{2})\nu(dz)\Bigg|<C,

(4.14)

and

\int_{z^{0}\vee\overline{z}}^{\infty}\left|{\mathcal{L}}^{z}(t,x,\sigma^{2})-\left(Ke^{-r\tau_{t}}\Phi(d^{-})-e^{x_{z}}\Phi(d^{+})\right)\right|\nu(dz)<C\sqrt{\tau_{t}}.

(4.15)

Remark that, although it is enough to see that the left hand side of (
4.15
) is less than some constant
C>0
to prove Theorem
3.2
,
we shall give an evaluation with higher order as above.

We show (
4.15
) firstly.
From the view of (
4.1
), we have only to evaluate

\int_{\overline{z}}^{\infty}\Phi(d^{+}_{\rho z})\nu(dz),

since
\overline{z}\leq z^{0}\vee\overline{z}
.
By the same manner as (
4.1
), we have

\displaystyle\int_{\overline{z}}^{\infty}\Phi(d^{+}_{\rho z})\nu(dz)

\displaystyle=\int_{\overline{z}}^{\infty}\int_{-\infty}^{d^{+}_{\rho z}}\phi(\vartheta)d\vartheta\nu(dz)=\int_{-\infty}^{\overline{d}^{+}}\int_{\overline{z}}^{h^{+}(\vartheta)}\nu(dz)\phi(\vartheta)d\vartheta

\displaystyle\leq C^{\nu}_{0}\gamma(\overline{z})\int_{-\infty}^{\overline{d}^{+}}(h^{+}(\vartheta)-\overline{z})\phi(\vartheta)d\vartheta

\displaystyle=C^{\nu}_{0}\gamma(\overline{z})\int_{-\infty}^{\overline{d}^{+}}\left\{z^{0}-\overline{z}+\frac{1}{|\rho|}\left(\frac{\sigma^{2}\tau_{t}}{2}-\sigma\sqrt{\tau_{t}}\vartheta\right)\right\}\phi(\vartheta)d\vartheta

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C^{\nu}_{0}}{|\rho|}\gamma(\overline{z})\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\left(r\tau_{t}+\frac{\sigma^{2}\tau_{t}}{2}+\sigma\sqrt{\tau_{t}}|\vartheta|\right)\phi(\vartheta)d\vartheta

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C^{\nu}_{0}}{|\rho|}\gamma(\overline{z})\left(r\tau_{t}+\frac{\sigma^{2}\tau_{t}}{2}+\sigma\sqrt{\frac{2\tau_{t}}{\pi}}\right),

(4.16)

where

\overline{d}^{+}:=d^{+}_{\rho\overline{z}}=\frac{x-\log K+r\tau_{t}+\rho\overline{z}+\frac{\sigma^{2}}{2}\tau_{t}}{\sigma\sqrt{\tau_{t}}}.

Note that the second inequality in (
4.1
) is derived from the fact that
z^{0}-\overline{z}\leq r\tau_{t}/|\rho|
holds.
As a consequence, we obtain

\int_{z^{0}\vee\overline{z}}^{\infty}\left|{\mathcal{L}}^{z}(t,x,\sigma^{2})-\left(Ke^{-r\tau_{t}}\Phi(d^{-})-e^{x_{z}}\Phi(d^{+})\right)\right|\nu(dz)\leq(e^{x}+K)\frac{C^{\nu}_{0}}{|\rho|}\gamma(\overline{z})\left(r\tau_{t}+\frac{\sigma^{2}\tau_{t}}{2}+\sigma\sqrt{\frac{2\tau_{t}}{\pi}}\right),

from which (
4.15
) follows.

Next, we aim to see (
4.14
), whose left hand side is decomposed as follows:

\displaystyle\Bigg|\int_{0}^{z^{0}\vee\overline{z}}{\mathcal{L}}^{z}(t,x,\sigma^{2})\nu(dz)\Bigg|

\displaystyle=\Bigg|\int_{0}^{z^{0}\vee\overline{z}}\left\{e^{x_{z}}(\Phi(d^{+})-\Phi(d^{+}_{\rho z}))-Ke^{-r\tau_{t}}(\Phi(d^{-})-\Phi(d^{-}_{\rho z}))\right\}\nu(dz)\Bigg|

\displaystyle\leq e^{x}\int_{0}^{z^{0}\vee\overline{z}}(1-e^{\rho z})(\Phi(d^{+})-\Phi(d^{+}_{\rho z}))\nu(dz)+\left|e^{x}-Ke^{-r\tau_{t}}\right|\int_{0}^{z^{0}\vee\overline{z}}(\Phi(d^{+})-\Phi(d^{+}_{\rho z}))\nu(dz)

\displaystyle\hskip 14.22636pt+Ke^{-r\tau_{t}}\left|\int_{0}^{z^{0}\vee\overline{z}}\left\{(\Phi(d^{+})-\Phi(d^{+}_{\rho z}))-(\Phi(d^{-})-\Phi(d^{-}_{\rho z}))\right\}\nu(dz)\right|.

(4.17)

Note that the first term of (
4.1
) is bounded, since

\int_{0}^{z^{0}\vee\overline{z}}(1-e^{\rho z})(\Phi(d^{+})-\Phi(d^{+}_{\rho z}))\nu(dz)\leq\int_{0}^{z^{0}\vee\overline{z}}(1-e^{\rho z})\nu(dz)\leq\int_{0}^{\infty}(1-e^{\rho z})\nu(dz).

In addition, the last term of (
4.1
) is evaluated as

\displaystyle\left|\int_{0}^{z^{0}\vee\overline{z}}\left\{(\Phi(d^{+})-\Phi(d^{+}_{\rho z}))-(\Phi(d^{-})-\Phi(d^{-}_{\rho z}))\right\}\nu(dz)\right|

\displaystyle=\left|\int_{0}^{z^{0}\vee\overline{z}}\left\{(\Phi(d^{+})-\Phi(d^{-}))-(\Phi(d^{+}_{\rho z})-\Phi(d^{-}_{\rho z}))\right\}\nu(dz)\right|

\displaystyle=\left|\int_{0}^{z^{0}\vee\overline{z}}\left\{\int_{d^{-}}^{d^{+}}\phi(\vartheta)d\vartheta-\int_{d^{-}_{\rho z}}^{d^{+}_{\rho z}}\phi(\vartheta)d\vartheta\right\}\nu(dz)\right|

\displaystyle=\left|\int_{0}^{z^{0}\vee\overline{z}}\int_{d^{-}}^{d^{+}}\left\{\phi(\vartheta)-\phi\left(\vartheta+\frac{\rho z}{\sigma\sqrt{\tau_{t}}}\right)\right\}d\vartheta\nu(dz)\right|

\displaystyle=\left|\int_{0}^{z^{0}\vee\overline{z}}\int_{d^{-}}^{d^{+}}\int_{\vartheta+\frac{\rho z}{\sigma\sqrt{\tau_{t}}}}^{\vartheta}\phi^{\prime}(\zeta)d\zeta d\vartheta\nu(dz)\right|\leq\int_{0}^{z^{0}\vee\overline{z}}\int_{d^{-}}^{d^{+}}\frac{|\rho|z}{\sqrt{2\pi e}\sigma\sqrt{\tau_{t}}}d\vartheta\nu(dz)

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{z^{0}\vee\overline{z}}\frac{|\rho|z}{\sqrt{2\pi e}}\nu(dz)\leq\frac{|\rho|}{\sqrt{2\pi e}}\int_{0}^{\infty}z\nu(dz).

Note that the first inequality is derived from that
|\phi^{\prime}(\zeta)|\leq\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi e}}
for any
\zeta\in{\mathbb{R}}
.

The second term of (
4.1
) remains to be evaluated.
First of all, we calculate

(e^{x}-Ke^{-r\tau_{t}})\int_{0}^{\frac{z^{0}}{2}}(\Phi(d^{+})-\Phi(d^{+}_{\rho z}))\nu(dz)

for the case where
x-\log K+r\tau_{t}>0
.
For any
z\in(0,\frac{z^{0}}{2}]
, we have

d^{+}_{\rho z}=\frac{x-\log K+r\tau_{t}+\rho z+\frac{1}{2}\sigma^{2}\tau_{t}}{\sigma\sqrt{\tau_{t}}}\geq\frac{x-\log K+r\tau_{t}+\sigma^{2}\tau_{t}}{2\sigma\sqrt{\tau_{t}}}>0,

which implies that

\Phi(d^{+})-\Phi(d^{+}_{\rho z})\leq\phi(d^{+}_{\rho z})(d^{+}-d^{+}_{\rho z})=\phi(d^{+}_{\rho z})\frac{|\rho|z}{\sigma\sqrt{\tau_{t}}}.

Now, since
ue^{-u^{2}}\leq\frac{1}{\sqrt{2e}}
holds for any
u>0
, we have

\displaystyle\frac{\phi(d^{+}_{\rho z})}{\sigma\sqrt{\tau_{t}}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}\sigma\sqrt{\tau_{t}}}\exp\left\{-\frac{(x-\log K+r\tau_{t}+\rho z+\frac{\sigma^{2}}{2}\tau_{t})^{2}}{2\sigma^{2}\tau_{t}}\right\}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}\sigma\sqrt{\tau_{t}}}\exp\left\{-\frac{\left(\frac{x-\log K+r\tau_{t}}{2}\right)^{2}}{2\sigma^{2}\tau_{t}}\right\}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}\sigma\sqrt{\tau_{t}}}\frac{2\sqrt{2}\sigma\sqrt{\tau_{t}}}{\sqrt{2e}(x-\log K+r\tau_{t})}\leq\sqrt{\frac{2}{\pi e}}\frac{1}{x-\log K+r\tau_{t}}

for any
z\in(0,\frac{z^{0}}{2}]
.
On the other hand, denoting
y:=x-\log K+r\tau_{t}<2\sigma^{2}+r\tau_{t}
, we have

\frac{e^{x}-Ke^{-r\tau_{t}}}{x-\log K+r\tau_{t}}=Ke^{-r\tau_{t}}\frac{e^{y}-1}{y}\leq Ke^{-r\tau_{t}}\frac{\exp\{2\sigma^{2}+r\tau_{t}\}-1}{2\sigma^{2}+r\tau_{t}}\leq\frac{Ke^{2\sigma^{2}}}{2\sigma^{2}}.

(4.18)

As a result, we obtain

\displaystyle(e^{x}-Ke^{-r\tau_{t}})\int_{0}^{\frac{z^{0}}{2}}(\Phi(d^{+})-\Phi(d^{+}_{\rho z}))\nu(dz)

\displaystyle\leq(e^{x}-Ke^{-r\tau_{t}})\int_{0}^{\frac{z^{0}}{2}}\frac{\phi(d^{\pm}_{\rho z})}{\sigma\sqrt{\tau_{t}}}|\rho|z\nu(dz)\leq\frac{e^{x}-Ke^{-r\tau_{t}}}{x-\log K+r\tau_{t}}\sqrt{\frac{2}{\pi e}}|\rho|\int_{0}^{\infty}z\nu(dz)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{Ke^{2\sigma^{2}}}{2\sigma^{2}}\sqrt{\frac{2}{\pi e}}|\rho|\int_{0}^{\infty}z\nu(dz).

(4.19)

Next, we evaluate

(e^{x}-Ke^{-r\tau_{t}})\int_{\frac{z^{0}}{2}}^{z^{0}\vee\overline{z}}(\Phi(d^{+})-\Phi(d^{+}_{\rho z}))\nu(dz)

(4.20)

for the case where
x-\log K+r\tau_{t}>0
.
Since
0<\Phi(d^{+})-\Phi(d^{+}_{\rho z})<1
, it suffices to evaluate
\nu([\frac{z^{0}}{2},z^{0}\vee\overline{z}])
.
When
\frac{z^{0}}{2}<1
, Assumption
2.3
ensures that

\displaystyle\nu\left(\left[\frac{z^{0}}{2},z^{0}\vee\overline{z}\right]\right)

\displaystyle\leq C^{\nu}_{0}\left\{\int_{\frac{z^{0}}{2}}^{1\wedge(z^{0}\vee\overline{z})}z^{-\frac{3}{2}}dz+\int_{1\wedge(z^{0}\vee\overline{z})}^{z^{0}\vee\overline{z}}z^{-\frac{1}{2}}dz\right\}

\displaystyle=C^{\nu}_{0}\left\{\frac{2\sqrt{2|\rho|}}{\sqrt{x-\log K+r\tau_{t}}}-\frac{2}{\sqrt{1\wedge(z^{0}\vee\overline{z})}}+2\sqrt{z^{0}\vee\overline{z}}-2\sqrt{1\wedge(z^{0}\vee\overline{z})}\right\}

\displaystyle\leq C^{\nu}_{0}\left\{\frac{2\sqrt{2|\rho|}}{\sqrt{x-\log K+r\tau_{t}}}+2\sqrt{z^{0}\vee\overline{z}}\right\}.

We have then

\displaystyle(e^{x}-Ke^{-r\tau_{t}})\int_{\frac{z^{0}}{2}}^{z^{0}\vee\overline{z}}(\Phi(d^{+})-\Phi(d^{+}_{\rho z}))\nu(dz)

\displaystyle\leq C^{\nu}_{0}(e^{x}-Ke^{-r\tau_{t}})\left\{\frac{2\sqrt{2|\rho|}}{\sqrt{x-\log K+r\tau_{t}}}+2\sqrt{z^{0}\vee\overline{z}}\right\}

\displaystyle\leq C^{\nu}_{0}Ke^{2\sigma^{2}}\left\{\frac{2\sqrt{2|\rho|}}{\sqrt{2\sigma^{2}+r\tau_{t}}}+2\sqrt{z^{0}\vee\overline{z}}\right\}\leq 2C^{\nu}_{0}Ke^{2\sigma^{2}}\left\{\frac{\sqrt{|\rho|}}{\sigma}+\sqrt{\frac{2\sigma^{2}+r}{|\rho|}}\right\},

(4.21)

since
z^{0}\vee\overline{z}\leq(2\sigma^{2}+r)/|\rho|
holds, and similar calculations to (
4.18
) provide

\frac{e^{x}-Ke^{-r\tau_{t}}}{\sqrt{x-\log K+r\tau_{t}}}\leq\frac{Ke^{2\sigma^{2}}}{\sqrt{2\sigma^{2}+r\tau_{t}}}\ \ \ \mbox{ and }\ \ \ e^{x}-Ke^{-r\tau_{t}}\leq Ke^{2\sigma^{2}}.

(4.22)

Moreover, when
\frac{z^{0}}{2}\geq 1
, we have

\nu\left(\left[\frac{z^{0}}{2},z^{0}\vee\overline{z}\right]\right)\leq C^{\nu}_{0}\int_{\frac{z^{0}}{2}}^{z^{0}\vee\overline{z}}z^{-\frac{1}{2}}dz\leq 2C^{\nu}_{0}\sqrt{z^{0}\vee\overline{z}}.

By using (
4.22
), we have

(e^{x}-Ke^{-r\tau_{t}})\int_{\frac{z^{0}}{2}}^{z^{0}\vee\overline{z}}(\Phi(d^{+})-\Phi(d^{+}_{\rho z}))\nu(dz)\leq 2C^{\nu}_{0}Ke^{2\sigma^{2}}\sqrt{\frac{2\sigma^{2}+r}{|\rho|}}.

(4.23)

From (
4.1
) and (
4.23
), we can find a constant
C>0
such that (
4.20
) is less than
C
.
Together with the result of (
4.1
), the second term of (
4.1
) is bounded when
x-\log K+r\tau_{t}>0
.

As the last step to evaluate the second term of (
4.1
), we treat the case where
x-\log K+r\tau_{t}\leq 0
and
x-\log K>-2\sigma^{2}
.
In this case,
z^{0}
takes a nonpositive value, so that
z^{0}\vee\overline{z}=\overline{z}
holds.
The following shows that
\phi(d^{+}_{\rho z})
is bounded as a function on
z\in(0,\overline{z}]
:
For any
z\in(0,\overline{z}]
, we have

\displaystyle\phi(d^{+}_{\rho z})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\exp\left\{-\frac{(x-\log K+r\tau_{t}+\rho z+\frac{\sigma^{2}}{2}\tau_{t})^{2}}{2\sigma^{2}\tau_{t}}\right\}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\exp\left\{-\frac{(x-\log K+r\tau_{t}+\rho z)^{2}}{2\sigma^{2}\tau_{t}}-\frac{x-\log K+r\tau_{t}+\rho z}{2}-\frac{\sigma^{2}\tau_{t}}{8}\right\}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\exp\left\{-\frac{(x-\log K+r\tau_{t})^{2}}{2\sigma^{2}\tau_{t}}\right\}\exp\left\{\frac{|x-\log K|+|\rho|\overline{z}}{2}\right\}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\exp\left\{-\frac{(x-\log K+r\tau_{t})^{2}}{2\sigma^{2}\tau_{t}}\right\}e^{2\sigma^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\frac{1}{\sqrt{2e}}\frac{\sqrt{2}e^{2\sigma^{2}}\sigma\sqrt{\tau_{t}}}{|x-\log K+r\tau_{t}|}=\frac{e^{2\sigma^{2}}\sigma\sqrt{\tau_{t}}}{\sqrt{2\pi e}|x-\log K+r\tau_{t}|},

since
|x-\log K|<2\sigma^{2}
and
ue^{-u^{2}}\leq\frac{1}{\sqrt{2e}}
for any
u>0
.
Thus, we obtain

\displaystyle(Ke^{-r\tau_{t}}-e^{x})\int_{0}^{\overline{z}}(\Phi(d^{+})-\Phi(d^{+}_{\rho z}))\nu(dz)

\displaystyle=(Ke^{-r\tau_{t}}-e^{x})\int_{0}^{\overline{z}}\int^{d^{+}}_{d^{+}_{\rho z}}\phi(\vartheta)d\vartheta\nu(dz)

\displaystyle\leq(Ke^{-r\tau_{t}}-e^{x})\frac{e^{2\sigma^{2}}\sigma\sqrt{\tau_{t}}}{\sqrt{2\pi e}|x-\log K+r\tau_{t}|}\int_{0}^{\overline{z}}\frac{|\rho|z}{\sigma\sqrt{\tau_{t}}}\nu(dz)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{e^{2\sigma^{2}}}{\sqrt{2\pi e}}\frac{e^{2\sigma^{2}+x}}{2\sigma^{2}}|\rho|\int_{0}^{\infty}z\nu(dz)=\frac{e^{4\sigma^{2}+x}|\rho|}{2\sqrt{2\pi e}\sigma^{2}}\int_{0}^{\infty}z\nu(dz).

(4.24)

Remark that the same sort of argument as (
4.18
) implies

\frac{Ke^{-r\tau_{t}}-e^{x}}{\log K-r\tau_{t}-x}\leq\frac{e^{2\sigma^{2}+x}}{2\sigma^{2}}

when
x-\log K+r\tau_{t}\leq 0
and
x-\log K>-2\sigma^{2}
.
Hence, (
4.1
) provides the boundedness of the second term of (
4.1
) as a function on
\tau_{t}
in this case.

Consequently, (
4.14
) holds when
|x-\log K|<2\sigma^{2}
, from which (
4.1
) follows for the case where
|x-\log K|<2\sigma^{2}
.
Together with Step 1, the proof of Theorem
3.2
is completed.

\Box

4.2
Proof of Theorem
3.3

From the views of Proposition
4.1
and Step 1 in the proof of Theorem
3.2
, it is enough to see that

\Bigg|\int_{z^{0}}^{\infty}\left\{{\mathcal{L}}^{z}(t,x,\sigma^{2})-\left(Ke^{-r\tau_{t}}-e^{x_{z}}\right)\right\}\nu(dz)\Bigg|\leq C\tau_{t}^{\frac{1}{2}}

(4.25)

for some
C>0
when
x-\log K\geq 2\sigma^{2}
.
Note that

1-\Phi(\vartheta)\leq\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}\vartheta}

holds true for any
\vartheta>0
, and

d^{\pm}\geq\frac{2\sigma^{2}\pm\frac{\sigma^{2}}{2}\tau_{t}}{\sigma\sqrt{\tau_{t}}}\geq\frac{3\sigma}{2\sqrt{\tau_{t}}},

since
x-\log K\geq 2\sigma^{2}
.
Thus, we have

1-\Phi(d^{\pm})\leq\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}d^{\pm}}\leq\sqrt{\frac{2}{\pi}}\frac{\sqrt{\tau_{t}}}{3\sigma},

which provides that

\displaystyle\int_{z^{0}}^{\infty}|{\mathcal{L}}^{z}(t,x,\sigma^{2})-(Ke^{-r\tau_{t}}-e^{x_{z}})|\nu(dz)

\displaystyle=\int_{z^{0}}^{\infty}\left|e^{x_{z}}\left(\Phi(d^{+}_{\rho z})-\Phi(d^{+})+1\right)-Ke^{-r\tau_{u}}\left(\Phi(d^{-}_{\rho z})-\Phi(d^{-})+1\right)\right|\nu(dz)

\displaystyle\leq\int_{z^{0}}^{\infty}\left\{e^{x}\Phi(d^{+}_{\rho z})+(e^{x}+K)\sqrt{\frac{2}{\pi}}\frac{\sqrt{\tau_{t}}}{3\sigma}+K\Phi(d^{-}_{\rho z})\right\}\nu(dz)

\displaystyle\leq(e^{x}+K)\int_{z^{0}}^{\infty}\Phi(d^{+}_{\rho z})\nu(dz)+(e^{x}+K)\sqrt{\frac{2}{\pi}}\frac{\sqrt{\tau_{t}}}{3\sigma}\nu([\overline{z},\infty))

\displaystyle\leq(e^{x}+K)\left\{\frac{C^{\nu}_{0}}{|\rho|}\gamma(\overline{z})\left(\frac{\sigma^{2}\tau_{t}}{2}+\sigma\sqrt{\frac{\tau_{t}}{2\pi}}\right)+\sqrt{\frac{2}{\pi}}\frac{\sqrt{\tau_{t}}}{3\sigma}\nu([\overline{z},\infty))\right\}.

(4.26)

Note that the second inequality is due to
\Phi(d^{+}_{\rho z})\geq\Phi(d^{-}_{\rho z})
and
z^{0}>\overline{z}
, and the last inequality is derived from (
4.1
).
In addition,
\nu([\overline{z},\infty))
is evaluated by using (
2.6
) as follows:

\nu([\overline{z},\infty))\leq C^{\nu}_{0}\left\{\int_{\overline{z}}^{1\vee\overline{z}}z^{-\frac{3}{2}}dz+\int_{1\vee\overline{z}}^{\infty}e^{-C^{\nu}_{1}z}dz\right\}\leq C^{\nu}_{0}\left\{\frac{2}{\sqrt{\overline{z}}}+\frac{1}{C^{\nu}_{1}}\right\}=C^{\nu}_{0}\left\{\frac{\sqrt{2|\rho|}}{\sigma}+\frac{1}{C^{\nu}_{1}}\right\}.

Together with (
4.2
), we obtain (
4.25
).
This completes the proof Theorem
3.3
.

\Box

5
Conclusions

Approximate expressions of call option prices for the BNS model have been developed in this paper.
As indicated by numerical results in Subsection 3.2, our approximate expressions are sufficiently effective for ITM and around ATM options with a short maturity.
Developments of approximation methods for implied volatilities and calibration procedures for model parameters are significant problems
as applications of our approximations, but these are left to future works.

Guhan Venkat

Abstract
. We study Stark–Heegner cycles attached to Bianchi modular forms, that is automorphic forms for GL ⁡ ( 2 ) \mathrm{GL}(2) over an imaginary quadratic field F F . The Stark–Heegner cycles are local cohomology classes in the p p -adic Galois representation associated to the Bianchi eigenform. They are conjectured to be the restriction (at a prime p p ) of global cohomology classes in the (semistable) Bloch–Kato Selmer group defined over ring class fields of a relative quadratic extension K / F K/F . In this article, we show that these conjectures hold when the Bianchi eigenform is the base-change of a classical elliptic cuspform.

1.
Introduction

The arithmetic theory of automorphic
L
-functions has been the subject of mathematical research for long. The Birch & Swinnerton–Dyer Conjecture (BSD) and its generalization, the Bloch–Kato Conjecture predict a mysterious relationship between the arithmetic of an automorphic form and the special values of its
L
-function. For instance, if
E/\mathbb{Q}
is an elliptic curve over the field of rational numbers, then BSD predicts that the order of vanishing of the Hasse–Weil
L
-function at
s=1
(analytic rank) equals the Mordell–Weil rank of the group of rational points (algebraic rank), i.e.

\mathrm{ord}_{s=1}L(E,s)=\mathrm{rank}_{\mathbb{Z}}E(\mathbb{Q}).

When the analytic rank is precisely one, the theory of Complex Multiplication via
Heegner points
plays a key role in the celebrated proof of BSD by Gross–Zagier (
[
GZ86
]
) and Kolyvagin (
[
Kol88
]
). In scenarios that go beyond the realms of the theory of Complex Multiplication, Darmon (
[
Dar01
]
) used
p
-adic methods to construct local points in the Mordell–Weil group of the curve, known as
Stark–Heegner points
. These points are conjectured to be global points and satisfy a reciprocity law under Galois automorphisms similar to the ones satisfied by Heegner points.

When
E
is replaced by the Galois representation
\rho_{/K}
attached to the quadratic base–change of a Bianchi modular form (i.e. a modular form over an imaginary quadratic field
F
), then the Bloch–Kato Conjecture predicts that the order of vanishing of its
L
-function at critical values equals the rank of a Selmer group attached to
\rho_{/K}
. Inspired by the ideas of Darmon and generalizing earlier works of Trifković (
[
Tri06
]
) & Rotger–Seveso (
[
RS12
]
), we construct local Selmer classes via
Stark–Heegner cycles
in
[
VW21
]
. We then conjecture these local classes to be global Selmer classes that satisfy a reciprocity isomorphism as in
[
Dar01
]
. The main aim of this article (Theorem
1.4
) is to show that the global rationality conjecture of
[
VW21
]
(Conjecture 6.8) holds when the Bianchi eigenform is the base–change to the imaginary quadratic field
F
of a classical elliptic eigenform. We also record the consequences of Theorem
1.4
towards Trifković’s global rationality conjecture for Stark–Heegner points attached to (modular) elliptic curves defined over imaginary quadratic fields (
[
Tri06
, Conjecture 6]
) in §
1.3
below. In particular, our results fit within Darmon’s conjectural program initiated in
[
Dar01
]
.

2.1.
Bianchi modular forms

Bianchi modular forms are adelic automorphic forms for
\mathrm{GL}_{2}
over the imaginary quadratic field
F
.
We recall here some basic properties of Bianchi modular forms. Let
U
be any open compact subgroup of
\mathrm{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{A}_{F}^{f})
, and for any
k\geq 0
, there exists a finite-dimensional
\mathbb{C}
-vector space
S_{\underline{k}+2}(U)
of
Bianchi cusp forms
of (parallel) weight
k+2
and level
U
, which are functions

\widehat{\mathcal{F}}:\mathrm{GL}_{2}(F)\backslash\mathrm{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{A}_{F})/U\longrightarrow V_{2k+2}(\mathbb{C})

that transform appropriately under the subgroup
\mathbb{C}^{\times}\hskip-1.5pt\cdot\hskip-1.5pt\ \mathrm{SU}_{2}(\mathbb{C})
, and also satisfy suitable harmonicity and growth conditions. We will be chiefly interested in the case where

U=U_{0}(\mathcal{N})=\big\{\left(\begin{smallmatrix}a&b\\
c&d\end{smallmatrix}\right)\in\mathrm{GL}_{2}(\widehat{\mathcal{O}}_{F}):c\equiv 0\hskip 2.0pt(\mathrm{mod}\hskip 2.0pt\mathcal{N})\big\},

where
\mathcal{N}=\mathfrak{p}\mathcal{M}\subset\mathcal{O}_{F}
is square–free and
\mathfrak{p}\nmid\mathcal{M}
as before.

Bianchi modular forms admit an analogue of
q
-expansions (cf.
[
Wil17
, §1.2]
), giving rise to a system of Fourier–Whittaker coefficients
c(I,\widehat{\mathcal{F}})
indexed by the ideals
I\subset\mathscr{D}^{-1}
(where
\mathscr{D}
is the different of
F/\mathbb{Q}
). These can be described as the eigenvalues of Hecke operators. In fact, one can define a family of (commuting) Hecke operators indexed by ideals
\mathfrak{m}\subset\mathcal{O}_{F}
, defined via double coset operators. When
\widehat{\mathcal{F}}
is a normalised Hecke eigenform (i.e.
c(1,\widehat{\mathcal{F}})=1
), then the eigenvalue
\lambda_{\mathfrak{m}}
of the
\mathfrak{m}
-th Hecke operator on
\widehat{\mathcal{F}}
is equal to
c(\mathfrak{m}\mathcal{D}^{-1},\widehat{\mathcal{F}})
(see
[
Hid94
, Cor. 6.2]
).

For
M
any module equipped with an action of the Hecke operators, and
\widehat{\mathcal{F}}
a cuspidal Bianchi eigenform, we denote by
M_{(\widehat{\mathcal{F}})}
for the
\widehat{\mathcal{F}}
-isotypic part of
M
. This is the generalised eigenspace where the Hecke operators act with the same eigenvalues as on
\widehat{\mathcal{F}}
.

For an integer

For an optimal embedding

In particular,

Definition A.1
.

The fundamental class associated to the tori

\widetilde{\eta}\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=\xi\cap\mathbbm{1}_{\widetilde{\mathcal{F}}}\in\textup{H}_{1}(T(\mathcal{O}_{F}),C(\widetilde{\mathcal{F}},\mathbb{Z})).

Further, by Shapiro’s lemma

\textup{H}_{1}(T(\mathcal{O}_{F}),C(\widetilde{\mathcal{F}},\mathbb{Z}))\cong\textup{H}_{1}(T(F),C_{c}(T(\mathbb{A}_{F}^{\infty})/U,\mathbb{Z}))

A.2.2.
Cohomology of arithmetic groups and Eichler–Shimura morphism

Let

Proposition A.2
.

With notation as above, we have an equality of cap products, i.e.

\langle C_{[\Psi]}^{\psi_{K}},\phi_{k}^{\#}\rangle=\mathcal{P}(\psi_{K},\phi_{k}^{\#})

Proof.

Note that it suffices to show that the two fundamental homology classes viz.
\eta

\widetilde{\eta}

Since

Again, by Shapiro’s Lemma, we may hence consider the class

A.2.3.
A Waldspurger type formula

We can now prove Theorem
5.10
in the main text which relates the cap products considered above to the central
L
-value of the quadratic base change of Bianchi newforms.

Theorem A.3
.

Let
\psi_{K}:\mathrm{Gal}(H_{K}/K)\rightarrow\mathbb{C}^{\times}
be an unramified character as above. Then

\mathbb{L}(\mathcal{F}_{k}^{\#},\psi_{K})=L^{\mathrm{alg}}(\mathcal{F}_{k}^{\#}/K,\psi_{K},k/2+1)\in E_{k}(\psi_{K})

where

(A.6)

L^{\mathrm{alg}}(\mathcal{F}_{k}^{\#}/K,\psi_{K},k/2+1)\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=\frac{Tu_{K}^{2}}{(\Omega_{k}^{\#})^{2}}\times\frac{\prod_{\nu\in\Sigma_{\infty}^{F}}C^{\prime}_{\nu}(K,\pi_{k},\psi_{K})}{\sqrt{\mathrm{N}_{F/\mathbb{Q}}(\mathcal{D}_{K/F})}}\cdot\frac{((k/2)!)^{4}}{(2\pi)^{2k+4}}L(\mathcal{F}_{k}^{\#}/K,\psi_{K},k/2+1)

where
u_{K}\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=\left[\mu(\mathcal{O}_{K}):\mu(\mathcal{O}_{F})\right]
and
T
and
C^{\prime}_{\nu}(K,\pi_{k},\psi_{K})
are some explicit constants.

Proof.

The main ingredient in the proof is Molina’s Waldspurger formula in higher cohomology. More precisely, it is shown in
[
Mol22
, Theorem 4.6]
that

\mathcal{P}(\psi_{K},\phi_{k}^{\#})^{2}=\frac{u_{K}^{2}}{(\Omega_{k}^{\#})^{2}}\cdot L(1/2,\pi_{k}\times\pi_{\psi_{K}})\cdot\prod_{\nu\nmid\infty}\alpha_{\pi_{k,\nu},\psi_{K,\nu}}(\phi_{k}^{\#})

where
\pi_{k}
is the automorphic representation of
G(\mathbb{A}_{F})
that the newform
\mathcal{F}_{k}^{\#}\in S_{\underline{k}+2}(U_{0}(\mathcal{M}))
generates,
\Omega_{k}^{\#}\in\mathbb{C}^{\times}
is the complex period of (
4.9
) and
\alpha_{\pi_{k,\nu},\psi_{K,\nu}}(\phi_{k}^{\#})
are explicit local factors (almost all one). Since
\phi_{k}^{\#}
is the modular symbol associated to the newform
\mathcal{F}_{k}^{\#}
, we may use the explicit Waldspurger formula of
[
MW09
]
given in terms of period integrals of Gross–Prasad test vectors
\phi\in\pi_{k}
. In particular, by
[
MW09
, Theorem 4.2]
, we get that

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}(\psi_{K},\phi_{k}^{\#})^{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{u_{K}^{2}}{(\Omega_{k}^{\#})^{2}}\times\lvert\int\phi(t)\psi_{K}^{-1}(t)dt\rvert^{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{u_{K}^{2}}{(\Omega_{k}^{\#})^{2}}\times\frac{L^{S_{2}(\pi_{k},K)}(1/2,\pi_{k}\times\pi_{\psi_{K}})\left(\phi,\phi\right)}{\left(\phi_{\pi},\phi_{\pi}\right)}\times\frac{L_{S(\psi_{K})}(1,\epsilon_{K/F})^{2}}{\sqrt{\mathrm{N}_{F/\mathbb{Q}}(\mathcal{D}_{K/F})}}

\displaystyle\times\frac{L_{S_{1}(\pi_{k},K)}(1,\epsilon_{K/F})}{L_{S_{1}(\pi_{k},K)}(1,1_{F})}\times\prod\limits_{\nu\in S_{2}(\pi_{k},K)}C^{\prime}(\pi_{k,\nu})\times\prod\limits_{\nu\in\Sigma_{\infty}^{F}}C^{\prime}_{\nu}(K,\pi_{k},\psi_{K}).

where

•

\phi_{\pi}
is a new vector for
\pi_{k}
,

•

S_{1}(\pi,K)=\{\mbox{places of }F\mbox{ where }\pi_{k}\mbox{ ramifies but }K\mbox{ doesn't.}\}

•

S_{2}(\pi,K)=\{\mbox{places of }F\mbox{ where both }\pi_{k}\mbox{ and }K\mbox{ ramify.}\}

•

S(\psi_{K})=\{\mbox{places of }F\mbox{ above which }\psi_{K}\mbox{ ramifies.}\}

•

C^{\prime}(\pi_{k,\nu})
and
C^{\prime}_{\nu}(K,\pi_{k},\psi_{K})
are certain non-archimedean and archimedean constants defined in Section 4.2.1 and 4.2.2 of
[
MW09
]
respectively.

In our setting, note that
S(\psi_{K})
and
S_{2}(\pi,K)
are both empty while
S_{1}(\pi,K)=\{\mathfrak{l}\mid\mathcal{M}\}
. In particular, all places in
S_{1}(\pi,K)
split in
K
under
(SH-Hyp)
. Further, the Gross–Prasad test vector
\phi
can be chosen to be a translate of the new vector
\phi_{\pi}
so that
\left(\phi,\phi\right)=T\left(\phi_{\pi},\phi_{\pi}\right)
for some scalar
T
. We can simplify the above equation as

(A.7)

\mathbb{L}(\mathcal{F}_{k}^{\#},\psi_{K})=\frac{Tu_{K}^{2}}{(\Omega_{k}^{\#})^{2}}\times\frac{\prod_{\nu\in\Sigma_{\infty}^{F}}C^{\prime}_{\nu}(K,\pi_{k},\psi_{K})}{\sqrt{\mathrm{N}_{F/\mathbb{Q}}(\mathcal{D}_{K/F})}}\times L(1/2,\pi_{k}\times\pi_{\psi_{K}}).

Using the relation

L(s,\pi_{k}\times\pi_{\psi_{K}})=\left(\frac{\Gamma(s+(k+1)/2)}{(2\pi)^{s+(k+1)/2}}\right)^{4}L(\mathcal{F}_{k}^{\#}/K,\psi_{K},k/2+1),

we get that

\displaystyle\mathbb{L}(\mathcal{F}_{k}^{\#},\psi_{K})

\displaystyle=\frac{Tu_{K}^{2}}{(\Omega_{k}^{\#})^{2}}\times\frac{\prod_{\nu\in\Sigma_{\infty}^{F}}C^{\prime}_{\nu}(K,\pi_{k},\psi_{K})}{\sqrt{\mathrm{N}_{F/\mathbb{Q}}(\mathcal{D}_{K/F})}}\cdot\frac{((k/2)!)^{4}}{(2\pi)^{2k+4}}\times L(\mathcal{F}_{k}^{\#}/K,\psi_{K},k/2+1)

\displaystyle=L^{\mathrm{alg}}(\mathcal{F}_{k}^{\#}/K,\psi_{K},k/2+1)\in E_{k}(\psi_{K}).

∎

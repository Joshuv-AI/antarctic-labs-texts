Multi-bump standing waves for nonlinear Schrödinger equations with a general nonlinearity: the topological effect of potential wells
Sangdon Jin

Abstract
. In this article, we are interested in multi-bump solutions of the singularly perturbed problem − ϵ 2 ​ Δ ​ v + V ⁡ ( x ) ​ v = f ⁡ ( v ) in ℝ N . -\epsilon^{2}\Delta v+V(x)v=f(v)\ \ \mbox{ in }\ \ \mathbb{R}^{N}. Extending previous results [ 5 , 16 , 43 ] , we prove the existence of multi-bump solutions for an optimal class of nonlinearities f f satisfying the Berestycki-Lions conditions and, notably, also for more general classes of potential wells than those previously studied. We devise two novel topological arguments to deal with general classes of potential wells. Our results prove the existence of multi-bump solutions in which the centers of bumps converge toward potential wells as ϵ → 0 \epsilon\rightarrow 0 . Examples of potential wells include the following: the union of two compact smooth submanifolds of ℝ N \mathbb{R}^{N} where these two submanifolds meet at the origin and an embedded topological submanifold of ℝ N \mathbb{R}^{N} .

1.
Introduction

One of the best-known nonlinear partial differential equations is the nonlinear Schrödinger equation, which appears in a variety of physical contexts including Bose-Einsten condensation
[
40
]
, nonlinear atom optics
[
35
,
36
]
and many others.
This paper is devoted to the study of the standing wave solutions to the nonlinear Schrödinger equation

(1)

i\hbar\frac{\partial\psi}{\partial t}+\frac{\hbar^{2}}{2}\Delta\psi-V(x)\psi+f(\psi)=0,\ \ (t,x)\in\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{N},

where
\hbar
denotes the Planck constant,
i
is the imaginary unit and assume that
f(\exp(i\theta)s)=\exp(i\theta)f(s)
for
s,\theta\in\mathbb{R}
. Considering the standing wave solutions of the form
\psi(x,t)=\exp(-iEt/\hbar)v(x)
,
\psi
is a standing wave solution of (
1
) if and only if the function
v
satisfies

\frac{\hbar^{2}}{2}\Delta v-(V(x)-E)v+f(v)=0\ \mbox{ in }\ \mathbb{R}^{N}.

We examine standing waves of the nonlinear Schrödinger equation (
1
) for small
\hbar>0
, which are referred to as semi-classical states.
As a result, we focus on the following equation

(2)

\begin{cases}\epsilon^{2}\Delta v-V(x)v+f(v)=0&\mbox{in }\mathbb{R}^{N},\\
v(x)>0&\mbox{in }\mathbb{R}^{N},\\
v(x)\rightarrow 0&\mbox{as }|x|\rightarrow\infty.\end{cases}

Throughout the paper, we assume that the potential
V
satisfies

(V1)

V\in C(\mathbb{R}^{N},\mathbb{R})
and
0<V_{0}=\inf_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}}V(x)\leq\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}}V(x)<\infty
;

(V2)

there is a bounded domain
O
such that
m=\inf_{x\in O}V(x)<\inf_{x\in\partial O}V(x)
.

Set
\mathcal{M}\equiv\{x\in O\ |\ V(x)=m\}
. Studying the solutions to (
2
) requires considering a limiting problem

(3)

\Delta u-mu+f(u)=0\mbox{ in }\mathbb{R}^{N}.

Berestycki and Lions showed in their celebrated paper
[
4
]
that the limiting problem (
3
) has a least-energy solution under the following conditions

(f1)

f\in C(\mathbb{R},\mathbb{R})
and
f(0)=\lim_{t\rightarrow 0}\frac{f(t)}{t}=0;

(f2)

there exist
\bar{C}>0
and
p\in(1,\frac{N+2}{N-2})
such that

|f(t)|\leq\bar{C}(1+t^{p})\mbox{ for all }t\geq 0;

(f3)

there exists a
t_{0}>0
such that

F(t_{0})>\frac{1}{2}mt_{0}^{2},\ \ \mbox{ where }\ \ F(t)=\int_{0}^{t}f(s)ds.

The groundbreaking work of Floer and Weinstein
[
23
]
is the first research in this direction. Adopting the Lyapunov-Schmidt method, they showed that as
\varepsilon\rightarrow 0
a family of single peak solutions of (
2
) concentrates around a non-degenerate critical point of
V
. The Lyapunov-Schmidt method requires a linearized non-degeneracy condition, namely, that for a critical point
x_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
of
V
and a positive solution
U_{x_{0}}\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
of a limiting problem
\Delta u-V(x_{0})u+f(u)=0\mbox{ in }\mathbb{R}^{N}
, if
\phi\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
satisfies
\Delta\phi-V(x_{0})\phi+f^{\prime}(U_{x_{0}})\phi=0
in
\mathbb{R}^{N}
, then
\phi=\sum_{i=1}^{N}a_{i}\frac{\partial U_{x_{0}}}{\partial x_{i}}
for some
a_{i}\in\mathbb{R}
. Subsequently, using the Lyapunov-Schmidt method, many authors have obtained more refined results; see
[
1
,
2
,
22
,
30
,
32
,
37
,
38
]
.

In
[
41
]
, Rabinowitz developed a variational approach, which does not require the non-degeneracy condition for limiting problems. Applying the mountain pass theorem, Rabinowitz showed the existence of a least-energy solution of (
2
) for small
\varepsilon>0
when

\liminf_{|x|\rightarrow\infty}V(x)>\inf_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}}V(x).

In
[
42

as
\varepsilon\rightarrow 0
. In
[
18

,
del Pino and Felmer proved the existence of a single peak solution whose maximum point converges to potential wells as
\varepsilon\rightarrow 0
. Other researchers have further developed variational methods for the construction of solutions under an optimal class of nonlinearities

[

8
,

, Byeon, Jeanjean and Tanaka constructed a single peak solution of (

\varepsilon\rightarrow 0
when the nonlinearity

satisfies (f1)-(f3) as well as the following condition

f\in C^{1}(\mathbb{R},\mathbb{R})
and there exists a

\lim_{t\rightarrow 0}\frac{f(t)}{t^{1+q_{0}}}=0.

We refer to

In this paper, we focus on the existence of multi-bump solutions to (

More specifically, we devise two novel topological arguments to investigate the existence of multi-bump solutions of (

where these two submanifolds meet at the origin, which is not a topological manifold; or a topological manifold that does not admit any differentiable structure (see

,

\mathcal{M}

Our new approach enables us to deal with general classes of sets
\mathcal{M}
, which are defined as follows:

(M1)

\mathcal{M}=\{x\in O\ |\ V(x)=m\}
is homeomorphic to a finite
d
-dimensional polyhedron
X
with
H_{d}(X;\mathbb{Z}_{2})\neq 0
for some
d=1,\cdots,N-1;

(M2)

\mathcal{M}=\{x\in O\ |\ V(x)=m\}
is a connected compact
d

\mathbb{R}^{N}
for some
d=1,\cdots,N-1
.

For the reader convenience, we recall the definitions of a simplicial complex, simplicial homology and an embedded topological submanifold. A simplicial complex is a set composed of points, line segments, triangles, tetrahedrons, and their
k
-dimensional equivalents satisfying some compatibilty conditions (see Appendix for the definition of a simplicial complex). If
K
is a simplicial complex then we put
|K|=\bigcup\{\sigma|\sigma\in K\}
. This is called the polyhedron of
K
. We can construct a group structure on
k
-dimensional equivalents of
K
(denoted by
C_{k}(K)
). Then we define the boundary homomorphism on these groups
C_{k}(K)
as follows: specify the value of the homomorphism on each generator of
C_{k}(K)
; then extend linearly to the other elements, known as boundary operators
\partial_{k}:C_{k}(K;\mathbb{Z}_{2})\rightarrow C_{k-1}(K;\mathbb{Z}_{2})
(see Appendix). It is well known that the composition
\partial_{k+1}\circ\partial_{k}
is the zero homomorphism. Then the
k
-th homology group of
|K|
is
H_{k}(|K|)=\ker\partial_{k}/\mbox{im}\partial_{k+1}
. An embedded topological submanifold of
M
is a subset
S\subset M
endowed with a topology such that it is a topological manifold (without boundary) and such that the inclusion is a topological embedding (see
[
31
]
).

The condition that
\mathcal{M}
has nontrivial homology is necessary in the sense that a family of multi-bump solutions cannot exist when
\mathcal{M}
is a point or a ball in
\mathbb{R}^{N}
(see
[
16
,
30
]
). We note that
(M1)
and
(M2)
are independent of each other. In fact,
(M1)
holds for the case
\mathcal{M}=S^{N-1}\cup(S^{N-1}+(0,\cdots,0,2))
, which is not a topological manifold, and for
n\geq 5
there exists a closed topological
n
-manifold that does not admit a simplicial triangulation (see
[
34
]
).

2.4.
Functional setting

The variational framework is the following. We define a norm

on

by

For

and any set

We also denote by

For

, let

It is standard to see that

and the critical points of

are solutions of

\Delta u-V_{\varepsilon}(x)u+f(u)=0\mbox{ in }\mathbb{R}^{N}.

Since we seek positive solutions, without loss of generality, we assume that

for all

. Then we can see from the maximum principle that any nontrivial solution of (

) is positive. We remark that
v(x)=u(x/\varepsilon)
satisfies (

) and we try to find critical points of

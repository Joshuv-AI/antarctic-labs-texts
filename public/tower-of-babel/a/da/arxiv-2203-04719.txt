Weighted GJMS operators on Smooth Metric Measure Spaces
Ayush Khaitan

Abstract
. We construct weighted GJMS operators on smooth metric measure spaces, and prove that they are formally self-adjoint. We also provide factorization formulas for them in the case of quasi-Einstein spaces and under Gover–Leitner conditions.



2010 Mathematics Subject Classification
Primary 53A30; Secondary 53A55, 31C12

1.
Introduction

Graham–Jenne–Mason–Sparling operators, commonly abbreviated as GJMS operators, are an important class of formally self-adjoint
[
17
]
conformally covariant operators
[
16
]
. In this paper, we construct a weighted analogue of these operators for smooth metric measure spaces.

A
smooth metric measure space
is a five-tuple
(M^{d},g,f,m,\mu)
, where
M^{d}
is a Riemannian manifold of dimension
d
,
f
a smooth function defined on
M
,
m\in\mathbb{R}
a dimensional parameter, and
\mu\in\mathbb{R}_{+}
an auxiliary curvature parameter
[
6
,
5
]
. If
m\in\mathbb{N}
, a smooth metric measure space may be thought of as the warped product
(M^{d}\times F^{m}(\mu),g\oplus f^{2}h)
, where
(F^{m}(\mu),h)
is the
m
-dimensional simply connected spaceform of constant curvature
\mu

[
6
]
.

The space of conformal densities of weight
w\in\mathbb{R}
is denoted by
\mathcal{E}[w]
. Also, a pointwise conformal transformation of
(M^{d},g,f,m,\mu)
with respect to a smooth function
\sigma\in C^{\infty}(M)
is the map

(M^{d},g,f,m,\mu)\mapsto(M^{d},e^{2\sigma}g,e^{\sigma}f,m,\mu).

Weighted GJMS operators are known in orders two and four
[
4
]
, and were formally defined by Case and Chang
[
2
]
to study fractional GJMS operators
[
17
]
via a curved analogue of the Caffarelli–Silvestre extension
[
1
,
9
,
8
,
7
]
. In this paper, we give a rigorous definition of the weighted GJMS operators, and develop some of their properties.

The ambient metric is a key tool in defining weighted GJMS operators, and a weighted analogue of the ambient metric has recently been defined by Case and the author
[
3
]
. By adapting the arguments in
[
16
]
, we construct the weighted GJMS operators.

Theorem 1.1
.

If
d+m\notin 2\mathbb{N}
, then for each positive integer
k
there is a conformally invariant operator

L_{2k,\phi}^{m}:\mathcal{E}[-\frac{1}{2}(d+m)+k]\to\mathcal{E}[-\frac{1}{2}(d+m)-k],

with leading term
(\Delta_{\phi})^{k}
. If
d+m\in 2\mathbb{N}
, the same result holds with the restriction
1\leq k\leq\frac{1}{2}(d+m)
.

Graham–Zworski proved the formal self-adjointness of GJMS operators
[
17
]
. Other proofs for formal self-adjointness are also known
[
10
,
12
,
18
]
. In this article, we prove that weighted GJMS operators are formally self-adjoint.

Theorem 1.2
.

L_{2k,\phi}^{m}
is a formally self-adjoint operator.

GJMS operators factor nicely for Einstein metrics
[
13
,
11
]
. Moreover, Case and Chang
[
2
]
have also formally proved a factorization of these operators for products of negatively-curved Einstein manifolds with positively-curved Einstein manifolds, using the explicit ambient metric for such spaces found by Gover and Leitner
[
15
]
. In this paper, we provide factorization formulas of GJMS operators for quasi-Einstein spaces
[
6
]
and under Gover–Leitner conditions
[
15
]
. Before we state our result, we provide the relevant definitions and formulas.

Let
(M^{d},g,f,m,\mu)
be a smooth metric measure space. When
m>0
, we set
\phi:=-m\log f
, so that

dv_{\phi}=e^{-\phi}\,\operatorname{dvol}_{g}.

In terms of
\phi
, it holds that

\displaystyle\text{Ric}_{\phi}^{m}

\displaystyle=\text{Ric}+\nabla^{2}\phi-\frac{1}{m}d\phi\otimes d\phi,

\displaystyle R_{\phi}^{m}

\displaystyle=R+2\Delta\phi-\frac{m+1}{m}|\nabla\phi|^{2}+m(m-1)\mu e^{2\phi/m}.

Recall that
weighted Schouten tensor
P_{\phi}^{m}
and the
weighted Schouten scalar
J_{\phi}^{m}
of
(M^{d},g,f,m,\mu)
are

\displaystyle P_{\phi}^{m}

\displaystyle:=\frac{1}{d+m-2}(\text{Ric}_{\phi}^{m}-J_{\phi}^{m}g),

\displaystyle J_{\phi}^{m}

\displaystyle:=\frac{1}{2(d+m-1)}R_{\phi}^{m}.

A
quasi-Einstein space

[
6
]
is a smooth metric measure space such that for some
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
,

P_{\phi}^{m}=\lambda g,\quad J_{\phi}^{m}=(d+m)\lambda.

In this paper, we prove a factorization formula of the weighted GJMS operator for quasi-Einstein spaces.

Theorem 1.3
.

The weighted GJMS operator

L_{2k,\phi}^{m}:\mathcal{E}\left[-\frac{d+m}{2}+k\right]\rightarrow\mathcal{E}\left[-\frac{d+m}{2}-k\right]

can be factorized as

\prod_{l=0}^{k-1}\left[\Delta_{\phi}+2\lambda\left(-\frac{d+m}{2}+k-2l\right)\left(\frac{d+m}{2}+k-1-2l\right)\right]

for quasi-Einstein spaces.

Weighted Gover–Leitner conditions
are a generalization to
m\notin\mathbb{N}_{0}
of the Gover–Leitner conditions defined in
[
14
]
, and are defined as

f(x)=1\quad\mu=1,\quad(\text{Ric}_{\phi}^{m})_{ij}=(-d+1)g.

In this paper, we also prove a factorization formula of the weighted GJMS operator for smooth metric measure spaces under weighted Gover–Leitner conditions. We thus provide a rigorous proof of the factorization formula constructed for Poincaré-Einstein spaces by Case and Chang
[
2
]
.

Theorem 1.4
.

The weighted GJMS operator

L_{2k,\phi}^{m}:\mathcal{E}\left[-\frac{d+m}{2}+k\right]\rightarrow\mathcal{E}\left[-\frac{d+m}{2}-k\right]

can be factorized as

L_{2k,\phi}^{m}=\prod_{j=0}^{k-1}\left[\Delta+\frac{(2k-4j-d-m)(2-d+m-2k+4l)}{4}\right]

under Gover–Leitner conditions.

This article is organized as follows: in
Section

2
, we discuss some properties of the weighted ambient space and the commutation relations satisfied by the differential operators relevant to this article. In
Section

3
, we derive weighted GJMS operators with the help of the weighted ambient space. In
Section

4
, we show that weighted GJMS operators have a power of the weighted Laplacian as the leading part. In
Section

5
, we derive the factorization formulas for weighted GJMS operators in the case of quasi-Einstein spaces, and also under Gover–Leitner conditions. In
Section

6
, we prove that weighted GJMS operators are formally self-adjoint.

2.
The weighted ambient space

(M^{d},g,f,m,\mu)
, consider the
(d+2)

\mathbb{R}_{+}\times M\times\mathbb{R}
with coordinates

is

(1)

\displaystyle\widetilde{g}

\displaystyle:=t^{2}g_{\rho}+2\rho dt^{2}+2tdtd\rho,

\displaystyle\widetilde{f}

\displaystyle:=tf_{\rho},

such that
\widetilde{\text{Ric}}_{\phi}^{m},\widetilde{F_{\phi}^{m}}=O(\rho^{j})
, where

or
\infty
depending on whether
d+m\in 2\mathbb{N}
or
d+m\notin 2\mathbb{N}
. The existence, and the uniqueness of
(g,f)
up to order
O(\rho^{j})
and of

up to order

[

]
. We denote

as
\widetilde{\mathcal{G}}
,
X^{I}
as the position vector on
\widetilde{\mathcal{G}}
, and
{\left.\kern-1.2pt\widetilde{\mathcal{G}}\right|_{\rho=0}}
as
\mathcal{G}
.

2.1.
Commutation relations

Equation

1
implies that

(2)

\widetilde{\nabla}_{I}X_{J}=\widetilde{g}_{IJ}.

Note that

2
can also be deduced from the fact that

1
is a straight weighted ambient metric (cf.
[
3
]
).
From
Equation

2

\widetilde{\nabla}_{K}\widetilde{\nabla}_{J}X_{I}-\widetilde{\nabla}_{J}\widetilde{\nabla}_{K}X_{I}=0
. Hence,

(3)

\widetilde{R}_{LKJI}X^{L}=0.

Now set

. From
Equation

1
, we conclude that it is a defining function for
\mathcal{G}
. From

2
, we compute that

(4)

\widetilde{\nabla}_{I}Q=2X_{I}.

Let us now define the following operators acting on functions on
\widetilde{\mathcal{G}}
.

\displaystyle\begin{split}x=-\frac{1}{4}Q,\quad y=\widetilde{\Delta}_{\phi},\quad h=X+\frac{1}{2}(d+m+2).\end{split}

Here
\widetilde{\Delta}_{\phi}
is the weighted Laplacian,
\widetilde{\Delta}_{\phi}:=\widetilde{\Delta}-\widetilde{\nabla}\phi
. Note that the degree of homogeneity of
f
with respect to

is
1

X=t\partial t

Theorem 2.1
.

The operators
x,y,z
satisfy the commutation relations

[x,y]=h,\quad[h,x]=2x,\quad[h,y]=-2y.

Proof.

First, we prove that
[x,y]=h
. Let
F
be a function defined on
\widetilde{\mathcal{G}}
. Using
Equation

2
,
Equation

4
and the fact that
\widetilde{g}^{IJ}\widetilde{g}_{IJ}=d+2
, we get

[x,y]F=[X+\frac{1}{2}(d+2)](F)-\frac{1}{4}(\widetilde{\nabla}^{I}\phi\widetilde{\nabla}_{I}Q)F.

As
\widetilde{\nabla}^{I}\phi\nabla_{I}Q=-2\frac{m}{f}\nabla_{X}f
, we have
-\frac{1}{4}(\widetilde{\nabla}^{I}\phi\widetilde{\nabla}_{I}Q)F=\frac{1}{2}mF
. Consequently,
[x,y]=h
.

Second, we prove that
[h,x]=2x
. This is easily seen using
Equation

4
.

Third, we prove that
[h,y]=-2y
. Using
Equation

3
, we conclude that

\displaystyle[X,\widetilde{\Delta}](F)

\displaystyle=-2\widetilde{\Delta}(F),

\displaystyle[\widetilde{\nabla}\phi,X](F)

\displaystyle=\widetilde{\nabla}\phi(F)-X^{I}(\widetilde{\nabla}^{J}\widetilde{\nabla}_{I}\phi)(\widetilde{\nabla}_{J}F).

On commuting
X_{I}
with
\widetilde{\nabla}^{J}
, using
Equation

2
, and noting that
\nabla_{X}\phi=-m
, we get
[\widetilde{\nabla}\phi,X](F)=2\widetilde{\nabla}\phi(F)
. Hence,
[h,y]=-2y
.∎

Using an induction argument, we get the following commutation relations (cf.
[
16
]

(5a)

\displaystyle\begin{split}[y^{k},x]&=-ky^{k-1}(h-k+1),\end{split}

(5b)

\displaystyle\begin{split}[x^{k},y]&=kx^{k-1}(h+k-1),\end{split}

(5c)

\displaystyle\begin{split}h^{k-1}x^{k-1}&=(-1)^{k-1}(k-1)!h(h+1)\dots(h+k-2)+xZ_{k},\end{split}

for some polynomial

3.
Weighted conformally invariant operators

In this section, we construct two weighted GJMS operators, and then prove that they are the same up to a constant.

In the rest of the paper,
w=-(d+m)/2+k
.

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
k\in\mathbb{N}
and
F\in\mathcal{E}[w]
. Then
{\left.\kern-1.2pt\widetilde{\Delta}_{\phi}^{k}\widetilde{F}\right|_{{\mathcal{G}}}}
is independent of the choice of smooth extension
\widetilde{F}
of
F
to
\mathcal{G}
. Thus,
L_{2k,\phi}^{m}:\mathcal{E}[w]\mapsto\mathcal{E}[w-2k]
, defined as
L_{2k,\phi}^{m}F:={\left.\kern-1.2pt\widetilde{\Delta}_{\phi}^{k}\widetilde{F}\right|_{\mathcal{G}}}
, is a conformally invariant operator.

Proof.

Any two extensions of
F
differ by a function of the form
QH
, where
H\in C^{\infty}(\mathcal{G})
is homogeneous of weight
w-2
. Now by
Equation

5a
, we have

\widetilde{\Delta}_{\phi}^{k}(QH)=Q\widetilde{\Delta}_{\phi}^{k}(H)+4k\widetilde{\Delta}_{\phi}^{k-1}(w+\frac{1}{2}(d+m)-k)H=Q\widetilde{\Delta}_{\phi}^{k}(H).

As
\widetilde{\Delta}_{\phi}
reduces the degree of homogeneity by
2
, it holds that
{\left.\kern-1.2pt\widetilde{\Delta}_{\phi}^{k}(F)\right|_{{\mathcal{G}}}}
belongs to
\mathcal{E}[w-2k]
.
∎

We now study the obstruction to constructing harmonic extensions of smooth conformal densities on
\mathcal{G}
.

Theorem 3.2
.

For
F\in\mathcal{E}[w]
,

(1)

if
k\notin\mathbb{N}
, then
F
has a unique formal harmonic extension to
\widetilde{\mathcal{G}}
, homogeneous of degree
w
;

(2)

if
k\in\mathbb{N}
, then
F
has a homogeneous extension
\widetilde{F}
uniquely determined modulo
O(Q^{k})
by the requirement that
\widetilde{\Delta}_{\phi}\widetilde{F}=0
modulo
O(Q^{k-1})
. The obstruction to a harmonic extension is
{\left.\kern-1.2ptQ^{1-k}\widetilde{\Delta}_{\phi}^{k}\widetilde{F}\right|_{{\mathcal{G}}}}
, which is independent of the extension
\widetilde{F}
mod
Q^{k}
, and hence is conformally invariant.

Proof.

Assume that we have an extension
\widetilde{F}_{l-1}
of
F
such that
\widetilde{\Delta}_{\phi}\widetilde{F}_{l-1}=0\bmod Q^{l-1}
. Now let
\widetilde{F}_{l}=\widetilde{F}_{l-1}+Q^{l}H
, where
H
is of weight
w-2l
. We have

\displaystyle\widetilde{\Delta}_{\phi}\widetilde{F}_{l}

\displaystyle=\widetilde{\Delta}_{\phi}\widetilde{F}_{l-1}+\widetilde{\Delta}_{\phi}(Q^{l}H)

\displaystyle=\widetilde{\Delta}_{\phi}\widetilde{F}_{l-1}+4lQ^{l-1}(k-l)H\bmod Q^{l},

If
k
is not a positive integer, we can choose a unique function
H
for each
l
such that the above expression is
0\bmod Q^{l}
. On the other hand, if
k
is a positive integer, then
\widetilde{\Delta}_{\phi}(\widetilde{F}_{k})=\widetilde{\Delta}_{\phi}(\widetilde{F}_{k-1})\bmod Q^{k}
. Note that
{\left.\kern-1.2ptQ^{1-k}\widetilde{\Delta}_{\phi}(\widetilde{F})\right|_{{\mathcal{G}}}}
depends only on
F
, and is homogeneous of degree
w-2k
.
∎

We now show that the two conformally invariant operators constructed above are scalar multiples of one another.

Theorem 3.3
.

If
k\in\mathbb{N}
, then

(6)

{\left.\kern-1.2pt\widetilde{\Delta}_{\phi}^{k}\widetilde{F}\right|_{{\mathcal{G}}}}=(-4)^{k-1}(k-1)!^{2}{\left.\kern-1.2ptQ^{1-k}\widetilde{\Delta}_{\phi}\widetilde{F}\right|_{{\mathcal{G}}}},

where
\widetilde{F}_{k-1}
is the extension of
F
such that
\widetilde{\Delta}_{\phi}\widetilde{F}_{k-1}=0\bmod Q^{k-1}
.

Proof.

Let
L={\left.\kern-1.2ptQ^{1-k}\widetilde{\Delta}_{\phi}\right|_{{\mathcal{G}}}}
be as in
Theorem

3.2
. Then
\widetilde{\Delta}_{\phi}\widetilde{F}_{k-1}=Q^{k-1}LF\bmod Q^{k}
. Now from
Equation

5c
, we know that

\widetilde{\Delta}_{\phi}^{k}\widetilde{F}_{k-1}=\widetilde{\Delta}_{\phi}^{k-1}(Q^{k-1}LF)=4^{k-1}(k-1)!h(h+1)\dots(h+k-2)LF\bmod Q.

But

. Using this, we verify
Equation

6
.
∎

In the rest of the paper, we shall denote
{\left.\kern-1.2pt\widetilde{\Delta}_{\phi}^{k}\widetilde{F}\right|_{{\mathcal{G}}}}
as

.

The leading order term

Let

. For

, let

be a homogeneous extension of weight

. The weighted Laplacian with respect to the ambient metric measure structure of the form

\widetilde{\Delta}_{\phi}(t^{w}\psi)=t^{w-2}\big[-2\rho\psi^{\prime\prime}+(2w+d+m-2-\rho g^{ij}g^{\prime}_{ij})\psi^{\prime}\\
+\Delta_{\phi}\psi+\frac{1}{2}w\psi g^{ij}g^{\prime}_{ij}+\frac{m}{f}f^{\prime}(w\psi-2\rho\psi^{\prime})\big],

where

For

(9)

Let

6.
Formal self-adjointness

We now prove
Theorem

1.2
. To begin, we identify the weighted GJMS operators in terms of the weighted Poincaré space
[
3
]
.

Theorem 6.1
.

Let
(X^{d+1},g_{+},f_{+},m,\mu)
be a weighted Poincaré space
[
3
]
for the smooth metric measure space
(M^{d},g,f,m,\mu)
, and let
v\in\mathcal{C}^{\infty}(M).
Also, let
k\in\mathbb{N}
and
s=(d+m)/2+k
, with
k\leq(d+m)/2
if
d+m\in 2\mathbb{N}
. Then there is a formal solution to the equation

\left((\Delta_{\phi_{+}}){}_{g_{+}}-s(d+m-s)\right)u=\mathcal{O}\left(r^{2k}\log r\right)

of the form

u=r^{\frac{d+m}{2}-k}\left(V+d_{k}L_{2k,\phi}^{m}v\,r^{2k}\log r\right),

where the function
V\in\mathcal{C}^{\infty}(\overline{X})
is uniquely determined by
g,f
and
v
modulo
O\left(r^{2k}\right)
,
{\left.\kern-1.2ptV\right|_{{M}}}=v
, and
d_{k}=[2^{2k-1}k!(k-1)!]^{-1}
.

Proof.

For a chosen
(g,f)
, we may assume without loss of generality
[
3
, Proposition 5.3]
that
(g_{+},f_{+})=(r^{-2}\left(g_{r}+dr^{2}\right),r^{-1}f_{r})
. A straightforward calculation shows that
\left[(\Delta_{\phi_{+}})_{g_{+}}-s(d+m-s)\right]\circ r^{d+m-s}=r^{d+m-s+1}\mathcal{D}_{s}
, where

(11)

\mathcal{D}_{s}=-r\partial_{r}^{2}+\left[2s-d-m-1-r\left(\frac{1}{2}g^{ij}g_{ij}^{\prime}+\frac{m}{f}f^{\prime}\right)\right]\partial_{r}\\
-(d+m-s)\left(\frac{1}{2}g^{ij}g_{ij}^{\prime}+\frac{m}{f}f^{\prime}\right)+r(\Delta_{\phi})_{g_{r}}.

Here
(g_{ij},f)
denotes
(g_{r},f_{r})
with
r
fixed, and
(g_{ij}^{\prime},f^{\prime})=(\partial_{r}g_{ij},\partial_{r}f)
. For
v_{j}\in

\mathcal{C}^{\infty}(M)
and
s=(d+m)/2+k
, one has

\mathcal{D}_{s}\left(v_{j}r^{j}\right)=j(2k-j)v_{j}r^{j-1}+\mathcal{O}\left(r^{j}\right).

Beginning with

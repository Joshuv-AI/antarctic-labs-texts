On Carlier’s inequality
Heinz H. Bauschke, Shambhavi Singh, Xianfu Wang

Abstract
The Fenchel-Young inequality is fundamental in Convex Analysis and Optimization. It states that the difference between certain function values of two vectors and their inner product is nonnegative. Recently, Carlier introduced a very nice sharpening of this inequality, providing a lower bound that depends on a positive parameter. In this note, we expand on Carlier’s inequality in three ways. First, a duality statement is provided. Secondly, we discuss asymptotic behaviour as the underlying parameter approaches zero or infinity. Thirdly, relying on cyclic monotonicity and associated Fitzpatrick functions, we present a lower bound that features an infinite series of squares of norms. Several examples illustrate our results.

1
Introduction

Throughout the paper, we assume that

X
is a real Hilbert space

(1)

with inner product
\left\langle{\cdot},{\cdot}\right\rangle
and induced norm
\|\cdot\|
.
We also assume throughout that

f\colon X\to\mathbb{R}\;\;\text{is convex, lower semicontinuous, and proper,}

(2)

and that

A\colon X\rightrightarrows X\;\;\text{is a maximally monotone operator on $X$.}

(3)

Recall that the
Fenchel conjugate

f^{*}
of
f
is defined by

f^{*}(x^{*})=\sup_{x\in X}(\left\langle{x},{x^{*}}\right\rangle-f(x))
.
The classical Fenchel-Young inequality states that
for
x
and
x^{*}
in
X
, we have

G(x,x^{*}):=G_{f}(x,x^{*}):=f(x)+f^{*}(x^{*})-\left\langle{x},{x^{*}}\right\rangle\geq 0,

(4)

and we have the well known equality characterization

G(x,x^{*})=0\;\;\Leftrightarrow\;\;x^{*}\in\partial f(x).

(5)

(We assume the reader has some basic knowledge of convex analysis and monotone
operator theory as can be found, e.g., in
[
3
]
,
[
11
]
,
[
12
]
,
and
[
13
]
.)
In
[
8
]
, Carlier proved recently the following stunningly
beautiful sharpening
of
Eq.

4
:

f(x)+f^{*}(x^{*})-\left\langle{x},{x^{*}}\right\rangle\geq\frac{\|x-\operatorname{Prox}_{\gamma f}(x+\gamma x^{*})\|^{2}}{\gamma},

(6)

where
\gamma>0
and
\operatorname{Prox}_{\gamma f}
is the proximal mapping of
\gamma f
.
He also discusses applications and connections to optimal transport,
the Brondsted–Rockafellar theorem, and tilted duality.

The aim of this paper is to expand on Carlier’s work in three ways:
(1) duality (see
Theorem

4.5
), (2) asymptotic behaviour (see
Theorem

5.1

and
Corollary

5.2
), and (3) cyclic monotonicity (
Corollary

7.4
).

The remainder of this paper is organized as follows.
In
Section

2
, inequalities are provided based on
the Fitzpatrick function.
Useful identities involving the Minty parametrization are presented in

Section

3
.
Carlier’s inequality and a new duality result are given in
Section

4
.
In
Section

5
, we discuss the behaviour of the right side
of
Eq.

6
when
\gamma\to 0^{+}
and
\gamma\to+\infty
.
Various examples are presented in
Section

6
to illustrate our
results. In
Section

7
, we obtain sharpenings when the underlying
operator
A
is cyclically monotone of an order bigger than
2
.

The notation employed in this paper is fairly standard and follows largely
[
3
]
.

2
The Fitzpatrick function

In this section, we start the approach to Carlier’s result.
Several of the proofs are implicit in Carlier’s work; however, we
include for completeness and the reader’s convenience.
Recall that the
Fitzpatrick function
for the operator
A

(see
Eq.

3
) at
(x,x^{*})\in X\times X
is given by

\displaystyle F_{A}(x,x^{*})

\displaystyle:=\left\langle{x},{x^{*}}\right\rangle-\inf_{(a,a^{*})\in\operatorname{gra}A}\left\langle{x-a},{x^{*}-a^{*}}\right\rangle

(7a)

\displaystyle=\sup_{(a,a^{*})\in\operatorname{gra}A}\big(\left\langle{x},{a^{*}}\right\rangle+\left\langle{a},{x^{*}}\right\rangle-\left\langle{a},{a^{*}}\right\rangle\big);

(7b)

see
[
10
]
for the original paper and also

[
14
]
for various extensions, applications, and further references.
It is known (see
[
10
]
) that

F_{A}(x,x^{*})\geq\left\langle{x},{x^{*}}\right\rangle,\hskip 9.24994pt\text{with equality if and only if\;\;}x^{*}\in Ax.

(8)

The next result will be useful later.

Proposition 2.1
.

Given
x,y,x^{*},y^{*}
in
X
, we have

F_{A}(x,y^{*})+F_{A}(y,x^{*})-\left\langle{x},{x^{*}}\right\rangle-\left\langle{y},{y^{*}}\right\rangle\geq\left\langle{y-x},{x^{*}-y^{*}}\right\rangle,

(9)

and equality holds if and only if
y^{*}\in Ax
and
x^{*}\in Ay
.

Proof
. By
Eq.

8
, we have

F_{A}(x,y^{*})\geq\left\langle{x},{y^{*}}\right\rangle\;\;\text{and}\;\;F_{A}(y,x^{*})\geq\left\langle{y},{x^{*}}\right\rangle;

(10)

moreover, equality holds for both inequalities if and only if

y^{*}\in Ax
and
x^{*}\in Ay
.
Adding the two inequalities in
Eq.

10
, followed by
subtracting
\left\langle{x},{x^{*}}\right\rangle+\left\langle{y},{y^{*}}\right\rangle
from both sides, gives

\displaystyle F_{A}(x,y^{*})+F_{A}(y,x^{*})-\left\langle{x},{x^{*}}\right\rangle-\left\langle{y},{y^{*}}\right\rangle

\displaystyle\geq\left\langle{x},{y^{*}}\right\rangle+\left\langle{y},{x^{*}}\right\rangle-\left\langle{x},{x^{*}}\right\rangle-\left\langle{y},{y^{*}}\right\rangle

(11a)

\displaystyle=\left\langle{y-x},{x^{*}-y^{*}}\right\rangle,

(11b)

as claimed.

\hfill\hskip 9.24994pt\blacksquare

Fact 2.2
.

(Fitzpatrick)

We have

f\oplus f^{*}\geq F_{\partial f}
.

Proof
. This is contained in
[
10
]
; see also the discussion in

[
4
, Section 2]
. For completeness, we include the short proof here.
Let
(a,a^{*})\in\operatorname{gra}\partial f
.
Then

\displaystyle\left\langle{a},{x^{*}}\right\rangle+\left\langle{x},{a^{*}}\right\rangle-\left\langle{a},{a^{*}}\right\rangle

\displaystyle=\big(\left\langle{a},{x^{*}}\right\rangle-f(a)\big)+\big(\left\langle{x},{a^{*}}\right\rangle-f^{*}(a^{*})\big)

(12a)

\displaystyle\leq f^{*}(x^{*})+f(x).

(12b)

The result follows by taking the supremum over
(a,a^{*})\in\operatorname{gra}\partial f
.

\hfill\hskip 9.24994pt\blacksquare

Corollary 2.3
.

(Carlier)

Given
x,y,x^{*},y^{*}
in
X
, we have

G_{f}(x,x^{*})+G_{f}(y,y^{*})\geq\left\langle{y-x},{x^{*}-y^{*}}\right\rangle

(13)

and

G(x,x^{*})+G(y,y^{*})=\left\langle{y-x},{x^{*}-y^{*}}\right\rangle\;\;\Leftrightarrow\;\;\big[y^{*}\in\partial f(x)\text{\;and\;}x^{*}\in\partial f(y)\big].

(14)

Proof
. (See also
[
8
, Section 1]
.)
Using
2.2
and
Proposition

2.1
, we always have

\displaystyle G(x,x^{*})+G(y,y^{*})

\displaystyle=\big(f(x)+f^{*}(x^{*})-\left\langle{x},{x^{*}}\right\rangle\big)+\big(f(y)+f^{*}(y^{*})-\left\langle{y},{y^{*}}\right\rangle\big)

(15a)

\displaystyle=\big(f(x)+f^{*}(y^{*})\big)+\big(f(y)+f^{*}(x^{*})\big)-\left\langle{x},{x^{*}}\right\rangle-\left\langle{y},{y^{*}}\right\rangle

(15b)

\displaystyle\geq F_{\partial f}(x,y^{*})+F_{\partial f}(y,x^{*})-\left\langle{x},{x^{*}}\right\rangle-\left\langle{y},{y^{*}}\right\rangle

(15c)

\displaystyle\geq\left\langle{y-x},{x^{*}-y^{*}}\right\rangle,

(15d)

which is
Eq.

13
.
We now turn to the proof of
Eq.

14
.

“
\Rightarrow
”: In this case, we have equality in
Eq.

15d
.
In turn,
the equality characterization in
Proposition

2.1
yields

y^{*}\in\partial f(x)
and
x^{*}\in\partial f(y)
.

“
\Leftarrow
”:
In this case,

f(x)+f^{*}(y^{*})=\left\langle{x},{y^{*}}\right\rangle
and
f(y)+f^{*}(x^{*})=\left\langle{y},{x^{*}}\right\rangle
.
It follows that

\displaystyle\big(f(x)+f^{*}(y^{*})\big)+\big(f(y)+f^{*}(x^{*})\big)-\left\langle{x},{x^{*}}\right\rangle-\left\langle{y},{y^{*}}\right\rangle

\displaystyle=\left\langle{x},{y^{*}}\right\rangle+\left\langle{y},{x^{*}}\right\rangle-\left\langle{x},{x^{*}}\right\rangle-\left\langle{y},{y^{*}}\right\rangle

(16a)

\displaystyle=\left\langle{y-x},{x^{*}-y^{*}}\right\rangle.

(16b)

Hence the chain of inequalities in
Eq.

15

is actually a chain of equalities and we are done.

\hfill\hskip 9.24994pt\blacksquare

3
Minty parametrization

In this section, we employ the Minty parametrization and derive
results that will be useful later.
Recalling the standing assumption
Eq.

3
, and given
points
x,x^{*}
in
X
and
\gamma>0
, we have the
well known equivalences (see, e.g.,
[
3
, Chapter 23]
):

x^{*}\in Ax\;\;\Leftrightarrow\;\;x+\gamma x^{*}\in x+\gamma Ax\;\;\Leftrightarrow\;\;x=J_{\gamma A}(x+\gamma x^{*}),

(17)

where
J_{\gamma A}=(\operatorname{Id}+\gamma A)^{-1}
is the resolvent of
\gamma A
.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
x,x^{*}
be in
X
, and let
\gamma>0
.
Set

a:=J_{\gamma A}(x+\gamma x^{*})\;\;\text{and}\;\;a^{*}:=\frac{x+\gamma x^{*}-J_{\gamma A}(x+\gamma x^{*})}{\gamma}.

(18)

Then

x+\gamma x^{*}=a+\gamma a^{*},

(19)

(a,a^{*})\in\operatorname{gra}A,

(20)

and

\left\langle{a-x},{x^{*}-a^{*}}\right\rangle=\frac{\|x-J_{\gamma A}(x+\gamma x^{*})\|^{2}}{\gamma}.

(21)

Moreover,

\|(x,x^{*})-(a,a^{*})\|^{2}=\big(1+\tfrac{1}{\gamma^{2}}\big)\|x-J_{\gamma A}(x+\gamma x^{*})\|^{2}

(22)

and

\big\|(x-a)-(x^{*}-a^{*})\big\|^{2}=\big(1+\tfrac{1}{\gamma}\big)^{2}\|x-J_{\gamma A}(x+\gamma x^{*})\|^{2}.

(23)

Finally, we have the equivalences (which may or may not hold)

x^{*}\in Ax\;\;\Leftrightarrow\;\;a=x\;\;\Leftrightarrow\;\;a^{*}=x^{*}\;\;\Leftrightarrow\;\;\left\langle{a-x},{x^{*}-a^{*}}\right\rangle=0.

(24)

Proof
. Clearly,
Eq.

18
implies
Eq.

19
.
By applying the classical Minty parametrization to
\gamma A
,
we have
(a,\gamma a^{*})\in\operatorname{gra}(\gamma A)
and thus
(a,a^{*})\in\operatorname{gra}A
,
i.e.,
Eq.

20
holds.
Note that

x-a=x-J_{\gamma A}(x+\gamma x^{*})

(25)

and

\displaystyle x^{*}-a^{*}

\displaystyle=\frac{\gamma x^{*}-\gamma a^{*}}{\gamma}=\frac{\gamma x^{*}-\big(x+\gamma x^{*}-J_{\gamma A}(x+\gamma x^{*}\big)}{\gamma}

(26a)

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{\gamma}\big(x-J_{\gamma A}(x+\gamma x^{*})\big).

(26b)

Combining
Eq.

25
and
Eq.

26
, we deduce

\displaystyle-\left\langle{x-a},{x^{*}-a^{*}}\right\rangle

\displaystyle=\frac{\|x-J_{\gamma A}(x+\gamma x^{*})\|^{2}}{\gamma},

(27)

which is
Eq.

21
.
Next, using
Eq.

25
and
Eq.

26
again, we obtain

\displaystyle\|(x,x^{*})-(a,a^{*})\|^{2}

\displaystyle=\|x-a\|^{2}+\|x^{*}-a^{*}\|^{2}

(28a)

\displaystyle=\|x-J_{\gamma A}(x+\gamma x^{*})\|^{2}+\frac{\|x-J_{\gamma A}(x+\gamma x^{*})\|^{2}}{\gamma^{2}},

(28b)

which yields
Eq.

22
.
Moreover, using
Eq.

25
,
Eq.

26
, and

Eq.

27
, we deduce that

\displaystyle\big\|(x-a)-(x^{*}-a^{*})\big\|^{2}

\displaystyle=\|x-a\|^{2}+\|x^{*}-a^{*}\|^{2}-2\left\langle{x-a},{x^{*}-a^{*}}\right\rangle

(29a)

\displaystyle=\big(1+\tfrac{1}{\gamma^{2}}+\tfrac{2}{\gamma}\big)\|x-J_{\gamma A}(x+\gamma x^{*})\|^{2},

(29b)

which yields
Eq.

23
.
We now turn to
Eq.

24
.
We rewrite
Eq.

28
as

\|x-a\|^{2}+\|x^{*}-a^{*}\|^{2}=\big(1+\tfrac{1}{\gamma^{2}}\big)\|x-a\|^{2}
.
Hence, invoking also
Eq.

27
, we obtain

\gamma\|x^{*}-a^{*}\|^{2}=\tfrac{1}{\gamma}\|x-a\|^{2}=-\left\langle{x-a},{x^{*}-a^{*}}\right\rangle,

(30)

which yields the equivalences

x=a

\Leftrightarrow

x^{*}=a^{*}

\Leftrightarrow

\left\langle{x-a},{x^{*}-a^{*}}\right\rangle=0
.
If
x=a
, then
(x,x^{*})=(a,a^{*})\in\operatorname{gra}A
by
Eq.

20
.
And if
(x,x^{*})\in\operatorname{gra}A
,
then
Eq.

17
yields
x=a
.
All this proves
Eq.

24
.

\hfill\hskip 9.24994pt\blacksquare

We now record a duality result.

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
x,x^{*}
be in
X
, and let
\gamma>0
.
Then

x^{*}-J_{\gamma^{-1}A^{-1}}(x^{*}+\gamma^{-1}x)=-\gamma^{-1}\big(x-J_{\gamma A}(x+\gamma x^{*})\big);

(31)

consequently,

\frac{\|x^{*}-J_{\gamma^{-1}A^{-1}}(x^{*}+\gamma^{-1}x)\|^{2}}{\gamma^{-1}}=\frac{\|x-J_{\gamma A}(x+\gamma x^{*})\|^{2}}{\gamma}.

(32)

Proof
. Using
[
3
, Proposition 23.20]
, we have

\displaystyle x^{*}-J_{\gamma^{-1}A^{-1}}(x^{*}+\gamma^{-1}x)

\displaystyle=x^{*}-\big(\operatorname{Id}-\gamma^{-1}J_{\gamma A}\circ\gamma\operatorname{Id}\big)(x^{*}+\gamma^{-1}x)

(33a)

\displaystyle=x^{*}-(x^{*}+\gamma^{-1}x)+\gamma^{-1}J_{\gamma A}(x+\gamma x^{*})

(33b)

\displaystyle=-\gamma^{-1}\big(x-J_{\gamma A}(x+\gamma x^{*})\big),

(33c)

which is
Eq.

31
and from which
Eq.

32
follows.

\hfill\hskip 9.24994pt\blacksquare

4
The Carlier bound and duality

This section contains a review of Carlier’s inequality and a new duality result.

Definition 4.1
.

(Carlier bound)

Recall
Eq.

3
, let
x
and
x^{*}
be in
X
, and let
\gamma>0
.
We define the associated
Carlier bound
by

C(x,x^{*}):=C_{A,\gamma}(x,x^{*}):=\frac{\|x-J_{\gamma A}(x+\gamma x^{*})\|^{2}}{\gamma}.

(34)

If
A=\partial f
, then

C(x,x^{*})=C_{\partial f,\gamma}(x,x^{*})=\frac{\|x-\operatorname{Prox}_{\gamma f}(x+\gamma x^{*})\|^{2}}{\gamma}.

(35)

Theorem 4.2
.

(Carlier)

Recall
Eq.

3
, and let
x,x^{*}
be in
X
.
Then

(\forall\gamma>0)\hskip 9.24994ptF_{A}(x,x^{*})-\left\langle{x},{x^{*}}\right\rangle\geq C_{A,\gamma}(x,x^{*}).

(36)

Proof
. (See also
[
8
, Section 2]
.)
Let
\gamma>0
and
set

a:=J_{\gamma A}(x+\gamma x^{*})\;\;\text{and}\;\;a^{*}:=\frac{x+\gamma x^{*}-J_{\gamma A}(x+\gamma x^{*})}{\gamma}.

(37)

By
Eq.

7
and
Eq.

21
, we have

\displaystyle F_{A}(x,x^{*})-\left\langle{x},{x^{*}}\right\rangle

\displaystyle=-\inf_{(b,b^{*})\in\operatorname{gra}A}\left\langle{x-b},{x^{*}-b^{*}}\right\rangle

(38a)

\displaystyle\geq\left\langle{a-x},{x^{*}-a^{*}}\right\rangle

(38b)

\displaystyle=\frac{\|x-J_{\gamma A}(x+\gamma x^{*})\|^{2}}{\gamma}

(38c)

\displaystyle=C_{A,\gamma}(x,x^{*}),

(38d)

as claimed.

\hfill\hskip 9.24994pt\blacksquare

Theorem 4.3
.

(Carlier)

Let
x
and
x^{*}
be in
X
, and let
\gamma>0
.
Then

G_{f}(x,x^{*})\geq F_{\partial f}(x,x^{*})-\left\langle{x},{x^{*}}\right\rangle\geq C_{\partial f,\gamma}(x,x^{*}).

(39)

Moreover, we have the characterization

\displaystyle\hskip-28.45274ptG_{f}(x,x^{*})=C_{\partial f,\gamma}(x,x^{*})\;\;\Leftrightarrow\;\;

(40a)

\displaystyle\big[\operatorname{Prox}_{\gamma f}(x+\gamma x^{*})\in\partial f^{*}(x^{*})\;\;\text{and}\;\;x+\gamma x^{*}-\operatorname{Prox}_{\gamma f}(x+\gamma x^{*})\in\gamma\partial f(x)\big].

(40b)

Proof
. (See also
[
8
, Section 1]
.)
Write
A=\partial f
so that
A^{-1}=\partial f^{*}
.
Now set

a:=J_{\gamma A}(x+\gamma x^{*})\;\;\text{and}\;\;a^{*}:=\frac{x+\gamma x^{*}-J_{\gamma A}(x+\gamma x^{*})}{\gamma}.

(41)

Then
Eq.

39
follows from
2.2
and
Theorem

4.2
.
We now derive this differently in order to characterize equality.
By
Eq.

20
, we have
(a,a^{*})\in\operatorname{gra}A
.
Hence,
Eq.

5
yields

G_{f}(a,a^{*})=0.

(42)

Applying now
Eq.

13
and
Eq.

21
yields

\displaystyle G_{f}(x,x^{*})

\displaystyle=G_{f}(x,x^{*})+G_{f}(a,a^{*})

(43a)

\displaystyle\geq\left\langle{a-x},{x^{*}-a^{*}}\right\rangle

(43b)

\displaystyle=\frac{\|x-J_{\gamma A}(x+\gamma x^{*})\|^{2}}{\gamma}.

(43c)

Moreover, thanks to
Eq.

14
, we have equality characterization

G_{f}(x,x^{*})=\frac{\|x-J_{\gamma A}(x+\gamma x^{*})\|^{2}}{\gamma}\;\;\Leftrightarrow\;\;\big[a^{*}\in Ax\;\text{and}\;x^{*}\in Aa],

(44)

which is precisely
Eq.

40
.

\hfill\hskip 9.24994pt\blacksquare

Remark 4.4
.

In view of
Theorem

4.2
,
the Carlier bound

C_{A,\gamma}(x,x^{*})=\frac{\|x-J_{\gamma A}(x+\gamma x^{*})\|^{2}}{\gamma}

(45)

is always less sharp than the Fitzpatrick function bound

F_{A}(x,x^{*})-\left\langle{x},{x^{*}}\right\rangle;

(46)

however, Carlier’s lower bound is sharper than the trivial lower bound

0
.
Computing Fitzpatrick functions is not an easy task
(see
[
4
]
) — there are many
more examples of prox operators available (see
[
3
]
and

[
5
]
).
See also
[
7
]
and
[
6
]
for recent work on Fitzpatrick functions and related objects.

Let us observe a new duality result, which links the Carlier bound of
A

to that of
A^{-1}
:

Theorem 4.5
.

(duality)

Recall
Eq.

3
, let
x,x^{*}
be in
X
, and let
\gamma>0
.
Then

C_{A,\gamma}(x,x^{*})=C_{A^{-1},\gamma^{-1}}(x^{*},x).

(47)

Proof
. Combine
Eq.

34
with
Eq.

32
.

\hfill\hskip 9.24994pt\blacksquare

We conclude this section by outlining another possible area where
Carlier’s inequality may be useful – Bregman distances!

Remark 4.6
.

(Bregman distance)

Recall
Eq.

2
and that the Bregman distance between
x\in X
and
y\in{\operatorname{int\,dom}}\,f

is defined by

D_{f}(x,y)=f(x)-f(y)-\left\langle{x-y},{\nabla f(y)}\right\rangle.

(48)

Note that

D_{f}(x,y)=G_{f}(x,\nabla f(y))\geq C_{\partial f,\gamma}(x,\nabla f(y))

(49)

by
Theorem

4.3
.
The Bregman distance plays a role, e.g., when analyzing the proximal gradient method (PGM).
If
(y_{n})_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}
is the sequence generated by the PGM and
x
is a solution, then

\sum_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}D_{f}(x,y_{n})<\infty
(see, e.g., the proof of
[
5
, Theorem 10.21]
). It follows
that
D_{f}(x,y_{n})\to 0
and also we learn from
Eq.

49
that

\sum_{{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}}C_{\partial f,\gamma}(x,\nabla f(y_{n}))=\sum_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}\frac{\|x-\operatorname{Prox}_{\gamma f}(x+\gamma\nabla f(y_{n}))\|^{2}}{\gamma}<+\infty.

(50)

This is a prototypical appearance of Carlier’s inequality in the context of the
analysis of algorithms.

5
Asymptotic behaviour

Let us now analyze the behaviour of Carlier’s bound

C_{A,\gamma}(x,x^{*})=\frac{\|x-J_{\gamma A}(x+\gamma x^{*})\|^{2}}{\gamma}

(51)

when
\gamma\to 0^{+}
. Because of
Theorem

4.5
, we also obtain information about the behaviour
when
\gamma\to+\infty
.

Theorem 5.1
.

Recall
Eq.

3
, let
x,x^{*}
be in
X
, and let
\gamma>0
.
Then the following hold:

(i)

If
x\notin\overline{\operatorname{dom}}\,A
, then
\lim_{\gamma\to 0^{+}}C_{A,\gamma}(x,x^{*})=+\infty
.

(ii)

If
x\in\operatorname{dom}A
, then
\lim_{\gamma\to 0^{+}}C_{A,\gamma}(x,x^{*})=0
.

Proof
.
Item

(i)
: Suppose that
x\notin\overline{\operatorname{dom}}\,A
.
Set
\delta:=d_{\overline{\operatorname{dom}}\,A}(x)>0
.
Because
{\operatorname{ran}}\,J_{\gamma A}=\operatorname{dom}(\gamma A)=\operatorname{dom}A
, we estimate

\frac{\|x-J_{\gamma A}(x+\gamma x^{*})\|^{2}}{\gamma}\geq\frac{\delta^{2}}{\gamma}.

(52)

This yields the conclusion.

Item

(ii)
: Suppose that
x\in\operatorname{dom}A
.
Recall that
\sideset{{}^{\gamma}}{}{\operatorname{}}\!Ax=(x-J_{\gamma A}x)/\gamma
by definition of the Yosida approximation.
Because resolvents are nonexpansive, we have

\|J_{\gamma A}x-J_{\gamma A}(x+\gamma x^{*})\|\leq\gamma\|x^{*}\|.

(53)

Clearly,

\displaystyle\|x-J_{\gamma A}(x+\gamma x^{*})\|^{2}

\displaystyle=\|x-J_{\gamma A}x\|^{2}+\|J_{\gamma A}x-J_{\gamma A}(x+\gamma x^{*})\|^{2}

(54a)

\displaystyle\hskip 18.49988pt+2\left\langle{x-J_{\gamma A}x},{J_{\gamma A}x-J_{\gamma A}(x+\gamma x^{*})}\right\rangle

(54b)

and this implies

\displaystyle\frac{\|x-J_{\gamma A}(x+\gamma x^{*})\|^{2}}{\gamma}

\displaystyle=\gamma\|\sideset{{}^{\gamma}}{}{\operatorname{}}\!Ax\|^{2}+\frac{\|J_{\gamma A}x-J_{\gamma A}(x+\gamma x^{*})\|^{2}}{\gamma}

(55a)

\displaystyle\hskip 18.49988pt+2\left\langle{\sideset{{}^{\gamma}}{}{\operatorname{}}\!Ax},{J_{\gamma A}x-J_{\gamma A}(x+\gamma x^{*})}\right\rangle.

(55b)

Because
x\in\operatorname{dom}A
, we learn from
[
3
, Corollary 23.46(i)]
that

\lim_{\gamma\to 0^{+}}\sideset{{}^{\gamma}}{}{\operatorname{}}\!Ax=\sideset{{}^{0}}{}{\operatorname{}}\!Ax=P_{Ax}(0).

(56)

Consider the three summands on the right side of
Eq.

55
.
It suffices to show that each one of them goes to
0
as
\gamma\to 0^{+}
.
First,

\sideset{{}^{\gamma}}{}{\operatorname{}}\!Ax\to P_{Ax}(0)
and thus

\gamma\|\sideset{{}^{\gamma}}{}{\operatorname{}}\!Ax\|^{2}\to 0\|P_{Ax}(0)\|^{2}=0
.
Second,
Eq.

53
yields

0\leq(1/\gamma)\|J_{\gamma A}x-J_{\gamma A}(x+\gamma x^{*})\|^{2}\leq\gamma\|x^{*}\|^{2}\to 0
and therefore

(1/\gamma)\|J_{\gamma A}x-J_{\gamma A}(x+\gamma x^{*})\|^{2}\to 0
.
Thirdly,
Eq.

53
shows that

J_{\gamma A}x-J_{\gamma A}(x+\gamma x^{*})\to 0
.
Combined with
Eq.

56
, we deduce that

2\left\langle{\sideset{{}^{\gamma}}{}{\operatorname{}}\!Ax},{J_{\gamma A}x-J_{\gamma A}(x+\gamma x^{*})}\right\rangle\to 0
.

\hfill\hskip 9.24994pt\blacksquare

Corollary 5.2
.

Recall
Eq.

3
, let
x,x^{*}
be in
X
, and let
\gamma>0
.
Then the following hold:

(i)

If

, then
\lim_{\gamma\to+\infty}C_{A,\gamma}(x,x^{*})=+\infty
.

(ii)

Proof

Theorem

4.5

C_{A,\gamma}(x,x^{*})=C_{A^{-1},\gamma^{-1}}(x^{*},x).

(57)

The result is now clear from

5.1
(applied to

.

\hfill\hskip 9.24994pt\blacksquare

6
Examples

In this section, we collect several examples to illustrate our results.

Example 6.1
.

(indicator of a subspace)

Suppose that
A=N_{U}
, where
U
is a closed linear subspace of
X
.
By
[
4
, Example 3.1]
, the Fitzpatrick bound at
(x,x^{*})\in X\times X
is

F_{N_{U}}(x,x^{*})-\left\langle{x},{x^{*}}\right\rangle=\iota_{U}(x)+\iota_{U^{\perp}}(x^{*})-\left\langle{x},{x^{*}}\right\rangle=\big(\iota_{U}\oplus\iota_{U^{\perp}}\big)(x,x^{*}).

(59)

Let
\gamma>0
. Because
J_{\gamma A}=P_{U}
is linear, we compute
Carlier’s bound via

\displaystyle C_{N_{U},\gamma}(x,x^{*})

\displaystyle=\frac{\|x-P_{U}(x+\gamma x^{*})\|^{2}}{\gamma}=\frac{\|P_{U^{\perp}}x\|^{2}+\gamma^{2}\|P_{U}x^{*}\|^{2}}{\gamma}

(60a)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\gamma}\|P_{U^{\perp}}x\|^{2}+\gamma\|P_{U}x^{*}\|^{2}.

(60b)

Note that
X\times X\times\mathbb{R}_{++}\to\mathbb{R}\colon(x,x^{*},\gamma)\mapsto C_{N_{U},\gamma}(x,x^{*})
is

not
convex; however,

X\times X\to\mathbb{R}\colon(x,x^{*})\mapsto C_{N_{U},\gamma}(x,x^{*})

and

\mathbb{R}_{++}\to\mathbb{R}\colon\gamma\mapsto C_{N_{U},\gamma}(x,x^{*})
are convex.
Let us discuss further

\mathbb{R}_{++}\to\mathbb{R}\colon\gamma\mapsto C_{N_{U},\gamma}(x,x^{*}).

(61)

This function is
(i) strictly increasing if
x\in U
and
x^{*}\notin U^{\perp}
;
(ii) strictly decreasing if
x\notin U
and
x^{*}\in U^{\perp}
;
(iii) first strictly decreasing then strictly increasing if

x\notin U
and
x^{*}\notin U^{\perp}
;
(iv) identically equal to
0
if
x\in U
and
x^{*}\in U^{\perp}
.
Moreover,

\lim_{\gamma\to 0^{+}}C_{N_{U},\gamma}(x,x^{*})=\iota_{U}(x)\;\;\text{and}\;\;\lim_{\gamma\to+\infty}C_{N_{U},\gamma}(x,x^{*})=\iota_{U^{\perp}}(x^{*}).

(62)

It follows that

\sup_{\gamma>0}C_{N_{U},\gamma}(x,x^{*})=\iota_{U}(x)+\iota_{U^{\perp}}(x^{*})

(63)

coincides with the Fitzpatrick bound in this case.

Example 6.2
.

(energy)

Suppose that
f=\tfrac{1}{2}\|\cdot\|^{2}
and hence
\nabla f=\operatorname{Id}
.
By
[
4
, Example 3.10]
, the Fitzpatrick bound at
(x,x^{*})\in X\times X
is

F_{\operatorname{Id}}(x,x^{*})-\left\langle{x},{x^{*}}\right\rangle=\tfrac{1}{4}\|x+x^{*}\|^{2}-\left\langle{x},{x^{*}}\right\rangle=\tfrac{1}{4}\|x-x^{*}\|^{2}.

(64)

Let
\gamma>0
. Then
\operatorname{Prox}_{\gamma f}=(\operatorname{Id}+\gamma\operatorname{Id})^{-1}=(1+\gamma)^{-1}\operatorname{Id}
and hence Carlier’s bound is

\displaystyle C_{\operatorname{Id},\gamma}(x,x^{*})

\displaystyle=\frac{\|x-\operatorname{Prox}_{\gamma f}(x+\gamma x^{*})\|^{2}}{\gamma}=\frac{\|x-(1+\gamma)^{-1}(x+\gamma x^{*})\|^{2}}{\gamma}

(65a)

\displaystyle=\frac{\gamma}{(1+\gamma)^{2}}\|x-x^{*}\|^{2}.

(65b)

If
x\neq x^{*}
, then

\gamma\mapsto C_{\operatorname{Id},\gamma}(x,x^{*})
is strictly concave
on
\left]0,2\right]
, strictly convex on
\left[2,+\infty\right[
,
and its unique global maximizer is
\gamma=1
for which

C_{\operatorname{Id},1}(x,x^{*})=\tfrac{1}{4}\|x-x^{*}\|^{2}=F_{\operatorname{Id}}(x,x^{*})
.

Example 6.3
.

(skew rotator)

Suppose that
X=\mathbb{R}^{2}
and that

A\colon\mathbb{R}^{2}\to\mathbb{R}^{2}\colon(x_{1},x_{2})\mapsto(-x_{2},x_{1})
, the counter-clockwise
rotator by
\pi/2
, which is a skew isometry.
By
[
2
, Proposition 7.4]
,

F_{A}(x,x^{*})=\iota_{\operatorname{gra}A}(x,x^{*})=F_{A}(x,x^{*})-\left\langle{x},{x^{*}}\right\rangle.

(66)

Let
\gamma>0
. Then
J_{\gamma A}=(1+\gamma^{2})^{-1}(\operatorname{Id}-\gamma A)\colon(x_{1},x_{2})\mapsto(1+\gamma^{2})^{-1}(x_{1}+\gamma x_{2},-\gamma x_{1}+x_{2})
.
Thus, after some algebra, we find that Carlier’s bound is

\displaystyle C_{A,\gamma}(x,x^{*})

\displaystyle=\frac{\|x-J_{\gamma A}(x+\gamma x^{*})\|^{2}}{\gamma}

(67)

\displaystyle=\frac{\gamma}{1+\gamma^{2}}\|Ax-x^{*}\|^{2}.

(68)

Therefore, if
Ax\neq x^{*}
, then

\gamma\mapsto C_{A,\gamma}(x,x^{*})
is strictly concave
on
\big]0,\sqrt{3}\big]
, strictly convex on

\textstyle\big[\sqrt{3},+\infty\big[
,
and its unique global maximizer is
\gamma=1
for which

C_{A,1}(x,x^{*})=\tfrac{1}{2}\|Ax-x^{*}\|^{2}
.

We now focus on the case when
X=\mathbb{R}

and thus
A=\partial f
.
For ease of notation, we will use
(x,y)
instead of
(x,x^{*})

as we do elsewhere.

Example 6.4
.

((negative) Burg entropy)

Suppose that
f(x)=-\ln(x)
when
x>0
, and
+\infty
elsewhere,
and let
\gamma>0
.
It is known
(see, e.g.,
[
5
, Example 6.9]
and
[
3
, Example 24.40]
)
that for
z\in\mathbb{R}
,

\operatorname{Prox}_{\gamma f}(z)=\frac{z+\sqrt{z^{2}+4\gamma}}{2}.

(69)

Hence, for
(x,y)\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
,

\displaystyle C_{\partial f,\gamma}(x,y)

\displaystyle=\frac{|x-\operatorname{Prox}_{\gamma f}(x+\gamma y)|^{2}}{\gamma}

(70a)

\displaystyle=\frac{\big(x-\tfrac{1}{2}(x+\gamma y)-\tfrac{1}{2}\sqrt{(x+\gamma y)^{2}+4\gamma}\big)^{2}}{\gamma}

(70b)

\displaystyle=\frac{\big((x-\gamma y)-\sqrt{(x+\gamma y)^{2}+4\gamma}\big)^{2}}{4\gamma};

(70c)

in particular,

\displaystyle C_{\partial f,\gamma}(0,y)

\displaystyle=\frac{\big((0-\gamma y)-\sqrt{(0+\gamma y)^{2}+4\gamma}\big)^{2}}{4\gamma}

(71a)

\displaystyle=\frac{\big(\gamma y+\sqrt{(\gamma y)^{2}+4\gamma}\big)^{2}}{4\gamma}

(71b)

\displaystyle=\frac{\big(\sqrt{\gamma}\sqrt{\gamma}y+\sqrt{\gamma}\sqrt{\gamma y^{2}+4}\big)^{2}}{(2\sqrt{\gamma})^{2}}

(71c)

\displaystyle=\bigg(\frac{\sqrt{\gamma}y+\sqrt{\gamma y^{2}+4}}{2}\bigg)^{2}

(71d)

\displaystyle\to 1\hskip 18.49988pt\text{as $\gamma\to 0^{+}$.}

(71e)

Combining with
Theorem

5.1
, we obtain

\lim_{\gamma\to 0^{+}}C_{\partial f,\gamma}(x,y)=\begin{cases}+\infty,&\text{if $x<0$;}\\
1,&\text{if $x=0$;}\\
0,&\text{if $x>0$}\end{cases}

(72)

which is convex — but not lower semicontinuous — as a function of
x
.

Example 6.5
.

((negative) Boltzmann-Shannon entropy)

Suppose that
f
at
x\in\mathbb{R}
is defined by

f(x)=\begin{cases}+\infty,&\text{if }x<0;\\
0,&\text{if }x=0;\\
x\ln(x)-x,&\text{if }x>0.\end{cases}

(73)

We start by showing that

\operatorname{Prox}_{\gamma f}(x)=\gamma W\left\lparen\frac{1}{\gamma}\exp\Big(\frac{x}{\gamma}\Big)\right\rparen

(74)

where
x\in\mathbb{R}
and
where
W
is the
Lambert W-function
as defined in
[
9
, Equation 1.5]
.
To see that, recall that (see, e.g.,
[
3
, Proposition 24.1]
) the
characterization of the proximal mapping

p=\operatorname{Prox}_{\gamma f}(x)\Leftrightarrow\gamma\nabla f(p)+p=x\Leftrightarrow\gamma\ln(p)+p=x,

(75)

where
x\in\mathbb{R}
and
p>0
.
Hence

\frac{p}{\gamma}\exp\Big(\frac{p}{\gamma}\Big)=\frac{1}{\gamma}\exp\Big(\frac{x}{\gamma}\Big)\Leftrightarrow p=\gamma W\left\lparen\frac{1}{\gamma}\exp\Big(\frac{x}{\gamma}\Big)\right\rparen

(76)

by the very definition of the
W
function, and this verifies
Eq.

74
.
Therefore

\displaystyle C_{\partial f,\gamma}(x,y)

\displaystyle=\frac{|x-\operatorname{Prox}_{\gamma f}(x+\gamma y)|^{2}}{\gamma}

(77a)

\displaystyle=\frac{\left\lparen x-\gamma W\left\lparen\frac{1}{\gamma}\exp\big((x+\gamma y)/\gamma\big)\right\rparen\right\rparen^{2}}{\gamma}.

(77b)

In particular,

\displaystyle C_{\partial f,\gamma}(0,y)

\displaystyle=\gamma\left\lparen W\left\lparen\tfrac{1}{\gamma}\exp(y)\right\rparen\right\rparen^{2}

(78a)

\displaystyle=\frac{\left\lparen W\left\lparen\frac{1}{\gamma}\exp(y)\right\rparen\right\rparen^{2}}{\tfrac{1}{\gamma}}.

(78b)

We wish to take now the limit as
\gamma\to 0^{+}
.
As numerator and denominator tend to
+\infty
, we shall use
L’Hospital’s rule. Using the fact that

W^{\prime}(z)=\frac{1}{(1+W(z))\exp(W(z))},

(79)

we obtain

\lim_{\gamma\to 0^{+}}C_{\partial f,\gamma}(0,y)=\lim_{\gamma\to 0^{+}}\frac{2W\big(\frac{1}{\gamma}\exp(y)\big)\exp(y)}{\exp\Big(W\big(\frac{1}{\gamma}\exp(y)\big)\Big)\left\lparen 1+W\big(\frac{1}{\gamma}\exp(y)\big)\right\rparen}.

(80)

Changing variables via

u=W\big(\frac{1}{\gamma}\exp(y)\big)
,
we finally obtain

\lim_{\gamma\to 0^{+}}C_{\partial f,\gamma}(0,y)=\lim_{u\to+\infty}\frac{2u\exp(y)}{\exp(u)(1+u)}=0.

(81)

Combining this with
Theorem

5.1
gives us

\lim_{\gamma\to 0^{+}}C_{\partial f,\gamma}(x,y)=\begin{cases}+\infty,&\text{if $x<0$;}\\
0,&\text{if $x\geq 0$}.\end{cases}

(82)

Remark 6.6
.

The formulas
Eq.

71
and
Eq.

81
illustrate
that the asymptotic behaviour at boundary points does not seem
to follow a simple pattern and thus warrants further study.

7
Cyclic monotonicity

In this section, we extend the analysis to
n
-cyclically monotone
operators.
Recall that
A
is
n
-cyclically monotone, where
n\in\{2,3,\ldots\}
, if

\left.\begin{array}[]{c}(a_{1},a_{1}^{*})\in\operatorname{gra}A\\
(a_{2},a_{2}^{*})\in\operatorname{gra}A\\
\;\;\vdots\\
(a_{n},a_{n}^{*})\in\operatorname{gra}A\\
a_{n+1}=a_{1}\end{array}\right\}\;\;\Rightarrow\;\;\sum_{k=1}^{n}\left\langle{a_{k+1}-a_{k}},{a_{k}^{*}}\right\rangle\leq 0.

(83)

2
-cyclic monotonicity is just regular monotonicity.
The
Fitzpatrick function of order
n
,
F_{A,n}
,
evaluated at
(x,x^{*})\in X\times X
, is the supremum over

(a_{1},a_{1}^{*}),\ldots,(a_{n-1},a_{n-1}^{*})
in
\operatorname{gra}A
of the expression

\left\langle{x},{x^{*}}\right\rangle+\left\langle{x-a_{n-1}},{a_{n-1}^{*}}\right\rangle+\left\langle{a_{1}-x},{x^{*}}\right\rangle+\sum_{k=1}^{n-2}\left\langle{a_{k+1}-a_{k}},{a_{k}^{*}}\right\rangle.

(84)

As a supremum of continuous affine functions,
the function
F_{A,n}
is convex and lower semicontinuous.
We also set
F_{A,\infty}=\sup_{n\geq 2}F_{A,n}
.

Fact 7.1
.

(See
[
1
, Corollary 2.8]
.)
Suppose that
A
is maximally
n
-cyclically monotone.
Then
F_{A,n}>\left\langle{\cdot},{\cdot}\right\rangle
outside
\operatorname{gra}A
, while
F_{A,n}=\left\langle{\cdot},{\cdot}\right\rangle
on
\operatorname{gra}A
.

We have (see
[
1
, Remark 2.10]
) the ordering

\left\langle{\cdot},{\cdot}\right\rangle\leq F_{A,2}\leq F_{A,3}\leq\cdots\leq F_{A,n}\to F_{A,\infty}.

(85)

Moreover, if
f
is as in
Eq.

2
, then

[
1
, Theorem 3.5]
yields for every
(x,x^{*})\in X\times X

F_{\partial f,\infty}(x,x^{*})=f(x)+f^{*}(x^{*}).

(86)

Computing
F_{A,n}
is nontrivial;
for some concrete examples, see

[
1
, Section 4]
and also
[
2
]
.

We shall need the following identity.

Lemma 7.2
.

Let
(x,x^{*})\in X
, and
let
(a_{1},a_{1}^{*}),\ldots,(a_{n-1},a_{n-1}^{*})
be in
X\times X
.
Then

\displaystyle\left\langle{x-a_{n-1}},{a_{n-1}^{*}}\right\rangle+\left\langle{a_{1}-x},{x^{*}}\right\rangle+\sum_{k=1}^{n-2}\left\langle{a_{k+1}-a_{k}},{a_{k}^{*}}\right\rangle

(87a)

\displaystyle=\left\langle{a_{1}-x},{x^{*}-a_{1}^{*}}\right\rangle+\sum_{k=2}^{n-1}\left\langle{a_{k}-x},{a_{k-1}^{*}-a_{k}^{*}}\right\rangle.

(87b)

Proof
. We prove this by induction on
n\geq 2
.
If
n=2
, then the left side of
Eq.

87
is

\left\langle{x-a_{1}},{a_{1}^{*}}\right\rangle+\left\langle{a_{1}-x},{x^{*}}\right\rangle=\left\langle{a_{1}-x},{x^{*}-a_{1}^{*}}\right\rangle,

(88)

which is also equal to the right side of
Eq.

87
.

Now assume the result is true for some
n\geq 2
.
We will show the result is also true for
n+1
.
Indeed, using the inductive hypothesis in
Eq.

89d
, we have

\displaystyle\hskip-28.45274pt\left\langle{x-a_{n}},{a_{n}^{*}}\right\rangle+\left\langle{a_{1}-x},{x^{*}}\right\rangle+\sum_{k=1}^{n-1}\left\langle{a_{k+1}-a_{k}},{a_{k}^{*}}\right\rangle

(89a)

\displaystyle=\left\langle{x-a_{n}},{a_{n}^{*}}\right\rangle-\left\langle{x-a_{n-1}},{a_{n-1}^{*}}\right\rangle+\left\langle{a_{n}-a_{n-1}},{a_{n-1}^{*}}\right\rangle

(89b)

\displaystyle\hskip 18.49988pt+\left\langle{x-a_{n-1}},{a_{n-1}^{*}}\right\rangle+\left\langle{a_{1}-x},{x^{*}}\right\rangle+\sum_{k=1}^{n-2}\left\langle{a_{k+1}-a_{k}},{a_{k}^{*}}\right\rangle

(89c)

\displaystyle=\left\langle{x-a_{n}},{a_{n}^{*}}\right\rangle-\left\langle{x-a_{n-1}},{a_{n-1}^{*}}\right\rangle+\left\langle{a_{n}-a_{n-1}},{a_{n-1}^{*}}\right\rangle

(89d)

\displaystyle\hskip 18.49988pt+\left\langle{a_{1}-x},{x^{*}-a_{1}^{*}}\right\rangle+\sum_{k=2}^{n-1}\left\langle{a_{k}-x},{a_{k-1}^{*}-a_{k}^{*}}\right\rangle

(89e)

\displaystyle=\left\langle{x-a_{n}},{a_{n}^{*}}\right\rangle-\left\langle{x-a_{n-1}},{a_{n-1}^{*}}\right\rangle+\left\langle{a_{n}-a_{n-1}},{a_{n-1}^{*}}\right\rangle-\left\langle{a_{n}-x},{a_{n-1}^{*}-a_{n}^{*}}\right\rangle

(89f)

\displaystyle\hskip 18.49988pt+\left\langle{a_{1}-x},{x^{*}-a_{1}^{*}}\right\rangle+\sum_{k=2}^{n}\left\langle{a_{k}-x},{a_{k-1}^{*}-a_{k}^{*}}\right\rangle

(89g)

(89h)

and

a_{2}=\cdots=a_{n}=P_{U}x
.
It follows that

\frac{\|x-\operatorname{Prox}_{\gamma_{1}f}(x+\gamma_{1}a_{0}^{*})\|^{2}}{\gamma_{1}}=\frac{\|x-P_{U}(x+\gamma_{1}x^{*})\|^{2}}{\gamma_{1}}=\frac{1}{\gamma_{1}}\|P_{U^{\perp}}x\|^{2}+\gamma_{1}\|P_{U}x^{*}\|^{2}

(100)

and that for every
k\geq 2

\displaystyle\frac{\|x-\operatorname{Prox}_{\gamma_{k}f}(x+\gamma_{k}a_{k-1}^{*})\|^{2}}{\gamma_{k}}

\displaystyle=\frac{\|x-P_{U}(x+\gamma_{k}a_{k-1}^{*})\|^{2}}{\gamma_{k}}=\frac{1}{\gamma_{k}}\|P_{U^{\perp}}x\|^{2}+\gamma_{k}\|P_{U}a_{k-1}^{*}\|^{2}

(101a)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\gamma_{k}}\|P_{U^{\perp}}x\|^{2}.

(101b)

Therefore, the lower bound in
Eq.

95
turns into

\gamma_{1}\|P_{U}x^{*}\|^{2}+\bigg(\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{\gamma_{k}}\bigg)\|P_{U^{\perp}}x\|^{2}

(102)

which is strictly larger than Carlier’s bound whenever
x\notin U
.

Example 7.7
.

Suppose that
f=\tfrac{1}{2}\|\cdot\|^{2}=f^{*}
, let

\gamma>0
and set
\gamma_{n}=\gamma
for all
n\geq 1
.
Then

\operatorname{Prox}_{\gamma_{k}f}=(\operatorname{Id}+\gamma_{k}\operatorname{Id})^{-1}=(1+\gamma)^{-1}\operatorname{Id}
.
Then for every
n\geq 1
, we have

a_{n}=\frac{x+\gamma a_{n-1}^{*}}{1+\gamma}

(103)

and

\displaystyle a_{n}^{*}

\displaystyle=\frac{x+\gamma a_{n-1}^{*}-a_{n}}{\gamma}=\frac{x+\gamma a_{n-1}^{*}-(1+\gamma)^{-1}(x+\gamma a_{n-1}^{*})}{\gamma}

(104a)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{1+\gamma}x+\frac{\gamma}{1+\gamma}a_{n-1}^{*}

(104b)

\displaystyle\;\;\vdots

(104c)

\displaystyle=\bigg(1-\frac{\gamma^{n}}{(1+\gamma)^{n}}\bigg)x+\frac{\gamma^{n}}{(1+\gamma)^{n}}x^{*}.

(104d)

It follows that

\frac{\|x-\operatorname{Prox}_{\gamma_{1}f}(x+\gamma_{1}a_{0}^{*})\|^{2}}{\gamma_{1}}=\frac{\|x-(1+\gamma)^{-1}(x+\gamma x^{*})\|^{2}}{\gamma}=\frac{\gamma}{(1+\gamma)^{2}}\|x-x^{*}\|^{2}

(105)

and that for every
k\geq 2

\displaystyle\frac{\|x-\operatorname{Prox}_{\gamma_{k}f}(x+\gamma_{k}a_{k-1}^{*})\|^{2}}{\gamma_{k}}

\displaystyle=\frac{\gamma}{(1+\gamma)^{2}}\|x-a_{k-1}^{*}\|^{2}

(106a)

\displaystyle=\frac{\gamma}{(1+\gamma)^{2}}\frac{\gamma^{2(k-1)}}{(1+\gamma)^{2(k-1)}}\|x-x^{*}\|^{2}

(106b)

\displaystyle=\frac{\gamma^{2k-1}}{(1+\gamma)^{2k}}\|x-x^{*}\|^{2}.

(106c)

That is,

(\forall k\geq 1)\hskip 9.24994pt\frac{\|x-\operatorname{Prox}_{\gamma_{k}f}(x+\gamma_{k}a_{k-1}^{*})\|^{2}}{\gamma_{k}}=\frac{\gamma^{2k-1}}{(1+\gamma)^{2k}}\|x-x^{*}\|^{2}.

(107)

Therefore, the lower bound in
Eq.

95
turns into

\bigg(\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{\gamma^{2k-1}}{(1+\gamma)^{2k}}\bigg)\|x-x^{*}\|^{2}=\frac{\gamma}{1+2\gamma}\|x-x^{*}\|^{2}

(108)

which is strictly greater than Carlier’s bound
\gamma(1+\gamma)^{-2}\|x-x^{*}\|^{2}

whenever
x\neq x^{*}
.

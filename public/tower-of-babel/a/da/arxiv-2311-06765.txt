Exponential Stability of the Inhomogeneous Navier-Stokes-Vlasov System in Vacuum
Hai-Liang Li, Ling-Yun Shou, Yue Zhang

Abstract
In this paper, we study the asymptotic behaviors of solutions to the inhomogeneous Navier-Stokes-Vlasov system in ℝ 3 × ℝ 3 \mathbb{R}^{3}\times\mathbb{R}^{3} , where the initial fluid density is allowed to vanish. We establish the uniform bound of the macroscopic density associated with the distribution function and prove the global existence and uniqueness of strong solutions to the Cauchy problem with vacuum for either small initial energy or large viscosity coefficient. The uniform boundedness and the presence of vacuum enable us to show that as the time evolves, the fluid velocity decays, while the distribution function concentrates towards a Dirac measure in velocity centred at 0 0 , with an exponential rate. In order to overcome the degeneracy in the momentum equations, we develop an energy argument based on higher order functional inequalities designed for fluid-particle coupled structures.

We investigate the inhomogeneous Navier-Stokes-Vlasov system for the fluid-particle motion:

\frac{{\rm{d}}}{{\rm{d}}t}E(t)+D(t)\leq 0.

\displaystyle n_{f}(t,x):=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}f(t,x,v){\rm{d}}v,\quad j_{f}(t,x):=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}vf(t,x,v){\rm{d}}v,\quad e_{f}(t,x):=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|v|^{2}f(t,x,v){\rm{d}}v.

,

.

Let

(\rho_{0},u_{0},f_{0})

\left\{\begin{aligned} &0\leq\rho_{0}\in L^{\frac{3}{2}}\cap L^{\infty}\cap H^{1},\\
&\sqrt{\rho_{0}}u_{0}\in L^{2},~~u_{0}\in D^{1}_{0,\sigma},\\
&0\leq f_{0}\in L^{1}_{x,v}\cap L^{\infty}_{x,v}\cap W^{1,\frac{3}{2}}_{x,v},\\
&{\rm{Supp}}_{v}f_{0}(x,v)\subset\{v\in\mathbb{R}^{3}|\,|v|\leq R_{0}\}.\end{aligned}\right.

Then, there exists a constant
\varepsilon_{0}>0
depending on

,
\|\rho_{0}\|_{L^{\frac{3}{2}}\cap L^{\infty}}
,
\|f_{0}\|_{L^{1}_{x,v}}

Remark 1.1
.

Some comments are in order.

•

In (
1.12
), the large-time behavior of the energy
E(t)
provides the
L^{2}
-decay of
\sqrt{\rho}u
but without the
L^{2}
-decay of

due to the influence of vacuum. However, we are able to derive the

-decay of

since we capture the exponential rates of the dissipation

and the high-order norm

for large times. Indeed, it follows from the Gagliardo-Nirenberg-Sobolev inequalities that

\|u(t)\|_{L^{6}\cap L^{\infty}}\leq C_{1}e^{-\lambda_{1}t}.

From the definition of

, we also obtain

\|\sqrt{f}(u-v)(t)\|_{L^{2}_{x,v}}^{2}\leq C_{1}e^{-\lambda_{1}t},

which implies the alignment between the fluid velocity

and the particle velocity

as the time evolves.

•

Different from the interesting works
[
28
,
34
]
concerning algebraic decay rates of the homogeneous incompressible Navier-Stokes-Vlasov equations
(
i.e.,
\rho\equiv 1
or
\rho
is bounded from below in (

)
)
, we obtain an exponential decay rate in the unbounded spatial domain

\frac{1}{2}\|\sqrt{\rho}u\|_{L^{2}}^{2}
can be completely controlled by the viscosity dissipation

]

•

For any given

is suitable small or the viscosity coefficient

•

For the global existence and uniqueness in Theorem

We explain the main strategies and ideas to prove Theorems
1.1
and
1.2
about the global existence and large-time behaviors of solutions to the Cauchy problem (
1.1
)–(
1.2
). To begin with, we prove the coercive estimate

D(t)\geq\lambda E(t)~~\text{with}~~\lambda\sim\frac{\min\{\mu,\kappa\}}{1+\displaystyle\sup_{t>0}\|(\rho,n_{f})(t)\|_{L^{\frac{3}{2}}}}.

(1.17)

By (
1.17
) and the energy inequality (
1.5
), we obtain the exponential stability of the basic energy
E(t)
(refer to Proposition
2.1
).
Since the transport nature in
\eqref{NSV}_{1}
with vacuum leads to the uniform evolution of the
L^{\frac{3}{2}}
-norm for the fluid density
\rho
, our key ingredient is to show the uniform bound

\sup_{t>0}\|n_{f}(t)\|_{L^{\frac{3}{2}}}<\infty.

(1.18)

To achieve it, we rewrite the Vlasov equation
\eqref{NSV}_{4}
as

f(t,x,v)=e^{3\kappa t}f_{0}(X(0;t,x,v),V(0;t,x,v)),

(1.19)

where the characteristic curves
X(\tau;t,x,v)
and
V(\tau;t,x,v)
are solved by

\left\{\begin{aligned} &\frac{{\rm{d}}}{{\rm{d}}\tau}X(\tau;t,x,v)=V(\tau;t,x,v),\\
&\frac{{\rm{d}}}{{\rm{d}}\tau}V(\tau;t,x,v)=\kappa(u(\tau,X(\tau;t,x,v))-V(\tau;t,x,v)),\\
&X(t;t,x,v)=x,~~V(t;t,x,v)=v.\end{aligned}\right.

(1.20)

Note that the formula (
1.19
) implies the time growth of the distribution function
f
, so one may not obtain (
1.18
) directly. Since the
L^{1}
-norm of
n_{f}
is conserved, it suffices to obtain the uniform upper bound of
n_{f}
. As in
[
37
,
29
,
34
]
, we need to derive the upper bound
2e^{-3\kappa t}
of the Jacobian matrix of the map
\Gamma^{-1}_{t,x}:V(0;t,x,v)\rightarrow v
(refer to Lemma
3.2
) such that

n_{f}(t,x)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}e^{3\kappa t}f_{0}(X(0;t,x,v),V(0;t,x,v)){\rm{d}}v\leq 2\|f_{0}\|_{L^{1}_{v}(L^{\infty}_{x})},

as long as the following uniform Lipschitz regularity of
u
holds

\int_{0}^{t}\kappa\|\nabla u(\tau)\|_{L^{\infty}}{\rm{d}}\tau\ll 1.

(1.21)

However, it is difficult to enclose the a-priori estimate (
1.21
) since the techniques of maximal regularity estimates used in
[
37
,
29
,
34
]
can not be applied to the momentum equations
\eqref{NSV}_{2}
containing the degenerate term
\rho u_{t}
near the vacuum. To overcome this difficulty, we construct some new functional inequalities and established time-weighted estimates for higher order norms of
u
. This method is inspired by the works
[
25
,
38
]
for the pure inhomogeneous Navier-Stokes equations and allows us to capture some additional dissipation structures due to fluid-particle interactions. To be more specific, given the basic energy inequality (
1.5
), we improve the estimates of
D(t)
in Lemma
3.4
and obtain a new functional inequality

\frac{{\rm{d}}}{{\rm{d}}t}D(t)+\mathcal{E}(t)\leq g_{1}(t)D(t),

(1.22)

where
g_{1}(t)
is uniformly integrable, and
\mathcal{E}(t)
is the high-order dissipation

\mathcal{E}(t):=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\rho|u_{t}|^{2}{\rm{d}}x+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}\times\mathbb{R}^{3}}\kappa^{2}|u-v|^{2}f{\rm{d}}v{\rm{d}}x.

(1.23)

Based on (
1.22
), it is natural to enhance the estimate of
\mathcal{E}(t)
. In Lemma
3.5
, we further obtain another new functional inequality

\frac{{\rm{d}}}{{\rm{d}}t}\mathcal{E}(t)+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\mu|\nabla u_{t}|^{2}{\rm{d}}x+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\kappa n_{f}|u_{t}|^{2}{\rm{d}}x\leq g_{2}(t)\mathcal{E}(t)+F(t),

(1.24)

for some uniformly integrable functions
g_{2}(t)
and
F(t)
. In particular, the new inequalities (
1.22
) and (
1.24
), together with the Stokes structure in
\eqref{NSV}_{2}
, implies the higher order estimates of
(u,P)
. With the aid of careful initial layer analysis on (
1.22
) and (
1.24
), we obtain the short-time integration part of (
1.21
). Meanwhile, the large-time integration part of (
1.21
) relies on the improvement of the exponential time-decay rate for
E(t)
(refer to Lemma
3.3
) to both
D(t)
and
\mathcal{E}(t)
. Based on these uniform estimates and a bootstrap argument, we enclose (
1.21
) and thence deduce the higher order regularities of
(\rho,u,f)
and the decay estimates (
1.12
).

The rest of this paper is organized as follows. In Section
2
, we devote to the exponential time-decay rate of the energy
E(t)
under the additional bound (
1.18
) of
n_{f}
. In Section
3
, we justify the uniform bound (
1.18
), enclose the a-priori Lipschitz regularity (
1.21
) of
u
and further obtain the higher order estimates and large-time behaviors of the solution. The main results, Theorems
1.1
and
1.2
, are proved in Section
4
. Some definitions and lemmas are presented in
Appendix
.

2
Conditional large-time behavior

In this section, we prove the exponential stability of solutions to the Cauchy problem (
1.1
)–(
1.2
) under the additional condition that the
L^{\frac{3}{2}}
-norm of the macroscopical density
n_{f}
is uniformly bounded in time. Compared with the use of the Poincaré inequality in the spatial periodic case
[
21
]
, our proof relies on the integrability of densities with vacuum.

Proposition 2.1
.

For given time
T>0
, let
(\rho,u,f)
be a strong solution to the Cauchy problem (
1.1
)–(
1.2
) for
t\in(0,T]
. Suppose that there exist some constant
C_{*}>0
independent of
T
such that

\sup_{t\in(0,T]}\|n_{f}(t)\|_{L^{\frac{3}{2}}}\leq C_{*}.

(2.1)

Then it holds for
t\in(0,T]
that

E(t)\leq e^{-2\alpha t}E_{0}

(2.2)

with the constant

\alpha:=\frac{\min\{\mu,\kappa\}}{2+C_{S}(\|\rho_{0}\|_{L^{\frac{3}{2}}}+2C_{*})}>0,

(2.3)

where
C_{S}>0
denotes the Sobolev constant such that
\|\cdot\|_{L^{6}}^{2}\leq C_{S}\|\nabla\cdot\|_{L^{2}}^{2}
holds.

Proof.

In view of (
1.1
), one can obtain the energy inequality (
1.5
) for any
t\in(0,T]
. We claim that

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\mu|\nabla u|^{2}{\rm{d}}x+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}\times\mathbb{R}^{3}}\kappa|u-v|^{2}f{\rm{d}}v{\rm{d}}x\geq 2\alpha E(t)

(2.4)

with
\alpha
given in (
2.3
). Based on (
2.4
), we are able to employ the Grönwall inequality to (
1.5
) and then derive the exponential stability (
2.2
).

Indeed, by the incompressible condition
\eqref{NSV}_{3}
and the transport nature of
\eqref{NSV}_{1}
, a direct computation (cf.
[
47
]
) deduces for
t\in(0,T]
that

\|\rho\|_{L^{\frac{3}{2}}}=\|\rho_{0}\|_{L^{\frac{3}{2}}},

(2.5)

which together with the Sobolev inequality yields

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\rho|u|^{2}{\rm{d}}x\leq\|\rho\|_{L^{\frac{3}{2}}}\|u\|_{L^{6}}^{2}\leq C_{S}\|\rho_{0}\|_{L^{\frac{3}{2}}}\|\nabla u\|_{L^{2}}^{2}.

(2.6)

Similarly, one has

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}\times\mathbb{R}^{3}}|u|^{2}f{\rm{d}}v{\rm{d}}x\leq C_{S}\|n_{f}\|_{L^{\frac{3}{2}}}\|\nabla u\|_{L^{2}}^{2}.

(2.7)

Therefore, by (
2.5
)–(
2.7
) and
\kappa\geq 1
, we obtain

\displaystyle E(t)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\rho|u|^{2}{\rm{d}}x+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}\times\mathbb{R}^{3}}(|u|^{2}+|u-v|^{2})f{\rm{d}}v{\rm{d}}x

\displaystyle\leq(2\alpha)^{-1}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\mu|\nabla u|^{2}{\rm{d}}x+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}\times\mathbb{R}^{3}}\kappa|u-v|^{2}f{\rm{d}}v{\rm{d}}x\right).

This gives rise to (
2.4
), and thus the proof is completed.
∎

3.1
The bootstrap

To extend the local solution globally in time, we establish the following uniform a-priori bound.

Proposition 3.2
.

There exists a constant
\varepsilon_{0}\in(0,1)
depending only on
\|\rho_{0}\|_{L^{\frac{3}{2}}\cap L^{\infty}}
,
\|f_{0}\|_{L^{1}_{x,v}}

and

such that if (

then it holds the following estimate that

\int_{0}^{T}(\mu^{-3}\|\nabla u(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{4}+\kappa\|u(t)\|_{L^{\infty}}+20\kappa\|\nabla u(t)\|_{L^{\infty}}){\rm{d}}t\leq\frac{1}{2}.

(3.2)

Before giving the proof of Proposition
3.2
, let us show some necessary uniform bounds on
(\rho,u,f)
. By standard maximal principles for the mass conservation equation
\eqref{NSV}_{1}
and the Vlasov equation
\eqref{NSV}_{4}
, one has the following lemma.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
T>0
and
(\rho,u,f)
be the smooth solution to (
1.1
) for
(t,x,v)\in(0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{3}\times\mathbb{R}^{3}
with the initial data
(\rho_{0},u_{0},f_{0})
satisfying (
1.8
). Then under the condition (
3.1
), it holds

0\leq\rho(t,x)\leq\|\rho_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}},~~0\leq f(t,x,v)\leq e^{3\kappa T}\|f_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}_{x,v}}.

(3.3)

In order to justify the key condition (
2.1
), we establish the uniform estimates on the moments
n_{f}
,
j_{f}
and

.

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
T>0
and
(\rho,u,f)
be the smooth solution to (
1.1
) for

with the initial data
(\rho_{0},u_{0},f_{0})

1.8

3.1
), it holds for

\sup_{t\in(0,T]}\|n_{f}(t)\|_{L^{p}}\leq 2\|f_{0}\|_{L^{1}_{x,v}}^{\frac{1}{p}}\|f_{0}\|_{L^{1}_{v}(L^{\infty}_{x})}^{1-\frac{1}{p}}.

(3.4)

Furthermore, one has the following uniform upper bounds

\displaystyle\sup_{t\in(0,T]}\|j_{f}(t)\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq 2\|(1+|v|)f_{0}\|_{L^{1}_{v}(L^{\infty}_{x})},

(3.5)

\displaystyle\sup_{t\in(0,T]}\|e_{f}(t)\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq 2\|(1+|v|^{2})f_{0}\|_{L^{1}_{v}(L^{\infty}_{x})}.

(3.6)

Proof.

The proof follows similar arguments as in
[
39
,
37
,
28
]
. Let the characteristic curves
X(\tau;t,x,v)
and
V(\tau;t,x,v)
be given by (
1.20
). Integrating
\eqref{NSV}_{4}
over
[0,t]\times\mathbb{R}^{3}\times\mathbb{R}^{3}
for
t\in(0,T]
, we get

\|n_{f}\|_{L^{1}}=\|f_{0}\|_{L^{1}_{x,v}}.

(3.7)

According to the definition of
n_{f}
and the formula (
1.19
) of
f
, it holds

\displaystyle n_{f}(t,x)

\displaystyle=e^{3\kappa t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}f_{0}(X(0;t,x,v),V(0;t,x,v)){\rm{d}}v

(3.8)

\displaystyle=e^{3\kappa t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}f_{0}(X(0;t,x,\Gamma_{t,x}^{-1}(w)),w)\left|{\rm{det}}\,D_{v}\Gamma_{t,x}(\Gamma_{t,x}^{-1}(w))\right|^{-1}{\rm{d}}w

with the mapping
\Gamma_{t,x}:v\mapsto w=V(0;t,x,v)
. To control (
3.8
), one needs to justify that
\Gamma_{t,x}
is
C^{1}
-diffeomorphism. Based on (
1.20
), one deduces that

e^{\kappa t}v-V(0;t,x,v)=\int_{0}^{t}e^{\kappa\tau}\kappa u(\tau,X(\tau;t,x,v)){\rm{d}}\tau.

(3.9)

Thence taking the derivative of (
3.9
) with respect to
v
, we have

e^{\kappa t}{\rm{Id}}-D_{v}\Gamma_{t,x}(v)=\int_{0}^{t}e^{\kappa\tau}\kappa\nabla u(\tau,X(\tau;t,x,v)D_{v}X(\tau;t,x,v){\rm{d}}\tau,

(3.10)

This requires a bound of
\|D_{v}X(\tau)\|_{L^{\infty}_{x,v}}
. To achieve it, we differentiate (
1.20
) with respect to
(x,v)
to obtain

\frac{{\rm{d}}}{{\rm{d}}\tau}D_{x,v}Z(\tau;t,x,v)=D_{x,v}W(\tau,Z(\tau;t,x,v))\cdot D_{x,v}Z(\tau;t,x,v),

where
Z=(X,V)
and
W=(V,\kappa(u-V))
. A use of Grönwall’s inequality yields

\|D_{x,v}Z(\tau)\|_{L^{\infty}_{x,v}}\leq\|D_{x,v}Z(t)\|_{L^{\infty}_{x,v}}\exp\left(\int^{t}_{\tau}\|D_{x,v}W\|_{L^{\infty}_{x,v}}{\rm{d}}s\right).

Since
D_{x,v}Z(t)={\rm Id}
and
\kappa\geq 1
, it implies that for
0\leq\tau\leq t\leq T
,

\|(\nabla_{v}X,\nabla_{v}V)(\tau)\|_{L^{\infty}_{x,v}}\leq\exp{\left(\kappa(t-\tau)+\kappa\int_{\tau}^{t}\|\nabla u\|_{L^{\infty}}{\rm{d}}s\right)}.

(3.11)

By (
3.1
), (
3.10
) and (
3.11
), we get

\displaystyle\|e^{-\kappa t}D_{v}\Gamma_{t,x}(v)-{\rm{Id}}\|_{L^{\infty}_{x,v}}

\displaystyle\leq\int_{0}^{t}e^{-\kappa(t-\tau)}\kappa\|\nabla u\|_{L^{\infty}}\|D_{v}X(\tau;t,x,v)\|_{L^{\infty}_{x,v}}{\rm{d}}\tau

\displaystyle\leq\int_{0}^{t}\kappa\|\nabla u\|_{L^{\infty}}{\rm{d}}\tau\,\exp{\left(\int_{0}^{t}\kappa\|\nabla u\|_{L^{\infty}}{\rm{d}}\tau\right)}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{20}e^{\frac{1}{20}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{9}.

It then follows from Lemma
A.3
with
\phi=e^{-\kappa t}\Gamma_{t,x}
that

|{\rm{det}}\,D_{v}\Gamma_{t,x}(v)|=e^{3\kappa t}|{\rm{det}}\,\nabla\phi|\geq\frac{1}{2}e^{3\kappa t},

(3.12)

which combined with (
3.8
) yields

\displaystyle n_{f}(t,x)

\displaystyle\leq e^{3\kappa t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}f_{0}(X(0;t,x,\Gamma_{t,x}^{-1}(w)),w)\cdot 2e^{-3\kappa t}{\rm{d}}w

(3.13)

\displaystyle\leq 2\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}f_{0}(X(0;t,x,\Gamma_{t,x}^{-1}(w)),w){\rm{d}}w

\displaystyle\leq 2\|f_{0}\|_{L^{1}_{v}(L^{\infty}_{x})}.

Hence, by using (
3.7
), (
3.13
) and Hölder’s inequality, we derive the estimate (
3.4
) for
n_{f}
.

As for
j_{f}
, due to the formula (
3.10
) and the bound (
3.12
),

fulfills the following description

\displaystyle j_{f}(t,x)

\displaystyle=e^{3\kappa t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\Gamma_{t,x}^{-1}(w)f_{0}(X(0;t,x,\Gamma_{t,x}^{-1}(w)),w)\left|{\rm{det}}\,D_{v}\Gamma_{t,x}(\Gamma_{t,x}^{-1}(w))\right|^{-1}{\rm{d}}w

(3.14)

\displaystyle\leq e^{3\kappa t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\left|\Gamma_{t,x}^{-1}(w)\right|f_{0}(X(0;t,x,\Gamma_{t,x}^{-1}(w)),w)\cdot 2e^{-3\kappa t}{\rm{d}}w

\displaystyle\leq 2\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}e^{-\kappa t}wf_{0}(X(0;t,x,\Gamma_{t,x}^{-1}(w)),w){\rm{d}}w

\displaystyle+2\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}e^{-\kappa(t-\tau)}\kappa\|u\|_{L^{\infty}}f_{0}(X(0;t,x,\Gamma_{t,x}^{-1}(w)),w){\rm{d}}w{\rm{d}}\tau,

and this as well as (
1.8
) and (
3.1
) leads to the estimate (
3.5
).

Similarly, it holds for

that

\displaystyle e_{f}(t,x)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}e^{3\kappa t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|\Gamma_{t,x}^{-1}(w)|^{2}f_{0}(X(0;t,x,\Gamma_{t,x}^{-1}(w)),w)\left|{\rm{det}}\,D_{v}\Gamma_{t,x}(\Gamma_{t,x}^{-1}(w))\right|^{-1}{\rm{d}}w

(3.15)

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\left|e^{-\kappa t}w+\int_{0}^{t}e^{-\kappa(t-\tau)}\kappa u(\tau,X(\tau;t,x,\Gamma_{t,x}^{-1}(w))){\rm{d}}\tau\right|^{2}f_{0}(X(0;t,x,\Gamma_{t,x}^{-1}(w)),w){\rm{d}}w

\displaystyle\leq 2e^{-2\kappa t}\||v|^{2}f_{0}\|_{L^{1}_{v}(L^{\infty}_{x})}+2\left|\int_{0}^{t}\kappa\|u\|_{L^{\infty}}{\rm{d}}\tau\ \right|^{2}\|f_{0}\|_{L^{1}_{v}(L_{x}^{\infty})}

\displaystyle\leq 2\||v|^{2}f_{0}\|_{L^{1}_{v}(L^{\infty}_{x})}+2\|f_{0}\|_{L^{1}_{v}(L_{x}^{\infty})}.

Therefore, we complete the proof of Lemma
3.2
.
∎

\widetilde{C}_{1}=\widetilde{C}_{1}(\|\rho_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}},\|f_{0}\|_{L^{1}_{v}(L^{\infty}_{x})})
such that

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\mu^{-3}\|\nabla u\|_{L^{2}}^{4}{\rm{d}}t

\displaystyle\leq\mu^{-5}\sup_{t\in(0,T]}\left(\mu\|\nabla u\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\right)\int_{0}^{T}\mu\|\nabla u\|_{L^{2}}^{2}{\rm{d}}t

(3.48)

\displaystyle\leq\widetilde{C}_{1}M(\mu^{-4}+\mu^{-5}\kappa)E_{0}

\displaystyle<\frac{1}{6},

provided that

E_{0}<\min\{\mu^{4},\,\mu^{5}\}\kappa^{-1}\varepsilon_{1}~~\text{with}~~\varepsilon_{1}:=\frac{1}{12\widetilde{C}_{1}M}.

Next, we establish the
L^{1}(0,T;L^{\infty})
-norm of
u
. It follows from the Gagliardo-Nirenberg inequality that

\int_{0}^{T}\kappa\|u\|_{L^{\infty}}{\rm{d}}t\leq C\kappa\int_{0}^{T}\|\nabla u\|_{L^{2}}^{\frac{1}{2}}\|\nabla^{2}u\|_{L^{2}}^{\frac{1}{2}}{\rm{d}}t.

For the short time
T\leq 1
, thanks to (
3.20
) with
\beta_{1}=1
, we get

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\|\nabla u\|_{L^{2}}^{\frac{1}{2}}\|\nabla^{2}u\|_{L^{2}}^{\frac{1}{2}}{\rm{d}}t

\displaystyle\leq C\mu^{-\frac{3}{4}}\sup_{t\in(0,1]}\Big(t\mu\|\nabla u\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\Big)^{\frac{1}{4}}\left(\int_{0}^{1}t\mu^{2}\|\nabla^{2}u\|_{L^{2}}^{2}{\rm{d}}t\right)^{\frac{1}{4}}\left(\int_{0}^{1}t^{-\frac{2}{3}}{\rm{d}}t\right)^{\frac{3}{4}}

\displaystyle\leq C\mu^{-\frac{3}{4}}E_{0}^{\frac{1}{2}}.

As for the large time
T\geq 1
, the time-integral on
(0,1]
can be handled similarly as above, and on the rest part
[1,T]
, one deduces from (
3.21
) that

\displaystyle\int_{1}^{T}\|\nabla u\|_{L^{2}}^{\frac{1}{2}}\|\nabla^{2}u\|_{L^{2}}^{\frac{1}{2}}{\rm{d}}t

\displaystyle\leq C\mu^{-\frac{3}{4}}\sup_{t\in[1,T]}\Big(e^{\alpha_{1}t}\mu\|\nabla u\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\Big)^{\frac{1}{4}}\left(\int_{1}^{T}e^{\alpha_{1}t}\mu^{2}\|\nabla^{2}u\|_{L^{2}}^{2}{\rm{d}}t\right)^{\frac{1}{4}}\left(\int_{1}^{T}e^{-\frac{2}{3}\alpha_{1}t}{\rm{d}}t\right)^{\frac{3}{4}}

\displaystyle\leq C\alpha_{1}^{-\frac{3}{4}}\mu^{-\frac{3}{4}}E_{0}^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\leq C(\mu^{-\frac{3}{4}}+\mu^{-\frac{3}{2}})E_{0}^{\frac{1}{2}},

where we used the fact from (
3.17
) that
\alpha_{1}^{-1}\leq C(1+\mu^{-1})
. It thus holds for some constant
\widetilde{C}_{2}=\widetilde{C}_{2}(\|\rho_{0}\|_{L^{\frac{3}{2}}\cap L^{\infty}},\|f_{0}\|_{L^{1}_{x,v}\cap L^{1}_{v}(L^{\infty}_{x})}))
that

\int_{0}^{T}\kappa\|u\|_{L^{\infty}}{\rm{d}}t\leq\widetilde{C}_{2}\kappa(\mu^{-\frac{3}{4}}+\mu^{-\frac{3}{2}})E_{0}^{\frac{1}{2}}<\frac{1}{6},

(3.49)

provided that

E_{0}<\min\{\mu^{\frac{3}{2}},\mu^{3}\}\kappa^{-2}\varepsilon_{2}~~\text{with}~~\varepsilon_{2}:=\frac{1}{144\widetilde{C}_{2}^{2}}.

Finally, it suffices to control the
L^{1}(0,T;L^{\infty})
-norm of
\nabla u
. It can be derived from the Gagliardo-Nirenberg inequality (
A.1
) that

\|\nabla u\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq C\|\nabla u\|_{L^{2}}^{\frac{1}{4}}\|\nabla^{2}u\|_{L^{6}}^{\frac{3}{4}},

(3.50)

which requires the estimate of
\|\nabla^{2}u\|_{L^{6}}
. To that matter, we employ (
A.4
) to
\eqref{NSV}_{2}
, making use of the bounds (
3.3
)–(
3.5
), and then get

\displaystyle\mu\|\nabla^{2}u\|_{L^{6}}+\|\nabla P\|_{L^{6}}

\displaystyle\leq C\Big\|\rho u_{t}+\rho u\cdot\nabla u+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\kappa(u-v)f{\rm{d}}v\Big\|_{L^{6}}

\displaystyle\leq C\|\rho\|_{L^{\infty}}\|u_{t}\|_{L^{6}}+C\|\rho\|_{L^{\infty}}\|u\|_{L^{6}}\|\nabla u\|_{L^{\infty}}+C\kappa\Big\|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}(u-v)f{\rm{d}}v\Big\|_{L^{2}}^{\frac{1}{3}}\Big\|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}(u-v)f{\rm{d}}v\Big\|_{L^{\infty}}^{\frac{2}{3}}

\displaystyle\leq C\|\rho_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}}\|\nabla u_{t}\|_{L^{2}}+C\|\rho_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}}\|\nabla u\|_{L^{2}}^{\frac{5}{4}}\|\nabla^{2}u\|_{L^{6}}^{\frac{3}{4}}

\displaystyle+C\kappa\|f_{0}\|_{L^{1}_{v}(L^{\infty}_{x})}^{\frac{1}{6}}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}\times\mathbb{R}^{3}}|u-v|^{2}f{\rm{d}}v{\rm{d}}x\right)^{\frac{1}{6}}\left(\|u\|_{L^{\infty}}\|f_{0}\|_{L^{1}_{v}(L^{\infty}_{x})}+\|(1+|v|)f_{0}\|_{L^{1}_{v}(L^{\infty}_{x})}\right)^{\frac{2}{3}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\mu}{2}\|\nabla^{2}u\|_{L^{6}}+C\|\nabla u_{t}\|_{L^{2}}+C\mu^{-3}\|\nabla u\|_{L^{2}}^{5}+C\kappa(1+\|u\|_{L^{\infty}})\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}\times\mathbb{R}^{3}}|u-v|^{2}f{\rm{d}}v{\rm{d}}x\right)^{\frac{1}{6}},

from which we infer

\displaystyle\|\nabla^{2}u\|_{L^{6}}+\mu^{-1}\|\nabla P\|_{L^{6}}

(3.51)

\displaystyle\leq C\mu^{-1}\|\nabla u_{t}\|_{L^{2}}+C\mu^{-\frac{13}{2}}D(t)^{\frac{5}{2}}+C\mu^{-1}\kappa^{\frac{5}{6}}(1+\|u\|_{L^{\infty}})D(t)^{\frac{1}{6}}.

Then, it follows from (
3.50
) and (
3.51
) that

\displaystyle\|\nabla u\|_{L^{\infty}}

\displaystyle\leq C\mu^{-\frac{5}{4}}D(t)^{\frac{1}{8}}\left(\mu\|\nabla u_{t}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\right)^{\frac{3}{8}}+C\mu^{-5}D(t)^{2}

(3.52)

\displaystyle+C\mu^{-\frac{7}{8}}\kappa^{-\frac{1}{8}}(1+\|u\|_{L^{\infty}}^{\frac{3}{4}})D(t)^{\frac{1}{4}}.

By virtue of (
3.52
), it can be derived from (
3.1
), (
3.16
), (
3.20
) and (
3.33
) that for
T\leq 1
,

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\|\nabla u\|_{L^{\infty}}{\rm{d}}t

\displaystyle\leq C\mu^{-\frac{5}{4}}\sup_{t\in(0,1]}\Big(tD(t)\Big)^{\frac{1}{8}}\left(\int_{0}^{1}t\mu\|\nabla u_{t}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}{\rm{d}}t\right)^{\frac{3}{8}}\left(\int_{0}^{1}t^{-\frac{4}{5}}{\rm{d}}t\right)^{\frac{5}{8}}

\displaystyle+C\mu^{-5}\sup_{t\in(0,T]}D(t)\int_{0}^{T}D(t){\rm{d}}t+C\mu^{-\frac{7}{8}}\kappa^{\frac{5}{8}}\left(\int_{0}^{1}D(t){\rm{d}}t\right)^{\frac{1}{4}}

\displaystyle+C\mu^{-\frac{7}{8}}\kappa^{-\frac{1}{8}}\left(\int_{0}^{1}\kappa\|u\|_{L^{\infty}}{\rm{d}}t\right)^{\frac{3}{4}}\left(\int_{0}^{1}D(t){\rm{d}}t\right)^{\frac{1}{4}}

\displaystyle\leq C\mu^{-\frac{5}{4}}(\mu+\kappa)^{\frac{3}{8}}\Big(\mu^{-5}(\mu+\kappa)E_{0}M+\mu^{-5}E_{0}+\mu^{-1}\kappa+E_{0}+1\Big)^{\frac{3}{8}}E_{0}^{\frac{1}{8}}M^{\frac{3}{8}}

\displaystyle+C\mu^{-5}(\mu+\kappa)E_{0}M+C\mu^{-\frac{7}{8}}\kappa^{\frac{5}{8}}E_{0}^{\frac{1}{4}}.

On the other hand, due to the large-time estimates (
3.21
) and (
3.34
), it also holds for
T\geq 1
that

\displaystyle\int_{1}^{T}\|\nabla u\|_{L^{\infty}}{\rm{d}}t

\displaystyle\leq C\mu^{-\frac{5}{4}}\sup_{t\in[1,T]}\left(e^{\alpha_{1}t}D(t)\right)^{\frac{1}{8}}\left(\int_{1}^{T}e^{\alpha_{1}t}\mu\|\nabla u_{t}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}{\rm{d}}t\right)^{\frac{3}{8}}\left(\int_{1}^{T}e^{-\frac{4}{5}\alpha_{1}t}{\rm{d}}t\right)^{\frac{5}{8}}

\displaystyle+C\mu^{-5}\sup_{t\in[1,T]}D(t)\int_{1}^{T}e^{\alpha_{1}t}D(t){\rm{d}}t

\displaystyle+C\mu^{-\frac{7}{8}}\kappa^{\frac{5}{8}}\left(\int_{1}^{T}e^{\alpha_{1}t}D(t){\rm{d}}t\right)^{\frac{1}{4}}\left(\int_{1}^{T}e^{-\frac{1}{3}\alpha_{1}t}{\rm{d}}t\right)^{\frac{3}{4}}

\displaystyle+C\mu^{-\frac{7}{8}}\kappa^{-\frac{1}{8}}\left(\int_{1}^{T}\kappa\|u\|_{L^{\infty}}{\rm{d}}t\right)^{\frac{3}{4}}\left(\int_{1}^{T}D(t){\rm{d}}t\right)^{\frac{1}{4}}

\displaystyle\leq C\mu^{-\frac{5}{4}}(1+\mu^{-1})^{\frac{5}{8}}\Big(\mu^{-5}(\mu+\kappa)E_{0}M+\mu^{-5}E_{0}+\mu^{-1}\kappa+E_{0}+1\Big)^{\frac{3}{8}}E_{0}^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle+C\mu^{-5}(\mu+\kappa)E_{0}M+C\mu^{-\frac{7}{8}}(1+\mu^{-1})^{\frac{3}{4}}\kappa^{\frac{5}{8}}E_{0}^{\frac{1}{4}}.

To sum up, noticing that
\kappa\geq 1
, it holds that

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}20\kappa\|\nabla u\|_{L^{\infty}}{\rm{d}}t

(3.53)

\displaystyle\leq\widetilde{C}_{3}\kappa^{2}\Big\{(\mu^{-\frac{7}{8}}+\mu^{-\frac{13}{8}})E_{0}^{\frac{1}{8}}+\mu^{-\frac{7}{8}}E_{0}^{\frac{1}{4}}+(\mu^{-\frac{7}{8}}+\mu^{-\frac{25}{8}})E_{0}^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle+(\mu^{-\frac{5}{4}}+\mu^{-\frac{15}{4}})E_{0}^{\frac{7}{8}}+(\mu^{-5}+\mu^{-4})E_{0}\Big\}<\frac{1}{6},

for some constant
\widetilde{C}_{3}=\widetilde{C}_{3}(M,\|\rho_{0}\|_{L^{\frac{3}{2}}\cap L^{\infty}},\|f_{0}\|_{L^{1}_{x,v}},\|(1+|v|^{2})f_{0}\|_{L_{v}^{1}(L_{x}^{\infty})})
. Thus, we let the following five quantities:

\displaystyle\Big(\mu^{-\frac{7}{8}}+\mu^{-\frac{13}{8}}\Big)E_{0}^{\frac{1}{8}},\quad\mu^{-\frac{7}{8}}E_{0}^{\frac{1}{4}},\quad\Big(\mu^{-\frac{7}{8}}+\mu^{-\frac{25}{8}}\Big)E_{0}^{\frac{1}{2}},

\displaystyle\Big(\mu^{-\frac{5}{4}}+\mu^{-\frac{15}{4}}\Big)E_{0}^{\frac{7}{8}},\quad\Big(\mu^{-5}+\mu^{-4}\Big)E_{0}

be less than
1/(30\widetilde{C}_{3}\kappa^{2})
, i.e., there exists a small constant
\varepsilon_{3}>0
only depending on
\widetilde{C}_{3}
such that

E_{0}<\min\{\mu^{\frac{10}{7}},\,\mu^{13}\}\kappa^{-16}\varepsilon_{3}.

Therefore, combining the above estimates (
3.48
), (
3.49
) and (
3.53
) and taking
E_{0}
satisfying (
1.9
) with
\varepsilon_{0}:=\min\{\varepsilon_{1},\varepsilon_{2},\varepsilon_{3}\}
, we end up with (
3.2
) and complete the proof of Proposition
3.2
.
∎

3.2
Large-time behavior of the distribution function

We further capture the concentration phenomena of the distribution function
f
.

Lemma 3.6
.

Let
T>0
and
(\rho,u,f)
be the smooth solution to (
1.1
) for
t\in(0,T]
with the initial data
(\rho_{0},u_{0},f_{0})
satisfying (
1.8
). Then under the condition (
3.1
), it holds for
\alpha_{2}=\min\left\{\frac{\kappa}{2},\frac{\alpha_{1}}{4}\right\}
that

\left\{\begin{aligned} &\|j_{f}(t)\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq C_{5}e^{-\alpha_{2}t}\left(\||v|{f_{0}}\|_{L^{1}_{v}(L^{\infty}_{x})}+E_{0}^{\frac{1}{2}}\right),\\
&\|e_{f}(t)\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq C_{5}e^{-\alpha_{2}t}\Big(\||v|^{2}f_{0}\|_{L^{1}_{v}(L^{\infty}_{x})}+E_{0}^{\frac{1}{2}}\Big),\\
&{\rm{Supp}}_{v}f(t,x,v)\subset\Big\{v\in\mathbb{R}^{3}~|~|v|\leq C_{5}e^{-\alpha_{2}t}\left(R_{0}+E_{0}^{\frac{1}{2}}\right)\Big\},\end{aligned}\right.

(3.54)

where
(t,x)\in(0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{3}
, and as
T\rightarrow+\infty
, there exists a profile
n^{*}
such that

W_{1}(f(t),n^{*}\otimes\delta_{v})+W_{1}(n_{f}(t),n^{*})\leq C_{5}e^{-\alpha_{1}t}E^{\frac{1}{2}}_{0}.

(3.55)

Here
E_{0}
and
\alpha_{1}
are given by (
1.6
) and (
3.17
), respectively, and
C_{5}>0
is a constant depending on
\mu
,
\kappa
,
M
,
\|\rho_{0}\|_{L^{\frac{3}{2}}\cap L^{\infty}}
and
\|f_{0}\|_{L^{1}_{x,v}\cap L^{1}_{v}(L^{\infty}_{x})}
.

Proof.

In terms of (
3.14
), (
3.20
) and (
3.21
), it holds for
\alpha_{2}=\min\left\{\frac{\kappa}{2},\frac{\alpha_{1}}{4}\right\}
that

\displaystyle\|j_{f}\|_{L^{\infty}}

\displaystyle\leq 2e^{-\kappa t}\||v|{f_{0}}\|_{L^{1}_{v}(L^{\infty}_{x})}

\displaystyle+2\|f_{0}\|_{L^{1}_{x}(L^{\infty}_{x})}\left\{\int_{0}^{1}+\int_{1}^{t}\right\}e^{-\kappa(t-\tau)}\kappa\|\nabla u\|_{L^{2}}^{\frac{1}{2}}\|\nabla^{2}u\|_{L^{2}}^{\frac{1}{2}}{\rm{d}}\tau

\displaystyle\leq 2e^{-\kappa t}\||v|{f_{0}}\|_{L^{1}_{v}(L^{\infty}_{x})}

\displaystyle+Ce^{-\kappa t}\kappa\mu^{-\frac{3}{4}}\sup_{\tau\in(0,1]}\left(\tau\mu\|\nabla u\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{4}}\left(\int_{0}^{1}\tau\mu^{2}\|\nabla^{2}u\|_{L^{2}}^{2}{\rm{d}}\tau\right)^{\frac{1}{4}}

\displaystyle+C\kappa\mu^{-\frac{3}{4}}\sup_{\tau\in[1,t]}\left(e^{\alpha_{1}\tau}\mu\|\nabla u\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{4}}\left(\int_{1}^{t}e^{\alpha_{1}\tau}\mu^{2}\|\nabla^{2}u\|_{L^{2}}^{2}{\rm{d}}\tau\right)^{\frac{1}{4}}\left(\int_{1}^{t}e^{-\frac{4}{3}\kappa(t-\tau)}e^{-\frac{2}{3}\alpha_{1}\tau}{\rm{d}}\tau\right)^{\frac{3}{4}}

\displaystyle\leq Ce^{-\alpha_{2}t}\left(\||v|{f_{0}}\|_{L^{1}_{v}(L^{\infty}_{x})}+\kappa\mu^{-\frac{3}{4}}E_{0}^{\frac{1}{2}}\right).

Similarly, based on (
3.15
), we deduce that

\displaystyle\|e_{f}\|_{L^{\infty}}

\displaystyle\leq 2e^{-2\kappa t}\||v|^{2}f_{0}\|_{L^{1}_{v}(L^{\infty}_{x})}

(3.56)

\displaystyle+2\|f_{0}\|_{L^{1}_{x}(L^{\infty}_{x})}\left\{\int_{0}^{1}+\int_{1}^{t}\right\}e^{-\kappa(t-\tau)}\kappa^{2}\|\nabla u\|_{L^{2}}\|\nabla^{2}u\|_{L^{2}}{\rm{d}}\tau

\displaystyle\leq 2e^{-2\kappa t}\||v|^{2}f_{0}\|_{L^{1}_{v}(L^{\infty}_{x})}

\displaystyle+Ce^{-\kappa t}\kappa^{2}\mu^{-\frac{3}{2}}\left(\int_{0}^{1}\mu\|\nabla u\|_{L^{2}}^{2}{\rm{d}}\tau\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\left(\int_{0}^{1}\mu^{2}\|\nabla^{2}u\|_{L^{2}}^{2}{\rm{d}}\tau\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle+C\kappa^{2}\mu^{-\frac{3}{2}}\sup_{\tau\in[1,t]}\left(\mu\|\nabla u\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\left(\int_{1}^{t}e^{\alpha_{1}\tau}\mu^{2}\|\nabla^{2}u\|_{L^{2}}^{2}{\rm{d}}\tau\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\left(\int_{1}^{t}e^{-2\kappa(t-\tau)}e^{-\alpha_{1}\tau}{\rm{d}}\tau\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\leq Ce^{-\alpha_{2}t}\left(\||v|^{2}f_{0}\|_{L^{1}_{v}(L^{\infty}_{x})}+\kappa^{2}\mu^{-\frac{3}{2}}M^{\frac{1}{2}}E_{0}^{\frac{1}{2}}\right).

Then, we deal with the large-time behavior of the compact support of
f
with respect to
v
. Due to the characteristics (
1.20
), it is straightforward to obtain

v=e^{-\kappa t}V(0;t,x,v)+\int_{0}^{t}e^{-\kappa(t-\tau)}\kappa u(\tau,X(\tau;t,x,v)){\rm{d}}\tau.

(3.57)

Recalling (
1.19
) that

f(t,x,v)=e^{3\kappa t}f_{0}(X(0;t,x,v),V(0;t,x,v)),

we deduce

\displaystyle f(t,x,v)\neq 0

\displaystyle\Rightarrow V(0;t,x,v)\in{\rm{Supp}}_{v}f_{0}(X(0;t,x,v),V(0;t,x,v))

\displaystyle\Rightarrow|V(0;t,x,v)|\leq R_{0}.

Hence, in a similar argument as for estimating
\|j_{f}\|_{L^{\infty}}
, one has

|v|\leq e^{-\kappa t}R_{0}+\int_{0}^{t}e^{-\kappa(t-\tau)}\kappa\|u\|_{L^{\infty}}{\rm{d}}\tau\leq Ce^{-\alpha_{2}t}\left(R_{0}+\kappa\mu^{-\frac{3}{4}}E_{0}^{\frac{1}{2}}\right),

for any
(t,x)\in(0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{3}
and
v\in{\rm{Supp}}_{v}f(t,x,v)
.

Furthermore, we establish the large-time behavior of the distribution function
f
and the moment
n_{f}
. By the Monge-Kantorovitch duality (refer to Lemma
A.4
), we have

\displaystyle W_{1}(f(t),n_{f}(t)\otimes\delta_{v})

\displaystyle=\sup_{\|\nabla_{x,v}\psi\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq 1}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}\times\mathbb{R}^{3}}f(t,x,v)\left(\psi(x,v)-\psi(x,0)\right){\rm{d}}v{\rm{d}}x

\displaystyle\leq\sup_{\|\nabla_{x,v}\psi\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq 1}\|\nabla_{v}\psi\|_{L^{\infty}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}\times\mathbb{R}^{3}}|v|f{\rm{d}}v{\rm{d}}x

\displaystyle\leq\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}\times\mathbb{R}^{3}}f{\rm{d}}v{\rm{d}}x\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}\times\mathbb{R}^{3}}|v|^{2}f{\rm{d}}v{\rm{d}}x\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\leq\sqrt{2}\|n_{f}\|_{L^{1}}^{\frac{1}{2}}E(t)^{\frac{1}{2}},

which together with (
3.7
) and (
3.16
) leads to

W_{1}(f(t),n_{f}(t)\otimes\delta_{v})\leq Ce^{-\frac{\alpha_{1}}{2}t}E_{0}^{\frac{1}{2}}.

(3.58)

In addition, the integration of
\eqref{NSV}_{4}
with respect to
v\in\mathbb{R}^{3}
yields

(n_{f})_{t}+{\rm{div}}_{x}j_{f}=0.

Thus it holds for any smooth function
\varphi(x)\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
that

\frac{{\rm{d}}}{{\rm{d}}t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\varphi n_{f}{\rm{d}}x-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\nabla_{x}\varphi j_{f}{\rm{d}}x=0,

which implies for any
t\in(0,T]
that

\displaystyle\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\varphi n_{f}(T){\rm{d}}x-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\varphi n_{f}(t){\rm{d}}x\right|

\displaystyle\leq\int_{t}^{T}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}\times\mathbb{R}^{3}}|v|^{2}f{\rm{d}}v{\rm{d}}x\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}n_{f}|\nabla_{x}\varphi|^{2}{\rm{d}}x\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}{\rm{d}}\tau

\displaystyle\leq\|\nabla_{x}\varphi\|_{L^{\infty}}\int_{t}^{T}\|n_{f}\|_{L^{1}}^{\frac{1}{2}}E(\tau)^{\frac{1}{2}}{\rm{d}}\tau.

More precisely, if
\|\nabla\varphi\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq 1
, in terms of (
3.7
) and (
3.16
), we have

\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\varphi n_{f}(T){\rm{d}}x-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\varphi n_{f}(t){\rm{d}}x\right|\leq\|{f_{0}}\|_{L^{1}_{x,v}}^{\frac{1}{2}}E_{0}^{\frac{1}{2}}\int_{t}^{T}e^{-\frac{\alpha_{1}}{2}\tau}{\rm{d}}\tau,

(3.59)

and this can be extended to Lipschitz functions
\varphi
by a standard approximation argument so that the Monge-Kantorovitch duality formula in Lemma
A.4
leads to

W_{1}(n_{f}(t),n_{f}(T))\leq CE_{0}^{\frac{1}{2}}\alpha_{1}^{-1}(e^{-\frac{\alpha_{1}}{2}t}-e^{-\frac{\alpha_{1}}{2}T}).

Therefore the Cauchy criterion for
n_{f}(t)
is verified as
T\rightarrow\infty
, which means there exist some measure
n^{*}
such that
n_{f}(t)\rightarrow n^{*}
as
t\rightarrow\infty
in the sense of
1
-Wasserstein distance. By letting
T\rightarrow\infty
in (
3.59
), we conclude

W_{1}(n_{f}(t),n^{*})\leq Ce^{-\frac{\alpha_{1}}{2}t}E_{0}^{\frac{1}{2}},

which together with (
3.58
) leads to

W_{1}(f(t),n^{*}\otimes\delta_{v})\leq Ce^{-\frac{\alpha_{1}}{2}t}E_{0}^{\frac{1}{2}}.

Thus, we complete the proof of Lemma
3.6
.
∎

3.3
Higher order estimates

We establish some regularity estimates of the solution
(\rho,u,f)
, which provides the higher order decay estimates of
u
and are useful for the further proof on the uniqueness of the strong solution.

Lemma 3.7
.

Let
T>1
and
(\rho,u,f)
be the smooth solution to (
1.1
) for
t\in(0,T]
with the initial data
(\rho_{0},u_{0},f_{0})
satisfying (
1.8
). Then under the condition (
3.1
), it holds

\int_{0}^{T}\|P(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}{\rm{d}}t\leq C_{6}.

(3.60)

Furthermore, for any
\beta_{3}\in[0,1]
, we have the short-time estimates

\displaystyle\sup_{t\in(0,1]}t^{1+\beta_{3}}\left(\|\nabla u(t)\|_{H^{1}}^{2}+\|P(t)\|_{H^{1}}^{2}\right)

(3.61)

\displaystyle+\int_{0}^{1}t^{1+\beta_{3}}\Big(\|u_{t}(t)\|_{L^{6}}^{2}+\|\nabla^{2}u(t)\|_{L^{6}}^{2}+\|\nabla P(t)\|_{L^{6}}^{2}\Big){\rm{d}}t

\displaystyle\leq C_{6},

and the large-time exponential stability

\displaystyle\sup_{t\in[1,T]}e^{\alpha_{1}t}\left(\|\nabla u(t)\|_{H^{1}}^{2}+\|P(t)\|_{H^{1}}^{2}\right)

(3.62)

\displaystyle+\int_{1}^{T}e^{\frac{\alpha_{1}}{4}t}\Big(\|u_{t}(t)\|_{L^{6}}^{2}+\|\nabla^{2}u(t)\|_{L^{6}}^{2}+\|\nabla P(t)\|_{L^{6}}^{2}\Big){\rm{d}}t

\displaystyle\leq C_{6},

where
C_{6}>0
is a constant only depending on
\mu
,
\kappa
,
M
,
\|\rho_{0}\|_{L^{\frac{3}{2}}\cap L^{\infty}}
,
\|f_{0}\|_{L^{1}_{x,v}}
and
\|(1+|v|^{2})f_{0}\|_{L^{1}_{v}(L^{\infty}_{x})}
.

Proof.

In view of (
A.3
) in Lemma
A.2
to
\eqref{NSV}_{2}
, it follows from (
2.5
), (
3.3
), (
3.4
) and the Gagliardo-Nirenberg inequality (
A.1
) that

\displaystyle\mu^{2}\|\nabla u\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|P\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq C\Big\|\rho u_{t}+\rho u\cdot\nabla u+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\kappa(u-v)f{\rm{d}}v\Big\|_{L^{\frac{6}{5}}}^{2}

(3.63)

\displaystyle\leq C\|\sqrt{\rho}\|_{L^{3}}^{2}\|\sqrt{\rho}u_{t}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+C\|\rho\|_{L^{\infty}}\|\rho\|_{L^{\frac{3}{2}}}\|u\|_{L^{6}}^{2}\|\nabla u\|_{L^{3}}^{2}

\displaystyle+\kappa^{2}\|n_{f}\|_{L^{\frac{3}{2}}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}\times\mathbb{R}^{3}}|u-v|^{2}f{\rm{d}}v{\rm{d}}x

\displaystyle\leq C\|\rho_{0}\|_{L^{\frac{3}{2}}}\|\sqrt{\rho}u_{t}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+C\|\rho_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}}\|\rho_{0}\|_{L^{\frac{3}{2}}}\|\nabla u\|_{L^{2}}^{3}\|\nabla^{2}u\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle+C\|f_{0}\|_{L_{x,v}^{1}}^{\frac{2}{3}}\|f_{0}\|_{L^{1}_{v}(L^{\infty}_{x})}^{\frac{1}{3}}\kappa^{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}\times\mathbb{R}^{3}}|u-v|^{2}f{\rm{d}}v{\rm{d}}x

\displaystyle\leq C\mathcal{E}(t)+\frac{1}{2}\mu^{2}\|\nabla^{2}u\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+C\mu^{-3}\|\nabla u\|_{L^{2}}^{4}D(t),

which, together with (
3.1
) and (
3.19
), leads to

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\|P\|_{L^{2}}^{2}{\rm{d}}t

\displaystyle\leq C\int_{0}^{T}(\mathcal{E}(t)+\mu^{2}\|\nabla^{2}u\|_{L^{2}}^{2}){\rm{d}}t+C\sup_{t\in(0,T]}D(t)\int_{0}^{T}\mu^{-3}\|\nabla u\|_{L^{2}}^{4}{\rm{d}}t

\displaystyle\leq C_{6}.

Moreover, by (
3.29
) and (
3.63
) one has

\displaystyle\mu^{2}\|\nabla u\|_{H^{1}}^{2}+\|P\|_{H^{1}}^{2}\leq C\mathcal{E}(t)+C\mu^{-5}D(t)^{3}.

(3.64)

It can be derived from (
3.20
), (
3.33
) and (
3.64
) that

\displaystyle\sup_{t\in(0,1]}t^{1+\beta_{3}}\Big(\mu^{2}\|\nabla u\|_{H^{1}}^{2}+\|P\|_{H^{1}}^{2}\Big)

(3.65)

\displaystyle\leq C\sup_{t\in(0,1]}t^{1+\beta_{3}}\mathcal{E}(t)+C\mu^{-5}\sup_{t\in(0,1]}\left(t^{\frac{{1+\beta_{3}}}{3}}D(t)\right)^{3}

\displaystyle\leq C_{6}.

Due to the Sobolev inequality, the estimate (
3.33
) with
\beta_{2}=\beta_{3}
directly gives

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}t^{1+\beta_{3}}\mu\|u_{t}\|_{L^{6}}^{2}{\rm{d}}t\leq C\int_{0}^{1}t^{1+\beta_{3}}\mu\|\nabla u_{t}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}{\rm{d}}t\leq C_{6}.

(3.66)

To deal with weighted
L^{2}(0,T;L^{6})
-norms of
\nabla^{2}u
and
\nabla P
, we recall (
3.51
) that

\displaystyle\mu^{2}\|\nabla^{2}u\|_{L^{6}}^{2}+\|\nabla P\|_{L^{6}}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq C\|\nabla u_{t}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+C\mu^{-11}D(t)^{5}+C\kappa^{\frac{5}{3}}(1+\|u\|_{L^{\infty}}^{2})D(t)^{\frac{1}{3}}.

It thus holds by (
3.19
), (
3.65
) and (
3.66
) that

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}t^{1+\beta_{3}}\Big(\mu^{2}\|\nabla^{2}u\|_{L^{6}}^{2}+\|\nabla P\|_{L^{2}}\Big){\rm{d}}t

(3.67)

\displaystyle\leq C\mu^{-1}\int_{0}^{1}t^{1+\beta_{3}}\mu\|\nabla u_{t}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}{\rm{d}}t+C\mu^{-11}\sup_{t\in(0,1]}\Big(t^{\frac{1+\beta_{3}}{5}}D(t)\Big)^{5}

\displaystyle+C\kappa^{\frac{5}{3}}\left(1+\mu^{-2}\sup_{t\in(0,1]}\Big(t^{1+\beta_{3}}\mu^{2}\|\nabla u\|_{H^{1}}^{2}\Big)\right)\sup_{t\in(0,1]}D(t)^{\frac{1}{3}}

\displaystyle\leq C_{6}.

Hence, we derive the short-time estimate (
3.61
) from the combination of (
3.65
)–(
3.67
).

Then, we turn to the large-time exponential stability (
3.62
). Making use of (
3.21
), (
3.34
) and (
3.64
), we have

\displaystyle\sup_{t\in[1,T]}e^{\alpha_{1}t}\Big(\mu^{2}\|\nabla u\|_{H^{1}}^{2}+\|P\|_{H^{1}}^{2}\Big)

(3.68)

\displaystyle\leq C\sup_{t\in[1,T]}e^{\alpha_{1}t}\mathcal{E}(t)+C\mu^{-5}\sup_{t\in[1,T]}\left(e^{\alpha_{1}t}D(t)\right)^{3}

\displaystyle\leq C_{6}.

One deduces from (
3.34
) that

\displaystyle\int_{1}^{T}e^{\alpha_{1}t}\mu\|u_{t}\|_{L^{6}}^{2}{\rm{d}}t\leq C\int_{0}^{T}e^{\alpha_{1}t}\mu\|\nabla u_{t}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}{\rm{d}}t\leq C_{6},

(3.69)

and owing to (
3.20
), (
3.34
) and (
3.68
), we also obtain

\displaystyle\int_{1}^{T}e^{\frac{1}{4}\alpha_{1}t}\Big(\mu^{2}\|\nabla^{2}u\|_{L^{6}}^{2}+\|\nabla P\|_{L^{6}}\Big){\rm{d}}t

(3.70)

\displaystyle\leq C\int_{1}^{T}e^{\alpha_{1}t}\|\nabla u_{t}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}{\rm{d}}t+C\mu^{-11}\sup_{t\in[1,T]}D(t)^{4}\int_{1}^{T}e^{\alpha_{1}t}D(t){\rm{d}}t

\displaystyle+C\kappa^{\frac{5}{3}}\left(1+\mu^{-2}\sup_{t\in[1,T]}\Big(e^{\alpha_{1}t}\mu^{2}\|\nabla u\|_{H^{1}}^{2}\Big)\right)\left(\int_{1}^{T}e^{\alpha_{1}t}D(t){\rm{d}}t\right)^{\frac{1}{3}}\left(\int_{1}^{T}e^{-\frac{\alpha_{1}}{8}t}{\rm{d}}t\right)^{\frac{2}{3}}

\displaystyle\leq C_{6}.

Thus, we conclude from (
3.68
)–(
3.70
) the estimate (
3.62
) and complete the proof of Lemma
3.7
.
∎

Lemma 3.8
.

Let
T>0
and
(\rho,u,f)
be the smooth solution to (

) for

with the initial data

(3.71)

Proof.

Applying
\nabla
to
\eqref{NSV}_{1}
and noticing the incompressible condition
\eqref{NSV}_{3}
, we have

\nabla\rho_{t}+u\cdot\nabla(\nabla\rho)+\nabla u\cdot\nabla\rho=0,

which gives rise to

\frac{1}{2}\frac{{\rm{d}}}{{\rm{d}}t}\|\nabla\rho\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\leq\frac{3}{2}\|\nabla u\|_{L^{\infty}}\|\nabla\rho\|_{L^{2}}^{2}.

(3.72)

Dividing (
3.72
) by
\sqrt{\|\nabla\rho\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\eta_{0}}
with
\eta_{0}>0
, integrating the resulting inequality over
[0,t]
for

\displaystyle\leq\|\nabla\rho_{0}\|_{L^{2}}\exp{\left(\frac{3}{2}\int_{0}^{T}\|\nabla u\|_{L^{\infty}}{\rm{d}}t\right)}

\displaystyle\leq 2\|\nabla\rho_{0}\|_{L^{2}},

where one has used (
3.2
). Thus, the proof of Lemma

is completed.

Lemma 3.9
.

Let
T>0
and
(\rho,u,f)
be the smooth solution to (
1.1
) for
t\in(0,T]
with the initial data
(\rho_{0},u_{0},f_{0})
satisfying (
1.8
). Then under the condition (
3.1
), it holds

\sup_{t\in(0,T]}\|f(t)\|_{W^{1,\frac{3}{2}}_{x,v}}\leq C_{T}\|f_{0}\|_{W^{1,\frac{3}{2}}_{x,v}}.

(3.73)

Proof.

Multiplying
\eqref{NSV}_{4}
by
f^{\frac{1}{2}}
and integrating the resulting equality by parts over
\mathbb{R}^{3}\times\mathbb{R}^{3}
, we get

\frac{2}{3}\frac{{\rm{d}}}{{\rm{d}}t}\|f\|_{L^{\frac{3}{2}}_{x,v}}^{\frac{3}{2}}\leq\kappa\|f\|_{L^{\frac{3}{2}}_{x,v}}^{\frac{3}{2}},

which implies that

\sup_{t\in(0,T]}\|f(t)\|_{L^{\frac{3}{2}}_{x,v}}\leq e^{\kappa T}\|f_{0}\|_{L_{x,v}^{\frac{3}{2}}}.

(3.74)

Moreover, it can be derived from
\eqref{NSV}_{4}
that

(\partial_{x}^{i}f)_{t}+v\cdot\nabla_{x}\partial_{x}^{i}f+\kappa\partial_{x}^{i}u\cdot\nabla_{v}f+\kappa(u-v)\cdot\nabla_{v}\partial_{x}^{i}f-3\kappa\partial_{x}^{i}f=0,

(3.75)

for
i=1,\,2,\,3
. Multiplying (
3.75
) by
|\partial_{x}^{i}f|^{\frac{1}{2}}{\rm{sign}}\,\partial_{x}^{i}f
, summing up for
i=1,2,3
, and integrating the resulting equation over
\mathbb{R}^{3}\times\mathbb{R}^{3}
, we obtain

\displaystyle\frac{2}{3}\frac{{\rm{d}}}{{\rm{d}}t}\|\nabla_{x}f\|_{L^{\frac{3}{2}}_{x,v}}^{\frac{3}{2}}

\displaystyle\leq\kappa\|\nabla u\|_{L^{\infty}}\|\nabla_{v}f\|_{L^{\frac{3}{2}}_{x,v}}^{\frac{1}{2}}\|\nabla_{x}f\|_{L^{\frac{3}{2}}_{x,v}}+\kappa\|\nabla_{x}f\|_{L^{\frac{3}{2}}_{x,v}}^{\frac{3}{2}}

(3.76)

\displaystyle\leq C(\kappa\|\nabla u\|_{L^{\infty}}+\kappa)\|(\nabla_{x}f,\nabla_{v}f)\|_{L^{\frac{3}{2}}_{x,v}}^{\frac{3}{2}}.

Similarly, it yields

\frac{2}{3}\frac{{\rm{d}}}{{\rm{d}}t}\|\nabla_{v}f\|_{L^{\frac{3}{2}}_{x,v}}^{\frac{3}{2}}\leq C\kappa\|(\nabla_{x}f,\nabla_{v}f)\|_{L^{\frac{3}{2}}_{x,v}}^{\frac{3}{2}},

which, together with (
3.76
), shows that

\frac{2}{3}\frac{{\rm{d}}}{{\rm{d}}t}\|(\nabla_{x}f,\nabla_{v}f)\|_{L^{\frac{3}{2}}_{x,v}}^{\frac{3}{2}}\leq C(\kappa\|\nabla u\|_{L^{\infty}}+\kappa)\|(\nabla_{x}f,\nabla_{v}f)\|_{L^{\frac{3}{2}}_{x,v}}^{\frac{3}{2}},

and then, by the Grönwall inequality, we have

\displaystyle\sup_{t\in(0,T]}\|(\nabla_{x}f,\nabla_{v}f)(t)\|_{L^{\frac{3}{2}}_{x,v}}

(3.77)

\displaystyle\leq\exp\left(C\int_{0}^{T}\kappa\|\nabla u\|_{L^{\infty}}{\rm{d}}t+C\kappa T\right)\|(\nabla_{x}f_{0},\nabla_{v}f_{0})\|_{L^{\frac{3}{2}}_{x,v}}.

Hence, we conclude the estimate (
3.73
) from the combination of (
3.2
), (
3.74
) and (
3.77
), and the proof of Lemma
3.9
is completed.

4
Proofs of main results

With the help of all the estimates obtained in Section

Proof of Theorem
1.1
.

Let
(\rho_{0},u_{0},f_{0})
be the initial data satisfying (
1.8
) and (
1.9
). According to Proposition
3.1
, there exists a maximal time
T_{*}>0
such that the Cauchy problem (
1.1
)–(
1.2
) has a unique strong solution
(\rho,u,f)

fulfilling (
1.10
) on
(0,T_{*}]\times\mathbb{R}^{3}\times\mathbb{R}^{3}
. Now we define

T_{0}:=\sup\left\{T\in(0,T_{*}]\Big|\int_{0}^{T}\left(\mu^{-3}\|\nabla u\|_{L^{2}}^{4}+\kappa\|u\|_{L^{\infty}}+20\kappa\|\nabla u\|_{L^{\infty}}\right){\rm{d}}t\leq 1\right\}.

(4.1)

By (
1.10
), there exists a small time
T_{1}\in(0,T_{*})
such that
T_{0}\geq T_{1}>0
.

We claim that

T_{*}=T_{0}=\infty,

as long as (
1.9
) holds. Indeed, if
T_{0}<T_{*}
, then one deduces from Proposition
3.2
that

\int_{0}^{T_{0}}\left(\mu^{-3}\|\nabla u\|_{L^{2}}^{4}+\kappa\|u\|_{L^{\infty}}+20\kappa\|\nabla u\|_{L^{\infty}}\right){\rm{d}}t\leq\frac{1}{2},

(4.2)

for all
T\in(0,T_{0}]
. This contradicts the definition of
T_{0}
in (
4.1
), and hence we have
T_{0}=T_{*}
. In order to show
T_{*}=\infty
, we assume
T_{*}<\infty
toward a contradiction. Since the uniform estimate (
4.2
) holds for all
T\in(0,T_{*}]
, by virtue of Proposition
3.2
and Lemmas
3.1
,
3.4
,
3.8
and
3.9
, one can take
(\rho,u,f)(t)
for a time
t
sufficiently close to
T_{*}
as new initial data and obtain the existence of solutions beyond the time
T_{*}
due to Proposition
3.1
, which contradicts the maximality of
T_{*}
. Therefore,
(\rho,u,f)
is indeed a global strong solution to the Cauchy problem (
1.1
)–(
1.2
). In addition, according to (
4.1
), Proposition
3.2
and Lemmas
3.1
–
3.9
, one can show that the global strong solution
(\rho,u,f)
satisfies the property (
1.10
).

To complete the proof of Theorem
1.1
, we finally prove the uniqueness issue. For given time
T>0
, let
(\rho_{1},u_{1},P_{1},f_{1})
and
(\rho_{2},u_{2},P_{2},f_{2})
be two solutions satisfying (
1.10
) on
[0,T]
subject to the same initial data
(\rho_{0},u_{0},f_{0})
. We set

(\tilde{\rho},\tilde{u},\tilde{P},\tilde{f})=(\rho_{1}-\rho_{2},u_{1}-u_{2},P_{1}-P_{2},f_{1}-f_{2}),

which satisfies

\left\{\begin{aligned} &\tilde{\rho}_{t}+u_{1}\cdot\nabla_{x}\tilde{\rho}=-\tilde{u}\cdot\nabla_{x}\rho_{2},\\
&\rho_{1}(\tilde{u}_{t}+u_{1}\cdot\nabla_{x}\tilde{u})+\nabla_{x}\tilde{P}-\mu\Delta_{x}\tilde{u}+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\kappa\tilde{u}f_{1}{\rm{d}}v\\
&=-\tilde{\rho}(u_{2})_{t}-\rho_{1}\tilde{u}\cdot\nabla_{x}u_{2}-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\kappa(u_{2}-v)\tilde{f}{\rm{d}}v,\\
&{\rm{div}}_{x}\tilde{u}=0,\\
&\tilde{f}_{t}+v\cdot\nabla_{x}\tilde{f}+\kappa{\rm{div}}_{v}((u_{1}-v)\tilde{f})=-\kappa\tilde{u}\cdot\nabla_{v}f_{2}.\end{aligned}\right.

(4.3)

Multiplying
\eqref{diso}_{1}
by
|\tilde{\rho}|^{\frac{1}{2}}{\rm{sign}}\,\tilde{\rho}
and integrating over
\mathbb{R}^{3}
, we have

\displaystyle\frac{2}{3}\frac{{\rm{d}}}{{\rm{d}}t}\|\tilde{\rho}\|_{L^{\frac{3}{2}}}^{\frac{3}{2}}

\displaystyle\leq\|\nabla u_{1}\|_{L^{\infty}}\|\tilde{\rho}\|_{L^{\frac{3}{2}}}^{\frac{3}{2}}+\|\nabla\rho_{2}\|_{L^{2}}\|\tilde{u}\|_{L^{6}}\|\tilde{\rho}\|_{L^{\frac{3}{2}}}^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\leq\|\nabla u_{1}\|_{L^{\infty}}\|\tilde{\rho}\|_{L^{\frac{3}{2}}}^{\frac{3}{2}}+C\|\nabla\tilde{u}\|_{L^{2}}\|\tilde{\rho}\|_{L^{\frac{3}{2}}}^{\frac{1}{2}},

which leads to

\displaystyle\|\tilde{\rho}\|_{L^{\frac{3}{2}}}

\displaystyle\leq C\exp{\left(\int_{0}^{t}\|\nabla u_{1}\|_{L^{\infty}}{\rm{d}}\tau\right)}\int_{0}^{t}\|\nabla\tilde{u}\|_{L^{2}}{\rm{d}}\tau

(4.4)

\displaystyle\leq Ct^{\frac{1}{2}}\left(\int_{0}^{t}\|\nabla\tilde{u}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}{\rm{d}}\tau\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

Next, multiplying
\eqref{diso}_{4}
by
|\tilde{f}|^{\frac{1}{5}}{\rm{sign}}\,\tilde{f}
and then integrating over
\mathbb{R}^{3}\times\mathbb{R}^{3}
, we get

\displaystyle\frac{5}{6}\frac{{\rm{d}}}{{\rm{d}}t}\|\tilde{f}\|_{L^{\frac{6}{5}}_{x,v}}^{\frac{6}{5}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\kappa}{2}\|\tilde{f}\|_{L^{\frac{6}{5}}_{x,v}}^{\frac{6}{5}}+\kappa\|\tilde{u}\|_{L^{6}}\|\nabla_{v}f_{2}\|_{L^{\frac{3}{2}}_{x}(L^{\frac{6}{5}}_{v})}\|\tilde{f}\|_{L^{\frac{6}{5}}_{x,v}}^{\frac{1}{5}}

(4.5)

\displaystyle\leq C\kappa\|\tilde{f}\|_{L^{\frac{6}{5}}_{x,v}}^{\frac{6}{5}}+C\kappa\|\nabla\tilde{u}\|_{L^{2}}\|\tilde{f}\|_{L^{\frac{6}{5}}_{x,v}}^{\frac{1}{5}},

where we have used
\|\nabla_{v}f_{2}\|_{L^{\frac{3}{2}}_{x}(L^{\frac{6}{5}}_{v})}\leq C\|\nabla_{v}f_{2}\|_{L^{\frac{3}{2}}_{x,v}}
due to the compact support of
f_{2}
with respect to
v
. Thus, the Grönwall inequality to (
4.5
) gives

\|\tilde{f}\|_{L^{\frac{6}{5}}_{x,v}}\leq C_{T}\int_{0}^{t}\kappa\|\nabla\tilde{u}\|_{L^{2}}{\rm{d}}\tau\leq C_{T}t^{\frac{1}{2}}\left(\int_{0}^{t}\|\nabla\tilde{u}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}{\rm{d}}\tau\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

(4.6)

Since
f
is compactly supported in
v
, the estimate (
4.6
) implies

\|(n_{\tilde{f}},j_{\tilde{f}})\|_{L^{\frac{6}{5}}}\leq C_{T}t^{\frac{1}{2}}\left(\int_{0}^{t}\|\nabla\tilde{u}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}{\rm{d}}\tau\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

(4.7)

Furthermore, multiplying
\eqref{diso}_{2}
by
\tilde{u}
, integrating the resulting equation by parts and using (
4.4
) and (
4.7
), we deduce that

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\frac{{\rm{d}}}{{\rm{d}}t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\rho_{1}|\tilde{u}|^{2}{\rm{d}}x+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\mu|\nabla\tilde{u}|^{2}{\rm{d}}x+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\kappa n_{f_{1}}|\tilde{u}|^{2}{\rm{d}}x

\displaystyle\leq\|(u_{2})_{t}\|_{L^{6}}\|\tilde{\rho}\|_{L^{\frac{3}{2}}}\|\tilde{u}\|_{L^{6}}+\|\nabla u_{2}\|_{L^{\infty}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\rho_{1}|\tilde{u}|^{2}{\rm{d}}x

\displaystyle+\|u_{2}\|_{L^{6}}\|\nabla u_{2}\|_{L^{\infty}}\|\tilde{\rho}\|_{L^{\frac{3}{2}}}\|\tilde{u}\|_{L^{6}}+\|u_{2}\|_{L^{\infty}}\|\tilde{u}\|_{L^{6}}\|n_{\tilde{f}}\|_{L^{\frac{6}{5}}}+\|\tilde{u}\|_{L^{6}}\|j_{\tilde{f}}\|_{L^{\frac{6}{5}}}

\displaystyle\leq C_{T}\Big(\|\nabla u\|_{L^{\infty}}+t\|\nabla(u_{2})_{t}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+t\|\nabla u_{2}\|_{H^{1}\cap W^{1,6}}^{2}\Big)\int_{0}^{t}\|\nabla\tilde{u}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}{\rm{d}}\tau+\frac{\mu}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|\nabla\tilde{u}|^{2}{\rm{d}}x,

from which we obtain for
t\in(0,T]
that

\frac{{\rm{d}}}{{\rm{d}}t}\widetilde{G}(t)\leq C_{T}\Big(\|\nabla u\|_{L^{\infty}}+t\|\nabla(u_{2})_{t}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+t\|\nabla u_{2}\|_{H^{1}\cap W^{1,6}}^{2}\Big)\widetilde{G}(t),

(4.8)

where

Due to

and

, making use of the Grönwall inequality on (

), one has
\widetilde{G}(t)\equiv 0
for
t\in(0,T]
. This together with (
4.4
) and (
4.6
) implies that
(\rho_{1},u_{1},f_{1})\equiv(\rho_{2},u_{2},f_{2})
. Finally, it follows from the Stokes estimate on

that

, and thus the proof of Theorem
1.1
is completed.
∎

Proof of Theorem

.

Owing to Theorem
1.1
, there exists a unique global strong solution

3.21
), (

)–(

\left\{\begin{aligned} &\rho^{\varepsilon}\overset{\ast}{\rightharpoonup}\rho&&\text{in}~~L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{\frac{3}{2}}\cap L^{\infty}),\\

&f^{\varepsilon}\overset{\ast}{\rightharpoonup}f&&\text{in}~~L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{\infty}_{x,v}),\end{aligned}\right.

and

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}(u^{\varepsilon}-v)f^{\varepsilon}{\rm{d}}v\overset{}{\rightharpoonup}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}(u-v)f{\rm{d}}v\quad\text{in}~~\mathcal{D}^{\prime}((0,T)\times\mathbb{R}^{3}).

Therefore, it is easy to check that
(\rho,u,f)
solves the system (
1.1
) in the sense of distributions. Due to the Fatou property,
(\rho,u,f)
satisfies the uniform estimates in Lemmas
3.1
–
3.5
and
3.7
, the large-time behavior
\eqref{r2}_{1}
and the energy inequality (
1.5
) for a.e.
t\in(0,T]
. From the renormalized arguments for
\eqref{NSV}_{1}
and
\eqref{NSV}_{4}
(refer to
[
47
,
8
]
), one has
\rho\in C([0,T];L^{\frac{3}{2}})
and
f\in C([0,T];L^{1}_{x,v})
. It follows from
\rho u\in L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{2})
and
(\rho u)_{t}\in L^{\infty}(0,T;H^{-1})
that
\rho u\in C([0,T];H^{-1})
, and therefore
(\rho,u,f)
is indeed a global weak solution to the Cauchy problem (
1.1
)–(
1.2
) in the sense of Definition
1.1
. Repeating the same argument as in Lemma
3.6
, we have the asymptotic behaviors
\eqref{r2}_{2}
–
\eqref{r2}_{3}
. Thus, the proof of Theorem
1.2
is completed.
∎

In this section, we provide some auxiliary lemmas that are used frequently throughout this paper. We first recall the Gagliardo-Nirenberg inequality (e.g., refer to
[
51
]
).

Lemma A.1
.

Let
f\in L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
and
D^{s}f\in L^{r}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
with
1\leq q,\,r\leq\infty
. For
0\leq k<s
, there exists a constant
C>0
such that

\|D^{k}f\|_{L^{p}}\leq C\|D^{s}f\|_{L^{r}}^{\theta}\|f\|_{L^{q}}^{1-\theta},

(A.1)

where

\frac{1}{p}-\frac{k}{3}=\left(\frac{1}{r}-\frac{s}{3}\right)\theta+\frac{1}{q}(1-\theta)~~\text{and}~~\frac{k}{s}\leq\theta<1.

Next, we consider the Cauchy problem for the Stokes equations below

\left\{\begin{aligned} &-\mu\Delta u+\nabla P=g,\quad x\in\mathbb{R}^{3},\\
&{\rm{div}}\,u=0,\\
&u(x)\rightarrow 0~~\text{as}~~|x|\rightarrow{\infty},\end{aligned}\right.

(A.2)

and we have the following regularity estimates.

Lemma A.2
.

If
g\in L^{\frac{6}{5}}\cap L^{p}
with
p\in(\frac{6}{5},\infty)
, there exists some positive constant
C
depending only on
p
such that a unique weak solution
(u,P)\in D_{0,\sigma}^{1}\times L^{2}
to the Cauchy problem (
A.2
) exists and satisfies

\mu\|\nabla u\|_{L^{2}}+\|P\|_{L^{2}}\leq C\|g\|_{L^{\frac{6}{5}}},

(A.3)

and

\mu\|\nabla^{2}u\|_{L^{p}}+\|\nabla P\|_{L^{p}}\leq C\|g\|_{L^{p}}.

(A.4)

Proof.

The existence and uniqueness of the solution to (
A.2
) is classical, e.g., cf.
[
30
]
. Multiplying
\eqref{steq}_{1}
by
u
and then integrating by parts together with
\eqref{steq}_{2}
, we obtain

\mu\|\nabla u\|_{L^{2}}^{2}=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}g\cdot u{\rm{d}}x\leq\|g\|_{L^{\frac{6}{5}}}\|u\|_{L^{6}}\leq C\|g\|_{L^{\frac{6}{5}}}\|\nabla u\|_{L^{2}},

which implies

\mu\|\nabla u\|_{L^{2}}\leq C\|g\|_{L^{\frac{6}{5}}}.

In addition, we can derive that

P=-\mu(-\Delta)^{-1}{\rm{div}}\Delta u-(-\Delta)^{-1}{\rm{div}}\,g,

which combined with the Sobolev inequality leads to

\|P\|_{L^{2}}\leq\|(-\Delta)^{-1}{\rm{div}}\,g\|_{L^{2}}\leq C\|g\|_{L^{\frac{6}{5}}}.

Thus, one can conclude (
A.3
). A similar argument leads to (
A.4
).
∎

Definition A.1
.

Abstract
. We show convergence of the gradients of the Schrödinger potentials to the (uniquely determined) gradient of Kantorovich potentials in the small-time limit under general assumptions on the marginals, which allow for unbounded densities and supports. Furthermore, we provide novel quantitative stability estimates for the optimal values and optimal couplings for the Schrödinger problem (SP), that we express in terms of a negative order weighted homogeneous Sobolev norm. The latter encodes the linearized behavior of the 2 2 -Wasserstein distance between the marginals. The proofs of both results highlight for the first time the relevance of gradient bounds for Schrödinger potentials, that we establish here in full generality, in the analysis of the short-time behavior of Schrödinger bridges. Finally, we discuss how our results translate into the framework of quadratic Entropic Optimal Transport, that is a version of SP more suitable for applications in machine learning and data science.

1.
Introduction

The Schrödinger problem (SP) as introduced by E. Schrödinger himself in the seminal work
[
47
]
consists in finding the most likely evolution of a cloud of independent Brownian particles between two observed configurations. When the two observation times are very close, SP converges in a suitable sense to the optimal transport (OT) problem. This connection was first understood by Mikami in
[
38
]
and deeply investigated thereafter. By now, researchers from diverse areas ranging from functional inequalities
[
24
]
to statistical machine learning
[
44
]
, control engineering
[
12
]
, and numerics for PDEs
[
7
]
find common ground on the study of SP.

In its classical formulation, given two probability measures
\mu,\,\nu
(i.e. the two observed configurations), SP consists in

\text{minimize }\mathcal{H}(\pi|\mathrm{R}_{0,T})\text{ under the constraint }\pi\in\Pi(\mu,\nu)\,,

where
\mathrm{R}_{0,T}\in\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{R}^{2d})
denotes the heat kernel

\mathrm{R}_{0,T}(\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}y)\propto\exp(-|x-y|^{2}/2T)\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}y
,
\Pi(\mu,\nu)
denotes the set of of couplings between
\mu
and
\nu
, whereas for any two probability measures
\mathfrak{p},\,\mathfrak{q}
the quantity
\mathcal{H}(\mathfrak{p}|\mathfrak{q})
is the relative entropy of
\mathfrak{p}
w.r.t.
\mathfrak{q}
, defined as

\mathcal{H}(\mathfrak{p}|\mathfrak{q})\coloneqq\begin{cases}\displaystyle{\int\log\frac{\mathrm{d}\mathfrak{p}}{\mathrm{d}\mathfrak{q}}\,\mathrm{d}\mathfrak{p}}\quad&\text{if }\mathfrak{p}\ll\mathfrak{q}\,,\\
+{\infty}\quad&\text{otherwise.}\end{cases}

We postpone to Section
2
a precise definition of the relative entropy
\mathcal{H}(\cdot|\mathfrak{q})
when the reference
\mathfrak{q}
is not a probability measure. The mathematical formulation of SP as an entropy minimization problem comes from the theory of large deviations (Sanov’s Theorem), which states that the large deviation rate function for the empirical measure of independent Brownian motions is equal to the relative entropy w.r.t. the stationary Wiener measure. Interestingly, an equivalent formulation of SP as a stochastic optimal control problem (via Girsanov’s Theorem,
[
36
, Theorem 2]
) is possible, that is, SP is equivalent to

\displaystyle\text{minimize }\|\alpha^{\mathrm{P}}(t,x)\|_{L^{2}(\mathrm{d}t\times\mathrm{d}\mathrm{P})}\,\text{ among the path-probabilities }\mathrm{P}\in\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{C}([0,T],\mathbb{R}^{d})\,\text{ s.t.}

\displaystyle\mathrm{P}\text{-a.s.}\,\text{ it holds }\quad\begin{cases}\mathrm{d}X_{t}=\alpha^{\mathrm{P}}(t,X_{t})\mathrm{d}t+\mathrm{d}B_{t}\,,\\
X_{0}\sim\mu,\quad X_{T}\sim\nu\,.\end{cases}

The above control problem has to be understood as choosing the drift
\alpha^{\mathrm{P}}
that steers the system from
\mu
to the target measure
\nu
and that at the same time minimizes the cost of such deviation (i.e.
\|\alpha^{\mathrm{P}}(t,x)\|_{L^{2}(\mathrm{d}t\times\mathrm{d}\mathrm{P})}
). For this reason the optimizer in the above problem is usually referred to as optimal control or optimal
corrector
. This dynamical interpretation of SP provides also a tight connection with PDEs. Indeed, the optimal corrector is of gradient type, i.e.
\alpha(t,x)=\nabla u_{t}(x)
, and
u_{t}
solves the Hamilton-Jacobi-Bellman equation

(1.1)

\begin{cases}\displaystyle{\partial_{t}u_{t}+\frac{1}{2}\Delta u_{t}+\frac{1}{2}|\nabla u_{t}|^{2}=0}\\
u_{T}=\psi\,,\end{cases}

for some measurable function
\psi
as final condition (see e.g.
[
37
, Proposition 6]
).

Remarkably, SP is also equivalent to the entropic optimal transport problem (EOT), which is of great interest in data science and machine learning, and which amounts to

\text{minimize }\int\frac{|x-y|^{2}}{2}\,\mathrm{d}\pi+\varepsilon\,\mathcal{H}(\pi|\mu\otimes\nu)\text{ under the constraint }\pi\in\Pi(\mu,\nu)\,,

with
\varepsilon>0
being a regularization parameter. Note indeed that, in the stochastic control interpretation of SP,
T
plays the role of a time horizon, so that the marginals
\mu,\nu
are respectively observed at the times
t=0
and
t=T
. But if the time interval
[0,T]
is rescaled to
[0,1]
, then
T
can be naturally interpreted as a diffusion/regularization parameter, as after algebraic manipulations it appears in (
1.1
) in front of the Laplacian. Hence, if
\mathsf{Ent}(\cdot)
denotes the relative entropy w.r.t. the Lebesgue measure and we take into account the following identity

T\,\mathcal{H}(\pi|\mathrm{R}_{0,T})-T\,\mathsf{Ent}(\mu)-T\,\mathsf{Ent}(\nu)=\int\frac{|x-y|^{2}}{2}\,\mathrm{d}\pi+T\,\mathcal{H}(\pi|\mu\otimes\nu)\quad\text{ for any }\pi\in\Pi(\mu,\nu),

we immediately deduce the equivalence between SP and EOT. This allows to translate results from SP to EOT and viceversa.
There are however some small differences between the two and, perhaps more importantly, some hypotheses that are natural from the viewpoint of particle systems, may not be so from a computational standpoint, and viceversa. We shall elaborate more on this point at various places throughout the article.

The present article aims at bringing some new insights on two basic questions, namely: the convergence of the gradient of the Schrödinger potentials (the entropic counterpart to Kantorovich potentials) in the small-time limit; and the stability of optimal solutions with respect to variations in the marginal inputs. In a nutshell, our main contributions are:

\blacktriangleright

Theorem
1.1
, where we show under a curvature-dimension condition and a finite Fisher information assumption a strong
L^{2}
-convergence result for the gradient of the Schrödinger potentials (resp. Schrödinger map) to the uniquely determined gradient of (any) Kantorovich potential (resp. to the Brenier map
[
9
]
). See (
1.5
) and Section
1.1
for the terminology.

\blacktriangleright

Theorem
1.2
, where we exhibit explicit upper bounds for the symmetric relative entropy between optimal plans for different sets of marginals. These stability bounds are expressed in terms of a weighted Sobolev norm between marginals and some universal constants depending on curvature lower bounds on the underlying manifold.

For both results, we shall provide right after their statements an extensive comparison with the rapidly developing literature on the subject and further comments. An aspect that, however, deserves to be pointed out at this stage is the fact that Theorem
1.1
can be linked with the Monge-Ampère equation. Indeed, it is well known that in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
(and under suitable assumptions that we will not detail; the reader may refer to the survey
[
18
]
) the Brenier map is the gradient of a convex function
u
solving

\det(D^{2}u(x))=\frac{\rho(x)}{\sigma(\nabla u(x))},\qquad\textrm{for }\mu\textrm{-a.e. }x,

coupled with the “boundary condition”
\nabla u(\mathrm{supp}(\mu))=\mathrm{supp}(\nu)
, where
\rho,\sigma
are the densities w.r.t. the Lebesgue measure of
\mu,\nu
respectively. This allows to state that
x-2T\varphi^{T}
is an “approximated” solution to the above Monge-Ampère equation,
\varphi^{T}
being the first Schrödinger potential in the pair
(\varphi^{T},\psi^{T})
associated with
\mu
and
\nu
(cf. (
1.5
)).

The backbone of our proof strategy are gradient estimates for Schrödinger potentials, that we call
corrector estimates
in view of the above stochastic control interpretation, since they provide contractive estimates for
\|\alpha^{\mathrm{P}}(t,x)\|_{L^{2}(\mathrm{d}\mathrm{P})}
for
t\in[0,T]
. These bounds have been put forward in
[
16
]
to show a quantitative form of convex entropy decay along entropic interpolations and are here proven under much weaker assumptions. So far, corrector estimates have found applications in the proof of new functional inequalities and in the study of the long-time behavior of entropic interpolations, see
[
16
,
50
]
for example, and are known to be equivalent to the celebrated Bakry-Émery condition, see
[
15
]
. However, to the best of our knowledge, the results of this paper are the first to show the interest of such bounds in the analysis of the convergence of SP towards OT, in which one has to deal with short-time limits instead of large-time limits. It is worth pointing out that the problem we consider here is the Schrödinger problem in its large deviations formulation.

Setting and notation

In this work we are going to consider a generalization of the classical SP to a more abstract setting and with a non-trivial dynamics. For this reason, let us consider a weighted manifold
(M,g,\mathfrak{m})
, where
(M,g)
is a smooth, connected, complete (possibly non-compact) Riemannian manifold without boundary and with metric tensor
g
, and
\mathfrak{m}
denotes the
\sigma
-finite invariant measure associated to the SDE

(1.2)

\mathrm{d}X_{t}=-\nabla U(X_{t})\,\mathrm{d}t+\sqrt{2}\,\mathrm{d}B_{t},

i.e.
\mathrm{d}\mathfrak{m}(x)=e^{-U(x)}{\rm vol}(\mathrm{d}x)
, where
U
is a
C^{2}
potential and
B_{t}
denotes a standard Brownian motion on the Riemannian manifold
(M,g)
. By
{\sf d}
we shall denote the geodesic distance induced by
g
, so that we will more often refer to
(M,{\sf d},\mathfrak{m})
rather than to
(M,g,\mathfrak{m})
.

Let
\mathrm{R}_{0,T}
denote the joint law at time
0
and
T
of the solution of the previous SDE started at its equilibrium measure
\mathfrak{m}
and denote its density by
{\sf p}_{T}=\frac{\mathrm{d}\mathrm{R}_{0,T}}{\mathrm{d}(\mathfrak{m}\otimes\mathfrak{m})}
. Then, for any two probability measures
\mu,\,\nu\in\mathcal{P}(M)
the Schrödinger problem and the Schrödinger cost are respectively defined as the following variational problem and its optimal value

(SP)

\mathcal{C}_{T}(\mu,\nu)\coloneqq\inf_{\pi\in\Pi(\mu,\nu)}\mathcal{H}(\pi|\mathrm{R}_{0,T})\,.

As we have already mentioned before, one of the reasons why SP has become more and more popular nowadays is that it can be seen as an entropic regularization for the Optimal Transport problem. This has been particularly studied in the more natural setting of EOT, whose abstract formulation reads as follows

(
\varepsilon
EOT)

\mathcal{S}^{\varepsilon}(\mu,\nu)\coloneqq\inf_{\pi\in\Pi(\mu,\nu)}\int c(x,y)\,\mathrm{d}\pi+\varepsilon\,\mathcal{H}(\pi|\mu\otimes\nu)\,,\qquad\varepsilon>0\,,

for any given cost function
c\in L^{1}(\mu\otimes\nu)
and noisy regularisation parameter
\varepsilon>0
.
We refer the reader to the lecture notes
[
40
]
for an extensive introduction to abstract EOT and its connections with SP.
The above problem admits a unique optimizer
{\pi}^{{\mu},{\nu}}_{\varepsilon}\in\Pi(\mu,\nu)
and its density is of the form

(1.3)

\frac{\mathrm{d}{\pi}^{{\mu},{\nu}}_{\varepsilon}}{\mathrm{d}(\mu\otimes\nu)}=\exp\bigg(\frac{\varphi_{\varepsilon}\oplus\psi_{\varepsilon}-c}{\varepsilon}\bigg)\qquad\mu\otimes\nu\text{-a.s.}

where
(\varphi_{\varepsilon}\oplus\psi_{\varepsilon})(x,y):=\varphi_{\varepsilon}(x)+\psi_{\varepsilon}(y)
, for two measurable functions
\varphi_{\varepsilon}\in L^{1}(\mu)
and
\psi_{\varepsilon}\in L^{1}(\nu)
, unique up to the trivial transformation
(\varphi_{\varepsilon},\psi_{\varepsilon})\mapsto(\varphi_{\varepsilon}+c,\psi_{\varepsilon}-c)
with
c\in\mathbb{R}
, whom we will refer to as the (entropic) potentials.

Related to SP and the EOT problem above, respectively taking the small-time and small-noise limit, is the dual formulation of the Monge-Kantorovich problem and the associated potentials. That is,

(1.4)

\frac{1}{4}\,\mathcal{W}_{2}^{2}(\mu,\nu)=\sup_{\varphi\in L^{1}(\mu),\,\psi\in L^{1}(\nu)\,\colon\varphi\oplus\psi\leq\frac{1}{4}{\sf d}^{2}(\cdot,\cdot)}\left(\int\varphi\,\mathrm{d}\mu+\int\psi\,\mathrm{d}\nu\right)\,,

where
\mathcal{W}_{2}^{2}(\mu,\nu)
denotes the Wasserstein distance of order two. Two functions
(\varphi_{0},\psi_{0})
forming an optimal dual pair are called Kantorovich potentials.

As concerns the ambient space, throughout the paper we are going to assume that
(M,\,{\sf d},\,\mathfrak{m})
satisfies a curvature-dimension condition, i.e. either one of the following holds

(CD)

\blacktriangleright

(M,\,{\sf d},\,\mathfrak{m})
satisfies
{\sf CD}(\kappa,N)
for some
\kappa\in\mathbb{R}
and
N<+{\infty}
, or

\blacktriangleright

(M,\,{\sf d},\,\mathfrak{m})
satisfies
{\sf CD}(\kappa,{\infty})
for some
\kappa\in\mathbb{R}
and
\mathfrak{m}(M)=1
,

For the reader who is not familiar with the Bakry-Émery theory of lower Ricci bounds for diffusion Markov semigroups, beside suggesting the monograph
[
5
]
for the precise definitions of the
{\sf CD}(\kappa,N)
and
{\sf CD}(\kappa,{\infty})
conditions, we would like to give a brief explanation. Recalling that
\mathfrak{m}:=e^{-U}{\rm vol}
, the constant
\kappa
plays the role of a lower bound on the Bakry-Émery Ricci curvature tensor
{\rm Ric}_{U}:={\rm Ric}_{g}+{\rm Hess}(U)
, while
N
has to be seen as an upper bound on the
effective
dimension of the generator induced by (
1.2
). In a more compact way,
{\sf CD}(\kappa,N)
holds if and only if

{\rm Ric}_{U}-\frac{\nabla U\otimes\nabla U}{N-n}\geq\kappa g,\qquad n:=\dim(M).

For instance, if
M
is endowed with
\mathfrak{m}={\rm vol}
and has Ricci curvature bounded from below by
\kappa
, then the
{\sf CD}(\kappa,N)
condition holds with
N=\dim(M)
, as in this case
U=0
and the Bakry-Émery Ricci curvature tensor coincides with the classical one, while the generator induced by (
1.2
) coincides with the Laplace-Beltrami operator. As an example of
{\sf CD}(\kappa,{\infty})
space, instead, one should think of
\mathbb{R}^{d}
endowed with the standard Gaussian
\gamma_{d}
. In this case,
{\sf CD}(1,{\infty})
holds but no
{\sf CD}(\kappa,N)
for
\kappa\in\mathbb{R}
can be satisfied (for instance, because
{\sf CD}(\kappa,N)
implies that
\mathfrak{m}
is locally doubling, which is not the case for
\gamma_{n}
). This shows why
N
is not a topological dimension but is instead related to the generator induced by (
1.2
), which in this case is the Witten Laplacian
\Delta_{g}-\nabla U\cdot\nabla
.

As concerns the marginals, we are going to assume that they satisfy

(H1)

\mu,\,\nu\in\mathcal{P}_{2}(M)\,\quad\text{and}\quad\,\mathcal{H}(\mu|\mathfrak{m}),\,\mathcal{H}(\nu|\mathfrak{m})<+{\infty}\,;

and that either

(H2)

\blacktriangleright

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{d}\mu}{\mathrm{d}\mathfrak{m}},\frac{\mathrm{d}\nu}{\mathrm{d}\mathfrak{m}}\in L^{\infty}(\mathfrak{m})
and are compactly supported, or

\blacktriangleright

for
\mathfrak{p}=\mu,\nu
,
\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{d}\mathfrak{p}}{\mathrm{d}\mathfrak{m}}
is locally bounded away from zero on
{\rm int}(\mathrm{supp}(\mathfrak{p}))
and
\mathfrak{p}(\partial\mathrm{supp}(\mathfrak{p}))=0
.

The curvature-dimension condition and (
H1
) imply (cf. Proposition
2.2
below) that SP admits a unique minimizer, which we will refer to as the Schrödinger optimal plan
{\pi}^{{\mu}\to{\nu},{T}}\in\Pi(\mu,\nu)
, and that there exist two measurable functions
\varphi=\varphi^{T},\,\psi=\psi^{T}
, known as the
Schrödinger potentials
and unique up to the trivial transformation
(\varphi,\psi)\mapsto(\varphi+c,\psi-c)
with
c\in\mathbb{R}
, such that

(1.5)

\frac{\mathrm{d}{\pi}^{{\mu}\to{\nu},{T}}}{\mathrm{d}\mathrm{R}_{0,T}}=\,e^{\varphi\oplus\psi}\qquad\mathrm{R}_{0,T}\textrm{ -a.s.}

Equivalently, there exist two non-negative measurable functions
f,g
, unique up to the trivial transformation
(f,g)\mapsto(cf,c^{-1}g)
with
c>0
, such that

(1.6)

\frac{\mathrm{d}{\pi}^{{\mu}\to{\nu},{T}}}{\mathrm{d}\mathrm{R}_{0,T}}=\,f\otimes g\qquad\mathrm{R}_{0,T}\textrm{ -a.s.}

where
(f\otimes g)(x,y):=f(x)g(y)
. The pair
(f,g)
is known in the literature under the name of
fg
-decomposition
.

We are going to show (cf. Proposition
2.4
below) that, solely under assumptions (
H1
) and (
H2
), the curvature-dimension condition entails the following estimates for the gradients of the Schrödinger potentials

(1.7)

\left\lVert\nabla\varphi_{T}^{T}\right\rVert_{L^{2}(\nu)}^{2}\leq\frac{1}{E_{2\kappa}(T)}\biggl[\mathcal{C}_{T}(\mu,\nu)-\mathcal{H}(\nu|\mathfrak{m})\biggr],\quad\left\lVert\nabla\psi_{0}^{T}\right\rVert_{L^{2}(\mu)}^{2}\leq\frac{1}{E_{2\kappa}(T)}\biggl[\mathcal{C}_{T}(\mu,\nu)-\mathcal{H}(\mu|\mathfrak{m})\biggr]

where
\psi_{0}^{T}\coloneqq\log P_{T}e^{\psi}
,
\varphi^{T}_{T}\coloneqq\log P_{T}e^{\varphi}
,
P_{T}
denotes the semigroup associated to the SDE (
1.2
) (defined for any non-negative measurable function
u
as the conditional expected value
P_{t}u(x)\coloneqq\mathbb{E}[u(X_{t})|X_{0}=x]
, cf.
[
5
]
) and where
E_{2\kappa}
is defined as

(1.8)

E_{2\kappa}(t)\coloneqq\int_{0}^{t}e^{2\kappa s}\mathrm{d}s.

We refer to this type of bounds as
corrector estimates
. Indeed, it is immediate to observe that
\varphi^{T}_{t}:=\log P_{t}e^{\varphi}
and
\psi^{T}_{t}:=\log P_{T-t}e^{\psi}
solve the following Hamilton-Jacobi-Bellman (HJB) equations

\begin{cases}\partial_{t}\psi^{T}_{t}+|\nabla\psi^{T}_{t}|^{2}+\Delta\psi^{T}_{t}=0\\
\psi^{T}_{T}=\psi\end{cases}\qquad\textrm{and}\qquad\begin{cases}\partial_{t}\varphi^{T}_{t}-|\nabla\varphi^{T}_{t}|^{2}-\Delta\varphi^{T}_{t}=0\\
\varphi^{T}_{0}=\varphi\end{cases}

on
[0,T]\times M
. The former (up to the scaling parameter
1/2
) corresponds to (
1.1
) and its validity comes from the fact that
\nabla\psi^{T}_{t}
is the optimal corrector in the stochastic optimal control formulation. The second HJB equation can be seen as a time-reversal of the former, and indeed
\nabla\varphi^{T}_{t}
provides the optimal corrector for a time-reversed SP. For this reason
\nabla\psi^{T}_{t}
and
\nabla\varphi^{T}_{t}
are also usually referred to respectively as
forward
and
backward
corrector. Let us further observe that above we consider the same semigroup
(P_{t})_{t\in[0,T]}
for both the forward and backward corrector since the underlying dynamics (
1.2
) is reversible in time.

The aim of this paper is showing that the curvature-dimension condition (
CD
) and the corrector estimates (
1.7
) allow to tackle two different aspects of the Schrödinger problem. On the one hand, they will be instrumental to show the convergence of the gradients of the potentials in the small-time limit; on the other hand, they will lead to new stability estimates for SP.

1.1.
Convergence of the gradients of the Schrödinger potentials

A cornerstone result in (quadratic) optimal transport
[
9
]
states (actually under weaker assumptions than the standing ones on
\mu,\nu
) the existence of a measurable function
\tau\colon M\to M
such that
\tau_{\#}\mu=\nu
and

(1.9)

\int{\sf d}^{2}(x,\tau(x))\,\mathrm{d}\mu=\mathcal{W}_{2}^{2}(\mu,\nu)=\inf_{\pi\in\Pi(\mu,\nu)}\int{\sf d}^{2}(x,y)\,\mathrm{d}\pi\,.

The function
\tau
is called the
Brenier map
from
\mu
to
\nu
. After
[
22
, Proposition 3.1]
,
[
23
, Theorem 1.1]
(see also the discussion therein) it is well known that
\tau=\operatorname{Id}-2\nabla\varphi^{0}
, where
\varphi^{0}
is a Kantorovich potential associated to (
1.4
). Our first main contribution is the strong convergence of the rescaled
gradients
of the Schrödinger potentials to
\nabla\varphi^{0}
, which is uniquely determined although
\varphi^{0}
is not, and thus the
Schrödinger map

{\rm Id}-2T\nabla\varphi^{T}
from
\mu
to
\nu
to the Brenier map from
\mu
to
\nu
. To state the result, we introduce the Fisher information, which is defined for any probability measure
\mathfrak{p}\ll\mathfrak{m}
as the quantity

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}(\mathfrak{p})\coloneqq\begin{cases}\left\lVert\nabla\log\frac{\mathrm{d}\mathfrak{p}}{\mathrm{d}\mathfrak{m}}\right\rVert_{L^{2}(\mathfrak{p})}^{2}\quad&\text{if }\nabla\log\frac{\mathrm{d}\mathfrak{p}}{\mathrm{d}\mathfrak{m}}\in L^{2}(\mathfrak{p})\,,\\
+{\infty}\quad&\text{otherwise.}\end{cases}

Theorem 1.1
.

Suppose
(M,{\sf d},\mathfrak{m})
satisfies (
CD
) and that (
H1
) holds true. If
\frac{\mathrm{d}\mu}{\mathrm{d}\mathfrak{m}}
is locally bounded away from
0
on
{\rm int}(\mathrm{supp}(\mu))
, if
\mu(\partial\,\mathrm{supp}(\mu))=0
and if the Fisher information
\mathcal{I}(\mu)
is finite, then the Schrödinger map from
\mu
to
\nu
converges strongly in
L^{2}(\mu)
to the Brenier map from
\mu
to
\nu
in the small-time limit. Equivalently, there are
(\varphi^{0},\psi^{0})
Kantorovich potentials such that as
T\downarrow 0

T\nabla\varphi^{T}\,\to\,\nabla\varphi^{0}\quad\text{strongly in }L^{2}(\mu)\,.

A similar result holds for
T\nabla\psi^{T}
under the corresponding assumptions on
\nu
.

Literature review

For both SP and EOT the small-time and small-noise limits have been extensively studied in the literature. For what concerns the convergence of the rescaled entropic cost to the squared Wasserstein distance, the first result is that of
[
38
]
, eventually generalized in
[
35
]
.
As a further step in the analysis of this convergence, in
[
1
]
(in dimension one), in
[
21
]
(for the multi-dimensional case) and most recently in
[
43
]
, the first-order Taylor expansion of the entropic cost has been investigated showing that the first-order coefficient is given by the sum of the relative entropies of the marginals. Under stronger assumptions on the marginals, the second and fourth authors determined in
[
16
]
the second-order Taylor expansion of the entropic cost showing that the second-order term is related to the average of the Fisher information along the geodesic between the marginals (an analogous result has also been obtained slightly later in
[
14
]
).
Most recently, in
[
11
]
the fourth author and his coauthors analyze the small-noise limit in the EOT setting under very general assumptions on the cost function
c
(which allow for non-uniqueness of the optimal transport coupling for instance).

Alongside this line of work, in
[
38
]
and
[
8
]
it is shown that in the small-time and small-noise limits the optimal solutions to SP and EOT respectively converge to the optimal coupling of the OT problem. When it comes to the convergence of dual optimizers,
in the EOT setting
it is proven in
[
41
]
that the entropic potentials
(\varphi_{\varepsilon},\psi_{\varepsilon})
converge to the Kantorovich potentials (when the latter are unique) associated to the Monge-Kantorovich problem with cost
c

\sup_{\varphi\in L^{1}(\mu),\,\psi\in L^{1}(\nu)\,\colon\varphi\oplus\psi\leq c}\left(\int\varphi\,\mathrm{d}\mu+\int\psi\,\mathrm{d}\nu\right)\,.

More precisely, in
[
41
]
the authors prove in the small-noise limit
\varepsilon\downarrow 0
that the sequences
\{\varphi_{\varepsilon}\}_{\varepsilon>0}
and
\{\psi_{\varepsilon}\}_{\varepsilon>0}
are (strongly) compact in
L^{1}(\mu)
and
L^{1}(\nu)
respectively, and that their accumulation points are optimizers of the above Monge-Kantorovich problem. Adapting their proof strategy and relying on
[
39
]
, we show under a curvature-dimension condition a similar convergence statement for SP, i.e. that the Schrödinger potentials
(T\,\varphi^{T},\,T\,\psi^{T})
, as defined in (
1.5
),
L^{1}
-strongly converge (up to restricting to a subsequence) to a pair of Kantorovich potentials
(\varphi^{0},\psi^{0})

1.2.
Quantitative stability in terms of a weighted homogeneous Sobolev norm

As a second main contribution, we are interested in explicitly quantifying how
sensitive are the optimal costs and optimal plans in SP to variations of the marginal constraints.
In this respect, we show (cf. Theorem
1.2
) that the curvature-dimension condition implies a rather general and explicit stability result in terms of the symmetric relative entropy, i.e.

\mathcal{H}^{\mathrm{sym}}(\mu,\bar{\mu})\coloneqq\mathcal{H}(\mu|\bar{\mu})+\mathcal{H}(\bar{\mu}|\mu)\,,

and in terms of a negative-order weighted homogeneous Sobolev norm, which is defined for any signed measure
\nu
as follows:

\left\lVert\nu\right\rVert_{\dot{H}^{-1}(\mu)}\coloneqq\sup\biggl\{\left\lvert\langle h,\nu\rangle\right\rvert\,:\left\lVert h\right\rVert_{\dot{H}^{1}(\mu)}\leq 1\biggr\}\,,\qquad\text{where }\left\lVert h\right\rVert_{\dot{H}^{1}(\mu)}^{2}\coloneqq\int\left\lvert\nabla h\right\rvert^{2}\mathrm{d}\mu\,.

This dual norm on the space of signed measures encodes the linearized behavior of the Wasserstein distance
\mathcal{W}_{2}
for infinitesimal perturbations (see
[
45
]
and references therein). For instance, if
\mu\in\mathcal{P}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
,
\mu\ll\mathcal{L}_{d}
and
\bar{\mu}^{\varepsilon}=(1+\varepsilon h)\mu
for some
h\in L^{\infty}(\mu)
with
\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}h\mathrm{d}\mu=0
, then
[
51
, Theorem 7.26]
implies

\left\lVert\mu-\bar{\mu}^{\varepsilon}\right\rVert_{\dot{H}^{-1}(\mu)}=\varepsilon\left\lVert h\,\mu\right\rVert_{\dot{H}^{-1}(\mu)}\quad\text{and}\quad\left\lVert h\,\mu\right\rVert_{\dot{H}^{-1}(\mu)}\leq\liminf_{\varepsilon\to 0}\frac{\mathcal{W}_{2}(\mu,\bar{\mu}^{\varepsilon})}{\varepsilon}\,.

Moreover,
[
45
]
provides non-asymptotic comparisons between this Sobolev norm and the Wasserstein distance. In particular, it always holds
\mathcal{W}_{2}(\mu,\bar{\mu})\leq 2\left\lVert\mu-\bar{\mu}\right\rVert_{\dot{H}^{-1}(\mu)}
, and when
(M,{\sf d},{\rm vol})
has non-negative Ricci curvature and if the densities
\frac{\mathrm{d}\mu}{\mathrm{d}{\rm vol}}
and
\frac{\mathrm{d}\bar{\mu}}{\mathrm{d}{\rm vol}}
are bounded away from
0
and
{\infty}
, the norm
\left\lVert\mu-\bar{\mu}\right\rVert_{\dot{H}^{-1}(\mu)}
is equivalent to the Wasserstein distance
\mathcal{W}_{2}(\mu,\bar{\mu})
.

Apart from the curvature-dimension condition (
CD
) and from (
H1
) and (
H2
), we assume the following integrability condition on the reference measure:

(I)

\exists\,r>0\,\colon\quad\int\mathfrak{m}\left(B_{\sqrt{T}}(x)\right)^{r\,T}e^{r\,{\sf d}^{2}(x,z)}\mathrm{d}\mathfrak{m}(x)<+{\infty}\quad\forall z\in M\,.

The reason why we assume the above condition is that our computations will heavily rely on integrating the Schrödinger potentials of one problem against the marginal constraints of the other, which requires enough integrability for the former.

Notice that if
\mathfrak{m}
is a probability measure, the previous assumption is met as soon as
e^{r\,{\sf d}^{2}(x,z_{0})}\in L^{1}(\mathfrak{m})
for some
r>0
and

, which is true for instance under a positive curvature condition

with

and then by means of Herbst’s argument

is a probability measure the above condition (

1.2.1.
Literature review

Recently, there has been an increasing interest in the quantitative stability for the EOT problem, which is strongly linked to SP. A first stability estimate for EOT in a general setting has been established in
[
26
]
. There, the authors, without any integrability assumption, manage to prove a qualitative stability result by relying on a geometric notion inspired by the cyclical monotonicity property in Optimal Transport.
To the best of our knowledge, the first quantitative stability result is due to Carlier and Laborde in
[
10
]
in the context of multi-marginal EOT. By considering bounded marginals equivalent to a common reference probability measure and a bounded cost, they show that the potentials are Lipschitz-continuous in
L^{2}
and
L^{\infty}
w.r.t. the densities of the marginals. As far as concerns quantitative stability for the primal optimizers, the first result appeared in the work
[
19
]
. There the authors prove on compact metric spaces a quantitative uniform stability along Sinkhorn’s algorithm which implies stability for EOT and more precisely an explicit
\mathcal{W}_{1}
-Lipschitzianity of the optimizers w.r.t. the marginals. More recently, in
[
20
]
a quantitative stability result that holds on general metric spaces is shown, thus removing the compactness assumption at the cost of requiring some exponential integrability with respect to the marginals (condition met for instance when the marginals are sub-Gaussians). More precisely the authors prove a
\frac{1}{2p}
-Hölder continuity for
\mathcal{W}_{p}
provided that the cost function satisfies an abstract condition, introduced there in order to bound the optimal values of EOT by means of the Wasserstein distance between the corresponding optimizers.
Such condition is met for a wide enough class of cost functions such as
c(x,y)=\left\lvert x-y\right\rvert^{p}
on the Euclidean space, with
p\in(1,{\infty})
. Nevertheless, it is not easily verifiable in general, and in particular when considering the case
c=-T\,\log{\sf p}_{T}
(which allows to translate results from EOT into results for SP). Lastly, it is worth mentioning that in the most recent
[
42
]
the authors study the qualitative stability of the Schrödinger potentials associated to EOT in a general setting assuming the cost function
c
satisfies
e^{\beta c}\in L^{1}(\mu\otimes\nu)
for some
\beta>0
, which leads to the convergence of Sinkhorn’s algorithm. The (
CD
) condition does not have a natural counterpart in the EOT setting, where the results we have just discussed have been established; for this reason our stability result is not implied by any of the stability bounds mentioned above.

Remark 1.3

(On the integrability condition (
I
))
.

The condition

e^{r\,{\sf d}^{2}(x,z_{0})}\in L^{1}(\mathfrak{m})\quad\text{for some }r>0\text{ and }z_{0}\in M,\quad\mathfrak{m}(M)=1

should be compared with (6.8) and (6.9) in

As we have already mentioned before, in the most recent work

Remark 1.5

(Sharpness in the long-time regime)
.

Under a non-negative curvature condition we know that in the long-time limit

[

2.
Preliminaries

This section is divided in two halves. In the first part, after recalling the setting, auxiliary results, and introducing further notation, in Proposition
2.2
we prove the existence of the
fg
-decomposition and of the Schrödinger potentials. The second half is dedicated to the proof of the corrector estimates (
1.7
) which is given in Proposition
2.4
.

2.1.
Setting

We start by recalling the assumptions on the triplet
(M,{\sf d},\mathfrak{m})
that will hold true throughout the whole paper:
M
is a smooth, connected, complete (possibly non-compact) Riemannian manifold without boundary and with metric tensor
g
;
{\sf d}
is the distance induced by
g
;
\mathfrak{m}
denotes the
\sigma
-finite measure
\mathrm{d}\mathfrak{m}(x)=e^{-U(x)}{\rm vol}(\mathrm{d}x)
, where
{\rm vol}
is the volume measure of
M
and
U
is a
C^{2}
potential. We denote by
\mathcal{P}_{p}(M)
,
p\geq 1
, the set of probability measures on
M
with finite
p
-moment, that is such that for a fixed
z_{0}\in M
it holds

M_{p}(\mathfrak{p})\coloneqq\int{\sf d}^{p}(y,z_{0})\mathrm{d}\mathfrak{p}(y)<+{\infty}\,.

Let us also notice that
\mathcal{P}_{2}(M)\subseteq\mathcal{P}_{1}(M)
and in particular
M_{1}(\mathfrak{p})\leq 1+M_{2}(\mathfrak{p})
for any
\mathfrak{p}\in\mathcal{P}_{2}(M)
.

The curvature assumption (
CD
) will also be always satisfied by the triplet
(M,{\sf d},\mathfrak{m})
and it yields many important consequences, both analytical and geometric. First of all, the heat kernel
{\sf p}_{t}=\frac{\mathrm{d}\mathrm{R}_{0,t}}{\mathrm{d}(\mathfrak{m}\otimes\mathfrak{m})}
enjoys a Gaussian lower bound. More precisely, under the
{\sf CD}(\kappa,N)
condition with
N<\infty
, from
[
32
, Theorem 1.1 and Theorem 1.2]
we know that for every
\delta>0
there exist
C_{1},C_{2}
depending only on
\kappa,N,\delta
such that

(2.1)

{\sf p}_{t}(x,y)\geq\frac{1}{C_{1}\mathfrak{m}(B_{\sqrt{t}}(x))}\exp\Big(-\frac{{\sf d}^{2}(x,y)}{(4-\delta)t}-C_{2}t\Big)

for all
x,y\in M
and all
t>0
. On the other hand, if we assume that
(M,{\sf d},\mathfrak{m})
satisfies
{\sf CD}(\kappa,{\infty})
with
\mathfrak{m}(M)=1
, then by
[
52
, Corollary 1.3]
it holds

(2.2)

{\sf p}_{t}(x,y)\geq\exp\Big(-\frac{\kappa{\sf d}^{2}(x,y)}{2(1-e^{-\kappa t})}\Big),\qquad\forall x,y\in M,\,\forall t>0.

As a further consequence of the
{\sf CD}(\kappa,\infty)
condition, the semigroup
P_{t}
satisfies the
L^{\infty}
-Lipschitz regularization
[
3
, Theorem 6.8]

(2.3)

\sqrt{2E_{2\kappa}(t)}{\rm Lip}(P_{t}u)\leq\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathfrak{m})},\qquad\forall t>0,\,u\in L^{\infty}(\mathfrak{m}),

where
E_{2\kappa}
is defined as in (
1.8
). Let us recall that also Hamilton’s gradient estimate
[
31
,
34
]
(see also
[
33
]
) holds true under
{\sf CD}(\kappa,\infty)
: for any positive
u\in L^{p}\cap L^{\infty}(\mathfrak{m})
for some
p\in[1,\infty)
it holds

(2.4)

t|\nabla\log P_{t}u|^{2}\leq(1+2\kappa^{-}t)\log\Big(\frac{\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathfrak{m})}}{P_{t}u}\Big).

Finally, let us recall that the
{\sf CD}(\kappa,N)
condition with
N<\infty
entails the Bishop-Gromov inequality (see for instance
[
49
, Theorem 2.3]
): for any
x\in\mathrm{supp}(\mathfrak{m})=M
and
0<r\leq R\leq\pi\sqrt{(N-1)/\kappa^{+}}
, where
(N-1)/\kappa^{+}=+\infty
if
\kappa\leq 0
, it holds

(2.5)

\frac{\mathfrak{m}(B_{r}(x))}{\mathfrak{m}(B_{R}(x))}\geq\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\displaystyle{\frac{\int_{0}^{r}\sin(t\sqrt{\kappa/(N-1)})^{N-1}\mathrm{d}t}{\int_{0}^{R}\sin(t\sqrt{\kappa/(N-1)})^{N-1}\mathrm{d}t}}\,,&\qquad\text{if }\kappa>0\,,\\
\\
\bigg(\displaystyle{\frac{r}{R}}\bigg)^{N}\,,&\qquad\text{if }\kappa=0\,,\\
\\
\displaystyle{\frac{\int_{0}^{r}\sinh(t\sqrt{-\kappa/(N-1)})^{N-1}\mathrm{d}t}{\int_{0}^{R}\sinh(t\sqrt{-\kappa/(N-1)})^{N-1}\mathrm{d}t}}\,,&\qquad\text{if }\kappa<0\,.\end{array}\right.

As a consequence of (
2.5
), in the following lemma we point out a technical integrability property of the reference measure
\mathfrak{m}
that in Proposition
2.2
will guarantee the existence and uniqueness of the solution to SP as well as the existence of the
fg
-decomposition.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
(M,{\sf d},\mathfrak{m})
satisfy
{\sf CD}(\kappa,N)
with
N<+{\infty}
. Then for any
r>0
it holds

\left(\log\mathfrak{m}\left(B_{r}(\cdot)\right)\right)^{+}\in L^{1}(\mu),\quad\forall\mu\in\mathcal{P}_{1}(M)\,.

Proof.

Assume
\kappa<0
. If we fix
\bar{x}\in M
and observe that
B_{r}(x)\subset B_{{\sf d}(x,\bar{x})+r}(\bar{x})
, then by (
2.5
)

\mathfrak{m}(B_{r}(x))\leq C_{1}\mathfrak{m}(B_{r}(\bar{x}))\int_{0}^{{\sf d}(x,\bar{x})+r}\sinh(t\sqrt{-\kappa/(N-1)})^{N-1}\mathrm{d}t\leq C_{2}\,e^{C_{3}{\sf d}(x,\bar{x})},

where
C_{1},C_{3}
only depend on
\kappa,N,r
and
C_{2}
also depends on
\bar{x}
. As a consequence,

(2.6)

\log\mathfrak{m}(B_{r}(x))\leq\log C_{2}+C_{3}{\sf d}(x,\bar{x})

and therefore
\left(\log\mathfrak{m}\left(B_{r}(\cdot)\right)\right)^{+}\in L^{1}(\mu)
for any
\mu\in\mathcal{P}_{1}(M)
.
In the case
\kappa\geq 0
, the argument works verbatim, the only difference being in the application of the Bishop-Gromov inequality.
∎

At this stage, let us also recall the definition of the relative entropy when the reference
\mathfrak{m}
is not a probability measure (see
[
17
, Section 2]
and
[
37
, Appendix A]
for more details). As
\mathfrak{m}
is
\sigma
-finite, there exists a measurable function
W\colon M\to[0,+{\infty})
such that

z_{W}\coloneqq\int e^{-W}\,\mathrm{d}\mathfrak{m}<+{\infty}\,.

Then, for any
\mathfrak{p}\in\mathcal{P}(M)
such that
W\in L^{1}(\mathfrak{p})
we can define the relative entropy with respect to
\mathfrak{m}
as

(2.7)

\mathcal{H}(\mathfrak{p}|\mathfrak{m})\coloneqq\mathcal{H}(\mathfrak{p}|\mathfrak{m}_{W})-\int W\,\mathrm{d}\mathfrak{p}-\log z_{W}\,,

where
\mathfrak{m}_{W}\coloneqq z_{W}^{-1}\,e^{-W}\,\mathfrak{m}\in\mathcal{P}(M)
and
\mathcal{H}(\mathfrak{p}|\mathfrak{m}_{W})
is defined via the standard definition of relative entropy between probabilities, given in the introduction. Moreover, it is immediate to see that the above definition is independent of the choice of the measurable function
W\colon M\to[0,+{\infty})
satisfying
z_{W}<+{\infty}
. In particular, thanks to
[
48
, Theorem 4.24]
,
W
can be chosen as
C{\sf d}^{2}(\cdot,x_{0})
for any
x_{0}\in M
and
C>0
sufficiently large, whence the lower semicontinuity of
\mathcal{H}(\cdot|\mathfrak{m})
w.r.t.
W_{2}
-convergence.
We are now ready to prove the main result of this subsection, about the existence of an
fg
-decomposition as in (
1.6
) for the unique minimizer in (
SP
).

Proposition 2.2
.

Let
(M,{\sf d},\mathfrak{m})
satisfy (
CD
) and suppose the marginals
\mu,\,\nu
satisfy (
H1
). Then (
SP
) admits a unique minimizer
{\pi}^{{\mu}\to{\nu},{T}}\in\Pi(\mu,\nu)
and there are two non-negative measurable functions
f,\,g
satisfying (
1.6
), i.e.,

\frac{\mathrm{d}{\pi}^{{\mu}\to{\nu},{T}}}{\mathrm{d}\mathrm{R}_{0,T}}(x,y)=f(x)\,g(y)\quad\mathrm{R}_{0,T}\text{-a.s.}

They are
\mathfrak{m}
-a.e. unique up to the trivial transformation
(f,g)\mapsto(cf,g/c)
for some
c>0

and moreover
\varphi=\log f\in L^{1}(\mu)
,
\psi=\log g\in L^{1}(\nu)
. Finally,
f
and
g
satisfy the Schrödinger system

(2.8)

\begin{cases}\mu=f\,P_{T}g\,\mathfrak{m}\,,\\
\nu=P_{T}f\,g\,\mathfrak{m}\,.\end{cases}

Since the
fg
-decomposition is unique up to a scalar multiplicative factor, through the whole paper we are going to assume the following symmetric normalization

(2.9)

\displaystyle\int\log f\,\mathrm{d}\mu-\mathcal{H}(\mu|\mathfrak{m})=

\displaystyle\int\log g\,\mathrm{d}\nu-\mathcal{H}(\nu|\mathfrak{m})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\left[\mathcal{C}_{T}(\mu,\nu)-\mathcal{H}(\mu|\mathfrak{m})-\mathcal{H}(\nu|\mathfrak{m})\right]=\frac{\mathcal{S}_{T}(\mu,\nu)}{2T}\,.

Proof.

The existence and uniqueness of
{\pi}^{{\mu}\to{\nu},{T}}\in\Pi(\mu,\nu)
and
f,g
, as well as the validity of the Schrödinger system (
2.8
) follow from
[
28
, Proposition 2.1]
once we establish that
\mathcal{H}(\mu\otimes\nu|\mathrm{R}_{0,T})
is finite.
In order to prove that, let us derive a lower bound for
\log{\sf p}_{T}
.
First, assume that
(M,\,{\sf d},\,\mathfrak{m})
satisfies
{\sf CD}(\kappa,{\infty})
for some
\kappa\in\mathbb{R}
and
\mathfrak{m}(M)=1
. From (
2.2
) we get the lower bound

(2.10)

\log{\sf p}_{T}(x,y)\geq-\frac{\kappa\,{\sf d}^{2}(x,y)}{2(e^{\kappa\,T}-1)}\,,

which, combined with the trivial inequalities
{\sf d}(x,y)^{2}\leq 2({\sf d}^{2}(x,z)+{\sf d}^{2}(y,z))
valid for any
z\in M
and
e^{\kappa\,T}-1\geq\kappa\,T
, implies

(2.11)

\log{\sf p}_{T}(x,y)\geq-\frac{{\sf d}^{2}(x,z)}{T}-\frac{{\sf d}^{2}(y,z)}{T}\,.

On the other hand, if
{\sf CD}(\kappa,N)
holds with
N<+{\infty}
, then from the heat kernel lower bound (
2.1
) we obtain

(2.12)

\log{\sf p}_{T}(x,y)\geq-\log\left[C_{1}\,\mathfrak{m}\left(B_{\sqrt{T}}(x)\right)\right]-C_{2}\,T-\frac{{\sf d}^{2}(x,z)}{T}-\frac{{\sf d}^{2}(y,z)}{T}\,.

In both cases, thanks to Assumption (
H1
) and Lemma
2.1
with
r=\sqrt{T}
we conclude that
-\int\log{\sf p}_{T}\,\mathrm{d}(\mu\otimes\nu)\in[-{\infty},+{\infty})
, which leads to the well-defined summation

(2.13)

\mathcal{H}(\mu\otimes\nu|\mathrm{R}_{0,T})=\mathcal{H}(\mu|\mathfrak{m})+\mathcal{H}(\nu|\mathfrak{m})-\int\log{\sf p}_{T}\,\mathrm{d}(\mu\otimes\nu)<+{\infty}\,.

Finally, by arguing as in (
2.13
), we know that
\mathcal{H}(\mu\otimes\nu|\mathrm{R}_{0,T}^{\mu})
is also finite, where
\mathrm{R}_{0,T}^{\mu}\in\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{R}^{2d})
is the probability measure defined as
\mathrm{R}_{0,T}^{\mu}(\mathrm{d}x,\mathrm{d}y)\coloneqq{\sf p}_{T}(x,y)\mu(\mathrm{d}x)\mathfrak{m}(\mathrm{d}y)
. Since for any
\pi\in\Pi(\mu,\nu)
it holds

\mathcal{H}(\pi|\mathrm{R}_{0,T})=\mathcal{H}(\mu|\mathfrak{m})+\mathcal{H}(\pi|\mathrm{R}_{0,T}^{\mu})\,,

clearly
{\pi}^{{\mu}\to{\nu},{T}}
minimizes
\mathcal{H}(\cdot|\mathrm{R}_{0,T}^{\mu})
as well, over the set of couplings
\Pi(\mu,\nu)
. Given the above premises, the integrability of
\varphi
and
\psi
follows from
[
40
, Corollary 1.13 and Remark 2.22]
applied to the independent coupling
\mu\otimes\nu
and to the probability reference
\mathrm{R}_{0,T}^{\mu}
.
∎

2.2.
Corrector estimates

We are going to prove the corrector estimates (
1.7
) via an approximation argument. First we will assume that the marginals have compact support and bounded densities, then we will extend it to the case in which
f,g\in L^{\infty}(\mathfrak{m})
, and finally to the case when the second condition in (
H2
) is satisfied by relying on a finite-dimensional approximation argument where the second marginal is fixed while the first marginal constraint is replaced by a finite-dimensional one. More precisely, since
(M,{\sf d})
is separable we know that there exists a countable dense family of bounded measurable functions
\{\phi_{i}\}_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
such that

(\mathrm{proj}_{x_{1}})_{\#}\pi=\mu\quad\Leftrightarrow\quad\int\phi_{i}(x)\mathrm{d}\pi(x,y)=\int\phi_{i}\mathrm{d}\mu\qquad\forall\,i\in\mathbb{N}\,.

Therefore for any fixed
K\in\mathbb{N}
we may define the convex and closed (in total variation) set

\mathcal{Q}^{\nu}_{K}\coloneqq\biggl\{\pi\in\mathcal{P}(M\times M)\,\colon(\mathrm{proj}_{x_{2}})_{\#}\pi=\nu\text{ and }\int\phi_{i}(x)\mathrm{d}\pi(x,y)=\int\phi_{i}\mathrm{d}\mu\quad\forall\,i=1,\dots,K\biggr\}

and then consider the associated minimization problem

(2.14)

\inf_{\pi\in\mathcal{Q}^{\nu}_{K}}\mathcal{H}(\pi|\mathrm{R}_{0,T})\,.

The next lemma, whose proof is postponed to the end of this section, shows that the above problem gives an approximation of SP.

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
(M,{\sf d},\mathfrak{m})
satisfy (
CD
) and suppose that the marginals
\mu,\,\nu
satisfy (
H1
). Then the above minimiziation problem (
2.14
) admits a unique optimizer
\pi^{K}\in\mathcal{Q}^{\nu}_{K}
, whose density is given by

(2.15)

\frac{\mathrm{d}\pi^{K}}{\mathrm{d}\mathrm{R}_{0,T}}(x,y)=f_{K}(x)\,g_{K}(y)\qquad\text{with }\,f_{K}=C\,\exp\left(\sum_{i=1}^{K}\lambda_{i}\,\phi_{i}\right)\in L^{\infty}(\mathfrak{m})

for some constant
C>0
and multipliers
\lambda_{i}\in\mathbb{R}
.
Moreover,
g_{K}
converges
\mathfrak{m}
-a.e. to
g
and
P_{T}f_{K}
converges
\mathfrak{m}
-a.e. to
P_{T}f
on
\mathrm{supp}(\nu)
. Finally, the optimal value
\mathcal{H}(\pi^{K}|\mathrm{R}_{0,T})
converges to
\mathcal{C}_{T}(\mu,\nu)
as
K\to\infty
.

Proposition 2.4
.

Let
(M,{\sf d},\mathfrak{m})
satisfy (
CD
), suppose the marginals
\mu,\,\nu
satisfy (
H1
) and let
f,\,g
be as in Proposition
2.2
and satisfying the normalization (
2.9
). Then, if
\frac{\mathrm{d}\nu}{\mathrm{d}\mathfrak{m}}
is locally bounded away from
0
on
{\rm int}(\mathrm{supp}(\nu))
and
\nu(\partial\mathrm{supp}(\nu))=0
it holds

(2.16)

\left\lVert\nabla\log P_{T}f\right\rVert_{L^{2}(\nu)}^{2}\leq\frac{1}{E_{2\kappa}(T)}\biggl[\mathcal{C}_{T}(\mu,\nu)-\mathcal{H}(\nu|\mathfrak{m})\biggr]\,,

where
E_{2\kappa}
is defined as in (
1.8
). In particular, it is part of the statement the fact that
\log P_{T}f\in W^{1,2}_{loc}({\rm int}(\mathrm{supp}(\nu)))
with
|\nabla\log P_{T}f|\in L^{2}(\nu)
.

Similarly, if
\frac{\mathrm{d}\mu}{\mathrm{d}\mathfrak{m}}
is locally bounded away from
0
on
{\rm int}(\mathrm{supp}(\mu))
and
\mu(\partial\mathrm{supp}(\mu))=0
, then
\log P_{T}g\in W^{1,2}_{loc}({\rm int}(\mathrm{supp}(\mu)))
with
|\nabla\log P_{T}g|\in L^{2}(\mu)
and it holds

(2.17)

\left\lVert\nabla\log P_{T}g\right\rVert_{L^{2}(\mu)}^{2}\leq\frac{1}{E_{2\kappa}(T)}\biggl[\mathcal{C}_{T}(\mu,\nu)-\mathcal{H}(\mu|\mathfrak{m})\biggr]\,.

In particular, both (
2.16
) and (
2.17
) hold true if the marginals satisfy (
H2
).

Proof.

We will only prove inequality (
2.16
), as (
2.17
) follows by completely analogous techniques.

As a preliminary step, let us assume that
\mu,\nu
have bounded supports and bounded densities
\frac{\mathrm{d}\mu}{\mathrm{d}\mathfrak{m}},\,\frac{\mathrm{d}\nu}{\mathrm{d}\mathfrak{m}}
. Under this extra hypothesis, it holds

(2.18)

\int\left\lvert\nabla\log P_{T}f\right\rvert^{2}\mathrm{d}\nu\leq e^{-2\kappa(T-t)}\int\left\lvert\nabla\log P_{t}f\right\rvert^{2}\mathrm{d}{\zeta}^{{\mu}\to{\nu,T}}_{t},

where
{\zeta}^{{\mu}\to{\nu,T}}_{t}=P_{t}f\,P_{T-t}g\,\mathfrak{m}
is the
T
-entropic interpolation from
\mu
to
\nu
at time
t
. If
(M,{\sf d},\mathfrak{m})
satisfies
{\sf CD}(\kappa,N)
with
N<\infty
, this is a consequence of Grönwall lemma applied to the functions
\alpha(t):=\int\left\lvert\nabla\log P_{t}f\right\rvert^{2}\mathrm{d}{\zeta}^{{\mu}\to{\nu,T}}_{t}
and
\beta(t):=e^{2\kappa(T-t)}\int\left\lvert\nabla\log P_{T}f\right\rvert^{2}\mathrm{d}\nu
, as on the one hand by
[
16
, Lemmas 3.6 and 3.7]
together with
[
28
, Proposition 4.8]
(which justifies the computations of
[
16
, Lemmas 3.6 and 3.7]
in the non-compact and possibly negatively curved setting) we have

(2.19)

\alpha\in C^{1}((0,T])\qquad\textrm{and}\qquad\alpha^{\prime}(t)\leq-2\kappa\alpha(t),\,\forall t\in(0,T]

while on the other hand it is readily verified that
\beta^{\prime}=-2\kappa\beta
; since
\alpha(T)=\beta(T)
, it must hold
\alpha(t)\geq\beta(t)
for all
t\in(0,T]
, namely (
2.18
).

If instead
(M,{\sf d},\mathfrak{m})
satisfies
{\sf CD}(\kappa,\infty)
and
\mathfrak{m}(M)=1
, the reader should refer to
[
17
, Lemma 2.2]
: this grants the validity of (
2.19
) for

\alpha_{\delta}(t):=c_{\delta}\int|\nabla\log(P_{t}f+\delta)|^{2}\,(P_{t}f+\delta)(P_{T-t}g+\delta)\,\mathrm{d}\mathfrak{m},

where
\delta>0
is any positive number and
c_{\delta}
is a normalization constant, so that
c_{\delta}(P_{t}f+\delta)(P_{T-t}g+\delta)\mathfrak{m}
is a probability measure. By considering
\beta_{\delta}(t):=e^{2\kappa(T-t)}\int\left\lvert\nabla\log(P_{T}f+\delta)\right\rvert^{2}(P_{T}f+\delta)g\,\mathrm{d}\mathfrak{m}
, by the same argument as before we obtain
\alpha_{\delta}(t)\geq\beta_{\delta}(t)
for all
t\in(0,T]
and
\delta>0
, whence (
2.18
) by passing to the limit as
\delta\downarrow 0
by the Dominated Convergence Theorem. Indeed, note first that

\begin{split}|\nabla\log(P_{t}f+\delta)|^{2}\,(P_{t}f+\delta)(P_{T-t}g+\delta)&=\frac{|\nabla P_{t}f|^{2}}{P_{t}f+\delta}(P_{T-t}g+\delta)\leq|\nabla\log P_{t}f|^{2}P_{t}f(P_{T-t}g+\delta)\\
&\leq C(\kappa,t)(P_{t}f)|\log P_{t}f|(P_{T-t}g+1)\end{split}

where the last inequality is due to Hamilton’s gradient estimate (
2.4
),
C(\kappa,t)
being some constant that only depends on
\kappa,t
. Then, thanks to the standing assumptions on
\mu,\nu
and the Gaussian heat kernel lower bound (
2.10
), by
[
28
, Proposition 2.1]
we know that
f,g\in L^{\infty}(\mathfrak{m})
. By the maximum principle this implies that the previous right-hand side is bounded, hence integrable as
\mathfrak{m}(M)=1
, and thus provides an admissible dominating function.

Now, by multiplying by
e^{2\kappa(T-t)}
and integrating over
t\in[0,T]
in (
2.18
) we deduce that

\begin{split}E_{2\kappa}(T)\int\left\lvert\nabla\log P_{T}f\right\rvert^{2}\mathrm{d}\nu&\leq\int_{0}^{T}\int\left\lvert\nabla\log P_{t}f\right\rvert^{2}\,\mathrm{d}{\zeta}^{{\mu}\to{\nu,T}}_{t}\mathrm{d}t=\mathcal{C}_{T}(\mu,\nu)-\mathcal{H}(\nu|\mathfrak{m})\,,\end{split}

where the identity is justified by the following representation of the entropic cost

(2.20)

\mathcal{C}_{T}(\mu,\nu)=\mathcal{H}(\nu|\mathfrak{m})+\int_{0}^{T}\int\left\lvert\nabla\log P_{t}f\right\rvert^{2}\,\mathrm{d}{\zeta}^{{\mu}\to{\nu,T}}_{t}\mathrm{d}t\,

(see
[
27
, Proposition 4.1]
paying attention to the different rescaling and Remark
2.5
below).
Inequality (
2.16
) is thus proved, as well as the last part of the statement.

Now let us remove the additional assumptions on
\mu,\nu
in a two-steps procedure.

1st step.
In addition to (
H1
), assume that
\mu,\,\nu
are such that the associated
f,g\in L^{\infty}(\mathfrak{m})
. Then fix
\bar{x}\in M
and introduce
f_{n}\coloneqq\mathds{1}_{B_{n}(\bar{x})}f
,
g_{n}\coloneqq\mathds{1}_{B_{n}(\bar{x})}g
, so that (
2.16
) holds true for

\mu_{n}\coloneqq c_{n}f_{n}P_{T}g_{n}\,\mathfrak{m}\qquad\textrm{and}\qquad\nu_{n}\coloneqq c_{n}g_{n}P_{T}f_{n}\,\mathfrak{m},

where
c_{n}
is a normalization constant (note that by self-adjointness of
P_{T}
it is the same for both measures
\mu_{n}
and
\nu_{n}
). Namely

\int\left\lvert\nabla\log P_{T}f_{n}\right\rvert^{2}\mathrm{d}\nu_{n}\leq\frac{1}{E_{2\kappa}(T)}\,\biggl[\mathcal{C}_{T}(\mu_{n},\nu_{n})-\mathcal{H}(\nu_{n}|\mathfrak{m})\biggr]\,.

Observing that
\left\lvert\nabla\log P_{T}f_{n}\right\rvert\geq\left\lvert\nabla\log(P_{T}f_{n}+\delta)\right\rvert
for any
\delta>0
, the inequality above implies in particular that

(2.21)

\int\left\lvert\nabla\log(P_{T}f_{n}+\delta)\right\rvert^{2}\mathrm{d}\nu_{n}\leq\frac{1}{E_{2\kappa}(T)}\,\biggl[\mathcal{C}_{T}(\mu_{n},\nu_{n})-\mathcal{H}(\nu_{n}|\mathfrak{m})\biggr]

for all
\delta>0
. Let us now pass to the limit as
n\to\infty
.

To this end, observe first that
P_{t}f_{n}\to P_{t}f
and
P_{T}g_{n}\to P_{T}g
pointwise as
n\to\infty
. Indeed

\begin{split}|P_{T}f_{n}(x)-P_{T}f(x)|&=\bigg|\int\big(\mathds{1}_{B_{n}(\bar{x})}(y)-1\big)f(y){\sf p}_{T}(x,y)\,\mathrm{d}\mathfrak{m}(y)\bigg|\\
&\leq\|f\|_{L^{\infty}}\int|\mathds{1}_{B_{n}(\bar{x})}(y)-1|{\sf p}_{T}(x,y)\,\mathrm{d}\mathfrak{m}(y)\end{split}

and the right-hand side vanishes as
n\to\infty
by dominated convergence. This allows to handle the right-hand side of (
2.21
) in the following way. As concerns the term
\mathcal{C}_{T}(\mu_{n},\nu_{n})
, on the one hand,

\begin{split}\int\log f_{n}\,\mathrm{d}\mu_{n}\leq\int\log f\,\mathrm{d}\mu_{n}&=\int(\log f)^{+}\,\mathrm{d}\mu_{n}-\int(\log f)^{-}\,\mathrm{d}\mu_{n}\\
&\leq c_{n}\int(\log f)^{+}\,\mathrm{d}\mu-\int(\log f)^{-}\,\mathrm{d}\mu_{n},\end{split}

where we have used
f_{n}\leq f
and, as a consequence of the maximum principle, also
P_{T}g_{n}\leq P_{T}g
. This implies that

\begin{split}\limsup_{n\to\infty}\int\log f_{n}\,\mathrm{d}\mu_{n}&\leq\int(\log f)^{+}\,\mathrm{d}\mu-\liminf_{n\to\infty}\int(\log f)^{-}\,\mathrm{d}\mu_{n}\\
&\leq\int(\log f)^{+}\,\mathrm{d}\mu-\int(\log f)^{-}\,\mathrm{d}\mu=\int\log f\,\mathrm{d}\mu,\end{split}

since
c_{n}\to 1
and
f_{n}\,P_{T}g_{n}\to f\,P_{T}g
pointwise (as already discussed before) together with Fatou lemma. On the other hand, one can show in an analogous fashion that

\limsup_{n\to\infty}\int\log g_{n}\,\mathrm{d}\nu_{n}\leq\int\log g\,\mathrm{d}\nu.

Since

\mathcal{C}_{T}(\mu_{n},\nu_{n})=\int\log f_{n}\,\mathrm{d}\mu_{n}+\int\log g_{n}\,\mathrm{d}\nu_{n}+\log c_{n},

we conclude that

(2.22)

\limsup_{n\to\infty}\mathcal{C}_{T}(\mu_{n},\nu_{n})\leq\mathcal{C}_{T}(\mu,\nu).

Secondly, as regards
\mathcal{H}(\nu_{n}|\mathfrak{m})
, note that
g_{n}P_{T}f_{n}\to gP_{T}f

\mathfrak{m}
-a.e. together with
g_{n}P_{T}f_{n}\leq gP_{T}f\in L^{1}(\mathfrak{m})
entails
g_{n}P_{T}f_{n}\to gP_{T}f
in
L^{1}(\mathfrak{m})
by dominated convergence, whence
\nu_{n}\rightharpoonup\nu
. Moreover,

\begin{split}|M_{2}(\nu_{n})-M_{2}(\nu)|&\leq\bigg|\int{\sf d}^{2}(\cdot,\bar{x})\,\mathrm{d}\nu_{n}-\int{\sf d}^{2}(\cdot,\bar{x})g_{n}P_{T}f_{n}\,\mathrm{d}\mathfrak{m}\bigg|\\
&\qquad+\bigg|\int{\sf d}^{2}(\cdot,\bar{x})g_{n}P_{T}f_{n}\,\mathrm{d}\mathfrak{m}-\int{\sf d}^{2}(\cdot,\bar{x})\,\mathrm{d}\nu\bigg|\\
&\leq(c_{n}-1)M_{2}(\nu)+\int{\sf d}^{2}(\cdot,\bar{x})\bigg|\frac{g_{n}P_{T}f_{n}}{gP_{T}f}-1\bigg|\,\mathrm{d}\nu\to 0,\quad\textrm{as }n\to\infty,\end{split}

again by
c_{n}\to 1
and dominated convergence (recall indeed that
\nu\in\mathcal{P}_{2}(M)
by (
H1
) and
g_{n}P_{T}f_{n}\leq gP_{T}f
, so that
\frac{g_{n}P_{T}f_{n}}{gP_{T}f}\in[0,1]
). Therefore
\mathcal{W}_{2}(\nu_{n},\nu)\to 0
and, by lower semicontinuity of the entropy w.r.t.
\mathcal{W}_{2}
-convergence,

(2.23)

\limsup_{n\to\infty}\Big(-\mathcal{H}(\nu_{n}|\mathfrak{m})\Big)\leq-\mathcal{H}(\nu|\mathfrak{m}).

Passing to the left-hand side of (
2.21
), fix
k\in\mathbb{N}
and note that for all
n\geq k
we have

\int\left\lvert\nabla\log(P_{T}f_{n}+\delta)\right\rvert^{2}\mathrm{d}\nu_{n}\geq\frac{1}{c_{k}}\int\left\lvert\nabla\log(P_{T}f_{n}+\delta)\right\rvert^{2}\mathrm{d}\nu_{k}=\frac{1}{c_{k}}\int_{\bar{B}_{k}(\bar{x})}\left\lvert\nabla\log(P_{T}f_{n}+\delta)\right\rvert^{2}\mathrm{d}\nu_{k},

since
c_{n}\geq 1
. We now claim that
|\nabla\log(P_{T}f_{n}+\delta)|\rightharpoonup G
in
L^{2}(\bar{B}_{k}(\bar{x}),\nu_{k})
for some
G
such that
|\nabla\log(P_{T}f+\delta)|\leq G
. To this end, observe that by the
L^{\infty}
-Lipschitz regularization (
2.3
) it holds
\left\lvert\nabla P_{T}f_{n}\right\rvert\leq C_{T,\kappa}\|f_{n}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathfrak{m})}
, where
C_{T,\kappa}
only depends on
T
and
\kappa
given by (
CD
). Since
f_{n}\leq f
, this implies

\int_{\bar{B}_{k}(\bar{x})}\left\lvert\nabla\log(P_{T}f_{n}+\delta)\right\rvert^{2}\,\mathrm{d}\mathfrak{m}\leq\frac{C_{T,\kappa}^{2}}{\delta^{2}}\mathfrak{m}(B_{k}(\bar{x}))\|f\|^{2}_{L^{\infty}(\mathfrak{m})}.

The functions
(|\nabla\log(P_{T}f_{n}+\delta)|)_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
are thus equi-bounded in
L^{2}(\bar{B}_{k}(\bar{x}),\mathfrak{m})
and this implies that, up to subsequences, they converge to some function
G\in L^{2}(\bar{B}_{k}(\bar{x}),\mathfrak{m})
weakly in
L^{2}(\bar{B}_{k}(\bar{x}),\mathfrak{m})
. Since
\log(P_{T}f_{n}+\delta)
converges
\mathfrak{m}
-a.e. to
\log(P_{T}f+\delta)
, by
[
2
, Lemma 4.3(b)]

|\nabla\log(P_{T}f+\delta)|\leq G
. As for
|\nabla\log(P_{T}f_{n}+\delta)|\rightharpoonup G
in
L^{2}(\bar{B}_{k}(\bar{x}),\nu_{k})
, this directly follows from the weak convergence in
L^{2}(\bar{B}_{k}(\bar{x}),\mathfrak{m})
and the fact that, under the current assumptions,
\frac{\mathrm{d}\nu}{\mathrm{d}\mathfrak{m}}\in L^{\infty}(\mathfrak{m})
. Combining the claim with the lower semicontinuity of the
L^{2}(\nu_{k})
-norm w.r.t. weak convergence we obtain

\liminf_{n\to\infty}\int_{\bar{B}_{k}(\bar{x})}\left\lvert\nabla\log(P_{T}f_{n}+\delta)\right\rvert^{2}\mathrm{d}\nu_{k}\,\geq\int_{\bar{B}_{k}(\bar{x})}G^{2}\mathrm{d}\nu_{k}\geq\int_{\bar{B}_{k}(\bar{x})}\left\lvert\nabla\log(P_{T}f+\delta)\right\rvert^{2}\mathrm{d}\nu_{k}.

From this inequality, (
2.22
) and (
2.23
) we end up with

\frac{1}{c_{k}}\int_{\bar{B}_{k}(\bar{x})}\left\lvert\nabla\log(P_{T}f+\delta)\right\rvert^{2}\mathrm{d}\nu_{k}\leq\frac{1}{E_{2\kappa}(T)}\,\biggl[\mathcal{C}_{T}(\mu,\nu)-\mathcal{H}(\nu|\mathfrak{m})\biggr]

and it is now sufficient to let first
k\to\infty
and then
\delta\downarrow 0
. In both cases, the monotone convergence theorem (and the fact that
c_{k}\to 1
) allows to handle the left-hand side and finally get the validity of (
2.16
) for
\mu,\nu
.

2nd step.
Now let
\mu,\nu
be as in (
H1
) and assume that
\frac{\mathrm{d}\nu}{\mathrm{d}\mathfrak{m}}
is locally bounded away from
0
on
{\rm int}(\mathrm{supp}(\nu))
and
\nu(\partial\mathrm{supp}(\nu))=0
. Fix
K\in\mathbb{N}
, let
f_{K},g_{K}
be defined as in Lemma
2.3
and let
\mu_{K}\coloneqq(\mathrm{proj}_{x_{1}})_{\#}\pi^{K}
be the first marginal of the optimizer
\pi^{K}
associated to (
2.14
). Since this approximation guarantees us only that
f_{K}\in L^{\infty}(\mathfrak{m})
, let us fix
n\in\mathbb{N}
and define
g_{K}^{n}\coloneqq\min\{g_{K},n\}\in L^{\infty}(\mathfrak{m})
so that the previous step applies to the marginals

\mu^{K}_{n}\coloneqq c_{K,n}f_{K}P_{T}g_{K}^{n}\,\mathfrak{m}\qquad\textrm{and}\qquad\nu^{K}_{n}\coloneqq c_{K,n}g_{K}^{n}P_{T}f_{K}\,\mathfrak{m},

where
c_{K,n}
is a normalization constant (again, this is the same for both
\mu_{n}^{K}
and
\nu_{n}^{K}
). Henceforth, in this case (
2.16
) reads as

\int\left\lvert\nabla\log P_{T}f_{K}\right\rvert^{2}\,\mathrm{d}\nu_{n}^{K}\leq\frac{1}{E_{2\kappa}(T)}\biggl[\mathcal{C}_{T}(\mu^{K}_{n},\nu^{K}_{n})-\mathcal{H}(\nu_{n}^{K}|\mathfrak{m})\biggr]\,.

Owing to algebraic manipulations, the normalizing constant
c_{K,n}
can be neglected and therefore it holds

\displaystyle\int\left\lvert\nabla\log P_{T}f_{K}\right\rvert^{2}\,g^{n}_{K}P_{T}f_{K}\,\mathrm{d}\mathfrak{m}\leq\frac{1}{E_{2\kappa}(T)}\biggl[\int f_{K}\log f_{K}\,P_{T}g^{n}_{K}\,\mathrm{d}\mathfrak{m}-\int P_{T}f_{K}\log(P_{T}f_{K})\,g_{K}^{n}\,\mathrm{d}\mathfrak{m}\biggr]\,.

Since by Lemma
2.3

f_{K}
is bounded away from 0 and
\infty
, it follows that
\log f_{K}\in L^{1}(\mu^{K})
and
\log(P_{T}f_{K})\in L^{1}(\nu)
. From this, by applying Fatou lemma to the left-hand side and the Dominated Convergence Theorem to the right-hand one, in the limit as
n\to{\infty}
we have

\int\left\lvert\nabla\log P_{T}f_{K}\right\rvert^{2}\,g_{K}P_{T}f_{K}\,\mathrm{d}\mathfrak{m}\leq\frac{1}{E_{2\kappa}(T)}\biggl[\int f_{K}\log f_{K}\,P_{T}g_{K}\,\mathrm{d}\mathfrak{m}-\int P_{T}f_{K}\log(P_{T}f_{K})\,g_{K}\,\mathrm{d}\mathfrak{m}\biggr]\,,

which is the corrector estimate (
2.16
) associated to
\mathcal{C}_{T}(\mu^{K},\nu)
:

(2.24)

\int\left\lvert\nabla\log P_{T}f_{K}\right\rvert^{2}\,\mathrm{d}\nu\leq\frac{1}{E_{2\kappa}(T)}\biggl[\mathcal{C}_{T}(\mu^{K},\nu)-\mathcal{H}(\nu|\mathfrak{m})\biggr]\,.

We wish now to take the limit as
K\to{\infty}
. However this point is more subtle, since we are interested in proving that
\nabla\log P_{T}f
is well defined also in the limit. At this stage, let us stress out that the extra-assumption on the density
\frac{\mathrm{d}\nu}{\mathrm{d}\mathfrak{m}}
and on
\mathrm{supp}(\nu)
has never been used and it is just needed in the following discussion. First, notice that the right-hand side above is uniformly bounded in
K\in\mathbb{N}
since Lemma
2.3
ensures that
\mathcal{C}_{T}(\mu^{K},\nu)=\mathcal{H}(\pi^{K}|\mathrm{R}_{0,T})
converges to
\mathcal{C}_{T}(\mu,\nu)
.
As concerns the left-hand side, fix
\bar{x}\in\mathrm{int}(\mathrm{supp}(\nu))
. By assumption there exist
r,\alpha>0
such that
\bar{B}_{r}(\bar{x})\subset\mathrm{supp}(\nu)
and
\frac{\mathrm{d}\nu}{\mathrm{d}\mathfrak{m}}\geq\alpha

\mathfrak{m}
-a.e. in
\bar{B}_{r}(\bar{x})
, so that, for any fixed
\delta>0
it holds

\int\left\lvert\nabla\log P_{T}f_{K}\right\rvert^{2}\mathrm{d}\nu\geq\int\left\lvert\nabla\log(P_{T}f_{K}+\delta)\right\rvert^{2}\mathrm{d}\nu\geq\alpha\int_{\bar{B}_{r}(\bar{x})}\left\lvert\nabla\log(P_{T}f_{K}+\delta)\right\rvert^{2}\,\mathrm{d}\mathfrak{m}\,.

As a byproduct of these bounds we have

\limsup_{K\to\infty}\int_{\bar{B}_{r}(\bar{x})}\left\lvert\nabla\log(P_{T}f_{K}+\delta)\right\rvert^{2}\,\mathrm{d}\mathfrak{m}\leq\alpha^{-1}\,E_{2\kappa}(T)^{-1}\,\biggl[\mathcal{C}_{T}(\mu,\nu)-\mathcal{H}(\nu|\mathfrak{m})\biggr]<+\infty\,.

The functions
(|\nabla\log(P_{T}f_{K}+\delta)|)_{K\in\mathbb{N}}
are thus equi-bounded in
L^{2}(\bar{B}_{r}(\bar{x}),\mathfrak{m})
and this implies that, up to subsequences, they converge to some function
G_{\bar{x}}\in L^{2}(\bar{B}_{r}(\bar{x}),\mathfrak{m})
weakly in
L^{2}(\bar{B}_{r}(\bar{x}),\mathfrak{m})
. Moreover, from Lemma
2.3
we also have that
\log(P_{T}f_{K}+\delta)
converges
\mathfrak{m}
-a.e. to
\log(P_{T}f+\delta)
in
\bar{B}_{r}(\bar{x})
. Therefore, relying again on
[
2
, Lemma 4.3(b)]
we conclude that
|\nabla\log(P_{T}f+\delta)|\leq G_{\bar{x}}
on
\bar{B}_{r}(\bar{x})
. In particular, it is worth stressing that in this case
[
2
, Lemma 4.3(b)]
also ensures that
\log(P_{T}f+\delta)\in W^{1,2}(\bar{B}_{r}(\bar{x}))
, which does not follow from the regularizing effect of
P_{T}
, because of the possible lack of integrability of
f
.

To replicate the proof given in the previous step we also need that
|\nabla\log(P_{T}f_{K}+\delta)|\rightharpoonup G_{\bar{x}}
in
L^{2}(\bar{B}_{r}(\bar{x}),\nu)
, but this may fail, as
\frac{\mathrm{d}\nu}{\mathrm{d}\mathfrak{m}}
needs not belong to
L^{\infty}(\mathfrak{m})
. For this reason, let us introduce
[\nu]_{N}\coloneqq\min\{g_{K}P_{T}f_{K},N\}\mathfrak{m}
for
N\in\mathbb{N}
, so that the left-hand side of (
2.24
) can be trivially estimated from below as

\int\left\lvert\nabla\log P_{T}f_{K}\right\rvert^{2}\,\mathrm{d}[\nu]_{N}\leq\int\left\lvert\nabla\log P_{T}f_{K}\right\rvert^{2}\,\mathrm{d}\nu\leq\frac{1}{E_{2\kappa}(T)}\biggl[\mathcal{C}_{T}(\mu^{K},\nu)-\mathcal{H}(\nu|\mathfrak{m})\biggr]\,.

Since now
\frac{\mathrm{d}[\nu]_{N}}{\mathrm{d}\mathfrak{m}}\in L^{\infty}(\mathfrak{m})
by construction,
|\nabla\log(P_{T}f_{K}+\delta)|\rightharpoonup G_{\bar{x}}
in
L^{2}(\bar{B}_{r}(\bar{x}),\mathfrak{m})
implies the weak convergence towards the same limit in
L^{2}(\bar{B}_{r}(\bar{x}),[\nu]_{N})
. Combining these considerations with the lower semicontinuity of the
L^{2}([\nu]_{N})
-norm, we obtain

\liminf_{K\to\infty}\int_{\bar{B}_{r}(\bar{x})}\left\lvert\nabla\log(P_{T}f_{K}+\delta)\right\rvert^{2}\mathrm{d}[\nu]_{N}\,\geq\int_{\bar{B}_{r}(\bar{x})}G^{2}_{\bar{x}}\mathrm{d}[\nu]_{N}\geq\int_{\bar{B}_{r}(\bar{x})}\left\lvert\nabla\log(P_{T}f+\delta)\right\rvert^{2}\mathrm{d}[\nu]_{N}

whence

\int_{\bar{B}_{r}(\bar{x})}\left\lvert\nabla\log(P_{T}f+\delta)\right\rvert^{2}\mathrm{d}[\nu]_{N}\leq\frac{1}{E_{2\kappa}(T)}\,\biggl[\mathcal{C}_{T}(\mu,\nu)-\mathcal{H}(\nu|\mathfrak{m})\biggr]\,.

Choosing now
(x_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{N}}\subset{\rm int}(\mathrm{supp}(\nu))
dense and denoting by
r_{k}
the radii associated to each
x_{k}
according to the previous construction, so that
{\rm int}(\mathrm{supp}(\nu))=\cup_{k}\bar{B}_{r_{k}}(x_{k})
, by a diagonal argument there exists
G\in L^{2}_{loc}(\mathfrak{m})
such that
|\nabla\log(P_{T}f_{K}+\delta)|\rightharpoonup G
in
L^{2}(\bar{B}_{r_{k}}(x_{k}),\mathfrak{m})
and
L^{2}(\bar{B}_{r_{k}}(x_{k}),[\nu]_{N})
for every
k\in\mathbb{N}
and, by
[
2
, Lemma 4.3(b)]
,
|\nabla\log(P_{T}f+\delta)|\leq G

\mathfrak{m}
-a.e since
\log(P_{T}f_{K}+\delta)\to\log(P_{T}f+\delta)

\mathfrak{m}
-a.e. in
\mathrm{supp}(\nu)
. Setting
B_{k}\coloneqq\cup_{i=1}^{k}\bar{B}_{r_{i}}(x_{i})
, by the same reasoning as above (noting that the choice of
N
does not depend on the point
x_{k}
) we obtain

\int_{B_{k}}\left\lvert\nabla\log(P_{T}f+\delta)\right\rvert^{2}\mathrm{d}[\nu]_{N}\leq\frac{1}{E_{2\kappa}(T)}\,\biggl[\mathcal{C}_{T}(\mu,\nu)-\mathcal{H}(\nu|\mathfrak{m})\biggr]\,,\qquad\forall k\in\mathbb{N}.

Taking the limit as
k\to\infty
, by
\nu(\partial\mathrm{supp}(\nu))=0
we infer that
|\nabla\log(P_{T}f+\delta)|
actually belongs to
L^{2}([\nu]_{N})
. Passing then to the limit as
N\to\infty
and
\delta\downarrow 0
, by monotonicity and again the fact that
\nu(\partial\mathrm{supp}(\nu))=0
we precisely obtain (
2.16
) for
\mu,\nu
as in the statement.
∎

Remark 2.5
.

In
[
27
, Proposition 4.1]
, the dynamical representation formula (
2.20
) for the entropic cost is actually proved under a
{\sf CD}(\kappa,N)
assumption with
N<\infty
. However, the very same argument works also if
(M,{\sf d},\mathfrak{m})
satisfies
{\sf CD}(\kappa,\infty)
and
\mathfrak{m}(M)=1
. Indeed, the proof of
[
27
, Proposition 4.1]
essentially relies on the regularity and integrability of
t\mapsto\rho_{t}\coloneqq P_{t}f\,P_{T-t}g
,
t\mapsto\log P_{t}f
,
t\mapsto\log P_{T-t}g
(and of their gradients) and these properties can be extended to the case
N=\infty
as follows.

As concerns the regularity, given that
\mu,\nu
have bounded densities and supports, the lower bound (
2.10
) on the heat kernel allows to deduce that
f,g\in L^{\infty}(\mathfrak{m})
with bounded supports too, exactly as in
[
28
, Proposition 2.1 and Theorem 2.2]
. Then the smoothing property of
P_{T}
entails
C^{\infty}
-regularity, see
[
30
, Theorem 3.1]
.

As regards the integrability, what is needed is the existence of an
L^{1}(\mathrm{d}t\otimes\mathfrak{m})
-function dominating, locally in
t
,
t\mapsto\log(P_{t}f)\rho_{t}
,
t\mapsto\log(P_{T-t}g)\rho_{t}
and
t\mapsto|\nabla\log P_{t}f|^{2}\rho_{t}
,
t\mapsto|\nabla\log P_{T-t}g|^{2}\rho_{t}
. By the maximum principle,
t\mapsto\log(P_{t}f)\rho_{t}
,
t\mapsto\log(P_{T-t}g)\rho_{t}
are dominated (up to multiplicative constants) by
t\mapsto P_{T-t}g
and
t\mapsto P_{t}f
, respectively. As for
t\mapsto|\nabla\log P_{t}f|^{2}
,
t\mapsto|\nabla\log P_{T-t}g|^{2}
, the desired domination follows from Hamilton’s gradient estimate under
{\sf CD}(\kappa,\infty
condition
[
33
]
and the bounds on
t\mapsto\log(P_{t}f)\rho_{t}
,
t\mapsto\log(P_{T-t}g)\rho_{t}
.

Proof of Lemma
2.3
.

The existence and uniqueness of the minimizer
\pi^{K}\in\mathcal{Q}^{\nu}_{K}
is guaranteed by
[
40
, Proposition 1.17]
. Moreover, from the same reference we have
{\pi}^{{\mu}\to{\nu},{T}}\ll\pi^{K}
and

(2.25)

\pi^{K}\to{\pi}^{{\mu}\to{\nu},{T}}\,\text{ in total variation}\quad\text{ and }\quad\mathcal{H}(\pi^{K}|\mathrm{R}_{0,T})\to\mathcal{H}({\pi}^{{\mu}\to{\nu},{T}}|\mathrm{R}_{0,T})=\mathcal{C}_{T}(\mu,\nu)\,.

Let us now prove (
2.15
). Firstly, notice that if we introduce the marginal
\mu^{K}\coloneqq(\mathrm{proj}_{x_{1}})_{\#}\pi^{K}
, then it immediately follows that
\pi^{K}\in\Pi(\mu^{K},\nu)\subseteq\mathcal{Q}^{\nu}_{K}
is the unique optimizer of the Schrödinger problem with marginals
\mu^{K},\,\nu
and
\mathcal{C}_{T}(\mu^{K},\nu)=\mathcal{H}(\pi^{K}|\mathrm{R}_{0,T})
. Owing to (
2.25
),
\mathcal{H}(\mu^{K}|\mathfrak{m})\leq\mathcal{H}(\pi^{K}|\mathrm{R}_{0,T})
is eventually finite for
K
large enough and therefore Proposition
2.2
yields the existence of two positive measurable functions
f_{K},\,g_{K}
such that
\mathrm{R}_{0,T}
-a.s. it holds
\frac{\mathrm{d}\pi^{K}}{\mathrm{d}\mathrm{R}_{0,T}}(x,y)=f_{K}(x)\,g_{K}(y)
with
\log f_{K}\in L^{1}(\mu^{K})
and
\log g_{K}\in L^{1}(\nu)
. Then, in view of the additive property of the relative entropy
[
37
, Equation (72) in Appendix A]
we may write

\mathcal{H}(\pi|\mathrm{R}_{0,T})=\mathcal{H}(\nu|\mathfrak{m})+\int\mathcal{H}\left(\pi(\cdot|\mathrm{proj}_{x_{2}}=y)|\mathfrak{m}P_{T}(y)\right)\,\mathrm{d}\nu(y)\quad\forall\pi\in\mathcal{Q}^{\nu}_{K}\,,

where
\mathfrak{m}P_{T}(y)=\mathrm{R}_{0,T}(\cdot|\mathrm{proj}_{x_{2}}=y)\in\mathcal{P}(M)
is the probability measure whose density is given by
{\sf p}_{T}(x,y)\mathrm{d}\mathfrak{m}(x)
.
Therefore minimizing (
2.14
) is equivalent to solving for any fixed
y\in\mathrm{supp}(\nu)
the problem

\inf_{\mathfrak{q}(y)\in\mathcal{Q}_{K}}\mathcal{H}(\mathfrak{q}(y)|\mathfrak{m}P_{T}(y))\quad\text{with }\mathcal{Q}_{K}\coloneqq\left\{\mathfrak{q}\in\mathcal{P}(M)\,\colon\,\int\phi_{i}\mathrm{d}\mathfrak{q}=\int\phi_{i}\mathrm{d}\mu\quad\forall\,i=1,\dots,K\right\}\,,

indeed if
\mathfrak{q}^{*}\colon M\to\mathcal{P}(M)
denotes the stochastic kernel associated to the minimizers of the above problem (whose existence and uniqueness, for any fixed
y\in\mathrm{supp}(\nu)
, are once again ensured by
[
40
, Proposition 1.17]
) then
\pi^{K}=\mathfrak{q}^{*}\otimes\nu
. Moreover, by arguing as in
[
40
, Example 1.18]
for any fixed
y\in\mathrm{supp}(\nu)
we can write

\frac{\mathrm{d}\mathfrak{q}^{*}(y)}{\mathrm{d}(\mathfrak{m}P_{T}(y))}(x)=c(y)\exp\left(\sum_{i=1}^{K}b_{i}(y)\phi_{i}(x)\right)

for some constants
c(y)>0
and
b_{i}(y)\in\mathbb{R}
, possibly depending on
y
.
By combining the above expression, the
f_{K},g_{K}
-decomposition and the Schrödinger system associated to
\mathcal{C}_{T}(\mu^{K},\nu)
(cf. (
2.8
) in Proposition
2.2
) we deduce that

\displaystyle f_{K}(x)g_{K}(y)=

\displaystyle\,\frac{\mathrm{d}\pi^{K}}{\mathrm{d}\mathrm{R}_{0,T}}(x,y)=\frac{\mathrm{d}(\mathfrak{q}^{*}\otimes\nu)}{\mathrm{d}(\mathfrak{m}P_{T}\otimes\mathfrak{m})}(x,y)=\frac{\mathrm{d}\nu}{\mathrm{d}\mathfrak{m}}(y)\,\frac{\mathrm{d}\mathfrak{q}^{*}(y)}{\mathrm{d}(\mathfrak{m}P_{T}(y))}(x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\,g_{K}(y)P_{T}f_{K}(y)\,c(y)\exp\left(\sum_{i=1}^{K}b_{i}(y)\phi_{i}(x)\right)\,.

Henceforth, for any
y\in\mathrm{supp}(\nu)
it holds

f_{K}(x)=P_{T}f_{K}(y)\,c(y)\exp\left(\sum_{i=1}^{K}b_{i}(y)\phi_{i}(x)\right)

and since the above left-hand side does not depend on the choice of

, we may choose a fixed

and write

f_{K}(x)=P_{T}f_{K}(y^{*})\,c(y^{*})\exp\left(\sum_{i=1}^{K}b_{i}(y^{*})\phi_{i}(x)\right)\in(0,+{\infty})\,.

This proves (
2.15
) with
C\coloneqq P_{T}f_{K}(y^{*})\,c(y^{*})
and
\lambda_{i}\coloneqq b_{i}(y^{*})
.

Finally, we claim that we can fix a renormalization for the decomposition
f_{K},g_{K}
(which is unique up to a multiplicative constant) such that
g_{K}
converges
\mathfrak{m}
-a.e. to
g
. In order to do so, note that the convergence in total variation in (
2.25
) implies that (along a non-relabeled subsequence)

f_{K}(x)g_{K}(y)=\frac{\mathrm{d}\pi^{K}}{\mathrm{d}\mathrm{R}_{0,T}}(x,y)\to\frac{\mathrm{d}{\pi}^{{\mu}\to{\nu},{T}}}{\mathrm{d}\mathrm{R}_{0,T}}(x,y)=f(x)g(y),\qquad\mathrm{R}_{0,T}\textrm{-a.s.}

and a fortiori
\mathfrak{m}\otimes\mathfrak{m}
-a.e., thanks to the (
CD
) assumption and the Gaussian lower bounds (
2.10
) and (
2.12
). If
A\subseteq M\times M
denotes the subset where the latter limit holds pointwise and
A^{c}
its complement, then
\mathfrak{m}\otimes\mathfrak{m}(A^{c})=0
and Fubini’s Theorem implies that for
\mathfrak{m}
-a.e.
x\in M
it holds
\mathfrak{m}(A^{c}_{x})=0
, where the section is defined as
A_{x}\coloneqq\{y\colon(x,y)\in A\}
. Combining this with
\mu\ll\mathfrak{m}
we deduce that there exists an element
x^{*}\in\mathrm{int}(\mathrm{supp}(\mu))
such that
f(x^{*})\neq 0
and
\mathfrak{m}(A^{c}_{x^{*}})=0
. We have therefore proven that

(2.26)

f_{K}(x^{*})g_{K}(y)\to f(x^{*})g(y)\quad\textrm{for }\mathfrak{m}\text{-a.e. }y.

By renormalizing the
f_{K},g_{K}
-decomposition in such a way that
f_{K}(x^{*})=f(x^{*})\in(0,+{\infty})
, (
2.26
) reads as

(2.27)

g_{K}\to g\quad\mathfrak{m}\text{-a.e.}

As a direct consequence of this and (
2.8
) we get the
\mathfrak{m}
-a.e. convergence of
P_{T}f_{K}
to
P_{T}f
on
\mathrm{supp}(\nu)
, since the marginal
\nu
is always the same at each step
K\in\mathbb{N}
.
∎

3.
Proof of the convergence of the gradients

As already mentioned in the Introduction, the first step in the proof of Theorem
1.1
is to show the convergence of the Schrödinger potentials to the Kantorovich ones as the parameter
T
vanishes. To achieve this, we will rely on
[
41
, Proposition 5.1]
and on
[
39
, Theorem 1.1]
, which states that uniformly on compact subsets of
M\times M
it holds

T\log{\sf p}_{T}(x,y)\,{\stackrel{{\scriptstyle T\downarrow 0}}{{\longrightarrow}}}\,-\frac{1}{4}\,{\sf d}^{2}(x,y)\,.

Before starting with the proof, let us fix some notations. First, let
\rho\coloneqq\frac{\mathrm{d}\mu}{\mathrm{d}\mathfrak{m}}
and
\sigma\coloneqq\frac{\mathrm{d}\nu}{\mathrm{d}\mathfrak{m}}
denote the densities of the marginals. Then let
(\varphi^{T},\psi^{T})
denote the unique Schrödinger potentials (as introduced in (
1.5
)) satisfying the symmetric normalization (
2.9
), i.e.

\int\varphi^{T}\,\mathrm{d}\mu-\mathcal{H}(\mu|\mathfrak{m})=\int\psi^{T}\,\mathrm{d}\nu-\mathcal{H}(\nu|\mathfrak{m})\,;

let
(\Phi_{T},\,\Psi_{T})
be the unique entropic potentials for (

ε

EOT
) (as defined in (
1.3
)) with
\varepsilon=T
and cost
c_{T}(x,y)\coloneqq-T\log{\sf p}_{T}(x,y)
satisfying the symmetric normalization

\int\Phi_{T}\,\mathrm{d}\mu=\int\Psi_{T}\,\mathrm{d}\nu

and, similarly, let
(\varphi^{0},\psi^{0})
be a pair of Kantorovich potentials (defined in (
1.4
)) satisfying the symmetric normalization

(3.1)

\int\varphi^{0}\,\mathrm{d}\mu=\int\psi^{0}\,\mathrm{d}\nu\,.

Let us stress that, when rescaled by a factor
T
, the potentials
(\varphi^{T},\psi^{T})
coincide with the entropic potentials for (

ε

EOT
) with
\varepsilon=T
and
\tilde{c}_{T}\coloneqq-T\log\frac{\mathrm{d}\mathrm{R}_{0,T}}{\mathrm{d}(\mu\otimes\nu)}
. Although similar, this EOT problem is different from the one associated with
c_{T}
; in particular,
(\varphi^{T},\psi^{T})
is different from
(\Phi_{T},\,\Psi_{T})
, whence the choice of the uppercase notation to avoid confusion.

Indeed

\tilde{c}_{T}(x,y)=c_{T}(x,y)+T\log\rho(x)+T\log\sigma(y),

and therefore, even if for
T
fixed the two EOT problems have the same minimizers and the same optimal values, the corresponding potentials differ. More precisely, it holds

(3.2)

T\varphi^{T}=\Phi_{T}+T\log\rho\quad\text{ and }\quad T\psi^{T}=\Psi_{T}+T\log\sigma\,.

Hence, even if
(\varphi^{T},\psi^{T})
are the Schrödinger potentials, from the EOT viewpoint it may be more convenient to work with
c_{T}
, and thus
(\Phi_{T},\,\Psi_{T})
, because of the dynamical interpretation of the cost as transition kernel of the associated SDE (
1.2
).

As concerns
(\varphi^{0},\psi^{0})
, due to the lack of regularity of the OT problem (
1.4
), even after the normalization (
3.1
) uniqueness of the Kantorovich potentials may fail. Some results in this direction are known for some specific examples, for instance in the Euclidean case if at least one marginal is absolutely continuous with respect to the Lebesgue measure and if its support is connected then uniqueness holds
[
25
, Appendix B]
. However here we are interested in the convergence of the Schrödinger map
\operatorname{Id}-2T\nabla\varphi^{T}
to the Brenier map
\tau
(as introduced above in (
1.9
)), whose uniqueness instead holds true in our setting. More precisely,
\tau=\operatorname{Id}-2\nabla\varphi^{0}
with
\varphi^{0}
a Kantorovich potential as fixed above (cf.
[
22
, Proposition 3.1]
,
[
23
, Theorem 1.1]
and discussion therein).

After this premise, let us show the strong convergence of the Schrödinger potentials (possibly along a subsequence), which will be pivotal in the subsequent proof of Theorem
1.1
.

Lemma 3.1

(
L^{1}
-convergence of the potentials)
.

Assume that
(M,{\sf d},\mathfrak{m})
satisfies (
CD
) and that
\mu,\nu
satisfy (
H1
).
Then it holds:

\blacktriangleright

\{\Phi_{T}\}_{T\in(0,1]}
and
\{\Psi_{T}\}_{T\in(0,1]}
are strongly precompact in
L^{1}(\mu)
and
L^{1}(\nu)
respectively and their accumulation points are Kantorovich potentials
(\varphi^{0},\psi^{0})
for (
1.4
);

\blacktriangleright

or equivalently,
\{T\varphi^{T}\}_{T\in(0,1]}
and
\{T\psi^{T}\}_{T\in(0,1]}
are strongly precompact in
L^{1}(\mu)
and
L^{1}(\nu)
respectively and their accumulation points are Kantorovich potentials
(\varphi^{0},\psi^{0})
for (
1.4
).

If the Kantorovich potentials
(\varphi^{0},\psi^{0})
associated to (
1.4
) are unique, then
it holds

\Phi_{T}\to\varphi^{0}\text{ strongly in }L^{1}(\mu)\quad\text{ and }\quad\Psi_{T}\to\psi^{0}\text{ strongly in }L^{1}(\nu)\,,

and equivalently

T\varphi^{T}\to\varphi^{0}\text{ strongly in }L^{1}(\mu)\quad\text{ and }\quad T\psi^{T}\to\psi^{0}\text{ strongly in }L^{1}(\nu)\,.

Proof.

Firstly, notice that the equivalence between the statements follows from (
3.2
) and the finite entropy condition in (
H1
).
By
[
41
, Proposition 5.1]
, to obtain the desired convergence for
\Phi_{T}
and
\Psi_{T}
it is sufficient to show that
c_{T}\coloneqq-T\log{\sf p}_{T}\to{\sf d}^{2}/4
uniformly on compact subsets as
T\to 0
and that there exists a function
\overline{c}(x,y)=\overline{c}_{1}(x)+\overline{c}_{2}(y)
with
\overline{c}_{1}\in L^{1}(\mu)
and
\overline{c}_{2}\in L^{1}(\nu)
such that
c_{T}\leq\overline{c}
for all
T
sufficiently small, say
T\leq 1
.

Under the
{\sf CD}(\kappa,N)
condition with
N<\infty
, using the heat kernel lower bound (
2.1
) and assuming without loss of generality that
T\leq 1
, so that
\log\mathfrak{m}(B_{\sqrt{T}}(x))\leq\log\mathfrak{m}(B_{1}(x))
, we see that

(3.3)

\begin{split}c_{T}(x,y)&\leq T\log C_{1}+T\log\mathfrak{m}(B_{\sqrt{T}}(x))+\frac{{\sf d}^{2}(x,y)}{4-\delta}+C_{2}T^{2}\\
&\leq T\log C_{1}+\left(T\log\mathfrak{m}(B_{1}(x))\right)^{+}+\frac{{\sf d}^{2}(x,y)}{4-\delta}+C_{2}T^{2}.\end{split}

By the trivial inequality
{\sf d}^{2}(x,y)\leq 2({\sf d}^{2}(x,z)+{\sf d}^{2}(y,z))
valid for any
z\in M
we conclude that
c_{T}(x,y)\leq c^{\prime}_{T}(x)+c^{\prime\prime}_{T}(y)
with

c^{\prime}_{T}(x)\coloneqq\left(T\log\mathfrak{m}(B_{1}(x))\right)^{+}+\frac{2}{4-\delta}{\sf d}^{2}(x,z),\qquad c^{\prime\prime}_{T}(y)\coloneqq T\log C_{1}+\frac{2}{4-\delta}{\sf d}^{2}(y,z)+C_{2}T^{2}.

By the fact that
\nu\in\mathcal{P}_{2}(M)
, it is clear that
c^{\prime\prime}_{T}
can be dominated by a
\nu
-integrable function not depending on
T
. As regards
c^{\prime}_{T}
it follows from
\mu\in\mathcal{P}_{2}(M)
and Lemma
2.1
which gives the
\mu
-integrability of the positive part of
\log\mathfrak{m}(B_{1}(\cdot))
.

On the other hand, if we assume that
(M,{\sf d},\mathfrak{m})
satisfies
{\sf CD}(\kappa,{\infty})
with
\mathfrak{m}(M)=1
, then leveraging on the heat kernel lower bound (
2.2
) we see that

c_{T}(x,y)\leq\frac{\kappa T}{2(1-e^{-\kappa T})}{\sf d}^{2}(x,y)

and in this case the trivial inequality
{\sf d}^{2}(x,y)\leq 2({\sf d}^{2}(x,z)+{\sf d}^{2}(y,z))
, valid for any
z\in M
, readily provides us with functions
c_{T}^{\prime},c_{T}^{\prime\prime}
which are
\mu
- and
\nu
-integrable respectively.

Finally, in both cases, the fact that
c_{T}\to{\sf d}^{2}/4
uniformly on compact subsets of
M\times M
as
T\downarrow 0
is ensured by
[
39
, Theorem 1.1]
. Therefore we can apply
[
41
, Proposition 5.1]
, which concludes our proof.
∎

Remark 3.2
.

Let us mention that the previous result gives as a direct consequence a new proof of the small-time limit of the renormalized Schrödinger cost, that is, under the same assumptions of Lemma
3.1
we have proven

\lim_{T\to 0}T\mathcal{C}_{T}(\mu,\nu)=\frac{\mathcal{W}_{2}^{2}(\mu,\nu)}{4}\,.

We are now ready to prove the convergence of the gradients.

Proof of Theorem
1.1
.

For the readers’ convenience we divide our proof into four steps.

1st step: weak compactness of the gradients in
\mathbf{L^{2}(\bm{\mu})}
.
From the identity
\mu=fP_{T}g\,\mathfrak{m}
we deduce that
\log\rho=\varphi^{T}+\psi^{T}_{0}
, where
\psi^{T}_{0}\coloneqq\log P_{T}e^{\psi^{T}}
and hence

\begin{split}\left\lVert T\nabla\varphi^{T}\right\rVert_{L^{2}(TM,\mu)}&=\left\lVert T\nabla\log\rho-T\nabla\psi^{T}_{0}\right\rVert_{L^{2}(TM,\mu)}\\
&\leq T\left\lVert\nabla\log\rho\right\rVert_{L^{2}(TM,\mu)}+T\left\lVert\nabla\psi^{T}_{0}\right\rVert_{L^{2}(TM,\mu)}.\end{split}

The correctors estimate (
1.7
) allows to control the last term as

\left\lVert\nabla\psi^{T}_{0}\right\rVert^{2}_{L^{2}(TM,\mu)}\leq\frac{1}{E_{2\kappa}(T)}\biggl[\mathcal{C}_{T}(\mu,\nu)-\mathcal{H}(\mu|\mathfrak{m})\biggr]

whence

\|T\nabla\varphi^{T}\|_{L^{2}(TM,\mu)}\leq T\sqrt{\mathcal{I}(\mu)}+\sqrt{\frac{T}{E_{2\kappa}(T)}}\sqrt{T\,\mathcal{C}_{T}(\mu,\nu)-T\,\mathcal{H}(\mu|\mathfrak{m})}.

This inequality together with the fact that
\lim_{T\downarrow 0}\frac{E_{2\kappa}(T)}{T}=1
,
\lim_{T\downarrow 0}T\mathcal{C}_{T}(\mu,\nu)=\frac{1}{4}\mathcal{W}_{2}^{2}(\mu,\nu)
(cf. Remark
3.2
) and
\mathcal{I}(\mu)<+\infty
implies that

(3.4)

\limsup_{T\rightarrow 0}\left\lVert T\nabla\varphi^{T}\right\rVert_{L^{2}(TM,\mu)}\leq\frac{\mathcal{W}_{2}(\mu,\nu)}{2}.

Therefore, the sequence
(T\nabla\varphi^{T})
is weakly compact in
L^{2}(\mu)
.

2nd step: weak convergence of the gradients in
\mathbf{L^{2}(\bm{\mu_{k}})}
.
Fix
k\in\mathbb{N}
and define
\rho_{k}:=\min\{\rho,k\}
,
\mu_{k}:=\rho_{k}\mathfrak{m}
(note that
\mu_{k}
needs not be a probability measure). We claim that for any
k
(large enough) and for any weakly convergent subsequence of
\{T\nabla\varphi^{T}\}_{T>0}
there exist a Kantorovich potential
\varphi^{0}
and a subsubsequence
T_{n}\downarrow 0
such that
T_{n}\nabla\varphi^{T_{n}}\rightharpoonup\nabla\varphi^{0}
in
L^{2}(TM,\mu_{k})
.

Firstly, note that for any subsequence of
\{T\nabla\varphi^{T}\}_{T>0}
, we can consider the corresponding subsequence of
\{T\varphi^{T}\}_{T>0}
and deduce from Lemma
3.1
that there exist a Kantorovich potential
\varphi^{0}\in L^{1}(\mu)
and a subsubsequence
T_{n}\downarrow 0
such that
T_{n}\varphi^{T_{n}}\to\varphi^{0}
strongly in
L^{1}(\mu)
, and a fortiori in
L^{1}(\mu_{k})
because
\mu_{k}\leq\mu
trivially. Moreover, the family
\{T\nabla\varphi^{T}\}_{T>0}
is weakly compact also in
L^{2}(TM,\mu_{k})
. Hence, given a subsequence of
\{T\nabla\varphi^{T}\}_{T>0}
weakly converging to some
\zeta_{k}\in L^{2}(TM,\mu_{k})
there exists
T_{n}\downarrow 0
such that

(3.5)

T_{n}\varphi^{T_{n}}\to\varphi^{0}\text{ in }L^{1}(\mu_{k})\qquad\text{and}\qquad T_{n}\nabla\varphi^{T_{n}}\rightharpoonup\zeta_{k}\text{ in }L^{2}(TM,\mu_{k})\,.

Our claim is proven once we show that the weak limit
\zeta_{k}
actually does not depend on
k
and coincides with the weak gradient
\nabla\varphi^{0}

\mathfrak{m}
-a.e.

To this end, fix
x\in{\rm int}(\mathrm{supp}(\mu))
. By assumption there exists an open neighbourhood
B\subset\mathrm{supp}(\mu)
of
x
and a constant
c>0
such that
\rho\geq c

\mathfrak{m}
-a.e. on
B
. Without loss of generality, we can assume that
B=B(x,r)
for some radius
r>0
. In this way compactness in
L^{2}(B,\mu)
implies compactness in
L^{2}(B,\mathfrak{m})
and, a fortiori, in
L^{2}(B,{\rm vol})
, since
\mathfrak{m}=e^{-U}{\rm vol}
and
c^{\prime}\leq e^{-U}\leq C^{\prime}
on
B
for some constants
c^{\prime},\,C^{\prime}>0
. More explicitly,

(3.6)

c^{\prime}T^{2}\int_{B}\left\lvert\nabla\varphi^{T}\right\rvert^{2}\,\mathrm{d}{\rm vol}\leq T^{2}\int_{B}\left\lvert\nabla\varphi^{T}\right\rvert^{2}\,\mathrm{d}\mathfrak{m}\leq\frac{T^{2}}{c}\int_{B}\left\lvert\nabla\varphi^{T}\right\rvert^{2}\,\mathrm{d}\mu\leq\frac{1}{c}\,\left\lVert T\nabla\varphi^{T}\right\rVert^{2}_{L^{2}(\mu)}

and the right-hand side is uniformly bounded in
T
by (
3.4
).

Now, inspired by the proof of
[
6
, Proposition 2.14]
, we observe that the Neumann Laplacian on the smooth compact Riemannian manifold with boundary
(\overline{B},g,{\rm vol}|_{\overline{B}})
has a spectral gap (see e.g.
[
29
]
). This is equivalent to the fact that
{\rm vol}|_{\overline{B}}
satisfies a Poincaré inequality, whence in particular

\int_{B}\left\lvert T\varphi^{T}\right\rvert^{2}\,\mathrm{d}{\rm vol}\leq\bigg(\int_{B}T\varphi^{T}\,\mathrm{d}{\rm vol}\bigg)^{2}+C_{P}\int_{B}\left\lvert T\nabla\varphi^{T}\right\rvert^{2}\,\mathrm{d}{\rm vol}

for some constant
C_{P}>0
. Note that the first term on the right-hand side is uniformly bounded in
T_{n}
, since by the fact that
\rho_{k}\geq c
(provided
k\geq c
) and
c^{\prime}{\rm vol}\leq\mathfrak{m}\leq C^{\prime}{\rm vol}

\mathfrak{m}
-a.e. in
B
, (
3.5
) implies
T_{n}\varphi^{T_{n}}\to\varphi^{0}
in
L^{1}(B,{\rm vol})
, so that
(\int_{B}T_{n}\varphi^{T_{n}}\,\mathrm{d}{\rm vol})^{2}
converges to
(\int_{B}\varphi^{0}\,\mathrm{d}{\rm vol})^{2}
as
T_{n}\downarrow 0
.
The second one is bounded as well by (
3.6
). Hence we obtain compactness in
L^{2}(B,{\rm vol})
for the subsequence
\{T_{n}\varphi^{T_{n}}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
.

As a consequence, there exist a non-relabeled subsequence and
\xi\in L^{2}(B,{\rm vol})
such that
T_{n}\varphi^{T_{n}}\rightharpoonup\xi
in
L^{2}(B,{\rm vol})
. On the other hand, by (
3.5
) we know that
T_{n}\varphi^{T_{n}}\to\varphi^{0}
in
L^{1}(\mu_{k})
. This implies that
\xi=\varphi^{0}

\mu_{k}
-a.s. on
B
and, as a byproduct, that

(3.7)

T_{n}\varphi^{T_{n}}\rightharpoonup\varphi^{0}\text{ in }L^{2}(B,{\rm vol})

without passing to a subsequence. Indeed let us consider an arbitrary non-negative
\phi\in C^{\infty}_{c}(B)
, so that
\phi\in L^{1}\cap L^{\infty}(B,{\rm vol})
, and start observing that

\left\lvert\int_{B}\phi(\varphi^{0}-\xi)\,\mathrm{d}{\rm vol}\right\rvert\leq\left\lvert\int_{B}\phi(\varphi^{0}-T_{n}\varphi^{T_{n}})\,\mathrm{d}{\rm vol}\right\rvert+\left\lvert\int_{B}\phi(T_{n}\varphi^{T_{n}}-\xi)\,\mathrm{d}{\rm vol}\right\rvert\,.

The second term vanishes since
T_{n}\varphi^{T_{n}}\rightharpoonup\xi
in
L^{2}(B,{\rm vol})
. As for the first one, note that

\begin{split}\left\lvert\int_{B}\phi(\varphi^{0}-T_{n}\varphi^{T_{n}})\,\mathrm{d}{\rm vol}\right\rvert&\leq\left\lVert\phi\right\rVert_{\infty}\int_{B}\left\lvert\varphi^{0}-T_{n}\varphi^{T_{n}}\right\rvert\,\mathrm{d}{\rm vol}\leq\frac{1}{c^{\prime}}\left\lVert\phi\right\rVert_{\infty}\int_{B}\left\lvert\varphi^{0}-T_{n}\varphi^{T_{n}}\right\rvert\,\mathrm{d}\mathfrak{m}\\
&\leq\frac{1}{cc^{\prime}}\left\lVert\phi\right\rVert_{\infty}\int_{B}\left\lvert\varphi^{0}-T_{n}\varphi^{T_{n}}\right\rvert\,\mathrm{d}\mu_{k}\leq\frac{1}{cc^{\prime}}\left\lVert\phi\right\rVert_{\infty}\left\lVert\varphi^{0}-T_{n}\varphi^{T_{n}}\right\rVert_{L^{1}(\mu_{k})}\end{split}

where for the second and third inequality we have used once more that
\mathfrak{m}\geq c^{\prime}{\rm vol}
and
\rho_{k}\geq c

\mathfrak{m}
-a.e. on
B
respectively. Hence, from
T_{n}\varphi^{T_{n}}\to\varphi^{0}
in
L^{1}(\mu_{k})
it follows the weak convergence
T_{n}\varphi^{T_{n}}\rightharpoonup\varphi^{0}
in
L^{2}(B,{\rm vol})
, without passing to any subsubsequence.

Now we are ready to prove the claim of this second step. Take
\beta\in C^{\infty}_{c}(B,TM)
and observe that the assumption
\mathcal{I}(\mu)<\infty
can be equivalently restated as
\nabla\sqrt{\rho}\in L^{2}(TM,\mathfrak{m})
. As a consequence, and by definition of
\rho_{k}
,
\nabla\sqrt{\rho_{k}}\in L^{2}(TM,\mathfrak{m})
too and this fact together with
\rho_{k}\leq k
justifies the validity of the chain rule
\nabla\rho_{k}=\nabla((\sqrt{\rho_{k}})^{2})=2\sqrt{\rho_{k}}\nabla\sqrt{\rho_{k}}
and proves that
|\nabla\rho_{k}|\in L^{2}(B,\mathfrak{m})
, whence the applicability of the integration by parts formula, so that

T\int\langle\nabla\varphi^{T},\beta\rangle\,\mathrm{d}\mu_{k}=-T\int\varphi^{T}{\rm div}(\beta)\,\mathrm{d}\mu_{k}-T\int\varphi^{T}\langle\beta,\nabla\rho_{k}\rangle\,\mathrm{d}\mathfrak{m}\,.

On the one hand, for the left-hand side we know that

\lim_{T_{n}\downarrow 0}T_{n}\int\langle\nabla\varphi^{T_{n}},\beta\rangle\,\mathrm{d}\mu_{k}=\int\langle\zeta_{k},\beta\rangle\,\mathrm{d}\mu_{k}=\int\langle\rho_{k}\,\zeta_{k},\beta\rangle\,\mathrm{d}\mathfrak{m}\,.

As concerns the right-hand side, the fact that
{\rm div}(\beta)
is bounded in
B
and the strong convergence in (
3.5
) ensure that

\lim_{T_{n}\downarrow 0}T_{n}\int\varphi^{T_{n}}{\rm div}(\beta)\,\mathrm{d}\mu_{k}=\int\varphi^{0}{\rm div}(\beta)\,\mathrm{d}\mu_{k}=\int\varphi^{0}\rho_{k}\,{\rm div}(\beta)\,\mathrm{d}\mathfrak{m}\,.

For the limit of the second term,
\langle\beta,\nabla\rho_{k}\rangle\in L^{2}(\mathfrak{m})
together with
\mathrm{supp}(\beta)\subset B
,
e^{-U/2}\leq\sqrt{C^{\prime}}
in
B
and
T\varphi^{T}\rightharpoonup\varphi^{0}
in
L^{2}(B,{\rm vol})
, proved in (
3.7
) above, allow us to deduce that

\lim_{T_{n}\downarrow 0}\,{T_{n}}\int\varphi^{T_{n}}\langle\beta,\nabla\rho_{k}\rangle\,\mathrm{d}\mathfrak{m}=\int\varphi^{0}\langle\beta,\nabla\rho_{k}\rangle\,\mathrm{d}\mathfrak{m}.

Hence, after rearrangement,

-\int\varphi^{0}\rho_{k}\,{\rm div}(\beta)\,\mathrm{d}\mathfrak{m}=\int\langle\rho_{k}\,\zeta_{k},\beta\rangle\,\mathrm{d}\mathfrak{m}+\int\varphi^{0}\langle\beta,\nabla\rho_{k}\rangle\,\mathrm{d}\mathfrak{m}=\int\langle\beta,\rho_{k}\,\zeta_{k}+\varphi^{0}\,\nabla\rho_{k}\rangle\,\mathrm{d}\mathfrak{m},

which means that
\nabla(\varphi^{0}\rho_{k})=\rho_{k}\,\zeta_{k}+\varphi^{0}\nabla\rho_{k}
on
B
by definition of weak gradient.

To conclude that
\zeta_{k}=\nabla\varphi^{0}
on
B
, recall that
\rho_{k}\in W^{1,2}(B)
. Using the lower bound
\rho_{k}\geq c

\mathfrak{m}
-a.e. on
B
, by chain rule we get that also
\rho_{k}^{-1}\in W^{1,2}(B)
and therefore, by Leibniz rule,

\nabla\varphi^{0}=\nabla(\varphi^{0}\rho_{k}\cdot\rho_{k}^{-1})=\frac{\nabla(\varphi^{0}\rho_{k})}{\rho_{k}}+\varphi^{0}\rho_{k}\nabla(\rho_{k}^{-1})=\zeta_{k}\qquad\mathfrak{m}\textrm{-a.e. on }B.

Since
B
was obtained starting from an arbitrary
x\in{\rm int}(\mathrm{supp}(\mu))
, we conclude that
\zeta_{k}=\nabla\varphi^{0}

\mathfrak{m}
-a.e. in
{\rm int}(\mathrm{supp}(\mu))
and therefore, up to changing the weak limit on a
\mu
-null set we have proven that

(3.8)

T_{n}\nabla\varphi^{T_{n}}\rightharpoonup\nabla\varphi^{0}\text{ in }L^{2}(TM,\mu_{k})\,.

3rd step: weak convergence of the gradients in
\mathbf{L^{2}(\bm{\mu})}
.
We now claim that the result above can be improved to

T_{n}\nabla\varphi^{T_{n}}\rightharpoonup\nabla\varphi^{0}\text{ in }L^{2}(TM,\mu)\,.

To this end, fix
\beta\in L^{2}\cap L^{\infty}(TM,\mu)
and start observing

\begin{split}\bigg|\int\langle T_{n}\nabla\varphi^{T_{n}}-\nabla\varphi^{0},\beta\rangle\,\mathrm{d}\mu\bigg|&\leq\bigg|T_{n}\int\langle\nabla\varphi^{T_{n}},\beta\rangle(\rho-\rho_{k})\,\mathrm{d}\mathfrak{m}\bigg|+\bigg|\int\langle T_{n}\nabla\varphi^{T_{n}}-\nabla\varphi^{0},\beta\rangle\,\mathrm{d}\mu_{k}\bigg|\\
&\quad+\bigg|\int\langle\nabla\varphi^{0},\beta\rangle(\rho-\rho_{k})\,\mathrm{d}\mathfrak{m}\bigg|\,.\end{split}

Recalling that
\mathcal{W}_{2}(\mu,\nu)=2\left\lVert\nabla\varphi^{0}\right\rVert_{L^{2}(TM,\mu)}
(see
[
2
, Theorem 10.3]
, paying attention to the different rescaling), we see that the third term on the right-hand side vanishes as
k\to\infty
by dominated convergence theorem. As concerns the first one, let us first estimate it as follows

\begin{split}\bigg|T_{n}\int\langle\nabla\varphi^{T_{n}},\beta\rangle(\rho-\rho_{k})\,\mathrm{d}\mathfrak{m}\bigg|&\leq T_{n}\int|\langle\nabla\varphi^{T_{n}},\beta\rangle|\Big(1-\frac{\rho_{k}}{\rho}\Big)\,\mathrm{d}\mu\leq\|\beta\|_{\infty}\int T_{n}|\nabla\varphi^{T_{n}}|\Big(1-\frac{\rho_{k}}{\rho}\Big)\,\mathrm{d}\mu\\
&\leq\|\beta\|_{\infty}\|T_{n}\nabla\varphi^{T_{n}}\|_{L^{2}(TM,\mu)}\Big\|1-\frac{\rho_{k}}{\rho}\Big\|_{L^{2}(\mu)}\end{split}

and then observe that
\|T_{n}\nabla\varphi^{T_{n}}\|_{L^{2}(TM,\mu)}
is bounded as
T_{n}\downarrow 0
by (
3.4
), while
\Big\|1-\frac{\rho_{k}}{\rho}\Big\|_{L^{2}(\mu)}\to 0
as
k\to\infty
by dominated convergence. This allows to deduce that, given
\varepsilon>0
there exists
k^{\prime}
independent of
T_{n}
so that

\bigg|\int\langle T_{n}\nabla\varphi^{T_{n}}-\nabla\varphi^{0},\beta\rangle\,\mathrm{d}\mu\bigg|\leq\bigg|\int\langle T_{n}\nabla\varphi^{T_{n}}-\nabla\varphi^{0},\beta\rangle\,\mathrm{d}\mu_{k^{\prime}}\bigg|+2\varepsilon.

Taking now the limit as
T_{n}\downarrow 0
, the right-hand side converges to
2\varepsilon
by (
3.8
) and the arbitrariness of
\varepsilon
together with the density of
L^{2}\cap L^{\infty}(TM,\mu)
in
L^{2}(TM,\mu)
allows to conclude that
T_{n}\nabla\varphi^{T_{n}}\rightharpoonup\nabla\varphi^{0}
in
L^{2}(TM,\mu)
as claimed.

4th step: strong convergence of the gradients in
\mathbf{L^{2}(\bm{\mu})}
.

The previous step and the uniqueness of the Brenier map
\tau=\operatorname{Id}-2\nabla\varphi^{0}
grant that the whole sequence of gradients converges

T\nabla\varphi^{T}\rightharpoonup\nabla\varphi^{0}\text{ in }L^{2}(TM,\mu_{k})\,,\qquad\forall k\in\mathbb{N},

which automatically implies

\liminf_{T\rightarrow 0}\left\lVert T\nabla\varphi^{T}_{0}\right\rVert_{L^{2}(TM,\mu_{k})}\geq\left\lVert\nabla\varphi^{0}\right\rVert_{L^{2}(TM,\mu_{k})}\,.

Recalling again that
\mathcal{W}_{2}(\mu,\nu)=2\left\lVert\nabla\varphi^{0}\right\rVert_{L^{2}(TM,\mu)}
, we deduce from (
3.4
) that

\lim_{T\rightarrow 0}\left\lVert T\nabla\varphi^{T}_{0}\right\rVert_{L^{2}(TM,\mu)}=\left\lVert\nabla\varphi^{0}\right\rVert_{L^{2}(TM,\mu)}.

Since in a Hilbert space such as
L^{2}(TM,\mu)
weak convergence plus convergence of the norm implies strong convergence, we obtain the desired conclusion.
∎

Remark 3.3
.

The previous proof runs exactly in the same way if we consider the entropic potentials
(\Phi_{T},\Psi_{T})
instead of the rescaled Schrödinger potentials
(T\varphi^{T},T\psi^{T})
. Moreover, the weak compactness in the first step can be proven without assuming finite Fisher information
\mathcal{I}(\mu)
, since

\left\lVert T\nabla\Phi_{T}\right\rVert_{L^{2}(\mu)}=\left\lVert T\nabla\varphi^{T}-T\nabla\log\rho\right\rVert_{L^{2}(\mu)}=T\,\left\lVert\psi^{T}_{0}\right\rVert_{L^{2}(\mu)}\,.

However, even though we are considering the entropic potentials
(\Phi_{T},\Psi_{T})
, the assumption of finite Fisher information
\mathcal{I}(\mu)<+{\infty}
is needed in the second step where we identify the weak limit of
\{\Phi_{T_{n}}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
with
\nabla\varphi^{0}
.

Corollary 3.4
.

Under the same assumptions of Theorem
1.1
, if
\mu
satisfies a Poincaré inequality, then the convergence of the potentials in Lemma
3.1
holds true also in
L^{2}(\mu)
.

4.
Proofs of the quantitative stability estimates

In this section we show how from the corrector estimates (

and as a byproduct we deduce in Proposition

a certain stability estimate for the entropic cost. Alongside we show how a finite Fisher information assumption gives similar bounds independent of the symmetric relative entropy between the marginals.
Finally, we conclude the section with an application of our results to the EOT setting with quadratic cost function.

Throughout the whole section
(f,g)
denotes the
fg
-decomposition for

while

denotes the corresponding one for
\mathcal{C}_{T}(\bar{\mu},\bar{\nu})
and we implicitly assume that both are renormalized according to (

Before moving to the proof of Theorem

Lemma 4.1
.

Assume (
CD
), (
I
) and (
H1
). Then
(P_{T}g)^{-1}\in L^{p}(\mathfrak{m})
and
(P_{T}f)^{-1}\in L^{p}(\mathfrak{m})
with

. More precisely, it holds

(4.1)

\displaystyle\left\lVert(P_{T}g)^{-1}\right\rVert_{p}\leq C_{1}\,\exp\left[\frac{M_{2}(\nu)}{T}-\frac{\mathcal{S}_{T}(\mu,\nu)}{2T}+C_{2}\,T\right]\,,

\displaystyle\left\lVert(P_{T}f)^{-1}\right\rVert_{p}\leq C_{1}\,\exp\left[\frac{M_{2}(\mu)}{T}-\frac{\mathcal{S}_{T}(\mu,\nu)}{2T}+C_{2}\,T\right]\,,

where the constants
C_{1}>0
and
C_{2}\geq 0
do not depend on the marginals
\mu,\nu
and neither on
f
or
g
. Moreover if
\kappa\geq 0
or if
{\sf CD}(\kappa,{\infty})
with
\mathfrak{m}(M)=1
holds, then
C_{2}=0
.

Proof.

We will only prove the first inequality since the proof of the second runs exactly in the same fashion. Firstly, notice that from Jensen’s inequality it follows

\displaystyle\log P_{T}g(x)

\displaystyle=\log\int g(y){\sf p}_{T}(x,y)\mathrm{d}\mathfrak{m}(y)=\log\int g(y){\sf p}_{T}(x,y)\sigma(y)^{-1}\mathrm{d}\nu(y)

\displaystyle\geq\int\log g\,\mathrm{d}\nu-\int\log\sigma\mathrm{d}\nu+\int\log{\sf p}_{T}(x,y)\,\mathrm{d}\nu(y)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\left[\mathcal{C}_{T}(\mu,\nu)-\mathcal{H}(\mu|\mathfrak{m})-\mathcal{H}(\nu|\mathfrak{m})\right]+\int\log{\sf p}_{T}(x,y)\,\mathrm{d}\nu(y)

\displaystyle=\frac{\mathcal{S}_{T}(\mu,\nu)}{2T}+\int\log{\sf p}_{T}(x,y)\,\mathrm{d}\nu(y)\,,

where the last step follows from (
2.9
).
On the one hand, if
(M,\,{\sf d},\,\mathfrak{m})
satisfies
{\sf CD}(\kappa,{\infty})
for some
\kappa\in\mathbb{R}
and if
\mathfrak{m}(M)=1
, then from (
2.11
) we deduce the lower bound

\displaystyle\log P_{T}g(x)\geq\frac{\mathcal{S}_{T}(\mu,\nu)}{2T}-\frac{{\sf d}^{2}(x,z_{0})}{T}-\frac{M_{2}(\nu)}{T}\,.

Therefore if we take
p=r\,T
we have, owing to (
I
),

\displaystyle\left\lVert(P_{T}g)^{-1}\right\rVert_{p}^{p}

\displaystyle=\int(P_{T}g)^{-p}\mathrm{d}\mathfrak{m}=\int e^{-p\log P_{T}g}\mathrm{d}\mathfrak{m}

\displaystyle\leq\exp\left[p\left(\frac{M_{2}(\nu)}{T}-\frac{\mathcal{S}_{T}(\mu,\nu)}{2T}\right)\right]\int e^{\frac{p}{T}{\sf d}^{2}(x,z_{0})}\mathrm{d}\mathfrak{m}(x)

\displaystyle\leq C_{1}^{p}\,\exp\left[p\left(\frac{M_{2}(\nu)}{T}-\frac{\mathcal{S}_{T}(\mu,\nu)}{2T}\right)\right]\,.

On the other hand if
{\sf CD}(\kappa,N)
holds with
N<+{\infty}
, the lower bound (
2.12
) leads to

\displaystyle\log P_{T}g(x)\geq\frac{\mathcal{S}_{T}(\mu,\nu)}{2T}-\log\left[C_{1}\,\mathfrak{m}\left(B_{\sqrt{T}}(x)\right)\right]-C_{2}\,T-\frac{{\sf d}^{2}(x,z_{0})}{T}-\frac{M_{2}(\nu)}{T}\,.

Therefore if we consider again
p=r\,T
, up to relabelling the positive constant
C_{1}
at each step, we have

\displaystyle\left\lVert(P_{T}g)^{-1}\right\rVert_{p}^{p}

\displaystyle=\int(P_{T}g)^{-p}\mathrm{d}\mathfrak{m}=\int e^{-p\log P_{T}g}\mathrm{d}\mathfrak{m}

\displaystyle\leq C_{1}^{p}\,\exp\left[p\left(\frac{M_{2}(\nu)}{T}-\frac{\mathcal{S}_{T}(\mu,\nu)}{2T}+C_{2}\,T\right)\right]\int\mathfrak{m}\left(B_{\sqrt{T}}(x)\right)^{p}e^{\frac{p}{T}\,{\sf d}^{2}(x,z_{0})}\mathrm{d}\mathfrak{m}(x)

\displaystyle\leq C_{1}^{p}\,\exp\left[p\left(\frac{M_{2}(\nu)}{T}-\frac{\mathcal{S}_{T}(\mu,\nu)}{2T}+C_{2}\,T\right)\right]\,.

This concludes our proof.
∎

We are now ready to prove the quantitative stability estimate for the optimizers of SP.

Proof of Theorem
1.2
.

Let us start with proving the first estimate (
1.10
). Let us assume that
\mathcal{H}^{\mathrm{sym}}(\mu,\bar{\mu}),\,\mathcal{H}^{\mathrm{sym}}(\nu,\bar{\nu})<{\infty}
, otherwise our claim is trivial. Firstly, notice that we can write

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}({\pi}^{{\mu}\to{\nu},{T}}|{\pi}^{{\bar{\mu}}\to{\bar{\nu}},{T}})

\displaystyle-\mathcal{H}(\nu|\bar{\nu})-\mathcal{H}(\mu|\bar{\mu})

\displaystyle=\int\log\frac{f(x)g(y)}{\bar{f}(x)\bar{g}(y)}\,\mathrm{d}{\pi}^{{\mu}\to{\nu},{T}}(x,y)-\int\log\frac{gP_{T}f}{\bar{g}P_{T}\bar{f}}\mathrm{d}\nu-\int\log\frac{fP_{T}g}{\bar{f}P_{T}\bar{g}}\mathrm{d}\mu

\displaystyle=\int\log\frac{P_{T}\bar{g}(x)\,P_{T}\bar{f}(y)}{P_{T}g(x)\,P_{T}f(y)}\,\mathrm{d}{\pi}^{{\mu}\to{\nu},{T}}(x,y)

\displaystyle=\int\biggl[\log P_{T}\bar{g}(x)+\log P_{T}\bar{f}(y)-\log P_{T}g(x)-\log P_{T}f(y)\biggr]\mathrm{d}{\pi}^{{\mu}\to{\nu},{T}}(x,y)\,.

We claim that the above right-hand side is equal to

(4.2)

\int\log P_{T}\bar{g}\mathrm{d}\mu+\int\log P_{T}\bar{f}\mathrm{d}\nu-\int\log P_{T}g\mathrm{d}\mu-\int\log P_{T}f\mathrm{d}\nu\,.

Since
\mathcal{H}(\mu|\mathfrak{m}),\,\mathcal{H}(\nu|\mathfrak{m})
are both finite, thanks to Proposition
2.2
we already know that
\log P_{T}g\in L^{1}(\mu)
and
\log P_{T}f\in L^{1}(\nu)
and that under our normalization (
2.9
) they read as

-\int\log P_{T}g\mathrm{d}\mu=-\int\log P_{T}f\mathrm{d}\nu=\frac{\mathcal{S}_{T}(\mu,\nu)}{2T}\in(-{\infty},+{\infty})\,.

Hence for the claim to be true it only remains to prove that

(4.3)

\left(\log P_{T}\bar{g}\right)^{-}\in L^{1}(\mu)\quad\text{ and }\quad\left(\log P_{T}\bar{f}\right)^{-}\in L^{1}(\nu)\,,

because this implies that
\int\log P_{T}\bar{g}\mathrm{d}\mu,\,\int\log P_{T}\bar{f}\mathrm{d}\nu>-{\infty}
, whence the fact that (
4.2
) is a well-defined summation. We will prove (
4.3
) by relying on Lemmata
4.1
and
A.1
. We consider two different cases.

1st case:
\mathfrak{m}\in\mathcal{P}(M)
.
Consider the positive measurable function

h=\mathbbm{1}_{\{P_{T}\bar{g}<1\}}\,P_{T}\bar{g}+\mathbbm{1}_{\{P_{T}\bar{g}\geq 1\}}

and notice that
(\log P_{T}\bar{g})^{-}=-\log h
. Since
\mathfrak{m}(M)=1
and
h\leq 1
we have
h\in L^{1}(\mathfrak{m})
; moreover
h^{-1}\in L^{rT}(\mathfrak{m})
since

\left\lVert h^{-1}\right\rVert_{rT}^{rT}=\int h^{-rT}\,\mathrm{d}\mathfrak{m}=\int\left[\mathbbm{1}_{\{P_{T}\bar{g}<1\}}(P_{T}\bar{g})^{-rT}+\mathbbm{1}_{\{P_{T}\bar{g}\geq 1\}}\right]\,\mathrm{d}\mathfrak{m}\leq\left\lVert(P_{T}\bar{g})^{-1}\right\rVert_{rT}^{rT}+\mathfrak{m}\{P_{T}\bar{g}\geq 1\}

and the right-hand side is finite because
\mathfrak{m}\left\{P_{T}\bar{g}\geq 1\right\}\leq 1
and
(P_{T}\bar{g})^{-1}\in L^{rT}(\mathfrak{m})
by Lemma
4.1
.
Therefore we may apply Lemma
A.1
with
\mathfrak{q}=\mathfrak{m}
and
\mathfrak{p}=\mu
and deduce

\displaystyle\int\left\lvert\log h\right\rvert\,\mathrm{d}\mu\leq 2\,e^{\mathcal{H}(\mu|\mathfrak{m})-1}\,\left\{\frac{1}{rT}\vee\log_{2}\left[1+\left(1+\left\lVert(P_{T}\bar{g})^{-1}\right\rVert_{rT}^{rT}\right)^{\frac{1}{rT}}\right]\right\}<+{\infty}\,.

Hence, we have proven that

\displaystyle 0\leq\int(\log P_{T}\bar{g})^{-}\,\mathrm{d}\mu=\int\left\lvert\log h\right\rvert\,\mathrm{d}\mu<+{\infty}\,,\quad\text{ which is equivalent to }\left(\log P_{T}\bar{g}\right)^{-}\in L^{1}(\mu)\,.

2nd case:
\mathfrak{m}\not\in\mathcal{P}(M)
.
The proof is similar to the previous case, however we work with the probability measure
\mathfrak{m}_{W}
(defined via (
2.7
)) instead of
\mathfrak{m}
. Indeed, since we are assuming
\mathcal{H}(\mu|\mathfrak{m})<+{\infty}
, there exists a positive measurable function
W\colon M\to[0,+{\infty})
such that

z_{W}=\int e^{-W}\mathrm{d}\mathfrak{m}<+{\infty}\,,\quad W\in L^{1}(\mu)\quad\text{ and }\quad\mathcal{H}(\mu|\mathfrak{m}_{W})<+{\infty}\,,

where
\mathfrak{m}_{W}\coloneqq z_{W}^{-1}e^{-W}\mathfrak{m}\in\mathcal{P}(M)
. By considering the same positive measurable function

h=\mathbbm{1}_{\{P_{T}\bar{g}<1\}}\,P_{T}\bar{g}+\mathbbm{1}_{\{P_{T}\bar{g}\geq 1\}}

we are guaranteed once again that
\left\lVert h\right\rVert_{L^{1}(\mathfrak{m}_{W})}\leq 1
and that

\displaystyle\left\lVert h^{-1}\right\rVert_{L^{rT}(\mathfrak{m}_{W})}^{rT}

\displaystyle=\int h^{-rT}\,\mathrm{d}\mathfrak{m}_{W}=\int\left[\mathbbm{1}_{\{P_{T}\bar{g}<1\}}\,(P_{T}\bar{g})^{-rT}+\mathbbm{1}_{\{P_{T}\bar{g}\geq 1\}}\right]\,\mathrm{d}\mathfrak{m}_{W}

\displaystyle\leq\left\lVert(P_{T}\bar{g})^{-1}\right\rVert^{rT}_{L^{rT}(\mathfrak{m}_{W})}+\mathfrak{m}_{W}\left\{P_{T}\bar{g}\geq 1\right\}\leq\left\lVert(P_{T}\bar{g})^{-1}\right\rVert^{rT}_{L^{rT}(\mathfrak{m}_{W})}+1\,,

By arguing in the same way as in the second half of Lemma

{\sf CD}(\kappa,N)
condition with

) we see that

\displaystyle\left\lVert(P_{T}\bar{g})^{-1}\right\rVert_{L^{rT}(\mathfrak{m}_{W})}^{rT}=\int(P_{T}\bar{g})^{-rT}\mathrm{d}\mathfrak{m}_{W}

\displaystyle\quad\leq C_{1}^{rT}\,\exp\left[rT\left(\frac{M_{2}(\bar{\nu})}{T}-\frac{\mathcal{S}_{T}(\bar{\mu},\bar{\nu})}{2T}+C_{2}\,T\right)\right]\int\mathfrak{m}\left(B_{\sqrt{T}}(x)\right)^{rT}e^{r\,{\sf d}^{2}(x,z_{0})}\mathrm{d}\mathfrak{m}_{W}(x)<+{\infty}\,,

where we have used the fact that

and the integrability condition (

is finite and once again from Lemma
A.1
(this time with
\mathfrak{q}=\mathfrak{m}_{W}
and
\mathfrak{p}=\mu
) we deduce

\displaystyle\int\left\lvert\log h\right\rvert\,\mathrm{d}\mu\leq 2\,e^{\mathcal{H}(\mu|\mathfrak{m}_{W})-1}\,\left\{\frac{1}{rT}\vee\log_{2}\left[1+\left(1+\left\lVert(P_{T}\bar{g})^{-1}\right\rVert_{L^{rT}(\mathfrak{m}_{W})}^{rT}\right)^{\frac{1}{rT}}\right]\right\}<+{\infty}\,.

Hence, we have proven that

\displaystyle 0\leq\int(\log P_{T}\bar{g})^{-}\,\mathrm{d}\mu=\int\left\lvert\log h\right\rvert\,\mathrm{d}\mu<+{\infty},\,\quad\text{ which is equivalent to }\left(\log P_{T}\bar{g}\right)^{-}\in L^{1}(\mu)\,.

By arguing in the same way we can also prove that
\left(\log P_{T}\bar{f}\right)^{-}\in L^{1}(\nu)
, whence the validity of (
4.3
). From this, we deduce that
\int\log P_{T}\bar{g}\mathrm{d}\mu,\,\int\log P_{T}\bar{f}\mathrm{d}\nu\in(-{\infty},+{\infty}]
and that (

) is a well-defined summation. As a consequence it follows

(4.4)

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}({\pi}^{{\mu}\to{\nu},{T}}|{\pi}^{{\bar{\mu}}\to{\bar{\nu}},{T}})

\displaystyle-\mathcal{H}(\nu|\bar{\nu})-\mathcal{H}(\mu|\bar{\mu})

\displaystyle=\int\log P_{T}\bar{g}\,\mathrm{d}\mu+\int\log P_{T}\bar{f}\,\mathrm{d}\nu-\int\log P_{T}g\,\mathrm{d}\mu-\int\log P_{T}f\,\mathrm{d}\nu\,.

By exchanging the roles of
\mu,\nu
and
\bar{\mu},\bar{\nu}
we can also write

(4.5)

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}({\pi}^{{\bar{\mu}}\to{\bar{\nu}},{T}}|{\pi}^{{\mu}\to{\nu},{T}})

\displaystyle-\mathcal{H}(\bar{\nu}|\nu)-\mathcal{H}(\bar{\mu}|\mu)

\displaystyle=\int\log P_{T}g\,\mathrm{d}\bar{\mu}+\int\log P_{T}f\,\mathrm{d}\bar{\nu}-\int\log P_{T}\bar{g}\,\mathrm{d}\bar{\mu}-\int\log P_{T}\bar{f}\,\mathrm{d}\bar{\nu}\,,

which added to (

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}^{\mathrm{sym}}({\pi}^{{\mu}\to{\nu},{T}},{\pi}^{{\bar{\mu}}\to{\bar{\nu}},{T}})=\mathcal{H}^{\mathrm{sym}}(\mu,\bar{\mu})+\mathcal{H}^{\mathrm{sym}}(\nu,\bar{\nu})-\int\log P_{T}g\,\mathrm{d}(\mu-\bar{\mu})-\int\log P_{T}\bar{g}\,\mathrm{d}(\bar{\mu}-\mu)

\displaystyle-\int\log P_{T}f\,\mathrm{d}(\nu-\bar{\nu})-\int\log P_{T}\bar{f}\,\mathrm{d}(\bar{\nu}-\nu)

Corollary 4.2
.

Let
(M,{\sf d},\mathfrak{m})
satisfy (
CD
) and (
I
). For any
(\mu,\nu)
and
(\bar{\mu},\bar{\nu})
satisfying (
H1
), (
H2
) and such that
\left\lVert\mu-\bar{\mu}\right\rVert_{\dot{H}^{-1}(\mu)},\,\left\lVert\bar{\mu}-\mu\right\rVert_{\dot{H}^{-1}(\bar{\mu})},\,\left\lVert\nu-\bar{\nu}\right\rVert_{\dot{H}^{-1}(\nu)}
, and

As a consequence we deduce that also

\log f,\,\log\bar{f}\in L^{1}(\mu)\cap L^{1}(\bar{\mu})\quad\text{ and }\quad\log g,\,\log\bar{g}\in L^{1}(\nu)\cap L^{1}(\bar{\nu})\,.

Proof.

As mentioned in the previous proof we already know, thanks to Proposition
2.2
, that

\log P_{T}g\in L^{1}(\mu),\,\log P_{T}f\in L^{1}(\nu),\,\log P_{T}\bar{g}\in L^{1}(\bar{\mu}),\text{ and }\log P_{T}\bar{f}\in L^{1}(\bar{\nu})\,.

Now, given our assumptions, the left-hand side of (
4.4
) is finite (thanks to Theorem
1.2
) and since we have
\log P_{T}g\in L^{1}(\mu),\,\log P_{T}f\in L^{1}(\nu)
and
\int\log P_{T}\bar{g}\mathrm{d}\mu,\,\int\log P_{T}\bar{f}\mathrm{d}\nu\in(-{\infty},+{\infty}]

\log P_{T}\bar{g}\in L^{1}(\mu)\quad\text{ and }\quad\log P_{T}\bar{f}\in L^{1}(\nu)\,.

Similarly, working with (

) we get
\log P_{T}g\in L^{1}(\bar{\mu})
and

.

Finally, from what we have just proven above and thanks to

being finite we deduce

\log\frac{f}{\bar{f}}\in L^{1}(\mu)\cap L^{1}(\bar{\mu})\quad\text{ and }\quad\log\frac{g}{\bar{g}}\in L^{1}(\nu)\cap L^{1}(\bar{\nu})\,,

which combined with
\log f\in L^{1}(\mu)
,
\log g\in L^{1}(\nu)
,

and
\log\bar{g}\in L^{1}(\bar{\nu})

∎

Resuming the proof of Theorem
1.2
.

Let us now prove (
1.11
). Without loss of generality we may assume that the Fisher information of the marginals and the negative Sobolev norms are all finite, otherwise the claimed estimate is trivial. Let us start by showing that this guarantees the finiteness of the symmetric relative entropies
\mathcal{H}^{\mathrm{sym}}(\mu,\bar{\mu}),\,\mathcal{H}^{\mathrm{sym}}(\nu,\bar{\nu})
. Indeed, since
\mathcal{I}(\mu)<+{\infty}
we have
\nabla\log\frac{\mathrm{d}\mu}{\mathrm{d}\mathfrak{m}}\in L^{2}(\mu)
and therefore by the definition of
\left\lVert\mu-\bar{\mu}\right\rVert_{\dot{H}^{-1}(\mu)}
we deduce

\left\lvert\int\log\frac{\mathrm{d}\mu}{\mathrm{d}\mathfrak{m}}\,\mathrm{d}(\mu-\bar{\mu})\right\rvert\leq\sqrt{\mathcal{I}(\mu)}\,\left\lVert\mu-\bar{\mu}\right\rVert_{\dot{H}^{-1}(\mu)}<+{\infty}\,.

Thanks to the above bound and to the finiteness of
\mathcal{H}(\mu|\mathfrak{m})
and
\mathcal{H}(\bar{\mu}|\mathfrak{m})
we are allowed to write

\displaystyle\int\log\frac{\mathrm{d}\bar{\mu}}{\mathrm{d}\mu}\,\mathrm{d}\bar{\mu}=\int\left[\frac{\mathrm{d}\bar{\mu}}{\mathrm{d}\mathfrak{m}}\log\frac{\mathrm{d}\bar{\mu}}{\mathrm{d}\mathfrak{m}}+\left(\frac{\mathrm{d}\mu}{\mathrm{d}\mathfrak{m}}-\frac{\mathrm{d}\bar{\mu}}{\mathrm{d}\mathfrak{m}}\right)\log\frac{\mathrm{d}\mu}{\mathrm{d}\mathfrak{m}}-\frac{\mathrm{d}\mu}{\mathrm{d}\mathfrak{m}}\log\frac{\mathrm{d}\mu}{\mathrm{d}\mathfrak{m}}\right]\mathrm{d}\mathfrak{m}

\displaystyle=\mathcal{H}(\bar{\mu}|\mathfrak{m})-\mathcal{H}(\mu|\mathfrak{m})+\int\log\frac{\mathrm{d}\mu}{\mathrm{d}\mathfrak{m}}\,\mathrm{d}(\mu-\bar{\mu})<+{\infty}\,,

which reads as
\mathcal{H}(\bar{\mu}|\mu)<+{\infty}
. Let us just mention that above the Radon-Nikodym derivative is well defined since, under our assumption,
\bar{\mu}\sim\mu
are equivalent. Indeed, if there were a Borel subset
A\subset M
such that
\mu(A)=0
and
\bar{\mu}(A)>0
, then by choosing as test function
h
(a mollified version of)
\mathbbm{1}_{A}
we would get
\left\lVert\mu-\bar{\mu}\right\rVert_{\dot{H}^{-1}(\mu)}=+{\infty}
which we are assuming to be finite; therefore
\bar{\mu}\ll\mu
. Similarly one can prove
\mu\ll\bar{\mu}
, and hence
\bar{\mu}\sim\mu
. We have thus proven that
\mathcal{H}(\bar{\mu}|\mu)
is finite. By reasoning in the same fashion one can prove that

\mathcal{H}(\mu|\bar{\mu})
,
\mathcal{H}(\bar{\nu}|\nu)
and
\mathcal{H}(\nu|\bar{\nu})
are also finite, whence the finiteness of the symmetric entropies.

At this stage, the proof is similar to the one already presented above. It is indeed enough to notice that

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}^{\mathrm{sym}}

\displaystyle({\pi}^{{\mu}\to{\nu},{T}},{\pi}^{{\bar{\mu}}\to{\bar{\nu}},{T}})

\displaystyle=\int\log f\,\mathrm{d}(\mu-\bar{\mu})+\int\log\bar{f}\,\mathrm{d}(\bar{\mu}-\mu)+\int\log g\,\mathrm{d}(\nu-\bar{\nu})+\int\log\bar{g}\,\mathrm{d}(\bar{\nu}-\nu)

\displaystyle\leq\left\lVert\nabla\log f\right\rVert_{L^{2}(\mu)}\left\lVert\mu-\bar{\mu}\right\rVert_{\dot{H}^{-1}(\mu)}+\left\lVert\nabla\log\bar{f}\right\rVert_{L^{2}(\bar{\mu})}\left\lVert\bar{\mu}-\mu\right\rVert_{\dot{H}^{-1}(\bar{\mu})}

\displaystyle+\left\lVert\nabla\log g\right\rVert_{L^{2}(\nu)}\left\lVert\nu-\bar{\nu}\right\rVert_{\dot{H}^{-1}(\nu)}+\left\lVert\nabla\log\bar{g}\right\rVert_{L^{2}(\bar{\nu})}\left\lVert\bar{\nu}-\nu\right\rVert_{\dot{H}^{-1}(\bar{\nu})}\,,

and that we can bound the
L^{2}
-norm of the gradient of the potentials in terms of the Fisher information, e.g.

\left\lVert\nabla\log f\right\rVert_{L^{2}(\mu)}\leq\left\lVert\nabla\log\frac{\mathrm{d}\mu}{\mathrm{d}\mathfrak{m}}\right\rVert_{L^{2}(\mu)}+\left\lVert\nabla\log P_{T}g\right\rVert_{L^{2}(\mu)}=\sqrt{\mathcal{I}(\mu)}+\left\lVert\nabla\log P_{T}g\right\rVert_{L^{2}(\mu)}

and analogously for the other three summands.
∎

Before moving to the proof of the stability estimates presented in (
1.12
) and (
1.13
), we need another technical result given by the following

Lemma 4.3
.

Let
(M,{\sf d},\mathfrak{m})
satisfy (
CD
) and (
I
). For any
(\mu,\nu)
and
(\bar{\mu},\bar{\nu})
satisfying (
H1
), (
H2
) and such that
\left\lVert\mu-\bar{\mu}\right\rVert_{\dot{H}^{-1}(\mu)},\,\left\lVert\bar{\mu}-\mu\right\rVert_{\dot{H}^{-1}(\bar{\mu})},\,\left\lVert\nu-\bar{\nu}\right\rVert_{\dot{H}^{-1}(\nu)}
, and
\left\lVert\bar{\nu}-\nu\right\rVert_{\dot{H}^{-1}(\bar{\nu})}
are all finite and with
\mathcal{H}^{\mathrm{sym}}(\mu,\bar{\mu}),\,\mathcal{H}^{\mathrm{sym}}(\nu,\bar{\nu})<{\infty}
, it holds

\displaystyle\int\log\frac{P_{T}f}{P_{T}\bar{f}}\,\mathrm{d}\nu\leq\int\log\frac{f}{\bar{f}}\,\mathrm{d}\mu\qquad\text{and}\qquad\int\log\frac{P_{T}g}{P_{T}\bar{g}}\,\mathrm{d}\mu\leq\int\log\frac{g}{\bar{g}}\,\mathrm{d}\nu\,.

Analogous bounds hold when we exchange the roles between
(\mu,\nu,f,g)
and
(\bar{\mu},\bar{\nu},\bar{f},\bar{g})
.

Proof.

Since
\mathcal{H}(\nu|\bar{\nu})\leq\mathcal{H}({\pi}^{{\mu}\to{\nu},{T}}|{\pi}^{{\mu}\to{\bar{\nu}},{T}})
, by means of the
fg
-decomposition the inequality reads as

\int\log\frac{P_{T}f}{P_{T}\bar{f}}\mathrm{d}\nu+\int\log\frac{g}{\bar{g}}\mathrm{d}\nu\leq\int\log\frac{f}{\bar{f}}\mathrm{d}\mu+\int\log\frac{g}{\bar{g}}\mathrm{d}\nu\,,

which yields the first inequality. The other bound can be proven in the same way.
∎

By following the same line of reasoning applied for the stability of the optimal plans, we can now deduce the following

Proposition 4.4
.

Let
(M,{\sf d},\mathfrak{m})
satisfy (
CD
) and (
I
). For any
(\mu,\nu)
and
(\bar{\mu},\bar{\nu})
satisfying (
H1
), (
H2
), and such that
\left\lVert\mu-\bar{\mu}\right\rVert_{\dot{H}^{-1}(\mu)},\,\left\lVert\bar{\mu}-\mu\right\rVert_{\dot{H}^{-1}(\bar{\mu})},\,\left\lVert\nu-\bar{\nu}\right\rVert_{\dot{H}^{-1}(\nu)}
, and
\left\lVert\bar{\nu}-\nu\right\rVert_{\dot{H}^{-1}(\bar{\nu})}
are all finite and with
\mathcal{H}^{\mathrm{sym}}(\mu,\bar{\mu}),\,\mathcal{H}^{\mathrm{sym}}(\nu,\bar{\nu})<{\infty}
, it holds

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}_{T}(\mu,\nu)-\mathcal{S}_{T}(\bar{\mu},\bar{\nu})\leq T\biggl[\mathcal{H}(\bar{\mu}|\mu)\wedge\mathcal{H}(\bar{\nu}|\nu)\biggr]

\displaystyle+\frac{T}{\sqrt{E_{2\kappa}(T)}}\,\sqrt{\mathcal{C}_{T}(\mu,\nu)-\mathcal{H}(\mu|\mathfrak{m})}\left\lVert\mu-\bar{\mu}\right\rVert_{\dot{H}^{-1}(\mu)}

\displaystyle+\frac{T}{\sqrt{E_{2\kappa}(T)}}\,\sqrt{\mathcal{C}_{T}(\mu,\nu)-\mathcal{H}(\nu|\mathfrak{m})}\left\lVert\nu-\bar{\nu}\right\rVert_{\dot{H}^{-1}(\nu)},

where
E_{2\kappa}
is defined as in (
1.8
), from which it follows

\displaystyle\left\lvert\mathcal{S}_{T}(\bar{\mu},\bar{\nu})-\mathcal{S}_{T}(\mu,\nu)\right\rvert\leq T\biggl[\mathcal{H}^{\mathrm{sym}}(\mu,\bar{\mu})\wedge\mathcal{H}^{\mathrm{sym}}(\nu,\bar{\nu})\biggr]

\displaystyle+\frac{T}{\sqrt{E_{2\kappa}(T)}}\biggl[\sqrt{\mathcal{C}_{T}(\mu,\nu)-\mathcal{H}(\mu|\mathfrak{m})}\left\lVert\mu-\bar{\mu}\right\rVert_{\dot{H}^{-1}(\mu)}+\sqrt{\mathcal{C}_{T}(\mu,\nu)-\mathcal{H}(\nu|\mathfrak{m})}\left\lVert\nu-\bar{\nu}\right\rVert_{\dot{H}^{-1}(\nu)}\biggr]

\displaystyle+\frac{T}{\sqrt{E_{2\kappa}(T)}}\biggl[\sqrt{\mathcal{C}_{T}(\bar{\mu},\bar{\nu})-\mathcal{H}(\bar{\mu}|\mathfrak{m})}\left\lVert\bar{\mu}-\mu\right\rVert_{\dot{H}^{-1}(\bar{\mu})}+\sqrt{\mathcal{C}_{T}(\bar{\mu},\bar{\nu})-\mathcal{H}(\bar{\nu}|\mathfrak{m})}\left\lVert\bar{\nu}-\nu\right\rVert_{\dot{H}^{-1}(\bar{\nu})}\biggr]\,.

Proof.

First of all, since the assumptions of Corollary
4.2
are met, the following computations are all well defined. With this said, let us start by noticing that

\mathcal{S}_{T}(\mu,\nu)=T\,\mathcal{C}_{T}(\mu,\nu)-T\,\mathcal{H}(\mu|\mathfrak{m})-T\,\mathcal{H}(\nu|\mathfrak{m})=-T\int\log P_{T}g\,\mathrm{d}\mu-T\int\log P_{T}f\,\mathrm{d}\nu\,,

and therefore

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}_{T}(\bar{\mu},\bar{\nu})-\mathcal{S}_{T}(\mu,\nu)=T\int\log P_{T}g\,\mathrm{d}\mu+T\int\log P_{T}f\,\mathrm{d}\nu-T\int\log P_{T}\bar{g}\,\mathrm{d}\bar{\mu}-T\int\log P_{T}\bar{f}\,\mathrm{d}\bar{\nu}

\displaystyle=T\int\log\frac{P_{T}g}{P_{T}\bar{g}}\mathrm{d}\mu+T\int\log\frac{P_{T}f}{P_{T}\bar{f}}\mathrm{d}\nu-T\int\log P_{T}\bar{g}\,\mathrm{d}(\bar{\mu}-\mu)-T\int\log P_{T}\bar{f}\,\mathrm{d}(\bar{\nu}-\nu)\,.

Applying Lemma
4.3
we deduce that

(4.6)

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}_{T}(\bar{\mu},\bar{\nu})-\mathcal{S}_{T}(\mu,\nu)

\displaystyle\leq T\int\log\frac{P_{T}g}{P_{T}\bar{g}}\mathrm{d}\mu+T\int\log\frac{f}{\bar{f}}\mathrm{d}\mu

\displaystyle-T\int\log P_{T}\bar{g}\,\mathrm{d}(\bar{\mu}-\mu)-T\int\log P_{T}\bar{f}\,\mathrm{d}(\bar{\nu}-\nu)

\displaystyle=T\int\log\frac{\mathrm{d}\mu}{\mathrm{d}\bar{\mu}}\mathrm{d}\mu-T\int\log P_{T}\bar{g}\,\mathrm{d}(\bar{\mu}-\mu)-T\int\log P_{T}\bar{f}\,\mathrm{d}(\bar{\nu}-\nu)

\displaystyle=T\mathcal{H}(\mu|\bar{\mu})-T\int\log P_{T}\bar{g}\,\mathrm{d}(\bar{\mu}-\mu)-T\int\log P_{T}\bar{f}\,\mathrm{d}(\bar{\nu}-\nu)\,.

Notice that above we could have used Lemma
4.3
on the other integral
\int\log\frac{P_{T}g}{P_{T}\bar{g}}\mathrm{d}\mu
and therefore we would have got
\mathcal{H}(\nu|\bar{\nu})
instead of
\mathcal{H}(\mu|\bar{\mu})
. Therefore we can state that

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}_{T}(\bar{\mu},\bar{\nu})-\mathcal{S}_{T}(\mu,\nu)

\displaystyle\leq T\Bigl[\mathcal{H}(\mu|\bar{\mu})\wedge\mathcal{H}(\nu|\bar{\nu})\Bigr]-T\int\log P_{T}\bar{g}\,\mathrm{d}(\bar{\mu}-\mu)-T\int\log P_{T}\bar{f}\,\mathrm{d}(\bar{\nu}-\nu)

\displaystyle\leq T\Bigl[\mathcal{H}(\mu|\bar{\mu})\wedge\mathcal{H}(\nu|\bar{\nu})\Bigr]+T\left\lVert\nabla\log P_{T}\bar{g}\right\rVert_{L^{2}(\bar{\mu})}\left\lVert\bar{\mu}-\mu\right\rVert_{\dot{H}^{-1}(\bar{\mu})}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad+T\left\lVert\nabla\log P_{T}\bar{f}\right\rVert_{L^{2}(\bar{\nu})}\left\lVert\bar{\nu}-\nu\right\rVert_{\dot{H}^{-1}(\bar{\nu})}.

By exchanging the roles between
(\mu,\nu)
and
(\bar{\mu},\bar{\nu})
and by arguing in the same way we also get

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}_{T}(\mu,\nu)-\mathcal{S}_{T}(\bar{\mu},\bar{\nu})\leq T\Bigl[\mathcal{H}(\bar{\mu}|\mu)\wedge\mathcal{H}(\bar{\nu}|\nu)\Bigr]

\displaystyle+T\left\lVert\nabla\log P_{T}g\right\rVert_{L^{2}(\mu)}\left\lVert\mu-\bar{\mu}\right\rVert_{\dot{H}^{-1}(\mu)}

\displaystyle+T\left\lVert\nabla\log P_{T}f\right\rVert_{L^{2}(\nu)}\left\lVert\nu-\bar{\nu}\right\rVert_{\dot{H}^{-1}(\nu)},

and therefore we deduce

\displaystyle\left\lvert\mathcal{S}_{T}(\bar{\mu},\bar{\nu})-\mathcal{S}_{T}(\mu,\nu)\right\rvert\leq

\displaystyle\,T\Bigl[\mathcal{H}^{\mathrm{sym}}(\mu,\bar{\mu})\wedge\mathcal{H}^{\mathrm{sym}}(\nu,\bar{\nu})\Bigr]

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\,T\left\lVert\nabla\log P_{T}g\right\rVert_{L^{2}(\mu)}\left\lVert\mu-\bar{\mu}\right\rVert_{\dot{H}^{-1}(\mu)}+T\left\lVert\nabla\log P_{T}f\right\rVert_{L^{2}(\nu)}\left\lVert\nu-\bar{\nu}\right\rVert_{\dot{H}^{-1}(\nu)}

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\,T\left\lVert\nabla\log P_{T}\bar{g}\right\rVert_{L^{2}(\bar{\mu})}\left\lVert\bar{\mu}-\mu\right\rVert_{\dot{H}^{-1}(\bar{\mu})}+T\left\lVert\nabla\log P_{T}\bar{f}\right\rVert_{L^{2}(\bar{\nu})}\left\lVert\bar{\nu}-\nu\right\rVert_{\dot{H}^{-1}(\bar{\nu})}.

Given the above, the thesis follows from the corrector estimates (cf. Proposition
2.4
).
∎

Remark 4.5
.

Notice that in the cases where we change just one marginal (e.g.
\mu=\bar{\mu}
), we can get rid of the relative entropy between the marginals in the above result. Therefore in order to get a bound without the symmetric relative entropy of the marginals it is enough to consider the case where one of the marginals is frozen by means of the trivial inequality

(4.7)

\left\lvert\mathcal{S}_{T}(\bar{\mu},\bar{\nu})-\mathcal{S}_{T}(\mu,\nu)\right\rvert\leq\left\lvert\mathcal{S}_{T}(\bar{\mu},\bar{\nu})-\mathcal{S}_{T}(\mu,\bar{\nu})\right\rvert+\left\lvert\mathcal{S}_{T}(\mu,\bar{\nu})-\mathcal{S}_{T}(\mu,\nu)\right\rvert\,.

Let us conclude by showing that, under the finite Fisher information assumption, we may write a stability estimate for the Schrödinger costs without involving the symmetric relative entropies on the right-hand side.

Proposition 4.6
.

Let
(M,{\sf d},\mathfrak{m})
satisfy (
CD
) and (
I
). For any
(\mu,\nu)
and
(\bar{\mu},\bar{\nu})
satisfying (

) and (

) we have

\displaystyle\left\lvert\mathcal{C}_{T}(\bar{\mu},\bar{\nu})-\mathcal{C}_{T}(\mu,\nu)\right\rvert\leq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\sqrt{E_{2\kappa}(T)}}\biggl[\sqrt{\mathcal{I}(\mu)}+\sqrt{\mathcal{C}_{T}(\mu,\nu)-\mathcal{H}(\mu|\mathfrak{m})}\biggr]\left\lVert\mu-\bar{\mu}\right\rVert_{\dot{H}^{-1}(\mu)}

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\sqrt{E_{2\kappa}(T)}}\biggl[\sqrt{\mathcal{I}(\bar{\mu})}+\sqrt{\mathcal{C}_{T}(\bar{\mu},\bar{\nu})-\mathcal{H}(\bar{\mu}|\mathfrak{m})}\biggr]\left\lVert\bar{\mu}-\mu\right\rVert_{\dot{H}^{-1}(\bar{\mu})}

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\sqrt{E_{2\kappa}(T)}}\biggl[\sqrt{\mathcal{I}(\nu)}+\sqrt{\mathcal{C}_{T}(\mu,\nu)-\mathcal{H}(\nu|\mathfrak{m})}\biggr]\left\lVert\nu-\bar{\nu}\right\rVert_{\dot{H}^{-1}(\nu)}

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\sqrt{E_{2\kappa}(T)}}\biggl[\sqrt{\mathcal{I}(\bar{\nu})}+\sqrt{\mathcal{C}_{T}(\bar{\mu},\bar{\nu})-\mathcal{H}(\bar{\nu}|\mathfrak{m})}\biggr]\left\lVert\bar{\nu}-\nu\right\rVert_{\dot{H}^{-1}(\bar{\nu})}\,,

where

is defined as in (

).

Proof.

The proof runs like the one given in Proposition
4.4
observing that, similarly to (

), we can write

\mathcal{C}_{T}(\bar{\mu},\bar{\nu})-\mathcal{C}_{T}(\mu,\nu)\leq\int\log\bar{f}\,\mathrm{d}(\bar{\mu}-\mu)+\int\log\bar{g}\,\mathrm{d}(\bar{\nu}-\nu)-\biggl[\mathcal{H}(\mu|\bar{\mu})\vee\mathcal{H}(\nu|\bar{\nu})\biggr]

and therefore it holds

\begin{split}\left\lvert\mathcal{C}_{T}(\bar{\mu},\bar{\nu})-\mathcal{C}_{T}(\mu,\nu)\right\rvert\leq\left\lVert\nabla\log f\right\rVert_{L^{2}(\mu)}\left\lVert\mu-\bar{\mu}\right\rVert_{\dot{H}^{-1}(\mu)}+\left\lVert\nabla\log\bar{f}\right\rVert_{L^{2}(\bar{\mu})}\left\lVert\bar{\mu}-\mu\right\rVert_{\dot{H}^{-1}(\bar{\mu})}\\
+\left\lVert\nabla\log g\right\rVert_{L^{2}(\nu)}\left\lVert\nu-\bar{\nu}\right\rVert_{\dot{H}^{-1}(\nu)}+\left\lVert\nabla\log\bar{g}\right\rVert_{L^{2}(\bar{\nu})}\left\lVert\bar{\nu}-\nu\right\rVert_{\dot{H}^{-1}(\bar{\nu})}\,.\end{split}

∎

Notice that in the above result we are able to consider the stability directly between the Schrödinger costs

and

. This is indeed due to the fact that we are working in a finite Fisher information setting.

4.1.
Application to EOT with quadratic cost

In this section we translate the stability results stated in Theorem
1.2
and Proposition
4.4
to the Euclidean EOT setting with quadratic cost

(4.8)

\mathcal{S}^{\varepsilon}(\mu,\nu)\coloneqq\inf_{\pi\in\Pi(\mu,\nu)}\int|x-y|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}\pi+\varepsilon\,\mathcal{H}(\pi|\mu\otimes\nu)\,,\qquad\varepsilon>0\,.

We will manage to do that under sufficiently general conditions, so that our results will apply to any couple of marginals
\mu,\nu\in\mathcal{P}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
with finite relative entropy w.r.t. the Lebesgue measure
\mathcal{L}_{d}
on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
and with densities w.r.t.
\mathcal{L}_{d}
locally bounded away from
0
on their support and such that
\mu(\partial\mathrm{supp}(\mu))=\nu(\partial\mathrm{supp}(\nu))=0

For later reference, let
{\pi}^{{\mu},{\nu}}_{\varepsilon}
denote the minimizer (which exists and is unique since

, cf.
[
40

).

In what follows we are going to consider a Schrödinger problem equivalent to the above (
4.8
), where the underlying stochastic dynamics is given by the law of the Ornstein–Uhlenbeck process

(4.9)

\mathrm{d}X_{t}=-\kappa X_{t}\mathrm{d}t+\sqrt{2}\,\mathrm{d}B_{t}\,,

(B_{t})_{t}
being a
d

\kappa>0
a curvature parameter (whose value will be specified later). The above SDE admits as unique invariant measure the Gaussian distribution
\mathfrak{m}\sim\mathcal{N}\bigl(0,\kappa^{-1}\operatorname{Id}\bigr)

\mathrm{d}\mathfrak{m}(x)\sim e^{-\frac{\kappa}{2}\left\lvert x\right\rvert^{2}}\mathrm{d}x

{\sf CD}(\kappa,{\infty})

\kappa^{-1}

[
5

. Then, Herbst’s argument
[
5

implies that
\mathfrak{m}
satisfies the integrability condition (
I

r<\kappa/2
. Notice that for any choice of
\kappa>0
we have

(4.10)

\mathcal{H}(\mu|\mathfrak{m})=\mathcal{H}(\mu|\mathcal{L}_{d})+\frac{\kappa}{2}\,M_{2}(\mu)+\frac{d}{2}\log\frac{2\pi}{\kappa}<+{\infty}

and similarly
\mathcal{H}(\nu|\mathfrak{m})<+{\infty}
.

Now, let us consider a final time in SP such that
\frac{\varepsilon\kappa}{4}=\sinh(\kappa T)
, i.e.

(4.11)

T\coloneqq\frac{1}{\kappa}\log\left(\frac{\varepsilon\kappa}{4}+\sqrt{\frac{\varepsilon^{2}\kappa^{2}}{16}+1}\right)\,

\mathrm{R}_{0,T}=\mathcal{L}(X_{0},X_{T})

0
and
T
of the Ornstein–Uhlenbeck process solving (
4.9

\mathfrak{m}
, whose density is given by the transition kernel

{\sf p}_{T}(x,y)=\frac{\mathrm{d}\mathrm{R}_{0,T}}{\mathrm{d}(\mathfrak{m}\otimes\mathfrak{m})}(x,y)=\frac{1}{(1-e^{-2\kappa T})^{\frac{d}{2}}}\,\exp\left\{-\frac{\left\lvert x\right\rvert^{2}-2e^{\kappa T}\,x\cdot y+\left\lvert y\right\rvert^{2}}{\frac{2}{\kappa}(e^{2\kappa T}-1)}\right\}\,.

Then, thanks to our choice of
T
, for any coupling
\pi\in\Pi(\mu,\nu)
it holds

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2d}}\left\lvert x-y\right\rvert^{2}\mathrm{d}\pi+\varepsilon\,\mathcal{H}(\pi|\mu\otimes\nu)=M_{2}(\mu)+M_{2}(\nu)-2\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2d}}x\cdot y\,\mathrm{d}\pi+\varepsilon\,\mathcal{H}(\pi|\mu\otimes\nu)

\displaystyle=-\frac{d\varepsilon}{2}\log(1-e^{-2\kappa T})+(1-e^{-\kappa T})\bigl(M_{2}(\mu)+M_{2}(\nu)\bigr)-\varepsilon\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2d}}\log{\sf p}_{T}(x,y)\,\mathrm{d}\pi+\varepsilon\,\mathcal{H}(\pi|\mu\otimes\nu)

\displaystyle=-\frac{d\varepsilon}{2}\log(1-e^{-2\kappa T})+(1-e^{-\kappa T})\bigl(M_{2}(\mu)+M_{2}(\nu)\bigr)+\varepsilon\left[\mathcal{H}(\pi|\mu\otimes\nu)-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2d}}\log\frac{\mathrm{d}\mathrm{R}}{\mathrm{d}(\mathfrak{m}\otimes\mathfrak{m})}\,\mathrm{d}\pi\right]

\displaystyle=-\frac{d\varepsilon}{2}\log(1-e^{-2\kappa T})+(1-e^{-\kappa T})\bigl(M_{2}(\mu)+M_{2}(\nu)\bigr)+\varepsilon\,\mathcal{H}(\pi|\mathrm{R}_{0,T})-\varepsilon\,\mathcal{H}(\mu|\mathfrak{m})-\varepsilon\,\mathcal{H}(\nu|\mathfrak{m})\,.

Therefore the above EOT problem (
4.8
) has the same minimizer of SP with reference given by the above-chosen
\mathrm{R}_{0,T}
and their values are linked according to the following identity

(4.12)

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}^{\varepsilon}(\mu,\nu)=

\displaystyle\varepsilon\,\mathcal{C}_{T}(\mu,\nu)-\varepsilon\,\mathcal{H}(\mu|\mathfrak{m})-\varepsilon\,\mathcal{H}(\nu|\mathfrak{m})-\frac{d\varepsilon}{2}\log(1-e^{-2\kappa T})+(1-e^{-\kappa T})\bigl(M_{2}(\mu)+M_{2}(\nu)\bigr)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\varepsilon}{T}\,\mathcal{S}_{T}(\mu,\nu)-\frac{d\varepsilon}{2}\log(1-e^{-2\kappa T})+(1-e^{-\kappa T})\bigl(M_{2}(\mu)+M_{2}(\nu)\bigr)\,.

Since we are interested in stability results, take also
\bar{\mu},\,\bar{\nu}\in\mathcal{P}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\frac{\varepsilon}{\sqrt{E_{2\kappa}(T)}}\biggl[\sqrt{\mathcal{C}_{T}(\mu,\nu)-\mathcal{H}(\mu|\mathfrak{m})}\left\lVert\mu-\bar{\mu}\right\rVert_{\dot{H}^{-1}(\mu)}+\sqrt{\mathcal{C}_{T}(\mu,\nu)-\mathcal{H}(\nu|\mathfrak{m})}\left\lVert\nu-\bar{\nu}\right\rVert_{\dot{H}^{-1}(\nu)}\biggr]

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\frac{\varepsilon}{\sqrt{E_{2\kappa}(T)}}\biggl[\sqrt{\mathcal{C}_{T}(\bar{\mu},\bar{\nu})-\mathcal{H}(\bar{\mu}|\mathfrak{m})}\left\lVert\bar{\mu}-\mu\right\rVert_{\dot{H}^{-1}(\bar{\mu})}+\sqrt{\mathcal{C}_{T}(\bar{\mu},\bar{\nu})-\mathcal{H}(\bar{\nu}|\mathfrak{m})}\left\lVert\bar{\nu}-\nu\right\rVert_{\dot{H}^{-1}(\bar{\nu})}\biggr]\,.

Since for any coupling

\displaystyle|\mathcal{S}^{\varepsilon}

\displaystyle+\left(1-e^{-\kappa T}\right)\left(\sqrt{M_{2}(\bar{\mu})}+\sqrt{M_{2}(\mu)}\right)\mathcal{W}_{2}(\bar{\mu},\mu)+\left(1-e^{-\kappa T}\right)\left(\sqrt{M_{2}(\bar{\nu})}+\sqrt{M_{2}(\nu)}\right)\mathcal{W}_{2}(\bar{\nu},\nu)

\displaystyle+\frac{\varepsilon}{\sqrt{E_{2\kappa}(T)}}\biggl[\sqrt{\mathcal{C}_{T}(\mu,\nu)-\mathcal{H}(\mu|\mathfrak{m})}\left\lVert\mu-\bar{\mu}\right\rVert_{\dot{H}^{-1}(\mu)}+\sqrt{\mathcal{C}_{T}(\mu,\nu)-\mathcal{H}(\nu|\mathfrak{m})}\left\lVert\nu-\bar{\nu}\right\rVert_{\dot{H}^{-1}(\nu)}\biggr]

\displaystyle+\frac{\varepsilon}{\sqrt{E_{2\kappa}(T)}}\biggl[\sqrt{\mathcal{C}_{T}(\bar{\mu},\bar{\nu})-\mathcal{H}(\bar{\mu}|\mathfrak{m})}\left\lVert\bar{\mu}-\mu\right\rVert_{\dot{H}^{-1}(\bar{\mu})}+\sqrt{\mathcal{C}_{T}(\bar{\mu},\bar{\nu})-\mathcal{H}(\bar{\nu}|\mathfrak{m})}\left\lVert\bar{\nu}-\nu\right\rVert_{\dot{H}^{-1}(\bar{\nu})}\biggr]\,.

Similarly, since

(4.15)

\displaystyle\varepsilon

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}^{\mathrm{sym}}({\pi}^{{\mu},{\nu}}_{\varepsilon},{\pi}^{{\bar{\mu}},{\bar{\nu}}}_{\varepsilon})=\frac{\varepsilon}{T}\,T\,\mathcal{H}^{\mathrm{sym}}({\pi}^{{\mu}\to{\nu},{T}},{\pi}^{{\bar{\mu}}\to{\bar{\nu}},{T}})\leq\,\varepsilon\,\mathcal{H}^{\mathrm{sym}}(\mu,\bar{\mu})+\varepsilon\,\mathcal{H}^{\mathrm{sym}}(\nu,\bar{\nu})

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\frac{\varepsilon}{\sqrt{E_{2\kappa}(T)}}\biggl[\sqrt{\mathcal{C}_{T}(\mu,\nu)-\mathcal{H}(\mu|\mathfrak{m})}\left\lVert\mu-\bar{\mu}\right\rVert_{\dot{H}^{-1}(\mu)}+\sqrt{\mathcal{C}_{T}(\mu,\nu)-\mathcal{H}(\nu|\mathfrak{m})}\left\lVert\nu-\bar{\nu}\right\rVert_{\dot{H}^{-1}(\nu)}\biggr]

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\frac{\varepsilon}{\sqrt{E_{2\kappa}(T)}}\biggl[\sqrt{\mathcal{C}_{T}(\bar{\mu},\bar{\nu})-\mathcal{H}(\bar{\mu}|\mathfrak{m})}\left\lVert\bar{\mu}-\mu\right\rVert_{\dot{H}^{-1}(\bar{\mu})}+\sqrt{\mathcal{C}_{T}(\bar{\mu},\bar{\nu})-\mathcal{H}(\bar{\nu}|\mathfrak{m})}\left\lVert\bar{\nu}-\nu\right\rVert_{\dot{H}^{-1}(\bar{\nu})}\biggr]\,.

Given the above bounds one can now get explicit estimates by choosing the curvature parameter
\kappa>0
.

Remark 4.7

(Small-noise limit)
.

Let us point out that the above bounds are stable when
\varepsilon\to 0
. Indeed (
4.11
) guarantees us that the small-noise limit
\varepsilon\to 0
is equivalent to the small-time limit
T\to 0
and moreover the ratio
\frac{\varepsilon}{T}
stays finite since

\displaystyle\frac{\varepsilon}{T}=\frac{\varepsilon\kappa}{\kappa T}=\frac{4\sinh(\kappa T)}{\kappa T}=2\,\frac{e^{\kappa T}-e^{-\kappa T}}{\kappa T}\,\to\,4\,,\quad\text{as $T\to 0$.}

Therefore in the small-noise limit, from (
1.14
) we deduce for (
4.8
) the following stability results

(4.16)

\displaystyle\limsup_{\varepsilon\to 0}\left\lvert\mathcal{S}^{\varepsilon}(\bar{\mu},\bar{\nu})-\mathcal{S}^{\varepsilon}(\mu,\nu)\right\rvert\leq

\displaystyle 2\,\mathcal{W}_{2}(\mu,\nu)\,\biggl[\left\lVert\mu-\bar{\mu}\right\rVert_{\dot{H}^{-1}(\mu)}+\left\lVert\nu-\bar{\nu}\right\rVert_{\dot{H}^{-1}(\nu)}\biggr]

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle 2\,\mathcal{W}_{2}(\bar{\mu},\bar{\nu})\,\biggl[\left\lVert\bar{\mu}-\mu\right\rVert_{\dot{H}^{-1}(\bar{\mu})}+\left\lVert\bar{\nu}-\nu\right\rVert_{\dot{H}^{-1}(\bar{\nu})}\biggr]\,,

\displaystyle\limsup_{\varepsilon\to 0}\,\varepsilon\,\mathcal{H}^{\mathrm{sym}}({\pi}^{{\mu},{\nu}}_{\varepsilon},{\pi}^{{\bar{\mu}},{\bar{\nu}}}_{\varepsilon})\leq

\displaystyle 2\,\mathcal{W}_{2}(\mu,\nu)\,\biggl[\left\lVert\mu-\bar{\mu}\right\rVert_{\dot{H}^{-1}(\mu)}+\left\lVert\nu-\bar{\nu}\right\rVert_{\dot{H}^{-1}(\nu)}\biggr]

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle 2\,\mathcal{W}_{2}(\bar{\mu},\bar{\nu})\,\biggl[\left\lVert\bar{\mu}-\mu\right\rVert_{\dot{H}^{-1}(\bar{\mu})}+\left\lVert\bar{\nu}-\nu\right\rVert_{\dot{H}^{-1}(\bar{\nu})}\biggr]\,,

which agree, up to a scaling constant, with Remark
1.7
in the Schrödinger setting.

Notice that the above small-noise limit is independent from the choice of
\kappa>0
. This suggests us to take the limit
\kappa\downarrow 0
directly in (
4.14
) and (
4.15
). Indeed from (
4.11
) it follows
T\to\,\varepsilon/4
as
\kappa\downarrow 0
.
Furthermore, by rearranging (
4.12
) we notice that

\begin{split}T\,\mathcal{C}_{T}(\mu,\nu)-T\,\mathcal{H}(\mu|\mathfrak{m})&=\frac{T}{\varepsilon}\,\mathcal{S}^{\varepsilon}(\mu,\nu)-\frac{T}{\varepsilon}(1-e^{-\kappa T})\biggl(M_{2}(\mu)+M_{2}(\nu)\biggr)+T\,\mathcal{H}(\nu|\mathfrak{m})\\
&\qquad+\frac{d\,T}{2}\log\left(1-e^{-2\kappa T}\right)\\
&=\frac{T}{\varepsilon}\,\mathcal{S}^{\varepsilon}(\mu,\nu)-\frac{T}{\varepsilon}(1-e^{-\kappa T})\biggl(M_{2}(\mu)+M_{2}(\nu)\biggr)+T\,\mathcal{H}(\nu|\mathcal{L}_{d})\\
&\qquad+\frac{\kappa T}{2}M_{2}(\nu)+\frac{d\,T}{2}\log\left(4\pi T\,\frac{1-e^{-2\kappa T}}{2\kappa T}\right)\end{split}

and therefore we have

\lim_{\kappa\to 0}\,T\,\mathcal{C}_{T}(\mu,\nu)-T\,\mathcal{H}(\mu|\mathfrak{m})=\frac{1}{4}\,\mathcal{S}^{\varepsilon}(\mu,\nu)+\frac{\varepsilon}{4}\,\mathcal{H}(\mu|\mathcal{L}_{d})+\frac{1}{4}\,C_{\varepsilon}\,,

where
C_{\varepsilon}=\frac{d\varepsilon}{2}\log(4\pi\varepsilon)
.
As a consequence, by taking the limit in the right-hand sides of (
4.14
) and (
4.15
), we deduce that

\displaystyle\left\lvert\mathcal{S}^{\varepsilon}(\bar{\mu},\bar{\nu})-\mathcal{S}^{\varepsilon}(\mu,\nu)\right\rvert\leq\varepsilon\Bigl[\mathcal{H}^{\mathrm{sym}}(\mu,\bar{\mu})\wedge\mathcal{H}^{\mathrm{sym}}(\nu,\bar{\nu})

\displaystyle\Bigr]

\displaystyle+2\biggl[\sqrt{\mathcal{S}^{\varepsilon}(\mu,\nu)+\varepsilon\,\mathcal{H}(\nu|\mathcal{L}_{d})+C_{\varepsilon}}\left\lVert\mu-\bar{\mu}\right\rVert_{\dot{H}^{-1}(\mu)}

\displaystyle+\sqrt{\mathcal{S}^{\varepsilon}(\mu,\nu)+\varepsilon\,\mathcal{H}(\mu|\mathcal{L}_{d})+C_{\varepsilon}}\left\lVert\nu-\bar{\nu}\right\rVert_{\dot{H}^{-1}(\nu)}\biggr]

\displaystyle+2\biggl[\sqrt{\mathcal{S}^{\varepsilon}(\bar{\mu},\bar{\nu})+\varepsilon\,\mathcal{H}(\bar{\nu}|\mathcal{L}_{d})+C_{\varepsilon}}\left\lVert\bar{\mu}-\mu\right\rVert_{\dot{H}^{-1}(\bar{\mu})}

\displaystyle+\sqrt{\mathcal{S}^{\varepsilon}(\bar{\mu},\bar{\nu})+\varepsilon\,\mathcal{H}(\bar{\mu}|\mathcal{L}_{d})+C_{\varepsilon}}\left\lVert\bar{\nu}-\nu\right\rVert_{\dot{H}^{-1}(\bar{\nu})}\biggr]

and

\displaystyle\varepsilon\,\mathcal{H}^{\mathrm{sym}}({\pi}^{{\mu},{\nu}}_{\varepsilon},{\pi}^{{\bar{\mu}},{\bar{\nu}}}_{\varepsilon})\leq\,\varepsilon\,\mathcal{H}^{\mathrm{sym}}(\mu,\bar{\mu})+\varepsilon\,\mathcal{H}^{\mathrm{sym}}(\nu,\bar{\nu})

\displaystyle+2\biggl[\sqrt{\mathcal{S}^{\varepsilon}(\mu,\nu)+\varepsilon\,\mathcal{H}(\nu|\mathcal{L}_{d})+C_{\varepsilon}}\left\lVert\mu-\bar{\mu}\right\rVert_{\dot{H}^{-1}(\mu)}

\displaystyle+\sqrt{\mathcal{S}^{\varepsilon}(\mu,\nu)+\varepsilon\,\mathcal{H}(\mu|\mathcal{L}_{d})+C_{\varepsilon}}\left\lVert\nu-\bar{\nu}\right\rVert_{\dot{H}^{-1}(\nu)}\biggr]

\displaystyle+2\biggl[\sqrt{\mathcal{S}^{\varepsilon}(\bar{\mu},\bar{\nu})+\varepsilon\,\mathcal{H}(\bar{\nu}|\mathcal{L}_{d})+C_{\varepsilon}}\left\lVert\bar{\mu}-\mu\right\rVert_{\dot{H}^{-1}(\bar{\mu})}

\displaystyle+\sqrt{\mathcal{S}^{\varepsilon}(\bar{\mu},\bar{\nu})+\varepsilon\,\mathcal{H}(\bar{\mu}|\mathcal{L}_{d})+C_{\varepsilon}}\left\lVert\bar{\nu}-\nu\right\rVert_{\dot{H}^{-1}(\bar{\nu})}\biggr]\,.

Notice that from the above bounds, we get the validity of (
4.16
) in the small-noise limit
\varepsilon\downarrow 0
,.

Appendix A
A log-integrability Lemma

Let us start by recalling a few interesting facts about Orlicz spaces.
Let
\mathfrak{q}
be a probability measure on a measurable space
(\Omega,\Sigma)
and consider the Young functions

\displaystyle\theta(t)\coloneqq e^{t}-1\,,\qquad\theta^{*}(s)=\begin{cases}s\,\log s-s+1\quad&\text{if }s>0,\\
1\quad&\text{if }s=0\,.\end{cases}

where
\theta^{*}(s)\coloneqq\sup_{t\in\mathbb{R}}\bigl\{st-\theta(t)\bigr\}
. The Orlicz space associated to the Young function
\theta
, denoted by
L_{\theta}(\mathfrak{q})
, is defined as the space of measurable functions
f:\Omega\to\mathbb{R}
with finite Luxemburg norm

\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{\theta}\coloneqq\inf\left\{b>0\colon\int\theta\left(\frac{\left\lvert f\right\rvert}{b}\right)\mathrm{d}\mathfrak{q}\leq 1\right\}\,.

To be precise we should make explicit reference to the underlying probability
\mathfrak{q}
, when talking about Luxemburg norms; however we will omit such reference when it is clear from the context.

When dealing with finite relative entropies, the Orlicz space associated to
\theta^{*}
(defined analogously as for
\theta
) plays a natural role. Indeed, for any probability
\mathfrak{p}
with
\mathcal{H}(\mathfrak{p}|\mathfrak{q})<+\infty
we have

\displaystyle\left\lVert\frac{\mathrm{d}\mathfrak{p}}{\mathrm{d}\mathfrak{q}}\right\rVert_{\theta^{*}}=

\displaystyle\inf\left\{b>0\colon\int\theta^{*}\left(\frac{1}{b}\,\frac{\mathrm{d}\mathfrak{p}}{\mathrm{d}\mathfrak{q}}\right)\mathrm{d}\mathfrak{q}\leq 1\right\}=\inf\left\{b>0\colon\frac{1}{b}\mathcal{H}(\mathfrak{p}|\mathfrak{q})-\frac{1}{b}\left(1+\log b\right)+1\leq 1\right\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\inf\left\{b>0\colon\mathcal{H}(\mathfrak{p}|\mathfrak{q})-1\leq\log b\right\}=e^{\mathcal{H}(\mathfrak{p}|\mathfrak{q})-1}\,.

Finally, let us recall that for any
f\in L_{\theta}(\mathfrak{q})
and
g\in L_{\theta^{*}}(\mathfrak{q})
we have

(A.1)

\int\left\lvert fg\right\rvert\mathrm{d}\mathfrak{q}\leq 2\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{\theta}\,\left\lVert g\right\rVert_{\theta^{*}}\,,

which is a consequence of the trivial inequality

st\leq\theta(t)+\theta^{*}(s)\quad\forall s,t>0\,.

After this digression, let us prove the following lemma, which is pivotal in our reasoning since it allows to deduce the log-integrability of any positive measurable function under the sole hypotheses of finite relative entropy and some positive and negative integrability of the same function.

Lemma A.1
.

Let
h
be a positive measurable function and
\mathfrak{p},\mathfrak{q}\in\mathcal{P}(\Omega)
with
\mathcal{H}(\mathfrak{p}|\mathfrak{q})<{\infty}
. If there exist
p,\,q>0
such that
h\in L^{q}(\mathfrak{q})
and
h^{-1}\in L^{p}(\mathfrak{q})
, then
\log h\in L^{1}(\mathfrak{p})
. More precisely, it holds

(A.2)

\int\left\lvert\log h\right\rvert\mathrm{d}\mathfrak{p}\leq 2\,e^{\mathcal{H}(\mathfrak{p}|\mathfrak{q})-1}\,\left[\frac{1}{1\wedge p\wedge q}\vee\log_{2}\left(\mathfrak{q}\{h\geq 1\}^{1-\frac{1}{q}}\left\lVert h\right\rVert_{L^{q}(\mathfrak{q})}+\mathfrak{q}\{h<1\}^{1-\frac{1}{p}}\left\lVert h^{-1}\right\rVert_{L^{p}(\mathfrak{q})}\right)\right]\,,

and also

(A.3)

\int\left\lvert\log h\right\rvert\mathrm{d}\mathfrak{p}\leq\frac{2\,e^{\mathcal{H}(\mathfrak{p}|\mathfrak{q})-1}}{p\wedge q}\,\left[1\vee\log_{2}\left(\left\lVert h\right\rVert_{L^{q}(\mathfrak{q})}^{p\wedge q}+\left\lVert h^{-1}\right\rVert_{L^{p}(\mathfrak{q})}^{p\wedge q}\right)\right]\,.

Proof.

By means of the Orlicz-Young inequality (
A.1
) we have

(A.4)

\int\left\lvert\log h\right\rvert\mathrm{d}\mathfrak{p}=\int\left\lvert\frac{\mathrm{d}\mathfrak{p}}{\mathrm{d}\mathfrak{q}}\,\log h\right\rvert\mathrm{d}\mathfrak{q}\leq 2\left\lVert\log h\right\rVert_{\theta}\,\left\lVert\frac{\mathrm{d}\mathfrak{p}}{\mathrm{d}\mathfrak{q}}\right\rVert_{\theta^{*}}=2\left\lVert\log h\right\rVert_{\theta}\,e^{\mathcal{H}(\mathfrak{p}|\mathfrak{q})-1}\,.

Therefore it suffices to show that the above Luxemburg norm
\left\lVert\log h\right\rVert_{\theta}
is finite.
For any
b>0
we have

(A.5)

\displaystyle\int\theta\left(\frac{\left\lvert\log h\right\rvert}{b}\right)\mathrm{d}\mathfrak{q}=\int\theta\left(\left\lvert\log h^{\frac{1}{b}}\right\rvert\right)\mathrm{d}\mathfrak{q}=\int_{\{h\geq 1\}}h^{\frac{1}{b}}\mathrm{d}\mathfrak{q}+\int_{\{h<1\}}h^{-\frac{1}{b}}\mathrm{d}\mathfrak{q}-1\,.

Before proceeding, let us point out that the above right-hand side is always non-negative since it is greater or equal to
\mathfrak{q}\{h\geq 1\}+\mathfrak{q}\{h<1\}-1=0
.

Now consider any parameter
\delta\in(0,{\infty})
(to be fixed later)
and fix
b\geq\frac{1}{\delta\wedge p\wedge q}
.
For the sake of notation, let
P\coloneqq\{h\geq 1\}
and its complementary
N\coloneqq\{h<1\}
. In what follows we are going to assume that both
\mathfrak{q}(P)>0
and
\mathfrak{q}(N)>0
; the case
\mathfrak{q}(P)\in\{0,1\}
can be treated in the same fashion. Now, introduce the probability measures on these sets induced by
\mathfrak{q}
, i.e.

\mathrm{d}\mathfrak{q}_{|_{P}}(x)\coloneqq\frac{\mathrm{d}\mathfrak{q}(x)}{\mathfrak{q}(P)}\qquad\text{and}\qquad\mathrm{d}\mathfrak{q}_{|_{N}}(x)\coloneqq\frac{\mathrm{d}\mathfrak{q}(x)}{\mathfrak{q}(N)}\,.

Since
qb\geq 1
, from Jensen’s inequality we deduce that

\displaystyle\int_{\{h\geq 1\}}h^{\frac{1}{b}}\mathrm{d}\mathfrak{q}=\mathfrak{q}(P)\int_{P}h^{\frac{1}{b}}\mathrm{d}\mathfrak{q}_{|_{P}}=\mathfrak{q}(P)\int_{P}h^{q\,\frac{1}{qb}}\mathrm{d}\mathfrak{q}_{|_{P}}\leq\mathfrak{q}(P)\left(\int_{P}h^{q}\mathrm{d}\mathfrak{q}_{|_{P}}\right)^{\frac{1}{qb}}

\displaystyle=\mathfrak{q}(P)^{1-\frac{1}{qb}}\left(\int_{P}h^{q}\mathrm{d}\mathfrak{q}\right)^{\frac{1}{qb}}\leq\mathfrak{q}(P)^{1-\frac{1}{qb}}\left\lVert h\right\rVert_{L^{q}(\mathfrak{q})}^{\frac{1}{b}}\,.

Similarly, from
pb\geq 1
we deduce that

\displaystyle\int_{\{h<1\}}\left(\frac{1}{h}\right)^{\frac{1}{b}}\mathrm{d}\mathfrak{q}=\mathfrak{q}(N)\int_{N}h^{-\frac{1}{b}}\mathrm{d}\mathfrak{q}_{|_{N}}=\mathfrak{q}(N)\int_{N}h^{-p\,\frac{1}{pb}}\mathrm{d}\mathfrak{q}_{|_{N}}\leq\mathfrak{q}(N)\left(\int_{N}h^{-p}\mathrm{d}\mathfrak{q}_{|_{N}}\right)^{\frac{1}{pb}}

\displaystyle=\mathfrak{q}(N)^{1-\frac{1}{pb}}\left(\int_{N}h^{-p}\mathrm{d}\mathfrak{q}\right)^{\frac{1}{pb}}\leq\mathfrak{q}(N)^{1-\frac{1}{pb}}\left\lVert h^{-1}\right\rVert_{L^{p}(\mathfrak{q})}^{\frac{1}{b}}\,.

For the sake of brevity let
\lambda\coloneqq\mathfrak{q}(P)
. Since also
\delta b\geq 1
, from (
A.5
) and the concavity of
x^{\frac{1}{\delta b}}
we deduce

\begin{split}\int\theta\left(\frac{\left\lvert\log h\right\rvert}{b}\right)\mathrm{d}\mathfrak{q}&\leq\lambda^{1-\frac{1}{qb}}\left\lVert h\right\rVert_{L^{q}(\mathfrak{q})}^{\frac{1}{b}}+(1-\lambda)^{1-\frac{1}{pb}}\left\lVert h^{-1}\right\rVert_{L^{p}(\mathfrak{q})}^{\frac{1}{b}}-1\\
&=\lambda\left(\frac{\left\lVert h\right\rVert_{L^{q}(\mathfrak{q})}^{\delta}}{\lambda^{\frac{\delta}{q}}}\right)^{\frac{1}{\delta b}}+(1-\lambda)\left(\frac{\left\lVert h^{-1}\right\rVert_{L^{p}(\mathfrak{q})}^{\delta}}{(1-\lambda)^{\frac{\delta}{p}}}\right)^{\frac{1}{\delta b}}-1\\
&\leq\left(\lambda^{1-\frac{\delta}{q}}\left\lVert h\right\rVert_{L^{q}(\mathfrak{q})}^{\delta}+(1-\lambda)^{1-\frac{\delta}{p}}\left\lVert h^{-1}\right\rVert_{L^{p}(\mathfrak{q})}^{\delta}\right)^{\frac{1}{\delta b}}-1\leq 1\,,\end{split}

where the last inequality holds as soon as
b\geq\frac{1}{\delta}\,\log_{2}\left(\lambda^{1-\frac{\delta}{q}}\left\lVert h\right\rVert_{L^{q}(\mathfrak{q})}^{\delta}+(1-\lambda)^{1-\frac{\delta}{p}}\left\lVert h^{-1}\right\rVert_{L^{p}(\mathfrak{q})}^{\delta}\right)
. Therefore, if we take

\bar{b}\coloneqq\frac{1}{\delta\wedge p\wedge q}\vee\frac{1}{\delta}\,\log_{2}\left(\lambda^{1-\frac{\delta}{q}}\left\lVert h\right\rVert_{L^{q}(\mathfrak{q})}^{\delta}+(1-\lambda)^{1-\frac{\delta}{p}}\left\lVert h^{-1}\right\rVert_{L^{p}(\mathfrak{q})}^{\delta}\right)\,,

we deduce that
\int\theta\left(\frac{\left\lvert\log h\right\rvert}{\bar{b}}\right)\mathrm{d}\mathfrak{q}\leq 1
,
and hence

(A.6)

\left\lVert\log h\right\rVert_{\theta}\leq\bar{b}=\frac{1}{\delta\wedge p\wedge q}\vee\frac{1}{\delta}\,\log_{2}\left(\lambda^{1-\frac{\delta}{q}}\left\lVert h\right\rVert_{L^{q}(\mathfrak{q})}^{\delta}+(1-\lambda)^{1-\frac{\delta}{p}}\left\lVert h^{-1}\right\rVert_{L^{p}(\mathfrak{q})}^{\delta}\right)\,.

From this last result and (
A.4
) it follows that
\log h\in L^{1}(\mathfrak{q})
and that

(A.7)

\int\left\lvert\log h\right\rvert\mathrm{d}\mathfrak{p}\leq 2\,e^{\mathcal{H}(\mathfrak{p}|\mathfrak{q})-1}\,\left[\frac{1}{\delta\wedge p\wedge q}\vee\frac{1}{\delta}\,\log_{2}\left(\lambda^{1-\frac{\delta}{q}}\left\lVert h\right\rVert_{L^{q}(\mathfrak{q})}^{\delta}+(1-\lambda)^{1-\frac{\delta}{p}}\left\lVert h^{-1}\right\rVert_{L^{p}(\mathfrak{q})}^{\delta}\right)\right]\,.

By taking
\delta=1
we immediately get (
A.2
). In order to get the second bound, take
\delta\coloneqq p\wedge q
. Then (
A.6
) reads as

(A.8)

\displaystyle\left\lVert\log h\right\rVert_{\theta}\leq\frac{1}{p\wedge q}\left[1\vee\log_{2}\left(\lambda^{1-\frac{p\wedge q}{q}}\left\lVert h\right\rVert_{L^{q}(\mathfrak{q})}^{p\wedge q}+(1-\lambda)^{1-\frac{p\wedge q}{p}}\left\lVert h^{-1}\right\rVert_{L^{p}(\mathfrak{q})}^{p\wedge q}\right)\right]\,.

Since
\lambda=\mathfrak{q}(P)\in[0,1]
we deduce that

\displaystyle\log_{2}\left(\mathfrak{q}(P)^{1-\frac{p\wedge q}{q}}\left\lVert h\right\rVert_{L^{q}(\mathfrak{q})}^{p\wedge q}+(1-\mathfrak{q}(P))^{1-\frac{p\wedge q}{p}}\left\lVert h^{-1}\right\rVert_{L^{p}(\mathfrak{q})}^{p\wedge q}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\log_{2}\sup_{\lambda\in[0,1]}\left(\lambda^{1-\frac{p\wedge q}{q}}\left\lVert h\right\rVert_{L^{q}(\mathfrak{q})}^{p\wedge q}+(1-\lambda)^{1-\frac{p\wedge q}{p}}\left\lVert h^{-1}\right\rVert_{L^{p}(\mathfrak{q})}^{p\wedge q}\right)

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}\log_{2}\left(\left\lVert h\right\rVert_{L^{q}(\mathfrak{q})}^{p}+\left\lVert h^{-1}\right\rVert_{L^{p}(\mathfrak{q})}^{p}\right)\quad\text{if }p\leq q\\
\log_{2}\left(\left\lVert h\right\rVert_{L^{q}(\mathfrak{q})}^{q}+\left\lVert h^{-1}\right\rVert_{L^{p}(\mathfrak{q})}^{q}\right)\quad\text{if }q<p\end{cases}

\displaystyle=\log_{2}\left(\left\lVert h\right\rVert_{L^{q}(\mathfrak{q})}^{p\wedge q}+\left\lVert h^{-1}\right\rVert_{L^{p}(\mathfrak{q})}^{p\wedge q}\right)\,,

and hence

(A.9)

\left\lVert\log h\right\rVert_{\theta}\leq\frac{1}{p\wedge q}\left[1\vee\log_{2}\left(\left\lVert h\right\rVert_{L^{q}(\mathfrak{q})}^{p\wedge q}+\left\lVert h^{-1}\right\rVert_{L^{p}(\mathfrak{q})}^{p\wedge q}\right)\right]\,.

As a byproduct of the above bound and (
A.4
) we get (
A.3
).
∎

Remark A.2
.

When
\mathfrak{q}\{h\geq 1\}\in\{0,1\}
, the bound (
A.3
) can be improved. Indeed from (
A.8
) we straightforwardly deduce that

(A.10)

\displaystyle\int\left\lvert\log h\right\rvert\mathrm{d}\mathfrak{p}\leq\,\begin{cases}2\,e^{\mathcal{H}(\mathfrak{p}|\mathfrak{q})-1}\,\displaystyle{\bigg(\frac{1}{p\wedge q}\vee\log_{2}\left\lVert h\right\rVert_{L^{q}(\mathfrak{q})}\bigg)}\qquad&\text{if }\mathfrak{q}\{h\geq 1\}=1\,,\\
\\
2\,e^{\mathcal{H}(\mathfrak{p}|\mathfrak{q})-1}\,\displaystyle{\bigg(\frac{1}{p\wedge q}\vee\log_{2}\left\lVert h^{-1}\right\rVert_{L^{p}(\mathfrak{q})}\bigg)}\qquad&\text{if }\mathfrak{q}\{h\geq 1\}=0\,.\end{cases}

Corollary A.3
.

If
p\wedge q\leq 1
in the previous Lemma, then it holds

(A.11)

\int\left\lvert\log h\right\rvert\mathrm{d}\mathfrak{p}\leq 2\,e^{\mathcal{H}(\mathfrak{p}|\mathfrak{q})-1}\,\left[\frac{1}{p\wedge q}+\left(\log_{2}\,\frac{\left\lVert h\right\rVert_{L^{q}(\mathfrak{q})}+\left\lVert h^{-1}\right\rVert_{L^{p}(\mathfrak{q})}}{2}\right)^{+}\,\right]\,.

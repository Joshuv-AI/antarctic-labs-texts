Mathematical theory of compressible magnetohydrodynamics driven by non–conservative boundary conditions
Eduard Feireisl, Piotr Gwiazda, Young–Sam Kwon, Agnieszka Świerczewska-Gwiazda

Abstract
We propose a new concept of weak solution to the equations of compressible magnetohydrodynamics driven by large boundary data. The system of the underlying field equations is solvable globally in time in the out of equilibrium regime characteristic for turbulence. The weak solutions comply with the weak–strong uniqueness principle; they coincide with the classical solution of the problem as long as the latter exists. The choice of constitutive relations is motivated by applications in stellar magnetoconvection.

1
Introduction

The large scale dynamics arising in stellar magnetoconvection is driven in an essential way by the boundary
conditions, see Gough
[
11
]
. The flux separation and pattern formation arise in the turbulent regime when the fluid flow is far from equilibrium. As the underlying field equations are non–linear, the existence of global in time smooth
solutions in such a regime is not known. Indeed the vast majority of
global
existence results in the class of classical solutions
is restricted to the initial state close to a stable equilibrium, see a.g. Matsumura and Nishida
[
13
]
,
Valli
[
18
]
, among others. What is more, the recent results of Merle et al.

[
14
]
,
[
15
]
indicate that classical solutions may develop singularities in a finite time.

In view of the above arguments, the concept of
weak solution
represents a suitable alternative to restore
global–in–time existence even for problems with large data and solutions remaining out of equilibrium in the long run. We consider a mathematical model of fully compressible
three dimensional fluid convection driven by an externally imposed magnetic field. The fluid occupies a bounded
domain
\Omega\subset R^{3}
with regular boundary. The time evolution of the density
\varrho=\varrho(t,x)
, the
(absolute) temperature
\vartheta=\vartheta(t,x)
, the velocity field
{\bf u}={\bf u}(t,x)
, and the magnetic field

{\bf B}={\bf B}(t,x)
is governed by the
compressible MHD system
of field equations (cf.
[
6
]
):

Equation of continuity

\partial_{t}\varrho+{\rm div}_{x}(\varrho{\bf u})=0.

(1.1)

Momentum equation

\partial_{t}(\varrho{\bf u})+{\rm div}_{x}(\varrho{\bf u}\otimes{\bf u})+\nabla_{x}p(\varrho,\vartheta)={\rm div}_{x}\mathbb{S}(\vartheta,\nabla_{x}{\bf u})+{\bf curl}_{x}{\bf B}\times{\bf B}+\varrho{\bf g}.

(1.2)

Induction equation

\partial_{t}{\bf B}+{\bf curl}_{x}({\bf B}\times{\bf u})+{\bf curl}_{x}(\zeta(\vartheta){\bf curl}_{x}{\bf B})=0,\ {\rm div}_{x}{\bf B}=0.

(1.3)

Internal energy balance

\displaystyle\partial_{t}(\varrho e(\varrho,\vartheta))+{\rm div}_{x}(\varrho e(\varrho,\vartheta){\bf u})

\displaystyle+{\rm div}_{x}{\bf q}(\vartheta,\nabla_{x}\vartheta)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{S}(\vartheta,\nabla_{x}{\bf u}):\nabla_{x}{\bf u}+\zeta(\vartheta)|{\bf curl}_{x}{\bf B}|^{2}-p(\varrho,\vartheta){\rm div}_{x}{\bf u}.

(1.4)

The pressure
p=p(\varrho,\vartheta)
and the internal energy
e=e(\varrho,\vartheta)
are interrelated through
Gibbs’ equation

\vartheta Ds=De+pD\left(\frac{1}{\varrho}\right),

(1.5)

where
s=s(\varrho,\vartheta)
is the entropy. Consequently, the internal energy balance (
1.4
) may be reformulated
in the form of
entropy balance equation

\displaystyle\partial_{t}(\varrho s(\varrho,\vartheta))+{\rm div}_{x}(\varrho s(\varrho,\vartheta){\bf u})

\displaystyle+{\rm div}_{x}\left(\frac{{\bf q}(\vartheta,\nabla_{x}\vartheta)}{\vartheta}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\vartheta}\left(\mathbb{S}(\vartheta,\nabla_{x}{\bf u}):\nabla_{x}{\bf u}-\frac{{\bf q}(\vartheta,\nabla_{x}\vartheta)\cdot\nabla_{x}\vartheta}{\vartheta}+\zeta(\vartheta)|{\bf curl}_{x}{\bf B}|^{2}\right),

(1.6)

see e.g. Gough
[
11
]
, Weiss and Proctor
[
20
]
, Tao et al.
[
16
]
.

The right–hand side of equation (
1.4
) represents the entropy production rate, which, in accordance with the Second law of thermodynamics, must be non–negative. Accordingly, we consider
Newtonian fluid
,
with the viscous stress tensor

\mathbb{S}(\vartheta,\nabla_{x}{\bf u})=\mu(\vartheta)\left(\nabla_{x}{\bf u}+\nabla_{x}^{t}{\bf u}-\frac{2}{3}{\rm div}_{x}{\bf u}\mathbb{I}\right)+\eta(\vartheta){\rm div}_{x}{\bf u}\mathbb{I},

(1.7)

where the viscosity coefficients
\mu>0
and
\eta\geq 0
are continuously differentiable functions of the temperature. Similarly, the heat flux obeys
Fourier’s law
,

{\bf q}(\vartheta,\nabla_{x}\vartheta)=-\kappa(\vartheta)\nabla_{x}\vartheta,

(1.8)

where the heat conductivity coefficient
\kappa>0
is a continuously differentiable function of the temperature.

The existence of global–in–time weak solutions for the compressible MHD system (
1.1
)–(
1.8
)
was shown in
[
6
]
under certain physically relevant restrictions imposed on constitutive equations and
transport coefficients. The boundary conditions considered in
[
6
]
are conservative,

{\bf u}|_{\partial\Omega}=0,\ \nabla_{x}\vartheta\cdot{\bf n}|_{\partial\Omega}=0,\ {\bf B}\cdot{\bf n}|_{\partial\Omega}=0,\ \left[{\bf B}\times{\bf u}+\zeta{\bf curl}_{x}{\bf B}\right]\times{\bf n}|_{\partial\Omega}=0

(1.9)

characteristic for closed systems. If the driving force
{\bf g}
in the momentum equation is conservative,
meaning
{\bf g}=\nabla_{x}G
,
G=G(x)
, the total energy of the system is conserved and the dynamics obeys the rather “boring” scenario formulated by the celebrated statement of Clausius:

The energy of the world is constant; its entropy tends to a maximum.

A rigorous mathematical proof of this statement for the Navier–Stokes–Fourier system was given in

[
10
]
.

As pointed out by Gough
[
11
]
, a rich fluid behaviour is conditioned by a proper choice of boundary
conditions. Motivated by models in astrophysics, we suppose the boundary
\partial\Omega
is impermeable
and the tangential component of the normal viscous stress vanishes on it,

{\bf u}\cdot{\bf n}|_{\partial\Omega}=0,\ (\mathbb{S}\cdot{\bf n})\times{\bf n}|_{\partial\Omega}=0.

(1.10)

However, the theory presented in this paper can accommodate more general boundary conditions for the velocity
field discussed in Section
6
.

Similarly to the well known Rayleigh–Bénard problem, see e.g. Davidson
[
5
]
, we impose the inhomogeneous
Dirichlet boundary conditions for the temperature,

\vartheta|_{\partial\Omega}=\vartheta_{B},\ \vartheta_{B}>0.

(1.11)

To incorporate the effect of an exterior magnetic field, we suppose that
{\bf B}
is (not necessarily small)
perturbation of a background magnetic field
{\bf B}_{B}
,
{\rm div}_{x}{\bf B}_{B}=0
,

{\bf B}={\bf B}_{B}+{\bf b}.

In the context of astrophysics, we may think of
{\bf B}_{B}
as being the magnetic field imposed by a massive star. As for
{\bf b}
, we
suppose either

{\bf b}\times{\bf n}|_{\partial\Omega}=0\ \Rightarrow\ {\bf B}\times{\bf n}|_{\partial\Omega}={\bf B}_{B}\times{\bf n}|_{\partial\Omega},

(1.12)

or

{\bf b}\cdot{\bf n}|_{\partial\Omega}=0\ \Rightarrow\ {\bf B}\cdot{\bf n}|_{\partial\Omega}={\bf B}_{B}\cdot{\bf n}|_{\partial\Omega}.

(1.13)

The boundary condition (
1.12
) is of Dirichlet type, compatible with the requirement of solenoidality of
{\bf B}
.
The condition (
1.13
) is of flux type, similar to the first condition (
1.10
) and must be accompanied
by another flux condition related to the electric field, namely

\Big[{\bf B}\times{\bf u}+\zeta{\bf curl}_{x}{\bf B}\Big]\times{\bf n}|_{\partial\Omega}=0.

(1.14)

The flux in (
1.14
) may be inhomogeneous as well. The borderline case
{\bf B}_{B}=0
in (
1.12
) and
(
1.13
) corresponds to a perfectly isolating and perfectly conducting boundary, respectively (see e.g.
Alekseev
[
1
]
).

For possibly technical but so far unsurmountable reasons, a mathematically tractable
weak formulation
of compresible MHD system cannot be based on
merely rewriting the system (
1.1
)–(
1.4
) in the sense of distributions. The available
a priori
bounds are not strong enough to render certain terms, notably
p(\varrho,\vartheta){\rm div}_{x}{\bf u}
in
the internal energy balance (
1.4
), integrable. A remedy proposed in
[
6
]
is replacing
(
1.4
) by the entropy equation (
1.6
). Unfortunately, the entropy production rate is
a priori

bounded only in the non–reflexive space
L^{1}
of integrable functions. As a result, any approximation scheme
provides merely an
inequality

\displaystyle\partial_{t}(\varrho s(\varrho,\vartheta))+{\rm div}_{x}(\varrho s(\varrho,\vartheta){\bf u})

\displaystyle+{\rm div}_{x}\left(\frac{{\bf q}(\vartheta,\nabla_{x}\vartheta)}{\vartheta}\right)

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{\vartheta}\left(\mathbb{S}(\vartheta,\nabla_{x}{\bf u}):\nabla_{x}{\bf u}-\frac{{\bf q}(\vartheta,\nabla_{x}\vartheta)\cdot\nabla_{x}\vartheta}{\vartheta}+\zeta(\vartheta)|{\bf curl}_{x}{\bf B}|^{2}\right)

(1.15)

satisfied in the sense of distributions.

Replacing entropy equation by inequality definitely enlarges the class of possible
solutions and gives rise to an underdetermined problem. To save well posedness, at least formally, the integrated
total

energy balance/inequality is appended to the system (
1.1
)–(
1.3
), (
1.15
) in
[
6
]
.
The total energy balance reads

\displaystyle\partial_{t}

\displaystyle\left(\frac{1}{2}\varrho|{\bf u}|^{2}+\varrho e(\varrho,\vartheta)+\frac{1}{2}|{\bf B}|^{2}\right)+{\rm div}_{x}\left[\left(\frac{1}{2}\varrho|{\bf u}|^{2}+\varrho e(\varrho,\vartheta)+\frac{1}{2}|{\bf B}|^{2}+p(\varrho,\vartheta)\right){\bf u}\right]

\displaystyle+{\rm div}_{x}\Big[({\bf B}\times{\bf u})\times{\bf B}+\zeta(\vartheta){\bf curl}_{x}{\bf B}\times{\bf B}\Big]-{\rm div}_{x}(\mathbb{S}(\vartheta,\nabla_{x}{\bf u})\cdot{\bf u})+{\rm div}_{x}{\bf q}(\vartheta,\nabla_{x}\vartheta)=\varrho{\bf g}\cdot{\bf u}.

(1.16)

If the system is
conservative
, the total energy flux vanishes on the boundary and (
1.16
) integrated over
\Omega
yields

\frac{{\rm d}}{\,{\rm d}t}\int_{\Omega}\left(\frac{1}{2}\varrho|{\bf u}|^{2}+\varrho e(\varrho,\vartheta)+\frac{1}{2}|{\bf B}|^{2}\right)\ \,{\rm d}{x}=\int_{\Omega}\varrho{\bf g}\cdot{\bf u}\ \,{\rm d}{x}.

(1.17)

It turns out that the equations (
1.1
)–(
1.3
), the inequality (
1.15
), together with the integral identity (
1.17
)
represent a suitable weak formulation for the
conservative
system. Indeed the existence of global–in–time weak solutions for any
finite energy (initial) data was proved in
[
6
]
. The weak solutions comply with a natural
compatibility
principle. Any sufficiently smooth weak solution is a classical solution of the problem. In addition, the weak solutions satisfy the weak–strong uniqueness principle: A weak solution coincides with
the strong solution on the life span of the latter, see
[
7
]
.

The situation becomes more delicate for open (non–conservative) systems, with inhomogeneous Dirichlet boundary conditions. The heat flux
{\bf q}\cdot{\bf n}
as well as the induction flux (the normal component of the electric field)
[{\bf B}\times{\bf u}\times{\bf B}+\zeta{\bf curl}_{x}{\bf B}\times{\bf B}]\cdot{\bf n}

do not necessarily
vanish on
\partial\Omega
and give rise to additional uncontrollable sources of energy in (
1.17
). In
[
4
]

(see also the monograph
[
9
]
), a new approach has been developed to handle the inhomogeneous Dirichlet
boundary conditions for the temperature. The total energy balance (
1.17
) is replaced by a similar inequality for
the ballistic energy

\left[\frac{1}{2}\varrho|{\bf u}|^{2}+\varrho e(\varrho,\vartheta)+\frac{1}{2}|{\bf B}|^{2}-\tilde{\vartheta}\varrho s(\varrho,\vartheta)\right],

where
\tilde{\vartheta}
is an arbitrary extension of the boundary temperature
\vartheta_{B}
inside
\Omega
. Pursuing the same
strategy, we introduce a “magnetic” variant of the ballistic energy

E_{BM}=\left[\frac{1}{2}\varrho|{\bf u}|^{2}+\varrho e+\frac{1}{2}|{\bf B}|^{2}-\tilde{\vartheta}\varrho s-{\bf B}_{B}\cdot{\bf B}\right],

(1.18)

After a straightforward manipulation, we deduce equality

\displaystyle\frac{{\rm d}}{\,{\rm d}t}

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\left(\frac{1}{2}\varrho|{\bf u}|^{2}+\varrho e+\frac{1}{2}|{\bf B}|^{2}-\tilde{\vartheta}\varrho s-{\bf B}_{B}\cdot{\bf B}\right)\ \,{\rm d}{x}

\displaystyle+\int_{\Omega}\frac{\tilde{\vartheta}}{\vartheta}\left(\mathbb{S}:\nabla_{x}{\bf u}-\frac{{\bf q}\cdot\nabla_{x}\vartheta}{\vartheta}+\zeta|{\bf curl}_{x}{\bf B}|^{2}\right)\ \,{\rm d}{x}

\displaystyle=-\int_{\Omega}\left(\varrho s\partial_{t}\tilde{\vartheta}+\varrho s{\bf u}\cdot\nabla_{x}\tilde{\vartheta}+\frac{{\bf q}}{\vartheta}\cdot\nabla_{x}\tilde{\vartheta}\right)\ \,{\rm d}{x}

\displaystyle\quad-\int_{\Omega}\Big({\bf B}\cdot\partial_{t}{\bf B}_{B}-({\bf B}\times{\bf u})\cdot{\bf curl}_{x}{\bf B}_{B}-\zeta{\bf curl}_{x}\ {\bf B}\cdot{\bf curl}_{x}{\bf B}_{B}\Big)\ \,{\rm d}{x}

\displaystyle\quad+\int_{\Omega}\varrho{\bf g}\cdot{\bf u}\ \,{\rm d}{x}

(1.19)

as soon as
\tilde{\vartheta}|_{\partial\Omega}=\vartheta_{B}
,
\tilde{\vartheta}>0
,
{\bf B}\times{\bf n}|_{\partial\Omega}={\bf B}_{B}\times{\bf n}|_{\partial\Omega}
. The same is true if
{\bf B}\cdot{\bf n}|_{\partial\Omega}={\bf B}_{B}\cdot{\bf n}|_{\partial\Omega}
and the vanishing tangential flux condition (
1.14
) holds.
Note carefully that (
1.19
) does not contain the boundary flux integrals; whence it is suitable for a weak formulation of the problem.

The mathematical theory we propose is based on imposing the weak form of the equations (
1.1
)–(
1.3
), together with
the entropy inequality (
1.15
), and the ballistic energy balance (
1.19
) as a weak formulation of
the compressible MHD system. The basic hypotheses concerning the state equation and the transport coefficients
as well as the exact definition of weak solution are given in Section
2
. In Section
3
we introduce the
basic tool to investigate stability properties in the class of weak solutions – the
relative energy inequality
. In Section
4
, we show that the weak solutions enjoy the weak–strong uniqueness property.
In Section
5
, the existence of global–in–time weak solutions is established by means of a multilevel approximation
scheme. The paper is concluded in Section
6
by a brief discussion on possible extensions of the theory to a larger class of boundary conditions.

2
Weak formulation

We start by a list of structural hypotheses imposed on the equation of state and the transport coefficients.

2.1
Equation of state

Our choice of the equation of state is motivated by
[
9
, Chapter 4]
. In particular, we consider the
radiation contribution to the pressure/internal energy relevant to problems in astrophysics, cf.
Battaner
[
3
]
. In accordance with Gibbs’ relation (
1.5
) we suppose that

\displaystyle p(\varrho,\vartheta)

\displaystyle=p_{M}(\varrho,\vartheta)+p_{R}(\vartheta),\ p_{M}(\varrho,\vartheta)=\vartheta^{\frac{5}{2}}P\left(\frac{\varrho}{\vartheta^{\frac{3}{2}}}\right),\ p_{R}(\vartheta)=\frac{a}{3}\vartheta^{4},

\displaystyle e(\varrho,\vartheta)

\displaystyle=e_{M}(\varrho,\vartheta)+e_{R}(\varrho,\vartheta),\ e_{M}(\varrho,\vartheta)=\frac{3}{2}\frac{\vartheta^{\frac{5}{2}}}{\varrho}P\left(\frac{\varrho}{\vartheta^{\frac{3}{2}}}\right),\ e_{R}(\varrho,\vartheta)=\frac{a}{\varrho}\vartheta^{4},\ a>0,

(2.1)

where the function
P\in C^{1}[0,\infty)
satisfies

P(0)=0,\ P^{\prime}(Z)>0\ \mbox{for}\ Z\geq 0,\ 0<\frac{\frac{5}{3}P(Z)-P^{\prime}(Z)Z}{Z}\leq c\ \mbox{for}\ Z>0.

(2.2)

This implies, in particular, that
Z\mapsto P(Z)/Z^{\frac{5}{3}}
is decreasing, and we suppose

\lim_{Z\to\infty}\frac{P(Z)}{Z^{\frac{5}{3}}}=p_{\infty}>0.

(2.3)

Note that
p_{M}
,
e_{M}
satisfy the relation characteristic for monoatomic gas,

p_{M}=\frac{2}{3}\varrho e_{M},

p_{R}
is the radiation pressure with the associated internal energy
e_{R}
, and (
2.3
) corresponds to the
presence of electron pressure under the degenerate gas regime.
It follows from (
2.2
) that
p
and
e
satisfy the
hypothesis of thermodynamic stability
:

\frac{\partial p(\varrho,\vartheta)}{\partial\varrho}>0,\ \frac{\partial e(\varrho,\vartheta)}{\partial\vartheta}>0.

(2.4)

In accordance with (
1.5
), the entropy takes the form

s(\varrho,\vartheta)=s_{M}(\varrho,\vartheta)+s_{R}(\varrho,\vartheta),\ s_{M}(\varrho,\vartheta)=\mathcal{S}\left(\frac{\varrho}{\vartheta^{\frac{3}{2}}}\right),\ s_{R}(\varrho,\vartheta)=\frac{4a}{3}\frac{\vartheta^{3}}{\varrho},

(2.5)

where

\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(Z)=-\frac{3}{2}\frac{\frac{5}{3}P(Z)-P^{\prime}(Z)Z}{Z^{2}}.

(2.6)

2.2
Transport coefficients

We suppose the viscosity coefficients in the Newtonian stress
\mathbb{S}(\vartheta,\nabla_{x}{\bf u})
are continuously
differentiable functions of the temperature satisfying

\displaystyle 0<\underline{\mu}\left(1+\vartheta^{\alpha}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\mu(\vartheta)\leq\overline{\mu}\left(1+\vartheta^{\alpha}\right),\ |\mu^{\prime}(\vartheta)|\leq c\ \mbox{for all}\ \vartheta\geq 0,

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle\leq\eta(\vartheta)\leq\overline{\eta}\left(1+\vartheta^{\alpha}\right)\ \mbox{for some}\ \frac{1}{2}\leq\alpha\leq 1.

(2.7)

Note that
\alpha=\frac{1}{2}
corresponds to Sutherland’s law relevant in astrophysics,
see Yang et al.
[
21
]
.

Similarly, the heat conductivity coefficient in the Fourier heat flux
{\bf q}(\vartheta,\nabla_{x}\vartheta)
is a
continuously differentiable function of the temperature satisfying

0<\underline{\kappa}\left(1+\vartheta^{\beta}\right)\leq\kappa(\vartheta)\leq\overline{\kappa}\left(1+\vartheta^{\beta}\right)\ \mbox{for some}\ \beta\geq 3.

(2.8)

Here, the case
\beta=3
reflects the effect of radiation.

Finally, we suppose the magnetic diffusivity coefficient
\zeta=\zeta(\vartheta)
is a continuously differentiable function of the temperature,

0<\underline{\zeta}(1+\vartheta)\leq\zeta(\vartheta)\leq\overline{\zeta}(1+\vartheta),\ |\zeta^{\prime}(\vartheta)|\leq c\ \mbox{for all}\ \vartheta\geq 0.

(2.9)

2.3
Boundary data

We suppose the background magnetic field
{\bf B}_{B}={\bf B}_{B}(t,x)
is sufficiently
smooth at least of class
C^{1,2}([0,T]\times\overline{\Omega};R^{3})
,

{\rm div}_{x}{\bf B}_{B}=0\ \mbox{in}\ (0,T)\times\Omega.

(2.10)

Similarly, we suppose the boundary temperature
\vartheta_{B}
can be extended inside
\Omega
,

\vartheta_{B}\in C^{1,2}([0,T]\times\overline{\Omega})
,

\vartheta_{B}>0\ \mbox{in}\ [0,T]\times\overline{\Omega}.

(2.11)

2.4
Weak solutions

Before discussing the concept of weak solution, it is useful to introduce the function spaces

\displaystyle H_{0,\tau}(\Omega;R^{3})

\displaystyle=\left\{{\bf b}\in L^{2}(\Omega;R^{3})\ \Big|\ {\bf curl}_{x}{\bf b}\in L^{2}(\Omega;R^{d}),\ {\rm div}_{x}{\bf b}=0,\ {\bf b}\times{\bf n}|_{\partial\Omega}=0\right\},

\displaystyle H_{0,n}(\Omega;R^{3})

\displaystyle=\left\{{\bf b}\in L^{2}(\Omega;R^{3})\ \Big|\ {\bf curl}_{x}{\bf b}\in L^{2}(\Omega;R^{d}),\ {\rm div}_{x}{\bf b}=0,\ {\bf b}\cdot{\bf n}|_{\partial\Omega}=0\right\}.

Both
H_{0,\tau}
and
H_{0,n}
are endowed with the Hilbert norm

\|{\bf b}\|^{2}_{H_{0}}=\|{\bf curl}_{x}{\bf b}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega;R^{3})}+\|{\bf b}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega;R^{3})},

see e.g. Alexander and Auchmuty

[
2
]
.

Definition 2.1
.

(Weak solution to the compressible MHD system)

A quantity
(\varrho,\vartheta,{\bf u},{\bf B})
is termed
weak solution
of the compressible MHD system
(
1.1
)–(
1.4
), with the boundary conditions (
1.10
), (
1.11
), (
1.12
) (respectively
(
1.13
)), and the initial data

\varrho(0,\cdot)=\varrho_{0},\ \vartheta(0,\cdot)=\vartheta_{0},\ {\bf u}(0,\cdot)={\bf u}_{0},\ {\bf B}(0,\cdot)={\bf B}_{0},

(2.12)

if the following holds:

•

Equation of continuity.

\varrho\in C_{\rm weak}([0,T];L^{\frac{5}{3}}(\Omega))
,
\varrho\geq 0
in
(0,T)\times\Omega
, and the integral
identity

\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega}\Big(\varrho\partial_{t}\varphi+\varrho{\bf u}\cdot\nabla_{x}\varphi\Big)\ \,{\rm d}{x}\ dt=-\left[\int_{\Omega}\varrho\varphi\ \,{\rm d}{x}\right]_{t=0}^{t=\tau},\ \varrho(0,\cdot)=\varrho_{0},

(2.13)

holds for any
\varphi\in C^{1}([0,T]\times\overline{\Omega})
. In addition, the renormalized version of (
2.13
)

\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega}\left(b(\varrho)\partial_{t}\varphi+b(\varrho){\bf u}\cdot\nabla_{x}\varphi+\Big(b(\varrho)-b^{\prime}(\varrho)\varrho\Big){\rm div}_{x}{\bf u}\right)\ \,{\rm d}{x}\,{\rm d}t=\left[\int_{\Omega}b(\varrho)\varphi\ \,{\rm d}{x}\right]_{t=0}^{t=\tau}

(2.14)

holds for any
\varphi\in C^{1}([0,T]\times\overline{\Omega})
and any
b\in C^{1}(R)
,
b^{\prime}\in C_{c}(R)
.

•

Momentum equation.

\varrho{\bf u}\in C_{\rm weak}([0,T];L^{\frac{5}{4}}(\Omega;R^{3})
,

{\bf u}\in L^{2}(0,T;W^{1,q}(\Omega;R^{3}))
,
q=\frac{8}{5-\alpha}
,
{\bf u}\cdot{\bf n}|_{\partial\Omega}=0
,
and the integral identity

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\tau}

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\Big(\varrho{\bf u}\cdot\partial_{t}\boldsymbol{\varphi}+\varrho{\bf u}\otimes{\bf u}:\nabla_{x}\boldsymbol{\varphi}+p(\varrho,\vartheta){\rm div}_{x}\boldsymbol{\varphi}\Big)\ \,{\rm d}{x}

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega}\mathbb{S}(\vartheta,\nabla_{x}{\bf u}):\nabla_{x}\boldsymbol{\varphi}\ \,{\rm d}{x}\,{\rm d}t-\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega}\left({\bf B}\otimes{\bf B}-\frac{1}{2}|{\bf B}|^{2}\mathbb{I}\right):\nabla_{x}\boldsymbol{\varphi}\ \,{\rm d}{x}\,{\rm d}t

\displaystyle-\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega}\varrho{\bf g}\cdot\boldsymbol{\varphi}\ \,{\rm d}{x}\,{\rm d}t+\left[\int_{\Omega}\varrho{\bf u}\cdot\boldsymbol{\varphi}\ \,{\rm d}{x}\right]_{t=0}^{t=\tau},\ (\varrho{\bf u})(0,\cdot)=\varrho_{0}{\bf u}_{0}

(2.15)

holds for any
\boldsymbol{\varphi}\in C^{1}([0,T]\times\overline{\Omega};R^{3})
,
\boldsymbol{\varphi}\cdot{\bf n}|_{\partial\Omega}=0
.

•

Induction equation.

{\bf B}\in C_{\rm weak}([0,T];L^{2}(\Omega;R^{3}))
,

{\rm div}_{x}{\bf B}(\tau,\cdot)=0\ \mbox{for any}\ \tau\in[0,T].

(2.16)

In addition

({\bf B}-{\bf B}_{B})\in L^{2}(0,T;H_{0,\tau}(\Omega;R^{3}))

(2.17)

if the boundary condition (
1.12
) is imposed or

({\bf B}-{\bf B}_{B})\in L^{2}(0,T;H_{0,n}(\Omega;R^{3}))

(2.18)

for the boundary conditions (
1.13
), (
1.14
).
The integral identity

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\tau}

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\Big({\bf B}\cdot\partial_{t}\boldsymbol{\varphi}-({\bf B}\times{\bf u})\cdot{\bf curl}_{x}\boldsymbol{\varphi}-\zeta(\vartheta){\bf curl}_{x}\ {\bf B}\cdot{\bf curl}_{x}\ \boldsymbol{\varphi}\Big)\ \,{\rm d}{x}\,{\rm d}t

\displaystyle=\left[\int_{\Omega}{\bf B}\cdot\boldsymbol{\varphi}\ \,{\rm d}{x}\right]_{t=0}^{t=\tau},\ {\bf B}(0,\cdot)={\bf B}_{0},

(2.19)

holds for any
\boldsymbol{\varphi}\in C^{1}([0,T]\times\overline{\Omega};R^{3})
,
{\rm div}_{x}\boldsymbol{\varphi}=0
,

\boldsymbol{\varphi}\times{\bf n}|_{\partial\Omega}=0

(2.20)

in the case of boundary conditions (
1.12
),

\boldsymbol{\varphi}\cdot{\bf n}|_{\partial\Omega}=0

(2.21)

for the boundary conditions (
1.13
), (
1.14
).

•

Entropy inequality.

\vartheta\in L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{4}(\Omega))
,
\vartheta>0
a.a. in
(0,T)\times\Omega
,

\vartheta-\vartheta_{B}\in L^{2}(0,T;W^{1,2}_{0}(\Omega))
,
\log(\vartheta)\in L^{2}(0,T;W^{1,2}(\Omega))

3
Relative energy

A suitable form of the
relative energy
for the compressible MHD system reads

\displaystyle E

\displaystyle\left(\varrho,\vartheta,{\bf u},{\bf B}\ \Big|r,\Theta,{\bf U},{\bf H}\right)=\frac{1}{2}\varrho|{\bf u}-{\bf U}|^{2}+\frac{1}{2}|{\bf B}-{\bf H}|^{2}

\displaystyle+\varrho e(\varrho,\vartheta)-\Theta\Big(\varrho s(\varrho,\vartheta)-rs(r,\Theta)\Big)-\Big(e(r,\Theta)-\Theta s(r,\Theta)+\frac{p(r,\Theta)}{r}\Big)(\varrho-r)-re(r,\Theta).

(3.1)

In applications, the quantity
(\varrho,\vartheta,{\bf u},{\bf B})
stands for a weak solution of the compressible MHD system while

(r,\Theta,{\bf U},{\bf H})
are arbitrary sufficiently smooth functions satisfying the compatibility
conditions

\displaystyle r

\displaystyle>0,\ \Theta>0\ \mbox{in}\ [0,T]\times\overline{\Omega},\ \Theta|_{\partial\Omega}=\vartheta_{B},\ {\bf U}\cdot{\bf n}|_{\partial\Omega}=0,

\displaystyle{\rm div}_{x}{\bf H}

\displaystyle=0\ \mbox{in}\ (0,T)\times\Omega,\ {\bf H}\times{\bf n}|_{\partial\Omega}={\bf B}_{B}\times{\bf n}|_{\partial\Omega},\ \mbox{or}\ {\bf H}\cdot{\bf n}|_{\partial\Omega}={\bf B}_{B}\cdot{\bf n}|_{\partial\Omega},\ \mbox{respectively}.

(3.2)

As a consequence of hypothesis of thermodynamic stability stated in (
2.4
), the energy

E(\varrho,\vartheta,{\bf u},{\bf B})=\frac{1}{2}\varrho|{\bf u}|^{2}+\varrho e(\varrho,\vartheta)+\frac{1}{2}|{\bf B}|^{2}

is a strictly convex l.s.c. function if expressed in the variables
(\varrho,S=\varrho s(\varrho,\vartheta),{\bf m}=\varrho{\bf u},{\bf B})
,
see
[
9
, Chapter 3, Section 3.1]
for details. Moreover, we have

\frac{\partial E(\varrho,S,{\bf m},{\bf B})}{\partial\varrho}=\left(e(\varrho,\vartheta)-\vartheta s(\varrho,\vartheta)+\frac{p(\varrho,\vartheta)}{\varrho}\right),\ \frac{\partial E(\varrho,S,{\bf m},{\bf B})}{\partial S}=\vartheta,

where
\left(e(\varrho,\vartheta)-\vartheta s(\varrho,\vartheta)+\frac{p(\varrho,\vartheta)}{\varrho}\right)
is Gibbs’ free energy. Consequently, the relative energy can be seen as Bregman divergence associate to the convex function
E
.
In particular,

E\left(\varrho,\vartheta,{\bf u},{\bf B}\ \Big|r,\Theta,{\bf U},{\bf H}\right)\geq 0,\ \mbox{and}\ E\left(\varrho,\vartheta,{\bf u},{\bf B}\ \Big|r,\Theta,{\bf U},{\bf H}\right)=0\ \Leftrightarrow\ (\varrho,\vartheta,{\bf u},{\bf B})=(r,\Theta,{\bf U},{\bf H})

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega}\left(\left(1-\frac{\Theta}{\vartheta}\right)\frac{{\bf q}(\vartheta,\nabla_{x}\vartheta)\cdot\nabla_{x}\vartheta}{\vartheta}+\left(1-\frac{\vartheta}{\Theta}\right)\frac{{\bf q}(\Theta,\nabla_{x}\Theta)\cdot\nabla_{x}\Theta}{\Theta}\right)\ \,{\rm d}{x}\,{\rm d}t

\displaystyle+\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega}\left(\frac{{\bf q}(\vartheta,\nabla_{x}\vartheta)}{\vartheta}-\frac{{\bf q}(\Theta,\nabla_{x}\Theta)}{\Theta}\right)\cdot(\nabla_{x}\Theta-\nabla_{x}\vartheta)\ \,{\rm d}{x}\,{\rm d}t

(4.17)

Thus (
4.13
) can be rewritten in the final form

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}E\left(\varrho,\vartheta,{\bf u},{\bf B}\ \Big|r,\Theta,{\bf U},{\bf H}\right)(\tau,\cdot)\ \,{\rm d}{x}

\displaystyle+\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega}\left(\frac{\Theta}{\vartheta}-1\right)\mathbb{S}(\vartheta,\nabla_{x}{\bf u})\ \,{\rm d}{x}\,{\rm d}t+\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega}\left(\frac{\vartheta}{\Theta}-1\right)\mathbb{S}(\Theta,\nabla_{x}{\bf U})\ \,{\rm d}{x}\,{\rm d}t

\displaystyle+\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega}\left(\frac{\Theta}{\vartheta}-1\right)\zeta(\vartheta)|{\bf curl}_{x}{\bf B}|^{2}\ \,{\rm d}{x}\,{\rm d}t+\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega}\left(\frac{\vartheta}{\Theta}-1\right)\zeta(\Theta)|{\bf curl}_{x}{\bf H}|^{2}\ \,{\rm d}{x}\,{\rm d}t

\displaystyle+\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega}\left(\left(1-\frac{\Theta}{\vartheta}\right)\frac{{\bf q}(\varrho,\nabla_{x}\vartheta)\cdot\nabla_{x}\vartheta}{\vartheta}+\left(1-\frac{\vartheta}{\Theta}\right)\frac{{\bf q}(\Theta,\nabla_{x}\Theta)\cdot\nabla_{x}\Theta}{\Theta}\right)\ \,{\rm d}{x}\,{\rm d}t

\displaystyle+\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega}\Big(\mathbb{S}(\vartheta,\nabla_{x}{\bf u})-\mathbb{S}(\Theta,\nabla_{x}{\bf U})\Big):\nabla_{x}\Big({\bf u}-{\bf U}\Big)\ \,{\rm d}{x}\,{\rm d}t

\displaystyle+\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega}\Big(\zeta(\vartheta){\bf curl}_{x}{\bf B}-\zeta(\Theta){\bf curl}_{x}{\bf H}\Big)\cdot{\bf curl}_{x}({\bf B}-{\bf H})\ \,{\rm d}{x}\,{\rm d}t

\displaystyle+\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega}\left(\frac{{\bf q}(\vartheta,\nabla_{x}\vartheta)}{\vartheta}-\frac{{\bf q}(\Theta,\nabla_{x}\Theta)}{\Theta}\right)\cdot(\nabla_{x}\Theta-\nabla_{x}\vartheta)\ \,{\rm d}{x}\,{\rm d}t

\displaystyle\leq\int_{0}^{\tau}\mathcal{R}_{2}\,{\rm d}t,

(4.18)

with
\mathcal{R}_{2}
given in (
4.14
).

\displaystyle E\left(\varrho,\vartheta,{\bf u},{\bf B}\Big|r,\Theta,{\bf U},{\bf H}\right)

\displaystyle\geq\left(1_{\rm res}+[\varrho|{\bf u}|^{2}]_{\rm res}+[\varrho e(\varrho,\vartheta)]_{\rm res}+[\varrho|s(\varrho,\vartheta)|]_{\rm res}+[|{\bf B}|^{2}]_{\rm res}\right),

(4.19)

where the constant depend on

,

4.4.1
Temperature gradient

The following crucial estimate was proved in

, Chapter 4, Section 4.2.2]
for

\beta=3

.

Lemma 4.4
.

Under the hypotheses of Theorem
4.1
, there is a constant
c>0
,

\displaystyle\|\vartheta-\Theta\|_{W^{1,2}(\Omega;R^{3})}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq c\int_{\Omega}\left(\left(\frac{\Theta}{\vartheta}-1\right)\frac{\kappa(\vartheta)|\nabla_{x}\vartheta|^{2}}{\vartheta}+\left(\frac{\vartheta}{\Theta}-1\right)\frac{\kappa(\Theta)|\nabla_{x}\Theta|^{2}}{\Theta}\right)\ \,{\rm d}{x}

\displaystyle+\int_{\Omega}\left(\frac{\kappa(\Theta)\nabla_{x}\Theta}{\Theta}-\frac{\kappa(\vartheta)\nabla_{x}\vartheta}{\vartheta}\right)\cdot(\nabla_{x}\Theta-\nabla_{x}\vartheta)\ \,{\rm d}{x}+\xi\int_{\Omega}E\left(\varrho,\vartheta,{\bf u},{\bf B}\Big|r,\Theta,{\bf U},{\bf H}\right)\ \,{\rm d}{x}

(4.20)

where
\xi>0
depends on
\Theta
,
\inf\Theta
, and
\|\vartheta\|_{L^{4}(\Omega)}
.

Remark 4.5
.

As we shall see below, the result holds whenever
\kappa(\vartheta)\approx 1+\vartheta^{\beta}
,
\beta\geq 2
.

Proof.

We consider first the essential part. Rewrite

\displaystyle\left(\frac{\Theta}{\vartheta}-1\right)\frac{\kappa(\vartheta)|\nabla_{x}\vartheta|^{2}}{\vartheta}+\left(\frac{\vartheta}{\Theta}-1\right)\frac{\kappa(\Theta)|\nabla_{x}\Theta|^{2}}{\Theta}+\left(\frac{\kappa(\Theta)\nabla_{x}\Theta}{\Theta}-\frac{\kappa(\vartheta)\nabla_{x}\vartheta}{\vartheta}\right)\cdot(\nabla_{x}\Theta-\nabla_{x}\vartheta)

\displaystyle\geq\Theta\frac{\kappa(\vartheta)}{\vartheta^{2}}|\nabla_{x}\vartheta-\nabla_{x}\Theta|^{2}+2\frac{\kappa(\vartheta)}{\vartheta^{2}}(\Theta-\vartheta)(\nabla_{x}\vartheta-\nabla_{x}\Theta)\cdot\nabla_{x}\Theta

\displaystyle+\left(\frac{\kappa(\vartheta)}{\vartheta}-\frac{\kappa(\Theta)}{\Theta}\right)(\nabla_{x}\vartheta-\nabla_{x}\Theta)\cdot\nabla_{x}\Theta+\left(\frac{\kappa(\vartheta)}{\vartheta}-\frac{\kappa(\Theta)}{\Theta}\right)\left(\frac{\Theta-\vartheta}{\Theta}\right)|\nabla_{x}\Theta|^{2}.

(4.21)

Applying Hölder’s inequality we deduce from (
4.21
)

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}|[\nabla_{x}\vartheta-\nabla_{x}\Theta]_{\rm ess}|^{2}\ \,{\rm d}{x}

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle<}}{{\sim}}\left[\left(\frac{\Theta}{\vartheta}-1\right)\frac{\kappa(\vartheta)|\nabla_{x}\vartheta|^{2}}{\vartheta}+\left(\frac{\vartheta}{\Theta}-1\right)\frac{\kappa(\Theta)|\nabla_{x}\Theta|^{2}}{\Theta}+\left(\frac{\kappa(\Theta)\nabla_{x}\Theta}{\Theta}-\frac{\kappa(\vartheta)\nabla_{x}\vartheta}{\vartheta}\right)\cdot(\nabla_{x}\Theta-\nabla_{x}\vartheta)\right]_{\rm ess}

\displaystyle+\xi\int_{\Omega}E\left(\varrho,\vartheta,{\bf u},{\bf B}\Big|r,\Theta,{\bf U},{\bf H}\right)_{\rm ess}\ \,{\rm d}{x},

(4.22)

where
\xi
depends on
\Theta
. As a matter of fact, the only hypotheses to be imposed on
\kappa
is that

\kappa(\vartheta)>0
whenever
\vartheta>0
.

As for the residual part, we first rewrite

\displaystyle\left(\frac{\Theta}{\vartheta}-1\right)\frac{\kappa(\vartheta)|\nabla_{x}\vartheta|^{2}}{\vartheta}+\left(\frac{\vartheta}{\Theta}-1\right)\frac{\kappa(\Theta)|\nabla_{x}\Theta|^{2}}{\Theta}+\left(\frac{\kappa(\Theta)\nabla_{x}\Theta}{\Theta}-\frac{\kappa(\vartheta)\nabla_{x}\vartheta}{\vartheta}\right)\cdot(\nabla_{x}\Theta-\nabla_{x}\vartheta)

\displaystyle=\frac{\Theta}{\vartheta}\frac{\kappa(\vartheta)|\nabla_{x}\vartheta|^{2}}{\vartheta}+\frac{\vartheta}{\Theta}\frac{\kappa(\Theta)|\nabla_{x}\Theta|^{2}}{\Theta}-\frac{\kappa(\vartheta)\nabla_{x}\vartheta}{\vartheta}\cdot\nabla_{x}\Theta-\frac{\kappa(\Theta)\nabla_{x}\Theta}{\Theta}\cdot\nabla_{x}\vartheta

(4.23)

Obviously,

\left[\frac{\vartheta}{\Theta}\frac{\kappa(\Theta)|\nabla_{x}\Theta|^{2}}{\Theta}\right]_{\rm res}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle<}}{{\sim}}\int_{\Omega}E\left(\varrho,\vartheta,{\bf u},{\bf B}\Big|r,\Theta,{\bf U},{\bf H}\right)_{\rm res}\ \,{\rm d}{x}.

Consequently, as
\inf\Theta>0
, it remains to handle a quantity

\left[\frac{\kappa(\vartheta)|\nabla_{x}\vartheta|^{2}}{\vartheta^{2}}-C\kappa(\vartheta)|\nabla_{x}\Theta|\right]_{\rm res}

where
C
is a positive constant depending on
\Theta,\inf\Theta
. Note that this step requires

\vartheta^{2}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle<}}{{\sim}}\kappa(\vartheta)
.

To proceed we consider
\overline{\vartheta}>0
to be fixed below. We write

\left[\frac{\kappa(\vartheta)|\nabla_{x}\vartheta|^{2}}{\vartheta^{2}}-C\kappa(\vartheta)|\nabla_{x}\Theta|\right]_{\rm res}\mathds{1}_{\vartheta<\overline{\vartheta}}+\left[\frac{\kappa(\vartheta)|\nabla_{x}\vartheta|^{2}}{\vartheta^{2}}-C\kappa(\vartheta)|\nabla_{x}\Theta|\right]_{\rm res}\mathds{1}_{\vartheta\geq\overline{\vartheta}}.

Obviously, there is
\xi=\xi(\overline{\vartheta},\Theta)
such that

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}|[\nabla_{x}\vartheta]_{\rm res}|^{2}\mathds{1}_{\vartheta<\overline{\vartheta}}\ \,{\rm d}{x}

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle<}}{{\sim}}\int_{\Omega}\left[\frac{\kappa(\vartheta)|\nabla_{x}\vartheta|^{2}}{\vartheta^{2}}-C\kappa(\vartheta)|\nabla_{x}\Theta|\right]_{\rm res}\mathds{1}_{\vartheta<\overline{\vartheta}}\ \,{\rm d}{x}+\xi\int_{\Omega}E\left(\varrho,\vartheta,{\bf u},{\bf B}\Big|r,\Theta,{\bf U},{\bf H}\right)_{\rm res}\ \,{\rm d}{x}.

(4.24)

Thus it remains to handle the integral

\int_{\Omega}\left[\frac{\kappa(\vartheta)|\nabla_{x}\vartheta|^{2}}{\vartheta^{2}}-C\kappa(\vartheta)|\nabla_{x}\Theta|\right]_{\rm res}\mathds{1}_{\vartheta\geq\overline{\vartheta}}\ \,{\rm d}{x}=\int_{\vartheta\geq\overline{\vartheta}}\left(\frac{\kappa(\vartheta)|\nabla_{x}\vartheta|^{2}}{\vartheta^{2}}-C\kappa(\vartheta)|\nabla_{x}\Theta|\right)\,{\rm d}{x}.

As
\kappa(\vartheta)\approx(1+\vartheta^{\beta})
, our task reduces to

\int_{\vartheta\geq\overline{\vartheta}}\left(\frac{\kappa(\vartheta)|\nabla_{x}\vartheta|^{2}}{\vartheta^{2}}-C\kappa(\vartheta)|\nabla_{x}\Theta|\right)\,{\rm d}{x}\approx\int_{\vartheta\geq\overline{\vartheta}}\left(|\nabla_{x}\vartheta^{\frac{\beta}{2}}|^{2}-C\vartheta^{\beta}|\nabla_{x}\Theta|\right)\,{\rm d}{x}.

First observe

\int_{\vartheta\geq\overline{\vartheta}}\vartheta^{\beta}|\nabla_{x}\Theta|\,{\rm d}{x}\leq\int_{\vartheta\geq\overline{\vartheta}}\left(\vartheta^{\frac{\beta}{2}}-{\overline{\vartheta}}^{\frac{\beta}{2}}\right)^{2}|\nabla_{x}\Theta|\,{\rm d}{x}+\int_{\vartheta\geq\overline{\vartheta}}\overline{\vartheta}^{\beta}|\nabla_{x}\Theta|\,{\rm d}{x},

where

\int_{\vartheta\geq\overline{\vartheta}}\overline{\vartheta}^{\beta}|\nabla_{x}\Theta|\,{\rm d}{x}\leq\zeta\int_{\Omega}E\left(\varrho,\vartheta,{\bf u},{\bf B}\Big|r,\Theta,{\bf U},{\bf H}\right)_{\rm res}\ \,{\rm d}{x}.

Finally, it remains to handle the integral

\displaystyle\int_{\vartheta\geq\overline{\vartheta}}\left(|\nabla_{x}\vartheta^{\frac{\beta}{2}}|^{2}-C\left(\vartheta^{\frac{\beta}{2}}-{\overline{\vartheta}}^{\frac{\beta}{2}}\right)^{2}|\nabla_{x}\Theta|\right)\,{\rm d}{x}

\displaystyle=\int_{\vartheta\geq\overline{\vartheta}}\left(\left|\nabla_{x}\left(\vartheta^{\frac{\beta}{2}}-{\overline{\vartheta}}^{\frac{\beta}{2}}\right)\right|^{2}-C\left(\vartheta^{\frac{\beta}{2}}-{\overline{\vartheta}}^{\frac{\beta}{2}}\right)^{2}|\nabla_{x}\Theta|\right)\,{\rm d}{x}.

On the one hand, using Sobolev–Poincaré inequality we get

\displaystyle\int_{\vartheta\geq\overline{\vartheta}}\left|\nabla_{x}\left(\vartheta^{\frac{\beta}{2}}-{\overline{\vartheta}}^{\frac{\beta}{2}}\right)\right|^{2}\,{\rm d}{x}=\int_{\Omega}\left|\nabla_{x}\left[\vartheta^{\frac{\beta}{2}}-{\overline{\vartheta}}^{\frac{\beta}{2}}\right]^{+}\right|^{2}\ \,{\rm d}{x}

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle>}}{{\sim}}\left(\int_{\Omega}\left|\left[\vartheta^{\frac{\beta}{2}}-{\overline{\vartheta}}^{\frac{\beta}{2}}\right]^{+}\right|^{6}\ \,{\rm d}{x}\right)^{\frac{1}{3}}=\left(\int_{\vartheta\geq\overline{\vartheta}}\left(\vartheta^{\frac{\beta}{2}}-{\overline{\vartheta}}^{\frac{\beta}{2}}\right)^{6}\,{\rm d}{x}\right)^{\frac{1}{3}}.

(4.25)

On the other hand, by Hölder’s inequality,

\int_{\vartheta\geq\overline{\vartheta}}\left(\vartheta^{\frac{\beta}{2}}-{\overline{\vartheta}}^{\frac{\beta}{2}}\right)^{2}|\nabla_{x}\Theta|\,{\rm d}{x}\leq\left(\int_{\vartheta\geq\overline{\vartheta}}\left(\vartheta^{\frac{\beta}{2}}-{\overline{\vartheta}}^{\frac{\beta}{2}}\right)^{4}\,{\rm d}{x}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\left(\int_{\vartheta\geq\overline{\vartheta}}|\nabla_{x}\Theta|^{2}\,{\rm d}{x}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

Next, by Jensen’s inequality,

\displaystyle\left(\int_{\vartheta\geq\overline{\vartheta}}\left(\vartheta^{\frac{\beta}{2}}-{\overline{\vartheta}}^{\frac{\beta}{2}}\right)^{4}\,{\rm d}{x}\right)^{\frac{3}{2}}=\left(\int_{\Omega}\left(\left[\vartheta^{\frac{\beta}{2}}-{\overline{\vartheta}}^{\frac{\beta}{2}}\right]^{+}\right)^{4}\ \,{\rm d}{x}\right)^{\frac{3}{2}}

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle<}}{{\sim}}\int_{\Omega}\left(\left[\vartheta^{\frac{\beta}{2}}-{\overline{\vartheta}}^{\frac{\beta}{2}}\right]^{+}\right)^{6}\ \,{\rm d}{x}=\int_{\vartheta\geq\overline{\vartheta}}\left(\vartheta^{\frac{\beta}{2}}-{\overline{\vartheta}}^{\frac{\beta}{2}}\right)^{6}\,{\rm d}{x};

whence

\left(\int_{\vartheta\geq\overline{\vartheta}}\left(\vartheta^{\frac{\beta}{2}}-{\overline{\vartheta}}^{\frac{\beta}{2}}\right)^{4}\,{\rm d}{x}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle<}}{{\sim}}\left(\int_{\vartheta\geq\overline{\vartheta}}\left(\vartheta^{\frac{\beta}{2}}-{\overline{\vartheta}}^{\frac{\beta}{2}}\right)^{6}\,{\rm d}{x}\right)^{\frac{1}{3}}.

(4.26)

Our final observation is that as
\|\vartheta\|_{L^{4}(\Omega)}
is bounded, for any
\delta>0
, there is

\overline{\vartheta}(\delta)
such that

\int_{\vartheta\geq\overline{\vartheta}}|\nabla_{x}\Theta|^{2}\,{\rm d}{x}\leq\delta.

Combining (
4.25
), (
4.26
) we may infer

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}|[\nabla_{x}\vartheta]_{\rm res}|^{2}\mathds{1}_{\vartheta\geq\overline{\vartheta}}\ \,{\rm d}{x}

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle<}}{{\sim}}\int_{\Omega}\left[\frac{\kappa(\vartheta)|\nabla_{x}\vartheta|^{2}}{\vartheta^{2}}-C\kappa(\vartheta)|\nabla_{x}\Theta|\right]_{\rm res}\ \,{\rm d}{x}+\xi\int_{\Omega}E\left(\varrho,\vartheta,{\bf u},{\bf B}\Big|r,\Theta,{\bf U},{\bf H}\right)_{\rm res}\ \,{\rm d}{x}

(4.27)

Putting together (
4.22
), (
4.24
), and (
4.27
) we obtain (
4.20
).

∎

Exactly as in

\displaystyle\left\|\nabla_{x}({\bf u}-{\bf U})+\nabla_{x}^{t}({\bf u}-{\bf U})-\frac{2}{3}{\rm div}_{x}({\bf u}-{\bf U})\mathbb{I}\right\|_{L^{q}(\Omega;R^{3\times 3})}^{2}

(4.29)

\displaystyle\left\|{\bf curl}_{x}({\bf B}-{\bf H})\right\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega;R^{3})}\leq c\left(\int_{\Omega}\left(\frac{\Theta}{\vartheta}-1\right)\zeta(\vartheta)|{\bf curl}_{x}{\bf B}|^{2}+\left(\frac{\vartheta}{\Theta}-1\right)\zeta(\Theta)|{\bf curl}_{x}{\bf H}|^{2}\ \,{\rm d}{x}\right.

(4.30)

\left\|{\bf v}\right\|_{W^{1,q}(\Omega;R^{3})}\leq c(\delta,q)\left(\left\|\nabla_{x}{\bf v}+\nabla_{x}^{t}{\bf v}-\frac{2}{3}{\rm div}_{x}{\bf v}\mathbb{I}\right\|_{L^{q}(\Omega;R^{3\times 3})}+\int_{\Omega}\varrho|{\bf v}|\ \,{\rm d}{x}\right)

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}E\left(\varrho,\vartheta,{\bf u},{\bf B}\ \Big|r,\Theta,{\bf U},{\bf H}\right)(\tau,\cdot)\ \,{\rm d}{x}

We may therefore state the following result concerning the existence of weak solutions to the compressible MHD system.

Theorem 5.1
.

(Existence of weak solution)

Let
\Omega\subset R^{3}
be a bounded domain of class at least
C^{3}
. Suppose the data

\vartheta_{B}
,
{\bf B}_{B}
are twice continuously differentiable,

{\inf}_{[0,T]\times\partial\Omega}\vartheta_{B}>0,\ {\rm div}_{x}{\bf B}_{B}=0\ \mbox{in}\ (0,T)\times\Omega.

Let the thermodynamic functions

p
,
e
,
s
satisfy the hypotheses (
2.1
)–(
2.6
) and the transport coefficients

\mu
,
\eta
,
\kappa
, and
\zeta
comply with (
2.7
)–(
2.9
), where

\frac{1}{2}\leq\alpha\leq 1,\ \beta>6,\ \lim_{Z\to\infty}\mathcal{S}(Z)=0.

(5.12)

Then for any initial data

\displaystyle\varrho_{0},\ \varrho_{0}

\displaystyle>0,\ \vartheta_{0},\vartheta_{0}>0,\ {\bf u}_{0},\ {\bf B}_{0},\ {\rm div}_{x}{\bf B}_{0}=0,

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\left[\frac{1}{2}\varrho_{0}|{\bf u}_{0}|^{2}+\varrho_{0}e(\varrho_{0},\vartheta_{0})+\frac{1}{2}|{\bf B}_{0}|^{2}\ \,{\rm d}{x}\right]<\infty,

Remark 5.2
.

As a matter of fact, positivity of the initial density and temperature is not really necessary in the weak framework. Expressing the initial data in terms of the initial density
\varrho_{0}
, the momentum
{\bf m}_{0}
,
the total entropy
S_{0}

{\bf B}_{0}

is finite.

Remark 5.3
.

The weak solutions resulting from the approximate scheme specified above satisfy the differential version
of the ballistic energy inequality (
2.24
).

Remark 5.4
.

The hypothesis

6
Concluding remarks

The theory can be extended in a straightforward manner to other types of boundary conditions.

•

The complete slip boundary conditions (
1.10
) can be replaced by the Dirichlet (no slip) boundary
conditions

{\bf u}|_{\partial\Omega}={\bf u}_{B}

as long as
{\bf u}_{B}\cdot{\bf n}=0
. This enables to include problems of Taylor–Coutte type. The extension to
general in/out flow boundary conditions for the velocity would require a more elaborated treatment in view of the
presence of the magnetic field. For the in/out flow boundary conditions for the Navier–Stokes–Fourier system in the absence of magnetic field see

[
9
]
.

•

The Dirichlet boundary conditions for the temperature can be replaced by a flux type condition on a part
of the boundary:

\partial\Omega=\Gamma_{1}\cup\Gamma_{2},\ \vartheta|_{\Gamma_{1}}=\vartheta_{B},\ {\bf q}\cdot{\bf n}|_{\Gamma_{2}}={\bf h}.

Note that this type of condition is particularly relevant in the modelling of stellar magnetoconvection,
see Thompson and Christensen–Dalsgaard
[
17
]
.

•

The induction flux (electric field) can be prescribed in (
1.14
):

\Big[{\bf B}\times{\bf u}+\zeta{\bf curl}_{x}{\bf B}\Big]\times{\bf n}|_{\partial\Omega}={\bf E}_{B}\times{\bf n}.

•

Mixed type boundary conditions imposed on
different
components of
\partial\Omega
can be accommodated without
essential difficulties.

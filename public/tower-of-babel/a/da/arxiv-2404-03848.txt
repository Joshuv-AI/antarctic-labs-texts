Introduction

Let
X
be a compact Hausdorff space as our phase space and
T
a Hausdorff topological group as our phase group, unless otherwise specified.
We say that

is a

\mathscr{X}
or
T\curvearrowright X

If

We shall consider the pointwise almost periodic inheritance of subnormal co-compact closed subgroup action (see Thm.

.

be any flow and

Proof.

(If

Step 1.
Assume

is any net with

in

for some

\mathbb{Z}\in\mathcal{S}^{\textsl{ns}}(\mathbb{R})

Proof.

Let

\overline{Hx}=\overline{Hy}
for all
y\in\overline{Hx}
.
It then follows from
5.0
b that
E_{x}[H]=E_{y}[H]
for all
y\in\overline{Hx}
. The proof is completed.
∎

Now we will prove that
E_{x}[H]
is a closed subgroup of
T
. However, it is “non-normal” in general. The following theorem has the flavor of
[
7
, Cor. 2.12]
that asserts that a closed subsemigroup that contains a normal syndetic subgroup is a subgroup of
T
.

5.3 Theorem
.

Let
T
be an
w​F
-group (cf. Lem.
1.7
). If
S
be a closed subsemigroup of
T
such that for all
V\in\mathfrak{N}_{e}(T)
there is a finite set
F
in
T
with
FVS=T
, then
S
is a subgroup of
T
.

Note
.

If
T
is a topological group, then ‘a finite set
F
’ may be relaxed as ‘a compact set
F
’ here.

Proof.

Let
s\in S
. We need only prove
s^{-1}\in S
. For this, take
V\in\mathfrak{N}_{e}(T)
. Then there is a finite subset
F
of
T
such that
FVS=T
. Let
k_{0}\in FV
; then
k_{0}s^{-1}=k_{1}s_{1}
for some
k_{1}\in FV
and
s_{1}\in S
. Further,
k_{1}s^{-1}=k_{2}s_{2}
for some
k_{2}\in FV
and
s_{2}\in S
. By induction, there are two sequences
k_{0},k_{1},k_{2},\dotsc
in
FV
and
s_{1},s_{2},s_{3},\dotsc
in
S
with
k_{i}s^{-1}=k_{i+1}s_{i+1}
for all
i=0,1,\dotsc
. Write
k_{i}=f_{i}v_{i}
with
f_{i}\in F
and
v_{i}\in V
for all
i
. Since
F
is finite, we can find some
0\leq m<n<\infty
such that
f_{m}=f_{n}=f
. Then

\begin{split}k_{n}^{-1}k_{m}s^{-1}&=k_{n}^{-1}k_{m+1}s_{m+1}=k_{n}^{-1}(k_{m+1}s^{-1})ss_{m+1}\\
&=k_{n}^{-1}k_{m+2}s_{m+2}ss_{m+1}\\
&=k_{n}^{-1}(k_{m+2}s^{-1})ss_{m+2}ss_{m+1}=\dotsm\\
&=k_{n}^{-1}k_{n}s_{n}ss_{n-1}\dotsm ss_{m+1}\\
&=s_{n}ss_{n-1}\dotsm ss_{m+1}.\end{split}

Hence
k_{n}^{-1}k_{m}s^{-1}\in S
and
v_{n}^{-1}v_{m}s^{-1}\in S
.
Thus
v_{m}s^{-1}\in v_{n}S\subseteq VS
. Taking
v_{m}\to e
in
T
, this implies that
s^{-1}\in VS
for all
V\in\mathfrak{N}_{e}(T)
. Next we can select two nets
v_{i}\to e
in
T
and
s_{i}\in S
such that
s^{-1}=v_{i}s_{i}
and then
v_{i}^{-1}s^{-1}=s_{i}\to s^{-1}\in S
, for
S
is closed. The proof is completed.
∎

5.3
A. Proposition
.

Let
S
be a closed subsemigroup of an
w​F
-group
T
, which includes a co-compact closed subgroup of
T
. Then
S
is a co-compact closed subgroup of
T
.

Proof.

Let
H\leq S
be a co-compact closed subgroup of
T
. Let
V\in\mathfrak{N}_{e}(T)
. Then there is a finite set
F
in
T
with
FVH\,(\subseteq FVS)=T
. Thus,
S
is a subgroup of
T
by Theorem
5.3
. Moreover, by Lemma
1.7
,
S
is co-compact.
∎

5.3
B. Proposition

(cf.
[

, Lem. 2.06]
or
[

for

.

If

Let

5.6 Corollary

(cf.

6.
Relation of orbital closure of
ncc
-subgroups

Let
\mathscr{X}
be a flow with compact Hausdorff phase space
X
and with phase mapping
T\times X\rightarrow X
,
(t,x)\mapsto tx
in the sequel of this section.

6.1
.

Given a subgroup
H
of
T
, let
H\curvearrowright X
be the induced subflow of
\mathscr{X}
. We define

R_{H}=\{(x,x^{\prime})\,|\,x,x^{\prime}\in X\textrm{ s.t. }x^{\prime}\in\overline{Hx}\}
,

which is called the
H
-orbital closure relation
of
\mathscr{X}
. Write

X/H
for
X/R_{H}
,
where
X/H
is equipped with the quotient topology.

If
R_{H}
is a closed
T
-invariant equivalence relation on
X
, then
X/H
is a compact Hausdorff space (by Lem.
6.3
below) so that

T\curvearrowright X/H
is a flow. If
H\curvearrowright X
is pointwise a.p., then
R_{H}
is an equivalence relation. However,
R_{H}
is generally not an invariant closed relation.

6.2 Lemma
.

If
\mathscr{X}
is a minimal flow and
H\in\mathcal{S}^{\textsl{ncc}}(T)
, then
R_{H}
is a closed invariant equivalence relation of
\mathscr{X}
.

Proof.

By Theorem
3.1
-(1) and (2), it follows that
R_{H}
is a
T
-invariant equivalence relation on
X
. Further by Theorem
3.1
-(3),
R_{H}
is closed in
X\times X
. The proof is completed.
∎

It turns out that, for
H\in\mathcal{S}^{\textsl{ncc}}(T)
,
X/R_{H}
is a compact Hausdorff space by the following folklore lemma in general topology, where the “compact Hausdorff” of
X
plays a role in its proof. We shall give a proof here for reader’s convenience, which is comparable with Lemma
1.8
b.

6.3 Lemma

(cf.
[
7
, Lem. 4.9]
without proof)
.

Let
R
be an equivalence relation on
X
. Then
R
is closed in
X\times X
iff
X/R
is a Hausdorff space.

Proof.

Let
\rho\colon X\rightarrow X/R
be the quotient map.
If
X/R
is Hausdorff, then
\Delta_{X/R}
is closed in
X/R\times X/R
so that
R=(\rho\times\rho)^{-1}[\Delta_{X/R}]
is closed in
X\times X
. (This still holds for
X
a topological space not necessarily compact and Hausdorff.)

Conversely, suppose
R
is closed in
X\times X
.
Let
A\not=\emptyset
be a closed subset of
X
and set
R[A]=\bigcup_{a\in A}R(a)\subseteq X
. Clearly,
R[A]=\rho^{-1}\rho[A]
is closed in
X
, for
R[A]
is exactly equal to the image of
A\times X\cap R
under the canonical map
(x_{1},x_{2})\mapsto x_{2}
.
So
\rho
is closed. If
U\subseteq X
is open, then

R^{*}(U):=\{x\in X\,|\,R(x)\subseteq U\}=X\setminus R[X\setminus U]=X\setminus\rho^{-1}\rho[X\setminus U]

is open. So
\rho[R^{*}(U)]
is open in
X\setminus R
. Now if
\rho(x)\not=\rho(y)
(or equivalently,
R(x)\cap R(y)=\emptyset
), then we can choose disjoint open sets
U_{x}
,
U_{y}
with
R(x)\subseteq U_{x}
and
R(y)\subseteq U_{y}
. Thus,
\rho[R^{*}(U_{x})]\in\mathfrak{N}_{\rho(x)}(X/R)
and
\rho[R^{*}(U_{y})]\in\mathfrak{N}_{\rho(y)}(X/R)
are disjoint, and,
X/R
is a Hausdorff space. The proof is completed.
∎

6.4 Remark
.

For the necessity proof above, “compact Hausdorff” of
X
has played a role in proving that
R[A]
is a closed subset of
X
.

If
R
is closed in
X\times X
, then the cells
R(x)
, for
x\in X
, are closed sets in
X
. However, the converse is false (so that this negates
[
6
, A.2.3]
). Let’s see a counterexample.

Example
.

Let
X=[0,1]\times[0,1]=\{x=(x_{1},x_{2})\,|\,0\leq x_{1},x_{2}\leq 1\}
. Define a relation
R
on
X
by the way: for
x=(x_{1},x_{2})
,
y=(y_{1},y_{2})\in X
,

x\sim y
iff
x_{1}=y_{1}
if
0\leq x_{1},y_{1}<1
;
x\sim y
iff
x=y
if
x_{1}=y_{1}=1
.

Clearly,
R
is an equivalence relation on
X
such that
R(x)
, for
x\in X
, is a closed set in
X
. However,
R
is not closed in
X\times X
. Thus,
X/R
is compact and not of Hausdorff.

6.5 Theorem
.

Let
\mathscr{X}
be a minimal flow and
H\in\mathcal{S}^{\textsl{ncc}}(T)
. Then for all
x\in X
, the evaluation mapping

\mathfrak{e}\colon T/E_{x}[H]\rightarrow X/H,\quad tE_{x}[H]\mapsto t\overline{Hx}

is an isomorphism from
T\curvearrowright T/E_{x}[H]
onto
T\curvearrowright X/H
, and moreover,
T\curvearrowright X/H
is an a.p. factor of
\mathscr{X}
.

Note
.

Consequently,
T\curvearrowright X/H
may be thought of as an a.p. coset flow of
T
if
H\in\mathcal{S}^{\textsl{ncc}}(T)
. On the other hand, if
H\in\mathcal{S}^{\textsl{sncc}}(T)\setminus\mathcal{S}^{\textsl{ncc}}(T)
, then the statement is generally false. For example, let
H\lhd K\lhd G
be as in Example
1.11
; let
X=G/H
and
x=[e]=H\in X
. Then for
T=G
,
E_{x}[H]=H
and by Example
3.4
A,
R_{H}
is not closed so that
X/H
is not Hausdorff by Lemma
6.3
. Since
G/E_{x}[H]=G/H
is Hausdorff, hence
G/E_{x}[H]\not\cong X/H
.

Proof.

At first, since
\{\overline{Hy}\,|\,y\in X\}
is a closed
T
-invariant partition of
X
into
H
-minimal sets by Theorem
3.1
. So
X/H
is a compact Hausdorff space and
T\curvearrowright X/H
is a minimal flow by Lemmas
6.2
and
6.3
. By Corollary
5.7
,
T\curvearrowright T/E_{x}[H]
is an a.p. minimal flow. Obviously
\mathfrak{e}
is a well-defined 1-1 onto map such that
\mathfrak{e}t=t\mathfrak{e}
for all
t\in T
. It remains to show that
\mathfrak{e}
is a continuous mapping. For this, consider the CD:

\begin{diagram}\qquad\textrm{where}\begin{cases}f\colon t\mapsto t\overline{Hx},\\
\rho\colon t\mapsto tE_{x}[H].\end{cases}

Notice that
t_{n}\to t
in
T
implies that
t_{n}x\to tx
; and
H\in\mathcal{S}^{\textsl{ncc}}(T)
and
t_{n}\to t
in
T
implies that
f(t_{n})\to f(t)
in
X/H
. Thus,
f
and
\rho
both are continuous maps. Moreover, if
tE_{x}[H]=sE_{x}[H]
(or equivalently, if
t^{-1}s\in E_{x}[H]
), then
f(t)=f(s)
so that
\mathfrak{e}
is continuous.
Thus,
T\curvearrowright X/H
is equicontinuous. The proof is completed.
∎

6.8 Theorem
.

Let
\mathscr{X}
be a minimal flow,

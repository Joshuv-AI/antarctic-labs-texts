Finite Presentability of Brin-Higman-Thompson Monoids via Free Jónsson-Tarski Algebras
Bill de Witt, Luke Elliott

Abstract
We show that the total monoids t ​ o ​ t ​ n ​ M k , 1 totnM_{k,1} introduced by Birget [ 1 , 2 ] (and their multi-rooted generalisations), which extend the Brin-Higman-Thompson groups, can be realised as the endomorphism monoids of higher dimensional Jónnson-Tarski algebras. We use this representation to show that they are finitely presented.

Finite Presentability

We now show that the monoids
totnM_{k,r}
are finitely presented. We will use a method similar to Thompson’s original proof of finite presentability for his monoid
tot1M_{2,1}
, which will involve converting to and working with an anti-isomorphic monoid, which involves the use of nested matrices.

Let
\mathcal{L}
be the set of labeled free generating sets for
\mathbb{F}_{n,k,r}
with labels from
\mathbb{N}
. That is

\mathcal{L}=\bigcup_{\begin{subarray}{c}F\text{ is a free generating set for }\mathbb{F}_{n,k,r}\end{subarray}}\mathbb{N}^{F}.

For each

(f)\sigma_{n,k,r}
denote the binary relation on

defined by

(f)\sigma_{n,k,r}:=\left\{(L_{1},L_{2})\in\mathcal{L}^{2}\;\middle|\;\begin{tabular}[]{@{}l@{}}$fL_{1}\supseteq L_{2}$\end{tabular}\right\}.

Let
\mathcal{L}_{I}:=\left\{f\in\mathcal{L}\;\middle|\;\begin{tabular}[]{@{}l@{}}$f$ is injective\end{tabular}\right\}
, that is
\mathcal{L}_{I}
is the set of labelled free generating sets for
\mathbb{F}_{n,k,r}
such that different elements have different labels. The
\sigma_{n,k,r}
function will be useful to us as it is injective, so along with a later definition of multiplication on its image, we will yield an anti-isomorphic monoid.

Lemma 4.1
.

If

(f)\sigma_{n,k,r}\cap(g)\sigma_{n,k,r}

.

Proof.

Let

be the unique function such that
fL_{1}=L_{2}
(in particular
f^{\prime}=L_{2}L_{1}^{-1}
which is defined because of the assumptions on

If
f\in\operatorname{End}(\mathbb{F}_{n,k,r})

(L_{1},L_{2})\in(f)\sigma_{n,k,r}
if and only if

.
As the domain of

is a free generating set for
\mathbb{F}_{n,k,r}
, there is a unique such endomorphism.
∎

Note that if
f,g\in\operatorname{End}(\mathbb{F}_{n,k,r})
and

(L_{1},L_{2})\in(f)\sigma_{n,k,r}\circ(g)\sigma_{n,k,r}

with

fL_{1}\supseteq L_{3}\quad\text{ and }\quad gL_{3}\supseteq L_{2}.

Hence

and

. So
(f)\sigma_{n,k,r}\circ(g)\sigma_{n,k,r}\subseteq(gf)\sigma_{n,k,r}
.
For all
f,g\in\operatorname{End}(\mathbb{F}_{n,k})
, we have

(fg)\sigma_{n,k,r}\supseteq(g)\sigma_{n,k,r}\circ(f)\sigma_{n,k,r}.

Since
(g)\sigma_{n,k,r}\circ(f)\sigma_{n,k,r}
contains a pair
(L_{1},L_{2})
whose first element is from
\mathcal{L}_{I}
, by Lemma
4
,

is the only element whose relation contains it.

Definition 4.2

We define a multiplication on the set

Closing Comments

If you try to do the same with Birget’s partial monoid, it doesn’t quite work.

Definition 5.1
.

Let
A
be an algebra. The partial endomorphism monoid of
A

\PEnd(A)
is the monoid of homomorphisms between subalgebras of
A
(considered as partial function on
A
).

Note that
\mathbb{F}_{n,k,1}
has uncountably many subalgebras for
n\geq 1

k\geq 2
, and so
\PEnd(\mathbb{F}_{n,k,1})
is uncountable, and hence
\PEnd(\mathbb{F}_{n,k,1})\not\cong nM_{k,1}
as defined by Birget in
[
1
]
.

Birget’s monoid is defined in terms of finitely generated right ideals of
n\{0,1,\dots,k-1\}^{\ast}
which corresponds to finitely generated subalgebras of
\mathbb{F}_{n,k,1}
. The uncountability of
\PEnd(\mathbb{F}_{n,k,1})
comes from the fact that it allows endomorphisms on non-finitely generated subalgebras, so perhaps restricting
\PEnd
(or extending
nM_{k,1}
) would yield isomorphisms.

Birget notes in
[
1
]
that it is an open question whether the partial monoids
nM_{k,1}
are finitely presented. The methods presented in this paper may be modifiable to work with these structures, as well as the more general
nM_{k,r}
.

Projection-Free Variance Reduction Methods for Stochastic Constrained Multi-Level Compositional Optimization
Wei Jiang, Sifan Yang, Wenhao Yang, Yibo Wang, Yuanyu Wan, Lijun Zhang

Abstract
This paper investigates projection-free algorithms for stochastic constrained multi-level optimization. In this context, the objective function is a nested composition of several smooth functions, and the decision set is closed and convex. Existing projection-free algorithms for solving this problem suffer from two limitations: 1) they solely focus on the gradient mapping criterion and fail to match the optimal sample complexities in unconstrained settings; 2) their analysis is exclusively applicable to non-convex functions, without considering convex and strongly convex objectives. To address these issues, we introduce novel projection-free variance reduction algorithms and analyze their complexities under different criteria. For gradient mapping, our complexities improve existing results and match the optimal rates for unconstrained problems. For the widely-used Frank-Wolfe gap criterion, we provide theoretical guarantees that align with those for single-level problems. Additionally, by using a stage-wise adaptation, we further obtain complexities for convex and strongly convex functions. Finally, numerical experiments on different tasks demonstrate the effectiveness of our methods.

Introduction

In this paper, we consider projection-free algorithms for stochastic constrained multi-level compositional optimization in the form of

\min_{\mathbf{x}\in\mathcal{X}}F(\mathbf{x})=f_{K}\circ f_{K-1}\circ\cdots\circ f_{1}(\mathbf{x}),

(1)

where
\mathcal{X}
is a closed convex set. We assume that each function
f_{i}
and its gradient can only be accessed through noisy estimations, symbolized as
f_{i}(\cdot;\xi)
and
\nabla f_{i}(\cdot;\xi)
such that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{\xi}\left[f_{i}(\cdot;\xi)\right]=f_{i}(\cdot)\quad\text{and}\quad\mathbb{E}_{\xi}\left[\nabla f_{i}(\cdot;\xi)\right]=\nabla f_{i}(\cdot),

where
\xi
denotes samples drawn from the oracle. Problem (
1

(
Dann et al., 2014
)
, government planning
(
Bruno et al., 2016
)
, risk management
(
Dentcheva et al., 2017
)
, model-agnostic meta-learning
(
Ji et al., 2020
)
, robust learning
(
Li et al., 2021
)
, risk-averse portfolio optimization
(
Shapiro et al., 2021
)
, and graph neural network training
(
Balasubramanian et al., 2021
)

;

;
Chen et al., 2021
;

should be in a simplex for portfolio optimization). Traditional constrained optimization typically employs a projection operation to ensure that the solutions are within the decision set. However, projection is usually complicated and time-consuming, and existing literature
(

)
begin to show interest in developing projection-free algorithms for constrained multi-level problems by replacing projection (a convex optimization problem) with multiple steps of more efficient linear minimization operation.

Projection-free methods typically require two oracles: 1) the Stochastic First-order Oracle (SFO), which takes a point
\mathbf{x}
and returns the pair
(f(\mathbf{x};\xi),\nabla f(\mathbf{x};\xi))
, where
\xi
is a sample drawn from the oracle; 2) the Linear Minimization Oracle (LMO), which takes a direction
\mathbf{d}
and outputs
\arg\min_{\mathbf{x}\in\mathcal{X}}\left\langle\mathbf{x},\mathbf{d}\right\rangle
. To evaluate different projection-free algorithms, the most widely used measures are the number of calls to SFO and LMO required to attain an acceptable solution. For non-convex functions, such a solution
\mathbf{x}
is usually defined by the Frank-Wolfe gap
(
Lacoste-Julien, 2016
)
, formalized as

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}(\mathbf{x})\coloneqq\max_{\hat{\mathbf{x}}\in\mathcal{X}}\langle\hat{\mathbf{x}}-\mathbf{x},-\nabla F(\mathbf{x})\rangle\leq\epsilon,

(2)

where
\epsilon
is a small value. More recently, the gradient mapping criterion
(
Qu et al., 2018
)
has been introduced, which is denoted as

\displaystyle\mathcal{G}(\mathbf{x})\coloneqq\left\|\beta\left(\mathbf{x}-\Pi_{\mathcal{X}}\left(\mathbf{x}-\frac{1}{\beta}\nabla F(\mathbf{x})\right)\right)\right\|^{2}\leq\epsilon,

(3)

where
\Pi_{\mathcal{X}}
denote the projection onto the domain
\mathcal{X}
and parameter
\beta
is a positive constant. Notably, if
\mathcal{X}=\mathbb{R}^{d}
, this gradient mapping criterion simplifies to the stationary point, i.e.,
\mathbb{E}\left[\left\|\nabla F(\mathbf{x})\right\|^{2}\right]\leq\epsilon
, which is the standard metric for stochastic unconstrained problems. When the objective function is convex or strongly convex, the optimal gap criterion is employed instead, expressed as

\displaystyle F(\mathbf{x})-\min_{\hat{\mathbf{x}}\in\mathcal{X}}F(\hat{\mathbf{x}})\leq\epsilon,

(4)

which measures the difference between the objective and the optimal value.

The current method for stochastic projection-free multi-level compositional optimization, named as LiNASA+ICG
(
Xiao et al., 2022
)
, integrates the linearized NASA
(
Ghadimi et al., 2020
)
algorithm with inexact conditional gradient
(
Balasubramanian & Ghadimi, 2018
)
, and provides theoretical guarantees under the gradient mapping criterion. This method can identify an acceptable point with
\mathcal{O}(\epsilon^{-2})
calls to SFO and
\mathcal{O}(\epsilon^{-3})
calls to LMO. However, it still suffers from several drawbacks. Firstly, its SFO complexity does not match the existing optimal rate of
\mathcal{O}(\epsilon^{-1.5})
for stochastic unconstrained problems. Secondly, its analysis solely concentrates on gradient mapping, and results for the more widely used Frank-Wolfe gap criterion are not provided. Finally, this method is restricted to non-convex objective functions, and it is unclear how to improve the rate for convex and strongly convex objectives.

To address these issues, we propose new algorithms that utilize the variance reduction estimator STORM
(
Cutkosky & Orabona, 2019
)
to obtain more accurate evaluations of both the inner function values and the overall gradient. By integrating these estimators with a specifically designed Frank-Wolfe algorithm
(
Jaggi, 2013
)
, we improve the rate for gradient mapping and are able to analyze the Frank-Wolfe gap. Besides, by employing a large batch size, we can reduce the iteration numbers and thus improve the LMO complexities while maintaining the same SFO rates. Moreover, we develop a stage-wise algorithm with a warm-start technique and provide theoretical guarantees for both convex and strongly convex functions. Compared with previous methods, this paper makes the following contributions:

1.

We establish the first theoretical guarantees for the Frank-Wolfe gap (Theorem
1
,
2
) under the multi-level setting. The rates we obtained match the results for single-level projection-free methods
(
Zhang et al., 2019
;
Yurtsever et al., 2019
)
.

2.

For gradient mapping (Theorem
3
,
4
), our approach achieves an improved SFO complexity of
\mathcal{O}(\epsilon^{-1.5})
and LMO complexity of
\mathcal{O}(\epsilon^{-2.5})
. The SFO complexity matches the low bound
(
Arjevani et al., 2019
)
, and the LMO complexity can be further reduced to
\mathcal{O}(\epsilon^{-2})
by using large batch sizes.

3.

We explore the complexities for convex (Theorem
5
,
6
) and strongly convex functions (Theorem
7
,
8
), and derive the optimal SFO rates for these problems, which have not been studied in previous projection-free multi-level literature.

We compare our theoretical results with existing methods in Table
1
, and validate the effectiveness of our method through numerical experiments in Section
4
.

2
Related Work

This section briefly reviews related work on stochastic multi-level compositional optimization and stochastic projection-free algorithms.

2.1
Stochastic Multi-Level Compositional Optimization

Stochastic Compositional Optimization has been explored extensively in the literature, and most research focuses on the two-level settings
(
Wang et al., 2017a
;
Wang et al., 2017b
;
Ghadimi et al., 2020
;
Zhang & Xiao, 2019
;
Chen et al., 2021
;
Qi et al., 2021
;
Jiang et al., 2022a
;
Jiang et al., 2023
;
Yu et al., 2024
)
. The problem of multi-level compositional optimization was first investigated by
Yang et al. (2019)
. Inspired by multi-timescale stochastic approximation
(
Wang et al., 2017a
)
, they introduced the multi-level stochastic gradient method, which achieves a sample complexity of
\mathcal{O}\left(1/\epsilon^{(7+K)/2}\right)
for
K
-level problems. When the function is strongly convex, this complexity can be further improved to
\mathcal{O}\left(1/\epsilon^{(3+K)/4}\right)
. Subsequently, motivated by the NASA algorithm
(
Ghadimi et al., 2020
)
,
Balasubramanian et al. (2021)
proposed using a linearized averaging stochastic estimator to track the function value, attaining a sample complexity of
\mathcal{O}\left(1/\epsilon^{4}\right)
for non-convex objectives. This rate was also obtained in a concurrent work
(
Chen et al., 2021
)
by employing variance reduction techniques to evaluate the function value.

Later,
Zhang & Xiao (2021)
employed nested variance reduction to approximate gradients, improving the sample complexity to the optimal
\mathcal{O}\left(1/\epsilon^{3}\right)
. However, this approach requires a large and increasing batch size on the order of
\mathcal{O}\left(1/\epsilon\right)
. To address this issue,
Jiang et al. (2022b)
developed a method called SMVR, which achieves the same optimal rate but only requires a constant batch size. SMVR also attains an improved rate of
\mathcal{O}\left(1/\epsilon^{2}\right)
for convex functions and
\mathcal{O}\left(1/\left(\lambda\epsilon\right)\right)
for
\lambda
-strongly convex objectives. More recently,
Gao (2023)
further introduced the decentralized stochastic multi-level optimization algorithm, which achieves the level-independent convergence rate under the decentralized setting. Despite these advancements, these algorithms are only applicable to unconstrained problems.

2.2
Stochastic Projection-Free Algorithms

The most well-known projection-free method, Frank-Wolfe algorithm
(
Frank & Wolfe, 1956
)
, was originally designed for smooth convex optimization with polyhedral domains and has been extended to any convex compact set by
Jaggi (2013)
.
In the stochastic setting,
Hazan & Kale (2012)
first developed a projection-free method for online smooth convex optimization. Later,
Hazan & Luo (2016)
applied variance reduction techniques to the stochastic Frank-Wolfe algorithm. Inspired by the accelerated gradient method
(
Nesterov, 1983
)
,
Lan & Zhou (2016)
proposed the stochastic conditional gradient sliding (SCGS) method, which offered an SFO complexity of
\mathcal{O}\left(\lambda^{-1}\epsilon^{-1}\right)
and an LMO complexity of
\mathcal{O}\left(\epsilon^{-1}\right)
for smooth
\lambda
-strongly convex optimization. Besides that, projection-free methods are also widely investigated in online convex optimization in recent years
(
Hazan & Minasyan, 2020
;
Wan et al., 2020
;
Wan et al., 2022
;
Mhammedi, 2022
;
Garber & Kretzu, 2023
;
Wang et al., 2024
)
.

For non-convex objectives,
Reddi et al. (2016)
introduced the SVFW method, achieving an SFO complexity of
\mathcal{O}(\epsilon^{-10/3})
and an LMO complexity of
\mathcal{O}(\epsilon^{-2})
under the Frank-Wolfe gap. Motivated by the variance reduction technique SPIDER
(
Fang et al., 2018
)
,
Yurtsever et al. (2019)
developed the SPIDER-FW algorithm, improving the SFO complexity to
\mathcal{O}(\epsilon^{-3})
by using a large batch size of
\mathcal{O}(\epsilon^{-1})
. To avoid relying on large batches,
Zhang et al. (2019)
proposed the one-sample stochastic Frank-Wolfe algorithm (1-SFW), which attains the same SFO complexity and obtains an LMO complexity of
\mathcal{O}(\epsilon^{-3})
. Rather than focusing on the Frank-Wolfe gap criterion,
Qu et al. (2018)
and
Balasubramanian & Ghadimi (2018)
explored the gradient mapping criterion, and attained
\mathcal{O}(\epsilon^{-2})
complexities for both the SFO and LMO at the cost of using a large batch of
\mathcal{O}(\epsilon^{-1})
in each iteration.

In the context of stochastic multi-level optimization,
Xiao et al. (2022)
recently proposed a projection-free conditional gradient-type algorithm, which combines the linearized NASA algorithm with the inexact conditional gradient technique
(
Balasubramanian & Ghadimi, 2018
)
. This method achieves an SFO complexity of
\mathcal{O}\left(1/\epsilon^{2}\right)
and an LMO complexity of
\mathcal{O}\left(1/\epsilon^{3}\right)
in terms of the gradient mapping criterion. However, its SFO complexity does not match the complexity of
\mathcal{O}\left(1/\epsilon^{1.5}\right)
achieved by variance reduction methods for unconstrained multi-level problems
1
1

1

Gradient mapping reduces to
\mathbb{E}\left[\left\|\nabla F(\mathbf{x})\right\|^{2}\right]\leq\epsilon
for unconstrained problems, and existing methods
(
Zhang & Xiao, 2021
;
Jiang et al., 2022b
)
ensure
\mathbb{E}\left[\left\|\nabla F(\mathbf{x})\right\|\right]\leq\epsilon
with a complexity of
\mathcal{O}(1/\epsilon^{3})
, implying a rate of
\mathcal{O}(1/\epsilon^{1.5})
for gradient mapping.
. Furthermore, they only consider non-convex functions, and solely focus on the gradient mapping criterion, which are the main drawbacks we aim to address in this paper.

3
The Proposed Methods

In this section, we first introduce the assumptions used in this paper. Then, we present the proposed algorithms, along with their corresponding theoretical guarantees for three different criteria: Frank-Wolfe gap, gradient mapping and optimal gap.

3.1
Assumptions

We adopt the following assumptions throughout the paper, which are commonly used in studies of stochastic compositional optimization
(
Yuan et al., 2019
;
Zhang & Xiao, 2019
;
Zhang & Xiao, 2021
;
Jiang et al., 2022b
)
and stochastic projection-free analysis
(
Qu et al., 2018
;
Yurtsever et al., 2019
;
Zhang et al., 2019
)
.

Assumption 1
.

(Constrained set)
The decision set
\mathcal{X}
is closed and convex with a bounded domain such that
\max_{\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y}\in\mathcal{X}}\left\|\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{y}\right\|\leq D
.

Assumption 2
.

(Smoothness and Lipschitz continuity)
All functions
f_{1},\dotsc,f_{K}
are
L_{f}
-Lipschitz continuous, and their Jacobians
\nabla f_{1},\dotsc,\nabla f_{K}
are
L_{J}
-Lipschitz continuous.

Assumption 3
.

(Bounded variance) For
1\leq i\leq K
, we assume that:

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{\xi_{t}^{i}}\left[f_{i}(\mathbf{x};\xi_{t}^{i})\right]=f_{i}(\mathbf{x}),

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{\xi_{t}^{i}}\left[\nabla f_{i}(\mathbf{x};\xi_{t}^{i})\right]=\nabla f_{i}(\mathbf{x}),

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{\xi_{t}^{i}}\left[\left\|f_{i}(\mathbf{x};\xi_{t}^{i})-f_{i}(\mathbf{x})\right\|^{2}\right]\leq\sigma^{2},

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{\xi_{t}^{i}}\left[\left\|\nabla f_{i}(\mathbf{x};\xi_{t}^{i})-\nabla f_{i}(\mathbf{x})\right\|^{2}\right]\leq\sigma_{J}^{2},

where
\{\xi_{t}^{i}\}_{i=1}^{K}
are mutually independent.

Assumption 4
.

(Average smoothness) For
1\leq i\leq K
, we assume that:

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{\xi_{t}^{i}}\left[\left\|f_{i}(\mathbf{x};\xi_{t}^{i})-f_{i}(\mathbf{y};\xi_{t}^{i})\right\|^{2}\right]

\displaystyle\leq\mathcal{L}_{f}^{2}\left\|\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{y}\right\|^{2},

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{\xi_{t}^{i}}\left[\left\|\nabla f_{i}(\mathbf{x};\xi_{t}^{i})-\nabla f_{i}(\mathbf{y};\xi_{t}^{i})\right\|^{2}\right]

\displaystyle\leq\mathcal{L}_{J}^{2}\left\|\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{y}\right\|^{2}.

Assumption 5
.

We suppose that
F\left(\mathbf{x}_{1}\right)-F_{\star}\leq\Delta_{F}
for the initial solution
\mathbf{x}_{1}
, where
F_{\star}=\min_{\mathbf{x}\in\mathcal{X}}F(\mathbf{x})
.

3.2
Results for Frank-Wolfe Gap

First, we examine the sample complexity under the criterion of Frank-Wolfe gap. The primary procedure of our algorithm involves estimating the gradient of the objective function and then employing the Frank-Wolfe method to replace the projection operation. Note that the gradient of the multi-level function exhibits a nested structure, and the estimation error would accumulate as the level becomes deeper. To address this issue, we resort to the variance reduction technique STORM
(
Cutkosky & Orabona, 2019
)
to estimate both the inner function value and the overall gradient. Specifically, we draw a batch of samples
\{\xi_{t}^{i,1},\cdots,\xi_{t}^{i,B_{1}}\}
with the batch size
B_{1}
for each level
i
at time step
t
, and then employ a variance reduction estimator
\mathbf{u}_{t}^{i}
to track each inner function value
f_{i}(\cdot)
as:

\displaystyle\mathbf{u}_{t}^{i}=(1-\alpha)\mathbf{u}_{t-1}^{i}+\frac{1}{B_{1}}\sum_{j=1}^{B_{1}}f_{i}(\mathbf{u}_{t}^{i-1};\xi_{t}^{i,j})

(5)

\displaystyle-(1-\alpha)\frac{1}{B_{1}}\sum_{j=1}^{B_{1}}f_{i}(\mathbf{u}_{t-1}^{i-1};\xi_{t}^{i,j}).

This evaluation ensures that the estimation error is reduced over time. Then, we employ a similar variance-reduced estimator
\mathbf{v}_{t}
to evaluate the overall gradient of the objective function
\nabla F(\mathbf{x}_{t})
as:

\displaystyle\mathbf{v}_{t}=(1-\alpha)\mathbf{v}_{t-1}+\frac{1}{B_{1}}\sum_{j=1}^{B_{1}}\left[\prod_{i=1}^{K}\nabla f_{i}(\mathbf{u}_{t}^{i-1};\xi_{t}^{i,j})\right]

(6)

\displaystyle-(1-\alpha)\frac{1}{B_{1}}\sum_{j=1}^{B_{1}}\left[\prod_{i=1}^{K}\nabla f_{i}(\mathbf{u}_{t-1}^{i-1};\xi_{t}^{i,j})\right].

After obtaining the gradient estimation, we follow the framework of the Frank-Wolfe algorithm
(
Jaggi, 2013
)
, but use the estimator
\mathbf{v}_{t}
to replace the gradient required in the original algorithm as follows:

\displaystyle\mathbf{z}_{t}=\arg\min_{\mathbf{x}\in\mathcal{X}}\langle\mathbf{x},\mathbf{v}_{t}\rangle,

\displaystyle\mathbf{x}_{t+1}=\mathbf{x}_{t}+\eta(\mathbf{z}_{t}-\mathbf{x}_{t}).

In this way, we develop our Projection-free Multi-level Variance Reduction (PMVR) method for stochastic multi-level problems. The complete algorithm is presented in Algorithm
1
. Note that in the first iteration (when
t=1
), we can simply set estimator
\mathbf{u}_{1}^{i}=\frac{1}{B_{0}}\sum_{j=1}^{B_{0}}f_{i}(\mathbf{u}_{1}^{i-1};\xi^{i,j}_{1})
and
\mathbf{v}_{1}=\frac{1}{B_{0}}\sum_{j=1}^{B_{0}}\left[\prod_{i=1}^{K}\nabla f_{i}(\mathbf{u}_{1}^{i-1};\xi_{1}^{i,j})\right]
, where
B_{0}
is the batch size for the first iteration.

Comparison with the SMVR method:
Although the SMVR algorithm
(
Jiang et al., 2022b
)
for unconstrained problems also utilizes the STORM estimator to assess inner function values and gradients at each level, our PMVR method differs from SMVR in the following aspects:
i) SMVR first estimates the gradient at each level using STORM and then computes the overall gradient through multiplication, requiring an additional gradient clipping for each level to ensure the overall gradient does not explode. This operation demands knowledge of the gradient upper bound of each level, which is impractical in real-world applications. In contrast, our PMVR method directly applies variance reduction to the overall gradient, eliminating the need for such gradient clipping. This technique is also mentioned in a concurrent work
(
Gao, 2023
)
;
ii) Our method employs a constant learning rate
\eta
and momentum parameter
\alpha
, which are easy to implement and fine-tune, whereas SMVR requires these parameters to decrease gradually, which is more complicated;
iii) Our PMVR is a projection-free algorithm specifically designed for constrained problems, with theoretical guarantees focused on the Frank-Wolfe gap and gradient mapping. In contrast, SMVR aims to find stationary points for unconstrained objectives.

)

Then, we investigate the complexities under the criterion of gradient mapping

defined in equation (

). To deal with the gradient mapping, our PMVR algorithm only requires minimal modifications to fit this criterion. Based on Proposition 2 of

, gradient mapping can be decomposed into two components:

\displaystyle\mathcal{G}(\mathbf{x}_{t})\leq-4\beta\min_{\mathbf{w}\in\mathcal{X}}g(\mathbf{w},\mathbf{x}_{t},\mathbf{v}_{t})+2\left\|\nabla F(\mathbf{x}_{t})-\mathbf{v}_{t}\right\|^{2},

where

\displaystyle g(\mathbf{w},\mathbf{x}_{t},\mathbf{v}_{t})=\langle\mathbf{v}_{t},\mathbf{w}-\mathbf{x}_{t}\rangle+\frac{\beta}{2}\|\mathbf{w}-\mathbf{x}_{t}\|^{2}.

Since our PMVR method has already employed variance-reduced techniques to ensure that the gradient estimation error
\mathbb{E}\left[\left\|\nabla F(\mathbf{x}_{t})-\mathbf{v}_{t}\right\|^{2}\right]
decreases over time, we can reuse this part and focus on bounding the
-\min_{\mathbf{w}\in\mathcal{X}}g(\mathbf{w},\mathbf{x}_{t},\mathbf{v}_{t})
term. To address this constrained quadratic minimization problem, we design a sub-algorithm, modified from the Frank-Wolfe method
(
Jaggi, 2013

, which runs for several loops. In each loop

\displaystyle\mathbf{s}=\arg\min_{\hat{\mathbf{s}}\in\mathcal{X}}\left\langle\mathbf{v}_{t}+\beta\left(\mathbf{w}_{n}-\mathbf{x}_{t}\right),\hat{\mathbf{s}}\right\rangle,

\displaystyle\mathbf{w}_{n+1}=\left(1-\gamma_{t}\right)\mathbf{w}_{n}+\gamma_{t}\mathbf{s}.

Note that
\mathbf{v}_{t}+\beta\left(\mathbf{w}_{n}-\mathbf{x}_{t}\right)
is the gradient of
g(\mathbf{w}_{n},\mathbf{x}_{t},\mathbf{v}_{t})
with respect to parameter
\mathbf{w}_{n}
. Overall, we only need to replace Step 9 in Algorithm
1
with a sub-algorithm presented in Algorithm

, and we can obtain the guarantees for gradient mapping below.

3.4
Results for Optimal Gap

In addition to the analyses for general non-convex functions in previous subsections, we further investigate the case for convex and strongly convex functions, defined as follows.

Definition 1
.

A function
F:\mathcal{X}\mapsto\mathbb{R}
is convex if

\displaystyle F(\mathbf{y})\geq F(\mathbf{x})+\langle\nabla F(\mathbf{x}),\mathbf{y}-\mathbf{x}\rangle,\forall\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y}\in\mathcal{X}.

Definition 2
.

A function
F:\mathcal{X}\mapsto\mathbb{R}
is
\lambda
-strongly convex if

\ \forall\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y}\in\mathcal{X}

\displaystyle F(\mathbf{y})\geq F(\mathbf{x})+\langle\nabla F(\mathbf{x}),\mathbf{y}-\mathbf{x}\rangle+\frac{\lambda}{2}\|\mathbf{y}-\mathbf{x}\|^{2}.

When the objective function is convex or strongly convex, a local optimal point becomes a global optimal point. Consequently, a natural criterion to consider is the optimal gap defined in equation (
4
).

4.1
Matrix Optimization with Low-Rank Constraints

Following the previous literature on projection-free multi-level optimization
(
Xiao et al., 2022
)
, we also conduct experiments on matrix optimization with low-rank constraints. Specifically, we consider the matrix-valued single-index model
(
Yang et al., 2017
)
with a low-rank constraint, expressed as:

\displaystyle y=\left|\langle A,B^{\star}\rangle_{F}\right|^{2}+\epsilon,\quad\text{rank}(B^{\star})\leq s,

where
A,B\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times n}
,
\epsilon\sim\mathcal{N}\left(0,\sigma^{2}\right),\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{F}
denotes the Frobenius inner product, and
s
is a positive integer smaller than both
m
and
n
. To recover a low-rank matrix
B^{\star}
given
A
and
y
, we can optimize the mean squared loss function with a nuclear norm constraint. The objective function can be formulated as:

\displaystyle\min_{B}F(B)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\left[\left(y-\left|\langle A,B\rangle_{F}\right|^{2}\right)^{2}\right]

\displaystyle\text{s.t. }\ \|B\|_{\star}\leq s.

Note that in this case, the projection operation onto the nuclear norm ball requires a full singular value decomposition (SVD), while the linear optimization used by Frank-Wolfe update only requires computing the singular vector pair corresponding to the largest singular value, which is much cheaper
(
Jaggi, 2013
)
. In line with the setup in
Xiao et al. (2022)
, we define the matrix
B^{\star}=vv^{\top}/\left\|vv^{\top}\right\|_{\star}
, and the matrix
A
is generated by
A=I+E
, with
E_{i,j}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle i.i.d.}}{{\sim}}\mathcal{N}\left(0,0.3\right)
.

In Figure
1
, we plot the value of Frank-Wolfe gap, as well as gradient mapping, against the running time of each algorithm. All the curves are averaged over 50 runs. As can be seen, our PMVR and PMVR-v2 methods demonstrate a more rapid decrease in both criteria compared to other approaches, especially for the gradient mapping criterion, demonstrating the superiority of our proposed methods.

4.2
Mean-variance Risk-averse Optimization

We first consider the problem of risk-averse portfolio optimization
(
Shapiro et al., 2021
)
, a commonly used benchmark in comparing various multi-level algorithms
(
Yang et al., 2019
;
Balasubramanian et al., 2021
;
Zhang & Xiao, 2021
;
Chen et al., 2021
)
. Suppose we have
d
assets to invest over time steps
\{1,\ldots,T\}
, and
\mathbf{r}_{t}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
represents the payoff of
d
assets at time step
t
. The goal is to maximize investment returns and minimize risk simultaneously. A suitable approach for this purpose is the mean-variance risk-averse optimization model, where risk is defined as the variance. This optimization problem is formulated as:

\displaystyle\min_{\mathbf{x}\in\mathcal{X}}F(\mathbf{x})=-\frac{1}{T}\sum_{t=1}^{T}\left\langle\mathbf{r}_{t},\mathbf{x}\right\rangle+\frac{\lambda}{T}\sum_{t=1}^{T}\left(\left\langle\mathbf{r}_{t},\mathbf{x}\right\rangle-\left\langle\bar{\mathbf{r}},\mathbf{x}\right\rangle\right)^{2},

where
\bar{\mathbf{r}}=\frac{1}{T}\sum_{t=1}^{T}\mathbf{r}_{t}
, and the decision variable
\mathbf{x}
denotes the investment quantities in
d
assets. The domain
\mathcal{X}
is a simplex, ensuring that
\left\|\mathbf{x}\right\|_{1}=1
for any
\mathbf{x}\in\mathcal{X}
. This problem can be modeled as a stochastic two-level constrained compositional optimization problem, with each layer expressed as follows:

\displaystyle f_{1}(\mathbf{x})=\left(-\frac{1}{T}\sum_{t=1}^{T}\langle\mathbf{r}_{t},\mathbf{x}\rangle,\mathbf{x}\right),

\displaystyle f_{2}(\mathbf{y}_{1},\mathbf{y}_{2})=\mathbf{y}_{1}+\frac{\lambda}{T}\sum_{t=1}^{T}\left(\left\langle\mathbf{r}_{t},\mathbf{y}_{2}\right\rangle+\mathbf{y}_{1}\right)^{2},

where
f_{1}(\cdot)
is the inner function and
f_{2}(\cdot)
is the outer function such that
F(\mathbf{x})=f_{2}(f_{1}(\mathbf{x}))
.

For experimental validation, we utilize real-world datasets Industry-10 and Industry-12 from the Kenneth R. French Data Library
2
2

2

https://mba.tuck.dartmouth.edu/pages/faculty/ken.french/
. These datasets include payoffs for 10 and 12 industrial assets over 25,105 consecutive periods. For non-projection-free methods, we implement the projection onto the simplex following a well-known efficient projection method
(
Duchi et al., 2008
)
. We report the loss value
F(\mathbf{x})
, as well as the Frank-Wolfe gap and gradient mapping criteria in Figure
2
, averaging all curves over 50 runs. We also include results concerning the number of iterations in Figure
4
in the Appendix
A
. It is observed that LiNASA+ICG, our PMVR and PMVR-v2, tend to converge more rapidly compared to other algorithms in all tasks. Specifically, PMVR demonstrates similar performance to LiNASA+ICG in the Industry 12 dataset and performs better than LiNASA+ICG in Industry 10. The loss value, Frank-Wolfe gap, and gradient mapping of our PMVR-v2 decrease most quickly in both datasets, validating the effectiveness of the proposed method.

4.3
Mean-deviation Risk-averse Optimization

Finally, we further conduct the experiment on a three-level compositional optimization problem. Here, we still consider the problem of risk-averse portfolio optimization as in the previous subsection, and the risk is quantified as the standard deviation this time. The mathematical formulation of this problem is:

\displaystyle\max_{\mathbf{x}\in\mathcal{X}}\frac{1}{T}\sum_{t=1}^{T}\left\langle\mathbf{r}_{t},\mathbf{x}\right\rangle-\lambda\sqrt{\frac{1}{T}\sum_{t=1}^{T}\left(\left\langle\mathbf{r}_{t},\mathbf{x}\right\rangle-\left\langle\mathbf{\bar{r}},\mathbf{x}\right\rangle\right)^{2}},

where
\mathbf{\bar{r}}=\frac{1}{T}\sum_{t=1}^{T}\mathbf{r}_{t}
,
\mathcal{X}
is the probability simplex, and the decision variable
\mathbf{x}
denotes the investment quantity vector in the
d
assets. This is a three-level compositional optimization problem according to the analysis by
Jiang et al. (2022b)
.

In the experiments, we also evaluated different methods on the real-world datasets Industry-10 and Industry-12. The results, including the loss value
F(\mathbf{x})
, the Frank-Wolfe gap, and the gradient mapping criteria, are reported in Figure
3

The loss value, Frank-Wolfe gap, and gradient mapping of our PMVR-v2 decreased most rapidly in both datasets, indicating the effectiveness of our proposed approach.

5
Conclusion

In this paper, we investigate projection-free algorithms for stochastic constrained multi-level optimization. Our proposed methods not only yield better results than previous approaches under the gradient mapping criterion but also provide guarantees for the Frank-Wolfe gap, an aspect previously absent in projection-free multi-level research. Additionally, we provide theoretical guarantees for convex and strongly convex objective functions, and validate the effectiveness of our proposed methods through numerical experiments.

Appendix B
Proof of Frank-Wolfe Gap (Theorem
1
and Theorem
2
)

In this section, we present the proofs for the Frank-Wolfe gap. First, to bound the estimation error of the gradient, we have the following lemma.

Lemma 1
.

The gradient estimation error can be divided into the following two parts.

\displaystyle\sum_{t=1}^{T}\mathbb{E}\left[\left\|\nabla F(\mathbf{x}_{t})-\mathbf{v}_{t}\right\|^{2}\right]\leq 2\sum_{t=1}^{T}\mathbb{E}\left[\left\|\mathbf{v}_{t}-\prod_{i=1}^{K}\nabla f_{i}(\mathbf{u}_{t}^{i-1})\right\|^{2}\right]+2KL_{F}^{2}\sum_{t=1}^{T}\sum_{i=1}^{K-1}\mathbb{E}\left[\left\|\mathbf{u}_{t}^{i}-f_{i}(\mathbf{u}_{t}^{i-1})\right\|^{2}\right].

Proof.

First, it is easy to show that:

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\left\|\mathbf{v}_{t}-\nabla F(\mathbf{x}_{t})\right\|^{2}\right]\leq

\displaystyle 2\mathbb{E}\left[\left\|\mathbf{v}_{t}-\prod_{i=1}^{K}\nabla f_{i}(\mathbf{u}_{t}^{i-1})\right\|^{2}\right]+2\mathbb{E}\left[\left\|\prod_{i=1}^{K}\nabla f_{i}(\mathbf{u}_{t}^{i-1})-\nabla F(\mathbf{x}_{t})\right\|^{2}\right].

Then, we define that
\mathbf{y}_{t}^{i}=f_{i}\circ f_{i-1}\circ\dotsc\circ f_{1}(\mathbf{w}_{t})
and
\nabla\widehat{F}_{i}(\mathbf{w}_{t})=\nabla f_{1}(\mathbf{w}_{t})\cdots\nabla f_{i}(\mathbf{u}^{i-1}_{t})
.

\displaystyle\left\|\nabla F_{1}(\mathbf{w}_{t})-\nabla\widehat{F}_{1}(\mathbf{w}_{t})\right\|

\displaystyle=0,

\displaystyle\left\|\nabla F_{2}(\mathbf{w}_{t})-\nabla\widehat{F}_{2}(\mathbf{w}_{t})\right\|

\displaystyle=\left\|\nabla f_{1}(\mathbf{w}_{t})\nabla f_{2}(\mathbf{y}^{1}_{t})-\nabla f_{1}(\mathbf{w}_{t})\nabla f_{2}(\mathbf{u}^{1}_{t})\right\|

\displaystyle\leq L_{f}L_{J}\left\|\mathbf{y}_{t}^{1}-\mathbf{u}^{1}_{t}\right\|,

\displaystyle\left\|\nabla F_{3}(\mathbf{w}_{t})-\nabla\widehat{F}_{3}(\mathbf{w}_{t})\right\|

\displaystyle=\left\|\nabla f_{1}(\mathbf{w}_{t})\nabla f_{2}(\mathbf{y}^{1}_{t})\nabla f_{3}(\mathbf{y}^{2}_{t})-\nabla f_{1}(\mathbf{w}_{t})\nabla f_{2}(\mathbf{u}^{1}_{t})\nabla f_{3}(\mathbf{u}^{2}_{t})\right\|

\displaystyle\leq L_{f}^{2}L_{J}\left(\left\|\mathbf{y}_{t}^{2}-\mathbf{u}_{t}^{2}\right\|+\left\|\mathbf{y}_{t}^{1}-\mathbf{u}^{1}_{t}\right\|\right),

\displaystyle\cdots

\displaystyle\left\|\nabla F_{K}(\mathbf{w}_{t})-\nabla\widehat{F}_{K}(\mathbf{w}_{t})\right\|

\displaystyle\leq L_{f}^{K-1}L_{J}\sum_{i=1}^{K-1}\left\|\mathbf{y}_{t}^{i}-\mathbf{u}_{t}^{i}\right\|,

Besides, we also have:

\displaystyle\left\|\mathbf{y}_{t}^{2}-\mathbf{u}_{t}^{2}\right\|

\displaystyle=\left\|f_{2}\circ f_{1}(\mathbf{w}_{t})-\mathbf{u}^{2}_{t}\right\|

\displaystyle\leq\left\|f_{2}\circ f_{1}(\mathbf{w}_{t})-f_{2}(\mathbf{u}^{1}_{t})\right\|+\left\|f_{2}(\mathbf{u}^{1}_{t})-\mathbf{u}^{2}_{t}\right\|

\displaystyle\leq L_{f}\left\|f_{1}(\mathbf{w}_{t})-\mathbf{u}^{1}_{t}\right\|+\left\|f_{2}(\mathbf{u}^{1}_{t})-\mathbf{u}^{2}_{t}\right\|,

\displaystyle\left\|\mathbf{y}_{t}^{3}-\mathbf{u}_{t}^{3}\right\|

\displaystyle=\left\|f_{3}\circ f_{2}\circ f_{1}(\mathbf{w}_{t})-\mathbf{u}^{3}_{t}\right\|

\displaystyle\leq\left\|f_{3}\circ f_{2}\circ f_{1}(\mathbf{w}_{t})-f_{3}(\mathbf{u}^{2}_{t})\right\|+\left\|f_{3}(\mathbf{u}^{2}_{t})-\mathbf{u}^{3}_{t}\right\|

\displaystyle\leq L_{f}\left\|\mathbf{y}_{t}^{2}-\mathbf{u}^{2}_{t}\right\|+\left\|f_{3}(\mathbf{u}^{2}_{t})-\mathbf{u}^{3}_{t}\right\|

\displaystyle\leq L_{f}(L_{f}\left\|f_{1}(\mathbf{w}_{t})-\mathbf{u}^{1}_{t}\right\|+\left\|f_{2}(\mathbf{u}_{t}^{1})-\mathbf{u}_{t}^{2}\right\|)+\left\|f_{3}(\mathbf{u}^{2}_{t})-\mathbf{u}^{3}_{t}\right\|

\displaystyle\cdots

\displaystyle\left\|\mathbf{y}_{t}^{i}-\mathbf{u}_{t}^{i}\right\|

\displaystyle\leq L_{f}\left\|\mathbf{y}_{t}^{i-1}-\mathbf{u}_{t}^{i-1}\right\|+\left\|f_{i}(\mathbf{u}_{t}^{i-1})-\mathbf{u}_{t}^{i}\right\|

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{j=1}^{i}L_{f}^{i-j}\left\|f_{j}(\mathbf{u}_{t}^{j-1})-\mathbf{u}_{t}^{j}\right\|

To this end, we can conclude that:

\displaystyle\left\|\nabla F(\mathbf{w}_{t})-\nabla\widehat{F}_{K}(\mathbf{w}_{t})\right\|

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{i=1}^{K-1}C_{i}\left\|f_{i}(\mathbf{u}^{i-1}_{t})-\mathbf{u}_{t}^{i}\right\|,

where
C_{i}\coloneqq L_{f}^{K-1}L_{J}(1+L_{f}+\dotsc+L_{f}^{K-i-1})
. Since
C_{i}\leq L_{F}
, we know that:

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\left\|\prod_{i=1}^{K}\nabla f_{i}(\mathbf{u}_{t}^{i-1})-\nabla F(\mathbf{x}_{t})\right\|^{2}\right]\leq K\sum_{i=1}^{K-1}A_{i}^{2}\mathbb{E}\left[\left\|f_{i}(\mathbf{u}_{t}^{i-1})-\mathbf{u}_{t}^{i}\right\|^{2}\right].

∎

Next, we bound the term
\mathbb{E}\left[\left\|\mathbf{v}_{t}-\prod_{i=1}^{K}\nabla f_{i}(\mathbf{u}_{t}^{i-1})\right\|^{2}\right]
and
\mathbb{E}\left[\left\|\mathbf{u}_{t}^{i}-f_{i}(\mathbf{u}_{t}^{i-1})\right\|^{2}\right]
separately.

Lemma 2
.

The gradient estimator
\mathbf{v}_{t}
enjoys the following guarantee:

\displaystyle\sum_{t=1}^{T}\mathbb{E}\left[\left\|\mathbf{v}_{t}-\prod_{i=1}^{K}\nabla f_{i}(\mathbf{u}_{t}^{i-1})\right\|^{2}\right]

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\mathbb{E}\left[\left\|\mathbf{v}_{1}-\prod_{i=1}^{K}\nabla f_{i}(\mathbf{u}_{1}^{i-1})\right\|^{2}\right]}{\alpha}+\frac{2K^{2}\alpha T\sigma_{J}^{2}}{B_{1}}L_{f}^{2K-2}

\displaystyle\qquad+\frac{2K}{\alpha B_{1}}\mathcal{L}_{J}^{2}L_{f}^{2K-2}\sum_{t=1}^{T}\sum_{i=1}^{K}\mathbb{E}\left[\left\|\mathbf{u}_{t}^{i-1}-\mathbf{u}_{t-1}^{i-1}\right\|^{2}\right]

Proof.

According to the definition of
\mathbf{v}_{t}
, we know that:

\displaystyle\mathbf{v}_{t}=\frac{1}{B_{1}}\sum_{j=1}^{B_{1}}\prod_{i=1}^{K}\nabla f_{i}(\mathbf{u}_{t}^{i-1};\xi_{t}^{i,j})+(1-\alpha_{t})\left(\mathbf{v}_{t-1}-\frac{1}{B_{1}}\sum_{j=1}^{B_{1}}\prod_{i=1}^{K}\nabla f_{i}(\mathbf{u}_{t-1}^{i-1};\xi_{t}^{i,j})\right).

Then we can obtain the following guarantee:

\displaystyle\left\|\mathbf{v}_{t}-\prod_{i=1}^{K}\nabla f_{i}(\mathbf{u}_{t}^{i-1})\right\|^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left\|\frac{1}{B_{1}}\sum_{j=1}^{B_{1}}\prod_{i=1}^{K}\nabla f_{i}(\mathbf{u}_{t}^{i-1};\xi_{t}^{i,j})+(1-\alpha_{t})\left(\mathbf{v}_{t-1}-\frac{1}{B_{1}}\sum_{j=1}^{B_{1}}\prod_{i=1}^{K}\nabla f_{i}(\mathbf{u}_{t-1}^{i-1};\xi_{t}^{i,j})\right)-\prod_{i=1}^{K}\nabla f_{i}(\mathbf{u}_{t}^{i-1})\right\|^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left\|(1-\alpha_{t})\left(\mathbf{v}_{t-1}-\prod_{i=1}^{K}\nabla f_{i}(\mathbf{u}_{t-1}^{i-1})\right)+\alpha_{t}\left(\frac{1}{B_{1}}\sum_{j=1}^{B_{1}}\prod_{i=1}^{K}\nabla f_{i}(\mathbf{u}_{t-1}^{i-1};\xi_{t}^{i,j})-\prod_{i=1}^{K}\nabla f_{i}(\mathbf{u}_{t-1}^{i-1})\right)\right.

\displaystyle\left.+\left(\prod_{i=1}^{K}\nabla f_{i}(\mathbf{u}_{t-1}^{i-1})-\prod_{i=1}^{K}\nabla f_{i}(\mathbf{u}_{t}^{i-1})-\frac{1}{B_{1}}\sum_{j=1}^{B_{1}}\prod_{i=1}^{K}\nabla f_{i}(\mathbf{u}_{t-1}^{i-1};\xi_{t}^{i,j})+\frac{1}{B_{1}}\sum_{j=1}^{B_{1}}\prod_{i=1}^{K}\nabla f_{i}(\mathbf{u}_{t}^{i-1};\xi_{t}^{i,j})\right)\right\|^{2}

Since the expectation of last two terms equals zero, such that

\displaystyle E\left[\frac{1}{B_{1}}\sum_{j=1}^{B_{1}}\prod_{i=1}^{K}\nabla f_{i}(\mathbf{u}_{t-1}^{i-1};\xi_{t}^{i,j})-\prod_{i=1}^{K}\nabla f_{i}(\mathbf{u}_{t-1}^{i-1})\right]=0,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\prod_{i=1}^{K}\nabla f_{i}(\mathbf{u}_{t-1}^{i-1})-\prod_{i=1}^{K}\nabla f_{i}(\mathbf{u}_{t}^{i-1})-\frac{1}{B_{1}}\sum_{j=1}^{B_{1}}\prod_{i=1}^{K}\nabla f_{i}(\mathbf{u}_{t-1}^{i-1};\xi_{t}^{i,j})+\frac{1}{B_{1}}\sum_{j=1}^{B_{1}}\prod_{i=1}^{K}\nabla f_{i}(\mathbf{u}_{t}^{i-1};\xi_{t}^{i,j})\right]=0,

we would have that:

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\left\|\mathbf{v}_{t}-\prod_{i=1}^{K}\nabla f_{i}(\mathbf{u}_{t}^{i-1})\right\|^{2}\right]

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle(1-\alpha)^{2}\mathbb{E}\left[\left\|\mathbf{v}_{t-1}-\prod_{i=1}^{K}\nabla f_{i}(\mathbf{u}_{t-1}^{i-1})\right\|^{2}\right]+\mathbb{E}\left[\left\|\alpha\left(\frac{1}{B_{1}}\sum_{j=1}^{B_{1}}\prod_{i=1}^{K}\nabla f_{i}(\mathbf{u}_{t-1}^{i-1};\xi_{t}^{i,j})-\prod_{i=1}^{K}\nabla f_{i}(\mathbf{u}_{t-1}^{i-1})\right)\right.\right.

\displaystyle\left.\left.+\left(\frac{1}{B_{1}}\sum_{j=1}^{B_{1}}\prod_{i=1}^{K}\nabla f_{i}(\mathbf{u}_{t-1}^{i-1};\xi_{t}^{i,j})-\frac{1}{B_{1}}\sum_{j=1}^{B_{1}}\prod_{i=1}^{K}\nabla f_{i}(\mathbf{u}_{t}^{i-1};\xi_{t}^{i,j})-\prod_{i=1}^{K}\nabla f_{i}(\mathbf{u}_{t-1}^{i-1})+\prod_{i=1}^{K}\nabla f_{i}(\mathbf{u}_{t}^{i-1})\right)\right\|^{2}\right]

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle(1-\alpha_{t})\mathbb{E}\left[\left\|\mathbf{v}_{t-1}-\prod_{i=1}^{K}\nabla f_{i}(\mathbf{u}_{t-1}^{i-1})\right\|^{2}\right]+2\alpha_{t}^{2}\mathbb{E}\left[\left\|\frac{1}{B_{1}}\sum_{j=1}^{B_{1}}\prod_{i=1}^{K}\nabla f_{i}(\mathbf{u}_{t-1}^{i-1};\xi_{t}^{i,j})-\prod_{i=1}^{K}\nabla f_{i}(\mathbf{u}_{t-1}^{i-1})\right\|^{2}\right]

\displaystyle+2\mathbb{E}\left[\left\|\frac{1}{B_{1}}\sum_{j=1}^{B_{1}}\prod_{i=1}^{K}\nabla f_{i}(\mathbf{u}_{t-1}^{i-1};\xi_{t}^{i,j})-\frac{1}{B_{1}}\sum_{j=1}^{B_{1}}\prod_{i=1}^{K}\nabla f_{i}(\mathbf{u}_{t}^{i-1};\xi_{t}^{i,j})-\prod_{i=1}^{K}\nabla f_{i}(\mathbf{u}_{t-1}^{i-1})+\prod_{i=1}^{K}\nabla f_{i}(\mathbf{u}_{t}^{i-1})\right\|^{2}\right]

Then, we would bound the last two terms, respectively. First, we have that:

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\left\|\frac{1}{B_{1}}\sum_{j=1}^{B_{1}}\prod_{i=1}^{K}\nabla f_{i}(\mathbf{u}_{t-1}^{i-1};\xi_{t}^{i,j})-\prod_{i=1}^{K}\nabla f_{i}(\mathbf{u}_{t-1}^{i-1})\right\|^{2}\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{B_{1}^{2}}\sum_{j=1}^{B_{1}}\mathbb{E}\left[\left\|\prod_{i=1}^{K}\nabla f_{i}(\mathbf{u}_{t-1}^{i-1};\xi_{t}^{i,j})-\prod_{i=1}^{K}\nabla f_{i}(\mathbf{u}_{t-1}^{i-1})\right\|^{2}\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{B_{1}}\mathbb{E}\left[\left\|\prod_{i=1}^{K}\nabla f_{i}(\mathbf{u}_{t-1}^{i-1};\xi_{t}^{i,j})-\prod_{i=1}^{K}\nabla f_{i}(\mathbf{u}_{t-1}^{i-1})\right\|^{2}\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{B_{1}}\mathbb{E}\left[\left\|\prod_{i=1}^{K}\nabla f_{i}(\mathbf{u}_{t-1}^{i-1};\xi_{t}^{i,j})-\nabla f_{1}(\mathbf{u}_{t-1}^{0})\prod_{i=2}^{K}\nabla f_{i}(\mathbf{u}_{t-1}^{i-1};\xi_{t}^{i,j})\right.\right.

\displaystyle\qquad\quad\left.\left.+\nabla f_{1}(\mathbf{u}_{t-1}^{0})\prod_{i=2}^{K}\nabla f_{i}(\mathbf{u}_{t-1}^{i-1};\xi_{t}^{i,j})-\nabla f_{1}(\mathbf{u}_{t-1}^{0})\nabla f_{2}(\mathbf{u}_{t-1}^{1})\prod_{i=3}^{K}\nabla f_{i}(\mathbf{u}_{t-1}^{i-1};\xi_{t}^{i,j})\right.\right.

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\cdots

\displaystyle\qquad\quad\left.\left.+\left(\prod_{i=1}^{K-1}\nabla f_{i}(\mathbf{u}_{t-1}^{i-1})\right)\nabla f_{K}(\mathbf{u}_{t-1}^{K-1};\xi_{t}^{i,j})-\prod_{i=1}^{K}\nabla f_{i}(\mathbf{u}_{t-1}^{i-1})\right\|^{2}\right]

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{K}{B_{1}}\mathbb{E}\left[\left\|\prod_{i=1}^{K}\nabla f_{i}(\mathbf{u}_{t-1}^{i-1};\xi_{t}^{i,j})-\nabla f_{1}(\mathbf{u}_{t-1}^{0})\prod_{i=2}^{K}\nabla f_{i}(\mathbf{u}_{t-1}^{i-1};\xi_{t}^{i,j})\right\|^{2}\right]

\displaystyle+\frac{K}{B_{1}}\mathbb{E}\left[\left\|\nabla f_{1}(\mathbf{u}_{t-1}^{0})\prod_{i=2}^{K}\nabla f_{i}(\mathbf{u}_{t-1}^{i-1};\xi_{t}^{i,j})-\nabla f_{1}(\mathbf{u}_{t-1}^{0})\nabla f_{2}(\mathbf{u}_{t-1}^{1})\prod_{i=3}^{K}\nabla f_{i}(\mathbf{u}_{t-1}^{i-1};\xi_{t}^{i,j})\right\|^{2}\right]

\displaystyle+...

\displaystyle+\frac{K}{B_{1}}\mathbb{E}\left[\left\|\left(\prod_{i=1}^{K-1}\nabla f_{i}(\mathbf{u}_{t-1}^{i-1})\right)\nabla f_{K}(\mathbf{u}_{t-1}^{K-1};\xi_{t}^{i,j})-\prod_{i=1}^{K}\nabla f_{i}(\mathbf{u}_{t-1}^{i-1})\right\|^{2}\right]

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{K}{B_{1}}\sigma_{J}^{2}(L_{f}^{2K-2}+L_{f}^{2K-2}+...+L_{f}^{2K-2})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{K^{2}}{B_{1}}\sigma_{J}^{2}L_{f}^{2K-2}

When dealing with the second term, note that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\left\|\frac{1}{B_{1}}\sum_{j=1}^{B_{1}}\left(\prod_{i=1}^{K}\nabla f_{i}(\mathbf{u}_{t-1}^{i-1};\xi_{t}^{i,j})-\prod_{i=1}^{K}\nabla f_{i}(\mathbf{u}_{t}^{i-1};\xi_{t}^{i,j})-\prod_{i=1}^{K}\nabla f_{i}(\mathbf{u}_{t-1}^{i-1})+\prod_{i=1}^{K}\nabla f_{i}(\mathbf{u}_{t}^{i-1})\right)\right\|\right]=0

So, we can have that:

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\left\|\frac{1}{B_{1}}\sum_{j=1}^{B_{1}}\prod_{i=1}^{K}\nabla f_{i}(\mathbf{u}_{t-1}^{i-1};\xi_{t}^{i,j})-\frac{1}{B_{1}}\sum_{j=1}^{B_{1}}\prod_{i=1}^{K}\nabla f_{i}(\mathbf{u}_{t}^{i-1};\xi_{t}^{i,j})-\prod_{i=1}^{K}\nabla f_{i}(\mathbf{u}_{t-1}^{i-1})+\prod_{i=1}^{K}\nabla f_{i}(\mathbf{u}_{t}^{i-1})\right\|^{2}\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{B_{1}^{2}}\mathbb{E}\left[\left\|\sum_{j=1}^{B_{1}}\left(\prod_{i=1}^{K}\nabla f_{i}(\mathbf{u}_{t-1}^{i-1};\xi_{t}^{i,j})-\prod_{i=1}^{K}\nabla f_{i}(\mathbf{u}_{t}^{i-1};\xi_{t}^{i,j})-\prod_{i=1}^{K}\nabla f_{i}(\mathbf{u}_{t-1}^{i-1})+\prod_{i=1}^{K}\nabla f_{i}(\mathbf{u}_{t}^{i-1})\right)\right\|^{2}\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{B_{1}^{2}}\sum_{j=1}^{B_{1}}\mathbb{E}\left[\left\|\prod_{i=1}^{K}\nabla f_{i}(\mathbf{u}_{t-1}^{i-1};\xi_{t}^{i,j})-\prod_{i=1}^{K}\nabla f_{i}(\mathbf{u}_{t}^{i-1};\xi_{t}^{i,j})-\prod_{i=1}^{K}\nabla f_{i}(\mathbf{u}_{t-1}^{i-1})+\prod_{i=1}^{K}\nabla f_{i}(\mathbf{u}_{t}^{i-1})\right\|^{2}\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{B_{1}}\mathbb{E}\left[\left\|\prod_{i=1}^{K}\nabla f_{i}(\mathbf{u}_{t-1}^{i-1};\xi_{t}^{i,j})-\prod_{i=1}^{K}\nabla f_{i}(\mathbf{u}_{t}^{i-1};\xi_{t}^{i,j})-\prod_{i=1}^{K}\nabla f_{i}(\mathbf{u}_{t-1}^{i-1})+\prod_{i=1}^{K}\nabla f_{i}(\mathbf{u}_{t}^{i-1})\right\|^{2}\right]

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{B_{1}}\mathbb{E}\left[\left\|\prod_{i=1}^{K}\nabla f_{i}(\mathbf{u}_{t-1}^{i-1};\xi_{t}^{i,j})-\prod_{i=1}^{K}\nabla f_{i}(\mathbf{u}_{t}^{i-1};\xi_{t}^{i,j})\right\|^{2}\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{B_{1}}\mathbb{E}\left[\left\|\prod_{i=1}^{K}\nabla f_{i}(\mathbf{u}_{t-1}^{i-1};\xi_{t}^{i,j})-\nabla f_{1}(\mathbf{u}_{t}^{0};\xi_{t}^{1,j})\prod_{i=2}^{K}\nabla f_{i}(\mathbf{u}_{t-1}^{i-1};\xi_{t}^{i,j})\right.\right.

\displaystyle\left.\left.\quad\quad+\nabla f_{1}(\mathbf{u}_{t}^{0};\xi_{t}^{1,j})\prod_{i=2}^{K}\nabla f_{i}(\mathbf{u}_{t-1}^{i-1};\xi_{t}^{i,j})-\nabla f_{1}(\mathbf{u}_{t}^{0};\xi_{t}^{1,j})\nabla f_{2}(\mathbf{u}_{t}^{1};\xi_{t}^{2,j})\prod_{i=3}^{K}\nabla f_{i}(\mathbf{u}_{t-1}^{i-1};\xi_{t}^{i,j})\right.\right.

\displaystyle\left.\left.\quad\quad\cdots\right.\right.

\displaystyle\left.\left.\quad\quad+\left(\prod_{i=1}^{K-1}\nabla f_{i}(\mathbf{u}_{t}^{i-1};\xi_{t}^{i,j})\right)\nabla f_{K}(\mathbf{u}_{t-1}^{K-1};\xi_{t}^{K,j})-\prod_{i=1}^{K}\nabla f_{i}(\mathbf{u}_{t}^{i-1};\xi_{t}^{i,j})\right\|^{2}\right]

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{K}{B_{1}}\mathbb{E}\left[\left\|\prod_{i=1}^{K}\nabla f_{i}(\mathbf{u}_{t-1}^{i-1};\xi_{t}^{i,j})-\nabla f_{1}(\mathbf{u}_{t}^{0};\xi_{t}^{1,j})\prod_{i=2}^{K}\nabla f_{i}(\mathbf{u}_{t-1}^{i-1};\xi_{t}^{i,j})\right\|^{2}\right]

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{K}{B_{1}}\mathbb{E}\left[\left\|\nabla f_{1}(\mathbf{u}_{t}^{0};\xi_{t}^{1,j})\prod_{i=2}^{K}\nabla f_{i}(\mathbf{u}_{t-1}^{i-1};\xi_{t}^{i,j})-\nabla f_{1}(\mathbf{u}_{t}^{0};\xi_{t}^{1,j})\nabla f_{2}(\mathbf{u}_{t}^{1};\xi_{t}^{2,j})\prod_{i=3}^{K}\nabla f_{i}(\mathbf{u}_{t-1}^{i-1};\xi_{t}^{i,j})\right\|^{2}\right]

\displaystyle\quad\cdots

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{K}{B_{1}}\mathbb{E}\left[\left\|\left(\prod_{i=1}^{K-1}\nabla f_{i}(\mathbf{u}_{t}^{i-1};\xi_{t}^{i,j})\right)\nabla f_{K}(\mathbf{u}_{t-1}^{K-1};\xi_{t}^{i,j})-\prod_{i=1}^{K}\nabla f_{i}(\mathbf{u}_{t}^{i-1};\xi_{t}^{i,j})\right\|^{2}\right]

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{K}{B_{1}}\mathcal{L}_{J}^{2}L_{f}^{2K-2}\sum_{i=1}^{K}\mathbb{E}\left[\left\|\mathbf{u}_{t}^{i-1}-\mathbf{u}_{t-1}^{i-1}\right\|^{2}\right]

To this end, we can conclude that:

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\left\|\mathbf{v}_{t}-\prod_{i=1}^{K}\nabla f_{i}(\mathbf{u}_{t}^{i-1})\right\|^{2}\right]

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle(1-\alpha_{t})\mathbb{E}\left[\left\|\mathbf{v}_{t-1}-\prod_{i=1}^{K}\nabla f_{i}(\mathbf{u}_{t-1}^{i-1})\right\|^{2}\right]+\frac{2K^{2}\alpha_{t}^{2}\sigma_{J}^{2}}{B_{1}}L_{f}^{2K-2}+\frac{2K}{B_{1}}\mathcal{L}_{J}^{2}L_{f}^{2K-2}\sum_{i=1}^{K}\mathbb{E}\left[\left\|\mathbf{u}_{t}^{i-1}-\mathbf{u}_{t-1}^{i-1}\right\|^{2}\right],

which implies that

\displaystyle\sum_{t=1}^{T}\mathbb{E}\left[\left\|\mathbf{v}_{t}-\prod_{i=1}^{K}\nabla f_{i}(\mathbf{u}_{t}^{i-1})\right\|^{2}\right]\leq\sum_{t=2}^{T}\left(\frac{1}{\alpha_{t+1}}-\frac{1}{\alpha_{t}}\right)\mathbb{E}\left[\left\|\mathbf{v}_{1}-\prod_{i=1}^{K}\nabla f_{i}(\mathbf{u}_{1}^{i-1})\right\|^{2}\right]

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{\alpha_{2}}\mathbb{E}\left[\left\|\mathbf{v}_{1}-\prod_{i=1}^{K}\nabla f_{i}(\mathbf{u}_{1}^{i-1})\right\|^{2}\right]+\frac{2\sigma_{J}^{2}K^{2}L_{f}^{2K-2}}{B_{1}}\sum_{t=1}^{T}\alpha_{t+1}+\frac{2K}{B_{1}}\mathcal{L}_{J}^{2}L_{f}^{2K-2}\sum_{t=1}^{T}\frac{1}{\alpha_{t+1}}\sum_{i=1}^{K}\mathbb{E}\left[\left\|\mathbf{u}_{t}^{i-1}-\mathbf{u}_{t-1}^{i-1}\right\|^{2}\right]

Note that
1/\alpha_{t+1}-1/\alpha_{t}=t^{1/2}-(t-1)^{1/2}\leq 1/2
and for
t\geq 2
,
\sum_{t=1}^{T}t^{-2/3}\leqslant 3T^{1/3}+1
.
As a result, we have that

\displaystyle\sum_{t=1}^{T}\mathbb{E}\left[\left\|\mathbf{v}_{t}-\prod_{i=1}^{K}\nabla f_{i}(\mathbf{u}_{t}^{i-1})\right\|^{2}\right]\leq

\displaystyle 4\mathbb{E}\left[\left\|\mathbf{v}_{1}-\prod_{i=1}^{K}\nabla f_{i}(\mathbf{u}_{1}^{i-1})\right\|^{2}\right]+\frac{16\sigma_{J}^{2}K^{2}L_{f}^{2K-2}}{B_{1}}T^{1/3}+\frac{4K}{B_{1}}\mathcal{L}_{J}^{2}L_{f}^{2K-2}\sum_{t=1}^{T}\frac{1}{\alpha_{t+1}}\sum_{i=1}^{K}\mathbb{E}\left[\left\|\mathbf{u}_{t}^{i-1}-\mathbf{u}_{t-1}^{i-1}\right\|^{2}\right]

∎

Lemma 3
.

The inner function estimator
\mathbf{u}_{t}
ensures that:

\displaystyle\sum_{t=1}^{T}\sum_{i=1}^{K}\mathbb{E}\left[\left\|\mathbf{u}_{t}^{i}-f_{i}(\mathbf{u}_{t}^{i-1})\right\|^{2}\right]\leq

\displaystyle\frac{\sum_{i=1}^{K}\mathbb{E}\left[\left\|\mathbf{u}_{1}^{i}-f_{i}(\mathbf{u}_{1}^{i-1})\right\|^{2}\right]}{\alpha}+\frac{2\alpha KT\sigma^{2}}{B_{1}}+\frac{2\mathcal{L}_{f}^{2}}{\alpha B_{1}}\sum_{t=1}^{T}\sum_{i=1}^{K}\mathbb{E}\left[\left\|\mathbf{u}_{t}^{i-1}-\mathbf{u}_{t-1}^{i-1}\right\|^{2}\right]

Proof.

Since
\mathbf{u}_{t}^{i}=\frac{1}{B_{1}}\sum_{j=1}^{B_{1}}f_{i}(\mathbf{u}_{t}^{i-1};\xi_{t}^{i,j})+(1-\alpha)(\mathbf{u}_{t-1}^{i}-\frac{1}{B_{1}}\sum_{j=1}^{B_{1}}f_{i}(\mathbf{u}_{t-1}^{i-1};\xi_{t}^{i,j}))
, we have:

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\left\|f_{i}(\mathbf{u}_{t}^{i-1})-\mathbf{u}_{t}^{i}\right\|^{2}\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\left\|(1-\alpha)\left(\mathbf{u}_{t-1}^{i}-f_{i}(\mathbf{u}_{t-1}^{i-1})\right)+\frac{\alpha}{B_{1}}\sum_{j=1}^{B_{1}}\left(f_{i}(\mathbf{u}_{t-1}^{i-1};\xi_{t}^{i,j})-f_{i}(\mathbf{u}_{t-1}^{i-1})\right)\right.\right.

\displaystyle\quad\quad\quad\left.\left.+f_{i}(\mathbf{u}_{t-1}^{i-1})-f_{i}(\mathbf{u}_{t}^{i-1})-\frac{1}{B_{1}}\sum_{j=1}^{B_{1}}\left(f_{i}(\mathbf{u}_{t-1}^{i-1};\xi_{t}^{i,j})-f_{i}(\mathbf{u}_{t}^{i-1};\xi_{t}^{i,j})\right)\right\|^{2}\right]

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle(1-\alpha)^{2}\mathbb{E}\left\|\mathbf{u}_{t-1}^{i}-f_{i}(\mathbf{u}_{t-1}^{i-1})\right\|^{2}+\frac{2\alpha^{2}}{B_{1}}\mathbb{E}\left[\left\|f_{i}(\mathbf{u}_{t-1}^{i-1};\xi_{t}^{i,j})-f_{i}(\mathbf{u}_{t-1}^{i-1})\right\|^{2}\right]

\displaystyle\qquad+2\mathbb{E}\left[\left\|\frac{1}{B_{1}}\sum_{j=1}^{B_{1}}\left(f_{i}(\mathbf{u}_{t-1}^{i-1};\xi_{t}^{i,j})-f_{i}(\mathbf{u}_{t}^{i-1};\xi_{t}^{i,j})-f_{i}(\mathbf{u}_{t-1}^{i-1})+f_{i}(\mathbf{u}_{t}^{i-1})\right)\right\|^{2}\right]

where the last inequality is due to:
\mathbb{E}\left[f_{i}(\mathbf{u}_{t-1}^{i-1})-f_{i}(\mathbf{u}_{t}^{i-1})-\frac{1}{B_{1}}\sum_{j=1}^{B_{1}}\left(f_{i}(\mathbf{u}_{t-1}^{i-1};\xi_{t}^{i,j})-f_{i}(\mathbf{u}_{t}^{i-1};\xi_{t}^{i,j})\right)\right]=0
, as well as
\mathbb{E}\left[\frac{\alpha}{B_{1}}\sum_{j=1}^{B_{1}}\left(f_{i}(\mathbf{u}_{t-1}^{i-1};\xi_{t}^{i,j})-f_{i}(\mathbf{u}_{t-1}^{i-1})\right)\right]=0
.

Also, since we know the fact that
\mathbb{E}\left[f_{i}(\mathbf{u}_{t-1}^{i-1})-f_{i}(\mathbf{u}_{t}^{i-1})-f_{i}(\mathbf{u}_{t-1}^{i-1};\xi_{t}^{i,j})-f_{i}(\mathbf{u}_{t}^{i-1};\xi_{t}^{i,j})\right]=0
, we have:

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\left\|\frac{1}{B_{1}}\sum_{j=1}^{B_{1}}\left(f_{i}(\mathbf{u}_{t-1}^{i-1};\xi_{t}^{i,j})-f_{i}(\mathbf{u}_{t}^{i-1};\xi_{t}^{i,j})-f_{i}(\mathbf{u}_{t-1}^{i-1})+f_{i}(\mathbf{u}_{t}^{i-1})\right)\right\|^{2}\right]

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{B_{1}^{2}}\sum_{j=1}^{B_{1}}\mathbb{E}\left[\left\|\left(f_{i}(\mathbf{u}_{t-1}^{i-1};\xi_{t}^{i,j})-f_{i}(\mathbf{u}_{t-1}^{i-1})+f_{i}(\mathbf{u}_{t}^{i-1})-f_{i}(\mathbf{u}_{t}^{i-1};\xi_{t}^{i,j})\right)\right\|^{2}\right]

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{B_{1}^{2}}\sum_{j=1}^{B_{1}}\mathbb{E}\left[\left\|\left(f_{i}(\mathbf{u}_{t-1}^{i-1};\xi_{t}^{i,j})-f_{i}(\mathbf{u}_{t}^{i-1};\xi_{t}^{i,j})\right)\right\|^{2}\right]

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{B_{1}}\mathbb{E}\left[\left\|f_{i}(\mathbf{u}_{t-1}^{i-1};\xi_{t}^{i,j})-f_{i}(\mathbf{u}_{t}^{i-1};\xi_{t}^{i,j})\right\|^{2}\right]

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{B_{1}}\mathcal{L}_{f}^{2}\left\|\mathbf{u}_{t-1}^{i-1}-\mathbf{u}_{t}^{i-1}\right\|^{2}.

As a result, we can conclude that:

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\left\|f_{i}(\mathbf{u}_{t}^{i-1})-\mathbf{u}_{t}^{i}\right\|^{2}\right]\leq

\displaystyle(1-\alpha)\mathbb{E}\left\|\mathbf{u}_{t-1}^{i}-f_{i}(\mathbf{u}_{t-1}^{i-1})\right\|^{2}+\frac{2\alpha^{2}\sigma^{2}}{B_{1}}+\frac{2}{B_{1}}\mathcal{L}_{f}^{2}\left\|\mathbf{u}_{t-1}^{i-1}-\mathbf{u}_{t}^{i-1}\right\|^{2}

This leads to the fact that:

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{K}\mathbb{E}\left[\left\|f_{i}(\mathbf{u}_{t}^{i-1})-\mathbf{u}_{t}^{i}\right\|^{2}\right]\leq

\displaystyle(1-\alpha)\sum_{i=1}^{K}\mathbb{E}\left\|\mathbf{u}_{t-1}^{i}-f_{i}(\mathbf{u}_{t-1}^{i-1})\right\|^{2}+\frac{2\alpha^{2}\sigma^{2}K}{B_{1}}+\frac{2}{B_{1}}\mathcal{L}_{f}^{2}\sum_{i=1}^{K}\left\|\mathbf{u}_{t-1}^{i-1}-\mathbf{u}_{t}^{i-1}\right\|^{2}

By summing up and rearranging, we can get:

\displaystyle\sum_{t=1}^{T}\sum_{i=1}^{K}\mathbb{E}\left[\left\|\mathbf{u}_{t}^{i}-f_{i}(\mathbf{u}_{t}^{i-1})\right\|^{2}\right]\leq

\displaystyle\frac{\sum_{i=1}^{K}\mathbb{E}\left[\left\|\mathbf{u}_{1}^{i}-f_{i}(\mathbf{u}_{1}^{i-1})\right\|^{2}\right]}{\alpha}+\frac{2\alpha KT\sigma^{2}}{B_{1}}+\frac{2\mathcal{L}_{f}^{2}}{\alpha B_{1}}\sum_{t=1}^{T}\sum_{i=1}^{K}\mathbb{E}\left[\left\|\mathbf{u}_{t}^{i-1}-\mathbf{u}_{t-1}^{i-1}\right\|^{2}\right],

which finishes the proof.
∎

Then, we try to bound the term
\sum_{i=1}^{K}\mathbb{E}\left[\left\|\mathbf{u}_{t+1}^{i-1}-\mathbf{u}_{t}^{i-1}\right\|^{2}\right]
.

Lemma 4
.

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{K}\mathbb{E}\left[\left\|\mathbf{u}_{t+1}^{i-1}-\mathbf{u}_{t}^{i-1}\right\|^{2}\right]\leq

\displaystyle\left(\sum_{i=1}^{K}\left(2\mathcal{L}_{f}^{2}\right)^{i-1}\right)\left(\mathbb{E}\left[\eta^{2}\left\|\mathbf{z}_{t}-\mathbf{x}_{t}\right\|^{2}\right]+\frac{2\alpha^{2}\sigma^{2}K}{B_{1}}+2\alpha^{2}K\sum_{i=1}^{K}\mathbb{E}\left[\left\|\mathbf{u}_{t}^{i}-f_{i}(\mathbf{u}_{t}^{i-1})\right\|^{2}\right]\right)

Proof.

We discuss the following two cases, separately.

1.

For the first level, i.e.,
i=1
, we have:

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\left\|\mathbf{u}_{t+1}^{i-1}-\mathbf{u}_{t}^{i-1}\right\|^{2}\right]=\mathbb{E}\left[\left\|\mathbf{x}_{t+1}-\mathbf{x}_{t}\right\|^{2}\right]=\mathbb{E}\left[\eta^{2}\left\|\mathbf{z}_{t}-\mathbf{x}_{t}\right\|^{2}\right].

2.

For other levels, i.e.,
2\leq i\leq K
, we have:

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\left\|\mathbf{u}_{t+1}^{i-1}-\mathbf{u}_{t}^{i-1}\right\|^{2}\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\left\|\alpha\left(f_{i-1}(\mathbf{u}_{t}^{i-2})-\mathbf{u}_{t}^{i-1}\right)+\frac{1}{B_{1}}\sum_{j=1}^{B_{1}}(f_{i-1}(\mathbf{u}_{t+1}^{i-2};\xi_{t+1}^{i-1})-f_{i-1}(\mathbf{u}_{t}^{i-2};\xi_{t+1}^{i-1}))\right.\right.

\displaystyle\quad\left.\left.+\alpha\left(\frac{1}{B_{1}}\sum_{j=1}^{B_{1}}f_{i-1}(\mathbf{u}_{t}^{i-2};\xi_{t+1}^{i-1})-f_{i-1}(\mathbf{u}_{t}^{i-2})\right)\right\|^{2}\right]

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 2\mathbb{E}\left[\left\|\alpha\left(f_{i-1}(\mathbf{u}_{t}^{i-2})-\mathbf{u}_{t}^{i-1}\right)+\alpha\left(\frac{1}{B_{1}}\sum_{j=1}^{B_{1}}f_{i-1}(\mathbf{u}_{t}^{i-2};\xi_{t+1}^{i-1})-f_{i-1}(\mathbf{u}_{t}^{i-2})\right)\right\|^{2}\right]

\displaystyle\qquad+2\mathcal{L}_{f}^{2}\mathbb{E}\left[\left\|\mathbf{u}_{t+1}^{i-2}-\mathbf{u}_{t}^{i-2}\right\|^{2}\right]

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 2\alpha^{2}\left\|f_{i-1}(\mathbf{u}_{t}^{i-2})-\mathbf{u}_{t}^{i-1}\right\|^{2}+\frac{2\alpha^{2}\sigma^{2}}{B_{1}}+2\mathcal{L}_{f}^{2}\mathbb{E}\left[\left\|\mathbf{u}_{t+1}^{i-2}-\mathbf{u}_{t}^{i-2}\right\|^{2}\right].

Denote
\Upsilon_{t}^{i}=\mathbb{E}\left[\left\|\mathbf{u}_{t}^{i}-f_{i}(\mathbf{u}_{t}^{i-1})\right\|^{2}\right]
and
Q^{i}=\mathbb{E}\left[\left\|\mathbf{u}_{t+1}^{i-1}-\mathbf{u}_{t}^{i-1}\right\|^{2}\right]
, we have
Q^{i}\leq 2\mathcal{L}_{f}^{2}Q^{i-1}+2\alpha^{2}\Upsilon_{t}^{i-1}+\frac{2\alpha^{2}\sigma^{2}}{B_{1}}
for
i\geq 2
. Then we can get:

\displaystyle Q^{1}

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{E}\left[\eta^{2}\left\|\mathbf{z}_{t}-\mathbf{x}_{t}\right\|^{2}\right]

\displaystyle Q^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\left(2\mathcal{L}_{f}^{2}\right)\mathbb{E}\left[\eta^{2}\left\|\mathbf{z}_{t}-\mathbf{x}_{t}\right\|^{2}\right]+\frac{2\alpha^{2}\sigma^{2}}{B_{1}}+2\alpha^{2}\Upsilon_{t}^{1}

\displaystyle Q^{3}

\displaystyle\leq\left(2\mathcal{L}_{f}^{2}\right)^{2}\mathbb{E}\left[\eta^{2}\left\|\mathbf{z}_{t}-\mathbf{x}_{t}\right\|^{2}\right]+\frac{2\alpha^{2}\sigma^{2}\left(1+2\mathcal{L}_{f}^{2}\right)}{B_{1}}+2\alpha^{2}\left(2\mathcal{L}_{f}^{2}\Upsilon_{t}^{1}+\Upsilon_{t}^{2}\right)

\displaystyle\cdots

\displaystyle Q^{i}

\displaystyle\leq\left(2\mathcal{L}_{f}^{2}\right)^{i-1}\mathbb{E}\left[\eta^{2}\left\|\mathbf{z}_{t}-\mathbf{x}_{t}\right\|^{2}\right]+\frac{2\alpha^{2}\sigma^{2}}{B_{1}}\sum_{j=1}^{i-1}\left(2\mathcal{L}_{f}^{2}\right)^{j-1}+2\alpha^{2}\sum_{j=1}^{i-1}\left(2\mathcal{L}_{f}^{2}\right)^{i-1-j}\Upsilon_{t}^{j}

\displaystyle\leq\left(2\mathcal{L}_{f}^{2}\right)^{i-1}\mathbb{E}\left[\eta^{2}\left\|\mathbf{z}_{t}-\mathbf{x}_{t}\right\|^{2}\right]+\frac{2\alpha^{2}\sigma^{2}}{B_{1}}\sum_{j=1}^{K}\left(2\mathcal{L}_{f}^{2}\right)^{j-1}+2\alpha^{2}\sum_{j=1}^{K}\sum_{l=1}^{K}\left(2L_{f}^{2}\right)^{K-l}\Upsilon_{t}^{j}.

When summing up, we have:

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{K}Q^{i}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{i=1}^{K}\left(2\mathcal{L}_{f}^{2}\right)^{i-1}\mathbb{E}\left[\eta^{2}\left\|\mathbf{z}_{t}-\mathbf{x}_{t}\right\|^{2}\right]+\frac{2\alpha^{2}\sigma^{2}K}{B_{1}}\sum_{i=1}^{K}\left(2\mathcal{L}_{f}^{2}\right)^{i-1}+2\alpha^{2}K\sum_{j=1}^{K}\sum_{l=1}^{K}\left(2L_{f}^{2}\right)^{K-l}\Upsilon_{t}^{j}

\displaystyle\leq\left(\sum_{i=1}^{K}\left(2\mathcal{L}_{f}^{2}\right)^{i-1}\right)\left(\mathbb{E}\left[\eta^{2}\left\|\mathbf{z}_{t}-\mathbf{x}_{t}\right\|^{2}\right]+\frac{2\alpha^{2}\sigma^{2}K}{B_{1}}+2\alpha^{2}K\sum_{i=1}^{K}\Upsilon_{t}^{i}\right)

So we have :

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{K}\mathbb{E}\left[\left\|\mathbf{u}_{t+1}^{i-1}-\mathbf{u}_{t}^{i-1}\right\|^{2}\right]\leq

\displaystyle\left(\sum_{i=1}^{K}\left(2\mathcal{L}_{f}^{2}\right)^{i-1}\right)\left(\mathbb{E}\left[\eta^{2}\left\|\mathbf{z}_{t}-\mathbf{x}_{t}\right\|^{2}\right]+\frac{2\alpha^{2}\sigma^{2}K}{B_{1}}+2\alpha^{2}K\sum_{i=1}^{K}\mathbb{E}\left[\left\|\mathbf{u}_{t}^{i}-f_{i}(\mathbf{u}_{t}^{i-1})\right\|^{2}\right]\right)

∎

Lemma 5
.

Denote that the constants as
L_{1}=\left(2K\mathcal{L}_{J}^{2}L_{f}^{2K-2}+4\mathcal{L}_{f}^{2}\right)\left(\sum_{i=1}^{K}\left(2\mathcal{L}_{f}^{2}\right)^{i-1}\right)
,
L_{2}=\max\{2,2KL_{F}^{2}\}
,
L_{3}=2L_{2}(2K\sigma_{J}^{2}L_{f}^{2K-2}+4K\sigma^{2}+2L_{1}\sigma^{2}K+L_{1}D^{2}+L_{1})
, we can ensure that:

\displaystyle\sum_{t=1}^{T}\mathbb{E}\left[\left\|\nabla F(\mathbf{x}_{t})-\mathbf{v}_{t}\right\|^{2}\right]\leq\frac{L_{3}}{\alpha B_{0}}+\frac{\alpha L_{3}T}{B_{1}}+\frac{L_{3}\eta^{2}T}{\alpha B_{1}}.

Proof.

Based on Lemma
1
, we have:

\displaystyle\sum_{t=1}^{T}\mathbb{E}\left[\left\|\nabla F(\mathbf{x}_{t})-\mathbf{v}_{t}\right\|^{2}\right]\leq

\displaystyle 2\sum_{t=1}^{T}\mathbb{E}\left[\left\|\mathbf{v}_{t}-\prod_{i=1}^{K}\nabla f_{i}(\mathbf{u}_{t}^{i-1})\right\|^{2}\right]\quad+2KL_{F}^{2}\sum_{t=1}^{T}\sum_{i=1}^{K-1}\mathbb{E}\left[\left\|\mathbf{u}_{t}^{i}-f_{i}(\mathbf{u}_{t}^{i-1})\right\|^{2}\right].

Noting that
\mathbb{E}\left[\left\|\mathbf{v}_{1}-\prod_{i=1}^{K}\nabla f_{i}(\mathbf{u}_{1}^{i-1})\right\|^{2}\right]\leq\frac{K^{2}\sigma_{J}^{2}L_{f}^{2K-2}}{B_{0}}
,
\sum_{i=1}^{K}\mathbb{E}\left[\left\|\mathbf{u}_{t}^{i}-f_{i}(\mathbf{u}_{t}^{i-1})\right\|^{2}\right]\leq\frac{K\sigma^{2}}{B_{0}}
and setting
2\alpha L_{1}K\leq B_{1}
, we can deduce that:

\displaystyle\sum_{t=1}^{T}\mathbb{E}\left[\left\|\mathbf{v}_{t}-\prod_{i=1}^{K}\nabla f_{i}(\mathbf{u}_{t}^{i-1})\right\|^{2}\right]+2\sum_{t=1}^{T}\sum_{i=1}^{K-1}\mathbb{E}\left[\left\|\mathbf{u}_{t}^{i}-f_{i}(\mathbf{u}_{t}^{i-1})\right\|^{2}\right]

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{K^{2}\sigma_{J}^{2}L_{f}^{2K-2}+2K\sigma^{2}}{\alpha B_{0}}+\frac{2\alpha K^{2}T\sigma_{J}^{2}L_{f}^{2K-2}+4\alpha KT\sigma^{2}}{B_{1}}+\frac{2K\mathcal{L}_{J}^{2}L_{f}^{2K-2}+4\mathcal{L}_{f}^{2}}{\alpha B_{1}}\sum_{t=1}^{T}\sum_{i=1}^{K}\mathbb{E}\left[\left\|\mathbf{u}_{t}^{i-1}-\mathbf{u}_{t-1}^{i-1}\right\|^{2}\right]

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{K^{2}\sigma_{J}^{2}L_{f}^{2K-2}+2K\sigma^{2}}{\alpha B_{0}}+\frac{2\alpha K^{2}T\sigma_{J}^{2}L_{f}^{2K-2}+4\alpha KT\sigma^{2}}{B_{1}}

\displaystyle+\frac{L_{1}}{\alpha B_{1}}\left(T\eta^{2}D^{2}+\frac{2T\alpha^{2}\sigma^{2}K}{B_{1}}+2\alpha^{2}K\sum_{t=1}^{T}\sum_{i=1}^{K}\mathbb{E}\left[\left\|\mathbf{u}_{t}^{i}-f_{i}(\mathbf{u}_{t}^{i-1})\right\|^{2}\right]\right)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{K^{2}\sigma_{J}^{2}L_{f}^{2K-2}+2K\sigma^{2}}{\alpha B_{0}}+\frac{2\alpha K^{2}T\sigma_{J}^{2}L_{f}^{2K-2}+4\alpha KT\sigma^{2}}{B_{1}}+\frac{L_{1}\eta^{2}D^{2}T}{\alpha B_{1}}+\frac{2\alpha L_{1}\sigma^{2}KT}{B_{1}^{2}}

\displaystyle+\sum_{t=1}^{T}\sum_{i=1}^{K}\mathbb{E}\left[\left\|\mathbf{u}_{t}^{i}-f_{i}(\mathbf{u}_{t}^{i-1})\right\|^{2}\right]

So, we have that:

\displaystyle\sum_{t=1}^{T}\mathbb{E}\left[\left\|\nabla F(\mathbf{x}_{t})-\mathbf{v}_{t}\right\|^{2}\right]

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle L_{2}\sum_{t=1}^{T}\mathbb{E}\left[\left\|\mathbf{v}_{t}-\prod_{i=1}^{K}\nabla f_{i}(\mathbf{u}_{t}^{i-1})\right\|^{2}\right]+L_{2}\sum_{t=1}^{T}\sum_{i=1}^{K-1}\mathbb{E}\left[\left\|\mathbf{u}_{t}^{i}-f_{i}(\mathbf{u}_{t}^{i-1})\right\|^{2}\right]

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle L_{2}\left(\frac{K^{2}\sigma_{J}^{2}L_{f}^{2K-2}+2K\sigma^{2}}{\alpha B_{0}}+\frac{2\alpha K^{2}T\sigma_{J}^{2}L_{f}^{2K-2}+4\alpha KT\sigma^{2}}{B_{1}}+\frac{L_{1}\eta^{2}D^{2}T}{\alpha B_{1}}+\frac{2\alpha L_{1}\sigma^{2}KT}{B_{1}^{2}}\right)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{L_{3}}{\alpha B_{0}}+\frac{\alpha L_{3}T}{B_{1}}+\frac{L_{3}\eta^{2}T}{\alpha B_{1}}

∎

Now we can finish the proof as follows. Denote the Frank-Wolfe Gap as
\mathcal{F}(\mathbf{x}):=\max_{\hat{\mathbf{x}}\in\mathcal{X}}\langle\hat{\mathbf{x}}-\mathbf{x},-\nabla F(\mathbf{x})\rangle
and
\mathbf{z}_{t}^{\star}=\arg\max_{\hat{\mathbf{x}}\in\mathcal{X}}\langle\hat{\mathbf{x}}-\mathbf{x},-\nabla F(\mathbf{x})\rangle
.

\displaystyle F\left(\mathbf{x}_{t+1}\right)

\displaystyle\leq F\left(\mathbf{x}_{t}\right)+\left\langle\nabla F\left(\mathbf{x}_{t}\right),\mathbf{x}_{t+1}-\mathbf{x}_{t}\right\rangle+\frac{L_{F}}{2}\left\|\mathbf{x}_{t+1}-\mathbf{x}_{t}\right\|^{2}

\displaystyle=F\left(\mathbf{x}_{t}\right)+\eta\left\langle\mathbf{v}_{t},\mathbf{z}_{t}-\mathbf{x}_{t}\right\rangle+\eta\left\langle\nabla F\left(\mathbf{x}_{t}\right)-\mathbf{v}_{t},\mathbf{z}_{t}-\mathbf{x}_{t}\right\rangle+\eta^{2}\frac{L_{F}}{2}D^{2}

\displaystyle\leq F\left(\mathbf{x}_{t}\right)+\eta\left\langle\mathbf{v}_{t},\mathbf{z}^{\star}_{t}-\mathbf{x}_{t}\right\rangle+\eta\left\langle\nabla F\left(\mathbf{x}_{t}\right)-\mathbf{v}_{t},\mathbf{z}_{t}-\mathbf{x}_{t}\right\rangle+\eta^{2}\frac{L_{F}}{2}D^{2}

\displaystyle=F\left(\mathbf{x}_{t}\right)+\eta\left\langle\nabla F\left(\mathbf{x}_{t}\right),\mathbf{z}^{\star}_{t}-\mathbf{x}_{t}\right\rangle+\eta\left\langle\nabla F\left(\mathbf{x}_{t}\right)-\mathbf{v}_{t},\mathbf{z}_{t}-\mathbf{z}^{\star}_{t}\right\rangle+\eta^{2}\frac{L_{F}}{2}D^{2}

\displaystyle\leq F\left(\mathbf{x}_{t}\right)-\eta\mathcal{F}\left(\mathbf{x}_{t}\right)+\eta D\left\|\nabla F\left(\mathbf{x}_{t}\right)-\mathbf{v}_{t}\right\|+\eta^{2}\frac{L_{F}}{2}D^{2}

That is to say:

\displaystyle\quad\frac{1}{T}\sum_{t=1}^{T}\mathcal{F}\left(\mathbf{x}_{t}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{F\left(\mathbf{x}_{1}\right)-F(\mathbf{x}_{T+1})}{\eta T}+\frac{D}{T}\sum_{t=1}^{T}\left\|\nabla F\left(\mathbf{x}_{t}\right)-\mathbf{v}_{t}\right\|+\eta\frac{L_{F}}{2}D^{2}

By setting
T=\mathcal{O}\left(\epsilon^{-2}\right)
,
\eta=\mathcal{O}\left(\epsilon\right),\alpha=\mathcal{O}\left(\epsilon\right)
,
B_{0}=B_{1}=\mathcal{O}\left(\epsilon^{-1}\right)
, we can ensure that
\mathcal{F}(\mathbf{x})\leq\epsilon
. Moreover, we can obtain the same guarantee by setting that
\alpha=\mathcal{O}\left(\epsilon^{2}\right),\eta=\mathcal{O}\left(\epsilon^{2}\right),B_{1}=\mathcal{O}\left(1\right),B_{0}=\mathcal{O}\left(\epsilon^{-1}\right),T=\mathcal{O}\left(\epsilon^{-3}\right)
.

In the previous analysis of Lemma
5
, we simply reduce
{\eta^{2}}\left\|\mathbf{z}_{t}-\mathbf{x}_{t}\right\|^{2}\leq\eta^{2}D^{2}
. To obtain the optimal rate, we have to keep this term. That is to say, we rewrite the Lemma

as follows

\displaystyle\frac{1}{T}\sum_{t=1}^{T}\mathbb{E}\left[\left\|\nabla F(\mathbf{x}_{t})-\mathbf{v}_{t}\right\|^{2}\right]\leq\frac{L_{3}}{\alpha B_{0}T}+\frac{\alpha L_{3}}{B_{1}}+\frac{\eta^{2}L_{3}}{\alpha B_{1}T}\sum_{t=1}^{T}\left\|\mathbf{z}_{t}-\mathbf{x}_{t}\right\|^{2}.

According to Proposition 2 of
Xiao et al. (2022)
, we know that the gradient mapping

\displaystyle\left\|\mathcal{G}(\mathbf{x}_{t},\beta)\right\|^{2}\leq-4\beta g(\mathbf{x}_{t},\mathbf{v}_{t})+2\mathbb{E}\left[\left\|\nabla F(\mathbf{x}_{t})-\mathbf{v}_{t}\right\|^{2}\right],

where
g(\mathbf{x}_{t},\mathbf{v}_{t})=\min_{\mathbf{y}\in\mathcal{X}}\left\{\langle\mathbf{v}_{t},\mathbf{y}-\mathbf{x}\rangle+\frac{\beta}{2}\|\mathbf{y}-\mathbf{x}_{t}\|^{2}\right\}
.

Due to the convergence of Frank-Wolfe algorithm
(
Jaggi, 2013
)
, we know that

\displaystyle\langle\mathbf{v}_{t},\mathbf{z}_{t}-\mathbf{x}\rangle+\frac{\beta}{2}\|\mathbf{z}_{t}-\mathbf{x}_{t}\|^{2}\leq g(\mathbf{x}_{t},\mathbf{v}_{t})+\frac{2\beta D^{2}}{N+2},

which is widely used in the analysis of Frank-Wolfe algorithm
(
Xiao et al., 2022
;
Zhang et al., 2019
;
Wan et al., 2021
;
Wan & Zhang, 2021
)
.
Now, we begin our proof

\displaystyle F\left(\mathbf{x}_{t+1}\right)

\displaystyle\leq F\left(\mathbf{x}_{t}\right)+\left\langle\nabla F\left(\mathbf{x}_{t}\right),\mathbf{x}_{t+1}-\mathbf{x}_{t}\right\rangle+\frac{L_{F}}{2}\left\|\mathbf{x}_{t+1}-\mathbf{x}_{t}\right\|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq F\left(\mathbf{x}_{t}\right)+\eta\left\langle\nabla F\left(\mathbf{x}_{t}\right),\mathbf{z}_{t}-\mathbf{x}_{t}\right\rangle+\eta^{2}\frac{L_{F}}{2}\left\|\mathbf{z}_{t}-\mathbf{x}_{t}\right\|^{2}

\displaystyle=F\left(\mathbf{x}_{t}\right)+\eta\left\langle\mathbf{v}_{t},\mathbf{z}_{t}-\mathbf{x}_{t}\right\rangle+\eta\left\langle\nabla F\left(\mathbf{x}_{t}\right)-\mathbf{v}_{t},\mathbf{z}_{t}-\mathbf{x}_{t}\right\rangle+\eta^{2}\frac{L_{F}}{2}\left\|\mathbf{z}_{t}-\mathbf{x}_{t}\right\|^{2}

\displaystyle=F\left(\mathbf{x}_{t}\right)+\eta\left\langle\mathbf{v}_{t},\mathbf{z}_{t}-\mathbf{x}_{t}\right\rangle+\frac{\eta\beta}{2}\|\mathbf{z}_{t}-\mathbf{x}_{t}\|^{2}+\eta\left\langle\nabla F\left(\mathbf{x}_{t}\right)-\mathbf{v}_{t},\mathbf{z}_{t}-\mathbf{x}_{t}\right\rangle-\frac{\eta\beta}{2}\|\mathbf{z}_{t}-\mathbf{x}_{t}\|^{2}+\eta^{2}\frac{L_{F}}{2}\left\|\mathbf{z}_{t}-\mathbf{x}_{t}\right\|^{2}

Denote
\mathbf{y}^{\star}=\min_{\mathbf{y}\in\mathcal{X}}\left\{\langle\mathbf{v}_{t},\mathbf{y}-\mathbf{x}\rangle+\frac{\beta}{2}\|\mathbf{y}-\mathbf{x}_{t}\|^{2}\right\}
. Set
\eta\leq\frac{\beta}{2L_{F}}
(note that
\beta
is a positive constant), and then we have,

\displaystyle F\left(\mathbf{x}_{t+1}\right)

\displaystyle\leq F\left(\mathbf{x}_{t}\right)+\eta\left\langle\mathbf{v}_{t},\mathbf{y}^{\star}-\mathbf{x}_{t}\right\rangle+\frac{\eta\beta}{2}\|\mathbf{y}^{\star}-\mathbf{x}_{t}\|^{2}+\frac{2\eta\beta D^{2}}{N+2}

\displaystyle\quad+\eta\left\langle\nabla F\left(\mathbf{x}_{t}\right)-\mathbf{v}_{t},\mathbf{z}_{t}-\mathbf{x}_{t}\right\rangle-\frac{\eta\beta}{2}\|\mathbf{z}_{t}-\mathbf{x}_{t}\|^{2}+\eta^{2}\frac{L_{F}}{2}\left\|\mathbf{z}_{t}-\mathbf{x}_{t}\right\|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq F(\mathbf{x}_{t})+\eta g(\mathbf{x}_{t},\mathbf{v}_{t})+\frac{2\beta D^{2}\eta}{N+2}+\eta\left\langle\nabla F\left(\mathbf{x}_{t}\right)-\mathbf{v}_{t},\mathbf{z}_{t}-\mathbf{x}_{t}\right\rangle-\frac{\eta\beta}{2}\|\mathbf{z}_{t}-\mathbf{x}_{t}\|^{2}

\displaystyle\qquad+\eta^{2}\frac{L_{F}}{2}\left\|\mathbf{z}_{t}-\mathbf{x}_{t}\right\|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq F(\mathbf{x}_{t})+\eta g(\mathbf{x}_{t},\mathbf{v}_{t})+\frac{2\beta D^{2}\eta}{N+2}+\eta\left\langle\nabla F\left(\mathbf{x}_{t}\right)-\mathbf{v}_{t},\mathbf{z}_{t}-\mathbf{x}_{t}\right\rangle-\frac{\eta\beta}{4}\|\mathbf{z}_{t}-\mathbf{x}_{t}\|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq F(\mathbf{x}_{t})+\eta g(\mathbf{x}_{t},\mathbf{v}_{t})+\frac{2\beta D^{2}\eta}{N+2}+\frac{2\eta}{\beta}\mathbb{E}\left[\left\|\nabla F(\mathbf{x}_{t})-\mathbf{v}_{t}\right\|^{2}\right]-\frac{\eta\beta}{8}\|\mathbf{z}_{t}-\mathbf{x}_{t}\|^{2}

As a result,

\displaystyle-g(\mathbf{x}_{t},\mathbf{v}_{t})

\displaystyle\leq\frac{F(\mathbf{x}_{t})-F\left(\mathbf{x}_{t+1}\right)}{\eta}+\frac{2\beta D^{2}}{N+2}+\frac{2}{\beta}\mathbb{E}\left[\left\|\nabla F(\mathbf{x}_{t})-\mathbf{v}_{t}\right\|^{2}\right]-\frac{\beta}{8}\|\mathbf{z}_{t}-\mathbf{x}_{t}\|^{2}

So, we have:

\displaystyle\left\|\mathcal{G}(\mathbf{x}_{t},\beta)\right\|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle-4\beta g(\mathbf{x}_{t},\mathbf{v}_{t})+2\mathbb{E}\left[\left\|\nabla F(\mathbf{x}_{t})-\mathbf{v}_{t}\right\|^{2}\right]

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{4\beta(F(\mathbf{x}_{t})-F\left(\mathbf{x}_{t+1}\right))}{\eta}+\frac{8\beta^{2}D^{2}}{N+2}+10\mathbb{E}\left[\left\|\nabla F(\mathbf{x}_{t})-\mathbf{v}_{t}\right\|^{2}\right]-\frac{\beta^{2}}{2}\|\mathbf{z}_{t}-\mathbf{x}_{t}\|^{2}

Finally, we have

\displaystyle\quad\frac{1}{T}\sum_{t=1}^{T}\left\|\mathcal{G}(\mathbf{x}_{t},\beta)\right\|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{4\beta\Delta_{F}}{\eta T}+\frac{8\beta^{2}D^{2}}{N}+\frac{10}{T}\sum_{t=1}^{T}\mathbb{E}\left[\left\|\nabla F(\mathbf{x}_{t})-\mathbf{v}_{t}\right\|^{2}\right]-\frac{\beta^{2}}{2}\frac{1}{T}\sum_{t=1}^{T}\|\mathbf{z}_{t}-\mathbf{x}_{t}\|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{4\beta\Delta_{F}}{\eta T}+\frac{8\beta^{2}D^{2}}{N}+\frac{10L_{3}}{\alpha B_{0}T}+\frac{10L_{3}\alpha}{B_{1}}+\left(10L_{3}\frac{\eta^{2}}{\alpha B_{1}}-\frac{\beta^{2}}{2}\right)\frac{1}{T}\sum_{t=1}^{T}\left\|\mathbf{z}_{t}-\mathbf{x}_{t}\right\|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{4\beta\Delta_{F}}{\eta T}+\frac{8\beta^{2}D^{2}}{N}+\frac{10L_{3}}{\alpha B_{0}T}+\frac{10L_{3}\alpha}{B_{1}}

The last inequality holds with
\eta\leq\sqrt{\frac{\beta^{2}\alpha B_{1}}{20L_{3}}}
.
By setting
\alpha=\mathcal{O}(\sqrt{\epsilon})
,
\eta=\mathcal{O}(1)
,
T=\mathcal{O}(\epsilon^{-1})
,
B_{0}=\mathcal{O}(\epsilon^{-0.5}),B_{1}=\mathcal{O}(\epsilon^{-0.5})
, and
N=\mathcal{O}(\epsilon^{-1})
, We can ensure that
\mathbb{E}\left[\left\|\mathcal{G}(\mathbf{x}_{t},\beta)\right\|^{2}\right]\leq\epsilon
. This guarantee can also be satisfied by setting
\alpha=\mathcal{O}\left(\epsilon\right),\eta=\mathcal{O}\left(\sqrt{\epsilon}\right),T=\mathcal{O}\left(\epsilon^{-1.5}\right),B_{0}=\mathcal{O}(\epsilon^{-0.5}),B_{1}=\mathcal{O}\left(1\right),N=\mathcal{O}(\epsilon^{-1})
.

Convex

According to the equation (C.21) of
Yurtsever et al. (2019)
, for algorithm 1 with convex objectives, we have that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[F\left(\mathbf{x}_{t+1}\right)\right]-F_{\star}\leq\left(1-\eta\right)\left(\mathbb{E}\left[F\left(\mathbf{x}_{t}\right)\right]-F_{\star}\right)+\eta D\mathbb{E}\left\|\nabla F\left(\mathbf{x}_{t}\right)-\mathbf{v}_{t}\right\|+\eta^{2}\frac{L_{F}D^{2}}{2}

Then we have:

\displaystyle\frac{1}{T}\sum_{t=1}^{T}\mathbb{E}\left[F\left(\mathbf{x}_{t+1}\right)\right]-F_{\star}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{\left(\mathbb{E}\left[F\left(\mathbf{x}_{1}\right)\right]-F_{\star}\right)}{\eta T}+\frac{D}{T}\sum_{t=1}^{T}\mathbb{E}\left\|\nabla F\left(\mathbf{x}_{t}\right)-\mathbf{v}_{t}\right\|+\eta\frac{L_{F}D^{2}}{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{\left(\mathbb{E}\left[F\left(\mathbf{x}_{1}\right)\right]-F_{\star}\right)}{\eta T}+D\sqrt{\frac{1}{T}\sum_{t=1}^{T}\mathbb{E}\left\|\nabla F\left(\mathbf{x}_{t}\right)-\mathbf{v}_{t}\right\|^{2}}+\eta\frac{L_{F}D^{2}}{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{\left(\mathbb{E}\left[F\left(\mathbf{x}_{1}\right)\right]-F_{\star}\right)}{\eta T}+D\sqrt{\frac{\mathbb{E}\left[L_{2}\left\|\mathbf{v}_{1}-\prod_{i=1}^{K}\nabla f_{i}(\mathbf{u}_{1}^{i-1})\right\|^{2}\right]+2L_{2}\sum_{i=1}^{K}\mathbb{E}\left[\left\|\mathbf{u}_{1}^{i}-f_{i}(\mathbf{u}_{1}^{i-1})\right\|^{2}\right]}{\alpha T}}

\displaystyle+D\sqrt{\frac{\alpha L_{3}}{B_{1}}+\frac{\eta^{2}L_{3}}{\alpha B_{1}}}+\eta\frac{L_{F}D^{2}}{2}

Next, we denote that
\Gamma_{s}=\frac{1}{T}\sum_{t=1}^{T}\left\|\mathbf{v}_{t}-\prod_{i=1}^{K}\nabla f_{i}(\mathbf{u}_{t}^{i-1})\right\|^{2}+\frac{2}{T}\sum_{t=1}^{T}\sum_{i=1}^{K}\left\|\mathbf{u}_{t}^{i}-f_{i}(\mathbf{u}_{t}^{i-1})\right\|^{2}
for stage
s
and we denote
\mathbf{x}^{s}
as the output of Algorithm 3 for the stage
s
. Then, we have:

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[F\left(\mathbf{x}^{s}\right)-F_{\star}\right]\leq\frac{\mathbb{E}\left[F\left(\mathbf{x}^{s-1}\right)-F_{\star}\right]}{\eta_{s}T_{s}}+D\sqrt{\frac{L_{2}\Gamma_{s-1}}{\alpha_{s}T_{s}}}+D\sqrt{\frac{\alpha_{s}L_{3}}{B_{1}^{s}}+\frac{\eta_{s}^{2}L_{3}}{\alpha_{s}B_{1}^{s}}}+\eta_{s}\frac{L_{F}D^{2}}{2}

Also, we have that:

\displaystyle\Gamma_{s}\leq

\displaystyle\frac{2\Gamma_{s-1}}{\alpha_{s}T_{s}}+\frac{\alpha_{s}L_{3}}{B_{1}^{s}}+\frac{L_{3}\eta_{s}^{2}}{\alpha_{s}B_{1}^{s}}

Set
\epsilon_{s}=\left(\frac{1}{2}\right)^{s-1}
,
\eta_{s}=\alpha_{s}\leq\frac{\epsilon_{s}}{4L_{F}D^{2}},T_{s}\geq\max\left\{\frac{(4\Delta_{F}+24+64D^{2}L_{2})}{\eta_{s}},\frac{\Gamma_{0}(16D^{2}L_{2}+6)}{\eta_{s}}\right\},B_{1}^{s}\geq\max\left\{\frac{12\eta_{s}L_{3}}{\epsilon_{s}^{2}},\frac{128L_{3}\eta_{s}D^{2}}{\epsilon_{s}^{2}}\right\}
. We can guarantee that
\mathbb{E}\left[F\left(\mathbf{x}^{s}\right)-F_{\star}\right]\leq\epsilon_{s}
and
\mathbb{E}\left[\Gamma_{s}\right]\leq\epsilon_{s}^{2}
.
We will use the induction to give the proof:

Proof.

When
s=1
, we have:

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[F\left(\mathbf{x}^{1}\right)-F_{\star}\right]

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\Delta_{F}}{\eta_{1}T_{1}}+D\sqrt{\frac{L_{2}\Gamma_{0}}{\alpha_{1}T_{1}}}+D\sqrt{\frac{\alpha_{1}L_{3}}{B_{1}^{1}}+\frac{\eta_{1}^{2}L_{3}}{\alpha_{1}B_{1}^{1}}}+\eta_{1}\frac{L_{F}D^{2}}{2}

\displaystyle\leq\epsilon_{1}=1

where
\Gamma_{0}=K^{2}\sigma_{J}^{2}L_{f}^{2K-2}+2K\sigma^{2}
. Also, we have that:

\displaystyle\Gamma_{1}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{2\Gamma_{0}}{\alpha_{1}T_{1}}+\frac{\alpha_{1}L_{3}}{B_{1}^{1}}+\frac{L_{3}\eta_{1}^{2}}{\alpha_{1}B_{1}^{1}}\leq\epsilon_{1}^{2}=1

Then, assume
\mathbb{E}\left[F\left(\mathbf{x}^{s}\right)-F_{\star}\right]\leq\epsilon_{s}
and
\mathbb{E}\left[\Gamma_{s}\right]\leq\epsilon_{s}^{2}
, we would prove that it holds for stage
s+1
.

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[F\left(\mathbf{x}^{s+1}\right)-F_{\star}\right]

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\epsilon_{s}}{\eta_{s+1}T_{s+1}}+D\sqrt{\frac{L_{2}\epsilon_{s}^{2}}{\alpha_{s+1}T_{s+1}}}+D\sqrt{\frac{\alpha_{s+1}L_{3}}{B_{1}^{s+1}}+\frac{\eta_{s+1}^{2}L_{3}}{\alpha_{s+1}B_{1}^{s+1}}}+\eta_{s+1}\frac{L_{F}D^{2}}{2}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\epsilon_{s}}{8}+\frac{\epsilon_{s}}{8}+\frac{\epsilon_{s}}{8}+\frac{\epsilon_{s}}{8}=\epsilon_{s+1}

We also know that

\displaystyle\Gamma_{s+1}\leq

\displaystyle\frac{2\epsilon_{s}^{2}}{\alpha_{s+1}T_{s+1}}+\frac{\alpha_{s+1}L_{3}}{B_{1}^{s+1}}+\frac{L_{3}\eta_{s+1}^{2}}{\alpha_{s+1}B_{1}^{s+1}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{\epsilon_{s}^{2}}{12}+\frac{\epsilon_{s}^{2}}{12}+\frac{\epsilon_{s}^{2}}{12}=\epsilon_{s+1}^{2}

So we prove
\mathbb{E}\left[F\left(\mathbf{x}^{s}\right)-F_{\star}\right]\leq\left(\frac{1}{2}\right)^{s-1}
with
\eta_{s}=\alpha_{s}\leq\frac{\epsilon_{s}}{4L_{F}D^{2}},B_{1}^{s}\geq\max\left\{\frac{12\eta_{s}L_{3}}{\epsilon_{s}^{2}},\frac{128L_{3}\eta_{s}D^{2}}{\epsilon_{s}^{2}}\right\}
,
T_{s}\geq\max\left\{\frac{(4\Delta_{F}+24+64D^{2}L_{2})}{\eta_{s}},\frac{\Gamma_{0}(16D^{2}L_{2}+6)}{\eta_{s}}\right\}
. This condition can be satisfied by setting that
\eta_{s}=\mathcal{O}(\epsilon_{s})
,
T_{s}=\mathcal{O}(\epsilon_{s}^{-1})
,
\alpha=\mathcal{O}(\epsilon_{s})
and
B_{1}^{s}=\mathcal{O}(\epsilon_{s}^{-1})
[Large batch version]. This can also be achieved by setting that
\eta_{s}=\mathcal{O}(\epsilon_{s}^{2})
,
T_{s}=\mathcal{O}(\epsilon_{s}^{-2})
,
\alpha=\mathcal{O}(\epsilon_{s}^{2})
and
B_{1}^{s}=\mathcal{O}(1)
[Constant Batch].

To ensure
\mathbb{E}\left[F\left(\mathbf{x}^{s}\right)-F_{\star}\right]\leq\epsilon
, set
S=\mathcal{O}(\log_{2}(\frac{1}{\epsilon}))
, and the SFO rate is
\sum_{s=1}^{S}T^{s}B_{1}^{s}=\mathcal{O}\left(\sum_{s=1}^{S}2^{(2s)}\right)=\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{1}{\epsilon^{2}}\right)
.
∎

D.2
Strongly Convex

In this section, we assume
F(\mathbf{x})
is
\lambda
-strongly convex function and we set
\eta\leq\frac{\lambda}{4L_{F}}
:

\displaystyle F\left(\mathbf{x}_{t+1}\right)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle F\left(\mathbf{x}_{t}\right)+\left\langle\nabla F\left(\mathbf{x}_{t}\right),\mathbf{x}_{t+1}-\mathbf{x}_{t}\right\rangle+\frac{L_{F}}{2}\left\|\mathbf{x}_{t+1}-\mathbf{x}_{t}\right\|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle F\left(\mathbf{x}_{t}\right)+\eta\left\langle\nabla F\left(\mathbf{x}_{t}\right),\mathbf{z}_{t}-\mathbf{x}_{t}\right\rangle+\eta^{2}\frac{L_{F}}{2}\left\|\mathbf{z}_{t}-\mathbf{x}_{t}\right\|^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle F\left(\mathbf{x}_{t}\right)+\eta\left\langle\mathbf{v}_{t},\mathbf{z}_{t}-\mathbf{x}_{t}\right\rangle+\eta\left\langle\nabla F\left(\mathbf{x}_{t}\right)-\mathbf{v}_{t},\mathbf{z}_{t}-\mathbf{x}_{t}\right\rangle+\frac{\eta\lambda}{4}\left\|\mathbf{z}_{t}-\mathbf{x}_{t}\right\|^{2}+(\eta^{2}\frac{L_{F}}{2}-\frac{\eta\lambda}{4})\left\|\mathbf{z}_{t}-\mathbf{x}_{t}\right\|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle F(\mathbf{x}_{t})+\eta\left\langle\nabla F(\mathbf{x}_{t})-\mathbf{v}_{t},\mathbf{z}_{t}-\mathbf{x}^{\star}\right\rangle+\eta\left\langle\nabla F(\mathbf{x}_{t}),\mathbf{x}^{\star}-\mathbf{x}_{t}\right\rangle+\frac{\eta\lambda}{4}\left\|\mathbf{x}^{\star}-\mathbf{x}_{t}\right\|^{2}+\frac{\eta\lambda D^{2}}{N}-\frac{\eta\lambda}{8}\left\|\mathbf{z}_{t}-\mathbf{x}_{t}\right\|^{2},

where the last inequality is due to the fact that

\displaystyle\eta\left\langle\mathbf{v}_{t},\mathbf{z}_{t}-\mathbf{x}_{t}\right\rangle+\frac{\eta\lambda}{4}\left\|\mathbf{z}_{t}-\mathbf{x}_{t}\right\|^{2}\leq\eta\left\langle\mathbf{v}_{t},\mathbf{x}^{\star}-\mathbf{x}_{t}\right\rangle+\frac{\eta\lambda}{4}\left\|\mathbf{x}^{\star}-\mathbf{x}_{t}\right\|^{2}+\frac{\eta\lambda D^{2}}{N}

For
\lambda
-strongly convex function, we have
\left\langle\nabla F(\mathbf{x}_{t}),\mathbf{x}^{\star}-\mathbf{x}_{t}\right\rangle\leq F_{\star}-F(\mathbf{x}_{t})-\frac{\lambda}{2}\left\|\mathbf{x}_{t}-\mathbf{x}^{\star}\right\|^{2}
. As a result:

\displaystyle F\left(\mathbf{x}_{t+1}\right)

\displaystyle\leq F(\mathbf{x}_{t})+\eta(F_{\star}-F(\mathbf{x}_{t}))-\frac{\eta\lambda}{4}\left\|\mathbf{x}^{\star}-\mathbf{x}_{t}\right\|^{2}+\frac{\eta\lambda}{16}\left\|\mathbf{z}_{t}-\mathbf{x}^{\star}\right\|^{2}+\frac{4\eta}{\lambda}\left\|\nabla F(\mathbf{x}_{t})-\mathbf{v}_{t}\right\|^{2}

\displaystyle\qquad+\frac{\eta\lambda D^{2}}{N}-\frac{\eta\lambda}{8}\left\|\mathbf{z}_{t}-\mathbf{x}_{t}\right\|^{2}

So we have:

F\left(\mathbf{x}_{t+1}\right)-F_{\star}\leq(1-\eta)(F\left(\mathbf{x}_{t}\right)-F_{\star})+\frac{4\eta}{\lambda}\left\|\nabla F(\mathbf{x}_{t})-\mathbf{v}_{t}\right\|^{2}+\frac{\eta\lambda D^{2}}{N}

Finally, we have:

\displaystyle\frac{1}{T}\sum_{i=1}^{T}(F\left(\mathbf{x}_{t}\right)-F_{\star})

\displaystyle\leq\frac{F\left(\mathbf{x}_{1}\right)-F_{\star}}{\eta T}+\frac{4}{\lambda T}\sum_{t=1}^{T}\left\|\nabla F\left(\mathbf{x}_{t}\right)-\mathbf{v}_{t}\right\|^{2}+\frac{\lambda D^{2}}{N}

Next, we denote
\mathbf{x}^{s}
as the output for stage
s
. Then, we have:

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[F\left(\mathbf{x}^{s}\right)\right]-F_{\star}\leq\frac{F\left(\mathbf{x}_{s-1}\right)-F_{\star}}{\eta_{s}T_{s}}+\frac{4\Gamma_{s-1}}{\lambda\alpha_{s}T_{s}}+\frac{4\alpha_{s}L_{3}}{\lambda B_{1}^{s}}+\frac{4\eta^{2}_{s}L_{3}}{\lambda\alpha_{s}B_{1}^{s}}+\frac{\lambda D^{2}}{N}

Also, we have that:

\displaystyle\Gamma_{s}\leq

\displaystyle\frac{2\Gamma_{s-1}}{\alpha_{s}T_{s}}+\frac{\alpha_{s}L_{3}}{B_{1}^{s}}+\frac{L_{3}\eta_{s}^{2}}{\alpha_{s}B_{1}^{s}}

Set that
\epsilon_{s}=(\frac{1}{2})^{s-1},B_{1}^{s}\geq\frac{72\alpha_{s}L_{3}}{\lambda\epsilon_{s}},N\geq\frac{6\lambda D^{2}}{\epsilon_{s}},T_{s}\geq\max\{\frac{72(\Delta_{F}+1)}{\eta_{s}},\frac{72(D^{2}+1)\Gamma_{0}}{\eta_{s}}\}
,
B_{0}=\max\{\lambda^{-1},1\}
. We can guarantee that
\mathbb{E}\left[F\left(\mathbf{x}^{s}\right)-F_{\star}\right]\leq\epsilon_{s}
and
\mathbb{E}\left[\Gamma_{s}\right]\leq\lambda\epsilon_{s}
.
We will use the induction to give the proof:

Proof.

When
s=1
, we have:

\displaystyle\Gamma_{1}\leq

\displaystyle\frac{2\Gamma_{0}}{\alpha_{1}T_{1}B_{0}}+\frac{\alpha_{1}L_{3}}{B_{1}^{1}}+\frac{L_{3}\eta_{1}^{2}}{\alpha_{1}B_{1}^{1}}\leq\lambda\epsilon_{1}=\lambda,

where
B_{0}
denotes the batch size used only in the first iteration of the first stage. Also, we have that:

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[F\left(\mathbf{x}^{1}\right)-F_{\star}\right]

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\Delta_{F}}{\eta_{1}T_{1}}+\frac{4\Gamma_{0}}{\lambda\alpha_{1}T_{1}B_{0}}+\frac{4\alpha_{1}L_{3}}{\lambda B_{1}^{1}}+\frac{4\eta^{2}_{1}L_{3}}{\lambda\alpha_{1}B_{1}^{1}}+\frac{\lambda D^{2}}{N}

\displaystyle\leq\epsilon_{1}=1

Then assume
\mathbb{E}\left[\Gamma_{s}\right]\leq\lambda\epsilon_{s}
and
\mathbb{E}\left[F\left(\mathbf{x}^{s}\right)-F_{\star}\right]\leq\epsilon_{s}
, we would prove that it holds for stage
s+1
.

\displaystyle\Gamma_{s+1}\leq

\displaystyle\frac{2\Gamma_{s}}{\alpha_{s+1}T_{s+1}}+\frac{\alpha_{s+1}L_{3}}{B_{1}^{s+1}}+\frac{L_{3}\eta_{s+1}^{2}}{\alpha_{s+1}B_{1}^{s+1}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{2\lambda\epsilon_{s}}{\alpha_{s+1}T_{s+1}}+\frac{\alpha_{s+1}L_{3}}{B_{1}^{s+1}}+\frac{L_{3}\eta_{s+1}^{2}}{\alpha_{s+1}B_{1}^{s+1}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{\lambda\epsilon_{s}}{2}=\lambda\epsilon_{s+1}

Besides, we know that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[F\left(\mathbf{x}^{s+1}\right)-F_{\star}\right]

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\epsilon_{s}}{\eta_{s+1}T_{s}}+\frac{4\Gamma_{s}}{\lambda\alpha_{s+1}T_{s+1}}+\frac{4\alpha_{s+1}L_{3}}{\lambda B_{1}^{s+1}}+\frac{4\eta^{2}_{s+1}L_{3}}{\lambda\alpha_{s+1}B_{1}^{s+1}}+\frac{\lambda D^{2}}{N}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\epsilon_{s}}{2}=\epsilon_{s+1}

∎

So we prove
\mathbb{E}\left[F\left(\mathbf{x}^{s}\right)-F_{\star}\right]\leq\left(\frac{1}{2}\right)^{s-1}
with
\epsilon_{s}=(\frac{1}{2})^{s-1},B_{1}^{s}\geq\frac{72\alpha_{s}L_{3}}{\lambda\epsilon_{s}},N\geq\frac{6\lambda D^{2}}{\epsilon_{s}},T_{s}\geq\frac{72(\Delta_{F}+1+\Gamma_{0})}{\eta_{s}}
,
B_{0}=\max\{\lambda^{-1},1\}
.

This condition can be satisfied by setting that
\eta_{s}=\alpha_{s}=\mathcal{O}(\lambda)
,
T_{s}=\mathcal{O}(\lambda^{-1})
,
B_{1}=\mathcal{O}(\epsilon_{s}^{-1}),N=\mathcal{O}(\frac{\lambda}{\epsilon_{s}})
(Large Batch) or by setting that
\eta_{s}=\alpha_{s}=\mathcal{O}(\lambda\epsilon_{s})
,
T_{s}=\mathcal{O}(\lambda^{-1}\epsilon_{s}^{-1})
,
B_{1}=\mathcal{O}(1),N=\mathcal{O}(\frac{\lambda}{\epsilon_{s}})
(Constant Batch). To ensure
\mathbb{E}\left[F\left(\mathbf{x}^{s}\right)-F_{\star}\right]\leq\epsilon
, set
S=\mathcal{O}(\log_{2}(\frac{1}{\epsilon}))
, and the SFO rate is
\sum_{s=1}^{S}T_{s}B_{1}^{s}=\sum_{s=1}^{S}\frac{\mathcal{O}(1)}{\lambda}2^{s-1}=\mathcal{O}(\frac{1}{\lambda\epsilon})
.

On a General Method for Resolving Integrals of Multiple Spherical Bessel Functions Against Power Laws into Distributions
Kiersten Meigs, Zachary Slepian

Abstract
We here present a method of performing integrals of products of spherical Bessel functions (SBFs) weighted by a power-law. Our method, which begins with double-SBF integrals, exploits a differential operator D ^ \hat{D} defined via Bessel’s differential equation. Application of this operator raises the power-law in steps of two. We also here display a suitable base integral expression to which this operator can be applied for both even and odd cases. We test our method by showing that it reproduces previously-known solutions. Importantly, it also goes beyond them, offering solutions in terms of singular distributions, Heaviside functions, and Gauss’s hypergeometric, F 1 2 \;{}_{2}{\rm F}_{1} for all double-SBF integrals with positive semi-definite integer power-law weight. We then show how our method for double-SBF integrals enables evaluating arbitrary triple-SBF overlap integrals, going beyond the cases currently in the literature. This in turn enables reduction of arbitrary quadruple, quintuple, and sextuple-SBF integrals and beyond into tractable forms.

2
Brief Review of Relevant Properties

The spherical Bessel functions are solutions to Bessel’s differential equation:

\displaystyle{x^{2}}{\frac{d^{2}f}{dx^{2}}}+{2x}{\frac{df}{dx}}+{[{x^{2}}-{\ell}{(\ell+1)}]f}=0,

(1)

where the solution
f(x)=j_{\ell}(x)
is the spherical Bessel of the first kind, and the solution
f(x)=y_{\ell}(x)
is the spherical Bessel of the second kind.

Spherical Bessel functions of the first kind are related to Bessel functions by

\displaystyle j_{\ell}(x)=\sqrt{\frac{\pi}{2x}}J_{\ell+1/2}(x).

(2)

The first few spherical Bessel functions of the first kind are:

\displaystyle j_{0}(x)

\displaystyle={\frac{\sin{x}}{x}}=\mathrm{sinc{(x)}},

\displaystyle j_{1}(x)

\displaystyle={\frac{\sin{x}}{x^{2}}}-{\frac{\cos{x}}{x}},

\displaystyle j_{2}(x)

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{3}{x^{2}}-1\right)\frac{\sin{x}}{x}-\frac{3\cos{x}}{x^{2}}.

(3)

Rayleigh’s formula generates the order-
\ell
SBF from the order-zero one, as

\displaystyle j_{\ell}(x)={(-x)^{\ell}}{\left(\frac{1}{x}\frac{d}{dx}\right)}^{\ell}{\frac{\sin{x}}{x}}.

(4)

The behavior for small values of
x
is

\displaystyle j_{\ell}(x)\approx{\frac{x^{\ell}}{(2{\ell}+1)!!}},

(5)

where
!!
denotes the double factorial, which is the product of all integers from one up to
n
(inclusive) that share the parity of
n
. The behavior for large values of
x
is

\displaystyle j_{\ell}(x)\approx{\frac{1}{x}}{\sin{\left(x-{\frac{{\ell}{\pi}}{2}}\right)}}.

(6)

The closure relation is:

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}x^{2}dx\;j_{\ell}(kx)j_{\ell}(k^{\prime}x)={\frac{\pi}{2kk^{\prime}}}{\delta_{\rm D}^{[1]}}(k-k^{\prime}),

(7)

where
\delta_{\rm D}^{[1]}
is a 1D Dirac delta function.

The orthogonality relation is:

\displaystyle\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}{j_{\ell}(x)}{j_{\ell^{\prime}}(x)}{dx}={\frac{\pi}{2{\ell}+1}}{\delta^{\rm K}_{\ell\ell^{\prime}}},

(8)

where
\delta^{\rm K}_{\ell\ell^{\prime}}
is the Kronecker delta, which is unity when
\ell=\ell^{\prime}
and zero otherwise.

3
Method and Fundamental Results Used

3.1
Integral Form and Operator

We are concerned with integrals of the form:

\displaystyle I_{\ell\ell^{\prime}}^{[n]}(r,r^{\prime})=\int_{0}^{\infty}dkk^{n}j_{\ell}(kr)j_{\ell^{\prime}}(kr^{\prime}).

(9)

In what follows, we suppress the upper and lower bounds as we will always be considering half-infinite integrals.

We may use Bessel’s differential equation to define an operator that will act on the SBF and by parametric differentiation introduce an additional power of
k^{2}
:

\displaystyle\hat{D}_{\ell,\chi}j_{\ell}(k\chi)\equiv-\left[\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial\chi^{2}}+\frac{2}{\chi}\frac{\partial}{\partial\chi}-\frac{\ell(\ell+1)}{\chi^{2}}\right]\;j_{\ell}(k\chi)=k^{2}j_{\ell}(k\chi).

(10)

This method was suggested and used to write a formal result for even powers of
k
against two SBFs of the same order in
[
19
]
.

To use this operator, we need a base integral for equation (
9
) for some value of
n
. There are certain
n
for which the integral is convergent; we begin with them. We will need a formula that has both an odd and an even allowed starting
n
since applying our operator cannot alter the parity of the power of
k
.

We observe that

\displaystyle\hat{D}_{\ell,r}I_{\ell\ell^{\prime}}^{[n]}(r,r^{\prime})=I_{\ell\ell^{\prime}}^{[n+2]}(r,r^{\prime})=\int dk\,k^{n+2}j_{\ell}(kr)j_{\ell^{\prime}}(kr^{\prime}).

(11)

The
\hat{D}_{\ell,r}
operator can be repeatedly applied to form a “ladder” of integrals with
n
ascending in steps of two. This means that an integral of the form (
9
) can be evaluated for arbitrary
n
by applying the
\hat{D}
operator a suitable number of times, as long as we have a result for some starting value of
n
; in particular we will need both even and odd parity-
n
starting cases as the operator cannot change the parity of the power.

6.3
Examples

To demonstrate the utility of our approach, we here give a few examples that would not be obtainable by the other methods previously in the literature.

6.3.1
n=2,\;\ell_{1}=0=\ell_{2},\;\ell_{3}=1

We seek to evaluate the integral

\displaystyle I_{001}^{[2]}(r_{1},r_{2},r_{3})=\int k^{2}dk\;j_{0}(kr_{1})j_{0}(kr_{2})j_{1}(kr_{3}).

(44)

We apply equation (
38
). We first set
L=0
in the innermost integral of that relation, and to evaluate it then use
[
30
]
Appendix B, first unnumbered result below their equation B3:

\displaystyle\int k^{2}dk\;j_{0}(kr_{1})j_{0}(kr_{2})j_{0}(ku)=\frac{1}{4}\frac{\pi\beta(\Delta)}{r_{1}r_{2}u},

(45)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\Delta=\frac{r_{1}^{2}+r_{2}^{2}-u^{2}}{2r_{1}r_{2}}.

Our middle integral, over
k^{\prime}
, is then

\displaystyle\int k^{\prime 2}dk^{\prime}\;j_{1}(k^{\prime}r_{3})j_{0}(k^{\prime}u)

\displaystyle=H(u-r_{3})\left[\frac{1}{r_{3}^{3}-r_{3}u^{2}}+\frac{{\rm arctanh}[r_{3}/u]}{r_{3}^{2}u}\right]

\displaystyle+H(r_{3}-u)\left[\frac{2u}{3(r_{3}^{3}-r_{3}u^{2})}+\frac{2\;{\rm arctanh}[u/r_{3}]}{3r_{3}^{2}}\right]

(46)

We note that this integral is a case that is marginally convergent as the out-of-phase behavior of the SBFs in the UV (
e.g.
see equation (
6
)) converts the otherwise-constant asymptotic (as
k^{2}\times(1/k)^{2}
) into an oscillating function. Hence, though it can be evaluated with our operator method, it also can be taken directly from
[
50
]
. Nonetheless this example is still a non-trivial case where our method of reducing a triple SBF integral is required, relative to what
[
31
]
would be able to evaluate.

The product of equations (
45
) and (
46
) must then be integrated against
u^{2}du

38
) dictates, for the final result. Importantly, the factor of
1/u
in equation (
45

46
) against a weight of
u\,du
. We also note that due to the factor of
\beta(\Delta)
in equation (
45

\displaystyle I_{u>}=-\frac{1}{2}r_{3}^{-1}\ln[r_{3}^{2}-u^{2}]+\frac{1}{2r_{3}}r_{3}^{-2}\left[u\;{\rm arctanh}[r_{3}/u]+\frac{1}{2}r_{3}\ln[r_{3}^{2}-u^{2}]\right],

\displaystyle I_{u<}=2\left[-\frac{u}{3r_{3}}+\frac{1}{6}\ln\left[\frac{u+r_{3}}{u-r_{3}}\right]\right]+\frac{1}{6}r_{3}^{-2}\left[2u^{2}\;{\rm arctanh}[r_{3}/u]+r_{3}\left(2u+r_{3}\ln\left[\frac{u-r_{3}}{u+r_{3}}\right]\right)\right].

(equation

(equation

6.3.2
n=4,\;\ell_{1}=0=\ell_{2},\;\ell_{3}=2

We now seek to evaluate the integral

\displaystyle I_{002}^{[4]}(r_{1},r_{2},r_{3})=\int k^{4}dk\;j_{0}(kr_{1})j_{0}(kr_{2})j_{2}(kr_{3}).

(49)

This integral is even-parity in the integrand, so the 3-

would allow it, yet the power of

is not applicable because the integral is even-parity and also not convergent.

First, we return to equation (

) and notice that we may use the same

and innermost integral as we did in §

. In this case our middle integral, over

, is

\displaystyle I_{\rm mid}\equiv\int k^{\prime 4}dk^{\prime}\;j_{2}(k^{\prime}r_{3})j_{0}(k^{\prime}u)

\displaystyle=\hat{D}_{0,u}\int k^{\prime 2}dk^{\prime}\;j_{2}(k^{\prime}r_{3})j_{0}(k^{\prime}u)

\displaystyle=\frac{3\pi}{2r_{3}^{3}}\left[\frac{1}{u}\delta_{\rm D}^{[1]}(r_{3}-u)-\frac{1}{2}\frac{\partial}{\partial u}\delta_{\rm D}^{[1]}(r_{3}-u)\right].

\displaystyle I_{002}^{[4]}(r_{1},r_{2},r_{3})

It may be computed by applying our operator to the known integral below on the righthand side, as

\displaystyle I_{\rm mid}=\hat{D}_{0,u}\int k^{\prime 2}dk^{\prime}\;j_{1}(k^{\prime}r_{3})j_{0}(k^{\prime}u).

7
Dealing with More than Three SBFs

To resolve all integrals of more than three SBFs we may use a technique suggested by
[
53
]
. Consider an integral

\displaystyle I_{\ell_{1}\ell_{2}\ell_{3}\ell_{4}}^{[n]}(r_{1},r_{2},r_{3},r_{4})\equiv\int k^{n}dk\;j_{\ell_{1}}(kr_{1})j_{\ell_{2}}(kr_{2})j_{\ell_{3}}(kr_{3})j_{\ell_{4}}(kr_{4}).

(55)

Using the same trick as in §
6
we see that

\displaystyle I_{\ell_{1}\ell_{2}\ell_{3}\ell_{4}}^{[n]}=\frac{2}{\pi}\int u^{2}du\;\int k^{\prime n}dk^{\prime}\;j_{\ell_{3}}(k^{\prime}r_{3})j_{\ell_{4}}(k^{\prime}r_{4})j_{L}(k^{\prime}u)\int k^{2}dk\;j_{\ell_{1}}(kr_{1})j_{\ell_{2}}(kr_{2})j_{L}(ku).

(56)

We make two observations. First, these integrals cannot just be done using the techniques of
[
15
]
because
i)
one of them (that over
k^{\prime}
) has a power-law other than
2
, and
ii)
we are not guaranteed that both sums
\ell_{1}+\ell_{2}+L
and
\ell_{3}+\ell_{4}+L
will be even. One might hope that at least
ii)
could be dealt with, but in fact one cannot choose an
L
that makes both of these sums even unless the sums
\ell_{1}+\ell_{2}
and
\ell_{3}+\ell_{4}
themselves have the same parity (see also discussion in Slepian and Hou in prep.). This is not guaranteed. Thus, for both reasons
i)
and
ii)
the technique outlined in §
6
is required to enable performing an arbitrary integral of four SBFs.

We now investigate how to split an integral of five SBFs. We have

\displaystyle I_{\ell_{1}\ell_{2}\ell_{3}\ell_{4}\ell_{5}}^{[n]}(r_{1},r_{2},r_{3},r_{4}),r_{5}\equiv\int k^{n}dk\;j_{\ell_{1}}(kr_{1})j_{\ell_{2}}(kr_{2})j_{\ell_{3}}(kr_{3})j_{\ell_{4}}(kr_{4})j_{\ell_{5}}(kr_{5}).

(57)

We use the trick of §
6
twice to find

\displaystyle I_{\ell_{1}\ell_{2}\ell_{3}\ell_{4}\ell_{5}}^{[n]}

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{2}{\pi}\right)^{2}\int u^{\prime 2}du^{\prime}\;\int u^{2}du\;\int k^{n}dk\;j_{\ell_{1}}(kr_{1})j_{L}(ku)j_{L^{\prime}}(ku^{\prime})

\displaystyle\times\int dk^{\prime}\;j_{\ell_{2}}(k^{\prime}r_{2})j_{\ell_{3}}(k^{\prime}r_{3})j_{L}(k^{\prime}u)\;\int dk^{\prime\prime}\;j_{\ell_{4}}(k^{\prime\prime}r_{4})j_{\ell_{5}}(k^{\prime\prime}r_{5})j_{L^{\prime}}(k^{\prime\prime}u^{\prime}).

(58)

We now see three triple-SBF integrals that we can perform using the results of §
6
. Furthermore, they will involve integrals of at most hypergeometrics, and these integrals, by change of variable, can be seen to result in powers of
e.g.

u
and
u^{\prime}
; hence the final outer integrals can be done in closed form too.

We note that if one wished to perform an integral of six SBFs, one could use our trick of §
6
just once; this would give the analog of equation (
56
) but with four SBFs each in the integrals over
k^{\prime}
and
k
(imagine adding
j_{\ell_{5}}(k^{\prime}r_{5})
to the product within the first and
j_{\ell_{6}}(kr_{6})
to that within the second, for instance). But we have already shown that any integral over four SBFs can be reduced as in equation (
56
); hence the result of the reduction on the six-SBF integral can be evaluated. Similar logic shows that an arbitrary number of SBFs in the integrand may be dealt with in this fashion.

8
Concluding Discussion

In this work, we have presented a solution to the overlap integral of any pair of spherical Bessel functions with any arbitrary (integer and positive semi-definite) power-law weight. This work has applications in cosmology as more fully outlined in §
1
. We have also shown how the ability to do any such double integral enables performing
any
triple-sBF integral, exploiting a technique originally due to
[
53
]
but which requires the ability to do an arbitrary double-sBF integral to enable an arbitrary (positive semi-definite integer power-law weight) triple one. Once any triple sBF integral can be done, further use of this Mehrem technique enables doing any quadruple, quintuple,
etc.
, as we outline. An intriguing direction of future work would be to extend these approaches to integrals with negative power-law weights,
i.e.

k^{-1}
,
etc.
We believe this could be done beginning from the integrals in the present work using a technique of
[
54
]
, wherein one performs “parametric integration”, to lower the power-law. Also of interest would be further investigating the reason for the singularity of some of these overlap integrals but not others; complementary perspectives may come from considering contour integration as an approach, as in
[
40
]
, and also from analyzing the asymptotic behavior of the phase (
e.g.
as in equation (
6
)) at large argument [Cahn & Slepian in prep.].

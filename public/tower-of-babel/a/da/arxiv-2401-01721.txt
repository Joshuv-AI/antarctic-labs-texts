Abstract
Discrete Fourier transform (DFT) codebook-based solutions are well-established for limited feedback schemes in frequency division duplex (FDD) systems. In recent years, data-aided solutions have been shown to achieve higher performance, enabled by the adaptivity of the feedback scheme to the propagation environment of the base station (BS) cell. In particular, a versatile limited feedback scheme utilizing Gaussian mixture models (GMMs) was recently introduced. The scheme supports multi-user communications, exhibits low complexity, supports parallelization, and offers significant flexibility concerning various system parameters. Conceptually, a GMM captures environment knowledge and is subsequently transferred to the mobile terminals (MTs) for online inference of feedback information. Afterward, the BS designs precoders using either directional information or a generative modeling-based approach. A major shortcoming of recent works is that the assessed system performance is only evaluated through synthetic simulation data that is generally unable to fully characterize the features of real-world environments. It raises the question of how the GMM-based feedback scheme performs on real-world measurement data, especially compared to the well-established DFT -based solution. Our experiments reveal that the GMM -based feedback scheme tremendously improves the system performance measured in terms of sum-rate, allowing to deploy systems with fewer pilots or feedback bits.



I
Introduction

In the upcoming generation of cellular communication systems (6G), the
BS
can adapt to dynamic channel conditions.
In
FDD
systems, this adaptation relies on
channel state information (CSI)
feedback from the
MTs
due to the absence of channel reciprocity
[
1
]
.
In particular, limited feedback systems characterized by only a few bits representing the feedback information are of significant interest
[
1
]
.
In this regard, two primary approaches can be identified.
The first entails estimating the
downlink (DL)
channel at the
MTs
and subsequently determining the feedback information using a codebook
[
1
,
2
]
.
In scenarios with spatial correlation due to specific antenna geometries,
DFT
-based codebooks are well-established and are part of 3GPP specifications
[
3
,
4
,
5
]
.
The second approach aims to circumvent channel estimation and instead encode feedback information directly from pilot observations, enabled through data-aided approaches, e.g., deep learning
[
6
,
7
,
8
]
.

A promising approach from the latter category, albeit it does not employ deep learning, is a versatile limited feedback scheme based on
GMMs

[
9
]
.
This scheme offers flexibility with respect to various system parameters such as the number of served
MTs
, the number of pilots, various
signal-to-noise ratio (SNR)
levels, and the selection of the precoding algorithm.
The scheme was further enhanced to support variable feedback bit lengths in
[
10
]
.

GMMs
are generative models with a discrete latent space.
Conceptually, a
GMM
is utilized to learn the underlying channel distribution of the environment of a
BS
cell.
The learned
GMM
is then transferred to all
MTs
within the coverage area of the
BS
, enabling the
MTs
to exploit the environment knowledge during the online phase to infer feedback information.
The computational complexity associated with the inference of the feedback index does not depend on the number of transmit antennas, which is particularly beneficial for massive
multiple-input multiple-output (MIMO)
systems.
Following the
MT
’s feedback report to the
BS
, the
BS
designs precoders using either a directional information-based method or a generative modeling-based approach.

Although the
GMM
-based limited feedback scheme is a promising candidate for supporting future generations of cellular systems (6G), it is yet unclear how the performance gain that was observed for synthetic data, e.g., through stochastic-geometric channel models, transfers to real-world systems.
Thereby, a comparison to the well-established
DFT
codebook-based feedback scheme is of particular practical importance.
In this work, we address the question of whether sharing the knowledge of a generative model, i.e., the
GMM
, at the
BS
and the
MTs
is advantageous as compared to shared
DFT
-based codebook knowledge by evaluating and comparing the two feedback schemes on real-world data from a measurement campaign.
Our analyses reveal that the
GMM
-based feedback scheme, which is tailored to the
BS
cell environment, provides a robust solution with huge performance gains as compared to the
DFT
-based codebook solution, especially in systems with low pilot overhead that are of crucial importance in massive
MIMO
systems
[
11
]
.
The performance gains offered by the
GMM
-based scheme can be exploited to deploy systems with fewer pilots and feedback bits.

II
System Model and Channel Data

II-A

Data Transmission Phase

We consider the
DL
of a single-cell multi-user system, where the
BS
equipped with
N_{\mathrm{tx}}
transmit antennas serves
J
single-antenna
MTs
.
We adopt linear precoding such that the precoded
DL
data vector is given as
\bm{x}=\sum_{j=1}^{J}\bm{v}_{j}s_{j}
, where
s_{j}
is the transmit signal of
MT

j
, with
\expec[s_{j}]=0
and
\expec[\left|s_{j}\right|^{2}]=1
, and
\bm{v}_{j}\in\mathbb{C}^{N_{\mathrm{tx}}}
is the precoding vector of
MT

j
.
The precoders satisfy the transmit power constraint
\sum_{j=1}^{J}\operatorname{tr}(\bm{v}_{j}^{\operatorname{H}}\bm{v}_{j})=\rho
.
The sum-rate is given as

R=\sum_{j=1}^{J}\log_{2}\Bigg(1+\dfrac{\left|\bm{h}_{j}^{\operatorname{T}}\bm{v}_{j}\right|^{2}}{\sum_{m\neq j}\left|\bm{h}_{j}^{\operatorname{T}}\bm{v}_{m}\right|^{2}+\sigma_{n}^{2}}\Bigg),

(1)

where
\bm{h}_{j}\in\mathbb{C}^{N_{\mathrm{tx}}}
denotes the channel of
MT

j
and
\sigma_{n}^{2}
is the noise variance.
In the considered limited feedback setup, the task of the
BS
is to design the precoders
\bm{v}_{j}
given each
MT
’s feedback information
k^{\star}_{j}
, which is encoded by
B
bits.

II-B

Pilot Transmission Phase

Prior to data transmission,
n_{p}
commonly known orthogonal pilots are broadcasted to all
MTs
, from which each
MT
infers its feedback information
k^{\star}_{j}
(see (
3
), (
9
), or (
10
)).
The received signal of each
MT
in the pilot transmission phase is given by

\bm{y}_{j}=\bm{P}\bm{h}_{j}+\bm{n}_{j}\in\mathbb{C}^{n_{p}}

(2)

with the
additive white Gaussian noise (AWGN)

\bm{n}_{j}\sim\mathcal{N}_{\mathbb{C}}(\mathbf{0},{\bm{\Sigma}})
with
{\bm{\Sigma}}=\sigma_{n}^{2}\mathbf{I}_{n_{p}}
.
We use a
2
D-DFT (sub)matrix, which is obtained through the Kronecker product of two
DFT
matrices, as the pilot matrix since a
uniform rectangular array (URA)
is employed at the
BS
, see, e.g.,
[
12
]
.
To satisfy the power constraint, we normalize each column
\bm{p}_{\ell}
of
\bm{P}^{\operatorname{T}}
, for all
\ell\in\{1,2,\dots,n_{p}\}
, i.e.,
\|\bm{p}_{\ell}\|_{2}^{2}=\rho
.
We examine scenarios with fewer pilots than transmit antennas, i.e.,
n_{p}<N_{\mathrm{tx}}
.

.

III
Conventional DFT Codebook-based Feedback

In conventional codebook-based feedback schemes, each
MT
first calculates an estimate of its channel
\hat{\bm{h}}_{j}
and then determines the feedback information as (see, e.g.,
[
2
]
):

k^{\star}_{j}=\argmax_{k}|\bm{c}_{k}^{\operatorname{H}}\hat{\bm{h}}_{j}|

(3)

where
\bm{c}_{k}\in\mathcal{C}
, with
\mathcal{C}=\{\bm{c}_{1},\dots,\bm{c}_{K}\}
being a codebook of cardinality
|\mathcal{C}|=K=2^{B}
, with
B
bits.
In scenarios with spatial correlation due to specific antenna array geometries at the
BS
,
DFT
-based codebooks offer well-established solutions
[
3
,
4
]
.
Since we have a
URA
deployed at the
BS
side, a 2D-
DFT
codebook, which is constructed by the Kronecker product of two under-/oversampled
DFT
matrices
\bm{F}_{N_{\mathrm{v}}}\in\mathbb{C}^{N_{\mathrm{v}}\times S_{\mathrm{v}}N_{\mathrm{v}}}
and
\bm{F}_{N_{\mathrm{h}}}\in\mathbb{C}^{N_{\mathrm{h}}\times S_{\mathrm{h}}N_{\mathrm{h}}}
, i.e., the codebook entries
\bm{c}_{i}\in\mathcal{C}
, with normalization
\|\bm{c}_{i}\|_{2}=1
, are given by the columns of the matrix
\bm{F}_{N_{\mathrm{v}}}\otimes\bm{F}_{N_{\mathrm{h}}}

[
4
]
.
The under-/oversampling factors
S_{\mathrm{v}}
and
S_{\mathrm{h}}
are chosen to satisfy
|\mathcal{C}|=K=2^{B}
for a given value of
B
, accordingly.
Given the feedback
k^{\star}_{j}
of each
MT
, the
BS
represents the channel of each
MT
as
[
15
]
,

\tilde{\bm{h}}_{j}=\bm{c}_{k^{\star}_{j}},

(4)

and utilizes the
regularized channel inversion (RCI)
method from
[
16
]
, with regularization factor
\frac{J\sigma_{n}^{2}}{\rho}
, in order to jointly design the precoders.

IV
GMM-based Feedback Scheme

Any channel
\bm{h}
of the environment depicted in
Figure

1
follows the
probability density function (PDF)

f_{\bm{h}}
.
Particularly, the channels
\bm{h}_{j}
of
MTs
located anywhere within the
BS
’s coverage area are realizations of a random variable with
PDF

f_{\bm{h}}
.
This
PDF
is not available analytically.
The scheme proposed in
[
9
]
utilizes a
GMM
to approximate this
PDF
and thereby captures the environment knowledge.
The learned model is then subsequently transferred to the
MTs
to equip them with model awareness.
In the online phase, the
MTs
can exploit this knowledge to infer their feedback information.
After the
MTs
report their feedback to the
BS
, it designs the precoders utilizing either a directional information-based method or a generative modeling-based approach.

IV-A

Capturing the Environment at the BS – Offline

The characteristics of the environment are captured offline with a
GMM
, which consists of
K=2^{B}
components:

f^{(K)}_{\bm{h}}(\bm{h}_{j})=\sum\nolimits_{k=1}^{K}\pi_{k}\mathcal{N}_{\mathbb{C}}(\bm{h}_{j};{\bm{\mu}}_{k},\bm{C}_{k}).

(5)

Each
GMM
-component is described by the mixing coefficients
\pi_{k}
, the means
{\bm{\mu}}_{k}
, and the covariance matrices
\bm{C}_{k}
.
Note that the parameters of the
GMM
, i.e.,
\{\pi_{k},{\bm{\mu}}_{k},\bm{C}_{k}\}_{k=1}^{K}
, are common to all
MTs
.
Given a training dataset
\mathcal{H}
(see
Section

VI
), an
expectation maximization (EM)
algorithm can be used to compute maximum likelihood estimates of the
GMM
parameters, see
[
17
, Subsec. 9.2.2]
.

With a
GMM
, the posterior probability that the channel of
MT

j
originates from component
k
can be computed as
[
17
, Sec. 9.2]
,

p(k\mid\bm{h}_{j})=\frac{\pi_{k}\mathcal{N}_{\mathbb{C}}(\bm{h}_{j};{\bm{\mu}}_{k},\bm{C}_{k})}{\sum_{i=1}^{K}\pi_{i}\mathcal{N}_{\mathbb{C}}(\bm{h}_{j};{\bm{\mu}}_{i},\bm{C}_{i})}.

(6)

These posterior probabilities are also referred to as responsibilities.

IV-B

Model Transfer to the MTs – Offline

To enable the
MTs
to infer their feedback information, the knowledge of the
GMM
’s parameters is a prerequisite.
Conceptually, this requires transferring the model parameters, i.e.,
\{\pi_{k},{\bm{\mu}}_{k},\bm{C}_{k}\}_{k=1}^{K}
, to the
MTs
upon entering the coverage area of the
BS
.
This transfer equips the
MTs
with environmental awareness and is required only once since the
GMM
is fixed for a specific
BS
environment.

By incorporating model-based insights, the number of
GMM
parameters and, thus, the transfer overhead can be reduced tremendously.
In particular, the
GMM
covariances can be constrained to exhibit a certain structure.
Since we have a
URA
deployed at the
BS
, a reasonable choice is to constrain the
GMM
covariance matrices to be block-Toeplitz matrices with Toeplitz blocks, which are expressed as
\bm{C}_{k}=\bm{D}^{\operatorname{H}}\diag(\bm{c}_{k})\bm{D}
, with
\bm{D}=\bm{D}_{N_{\mathrm{v}}}\otimes\bm{D}_{N_{\mathrm{h}}}
, where
\bm{D}_{T}
(with
T\in\{N_{\mathrm{v}},N_{\mathrm{h}}\}
) contains the first
T
columns of a
2T\times 2T

DFT
matrix and
\bm{c}_{k}\in\mathbb{R}_{+}^{4N}
, cf.
[
14
,
10
]
.
Consequently, the covariance matrices of each component are fully characterized by the vectors
\bm{c}_{k}
.

, we depict the number of covariance parameters for different numbers
B
of feedback bits.
We can see that the structural constraints decrease the number of covariance parameters.

IV-C

Inferring the Feedback Information at the MTs – Online

In the online phase, each
MT
’s task is to infer its feedback information given the pilot observation
\bm{y}_{j}
with the help of the
GMM
.
Due to the joint Gaussianity of each
GMM
component (cf. (
5
)) together with the
AWGN
, the
GMM
of the observations can be simply computed with the
GMM
from (
5
) as

f_{\bm{y}}^{(K)}(\bm{y}_{j})=\sum\nolimits_{k=1}^{K}\pi_{k}\mathcal{N}_{\mathbb{C}}(\bm{y}_{j};\bm{P}{\bm{\mu}}_{k},\bm{P}\bm{C}_{k}\bm{P}^{\operatorname{H}}+{\bm{\Sigma}}).

(7)

Accordingly, each
MT

j
can compute the responsibilities given the observations
\bm{y}_{j}
as

p(k\mid\bm{y}_{j})=\frac{\pi_{k}\mathcal{N}_{\mathbb{C}}(\bm{y}_{j};\bm{P}{\bm{\mu}}_{k},\bm{P}\bm{C}_{k}\bm{P}^{\operatorname{H}}+{\bm{\Sigma}})}{\sum_{i=1}^{K}\pi_{i}\mathcal{N}_{\mathbb{C}}(\bm{y}_{j};\bm{P}{\bm{\mu}}_{i},\bm{P}\bm{C}_{i}\bm{P}^{\operatorname{H}}+{\bm{\Sigma}})}.

(8)

The feedback information
k_{j}^{\star}
is then determined as

k^{\star}_{j}=\argmax_{k}~{p(k\mid\bm{y}_{j})},

(9)

i.e., the index with the highest responsibility of the observed pilot signal
\bm{y}_{j}
of each
MT
serves as the respective feedback information.
The responsibilities evaluate how well each component of the
GMM
explains the underlying channel
\bm{h}_{j}
of the observed pilot signal
\bm{y}_{j}
.
The feedback information is thus simply the index of the
GMM
component that explains the channel best.

As a reference for performance analysis, perfect
CSI
can be used to determine the feedback information

k^{\star}_{j}=\argmax_{k}~{p(k\mid\bm{h}_{j})}.

(10)

IV-D

Designing the Precoders at the
BS
– Online

Given the feedback information
k_{j}^{\star}
of each
MT
, the
BS
has two options for jointly designing the precoders
\bm{v}_{j}
.

IV-D
1
Directional Information-based Precoder Design

The first option utilizes the directional information, which can be associated with each
GMM
component, in order to approximate each
MT
’s channel:

\tilde{\bm{h}}_{j}=\text{eigv}(\bm{C}_{k_{j}^{\star}}+{\bm{\mu}}_{k_{j}^{\star}}{\bm{\mu}}_{k_{j}^{\star}}^{\operatorname{H}}),

(11)

where
\text{eigv}(\bm{X})
extracts the dominant eigenvector of
\bm{X}
.
Accordingly, the channel is represented by the dominant eigenvector of the correlation matrix of the respective
GMM
component.
Note that using the dominant eigenvector of the correlation matrix as representative is inspired by the centroid condition of the Lloyd clustering approach, cf., e.g.,
[
18
]
.
The dominant eigenvectors of the correlation matrices per
GMM
component can be precomputed in the offline phase.
With the representations of the
MTs
’ channels as found in (
11
), the
BS
can then design the precoders employing the
RCI
method, see
[
16
]
.

With the
GMM
-based feedback approach, an iterative update of the codebook, as is the case for Lloyd’s clustering approach, is circumvented.
In principle, one would have to partition the training data utilizing the
GMM
into
K
disjoint sets as proposed in
[
9
]
in order to calculate cluster representatives, cf.
[
9
]
.
In the case of single-antenna
MTs
, the cluster representatives are computed in closed form via (
11
).

IV-D
2
Generative Modeling-based Precoder Design

The second option is based on generative modeling, where the channels of the
MTs
are treated as random variables, and a stochastic version of the well-known iterative
weighted minimum mean square error (WMMSE)
algorithm
[
19
]
, i.e., the
stochastic WMMSE (SWMMSE)
algorithm from
[
20
,
21
]
is employed for precoder design.
In particular, with the feedback information
k_{j}^{\star}
of each
MT
, see (
9
), the
GMM
enables the generation of samples via

\bm{h}_{j,\text{sample}}\sim\mathcal{N}_{\mathbb{C}}({\bm{\mu}}_{k^{\star}_{j}},\bm{C}_{k^{\star}_{j}}),

(12)

which resemble the distribution of the channel of
MT

j
.
The
BS
utilizes these generated samples in each iteration step of the
SWMMSE
algorithm in order to jointly design the precoders, cf.
[
9
]
.

IV-E

Complexity Analysis

The online computational complexity for inferring the feedback information of the
GMM
-based feedback scheme is dominated by matrix-vector multiplications.
This is because the computation of the responsibilities in (
8
) involves evaluating Gaussian densities, and the associated determinant and inverse expressions can be pre-calculated for a specific
SNR
level due to the fixed
GMM
parameters.
Accordingly, inferring the feedback information at the
MTs
via (
9
) in the online phase has a complexity of
\mathcal{O}(Kn_{p}^{2})
.
A significant advantage of the
GMM
-based feedback scheme is that the complexity is independent of the number
N_{\mathrm{tx}}
of transmit antennas.
This is especially beneficial for massive
MIMO
systems where the
BS
is typically equipped with a large antenna array.
Additionally, the
GMM
-based feedback scheme enables parallelization concerning the number of components
K
, allowing the simultaneous evaluation of all
K
responsibilities.

V
Discussion:

Generative Modeling versus DFT Codebooks

Although
DFT
-based codebook solutions are well-established in scenarios with spatial correlation, they heavily depend on accurate
DL

CSI
estimation since the knowledge of the channel is a prerequisite to determine the best-fitting codebook entry.
This generally holds for codebook-based solutions, including data-based codebook design approaches (cf.
[
22
,
18
]
), which utilize Lloyd’s algorithm.
Obtaining accurate channel estimates at the
MTs
often requires the transmission of numerous pilots from the
BS
to the
MTs
.
However, in massive
MIMO
systems, where the
BS
typically features many antenna elements, the pilot overhead necessary for full channel illumination is unaffordable
[
11
]
.
As a result, there is considerable interest in feedback schemes that perform well even when the number of pilots is fewer than the number of transmit antennas.
The
GMM
-based feedback scheme provides an ideal solution by circumventing explicit
DL

CSI
estimation and instead directly deducing feedback information from pilot observations.
Intuitively, the
GMM
-based feedback scheme is well-suited for this task because it can effectively handle the presented prior information captured during training and exploit it online to determine the feedback information.

Additionally, the
GMM
-based feedback scheme provides a low-complexity solution for inferring feedback information directly from pilot observations and even supports parallelization, see Subsection
IV-E
.
This contrasts with the
DFT
-based codebook solution, which undergoes a two-stage process involving channel estimation and subsequent determination of feedback information, introducing additional complexity.

Moreover, as opposed to the
DFT
-based codebook approach, which solely permits the usage of directional information for precoder design, the
GMM
-based feedback scheme additionally enables generative modeling-based precoder design due to the sample generation capability of the
GMM
.
These options allow for a trade-off between improved performance and required processing time for designing the precoders (see
Section

VI
).

Finally, the
GMM
framework even allows the
MTs
to estimate their channel by computing an observation-dependent convex combination of component-wise
linear minimum mean square error (LMMSE)
filters, which are parametrized by the
GMM
, cf.
[
23
]
.
The
GMM
-based channel estimator outperforms many other state-of-the-art estimators and thus even enhances the overall performance of the
DFT
codebook-based approach if used before codebook entry selection.
We include this baseline in the simulation results for performance comparison.
Nevertheless, it has to be noted that the computation of a channel estimate via the
GMM
already exhibits higher complexity than the feedback scheme from
Section

IV
.

Simulation Results

GMM
is fitted using the training set
\mathcal{H}
, which consists of
L=10^{5}
samples from the measurement scenario described in Subsection
II-C
.

10^{4}
channels.
The data samples are normalized to satisfy
\expec[\|\bm{h}\|^{2}]=N

Additionally, we fix
\rho=1

SNR

\frac{1}{\sigma_{n}^{2}}

We employ the sum-rate as the performance metric, averaging over

[

VII
Conclusion

In this work, we utilized real-world measurement data to assess the performance of the recently proposed
GMM
-based feedback scheme.
Despite exhibiting low complexity and enabling parallelization for inferring the feedback information, our experiments show that the
GMM
-based feedback scheme, which is tailored to a communications environment, outperforms the well-established
DFT
-based codebook solution in the considered system setups.
We conclude that the
GMM
-based feedback scheme offers great potential for deployment in future wireless communication systems.

Electromagnetic field energy in an absorptive medium with temporal and spatial dispersion
A.G. Zagorodny ? {}^{\textsf{\footnotesize{\mbox{?}}}}, S.A. Trigger ? {}^{\textsf{\footnotesize{\mbox{?}}}}, A.I. Momot ? {}^{\textsf{\footnotesize{\mbox{?}}}}

Abstract
General relations for electromagnetic field energy outside the transparency domain are proposed. It is shown that charged particle contribution to the energy of electromagnetic perturbations in the general case can be described in terms of a bilinear combination of the dielectric polarizability of the medium. The explicit form of such contribution is found. The relations obtained are used to generalize the Planck law to the case of an absorptive medium. Key words: absorptive medium, temporal and spatial dispersion, electromagnetic field energy

,
f_{0\text{m}}(\mathbf{v})
is the distribution function of bound particles (atoms, or molecules),
e_{\text{b}}
and
m_{\text{b}}
are the effective charge and the reduced mass of a bound electron.

Thus, in the case of a plasma-molecular system, the energy of a perturbation may be written as

\displaystyle W

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle W_{\text{F}}+W_{\text{K}}+W_{\text{U}}=\frac{1}{8\piup}\int\frac{\mathrm{d}\mathbf{k}}{(2\piup)^{3}}\int\frac{\mathrm{d}\mathbf{k}^{\prime}}{(2\piup)^{3}}\int\frac{\mathrm{d}\omega}{2\piup}\int\frac{\mathrm{d}\omega^{\prime}}{2\piup}\,\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}(\mathbf{k}-\mathbf{k}^{\prime})\mathbf{r}}\;\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}(\omega-\omega^{\prime})t}

(3.10)

\displaystyle\times

\displaystyle\Biggl[\left(\delta_{ij}-\frac{k_{i}k^{\prime}_{j}}{\mathbf{k}\mathbf{k}^{\prime}}\right)\left(1+\frac{c^{2}\mathbf{k}\mathbf{k}^{\prime}}{\omega^{2}}\right)+\frac{k_{i}k^{\prime}_{j}}{\mathbf{k}\mathbf{k}^{\prime}}+\sum_{\alpha=\text{e,i}}\frac{\omega^{2}}{\omega_{\text{p}\alpha}^{2}}\chi_{ki}^{(\alpha)}(\mathbf{k},\omega)\chi_{kj}^{(\alpha)*}(\mathbf{k}^{\prime},\omega^{\prime})

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\frac{\omega^{2}+\omega_{0}^{2}}{\omega_{\text{pm}}^{2}}\,\chi_{ki}^{(\text{m})}(\mathbf{k},\omega)\chi_{kj}^{(\text{m})*}(\mathbf{k}^{\prime},\omega^{\prime})\Biggr]E_{i\mathbf{k}\omega}E_{j\mathbf{k}^{\prime}\omega}^{*}\,.

This equation remains valid in the case of quantum description provided the polarizabilities
\chi_{ij}^{(\alpha)}(\mathbf{k},\omega)
(
\alpha=\text{e,\,i,\,m}
) are calculated appropriately (see, for example,
[
22
,
24
]
).

In the case of the monochromatic field
\mathbf{E}(\mathbf{r},t)={1}/{2}\left[\mathbf{E}(\mathbf{r})\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}\omega t}+\mathbf{E}^{*}(\mathbf{r})\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\omega t}\right]
, after the averaging over the oscillation period
T=2\piup/\omega
and the volume of the system
V
, equation (
3.10
) is reduced to

\displaystyle\overline{W}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{16\piup V}\int\frac{\mathrm{d}\mathbf{k}}{(2\piup)^{3}}\Biggl[\left(\delta_{ij}-\frac{k_{i}k_{j}}{k^{2}}\right)\left(1+\frac{c^{2}k^{2}}{\omega^{2}}\right)+\frac{k_{i}k_{j}}{k^{2}}

(3.11)

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\sum_{\alpha=\text{e,i}}\frac{\omega^{2}}{\omega_{\text{p}\alpha}^{2}}\,\chi_{ki}^{(\alpha)}(\mathbf{k},\omega)\chi_{kj}^{(\alpha)*}(\mathbf{k},\omega)+\frac{\omega^{2}+\omega_{0}^{2}}{\omega_{\text{pm}}^{2}}\,\chi_{ki}^{(\text{m})}(\mathbf{k},\omega)\chi_{kj}^{(\text{m})*}(\mathbf{k},\omega)\Biggr]\ \overline{E_{\mathbf{k}i}E_{\mathbf{k}j}^{*}}.

Let us consider the case when the spatial dispersion is formally neglected, i.e., we put

\chi_{ij}^{(\alpha)}(\mathbf{k},\omega)=\chi_{ij}^{(\alpha)}(\omega)
. In this case, equation (
3.11
) is simplified to

\overline{W}\equiv\overline{W}_{E}+\overline{W}_{B}\,,

(3.12)

where

\displaystyle\overline{W}_{E}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{16\piup}\Biggl[\delta_{ij}+\sum_{\alpha=\text{e,i,m}}\frac{\omega^{2}+\omega_{0\alpha}^{2}}{\omega_{\text{p}\alpha}^{2}}\,\chi_{ki}^{(\alpha)}(\omega)\chi_{kj}^{(\alpha)*}(\omega)\Biggr]\ \overline{E_{i}E^{*}_{j}}\,,

\displaystyle\overline{W}_{B}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{16\piup}\overline{|\mathbf{B}|^{2}},\quad\overline{|\mathbf{B}|^{2}}=\frac{1}{V}\int\mathrm{d}\mathbf{r}|\mathbf{B}(\mathbf{r})|^{2},

\displaystyle\overline{E_{i}E^{*}_{j}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{V}\int\mathrm{d}\mathbf{r}E_{i}(\mathbf{r})E_{j}^{*}(\mathbf{r}),

\displaystyle\omega_{0\alpha}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{cc}0,&\alpha=\text{e,\,i},\\
\omega_{0},&\alpha=\text{m}.\end{array}\right.\,

Using (
3.12
), (
3
) it is easy to recover the results obtained in
[
10
,
11
,
12
]
for the electric field energy density outside the transparency domain. For example, in the case of a cold molecular system

\chi_{ij}^{(\text{m})}(\omega)=-\delta_{ij}\frac{\omega_{\text{pm}}^{2}}{\omega^{2}-\omega_{0}^{2}+\mathrm{i}\gamma\omega}\,,

(3.16)

which leads to

\overline{W}_{E}=\frac{1}{16\piup}\overline{|\mathbf{E}|^{2}}\left[1+\frac{\omega_{\text{pm}}^{2}(\omega^{2}+\omega_{0}^{2})}{(\omega^{2}-\omega_{0}^{2})^{2}+\gamma^{2}\omega^{2}}\right].

(3.17)

In the case of a cold plasma

\chi_{ij}^{(\text{e})}(\omega)=-\delta_{ij}\frac{\omega_{\text{pe}}^{2}}{\omega(\omega+\mathrm{i}\nu_{\text{e}})}\,,

(3.18)

where
\nu_{\text{e}}
is the effective collision frequency, that gives

\overline{W}_{E}=\frac{1}{16\piup}\left(1+\frac{\omega_{\text{pe}}^{2}}{\omega^{2}+\nu_{\text{e}}}\right)\overline{|\mathbf{E}|^{2}}.

(3.19)

Equations (
3.17
) and (
3.19
) are in agreement with the well-known relation [see the first term in equation (
2.10
)]

\overline{W}=\frac{1}{16\piup}\frac{\partial}{\partial\omega}\left[\omega\mathop{Re}\nolimits\varepsilon_{ij}(\omega)\right]\overline{E_{i}E_{j}^{*}}

(3.20)

only in the case of nondissipative systems (
\gamma=0
and
\nu_{\text{e}}=0
).

4
Energy density of the electromagnetic field fluctuations

Within the context of the theory of electromagnetic fluctuations it is easy to show that equation (
3.10
) may be also applied to the description of the energy density of fluctuations. The statistical averaging of equation (
3.10
) yields

\displaystyle\langle W\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{8\piup}\int\frac{\mathrm{d}\mathbf{k}}{(2\piup)^{3}}\int\frac{\mathrm{d}\omega}{2\piup}\Biggl[\frac{k_{i}k_{j}}{k^{2}}+\left(1+\frac{c^{2}k^{2}}{\omega^{2}}\right)\left(\delta_{ij}-\frac{k_{i}k_{j}}{k^{2}}\right)

(4.1)

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\sum_{\alpha=\text{e,i,m}}\frac{\omega^{2}+\omega_{0\alpha}^{2}}{\omega_{\text{p}\alpha}^{2}}\,\chi_{ki}^{(\alpha)}(\mathbf{k},\omega)\chi_{kj}^{(\alpha)*}(\mathbf{k},\omega)\Biggr]\langle\delta E_{i}\delta E_{j}\rangle_{\mathbf{k}\omega}.

When deriving equation (
4.1
) we take into account that

\langle\delta E_{i\mathbf{k}\omega}\delta E_{j\mathbf{k}^{\prime}\omega^{\prime}}^{*}\rangle=(2\piup)^{4}\delta(\mathbf{k}-\mathbf{k}^{\prime})\delta(\omega-\omega^{\prime})\langle\delta E_{i}\delta E_{j}\rangle_{\mathbf{k}\omega}\,,

(4.2)

where

\displaystyle\langle\delta E_{i}\delta E_{j}\rangle_{\mathbf{k}\omega}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int\!\mathrm{d}\mathbf{R}\,\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}\mathbf{k}\mathbf{R}}\!\int\mathrm{d}\omega\,\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\omega\tau}\langle\delta E_{i}(\mathbf{r},t)\delta E_{j}(\mathbf{r}^{\prime},t^{\prime})\rangle_{\mathbf{k}\omega}\,,

(4.3)

\displaystyle\mathbf{R}=\mathbf{r}-\mathbf{r}^{\prime},\qquad\tau=t-t^{\prime}.

In the case of an equilibrium system
\langle\delta E_{i}\delta E_{j}\rangle_{\mathbf{k}\omega}
is given by the fluctuation dissipation theorem (see, for example,
[
3
,
4
]
)

\langle\delta E_{i}\delta E_{j}\rangle_{\mathbf{k}\omega}=\frac{4\piup\mathrm{i}}{\omega}\theta(\omega)\left[\Lambda_{ij}^{-1}(\mathbf{k},\omega)-\Lambda_{ji}^{-1*}(\mathbf{k},\omega)\right].

(4.4)

Here,

\displaystyle\theta\equiv\frac{\hbar\omega}{2}\coth\frac{\hbar\omega}{2T}\,,\qquad\Lambda_{ij}(\mathbf{k},\omega)=\varepsilon_{ij}(\mathbf{k},\omega)-\frac{k^{2}c^{2}}{\omega^{2}}\left(\delta_{ij}-\frac{k_{i}k_{j}}{k^{2}}\right).

(4.5)

Further simplification of (
4.1
), (
4.5
) can be done in the case of an isotropic system for which

\varepsilon_{ij}(\mathbf{k},\omega)=\varepsilon_{\mathrm{T}}(k,\omega)\left(\delta_{ij}-\frac{k_{i}k_{j}}{k^{2}}\right)+\varepsilon_{\mathrm{L}}(k,\omega)\frac{k_{i}k_{j}}{k^{2}}\,,

(4.6)

where
\varepsilon_{\mathrm{T}}(k,\omega)
and
\varepsilon_{\mathrm{L}}(k,\omega)
are the transverse and longitudinal parts of the dielectric permittivity tensor.

Substituting (
4.6
) into (
4.4
) and (
4.1
) yields

\langle W\rangle=\int\limits_{0}^{\infty}\langle W\rangle_{\omega}\mathrm{d}\omega,

(4.7)

where for the general case of the non-transparent medium we have

\displaystyle\langle W\rangle_{\omega}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\theta(\omega)}{2\piup^{3}\omega}\int\limits_{0}^{\infty}\mathrm{d}k\,k^{2}\Biggl\{\frac{\mathop{Im}\nolimits\varepsilon_{\mathrm{L}}(k,\omega)}{|\varepsilon_{\mathrm{L}}(k,\omega)|^{2}}\Biggl[1+\sum_{\alpha=\text{e,i,m}}\frac{\omega^{2}+\omega_{0\alpha}^{2}}{\omega_{\text{p}\alpha}^{2}}|\chi_{\mathrm{L}}^{(\alpha)}(k,\omega)|^{2}\Biggr]

(4.8)

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\frac{2\mathop{Im}\nolimits\varepsilon_{\mathrm{T}}(k,\omega)}{|\varepsilon_{\mathrm{T}}(k,\omega)-k^{2}c^{2}/\omega^{2}|^{2}}\Biggl[1+\frac{k^{2}c^{2}}{\omega^{2}}+\sum_{\alpha=\text{e,i,m}}\frac{\omega^{2}+\omega_{0\alpha}^{2}}{\omega_{\text{p}\alpha}^{2}}|\chi_{\mathrm{T}}^{(\alpha)}(k,\omega)|^{2}\Biggr]\Biggr\}\,,

that describes the contribution of both longitudinal and transverse electromagnetic fields.

In the case of negligible dissipation, we can use the approximation of the type

\displaystyle\frac{\mathop{Im}\nolimits\varepsilon_{\mathrm{T}}(k,\omega)}{|\varepsilon_{\mathrm{T}}(k,\omega)-k^{2}c^{2}/\omega^{2}|^{2}}\simeq\piup\delta\left(\mathop{Re}\nolimits\varepsilon_{\mathrm{T}}(k,\omega)-\frac{k^{2}c^{2}}{\omega^{2}}\right).

(4.9)

In the case of cold plasma for
\omega\gg\nu
we have

\varepsilon_{\mathrm{T}}(\omega)\simeq 1-\frac{\omega_{\text{p}}^{2}}{\omega^{2}}

and the part of the energy associated with the transverse electromagnetic field is given by

\displaystyle\langle W\rangle_{\omega}=\frac{\omega^{2}\theta(\omega)}{\piup^{2}c^{3}}\sqrt{\varepsilon(\omega)},\qquad(\omega>\omega_{\text{p}}).

(4.10)

This relation is in agreement with the well-known result for the energy density in the dispersive transparent medium
[
29
]
and reproduces the energy density for transparent plasmas
[
30
]
.

In the case of a molecular medium with weak absorption
\gamma\to 0

\varepsilon_{\text{b}}(\omega)=1-\frac{\omega_{\text{pm}}^{2}}{\omega^{2}-\omega_{0}^{2}}

(4.11)

and thus,

\langle W\rangle_{\omega}=\frac{\omega^{2}\theta(\omega)}{2\piup^{2}c^{3}}\sqrt{\mathop{Re}\nolimits\varepsilon_{\text{b}}(\omega)}\left[1+\mathop{Re}\nolimits\varepsilon_{\text{b}}(\omega)+\frac{\omega_{\text{pm}}^{2}(\omega^{2}+\omega_{0}^{2})}{(\omega^{2}-\omega_{0}^{2})^{2}+\gamma^{2}\omega^{2}}\right]\,.

For
\omega\gg\omega_{0}
we come back to the equation of the type (
4.10
).

For
\omega\ll\omega_{0}
, the frequency dispersion can be neglected and we obtain the result for nondispersive transparent medium
[
29
]

\langle W\rangle_{\omega}=\frac{\omega^{2}\theta(\omega)}{2\piup^{2}c^{3}}\tilde{\varepsilon}^{3/2},

(4.12)

where

\tilde{\varepsilon}=\lim\limits_{\omega\to 0}\,\varepsilon_{\text{b}}(\omega).

In the general case, equation (
4.8
) may be rewritten in the form

\langle W\rangle_{\omega}=\frac{\hbar\omega^{3}}{\piup^{2}c^{3}}\left(\frac{1}{2}+\frac{1}{\mathrm{e}^{\hbar\omega/T}-1}\right)S(\omega),

(4.13)

where
S(\omega)
is the function describing the effect of the medium

\displaystyle S(\omega)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{c^{3}}{2\piup\omega^{3}}\int\limits_{0}^{\infty}\mathrm{d}k\,k^{2}\Biggl\{\frac{\mathop{Im}\nolimits\varepsilon_{\mathrm{L}}(k,\omega)}{|\varepsilon_{\mathrm{L}}(k,\omega)|^{2}}\left[1+\sum_{\alpha=\text{e,i,m}}\frac{\omega^{2}+\omega^{2}_{0\alpha}}{\omega^{2}_{\text{p}\alpha}}|\chi_{\mathrm{L}}^{(\alpha)}(k,\omega)|^{2}\right]

(4.14)

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\frac{2\mathop{Im}\nolimits\varepsilon_{\mathrm{T}}(k,\omega)}{|\varepsilon_{\mathrm{T}}(k,\omega)-k^{2}c^{2}/\omega^{2}|^{2}}\Biggl[1+\frac{k^{2}c^{2}}{\omega^{2}}+\sum_{\alpha=\text{e,i,m}}\frac{\omega^{2}+\omega^{2}_{0\alpha}}{\omega^{2}_{\text{p}\alpha}}|\chi_{\mathrm{T}}^{(\alpha)}(k,\omega)|^{2}\Biggr]\Biggr\}.

5
Numerical analysis of the fluctuation field energy spectrum

As mentioned above, equations (
4.8
) and (
4.14
) take into account the contribution of both longitudinal and transverse electromagnetic field fluctuations. Particularly, the contribution of transverse fields into the function
S(\omega)
is given by

S_{\text{T}}(\omega)=\frac{c^{3}}{\piup\omega^{3}}\int\limits_{0}^{\infty}\mathrm{d}k\,k^{2}\frac{\mathop{Im}\nolimits\varepsilon_{\text{T}}(k,\omega)}{|\varepsilon_{\text{T}}(k,\omega)-k^{2}c^{2}/\omega^{2}|^{2}}\left[1+\frac{k^{2}c^{2}}{\omega^{2}}+\sum_{\alpha=\text{e,i,m}}\frac{\omega^{2}+\omega_{0}^{2}}{\omega_{\text{p}\alpha}^{2}}|\chi_{\text{T}}^{(\alpha)}(k,\omega)|^{2}\right],

(5.1)

and thus,

\langle W\rangle_{\omega}^{\mathrm{T}}=\frac{\hbar\omega^{3}}{\piup^{2}c^{3}}\left(\frac{1}{2}+\frac{1}{\mathrm{e}^{\hbar\omega/T}-1}\right)\,S_{\text{T}}(\omega)

(5.2)

can be considered as a generalization of the Planck formula to the case of absorptive medium. The function
S_{\text{T}}(\omega)
makes it possible to see what is the influence of the medium on the equilibrium radiation.

Particularly, in the case of a transparent medium without spatial dispersion [
\varepsilon_{\text{T}}(k,\omega)\equiv\varepsilon_{\text{T}}(\omega)
]

S_{\text{T}}(\omega)=\sqrt{\varepsilon(\omega)}

and in the case of transparent nondispersive medium [
\varepsilon_{\text{T}}(\omega)\equiv\varepsilon_{\text{T}}
]

S_{\text{T}}(\omega)=\varepsilon_{\text{T}}^{3/2}.

Now, let us consider the results of numerical calculation of the quantity
S_{\text{T}}(\omega)
in the case of collisional plasmas.

The calculations have been performed for one-component classical electron plasma, i.e.,

\displaystyle S_{\text{T}}(\omega)=\frac{c^{3}}{\piup\omega^{3}}\int\limits_{0}^{\infty}\mathrm{d}k\,k^{2}\frac{\mathop{Im}\nolimits\varepsilon_{\text{T}}(k,\omega)}{|\varepsilon_{\text{T}}(k,\omega)-k^{2}c^{2}/\omega^{2}|^{2}}\left[1+\frac{\omega^{2}}{\omega_{\text{p}}^{2}}\left|\chi_{\text{T}}(k,\omega)\right|^{2}+\frac{k^{2}c^{2}}{\omega^{2}}\right].

(5.3)

The quantity
\chi_{\text{T}}(k,\omega)
was calculated on the basis of the Bhatnagar-Gross-Krook model

\chi_{\text{T}}(k,\omega)=-\frac{\omega_{\text{p}}^{2}}{\omega(\omega+\mathrm{i}\nu)}\left[1-W\left(\frac{\omega+\mathrm{i}\nu}{kv_{\text{T}}}\right)\right],

where
v_{\text{T}}^{2}=T_{\text{e}}/m_{\text{e}}
,
\nu
is the collision frequency,
W(z)
is the plasma dispersion function

W(z)=1-z\mathrm{e}^{-z^{2}/2}\int\limits_{0}^{z}\,\mathrm{d}y\,\mathrm{e}^{y^{2}/2}+\mathrm{i}\left(\frac{\piup}{2}\right)^{1/2}z\mathrm{e}^{-z^{2}/2}\,.

S_{\text{T}}(\omega)
can be divided into two parts

S_{\text{T}}^{\mathrm{EK}}(\omega)=\frac{c^{3}}{\piup\omega^{3}}\int\limits_{0}^{\infty}\mathrm{d}k\,k^{2}\frac{\mathop{Im}\nolimits\varepsilon_{\text{T}}(k,\omega)}{|\varepsilon_{\text{T}}(k,\omega)-k^{2}c^{2}/\omega^{2}|^{2}}\left[1+\frac{\omega^{2}}{\omega_{\text{p}}^{2}}\left|\chi_{\text{T}}(k,\omega)\right|^{2}\right]

(5.4)

and

S_{\text{T}}^{B}(\omega)=\frac{c^{5}}{\piup\omega^{5}}\int\limits_{0}^{\infty}\mathrm{d}k\,k^{4}\frac{\mathop{Im}\nolimits\varepsilon_{\text{T}}(k,\omega)}{|\varepsilon_{\text{T}}(k,\omega)-k^{2}c^{2}/\omega^{2}|^{2}}.

(5.5)

The first one is associated with the electric field energy and particle kinetic energy, and the second one with the magnetic field fluctuations.

S_{\text{T}}^{B}(\omega)
in its turn can be presented as

S_{\text{T}}^{B}(\omega)=S_{\text{T}}^{B\rdiv}(\omega)+S_{\text{T}}^{B\,\mathrm{reg}}(\omega),

(5.6)

where

S_{\text{T}}^{B\rdiv}=\frac{c}{\piup\omega}\int\limits_{0}^{\infty}\mathrm{d}k\,\mathop{Im}\nolimits\varepsilon_{\text{T}}(k,\omega),

(5.7)

S_{\text{T}}^{B\,\mathrm{reg}}=\frac{c}{\piup\omega}\int\limits_{0}^{\infty}\mathrm{d}k\,\mathop{Im}\nolimits\left[-\frac{\varepsilon_{\text{T}}^{2}(k,\omega)}{\varepsilon_{\text{T}}-k^{2}c^{2}/\omega^{2}}\right].

(5.8)

As it easy to show, the integrals in equations (
5.4
) and (
5.8
) are convergent. On the contrary, the integral in equation (
5.7
) with
\varepsilon_{\text{T}}(k,\omega)
under consideration is divergent and the restriction of integration to the cut-off wavenumber
k_{\mathrm{max}}
is needed. This problem is well-known and has been discussed in detail in
[
31
,
32
,
33
]
. Notice that in the case of quantum calculations of
\varepsilon_{\text{T}}(k,\omega)
, the integral in equation (
5.7
) is convergent which follows from the explicit form of
\mathop{Im}\nolimits\varepsilon_{\text{T}}(k,\omega)
in such a case
[
34
]
. However, in the case of classical calculations, the problem of the choice of
k_{\mathrm{max}}
arises. In order to obtain the qualitative picture of the energy spectrum behaviour, we take the simplest well-known approximation
k_{\mathrm{max}}=T/e^{2}
.

S_{\text{T}}(\omega)
,
S_{\text{T}}^{\mathrm{EK}}(\omega)

S_{\text{T}}^{B\rdiv}

for three kinds of plasmas: solar corona, high-pressure gas discharge and electron-positron plasma at the beginning of primordial nucleosynthesis. The parameters of plasmas are presented in table

. The previous expressions are reduced to a dimensionless form. In this manner, the energy density depends on dimensionless parameters
w/w_{\text{p}}
,
c/v_{\text{T}}
,
\nu/\omega_{\text{p}}
. The dimensionless value of
k_{\mathrm{max}}
is

y_{\mathrm{max}}=\frac{cT}{\omega_{\text{pe}}e^{2}}.

(5.9)

As it follows from the numerical results, the dissipation of electromagnetic field results in crucial changes of the energy spectrum in the domain in which transverse electromagnetic perturbations cannot propagate (see, figures
2
,
3
,
4
). It looks as if in such a case the energy of fluctuating electromagnetic fields can be accumulated in the nontransparent frequency domain, since the generated fields cannot be efficiently emitted from the region of their generation (the radiation mechanism of the energy loss does not work).

This is in a distinct contrast to the case of transparent medium for which

S_{\text{T}}^{\mathrm{EK}}(\omega)=\frac{1}{2}\sqrt{\varepsilon_{\text{T}}(\omega)}\left[2-\varepsilon_{\text{T}}(\omega)\right]\theta(\omega-\omega_{\text{p}}),

S_{\text{T}}^{B\,\mathrm{reg}}(\omega)=\frac{1}{2}[\varepsilon_{\text{T}}(\omega)]^{3/2}\theta(\omega-\omega_{\text{p}}),

S_{\text{T}}^{B\rdiv}(\omega)=0

and thus
S_{\text{T}}(\omega)=\sqrt{\varepsilon_{\text{T}}(\omega)}\theta(\omega-\omega_{\text{p}})
.

One more essential feature of the energy spectrum outside the transparency domain is that the dominant contribution to the spectrum is given by
S_{\text{T}}^{B\rdiv}(\omega)
, i.e., by the part directly related to the
\mathop{Im}\nolimits\varepsilon_{\text{T}}(\omega)
. Thus, the role of the cut-off wavenumber is very important (as is seen from figures
1
,
2
). Notice that appropriate calculations show that the magnetic fluctuations still dominate in the case of
\varepsilon_{\text{T}}(\omega)
calculated within the quantum approach. It can be explained by the fact that in such a case
\mathop{Im}\nolimits\varepsilon_{\text{T}}(\omega)
decreases considerably only at
k>\sqrt{2mT/\hbar}
.

6
Conclusions

Thus, in the present contribution we derive general relations for the electromagnetic-field energy density in an absorptive medium with temporal and spatial dispersion. The treatment is based on the assumption that the energy density of an electromagnetic perturbation contains both the electromagnetic field energy and the particle energy acquired in the perturbation field. The results obtained provide a possibility to generalize the Planck law to the case of an absorptive dispersive medium. The analysis shows that outside the transparency domain, the dominant contribution to the energy spectrum is given by the magnetic field fluctuations.

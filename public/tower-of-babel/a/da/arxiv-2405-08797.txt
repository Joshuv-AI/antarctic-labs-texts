Introduction

For positive integers
n,k
, let
[n]:=\{1,\dots,n\}
denote the standard
n
-element set and let
{[n]\choose k}:=\{F\subset[n]:|F|=k\}
be the set of its
k
-element subsets. For a set
X
we denote its power set by
2^{X}
. A
family
is a collection of sets
\mathcal{F}\subset 2^{X}
. We say that a family
\mathcal{F}
is
intersecting
if for any
A,B\subset\mathcal{F}
, we have
A\cap B\neq\emptyset
. One of the classical results in extremal combinatorics is the Erdős–Ko–Rado theorem
[
4
]
, which states that for
n\geq 2k
the largest intersecting family in
{[n]\choose k}
has size at most
{n-1\choose k-1}
. For
n>2k
equality holds only if
\mathcal{F}
is of the form
\mathcal{S}_{x}:=\{F\subset{[n]\choose k}:x\in F\}
, for some
x\in[n]
. The
covering number

\tau(\mathcal{F})
of
\mathcal{F}
is the size of the smallest set
X
such that
X\cap F\neq\emptyset
for all
F\in\mathcal{F}
. Then
\mathcal{S}_{x}
has covering number
1
. Such families are called
stars
or
trivial
, and
x
is called their
center
. Hilton and Milner
[
8
]
gave a tight upper bound on the size of intersecting family in
{[n]\choose k}
with covering number at least
2
for
n>2k
:

|\mathcal{F}|\leq{n-1\choose k-1}-{n-k-1\choose k-1}+1.

(1.1)

The extremal families have the property that all but one ‘exceptional’ set can be pierced by a single element.

The following generalization of the Erdős–Ko–Rado theorem was proposed by Erdős
[
3
]
: for given
n,k,s
, determine, what is the largest size of a family
\mathcal{F}\subset{[n]\choose k}
such that
\mathcal{F}
is a union of at most
s
intersecting families. It is a natural conjecture to make that for triples of parameters
(n,k,s)
where
n
is somewhat large in comparison to
k,s
, the extremal example is a union of
s
stars. Erdős indeed conjectured this for all
n\geq 2k+s-1
. In this case,
|\mathcal{F}|={n\choose k}-{n-s\choose k}
. Frankl and Füredi
[
5
]
showed that the conjecture is true for
s=2
and
n>ck
with
c\approx 2.62
. However, it is not true in general. This was shown for
n=2k+s-1
by Hilton and Füredi. Frankl and Füredi also gave an example which shows that the extreme is different even for
s=2
, when
n\leq 2k+c_{0}\sqrt{k}
for
n\geq n_{0}
and some positive constant
c_{0}
. Ellis and Lifshitz
[
2
]
recently proved that for constant
s
and
n>2k+C(s)k^{2/3}
the union of stars is optimal, where
C(s)
is a function of
s
.

It is very natural to interpret this problem in terms of Kneser graphs. Consider a
Kneser graph

KG_{n,k}
, whose vertex set is
{[n]\choose k}
, and edges connect pairs of disjoint sets. Kneser showed that
\chi(KG_{n,k})\leq n-2k+2
and conjectured that this is tight. Lovász proved
[
10
]
that this indeed holds. By now, there is a series of results on the chromatic number of Kneser graphs and different subgraphs (e.g., see the recent paper of Kaiser and Stehlík
[
11
]
, where they construct edge-critical subgraphs of Kneser graphs with the same chromatic number). However, all these proofs are topological and thus are difficult to adapt to other similar settings. The relation to the above question is not difficult to discern. Actually, intersecting families in
{[n]\choose k}
are exactly the independent sets in
KG_{n,k},
and thus are the collections of vertices that can be colored by the same color. In these terms, the question of Erdős may be reformulated as follows: what is the maximum number of vertices of
KG_{n,k}
that can be properly colored in
s
colors?

In this paper, we answer this question in the regime when the number of colors can be very large, but
n
is somewhat large compared to
k
.

Theorem 1
.

Let
\epsilon>0
and let
n,k
be integers such that
n>(2+\epsilon)k^{2}
and
n
is sufficiently large. If
\mathcal{F}
is a union of
s
non-trivial intersecting families,
s<n
, then
|\mathcal{F}|<{n\choose k}-{n-s\choose k}
.

A simple argument using the Hilton–Milner theorem gives that the conclusion of the theorem is valid for, roughly speaking,
n\geq 2k^{3}
. We present it in the next section.

Why do we consider the case when all families are non-trivial? If
\mathcal{F}_{1},\dots,\mathcal{F}_{s}\subset 2^{[n]}
are
s
different stars, then clearly
|\cup_{i\in[s]}\mathcal{F}_{i}|={n\choose k}-{n-s\choose k}
. Now let
\mathcal{F}_{1}
be a star with the center in
1
. Remove
1
from the ground set, also removing all sets which contain
1
, and
\mathcal{F}_{1}
in particular. The problem for

(n,k,s)
is reduced to the problem for
(n-1,k,s-1)
. Assuming that we have
|\cup_{i=2}^{s}\mathcal{F}_{i}|\leq{n-1\choose k}-{n-1-(s-1)\choose k}={n-1\choose k}-{n-s\choose k}
, we get
|\cup_{i\in[s]}\mathcal{F}_{i}|\leq{n-1\choose k-1}+{n-1\choose k}-{n-s\choose k}={n\choose k}-{n-s\choose k}
. If we get rid of the assumption of all families being non-trivial then we get the following corollary.

Theorem 2
.

Let
\epsilon>0
and let
n,s,k
be integers such that
n>(2+\epsilon)k^{2}+s
and
n
is sufficiently large. If
\mathcal{F}
is a union of
s
intersecting families then
|\mathcal{F}|\leq{n\choose k}-{n-s\choose k}
. Equality is possible only if
\mathcal{F}
is a union of
s
stars.

We also show that the bounds in Theorem
1
are not far from being tight.

Theorem 3
.

Let
n=(k-1)(k+1-m)+m
, where
k,m
are positive integers and
m\leq\frac{k}{4}
and
k
is sufficiently large. Then there exists a collection of
s:=n-2k+2-m
non-trivial intersecting families in
{[n]\choose k}
such that their union has size bigger than
{n\choose k}-{n-s\choose k}
.

For the proof of Theorem
1
we rely on the techniques from the paper of Kiselev and Kupavskii
[
12
]
. There, the authors studied colorings of
KG_{n,k}
into
n-2k+2
colors without trivial colors, and managed to show that such colorings do not exist for
n>(2+\epsilon)k^{2}
. The question of Erdős is related to the famous Erdős Matching Conjecture. In terms of
KG_{n,k}
, it asks for the largest subset of vertices with no
(s+1)
-clique. The extremal examples for
n>Csk
are the same as for the problem discussed above, albeit the regimes are different: the EMC becomes trivial for
n<(s+1)k
. See the papers of Frankl, Kolupaev and the second author
[
6
,
13
]
for the most recent results. It is also worth mentioning a recent paper of Frankl and the second author
[
7
]
, in which they studied the size of the largest intersecting family in
{[n]\choose k}
with large
\tau
and showed that there is a certain ‘phase transition’ around
n=k^{2}
.

Another question that we address concerns the choice number of
KG_{n,k}.
Recall that the choice number
ch(G)
of a graph
G
is the smallest
\ell
such that, whenever each vertex of the graph is assigned a list of colors of size at least
\ell,
we can choose one color from the list for each vertex so that the resulting coloring is proper (i.e., does not have monochromatic edges).
Bulankina and Kupavskii
[
1
]
showed that
ch(KG_{n,k})\geq\frac{1}{2\ell^{2}}n\log n
for
k\leq n^{\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{\ell}}
and sufficiently large
n
. They also showed the upper bound
ch(KG_{n,k})\leq n\ln\frac{n}{k}+n,
valid for any
n\geq 2k.
In this paper, we improve their lower bound.

Theorem 4
.

Fix
\varepsilon>0
and let
n\geq n_{0}(\varepsilon)
. Suppose that
3\leq k\leq\left(\frac{n}{1+\varepsilon}\right)^{1/2}
. Then

ch(KG_{n,k})>\left(\frac{1}{4}-\frac{1}{2k}-\varepsilon\right)\frac{n\log{n}}{2}.

The proofs of Theorems
1
and
4
both rely on structural properties of intersecting families that were studied in the paper of Kiselev and the second author
[
12
]
.

Thus, the conclusion of the proposition holds, as long as

Construction of intersecting families whose union is bigger than the union of stars. Red boxes correspond to the parts of the ground set. Their sizes are indicated on the right. Green ellipses are the 2-element sets that define

It is not difficult to see that the families
\mathcal{HM}_{y}^{i}
and
\mathcal{T}_{i}
are all non-trivial intersecting, moreover, their total number is
(k-1)(k-2-m)+k-1=n-2k+2-m.

Let us determine, how many sets
A\in{G\choose k}
are not covered by these families. If
|A\cap T_{i}|\geq 2
then
A
is covered by
\mathcal{T}_{i}
.
y\in A\cap B_{i}
and
|A\cap(T_{i}\sqcup B_{i})|\geq 2
then
A\in\mathcal{HM}_{y}^{i}
. Thus, all
A
with
|A\cap(T_{i}\sqcup B_{i})|\geq 2
are covered by our families.

If
A
is such that
|A\cap(T_{i}\sqcup B_{i})|\leq 1
for all
i\in[k-1]
, then, by the pigeon-hole principle,
|A\cap M|\geq 1
. If at the same time
y\in A\cap B_{i}
for some
i
then
A\in\mathcal{HM}_{y}^{i}
.

Concluding,
A
is not covered by these families if (and only if)
A\subset M\sqcup\bigsqcup_{i=1}^{k-1}T_{i}
and
|A\cap T_{i}|\leq 1
for each
i\in[k-1]
. The number of such sets is

\sum_{j=1}^{m}{m\choose j}{k-1\choose k-j}3^{k-j}.

For
m=1
this sum is equal to
3^{k-1}
. On the other hand, the number of sets not covered by a union of
n-2k+2-m
stars is

{2k-2+m\choose k}.

For
m=1
, it is equal to
(4+o(1))^{k}
. It is not difficult, although tedious, to check that the first expression is smaller than the second for large enough
k
and
m\leq k/4
. We decided to omit these calculations.

2.4
Proof of Theorem
4

We employ the probabilistic method. Put
u:=Cn\log{n}
with some
C
that we specify later on. Take a set of
u
colors and correspond to each vertex of
KG_{n,k}
a random subset of colors of size
\frac{u}{2}
. Any independent set of vertices of
KG_{n,k}
is an intersecting family in
{[n]\choose k}
. Using Corollary
1
, we get that any such independent set is contained in one of the families from
\mathcal{C}
, where
\mathcal{C}
consists of families of the form
\mathcal{A}\cup\mathcal{B}
or
\mathcal{S}_{x}
.
Note that

|\mathcal{C}|\leq{{n\choose 2}\choose k}{{n\choose\sqrt{k}}\choose k^{\sqrt{k}}}+n.

We say that a partition
X=X_{1}\sqcup\dots\sqcup X_{m}
of a set
X

lies in a cover
of this set
X=X^{\prime}_{1}\cup\dots\cup X^{\prime}_{m}
, if

X_{i}\subset X^{\prime}_{i}
for each
i\in[m]
. Hence, any partition of
{[n]\choose k}
lies in one of

|\mathcal{C}|^{u}\leq\left({{n\choose 2}\choose k}{{n\choose\sqrt{k}}\choose k^{\sqrt{k}}}+n\right)^{u}\leq n^{\left(2k+k^{\sqrt{k}+\frac{1}{2}}\right)u}

possible covers, formed by a
u
-tuple of families from
\mathcal{C}
.

For a fixed cover
\mathcal{K}=\mathcal{K}_{1}\cup\dots\cup\mathcal{K}_{u}
from
\mathcal{C}
we are going to bound from above the probability of the event
\mathcal{A_{K}}
that
KG_{n,k}
can be colored in one of the colorings of
{[n]\choose k}
that lies in
\mathcal{K}
and that respects the lists of colors assigned to the
k
-sets.

First, we show that families of type
\mathcal{B}
cover only a small part of vertices of
KG_{n,k}
. Consider an arbitrary family of type
\mathcal{B}
. Clearly, it covers less than
k^{\sqrt{k}}{n-\sqrt{k}\choose k-\sqrt{k}}

k
-sets. We have at most
u
families of such type, and thus together they can cover at most

uk^{\sqrt{k}}{n-\sqrt{k}\choose k-\sqrt{k}}\leq Cn\log{n}\>k^{\sqrt{k}}\left(\frac{k}{n}\right)^{\sqrt{k}}{n\choose k}

sets. We have

\frac{Cn\log{n}\>k^{2\sqrt{k}}}{n^{\sqrt{k}}}<\frac{1}{6}

for large enough
n
that also satisfies
n>(1+\varepsilon)k^{2}
. Therefore, the number of
k
-sets covered by families of type
\mathcal{B}
is less than
\frac{1}{6}{n\choose k}
.

Second, we analyze, what could be covered by the families
\mathcal{A}
and
\mathcal{S}_{x}
. Consider a random
k
-set
S
from
[n]
. Let
\eta_{1}
denote the number of
x\in S
such that
x
is a center of some

\mathcal{S}_{x}
. Let
\eta_{2}
denote the number of pairs
\{x,y\}\subset S
such that
\{x,y\}
is contained in
\mathcal{E}(\mathcal{A})
for some
\mathcal{A}.
For convenience, we also call such pairs
centers
. Then the random variable
\eta_{1}+\eta_{2}
equals to the number of
centers
of type
\mathcal{S}_{x}
or
\mathcal{A}
in a random
k
-set.

Suppose that there are
u_{1}
families of type
\mathcal{S}_{x}
and
u_{2}
families of type
\mathcal{A}
in the cover
\mathcal{K}
. Using that each family of type
\mathcal{A}
consists of at most
k
pairs and that
n\geq k^{2}
, we can upper bound the expectation of
\eta_{1}+\eta_{2}
:

\E[\eta_{1}+\eta_{2}]\leq u_{1}\frac{k}{n}+u_{2}\frac{k{k\choose 2}}{{n\choose 2}}\leq u_{1}\frac{k}{n}+u_{2}\frac{k^{3}}{n^{2}}\leq u_{1}k/n+u_{2}k/n\leq uk/n.

Using Markov’s inequality, we get that

\Pr\left[\text{ a uniformly random set from }{[n]\choose k}\text{ contains more than }\frac{2uk}{n}\text{ centers }\right]\leq\frac{1}{2}.

Thus, at least half of all
{[n]\choose k}
sets contain at most
\frac{2uk}{n}

centers
. It means that each of such sets can be colored in at most
\frac{2uk}{n}
colors of type
\mathcal{S}_{x}
or
\mathcal{A}
. (At this point, we ignore the color lists assigned to the vertices.) From the previous paragraph, we also know that most of them cannot be colored in colors of type
\mathcal{B}
. Let
\mathcal{X}
denote the family of sets which can only be colored in at most
\frac{2uk}{n}
colors of types
\mathcal{S}_{x}
or
\mathcal{A}
. Then

|\mathcal{X}|\geq\frac{1}{3}{n\choose k}.

Given
X\in\mathcal{X}
and the color list assigned to
X
, let
B_{X,\mathcal{K}}
stand for the event that
X
cannot be colored with a coloring that lies in
\mathcal{K}
.
X
can be colored in color
j
, where
\mathcal{K}_{j}
of type
\mathcal{S}_{x}
or
\mathcal{A}
only if
j
belongs to the randomly chosen subset of
\frac{u}{2}
colors that was assigned to
\mathcal{X}
. There are
2uk/n=2Ck\log_{2}n=:z
colors that contain
X
. Then the probability
p
of the event
B_{X,\mathcal{K}}
is at least the probability that the colors for
X
were chosen from the
complement of the set of possible colors:

p\geq\frac{{u-z\choose u/2}}{{u\choose u/2}}=2^{-z}(1-o(1))\geq\frac{1}{2}n^{-2Ck}.

Recall that
\mathcal{A_{K}}
is the event that
KG_{n,k}
can be colored in one of the colorings of
{[n]\choose k}
that lies in
\mathcal{K}
and that respects the lists of colors assigned to the
k
-sets. We have
\mathcal{A}_{\mathcal{K}}\subset\bigcap_{X\in\mathcal{X}}\bar{B}_{X,\mathcal{K}}
, and thus

\Pr[\mathcal{A}_{\mathcal{K}}]\leq(1-p)^{|\mathcal{X}|}\leq e^{-p|\mathcal{X}|}.

We have

p|\mathcal{X}|\geq\frac{1}{6}n^{-2Ck}{n\choose k}\geq\frac{1}{6}n^{-2Ck}\left(\frac{n}{k}\right)^{k}\geq n^{k/2-2Ck},

for large enough
n>(1+\varepsilon)k^{2}
, therefore

\Pr[\mathcal{A}_{\mathcal{K}}]\leq e^{-n^{k/2-2Ck}}.

Let
U
denote the event that there exists a proper coloring of
KG_{n,k}
that respects the lists assigned to the vertices.

To conclude the proof, it is enough to verify the last inequality below.

\Pr[U]\leq\sum_{\mathcal{K}\in\mathcal{C}^{u}}\Pr[\mathcal{A}_{\mathcal{K}}]\leq n^{\left(2k+k^{\sqrt{k}+\frac{1}{2}}\right)u}\cdot e^{-n^{k/2-2Ck}}<1.

Comparing the exponents, it is equivalent to

2k+k^{\sqrt{k}+1/2}<\frac{n^{k/2-1-2Ck}}{C\log^{2}{n}}.

The RHS is at least
2n^{k(1/2-2C)-1-\delta}
for an arbitrary
\delta>0
and
n\geq n_{0}(\delta)
, and the LHS is at most
2k^{\sqrt{k}+1/2}
. Thus, it is sufficient to check the inequality

k^{\sqrt{k}+1/2}<n^{k(1/2-2C)-1-\delta}.

(2.5)

It is easy to check that it holds if
1/2-2C\geq\delta+\frac{1}{k}
and
n
sufficiently large. The inequality we get on
C
is
C\leq\frac{1}{4}-\frac{1}{2k}-\epsilon.
For
k=3
, for example, this gives
\frac{1}{12}-\epsilon.
The theorem is proved.

Chow rings and augmented Chow rings of uniform matroids and their q q -analogs
Hsin-Chieh Liao

Abstract
. We study the Hilbert series and the representations of 𝔖 n {\mathfrak{S}}_{n} and G ​ L n ​ ( 𝔽 q ) GL_{n}(\mathbb{F}_{q}) on the (augmented) Chow rings of uniform matroids U r , n U_{r,n} and q q -uniform matroids U r , n ​ ( q ) U_{r,n}(q) . The Frobenius series for uniform matroids and their q q -analogs are computed. As a byproduct, we recover Hameister, Rao, and Simpson’s formula for the Hilbert series of Chow rings of q q -uniform matroids in terms of permutations and further obtain their augmented counterpart in terms of decorated permutations. We also show that the equivariant Charney–Davis quantity of the (augmented) Chow ring of a matroid is nonnegative (i.e., a genuine representation of a group of automorphisms of the matroid). When the matroid is a uniform matroid and the group is 𝔖 n {\mathfrak{S}}_{n} , the representation either vanishes or is a Foulkes representation (i.e., a Specht module of a ribbon shape). Specializing to the usual Charney–Davis quantities, we obtain an elegant combinatorial interpretation of Hameister, Rao, and Simpson’s formula for Chow rings of q q -uniform matroids and its augmented counterpart.



1.
Introduction

Matroids are combinatorial structures that unify various notions of independence across mathematics. Given their generality, they arise in a wide range of settings. Perhaps the most familiar example is from linear algebra, where a matroid abstracts the concept of linear independence of a collection of vectors; such matroids are called linear or realizable. Other important classes include algebraic matroids, coming from algebraic independence relations in field extensions, graphical matroids, whose bases are the forests of a graph, and transversal matroids, defined by partial matchings in a bipartite graph, etc.

Recently, the interactions between matroid theory and algebraic geometry have led to the resolution of several long-standing conjectures about unimodality, log-concavity, and top-heaviness of combinatorial sequences coming from matroids.
In particular, Adiprasito, Huh, and Katz
[
AHK18
]
proved the long-standing Heron-Rota-Welsh conjecture, which asserts the log-concavity of the Whitney numbers of the first kind for a matroid.
Following the seminal paper
[
AHK18
]
, Braden, Huh, Matherne, Proudfoot, and Wang
[
BHM+20
]
resolved the Dowling–Wilson conjecture, which states that the Whitney numbers of the second kind of a matroid are top-heavy.
The Chow ring
A(M)
and the augmented Chow ring
\widetilde{A}(M)

M
play important roles in the respective proofs of the two conjectures.
They were shown to satisfy the so-called “

Poincaré duality

Hard Lefschetz property
, and the
Hodge-Riemann relations

Since then, extensive studies of the Chow rings of matroids and their extensions have been conducted, for example
[

1.1.
Hilbert series:

The augmented counterpart we establish involves the combinatorics parallel to the Eulerian story, which we refer to as the

Theorem 1.3
.

\displaystyle 1+t\sum_{j=0}^{r-1}{n\brack j}_{q}A_{j}(q,t)(1+t+\ldots+t^{r-j-1})

(ii)

\displaystyle\sum_{\sigma\in\widetilde{{\mathfrak{S}}}_{n}}q^{{\mathsf{maj}}(\sigma)-{\mathsf{exc}}(\sigma)}t^{{\mathsf{exc}}(\sigma)+1}-\sum_{j=r}^{n-1}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\sigma\in\widetilde{\mathfrak{S}}_{n}\\

1.2.
Frobenius series: uniform matroids

Parallel to the

Theorem 1.6
.

The graded Frobenius series of the Chow ring of an arbitrary uniform matroid

(i)

\displaystyle\sum_{j=0}^{r-1}h_{n-j}Q_{j}^{0}({\mathbf{x}},t)(1+t+\cdots+t^{r-j-1})

(ii)

\displaystyle Q_{n}({\mathbf{x}},t)-\sum_{j=r}^{n-1}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\sigma\in{\mathfrak{S}}_{n}\\
{\mathsf{fix}}(\sigma)\geq n-j\end{subarray}}F_{{\mathsf{DEX}}(\sigma),n}t^{j-{\mathsf{exc}}(\sigma)}

Corollary 1.7

For the special case that

(i)

\displaystyle{\mathsf{grFrob}}(A(U_{n,n})_{\mathbb{C}},t)=Q_{n}({\mathbf{x}},t),

\displaystyle{\mathsf{grFrob}}(A(U_{n-1,n})_{\mathbb{C}},t)=t^{-1}Q_{n}^{0}({\mathbf{x}},t).

1.3.
Equivariant Charney–Davis quantities for general matroids

In our study of the Charney–Davis quantities, we generalize Hameister, Rao, and Simpson’s formula (
1

Denote by

{\mathsf{Even}}(k)=\{2,4,6,\ldots,k\},\quad{\mathsf{Odd}}(\ell)=\{1,3,5,\ldots,\ell\}.

Let
G
be a subgroup of the automorphism group
{\mathrm{Aut}}(M)
of a loopless matroid
M
.
Consider the
G
-equivariant Hilbert series of

.
Then the following theorem gives an interpretation for the

Theorem 1.10
.

Let
M
be a (loopless) matroid on
[n]

\sum_{i=0}^{r-1}(-1)^{i}A^{i}(M)_{\mathbb{C}}\cong_{G}\begin{cases}0&\text{ if $r$ is even}\\
(-1)^{\frac{r-1}{2}}\tilde{H}_{\frac{r-3}{2}}\left({\mathcal{L}}(M)_{{\mathsf{Even}}(r-1)}\right)&\text{ if $r$ is odd}\end{cases}.

In particular, the

Theorem 1.11

Let

[n]
of rank

\sum_{i=0}^{r}(-1)^{i}\widetilde{A}^{i}(M)_{\mathbb{C}}\cong_{G}\begin{cases}(-1)^{\frac{r}{2}}\tilde{H}_{\frac{r-2}{2}}\left({\mathcal{L}}(M)_{{\mathsf{Odd}}(r-1)}\right)&\text{ if $r$ is even}\\

2.
Background

2.1.
Matroids

We first briefly review some basics of matroids.
For a more thorough introduction to matroids, we refer the readers to Oxley
[
Oxl11
]
. Some familiarity with terminologies on partially ordered sets (posets) is assumed. See Stanley
[
Sta11
, Chapter 3]
for a good reference.
There are many ways to define a matroid, and they are
cryptomorphic
to each other (equivalent, but not obviously). Due to our purpose, here we define a matroid in terms of its
flats
.

Definition 2.1
.

A matroid
M=(E,{\mathcal{L}}(M))
consists of a finite ground set
E
and a collection of subsets
{\mathcal{L}}(M)\subseteq 2^{E}
satisfy the following axioms:

(
F_{1}
)

E\in{\mathcal{L}}(M)
.

(
F_{2}
)

If
F
,
F^{\prime}\in{\mathcal{L}}(M)
, then
F\cap F^{\prime}\in{\mathcal{L}}(M)
.

(
F_{3}
)

For any
F\in{\mathcal{L}}(M)
and any
i\in E\setminus F
, there exists a unique
F^{\prime}
containing
i
and
covering

F
, in the sense that
F\subsetneq F^{\prime}
and no other
F^{\prime\prime}\in{\mathcal{L}}(M)
such that
F\subsetneq F^{\prime\prime}\subsetneq F^{\prime}
.

The subsets in
{\mathcal{L}}(M)
are called the
flats
of
M
.
These axioms are the abstraction of the model where
E
is a collection of vectors
v_{1},\ldots,v_{n}
in a vector space over some field
\mathbb{F}
, and
{\mathcal{L}}(M)
is the set of subspaces
\{\mathrm{span}_{\mathbb{F}}\{v_{i}\}_{i\in S}:S\subseteq E\}
in which each subspace
W
is represented by the maximal subcollection of
v_{1},\ldots,v_{n}
that spans
W
.

The axioms
(F_{1})
,
(F_{2})
imply that
{\mathcal{L}}(M)
with the inclusion order forms a lattice with
meet

F\wedge F^{\prime}=F\cap F^{\prime}
and
join

F\vee F^{\prime}=\bigcap_{F^{\prime\prime}\supseteq F,F^{\prime}}F^{\prime\prime}
for any
F
,
F^{\prime}\in{\mathcal{L}}(M)
.
The axiom
(F_{3})
further implies that
{\mathcal{L}}(M)
is a
geometric lattice
. We call
{\mathcal{L}}(M)
the
lattice of flats of
M
. It is well-known
[
Sta11
, Chapter 3]
that any finite geometric lattice is a lattice of flats of some matroid.

Denote
{\mathrm{rk}}_{M}
the rank function of
{\mathcal{L}}(M)
. We say the
rank
of the matroid
M
is
r
if
{\mathrm{rk}}_{M}(E)=r
.
For any subset
S\subseteq E
, the closure
\overline{S}
of
S
is the minimal flat containing
S
; in other words,
\overline{S}=\bigcap_{F\supseteq S,F\in{\mathcal{L}}(M)}F
. Then one can extend the rank function
{\mathrm{rk}}_{M}
to
2^{E}\rightarrow\mathbb{N}
by defining
{\mathrm{rk}}_{M}(S)\coloneqq{\mathrm{rk}}_{M}(\overline{S})
for any
S\subseteq E
. We call an element
i\in E
a
loop
if
{\mathrm{rk}}_{M}(i)=0
.

A permutation
\sigma
on
E
is an
automorphism of the matroid

M
if
{\mathrm{rk}}_{M}(\sigma(X))={\mathrm{rk}}_{M}(X)
for all subsets
X\subseteq E
.
Denote
{\mathrm{Aut}}(M)
the set of automorphisms of
M
.
In particular, an automorphism
\sigma\in{\mathrm{Aut}}(M)
maps a flat
F\in{\mathcal{L}}(M)
to a flat
\sigma(F)\in{\mathcal{L}}(M)
and
{\mathrm{rk}}_{M}(\sigma(F))={\mathrm{rk}}_{M}(F)
for all
F\in{\mathcal{L}}(M)
. Hence, an automorphism
\sigma
permutes the chains in
{\mathcal{L}}(M)
.

In this paper, most of the time we will focus on the uniform matroids and their
q
-analogs.
The
uniform matroid

U_{r,n}
(

) is a matroid on

of rank

\{S\subseteq[n]:|S|\leq r-1\}\cup\{[n]\}.

The lattice of flats

In particular, when

is the Boolean lattice

, hence

is also called a Boolean matroid.

2.2.
Chow rings and augmented Chow rings of matroids

In the following, we assume
M
to be a loopless matroid.

Definition 2.3
.

Let
S
be the polynomial ring
\mathbb{Q}[x_{F}:F\in{\mathcal{L}}(M)\setminus\{\emptyset\}]
. The
Chow ring

A(M)
of a matroid

is a quotient
\mathbb{Q}
-algebra

A(M)\coloneqq S/(I+J)

where
I
and
J
are the following ideals of

I=\left\langle x_{F}x_{F^{\prime}}:F,F^{\prime}\text{ are incomparable in }{\mathcal{L}}(M)\right\rangle\quad\text{and}\quad J=\left\langle\sum_{F:F\ni i}x_{F}:i\in E\right\rangle.

This Chow ring was introduced and studied by Feichtner and Yuzvinsky
[

]

{\mathcal{L}}
equipped with a “building set”

. (The Chow ring
A(M)
is the Chow ring of the lattice
{\mathcal{L}}(M)
of flats with the maximal building set.) In particular, they found a Gröbner basis for the ideal
I+J
, thereby obtaining a basis for
A(M)
consisting of the standard monomials, which we refer to as the

(FY-basis).

Theorem 2.4
.

[

, Corollary 1]

The following set of monomials forms a basis for

1\leq a_{i}\leq{\mathrm{rk}}_{M}(F_{i})-{\mathrm{rk}}_{M}(F_{i-1})-1\end{subarray}\right\}.

(3)

We write
FY^{j}(M)
as the subset of the degree

.

To prove the Dowling–Wilson conjecture, Braden, Huh, Matherne, Proudfoot, and Wang
[

BHM+20

Definition 2.5

[

•

•

In the author’s previous work

(4)

Denote by

When

2.3.
Charney–Davis quantity and its equivariant extension

Let
\mathbb{F}
be any field and
A=\bigoplus_{j\in\mathbb{N}}A^{j}
be an
\mathbb{N}
-graded
\mathbb{F}
-algebra such that each degree-
j
homogeneous component
A^{j}
(
j\in\mathbb{N}
) satisfies
\dim_{\mathbb{F}}A^{j}<\infty
.
Then the Hilbert series of
A
is defined to be

{\mathsf{Hilb}}(A,t)\coloneqq\sum_{j\in\mathbb{N}}\dim_{\mathbb{F}}(A^{j})t^{j}.

We say that the Hilbert series of
A
is
palindromic
, if there is some
n\geq 0
such that
\dim_{\mathbb{F}}(A^{N})=0
for
N>n
and
{\mathsf{Hilb}}(A,t)=\sum_{j=0}^{n}a_{j}t^{j}
with
a_{j}=a_{n-j}
for
j\leq n/2
. When the Hilbert series of
A
is a polynomial of degree
n
, we call the number

CD(A)\coloneqq\begin{cases}(-1)^{\frac{n}{2}}{\mathsf{Hilb}}(A,-1)&\text{ if $n$ is even}\\
{\mathsf{Hilb}}(A,-1)&\text{ if $n$ is odd}\end{cases}

the
Charney–Davis quantity
of
A
. In particular, if
A
has a palindromic Hilbert series of odd degree, then
CD(A)=0
.

This quantity was first introduced by Charney and Davis in
[
CD95
]
in connection with the local form of the Hopf conjecture for piecewise Euclidean cubical manifolds.
The conjecture has an equivalent form stating that the
h
-polynomial
h_{\Delta}(t)
of a simplicial
(2n-1)
-sphere
\Delta
satisfies
(-1)^{n}h_{\Delta}(-1)\geq 0
if
\Delta
is a
flag
simplicial complex.
The value
(-1)^{n}h_{\Delta}(-1)
is now called the Charney–Davis quantity of
\Delta
, and the conjecture itself is known as the
Charney–Davis conjecture
.
For further details, see
[
Zhe19
]
and
[
Ath18
, Section 3]
.

The Chow ring
A(M)
and the augmented Chow ring
\widetilde{A}(M)
admit alternative interpretations of their Charney–Davis quantities.
For a matroid
M
of rank
r
, the Hilbert series of
A(M)
and
\widetilde{A}(M)
are palindromic of degree
r-1
and
r
respectively (see Remark
2.1
). Consequently:

•

If
r
is even, then
CD(A(M))=0
; if
r
is odd, then
CD(A(M))
equals the signature of the quadratic form on
A^{\frac{r-1}{2}}(M)
(see Remark
2.2
).

•

Likewise, if
r
is odd, then
CD(\widetilde{A}(M))=0
; if
r
is even, then
CD(\widetilde{A}(M))
equals the signature of the quadratic form on
\widetilde{A}^{\frac{r}{2}}(M)
.

Remark 2.1
.

Adiprasito, Huh, and Katz
[
AHK18
]
and Braden, Huh, Matherne, Proudfoodt, and Wang
[
BHM+20
]
showed that
A(M)
and
\widetilde{A}(M)
satisfy the so-called
Kähler package
, which includes Poincaré duality, Hard Lefschetz theorem, and Hodge-Riemann relations.
In particular, Poincaré duality for
A(M)
asserts that for each
0\leq i\leq r-1
the multiplication map

A^{i}(M)\times A^{r-1-i}(M)\longrightarrow A^{r-1}(M)

induce an isomorphism

A^{r-1-i}(M)\longrightarrow{\mathrm{Hom}}_{\mathbb{Q}}(A^{i}(M),A^{r-1}(M)).

Composing this with a degree map
\deg:A^{r-1}(M)\longrightarrow\mathbb{Q}
gives
A^{r-1-i}(M)\cong{\mathrm{Hom}}_{\mathbb{Q}}(A^{i}(M),\mathbb{Q})
, so
\dim_{\mathbb{Q}}(A^{i}(M))=\dim_{\mathbb{Q}}(A^{r-1-i}(M))
for all
i
.
Hence, the Hilbert series of
A(M)
is palindromic, and the same argument applies to
\widetilde{A}(M)
.

Remark 2.2
.

In the non-augmented case, if
r
is odd, the multiplication map

Q:A^{\frac{r-1}{2}}(M)\times A^{\frac{r-1}{2}}(M)\longrightarrow A^{r-1}(M)\cong\mathbb{Q},\qquad Q(x)=x^{2}

defines a quadratic form
on
A^{\frac{r-1}{2}}(M)
whose signature, by the the Hodge-Riemann relation for
A(M)
(cf.
[
LR02
, proof of Theorem 1.1]
), is equal to the Charney–Davis quantity of
A(M)
.
Similarly, in the augmented case, when
r
is even, the analogous form on
\widetilde{A}^{\frac{r}{2}}(M)
has signature equal to the Charney–Davis quantity of
\widetilde{A}(M)
.

Suppose now
\mathbb{F}
is the field of complex numbers
\mathbb{C}
and the graded algebra
A=\bigoplus_{i=0}^{n}A^{i}
further carries a graded
\mathbb{C}G
-module structure for a finite group
G
. Let
R_{\mathbb{C}}(G)
be the representation ring (or virtual character ring) of
G
and the
G
-equivariant Hilbert series
{\mathsf{Hilb}}^{G}(A,t)=\sum_{i=0}^{n}A^{i}t^{i}
be a polynomial in
R_{\mathbb{C}}(G)[t]
. Then we may define the
G
-equivariant Charney–Davis quantity
of
A
as

CD^{G}(A)\coloneqq\begin{cases}(-1)^{\frac{n}{2}}{\mathsf{Hilb}}^{G}(A,-1)&\text{ if $n$ is even}\\
{\mathsf{Hilb}}^{G}(A,-1)&\text{ if $n$ is odd}\end{cases}.

The resulting quanitiy
CD^{G}(A)
lies in
R_{\mathbb{C}}(G)
. We say that
CD^{G}(A)
is
nonnegative
if it is from a representation of
G
. One can view
CD^{G}(A)
as an alternating sum of the corresponding characters of
G
, and it is nonnegative if the result of the alternating sum indeed gives a character of a representation of
G
.

2.4.
Symmetric functions and representations of symmetric groups

We assume some familiarity with symmetric functions and the representation theory of the symmetric group
{\mathfrak{S}}_{n}
.
Below, we briefly review how the
ring of symmetric functions

\Lambda
encodes the representations of symmetric groups; see Sagan
[
Sag13
]
and Stanley
[
Sta99
]
for references.

Let
\Lambda_{\mathbb{Z}}
be the ring of symmetric functions of bounded degrees with infinite variables
{\mathbf{x}}=(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3},\ldots)
.
It can be viewed as the polynomial ring
\mathbb{Z}[e_{1},e_{2},e_{3},\ldots]=\mathbb{Z}[h_{1},h_{2},h_{3},\ldots]
where
e_{n}
and
h_{n}
are the
elementary symmetric function
and the
complete homogeneous symmetric function
of degree
n
respectively.
The ring
\Lambda_{\mathbb{Z}}=\bigoplus_{i\geq 0}\Lambda_{i}
, where
\Lambda_{i}
is the space of symmetric functions homogeneous of degree
i
, forms a graded
\mathbb{Z}
-algebra via the usual multiplication.

For any positive integer
n
, let
{\mathcal{C}}({\mathfrak{S}}_{n})
be the space of virtual characters of
{\mathfrak{S}}_{n}
, i.e. the free
\mathbb{Z}
-module generated by the irreducible characters
\chi^{\lambda}
of
{\mathfrak{S}}_{n}
for any partition
\lambda
of
n
, denoted by
\lambda\vdash n
. The irreducible representation corresponding to the character
\chi^{\lambda}
is called the
Specht module
S^{\lambda}
for any partition
\lambda
.
Then the direct sum

{\mathcal{C}}({\mathfrak{S}})\coloneqq\bigoplus_{n\geq 0}{\mathcal{C}}({\mathfrak{S}}_{n})

forms a graded
\mathbb{Z}
-algebra under the
induction product
defined by

\displaystyle*:{\mathcal{C}}({\mathfrak{S}}_{n_{1}})\times{\mathcal{C}}({\mathfrak{S}}_{n_{2}})

\displaystyle\longrightarrow{\mathcal{C}}({\mathfrak{S}}_{n_{1}+n_{2}})

\displaystyle(f,g)

\displaystyle\longmapsto f*g\coloneqq(f\otimes g)\uparrow_{{\mathfrak{S}}_{n_{1}}\times{\mathfrak{S}}_{n_{2}}}^{{\mathfrak{S}}_{n_{1}+n_{2}}}

where for a subgroup
H
of
G
the operation
(-)\uparrow_{H}^{G}
is the usual induction from the class functions of
H
to the class functions of
G
.

There is a
\mathbb{Z}
-algebra isomorphism between
{\mathcal{C}}({\mathfrak{S}})
and
\Lambda_{\mathbb{Z}}
called the
Frobenius characteristic map

{\mathrm{ch}}
such that

\displaystyle{\mathrm{ch}}:{\mathcal{C}}({\mathfrak{S}})

\displaystyle\longrightarrow\Lambda_{\mathbb{Z}}

\displaystyle\mathbf{1}_{{\mathfrak{S}}_{n}}

\displaystyle\longmapsto h_{n}

\displaystyle{\rm sgn}_{{\mathfrak{S}}_{n}}

\displaystyle\longmapsto e_{n}

\displaystyle{\chi}^{\lambda}

\displaystyle\longmapsto s_{\lambda}

where
\mathbf{1}_{{\mathfrak{S}}_{n}}
,
{\rm sgn}_{{\mathfrak{S}}_{n}}
are the trivial and signed characters of
{\mathfrak{S}}_{n}
respectively, and
s_{\lambda}
is the
Schur function
associated to
\lambda
.
To simplify the notations, we sometimes denote the representation by the corresponding character.
For a composition
\nu=(\nu_{1},\nu_{2},\ldots,\nu_{\ell})
of
n
, let
{\mathfrak{S}}_{\nu}
be the corresponding Young subgroup.
Then the
{\mathfrak{S}}_{n}
-permutation module whose pointwise-stabilizer is
{\mathfrak{S}}_{\nu}
is isomorphic to the induced representation
\mathbf{1}_{{\mathfrak{S}}_{\nu}}\uparrow_{{\mathfrak{S}}_{\nu}}^{{\mathfrak{S}}_{n}}
and has the Frobenius characteristic

{\mathrm{ch}}(\mathbf{1}_{{\mathfrak{S}}_{\nu}}\uparrow_{{\mathfrak{S}}_{\nu}}^{{\mathfrak{S}}_{n}})={\mathrm{ch}}(1_{{\mathfrak{S}}_{\nu_{1}}}*1_{{\mathfrak{S}}_{\nu_{2}}}*\ldots*1_{{\mathfrak{S}}_{\nu_{\ell}}})=h_{\nu_{1}}h_{\nu_{2}}\ldots h_{\nu_{\ell}}\eqqcolon h_{\nu}.

Furthermore, the dimension of the permutation module is given by

\dim(\mathbf{1}_{{\mathfrak{S}}_{\nu}}\uparrow_{{\mathfrak{S}}_{\nu}}^{{\mathfrak{S}}_{n}})=\dim(\mathbb{C}[{\mathfrak{S}}_{n}/{\mathfrak{S}}_{\nu}])=|{\mathfrak{S}}_{n}:{\mathfrak{S}}_{\nu}|=\frac{n!}{\nu_{1}!\ldots\nu_{\ell}!}=\binom{n}{\nu_{1},\ldots,\nu_{\ell}}.

Let
V=\bigoplus_{i}V^{i}
be a graded
\mathbb{C}[{\mathfrak{S}}_{n}]
-module. The
graded Frobenius series
of
V
is defined to be
{\mathsf{grFrob}}(V,t)\coloneqq\sum_{i}{\mathrm{ch}}(V^{i})t^{i}
. In this way, one can encode representations of
{\mathfrak{S}}_{n}
in terms of symmetric functions via
{\mathrm{ch}}
and this will be the method we apply to study the representations of
{\mathfrak{S}}_{n}
in this paper.

2.5.
Eulerian polynomials for derangements, permutations, and decorated permutations and their extensions

2.5.1.
Eulerian story

For any permutation
\sigma=\sigma_{1}\sigma_{2}\ldots\sigma_{n}
in
{\mathfrak{S}}_{n}
, one can consider the following statistics:

•

Excedance set

{\mathsf{EXC}}(\sigma)\coloneqq\{i\in[n-1]:\sigma_{i}>i\}
and
excedance number

{\mathsf{exc}}(\sigma)\coloneqq|{\mathsf{EXC}}(\sigma)|
.

•

Descent set

{\mathsf{DES}}(\sigma)\coloneqq\{i\in[n-1]:\sigma_{i}>\sigma_{i+1}\}
and
descent number

{\mathsf{des}}(\sigma)\coloneqq|{\mathsf{DES}}(\sigma)|
.

•

Inversion number

{\mathsf{inv}}(\sigma)\coloneqq|\{(i,j)\in[n]\times[n]:i<j,~\sigma_{i}>\sigma_{j}\}|
.

•

Major index

{\mathsf{maj}}(\sigma)\coloneqq\sum_{i\in{\mathsf{DES}}(\sigma)}i
.

It is well known that
{\mathsf{exc}}
and
{\mathsf{des}}
are equidistributed statistics on
{\mathfrak{S}}_{n}
and belong to the class of
Eulerian statistics
. The generating function of their distribution

A_{n}(t)\coloneqq\sum_{\sigma\in{\mathfrak{S}}_{n}}t^{{\mathsf{des}}(\sigma)}=\sum_{\sigma\in{\mathfrak{S}}_{n}}t^{{\mathsf{exc}}(\sigma)}

(5)

is known as the
Eulerian polynomial
and its coefficients are called the
Eulerian numbers
.
Recall that
D_{n}
is the subset of derangements in
{\mathfrak{S}}_{n}
, i.e., permutations without fixed points.
Similarly, we can consider the analogous Eulerian polynomial for derangements

d_{n}(t)\coloneqq\sum_{\sigma\in D_{n}}t^{{\mathsf{exc}}(\sigma)}.

Led by their study on Rees product of posets, Shareshian and Wachs
[
SW10
]
studied certain
q
-analogs and symmetric function analogs of the Eulerian polynomials
A_{n}(t)
and
d_{n}(t)
, which we will introduce in the following.

Definition 2.7
.

Let
[\overline{n}]\coloneqq\{\overline{1},\overline{2},\ldots,\overline{n}\}
and assign to
[n]\cup[\overline{n}]
the total order
\overline{1}<\ldots<\overline{n}<1<\ldots<n
. Let
\overline{\sigma}=\overline{\sigma}_{1}\ldots\overline{\sigma}_{n}
be a word over
[n]\cup[\overline{n}]
obtained from
\sigma
by letting
\overline{\sigma}_{i}=\overline{j}
if
\sigma_{i}=j
and
i\in{\mathsf{EXC}}(\sigma)
, letting
\overline{\sigma}_{i}=\sigma_{i}
if
i\notin{\mathsf{EXC}}(\sigma)
. Define the set statistic

{\mathsf{DEX}}(\sigma)\coloneqq{\mathsf{DES}}(\overline{\sigma}).

The statistic
{\mathsf{DEX}}
is a variant of descent set
{\mathsf{DES}}
and has intriguing properties that connect the classical statistics
{\mathsf{maj}}
and
{\mathsf{exc}}
.

Theorem 2.8
.

[
SW10
, Lemma 2.2]

For all
\sigma\in{\mathfrak{S}}_{n}
,

\sum_{i\in{\mathsf{DEX}}(\sigma)}i={\mathsf{maj}}(\sigma)-{\mathsf{exc}}(\sigma)

and

|{\mathsf{DEX}}(\sigma)|=\begin{cases}{\mathsf{des}}(\sigma)&\text{ if }\sigma(1)=1\\
{\mathsf{des}}(\sigma)-1&\text{ if }\sigma(1)\neq 1\end{cases}.

For an integer
n\geq 1
and
S\subseteq[n-1]
, define Gessel’s
fundamental quasisymmetric function

F_{S,n}({\mathbf{x}})\coloneqq\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}i_{1}\geq i_{2}\geq\ldots\geq i_{n}\\
j\in S\Rightarrow i_{j}>i_{j+1}\end{subarray}}x_{i_{1}}x_{i_{2}}\ldots x_{i_{n}}

and
F_{\phi,0}\coloneqq 1
. In particular, when
S=\emptyset
,
F_{\emptyset,n}=h_{n}
; when
S=[n-1]
,
F_{[n-1],n}=e_{n}
.

Definition 2.9
.

For integer
n\geq 1
, define

Q_{n}({\mathbf{x}},t)=\sum_{\sigma\in{\mathfrak{S}}_{n}}F_{{\mathsf{DEX}}(\sigma),n}({\mathbf{x}})t^{{\mathsf{exc}}(\sigma)}

and

Q_{n}^{0}({\mathbf{x}},t)=\sum_{\sigma\in D_{n}}F_{{\mathsf{DEX}}(\sigma),n}({\mathbf{x}})t^{{\mathsf{exc}}(\sigma)}.

It is useful to set
Q_{0}({\mathbf{x}},t)\coloneqq 1
. For
0\leq j\leq n-1
, we denote by
Q_{n,j}({\mathbf{x}})
(resp.
Q^{0}_{n,j}({\mathbf{x}})
) the quasisymmetric function coefficient of
t^{j}
in
Q_{n}({\mathbf{x}},t)
(resp.
Q^{0}_{n}({\mathbf{x}},t)
.

Shareshian and Wachs showed that
Q_{n,j}({\mathbf{x}})
and
Q^{0}_{n,j}({\mathbf{x}})
are symmetric functions for all
j
.
The polynomials
Q_{n}({\mathbf{x}},t)
and
Q^{0}_{n}({\mathbf{x}},t)
can be viewed as symmetric-function analogs of the Eulerian polynomials
A_{n}(t)
and
d_{n}(t)
, respectively.
The functions
Q_{n,j}({\mathbf{x}})
and
Q^{0}_{n,j}({\mathbf{x}})
are the symmetric-function analogs of the Eulerian numbers and were referred to as the
Eulerian quasisymmetric function
in
[
SW10
]
.

The symmetric function
Q_{n,j}({\mathbf{x}})
is
h
-positive (has positive coefficients when expanded as a linear combination of
\{h_{\lambda}\}_{\lambda\vdash n}
) and appears as the Frobenius characteristic of several natural
{\mathfrak{S}}_{n}
-modules, including the cohomology of the
permutahedral variety

[
Sta89
,
Ste92
,
Ste94
]
, the Chow ring of the Boolean matroid
\mathsf{B}_{n}=U_{n,n}
, which is isomorphic (as a ring) to that cohomology
[
Lia23
,
Lia24b
]
, and the poset homology of Rees product of the Boolean lattice with certain tree-like posets
[
SW09
,
LSW12
,
Ath20
]
.
In particular, identifying
Q_{n,j}({\mathbf{x}})
with the Frobenius characteristic of the permutation representation on the Chow ring
A(\mathsf{B}_{n})
yields the following theorem.

Theorem 2.10
.

[
Lia24b
, Proposition 3.5, Theorem 3.6]

For

, the degree

is a permutation representation of

and

{\mathrm{ch}}(\mathbb{C}{\mathfrak{S}}_{n}FY^{j}(\mathsf{B}_{n}))=Q_{n,j}({\mathbf{x}}).

The
stable principal specialization
of a (quasi)symmetric function

is defined by

. The following lemma is implicit in the proof of Lemma 5.2 in

Lemma 2.11
.

For all

,

{\mathrm{ps}_{q}}(F_{S,n})=\frac{q^{\sum_{i\in S}i}}{(1-q)(1-q^{2})\ldots(1-q^{n})}.

It follows from Lemma

that Theorem

implies the following corollary.

Corollary 2.12

For all

By Corollary

, the stable principal specialization of
Q_{n}({\mathbf{x}},t)
and
Q_{n}^{0}({\mathbf{x}},t)
gives the following interesting

and

.

Theorem 2.13
.

For all
n\geq 1
,

(a)

\prod_{i=1}^{n}\left(1-q^{i}\right){\mathrm{ps}_{q}}(Q_{n}(\mathbf{x},t))=\sum_{\sigma\in{\mathfrak{S}}_{n}}q^{{\mathsf{maj}}(\sigma)-{\mathsf{exc}}(\sigma)}t^{{\mathsf{exc}}(\sigma)}
,

(b)

\prod_{i=1}^{n}\left(1-q^{i}\right){\mathrm{ps}_{q}}(Q_{n}^{0}(\mathbf{x},t))=\sum_{\sigma\in D_{n}}q^{{\mathsf{maj}}(\sigma)-{\mathsf{exc}}(\sigma)}t^{{\mathsf{exc}}(\sigma)}
.

Throughout this paper, we denote

A_{n}(q,t)\coloneqq\sum_{\sigma\in{\mathfrak{S}}_{n}}q^{{\mathsf{maj}}(\sigma)-{\mathsf{exc}}(\sigma)}t^{{\mathsf{exc}}(\sigma)},

(6)

d_{n}(q,t)\coloneqq\sum_{\sigma\in D_{n}}q^{{\mathsf{maj}}(\sigma)-{\mathsf{exc}}(\sigma)}t^{{\mathsf{exc}}(\sigma)}.

2.5.2.
Binomial Eulerian story

The
binomial Eulerian polynomial

\widetilde{A}_{n}(t)\;\coloneqq\;1\;+\;t\sum_{k=1}^{n}\binom{n}{k}A_{k}(t),

named by Shareshian and Wachs
[
SW20
]
, shares many interesting properties with the classical Eulerian polynomial
A_{n}(t)
. In particular, it is palindromic and also arises as the
h
-polynomial of a simple polytope called the
stellohedron
. This polytopal interpretation was first uncovered by Postnikov, Reiner, and Williams
[
PRW08
]
. The palindromicity of
\widetilde{A}_{n}(t)
is equivalent to an intriguing symmetrical Eulerian identity studied by Chung, Graham, and Knuth
[
CGK10
]
.

A natural
q
-analog and a symmetric‑function analogue of
\widetilde{A}_{n}(t)
were introduced by Shareshian and Wachs
[
SW20
]
:

\widetilde{A}_{n}(q,t)\coloneqq 1+t\sum_{k=1}^{n}{n\brack k}_{q}A_{k}(q,t),

(7)

\widetilde{Q}_{n}({\mathbf{x}},t)\coloneqq h_{n}({\mathbf{x}})+t\sum_{k=1}^{n}h_{n-k}({\mathbf{x}})Q_{k}({\mathbf{x}},t).

(8)

Just as
Q_{n}({\mathbf{x}},t)
is the graded Frobenius series of the natural
{\mathfrak{S}}_{n}
-representation on the cohomology ring of the permutahedral variety, Shareshian and Wachs proved that
\widetilde{Q}_{n}({\mathbf{x}},t)
is the graded Frobenius series of the cohomology of the stellahedral variety, which, by definition (
8
), carries a permutation representation of
{\mathfrak{S}}_{n}
.
This cohomology ring is isomorphic to the augmented Chow ring of the Boolean matroid. In particular, one obtains the following.

Theorem 2.14
.

[
Lia24b
, Theorems 4.6, 4.8]

For
0\leq j\leq n
, the degree
j
component of the augmented Chow ring
\widetilde{A}^{j}(\mathsf{B}_{n})=\mathbb{C}{\mathfrak{S}}_{n}\widetilde{FY}^{j}(\mathsf{B}_{n})
is a permutation representation of
{\mathfrak{S}}_{n}
and

{\mathrm{ch}}(\mathbb{C}{\mathfrak{S}}_{n}\widetilde{FY}^{j}(\mathsf{B}_{n}))=\widetilde{Q}_{n,j}({\mathbf{x}}),

where
\widetilde{Q}_{n,j}({\mathbf{x}})
is the coefficient of
t^{j}
of
\widetilde{Q}_{n}({\mathbf{x}},t)
.

Shareshian and Wachs further showed that
\widetilde{A}_{n}(q,t)
is the appropriate
q
-analog of
\widetilde{A}_{n}(t)
to consider:

\prod_{i=1}^{n}(1-q^{i}){\mathrm{ps}_{q}}(\widetilde{Q}_{n}({\mathbf{x}},t))=\widetilde{A}_{n}(q,t).

(9)

As an analog of the set of permutations
\mathfrak{S}_{n}

A

2.6.
Group actions on rank-selected posets

In this section, we recall some terminology and facts about rank-selected posets from
[
Sta82
]
and
[
Wac07
]
.

Let
P
be a finite poset with a unique minimal element
\hat{0}
and a unique maximal element
\hat{1}
. If
P
is graded of rank
n
with rank function
{\mathrm{rk}}_{P}:P\longrightarrow\{0,1,\ldots,n\}
, then for
S\subseteq[n-1]
, we can define the
rank-selected subposet
of
P
by

P_{S}\coloneqq\{x\in P~:~{\mathrm{rk}}_{P}(x)\in S\}\cup\{\hat{0},\hat{1}\}.

Let
G
be a group of automorphisms of
P
. The action of
G
preserves the rank of the elements in
P
; hence for any
S\subseteq[n-1]
, the group
G
permutes the maximal chains in
P_{S}
. Let us denote
\alpha_{P}(S)
the permutation representation of
G
generated by the maximal chains in
P_{S}
. Consider the virtual representation

\beta_{P}(S)\coloneqq\sum_{T\subseteq S}(-1)^{|S|-|T|}\alpha_{P}(T).

By Möbius inversion, we also have

\alpha_{P}(S)=\sum_{T\subseteq S}\beta_{P}(T).

Theorem 2.20
.

[
Sta82
, Theorem 1.2]

If
P
is Cohen-Macaulay, then
\beta_{P}(S)
is a genuine representation of
G
and

\beta_{P}(S)\cong_{G}\tilde{H}_{|S|-1}(P_{S}).

Throughout this paper, the poset
P
we care about is always the lattice of flats of a matroid. It is well known that the lattice of flats of a matroid is always Cohen-Macaulay (see
[
Fol66
, Theorem 4.1]
and
[
BGS82
, p.14]
).

When
P
is the Boolean lattice
B_{n}
and
G
is the symmetric group
{\mathfrak{S}}_{n}
, the structure of representation
\beta_{B_{n}}(S)\cong\tilde{H}_{|S|-1}({(B_{n})}_{S})
is known for any selected rank set
S\subseteq[n-1]
. Below, we follow the notations from
[
Wac07
, chapter 2]
.

Let
\lambda
,
\mu
be two partitions such that
\mu\subseteq\lambda
(i.e.
\mu_{i}\leq\lambda_{i}
for all
i
). The connected skew shape
\lambda/\mu
is said to be a
ribbon
(or a
skew hook
or a
border strip
) if two consecutive rows always overlap in exactly one cell. Such a skew shape can be described by its number of cells and its
descent set
. Let
H
be a ribbon of
n
cells. Number the cells of
H
from
1
to
n
, filling left to right in each row and filling the rows from bottommost to topmost. We call a cell
i
a
descent
of
H
if cell
i+1
is directly above cell
i
. The descent set of
H
is the set of all descents of
H
. If the descent set of
H
is

, then we denote

by

.

In this section, we prove Theorem

We proceed by calculating the difference of the graded Frobenius series of

between two consecutive rank

\displaystyle\Delta_{n,r}({\mathbf{x}})

\displaystyle\coloneqq{\mathsf{grFrob}}(A(U_{r+1,n})_{\mathbb{C}},t)-{\mathsf{grFrob}}(A(U_{r,n})_{\mathbb{C}},t)

\displaystyle=a_{n,r}^{(1)}({\mathbf{x}})t+a_{n,r}^{(2)}({\mathbf{x}})t^{2}+\cdots+a_{n,r}^{(r-1)}({\mathbf{x}})t^{r-1}+a_{n,r}^{(r)}t^{r}

for

\Delta_{n,r}({\mathbf{x}})
can be expressed nicely in two different ways. Then the graded Frobenuis series of

can be obtained by considering either

{\mathsf{grFrob}}(A(U_{r,n})_{\mathbb{C}},t)={\mathsf{grFrob}}(A(U_{1,n})_{\mathbb{C}},t)+\sum_{j=1}^{r-1}\Delta_{n,j}({\mathbf{x}})

{\mathsf{grFrob}}(A(U_{r,n})_{\mathbb{C}},t)={\mathsf{grFrob}}(A(U_{n,n})_{\mathbb{C}},t)-\sum_{j=r}^{n-1}\Delta_{n,j}({\mathbf{x}}),

which with a proper choice of expression of
\Delta_{n,j}({\mathbf{x}})
leads to Theorem
1.6
(i) and (ii) respectively.

Before proving our main results, we collect several facts that will be useful for expressing
\Delta_{n,r}({\mathbf{x}})
.
In Theorem
2.10
, we saw that the graded Frobenius series of the Chow ring of the Boolean matroid coincides with the Eulerian quasisymmetric function

{\mathsf{grFrob}}(A(\mathsf{B}_{n})_{\mathbb{C}},t)=Q_{n}({\mathbf{x}},t)=\sum_{\sigma\in{\mathfrak{S}}_{n}}F_{{\mathsf{DEX}}(\sigma),n}({\mathbf{x}})t^{{\mathsf{exc}}(\sigma)},

whose generating function admits a closed form (
[
SW10
, Theorem 1.2]
):

1+\sum_{n\geq 1}Q_{n}({\mathbf{x}},t)z^{n}=\frac{(1-t)H(z)}{H(tz)-tH(z)},

(10)

where
H(z)=\sum_{n\geq 0}h_{n}({\mathbf{x}})z^{n}
.
In fact, Shareshian and Wachs further refined this by tracking fixed points.
They defined

Q_{n,j,k}({\mathbf{x}})\coloneqq\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\sigma\in{\mathfrak{S}}_{n}\\
{\mathsf{exc}}(\sigma)=j,~{\mathsf{fix}}(\sigma)=k\end{subarray}}F_{{\mathsf{DEX}}(\sigma),n}({\mathbf{x}})

and proved that

1+\sum_{n\geq 1,j,k\geq 0}Q_{n,j,k}({\mathbf{x}})t^{j}r^{k}z^{n}=\frac{(1-t)H(rz)}{H(tz)-tH(z)}.

We give a refinement of Theorem
2.10
regarding
Q_{n,j,k}({\mathbf{x}})
that will be used to express
\Delta_{n,r}({\mathbf{x}})
.
Let the subset

FY^{j,k}(\mathsf{B}_{n})\coloneqq\left\{x_{F_{1}}^{a_{1}}\ldots x_{F_{\ell}}^{a_{\ell}}\in FY^{j}(\mathsf{B}_{n}):|[n]-F_{\ell}|=k\right\}

(11)

and
A^{j,k}(\mathsf{B}_{n})
be the subspace spanned by
FY^{j,k}(\mathsf{B}_{n})
, which is obviously
{\mathfrak{S}}_{n}
-invariant.

Proposition 3.1
.

For
0\leq j\leq n-1
and
0\leq k\leq n
, we have

{\mathrm{ch}}(A^{j,k}(\mathsf{B}_{n})_{\mathbb{C}})=Q_{n,j,k}({\mathbf{x}}).

Proof.

To prove the result, we use combinatorial objects only needed in this proof called
Stembridge codes
. The readers are referred to Section 3.1 and Theorem 3.6 in
[
Lia24b
]
for their definition and an
{\mathfrak{S}}_{n}
-equivariant bijection between Stembridge codes and
FY(\mathsf{B}_{n})
. Stembridge
[
Ste92
, Lemma 3.1]
showed that the set
{\mathtt{Code}}_{n,j}
of Stembridge codes with index
j
generated a
\mathbb{C}{\mathfrak{S}}_{n}
-module satisfying

1+\sum_{n\geq 1}\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}{\mathrm{ch}}(\mathbb{C}{\mathtt{Code}}_{n,j})t^{j}z^{n}=\frac{(1-t)H(z)}{H(tz)-tH(z)}.

Stembridge’s calculation actually extends without effort to

1+\sum_{n\geq 1,j,k\geq 0}{\mathrm{ch}}\left(\mathbb{C}{\mathtt{Code}}_{n,j,k}\right)t^{j}r^{k}z^{n}=\frac{(1-t)H(rz)}{H(tz)-tH(z)},

or equivalently,
{\mathrm{ch}}(\mathbb{C}{\mathtt{Code}}_{n,j,k})=Q_{n,j,k}({\mathbf{x}})
, where
{\mathtt{Code}}_{n,j,k}
is the subset of Stembridge codes of index
j
and
k
zeros.
Consider the
{\mathfrak{S}}_{n}
-equivariant bijection
\phi
between
FY(\mathsf{B}_{n})
and Stembridge codes in
[
Lia24b
, Theorem 3.6]
. The restriction of
\phi
to
FY^{j,k}(\mathsf{B}_{n})
gives an
{\mathfrak{S}}_{n}
-equivariant bijection
FY^{j,k}(\mathsf{B}_{n})\longrightarrow{\mathtt{Code}}_{n,j,k}
. Hence we have

{\mathrm{ch}}(A^{j,k}(\mathsf{B}_{n}))={\mathrm{ch}}(\mathbb{C}{\mathtt{Code}}_{n,j,k})=Q_{n,j,k}({\mathbf{x}}).

∎

We need the following two properties of
Q_{n,j,k}
. The first one is about the palindromicity of
Q_{n,j,k}
and is a special case of
[
SW10
, Theorem 5.9]
.

Lemma 3.2
.

For positive integers
n
,
0\leq k\leq n
, and
0\leq j\leq n-k
,

Q_{n,j,k}({\mathbf{x}})=Q_{n,n-k-j,k}({\mathbf{x}}).

Lemma 3.3
.

[
SW10
, Corollary 3.4]

For all
n,j,k
,

Q_{n,j,k}({\mathbf{x}})=h_{k}Q_{n-k,j,0}({\mathbf{x}}).

Now we have the following interpretations of the difference
\Delta_{n,r}({\mathbf{x}})
between
{\mathsf{grFrob}}(A(U_{r+1,n}),t)
and
{\mathsf{grFrob}}(A(U_{r,n}),t)
.

Proposition 3.4
.

For
1\leq r\leq n-1
, the polynomial
\Delta_{n,r}({\mathbf{x}})\in\Lambda_{n}[t]
has two expressions:

\sum_{i=0}^{r}h_{n-r+i}Q_{r-i}^{0}({\mathbf{x}},t)t^{i}

and

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\sigma\in{\mathfrak{S}}_{n}\\
{\mathsf{fix}}(\sigma)\geq n-r\end{subarray}}F_{{\mathsf{DEX}}(\sigma),n}({\mathbf{x}})t^{r-{\mathsf{exc}}(\sigma)}.

Proof.

Note that for
0\leq k\leq n-1
, as sets we have

FY^{k}(U_{1,n})\subseteq FY^{k}(U_{2,n})\subseteq\ldots\subseteq FY^{k}(U_{n,n}).

The coefficient
a_{n,r}^{(k)}({\mathbf{x}})
of
t^{k}
in
\Delta_{n,r}({\mathbf{x}})
is the Frobenius characteristic of the permutation representation generated by
FY^{k}(U_{r+1,n})\setminus FY^{k}(U_{r,n})
, which consists of the monomial

x_{[n]}^{r}\quad\text{ if }k=r

and monomials

x_{F_{1}}^{a_{1}}\ldots x_{F_{\ell}}^{a_{\ell}}x_{[n]}^{i}\in FY^{k}(U_{r+1,n})\quad\text{such that}\quad i=(r+1)-|F_{\ell}|-1=r-|F_{\ell}|

for
i=0,1,\ldots,k-1
if
1\leq k\leq r-1
. Hence
a_{n,r}^{(r)}({\mathbf{x}})=h_{n}
; and for
1\leq k\leq r-1
, one can construct an easy
{\mathfrak{S}}_{n}
-equivariant bijection
x_{F_{1}}^{a_{1}}\ldots x_{F_{\ell}}^{a_{\ell}}x_{[n]}^{i}\mapsto x_{F_{1}}^{a_{1}}\ldots x_{F_{\ell}}^{a_{\ell}}
that maps
FY^{k}(U_{r+1,n})\setminus FY^{k}(U_{r,n})
to the set of monomials
x_{F_{1}}^{a_{1}}\ldots x_{F_{\ell}}^{a_{\ell}}
such that
F_{1}\subsetneq\ldots\subsetneq F_{\ell}
and
a_{1},\ldots,a_{\ell}
satisfy

•

|F_{\ell}|=r-i
,

•

a_{j}\leq|F_{j}|-|F_{j-1}|-1
for all
j
,

•

a_{1}+\ldots+a_{\ell}=k-i
,

for
i=0,1,\ldots,k-1
. Recalling our notation in (
11
), the above set of monomials can be viewed as
\bigcup_{i=0}^{k-1}FY^{k-i,n-r+i}(\mathsf{B}_{n})
; since
FY^{k-i,n-r+i}(\mathsf{B}_{n})
is a permutation basis for
A^{k-i,n-r+i}(\mathsf{B}_{n})_{\mathbb{C}}
, by Proposition
3.1
, the coefficient of
t^{k}
in
\Delta_{n,r}({\mathbf{x}})
is

a_{n,r}^{(k)}({\mathbf{x}})=\sum_{i=0}^{k-1}Q_{n,k-i,n-r+i}.

(12)

Then

\displaystyle\Delta_{n,r}({\mathbf{x}})

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=1}^{r}a_{n,r}^{(k)}({\mathbf{x}})t^{k}=h_{n}t^{r}+\sum_{k=1}^{r-1}\sum_{i=0}^{k-1}h_{n-r+i}Q_{r-i,k-i,0}t^{k}\quad(\text{by Lemma \ref{lem:ExtractHQ}})

\displaystyle=h_{n}t^{r}+\sum_{i=0}^{r-2}h_{n-r+i}\left(\sum_{k=i+1}^{r-1}Q_{r-i,k-i,0}t^{k-i}\right)t^{i}=h_{n}t^{r}+\sum_{i=0}^{r-2}h_{n-r+i}Q_{r-i}^{0}({\mathbf{x}},t)t^{i}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=0}^{r}h_{n-r+i}Q_{r-i}^{0}({\mathbf{x}},t)t^{i}\quad(\text{since }Q_{1}^{0}({\mathbf{x}},t)=0).

Note that
\Delta_{n,1}({\mathbf{x}})=h_{n}t
.

On the other hand, we can obtain a different expression from (
12
) as follows

\displaystyle\Delta_{n,r}({\mathbf{x}})

\displaystyle=h_{n}t^{r}+\sum_{k=1}^{r-1}\sum_{i=0}^{k-1}Q_{n,k-i,n-r+i}t^{k}

\displaystyle=h_{n}t^{r}+\sum_{k=1}^{r-1}\sum_{i=0}^{k-1}Q_{n,r-k,n-r+i}t^{k}\quad(\text{by Lemma \ref{lem:PalinQ}})

\displaystyle=h_{n}t^{r}+\sum_{k=1}^{r-1}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\sigma\in{\mathfrak{S}}_{n}\\
{\mathsf{exc}}(\sigma)=r-k\\
{\mathsf{fix}}(\sigma)\geq n-r\end{subarray}}F_{{\mathsf{DEX}}(\sigma),n}({\mathbf{x}})t^{k}

\displaystyle=F_{{\mathsf{DEX}}(e),n}({\mathbf{x}})t^{r}+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\sigma\in{\mathfrak{S}}_{n}\setminus\{e\}\\
{\mathsf{fix}}(\sigma)\geq n-r\end{subarray}}F_{{\mathsf{DEX}}(\sigma),n}({\mathbf{x}})t^{r-{\mathsf{exc}}(\sigma)}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\sigma\in{\mathfrak{S}}_{n}\\
{\mathsf{fix}}(\sigma)\geq n-r\end{subarray}}F_{{\mathsf{DEX}}(\sigma),n}({\mathbf{x}})t^{r-{\mathsf{exc}}(\sigma)}

where
e
is the identity in
{\mathfrak{S}}_{n}
.
∎

We are ready to prove Theorem
1.6
, which we restate here.

Theorem
1.6
.

The graded Frobenius series of the Chow ring of arbitrary uniform matroids
{\mathsf{grFrob}}(A(U_{r,n})_{\mathbb{C}},t)
has two expressions:

(i)

\displaystyle\sum_{j=0}^{r-1}h_{n-j}Q_{j}^{0}({\mathbf{x}},t)(1+t+\ldots+t^{r-j-1})
,

(ii)

\displaystyle Q_{n}({\mathbf{x}},t)-\sum_{j=r}^{n-1}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\sigma\in{\mathfrak{S}}_{n}\\
{\mathsf{fix}}(\sigma)\geq n-j\end{subarray}}F_{{\mathsf{DEX}}(\sigma),n}t^{j-{\mathsf{exc}}(\sigma)}
.

Proof.

To prove expression (i), we apply the first expression from Proposition
3.4
and obtain

\displaystyle{\mathsf{grFrob}}(A(U_{r,n})_{\mathbb{C}},t)

\displaystyle={\mathsf{grFrob}}(A(U_{1,n})_{\mathbb{C}},t)+\sum_{j=1}^{r-1}\Delta_{n,j}({\mathbf{x}})=h_{n}+\sum_{j=1}^{r-1}\Delta_{n,j}({\mathbf{x}})

\displaystyle=h_{n}+\sum_{j=1}^{r-1}\sum_{i=0}^{j}h_{n-j+i}Q_{j-i}^{0}({\mathbf{x}},t)t^{i}=h_{n}+\sum_{j=1}^{r-1}\sum_{i=0}^{j}h_{n-i}Q_{i}^{0}({\mathbf{x}},t)t^{j-i}

\displaystyle=h_{n}+h_{n}(t+t^{2}+\ldots+t^{r-1})+\sum_{j=1}^{r-1}\sum_{i=1}^{j}h_{n-i}Q_{i}^{0}({\mathbf{x}},t)t^{j-i}

\displaystyle=h_{n}(1+t+\ldots+t^{r-1})+\sum_{i=1}^{r-1}\sum_{j=i}^{r-1}h_{n-i}Q_{i}^{0}({\mathbf{x}},t)t^{j-i}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=0}^{r-1}h_{n-i}Q_{i}^{0}({\mathbf{x}},t)(1+t+\ldots+t^{r-1-i}).

To prove expression (ii), we apply the second expression from Proposition
3.4
and obtain

\displaystyle{\mathsf{grFrob}}(A(U_{r,n})_{\mathbb{C}},t)

\displaystyle={\mathsf{grFrob}}(A(U_{n,n})_{\mathbb{C}},t)-\sum_{j=r}^{n-1}\Delta_{n,j}({\mathbf{x}})

\displaystyle=Q_{n}({\mathbf{x}},t)-\sum_{j=r}^{n-1}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\sigma\in{\mathfrak{S}}_{n}\\
{\mathsf{fix}}(\sigma)\geq n-j\end{subarray}}F_{{\mathsf{DEX}}(\sigma),n}t^{j-{\mathsf{exc}}(\sigma)}.

∎

Now Corollary
1.7
, which we restate here, can be proved directly from Theorem
1.6
.

Corollary
1.7
.

For the special case that
r=n
and
r=n-1
we have

(i)

\displaystyle{\mathsf{grFrob}}(A(U_{n,n})_{\mathbb{C}},t)=Q_{n}({\mathbf{x}},t).

(ii)

\displaystyle{\mathsf{grFrob}}(A(U_{n-1,n})_{\mathbb{C}},t)=t^{-1}Q_{n}^{0}({\mathbf{x}},t).

Proof.

When
r=n
, the matroid is Boolean. See Remark
1.1
. When
r=n-1
, by Theorem
1.6
(ii), we have

\displaystyle{\mathsf{grFrob}}(A(U_{r,n})_{\mathbb{C}},t)

\displaystyle=Q_{n}({\mathbf{x}},t)-\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\sigma\in{\mathfrak{S}}_{n}\\
{\mathsf{fix}}(\sigma)\geq 1\end{subarray}}F_{{\mathsf{DEX}}(\sigma),n}t^{n-1-{\mathsf{exc}}(\sigma)}.

Since
Q_{n}({\mathbf{x}},t)=\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}Q_{n,j}({\mathbf{x}})t^{j}
and
Q_{n,j}=Q_{n,n-1-j}
by Lemma
3.2
, we have

\displaystyle{\mathsf{grFrob}}(A(U_{n-1,n})_{\mathbb{C}},t)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\sigma\in{\mathfrak{S}}_{n}}F_{{\mathsf{DEX}}(\sigma),n}t^{n-1-{\mathsf{exc}}(\sigma)}-\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\sigma\in{\mathfrak{S}}_{n}\\
{\mathsf{fix}}(\sigma)\geq 1\end{subarray}}F_{{\mathsf{DEX}}(\sigma),n}t^{n-1-{\mathsf{exc}}(\sigma)}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\sigma\in{\mathfrak{S}}_{n}\\
{\mathsf{fix}}(\sigma)=0\end{subarray}}F_{{\mathsf{DEX}}(\sigma),n}t^{n-1-{\mathsf{exc}}(\sigma)}=\sum_{j=1}^{n-1}Q_{n,j,0}({\mathbf{x}})t^{n-1-j}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{n-1}Q_{n,n-j,0}({\mathbf{x}})t^{n-1-j}\quad\text{(by Lemma \ref{lem:PalinQ})}

\displaystyle=t^{-1}Q_{n}^{0}({\mathbf{x}},t).

∎

3.2.
Augmented Chow rings of uniform matroids

In this section, our goal is to prove Theorem
1.8
, the augmented analog of Theorem
1.6
. Similar to the proof of the non-augmented case, we will proceed by calculating the difference between the graded Frobenius series of the augmented Chow ring of

for consecutive

\displaystyle\widetilde{\Delta}_{n,r}({\mathbf{x}})

\displaystyle\coloneqq{\mathsf{grFrob}}(\widetilde{A}(U_{r+1,n})_{\mathbb{C}},t)-{\mathsf{grFrob}}(\widetilde{A}(U_{r,n})_{\mathbb{C}},t)

\displaystyle=\widetilde{a}_{n,r}^{(1)}({\mathbf{x}})t+\widetilde{a}_{n,r}^{(2)}({\mathbf{x}})t^{2}+\cdots+\widetilde{a}_{n,r}^{(r+1)}({\mathbf{x}})t^{r+1}.

We give two expressions for
\widetilde{\Delta}_{n,r}({\mathbf{x}})
in Proposition
3.8
. Then we derive the two formulas in Theorem
1.8
from these two expressions by considering

{\mathsf{grFrob}}(\widetilde{A}(U_{r,n})_{\mathbb{C}},t)={\mathsf{grFrob}}(\widetilde{A}(U_{1,n}),t)+\sum_{j=1}^{r-1}\widetilde{\Delta}_{n,j}({\mathbf{x}})

and

{\mathsf{grFrob}}(\widetilde{A}(U_{r,n})_{\mathbb{C}},t)={\mathsf{grFrob}}(\widetilde{A}(U_{n,n}),t)-\sum_{j=r}^{n-1}\widetilde{\Delta}_{n,j}({\mathbf{x}}).

We first establish some facts that will be useful in expressing
\widetilde{\Delta}_{n,r}({\mathbf{x}})
. These facts are parallel to what we did in the non-augmented case. In Theorem
2.14
, we saw that the graded Frobenius series of the augmented Chow ring of the Boolean matroid is given by the Binomial Eulerian quasisymmetric function

{\mathsf{grFrob}}\left(\widetilde{A}(\mathsf{B}_{n})_{\mathbb{C}},t\right)=\widetilde{Q}_{n}({\mathbf{x}},t)=\sum_{\sigma\in\widetilde{{\mathfrak{S}}}_{n}}F_{{\mathsf{DEX}}(\sigma),n}({\mathbf{x}})t^{{\mathsf{exc}}(\sigma)+1}.

We want an analog of
Q_{n,j,k}({\mathbf{x}})
for the binomial Eulerian quasisymmetric function
\widetilde{Q}_{n}({\mathbf{x}},t)
.
Define the following variant of the binomial Eulerian quasisymmetric function

\widetilde{Q}_{n}({\mathbf{x}},t,r)\coloneqq h_{n}r^{n}+t\sum_{k=1}^{n}h_{n-k}Q_{k}({\mathbf{x}},t)r^{n-k}.

Then
\widetilde{Q}_{n}({\mathbf{x}},t,1)=\widetilde{Q}_{n}({\mathbf{x}},t)
. Write
\widetilde{Q}_{n}({\mathbf{x}},t,r)=\sum_{j\geq 0,k\geq 0}\widetilde{Q}_{n,j,k}({\mathbf{x}})t^{j}r^{k}
.

Theorem 3.5
.

For any integer
n\geq 1
,

\widetilde{Q}_{n}({\mathbf{x}},t,r)=\sum_{\sigma\in\widetilde{\mathfrak{S}}_{n}}F_{{\mathsf{DEX}}(\sigma),n}({\mathbf{x}})t^{{\mathsf{exc}}(\sigma)+1}r^{{\mathsf{fix_{2}}}(\sigma)}.

Or equivalently, for all
0\leq j,~k\leq n
,

\widetilde{Q}_{n,j,k}({\mathbf{x}})=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\sigma\in\widetilde{{\mathfrak{S}}}_{n}\\
{\mathsf{exc}}(\sigma)=j-1\\
{\mathsf{fix_{2}}}(\sigma)=k\end{subarray}}F_{{\mathsf{DEX}}(\sigma),n}({\mathbf{x}}).

In particular,

\widetilde{Q}_{n,j,0}({\mathbf{x}})=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\sigma\in{\mathfrak{S}}_{n}\\
{\mathsf{exc}}(\sigma)=j-1\end{subarray}}F_{{\mathsf{DEX}}(\sigma),n}({\mathbf{x}})=Q_{n,j-1}({\mathbf{x}}).

Proof.

The proof is similar to the proof of Theorem 6.21 in
[
Lia24b
]
. By definition of
\widetilde{Q}_{n}({\mathbf{x}},t,r)
and
h_{n}=F_{\emptyset,n}({\mathbf{x}})
, we have

\displaystyle\widetilde{Q}_{n}({\mathbf{x}},t,r)

\displaystyle=F_{\emptyset,n}({\mathbf{x}})r^{n}+\sum_{k=1}^{n}\sum_{\sigma\in{\mathfrak{S}}_{k}}F_{\emptyset,n-k}({\mathbf{x}})F_{{\mathsf{DEX}}(\sigma),k}({\mathbf{x}})t^{{\mathsf{exc}}(\sigma)+1}r^{n-k}

\displaystyle=F_{\emptyset,n}({\mathbf{x}})r^{n}+\sum_{k=1}^{n}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\pi\in\widetilde{{\mathfrak{S}}}_{n}\\
{\mathsf{fix_{2}}}(\pi)=n-k\end{subarray}}F_{{\mathsf{DEX}}(\sigma),n}({\mathbf{x}})t^{{\mathsf{exc}}(\pi)+1}r^{n-k}.

\displaystyle=F_{\emptyset,n}({\mathbf{x}})r^{n}+\sum_{\pi\in\widetilde{{\mathfrak{S}}}_{n}\setminus\{\theta\}}F_{{\mathsf{DEX}}(\sigma),n}({\mathbf{x}})t^{{\mathsf{exc}}(\pi)+1}r^{{\mathsf{fix_{2}}}(\pi)}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\pi\in\widetilde{{\mathfrak{S}}}_{n}}F_{{\mathsf{DEX}}(\sigma),n}({\mathbf{x}})t^{{\mathsf{exc}}(\pi)+1}r^{{\mathsf{fix_{2}}}(\pi)}.

∎

Now we give a refinement of Theorem
2.14
regarding
\widetilde{Q}_{n,j,k}({\mathbf{x}})
that will be used to express
\widetilde{\Delta}_{n,r}({\mathbf{x}})
.
Let the subset

\widetilde{A}^{j,k}(\mathsf{B}_{n})
be the subspace spanned by

{\mathfrak{S}}_{n}
-invariant.

Proposition 3.6
.

For any

{\mathrm{ch}}(\widetilde{A}^{j,k}(\mathsf{B}_{n})_{\mathbb{C}})=\widetilde{Q}_{n,j,k}({\mathbf{x}}).

Proof.

To prove this result, we use the combinatorial objects called

in the binomial Eulerian story analogous to Stembridge codes in the Eulerian story. We only need extended codes in this proof. The readers are referred to Section 4.1.2 and Theorem 4.7 in
[

\widetilde{{\mathtt{Code}}}_{n,j-1,k}

\infty

-equivariant bijection

∎

The following property of

is analogous to Lemma

Lemma 3.7

For all

\widetilde{Q}_{n,j,k}({\mathbf{x}})=h_{k}({\mathbf{x}})\widetilde{Q}_{n-k,j,0}({\mathbf{x}}).

By Theorem

\displaystyle h_{k}({\mathbf{x}})\widetilde{Q}_{n-k,j,0}({\mathbf{x}})=

\displaystyle F_{\emptyset,k}({\mathbf{x}})\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\sigma\in\widetilde{{\mathfrak{S}}}_{n-k}\\
{\mathsf{exc}}(\sigma)=j-1\\
{\mathsf{fix_{2}}}(\sigma)=0\end{subarray}}F_{{\mathsf{DEX}}(\sigma),n-k}({\mathbf{x}})=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\sigma\in{\mathfrak{S}}_{n-k}\\

\overline{\sigma}
and
k

0
’s.

Now, for any fixed

and the set of decorated permutations of length

with
k

0

Applying the bijection, we have

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\sigma\in{\mathfrak{S}}_{n-k}\\
{\mathsf{exc}}(\sigma)=j-1\end{subarray}}\sum_{\pi^{\prime}\in 0^{k}\shuffle\overline{\sigma}}F_{{\mathsf{DES}}(\pi^{\prime}),n}({\mathbf{x}})=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\pi\in\widetilde{{\mathfrak{S}}}_{n}\\
{\mathsf{exc}}(\pi)=j-1\\
{\mathsf{fix_{2}}}(\sigma)=k\end{subarray}}F_{{\mathsf{DEX}}(\pi),n}({\mathbf{x}})=\widetilde{Q}_{n,j,k}({\mathbf{x}}).

∎

Using the lemmas and propositions above, we are able to interpret the difference
\widetilde{\Delta}_{n,r}({\mathbf{x}})
between
{\mathsf{grFrob}}\left(\widetilde{A}(U_{r+1,n}),t\right)
and
{\mathsf{grFrob}}\left(\widetilde{A}(U_{r,n}),t\right)
.

Proposition 3.8
.

For
1\leq r\leq n-1
, the polynomial
\widetilde{\Delta}_{n,r}({\mathbf{x}})\in\Lambda_{n}[t]
has two expressions:

\sum_{j=0}^{r}h_{n-j}Q_{j}({\mathbf{x}},t)t^{r-j+1}

and

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\sigma\in\widetilde{{\mathfrak{S}}}_{n}\\
{\mathsf{fix_{2}}}(\sigma)\geq n-r\end{subarray}}F_{{\mathsf{DEX}}(\sigma),n}({\mathbf{x}})t^{r-{\mathsf{exc}}(\sigma)}.

Proof.

Similar to the case of Chow rings, for
1\leq k\leq n
we have

\widetilde{FY}^{k}(U_{1,n})\subsetneq\widetilde{FY}^{k}(U_{2,n})\subsetneq\ldots\subsetneq\widetilde{FY}^{k}(U_{n,n}).

The symmetric function
\widetilde{a}_{n,r}^{(k)}({\mathbf{x}})
is the Frobenius characteristic of the permutation module generated by
\widetilde{FY}^{k}(U_{r+1,n})\setminus\widetilde{FY}^{k}(U_{r,n})
, which consists of monomial

x_{[n]}^{r+1}\quad\text{ if }k=r+1

and monomials

x_{F_{1}}^{a_{1}}\ldots x_{F_{\ell}}^{a_{\ell}}x_{[n]}^{i}\in\widetilde{FY}^{k}(U_{r+1,n})\quad\text{ such that }\quad|F_{\ell}|=r-i

for
i=0,1,\ldots,k-1
if
1\leq k\leq r
. Hence,
\widetilde{a}_{n,r}^{(r+1)}({\mathbf{x}})=h_{n}
. For
1\leq k\leq r
, One can construct an
{\mathfrak{S}}_{n}
-equivariant bijection from
\widetilde{FY}^{k}(U_{r+1,n})\setminus\widetilde{FY}^{k}(U_{r,n})
to
\bigcup_{i=0}^{k-1}\widetilde{FY}^{k-i,n-r-i}(\mathsf{B}_{n})
defined by
x_{F_{1}}^{a_{1}}\ldots x_{F_{\ell}}^{a_{\ell}}x_{[n]}^{i}\mapsto x_{F_{1}}^{a_{1}}\ldots x_{F_{\ell}}^{a_{\ell}}
. By Proposition
3.6
, we have

\widetilde{a}_{n,r}^{(k)}=\sum_{i=0}^{k-1}\widetilde{Q}_{n,k-i,n-r+i}({\mathbf{x}})=\sum_{i=0}^{k-1}h_{n-r+i}\widetilde{Q}_{r-i,k-i,0}({\mathbf{x}})\quad\text{(by Lemma \ref{lem:ExtractHTildeQ})}.

Then since for any
n
and
0\leq j\leq n-1
,

\widetilde{Q}_{n,j,0}({\mathbf{x}})=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\sigma\in\widetilde{{\mathfrak{S}}}_{n}\\
{\mathsf{exc}}(\sigma)=j-1\\
{\mathsf{fix_{2}}}(\sigma)=0\end{subarray}}F_{{\mathsf{DEX}}(\sigma),n}({\mathbf{x}})=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\sigma\in{\mathfrak{S}}_{n}\\
{\mathsf{exc}}(\sigma)=j-1\end{subarray}}F_{{\mathsf{DEX}}(\sigma),n}({\mathbf{x}})=Q_{n,j-1}({\mathbf{x}}),

we have

\displaystyle\widetilde{\Delta}_{n,r}({\mathbf{x}})

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=1}^{r+1}\widetilde{a}_{n,r}^{(k)}({\mathbf{x}})t^{k}=h_{n}t^{r+1}+\sum_{k=1}^{r}\sum_{i=0}^{k-1}h_{n-r+i}\widetilde{Q}_{r-i,k-i,0}t^{k}

(13)

\displaystyle=h_{n}t^{r+1}+\sum_{k=1}^{r}\sum_{i=0}^{k-1}h_{n-r+i}Q_{r-i,k-i-1}t^{k}

\displaystyle=h_{n}t^{r+1}+\sum_{i=0}^{r-1}h_{n-r+i}t^{i+1}\left(\sum_{k=i+1}^{r}Q_{r-i,k-i-1}t^{k-i-1}\right)

\displaystyle=h_{n}t^{r+1}+\sum_{i=0}^{r-1}h_{n-r+i}t^{i+1}Q_{r-i}({\mathbf{x}},t)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=0}^{r}h_{n-r+i}Q_{r-i}({\mathbf{x}},t)t^{i+1}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=0}^{r}h_{n-j}Q_{j}({\mathbf{x}},t)t^{r-j+1}.

On the other hand, by Theorem
3.5
and Lemma
3.2
,
\widetilde{Q}_{r-i,k-i,0}=Q_{r-i,k-i-1}({\mathbf{x}})=Q_{r-i,r-k}({\mathbf{x}})=\widetilde{Q}_{r-i,r-k+1,0}
. Therefore, equation (
13
) can be expressed in a different way as follows

\displaystyle\widetilde{\Delta}_{n,r}({\mathbf{x}})

\displaystyle=h_{n}t^{r+1}+\sum_{k=1}^{r}\sum_{i=0}^{k-1}h_{n-r+i}\widetilde{Q}_{r-i,r-k+1,0}t^{k}

\displaystyle=h_{n}t^{r+1}+\sum_{k=1}^{r}\sum_{i=0}^{k-1}\widetilde{Q}_{n,r-k+1,n-r+i}t^{k}\quad\text{(by Lemma \ref{lem:ExtractHTildeQ}})

\displaystyle=h_{n}t^{r+1}+\sum_{k=1}^{r}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\sigma\in\widetilde{{\mathfrak{S}}}_{n}\\
{\mathsf{exc}}(\sigma)=r-k\\
{\mathsf{fix_{2}}}(\sigma)\geq n-r\end{subarray}}F_{{\mathsf{DEX}}(\sigma),n}({\mathbf{x}})t^{k}

\displaystyle=F_{{\mathsf{DEX}}(\theta),n}({\mathbf{x}})t^{r-(-1)}+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\sigma\in\widetilde{{\mathfrak{S}}}_{n}\setminus\{\theta\}\\
{\mathsf{fix_{2}}}(\sigma)\geq n-r\end{subarray}}F_{{\mathsf{DEX}}(\sigma),n}({\mathbf{x}})t^{r-{\mathsf{exc}}(\sigma)}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\sigma\in\widetilde{{\mathfrak{S}}}_{n}\\
{\mathsf{fix_{2}}}(\sigma)\geq n-r\end{subarray}}F_{{\mathsf{DEX}}(\sigma),n}({\mathbf{x}})t^{r-{\mathsf{exc}}(\sigma)}.

∎

We are ready to prove Theorem
1.8
, which we restate here.

Theorem
1.8
.

The graded Frobenius series of the augmented Chow ring of arbitrary uniform matroids
{\mathsf{grFrob}}\left(\widetilde{A}(U_{r,n})_{\mathbb{C}},t\right)
has two expressions:

(i)

\displaystyle h_{n}+t\sum_{j=0}^{r-1}h_{n-j}Q_{j}({\mathbf{x}},t)(1+t+\ldots+t^{r-j-1})
,

(ii)

\displaystyle\widetilde{Q}_{n}({\mathbf{x}},t)-\sum_{j=r}^{n-1}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\sigma\in\widetilde{{\mathfrak{S}}}_{n}\\
{\mathsf{fix_{2}}}(\sigma)\geq n-j\end{subarray}}F_{{\mathsf{DEX}}(\sigma),n}t^{j-{\mathsf{exc}}(\sigma)}
.

Proof.

For (i), applying the first expression of Proposition
3.8
, we have

\displaystyle{\mathsf{grFrob}}(\widetilde{A}(U_{r,n})_{\mathbb{C}},t)

\displaystyle={\mathsf{grFrob}}(\widetilde{A}(U_{1,n}),t)+\sum_{j=1}^{r-1}\widetilde{\Delta}_{n,j}({\mathbf{x}})

\displaystyle=h_{n}(1+t)+\sum_{j=1}^{r-1}\sum_{i=0}^{j}h_{n-i}Q_{i}({\mathbf{x}},t)t^{j-i+1}

\displaystyle=h_{n}(1+t)+\sum_{j=1}^{r-1}h_{n}t^{j+1}+\sum_{j=1}^{r-1}\sum_{i=1}^{j}h_{n-i}Q_{i}({\mathbf{x}},t)t^{j-i+1}

\displaystyle=h_{n}+t\sum_{j=0}^{r-1}h_{n}t^{j}+t\sum_{i=1}^{r-1}h_{n-i}Q_{i}({\mathbf{x}},t)\sum_{j=i}^{r-1}t^{j-i}

\displaystyle=h_{n}+t\sum_{i=0}^{r-1}h_{n-i}Q_{i}({\mathbf{x}},t)(1+t+\ldots+t^{r-i-1}).

For (ii), applying the second expression from Proposition
3.8
, we have

\displaystyle{\mathsf{grFrob}}(\widetilde{A}(U_{r,n})_{\mathbb{C}},t)

\displaystyle={\mathsf{grFrob}}(\widetilde{A}(U_{n,n})_{\mathbb{C}},t)-\sum_{j=r}^{n-1}\widetilde{\Delta}_{n,j}({\mathbf{x}})

\displaystyle=\widetilde{Q}_{n}({\mathbf{x}},t)-\sum_{j=r}^{n-1}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\sigma\in\widetilde{{\mathfrak{S}}}_{n}\\
{\mathsf{fix_{2}}}(\sigma)\geq n-j\end{subarray}}F_{{\mathsf{DEX}}(\sigma),n}({\mathbf{x}})t^{j-{\mathsf{exc}}(\sigma)}.

∎

Then Corollary
1.9
, which we restate below, follows directly from Theorem
1.8
.

Corollary
1.9
.

For the special cases that
r=n
and
r=n-1
, we have

(i)

\displaystyle{\mathsf{grFrob}}(\widetilde{A}(U_{n,n})_{\mathbb{C}},t)=\widetilde{Q}_{n}({\mathbf{x}},t),

(ii)

\displaystyle{\mathsf{grFrob}}(\widetilde{A}(U_{n-1,n})_{\mathbb{C}},t)=Q_{n}({\mathbf{x}},t).

Proof.

When
r=n
, nothing is being subtracted on the right-hand side of Theorem
2
(ii); hence (i) follows.

When
r=n-1
, by Theorem
2
(ii), we have

{\mathsf{grFrob}}(\widetilde{A}(U_{n-1,n})_{\mathbb{C}},t)=\widetilde{Q}_{n}({\mathbf{x}},t)-\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\sigma\in\widetilde{{\mathfrak{S}}}_{n}\\
{\mathsf{fix_{2}}}(\sigma)\geq 1\end{subarray}}F_{{\mathsf{DEX}}(\sigma),n}t^{n-1-{\mathsf{exc}}(\sigma)}.

(14)

Recall from Corollary 3.6 in
[
SW20
]
that
\widetilde{Q}_{n}({\mathbf{x}},t)
as a polynomial in
\Lambda_{n}[t]
is palindromic; hence

\widetilde{Q}_{n}({\mathbf{x}},t)=\sum_{\sigma\in\widetilde{{\mathfrak{S}}}_{n}}F_{{\mathsf{DEX}}(\sigma),n}t^{{\mathsf{exc}}(\sigma)+1}=\sum_{\sigma\in\widetilde{{\mathfrak{S}}}_{n}}F_{{\mathsf{DEX}}(\sigma),n}t^{n-1-{\mathsf{exc}}(\sigma)}.

Then (
14
) can be rewritten as

\displaystyle{\mathsf{grFrob}}(\widetilde{A}(U_{n-1,n})_{\mathbb{C}},t)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\sigma\in\widetilde{{\mathfrak{S}}}_{n}\\
{\mathsf{fix_{2}}}(\sigma)=0\end{subarray}}F_{{\mathsf{DEX}}(\sigma),n}t^{n-1-{\mathsf{exc}}(\sigma)}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\sigma\in{\mathfrak{S}}_{n}}F_{{\mathsf{DEX}}(\sigma),n}t^{n-1-{\mathsf{exc}}(\sigma)}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\sigma\in{\mathfrak{S}}_{n}}F_{{\mathsf{DEX}}(\sigma),n}t^{{\mathsf{exc}}(\sigma)}\quad\text{(palindromic by Lemma \ref{lem:PalinQ})}

\displaystyle=Q_{n}({\mathbf{x}},t).

∎

4.
Parallelity between
U_{r,n}
and
U_{r,n}(q)

Our goal in this section is to derive the formulas of the Hilbert series for the
q
-uniform matroids in Section
1.1
. While one could adapt the proof strategy of Section
3.1
and
3.2
, we instead present a more conceptual argument using Steinberg’s results on unipotent representations of the general linear group
GL_{n}(q)\coloneqq GL_{n}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
from
[
Ste51
]
. This approach yields Theorem
4.2
, which simultaneously unifies the Frobenius-series formulas for the (augmented) Chow rings of
U_{r,n}
and the Hilbert-series formulas for those of
U_{r,n}(q)
.

Recall from the final paragraph of Section
2.2
that, for every
r
, the Chow ring
A(U_{r,n})
and the augmented Chow ring
\widetilde{A}(U_{r,n})
are
{\mathfrak{S}}_{n}
-permutation modules with permutation bases
FY(U_{r,n})
and
\widetilde{FY}(U_{r,n})
respectively.
So are
A(U_{r,n})_{\mathbb{C}}
and
\widetilde{A}(U_{r,n})_{\mathbb{C}}
.
Since the basis elements are monomials indexed by chains in the Boolean lattice
B_{n}
, recalling our notation from Section
2.6
, both
A(U_{r,n})_{\mathbb{C}}
and
\widetilde{A}(U_{r,n})_{\mathbb{C}}
are isomorphic to the direct sum of
{\mathfrak{S}}_{n}
-permutation modules
\alpha_{B_{n}}(S)
. If
S=\{s_{1}<s_{2}<\ldots<s_{\ell}\}\subseteq[n-1]
, then

\alpha_{B_{n}}(S)\cong_{{\mathfrak{S}}_{n}}\mathbb{C}[{\mathfrak{S}}_{n}/{\mathfrak{S}}_{\nu}]\cong_{{\mathfrak{S}}_{n}}\mathbf{1}_{{\mathfrak{S}}_{\nu}}\uparrow_{{\mathfrak{S}}_{\nu}}^{{\mathfrak{S}}_{n}},

(15)

where
\nu
is the composition
\nu(S)=(s_{1},s_{2}-s_{1},\ldots,s_{\ell}-s_{\ell-1},n-s_{\ell})
and
{\mathfrak{S}}_{\nu}
is the Young subgroup for the composition
\nu
. A well-known fact in the representation theory of
{\mathfrak{S}}_{n}
states that the induced representation (
15
) can be decomposed into irreducible
{\mathfrak{S}}_{n}
-modules
\{S^{\lambda}\}_{\lambda\vdash n}
as

\mathbf{1}_{{\mathfrak{S}}_{\nu}}\uparrow_{{\mathfrak{S}}_{\nu}}^{{\mathfrak{S}}_{n}}\cong_{{\mathfrak{S}}_{n}}\bigoplus_{\lambda\vdash n}K_{\lambda,\nu}S^{\lambda}

(16)

where
K_{\lambda,\nu}
is the well-known
Kostka number
(see Sagan
[
Sag13
, Propostion 5.3.6]
).

Similarly, since the general linear group
GL_{n}(q)
acts on the
q
-Boolean lattice
B_{n}(q)
, i.e., the subspace lattice of
\left(\mathbb{F}_{q}\right)^{n}
, in an obvious way, both
A(U_{r,n}(q))_{\mathbb{C}}
and
\widetilde{A}(U_{r,n}(q))_{\mathbb{C}}
are permutation representations of
GL_{n}(q)
with permutation bases
FY(U_{r,n}(q))
and
\widetilde{FY}(U_{r,n}(q))
, respectively. Since the basis elements are monomials indexed by chains in
B_{n}(q)
, both
A(U_{r,n}(q))_{\mathbb{C}}
and
\widetilde{A}(U_{r,n}(q))_{\mathbb{C}}
are isomorphic to the direct sum of
GL_{n}(q)
-permutation modules
\alpha_{B_{n}(q)}(S)
. If
S=\{s_{1}<s_{2}<\ldots<s_{\ell}\}\subseteq[n-1]
, then

\alpha_{B_{n}(q)}(S)\cong_{GL_{n}(q)}\mathbb{C}[GL_{n}(q)/P_{\nu}]\cong_{GL_{n}(q)}\mathbf{1}_{P_{\nu}}\uparrow_{P_{\nu}}^{GL_{n}(q)},

where
\nu=(s_{1},s_{2}-s_{1},\ldots,s_{\ell}-s_{\ell-1},n-s_{\ell})
and
P_{\nu}
is the maximal parabolic subgroup of
GL_{n}(q)
that stabilizes the flag

\langle e_{1},\ldots,e_{s_{1}}\rangle\subsetneq\langle e_{1},\ldots,e_{s_{1}},\ldots,e_{s_{2}}\rangle\subsetneq\ldots\subsetneq\langle e_{1},\ldots,e_{s_{1}},\ldots,e_{s_{2}},\ldots,e_{s_{\ell}}\rangle,

i.e., the subgroup of matrices having main diagonal block matrices of size
s_{1}
,
s_{2}-s_{1}
,
\ldots
,
n-s_{\ell}
and
0
for entries below the main diagonal block matrices.

There is a parallelity between the representations of
{\mathfrak{S}}_{n}
and some representations of
GL_{n}(q)
. In the following, we will briefly describe this story, which mostly follows
[
Sta82
, Section 5]
and the lecture notes
[
GR14
, Section 4.2, 4.6, 4.7]
.

Steinberg
[
Ste51
]
showed that the decomposition of
\mathbf{1_{P_{\nu}}}\uparrow_{P_{\nu}}^{GL_{n}(q)}
into irreducible representations of
GL_{n}(q)
is parallel to the decomposition of
\mathbf{1_{{\mathfrak{S}}_{\nu}}}\uparrow_{{\mathfrak{S}}_{\nu}}^{{\mathfrak{S}}_{n}}
given in (
16
).
More precisely, there is a distinguished family
\{S^{\lambda}_{q}\}
of irreducible representations of
GL_{n}(q)
, indexed by partitions
\lambda
of
n
, such that for any composition
\nu
one has

\mathbf{1}\uparrow_{P_{\nu}}^{GL_{n}(q)}\cong_{GL_{n}(q)}\bigoplus_{\lambda\vdash n}K_{\lambda,\nu}S^{\lambda}_{q}.

(17)

These
\{S^{\lambda}_{q}\}_{\lambda\vdash n}
, originally studied by Steinberg, are now referred to as the
unipotent representations
of
GL_{n}(q)
.
Let
\chi_{q}^{\lambda}
denote the character of the unipotent representation
S_{q}^{\lambda}
for each partition
\lambda
. For each
n
, let
{\mathcal{C}}(GL_{n}(q))
be the free
\mathbb{Z}
-module spanned by the characters of unipotent reprentations of
GL_{n}(q)
and define
{\mathcal{C}}(GL(q))\coloneqq\bigoplus_{n=0}^{\infty}{\mathcal{C}}(GL_{n}(q))
.
One can make
{\mathcal{C}}(GL(q))
a
\mathbb{Z}
-graded algebra via the
parabolic induction product
:

\displaystyle{\mathcal{C}}(GL_{n_{1}}(q))\times{\mathcal{C}}(GL_{n_{2}}(q))

\displaystyle\longrightarrow{\mathcal{C}}(GL_{n_{1}+n_{2}}(q))

\displaystyle(f_{1},f_{2})

\displaystyle\longmapsto f_{1}*f_{2}\coloneqq\left(f_{1}\otimes f_{2}\Uparrow_{GL_{n_{1}}(q)\times GL_{n_{2}}(q)}^{P_{(n_{1},n_{2})}}\right)\uparrow_{P_{(n_{1},n_{2})}}^{GL_{n_{1}+n_{2}}(q)},

where
(-)\Uparrow_{GL_{n_{1}}(q)\times GL_{n_{2}}(q)}^{P_{n_{1},n_{2}}}
is the
inflation operation
that precomposes a representation of
GL_{n_{1}}(q)\times GL_{n_{2}}(q)
with the surjective map
P_{n_{1},n_{2}}\twoheadrightarrow GL_{n_{1}}(q)\times GL_{n_{2}}(q)
given by

\left[\begin{array}[]{cc}A&C\\
0&B\end{array}\right]\mapsto\left[\begin{array}[]{cc}A&0\\
0&B\end{array}\right].

Because of the parallelity in (
17
), there is a
q
-analog of the Frobenius characteristic map, which is a
\mathbb{Z}
-graded isomorphism between
{\mathcal{C}}(GL(q))
and the ring of symmetric functions
\Lambda_{\mathbb{Z}}
over
\mathbb{Z}
,

{\mathrm{ch}}_{q}:{\mathcal{C}}(GL(q))\longrightarrow\Lambda_{\mathbb{Z}}

sending
{\mathrm{ch}}_{q}(\chi^{\lambda}_{q})=s_{\lambda}
and
{\mathrm{ch}}_{q}(\mathbf{1}_{GL_{n}(q)})=h_{n}
. If
\chi
is a character of a representation
V
, we sometimes write
{\mathrm{ch}}_{q}(V)
to mean
{\mathrm{ch}}_{q}(\chi)
.

Hence for a graded
\mathbb{C}[GL_{n}(q)]
-module
W=\bigoplus_{j}W^{j}
constituted by unipotent representations of
GL_{n}(q)
, one can define the
q
-graded Frobenius series
of
W
by

{\mathsf{grFrob}}_{q}(W,t)\coloneqq\sum_{j}{\mathrm{ch}}_{q}(W^{j})t^{j}.

For any subset
S\subseteq[n-1]
determining the composition
\nu=\nu(S)=(\nu_{1},\ldots,\nu_{\ell+1})
, the induced representations satisfy

{\mathrm{ch}}_{q}\left(\alpha_{B_{n}(q)}(S)\right)={\mathrm{ch}}_{q}\left(\mathbf{1}\uparrow_{P_{\nu}}^{GL_{n}(q)}\right)=h_{\nu_{1}}h_{\nu_{2}}\ldots h_{\nu_{\ell+1}}={\mathrm{ch}}(\mathbf{1}_{{\mathfrak{S}}_{\nu}}\uparrow_{{\mathfrak{S}}_{\nu}}^{{\mathfrak{S}}_{n}})={\mathrm{ch}}\left(\alpha_{B_{n}}(S)\right).

Moreover, the dimension of the induced representation of
GL_{n}(q)
is given by the stable principal specialization of the corresponding induced representation of
{\mathfrak{S}}_{n}
:

\displaystyle\dim_{\mathbb{C}}(\mathbf{1}\uparrow_{P_{\nu}}^{GL_{n}(q)})=

\displaystyle|GL_{n}(q)/P_{\nu}|={n\brack\nu_{1},\ldots,\nu_{\ell+1}}_{q}=\prod_{i=1}^{n}(1-q^{i}){\mathrm{ps}_{q}}(h_{\nu})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\prod_{i=1}^{n}(1-q^{i}){\mathrm{ps}_{q}}\left({\mathrm{ch}}(\mathbf{1}_{{\mathfrak{S}}_{\nu}}\uparrow_{{\mathfrak{S}}_{\nu}}^{{\mathfrak{S}}_{n}})\right).

(18)

Now let us revisit the structure of the representations on the Chow rings. Recall from (
3
) the combinatorial constraints on the monomials in
FY(U_{r,n})
and
FY(U_{r,n}(q))
. Fix an integer
0\leq i\leq r-1
and a subset
S=\{s_{1}<s_{2}<\cdots<s_{\ell}\}\subseteq[n-1]
.
Then the multiplicities of
\alpha_{B_{n}}(S)
in
A^{i}(U_{r,n})_{\mathbb{C}}
and
\alpha_{B_{n}(q)}(S)
in
A^{i}(U_{r,n}(q))_{\mathbb{C}}

are both equal to the number of integer solutions
(a_{1},\dots,a_{\ell},b)
of

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}a_{1}+a_{2}+\ldots+a_{\ell}+b=i\\
1\leq a_{j}\leq s_{j}-s_{j-1}-1\text{ for }j=1,\ldots,\ell\quad(\text{set }s_{0}\coloneqq 0)\\
0\leq b\leq r-s_{\ell}-1\end{array}\right..

It follows that, under the usual Frobenius and
q
-Frobenius characteristic maps, the
\mathfrak{S}_{n}
-representation
A^{i}(U_{r,n})_{\mathbb{C}}
and the
GL_{n}(q)
-representation
A^{i}(U_{r,n}(q))_{\mathbb{C}}
have identical characters. The same holds for the representations on the augmented Chow rings.

Proposition 4.1
.

For all
1\leq r\leq n
, we have

{\mathsf{grFrob}}_{q}(A(U_{r,n}(q))_{\mathbb{C}},t)={\mathsf{grFrob}}(A(U_{r,n})_{\mathbb{C}},t),

{\mathsf{grFrob}}_{q}(\widetilde{A}(U_{r,n}(q))_{\mathbb{C}},t)={\mathsf{grFrob}}(\widetilde{A}(U_{r,n})_{\mathbb{C}},t).

Moreover, combining the multiplicity argument with (
18
) shows that, for every
i
, the
\mathbb{Q}
-dimensions of
A^{i}(U_{r,n}(q))
and
\widetilde{A}^{i}(U_{r,n}(q))
can be obtained by taking the stable principal specialization of
{\mathrm{ch}}(A^{i}(U_{r,n})_{\mathbb{C}})
and
{\mathrm{ch}}(\widetilde{A}^{i}(U_{r,n})_{\mathbb{C}})
, respectively.
Consequently, we arrive at the following result.

Theorem 4.2
.

For all
1\leq r\leq n
, the Hilbert series of the Chow ring and augmented Chow ring of arbitrary
q
-uniform matroids are given by

{\mathsf{Hilb}}\left(A(U_{r,n}(q)),t\right)=\prod_{i=1}^{n}(1-q^{i}){\mathrm{ps}_{q}}\left({\mathsf{grFrob}}\left(A(U_{r,n})_{\mathbb{C}},t\right)\right),

{\mathsf{Hilb}}\left(\widetilde{A}(U_{r,n}(q)),t\right)=\prod_{i=1}^{n}(1-q^{i}){\mathrm{ps}_{q}}\left({\mathsf{grFrob}}\left(\widetilde{A}(U_{r,n})_{\mathbb{C}},t\right)\right).

By Theorem
4.2
and the relations in Table
1
, applying the stable principle specialization to the graded Frobenius series for uniform matroids in Theorems
1.6
,
1.8
and Corollaries
1.7
,
1.9
, we derive the Hilbert series formula for
q
-uniform matroids in Theorems
1.1
,
1.5
,
1.3
and Corollaries
1.2
,
1.4
.

Similarly, the stable principal specialization of the differences
\Delta_{n,r}({\mathbf{x}})
and
\widetilde{\Delta}_{n,r}({\mathbf{x}})
in Proposition
3.4
and
3.8
gives us the difference of the Hilbert series for
q
-uniform matroids. The results might be of interest, so we record them here.

Corollary 4.3
.

For
1\leq r\leq n-1
,

(i)

the difference
{\mathsf{Hilb}}(A(U_{r+1,n}(q)),t)-{\mathsf{Hilb}}(A(U_{r,n}(q)),t)
has two expressions:

\sum_{i=0}^{r}{n\brack i}_{q}d_{i}(q,t)t^{r-i}\quad\text{ and }\quad\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\sigma\in{\mathfrak{S}}_{n}\\
{\mathsf{fix}}(\sigma)\geq n-r\end{subarray}}q^{{\mathsf{maj}}(\sigma)-{\mathsf{exc}}(\sigma)}t^{r-{\mathsf{exc}}(\sigma)}.

(ii)

the difference
{\mathsf{Hilb}}(\widetilde{A}(U_{r+1,n}(q)),t)-{\mathsf{Hilb}}(\widetilde{A}(U_{r,n}(q)),t)
has two expressions:

\sum_{i=0}^{r}{n\brack i}_{q}A_{i}(q,t)t^{r+1-i}\quad\text{ and }\quad\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\sigma\in\widetilde{{\mathfrak{S}}}_{n}\\
{\mathsf{fix_{2}}}(\sigma)\geq n-r\end{subarray}}q^{{\mathsf{maj}}(\sigma)-{\mathsf{exc}}(\sigma)}t^{r-{\mathsf{exc}}(\sigma)}.

5.
Charney–Davis quantities for (augmented) Chow rings of matroids

We prove our two general theorems, Theorem
1.10
and Theorem
1.11
, of the equivariant Charney–Davis quantities for general matroids in Section
5.1
. Then in Section
5.2
we consider the special cases of uniform matroids and
q
-uniform matroids.

5.1.
Charney–Davis quantities for general matroids

Let
M
be a matroid on
E=[n]
of rank
r
with the lattice of flats
{\mathcal{L}}(M)
. Let
G
be any subgroup of
{\mathrm{Aut}}(M)
. In this section, we will prove Theorem
1.10
and Theorem
1.11
, which are our formulas for the
G
-equivariant Charney–Davis quantities of (augmented) Chow rings of general matroids.

Recall from Section
2.6
the background on rank-selected subposets.
Consider the permutation representation
\alpha_{{\mathcal{L}}(M)}(S)
and the virtual representation
\beta_{{\mathcal{L}}(M)}(S)
of
G
for
S\subseteq[r-1]
. We have, by Theorem
2.20
, that

\beta_{{\mathcal{L}}(M)}(S)\cong_{G}\tilde{H}_{|S|-1}({\mathcal{L}}(M)_{S}).

(19)

On the other hand, the action of
G
on
{\mathcal{L}}(M)
also induces actions on the Chow ring
A(M)
and the augmented Chow ring
\widetilde{A}(M)
.
Recall from (
2.4
) and Corollary
2.6
that the Feichtner–Yuzvinsky bases for the
A(M)
and
\widetilde{A}(M)
, respectively, are

FY(M)=\left\{x_{F_{1}}^{a_{1}}x_{F_{2}}^{a_{2}}\ldots x_{F_{\ell}}^{a_{\ell}}:\begin{subarray}{c}~\emptyset=F_{0}\subsetneq F_{1}\subsetneq F_{2}\subsetneq\ldots\subsetneq F_{\ell}\subseteq{\mathcal{L}}(M)\text{ for }0\leq\ell\leq n\\
1\leq a_{i}\leq{\mathrm{rk}}_{M}(F_{i})-{\mathrm{rk}}_{M}(F_{i-1})-1\end{subarray}\right\}

and

\widetilde{FY}(M)\coloneqq\left\{x_{F_{1}}^{a_{1}}x_{F_{2}}^{a_{2}}\ldots x_{F_{\ell}}^{a_{\ell}}:\begin{subarray}{c}\emptyset\subsetneq F_{1}\subsetneq F_{2}\subsetneq\ldots\subsetneq F_{\ell}\text{ for }0\leq\ell\leq n\\
1\leq a_{1}\leq{\mathrm{rk}}_{M}(F_{1}),~a_{i}\leq{\mathrm{rk}}_{M}(F_{i})-{\mathrm{rk}}_{M}(F_{i-1})-1\text{ for }i\geq 2\end{subarray}\right\}.

Similar to the case of uniform matroids described in Section
4
, the subsets
FY^{k}(M)
and
\widetilde{FY}^{k}(M)
of degree
k
monomials form permutation bases for the
G
-module
A^{k}(M)
and
\widetilde{A}^{k}(M)
respectively for every
k
.
Therefore, as representations of
G
, both
A(M)
and
\widetilde{A}(M)
are the direct sums of permutation representations of the form
\alpha_{{\mathcal{L}}(M)}(S)
for some
S\subseteq[r-1]
.

Now we are ready to prove Theorem
1.10
, which we restate here.

Theorem
1.10
.

Let
M
be a (loopless) matroid on
[n]
of rank
r
,

\sum_{i=0}^{r-1}(-1)^{i}A^{i}(M)_{\mathbb{C}}\cong_{G}\begin{cases}0&\text{ if $r$ is even}\\
(-1)^{\frac{r-1}{2}}\tilde{H}_{\frac{r-3}{2}}\left({\mathcal{L}}(M)_{{\mathsf{Even}}(r-1)}\right)&\text{ if $r$ is odd}\end{cases}.

Proof.

Following from the constraints of exponents of the FY-basis elements in
FY(M)
, we have

\sum_{i=0}^{r-1}A^{i}(M)_{\mathbb{C}}t^{i}\cong_{G}\sum_{S=\{r_{1}<\ldots<r_{\ell}\}\subseteq[r]}\prod_{j=1}^{\ell}\frac{t(1-t^{r_{i}-r_{i-1}-1})}{1-t}\alpha_{{\mathcal{L}}(M)}(S)

(20)

with the convention that
r_{0}\coloneqq 0
.
Notice that here we allow
S
to be any subset of
[r]
, because the definiton of
\alpha_{{\mathcal{L}}(M)}(S)
can be easily extended to
S
containing
r
and
\alpha_{{\mathcal{L}}(M)}(S)\cong_{G}\alpha_{{\mathcal{L}}(M)}(S\setminus\{r\})
for any
S
containing
r
. Then (
20
) can be rewritten as

\displaystyle\sum_{i=0}^{r-1}A^{i}(M)_{\mathbb{C}}t^{i}

\displaystyle\cong_{G}

\displaystyle\sum_{\emptyset\neq S=\{r_{1}<\ldots<r_{\ell}\}\subseteq[r-1]}\prod_{j=1}^{\ell}\frac{t(1-t^{r_{j}-r_{j-1}-1})}{1-t}\left(1+\frac{t(1-t^{r-r_{\ell}-1})}{1-t}\right)\alpha_{{\mathcal{L}}(M)}(S)

\displaystyle+\left(1+\frac{t(1-t^{r-1})}{1-t}\right)\alpha_{{\mathcal{L}}(M)}(\emptyset).

Evaluating the above identity in
R_{\mathbb{C}}(G)[t]
at
t=-1
, for a term in the summation to be nonzero, all
r_{i}
’s are forced to be even. The product
\prod_{j=1}^{\ell}\frac{t(1-t^{r_{j}-r_{j-1}-1})}{1-t}
evaluated at
t=-1
gives
(-1)^{\ell}
. If
r
is also even, then both
1+\frac{t(1-t^{r-1})}{1-t}
and
1+\frac{t(1-t^{r-r_{\ell}-1})}{1-t}
evaluated at
t=-1
give

1+\frac{(-1)(1-(-1))}{1-(-1)}=1+(-1)=0.

Therefore,
\sum_{i=0}^{r-1}(-1)^{i}A^{i}(M)_{\mathbb{C}}=0
if
r
is even.

If
r
is odd, then both
\frac{t(1-t^{r-1})}{1-t}
and
\frac{t(1-t^{r-r_{\ell}-1})}{1-t}
vanish after evaluated at
t=-1
. In this case, as a virtual representation of
G
, the evaluation at
t=-1
gives

\displaystyle\sum_{i=0}^{r-1}(-1)^{i}A^{i}(M)_{\mathbb{C}}

\displaystyle\cong_{G}\sum_{S\subseteq{\mathsf{Even}}(r-1)}(-1)^{|S|}\alpha_{{\mathcal{L}}(M)}(S)

\displaystyle=(-1)^{\frac{r-1}{2}}\sum_{S\subseteq{\mathsf{Even}}(r-1)}(-1)^{\frac{r-1}{2}-|S|}\alpha_{{\mathcal{L}}(M)}(S)

\displaystyle=(-1)^{\frac{r-1}{2}}\beta_{{\mathcal{L}}(M)}({\mathsf{Even}}(r-1))

\displaystyle\cong_{G}(-1)^{\frac{r-1}{2}}\tilde{H}_{\frac{r-3}{2}}\left({\mathcal{L}}(M)_{{\mathsf{Even}}(r-1)}\right)\quad(\text{by }(\ref{eq:IsHomology})).

∎

Next, we prove the augmented analog of Theorem
1.10
, Theorem
1.11
, which we restate below.
Recall that we have a similar
G
-action on the FY-basis
\widetilde{FY}(M)
for
\widetilde{A}(M)
.

Theorem
1.11
.

Let
M
be a matroid on
[n]
of rank
r
. Then

\sum_{i=0}^{r}(-1)^{i}\widetilde{A}^{i}(M)_{\mathbb{C}}\cong_{G}\begin{cases}(-1)^{\frac{r}{2}}\tilde{H}_{\frac{r-2}{2}}\left({\mathcal{L}}(M)_{{\mathsf{Odd}}(r-1)}\right)&\text{ if $r$ is even}\\
0&\text{ if $r$ is odd}\end{cases}.

Proof.

The proof is similar to that of Theorem
1.10
. Following from the restriction of the exponents of the FY-basis elements in
\widetilde{FY}(M)
, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{i=0}^{r}\widetilde{A}^{i}(M)_{\mathbb{C}}t^{i}\cong_{G}\sum_{S=\{r_{1}<\ldots<r_{\ell}\}\subseteq[r]}\frac{t(1-t^{r_{1}})}{1-t}\prod_{j=2}^{\ell}\frac{t(1-t^{r_{i}-r_{i-1}-1})}{1-t}\alpha_{{\mathcal{L}}(M)}(S)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{\emptyset\neq S=\{r_{1}<\ldots<r_{\ell}\}\subseteq[r-1]}\frac{t(1-t^{r_{1}})}{1-t}\prod_{j=2}^{\ell}\frac{t(1-t^{r_{j}-r_{j-1}-1})}{1-t}\left(1+\frac{t(1-t^{r-r_{\ell}-1})}{1-t}\right)\alpha_{{\mathcal{L}}(M)}(S)

\displaystyle+\left(1+\frac{t(1-t^{r})}{1-t}\right)\alpha_{{\mathcal{L}}(M)}(\emptyset).

After evaluating at
t=-1
, the right-hand side of the last identity is nonvanishing only when all
r_{i}
’s are odd and
r
is even. Thus, in this case, the
(-1)
-evaluation gives

\displaystyle\sum_{i=0}^{r}(-1)^{i}\widetilde{A}(M)_{\mathbb{C}}

\displaystyle\cong_{G}\sum_{S\subseteq{\mathsf{Odd}}(r-1)}(-1)^{|S|}\alpha_{{\mathcal{L}}(M)}(S)

\displaystyle=(-1)^{\frac{r}{2}}\sum_{S\subseteq{\mathsf{Odd}}(r-1)}(-1)^{\frac{r}{2}-|S|}\alpha_{{\mathcal{L}}(M)}(S)

\displaystyle=(-1)^{\frac{r}{2}}\beta_{{\mathcal{L}}(M)}({\mathsf{Odd}}(r-1))

\displaystyle\cong_{G}(-1)^{\frac{r}{2}}\tilde{H}_{\frac{r-2}{2}}({\mathcal{L}}(M)_{{\mathsf{Odd}}(r-1)})\quad(\text{by }(\ref{eq:IsHomology}))

∎

5.2.
Special cases of uniform matroids and their
q
-analogs

Let
M
be the uniform matroid
U_{r,n}
. Then the automorphism group
{\mathrm{Aut}}(U_{r,n})
is the symmetric group
{\mathfrak{S}}_{n}
. Note that
{\mathcal{L}}(U_{r,n})
is the rank-selected poset
{(B_{n})}_{[r-1]}
of the Boolean lattice
B_{n}
. Then by Theorem
2.22
,

\tilde{H}_{\frac{r-3}{2}}\left({\mathcal{L}}(U_{r,n})_{{\mathsf{Even}}(r-1)}\right)=\tilde{H}_{\frac{r-3}{2}}\left({(B_{n})}_{{\mathsf{Even}}(r-1)}\right)\cong_{{\mathfrak{S}}_{n}}S^{H_{{\mathsf{Even}}(r-1),n}}.

Theorem
1.10
in the case of uniform matroids
U_{r,n}
together with the above isomorphism yields the following new results.

Theorem 5.1
.

For any positive integer
n
and
1\leq r\leq n
, the
{\mathfrak{S}}_{n}
-equivariant Charney–Davis quantity of the Chow ring of a uniform matroid is given by

\sum_{i=0}^{r-1}(-1)^{i}A^{i}(U_{r,n})_{\mathbb{C}}\cong_{{\mathfrak{S}}_{n}}\begin{cases}0&\text{ if $r$ is even}\\
(-1)^{\frac{r-1}{2}}S^{H_{{\mathsf{Even}}(r-1),n}}&\text{ if $r$ is odd}\end{cases}.

or, equivalently, as graded Frobenius series

{\mathsf{grFrob}}(A(U_{r,n})_{\mathbb{C}},-1)=\begin{cases}0&\text{ if $r$ is even}\\
(-1)^{\frac{r-1}{2}}s_{H_{{\mathsf{Even}}(r-1),n}}&\text{ if $r$ is odd}\end{cases}.

where
s_{D}
is the Schur function of skew shape
D
.

Similarly, for the augmented case, we obtain the following.

Theorem 5.2
.

For any positive integer
n
and
1\leq r\leq n
, the
{\mathfrak{S}}_{n}
-equivariant Charney–Davis quantity of the augmented Chow ring of a uniform matroid is given by

\sum_{i=0}^{r}(-1)^{i}\widetilde{A}^{i}(U_{r,n})_{\mathbb{C}}\cong_{{\mathfrak{S}}_{n}}\begin{cases}(-1)^{\frac{r}{2}}S^{H_{{\mathsf{Odd}}(r-1),n}}&\text{ if $r$ is even}\\
0&\text{ if $r$ is odd}\end{cases},

or, equivalently, as graded Frobenius series

{\mathsf{grFrob}}(\widetilde{A}(U_{r,n})_{\mathbb{C}},-1)=\begin{cases}(-1)^{\frac{r}{2}}s_{H_{{\mathsf{Odd}}(r-1),n}}&\text{ if $r$ is even}\\
0,&\text{ if $r$ is odd}\end{cases}

Apply
\prod_{i=1}^{n}(1-q^{i}){\mathrm{ps}_{q}}(-)
to the symmetric function identities in Theorem
5.1
and
5.2
. Since for
R\subseteq[n-1]
, we have

\prod_{i=1}^{n}(1-q^{i}){\mathrm{ps}_{q}}(s_{R,n})=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\sigma\in{\mathfrak{S}}_{n}\\
{\mathsf{DES}}(\sigma)=R\end{subarray}}q^{{\mathsf{maj}}(\sigma^{-1})}=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\sigma\in{\mathfrak{S}}_{n}\\
{\mathsf{DES}}(\sigma)=R\end{subarray}}q^{{\mathsf{inv}}(\sigma)}

(see
[
SW20
, Theorem 4.4]
and its proof for a detailed discussion), then Theorem
4.2
together with the above identity leads to our formulas of the Charney–Davis quantities for
q
-uniform matroids
U_{r,n}(q)
.
In Theorem
5.3
(i) below, Hameister, Rao, and Simpson’s formula (
1
) of the Charney–Davis quantity is simplified to an elegant combinatorial interpretation, which is new even in the case of
q=1
.

Theorem 5.3
.

For any positive integer
n
and
1\leq r\leq n
, we have:

(i)

The Charney–Davis quantity of the Chow ring of an arbitrary
q
-uniform matroid is given by

{\mathsf{Hilb}}(A(U_{r,n}(q)),-1)=\begin{cases}0&\mbox{ if $r$ is even}\\
\displaystyle(-1)^{\frac{r-1}{2}}\!\!\!\!\!\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\sigma\in{\mathfrak{S}}_{n}\\
{\mathsf{DES}}(\sigma)={\mathsf{Even}}(r-1)\end{subarray}}q^{{\mathsf{inv}}(\sigma)}&\mbox{ if $r$ is odd}\end{cases}.

(ii)

The Charney–Davis quantity of the augmented Chow ring of an arbitrary
q
-uniform matroid is given by

{\mathsf{Hilb}}(\widetilde{A}(U_{r,n}(q)),-1)=\begin{cases}\displaystyle(-1)^{\frac{r}{2}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\sigma\in{\mathfrak{S}}_{n}\\
{\mathsf{DES}}(\sigma)={\mathsf{Odd}}(r-1)\end{subarray}}q^{{\mathsf{inv}}(\sigma)}&\mbox{ if $r$ is even}\\
0&\mbox{if $r$ is odd}\end{cases}.

In particular, both
CD(A(U_{r,n}(q)))
and
CD(\widetilde{A}\left(U_{r,n}(q))\right)
are polynomials in
\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}[q]
.

Remark 5.1
.

Following from Stanley
[
Sta11
, Example 2.2.4]
, the
q
-polynomial
\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\sigma\in{\mathfrak{S}}_{n}\\
{\mathsf{DES}}(\sigma)={\mathsf{Even}}(r-1)\end{subarray}}q^{{\mathsf{inv}}(\sigma)}
in Theorem
5.3
(i) can be expressed as one determinant

[n]_{q}!\left|\begin{array}[]{cccccc}\frac{1}{[2]_{q}!}&1&0&\ldots&0&0\\
\frac{1}{[4]_{q}!}&\frac{1}{[2]_{q}!}&1&\ldots&0&0\\
\frac{1}{[6]_{q}!}&\frac{1}{[4]_{q}!}&\frac{1}{[2]_{q}!}&\ldots&0&0\\
\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots&\vdots\\
\frac{1}{[r-1]_{q}!}&\frac{1}{[r-3]_{q}!}&\frac{1}{[r-5]_{q}!}&\ldots&\frac{1}{[2]_{q}!}&1\\
\frac{1}{[n]_{q}!}&\frac{1}{[n-2]_{q}!}&\frac{1}{[n-4]_{q}!}&\ldots&\frac{1}{[n-r+3]_{q}!}&\frac{1}{[n-r+1]_{q}!}\end{array}\right|,

which simplifies Hameister, Rao, and Simpson’s formula (
2
). The
q
-polynomial can also be obtained by realizing the alternating sum in (
1
) as a process of inclusion-exclusion.

Remark 5.2
.

Combining with Corollary
1.2
, Theorem
5.3
(i) generalizes Foata and Han’s result in
[
FH10
, Theorem 1]
.

Remark 5.3
.

Similarly, the
q
-polynomial
\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\sigma\in{\mathfrak{S}}_{n}\\
{\mathsf{DES}}(\sigma)={\mathsf{Odd}}(r-1)\end{subarray}}q^{{\mathsf{inv}}(\sigma)}
in Theorem
5.3
(ii) can be expressed as

[n]_{q}!\left|\begin{array}[]{cccccc}\frac{1}{[1]_{q}!}&1&0&\ldots&0&0\\
\frac{1}{[3]_{q}!}&\frac{1}{[2]_{q}!}&1&\ldots&0&0\\
\frac{1}{[5]_{q}!}&\frac{1}{[4]_{q}!}&\frac{1}{[2]_{q}!}&\ldots&0&0\\
\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots&\vdots\\
\frac{1}{[r-1]_{q}!}&\frac{1}{[r-2]_{q}!}&\frac{1}{[r-4]_{q}!}&\ldots&\frac{1}{[2]_{q}!}&1\\
\frac{1}{[n]_{q}!}&\frac{1}{[n-1]_{q}!}&\frac{1}{[n-3]_{q}!}&\ldots&\frac{1}{[n-r+3]_{q}!}&\frac{1}{[n-r+1]_{q}!}\end{array}\right|.

On the other hand, one can obtain the following augmented analog of Hameister, Rao, and Simpson’s formula (
1
) by the principle of inclusion-exclusion

(-1)^{\frac{r}{2}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\sigma\in{\mathfrak{S}}_{n}\\
{\mathsf{DES}}(\sigma)={\mathsf{Odd}}(r-1)\end{subarray}}q^{{\mathsf{inv}}(\sigma)}=1+\sum_{k=0}^{\frac{r-2}{2}}(-1)^{k+1}{n\brack 2k+1}_{q}E^{*}_{2k+1}(q),

where
E^{*}_{2k+1}(q)=\sum_{\sigma\in{\mathsf{Alt}}_{2k+1}}q^{{\mathsf{inv}}(\sigma)}
and
{\mathsf{Alt}}_{2k+1}=\{\sigma\in{\mathfrak{S}}_{2k+1}:\sigma_{1}>\sigma_{2}<\sigma_{3}\ldots<\sigma_{2k+1}\}
is the set of
alternating permutations
in
{\mathfrak{S}}_{2k+1}
. Note that
E_{n}(q)
and
E^{*}_{n}(q)
are related by
E^{*}_{n}(q)=q^{\binom{n}{2}}E_{n}(q)
.

Again, because Theorem
4.2
tells us that the Hilbert series for
U_{r,n}(q)
agrees with the stable principle specialization of the Frobenius characteristics for
U_{r,n}
, letting
q=1
gives us the Hilbert series for
U_{r,n}
.
Therefore, we obtain the Charney–Davis quantities for
U_{r,n}
by setting
q=1
in Theorem
5.3
.

Corollary 5.4
.

For any positive integer
n
and
1\leq r\leq n
, we have:

(i)

The Charney–Davis quantity of the Chow ring of an arbitrary uniform matroid is given by

{\mathsf{Hilb}}(A(U_{r,n}),-1)=\begin{cases}0&\mbox{ if $r$ is even}\\
(-1)^{\frac{r-1}{2}}|\{\sigma\in{\mathfrak{S}}_{n}:{\mathsf{DES}}(\sigma)={\mathsf{Even}}(r-1)\}|&\mbox{ if $r$ is odd}\end{cases}.

(ii)

The Charney–Davis quantity of the augmented Chow ring of an arbitrary uniform matroid is given by

{\mathsf{Hilb}}\left(\widetilde{A}(U_{r,n}),-1\right)=\begin{cases}(-1)^{\frac{r}{2}}|\{\sigma\in{\mathfrak{S}}_{n}:{\mathsf{DES}}(\sigma)={\mathsf{Odd}}(r-1)\}|&\mbox{ if $r$ is even}\\
0&\mbox{ if $r$ is odd}\end{cases}.

In particular, both
CD(A(U_{r,n}))
and
CD\left(\widetilde{A}(U_{r,n})\right)
are nonnegative.

Remark 5.4
.

When
r=n
, the Hilbert series of
A(U_{n,n})
and
\widetilde{A}(U_{n,n})
are exactly the
h
-polynomials of the permutahedron
\mathsf{Perm}_{n}
and the stellohedron
\mathsf{St}_{n}
, respectively. Hence Corollary
5.4
(i) and (ii) recover the facts that the Charney–Davis quantities of the permutahedron and the stellohedron give the tangent number and the secant number, respectively:

CD(\mathsf{Perm}_{n})=\begin{cases}0&\text{ if $n$ is even}\\
E_{n}&\text{ if $n$ is odd}\end{cases};\qquad CD(\mathsf{St}_{n})=\begin{cases}E_{n}&\text{ if $n$ is even}\\
0&\text{ if $n$ is odd}\end{cases}.

6.
Some Further developements

Within the framework of palindromic polynomials, there is a generalization of the nonnegativity of the Charney–Davis quantity called
\gamma
-positivity
or
\gamma
-nonnegativity
.
It has been shown independently by Ferroni, Matherne, Stevens, Vecchi
[
FMSV24
, Theorem 3.25]
and Wang (see
[
FMSV24
, p. 29]
) that the Hilbert series of
A(M)
and
\widetilde{A}(M)
are
\gamma
-positive, which follows from the work of Braden, Huh, Matherne, Proudfoot, and Wang
[
BHM+22
]
on the
semismall decomposition
of Chow rings.

One also has a notion of
equivariant
\gamma
-positivity
for a graded representation
A=\bigoplus_{i=0}^{r}A^{i}
, which was first considered by Shareshian and Wachs
[
SW20
, Section 5]
.
Consider the subgroup
G
of
{\mathrm{Aut}}(M)
acting on
A(M)
and
\widetilde{A}(M)
. It has been conjectured by Angarone, Nathanson, and Reiner
[
ANR25
, Conjecture 5.2]
that
A(M)_{\mathbb{C}}=\bigoplus_{i=0}^{r-1}A^{i}(M)
is
G
-equivariant
\gamma
-positive. In
[
Lia24a
]
, Liao generalized Theorem
1.10
and
1.11
, showing that both
A(M)
and
\widetilde{A}(M)
are
G
-equivariant
\gamma
-positive. This result confirms the conjecture of Angarone, Nathanson, and Reiner.

Since the preliminary version of this paper appeared on
arXiv
, there have been several interesting developments beyond the equivariant
\gamma
-positivity results previously mentioned.

Ferroni, Matherne, and Vecchi
[
FMV24
]
generalized the notion of the (augmented) Chow polynomial of a matroid—that is, the Hilbert series of the (augmented) Chow ring—to any finite graded poset and integrated it into the Kazhdan–Lusztig–Stanley theory (see
[
Sta92
,
Pro18
]
). Theorems
1.10
and
1.11
can be extended to the equivariant Chow polynomial for Cohen–Macaulay posets using the same proof.

Hoster
[
Hos24
]
gave interpretations to the (augmented) Chow polynomials of
U_{r,n}
, distinct from Theorems
1.1
and
1.3
, in terms of
“permuted” Schubert matroids
with
cogirths
greater than
n-r
.
Since cogirth is invariant under matroid isomorphism, this interpretation can be extended to the
h
-expansion of
{\mathfrak{S}}_{n}
-equivariant (augmented) Chow polynomials.
In the case of
U_{n,n}
, the interpretations can be proven via an
{\mathfrak{S}}_{n}
-equivariant bijection from
\widetilde{FY}(U_{n,n})
to permuted Schubert matroids, as given by Eur, Huh, and Larson
[
EHL23
, Proof of Theorem 7.7]
. However, restricting this bijection to
\widetilde{FY}(U_{r,n})
does not yield Hoster’s interpretation. It would be interesting to characterize the image of this restriction and compare it with Hoster’s interpretation.

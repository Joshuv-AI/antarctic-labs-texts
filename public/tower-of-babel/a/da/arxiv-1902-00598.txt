Dynamic Equivalence of Control Systems and Infinite Permutation Matrices
Jeanne N. CLELLAND †, Yuhao HU † and Matthew W. STACKPOLE ‡

Abstract
To each dynamic equivalence of two control systems is associated an infinite permutation matrix. We investigate how such matrices are related to the existence of dynamic equivalences.

Introduction

A
control system
is an underdetermined ODE system of the form

\displaystyle\dot{\bf x}={\bf f}(t,{\bf x},{\bf u}),

where
{\bf x}=(x_{i})
are called the
state variables
and
{\bf u}=(u_{\alpha})
the
control variables
. The meaning of “control” is clear: Under suitable regularity conditions, specifying a control function
{\bf u}(t)
and an initial state
{\bf x}(t_{0})
uniquely determines a local “trajectory”
{\bf x}(t)
that satisfies the
ODE system and the initial state. When
{\bf f}
does not explicitly depend on
t
, the control system is said to be
autonomous
, which we will assume throughout this paper.

Let
\dot{\bf x}={\bf f}({\bf x},{\bf u})
and
\dot{\bf y}={\bf g}({\bf y},{\bf v})
be two control systems. Suppose that there exist mappings
\phi=\phi\big({\bf x},{\bf u},\dot{\bf u},\ddot{\bf u},\dots,{\bf u}^{(p)}\big)
and
\psi=\psi\big({\bf y},{\bf v},\dot{\bf v},\ddot{\bf v},\dots,{\bf v}^{(q)}\big)
such that,

•

for any solution
({\bf x}(t),{\bf u}(t))
of the first system, the function
({\bf y},{\bf v})=\phi\big({\bf x},{\bf u},\dot{\bf u},\ddot{\bf u},\dots,{\bf u}^{(p)}\big)
is a solution of the second;

•

for any solution
({\bf y}(t),{\bf v}(t))
of the second system, the function
({\bf x},{\bf u})=\psi\big({\bf y},{\bf v},\dot{\bf v},\ddot{\bf v},\dots,\allowbreak{\bf v}^{(q)}\big)
is a solution of the first;

•

moreover, applying
\phi
and
\psi
successively to a solution
({\bf x}(t),{\bf u}(t))
of the first system yields the same solution
({\bf x}(t),{\bf u}(t))
, and, similarly, applying
\psi
and
\phi
successively to a solution
({\bf y}(t),{\bf v}(t))
of the second system yields the same solution
({\bf y}(t),{\bf v}(t))
.

If all these conditions are satisfied, we say that the pair of maps
(\phi,\psi)
establishes a
dynamic equivalence
between the two control systems. Intuitively, a dynamic equivalence provides a one-to-one correspondence between the solutions of one control system with those of the other.

Fixing a dynamic equivalence
(\phi,\psi)
, one can always find the smallest
p,q\geq 0
so that
\phi=\phi\big({\bf x},{\bf u},\dot{\bf u},\ddot{\bf u},\dots,{\bf u}^{(p)}\big)
and
\psi=\psi\big({\bf y},{\bf v},\dot{\bf v},\ddot{\bf v},\dots,{\bf v}^{(q)}\big)
. We call such a pair
(p,q)
the
height
of the corresponding dynamic equivalence. A dynamic equivalence with height
(0,0)
is known as a
static
(
feedback
)
equivalence
, in which case
\phi
,
\psi
are inverses of each other as diffeomorphisms.

An immediate question is:
How much more general is the notion of dynamic equivalence than that of static equivalence?
Classical results (see
[
10
,
12
]
) suggest that the answer depends on the number of control variables. In particular, a dynamic equivalence between two control systems with a single control variable is necessarily static. It is also well known that the number of control variables is invariant under a dynamic equivalence. However, in the cases of 2 or more controls, a precise answer to the question above remains largely unknown.

In
[
14
]
, the author considered all
control-affine
systems with 3 states and 2 controls, proving that three statically non-equivalent systems are pairwise dynamically equivalent at height
(1,1)
. In addition, he introduced a new method of studying dynamic equivalences of two control systems. He found that to each dynamic equivalence is associated an infinite permutation matrix. Intuitively, such a matrix tells us how the ‘generating
1
-forms’ of certain prolongations of the two control systems (viewed as Pfaffian systems), when chosen appropriately, relate under a dynamic equivalence.

In the current work, we present further properties of dynamic equivalences that can be derived using the associated infinite permutation matrices. First we prove that there is a
rank matrix
(Definition
3.6
) associated to a dynamic equivalence, which has a more ‘invariant’ nature than an associated infinite permutation matrix (Proposition
3.7
). Then we prove several inequalities and equalities (Propositions
4.1
and
4.2
) satisfied by the rank matrix. Using these results, we prove an inequality satisfied by the height
(p,q)
of a dynamic equivalence (Theorem
4.3
). In particular,
this inequality implies the

Theorem
4.5
.

The height
(p,q)
of a dynamic equivalence between two control systems with
n_{1}
and
n_{2}
states, respectively, and
2
controls must satisfy

n_{1}+p=n_{2}+q
.

2.3
Dynamic equivalence

Given two control systems, it is natural to regard them as equivalent if one can establish a one-to-one correspondence between their solutions.

Of course, two control systems
\dot{\bf x}={\bf f}({\bf x},{\bf u})
and
\dot{\bf y}={\bf g}({\bf y},{\bf v})
are equivalent in the sense above when they can be transformed into each other by a change of variables of the form
{\bf y}=\phi({\bf x})
,
{\bf v}=\xi({\bf x},{\bf u})
and
{\bf x}=\phi^{-1}({\bf y})
,
{\bf u}=\rho({\bf y},{\bf v})
. This notion of equivalence is called
static equivalence
, which, in particular, requires the two equivalent control systems to have same number of states and the same number of controls. However, it is possible for two systems with different numbers of states to have a one-to-one correspondence between their solutions, as is indicated by the following standard property of jet bundles.

Proposition 2.3
.

Let
(M,\mathcal{C}_{M})
be a control system. Let
\pi\colon M^{(k)}\rightarrow M
be the canonical projection from its
k
-th total prolongation. Any integral curve
\tau\colon\mathbb{R}\rightarrow M
of
(M,\mathcal{C}_{M})
has a unique lifting
\tau^{(k)}\colon\mathbb{R}\rightarrow M^{(k)}

(
i.e., satisfying
\pi\circ\tau^{(k)}=\tau)
to an integral curve of
\big(M^{(k)},\mathcal{C}^{(k)}\big)
. In addition, for each integral curve
\sigma\colon\mathbb{R}\rightarrow M^{(k)}
of
\big(M^{(k)},\mathcal{C}^{(k)}\big)
, its projection
\pi\circ\sigma
is an integral curve of
(M,\mathcal{C}_{M})
.

In other words, given two control systems, a one-to-one correspondence between their solutions may involve differentiation. This motivates the following notion of equivalence.

\displaystyle\equiv-A^{0}_{p}{\boldsymbol{\omega}}^{p+1}\wedge\mathrm{d}t\mod{\boldsymbol{\omega}}^{0},{\boldsymbol{\omega}}^{1},\dots,{\boldsymbol{\omega}}^{p}.

3.1
Two classical theorems

Concerning dynamic equivalences between two control systems, the following two classical theorems are of fundamental importance.

Theorem 3.1

(
[
10
,
12
]
)
.

Two dynamically equivalent control systems must have the same number of control variables.

Theorem 3.2

(
[
4
,
13
]
)
.

A dynamic equivalence between two control systems with
n
states and single control can only have height
(p,q)=(0,0)

(
i.e., the equivalence is static
)
.

Remark 3.3
.

The original result of Cartan takes a different form. In particular, the notion of equivalence considered in
[
4
]
is
absolute equivalence
. On the other hand, it is a consequence of Corollary 26 in
[
13
]
that two control systems are absolutely equivalent (in an appropriate sense as

3.2
may be regarded as a version of Cartan’s result (see also Corollary 12 in
[
13
]
).

It turns out that these two theorems are consequences of the fact that the following matrices are inverses of each other, after taking into account the maps
\Phi^{(\infty)}
and

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{A}=\left(\begin{matrix}A^{0}_{0}&\cdots&A^{0}_{p}&\boldsymbol{0}&\cdots\\

\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\ddots\end{matrix}\right),\qquad\boldsymbol{B}=\left(\begin{matrix}B^{0}_{0}&\cdots&B^{0}_{q}&\boldsymbol{0}&\cdots\\
B^{1}_{0}&\cdots&B^{1}_{p}&B_{\infty}&\cdots\\
\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\ddots\end{matrix}\right).

(3.3)

In fact, suppose that
(M,\mathcal{C}_{M})
and
(N,\mathcal{C}_{N})
have

n_{2}
states and
m_{1}

m_{2}
controls, respectively. We can arrange that

by partially prolonging one of the systems, if necessary. Moreover, since partial prolongations preserve the number of controls, this will have no effect on the values of

, there are two possible cases:

More generally, we have the

Suppose that

Proof.

This is because

(4.10)

Furthermore, we have

\displaystyle E:=(q-1)(m-r_{1})-\big(n_{1}-r^{0}_{0}-r_{2}\big)-C=(p-1)(m-r_{2})-\big(n_{2}-r^{0}_{0}-r_{1}\big)-D.

Using (
4.10
), we obtain

\displaystyle\max\{r_{1}p+n_{1},r_{2}q+n_{2}\}\leq E-r^{0}_{0}+n_{1}+n_{2}

\displaystyle\qquad{}\leq\min\{(p-1)\delta+r_{1}p+n_{1},(q-1)\delta+r_{2}q+n_{2}\}.

The conclusion follows.∎

Corollary 4.4
.

Let
\mathcal{E}
be a dynamic equivalence between two control systems with
n_{1}
,
n_{2}
states, respectively, and
m
controls. If
\operatorname{rank}(A_{\infty})+\operatorname{rank}(B_{\infty})=m
, then the height
(p,q)
of
\mathcal{E}
must satisfy: when
p,q>0
, we have

\displaystyle n_{1}+\operatorname{rank}(A_{\infty})\cdot p=n_{2}+\operatorname{rank}(B_{\infty})\cdot q.

(4.11)

Proof.

This is an immediate consequence of setting
\delta=0
in (
4.9
).∎

Theorem 4.5
.

The height
(p,q)
of a dynamic equivalence between two control systems with
n_{1}
and
n_{2}
states, respectively, and
2
controls must satisfy
n_{1}+p=n_{2}+q
.

Proof.

Let
(M,\mathcal{C}_{M})
and
(N,\mathcal{C}_{N})
be the two control systems in question with a dynamic equivalence given by submersions
\Phi\colon M^{(p)}\rightarrow N
and
\Psi\colon N^{(q)}\rightarrow M
.

First consider the case when
n_{1}=n_{2}
. By Proposition
2.8
, we have either
p=q=0
or
p,q>0
. In the former case, there is nothing to prove. In the latter case, when
m=2
, Lemma
3.4
implies that the only possibility is
\operatorname{rank}(A_{\infty})=\operatorname{rank}(B_{\infty})=1
; then apply Corollary
4.4
.

It remains to consider the case when
n_{1}\neq n_{2}
. Assume that
n_{1}>n_{2}
. The case when
p,q>0
follows from a similar argument as the above. Thus, it suffices to consider the case when
p=0
. Suppose that, under a choice of coordinates
(t,{\bf y},{\bf v})
on
N
,
\Psi
depends nontrivially on
v_{\alpha}^{(q)}
for some
\alpha
. Fixing an index
\beta\neq\alpha
, we can construct a partial prolongation
\big(\bar{N},\bar{\mathcal{C}}_{N}\big)
by letting
\bar{N}=N\times\mathbb{R}^{n_{1}-n_{2}}
with coordinates
\big(t,{\bf y},{\bf v},v_{\beta}^{(1)},\dots,v_{\beta}^{(n_{1}-n_{2})}\big)
and
\bar{\mathcal{C}}_{N}
being generated by

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}_{N},\,\mathrm{d}v_{\beta}-v_{\beta}^{(1)}\mathrm{d}t,\,\dots,\,\mathrm{d}v_{\beta}^{(n_{1}-n_{2}-1)}-v_{\beta}^{(n_{1}-n_{2})}\mathrm{d}t.

Let
\bar{\Phi}\colon M^{(n_{1}-n_{2})}\rightarrow\bar{N}
(indicated by the dashed arrow in the diagram below) denote the submersion induced by
\Phi
. Let
\bar{\pi}^{(q)}\colon\bar{N}^{(q)}\rightarrow N^{(q)}
be the submersion induced by the natural projection
\bar{\pi}\colon\bar{N}\rightarrow N

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces M^{(n_{1}-n_{2})}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\bar{N}^{(q)}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces N^{(q)}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces M}

\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Phi}

It is easy to see that the pair of submersions
\bar{\Phi}

establishes a dynamic equivalence between
(M,\mathcal{C}_{M})

Remark 4.6

) does not need to hold. For details, see Example

Example 4.8
.

Let
(M,\mathcal{C}_{M})
(
\dot{\bf x}={\bf f}({\bf x},{\bf u})

\dot{y}_{2}=v_{2},\\

Example 4.9
.

Assume
n_{1}>n_{2}
in Theorem

. This theorem tells us that even though

(in order for

) as long as

\displaystyle q\geq\frac{n_{1}-n_{2}}{2},

. One may compare this fact with Theorem 52 in
[
13
]
.

As an example, we consider the PVTOL system (see
[
10
]
).

Using the coordinates
(t,(x,z,\theta,\dot{x},\dot{z},\dot{\theta}),(u_{1},u_{2}))
on
M
, let
\mathcal{C}_{M}
be generated by the six
1

\displaystyle\mathrm{d}x-\dot{x}\mathrm{d}t,

\displaystyle\mathrm{d}z-\dot{z}\mathrm{d}t,

\displaystyle\mathrm{d}\theta-\dot{\theta}\mathrm{d}t,

\displaystyle\mathrm{d}\dot{x}-(-u_{1}\sin\theta+\epsilon u_{2}\cos\theta)\mathrm{d}t,

\displaystyle\mathrm{d}\dot{z}-(u_{1}\cos\theta+\epsilon u_{2}\sin\theta-1)\mathrm{d}t,

\displaystyle\mathrm{d}\dot{\theta}-u_{2}\mathrm{d}t,

]

On a conjecture of Laplacian energy of trees
Hilal A. Ganie, Bilal A. Rather, S. Pirzada

Abstract




Abstract.

Let
G
be a simple graph with
n
vertices,
m
edges having Laplacian eigenvalues
\mu_{1},\mu_{2},\dots,\mu_{n-1},\mu_{n}=0
. The Laplacian energy
LE(G)
is defined as
LE(G)=\sum_{i=1}^{n}|\mu_{i}-\overline{d}|
, where
\overline{d}=\frac{2m}{n}
is the average degree of
G
. Radenković and Gutman conjectured that among all trees of order
n
, the path graph
P_{n}
has the smallest Laplacian energy. Let
\mathcal{T}_{n}(d)
be the family of trees of order
n
having diameter
d
. In this paper, we show that Laplacian energy of any tree
T\in\mathcal{T}_{n}(4)
is greater than the Laplacian energy of
P_{n}
, thereby proving the conjecture for all trees of diameter
4
. We also show the truth of conjecture for all trees with number of non-pendent vertices at most
\frac{9n}{25}-2
. Further, we give some sufficient conditions for the conjecture to hold for a tree of order
n
.

Keywords: Laplacian matrix, Laplacian energy, diameter, conjecture.

AMS subject classification: 05C50, 05C12, 15A18.

1
Introduction

Let
G=(V,E)
be a simple graph with vertex set
V(G)=\{v_{1},v_{2},\dots,v_{n}\}
and edge set
E(G)=\{e_{1},e_{2},\dots,e_{m}\}
. The parameter
n=|V(G)|
is the order while
m=|E(G)|
is the size of the graph
G
.
For a vertex
v_{i}\in V(G)
let
d(v_{i})
(or simply by
d_{i}
, if underling graph
G
is clear from the context) denotes the degree of vertex
v_{i}
in
G.
A vertex of degree one is called a pendent vertex or a leaf. A tree of order
n
denoted by
T_{n}
is a connected acyclic graph (that is a graph having no cycle). A path
P_{n}
of order
n
is a tree with two pendent vertices. A star
S_{n}
on
n
vertices is a tree having
n-1
pendent vertices and one vertex of degree
n-1
. For other notations and undefined terminology from graph theory and matrix theory, we refer reader to any standard text, such as
[
2
,
15
,
22
]
.

Let
A(G)
be the
(0,1)
-adjacency matrix of
G
with eigenvalues
\lambda_{1}(G)\geq\lambda_{2}(G)\geq\dots\geq\lambda_{n}(G)
. The concept of energy
\mathbb{E}(G)
of a graph
G
was introduced in 1978 by I. Gutman
[
8
]
and is defined as

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}(G)=\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}|\lambda_{i}|.

This quantity has been studied both chemically and mathematically. The concept of energy was not only extended to other type of matrices associated with a graph, in fact it has been extended to general matrices
[
20
]
. A lot of work has been done in this direction and the research is still on as there are various unsettled problems. For some recent work regarding graph energy and related result we refer to
[
18
,
23
]
and the references therein.

Let
D(G)=diag(d_{1},d_{2},\dots,d_{n})
be the diagonal matrix of vertex degrees
d_{i}=d_{G}(v_{i}),i=1,2,\dots,n
of graph
G.
The real symmetric positive semi-definite matrix
L(G)=D(G)-A(G)
is the Laplacian matrix of the graph
G
and its eigenvalues which we denote by
\mu_{1}(G)\geq\mu_{2}(G)\geq\dots\geq\mu_{n-1}\geq\mu_{n}(G)=0
are the Laplacian eigenvalues of
G
. The Laplacian energy
LE(G)
of a graph
G
was introduced in 2006 by Gutman and Zhou
[
9
]
and is defined as

\displaystyle LE(G)=\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}\left|\mu_{i}-\overline{d}\right|,

where
\overline{d}=\frac{2m}{n}
is the average degree of
G
. Using the fact
\sum\limits_{1=i}^{n-1}\mu_{i}=2m
, it is easy to see that
[
6
]

LE(G)=2\left(\sum\limits_{i=1}^{\sigma}\mu_{i}-\sigma\overline{d}\right)=2\max_{1\leq k\leq n}\left(\sum\limits_{i=1}^{k}\mu_{i}-k\overline{d}\right),

(1.1)

where
\sigma
is the number of Laplacian eigenvalues greater than or equal to average degree
\overline{d}
. This quantity is extensively studied because of its applications and the connections with other important spectral parameters of the graph
G
. The parameter
\sigma
is also an active component of the present research and some works mostly on trees can be found in the literature
[
26
]
. For some recent works on Laplacian energy and related results we refer to
[
14
]
and the references therein.

An important problem in the spectral graph theory is to determine the extremal value of some spectral parameter and the characterization of the graph/graphs which attain this extremal value among all the graph of order
n
or among a class of graph of order
n
. This problem has been considered for energy
\mathbb{E}(G)
of a graph for various families (like trees, unicyclic graphs, etc) and the graphs attaining the maximum/minimum values are completely determined. For trees, it is shown in
[
7
]
that the star graph
S_{n}
has the minimum energy while the path graph
P_{n}
has the maximum energy among all trees of order
n
.

Like energy of a graph, a natural extremal question involving the Laplacian energy of a graph is to ask for the extremal value of this parameter among all
n
-vertex graphs, and for the
n
-vertex graph (or for the family of such graphs)
whose Laplacian energy achieves this extremal value. However, this seems to be a hard problem, which,
to the best of our knowledge, is still open. Radenković and Gutman
[
25
]
studied the correlation between the energy and the Laplacian energy of trees. They computed the energy and Laplacian energy of all trees up to
14
vertices and formulated the following conjecture.

Conjecture 1

:
Let
T
be a tree on
n
vertices. Then

\displaystyle LE(P_{n})\leq LE(T)\leq LE(S_{n}).

For trees of diameter
3
, it is shown in
[
27
]
that the Conjecture
1
is true. In the same paper
[
27
]
the authors have shown by direct computations that the Conjecture
1
is true for all trees up to
18
vertices. The right inequality of the Conjecture
1
is shown to be true by Fritscher et al. in
[
6
]
for all trees of order
n
. For the left inequality, Chang et al. in
[
3
]
verified the Conjecture
1
for trees of diameter
4
and
5
with perfect matching. Recently, Rahman et al.
[
24
]
considered some families of trees of diameter
4
(not considered in
[
3
]
) and verified the truth of left inequality of the Conjecture
1
for trees belonging to these families. But as a whole the left hand inequality of the Conjecture
1
is still open. It is clear from (
1.1
) that if we want to compare the Laplacian energies of two graphs with same number of vertices and edges, we need information (either exact or very close to exact) about the sum of their largest Laplacian eigenvalues and about the number of Laplacian eigenvalues that are larger than the average degree. Both these informations are closely related to the structure of the graph and so considering this problem among a class of graphs is a very hard problem.

In this paper our aim is to verify the truth of left hand inequality of the Conjecture
1
for trees of diameter
4
completely.

It is worth to mention that Conjecture
1
is also of interest from Matrix theory point of view. Let
\mathbb{M}_{n}(\mathbb{C})
be the set of all square matrices of order
n
with complex entries. The trace norm of a matrix
M\in\mathbb{M}_{n}(\mathbb{C})
is defined as
\lVert M\rVert_{*}=\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}\sigma_{i}(M)
, where
\sigma_{1}(M)\geq\sigma_{2}(M)\geq\cdots\geq\sigma_{n}(M)
are the singular values of
M
(i.e. the square roots of the eigenvalues of
MM^{*}
, where
M^{*}
is the complex conjugate of
M
). It is well known that for a symmetric matrix
M
, if
\sigma_{i}(M)
are the singular values and
\lambda_{i}(M)
,
i=1,2,\dots,n,
are the eigenvalues, then
\sigma_{i}(M)=|\lambda_{i}(M)|
. In the light of this definition, it follows that the Laplacian energy
LE(G)
of a graph
G
is the trace norm of the matrix
L(G)-\frac{2m}{n}I_{n}
, where
I_{n}
is the identity matrix of order
n
. It is an interesting problem in Matrix theory to determine among a given class of matrices the matrix (or the matrices) which attain the maximum value and the minimum value for the trace norm. The trace norm of matrices associated with the graphs and digraphs are extensively studied. For some recent papers in this direction see
[
19
]
and the references therein.
Using trace norm concept we can reformulate the Conjecture
1
as:

\displaystyle\lVert L(P_{n})-\frac{2(n-1)}{n}I_{n}\rVert_{*}\leq\lVert L(T)-\frac{2(n-1)}{n}I_{n}\rVert_{*}\leq\lVert L(S_{n})-\frac{2(n-1)}{n}I_{n}\rVert_{*}.

This gives another motivation for the study of left inequality of Conjecture
1
.

We use the computational software’s,
Wolfram Mathematica
and
Sage Math
, for various computations in the paper. For more literature in this direction, we refer the following references
[
26
,
28
]
and the references therein.

The paper is organised as follows. In Section
2
, we state some already known results. In Section
3
we obtain a lower for the Laplacian energy of a tree
T
and using this lower bound we obtain condition on the number of non-pendent vertices, which guarantees the truth of Conjecture
1
. We also devise a recursive method which is helpful to verify the truth of the Conjecture
1
. In Section
4
, we verify truth of the conjecture
1
for the trees of diameter at most
4
. Lastly, in Section
5
, we give some sufficient conditions for the Conjecture
1
to hold for a tree of order
n
.

2
Preliminaries

In this section, we mention an algorithm and some known results which are helpful throughout the paper.

The following observation which gives the multiplicity of eigenvalue
1
of the matrix
L(G)
and can be found in
[
4
]

Lemma 2.1

[
4
]

Let
p
and
q
be the number of leaves and neighbors of
G
, respectively. Then
1
is a Laplacian eigenvalue of
G
of multiplicity at least
p-q\geq 0

The following lemma gives a lower bound for the
i^{th}
-largest Laplacian eigenvalue in terms of
i^{th}
-largest degree of the graph
G
.

Lemma 2.2

[
1
]

Let
\mu_{1}\geq\mu_{2}\geq\dots\geq\mu_{n}
be the Laplacian eigenvalues of a connected graph
G
. Then

\displaystyle\mu_{i}\geq d_{i}-i+2,\qquad i=1,2,\dots,n,

where
d_{1}\geq\dots\geq d_{n}
is the degree sequence of the vertices of
G.

The main tool to prove our results is the following algorithm
[
5
,
17
]
, which can be used to
estimate the Laplacian eigenvalues of a given tree.

Algorithm (I).

The algorithm associates with each vertex
v
, a rational function
a(v)=\frac{r}{s}
. Here
r
and
s
are members of the polynomial ring
\mathbb{Q}[\lambda].
These are computed bottom-up starting with the leaves which are assigned
\lambda-1
(the trees can be rooted in an arbitrary way). Once all the children of
v
have been processed,
v
is assigned the function

\displaystyle a(v)=\lambda-d_{v}-\sum_{c\in C}\frac{1}{a(c)},

where
C
is the set of its children and
d_{v}
is the degree of
v
. After all the vertices have been processed, we compute the characteristic polynomial by taking the product of all functions
a(v)
:

\displaystyle p(\lambda)=\prod_{v\in V}a(v).

Algorithm (II)
:
[
16
]

Input: tree
T
, scalar
\alpha

Output: diagonal matrix D congruent to
L(T)+\alpha I

Algorithm Diagonalize (
T,\alpha
)

initialize
a(v):=d(v)+\alpha,
for all vertices
v

order vertices bottom up

for

k=1\ \text{to}\ n

if

v_{k}
is a leaf then continue

else if

a(c)\neq 0
for all children
c
of
v_{k}
then

a(v_{k}):=a(v_{k})-\sum\frac{1}{a(c)},
summing over all children of
v_{k}

else

select one child
v_{j}
of
v_{k}
for which
a(v_{j})=0

a(v_{k}):=-\frac{1}{2}

a(v_{j}):=2

if

v_{k}
has a parent
v_{l}
, remove the edge
v_{k}v_{l}

end loop

This algorithm is useful to calculate the number of eigenvalues of the Laplacian matrix of
T
lying in a
given interval and hence helps us to estimate
\sigma
for a tree. It is worth to mention that the diagonal elements of the output matrix correspond precisely to the values
a(v)
on each node
v
of the tree. The following observation due to Jacobs and Trevisan
[
16
]
is helpful throughout the paper.

Lemma 2.3

[
16
]

Let
T
be a tree and
D
be the diagonal matrix produced by the algorithm Diagonalize
(T,-\alpha)
. Then the following assertions hold.

(a)
The number of positive entries in
D
is the number of Laplacian eigenvalues of
T
that are greater than
\alpha.

(b)
The number of negative entries in
D
is the number of Laplacian eigenvalues of
T
that are smaller than
\alpha.

(c)
If there are
j
zero diagonal entries in
D
, then
\alpha
is a Laplacian eigenvalues of
T
with multiplicity
j
.

Using direct computation and the fact that

\displaystyle\frac{\pi}{n}\sum\limits_{j=1}^{\lfloor\frac{n}{2}\rfloor}\cos\frac{\pi j}{n}\leq\int\limits_{0}^{\frac{\pi}{2}}\sin xdx=1,

the following upper bound for the Laplacian energy of a path
P_{n}
was established in
[
27
]
.

Lemma 2.4

[
27
]

Let
P_{n}
be a path on
n
vertices. Then

\displaystyle LE(P_{n})\leq 2+\frac{4n}{\pi}.

The following observation can be found in
[
11
]
and gives the interlacing property of the Laplacian eigenvalues of a graph and its spanning subgraph.

Lemma 2.5

If
G^{\prime}=G+e
is the graph obtained from
G
by adding a new edge
e
, then the Laplacian
eigenvalues of
G
interlace the Laplacian eigenvalues of
G^{\prime}
, that is,

\displaystyle\mu_{1}(G^{\prime})\geq\mu_{1}(G)\geq\mu_{2}(G^{\prime})\geq\mu_{2}(G)\geq\cdots\geq\mu_{n}(G^{\prime})\geq\mu_{n}(G)=0.

The following result about the distribution of Laplacian eigenvalues of trees can be found in
[
26
]
.

Lemma 2.6

The number of Laplacian eigenvalues less than the average degree
2-\frac{2}{n}
of a tree
T
of order

is at least
\lceil\frac{n}{2}\rceil
.

3
Laplacian energy of a tree

In this section, we obtain a lower bound for the Laplacian energy of a tree
T
and using this lower bound we obtain condition on the number of non-pendent vertices, which guarantees the truth of Conjecture
1
. We also devise a recursive method which is helpful to verify the truth of Conjecture
1
.

For non-increasing real sequences
(x)=(x_{1},x_{2},\dots,x_{n})
and
(y)=(y_{1},y_{2},\dots,y_{n})
of length
n
, we say that
(x)
is majorized by
(y)
or
(y)
majorizes
(x)
, denoted by
(x)\preceq(y)
if

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}x_{i}=\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}y_{i}~~~\text{and}~~~\sum\limits_{i=1}^{k}x_{i}\leq\sum\limits_{i=1}^{k}y_{i},~~\text{for all}~~k=1,2,\dots,n-1.

The eigenvalues of the Laplacian matrix
L(G)
and majorization are connected by the
majorization relation between the eigenvalues and the diagonal elements. The following observation can be found in
[
10
]
.

Lemma 3.1

Let
G
be a graph of order
n
having Laplacian eigenvalues
\mu_{1}\geq\mu_{2}\geq\dots\geq\mu_{n}\geq 0
and vertex degrees
d_{1}\geq d_{2}\geq\dots\geq d_{n}
. Then
(d_{1},d_{2},\dots,d_{n})\preceq(\mu_{1},\mu_{2},\dots,\mu_{n})
, that is

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{i=1}^{k}\mu_{i}\geq 1+\sum\limits_{i=1}^{k}d_{i}~~\text{for all}~~k=1,2,\dots,n-1.

Theorem 3.2

Let
T
be a tree of order
n\geq 4
and let
P_{n}
be the path graph on
n
vertices. If
T
has
s
internal vertices(non-pendent) vertices, then

\displaystyle LE(T)\geq LE(P_{n}),

provided that
\Big(\frac{\pi-2}{\pi}\Big)n\geq s+2-\frac{2s}{n}
.

Proof.
Let
\mu_{1}(T)\geq\mu_{2}(T)\geq\cdots\geq\mu_{n-1}(T)\geq\mu_{n}(T)=0
be the Laplacian eigenvalues of
T
. Let
s\geq 1
be the number of internal vertices (that is, the vertices having degree greater than 1) of
T
, then the number of pendent vertices
p
of
T
is
p=n-s
. Since
1\leq s\leq n-2
, therefore using definition of Laplacian energy (
1.1
), we have

\displaystyle LE(T)=

\displaystyle 2\max_{1\leq k\leq n}\left(\sum\limits_{1=i}^{k}\mu_{i}(T)-k\overline{d}\right)

\displaystyle\geq 2\Big(\sum\limits_{i=1}^{s}\mu_{i}(T)-s\overline{d}\Big).

(3.2)

Let
d_{1}(T)\geq d_{2}(T)\geq\cdots\geq d_{n}(T)
be the vertex degree of
T
, then by Lemma
3.1
, we have

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{i=1}^{k}\mu_{i}(T)\geq 1+\sum\limits_{i=1}^{k}d_{i},~~~\text{for~~ all}~~~k=1,2,\dots,n-1.

Using this in (
3
), we get

\displaystyle LE(T)\geq 2\Big(1+\sum\limits_{1=i}^{s}d_{i}-s\overline{d}\Big).

(3.3)

Since
d_{1}+d_{2}+\cdots+d_{n}=2m=2n-2
and
T
has
p=n-s
pendent vertices, it follows that
d_{1}+d_{2}+\cdots+d_{s}+n-s=2n-2
, which implies that
\sum\limits_{1=i}^{s}d_{i}=n+s-2
. Using this in (
3.3
), we get

\displaystyle LE(T)\geq 2n+2s-2-2s\overline{d}\geq 2+\frac{4n}{\pi},

provided that
\Big(\frac{\pi-2}{\pi}\Big)n\geq s+2-\frac{2s}{n}
. Now, using Lemma
2.4
, the result follow.

The following observation is clear from the proof of Theorem
3.2
.

Corollary 3.3

For a tree
T
on
n
vertices having vertex degrees
d_{1}\geq d_{2}\geq\cdots\geq d_{n}
. If
k
,
1\leq k\leq n-1
is any positive integer, then

\displaystyle LE(T)\geq 2\Big(1+\sum\limits_{1=i}^{k}d_{i}-k\overline{d}\Big).

From this Corollary
3.3
, it follows that any information about the degrees of a tree
T
can be used to obtain a lower bound for Laplacian energy
LE(T)
of
T
, which in turn can be helpful to get an insight for the possible solution of the Conjecture
1
.

The following observation is also immediate from Theorem
3.2
.

Corollary 3.4

Let
T
be a tree on
n
vertices having Laplacian eigenvalues
\mu_{1}\geq\mu_{2}\geq\cdots\geq\mu_{n}
. If
k
,
1\leq k\leq n-1
is any positive integer, then

\displaystyle LE(T)\geq 2\Big(\sum\limits_{i=1}^{k}\mu_{i}-k\overline{d}\Big).

From Corollary
3.4
, it is clear that any lower bound for the sum of
k
largest Laplacian eigenvalues
S_{k}(T)=\sum\limits_{i=1}^{k}\mu_{i}
of
T
can be used to obtain a lower bound for Laplacian energy
LE(T)
, which in turn can be helpful to get an insight for the possible solution of Conjecture
1
.

The following observation gives the insight how Theorem
3.2
can be helpful to prove the Conjecture
1
.

Corollary 3.5

Let
T
be a tree of order
n\geq 4
having
s
internal vertices(non-pendent) vertices.

(i)
If
s=1
, then Conjecture
1
holds for all
n\geq 9
.
(ii)
If
s=2
, then Conjecture
1
holds for all
n\geq 12
.
(iii)
If
s=3
, then Conjecture
1
holds for all
n\geq 14
.
(iv)
If
s=4
, then Conjecture
1
holds for all
n\geq 17
.
(v)
If
s=5
, then Conjecture
1
holds for all
n\geq 20
.
(vi)
If
s=6
, then Conjecture
1
holds for all
n\geq 23
.
(vii)
If
s=7
, then Conjecture
1
holds for all
n\geq 25
.
(viii)
If
s\leq\frac{9n}{25}-2
, then Conjecture
1
holds for all

.

The result gives a lower bound for the Laplacian energy of a tree
T
in terms of sum of
k_{i}
largest Laplacian eigenvalues of
T_{i}
, where
T_{i}
, for
i=1,2
are components of
T
obtained by deleting any non-pendent edge.

Theorem 3.6

Let
T
be a tree of order
n\geq 8
and let
e
be a non-pendent edge of
T
. Let
T-e=T_{1}\cup T_{2}
and let
\sigma
be the number of Laplacian eigenvalues of
T-e
which are greater than or equal to average degree
\overline{d}(T-e)
. Then

\displaystyle LE(T)\geq 2S_{k_{1}}(T_{1})+2S_{k_{2}}(T_{2})-4\sigma+\frac{4\sigma}{n},

where
k_{1},k_{2}
are respectively, the number of Laplacian eigenvalues of
T_{1}
,
T_{2}
which are greater than or equal to
\overline{d}(T-e)
with
k_{1}+k_{2}=\sigma
and
S_{k}(T)
is the sum of
k
largest Laplacian eigenvalues of
T
.

Proof.
Let
\mu_{1}(T)\geq\mu_{2}(T)\geq\cdots\geq\mu_{n-1}(T)\geq\mu_{n}(T)=0
be the Laplacian eigenvalues of
T
having average degree
\overline{d}(T)=2-\frac{2}{n}
. Let
e
be a non-pendent edge in
T
and let
T_{1}
,
T_{2}
be the components of
T-e
. Let
|V(T_{i})|=n_{i}
, for
i=1,2
and
\overline{d}(T-e)
be the average degree of
T-e
. Then
n=n_{1}+n_{2}
and
\overline{d}(T-e)=2-\frac{4}{n}
. Let
\sigma
be the number of Laplacian eigenvalues of
T-e=T_{1}\cup T_{2}
, which are greater than or equal to average degree
\overline{d}(T-e)
, therefore
1\leq\sigma\leq n-2
.
It is well-known that
1\leq\sigma\leq n-2
, therefore using definition of Laplacian energy (
1.1
), we have

LE(T)=2\max_{1\leq k\leq n}\left(\sum\limits_{i=1}^{k}\mu_{i}(T)-k\overline{d}(T)\right)\geq 2\Big(\sum\limits_{i=1}^{\sigma}\mu_{i}(T)-\sigma(2-\frac{2}{n})\Big).

(3.4)

Let
\mu_{1}(T-e)\geq\mu_{2}(T-e)\geq\cdots\geq\mu_{n-2}(T-e)\geq\mu_{n-1}(T-e)=\mu_{n}(T-e)=0
be the Laplacian eigenvalues of
T-e
. By Lemma
2.5
, we have
\mu_{i}(T)\geq\mu_{i}(T-e)
giving that
\sum\limits_{i=1}^{\sigma}\mu_{i}(T)\geq\sum\limits_{i=1}^{\sigma}\mu_{i}(T-e)
. With this it follows from (
3.4
) that

\displaystyle LE(T)\geq 2\sum\limits_{i=1}^{\sigma}\mu_{i}(T-e)-4\sigma+\frac{4\sigma}{n}.

(3.5)

Since Laplacian spectrum of
T_{1}\cup T_{2}
is the union of Laplacian spectrum of
T_{1}
and Laplacian spectrum of
T_{2}
. Let
k_{i}\geq 1
be the number of Laplacian eigenvalues of
T_{i}
which are greater than or equal to
\overline{d}(T-e)
, then
k_{1}+k_{2}=\sigma
. Therefore, we have from (
3.5
) that

\displaystyle LE(T)\geq 2\Big(\sum\limits_{i=1}^{k_{1}}\mu_{i}(T_{1})+\sum\limits_{i=1}^{k_{2}}\mu_{i}(T_{2})\Big)-4\sigma+\frac{4\sigma}{n}.

The result now follows.

Let
\sigma_{i}
be the number of Laplacian eigenvalues of
T_{i}
which are greater than or equal to average degree
\overline{d}(T_{i})=2-\frac{2}{n_{i}}
. If
n_{1}\geq n_{2}
, the it is easy to see that
\overline{d}(T_{1})\geq\overline{d}(T-e)
and

for all pendent vertices
u
of
T_{1}
. This gives that
k_{1}\leq r_{1}
. Combining
k_{1}\geq r_{1}
and
k_{1}\leq r_{1}
, we get
k_{1}=r_{1}=\sigma_{1}
. That proves our claim. Let
\sigma_{2}
be the number of Laplacian eigenvalues of
T_{2}
which are greater than or equal to average vertex degree
\overline{d}(T_{2})=2-\frac{2}{n_{2}}
. By given

is a tree of diameter at most
3
, it implies that

or

, a double broom of diameter

. If
T_{2}=K_{1,n_{2}-1}
, then
\sigma_{2}=1
, the number of non-pendent vertices. Since
T_{2}
has at
least two edge and
\sigma_{2}=1
it follows that
k_{2}=\sigma_{2}=1
. On the other hand, if
T_{2}=T(a,b)
, a double broom of diameter
3
, then as shown in

]
, we have
\sigma_{2}=2
, the number of non-pendent vertices. Since
T_{2}
is of order
n_{2}\geq 6
and
\sigma_{2}=2
, the number of non-pendent vertices, we must have
k_{2}=\sigma_{2}=2
. Thus, we have shown that the trees
T_{1}
and
T_{2}
have the property that

and
k_{2}=\sigma_{2}
. Since the inequality (

) always holds for

, by Theorem
4.1
, it follows by using Corollary
3.7
that inequality (

) (and hence Conjecture
1
) holds for

, provided that

Theorem 5.2

4

T(4;2a,2b),T^{{}^{\prime}},T^{{}^{\prime\prime}}

with

. Let
\sigma_{1}
be the number of Laplacian eigenvalues of
T_{1}
greater than or equal to average vertex degree
\overline{d}(T_{1})=2-\frac{2}{n_{1}}
. Let
T
be the tree obtained by joining an edge between a vertex in
T_{1}
and a vertex in
T_{2}
. If
\sigma_{1}=r_{1}
, then inequality (
4.7
) (and hence Conjecture
1
) holds for
T
, provided that
LE(T_{1})\geq 2+\frac{4n_{1}}{\pi}
.

The following result gives that if Conjecture
1
holds for two components obtained by deleting a non-pendent edge of a tree
T

T_{1}

Theorem 5.3

Let
T_{1}
be a tree of order
n_{1}
having Laplacian eigenvalues
\mu_{1}(T_{1})\geq\dots\geq\mu_{n_{1}-1}(T_{1}),\mu_{n_{1}}(T_{1})=0
and let
T_{2}
be a tree of diameter at most
3
or an SNS-tree of diameter
4
other than the trees
T(4;2a,2b),T^{{}^{\prime}},T^{{}^{\prime\prime}}
having order
n_{2}
with
n_{1}\geq n_{2}\geq 6
. Let
\sigma_{1}
be the number of Laplacian eigenvalues of
T_{1}
greater than or equal to average vertex degree
\overline{d}(T_{1})=2-\frac{2}{n_{1}}
.
Let
T
be the tree of order
n=n_{1}+n_{2}
obtained by joining an edge between a vertex in
T_{1}
and a vertex in
T_{2}
. If
\mu_{\sigma_{1}+1}-\overline{d}(T_{1})<\frac{-2}{n}
, then inequality (
4.7
) (and hence Conjecture
1
) holds for
T
, provided that
LE(T_{1})\geq 2+\frac{4n_{1}}{\pi}
.

Proof.
Let
\sigma
be the number of Laplacian eigenvalues of
T_{1}\cup T_{2}
which are greater than or equal to average vertex degree
\overline{d}(T_{1}\cup T_{2})=2-\frac{4}{n}
,
n_{1}+n_{2}=n
. Let
k_{i}
be the number of Laplacian eigenvalues of
T_{i}
which are greater than or equal to average vertex degree
\overline{d}(T_{1}\cup T_{2})=2-\frac{4}{n}
. Since
n_{1}\geq n_{2}
gives that
\overline{d}(T_{1})\geq\overline{d}(T_{1}\cup T_{2})
and
\overline{d}(T_{2})\leq\overline{d}(T_{1}\cup T_{2})
, it follows that
k_{1}\geq\sigma_{1}
. Since,
\mu_{\sigma_{1}+1}-\overline{d}(T_{1})<\frac{-2}{n}
gives that
\mu_{\sigma_{1}+1}-\overline{d}(T_{1}\cup T_{2})=\mu_{\sigma_{1}+1}-2+\frac{4}{n}=\mu_{\sigma_{1}+1}-2+\frac{2}{n_{1}}+\frac{4}{n}-\frac{2}{n_{1}}<\frac{-2}{n}+\frac{4}{n}-\frac{2}{n_{1}}<0
, as
n>n_{1}
. It follows that
k_{1}\leq\sigma_{1}
and so we must have
\sigma_{1}=k_{1}
. Now, proceeding similar to Theorem
5.1
the result follows.

In this note we observe some exotic way to produce the spectrum of auxiliary transfer matrices for
\mathcal{U}_{q}(\widehat{sl}_{2})
models. This way is equivalent to the standard Bethe Ansatz, however it uses different variables. As a by-product, the method is related to generalisations of the Rogers-Ramanujan identity
[
1
]
mentioned in the Abstract. Note, we observe the Rogers-Ramanujan identities of a type different from those obtained by Andrews, Baxter and Forrester
[
2
]
.

Our staring point is the Baxter’s equation,

t(x)Q(x)\;=\;(1-\xi x)^{N}Q(qx)\>+\;\omega q^{S}(\xi-x)^{N}Q(x/q)\;,

(1)

where
t(x)
stands for the auxiliary transfer-matrix,

t(x)\;=\;1+\omega q^{S}\xi^{N}+t_{1}x+\cdots+t_{N-1}x^{N-1}+\;(\omega+q^{S}\xi^{N})(-x)^{N}\;,

(2)

N
is the chain length,
S
is “total spin”,
\omega
is a field, and
Q(x)
is a polynomial of degree
S
. Standard finite-dimensional case corresponds to
\xi=q^{-j}
where
j
is a half-integer local spin. However, we assume here another regime,

\displaystyle|q|\;<\;1\;,\quad|\xi|\;<\;1\;.

(3)

Lowest and highest terms of
t(x)
as well the other terms in (
1
) come from unessential details of properly defined
\mathcal{U}_{q}(\widehat{sl}_{2})

L
-operators.
The Bethe-Ansatz equations

(1-\xi x)^{N}Q(qx)\>+\;\omega q^{S}(\xi-x)^{N}Q(x/q)\;=\;0\quad\textrm{on}\quad Q(x)\;=\;0\;,

(4)

are regarded as a closed system of equations for
S
zeros of
Q(x)
, solution of (
4
) provides eigenvalue of
t(x)
.

However, equation (
1
) allows one to introduce different than
Q(x)
objects. Consider two more
TQ
-type equations,

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\displaystyle t(x)H(x)\;=\;H(x/q)\;+\;\omega q^{S}(1-\xi x)^{N}(\xi-qx)^{N}H(qx)\;,\\
\\
\displaystyle t^{\prime}(x)H^{\prime}(x)\;=\;H^{\prime}(qx)\;+\;\omega^{-1}q^{S}(1-\xi/x)^{N}(\xi-q/x)^{N}H^{\prime}(x/q)\;,\end{array}\right.

(5)

where

t^{\prime}(x)\;=\;t(x)/\omega(-x)^{N}\;,

(6)

and

H(x)\;=\;1+h_{1}x+h_{2}x^{2}+\cdots\;,\quad H^{\prime}(x)\;=\;1+h_{1}^{\prime}/x+h_{2}^{\prime}/x^{2}+\cdots\;.

(7)

For an arbitrary
t(x)
,
H
and
H^{\prime}
are the uniquely defined seria convergent everywhere except
x=\infty
and
x=0
correspondingly.
Moreover, the functions
H
and
H^{\prime}
have the remarkable matrix product representations. Let

L(x)\;=\;\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}\displaystyle t(x)&-\gamma(x)\\
\displaystyle 1&\displaystyle 0\end{array}\right)\;,\quad L^{\prime}(x)\;=\;\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}\displaystyle t^{\prime}(x)&\displaystyle 1\\
\displaystyle-\gamma^{\prime}(x)&\displaystyle 0\end{array}\right)\;,

(8)

where (see (
5
))

\gamma(x)\;=\;\omega q^{S}(1-\xi x)^{N}(\xi-qx)^{N}\;,\quad\gamma^{\prime}(x)\;=\;\omega^{-1}q^{S}(1-\xi/x)^{N}(\xi-q/x)^{N}\;.

(9)

The following semi-infinite matrix products are well defined:

L(x)L(qx)(q^{2}x)\cdots\;=\;\frac{1}{1-\omega q^{S}\xi^{N}}\left(\begin{array}[]{c}\displaystyle H(x/q)\\
H(x)\end{array}\right)\left(1,-\omega q^{S}\xi^{N}\right)\;,

(10)

and

\cdots L^{\prime}(x/q^{2})L^{\prime}(x/q)L^{\prime}(x)\;=\;\frac{1}{1-\omega^{-1}q^{S}\xi^{N}}\left(\begin{array}[]{c}\displaystyle 1\\
\displaystyle-\omega^{-1}q^{S}\xi^{N}\end{array}\right)\left(H^{\prime}(qx),H^{\prime}(x)\right)\;.

(11)

Next, for general
t(x)
, the Wronskian of
H,H^{\prime}
,

\Theta(x)\;=\;H^{\prime}(x)H(x/q)-q^{S}(\xi-x)^{N}(\xi-q/x)^{N}H^{\prime}(x/q)H(x)\;,

(12)

satisfies

\Theta(x)\;=\;\omega(-x)^{N}\Theta(qx)\;,

(13)

so that the
\theta
-function expansion holds:

\Theta(x)\;=\;\Theta_{0}\;\prod_{k=1}^{N}(x/z_{k};q)_{\infty}(qz_{k}/x;q)_{\infty}\;,\quad\prod_{k=1}z_{k}\;=\;\omega^{-1}\;.

(14)

Matrix product representation allows one to write
\Theta(x)
as a matrix product infinite in both directions.
The set of
\{z_{k}\}
is the set of variables alternative to the standard Bethe variables, i.e. to the set of zeros of
Q(x)
. The real aim of this note is to reformulate the Bethe Ansatz equations (
4
) in the terms of
\{z_{k}\}
.

To do this, let us assume (
1
) and compute the Wronskians of
H^{\#}
and
Q
:

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\displaystyle H(x/q)Q(x)-\omega q^{S}(\xi-x)^{N}H(x)Q(x/q)\;=\;(1-\omega q^{S}\xi^{N})(\xi x;q)_{\infty}^{N}\;,\\
\\
\displaystyle H^{\prime}(x)Q^{\prime}(x/q)-\omega^{-1}q^{S}(\xi-q/x)^{N}H^{\prime}(x/q)Q^{\prime}(x)\;=\;\kappa(1-\omega^{-1}q^{S}\xi^{N})(q\xi/x;q)_{\infty}^{N}\;,\end{array}\right.

(15)

where
Q(0)=1
,
Q^{\prime}(x)\;=\;Q(x)/x^{S}
and
\kappa=Q^{\prime}(\infty)
. Consequence of (
15
) is

Q(x)\;=\;(1-\omega q^{S}\xi^{N})\frac{H^{\prime}(x)(\xi x;q)_{\infty}^{N}}{\Theta(x)}+\kappa x^{S}(1-\omega^{-1}q^{S}\xi^{N})\frac{H(x)(\xi/x;q)_{\infty}^{N}}{\Theta(qx)}\;.

(16)

Thus we obtain the quantisation condition equivalent to the initial Bethe-Ansatz equations (
4
). Namely, for given arbitrary
t(x)
, one computes
H^{\#}(x)
and therefore
\{z_{k}\}
. Then, the spectral equations, alternative but equivalent to (
4
), are

H^{\prime}(x)(\xi x;q)_{\infty}^{N}+\kappa^{\prime}x^{S}(-x)^{N}H(x)(\xi/x;q)_{\infty}^{N}\;=\;0\quad\textrm{on}\quad x\in\{z_{k}\}\;,

(17)

where
\kappa^{\prime}
in this approach is an extra parameter, i.e. in fact the number of equations in (
17
) is
N-1
in accordance to the number of independent
\{z_{k}\}
,
\prod z_{k}=\omega^{-1}
.

Note that for the case of the modular double and quantum dilogarithms, when polynomial
Q(x)
is undefined, equations of the type (
17
) play the role of the major spectral equations, see
[
3
,
4
,
5
,
6
]
.

Now suppose that all spectral equations are solved, i.e. (
4
) or (
17
) are satisfied, and equation (
16
) becomes an identity. Equations (
15
) can be solved with respect to
H^{\#}
,

H(x/q)\;=\;(1-\omega q^{S}\xi^{N})Q(x/q)(\xi x;q)_{\infty}^{N}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{\displaystyle(\omega q^{S}\xi^{N})^{n}}{Q(q^{n-1}x)Q(q^{n}x)}\frac{(x/\xi;q)_{n}^{N}}{(\xi x;q)_{n}^{N}}\;,

(18)

and

H^{\prime}(qx)\;=\;\kappa(1-\omega^{-1}q^{S}\xi^{N})Q^{\prime}(qx)(\xi/x;q)_{\infty}^{N}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{\displaystyle(\omega^{-1}q^{S}\xi^{N})^{n}}{Q^{\prime}(q^{n+1}x)Q^{\prime}(q^{n}x)}\frac{(1/\xi x;q)_{n}^{N}}{(\xi/x;q)_{n}^{N}}\;.

(19)

Using this, one can bring the identity (
16
) to the form

\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\frac{\displaystyle(\omega q^{S}\xi^{N})^{n}}{Q(q^{n-1}x)Q(q^{n}x)}\frac{(x/\xi;q)_{n}^{N}}{(\xi x;q)_{n}^{N}}\;=\;\frac{(q/x)^{S}}{\kappa(1-\omega q^{S}\xi^{N})(1-\omega^{-1}q^{S}\xi^{N})}\;\frac{\displaystyle\Theta(x)}{\displaystyle(\xi x;q)_{\infty}^{N}(q\xi/x;q)_{\infty}^{N}}\;.

(20)

In particular, in the case
N=1
and
S=0
,
Q(x)=1
, equation (
20
) becomes the Rogers-Ramanujan identity mentioned in the Appendix (we did not discuss the normalisation constant
\Theta_{0}
).

Relation (
20
) is a particular case of more general one,

\psi(x)\;\stackrel{{\scriptstyle def}}{{=}}\;\sum_{n}z^{n}f(q^{n}x)\prod_{k=1}^{N}\frac{\displaystyle(x/a_{k};q)_{n}}{\displaystyle(x/b_{k};q)_{n}}\;=\;\frac{\displaystyle W(x)}{\displaystyle\prod_{k=1}^{N}(x/b_{k};q)_{\infty}(qa_{k}/x;q)_{\infty}}\;,

(21)

where, since

\psi(x)\;=\;z\prod_{k=1}^{N}\frac{(1-x/a_{k})}{(1-x/b_{k})}\;\psi(qx)\;,

(22)

function
W(x)
satisfies

W(x)\;=\;z\frac{(-x)^{N}}{\prod_{k}a_{k}}\;W(qx)\;.

(23)

This note gives an example of way how to avoid extra poles of
f(x)
in (
21
), what is the Bethe Ansatz equations (
4
), so that
W(x)
becomes a theta-function, and clarifies the structure of this theta-function.

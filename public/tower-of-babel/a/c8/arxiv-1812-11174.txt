Equivalence of OEIS A007729 and A174868
Michael J. Collins

Abstract
We verify the conjecture that the sixth binary partition function [ 2 ] is equal (aside from the initial zero term) to the partial sums of the Stern-Brocot sequence [ 3 ] : 0 , 1 , 2 , 4 , 5 , 8 , 10 , 13 , 14 , 18 , 21 , 26 , 28 , 33 , 36 , 40 , 41 , 46 ​ … 0,1,2,4,5,8,10,13,14,18,21,26,28,33,36,40,41,46\ldots .



Let
b^{\prime}_{k}
be the
sixth binary partition function
, which is the number of ways to write
k
as a sum

k=\sum_{i\geq 0}\varepsilon_{i}2^{i}

with
\varepsilon_{i}\in\{0,1,2,3,4,5\}
.
We obtain

b^{\prime}_{2k}=b^{\prime}_{k}+b^{\prime}_{k-1}+b^{\prime}_{k-2}

(1)

by counting the number of representations of
2k
with
\varepsilon_{0}=0,2
or
4
; in each case we have a representation
\hat{\varepsilon}
of
\frac{2k-\varepsilon_{0}}{2}
by taking
\hat{\varepsilon}_{i}=\varepsilon_{i+1}
, and the correspondence is clearly one-to-one. Also
b^{\prime}_{2k+1}=b^{\prime}_{2k}
, since we can get a representation of
2k+1
only by taking a representation of
2k
and adding 1 to
\varepsilon_{0}
. Thus

\displaystyle b^{\prime}_{2k}

\displaystyle=2b^{\prime}_{k-1}+b^{\prime}_{k}\ \ \ (k\mbox{ even})

(2)

\displaystyle b^{\prime}_{2k}

\displaystyle=2b^{\prime}_{k-1}+b^{\prime}_{k-2}\ \ \ (k\mbox{ odd})

since
b^{\prime}_{k-1}
equals either
b^{\prime}_{k}
or
b^{\prime}_{k-2}
.

We eliminate the even/odd repetition by defining
b_{k}=b^{\prime}_{2k}
. Then
b_{0}=1,b_{1}=2
and

\displaystyle b_{2k}=2b^{\prime}_{2k-1}+b^{\prime}_{2k}

\displaystyle=2b_{k-1}+b_{k}

(3)

\displaystyle b_{2k+1}=2b^{\prime}_{2k}+b^{\prime}_{2k-1}

\displaystyle=2b_{k}+b_{k-1}\ .

This is A007729. If we prepend a zero, defining
\hat{b}_{0}=0
and
\hat{b}_{k}=b_{k-1}
we obtain

\displaystyle\hat{b}_{2k}

\displaystyle=2\hat{b}_{k}+\hat{b}_{k-1}

(4)

\displaystyle\hat{b}_{2k+1}

\displaystyle=2\hat{b}_{k}+\hat{b}_{k+1}\ .

The same recurrence with the same initial conditions gives A174868, the partial sums of the Stern-Brocot sequence
[
1
]
. The Stern-Brocot sequence itself can be defined by
s_{0}=0,s_{1}=1
, and

\displaystyle s_{2k}

\displaystyle=s_{k}

(5)

\displaystyle s_{2k+1}

\displaystyle=s_{k}+s_{k+1}\ .

The partial sums are
\sigma_{k}=\sum_{0\leq i\leq k}s_{k}
. Letting
\ell_{j}=s_{2j-1}+s_{2j}=2s_{j}+s_{j-1}
we get

\sigma_{2k}=\sum_{1\leq j\leq k}\ell_{j}=2\sigma_{k}+\sigma_{k-1}

and similarly with
\ell^{\prime}_{j}=s_{2j}+s_{2j+1}=2s_{j}+s_{j+1}
,

\sigma_{2k+1}=\sum_{0\leq j\leq k}\ell^{\prime}_{j}=2\sigma_{k}+\sigma_{k+1}\ .

The α → 1 α\to 1 Limit of the Sharp Quantum Rényi Divergence
Bjarne Bergh, Robert Salzmann, Nilanjana Datta

Abstract
Fawzi and Fawzi [ 1 ] recently defined the sharp Rényi divergence, D α # D_{\alpha}^{\#} , for α ∈ ( 1 , ∞ ) \alpha\in(1,\infty) , as an additional quantum Rényi divergence with nice mathematical properties and applications in quantum channel discrimination and quantum communication. One of their open questions was the limit α → 1 α\to 1 of this divergence. By finding a new expression of the sharp divergence in terms of a minimization of the geometric Rényi divergence, we show that this limit is equal to the Belavkin-Staszewski relative entropy. Analogous minimizations of arbitrary generalized divergences lead to a new family of generalized divergences that we call kringel divergences , and for which we prove various properties including the data-processing inequality.

We show in Theorem

Let

For a fixed

3
Kringel divergences and their properties

A key ingredient of our main result, Theorem
7
, was Proposition
1
, which allowed us to express
the sharp divergence as a minimization of the geometric Rényi divergence. Analogous minimizations of arbitrary generalized divergences lead to an interesting new family of generalized divergences which we call
kringel divergences
4
4

4

Since the symbols
D
,
\widetilde{D}_{\alpha}
,
\widehat{D}_{\alpha}
,
D^{\flat}_{α}
and
D^{\#}_{\alpha}
all refer to existing divergences, and generalized divergences are usually denoted as
D
, we use the symbol
{\textbf{D}}^{\circ}
to denote this new family; hence the name

kringel divergences
(kringel = circle in German).

. We introduce them in this section and prove some of their properties, including the data-processing inequality.

For a function
{\textbf{D}}:\mathscr{P}(\mathcal{H})\times\mathscr{P}(\mathcal{H})\to\mathbb{R}\cup\{-\infty,\infty\}
we define for
\rho,\sigma\in\mathscr{P}(\mathcal{H})

\displaystyle{\textbf{D}}^{\circ}(\rho\|\sigma)=\inf_{A\geq\rho}{\textbf{D}}(A\|\sigma).

(35)

Moreover, we say
{\textbf{D}}:\mathscr{P}(\mathcal{H})\times\mathscr{P}(\mathcal{H})\to\mathbb{R}\cup\{-\infty,\infty\}
is a
generalized divergence
if it satisfies the data-processing inequality, i.e. for any
\rho,\sigma\in\mathscr{P}(\mathcal{H})
and any quantum channel
\mathcal{N}
, we have

\displaystyle{\textbf{D}}(\mathcal{N}(\rho)\|\mathcal{N}(\sigma))\leq{\textbf{D}}(\rho\|\sigma).

(36)

Remark 8
.

Note that the above definition of the generalized divergence is an extension of the standard definition (see e.g. in
[
12
]
). In the latter, the generalized divergence is considered as a function
{\textbf{D}}:\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{H})\times\mathscr{P}(\mathcal{H})\to\mathbb{R}\cup\{\infty\}
, where
\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{H})
denotes the set of density matrices (quantum states) on
\mathcal{H}
.

The following lemma shows that if
D
is a generalized divergence then so is
{\textbf{D}}^{\circ}
. In this case we call
{\textbf{D}}^{\circ}
the
kringel divergence
of
D
.

Lemma 9

(Data-processing inequality)
.

Let
D
be a generalized divergence,
\rho,\sigma\in\mathscr{P}(\mathcal{H})
and
\mathcal{N}
be a quantum channel. Then

\displaystyle{\textbf{D}}^{\circ}(\mathcal{N}(\rho)\|\mathcal{N}(\sigma))\leq{\textbf{D}}^{\circ}(\rho\|\sigma).

(37)

Proof.

We have

\displaystyle{\textbf{D}}^{\circ}(\mathcal{N}(\rho)\|\mathcal{N}(\sigma))

\displaystyle=\inf_{A\geq\mathcal{N}(\rho)}{\textbf{D}}(A\|\mathcal{N}(\sigma))\leq\inf_{\mathcal{N}(A)\geq\mathcal{N}(\rho)}{\textbf{D}}(\mathcal{N}(A)\|\mathcal{N}(\sigma))

\displaystyle\leq\inf_{\mathcal{N}(A)\geq\mathcal{N}(\rho)}{\textbf{D}}(A\|\sigma)\leq\inf_{A\geq\rho}{\textbf{D}}(A\|\sigma)={\textbf{D}}^{\circ}(A\|\sigma).

(38)

Here, for the first inequality we have used the fact that the minimum increases when we only optimise over operators of the form
\mathcal{N}(A)
and for the second inequality we have used the data-processing inequality for
D
. For the third inequality we have used the fact that
A\geq\rho
implies
\mathcal{N}(A)\geq\mathcal{N}(\rho)
, which follows by positivity and linearity of
\mathcal{N}
.
∎

Remark 10
.

By Proposition
1
we know that for
\alpha\in(1,\infty)
we have

\displaystyle\widehat{D}^{\circ}_{\alpha}=D^{\#}_{\alpha},

(39)

where the left hand side denotes the kringel divergence corresponding to the geometric Rényi divergence (
4
). Note that even though the geometric Rényi divergence satisfies the data-processing inequality only for
\alpha\in(0,1)\cup(1,2]
, by
[
fawzi_defining_2020-1
, Proposition 3.2]
its corresponding kringel divergence actually satisfies the data-processing inequality for all
\alpha\in(0,1)\cup(1,\infty).

Lemma 11
.

If
D
is subadditive then so is
{\textbf{D}}^{\circ}
, i.e. for all
\rho_{1},\rho_{2},\sigma_{1},\sigma_{2}\in\mathscr{P}(\mathcal{H})
we have

\displaystyle{\textbf{D}}^{\circ}(\rho_{1}\otimes\rho_{2}\|\sigma_{1}\otimes\sigma_{2})\leq{\textbf{D}}^{\circ}(\rho_{1}\|\sigma_{1})+{\textbf{D}}^{\circ}(\rho_{2}\|\sigma_{2}).

(40)

Proof.

We have

\displaystyle{\textbf{D}}^{\circ}(\rho_{1}\otimes\rho_{2}\|\sigma_{1}\otimes\sigma_{2})

\displaystyle=\inf_{A\geq\rho_{1}\otimes\rho_{2}}{\textbf{D}}(A\|\sigma_{1}\otimes\sigma_{2})\leq\inf_{A_{1}\otimes A_{2}\geq\rho_{1}\otimes\rho_{2}}{\textbf{D}}(A_{1}\otimes A_{2}\|\sigma_{1}\otimes\sigma_{2})

\displaystyle\leq\inf_{A_{1}\otimes A_{2}\geq\rho_{1}\otimes\rho_{2}}\Big({\textbf{D}}(A_{1}\|\sigma_{1})+{\textbf{D}}(A_{2}\|\sigma_{2})\Big)

\displaystyle=\inf_{A_{1}\geq\rho_{1}}{\textbf{D}}(A_{1}\|\sigma_{1})+\inf_{A_{2}\geq\rho_{2}}{\textbf{D}}(A_{2}\|\sigma_{2})

\displaystyle={\textbf{D}}^{\circ}(\rho_{1}\|\sigma_{1})+{\textbf{D}}^{\circ}(\rho_{2}\|\sigma_{2}).

(41)

The third line above follows from the fact that
A_{1}\geq\rho_{1}
and
A_{2}\geq\rho_{2}
implies that
A_{1}\otimes A_{2}\geq\rho_{1}\otimes\rho_{2}
because
A_{1}\otimes A_{2}-\rho_{1}\otimes\rho_{2}=\left(A_{1}-\rho_{1}\right)\otimes A_{2}+\rho_{1}\otimes\left(A_{2}-\rho_{2}\right)
.
∎

3.1
Kringel divergences for
\alpha
-Rényi divergences

We say
{\textbf{D}}_{\alpha}:\mathscr{P}(\mathcal{H})\times\mathscr{P}(\mathcal{H})\to\mathbb{R}\cup\{-\infty,\infty\}
is a
quantum generalization of the
\alpha
-Rényi divergence
if it reduces to the corresponding classical Rényi divergence if both entries commute. That is, if
\rho,\sigma\in\mathscr{P}(\mathcal{H})
are commuting operators then

\displaystyle{\textbf{D}}_{\alpha}(\rho\|\sigma)=\frac{1}{\alpha-1}\log\Big(\sum_i p_i^{\alpha} q_i^{1-\alpha}\Big),

(42)

where the
\{p_{i}\}_{i}
and
\{q_{i}\}_{i}
are eigenvalues of
\rho
and
\sigma
respectively with respect to a simultaneous eigenbasis.
The following lemma states that, for
\alpha>1
, if
{\textbf{D}}_{\alpha}
is a quantum generalization of the Rényi relative entropy, then
{\textbf{D}}^{\circ}_{\alpha}
too reduces to the classical Rényi divergence in the commuting case, and hence is itself a quantum generalization of the Rényi relative entropy.

Lemma 12
.

Let
\alpha>1
and
{\textbf{D}}_{\alpha}
be a quantum generalization of the
\alpha
-Rényi divergence satisfying the data-processing inequality (
36
). Then also
{\textbf{D}}^{\circ}_{\alpha}
is a quantum generalization of the
\alpha
-Rényi divergence.

Proof.

Let
\rho,\sigma\in\mathscr{P}(\mathcal{H})
such that
[\rho,\sigma]=0
. Then there exists a simultaneous eigenbasis
\{\ket{i}\}_{i}
of
\rho
and
\sigma
such that

\displaystyle\rho=\sum_{i}p_{i}|i\rangle\!\langle i|,\quad\quad\sigma=\sum_{i}q_{i}|i\rangle\!\langle i|.

(43)

Clearly,

\displaystyle{\textbf{D}}_{\alpha}^{\circ}(\rho\|\sigma)\leq{\textbf{D}}_{\alpha}(\rho\|\sigma)=\frac{1}{\alpha-1}\log\Big(\sum_i p_i^{\alpha} q_i^{1-\alpha}\Big).

(44)

For the reversed inequality let
\mathcal{P}
be the pinching map defined as
\mathcal{P}(\cdot):=\sum_{i}|i\rangle\!\langle i|\cdot|i\rangle\!\langle i|
. Hence, denoting for
A\geq\rho
the diagonal entries by
a_{i}=\bra{i}A\ket{i}\geq p_{i}
, we see

\displaystyle{\textbf{D}}_{\alpha}^{\circ}(\rho\|\sigma)

\displaystyle=\inf_{A\geq\rho}{\textbf{D}}_{\alpha}(A\|\sigma)\geq\inf_{A\geq\rho}{\textbf{D}}_{\alpha}(\mathcal{P}(A)\|\mathcal{P}(\sigma))

\displaystyle=\inf_{A\geq\rho}\frac{1}{\alpha-1}\log\Big(\sum_{i} a_i^{\alpha}q_i^{1-\alpha} \Big)=\frac{1}{\alpha-1}\log\Big(\sum_{i} p_i^{\alpha}q_i^{1-\alpha}\Big),

(45)

where we have used data-processing inequality for
{\textbf{D}}_{\alpha}
in the first inequality.
∎

Remark 13
.

Note that for
\alpha\in(0,1)
and
{\textbf{D}}_{\alpha}
being a quantum generalization of the
\alpha
-Rényi divergence, one easily sees that

\displaystyle{\textbf{D}}_{\alpha}^{\circ}(\rho\|\sigma)=-\infty,

(46)

for all
\rho,\sigma\in\mathscr{P}(\mathcal{H})
with
\sigma\neq 0
,
which is why we excluded this range of

Important quantum generalizations of the

\displaystyle D_{\alpha}(\rho||\sigma)

\displaystyle:=\frac{1}{\alpha-1}\log\Tr\left(\rho^{\alpha}\sigma^{1-\alpha}\right),

\displaystyle\widetilde{D}_{\alpha}(\rho||\sigma)

\displaystyle:=\frac{1}{\alpha-1}\log\Tr\left(\sigma^{\frac{1-\alpha}{2\alpha}}\rho\sigma^{\frac{1-\alpha}{2\alpha}}\right)^{\alpha}

), as was shown

[

tomamichel_fullyquantum_2009
]
and
[

]
, respectively. Therefore, for both of these divergences the following proposition applies in the corresponding ranges of
\alpha
.

Proposition 14
.

Let
\alpha>1
and
{\textbf{D}}_{\alpha}
be a quantum generalization of the
\alpha
-Rényi divergence which is subadditive and satisfies the data-processing inequality (

). Then

\displaystyle\widetilde{D}_{\alpha}\leq{\textbf{D}}^{\circ}_{\alpha}\leq D^{\#}_{\alpha}.

(48)

In particular, this gives for any

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}{\textbf{D}}^{\circ}_{\alpha}(\rho^{\otimes n}\|\sigma^{\otimes n})=\widetilde{D}_{\alpha}(\rho\|\sigma).

(49)

Moreover, in the case of the sandwiched divergence

\displaystyle\widetilde{D}^{\circ}_{\alpha}=\widetilde{D}_{\alpha}.

(50)

Proof.

As

satisfies the data-processing inequality, we know that
{\textbf{D}}_{\alpha}\leq\widehat{D}_{\alpha}
; this follows from the argument

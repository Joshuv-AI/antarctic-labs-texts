POD-ROMs for incompressible flows including snapshots of the temporal derivative of the full order solution: Error bounds for the pressure
Bosco García-Archilla, Volker John, Sarah Katz, Julia Novo

Abstract
Reduced order methods (ROMs) for the incompressible Navier–Stokes equations, based on proper orthogonal decomposition (POD), are studied that include snapshots which approach the temporal derivative of the velocity from a full order mixed finite element method (FOM). In addition, the set of snapshots contains the mean velocity of the FOM. Both the FOM and the POD-ROM are equipped with a grad-div stabilization. A velocity error analysis for this method can be found already in the literature. The present paper studies two different procedures to compute approximations to the pressure and proves error bounds for the pressure that are independent of inverse powers of the viscosity. Numerical studies support the analytic results and compare both methods.



AMS subject classifications.
65M12, 65M15, 65M60.

Keywords.
incompressible Navier–Stokes equations, proper orthogonal decomposition (POD),
reduced order models (ROMs), snapshots of the temporal derivative, grad-div stabilization,
robust pointwise in time estimates, pressure bounds

1
Introduction

It is sometimes necessary to compute numerical approximations of solutions of partial differential
equations very fast without requiring the highest accuracy. An example is the solution of optimal control
problems, which needs in each iteration the solution of a time-dependent partial differential
equation with only slightly changed data. A popular approach for performing highly efficient simulations
consists in using so-called reduced order models (ROMs). These models utilize a Galerkin approach and the
corresponding basis functions are extracted from one accurate numerical solution, which is called in this context
the full order model (FOM).

This paper studies incompressible flow problems that are modeled by the
incompressible Navier–Stokes equations

\begin{array}[]{rcll}\partial_{t}\boldsymbol{u}-\nu\Delta\boldsymbol{u}+(\boldsymbol{u}\cdot\nabla)\boldsymbol{u}+\nabla p&=&\boldsymbol{f}&\text{in }\ (0,T]\times\Omega,\\
\nabla\cdot\boldsymbol{u}&=&0&\text{in }\ (0,T]\times\Omega,\end{array}

(1)

in a bounded domain
\Omega\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
,
d\in\{2,3\}
.
The boundary of
\Omega
is assumed to be polyhedral and Lipschitz.
In (
1
),

\boldsymbol{u}
denotes the velocity field,
p
the kinematic pressure,
\nu>0
the kinematic viscosity coefficient,
and
\boldsymbol{f}
represents the accelerations due to external body forces acting
on the fluid. The Navier–Stokes equations (
1
) have to be complemented
with an initial condition
\boldsymbol{u}(0,\boldsymbol{x})=\boldsymbol{u}^{0}(\boldsymbol{x})
and with
boundary conditions. For simplicity,
we only consider the case of homogeneous
Dirichlet boundary conditions
\boldsymbol{u}=\boldsymbol{0}
on
[0,T]\times\partial\Omega
.

The most popular approach for computing basis functions for ROMs is probably the use of the proper
orthogonal decomposition (POD), which will be also considered here. The complete method is often called
POD-ROM. In the past few years there has been a tremendous progress in the numerical analysis of
POD-ROM methods, e.g., see
[
22
,
16
,
20
,
18
,
21
,
7
,
15
,
12
,
13
,
11
]
.
In
[
11
]
, we introduced a POD-ROM model based on a set of snapshots including
the mean value of the velocity at different time instants together with approximations to the velocity
time derivative at different times. Including grad-div stabilization, both in the snapshots computation
and the POD-ROM method, we were able to prove error bounds for the velocity that are convection-robust, i.e.,
the constants in the error bounds are independent of inverse powers of the viscosity.

There are applications that require the pressure solution for computing quantities of interest, e.g.,
lift or drag coefficients at bodies in the flow field. In other applications, the pressure is even more
important than the velocity, e.g., the pressure gradient is the most important biomarker for detecting
stenoses in blood vessels. Using in the FOM a pair of finite element spaces that satisfies a discrete
inf-sup condition, then the basis functions from the POD are discretely divergence-free. Consequently,
the POD-ROM, as a Galerkin method, does not contain the pressure. This situation was considered in
[
11
]
.

The goal of this paper consists in analyzing two algorithms for computing a reduced order pressure. Both
algorithms use a set of velocity snapshots that are based on approximations of the temporal derivative
of the FOM velocity and on pressure snapshots that are solutions of the FOM. The first algorithm is
the supremizer enrichment algorithm from
[
21
]
. In the present paper, the analysis of
[
21
]
is extended
from a ROM that uses standard FOM velocity snapshots
to a ROM that utilizes the above mentioned approximations of the temporal derivative of the velocity. The
second algorithm is the so-called stabilization-motivated POD-ROM proposed in
[
6
]
. This method
is already analyzed in
[
7
]
and the present paper aims to improve this analysis in several
aspects. First, we avoid the strong restriction
h\leq C\Delta t
, where
\Delta t
and
h
are
the time step and spatial mesh diameter, respectively. Second, differently to
[
7
]
,
we use the same procedure to compute a POD-ROM pressure in two and three spatial dimensions. Note that in

[
7
]
a so-called truncation of the velocity approximation is applied in the nonlinear
convective term to compute the reduced order pressure. Third, in contrast to
[
7
]
,
where only a first order convergence for the considered norm could be proved, our analysis shows for the same
norm an order that is induced from the pair of mixed finite element spaces that were used for performing the FOM simulation.
And finally, again in contrast to
[
7
]
, the constant in the derived error bound does not
blow up if the viscosity coefficient tends to zero.

Numerical studies will support the analytic results. These studies will also provide an initial comparison
of the supremizer enrichment and the stabilization-motivated pressure ROMs.

The paper is organized as follows. Section
2
introduces some notations, the considered finite
element spaces, recalls some inequalities used in the numerical analysis, and presents the FOM. In
Section
3
the error estimates for the POD-ROM velocity from
[
11
]
are recalled and a few
new estimates for velocity terms are proved. The numerical analysis for the pressure ROMs is presented
in Section
4
and Section
5
contains the numerical studies.
Finally, a summary of the results and an outlook is given in Section
6
.

2
Finite Element Spaces and the FOM

Standard symbols will be used for Lebesgue and Sobolev spaces, with the usual convention that

W^{s,2}(\Omega)=H^{s}(\Omega)
,
s\geq 1
. The inner product in
L^{2}(\Omega)^{d}
,
d\geq 1
, is denoted
by
(\cdot,\cdot)
.
In the sequel we denote

L_{0}^{2}(\Omega)=\{q\in L^{2}(\Omega)\ \mid\ (q,1)=0\}
.
Let us recall
the Poincaré inequality,

\|\boldsymbol{v}\|_{0}\leq C_{p}\|\nabla\boldsymbol{v}\|_{0}\quad\forall\ \boldsymbol{v}\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)^{d},

(2)

and the estimate of the divergence of a velocity field by its gradient,
see
[
14
, Remark 3.35]
,

\|\nabla\cdot\boldsymbol{v}\|_{0}\leq\|\nabla\boldsymbol{v}\|_{0}\quad\forall\ \boldsymbol{v}\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)^{d}.

(3)

The following Sobolev embeddings
[
1
]
will be used in the analysis: For

q\in[1,\infty)
, there exists a constant
C=C(\Omega,q)
such
that

\|v\|_{L^{q^{\prime}}}\leq C\|v\|_{W^{s,q}},\,\,\quad\frac{1}{q^{\prime}}\geq\frac{1}{q}-\frac{s}{d}>0,\quad q<\infty,\quad v\in W^{s,q}(\Omega)^{d}.

(4)

Generic constants independent of inverse powers of the viscosity and the mesh width are denoted by

C
,
C_{0}
and similar symbols.

Let us denote by
\{\mathcal{T}_{h}\}=\{(K_{j},\phi_{j}^{h})_{j\in J_{h}}\}
,
h>0
, a family of partitions of

\overline{\Omega}
, where
h
is the maximum diameter of the mesh cells
K_{j}\in\mathcal{T}_{h}

and
\phi_{j}^{h}
are the mappings from the reference simplex
K_{0}
onto
K_{j}
.
We assume that the family of partitions is shape-regular and quasi-uniform.
On
\mathcal{T}_{h}
, the following finite element spaces are defined

\displaystyle Y_{h}^{l}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left\{v_{h}\in C^{0}(\overline{\Omega})\ \mid\ {v_{h}}_{\mid_{K}}\in{\mathbb{P}}_{l}(K),\ \forall\ K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}\right\},\ l\geq 1,\quad{\boldsymbol{Y}}_{h}^{l}=\left(Y_{h}^{l}\right)^{d},

\displaystyle{\boldsymbol{X}}_{h}^{l}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\boldsymbol{Y}}_{h}^{l}\cap H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)^{d},

\displaystyle Q_{h}^{l}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle Y_{h}^{l}\cap L_{0}^{2}(\Omega),

\displaystyle{\boldsymbol{V}}_{h}^{l}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\boldsymbol{X}}_{h}^{l}\cap\left\{{\boldsymbol{v}}_{h}\in H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)^{d}\ \mid\ (\nabla\cdot{\boldsymbol{v}}_{h},q_{h})=0\ \forall\ q_{h}\in Q_{h}^{l-1}\right\},\quad l\geq 2.

Hence,
{\boldsymbol{V}}_{h}^{l}
is the space of discretely divergence-free functions.

The Lagrangian interpolant in
{\boldsymbol{X}}_{h}^{l}
is denoted by
I_{h}(\cdot)
. It is well known, e.g.,
see
[
4
]
, that there exists a constant
C>0
such that
for every
K\in{\cal T}_{h}

\left\|\boldsymbol{u}-I_{h}(\boldsymbol{u})\right\|_{0,K}+h\left\|\nabla(\boldsymbol{u}-I_{h}(\boldsymbol{u}))\right\|_{0,K}+h^{2}\|\Delta(\boldsymbol{u}-I_{h}(\boldsymbol{u}))\|_{0,K}\leq C\|\boldsymbol{u}\|_{l+1,K}h^{l+1}.

(5)

The inverse inequality

|v_{h}|_{W^{m,p}(K)}\leq c_{\mathrm{inv}}h_{K}^{n-m-d\left(\frac{1}{q}-\frac{1}{p}\right)}\quad|v_{h}|_{W^{n,q}(K)}\quad\forall\ v_{h}\in Y_{h}^{l},

(6)

holds,
with
0\leq n\leq m\leq 1
,
1\leq q\leq p\leq\infty
, and
h_{K}

being the diameter of
K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}
, because the family of partitions is quasi-uniform, e.g., see
[
8
, Theorem 3.2.6]
. Define
h=\max_{K\in{\cal T}_{h}}h_{K}
.

In this paper, we consider Taylor–Hood pairs of finite element spaces
[
5
,
24
]
, i.e.,
pairs of the form
({\boldsymbol{X}}_{h}^{l},Q_{h}^{l-1})
,
l\geq 2
. These pairs satisfy
a discrete inf-sup condition, see
[
2
,
3
]
, that is, there is a constant

\beta_{\rm is}>0
independent of
h
such that

\inf_{q_{h}\in Q_{h}^{l-1}}\sup_{\boldsymbol{v}_{h}\in{\boldsymbol{X}}_{h}^{l}}\frac{(\nabla\cdot\boldsymbol{v}_{h},q_{h})}{\|\nabla\boldsymbol{v}_{h}\|_{0}\|q_{h}\|_{0}}\geq\beta_{\rm{is}}.

(7)

Taylor–Hood pairs, in particular for
l=2
, are probably the most popular pairs of
inf-sup stable finite element spaces.

Let
{\boldsymbol{V}}
be the space of functions in
H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)^{d}
with
\nabla\cdot\boldsymbol{u}=0
.
The following modified Stokes projection
\boldsymbol{s}_{h}^{m}\ :\ {\boldsymbol{V}}\rightarrow{\boldsymbol{V}}_{h}^{l}
was introduced in
[
9
]
and is defined by

(\nabla\boldsymbol{s}_{h}^{m},\nabla\boldsymbol{\varphi}_{h})=(\nabla\boldsymbol{u},\nabla\boldsymbol{\varphi}_{h}),\quad\forall\ \boldsymbol{\varphi}_{h}\in{\boldsymbol{V}_{h}^{l}}.

(8)

This projection satisfies the following error bound, see
[
9
]
,

\|\boldsymbol{u}-\boldsymbol{s}_{h}^{m}\|_{0}+h\|\boldsymbol{u}-\boldsymbol{s}_{h}^{m}\|_{1}\leq C\|\boldsymbol{u}\|_{j}h^{j},\quad 1\leq j\leq l+1.

(9)

As FOM, we use a Galerkin method with grad-div stabilization. The continuous-in-time method reads as follows:
Find
(\boldsymbol{u}_{h},p_{h})\in{\boldsymbol{X}}_{h}^{l}\times Q_{h}^{l-1}
such that

\begin{array}[]{rcll}\left(\partial_{t}\boldsymbol{u},\boldsymbol{v}_{h}\right)+\nu(\nabla\boldsymbol{u}_{h},\nabla\boldsymbol{v}_{h})+b(\boldsymbol{u}_{h},\boldsymbol{u}_{h},\boldsymbol{v}_{h})\\
-(\nabla\cdot\boldsymbol{v}_{h},p_{h})+\mu(\nabla\cdot\boldsymbol{u}_{h},\nabla\cdot\boldsymbol{v}_{h})&=&({\boldsymbol{f}},\boldsymbol{v}_{h})&\forall\ \boldsymbol{v}_{h}\in{\boldsymbol{X}}_{h}^{l},\\
(\nabla\cdot\boldsymbol{u}_{h},q_{h})&=&0&\forall\ q_{h}\in Q_{h}^{l-1},\end{array}

where
\mu
is the positive grad-div stabilization parameter and

b(\boldsymbol{u},\boldsymbol{v},\boldsymbol{w})=((\boldsymbol{u}\cdot\nabla)\boldsymbol{v},\boldsymbol{w})+\frac{1}{2}((\nabla\cdot\boldsymbol{u})\boldsymbol{v},\boldsymbol{w}).

The pressure drops out if the problem is considered for functions
from the discretely divergence-free space
\boldsymbol{V}_{h}^{l}
, since

\boldsymbol{u}_{h}\in\boldsymbol{V}_{h}^{l}
satisfies

\displaystyle\left(\partial_{t}\boldsymbol{u}_{h},\boldsymbol{v}_{h}\right)+\nu(\nabla\boldsymbol{u}_{h},\nabla\boldsymbol{v}_{h})+b(\boldsymbol{u}_{h},\boldsymbol{u}_{h},\boldsymbol{v}_{h})

\displaystyle+\mu(\nabla\cdot\boldsymbol{u}_{h},\nabla\cdot\boldsymbol{v}_{h})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle({\boldsymbol{f}},\boldsymbol{v}_{h})\quad\forall\ \boldsymbol{v}_{h}\in{\boldsymbol{V}}_{h}^{l}.

(10)

For this method the following bound holds, see
[
9
]
for the first term and
the explanation in
[
11
]
for the second term,

\|\boldsymbol{u}(t,\cdot)-\boldsymbol{u}_{h}(t,\cdot)\|_{0}+h\|\boldsymbol{u}(t,\cdot)-\boldsymbol{u}_{h}(t,\cdot)\|_{1}\leq C(\boldsymbol{u},p,l+1)h^{l},\quad t\in(0,T],

(11)

where the constant
C(\boldsymbol{u},p,l+1)
does not explicitly depend on inverse powers of
\nu
.
To bound the error in the pressure, arguing as in
[
9
]
, and using results in
[
10
]
, one can prove

\left(\sum_{j=1}^{n}\Delta t\left\|p^{j}-p_{h}^{j}\right\|_{0}^{2}\right)^{1/2}\leq C_{\rm press}(\boldsymbol{u},p,l+1)h^{l-1/2}.

(12)

Velocity POD-ROMs

This section describes briefly the velocity POD-ROMs that were investigated in
[
11
]
and summarizes
the error estimates derived in this paper.

3.1
The General Method

Let
M\in\mathbb{N}
be a positive integer and set
\Delta t=T/M
, i.e., the time instants are given
by
t_{j}=j\Delta t
,
j=0,\ldots,M
. For simplicity of presentation, let
\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{j}=\boldsymbol{u}_{h}(t_{j},\cdot)
,

p_{h}^{j}=p_{h}(t_{j},\cdot)

denote the FOM approximation of the velocity at time instant
t_{j}
and
\partial_{t}\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{j}=\partial_{t}\boldsymbol{u}_{h}(t_{j},\cdot)

the approximation of the temporal derivative (e.g., see
[
11
, Remark 2.1]
on the computation of time derivatives). The mean value of the velocity snapshots is defined by

\overline{\boldsymbol{u}}_{h}=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{j=0}^{M}\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{j}
with
N=M+1
. Then, the following space, which is
based on data from the FOM simulation, is defined

{\mathcal{\boldsymbol{U}}}=\mbox{span}\left\{\sqrt{N}\overline{\boldsymbol{u}}_{h},\tau\partial_{t}\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{1},\ldots,\tau\partial_{t}\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{M}\right\}=\mbox{span}\left\{\boldsymbol{y}_{h}^{1},\boldsymbol{y}_{h}^{2},\ldots,\boldsymbol{y}_{h}^{N}\right\}.

(13)

The factor
\tau
in front of the temporal derivatives is a time scale. Its introduction aims
to make the vectors
\boldsymbol{y}_{h}^{j}
,
j=1,\ldots,N
, dimensionally correct, i.e.,
all vectors possess the physical unit
\nicefrac{\mathrm{m}}{\mathrm{s}}
. The dimension of
\mathcal{\boldsymbol{U}}
is denoted by
d_{v}
.

Notice that by taking derivatives with respect to time in the second equation in (
2
)
it follows that
\partial_{t}\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{j}
is discretely divergence-free, i.e.,
\partial_{t}\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{j}\in\boldsymbol{V}_{h,l}
for all
j=1,\ldots,M
, so that
\mathcal{\boldsymbol{U}}\subset\boldsymbol{V}_{h,l}
.

Let the space
\mathcal{\boldsymbol{U}}
be equipped with an inner product, denoted by
(\cdot,\cdot)_{X}
. For the
Navier–Stokes equations, this might be the product from
L^{2}(\Omega)^{d}
or from

H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)^{d\times d}
. Both cases will be considered in the present paper. Then, the
first step of the POD approach consists in defining the correlation matrix

K^{\mathrm{v}}=((k_{i,j}^{\mathrm{v}}))\in{\mathbb{R}}^{N\times N}

with the entries

k_{i,j}^{\mathrm{v}}=\frac{1}{N}\left(\boldsymbol{y}_{h}^{i},\boldsymbol{y}_{h}^{j}\right)_{X},\quad i,j=1,\ldots,N.

Denote by
\lambda_{1}\geq\lambda_{2},\ldots\geq\lambda_{d_{v}}>0
the positive eigenvalues
of
K^{\mathrm{v}}
and by

\boldsymbol{v}_{1},\ldots,\boldsymbol{v}_{d_{v}}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{N}
associated eigenvectors with Euclidean norm
1
.
Then, the POD basis functions of
\mathcal{\boldsymbol{U}}
, which are orthonormal, are defined by

\boldsymbol{\varphi}_{k}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{N}}\frac{1}{\sqrt{\lambda_{k}}}\sum_{j=1}^{N}v_{k}^{j}\boldsymbol{y}_{h}^{j},

with
v_{k}^{j}
being the
j
-th component of
\boldsymbol{v}_{k}
.
The following representation of the error was derived in
[
19
, Proposition 1]

\frac{1}{N}\sum_{j=1}^{N}\left\|\boldsymbol{y}_{h}^{j}-\sum_{k=1}^{r}\left(\boldsymbol{y}_{h}^{j},\boldsymbol{\varphi}_{k}\right)_{X}\boldsymbol{\varphi}_{k}\right\|_{X}^{2}=\sum_{k=r+1}^{d_{v}}\lambda_{k}.

Inserting the functions from (
13
) yields

\displaystyle\left\|\overline{\boldsymbol{u}}_{h}-\sum_{k=1}^{r}\left(\overline{\boldsymbol{u}}_{h},\boldsymbol{\varphi}_{k}\right)_{X}\boldsymbol{\varphi}_{k}\right\|_{X}^{2}

\displaystyle+\frac{\tau^{2}}{M+1}\sum_{j=1}^{M}\left\|\partial_{t}\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{j}-\sum_{k=1}^{r}\left(\partial_{t}\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{j},\boldsymbol{\varphi}_{k}\right)_{X}\boldsymbol{\varphi}_{k}\right\|_{X}^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{k=r+1}^{d_{v}}\lambda_{k}.

(14)

The stiffness matrix of the POD basis is defined by
S^{\mathrm{v}}=((s_{i,j}^{\mathrm{v}}))\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d_{v}\times d_{v}}
, where

s_{i,j}^{\mathrm{v}}=(\nabla\boldsymbol{\varphi}_{j},\nabla\boldsymbol{\varphi}_{i})_{X}
. In the case
X=L^{2}(\Omega)^{d}
, the following estimate
was shown in
[
19
, Lemma 2]
:

\|\nabla\boldsymbol{v}\|_{0}\leq\sqrt{\|S^{\mathrm{v}}\|_{2}}\|\boldsymbol{v}\|_{0}\quad\forall\ \boldsymbol{v}\in{\mathcal{\boldsymbol{U}}}.

(15)

We will denote the space spanned by the first
r
POD basis functions by

{\mathcal{\boldsymbol{U}}}^{r}=\mbox{span}\left\{\boldsymbol{\varphi}_{1},\boldsymbol{\varphi}_{2},\ldots,\boldsymbol{\varphi}_{r}\right\},\quad 1\leq r\leq d_{v},

and by
P_{r}^{\mathrm{v}}\ :\ {\boldsymbol{X}}_{h}^{l}\to{\mathcal{\boldsymbol{U}}}^{r}
, the
X
-orthogonal projection onto
{\mathcal{\boldsymbol{U}}}^{r}
.

In
[
11
, (3.11)]
, we proved for any Banach space
Y
defined on
\Omega
the estimate

\max_{0\leq k\leq N}\|\boldsymbol{z}^{k}\|_{Y}^{2}\leq{3}\|\overline{\boldsymbol{z}}\|_{Y}^{2}+\frac{12T^{2}}{M}\sum_{n=1}^{M}\|\partial_{t}\boldsymbol{z}^{n}\|_{Y}^{2}+\frac{16T}{3}(\Delta t)^{2}\int_{0}^{T}\|\partial_{tt}\boldsymbol{z}\|_{Y}^{2}\ ds,

(16)

provided that
\boldsymbol{z}\in H^{2}(0,T;Y)
.
Applying (
16
) to
\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{n}-P_{r}^{\mathrm{v}}\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{n}
yields

\displaystyle\max_{0\leq n\leq M}\|\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{n}-P_{r}^{\mathrm{v}}\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{n}\|_{Y}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 3\|\overline{\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{n}-P_{r}^{\mathrm{v}}\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{n}}\|_{Y}^{2}+\frac{12T^{2}}{M}\sum_{n=1}^{M}\|\partial_{t}\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{n}-P_{r}^{\mathrm{v}}\partial_{t}\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{n}\|_{Y}^{2}

(17)

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{16T}{3}(\Delta t)^{2}\int_{0}^{T}\|\partial_{tt}\boldsymbol{u}_{h}-P_{r}^{\mathrm{v}}\partial_{tt}\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{n}\|_{Y}^{2}\ ds.

Let
Y=X
, the space that is connected to the projection
P_{r}^{\mathrm{v}}
. Using the
X
-stability
of the projection gives

\|\partial_{tt}\boldsymbol{u}_{h}-P_{r}^{\mathrm{v}}\partial_{tt}\boldsymbol{u}_{h}\|_{X}^{2}\leq 2\|P_{r}^{\mathrm{v}}\partial_{tt}\boldsymbol{u}_{h}\|_{X}^{2}+2\|\partial_{tt}\boldsymbol{u}_{h}\|_{X}^{2}\leq 4\|\partial_{tt}\boldsymbol{u}_{h}\|_{X}^{2},

so that we obtain with (
3.1
)

\max_{0\leq n\leq M}\|\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{n}-P_{r}^{\mathrm{v}}\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{n}\|_{X}^{2}\leq C_{X}^{2}=\rho^{2}\sum_{k={r+1}}^{d_{v}}\lambda_{k}+\frac{64}{3}T(\Delta t)^{2}\int_{0}^{T}\|\partial_{tt}\boldsymbol{u}_{h}\|_{X}^{2}\ ds,

(18)

where

\rho=\max\left\{\sqrt{3},\frac{\sqrt{24}T}{\tau}\right\}.

(19)

All explicit constants in the bounds (
16
)–(
19
) will immediately be absorbed in generic
constants in the following analysis.

Summing over all time steps leads to

\frac{1}{M}\sum_{n=1}^{M}\left\|\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{n}-P_{r}^{\mathrm{v}}\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{n}\right\|_{X}^{2}\leq C_{X}^{2}.

(20)

3.2
Velocity Error Estimate for
X=H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)^{d\times d}

As usual in the analysis of discretizations of the Navier–Stokes equations, errors for the pressure
are bounded by velocity errors, which have been estimated before. The velocity error bounds were derived
in
[
11
]
and they are provided here for completeness of presentation.

For the sake of concentrating the numerical analysis to the essential points, the grad-div POD-ROM model
studied in
[
11
]
was equipped with the implicit Euler method as temporal discretization:
For
n\geq 1
, find
\boldsymbol{u}_{r}^{n}\in{\mathcal{\boldsymbol{U}}}^{r}
such that

\displaystyle\hskip-90.00014pt\left(\frac{\boldsymbol{u}_{r}^{n}-\boldsymbol{u}_{r}^{n-1}}{\Delta t},\boldsymbol{\varphi}\right)+\nu(\nabla\boldsymbol{u}_{r}^{n},\nabla\boldsymbol{\varphi})+b\left(\boldsymbol{u}_{r}^{n},\boldsymbol{u}_{r}^{n},\boldsymbol{\varphi}\right)+\mu(\nabla\cdot\boldsymbol{u}_{r}^{n},\nabla\cdot\boldsymbol{\varphi})

(21)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(\boldsymbol{f}^{n},\boldsymbol{\varphi})\quad\forall\ \boldsymbol{\varphi}\in{\mathcal{\boldsymbol{U}}}^{r}.

Since
\partial_{t}\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{j}\in\boldsymbol{V}_{h,l}
, it follows that
\boldsymbol{u}_{r}^{n}
belongs to the
space of discretely divergence-free functions. Hence, there is no pressure term in
(
21
) and this equation does not need to be augmented with a requirement on the
divergence of the solution.

The velocity error estimates in
[
11
]
that we present below are convection-robust, i.e., all constants do not
depend on inverse powers of the viscosity.
Denoting by
\boldsymbol{e}_{r}^{j}=\boldsymbol{u}_{r}^{j}-P_{r}^{\mathrm{v}}\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{j}
the following estimate holds for
j=1,\ldots,M
, see
[
11
, (4.21)]
,

\displaystyle\|\boldsymbol{e}_{r}^{j}\|_{0}^{2}+2\nu\sum_{j=1}^{M}\Delta t\|\nabla\boldsymbol{e}_{r}^{j}\|_{0}^{2}+\mu\sum_{j=1}^{M}\Delta t\|\nabla\cdot\boldsymbol{e}_{r}^{j}\|_{0}^{2}

(22)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C_{0}\|\boldsymbol{e}_{r}^{0}\|_{0}^{2}+C_{1}\sum_{k=r+1}^{d_{v}}\lambda_{k}+C_{2}(\Delta t)^{2}\int_{0}^{T}\|\nabla\partial_{tt}\boldsymbol{u}_{h}\|_{0}^{2}\ ds.

The following estimate
is proved in
[
11
, Theorem 4.1]
.

\displaystyle\frac{1}{T}\sum_{j=1}^{M}\Delta t\|\boldsymbol{u}_{r}^{j}-\boldsymbol{u}^{j}\|_{0}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C_{0}\|\boldsymbol{u}_{r}^{0}-\boldsymbol{u}_{h}(0)\|_{0}^{2}+C_{1}\sum_{k=r+1}^{d_{v}}\lambda_{k}+C^{2}(\boldsymbol{u},p,l+1)h^{2l}

(23)

\displaystyle{}+C_{2}(\Delta t)^{2}\int_{0}^{T}\|\nabla(\partial_{tt}\boldsymbol{u}_{h})\|_{0}^{2}\ ds.

The term with the second order time derivative can be bounded, see
[
11
, Appendix]
. However,
a robust bound in the case
X=H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)^{d\times d}
was obtained only for
l\geq 3
. In addition,
in
[
11
, Theorem 4.3]
, it is shown that the right-hand side of (
23
) is also an upper
bound of pointwise-in-time estimates, that is

\displaystyle\max_{0\leq n\leq M}\|\boldsymbol{u}_{r}^{n}-\boldsymbol{u}^{n}\|_{0}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C_{0}\|\boldsymbol{u}_{r}^{0}-\boldsymbol{u}_{h}(0)\|_{0}^{2}+C_{1}\sum_{k=r+1}^{d_{v}}\lambda_{k}+C^{2}(\boldsymbol{u},p,l+1)h^{2l}

\displaystyle+C_{2}(\Delta t)^{2}\int_{0}^{T}\|\nabla(\partial_{tt}\boldsymbol{u}_{h})\|_{0}^{2}\ ds.

3.3

The following bound can be found in

\displaystyle+C(\boldsymbol{u},p,l+1)h^{2l}

The next lemma provides estimates for the convective term.

Lemma 3.1

There exist constants
C_{3}
and
C_{4}
such that the following bounds hold for

\displaystyle|b(\boldsymbol{u}_{r}^{n},\boldsymbol{u}_{r}^{n},\boldsymbol{\eta})-b(\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{n},\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{n},\boldsymbol{\eta})|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C_{3}\|\boldsymbol{u}_{r}^{n}-\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{n}\|_{0}\|\nabla\boldsymbol{\eta}\|_{0}\quad\forall\ \boldsymbol{\eta}\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)^{d},

(28)

\displaystyle\left\|(\boldsymbol{u}^{n}\cdot\nabla)\boldsymbol{u}^{n}-(\boldsymbol{u}_{r}^{n}\cdot\nabla)\boldsymbol{u}_{r}^{n}\right\|_{0}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C_{4}\left\|\nabla(\boldsymbol{u}^{n}-\boldsymbol{u}_{r}^{n})\right\|_{0},

(29)

where
C_{3}
depends on
\|\boldsymbol{u}_{r}^{n}\|_{\infty}
,
\|\nabla\boldsymbol{u}_{r}^{n}\|_{L^{2d/(d-1)}}
,
\|\nabla\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{n}\|_{L^{2d/(d-1)}}
,

\|\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{n}\|_{\infty}
and
C_{4}
depends on
\|\boldsymbol{u}_{r}^{n}\|_{\infty}
,
\|\nabla\boldsymbol{u}^{n}\|_{L^{2d/(d-1)}}
.

Proof:

We argue as in
[
21
, (83)]
. Applying the skew-symmetric property of the trilinear form and (
4
), we get

b(\boldsymbol{u}_{r}^{n},\boldsymbol{u}_{r}^{n},\boldsymbol{\eta})-b(\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{n},\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{n},\boldsymbol{\eta})=b(\boldsymbol{u}_{r}^{n},\boldsymbol{u}_{r}^{n}-\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{n},\boldsymbol{\eta})+b(\boldsymbol{u}_{r}^{n}-\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{n},\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{n},\boldsymbol{\eta}).

(30)

Noticing that
b(\boldsymbol{u}_{r}^{n},\boldsymbol{u}_{r}^{n}-\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{n},\boldsymbol{\eta})=-b(\boldsymbol{u}_{r}^{n},\boldsymbol{\eta},\boldsymbol{u}_{r}^{n}-\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{n})
and applying Hölder’s
inequality gives

\displaystyle\left|b(\boldsymbol{u}_{r}^{n},\boldsymbol{u}_{r}^{n}-\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{n},\boldsymbol{\eta})\right\|

(31)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left(\|\boldsymbol{u}_{r}^{n}\|_{\infty}\|\nabla\boldsymbol{\eta}\|_{0}+\frac{1}{2}\|\nabla\cdot\boldsymbol{u}_{r}^{n}\|_{L^{2d/(d-1)}}\|\boldsymbol{\eta}\|_{L^{2d}}\right)\|\boldsymbol{u}_{r}^{n}-\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{n}\|_{0}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\left(\|\boldsymbol{u}_{r}^{n}\|_{\infty}+\frac{1}{2}\|\nabla\cdot\boldsymbol{u}_{r}^{n}\|_{L^{2d/(d-1)}}C_{p}^{\frac{3-d}{2}}\right)\|\nabla\boldsymbol{\eta}\|_{0}\|\boldsymbol{u}_{r}^{n}-\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{n}\|_{0},

where, in the last inequality, we have applied Sobolev’s estimate (
4
) and the Poincaré inequality
(
2
) to bound

\|\boldsymbol{\eta}\|_{L^{2d}}\leq C\|\nabla\boldsymbol{\eta}\|_{0}
if

interpolation inequality to obtain
\|\boldsymbol{\eta}\|_{L^{4}}\leq C\|\boldsymbol{\eta}\|_{1/2}\leq C(\|\boldsymbol{\eta}\|_{0}\|\nabla\boldsymbol{\eta}\|_{0})^{1/2}\leq CC_{p}^{1/2}\|\nabla\boldsymbol{\eta}\|_{0}
.
For the second term on the right-hand side of (
30
), by writing

b(\boldsymbol{u}_{r}^{n}-\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{n},\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{n},\boldsymbol{\eta})=\frac{1}{2}((\boldsymbol{u}_{r}^{n}-\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{n})\cdot\nabla\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{n},\boldsymbol{\eta})-\frac{1}{2}((\boldsymbol{u}_{r}^{n}-\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{n})\cdot\nabla\boldsymbol{\eta},\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{n}),

we find with similar arguments that

\displaystyle\left|b(\boldsymbol{u}_{r}^{n}-\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{n},\boldsymbol{u}_{h},\boldsymbol{\eta})\right|

(32)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\left(\|\nabla\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{n}\|_{L^{2d/(d-1)}}\|\boldsymbol{\eta}\|_{L^{2d}}+\|\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{n}\|_{\infty}\|\nabla\boldsymbol{\eta}\|_{0}\right)\|\boldsymbol{u}_{r}^{n}-\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{n}\|_{0}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\left(\|\nabla\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{n}\|_{L^{2d/(d-1)}}C_{p}^{\frac{3-d}{2}}+\|\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{n}\|_{\infty}\right)\|\nabla\boldsymbol{\eta}\|_{0}\|\boldsymbol{u}_{r}^{n}-\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{n}\|_{0}.

Thus, (
28
) follows from (
31
), (
32
) and by applying estimates (
25
), (
27
)
one obtains that the constant

is bounded.

Similarly, by using the identity

(\boldsymbol{u}^{n}\cdot\nabla)\boldsymbol{u}^{n}-(\boldsymbol{u}_{r}^{n}\cdot\nabla)\boldsymbol{u}_{r}^{n}=((\boldsymbol{u}^{n}-\boldsymbol{u}_{r}^{n})\cdot\nabla)\boldsymbol{u}^{n}+(\boldsymbol{u}_{r}^{n}\cdot\nabla)(\boldsymbol{u}^{n}-\boldsymbol{u}_{r}^{n}),

it follows that

\displaystyle\left\|(\boldsymbol{u}^{n}\cdot\nabla)\boldsymbol{u}^{n}-(\boldsymbol{u}_{r}^{n}\cdot\nabla)\boldsymbol{u}_{r}^{n}\right\|_{0}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left\|\boldsymbol{u}^{n}-\boldsymbol{u}_{r}^{n}\right\|_{L^{2d}}\left\|\nabla\boldsymbol{u}^{n}\right\|_{L^{2d/(d-1)}}

\displaystyle+\left\|\boldsymbol{u}_{r}^{n}\right\|_{\infty}\left\|\nabla(\boldsymbol{u}^{n}-\boldsymbol{u}_{r}^{n})\right\|_{0}.

Then, (

2

\left\|\boldsymbol{u}^{n}-\boldsymbol{u}_{r}^{n}\right\|_{L^{2d}}\leq CC_{p}^{(3-d)/2}\left\|\nabla(\boldsymbol{u}^{n}-\boldsymbol{u}_{r}^{n})\right\|_{0}

\sum_{j=1}^{n}\left|\Delta t\left\|\partial_{t}\boldsymbol{e}_{h}^{j}\right\|_{0}^{2}-\int_{t_{j-1}}^{t_{j}}\left\|\partial_{t}\boldsymbol{e}_{h}(t)\right\|_{0}^{2}\,dt\right|\leq Ch^{2(l-3/2)}\Delta t,

\displaystyle\biggr|\Delta t\left\|\partial_{t}\boldsymbol{e}_{h}^{j}\right\|_{0}^{2}

4
Reduced Order Pressure Approximations

In this section, we analyze two different approaches for approximating the pressure within
the context of a POD-ROM, namely the supremizer enrichment algorithm following
[
21
]

and a stabilization-motivated approach introduced in
[
6
]
.

Define the space spanned by the snapshots of the FOM pressure by

{\mathcal{W}}=\mbox{span}\left\{p_{h}^{1},\ldots,p_{h}^{M}\right\},

i.e.,
the situation of using
the FOM pressure solutions at all time instants, after the initial time, i.e.,
M=n
, will be considered.
For the sake of brevity, we consider only the situation that the
L^{2}(\Omega)
inner product is used for the pressure.
Thus, let
K^{\mathrm{p}}
be the correlation matrix

K^{\mathrm{p}}=((k_{i,j}^{\mathrm{p}}))\in{\mathbb{R}}^{M\times M}
with

k_{i,j}^{\mathrm{p}}=\frac{1}{M}\left(p_{h}^{j},p_{h}^{i}\right).

We denote by

\gamma_{1}\geq\gamma_{2},\ldots\geq\gamma_{d_{p}}>0
the positive eigenvalues of
K^{\mathrm{p}}
and by

\boldsymbol{w}_{1},\ldots,\boldsymbol{w}_{d_{p}}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{M}
the associated eigenvectors.
An orthonormal basis of
\mathcal{W}
is then given by

\psi_{k}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{M}}\frac{1}{\sqrt{\gamma_{k}}}\sum_{j=1}^{M}w_{k}^{j}p_{h}(t_{j},\cdot),

where
w_{k}^{j}
is the
j
-th component of
\boldsymbol{w}_{k}
. The following relation is proved in

[
19
, Proposition 1]
.

\frac{1}{M}\sum_{j=1}^{M}\left\|p_{h}^{j}-\sum_{k=1}^{r}\left(p_{h}^{j},\psi_{k}\right)\psi_{k}\right\|_{0}^{2}=\sum_{k=r+1}^{d_{p}}\gamma_{k}.

(37)

Similarly as for the velocity, we define the stiffness matrix
S^{\mathrm{p}}=((s_{i,j}^{\mathrm{p}}))\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d_{p}\times d_{p}}
, with

s_{i,j}^{\mathrm{p}}=(\nabla\psi_{j},\nabla\psi_{i})
. Then the analog to (
15
) is

\|\nabla q\|_{0}\leq\sqrt{\|S^{\mathrm{p}}\|_{2}}\|q\|_{0}\quad\forall\ q\in{\mathcal{W}}.

(38)

The POD-ROM pressure space will be denoted by

{\mathcal{W}}^{r}=\mbox{span}\left\{\psi_{1},\psi_{2},\ldots,\psi_{r}\right\},\quad 1\leq r\leq d_{p},

where
r
is the same dimension as for the velocity POD-ROM space. The orthogonal projection onto
{\mathcal{W}}^{r}

with respect to the
L^{2}(\Omega)
inner product is denoted by
P_{r}^{\mathrm{p}}\ :\ L^{2}_{0}(\Omega)\to{\mathcal{W}}^{r}
.

4.1
A Supremizer Enrichment Algorithm

Following
[
17
,
21
]
we study a first way to compute a POD-ROM pressure approximation.
The main
idea of the supremizer enrichment algorithm consists in computing a basis of a
r
-dimensional subspace of the
orthogonal complement (with respect to the inner product in
H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)^{d}
) of
{\boldsymbol{V}_{h}^{l}}
,
and then to compute a POD-ROM pressure via the discrete
momentum equation, where the already computed POD-ROM velocity is an input data.

Given a function
p_{r}\in{\mathcal{W}}^{r}
, we consider the following problem: find
\boldsymbol{w}_{h}\in{\boldsymbol{X}}_{h}^{l}
such that

(\nabla\boldsymbol{w}_{h},\nabla\boldsymbol{v}_{h})=-(\nabla\cdot\boldsymbol{v}_{h},p_{r}),\quad\forall\ \boldsymbol{v}_{h}\in{\boldsymbol{X}}_{h}^{l}.

Solving this equation for each basis function
\psi_{k}
,
k=1,\cdots,r
, gives a set of linearly independent
solutions. Since
p_{r}\in Q_{h}^{l}
, it follows that

(\nabla\boldsymbol{w}_{h},\nabla\boldsymbol{v}_{h})=0,\quad\forall\ \boldsymbol{v}_{h}\in{\boldsymbol{V}}_{h}^{l}.

Applying a Gram–Schmidt orthonormalization procedure to the set of linearly independent solutions, with respect to the inner product of
H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)^{d}
,
results in a set of basis functions
\left\{\boldsymbol{\zeta}_{1},\boldsymbol{\zeta}_{2},\ldots,\boldsymbol{\zeta}_{r}\right\}
. Denoting by

{\boldsymbol{S}}^{r}=\mbox{span}\left\{\boldsymbol{\zeta}_{1},\boldsymbol{\zeta}_{2},\ldots,\boldsymbol{\zeta}_{r}\right\}\subset\left({\boldsymbol{V}}_{h}^{l}\right)^{\bot}\subset{\boldsymbol{X}}_{h}^{l},

then the following inf-sup stability condition holds for the spaces
{\boldsymbol{S}}^{r}
and
{\mathcal{W}}^{r}
, see
[
17
, Lemma 4.2]
:

\beta_{r}=\inf_{\psi\in{\mathcal{W}}^{r}}\sup_{\boldsymbol{\zeta}\in{{\boldsymbol{S}}^{r}}}\frac{(\nabla\cdot\boldsymbol{\zeta},\psi)}{\|\nabla\boldsymbol{\zeta}\|_{0}\|\psi\|_{0}}\geq\beta_{\rm{is}},

(39)

where
\beta_{\rm{is}}
is the constant in the inf-sup condition (
7
).
Using the space
{\boldsymbol{S}}^{r}
, a pressure
p_{r}^{n}\in{\mathcal{W}}^{r}
can be computed satisfying for all
\boldsymbol{\zeta}\in{\boldsymbol{S}}^{r}

\displaystyle(\nabla\cdot\boldsymbol{\zeta},p_{r}^{n})=\left(\frac{\boldsymbol{u}_{r}^{n}-\boldsymbol{u}_{r}^{n-1}}{\Delta t},\boldsymbol{\zeta}\right)+b(\boldsymbol{u}_{r}^{n},\boldsymbol{u}_{r}^{n},\boldsymbol{\zeta})+\mu(\nabla\cdot\boldsymbol{u}_{r}^{n},\nabla\cdot\boldsymbol{\zeta})-(\boldsymbol{f}^{n},\boldsymbol{\zeta}).

(40)

Notice that the viscous term is not present since
\boldsymbol{\zeta}\in{\boldsymbol{S}}^{r}\subset({\boldsymbol{V}}_{h}^{l})^{\bot}
and
\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{n}\in{\boldsymbol{V}}_{h}^{l}
implies that
\nu(\nabla\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{n},\nabla\boldsymbol{\zeta})=0
.

Define the following norms

\|\boldsymbol{v}\|_{{\boldsymbol{S}}^{r,*}}=\sup_{\boldsymbol{\zeta}\in{\boldsymbol{S}}^{r}}\frac{(\boldsymbol{v},\boldsymbol{\zeta})}{\|\nabla\boldsymbol{\zeta}\|_{0}},\quad\|\boldsymbol{v}\|_{{\mathcal{\boldsymbol{U}}}^{r,*}}=\sup_{\boldsymbol{\zeta}\in{\mathcal{\boldsymbol{U}}}^{r}}\frac{(\boldsymbol{v},\boldsymbol{\zeta})}{\|\nabla\boldsymbol{\zeta}\|_{0}},

(41)

denote by
\alpha\in[0,1)
the constant in the strengthened Cauchy–Schwarz inequality between the spaces
{\mathcal{\boldsymbol{U}}}^{r}
and
{\boldsymbol{S}}^{r}
, that is,

\left|({\boldsymbol{\zeta}},\boldsymbol{\varphi})\right|\leq\alpha\left\|{\boldsymbol{\zeta}}\right\|_{0}\left\|{\boldsymbol{\varphi}}\right\|_{0},\qquad{\boldsymbol{\zeta}}\in{\boldsymbol{S}}^{r},\quad{\boldsymbol{\varphi}}\in{\boldsymbol{U}}^{r},

and by

C_{r}^{H^{1}}=\sum_{k=1}^{r}\|\nabla\boldsymbol{\varphi}_{k}\|_{0}.

(42)

The right-hand side in (
42
) is bounded by
r\|S^{\mathrm{v}}\|_{2}^{1/2}
in the case
X=L^{2}(\Omega)^{d}
and
by
r
in the case
X=H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)^{d\times d}
, for the velocity projections.
Since in practice
\|S^{\mathrm{v}}\|_{2}>1
, e.g., see
[
21
]
,
the constant
C_{r}^{H^{1}}
is usually smaller in the case
X=H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)^{d\times d}
.
Letting
\boldsymbol{\varphi}\in{\mathcal{\boldsymbol{U}}}^{r}
, it holds, see
[
17
, Lemma 5.14]
,

\displaystyle\|\boldsymbol{\varphi}\|_{{\boldsymbol{S}}^{r,*}}\leq\alpha C_{p}C_{r}^{H^{1}}\|\boldsymbol{\varphi}\|_{{\mathcal{\boldsymbol{U}}}^{r,*}},

(43)

where
C_{p}
is the constant in the Poincaré inequality (
2
).

\displaystyle+C\left(1+\frac{C_{p}C_{r}^{H^{1}}}{\beta_{r}}\right)\left[(TC_{3}^{2}+\nu+\mu)C_{\ref{eq:pre_cota_finalSUPv}}+(C_{3}^{2}C_{p}^{2}+\nu^{2}+\mu^{2})TC_{\ref{eq:cota_pod_0}}\right],

Proof:

Adding and subtracting

\displaystyle\left(\nabla\cdot\boldsymbol{\zeta},P_{r}^{\mathrm{p}}p_{h}^{n}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\partial_{t}\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{n},\boldsymbol{\zeta}\right)+b(\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{n},\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{n},\boldsymbol{\zeta})+\mu(\nabla\cdot\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{n},\nabla\cdot\boldsymbol{\zeta})-(\boldsymbol{f}^{n},\boldsymbol{\zeta})

(45)

\displaystyle+\left(\nabla\cdot\boldsymbol{\zeta},P_{r}^{\mathrm{p}}p_{h}^{n}-p_{h}^{n}\right)\quad\forall\ \boldsymbol{\zeta}\in{\boldsymbol{S}}^{r}.

Decomposing the error

subtracting the POD-ROM problem (

(

the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality, and (

\displaystyle\leq

4.2
Stabilization-Motivated Pressure ROM

In this section we consider a different procedure for computing a POD-ROM pressure, which was proposed in
[
6
]
.
The starting point of this method consists in formally considering a residual-based stabilization of a
coupled velocity-pressure POD-ROM system. Since the functions from the velocity POD-ROM space are
discretely divergence-free, the system decouples and one obtains a Poisson-type equation for a
POD-ROM pressure whose right-hand side contains terms with the velocity POD-ROM approximation.
This method was recently analyzed in
[
7
]
. However, the error analysis in

[
7
]
has some drawbacks that will be overcome in the subsequent analysis.

The stabilization-motivated approach results in the following problem for a POD-ROM pressure:
Find
p_{r}^{n}\in{\mathcal{W}}^{r}
such that for all
\psi\in{\mathcal{W}}^{r}

\displaystyle\sum_{K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}\tau_{K}\left(\nabla p_{r}^{n},\nabla\psi\right)_{K}

(47)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\sum_{K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}\tau_{K}\left(\frac{\boldsymbol{u}_{r}^{n}-\boldsymbol{u}_{r}^{n-1}}{\Delta t}+(\boldsymbol{u}_{r}^{n}\cdot\nabla)\boldsymbol{u}_{r}^{n}-\nu\Delta\boldsymbol{u}_{r}^{n}-\boldsymbol{f}^{n},\nabla\psi\right)_{K},

where
\tau_{K}
are positive parameters of order
h_{K}^{2}
:

c_{1}h_{K}^{2}\leq\tau_{K}\leq c_{2}h_{K}^{2},\quad K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}.

(48)

We notice that, as it is usually the case in the literature, the nonlinear terms do not involve
\frac{1}{2}(\nabla\cdot\boldsymbol{u}_{r}^{n}\boldsymbol{u}_{r}^{n})
. Also we observe that although the POD velocities
\boldsymbol{u}_{r}^{j}
,

, are discretely divergence-free, the term
(\boldsymbol{u}_{r}^{n}-\boldsymbol{u}_{r}^{n-1})/\Delta t
cannot be omitted in (
47
) unless all the
\tau_{K}
are equal.

It was already mentioned in
[
6
]
that on quasi-uniform triangulations the choice of
\tau_{K}
in terms
of
h_{K}
is of minor importance, since these parameters appear on both sides of (
47
). The following
analysis can be performed with a generic parameters. However, the concrete norm on the left-hand sides of the error estimates
in Theorem
4.3
and the order of convergence on the right-hand sides depend on the concrete choice given in (
48
). We also performed numerical simulations with the choice

\tau_{K}=Ch_{K}
, as it was proposed in
[
6
]
. The results are very similar to
those presented in Section

of brevity.

5.1.4
Summary

The well-known benefit of grad-div stabilization on velocity errors could be seen also in this no-flow problem.
It was observed, for small viscosity coefficients, that poor velocity results from FOM simulations without grad-div
stabilization might have a strong impact on POD-ROM pressure errors, because the poor velocity results might induce
inaccurate pressure results and thus lead to pressure snapshots of poor quality. In this respect, using grad-div
stabilization is also beneficial for computing accurate POD-ROM pressure solutions. The numerical studies supported
the analytic results with respect to the robustness of the errors for small viscosity coefficients and with respect
to convergence orders. Concerning the error in
l^{2}(L^{2})
, using SE-ROM led to slightly more accurate results
than using SM-ROM. In all other aspects, both methods behaved very similarly.

6
Summary and Outlook

This paper presents the error analysis for two ways of computing a pressure approximation in POD-ROM
simulations of incompressible flow problems. Based on using a grad-div stabilization for both the
FOM and the velocity POD-ROM, error bounds were derived whose constants do not blow up as the viscosity
tends to zero. For the supremizer enrichment POD-ROM (SE-ROM), the presented analysis covers a different set of generating
elements of the velocity ROM space than considered so far in the literature. Concerning the
stabilization-motivated POD-ROM (SM-ROM), the results of the literature are improved considerably with respect
to several aspects, compare the description in the Introduction. Numerical studies support the analytic
results and provide an initial comparison of the behavior of both methods for computing a POD-ROM pressure.

The numerical results comparing SE-ROM and SM-ROM are not yet conclusive. Often, the results are quite
similar. In the first example, the SE-ROM was more accurate with respect to the error in the
l^{2}(L^{2})
norm
and in the second example, SM-ROM was more accurate with respect to the maximal lift coefficient. Further
numerical studies are needed for obtaining a better understanding of the performance of both methods
in practice.

Nonlinear photomagnetization in insulators
Bernardo S. Mendoza, Norberto Arzate-Plata, Nicolas Tancogne-Dejean, Benjamin M. Fregoso

Abstract
Nonlinear photomagnetization is a process by which an oscillating electric field induces a static magnetization. We show that all 32 crystallographic point groups admit such spin polarization using circularly polarized electric fields to second order (as in the usual spin orientation or inverse Faraday effect) but only 29 points groups admit spin polarization using linearly polarized electric fields to second order. The excluded point groups are the highly symmetric m m -3 m m , - 43 ​ m 43m and 432. Using density functional theory we compute the spectrum of the second-order electric spin susceptibility of prototypical semiconductors Te, Se, SnS 2 , GaAs, InSb and Si which corresponds to nonmagnetic materials with and without inversion symmetry. We show that nonlinear photomagnetization can be comparable to those of naturally occurring ferromagnets.

II
Symmetry constrains

Consider the expansion of the spin polarization in powers of an electric field to second order. More generally, consider the expansion of a macroscopic (time-independent)
pseudovector

\boldsymbol{\bar{\mathcal{V}}}
in powers of a (time-dependent) vector
\mathbf{V}
(schematically),

\displaystyle\bar{\mathcal{V}}^{a}(\omega)=\zeta^{abc}(\omega)V^{b}(\omega)V^{c}(-\omega),

(1)

where
\zeta
is a third-rank pseudotensor and
\mathbf{V}(\omega)
is the Fourier component of a homogeneous vector given by

\displaystyle\mathbf{V}=\mathbf{V}(\omega)e^{-i\omega t}+c.c.

(2)

For clarity we omit all frequency dependance in subsequent equations. Summation over repeated indices is implied. In metals the leading contribution to spin polarization is linear, i.e., the Edelstein effect
[
38
]
, but in insulators Eq. (
1

[
39
]

A transformation of the coordinate frame by an element
M

\displaystyle\zeta^{a^{\prime}b^{\prime}c^{\prime}}=\textrm{det}(M)M^{aa^{\prime}}M^{bb^{\prime}}M^{cc^{\prime}}\zeta^{abc},

(3)

where
\textrm{det}(M)
introduces a negative sign for improper transformations. In the case of the observable being a vector
\boldsymbol{\mathcal{V}}
, such as polarization, instead of a pseudovector, we would have to second order

\displaystyle\mathcal{V}^{a}=\chi^{abc}V^{b}V^{c},

(4)

where
\chi
is a third-rank tensor with transformation law

\displaystyle\chi^{a^{\prime}b^{\prime}c^{\prime}}=M^{aa^{\prime}}M^{bb^{\prime}}M^{cc^{\prime}}\chi^{abc}.

(5)

If the crystal point group does not contain improper transformations the factor det
(M)
is irrelevant and the tensor and pseudotensor have the same symmetry constrains. But if the point group contains improper transformations, the symmetry constrains on tensor and pseudotensors can have profound consequences. Using Eqs. (
3
) and (
5
) we find the nonzero components of the response tensor and pseudotensor for the 32 crystallographic points groups (see Table
1
).

[

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{\bar{\mathcal{V}}}

\displaystyle=\nu_{2}|\mathbf{V}|^{2}+v_{2}\mathbf{V}\times\mathbf{V}^{*},

(6)

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{\mathcal{V}}

\displaystyle=\sigma_{2}|\mathbf{V}|^{2}+\eta_{2}\mathbf{V}\times\mathbf{V}^{*},

IV
Discussion

We showed that all 32 crystallography point groups admit CPE spin polarization, but only 29 of those admit LPE spin polarization. The point groups 432,-43
m
and
m
-
3m
do not develop LPE-spin polarization. As shown in Ref.
[
39
]
, CPE spin polarization does not require SOC, inversion symmetry or quantum coherence. LPE spin polarization, on the other hand, requires SOC, quantum coherence, and, as shown here, a crystal that is not too symmetric. An intuitive picture emerges as follows: at every
\mathbf{k}
point in the Brillouin zone (BZ) there is a slightly different local magnetic field (due to SOC), and hence spin-up and spin-down states precess about slightly different directions. Addition of all such spins gives a net polarization only for certain types trajectories in the BZ. For highly symmetric crystals the addition of spins cancels. Because the off-diagonal components of the spin are involved in the sum we require quantum coherence.

For practical purposes, LPE spin polarization provides a finer control knob over the spin, namely, the electric field polarization direction. We have shown with realistic materials’ DFT computations that photomagnetization with LPE and CPE could be comparable (Table
3
) hence broadening options for technological applications in magnet-optics. We have not discussed the contribution to the orbital magnetization which we expect to be present. In a future work we will address this important topic. Our results also give more insight into the materials studied recently in Ref.
[
62
]
. For example
BiH
has point point -3
m
and hence allows LPE(CPE) spin polarization with two components
xxx=-xyy=-yyx,xyz=-yxz
(
xyz=-yxz,zxy
).

Because of the requirement of quantum coherence we expect our results to be observed at low enough temperatures and ultrashort time scales, e.g., pump-probe experiments. Note also that the classification of responses using spatial groups (instead of magnetic groups) assumes weak SOC.

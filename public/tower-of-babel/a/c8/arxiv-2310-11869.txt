Quark propagator and di-lepton production rate in a hot, dense and very strongly magnetized rotating Quark-Gluon Plasma
Aritra Das

Abstract
In this paper, a theoretical calculation of thermal di-lepton production rate is reported from a hot and dense, unbounded rotating quark gluon plasma in the presence of very strong uniform background magnetic field typically generated at heavy-ion collision experiments. In this extreme magnetic field, the quarks as well as anti-quarks are approximated to be confined in the lowest Landau level (LLL). Firstly, I have converted the expression of the quark propagator in LLL approximation to the momentum space representation. After that, using the derived quark propagator, the di-lepton production rate (DPR) is calculated from the photon polarization tensor (PPT) with the help of the framework of thermal field theory. The system is first confined in a cylinder of radius R ⟂ R_{\scriptscriptstyle{\perp}} and then R ⟂ R_{\scriptscriptstyle{\perp}} is taken to be very large in order to consider the situation of unbounded system. Depending on the charge of participating quarks, the role of rotation is to alter the chemical potential. The DPR is suppressed prominently for low invariant mass with respect to the LLL-approximated non-rotating case that was reported earlier.

II
Setup

In this section, I discuss about Dirac equation for a quark in the presence of an uniform space-time independent background magnetic field with magnitude
B
in a reference frame rotating with an angular velocity
\Omega
. Keeping in mind the situation created in HIC experiments, both the magnetic field
B
and the angular velocity
\Omega
are taken along the same direction. Along that direction, I orient the
z
-axis of the coordinate system. Also, the direction of propagation of the two participating ions is taken along the
x
-axis. In this case, the metric appropriate to the rotating frame is written as

\displaystyle g_{\mu\nu}=\begin{pmatrix}1-(x^{2}+y^{2})\Omega^{2}&y\Omega&-x\Omega&0\\
y\Omega&-1&0&0\\
-x\Omega&0&-1&0\\
0&0&0&-1\end{pmatrix}.

(1)

The Dirac equation, describing a quark of flavor
f
in a rotating frame, is written as

\displaystyle\left[i\gamma^{\mu}\left(\partial_{\mu}+\Gamma_{\mu}\right)-m_{f}\right]\psi=0,

(2)

where
m_{f}
is the mass of the quark and
\Gamma^{\mu}
is the affine connection which is defined by

\displaystyle\Gamma^{\mu}=-\frac{i}{4}\omega_{\mu\hat{i}\hat{j}}\sigma^{\hat{i}\hat{j}},

(3)

where Latin indices with hat, i.e.,
\hat{i},\hat{j},\ldots=\hat{t},\hat{x},\hat{y},\hat{z}
, denote the directions of the Cartesian coordinate system in the laboratory frame and Greek indices
\mu,\nu,\ldots=t,x,y,z
denote the general coordinates in the rotating frame. The quantities
\omega_{\mu\hat{i}\hat{j}}
,
\sigma^{\hat{i}\hat{j}}
in Eq. (
3
), are defined by

\displaystyle\omega_{\mu\hat{i}\hat{j}}\equiv g_{\lambda\rho}e^{\lambda}_{\hat{i}}\left(\partial_{\mu}e^{\rho}_{\hat{j}}+\Gamma^{\rho}_{\mu\nu}e^{\nu}_{\hat{j}}\right),\qquad\sigma^{\hat{i}\hat{j}}=\frac{i}{2}\left[\gamma^{\hat{i}},\gamma^{\hat{j}}\right],

(4)

where

\displaystyle\Gamma^{\lambda}_{\mu\nu}=\frac{1}{2}g^{\lambda\sigma}\left(\partial_{\mu}g_{\sigma\nu}+\partial_{\nu}g_{\mu\sigma}-\partial_{\sigma}g_{\mu\nu}\right)

(5)

is the Christoffel connection and
e^{\mu}_{\hat{i}}
is the vierbein that connects the general coordinates with the Cartesian coordinate is written as
x^{\mu}=e^{\mu}_{\hat{i}}x^{\hat{i}}
. In this case, the components of vierbein are given by

\displaystyle e^{t}_{\hat{t}}=e^{x}_{\hat{x}}=e^{y}_{\hat{y}}=e^{z}_{\hat{z}}=1,\quad e^{x}_{\hat{t}}=y\Omega,\quad e^{y}_{\hat{t}}=-x\Omega,

(6)

with other components being zero. For the Christoffel connection, the non-zero components are written as

\displaystyle\Gamma^{x}_{ty}=\Gamma^{x}_{yt}=-\Omega,\quad\Gamma^{y}_{tx}=\Gamma^{y}_{xt}=\Omega,\quad\Gamma^{x}_{tt}=-x\Omega^{2},\quad\Gamma^{y}_{tt}=-y\Omega^{2}.

(7)

With this, the only component of affine connection that survives is given as

\displaystyle\Gamma_{t}=-\frac{i}{2}\Omega\sigma^{\hat{x}\hat{y}}.

(8)

Now the components of the gamma matrices in the rotating frame are given by
\gamma^{\mu}=e^{\mu}_{\hat{i}}\gamma^{\hat{i}}
. In this case, one has

\displaystyle\gamma^{t}=\gamma^{\hat{t}},\quad\gamma^{x}=y\Omega\gamma^{\hat{i}}+\gamma^{\hat{x}},\quad\gamma^{y}=-x\Omega\gamma^{\hat{t}}+\gamma^{\hat{y}},\quad\gamma^{z}=\gamma^{\hat{z}}.

(9)

Now, in the presence of background magnetic field, the partial derivatives
\partial_{\mu}
are replaced by covariant derivatives
D_{\mu}
as
\partial_{\mu}\rightarrow D_{\mu}=\partial_{\mu}-iq_{f}\mathcal{A}_{\mu}
, where
\mathcal{A}_{\mu}
is the gauge field responsible for the generation of the background magnetic field. It can be chosen in various gauges, but for our purpose I choose it in the symmetric gauge where
\mathcal{A}^{\mu}=\dfrac{B}{2}(0,-y,x,0)
. Also
q_{f}
is the charge of quark of flavor
f
. In this paper, I consider only light
u
and
d
quarks whose charges are given by
q_{u}=\dfrac{2}{3}e
and
q_{d}=-\dfrac{1}{3}e
, respectively. Here
e
, taken as the absolute value of electron’s charge, is positive. Thus, the general form of the Dirac equation describing rotating fermions in the magnetized QGP is given as

\displaystyle\left[i\gamma^{\mu}\left(D_{\mu}+\Gamma_{\mu}\right)-m_{f}\right]\psi=0.

(10)

In the case of uniform rotation
\bm{\Omega}=\Omega\bm{\hat{z}}
, and magnetic field
\bm{B}=B\bm{\hat{z}}
, it becomes

\displaystyle\left[i\gamma^{\hat{t}}\left(\partial_{t}+\Omega y\partial_{x}-\Omega x\partial_{y}-\frac{i}{2}\Omega\sigma^{\hat{x}\hat{y}}\right)+i\gamma^{\hat{x}}\left(\partial_{x}+i\frac{q_{f}B}{2}y\right)+i\gamma^{\hat{y}}\left(\partial_{y}-i\frac{q_{f}B}{2}x\right)+i\gamma^{\hat{z}}\partial_{z}-m_{f}\right]\psi=0.

(11)

Note that I have retained gamma matrices in the laboratory frame but coordinates in the rotating frame for the convenience of our calculation. From now on, I simply write
\gamma^{\hat{t}},\gamma^{\hat{x}},\cdots
as
\gamma^{0},\gamma^{1},\cdots
. In this way

\displaystyle\sigma^{\hat{x}\hat{y}}=\sigma^{12}=\begin{pmatrix}\sigma_{z}&&0\\
0&&\sigma_{z}\end{pmatrix},

(12)

where
\sigma_{z}
is the
z
component of the Pauli spin matrix.

III
The Quark Propagator

This section deals with the expression of the quark propagator in the rotato-magnetic background that is necessary for the calculation of DPR. I shall quote the expression of the quark propagator in a magnetised rotating medium in subsec.
III.1
of this section. Then, in subsec.
III.2

III.1
The Quark Propagator in a bounded magneto-rotating system

The equation that the quark propagator of flavor
f
satisfies is given as

\displaystyle\bigg[i\gamma^{0}\left(\partial_{t}+\Omega y\partial_{x}-\Omega x\partial_{y}-\frac{i}{2}\Omega\sigma^{12}\right)+i\gamma^{1}\left(\partial_{x}+i\frac{q_{f}B}{2}y\right)+i\gamma^{2}\left(\partial_{y}-i\frac{q_{f}B}{2}x\right)+i\gamma^{3}\partial_{z}-m_{f}\Bigg]S^{\scriptscriptstyle{(f)}}_{\alpha\beta}(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{X}^{\prime})

\displaystyle\hskip 369.88582pt=\delta^{(4)}(\mathcal{X}-\mathcal{X}^{\prime})\delta_{\alpha\beta},

(13)

where I have collectively denoted the space-time coordinates by
\mathcal{X}^{\mu}=(t,x,y,z)=(t,\bm{r})
and
{\mathcal{X}^{\prime}}^{\mu}=(t^{\prime},x^{\prime},y^{\prime},z^{\prime})=(t^{\prime},\bm{r}^{\prime})
. Before proceeding to the solution of Eq. (
13
), one can note that it is convenient to confine the system in a cylinder with radius
R_{\scriptscriptstyle{\perp}}
and work with the cylindrical polar coordinates where
x=r_{\scriptscriptstyle{\perp}}\cos\phi
,
y=r_{\scriptscriptstyle{\perp}}\sin\phi
, with
t,z
remaining unchanged. Here

is the distance of point

from the axis of rotation. The solution of Eq. (
13
) was investigated thoroughly in Ref.
Sadooghi:2021upd
. Below, I just quote the expression

\displaystyle S^{\scriptscriptstyle{(f)}}_{\alpha\beta}(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{X}^{\prime})

\displaystyle=i\sum_{n,\ell}\int\frac{dk_{0}dk_{z}}{(2\pi)^{2}}\mathcal{C}_{n,\ell,s_{q}}^{2}\,e^{-ik_{0}(t-t^{\prime})+ik_{z}(z-z^{\prime})}\left[\mathbbm{P}^{\scriptscriptstyle{(f)}}_{\lambda_{n},\ell}(\xi,\phi)\right]_{\alpha\rho}\left(\frac{\gamma\cdot\tilde{k}_{\lambda_{n},\ell,+}+m_{\scriptscriptstyle{f}}}{(k_{0}+\Omega j)^{2}-\epsilon^{\scriptscriptstyle{(f)}\,2}_{\lambda_{n},k_{z}}}\right)_{\rho\lambda}\left[\mathbbm{P}^{\scriptscriptstyle{(f)}}_{\lambda_{n},\ell}(\xi^{\prime},\phi^{\prime})\right]^{\dagger}_{\lambda\beta}.

(14)

As we can see Eq. (
14
) involves many terms, which are explained one after another. Here
\alpha
,
\beta
,
\lambda
,
\rho
denotes Lorentz indices. The term

is a positive or negative half-integer written as

\displaystyle j\equiv\ell+\frac{1}{2},

(15)

and
\tilde{k}_{\lambda_{n},\ell,\kappa}
is the
Ritus momentum
which is given by

\displaystyle\tilde{k}_{\lambda_{n},\ell,\kappa}=\left(k_{0}+\kappa\Omega j,0,\kappa s_{\ell}\sqrt{2\lambda_{n}|q_{f}B|},k_{z}\right),

(16)

with
s_{\ell}=\textsf{sgn}(\ell)
,
\kappa=\pm 1
. Here
\kappa=1
and
\kappa=-1
are related to the particle and antiparticle solution of the Dirac equation in rotato-magnetic medium, respectively.
Also, for any four vector

, I define

\displaystyle\gamma\cdot a=\not{a}=\gamma^{0}a_{0}-\gamma^{1}a^{1}-\gamma^{2}a^{2}-\gamma^{3}a^{3},\quad a^{\mu}_{\shortparallel}=(a_{0},0,0,a_{z}),\quad a^{\mu}_{\scriptscriptstyle{\perp}}=(0,a_{x},a_{y},0)

\displaystyle\gamma\cdot a_{\shortparallel}=\not{a}_{\shortparallel}=\gamma^{0}a_{0}-\gamma^{3}a^{3},\quad\gamma\cdot a_{\scriptscriptstyle{\perp}}=\gamma^{1}a^{1}+\gamma^{2}a^{2},

\displaystyle A^{2}=a_{0}^{2}-a_{x}^{2}-a_{y}^{2}-a_{z}^{2},\quad a_{\shortparallel}^{2}=a_{0}^{2}-a_{z}^{2},\quad a_{\scriptscriptstyle{\perp}}^{2}=a_{x}^{2}+a_{y}^{2},\quad a^{2}=|\bm{a}|^{2}=a_{x}^{2}+a_{y}^{2}+a_{z}^{2}.

(17)

The factor
\mathbbm{P}^{\scriptscriptstyle{(f)}}_{\lambda_{n},\ell}(\xi,\phi)
is given as

\displaystyle\mathbbm{P}^{\scriptscriptstyle{(f)}}_{\lambda_{n},\ell}(\xi)=\mathit{P}_{+}f^{+}_{\lambda_{n},\ell,s_{q}}(\xi,\phi)+\mathit{P}_{-}f^{-}_{\lambda_{n},\ell,s_{q}}(\xi,\phi),\qquad\text{with}\quad\xi=\frac{|q_{f}B|r_{\scriptscriptstyle{\perp}}^{2}}{2},

(18)

where
\mathit{P}_{\pm}=\dfrac{1}{2}\left(\openone\pm i\gamma^{1}\gamma^{2}\right)
and

\displaystyle f^{+}_{\lambda_{n},\ell,s_{q}}(\xi,\phi)=\mathcal{C}^{+}_{n,\ell,s_{q}}e^{i\ell\phi}\,\,\Phi^{+}_{\lambda_{n},\ell,s_{q}}(\xi),

\displaystyle f^{-}_{\lambda_{n},\ell,s_{q}}(\xi,\phi)=\mathcal{C}^{-}_{n,\ell,s_{q}}e^{i(\ell+1)\phi}\,\,\Phi^{-}_{\lambda_{n},\ell,s_{q}}(\xi).

(19)

The subscripts and superscripts appearing in
f
bear different meanings which are explained one after another:

(i)

The indices
+
and
-
in the superscript of the function

are related to the spin up and spin down of a quark, respectively.

(ii)

s_{q}=\textsf{sgn}(q_{f}B)
, with

. In this paper, I have taken the background magnetic field to point along the

(iii)

\lambda_{n}
, with (

), is a parameter analogous to Landau levels in the case of non-rotating magnetic medium. The method to determine it’s value, for system with finite
R_{\scriptscriptstyle{\perp}}
, is discussed later in this subsection. In the limit of
R_{\scriptscriptstyle{\perp}}\rightarrow\infty
, it takes the value of positive integer including zero.

(iv)

\ell
is the orbital angular momentum quantum number whose range depends on

1

The pre-factors
\mathcal{C}^{\pm}_{n,\ell,s_{q}}
are defined by

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}^{\pm}_{n,\ell,s_{q}}=\left(\frac{|q_{f}B|}{2\pi\int\limits^{\alpha_{b}}_{0}d\xi\left[\Phi^{\pm}_{\lambda_{n},\ell,s_{q}}(\xi)\right]^{2}}\right)^{1/2},

(20)

where

.
The functions

appearing in Eq. (

\displaystyle\Phi^{+}_{\lambda_{n},\ell,s_{q}}(\xi)=\frac{1}{|\ell|!}\left(\frac{|q_{f}B|}{2\pi}\frac{\left(\mathcal{N}^{+}_{\lambda_{n},s_{q}}+|\ell|\right)!}{\mathcal{N}^{+}_{\lambda_{n},s_{q}}!}\right)^{1/2}e^{-\xi/2}\xi^{\frac{|\ell|}{2}}\,\,{{}_{1}F_{1}\left(-\mathcal{N}^{+}_{\lambda_{n},s_{q}};|\ell|+1;\xi\right)},

\displaystyle\Phi^{-}_{\lambda_{n},\ell,s_{q}}(\xi)=\frac{1}{|\ell+1|!}\left(\frac{|q_{f}B|}{2\pi}\frac{\left(\mathcal{N}^{-}_{\lambda_{n},s_{q}}+|\ell+1|\right)!}{\mathcal{N}^{-}_{\lambda_{n},s_{q}}!}\right)^{1/2}e^{-\xi/2}\xi^{\frac{|\ell+1|}{2}}\,\,{{}_{1}F_{1}\left(-\mathcal{N}^{-}_{\lambda_{n},s_{q}};|\ell+1|+1;\xi\right)}.

(21)

The function
{}_{1}F_{1}
in Eq. (

) is the confluent hypergeometric function. The terms

are defined as

\displaystyle\mathcal{N}^{+}_{\lambda_{n},s_{q}}

\displaystyle=\lambda_{n}+\frac{s_{q}(\ell+1)-|\ell|-1}{2},

\displaystyle\mathcal{N}^{-}_{\lambda_{n},s_{q}}

\displaystyle=\lambda_{n}+\frac{s_{q}\ell-|\ell+1|-1}{2}.

(for

) are Landau level like numbers (

In this case, the corresponding processes are decay processes

\displaystyle\bigg(\textsf{Im}\Pi^{\mu}_{f\;\mu}\bigg)_{\text{II}_{\pm}}=\pm N_{c}q_{f}^{2}m_{q}^{2}\frac{|q_{f}B|}{8\pi}\exp\left(-\frac{p_{\scriptscriptstyle{\perp}}^{2}}{2|q_{f}B|}\right)

In the last line I have introduced two modes, independent of the sign of

VI
Discussions

To arrive at the simplified result of differential DPR, I have made some assumptions, working best in the early stage of heavy ion collision, under which the validity of our calculation can be justified.
4
4

4

The typical scale hierarchy that works best in my calculation is
\sqrt{|eB|}>T>\mu>\Omega
.
These are listed below —

(I)

At the initial stage of HIC, the generated magnetic field is very high. It is the highest among all the scales present in the problem. In this scenario, one can assume that the energy gap between the zero-th and first Landau level is so high that the quark antiquark pairs are confined in the LLL
Hattori:2023egw
;
Fukushima:2018grm
. Thus, the LLL approximation is justified only in the initial stage.

(II)

Next, during the early stages of HIC, the radial expansion is not yet developed by particle interactions. In this case one can assume that the quarks are totally dragged by the vortical motion of the fluid. Therefore, the approximation
\bar{\phi}+\Omega\bar{t}=0
is a good one
Ayala:2021osy
in this scenario.

(III)

In my calculation, I have assumed no radial boundary that confines the quarks from escaping the rotating medium. However, to preserve causality, one must have
g_{00}\leqslant 1
. This is ensured by the condition
\Omega R_{\scriptscriptstyle{\perp}}\leqslant 1

Chernodub:2016kxh
. Thus, to secure the validity of our calculation, I have considered the case of slow rotation.

(IV)

When tackling the problem of rotating medium, one should keep in mind that the temperature of the plasma depends on
r_{\scriptscriptstyle{\perp}}
, the distance of the point in concern from the axis of rotation. Specifically, it changes with distance as
T\left(r_{\scriptscriptstyle{\perp}}\right)=T\left(r_{\scriptscriptstyle{\perp}}=0\right)/\sqrt{g^{00}}
, where
g^{00}
is the zero-th component of metric tensor in curved space time
Tolman:1930zza
. For the rigid rotation studied here
g^{00}=1-\Omega^{2}r_{\scriptscriptstyle{\perp}}^{2}
. Following
Braguta:2021jgn
, I have neglected the variation of
T
with
r_{\scriptscriptstyle{\perp}}
by considering it the same with all over the space that at the rotation axis.

2.

\bm{eB=0}

\bm{\Omega\neq 0:}

In the absence of background
B

\Omega

consider two observers sitting on a rotating plate at the same radial coordinate but with different angles will agree on some measurement. From the intuition of classical mechanics, it is clear that they will have different radial velocity.

3.

\bm{eB\neq 0}

and

\bm{\Omega\neq 0:}

\Omega

In the presence of background B-field and rotations, the factor that breaks translational invariance, denoted by

\displaystyle\Xi\left(\bar{t},\xi,\bar{\phi};\xi^{\prime}\right)=\frac{\xi+\xi^{\prime}}{2}-\sqrt{\xi\xi^{\prime}}e^{i(\bar{\phi}+\Omega\bar{t})}\quad\text{with}\quad\bar{t}=t-t^{\prime},\,\bar{\phi}=\phi-\phi^{\prime}.

•

\displaystyle=\exp\left[-\frac{|q_{f}B|}{2}\left\{r_{\scriptscriptstyle{\perp}}^{2}+{r_{\scriptscriptstyle{\perp}}^{\prime}}^{2}-2r_{\scriptscriptstyle{\perp}}r_{\scriptscriptstyle{\perp}}^{\prime}\cos\left(\bar{\phi}+\Omega\bar{t}\right)\right\}\right].

VIII
Conclusion

In this work, I have calculated an analytical expression of dilepton production rate from a rotating color deconfined QGP medium in the presence of strong background magnetic field. I have considered the case of
\bm{B}\parallel\bm{\Omega}
, which is suitable in the context of heavy ion collision experiments. To achieve this goal, I have reformulated the expression of quark propagator in expressed in terms of four-position basis to four momentum basis under the approximation that fermions are totally dragged by the vortical motion of the created medium. In this reformulation of propagator, I only worked with lowest Landau level approximation which is valid when the generated magnetic field is stronger than all the scales present in the theory. In LLL, only spin up
u
,
\bar{d}
quark and spin down
\bar{u}
,
d
can propagate. The effect of rotation can be interpreted as an alteration of the quark chemical potential of the system which depends on the charge of the quark under consideration. The dilepton production rate is suppressed compared to the non-rotating due to the presence of rotation. When the invariant mass is reduced, the rate of suppression is found out to be more rapid for higher values of
\Omega
. With increasing
|eB|
, the rate gets enhanced in this calculations.

As an outlook one can take a more pragmatic approach to study the DPR by lifting the first three approximations mentioned in Sec.
VI
. One such approach is to relax the condition that both the quark and antiquark is in LLL. In this circumstance, it is reasonable to expect that there will be contributions to the rate due to the transition of quarks (anti-quarks) between various Landau levels like
\Omega=0
case
Das:2021fma
;
Wang:2022jxx
. Also, one can take magneto-rotating system bounded in a cylindrical box whose radius is of the order of the size of the system (
R\sim 10
fm). Then, one must take into a boundary condition (e.g, MIT boundary condition
Chernodub:2016kxh
, global boundary condition
Sadooghi:2021upd
) to strictly ensure that causality in the calculation. Lastly, one can reformulate the rate-formula of DPR from the very beginning by employing the tools of basic thermal quantum field theory when the initial quarks are not in momentum eigenstates. In this direction, one can utilize the skill of QFT in non-trivial backgrounds and consult the classic paper of Weldon
Weldon:1990iw
.

Appendix A
Fermionic Frequency Sum

The frequency sum of interest is written as

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}\equiv T\!\sum_{k_{0}}\frac{1}{\left[(k_{0}+a)^{2}-E_{1}^{2}\right]}\frac{1}{(p_{0}-k_{0}-\bar{a})^{2}-E_{2}^{2}}.

(81)

In the ITF formalism of thermal field theory, the fermionic momentum at finite density is replaced by
k_{0}\rightarrow i(2l+1)\pi T+\mu=\widetilde{\omega}_{l}+\mu
and
p_{0}\rightarrow i2\pi mT=\omega_{m}
. Thus, the frequency sum can be rewritten as

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}

\displaystyle=T\!\sum_{l=-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{1}{\left[(i\widetilde{\omega}_{l}+\mu+a)^{2}-E_{1}^{2}\right]}\frac{1}{(i\omega_{m}-i\widetilde{\omega}_{l}-\mu-\bar{a})^{2}-E_{2}^{2}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{r_{1}=\pm 1}\sum_{r_{2}=\pm 1}\frac{r_{1}r_{2}}{4E_{1}E_{2}}T\!\sum_{l=-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{1}{i\widetilde{\omega}_{l}+\mu+a-r_{1}E_{1}}\frac{1}{i(\omega_{m}-\widetilde{\omega}_{l})-\mu-\bar{a}-r_{2}E_{2}}.

(82)

The fraction in the right hand side of the above equation can be written as

\displaystyle\frac{1}{i\widetilde{\omega}_{l}+\mu+a-r_{1}E_{1}}

\displaystyle=\tilde{n}^{\scriptscriptstyle{(+)}}\left(r_{1}E_{1}-a\right)\int\limits_{0}^{\beta}d\tau_{1}\,e^{-\tau_{1}\left(i\widetilde{\omega}_{l}+\mu+a-r_{1}E_{1}\right)}

\displaystyle\frac{1}{i(\omega_{m}-\widetilde{\omega}_{l})-\mu-\bar{a}-r_{2}E_{2}}

\displaystyle=\tilde{n}^{\scriptscriptstyle{(-)}}\left(r_{2}E_{2}+\bar{a}\right)\int\limits_{0}^{\beta}d\tau_{2}\,e^{-\tau_{2}\left[i(\omega_{m}-\widetilde{\omega}_{l})-\mu-\bar{a}-r_{2}E_{2}\right]}.

(83)

where
\beta\equiv T^{-1}
,
\tilde{n}^{\scriptscriptstyle{\pm}}(E)\equiv\left[e^{\beta(E\mp\mu)}+1\right]^{-1}
.
Thus, substituting Eq. (
83
) in Eq. (
82

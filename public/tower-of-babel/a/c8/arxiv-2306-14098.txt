Nonexistence of stable discrete maps into some homogeneous spaces of nonnegative curvature
Toru Kajigaya

Abstract
. We consider stabilities for the weighted length or energy functional of a discrete map from a finite weighted graph ( X , m E ) (X,m_{E}) into a smooth Riemannian manifold ( M , g ) (M,g) . We prove the non-existence of a stable discrete minimal immersion or a non-constant stable discrete harmonic map from a finite weighted graph into certain homogeneous spaces, such as Kähler C C -spaces of positive holomorphic sectional curvature and some simply-connected compact Riemannian symmetric spaces.

Introduction

Let
(X,m_{E})
be a finite weighted graph with a positive valued weight function
m_{E}:E\to\mathbb{R}_{>0}
on the set of (oriented) edges
E
and
(M,g)
a smooth closed Riemannian manifold with a Riemannian metric
g
. We consider a piecewise smooth map
f:X\to M
which realizes a configuration of the graph
X
in the space
M
, such as a triangulation of surface and a net in a higher dimensional space.
We define the (weighted)
length

{\bf L}
and the
energy

{\bf E}
of
f:(X,m_{E})\to(M,g)
by

{\bf L}(f):=\sum_{e\in E}m_{E}(e)\int_{0}^{1}\|\dot{f}_{e}\|_{g}\,dt,\quad{\bf E}(f):=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{e\in E}m_{E}(e)\int_{0}^{1}\|\dot{f}_{e}\|_{g}^{2}\,dt,

where
\dot{f}_{e}
is the derivative of the restricted map
f_{e}=f|_{e}
for each edge
e\in E
. These functionals are natural generalizations of the length or energy of a closed curve, and a map that appears as a critical point of each functional is regarded as a generalization of the closed geodesic.

A critical point of the energy functional
{\bf E}
is called
discrete harmonic map
, and it was introduced by Colin de Verdière
[
5
]
to consider a triangulation of surface consisting of geodesic edges. Kotani-Sunada
[
14
]
studied a special type of discrete harmonic map into a flat torus of arbitrary dimension, known as the standard realization, which provides a periodic realization of a graph in the Euclidean space
\mathbb{R}^{n}
with large symmetry. Recently, Lam
[
16
]
pointed out that the standard realization in a 2-dimensional flat torus is actually obtained by a weighted Delaunay decomposition of the flat torus. Moreover, the existence of a hyperbolic version of the standard realization of a graph in a closed Riemann surface of arbitrary genus has been proved by the author and Tanaka
[
15
]
under a mild assumption of the discrete map.
Also, a geodesic immersion of a graph with uniform weight function that attains a critical point of
{\bf L}
is referred to as
stationary (or minimal) geodesic net
and studied by many researchers. For instance, various existence results of stationary geodesic nets have been established by Nabutovsky-Rotman
[
21
]
and Liokumovich-Staffa
[
18
]
. In this paper, more generally, we shall call an immersion

(see Proposition
2.1
). On the other hand, a discrete harmonic map (i.e. a critical point of
{\bf E}
) is not necessarily an immersion.

that is a critical point of

.
Note that a critical point of

is not necessarily a critical point of

as long as we fix the weight function

. However, they are closely related if we permit a change of weight function (see Section

It is known that if the sectional curvature of
(M,g)
is non-positive, any discrete harmonic map into
M
minimizes the energy in its homotopy class (see
[
5
,
14
]
). Thus, we obtain a non-trivial energy minimizing discrete map into such

whenever the map is not homotopic to a point. For instance, this is the case of previous works
[

,
14

15

16
]
in flat tori or hyperbolic surfaces.
However, the situation is different if the sectional curvature of
(M,g)
is not always non-positive. For example, the theorem of Synge implies that, if
M
is an even-dimensional oriented closed Riemannian manifold with positive sectional curvature, then there is no non-trivial length/energy minimizing closed geodesic in
M
, and in particular,
M
is simply-connected since a minimizing closed geodesic always exists if
M
is not simply-connected (indeed, each non-trivial homotopy class in
\pi_{1}(M)
contains such a geodesic by the classical result).

When the target manifold
M
is simply-connected, any continuous map
f:X\to M
from a graph
X
is homotopic to a point, and therefore, there is no non-trivial global minimizer of the functional
{\bf F}={\bf L}
or
{\bf E}
in the homotopy class of
f
. However, it is still an interesting problem to investigate whether a critical point attains a local minimum of
{\bf F}
or not.
We say a critical point
f

stable
if
d^{2}{\bf F}(f_{s})/ds^{2}|_{s=0}\geq 0
for any piecewise smooth deformation
\{f_{s}\}_{s}
of
f=f_{0}
. Obviously, any local minimizer must be a stable map.

The proof of Synge’s theorem actually shows that there is no stable closed geodesic if
M
is even-dimensional, oriented and has positive sectional curvature. However, this result is not necessarily true for an odd-dimensional space. Indeed, some concrete examples of stable closed geodesic are given by Ziller
[
35
]
. For example, he showed that the closed geodesic in the direction of the Reeb vector field in a
3
-dimensional Berger sphere
S^{3}
is stable (see
[
35
, Example 1]
). Moreover, a generalization of his example in a higher dimensional Berger sphere
S^{2n+1}
is given by Torralbo-Urbano (see
[
31
, Proposition 6]
). Note that the Berger sphere is a Riemannian homogeneous space of strictly positive sectional curvature. Thus, this provides a simple example of stable (discrete) minimal immersion or harmonic map into a simply-connected positively curved homogeneous space.

Furthermore, Cheng
[
6
]
recently proved that there exists a convex hypersurface
M^{n}
in
\mathbb{R}^{n+1}

(n\geq 2)
that contains a stable stationary geodesic net consisting of a bouquet graph without any closed geodesic. He also states that
M
is homeomorphic to
S^{n}
and the metric has positive sectional curvature. Note that, by Proposition
2.10
in the present paper, this also implies the existence of stable discrete harmonic immersion from a weighted graph into such
M
. In particular, Cheng’s result shows that the non-existence of stable discrete maps does not hold in general even if
M
satisfies the same assumption of Synge’s theorem.

Therefore, it is a natural question to ask whether a stable discrete minimal immersion or a stable discrete harmonic map exists in a given simply-connected Riemannian manifold
M
of positive or non-negative curvature.
In the present paper, we prove the non-existence of stable maps for certain compact Riemannian homogeneous spaces with an extra geometric structure.
Our first main result is as follows.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
(M,J,g)
be a simply-connected compact homogeneous Kähler manifold of positive holomorphic sectional curvature. Then there do not exist any
stable discrete minimal immersion or a non-constant stable discrete harmonic map from a finite weighted graph
(X,m_{E})
into
(M,g)
.

A typical example of Kähler manifold satisfying the assumption of Theorem
1.1
is given by a Hermitian symmetric space of compact type. Moreover, a simply-connected compact homogeneous Kähler manifold is also known as a
Kähler
C
-space
(or a
generalized flag manifold
), and it is conjectured that any Kähler
C
-space has positive holomorphic sectional curvature (cf.
[
22
]

[
11
]
and Lohove
[
19
]
for some Kähler
C
-spaces with small second Betti number.

Next, we consider a simply-connected compact Riemannian symmetric space (RSS for short) as a target manifold
(M,g)
. Note that the sectional curvature of a compact RSS is non-negative. In the present paper, we show the following result.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
(M,g)
be a simply-connected, compact irreducible Riemannian symmetric space.

(i)

If
(M,g)
is either a strongly unstable RSS, a Hermitian symmetric space, or satisfies that
{\rm rank}M\leq 3
and
M\neq SO(q+3)/SO(q)\times SO(3)

, then there is no stable discrete minimal immersion from a finite weighted graph
(X,m_{E})
into
(M,g)
.

(ii)

If
(M,g)
is either a strongly unstable RSS, a Hermitian symmetric space or the exceptional Lie group
G_{2}
, then there is no non-contant stable discrete harmonic map from a finite weighted graph
(X,m_{E})
into
(M,g)
.

Here, a compact RSS
M
is said to be
strongly unstable
if there is no non-constant stable
smooth
harmonic map from any compact Riemannian manifold
(N,h)
into
(M,g)
. Note that there are several equivalent conditions for the strongly instability (see
[
10
, Theorem 5.3]
and
[
24
, Theorem 4]
). For example,

is strongly unstable if and only if the identity map

is not a stable harmonic map.
There is a complete classification of strongly unstable, simply-connected compact irreducible RSSs due to Howord-Wei
[
10
]
and Ohnita
[
24
]
(see Table
1
in Section
4.4

We conjecture that the non-existence of stable discrete minimal immersion/harmonic map holds for any simply-connected compact RSS. However, there are some difficulties in our method when
M
is not the space given in Theorem
1.2
and these cases are remaining problems. See Section
4.4
for details and further discussion.

Our proof is based on the averaging method, which was first used by Lawson-Simons
[
17
]
to prove the non-existence of stable varifolds in the standard sphere. Burns-Burstall-Bartolomeis-Rawnsley
[
3
]
and Ohnita-Udagawa
[
26
]
generalized this method to harmonic maps from a Riemann surface into a compact Hermitian symmetric space, and if this is the case, they proved that the stable harmonic map must be a holomorphic or anti-holomorphic map. Howard-Wei
[
10
]
and Ohnita
[
24
]
applied the method to a Riemannian manifold isometrically immersed in the Euclidean space, and gave a complete classification of strongly unstable simply-connected compact irreducible RSS. Ohnita
[
25
]
also used this method to prove the non-existence of stable rectifiable
p
-current of
p
less than the dimension of the Helgason sphere in some compact RSSs.

In this paper, we show that their methods are valid even for discrete maps. In fact, Theorem
1.1
is proved by applying the method used in
[
3
,
26
]
to the energy functional
{\bf E}
. Furthermore, if
M
is isometrically immersed into the Euclidean space, we obtain some criteria for the non-existence by improving the method given in
[
10
,
24
]
(Corollary
4.4
and Proposition
4.6
). Theorem
1.2
is proved by showing these criteria for an equivariant isometric immersion of symmetric space. However, our proof of Theorem
1.2
is divided into several cases and requires a detailed computation for an exceptional case. More precisely, we construct an explicit isometric immersion of the rank
2
exceptional compact symmetric space
G_{2}/SO(4)
in order to prove the non-existence of discrete minimal immersion into
G_{2}/SO(4)
(See Section
5
for details).

The paper is organized as follows. In Section
2
, we summarize basic facts on discrete minimal immersion and discrete (
p
-)harmonic map. In particular, we give a useful characterization and mention a relationship between these maps. In Section
3
, we give a proof of Theorem

. In Section

is isometrically immersed into
\mathbb{R}^{n+k}
. By applying this result to a certain isometric immersion of symmetric space, we prove Theorem

, and we give the details in Section

Let
X=(V,E)
be a finite graph, where

. We denote the origin and the terminate of an edge

\overline{e}

can be joined by a path

satisfying that

,

and
t(e_{N})=y
. Throughout this paper, we assume

is a connected graph. A
weight function

m_{E}:E\to\mathbb{R}_{>0}
is a positive valued function on

satisfying that
m_{E}(\overline{e})=m_{E}(e)
for any edge
e\in E
. We call the pair
(X,m_{E})

weighted graph
.

In the following, we identify
X
with a CW-complex as described in
[
14
]
. In particular, each edge
e\in E
is identified with the closed interval
[0,1]
and we denote the parameter of
[0,1]
by
t
. Note that the reversed edge
\overline{e}
is parameterized by
1-t
.
For a given smooth manifold

, we consider a piecewise
C^{k}
-map

, which we simply say a
discrete (
C^{k}
-)map
. Here,
piecewise

means that the restricted map
f_{e}:=f|_{e}:[0,1]\to M
is a
C^{k}
-map for each

. Note that

in this notation.
In the present paper, we assume

for our purpose.

For a given discrete map
f:X\to M
, an edge

is said to be
degenerate
if
f_{e}
is a constant map, otherwise we say

.
A discrete map

is said to be a (discrete)

if

, i.e.

. If

We denote the set of piecewise smooth vector filed along

by
\Gamma(f^{-1}TM)
. More precisely, we set

\Gamma(f^{-1}TM):=\left\{W:X\to TM\;\left|\;\begin{gathered}W_{e}\in\Gamma(f^{-1}_{e}TM)\ \forall e\in E\end{gathered}\right.\right\}
,
where

Discrete minimal immersion and (
p

Although our main interest in the present paper is the length or energy functional, we introduce a general notion in order to deal with both functionals simultaneously.

Let
(X,m_{E})
be a finite weighted graph and
(M,g)
a smooth Riemannian manifold.
For an integer

{\bf E}^{p}(f):=\frac{1}{p}\sum_{e\in E}m_{E}(e)\int_{0}^{1}\|\dot{f}_{e}\|_{g}^{p}\,dt,

where
\|\dot{f}_{e}\|_{g}=g(\dot{f}_{e},\dot{f}_{e})^{1/2}

is regarded as a discrete analogue of the

4

and it coincides with the (twice of) sum of weighted length of

and it coincides with the discrete energy considered in
[
5

14
,
15
]

We make a technical, but a reasonable assumption only in the case when

we assume

is an immersion if we consider the critical point of
{\bf E}^{1}={\bf L}
. Under this assumption, we obtain the following useful characterization of the critical point of

.

(M,g)
be a smooth Riemannian manifold,

(1)

Suppose

(i)

Remark 2.2
.

The minimal geodesic immersion with uniform weight function

Notice that any discrete minimal immersion

p
-harmonic immersions: Let
f:(X,m_{E})\to(M,g)
be a minimal geodesic immersion. We define a discrete map
f^{p}:(X,m_{E}^{p})\to(M,g)
by

(3)

\displaystyle\textup{ $f^{p}:=f$\quad and\quad$m_{E}^{p}(e):=m_{E}(e)\|T_{e}\|^{1-p}$ for $e\in E$}.

Then,
m_{E}^{p}:E\to\mathbb{R}
is a positive weight function since
\|T_{e}\|
is a non-zero constant along each
e\in E
. Note that the definition of
m_{E}^{p}
depends on
f
. By definition,
f^{p}:(X,m_{E}^{p})\to(M,g)
is a geodesic map satisfying the
p
-balanced condition. Therefore,
f^{p}
is a
p
-harmonic immersion by Proposition
2.1
.
Conversely, if
f
is a
p
-harmonic immersion, we obtain a minimal geodesic immersion by the reverse procedure. We summarize the result as follows.

Proposition 2.3
.

Any minimal geodesic immersion
f:(X,m_{E})\to(M,g)
yields a
p
-harmonic immersion
f^{p}:(X,m_{E}^{p})\to(M,g)
for any
p\geq 2
by changing the weight function, and the converse construction also holds.

Example 2.4
.

Any closed geodesic in a Riemannian manifold
M
is regarded as a discrete minimal or
p
-harmonic immersion of a loop
X=(V,E)=(\{\gamma(0)\},\{\gamma,\overline{\gamma}\})
with arbitrary weight
m_{E}
since the balanced condition is automatically satisfied at the vertex.
Similarly, if there exist finitely many closed geodesics
\gamma_{1},\ldots,\gamma_{N}
starting from the same point
w\in M
, then the union of geodesics with arbitrary weight function
m_{E}
can be regarded as a discrete minimal or a
p
-harmonic immersion of an
N
-bouquet graph
with a single vertex.

More generally, let
\gamma_{1},\ldots,\gamma_{N}

(N\geq 2)
be simple closed geodesics in
M
such that any two distinct geodesics intersect transversally, i.e. for any two distinct numbers
i,j\in\{1,\ldots,N\}
,
\gamma_{i}([0,1])\cap\gamma_{j}([0,1])
is either an empty set or consists of finite number of points. We suppose that, for any
i
, there exists
j\neq i
such that
\gamma_{i}([0,1])\cap\gamma_{j}([0,1])\neq\phi
so that there is no isolated geodesic. Then we define the set of vertices
V
by the set of intersection points:

\displaystyle V

\displaystyle:=\{w\in M\mid w\in\gamma_{i}([0,1])\cap\gamma_{j}([0,1])\ \textup{for some distinct $i,j\in\{1,\ldots,N\}$}\}.

If there are at least two intersection points on
\gamma_{i}
, then
\gamma_{i}
is divided into some geodesic segments and we regard each geodesic segment as a (non-oriented) edge. If there is a single intersection point on
\gamma_{i}
, we regard
\gamma_{i}
as a (non-oriented) loop. Then, we obtain a finite graph
X=(V,E)
and an embedding
f:X\to M
such that
f_{e}:[0,1]\to M
is a geodesic map that image coincides with the corresponding geodesic segment for each
e\in E
. If we consider a uniform weight
m_{E}\equiv 1
, then
f:(X,m_{E})\to(M,g)
becomes a discrete minimal geodesic immersion. Note that
f
is not necessarily a
p
-harmonic map.

A typical example of
(M,g)
admitting such a union of closed geodesics is given by a
compact rank one symmetric space
(CROSS for short) (i.e.
M
is either
S^{n}
,
\mathbb{R}P^{n}
,
\mathbb{C}P^{n}
,
\mathbb{H}P^{n}
or
\mathbb{O}P^{2}
with the canonical Riemannian metric as a symmetric space). In fact, if
M
is a CROSS, any geodesic is periodic with the same period
l
.

Moreover, we can construct a discrete minimal immersion into a compact Riemannian symmetric space
M
of higher rank by a similar method.
Note that not all geodesics are periodic if the rank of
M
is grater than
1
. Indeed, the flat torus
T^{n}
(
n\geq 2
) has infinitely many non-periodic geodesics, and so does
M
since any compact Riemannian symmetric space contains a totally-geodesic flat torus
T^{r}
whose dimension
r
coincides with the rank of
M
(
T^{r}
is called the
maximal torus
of
M
). However, the flat torus
T^{r}
also contains infinitely many periodic geodesics. Since any point
w\in M
is contained in a maximal torus
T^{r}
, there exist infinitely many direction
v\in T_{w}T^{r}\subseteq T_{w}M
such that the geodesic in the direction
v
is periodic.
Therefore, one may construct a union of (simple) closed geodesics
\gamma_{1},\ldots,\gamma_{N}
in
M
even if
M
is of rank grater than 1, and this yields a discrete minimal immersion into
M
.

See
[
18
,
21
]
for some existence results of discrete minimal immersion in a closed Riemannian manifold.
Note also that, if
M
is a compact Riemannian manifold, there exists at least one discrete
2
-harmonic map
f:(X,m_{E})\to(M,g)
(possibly a constant map) in a given homotopy class
\mathcal{C}=[f_{0}]
of arbitrary piecewise continuous map
f_{0}:X\to M
(See
[
5
,
14
]
).
See also
[
14
,
15
]
for some explicit examples of
2
-harmonic map into a flat torus or a closed Riemann surface.

2.2.
Second variation

Throughout this subsection, we suppose
f:(X,m_{E})\to(M,g)
is either a minimal immersion with
p=1
or a
p
-harmonic map with
p\geq 2
.
In this section, we shall compute the second variation of
{\bf E}^{p}
.

For
V\in\Gamma(f^{-1}TM)
, we denote the orthogonal projection onto the tangent space and normal space of
f_{e}
by
V^{\top_{e}}
and
V^{\perp_{e}}
, respectively.

Remark 2.5
.

For a given
V\in\Gamma(f^{-1}TM)
,
\{V_{e}^{\perp_{e}}\}_{e\in E}
(and also
\{V_{e}^{\top_{e}}\}_{e\in E}
) does
not
define an element in
\Gamma(f^{-1}TM)
in general (although
V_{e}^{\perp_{e}}\in\Gamma(f_{e}^{-1}TM)
for each
e\in E
), because it could admit a vertex
x
such that
V_{e}^{\perp_{e}}(x)\neq V_{e^{\prime}}^{\perp_{e^{\prime}}}(x)
for some
e,e^{\prime}\in E_{x}
.

Proposition 2.6
.

Let
f:(X,m_{E})\to(M,g)
be either a minimal immersion (not necessarily a geodesic map) with
p=1
or a
p
-harmonic map with
p\geq 2
. Then,

(4)

\displaystyle\frac{d^{2}}{ds^{2}}{\bf E}^{p}(f_{s})\Big|_{s=0}=\sum_{e\in E}m_{E}(e)\int_{0}^{1}\Big\{\|\nabla_{T_{e}}V_{e}\|^{2}-g(R(V_{e},T_{e})T_{e},V_{e})+(p-2)\|(\nabla_{T_{e}}V_{e})^{\top_{e}}\|^{2}\Big\}\|T_{e}\|^{p-2}\,dt,

for any picewise smooth defomation
\{f_{s}\}_{s\in(-\epsilon,\epsilon)}
of
f=f_{0}
.

Proof.

By direct computation, we see

(5)

\displaystyle\frac{d^{2}}{ds^{2}}{\bf E}^{p}(f_{s})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{p}\sum_{e\in E}m_{E}(e)\int_{0}^{1}\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial s^{2}}g(T_{e},T_{e})^{\frac{p}{2}}\,dt=\sum_{e\in E}m_{E}(e)\int_{0}^{1}\frac{\partial}{\partial s}g(\nabla_{V_{e}}T_{e},\|T_{e}\|^{p-2}T_{e})\,dt

\displaystyle=\sum_{e\in E}m_{E}(e)\int_{0}^{1}g(\nabla_{V_{e}}T_{e},\nabla_{V_{e}}(\|T_{e}\|^{p-2}T_{e}))+g(\nabla_{V_{e}}\nabla_{V_{e}}T_{e},T_{e})\|T_{e}\|^{p-2}\,dt

\displaystyle=\sum_{e\in E}m_{E}(e)\int_{0}^{1}g(\nabla_{T_{e}}V_{e},\nabla_{V_{e}}(\|T_{e}\|^{p-2}T_{e}))+\{-g(R(V_{e},T_{e})T_{e},V_{e})+g(\nabla_{T_{e}}\nabla_{V_{e}}V_{e},T_{e})\}\|T_{e}\|^{p-2}\,dt,

where we used
[V_{e},T_{e}]=0
. On a subset satisfying that
\|T_{e}\|\neq 0
, we compute

\displaystyle g(\nabla_{T_{e}}V_{e},\nabla_{V_{e}}(\|T_{e}\|^{p-2}T_{e}))

\displaystyle=V_{e}\|T_{e}\|^{p-2}\cdot g(\nabla_{T_{e}}V_{e},T_{e})+\|T_{e}\|^{p-2}\|\nabla_{T_{e}}V_{e}\|^{2}

\displaystyle=(p-2)\|T_{e}\|^{p-4}g(\nabla_{T_{e}}V_{e},T_{e})^{2}+\|T_{e}\|^{p-2}\|\nabla_{T_{e}}V_{e}\|^{2}

Since
g(\nabla_{T_{e}}V_{e},T_{e})^{2}=\|T_{e}\|^{2}\|(\nabla_{T_{e}}V_{e})^{\top_{e}}\|^{2}
, we obtain

(6)

\displaystyle g(\nabla_{T_{e}}V_{e},\nabla_{V_{e}}(\|T_{e}\|^{p-2}T_{e}))

\displaystyle=\{(p-2)\|(\nabla_{T_{e}}V_{e})^{\top_{e}}\|^{2}+\|\nabla_{T_{e}}V_{e}\|^{2}\}\cdot\|T_{e}\|^{p-2}.

Notice that this equation still holds if
\|T_{e}\|=0
(Recall that
T_{e}\neq 0
if
p=1
).

Next, we shall show the last term of the formula (
5
) is vanishing since
f=f_{0}
is either a minimal immersion or a
p
-harmonic map. Indeed, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{e\in E}m_{E}(e)\int_{0}^{1}g(\nabla_{T_{e}}\nabla_{V_{e}}V_{e},T_{e})\|T_{e}\|^{p-2}\,dt\Big|_{s=0}

\displaystyle=\sum_{e\in E}m_{E}(e)\int_{0}^{1}\frac{\partial}{\partial t}g(\nabla_{V_{e}}V_{e},\|T_{e}\|^{p-2}T_{e})-g(\nabla_{V_{e}}V_{e},\nabla_{T_{e}}(\|T_{e}\|^{p-2}T_{e}))\,dt\Big|_{s=0}

\displaystyle=\sum_{e\in E}m_{E}(e)\Big[g(\nabla_{V_{e}}V_{e},\|T_{e}\|^{p-2}T_{e})\Big]_{t=0}^{t=1},

where we used the fact
{\rm(i)}^{\prime}

\nabla_{T_{e}}(\|T_{e}\|^{p-2}T_{e})=0
as
f_{0}
is minimal or
p
-harmonic (See proof of Proposition
2.1
). Since
\nabla_{V_{e}}V_{e}|_{t=1}=\nabla_{V_{\overline{e}}}V_{\overline{e}}|_{t=0}
and
\nabla_{V_{e}}V_{e}(x)
does not depend on
e\in E_{x}
, the
p
-balanced condition implies that

\displaystyle\sum_{e\in E}m_{E}(e)\Big[g(\nabla_{V_{e}}V_{e},\|T_{e}\|^{p-2}T_{e})\Big]_{t=0}^{t=1}

\displaystyle=-2\sum_{x\in V}\sum_{e\in E_{x}}m_{E}(e)g(\nabla_{V_{e}}V_{e},\|T_{e}\|^{p-2}T_{e})_{x}=0.

Therefore, substituting (
6
) to (
5
), we obtain the formula.
∎

According to this proposition, we set

(7)

\displaystyle{\rm Hess}_{f}^{p}(V,W):=\sum_{e\in E}m_{E}(e)

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}\Big\{g(\nabla_{T_{e}}V_{e},\nabla_{T_{e}}W_{e})-g(R({V_{e}},T_{e})T_{e},W_{e})

\displaystyle\qquad+(p-2)g((\nabla_{T_{e}}V_{e})^{\top_{e}},(\nabla_{T_{e}}W_{e})^{\top_{e}})\Big\}\|T_{e}\|^{p-2}\,dt

for
V,W\in\Gamma(f^{-1}TM)
, and we call the
Hessian
of
{\bf E}^{p}
at
f
.

The following expression of the Hessian is also useful.

Lemma 2.7
.

Let
f:(X,m_{E})\to(M,g)
be either a minimal immersion (not necessarily a geodesic map) with
p=1
or a
p
-harmonic map with
p\geq 2
. Then, we have

(8)

\displaystyle{\rm Hess}_{{f}}^{p}(V,W)

\displaystyle=\sum_{e\in E}m_{E}(e)\int_{0}^{1}\Big\{g(\nabla_{T_{e}}V_{e}^{\perp_{e}},\nabla_{T_{e}}W_{e}^{\perp_{e}})-g(R(V_{e}^{\perp_{e}},T_{e})T_{e},W_{e}^{\perp_{e}})

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad+(p-1)g(\nabla_{T_{e}}V_{e}^{\top_{e}},\nabla_{T_{e}}W_{e}^{\top_{e}})\Big\}\|T_{e}\|^{p-2}\,dt

for
V,W\in\Gamma(f^{-1}TM)
. In particular, if
p=1
, then
{\rm Hess}_{f}^{1}(V,W)
depends only on
\{V_{e}^{\perp_{e}}\}_{e\in E}
and
\{W_{e}^{\perp_{e}}\}_{e\in E}
.

Proof.

Let
U_{e}
be any tangent vector field along
f_{e}
and
N_{e}
be any normal vector field along
f_{e}
.
Since
(\nabla_{T_{e}}T_{e})^{\perp_{e}}=0
in any case, we have
g(\nabla_{T_{e}}N_{e},T_{e})=-g(N_{e},\nabla_{T_{e}}T_{e})=0
, and hence,
\nabla_{T_{e}}N_{e}
is orthogonal to
T_{e}
. Moreover, we see
g(\nabla_{T_{e}}U_{e},N_{e})=-g(U_{e},\nabla_{T_{e}}N_{e})=0
since
U_{e}
is tangent to
f_{e}
. Therefore
\nabla_{T_{e}}U_{e}
is also parallel to
T_{e}
. In particular, for any vector field
X_{e}
along
f_{e}
, the decomposition
\nabla_{T_{e}}X_{e}=\nabla_{T_{e}}X_{e}^{\top_{e}}+\nabla_{T_{e}}X_{e}^{\perp_{e}}
implies that

(9)

\displaystyle(\nabla_{T_{e}}X_{e})^{\top_{e}}=\nabla_{T_{e}}X_{e}^{\top_{e}}\quad{\rm and}\quad(\nabla_{T_{e}}X_{e})^{\perp_{e}}=\nabla_{T_{e}}X_{e}^{\perp_{e}}.

Using this relation, it is easy to see that (
8
) follows from (
7
).
∎

We shall derive another expression of the Hessian for a geodesic map which will be used in the proof of our main theorem.

Lemma 2.8
.

Suppose
f
is either a minimal geodesic immersion with
p=1
or a
p
-harmonic map with
p\geq 2
. Then, we have

(10)

\displaystyle{\rm Hess}_{f}^{p}(V,W)=\sum_{e\in E}m_{E}(e)\int_{0}^{1}g(\mathcal{J}_{f,e}^{p}(V_{e}),W_{e})\,dt+2\sum_{x\in V}\sum_{e\in E_{x}}m_{E}(e)g(\mathcal{B}_{f,e}^{p}(V_{e}),W_{e})_{x},

where
\mathcal{J}_{f,e}^{p}
and
\mathcal{B}_{f,e}^{p}
are defined as follows:

\displaystyle\mathcal{J}_{f,e}^{p}(V_{e})

\displaystyle:=\{-\nabla_{T_{e}}\nabla_{T_{e}}V_{e}-R(V_{e},T_{e})T_{e}-(p-2)(\nabla_{T_{e}}\nabla_{T_{e}}V_{e})^{\top_{e}}\}\|T_{e}\|^{p-2}.

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}_{f,e}^{p}(V_{e})

\displaystyle:=\{-\nabla_{T_{e}}V_{e}-(p-2)(\nabla_{T_{e}}V_{e})^{\top_{e}}\}\|T_{e}\|^{p-2}.

Proof.

We shall compute the first and the last integrals in the Hessian formula (
7
). Note that
\|T_{e}\|
is constant along each
e\in E
since we assume
f
is a geodesic map. Then, by using the integration by parts, we have

(11)

\displaystyle\sum_{e\in E}m_{E}(e)\int_{0}^{1}g(\nabla_{T_{e}}V_{e},\nabla_{T_{e}}W_{e})\|T_{e}\|^{p-2}\,dt

\displaystyle=\sum_{e\in E}m_{E}(e)\Big[g(\nabla_{T_{e}}V_{e},W_{e})\cdot\|T_{e}\|^{p-2}\Big]_{t=0}^{t=1}-\sum_{e\in E}m_{E}(e)\int_{0}^{1}g(\nabla_{T_{e}}\nabla_{T_{e}}V_{e},W_{e})\cdot\|T_{e}\|^{p-2}\,dt

\displaystyle=-2\sum_{x\in V}\sum_{e\in E_{x}}m_{E}(e)g(\nabla_{T_{e}}V_{e},W_{e})_{x}\cdot\|T_{e}\|^{p-2}-\sum_{e\in E}m_{E}(e)\int_{0}^{1}g(\nabla_{T_{e}}\nabla_{T_{e}}V_{e},W_{e})\cdot\|T_{e}\|^{p-2}\,dt.

Similar computation shows that

(12)

\displaystyle\sum_{e\in E}m_{E}(e)\int_{0}^{1}g((\nabla_{T_{e}}V_{e})^{\top_{e}},(\nabla_{T_{e}}W_{e})^{\top_{e}})\|T_{e}\|^{p-2}\,dt

\displaystyle=-2\sum_{x\in V}\sum_{e\in E_{x}}m_{E}(e)g((\nabla_{T_{e}}V_{e})^{\top_{e}},W_{e})_{x}\cdot\|T_{e}\|^{p-2}-\sum_{e\in E}m_{E}(e)\int_{0}^{1}g(\nabla_{T_{e}}(\nabla_{T_{e}}V_{e})^{\top_{e}},W_{e})\cdot\|T_{e}\|^{p-2}\,dt.

Here, we have that

(13)

\displaystyle\nabla_{T_{e}}(\nabla_{T_{e}}V_{e})^{\top_{e}}=(\nabla_{T_{e}}\nabla_{T_{e}}V_{e})^{\top_{e}}.

Indeed, if
\|T_{e}\|\equiv 0
(note that this occurs only when
p\geq 2
), then (
13
) is obvious. Suppose
\|T_{e}\|\not\equiv 0
, and put
\widetilde{T}_{e}:=\|T_{e}\|^{-1}{T_{e}}
. Then, it holds that

\nabla_{T_{e}}\widetilde{T}_{e}=0

since
f
is a geodesic map. Therefore, we see

\displaystyle\nabla_{T_{e}}(\nabla_{T_{e}}V_{e})^{\top_{e}}=\nabla_{T_{e}}\{g(\nabla_{T_{e}}V_{e},\widetilde{T}_{e})\widetilde{T}_{e}\}=g(\nabla_{T_{e}}\nabla_{T_{e}}V_{e},\widetilde{T}_{e})\widetilde{T}_{e}=(\nabla_{T_{e}}\nabla_{T_{e}}V_{e})^{\top_{e}}

as required.

Thus, substituting (
11
)–(
13
) to the Hessian formula (
7
), we obtain the formula (
10
).
∎

Notice that, if
p=2
,
\mathcal{J}_{f,e}^{2}
coincides with the classical Jacobi operator for the map
f_{e}:[0,1]\to M
. It should be noted that, in our discrete setting, the Hessian formula (
10
) involves the boundary term in general. However, there is a reasonable subset of variational vector fields along
f
to simplify the Hessian formula (
10
): For a discrete map
f:(X,m_{E})\to(M,g)
, we put

(14)

\displaystyle\Gamma_{bal}(f^{-1}TM):=\Big\{V\in\Gamma(f^{-1}TM)\mid\sum_{e\in E_{x}}m_{E}(e)\nabla_{T_{e}}V_{e}(x)=0\,\,\textup{for any vertex $x$}\Big\}.

Geometrically,
\Gamma_{bal}(f^{-1}TM)
is regarded as the tangent space at
f
of the space of piecewise
C^{\infty}
maps satisfying the
2
-balanced condition (cf.
[
15
, Lemma B.2]
). If
V\in\Gamma_{bal}(f^{-1}TM)
, then we have

\displaystyle\sum_{x\in V}\sum_{e\in E_{x}}m_{E}(e)g(\mathcal{B}_{f,e}^{2}(V_{e}),W_{e})_{x}=0

for any
W\in\Gamma(f^{-1}TM)
since
W_{e}(x)
does not depend on
e
. Thus, if this is the case, the Hessian formula (
8
) for the
2
-harmonic map can be written by

{\rm Hess}_{f}^{2}(V,W)=\sum_{e\in E}m_{E}(e)\int_{0}^{1}g(\mathcal{J}_{f,e}^{2}(V_{e}),W_{e})\,dt.

2.3.
Stabilities

A critical point
f
of
{\bf E}^{p}
is said to be
stable
if
\frac{d^{2}}{ds^{2}}{\bf E}^{p}(f_{s}){|}_{s=0}\geq 0
for any piecewise smooth deformation
\{f_{s}\}_{s\in(-\epsilon,\epsilon)}
of
f=f_{0}
. If
f
is either a discrete minimal immersion or a
p
-harmonic map, the stability is equivalent to
{\rm Hess}_{f}^{p}(V,V)\geq 0
for any
V\in\Gamma(f^{-1}TM)
.

The following theorem is an immediate consequence of the second variational formula (
4
).

Theorem 2.9
.

Let
f:(X,m_{E})\to(M,g)
be either a discrete minimal immersion or a discrete
p
-harmonic map. If the sectional curvature of
(M,g)
is non-positive, then
f
is stable.

In contrast to this fact, if the sectional curvature of
(M,g)
is not always non-positive, the stability is a non-trivial property.

We mention some useful facts on the stability of discrete maps.
Firstly, if
f
is a discrete minimal immersion, then
f
is stable if and only if the associated minimal geodesic immersion
f_{geo}
is stable. This is because the weighted length
{\bf L}(f)={\bf E}^{1}(f)
is independent of the choice of parameter of
f_{e}
.

Secondly, we recall that any minimal geodesic immersion
f:(X,m_{E})\to(M,g)
yields a
p
-harmonic immersion
f^{p}:(X,m_{E}^{p})\to(M,g)
by letting
m_{E}^{p}(e):=m_{E}(e)\|T_{e}\|^{1-p}
for each
e\in E
(see Proposition
2.3
). Then, we have the following fact.

Proposition 2.10
.

Let
f:(X,m_{E})\to(M,g)
be a minimal geodesic immersion. If
f
is stable with respect to
{\bf E}^{1}={\bf L}
, then the associated
p
-harmonic immersion
f^{p}:(X,m_{E}^{p})\to(M,g)
is also stable with respect to
{\bf E}^{p}
.

In particular, if every non-constant
p
-harmonic map into
(M,g)
is unstable for some
p\geq 2
, then there is no stable discrete minimal immersion into
(M,g)
.

Proof.

By Lemma
2.7
, we have

(15)

\displaystyle{\rm Hess}_{{f}}^{p}(V,V)=\sum_{e\in E}m_{E}(e)\int_{0}^{1}\Big\{\|\nabla_{T_{e}}V_{e}^{\perp_{e}}\|^{2}-g(R(V_{e}^{\perp_{e}},T_{e})T_{e},V_{e}^{\perp_{e}})+(p-1)\|\nabla_{T_{e}}V_{e}^{\top_{e}}\|^{2}\Big\}\|T_{e}\|^{p-2}\,dt,

where
f
is either a discrete minimal immersion with
p=1
or a discrete
p
-harmonic map with
p\geq 2
.

Now, we suppose
f:(X,m_{E})\to(M,g)
is a minimal geodesic immersion and
f^{p}:(X,m_{E}^{p})\to(M,g)
is the associated
p
-harmonic immersion. Note that
\|T_{e}\|=\|\dot{f}_{e}\|
is constant for each
e\in E
since
f
is a geodesic map. Moreover, by construction of
f^{p}
, we have
\|T_{e}\|=\|\dot{f}_{e}\|=\|\dot{f^{p}_{e}}\|
for any
e
and
\Gamma(f^{-1}TM)=\Gamma((f^{p})^{-1}TM)
. Then, the formula (
15
) shows that

(16)

\displaystyle{\rm Hess}_{f^{p}}^{p}(V,V)

\displaystyle={\rm Hess}_{f}^{1}(V,V)+(p-1)\sum_{e\in E}m_{E}(e)\int_{0}^{1}\|\nabla_{T_{e}}{V}_{e}^{\top_{e}}\|^{2}\|T_{e}\|^{-1}\,dt.

In particular, it holds that

{\rm Hess}_{f^{p}}^{p}(V,V)\geq{\rm Hess}_{f}^{1}(V,V)

for any
V\in\Gamma(f^{-1}TM)
, and the equality holds if and only if
\nabla_{T_{e}}V_{e}^{\top_{e}}\equiv 0
for each
e\in E
. Therefore, if
f
is stable then so is
f^{p}
.
∎

Remark 2.11
.

More generally, if
f:(X,m_{E})\to(M,g)
is a
p^{\prime}
-harmonic immersion, then we obtain a
p
-harmonic immersion
f^{p}:(X,m_{E}^{p})\to(M,g)
by letting
m_{E}^{p}(e):=m_{E}(e)\|T_{e}\|^{p^{\prime}-p}
for any integer
p\geq 2
. Moreover, a similar argument shows that we have
{\rm Hess}_{f^{p}}^{p}(V,V)\geq{\rm Hess}_{f}^{p^{\prime}}(V,V)
if
p\geq p^{\prime}
. Therefore, the stability of
f
implies the stability of
f^{p}
for any
p\geq p^{\prime}
.

Remark 2.12
.

A corresponding argument for smooth harmonic maps was given in
[
27
, Lemma 7.1]
. More precisely, it was proved in
[
27
]
that
if
F:N\to M
is a totally geodesic isometric immersion between smooth compact Riemannian manifolds
N,M
, then
F
is stable as a harmonic map if and only if
F
is stable as a minimal immersion and the identity map
{\rm id}_{N}
is stable
. This fact follows from the following splitting of the Jacobi operator:

(17)

\displaystyle J_{F}(V)=J_{F}^{{min}}(V^{\perp})+J_{{\rm id}_{N}}(V^{\top}).

where
J_{F}
,
J_{{\rm id_{N}}}
and
J_{F}^{min}
are the Jacobi operator of the harmonic map
F
,
{\rm id}_{N}
and the minimal immersion
F
, respectively, and
V^{\top}
(resp.
V^{\perp}
) denotes the tangential (resp. normal) vector field of
V
along
F
. In particular, for any normal vector field
V=V^{\perp}
along
F
, we have
J_{F}^{{min}}(V^{\perp})=J_{F}(V)
, and this implies that, if
F
is a stable harmonic map, then
F
is also stable as a minimal immersion.

We remark that, although the equation (
16
) is an analogous formula of (
17
), we cannot use the same logic for the discrete map
f
since
\{V_{e}^{\perp_{e}}\}_{e\in E}
does not define a “normal vector field along
f
” (i.e. it possibly takes a multivalue at some vertex, and hence, we do not obtain a well-defined element in
\Gamma(f^{-1}TM)
in general. See Remark
2.5
). It would be an interesting problem to ask whether the converse statement of Proposition
2.10
holds or not.

3.
Non-existence of stable discrete maps I

In the following, we consider the following properties of a Riemannian manifold
(M,g)
:

({\bf N}_{1})

There is no stable discrete minimal immersion from a finite weighted graph
(X,m_{E})
into
(M,g)
.

({\bf N}_{p})

There is no non-constant stable discrete
p
-harmonic map from a finite weighted graph
(X,m_{E})
into
(M,g)
.

Note that if
(M,g)
has property
({\bf N}_{1})
(or
({\bf N}_{p})
), then
M
must be simply-connected because there is a stable closed geodesic if
\pi_{1}(M)\neq\{0\}
by the classical result. Moreover, by Proposition
2.10
and Remark
2.11
, it holds that

({\bf N}_{p})\,\Longrightarrow\,({\bf N}_{p^{\prime}})
for any
1\leq p^{\prime}\leq p
.

In this section, we apply the averaging method used in
[
3
,
26
]
to our discrete maps. As a result, we prove the following non-existence theorem.

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
(M,J,g)
be a simply-connected compact homogeneous Kähler manifold of positive holomorphic sectional curvature. Then
(M,g)
has properties
({\bf N}_{1})
and
({\bf N}_{2})
.

Before starting the proof, we prove a lemma which is easy to see, but will be a key in our proof.

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
f:(X,m_{E})\to(M,g)
be a discrete map satisfying the
2
-balanced condition (not necessarily a critical point of
{\bf E}^{2}
). If
V
is a Killing vector field on
M
, then
f^{-1}{V}\in\Gamma_{bal}(f^{-1}TM)
.

Proof.

We fix an arbitrary vertex
x
, and take any tangent vector
U\in T_{x}M
.
Since
{V}
is a Killing vector field on
M
, we have
g(\nabla_{X}{V},Y)+g(\nabla_{Y}{V},X)=0
for any
X,Y\in\Gamma(TM)
. Thus, we see

\displaystyle g\Big(\sum_{e\in E_{x}}m_{E}(e)\nabla_{T_{e}}{V},U\Big)_{x}

\displaystyle=-\sum_{e\in E_{x}}m_{E}(e)g(\nabla_{U}{V},T_{e})_{x}

\displaystyle=-g\Big(\nabla_{U}{V},\sum_{e\in E_{x}}m_{E}(e)T_{e}\Big)_{x}=0

due to the
2
-balanced condition, where we used the fact that
\nabla_{U}V(x)
does not depend on
e\in E_{x}
. Since
U
is arbitrary, this shows
f^{-1}{V}\in\Gamma_{bal}(f^{-1}TM)
.
∎

Next, we recall a basic fact on a simply-connected compact homogeneous Kähler manifold
M
. It is well-known that
M
is isomorphic to an adjoint orbit of a connected compact Lie group
G
, equipped with the canonical complex structure
J
and a
G
-invariant Kähler metric
g
on the orbit (see
[
2
, Theorem 8.89]
). Thus, we assume
M
is an adjoint orbit of
G
in the following. We will use a property of the canonical complex structure
J
, and thus, we briefly recall the definition of
J
. We refer to
[
2
, Section 8.B]
for details.

We denote the Lie algebra of
G
by
\mathfrak{g}
and fix an
{\rm Ad}(G)
-invariant inner product
\langle,\rangle_{\mathfrak{g}}
on
\mathfrak{g}
.
Let
M
be an adjoint orbit in
\mathfrak{g}
, and take an arbitrary point
w\in M\subset\mathfrak{g}
so that
M={\rm Ad}(G)w=\{{\rm Ad}(g)w\mid g\in G\}
. We always assume
w\neq 0
. We denote the isotropy subgroup of
G
at
w
by
K_{w}
and write its Lie algebra by
\mathfrak{k}_{w}
. Let
\mathfrak{m}_{w}
be the orthogonal complement of
\mathfrak{k}_{w}
in
\mathfrak{g}
. Note that
\mathfrak{m}_{w}
is identified with the tangent space
T_{w}M
via the correspondence
X\mapsto X^{*}_{w}=\frac{d}{dt}{\rm Ad}({\rm exp}(tX))w|_{t=0}
.

Let
S_{w}
be the identity component of the center of
K_{w}
. We denote the Lie algebra of
S_{w}
by
\mathfrak{s}_{w}
. It is easy to see that
w\in\mathfrak{s}_{w}
, and hence, we may assume
S_{w}
is non-trivial. Then, we obtain an orthogonal decomposition (see
[
2
, (8.32)]
)

\mathfrak{m}_{w}=\bigoplus_{\alpha\in\Sigma_{+}}\mathfrak{m}_{w,\alpha},\quad{\rm where}\ \mathfrak{m}_{w,\alpha}=\{X\in\mathfrak{m}_{w}\mid{\rm ad}(Z)^{2}X=-\alpha(Z)^{2}X,\ \forall Z\in\mathfrak{s}_{w}\},

where
\alpha
is a non-zero element in
\mathfrak{s}_{w}^{*}
satisfying that
\alpha(w)>0
and
\mathfrak{m}_{w,\alpha}\neq\{0\}
, and we denote the set of such
1
-forms by
\Sigma_{+}
. Using this decomposition, the canonical almost complex structure
J_{w}
on
\mathfrak{m}_{w}
is defined by

(18)

\displaystyle J_{w}X_{\alpha}=\frac{1}{\alpha(w)}[w,X_{\alpha}]\quad{\rm if}\ X_{\alpha}\in\mathfrak{m}_{w,\alpha}.

Note that each
\mathfrak{m}_{w,\alpha}
is a
J_{w}
-invariant subspace.
It then turns out that the canonical almost complex structure
J
on
M
is
G
-invariant and integrable (see
[
2
]
for details).

For each
w\in M
, we extend
J_{w}
to
\mathfrak{g}
by letting
J_{w}Z=0
for any
Z\in\mathfrak{k}_{w}
so that
J_{w}:\mathfrak{g}\to\mathfrak{g}
defines an endomorphism on
\mathfrak{g}
.
We use the following fact which was stated in
[
3
]
without proof.

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
\langle,\rangle_{\mathfrak{g}}
be an arbitrary
{\rm Ad}(G)
-invariant inner product on
\mathfrak{g}
. Then, for every
w\in M
,
J_{w}
is skew-symmetric with respect to
\langle,\rangle_{\mathfrak{g}}
.

Proof.

Since
\langle,\rangle_{\mathfrak{g}}
is
{\rm Ad}(G)
-invariant, we have
\langle[w,X],Y\rangle_{\mathfrak{g}}+\langle X,[w,Y]\rangle_{\mathfrak{g}}=0
for any
X,Y\in\mathfrak{g}
. Thus, (
18
) implies that
J_{w}
is skew-symmetric w.r.t.
\langle,\rangle_{\mathfrak{g}}
.
∎

Let
g
be a
G
-invariant Kähler metric on
M
compatible with the canonical complex structure
J
. Such a metric always exists, in fact, there exists a unique (up to homothety) Kähler-Einstein metric on
M
(see
[
2
]
). However,
g
is not necessarily Einstein in the following. Also, any
{\rm Ad}(G)
-invariant inner product
\langle,\rangle_{\mathfrak{g}}
defines a
G
-invariant metric
g_{0}
on
M
since the
{\rm Ad}(G)
-invariant inner product
\langle,\rangle_{\mathfrak{g}}
defines a
{\rm Ad}_{G}(K_{w})
-invariant inner product on
\mathfrak{m}_{w}
. Nevertheless, we emphasize that the
G
-invariant metric
g
is not necessarily
g_{0}
in our argument.

Now, we give a proof of Theorem
3.1
.

Proof of Theorem
3.1
.

By Proposition
2.10
, it is sufficient to show the instability of any non-constant discrete 2-harmonic map. Thus, we suppose
f:(X,m_{E})\to(M,g)
is a discrete
2
-harmonic map. For simplicity, we abbreviate the integer
p
in this proof. For example,
{\rm Hess}_{f}
means
{\rm Hess}_{f}^{2}
, etc.

Let
(M,J,g)
be a simply-connected homogeneous Kähler manifold of positive holomorphic sectional curvature. We identify
M
with an adjoint of orbit of a compact Lie group
G
as described above. Since
G
acts on
M
isometrically, any element
V\in\mathfrak{g}
gives rise to a Killing vector field
V^{*}
on
M
which is defined by

V^{*}_{w}:=\frac{d}{dt}{\rm Ad}(\exp(tV))w|_{t=0}

at each
w\in M
.
We consider a deformation of
f
generated by
f^{-1}(JV^{*})\in\Gamma(f^{-1}TM)
. We denote
f^{-1}(JV^{*})
simply by
JV^{*}
, and define a quadratic form
q:\mathfrak{g}\to\mathbb{R}
by

q(V):={\rm Hess}_{f}(JV^{*},JV^{*}).

We shall compute the trace of
q
with respect to an
{\rm Ad}(G)
-invariant inner product
\langle,\rangle_{\mathfrak{g}}
.

Since
V^{*}
is a Killing vector field, we have
f^{-1}V^{*}\in\Gamma_{bal}(f^{-1}TM)
by Lemma
3.2
, and moreover, the Kähler condition
\nabla J=0
implies that
f^{-1}(JV^{*})\in\Gamma_{bal}(f^{-1}TM)
(see (
14
)). Therefore, by Lemma
2.8
, the Hessian becomes the following simple form:

(19)

\displaystyle{\rm Hess}_{f}(JV^{*},W)=\sum_{e\in E}m_{E}(e)\int_{0}^{1}g(\mathcal{J}_{f,e}(JV_{e}^{*}),W_{e})\,dt.

for any
W\in\Gamma(f^{-1}TM)
.

It is known that any Killing vector field
X
satisfies that
(\nabla^{2}X)(Z,W)+R(X,Z)W=0
for any
Z,W\in\Gamma(TM)
. Since
f
is a geodesic map (i.e.
\nabla_{T_{e}}T_{e}=0
) and
\nabla J=0
, we see

\displaystyle\nabla_{T_{e}}\nabla_{T_{e}}JV^{*}_{e}

\displaystyle=J\nabla_{T_{e}}\nabla_{T_{e}}V^{*}_{e}=J(\nabla^{2}V^{*})(T_{e},T_{e})=-JR(V^{*}_{e},T_{e})T_{e}.

Therefore, we obtain

(20)

\displaystyle\mathcal{J}_{f,e}(JV_{e}^{*})=JR(V_{e}^{*},T_{e})T_{e}-R(JV_{e}^{*},T_{e})T_{e},

and hence,

\displaystyle q(V)=\sum_{e\in E}m_{E}(e)\int_{0}^{1}q_{w}(V)\,dt,

\displaystyle{\rm where}

\displaystyle\ q_{w}(V):=g(R(V_{e}^{*},T_{e})T_{e},V_{e}^{*})_{w}-g(R({JV_{e}^{*}},T_{e})T_{e},JV_{e}^{*})_{w}

for
w\in f(e)
.

Take an arbitrary
{\rm Ad}(G)
-invariant inner product
\langle,\rangle_{\mathfrak{g}}
on
\mathfrak{g}
. We shall compute the trace of
q_{w}
for each
w
. Since
J_{w}
is skew-symmetric w.r.t.
\langle,\rangle_{\mathfrak{g}}
by Lemma
3.3
, we can take an orthonormal basis of
\mathfrak{g}
of the form

\{Z_{1},\ldots,Z_{k},X_{1},\ldots,X_{n},J_{w}X_{1},\ldots,J_{w}X_{n}\},

where
\{Z_{1},\ldots,Z_{k}\}
is a basis of
\mathfrak{k}_{w}
and
\{X_{1},\ldots,X_{n},J_{w}X_{1},\ldots,J_{w}X_{n}\}
is a basis of
\mathfrak{m}_{w}
. Note that this choice of basis depends on
w\in M
. By definition, we have
(Z_{i})^{*}_{w}=0
for any
i=1,\ldots,k
, and
(J_{w}X_{i})_{w}^{*}=J_{w}(X_{i})_{w}^{*}
for any
i=1,\ldots,n
under the natural identification
\mathfrak{m}_{w}\simeq T_{w}M
. Therefore, by using this orthonormal basis of
\mathfrak{g}
, it is easy to see that

{\rm Tr}\,q_{w}=\sum_{i=1}^{k}q_{w}(Z_{i})+\sum_{i=1}^{n}\{q_{w}(X_{i})+q_{w}(J_{w}X_{i})\}=0.

Therefore, the trace of
q
over
\mathfrak{g}
is also equal to
0
.

Now, we suppose that
f
is stable. Since
{\rm Tr}\ q=0
, the stability implies that
{\rm Hess}_{f}(JV^{*},JV^{*})=0
for any
V\in\mathfrak{g}
. Moreover,
{\rm Hess}_{f}
defines a positive semi-definite inner product on
\Gamma(f^{-1}TM)
, and therefore, the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality shows that

{\rm Hess}_{f}(JV^{*},W)^{2}\leq{\rm Hess}_{f}(JV^{*},JV^{*}){\rm Hess}_{f}(W,W)=0,

and hence, we obtain

{\rm Hess}_{f}(JV^{*},W)=0

for any
V\in\mathfrak{g}
and
W\in\Gamma(f^{-1}TM)
. Since
{\rm Hess}_{f}
is given by (
19
), by taking
W
whose support is contained in an edge
e\in E
, we obtain
\mathcal{J}_{f,e}(JV_{e}^{*})=0
. By (
20
), this is equivalent to

(21)

\displaystyle JR(V_{e}^{*},T_{e})T_{e}-R(JV_{e}^{*},T_{e})T_{e}=0

for each edge
e\in E
and for any
V\in\mathfrak{g}
.

Fix an arbitrary point
w\in f(e)
. Then, we can take
V
so that
V^{*}(w)=T_{e}(w)
. Then, (
21
) shows that

R(JT_{e},T_{e})T_{e}(w)=0.

Therefore, if
T_{e}(w)\neq 0
, this implies the holomorphic sectional curvature of the complex plane spanned by
\{T_{e}(w),JT_{e}(w)\}
is equal to
0
. This contradicts to the assumption that
M
has positive holomorphic sectional curvature. Thus, it must hold that
T_{e}(w)=0
for any
w\in f(e)
and any
e\in E
, and hence,
f
is a constant map.
∎

Remark 3.4
.

This method is not valid for
p\geq 3
. Indeed, if we put
{q}^{p}(V):={\rm Hess}_{f}^{p}(JV^{*},JV^{*})
for
V\in\mathfrak{g}
, then we have
{\rm Tr}q^{p}\geq 0
if
p\geq 3
, and possibly
{\rm Tr}q^{p}>0
.

4.
Non-existence of stable discrete maps II

In this section, we shall apply another averaging method used in
[
10
,
17
,
24
]
, and show the non-existence of stable maps in some compact symmetric spaces.

4.1.
Criteria for the non-existence

Let
(M^{n},g)
be an
n
-dimensional Riemannian manifold.
Suppose that
(M^{n},g)
is isometrically immersed into the Euclidean space
\mathbb{R}^{n+k}
by a map
F:M^{n}\to\mathbb{R}^{n+k}
. For each
w\in M
we denote the orthogonal projection onto the tangent (resp. normal) space of
F
at
w
by
(\top_{F})_{w}:T_{w}\mathbb{R}^{n+k}\to T_{w}M
(resp.
(\perp_{F})_{w}:T_{w}\mathbb{R}^{n+k}\to T_{w}^{\perp}M
). Then, the second fundamental form
B
and the shape operator
A
of
F
is defined by
B(Y,Z):=(\overline{\nabla}_{Y}Z)^{\perp_{F}}
and
A_{\xi}(Z):=-(\overline{\nabla}_{Z}\xi)^{\top_{F}}
for
Y,Z\in T_{w}M
and
\xi\in T^{\perp}_{w}M
. Note that
B
is the symmetric tensor satisfying that
g(B(Y,Z),\xi)=g(A_{\xi}(Y),Z)
.

For a non-zero vector
v\in\mathbb{R}^{n+k}
, we take the parallel vector field
v
on
\mathbb{R}^{n+k}
, that is, a vector field on
\mathbb{R}^{n+k}
satisfying that
\overline{\nabla}v=0
. Then, we obtain smooth vector fields
v^{\top_{F}}
and
v^{\perp_{F}}
along
F
. The following properties are useful.

Lemma 4.1
.

For any
v\in\mathbb{R}^{n+k}\setminus\{0\}
and
Z\in T_{w}M
, we have

(22)

\displaystyle\nabla_{Z}v^{\top_{F}}

\displaystyle=A_{v^{\perp_{F}}}Z,

(23)

\displaystyle\nabla^{\perp}_{Z}v^{\perp_{F}}

\displaystyle=-B(Z,v^{\top_{F}}),

where
\nabla^{\perp}
denote the normal connection of
F
.

Proof.

Since
\overline{\nabla}v=0
, we see

\nabla_{Z}v^{\top_{F}}=(\overline{\nabla}_{Z}v^{\top_{F}})^{\top_{F}}=(\overline{\nabla}_{Z}v-\overline{\nabla}_{Z}v^{\perp_{F}})^{\top_{F}}=A_{v^{\perp_{F}}}Z.

This proves (
22
). Moreover, by definition of normal connection, we see

\displaystyle\nabla^{\perp}_{Z}v^{\perp_{F}}

\displaystyle=\overline{\nabla}_{Z}v^{\perp_{F}}+A_{v^{\perp_{F}}}(Z)

\displaystyle=\overline{\nabla}_{Z}(v-v^{\top_{F}})+\nabla_{Z}v^{\top_{F}}

\displaystyle=-\overline{\nabla}_{Z}v^{\top_{F}}+\nabla_{Z}v^{\top_{F}}=-B(Z,v^{\top_{F}}).

This proves (
23
).
∎

Remark 4.2
.

By (
22
), if a discrete map
f:(X,m_{E})\to(M,g)
satisfies the
2
-balanced condition, then we have

(F\circ f)^{-1}v^{\top_{F}}\in\Gamma_{bal}(f^{-1}TM).

Indeed, for any vertex
x
, the balanced condition shows that

\displaystyle\sum_{e\in E}m_{E}(e)\nabla_{T_{e}}v^{\top_{F}}_{e}(x)

\displaystyle=\sum_{e\in E}m_{E}(e)A_{v^{\perp_{F}}}(T_{e})_{x}=A_{v^{\perp_{F}}}\Big(\sum_{e\in E}m_{E}(e)T_{e}(x)\Big)=0

since
A_{v^{\perp_{F}}}
depends only on
x
.

Let
f:(X,m_{E})\to(M,g)
be either a minimal immersion with
p=1
or a
p
-harmonic map with
p\geq 2
.
We shall consider the vector field
(F\circ f)^{-1}v^{\top_{F}}\in\Gamma(f^{-1}TM)
, and will be denoted by the same symbol
v^{\top_{F}}
if there is no confusion. Then, we define a quadratic form on
\mathbb{R}^{n+k}
by

(24)

\displaystyle q_{F}^{p}(v):={\rm Hess}_{f}^{p}(v^{\top_{F}},v^{\top_{F}}),

and we shall consider the trace of
q_{F}^{p}
over
\mathbb{R}^{n+k}
. The following trace formula is an extension of the results due to Howard-Wei
[
10
]
and Ohnita
[
24
]
.

Theorem 4.3
.

Let
(M,g)
be an
n
-dimensional smooth Riemannian manifold and
f:(X,m_{E})\to(M^{n},g)
be either a discrete minimal immersion with
p=1
or a discrete
p
-harmonic map with
p\geq 2
. Suppose that
(M,g)
is isometrically immersed into
\mathbb{R}^{n+k}
by a map
F:M\to\mathbb{R}^{n+k}
.

We define a tensor field on
M
by

Q_{F}(X,Y):=g(H,B(X,Y))-2{\rm Ric}(X,Y),

where
B
is the second fundamental form of
F
,
H
is the mean curvature vector of
F
and
{\rm Ric}
is the Ricci tensor of
(M,g)
. Then, we have

(25)

\displaystyle{\rm Tr}\,q_{F}^{p}=\sum_{e\in E\setminus E_{deg}}m_{E}(e)\int_{0}^{1}\{Q_{F}(T_{e},T_{e})+(p-2)\|B(\widetilde{T}_{e},\widetilde{T}_{e})\|^{2}\|T_{e}\|^{2}\}\|T_{e}\|^{p-2}\,dt,

where
\widetilde{T}_{e}:=T_{e}/\|T_{e}\|
and
E_{deg}
denotes the set of degenerate edges.

Proof.

Note that if
p\geq 2
,
f
could admit a degenerate edge. However,
e\in E_{deg}
if and only if
f_{e}
is a constant map, or equivalently,
T_{e}\equiv 0
along
f_{e}
since
f
is a geodesic map if
p\geq 2
. Thus, the degenerate edge does not affect the Hessian, and hence, we assume
e\in E\setminus E_{deg}
(or equivalently
\|T_{e}\|\neq 0
) in the following.

We use the expression (
4
) of the second variation.
We put

\displaystyle q_{F,w}^{p}(v):=\|\nabla_{T_{e}}v^{\top_{F}}_{e}\|_{w}^{2}+(p-2)\|(\nabla_{T_{e}}v_{e}^{\top_{F}})^{\top_{e}}\|_{w}^{2}-g(R(v^{\top_{F}}_{e},T_{e})T_{e},v^{\top_{F}}_{e})_{w}.

for each
w\in f(e)
so that

\displaystyle q_{F}^{p}(v)=\sum_{e\in E\setminus E_{deg}}m_{E}(e)\int_{0}^{1}q_{F,w}^{p}(v)\|T_{e}\|_{w}^{p-2}\,dt.

We fix arbitrary point
w\in f(e)
, and take an orthonormal basis
\{\mu^{1},\ldots,\mu^{n},\nu^{n+1},\ldots,\nu^{n+k}\}
of
T_{w}\mathbb{R}^{n+k}
satisfying that
\{\mu^{i}\}_{i=1}^{n}
is a basis of
T_{w}M
and
\{\nu^{\alpha}\}_{\alpha=n+1}^{n+k}
is a basis of the normal space
T_{w}^{\perp}M
in
\mathbb{R}^{n+k}
. We regard
\mu^{i}
(
i=1,\ldots,n
) and
\nu^{\alpha}
(
\alpha=n+1,\ldots,n+k)
as parallel vector fields on
\mathbb{R}^{n+k}
. Then, by (
22
), we have
\nabla_{T_{e}}(\mu^{i})_{e}^{\top_{F}}(w)=A_{(\mu^{i})^{\perp_{F}}}T_{e}(w)=0
for each
i=1,\ldots,n
, and hence, we obtain

\sum_{i=1}^{n}q_{F,w}^{p}(\mu^{i})=-\sum_{i=1}^{n}g(R(\mu^{i},T_{e})T_{e},\mu^{i})_{w}=-{\rm Ric}(T_{e},T_{e})_{w}.

On the other hand, since
\nabla_{T_{e}}(\nu^{\alpha})_{e}^{\top_{F}}(w)=A_{\nu^{\alpha}}T_{e}(w)
by (
22
), we have

\displaystyle\sum_{\alpha=n+1}^{n+k}q_{F,w}^{p}(\nu^{\alpha})

\displaystyle=\sum_{\alpha=n+1}^{n+k}\|A_{\nu^{\alpha}}T_{e}\|^{2}_{w}+(p-2)\|(A_{\nu^{\alpha}}T_{e})^{\top_{e}}\|^{2}_{w}.

Here, the Gauss equation for the isometric immersion
F:M\to\mathbb{R}^{n+k}
shows that

\displaystyle\sum_{\alpha=n+1}^{n+k}\|A_{\nu^{\alpha}}T_{e}\|^{2}_{w}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\alpha=n+1}^{n+k}\sum_{i=1}^{n}g(A_{\nu^{\alpha}}T_{e},\mu^{i})_{w}^{2}=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\|B(T_{e},\mu^{i})\|^{2}_{w}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\Big\{g(B(\mu^{i},\mu^{i}),B(T_{e},T_{e}))_{w}-g(R(T_{e},\mu^{i})\mu^{i},T_{e})_{w}\Big\}

\displaystyle=g(H_{e},B(T_{e},T_{e}))_{w}-{\rm Ric}(T_{e},T_{e})_{w}.

Moreover, by letting
\widetilde{T}_{e}=T_{e}/\|T_{e}\|
, we see

\displaystyle\sum_{\alpha=n+1}^{n+k}\|(A_{\nu^{\alpha}}T_{e})^{\top_{e}}\|^{2}_{w}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\alpha=n+1}^{n+k}g(A_{\nu^{\alpha}}T_{e},\widetilde{T}_{e})_{w}^{2}=\sum_{\alpha=n+1}^{n+k}g(B(\widetilde{T}_{e},\widetilde{T}_{e}),\nu^{\alpha})_{w}^{2}\cdot\|T_{e}\|_{w}^{2}=\|B(\widetilde{T}_{e},\widetilde{T}_{e})\|_{w}^{2}\cdot\|T_{e}\|_{w}^{2}.

Therefore, we obtain

\displaystyle{\rm Tr}\,q_{F}^{p}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{n}q_{F,w}^{p}(\mu^{i})+\sum_{\alpha=n+1}^{n+k}q_{F,w}^{p}(\nu^{\alpha})

\displaystyle=g(H_{e},B(T_{e},T_{e}))_{w}-2{\rm Ric}(T_{e},T_{e})_{w}+(p-2)\|B(\widetilde{T}_{e},\widetilde{T}_{e})\|_{w}^{2}\|T_{e}\|_{w}^{2}.

This proves the formula.
∎

If the map
f
satisfies
{\rm Tr}\,q_{F}^{p}<0
, then
f
must be unstable. By the trace formula (
25
), we obtain the following sufficient condition to be
{\rm Tr}\,q_{F}^{p}<0
(resp.
{\rm Tr}\,q_{F}^{1}<0
) for any discrete
p
-harmonic map (resp. discrete minimal immersion):
For the isometric immersion
F:M\to\mathbb{R}^{n+k}
, we set

\mathcal{S}_{F}:=\{\|B(Z,Z)\|_{w}^{2}\mid w\in M,\,Z\in T_{w}M\,{\rm with}\,\|Z\|=1\}
and set

\displaystyle\beta_{F}:={\rm inf}\mathcal{S}_{F},\quad\gamma_{F}:={\rm sup}\mathcal{S}_{F}.

Corollary 4.4
.

Let
F:(M,g)\to\mathbb{R}^{n+k}
be an isometric immersion. Then,

(i)

If
Q_{F}<\beta_{F}g
, then
(M,g)
has property
({\bf N}_{1})
.

(ii)

If
Q_{F}<-(p-2)\gamma_{F}g
, then
(M,g)
has property
({\bf N}_{p})
.

For example, if
F=F_{S}:S^{n}(1)\to\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
is the natural embedding of the unit sphere
S^{n}(1)
equipped with the standard metric
g_{S}
, then we easily see that

Q_{F_{S}}=-(n-2)g

since
B(\cdot,\cdot)=-g(\cdot,\cdot)\nu
for the outer unit normal vector
\nu
of
S^{n}(1)
and
{\rm Ric}=(n-1)g
. On the other hand, we have
\beta_{F_{S}}=\gamma_{F_{S}}=1
. Thus, Corollary
4.4
shows that the standard sphere
(S^{n},g_{S})
has property
({\bf N}_{p})
if
n\geq p+1
. In particular, the standard
n
-sphere of
n\geq 2
has property
({\bf N}_{1})
. Note that when
n=2
, the property
({\bf N}_{2})
also holds since
S^{2}
is isometric to
\mathbb{C}P^{1}
and
\mathbb{C}P^{1}
has property
({\bf N}_{2})
by Theorem
3.1
. We remark that this latter fact gives a negative answer for the question posed by Colin de Verdière
[
5
, p.203]
.

Next, we shall deal with the case when
{\rm Tr}\,q_{F}^{p}=0
. In this special case, we obtain the following obstructions for stable discrete maps:

Lemma 4.5
.

Suppose
(M,g)
is isometrically immersed into
\mathbb{R}^{n+k}
by the immersion
F:M\to\mathbb{R}^{n+k}
.

(i)

If
f:(X,m_{E})\to(M,g)
is a stable discrete minimal geodesic immersion with
{\rm Tr}\,q_{F}^{1}=0
, then it holds that

K(N\wedge\widetilde{T}_{e})_{w}=\|B(N,\widetilde{T}_{e})\|_{w}^{2}

for any
w\in f(e)
,
e\in E
and any unit vector
N\in T_{w}M
orthogonal to
T_{e}
, where
K(X\wedge Y)
denotes the sectional curvature of the plane spanned by
X,Y
in
(M,g)
.

(ii)

If
f:(X,m_{E})\to(M,g)
is a non-constant stable discrete
p
-harmonic map with
{\rm Tr}\,q_{F}^{p}=0
, then
K(N\wedge\widetilde{T}_{e})_{w}=0
for any
w\in f(e)
,
e\in E\setminus E_{deg}
and any unit vector
N\in T_{w}M
orthogonal to
T_{e}
. Moreover,
F\circ f:(X,m_{E})\to\mathbb{R}^{n+k}
is also a
p
-harmonic map.

Proof.

Suppose
{\rm Tr}\,q^{p}_{F}=0
and
f
is stable. Then we have
q_{F}^{p}(v)={\rm Hess}_{f}^{p}(v^{\top_{F}},v^{\top_{F}})=0
for any
v\in\mathbb{R}^{n+k}
, otherwise there exists
v\in\mathbb{R}^{n+k}
so that
{\rm Hess}_{f}^{p}(v^{\top_{F}},v^{\top_{F}})<0
. Moreover, since
{\rm Hess}_{f}^{p}
is positive semi-definite on
\Gamma(f^{-1}TM)
, the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality implies that

{\rm Hess}_{f}^{p}(v^{\top_{F}},W)=0
for any
v\in\mathbb{R}^{n+k}
and
W\in\Gamma(f^{-1}TM)
.
Thus, the Hessian formula (
10
) shows that

\displaystyle 0={\rm Hess}_{f}^{p}(v^{\top_{F}},W)=\sum_{e\in E}m_{E}(e)\int_{0}^{1}g(\mathcal{J}_{f,e}^{p}(v^{\top_{F}}_{e}),W_{e})\,dt+2\sum_{x\in V}\sum_{e\in E_{x}}m_{E}(e)g(\mathcal{B}_{f,e}^{p}(v^{\top_{F}}_{e}),W_{e})_{x}.

Since
W
is arbitrary, by taking
W
whose support is contained in
e
, we obtain that
\mathcal{J}_{f,e}^{p}(v^{\top_{F}}_{e})=0
along each
e
. In particular, for any non-degenerate edge
e
, we have

(26)

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle=-\nabla_{T_{e}}\nabla_{T_{e}}v^{\top_{F}}_{e}-(p-2)(\nabla_{T_{e}}\nabla_{T_{e}}v^{\top_{F}}_{e})^{\top_{e}}-R(v^{\top_{F}}_{e},T_{e})T_{e}

\displaystyle=-(\nabla_{T_{e}}\nabla_{T_{e}}v^{\top_{F}}_{e})^{\perp_{e}}-(p-1)(\nabla_{T_{e}}\nabla_{T_{e}}v^{\top_{F}}_{e})^{\top_{e}}-R(v^{\top_{F}}_{e},T_{e})T_{e}.

Here, by using (
22
), (
23
) and the assumption that
f
is a geodesic map, we see

(27)

\displaystyle\nabla_{T_{e}}\nabla_{T_{e}}v^{\top_{F}}_{e}

\displaystyle=\nabla_{T_{e}}(A_{v^{\perp_{F}}}({T}_{e}))

\displaystyle=(\nabla_{{T}_{e}}A)_{v^{\perp_{F}}}({T}_{e})+A_{v^{\perp_{F}}}(\nabla_{{T}_{e}}{T}_{e})+A_{\nabla^{\perp}_{{T}_{e}}v^{\perp_{F}}}({T}_{e})

\displaystyle=(\nabla_{T_{e}}A)_{v^{\perp_{F}}}(T_{e})-A_{B(T_{e},v^{\top_{F}})}(T_{e}).

Notice that the last equation is tensorial. Thus, we fix an arbitrary point
w\in f(e)
, and for any unit tangent vector
N\in T_{w}M
orthogonal to
T_{e}
, we take
v\in\mathbb{R}^{n+k}
so that
v(w)=N
. Then we have
v^{\top_{F}}(w)=N
and
v^{\perp_{F}}(w)=0
, and by (
26
) and (
27
), we see

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle=g\Big(-(\nabla_{T_{e}}\nabla_{T_{e}}v^{\top_{F}}_{e})^{\perp_{e}}-R(v^{\top_{F}}_{e},T_{e})T_{e},N\Big)_{w}

\displaystyle=g\Big(A_{B({T}_{e},N)}({T}_{e})-R(N,{T}_{e}){T}_{e},N\Big)_{w}=\{\|B(\widetilde{T}_{e},N)\|^{2}_{w}-K(N\wedge\widetilde{T}_{e})_{w}\}\cdot\|T_{e}\|^{2}_{w}

for any unit vector
N\in T_{w}M
orthogonal to
T_{e}
and
w\in f(e)
. Therefore, we obtain

(28)

\displaystyle K(N\wedge\widetilde{T}_{e})_{w}=\|B(\widetilde{T}_{e},N)\|^{2}_{w}

if
e\in E\setminus E_{deg}
. Moreover if
p=1
, this proves (i).

Next, we consider the case when
p\geq 2
. In this case, by taking the inner product of (
26
) with
T_{e}
, we have

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle=(p-1)g(\nabla_{T_{e}}\nabla_{T_{e}}v^{\top_{F}}_{e},T_{e})_{w}

\displaystyle=(p-1)\{g((\nabla_{T_{e}}A)_{v^{\perp_{F}}}(T_{e}),T_{e})_{w}-g(B(T_{e},v^{\top_{F}}),B(T_{e},T_{e}))_{w}\}

by (
27
). Thus, by taking
v\in\mathbb{R}^{n+k}
so that
v(w)=T_{e}(w)
for a fixed
w\in f(e)
, we obtain

\|B(T_{e},T_{e})\|_{w}^{2}=0.

Since
w
is arbitrary, this shows
B(T_{e},T_{e})\equiv 0
along each
e
.
Because
f_{e}:[0,1]\to(M,g)
is a geodesic map, this implies that
F\circ f_{e}:[0,1]\to\mathbb{R}^{n+k}
is also a geodesic map. Since the
p
-balanced condition still holds,
F\circ f
is a
p
-harmonic map into
\mathbb{R}^{n+k}
. Moreover, the Gauss equation for the isometric immersion
F:M\to\mathbb{R}^{n+k}
shows that

(29)

\displaystyle 0=K(N\wedge\widetilde{T}_{e})-g(B(\widetilde{T}_{e},\widetilde{T}_{e}),B(N,N))+\|B(\widetilde{T}_{e},N)\|^{2}=2K(N\wedge\widetilde{T}_{e}),

where we used (
28
) and
B(\widetilde{T}_{e},\widetilde{T}_{e})=0
. Therefore,
K(N\wedge\widetilde{T}_{e})=0
for any
e\in E\setminus E_{deg}
. This proves (ii).
∎

4.2.
The case of minimal immersion into the sphere

If
M
is minimally immersed into the hypersphere
S^{n+k-1}(r)
in
\mathbb{R}^{n+k}
, the above results are refined as follows:
Let
F:M^{n}\to\mathbb{R}^{n+k}
be an isometric immersion and suppose
M
is minimally immersed into
S^{n+k-1}(r)
. We denote the minimal immersion by
\widetilde{F}:M\to S^{n+k-1}(r)
, and write the second fundamental form of
\widetilde{F}
by
B_{\widetilde{F}}
. Then,
B=B_{F}
and
B_{\widetilde{F}}
are related by

(30)

\displaystyle B_{F}(X,Y)=B_{\widetilde{F}}(X,Y)-\frac{1}{r}g(X,Y)\nu

for
X,Y\in\Gamma(TM)
, where
\nu
is the outer unit normal vector of
S^{n+k-1}(r)
in
\mathbb{R}^{n+k}
.
Since
\widetilde{F}
is a minimal immersion, the relation (
30
) shows that the mean curvature
H
of
F:M\to\mathbb{R}^{n+k}
is given by
H=-(n/r)\nu
, and hence, we obtain

(31)

\displaystyle Q_{F}=\frac{n}{r^{2}}g-2{\rm Ric}.

In particular, Corollary
4.4
and Lemma
4.5
implies the following criteria for the property
{({\bf N}_{1})}
or
{({\bf N}_{2})}
.

Proposition 4.6
.

Suppose
\widetilde{F}:(M,g)\to S^{n+k-1}(r)
is a minimal immersion.

(i)

If
(M,g)
satisfies one of the following two conditions, then
(M,g)
has property
{({\bf N}_{1})}
.

(a)

\displaystyle{\rm Ric}>\frac{1}{2}\Big(\frac{n-1}{r^{2}}-\beta_{\widetilde{F}}\Big)g
, where
\beta_{\widetilde{F}}:={\rm inf}\{\|B_{\widetilde{F}}(Z,Z)\|_{w}^{2}\mid w\in M,\,Z\in T_{w}M\,{\rm with}\,\|Z\|=1\}
.

(b)

{\rm Ric}=((n-1)/2r^{2})g
and for any
w\in M
and any unit tangent vector
Y\in T_{w}M
, there exists a unit tangent vector
Z\in T_{w}M
orthogonal to
Y
such that
K(Y\wedge Z)=0
.

(ii)

If
(M,g)
satisfies
{\rm Ric}\geq(n/2r^{2})g
, then
(M,g)
has property
({\bf N}_{2})
.

Proof.

(i) By Corollary
4.4
,
(M,g)
has property
{({\bf N}_{1})}
if

(32)

\displaystyle Q_{F}<\beta_{F}g\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad{\rm Ric}>\frac{1}{2}\Big(\frac{n}{r^{2}}-\beta_{F}\Big)g=\frac{1}{2}\Big(\frac{n-1}{r^{2}}-\beta_{\widetilde{F}}\Big)g.

where we used the fact
\beta_{F}=\beta_{\widetilde{F}}+(1/r^{2})
.
Thus, the condition (a) provides a sufficient condition for the property
{({\bf N}_{1})}
.

Suppose the condition (b) holds. In this case, we have
Q_{F}=\frac{1}{r^{2}}g
by (
31
), and hence, the trace formula (
25
) for the map
F:M\to\mathbb{R}^{n+k}
and (
30
) show that

\displaystyle{\rm Tr}\,q_{F}^{1}

\displaystyle=\sum_{e\in E}m_{E}(e)\int_{0}^{1}-\|B_{\widetilde{F}}(\widetilde{T}_{e},\widetilde{T}_{e})\|^{2}\cdot\|T_{e}\|\,dt,

where
B_{\widetilde{F}}
is the second fundamental form of
\widetilde{F}:M\to S^{n+k-1}(r)
.
Thus, if the discrete minimal immersion
f:X\to M
were stable, it holds that

(33)

{\rm Tr}\,q_{F}^{1}=0
and

B_{\widetilde{F}}(\widetilde{T}_{e},\widetilde{T}_{e})=0
along each
e\in E
.

We may assume
f
is a minimal geodesic immersion. Then, Lemma
4.5
(i) implies that

(34)

\displaystyle K(N\wedge\widetilde{T}_{e})=\|B_{F}(N,\widetilde{T}_{e})\|^{2}=\|B_{\widetilde{F}}(N,\widetilde{T}_{e})\|^{2},

for any unit tangent vector
N\in T_{w}M
orthogonal to
T_{e}(w)
.

By (
33
) and (
34
), the Gauss equation for
\widetilde{F}:M\to S^{n+k-1}(r)
becomes

\displaystyle K_{S}(N\wedge\widetilde{T}_{e})

\displaystyle=K(N\wedge\widetilde{T}_{e})-g(B_{\widetilde{F}}(\widetilde{T}_{e},\widetilde{T}_{e}),B_{\widetilde{F}}(N,N))+\|B_{\widetilde{F}}(N,\widetilde{T}_{e})\|^{2}

\displaystyle=2K(N\wedge\widetilde{T}_{e}).

for any unit vector
X\in T_{w}M
orthogonal to
T_{e}(w)
, where
K_{S}
denotes the sectional curvature of
S^{n+k-1}(r)
.
Therefore, we conclude

K(N\wedge\widetilde{T}_{e})>0

for any unit vector
N\in T_{w}M
orthogonal to
T_{e}(w)
. However, this contradicts to the assumption of (b). Thus, there is no stable discrete minimal immersion into
M
.

(ii) By Corollary
4.4
,
(M,g)
has property
{({\bf N}_{2})}
if
Q_{F}<0
, and this is equivalent to
{\rm Ric}>(n/2r^{2})g
. Suppose
{\rm Ric}=(n/2r^{2})g
, or equivalently,
Q_{F}=0
. Then, we have
{\rm Tr}q_{F}^{2}=0
by the trace formula. Thus, by Lemma
4.5
(ii), if a non-constant
2
-harmonic map
f:(X,m_{E})\to(M,g)
were stable, then
F\circ f
is also a
2
-harmonic map into
\mathbb{R}^{n+k}
, and hence,
F(M)
contains a line segment in
\mathbb{R}^{n+k}
. However, this yields a contradiction because
F(M)\subseteq S^{n+k-1}(r)
and
S^{n+k-1}(r)
does not contain any line segment. Thus, there is no non-constant stable discrete 2-harmonic map into
M
.
∎

4.3.
Isotropy irreducible homogeneous spaces

As studied in
[
10
,
24
]
, we shall apply our non-existence criteria to an isotropy irreducible compact Riemannian homogeneous space.

Let
(M,g)
be a compact Riemannian manifold and suppose a closed Lie subgroup
G\subseteq{\rm Isom}(M,g)
acts on
M
transitively. We denote the isotropy subgroup at a point
w\in M
by
K
so that
M
is diffeomorphic to
G/K
, and we always use this identification in the following. Note that
G
is a compact Lie group since
M
is compact. For the sake of convenience, we fix an
{\rm Ad}(G)
-invariant inner product
\langle,\rangle
on the Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
of
G
. We denote the Lie algebra of
K
by
\mathfrak{k}
and write the orthogonal complement of
\mathfrak{k}
in
\mathfrak{g}
by
\mathfrak{m}
. Then, we obtain a reductive decomposition
\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{k}\oplus\mathfrak{m}
, and
\mathfrak{m}
is identified with the tangent space
T_{o}(G/K)
at the origin
o
.

We say
(M=G/K,g)
is
isotropy irreducible
if the isotropy representation is irreducible, or equivalently, the restriction
{\rm Ad}|_{K}:K\to GL(\mathfrak{m})
is an irreducible representation. If this is the case, every
{\rm Ad}_{G}(K)
-invariant inner products on
\mathfrak{m}
is proportional by Schur’s lemma, and hence, a
G
-invariant Riemannian metric
g
is uniquely determined (up to homothety). In particular, we may assume
g
is obtained by the extension of the restriction
\langle,\rangle|_{\mathfrak{m}}
of the
{\rm Ad}(G)
-invariant inner product, namely,
g
is a normal homogeneous metric. Moreover, it turns out that
g
is actually an Einstein metric (see
[
33
]
) and the sectional curvature of
(M,g)
is non-negative (see (
35
) below for the precise expression of the curvature). If
M
is flat, every discrete harmonic map is stable by Theorem
2.9
. Thus we assume
M
is a non-flat space for our purpose. See
[
32
,
33
]
for the classification of simply-connected isotropy irreducible Riemannian homogeneous spaces.

Let us recall the
i
-th standard immersion
\Phi_{i}:M^{n}\to\mathbb{R}^{n+k}
of
M=G/K
. For a positive integer
i\geq 1
, we denote the
i
-th (positive) eigenvalue of the Laplace operator
\Delta=d\delta+\delta d
acting on
C^{\infty}(M)
by
\lambda_{i}>0
. We denote the
i
-th eigenspace in
L^{2}(M)
by
V_{i}
and set
m_{i}:={\rm dim}V_{i}
. Take an
L^{2}
-orthonormal basis
\{f_{1},\ldots,f_{m_{i}}\}
of
V_{i}
and define an immersion
\Phi_{i}:M\to V_{i}\simeq\mathbb{R}^{m_{i}}
by

\Phi_{i}:=\alpha\cdot(f_{1},\ldots,f_{m_{i}}),

where
\alpha
is some positive constant.
It turns out that
\Phi_{i}
is a
G
-equivariant map with respect to some orthogonal representation
\rho:G\to O(V_{i})
, i.e. it holds that
\Phi_{i}(gw)=\rho(g)\Phi_{i}(w)
for any
g\in G
and
w\in M
(see
[
1
, Subsection 2.4.2]
for details). Thus, the induced metric is also
G
-invariant, and proportional to
g
since
M=G/K
is isotropy irreducible. Therefore, we can choose
\alpha
so that
\Phi_{i}:(M,g)\to\mathbb{R}^{m_{i}}
is an isometric immersion. Then, the map
\Phi_{i}
is called the
i
-th standard isometric immersion of
(M,g)
.
In the following, we suppose
\Phi_{i}
is an isometric immersion.

Because
\Phi_{i}
is equivariant under the representation
\rho:G\to O(V_{i})
,
\Phi_{i}
is an immersion into the hypersphere
S^{m_{i}-1}(r)
of some radius
r
. We denote the immersion by
\widetilde{\Phi}_{i}:M\to S^{m_{i}-1}(r)
. Then, by the Theorem of Takahashi,
\widetilde{\Phi}_{i}
becomes a minimal immersion. Indeed, a well-known formula for an isometric immersion into the Euclidean space shows that
\Delta\Phi_{i}=-H
, where
H
is the mean curvature vector of
\Phi_{i}
. Since the mean curvature vector
\widetilde{H}
of
\widetilde{\Phi}_{i}
and
H
is related by
H=\widetilde{H}-\frac{n}{r}\nu
, we actually have
\Delta\Phi_{i}=-\widetilde{H}+\frac{n}{r}\nu
. On the other hand, we have
\Delta\Phi_{i}=\lambda_{i}\Phi_{i}=\lambda_{i}\cdot r\nu
by definition of
\Phi_{i}
. Combining these two equations, we see that

\widetilde{H}=0
and
r^{2}={n/\lambda_{i}}
,
namely,
\widetilde{\Phi}_{i}:M\to S^{m_{i}-1}(r)
is a minimal immersion into the hypersphere of radius
r=\sqrt{n/\lambda_{i}}
.

Since
g
is an Einstein metric, we have
{\rm Ric}=c\cdot g
for some constant
c
. Note that
c
is written by
c={\rm scl}(M)/n
, where
{\rm scl}(M)
is the scalar curvature of
(M,g)
. Since
\widetilde{\Phi}_{i}
is a minimal immersion, (
31
) shows that

\displaystyle Q_{\Phi_{i}}=(\lambda_{i}-2c)g.

Moreover, by applying Proposition
4.6
to the minimal immersion
\widetilde{\Phi}_{i}
, we obtain the following:

Proposition 4.7
.

Let
M^{n}=G/K
be a non-flat, isotropy irreducible compact homogeneous space with
{\rm Ric}=c\cdot g
.

(i)

If
(M,g)
satisfies one of the following two conditions, then
(M,g)
has property
({\bf N}_{1})
.

{\rm(a)}^{\prime}

\lambda_{i}<\frac{n}{n-1}(2c+\beta_{\widetilde{\Phi}_{i}})
for some
i
.

{\rm(b)}^{\prime}

\lambda_{1}=2cn/(n-1)
and
{\rm dim}\mathfrak{a}_{Y}\geq 2
for any
Y\in\mathfrak{m}
, where
\mathfrak{a}_{Y}
is a maximal abelian subspace in
\mathfrak{m}
containing
Y
.

(ii)

If
(M,g)
satisfies
\lambda_{1}\leq 2c
, then
(M,g)
has property
({\bf N}_{2})
.

Proof.

By Proposition
4.6
(i)-(a), the property (
{\bf N}_{1}
) is provided if
\widetilde{\Phi}_{i}
satisfies
{\rm Ric}>\frac{1}{2}(\frac{n-1}{r^{2}}-\beta_{\widetilde{\Phi}_{i}})g
for some
i
. Since
{\rm Ric}=cg
and
r=\sqrt{n/\lambda_{i}}
, this condition is equivalent to
{\rm(a)}^{\prime}
.

Next, we shall apply Proposition
4.6
(i)-(b) to the isometric immersion
\widetilde{\Phi}_{i}
.
It is easy to see that
{\rm Ric}=((n-1)/2r^{2})g
if and only if
\lambda_{i}=2cn/(n-1)
since
r=\sqrt{n/\lambda_{i}}
and
g
is an Einstein metric. If
i\geq 2
, then
\lambda_{1}<\lambda_{i}=2cn/(n-1)
and this is reduced to the case
{\rm(a)}^{\prime}
. Thus, we suppose
\lambda_{1}=2cn/(n-1)
.

We consider the obstruction for the sectional curvature given in Proposition
4.6
(i)-(b). As mentioned above, we may assume
g
is a normal homogeneous metric.
Thus, by using the general formula of the curvature tensor of Riemannian homogeneous space (see
[
2
, Theorem 7.30]
), we obtain that

(35)

\displaystyle K(Y\wedge Z)=|[Y,Z]_{\mathfrak{k}}|^{2}+\frac{1}{4}|[Y,Z]_{\mathfrak{m}}|^{2}

for any orthonormal pair
Y,Z\in\mathfrak{m}
, where
Y_{\mathfrak{k}}
and
Z_{\mathfrak{m}}
denote the orthogonal projection onto
\mathfrak{k}
and
\mathfrak{m}
, respectively. In particular, we see that
K(Y\wedge Z)=0
if and only if
[Y,Z]=0
. Therefore, for a given
Y\in\mathfrak{m}
, there exists a unit vector
Z\in\mathfrak{m}
orthogonal to
X
such that
K(Y\wedge Z)=0
if and only if
{\rm dim}\mathfrak{a}_{Y}\geq 2
. Since
M
is homogeneous, this shows that the condition
{\rm(b)}^{\prime}
implies the property
({\bf N}_{1})
. This proves (i).

Similarly, by applying Proposition
4.6
-(ii) to
\widetilde{\Phi}_{i}
, we obtain (ii).
∎

Remark 4.8
.

It is not necessary to assume
M
is simply-connected in this proposition. In particular, if there exists a stable discrete minimal immersion into an isotropy irreducible space
M=G/K
(e.g.
\pi_{1}(M)\neq\{0\}
), then we have

\displaystyle\lambda_{1}\geq\frac{2cn}{n-1}=\frac{2{\rm scl}(M)}{n-1}.

We remark that, it is known
\lambda_{1}\geq{\rm scl}(M)/(n-1)
for any isotropy irreducible compact homogeneous space (see
[
20
, Theorem 2]
). Thus, an improved estimate of
\lambda_{1}
is provided if
M
admits a stable discrete minimal immersion.

Notice that if

(36)

\displaystyle\lambda_{1}<2c

(or equivalently
Q_{\Phi_{i}}<0
for some
i
), then
(M,g)
has properties
({\bf N}_{1})
and
({\bf N}_{2})
.
It is known that this condition (
36
) is actually equivalent to the
strongly instability
of
M=G/K
in the sense of
[
10
]
(see
[
10
, Theorem 5.3]
. See also
[
24
, Theorem 4]
). If
M
is strongly unstable, there is no non-constant stable
smooth
harmonic map from any compact Riemannian manifold
N
into
M
. Our result shows that a similar non-existence result still holds for
discrete
harmonic maps.

Corollary 4.9
.

If
(M,g)
is a strongly unstable, isotropy irreducible compact homogeneous space, then
(M,g)
has properties
({\bf N}_{1})
and
({\bf N}_{2})
.

For the property

Corollary 4.11
.

Let
(M^{n},g_{K})
be an irreducible compact Riemannian symmetric space of rank

. If the inequality

(37)

\displaystyle\lambda_{1}\leq\frac{n}{n-1}

holds, then
(M,g_{K})
has property

Proof.

It is well-known that, if
M
is a symmetric space, the isotropy representation is a polar action and the section is given by a maximal abelian subspace
\mathfrak{a}
in
\mathfrak{m}
. Namely, any element
Y\in\mathfrak{m}
is expressed by
Y={\rm Ad}(k)Y^{\prime}
for some

and

. Then,
\mathfrak{a}_{Y}:={\rm Ad}(k)\mathfrak{a}
is a maximal abelian subspace in
\mathfrak{m}
containing

since
{\rm dim}\mathfrak{a}_{Y}={\rm dim}\mathfrak{a}
. Because we assume
{\rm rank}M\geq 2
, it automatically satisfies that
{\rm dim}\mathfrak{a}_{Y}\geq 2
for any

. Thus, Proposition
4.7
(i) implies the desired result.
∎

By the classification result and the data of the first eigenvalue given in

\displaystyle SU(3)/SO(3),\quad SU(4)/SO(4)\simeq SO(6)/SO(3)\times SO(3),\quad Spin(7),\quad G_{2}.

Let us consider the case when rank

\displaystyle G_{2}/SO(4),\quad SO(q+3)/SO(q)\times SO(3)\ (q\geq 4).

(a)

5.
The case of
G_{2}/SO(4)

In this section, we consider the case when
M=G_{2}/SO(4)
, a simply-connected exceptional compact symmetric space of rank
2
, and show the following result.

Theorem 5.1
.

The exceptional compact symmetric space
G_{2}/SO(4)
has property
({\bf N}_{1})
.

To prove this, we shall explicitly construct an immersion
\Phi
of
G_{2}/SO(4)
into the Euclidean space, and apply Corollary
4.4
to the map
\Phi
combining with geometric properties of
\Phi
. The description of
\Phi
is based on geometry of symmetric R-space. We thus recall some basic facts on symmetric R-spaces and prove some lemmas.

5.1.
Preliminaries on (symmetric) R-space

We refer to
[
1
]
for details.

Let
U/U^{\prime}
be a Riemannian symmetric space of noncompact type, where
U
is a connected semi-simple Lie group and
U^{\prime}
is a connected closed subgroup of
U
. We denote the Lie algebra of
U
and
U^{\prime}
by
\mathfrak{u}
and
\mathfrak{u}^{\prime}
, respectively. The Cartan involution on
\mathfrak{u}
is denoted by
\theta:\mathfrak{u}\to\mathfrak{u}
, and we write the associated Cartan decomposition by
\mathfrak{u}=\mathfrak{u}^{\prime}\oplus\mathfrak{p}
. As usual, we identify
\mathfrak{p}
with
T_{o}(U/U^{\prime})
. We define an inner product
\langle,\rangle_{\mathfrak{u}}
on
\mathfrak{u}
by
\langle X,Y\rangle_{\mathfrak{u}}:=-{\bf B}(X,\theta Y)
for
X,Y\in\mathfrak{u}
, where
{\bf B}
is the Killing-form on
\mathfrak{u}
. The restriction on
\mathfrak{p}
defines an
{\rm Ad}_{U}(U^{\prime})
-invariant inner product
\langle,\rangle_{\mathfrak{p}}
on
\mathfrak{p}
. In this way, we identify
\mathfrak{p}
with the Euclidean space.

We take a maximal abelian subspace
\mathfrak{a}
of
\mathfrak{p}
. Then, we have the following direct-sum decomposition of
\mathfrak{u}
(called the
restricted root decomposition
with respect to
\mathfrak{a}
):

\displaystyle\mathfrak{u}=\mathfrak{u}_{0}\oplus\bigoplus_{\alpha\in\Sigma}\mathfrak{u}_{\alpha}.

Here,
\alpha
is a non-zero element in the dual space
\mathfrak{a}^{*}
of
\mathfrak{a}
such that
\mathfrak{u}_{\alpha}:=\{X\in\mathfrak{u}\mid{\rm ad}(h)X=\alpha(h)X\ \forall h\in\mathfrak{a}\}\neq\phi
, and
\mathfrak{u}_{0}=\mathfrak{u}_{0}^{\prime}\oplus\mathfrak{a}
where
\mathfrak{u}_{0}^{\prime}=\{Z\in\mathfrak{u}^{\prime}\mid[Z,h]=0\ \forall h\in\mathfrak{a}\}
. We call
\alpha
a
root
, and denote the set of roots by
\Sigma
. It is known that
\Sigma
becomes a root system. We define an ordering on
\mathfrak{a}
by using the simple roots of
\Sigma
, and denote the set of positive roots by
\Sigma_{+}
. The restricted root decomposition satisfies the following properties:

•

[\mathfrak{u}_{\alpha},\mathfrak{u}_{\beta}]\subset\mathfrak{u}_{\alpha+\beta}
for any
\alpha,\beta\in\Sigma
, where
[\mathfrak{u}_{\alpha},\mathfrak{u}_{\beta}]=0
if
\alpha+\beta\not\in\Sigma
.

•

Suppose
\alpha,\beta\in\Sigma\cup\{0\}
and
\alpha\neq\beta
. Then,
\mathfrak{u}_{\alpha}
and
\mathfrak{u}_{\beta}
are orthogonal to each other if and only if
\beta\neq-\alpha
.

We set

\mathfrak{u}^{\prime}_{\alpha}:=\mathfrak{u}^{\prime}\cap(\mathfrak{u}_{\alpha}\oplus\mathfrak{u}_{-\alpha})
,

\mathfrak{p}_{\alpha}:=\mathfrak{p}\cap(\mathfrak{u}_{\alpha}\oplus\mathfrak{u}_{-\alpha})

so that

\mathfrak{u}^{\prime}=\mathfrak{u}^{\prime}_{0}\oplus\bigoplus_{\alpha\in\Sigma_{+}}\mathfrak{u}^{\prime}_{\alpha},\quad\mathfrak{p}=\mathfrak{a}\oplus\bigoplus_{\alpha\in\Sigma_{+}}\mathfrak{p}_{\alpha}.

Note that
\mathfrak{u}^{\prime}_{\alpha}=\mathfrak{u}^{\prime}_{-\alpha}
and
\mathfrak{p}_{\alpha}=\mathfrak{p}_{-\alpha}
for any
\alpha\in\Sigma
. Moreover, by letting
\mathfrak{p}_{0}=\mathfrak{a}
, we have

(38)

\displaystyle[\mathfrak{u}^{\prime}_{\alpha},\mathfrak{u}^{\prime}_{\beta}]\subset\mathfrak{u}^{\prime}_{\alpha+\beta}\oplus\mathfrak{u}^{\prime}_{\alpha-\beta},\quad[\mathfrak{u}^{\prime}_{\alpha},\mathfrak{p}_{\beta}]\subset\mathfrak{p}_{\alpha+\beta}\oplus\mathfrak{p}_{\alpha-\beta},\quad[\mathfrak{p}_{\alpha},\mathfrak{p}_{\beta}]\subset\mathfrak{u}^{\prime}_{\alpha+\beta}\oplus\mathfrak{u}^{\prime}_{\alpha-\beta}

for any
\alpha,\beta\in\Sigma\cup\{0\}
since
[\mathfrak{u}_{\alpha},\mathfrak{u}_{\beta}]\subset\mathfrak{u}_{\alpha+\beta}
,
[\mathfrak{u}^{\prime},\mathfrak{u}^{\prime}]\subset\mathfrak{u}^{\prime}
,
[\mathfrak{u}^{\prime},\mathfrak{p}]\subset\mathfrak{p}
and
[\mathfrak{p},\mathfrak{p}]\subset\mathfrak{u}^{\prime}
.

It is known that the isotropy representation of
U/U^{\prime}
is equivalent to
{\rm Ad}|_{U^{\prime}}:U^{\prime}\to O(\mathfrak{p})
.
We take an element
\eta\in\mathfrak{p}
and consider the adjoint orbit through
\eta

\mathcal{O}_{\eta}:=U^{\prime}\cdot\eta={\rm Ad}_{U}(U^{\prime})\eta.

The orbit
\mathcal{O}_{\eta}
is called an
R-space
. We denote the stabilizer subgroup at
\eta
by
U^{\prime\prime}
so that
\mathcal{O}_{\eta}
is diffeomorphic to
U^{\prime}/U^{\prime\prime}
. We induce a Riemannian metric on
\mathcal{O}_{\eta}
from
\mathfrak{p}
and denote the isometric embedding of
\mathcal{O}_{\eta}
into
\mathfrak{p}
by

\varphi:\mathcal{O}_{\eta}\to\mathfrak{p}.

Suppose that
\mathfrak{a}
is a maximal abelian subspace in
\mathfrak{p}
containing
\eta
. Then the geometry of
\varphi
is described by using the associated restricted root system. For example, the tangent space
T_{\eta}\mathcal{O}_{\eta}
and the normal space
T^{\perp}_{\eta}\mathcal{O}_{\eta}
at
\eta
are expressed by (see
[
1
, Subsection 2.3.2]
)

(39)

\displaystyle T_{\eta}\mathcal{O}_{\eta}=\bigoplus_{\alpha\in\Sigma_{>0}}\mathfrak{p}_{\alpha},\quad T^{\perp}_{\eta}\mathcal{O}_{\eta}=\mathfrak{a}\oplus\bigoplus_{\alpha\in\Sigma_{0}}\mathfrak{p}_{\alpha},

where we put
\Sigma_{>0}:=\{\alpha\in\Sigma_{+}\mid\alpha(\eta)>0\}
and
\Sigma_{0}:=\{\alpha\in\Sigma_{+}\mid\alpha(\eta)=0\}
.
According to (
39
), we write a tangent vector
X\in T_{\eta}\mathcal{O}_{\eta}
and a normal vector
\xi\in T^{\perp}_{\eta}\mathcal{O}_{\eta}
by

\displaystyle X=\sum_{\alpha\in\Sigma_{>0}}X_{\alpha}\quad(X_{\alpha}\in\mathfrak{p}_{\alpha})\quad{\rm and}\quad\xi=\xi_{0}+\sum_{\mu\in\Sigma_{0}}\xi_{\mu}\quad(\xi_{0}\in\mathfrak{a},\ \xi_{\mu}\in\mathfrak{p}_{\mu}).

We denote the second fundamental form and the shape operator of
\varphi
by
B_{\varphi}
and
A_{\varphi}
, respectively. Then, we have the following formula:

Proposition 5.2

(cf.
[
29
]
)
.

For any unit normal vector
\xi\in T^{\perp}_{\eta}\mathcal{O}_{\eta}
, we have

(40)

\displaystyle(A_{\varphi})_{\xi}(X)=\sum_{\alpha\in\Sigma_{>0}}\Big\{-\frac{\alpha(\xi_{0})}{\alpha(\eta)}X_{\alpha}-\frac{1}{\alpha(\eta)^{2}}\sum_{\mu\in\Sigma_{0}}[\xi_{\mu},[\eta,X_{\alpha}]]\Big\}

for any
X\in T_{\eta}\mathcal{O}_{\eta}
.

Proof.

We give a proof for the sake of convenience of the reader. We remark that the sign is different from
[
29
]
at some places because our description is based on the noncompact dual of
[
29
]
.

Since
\mathcal{O}_{\eta}={\rm Ad}_{U}(U^{\prime})\eta
, the tangent space of
\mathcal{O}_{\eta}
at
\eta
is written by
T_{\eta}\mathcal{O}_{\eta}=\{Z^{*}_{\eta}\mid Z\in\mathfrak{u}^{\prime}\}
, where

(41)

\displaystyle Z^{*}_{w}:=\frac{d}{dt}{\rm Ad}({\rm exp}tZ)w\Big|_{t=0}={\rm ad}(Z)w=[Z,w]\quad\textup{ for $w\in\mathfrak{p}$}.

Note that
Z^{*}
defines a Killing vector field on
\mathfrak{p}
since
{\rm Ad}_{U}(U^{\prime})\subset O(\mathfrak{p})
. Fix an arbitrary
X\in T_{\eta}\mathcal{O}_{\eta}
, and we take
Z\in\mathfrak{u}^{\prime}
so that
Z^{*}_{\eta}=[Z,\eta]=X
. More precisely,
Z
is given by

(42)

\displaystyle Z=\sum_{\alpha\in\Sigma_{>0}}-\frac{1}{\alpha(\eta)^{2}}[\eta,X_{\alpha}].

Indeed, we see
Z\in\mathfrak{u}^{\prime}
since
[\mathfrak{p},\mathfrak{p}]\subset\mathfrak{u}^{\prime}
and a direct computation shows that
[Z,\eta]=X
.

Take any
Y\in T_{\eta}\mathcal{O}_{\eta}
and
\xi\in T_{\eta}^{\perp}\mathcal{O}_{\eta}
, and we extend
\xi
so that
\langle Z^{*},\xi\rangle=0
on a neighborhood of
\eta
in
\mathfrak{p}
. Then, we have

(43)

\displaystyle\langle(A_{\varphi})_{\xi}(X),Y\rangle_{\eta}=\langle(A_{\varphi})_{\xi}(Y),X\rangle_{\eta}=-\langle\overline{\nabla}_{Y}\xi,Z^{*}\rangle_{\eta}=\langle\overline{\nabla}_{Y}Z^{*},\xi\rangle_{\eta}=-\langle\overline{\nabla}_{\xi}Z^{*},Y\rangle_{\eta},

where
\overline{\nabla}
is the Levi-Civita connection on
\mathfrak{p}
, and we used the fact that
Z^{*}
is a Killing vector field on
\mathfrak{p}
, which implies
\langle\overline{\nabla}_{u}Z^{*},v\rangle+\langle\overline{\nabla}_{v}Z^{*},u\rangle=0
for any
u,v\in T_{\eta}\mathfrak{p}
. Since
Y
is arbitrary, (
43
) shows that

(A_{\varphi})_{\xi}(X)=-(\overline{\nabla}_{\xi}Z^{*})^{\top}.

Moreover, by taking a curve
c:(-\epsilon,\epsilon)\to\mathfrak{p}
satisfying that
c(0)=\eta
and
c^{\prime}(0)=\xi
, we see

\displaystyle\overline{\nabla}_{\xi}Z^{*}=\frac{d}{ds}Z^{*}(c(s))\Big|_{s=0}=\frac{d}{ds}\frac{d}{dt}{\rm Ad}({\rm exp}tZ)c(s)\Big|_{s=t=0}=\frac{d}{dt}{\rm Ad}({\rm exp}tZ)\xi\Big|_{t=0}=[Z,\xi].

Therefore, we obtain

(A_{\varphi})_{\xi}(X)=[\xi,Z]^{\top}.

Here, we have

(44)

\displaystyle[\xi,Z]

\displaystyle=\sum_{\alpha\in\Sigma_{>0}}\Big\{-\frac{1}{\alpha(\eta)^{2}}[\xi_{0},[\eta,X_{\alpha}]]-\frac{1}{\alpha(\eta)^{2}}\sum_{\mu\in\Sigma_{0}}[\xi_{u},[\eta,X_{\alpha}]]\Big\}.

since
Z
is given by (
42
). In (
44
), we shall show

\displaystyle[\xi_{0},[\eta,X_{\alpha}]]=\alpha(\xi_{0})\alpha(\eta)X_{\alpha}\quad{\rm and}\quad[\xi,Z]=[\xi,Z]^{\top}

which implies the desired formula (
40
).
Indeed, by definition of
\mathfrak{p}_{\gamma}
(
\gamma\in\Sigma_{+}
), any element
X_{\gamma}\in\mathfrak{p}_{\gamma}
is written by

X_{\gamma}=\widetilde{X}_{\gamma}+\widetilde{X}_{-\gamma}

for some elements
\widetilde{X}_{\gamma}\in\mathfrak{u}_{\gamma}
and
\widetilde{X}_{-\gamma}\in\mathfrak{u}_{-\gamma}
. Thus, we have

\displaystyle[h_{1},X_{\gamma}]=\gamma(h_{1})(\widetilde{X}_{\gamma}-\widetilde{X}_{-\gamma}),\quad[h_{2},[h_{1},X_{\gamma}]]=\gamma(h_{2})\gamma(h_{1})X_{\gamma}

for any
h_{1},h_{2}\in\mathfrak{a}
. This implies
[\xi_{0},[\eta,X_{\alpha}]]=\alpha(\xi_{0})\alpha(\eta)X_{\alpha}
.
On the other hand, we have that
[\xi_{\mu},[\eta,X_{\alpha}]]\in\mathfrak{p}_{\mu+\alpha}\oplus\mathfrak{p}_{\mu-\alpha}
by (
38
). Since
\mu\in\Sigma_{0}
and
\alpha\in\Sigma_{>0}
, we see
(\mu\pm\alpha)(\eta)\neq 0
, and hence,
[\xi_{\mu},[\eta,X_{\alpha}]]\in T_{\eta}\mathcal{O}_{\eta}
. Therefore, we see
[\xi,Z]\in T_{\eta}\mathcal{O}_{\eta}
, i.e.
[\xi,Z]=[\xi,Z]^{\top}
as required.
∎

If
X
belongs to a special subspace in
T_{\eta}\mathcal{O}_{\eta}
, we obtain the following simple formulas:

Lemma 5.3
.

Let
S
be a subset of
\Sigma_{>0}
and set

\mathfrak{p}_{S}:=\bigoplus_{\alpha\in S}\mathfrak{p}_{\alpha}\subset T_{\eta}\mathcal{O}_{\eta}.

Suppose
S
consists of strongly orthogonal roots i.e.
\alpha\pm\beta\notin\Sigma
for any
\alpha,\beta\in S
. Then we have

(45)

\displaystyle B_{\varphi}(X,Y)=\sum_{\alpha\in S}-\frac{\langle X_{\alpha},Y_{\alpha}\rangle}{\alpha(\eta)}\alpha^{\sharp}\in\mathfrak{a}\quad\textup{for any $X,Y\in\mathfrak{p}_{S}$},

where
\alpha^{\sharp}
denotes the dual vector of the root
\alpha
with respect to the inner product on
\mathfrak{a}
.

Furthermore, we have

(46)

\displaystyle|B_{\varphi}(X,X)|^{2}=\sum_{\alpha\in S}\frac{|\alpha|^{2}}{\alpha(\eta)^{2}}|X_{\alpha}|^{4}\quad\textup{for any $X\in\mathfrak{p}_{S}$.}

Proof.

Suppose
S
consists of strongly orthogonal roots. Fix arbitrary elements
\alpha,\beta\in S\subset\Sigma_{>0}
(possibly
\alpha=\beta
) and
\mu\in\Sigma_{0}
. Then, for any
X_{\alpha}\in\mathfrak{p}_{\alpha}
,
Y_{\beta}\in\mathfrak{p}_{\beta}
and
\xi_{\mu}\in\mathfrak{p}_{\mu}
, we have

\displaystyle\langle[\xi_{\mu},[\eta,X_{\alpha}]],Y_{\beta}\rangle_{\mathfrak{p}}={\bf B}([\xi_{\mu},[\eta,X_{\alpha}]],Y_{\beta})=-{\bf B}([\eta,X_{\alpha}],[\xi_{\mu},Y_{\beta}])=\langle[\eta,X_{\alpha}],[\xi_{\mu},Y_{\beta}]\rangle_{\mathfrak{u}}

since the Killing form
{\bf B}
is
{\rm Ad}(U)
-invariant. By properties of root decomposition, we have

\displaystyle[\eta,X_{\alpha}]\in\mathfrak{u}_{\pm\alpha}\quad{\rm and}\quad[\xi_{\mu},Y_{\beta}]\in\mathfrak{u}_{\pm(\mu+\beta)}\oplus\mathfrak{u}_{\pm(\mu-\beta)},

where we put
\mathfrak{u}_{\pm\gamma}=\mathfrak{u}_{\gamma}\oplus\mathfrak{u}_{-\gamma}
. Since
\alpha\pm\beta\not\in\Sigma
by assumption, we have
\mu\pm\beta\neq\pm\alpha
. Therefore,
[\eta,X_{\alpha}]
and
[\xi_{\mu},Y_{\beta}]
are orthogonal to each other, and hence, we obtain

\langle[\xi_{\mu},[\eta,X_{\alpha}]],Y_{\beta}\rangle_{\mathfrak{p}}=0.

In particular, for any
X,Y\in\mathfrak{p}_{S}
and
\xi\in T^{\perp}_{\eta}\mathcal{O}_{\eta}
, the formula (
40
) implies that

\displaystyle\langle(A_{\varphi})_{\xi}(X),Y\rangle=\sum_{\alpha\in S}-\frac{\alpha(\xi_{0})}{\alpha(\eta)}\langle X_{\alpha},Y\rangle=\sum_{\alpha\in S}-\frac{\alpha(\xi_{0})}{\alpha(\eta)}\langle X_{\alpha},Y_{\alpha}\rangle.

Therefore, by using an orthonormal basis
\{h_{k}\}_{k}
of
\mathfrak{a}
, we see

B_{\varphi}(X,Y)=\sum_{k}\langle A^{\varphi}_{h_{k}}(X),Y\rangle h_{k}=\sum_{k}\sum_{\alpha\in S}-\frac{\alpha(h_{k})}{\alpha(\eta)}\langle X_{\alpha},Y_{\alpha}\rangle h_{k}=\sum_{\alpha\in S}-\frac{\langle X_{\alpha},Y_{\alpha}\rangle}{\alpha(\eta)}\alpha^{\sharp}

for any
X,Y\in\mathfrak{p}_{S}
. This proves the formula (
45
).

Furthermore, we have

|B_{\varphi}(X,X)|^{2}=\sum_{\alpha,\beta\in S}\frac{|X_{\alpha}|^{2}|X_{\beta}|^{2}}{\alpha(\eta)\beta(\eta)}\langle\alpha,\beta\rangle.

Since
\Sigma
is an abstract root system in the sense of
[
12
]
(see
[
12
, Corollary 6.53]
), Proposition 2.48 (f) in
[
12
]
shows that if
\alpha\pm\beta\notin\Sigma\cup\{0\}
, then
\langle\alpha,\beta\rangle=0
. Because we suppose
\alpha\pm\beta\not\in\Sigma
for any
\alpha,\beta\in S\subset\Sigma_{>0}
,
\langle\alpha,\beta\rangle\neq 0
if and only if
\alpha=\beta
.
Therefore, we obtain (
46
).
∎

In the following, we restrict our attention to a special element in
\mathfrak{p}
. Suppose that

is an element in
\mathfrak{p}
such that

Note that the eigen-decomposition

Then, the orbit

is a Riemannian symmetric space with respect to the induced metric (see

. Then, we obtain a reductive decomposition

\mathfrak{u}^{\prime}=\mathfrak{u}^{\prime\prime}\oplus\mathfrak{p}^{\prime},\quad\textup{where $\mathfrak{u}^{\prime\prime}=\mathfrak{u}^{\prime}\cap\mathfrak{u}^{0}$ and $\mathfrak{p}^{\prime}=\mathfrak{u}^{\prime}\cap(\mathfrak{u}^{-1}\oplus\mathfrak{u}^{1})$.}

Indeed, we have
\mathfrak{u}^{\prime\prime}=\mathfrak{u}^{\prime}\cap\mathfrak{u}^{0}
since
{\rm Ad}_{U}(U^{\prime\prime})\eta=\eta

,

is decomposed into
X=X^{0}+X^{-1}+X^{1}
where

{\rm ad}(\eta)^{2}X=X^{-1}+X^{1}
. Since
[\mathfrak{p},\mathfrak{p}]\subset\mathfrak{u}^{\prime}
,
[\mathfrak{u}^{\prime},\mathfrak{p}]\subset\mathfrak{p}
and
\eta\in\mathfrak{p}

\mathfrak{p}^{\prime}
is an
{\rm Ad}_{U}(U^{\prime\prime})
-invariant subspace, and hence,

As usual, we identify

with

Since

(

), the tangent vector

corresponds to

-{\rm ad}(\eta)|_{\mathfrak{p}^{\prime}}:\mathfrak{p}^{\prime}\to T_{\eta}\mathcal{O}_{\eta}.

Lemma 5.4
.

Suppose
\mathcal{O}_{\eta}\simeq U^{\prime}/U^{\prime\prime}
is a symmetric R-space. Then we have the following:

(i)

-{\rm ad}(\eta)|_{\mathfrak{p}^{\prime}}:\mathfrak{p}^{\prime}\to T_{\eta}\mathcal{O}_{\eta}
is an
{\rm Ad}_{U}(U^{\prime\prime})
-equivariant isomorphism satisfying that
[-{\rm ad}(\eta)X,-{\rm ad}(\eta)Y]=-[X,Y]
for any
X,Y\in\mathfrak{p}^{\prime}
. In particular, there exists a 1-1 correspondence between abelian subspaces in
\mathfrak{p}^{\prime}
and abelian subspaces in
T_{\eta}\mathcal{O}_{\eta}\subset\mathfrak{p}
.

(ii)

\alpha(\eta)=1
for any
\alpha\in\Sigma_{>0}
.

Proof.

(i) For any
X\in\mathfrak{p}^{\prime}
and
u^{\prime\prime}\in U^{\prime\prime}
, we see

{\rm Ad}(u^{\prime\prime})\circ(-{\rm ad}(\eta))X={\rm Ad}(u^{\prime\prime})[X,\eta]=[{\rm Ad}(u^{\prime\prime})X,{\rm Ad}(u^{\prime\prime})\eta]=[{\rm Ad}(u^{\prime\prime})X,\eta]=-{\rm ad}(\eta)\circ{\rm Ad}(u^{\prime\prime})X.

This shows
T_{\eta}\mathcal{O}_{\eta}
is also an
U^{\prime\prime}
-invariant subspace and
-{\rm ad}(\eta)|_{\mathfrak{p}^{\prime}}
is
U^{\prime\prime}
-equivariant.

Since
\mathfrak{u}=\mathfrak{u}^{-1}\oplus\mathfrak{u}^{0}\oplus\mathfrak{u}^{1}
is a gradation of
\mathfrak{u}
, we have that
[\mathfrak{u}^{1},\mathfrak{u}^{1}]=[\mathfrak{u}^{-1},\mathfrak{u}^{-1}]=\{0\}
. In particular, for any
X,Y\in\mathfrak{u}^{-1}\oplus\mathfrak{u}^{1}
, it is easy to see

[{\rm ad}(\eta)X,{\rm ad}(\eta)Y]=-[X,Y].

Because
\mathfrak{p}^{\prime}\subset\mathfrak{u}^{-1}\oplus\mathfrak{u}^{1}
, this shows that we have
[-{\rm ad}(\eta)X,-{\rm ad}(\eta)Y]=-[X,Y]
for any
X,Y\in\mathfrak{p}^{\prime}
.
In particular, if
\mathfrak{a}^{\prime}
is an abelian subspace in
\mathfrak{p}^{\prime}
, then
-{\rm ad}(\eta)(\mathfrak{a}^{\prime})
is also an abelian subspace in
T_{\eta}\mathcal{O}_{\eta}
, and this correspondence is invertible. This proves (i).

(ii) For any
\alpha\in\Sigma_{>0}
, we take a non-zero element
X_{\alpha}\in\mathfrak{p}_{\alpha}
, and decompose
X_{\alpha}
into
X_{\alpha}=X_{\alpha}^{-1}+X_{\alpha}^{0}+X_{\alpha}^{1}
with respect to the eigen-decomposition
\mathfrak{u}=\mathfrak{u}^{-1}\oplus\mathfrak{u}^{0}\oplus\mathfrak{u}^{1}
. Since
X_{\alpha}\in\mathfrak{p}_{\alpha}
, we have
{\rm ad}(\eta)^{2}X_{\alpha}=\alpha(\eta)^{2}X_{\alpha}
and this implies that
X_{\alpha}^{-1}+X_{\alpha}^{1}=\alpha(\eta)^{2}(X_{\alpha}^{-1}+X_{\alpha}^{0}+X_{\alpha}^{1})
. Because
\alpha(\eta)>0
, we obtain
X_{\alpha}^{0}=0
and
\alpha(\eta)=1
, as required.
∎

Finally, we prove an algebraic lemma which we use later.

Lemma 5.5
.

Suppose
\mathcal{O}_{\eta}
is a symmetric
R
-space. Let
\mathfrak{a}_{0}
be a two-dimensional subspace in
T_{\eta}\mathcal{O}_{\eta}

which is spanned by
\{e_{1},e_{2}\}
of the form

\displaystyle e_{1}=P_{1}+P_{2}\ or\ P_{1}-P_{2},\quad e_{2}=P_{2}+P_{3}

for some unit vector
P_{i}\in\mathfrak{p}_{\alpha_{i}}

(i=1,2,3)
, where
\alpha_{1}
,
\alpha_{2}
and
\alpha_{3}
are elements in
\Sigma_{>0}
that are not proportional to each other. Moreover, we suppose

(a)

\alpha_{i}-\alpha_{j}\notin\Sigma
for any
i,j=1,2,3
.

(b)

|\alpha_{1}|=|\alpha_{2}|=|\alpha_{3}|=D
for some constant
D>0
.

Then, we have

|B_{\varphi}(v,v)|^{2}=\frac{D^{2}}{2}=(\textup{const.})\quad\textup{\it for any unit vector $v\in\mathfrak{a}_{0}$}.

Proof.

It suffices to prove in the case when
e_{1}=P_{1}+P_{2}
and
e_{2}=P_{2}+P_{3}
. We first note that, by Lemma
5.4
-(ii),

\alpha+\beta\notin\Sigma
for any
\alpha,\beta\in\Sigma_{>0}
. Thus, we may assume
{\rm(a)}^{\prime}

\alpha_{i}\pm\alpha_{j}\notin\Sigma
for any
i,j=1,2,3
, i.e.
\{\alpha_{i}\}_{i=1}^{3}
consists of strongly orthogonal roots. In particular, we are able to apply Lemma
5.3
to the subset
S=\{\alpha_{i}\}_{i=1}^{3}
. By condition (b) and Lemma
5.4
-(ii), the formula (
46
) becomes

|B_{\varphi}(v,v)|^{2}=\sum_{i=1}^{3}D^{2}|v_{\alpha_{i}}|^{4}

for any
v\in\mathfrak{p}_{S}
. Since
v\in\mathfrak{a}_{0}\subset\mathfrak{p}_{S}
, we may write
v=v_{1}e_{1}+v_{2}e_{2}=v_{1}P_{1}+(v_{1}+v_{2})P_{2}+v_{2}P_{3}
for some

,
v_{\alpha_{2}}=(v_{1}+v_{2})P_{2}
and
v_{\alpha_{3}}=v_{2}P_{3}

In particular, we see

|v|^{2}=1\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad v_{1}^{2}+(v_{1}+v_{2})^{2}+v_{2}^{2}=1\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad v_{1}^{2}+v_{2}^{2}+v_{1}v_{2}=\frac{1}{2}.

Thus, for any unit vector

\displaystyle|B_{\varphi}(v,v)|^{2}=\sum_{i=1}^{3}D^{2}|v_{\alpha_{i}}|^{4}=D^{2}\cdot\{v_{1}^{4}+(v_{1}+v_{2})^{4}+v_{2}^{4}\}=D^{2}\cdot 2(v_{1}^{2}+v_{2}^{2}+v_{1}v_{2})^{2}=\frac{D^{2}}{2}

∎

In particular, we can take a basis

of

as follows (cf.

):

\displaystyle V_{1}

\displaystyle=G_{23}-G_{67},\quad V_{2}=G_{45}-G_{67},\quad V_{3}=G_{13}+G_{57},\quad V_{4}=G_{46}+G_{57},\quad V_{5}=G_{12}-G_{56},

\displaystyle V_{6}

\displaystyle V_{11}

We set

by the composition map

of the natural embedding

and the covering map

\widetilde{G}_{3}(\mathbb{R}^{7})

{G}_{3}(\mathbb{R}^{7})

Denote the Lie algebra of

and
K
by
\mathfrak{g}
and

Hence, by letting

satisfies that

is also a symmetric space with respect to the induced metric.

By using the correspondence (
5.3
), we obtain a linear isomorphism

\mathfrak{m}\simeq\widetilde{\mathfrak{m}}:={\rm span}_{\mathbb{R}}\{\widetilde{V}_{7},\ldots\widetilde{V}_{14}\}\subset T_{\eta}\mathcal{O}_{\eta}

where

is defined by replacing each

in the definition of

,

, etc. Note that the isomorphism

is

-equivariant since

and the isomorphism

is

5.4
(i).

We divide
\Sigma_{>0}
into the following four disjoint subsets:

\Sigma_{>0}=S_{1}\sqcup S_{2}\sqcup S_{3}\sqcup S_{4},

where we set

\displaystyle S_{1}=\{\epsilon_{1}-\epsilon_{5},\epsilon_{2}-\epsilon_{6},\epsilon_{3}-\epsilon_{7}\},\quad S_{2}=\{\epsilon_{1}-\epsilon_{4},\epsilon_{2}-\epsilon_{7},\epsilon_{3}-\epsilon_{6}\},

\displaystyle S_{3}=\{\epsilon_{1}-\epsilon_{7},\epsilon_{2}-\epsilon_{4},\epsilon_{3}-\epsilon_{5}\},\quad S_{4}=\{\epsilon_{1}-\epsilon_{6},\epsilon_{2}-\epsilon_{5},\epsilon_{3}-\epsilon_{4}\}.

We also set
\mathfrak{p}_{S_{i}}=\bigoplus_{\alpha\in S_{i}}\mathfrak{p}_{\alpha}
. Notice that each

consists of strongly orthogonal roots.
Then, we have an orthogonal decomposition

(55)

\displaystyle\widetilde{\mathfrak{m}}=\bigoplus_{i=1}^{4}\widetilde{\mathfrak{a}}_{i},

where

\displaystyle\widetilde{\mathfrak{a}}_{1}:={\rm span}_{\mathbb{R}}\{\widetilde{V}_{7},\widetilde{V}_{8}\}\subset\mathfrak{p}_{S_{1}},\quad\widetilde{\mathfrak{a}}_{2}:={\rm span}_{\mathbb{R}}\{\widetilde{V}_{9},\widetilde{V}_{10}\}\subset\mathfrak{p}_{S_{2}},

\displaystyle\widetilde{\mathfrak{a}}_{3}:={\rm span}_{\mathbb{R}}\{\widetilde{V}_{11},\widetilde{V}_{12}\}\subset\mathfrak{p}_{S_{3}},\quad\widetilde{\mathfrak{a}}_{4}:={\rm span}_{\mathbb{R}}\{\widetilde{V}_{13},\widetilde{V}_{14}\}\subset\mathfrak{p}_{S_{4}}.

Note that each

is an abelian subspace in
\mathfrak{p}

5.4
(i),
\widetilde{\mathfrak{a}}_{i}
corresponds to an abelian subspace
\mathfrak{a}_{i}
in
\mathfrak{p}^{\prime}
contained in
\mathfrak{m}\simeq T_{o}M
. In particular, each
\widetilde{\mathfrak{a}}_{i}
is regarded as a maximal abelian subspace in
\mathfrak{m}
since the rank of
M=G_{2}/SO(4)
is equal to 2.

Now, we are ready to give a proof of Proposition
5.6
.

Proof of Proposition
5.6
.

First, we show that
\widetilde{\Phi}:G_{2}/SO(4)\to S^{26}(|\eta|)
is a minimal immersion. Note that, an immersion
\widetilde{F}:M^{n}\to S^{n+k-1}(r)
is minimal if and only if the mean curvature of
F:M\to\mathbb{R}^{n+k}
satisfies

H_{w}=-(n/r^{2})\cdot{\bm{w}}

for any
w\in M
, where
{\bm{w}}
is the position vector of
F(w)
.
We show the mean curvature vector of
\Phi:G_{2}/SO(4)\to\mathfrak{p}
satisfies this equation.

Since
\Phi
is an equivariant isometric immersion, it suffices to consider at the point
\eta=\Phi(o)
. We identify
T_{o}M
with
\widetilde{\mathfrak{m}}\subset T_{\eta}\mathcal{O}_{\eta}
as described above.
We put
g_{ij}:=\langle\widetilde{V}_{i},\widetilde{V}_{j}\rangle
, and denote the inverse matrix of
(g_{ij})_{i,j=7,\ldots,14}
by
(g^{ij})_{i,j=7,\ldots,14}
. Since
\iota:G_{2}/SO(4)\to SO(7)/S(O(3)\times O(4))
is a totally geodesic immersion, the mean curvature vector
H_{\eta}
of
\Phi=\varphi\circ\iota:G_{2}/SO(4)\to\mathfrak{p}
at
\eta
is expressed by

H_{\eta}=\sum_{i,j=7}^{14}g^{ij}B_{\varphi}(\widetilde{V}_{i},\widetilde{V}_{j}),

where
B_{\varphi}
is the second fundamental form of the symmetric R-space
\varphi:SO(7)/S(O(3)\times O(4))\to\mathfrak{p}
.
Since (
55
) is an orthogonal decomposition, we actually have

(56)

\displaystyle H_{\eta}=\sum_{i,j=7}^{8}g^{ij}B_{\varphi}(\widetilde{V}_{i},\widetilde{V}_{j})+\sum_{i,j=9}^{10}g^{ij}B_{\varphi}(\widetilde{V}_{i},\widetilde{V}_{j})+\sum_{i,j=11}^{12}g^{ij}B_{\varphi}(\widetilde{V}_{i},\widetilde{V}_{j})+\sum_{i,j=13}^{14}g^{ij}B_{\varphi}(\widetilde{V}_{i},\widetilde{V}_{j}).

We shall compute the first term. Recall that
\widetilde{\mathfrak{a}}_{1}
is a subspace in
T_{\eta}\mathcal{O}_{\eta}
spanned by

\widetilde{V}_{7}=P_{15}-P_{37}

and

\widetilde{V}_{8}=P_{26}+P_{37}.

Since
P_{ij}
is a basis of
\mathfrak{p}_{\epsilon_{i}-\epsilon_{j}}
with
|P_{ij}|^{2}=2C
, we see that

\begin{bmatrix}g_{77}&g_{78}\\
g_{87}&g_{88}\end{bmatrix}=\begin{bmatrix}4C&-2C\\
-2C&4C\end{bmatrix}\quad{\rm and}\quad\begin{bmatrix}g^{77}&g^{78}\\
g^{87}&g^{88}\end{bmatrix}=\begin{bmatrix}\frac{1}{3C}&\frac{1}{6C}\\
\frac{1}{6C}&\frac{1}{3C}\end{bmatrix}.

Moreover, since

\widetilde{\mathfrak{a}}_{1}\subset\mathfrak{p}_{S_{1}}

and
S_{1}
consists of strongly orthogonal roots, Lemma
5.3
and the fact that
\alpha(\eta)=1
for any
\alpha\in\Sigma_{>0}
(Lemma
5.4
(ii)) show that

\displaystyle\sum_{i,j=7}^{8}g^{ij}B_{\varphi}(\widetilde{V}_{i},\widetilde{V}_{j})

\displaystyle=\sum_{i,j=7}^{8}g^{ij}\sum_{\alpha\in S_{1}}-\langle(\widetilde{V}_{i})_{\alpha},(\widetilde{V}_{j})_{\alpha}\rangle\alpha^{\sharp}

\displaystyle=-g^{77}\{|P_{15}|^{2}(\epsilon_{1}-\epsilon_{5})^{\sharp}+|P_{37}|^{2}(\epsilon_{3}-\epsilon_{7})^{\sharp}\}-2g^{78}\{-|P_{37}|^{2}(\epsilon_{3}-\epsilon_{7})^{\sharp}\}

\displaystyle\quad-g^{88}\{|P_{26}|^{2}(\epsilon_{2}-\epsilon_{6})^{\sharp}+|P_{37}|^{2}(\epsilon_{3}-\epsilon_{7})^{\sharp}\}

\displaystyle=-\frac{2}{3}\{(\epsilon_{1}-\epsilon_{5})^{\sharp}+(\epsilon_{2}-\epsilon_{6})^{\sharp}+(\epsilon_{3}-\epsilon_{7})^{\sharp}\}

\displaystyle=-\frac{2}{3}\sum_{\alpha\in S_{1}}\alpha^{\sharp}.

We obtain similar results for the latter three terms in (
56
). Namely, we have

\displaystyle H_{\eta}=\sum_{i=1}^{4}-\frac{2}{3}\sum_{\alpha\in S_{i}}\alpha^{\sharp}=-\frac{2}{3}\sum_{\alpha\in\Sigma_{>0}}\alpha^{\sharp}=-\frac{2}{3}\Big(4\sum_{i=1}^{3}\epsilon_{i}-3\sum_{j=4}^{7}\epsilon_{j}\Big)^{\sharp}.

On the other hand, by using (
49
) and (
50
) with
C=14
, we see that the element
\eta
defined by (
5.2
) is expressed by

\eta=2\Big(4\sum_{i=1}^{3}\epsilon_{i}-3\sum_{j=4}^{7}\epsilon_{j}\Big)^{\sharp}.

Therefore, we obtain

H_{\eta}=-\frac{1}{3}\eta=-\frac{{\rm dim}M}{|\eta|^{2}}\cdot\eta,

since
{\rm dim}M={\rm dim}(G_{2}/SO(4))=8
and
|\eta|^{2}=24
. This shows that
\widetilde{\Phi}:G_{2}/SO(4)\to S^{26}(|\eta|)
is a minimal immersion.

Next, we show that the second fundamental form
B_{\Phi}
of
\Phi:G_{2}/SO(4)\to\mathfrak{p}
is isotropic. We consider at the point
\eta
as usual since
\Phi
is equivariant. Moreover, it is sufficient to show that
|B_{\Phi}(v,v)|
is constant for any unit vector
\widetilde{v}\in\widetilde{\mathfrak{a}}_{1}
. Indeed, for a symmetric space
G/K
with the associated Cartan decomposition
\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{k}\oplus\mathfrak{m}
, it is known that the
{\rm Ad}_{G}(K)
-action on
\mathfrak{m}
is a polar action, and a maximal abelian subspace
\mathfrak{a}
in
\mathfrak{m}
becomes its section. Namely, any element
X\in\mathfrak{m}
is expressed by
X={\rm Ad}(k)v
for some
k\in K
and
v\in\mathfrak{a}
. Since
\widetilde{\mathfrak{a}}_{1}
corresponds to a maximal abelian subspace
\mathfrak{a}_{1}
in
\mathfrak{m}
and we have an
{\rm Ad}_{U}(K)
-equivariant isomorphism between
\mathfrak{m}
and
\widetilde{\mathfrak{m}}
, we see that any element
\widetilde{X}\in\widetilde{\mathfrak{m}}
is expressed by
\widetilde{X}={\rm Ad}(k)\widetilde{v}
for some
k\in K
and
\widetilde{v}\in\widetilde{\mathfrak{a}}_{1}
. Moreover, because
{\rm Ad}(k)
is an isometry on
\mathfrak{p}
, we have

|B_{\Phi}(\widetilde{X},\widetilde{X})|^{2}=|B_{\Phi}({\rm Ad}(k)\widetilde{v},{\rm Ad}(k)\widetilde{v})|^{2}=|{\rm Ad}(k)B_{\Phi}(\widetilde{v},\widetilde{v})|^{2}=|B_{\Phi}(\widetilde{v},\widetilde{v})|^{2}.

Therefore, it is sufficient to consider elements in
\widetilde{\mathfrak{a}}_{1}
.

Recall that
\widetilde{\mathfrak{a}}_{1}
is a two-dimensional subspace in
T_{\eta}\mathcal{O}_{\eta}
which is spanned by
\widetilde{V}_{7}=P_{15}-P_{37}
and
\widetilde{V}_{8}=P_{26}+P_{37}
, and the subset
S_{1}=\{\epsilon_{1}-\epsilon_{5},\epsilon_{2}-\epsilon_{6},\epsilon_{3}-\epsilon_{7}\}
consists of strongly orthogonal roots. Moreover, by using the simple roots, we have

\epsilon_{1}-\epsilon_{5}=\sum_{i=1}^{4}(\epsilon_{i}-\epsilon_{i+1}),

and hence, the relation (
50
) shows that

|\epsilon_{1}-\epsilon_{5}|^{2}=\Big|\sum_{i=1}^{4}\frac{1}{C}H_{i}\Big|^{2}=\frac{1}{C^{2}}\sum_{i,j=1}^{4}\langle H_{i},H_{j}\rangle=\frac{2}{C},

where we used the facts
|H_{i}|^{2}=2C
and
\langle H_{i},H_{i+1}\rangle=\langle H_{i-1},H_{i}\rangle=-C
(otherwise
\langle H_{i},H_{j}\rangle=0
).
Similar computations show that

|\epsilon_{1}-\epsilon_{5}|=|\epsilon_{2}-\epsilon_{6}|=|\epsilon_{3}-\epsilon_{7}|=\sqrt{\frac{2}{C}}.

Therefore, we can apply Lemma
5.5
to obtain

(57)

\displaystyle|B_{\Phi}(v,v)|^{2}=|B_{\varphi}(v,v)|^{2}=\frac{1}{2}\cdot\frac{2}{C}=\frac{1}{C}

for any unit tangent vector
v\in\widetilde{\mathfrak{a}}_{1}
, where we used the fact that
\iota:G_{2}/SO(4)\to G_{3}(\mathbb{R}^{7})
is totally geodesic. This proves that
B_{\Phi}
is isotropic.
∎

5.4.
Proof of Theorem
5.1

Recall that when
\widetilde{F}:M^{n}\to S^{n+k-1}(r)
is a minimal immersion, Corollary
4.4
shows that
M
has the property
({\bf N}_{1})
if
F:M^{n}\to\mathbb{R}^{n+k}
satisfies the following inequality (see (
32
)):

(58)

\displaystyle{\rm Ric}>\frac{1}{2}\Big(\frac{n}{r^{2}}-\beta_{F}\Big)g.

By Proposition
5.6
(i), the symmetric space
M=G_{2}/SO(4)
is minimally immersed into
S^{26}(|\eta|)
via the map
\Phi:G_{2}/SO(4)\to\mathfrak{p}
. We shall show
F=\Phi
satisfies the inequality (
58
).

Proof of Theorem
5.1
.

We denote the induced metric on
M=G_{2}/SO(4)
via the immersion
\Phi
by
g
. Due to the construction of
\Phi
, it holds that
g=c\cdot g_{K}
for some positive constant
c
, where
g_{K}
is the standard metric on the symmetric space
M
defined by the Killing-form of
G_{2}
. We shall determine the constant
c
.

First, we derive an explicit formula of the Killing-form on
\mathfrak{g}_{2}
.
We denote the complexification of
\mathfrak{g}_{2}
and
\mathfrak{o}(7)
by
\mathfrak{g}_{2}^{\mathbb{C}}
and
\mathfrak{o}(7,\mathbb{C})
, respectively. We write the Killing forms on
\mathfrak{o}(7,\mathbb{C})
and
\mathfrak{g}_{2}^{\mathbb{C}}
by
{\bf B}_{1}
and
{\bf B}_{2}
, respectively. It is known that
{\bf B}_{1}(X,Y)=5{\rm tr}(XY)
for
X,Y\in\mathfrak{o}(7,\mathbb{C})
.

As mentioned above,
\mathfrak{g}_{2}
is realized as a subalgebra of
\mathfrak{o}(7)
. Thus, we regard
\mathfrak{g}_{2}^{\mathbb{C}}
as a Lie subalgebra of
\mathfrak{o}(7,\mathbb{C})
and set
{\bf B}_{2}^{\prime}:={\bf B}_{1}|_{\mathfrak{g}_{2}^{\mathbb{C}}}
. Since
{\bf B}_{1}
is a non-degenerate symmetric
2
-form satisfying that
{\bf B}([X,Y],Z)+{\bf B}(Y,[X,Z])=0
, so does
{\bf B}_{2}^{\prime}
on
\mathfrak{g}_{2}^{\mathbb{C}}
. Thus, Schur’s lemma implies that
{\bf B}_{2}^{\prime}
coincides with a constant multiple of
{\bf B}_{2}
. In particular, we may write
{\bf B}_{2}(X,Y)=\lambda{\rm tr}(XY)
for some constant
\lambda
, where
X,Y\in\mathfrak{g}_{2}^{\mathbb{C}}
and we regard
X,Y
as elements in
\mathfrak{o}(7,\mathbb{C})
. By a direct computation by using the basis
\{V_{1},\ldots,V_{14}\}
of
\mathfrak{g}_{2}
, we see that
{\bf B}_{2}(V_{1},V_{1}):={\rm tr}({\rm ad}(V_{1})^{2})=-16
, where
{\rm ad}(V_{1})^{2}:\mathfrak{g}_{2}\to\mathfrak{g}_{2}
is an endomorphism on
\mathfrak{g}_{2}
. On the one hand, we have

{\rm tr}(V_{1}^{2})=-4
as
V_{1}\in\mathfrak{o}(7,\mathbb{C})
. Therefore
\lambda=(-16)/(-4)=4
, and hence, we obtain
{\bf B}_{2}(X,Y)=4{\rm tr}(XY)
for any
X,Y\in\mathfrak{g}_{2}^{\mathbb{C}}\subset\mathfrak{o}(7,\mathbb{C})
. Moreover, the restriction
{\bf B}_{2}|_{\mathfrak{g}_{2}}
yields the Killing-form on
\mathfrak{g}_{2}
.

Then, the inner product on
\mathfrak{m}\subset\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{g}_{2}
defined by the Killing form
{\bf B}_{2}
is expressed by
\langle X,Y\rangle_{K}:=-{\bf B}_{2}(X,Y)=-4{\rm tr}(XY)
. By letting
X=Y=V_{7}=G_{15}-G_{37}\in\mathfrak{g}_{2}
, we see
|V_{7}|_{K}^{2}=16
. On the other hand,
V_{7}
corresponds to
\widetilde{V}_{7}=P_{15}-P_{37}
in
\widetilde{\mathfrak{m}}\subset T_{\eta}\mathcal{O}_{\eta}\subset\mathfrak{p}
, and the square norm with respect to the inner product on
\mathfrak{p}
is given by
|\widetilde{V}_{7}|^{2}_{\mathfrak{p}}=4C
. Therefore, the constant
c
is given by
c=(4C)/16=7/2
.

Since
g=c\cdot g_{K}
, the Ricci curvature
{\rm Ric}
w.r.t.
g
is the same as
{\rm Ric}_{K}
w.r.t.
g_{K}
. Recall that we have
{\rm Ric}_{K}=\frac{1}{2}g_{K}
if
M=G/K
is a semi-simple compact Riemannian symmetric space. In particular, we have
{\rm Ric}={\rm Ric}_{K}=\frac{1}{2}g_{K}=\frac{1}{2c}g=\frac{1}{7}g
. On the other hand, by Proposition
5.6
,
B_{\Phi}
is isotropic and we know that
\beta_{\Phi}=1/C=1/14
by (
57
). Therefore, we see

\frac{1}{2}\Big(\frac{n}{r^{2}}-\beta_{\Phi}\Big)=\frac{1}{2}\Big(\frac{8}{24}-\frac{1}{14}\Big)=\frac{11}{84}<\frac{12}{84}=\frac{1}{7}.

Namely, the inequality (
58
) is satisfied. Thus,
M=G_{2}/SO(4)
has property
({\bf N}_{1})
.
∎

5.5.
Remark on Ohnita’s conjecture

We remark that the above computational result also confirms Ohnita’s conjecture given in
[
25
]
for the case when
M=G_{2}/SO(4)
.

In
[
8
]
, Helgason proved that, for any irreducible compact Riemannian symmetric space
M
, there exists a totally geodesic submanifold in
M
whose sectional curvature is constant and the constant coincides with the maximum of the sectional curvature of
M
. Moreover, whenever
M\neq\mathbb{R}P^{n}
, such a totally geodesic submanifold with maximal dimension is topologically sphere and is called the
Helgason sphere
. In
[
25
]
, Ohnita showed that the Helgason sphere is actually a stable minimal submanifold in
M
. Moreover, he conjectured that, if
M
is a simply-connected irreducible compact Riemannian symmetric space, then there exists no stable rectifiable
p
-current in
M
for
1\leq p\leq m-1
or
n-m+1\leq p\leq n-1
, where
n={\rm dim}M
and
m
is the dimension of the Helgason sphere. Although a partial answer was given by himself
[
25
, Theorem 7.7]
, this conjecture is still open.

When
M=G_{2}/SO(4)
, the dimension of Helgason sphere is equal to
2
(see
[
25
]
), and hence, there is a
2
-dimensional stable minimal submanifold in
G_{2}/SO(4)
. However, we obtain the following result, which confirms Ohnita’s conjecture for
M=G_{2}/SO(4)
.

Proposition 5.9
.

There is no stable rectifiable 1-current (or
7
-current) in the compact symmetric space
G_{2}/SO(4)
.

Proof.

Apply
[
25
, Theorem 7.4]
to the minimal immersion
\widetilde{\Phi}:G_{2}/SO(4)\to S^{26}(|\eta|)
. Note that we have proved that
0<2{\rm Ric}-(n/r^{2}-\beta_{\Phi})g=2{\rm Ric}-((n-1)/r^{2}-\beta_{\widetilde{\Phi}})g
.
∎

2.2
Weights

Let

is pro-

For a weight
\sigma

and

are one-dimensional, and that the natural composition map

). This implies there exists a unique
\lambda_{\beta_{K},\sigma}\in\overline{\mathbf{F}}_{p}
, such that

, for

. By
[
HV12
, Proposition 3.16]
and the fact that

, the scalar

is zero if

, and is equal to

if

.

Proposition 3.2

We have:

(1)
. Let

For

4.1
Some recaps

In this subsection, we recall briefly the restriction of a principal series to a Borel subgroup. For readers’ convenience, we reproduce certain details below where we mainly follow the approach in
[
Vig08
]
.

For a character
\varepsilon
of
B
, consider the principal series
\text{ind}^{G}_{B}\varepsilon
. Recall that
\text{ind}^{G}_{B}\varepsilon
is reducible if and only if
\varepsilon=\eta\circ\text{det}
for some character
\eta
, and in this case it is of length two.
Evaluating an

at the identity, we get a
B
-map from the principal series to the character
\varepsilon
. Denote the kernel by
\kappa_{\varepsilon}
. Then we have a short exact sequence of

-representations:

By almost the same argument of
[

, Theorem 5]
, one shows that the

-representation
\kappa_{\varepsilon}
is irreducible. Indeed, as explained below, one may even prove
\kappa_{\varepsilon}
is irreducible as a representation of
\alpha^{\mathbb{Z}}N
. But by the same map
\Phi
below, one may verify that

. This gives irreducibility of

.

Lemma 4.1
.

The
B
-representation
\kappa_{\varepsilon}
is irreducible.

Proof.

1). We firstly identify the underlying space of
\kappa_{\varepsilon}
with
C^{\infty}_{c}(N)
.

\Phi:\kappa_{\varepsilon}\rightarrow C^{\infty}_{c}(N),f\mapsto\Phi(f),\Phi(f)(u)=f(\beta u),\forall u\in N.

\Psi:C^{\infty}_{c}(N)\rightarrow\kappa_{\varepsilon},f\mapsto\Psi(f),\Psi(f)(b\beta u)=\varepsilon(b)f(u),\Psi(f)(b)=0,\forall b\in B,u\in N.

For
b=hu_{1}\in B
where
h\in H,u_{1}\in N
, and
f\in C^{\infty}_{c}(N)
, we put
b\cdot f(u)=\varepsilon(h^{s})f((h^{-1}uh)u_{1})
, where

denotes
\beta h\beta
. This gives
C^{\infty}_{c}(N)
a structure of
B
-representation. We check easily that
\Psi
and
\Phi
are both
B
-equivariant, and are inverse to each other.

2). We modify the argument of
[
Ly15
, Proposition 5.2]
to our case. Let
V
be a non-zero
B
-stable subspace of
C^{\infty}_{c}(N)
, and
f
be a non-zero function in
V
. As
f
is compactly supported and
N
has the decreasing open compact cover
(N_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}
, we may assume the support of
f
is contained in
N_{k}
for some integer
k
. Write
V_{k}
for the subspace of

consisting of functions supported in
N_{k}
, we have
V_{k}\neq 0
. By
[
BL95
, Lemma 1]
, we know
V^{N_{k}}_{k}\neq 0
. This shows that

contains the characteristic function

of

.

Now for any

, we have
u\alpha^{n}\cdot 1_{N_{k}}=\varepsilon(\alpha^{-n})1_{N_{k-2n}u^{-1}}
, and as
V
is

-stable we conclude

contains
1_{N_{k-2n}u^{-1}}
. Note that
1_{N_{k-1}}=\sum_{u\in N_{k-1}/N_{k}}u^{-1}\cdot 1_{N_{k}}
, so we have
1_{N_{k-1}}\in V
. Repeating the previous process, we conclude

contains
1_{N_{k-2n-1}u^{-1}}

contains all the functions

for any

and

∎

5.1
Definition

Recall we have defined the Hecke operator

in subsection

. To define the supersingular representations, we modify it in the following way. If
\textnormal{dim}~\sigma=1
, we put

; otherwise, we put

. Note that this modification reflects the fact the group
G
has two maximal compact open subgroups, up to conjugacy.

Definition 5.1
.

An irreducible smooth
\overline{\mathbf{F}}_{p}
-representation
\pi
of

is called supersingular if it is a quotient of
\textnormal{ind}^{G}_{K}\sigma/(T_{\sigma})
, for some weight
\sigma
of

.

5.2
A key property

Let
\pi
be an irreducible smooth representation of

, and
\sigma
be a weight of

contained in

. By
[

, Theorem 1.1]
, the representation

admits Hecke eigenvalues for the spherical Hecke algebra

. This implies that the representation
\pi
is a quotient of
\text{ind}^{G}_{K}\sigma/(T_{\sigma}-\lambda)

, the representation

is supersingular if

.

Lemma 5.2

. Assume

\pi
of
G
, we have

\pi_{N}=0
,

i.e., the usual Jacquet module of
\pi
(with respect to
B
) vanishes.

Proof.

Recall that
\pi_{N}=\pi/\pi(N)
, and the space
\pi(N):=\langle\pi(u)\cdot v-v\mid u\in N,v\in\pi\rangle
is
B
-stable and non-zero. The assertion follows by Theorem
5.8
.
∎

5.5
Proof of
(2)
of Theorem
1.2

Theorem 5.10
.

Give two smooth representations
\pi
and
\pi^{\prime}
of
G
. Suppose
\pi
is supersingular. Then, we have

\textnormal{Hom}_{G}(\pi,\pi^{\prime})\cong\textnormal{Hom}_{B}(\pi,\pi^{\prime}).

Proof.

As we have one side inclusion
\textnormal{Hom}_{G}(\pi,\pi^{\prime})\hookrightarrow\textnormal{Hom}_{B}(\pi,\pi^{\prime})
, it suffices to prove any non-zero map
\phi
in
\textnormal{Hom}_{B}(\pi,\pi^{\prime})
is also a
G
-map.

We begin with a non-zero vector
v\in\pi^{I_{1,K}}
, and by replacing
v
by some other vector in the space
\langle I_{K}\cdot v\rangle
, we may assume
I_{K}
acts on
v
by a character
\chi
. As
\pi\mid_{B}
is irreducible (Theorem
5.5
), the map
\phi
is injective whence
\phi(v)\neq 0
. As
\pi^{\prime}
is smooth,
\phi(v)
is fixed by
N^{\prime}_{m_{K}+2m}
for some
m\geq 0
.

We assume
m\geq 1
, and proceed to find a non-zero vector
w\in\pi^{I_{1,K}}\cap\langle B\cdot v\rangle
such that
\phi(w)
is fixed by
N^{\prime}_{m_{K}+2m-2}
.

Case I:
S_{+}v\neq 0
.
We take
w
as
S_{+}v
, so it lies in
\pi^{I_{1,K}}\cap\langle B\cdot v\rangle
. Note that
I_{K}
acts on
w
still by the character
\chi
. Using Lemma
3.3
, we see the vector
\phi(w)=S_{+}\phi(v)
is fixed by
N^{\prime}_{m_{K}+2m-2}
.

Case II:
S_{+}v=0
and
S_{-}v\neq 0
.
We take
S_{-}v
as
w
, which lies in
\pi^{I_{1,K}}
(Lemma
3.2
). Our assumption
S_{+}v=0
means

S_{K}w=0
,

which is equivalent to

w=-\sum_{u\in(N_{n_{K}}\smallsetminus N_{n_{K}+1})/N_{n_{K}+2}}\beta_{K}u\alpha^{-1}v

An application of (
1
) gives that for
u=n(\ast,\varpi^{n_{K}}_{E}t)\in N_{n_{K}}\smallsetminus N_{n_{K}+1}

\beta_{K}u\alpha^{-1}=n(\ast,\varpi^{n_{K}}_{E}t^{-1})\alpha^{-1}h(\bar{t}^{-1})n^{\prime}(\ast,\varpi^{m_{K}+1}_{E}t^{-1})
,

hence we have
\beta_{K}u\alpha^{-1}\cdot v=\chi(h(\bar{t}^{-1}))n(\ast,\varpi^{n_{K}}_{E}t^{-1})\alpha^{-1}\cdot v
. In all we get

w=-\sum_{u\in(N_{n_{K}}\smallsetminus N_{n_{K}+1})/N_{n_{K}+2}}\chi(h(\bar{t}^{-1}))u\alpha^{-1}\cdot v\in\langle B\cdot v\rangle
,

whence that

\phi(w)=-\sum_{u\in(N_{n_{K}}\smallsetminus N_{n_{K}+1})/N_{n_{K}+2}}\chi(h(\bar{t}^{-1}))u\alpha^{-1}\cdot\phi(v)
.

This, combined with Lemma
3.3
(especially (
3
)) and Remark
3.4
, shows
\phi(w)
is fixed by
N^{\prime}_{m_{K}+2m-2}
.

Case III
:
S_{-}v=0
(whence
S_{+}v=0
). By the definition of
S_{-}
, this assumption is same as
\sum_{u\in N_{n_{K}+1}/N_{n_{K}+2}}u\alpha^{-1}\cdot v=0
. Then by almost the same argument we have

\beta_{K}v=-\sum_{u\in(N_{n_{K}+1}\smallsetminus N_{n_{K}+2})/N_{n_{K}+2}}\chi(h(\bar{t}^{-1}))u\alpha^{-1}\cdot v\in\langle B\cdot v\rangle
.

Thus

\phi(\beta_{K}v)=-\sum_{u\in(N_{n_{K}+1}\smallsetminus N_{n_{K}+2})/N_{n_{K}+2}}\chi(h(\bar{t}^{-1}))u\alpha^{-1}\cdot\phi(v)
.

We conclude that
\phi(\beta_{K}v)
is fixed by
N^{\prime}_{m_{K}+2m-2}
, using Lemma
3.3
(especially (
3
)) and Remark
3.4
. Based on this, one verifies further that, for any
u\in N_{n_{K}}/N_{n_{K}+1}
, the vector
\phi(u\beta_{K}v)
is still fixed by
N^{\prime}_{m_{K}+2m-2}
, using Lemma
3.3
again.

\bullet~S_{K}v=\sum_{u\in N_{n_{K}}/N_{n_{K}+1}}u\beta_{K}v\neq 0
. In this case, we take
S_{K}v
as
w
. As

\phi(w)=\sum_{u\in N_{n_{K}}/N_{n_{K}+1}}\phi(u\beta_{K}v)
,

we conclude that
\phi(w)
is fixed by
N^{\prime}_{m_{K}+2m-2}
by the proceeding remarks.

\bullet~S_{K}v=\sum_{u\in N_{n_{K}}/N_{n_{K}+1}}u\beta_{K}v=0
. This assumption gives us that

v\in\langle u\beta_{K}v|u\in(N_{n_{K}}\smallsetminus N_{n_{K}+1})/N_{n_{K}+1}\rangle
.

Explicitly, under this assumption we have

v=-\sum_{u\in(N_{n_{K}}\smallsetminus N_{n_{K}+1})/N_{n_{K}+1}}\chi(h(\bar{t}^{-1}))u\beta_{K}\cdot v

Consider the
K
-representation
\tau=\langle K\cdot v\rangle
. It is spanned by the set
\{v,u\beta_{K}v\mid u\in N_{n_{K}}/N_{n_{K}+1}\}
. Then we see it can be spanned by the set

\{u\beta_{K}v\mid u\in(N_{n_{K}}\smallsetminus N_{n_{K}+1})/N_{n_{K}+1}\}

Now take a weight
\sigma
contained in
\tau
, and the unique line
\sigma^{I_{1,K}}
is given by a non-zero vector which we take as our

. Then
w
is a linear combination of the elements from the above set. This implies, as before that
\phi(w)
is fixed by
N^{\prime}_{m_{K}+2m-2}

Note that in this case
w
can be chosen as
v
, but as the argument indicates there are other possibilities. This means that, under the assumption

, the vector
\phi(v)
is already fixed by the whole group
I_{1,K}

.

Note that in each case the group
I_{K}
acts as a character on the vector
w
we find. By repeating the process, we find a non-zero

on which
I_{K}
acts as a character, such that
\phi(w)
is fixed by
N^{\prime}_{m_{K}}
. Note that
\phi(w)
is automatically fixed by

, whence the vector
\phi(w)
lies in

.

We proceed to complete the proof. Recall our condition that
\pi
is supersingular. We apply Proposition
5.4
to the vector
w
. We find some

such that
S^{k}_{+}w=0
but
S^{k-1}_{+}w\neq 0
. We write the vector
S^{k-1}_{+}w
as

. Then

is a non-zero

-invariant such that

S_{+}w^{\prime}=0
. This, as we already recorded in (

), gives that

\beta_{K}w^{\prime}=-\sum_{u}u\alpha^{-2}h(\bar{t}^{-1})w^{\prime}-\sum_{u}u\alpha^{-1}h(\bar{t}^{-1})w^{\prime}

where, in the first sum
u=n(\ast,\varpi^{n_{K}+2}_{E}t)
goes through
(N_{n_{K}+2}\smallsetminus N_{n_{K}+3})/N_{n_{K}+4}
, which we denote by

; in the second sum

, which we denote by

\phi(\beta_{K}w^{\prime})=-\sum_{u\in\mathfrak{N}_{1}}u\alpha^{-2}h(\bar{t}^{-1})\phi(w^{\prime})-\sum_{u\in\mathfrak{N}_{2}}u\alpha^{-1}h(\bar{t}^{-1})\phi(w^{\prime})

But we also have
\phi(S_{+}w^{\prime})=S_{+}\phi(w^{\prime})=0
, which gives us similarly that

\beta_{K}\phi(w^{\prime})=-\sum_{u\in\mathfrak{N}_{1}}u\alpha^{-2}h(\bar{t}^{-1})\phi(w^{\prime})-\sum_{u\in\mathfrak{N}_{2}}u\alpha^{-1}h(\bar{t}^{-1})\phi(w^{\prime})

\phi(\beta_{K}w^{\prime})=\beta_{K}\phi(w^{\prime})
.

Recall that the vector

Remark 5.11
.

In early versions of this paper, we didn’t find a full proof of Theorem

is an analogue of the element

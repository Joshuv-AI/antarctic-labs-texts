Global well-posedness of the energy-critical stochastic nonlinear Schrödinger equation on the three-dimensional torus
Guopeng Li, Mamoru Okamoto, and Liying Tao

Abstract
. We study the Cauchy problem of the defocusing energy-critical stochastic nonlinear Schrödinger equation (SNLS) on the three dimensional torus, forced by an additive noise. We adapt the atomic spaces framework in the context of the energy-critical nonlinear Schrödinger equation, and employ probabilistic perturbation arguments in the context of stochastic PDEs, establishing the global well-posedness of the defocusing energy-critical quintic SNLS in the energy space. It is the first global well-posedness result for the periodic SNLS in a critical space.

35Q55, 60H15

1.
Introduction

1.1.
Stochastic nonlinear Schrödinger equation

We consider the Cauchy problem for the stochastic nonlinear Schrödinger equation (SNLS) with an additive noise:

(1.1)

\begin{cases}i\partial_{t}u+\Delta u=|u|^{4}u+\phi\xi\\
u|_{t=0}=u_{0},\end{cases}\qquad(t,x)\in\mathbb{R}_{+}\times\mathbb{T}^{3},

where
\xi(t,x)
denotes a space-time white noise on
\mathbb{R}_{+}\times\mathbb{T}^{3}
and
\phi
is a bounded operator on
L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{3})
. In this paper, we restrict our attention to the defocusing case.
Our main goal is to establish the global well-posedness in
H^{1}(\mathbb{T}^{3})
of (
1.1
)
via atomic spaces machinery.

Let us first go over the notion of the scaling-critical regularity
for the (deterministic) defocusing nonlinear Schrödinger equation (NLS) on
\mathbb{R}^{3}
:

(1.2)

i\partial_{t}u+\Delta u=|u|^{4}u.

The NLS (
1.2
) on
\mathbb{R}^{3}
is known to enjoy the following dilation symmetry:

u(t,x)\longmapsto u_{\lambda}(t,x)=\lambda^{\frac{1}{2}}u(\lambda^{2}t,\lambda x)

for
\lambda>0
. Namely, if
u
is a solution to (
1.2
) on
\mathbb{R}^{3}
, then the scaled function
u_{\lambda}
is also a solution to (
1.2
) on
\mathbb{R}^{3}
.
A direct computation yields

\|u(0)\|_{\dot{H}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}=\|u_{\lambda}(0)\|_{\dot{H}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{3})},

which shows that the
scaling-critical Sobolev index is
s_{\text{crit}}=1
.
It is also easy to check that
the energy

\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|\nabla u(t,x)|^{2}dx+\frac{1}{6}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|u(t,x)|^{6}dx

is invariant under
this dilation symmetry.
For these reasons,
we call (
1.2
) the energy critical NLS.
While there is no dilation symmetry on
\mathbb{T}^{3}
,
we still refer to (
1.2
) on
\mathbb{T}^{3}
as energy critical.

The study of energy-critical NLS has always been a challenging problem.
Especially on periodic domains,
due to the lack of dispersion,
the well-posedness problem has been notably arduous, which requires much more delicate arguments.
For the quintic NLS on
\mathbb{T}^{3}
,
the small data global well-posedness result in
H^{1}(\mathbb{T}^{3})
was done by
Herr-Tataru-Tzvetkov
[
13
]
,
where they made the first energy-critical global well-posedness result in
the setting of compact manifolds.
However, the removal of this smallness condition
is a remaining difficult issue.
Still, after substantial efforts via
profile decomposition, Ionescu-Pausader
[
15
]

proved large data global well-posedness in
H^{1}(\mathbb{T}^{3})
,
in the defocusing case, and it was the first critical result for NLS on a compact manifold.
To see more deterministic energy-critical NLS in other settings in

[
2
,
9
,
16
,
17
,
19
,
20
,
23
,
25
,
26
]

In general, the study of SNLS at critical regularities has only a handful of results; see

[
1
,
6
,
21
,
22
]
for those are the most relevant to the current work,
and see also
[
10
,
11
]
with the approach using subcritical approximation.
It is noteworthy that all of these works focus on the Euclidean space.
To the best of our knowledge,
the result presented in this work is the first study of
global result for the energy-critical SNLS in the periodic setting.

1.2.
Main result

Our main goal in this paper is to prove global well-posedness of the defocusing energy-critical SNLS (
1.1
). We say that
u
is a solution to (
1.1
) if it satisfies the following Duhamel formulation (or mild formulation):

u(t)=S(t)u_{0}-i\int_{0}^{t}S(t-t^{\prime})(|u|^{4}u)(t^{\prime})dt^{\prime}-i\int_{0}^{t}S(t-t^{\prime})\phi\xi(dt^{\prime}),

where
S(t)\coloneqq e^{it\Delta}
denotes the linear Schrödinger propagator.
The last term is known as the stochastic convolution and we denote it by

(1.3)

\Psi(t)\coloneqq-i\int_{0}^{t}S(t-t^{\prime})\phi\xi(dt^{\prime}),

see (
2.15
) below for a precise definition. The regularity of
\Psi(t)
is dictated by the nature of
\phi
. In particular, if we assume that
\phi\in\textup{HS}(L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{3});H^{s}(\mathbb{T}^{3}))
for appropriate values of
s\in\mathbb{R}
, guaranteeing that
\Psi\in C(\mathbb{R}_{+};H^{s}(\mathbb{T}^{3}))
almost surely, see Lemma
2.12
. In Subsection
2.3
, we give a further discussion on the stochastic convolution.

The following is the main result of this paper.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
\phi\in\textup{HS}(L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{3});H^{1}(\mathbb{T}^{3}))
.
Then, the defocusing energy-critical SNLS (
1.1
) is globally well-posed
in
H^{1}(\mathbb{T}^{3})
.
In particular, solutions are unique in the class

\Psi+X^{1}(\mathbb{R}_{+}),

where

X^{1}(\mathbb{R}_{+})

is defined in (
2.4
) below.

Let us briefly go over our method of the proof.
We first notice that we apply the first order expansion to
a solution to (
1.1
), which we denote as

u=v+\Psi
.
Then, by using this solution,
we now rewrite the equation (
1.1
) and

v
satisfies

(1.4)

\displaystyle\begin{cases}i\partial_{t}v+\Delta v=|v|^{4}v+e\\
v|_{t=0}=u_{0}.\end{cases}

Here,
e=e(v,\Psi)
is defined to be

(1.5)

\displaystyle\begin{split}e&:=|v+\Psi|^{4}(v+\Psi)-|v|^{4}v.\end{split}

The equation (
1.4
) can be viewed as the energy-critical NLS (
1.2
) with the perturbation
e
.

Our main approach to prove Theorem
1.1
is that we adapt the atomic spaces machinery to the stochastic setting.
Our main difficulty comes from the local theory, particularly,
the stochastic convolution does not belong to a
V^{2}
-type space,
which is a space of bounded
2
-variation functions.
See (
2.3
) below.
Indeed,
Lévy’s modulus of continuity theorem
shows that
the Brownian motion has a finite
p
-variation
if and only if
p>2
.
Hence, the stochastic convolution belongs only to a
V^{p}
-type space for
p>2
.
See also
[
7
]
.
We then notice that the proof of
[
13
, Proposition 3.5]
cannot be directly applied to terms involving stochastic objects.
However, thanks to the spatial integrability of the stochastic convolution,
we can still obtain trilinear estimates that involve stochastic terms (Proposition
2.11
).

As for the quintic-linear estimates,
we decompose the nonlinearity into three parts:
the purely deterministic contribution, the purely stochastic contribution,
and the mixed interaction terms involving both the deterministic component and the stochastic forcing term.
The machinery of atomic spaces is primarily employed to handle the purely deterministic contribution,
following the well-established results in
[
13
,
15
]
.
The purely stochastic part can be estimated directly using elementary inequalities;
see the beginning of the proof of Proposition
3.1
for details.
The mixed interaction terms constitute the main novelty of this paper.
These terms require a careful combination of atomic space techniques and stochastic calculus.
We then apply careful case-by-case analysis with Proposition
2.11
.

In the global theory,
the regularity properties of
\Psi
(Subsection
2.3
below)
and the a priori bound on the energy will allow us to invoke
the perturbation lemma from
[
15
]
(Lemma
3.5
below)
on
e
iteratively over any given target time
T>0

to construct a solution
v
to (
1.4
). Hence, we finish our proof of Theorem
1.1
.

Remark 1.2
.

Theorem
1.1
extends to
\mathbb{T}^{4}
with a slightly modification on the trilinear and
quinticlinear estimates;
see
[
26
]
for the deterministic study and then an approach similar to that of this paper applies.

The rest of the paper is organized as follows.
In Section
2
,
we introduce some notations,
recall atomic spaces machinery,
and state regularity properties of the stochastic convolution.
Then, we give a proof of local well-posedness of the perturbed NLS (
1.4
)
in Section

3
,
we also present the key perturbation lemma.
Next, we apply the perturbation lemma to show the global existence of solutions to the deterministic perturbed NLS.
Finally, Section

4

is devoted to proving Theorem
1.1
.

2.
Preliminary results and atomic spaces

In the following, we denote that
A\lesssim B
if
A\leq CB
for some constants
C>0
. We also write
A\ll B
if the implicit
constant is regarded as small.

Set

\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}f(x)dx:=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{3}}\int_{[-\pi,\pi]^{3}}f(x)dx.

We define the spatial Fourier coefficients

\widehat{f}(\zeta):=\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}e^{-i\zeta\cdot x}f(x)dx\quad\text{for }\zeta\in\mathbb{Z}^{3}.

For periodic distributions
f
,
the Fourier series expansion holds:

f(x)=\sum_{\zeta\in\mathbb{Z}^{3}}\widehat{f}(\zeta)e^{i\zeta\cdot x}.

Given
1\leq q,r\leq\infty
and a time interval
I\subset\mathbb{R}
,
we consider the mixed Lebesgue space
L^{q}(I;L^{r}(\mathbb{T}^{3}))
of space-time functions
f(t,x)
.
In the computation, we may even write
L^{q}(I;L^{r}(\mathbb{T}^{3}))=L_{t}^{q}L_{x}^{r}(I\times\mathbb{T}^{3})

and
L^{q}([0,T];L^{r}(\mathbb{T}^{3}))=L_{T}^{q}L_{x}^{r}
for
T>0
.

We fix a non-negative, even function
\psi\in C_{0}^{\infty}((-2,2))
with
\psi(s)=1
for
|s|\leq 1
,
in order to define a partition of unity:
for a dyadic number
N\geq 1
,
we set

\psi_{N}(\zeta)=\psi\Big(\frac{|\zeta|}{N}\Big)-\psi\Big(\frac{2|\zeta|}{N}\Big)\,\quad\text{for }N\geq 2,

and
\psi_{1}(\zeta)=\psi(|\zeta|)
.
We define the frequency localization operators

P_{N}
as the Fourier multiplier with symbol
\psi_{N}
.
More generally, for a set
S\subset\mathbb{Z}^{3}
,
we define
P_{S}
as the Fourier projection operator with symbol
\mathbf{1}_{S}
, where
\mathbf{1}_{S}
denotes the characteristic function of
S
.

Let
s\in\mathbb{R}
.
We denote the Fourier multiplier with the symbol
\langle\zeta\rangle^{s}
as
\langle\nabla\rangle^{s}
.
For
1\leq q\leq\infty
,
we define the Sobolev space
W^{s,q}(\mathbb{T}^{3})
as the space of all periodic distributions
f
for which the norm

\|f\|_{W^{s,q}}:=\|\langle\nabla\rangle^{s}f\|_{L^{q}}<\infty.

We write
H^{s}(\mathbb{T}^{3}):=W^{s,2}(\mathbb{T}^{3})
.
The square function estimate (see
[
24
, Theorem 5 on page 104]
, for example) yields that

(2.1)

\|f\|_{W^{s,q}}\sim\bigg\|\Big(\sum_{N\geq 1}N^{2s}|P_{N}f|^{2}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}\bigg\|_{L^{q}}

for
1<q<\infty
.

Given
1<q,r<\infty
,
s\in\mathbb{R}
, and a time interval
I
,
we define
the space

\ell^{2}L_{t}^{q}W_{x}^{s,r}(I\times\mathbb{T}^{3})

equipped with the norm

\|f\|_{\ell^{2}L_{t}^{q}W_{x}^{s,r}(I\times\mathbb{T}^{3})}:=\Big(\sum_{N\geq 1}N^{2s}\|P_{N}f\|_{L_{t}^{q}L_{x}^{r}(I\times\mathbb{T}^{3})}^{2}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

Minkowski’s integral inequality with (
2.1
) yields that

(2.2)

\|f\|_{L_{t}^{q}W_{x}^{s,r}(I\times\mathbb{T}^{3})}\lesssim\|f\|_{\ell^{2}L_{t}^{q}W_{x}^{s,r}(I\times\mathbb{T}^{3})}

for
2\leq q,r<\infty
.

Atomic spaces machinery

Throughout this section let
H
be a separable Hilbert space over
\mathbb{C}
.
We recall the well-known atomic spaces
U^{p}

and
V^{p}
machinery in this subsection.
This technique has made great progress in treating critical problems in recent years;
for instance
see those development (definition to the atomic spaces and related properties)
in
[
12
,
13
,
14
,
15
,
18
]
.

Let
\mathcal{Z}
be the set of finite partitions

-\infty<t_{0}<t_{1}<\dots<t_{K}\leq\infty
of the real line. If

t_{K}=\infty
, we use the convention that
v(t_{K}):=0
for all functions

v:\mathbb{R}\to H
.
Then, we give the same definition of
U^{p}
and
V^{p}
spaces as in
[
12
]
.

Definition 2.1
.

Let
1\leq p<\infty
. For
\{t_{k}\}_{k=0}^{K}\in\mathcal{Z}
and

\{\phi_{k}\}_{k=0}^{K-1}\subset H
with

\sum_{k=0}^{K-1}\|\phi_{k}\|_{H}^{p}=1
.
We call the piecewise
defined function
a:\mathbb{R}\to H
such that

a(t)=\sum_{k=1}^{K}\mathbf{1}_{[t_{k-1},t_{k})}(t)\phi_{k-1}

a
U^{p}
-atom,
and then we define the atomic space
U^{p}(\mathbb{R};H)
of all
functions
u:\mathbb{R}\to H
such that

u=\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\lambda_{j}a_{j}\;\quad\text{ for }\,U^{p}\text{-atoms }a_{j}\;\quad\text{ and }\quad\{\lambda_{j}\}_{j\in\mathbb{N}}\in\ell^{1}(\mathbb{N};\mathbb{C}),

with the norm

\|u\|_{U^{p}(\mathbb{R};H)}:=\inf\Big\{\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}|\lambda_{j}|:\;u=\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\lambda_{j}a_{j},\,\{\lambda_{j}\}_{j\in\mathbb{N}}\in\ell^{1}(\mathbb{N};\mathbb{C}),\;a_{j}\,\text{is $U^{p}$-atom}\Big\}.

Here,
\ell^{1}(\mathbb{N};\mathbb{C})
denotes the space of all absolutely summable
\mathbb{C}
-valued sequences.

Definition 2.2
.

Let
1\leq p<\infty
.
We define
V^{p}(\mathbb{R};H)
as the space of all functions
v:\mathbb{R}\to H
such that

(2.3)

\|v\|_{V^{p}(\mathbb{R};H)}^{p}:=\sup_{\{t_{k}\}_{k=0}^{K}\in\mathcal{Z}}\sum_{k=1}^{K}\|v(t_{k})-v(t_{k-1})\|_{H}^{p}

is finite
1
1

1

Notice that here we use the convention
v(\infty)=0
.
Furthermore, let
V^{p}_{rc}(\mathbb{R};H)
denote the closed subspace of all right-continuous functions
v:\mathbb{R}\to H
such that
\lim_{t\to-\infty}v(t)=0
, endowed with the same norm (
2.3
).

It is worth mentioning here that all the spaces defined in Definitions
2.1
and
2.2
are
Banach spaces, and
embedded into
L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R};H)
.
Moreover, for
1\leq p<q<\infty
,
we have
V^{p}_{rc}(\mathbb{R};H)\hookrightarrow U^{q}(\mathbb{R};H)
.
We refer to
[
12
]
for details.

Corresponding to the linear Schrödinger flow,
we define the space-time spaces as follows.

Definition 2.3
.

For
s\in\mathbb{R}
, we let
U^{p}_{\Delta}H^{s}
resp.
V^{p}_{\Delta}H^{s}

be the spaces of all functions
u:\mathbb{R}\to H^{s}(\mathbb{T}^{3})

is in

\|u\|_{U^{p}_{\Delta}H^{s}}=\|e^{-it\Delta}u\|_{U^{p}(\mathbb{R};H^{s}(\mathbb{T}^{3}))},\qquad\|u\|_{V^{p}_{\Delta}H^{s}}=\|e^{-it\Delta}u\|_{V^{p}(\mathbb{R};H^{s}(\mathbb{T}^{3}))}.

Next, we also define spaces that only have a small variation of the above.

Definition 2.4
.

(i)
For
s\in\mathbb{R}
, we define
\widehat{X}^{s}
as the space of all functions
u:\mathbb{R}\to H^{s}(\mathbb{T}^{3})

such that, for every
\zeta\in\mathbb{Z}^{3}
, the map
t\mapsto e^{it|\zeta|^{2}}\widehat{u}(t)(\zeta)
is in
U^{2}(\mathbb{R},\mathbb{C})
, and for which the norm

\|u\|^{2}_{\widehat{X}^{s}}:=\sum_{\zeta\in\mathbb{Z}^{3}}\langle\zeta\rangle^{2s}\|e^{it|\zeta|^{2}}\widehat{u}(t)(\zeta)\|_{U^{2}_{t}(\mathbb{R};\mathbb{C})}^{2}<\infty.

We define a Banach subspace of continuous functions in
\widehat{X}^{s}

to be
X^{s}=\widehat{X}^{s}\cap C(\mathbb{R};H^{s}(\mathbb{T}^{3}))
.

(ii)
For
s\in\mathbb{R}
, we define
\widehat{Y}^{s}
as the space of all functions
u:\mathbb{R}\to H^{s}(\mathbb{T}^{3})
such that,
for every

\zeta\in\mathbb{Z}^{3}
, the map
t\mapsto e^{it|\zeta|^{2}}\widehat{u}(t)(\zeta)
is in
V^{2}_{rc}(\mathbb{R},\mathbb{C})
, and for which the norm

\|u\|^{2}_{\widehat{Y}^{s}}:=\sum_{\zeta\in\mathbb{Z}^{3}}\langle\zeta\rangle^{2s}\|e^{it|\zeta|^{2}}\widehat{u}(t)(\zeta)\|_{V^{2}_{t}(\mathbb{R};\mathbb{C})}^{2}<\infty.

Similarly, we define
Y^{s}=\widehat{Y}^{s}\cap C(\mathbb{R};H^{s}(\mathbb{T}^{3}))
.

We note that functions in
\widehat{X}^{s}
and
\widehat{Y}^{s}

are not necessarily continuous,
such functions are,
of course, in
L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R};H^{s}(\mathbb{T}^{3}))
.
In particular,
the following embeddings hold. See
[
13
]
for details.

Proposition 2.5
.

For
s\in\mathbb{R}
,
we have

U^{2}_{\Delta}H^{s}\hookrightarrow\widehat{X}^{s}\hookrightarrow\widehat{Y}^{s}\hookrightarrow V^{2}_{\Delta}H^{s}\hookrightarrow L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R};H^{s}(\mathbb{T}^{3})).

Moreover, for

,

For

and

we define the restriction norms

\displaystyle X^{s}(I)

\displaystyle:=\{u\in C(I;H^{s}(\mathbb{T}^{3})):\ u=v|_{I}\ \text{ for some }v\in X^{s}\},

\displaystyle\|u\|_{X^{s}(I)}

\displaystyle:=\inf\{\|v\|_{X^{s}}:\ v\in X^{s}\text{ such that }u=v|_{I}\},

.
Assume that functions
u_{\ell}
and
f_{\ell}
satisfy

P_{N_{\ell}}u_{\ell}=u_{\ell},\quad P_{N_{\ell}}f_{\ell}=f_{\ell}

for
\ell=1,2,3
.
For any permutation
(i,j,k)
of
(1,2,3)
and
p>4
,
we have

(2.10)

\displaystyle\|u_{i}f_{j}f_{k}\|_{L^{2}_{t,x}(I\times\mathbb{T}^{3})}

\displaystyle\lesssim N_{2}^{\frac{3}{2}-\frac{5}{p}}\|u_{i}\|_{Y^{0}(I)}\|f_{j}\|_{L_{t,x}^{\frac{4p}{p-2}}(I\times\mathbb{T}^{3})}\|f_{k}\|_{L_{t,x}^{\frac{4p}{p-2}}(I\times\mathbb{T}^{3})},

(2.11)

\displaystyle\|f_{i}u_{j}u_{k}\|_{L_{t,x}^{2}(I\times\mathbb{T}^{3})}

\displaystyle\lesssim N_{2}^{3-\frac{10}{p}}\|f_{i}\|_{L_{t,x}^{\frac{2p}{p-4}}(I\times\mathbb{T}^{3})}\|u_{j}\|_{Y^{0}(I)}\|u_{k}\|_{Y^{0}(I)}.

Proof.

Since (
2.11
) follows from a similar calculation,
we only prove (
2.10
).
We focus on the estimate

(2.12)

\|u_{1}f_{2}f_{3}\|_{L^{2}_{t,x}(I\times\mathbb{T}^{3})}\\
\lesssim N_{2}^{\frac{3}{2}-\frac{5}{p}}\|u_{1}\|_{Y^{0}(I)}\|f_{2}\|_{L^{q}_{t,x}(I\times\mathbb{T}^{3})}\|f_{3}\|_{L^{q}_{t,x}(I\times\mathbb{T}^{3})}

for
p>4
and
q
with
\frac{1}{p}+\frac{2}{q}=\frac{1}{2}
.
The other cases, such as

\|f_{1}u_{2}f_{3}\|_{L^{2}_{t,x}}\quad\quad\text{and}\quad\quad\|f_{1}f_{2}u_{3}\|_{L^{2}_{t,x}},

are similarly handled.

We use the same argument as in
[
13
, Proposition 3.5]
.
Given a partition
\mathbb{Z}^{3}=\bigcup_{k\in\mathbb{N}}C_{k}
into cubes
C_{k}\in\mathcal{C}_{N_{2}}
of size
N_{2}
,
the output

(P_{C_{k}}u_{1})f_{2}f_{3}

are almost orthogonal in
L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{3})
.
Hence, we obtain

(2.13)

\displaystyle\|u_{1}f_{2}f_{3}\|^{2}_{L^{2}_{t,x}(I\times\mathbb{T}^{3})}

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{k\in\mathbb{N}}\|(P_{C_{k}}u_{1})f_{2}f_{3}\|^{2}_{L^{2}_{t,x}(I\times\mathbb{T}^{3})}

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{k\in\mathbb{N}}\|P_{C_{k}}u_{1}\|^{2}_{L^{p}_{t,x}(I\times\mathbb{T}^{3})}\|f_{2}\|^{2}_{L^{q}_{t,x}(I\times\mathbb{T}^{3})}\|f_{3}\|^{2}_{L^{q}_{t,x}(I\times\mathbb{T}^{3})}

for

p>4
and
q
with
\frac{1}{p}+\frac{2}{q}=\frac{1}{2}
.
Corollary
2.9
and Proposition
2.5

imply that

\|P_{C_{k}}u_{1}\|_{L^{p}_{t,x}(I\times\mathbb{T}^{3})}\lesssim N_{2}^{\frac{3}{2}-\frac{5}{p}}\|\mathbf{1}_{I}P_{C_{k}}u_{1}\|_{U^{p}_{\Delta}L^{2}}\lesssim N_{2}^{\frac{3}{2}-\frac{5}{p}}\|\mathbf{1}_{I}P_{C_{k}}u_{1}\|_{V^{2}_{\Delta}L^{2}}.

By using Corollary
2.6
and the square summability of the
Y^{0}
-norm, we have

(2.14)

\Big(\sum_{k\in\mathbb{N}}\|P_{C_{k}}u_{1}\|^{2}_{L^{p}_{t,x}(I\times\mathbb{T}^{3})}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}\lesssim N_{2}^{\frac{3}{2}-\frac{5}{p}}\|u_{1}\|_{Y^{0}(I)}.

By (
2.13
) and (
2.14
),
we obtain (
2.12
).
∎

2.3.
Regularity of stochastic convolutions

In this subsection,
we give a precise definition of the stochastic convolution
\Psi
(see (
1.3
)).
Moreover, we record its standard regularity properties.

Given two separable Hilbert spaces
H
and
K
,
we denote by
\textup{HS}(H;K)
the space of Hilbert-Schmidt operators

\phi
from
H
to
K
, endowed with the norm:

\|\phi\|_{\textup{HS}(H;K)}=\Big(\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\|\phi h_{n}\|_{K}^{2}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}},

where
\{h_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}
is an orthonormal basis of
H
.
Note that the Hilbert-Schmidt norm does not depend on the choice of
\{h_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}
.

Let
(\Omega,\mathcal{F},\mathbb{P})
be a probability space.
Let
W
be the
L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{3})
-cylindrical Wiener process given by

W(t,x,\omega)\coloneqq\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}^{3}}\beta_{n}(t,\omega)e_{n}(x),

where
\{\beta_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{Z}^{3}}
is a family of independent complex-valued Brownian motions
and
e_{n}(x)\coloneqq e^{in\cdot x},

n\in\mathbb{Z}^{3}
.
The space-time white noise
\xi
is defined as the (distributional) time derivative of
W
, i.e., formally represented as “
\xi dt=dW
”.
Here, the spatial regularity of
W
is very rough.
More precisely, for each fixed
t\geq 0
,
W(t)
belongs to
H^{-\frac{3}{2}-\varepsilon}(\mathbb{T}^{3})
almost surely for any
\varepsilon>0
.

As mentioned earlier, we apply the operator
\phi\in\textup{HS}(L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{3});H^{s}(\mathbb{T}^{3}))
for some
s\in\mathbb{R}
and consider a smoothed-out version
\phi W
.
Then,
the stochastic convolution

\Psi(t)
is defined as follows

(2.15)

\Psi(t)=-i\int_{0}^{t}S(t-t^{\prime})\phi dW(t^{\prime})=-i\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}^{3}}\phi(e_{n})\int_{0}^{t}e^{-i(t-t^{\prime})|n|^{2}}d\beta_{n}(t^{\prime}).

Next, we state the regularity properties of the stochastic
convolution.

Lemma 2.12
.

Let
T>0
and
s\in\mathbb{R}
.
Suppose that

\phi\in\textup{HS}(L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{3});H^{s}(\mathbb{T}^{3}))
.

(i)

\Psi\in C([0,T];H^{s}(\mathbb{T}^{3}))
almost surely.
Moreover, for any
1\leq p<\infty
, we have

\mathbb{E}\Big[\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}\|\Psi(t)\|^{p}_{H^{s}}\Big]\leq C\|\phi\|_{\textup{HS}(L^{2};H^{s})}^{p},

where
C=C(T,p)>0
.

(ii)

For
1\leq q<\infty
and
2\leq r<\infty
,
we have

\Psi\in L^{q}([0,T];W^{s,r}(\mathbb{T}^{3}))
almost surely.
Moreover,
for any
1\leq p<\infty
, it holds that

(2.16)

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\big[\|\Psi\|_{L^{q}_{T}W_{x}^{s,r}}^{p}\big]\leq C\|\phi\|^{p}_{\textup{HS}(L^{2};H^{s})},

where
C=C(T,p,q,r)>0
.

The continuity of the additive noise for part (i)
can be in
[
5
, Lemma 3.4]
;
and for
part (ii) one can follow a similar proof as in

[
3
, Lemma 2.8]
. We omit the proof.
Replacing
L_{T}^{q}W_{x}^{s,r}
in (
2.16
) with
\ell^{2}L_{T}^{q}W_{x}^{s,r}
yields the following lemma.

Lemma 2.13
.

Let
d\geq 1
,
T>0
, and
s\in\mathbb{R}
.
Suppose that
\phi\in\textup{HS}(L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{3});H^{s}(\mathbb{T}^{3}))
.
Then,
for any
1\leq p,q,r<\infty
, we have

\mathbb{E}\big[\|\Psi\|_{\ell^{2}L^{q}_{T}W_{x}^{s,r}}^{p}\big]\leq C\|\phi\|^{p}_{\textup{HS}(L^{2};H^{s})},

where
C=C(T,p,q,r)>0
.

Proof.

For
p\geq 2
,
Minkowski’s integral inequality
and
Lemma
2.12

yield that

\displaystyle\big\|\|\Psi\|_{\ell^{2}L^{q}_{T}W_{x}^{s,r}}\big\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\leq\big\|\|P_{N}\Psi\|_{L^{p}(\Omega;L^{q}_{T}W_{x}^{s,r})}\big\|_{\ell^{2}_{N}}

\displaystyle\leq C\big\|\|P_{N}\phi\|_{\textup{HS}(L^{2};H^{s})}\big\|_{\ell^{2}_{N}}

\displaystyle=C\|\phi\|_{\textup{HS}(L^{2};H^{s})}.

∎

3.
Energy-critical NLS with a perturbation

In this section, we first consider the following defocusing energy-critical NLS with a perturbation:

(3.1)

\begin{cases}i\partial_{t}v+\Delta v=\mathcal{N}(v+f)\\
v|_{t=t_{0}}=v_{0}\end{cases}

for some
given deterministic function
f
satisfying certain regularity conditions.
Here,
we denote the nonlinearity by

(3.2)

\displaystyle\mathcal{N}(u):=|u|^{4}u.

After proving local well-posedness for (
3.1
),
we transform (
1.1
) into the form of (
3.1
) (with
f=\Psi
) by first-order expansion. Combining this with the regularity properties of
\Psi
, we establish local well-posedness for (
1.1
).

To estimate the stochastic convolution,
we use the
Z(I)
-norm defined by

(3.3)

\displaystyle\|f\|_{Z(I)}:=\|f\|_{\ell^{2}L_{t}^{\frac{2p_{0}}{p_{0}-4}}W_{x}^{1,\frac{2p_{0}}{p_{0}-4}}(I\times\mathbb{T}^{3})},

where
p_{0}
is given by (
2.7
).
Note that

\frac{2p_{0}}{p_{0}-4}=82.

We have the following nonlinear estimate.

Proposition 3.1
.

Let

I
be an interval with
|I|\leq 1
.
Then,
for
v\in X^{1}(I)
and
f\in Z(I)
,
we have

(3.4)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \big\|\mathcal{N}(v+f)\big\|_{N^{1}(I)}&\lesssim\|v\|_{X^{1}(I)}\|v\|_{M(I)}^{4}+|I|^{\theta}\Big(\|v\|_{X^{1}(I)}^{2}\|v\|^{2}_{M(I)}\|f\|_{Z(I)}\\
&\qquad+\sum_{k=2}^{5}\|v\|_{X^{1}(I)}^{5-k}\|f\|_{Z(I)}^{k}\Big),\end{aligned}

where

\theta>0
is a constant
and

N^{1}(I)
-,
M(I)
-, and
Z(I)
-norms are defined in (
2.5
), (
2.9
), and (
3.3
), respectively.

Before we proof the Proposition
3.1
, we make the following remark to
comparing this estimate to its
deterministic counterpart.

Remark 3.2
.

As we mentioned in the introduction,
the stochastic convolution belongs only
to a
V^{p}
-type space for
p>2
.
Hence,
a separate analysis is required for those contributions involving stochastic components.
Since
the stochastic convolution has better spatial integrability,
we can use the
Z(I)
-norm to estimate the perturbation part
f
instead of the
X^{1}(I)
-norm.
Moreover,
thanks to the improved integrability,
we also obtain the factor
|I|^{\theta}
in the estimate of the parts containing
f
.

Proof of Proposition
3.1
.

We first observe that
when
f\equiv 0
.
It follows from
[
15
, Lemma 3.2]
that

\displaystyle\big\|\mathcal{N}(v)\big\|_{N^{1}(I)}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|v\|_{X^{1}(I)}\|v\|^{4}_{M(I)}.

Moreover,
when
v\equiv 0
,
Proposition
2.8
with Hölder’s inequality yields that

\displaystyle\big\|\mathcal{N}(f)\big\|_{N^{1}(I)}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|f^{5}\|_{L_{t}^{1}H_{x}^{1}(I\times\mathbb{T}^{3})}\lesssim\|f\|_{L_{t}^{5}H_{x}^{1}(I\times\mathbb{T}^{3})}\|f\|_{L_{t}^{5}L_{x}^{\infty}(I\times\mathbb{T}^{3})}^{4}\lesssim|I|^{1-5\frac{p_{0}-4}{2p_{0}}}\|f\|_{Z(I)}^{5}.

Here,
we used the bound

\|f\|_{L_{t}^{5}H_{x}^{1}(I\times\mathbb{T}^{3})}+\|f\|_{L_{t}^{5}L_{x}^{\infty}(I\times\mathbb{T}^{3})}\lesssim|I|^{\frac{1}{5}-\frac{p_{0}-4}{2p_{0}}}\|f\|_{Z(I)},

which follows from Hölder’s and Sobolev’s inequalities, (
2.2
), and (
3.3
).

Next, we focus on the cross terms, defined as those containing at least one
v
and one
f
in the nonlinearity.
By Proposition
2.8
, we have

\big\|\mathcal{N}(v+f)\|_{N^{1}(I)}\lesssim\sup_{\|g\|_{Y^{-1}(I)}=1}\bigg|\iint_{I\times\mathbb{T}^{3}}\mathcal{N}(v,f)(t,x)\overline{g(t,x)}dxdt\bigg|.

To obtain the factor
|I|^{\theta}
for some
\theta>0
,
we use the norm

\|f\|_{\widetilde{Z}(I)}:=\|f\|_{\ell^{2}L_{t}^{\frac{2q_{0}}{q_{0}-4}}W_{x}^{1,\frac{2q_{0}}{q_{0}-4}}(I\times\mathbb{T}^{3})},

where
q_{0}
is given by

(3.5)

q_{0}:=4+\frac{1}{2}.

Note that

\frac{2q_{0}}{q_{0}-4}=18<\frac{2p_{0}}{p_{0}-4}.

We need to prove the multilinear estimate

(3.6)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \bigg|\iint_{I\times\mathbb{T}^{3}}\big(\mathcal{N}(v+f)-\mathcal{N}(v)-\mathcal{N}(f)\big)\overline{g}dxdt\bigg|&\lesssim\|g\|_{Y^{-1}(I)}\Big(\|v\|_{X^{1}(I)}^{2}\|v\|_{M(I)}^{2}\|f\|_{\widetilde{Z}(I)}\\
&\quad+\sum_{k=2}^{4}\|v\|_{X^{1}(I)}^{5-k}\|f\|_{\widetilde{Z}(I)}^{k}\Big).\end{aligned}

Set

w_{0}=g
.
Let
w_{j}
denote either
v
or
f
for
j=1,\dots,5
.
We dyadically decompose

w_{j}=\sum_{N_{j}\geq 1}P_{N_{j}}w_{j}

for
j=0,\dots,5
.
We omit the conjugate signs as it does not contribute to our analysis.
From (
3.6
) and (
3.2
),
we estimate the following:

(3.7)

\displaystyle\bigg|\int_{I\times\mathbb{T}^{3}}\prod_{j=0}^{5}w_{j}dxdt\bigg|

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{N_{0},\dots,N_{5}\geq 1}\bigg|\int_{I\times\mathbb{T}^{3}}\prod_{j=0}^{5}P_{N_{j}}w_{j}dxdt\bigg|

\displaystyle=:\sum_{\mathbf{N}}S_{\mathbf{N}},

where
\mathbf{N}:=(N_{0},\dots,N_{5})
.
Note that
S_{\mathbf{N}}
becomes zero
unless the largest two frequencies are comparable.

Case A:
vvvvf
case
.

Without loss of generality, we may
assume
w_{j}=v
for
j=1,\dots,4
and
w_{5}=f

such that

N_{1}\geq N_{2}\geq N_{3}\geq N_{4}\geq 1

:

.

Combining Propositions
2.5
,
2.10
and
2.11
with (

), we obtain

\displaystyle S_{\mathbf{N}}

\displaystyle\leq\|P_{N_{0}}gP_{N_{2}}vP_{N_{5}}f\|_{L_{t,x}^{2}}\|P_{N_{1}}vP_{N_{3}}vP_{N_{4}}v\|_{L_{t,x}^{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\med(N_{0},N_{2},N_{5})^{3-\frac{10}{q_{0}}}\|P_{N_{0}}g\|_{Y^{0}(I)}\|P_{N_{2}}v\|_{Y^{0}(I)}\|P_{N_{5}}f\|_{L_{t,x}^{\frac{2q_{0}}{q_{0}-4}}(I\times\mathbb{T}^{3})}

\displaystyle\qquad\times\Big(\frac{N_{4}}{N_{1}}+\frac{1}{N_{3}}\Big)^{\delta}\|P_{N_{1}}v\|_{Y^{0}(I)}\|P_{N_{3}}v\|_{M(I)}\|P_{N_{4}}v\|_{M(I)}

\displaystyle\qquad\times\|P_{N_{0}}g\|_{Y^{-1}(I)}\|P_{N_{1}}v\|_{X^{1}(I)}\|P_{N_{2}}v\|_{X^{1}(I)}\|v\|_{M(I)}^{2}\|P_{N_{5}}f\|_{\widetilde{Z}(I)}.

Note that
N_{0}\lesssim N_{1}
and

are equivalent to
the following three cases:

Since
(
3.5
) yields

,

N_{0}^{2}N_{1}^{-1}\max(N_{0},N_{2},N_{5})^{-1}\med(N_{0},N_{2},N_{5})^{2-\frac{10}{q_{0}}}\lesssim\begin{cases}\max(N_{2},N_{5})^{2-\frac{10}{q_{0}}},&\text{if }A_{1,1},\\
\max(N_{0},N_{2})^{2-\frac{10}{q_{0}}},&\text{if }A_{1,2},\\
N_{1}^{2-\frac{10}{q_{0}}},&\text{if }A_{1,3}.\end{cases}

We therefore obtain

\sum_{N_{0}\lesssim N_{1}}S_{\mathbf{N}}\lesssim\|g\|_{Y^{-1}(I)}\|v\|_{X^{1}(I)}^{2}\|v\|_{M(I)}^{2}\|f\|_{\widetilde{Z}(I)},

which shows (
3.6
).

Subcase A
2
:
N_{0}\sim N_{5}\gg N_{1}
.

Propositions
2.10
and
2.11

) and

\displaystyle S_{\mathbf{N}}

\displaystyle\leq\|P_{N_{0}}gP_{N_{3}}vP_{N_{4}}v\|_{L_{t,x}^{2}}\|P_{N_{1}}vP_{N_{2}}vP_{N_{5}}f\|_{L_{t,x}^{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\Big(\frac{N_{3}}{N_{0}}+\frac{1}{N_{2}}\Big)^{\delta}\|P_{N_{0}}g\|_{Y^{0}(I)}\|P_{N_{3}}v\|_{M(I)}\|P_{N_{4}}v\|_{M(I)}

\displaystyle\qquad\times N_{1}^{3-\frac{10}{q_{0}}}\|P_{N_{1}}v\|_{Y^{0}(I)}\|P_{N_{2}}v\|_{Y^{0}(I)}\|P_{N_{5}}f\|_{L_{t,x}^{\frac{2q_{0}}{q_{0}-4}}(I\times\mathbb{T}^{3})}

\displaystyle\lesssim N_{1}^{2-\frac{10}{q_{0}}}\|P_{N_{0}}g\|_{Y^{-1}(I)}\|v\|_{X^{1}(I)}^{2}\|v\|_{M(I)}^{2}\|P_{N_{5}}f\|_{\widetilde{Z}(I)}.

By

we have (

).

Case B:
vvvff
case
.

Without loss of generality, we may
assume
w_{j}=v

for

and

\displaystyle S_{\mathbf{N}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\med(N_{0},N_{2},N_{5})^{3-\frac{10}{q_{0}}}\|P_{N_{0}}g\|_{Y^{0}(I)}\|P_{N_{2}}v\|_{Y^{0}(I)}\|P_{N_{5}}f\|_{L_{t,x}^{\frac{2q_{0}}{q_{0}-4}}(I\times\mathbb{T}^{3})}

\displaystyle\qquad\times\med(N_{1},N_{3},N_{4})^{3-\frac{10}{q_{0}}}\|P_{N_{1}}v\|_{Y^{0}(I)}\|P_{N_{3}}v\|_{Y^{0}(I)}\|P_{N_{4}}f\|_{L_{t,x}^{\frac{2q_{0}}{q_{0}-4}}(I\times\mathbb{T}^{3})}

\displaystyle\lesssim N_{0}^{2}\min(N_{0},N_{2},N_{5})^{-1}\max(N_{0},N_{2},N_{5})^{-1}\med(N_{0},N_{2},N_{5})^{2-\frac{10}{q_{0}}}

\displaystyle\qquad\times\|P_{N_{0}}g\|_{Y^{-1}(I)}\bigg(\prod_{j=1}^{2}\|P_{N_{j}}v\|_{X^{1}(I)}\|P_{N_{j+3}}f_{j+3}\|_{\widetilde{Z}(I)}\bigg)\|P_{N_{3}}v\|_{X^{1}(I)}.

).

Case C:
vvfff
case
.

Without loss of generality, we may

for

\displaystyle\lesssim\med(N_{0},N_{2},N_{5})^{3-\frac{10}{q_{0}}}\|P_{N_{0}}g\|_{Y^{0}(I)}\|P_{N_{2}}v\|_{Y^{0}(I)}\|P_{N_{5}}f\|_{L_{t,x}^{\frac{2q_{0}}{q_{0}-4}}(I\times\mathbb{T}^{3})}

\displaystyle N_{0}\sim N_{1}\gtrsim N_{3},\quad N_{0}\sim N_{3}\gtrsim N_{1},\quad N_{1}\sim N_{3}\gtrsim N_{0},

\displaystyle N_{1}\sim N_{2}\gtrsim N_{0},N_{3},\ \text{ or }\ N_{3}\sim N_{4}\gtrsim N_{0},N_{1}.

In particular,

N_{0}^{2}\max(N_{0},N_{2},N_{5})^{-1}\max(N_{1},N_{3},N_{4})^{-1}\lesssim 1.

Thanks to the presence of

\med(N_{0},N_{2},N_{5})^{2-\frac{10}{q_{0}}}\med(N_{1},N_{3},N_{4})^{\frac{1}{2}-\frac{5}{q_{0}}}
,
we obtain

\sum_{\mathbf{N}}S_{\mathbf{N}}\lesssim\|g\|_{Y^{-1}(I)}\|v\|_{X^{1}(I)}^{2}\|f\|_{\widetilde{Z}(I)}^{3},

which shows (
3.6
).

Case D:
vffff
case
.

This case also is similarly handled as in Case B.
We may
assume
w_{1}=v_{1}
,
and
w_{i}=f_{i}
for
i=2,3,4,5

such that

N_{1}\geq 1

and
N_{2}\geq N_{3}\geq N_{4}\geq N_{5}\geq 1
.
Proposition
2.11
with (
3.7
) yields that

\displaystyle S_{\mathbf{N}}

\displaystyle\leq\|P_{N_{0}}gP_{N_{3}}fP_{N_{5}}f\|_{L_{t,x}^{2}}\|P_{N_{1}}vP_{N_{2}}fP_{N_{4}}f\|_{L_{t,x}^{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\med(N_{0},N_{3},N_{5})^{\frac{3}{2}-\frac{5}{q_{0}}}\|P_{N_{0}}g\|_{Y^{0}(I)}\|P_{N_{3}}f\|_{L_{t,x}^{\frac{4q_{0}}{q_{0}-2}}(I\times\mathbb{T}^{3})}\|P_{N_{5}}f\|_{L_{t,x}^{\frac{4q_{0}}{q_{0}-2}}(I\times\mathbb{T}^{3})}

\displaystyle\qquad\times\med(N_{1},N_{2},N_{4})^{\frac{3}{2}-\frac{5}{q_{0}}}\|P_{N_{1}}v\|_{Y^{0}(I)}\|P_{N_{2}}f\|_{L_{t,x}^{\frac{4q_{0}}{q_{0}-2}}(I\times\mathbb{T}^{3})}\|P_{N_{4}}f\|_{L_{t,x}^{\frac{4q_{0}}{q_{0}-2}}(I\times\mathbb{T}^{3})}

\displaystyle\lesssim N_{0}^{2}\min(N_{0},N_{3},N_{5})^{-1}\max(N_{0},N_{3},N_{5})^{-1}\med(N_{0},N_{3},N_{5})^{\frac{1}{2}-\frac{5}{q_{0}}}

\displaystyle\qquad\times\min(N_{1},N_{2},N_{4})^{-1}\max(N_{1},N_{2},N_{4})^{-1}\med(N_{1},N_{2},N_{4})^{\frac{1}{2}-\frac{5}{q_{0}}}

\displaystyle\qquad\times\|P_{N_{0}}g\|_{Y^{-1}(I)}\|P_{N_{1}}v\|_{X^{1}(I)}\bigg(\prod_{j=2}^{5}\|P_{N_{j}}f\|_{\widetilde{Z}(I)}\bigg).

We need to consider the following five cases:

\displaystyle N_{0}\sim N_{1}\gtrsim N_{2},\quad N_{0}\sim N_{2}\gtrsim N_{1},\quad N_{1}\sim N_{2}\gtrsim N_{0},\ \text{ or }\ N_{2}\sim N_{3}\gtrsim N_{0},N_{1}.

In all the cases,
we have

N_{0}\lesssim\max(N_{1},N_{2}).

In particular,

N_{0}^{2}\max(N_{0},N_{3},N_{5})^{-1}\max(N_{1},N_{2},N_{4})^{-1}\lesssim 1.

Thanks to the presence of

\med(N_{0},N_{3},N_{5})^{\frac{1}{2}-\frac{5}{q_{0}}}\med(N_{1},N_{2},N_{4})^{\frac{1}{2}-\frac{5}{q_{0}}}
,
we obtain

\sum_{\mathbf{N}}S_{\mathbf{N}}\lesssim\|g\|_{Y^{-1}(I)}\|v\|_{X^{1}(I)}\|f\|_{\widetilde{Z}(I)}^{4},

which shows (
3.6
).
∎

3.1.
Local well-posedness of the perturbed NLS

We are now ready to present the local well-posedness of the perturbed NLS.
A similar proof can be found in
[
1
, Proposition 6.3]
.
See also
[
15
, Proposition 3.3]
.

Proposition 3.3
.

Let

I=[t_{0},t_{1}]\subset\mathbb{R}
with
|I|\leq 1
.
Suppose that

\displaystyle\|v_{0}\|_{H^{1}(\mathbb{T}^{3})}\leq R\quad\text{and}\quad\|f\|_{L^{\infty}_{t}H^{1}_{x}(I\times\mathbb{T}^{3})}\leq M

for some
R,M>0
.
Then, there exists some small
\eta_{0}=\eta_{0}(R,M)>0

such that if

\max\big(\|S(t-t_{0})v_{0}\|_{\widetilde{X}^{1}(I)},\|f\|_{Z(I)}\big)\leq\eta

for some
\eta\leq\eta_{0}
,
then
there exists a unique solution

v\in X^{1}(I)

to (
3.1
). Moreover, we have

(3.8)

\|v-S(t-t_{0})v_{0}\|_{X^{1}(I)}\lesssim\eta,

where the implicit constant is independent of
R
and
M
.

Proof.

Set

B_{\widetilde{R},\eta}:=\{v\in X^{1}(I)\;:\>\|v\|_{X^{1}(I)}\leq 2\widetilde{R},\ \|v\|_{\widetilde{X}^{1}(I)}\leq 2\eta\}

where

\widetilde{R}:=\max(R,M,1)
.
It follows from (
2.8
) that
B_{\widetilde{R},\eta}
is a closed subset of
X^{1}(I)
with respect to the metric

d(u,v):=\|u-v\|_{X^{1}(I)}.

We define the solution map
\Gamma
of (
3.1
) by

\Gamma(v)(t)\coloneqq S(t-t_{0})v_{0}-i\int_{0}^{t}S(t-t^{\prime})\mathcal{N}(v+f)(t^{\prime})dt^{\prime}.

We show that the map
\Gamma

is contraction on
(B_{\widetilde{R},\eta},d)
.

We choose
\eta_{0}\ll\widetilde{R}^{-3}
.
In particular,
we have
\eta_{0}\ll\widetilde{R}^{-1}\leq 1
.
Fix
\eta\leq\eta_{0}
in the following.
By Propositions
2.7
and
3.1
,
(
2.9
),
and Young’s inequality,
we have

(3.9)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \|\Gamma(v)\|_{X^{1}(I)}&\leq\|S(t-t_{0})v_{0}\|_{X^{1}(I)}+\|\Gamma(v)-S(t-t_{0})v_{0}\|_{X^{1}(I)}\\
&\leq\|v_{0}\|_{H^{1}}+C\big(\widetilde{R}^{3}\eta^{2}+\eta^{5}\big)\\
&<2\widetilde{R}\end{aligned}

for

v\in B_{\widetilde{R},\eta}
.
The difference
is similarly handed:

\begin{split}\|\Gamma(v_{1})-\Gamma(v_{2})\|_{X^{1}(I)}&=\big\|\mathcal{N}(v_{1}+f)-\mathcal{N}(v_{2}+f)\big\|_{N^{1}(I)}\\
&\leq C\|v_{1}-v_{2}\|_{X^{1}(I)}\sum_{j=1}^{2}\big(\|v_{1}\|_{X^{1}(I)}^{2}\|v_{j}\|_{M(I)}^{2}+\|v_{1}\|_{X^{1}(I)}^{2}\|f\|_{Z(I)}^{2}\\
&\qquad+\|v_{1}\|_{X^{1}(I)}\|f\|_{Z(I)}^{3}+\|f\|_{Z(I)}^{4}\big)\\
&\leq C\widetilde{R}^{3}\eta\|v_{1}-v_{2}\|_{X^{1}(I)}<\frac{1}{2}\|v_{1}-v_{2}\|_{X^{1}(I)}\end{split}

for

v_{1},v_{2}\in B_{\widetilde{R},\eta}
.
Moreover, with (
2.8
) and (
3.9
),
we have

\begin{split}\|\Gamma(v)\|_{\widetilde{X}^{1}(I)}&\leq\|S(t-t_{0})v_{0}\|_{\widetilde{X}^{1}(I)}+C\|\Gamma(v)-S(t-t_{0})v_{0}\|_{X^{1}(I)}\\
&<2\eta\end{split}

for

v\in B_{\widetilde{R},\eta}
.
Hence,
\Gamma
is a contraction on
(B_{\widetilde{R},\eta},d)
.
The estimate (
3.8
) follows from
the choice of
\eta_{0}

and (
3.9
).
∎

Next,
we will recall the stability theory of the defocusing energy-critical NLS.
First, we recall the following global space-time bound on the solution to the defocusing energy-critical NLS on
\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{T}^{3}
;
see
[
15
, Theorem 1.1]
.

Lemma 3.4
.

Let

w_{0}\in H^{1}(\mathbb{T}^{3})
,

t_{0}\in\mathbb{R}
,
and

w
be the solution to the defocusing energy-critical NLS

(3.10)

\begin{cases}i\partial_{t}w+\Delta w=|w|^{4}w\\
w|_{t=t_{0}}=w_{0}.\end{cases}

Then, there exists a unique global solution
w\in X^{1}(\mathbb{R})
such that
the following space-time bound holds:

\|w\|_{X^{1}(\mathbb{R})}\leq C(\|w_{0}\|_{H^{1}(\mathbb{T}^{3})}).

We also recall the following perturbation lemma from

[
15
, Proposition 3.4]
.

Lemma 3.5
.

Let
v_{0}\in H^{1}(\mathbb{T}^{3})
,
t_{0}\in\mathbb{R}
, and
I
is an open bounded interval containing
t_{0}
.
Assume
v
is a solution to the following perturbed equation

\begin{cases}i\partial_{t}v+\Delta v=|v|^{4}v+e\\
v|_{t=t_{0}}=v_{0}\end{cases}

on
I\times\mathbb{T}^{3}
,
where
e
is an given function.
Also, assume there exists some
1\leq R<\infty
such that

v
satisfies

\|v\|_{\widetilde{X}^{1}(I)}+\|v\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}H_{x}^{1}(I\times\mathbb{T}^{3})}\leq R.

Let
w_{0}\in H^{1}(\mathbb{T}^{3})
be the initial data of (
3.10
) such that the smallness condition

(3.11)

\displaystyle\|v_{0}-w_{0}\|_{H^{1}(\mathbb{T}^{3})}+\|e\|_{N^{1}(I)}\leq\varepsilon

holds for some
0<\varepsilon<\varepsilon_{0}
,
where
\varepsilon_{0}
is a small constant

0<\varepsilon_{0}=\varepsilon_{0}(R)<1
.
Then, there exists a solution
w\in X^{1}(I)
to the
defocusing energy-critical NLS (
3.10
) and

\displaystyle\|v\|_{X^{1}(I)}+\|w\|_{X^{1}(I)}\leq C(R),\quad\quad\|v-w\|_{X^{1}(I)}\leq C(R)\varepsilon,

where
C(R)
is a nondecreasing function of

.

4.
Proof of global well-posedness

In this section, we prove the global well-posedness of the defocusing energy-critical SNLS (
1.1
).
Its proof is based on
an iterative argument provided
the almost sure energy bound (Proposition
4.2
),
the global-in-time space-time bound on the solution (Lemma
3.4
),
and the stability theory (Lemma
3.5
).

Proposition
3.3
with
Lemmas
2.12
and

2.13

yields
the local well-posedness of (
1.1
).

Corollary 4.1
.

Let
\phi\in\textup{HS}(L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{3});H^{1}(\mathbb{T}^{3}))
.
Then, given any
u_{0}\in H^{1}(\mathbb{T}^{3})
, there exists an almost surely positive stopping time
T=T_{\omega}(u_{0})

and a unique local-in-time solution

u\in C([0,T];H^{1}(\mathbb{T}^{3}))
to the energy-critical SNLS (
1.1
).
In particular, solutions are unique in the class

\Psi+X^{1}([0,T]).

Then, next proposition provides a priori control on the energy
E(u(t))
defined by

(4.1)

E(u(t)):=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}|\nabla u(t,x)|^{2}dx+\frac{1}{6}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}|u(t,x)|^{6}dx.

The a priori bound on the energy follows from Ito’s lemma and the Burkholder-Davis-Gundy inequality.
Note that one requires a proper justification on the application of Ito’s lemma,
which needs to go through a certain approximation argument (with local well-posedness theory; Corollary
4.1
).
See
[
5
, Proposition 5.1]
for a similar proof on
\mathbb{T}^{d}
,
and
[
4
,
6
,
8
,
21
]
on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
.

Proposition 4.2
.

Let
\phi\in\textup{HS}(L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{3});H^{1}(\mathbb{T}^{3}))

and
u_{0}\in H^{1}(\mathbb{T}^{3})
. Let
u

be the solution to the defocusing energy-critical SNLS (
1.1
) and let
T^{\ast}=T^{\ast}(\omega,u_{0})
be the forward maximal time of existence. Then, given any
T_{0}>0
, there exists
C=C(\|u_{0}\|_{H^{1}},\|\phi\|_{\textup{HS}(L^{2};H^{1})},T_{0})>0
such that for any stopping time
T
with
0<T<\min(T^{\ast},T_{0})
almost surely, we have

\mathbb{E}\Big[\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}\big(\|u(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+E(u(t))\big)\Big]\leq C.

Finally, we have everything we need to
prove Theorem
1.1
.
While the argument is the same as the proof of Proposition 7.2 in
[
1
]
,
we give a proof here for completeness.

Proof of Theorem
1.1
.

Let
T>0
.
It follows from Lemmas
2.12
and
2.13
with (
3.3
) that

(4.2)

\|\Psi\|_{L^{\infty}_{T}H^{1}_{x}}+\|\Psi\|_{Z([0,T])}\leq M(\omega,T,\|\phi\|_{\textup{HS}(L^{2};H^{1})})=:M.

Suppose that the solution
u
to (
1.1
) exists on
[0,T]
.
Then,
it follows from (
4.1
),
(
4.2
),
and Proposition
4.2

that

(4.3)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \|v\|_{L^{\infty}_{T}H^{1}_{x}}&\leq\|u\|_{L^{\infty}_{T}H^{1}_{x}}+\|\Psi\|_{L^{\infty}_{T}H^{1}_{x}}\leq\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}\big(\|u(t)\|_{L^{2}}+E(u(t))^{\frac{1}{2}}\big)+\|\Psi\|_{L^{\infty}_{T}H^{1}_{x}}\\
&\leq C(\omega,T,\|u_{0}\|_{H^{1}},\|\phi\|_{\textup{HS}(L^{2};H^{1})})=:R.\end{aligned}

We pick any
t_{0}\in(0,T)

and suppose that the solution
v
to (
1.4
)
has already been constructed on
[0,t_{0}]
.
We are going to show
the existence of a unique solution
v

to (
1.4
) on
[t_{0},t_{0}+\tau]\cap[0,T]
with
\tau>0
independent of
v(t_{0})
.
Then, we can iterate the argument, and the
solution
v
to (
1.4
)
can be constructed on
[0,T]
.
In this way,
we prove Theorem
1.1
.

Let
w
be the global solution to
the deterministic defocusing energy-critical NLS (
3.10
)
with
w_{0}=v(t_{0})
.
By (
4.3
), we have
\|w(t_{0})\|_{H^{1}}\leq R
.
From (
2.8
) and Lemma
3.4
,
we obtain

(4.4)

\|w\|_{\widetilde{X}^{1}([t_{0},T])}\lesssim\|w\|_{X^{1}([t_{0},T])}\leq C(R).

Note that the critical
X^{1}
-norm is never small when we shrink the time interval.
However, the weaker critical
\widetilde{X}^{1}
-norm (see (
2.6
))
shrinks when the time interval gets small.
Hence,
by letting
0<\eta\ll 1
small to be chosen later,
we can divide the interval

[t_{0},T]
into
J=J(R,\eta)
subintervals
I_{j}=[t_{j},t_{j+1}]

such that

(4.5)

\|w\|_{\widetilde{X}^{1}(I_{j})}\leq\eta

for
j=0,1,\dots,J-1
.
Moreover,
by
(
4.2
), (
3.3
),
and retaking time intervals, if necessary,
we have

(4.6)

\|\Psi\|_{Z(I_{j})}\leq\eta

for
j=0,1,\dots,J-1
.

Fix
\tau>0
to be chosen later.
Write
[t_{0},t_{0}+\tau]=\bigcup_{j=0}^{J^{\prime}}([t_{0},t_{0}+\tau]\cap I_{j})

for some
J^{\prime}\leq J-1
,
where

[t_{0},t_{0}+\tau]\cap I_{j}\neq\emptyset
for
j\leq J^{\prime}

and

[t_{0},t_{0}+\tau]\cap I_{j}=\emptyset
for
j>J^{\prime}
.

One can observe from above that the nonlinear flow
w
is small on each
I_{j}
,
it follows that the linear evolution
S(t-t_{j})w(t_{j})
is also small on each
I_{j}
.
From
the Duhamel formula

S(t-t_{j})w(t_{j})=w(t)+i\int_{t_{j}}^{t}S(t-t^{\prime})(|w|^{4}w)(t^{\prime})dt^{\prime},

the nonlinear estimate (
3.4
)
and (
2.9
),
we have

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \|S(t-t_{j})w(t_{j})\|_{\widetilde{X}^{1}(I_{j})}&\leq\|w\|_{\widetilde{X}^{1}(I_{j})}+\big\||w|^{4}w\big\|_{N^{1}(I_{j})}\\
&\leq\eta+C\|w\|_{X^{1}(I_{j})}^{3}\|w\|_{\widetilde{X}^{1}(I_{j})}^{2}\end{aligned}

for
j=0,1,\dots,J-1
.
By (
4.4
) and (
4.5
),
we choose
0<\eta\ll 1
sufficiently small
such that

C\cdot C(R)^{3}\eta^{2}\leq\eta,

which yields that

(4.7)

\|S(t-t_{j})w(t_{j})\|_{\widetilde{X}^{1}(I_{j})}\leq 2\eta

for
j=0,1,\dots,J-1
.

We now consider the estimate of
v
on the first time interval

I_{0}
.
It follows from
v(t_{0})=w(t_{0})
and (
4.7
) that

(4.8)

\|S(t-t_{0})v(t_{0})\|_{\widetilde{X}^{1}(I_{0})}=\|S(t-t_{0})w(t_{0})\|_{\widetilde{X}^{1}(I_{0})}\leq 2\eta.

Let
\eta_{0}=\eta_{0}(R,M)>0
be as in Proposition
3.3
.
By Proposition
3.3

along with (
4.2
), (
4.3
), (
4.6
), and (
4.8
),
we have

(4.9)

\displaystyle\|v\|_{X^{1}(I_{0})}

\displaystyle\leq\|S(t-t_{0})v(t_{0})\|_{X^{1}(I_{0})}+\|v-S(t-t_{0})v(t_{0})\|_{X^{1}(I_{0})}

\displaystyle\leq R+C\eta\leq 2R

as long as

0<2\eta\leq\eta_{0}
.

Next, we estimate the error term
e
defined in (
1.5
).
By using Proposition
3.1
with
(
4.6
) and (
4.9
),
we have

\displaystyle\|e\|_{N^{1}(I_{0})}

\displaystyle\lesssim|I_{0}|^{\theta}\Big(\|v\|_{X^{1}(I_{0})}^{2}\sum_{k=1}^{3}\|v\|_{X^{1}(I_{0})}^{3-k}\|\Psi\|_{Z(I_{0})}^{k}+\|v\|_{X^{1}(I_{0})}\|\Psi\|_{Z(I_{0})}^{4}+\|\Psi\|_{Z(I_{0})}^{5}\Big)

\displaystyle\lesssim\tau^{\theta}\big(R^{4}+1\big).

Given
\varepsilon>0
,
by taking
\tau=\tau(R,\varepsilon)\ll 1
,
we obtain

\displaystyle\|e\|_{N^{1}(I_{0})}\leq\varepsilon.

In particular,
for
\varepsilon<\varepsilon_{0}
with
\varepsilon_{0}=\varepsilon_{0}(R)>0

dictated by Lemma
3.5
, the smallness condition (
3.11
) is
satisfied on
I_{0}
.

Therefore, all the conditions of Lemma
3.5
are satisfied on the first interval
I_{0}
,
provided that
\tau=\tau(R)
is chosen sufficiently small.
Thus, we have

\displaystyle\|v\|_{X^{1}(I_{0})}+\|w\|_{X^{1}(I_{0})}\leq C_{0}(R),\quad\quad\|v-w\|_{X^{1}(I_{0})}\leq C_{0}(R)\varepsilon.

In particular, with
C_{1}(R):=C_{0}(R)
,
we have

(4.10)

\displaystyle\|v(t_{1})-w(t_{1})\|_{H^{1}}\leq C_{1}(R)\varepsilon.

If
t_{1}\geq\tau
,
we are done.
If
t_{1}<\tau
,
we then use the iterative argument to move on the next time interval
I_{1}
.
By the triangle inequality,
Proposition
2.7
,
(
4.7
), and (
4.10
), we derive

\displaystyle\|S(t-t_{1})v(t_{1})\|_{\widetilde{X}^{1}(I_{1})}

\displaystyle\leq\|S(t-t_{1})w(t_{1})\|_{\widetilde{X}^{1}(I_{1})}+\|S(t-t_{1})(v-w)(t_{1})\|_{\widetilde{X}^{1}(I_{1})}

\displaystyle\leq 2\eta+\widetilde{C}_{1}(R)\varepsilon\leq 3\eta,

where
\varepsilon=\varepsilon(R,\eta)>0

sufficiently small such that

\widetilde{C}_{1}(R)\varepsilon<\eta
.
From (
4.2
), (
4.3
), and (
4.6
),
we can apply Proposition
3.3
on
I_{1}

as long as
3\eta\leq\eta_{0}
.
Then, we have

(4.11)

\displaystyle\|v\|_{X^{1}(I_{1})}

\displaystyle\leq\|S(t-t_{1})v(t_{1})\|_{X^{1}(I_{1})}+\|v-S(t-t_{1})v(t_{1})\|_{X^{1}(I_{1})}

\displaystyle\leq R+C\eta\leq 2R

as long as
3\eta\leq\eta_{0}
.
Moreover,
it follows
from Proposition
3.1

with (
4.6
) and (
4.11
)
that

\|e\|_{N^{1}(I_{1})}\lesssim\tau^{\theta}\big(R^{4}+1\big)\leq\varepsilon

by choosing
\tau=\tau(R,\varepsilon)>0

sufficiently small.
Therefore, all the conditions of Lemma
3.5
are satisfied on the interval
I_{1}
,
provided that
\tau=\tau(\omega,M,R,T,\varepsilon)>0

is chosen sufficiently small and that

(C_{1}(R)+1)\varepsilon<\varepsilon_{0}
.
Then, by Lemma
3.5
,
we get

\displaystyle\|v-w\|_{X^{1}(I_{1})}\leq C_{0}(R)(C_{1}(R)+1)\varepsilon=:C_{2}(R)\varepsilon.

In particular, we have

\displaystyle\|v(t_{2})-w(t_{2})\|_{H^{1}}\leq C_{2}(R)\varepsilon.

Set

C_{j}(R):=C_{0}(R)(C_{j-1}(R)+1)

for
j=2,\dots,J^{\prime}
.
We can argue inductively and obtain

\|w(t_{j})-v(t_{j})\|_{H^{1}}\leq C_{j}(R)\varepsilon

for all

j=0,\dots,J^{\prime}
,
as long as

\eta
,
\tau
, and
\varepsilon
satisfy

(J^{\prime}+2)\eta\leq\eta_{0},\quad(C_{J^{\prime}}(R)+1)\varepsilon<\varepsilon_{0},\quad\tau^{\theta}\big(R^{4}+1\big)\ll\varepsilon.

This guarantees existence of the solution
v
to (
1.4
) on

[t_{0},t_{0}+\tau]\cap[0,T]
.
This completes the proof of Theorem
1.1
.
∎

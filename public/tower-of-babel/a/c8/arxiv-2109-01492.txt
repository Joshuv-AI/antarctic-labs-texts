1.
Introduction

Throughout this paper
M
will be a complex manifold of dimension
n
with a complex structure
J\in\mathrm{End}(TM)
and
L
will be a positive line bundle over
M
with a Hermitian metric
h
such that the Chern form
\omega=c_{1}(L,h)
associated to
h
is a Kähler form.
We denote by
\nabla
the Chern connection of
(L,h)
.
We consider the
k
-th tensor power
L^{k}
of
L
.
Let
f,g
be sections of
L^{k}
.
We denote by
\langle f,g\rangle_{h^{k}}
the pointwise scalar product and
define the integral product

(f,g)_{h^{k}}=\int_{M}\langle f,g\rangle_{h^{k}}\omega_{n}

where
\omega_{n}=\omega^{n}/n!
.
We write
|f|^{2}_{h^{k}}=\langle f,f\rangle_{h^{k}}
and
\|f\|^{2}_{h^{k}}=(f,f)_{h^{k}}
.
Let
L^{2}(M,L^{k})
be the Hilbert space of square integrable sections of
L^{k}
.
We define
H^{0}_{(2)}(M,L^{k})=H^{0}(M,L^{k})\cap L^{2}(M,L^{k})
.
The Bergman kernel
K_{k}(x,y)
of
H_{(2)}^{0}(M,L^{k})
is the reproducing kernel for the Hilbert space
H^{0}_{(2)}(M,L^{k})
, that is,
f(x)=(f(\cdot),K(x,\cdot))_{h^{k}}
for any
f\in H^{0}_{(2)}(M,L^{k})
and
x\in M
.
It is a well known property that the Bergman kernel function
B_{k}(x)=|K_{k}(x,x)|_{h^{k}}
is characterized by

B_{k}(x)=\sup_{f\in H^{0}_{(2)}(M,L^{k}),\,f\neq 0}\frac{|f(x)|^{2}_{h^{k}}}{\|f\|^{2}_{h^{k}}}.

The asymptotic behavior, as
k\to+\infty
of the Bergman kernel function is studied in detail, and the asymptotic series expansion formula of
B_{k}(x)
is proved when
M
is projective
(see
[
16
]
,
[
4
]
,
[
20
]
).
Berndtsson
[
3
]
gave a simple proof for the leading order term

(1)

B_{k}(x)\sim k^{n}\quad(k\to+\infty).

In this paper, we estimate holomorphic sections in
H^{0}_{(2)}(M,L^{k})
on a Bohr-Sommerfeld Lagrangian submanifold and provide an analogue result of (
1
).

Let
X\subset M
be a Lagrangian submanifold of
(M,\omega)
, that is,
X
is a real
n
-dimensional submanifold of
M
such that
\iota^{*}\omega=0
.
Here
\iota:X\to M
is the inclusion map.
Let
\nabla^{X}
be the connection induced by
\nabla
on
\iota^{*}L
.
Then
(\iota^{*}L,\nabla^{X})
is flat since
\iota^{*}\omega=0
.
We say that
(X,\nabla^{X})
satisfies the Bohr-Sommerfeld condition if there exists a non-vanishing smooth section
\zeta\in C^{\infty}(X,\iota^{*}L)
satisfying
\nabla^{X}\zeta=0
(cf.
[
14
]
,
[
17
]
).
Hence
(X,\nabla^{X})
satisfies the Bohr-Sommerfeld condition if and only if the holonomy of
(\iota^{*}L,\nabla^{X})
is trivial.
If we take
\zeta
as
|\zeta|_{h}=1
on
X
, we call the data of
(X,\zeta)
the Bohr-Sommerfeld Lagrangian submanifold.
We define
\mathrm{Vol}(X,\omega)
by the integral of the Riemannian density induced by
\omega
on
X
.
Our first result is the following asymptotic estimate.

Theorem 1
.

Let
X\subset M
be a Lagrangian submanifold of
(M,\omega)
.
Assume that
(X,\nabla^{X})
satisfies the Bohr-Sommerfeld condition.
Then

(2)

\limsup_{k\to+\infty}\left(\frac{\mathrm{Vol}(X,\omega)}{(2k)^{n/2}}\sup_{f\in H^{0}_{(2)}(M,L^{k}),\,f\neq 0}\frac{\inf_{x\in X}|f(x)|^{2}_{h^{k}}}{\|f\|^{2}_{h^{k}}}\right)\leq 1.

We do not assume
M
is projective nor Stein in Theorem
1
.
The next result shows that (
2
) is an optimal estimate in some cases.
Let
A=\{a_{1},a_{2},\ldots,a_{N}\}
be a finite sequence of points in
M\setminus X
(possibly empty).
We denote by

H^{0}_{(2),A}(M,L^{k})
the Hilbert space of holomorphic sections
f\in H^{0}_{(2)}(M,L^{k})
which has a zero at each point
a_{j}
(
j=1,\ldots,N
).
Here, if
a\in M
occurs
l
times in
A
, then
f
vanishes to order
l-1
at
a
.

Theorem 2
.

Let
X\subset M
be a Lagrangian submanifold of
(M,\omega)
.
Assume that
(X,\nabla^{X})
satisfies the Bohr-Sommerfeld condition.
Let
A
be a finite sequence of points in
M\setminus X
.
We assume one of the following three conditions:

(i)

M
is a projective manifold.

(ii)

M
is a Stein manifold and the Ricci form
Ric(\omega)
of
\omega
satisfies

Ric(\omega)\geq-C\omega
on
M
for some
C>0
.

(iii)

M
is a pseudoconvex domain in
\mathbb{C}^{n}
.

Then

(3)

\sup_{f\in H^{0}_{(2),A}(M,L^{k}),\,f\neq 0}\frac{\inf_{x\in X}|f(x)|^{2}_{h^{k}}}{\|f\|^{2}_{h^{k}}}\sim\frac{(2k)^{n/2}}{\mathrm{Vol}(X,\omega)}\quad(k\to+\infty).

One of our motivations for Theorem
2
is to study a quantitative version of the theorem due to
[
8
]
and
[
10
]
.

Theorem 3
.

(
Theorem 2.8 of
[
10
]
)

Assume that
M
is a projective manifold.
Let
X\subset M
be a totally real submanifold and
\iota:X\to M
be the inclusion map.
The following are equivalent:

(a)

X
is rationally convex.

(b)

There exists a smooth Hodge form
\theta
for
M
such that
\iota^{*}\theta=0
.

Here the condition (a) means that
M\setminus X
is equal to a union of positive divisors of
M
.
Theorem
3
was first proved by
[
8
]
in the case of
M=\mathbb{C}^{n}
.

It would be an interesting problem whether the left hand side of (
3
) has the asymptotic series expansion.
Bohr-Sommerfeld Lagrangian submanifolds and related asymptotic series expansion formulas are studied in symplectic settings (cf.
[
14
]
).

In the proof of Theorem
1
, we use Demailly’s Jensen-Lelong formula (cf. Chapter III of
[
7
]
) with a potential function which satisfies the complex Monge-Ampère equation outside
X
.
There exists such a potential function if
X
is a real-analytic manifold (
[
11
]
).
We note that similar computations appear in
[
2
]
.
In Section
3
, we reduce our situation to the real-analytic case.

We introduce some notation.
We use the notation
f\lesssim g
to mean that
|f|\leq c|g|
for some positive number
c
which does not depend on
k
.
We write
f_{k}=O(k^{-\infty})
if
f_{k}\lesssim k^{-m}
for any
m\in\mathbb{N}
.
When
f(z_{1},\ldots,z_{n})
is a function on an open set in
\mathbb{C}^{n}
,
we write
|\partial_{z}f|=(|\frac{\partial f}{\partial z_{1}}|^{2}+\ldots+|\frac{\partial f}{\partial z_{n}}|^{2})^{1/2}
,
|\overline{\partial}_{z}f|=(|\frac{\partial f}{\partial\overline{z}_{1}}|^{2}+\ldots+|\frac{\partial f}{\partial\overline{z}_{n}}|^{2})^{1/2}
.
When
\sigma
is an
L^{k}
-valued form, we denote by
|\sigma|_{h^{k},\omega}
(resp.
\|\sigma\|_{h^{k},\omega}
) the pointwise (resp. integral) norm of
\sigma
induced by
(h^{k},\omega)
.

2.
Bohr-Sommerfeld condition

Let
X\subset M
be a Lagrangian submanifold of
(M,\omega)
and
\iota:X\to M
be the inclusion map.
We take a sufficiently small Stein tubular neighborhood
U\subset M
of
X
.
Since the de Rham cohomology class
[c_{1}(L,h)]
vanishes on
U
, there exists
l\in\mathbb{N}
such that
L|_{U}^{l}
is a trivial holomorphic line bundle (cf.
[
10
]
).
We introduce a basic property of the Bohr-Sommerfeld condition.

Proposition 1
.

The following conditions are equivalent.

(a)

(X,\nabla^{X})
satisfies the Bohr-Sommerfeld condition.

(b)

There exists a non-vanishing smooth section
s\in C^{\infty}(U,L)
which satisfies
\nabla s=0
on
X
.

(c)

For any
m\in\mathbb{N}
,
there exists a non-vanishing smooth section
s\in C^{\infty}(U,L)
such that
\log|s|^{2}_{h}=0
to order one on
X
and
\nabla^{\prime\prime}s=0
to order
m
on
X
.

(d)

There exists a non-vanishing holomorphic section
s_{0}\in H^{0}(U,L)
which satisfies

\int_{\gamma}d^{c}\log|s_{0}|^{2}_{h}\in 4\pi\mathbb{Z}
for any
\gamma\in H_{1}(X,\mathbb{Z})
.

Here
\nabla^{\prime\prime}
is the
(0,1)
-part of
\nabla
and
d^{c}
is defined by
d^{c}f(v)=-df(Jv)
for
v\in TM
.

Proof.

Assume (a).
There exists
\zeta\in C^{\infty}(X,\iota^{*}L)
such that
\nabla^{X}\zeta=0
and
|\zeta|_{h}=1
on
X
.
This implies that
L|_{U}
is a trivial smooth line bundle, and it is holomorphically trivial by the Oka principle.
We take a non-vanishing holomorphic section
s_{0}\in H^{0}(U,L|_{U})

and put
\varphi_{0}=-\log|s_{0}|^{2}_{h}
.
For any
m\in\mathbb{N}
, a smooth function
\zeta/s_{0}
on
X
can be extended to a function
\xi\in C^{\infty}(U)
with
\overline{\partial}\xi=0
to order
m
on
X
by the Hörmander-Wermer Lemma (cf.
[
13
]
, Proposition 5.55 of
[
5
]
).
By taking
U
sufficiently small, we may assume
\xi\neq 0
.
Put
s=\xi s_{0}
.
For
p\in X
, we have

(4)

\nabla s(p)=(d\xi-\xi\partial\varphi_{0})s_{0}(p)=(\partial\log(|\xi|^{2}e^{-\varphi_{0}}))s(p)=(\partial\log|s|^{2}_{h})s(p).

Since
|s|_{h}(p)=|\zeta|_{h}(p)=1
and
\nabla_{v}s=\nabla^{X}_{v}\zeta=0
for any
v\in TX
,
we have

\log|s|_{h}^{2}(p)=0,\quad\langle\partial\log|s|^{2}_{h},v\rangle=\frac{1}{2}\langle d\log|s|^{2}_{h},v\rangle+\frac{\sqrt{-1}}{2}\langle d^{c}\log|s|^{2}_{h},v\rangle=0.

This shows
(a)\Rightarrow(c)
.

On the other hand, if
s\in C^{\infty}(U,L)
satisfies the condition of (c) for
m\in\mathbb{N}
,
it follows that
\nabla s=0
on
X
by (
4
) and
(X,\nabla^{X})
satisfies the Bohr-Sommerfeld condition.
Hence (a), (b) and (c) are equivalent.

Now we prove
(a)\Rightarrow(d)
.
We take
\varphi_{0},\xi\in C^{\infty}(U)
as above.
Put
\tau=\log\xi:U\to\mathbb{C}/2\pi\sqrt{-1}\mathbb{Z}
.
We have
d\varphi_{0}(p)=2d\,\mathrm{Re}\,\tau(p)
for any
p\in X
since
d\log|s|^{2}_{h}(p)=0
.
This implies
d^{c}\varphi_{0}(p)=2d^{c}\,\mathrm{Re}\,\tau(p)=2d\,\mathrm{Im}\,\tau(p)

by the Cauchy-Riemann equation.
Let
\gamma:[0,1]\to X
be a smooth closed curve.
Then

\int_{\gamma}d^{c}\varphi_{0}=2\int_{\gamma}d\,\mathrm{Im}\,\tau=2\mathrm{Im}\,\tau(\gamma(1))-2\mathrm{Im}\,\tau(\gamma(0))\in 4\pi\mathbb{Z}

and (d) holds.

Conversely, we assume (d).
Put
\varphi_{0}=-\log|s_{0}|^{2}_{h}
and
define smooth function
g
on X by
g=\exp(\frac{1}{2}\varphi_{0}+\frac{\sqrt{-1}}{2}\int d^{c}\varphi_{0})
.
Using Hörmander-Wermer Lemma, we extend
g
to a function
\tilde{g}\in C^{\infty}(U)
with
\overline{\partial}\tilde{g}=0
to order
m\in\mathbb{N}
on
X
.
Put
\tilde{g}=e^{G}
locally.
On
TX
, we have

d(2\mathrm{Re}\,G-\varphi_{0})=d(\varphi_{0}-\varphi_{0})=0,

d^{c}(2\mathrm{Re}\,G-\varphi_{0})=2d\,\mathrm{Im}\,G-d^{c}\varphi_{0}=d\int d^{c}\varphi_{0}-d^{c}\varphi_{0}=0.

This shows that
s=\tilde{g}s_{0}\in C^{\infty}(U,L)
satisfies the condition of
(c)
.
∎

Assume there exists a non-vanishing holomorphic section
s_{l,0}\in H^{0}(U,L^{l})
.
Put
\varphi_{0}=-\frac{1}{l}\log|s_{l,0}|^{2}_{h}
.
For any
\varepsilon>0
and any
q\in\mathbb{N}
, there exists
\varphi_{0}^{\prime}\in C^{\infty}(U)
and
l^{\prime}\in\mathbb{N}

such that
|\varphi_{0}-\varphi_{0}^{\prime}|_{C^{q}}<\varepsilon
on
U
,
\varphi_{0}=\varphi_{0}^{\prime}
on
X
,
\iota^{*}dd^{c}\varphi^{\prime}_{0}=0
and that
l^{\prime}\int_{\gamma}d^{c}\varphi_{0}^{\prime}\in 4\pi\mathbb{Z}
for any
\gamma\in H_{1}(X,\mathbb{Z})
(see the proof of Lemma 3.2 of
[
8
]
).
Hence we can approximate
h
by a Hermitian metric
h^{\prime}
such that the Chern connection associated to
(ll^{\prime}L,ll^{\prime}h^{\prime})
induces the connection which satisfies the Bohr-Sommerfeld condition on
X
.

3.
Reduction to the real-analytic case

Let
M
be a complex manifold of dimension
n
.
Let
X\subset M
be a Lagrangian submanifold of
(M,\omega)
such that
(X,\nabla^{X})
satisfies the Bohr-Sommerfeld condition.
Let
U\subset M
be a sufficiently small Stein tubular neighborhood of
X
.
We take a non-vanishing section
s_{0}\in H^{0}(U,L)
and put
\varphi_{0}=-\log|s_{0}|^{2}_{h}
.
Take
m\in\mathbb{N}
to be sufficiently large
and take
s\in C^{\infty}(U,L)
which satisfies the condition of (c) in Proposition
1
, that is, one-jet of
\log|s|_{h}^{2}
vanishes on
X
and
\nabla^{\prime\prime}s=0
to order
m
on X.
We write
s=\xi s_{0}
for
\xi\in C^{\infty}(U)
.
Put
\varphi=-\log|s|_{h}^{2}
.
Let
f_{k}\in H^{0}_{(2)}(M,L^{k})
which satisfies
\|f_{k}\|_{h^{k}}=1
and let
u_{k}\in C^{\infty}(U)
such that
f_{k}=u_{k}s^{k}
on
U
.
By Whitney’s theorem (
[
19
]
), there exists a real-analytic manifold
Y
which is diffeomorphic to
X
.
Let
N
be a Stein complexification of
Y
(see
[
9
]
).
By shrinking
N
and
U
if necessary,
there exists a diffeomorphism
\Phi:N\to U
such that
\Phi(Y)=X
and that the
m
-jet of
\overline{\partial}\Phi
vanishes on
Y
(cf. Proposition 5.55 of
[
5
]
).
Put
\frac{1}{4\pi}dd^{c}(\varphi\circ\Phi)=\omega^{\prime}
and
\omega^{\prime}_{n}=(\omega^{\prime})^{n}/n!
.
We have
\omega^{\prime}=\frac{1}{4\pi}\Phi^{*}dd^{c}\varphi=\Phi^{*}\omega
on
Y

and
Y
is a Lagrangian submanifolds of
(N,\omega^{\prime})
.
We note that
\varphi\circ\Phi
is a strictly plurisubharmonic function near
Y
.
Let
d_{Y}(z)
be the distance from
z\in N
to
Y
with respect to the Riemannian metric induced by
\omega^{\prime}
.
Put
W_{k}=\{z\in N\,|\,d_{Y}(z)<\frac{2\log k}{\sqrt{k}}\}
.

Lemma 1
.

\int_{W_{k}}|\overline{\partial}(u_{k}\circ\Phi)|^{2}_{\omega^{\prime}}e^{-k\varphi\circ\Phi}\omega^{\prime}_{n}=O(k^{-m+4}).

Proof.

Assume that
k\in\mathbb{N}
is sufficiently large number.
For simplicity we abuse notation and write
|\cdot|
for the norm of a form.
Because
\overline{\partial}(u_{k}\xi^{k})=0
, we have
\overline{\partial}u_{k}=-ku_{k}\xi^{-1}\overline{\partial}\xi
.
Then

|\overline{\partial}(u_{k}\circ\Phi)|^{2}\lesssim|\partial u_{k}|^{2}|\overline{\partial}\Phi|^{2}+|\overline{\partial}u_{k}|^{2}|\overline{\partial}\overline{\Phi}|^{2}\lesssim|\partial u_{k}|^{2}|\overline{\partial}\Phi|^{2}+k^{2}|u_{k}\overline{\partial}\xi|^{2}|\partial\Phi|^{2}.

It follows that

k^{2}\int_{W_{k}}|u_{k}\overline{\partial}\xi|^{2}|\partial\Phi|^{2}e^{-k\varphi\circ\Phi}\omega^{\prime}_{n}\lesssim k^{2}\int_{\Phi(W_{k})}|u_{k}|^{2}|\overline{\partial}\xi|^{2}e^{-k\varphi}\omega_{n}=O(\frac{(\log k)^{2m}}{k^{m-2}}).

Let
\chi\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})
be a function such that
0\leq\chi\leq 1
,
\chi=1
on
(-\infty,1/2]
and that
\chi=0
on
[1,+\infty)
.
Put
\tilde{\chi}_{k}=\chi(\frac{\sqrt{k}d_{Y}}{4\log k})
.
Then

\int_{W_{k}}|\partial u_{k}|^{2}|\overline{\partial}\Phi|^{2}e^{-k\varphi\circ\Phi}\omega^{\prime}_{n}\leq\int\tilde{\chi}^{2}_{k}|\partial u_{k}|^{2}|\overline{\partial}\Phi|^{2}e^{-k\varphi\circ\Phi}\omega^{\prime}_{n}.

Denote by
I_{k}
the right hand side of the above inequality.
We have

\displaystyle I_{k}

\displaystyle\lesssim\int\tilde{\chi}^{2}_{k}|\overline{\partial}\Phi|^{2}e^{-k\varphi}\partial u_{k}\wedge\overline{\partial u_{k}}\wedge\omega^{n-1}

\displaystyle\lesssim\int\tilde{\chi}^{2}_{k}|\overline{\partial}\Phi|^{2}e^{-k\varphi}|u_{k}||\partial\overline{\partial}u_{k}|\omega_{n}+k\int\tilde{\chi}^{2}_{k}|\overline{\partial}\Phi|^{2}e^{-k\varphi}|u_{k}||\partial u_{k}|\omega_{n}

\displaystyle\qquad+\int|\partial\left((\tilde{\chi}^{2}_{k}|\overline{\partial}\Phi|^{2})\circ\Phi^{-1}\right)|e^{-k\varphi}|u_{k}||\partial u_{k}|\omega_{n}

\displaystyle\lesssim k\left(\int\tilde{\chi}^{2}_{k}|\overline{\partial}\Phi|^{2}e^{-k\varphi}|{u}_{k}|^{2}\omega_{n}+\int\tilde{\chi}^{2}_{k}|\overline{\partial}\Phi|^{2}e^{-k\varphi}|u_{k}||\partial u_{k}|\omega_{n}\right.

\displaystyle\qquad+\left.\int\tilde{\chi}_{k}|\overline{\partial}\Phi|^{2}e^{-k\varphi}|u_{k}||\partial u_{k}|\omega_{n}\right)+\int\tilde{\chi}^{2}_{k}|\overline{\partial}\Phi||D\overline{\partial}\Phi|e^{-k\varphi}|u_{k}||\partial u_{k}|\omega_{n}

where
D
is a differential operator of order one.
We have

k\int\tilde{\chi}^{2}_{k}|\overline{\partial}\Phi|^{2}e^{-k\varphi}|{u}_{k}|^{2}\omega_{n}=O(\frac{(\log k)^{2m}}{k^{m-1}}).

Put
W^{\prime}_{k}=\{w\in N\,|\,d_{Y}(w)<\frac{4\log k}{\sqrt{k}}\}
.
The Cauchy-Schwarz inequality implies

\displaystyle k\int\tilde{\chi}^{2}_{k}|\overline{\partial}\Phi|^{2}e^{-k\varphi}|u_{k}||\partial u_{k}|\omega_{n}\leq k\int\tilde{\chi}_{k}|\overline{\partial}\Phi|^{2}e^{-k\varphi}|u_{k}||\partial u_{k}|\omega_{n}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle k\left(\int_{\Phi(W^{\prime}_{k})}|\overline{\partial}\Phi|^{2}|u_{k}|^{2}e^{-k\varphi}\omega_{n}\right)^{1/2}\left(\int\tilde{\chi}^{2}_{k}|\partial u_{k}|^{2}|\overline{\partial}\Phi|^{2}e^{-k\varphi}\omega_{n}\right)^{1/2}\lesssim\frac{(\log k)^{m}}{k^{m/2-1}}I_{k}^{1/2}

and

\displaystyle\int\tilde{\chi}^{2}_{k}|\overline{\partial}\Phi||D\overline{\partial}\Phi||u_{k}||\partial u_{k}|e^{-k\varphi}\omega_{n}

\displaystyle\leq\left(\int\tilde{\chi}^{2}_{k}|D\overline{\partial}\Phi|^{2}|u_{k}|^{2}e^{-k\varphi}\omega_{n}\right)^{1/2}\left(\int\tilde{\chi}^{2}_{k}|\overline{\partial}\Phi|^{2}|\partial u_{k}|^{2}e^{-k\varphi}\omega_{n}\right)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{(\log k)^{m-1}}{k^{m/2-1/2}}I_{k}^{1/2}.

Hence there exists
c>0
which does not depend on
k
and

I_{k}\leq c\left(\frac{1}{k^{m-2}}+\frac{1}{k^{m/2-2}}I_{k}^{1/2}\right)\leq c\left(\frac{1}{k^{m-2}}+\frac{2c}{k^{m-4}}+\frac{1}{2c}I_{k}\right).

We have
I_{k}=O(k^{-m+4})
and we complete the proof.
∎

For sufficiently large
k\in\mathbb{N}
,
W_{k}
is a Stein manifold.
By regarding
u_{k}\circ\Phi
as a
\bigwedge^{n}T^{(1,0)}N|_{W_{k}}
-valued
(n,0)
-form,
there exists
v_{k}\in C^{\infty}(W_{k})
such that
\overline{\partial}v_{k}=\overline{\partial}(u_{k}\circ\Phi)
and that
\int_{W_{k}}|v_{k}|^{2}e^{-k\varphi\circ\Phi}\omega^{\prime}_{n}=O(k^{-m+4})
(cf.
[
7
]
).
Let
\beta_{k}=u_{k}\circ\Phi-v_{k}
.
Then
\beta_{k}
is a holomorphic function on
W_{k}
.
If
m>4
, we have

(5)

\limsup_{k\to+\infty}\int_{W_{k}}|\beta_{k}|^{2}e^{-k\varphi\circ\Phi}\omega^{\prime}_{n}\leq 1.

Our next task is to estimate
|v_{k}|
on
Y
.
We first estimate
u_{k}
near
X
.

Lemma 2
.

Let
r>0
be a sufficiently small number.
Let
c>0
be a positive number which is larger than the
C^{1}
-norm of
\varphi_{0}
on a neighborhood of
X
.
Let
D
be a differential operator of order one with bounded coefficients.
Then there exists
\delta>0
which does not depend on
r
and the following estimates hold:

(i)

\sup_{\{z\in M,d_{X}(z)<\delta r\}}|u_{k}|^{2}e^{-k\varphi}\lesssim e^{ckr}/r^{2n},

(ii)

\sup_{\{z\in M,d_{X}(z)<\delta r\}}|Du_{k}|^{2}e^{-k\varphi}\lesssim e^{4ckr}/r^{2+2n}+k^{2}e^{ckr}/r^{2n}.

Proof.

We take open sets
\{V_{i}\}_{i=1}^{l}
,
\{V^{\prime}_{i}\}_{i=1}^{l}
(

\|s_{k}\|^{2}_{h^{k}}\sim\frac{\mathrm{Vol}(X,\omega)}{(2k)^{n/2}}
(
k\to+\infty
) and we can prove Lemma
6
by the same argument as in Lemma
4
.
∎

Lemma 7
.

We have

|t_{k}|^{2}_{h^{k}}=O(k^{2+2n-m})

uniformly on
X
.

Proof.

Let
k\in\mathbb{N}
be a sufficiently large number.
Let
p\in X
and

(V,(z_{1},\ldots,z_{n}))
(
p\in V\subset M
) be a holomorphic local coordinate system.
Let
v_{k}\in C^{\infty}(V)
such that
t_{k}=v_{k}\xi^{k}s^{k}_{0}
.
By Lemma 15.1.8 of
[
12
]
,
we have

|v_{k}\xi^{k}(p)|^{2}\lesssim k^{-2}\sup_{B(p,k^{-1})}|\overline{\partial}_{z}(v_{k}\xi^{k})|^{2}+k^{2n}\int_{B(p,k^{-1})}|v_{k}\xi^{k}|^{2}d\mu_{\mathrm{Leb}}.

Here
B(p,r)
is the Euclidean ball of center
p
and radius
r
, and
d\mu_{\mathrm{Leb}}
is the Lebesgue measure on
V
.
We have
\overline{\partial}_{z}(v_{k}\xi^{k})=\overline{\partial}_{z}(\chi_{k}\xi^{k})
.
Let
\eta=\sup_{B(p,k^{-1})}|\varphi_{0}(p)-\varphi_{0}(z)|
.
We have
\eta=O(k^{-1})
.
Then

\displaystyle|t_{k}(p)|^{2}_{h^{k}}

\displaystyle\lesssim k^{-2}e^{k\eta}\sup_{B(p,k^{-1})}(|\overline{\partial}\chi_{k}\xi^{k}|^{2}+k^{2}|\chi_{k}\xi^{k-1}\overline{\partial}\xi|^{2})e^{-k\varphi_{0}}+k^{2n}e^{k\eta}\int_{B(p,k^{-1})}|t_{k}|^{2}_{h^{k}}\omega_{n}

\displaystyle\lesssim k^{-2}\sup_{B(p,k^{-1})}(|\overline{\partial}\chi_{k}|^{2}+k^{2}|\chi_{k}\overline{\partial}\xi|^{2})e^{-k\varphi}+O(k^{2+2n-m}).

It follows that

k^{-2}\sup_{B(p,k^{-1})}|\overline{\partial}\chi_{k}|^{2}e^{-k\varphi}\lesssim\frac{1}{k(\log k)^{2}}\sup_{B(p,k^{-1})}\large|\chi^{\prime}(\frac{\sqrt{k}d_{X}}{\log k})\large|^{2}e^{-k\pi(d_{X})^{2}}=O(k^{-\infty}),

|\chi_{k}\overline{\partial}\xi|^{2}e^{-k\varphi}\lesssim\chi_{k}(d_{X})^{2m}e^{-k\pi(d_{X})^{2}}=O(k^{-m}).

Since
X
is compact, the above estimates do not depend on
p
and the lemma is proved.
∎

If we take
m>2+2n
, we have
\lim_{k\to+\infty}|\alpha_{k}|^{2}_{h^{k}}=1
uniformly on
X
.
Hence we obtain

\liminf_{k\to+\infty}\left(\frac{\mathrm{Vol}(X,\omega)}{(2k)^{n/2}}\sup_{f\in H^{0}_{(2),A}(M,L^{k}),f\neq 0}\frac{\inf_{x\in X}|f(x)|^{2}_{h^{k}}}{\|f\|^{2}_{h^{k}}}\right)\geq 1.

This completes the proof of Theorem
2
.

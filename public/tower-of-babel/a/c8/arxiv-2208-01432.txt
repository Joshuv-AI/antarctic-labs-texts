c-ideals in complemented posets
Ivan Chajda, Miroslav Kolařík, Helmut Länger

Abstract
In their recent paper on posets with a pseudocomplementation denoted by ∗ * the first and the third author introduced the concept of a ∗ * -ideal. This concept is in fact an extension of a similar concept introduced in distributive pseudocomplemented lattices and semilattices by several authors, see References. Now we apply this concept of a c-ideal (dually, c-filter) to complemented posets where the complementation need neither be antitone nor an involution, but still satisfies some weak conditions. We show when an ideal or filter in such a poset is a c-ideal or c-filter, respectively, and we prove basic properties of them. Finally, we prove so-called Separation Theorems for c-ideals. The text is illustrated by several examples.

Now we can formulate our second Separation Theorem for distributive complemented posets with antitone complementation satisfying the Descending Chain Condition, in particular for finite distributive complemented posets with an antitone involution. Theorem 20
.

(
Second Separation Theorem
)
Let
\mathbf{P}=(P,\leq,{}^{\prime},0,1)
be a distributive complemented poset with antitone complementation satisfying the Descending Chain Condition and let
I
be an ideal and
F
an ultrafilter of
\mathbf{P}
. Then there exists some
g\in F
with
U(g)=F
. Now assume that
x\wedge g
exists for every
x\in P\setminus F
and that
I\cap F=\emptyset
. Then there exists some c-ideal
J
of
\mathbf{P}
with
I\subseteq J
and
J\cap F=\emptyset
.

Proof.

Let
a\in P\setminus F
and
b\in P
. Since
\mathbf{P}
satisfies the Descending Chain Condition we have that
F
is principal according to Lemma
3
, i.e. there exists some
g\in F
with
U(g)=F
. Because of
L(a,g)\subseteq L(a,f)
for all
f\in F
we have
UL(a,f)\subseteq UL(a,g)
for all
f\in F
and hence
\bigcup\{UL(a,f)\mid f\in F\}=UL(a,g)=UL(a\wedge g)=U(a\wedge g)
which shows that
\bigcup\{UL(a,f)\mid f\in F\}
is a filter of
\mathbf{P}
. According to Theorem
14
we conclude that
F
satisfies the c-condition. Moreover,

\displaystyle L(b^{\prime\prime})

\displaystyle=L(1,b^{\prime\prime})=L\big(U(b^{\prime},b),b^{\prime\prime}\big)=LU\big(L(b^{\prime},b^{\prime\prime}),L(b,b^{\prime\prime})\big)=LU\big(0,L(b,b^{\prime\prime})\big)=

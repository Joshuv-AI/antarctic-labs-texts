Operad and cohomology of associative algebras with generalized derivation
Jiang-Nan Xu, Yan-Hong Bao

Abstract
. An associative algebra with a generalized derivation is called an AsGDer triple. We introduce the operad that encodes AsGDer triples, and prove it is a Koszul operad. Using its Koszul dual cooperad, we introduce the homotopy version of AsGDer triples. As an application, we construct the AsGDer cohomology theory for AsGDer triples, and show that the formal deformation of an AsGDer triple is controlled by the AsGDer cohomology.

Introduction

Derivations are a fundamental invariant for various kinds of algebras,
which is closely related to cohomology theory, deformation theory, high ordered structure and so on.
As applications of algebra, derivations play an important role in differential geometry,
control theory and gauge theories in quantum field theory.

In this paper, we mainly study associative algebra with a generalized derivation.
The notion of generalized derivations was firstly introduced by Bre
\check{\rm s}
ar in
[
Br
]
,
which was used to study a certain generalization of Posner’s theorem
[
Po
]
.
Let
A
be an associative algebra and
h
a derivation of
A
.
Recall that a linear map
\delta\colon A\to A
is called a
generalized
h
-derivation
(or
generalized derivation
for short)
if
\delta(ab)=\delta(a)b+ah(b)
for all
a,b\in A
.
Such a triple
(A,h,\delta)
of an associative algebra
A
with a derivation
h
and a generalized
h
-derivation
\delta
is called an AsGDer triple.
Clearly, the notion of generalized derivations covers the notions of derivations and left multipliers (that is, a linear map
\varphi\colon A\to A

satisfying
\varphi(ab)=\varphi(a)b
for all
a,b
).
In fact, any generalized derivation can be written as a sum of left multiplier and a derivation.
The algebraic properties of generalized derivations have been extensively investigated by numerous researchers,
see
[
AC
,

,

,
Hv
,

,

,

,

,

]
,
and the concept of generalized derivations was further developed to encompass (e.g. higher order derivations)
[
Na2
]

and nonassociative settings
[
LL
]
.
In
[
HMPS
]
, the authors developed differential geometry (connection, curvature, etc.) based on generalized derivations.

Algebras with derivations are studied from operadic viewpoint,
[
DL
,
Lo
]
.
Operad theory originated from algebraic topology and have been widely used in algebra, combinatorics, geometry, mathematical
physics and topology
[
LV
,
Gi
,
MSS
]
.
Operad is an algebraic object that models the operations with multiple inputs and one output.
An algebra of a certain type is usually defined by generating operations and relations, and they can be encoded by operads.
For example, associative algebras, Lie algebras and Poisson algebras are encoded by the operad
\mathcal{A}\mathsf{s}
,
\mathcal{L}\mathsf{ie}
and
\mathcal{P}\mathsf{ois}
respectively.
This motivates us to use operad theory to study AsGDer triples.

.

Cohomology theories for various kinds of algebras have been investigated by many mathematicians.
The deformation theory of algebraic structures began with the famous work of Gerstenhaber for associative algebra.
Recently, the simultaneous deformation of algebraic operation and their additional structure, such as derivations and algebra morphisms,
are widely studied.
For examples, Lie algebras with derivations are studied from cohomological viewpoint in
[

Associative algebras and Leibniz algebras with a derivation were studied in

1.1.
Operads and cooperads

Let
\mathbb{N}\mbox{-{\rm Mod}}
be the category of
\mathbb{N}
-graded vector spaces.
For
\mathbb{N}
-graded vector spaces
\mathcal{P}=\{\mathcal{P}(n)\}_{n\geq 0}
and

\mathcal{P}\circ\mathcal{Q}
is given by

(\mathcal{P}\circ\mathcal{Q})(n):=\bigoplus_{k\geq 1}\mathcal{P}(k)\otimes\left(\bigoplus_{i_{1}+\cdots+i_{k}=n}\mathcal{Q}(i_{1})\otimes\cdots\otimes\mathcal{Q}(i_{k})\right).

Note that the composition product satisfies the associativity, and the triple
(\mathbb{N}\mbox{-{\rm Mod}},\circ,\mathcal{I})
form a monoidal category, where

\mathcal{I}
is the unit object with
\mathcal{I}(1)=\Bbbk
and
\mathcal{I}(n)=0
for all
n\neq 1
.
For an element
\theta\in{\mathcal{P}}(n)

\theta
is
n

.

Definition 1.1
.

A

is a monoid

in the monoidal category

A

is a comonoid

in the monoidal category

, that is, there is a

\Delta\colon\mathcal{C}\to\mathcal{C}\circ\mathcal{C}
, and a counit map
\varepsilon\colon\mathcal{C}\to\mathcal{I}

A nonsymmetric operad (ns operad for short) is also called a
non-
\Sigma
operad
or

.
In this paper, we will also use the partial definition of a ns

1.6.
Generalized derivation

Let
A
be an associative algebra.
Recall that a linear map
\delta\colon A\to A
is called a
generalized derivation
of
A
if
there exists a derivation
h
of
A
such that

\delta(ab)=\delta(a)b+ah(b)

for all
a,b\in A
, see
[
Br
]
. In this case,
\delta
is also called a
generalized
h
-derivation
.

Here we give some examples of generalized derivations.

Example 1.9

(
[
Br
]
)
.

Let
A
be an associative algebra.
Fixing two elements
a,b\in A
, we consider the linear map

\delta_{a,b}\colon A\to A,\quad\delta_{a,b}(x):=ax+xb,

for all
x\in A
.
Clearly,
\delta_{a,b}
is a generalized
\operatorname{ad}_{b}
-derivation.
In this case,
\delta_{a,b}
is called a
generalized inner derivation
.

Example 1.10
.

Let
V
be a vector space, and
\overline{T}(V)=\bigotimes_{n\geq 1}V^{\otimes n}
be the reduced tensor algebra with the concatenation product.
Suppose
d
and
\delta
are linear maps from
V
to
\overline{T}(V)
.
Define the linear maps
\overline{d},\overline{\delta}\colon\overline{T}(V)\rightarrow\overline{T}(V)
given by:

\displaystyle\overline{d}(v_{1}\otimes v_{2}\cdots\otimes v_{n})

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{n}v_{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes d(v_{i})\otimes\cdots\otimes v_{n},

\displaystyle\overline{\delta}(v_{1}\otimes v_{2}\cdots\otimes v_{n})

\displaystyle=\delta(v_{1})\otimes v_{2}\otimes\cdots\otimes v_{n}+\sum_{i=2}^{n}v_{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes d(v_{i})\otimes\cdots\otimes v_{n}.

It is easy to verify that
\overline{d}
is a derivation of
\overline{T}(V)
and
\overline{\delta}
is a generalized
\overline{d}
-derivation. In fact, any generalized derivation on
\overline{T}(V)
arises in this way.

Let
A
be a unital associative algebra with the identity
1_{A}
.
Suppose that
h
is a derivation of
A
and
\delta
is a generalized
h
-derivation. Then, for any
a\in A
, we have

\delta(a)=\delta(1_{A}\cdot a)=\delta(1_{A})\cdot a+h(a).

Therefore,
\delta
is uniquely determined by the element
\delta(1_{A})\in A
.

Let
A
be a non-unital associative algebra, and
\delta
be a generalized
h
-derivation of
A
.
Then the triple
(A,h,\delta)
is called an
AsGDer triple
.
Let
(A,h_{A},\delta_{A})
and
(B,h_{B},\delta_{B})
be AsGDer triples.
A
morphism
of AsGDer triples is defined to be
a homomorphism
f\colon A\to B
of associative algebras satisfying

h_{B}\circ f=f\circ h_{A},\quad\delta_{B}\circ f=f\circ\delta_{A}.

We denote by
\mathbf{AsGDer}
the category of AsGDer triples.

2.
Operads of associative algebra with generalized derivations

In this section, we construct the operad
\mathcal{A}\mathsf{g}\mathsf{d}
that encodes AsGDer triples and calculate the Koszul dual operad of
\mathcal{A}\mathsf{g}\mathsf{d}
.

2.1.
The AsGDer operad

Definition 2.1
.

Let
(E,R)
be a quadratic data, where

is an
\mathbb{N}
-graded vector space with

(E2.1.1)

\displaystyle\mu\underset{1}{\circ}\mu-\mu\underset{2}{\circ}\mu,\quad h\underset{1}{\circ}\mu-\mu\underset{1}{\circ}h-\mu\underset{2}{\circ}h\quad{\rm and}\quad\delta\underset{1}{\circ}\mu-\mu\underset{1}{\circ}\delta-\mu\underset{2}{\circ}h.

Then the quadratic operad associated to the quadratic data

is called

, denoted by

Let
A
be an
\mathcal{A}\mathsf{g}\mathsf{d}
-algebra given by the operad morphism
\gamma\colon\mathcal{A}\mathsf{g}\mathsf{d}\to\mathcal{E}\mathsf{n}\mathsf{d}_{A}
.
For convenience, we still denote the operation

\delta
and
\mu

By the definition of

,
and
\delta

Conversely, it is easily seen that an AsGDer triple

Theorem 2.2
.

The ns operad
\mathcal{A}\mathsf{g}\mathsf{d}
is Koszul.

Proof.

By Definition
2.1
, we consider the ordering of generators of
\mathcal{A}\mathsf{g}\mathsf{d}
given by

\mu<h<\delta

are

\mu\underset{1}{\circ}\mu

h\underset{1}{\circ}\mu
and
\delta\underset{1}{\circ}\mu

\mu\underset{1}{\circ}(\mu\underset{1}{\circ}\mu),\quad h\underset{1}{\circ}(\mu\underset{1}{\circ}\mu),\quad\delta\underset{1}{\circ}(\mu\underset{1}{\circ}\mu).

To prove

is Koszul, we only need to check that these critical monomials are confluent, it is well-known that
\mu\underset{1}{\circ}(\mu\underset{1}{\circ}\mu)
is confluent, for
\delta\underset{1}{\circ}(\mu\underset{1}{\circ}\mu)=(\delta\underset{1}{\circ}\mu)\underset{1}{\circ}\mu

\displaystyle\delta\underset{1}{\circ}(\underline{\mu\underset{1}{\circ}\mu})

\displaystyle\mapsto\delta\underset{1}{\circ}(\mu\underset{2}{\circ}\mu)=(\underline{\delta\underset{1}{\circ}\mu})\underset{2}{\circ}\mu\mapsto(\mu\underset{1}{\circ}\delta+\mu\underset{2}{\circ}h)\underset{2}{\circ}\mu=(\mu\underset{1}{\circ}\delta)\underset{2}{\circ}\mu+\mu\underset{2}{\circ}(\underline{h\underset{1}{\circ}\mu})

\displaystyle\mapsto(\mu\underset{1}{\circ}\delta)\underset{2}{\circ}\mu+\mu\underset{2}{\circ}(\mu\underset{1}{\circ}h)+\mu\underset{2}{\circ}(\mu\underset{2}{\circ}h)=(\mu\underset{1}{\circ}\delta)\underset{2}{\circ}\mu+(\mu\underset{2}{\circ}\mu)\underset{2}{\circ}h+(\mu\underset{2}{\circ}\mu)\underset{3}{\circ}h,

\displaystyle(\underline{\delta\underset{1}{\circ}\mu})\underset{1}{\circ}\mu

\displaystyle\mapsto(\mu\underset{1}{\circ}\delta)\underset{2}{\circ}\mu+(\mu\underset{2}{\circ}h)\underset{2}{\circ}\mu=(\mu\underset{1}{\circ}\delta)\underset{2}{\circ}\mu+\mu\underset{2}{\circ}(\underline{h\underset{1}{\circ}\mu})

\displaystyle\mapsto(\mu\underset{1}{\circ}\delta)\underset{2}{\circ}\mu+\mu\underset{2}{\circ}(\mu\underset{1}{\circ}h)+\mu\underset{2}{\circ}(\mu\underset{2}{\circ}h)=(\mu\underset{1}{\circ}\delta)\underset{2}{\circ}\mu+(\mu\underset{2}{\circ}\mu)\underset{2}{\circ}h+(\mu\underset{2}{\circ}\mu)\underset{3}{\circ}h.

\delta\underset{1}{\circ}(\mu\underset{1}{\circ}\mu)

Similarly, one can show that

is confluent. By Theorem

\mathcal{A}\mathsf{g}\mathsf{d}
is Koszul.
∎

Let

\displaystyle h(g_{1}v_{1}\otimes g_{2}v_{2}\cdots\otimes g_{n}v_{n})

Proposition 2.5

Retain the above notation. Let

\textstyle{V\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{i}
\scriptstyle{\varphi}
\textstyle{\overline{T}(\overline{\overline{V}})\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\bar{\varphi}}
\textstyle{B}

We define a linear map

as follows: for any

(i_{k},j_{k}\geq 0)
, and

for

-algebra.

4.2.

Next, we consider the cohomology of an AsGDer triple with coefficients in it’s module.

Definition 4.4

Let

\delta_{M}(ma)&=\delta_{M}(m)a+mh(a),\end{split}

It is easily seen that the

Suppose that

A
-

.

\displaystyle(a,m)\cdot(b,n)=(ab,an+mb).

Lemma 4.5
.

Let
(A,h,\delta)
be an AsGDer triple and
(M,h_{M},\delta_{M})
be an AsGDer-module over

Proof.

By

is a derivation.
For any

\displaystyle(\delta\oplus\delta_{M})((a,m)\cdot(b,n))

\displaystyle=~

\displaystyle(\delta(ab),\delta_{M}(an)+\delta_{M}(mb))

\displaystyle=~

\displaystyle(\delta(a)b+ah(b),\delta(a)n+ah_{M}(n)+\delta_{M}(m)b+mh(b))

\displaystyle=~

\displaystyle(\delta(a)b,\delta(a)n+\delta_{M}(m)b)~+~(ah(b),ah_{M}(n)+mh(b))

\displaystyle=~

\displaystyle(\delta(a),\delta_{M}(m))\cdot(b,n)~+~(a,m)\cdot(h(b),h_{M}(n))

\displaystyle=~

\displaystyle(\delta\oplus\delta_{M})(a,m)\cdot(b,n)~+~(a,m)\cdot(h\oplus h_{M})(b,n).

4.3.

Let

A

\operatorname{C}^{n}_{\operatorname{Hoch}}(A,M)=\begin{cases}0,&n=0,\\

\operatorname{C}_{\operatorname{Hoch}}^{0}(A,M)=0

rather than the space of outer derivations of

with values in

.

Recall that an associative algebra

, denoted by

,

Definition 4.7

(

Let

\partial^{\prime}\colon\operatorname{C}^{n}_{\mathrm{AssDer}}(A,M)\rightarrow\operatorname{C}^{n+1}_{\mathrm{AssDer}}(A,M)

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\partial^{\prime}(f)=(d_{\mathrm{Hoch}}(f),-\Delta(f)),&\text{ for }f\in\operatorname{C}^{1}_{\mathrm{AssDer}}(A,M)=\mathrm{Hom}(A,M),\\

\varphi_{t}\colon A[[t]]\to A[[t]]

Therefore, we may write
\varphi_{t}(a)=\sum_{i\geq 0}\varphi_{i}(a)t^{i}

\Bbbk

A formal deformation of
(A,h,\delta)
means an AsGDer triple
(A_{t},h_{t},\delta_{t})
over

A_{t}

\displaystyle h_{t}=

\displaystyle\sum_{i\geq 0}h_{i}t^{i},~~~~~h_{i}\in\mathrm{Hom}(A,A)~~\text{ and }h_{0}=h,

\displaystyle\delta_{t}=

\displaystyle\sum_{i\geq 0}\delta_{i}t^{i},~~~~~\delta_{i}\in\mathrm{Hom}(A,A)~~\text{ and }\delta_{0}=\delta.

Similarly, we may write the multiplication
\mu_{t}
of

\mu_{t}(a,b)=\sum_{i\geq 0}\mu_{i}(a,b)t^{i}

\mu_{i}\colon A\otimes A\to A

\Bbbk

By easy computing, one can obtain the following deformation equations for AsGDer triples:

(DE

-1)

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{i+j=n\atop i,j>0}

(DE

As we expected, the AsGDer cohomology controls the formal deformation of AsGDer triples.

The triple
(\mu_{1},h_{1},\delta_{1})
is called an
infinitesimal deformation
of the AsGDer triple
(A,h,\delta)
,
which gives an AsGDer triple structure on the quotient space
A[[t]]/(t^{2})

We denote
\mathrm{Ob}^{\prime}_{n}\colon A^{\otimes 3}\to A
and
\mathrm{Ob}^{\prime\prime}_{n},\mathrm{Ob}^{\prime\prime\prime}_{n}\colon A^{\otimes 2}\to A

\displaystyle\mathrm{Ob}^{\prime}_{n}(a,b,c)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i+j=n,\atop i,j>0}\mu_{i}(\mu_{j}(a,b),c)-\mu_{i}(a,\mu_{j}(b,c)),

\displaystyle\mathrm{Ob}^{\prime\prime}_{n}(a,b)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i+j=n,\atop i,j>0}h_{i}(\mu_{j}(a,b))-\mu_{i}(h_{j}(a),b)-\mu_{i}(a,h_{j}(b)),

\displaystyle\mathrm{Ob}^{\prime\prime\prime}_{n}(a,b)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i+j=n,\atop i,j>0}\delta_{i}(\mu_{j}(a,b))-\mu_{i}(\delta_{j}(a),b)-\mu_{i}(a,h_{j}(b)).

Being similar to the case of associative algebras, the formal deformation of an AsGDer will meet so-called obstructions
in the AsGDer cohomology, which are all deducible from the deformation Equations (
DEn-1
), (
DEn-2
) and (

By a routine check, we have the following result.

Theorem 4.17
.

Let
(A,h,\delta)
be an AsGDer triple and
H_{\operatorname{AsGDer}}^{n}(A,A)
be the
n
-th AsGDer cohomology group of
(A,h,\delta)
.

(i)

The triple
(\mu_{1},h_{1},\delta_{1})
is an infinitesimal deformation of
(A,h,\delta)
if and only if

(\mu_{1},h_{1},\delta_{1})
is a
2
-cocycle in the AsGDer complex of
A
. In particular, if
H_{\operatorname{AsGDer}}^{2}(A,A)=0
,
then
(A,h,\delta)
has no nontrivial formal deformation.

(ii)

For each
n\geq 2
, if the deformation equations
(DE
k
-1)
,
(DE
k
-2)
and
(DE
k
-3)
hold for
k=1,\cdots,n-1

then
(\mathrm{Ob}^{\prime}_{n},\mathrm{Ob}^{\prime\prime}_{n},\mathrm{Ob}^{\prime\prime\prime}_{n})
is a
3
-cocycle in the AsGDer complex, and hence

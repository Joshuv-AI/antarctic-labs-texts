Risk aggregation and capital allocation using a new generalized Archimedean copula
Fouad Marri, Khouzeima Moutanabbir

Abstract
In this paper, we address risk aggregation and capital allocation problems in the presence of dependence between risks. The dependence structure is defined by a mixed Bernstein copula which represents a generalization of the well-known Archimedean copulas. Using this new copula, the probability density function and the cumulative distribution function of the aggregate risk are obtained. Then, closed-form expressions for basic risk measures, such as tail value-at-risk (TVaR) and TVaR-based allocations, are derived. ​ K ​ e ​ y ​ w ​ o ​ r ​ d ​ s \emph{Keywords} : Bernstein copulas ; Capital allocation ; Copulas ; Dependence ; Tail value at risk ; Value-at-Risk

Introduction

Risk aggregation and risk-based capital allocation have attracted considerable attention in actuarial sciences and quantitative risk management over the past years. One of the crucial applications of risk aggregation is to determine the required regulatory capitals and to price insurance and reinsurance products. For this purpose, an adequate risk measure should be used to evaluate the whole level of risk for a given portfolio. An important risk measure is the Value at Risk (VaR), which is defined as a threshold value such that the probability the loss on a portfolio exceeds this value is a given probability
1-\kappa
. Another interesting risk measure is the Tail Value at Risk (TVaR), also known as the conditional tail expectation (CTE), which represents the average amount of a loss given that the loss exceeds a specified quantile. The TVaR is known to be a coherent risk measure over the space of continuous random variables (see
Dhaene et al. 2008
;
Furman and Zitikis 2008a
;
Furman and Landsman 2006
).

In this paper, we address risk aggregation and capital allocation using the TVaR risk measure. Consider a vector of
n
continuous and non-negative random variables (rv’s)
\mathbf{X}=\left(X_{1},\cdots,X_{n}\right)
, the component
X_{i}
denotes the marginal risk (claim or loss). We define the aggregate loss as
S_{n}=X_{1}+\cdots+X_{n}
. For a given confidence level
\kappa
, the value at risk of
S_{n}
,
VaR_{\kappa}(S_{n})
, is defined by

VaR_{\kappa}(S_{n})=inf\left(x\in\mathbb{R},F_{S_{n}}(x)\geq\kappa\right),

where
0<\kappa<1
and
F_{S_{n}}
is the cumulative distribution function (cdf) of
S_{n}
. We also define the tail value at risk of
S_{n}
,
TVaR_{\kappa}(S_{n})
, as follows

TVaR_{\kappa}(S_{n})=E\left(S_{n}\mid S_{n}>VaR_{\kappa}(S_{n})\right).

(1.1)

The contribution of the
i
-th risk
X_{i}
to the aggregate risk
S_{n}
is given by

\displaystyle TVaR_{\kappa}(X_{i};S_{n})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle E\left(X_{i}\mid S_{n}>VaR_{\kappa}(S_{n})\right),

(1.2)

for
i=1,\cdots,n.
The additivity of the expectation allows the decomposition of the TVaR into the sum of TVaR contributions as follows

TVaR_{\kappa}(S_{n})=\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}TVaR_{\kappa}(X_{i};S_{n}).

Adequate economic capital and capital allocation relay on accurate dependences modeling between different components of the portfolio. For this reason, multivariate risk models incorporating dependence are very important in risk modeling in finance and insurance. In the literature, several classes of multivariate distributions have been proposed.
Landsman and Valdez 2003
obtained the explicit formulas of
TVaR_{\kappa}(S_{n})
and
TVaR_{\kappa}(X_{i};S_{n})
for the multivariate elliptical distributions, which include the distributions such as multivariate normal, stable, student, etc. Other closed-form expressions for the economic capital and risk contribution under multivariate phase-type
distributed risks have been given in
Cai and Li 2005
. The case of multivariate gamma
distribution for risks has been studied in
Furman and Landsman 2005
as well as a multivariate Tweedie distribution in
Furman and Landsman 2010
.
Chiragiev and Landsman 2007
consider the case of multivariate Pareto risks. In these papers, explicit expressions for the
TVaR_{\kappa}(S_{n})
and the TVaR-based allocation are derived. For further details on the TVaR-based
allocation of risk capital,
Kim 2007
and references within can be consulted. Other researchers addressed the risk aggregation problem using copulas. For example,
Cossette et al. 2013
consider a portfolio of dependent risks whose multivariate distribution is the Farlie-Gumbel-Morgenstern copula with mixed Erlang marginal distributions.
Sarabia et al. 2016
give explicit formulas for the probability density function of
S_{n}
for some multivariate mixed exponential distributions for which the dependence structure is an Archimedean copula. Recently,
Marri et al. 2018
derive explicit expressions for the higher moments of the discounted aggregate renewal claims with dependence.

The focus of this paper is to derive explicit formulas of
TVaR_{\kappa}(S_{n})
and
TVaR_{\kappa}(X_{i};S_{n})
for
i=1,\cdots,n
, with a more general dependence structure that allows us to capture different types of dependence between structure. The multivariate model that we suggest is a mixed Bernstein copula. The mixed Bernstein copulas have many attractive properties and, in particular, they are non-exchangeable. This is useful for risk aggregation in many insurance and financial applications. Our model provides a new generalization of the well-known Archimedean copulas. The remainder of this paper is structured as follows. In Section (
2
), we introduce the mixed Bernstein copulas. The distribution of the aggregated risk is investigated in Section (
3
). In Section (
4
), we derive closed-formulas of the
TVaR_{\kappa}(S_{n})
and
TVaR_{\kappa}(X_{i};S_{n})
for
n
dependent rv’s
X_{1},X_{2},\cdots,X_{n}
joined by the mixed Bernstein copulas. We obtain specific expressions for the
aggregated distribution in Section (
5
). The results are illustrated with numerical applications in Section (
6
).

2
Mixed Bernstein copulas

In this section, we will construct a new family of copulas that will be used in the different models.

Let
\mathbf{X}=\left(X_{1},\cdots,X_{n}\right)
be a vector of
n
continuous and positive random variables (rv’s) with joint survival distribution function (sf) denoted by
\bar{H}
and univariate survival marginal distributions
\bar{H}_{i},\quad i=1,\cdots,n
. Let
\Theta
be a positive random variable (rv) with probability density function (pdf)
f_{\Theta}
, cumulative distribution function (cdf)
F_{\Theta}
, and Laplace transform
f_{\Theta}^{\star}(s)=\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-s\theta}f_{\Theta}(\theta)d\theta
. In this paper, we assume that
X_{1},\cdots,X_{n}
are
n
dependent, positive and continuous rv’s such that

\displaystyle{\left(X_{1},\cdots,X_{n}\right)^{\top}=\left(\frac{Z_{1}}{\Theta},\cdots,\frac{Z_{n}}{\Theta}\right)^{\top}},

(2.1)

where
\left(Z_{1},\cdots,Z_{n}\right)
is a vector of
n
continuous rv’s with joint survival distribution function denoted by
\bar{F}
and with standard exponential marginal distributions (with mean
1
).

In
Sarabia et al. 2018
and
Albrecher et al. 2011
, the rv’s
Z_{i}
are supposed to be independent. While such an assumption significantly simplifies the model setup, it is also to possible that it leads to a misidentification of the dependence structure. Indeed, the variable
\Theta
only capture the common factor of dependence between all the n variables (e.g., climate conditions, age,
\cdots
,etc.). In this paper, we add another level of dependence by assuming the vector
Z_{i}
has dependent components. According to Sklar’s theorem for survival functions (see e.g.
Sklar 1959
), the multivariate survival function of
{Z_{1}},\cdots,{Z_{n}}
can be written as a function of the marginal survival functions and a copula
C_{1}
describing the dependence structure as follows:

\bar{F}(z_{1},\cdots,z_{n})={C_{1}}\left(e^{-z_{1}},\cdots,e^{-z_{n}}\right),
for
n\in\left\{2,3,\cdots\right\},\,
and
z_{1},\cdots,z_{n}\geq 0.

In this paper, we assume that
C_{1}
is defined with the Bernstein copulas
C_{B}
introduced in
Sancetta and Satchell 2004
and defined under some specifics conditions on the function
\alpha
, by

\displaystyle C_{B}(u_{1},\cdots,u_{n})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\displaystyle\sum\limits_{\nu_{1}=0}^{m_{1}}\cdots\displaystyle\sum\limits_{\nu_{n}=0}^{m_{n}}\alpha\left(\frac{\nu_{1}}{m_{1}},\cdots,\frac{\nu_{n}}{m_{n}}\right)\prod\limits_{i=1}^{n}G_{\nu_{i}:m_{i}}(u_{i}),

(2.2)

for every
(u_{1},\cdots,u_{n})
in
\left[0,1\right]^{n}
such that
0\leq\nu_{i}\leq m_{i}\in\mathbb{N}
, where

\displaystyle G_{\nu_{i}:m_{i}}(u_{i})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\binom{m_{i}}{\nu_{i}}u_{i}^{\nu_{i}}(1-u_{i})^{m_{i}-\nu_{i}},\quad\nu_{i}=0,1,\cdots,m_{i},

(2.3)

is the
\nu_{i}
th Bernstein polynomial of order
m_{i},\quad i=1,\cdots,n
.
Sancetta and Satchell 2004
showed that the coefficients of the Bernstein copulas
C_{B}
have a direct interpretation as
the points of some arbitrary approximated copula
C_{1}
, i.e.,
C_{1}\left(\frac{\nu_{1}}{m_{1}},\cdots,\frac{\nu_{n}}{m_{n}}\right)=\alpha\left(\frac{\nu_{1}}{m_{1}},\cdots,\frac{\nu_{n}}{m_{n}}\right).
This justifies our choice of Bernstein copulas since our mixed Bernstein model could approximate many different dependence structures. In
Cottin and Pfeifer 2014
, it has been proved that any copula function can be approximated uniformly using Bernstein polynomials. In the following theorem, we give sufficient conditions for
C_{B}(u_{1},\cdots,u_{n})
to be a copula.

Theorem 2.1
.

The Bernstein polynomial
C_{B}(u_{1},\cdots,u_{n})
is a copula function if conditions

\displaystyle\displaystyle\sum\limits_{l_{1}=0}^{1}\cdots\displaystyle\sum\limits_{l_{n}=0}^{1}(-1)^{n+l_{1}+\cdots+l_{n}}\alpha\left(\frac{\nu_{1}+l_{1}}{m_{1}},\cdots,\frac{\nu_{n}+l_{n}}{m_{n}}\right)

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle 0,

(2.4)

for all
0\leq\nu_{i}\leq m_{i}-1,\quad i=1,\cdots,n,

\displaystyle\alpha\left(\frac{\nu_{1}}{m_{1}},\cdots,\frac{\nu_{i-1}}{m_{i-1}},0,\frac{\nu_{i+1}}{m_{i+1}}\cdots,\frac{\nu_{n}}{m_{n}}\right)=0,\quad\forall i=1,\cdots,n,

(2.5)

and

\displaystyle\alpha\left(1,\cdots,1,\frac{\nu_{i}}{m_{i}},1,\cdots,1\right)=\frac{\nu_{i}}{m_{i}},\quad\forall i=1,\cdots,n

(2.6)

hold. Moreover, (
2.5
) and (
2.6
) are necessary for
C_{B}(u_{1},\cdots,u_{n})
to be a copula.

Proof.

A proof of this theorem was given by
Yang et al. 2020
.∎

The application of Bernstein copulas in actuarial science is recent.
Salmon et al. 2006
and
Hurd et al. 2007
apply Bernstein copulas to the pricing of two-asset derivatives written on foreign exchange rates.
Diers et al. 2012
use Bernstein copulas to model the dependence between non-life insurance risks.
Tavin 2013
analyze properties of Bernstein copulas in a context of multi-asset derivatives pricing. In contrast to Archimedean copulas, Bernstein copulas can model non-exchangeable dependence structures.

From
Sancetta and Satchell 2004
and
Cottin and Pfeifer 2014
, the corresponding pdf of the copula
C_{B}
is given by

\displaystyle c_{B}(u_{1},\cdots,u_{n})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\displaystyle\sum\limits_{\nu_{1}=0}^{m_{1}-1}\cdots\displaystyle\sum\limits_{\nu_{n}=0}^{m_{n}-1}\gamma\left(\frac{\nu_{1}}{m_{1}},\cdots,\frac{\nu_{n}}{m_{n}}\right)\left(\prod\limits_{i=1}^{n}m_{i}G_{\nu_{i}:m_{i}-1}(u_{i})\right),

(2.7)

where

\displaystyle\gamma\left(\frac{\nu_{1}}{m_{1}},\cdots,\frac{\nu_{n}}{m_{n}}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\displaystyle\sum\limits_{l_{1}=0}^{1}\cdots\displaystyle\sum\limits_{l_{n}=0}^{1}(-1)^{n+l_{1}+\cdots+l_{n}}\alpha\left(\frac{\nu_{1}+l_{1}}{m_{1}},\cdots,\frac{\nu_{n}+l_{n}}{m_{n}}\right).

Cottin and Pfeifer 2014
show that the Bernstein copulas density function can also be expressed as

\displaystyle c_{B}(u_{1},\cdots,u_{n})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\displaystyle\sum\limits_{\nu_{1}=0}^{m_{1}-1}\cdots\displaystyle\sum\limits_{\nu_{n}=0}^{m_{n}-1}\Pr\left(N_{1}=\nu_{1},\cdots,N_{n}=\nu_{n}\right)\left(\prod\limits_{i=1}^{n}m_{i}G_{\nu_{i}:m_{i}-1}(u_{i})\right),

(2.8)

where
\left(N_{1},\cdots,N_{n}\right)
is a random vector whose marginal component
N_{i}
follows a discrete uniform distribution over
\left\{0,1,\cdots,m-1\right\}
and
\Pr\left(N_{1}=\nu_{1},\cdots,N_{n}=\nu_{n}\right)=\gamma\left(\frac{\nu_{1}}{m_{1}},\cdots,\frac{\nu_{n}}{m_{n}}\right).
For more details about Bernstein copulas, we refer
readers to
Kulpa 1999
and
Sancetta and Satchell 2004
.

From (
2.1
), the joint survival function of
X_{1},\cdots,X_{n}
can be written as

\displaystyle\bar{H}\left(x_{1},\cdots,x_{n}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}C_{B}\left(e^{-\theta x_{1}},\cdots,e^{-\theta x_{n}}\right)f_{\Theta}(\theta)d\theta,

(2.9)

for
x_{1},\cdots,x_{n}\geq 0
. It implies that the marginal survival function of
{X_{i}}
is given by

\displaystyle\bar{H}_{i}(x)=\Pr({X_{i}}\geq x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle f_{\Theta}^{\star}(x),

(2.10)

for
i=1,\cdots,n,
where
f_{\Theta}^{\star}
is the Laplace transforms of
F_{\Theta}
. Note that the marginal random variables
X_{i}
are necessarily completely monotone (see, e.g.,
Oakes 1989
).

A closed-form expression for the survival function of
(X_{1},\cdots,X_{n})
is given in the next theorem.

Theorem 2.2
.

Let
\mathbf{X}=\left(X_{1},\cdots,X_{n}\right)
be a vector of
n
continuous and non-negative rv’s defined by the stochastic representation (
2.1
). Then the survival function of
(X_{1},\cdots,X_{n})
is given by

\displaystyle\bar{H}\left(x_{1},\cdots,x_{n}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\displaystyle\sum\limits_{\ell_{1}=0}^{m_{1}}\cdots\displaystyle\sum\limits_{\ell_{n}=0}^{m_{n}}\beta_{\ell_{1},\cdots,\ell_{n}}f_{\Theta}^{\star}\left(\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}\ell_{i}x_{i}\right),

(2.11)

for
x_{1},\cdots,x_{n}\geq 0
, where

\beta_{\ell_{1},\cdots,\ell_{n}}=\displaystyle\sum\limits_{\nu_{1}=0}^{\ell_{1}}\cdots\displaystyle\sum\limits_{\nu_{n}=0}^{\ell_{n}}(-1)^{\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}(\ell_{i}-\nu_{i})}\left[\prod\limits_{i=1}^{n}\binom{m_{i}-\nu_{i}}{m_{i}-\ell_{i}}\right]\left[\prod\limits_{i=1}^{n}\binom{m_{i}}{\nu_{i}}\right]\alpha\left(\frac{\nu_{1}}{m_{1}},\cdots,\frac{\nu_{n}}{m_{n}}\right).

Proof.

From (
2.2
) and (
2.9
), we have

\displaystyle\bar{H}\left(x_{1},\cdots,x_{n}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\displaystyle\sum\limits_{\nu_{1}=0}^{m_{1}}\cdots\displaystyle\sum\limits_{\nu_{n}=0}^{m_{n}}\alpha\left(\frac{\nu_{1}}{m_{1}},\cdots,\frac{\nu_{n}}{m_{n}}\right)\int_{0}^{\infty}\prod\limits_{i=1}^{n}G_{\nu_{i}:m_{i}}(e^{-\theta x_{i}})f_{\Theta}(\theta)d\theta.

(2.12)

Otherwise, applying the binomial theorem to
(1-e^{-\theta x_{i}})^{m_{i}-\nu_{i}}
and using (
2.3
) yield

\displaystyle\prod\limits_{i=1}^{n}G_{\nu_{i}:m_{i}}\left(e^{-\theta x_{i}}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left[\prod\limits_{i=1}^{n}\binom{m_{i}}{\nu_{i}}\right]\displaystyle\sum\limits_{\ell_{1}=0}^{m_{1}-\nu_{1}}\cdots\displaystyle\sum\limits_{\ell_{n}=0}^{m_{n}-\nu_{n}}\left[\prod\limits_{i=1}^{n}\binom{m_{i}-\nu_{i}}{\ell_{i}}\right]e^{-\theta\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}x_{i}\left({\nu_{i}}+\ell_{i}\right)}\left(-1\right)^{\displaystyle\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}\ell_{i}}.

Substituting the last expression into (
2.12
), we obtain

\displaystyle\bar{H}\left(x_{1},\cdots,x_{n}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\displaystyle\sum\limits_{\nu_{1}=0}^{m_{1}}\cdots\displaystyle\sum\limits_{\nu_{n}=0}^{m_{n}}\alpha\left(\frac{\nu_{1}}{m_{1}},\cdots,\frac{\nu_{n}}{m_{n}}\right)\left[\prod\limits_{i=1}^{n}\binom{m_{i}}{\nu_{i}}\right]\displaystyle\sum\limits_{\ell_{1}=0}^{m_{1}-\nu_{1}}\cdots\displaystyle\sum\limits_{\ell_{n}=0}^{m_{n}-\nu_{n}}\left[\prod\limits_{i=1}^{n}\binom{m_{i}-\nu_{i}}{\ell_{i}}\right](-1)^{\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}\ell_{i}}f_{\Theta}^{\star}\left(\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}\left(\nu_{i}+\ell_{i}\right)x_{i}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\displaystyle\sum\limits_{\nu_{1}=0}^{m_{1}}\cdots\displaystyle\sum\limits_{\nu_{n}=0}^{m_{n}}\alpha\left(\frac{\nu_{1}}{m_{1}},\cdots,\frac{\nu_{n}}{m_{n}}\right)\left[\prod\limits_{i=1}^{n}\binom{m_{i}}{\nu_{i}}\right]\displaystyle\sum\limits_{\ell_{1}=\nu_{1}}^{m_{1}}\cdots\displaystyle\sum\limits_{\ell_{n}=\nu_{n}}^{m_{n}}\left[\prod\limits_{i=1}^{n}\binom{m_{i}-\nu_{i}}{\ell_{i}-\nu_{i}}\right](-1)^{\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}(\ell_{i}-\nu_{i})}f_{\Theta}^{\star}\left(\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}\ell_{i}x_{i}\right).

Inverting the order of summation with respect to the variables
\ell_{i}
and
\nu_{i},

i=1,\cdots,n
, one readily obtains

\displaystyle\bar{H}\left(x_{1},\cdots,x_{n}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\displaystyle\sum\limits_{\ell_{1}=0}^{m_{1}}\cdots\displaystyle\sum\limits_{\ell_{n}=0}^{m_{n}}\beta_{\ell_{1},\cdots,\ell_{n}}f_{\Theta}^{\star}\left(\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}\ell_{i}x_{i}\right),

from which we get the desired result.
∎

On the other hand, according to Sklar’s theorem for survival functions, see e.g.
Sklar 1959
, the joint survival function of
X_{1},\cdots,X_{n}
can be written as a function of the marginal survival functions
\bar{H}_{i},\,i=1,\cdots,n,
and a copula
C
describing the dependence structure as follows:

\displaystyle\bar{H}\left(x_{1},\cdots,x_{n}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{C}\left(\bar{H}_{1}(x_{1}),\cdots,\bar{H}_{n}(x_{n})\right),

(2.13)

for
n\in\left\{2,3,\cdots\right\},\,
and
x_{1},\cdots,x_{n}\geq 0.
Then, the following proposition holds.

Proposition 2.1
.

The copula function
C:[0,1]^{n}\to[0,1]
that corresponds to the general dependence structure defined in Theorem (
2.2
) is given, for
u_{i}\in[0,1],i=1,\cdots,n,
by

\displaystyle C\left(u_{1},\cdots,u_{n}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\displaystyle\sum\limits_{\ell_{1}=0}^{m_{1}}\cdots\displaystyle\sum\limits_{\ell_{n}=0}^{m_{n}}\beta_{\ell_{1},\cdots,\ell_{n}}f_{\Theta}^{\star}\left(\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}\ell_{i}f_{\Theta}^{\star-1}(u_{i})\right),

(2.14)

where

\beta_{\ell_{1},\cdots,\ell_{n}}=\displaystyle\sum\limits_{\nu_{1}=0}^{\ell_{1}}\cdots\displaystyle\sum\limits_{\nu_{n}=0}^{\ell_{n}}(-1)^{\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}(\ell_{i}-\nu_{i})}\left[\prod\limits_{i=1}^{n}\binom{m_{i}-\nu_{i}}{m_{i}-\ell_{i}}\right]\left[\prod\limits_{i=1}^{n}\binom{m_{i}}{\nu_{i}}\right]\alpha\left(\frac{\nu_{1}}{m_{1}},\cdots,\frac{\nu_{n}}{m_{n}}\right).

Proof.

The result follows easily from (
2.10
), (
2.11
) and (
2.13
).

3
The distribution of the aggregated risk
{S}_{n}=\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}X_{i}

In this section, we obtain the probability density function and the survival
function of the aggregated risk
S_{n}
.

The joint probability density function (pdf) of
\left({Z_{1}},\cdots,{Z_{n}}\right)
is given by

\displaystyle{f}_{{Z_{1}},\cdots,{Z_{n}}}\left(z_{1},\cdots,z_{n}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{c_{B}}\left(e^{-z_{1}},\cdots,e^{-z_{n}}\right)e^{-\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}z_{i}}.

(3.1)

Combining (
2.7
) and (
3.1
), the pdf of
\left({Z_{1}},\cdots,{Z_{n}}\right)
becomes

\displaystyle f_{Z_{1},Z_{2},\cdots,Z_{n}}(z_{1},\cdots,z_{n})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle m^{n}e^{-\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}z_{i}}\displaystyle\sum\limits_{\nu_{1}=0}^{m-1}\cdots\displaystyle\sum\limits_{\nu_{n}=0}^{m-1}\gamma\left(\frac{\nu_{1}}{m},\cdots,\frac{\nu_{n}}{m}\right)\prod\limits_{i=1}^{n}\left[\binom{m-1}{\nu_{i}}e^{-\nu_{i}z_{i}}(1-e^{-z_{i}})^{m-1-\nu_{i}}\right]

(3.2)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\displaystyle\sum\limits_{\nu_{1}=0}^{m-1}\cdots\displaystyle\sum\limits_{\nu_{n}=0}^{m-1}\gamma\left(\frac{\nu_{1}}{m},\cdots,\frac{\nu_{n}}{m}\right)\prod\limits_{i=1}^{n}f_{W_{i}}(z_{i}),

where
W_{i}
are
n
independent rv’s with pdf
f_{W_{i}}(z)=m\binom{m-1}{\nu_{i}}e^{-(\nu_{i}+1)z}(1-e^{-z})^{m-1-\nu_{i}}.
Note that
f_{W_{i}}
is the density function of the
m-\nu_{i}
th of the smallest order statistic from
m
i.i.d. rv’s
D_{i},

i=1,\cdots,m,
exponentially distributed with mean 1
\left(W_{i}\mathrel{\mathop{\kern 0.0pt=}\limits^{d}}{D_{m-\nu_{i}:m}}\right)
. For more details about order statistics, we refer readers to
David and Nagaraja 2004
. Furthermore, it is known that (see, e.g.,
Basu and Singh 1998
)

\displaystyle D_{m-\nu_{i}:m}\mathrel{\mathop{\kern 0.0pt=}\limits^{d}}\sum\limits_{j=1}^{m-\nu_{i}}\frac{Q_{j}}{m-j+1},

(3.3)

where
Q_{j}
are
m-\nu_{i}
independent exponential distributions with mean 1. (Here,
\mathrel{\mathop{\kern 0.0pt=}\limits^{d}}
stands for the ’equality in distribution).

To derive the pdf of the aggregated random variable
S_{n}=X_{1}+\cdots+X_{n}
with the mixed Bernstein copulas presented in Section (

), the following result is needed.

Theorem 3.1
.

Assume that the rv’s
Z_{1},\cdots,Z_{n}
are dependent and defined with the Bernstein copulas and distributed as exponential
Exp(1)
, such as assumed in Section (
2
). Then, the pdf of the aggregated random variable
\tilde{S}_{n}=Z_{1}+\cdots+Z_{n}
is given by

\displaystyle f_{\tilde{S}_{n}}(x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{l=n}^{\infty}A_{l}e^{-mx}x^{l-1}\frac{m^{l}}{\Gamma(l)},

(3.4)

where
A_{l}=\Pr\left({\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}\sum\limits_{j=N_{i}+1}^{m}\Delta_{i,j}}=l\right)
and
\Delta_{i,j}
form a sequence of independent rv’s, independent of
N_{i},
for
i=1,\cdots,n
and
j=1,\cdots,m

such that
\Delta_{i,j}
follow a shifted geometric distribution
Geom(\frac{j}{m}),
with probability mass function
\Pr\left(\Delta_{i,j}=l\right)=\frac{j}{m}\left(1-\frac{j}{m}\right)^{l-1},
for for
i=1,\cdots,n,\,

j=1,\cdots,m
and
l=1,2,\cdots.

Proof.

From (
3.2
) and (
3.3
), the Laplace transform of
\tilde{S}_{n}
is

\displaystyle f_{\tilde{S}_{n}}^{\star}(s)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\displaystyle\sum\limits_{\nu_{1}=0}^{m-1}\cdots\displaystyle\sum\limits_{\nu_{n}=0}^{m-1}\gamma\left(\frac{\nu_{1}}{m},\cdots,\frac{\nu_{n}}{m}\right)\prod\limits_{i=1}^{n}\prod\limits_{j=1}^{m-\nu_{i}}\frac{m-j+1}{m-j+1+s}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\displaystyle\sum\limits_{\nu_{1}=0}^{m-1}\cdots\displaystyle\sum\limits_{\nu_{n}=0}^{m-1}\gamma\left(\frac{\nu_{1}}{m},\cdots,\frac{\nu_{n}}{m}\right)\prod\limits_{i=1}^{n}\prod\limits_{j=\nu_{i}+1}^{m}\frac{j}{j+s}.

In fact, we can rewrite the above result as

\displaystyle f_{\tilde{S}_{n}}^{\star}(s)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\displaystyle\sum\limits_{\nu_{1}=0}^{m-1}\cdots\displaystyle\sum\limits_{\nu_{n}=0}^{m-1}\gamma\left(\frac{\nu_{1}}{m},\cdots,\frac{\nu_{n}}{m}\right)\prod\limits_{i=1}^{n}\prod\limits_{j=1}^{m}\left(\frac{j}{j+s}\right)^{\mathbbm{1}_{\left\{j\geq\nu_{i}+1\right\}}},

where
\mathbbm{1}_{\left\{\mathcal{A}\right\}}
denoting the indicator function on
{\mathcal{A}}
, we readily obtain that

\displaystyle f_{\tilde{S}_{n}}^{\star}(s)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{\nu_{1}=0}^{m-1}\cdots\sum\limits_{\nu_{n}=0}^{m-1}\gamma\left(\frac{\nu_{1}}{m_{1}},\cdots,\frac{\nu_{n}}{m_{n}}\right)\prod\limits_{j=1}^{m}\left(\frac{j}{j+s}\right)^{\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}\mathbbm{1}_{\left\{j\geq\nu_{i}+1\right\}}}.

On the other hand, we have
\prod\limits_{j=1}^{m}\left(\frac{j}{j+s}\right)^{\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}\mathbbm{1}_{\left\{j\geq\nu_{i}+1\right\}}}=\left(\frac{m}{m+s}\right)^{mn-\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}\nu_{i}}\prod\limits_{j=1}^{m}\left(\frac{\frac{j}{m}}{1-\left(1-\frac{j}{m}\right)\left(\frac{m}{m+s}\right)}\right)^{\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}\mathbbm{1}_{\left\{j\geq\nu_{i}+1\right\}}}.

f_{\tilde{S}_{n}}^{\star}
can then be rewritten as

\displaystyle f_{\tilde{S}_{n}}^{\star}(s)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle E\left[\left(\frac{m}{m+s}\right)^{\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}\sum\limits_{j=N_{i}+1}^{m}\Delta_{i,j}}\right]

(3.5)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{l=0}^{\infty}\left(\frac{m}{m+s}\right)^{l}\Pr\left({\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}\sum\limits_{j=N_{i}+1}^{m}\Delta_{i,j}}=l\right),

where
\Delta_{i,j},
form a sequence of independent rv’s, independent of
N_{i},
for
i=1,\cdots,n
and
j=1,\cdots,m

such that
\Delta_{i,j}
follow a shifted geometric distribution
Geom(\frac{j}{m}),
for
i=1,\cdots,n
and
j=1,\cdots,m
. The inversion of (
3.5
) with respect to
s
yields (
3.4
) with

\displaystyle A_{l}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{\nu_{1}=0}^{m-1}\cdots\sum\limits_{\nu_{n}=0}^{m-1}\gamma\left(\frac{\nu_{1}}{m_{1}},\cdots,\frac{\nu_{n}}{m_{n}}\right)\Pr\left(\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}\sum\limits_{j=\nu_{i}+1}^{m}\Delta_{i,j}=l\right).\qquad

(3.6)

Since the support of the rv
{\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}\sum\limits_{j=N_{i}+1}^{m}\Delta_{i,j}}
is
\{n,n+1,\cdots\},
then
A_{l}=0
for
l=0,\cdots,n-1.
The expression in (
3.4
) follows immediately.
∎

Now, we are in position to derive a closed-form expression of the pdf of
S_{n}=\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}X_{i}.

In the following corollary, we use Theorem (
3.1
) to derive the pdf of the aggregated random variable
S_{n}=X_{1}+\cdots+X_{n}.

Corollary 3.1
.

Let
\mathbf{X}=\left(X_{1},\cdots,X_{n}\right)
be a vector of
n
continuous and non-negative rv’s defined by the stochastic representation (
2.1
). Then, the pdf of the aggregated random variable
S_{n}
is given by

\displaystyle f_{S_{n}}(x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{l=n}^{\infty}A_{l}(-1)^{l}\frac{m^{l}}{\Gamma(l)}x^{l-1}{f_{\Theta}^{\star}}^{(l)}\left(mx\right),

(3.7)

for
x>0.

Proof.

From (
2.1
), we have
f_{S_{n}}(x)=\int_{0}^{\infty}\theta f_{\tilde{S}_{n}}(x\theta)f_{\Theta}(\theta)d\theta.
Substitution of (
3.4
) into the last expression yields the required result.
∎

Remark 3.1
.

If
m=1
, It follows from (
3.6
) that

\displaystyle A_{l}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}1&\mbox{if }l=n,\\
0&\mbox{if }l\neq n.\end{array}\right.

Substituting the last expression into (
3.7
), one obtains the pdf of the aggregated risk
S_{n}
discussed in
Sarabia et al. 2018
and given by

\displaystyle f_{S_{n}}(x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{(-1)^{n}}{\Gamma(n)}x^{n-1}{f_{\Theta}^{\star}}^{(n)}(x).

We further derive the survival function of the distribution of
S_{n}=\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}X_{i}.

Corollary 3.2
.

The survival function of the distribution of
S_{n}
is given by

\displaystyle\Pr\left(S_{n}>x\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{i=0}^{\infty}(-1)^{i}B_{i}\frac{(mx)^{i}}{i!}{f_{\Theta}^{\star}}^{(i)}\left(mx\right),

for
x>0,
where
B_{i}=\sum\limits_{\ell=\max(i+1,n)}^{\infty}A_{\ell}=\Pr\left({\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}\sum\limits_{j=N_{i}+1}^{m}\Delta_{i,j}}\geq\max(i+1,n)\right).

Proof.

From (
3.7
), we have

\displaystyle\Pr\left(S_{n}>x\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{l=n}^{\infty}A_{l}(-1)^{l}\frac{m^{l}}{\Gamma(l)}\int_{x}^{\infty}z^{l-1}{f_{\Theta}^{\star}}^{(l)}(mz)dz

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{l=n}^{\infty}A_{l}\frac{(-1)^{l}}{\Gamma(l)}\int_{mx}^{\infty}z^{l-1}{f_{\Theta}^{\star}}^{(l)}(z)dz.

By integration by parts (see
Hartman and Mikusinski 2014
), one gets

\displaystyle\Pr\left(S_{n}>x\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{l=n}^{\infty}A_{l}\sum\limits_{i=0}^{l-1}(-1)^{i}\frac{(mx)^{i}}{i!}{f_{\Theta}^{\star}}^{(i)}(mx),

which completes the proof.
∎

Remark 3.2
.

If
m=1
, then one gets the survival function of the aggregated risk
S_{n}
discussed in
Sarabia et al. 2018
and given by

\displaystyle\Pr\left(S_{n}>x\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{i=0}^{n-1}\frac{x^{i}}{i!}(-1)^{i}{f_{\Theta}^{\star}}^{(i)}(x).

In this section we derive an expression of the TVaR for
n
dependent rv’s
X_{1},\cdots,X_{n}
joined by the mixed Bernstein copulas with generator
{f_{\Theta}^{\star}}.

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
X_{1},\cdots,X_{n}

n
dependent rv’s joined by the mixed Bernstein copulas with generator

{f_{\Theta}^{\star}}
, then the TVaR of the aggregate risk
S_{n}=\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}X_{i}
is

\displaystyle TVaR_{\kappa}(S_{n})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{1-\kappa}\sum\limits_{\nu=1}^{\infty}{P_{\nu}}\frac{(-1)^{\nu+1}m^{\nu-1}VaR_{\kappa}^{\nu}(S_{n})}{\nu!}{f_{\Theta}^{\star}}^{(\nu-1)}\left(mVaR_{\kappa}(S_{n})\right)

(4.1)

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\frac{n}{(1-\kappa)}\int_{mVaR_{\kappa}(S_{n})}^{\infty}{f_{\Theta}^{\star}}(x)dx,

where
P_{\nu}=E\left[\left({\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}\sum\limits_{j=N_{i}+1}^{m}\Delta_{i,j}}\right)\mathbbm{1}_{\left\{\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}\sum\limits_{j=N_{i}+1}^{m}\Delta_{i,j}\geq\max(\nu,n)\right\}}\right]
and
\Delta_{i,j}
form a sequence of independent rv’s, independent of
N_{i},
for
i=1,\cdots,n
and
j=1,\cdots,m
such that
\Delta_{i,j}
follow a shifted geometric distribution
Geom(\frac{j}{m})
.

Proof.

The combination of (
1.1
) and (
3.7
) yields

\displaystyle TVaR_{\kappa}(S_{n})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{1-\kappa}\sum\limits_{l=n}^{\infty}{A_{l}(-1)^{l}}\frac{m^{l}}{\Gamma(l)}\int_{VaR_{\kappa}(S_{n})}^{\infty}x^{l}{f_{\Theta}^{\star}}^{(l)}(mx)(x)dx

(4.2)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{1-\kappa}\sum\limits_{l=n}^{\infty}\frac{A_{l}(-1)^{l}}{m\Gamma(l)}\int_{mVaR_{\kappa}(S_{n})}^{\infty}x^{l}{f_{\Theta}^{\star}}^{(l)}(x)dx.\qquad

On the other hand, by integration by parts (see
Spiegel et al. 2013
), one gets

\displaystyle\int_{mVaR_{\kappa}(S_{n})}^{\infty}x^{l}{f_{\Theta}^{\star}}^{(l)}(x)dx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{i=1}^{l}(-1)^{l-i+1}\frac{m^{i}l!}{i!}VaR_{\kappa}^{i}(S_{n}){f_{\Theta}^{\star}}^{(i-1)}\left(mVaR_{\kappa}(S_{n})\right)+(-1)^{l}l!\int_{mVaR_{\kappa}(S_{n})}^{\infty}{f_{\Theta}^{\star}}(x)dx.

Combining the last expression with (
4.2
) gives

\displaystyle TVaR_{\kappa}(S_{n})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{1-\kappa}\sum\limits_{l=n}^{\infty}\sum\limits_{i=1}^{l}{lA_{l}}(-1)^{i+1}\frac{m^{i-1}}{i!}VaR_{\kappa}^{i}(S_{n}){f_{\Theta}^{\star}}^{(i-1)}\left(mVaR_{\kappa}(S_{n})\right)

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\frac{1}{m(1-\kappa)}\sum\limits_{l=n}^{\infty}{lA_{l}}\int_{mVaR_{\kappa}(S_{n})}^{\infty}{f_{\Theta}^{\star}}(x)dx.

Otherwise, from Theorem (
3.1
), we have
\sum\limits_{l=n}^{\infty}{lA_{l}}=E\left[\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}\sum\limits_{j=N_{i}+1}^{m}\Delta_{i,j}\right]=\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}\sum\limits_{j=1}^{m}\frac{m}{j}\Pr\left[N_{i}\leq j-1\right]=mn.
Thus (
4.1
) holds.
∎

Corollary 4.1
.

If we take
m=1
, then the
TVaR_{\kappa}(S_{n})
in Theorem (
4.1
) reduces to

\displaystyle TVaR_{\kappa}(S_{n})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{n}{1-\kappa}\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}\frac{(-1)^{i+1}}{i!}VaR_{\kappa}^{i}(S_{n}){f_{\Theta}^{\star}}^{(i-1)}(VaR_{\kappa}(S_{n}))+\frac{n}{(1-\kappa)}\int_{VaR_{\kappa}(S_{n})}^{\infty}{f_{\Theta}^{\star}}(x)dx.

Proof.

Substituting
m=1
into (
4.1
), we get the desired result.
∎

To derive the TVaR-based contribution of risk
i
,

to the sum
S_{n}
with the mixed Bernstein copulas presented in Section (

Lemma 4.2
.

Assume that the rv’s

are dependent and defined with the Bernstein copulas and distributed as exponential

, such as assumed in Section (
2
). Then, the pdf of the random vector

\displaystyle f_{Z_{i},\tilde{S}_{n}-Z_{i}}(x,y)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{k=1}^{\infty}\sum\limits_{l=n-1}^{\infty}q_{k,l}^{(i)}\tau_{k,m}(x)\tau_{l,m}(y),

(4.3)

for

and

where
\tau_{k,\beta}\left(x\right)=\frac{\beta^{k}x^{k-1}e^{-\beta x}}{\left(k-1\right)!},

\displaystyle q_{k,l}^{(i)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Pr\left(\sum\limits_{j=N_{i}+1}^{m}\Delta_{i,j}=k,{\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}\nu=1\\
\nu\neq i\end{subarray}}^{n}\sum\limits_{j=N_{\nu}+1}^{m}{\Delta}_{\nu,j}}=l\right),

and
\Delta_{\nu,j}
form a sequence of independent rv’s, independent of
N_{\nu},
for
\nu=1,\cdots,n

\Delta_{\nu,j}

Proof.

Let
f_{Z_{i},\tilde{S}_{n}-Z_{i}}^{\star}
be the joint Laplace transform of the random vector
(Z_{i},\tilde{S}_{n}-Z_{i})
defined by
f_{Z_{i},\tilde{S}_{n}-Z_{i}}^{\star}(s_{1},s_{2})=\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-(xs_{1}+ys_{2})}f_{Z_{i},\tilde{S}_{n}-Z_{i}}(x,y)dxdy.

) and (

\displaystyle f_{Z_{i},\tilde{S}_{n}-Z_{i}}^{\star}(s_{1},s_{2})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\displaystyle\sum\limits_{\nu_{1}=0}^{m-1}\cdots\displaystyle\sum\limits_{\nu_{n}=0}^{m-1}\gamma\left(\frac{\nu_{1}}{m},\cdots,\frac{\nu_{n}}{m}\right)\left(\prod\limits_{j=1}^{m-\nu_{i}}\frac{m-j+1}{m-j+1+s_{1}}\right)\left(\prod\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}k=1\\

(4.4)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\displaystyle\sum\limits_{\nu_{1}=0}^{m-1}\cdots\displaystyle\sum\limits_{\nu_{n}=0}^{m-1}\gamma\left(\frac{\nu_{1}}{m},\cdots,\frac{\nu_{n}}{m}\right)\left(\prod\limits_{\ell=\nu_{i}+1}^{m}\frac{\ell}{\ell+s_{1}}\right)\left(\prod\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}k=1\\

After some rearrangements, (

\displaystyle f_{Z_{i},\tilde{S}_{n}-Z_{i}}^{\star}(s_{1},s_{2})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\displaystyle\sum\limits_{\nu_{1}=0}^{m-1}\cdots\displaystyle\sum\limits_{\nu_{n}=0}^{m-1}\gamma\left(\frac{\nu_{1}}{m},\cdots,\frac{\nu_{n}}{m}\right)\left(\prod\limits_{\ell=\nu_{i}+1}^{m}\frac{\ell}{\ell+s_{1}}\right)\left(\prod\limits_{j=1}^{m}\left(\frac{j}{j+s_{2}}\right)^{\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}k=1\\
k\neq i\end{subarray}}^{n}\mathbbm{1}_{\left\{j\geq\nu_{k}+1\right\}}}\right).\quad\qquad

(4.5)

On the other hand, we have
\prod\limits_{\ell={\nu_{i}+1}}^{m}\left(\frac{\ell}{\ell+s_{1}}\right)=\left(\frac{m}{m+s_{1}}\right)^{m-\nu_{i}}\prod\limits_{\ell={\nu_{i}+1}}^{m}\left(\frac{\frac{\ell}{m}}{1-\left(1-\frac{\ell}{m}\right)\left(\frac{m}{m+s_{1}}\right)}\right)

for

and

such that

Theorem 4.3
.

Let

n

\displaystyle TVaR_{\kappa}(X_{i};S_{n})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{1-\kappa}\sum\limits_{\nu=1}^{\infty}{P}_{\nu}^{(i)}\frac{(-1)^{\nu+1}m^{\nu-1}VaR_{\kappa}^{\nu}(S_{n})}{\nu!}{f_{\Theta}^{\star}}^{(\nu-1)}({mVaR_{\kappa}(S_{n})})

(4.7)

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\frac{1}{1-\kappa}\int_{mVaR_{\kappa}(S_{n})}^{\infty}{f_{\Theta}^{\star}}(x)dx,\quad\quad

where
{P}_{\nu}^{(i)}=E\left[\left({\sum\limits_{j=N_{i}+1}^{m}\Delta_{i,j}}\right)\mathbbm{1}_{\left\{\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}\sum\limits_{j=N_{i}+1}^{m}\Delta_{i,j}\geq\max(\nu,n)\right\}}\right]

\Delta_{i,j}
form a sequence of independent rv’s, independent of
N_{i},
for

and

\Delta_{i,j}
follow a shifted geometric distribution
Geom(\frac{j}{m})
.

Proof.

From (

) and (

), the capital attributed to the continuous distributed risk

\displaystyle TVaR_{\kappa}(X_{i};S_{n})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{1-\kappa}E\left(X_{i}.1_{\left\{S_{n}>VaR_{\kappa}(S_{n})\right\}}\right)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{1-\kappa}\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{\theta}E\left(Z_{i}.1_{\left\{\tilde{S}_{n}>\theta VaR_{\kappa}(S_{n})\right\}}\right)f_{\Theta}(\theta)d\theta

\displaystyle\frac{1}{1-\kappa}\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{\theta}\int_{\theta VaR_{\kappa}(S_{n})}^{\infty}\int_{0}^{s}x{f_{Z_{i},\tilde{S}_{n}-Z_{i}}}(x,s-x)f_{\Theta}(\theta)dxdsd\theta,

where
\tilde{S}_{n}=\sum\limits_{j=1}^{n}Z_{j}
and

(Z_{i},\tilde{S}_{n}-Z_{i})

Using Lemma (

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{s}xf_{Z_{i},\tilde{S}_{n}-Z_{i}}(x,s-x)dx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{k=1}^{\infty}\sum\limits_{l=n-1}^{\infty}q_{k,l}^{(i)}\frac{k}{m}\tau_{k+l+1,m}(s)=\sum\limits_{r=n}^{\infty}a_{r}^{(i)}\tau_{r+1,m}(s),

\displaystyle TVaR_{\kappa}(X_{i};S_{n})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{1-\kappa}\sum\limits_{r=n}^{\infty}a_{r}^{(i)}\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{\theta}\int_{\theta VaR_{\kappa}(S_{n})}^{\infty}\tau_{r+1,m}(s)f_{\Theta}(\theta)dsd\theta

\displaystyle\frac{1}{1-\kappa}\sum\limits_{r=n}^{\infty}a_{r}^{(i)}\sum\limits_{\nu=0}^{r}\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{\theta}\frac{\left(m\theta VaR_{\kappa}(S_{n})\right)^{\nu}}{\nu!}e^{-m\theta VaR_{\kappa}(S_{n})}f_{\Theta}(\theta)d\theta.

\sum\limits_{r=n}^{\infty}a_{r}^{(i)}=\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{0}^{s}xf_{Z_{i},\tilde{S}_{n}-Z_{i}}(x,s-x)dx=1,
it follows that

\displaystyle TVaR_{\kappa}(X_{i};S_{n})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{1-\kappa}\sum\limits_{r=n}^{\infty}a_{r}^{(i)}\sum\limits_{\nu=1}^{r}\frac{\left(-mVaR_{\kappa}(S_{n})\right)^{\nu}}{\nu!}{f_{\Theta}^{\star}}^{(\nu-1)}({mVaR_{\kappa}(S_{n})})+\frac{1}{1-\kappa}\int_{mVaR_{\kappa}(S_{n})}^{\infty}{f_{\Theta}^{\star}}(x)dx.

Inverting the order of summation w.r.t. the variables
r
and
\nu
in the last expression, one readily obtains

\displaystyle TVaR_{\kappa}(X_{i};S_{n})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{1-\kappa}\sum\limits_{\nu=1}^{\infty}\sum\limits_{r=max(\nu,n)}^{\infty}a_{r}^{(i)}\frac{\left(-mVaR_{\kappa}(S_{n})\right)^{\nu}}{\nu!}{f_{\Theta}^{\star}}^{(\nu-1)}({mVaR_{\kappa}(S_{n})})

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\frac{1}{1-\kappa}\int_{mVaR_{\kappa}(S_{n})}^{\infty}{f_{\Theta}^{\star}}(x)dx,

where
{P}_{\nu}^{(i)}=\sum\limits_{r=max(\nu,n)}^{\infty}\sum\limits_{k=1}^{r-n+1}{k}q_{k,r-k}^{(i)}=E\left[\left({\sum\limits_{j=N_{i}+1}^{m}\Delta_{i,j}}\right)\mathbbm{1}_{\left\{{\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}\nu=1\end{subarray}}^{n}\sum\limits_{j=N_{\nu}+1}^{m}{\Delta}_{\nu,j}}\geq\max(\nu,n)\right\}}\right],
and then (
4.7
) immediately follows.
∎

Corollary 4.2
.

If we take
m=1
, then (
4.7
) reduces to

\displaystyle TVaR_{\kappa}(X_{i};S_{n})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{1-\kappa}\sum\limits_{\nu=1}^{n}(-1)^{\nu+1}\frac{\left(VaR_{\kappa}(S_{n})\right)^{\nu}}{\nu!}{f_{\Theta}^{\star}}^{(\nu-1)}({VaR_{\kappa}(S_{n})})+\frac{1}{1-\kappa}\int_{VaR_{\kappa}(S_{n})}^{\infty}{f_{\Theta}^{\star}}(x)dx.

Proof.

If we take
m=1
, it follows from (
4.7
) that
{{{P}_{\nu}^{(i)}=1}}
if
\nu=1,2,\cdots,n
and
0
if
\nu>n.
Therefore, the proof is complete.
∎

Corollary 4.3
.

Let
X_{1},\cdots,X_{n}

n
dependent rv’s joined by the mixed Bernstein copulas with generator
{f_{\Theta}^{\star}}
. Then

\displaystyle TVaR_{\kappa}(S_{n})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}TVaR_{\kappa}(X_{i};S_{n}).

Proof.

The result follows easily from Theorems (
4.1
) and (
4.3
).
∎

Corollary 4.4
.

Let
X_{1},\cdots,X_{n}

n
dependent rv’s joined by the mixed Bernstein copulas with generator
{f_{\Theta}^{\star}}
. If the joint probability mass function of the random vector
\left(N_{1},\cdots,N_{n}\right)
is exchangeable, then

\displaystyle TVaR_{\kappa}(X_{i};S_{n})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{n}TVaR_{\kappa}(S_{n}).

Proof.

The proof follows from Theorem (
4.3
) by using elementary calculus.
∎

5
Models

In this section, we present some results as consequences of our main results stated previously. We will consider dependent models with different claim distributions of the type Pareto and Gamma distributions, see
Albrecher et al. 2011
and
Sarabia et al. 2018
for more details.

5.1
Pareto claims with Clayton copula dependence

We assume that
\Theta
has a gamma distribution,
\Theta\sim Gamma(a,b),
with pdf
f_{\Theta}(\theta)=\frac{b^{a}}{\Gamma(a)}\theta^{a-1}e^{-b\theta}
, and a Laplace transform
f_{\Theta}^{\star}
defined by

\displaystyle f_{\Theta}^{\star}(x)=\left(1+\frac{x}{b}\right)^{-a},\quad a\geq 1.

(5.1)

It follow that
X_{i}\sim Pareto(a,b)
with survival function given by
\bar{H}_{i}(x)=f_{\Theta}^{\star}(x)=\left(1+\frac{x}{b}\right)^{-a},\quad a\geq 1,
for
i=1,\cdots,n.
Using (
5.1
), the expression for
C\left(u_{1},\cdots,u_{n}\right)
in (
2.14
) turns into

\displaystyle C\left(u_{1},\cdots,u_{n}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\displaystyle\sum\limits_{\ell_{1}=0}^{m}\cdots\displaystyle\sum\limits_{\ell_{n}=0}^{m}b_{\ell_{1},\cdots,\ell_{n}}\left(1+\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}\ell_{i}(u_{i}^{-\frac{1}{a}}-1)\right)^{-a},

(5.2)

where

b_{\ell_{1},\cdots,\ell_{n}}=\displaystyle\sum\limits_{\nu_{1}=0}^{\ell_{1}}\cdots\displaystyle\sum\limits_{\nu_{n}=0}^{\ell_{n}}(-1)^{\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}(\ell_{i}-\nu_{i})}\left[\prod\limits_{i=1}^{n}\binom{m-\nu_{i}}{m-\ell_{i}}\right]\left[\prod\limits_{i=1}^{n}\binom{m}{\nu_{i}}\right]\alpha\left(\frac{\nu_{1}}{m},\cdots,\frac{\nu_{n}}{m}\right).

Using (
2.13
), the expression for the survival function of
(X_{1},\cdots,X_{n})
turns into

\displaystyle\bar{H}\left(x_{1},\cdots,x_{n}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\displaystyle\sum\limits_{\ell_{1}=0}^{m}\cdots\displaystyle\sum\limits_{\ell_{n}=0}^{m}b_{\ell_{1},\cdots,\ell_{n}}\left(1+\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}\frac{\ell_{i}x_{i}}{b}\right)^{-a},

(5.3)

which is the joint survival function of a Pareto distribution.

Remark 5.1
.

Note that if
m=1
, then the survival function
\bar{H}\left(x_{1},\cdots,x_{n}\right)
in (
5.3
) reduces to the joint survival function of a Pareto type II distribution proposed by
Arnold 1983
,
Arnold 2015
and given by
\bar{H}\left(x_{1},\cdots,x_{n}\right)=\left(1+\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}\frac{x_{i}}{b}\right)^{-a}.

In the following theorem, we give a close expression for the pdf of the aggregated risk for the special case of the Pareto claim with Clayton copula dependence.

Theorem 5.1
.

Let
S_{n}=X_{1}+\cdots+X_{n}
be the sum of
n
dependent rv’s with joint cdf defined by the multivariate mixed Bernstein copulas. Then the pdf of the aggregated random variable is given by

\displaystyle f_{S_{n}}(x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{\ell=n}^{\infty}A_{l}\frac{m^{l}x^{l-1}}{b^{l}B(l,a){\left(1+mx/b\right)}^{a+l}},

(5.4)

for
x>0,
where
B(l,a)=\frac{\Gamma(l)\Gamma(a)}{\Gamma(l+a)}
denotes the Beta function.

Proof.

Taking the
l
th order derivative of (
5.1
) with respect to
x
yields

{f_{\Theta}^{\star}}^{(l)}(x)=\frac{(-1)^{l}\Gamma(a+l)}{b^{l}\Gamma(a){\left(1+x/b\right)}^{a+l}},\quad l=0,1,\cdots.

(5.5)

Substituting the last expression into (
3.7
), we get the desired result.
∎

Remark 5.2
.

If we take
m=1
in (
5.4
), then the pdf of the aggregated risk reduces to

\displaystyle f_{S_{n}}(x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{x^{n-1}}{b^{n}B(n,a){\left(1+x/b\right)}^{a+n}},

(5.6)

which is the pdf of the aggregated risk
S_{n}
with Pareto
marginal distribution with shape parameter
a
, scale parameter
b
and Clayton survival
copula discussed in
Sarabia et al. 2016
,
Sarabia et al. 2018
.

5.2
Gamma claims with dependence claims

Our next model is based on a Gamma claim distribution,
X_{i}\sim Gamma(a,\lambda)
, for
a\leq 1,
it follows that the survival function of the claim
X_{i}
is

\displaystyle\bar{H}_{i}(x)=f_{\Theta}^{\star}(x)=\frac{\Gamma(a,\lambda x)}{\Gamma(a)},\qquad a\leq 1.

(5.7)

Using (
2.11
), the multivariate survival function of
(X_{1},\cdots,X_{n})
can be written as

\displaystyle\bar{H}\left(x_{1},\cdots,x_{n}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\displaystyle\sum\limits_{\ell_{1}=0}^{m}\cdots\displaystyle\sum\limits_{\ell_{n}=0}^{m}b_{\ell_{1},\cdots,\ell_{n}}\frac{\Gamma\left(a,\lambda\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}\ell_{i}x_{i}\right)}{\Gamma(a)}.

We are now ready to apply the preceding result to derive a closed expression for the pdf of the aggregated risk with dependent
Gamma claim.

Theorem 5.2
.

Let
S_{n}=X_{1}+\cdots+X_{n}
be the sum of
n
dependent rv’s with gamma marginal distributions and with joint cdf defined by the multivariate mixed Bernstein copulas. Then the pdf of the aggregated random variable can be written as a finite mixture of Gamma distributions

\displaystyle f_{S_{n}}(x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{k=1}^{\infty}\omega_{k}f_{\mathcal{G}(a+k-1,\lambda m)}(x),

(5.8)

where
\omega_{k}=\sum\limits_{l=max(k,n)}^{\infty}\frac{A_{l}\Gamma(a+k-1)}{\Gamma(k)\Gamma(l-k+1)\Gamma(a)}(-1)^{l-k}(a-1)_{l-k}.

Proof.

According to (
5.7
), the
l
th-order derivative of
f_{\Theta}^{\star}
is given by

{f_{\Theta}^{\star}}^{(l)}(x)=-\frac{\lambda^{a}}{\Gamma(a)}\sum\limits_{k=0}^{l-1}\binom{l-1}{k}(-1)^{l-k-1}\lambda^{l-k-1}e^{-\lambda x}(a-1)_{k}x^{a-k-1},

(5.9)

with
(a)_{n}=a(a-1)\cdots(a-n+1)
is the Pochhammer symbol.
Substituting (
5.9
) into (
3.7
), one gets

\displaystyle f_{S_{n}}(x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{l=n}^{\infty}\frac{A_{l}}{\Gamma(l)\Gamma(a)}\sum\limits_{k=0}^{l-1}\binom{l-1}{k}(-1)^{k}m^{a+l-k-1}\lambda^{a+l-k-1}(a-1)_{k}x^{a+l-k-2}e^{-\lambda mx}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{l=n}^{\infty}\frac{A_{l}}{\Gamma(l)\Gamma(a)}\sum\limits_{k=1}^{l}\binom{l-1}{l-k}(-1)^{l-k}m^{a+k-1}\lambda^{a+k-1}(a-1)_{l-k}x^{a+k-2}e^{-\lambda mx}.

Equation (
5.8
) automatically follows.
∎

Remark 5.3
.

Substituting
m=1
into (
5.8
), one obtains,

\displaystyle f_{S_{n}}(x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{k=1}^{n}\omega_{k}f_{\mathcal{G}(a+k-1,\lambda)}(x),

(5.10)

where

\displaystyle\omega_{k}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\frac{\Gamma(a+k-1)}{\Gamma(k)\Gamma(n-k+1)\Gamma(a)}(-1)^{n-k}(a-1)_{n-k}&\mbox{if }k=1,2,\cdots,n\\
0&\mbox{if }k=n+1,n+2,\cdots,\end{array}\right.

then (
5.10
) reduces to the pdf of the aggregated risk
S_{n}
with gamma
marginal distributions discussed in
Sarabia et al. 2018
.

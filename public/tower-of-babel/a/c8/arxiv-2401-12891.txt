Determination of a pair of newforms from the product of their twisted central values
Pramath Anamby, Ritwik Pal

Abstract
. We show that a pair of newforms ( f , g ) (f,g) can be uniquely determined by the product of the central L L -values of their twists. To achieve our goal, we prove an asymptotic formula for the average of the product of the central values of two twisted L L -functions- L ⁡ ( 1 / 2 , f × χ ) ​ L ​ ( 1 / 2 , g × χ ​ ψ ) L(1/2,f\times\chi)L(1/2,g\times\chi\psi) , where ( f , g ) (f,g) is a pair of newforms. The average is taken over the primitive Dirichlet characters χ \chi and ψ \psi of distinct prime moduli.

1.
Introduction

Distinguishing two modular forms using the
arithmetic
and
analytic
data associated with them is an important problem in the theory of modular forms. The classical
multiplicity-one
results use the arithmetic aspects, i.e., the Hecke eigenvalues and the Fourier coefficients to distinguish two Hecke newforms. On the other hand, it was shown in
[
LR97
]
that the analytic data like the central values of twists of the corresponding
L
-functions can also be used for such purposes. This result has its roots in the Waldspurger’s formula that relates the squares of the Fourier coefficients of a half integral weight modular form to the central values of the twists of the
L
-function associated with an elliptic newform. In this direction, several interesting results dealing with quadratic twists (see
[
Mun10
]
), Rankin-Selberg convolutions (see
[
GHS09
]
,
[
MS15a
]
) are available. This question has also been studied in the context of
GL(3)
-automorphic forms (see
[
CD05
]
,
[
MS15
]
).

In this article, we address the question of distinguishing a pair of modular forms of arbitrary weight and level from the product of their central values. We do this by investigating the behaviour of a twisted average of central
L
-values over a suitable family of Dirichlet characters. We obtain an asymptotic formula for the twisted average of product of the central values of two
GL(2)

L
-functions. A crucial step in this direction is the estimation of a sum involving Hecke eigenvalues and a product of classical Kloosterman sums. In this regard, it is worth mentioning that in
[
MS15
]
, an asymptotic for the twisted average of central values of
GL(3)

L
-functions was obtained by estimating a sum of
2
-dimensional Kloosterman sums. Using this asymptotic expression, we can also study the simultaneous non-vanishing of the central values in this family. The study of simultaneous non-vanishing of
L
-values has a rich history (see
[
MV02
]
,
[
RR
]
,
[
Akb06
]
,
[
Xu11
]
,
[
Kha12
]
,
[
Mun12
]
,
[
DK15
]
). These non-vanishing results have several important implications in analytic number theory and the theory of automorphic forms (see for example
[
LRS99
]
,
[
IS00
]
,
[
MV02
]
).

Let
S_{k}(N)
denote the space of cusp forms of weight
k
and level
N
. We prove the following unique determination result for a pair of Hecke newforms
(f,g)
of different weights and levels.

Theorem 1.1
.

For
i=1,2
, let
f_{i}\in S_{k_{i}}(N_{i})
and
g_{i}\in S_{l_{i}}(M_{i})
be Hecke newforms such that

(1.1)

L(1/2,f_{1}\times\chi)L(1/2,g_{1}\times\chi\psi)=L(1/2,f_{2}\times\chi)L(1/2,g_{2}\times\chi\psi)

for all primitive characters
\chi
and
\psi
of prime moduli. Then
k_{1}=k_{2},\ l_{1}=l_{2},\ N_{1}=N_{2},\ M_{1}=M_{2}
and
f_{1}=f_{2},\ g_{1}=g_{2}
.

To prove Theorem
1.1
, we first prove an asymptotic for a twisted average of product of central
L
-values. Before proceeding to state the asymptotic, let us set up the following notations. Let
f,g
be a pair of Hecke newforms of weights
k_{1},k_{2}
and levels
N_{1},N_{2}
, respectively. Let
Q_{1}
be a positive real parameter and
q_{2}
be any prime number such that
(q_{2},N_{2})=1
and
q_{2}\asymp Q_{1}^{c}
(for
c\geq 203/100
). We consider the family
\mathcal{F}=\{L(1/2,f\times\chi)L(1/2,g\times\chi\psi),(\chi\text{ mod }q_{1},\psi\text{ mod }q_{2}):\chi,\psi\textit{ primitive },q_{1}\in\mathcal{D}\}
, where
\mathcal{D}
is the set defined as below:

(1.2)

\mathcal{D}:=\{q_{1}\text{ prime }:Q_{1}<q_{1}<2Q_{1},(q_{1},N_{1}N_{2})=1\}.

For a twisted average of the product of
L
-values
L(1/2,f\times\chi)L(1/2,g\times\chi\psi)
in the family
\mathcal{F}
, we prove the following asymptotic.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
f
,
g
be two newforms of weight
k_{1},k_{2}
and level
N_{1},N_{2}
respectively. Then for any prime
p<\log Q_{1}
with
(p,N_{1}N_{2})=1
, we have

(1.3)

\begin{split}&\sum_{q_{1}\in\mathcal{D}}\sideset{}{{}^{*}}{\sum}_{\chi(q_{1})}\sideset{}{{}^{*}}{\sum}_{\psi(q_{2})}\overline{\chi}(p)\overline{\psi}(p)\overline{G(\chi)}^{2}L(1/2,f\times\chi)L(1/2,g\times\chi\psi)\\
&=\frac{\lambda_{g}(p)}{p^{1/2}}\pi_{2}(Q_{1})q_{2}+O(Q_{1}^{599/200+\epsilon}q_{2})+O(Q_{1}^{701/200+\epsilon}q_{2}^{3/4+\epsilon}),\end{split}

where the constants depend only on
\epsilon
,
f
and
g
. Here
\sum^{*}
- denotes that the summation is over the primitive characters and
\pi_{r}(X)
denotes the sum over prime powers, defined as below:

(1.4)

\pi_{r}(X):=\sum\nolimits_{X<p<2X}p^{r}.

Using partial summation and the Prime number theorem it can be easily established that

\pi_{r}(X)\asymp\frac{X^{r}\pi(X)(2^{r+1}-1)}{r+1}.

Thus for a fixed
p
, such that
\lambda_{g}(p)\neq 0
, the main term in (
1.3
) is of the magnitude
Q_{1}^{3}q_{2}/\log Q_{1}
. With our choice of the parameters
Q_{1}
and
q_{2}
(i.e.,
q_{2}\gg Q_{1}^{203/100}
), for every small enough
\epsilon
, the main term is always bigger in magnitude than the error terms. Among the error terms, writing
q_{2}=Q_{1}^{c}
for
c\geq 203/100
, we note that

Q_{1}^{599/200+\epsilon}q_{2}\begin{cases}\ll Q_{1}^{701/200+\epsilon}q_{2}^{3/4+\epsilon}&\text{ if }203/100\leq c\leq 51/25;\\
\gg Q_{1}^{701/200+\epsilon}q_{2}^{3/4+\epsilon}&\text{ if }c>51/25.\end{cases}

Here we want to make a note of the fact that as per the methods of this article
c
can be taken anything bigger than
101/50
with a cost of different power-saving error terms than mentioned in (
1.3
). However, for the sake of not complicating the exposition, we take
c\geq 203/100
. We also note that
p
can be taken to be
\ll Q_{1}^{\delta}
for some small
\delta>0
. But in order to avoid keeping track of an additional parameter during the course of calculation, we take
p\ll\log Q_{1}
.

To see that Theorem
1.1
follows from Theorem
1.2
, we observe that by taking
q_{2}=Q_{1}^{5/2}+O(Q_{1}^{2})
and
Q_{1}\rightarrow\infty
in Theorem
1.2
and invoking the strong multiplicity-one result for the elliptic newforms on
g_{1}
and
g_{2}
, we can conclude
g_{1}=g_{2}
. After this, we prove a result about twisted average of central
L
-values over prime moduli (see Lemma
5.1
). Then we first apply it on
g\times\chi
to show that for any fixed primitive
\chi\bmod{q_{1}}
there exists a primitive
\psi\bmod{q_{2}}
such that
L(1/2,g\times\chi\psi)
is non-zero. This leads us to apply Lemma
5.1
again on
f_{1}
and
f_{2}
separately. Eventually, by invoking the strong multiplicity-one theorem, we conclude
f_{1}=f_{2}
.

It is worth mentioning here that
[
Blo+18
, Theorem 5.1]
has a stronger asymptotic result for a twisted average of central values, where the average is taken over the primitive characters of a single prime moduli. After taking
l=1,l^{\prime}=p
in the main term of
[
Blo+18
, Theorem 5.1]
, we apply the Hecke relation for
\lambda_{f}(np)
and
\lambda_{g}(np)
in
[
Blo+18
, (5.2)]
and write the main term in terms of polynomials in
\lambda_{f}(p)
and
\lambda_{g}(p)
(after a suitable truncation of
n
in terms of
q
). We see that in the asymptotic expression of
\mathcal{Q}(f,g,1/2;1,p)
in
[
Blo+18
, Theorem 5.1]
, the error term in terms of
q
is
q^{-1/144+\epsilon}
and the main term has
p^{1/2}
in the denominator. Thus to have the main term dominate the error term one needs to take
p\ll q^{1/72}
. Hence, the polynomials in
\lambda_{f}(p)
and
\lambda_{g}(p)
will be of degree at least
72
due to the size of the error term in terms of
q
. Thus, to conclude the unique determination of a pair
(f,g)
from their result boils it down to the following question.

Question–
Let
f_{1},g_{1},f_{2},g_{2}
be holomorphic non-CM newforms. Let
P_{1},P_{2},P_{3},P_{4}
be four one variable polynomials of degree at least
72
, such that
P_{1}(\lambda_{f_{1}}(p))+P_{2}(\lambda_{g_{1}}(p))=P_{3}(\lambda_{f_{2}}(p))+P_{4}(\lambda_{g_{2}}(p))
for all but finitely many primes
p
. Then
(f_{1},g_{1})=(f_{2},g_{2})
.

The solution to the above question does not seem to be trivial. Even a simpler version of the question after dropping
g_{1},g_{2}
and
P_{2},P_{4}
seems rather non-trivial. As unique determination of a pair
(f,g)
is the main aim of this article, the Theorem 5.1 in
[
Blo+18
]
does not seem to be employable here.

The proof of Theorem
1.2
begins by expanding the left-hand side of (
1.3
) by using the approximate functional equations (AFEs) for
L(1/2,f\times\chi)
and
L(1/2,g\times\chi\psi)
(see section
2.1
). Naturally, this transforms the problem into estimating an average over the Hecke-eigenvalues with the character twists. The main term of the asymptotic comes from estimating certain character sums associated simultaneously with the
\lambda_{f}(1)
and
\lambda_{g}(p)
, that appear in the
dual
and the
non-dual
parts of the approximate functional equations of
L(1/2,f\times\chi)
and
L(1/2,g\times\chi\psi)
, respectively.

The other parts of the AFE, contribute to the error terms. By introducing the Gauss sum
\overline{G(\chi)}^{2}
, we can reduce the problem to dealing with the product of classical Kloosterman sums when estimating the error terms.
Otherwise, it would have resulted in estimating the averages of hyper-Kloosterman sums of degree
3
. A significant component of the error term estimation entails a meticulous analysis of the average of Kloosterman sums derived from the dual components of the AFEs of
L(1/2,f\times\chi)
and
L(1/2,g\times\chi\psi)
. The additional sum over the character
\psi\text{ mod }q_{2}
plays a vital role in this estimate, without which the asymptotic would
fail by
Q_{1}^{\epsilon}
.

Another consequence of the asymptotic in Theorem
1.2
is the following simultaneous non-vanishing result for the central values in the family
\mathcal{F}
. For any prime number
q_{2}
such that
(q_{2},N_{2})=1
and
q_{2}\asymp Q_{1}^{c}
(
c\geq 203/100
), let
\mathcal{M}
denote the set of characters given by

(1.5)

\{(\chi\text{ mod }q_{1},\psi\text{ mod }q_{2}):\chi,\psi\textit{ primitive },q_{1}\in\mathcal{D},L(1/2,f\times\chi)L(1/2,g\times\chi\psi)\neq 0\}.

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
f
,
g
be two newforms of weight
k_{1},k_{2}
and level
N_{1},N_{2}
respectively. Then we have

(1.6)

\#\mathcal{M}\gg\frac{Q_{1}^{2}}{(\log Q_{1})^{3}}q_{2}^{1-1/c-\epsilon},

as
Q_{1}\rightarrow\infty
. The implied constants depend only on
f
and
g
.

Let us call the ratio
\mathcal{P}=\frac{|\mathcal{M}|}{|\mathcal{F}|}
as the proportion of non-vanishing. Then from Theorem
1.3
we can conclude that
\mathcal{P}\gg 1/q_{2}^{1/c+\epsilon}(\log Q_{1})^{2}
. The estimate (
1.6
) for the size of
\mathcal{M}
is obtained by employing the large sieve inequality for Dirichlet characters and the asymptotic for the twisted average from Theorem
1.2
. An important point to note here is the choice of
p
in Theorem
1.2
. Using the Prime number theorem, we show that it is possible to choose
p
such that
\lambda_{g}(p)
is greater than an absolute constant and with
p\ll_{k_{2},N_{2}}1
. This allows us to obtain the estimate (
1.6
) for
\mathcal{M}
, which is independent of the prime
p
.

2.
Preliminaries

2.1.
Approximate functional equation:

Let
h
be a newform of weight
k
and level
N
. Let
\chi
be a Dirichlet character with conductor
q
with
(q,N)=1
. Denote by
G(\chi)
, the Gauss sum associated with
\chi
. Then we have (see
[
IK21
, Theorem 5.3]

(2.1)

L(1/2,h\times\chi)=\sum_{n\geq 1}\frac{\lambda_{h}(n)\chi(n)}{\sqrt{n}}V_{h}\left(\frac{nX}{q_{h,\chi}^{1/2}}\right)+\frac{G(\chi)^{2}}{q}\sum_{n\geq 1}\frac{\overline{\lambda_{h}(n)\chi(n)}}{\sqrt{n}}V_{h}\left(\frac{n}{Xq_{h,\chi}^{1/2}}\right),

where
X>0
,
q_{h,\chi}=Nq^{2}
and the smooth function
V_{h}(y)
is given by

(2.2)

V_{h}(y)=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{(3)}y^{-s}\frac{\gamma_{h}(\tfrac{1}{2}+s)}{\gamma_{h}(\tfrac{1}{2})}\frac{ds}{s}

and it satisfies the following properties:

(2.3)

V_{h}(y)=1+O(y^{1/4}),

(2.4)

V_{h}(y)\ll(1+\frac{y}{k^{2}})^{-A}\quad\text{ for any }A>0.

For the sake of simplicity, whenever the form
h
is fixed and
\chi
is varying, we adopt the notation
q_{\chi}:=q_{h,\chi}
.

In the rest of the article, the two terms in the right-hand side of (
2.1
) are referred to as
non-dual
and
dual
parts of the approximate functional equation, respectively.

2.2.
Some character relations:

Let
r,n,m
be any positive integer such that
(r,m)=1
and
(n,m)=1
. Then we have

(2.5)

\sideset{}{{}^{*}}{\sum}_{\chi\text{ mod }m}\chi(n)\overline{\chi}(r)=\phi(m)\delta_{n\equiv r\bmod m}-1,

(2.6)

\sideset{}{{}^{*}}{\sum}_{\chi\text{ mod }m}G(\chi)^{2}\overline{\chi}(r)=\phi(m)S(1,r;m)-1.

Let
\chi,\psi
be primitive characters with conductors
q_{1},q_{2}
respectively with
(q_{1},q_{2})=1
. Then we have

(2.7)

G(\chi\psi)=G(\chi)G(\psi)\chi(q_{2})\psi(q_{1}).

2.3.
The set-up:

Let
f,g
be two modular forms of weight
k_{1},k_{2}
and level
N_{1},N_{2}
respectively. For a fixed prime
p
, we consider the sum

(2.8)

\mathbf{S}=\sum_{q_{1}\in\mathcal{D}}\sideset{}{{}^{*}}{\sum}_{\chi(q_{1})}\sideset{}{{}^{*}}{\sum}_{\psi(q_{2})}\overline{\chi}(p)\overline{\psi}(p)\overline{G(\chi)}^{2}L(1/2,f\times\chi)L(1/2,g\times\chi\psi).

Using the approximate functional equation from (
2.1
) for the
L
-values
L(1/2,f\times\chi)
and
L(1/2,g\times\chi\psi)
, we write

(2.9)

\mathbf{S}=\mathbf{S}_{nn}+\mathbf{S}_{nd}+\mathbf{S}_{dn}+\mathbf{S}_{dd},

where the terms
\mathbf{S}_{**}
correspond to the dual and non-dual parts of the approximate functional equation and are given by-

(2.10)

\displaystyle\mathbf{S}_{nn}

\displaystyle=\sum_{q_{1}\in\mathcal{D}}\sideset{}{{}^{*}}{\sum}_{\chi(q_{1})}\sideset{}{{}^{*}}{\sum}_{\psi(q_{2})}\sum_{m,n}\overline{G(\chi)}^{2}\overline{\chi}(\bar{m}np)\overline{\psi}(np)\frac{\lambda_{f}(m)\lambda_{g}(n)}{(mn)^{1/2}}V_{f}\Big(\tfrac{mX}{q_{\chi}^{1/2}}\Big)V_{g}\Big(\tfrac{nY}{q_{\chi\psi}^{1/2}}\Big),

(2.11)

\displaystyle\mathbf{S}_{nd}

\displaystyle=\sum_{q_{1}\in\mathcal{D}}\sideset{}{{}^{*}}{\sum}_{\chi(q_{1})}\sideset{}{{}^{*}}{\sum}_{\psi(q_{2})}\sum_{m,n}\frac{q_{1}G(\psi)^{2}}{q_{2}}\overline{\chi}(\bar{m}np\bar{q_{2}}^{2})\overline{\psi}(np\bar{q_{1}}^{2})\frac{\lambda_{f}(m)\lambda_{g}(n)}{(mn)^{1/2}}V_{f}\left(\tfrac{mX}{q_{\chi}^{1/2}}\right)V_{g}\left(\tfrac{n}{Yq_{\chi\psi}^{1/2}}\right),

(2.12)

\displaystyle\mathbf{S}_{dn}

\displaystyle=\sum_{q_{1}\in\mathcal{D}}\sideset{}{{}^{*}}{\sum}_{\chi(q_{1})}\sideset{}{{}^{*}}{\sum}_{\psi(q_{2})}\sum_{m,n}\frac{|G(\chi)|^{4}}{q_{1}}\chi(\bar{m}n\bar{p})\psi(n\bar{p})\frac{\lambda_{f}(m)\lambda_{g}(n)}{(mn)^{1/2}}V_{f}\Big(\tfrac{m}{Xq_{\chi}^{1/2}}\Big)V_{g}\Big(\tfrac{nY}{q_{\chi\psi}^{1/2}}\Big),

(2.13)

\displaystyle\mathbf{S}_{dd}

\displaystyle=\sum_{q_{1}\in\mathcal{D}}\sideset{}{{}^{*}}{\sum}_{\chi(q_{1})}\sideset{}{{}^{*}}{\sum}_{\psi(q_{2})}\sum_{m,n}\frac{G(\psi)^{2}G(\chi)^{2}}{q_{2}}\overline{\chi}(mnp\bar{q_{2}}^{2})\overline{\psi}(np\bar{q_{1}}^{2})\frac{\lambda_{f}(m)\lambda_{g}(n)}{(mn)^{1/2}}V_{f}\Big(\tfrac{m}{Xq_{\chi}^{1/2}}\Big)V_{g}\Big(\tfrac{n}{Yq_{\chi\psi}^{1/2}}\Big).

3.
Main Term

3.1.
Evaluating
\mathbf{S}_{dn}
:

First, from the approximate functional equations, we consider the
dual
part of
L(1/2,f\times\chi)
and the
non-dual
part of
L(1/2,g\times\chi\psi)
. Let us denote its contribution to
\mathbf{S}
by
\mathbf{S}_{dn}
. A straight-forward calculation gives us

(3.1)

\begin{split}\mathbf{S}_{dn}&=\sum_{q_{1}\in\mathcal{D}}\sum_{m,n}q_{1}\frac{\lambda_{f}(m)\lambda_{g}(n)}{(mn)^{1/2}}\Big(\sideset{}{{}^{*}}{\sum}_{\chi(q_{1})}\chi(\bar{m}n\bar{p})\Big)\Big(\sideset{}{{}^{*}}{\sum}_{\psi(q_{2})}\psi(n\bar{p})\Big)V_{f}\Big(\tfrac{m}{Xq_{\chi}^{1/2}}\Big)V_{g}\Big(\tfrac{nY}{q_{\chi\psi}^{1/2}}\Big).\\
\end{split}

By evaluating the character sums using (
2.5
) and (
2.6
), we can write

(3.2)

\mathbf{S}_{dn}=U_{1}-U_{2}-U_{3}+U_{4},

where
U_{1},U_{2},U_{3},U_{4}
are given by

(3.3)

U_{1}=\sum_{q_{1}\in\mathcal{D}}q_{1}\phi(q_{1}q_{2})\underset{n\equiv mp\bmod{q_{1}}}{\underset{n\equiv p\bmod{q_{2}}}{\sum\nolimits_{m,n}}}\frac{\lambda_{f}(m)\lambda_{g}(n)}{(mn)^{1/2}}V_{f}\Big(\tfrac{m}{Xq_{\chi}^{1/2}}\Big)V_{g}\Big(\tfrac{nY}{q_{\chi\psi}^{1/2}}\Big),

(3.4)

U_{2}=\sum_{q_{1}\in\mathcal{D}}q_{1}\phi(q_{1})\underset{n\equiv mp\bmod{q_{1}}}{\underset{n\not\equiv p\bmod{q_{2}}}{\sum\nolimits_{m,n}}}\frac{\lambda_{f}(m)\lambda_{g}(n)}{(mn)^{1/2}}V_{f}\Big(\tfrac{m}{Xq_{\chi}^{1/2}}\Big)V_{g}\Big(\tfrac{nY}{q_{\chi\psi}^{1/2}}\Big),

(3.5)

U_{3}=\sum_{q_{1}\in\mathcal{D}}q_{1}\phi(q_{2})\underset{n\not\equiv mp\bmod{q_{1}}}{\underset{n\equiv p\bmod{q_{2}}}{\sum\nolimits_{m,n}}}\frac{\lambda_{f}(m)\lambda_{g}(n)}{(mn)^{1/2}}V_{f}\Big(\tfrac{m}{Xq_{\chi}^{1/2}}\Big)V_{g}\Big(\tfrac{nY}{q_{\chi\psi}^{1/2}}\Big),

(3.6)

U_{4}=\sum_{q_{1}\in\mathcal{D}}q_{1}\underset{n\not\equiv mp\bmod{q_{1}}}{\underset{n\not\equiv p\bmod{q_{2}}}{\sum\nolimits_{m,n}}}\frac{\lambda_{f}(m)\lambda_{g}(n)}{(mn)^{1/2}}V_{f}\Big(\tfrac{m}{Xq_{\chi}^{1/2}}\Big)V_{g}\Big(\tfrac{nY}{q_{\chi\psi}^{1/2}}\Big).

Using the decay of
V_{f}
and
V_{g}
from (
2.4
), the sums over
m
and
n
can be truncated at
\ll_{k_{1},N_{1}}q_{1}^{1+\epsilon}X
and
\ll_{k_{2},N_{2}}(q_{1}q_{2})^{1+\epsilon}/Y
respectively with negligible error in
Q_{1}
and
q_{2}
. Thus for the rest of the calculation we restrict to
m\ll_{k_{1},N_{1}}q_{1}^{1+\epsilon}X
and
n\ll_{k_{2},N_{2}}(q_{1}q_{2})^{1+\epsilon}/Y
.

Calculation of
U_{1}
: We note that

\{(m,n)|n\equiv p\bmod{q_{2}},n\equiv mp\bmod{q_{1}},m\leq q_{1}^{1+\epsilon}X,n\leq(q_{1}q_{2})^{1+\epsilon}/Y\}=A_{1}\cup A_{2}\cup A_{3},

where

(3.7)

\displaystyle A_{1}

\displaystyle=\{(m,n)|m=1,n=p\},

\displaystyle A_{2}

\displaystyle=\{(m,n)|m>q_{1},n=p,m\equiv 1(\bmod q_{1}),m\leq q_{1}^{1+\epsilon}X\},

\displaystyle A_{3}

\displaystyle=\{(m,n)|n>q_{2},n\equiv p\bmod{q_{2}},n\equiv mp\bmod{q_{1}},m\leq q_{1}^{1+\epsilon}X,n\leq(q_{1}q_{2})^{1+\epsilon}/Y\}.

Let the summand of
U_{1}
under the three disjoint sets
A_{1},A_{2},A_{3}
be
U_{1,1},U_{1,2},U_{1,3}
respectively. Using (
2.3
) and the fact that
q_{\chi}\asymp_{k_{1},N_{1}}q_{1}^{2},q_{\chi\psi}\asymp_{k_{2},N_{2}}(q_{1}q_{2})^{2}
, we calculate

(3.8)

\begin{split}U_{1,1}&=\frac{\lambda_{g}(p)}{p^{1/2}}\sum_{q_{1}\in\mathcal{D}}q_{1}\phi(q_{1}q_{2})+O(Q_{1}^{11/4}q_{2}X^{-1/4})+O(Q_{1}^{11/4}q_{2}^{3/4}Y^{1/4}).\\
&=\frac{\lambda_{g}(p)}{p^{1/2}}\pi_{2}(Q_{1})q_{2}+O(Q_{1}^{11/4}q_{2}X^{-1/4})+O(Q_{1}^{11/4}q_{2}^{3/4}Y^{1/4}).\end{split}

For
U_{1,2}
, the number of
m\leq q_{1}^{1+\epsilon}X
, satisfying
m\equiv 1\bmod{q_{1}}
is
O(q_{1}^{\epsilon}X)
. Thus using (
2.4
) we calculate

(3.9)

Clouds in Gromov–Hausdorff Class: their completeness and centers
S. I. Bogataya, S. A. Bogatyy, V. V. Redkozubov, A. A. Tuzhilin

Abstract
We consider the proper class of all metric spaces endowed with the Gromov--Hausdorff distance. Its maximal subclasses, consisting of the spaces on finite distance from each other, we call clouds. Multiplying all distances in a metric space by the same positive real number, we obtain a similarity transformation of the Gromov--Hausdorff class. In our previous work, we observed that with such a transformation, some clouds can jump to others. To characterize the phenomenon, we studied the stabilizers of the similarity action. In this paper, we prove that every cloud with a nontrivial stabilizer has a center, i.e., a metric space for which all similarities from the stabilizer generate a new space at zero distance. Moreover, the center is unique modulo zero distance. The proof is based on the cloud completeness theorem. 1 1 1 The authors thank P.A. Borodin, E.A. Reznichenko and O.V. Sipacheva. The work was done in Moscow State University, A.A Tuzhilin was supported by RSF grant N21-11-00355. Keywords: Metric space, Gromov–Hausdorff distance, group action

Introduction

The present work is devoted to the geometry of the Gromov–Hausdorff distance

defined on the collection of all non-empty metric spaces. It is well-known that this distance is a generalized pseudometric vanishing on isometric spaces (“generalize” means that infinite distances may occur, and the prefix “pseudo-” that the distances between different spaces may vanish). Traditionally, the distance is studied on the Gromov–Hausdorff space, in which all metric spaces are compact
[
4

and considered up to isometry.

M. Gromov in his “Metric structures for Riemannian and non-Riemannian spaces”
[
3

made a short remark: “One can also make a moduli space of isometry classes of non-compact spaces

lying within a finite Hausdorff distance from a given

, e.g.

. Such moduli spaces are also complete and contractible.” This observation was not proved in
[
3

because it probably seemed obvious.

In the present paper we will prove the completeness of the Gromov–Hausdorff distance in general. Let us note that our proof is not difficult indeed, and we present it mainly to show how the use of correspondences instead of mappings may simplify the construction of direct limits. Our study of stabilizers is based on the completeness theorem, which we considered possible to use only after proof has been presented. Moreover, we cannot prove the second statement of the last sentence of Gromov’s quotation and we fully admit that it may turn out to be non-trivial or even incorrect.

In von Neumann-Bernays-Gödel (NGB) set theory, all objects are called
classes
. A distance function can be specified on each class. We will use the following terminology:

•

a
distance function
or, in short, a

on a class

is an arbitrary mapping
\rho\colon\mathcal{A}\times\mathcal{A}\to[0,\infty]
, for which always
\rho(x,x)=0
and
\rho(x,y)=\rho(y,x)
(symmetry) hold;

•

if the distance satisfies the triangle inequality, i.e., if
\rho(x,z)\leq\rho(x,y)+\rho(y,z)
is always satisfied, then
\rho
is called
generalized pseudometric
(the word “generalized” corresponds to the possibility of taking the value

•

if the positive definiteness condition is additionally satisfied for a generalized pseudometric, i.e., if
\rho(x,y)=0
always implies
x=y
, then we call such
\rho

generalized metric
;

•

finally, if
\rho
does not take the value
\infty
, then in the above definitions we will omit the word “generalized”, and sometimes, to emphasize the absence of
\infty
, we call this distance
finite
.

As is customary in metric geometry, instead of
\rho(x,y)
we write
|xy|
as a rule.

Let us now recall the definitions we need from metric geometry, namely, the Hausdorff and Gromov–Hausdorff distances.

1.1
Hausdorff distance

Let
X
be an arbitrary metric space,
x\in X
,
r>0
and
s\geq 0
be real numbers. By
U_{r}(x)
and
B_{s}(x)
we denote, respectively, the
open
and
closed balls
centered at the point

and with the radii
r
and
s
. If
A
and
B
are non-empty subsets of
X
, then we put
|AB|=|BA|=\inf\bigl\{|ab|:a\in A,\,b\in B\bigr\}
. Next, we define the
open
r

by setting
U_{r}(A)=\bigl\{x\in X:|xA|<r\bigr\}
. Finally, the
Hausdorff distance
between
A
and
B
is the value

d_{H}(A,B)=\inf\bigl\{r:A\subset U_{r}(B),\ \ B\subset U_{r}(A)\bigr\}.

The Hausdorff distance is a generalized pseudometric: it can be infinite, as in the case of the straight line
\mathbb{R}
and any of its points, and also equal to zero between different subsets, for example, between the segment
[0,1]
and the interval
(0,1)
. It is important to note that the distance between a set and its dense subset is zero. Therefore, the distance between a metric space and its completion is zero. As a result, one can often assume the completeness of the space under consideration. Nevertheless, on the set consisting of all non-empty bounded closed subsets of a metric space
X
, the Hausdorff distance is a metric.

1.2
Gromov–Hausdorff distance

We denote by
\operatorname{\mathcal{VG\!H}}
the class consisting of all non-empty metric spaces. On this class, we define a distance function called the
Gromov–Hausdorff distance
:

d_{GH}(X,Y)=\inf\bigl\{d_{H}(X^{\prime},Y^{\prime}):X^{\prime},Y^{\prime}\subset Z\in\operatorname{\mathcal{VG\!H}},\,X^{\prime}\approx X,\,Y^{\prime}\approx Y\bigr\},

where for the metric spaces
U
and
V
the expression
U\approx V
means that these spaces are isometric.

The following theorem is well known.

Theorem 1

(
[
4
]
)
.

The Gromov–Hausdorff distance is a generalized pseudometric vanishing on each pair of isometric spaces.

This theorem allows us to investigate the Gromov–Hausdorff distance on a “less wild” so called
Gromov–Hausdorff class

\operatorname{\mathcal{G\!H}}
obtained from
\operatorname{\mathcal{VG\!H}}

Together with

Consider the relation

Since our base object is a metric space, we will denote by Latin capital letter

\operatorname{\mathcal{VG\!H}}

is also understood as an element in
\operatorname{\mathcal{G\!H}}

we denote the class of all spaces

, i.e.

is class of all spaces on zero distance from

and can be considered as element of

. The cloud of the space

and clouds can be considered as elements of

The main question investigated in the paper can be informally formulated as follows.

Is it possible to find a center in a cloud of unbounded space, i.e. such a space that, similarly to the

2
Completeness of the Gromov–Hausdorff class

The purpose of this subsection is to prove one more fundamental property of the Gromov–Hausdorff class. Since we will use in our proof the technique of correspondences, let us recall the definition and some necessary well-known results.

Let
X
and
Y
be some sets. A
relation
between
X
and
Y
is an arbitrary subset
R\subset X\times Y
. If
X,Y\in\operatorname{\mathcal{VG\!H}}
are non-empty metric spaces, and
\sigma
is a non-empty relation, its
distortion
\operatorname{dis}\sigma
is defined as follows:

\operatorname{dis}\sigma=\sup\Bigl\{\bigl||xx^{\prime}|-|yy^{\prime}|\bigr|:(x,y),\,(x^{\prime},y^{\prime})\in\sigma\Bigr\}.

A relation
R\subset X\times Y
between sets
X
and
Y
is called a
correspondence
if for any
x\in X
there exists
y\in Y
, and for any
y\in Y
there exists
x\in X
, such that
(x,y)\in R
. Thus, the correspondences can be considered as multivalued surjective mappings.

For a correspondence
R\subset X\times Y
and
x\in X
, we put
R(x)=\big\{y\in Y:(x,y)\in R\big\}
and call
R(x)
the
image of the element
x
under the correspondence
R
. By
\mathcal{R}(X,Y)
we denote the set of all correspondences between
X
and
Y
. The following result is well-known.

Theorem 3

(see
[
4
]
)
.

For any
X,Y\in\operatorname{\mathcal{VG\!H}}
, it holds

d_{GH}(X,Y)=\frac{1}{2}\inf\bigl\{\operatorname{dis}R:R\in\mathcal{R}(X,Y)\bigr\}.

Theorem 4
.

The Gromov–Hausdorff class
\operatorname{\mathcal{VG\!H}}
is complete. In particular, all clouds are complete.

Proof.

Let
X_{1},X_{2},\ldots
be a fundamental sequence. Without loss of generality, we will assume that
2d_{GH}(X_{n},X_{n+1})\leq 1/2^{n}
. For each
n
, choose a correspondence
R_{n}\in\mathcal{R}(X_{n},X_{n+1})
such that
\operatorname{dis}R_{n}<1/2^{n}
. We put
\overline{X}=\sqcup_{n=1}^{\infty}X_{n}
and for each
x_{1}\in X_{1}
we call the sequence
x_{1},x_{2},\ldots\in\overline{X}
a
thread starting at
x_{1}
, if for any
n\geq 1
it holds
x_{n+1}\in R_{n}(x_{n})
. We denote the set of all such threads by
N(\overline{X})
. For threads
\nu=(x_{1},x_{2},\ldots)
and
\nu^{\prime}=(x^{\prime}_{1},x^{\prime}_{2},\ldots)
consider the sequence
|x_{1}x^{\prime}_{1}|,|x_{2}x^{\prime}_{2}|,\ldots
of real numbers. Since
\operatorname{dis}R_{n}<1/2^{n}
, then
\bigl||x_{n}x^{\prime}_{n}|-|x_{n+1}x^{\prime}_{n+1}|\bigr|<1/2^{n}
, whence for any
m\geq 1
we have

\bigl||x_{n}x^{\prime}_{n}|-|x_{n+m}x^{\prime}_{n+m}|\bigr|=\\
\bigl||x_{n}x^{\prime}_{n}|-|x_{n+1}x^{\prime}_{n+1}|+|x_{n+1}x^{\prime}_{n+1}|-\cdots-|x_{n+m}x^{\prime}_{n+m}|\bigr|\leq\\
\sum_{k=1}^{m}\bigl||x_{n+k-1}x^{\prime}_{n+k-1}|-|x_{n+k}x^{\prime}_{n+k}|\bigr|<\sum_{k=1}^{m}\frac{1}{2^{n+k-1}}<\frac{1}{2^{n-1}},

therefore, the sequence of numbers
|x_{1}x^{\prime}_{1}|,|x_{2}x^{\prime}_{2}|,\ldots
is fundamental and, therefore, there is a limit, which we denote by
|\nu\nu^{\prime}|
. It is clear that these limits define a distance function on
N(\overline{X})
. Since for any three threads
\nu=(x_{1},x_{2},\ldots)
,
\nu^{\prime}=(x^{\prime}_{1},x^{\prime}_{2},\ldots)
,
\nu^{\prime\prime}=(x^{\prime\prime}_{1},x^{\prime\prime}_{2},\ldots)
, and for each
n
, the triangle inequalities hold for the points
x_{n},x^{\prime}_{n},x^{\prime\prime}_{n}
, the same is true for the distances between
\nu
,
\nu^{\prime}
and
\nu^{\prime\prime}
. Thus, we have defined a pseudometrics on
N(\overline{X})
. Having factorized the resulting pseudometric space with respect to the equivalence relation generated by zero distances, we obtain a metric space, which we denote by
X
. For a thread
\nu\in N(\overline{X})
, the class of this equivalence containing
\nu
will be denoted by
[\nu]
.

For each
n
, consider the relation
R^{\prime}_{n}\subset X\times X_{n}
defined as follows: for each
x\in X
, consider all threads
\nu=(x_{1},x_{2},\ldots,x_{n},\ldots)\in x
and put all
(x,x_{n})
into
R^{\prime}_{n}
. Since each
x_{n}
belongs to some thread, then
R^{\prime}\in\mathcal{R}(X,X_{n})
. Let us estimate the distortion of the correspondence
R^{\prime}
. Choose arbitrary
x,x^{\prime}\in X
, threads
\nu=(x_{1},x_{2},\ldots,x_{n},\ldots)\in x
and
\nu^{\prime}=(x^{\prime}_{1},x^{\prime}_{2},\ldots,x^{\prime}_{n},\ldots)\in x^{\prime}
, then
|\nu\nu^{\prime}|=\lim_{k\to\infty}|x_{k}x^{\prime}_{k}|
, and as shown above,
\bigl||x_{n}x^{\prime}_{n}|-|x_{n+m}x^{\prime}_{n+m}|\bigr|<1/2^{n-1}
, whence
\bigl||x_{n}x^{\prime}_{n}|-|\nu\nu^{\prime}|\bigr|\leq 1/2^{n-1}
. Thus,
\operatorname{dis}R^{\prime}\leq 1/2^{n-1}
, so
X_{n}\xrightarrow{\operatorname{\mathcal{G\!H}}}X
.
∎

3
Collective isometric embedding

One of the famous M. Gromov theorems states that for each totally bounded family
\mathcal{C}
consisting of non-empty compact metric spaces, there exists a compact set
K
of the Banach space
\ell_{\infty}
such that each space
X\in\mathcal{C}
can be isometrically embedded into
K
, see
[
4
]
for example. We need a rather simple result: let we are given with

•

a family
\mathcal{C}
of (not necessarily compact) metric spaces;

•

a tree
G
(connected acyclic graph) with vertex set
\mathcal{C}
and edge set
E
;

•

a “weight” function
\omega
: for each
e\in E
we choose an arbitrary correspondence
R_{XY}\in\mathcal{R}(X,Y)
, we put
R_{YX}=R_{XY}^{-1}\in\mathcal{R}(Y,X)
, and we define
\omega(e)=\frac{1}{2}\operatorname{dis}R_{XY}=\frac{1}{2}\operatorname{dis}R_{YX}
;

•

we assume that for all
e\in E
we have
0<\omega(e)<\infty
.

We wish to construct a metric on
Z:=\sqcup_{X\in\mathcal{C}}X
which extends the metrics of all the
X
in such a way that
d_{H}(X,Y)=\omega(XY)
for all
XY\in E
. To do that, we use the ideas described in
[
10

From a metric space

Proposition 8

(

Proposition 9

Remark 3

Proposition

Example 1

Consider the space
\ell_{1}
. Enumerate the coordinate lines (the basic vectors) by the positive integers, together with the
\infty

) with the coordinate

n

Let us form two spaces

Proposition 22
.

The
X
and

are countable complete bounded metric spaces. The space
X
is discrete. The space

has exactly one non-isolated point, namely,

. It holds

.

Proof.

If we place the both

and

on the same coordinate line, we get

In contrast to the discrete hedgehog, not only the ends of the needles are considered, but also some intermediate points on the needles. The distance in them is intrinsic, i.e., for points on different needles it is measured through the center — the zero point. The mapping

Corollary 26
.

If
d_{GH}\bigl(T,\widehat{\mathbb{Q}_{+}}\bigr)=0
for a metric space
T

.

Proof.

Let
T

21
, we can assume that
T=\widehat{X}

. According to Corollary
23
, the set
X

Theorem 27
.

The center
Z[\widehat{\mathbb{Q}_{+}}]
has the following properties
:

(1)

For every countable dense subgroup
H\subseteq\mathbb{R}_{+}
the hedgehog
\hat{H}
is in the center
Z[\widehat{\mathbb{Q}_{+}}]
and
\operatorname{St}\hat{H}=H
.

(2)

|Z[\widehat{\mathbb{Q}_{+}}]|=\mathfrak{c}=2^{\aleph_{0}}
.

Proof.

The validity of the first assertion follows from Corollaries
23
and
18
.

Any space from the center is a hedgehog over a countable subset of the product
(0,\infty)\times\mathbb{N}
. There are exactly continuum of countable subsets in this set. So the inequality
\bigl|Z[\widehat{\mathbb{Q}_{+}}]\bigr|\leq\mathfrak{c}=2^{\aleph_{0}}
is proved. The group
\mathbb{R}_{+}
has exactly continuum of dense countable subgroups, so the reverse inequality follows from the first assertion.
∎

Remark 4
.

In fact, for any subgroup

is constructed by a more complicated procedure, then we drop it.

Corollary

Corollary 28

For a subgroup

Theorem 29
.

The center
Z[\widehat{\mathbb{R}_{+}}]
has the following properties
:

(1)

H\subseteq\mathbb{R}_{+}
of cardinality
\mathfrak{c}
, the hedgehog
\hat{H}

(2)

\bigl|Z[\widehat{\mathbb{R}_{+}}]\bigr|=2^{\mathfrak{c}}=2^{2^{\aleph_{0}}}

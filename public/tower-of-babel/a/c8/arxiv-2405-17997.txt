Abstract
. We show that the cone multiplier satisfies local L p L^{p} - L q L^{q} bounds only in the trivial range 1 ≤ q ≤ 2 ≤ p ≤ ∞ 1\leq q\leq 2\leq p\leq\infty . To do so, we suitably adapt to this setting the proof of Fefferman for the ball multiplier. As a consequence we answer negatively a question by Békollé and Bonami [ 1 ] , regarding the continuity from L p → L q L^{p}\to L^{q} of the Cauchy-Szegö projections associated with a class of bounded symmetric domains in ℂ n {\mathbb{C}}^{n} with rank r ≥ 2 r\geq 2 .

3.

In this section we show how to extend the validity of Theorem
1.5
from light-cones to general symmetric cones
\Omega
. This requires the use of appropriate coordinates in the underlying space
V={\mathbb{R}}^{n}
, regarding it as a Jordan algebra. In what follows we shall use standard notation and terminology from the text
[
7
]
. For readers less familiar with this terminology, one may consider as a guideline the example
V=\text{Sym}(r,{\mathbb{R}})
with
\Omega
the cone of positive definite symmetric matrices.

Let
\Omega
be an irreducible symmetric cone in
V={\mathbb{R}}^{n}
. As discussed in
[
7
, Ch III]
, this induces in
V
a structure of Euclidean Jordan algebra, which we shall assume of rank
r\geq 2
.
We denote by
{\bf e}
the identity element in
V
, and by
{\Delta}
(or
\det
) the associated determinant function.

An idempotent is a vector
c\in V
such that
c^{2}=c
; it is said to be a
primitive idempotent
if it is non-zero and cannot be written as the sum of two non-zero idempotents. In the case
V=\text{Sym}(r,{\mathbb{R}})
these are the matrices of the form
c_{j}=\mbox{diag }(0,\ldots,1,\ldots,0)
and their rotations
kc_{j}k^{\prime}
,
k\in\text{SO}(r)
.

A complete system of orthogonal primitive idempotents
\{c_{1},\ldots,c_{r}\}
, with
c_{1}+\ldots+c_{r}={\bf e}
, is called a
Jordan frame
. Each Jordan frame induces in
V
an orthogonal decomposition

V=\bigoplus_{1\leq i\leq j\leq r}V_{ij},

which formally allows to regard
V
as
an algebra of symmetric matrices with entries in the subspaces
V_{ij}
.
This is called a
Peirce decomposition
of
V
, see
[
7
, Ch IV]
. With this notation, the cone
\Omega
can in particular be described as

(3.1)

\Omega=\Big\{\xi\in V{\,\,\,\colon\,\,\,}{\Delta}_{\ell}(\xi)>0,\quad\ell=1,2,\ldots,r\Big\},

where the
principal minor

{\Delta}_{\ell}(\xi)
is the determinant of the projection of
\xi
onto the Jordan subalgebra
V^{(\ell)}=\oplus_{1\leq i\leq j\leq\ell}V_{ij}
; see e.g.
[
7

4.
Boundedness of Cauchy-Szegö projections

In this section we describe in more detail the question posed at the end of §1, and derive Corollary
1.7
from Theorem
3.9
and the transfer principle proved in
[
1
]
.

We begin by recalling some basic terminology, referring to the survey paper
[
18
]
for more detailed definitions and further literature.

4.1.
Cauchy-Szegö projection in a bounded symmetric domain

Let
D
be a bounded symmetric domain in
{\mathbb{C}}^{n}
in a standard realization
1
1

1

In particular, as in
[
2
]
, we assume
0\in D
and
D
convex and circular, ie
e^{i\theta}z\in D
for all
\theta\in{\mathbb{R}}
,
z\in D
.
, and let
\Sigma
denote its Shilov boundary. We write
d\sigma
for the (normalized) measure in
\Sigma
which is invariant under the subgroup of conformal automorphisms of
D
that preserve the origin. The Hardy space
H^{p}(D)
,
1\leq p<\infty
, is the set of
holomorphic functions
g:D\to{\mathbb{C}}
such that

\|g\|_{H^{p}}:=\sup_{0<r<1}\Big[\int_{\Sigma}|g(rw)|^{p}\,d\sigma(w)\Big]^{\frac{1}{p}}<\infty.

Every
g\in H^{p}(D)
has a boundary value

g_{0}(w):=\lim_{r\nearrow 1}g(rw),\quad w\in\Sigma,

with convergence in
L^{p}(\Sigma)
(and a.e.), and moreover
\|g\|_{H^{p}}=\|g_{0}\|_{L^{p}(\Sigma)}
; see
[
2
]
or
[
18
, p. 278]
. In particular,
H^{2}(D)
is a Hilbert space, and there is an orthogonal projection
S_{D}:L^{2}(\Sigma)\to H^{2}(D)
.
Since point evaluations are continuous linear functionals in
H^{2}
, this operator can be expressed as

S_{D}g(z)=\int_{\Sigma}S_{D}(z,w)g(w)\,d\sigma(w),\quad z\in D,

where
S_{D}(z,w)
is called the
Cauchy-Szegö kernel
of
D
. Passing to boundary values, we consider the operator, defined at least for
g\in L^{2}(\Sigma)
,

(4.1)

{\mathbb{S}}_{D}g(\xi):=\lim_{r\nearrow 1}\int_{\Sigma}S_{D}(r\xi,w)g(w)\,d\sigma(w),\quad\xi\in\Sigma,

which we shall call the
Cauchy-Szegö projection
of
D
.

Question 1:

For what values
1\leq p,q\leq\infty
there exists a continuous extension of
{\mathbb{S}}_{D}:L^{p}(\Sigma)\to L^{q}(\Sigma)
?

When
D
is the unit ball of
{\mathbb{C}}^{n}
, a classical theorem of Korányi and Vági establishes that
{\mathbb{S}}_{D}
maps
L^{p}\to L^{p}
for all
1<p<\infty
, see
[
11
]
or
[
14
, Ch 6]
; hence the above question has a positive answer of all
1\leq q\leq p\leq\infty
, except at the endpoints
p=q=1
and
p=q=\infty
.

However, for (irreducible) domains
D
of higher rank
\geq 2
the situation is quite different. If
D
is a domain of
tube-type
, that is, conformally equivalent to a tube domain
{T_{\Omega}}={\mathbb{R}}^{n}+i\Omega
over a symmetric cone
\Omega\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
, then Békollé and Bonami showed in
[
1
, Thm 6]
that
{\mathbb{S}}_{D}
maps
L^{p}\to L^{p}
iff
p=2
, and left open the study of
(p,q)
inequalities. Moreover, if
D
is conformally equivalent to a Siegel domain of type II (ie., not of tube type), then the question is completely open, even regarding
L^{p}\to L^{p}
boundedness (for
p\not=2
).

We remark that, although the kernels
S_{D}(z,w)
have geometrically explicit formulas, see e.g.
[
6
]
, these are not so easily manageable for
handling the operator
{\mathbb{S}}_{D}
. A common strategy is to transfer the problem, via conformal mappings, to an unbounded realization of
D
, which makes possible to use Fourier transform techniques. We present in the next subsection the needed terminology from the unbounded setting; see
[
17
, Chapter 3]
or
[
7
, Ch IX.4]

for a more detailed exposition.

4.2.
Cauchy-Szegö projection in tube domains

Let

{T_{\Omega}}:={\mathbb{R}}^{n}+i\Omega\subset{\mathbb{C}}^{n}

(unrestrictedly in

[
17

2
2

2

The pointwise restricted convergence at a.e.

F(w^{\prime}):=c_{0}^{-1}\,\bar{J}_{\Phi}(w^{\prime})^{-1/2}\,f(\Phi(w^{\prime}))\,|J_{\Phi}(w^{\prime})|.

4.5.
An application: duality of
H^{p}
spaces

Let
D
be a bounded symmetric domain in
{\mathbb{C}}^{n}
, and let
H^{p}
,
1\leq p<\infty
, be the associated Hardy space as in §
4.1
.
It is well-known that, with the usual duality pairing, the (isomorphic) identity

(H^{p})^{*}=H^{p^{\prime}}

holds under the assumption that
{\mathbb{S}}_{D}:L^{p}\to L^{p}
is bounded (where as usual,
\frac{1}{p}+\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}=1
).
In particular, if
D
is the unit ball of
{\mathbb{C}}^{n}
, the identity holds for all
1<p<\infty
.
However, in (irreducible) bounded symmetric domains of higher rank, where the boundedness of
{\mathbb{S}}_{D}
fails (or is unknown),
the structure of the dual space
(H^{p})^{*}
is still quite open; see e.g. the question posed in
[
20
, §5.1]
.

An abstract argument, based in the Hahn-Banach theorem, gives the following known identification.

L
EMMA
4.15
.

Let
1\leq p\leq 2
. Then, with the usual duality pairing,
the following isometrical identity holds

(4.16)

(H^{p})^{*}=S_{D}(L^{p^{\prime}}),

where the right hand side is the space of all
G=S_{D}(g)
, for some
g\in L^{p^{\prime}}(\Sigma)
, endowed with the norm

\|G\|_{S_{D}(L^{p^{\prime}})}:=\inf_{g\in L^{p^{\prime}}\colon S_{D}(g)=G}\|g\|_{L^{p^{\prime}}}.

Proof.

We sketch the standard proof for completeness.
First note that we may regard
S_{D}(L^{p^{\prime}})
as a subspace of
H^{2}
, since
1\leq p\leq 2
.
Next, every
G=S_{D}(g)
, with
g\in L^{p^{\prime}}(\Sigma)
, defines an element
\Phi_{G}
in
(H^{p})^{*}
by the standard duality pairing,
namely

\Phi_{G}(F):={\langle{F},{G}\rangle}={\langle{f_{0}},{{\mathbb{S}}_{D}g}\rangle}=\int_{\Sigma}f_{0}\,\bar{g}\,d\sigma,\quad F\in H^{p}\cap H^{2},

where
f_{0}
is the boundary limit of
F
. Moreover,

\big|\Phi_{G}(F)\big|\leq\|f_{0}\|_{L^{p}}\|g\|_{L^{p^{\prime}}}=\|F\|_{H^{p}}\|g\|_{L^{p^{\prime}}},

so taking the infimum over all
g
with
S_{D}(g)=G
one obtains

\|\Phi_{G}\|_{(H^{p})^{*}}\leq\|G\|_{S_{D}(L^{p^{\prime}})}.

The correspondence
G\mapsto\Phi_{G}
is injective, since testing with
K_{z}:=S_{D}(\cdot,z)\in H^{2}\cap H^{p}
, for each fixed
z\in D
, gives

\Phi_{G}(K_{z})=\overline{\int_{\Sigma}S_{D}(z,w)g(w)\,d\sigma(w)}=\overline{G(z)};

so, if
\Phi_{G}=0
then
G=0
.

Finally, to see surjectivity, let
\Phi\in(H^{p})^{*}
. Passing to boundary values we may regard
H^{p}(D)
as a closed subspace of
L^{p}(\Sigma)
.
So, by the Hahn-Banach theorem
\Phi
has a continuous extension
{\widetilde{\raisebox{-0.86108pt}[5.38193pt][0.0pt]{$\Phi$}}}
to
L^{p}(\Sigma)
with

\|{\widetilde{\raisebox{-0.86108pt}[5.38193pt][0.0pt]{$\Phi$}}}\|_{(L^{p})^{*}}\leq\|\Phi\|_{(H^{p})^{*}}.

Thus, we can find
g\in L^{p^{\prime}}(\Sigma)
with
\|g\|_{L^{p^{\prime}}}=\|{\widetilde{\raisebox{-0.86108pt}[5.38193pt][0.0pt]{$\Phi$}}}\|_{(L^{p})^{*}}
and

{\widetilde{\raisebox{-0.86108pt}[5.38193pt][0.0pt]{$\Phi$}}}(f)=\int_{\Sigma}f\,\bar{g}\,d\sigma,\quad f\in L^{p}.

Then, letting
G:=S_{D}(g)
, and using the above notation one has

\Phi_{G}(F)={\langle{F},{G}\rangle}=\int_{\Sigma}f_{0}\,\bar{g}\,d\sigma={\widetilde{\raisebox{-0.86108pt}[5.38193pt][0.0pt]{$\Phi$}}}(f_{0})=\Phi(F),\quad F\in H^{p}\cap H^{2},

which implies that
\Phi_{G}=\Phi
. Moreover,

\|G\|_{S_{D}(L^{p^{\prime}})}\leq\|g\|_{L^{p^{\prime}}}=\|{\widetilde{\raisebox{-0.86108pt}[5.38193pt][0.0pt]{$\Phi$}}}\|_{(L^{p})^{*}}\leq\|\Phi\|_{(H^{p})^{*}}.

This shows that the pairing
G\mapsto\Phi_{G}
is an (anti)-linear isometric isomorphism from
S_{D}(L^{p^{\prime}})
into
(H^{p})^{*}
.
∎

As a consequence of Lemma
4.15
and Corollary
1.7
we obtain the following.

C
OROLLARY
4.17
.

Let
D\subset{\mathbb{C}}^{n}
be an irreducible bounded symmetric domain of tube type with rank
\geq 2
. If
1\leq p\leq 2
then (for the canonical inclusion) we have

(4.18)

(H^{p})^{*}\not\hookrightarrow H^{q},\quad\forall q>2.

Proof.

In view of Lemma
4.15
, we may regard
(H^{p})^{*}
as a space of
holomorphic functions in
D
, whose boundary values exist and belong at least to
L^{2}(\Sigma)
.
Now, if the inclusion in (
4.18
) was true this would imply, for some constant
C>0
, that

\|{\mathbb{S}}_{D}g\|_{L^{q}}=\|S_{D}g\|_{H^{q}}\leq\,C\,\|S_{D}g\|_{(H^{p})^{*}}\leq\,C\,\|g\|_{L^{p^{\prime}}}\,,

using in the last step Lemma
4.15
. Thus
{\mathbb{S}}_{D}
maps
L^{p^{\prime}}\to L^{q}
with norm bounded by
C
, which contradicts Corollary
1.7
if
q>2
.
∎

R
EMARK
4.19
.

For
p=1

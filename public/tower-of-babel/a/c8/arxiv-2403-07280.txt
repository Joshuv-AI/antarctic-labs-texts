A π 1 \pi_{1} obstruction to having finite index monodromy and an unusual subgroup of infinite index in Mod ​ ( Σ g ) \textrm{Mod}(\Sigma_{g})
Ishan Banerjee

Abstract
Let X X be an algebraic surface with ℒ \mathcal{L} an ample line bundle on X X . Let Γ ⁡ ( X , ℒ ) \Gamma(X,\mathcal{L}) be the geometric monodromy group a associated to family of nonsingular curves in X X that are zero loci of sections of ℒ \mathcal{L} . We provide obstructions to Γ ⁡ ( X , ℒ ) \Gamma(X,\mathcal{L}) being finite index in the mapping class group. We also show that for any k ≥ 0 k\geq 0 , the image of monodromy is finite index in appropriate subgroups of the quotient of the mapping class group by the k k th term of the Johnson filtration assuming that ℒ \mathcal{L} is sufficiently ample. This enables us to construct several subgroups of the mapping class group with unusual properties, in some cases providing the first examples of subgroups with those properties.

Introduction

Let
X
be a smooth complex projective surface. Let
\mathcal{L}
be an ample line bundle on
X
. Let
H^{0}(X,\mathcal{L})
be the space of global sections of
\mathcal{L}
. Let
\mathbb{P}H^{0}(X,\mathcal{L})
be its projective space. Since two sections of
\mathcal{L}
are scalar multiples of each other if and only if their zero loci are the same, we may identify
\mathbb{P}H^{0}(X,\mathcal{L})
with the space of complex curves in
X
which arise as zeroes of sections of
\mathcal{L}
.

Let

\bar{E}(X,\mathcal{L})=\{(p,f)\in X\times\mathbb{P}H^{0}(X,\mathcal{L})|f(p)=0\}.

Let
\pi:\bar{E}(X,\mathcal{L})\to\mathbb{P}H^{0}(X,\mathcal{L})
be the projection map. The fibres of
\pi
are curves in
X
.

Let

U(X,\mathcal{L})=\{f\in\mathbb{P}H^{0}(X,\mathcal{L})|\not\exists p\in X,f(p)=0\textrm{ and }df(p)=0\}.

The space
U(X,\mathcal{L})
parametrizes
nonsingular
curves in
X

that are zero loci of sections of
\mathcal{L}
. It is an open subset of
\mathbb{P}H^{0}(X,\mathcal{L}).

Let

E(X,\mathcal{L})=\pi^{-1}(U(\mathcal{L})).

The map
\pi:\bar{E}(X,\mathcal{L})\to\mathbb{P}H^{0}(X,\mathcal{L})
restricts to a map
E(X,\mathcal{L})\to U(X,\mathcal{L})
. It is a proper submersion, so by Ehresmann’s fibration theorem it is a
C^{\infty}
fibre bundle. Let
f\in U(X,\mathcal{L})
, and let
C\subseteq X
be the zero locus of
f
. Associated to this fiber bundle is a monodromy representation
\rho:\pi_{1}(U(X,\mathcal{L}),f)\to\textrm{Mod}(C)
, where
\textrm{Mod}(C)
is the mapping class group of
C
. We will denote the image of
\rho
by
\Gamma(X,\mathcal{L})
.

In the case when
X
is a toric surface the group
\Gamma(X,\mathcal{L})
is completely understood by work of Salter and Calderon- Salter. This is done in the papers
[
11
]
and
[
1
]
. However a toric surface is incredibly special and part of the proof relies on constructing families of vanishing cycles in an extremely combinatorial fashion (called A- cycles and B-cycles in those paper) that does not seem likely to generalise to other kids of surfaces.

In this paper we will prove some results about the group
\Gamma(X,\mathcal{L})
under various
topological
hypotheses on
X
, specifically hypotheses on
\pi_{1}(X)
. Our results will be far weaker than those in
[
11
]
and
[
1
]
. However the class of surfaces that we deal with is far larger.

Our main theorems are as follows:

Theorem 1
.

Let
i:C\to X
be the inclusion map. Let
i_{*}:\pi_{1}(C)\to\pi_{1}(X)
denote the induced map on
\pi_{1}
.
The group
\Gamma(X,\mathcal{L})
is contained in the group
\mathcal{A}[i_{*}]
where
\mathcal{A}[i_{*}]\subseteq\textrm{Mod}(C)
is the subgroup generated by Dehn twists about nonseparating curves in
\ker i_{*}
. If
\pi_{1}X
is torsion-free and infinite, or if
H^{1}(X,\mathbb{Q})\neq 0
,
\mathcal{A}[i_{*}]
is of infinite index in
\textrm{Mod}(C)
.

The reader may be concerned that it may be the case that
\pi_{1}X
is infinite and the map
i_{*}
is
0
, in which case
\mathcal{A}[i_{*}]
would be all of
\textrm{Mod}(C)
.
We note here that by the Lefschetz hyperplane theorem
i_{*}
is surjective, so this situation can not arise.

Theorem 2
.

Let
k>0,r\gg k
. Let
C
be the zero locus of a section of
\mathcal{L}^{\otimes r}
in
X
. Let
i:C\to X
be the inclusion. Let
V=i^{*}(H^{1}(X))\subseteq H^{1}(C)
. Let
\mathcal{A}[V]\subseteq\textrm{Mod}(C)
be the subgroup generated by Dehn twists about nonseparting simple closed curves in
C
whose associated homology class in
H^{1}(C)
is in
V^{\perp}
. Let
J_{k}\subseteq\textrm{Mod}(C)
denote the
k
th term of the Johnson filtration. Then

\Gamma(X,\mathcal{L}^{\otimes r})/J_{k}\cap\Gamma(X,\mathcal{L}^{\otimes r})

is a finite index subgroup of
\mathcal{A}[V]/J_{k}\cap\mathcal{A}[V].

There is a tension between the results of Theorems
1
and
2
. For instance, suppose
\pi_{1}X
is infinite and torsion free and
H^{1}(X,\mathbb{Q})=0
. In these cases the group
\Gamma(X,\mathcal{L}^{\otimes r})
is infinite index in
\textrm{Mod}(C)
but for
r\gg k
, the group is finite index in
\textrm{Mod}(C)/J_{k}
. Thus the fact that
\Gamma(X,\mathcal{L}^{\otimes r})
is infinite index must involve it not containing lots of elements deep in the Johnson filtration.

In order to establish Theorem
2
we establish some of the properties of the higher Johnson-Morita homomorphisms when restricted to
\mathcal{A}[V]
. This is covered in Section 5 . We believe this to be of independent interest.

As a result of the above Theorems and also Theorem
3
, we construct subgroups of
\textrm{Mod}(C)

with unusual properties.

Corollary 1
.

Let
k>0
. Let
X
be an smooth projective surface such that
\pi_{1}X
is a torsion free lattice in
Sp_{4}(\mathbb{Z})
. Let
\mathcal{L}
be an ample line bundle on
X
. Let

. Let

\textrm{Mod}(C)
.

2.

\Gamma(X,\mathcal{L}^{\otimes r})/\Gamma(X,\mathcal{L}^{\otimes r})J_{k}
is finite index in
\textrm{Mod}(C)/J_{k}
.

Corollary 2
.

Let
k>0
. Let
X
be an smooth projective surface such that
\pi_{1}X
is the product of a lattice in
Sp_{4}(\mathbb{Z})
and
\mathbb{Z}^{2m}
. Let
\mathcal{L}

. Let

Then for

\Gamma(X,\mathcal{L}^{\otimes r})

\mathcal{A}[V]
.

1.1
Vanishing cycles and their relation to monodromy

In this section we will provide context and motivation for our results.
Suppose that we have a
C^{\infty}
map
f:\mathbb{D}\to\mathbb{P}(H^{0}(X,\mathcal{L}))
such that
f(\mathbb{D}\setminus\{0\})\subseteq U(X,\mathcal{L})
.

Suppose furthermore that
f(1)=C
and that
f^{*}\pi
defines a Lefschetz fibration over
\mathbb{D}
, with the fiber over
0
,
C_{0}
being the only singular fiber. We assume
C_{0}
has a unique nodal point.

In this case the theory of Lefschetz fibrations tells us that the topology of the family
f^{*}\pi
may be described in terms of a simple closed curve
\alpha\subseteq C
that is ’pinched’ under parallel transport to the nodal fiber
C_{0}
. Such an

\alpha
is called a vanishing cycle.
A question of Donaldson
[
4
]
asks which nonseparating simple closed curves in
C

arise as vanishing cycles.

This question is related to (and is in fact almost equivalent to) the problem of determining the monodromy group
\Gamma(X,\mathcal{L})
associated to
\mathcal{L}
.

In our situation, it is well known that the monodromy group
\Gamma(X,\mathcal{L})
is generated by Dehn twists about vanishing cycles. More precisely we have the following propositions.

Proposition 1
.

Let
X,\mathcal{L},C
be as above. Assume
\mathcal{L}
is very ample. Suppose that a nonseparating simple closed curve
\alpha\subseteq C
is a vanishing cycle. Then the Dehn twist
T_{\alpha}\in\Gamma(X,\mathcal{L})
. Furthermore,
\Gamma(X,\mathcal{L})
is generated by Dehn twists about nonseparating vanishing cycles.

Proposition 2
.

Let
X,\mathcal{L},C
be as above. Assume
\mathcal{L}
is very ample. Let
\Delta
be the set of nonseparating simple closed curves arising as vanishing cycles.
Then if
\phi\in\Gamma(X,\mathcal{L})
and
\delta\in\Delta
,
\phi(\delta)\in\Delta
. Thus,
\Gamma(X,\mathcal{L})
acts on
\Delta
. Furthermore this action is transitive.

A reference for the above two Propositions may be found in
[
12
]
Section 3.2.3, note that the reference only deals with homology classes of vanishing cycles, but the proofs go through to establish the above propositions after the obvious adjustments.

While we will not prove Propositions
1
and
2
here, we will explain some of the ideas that go into the proofs.

It is a general fact that
\pi_{1}(\mathbb{P}^{n}\setminus D,x)
where
D
is some irreducible hypersurface and
x\in\mathbb{P}^{n}\setminus D
is generated by meridianal loops that are all conjugate to each other. A meridianal loop is defined as follows:

1.

Let
y
be a point in the smooth locus of
D
.

2.

Let
\gamma
be a path starting at
x
and ending at
y
that lies in
\mathbb{P}^{n}\setminus D
except at the endpoint.

3.

The meridianal loop
M_{\gamma}
is the loop that starts at
x
, follows
\gamma
till it reaches a small neighbourhood of
y
, goes counterclockwise around the divisor
D
and returns along
\gamma^{-1}
.

In our case, one can argue that the monodromy of these meridianal loops are Dehn twists about nonseparating simple closed curves in
C
. The fact that the meridianal loops are Dehn twists about
nonseparating
curves is a consequence of the fact that a smooth point in
\Sigma(\mathcal{L})
defines an irreducible nodal curve. The fact that these meridians are all conjugate leads to the transitivity of the group
\Gamma(X,\mathcal{L})
on
\Delta
.

As a result of the above two Propositions, every cycle is a vanishing cycle if and only if
\Gamma(X,\mathcal{L})=\textrm{Mod}(C)
.

1.2
Existing work and a general question

As mentioned earlier, in the case when
X
is a toric surface and
\mathcal{L}
is an ample line bundle, Salter
[
11
]
proves that the monodromy group is either hyperelliptic or finite index in the entire mapping class group. Later work of Calderon-Salter
[
1
]
proves that the monodromy group is either hyperelliptic or the subgroup of the mapping class group preserving a higher spin structure( see
[
11
]
for further details on what a higher spin structure is). Furthermore if we fix an ample line bundle
\mathcal{L}
, and look at
\Gamma(X,\mathcal{L}^{\otimes r})
for
r
sufficiently large, then the monodromy group is not hyperelliptic, and hence is finite index. This leads us to the following question.

Question 1
.

Let X be a smooth projective complex surface. Let
\mathcal{L}
be an ample line bundle. Is
\Gamma(X,\mathcal{L}^{\otimes r})
finite index for
r
sufficiently large?

Theorem
1
asserts that if
H^{1}(X,\mathbb{Q})\neq 0
or if
\pi_{1}(X)
is torsionfree and infinite, then the answer is no. Theorem
2
asserts that while the answer to the above question is no, a weakening of the question has a positive answer, namely
\Gamma(X,\mathcal{L}^{\otimes r})
surjects onto a finite index subgroup of the image of
\mathcal{A}(V)
in the quotient of the mapping class group by a term of the Johnson filtration.

Even in the case when
X
is simply connected or has finite fundamental group, Theorem
1
and Theorem
2
shut down one avenue of attack on Question
1
, i.e. to prove that the monodromy groups
\Gamma(X,\mathcal{L})
are of finite index. Namely one could hope that subgroups satisfying properties akin to those in Theorem
2
and that of Lemma
1
are automatically finite index in the mapping class group, indeed this was the hope of the author going into this project. One could imagine that if a subgroup of the mapping class group contains subgroups that are mapping class groups of sufficiently large subsurfaces of
C
and contain enough Dehn twists to virtually surject onto
Sp(H_{1}(C)),
then the subgroup would be forced to be finite index. However this is simply not the case.

Containment

In this section we will establish that the monodromy group
\Gamma(X,\mathcal{L})
is contained in
\mathcal{A}[i_{*}]
.

We will first study stabilizer subgroups.

Definition 1
.

Let
G
be a group. Let
\phi:\pi_{1}(C)\to G
be a surjective homomorphism. Let
S
be the set of conjugacy classes of surjective homomorphisms from
\pi_{1}(C)
to G.
There is a right action of
\textrm{Mod}(C)
on
S
arising from precomposition.

We define
\textrm{Stab}[\phi]
to be the stabilizer subgroup of
\phi
under this action.

The following proposition characterises the Dehn twists about non-separating simple closed curves that lie in
\textrm{Stab}[\phi]
.

Proposition 3
.

Let
\alpha\subseteq\Sigma_{g}
be a non-separating simple closed curve, disjoint from a basepoint
*
. Let
T_{\alpha}
denote the Dehn twist about
\alpha
. Then
T_{\alpha}
stabilizes
\phi
up to conjugacy if and only if
\alpha\in\ker\phi
.

Proof.

Suppose
\alpha\in\ker\phi
. Then one can explicitly find a model of
\Sigma_{g}
with a basepoint
*
, and a geometric basis of curves
\{a_{1},b_{1},\dots a_{g},b_{g}\}
passing through
*
such that
\alpha
is freely isotopic to
a_{1}
. The element
a_{1}
is mapped to
1
under
\phi
. Consider an explicit representative curve
\bar{\alpha}
of
\alpha
such that

1.

\bar{\alpha}
is disjoint from
*
.

2.

\bar{\alpha}
is disjoint from
\{a_{1},\dots,a_{g},b_{2},\dots b_{g}\}.

3.

\bar{\alpha}
intersects
b_{1}
once transversally.

.
Then
T_{\bar{\alpha}}(a_{i})=a_{i}
for all
i
,
T_{\bar{\alpha}}(b_{i})=b_{i}
for
i\neq 1
and
T_{\bar{\alpha}}(b_{1})b_{1}^{-1}
is conjugate to
a_{1}
. Hence,
\phi\circ T_{\bar{\alpha}}=\phi
which is what we wanted to prove.

Suppose
T_{\alpha}
stabilizes
\phi
up to conjugacy. Then there exists a lift
T
of
T_{\alpha}
in
\textrm{Mod}(\Sigma_{g},*)
( the mapping class group of
\Sigma_{g}
preserving the marked point
*
) that stabilizes
\phi
exactly, i.e.
\phi\circ T=i_{*}
. However any lift of
T_{\alpha}
must be of the form
T_{\bar{\alpha}}
where
\bar{\alpha}
is a simple closed curve, disjoint from
*
, freely (i.e. without necessarily respecting the basepoint
*
) isotopic to
\alpha
.
But then we may again choose a geometric basis of
\Sigma_{g}
,
\{a_{1},b_{1},\dots,a_{g},b_{g}\}
such that:

1.

\bar{\alpha}
is disjoint from
\{a_{1},\dots,a_{g},b_{2},\dots b_{g}\}.

2.

\bar{\alpha}
intersects
b_{1}
once.

3.

\bar{\alpha}
is freely isotopic to
a_{1}
.

Then
T_{\bar{\alpha}}(b_{1})b_{1}^{-1}
is conjugate to
a_{1}
and hence
\phi(T_{\bar{\alpha}}(b_{1}))=\phi(b_{1})
if and only if
a_{1}\in\ker i_{*}
which is what we wanted to prove.

∎

Now we establish that our monodromy group lies in the stabilizer subgroup.

Proposition 4
.

Let
X
be a smooth projective surface,
\mathcal{L}
an ample line bundle,
C
a smooth zero locus of a section of
\mathcal{L}
. Let
i:C\to X
denote the inclusion map. Let
\Gamma(X,\mathcal{L})\subseteq\textrm{Mod}(C)
denote the monodromy group. Then
\Gamma(X,\mathcal{L})\subseteq\textrm{Stab}[i_{*}]
, and furthermore
\Gamma(X,\mathcal{L})\subseteq\mathcal{A}[i_{*}]
.

Proof.

Let
\phi\in\Gamma(X,\mathcal{L})
. Let
i:C\to X
be the inclusion map. Then
i
is homotopic to
i\circ\phi
essentially by construction, the parallel transport involved in defining the monodromy gives a homotopy between the two maps. Hence they induce the same homomorphism between
\pi_{1}(C)
and
\pi_{1}(X)
up to conjugation (the fact that we need to deal with homomorphisms up to conjugation is due to the fact that the homotopy need not preserve a base point).

Hence
\phi\in\textrm{Stab}[i_{*}]
.
If a simple nonseparating closed curve
\alpha
is such that
T_{\alpha}
in
\textrm{Stab}[i]
, Then
\alpha\in\ker i_{*}
by Proposition
3
. Hence,
T_{\alpha}\in\mathcal{A}[i]
. Since
\Gamma(X,\mathcal{L})
is generated by such
T_{\alpha}
,
\Gamma(X,\mathcal{L})\subseteq\mathcal{A}[i]
.
∎

3
Infinite index

In this section we will establish that under the assumptions mentioned in Theorem
1
,
\mathcal{A}[i_{*}]
is of infinite index in the mapping class group.

Let
g>0
. Let
\Sigma_{g}
be a Riemann surface of genus
g
. Let
\gamma_{1},\dots\gamma_{r}
be simple closed curves in
\Sigma_{g}
, Let
N\subseteq\pi_{1}(\Sigma_{g})
be the normal subgroup generated by
\gamma_{1},\dots\gamma_{r}
. Let
G=\pi_{1}(\Sigma_{g})/N
. Let
\rho:\pi_{1}(\Sigma_{g})\to G
denote the quotient map. Let
S
be the set
\textrm{Hom}(\pi_{1}(\Sigma_{g}),G)/conj
, i.e the set of homomorphisms from
\pi_{1}(\Sigma_{g})
to
G
, up to conjugation. The group
\textrm{Mod}(\Sigma_{g})
acts on
S
and
\rho
is an element of
S
.

Proposition 5
.

Suppose that
G\neq 0
is torsion-free.
The stabilizer subgroup of
\rho
in
\textrm{Mod}(\Sigma_{g})
is of infinite index.

Proof.

We first establish that there is some pair of essential simple closed curves
\alpha,\beta
on
\Sigma_{g}
, where
\alpha
and
\beta
have geometric intersection number 1, with
\alpha\in\ker\rho
and
\beta\not\in\ker\rho
. Let
\mathcal{C}
be the graph whose vertices are essential simple closed curves in
\Sigma_{g}
and whose edges consist of curves with geometric intersection number 1. The graph
\mathcal{C}
is connected. If there are no pairs
\alpha,\beta
of geometric intersection number 1, with
\alpha\in\ker\rho
and
\beta\not\in\ker\rho
, then
\mathcal{C}
would not be connected and
\mathcal{C}
could be separated into two disjoint subgraphs
\mathcal{C}_{1},\mathcal{C}_{2}
each consisting of curves in the kernel of
\rho
and not in the kernel of
\rho
respectively.

Let
\alpha,\beta
on
\Sigma_{g}
, where
\alpha
and
\beta
have geometric intersection number 1, with
\alpha\in\ker\rho
and
\beta\not\in\ker\rho
. Let
T_{\beta}
be the Dehn twist about
\beta
.
Then
\rho(T_{\beta}^{k}(\alpha))=\rho(\beta)^{k}\neq 1
. Hence no power of
T_{\beta}
stabilizes
\rho
as otherwise
\rho(T_{\beta}^{k}(\alpha))
would be conjugate to
\rho(\alpha)=1
. Hence the stabilizer subgroup is not finite index.
∎

Proposition 6
.

Let
C,X,\mathcal{L},i
be as above. If
H^{1}(X,\mathbb{Q})\neq 0
,
\textrm{Stab}[i_{*}]
is infinite index in the mapping class group.

Proof.

Let
\alpha\in H^{1}(X,\mathbb{Q})
be a nonzero class. The Lefschetz hyperplane theorem implies that
i^{*}\alpha\neq 0
. Then for any
\phi\in\textrm{Stab}[i_{*}]
,
\phi^{*}(i^{*}\alpha)=i^{*}\alpha
. Let
mi^{*}\alpha
be a multiple of
i^{*}\alpha
that is integral.

Consider the surjective map
\textrm{Mod}(C)\to Sp(H^{1}(C),\mathbb{Z})
The above implies that
\textrm{Stab}[i_{*}]
is mapped into the stabilizer subgroup of the vector
mi^{*}(\alpha)
. But this stabilizer subgroup is not finite index in
Sp(H^{1}(C,\mathbb{Z}))
and hence
\textrm{Stab}[i_{*}]
is not finite index in
\textrm{Mod}(C)
.

∎

With the above Propositions we can now prove Theorem
1
.

Proof of Theorem
1
.

The group
\Gamma(X,\mathcal{L})
is contained in
\mathcal{A}[i_{*}]
by Proposition
4
.

The group
\mathcal{A}[i_{*}]\subseteq\textrm{Stab}[i_{*}]
. The group
\textrm{Stab}[i_{*}]
in the cases when
H^{1}(X,\mathbb{Q})\neq 0
or
\pi_{1}X
is torsion free and infinite, is of infinite index in
\textrm{Mod}(C)
by Propositions
5
and
6
. Hence
\mathcal{A}[i_{*}]
is also infinite index.
∎

4
\Gamma(X,\mathcal{L}^{\otimes r})
contains framed mapping class groups of subsurfaces

Lemma 1
.

Let
X,\mathcal{L}
be as above. Let
r>0
. Let
C
be the zero locus a section of
\mathcal{L}^{\otimes r}.
Let
i:C\to X
be the inclusion. Suppose
i^{*}(H^{1}(X,\mathbb{Z}))=V\subseteq H^{1}(C,\mathbb{Z})
. Then for
r
sufficiently large:

1.

\Gamma(X,\mathcal{L}^{\otimes r})
surjects onto a finite index subgroup of
Sp(V^{\perp},\mathbb{Z})
.

2.

Fix a plane curve singularity
f:\mathbb{C}^{2}\to\mathbb{C}
. Let
C_{0}
be a Milnor fiber of
f
. Then one can embed
C_{0}
as a subsurface of
C
. Let
\Gamma_{v}
be the versal deformation monodromy subgroup of
\textrm{Mod}(C_{0})
. Let
\phi:\textrm{Mod}(C_{0})\to\textrm{Mod}(C)
be the homomorphism induced by inclusion. Then
\phi(\Gamma_{v})\subseteq\Gamma(X,\mathcal{L}^{\otimes r})
.

For details on what the versal monodromy subgroup is, see
[
3
]
.
We note that the requirement on
r
depends on the choice of plane curve singularity
f
, one needs larger vales of
r
for more complicated singularities.

We will actually prove something stronger than (2)- we will show that that there is a singular curve
C_{sing}\subseteq X
that is the zero locus of a section of
\mathcal{L}^{\otimes r}
. The curve
C_{sing}
will have precisely one singularity that is isomorphic to
f
and further more all deformations of
f
can be realized as deformations of
C_{sing}
in
X
. This will crucially rely on the description of the versal deformation space in
[
5
]
.

In this section we will establish Lemma
1
. We will start by providing a reference for part (1) of Lemma
1
as this was already known.

Proof of Part(1) of Lemma
1
.

The fact that
\Gamma(X,\mathcal{L}^{\otimes r})
surjects onto a finite index subgroup of
Sp(V^{\perp},\mathbb{Z})
is part of Jannsen’s thesis (Chapter 3) and follows from Theorem 3.8 of
[
7
]
. This establishes (1).
∎

The proof of part (2) of Lemma
1
will involve embedding a versal deformation space of an isolated plane curve singularity into
\mathbb{P}H^{0}(X,\mathcal{L}^{\otimes r})
.

Let

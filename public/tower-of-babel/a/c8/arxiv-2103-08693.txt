Abstract
. In this paper, we study the strong convergence of an algorithm to solve the variational inequality problem which extends a recent paper (Thong et al, Numerical Algorithms. 78, 1045-1060 (2018)). We reduce and refine some of their algorithm conditions and we prove the convergence of the algorithm in the presence of some computational errors. Then using MATLAB software, the result will be illustrated with some numerical examples. Also, we compare our algorithm with some other well known algorithms.

1.
Introduction

Let
H
be a real Hilbert space with the inner product
\langle.,.\rangle
and the norm
\|.\|
, and
C
be a nonempty, closed and convex subset of
H
. The variational inequality
(VI)
is to find a point
x\in C
such that

\langle Ax,y-x\rangle\geq 0,\;\;\forall y\in C,

(1.1)

where
A
is a mapping of C into
H
. The solutions set
of (
1.1
) is denoted by
VI(A,C)
.
Variational inequalies arise in the study of network equilibriums, optimization problems, saddle point problem, Nash equilibrium problems in noncooperative games etc.; sSee for example
[

,

]
and the references therein.

A new algorithm was proposed by Korpelevich
[
10
]
for solving problem
(VI)
in the Euclidean space which is known as the extragradient method. Let
x_{1}
be an arbitrarily element in
H
and consider

\begin{cases}y_{n}=P_{C}(x_{n}-\lambda Ax_{n}),\\
x_{n+1}=P_{C}(x_{n}-\lambda Ay_{n}).\end{cases}

(1.2)

where
\lambda
is a number in
(0,1)
,
P_{C}
is the Euclidean least distance projection of
H
onto
C
and

A:C\rightarrow H
is a monotone operator. The next algorithm (
1.3
) was introduced by Tseng
[
17
]
and applying the modified forward-backward(F-B) method is a good alternative to the extragradient method (TEGM):

\begin{cases}y_{n}=P_{C}(x_{n}-\lambda Ax_{n}),\\
x_{n+1}=P_{X}(y_{n}-\lambda(Ay_{n}-Ax_{n})),\end{cases}

(1.3)

where
X=C
and
X=H
if
A
is Lipschitz continuous.
The following algorithm (
1.4
) was proposed by Shehu and Iyiola
[
14
]
which a viscosity type subgradient extragradient
method (VSEGM):

\begin{cases}y_{n}=P_{C}(x_{n}-\lambda_{n}Ax_{n}),\\
T_{n}=\{z\in H:\langle x_{n}-\lambda_{n}Ax_{n}-y_{n},z-y_{n}\rangle\leq 0\},\\
z_{n}=P_{T_{n}}(x_{n}-\lambda_{n}Ay_{n}),\\
x_{n+1}=\alpha_{n}f(x_{n})+(1-\alpha_{n})z_{n},\end{cases}

(1.4)

where the operator
A
is monotone and Lipschitz continuous,
f
is a strict
contraction mapping,
l\in(0,1),\;\mu\in(0,1)
and
\lambda_{n}=l^{m_{n}}
where
m_{n}
is the smallest nonnegative integer
m
such that

\displaystyle\lambda\|Ax_{n}-Ay_{n}\|\leq\mu\|r_{l^{m_{n}}}(x_{n})\|,

where
r_{l^{m_{n}}}(x)=x-P_{C}(x-l^{m_{n}}A(x))
for all
x\in C
.
Recently, the sequence produced by the following algorithm was introduced by Thong and Hieu
[
15
]
based on Tseng’s method (THEGM):

\begin{cases}y_{n}=P_{C}(x_{n}-\lambda_{n}Ax_{n}),\\
z_{n}=y_{n}-\lambda_{n}(Ay_{n}-Ax_{n}),\\
x_{n+1}=\alpha_{n}f(x_{n})+(1-\alpha_{n})z_{n},\end{cases}

(1.5)

where the operator
A
is monotone and Lipschitz continuous,
\gamma>0,\;l\in(0,1),\;\mu\in(0,1)
and
\lambda_{n}
is chosen to be the largest
\lambda\in\{\gamma,\gamma l,\gamma l^{2},...\}
satisfying

\displaystyle\lambda\|Ax_{n}-Ay_{n}\|\leq\mu\|x_{n}-y_{n}\|.

(1.6)

In this paper substituting a sequence
\{\beta_{n}\}\subset(0,1)
of coefficients instead of the sequence
\{1-\alpha_{n}\}
in the algorithm (
1.5
), we extend algorithm (
1.5
). Moreover, condition (
1.6
) will be removed just by a slight change in the coefficients
\{\lambda_{n}\}
. Also a sequence of computational errors in our algorithm is considered. The strong convergence of the proposed algorithm to a point of the variational inequality
VI(C,A)
will be proved under the presence of computational errors. Finally, some examples will be presented which will examine the convergence of the proposed algorithm in different situations.

2.
preliminaries

In this section, some basic concepts are presented.

Let
H
be a real Hilbert space with the inner product
\langle.,.\rangle
and norm
\|.\|
and suppose that
C
is
a nonempty closed convex subset of
H

A:C\rightarrow H

A
is said to be

(i) monotone if

\displaystyle\langle Ax-Ay,x-y\rangle\geq 0,\;\;\forall x,y\in C;

(ii)
L

L>0
such that

\displaystyle\|Ax-Ay\|\leq L\|x-y\|,\;\;\forall x,y\in C.

For the main results of this paper we need the following useful lemmas.

.

Let
H
be a real Hilbert space. Then, we have the following well-known
results:

\displaystyle\|x+y\|^{2}

\displaystyle=\|x\|^{2}+2\langle x,y\rangle+\|y\|^{2},\;\;\forall x,y\in H.

\displaystyle\|x+y\|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\|x\|^{2}+2\langle y,x+y\rangle,\;\;\forall x,y\in H.

.

(

\{\alpha_{n}\}\subset[0,1],\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\alpha_{n}=\infty
,

(b)

Then,

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
C
be a nonempty closed convex subset of a real Hilbert space
H
,
A
be a
L
-Lipschitz continuous mapping on
C
and
\lambda\in(0,1)
such that
\lambda L<1
. Suppose that
f:H\rightarrow H
is a contraction mapping with a constant
\rho\in[0,1)
. Let
\{e_{n}\}\subseteq H
be a sequence of computational errors,
x_{0}\in H
be arbitrary and
\{x_{n}\}
,
\{y_{n}\}
and
\{z_{n}\}
be the sequences generated by

\begin{cases}y_{n}=P_{C}(x_{n}-\lambda Ax_{n}),\\
z_{n}=y_{n}-\lambda(Ay_{n}-Ax_{n}),\\
x_{n+1}=\alpha_{n}f(x_{n})+\beta_{n}z_{n}+e_{n},\end{cases}

(3.1)

where
\{\alpha_{n}\}
and
\{\beta_{n}\}
are real sequences in
[0,1]
such that
\alpha_{n}+\beta_{n}\leq 1

.
Also assume the following conditions:

(a)

\Sigma_{n=1}^{\infty}\alpha_{n}\beta_{n}=\infty,

(b)

\Sigma_{n=1}^{\infty}(1-\alpha_{n}-\beta_{n})<\infty,

(c)

\displaystyle\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{(1-\alpha_{n}-\beta_{n})}{\alpha_{n}}=\displaystyle\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\alpha_{n}=0
,

(d)

\Sigma_{n=1}^{\infty}\|e_{n}\|<\infty
.

Then

(i)

VI(C,A)\neq\emptyset
if and only if
\{x_{n}\}
is bounded and
\displaystyle\liminf_{n\rightarrow\infty}\|x_{n}-y_{n}\|=0
.

Suppose
VI(C,A)\neq\emptyset
. Then

(ii)

if
\displaystyle\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\|e_{n}\|}{\alpha_{n}}=0

P_{VI(C,A)}\circ f(x)=P_{VI(C,A)}(f(x))

\displaystyle\langle x_{n_{i_{k}}}-y_{n_{i_{k}}},z-y_{n_{i_{k}}}\rangle-\lambda\langle Ax_{n_{i_{k}}},z-x_{n_{i_{k}}}\rangle-\lambda\langle Ax_{n_{i_{k}}},x_{n_{i_{k}}}-y_{n_{i_{k}}}\rangle

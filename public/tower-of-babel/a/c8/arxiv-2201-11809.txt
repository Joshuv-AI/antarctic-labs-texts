Extremal singular values of random matrix products and Brownian motion on 𝖦𝖫 ⁡ ( N , ℂ ) \mathsf{GL}(N,\mathbb{C})
Andrew Ahn

Abstract
. We establish universality for the largest singular values of products of random matrices with right unitarily invariant distributions, in a regime where the number of matrix factors and size of the matrices tend to infinity simultaneously. The behavior of the largest log singular values coincides with the large N N limit of Dyson Brownian motion with a characteristic drift vector consisting of equally spaced coordinates, which matches the large N N limit of the largest log singular values of Brownian motion on 𝖦𝖫 ⁡ ( N , ℂ ) \mathsf{GL}(N,\mathbb{C}) . Our method utilizes the formalism of multivariate Bessel generating functions, also known as spherical transforms, to obtain and analyze combinatorial expressions for observables of these processes.



1.
Introduction

Suppose
X(1),X(2),\ldots
is a sequence of
N\times N
independent random matrices, and let

Y(M):=X(M)\cdots X(1).

As a natural model for systems exhibiting progressive scattering, the study of random matrix products has motivations from a variety of contexts including chaotic dynamical systems
[
FK60
,
CPV93
]
, deep neural network
[
PSG17
,
Han18
,
HN20
]
, and wireless communications
[
TV04
]
. If the matrices
X(m)
are complex and nonsingular, as in this paper, the discrete time (in
M
) stochastic process
\{Y(M)\}_{M\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}}
is a random walk on
\mathsf{GL}(N,\mathbb{C})
.

In this paper, we establish universality of the largest singular values of
Y(M)
in the limit as the number of matrix factors
M
and matrix size
N
tend to infinity with
M\asymp N
. We focus on random complex matrices
X(m)
which are
right unitarily invariant
, i.e. the distribution of
X(m)U
matches the distribution of
X(m)
for any matrix
U
in the unitary group
\mathsf{U}(N)
. Under assumptions imposing weak concentration of the support in
\mathbb{R}_{>0}
and nonvanishing of the average (over
m
) variance of the empirical measures of
X(m)
, we show that the fluctuations of the largest singular values match those of the
N\to\infty
limit of
Brownian motion
\mathsf{Y}^{(N)}(t)
on
\mathsf{GL}(N,\mathbb{C})
.

The approach used in this work is rooted in tools and ideas from integrable probability (summarized in
Section

1.5
). This departs from previous local universality results for random matrices, such as for Wigner matrices (e.g.
[
EPR+10
,
TV11
]
), which typically employ non-integrable methods.

1.1.
Brownian motion on
\mathsf{GL}(N,\mathbb{C})
and Dyson Brownian motion

We introduce Brownian motion on
\mathsf{GL}(N,\mathbb{C})
and describe the
N\to\infty
limit of its singular values.

Definition 1.1
.

Brownian motion on
\mathsf{GL}(N,\mathbb{C})
is a diffusion on
\mathsf{GL}(N,\mathbb{C})
with infinitesimal generator given by
\tfrac{1}{2}\Delta
where
\mathsf{Y}^{(N)}(0)
is the identity and
\Delta
is the Laplace-Beltrami operator on
\mathsf{GL}(N,\mathbb{C})
with respect to the metric induced by the Hilbert-Schmidt inner product

\langle X,Y\rangle:=\tr(XY^{*})

on the Lie algebra
\mathfrak{gl}(N,\mathbb{C})
. Equivalently,
\mathsf{Y}^{(N)}(t)
is the
\mathsf{GL}(N,\mathbb{C})
-valued stochastic process
\{\mathsf{Y}^{(N)}(t)\}_{t\geq 0}
satisfying the Stratonovich equation

d\mathsf{Y}^{(N)}(t)=\mathsf{Y}^{(N)}(t)\circ dW^{(N)}(t),\quad\quad\mathsf{Y}^{(N)}(0)=1_{N}

where
1_{N}\in\mathsf{GL}(N,\mathbb{C})
is the identity and

W^{(N)}(t):=\sum_{b\in\beta}W_{b}(t)b

is additive Brownian motion on
\mathfrak{gl}(N,\mathbb{C})
, taking
\beta
to be any orthonormal basis of
\mathfrak{gl}(N,\mathbb{C})
with respect to the Hilbert-Schmidt inner product.

The large
N
limit of
\mathsf{Y}^{(N)}(t)
, in the sense of *-distribution, is the free multiplicative Brownian motion
[
Kem16
]
. Additional aspects of the
N\to\infty
limit are known, including global fluctuations
[
CK14
]
and the limit of the Brown measure (a candidate for the limit of the eigenvalue empirical distribution). In this paper, we consider the
N\to\infty
limit of the singular values of
\mathsf{Y}^{(N)}(t)
.

Let
\boldsymbol{\xi}^{(N)}(t)=(\xi_{1}^{(N)}(t)\geq\cdots\geq\xi_{N}^{(N)}(t))
denote the logarithm of the squared singular values of
\mathsf{Y}^{(N)}(t)
. It is a remarkable fact (see
[
JO06
, Corollary 3.3]
,
[
Bia95
]
, and
[
IS16
]
) that the evolution of
\boldsymbol{\xi}^{(N)}(t/4)
coincides with that Dyson Brownian motion with drift:

Theorem 1.2

(
[
JO06
, Corollary 3.3]
)
.

The process
\boldsymbol{\xi}^{(N)}(t/4)
evolves as Brownian motion on
\mathbb{R}^{N}
with drift

\left(\frac{N-1}{2},\frac{N-3}{2},\ldots,\frac{-N+3}{2},\frac{-N+1}{2}\right),

started at the origin, and
conditioned to remain in the set
\{\boldsymbol{x}=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{N}):x_{1}\geq\cdots\geq x_{N}\}
.

The original statement from
[
JO06
]
was in terms of the singular values of
\mathsf{Y}^{(N)}(t)
. We note that
[
JO06
]
also provided analogous descriptions for Brownian motions on symmetric spaces
G/K

G
is a complex semi-simple non-compact connected Lie group with finite center and
K
is a maximal compact subgroup. In this framework, the drift vector is the sum of the associated positive roots, where our setting corresponds to Type A.

From the identification with Dyson Brownian motion with drift, the process
\boldsymbol{\xi}^{(N)}(t)
admits determinantal spacetime correlation functions with exact formulas for the correlation kernel. In
[
Joh04
]
, the
N\to\infty
limit of

was studied via these kernels, where it was shown to exhibit number variance saturation. In this paper, we prove a stronger form of convergence using machinery from
[

.

Theorem 1.3
.

There exists a limiting infinite line ensemble (in the sense of
[
CH14
]
, see

\boldsymbol{\xi}(t):=(\xi_{1}(t),\xi_{2}(t),\ldots)=\lim_{N\to\infty}\boldsymbol{\xi}^{(N)}\left(\frac{t}{4}\right)-\frac{Nt}{2}-\log N

T>0
and any positive integer

, the random continuous function
\left(\xi_{1}^{(N)}(\tfrac{t}{4})-\tfrac{Nt}{2}-\log N,\ldots,\xi_{k}^{(N)}(\tfrac{t}{4})-\tfrac{Nt}{2}-\log N\right)
converges to
(\xi_{1}(t),\ldots,\xi_{k}(t))
as
N\to\infty
in the weak-* topology of probability measures on
C([\tfrac{1}{T},T])^{k}

In view of
Theorem

\boldsymbol{\xi}(t)
may be interpreted as a
\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 1}
-tuple of non-intersecting Brownian motions with drift where the
i

1.2.
Main result

Our main result is that the largest log squared singular values of random matrix products
Y^{(N)}(M)
in the limit
N,M\to\infty
with
N\asymp M
converges to the infinite line ensemble
\{\boldsymbol{\xi}(t)\}_{t>0}
in finite dimensional distribution, under mild assumptions. Given an
N\times N
matrix
X
, let

|X|^{2}:=X^{*}X.

Theorem 1.4
.

Suppose
X^{(N)}(1),X^{(N)}(2),\ldots
are random complex
N\times N
matrices with right unitarily invariant distributions and denote by

y^{(N)}_{1}(M)\geq\cdots\geq y^{(N)}_{N}(M)

the squared singular values of
X^{(N)}(M)\cdots X^{(N)}(1)
. Assume that

(i)

there exists
C>0
such that

(1.1)

\displaystyle 1-\mathbb{P}\left(\mbox{all squared singular values of $X^{(N)}(m)$ are contained in $[C^{-1},C]$}\right)=o(1/N)

uniformly over
m=1,2,\ldots
, and

(ii)

there exists a continuous
\gamma:\mathbb{R}_{>0}\to\mathbb{R}_{>0}
such that
\lim_{t\searrow 0}\gamma(t)=0
and

(1.2)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{N}\sum_{m=1}^{\lfloor tN\rfloor}\frac{\tr(|X^{(N)}(m)|^{4})-\tr(|X^{(N)}(m)|^{2})^{2}}{\tr(|X^{(N)}(m)|^{2})^{2}}=\gamma(t)+o_{\mathbb{P}}(1)

for each
t>0
.

For each positive integer
h
, the process (in time
t
)

\log y^{(N)}_{j}\left(\lfloor tN\rfloor\right)-\sum_{m=1}^{\lfloor tN\rfloor}\log\left(\tr|X^{(N)}(m)|^{2}\right)-\log N,\quad\quad j=1,\ldots,h

converges in finite dimensional distributions to the top
h
paths
\xi_{1}(\gamma(t)),\ldots,\xi_{h}(\gamma(t))
of
\boldsymbol{\xi}(\gamma(t))
.

The key assumptions, besides unitary invariance, are given by (
1.1
) and (
1.2
). Condition (
1.1
) ensures that the largest singular values are sufficiently unlikely to escape to infinity. We expect that this condition can be relaxed to include non-singular matrices, i.e. replace
[C^{-1},C]
with
[0,C]
. Condition (
1.2
) ensures that the time parameter of the process converges to a nontrivial deterministic limit, where the individual summand

\frac{1}{N}\frac{\tr(|X^{(N)}(m)|^{4})-\tr(|X^{(N)}(m)|^{2})^{2}}{\tr(|X^{(N)}(m)|^{2})^{2}}

is the increment of time that the matrix factor
X^{(N)}(m)
contributes.

An immediate corollary for the case where the matrix factors are i.i.d. is given below.

Corollary 1.5
.

Suppose
X^{(N)}(1),X^{(N)}(2),\ldots
is an i.i.d. sequence of random complex
N\times N
matrices with right unitarily invariant distributions and denote by

y^{(N)}_{1}(M)\geq\cdots\geq y^{(N)}_{N}(M)

the squared singular values of
X^{(N)}(M)\cdots X^{(N)}(1)
.
Suppose
X^{(N)}(1),X^{(N)}(2),\ldots
satisfy (
1.1
), and

\displaystyle\tr(|X^{(N)}(m)|^{2})\quad\mbox{and}\quad\frac{\tr(|X^{(N)}(m)|^{4})-\tr(|X^{(N)}(m)|^{2})^{2}}{\tr(|X^{(N)}(m)|^{2})^{2}}

converge in probability as
N\to\infty
, where the latter has a positive limit
\mathfrak{a}
. For each positive integer
h
, the process (in time
t
)

\log y^{(N)}_{j}\left(\lfloor tN\rfloor\right)-\sum_{m=1}^{\lfloor tN\rfloor}\log\left(\tr|X^{(N)}(m)|^{2}\right)-\log N,\quad\quad j=1,\ldots,h

converges in finite dimensional distributions to the top
h
paths
\xi_{1}(\mathfrak{a}t),\ldots,\xi_{h}(\mathfrak{a}t)
of
\boldsymbol{\xi}(\mathfrak{a}t)

Prior to this work, convergence of the largest log singular values to
\boldsymbol{\xi}(t)
was known for products of Ginibre and truncated unitary matrices

ABK19
,
Ahn19
]
. Recall that a
complex Ginibre matrix
is a rectangular matrix of i.i.d. standard complex Gaussian entries, and a
truncated unitary matrix
is a rectangular submatrix of a Haar distributed unitary matrix. The distribution of the former is parametrized by the matrix dimensions and the latter is parametrized by the matrix dimensions and the size of the ambient Haar unitary matrix.

For products of square Ginibre matrices, the convergence of the largest log singular values to
\boldsymbol{\xi}(t)
for fixed time was shown by
[
ABK19
,
ABK20
,
LWW18
]
, with generalizations to products of rectangular Ginibre matrices indicated in
[
LWW18
]
. Extensions to joint time convergence and for products of truncated unitary matrices were established in
[
Ahn19
]
. The accessibility of these examples are due to determinantal and related structures available in those cases
[
AKW13
,
AIK13
,
KKS16
,
BGS18
,
Ahn19
]
. In our setting, this structure is not available in general, thus we appeal to alternative methods which we detail later.

1.3.
Discussion

Theorem

1.4
appears closely related to the functional central limit theorem for
\mathsf{GL}(N,\mathbb{C})
. Much like Donsker’s invariance theorem for random walks on
\mathbb{R}
, a random walk on a connected Lie group
G
converges to an appropriate diffusion when the increments approach the identity and the number of steps is properly rescaled
[
SV73
]
. Indeed, making right unitary symmetry and mean zero (of log of the increments) assumptions, random walks on
\mathsf{GL}(N,\mathbb{C})
converge to
\mathsf{Y}^{(N)}
with time parametrization dictated by the increments of the original random walk. However, a key distinction which separates
Theorem

1.4
from the
\mathsf{GL}(N,\mathbb{C})
functional limit theorem, besides the fact that
N\to\infty
in the former, is that the increments are not approaching the identity. The connection with
\mathsf{Y}^{(N)}
is even more striking when comparing to previous results on global limit shapes
[
New86b
,
New86a
,
IN92
]
and fluctuations
[
GS18
]
of products of right unitarily invariant random matrices, where the behavior was shown to
non-universal
and independent of the relative growth between
N
and
M
.

This paper focuses on the regime
N,M\to\infty
where
N\asymp M
. However, for products of Ginibre matrices, the regimes
N\gg M
and
N\ll M
are also known
[
LWW18
,
ABK19
,
ABK20
]
. For
N\ll M
, the so-called picket fence statistics appear, where the
i
th largest log singular value concentrates near
-i+\tfrac{1}{2}
after suitable rescaling. For
N\gg M
, GUE statistics appear. Thus the process
\boldsymbol{\xi}(t)
may also be viewed as an interpolating process between these two regimes. The appearance of the picket fence statistics is directly related to separation of the curves in
\boldsymbol{\xi}(t)
as
t\to\infty
according the drift sequence
-\tfrac{1}{2},-\tfrac{3}{2},-\tfrac{5}{2},\ldots
. The appearance of GUE statistics corresponds to forgetting the drift as
t\to 0
.

In view of this description for Ginibre matrices,
Theorem

1.4
suggests (though does not directly imply) that for
N\ll M
, the log squared singular values of the random walk
Y^{(N)}(M)
converge to the picket fence. This can be recast into a statement about universality of Lyapunov exponents, by interpreting the limit
N\ll M
as taking limits
M\to\infty
and
N\to\infty
in that order. More precisely, Oseledets’ multiplicative ergodic theorem
[
Ose68
]
asserts the existence of Lyapunov exponents

\lambda_{i}^{(N)}=\lim_{M\to\infty}\frac{1}{2M}\log y_{i}^{(N)}(M),\quad\quad 1\leq i\leq N

for fixed
N
. While these Lyapunov exponents are not universal, we expect that under general assumptions as
N\to\infty
the largest Lyapunov exponents should converge to picket fence statistics upon properly rescaling and translating. Results of this type were established for products of truncated unitary and Ginibre matrices in
[
AVP21
]
.

Likewise,
Theorem

1.4
also suggests that for
N\gg M
, the largest singular values of
Y^{(N)}(M)
should converge to GUE statistics given by the Airy point process. In the extreme case where the number of matrix factors
M
is fixed and
N\to\infty
, the global limit shape can be understood in terms of free probability
[
Voi87
]
. However, local statistics are far less understood, though significant progress
[
Ji21
,
DJ20
]
has been made in the form of regularity and local law results at the edge. The belief is that the Airy point process should appear as long as the empirical distribution of the matrix factors are suitably regular, and that the regularity can be relaxed as the number of matrix factors
M
increases. A similar phenomenon was confirmed for local statistics of sums of random Hermitian matrices
[
Ahn20
]
.

1.4.
Further directions

A natural question is whether the large
N
limit of the statistics of
\mathsf{Y}^{(N)}(t)
appear for products of random matrices beyond the edge. Returning to the case of products of complex Ginibre matrices, it is known
[
ABK20
]
that the bulk statistics in the regime
M\asymp N
continue to the match that of
\mathsf{Y}^{(N)}(t)
, suggesting that this universality holds in the bulk. It is worth noting that for products of square Ginibre matrices, there is a hard edge which is absent for
\mathsf{Y}^{(N)}(t)
.

Let us remark on several related models which do not exhibit right unitary symmetry. For products of complex Wishart matrices (matrices with centered, variance
1/n
, i.i.d. random variables not necessarily Gaussian), we conjecture that the large
N
limit of
\mathsf{Y}^{(N)}(t)
statistics continue to appear at the edge and bulk as well. In another direction, one can consider other symmetry classes, such as products of real matrices with right orthogonal invariance or of quaternionic matrices with right symplectic invariance. Natural examples are products of real Ginibre matrices and truncated Haar orthogonal matrices for the former, and products of quaternionic Ginibre matrices and truncated Haar symplectic matrices for the latter. These models, and one-parameter deformations of these models in the Dyson index
\beta
in the spirit of
\beta
-ensembles, were considered in
[
Ahn19
]
where tightness results were obtained at the edge in the regime
M\asymp N
. While convergence results beyond
\beta=2
are unavailable, we expect
N\to\infty
limits of
\beta
-deformations of
\mathsf{Y}^{(N)}(t)
to appear, where for
\beta=1
and
4
this should be the corresponding diffusion on
\mathsf{GL}(N,\mathbb{R})
and
\mathsf{GL}(N,\mathbb{H})
. respectively (where
\mathbb{H}
is the skew field of real quaternions).

Although convergence results for products of right orthogonally invariant real matrices are not available, there is a similar model which may be accessible via existing methods. The eigenvalues of
X_{M}^{T}\cdots X_{1}^{T}AX_{1}\cdots X_{M}
where
X_{1},\ldots,X_{M}
are real Ginibre matrices and
A
is some real antisymmetric matrix are determinantal
[
KFI19
,
FILZ19
]
, structure which arises from the orthogonal Harish-Chandra-Itzykson-Zuber integral. The behavior of the eigenvalues in the regime
M\asymp N
have not been studied, though may be accessible by analysis of correlation kernels. It would be interesting to see what behavior arises in this regime.

There is also recent progress on singular numbers of products of
p
-adic matrices
[
VP20
]
which exhibit some parallels to our setting. In particular, there is a universality phenomenon where objects corresponding to Lyapunov exponents converge in the large
N
limit to a geometric progression. It is possible that there may be analogues of our result in this setting.

1.5.
Method

The methods in this paper rely on an analogue of the Mellin transform for distributions of right unitarily invariant matrices. Given a random
X
in
\mathsf{GL}(N,\mathbb{C})
, define

\Phi_{X}(\boldsymbol{z})=\mathbb{E}\left[\int_{\mathcal{U}(N)}|UX^{*}XU^{-1}|^{\boldsymbol{z}}\,dU\right],\quad\quad\boldsymbol{z}\in\mathbb{C}^{N}

abusing notation by using the random
X
as a subscript of
\Phi
, where

|Y|^{\boldsymbol{z}}:=(\det Y)^{z_{N}}\prod_{j=1}^{N-1}\left(\det Y_{j\times j}\right)^{z_{j}-z_{j+1}-1}

is a generalization of the power function for positive definition matrices;
Y_{j\times j}
is the
j\times j
top left corner submatrix of
Y
which itself is positive definite by Sylvester’s criterion. The expectation satisfies the factorization property

\Phi_{XY}(\boldsymbol{z})=\Phi_{X}(\boldsymbol{z})\Phi_{Y}(\boldsymbol{z})

where
X,Y
are independent random right unitarily invariant complex
N\times N
matrices. These ideas, and their additive analogue, have been used to compute exact density formulas for a variety of matrix ensembles as in
[
KR19
,
KZ20
,
FKK21
,
ZKF21
]
under a common framework.

The integral within the expectation of
\Phi_{X}(\boldsymbol{z})
is known as the Gelfand-Naĭmark integral. It can be explicitly evaluated
[
GN50
]

\int_{\mathcal{U}(N)}|U\diag(x_{1},\ldots,x_{N})U^{-1}|\,dU=\Delta(\rho_{N})\frac{\det x_{j}^{z_{i}}}{\Delta(\boldsymbol{z})\Delta(\boldsymbol{x})},\quad\quad\Delta(u_{1},\ldots,u_{N})=\prod_{1\leq i<j\leq N}(u_{i}-u_{j})

and may be viewed as a multiplicative analogue of the Harish-Chandra-Itzykson-Zuber integral
[
HC57
,
IZ80
]
. The right hand side is a (normalized) multivariate Bessel function, a continuous analogue of a Schur function. This connection with symmetric functions yields a collection of tools for the study of right unitarily invariant random matrix products. In particular, we can act on the spherical transforms by certain operators diagonalized by the multivariate Bessel functions to obtain observables for singular values of matrix products. Similar ideas were used to study other random processes with connections to symmetric functions, including polymers
[
BC14
]
, measures arising from representation theory
[
BG15
,
BG18
,
BG19
]
, the
\beta
-Jacobi corners process from
\beta
-ensembles
[
BG15
,
GZ18
]
, and many more. This idea was used by
[
GS18
]
to study global fluctuations of random matrix products, where the spherical transform was referred to as the multivariate Bessel generating function, as we will in the body of this paper due to methodological connections with their work.

Our method relies on the extraction of joint observables for singular values of
Y^{(N)}(m)
via the appropriate family of operators. The observables and corresponding operators used in
[
GS18
]
are not amenable for the analysis of edge statistics in our setting. Thus, we consider a family of operators suitable for our regime corresponding to observables which give the joint Laplace transform of the log singular values of
Y^{(N)}(M)
(over varying
M
). This leads to considerable differences from the analyses of
[
GS18
]
. Our observables allow us to access the edge in a similar manner as the method of high moments in
[
Sos99
]
probed the edge for Wigner matrices. In short, joint Laplace transforms of the singular values are dominated by the largest singular values in our limit. Using exact formulas, these observables have expressions in terms of large combinatorial sums which can be asymptotically identified with expressions which correspond to the large
N
limit of observables of
\mathsf{Y}^{(N)}(t)
.

Organization

The remainder of this paper is organized as follows. In

, containing the core asymptotic analysis of this paper.

2.
Joint Laplace Transforms and Multivariate Bessel Functions

In this section, we introduce the multivariate Bessel generating function, also known as the spherical transform, which are expectations of random matrices over the multivariate Bessel function, see e.g.
[
GM18
]
and references therein. The multivariate Bessel generating functions cohere well with matrix products, a fact which furnishes us with expressions for observables of squared singular values of random matrix products.

Definition 2.1
.

The
multivariate Bessel function
indexed by
\boldsymbol{a}=(a_{1}\geq\cdots\geq a_{N})\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
is the function

\mathcal{B}_{\boldsymbol{a}}(\boldsymbol{z})=\frac{\det\left[e^{z_{i}a_{j}}\right]_{i,j=1}^{N}}{\Delta(\boldsymbol{z})}

which is holomorphic for
\boldsymbol{z}\in\mathbb{C}^{N}
, where

\Delta(\boldsymbol{z})=\prod_{1\leq i<j\leq N}(z_{i}-z_{j}).

Definition 2.2
.

Let
X
be a random matrix in
\mathsf{GL}(N,\mathbb{C})
with right unitarily invariant distribution, Denote by
\boldsymbol{x}=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{N})
the squared singular values of
X
. The
multivariate Bessel generating function
of
X
is defined by

\Phi_{X}(z_{1},\ldots,z_{N})=\mathbb{E}\left[\frac{\mathcal{B}_{\log\boldsymbol{x}}(z_{1},\ldots,z_{N})}{\mathcal{B}_{\log\boldsymbol{x}}(\rho_{N})}\right],

where
\rho_{N}:=(N-1,N-2,\ldots,0)
, given that this expectation exists in a neighborhood of
(N-1,N-2,\ldots,0)
.

The normalized multivariate Bessel function within the expectation is the zonal spherical function for the Gelfand pair
\mathsf{GL}(N,\mathbb{C}),\mathsf{U}(N)

[
Mac95
, Chapter VII]
. If
X
is a scalar (i.e.
N=1
), then the multivariate Bessel generating function reduces to

\Phi_{X}(z)=\mathbb{E}\left[|X|^{2z}\right]

which is the Mellin transform for the distribution of
|X|^{2}
. Thus, for general
N
the function
\Phi_{X}
is an extension of the Mellin transform for positive definite matrices
X^{*}X
. Moreover, we can define a generalized power function on
N\times N
positive definite matrices
Y
:

|Y|^{\boldsymbol{z}}:=(\det Y)^{z_{N}}\prod_{j=1}^{N-1}\left(\det Y_{j\times j}\right)^{z_{j}-z_{j+1}-1}

where
Y_{j\times j}
is the
j\times j
top left corner submatrix of
Y
. Indeed, by Sylvester’s criterion, the determinants are positive, thus the complex exponentials are well-defined. Then

\frac{\mathcal{B}_{\log\boldsymbol{x}}(\boldsymbol{z})}{\mathcal{B}_{\log\boldsymbol{x}}(\rho_{N})}=\Delta(\rho_{N})\frac{\det\left[x_{j}^{z_{i}}\right]_{i,j=1}^{N}}{\Delta(\boldsymbol{z})\Delta(\boldsymbol{x})}=\int_{\mathsf{U}(N)}\left|U\diag(\boldsymbol{x})U^{-1}\right|^{\boldsymbol{z}}\,dU

where the integral is over the normalized (with volume
1
) Haar measure on
\mathsf{U}(N)
. The latter integral is known as the Gelfand-Naĭmark integral
[
GN50
]
. From this perspective, the multivariate Bessel functions are unitarily invariant (under the conjugation action) generalized power functions on positive definite matrices.

Just as products of independent random variables factor under the Mellin transform, the multivariate Bessel generating functions satisfy the following factorization property:

Lemma 2.3
.

If
X
and
Y
are independent
N\times N
random matrices with right unitarily invariant distributions, then

\Phi_{XY}=\Phi_{X}\cdot\Phi_{Y}.

Proof.

If
X
and
Y
have deterministic squared singular values
\boldsymbol{x}\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}^{N}
and
\boldsymbol{y}\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}^{N}
respectively, then this follows from the identity

\frac{\mathcal{B}_{\log\boldsymbol{x}}(\boldsymbol{z})}{\mathcal{B}_{\log\boldsymbol{x}}(\rho_{N})}\frac{\mathcal{B}_{\log\boldsymbol{y}}(\boldsymbol{z})}{\mathcal{B}_{\log\boldsymbol{x}}(\rho_{N})}=\mathbb{E}\left[\frac{\mathcal{B}_{\log\boldsymbol{w}}(\boldsymbol{z})}{\mathcal{B}_{\log\boldsymbol{w}}(\rho_{N})}\right]

for zonal spherical functions, where
\boldsymbol{w}
are the squared singular values of
XY
, see
[
Mac95
, Chapter VII]
. The general case follows from taking mixtures of the aforementioned case.
∎

We can iterate
Lemma

2.3
. Let
X(1),X(2),\ldots
be independent
N\times N
right-unitarily invariant complex random matrices. Given that the multivariate Bessel generating functions
\Phi_{X(m)}
(
m\geq 1
) exist, the product
Y(M):=X(M)\cdots X(1)
has multivariate Bessel generating function

\prod_{m=1}^{M}\Phi_{X(m)}(z_{1},\ldots,z_{N}).

Given
c\in\mathbb{C}
, define

\mathcal{D}_{c}:=\mathcal{D}_{c}^{(N)}:=\sum_{i=1}^{N}\left(\prod_{j\neq i}\frac{c+z_{i}-z_{j}}{z_{i}-z_{j}}\right)\mathcal{T}_{c,z_{i}}

where
\mathcal{T}_{c,z_{i}}f(z_{1},\ldots,z_{N})=f(z_{1},\ldots,z_{i}+c,\ldots,z_{N})
. Then

(2.1)

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}_{c}\mathcal{B}_{\log\boldsymbol{x}}(z_{1},\ldots,z_{N})=\left(\sum_{i=1}^{N}x_{i}^{c}\right)\mathcal{B}_{\log\boldsymbol{x}}(z_{1},\ldots,z_{N}).

Proposition 2.4
.

Let
X(1),X(2),\ldots
be independent nonsingular
N\times N
random matrices with right unitarily invariant distributions and multivariate Bessel generating functions
\varphi_{1},\varphi_{2},\ldots
respectively. Assume that the multivariate Bessel generating functions are analytic on
\mathbb{C}^{N}
. Fix real numbers
c_{1},\ldots,c_{k}>0
and integers
M_{1}\geq\cdots\geq M_{k}>M_{k+1}=0
. Suppose
\boldsymbol{y}(M)\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}^{N}
is the vector of squared singular values of
Y(M)=X(M)\cdots X(1)
. Then

\mathbb{E}\left[\prod_{i=1}^{k}\sum_{j=1}^{N}y_{j}(M_{i})^{c_{i}}\right]=\left.\mathcal{D}_{c_{1}}\prod_{m_{1}=M_{2}+1}^{M_{1}}\varphi_{m_{1}}(z_{1}\ldots,z_{N})\cdots\mathcal{D}_{c_{k}}\prod_{m_{k}=M_{k+1}+1}^{M_{k}}\varphi_{m_{k}}(z_{1},\ldots,z_{N})\right|_{\boldsymbol{z}=\rho_{N}}

where the operators
\mathcal{D}_{c}
act on everything to the right of it.

With
Theorem

1.2
, we can compute the multivariate Bessel generating function for Brownian motion on
\mathsf{GL}(N,\mathbb{C})
using the following general result:

Proposition 2.5
.

Suppose that
\boldsymbol{\eta}(t)
is the vector of
N
-intersecting Brownian motions with drift
\boldsymbol{\mu}=(\mu_{1}\geq\cdots\geq\mu_{N})
and
\boldsymbol{\eta}(0)=\boldsymbol{0}
. If

\boldsymbol{\eta}(t)+a:=(x_{1}(t)+a,\ldots,x_{N}(t)+a)

for some
a\in\mathbb{R}
, then

\mathbb{E}\left[\frac{\mathcal{B}_{\boldsymbol{\eta}(t)+a}(\boldsymbol{z})}{\mathcal{B}_{\boldsymbol{\eta}(t)+a}(\boldsymbol{\mu})}\right]=\prod_{i=1}^{N}\frac{e^{\frac{t}{2}(z_{i}+\frac{a}{t})^{2}}}{e^{\frac{t}{2}(\mu_{i}+\frac{a}{t})^{2}}}.

In particular,
Propositions

2.4
and
2.5
imply

Corollary 2.6
.

Given
c_{1},\ldots,c_{k}>0
and
t_{1}>\cdots>t_{k}>t_{k+1}=0
, we have

(2.2)

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\prod_{i=1}^{k}\sum_{j=1}^{N}e^{c_{i}\left(\xi_{j}^{(N)}(\frac{t_{i}}{4})-\frac{Nt_{i}}{2}\right)}\right]=\left.\mathcal{D}_{c_{1}}\prod_{i=1}^{N}\frac{e^{\frac{1}{2}(t_{1}-t_{2})(z_{i}-N+\frac{1}{2})^{2}}}{e^{\frac{1}{2}(t_{1}-t_{2})(-i+\frac{1}{2})^{2}}}\cdots\mathcal{D}_{c_{k}}\prod_{i=1}^{N}\frac{e^{\frac{1}{2}(t_{k}-t_{k+1})(z_{i}-N+\frac{1}{2})^{2}}}{e^{\frac{1}{2}(t_{k}-t_{k+1})(-i+\frac{1}{2})^{2}}}\right|_{\boldsymbol{z}=\rho_{N}}

where the operators
\mathcal{D}_{c}
act on everything to the right of it. The right hand side can be expressed as a multiple contour intergral:

(2.3)

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\mathbb{E}\left[\prod_{i=1}^{k}\sum_{j=1}^{N}e^{c_{i}\left(\xi_{j}(\frac{t_{i}}{4})-\frac{Nt_{i}}{2}\right)}\right]\\
&\quad\quad=\frac{1}{(2\pi\mathbf{i})^{k}}\oint\cdots\oint\prod_{1\leq i<j\leq k}\frac{(z_{i}-z_{j})(z_{i}+c_{i}-z_{j}-c_{j})}{(z_{i}-z_{j}-c_{j})(z_{i}+c_{i}-z_{j})}\prod_{i=1}^{k}\frac{e^{\frac{t_{i}}{2}(z_{i}+c_{i}-\frac{1}{2})^{2}}}{e^{\frac{t_{i}}{2}(z_{i}-\frac{1}{2})^{2}}}\frac{\Gamma(z_{i}+c_{i}+N)\Gamma(z_{i})}{\Gamma(z_{i}+c_{i})\Gamma(z_{i}+N)}\frac{dz_{i}}{c_{i}}\end{split}

where the
z_{i}
contour is positively oriented around
0,-1,\ldots,-N+1
for
1\leq i\leq k
and the
z_{j}
contour contains
z_{i}+c_{i}
and
z_{i}-c_{j}
for each
1\leq i<j\leq k
.

Remark 1
.

The contour integral formula (
2.3
) can be viewed as a special case of
[
Ahn20
, Propositions 2.8 and 2.9]
and is closely related to the formula
[
Ahn19
, Theorem B.2]
for observables of Schur processes. These ideas go further back to the work of
[
BC14
]
where Macdonald processes, generalizations of the Schur processes, were introduced to study directed polymers. In this work, a family of contour integral formulas for observables of Macdonald processes were used to access these polymer models.

We now provide the proofs of these results.

Proof of
Proposition

2.4
.

We show that

(2.4)

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\mathbb{E}\left[\left(\prod_{i=1}^{k}\sum_{j=1}^{N}y_{j}(M_{i})^{c_{i}}\right)\frac{\mathcal{B}_{\log\boldsymbol{y}(M_{1})}(z_{1},\ldots,z_{N})}{\mathcal{B}_{\log\boldsymbol{y}(M_{1})}(\rho_{N})}\right]\\
&\quad\quad=\mathcal{D}_{c_{1}}\prod_{m_{1}=M_{2}+1}^{M_{1}}\varphi_{m_{1}}(z_{1},\ldots,z_{N})\cdots\mathcal{D}_{c_{k}}\prod_{m_{k}=M_{k+1}+1}^{M_{k}}\varphi_{m_{k}}(z_{1},\ldots,z_{N})\end{split}

by induction on
k
. The result follows from evaluating the expression above at
\boldsymbol{z}=\rho_{N}
. Indeed, (
2.1
) and
Lemma

2.3
imply that

\mathcal{D}_{c}\left[\left(\sum_{j=1}^{N}y_{j}(M)^{c}\right)\frac{\mathcal{B}_{\log\boldsymbol{y}(M)}(z_{1},\ldots,z_{N})}{\mathcal{B}_{\log\boldsymbol{y}(M)}(\rho_{N})}\right]=\mathcal{D}_{c}\prod_{m=1}^{M}\varphi_{m}(z_{1},\ldots,z_{N})

which is the
k=1
base step in the induction. Next, suppose we know that

(2.5)

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\mathbb{E}\left[\left(\prod_{i=2}^{k}\sum_{j=1}^{N}y_{j}(M_{i})^{c_{i}}\right)\frac{\mathcal{B}_{\log\boldsymbol{y}(M_{2})}(z_{1},\ldots,z_{N})}{\mathcal{B}_{\log\boldsymbol{y}(M_{2})}(\rho_{N})}\right]\\
&\quad\quad=\mathcal{D}_{c_{2}}\prod_{m_{2}=M_{3}+1}^{M_{2}}\varphi_{m_{2}}(z_{1},\ldots,z_{N})\cdots\mathcal{D}_{c_{k}}\prod_{m_{k}=M_{k+1}+1}^{M_{k}}\varphi_{m_{k}}(z_{1},\ldots,z_{N})\end{split}

which is equivalent to assuming the induction hypothesis for
k-1
. Multiply both sides by

\prod_{m_{1}=M_{2}+1}^{M_{1}}\varphi_{m_{1}}(z_{1},\ldots,z_{N})

and apply
\mathcal{D}_{c_{1}}
. Then the right hand side of (
2.5
) becomes the right hand side of (
2.4
). The left hand side of (
2.5
) becomes

(2.6)

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\mathcal{D}_{c_{1}}\prod_{m_{1}=M_{2}+1}^{M_{1}}\varphi_{m_{1}}(z_{1},\ldots,z_{N})\cdot\mathbb{E}\left[\left(\prod_{i=2}^{k}\sum_{j=1}^{N}y_{j}(M_{i})^{c_{i}}\right)\frac{\mathcal{B}_{\log\boldsymbol{y}(M_{2})}(z_{1},\ldots,z_{N})}{\mathcal{B}_{\log\boldsymbol{y}(M_{2})}(\rho_{N})}\right]\\
&\quad\quad=\mathbb{E}\left[\left(\prod_{i=2}^{k}\sum_{j=1}^{N}y_{j}(M_{i})^{c_{i}}\right)\mathcal{D}_{c_{1}}\frac{\mathcal{B}_{\log\boldsymbol{y}(M_{2})}(z_{1},\ldots,z_{N})}{\mathcal{B}_{\log\boldsymbol{y}(M_{2})}(\rho_{N})}\prod_{m_{1}=M_{2}+1}^{M_{1}}\varphi_{m_{1}}(z_{1},\ldots,z_{N})\right]\end{split}

which we want to match with the left hand side of (
2.4
). Observe that

\frac{\mathcal{B}_{\log\boldsymbol{y}(M_{2})}(z_{1},\ldots,z_{N})}{\mathcal{B}_{\log\boldsymbol{y}(M_{2})}(\rho_{N})}\prod_{m_{1}=M_{2}+1}^{M_{1}}\varphi_{m_{1}}(z_{1},\ldots,z_{N})=\mathbb{E}\left[\left.\frac{\mathcal{B}_{\log\boldsymbol{y}(M_{1})}(z_{1},\ldots,z_{N})}{\mathcal{B}_{\log\boldsymbol{y}(M_{1})}(\rho_{N})}\right|\boldsymbol{y}(M_{2})\right]

by
Lemma

2.3
. In words, the left hand side is the multivariate Bessel generating function for the matrix product
X(M_{1})X(M_{1}-1)\cdots X(M_{2}+1)Y(M_{2})=Y(M_{1})
where we condition
Y(M_{2})
to have squared singular values given by
\boldsymbol{y}(M_{2})
. Using the identity above, (
2.6
) becomes

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\left(\prod_{i=2}^{k}\sum_{j=1}^{N}y_{j}(M_{i})^{c_{i}}\right)\mathcal{D}_{c_{1}}\mathbb{E}\left[\left.\frac{\mathcal{B}_{\log\boldsymbol{y}(M_{1})}(z_{1},\ldots,z_{N})}{\mathcal{B}_{\log\boldsymbol{y}(M_{1})}(\rho_{N})}\right|\boldsymbol{y}(M_{2})\right]\right]

\displaystyle\quad\quad=\mathbb{E}\left[\left(\prod_{i=2}^{k}\sum_{j=1}^{N}y_{j}(M_{i})^{c_{i}}\right)\mathbb{E}\left[\sum_{j=1}^{N}y_{j}(M_{1})^{c_{1}}\left.\frac{\mathcal{B}_{\log\boldsymbol{y}(M_{1})}(z_{1},\ldots,z_{N})}{\mathcal{B}_{\log\boldsymbol{y}(M_{1})}(\rho_{N})}\right|\boldsymbol{y}(M_{2})\right]\right]

by commuting
\mathcal{D}_{c_{1}}
with the conditional expectation. Thus we obtain the right hand side of (
2.4
) by consolidating the expectations.
∎

Proof of
Proposition

2.5
.

We prove the statement for
a=0
, the general case follows from the identity

\mathcal{B}_{\boldsymbol{\eta}+a}(z_{1},\ldots,z_{N})=\left(\prod_{i=1}^{N}e^{az_{i}}\right)\mathcal{B}_{\boldsymbol{\eta}}(z_{1},\ldots,z_{N}).

The density at time
t
of
N
Brownian bridges starting at
\boldsymbol{a}
(at time
t=0
), ending at
\boldsymbol{b}
(at time
T
), and conditioned to never intersect is given by

\displaystyle\frac{1}{N!\det\left[p_{T}(a_{i},b_{j})\right]_{i,j=1}^{N}}\det\left[p_{t}(a_{i},\eta_{j})\right]_{i,j=1}^{N}\det\left[p_{T-t}(\eta_{i},b_{j})\right]_{i,j=1}^{N},\quad\quad p_{t}(x,y)=\frac{e^{-\frac{(x-y)^{2}}{2t}}}{\sqrt{2\pi t}}

which expands out to

\displaystyle\frac{1}{(2\pi t(1-\frac{t}{T}))^{N/2}}\left(\prod_{i=1}^{N}e^{-\frac{a_{i}^{2}}{2t}+\frac{a_{i}^{2}}{2T}-\frac{b_{i}^{2}}{2(T-t)}+\frac{b_{i}^{2}}{2T}}e^{-\frac{T\eta_{i}^{2}}{2t(T-t)}}\right)\frac{\det\left[e^{\frac{a_{i}\eta_{j}}{t}}\right]_{i,j=1}^{N}\det\left[e^{\frac{b_{i}\eta_{j}}{T-t}}\right]_{i,j=1}^{N}}{N!\det\left[e^{\frac{a_{i}b_{j}}{T}}\right]_{i,j=1}^{N}}

supported on
\boldsymbol{\eta}\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
, by e.g.
[
Joh01
]
. Here, the density is on the unordered positions of the Brownian motions. Take
a_{i}=\varepsilon(N-i)
and
b_{i}=T\mu_{i}
. Then the density becomes

\frac{1}{N!(2\pi t(1-\frac{t}{T}))^{N/2}}\left(\prod_{i=1}^{N}e^{-\frac{\varepsilon^{2}(T-t)(N-i)^{2}}{2tT}-\frac{tT\mu_{i}^{2}}{2(T-t)}}e^{-\frac{T\eta_{i}^{2}}{2t(T-t)}}\right)\det\left[e^{\frac{T\mu_{i}\eta_{j}}{T-t}}\right]_{i,j=1}^{N}\prod_{1\leq i<j\leq N}\frac{e^{\frac{\varepsilon\eta_{i}}{t}}-e^{\frac{\varepsilon\eta_{j}}{t}}}{e^{\varepsilon\mu_{i}}-e^{\varepsilon\mu_{j}}}

where we use the Vandermonde determinant identity

\Delta(\boldsymbol{z})=\prod_{1\leq i<j\leq N}(z_{i}-z_{j})=\det\left[z_{i}^{N-j}\right]_{1\leq i,j\leq N}.

Sending
\varepsilon\to 0
, then
T\to\infty
, we obtain

\frac{1}{N!(2\pi t)^{N/2}}\left(\prod_{i=1}^{N}e^{-\frac{t\mu_{i}^{2}}{2}-\frac{\eta_{i}^{2}}{2t}}\right)\det\left[e^{\mu_{i}\eta_{j}}\right]_{i,j=1}^{N}\frac{\Delta(\boldsymbol{\eta}/t)}{\Delta(\boldsymbol{\mu})}.

This is the time
t
marginal density for Brownian motion on
\mathbb{R}^{N}
starting at
\boldsymbol{0}
with drift vector
\boldsymbol{\mu}
, more specifically this density corresponds to the unordered coordinates of this Brownian motion (so the density corresponds to a measure on
\mathbb{R}^{N}
rather than on the Weyl chamber
\{x_{1}\geq\cdots\geq x_{N}\}
). We have

\frac{\mathcal{B}_{\boldsymbol{\eta}}(\boldsymbol{z})}{\mathcal{B}_{\boldsymbol{\eta}}(\boldsymbol{\mu})}=\frac{\det\left[e^{z_{i}\eta_{j}}\right]_{i,j=1}^{N}}{\Delta(\boldsymbol{z})}\frac{\Delta(\boldsymbol{\mu})}{\det\left[e^{\mu_{i}\eta_{j}}\right]_{i,j=1}^{N}}.

Then

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\frac{\mathcal{B}_{\boldsymbol{\eta}(t)}(\boldsymbol{z})}{\mathcal{B}_{\boldsymbol{\eta}(t)}(\boldsymbol{\mu})}\right]

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{N!(2\pi t)^{N/2}}\left(\prod_{i=1}^{N}e^{-\frac{t\mu_{i}^{2}}{2}}\right)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\det\left[e^{z_{i}\eta_{j}}\right]_{i,j=1}^{N}\frac{\Delta(\boldsymbol{\eta}/t)}{\Delta(\boldsymbol{z})}\prod_{i=1}^{N}e^{-\frac{\eta_{i}^{2}}{2t}}\,d\boldsymbol{\eta}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{N!(2\pi t)^{N/2}}\left(\prod_{i=1}^{N}e^{-\frac{t\mu_{i}^{2}}{2}}\right)\frac{1}{\Delta(\boldsymbol{z})}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\det\left[e^{z_{i}\eta_{j}}\right]_{i,j=1}^{N}\det\left[\left(\frac{\eta_{i}}{t}\right)^{N-j}e^{-\frac{\eta_{i}^{2}}{2t}}\right]_{i,j=1}^{N}\,d\boldsymbol{\eta}

By Andréief’s identity, we obtain

(2.7)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\mathbb{E}\left[\frac{\mathcal{B}_{\boldsymbol{\eta}(t)}(\boldsymbol{z})}{\mathcal{B}_{\boldsymbol{\eta}(t)}(\boldsymbol{\mu})}\right]&=\frac{1}{(2\pi t)^{N/2}}\left(\prod_{i=1}^{N}e^{-\frac{t\mu_{i}^{2}}{2}}\right)\frac{1}{\Delta(\boldsymbol{z})}\det\left[\int_{\mathbb{R}}\left(\frac{x}{t}\right)^{N-j}e^{xz_{i}-\frac{x^{2}}{2t}}\,dx\right]_{i,j=1}^{N}\\
&=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{N/2}}\left(\prod_{i=1}^{N}e^{\frac{t(z_{i}^{2}-\mu_{i}^{2})}{2}}\right)\frac{1}{\Delta(\boldsymbol{z})}\det\left[M_{N-j}(z_{i})\right]_{i,j=1}^{N}\end{split}

where

M_{n}(z):=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\left(\frac{x}{t}\right)^{n}e^{-\frac{(x-tz)^{2}}{2t}}\,\frac{dx}{\sqrt{t}}=\sqrt{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}}x^{n}e^{-\frac{t(x-z)^{2}}{2}}\,dx.

We claim that
M_{n}(z)
is a degree
n
polynomial in
z
with leading coefficient
\sqrt{2\pi}
. We proceed by induction on
n
. Clearly,
M_{0}(z)=\sqrt{2\pi}
. Observe that

\displaystyle M_{n}(0)=\sqrt{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}}x^{n}e^{-\frac{tx^{2}}{2}}\,dx=\sqrt{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}}(x-z)^{n}e^{-\frac{t(x-z)^{2}}{2}}\,dx=\sqrt{t}\sum_{k=0}^{n}\binom{n}{k}(-z)^{n-k}M_{k}(z).

Rearranging, we get

M_{n}(z)=t^{-1/2}M_{n}(0)-\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\binom{n}{k}(-z)^{n-k}M_{k}(z)

By our induction hypothesis, we have

M_{n}(z)=-\left(\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\binom{n}{k}(-1)^{n-k}\right)\sqrt{2\pi}z^{n}+\mbox{lower degree terms}

Thus the top degree term is
\sqrt{2\pi}z^{n}
, completing the induction. Applying row operations, we have

\det[M_{N-j}(z_{i})]_{i,j=1}^{N}=(2\pi)^{N/2}\Delta(\boldsymbol{z}).

Plugging this into (
2.7
) completes the proof.
∎

Proof of
Corollary

2.6
.

Set

\Phi_{t}(z_{1},\ldots,z_{N}):=\mathbb{E}\left[\frac{\mathcal{B}_{\boldsymbol{\xi}^{(N)}(\frac{t}{4})-\frac{Nt}{2}}(z_{1},\ldots,z_{N})}{\mathcal{B}_{\boldsymbol{\xi}^{(N)}(\frac{t}{4})-\frac{Nt}{2}}(\rho_{N})}\right].

The joint distribution of

. Then
Proposition

implies

\mathbb{E}\left[\prod_{i=1}^{k}\sum_{j=1}^{N}e^{c_{i}\left(\xi_{j}^{(N)}(\frac{t_{i}}{4})-\frac{Nt_{i}}{2}\right)}\right]=\left.\mathcal{D}_{c_{1}}\Phi_{t_{1}-t_{2}}(z_{1},\ldots,z_{N})\cdots\mathcal{D}_{c_{k}}\Phi_{t_{k}-t_{k+1}}(z_{1},\ldots,z_{N})\right|_{\boldsymbol{z}=\rho_{N}}.

\Phi_{t}
. By

,
\boldsymbol{\xi}^{(N)}(\frac{t}{4})-\frac{Nt}{2}

\boldsymbol{\eta}(t)-(N-\tfrac{1}{2})t

\boldsymbol{\eta}(s)

N

, started at the origin.

2.5

\displaystyle\Phi_{t}(z_{1},\ldots,z_{N})

).

We now show (

\displaystyle\quad\quad\times\frac{1}{(2\pi\mathbf{i})^{k}}\oint\cdots\oint\prod_{1\leq i<j\leq k}\frac{(w_{i}-w_{j})(w_{i}+c_{i}-w_{j}-c_{j})}{(w_{i}-w_{j}-c_{j})(w_{i}+c_{i}-w_{j})}\prod_{i=1}^{k}\left(\prod_{\ell=i}^{k}\frac{f_{\ell}(w_{i}+c_{i})}{f_{\ell}(w_{i})}\right)\left(\prod_{j=1}^{N}\frac{w_{i}+c_{i}-z_{j}}{w_{i}-z_{j}}\right)\frac{dw_{i}}{c_{i}}

where the
w_{i}

z_{1},\ldots,z_{N}
for

and the

for

. This can be proved by induction on

, see e.g.

Ahn19

.

If we set

f_{\ell}(z)=e^{(t_{\ell}-t_{\ell+1})(z-N+\frac{1}{2})^{2}}

for
\ell=1,\ldots,k
, and apply (
2.2
), we obtain

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\prod_{i=1}^{k}\sum_{j=1}^{N}e^{c_{i}\left(\xi_{j}^{(N)}(\frac{t_{i}}{4})-\frac{Nt_{i}}{2}\right)}\right]=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{(2\pi\mathbf{i})^{k}}\oint\cdots\oint\prod_{1\leq i<j\leq k}\frac{(w_{i}-w_{j})(w_{i}+c_{i}-w_{j}-c_{j})}{(w_{i}-w_{j}-c_{j})(w_{i}+c_{i}-w_{j})}

\displaystyle\quad\quad\times\prod_{i=1}^{k}\left(\prod_{\ell=i}^{k}\frac{e^{\frac{(t_{\ell}-t_{\ell+1})}{2}(w_{i}+c_{i}-N+\frac{1}{2})^{2}}}{e^{\frac{(t_{\ell}-t_{\ell+1})}{2}(w_{i}-N+\frac{1}{2})^{2}}}\right)\left(\prod_{j=1}^{N}\frac{w_{i}+c_{i}-N+j}{w_{i}-N+j}\right)\frac{dw_{i}}{c_{i}}.

Observe that

\prod_{j=1}^{N}\frac{w+c_{i}-N+j}{w-N+j}=\frac{\Gamma(w_{i}+c_{i}+1)\Gamma(w_{i}-N+1)}{\Gamma(w_{i}+c_{i}-N+1)\Gamma(w_{i}+1)}.

By changing variables
w_{i}=z_{i}+N-1
and consolidating the product over
\ell
, (
2.3
) follows.
∎

3.
Limiting Line Ensemble

The purpose of this section is to introduce line ensembles introduced in
[
CH14
]
, prove the existence of the limiting line ensemble
\boldsymbol{\xi}(t)
and the convergence result
Theorem

1.3
. We prove auxiliary lemmas on the way to the proof of
Theorem

1.3
for later usage.

Definition 3.1
.

Let
\Sigma\subset\mathbb{Z}
and
\Lambda\subset\mathbb{R}
be intervals. Consider the topological space
C(\Sigma\times\Lambda)
with the topology of uniform convergence on compact subsets of
\Sigma\times\Lambda
. We may view
C(\Sigma\times\Lambda)
as the space
\Lambda\times C(\Lambda)
of sequences
(\eta_{i}(t))_{i\in\Sigma}
of continuous functions on
\Lambda
by the identification
\eta(i,t)=\eta_{i}(t)
for
\boldsymbol{\eta}\in C(\Sigma\times\Lambda)
. A
line ensemble (on
\Lambda
)
is a probability measure on
C(\Sigma\times\Lambda)
with respect to the Borel
\sigma
-algebra. For us, the set
\Sigma
will always be
\{1,\ldots,k\}
for some
k
or
\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
. An
infinite line ensemble
will then be a line ensemble with
\Sigma=\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
. A line ensemble
\boldsymbol{\eta}
is
non-intersecting
if
\eta_{i}(t)>\eta_{j}(t)
for
i<j
and
t\in\Lambda
almost surely.

Theorem

1.3
claims the existence of an infinite line ensemble
\{\boldsymbol{\xi}(t)\}_{t>0}
which is the limit of
\{\boldsymbol{\xi}^{(N)}(\tfrac{t}{4})-\tfrac{Nt}{2}-\log N\}_{t>0}
. The following theorem gives explicit expressions for certain observables of
\boldsymbol{\xi}(t)
.

Theorem 3.2
.

We have:

(i)

For
c_{1},\ldots,c_{k}>0
,

(3.1)

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\mathbb{E}\left[\prod_{i=1}^{k}\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}e^{c_{i}\xi_{j}(t_{i})}\right]\\
&\quad=\int\frac{dz_{1}}{2\pi\mathbf{i}c_{1}}\cdots\int\frac{dz_{k}}{2\pi\mathbf{i}c_{k}}\left(\prod_{1\leq i<j\leq k}\frac{(z_{i}-z_{j})(z_{i}+c_{i}-z_{j}-c_{j})}{(z_{i}+c_{i}-z_{j})(z_{i}-z_{j}-c_{j})}\right)\prod_{i=1}^{k}\frac{e^{\frac{t_{i}}{2}(z_{i}+c_{i}-\frac{1}{2})^{2}}}{e^{\frac{t_{i}}{2}(z_{i}-\frac{1}{2})^{2}}}\frac{\Gamma(z_{i})}{\Gamma(z_{i}+c_{i})}\end{split}

where the
z_{i}
contour is an infinite contour positively oriented around
0,-1,-2,\ldots
which starts at
-\infty-\mathbf{i}\epsilon
and ends at
-\infty+\mathbf{i}\epsilon
for
1\leq i\leq k
, and the
z_{j}
contour encloses
z_{i}+c_{i}
and
z_{i}-c_{j}
whenever
1\leq i<j\leq k
.

(ii)

The spacetime correlation kernel for
\boldsymbol{\xi}(t)
is given by

\rho_{k}(t_{1},x_{1};\ldots;t_{k},x_{k})=\det\left[K(t_{i},x_{i};t_{j},x_{j})\right]_{1\leq i,j\leq k}

where

(3.2)

\displaystyle K(s,x;t,y)=-\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi(t-s)}}e^{-\frac{(x-y)^{2}}{2(t-s)}}\mathbf{1}[t>s]+\int\frac{dz}{2\pi\mathbf{i}}\int_{c-\mathbf{i}\infty}^{c+\mathbf{i}\infty}\frac{dw}{2\pi\mathbf{i}}\frac{e^{\frac{tw^{2}}{2}-yw}}{e^{\frac{sz^{2}}{2}-xz}}\frac{1}{w-z}\frac{\Gamma(z+\frac{1}{2})}{\Gamma(w+\frac{1}{2})}.

and the
z
contour is an infinite contour positively oriented around
-\tfrac{1}{2},-\tfrac{3}{2},-\tfrac{5}{2},\ldots
which starts at
-\infty-\mathbf{i}\epsilon
and ends at
-\infty+\mathbf{i}\epsilon
.

Remark 2
.

The explicit expression for the correlation function will not be used directly in this paper, we only use the fact that it is determinantal.

The remainder of this section is devoted to the proofs of
Theorems

1.3
and
3.2
. Our first step is to show the convergence of joint Laplace transforms and correlation functions.

Proposition 3.3
.

Fix
t_{1}\geq\cdots\geq t_{k}>0
. Suppose
\tau_{1}(N)\geq\cdots\geq\tau_{k}(N)>0
such that
t_{i}:=\lim_{N\to\infty}\tau_{i}(N)
for
1\leq i\leq k
.

(i)

For any
c_{1},\ldots,c_{k}>0
,

\displaystyle\lim_{N\to\infty}\mathbb{E}\left[\prod_{i=1}^{k}\sum_{j=1}^{N}e^{c_{i}\left(\xi_{j}^{(N)}(\frac{\tau_{i}(N)}{4})-\frac{N\tau_{i}(N)}{2}-\log N\right)}\right]

\displaystyle\quad\quad=\int\frac{dz_{1}}{2\pi\mathbf{i}c_{1}}\cdots\int\frac{dz_{k}}{2\pi\mathbf{i}c_{k}}\left(\prod_{1\leq i<j\leq k}\frac{(z_{i}-z_{j})(z_{i}+c_{i}-z_{j}-c_{j})}{(z_{i}+c_{i}-z_{j})(z_{i}-z_{j}-c_{j})}\right)\prod_{i=1}^{k}\frac{e^{\frac{t_{i}}{2}(z_{i}+c_{i}-\frac{1}{2})^{2}}}{e^{\frac{t_{i}}{2}(z_{i}-\frac{1}{2})^{2}}}\frac{\Gamma(z_{i})}{\Gamma(z_{i}+c_{i})}

where
c_{1},\ldots,c_{k}>0
, the
z_{i}
contour is an infinite contour positively oriented around
0,-1,-2,\ldots
which starts at
-\infty-\mathbf{i}\epsilon
and ends at
-\infty+\mathbf{i}\epsilon
for
1\leq i\leq k
, and the
z_{j}
contour encloses
z_{i}+c_{i}
and
z_{i}-c_{j}
whenever
1\leq i<j\leq k
.

(ii)

Let
\rho^{(N)}_{k}(\tau_{1},x_{1};\ldots,\tau_{k},x_{k})
denote the
k
th space-time correlation function of

\left(\xi_{1}^{(N)}\left(\frac{\tau}{4}\right)-\frac{N\tau}{2}-\log N,\ldots,\xi_{N}^{(N)}\left(\frac{\tau}{4}\right)-\frac{N\tau}{2}-\log N\right)_{\tau\geq 0}.

Then

\lim_{N\to\infty}\rho^{(N)}_{k}(\tau_{1}(N),x_{1};\ldots,\tau_{k}(N),x_{k})=\det\left[K(t_{i},x_{i};t_{j},x_{j})\right]_{1\leq i,j\leq k}

where
K(s,x;t,y)
is given by (
3.2
).

Proof of
Proposition

3.3
.

Let
c_{1},\ldots,c_{k}>0
. By
Corollary

2.6
, we have

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\mathbb{E}\left[\prod_{i=1}^{k}\sum_{j=1}^{N}e^{c_{i}\left(\xi_{j}(\frac{\tau_{i}(N)}{4})-\frac{N\tau_{i}(N)}{2}-\log N\right)}\right]=\left(\prod_{i=1}^{k}N^{-c_{i}}\right)\\
&\quad\quad\times\oint\frac{dz_{1}}{2\pi\mathbf{i}c_{1}}\cdots\oint\frac{dz_{k}}{2\pi\mathbf{i}c_{k}}\left(\prod_{1\leq i<j\leq k}\frac{(z_{i}-z_{j})(z_{i}+c_{i}-z_{j}-c_{j})}{(z_{i}-z_{j}-c_{j})(z_{i}+c_{i}-z_{j})}\right)\prod_{i=1}^{k}\frac{e^{\frac{\tau_{i}(N)}{2}(z_{i}+c_{i}-\frac{1}{2})^{2}}}{e^{\frac{\tau_{i}(N)}{2}(z_{i}-\frac{1}{2})^{2}}}\frac{\Gamma(z_{i}+c_{i}+N)\Gamma(z_{i})}{\Gamma(z_{i}+c_{i})\Gamma(z_{i}+N)}\end{split}

where the
z_{i}
contour is positively oriented around
0,-1,\ldots,-N+1
for
1\leq i\leq k
and the
z_{j}
contour contains
z_{i}+c_{i}
and
z_{i}-c_{j}
for
1\leq i<j\leq k
. From Stirling’s formula
[
OLBC10
, p141]
(see also
[
Ahn19
, Lemma 6.6]
) for the Gamma function, we have

\frac{\Gamma(z_{i}+c_{i}+N)}{\Gamma(z_{i}+N)}=\frac{(z_{i}+c_{i}+N)^{z_{i}+c_{i}+N-\frac{1}{2}}}{(z_{i}+N)^{z_{i}+N-\frac{1}{2}}}e^{-c_{i}}(1+O(1/N))=N^{c_{i}}(1+O(1/N))

which holds uniformly on compact subsets of the
z_{i}
contour. Combining this with the decay of the integrand for
\Re z\ll 0
but
\Re z>-N-1
and
|\Im z|
bounded away from
0
, we obtain the desired expression

\int\frac{dz_{1}}{2\pi\mathbf{i}c_{1}}\cdots\int\frac{dz_{k}}{2\pi\mathbf{i}c_{k}}\left(\prod_{1\leq i<j\leq k}\frac{(z_{i}-z_{j})(z_{i}+c_{i}-z_{j}-c_{j})}{(z_{i}+c_{i}-z_{j})(z_{i}-z_{j}-c_{j})}\right)\prod_{i=1}^{k}\frac{e^{\frac{t_{i}}{2}(z_{i}+c_{i}-\frac{1}{2})^{2}}}{e^{\frac{t_{i}}{2}(z_{i}-\frac{1}{2})^{2}}}\frac{\Gamma(z_{i})}{\Gamma(z_{i}+c_{i})}

in the limit as
N\to\infty
. Note that the decay of the exponential terms at infinity along the contour is clear. To see the decay of the gamma quotient, we may use the reflection formula for the Gamma function

\Gamma(z)\Gamma(1-z)=\frac{\pi}{\sin(\pi z)}.

Recalling
Theorem

1.2
, we can explicitly write down the spacetime correlation kernel for
\{\boldsymbol{\xi}^{(N)}(\frac{t}{4})-\frac{Nt}{2}\}_{t>0}
by
[
Joh01
]
(see also
[
CP16
, Proposition 4.1]
)

\displaystyle K_{N}(s,x;t,y)=-\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi(t-s)}}e^{-\frac{(x-y)^{2}}{2(t-s)}}\mathbf{1}[t>s]+\oint_{\gamma}\frac{dz}{2\pi\mathbf{i}}\int_{\Gamma_{c}}\frac{dw}{2\pi\mathbf{i}}\frac{e^{\frac{tw^{2}}{2}-yw}}{e^{\frac{sz^{2}}{2}-xz}}\frac{1}{w-z}\prod_{i=1}^{N}\frac{w+i-\frac{1}{2}}{z+i-\frac{1}{2}}

where
\gamma
is a simple closed curve positively oriented around
\{-i+\tfrac{1}{2}\}_{i=1}^{N}
and
\Gamma_{c}:\tau\mapsto c+\mathbf{i}\tau,\tau\in\mathbb{R}
such that
\gamma
and
\Gamma_{c}
are disjoint. Thus

\rho_{k}^{(N)}(\tau_{1},x_{1};\ldots;\tau_{k},x_{k})=\det\left[K_{N}(\tau_{i},x_{i}+\log N;\tau_{j},x_{j}+\log N)\right]_{i,j=1}^{k}.

We can write

\displaystyle K_{N}(s,x+\log N;t,y+\log N)

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi(t-s)}}e^{-\frac{(x-y)^{2}}{2(t-s)}}\mathbf{1}[t>s]

\displaystyle\quad\quad+\oint_{\gamma}\frac{dz}{2\pi\mathbf{i}}\int_{\Gamma_{c}}\frac{dw}{2\pi\mathbf{i}}\frac{e^{\frac{tw^{2}}{2}-yw}}{e^{\frac{sz^{2}}{2}-xz}}\frac{N^{z-w}}{w-z}\frac{\Gamma(w+N+\frac{1}{2})}{\Gamma(z+N+\frac{1}{2})}\frac{\Gamma(z+\frac{1}{2})}{\Gamma(w+\frac{1}{2})}.

From Stirling’s formula for the Gamma function as before, we find

\frac{\Gamma(w+N+\frac{1}{2})}{\Gamma(z+N+\frac{1}{2})}=\frac{(w+N+\frac{1}{2})^{w+N}}{(z+N+\frac{1}{2})^{z+N}}e^{z-w}(1+O(1/N))=N^{w-z}(1+O(1/N)).

Thus, we have

\displaystyle\lim_{N\to\infty}K_{N}(s,x+\log N;t,y+\log N)

\displaystyle\quad\quad=-\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi(t-s)}}e^{-\frac{(x-y)^{2}}{2(t-s)}}\mathbf{1}[t>s]+\int\frac{dz}{2\pi\mathbf{i}}\int_{\Gamma_{c}}\frac{dw}{2\pi\mathbf{i}}\frac{e^{\frac{tw^{2}}{2}-yw}}{e^{\frac{sz^{2}}{2}-xz}}\frac{1}{w-z}\frac{\Gamma(z+\frac{1}{2})}{\Gamma(w+\frac{1}{2})}

where the
z
contour is an infinite contour positively oriented around
-\tfrac{1}{2},-\tfrac{3}{2},-\tfrac{5}{2},\ldots
, starting at
-\infty-\mathbf{i}\epsilon
and ending at
-\infty+\mathbf{i}\epsilon
. For full rigor, we must control the tail of the
z
-contour for
\Re z\ll 0
. This is managed by the reflection formula for the gamma function and the
e^{-\frac{sz^{2}}{2}}
term, as before.
∎

The next two lemmas are the key to proving
Theorems

1.3
and
3.2
. They are stated in a manner convenient for later usage. The first lemma establishes the existence of a limiting process.

Lemma 3.4
.

There exists a process
\{\boldsymbol{\xi}(t):=(\xi_{1}(t),\xi_{2}(t),\ldots)\}_{t>0}
with joint Laplace transform given by (
3.1
) and spacetime correlation kernel given by (
3.2
).

The next lemma links convergence of Laplace transforms with convergence in finite dimensional distributions.

Lemma 3.5
.

Fix
t_{1}\geq\cdots\geq t_{k}>0
. Let
\tau_{1}(N)\geq\cdots\geq\tau_{k}(N)>0
such that
t_{i}:=\lim_{N\to\infty}\tau_{i}(N)
for
1\leq i\leq k
. Suppose
\left\{(\mathsf{y}_{1}^{(N)}(\tau)\geq\cdots\geq\mathsf{y}_{N}^{(N)}(\tau)\right\}_{\tau>0}
is a random
\mathbb{R}^{N}
-valued process such that

(3.3)

\displaystyle\lim_{N\to\infty}\mathbb{E}\left[\prod_{i=1}^{k}\sum_{j=1}^{N}e^{c_{i}\mathsf{y}_{j}^{(N)}(\tau_{i}(N))}\right]=\mathbb{E}\left[\prod_{i=1}^{k}\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}e^{c_{i}\xi_{j}(t_{i})}\right]

for
0<c_{1},\ldots,c_{k}\leq\varepsilon
and some
\varepsilon>0
(which may vary with
k
). Then

\lim_{N\to\infty}\mathbb{P}\left(\mathsf{y}_{j}(\tau_{i}(N))\leq a_{i,j}:1\leq i\leq k,1\leq j\leq h\right)=\mathbb{P}\left(\xi_{j}(t_{i})\leq a_{i,j}:1\leq i\leq k,1\leq j\leq h\right)

for any real numbers
a_{i,j}
(
1\leq i\leq k
,
1\leq j\leq h
) and any positive integer
k
.

Proof of
Lemmas

3.4
and
3.5
.

The argument below closely follows the ideas from
[
Sos99
, Section 5]
and
[
Oko00
, Section 4.1.3]
to show that the convergence of Laplace transforms of the correlation functions implies the desired convergence in finite dimensional distributions. Let
\rho^{(N)}_{k}(\tau_{1},x_{1};\ldots;\tau_{k},x_{k})
denote the space-time correlation function for the process

\left(\mathsf{y}_{1}^{(N)}(\tau),\ldots,\mathsf{y}_{k}^{(N)}(\tau)\right).

Our assumption (
3.3
) implies the existence of the limits

(3.4)

\displaystyle\lim_{N\to\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{k}}e^{c_{1}x_{1}+\cdots+c_{k}x_{k}}\rho^{(N)}_{k}(\tau_{1}(N),x_{1};\ldots;\tau_{k}(N),x_{k})dx_{1}\cdots dx_{k}

for
0<c_{1},\ldots,c_{k}<\varepsilon
where the limit is given by a finite linear combination of (
3.1
). We want to show that this limit is given by some limiting measure
\rho_{k}(t_{1},x_{1};\ldots;t_{k},x_{k})
. For this, define the measure

\varrho^{(N)}_{k}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{k})dx_{1}\cdots dx_{k}:=e^{\theta x_{1}+\cdots+\theta x_{k}}\rho^{(N)}_{k}(\tau_{1}(N),x_{1};\ldots;\tau_{k}(N),x_{k})dx_{1}\cdots dx_{k}

where
\theta=\varepsilon/2
. The existence and form of the limits (
3.4
) implies the weak convergence of
\varrho^{(N)}_{k}
to some limiting finite measure
\varrho_{k}
as measures on
\mathbb{R}^{k}
where the latter measure is finite. Define
\rho_{k}
by

\rho_{k}(t_{1},x_{1};\ldots;t_{k},x_{k})dx_{1}\cdots dx_{k}:=e^{-\theta x_{1}-\cdots-\theta x_{k}}\varrho_{k}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{k})dx_{1}\cdots dx_{k}

where we note the suppression of the dependence on the
\tau
’s and
t
’s in the notation for
\varrho^{(N)}_{k}
and
\varrho_{k}
. Thus

\rho^{(N)}_{k}(\tau_{1}(N),x_{1};\ldots;\tau_{k}(N),x_{k})dx_{1}\cdots dx_{k}\to\rho_{k}(t_{1},x_{1};\ldots;t_{k},x_{k})dx_{1}\cdots dx_{k}

weakly on
\mathbb{R}^{k}
. By
Proposition

3.3
, this convergence holds in particular for
\mathsf{y}^{(N)}(\tau)=\boldsymbol{\xi}^{(N)}(\frac{\tau}{4})-\frac{N\tau}{2}-\log N
so that

\rho_{k}(t_{1},x_{1};\ldots,t_{k},x_{k})=\det\left[K(t_{i},x_{i};t_{j},x_{j})\right]_{1\leq i,j\leq k}

where
K(s,x;t,y)
is given by (
3.2
).

The weak convergence of the correlation functions implies that the joint moments of random variables of the form

\mathcal{Y}_{\tau_{i}(N)}^{(N)}(S):=|\{j:\mathsf{y}_{j}^{(N)}(\tau_{i}(N))\in S\}|,\quad\quad S\subset[c,\infty),\quad\quad 1\leq i\leq k,\quad\quad c>0

converge to corresponding joint moments of some limiting random variables

\mathcal{Y}_{t_{i}}(S),\quad\quad S\subset[c,\infty),\quad\quad 1\leq i\leq k,\quad\quad c>0.

Since the limit
\rho_{k}
is determinantal, the joint moments of the
\mathcal{Y}_{t_{i}}(S)
do not grow faster than factorials so that the convergence of joint moments implies convergence in distribution. Therefore the probabilities

\mathbb{P}\left(\mathsf{y}_{j}^{(N)}(\tau_{i}(N))\leq a_{i,j}:1\leq i\leq k,1\leq j\leq h\right)

converge as
N\to\infty
as they can be expressed as a finite linear combination of probabilities of the form

\mathbb{P}\left(\mathcal{Y}_{\tau_{1}(N)}^{(N)}(S_{1,1})=n_{1,1},\ldots\mathcal{Y}_{\tau_{1}(N)}^{(N)}(S_{1,r_{1}})=n_{1,r_{1}},\ldots,\mathcal{Y}_{\tau_{k}(N)}^{(N)}(S_{k,1})=n_{k,1},\ldots,\mathcal{Y}_{\tau_{k}(N)}^{(N)}(S_{k,r_{k}})=n_{k,r_{k}}\right),

where the sets
S_{i,r}
are among
(a_{i,1},\infty),(a_{i,2},a_{i,1}],\ldots,(a_{i,h},a_{i,h-1}]
. This proves the existence of the limit (in finite dimensional distributions) process
\{(\xi_{1},\xi_{2},\ldots)\}_{t>0}
, where the Laplace transform and spacetime correlation kernel are necessarily given by (
3.1
) and (
3.2
). Thus
Lemmas

3.4
and
3.5
follow.
∎

4.
The
S
-Transform and
\psi

Given a probability measure
\mu
on
\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
, we can define its
\psi
-function and
S
-transform. The former is a generating function for the moments of
\mu
and the latter plays the role of the log characteristic function from classical probability in the context of free probability, where the multiplicative free convolution corresponds to summation of independent random variables, see e.g.
[
Voi87
,
BV92
]
. We collect several properties of these functions for the analysis in subsequent sections.

Definition 4.1
.

Given a probability measure
\mu
supported in
\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
, let

\psi_{\mu}(z):=\int\frac{zx}{1-zx}\,d\mu(x),\quad\quad z\in\mathbb{C}\setminus\supp\mu.

Definition 4.2
.

Let
\mathcal{M}
denote the set of compactly supported Borel probability measures on
\mathbb{R}_{>0}
, in particular
\inf\supp\mu>0
for
\mu\in\mathcal{M}
. We view
\mathcal{M}
as a topological space under the weak topology. Given a closed interval
I\subset\mathbb{R}_{>0}
, let
\mathcal{M}_{I}\subset\mathcal{M}
denote the subset of probability measures supported in
I
, which is compact under the weak topology.

Assume that
\mu\in\mathcal{M}
. Then
\psi_{\mu}
is analytic on
(\mathbb{C}\cup\{\infty\})\setminus J
where
J
is some bounded interval in
\mathbb{R}_{>0}
which contains
\{x^{-1}:\supp\mu\}
. Moreover,

\psi_{\mu}^{\prime}(z)=\int\frac{x}{(1-zx)^{2}}\,d\mu(x)

which is positive for
z\leq 0
. Thus there exists an inverse
\psi_{\mu}^{-1}
defined in a neighborhood of
[-1,0]
which is meromorphic with a simple pole at
-1
and a zero at
0
.

Definition 4.3
.

The
S
-transform
of
\mu\in\mathcal{M}
is given by

S_{\mu}(u):=\frac{1+u}{u}\psi_{\mu}^{-1}(u).

In view of the discussion above, for
\mu
compactly supported in
\mathbb{R}_{>0}
, the
S
-transform is defined in a neighborhood of
[-1,0]
. From our discussion, we see that the following properties hold:

Proposition 4.4
.

Fix a compact subset
I\subset\mathbb{R}_{>0}
. Then there exists a neighborhood
U\subset\mathbb{C}
of
[-1,0]
such that for all
\mu\in\mathcal{M}_{I}

(i)

\psi_{\mu}^{-1}(z)
is well-defined, bijective, meromorphic function on
U
with a unique pole at
-1
and zero at
0
;

(ii)

S_{\mu}(z)
is holomorphic with no zeros on
U
, and

(iii)

the maps
\mu\mapsto S_{\mu}
and
\mu\mapsto\psi_{\mu}^{-1}
on
\mathcal{M}_{I}
are continuous where the topology of the images are with respect to uniform convergence on compact subsets of
U
.

Here are some properties of the
S
-transform which follow from
[
BV92
, Proposition 3.1]
:

Proposition 4.5
.

(i)

S_{\mu}^{\prime}(u)\leq 0
for
u\in[-1,0]
.

(ii)

S_{\mu}(u)>0
for
u\in[-1,0]
.

(iii)

\overline{S_{\mu}(u)}=S_{\mu}(\overline{u})
.

We record a lemma which evaluates the
S
-transform and its first and second derivatives at
0
.

Lemma 4.6
.

Suppose
\mu\in\mathcal{M}
. Let

\kappa_{1}(\mu):=\int x\,d\mu(x),\quad\quad\kappa_{2}(\mu):=\int x^{2}\,d\mu(x)-\left(\int x\,d\mu(x)\right)^{2}

denote the mean and variance of
\mu
respectively. Then

\displaystyle S_{\mu}(0)=\frac{1}{\kappa_{1}(\mu)},\quad\quad S_{\mu}^{\prime}(0)=-\frac{\kappa_{2}(\mu)}{\kappa_{1}(\mu)^{3}},

\displaystyle S_{\mu}^{\prime\prime}(0)=4\frac{\left(\int x^{2}\,d\mu(x)\right)^{2}}{\left(\int x\,d\mu(x)\right)^{5}}-2\frac{\int x^{3}\,d\mu(x)}{\left(\int x\,d\mu(x)\right)^{4}}-2\frac{\int x^{2}\,d\mu(x)}{\left(\int x\,d\mu(x)\right)^{3}}.

Proof.

From the expansion

\psi_{\mu}(z)=z\int x\,d\mu(x)+z^{2}\int x^{2}\,d\mu(x)+z^{3}\int x^{3}\,d\mu(x)+O(|z|^{4}),\quad\quad|z|\to 0,

we get

\psi_{\mu}^{-1}(u)=u\frac{1}{\int x\,d\mu(x)}-u^{2}\frac{\int x^{2}\,d\mu(x)}{\left(\int x\,d\mu(x)\right)^{3}}+u^{3}\left(2\frac{\int x^{2}\,d\mu(x)}{\left(\int x\,d\mu(x)\right)^{5}}-\frac{\int x^{3}\,d\mu(x)}{\left(\int x\,d\mu(x)\right)^{4}}\right)+O(|u|^{4}),\quad\quad|u|\to 0

so that

\displaystyle S_{\mu}(u)=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\int x\,d\mu(x)}+\left(\frac{1}{\int x\,d\mu(x)}-\frac{\int x^{2}\,d\mu(x)}{\left(\int x\,d\mu(x)\right)^{3}}\right)u

\displaystyle\quad\quad+\left(2\frac{\left(\int x^{2}\,d\mu(x)\right)^{2}}{\left(\int x\,d\mu(x)\right)^{5}}-\frac{\int x^{3}\,d\mu(x)}{\left(\int x\,d\mu(x)\right)^{4}}-\frac{\int x^{2}\,d\mu(x)}{\left(\int x\,d\mu(x)\right)^{3}}\right)u^{2}+O(|u|^{3})

as
|u|\to 0
. The result follows.
∎

\psi
-function, for later use.

,

Remark 3
.

From

4.4

of

and extend by continuity on

.

Proof of
Lemma

4.7
.

Our starting point is a proof of the case

, restated in the following claim:

Claim 4.8
.

Fix a compact subset
I\subset\mathbb{R}_{>0}
. Then there exists a neighborhood
U\subset\mathbb{C}
of
[-1,0]
such that for all
\mu\in\mathcal{M}_{I}
and
u,v\in U
, we have

(4.3)

\displaystyle C^{-1}<\frac{1}{\psi_{\mu}^{-1}(u)-\psi_{\mu}^{-1}(v)}\frac{u-v}{\sqrt{\psi_{\mu}^{\prime}(\psi_{\mu}^{-1}(u))\psi_{\mu}^{\prime}(\psi_{\mu}^{-1}(v))}}<C

for some constant

\mu\in\mathcal{M}_{I}
. Moreover,

(4.4)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\psi_{\mu}^{-1}(u)-\psi_{\mu}^{-1}(v)}\frac{u-v}{\sqrt{\psi_{\mu}^{\prime}(\psi_{\mu}^{-1}(u))\psi_{\mu}^{\prime}(\psi_{\mu}^{-1}(v))}}=1+O(|u-v|^{2})

\mu\in\mathcal{M}_{I}
and

.

Proof of
4.8
.

Choose
U\supset[-1,0]
so that
\psi_{\mu}^{-1}
is analytic on its closure for every
\mu\in\mathcal{M}_{I}
, where existence is guaranteed by
Proposition

4.4
. Observe that

\mathrm{C}(u,v):=\frac{1}{\psi_{\mu}^{-1}(u)-\psi_{\mu}^{-1}(v)}\frac{u-v}{\sqrt{\psi_{\mu}^{\prime}(\psi_{\mu}^{-1}(u))\psi_{\mu}^{\prime}(\psi_{\mu}^{-1}(v))}}

and its reciprocal have no poles of codimension
1
and are thus holomorphic on
\mathrm{cl}(U)^{2}
by Riemann’s second extension theorem
[
GR84
, Theorem 7.1.2]
, as in
[
GS18
, Proof of Lemma 3.5]
. Therefore
\mathrm{C}(u,v)
is bounded and does not vanish on
U
. This implies (
4.3
) where the uniformity of
C
follows from the compactness of
\mathcal{M}_{I}
and
\mathrm{cl}(U)
, and the continuity of
\mathrm{C}(u,v)
as a function of
\mu
,
u
, and
v
.

It remains to show (
4.4
). Assume without loss of generality that
I=[a^{-1},a]
for some
a>1
. Fix
\delta>0
small and let
W_{\delta}:=\{w\in U:|w+1|\geq\delta\}
.

We start by showing (
4.4
) for
u,v\in W_{\delta}
. Assuming
u,v\in W_{\delta}
, since

\psi_{\mu}^{-1}(u)-\psi_{\mu}^{-1}(v)=\frac{1}{\psi_{\mu}^{\prime}(\psi_{\mu}^{-1}(v))}(u-v)-\frac{1}{2}\frac{\psi_{\mu}^{\prime\prime}(\psi_{\mu}^{-1}(v))}{\psi_{\mu}^{\prime}(\psi_{\mu}^{-1}(v))^{3}}(u-v)^{2}+O(|u-v|^{3})

we have

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\psi_{\mu}^{-1}(u)-\psi_{\mu}^{-1}(v)}\frac{u-v}{\sqrt{\psi_{\mu}^{\prime}(\psi_{\mu}^{-1}(u))\psi_{\mu}^{\prime}(\psi_{\mu}^{-1}(v))}}

\displaystyle\quad\quad=\frac{\sqrt{\psi_{\mu}^{\prime}(\psi_{\mu}^{-1}(v))}}{\sqrt{\psi_{\mu}^{\prime}(\psi_{\mu}^{-1}(u))}}\frac{1}{1-\frac{1}{2}\frac{\psi_{\mu}^{\prime\prime}(\psi_{\mu}^{-1}(v))}{\psi_{\mu}^{\prime}(\psi_{\mu}^{-1}(v))^{2}}(u-v)+O(|u-v|^{2})}

\displaystyle\quad\quad=\frac{\sqrt{\psi_{\mu}^{\prime}(\psi_{\mu}^{-1}(v))}}{\sqrt{\psi_{\mu}^{\prime}(\psi_{\mu}^{-1}(u))}}\left(1+\frac{1}{2}\frac{\psi_{\mu}^{\prime\prime}(\psi_{\mu}^{-1}(v))}{\psi_{\mu}^{\prime}(\psi_{\mu}^{-1}(v))^{2}}(u-v)+O(|u-v|^{2})\right).

Since

\log\psi_{\mu}^{\prime}(\psi_{\mu}^{-1}(u))=\log\psi_{\mu}^{\prime}(\psi_{\mu}^{-1}(v))+\frac{\psi_{\mu}^{\prime\prime}(\psi_{\mu}^{-1}(v))}{\psi_{\mu}^{\prime}(\psi_{\mu}^{-1}(v))^{2}}(u-v)+O(|u-v|^{2}),

we have

\displaystyle\frac{\sqrt{\psi_{\mu}^{\prime}(\psi_{\mu}^{-1}(v))}}{\sqrt{\psi_{\mu}^{\prime}(\psi_{\mu}^{-1}(u))}}

\displaystyle=\exp\left(\frac{1}{2}\log\psi_{\mu}^{\prime}(\psi_{\mu}^{-1}(v))-\frac{1}{2}\log\psi_{\mu}^{\prime}(\psi_{\mu}^{-1}(u))\right)

\displaystyle=\exp\left(-\frac{1}{2}\frac{\psi_{\mu}^{\prime\prime}(\psi_{\mu}^{-1}(v))}{\psi_{\mu}^{\prime}(\psi_{\mu}^{-1}(v))^{2}}(u-v)+O(|u-v|^{2})\right)

\displaystyle=1-\frac{1}{2}\frac{\psi_{\mu}^{\prime\prime}(\psi_{\mu}^{-1}(v))}{\psi_{\mu}^{\prime}(\psi_{\mu}^{-1}(v))^{2}}(u-v)+O(|u-v|^{2}).

Combining these estimates proves (
4.4
) holds for
u,v\in W_{\delta}
.

To complete the proof, we show that (
4.4
) hold for
u,v\in\{w\in U:|w|\geq\delta\}=-(W_{\delta}+1)
. For
\delta
sufficiently small,
W_{\delta}
and
-(W_{\delta}+1)
cover
U
. This is sufficient since the estimate (
4.4
) holds trivially if
u,v
are separated. We prove this by reduction to the case for
W_{\delta}
. We may write

\frac{1}{\psi_{\mu}^{-1}(u)-\psi_{\mu}^{-1}(v)}\frac{u-v}{\sqrt{\psi_{\mu}^{\prime}(\psi_{\mu}^{-1}(u))\psi_{\mu}^{\prime}(\psi_{\mu}^{-1}(v))}}=\frac{1}{\psi_{\nu}^{-1}(\widetilde{u})-\psi_{\nu}^{-1}(\widetilde{v})}\frac{\widetilde{u}-\widetilde{v}}{\sqrt{\psi_{\nu}^{\prime}(\psi_{\nu}^{-1}(\widetilde{u}))\psi_{\nu}^{\prime}(\psi_{\nu}^{-1}(\widetilde{v}))}}

where
\widetilde{u}=-(u+1)
,
\widetilde{v}=-(v+1)
, and let
\nu
denote the Borel probability measure on
\mathbb{R}_{>0}
determined by
\nu([c_{1},c_{2}])=\mu([c_{2}^{-1},c_{1}^{-1}])
for any
0<c_{1}\leq c_{2}<\infty
. Indeed, observe

\displaystyle\psi_{\nu}(z)

\displaystyle=-\psi_{\mu}(z^{-1})-1

\displaystyle\psi_{\nu}^{-1}(w)

\displaystyle=\psi_{\mu}^{-1}(-(w+1))^{-1}

\displaystyle\psi_{\nu}^{\prime}(z)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{z^{2}}\psi_{\mu}^{\prime}(z^{-1})

\displaystyle\psi_{\nu}^{\prime}(\psi_{\nu}^{-1}(w))

\displaystyle=\psi_{\mu}^{-1}(-(w+1))^{2}\psi_{\mu}^{\prime}(\psi_{\mu}^{-1}(-(w+1))).

Since
\nu\in\mathcal{M}_{I}
(recall
I=[a^{-1},a]
), this completes the proof.
∎

By the Cauchy determinant formula, which states

\det\left(\frac{1}{a_{i}-b_{j}}\right)_{1\leq i,j\leq k}=\frac{\prod_{1\leq i<j\leq k}(a_{i}-a_{j})(b_{j}-b_{i})}{\prod_{i,j=1}^{k}(a_{i}-b_{j})},

we have

\displaystyle\frac{\det\left(\frac{1}{\psi_{\mu}^{-1}(u_{i})-\psi_{\mu}^{-1}(v_{j})}\right)_{1\leq i,j\leq k}}{\det\left(\frac{1}{u_{i}-v_{j}}\right)_{1\leq i,j\leq k}}\prod_{i=1}^{k}\frac{1}{\sqrt{\psi_{\mu}^{\prime}(\psi_{\mu}^{-1}(u_{i}))\psi_{\mu}^{\prime}(\psi_{\mu}^{-1}(v_{i}))}}

\displaystyle\quad\quad=\prod_{i=1}^{k}\frac{u_{i}-v_{i}}{\psi_{\mu}^{-1}(u_{i})-\psi_{\mu}^{-1}(v_{i})}\frac{1}{\sqrt{\psi_{\mu}^{\prime}(\psi_{\mu}^{-1}(u_{i}))\psi_{\mu}^{\prime}(\psi_{\mu}^{-1}(v_{i}))}}

\displaystyle\quad\quad\quad\quad\times\prod_{1\leq i<j\leq k}\frac{u_{i}-v_{j}}{\psi_{\mu}^{-1}(u_{i})-\psi_{\mu}^{-1}(v_{j})}\frac{v_{i}-u_{j}}{\psi_{\mu}^{-1}(v_{i})-\psi_{\mu}^{-1}(u_{j})}\frac{\psi_{\mu}^{-1}(u_{i})-\psi_{\mu}^{-1}(u_{j})}{u_{i}-u_{j}}\frac{\psi_{\mu}^{-1}(v_{i})-\psi_{\mu}^{-1}(v_{j})}{v_{i}-v_{j}}.

Setting

\mathrm{C}(u,v):=\frac{u-v}{\psi_{\mu}^{-1}(u)-\psi_{\mu}^{-1}(v)}\frac{1}{\sqrt{\psi_{\mu}^{\prime}(\psi_{\mu}^{-1}(u))\psi_{\mu}^{\prime}(\psi_{\mu}^{-1}(v))}},

we obtain

\frac{\det\left(\frac{1}{\psi_{\mu}^{-1}(u_{i})-\psi_{\mu}^{-1}(v_{j})}\right)_{1\leq i,j\leq k}}{\det\left(\frac{1}{u_{i}-v_{j}}\right)_{1\leq i,j\leq k}}\prod_{i=1}^{k}\frac{1}{\sqrt{\psi_{\mu}^{\prime}(\psi_{\mu}^{-1}(u_{i}))\psi_{\mu}^{\prime}(\psi_{\mu}^{-1}(v_{i}))}}=\prod_{i=1}^{k}\mathrm{C}(u_{i},v_{i})\prod_{1\leq i<j\leq k}\frac{\mathrm{C}(u_{i},v_{j})\mathrm{C}(v_{i},u_{j})}{\mathrm{C}(u_{i},u_{j})\mathrm{C}(v_{i},v_{j})}.

Then
4.8
implies the bound (
4.1
).

For the estimate (
4.2
), first note that

\mathrm{C}(u_{i},v_{i})=1+O(|u_{i}-v_{i}|^{2})

by
4.8
, and

\frac{\mathrm{C}(u_{i},v_{j})\mathrm{C}(v_{i},u_{j})}{\mathrm{C}(u_{i},u_{j})\mathrm{C}(v_{i},v_{j})}=1+O\left(\max(|u_{i}-v_{i}|^{2},|u_{j}-v_{j}|^{2})\right),

which can be seen by Taylor expanding in
u_{i}
near
v_{i}
and
u_{j}
near
v_{j}
.
∎

5.
Asymptotics of Multivariate Bessel Functions

Given
v_{1},\ldots,v_{k}\in\{N-1,N-2,\ldots,0\}
, define

B_{\mu}^{(N)}(u_{1},\ldots,u_{k};v_{1},\ldots,v_{k}):=\frac{\mathcal{B}_{\boldsymbol{a}}(u_{1},\ldots,u_{k},N-1,\ldots,\widehat{v_{1}},\ldots,\widehat{v_{k}},\ldots,0)}{\mathcal{B}_{\boldsymbol{a}}(N-1,\ldots,0)}

where
\mu:=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\delta_{e^{a_{i}}}
and the hat notation means that
v_{1},\ldots,v_{k}
are omitted from
N-1,N-2,\ldots,0
. In other words, the multivariate Bessel function in the numerator takes as input
\rho_{N}
with
v_{1},\ldots,v_{k}
replaced by
u_{1},\ldots,u_{k}
. In this section, we obtain asymptotics for these normalized multivariate Bessel functions in preparation for proving
Theorem

1.4
. We note that the asymptotics from this section are refinements of those from
[
GS18
, Theorem 3.4]
. Moreover, we obtain our asymptotics by bootstrapping off the latter.

Definition 5.1
.

Define

H_{\mu}(u):=-(u+1)\log S_{\mu}(u)-\int\log\left((u+1)S_{\mu}(u)^{-1}-ux\right)d\mu(x)

where the logarithms are given by the standard branch.

Observe that

H_{\mu}(u)=-(u+1)\log(u+1)+u\log u-u\log\psi_{\mu}^{-1}(u)-\int\log(1-x\psi_{\mu}^{-1}(u))d\mu(x).

Using the fact that

-u-\int\frac{x\psi_{\mu}^{-1}(u)}{1-x\psi_{\mu}^{-1}(u)}d\mu(x)=u-\psi_{\mu}(\psi_{\mu}^{-1}(u))=0,

we have

(5.1)

\displaystyle H_{\mu}^{\prime}(u)=-\log(u+1)+\log u-\log\psi_{\mu}^{-1}(u)=-\log S_{\mu}(u)

and

(5.2)

\displaystyle H_{\mu}^{\prime\prime}(u)=-\frac{S_{\mu}^{\prime}(u)}{S_{\mu}(u)}.

Definition 5.2
.

Let
\mathcal{R}^{N}
denote the subset of
\mathcal{M}_{I}
consisting of probability measures of the form

\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\delta_{x_{i}}

where
x_{1},\ldots,x_{N}\in I
.

Theorem 5.3
.

Fix a closed interval
I\subset\mathbb{R}_{>0}
. There exists an open neighborhood
U
of
[-1,0]
such that

\displaystyle B_{\mu}(N(u_{1}+1),\ldots,N(u_{k}+1);N(v_{1}+1),\ldots,N(v_{k}+1))

\displaystyle=\frac{\det\left(\frac{1}{\psi_{\mu}^{-1}(u_{i})-\psi_{\mu}^{-1}(v_{j})}\right)_{1\leq i,j\leq k}}{\det\left(\frac{1}{u_{i}-v_{j}}\right)_{1\leq i,j\leq k}}\prod_{i=1}^{k}\left[\frac{1}{\sqrt{\psi_{\mu}^{\prime}(\psi_{\mu}^{-1}(u_{i}))\psi_{\mu}^{\prime}(\psi_{\mu}^{-1}(v_{i}))}}\frac{\sqrt{S_{\mu}(v_{i})}e^{NH_{\mu}(u_{i})}}{\sqrt{S_{\mu}(u_{i})}e^{NH_{\mu}(v_{i})}}(1+o(|u_{i}-v_{i}|))\right]

as
N\to\infty
, uniformly over
\mu\in\mathcal{M}_{I}\cap\mathcal{R}^{N}
,
u_{1},\ldots,u_{k}\in U
, and
v_{1},\ldots,v_{k}\in\tfrac{1}{N}\mathbb{Z}\cap[-1,0]
.

Remark 4
.

To translate between our notation and that of
[
GS18
]
, our
H_{\mu}
corresponds to their
\widetilde{\Psi}_{\rho_{N}}
and our
\psi_{\mu}
corresponds to their
M_{\widetilde{\rho}_{N}}
.

Remark 5
.

We note the peculiarity in
Theorem

5.3
that the uniformity
\mu\in\mathcal{M}_{I}\cap\mathcal{R}^{N}
is over a set varying with
N
.

Proof of
Theorem

5.3
.

Our starting point is
[
GS18
, Theorem 3.4]
which states that there is some neighborhood
U
of
[-1,0]
such that

\displaystyle B_{\mu}^{(N)}(N(u_{1}+1),\ldots,N(u_{k}+1);N(v_{1}+1),\ldots,N(v_{k}+1))

\displaystyle=\frac{\det\left(\frac{1}{\psi_{\mu}^{-1}(u_{i})-\psi_{\mu}^{-1}(v_{j})}\right)_{1\leq i,j\leq k}}{\det\left(\frac{1}{u_{i}-v_{j}}\right)_{1\leq i,j\leq k}}\prod_{i=1}^{k}\left[\frac{1}{\sqrt{\psi_{\mu}^{\prime}(\psi_{\mu}^{-1}(u_{i}))\psi_{\mu}^{\prime}(\psi_{\mu}^{-1}(v_{i}))}}\frac{\sqrt{S_{\mu}(v_{i})}e^{NH_{\mu}(u_{i})}}{\sqrt{S_{\mu}(u_{i})}e^{NH_{\mu}(v_{i})}}(1+o(1))\right]

as
N\to\infty
, uniformly for
u_{1},\ldots,u_{k},v_{1},\ldots,v_{k}\in U
and
\mu\in\mathcal{M}_{I}\cap\mathcal{R}^{N}
. We note that the original statement of
[
GS18
, Theorem 3.4]
is in the regime where
\mu=\mu_{N}
converges weakly to a measure in
\mathcal{M}_{I}
as
N\to\infty
, but the proof also implies uniform asymptotics for
\mu\in\mathcal{M}_{I}\cap\mathcal{R}^{N}
. Thus, it remains to improve the relative
o(1)
error.

Define

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\mathfrak{B}_{\mu}^{(N)}(u_{1},\ldots,u_{k};v_{1},\ldots,v_{k})\\
&\quad\quad=\frac{\det\left(\frac{1}{\psi_{\mu}^{-1}(u_{i})-\psi_{\mu}^{-1}(v_{j})}\right)_{1\leq i,j\leq k}}{\det\left(\frac{1}{u_{i}-v_{j}}\right)_{1\leq i,j\leq k}}\prod_{i=1}^{k}\left[\frac{1}{\sqrt{\psi_{\mu}^{\prime}(\psi_{\mu}^{-1}(u_{i}))\psi_{\mu}^{\prime}(\psi_{\mu}^{-1}(v_{i}))}}\frac{\sqrt{S_{\mu}(v_{i})}e^{NH_{\mu}(u_{i})}}{\sqrt{S_{\mu}(u_{i})}e^{NH_{\mu}(v_{i})}}\right]\end{split}

for
u_{1},\ldots,u_{k},v_{1},\ldots,v_{k}\in U
. Then for
\varepsilon>0
sufficiently small,

B_{\mu}^{(N)}(N(u_{1}+1),\ldots,N(u_{k}+1);N(v_{1}+1),\ldots,N(v_{k}+1))=\mathfrak{B}_{\mu}^{(N)}(u_{1},\ldots,u_{k};v_{1},\ldots,v_{k})(1+o(1))

as
N\to\infty
, uniformly over
u_{1},\ldots,u_{k}\in U_{\varepsilon}
, and
v_{1},\ldots,v_{k}\in\tfrac{1}{N}\mathbb{Z}\cap[-1,0]
, where
U_{\varepsilon}
denotes the
\varepsilon
-neighborhood of
[-1,0]
.

By
Lemma

4.7
, the quotient of Cauchy determinants in the definition of
\mathfrak{B}_{\mu}^{(N)}
is bounded and bounded away from
0
for
u_{1},\ldots,u_{k},v_{1},\ldots,v_{k}\in U_{\varepsilon}
, for
\varepsilon>0
sufficiently small. Similarly, since (see
Proposition

4.4
)

\psi_{\mu}^{\prime}(\psi_{\mu}^{-1}(0))=\psi_{\mu}^{\prime}(0)\neq 0\quad\quad\mbox{and}\quad\quad S_{\mu}(0)\neq 0,

we have

\frac{1}{\sqrt{\psi_{\mu}^{\prime}(\psi_{\mu}^{-1}(u))\psi_{\mu}^{\prime}(\psi_{\mu}^{-1}(v))}}\frac{\sqrt{S_{\mu}(v)}}{\sqrt{S_{\mu}(u)}}

is bounded and bounded away from
0
for
u_{1},\ldots,u_{k},v_{1},\ldots,v_{k}\in U_{\varepsilon}
, given that
\varepsilon
is sufficiently small. For each integer
k\geq 1
, define

\displaystyle F_{k}^{(N)}(u_{1},\ldots,u_{k};v_{1},\ldots,v_{k})

\displaystyle:=\log\left(\frac{B_{\mu}^{(N)}(N(u_{1}+1),\ldots,N(u_{k}+1);N(v_{1}+1),\ldots,N(v_{k}+1))}{B_{\mu}^{(N)}(N(u_{1}+1),\ldots,N(u_{k-1}+1);N(v_{1}+1),\ldots,N(v_{k-1}+1))}\right)

\displaystyle\mathfrak{F}_{k}^{(N)}(u_{1},\ldots,u_{k};v_{1},\ldots,v_{k})

\displaystyle:=\log\left(\frac{\mathfrak{B}_{\mu}^{(N)}(Nu_{1},\ldots,Nu_{k};Nv_{1},\ldots,Nv_{k})}{\mathfrak{B}_{\mu}^{(N)}(Nu_{1},\ldots,Nu_{k-1};Nv_{1},\ldots,Nv_{k-1})}\right)

where in the case
k=1
, we take the denominator in the logarithm to be
1
. Then
F_{k}^{(N)}
and
\mathfrak{F}_{k}^{(N)}
are analytic for
u_{1},\ldots,u_{k}\in U_{\varepsilon}
, where
v_{1},\ldots,v_{k}\in\tfrac{1}{N}\mathbb{Z}\cap[-1,0]
and
N
is sufficiently large. Furthermore,
F_{k}^{(N)}
and
\mathfrak{F}_{k}^{(N)}
vanish whenever
u_{k}=v_{k}
.

Then

\frac{1}{u_{i}-v_{i}}F_{i}^{(N)}(u_{1},\ldots,u_{i};v_{1},\ldots,v_{i})\quad\quad\mbox{and}\quad\quad\frac{1}{u_{i}-v_{i}}\mathfrak{F}_{i}^{(N)}(u_{1},\ldots,u_{i};v_{1},\ldots,v_{i})

are analytic for
u_{1},\ldots,u_{i}\in U_{\varepsilon}
, where
v_{1},\ldots,v_{i}\in\tfrac{1}{N}\mathbb{Z}\cap[-1,0]
and
N
is sufficiently large. Restricting to
|u_{1}|=\cdots=|u_{i}|=2\varepsilon/3
, we have

\frac{1}{u_{i}-v_{i}}F_{i}^{(N)}(u_{1},\ldots,u_{i};v_{1},\ldots,v_{i})-\frac{1}{u_{i}-v_{i}}\mathfrak{F}_{i}^{(N)}(u_{1},\ldots,u_{i};v_{1},\ldots,v_{i})=o(1).

uniformly for
u_{1},\ldots,u_{i}\in\partial U_{2\varepsilon/3}
and
v_{1},\ldots,v_{i}\in\tfrac{1}{N}\mathbb{Z}\cap[-1,0]
. By Cauchy integral formula, the estimate above is valid for
u_{1},\ldots,u_{i}\in U_{\varepsilon/2}
and
v_{1},\ldots,v_{i}\in\tfrac{1}{N}\mathbb{Z}\cap[-1,0]
. Therefore,

\displaystyle B_{\mu}^{(N)}(N(u_{1}+1),\ldots,N(u_{k}+1);N(v_{1}+1),\ldots,N(v_{k}+1))

\displaystyle\quad=\exp\left(\sum_{i=1}^{k}F_{i}^{(N)}(u_{1},\ldots,u_{i};v_{1},\ldots,v_{i})\right)

\displaystyle\quad=\exp\left(\sum_{i=1}^{k}\Big(\mathfrak{F}_{i}^{(N)}(u_{1},\ldots,u_{i};v_{1},\ldots,v_{i})+o(|u_{i}-v_{i}|)\Big)\right)

\displaystyle\quad=\mathfrak{B}_{\mu}^{(N)}(u_{1},\ldots,u_{k};v_{1},\ldots,v_{k})(1+o(\max_{i}|u_{i}-v_{i}|))

as
N\to\infty
, uniformly for
u_{1},\ldots,u_{k}\in U_{\varepsilon/2}
and
v_{1},\ldots,v_{k}\in\tfrac{1}{N}\mathbb{Z}\cap[-1,0]
. This completes the proof of
Theorem

5.3
.
∎

The key step is to establish the following intermediate result.

Theorem 6.1

Suppose that the hypotheses of

\displaystyle\left.\prod_{j\neq i}\frac{c_{\ell}+c_{r}^{\prime}+z_{i}-z_{j}}{z_{i}-z_{j}}\right|_{\boldsymbol{z}=\rho_{N}}

\displaystyle=\prod_{j\neq i}\frac{c_{\ell}+c_{r}^{\prime}+j-i}{j-i}

\displaystyle\leq\prod_{j\neq 1}\frac{c_{\ell}+c_{r}^{\prime}+j-1}{j-1}

\displaystyle\leq\prod_{j\neq 1}\frac{c_{1}+\cdots+c_{\ell}+j-1}{j-1}

\displaystyle=\left.\mathcal{T}_{c_{1},z_{1}}\cdots\mathcal{T}_{c_{\ell-1},z_{1}}\prod_{j\neq 1}\frac{c_{\ell}+z_{1}-z_{j}}{z_{1}-z_{j}}\right|_{\boldsymbol{z}=\rho_{N}}

where the third line uses the fact that
c_{\ell}+c_{r}^{\prime}\leq c_{1}+\cdots+c_{\ell}
, recalling that products are restricted over
1\leq j\leq N
. Combining these inequalities, the claim follows.
∎

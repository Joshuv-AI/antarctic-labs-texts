Abstract
. Let ( M , g ) (M,g) be a closed Riemannian manifold. The aim of this work is to prove the Lebeau-Robbiano spectral inequality for a positive elliptic pseudo-differential operator E ⁡ ( x , D ) E(x,D) on M , M, of order ν > 0 , \nu>0, in the Hörmander class Ψ ρ , δ ν ​ ( M ) . \Psi^{\nu}_{\rho,\delta}(M). In control theory this has been an open problem prior to this work. As an application of this fundamental result, we establish the null-controllability of the (fractional) heat equation associated with E ⁡ ( x , D ) . E(x,D). The sensor ω ⊂ M \omega\subset M in the observability inequality is an open subset of M . M. The obtained results (that are, the corresponding spectral inequality for an elliptic operator and the null-controllability for its diffusion model) extend in the setting of closed manifolds, classical results of the control theory, as the spectral inequality due to Lebeau and Robbiano and their result on the null-controllability of the heat equation giving a complete picture of the subject in the setting of closed manifolds. As a consequence of our analysis we prove the Donnelly-Fefferman inequality for positive pseudo-differential operators for sums of eigenfunctions. For the proof of the spectral inequality we introduce a periodization approach in time inspired by the global pseudo-differential calculus due to Ruzhansky and Turunen.

1.
Introduction

In 1995 Labeau and Robbiano
[
15
]
proved the null-controllability of the heat equation for the Laplacian on a compact manifold (with or without smooth boundary). An instrumental tool in the proof of Labeu-Robbiano’s result
is a spectral inequality for the wave packets of eigenfunctions of the Laplacian originated from
[
15
,
13
]
. In the case of a single eigenfuction
\rho_{j},
the Lebeau-Robbiano spectral inequality implies the doubling property proved by Donnelly and Fefferman
[
6
]
. The spectral inequality from
[
15
,
13
]
has been used in many areas of analysis, for instance, in the aforementioned result of the control theory, or as in
[
6
]
in the geometric analysis of the Hausdorff measure of nodal sets. In this work we extend such a spectral inequality to the case of a positive elliptic pseudo-differential operator
E(x,D)
of arbitrary order
\nu>0
and we apply this spectral inequality to the null-controllability of the heat equation (and also of the fractional heat equation) associated to
E(x,D).

The analysis here includes the family of elliptic differential operators of arbitrary order with smooth coefficients where the main obstruction in proving the Lebeau-Robbiano spectral inequality is the lack of Carleman estimates. Indeed, in the high-dimensional case, the so-called Hörmander subellipticity
condition does not hold, which is typical for product operators. Our approach compensates it with the use of the calculus of (possibly non-local) pseudo-differential operators on compact manifolds due to Hörmander
[
12
]
. Moreover, we prove that any positive elliptic pseudo-differential operator of positive order in the standard
(\rho,\delta)
-Hörmander class on a closed manifold
M
satisfies the Lebeau-Robbiano spectral inequality.

In general pseudo-differential operators are non-local and the use of Carleman estimates, which is the analytical tool by excellence in the proof of the Lebeau-Robbiano spectral inequality and their subsequent generalisations, are not valid. This work introduces a new approach in the proof of these spectral inequalities for elliptic differential operators, embedding this problem in the general setting of the pseudo-differential calculus due to Hörmander where we exploit e.g. the Calderón-Vaillancourt theorem and other microlocal properties of the pseudo-differential calculus.

The validity of the Lebeau-Robbiano spectral inequality for an elliptic pseudo-differential operator implies the null-controllability for its corresponding diffusion model. This is a very well known consequence of the equivalence between the null-controllability of a linear model and its corresponding observability inequality due to the Hilbert Uniqueness Method developed by J. -L. Lions
[
18
]
. The null-controllability for the diffusion model associated to the operator
E(x,D)^{\alpha}
as well as its corresponding spectral inequality are presented in Theorem
1.2
and in Theorem
1.4
below.

1.1.
Outline and main results

To discuss the contributions of this work we precise some notations.

The point of view that we adopt, and that is one of the novelties of this work, is the use of the pseudo-differential calculus on compact manifolds without boundary (see Hörmander
[
12
]
) in the proof of the spectral inequality (
1.3
). One of the reasons for introducing this technique comes from the lack of Carleman estimates for pseudo-differential operators (which in general are not local operators) (see e.g. the discusssion by Rousseau and Robbiano in
[
24
, Subsection 1.4]
). Nevertheless, when using the theory of pseudo-differential operators, other techniques of the modern microlocal analysis as the Calderón-Vaillancourt theorem for closed manifolds, and the analytic functional calculus of elliptic operators can be applied in order to prove an interpolation inequality of the form
\|F\|_{H^{1}(M\times(\alpha,T-\alpha))}\lesssim\|F\|_{H^{1}(M\times(0,T)}^{\kappa}\|F\|^{1-\kappa}_{L^{2}(\omega)}
for a suitable wave function
F
and that is a crucial tool in the proof of (
1.3
). One of the difficulties in the proof of this inequality is that the operators
E(x,D)
could be in general of non-local type.

We also observe that the main reason in considering only in our analysis the case of a compact manifold without boundary is the use of the pseudo-differential calculus by itself. Indeed, when the manifold
M
has a non-empty smooth boundary
\partial M
, the interior
\textnormal{int}(M)
of
M
is an open submanifold, and the pseudo-differential calculus for
M
requires a more delicate analysis. In this setting, pseudo-differential operators are matrices acting from
C^{\infty}(\textnormal{int}(M))\oplus C^{\infty}(\partial M)
into itself and the calculus uses the transmission property introduced by Hörmander. We refer the reader to the seminal work of Boutet de Monvel in
[
4
]
for details.

\varkappa(x)=\sum_{\lambda_{j}\leq\lambda}a_{j}\rho_{j}(x).

(2.6)

Observe that for the proof of the spectral inequality (
2.4

F(x,t):=\sum_{\lambda_{j}\leq\lambda}\frac{\sinh(\lambda_{j}t)}{\lambda_{j}}a_{j}\rho_{j}(x),\,\,(x,t)\in M_{T}:=M\times[0,T],

(2.7)

satisfies the interpolation inequality

\|F\|_{H^{1}(M\times(\alpha,T-\alpha))}\leq C\|F\|_{H^{1}(M_{T})}^{\kappa}\|\varkappa\|_{L^{2}(\omega)}^{1-\kappa}.

(2.8)

Indeed, one can follows e.g. the analysis in
[
16
]
. It starts using the Parseval theorem in order to have

\displaystyle\|F\|^{2}_{H^{1}(M\times(\alpha,T-\alpha))}

\displaystyle\geq C\|F\|^{2}_{L^{2}(M\times(\alpha,T-\alpha))}

\displaystyle=\smallint_{\alpha}^{T-\alpha}\smallint\limits_{M}\left|\sum_{\lambda_{j}\leq\lambda}\frac{\sinh(\lambda_{j}t)}{\lambda_{j}}a_{j}\rho_{j}(x)\right|^{2}dx\,dt

\displaystyle=\sum_{\lambda_{j}\leq\lambda}|a_{j}|^{2}\smallint_{\alpha}^{T-\alpha}\left|\frac{\sinh(\lambda_{j}t)}{\lambda_{j}}\right|^{2}dt

\displaystyle\geq\sum_{\lambda_{j}\leq C\lambda}|a_{j}|^{2}\smallint_{\alpha}^{T-\alpha}t^{2}dt

\displaystyle=C_{\alpha}\sum_{\lambda_{j}\leq\lambda}|a_{j}|^{2}.

Observing that

\partial_{t}F(x,0)=\sum_{\lambda_{j}\leq\lambda}a_{j}\rho_{j}(x),\textnormal{ and that, }\|F\|_{H^{1}(M_{T})}^{2}\lesssim e^{2T\lambda}\lambda^{2}\sum_{\lambda_{j}\leq\lambda}|a_{j}|^{2},

(2.9)

we deduce that

C_{\alpha}\sum_{\lambda_{j}\leq\lambda}|a_{j}|^{2}\lesssim_{\alpha,T}\left(e^{2T\lambda}\lambda^{2}\sum_{\lambda_{j}\leq\lambda}|a_{j}|^{2}\right)^{\kappa}\left\|\sum_{\lambda_{j}\leq\lambda}a_{j}\rho_{j}(x)\right\|_{L^{2}(\omega)}^{2(1-\kappa)},

(2.10)

from which we deduce that

\displaystyle\left(\sum_{\lambda_{j}\leq\lambda}|a_{j}|^{2}\right)^{1-\kappa}\lesssim e^{2T\lambda}\lambda^{2}\left\|\sum_{\lambda_{j}\leq\lambda}a_{j}\rho_{j}(x)\right\|_{L^{2}(\omega)}^{2(1-\kappa)},

and in consequence

\displaystyle\left(\sum_{\lambda_{j}\leq\lambda}|a_{j}|^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\lesssim e^{{T\lambda/(1-\kappa)}}\lambda^{{1/(1-\kappa)}}\left\|\sum_{\lambda_{j}\leq\lambda}a_{j}\rho_{j}(x)\right\|_{L^{2}(\omega)},

proving (
2.4
). Indeed, one can estimate
e^{{T\lambda/(1-\kappa)}}\lambda^{{1/(1-\kappa)}}\lesssim e^{{T\lambda/(1-\kappa)}}.

2.2.5.
Proof of the interpolation inequality

Now, we are ready for the proof of the interpolation inequality

\|F\|_{H^{1}(M\times(\alpha,T-\alpha))}\leq C_{s_{0},s_{00}}\|F\|_{H^{1}(M_{T})}^{\kappa}\|\varkappa\|_{L^{2}(\omega)}^{1-\kappa}.

In the identity (
2.24
) below, we will prove that with
\phi
defined in (
2.16
), we have that

\displaystyle\|(-\partial_{t}^{2}-\Delta_{g})\phi(x,t)\|_{L^{2}(M_{T+\varepsilon})}=1/\sqrt{2}\|(-\partial_{t}^{2}-\Delta_{g})\phi(x,t)\|_{L^{2}(M\times\mathbb{T}_{T,\varepsilon})}.

The positivity condition

E(x,D)\geq cI,\,\,\,c>0,

and the spectral mapping theorem gives the lower bound

E(x,D)^{\frac{2}{\nu}}\geq c^{\frac{2}{\nu}}I,\,\,\,c>0,

which
gives the invertibility of the pseudo-differential operator

E(t,x,D,\partial_{t})=-\partial_{t}^{2}+E(x,D)^{\frac{2}{\nu}}:H^{2}(M\times\mathbb{T}_{T,\varepsilon})\rightarrow L^{2}(M\times\mathbb{T}_{T,\varepsilon}),

that is, the operator
,

E(t,x,D,\partial_{t})^{-1}:L^{2}(M\times\mathbb{T}_{T,\varepsilon})\rightarrow H^{2}(M\times\mathbb{T}_{T,\varepsilon})

is bounded, see Lemma
6.1
.
Moreover,

\displaystyle\|(-\partial_{t}^{2}-\Delta_{g})\phi(x,t)\|_{L^{2}(M\times\mathbb{T}_{T,\varepsilon})}\leq C\|(-\partial_{t}^{2}+E(x,D)^{\frac{2}{\nu}})\phi(x,t)\|_{L^{2}(M\times\mathbb{T}_{T,\varepsilon})},

where the constant
C
is independent of
\varepsilon>0.
Indeed,

\|(-\partial_{t}^{2}-\Delta_{g})\phi(x,t)\|_{L^{2}(M\times\mathbb{T}_{T,\varepsilon})}

=\|(-\partial_{t}^{2}-\Delta_{g})(-\partial_{t}^{2}+E(x,D)^{2/\nu})^{-1}(-\partial_{t}^{2}+E(x,D)^{2/\nu})\phi(x,t)\|_{L^{2}(M\times\mathbb{T}_{T,\varepsilon})}.

From Lemma
7.1
, the operator
(-\partial_{t}^{2}-\Delta_{g})(-\partial_{t}^{2}+E(x,D)^{2/\nu})^{-1}
belongs to the class
\Psi^{0}_{1,\delta}(M)
and the Calderón-Vaillancourt theorem gives its boundedness on
L^{2},
with its operator norm bounded by a constant
C>0,
independent of
\varepsilon>0.

Consequently,

\|(-\partial_{t}^{2}-\Delta_{g})(-\partial_{t}^{2}+E(x,D)^{2/\nu})^{-1}(-\partial_{t}^{2}+E(x,D)^{2/\nu})\phi(x,t)\|_{L^{2}(M\times\mathbb{T}_{T,\varepsilon})}

\leq C\|(-\partial_{t}^{2}+E(x,D)^{2/\nu})\phi(x,t)\|_{L^{2}(M\times\mathbb{T}_{T,\varepsilon})}.

In what follows we estimate this norm.
Let us denote

Z_{1}:=\|(-\partial_{t}^{2}+E(x,D)^{\frac{2}{\nu}})\phi(x,t)\|_{L^{2}(M\times\mathbb{T}_{T,\varepsilon})},

and let us keep in mind that the partial analysis above gives us the inequality

\|\phi\|_{H^{1}(M\times(\alpha,T-\alpha))}\lesssim\|\phi\|_{H^{1}(M_{T+\varepsilon})}^{\kappa}(Z_{1}+|\psi(0)|\|\varkappa\|_{L^{2}(\omega)})^{1-\kappa},

(2.21)

where we have used that

\psi(0)\varkappa=\partial_{t}\phi(x,0)=\psi(0)\partial_{t}F(x,0)=\psi(0)\sum_{\lambda_{j}\leq\lambda}a_{j}\rho_{j}(x).

Indeed, we record that
for
t\in[0,T],
we have
the identities

\partial_{t}F(x,t)=\sum_{\lambda_{j}\leq\lambda}{\sinh(\lambda_{j}t)}a_{j}\rho_{j}(x),

\partial_{t}^{2}F(x,t)=\sum_{\lambda_{j}\leq\lambda}{\sinh(\lambda_{j}t)}\lambda_{j}a_{j}\rho_{j}(x).

2.2.6.
Estimate of
Z_{1}

Observe that

E(x,D)^{\frac{2}{\nu}}F(x,t)=\sum_{\lambda_{j}\leq\lambda}\frac{\sinh(\lambda_{j}t)}{\lambda_{j}}a_{j}E(x,D)^{\frac{2}{\nu}}(\rho_{j})(x)=\sum_{\lambda_{j}\leq\lambda}\frac{\sinh(\lambda_{j}t)}{\lambda_{j}}a_{j}\lambda_{j}^{2}\rho_{j}(x)

Since
\phi(x,t)=\psi(t)F(x,t)
on
[0,T],
and
\psi
is constant on
[0,T]
we have that

\forall x\in M,\,\forall t\in(0,T),\,(-\partial_{t}^{2}+E(x,D)^{\frac{2}{\nu}})\phi(x,t)=0.

(2.22)

First, note that the following symmetry property is valid due to the identity
\phi(x,t)=-\phi(x,-t),

\|(-\partial_{t}^{2}+E(x,D)^{\frac{2}{\nu}})\phi(x,t)\|_{L^{2}(M\times\mathbb{T}_{T,\varepsilon})}^{2}=2\|(-\partial_{t}^{2}+E(x,D)^{\frac{2}{\nu}})\phi(x,t)\|^{2}_{L^{2}(M_{T+\varepsilon})}.

(2.23)

Moreover, once proved (
2.23
), we have in the particular case (where
E(x,D)=-\Delta_{g}
) of the Laplacian, the following inequality.

\|(-\partial_{t}^{2}-\Delta_{g})\phi(x,t)\|_{L^{2}(M\times\mathbb{T}_{T,\varepsilon})}^{2}=2\|(-\partial_{t}^{2}-\Delta_{g})\phi(x,t)\|^{2}_{L^{2}(M_{T+\varepsilon})}.

(2.24)

Indeed, for the proof of (
2.23
) observe that

\displaystyle\|(-\partial_{t}^{2}+E(x,D)^{\frac{2}{\nu}})\phi(x,t)\|_{L^{2}(M\times\mathbb{T}_{T,\varepsilon})}^{2}

\displaystyle=\smallint\limits_{M}\smallint\limits_{-T-\varepsilon}^{0}|(-\partial_{t}^{2}+E(x,D)^{\frac{2}{\nu}})\phi(x,t)|^{2}dt\,dx+\smallint\limits_{M}\smallint\limits_{0}^{T+\varepsilon}|(-\partial_{t}^{2}+E(x,D)^{\frac{2}{\nu}})\phi(x,t)|^{2}dt\,dx

\displaystyle=\smallint\limits_{M}\smallint\limits_{-T-\varepsilon}^{0}|(-\partial_{t}^{2}+E(x,D)^{\frac{2}{\nu}})(-\phi(x,-t)|^{2}dt\,dx+\smallint\limits_{M}\smallint\limits_{0}^{T+\varepsilon}|(-\partial_{t}^{2}+E(x,D)^{\frac{2}{\nu}})\phi(x,t)|^{2}dt\,dx

\displaystyle=\smallint\limits_{M}\smallint\limits_{-T-\varepsilon}^{0}|\phi_{tt}(x,-t)-E(x,D)^{\frac{2}{\nu}}\phi(x,-t)|^{2}dt\,dx+\smallint\limits_{M}\smallint\limits_{0}^{T+\varepsilon}|(-\partial_{t}^{2}+E(x,D)^{\frac{2}{\nu}})\phi(x,t)|^{2}dt\,dx

\displaystyle=\smallint\limits_{M}\smallint\limits_{0}^{T+\varepsilon}|-\phi_{tt}(x,t)+E(x,D)^{\frac{2}{\nu}}\phi(x,t)|^{2}dt\,dx+\smallint\limits_{M}\smallint\limits_{0}^{T+\varepsilon}|(-\partial_{t}^{2}+E(x,D)^{\frac{2}{\nu}})\phi(x,t)|^{2}dt\,dx

\displaystyle=2\smallint\limits_{M}\smallint\limits_{0}^{T+\varepsilon}|(-\partial_{t}^{2}+E(x,D)^{\frac{2}{\nu}})\phi(x,t)|^{2}dt\,dx.

The cancellation property in (
2.22

\phi(x,t)=-\phi(x,-t),
and the positivity of the operator
(-\partial_{t}^{2}+E(x,D)^{\frac{2}{\nu}})
on

(that is, making integration by parts with respect to the operator

\displaystyle\|(-\partial_{t}^{2}+E(x,D)^{\frac{2}{\nu}})

\displaystyle\phi(x,t)\|_{L^{2}(M_{T+\varepsilon})}^{2}=\frac{1}{2}\|(-\partial_{t}^{2}+E(x,D)^{\frac{2}{\nu}})\phi(x,t)\|_{L^{2}(M\times\mathbb{T}_{T,\varepsilon})}^{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}|((-\partial_{t}^{2}+E(x,D)^{\frac{2}{\nu}})\phi),(-\partial_{t}^{2}+E(x,D)^{\frac{2}{\nu}})\phi)_{L^{2}(M\times\mathbb{T}_{T,\varepsilon})}|

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}|((-\partial_{t}^{2}+E(x,D)^{\frac{2}{\nu}})^{2}\phi),\phi)_{L^{2}(M\times\mathbb{T}_{T,\varepsilon})}|

\displaystyle\leq\smallint\limits_{M}\smallint\limits_{[0,T+\varepsilon)}|(-\partial_{t}^{2}+E(x,D)^{\frac{2}{\nu}})^{2}\phi(x,t)||\overline{\phi(x,t)}|dxdt

\displaystyle=\smallint\limits_{M}\smallint\limits_{[T,T+\varepsilon)}|(-\partial_{t}^{2}+E(x,D)^{\frac{2}{\nu}})^{2}\phi(x,t)||{\phi(x,t)}|dxdt.

Therefore, we have the estimate

\displaystyle\|(-\partial_{t}^{2}+E(x,D)^{\frac{2}{\nu}})\phi(x,t)\|_{L^{2}(M_{T+\varepsilon})}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\smallint\limits_{M}\smallint\limits_{[T,T+\varepsilon)}|(-\partial_{t}^{2}+E(x,D)^{\frac{2}{\nu}})^{2}\phi(x,t)||\phi(x,t)|dxdt

\displaystyle\leq\smallint\limits_{M}\smallint\limits_{[T,T+\varepsilon)}|\phi(x,t)|dxdt\times\|(-\partial_{t}^{2}+E(x,D)^{\frac{2}{\nu}})^{2}\phi\|_{L^{\infty}}

\displaystyle=I\times II,

where

I=\smallint\limits_{M}\smallint\limits_{[T,T+\varepsilon)}|\phi(x,t)|dx\,dt,\,II=\|(-\partial_{t}^{2}+E(x,D)^{\frac{2}{\nu}})^{2}\phi\|_{L^{\infty}(M\times[T,T+\varepsilon))}.

2.2.7.
Estimate for
I

Note that

\displaystyle I\leq\textnormal{Vol}(M)\times\varepsilon\times\|\phi\|_{L^{\infty}(M\times[T,T+\varepsilon])}=\textnormal{Vol}(M)\times\varepsilon\|\psi(t)\|_{L^{\infty}[T,T+\varepsilon]}\|F(x,t)\|_{L^{\infty}}.

Now, for any
t
fixed, and
s_{0}\in\mathbb{N}
observe that

|F(x,t)|\leq\sup_{0\leq s\leq s_{0}}\|(1+E(x,D))^{\frac{s}{\nu}}F(\cdot,t)\|_{L^{\infty}(M)}.

(2.25)

Using the Sobolev embedding theorem, we have that for any
s_{00}>n/2,

\displaystyle\sup_{0\leq s\leq s_{0}}\|(1+E(x,D))^{\frac{s}{\nu}}F(\cdot,t)\|_{L^{\infty}(M)}

\displaystyle\leq\sup_{0\leq s\leq s_{0}}\|(1+E(x,D))^{\frac{s+s_{00}}{\nu}}F(\cdot,t)\|_{L^{2}(M)}

\displaystyle\leq\sup_{0\leq s\leq s_{0}+s_{00}}\|(1+E(x,D))^{\frac{s}{\nu}}F(\cdot,t)\|_{L^{2}(M)}.

Now, let us use the spectral theory of the operator
(1+E(x,D))^{\frac{s}{\nu}}
on
L^{2}(M).

Since

\displaystyle\|(1+E(x,D))^{\frac{s}{\nu}}F(\cdot,t)\|_{L^{2}(M)}^{2}

\displaystyle=\left\|\sum_{\lambda_{j}\leq\lambda}\frac{\sinh(\lambda_{j}t)}{\lambda_{j}}(1+\lambda_{j}^{\nu})^{\frac{s}{\nu}}a_{j}\rho_{j}(x)\right\|^{2}_{L^{2}(M)}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\lambda_{j}\leq\lambda}\left|\frac{\sinh(\lambda_{j}t)}{\lambda_{j}}\right|^{2}(1+\lambda_{j}^{\nu})^{\frac{2s}{\nu}}|a_{j}|^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{\lambda_{j}\leq\lambda}\left|\frac{\sinh(\lambda_{j}t)}{\lambda_{j}}\right|^{2}\lambda_{j}^{2s}|a_{j}|^{2}\lesssim\sum_{\lambda_{j}\leq\lambda}e^{\lambda_{j}t}\lambda_{j}^{2s-1}|a_{j}|^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim_{s_{0},s_{00}}e^{\lambda T}\sum_{\lambda_{j}\leq\lambda}|a_{j}|^{2}

\displaystyle=e^{\lambda T}\|\partial_{t}F(\cdot,0)\|_{L^{2}(M)}^{2}.

In consequence, we deduce the inequality

\forall s\in[0,s_{0}],\,\forall s_{00}>n/2,\,\,\|(1+E(x,D))^{\frac{s}{\nu}}F\|_{L^{\infty}}\lesssim_{s_{0},s_{00}}e^{T\lambda/2}\|\partial_{t}F(\cdot,0)\|_{L^{2}(M)},

(2.26)

as well as the Sobolev estimate

\forall s_{00}>n/2,\,\forall s\in[0,s_{0}+s_{00}],\,\,\,\|(1+E(x,D))^{\frac{s}{\nu}}F\|_{L^{2}}\lesssim_{s_{0},s_{00}}e^{T\lambda/2}\|\partial_{t}F(\cdot,0)\|_{L^{2}(M)}.

(2.27)

With
s_{0}=0,
we have that
\|F\|_{L^{\infty}}\lesssim_{s_{0},s_{00}}e^{T\lambda/2}\|\partial_{t}F(\cdot,0)\|_{L^{2}(M)}.
The analysis above gives us the inequality:

I\lesssim\textnormal{Vol}(M)\times\varepsilon\|\psi(t)\|_{L^{\infty}[T,T+\varepsilon]}e^{T\lambda/2}\|\partial_{t}F(\cdot,0)\|_{L^{2}(M)}=\textnormal{Vol}(M)\times\varepsilon\|\psi(t)\|_{L^{\infty}[T,T+\varepsilon]}e^{T\lambda/2},

(2.28)

where we have used that
\|\partial_{t}F(\cdot,0)\|_{L^{2}(M)}=\|\varkappa\|_{L^{2}(M)}=1.
Summarising

I\lesssim\textnormal{Vol}(M)\times\varepsilon\|\psi(t)\|_{L^{\infty}[T,T+\varepsilon]}e^{T\lambda/2}.

2.2.8.
Estimate for

:

To estimate the second term, we start by observing the inequality

II=\|(-\partial_{t}^{2}+E(x,D)^{\frac{2}{\nu}})^{2}\phi\|_{L^{\infty}(M\times[T,T+\varepsilon))}=\|(-\partial_{t}^{2}+E(x,D)^{\frac{2}{\nu}})^{2}[\psi(t)F(x,T)]\|_{L^{\infty}(M\times[T,T+\varepsilon))}.

Since

\displaystyle(-\partial_{t}^{2}+E(x,D)^{\frac{2}{\nu}})^{2}

\displaystyle[\psi(t)F(x,T)]=(-\partial_{t}^{2}+E(x,D)^{\frac{2}{\nu}})(-\partial_{t}^{2}+E(x,D)^{\frac{2}{\nu}})[\psi(t)F(x,T)]

\displaystyle=(-\partial_{t}^{2}+E(x,D)^{\frac{2}{\nu}})[-\psi_{tt}(t)F(x,T)+\psi(t)E(x,D)^{\frac{2}{\nu}}F(x,T)]

\displaystyle=\psi^{(4)}(t)F(x,T)-2\psi_{tt}(t)E(x,D)^{\frac{2}{\nu}}F(x,T)

\displaystyle\,\,\,\,+\psi(t)E(x,D)^{\frac{4}{\nu}}(F(x,T)),

for
s_{0}\geq 4,
and with
s_{00}>n/2,
the Sobolev inequality in (
2.26
) implies that

\displaystyle\|(-\partial_{t}^{2}+E(x,D)^{\frac{2}{\nu}})^{2}[\psi(t)F(x,T)]\|_{L^{\infty}(M\times[T,T+\varepsilon))}

\displaystyle\leq\|\psi^{(4)}\|_{L^{\infty}[T,T+\varepsilon]}\|F(x,T)\|_{L^{\infty}}

\displaystyle+2\|\psi_{tt}\|_{L^{\infty}[T,T+\varepsilon]}\|E(x,D)^{\frac{2}{\nu}}F(x,T)\|_{L^{\infty}}+\|\psi\|_{{L^{\infty}[T,T+\varepsilon]}}\|E(x,D)^{\frac{4}{\nu}}F(x,T)\|_{L^{\infty}}

\displaystyle\leq\|\psi^{(4)}\|_{L^{\infty}[T,T+\varepsilon]}\|F(x,T)\|_{L^{\infty}}

\displaystyle+2\|\psi_{tt}\|_{L^{\infty}[T,T+\varepsilon]}\|(1+E(x,D))^{\frac{s_{00}+2}{\nu}}F(x,T)\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle+\|\psi\|_{{L^{\infty}[T,T+\varepsilon]}}\|(1+E(x,D))^{\frac{s_{00}+4}{\nu}}F(x,T)\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle=e^{{T}{\lambda}/2}\|\varkappa\|_{L^{2}(M)}(\|\psi^{(4)}\|_{L^{\infty}[T,T+\varepsilon]}+2\|\psi_{tt}\|_{L^{\infty}[T,T+\varepsilon]}+\|\psi\|_{{L^{\infty}[T,T+\varepsilon]}})

\displaystyle=e^{{T}{\lambda}/2}(\|\psi^{(4)}\|_{L^{\infty}[T,T+\varepsilon]}+2\|\psi_{tt}\|_{L^{\infty}[T,T+\varepsilon]}+\|\psi\|_{{L^{\infty}[T,T+\varepsilon]}}).

2.2.9.
Estimate for
Z_{1}

Having proved these estimates for

and

we have that

\displaystyle\|(-\partial_{t}^{2}+E(x,D)^{\frac{2}{\nu}})\phi(x,t)\|_{L^{2}(M_{T+\varepsilon})}^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim\textnormal{Vol}(M)\times\varepsilon\|\psi(t)\|_{L^{\infty}[T,T+\varepsilon]}e^{T\lambda/2}\times e^{{T}{\lambda}/2}(\|\psi^{(4)}\|_{L^{\infty}[T,T+\varepsilon]}+2\|\psi_{tt}\|_{L^{\infty}[T,T+\varepsilon]}+\|\psi\|_{{L^{\infty}[T,T+\varepsilon]}})

\displaystyle=\textnormal{Vol}(M)\times\varepsilon\|\psi(t)\|_{L^{\infty}[T,T+\varepsilon]}e^{T\lambda}(\|\psi^{(4)}\|_{L^{\infty}[T,T+\varepsilon]}+2\|\psi_{tt}\|_{L^{\infty}[T,T+\varepsilon]}+\|\psi\|_{{L^{\infty}[T,T+\varepsilon]}}).

2.2.10.
Final Analysis

The estimates above for
Z_{1}
lead to the following inequality in view of the interpolation inequality

\displaystyle\|\phi\|_{H^{1}(M\times(\alpha,T-\alpha))}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|\phi\|_{H^{1}(M_{T+\varepsilon})}^{\kappa}((\textnormal{Vol}(M)\times\varepsilon\|\psi(t)\|_{L^{\infty}[T,T+\varepsilon]}e^{T\lambda}(\|\psi^{(4)}\|_{L^{\infty}[T,T+\varepsilon]}+2\|\psi_{tt}\|_{L^{\infty}[T,T+\varepsilon]}+\|\psi\|_{{L^{\infty}[T,T+\varepsilon]}}))^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle+|\psi(0)|\|\varkappa\|_{L^{2}(\omega)})^{1-\kappa}.

Now, dividing both sides of this inequality by
|\phi(0)|
and using that
\psi(0)=\psi(T)=\psi(t),

0\leq t\leq T,
we get

\displaystyle\|F(x,t)\|_{H^{1}(M\times(\alpha,T-\alpha))}=\left\|\frac{\psi(t)F(x,t)}{\psi(0)}\right\|_{H^{1}(M\times(\alpha,T-\alpha))}

\displaystyle\lesssim\left\|\frac{\psi(t)F(x,t)}{\psi(0)}\right\|_{H^{1}(M_{T+\varepsilon})}^{\kappa}

\displaystyle\times\frac{1}{|\psi(0)|^{1-\kappa}}((\textnormal{Vol}(M)\times\varepsilon\|\psi(t)\|_{L^{\infty}[T,T+\varepsilon]}e^{T\lambda}(\|\psi^{(4)}\|_{L^{\infty}[T,T+\varepsilon]}+2\|\psi_{tt}\|_{L^{\infty}[T,T+\varepsilon]}+\|\psi\|_{{L^{\infty}[T,T+\varepsilon]}}))^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle+|\psi(0)|\|\varkappa\|_{L^{2}(\omega)})^{1-\kappa}

\displaystyle=\left\|\frac{\psi(t)F(x,t)}{\psi(0)}\right\|_{H^{1}(M_{T+\varepsilon})}^{\kappa}

\displaystyle\times(\left(\textnormal{Vol}(M)\times\varepsilon e^{T\lambda}\frac{\|\psi(t)\|_{L^{\infty}[T,T+\varepsilon]}}{|\psi(0)|}\left(\frac{\|\psi^{(4)}\|_{L^{\infty}[T,T+\varepsilon]}}{\|\psi(0)|}+\frac{2\|\psi_{tt}\|_{L^{\infty}[T,T+\varepsilon]}}{|\psi(0)|}+1\right)\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle+\|\varkappa\|_{L^{2}(\omega)})^{1-\kappa}

\displaystyle=\left\|\frac{\psi(t)F(x,t)}{\psi(T)}\right\|_{H^{1}(M_{T+\varepsilon})}^{\kappa}

\displaystyle\times(\left(\textnormal{Vol}(M)\times\varepsilon e^{T\lambda}\frac{\|\psi(t)\|_{L^{\infty}[T,T+\varepsilon]}}{|\psi(T)|}\left(\frac{\|\psi^{(4)}\|_{L^{\infty}[T,T+\varepsilon]}}{|\psi(T)|}+\frac{2\|\psi_{tt}\|_{L^{\infty}[T,T+\varepsilon]}}{|\psi(T)|}+1\right)\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle+\|\varkappa\|_{L^{2}(\omega)})^{1-\kappa}.

Consequently, the previous analysis leads to the auxiliary inequality

\displaystyle\|F(x,t)\|_{H^{1}(M\times(\alpha,T-\alpha))}\lesssim\left\|\frac{\psi(t)F(x,t)}{\psi(T)}\right\|_{H^{1}(M_{T+\varepsilon})}^{\kappa}

\displaystyle\times(\left(\textnormal{Vol}(M)\times\varepsilon e^{T\lambda}\frac{\|\psi(t)\|_{L^{\infty}[T,T+\varepsilon]}}{|\psi(T)|}\left(\frac{\|\psi^{(4)}\|_{L^{\infty}[T,T+\varepsilon]}}{|\psi(T)|}+\frac{2\|\psi_{tt}\|_{L^{\infty}[T,T+\varepsilon]}}{|\psi(T)|}+1\right)\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle+\|\varkappa\|_{L^{2}(\omega)})^{1-\kappa},

and taking the limit when
\varepsilon\rightarrow 0^{+}
in both sides of this estimate
we conclude the expected inequality,

\|F\|_{H^{1}(M\times(\alpha,T-\alpha))}\leq C_{s_{0},s_{00}}\|F\|_{H^{1}(M_{T})}^{\kappa}\|\varkappa\|_{L^{2}(\omega)}^{1-\kappa},

(2.29)

where we have used that
\psi(t)/\psi(0)=\psi(t)/\psi(T)=1,

0\leq t\leq T,
the smoothness of
\psi,
the fact that (see Lemma
5.1
)

\lim_{\varepsilon\rightarrow 0^{+}}\|\psi^{(4)}\|_{L^{\infty}[T,T+\varepsilon]}=\psi_{tttt}(T)=\lim_{\varepsilon\rightarrow 0^{+}}\|\psi_{tt}\|_{L^{\infty}[T,T+\varepsilon]}=\psi_{tt}(T)=0,

and the properties

•

\lim_{\varepsilon\rightarrow 0^{+}}\left\|{\psi(t)}/{\psi(T)}\right\|_{L^{\infty}[T,T+\varepsilon]}=1,

(2.30)

and

•

\lim_{\varepsilon\rightarrow 0}\left\|{\psi(t)F(x,t)}/{\psi(0)}\right\|_{H^{1}(M_{T+\varepsilon})}=\|F(x,t)\|_{H^{1}(M_{T})}.

(2.31)

For the proof of (
2.31
) note that for
\tilde{\psi}:=\psi(t)/\psi(0),
and using that
F(x,t)=F(x,T)
if
0\leq t\leq T+\varepsilon,
we have

\lim_{\varepsilon\rightarrow 0}\left\|{\psi(t)F(x,t)}/{\psi(0)}\right\|^{2}_{H^{1}(M_{T+\varepsilon})}=\lim_{\varepsilon\rightarrow 0}\sum_{j=0,1}\smallint\limits_{0}^{T+\varepsilon}\|\partial_{t}^{(j)}(\tilde{\psi}(t)F(x,t))\|^{2}_{H^{1}(M)}dt

=\lim_{\varepsilon\rightarrow 0}\sum_{j=0,1}\smallint\limits_{0}^{T}\|\partial_{t}^{(j)}(\tilde{\psi}(t)F(x,t))\|^{2}_{H^{1}(M)}dt+\lim_{\varepsilon\rightarrow 0}\sum_{j=0,1}\smallint\limits_{T}^{T+\varepsilon}\|\partial_{t}^{(j)}(\tilde{\psi}(t)F(x,t))\|^{2}_{H^{1}(M)}dt

=\sum_{j=0,1}\smallint\limits_{0}^{T}\|\partial_{t}^{(j)}(F(x,t))\|^{2}_{H^{1}(M)}dt+\lim_{\varepsilon\rightarrow 0}\sum_{j=0,1}\smallint\limits_{T}^{T+\varepsilon}\|\tilde{\psi}^{(j)}(t)F(x,T)\|^{2}_{H^{1}(M)}dt

=\|F(x,t)\|_{H^{1}(M_{T})}^{2},

where we have used that when
t\rightarrow T,

\tilde{\psi}^{(j)}(t)\rightarrow 0.

Now, from (
2.29
) we can follow the standard Lebeau-Robbiano argument that has been described at the beginning of the section to conclude the proof of the spectral inequality. Having proved (
2.4
), the proof of Proposition
2.1
is complete.
∎

Proof of Theorem
1.4
.

Let
\mu,c>0
be two positive parameters and assume that
0<c<\mu^{\nu}
. Define

\tilde{E}(x,D)=E(x,D)+c.

Observe that the principal symbol
\tilde{E}(x,\xi)
of the operator
\tilde{E}(x,D)
satisfies the lower bound

\tilde{E}(x,\xi)\geq c,\,\,\xi\in T^{*}_{x}M,

and also that
\tilde{E}(x,D)\geq cI.
If
\{\mu_{j}:=\lambda_{j}^{\nu},\rho_{j}\}
are the corresponding spectral data

E(x,D)\rho_{j}=\lambda_{j}^{\nu}\rho_{j},\,\,\lambda_{j}\geq 0,

of
E(x,D),
then
\{\mu_{j}+c:=\lambda_{j}^{\nu}+c,\rho_{j}\}
are the corresponding spectral data

\tilde{E}(x,D)\rho_{j}=(\lambda_{j}^{\nu}+c)\rho_{j},\,\,\lambda_{j}\geq 0

of the operator
\tilde{E}(x,D).
Let
\lambda:=(\mu^{\nu}+c)^{\frac{1}{\nu}}.
From Proposition
2.1
we deduce the spectral inequality

\left(\sum_{(\lambda_{j}^{\nu}+c)^{\frac{1}{\nu}}\leq\lambda}a_{j}^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\leq C_{1}e^{C_{2}{\lambda}}\left\|\sum_{{(\lambda_{j}^{\nu}+c)^{\frac{1}{\nu}}\leq\lambda}}a_{j}\rho_{j}(x)\right\|_{L^{2}(\omega)}.

(2.32)

Note that
(\lambda_{j}^{\nu}+c)^{\frac{1}{\nu}}\leq\lambda
becomes equivalent to the inequality
\lambda_{j}\leq\mu
and since
0<c<\mu^{\nu},
then
\lambda<2\mu.
Thus, we have proved the spectral inequality

\left(\sum_{\lambda_{j}\leq\mu}a_{j}^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\leq C_{1}e^{2C_{2}{\mu}}\left\|\sum_{\lambda_{j}\leq\mu}a_{j}\rho_{j}(x)\right\|_{L^{2}(\omega)}.

(2.33)

In consequence the proof of Theorem
1.4
is complete.
∎

2.3.
Null-controllability for the diffusion model

Now, we present the proof of Theorem
1.2
which we present in the following way.

Theorem 2.3
.

Let
E(x,D)
be a positive and elliptic pseudo-differential operator of order
\nu>0
in the Hörmander class
\Psi^{\nu}_{\rho,\delta}(M).
Let
(x,\xi)\in T^{*}M,
and assume that for any
\xi\neq 0,

E(x,\xi)>0
is strictly positive.

Then, for any
\alpha>1/\nu,
the fractional diffusion model

\begin{cases}u_{t}(x,t)+E(x,D)^{\alpha}u(x,t)=g(x,t)\cdot 1_{\omega}(x),&(x,t)\in M\times(0,T),\\
u(0,x)=u_{0},\end{cases}

(2.34)

is null-controllable at any time
T>0,
that is, there exists an input function
g\in L^{2}(M)
such that for any
x\in M,

u(x,T)=0.

Proof.

The spectral inequality in (
2.4
) allows us to make use of Theorem
4.17
with
A=E(x,D)^{\alpha}
. Indeed, since
A^{\gamma}=E(x,D)^{\alpha\gamma}=E(x,D)^{\frac{1}{\nu}},
satisfies (
2.4
) (that is, the inequality (
4.27
) holds) for
\alpha\gamma=1/\nu.
Because
\gamma\in(0,1)
if an only if
\alpha>1/\nu,
Theorem
4.17
guarantees that this inequality on the fractional order
\alpha
is a sufficient condition in order that (
2.34
) will be null-controllable in time
T>0.
The proof of Theorem
2.3
is complete.
∎

2.4.
The controllability cost for the diffusion model over short times

In the following result we analyse the controllability cost of the model (
2.34
) when the time is small.

Corollary 2.4
.

The controllability cost
C_{T}
for the fractional heat equation (
2.34
) over short times
T\in(0,1)
satisfies

C_{T}\leq C_{1}e^{C_{2}T^{-\beta}},

(2.35)

where
\beta>1/(\alpha\nu-1).

Proof.

For the proof, note that
A^{\gamma}=E(x,D)^{\alpha\gamma}=E(x,D)^{\frac{1}{\nu}},
satisfies (
2.4
) for
\alpha\gamma=1/\nu.
Then, from Theorem
4.17
we have the estimate
C_{T}\leq C_{1}e^{C_{2}T^{-\beta}}
for any
\beta>\gamma/(\gamma-1)=1/(\alpha\nu-1).
The proof of Corollary
2.4
is complete.
∎

3.
Conclusions and some open problems

This work deals with the null-controllability of the fractional heat equation associated to an invertible positive elliptic pseudo-differential operator on a compact Riemannian manifold. Concrete examples are indeed invertible positive elliptic partial differential operators of (necessarily) even order.

In view of the difficulties when working with Carleman estimates for high-order differential operators or even for pseudo-differential operators which in general are non-local operators, we have shown that the theory of pseudo-differential operators can be effectively used. Moreover, this theory was strongly used in deriving the interpolation inequality in (
2.29
), that is

\|F\|_{H^{1}(M\times(\alpha,T-\alpha))}\leq C_{s_{0},s_{00}}\|F\|_{H^{1}(M_{T})}^{\kappa}\|\varkappa\|_{L^{2}(\omega)}^{1-\kappa}.

(3.1)

In conclusion with this work

•

we show that the theory of pseudo-differential operators is an effective microlocal tool for the control theory of parabolic problems, even when we have the lack of Carleman estimates.

The following are some open problems that arise from this investigation.

•

To extend the spectral inequality in (
2.29
) to the case of suitable boundary value problem on compact manifolds with smooth boundary. Just to mention a few, the case of the Laplacian
-\Delta_{g}
with the corresponding Dirichlet boundary conditions was derived in the classical Lebeau-Robianno result in
[
15
]
, and the case of the bi-Laplacian
(-\Delta_{g})^{2}
endowed with the so called
clamped
boundary conditions was analysed in the recent work of Rousseau and Robbiano
[
24
]
. Even, in
[
24
]
the authors leave open the question of the validity of the spectral inequality for the powers
(-\Delta_{g})^{k},

k\geq 3,
when
\partial M\neq 0.

•

The following problem could be of interest in global analysis: to analyse for which kind of manifolds
(M,g)
the condition
\rho\geq 1-\delta
can be removed in Theorem
1.4
. Indeed, the inequality
\rho\geq 1-\delta
is the required condition in order to have the invariance of the pseudo-differential calculus under changes of coordinates. We propose the following conjecture: in the case where
M
is a manifold with symmetries, and under a suitable positivity condition on the symbol of the operator
E(x,D)
, for any
0\leq\delta<\rho\leq 1,
the spectral inequality in Theorem
1.4
is still valid.

Appendix I: Pseudo-differential operators and null-controllability on Hilbert spaces

In this section we present the preliminaries about the theory of pseudo-differential operators used in this work as well as some results on the null-controllability for the Cauchy problem on Hilbert spaces. We use the following notation.

•

For any pair
(X,Y)
of Hilbert spaces, we denote by
\mathscr{B}(X,Y)
the family of bounded and linear operators
T:X\rightarrow Y.

•

The spectrum of a densely defined linear operator
A:\textnormal{Dom}(A)\subset{X}\rightarrow X
will be denoted by
\sigma(A).

4.1.
Hörmander classes of pseudo-differential operators on compact manifolds

In this subsection we present the construction of the classes of pseudo-differential operators on compact manifolds by using local coordinate systems (see Hörmander
[
12
]
and e.g. M. Taylor
[
27
]
for an introductory presentation).

Let us briefly introduce these classes starting with the definition in the Euclidean setting.

Definition 4.1

(Symbol classes)
.

Let
U
be an open subset of
\mathbb{R}^{n}.
We say that the
symbol

a\in C^{\infty}(U\times\mathbb{R}^{n},\mathbb{C})
belongs to the Hörmander class of order
m
and of
(\rho,\delta)
-type,
S^{m}_{\rho,\delta}(U\times\mathbb{R}^{n}),

0\leqslant\rho,\delta\leqslant 1,
if for every compact subset
K\subset U
and for all
\alpha,\beta\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{n}
, the symbol inequalities

|\partial_{x}^{\beta}\partial_{\xi}^{\alpha}a(x,\xi)|\leqslant C_{\alpha,\beta,K}(1+|\xi|)^{m-\rho|\alpha|+\delta|\beta|},

(4.1)

hold true uniformly in
x\in K
for all
\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{n}.

Then, a continuous linear operator
A:C^{\infty}_{0}(U)\rightarrow C^{\infty}(U)

is a pseudo-differential operator of order
m
of
(\rho,\delta)
-type, if there exists
a symbol
a\in S^{m}_{\rho,\delta}(U\times\mathbb{R}^{n})
such that

Af(x)=\smallint\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}e^{2\pi ix\cdot\xi}a(x,\xi)(\mathscr{F}_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}{f})(\xi)d\xi,

for all
f\in C^{\infty}_{0}(U),
where

(\mathscr{F}_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}{f})(\xi):=\smallint\limits_{U}e^{-i2\pi x\cdot\xi}f(x)dx

is the Euclidean Fourier transform of
f
at
\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{n}.
In this case we denote the class of pseudo-differential operators with symbols in the family
S^{m}_{\rho,\delta}(U\times\mathbb{R}^{n})
as
\Psi^{m}_{\rho,\delta}(U).

Once the definition of Hörmander classes on open subsets of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
is given, it can be extended to smooth manifolds as follows.

Remark 4.2

(Pseudo-differential operators on compact manifolds)
.

Given a compact manifold without boundary
M,
a linear continuous operator
A:C^{\infty}_{0}(M)\rightarrow C^{\infty}(M)
is a pseudo-differential operator of order
m
of
(\rho,\delta)
-type, with
\rho\geqslant 1-\delta,
and
0\leq\delta<\rho\leq 1,
if for every local coordinate patch
\omega:M_{\omega}\subset M\rightarrow U_{\omega}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n},

and for every
\phi,\psi\in C^{\infty}_{0}(U_{\omega}),
the operator

Tu:=\psi(\omega^{-1})^{*}A\omega^{*}(\phi u),\,\,u\in C^{\infty}(U_{\omega}),

is a standard pseudo-differential operator with symbol
a_{T}\in S^{m}_{\rho,\delta}(U_{\omega}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}).
As usually,
\omega^{*}
and
(\omega^{-1})^{*}
are the pullbacks, induced by the maps
\omega
and
\omega^{-1}
respectively. In this case we write
A\in\Psi^{m}_{\rho,\delta}(M).

Remark 4.3

(The principal symbol)
.

The symbol of a pseudo-differential operator
A
is unique as an element in
\Psi^{m}_{\rho,\delta}(M)/\Psi^{m}_{\rho,\delta}(M).
To this element we will call the
principal symbol of

A.
We denote it by

a_{m}(x,\xi),\,\,(x,\xi)\in T^{*}M.

(4.2)

The main feature of the principal symbol of a pseudo-differential operator is that it remains invariant under coordinates changes.

In the next theorem we describe some fundamental properties of the Hörmander calculus
[
12
]
.

Theorem 4.4
.

Let
0\leqslant\delta<\rho\leqslant 1,
be such that
\rho\geq 1-\delta.
Then
\Psi^{\infty}_{\rho,\delta}(M):=\cup_{m\in\mathbb{R}}\Psi^{m}_{\rho,\delta}(M)
is an algebra of operators stable under compositions and adjoints, that is:

-

the mapping
A\mapsto A^{*}:\Psi^{m}_{\rho,\delta}(M)\rightarrow\Psi^{m}_{\rho,\delta}(M)
is a continuous linear mapping between Fréchet spaces.

-

The mapping
(A_{1},A_{2})\mapsto A_{1}\circ A_{2}:\Psi^{m_{1}}_{\rho,\delta}(M)\times\Psi^{m_{2}}_{\rho,\delta}(M)\rightarrow\Psi^{m_{1}+m_{2}}_{\rho,\delta}(M)
is a continuous bilinear mapping between Fréchet spaces.

Moreover, any operator in the class
\Psi^{0}_{\rho,\delta}(M)
admits a bounded extension from
L^{2}(G)
to
L^{2}(G).

Remark 4.5
.

The
L^{2}
-boundedness result in Theorem
4.4
is the microlocalised version of the Calderón-Vaillancourt theorem, see
[
7
]
. Moreover, if
A\in\Psi^{0}_{\rho,\delta}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
is such that
0\leq\delta\leq\rho\leq 1,

\rho\neq 1,
then

\|A\|_{\mathscr{B}(L^{2})}\lesssim\sup_{|\alpha|+|\beta|\leq[\frac{n}{2}]+1}C_{\alpha,\beta},

(4.3)

where

C_{\alpha,\beta}:=\sup_{(x,\xi)\in\mathbb{R}^{2n}}(1+|\xi|)^{\rho|\alpha|-\delta|\beta|}|\partial_{x}^{\beta}\partial_{\xi}^{\alpha}a(x,\xi)|.

Remark 4.6
.

If
U
is an open subset of a close manifold
M
of dimension
n,
and
f\in C^{\infty}_{0}(U),
then, by microlocalising
A\in\Psi^{0}_{\rho,\delta}(M)
when
0\leq\delta<\rho\leq 1,
and
\rho\geq 1-\delta,
one has

\|Af\|_{L^{2}}\lesssim_{U}\sup_{|\alpha|+|\beta|\leq[\frac{n}{2}]+1}C_{\alpha,\beta,U}\|f\|_{L^{2}(M)},

(4.4)

where

C_{\alpha,\beta,U}:=\sup_{(x,\xi)\in U\times\mathbb{R}^{n}}(1+|\xi|)^{\rho|\alpha|-\delta|\beta|}|\partial_{x}^{\beta}\partial_{\xi}^{\alpha}a(x,\xi)|,

by making the identification of
U
with an open subset of
\mathbb{R}^{n}.

In this work we will use the functional calculus for elliptic pseudo-differential operators on
M.
We record that a pseudo-differential operator
A\in\Psi^{m}_{\rho,\delta}(M)
is elliptic if in any local coordinate system
U
, there exists
R>0,
such that its symbol satisfies uniformly on any compact subset
K\subset{U}
the growth estimate

C_{1}(1+|\xi|)^{m}\leq|a(x,\xi)|\leq C_{2}(1+|\xi|)^{m},\,|\xi|\geq R,

(4.5)

uniformly in
x\in K,
and
\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{n}.
The ellipticity condition (
4.5
) is not enough for constructing the complex powers of an elliptic operator. We present such a construction in the following sub-section. However, one of the main aspects of the spectral theory of an elliptic operator is that it spectrum is purely discrete
[
12
]
.

4.2.
Complex powers of an elliptic pseudo-differential operator

In this subsection to a complex sector

\Lambda\subset\mathbb{C},

we will associate a class class of elliptic operators

(4.7)

Remark 4.8

Theorem 4.9

(Complex powers of elliptic operators)

F(x,t,D,\partial_{t})
is a bounded operator on
L^{2}(M\times\mathbb{T}_{T,\varepsilon}).
Note that in any local coordinate system
U\subset M,
where
U
then, is diffeomorphic to an open subset
\tilde{U}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n},
the operator
has the form

F(x,t,D,\partial_{t})u(t,x)=\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}}\smallint\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}e^{\frac{i2\pi(t,k)}{2(T+\varepsilon)}+2\pi(x,\xi)}\sigma(t,x,k,\xi)\widehat{u}(k,\xi)d\xi,

(7.1)

where, for any
u\in C^{\infty}_{0}(\mathbb{T}_{T,\varepsilon}\times\tilde{U}),
the Fourier transform of
u
is defined by

\widehat{u}(k,\xi)=\smallint\limits_{\mathbb{T}_{T,\varepsilon}}\smallint\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}e^{-\frac{i2\pi(t,k)}{2(T+\varepsilon)}-2\pi(x,\xi)}{u}(t,x)dtdx,\,k\in\mathbb{Z},\,\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{n}.

(7.2)

For the toroidal variable, we have used the toroidal Ruzhansky-Turunen calculus, see Subsection
4.3
or
[
25
]
for details. From now,
\Delta_{k}
denotes the difference operator on a lattice.
Note that the principal symbol of the operator
F(x,t,D,\partial_{t})
viewed on
\tilde{U}
is given by

\sigma_{0}(t,x,k,\xi)=\frac{\left(\frac{\pi k}{T+\varepsilon}\right)^{2}+4\pi^{2}|\xi|^{2}}{\left(\frac{\pi k}{T+\varepsilon}\right)^{2}+E(x,\xi)^{\frac{2}{\nu}}},\,\,|\xi|\geq 1,

(7.3)

where
E(x,\xi)^{\frac{2}{\nu}}
denotes the symbol of
E(x,D)^{\frac{2}{\nu}},
when microlocalised to the open
\tilde{U}.
Note that the ellipticity of
E(x,D)^{\frac{2}{\nu}},
that is

C_{1}|\xi|^{2}\leq E(x,\xi)^{\frac{2}{\nu}}\leq C_{2}|\xi|^{2},\,|\xi|\geq 1,

and the positivity condition
E(x,\xi)\geq c,
implies that the symbol
\sigma_{0}
satisfies the estimates

\tilde{C}_{1}\leq|\sigma_{0}(t,x,k,\xi)|\leq\tilde{C}_{2},

with
C_{1}
and
C_{2}
independent of
\varepsilon\in(0,1).

Moreover, a detailed analysis of the derivatives of
\sigma_{0},
shows that the symbol inequalities

|\partial_{x,t}^{\beta}\partial_{\xi}^{\alpha}\Delta_{k}^{\gamma}\sigma_{0}(t,x,k,\xi)|\leqslant C_{\alpha,\beta,\gamma,K}(1+|k|+|\xi|)^{-|\alpha|-|\gamma|+\delta|\beta|},

hold true uniformly in
\varepsilon\in(0,1),

x\in K
for all
\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{n},

where
\Delta_{k}
stands for the standard difference operator on
\mathbb{Z}.
Since the Calderón-Vaillancourt estimates the
L^{2}
-boundendess of
F
when it is microlocalized to
\tilde{U},
in terms of the constants
C_{\alpha,\beta,\gamma,K}
and of
\tilde{C}_{2},
(see Remark
4.6
) that is, for any
u\in C^{\infty}_{0}(\mathbb{T}_{T,\varepsilon}\times\tilde{U}),

\|F(x,t,D,\partial_{t})u\|_{L^{2}}\leq\left(\sup_{|\alpha|+|\beta|+|\gamma|\leq[\frac{n+1}{2}]+1}{\{C_{\alpha,\beta,\gamma,K},\tilde{C_{2}}\}}\right)\|u\|_{L^{2}}.

(7.4)

Since
M
is a compact manifold, the estimate in (
7.4
) can be extended from local coordinate systems of
M
to the whole manifold. We have proved the following lemma.

Lemma 7.1
.

Let
0<\varepsilon<1,
and let us consider the operator norm

C_{\varepsilon}=\|(-\partial_{t}^{2}-\Delta_{g})E(t,x,D,\partial_{t})^{-1}\|_{\mathscr{B}(L^{2}(M\times\mathbb{T}_{T,\varepsilon}))}.

Then

C:=\sup_{0<\varepsilon<1}C_{\varepsilon}<\infty.

(7.5)

Moreover, there is a finite family of compact subsets
K_{1},K_{2},\cdots,K_{\ell}
covering
M,
and such that

C\lesssim\left(\max_{1\leq i\leq\ell}\sup_{|\alpha|+|\beta|+|\gamma|\leq[\frac{n+1}{2}]+1}{\{C_{\alpha,\beta,\gamma,K_{i}},\tilde{C_{2}}\}}\right).

(7.6)

Proof.

Let
U_{i}
be an atlas on
M
in such a way that
M
can be covered by a finite family of compact subsets
K_{i}\subset U_{i}.
Using a partition of the unity
\phi_{i}
subordinated to the
U_{i},
’s with
\textnormal{supp}(\psi_{i})\subset K_{i},
we can
microlocalise the operator
F(x,t,D,\partial_{t})
on any
K_{i}.
Using (
7.4
), we have that

C_{\varepsilon}=\|(-\partial_{t}^{2}-\Delta_{g})E(t,x,D,\partial_{t})^{-1}\|_{\mathscr{B}(L^{2}(M\times\mathbb{T}_{T,\varepsilon}))}\lesssim\sum_{i=1}^{\ell}\left(\sup_{|\alpha|+|\beta|+|\gamma|\leq[\frac{n+1}{2}]+1}{\{C_{\alpha,\beta,\gamma,K_{i}},\tilde{C_{2}}\}}\right)

\lesssim\ell\left(\max_{1\leq i\leq\ell}\sup_{|\alpha|+|\beta|+|\gamma|\leq[\frac{n+1}{2}]+1}{\{C_{\alpha,\beta,\gamma,K_{i}},\tilde{C_{2}}\}}\right).

Thus, the proof of Lemma
7.1
is complete.
∎

8.
Proof of the Donnelly-Fefferman type inequality for pseudo-differential operators

Proof of Theorem
1.5
.

For the proof of (
1.5
) we can use (
1.3
) and the Sobolev embedding theorem. Indeed, let
R>0
and let us consider
s\in\mathbb{R}
such that
s>n/2.
With
\omega=B(x,R)
a ball of radius
R>0
we have that

\|\varkappa\|_{L^{\infty}(B(x,2R))}\leq\|\varkappa\|_{L^{\infty}(G)}.

(8.1)

Now, the Sobolev embedding theorem and the inequality in (
1.3
) imply that

\displaystyle\|\varkappa\|_{L^{\infty}(G)}\lesssim\|(1+E(x,D))^{\frac{s}{\nu}}\varkappa\|_{L^{2}(G)}\lesssim(1+\lambda)^{s}\|\varkappa\|_{L^{2}(G)}

\lesssim(1+\lambda)^{s}C_{1,R}e^{C_{2,R}\lambda}\|\varkappa\|_{L^{2}(B(x,R))}.

By using (
8.1
) we conclude this analysis with the inequality

\displaystyle\|\varkappa\|_{L^{\infty}(B(x,2R))}\lesssim\|\varkappa\|_{L^{\infty}(G)}\leq e^{C_{2,R}^{\prime}+C_{1,R}^{\prime}\lambda}\|\varkappa\|_{L^{2}(B(x,2R))}\leq e^{C_{2,R}^{\prime}+C_{1,R}^{\prime}\lambda}\|\varkappa\|_{L^{\infty}(B(x,2R))},

for some
C_{1,R}^{\prime}>C_{1,R}
and
C_{2,R}^{\prime}>C_{2,R}.
The proof of Theorem
1.2
is complete.

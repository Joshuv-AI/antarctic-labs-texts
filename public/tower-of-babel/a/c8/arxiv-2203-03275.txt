Arbitrarily high-order energy-conserving methods for Hamiltonian problems with quadratic holonomic constraints
P. Amodio, L. Brugnano, G. Frasca-Caccia, F. Iavernaro

Abstract
In this paper, we define arbitrarily high-order energy-conserving methods for Hamiltonian systems with quadratic holonomic constraints. The derivation of the methods is made within the so-called line integral framework . Numerical tests to illustrate the theoretical findings are presented. Keywords: constrained Hamiltonian systems; quadratic holonomic constraints; energy-conserving methods; line integral methods; Hamiltonian Boundary Value Methods; HBVMs. MSC: 65P10, 65L80, 65L06.

Motivated by this fact, we here investigate the numerical approximation of a constrained Hamiltonian dynamics, described by the separable Hamiltonian

H(q,p)=\frac{1}{2}p^{\top}M^{-1}p-U(q),\qquad q,p\in\mathbb{R}^{m},

(1)

where
M
is a symmetric and positive-definite (spd) matrix, subject to
\nu
quadratic
holonomic
constraints,

g(q)=0\in\mathbb{R}^{\nu},\qquad\nu\leq m,

(2)

i.e., the entries of
g
are quadratic polynomials. Hereafter, we shall assume all points be regular for the constraints, i.e.,
\nabla g(q)\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times\nu}
has full column rank or, equivalently,

\nabla g(q)^{\top}M^{-1}\nabla g(q)\in\mathbb{R}^{\nu\times\nu}\qquad\mbox{is~spd}.

(3)

Moreover, we shall assume that its smallest eigenvalue is bounded away from 0, in the domain of interest. Also,
for sake of simplicity, in the same domain the potential
U
will be assumed to be analytic.

It is well-known that the problem defined by (
1
)–(
2
) can be cast in Hamiltonian form by defining the augmented Hamiltonian

\hat{H}(q,p,\lambda)=H(q,p)+\lambda^{\top}g(q),

(4)

where
\lambda
is the vector of the Lagrange multipliers. The resulting constrained Hamiltonian system reads:

\dot{q}=M^{-1}p,\qquad\dot{p}=\nabla U(q)-\nabla g(q)\lambda,\qquad g(q)=0,\qquad t\in[0,T],

(5)

and is subject to consistent initial conditions,

q(0)=q_{0},\qquad p(0)=p_{0},

(6)

such that

g(q_{0})=0,\qquad\nabla g(q_{0})^{\top}M^{-1}p_{0}=0.

(7)

Clearly,
H(q,p)\equiv\hat{H}(q,p,\lambda)
, provided that the constraints (
2
) are satisfied, and a straightforward calculation proves that both are conserved along the solution trajectory.

We notice that the condition
g(q_{0})=0
ensures that
q_{0}
belongs to the manifold

{\cal M}=\left\{q\in\mathbb{R}^{m}:g(q)=0\right\},

(8)

as required by the constraints, whereas the condition
\nabla g(q_{0})^{\top}M^{-1}p_{0}
means that the motion initially stays on the tangent space to
{\cal M}
at
q_{0}
. This condition is satisfied by all points on the solution trajectory, since, in order for the constraints to be conserved, the following condition needs to be satisfied as well:

\dot{g}(q)=\nabla g(q)^{\top}\dot{q}=\nabla g(q)^{\top}M^{-1}p=0\in\mathbb{R}^{\nu}.

(9)

These latter constraints are usually referred to as
hidden constraints
, and allow the derivation of the vector of the Lagrange multiplier
\lambda
. In fact, from (
9
) and (
5
)-(
6
), one obtains

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\nabla g(q(t))^{\top}M^{-1}p(t)

(10)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\nabla g(q(t))^{\top}M^{-1}\left[p_{0}+\int_{0}^{t}\nabla U(q(\zeta))\mathrm{d}\zeta-\int_{0}^{t}\nabla g(q(\zeta))\lambda(\zeta)\mathrm{d}\zeta\right],

from which one derives the integral equation:

\displaystyle\nabla g(q(t))^{\top}M^{-1}\int_{0}^{t}\nabla g(q(\zeta))\lambda(\zeta)\mathrm{d}\zeta

(11)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\nabla g(q(t))^{\top}M^{-1}\left[p_{0}+\int_{0}^{t}\nabla U(q(\zeta))\mathrm{d}\zeta\right].

Remark 1

From (
10
)-(
11
), one deduces that the vector of the Lagrange multipliers depends on the functions
q
and
p
. We shall denote this by using the notation:

\lambda(t):=\lambda(q(t),p(t)).

(12)

We stress that the condition (
9
) can be conveniently relaxed for a numerical approximation: in fact, we only ask for
\nabla g(q)^{\top}M^{-1}p
to be suitably small along the numerical solution. Consequently, when solving the problem on the interval
[0,h]
, we require that the approximations,

q_{1}\approx q(h),\qquad p_{1}\approx p(h),

(13)

satisfy the conservation of both the Hamiltonian and the constraints,

H(q_{1},p_{1})=H(q_{0},p_{0}),\qquad g(q_{1})=0,

(14)

whereas the hidden constraints, see (
9
), become:

\nabla g(q(h))^{\top}M^{-1}p(h)=O(h^{\bar{r}}),

(15)

for a convenient
\bar{r}\geq 1

For sake of completeness, we recall that a formal expression for the vector
\lambda
is obtained by further differentiating (
9

\ddot{g}(q)=\nabla^{2}g(q)(M^{-1}p,M^{-1}p)+\nabla g(q)^{\top}M^{-1}\left[\nabla U(q)-\nabla g(q)\lambda\right].

(16)

In fact, by imposing the vanishing of this derivative yields

\left[\nabla g(q)^{\top}M^{-1}\nabla g(q)\right]\lambda~=~\nabla^{2}g(q)(M^{-1}p,M^{-1}p)+\nabla g(q)^{\top}M^{-1}\nabla U(q).

(17)

Consequently, the following result follows.

Theorem 1

The vector
\lambda
exists and is uniquely determined, provided that (
3

In fact, in such a case, from (
17

\lambda=\left[\nabla g(q)^{\top}M^{-1}\nabla g(q)\right]^{-1}\left[\nabla^{2}g(q)(M^{-1}p,M^{-1}p)+\nabla g(q)^{\top}M^{-1}\nabla U(q)\right].

2.1
Constraints conservation

Let us now study the conservation of the holonomic constraints in (
14
), i.e.
under which conditions
g(q_{1})=0
. One has, by virtue of (

\displaystyle g(q_{1})

\displaystyle\equiv

\displaystyle g(u(h))~=~h\int_{0}^{1}\nabla g(u(ch))^{\top}\dot{u}(ch)\mathrm{d}c

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle h\sum_{i=1}^{s}b_{i}\nabla g(u(c_{i}h))^{\top}\dot{u}(c_{i}h)~=~h\sum_{i=1}^{s}b_{i}\nabla g(u(c_{i}h))^{\top}M^{-1}v(c_{i}h),\qquad\quad

where the third equality follows from the fact that the integrand has degree

Consequently, the following result is proved.

Theorem 2

g(q_{1})=0
, provided that:

\nabla g(u(c_{i}h))^{\top}M^{-1}v(c_{i}h)=0\in\mathbb{R}^{\nu},\qquad i=1,\dots,s.

(26)

2.2
Energy conservation

Let us now study the conservation of the Hamiltonian function (
1
). By using the usual line integral argument, from (
21
) one has:

\displaystyle H(q_{1},p_{1})-H(q_{0},p_{0})~\equiv~H(u(h),v(h))-H(u(0),v(0))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{h}\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}H(u(t),v(t))\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle h\int_{0}^{1}\left[H_{q}(u(ch),v(ch))^{\top}\dot{u}(ch)+H_{p}(u(ch),v(ch))^{\top}\dot{v}(ch)\right]\mathrm{d}c

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle h\int_{0}^{1}\left[-\nabla U(u(ch))^{\top}M^{-1}\sum_{j=0}^{s-1}P_{j}(c)\gamma_{j}(v)+v(ch)^{\top}M^{-1}\sum_{j=0}^{s-1}P_{j}(c)\psi_{j}(u)\right.

\displaystyle\left.\qquad~~-~v(ch)^{\top}M^{-1}\sum_{j=1}^{s}\ell_{j}(c)\rho_{j}\right]\mathrm{d}c

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-~h\sum_{j=0}^{s-1}\overbrace{\left[\int_{0}^{1}P_{j}(c)\nabla U(u(ch))\mathrm{d}c\right]^{\top}}^{=\psi_{j}(u)^{\top}}M^{-1}\gamma_{j}(v)

\displaystyle+~h\sum_{j=0}^{s-1}\overbrace{\left[\int_{0}^{1}P_{j}(c)v(ch)\mathrm{d}c\right]^{\top}}^{=\gamma_{j}(v)^{\top}}M^{-1}\psi_{j}(u)-h\int_{0}^{1}\sum_{j=1}^{s}v(ch)^{\top}M^{-1}\ell_{j}(c)\rho_{j}\mathrm{d}c

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-h\sum_{j=0}^{s-1}\psi_{j}(u)^{\top}M^{-1}\gamma_{j}(v)+h\sum_{j=0}^{s-1}\gamma_{j}(v)^{\top}M^{-1}\psi_{j}(u)-h\sum_{i=1}^{s}b_{i}v(c_{i}h)^{\top}M^{-1}\rho_{i}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-h\sum_{i=1}^{s}b_{i}v(c_{i}h)^{\top}M^{-1}\nabla g(u(c_{i}h))\lambda_{i}.

In fact, considering that the Gauss-Legendre formula has order
2s
,

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}\sum_{j=1}^{s}v(ch)^{\top}M^{-1}\ell_{j}(c)\rho_{j}\mathrm{d}c~=~\sum_{i,j=1}^{s}b_{i}v(c_{i}h)^{\top}M^{-1}\ell_{j}(c_{i})\rho_{j}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{s}b_{i}v(c_{i}h)^{\top}M^{-1}\rho_{i}~=~\sum_{i=1}^{s}b_{i}v(c_{i}h)^{\top}M^{-1}\nabla g(u(c_{i}h))\lambda_{i}.

Consequently, the following result is proved.

Theorem 3

Assume that the conditions (
26
) hold true. Then,
H(q_{1},p_{1})=H(q_{0},p_{0})
, i.e., the energy is conserved.

\gamma_{s-1}(v)\end{array}\right)\in\mathbb{R}^{sm},

\displaystyle{\bm{\zeta}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\begin{array}[]{c}\zeta_{0}(u,{\bm{\lambda}})\\
\vdots\\
\zeta_{s-1}(u,{\bm{\lambda}})\end{array}\right)\in\mathbb{R}^{sm},\qquad\hat{\bm{e}}\,=\,\left(\begin{array}[]{c}1\\
\vdots\\
1\end{array}\right)\in\mathbb{R}^{k}.

One then obtains, by also taking into account (
53
), the following discrete problem:

\displaystyle\nabla\bm{g}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\nabla g({\bm{e}}\otimes q_{0}+h{\cal I}_{s}\otimes M^{-1}{\bm{\gamma}})

\displaystyle{\bm{\lambda}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\nabla\bm{g}^{\top}\,h{\cal I}_{s}{\cal P}_{s}^{\top}\Omega\otimes M^{-1}\,\nabla\bm{g}\right)^{-1}\nabla\bm{g}^{\top}\left[{\bm{e}}\otimes M^{-1}p_{0}+h{\cal I}_{s}\otimes M^{-1}\hat{\bm{\psi}}\right]

\displaystyle{\bm{\zeta}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\cal P}_{s}^{\top}\Omega\otimes I_{m}(\nabla\bm{g}\cdot{\bm{\lambda}})

(111)

\displaystyle{\bm{\gamma}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\cal P}_{s}^{\top}\Omega\otimes I_{m}\left[{\bm{e}}\otimes p_{0}+h{\cal I}_{s}\otimes I_{m}(\hat{\bm{\psi}}-{\bm{\zeta}})\right]

\displaystyle\hat{\bm{\psi}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\hat{\cal P}_{s}^{\top}\hat{\Omega}\otimes I_{m}\,\nabla U(\hat{\bm{e}}\otimes q_{0}+h\hat{\cal I}_{s}\otimes M^{-1}{\bm{\gamma}})

where, considering that

\displaystyle{\bm{e}}\otimes q_{0}+h{\cal I}_{s}\otimes M^{-1}{\bm{\gamma}}

\displaystyle\equiv

\displaystyle\left(\begin{array}[]{c}u(c_{1}h)\\
\vdots\\
u(c_{s}h)\end{array}\right),

\displaystyle\hat{\bm{e}}\otimes q_{0}+h\hat{\cal I}_{s}\otimes M^{-1}{\bm{\gamma}}

\displaystyle\equiv

\displaystyle\left(\begin{array}[]{c}u(\hat{c}_{1}h)\\
\vdots\\
u(\hat{c}_{k}h)\end{array}\right),

\displaystyle{\bm{e}}\otimes p_{0}+h{\cal I}_{s}\otimes I_{m}(\hat{\bm{\psi}}-{\bm{\zeta}})

\displaystyle\equiv

\displaystyle\left(\begin{array}[]{c}v(c_{1}h)\\
\vdots\\
v(c_{s}h)\end{array}\right),

it is meant that:

\nabla U(\hat{\bm{e}}\otimes q_{0}+h\hat{\cal I}_{s}\otimes M^{-1}{\bm{\gamma}})=\left(\begin{array}[]{c}\nabla U(u(\hat{c}_{1}h))\\
\vdots\\
\nabla U(u(\hat{c}_{k}h))\end{array}\right)\in\mathbb{R}^{km},

and, similarly,

\nabla g({\bm{e}}\otimes q_{0}+h{\cal I}_{s}\otimes M^{-1}{\bm{\gamma}})
computes the
sm\times s\nu
matrix
\nabla\bm{g}
defined in (
2.3
).

Remark 4

In the case where the holonomic constraints are not present, i.e.
{\bm{\zeta}}={\bm{0}}
in (
111
), the first three equations are void. Moreover,
the last two equations, combined together, provide the discrete problem of a HBVM
(k,s)
method applied to the corresponding unconstrained problem.
For this reason, we shall continue to refer to (
111
) as a HBVM
(k,s)
method applied for solving the constrained Hamiltonian problem (
5
).

We conclude this section by observing that the discrete problem (
111
), which has to be solved within machine accuracy, in order to gain the conservation properties, can be numerically solved by suitably modifying the Newton-type iterations studied in
[
11
,
5
]
(see also
[
14
,
15
]
). However, for carrying out the numerical tests of Section
5
, we shall use the following straightforward fixed-point iteration,

\displaystyle\nabla\bm{g}^{\ell}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\nabla g({\bm{e}}\otimes q_{0}+h{\cal I}_{s}\otimes M^{-1}{\bm{\gamma}}^{\ell})

\displaystyle{\bm{\lambda}}^{\ell}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left((\nabla\bm{g}^{\ell})^{\top}\,h{\cal I}_{s}{\cal P}_{s}^{\top}\Omega\otimes M^{-1}\,\nabla\bm{g}^{\ell}\right)^{-1}

\displaystyle\qquad(\nabla\bm{g}^{\ell})^{\top}\left[{\bm{e}}\otimes M^{-1}p_{0}+h{\cal I}_{s}\otimes M^{-1}\hat{\bm{\psi}}^{\ell}\right]

\displaystyle{\bm{\zeta}}^{\ell}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\cal P}_{s}^{\top}\Omega\otimes I_{m}(\nabla\bm{g}^{\ell}\cdot{\bm{\lambda}}^{\ell})

(115)

\displaystyle{\bm{\gamma}}^{\ell+1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\cal P}_{s}^{\top}\Omega\otimes I_{m}\left[{\bm{e}}\otimes p_{0}+h{\cal I}_{s}\otimes I_{m}(\hat{\bm{\psi}}^{\ell}-{\bm{\zeta}}^{\ell})\right]

\displaystyle\hat{\bm{\psi}}^{\ell+1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\hat{\cal P}_{s}^{\top}\hat{\Omega}\otimes I_{m}\,\nabla U(\hat{\bm{e}}\otimes q_{0}+h\hat{\cal I}_{s}\otimes M^{-1}{\bm{\gamma}}^{\ell}),\qquad\quad\ell=0,1,\dots,

which can be conveniently started from

{\bm{\gamma}}^{0}=\hat{\bm{\psi}}^{0}={\bm{0}}\in\mathbb{R}^{sm}.

Assuming a continuous Lipschitz condition for
\nabla U
, and recalling that the constraints are quadratic, the convergence of the previous iteration can be seen to be granted for all sufficiently small timesteps
h
.
Once (
115
) has been iterated until convergence, the new approximations are given by:

q_{1}=q_{0}+h\gamma_{0},\qquad p_{1}=p_{0}+h(\hat{\psi}_{0}-\zeta_{0}),

(116)

where we have removed the arguments of the Fourier coefficients, in order to have a more algorithmic description. An approximation of the vector of the Lagrange multipliers can be similarly obtained as

\bar{\lambda}=\sum_{i=1}^{s}\ell_{i}(1)\lambda_{i},

(117)

\lambda_{1},\dots,\lambda_{s}
being, according to (
2.3
), the (block) entries of the vector
{\bm{\lambda}}
at convergence.

6
Conclusions

In this paper we have introduced a class of energy-conserving methods for Hamiltonian problems with quadratic holonomic constraints. The methods reduce to the class of Hamiltonian Boundary Value Methods (HBVMs), when the constraints are not given. Arbitrarily high-order methods are obtained within this class. A detailed analysis of the methods has been presented, along with their practical implementation and solution of the generated discrete problems. A number of numerical tests duly confirm the theoretical results, also proving the usefulness of the approach.
Future directions of investigation will include the extension of the methods for numerically solving Hamiltonian problems with general holonomic constraints.

Gauge transformations and Galilean covariance in nonlinear gauge-coupled quantum fluids Preprint: APS/123-QED
Yvan Buggy, Patrik Öhberg

Abstract
We investigate certain invariance properties of quantum fluids subject to a nonlinear gauge potential. In particular, we derive the covariant transformation laws for the nonlinear potentials under a space-time Galilean boost and consider U ⁡ ( 1 ) U\left(1\right) gauge transformations. We find that the hydrodynamic canonical field equations are form-invariant in the case of external gauge functions χ ⁡ ( 𝐫 , t ) \chi(\mathbf{r},t) , but not for nonlinear gauge functionals χ ⁡ [ ρ ] \chi[\rho] . Hence, nonlinear gauge potentials are non-trivial potentials which may not be ”gauged-away”. Notably, for a superfluid in dimension d = 1 d=1 , attempting to do so generates the gauge-pressure of the fluid in the Hamiltonian density. Further, we investigate how the field equations transform under arbitrary Galilean transformations. We find that the immediate lack of Galilean covariance is restored under a suitably chosen transformation rule set for the potentials, which is identical in form to that of a Schrödinger particle coupled to external scalar and vector potentials.



I
Introduction

The pursuit for implementing artificial electromagnetic potentials for charge neutral systems has received a steady stream of interest over the last two decades.
These potentials are generally engineered through combined-interactions, such that a system exhibits spatially varying local eigenstates, which, in turn, impart a geometric phase onto the wavefunction
Dalibard et al. 2011
;
Goldman et al. 2014
;
Berry 1984
;
Peskin and Tonomura 1989
.
Although initial implementations involved static synthetic fields, further proposals have since been put forward for creating ”dynamical” gauge fields which are described by their own Hamiltonian and not merely imposed on the system
Kapit and Mueller 2011
;
Wiese 2013
;
Banerjee et al. 2012
;
Tagliacozzo et al. 2013a
;
Zohar et al. 2013
;
Tagliacozzo et al. 2013b
.
One possibility for realising a dynamical gauge potential, albeit not a field in the strict sense, is to introduce a ”back-action” where the dynamics of the gauge potential are tied to the motion of the condensate.
Such a prospect has been proposed in
Edmonds et al. 2013a
;
Edmonds et al. 2013b
;
Keilmann et al. 2011
;
Greschner et al. 2014
;
Cardarelli et al. 2016
;
Greschner and Santos 2015
;
Sträter et al. 2016
, where the effective field depends on the spatial configuration of the atoms, in particular, the atomic density.
More recently, a density-dependent gauge potential has been experimentally demonstrated in a two-dimensional optical lattice, by modulating the interaction strength in synchrony with the lattice shaking
Clark et al. 2018
, where the tunnelling rate depends on the occupation number.

Density-dependent gauge potentials, which we shall refer to as nonlinear gauge potentials, have been shown to give rise to a number of interesting properties, such as chiral solitons
Edmonds et al. 2013a
;
Dingwall et al. 2018
;
Dingwall and Öhberg 2019
and anyonic structures
Aglietti et al. 1996
. The superfluid properties can also be dramatically affected by the gauge potential, where for instance drag forces will become directionally dependent
Buggy 2019
. From a dynamical perspective, perhaps the most striking feature of a density-dependent gauge potential is the occurrence of an exotic flow-dependent nonlinear term in the wave equation for the quantum phase.
This is exemplified notably by the stress tensor of the fluid, which features a ”gauge pressure” term related to the overlap of the gauge potential and the gauge-covariant current
Buggy et al. 2020
.
One would expect such a term to carry important implications for the symmetry properties of the fluid.
For instance, a flow-dependent fluid pressure evidently transforms between Galilean frames.
This suggests that additional transformation rules should be implemented in order to ”restore” the covariance of the dynamical equations under a Galilean boost.

In this paper, we derive these transformation rules and also investigate the invariance of the dynamical equations of such systems under both external and nonlinear gauge transformations.
We work within a hydrodynamic canonical formalism and consider transformations at the level of the field equations, rather than the wave equations proceeding from these.
Furthermore, although the motivation for our study stems from a well-established microscopic model proposed in
Edmonds et al. 2013a
, we follow a general approach adopted from a previous paper
Buggy et al. 2020
, which is not tied to any particular model.
Here, we simply assume that a density-dependent gauge potential
\mathbf{A}\left(\rho\right)
enters the Hamiltonian functional, whose components take the form of arbitrary density-dependent functions.
Hence, our concern lies not with the physical origin of the potentials, but rather, the formal structure of the dynamical equations which result from such a coupling.
We shall refer to this class of fluids as
nonlinear gauge-coupled quantum fluids
.

The paper is outlined as follows.
In section
II
, we begin by reviewing the canonical field formalism and hydrodynamic equations for such fluids, which will be used subsequently in the text.
Further, in section
III
, we consider gauge transformations, treating both the case of external gauge functions and nonlinear gauge functionals.
Finally, in section
IV
, we investigate the covariance of the canonical field equations under general Galilean transformations.

II
Hydrodynamic equations of the fluid

Our study pertains to the class of nonlinear quantum fluids whose effective meanfield Hamiltonian may be written in the form

\hat{H}_{MF}=\frac{\left(\hat{\mathbf{p}}-\mathbf{A}\left(\rho\right)\right)^{2}}{2m}+\eta\left(\rho\right),

(1)

where
\eta
and
\mathbf{A}
are nonlinear effective potentials which depend on the density of the fluid.
After performing a Madelung transformation,
\psi=\sqrt{\rho}e^{i\theta/\hbar}
, on the macroscopic condensate wavefunction
\psi
, it is possible to show
Buggy et al. 2020
that the dynamics of the field components
\rho
and
\theta
, may be expressed in the Hamiltonian form

\displaystyle\dot{\rho}\left(\mathbf{r}\right)

\displaystyle-\frac{\delta H}{\delta\theta\left(\mathbf{r}\right)}=0,

(2)

\displaystyle\dot{\theta}\left(\mathbf{r}\right)

\displaystyle+\frac{\delta H}{\delta\rho\left(\mathbf{r}\right)}=0,

(3)

where the Hamiltonian functional of the field, is given by

H\left[\rho,\theta\right]=\int d^{3}\mathbf{r}\left\{\rho\left[\frac{\left(\mbox{\boldmath$\nabla$}\theta-\mathbf{A}\left(\rho\right)\right)^{2}}{2m}+\eta\left(\rho\right)\right]+\mathcal{Q}\right\},

(4)

with
\mathcal{Q}=\left(\hbar\mbox{\boldmath$\nabla$}\rho\right)^{2}/\left(8m\rho\right)
representing a quantum energy density.
Note that the derivatives of the Hamiltonian appearing in Eqs. (
2
) and (
3
) are functional, or variational derivatives.
As mentioned previously, a defining feature of a density-dependent gauge potential is the occurrence of a nonlinear flow term in the wave equation for
\theta
.
Indeed, substituting the above expression for
H
into the canonical field equations (
2
) and (
3
), yields, respectively, the wave equations

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\rho

\displaystyle+\mbox{\boldmath$\nabla$}\cdot\left(\rho\mathbf{v}\right)=0,

(5)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\theta

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{2}mv^{2}+\Phi\left(\rho,\mathbf{u}\right)+Q=0,

(6)

where

\mathbf{v}=\mathbf{u}-\mathbf{A}/m,

(7)

is the physical velocity or mechanical flow,
\mathbf{u}=\mbox{\boldmath$\nabla$}\theta/m
is the canonical or phase flow and
Q=-\hbar^{2}\nabla^{2}\sqrt{\rho}/\left(2m\sqrt{\rho}\right)
is the quantum potential.
Moreover, the effective potentials lead to a non-trivial nonlinear term

\Phi=\eta+\rho\frac{\partial\eta}{\partial\rho}-\rho\mathbf{v}\cdot\frac{\partial\mathbf{A}}{\partial\rho},

(8)

in the wave equation of the phase, which is linear in the canonical flow
\mathbf{u}
.
As a consequence, the fluid pressure also inherits a flow-dependent term.
This may be seen by expressing the hydrodynamic equations (
5
) and (
6
) in the reference frame of the fluid, which yield
Buggy et al. 2020
, respectively

\displaystyle\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial t}+\mathbf{v}\cdot\mbox{\boldmath$\nabla$}\right)\rho+\rho\mbox{\boldmath$\nabla$}\cdot\mathbf{v}=0,

(9)

\displaystyle m\rho\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial t}+\mathbf{v}\cdot\mbox{\boldmath$\nabla$}\right)u_{k}=\nabla_{j}\left(-\delta_{jk}P+\sigma_{jk}\right),

(10)

where

\sigma_{jk}=-\frac{\hbar^{2}}{4m\rho}\nabla_{j}\rho\nabla_{k}\rho,

(11)

is the quantum stress tensor, and
P
is the fluid pressure, given by

P=-\frac{\hbar^{2}}{4m}\nabla^{2}\rho+\rho\left(\Phi-\eta\right).

(12)

Note the distinction between
\mathbf{v}
and
\mathbf{u}
in equation (
10
).
As such, this equation describes the transport of
canonical
momentum rather than mechanical momentum.
Recalling expression (
8
) for
\Phi
, we see that
P
depends explicitly on the flow profile of the fluid.
In particular, the fluid pressure may be viewed as a combination of the following three terms:

P=P_{Q}+P_{\eta}+P_{\mathbf{A}},

(13)

where,
P_{Q}=-\hbar^{2}\nabla^{2}\rho/(4m)
, is the quantum pressure,
P_{\eta}=\rho^{2}\partial_{\rho}\eta
, is the pressure contribution from the nonlinear scalar potential, and,
P_{\mathbf{A}}=-\rho^{2}\mathbf{v}\cdot\partial_{\rho}\mathbf{A}
, is the pressure contribution from the nonlinear gauge potential, which we refer to as the
gauge pressure
.

III
Gauge transformations

Let us investigate the implications of a nonlinear gauge potential on the symmetry properties of the fluid.
To begin with, we consider gauge transformations of the form

\displaystyle\theta\rightarrow

\displaystyle\theta^{\prime}=\theta+\chi,

(14)

\displaystyle\mathbf{A}\rightarrow

\displaystyle\mathbf{A}^{\prime}=\mathbf{A}+\mbox{\boldmath$\nabla$}\chi,

(15)

\displaystyle\eta\rightarrow

\displaystyle\eta^{\prime}=\eta-\partial_{t}\chi.

(16)

We shall treat both the case in which
\chi
is an external single-valued scalar function of space and time,
\chi=\chi\left(\mathbf{r},t\right)
, and a functional of the density,
\chi=\chi\left[\rho\right]
.
Notice that the latter is required for evaluating whether nonlinear gauge potentials are physical potentials which may not be trivially gauged away, since any attempt to do so involves a gauge functional given by the contour integral of some function of the density.
In both cases, the transformed canonical field equations may be written in the generic form

\displaystyle\dot{\rho}\left(\mathbf{r}\right)

\displaystyle-\frac{\delta H^{\prime}}{\delta\theta^{\prime}\left(\mathbf{r}\right)}=0,

(17)

\displaystyle\dot{\theta}^{\prime}\left(\mathbf{r}\right)

\displaystyle+\frac{\delta H^{\prime}}{\delta\rho\left(\mathbf{r}\right)}=0,

(18)

where the Hamiltonian transforms according to

H^{\prime}=\int d^{3}\mathbf{r}\left\{\rho\left[\frac{\left(\mbox{\boldmath$\nabla$}\theta^{\prime}-\mathbf{A}^{\prime}\right)^{2}}{2m}+\eta^{\prime}\right]+\mathcal{Q}\right\}.

(19)

In the Madelung representation of the fluid, it becomes readily apparent that the combination of transformations (
14
) and (
15
) leaves the mechanical flow
\mathbf{v}
from Eq. (
7
) unchanged, i.e.
\mathbf{v}
is gauge invariant.
Hence, according to Eq. (
19
), the new and old Hamiltonians are related by

H^{\prime}\left[\rho,\theta^{\prime}\right]=H\left[\rho,\theta\right]-\int d^{3}\mathbf{r}\rho\frac{\partial\chi}{\partial t}.

(20)

III.1
External gauge functions
\chi\left(\mathbf{r},t\right)

Let us examine the situation where
\chi
is an externally prescribed scalar function.
Clearly, in this case,
\delta\chi=0
for arbitrary field variations
\delta\rho
and
\delta\theta
.
Hence, the first variation of Eq. (
20
), yields

\displaystyle\frac{\delta H^{\prime}}{\delta\theta^{\prime}\left(\mathbf{r}\right)}

\displaystyle=\frac{\delta H}{\delta\theta\left(\mathbf{r}\right)},

(21)

\displaystyle\frac{\delta H^{\prime}}{\delta\rho\left(\mathbf{r}\right)}

\displaystyle=\frac{\delta H}{\delta\rho\left(\mathbf{r}\right)}-\frac{\partial\chi\left(\mathbf{r},t\right)}{\partial t},

(22)

Inserting the first and second of these into, respectively, the transformed field Eqs. (
17
) and (
18
), we immediately recover the original field Eqs. (
2
) and (
3
).
Therefore, the canonical equations of the nonlinear fluid are form invariant under gauge transformations generated by external gauge functions
\chi\left(\mathbf{r},t\right)
.
Inspecting the form of the nonlinear term in the wave equation for the phase,
\Phi
from Eq. (
8
), this is not surprising.
Indeed, the nonlinear flow term results from a particular form of density-dependence of
\mathbf{A}
, which remains unchanged by an external gauge function.

III.2
Nonlinear gauge functionals
\chi\left[\rho\right]

Next we examine the case where
\chi
takes the form of some functional of the density.
To begin with, observe how the canonical structure of the field equations (
2
) and (
3
), implies that the field components
\rho
and
\theta
play the role of conjugate variables, where the Poisson bracket of two dynamical variables
f
and
g
on phase space, reads

\left\{f\left(\mathbf{x}\right),g\left(\mathbf{y}\right)\right\}_{\rho,\theta}\\
=\int d^{3}\mathbf{r}\left(\frac{\delta f\left(\mathbf{x}\right)}{\delta\rho\left(\mathbf{r}\right)}\frac{\delta g\left(\mathbf{y}\right)}{\delta\theta\left(\mathbf{r}\right)}-\frac{\delta f\left(\mathbf{x}\right)}{\delta\theta\left(\mathbf{r}\right)}\frac{\delta g\left(\mathbf{y}\right)}{\delta\rho\left(\mathbf{r}\right)}\right).

(23)

Now, for gauge transformations generated by
\chi\left[\rho\right]
, the field variables transform according to

\begin{pmatrix}\rho\\
\theta\end{pmatrix}\rightarrow\begin{pmatrix}\rho^{\prime}=\rho\\
\theta^{\prime}=\theta+\chi\left[\rho\right]\end{pmatrix}.

(24)

Let us check whether the new variables form a canonically conjugate pair.
To this end, we evaluate the Poisson bracket of the new variables with respect to the old variables, which gives

\left\{\rho^{\prime}\left(\mathbf{x}\right),\theta^{\prime}\left(\mathbf{y}\right)\right\}_{\rho,\theta}\\
=\int d^{3}\mathbf{r}\left(\frac{\delta\rho^{\prime}\left(\mathbf{x}\right)}{\delta\rho\left(\mathbf{r}\right)}\frac{\delta\theta^{\prime}\left(\mathbf{y}\right)}{\delta\theta\left(\mathbf{r}\right)}-\frac{\delta\rho^{\prime}\left(\mathbf{x}\right)}{\delta\theta\left(\mathbf{r}\right)}\frac{\delta\theta^{\prime}\left(\mathbf{y}\right)}{\delta\rho\left(\mathbf{r}\right)}\right)\\
=\delta\left(\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{y}\right),

(25)

on account of
\rho^{\prime}
being independent of
\theta
.
Hence
\theta^{\prime}
and
\rho
may be treated as independent variables.
We shall make use of this property further in section
III.3
.

Following the same line of reasoning as section
III.1
, let us evaluate the first variation of Eq. (
20
) for the case of a nonlinear gauge functional.
Since
\chi
is independent of
\theta
, Eq. (
21
) remains valid and the transformed canonical equation (
17
) for
\rho
again reduces to the original canonical equation (
2
).
However, the canonical equation for
\theta
is no longer form invariant, but transforms according to

\dot{\theta}^{\prime}+\frac{\delta H}{\delta\rho}-\frac{\delta}{\delta\rho}\int d^{3}\mathbf{r}\rho\partial_{t}\chi\left[\rho\right]=0,

(26)

where an additional term appears due to
\chi\left[\rho\right]
.
Although the form taken by this term will depend on
\chi\left[\rho\right]
, one may see that current terms will generally be involved, through
\partial_{t}\chi\left[\rho\right]
.
In the following section, we examine one such gauge functional.
We may therefore conclude that density-dependent gauge potentials are physical potentials which may not be trivially gauged away.

III.3
The one-dimensional gauge-coupled superfluid

Typically, one may be interested in gauge functionals which eliminate the gauge potential from the kinetic energy density.
Let us examine one such case.
As our system, we consider the superfluid fraction of particles in an optically-addressed weakly-interacting dilute Bose gas of two-level atoms, first proposed in
Edmonds et al. 2013a
.
Confining the dynamics to one of the condensate components, a density-modulated gauge potential emerges in the form
\mathbf{A}=\mathbf{a}\rho
, where
\rho
is the atomic density of the relevant condensate component, while
\mathbf{a}=\left(g_{11}-g_{22}\right)\mbox{\boldmath$\nabla$}\phi/\left(8\Omega\right)
controls the effective strength and orientation of the nonlinear vector potential.
Here,
\Omega
is the generalised Rabi-frequency of the light-matter coupling,
\phi
is the phase of the incident laser field and
g_{ij}=4\pi\hbar^{2}a_{ij}/m
denote the coupling constants of the channels stemming from pairwise interactions between the atoms, where
a_{ij}
are the associated s-wave scattering lengths.

The system is described by a wave equation of the form (
6
), with
\Phi=-\mathbf{a}\cdot\mathbf{J}+g\rho
and
g=\left(g_{11}+g_{22}+2g_{12}\right)/4
.
For a superfluid confined to dimension
d=1
, the wave equation for the phase reads

\partial_{t}\theta+\frac{1}{2}mv^{2}-aJ+g\rho+Q=0,

(27)

where,
v=\left(\partial_{x}\theta-a\rho\right)/m
and
J=\rho v
.
We choose
d=1
, since the last term in Eq. (
26
) becomes integrable in this case.
In turn, the fluid pressure again takes the form of equation (
13
), where the quantum pressure is
P_{Q}=-\hbar^{2}\partial_{x}^{2}\rho/4m
,
P_{\eta}=g\rho^{2}/2
, and the gauge pressure reads
P_{A}=-JA
.

The gauge functional which eliminates
\mathbf{A}
from the kinetic energy density, is

\chi=-\int_{-\infty}^{x}dya\rho\left(y,t\right).

(28)

Invoking this relation in Eq. (
20
), we find that the new and old Hamiltonians are related by

H^{\prime}=H-\int dx\rho^{2}av^{\prime},

(29)

where
v^{\prime}=\partial_{x}\theta^{\prime}/m
.
Hence, an additional term
-\int dxJ^{\prime}A=-\int dxJA
, appears in the Hamiltonian under the nonlinear gauge transformation, where
J^{\prime}=\rho v^{\prime}
.
In other words, the gauge-pressure,
P_{A}
, is generated in the Hamiltonian density when one attempts to eliminate the nonlinear gauge potential.
Inserting expression (
29
) for
H^{\prime}
into the field Eq. (
18
), yields the wave equation

\partial_{t}\theta^{\prime}+\frac{1}{2}mv^{\prime}v^{\prime}-2aJ^{\prime}+g\rho+Q=0,

(30)

where we have used
\delta v^{\prime}/\delta\rho=0
, which holds on account of
\rho
and
\theta^{\prime}
being independent (see section
III.2
).
Comparing Eqs. (
27
) and (
30
), we see that the wave equation has transformed under the nonlinear gauge transformation.
In particular, the attempt to absorb the nonlinear gauge potential into the phase has produced an additional current term in the wave equation.

IV
Galilean Transformations

We now turn our attention to a space-time symmetry group associated with coordinate transformations, namely, Galilean transformations.
The coordinate transformations from some inertial frame of reference
\Sigma
, to another frame
\Sigma^{\prime}
moving with a uniform, nonrelativistic relative velocity
\mathbf{w}
, may be written in the general form
Holland 1995
;
Arnold et al. 2007
;
Iro 2015

\displaystyle x_{i}

\displaystyle\rightarrow x_{i}^{\prime}=R_{ij}x_{j}-w_{i}t+a_{i},

\displaystyle t

\displaystyle\rightarrow t^{\prime}=t+t_{0},

(31)

where
a_{i}
and
t_{0}
are constants representing offsets in position and time respectively, and
R_{ij}
is a unitary rotational matrix satisfying
R_{ij}R_{jk}=\delta_{ik}
.
Differential operators also transform between frames under (
31
), such that

\displaystyle\nabla_{i}

\displaystyle\rightarrow\nabla_{i}^{\prime}=R_{ij}\nabla_{j},

(32)

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial t}

\displaystyle\rightarrow\frac{\partial}{\partial t^{\prime}}=\frac{\partial}{\partial t}+w_{i}R_{ij}\nabla_{j}.

(33)

In turn, the velocity field, transforms as

v_{i}\rightarrow v_{i}^{\prime}=R_{ij}v_{j}-w_{i}.

(34)

Since the energy and momentum of the fluid transforms under a Galilean boost, the phase also transforms between reference frames.
Let us denote this transformation, by

\theta\rightarrow\theta^{\prime}=\theta+\chi,

(35)

where
\chi=\chi\left(\mathbf{r},t\right)
is a single-valued scalar function of space and time.
Furthermore, we should not exclude the prospect that the potentials may be subject to transformations in order that the dynamical equations be form invariant.
In fact, this is already the situation for a nonrelativistic quantum mechanical particle coupled to both external scalar and vector potentials
Takagi 1991
;
DeWitt 1957
;
Brown and Holland 1999
.
Accordingly, let us denote the transformations of the nonlinear potentials, as

\displaystyle\eta

\displaystyle\rightarrow\eta^{\prime}=\eta+\mu,

(36)

\displaystyle A_{i}

\displaystyle\rightarrow A_{i}^{\prime}=R_{ij}A_{j}+G_{i},

(37)

where
\mu
and
\mathbf{G}
are unspecified.
However, in view of the nonlinear character of
\eta
and
\mathbf{A}
, we allow for the possibility that these also depend on the density.

In the frame
\Sigma^{\prime}
, the canonical field equations (
2
) and (
3
), read

\displaystyle\frac{\partial\rho^{\prime}}{\partial t^{\prime}}

\displaystyle-\frac{\delta H^{\prime}}{\delta\theta^{\prime}}=0,

(38)

\displaystyle\frac{\partial\theta^{\prime}}{\partial t^{\prime}}

\displaystyle+\frac{\delta H^{\prime}}{\delta\rho^{\prime}}=0,

(39)

where the Hamiltonian from Eq. (
4
), transforms to

H^{\prime}=\int d^{3}\mathbf{r}^{\prime}\left[\rho^{\prime}\left(\frac{1}{2}m\mathbf{v}^{\prime}\cdot\mathbf{v}^{\prime}+\eta^{\prime}\right)+\mathcal{Q}^{\prime}\right],

(40)

with
\mathbf{v}^{\prime}=\left(\mbox{\boldmath$\nabla$}^{\prime}\theta^{\prime}-\mathbf{A}^{\prime}\right)/m
.
Since
\rho^{\prime}=\rho
and
\mathbf{v}^{\prime}
is restricted by Eq. (
34
), the Hamiltonians in the two frames are related, by

H^{\prime}\left[\rho^{\prime},\theta^{\prime}\right]=H\left[\rho,\theta\right]+\int d^{3}\mathbf{r}\rho\left(\frac{1}{2}mw^{2}-mw_{i}R_{ij}v_{j}+\mu\right).

(41)

The first variation of the above equation, yields

\displaystyle\frac{\delta H^{\prime}}{\delta\rho^{\prime}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\delta H}{\delta\rho}+\frac{1}{2}mw^{2}-mw_{i}R_{ij}v_{j}

(42)

\displaystyle+\mu+\rho\left(\frac{\partial\mu}{\partial\rho}+w_{i}R_{ij}\frac{\partial A_{j}}{\partial\rho}\right),

\displaystyle\frac{\delta H^{\prime}}{\delta\theta^{\prime}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\delta H}{\delta\theta}+w_{i}R_{ij}\nabla_{j}\rho.

(43)

Note that in order to obtain Eq. (
43
), we have substituted Eq. (
7
) for
\mathbf{v}
into Eq. (
41
) and integrated by parts accordingly.
Let us investigate the implications of these relations on the transformed canonical equations (
38
) and (
39
).
Invoking Eqs. (
33
) and (
43
) in Eq. (
38
), we immediately recover the original field equation for
\rho
.
Hence the canonical equation (
2
) is form invariant under a Galilean transformation.
Notice here that no demands are being made of
\mu
,
\mathbf{G}
or
\chi
, other than the restriction that
\chi
be independent of
\theta
, since the violation of the latter introduces additional terms in Eq. (
43
).
Turning our attention to the field equation for
\theta
, let us substitute Eqs. (
33
), (
35
) and (
41
) into Eq. (
39
), which gives

\dot{\theta}+\frac{\delta H}{\delta\rho}+\xi=0,

(44)

where we have grouped the additional terms generated by the Galilean transformation, in the function
\xi=\xi_{\chi}+\xi_{\pi}
, with

\displaystyle\xi_{\chi}

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial t}+w_{i}R_{ij}\nabla_{j}\right)\chi+w_{i}R_{ij}\left(\nabla_{j}\theta-mv_{j}\right)+\frac{1}{2}mw^{2},

(45)

\displaystyle\xi_{\pi}

\displaystyle=\mu+w_{i}R_{ij}A_{j}+\rho\left(\frac{\partial\mu}{\partial\rho}+w_{i}R_{ij}\frac{\partial A_{j}}{\partial\rho}\right).

(46)

In other words, a suitably chosen set of transformations (
35
), (
36
) and (
37
) which solves
\xi=0
, signals the form invariance of the field equation
(
3
).
Such a prospect is readily achieved by setting both Eqs. (
45
) and (
46
) to zero.
The condition,
\xi_{\chi}=0
, is upheld by a suitable phase transformation, while the imposition,
\xi_{\pi}=0
, leads to a transformation rule for the potentials.
Indeed, notice that the phase factor,
\chi=mw^{2}t/2-mw_{i}R_{ij}x_{j}
, solves the first of these two conditions, such that
Holland 1995
;
Brown and Holland 1999

\theta\rightarrow\theta+\frac{1}{2}mw^{2}t-mw_{i}R_{ij}x_{j},

(47)

while the second condition, is ensured for
\mu=-w_{i}R_{ij}A_{j}
.
Thus, the nonlinear potentials transform according to

\displaystyle\eta

\displaystyle\rightarrow\eta-w_{i}R_{ij}A_{j},

(48)

\displaystyle A_{i}

\displaystyle\rightarrow R_{ij}A_{j}.

(49)

These transformations are identical to those which appear for a Schrödinger field coupled to external scalar and vector potentials
Brown and Holland 1999
, but represent a nonlinear transformation.
Note that the above transformations also appear for a classical charge, emerging as the magnetic limit of Galilean electromagnetism
Le Bellac and Lévy-Leblond 1973
.
In turn, performing transformations (
34
), (
48
) and (
49
) in Eq. (
8
) for the nonlinear scalar term in the wave equation for the phase,
\Phi
, yields

\Phi\rightarrow\Phi-w_{i}R_{ij}A_{j}.

(50)

Hence
\Phi
and
\eta
undergo an identical transformation.
Furthermore, although the fluid pressure
P
from Eq. (
12
) also depends explicitly on
\mathbf{v}
, performing transformations (
48
) and (
50
) in Eq. (
12
) reveals that the covariant transformations of the potentials between frames ensure that the pressure is invariant.

V
Conclusion

The dynamical equations of a nonlinear gauge-coupled quantum fluid are invariant under local gauge transformations, insofar as the gauge function
\chi
takes the form of an external scalar function of
\mathbf{r}
and
t
.
However, when
\chi
becomes a functional of the density, the form-invariance of the dynamical equation for the phase
\theta
is lost.
As a result, density-dependent gauge potentials are physical, non-trivial potentials which cannot be absorbed into the phase, since attempting to do so invariably destroys the form of the dynamical equations.
For a gauge-coupled one-dimensional superfluid, the additional term in the field equation generated by a nonlinear gauge transformation becomes integrable.
In particular, the gauge-pressure of the nonlinear fluid is generated in the Hamiltonian density for gauge transformations which eliminate the gauge potential from the kinetic energy density.
Finally, the immediate lack of Galilean covariance of the nonlinear fluid may be restored by subjecting the potentials to a nonlinear transformation.
As a result, the flow-dependent fluid pressure inherent to such systems, becomes invariant under a Galilean transformation.
These transformations are identical in form to those of a Schrödinger field subject to external scalar and vector potentials, but represent nonlinear transformations.

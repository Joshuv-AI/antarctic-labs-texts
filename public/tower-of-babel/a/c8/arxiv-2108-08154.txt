A note on numerical ranges of tensors Journal: arXiv.org
Nirmal Chandra Rout †a , Krushnachandra Panigrahy †b , Debasisha Mishra †c

Abstract
Theory of numerical range and numerical radius for tensors is not studied much in the literature. In 2016, Ke et al. [Linear Algebra Appl., 508 (2016) 100-132] introduced first the notion of numerical range of a tensor via the k k -mode product. However, the convexity of the numerical range via the k k -mode product was not proved by them. In this paper, the notion of numerical range and numerical radius for even-order square tensors using inner product via the Einstein product are introduced first. We provide some sufficient conditions using numerical radius for a tensor to being unitary. The convexity of the numerical range is also proved. We also provide an algorithm to plot the numerical range of a tensor. Furthermore, some properties of the numerical range for the Moore–Penrose inverse of a tensor are discussed.

1.

The set of invertible tensors

2.

The tensor formulation via the Einstein product preserves the low-rank structure in the solution and the right-hand side. Such as, in high-dimensional Poisson problem, the solution and the right-hand side, both represented as

The matrix unfolding of a tensor may give rise to larger bandwidths than the original tensor which increases the number of operations and storage locations. For example, the Laplacian matrices in high dimensions have larger bandwidths than the Laplacian tensors.

2

Numerical range of a tensor

In this section, a possible extension of numerical range for a tensor via the Einstein product is introduced. Spectral containment and convexity are two important requirements in the generalization.

For two tensors
\mathcal{X},\mathcal{Y}\in\mathbb{C}^{I_{1\ldots N}}
, we define an inner product
\left\langle\mathcal{X},\mathcal{Y}\right\rangle=\mathcal{Y}^{H}{*_{N}}\mathcal{X}
and a norm induced by this inner product as
\|\mathcal{X}\|=\left\langle\mathcal{X},\mathcal{X}\right\rangle^{1/2}
. A tensor
\mathcal{X}\in\mathbb{C}^{I_{1\ldots N}}
is said to be a
unit tensor
, if
\|\mathcal{X}\|=1
. According to (
1
), it is natural to consider the following generalization.

Definition 2.1
.

The numerical range of an even-order square tensor
\mathcal{A}\in\mathbb{C}^{I_{1\ldots N}\times I_{1\ldots N}}
, denote it by
W(\mathcal{A})
, is defined as

\displaystyle W(\mathcal{A})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\{\left\langle\mathcal{A}{*_{N}}\mathcal{X},\mathcal{X}\right\rangle:\;\mathcal{X}\text{ is a unit tensor in }\mathbb{C}^{I_{1\ldots N}}\}.

(8)

With some elementary calculation it can be shown that,

W(\mathcal{A})=\left\{\frac{\left\langle\mathcal{A}{*_{N}}\mathcal{X},\mathcal{X}\right\rangle}{\|\mathcal{X}\|^{2}}:\;\mathcal{O}\neq\mathcal{X}\in\mathbb{C}^{I_{1\ldots N}}\right\}.

(9)

Note that, in the above Definition
2.1
when
N=1
, it coincides with the matrix numerical range defined in (
1
). The spectrum of a tensor is always contained in it’s numerical range is shown in the next theorem.

Theorem 2.2
.

The spectrum of
\mathcal{A}
always lies in

The real part and imaginary part of the numerical ranges of a tensor are same as the numerical range of the Hermitian part and skew-Hermitian part of that tensor, respectively. .

An algorithm for finding tensor numerical range

In this section, we provide an algorithm to plot the boundary of the numerical range of a tensor.
The set of tensors
\{\mathcal{U}_{1},\mathcal{U}_{2},\ldots,\mathcal{U}_{n}\}
, where
\mathcal{U}_{i}\in\mathbb{C}^{I_{1\ldots N}}
, is called orthogonal if for
i\neq j
,
\left\langle\mathcal{U}_{i},\mathcal{U}_{j}\right\rangle=0
, and further if it satisfies
\left\langle\mathcal{U}_{i},\mathcal{U}_{i}\right\rangle=1
then it is called orthonormal.

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
\mathcal{A}\in\mathbb{C}^{I_{1\ldots N}\times I_{1\ldots N}}
and
\mathcal{X}\in\mathbb{C}^{I_{1\ldots N}}
. Then the following are equivalent.

1.

Re\left\langle\mathcal{A}{*_{N}}\mathcal{X},\mathcal{X}\right\rangle=\max\{Re(z):\,z\in W(\mathcal{A})\}
.

2.

\left\langle H(\mathcal{A}){*_{N}}\mathcal{X},\mathcal{X}\right\rangle=\max\{r:\,r\in W(H(\mathcal{A}))\}
.

3.

H(\mathcal{A}){*_{N}}\mathcal{X}=\lambda_{\max}\mathcal{X}
, where
\lambda_{\max}=\max\{\lambda:\lambda\in\sigma(H(\mathcal{A}))\}
.

Next, an immediate consequence of the above Theorem
3.1
is presented as a corollary without proof.

Corollary 3.2
.

Let
\mathcal{A}\in\mathbb{C}^{I_{1\ldots N}\times I_{1\ldots N}}
. Then,

\max\{Re(z):\,z\in W(\mathcal{A})\}=\max\{r:\,r\in W(H(\mathcal{A}))\}=\max\{\lambda:\lambda\in\sigma(H(\mathcal{A}))\}.

Note that, according to Theorem
2.4
, we have
e^{-i\theta}W(e^{i\theta}\mathcal{A})=W(\mathcal{A})
for all
0\leq\theta\leq 2\pi
.

Theorem 3.2
.

Let
\mathcal{A}\in\mathbb{C}^{I_{1\ldots N}\times I_{1\ldots N}}
and
\mathcal{X}_{\theta}
be the normalized eigentensor corresponding to the maximum eigenvalue of
H(e^{i\theta}\mathcal{A})
for some
\theta\in[0,2\pi]
. Then, the complex number
\mathcal{X}_{\theta}^{H}{*_{N}}\mathcal{A}{*_{N}}\mathcal{X}_{\theta}=\left\langle\mathcal{A}{*_{N}}\mathcal{X}_{\theta},\mathcal{X}_{\theta}\right\rangle
is a boundary point of
W(\mathcal{A})
.

Numerical radius of a tensor

In this section, we introduce the notion of numerical radius for tensors and investigate its properties.

Definition 4.1
.

The numerical radius of an even-order square tensor
\mathcal{A}\in\mathbb{C}^{I_{1\ldots N}\times I_{1\ldots N}}
is denoted as
w(\mathcal{A})
and is defined as

w(\mathcal{A})=\max\{|z|\mid z\in W(\mathcal{A})\}.

(10)

Tensors satisfy the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality. Next we state this without proof. One can follow the steps of existing proofs in the literature to verify the inequality.

Theorem 4.2
.

Let
\mathcal{A},\mathcal{B}\in\mathbb{C}^{I_{1\ldots N}}
. Then
|\langle\mathcal{A},\mathcal{B}\rangle|\leq\|\mathcal{A}\|\|\mathcal{B}\|
.

Now we state a very popular theorem of numerical radius inequality for tensor which may contribute to develop new theories of numerical radius for tensors.
For
\mathcal{A}\in\mathbb{C}^{I_{1\ldots N}\times I_{1\ldots N}}
and
\mathcal{X},\mathcal{Y}\in\mathbb{C}^{I_{1\ldots N}}
we define
\|\mathcal{A}\|=\sup\{\|\mathcal{A}{*_{N}}\mathcal{X}\|:\|\mathcal{X}\|=1\}=\sup\{\left|\langle\mathcal{A}{*_{N}}\mathcal{X},\mathcal{Y}\rangle\right|:\|\mathcal{X}\|=\|\mathcal{Y}\|=1\}
.

Theorem 4.3
.

Let
\mathcal{A}\in\mathbb{C}^{I_{1\ldots N}\times I_{1\ldots N}}
, then

\dfrac{1}{2}\|\mathcal{A}\|\leq w(\mathcal{A})\leq\|\mathcal{A}\|.

Next we present a corollary as an immediate consequence of Theorem
4.3
without proof.

Corollary 4.3
.

Let
\mathcal{A},\mathcal{B}\in\mathbb{C}^{I_{1\ldots N}\times I_{1\ldots N}}
. Then
w(\mathcal{A}{*_{N}}\mathcal{B})\leq 4w(\mathcal{A})w(\mathcal{B})
.

Next, we recall two results on the determinant of tensors due to Liang
et al.

[
19
]
.

Theorem 4.4

(Theorem 3.16,
[
19
]
)
.

Let
\mathcal{A},\mathcal{B}\in\mathbb{R}^{I_{1\ldots N}\times I_{1\ldots N}}
be two tensors. Then
\det(\mathcal{A}{*_{N}}\mathcal{B})=\det(\mathcal{A})\det(\mathcal{B})
.

Theorem 4.5

(Theorem 4.10,
[
19
]
)
.

Let
\mathcal{A}=(a_{i_{1}\ldots i_{N}j_{1}\ldots j_{N}})\in\mathbb{C}^{I_{1\ldots N}\times I_{1\ldots N}}
be a given tensor, then
\det(\mathcal{A})=\prod_{i_{1},\ldots,i_{N}}\lambda_{i_{1}\ldots i_{N}}
.

For two tensors
\mathcal{A},\mathcal{B}\in\mathbb{C}^{I_{1\ldots N}\times I_{1\ldots N}}
, define
|\mathcal{A}|:=(\mathcal{A}^{H}{*_{N}}\mathcal{A})^{1/2}
and
\mathcal{A}\geq\mathcal{B}
means
\mathcal{A}-\mathcal{B}
is a positive definite tensor. The following result provides a sufficient condition for unitarity of a tensor.

Lemma 4.6
.

Let
\mathcal{A}\in\mathbb{C}^{I_{1\ldots N}\times I_{1\ldots N}}
be an invertible tensor such that
w(\mathcal{A})\leq 1

and
|\mathcal{A}|\geq\mathcal{I}.
Then
\mathcal{A}
is unitary.

Lemma
4.6
is helpful in the next theorem to prove a new necessary and sufficient condition for a unitary tensor.

Theorem 4.7
.

\mathcal{A}\in\mathbb{C}^{I_{1\ldots N}\times I_{1\ldots N}}
is an invertible tensor such that
w(\mathcal{A})\leq 1
and
w(\mathcal{A}^{-1})\leq 1
if, and only if,
\mathcal{A}
is unitary.

Proof.

Let
\mathcal{A}=\mathcal{U}{*_{N}}|\mathcal{A}|
be the polar decomposition of
\mathcal{A}.
Then
(\mathcal{A}^{-1})^{H}=((\mathcal{U}{*_{N}}|\mathcal{A}|)^{-1})^{H}=(|\mathcal{A}|^{-1}{*_{N}}\mathcal{U}^{-1})^{H}=\mathcal{U}{*_{N}}(|\mathcal{A}|^{-1})^{H}=\mathcal{U}{*_{N}}|\mathcal{A}|^{-1}.
Since
w(\mathcal{A}^{-1})\leq 1
, so for any unit tensor
\mathcal{X}\in\mathbb{C}^{I_{1\ldots N}}
, we have

|\left\langle\mathcal{U}{*_{N}}|\mathcal{A}|^{-1}{*_{N}}\mathcal{X},\mathcal{X}\right\rangle|=|\left\langle(\mathcal{A}^{-1})^{H}{*_{N}}\mathcal{X},\mathcal{X}\right\rangle|=|\left\langle\mathcal{A}^{-1}{*_{N}}\mathcal{X},\mathcal{X}\right\rangle|\leq 1.

Let
\mathcal{B}:=\mathcal{U}{*_{N}}\frac{|\mathcal{A}|+|\mathcal{A}|^{-1}}{2}.
Here
|\mathcal{B}|=\frac{|\mathcal{A}|+|\mathcal{A}|^{-1}}{2}.
Now,

\displaystyle|\left\langle\mathcal{B}{*_{N}}\mathcal{X},\mathcal{X}\right\rangle|

\displaystyle=\left|\left\langle\left(\mathcal{U}{*_{N}}\frac{|\mathcal{A}|+|\mathcal{A}|^{-1}}{2}\right){*_{N}}\mathcal{X},\mathcal{X}\right\rangle\right|

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}|\left\langle\mathcal{U}{*_{N}}|\mathcal{A}|{*_{N}}\mathcal{X},\mathcal{X}\right\rangle+\left\langle\mathcal{U}{*_{N}}|\mathcal{A}|^{-1}{*_{N}}\mathcal{X},\mathcal{X}\right\rangle|

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}|\left\langle\mathcal{A}{*_{N}}\mathcal{X},\mathcal{X}\right\rangle+\left\langle\mathcal{A}^{-1}{*_{N}}\mathcal{X},\mathcal{X}\right\rangle|

\displaystyle\leq 1.

Thus
w(\mathcal{B})\leq 1.
Again, since
|\mathcal{A}|>0
and
|\mathcal{A}|^{-1}>0
, so
|\mathcal{A}|+|\mathcal{A}|^{-1}-2\mathcal{I}=(|\mathcal{A}|^{1/2}-|\mathcal{A}|^{-1/2})^{2}\geq 0.
Hence
\frac{|\mathcal{A}|+|\mathcal{A}|^{-1}}{2}\geq\mathcal{I}
, i.e.,
|\mathcal{B}|\geq\mathcal{I}.
Thus
\mathcal{B}
is unitary due to Lemma
4.6
and hence
|\mathcal{B}|=\mathcal{I}
, i.e.,
|\mathcal{A}|+|\mathcal{A}|^{-1}=2\mathcal{I}.
This leads to
|\mathcal{A}|=\mathcal{I}.
Thus
\mathcal{A}
is unitary. Converse part is obvious as
w(\mathcal{A})\leq\|\mathcal{A}\|
by Theorem
4.3
.

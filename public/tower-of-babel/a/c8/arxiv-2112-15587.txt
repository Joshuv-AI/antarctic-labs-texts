Nakayama algebras and Fuchsian singularities Thanks: ∗ Corresponding author
Helmut Lenzing, Hagen Meltzer and Shiquan Ruan ∗

Abstract
. This present paper is devoted to the study of a class of Nakayama algebras N n ​ ( r ) N_{n}(r) given by the path algebra of the equioriented quiver 𝔸 n \mathbb{A}_{n} subject to the nilpotency degree r r for each sequence of r r consecutive arrows. We show that the Nakayama algebras N n ​ ( r ) N_{n}(r) for certain pairs ( n , r ) (n,r) can be realized as endomorphism algebras of tilting objects in the bounded derived category of coherent sheaves over a weighted projective line, or in its stable category of vector bundles. Moreover, we classify all the Nakayama algebras N n ​ ( r ) N_{n}(r) of Fuchsian type, that is, derived equivalent to the bounded derived categories of extended canonical algebras. We also provide a new way to prove the classification result on Nakayama algebras of piecewise hereditary type, which have been done by Happel–Seidel before.

1.
Introduction

Let
k
be an algebraically closed field.
A finite dimensional algebra over
k
is called a
Nakayama algebra
if its indecomposable projective or injective modules have a unique composition series.
A very natural class of such algebras is formed by the algebras
N_{n}(r)
, given as the path algebra of the equioriented quiver

\textstyle{1}
\textstyle{\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces 2}
\scriptstyle{x}
\textstyle{\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces 3}
\scriptstyle{x}
\textstyle{\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\cdots}
\scriptstyle{x}
\textstyle{\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces n-1}
\scriptstyle{x}
\textstyle{\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces n}
\scriptstyle{x}

of type
\mathbb{A}_{n}
subject to all relations
x^{r}=0
.
The representation theory of the algebras
N_{n}(r)
is well-understood. They are all representation finite and even simply connected. This changes drastically if one asks for a description of their bounded derived categories.

A finite dimensional algebra
\Lambda
over
k
is said to be
piecewise hereditary
, if its bounded derived category
D^{b}(\Lambda)
is equivalent to
D^{b}(\mathcal{H})
of a hereditary abelian category
\mathcal{H}
. The possible types of
\mathcal{H}
occurring in this
situation have been described in
[
9
]
. More precisely, either it is the module category of finite
dimensional modules over a finite dimensional basic hereditary
k
-algebra,
or it is the category of coherent sheaves over a weighted projective line in the sense of
[
6
]
.
Since
k
is assumed
to be algebraically closed, for the former case
\Lambda
is derived equivalent to a path algebra over
k
of a finite quiver without oriented cycles, while for the latter case,
\Lambda
is derived equivalent to a canonical algebra in the sense of
[
28
]
.
In the first case we call
\Lambda
of
module type
and
in the second case we call
\Lambda
of
sheaf type
.
We refer to
[
12
,
13
]
for properties of piecewise hereditary algebras.

Associated to a weighted projective line
\mathbb{X}
of negative Euler characteristic, there is a graded algebra
R=\bigoplus_{n\geq 0}\mathrm{Hom}({\mathcal{O}}_{\mathbb{X}},\tau^{n}{\mathcal{O}}_{\mathbb{X}})
, where
{\mathcal{O}}_{\mathbb{X}}
stands for the structure sheaf of
\mathbb{X}
and
\tau
is the Auslander-Reiten translation in the category of coherent sheaves over
\mathbb{X}
. The graded algebra
R
is isomorphic to an algebra of automorphic forms, called
Fuchsian singularity
, with respect to certain Fuchsian group action on the upper half plane for the base field of complex numbers, (more precisely, the orbit fundamental group of
\mathbb{X}
action on its universal covering space), see
[
25
,
26
]
.
The
k
-algebra
R
is commutative, graded integral
Gorenstein, in particular Cohen–Macaulay, of Krull dimension two, c.f.
[
17
,
20
]
.

The graded singularity category of
R
was considered by Buchweitz
[
4
]
and Orlov
[
27
]
, see also
Krause’s account
[
15
]
for a related, but slightly different approach.
It is remarkable that this singularity category is equivalent to the stable category
\underline{\mathrm{vect}}^{\mathbb{Z}}\textrm{-}\mathbb{X}:=\mathrm{vect}\textrm{-}\mathbb{X}/[\tau^{\mathbb{Z}}{\mathcal{O}}_{\mathbb{X}}]
of vector bundles over
\mathbb{X}
modulo the
\tau
-orbit
\tau^{\mathbb{Z}}{\mathcal{O}}_{\mathbb{X}}
of the structure sheaf, and also equivalent to the bounded derived category of the
extended canonical algebra
arising from the canonical algebra (associated with
\mathbb{X}
) via one-point extension by an indecomposable
projective module.
We refer to
[
19
,
23
]
for more details.

In their remarkable paper
[
11
]
, Happel and Seidel classify all the Nakayama algebras which are piecewise hereditary. The aim of this paper is to classify all the Nakayama algebras of
Fuchsian type
, that is, the Nakayama algebras which are derived equivalent to Fuchsian singularities, or equivalently, derived equivalent to extended canonical algebras. The main result of this paper is contained in Figure

From Figure

we see that the Nakayama algebras of Fuchsian type (red area) contain two (infinite) families:
N_{r+5}(r)\;(r\geq 7)
and
N_{r+6}(r)\;(r\geq 6)
, having Fuchsian type
\langle 2,3,r]
and
\langle 2,3,r+1]
respectively; and some further (finite) sporadic cases.

In order to obtain these two infinite families of Fuchsian Nakayama categories(=bounded derived categories of Nakayama algebras of Fuchsian type), we first construct a tilting complex in the bounded derived category of coherent sheaves over the weighted projective line of weight type
(2,3,r)
, whose endomorphism algebra is isomorphic to the Nakayama algebra
N_{r+4}(r)
(see Proposition
3.1
); and then by using one-point extension approach to show that
N_{r+5}(r)
have Fuchsian type
\langle 2,3,r]
(see Corollary
3.3
), and
N_{r+6}(r)
have Fuchsian type
\langle 2,3,r+1]
(see Proposition
3.5
). During this procedure, an important strategy is to endow the projective, injective and simple
N_{r+4}(r)
-modules with the degree-rank data, inherited from that of coherent sheaves (see Remark
3.2
).

The most difficult part of this paper is to deal with the sporadic Nakayama algebras of Fuchsian types appearing in Figure
1
, namely, to show those sporadic Nakayama algebras have the desired Fuchsian types. For this we use two further strategies. The first one is to develop the extended Happel-Seidel symmetries between distinct Nakayama algebras (see Proposition
4.1
); and the second one is to construct tilting objects consisting of line bundles in the stable category
\underline{\mathrm{vect}}^{\mathbb{Z}}\textrm{-}\mathbb{X}
for certain weight types, whose endomorphism algebras are Nakayama algebras (see Theorem
5.5
).

Recall that there is
Happel-Seidel symmetry
developed in
[
11
]
. More precisely, for any
a,b\geq 2
, there is an equivalence

D^{b}(N_{n}(a))\simeq D^{b}(N_{n}(b))

for
n=(a-1)(b-1)
. This symmetry has a nice explanation from the knowledge of weighted projective lines, since both of these two categories are equivalent to the stable category of vector bundles

\underline{\mathrm{vect}}\textrm{-}\mathbb{X}:=\mathrm{vect}\textrm{-}\mathbb{X}/[\mathcal{L}]
, obtained from
\mathrm{vect}\textrm{-}\mathbb{X}
by factoring out all line bundles
\mathcal{L}
,
for the weighted projective line
\mathbb{X}
of weight type
(2,a,b)
, c.f.
[
16
]
. By analysing the realization of Nakayama algebras in the stable category
\underline{\mathrm{vect}}\textrm{-}\mathbb{X}
, we obtain further symmetries between Nakayama algebras, called
extended Happel-Seidel symmetry
as follows (see Proposition
4.1
):

D^{b}(N_{n\pm 1}(a))\simeq D^{b}(N_{n\pm 1}(b)).

With the help of the (extended) Happel-Seidel symmetries, we provide a new (simple) proof for the classification result of piecewise hereditary Nakayama categories obtained in
[
11
]
, by taking perpendicular category approach (see Theorem
4.4
).

We will give a brief outline of this paper.
In Section 2 we recall some basic materials and explain some notations. In Section 3, we construct a tilting complex in the bounded derived category of coherent sheaves over the weighted projective line of weight type
(2,3,r)
, whose endomorphism algebra is isomorphic to the Nakayama algebra
N_{r+4}(r)
. Then by using perpendicular category and one-point extension approaches we obtain four families of Nakayama algebras of module type or Fuchsian type.
In Section 4, we state the extended Happel-Seidel symmetries between distinct Nakayama algebras, and then
provide a new proof for the classification result of piecewise hereditary Nakayama categories obtained in
[
11
]
. In order to obtain the sporadic Fuchsian Nakayama categories in Figure
1
, we investigate the stable category
\underline{\mathrm{vect}}^{\mathbb{Z}}\textrm{-}\mathbb{X}
of vector bundles in more details in Section 5. We finally construct tilting objects consisting of line bundles in
\underline{\mathrm{vect}}^{\mathbb{Z}}\textrm{-}\mathbb{X}
for weight types
(2,4,5),(2,4,7),(2,5,5)
and (2,5,6) respectively, whose endomorphism algebras are Nakayama algebras. The proofs of the extension-free properties need to be considered case by case, which are put in Appendix
A
. In the final Section 6, we give the explicit formulas of the Coxeter polynomials for a class of Nakayama algebras, and then
obtain the main classification result for Nakayama algebras of Fuchsian type, under certain mild conjectures.

2.
Preliminary

2.1.
The category of coherent sheaves
\mathrm{coh}\textrm{-}\mathbb{X}

Let
k
be an algebraically closed field and
\mathbb{X}=\mathbb{X}(p_{1},p_{2},p_{3})
be a weighted projective line over
k
of weight type
(p_{1},p_{2},p_{3})
, where
p_{i}\geq 2
is an integer for each
i
.
Following
[
6
]
, the category
\mathrm{coh}\textrm{-}\mathbb{X}
of coherent sheaves on
\mathbb{X}
is obtained by applying Serre’s construction to the graded triangle singularity
x_{1}^{p_{1}}+x_{2}^{p_{2}}+x_{3}^{p_{3}}
.

More precisely, the Picard group of
\mathbb{X}
is naturally isomorphic to the rank one abelian group
\mathbb{L}=\mathbb{L}(p_{1},p_{2},p_{3})
on generators
\vec{x}_{1},\vec{x}_{2},\vec{x}_{3}
subject to the relations
p_{1}\vec{x}_{1}=p_{2}\vec{x}_{2}=p_{3}\vec{x}_{3}:=\vec{c}
, where
\vec{c}
is called the
canonical element
. The coordinate algebra

(2.1)

S:=k[x_{1},x_{2},x_{3}]/(x_{1}^{p_{1}}+x_{2}^{p_{2}}+x_{3}^{p_{3}})

of
\mathbb{X}
is then
\mathbb{L}
-graded with
\deg(x_{i})=\vec{x}_{i}
for each
i
. A quick way to arrive at the category
\mathrm{coh}\textrm{-}\mathbb{X}
of coherent sheaves on
\mathbb{X}
is by forming the Serre quotient

\mathrm{coh}\textrm{-}\mathbb{X}:=\frac{\text{mod}^{\mathbb{L}}S}{\text{mod}^{\mathbb{L}}_{0}S},

where
\text{mod}^{\mathbb{L}}S
(
resp.

\text{mod}^{\mathbb{L}}_{0}S
) is the category of finitely generated
\mathbb{L}
-graded modules over
S
(
resp.
those of finite length).

The category
\mathrm{coh}\textrm{-}\mathbb{X}
satisfies the Serre duality of the form

D\mathrm{Ext}^{1}(X,Y)\cong\mathrm{Hom}(Y,X(\vec{\omega}))

functorially in
X,Y\in\mathrm{coh}\textrm{-}\mathbb{X}
,
where
\vec{\omega}=\vec{c}-\sum_{i=1}^{3}\vec{x}_{i}
is called the
dualizing element
in
\mathbb{L}
. This implies
the existence of almost split sequences in
\mathrm{coh}\textrm{-}\mathbb{X}
with the
Auslander–Reiten translation
\tau
given by the degree shift with

\vec{\omega}
.
By
\mathrm{vect}\textrm{-}\mathbb{X}
we denote the full subcategory of the category
\mathrm{coh}\textrm{-}\mathbb{X}
formed by all vector bundles, that is, all locally free sheaves.

Denote by
p={\rm l.c.m.}(p_{1},p_{2},p_{3})
. There is a group homomorphism
\delta\colon\mathbb{L}(p_{1},p_{2},p_{3})\rightarrow\mathbb{Z}
given by
\delta(\vec{x}_{i})=\frac{p}{p_{i}}
for
1\leq i\leq 3
.
The complexity of
\mathrm{coh}\textrm{-}\mathbb{X}
(or
\mathrm{vect}\textrm{-}\mathbb{X}
) with regard to the classification of indecomposable objects is largely determined by the
(orbifold) Euler characteristic
of
\mathbb{X}
, given by the expression

\chi_{\mathbb{X}}=-\frac{1}{p}\delta(\vec{\omega})=2-\sum_{i=1}^{3}(1-\frac{1}{p_{i}}).

Each element
\vec{x}
in
\mathbb{L}
has the normal form
\vec{x}=\sum_{i=1}^{3}l_{i}\vec{x}_{i}+l\vec{c}
, where
0\leq l_{i}\leq p_{i}-1
for each
i
and
l\in\mathbb{Z}
. Up to isomorphisms, the line bundles in
\mathrm{coh}\textrm{-}\mathbb{X}
are given by the system
\mathcal{L}
of twisted structure sheaves
{\mathcal{O}}(\vec{x})
with
\vec{x}\in\mathbb{L}
.
The Auslander–Reiten translation
\tau
acts on
\mathcal{L}
, and the index
[\mathbb{L}:\mathbb{Z}\vec{\omega}]
counts the number of
\tau
-orbits of line bundles.

There is a canonical tilting sheaf
T_{\text{can}}=\bigoplus_{0\leq\vec{x}\leq\vec{c}}{\mathcal{O}}(\vec{x})
in
\mathrm{coh}\textrm{-}\mathbb{X}
, whose endomorphism algebra is isomorphic to the canonical algebra
C(p_{1},p_{2},p_{3})
in the sense of Ringel
[
28
]
. Consequently, the Grothendieck group
K_{0}(\mathbb{X})
of
\mathrm{coh}\textrm{-}\mathbb{X}
is the free abelian group generated by the isomorphism classes
[{\mathcal{O}}(\vec{x})]
for
0\leq\vec{x}\leq\vec{c}
. The
determinant
function on
K_{0}(\mathbb{X})
(or on
\mathrm{coh}\textrm{-}\mathbb{X}
) is defined via
\det({\mathcal{O}}(\vec{x}))=\vec{x}
, while the
degree
function is determined by
\deg({\mathcal{O}}(\vec{x}))=\delta(\vec{x})
and the
rank
function is determined by
\text{rank}({\mathcal{O}}(\vec{x}))=1
, for any
\vec{x}\in\mathbb{L}
.

2.2.
\mathcal{L}
-exact structure on
\mathrm{vect}\textrm{-}\mathbb{X}

By
[
16
]
, the category
\CM^{\mathbb{L}}S
of maximal Cohen–Macaulay
\mathbb{L}
-graded modules over
S
is equivalent to the category
\mathrm{vect}\textrm{-}\mathbb{X}
of vector bundles, which induces a distinguished exact structure on
\mathrm{vect}\textrm{-}\mathbb{X}
. More precisely, an exact sequence

\textstyle{0\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{E_{1}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{E_{2}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{E_{3}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{0}
in
\mathrm{vect}\textrm{-}\mathbb{X}
is called
distinguished exact
if

\textstyle{0\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{\mathrm{Hom}(L,E_{1})\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{\mathrm{Hom}(L,E_{2})\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{\mathrm{Hom}(L,E_{3})\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{0}

is an exact sequence for any line bundle
L
.

Recall that an exact category in the sense of Quillen is called a
Frobenius category
if it has enough projective objects and injective objects, and the projectives and the injectives coincide.
Under the distinguished exact structure,
\mathrm{vect}\textrm{-}\mathbb{X}
becomes a Frobenius category with indecomposable projective-injectives the system of all line bundles
\mathcal{L}
.
Therefore, the corresponding stable category
\underline{\mathrm{vect}}\textrm{-}\mathbb{X}:=\mathrm{vect}\textrm{-}\mathbb{X}/[\mathcal{L}]
is a triangulated category, see
[
8
]
. Here,
[\mathcal{L}]
denotes the two-sided ideal of morphisms in
\mathrm{vect}\textrm{-}\mathbb{X}
factoring through
\text{add}(\mathcal{L})
.

2.3.
\tau^{\mathbb{Z}}{\mathcal{O}}
-exact structure on
\mathrm{vect}\textrm{-}\mathbb{X}

For
\chi_{\mathbb{X}}\neq 0
, by restricting the grading of
S
to the subgroup
\mathbb{Z}\vec{\omega}
, we obtain a graded surface singularity
R=\bigoplus_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}R_{n}
, where
R_{n}=S_{n\vec{\omega}}
, see
[
7
]
for details. This algebra
R
turns out to be isomorphic–as a
\mathbb{Z}
-graded algebra–either to the algebra of invariants of the action of a binary polyhedral group on the polynomial algebra
\mathbb{C}[X,Y]
(for
\chi_{\mathbb{X}}>0
), or else to the algebra of entire automorphic forms with regard to an action of a Fuchsian group of the first kind on the upper complex half plane (for
\chi_{\mathbb{X}}<0
), see for instance
[
17
]
.

The category
\CM^{\mathbb{Z}}R
of maximal Cohen–Macaulay
\mathbb{Z}
-graded modules over
R
is equivalent to the category
\mathrm{vect}\textrm{-}\mathbb{X}
of vector bundles, which induces a
\tau^{\mathbb{Z}}{\mathcal{O}}
-exact structure on
\mathrm{vect}\textrm{-}\mathbb{X}
. Here,
{\mathcal{O}}:={\mathcal{O}}_{\mathbb{X}}
denotes the structure sheaf over
\mathbb{X}
,
\tau
is the Auslander-Reiten translation of
\mathrm{coh}\textrm{-}\mathbb{X}
, and
\tau^{\mathbb{Z}}{\mathcal{O}}
denotes the
\tau
-orbit of
{\mathcal{O}}
. More precisely, an exact sequence
\textstyle{0\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{E_{1}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{E_{2}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{E_{3}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{0}
in
\mathrm{vect}\textrm{-}\mathbb{X}
is called
\tau^{\mathbb{Z}}{\mathcal{O}}
-exact
if

\textstyle{0\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{\mathrm{Hom}(\tau^{n}{\mathcal{O}},E_{1})\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{\mathrm{Hom}(\tau^{n}{\mathcal{O}},E_{2})\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{\mathrm{Hom}(\tau^{n}{\mathcal{O}},E_{3})\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{0}

is an exact sequence for any
n\in\mathbb{Z}
.

Under
\tau^{\mathbb{Z}}{\mathcal{O}}
-exact structure,
\mathrm{vect}\textrm{-}\mathbb{X}
also becomes a Frobenius category, whose indecomposable projective-injectives are the
\tau
-orbit
\tau^{\mathbb{Z}}{\mathcal{O}}
of the structure sheaf.
Therefore, the corresponding stable category
\underline{\mathrm{vect}}^{\mathbb{Z}}\textrm{-}\mathbb{X}:=\mathrm{vect}\textrm{-}\mathbb{X}/[\tau^{\mathbb{Z}}{\mathcal{O}}]
is also a triangulated category.

2.4.
Nakayama algebra and Nakayama category

A finite dimensional algebra over
k
is called a
Nakayama algebra
if its indecomposable projective or injective modules are uniserial, that is, have a unique composition series.
A very natural class of such algebras is formed by the algebras
N_{n}(r)
, given as the path algebra of the equioriented quiver

\textstyle{1}
\textstyle{\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces 2}
\scriptstyle{x}
\textstyle{\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces 3}
\scriptstyle{x}
\textstyle{\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\cdots}
\scriptstyle{x}
\textstyle{\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces n-1}
\scriptstyle{x}
\textstyle{\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces n}
\scriptstyle{x}

of type
\mathbb{A}_{n}
subject to all relations
x^{r}=0
.
The representation theory of the algebras
N_{n}(r)
is well-understood. This changes drastically if one asks for a description of the bounded derived category
\mathcal{N}_{n}(r):=\mathrm{D}^{b}(N_{n}(r))
, which is called a
Nakayama category
in this paper. The following four types of Nakayama categories will be considered later.

For a triple
a,b,c
, the symbols
[a,b,c],(a,b,c),\langle a,b,c]
and
\langle a,b,c\rangle
each denotes the derived equivalence type of some triangulated category, called of
module type
, of
sheaf type
, of
Fuchsian type
or of
triangle type
, respectively.

-

[a,b,c]
: denotes the type of the bounded derived category of the hereditary star quiver with three branches of length
a,b,c
respectively.

-

(a,b,c)
: denotes the type of the bounded derived category of coherent sheaves
D^{b}(\mathrm{coh}\textrm{-}\mathbb{X})
for the weighted projective line
\mathbb{X}:=\mathbb{X}(a,b,c)
.

-

\langle a,b,c]
: denotes the type of the stable category of vector bundles
\underline{\mathrm{vect}}^{\mathbb{Z}}\textrm{-}\mathbb{X}
for the weighted projective line
\mathbb{X}:=\mathbb{X}(a,b,c)
.

-

\langle a,b,c\rangle
: denotes the type of the stable category of vector bundles
\underline{\mathrm{vect}}\textrm{-}\mathbb{X}

for the weighted projective line
\mathbb{X}:=\mathbb{X}(a,b,c)
.

We say that a Hom-finite triangulated category
\mathcal{T}
is
derived hereditary
if
\mathcal{T}
is triangle equivalent to the category
\mathrm{D}^{b}(\mathrm{mod}\textrm{-}H)
, where
H
is a finite dimensional hereditary
k
-algebra. More generally, we say that
\mathcal{T}
is
piecewise hereditary
if there exists a Hom-finite hereditary abelian
k
-category
\mathcal{H}
such that
\mathcal{T}
is triangle equivalent to the category
\mathrm{D}^{b}(\mathcal{H})
. By a result of Happel
[
10
]
, a piecewise hereditary
k
-category
\mathcal{T}
with a tilting object is derived hereditary or is triangle equivalent to the bounded derived category
\mathrm{D}^{b}(\mathrm{coh}\textrm{-}\mathbb{X})
of coherent sheaves on a weighted projective line. Piecewise hereditary categories with a tilting object, equivalently piecewise hereditary
k
-algebras, have been investigated, for instance, in
[
11
,
12
,
13
]
.

2.5.
One-point extension of an algebra

We recall the concept of a one-point extension of an algebra
\Lambda
. Let
M\in\mathrm{mod}\textrm{-}\Lambda
. Then the one-point extension of
\Lambda
by
M
is
denoted by
\Lambda[M]
and defined as the triangular matrix ring

\Lambda[M]=\left[\begin{array}[]{cc}\Lambda&M\\
0&k\end{array}\right],

with multiplication given by

\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}\lambda&\alpha\\
0&c\end{array}\right)\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}\lambda^{\prime}&\alpha^{\prime}\\
0&c^{\prime}\end{array}\right)=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}\lambda\lambda^{\prime}&\lambda\alpha^{\prime}+\alpha c\\
0&cc^{\prime}\end{array}\right)

for
\lambda,\lambda^{\prime}\in\Lambda,\alpha,\alpha^{\prime}\in M
and
c,c^{\prime}\in k
. For details we refer to
[
28
]
.

We emphasize that a derived equivalence between two algebras can be extended to one-point extensions, see
[
2
]
. More precisely, assume there is an equivalence
\textstyle{D^{b}(\Lambda)\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\simeq}
\textstyle{D^{b}(\Lambda^{\prime}),}
which sends
M\in\mathrm{mod}\textrm{-}\Lambda
to
M^{\prime}\in\mathrm{mod}\textrm{-}\Lambda^{\prime}
, then
D^{b}(\Lambda[M])\simeq D^{b}(\Lambda^{\prime}[M^{\prime}])
.
As applications to Nakayama algebras, we obtain that
N_{n+1}(r)=N_{n}(r)[I_{n+2-r}]
is derived equivalent to
N_{n}(r)[P_{n+2-r}]
, where
I_{n+2-r}
(resp.
P_{n+2-r}
) is the projective (resp. injective)
N_{n}(r)
-module corresponding to the vertex
n+2-r
, see for example
[
11
]
.

The one-point extension of canonical algebras is an important subject in this paper. Let
P
be an indecomposable projective module over a canonical algebra
C=C(p_{1},p_{2},p_{3})
.
The one-point extension
A=C[P]
is
called an
extended canonical algebra
. It does not matter which indecomposable projective one takes for the one-point extension. These algebras all happen to be derived equivalent. Moreover, their bounded derived categories are equivalent to the stable category
\underline{\mathrm{vect}}^{\mathbb{Z}}\textrm{-}\mathbb{X}
for the weighted projective line
\mathbb{X}=\mathbb{X}(p_{1},p_{2},p_{3})
when
\frac{1}{p_{1}}+\frac{1}{p_{2}}+\frac{1}{p_{3}}<1
.

2.6.
Perpendicular category

Recall from
[
30
]
that for any
\Lambda
module
M
, the full subcategory

M^{\perp}:=\{X\in\mathrm{mod}\textrm{-}\Lambda\,|\,\mathrm{Hom}_{\Lambda}(M,X)=0=\mathrm{Ext}^{1}_{\Lambda}(M,X)\}

of
\mathrm{mod}\textrm{-}\Lambda
is called the
right perpendicular category
of
M
. For example, the right perpendicular category
P_{n}^{\perp}
of
\mathrm{mod}\textrm{-}N_{n}(r)
with respect to the last indecomposable projective module
P_{n}
is equivalent to
\mathrm{mod}\textrm{-}N_{n-1}(r)
. We refer to
[
7
]
for the perpendicular category of a hereditary abelian category.

For a triangulated Hom-finite
k
-category
\mathcal{T}
, we have the right perpendicular category
{\{E_{1},\ldots,E_{r}\}}^{\bot}
with regard to an exceptional sequence
(E_{1},\ldots,E_{r})
in
\mathcal{T}
, which is a triangulated subcategory of
\mathcal{T}
. The full embedding
{\{E_{1},\ldots,E_{r}\}}^{\bot}\hookrightarrow\mathcal{T}
then has a left adjoint, see
[
3
]
.

Recall that there is a canonical tilting sheaf in
\mathrm{coh}\textrm{-}\mathbb{X}
consisting of line bundles for
\mathbb{X}=\mathbb{X}(p_{1},p_{2},p_{3})
, whose endomorphism algebra is isomorphic to the canonical algebra
C=C(p_{1},p_{2},p_{3})
. Therefore, we have an equivalence
D^{b}(\mathrm{coh}\textrm{-}\mathbb{X})\simeq D^{b}(\mathrm{mod}\textrm{-}C)
. Since the group
\mathbb{L}
acts on line bundles transitively, the right perpendicular category of
D^{b}(\mathrm{coh}\textrm{-}\mathbb{X})
with respect to any line bundle is equivalent to bounded derived category of the hereditary star quiver with three branches of length
p_{1},p_{2},p_{3}
respectively, hence of type
[p_{1},p_{2},p_{3}]
.

2.7.
Coxeter polynomial

Let
\{P(i)|1\leq i\leq n\}
be a complete set of representatives from the isomorphism classes of the indecomposable projective
\Lambda
-modules.
The
Cartan matrix

C=C_{\Lambda}
is defined to be the integer-valued
n\times n
-matrix with entries

c_{ij}=\textup{dim}_{k}\textup{Hom}_{\Lambda}(P(i),P(j)).

The
Coxeter matrix

\Phi_{\Lambda}
is defined to be the matrix

\Phi_{\Lambda}=-C^{-t}C.

The characteristic polynomial
\chi_{\Lambda}
of
\Phi_{\Lambda}
:

\chi_{\Lambda}=\textup{det}(\lambda E_{n}-\Phi_{\Lambda})

is called the
Coxeter polynomial
of
\Lambda
, where
E_{n}
is the identity matrix of size
n\times n
.
The minimal number
m
such that
\Phi_{\Lambda}^{m}=E_{n}
is called the
Coxeter number
of
\Lambda
.

The Coxeter polynomial plays an important role for understanding the structure of the module category and its bounded derived category for an algebra. We refer to
[
29
,
9
,
18
,
22
]
for properties of Coxeter polynomials.

For the Nakayama algebra
N_{n}(r)
, we denote its Cartan matrix by
\Phi_{(n,r)}
and its Coxeter polynomial by
\chi_{(n,r)}
. Then by direct computations, we have

-

\chi_{(17,8)}=(\lambda+1)(\lambda^{16}+\lambda^{8}+1)
;

-

\chi_{(16,3)}=(\lambda+1)(\lambda^{6}-\lambda^{3}+1)(\lambda^{9}+1)
;

-

\chi_{(15,6)}=\chi_{(15,4)}=(\lambda+1)(\lambda^{8}+\lambda^{4}+1)(\lambda^{6}+1)
;

-

\chi_{(15,5)}=(\lambda+1)(\lambda^{4}+1)(\lambda^{5}+1)^{2}
;

-

\chi_{(14,7)}=(\lambda+1)(\lambda^{6}-\lambda^{3}+1)(\lambda^{7}+1)
.

Consequently, the Coxeter numbers for
N_{(17,8)},N_{(16,3)},N_{(15,6)},N_{(15,5)},N_{(15,4)},N_{(14,7)}

are given by
24,18,12,40,12,126
respectively.

3.
Nakayama categories associated to a tilting complex

In this section, we first construct a tilting complex in the category
\mathrm{coh}\textrm{-}\mathbb{X}
of coherent sheaves of weight type
(2,3,r)
with
r\geq 4
, whose endomorphism algebra is isomorphic to the Nakayama algebra
N_{r+4}(r)
. Then by the technique of one-point extension, we show that the Nakayama algebras
N_{r+5}(r)\,(r\geq 7)
and
N_{r+6}(r)\,(r\geq 6)
are of Fuchsian type; while by considering the perpendicular subcategories, we obtain that
N_{r+3}(r)
and
N_{r+2}(r)
for
r\geq 3
are of module type.

The following result is essential in this section. Recall that the Serre functor of
\mathrm{D}^{b}(\mathrm{coh}\textrm{-}\mathbb{X})
is denoted by
{\mathbb{S}}=\tau[1]
.

Proposition 3.1
.

Let
\mathbb{X}
be a weighted projective line of weight type
(2,3,r)
with
r\geq 4
.
Then the system

(3.1)

\displaystyle\begin{split}{\mathbb{S}}^{-1}(&\mathcal{O}((r-4)\vec{x}_{3}))\to{\mathbb{S}}^{-1}(\mathcal{O}((r-3)\vec{x}_{3}))\to{\mathbb{S}}^{-1}(S_{20})\to\\
&\mathcal{O}\to\mathcal{O}(\vec{x}_{3})\to\cdots\to\mathcal{O}((r-4)\vec{x}_{3})\to\mathcal{O}((r-3)\vec{x}_{3})\to S_{20}\to{\mathbb{S}}\mathcal{O}\to{\mathbb{S}}(\mathcal{O}(\vec{x}_{3}))\end{split}

forms a tilting complex in
\mathrm{D}^{b}(\mathrm{coh}\textrm{-}\mathbb{X})
whose endomorphism algebra is isomorphic to the Nakayama algebra
N_{r+4}(r)
.
Consequently,
N_{r+4}(r)
is derived equivalent to
\mathrm{coh}\textrm{-}\mathbb{X}
.

Proof.

Set

Y_{0}=\bigoplus\limits_{k=0}^{r-3}\mathcal{O}(k\vec{x}_{3}),\quad Y_{1}={\mathbb{S}}(\mathcal{O}\oplus\mathcal{O}(\vec{x}_{3})),\quad Y_{-1}={\mathbb{S}}^{-1}(\mathcal{O}((r-4)\vec{x}_{3})\oplus\mathcal{O}((r-3)\vec{x}_{3})).

We only need to show that

T=Y_{-1}\oplus{\mathbb{S}}^{-1}(S_{20})\oplus Y_{0}\oplus S_{20}\oplus Y_{1}

forms a tilting complex in
\mathrm{D}^{b}(\mathrm{coh}\textrm{-}\mathbb{X})
.

For any line bundle
L
and any element
\vec{y}\in\mathbb{L}
,
it is well-known that
L\oplus L(\vec{y})
is extension-free if and only if
-\vec{c}\leq\vec{y}\leq\vec{c}
, hence each
Y_{i}
is extension-free for
-1\leq i\leq 1
.
For
0\leq a<b\leq r-3
, by Serre duality we have

\mathrm{Hom}_{D^{b}(\mathrm{coh}\textrm{-}\mathbb{X})}({\mathcal{O}}(a\vec{x}_{3}),{\mathbb{S}}({\mathcal{O}}(b\vec{x}_{3})))=D\mathrm{Hom}_{D^{b}(\mathrm{coh}\textrm{-}\mathbb{X})}({\mathcal{O}}(b\vec{x}_{3}),{\mathcal{O}}(a\vec{x}_{3}))=0.

Moreover, since
\mathrm{coh}\textrm{-}\mathbb{X}
is a hereditary category, we have
\mathrm{Hom}_{D^{b}(\mathrm{coh}\textrm{-}\mathbb{X})}(X,{\mathbb{S}}^{k}Y)=0
for any
k\geq 2
and
X,Y\in\mathrm{coh}\textrm{-}\mathbb{X}
. Then by using Lemma
A.1
and Lemma
A.2
, we obtain that
T
is extension-free.

Note that the number of indecomposable direct summands of
T
coincides with the rank of the Grothendieck group of
\mathrm{D}^{b}(\mathrm{coh}\textrm{-}\mathbb{X})
, hence
T
is a tilting complex in
\mathrm{D}^{b}(\mathrm{coh}\textrm{-}\mathbb{X})
. It is straightforward to show that the endomorphism algebra of
T
is isomorphic to
N_{r+4}(r)
. Then the proof is finished.
∎

Remark 3.2
.

Let
\Psi
be the derived equivalence induced by the tilting complex
T
in (
3.1
):

(3.2)

\textstyle{\Psi:\mathrm{D}^{b}(\mathrm{coh}\textrm{-}\mathbb{X}(2,3,r))\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\simeq}
\textstyle{\mathrm{D}^{b}(N_{r+4}(r)).}

Then each indecomposable projective
N_{r+4}(r)
-module
P_{i}
corresponds to the direct summand
T_{i}
of
T
, and each indecomposable injective module
I_{i}={\mathbb{S}}(P_{i})
corresponds to
{\mathbb{S}}(T_{i})
. By using projective resolution, we obtain that the simple
N_{r+4}(r)
-module
S_{i}
(for
5\leq i\leq r
) corresponds to the torsion sheaf
S_{3,i-4}
in
\mathrm{coh}\textrm{-}\mathbb{X}(2,3,r)
.

Moreover, the functions defined on
\mathrm{D}^{b}(\mathrm{coh}\textrm{-}\mathbb{X}(2,3,r))
or
K_{0}(\mathrm{coh}\textrm{-}\mathbb{X})
, such as rank and degree functions, can take values for
N_{r+4}(r)
-modules in a natural way. For example, the rank of projective or injective
N_{r+4}(r)
-modules are given by

{\rm{rank}}(P_{i})=-{\rm{rank}}(I_{i})=\left\{\begin{array}[]{lll}-1,&&i=1,2,r+3,r+4;\\
0,&&i=3,r+2;\\
1,&&4\leq i\leq r+1;\end{array}\right.

while the rank data for the simple modules are

{\rm{rank}}(S_{i})=\left\{\begin{array}[]{lll}-1,&&i=1,r+1,r+2;\\
1,&&i=3,4,r+4;\\
0,&&{\text{otherwise}}.\end{array}\right.

As a consequence, we can obtain the following result due to Happel and Seidel.

Corollary 3.3

(
[
11
]
)
.

The following statements hold.

(1)

For
r\geq 3
,
N_{r+3}(r)
is derived hereditary of type
[2,3,r]
.

(2)

For
r\geq 3

N_{r+2}(r)

[2,3,r-1]

(3)

For

Proof.

For the first two statements, we recall that there exists a tilting complex

Note that

is derived hereditary of type
[2,3,r-1]

For (3), observe that

is derived equivalent to

by

(

Remark 3.4
.

During the proof of the statement (3) in the above corollary, we obtain that the projective

By using the technique of one-point extension for

twice, we find a new family of Nakayama algebras of Fuchsian type.

Proposition 3.5
.

For
r\geq 6
,
N_{r+6}(r)
has Fuchsian type
{\langle 2,3,r+1]}
.

Proof.

During the proof, we denote by
A=N_{r+6}(r)
for convenience.

Obviously, the injective module
\bigoplus_{i=1}^{r+6}I_{i}
is a tilting module in
\mathrm{mod}\textrm{-}A
. Note that
I_{r+6}
and
\tau I_{r+6}
are both simple

Hence, we obtain an APR tilting

T=(\bigoplus_{i=1}^{r+5}I_{i})\oplus\tau I_{r+6}.

Let
\Gamma=\mathrm{End}_{A}(T)
. Then we have
D^{b}(A)\simeq D^{b}(\Gamma)
.
Set

T^{\prime}=T\backslash{I_{r+5}}=(\bigoplus_{i=1}^{r+4}I_{i})\oplus\tau I_{r+6}\quad\text{and}\quad T^{\prime\prime}=T^{\prime}\backslash{\tau I_{r+6}}=\bigoplus_{i=1}^{r+4}I_{i}.

Denote by

\Gamma^{\prime}=\mathrm{End}_{A}(T^{\prime})
and
\Gamma^{\prime\prime}=\mathrm{End}_{A}(T^{\prime\prime})
, respectively. Then we have

\Gamma=\Gamma^{\prime}[M]
and
\Gamma^{\prime}=\Gamma^{\prime\prime}[N]
, where
M=\mathrm{Hom}_{A}(T^{\prime},I_{r+5})
and
N=\mathrm{Hom}_{A}(T^{\prime\prime},\tau I_{r+6})
.

Observe that
\Gamma^{\prime\prime}\cong N_{r+4}(r)
, which yields an equivalence

\textstyle{\Phi^{\prime\prime}:D^{b}(\Gamma^{\prime\prime})\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\simeq}
\textstyle{\mathrm{D}^{b}(\mathrm{coh}\textrm{-}\mathbb{X}(2,3,r)),}

sending projective
\Gamma^{\prime\prime}
-modules
\mathrm{Hom}_{A}(T^{\prime\prime},I_{i})
(for
1\leq i\leq r+4
) to the indecomposable direct summands of the tilting complex (
3.1
).

For
1\leq i\leq r+4
,
\mathrm{Hom}_{A}(I_{i},\tau I_{r+6})\neq 0
if and only if
i=6
. Hence
N
is a simple
\Gamma^{\prime\prime}
-module (associated to the vertex

in
\mathrm{coh}\textrm{-}\mathbb{X}(2,3,r)
under

by Remark

\textstyle{\Phi^{\prime}:D^{b}(\Gamma^{\prime})=D^{b}(\Gamma^{\prime\prime}[N])\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\simeq}
\textstyle{\mathrm{D}^{b}(\mathrm{coh}\textrm{-}\mathbb{X}(2,3,r+1)).}

Moreover, we have the following commutative diagram:

\textstyle{D^{b}(\Gamma^{\prime\prime})\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\iota_{1}}
\scriptstyle{\Phi^{\prime\prime}}
\scriptstyle{\simeq}
\textstyle{D^{b}(\Gamma^{\prime})\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\simeq}
\scriptstyle{\Phi^{\prime}}
\textstyle{\mathrm{D}^{b}(\mathrm{coh}\textrm{-}\mathbb{X}(2,3,r))\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\iota_{2}}
\textstyle{\mathrm{D}^{b}(\mathrm{coh}\textrm{-}\mathbb{X}(2,3,r+1)),}

where
\iota_{1}
(
resp.

\iota_{2}

(
resp.

S_{3,2}

Let
M^{\prime}=\mathrm{Hom}_{A}(T^{\prime\prime},I_{r+5})\in\mathrm{mod}\textrm{-}\Gamma^{\prime\prime}

\iota_{1}(M^{\prime})=M

4.
Classification of Piecewise hereditary Nakayama categories

In this section, we provide a new proof of the classification result for the Nakayama categories
\mathcal{N}_{n}(r):=D^{b}(N_{n}(r))
of piecewise hereditary type, which have been obtained by Happel and Seidel in
[
11
]
.

Let us first introduce the extended version of the Happel-Seidel symmetry, which was first encountered in
[
11
]
and plays an important role in the study of Nakayama algebras.

Proposition 4.1
.

Assume
a,b\geq 2
. Put
n=(a-1)(b-1)
. Then

(1)

\mathcal{N}_{n}(a)\simeq\mathcal{N}_{n}(b)
;

(2)

\mathcal{N}_{n-1}(a)\simeq\mathcal{N}_{n-1}(b)
;

(3)

\mathcal{N}_{n+1}(a)\simeq\mathcal{N}_{n+1}(b)
.

Proof.

The first statement has been proved in
[
16
, Theorem 6.11]
, by observing that both categories
\mathcal{N}_{n}(a)
and
\mathcal{N}_{n}(b)
are equivalent to the stable category
\underline{\mathrm{vect}}\textrm{-}\mathbb{X}(2,a,b)
. For the proof of the other two statements, we recall from
[
16
]
that

(4.1)

T=\bigoplus\limits_{i=0}^{b-2}\bigoplus\limits_{j=0}^{a-2}\mathbb{S}^{i}(E\langle j\vec{x}_{2}\rangle)

is a tilting object in the stable category
\underline{\mathrm{vect}}\textrm{-}\mathbb{X}(2,a,b)
with endomorphism algebra
N_{n}(a)
, where
\mathbb{S}=\tau[1]
, and
E\langle j\vec{x}_{2}\rangle
(for
0\leq j\leq a-2
) is the extension bundle determined by the non-split exact sequence

\textstyle{0\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{\mathcal{O}(\vec{\omega})\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{E\langle j\vec{x}_{2}\rangle\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{\mathcal{O}(j\vec{x}_{2})\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{0.}

According to
[
16
, Proposition 6.6]
, we know that
E\langle(a-2)\vec{x}_{2}\rangle
is an Auslander bundle, and
\mathbb{S}(F)=\tau F[1]=F(\vec{\omega}+\vec{x}_{1})
for any extension bundle
F
. It follows that the last member
\mathbb{S}^{b-2}(E\langle(a-2)\vec{x}_{2}\rangle)
of
T
is an Auslander bundle, hence
\mathcal{N}_{n-1}(a)
is equivalent to the right perpendicular category of an Auslander bundle in
\underline{\mathrm{vect}}\textrm{-}\mathbb{X}(2,a,b)
. By exchanging the weights
a
and
b
in
(2,a,b)
(using
\underline{\mathrm{vect}}\textrm{-}\mathbb{X}(2,a,b)\simeq\underline{\mathrm{vect}}\textrm{-}\mathbb{X}(2,b,a)
), we know that
\mathcal{N}_{n-1}(b)
is equivalent to the right perpendicular category of
\mathbb{S}^{a-2}(E\langle(b-2)\vec{x}_{3}\rangle)
, which is also an Auslander bundle. Note that there exists an automorphism (induced by degree shift) of
\underline{\mathrm{vect}}\textrm{-}\mathbb{X}(2,a,b)
exchanging two given Auslander bundles. It follows that
\mathcal{N}_{n-1}(a)\simeq\mathcal{N}_{n-1}(b)
.

For the statement (3), recall that
N_{n+1}(a)=N_{n}(a)[I_{n+2-a}]
, which is derived equivalent to
N_{n}(a)[P_{n+2-a}]
by
[
2
]
. Here,
I_{n+2-a}
(resp.
P_{n+2-a}
) is the injective (resp. projective)

-module corresponding to the vertex
n+2-a
. Moreover, by the explicit realization of
N_{n}(a)
in
\underline{\mathrm{vect}}\textrm{-}\mathbb{X}(2,a,b)
as in (

),
P_{n+2-a}
is given by the Auslander bundle
{\mathbb{S}}^{b-2}E
, (i.e.,
j=0
). Similar result holds for
N_{n+1}(b)
, that is,
N_{n+1}(b)
is derived equivalent to
N_{n}(b)[P_{n+2-b}]
, where

P_{n+2-b}
is given by the Auslander bundle
{\mathbb{S}}^{a-2}E
.

Note that
\mathcal{N}_{n}(a)\simeq\underline{\mathrm{vect}}\textrm{-}\mathbb{X}(2,a,b)\simeq\mathcal{N}_{n}(b)
by (1), and there exists an automorphism of
\underline{\mathrm{vect}}\textrm{-}\mathbb{X}(2,a,b)
exchanging two given Auslander bundles. It follows that there exists an equivalence
\textstyle{\mathcal{N}_{n}(a)\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\simeq}
\textstyle{\mathcal{N}_{n}(b)}

sending projective
N_{n}(a)
-module
P_{n+2-a}
to projective
N_{n}(b)
-module
P_{n+2-b}
.
Then by
[
2

, we obtain
N_{n+1}(a)
and
N_{n+1}(b)
are derived equivalent, that is,
\mathcal{N}_{n+1}(a)\simeq\mathcal{N}_{n+1}(b)
.
∎

Lemma 4.2
.

Assume that the Nakayama category
\mathcal{N}_{n}(r)
is piecewise hereditary. Then also
\mathcal{N}_{n-1}(r)
is piecewise hereditary.

Proof.

The indecomposable projective modules
P_{i}

(1\leq i\leq n)
over
N_{n}(r)
are exceptional objects in
\mathcal{N}_{n}(r)
. Since the right perpendicular category of
\mathcal{N}_{n}(r)
with regard to the last projective
P_{n}
is triangle equivalent to
\mathcal{N}_{n-1}(r)
, the assertion follows from
[
7
]
and
[
5

∎

Proposition 4.3
.

A Fuchsian singularity category cannot be piecewise hereditary.

Proof.

Following the pattern of
[
16
]
one deduces that the singularity category of a Fuchsian singularity, as studied in
[
23
]
, is triangle equivalent to the stable category of vector bundles
\underline{\mathrm{vect}}^{\mathbb{Z}}\textrm{-}\mathbb{X}
over a weighted projective line
\mathbb{X}
.
Since, by
[

\mathrm{vect}\textrm{-}\mathbb{X}

\underline{\mathrm{vect}}^{\mathbb{Z}}\textrm{-}\mathbb{X}

∎

5.

We assume

In order to classify all the Fuchsian Nakayama categories, we need to do further investigation on the stable category
\underline{\mathrm{vect}}^{\mathbb{Z}}\textrm{-}\mathbb{X}
of vector bundles in this section. After describing the projective covers and homomorphisms for line bundles, we finally construct tilting objects consisting of line bundles in
\underline{\mathrm{vect}}^{\mathbb{Z}}\textrm{-}\mathbb{X}
for certain

Recall that
\mathcal{L}
denotes the system of all line bundles in
\mathrm{coh}\textrm{-}\mathbb{X}

\tau
-orbits of
\mathcal{L}
is given by
[\mathbb{L}:\mathbb{Z}\vec{\omega}]=-\chi_{\mathbb{X}}\cdot\prod_{i=1}^{3}p_{i}

[

\tau
-orbits of
\mathcal{L}

(5.1)

\mathcal{S}:=\{\vec{x}\,|\,0\leq\vec{x}\leq n\vec{\omega}+\vec{c}\text{\ \ for\ any }n\geq 2\}.

Proposition 5.1
.

There exists a bijection between the
\tau
-orbits of line bundles in
\mathrm{coh}\textrm{-}\mathbb{X}
and

\mathcal{S}
.

Proof.

For any
\vec{x}\in\mathbb{L}
, by the assumption
\chi_{\mathbb{X}}<0
, i.e.,
\delta(\vec{\omega})>0
, there exists a maximal integer
m=m(\vec{x})
, such that
\mathrm{Hom}({\mathcal{O}}(m\vec{\omega}),{\mathcal{O}}(\vec{x}))\neq 0

\vec{x}-m\vec{\omega}\geq 0
and
\vec{x}-(m+n)\vec{\omega}\leq\vec{\omega}+\vec{c}
for any

\vec{x}-m\vec{\omega}\in\mathcal{S}
.
Therefore, the following map from the
\tau

\mathcal{L}/\langle\tau\rangle
to the set
\mathcal{S}
is well-defined

\varphi:\mathcal{L}/\langle\tau\rangle\to\mathcal{S};\quad{\mathcal{O}}(\vec{x})\mapsto\vec{x}-m(\vec{x})\vec{\omega}.

Obviously,
\varphi
is surjective. Moreover,
\varphi({\mathcal{O}}(\vec{x}))=\varphi({\mathcal{O}}(\vec{y}))
implies
{\mathcal{O}}(\vec{x})
and
{\mathcal{O}}(\vec{y})
belong to the same
\tau

\varphi
is injective.
This finishes the proof.

\begin{pmatrix}O_{(r-8)\times 8}\\
E_{8}\end{pmatrix}&\begin{pmatrix}-E_{r-1}\\
\varepsilon_{r-1}\end{pmatrix}\end{bmatrix},

where

denotes the identity matrix of size
n\times n

denotes the zero matrix of size
m\times n
, and
\varepsilon_{r-1}
denotes the row vector
(1,1,1,\cdots,1)

(r-1)
-entries.
By definition, the Coxeter polynomial
\chi_{(r+7,r)}

-row indices and

be lists of

-column indices of

.
For any

, the submatrix
\Delta({\bf{r}},{\bf{c}}_{i})

{\bf{r}}
and any column in the list

\Delta({\bf{r}},{\bf{c}}_{i})=\begin{pmatrix}J_{i}&0\\

where

J_{i}=\begin{pmatrix}\lambda&-1&0&\cdots&0\\

\vdots&\ddots&\ddots&\ddots&0\\
\vdots&&\ddots&\ddots&-1\\

\Delta^{\prime}({\bf{r}},{\bf{c}}_{i})

{\bf{r}}
and columns in the list

For

0&0&0&\lambda&-1&0&0&0\\

For

\displaystyle\textup{det}(\Delta)

\displaystyle=(-1)^{9+10+\cdots+(r-1)}\sum_{i=9}^{r}(-1)^{9+10+\cdots+\hat{i}+\cdots+r}\cdot\textup{det}(\Delta({\bf{r}},{\bf{c}}_{i}))\cdot\textup{det}(\Delta^{\prime}({\bf{r}},{\bf{c}}_{i}))

Appendix A A
Extension-free property

Let
\mathcal{D}
be a Hom-finite triangulated category with Serre functor
{\mathbb{S}}=\tau[1]
, where
\tau
is the Auslander-Reiten translation and
[1]
denotes the suspension functor of
\mathcal{D}
.
The Serre duality of
\mathcal{D}
is given by the following natural isomorphism

{\rm{Hom}}_{\mathcal{D}}(X,Y)\cong D{\rm{Hom}}_{\mathcal{D}}(Y,{\mathbb{S}}X),

which are functorial in
X,Y\in\mathcal{D}
.

Lemma A.1
.

Let
X\in\mathcal{D}
and
U=\bigoplus\limits_{k=0}^{m}{\mathbb{S}}^{k}X
for some
m\geq 0
. Then
U
is extension-free in
\mathcal{D}
if and only if
{\rm{Hom}}_{\mathcal{D}}(X,{\mathbb{S}}^{k}X[n])=0
for any
1\leq k\leq m+1
and any non-zero integer
n
.

Proof.

By definition,
U
is extension-free if and only if for any
n\neq 0
and
0\leq i,j\leq m
,
{\rm{Hom}}_{\mathcal{D}}({\mathbb{S}}^{i}X,{\mathbb{S}}^{j}X[n])=0
, or equivalently,
{\rm{Hom}}_{\mathcal{D}}(X,{\mathbb{S}}^{k}X[n])=0
for any
n\neq 0
and
-m\leq k\leq m
. For
-m\leq k\leq 0
, we have
{\rm{Hom}}_{\mathcal{D}}(X,{\mathbb{S}}^{k}X[n])\cong D{\rm{Hom}}_{\mathcal{D}}(X,{\mathbb{S}}^{1-k}X[-n])
by using Serre duality, where
1\leq 1-k\leq m+1
. This finishes the proof.
∎

Lemma A.2
.

Let
X,Y\in\mathcal{D}
and
U=\bigoplus\limits_{k=0}^{m}{\mathbb{S}}^{k}X
,
V=\bigoplus\limits_{k=0}^{r}{\mathbb{S}}^{k}Y
for some
m,r\geq 0
. Assume both of
U
and
V
are extension-free. Then
U\oplus V
is extension-free if and only if
{\rm{Hom}}_{\mathcal{D}}(X,{\mathbb{S}}^{k}Y[n])=0
for any
-m\leq k\leq r+1
and any non-zero integer
n
.

Proof.

By definition,
U\oplus V
is extension-free if and only if for any
n\neq 0
,
0\leq i\leq m
and
0\leq j\leq r
,
{\rm{Hom}}_{\mathcal{D}}({\mathbb{S}}^{i}X,{\mathbb{S}}^{j}Y[n])=0
and
{\rm{Hom}}_{\mathcal{D}}({\mathbb{S}}^{j}Y[n],{\mathbb{S}}^{i}X)=D{\rm{Hom}}_{\mathcal{D}}({\mathbb{S}}^{i}X,{\mathbb{S}}^{j+1}Y[n])=0
.
Equivalently,
{\rm{Hom}}_{\mathcal{D}}(X,{\mathbb{S}}^{k}Y[n])=0
for any
n\neq 0
and
-m\leq k\leq r+1
. This proves the result.
∎

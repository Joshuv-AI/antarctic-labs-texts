Quantum resetting in continuous measurement induced dynamics of a qubit
Varun Dubey, Raphael Chetrite, Abhishek Dhar

Abstract
We study the evolution of a two-state system that is monitored continuously but with interactions with the detector tuned so as to avoid the Zeno affect. The system is allowed to interact with a sequence of prepared probes. The post-interaction probe states are measured and this leads to a stochastic evolution of the system’s state vector, which can be described by a single angle variable. The system’s effective evolution consists of a deterministic drift and a stochastic resetting to a fixed state at a rate that depends on the instantaneous state vector. The detector readout is a counting process. We obtain analytic results for the distribution of number of detector events and the time-evolution of the probability distribution. Earlier work on this model found transitions in the form of the steady state on increasing the measurement rate. Here we study transitions seen in the dynamics. As a spin-off we obtain, for a general stochastic resetting process with diffusion, drift and position dependent jump rates, an exact and general solution for the evolution of the probability distribution.

Introduction

The problem of repeated measurements on quantum systems is of great interest in the context of monitoring and controlling its time evolution and in the context of answering questions such as that of the time of arrival. Of particular interest is the situation where a system is coupled to a probe and repeated measurements are performed on the probe. An obviously interesting question is as to what these measurements on the probe can tell us about the system. A number of recent experiments have looked at the trajectories of quantum systems subjected to repeated measurements

A quantum system that is continuously monitored via direct measurements remains frozen in its state. This is the well known quantum Zeno effect

[

Furthermore, when the interaction strength between the object system and the probes scales as
\tau^{-1/2}
, the reduced density matrix of the system evolves via a Lindblad equation.
Any given stochastic trajectory of the wavefunction corresponds to the system’s evolution for a particular outcome of the measurement sequence. Averaging over these outcomes corresponds to the case of blind measurements and the entire information of the system’s evolution is contained in the reduced density matrix.

The basis of the current work is the model described in Ref.

Note that usual Zeno effect refers to the phenomena whereby a system’s dynamics gets frozen as a result of continuous measurements on it. Interestingly, signatures of this freezing appear even at large but finite mesaurement strengths, with parts of the Hilbert space becoming inaccessible — this is referred to as Zeno effect appearing in stages.

Counting Statistics

A stochastic process is in general described in terms of its finite dimensional probability densities. Given an initial state

4.4
General formulation and explicit time Laplace transform solution for the transition probability

We consider here a more general set-up where the transition probability

P_{t}(\theta|\theta^{\prime})
, that the process pass from
\theta^{\prime}
at time

0

\theta

t

\displaystyle\partial_{t}P_{t}(\theta|\theta^{\prime})=L\left(\theta\right)P_{t}(\theta|\theta^{\prime})-\gamma(\theta)P_{t}(\theta|\theta^{\prime})+\mu(\theta)\left(\int_{0}^{2\pi}d\theta^{\prime\prime}\gamma(\theta^{\prime\prime})P_{t}(\theta^{\prime\prime}|\theta^{\prime})\right).

Here,

is a second (resp. first) order differential operator in
\theta

\theta
chosen from the probability distribution
\mu(\theta)
and the positive function
\gamma(\theta)
is the jump rate for escape from state
\theta
. The
associated master equation for the probability density
P(\theta,t)

is then

\displaystyle\partial_{t}P(\theta,t)=L\left(\theta\right)P(\theta,t)-\gamma(\theta)P(\theta,t)+\mu(\theta)\left(\int_{0}^{2\pi}d\theta^{\prime\prime}\gamma(\theta^{\prime\prime})P(\theta^{\prime\prime},t)\right).

(62)

Let us define the operators

\displaystyle L_{0}\left[f\right]\left(\theta\right)\equiv L\left(\theta\right)\left[f\right]\left(\theta\right)-\left\langle\mu,1\right\rangle\gamma(\theta)f(\theta),

(63)

\displaystyle L_{1}\left[f\right]\left(\theta\right)\equiv\mu(\theta)\left\langle\gamma,f\right\rangle,

(64)

for any function
f
on
\left[0,2\pi\right]
and where have used the following inner product definition:

\left\langle f,g\right\rangle=\int_{0}^{2\pi}d\theta f(\theta)g(\theta).

The master equation (
31
), and more generally the set-up of the previous section, is a particular case of this general theory when
L\left(\theta\right)[f]=-\partial_{\theta}[\Omega\left(\theta\right)f(\theta)]
,
\gamma\left(\theta\right)=\gamma\sin^{2}\left(\frac{\theta}{2}\right)

and the time Laplace transform is the resolvent

\displaystyle\left(\mathcal{\mathfrak{L}}P_{t}\right)(s)

\displaystyle\equiv\int_{0}^{\infty}dt\exp\left(-st\right)\exp\left(t\left(L_{0}+L_{1}\right)\right)=\left(s-L_{0}-L_{1}\right)^{-1}

(65)

\displaystyle=\left(s-L_{0}\right)^{-1}+\left(s-L_{0}\right)^{-1}L_{1}\left(s-L_{0}-L_{1}\right)^{-1}.

(66)

We thus find the auto-consistency relation

\left(\mathcal{\mathfrak{L}}P_{t}\right)(s)=\left(\mathcal{\mathfrak{L}}P_{t}^{(0)}\right)(s)+\left(\mathcal{\mathfrak{L}}P_{t}^{(0)}\right)(s)L_{1}\left(\mathcal{\mathfrak{L}}P_{t}\right)(s),

where
P_{t}^{(0)}=\exp\left(tL_{0}\right)
.
By plugging in this relation the expression for

(

\displaystyle\left(\mathcal{\mathfrak{L}}P_{t}\right)(s)(\theta|\theta^{\prime})=\left(\mathcal{\mathfrak{L}}P_{t}^{(0)}\right)(s)(\theta|\theta^{\prime})+\left\langle\left(\mathfrak{L}P_{t}^{(0)}\right)(s)\left(\theta|.\right),\mu(.)\right\rangle\left\langle\gamma(.),\left(\mathcal{\mathfrak{L}}P_{t}\right)(s)(.|\theta^{\prime})\right\rangle,

(67)

where
.
indicates the inner product integration variable.
Multiplying with
\gamma(\theta)
and integrating over
\theta
solves for the unknown last term in the above equation

\displaystyle\left\langle\gamma(.),\left(\mathcal{\mathfrak{L}}P_{t}\right)(s)(.|\theta^{\prime})\right\rangle=\frac{\left\langle\gamma(.),\left(\mathcal{\mathfrak{L}}P^{0}_{t}\right)(s)(.|\theta^{\prime})\right\rangle}{1-\left\langle\gamma(..),\left\langle\left(\mathfrak{L}P_{t}^{(0)}\right)(s)\left(..|.\right),\mu(.)\right\rangle\right\rangle}.

(68)

68

\displaystyle\left(\mathcal{\mathfrak{L}}P_{t}\right)(s)(\theta|\theta^{\prime})=\left(\mathcal{\mathfrak{L}}P_{t}^{(0)}\right)(s)(\theta|\theta^{\prime})+\frac{\left\langle\left(\mathfrak{L}P_{t}^{(0)}\right)(s)\left(\theta|.\right),\mu(.)\right\rangle\left\langle\gamma(.),\left(\mathcal{\mathfrak{L}}P^{0}_{t}\right)(s)(.|\theta^{\prime})\right\rangle}{1-\left\langle\gamma(..),\left\langle\left(\mathfrak{L}P_{t}^{(0)}\right)(s)\left(..|.\right),\mu(.)\right\rangle\right\rangle},

(69)

which expresses
\left(\mathcal{\mathfrak{L}}P_{t}\right)(s)
in terms of
\left(\mathcal{\mathfrak{L}}P_{t}^{(0)}\right)(s)
. So, in the case where the second is explicit, so is the first. It is instructive to write this equation in the time domain. To this end, we denote by
\bar{\gamma}(t)
the inverse Laplace transform of the l.h.s of Eq.
68
, which is simply

\displaystyle\bar{\gamma}_{t}(\theta^{\prime})=\int_{0}^{2\pi}d\theta^{\prime\prime}~\gamma(\theta^{\prime\prime})P_{t}(\theta^{\prime\prime}|\theta^{\prime}),

(70)

which is just the average of transition rate
\gamma(\theta_{t})
conditioned by the initial condition
\theta_{0}=\theta^{\prime}
. Then Eq.
69
in the time domain is given by

\displaystyle P_{t}(\theta|\theta^{\prime})=P^{0}_{t}(\theta|\theta^{\prime})+\int_{0}^{t}d\tau\langle P^{0}_{\tau}(\theta|.),\mu(.)\rangle\bar{\gamma}_{t-\tau}(\theta^{\prime}),

(71)

which we see has the same structure as the renewal equation in Eq. (
50
). The Eq. (
68
) gives the Laplace transform of
\bar{\gamma}_{t}
, and is then Eq. (
52
), and for our specific example we were able to compute

explicitly (from the inverse Laplace and also using a renewal approach). In general, we would have an explicit solution for

from Eq. (

), provided we are able to evaluate

explicitly from Eq. (
68
).

Spectral Analysis

The Fokker-Planck operator corresponding to Eq. (
31
) has interesting spectral properties which were pointed out in
[
19
]
. We wish to extend those studies and in particular, for the case
0<\lambda\leq 1
, we report some new results and some subtle features. One seeks solutions to Eq. (
31
) in the form
P(\theta,t)=\exp(2\gamma_{0}\,\nu\,t)\,f_{\nu}(\theta)
. This leads to the following eigenvalue problem for the operator
\mathcal{L}

\mathcal{L}f_{\nu}=(1+\lambda\sin\theta)\partial_{\theta}f_{\nu}+\lambda\left(2\cos\theta-1\right)\,f_{\nu}+2\lambda\delta(\theta-\pi)\int_{0}^{2\pi}\sin^{2}(\theta^{\prime}/2)f_{\nu}(\theta^{\prime})\,d\theta^{\prime}=\nu\,f_{\nu}.~~~

(72)

Noting that
\pi
and
-\pi
are identified, we integrate the above equation over a small interval across
\pi
. Assuming that
f_{\nu}(\theta)
has no divergence at
\pi
this then gives a discontinuity of
f_{\nu}
across
\pi
and we are led to the following equivalent set of equations:

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}f_{\nu}=(1+\lambda\sin\theta)\partial_{\theta}f_{\nu}+\lambda\left(2\cos\theta-1\right)\,f_{\nu}=\nu\,f_{\nu},

(73)

\displaystyle f_{\nu}(\pi-0)-f_{\nu}(\pi+0)=2\lambda\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}\sin^{2}\left(\theta/2\right)\,f_{\nu}(\theta)\,d\theta.

(74)

We also define an operator
\mathcal{L}_{0}
which satisfies Eq. (
73
) but with boundary conditions satisfying
f(\pi-0)=f(\pi+0)
.
The adjoint operator
\mathcal{L}_{0}^{\dagger}
acts on square integrable functions
g(\theta)
as

\mathcal{L}_{0}^{\dagger}g=-\left(1+\lambda\sin\theta\right)\partial_{\theta}g-2\lambda\sin^{2}\left(\theta/2\right)\,g,\quad g(\pi-0)=g(\pi+0).

(75)

Since
\mathcal{L}_{0}\mathcal{L}_{0}^{\dagger}\neq\mathcal{L}_{0}^{\dagger}\mathcal{L}_{0}
,
\mathcal{L}_{0}
is not a normal operator and it’s eigenfunctions do not form an orthonormal basis for the Hilbert space
\mathbb{L}^{2}[-\pi,\pi]
. The same holds for the operator pair
\mathcal{L}
,
\mathcal{L}^{\dagger}
. Because of the integral boundary condition in Eq. (
74
), one has

\mathcal{L}^{\dagger}h=-\left(1+\lambda\sin\theta\right)\partial_{\theta}h-2\lambda\sin^{2}\left(\theta/2\right)\,(h-h(\pi)),\quad h(\pi-0)=h(\pi+0).~~

(76)

We see below that the eigenfunctions of
\mathcal{L}_{0}
and
\mathcal{L}_{0}^{\dagger}
together form a bi-orthonormal basis.

6
Conclusion

We studied the dynamics of a qubit that is continuously monitored via measurements on a detector qubit with which it interacts strongly so as to avoid the zeno limit. For the special choice of system Hamiltonian and initial conditions that we considered here, the qubit state remains confined at all times on the
yz
plane of the Bloch sphere so that it can represented by a single angle variable. The state
|\psi(t)\rangle
follows a stochastic dynamics with drift and jump terms. We obtained various results for this dynamics. We summarize here our main findings:

•

We point out that the stochastic wavefunction dynamics can be naturally interpreted as a resetting process, with a resetting rate that depends on the instantaneous state. The strength of the resetting rate
\lambda
quantifies the strength of measurements.

•

We obtain exact results on the number of resetting events,
N_{t}
, in a specified time
t
. We show that the form of the time-dependence, of the mean number of events Eqs.
46
, has a transition at
\lambda=2
.

•

Using two different approaches, a renewal approach and one based on non-perturbative resolvent (or Green’s function) approach, we obtain the exact form of the probability distribution
P(\theta,t)
for the system to be in the quantum state,
|\theta\rangle=\left[{\begin{array}[]{c}\cos\left(\theta/2\right)\\
\rmi\sin\left(\theta/2\right)\end{array}}\right]
, at time
t
. At long times we recover the steady state form known from earlier studies. We show that as for the steady state, the time evolution has three different forms for the regimes
0\leq\lambda<1
,
\lambda=1
and
\lambda>1
.

•

For the cases
0\leq\lambda<1
and
\lambda=1
we evaluate the complete spectrum of the Fokker-Planck operator which forms a bi-orthonormal set. This provides another solution for the time evolution of
P(\theta,t)
, that is especially useful at long times.
A future study will explore the more difficult case
\lambda>1
.

We note that the average density matrix of the qubit is given by
\hat{\rho}(t)=\int_{0}^{2\pi}d\theta P(\theta,t)|\theta\rangle\langle\theta|
. However, this density matrix contains much less information about the system. For example, in the steady state we have
\hat{\rho}(t)\to(1/2)\hat{I}
, while the distribution of states
P_{\infty}(\theta)
is highly non-trivial. The mean number of detector clicks and the complete distribution
P(\theta,t)
is experimentally accessible using the methods of quantum tomography and our results could be experimentally verified. Finally, we hope that the connection between the stochastic Schrödinger equation and resetting dynamics, pointed out in this work, will lead to further useful results and insights in both areas.

Appendix A
Computation of survival probability

We use the notation introduced below Eq. (
22
). When
0\leq\lambda<1
, then
H_{\mathit{eff}}
admits the orthogonal decomposition

\frac{1}{2\beta}\left[\begin{array}[]{cc}c&c^{*}\\
-c^{*}&c\end{array}\right]\left[\begin{array}[]{cc}-(c^{*})^{2}&0\\
0&c^{2}\end{array}\right]\left[\begin{array}[]{cc}c&-c^{*}\\
c^{*}&c\end{array}\right]

where
c=\exp\left[\rmi(\frac{2\phi-\pi}{4})\right]
. From the above and the fact that
c^{2}+(c^{*})^{2}=2\beta
, one has

\rme^{-\rmi\gamma_{0}tH_{\mathit{eff}}}=\frac{1}{2\beta}\left[\begin{array}[]{cc}c&c^{*}\\
-c^{*}&c\end{array}\right]\left[\begin{array}[]{cc}\exp[\rmi\gamma_{0}t(c^{*})^{2}]&0\\
0&\exp[-\rmi\gamma_{0}tc^{2}]\end{array}\right]\left[\begin{array}[]{cc}c&-c^{*}\\
c^{*}&c\end{array}\right].

With the initial condition
\psi(0)=\psi_{0}
, from Eq. (
20
) one has

\tilde{\psi}(t)=\rme^{-\rmi\gamma_{0}tH_{\mathit{eff}}}\psi_{0}=\frac{1}{\beta}\left[\begin{array}[]{c}{\rm Re}[c^{2}\exp(\rmi\gamma_{0}t(c^{*})^{2})]\\
-\rmi\,{\rm Im}[\exp(\rmi\gamma_{0}t(c^{*})^{2})]\end{array}\right].

The formula for survival probability now follows from the definition in Eq. (
21
). The calculation for
\lambda>1
is similar.

For
\lambda=1
, as noted earlier
H_{\mathit{eff}}
is non-diagonalizable. The Jordan decomposition of
H_{\mathit{eff}}
is

\left[\begin{array}[]{cc}1&\rmi\\
0&1\end{array}\right]\left[\begin{array}[]{cc}-\rmi&0\\
1&-\rmi\end{array}\right]\left[\begin{array}[]{cc}1&-\rmi\\
0&1\end{array}\right].

Once again, matrix exponentiation gives

\rme^{-\rmi\gamma_{0}tH_{\mathit{eff}}}=\rme^{-\gamma_{0}t}\left[\begin{array}[]{cc}1+\gamma_{0}t&-\rmi\gamma_{0}t\\
-\rmi\gamma_{0}t&1-\gamma_{0}t\end{array}\right].

With the same initial condition as before, one obtains

\tilde{\psi}(t)=\rme^{-\gamma t/4}\left[\begin{array}[]{c}1+\gamma_{0}t\\
-\rmi\gamma_{0}t\\
\end{array}\right]

(97)

and the expression for
S(t)
follows.

Appendix B
Evolution of a qubit on the Bloch sphere

The standard representation of a qubit state on the Bloch sphere is given by

|\psi\rangle=\cos\frac{\chi}{2}|\psi_{0}\rangle+\rme^{\rmi\xi}\sin\frac{\chi}{2}|\psi_{1}\rangle

for
0\leq\chi\leq\pi
and
0\leq\xi\leq 2\pi
. In the spherical polar coordinate system,
\chi
is the polar angle and
\xi
the azimuthal angle. In the
yz
plane,
\xi=\pi/2
for
y>0
and
\xi=3\pi/2
for
y<0
.
\xi
is undefined on the
z
axis. Substituting the above in Eq. (
24
), the following coupled system is obtained

\displaystyle\sin\frac{\chi}{2}\,\left[\rmi\dot{\chi}+2\gamma_{0}(\rmi\lambda\sin\chi+\rme^{\rmi\xi})\right]=0,

\displaystyle\cos\frac{\chi}{2}\,\left[\rmi\dot{\chi}-2\gamma_{0}(-\rmi\lambda\sin\chi+\rme^{-\rmi\xi})\right]-2\dot{\xi}\,\sin\frac{\chi}{2}=0.

The trajectory of constant
\xi
is for
\cos\xi_{0}=0
which corresponds to
\xi_{0}=\pi/2
and
\xi_{0}=3\pi/2
. For
\xi_{0}=\pi/2
,
\chi
evolves in accordance with

\dot{\chi}=-2\gamma_{0}(\lambda\sin\chi+1),

whereas for
\xi_{0}=3\pi/2
,
\chi
evolves in accordance with

\dot{\chi}=-2\gamma_{0}(\lambda\sin\chi-1).

On the part of the evolution for
\xi=\pi/2
, define
\theta=\chi
and on the part of the evolution for
\xi=3\pi/2
, define
\theta=-\chi
. Then the entire evolution of the state vector in Eq. (
25
) happens via Eq. (
26
).

Laplace Transform of the moment generating function

Consider the case

98

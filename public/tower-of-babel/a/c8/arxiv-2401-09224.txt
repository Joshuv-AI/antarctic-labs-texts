Abstract
. Let p p be a prime. In this paper we provide a lower bound for the number of almost p p -rational characters of degree coprime to p p in the principal p p -block of a finite group of order divisible by p p . We further describe the p p -local structure of the groups for which the above-mentioned bound is sharp.

1.
Introduction

Let
G
be a finite group and let
p
be a prime dividing
|G|
. For a positive integer
k
, let
\xi_{k}
be a primitive
k
th root of unity. We denote by
\mathbb{Q}_{k}=\mathbb{Q}(\xi_{k})
the
k
th cyclotomic extension of
\mathbb{Q}
.
Let
\sigma\in{\rm Gal}({\mathbb{Q}}^{\mathrm{ab}}/{\mathbb{Q}})
be the Galois automorphism that sends
p
-power roots of unity to their
1+p
power and fixes roots of unity of order coprime to
p
(by abusing notation, we use
\sigma
to denote the restriction to any cyclotomic extension of
\mathbb{Q}
).
Although the definition of
\sigma
depends on the prime
p
, it will be clear from context throughout the article. Following
[
HMM22
]
, we say that a character
\chi
is almost
p
-rational if all the values of
\chi
lie in some cyclotomic extension
{\mathbb{Q}}_{pm}
where
(p,m)=1
.
Write
|G|=p^{a}n
with
(p,n)=1
and
a
is a positive integer. If
p
is odd, then
{\rm Gal}({\mathbb{Q}}_{|G|}/{\mathbb{Q}}_{pn})=\langle\sigma\rangle
. In particular, a character
\chi
of
G
is almost
p
-rational if, and only if,
\chi
is
\sigma
-invariant.
If
p=2
, then
{\mathbb{Q}}_{2n}={\mathbb{Q}}_{n}
, so almost
2
-rational characters are
2
-rational. As
{\rm Gal}({\mathbb{Q}}_{|G|}/{\mathbb{Q}}_{n})
is not generally cyclic, we cannot characterize
2
-rational characters as those invariant under the action of
\langle\sigma\rangle
(for instance, dihedral groups of order
2^{k}>8
have irreducible characters that are
\sigma
-invariant but not 2-rational). However, when dealing with irreducible characters
\chi
of odd degree, it was first noticed in
[
VR23
, Theorem 2.5]
that

\chi
is
2
-rational if, and only if,
\chi
is
\sigma
-invariant (or equivalently
\langle\sigma\rangle
-fixed).

Let
G
be a finite group and let
p
be a prime. For a
p
-block
B
of
G
, let
k(B)
denote the number of complex irreducible characters in
B
. In
[
HK00
]
, Héthelyi and Külshammer conjecture that if
k(B)>1
then

k(B)\geq 2\sqrt{p-1}.

This conjecture remains open even for solvable groups.
Recently, Hung and the third-named author of this article have shown that the Héthelyi–Külshammer conjecture holds for principal blocks in
[
HS23
]
.
Our aim in this article is to show that a much stronger bound is true for principal blocks. More precisely, we show that
2\sqrt{p-1}
is also a lower bound for the
number of almost
p
-rational characters of degree coprime to
p
lying in the principal
p
-block of groups of order divisible by
p
. From the discussion above, this means we are interested in studying

k_{0,\sigma}(B_{0}(G)),

the size of the set
\displaystyle\operatorname{Irr}_{p^{\prime},\sigma}(B_{0}(G))

of irreducible characters of degree coprime to
p
in the principal
p
-block of
G
fixed by the action of
\langle\sigma\rangle
.
We care to remark that the numbers
k_{0,\sigma}(B_{0}(G))
have been recently shown to influence the number of generators of the Sylow
p
-subgroups in
[
NT19
,
RSV20
,
VR23
]
. We denote by
\Phi(G)
the Frattini subgroup of a group
G
and by
\lceil x\rceil\in{\mathbb{Z}}
the ceiling of a real number
x
.
Our main result is the following.

Theorem A
.

Let
G
be a finite group,
p
a prime and
P\in\operatorname{Syl}_{p}(G)
. If
P>1
, then

\displaystyle k_{0,\sigma}(B_{0}(G))\geq\lceil 2\sqrt{p-1}\rceil\,

with equality if, and only if, the following conditions hold:

(i)

\lceil 2\sqrt{p-1}\rceil=e+\frac{p-1}{e}
for some divisor
e
of
p-1
;

(ii)

P
is cyclic; and

(iii)

the local quotient group
{\bf N}_{G}(P)/\Phi(P){\bf O}_{p^{\prime}}({\bf N}_{G}(P))
is isomorphic to one of the following Frobenius groups
\displaystyle\mathsf{C}_{p}\rtimes\mathsf{C}_{e}
or
\displaystyle\mathsf{C}_{p}\rtimes\mathsf{C}_{\frac{p-1}{e}}
.

The conclusion of Theorem A can be proven to be a consequence of the Alperin–McKay–Navarro conjecture
[
Nav04
, Conjecture B]
.
In this sense, Theorem A provides new evidence of the validity of this conjecture, which as of the writing of this article is quite far from being proved. (For example, it has not yet been reduced to a problem on simple groups.) We give further details in Section
7.2
, where we also speculate about the possibility of extending the inequality in Theorem
A
to arbitrary blocks.

Given any finite group
G
of order divisible by a prime
p
, it was proven in
[
Mar16
]
, by the first-named author of this article, that the number
k(G)

of conjugacy classes of
G
(or equivalently the number of irreducible complex characters of
G
) is bounded below by
2\sqrt{p-1}
.
Moreover, the results of
[
Mar16
]
show that
the bound
k(G)=2\sqrt{p-1}
is attained only if
a=\sqrt{p-1}\in\mathbb{N}
and
G
is isomorphic to the Frobenius group
{\sf C}_{p}\rtimes{\sf C}_{a}
.
Our proof of the equality in Theorem
A
ultimately depends on the
analysis of the equality
k(G)=\lceil 2\sqrt{p-1}\rceil
in groups
G
with a nontrivial normal elementary abelian Sylow
p
-subgroup.
We carry out this analysis, improving upon
[
Mar16
, Theorem 1.1]
, in Section
2
.
Additionally, our proof of Theorem
A
depends on the following statements regarding mostly finite simple groups.

Theorem B
.

Let
S
be a finite nonabelian simple group of order divisible by a prime
p\geq 5
. Let
A=\operatorname{Aut}(S)
.

(i)

There is some
X
-invariant
1_{S}\neq\theta\in\operatorname{Irr}_{p^{\prime},\sigma}(B_{0}(S))
, where
X/S\in\operatorname{Syl}_{p}(A/S)
.

(ii)

If

has non-cyclic Sylow

-subgroups and

then

(a)

,

(b)

the number of

\operatorname{Irr}_{p^{\prime},\sigma}(B_{0}(S))
is at least
2(p-1)^{1/4}
.

(iii)

Suppose that

has cyclic Sylow

-subgroups. Write

with

and suppose that

with

Then

where

.

We note that Theorem

(i) implies that there is a
\sigma
-invariant extension of

to
X
, using
[
Nav18
, Cors. 6.2 and 6.4]
. With this in mind, we prove a stronger statement in the case that

is a simple group of Lie type in non-defining characteristic in Lemma

below, which might be useful for related future applications.

Notice also that Theorem

(iii) states that the principal
p
-block of a group

as described in its statement satisfies
[

, Conjecture D]
. In
[
HMM22
, Theorem 4.4]
, the authors prove a weaker version of the statement of Theorem B(iii) by using the Classification of Finite Simple Groups (CFSG, for short) when proving that

is a lower bound for the number of almost
p
-rational irreducible characters of degree coprime to
p
in a group of order divisible by
p
.
Remarkably, we prove Theorem

(iii) without invoking the CFSG in Section
5
.

This paper is organized as follows. In Section

we show that if

is a finite group of order divisible by

and

has cyclic Sylow

2.
An improved bound on the number of conjugacy classes

The aim of this section is to characterize the finite groups
G
with an elementary abelian nontrivial Sylow
p
-subgroup such that
k(G)=\lceil 2\sqrt{p-1}\rceil
.
By
[
Mar16
]
, the inequality
k(G)\geq\lceil 2\sqrt{p-1}\rceil
always holds.
We will show that
k(G)=\lceil 2\sqrt{p-1}\rceil
implies that
|G|_{p}=p
, where

is the
p

n
.
In particular,
k(G)=a+\frac{p-1}{a}
for some divisor
a
of
p-1
, and

is isomorphic to one of the Frobenius groups

{\sf C}_{p}\rtimes{\sf C}_{a}
or
\displaystyle{\sf C}_{p}\rtimes{\sf C}_{\frac{p-1}{a}}
.

2.1.
Statement of results

Let
G
be a finite group and

a prime. Let

be the number of conjugacy classes of

. For a normal subgroup

in

, let

denote the number of orbits of

on

(acting by conjugation).

The purpose of this section is to prove the following.

Theorem 2.1
.

If
G
is a finite group having an elementary abelian minimal normal subgroup
V
of
p
-rank at least
2
and
|G/V|
is not divisible by the prime
p
, then
k(G)\geq 2\sqrt{p-1}+1
.

We make a few remarks on Theorem
2.1
.

In order to prove Theorem
2.1
, we may assume that
G
is not nilpotent. For if
G
is nilpotent, then
k(G)\geq|V|\geq p^{2}\geq 2\sqrt{p-1}+1
. In the paper
[
VLS07
]
and its prequels, all non-nilpotent finite groups are classified with at most
14
conjugacy classes. By going through these lists of groups, we see that no group
G
is a counterexample to Theorem
2.1
with
k(G)\leq 14
. This means that in Theorem
2.1
we can assume that
2\sqrt{p-1}+1\geq 15
. In other words,
p\geq 53
.

Héthelyi and Külshammer
[
HK03
]
proved that if
X
is a finite solvable group whose order is divisible by the square of a prime
p
then
k(X)\geq(49p+1)/60
. Thus if
G
is solvable (and
p\geq 53
), then
k(G)\geq(49p+1)/60\geq 2\sqrt{p-1}+1
. We may assume in Theorem
2.1
that
G
is not solvable.

Since
k(G)\geq k(G/V)+n(G,V)-1
by the Clifford-Gallagher formula (see the comment after
[

, Theorem 1.10a]
and also
[
Sch07
, Proposition 3.1b]
), it is sufficient to show that
k(G/V)+n(G,V)\geq 2\sqrt{p-1}+2
. Since
k(G/V)\geq k(G/{\bf C}_{G}(V))
and

, we may assume that

acts faithfully on

, that is,

is a faithful and irreducible

-module.

We find that Theorem
2.1
would be a consequence of the following statement (where the new

is

in Theorem
2.1
).

Theorem 2.2
.

Let
V
be an irreducible and faithful

-module for some finite non-solvable group

and finite field

of characteristic

at least
53
. If

does not divide

Lemma 3.7
.

Let

be a prime and let

. Assume that

\chi\in\operatorname{Irr}_{p^{\prime},\sigma}(B_{0}(G))

Notice that

[

Now let

4.

The aim of this section is to prove parts (i) and (ii) from Theorem

We begin with the following observation about sporadic and alternating groups.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let

For the sporadic and Tits groups, we can see this directly from the Character Table Library in GAP

]
. Now let

be an alternating group and let

be an irreducible character of

contains at most two irreducible constituents, which must be acted on by

Much of the work will be centered around groups of Lie type, so we begin by fixing some notation in that situation.

4.1.

Before continuing, we set some notation for the remainder of this section, surrounding simple groups of Lie type.
So for now, let
S
be a simple group of Lie type. We will exclude the Tits group

We have

The following is an application of the work of Tiep–Zalesski

Lemma 4.2
.

Let

be as above, with

is
\sigma
-invariant.

Proof.

This follows from
[

Now, let

and

be dual to

and

of semisimple elements (that is,

The unipotent characters will also play an important role. Thanks to the work of Lusztig

In the bijection

It is straightforward to check that

Using Lemma

Furthermore,

.
The second part of the statement follows from the first one.
∎

Proposition 5.3

We now show that the principal

Proposition 5.4
.

Let
N\triangleleft G
and let

be a prime. Suppose that

-subgroups, and that

Notice that under our assumptions

. By the definition of

,
[

, Definition 3.6]
and Brauer’s Third Main theorem

, this implies that

The statement of Theorem B(iii) is now a corollary.

Corollary 5.5
.

Let

has nontrivial cyclic Sylow

-subgroups. Write

Suppose that

Let

Since

has a normal Sylow

Now, using that

∎

6.
Proof of Theorem A

We begin by proving the inequality part in Theorem A.

Theorem 6.1
.

Let
p
be a prime and
G
be a finite group of order divisible by
p
. Then

k_{0,\sigma}(B_{0}(G))\geq 2\sqrt{p-1}
.

Proof.

If
G
has a normal Sylow
p
-subgroup

, then using Lemma
3.4
we have
k_{0,\sigma}(B_{0}(G))=k(G/\Phi(P){\bf O}_{p^{\prime}}(G))\geq 2\sqrt{p-1}
by the main result of
[
Mar16
]
and we are done in this case.

We proceed by induction on
|G|
. By Lemma
3.1
(ii) and the inductive hypothesis, we may assume that
{\bf O}_{p^{\prime}}(G)=1
.
If
M\triangleleft\,G
and
p\mid|G:M|
then by induction and Lemma
3.1
(i) we have
k_{0,\sigma}(B_{0}(G))\geq k_{0,\sigma}(B_{0}(G/M))\geq 2\sqrt{p-1}
and we are done.
Thus we may assume that
G
has a unique minimal normal subgroup
N
, which is semisimple, of order divisible by
p
and
p\nmid|G:N|
.

Say
N\cong S^{t}
, where
S
is nonabelian simple of order divisible by
p
. Suppose that
S
has cyclic Sylow
p
-subgroups. If
t=1
, then the Sylow
p
-subgroups of

are also cyclic and we are done by Lemma
5.2
.
If

then we apply Theorem
B
(iii).

Therefore, we may assume that the Sylow
p
-subgroups of
S
are not cyclic. Let
k
be the number of
\operatorname{Aut}(S)
-orbits on
\operatorname{Irr}_{p^{\prime},\sigma}(B_{0}(S))
. If

, since

, then

has at least

\binom{k+t-1}{t}

orbits on
\operatorname{Irr}_{p^{\prime},\sigma}(B_{0}(N))
. By Theorem
B
(ii)(b) we have that

. In particular

has at least

\frac{k(k+1)}{2}=2\sqrt{p-1}+(p-1)^{1/4}\geq 2\sqrt{p-1}+1>\lceil 2\sqrt{p-1}\rceil

\operatorname{Irr}_{p^{\prime},\sigma}(B_{0}(N))
.
Since
|G:N|
is not divisible by

, every such orbit lies under a distinct
\chi\in\operatorname{Irr}_{p^{\prime},\sigma}(B_{0}(G))
so we are done using Corollary
3.6
. If

, then the result follows from Theorem

(ii)(a).
∎

Theorem 6.2
.

Assume
k_{0,\sigma}(B_{0}(G))=\lceil 2\sqrt{p-1}\rceil
. Then the Sylow
p
-subgroups of

are cyclic.

Proof.

Let
G
be a minimal counterexample to the statement. In particular
P\in{\rm Syl}_{p}(G)
is not cyclic.

Step 1: We have
p\geq 5
.

If
p\leq 3
, then
\lceil 2\sqrt{p-1}\rceil=p
, so
k_{0,\sigma}(B_{0}(G))=\lceil 2\sqrt{p-1}\rceil
implies that
P
is cyclic by the main result of
[
RSV20
]
, contradicting the choice of
G
as a minimal counterexample.

Step 2: If
N
is a minimal normal subgroup of

, then
p
divides
|N|
and
p
does not divide
|G:N|
.

Assume otherwise. If
p\nmid|N|
then
\operatorname{Irr}(B_{0}(G))=\operatorname{Irr}(B_{0}(G/N))
by Lemma
3.1
(ii). By the minimality of

we have that
G/N
has cyclic Sylow
p
-subgroups, then so does

, a contradiction.

If
p
divides
|G:N|
then by Theorem

we have

\lceil 2\sqrt{p-1}\rceil\leq\operatorname{Irr}_{p^{\prime},\sigma}(B_{0}(G/N))\leq\operatorname{Irr}_{p^{\prime},\sigma}(B_{0}(G))=\lceil 2\sqrt{p-1}\rceil.

Thus

has cyclic Sylow
p
-subgroups and every

-degree almost

-rational character of

lies over

(and in the principal block of

).

Suppose that

is a
p
-group. Let

and notice that

is not cyclic but

is cyclic and nontrivial. We have

. Let

be such that

is not contained in

. Then

is
\sigma
-invariant and linear. Write

and

there is some

. By Frobenius reciprocity,

so

, a contradiction.

Therefore, we may assume that

is a direct product of copies of a nonabelian simple group

of order divisible by

. Write

, where

for every

and the hypothesis that

, we have that

so it suffices to show that there is some

such that

is not contained in
{\rm ker}(\chi)
.

Write

and

(i) and Lemma

), there is some extension

Let

be a minimal normal subgroup of

.
By Step 2 we have

is not divisible by

is a

7.
Final comments

7.1.
Related problems

Fix a prime number
p
and write

\mathcal{S}_{p}=\{e+\frac{p-1}{e}\ |\ e\text{ divides }p-1\}.

In
[
HMM22
]
, Hung, Malle and the first-named author of this article ask the following question.

Question 7.1

(Question 1.5 of
[
HMM22
]
)
.

Is it true that the Sylow
p
-subgroups of a group
G
of order divisible by
p
are cyclic if

k_{0,\sigma}(B_{0}(G))\in\mathcal{S}_{p}
?

Notice that when
p-1
is a perfect square, then
\lceil 2\sqrt{p-1}\rceil=2\sqrt{p-1}={\rm min}(\mathcal{S}_{p})
. Then our main theorem provides a partial positive answer to the above question. It is still unknown whether there are finitely many primes
p
satisfying that
p-1
is a perfect square. In fact, this is one of the 4 problems on prime numbers that Landau presented in his talk at the International Congress of Mathematicians in 1912.

In
[
CK23
]
the authors put forward the following conjecture.

Conjecture 7.2

(Cınarcı–Keller)
.

Let
p
be a prime. Let
a
and
b
be positive integers such
that
p-1=ab
and such that
|a-b|
is minimal.
Then, for any finite group
G
of order divisible by
p
, we have
k(G)\geq a+b
and

k(G)=a+b
if, and only if,
G\cong\mathsf{C}_{p}\rtimes\mathsf{C}_{a}
or
\mathsf{C}_{p}\rtimes\mathsf{C}_{b}
.

It is straightforward to see that
a+b
is precisely the minimum of
\mathcal{S}_{p}
, where
\mathcal{S}_{p}
is defined as above. Notice that
\lceil 2\sqrt{p-1}\rceil\leq\min\mathcal{S}_{p}
, so our Theorem
2.1
provides evidence for the Cınarcı–Keller conjecture in the case where
\lceil 2\sqrt{p-1}\rceil=\min\mathcal{S}_{p}
or, equivalently, when
\lceil 2\sqrt{p-1}\rceil\in\mathcal{S}_{p}
. This happens significantly more often than
\sqrt{p-1}\in\mathbb{Z}
(a quick check in
[
GAP22
]
shows that
\lceil 2\sqrt{p-1}\rceil\in\mathcal{S}_{p}
for
77
out of the
168
primes smaller than
1000
, whereas there are only
10
primes smaller than
1000
with
\sqrt{p-1}\in\mathbb{Z}
).

7.2.
Arbitrary blocks

Recall that the Héthelyi-Külshammer conjecture predicts that

k(B)\geq 2\sqrt{p-1}

whenever
B
is a
p
-block with nontrivial defect of some finite group
G
.
The inequality holds for
p\leq 3
by the weak block orthogonality relation. As already mentioned in the Introduction, it was recently settled for principal blocks in
[
HS23
]
.
However, outside these cases, little is known about this conjecture. In particular, it is not known whether it follows from the Alperin-McKay conjecture nor whether it holds for solvable groups.

The following problem appeared recently in
[
Nav23
]
.

Problem 7.3

(Problem 2.5 of
[
Nav23
]
)
.

Let
V
be a finite dimensional
\mathbb{F}_{p}H
-module, where
H
is a finite
p^{\prime}
-group and
\mathbb{F}_{p}
is the field of
p
elements. Let
G=VH
be the semidirect product. Let
Z={\bf O}_{p^{\prime}}(G)={\bf C}_{H}(V)
and assume
Z\subseteq{\bf Z}(G)
. Let
\lambda\in\operatorname{Irr}(Z)
. Then
|\operatorname{Irr}(G|\lambda)|\geq 2\sqrt{p-1}
.

As mentioned before its statement in
[
Nav23
]
, if Problem
7.3
has a positive answer, then the blocks
B
satisfying the Alperin–McKay conjecture also satisfy the Héthelyi–Külshammer conjecture. Recall that the Alperin-McKay conjecture holds for a block
B
if
k_{0}(B)=k_{0}(b)
, where
b
is the Brauer first main correspondent of
B
.
If instead we assume that the block
B
satisfies the Alperin–McKay–Navarro conjecture
[
Nav04
, Conjecture]
, then
B
would satisfy the blockwise version of our Theorem
A
. We provide a proof of this fact in Proposition
7.5
below, but let us first recall the statement of the Alperin–McKay–Navarro conjecture.

For a fixed prime
p
, let
\mathcal{H}\leq{\rm Gal}(\mathbb{Q}_{|G|}/\mathbb{Q})
be the subgroup generated by the field automorphisms
\tau
which send
p^{\prime}
-roots of unity
\xi
to
\xi^{p^{e}}
for some integer
e
. For a
p
-block
B
we let
\operatorname{Irr}_{0}(B)
denote the set of characters of
B
with height zero. The group
\mathcal{H}
acts on the set
\{{\rm Irr}(B)\ |\ B\text{ $p$-block of $G$}\}
, and we denote by
\mathcal{H}_{B}
the stabilizer of
{\rm Irr}(B)
under the action of
\mathcal{H}
.

Conjecture 7.4

(Alperin–McKay–Navarro)
.

Let
B
be a
p
-block of
G
with defect group
D
and let
b
be its Brauer correspondent block in
{\bf N}_{G}(D)
. Then there is an
\mathcal{H}_{B}
-equivariant bijection

\operatorname{Irr}_{0}(B)\rightarrow\operatorname{Irr}_{0}(b)
.

Notice that our field automorphism
\sigma
from the introduction is an element of
\mathcal{H}
.
Moreover the action of
\langle\sigma\rangle
stabilizes
{\rm Irr}(B)
for every
p
-block
B
(because Brauer characters are
\sigma
-invariant).
In particular, Conjecture
7.4
predicts that the action of
\langle\sigma\rangle
on characters fixes the same number of height zero characters in
B
as it does in its Brauer correspondent block

, that is

k_{0,\sigma}(B)=k_{0,\sigma}(b)\,.

Proposition 7.5
.

Let
G
be a finite group of order divisible by
p
,
B
a
p
-block of
G
with nontrivial defect group
D
. Assume Problem
7.3
has a positive answer for all finite groups and that the Alperin–McKay–Navarro conjecture holds for

. Then

k_{0,\sigma}(B)\geq 2\sqrt{p-1}\,.

Proof.

Let
b
be the Brauer correspondent block of
B
in
{\bf N}_{G}(D)
. We have that
k_{0,\sigma}(B)=k_{0,\sigma}(b)
by the Alperin–McKay–Navarro conjecture. By
[

, Lemma 2.2]
we have that

k_{0,\sigma}(b)=k(\overline{b})

where
\overline{b}
is a block of
{\bf N}_{G}(D)/\Phi(D)
with defect group
D/\Phi(D)
.
By
[
Rey63
, Theorem 6]
applied to

there is a block

of a finite group

with normal defect group

,

V\cong D/\Phi(D)
and with a height-preserving bijection
\operatorname{Irr}(\overline{b})\rightarrow\operatorname{Irr}(b^{\prime})
. It follows that

k_{0,\sigma}(B)=k(b^{\prime})
. By Schur–Zassenhaus, the group
V
has a complement
H
in
K
, so
K=VH
with
V
an
\mathbb{F}_{p}H
-module.
By Fong’s theorem
[
Nav98
, Theorem 10.20]
, there is some
K
-invariant
\lambda\in\operatorname{Irr}({\bf O}_{p^{\prime}}(K))
with
\operatorname{Irr}(b^{\prime})=\operatorname{Irr}(K|\lambda)
.
By character triple isomorphisms
[
Nav98
, Problem 8.13]
we may assume
{\bf O}_{p^{\prime}}(K)\subseteq{\bf Z}(K)
. Using the assumption that Problem
7.3
has a positive answer, we have

k_{0,\sigma}(B)=k(b^{\prime})=|\operatorname{Irr}(K|\lambda)|\geq 2\sqrt{p-1}

as desired.
∎

The version for principal blocks of Problem
7.3
is the case where
Z=1
, and a positive answer in this case is guaranteed by
[
Mar16
]
. Arguing as above, we get that if the Alperin–McKay–Navarro conjecture holds for
B_{0}(G)
then

k_{0,\sigma}(B_{0}(G))=k({\bf N}_{G}(P)/\Phi(P){\bf O}_{p^{\prime}}({\bf N}_{G}(P)))

and the equality part of Theorem
A
follows from Theorem
2.1
.

We close our comments with a last observation. Notice that for every group
G
and every prime
p
we have that
k_{0,\sigma}(B_{0}(G))\geq 1
because the principal
p
-block contains the trivial character
1_{G}
. However, the existence of almost
p
-rational characters of height zero in arbitrary
p
-blocks had not been observed until quite recently (see
[
NR24
]
). Now that we know that
k_{0,\sigma}(B)\geq 1
for arbitrary blocks and primes, we can guarantee that the lower bound

k_{0,\sigma}(B)\geq 2\sqrt{p-1}

holds whenever
p\leq 3
by a direct application of
[
RSV20
, Lemma 1.4]
.

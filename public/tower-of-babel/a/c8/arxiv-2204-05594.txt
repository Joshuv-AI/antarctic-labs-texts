Generalization of Gurjar’s Hyperplane section Theorem to arbitrary analytic varieties and A 𝔪 \mathfrak{m} AC classes
A.J. Parameswaran, Mohit Upmanyu

Abstract
This paper aims to generalize the hyperplane section Theorem of R.V. Gurjar to arbitrary (local) analytic varieties even if the intersection with hyperplanes is not necessarily isolated. In the case of formal varieties, we generalize the statement to work for different classes of hypersurfaces other than hyperplanes. We call the classes of functions (which are subsets of the formal power series ring) defining these classes of hypersurface algebraic 𝔪 \mathfrak{m} -adically closed (A 𝔪 \mathfrak{m} AC) classes.

The next two results are semi-continuity results which will be used throughout this paper at various places to prove that certain sets are Zariski constructible.

Lemma 2.8
.

Let
B=\mathbb{K}[\![x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}]\!]/I
and
\mathfrak{m}
be its maximal ideal. Let
I_{1},\dots,I_{k}
be
\mathfrak{m}
-primary ideals and
\widetilde{I}:=I_{1}+\cdots+I_{k}
. Define

\lambda_{\widetilde{I}}:B/I_{1}\times\cdots\times B/I_{k}\to\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}

\lambda_{\widetilde{I}}(y_{1},\dots,y_{k})=\lambda(\frac{B}{\widetilde{I}+\langle y_{1},\dots,y_{k}\rangle})

Then
\lambda_{\widetilde{I}}
is upper semi-continuous, when
B/I_{1}\times\cdots\times B/I_{k}
is given the Zariski topology.

Proof.

\lambda\bigg(\frac{B}{\widetilde{I}+\langle y_{1},\dots,y_{k}\rangle}\bigg)=\lambda\bigg(\frac{B}{\widetilde{I}}\bigg)-\lambda\bigg(\frac{\widetilde{I}+\langle y_{1},\dots,y_{k}\rangle}{\widetilde{I}}\bigg)

So it is enough to prove that that
\lambda(\frac{\widetilde{I}+\langle y_{1},\ldots,y_{k}\rangle}{\widetilde{I}})
is lower semi-continuous, since
\mathbb{K}
is algebraically closed
\lambda=\dim_{\mathbb{K}}
.

Define the linear map,
(B/\widetilde{I})^{k}\to B/\widetilde{I}
, given by
(a_{i})\mapsto\sum_{i=i}^{k}y_{i}a_{i}
.
Note that the image is exactly
\frac{\widetilde{I}+\langle y_{1},\dots,y_{k}\rangle}{\widetilde{I}}
, therefore the rank of this map is exactly
\dim_{\mathbb{K}}(\frac{\widetilde{I}+\langle y_{1},\dots,y_{k}\rangle}{\widetilde{I}})
. But rank is lower semi-continuous (in the variable
(y_{1},\dots,y_{k})
) as needed.
∎

Notation:
Let
B=\mathbb{K}[\![x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]\!]/I
and
h\in B
then by

we will denote the corresponding subvariety, i.e.

Lemma 2.9
.

Let
B=\mathbb{K}[\![x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]\!]/I
be a ring of dimension
\alpha+1
and
\mathfrak{m}
be its maximal ideal. Define

\lambda_{\Sigma}:B/\mathfrak{m}^{r}\to\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}

\lambda_{\Sigma}(h)=\lambda(\mathcal{O}_{\Sigma^{\alpha}_{H}}/\mathfrak{m}^{r-1})

\lambda_{\Sigma}
is well defined and upper semi-continuous, when

is given the Zariski topology

Proof.

Let
A=\mathbb{K}[\![x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}]\!]
and
I=\langle f_{1},\ldots,f_{k}\rangle

Define

\begin{array}[]{ccccc}A/\mathfrak{m}^{r}&\to&(A/\mathfrak{m}^{r})\times(A/\mathfrak{m}^{r-1})^{{k+1\choose\alpha}\times{n\choose\alpha}}&\to&\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}\\
\overline{h}&\mapsto&(\overline{h},n-\alpha\times n-\alpha\text{ minors of Jacobian of }(\overline{h},f_{1},\ldots,f_{k}))&\mapsto&\lambda(\frac{A}{\mathfrak{m}^{r-1}+I+\langle h\rangle+Jac_{n-\alpha}(H)})\end{array}

Choose

3
Algebraic
\mathfrak{m}
-adically closed class

Here we introduce the most important concept of algebraic
\mathfrak{m}
-adically closed classes that are critical in proving the main Theorem

.

Definition 3.1
.

Let
\mathbb{K}
be an algebraically closed uncountable field. Let
A=\mathbb{K}[\![x_{1},x_{2},\ldots,x_{n}]\!]
and
\mathfrak{m}
denote its maximal ideal.
A subset
P\subset B=\mathbb{K}[\![x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}]\!]/I
is said to be an
algebraic
\mathfrak{m}
-adically closed (A
\mathfrak{m}
AC) class
, if
P=\underleftarrow{\lim}P_{i}
where
P_{i}
are constructible subsets of
B/\mathfrak{m}^{i}
(in Zariski topology).
Since
P
is an inverse limit,
P
is automatically
\mathfrak{m}
-adically closed (see
3.2
).

If
U
is a set complement of an A
\mathfrak{m}
AC then we refer to it as an
algebraic
\mathfrak{m}
-adically open (A
\mathfrak{m}
AO) class
.

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
P\subset B=\mathbb{K}[\![x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}]\!]/I
. Then, the following are equivalent:

1.

P=\underleftarrow{\lim}P_{i}
, where
P_{i}\subset B/\mathfrak{m}^{i}
.

2.

P
is an
\mathfrak{m}
-adically closed subset.

Proof.

(1)
\implies
(2)

The
\mathfrak{m}
-adic topology is given by
B=\underleftarrow{\lim}B/\mathfrak{m}^{i}
, where
B/\mathfrak{m}^{i}
is given the discrete topology. But
P_{i}
is closed in the discrete topology of
B/\mathfrak{m}^{i}
. So
P=\underleftarrow{\lim}P_{i}
is
\mathfrak{m}
-adically closed since it is the inverse limit of closed subsets.

(2)
\implies
(1)

Let
\pi_{i}:B\to B/\mathfrak{m}^{i}
be the canonical projection map.
Let
P_{i}:=\pi_{i}(P)
. Define
\overline{P}:=\underleftarrow{\lim}P_{i}
.
As seen before,
\overline{P}
is
\mathfrak{m}
-adically closed. We will prove that
\overline{P}
is the closure of
P
. Since
P
is also
\mathfrak{m}
-adically closed, we will obtain
P=\overline{P}=\underleftarrow{\lim}P_{i}
.

Let
p\in\overline{P}
, then
p=(\dots,\pi_{i}(p_{i}),\pi_{i+1}(p_{i+1}),\dots)
. and
p_{i}\in P
. The sequence
p_{i}
converges to
p
in the
\mathfrak{m}
-adic topology (since
\forall i\ \exists p_{i}=p
mod
\mathfrak{m}^{i}
). Hence
\overline{P}
is the closure of
P
.
∎

Remark 3.3
.

Note that
P_{i}
in general are
neither
closed
nor
open in the Zariski topology, but are instead closed in discrete topology (since every subset is closed in discrete topology).

In
[
Mor01
]
, Mori proves the non-emptiness of a certain fixed subset of
\mathbb{C}[\![x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]\!]
by proving that it is an inverse limit of the Zariski constructible set. We in turn have turned this into the definition of an A
\mathfrak{m}
AC class. The following
non-emptiness lemma
is unsurprising.

Lemma 3.4
.

Let
P=\underleftarrow{\lim}P_{i}
be an A
\mathfrak{m}
AC class.
P\neq\emptyset
iff
P_{i}\neq\emptyset\ \forall i
.

Proof.

We have a sequence of maps
\cdots\to P_{r+1}\to P_{r}\to P_{r-1}\to\cdots
given by the restriction of projection maps.

Define
W_{r,s}:=
closure of
image(P_{r}\to P_{s})
. (Closure is taken in
P_{s}
w.r.t. Zariski topology).

Define
W_{s}:=\bigcap_{r>s}W_{r,s}
.

This is an intersection of closed subsets in a Noetherian space, so this must be a finite intersection. So
W_{s}=W_{t,s}
for
t
sufficiently larger than
s
. So the map from
W_{t}\to W_{s}
is a dominant map, for all
t,s
such that
t>s
.

Now we can get a sequence of spaces
\cdots\to W_{s+1}\to W_{s}\to\cdots

that are subsets of
\cdots,P_{s+1},P_{s},\ldots
and are non-empty and the maps are dominant.

This implies that
W_{s}-X_{r,s}\subset Im(W_{r}\to W_{s})
, where
X_{r,s}
are proper closed subsets. Since
\mathbb{K}
is uncountable,
W_{s}-\bigcup X_{r,s}\neq\emptyset
.

Now we can choose a sequence of elements
\phi_{s}\in W_{s}
such that
\phi_{s+1}\mapsto\phi_{s}
.
This gives an element
\phi=(\dots,\phi_{s},\phi_{s+1},\dots)
of the inverse limit
P
.
∎

Remark 3.5
.

As seen in the previous proof, we can always assume that the maps between
P_{i}
are dominant.

Lemma 3.6

Let

\underleftarrow{\lim}\bigcap P^{j}_{i}\subset\underleftarrow{\lim}\bigcap_{j\leq i}P^{j}_{i}

(1)

(4)

Now let

Definition 4.1
.

Let

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
K
be an infinite field. Let
R
be a Noetherian local complete K-algebra of dimension
1
with maximal ideal
\mathfrak{m}
such that
R/\mathfrak{m}=K
. Let
t
and
e
be
\lambda(T(R,\mathfrak{m}))
and
e(R,\mathfrak{m})
respectively. Then we have the following.

For a generic
x\in\mathfrak{m}-\mathfrak{m}^{2}
, and for all
k\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}

ek\leq\lambda(\frac{R}{\langle x^{k+1}\rangle})\leq ek+t

(4)

And if
k\leq t
, we can improve the lower bound as follows.

\lambda(\frac{R}{\langle x^{k+1}\rangle})\geq(e+1)k

(5)

And if
k\geq t
, the upper bound is in fact achieved.

\lambda(\frac{R}{\langle x^{k+1}\rangle})=ek+t

(6)

Proof.

Use Noether normalization to get that
R
is integral over
K[\![x]\!]
.
Also, choose
x
so that the multiplicity of
R
w.r.t
\langle x\rangle
is the same as the multiplicity of
R
w.r.t
\mathfrak{m}
.

But
K[\![x]\!]
is a PID. So torsion-free finitely generated modules are free.
Torsion w.r.t
x
is the same as the torsion w.r.t
\mathfrak{m}
as
\sqrt{\langle x\rangle}=\mathfrak{m}
.
So
R/T(R,\mathfrak{m})
is a free
K[\![x]\!]
module. Let
\alpha
be its rank. Now,

\displaystyle\begin{split}e&=\text{the multiplicity of }R\text{ w.r.t }\langle x\rangle\\
&=\text{the multiplicity of }R/T(R,\mathfrak{m})\text{ w.r.t }\langle x\rangle\\
&=\text{the rank of }R/T(R,\mathfrak{m})\text{ over }K[\![x]\!]\ (\text{As the Hilbert polynomial of the free module is }\alpha n)\\
&=\alpha\end{split}

In particular
R\cong K[\![x]\!]^{e}\oplus T(R,\mathfrak{m})
as a
K[\![x]\!]
-module.
The inequalities (4) are just the length of this module (modulo
\langle x^{k}\rangle
) with and without torsion.
Also note that for large
k
, the length must be
ek+t
.

For (5) and (6), we first note that

\lambda\bigg(\frac{R}{\langle x^{k+1}\rangle}\bigg)=\lambda\bigg(\frac{R}{\langle x\rangle}\oplus\frac{\langle x\rangle}{\langle x^{2}\rangle}\oplus\cdots\oplus\frac{\langle x^{k}\rangle}{\langle x^{k+1}\rangle}\bigg)=\sum_{i=0}^{k}\lambda\bigg(\frac{\langle x^{i}\rangle}{\langle x^{i+1}\rangle}\bigg)

We have a sequence of maps

\frac{R}{\langle x\rangle}\xrightarrow{\times x}\frac{\langle x\rangle}{\langle x^{2}\rangle}\xrightarrow{\times x}\cdots\xrightarrow{\times x}\frac{\langle x^{k}\rangle}{\langle x^{k+1}\rangle}\xrightarrow{\times x}\frac{\langle x^{k+1}\rangle}{\langle x^{k+2}\rangle}\xrightarrow{\times x}\cdots

We see that each of these maps is surjective. In particular, the length of these is a decreasing function and eventually must become a constant
e
(which is the rank of the free module).
So there exists
q
such that

\lambda(\frac{\langle x^{i}\rangle}{\langle x^{i+1}\rangle})\begin{cases}\geq e+1,\text{ if }i\leq q\\
=e,\text{ if }i>q\end{cases}

Now
q
can be at most
t
because we have a finite amount of torsion given by
\lambda(T(R,\mathfrak{m}))
.

Finally, we sum up the inequalities above, from
i=1
to
k
. Since we are adding
e
, for every
k>q
, and
ek+t
must be eventually obtained. So
ek+t
must be obtained at
q
itself. In conclusion, after summing up we get the following which proves the Lemma:

\lambda(\frac{R}{\langle x^{k+1}\rangle})\begin{cases}\geq(e+1)k,\text{ if }k\leq q\\
=ek+t\geq(e+1)k,\text{ if }q<k\leq t\\
=ek+t,\text{ if }t<k\end{cases}

∎

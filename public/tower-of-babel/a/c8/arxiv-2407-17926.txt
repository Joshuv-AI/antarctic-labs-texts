Periodic Exponential Turnpike Phenomenon in Mean-Field Stochastic Linear-Quadratic Optimal Control
Jingrui Sun Lvning Yuan Jiaqi Zhang

Abstract




Abstract.

The paper establishes the exponential turnpike property for a class of mean-field stochastic
linear-quadratic (LQ) optimal control problems with periodic coefficients.
It first introduces the concepts of stability, stabilizability, and detectability for stochastic
linear systems.
Then, the long-term behavior of the associated Riccati equations is analyzed under stabilizability
and detectability conditions.
Subsequently, a periodic mean-field stochastic LQ problem is formulated and solved.
Finally, a linear transformation of the periodic extension of its optimal pair
is shown to be the turnpike limit of the initial optimal control problem.

Key words.

Exponential turnpike property, stochastic optimal control, periodic, mean-field, linear-quadratic,
Riccati equation, stabilizability, detectability.

49N10, 49N20, 49N80, 93E20.

Introduction

Let
(\varOmega,\mathscr{F},\mathbb{P})
be a complete probability space on which a standard one-dimensional
Brownian motion
W=\{W(t),\mathscr{F}_{t};\,t\geqslant 0\}
is defined, where
\mathbb{F}=\{\mathscr{F}_{t}\}_{t\geqslant 0}

is a filtration satisfying the usual conditions. Consider the following controlled linear
mean-field stochastic differential equation (SDE, for short)

\left\{\begin{aligned} dX(t)&=\big\{A(t)X(t)+\bar{A}(t)\mathbb{E}[X(t)]+B(t)u(t)+\bar{B}(t)\mathbb{E}[u(t)]+b(t)\big\}dt\\
&\hphantom{=\ }+\big\{C(t)X(t)+\bar{C}(t)\mathbb{E}[X(t)]+D(t)u(t)+\bar{D}(t)\mathbb{E}[u(t)]+\sigma(t)\big\}dW(t),\\
X(0)&=x\end{aligned}\right.

(1.1)

and the quadratic cost functional

\displaystyle J_{\scriptscriptstyle T}(x;u(\cdot))

\displaystyle\triangleq\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{T}\Bigg[\Big\langle\!\!\begin{pmatrix}[l]Q(t)&\!S(t)^{\top}\\
S(t)&\!R(t)\end{pmatrix}\!\begin{pmatrix}X(t)\\
u(t)\end{pmatrix}\!,\begin{pmatrix}X(t)\\
u(t)\end{pmatrix}\!\!\Big\rangle+2\Big\langle\!\!\begin{pmatrix}q(t)\\

\displaystyle\hphantom{=\ }+\Big\langle\!\!\begin{pmatrix}[l]\bar{Q}(t)&\!\bar{S}(t)^{\top}\\
\bar{S}(t)&\!\bar{R}(t)\end{pmatrix}\!\begin{pmatrix}\mathbb{E}[X(t)]\\
\mathbb{E}[u(t)]\end{pmatrix}\!,\begin{pmatrix}\mathbb{E}[X(t)]\\
\mathbb{E}[u(t)]\end{pmatrix}\!\!\Big\rangle\Bigg]dt,

(1.2)

where the coefficients
A(\cdot)
,
\bar{A}(\cdot)
,
B(\cdot)
,
\bar{B}(\cdot)
,
b(\cdot)
,
C(\cdot)
,
\bar{C}(\cdot)
,

,
\bar{D}(\cdot)
,
\sigma(\cdot)
and
Q(\cdot)
,
\bar{Q}(\cdot)
,
S(\cdot)
,
\bar{S}(\cdot)
,
R(\cdot)
,
\bar{R}(\cdot)
,

,

are suitable dimensional deterministic matrix/vector-valued functions defined on

,

\displaystyle J_{\scriptscriptstyle T}(x;\bar{u}_{\scriptscriptstyle T}(\cdot))=\inf_{u(\cdot)\in\mathscr{U}[0,T]}J_{\scriptscriptstyle T}(x;u(\cdot))\equiv V_{\scriptscriptstyle T}(x).

(1.3)

The process

Different from the above-mentioned literature primarily focused on a fixed time horizon

The turnpike property, originally discussed by Ramsey

[

and subsequently named by Dorfman–Samuelson–Solow
[
6

, characterizes
the tendency of optimal trajectories to spend a significant amount of time in the vicinity of a particular
steady state, independent of the time horizon.
Extensive progress has been made in the study of turnpike properties for deterministic optimal
control problems, encompassing diverse perspectives such as finite and infinite-dimensional problems,
as well as discrete-time and continuous-time systems, with noteworthy contributions; see, for example,
[
3
,
16
,

,

In stochastic cases, Sun–Wang–Yong
[
20
]
established a weak exponential turnpike
property for an LQ optimal control problem with constant coefficients, followed by a deeper study

[
21
]
on mean-field LQ problems.
For the case of periodic coefficients, Sun–Yong
[
22
]
explored the turnpike property
for the stochastic LQ problem without mean-field terms.

Compared to the aforementioned studies, this paper represents a significant extension by exploring
the turnpike property in mean-field stochastic LQ optimal control problems with periodic coefficients.
The key contributions and novelties of our work can be summarized as follows.

\bullet

Both the state equation and the cost functional incorporate expectations of the state and control
processes. This framework exhibits greater generality and holds significant potential for diverse
applications.

\bullet

The exponential turnpike property is established under the exact detectability condition for
stochastic linear systems, which is weaker than the positive definiteness condition assumed
in
[
20
,
21
,
22
]
.

\bullet

A periodic mean-field stochastic LQ optimal control problem is formulated and solved,
with a linear transformation of its optimal pair’s periodic extension
demonstrated to represent the turnpike limit for Problem (MFLQ)
T
.

\bullet

The exponential turnpike property is established not only in terms of trajectory
but also in the distributional sense.
This provides broader potential applications in practical settings.

Let

For

For a metric space

\displaystyle L^{\infty}(0,\infty;\mathbb{H})\triangleq\left\{\varphi:[0,\infty)\mapsto\mathbb{H}\bigm|\varphi\hbox{ is Lebesgue essentially bounded}\right\},

\displaystyle C([0,\infty);\mathbb{H})\triangleq\left\{\varphi:[0,\infty)\mapsto\mathbb{H}\bigm|\varphi\hbox{ is continuous}\right\},

\displaystyle C([0,T];\mathbb{H})\triangleq\left\{\varphi:[0,T]\mapsto\mathbb{H}\bigm|\varphi\hbox{ is continuous}\right\}.

3
Stability, stabilizability, and detectability

In this section, we introduce the concepts of mean-square exponential stability
and stabilizability for stochastic linear systems, as well as exact detectability
when observation variables exist.
As shown in the subsequent sections, these concepts are crucial to our discussion
of the turnpike property.

Denote by
[A(\cdot),C(\cdot)]
the uncontrolled stochastic linear system

\displaystyle dX(t)=A(t)X(t)dt+C(t)X(t)dW(t),

(3.1)

and by
[A(\cdot),C(\cdot)\,|\,E(\cdot)]
the following system with an observation process
Y(\cdot)
:

\left\{\begin{aligned} dX(t)&=A(t)X(t)dt+C(t)X(t)dW(t),\\
Y(t)&=E(t)X(t),\end{aligned}\right.

(3.2)

where
E(\cdot)\in L^{\infty}(0,\infty;\mathbb{R}^{l\times n})
is a
\tau
-periodic function.
The solution of (
3.1
) with initial state
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
is denoted by
X(\cdot\,;x)
,
and the corresponding observation process is denoted by
Y(\cdot\,;x)
.
Let
\varPhi(\cdot)
be the solution to the following matrix SDE:

d\varPhi(t)=A(t)\varPhi(t)dt+C(t)\varPhi(t)dW(t),\quad\varPhi(0)=I_{n},\quad t\geqslant 0.

Then the state process
X(\cdot\,;x)
and the observation process
Y(\cdot\,;x)
can be represented as follows:

X(t;x)=\varPhi(t)x,\quad Y(t;x)=E(t)\varPhi(t)x,\quad\forall t\geqslant 0.

Definition 3.1
.

The system
[A(\cdot),C(\cdot)]
is called
mean-square exponentially stable

if there exist constants
K,\lambda>0
such that

\mathbb{E}|\varPhi(t)|^{2}\leqslant Ke^{-\lambda t},\quad\forall t\geqslant 0.

Remark 3.2
.

It was shown in
[
22
, Proposition 3.3]
that
[A(\cdot),C(\cdot)]

is mean-square exponentially stable if and only if for each (or equivalently, for some)

\tau
-periodic, uniformly positive definite function
\varLambda(\cdot)\in L^{\infty}(0,\infty;\mathbb{S}_{+}^{n})
,
the Lyapunov differential equation

\displaystyle\dot{P}(t)+P(t)A(t)+A(t)^{\top}P(t)+C(t)^{\top}P(t)C(t)+\varLambda(t)=0

(3.3)

admits a
\tau
-periodic, uniformly positive definite solution
P(\cdot)\in C([0,\infty);\mathbb{S}_{+}^{n})
.

Definition 3.3
.

The system
[A(\cdot),C(\cdot)\,|\,E(\cdot)]
is called
exactly detectable

if

\lim_{t\to\infty}\mathbb{E}|X(t;x)|^{2}=0

for any initial state
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
satisfying

Y(t;x)=0,\quad\text{a.s.}

for almost every
t\geqslant 0
.

Remark 3.4
.

If
[A(\cdot),C(\cdot)]
is mean-square exponentially stable, then by definition,

[A(\cdot),C(\cdot)\,|\,E(\cdot)]
is exactly detectable for all
E(\cdot)\in L^{\infty}(0,\infty;\mathbb{R}^{l\times n})
.
Conversely, if
l\geqslant n
and
E(t)
has rank
n
for almost every
t\geqslant 0
,
then
[A(\cdot),C(\cdot)\,|\,E(\cdot)]
is exactly detectable.

The following result establishes a connection between mean-square exponential stability
and exact detectability. For the proof, we refer the reader to
[
5
, Theorem 3.1]
.

Proposition 3.5
.

Suppose that
[A(\cdot),C(\cdot)\,|\,E(\cdot)]
is exactly detectable. If for

\varLambda(\cdot)\triangleq E(\cdot)^{\top}E(\cdot),

equation (
3.3
) admits a
\tau
-periodic solution
P(\cdot)\in C([0,\infty);\bar{\mathbb{S}}_{+}^{n})
,
then
[A(\cdot),C(\cdot)]
is mean-square exponentially stable.

Next, we consider the following controlled linear system, which we denote by

[A(\cdot),C(\cdot);B(\cdot),D(\cdot)]
for simplicity:

dX(t)=[A(t)X(t)+B(t)u(t)]dt+[C(t)X(t)+D(t)u(t)]dW(t),\quad t\geqslant 0.

(3.4)

When
C(\cdot)=0
and
D(\cdot)=0
, the above system
[A(\cdot),0\,;B(\cdot),0]

degenerates into a controlled linear ODE system.

Definition 3.6
.

The system
[A(\cdot),C(\cdot);B(\cdot),D(\cdot)]
is called
mean-square exponentially stabilizable

if there exists a
\tau
-periodic function
\varTheta(\cdot)\in L^{\infty}(0,\infty;\mathbb{R}^{m\times n})
such
that the uncontrolled system
[A(\cdot)+B(\cdot)\varTheta(\cdot),C(\cdot)+D(\cdot)\varTheta(\cdot)]
is mean-square
exponentially stable.
In this case,
\varTheta(\cdot)
is called a
stabilizer
of
[A(\cdot),C(\cdot);B(\cdot),D(\cdot)]
.

In preparation for establishing a connection between mean-square exponential stabilizability
and exact detectability, we first present the following lemma.

Lemma 3.7
.

Let
N(\cdot)\in L^{\infty}(0,\infty;\mathbb{S}^{m})
be a
\tau
-periodic, uniformly positive definite function.
Let
\varTheta(\cdot)\in L^{\infty}(0,\infty;\mathbb{R}^{m\times n})
also be
\tau
-periodic,
and set
E_{\scriptscriptstyle\varTheta}(t)\triangleq\begin{pmatrix}E(t)\\
N(t)^{1/2}\varTheta(t)\end{pmatrix}
.
If
[A(\cdot),C(\cdot)\,|\,E(\cdot)]
is exactly detectable,
then
[A(\cdot)+B(\cdot)\varTheta(\cdot),C(\cdot)+D(\cdot)\varTheta(\cdot)\,|\,E_{\scriptscriptstyle\varTheta}(\cdot)]
is also exactly detectable.

Proof.

Denote by
X_{\scriptscriptstyle\varTheta}(\cdot\,;x)
the solution of

\left\{\begin{aligned} dX_{\scriptscriptstyle\varTheta}(t)&=[A(t)+B(t)\varTheta(t)]X_{\scriptscriptstyle\varTheta}(t)dt+[C(t)+D(t)\varTheta(t)]X_{\scriptscriptstyle\varTheta}(t)dW(t),\quad t\geqslant 0,\\
X_{\scriptscriptstyle\varTheta}(0)&=x.\end{aligned}\right.

(3.5)

If
E_{\scriptscriptstyle\varTheta}(t)X_{\scriptscriptstyle\varTheta}(t;x)=0

a.s.
for
\text{a.e.}~t\geqslant 0
, then

E(t)X_{\scriptscriptstyle\varTheta}(t;x)=0,\quad N(t)^{1/2}\varTheta(t)X_{\scriptscriptstyle\varTheta}(t;x)=0,\quad\text{a.s.}~\text{a.e.}~t\geqslant 0,

which is equivalent to

\displaystyle E(t)X_{\scriptscriptstyle\varTheta}(t;x)=0,\quad\varTheta(t)X_{\scriptscriptstyle\varTheta}(t;x)=0,\quad\text{a.s.}~\text{a.e.}~t\geqslant 0,

(3.6)

since
N(t)
is positive definite for
\text{a.e.}~t\geqslant 0
.
Consequently, the SDE (
3.5
) reduces to

\left\{\begin{aligned} dX_{\scriptscriptstyle\varTheta}(t)&=A(t)X_{\scriptscriptstyle\varTheta}(t)dt+C(t)X_{\scriptscriptstyle\varTheta}(t)dW(t),\quad t\geqslant 0,\\
X_{\scriptscriptstyle\varTheta}(0)&=x.\end{aligned}\right.

Because
X_{\scriptscriptstyle\varTheta}(\cdot\,;x)
and
X(\cdot\,;x)
satisfy the same SDE,
we must have
X_{\scriptscriptstyle\varTheta}(\cdot\,;x)=X(\cdot\,;x)
. By the first equation in (
3.6
)
and the exact detectability of
[A(\cdot),C(\cdot)\,|\,E(\cdot)]
, we obtain

\lim_{t\to\infty}\mathbb{E}|X_{\scriptscriptstyle\varTheta}(t;x)|^{2}=\lim_{t\to\infty}\mathbb{E}|X(t;x)|^{2}=0.

This proves the exact detectability of
[A(\cdot)+B(\cdot)\varTheta(\cdot),C(\cdot)+D(\cdot)\varTheta(\cdot)\,|\,E_{\scriptscriptstyle\varTheta}(\cdot)]
.
∎

We now establish a connection between mean-square exponential stabilizability
and exact detectability.

Proposition 3.8
.

Suppose that
[A(\cdot),C(\cdot)\,|\,E(\cdot)]
is exactly detectable, and let
M(\cdot)\triangleq E(\cdot)^{\top}E(\cdot)
.
Then the system
[A(\cdot),C(\cdot);B(\cdot),D(\cdot)]
is mean-square exponentially stabilizable if and only if
for some (or equivalently, for any)
\tau
-periodic, uniformly positive definite function
N(\cdot)\in L^{\infty}(0,\infty;\mathbb{S}^{m})
,
the differential Riccati equation

\displaystyle\dot{P}+PA+A^{\top}P+C^{\top}PC+M

(3.7)

\displaystyle-(PB+C^{\top}PD)(N+D^{\top}PD)^{-1}(B^{\top}P+D^{\top}PC)=0

admits a unique
\tau
-periodic solution
P(\cdot)\in C([0,\infty);\bar{\mathbb{S}}_{+}^{n})
.
In this case, the function
\varTheta(\cdot)
defined by

\varTheta(t)\triangleq-[N(t)+D(t)^{\top}P(t)D(t)]^{-1}[B(t)^{\top}P(t)+D(t)^{\top}P(t)C(t)]

(3.8)

is a stabilizer of
[A(\cdot),C(\cdot);B(\cdot),D(\cdot)]
.

Proof.

Sufficiency.

Suppose that (
3.7
) admits a
\tau
-periodic solution

Recall from

Proposition 4.4

–

Proof.

First, using similar arguments as in the proofs of

(i) For any

\left\{\begin{aligned} &\dot{\bar{\varPi}}+(\widehat{A}+\widehat{B}\widehat{\varTheta}_{2})^{\top}\bar{\varPi}+\bar{\varPi}(\widehat{A}+\widehat{B}\widehat{\varTheta}_{2})+\varGamma=0,\quad t\in[0,T_{1}],\\

\displaystyle\varGamma

\displaystyle\triangleq\widehat{C}^{\top}\bar{P}\widehat{C}-\widehat{\varTheta}_{2}^{\top}\widehat{B}^{\top}\bar{\varPi}-\bar{\varPi}\widehat{B}\widehat{\varTheta}_{2}-\widehat{\varTheta}_{2}^{\top}(\widehat{R}+\widehat{D}^{\top}P_{\scriptscriptstyle T_{2}}\widehat{D})\widehat{\varTheta}_{2}+\widehat{\varTheta}_{1}^{\top}(\widehat{R}+\widehat{D}^{\top}P_{\scriptscriptstyle T_{1}}\widehat{D})\widehat{\varTheta}_{1}

\dot{\varLambda}_{\scriptscriptstyle T}(t)+\varLambda_{\scriptscriptstyle T}(t)[\widehat{A}(t)+\widehat{B}(t)\widehat{\varTheta}(t)]+[\widehat{A}(t)+\widehat{B}(t)\widehat{\varTheta}(t)]^{\top}\varLambda_{\scriptscriptstyle T}(t)+\varGamma(t)=0,

\varGamma\triangleq(\widehat{C}+\widehat{D}\widehat{\varTheta})^{\top}\varSigma_{\scriptscriptstyle T}(\widehat{C}+\widehat{D}\widehat{\varTheta})+(\widehat{\varTheta}-\widehat{\varTheta}_{\scriptscriptstyle T})^{\top}(\widehat{R}+\widehat{D}^{\top}P_{\scriptscriptstyle T}\widehat{D})(\widehat{\varTheta}-\widehat{\varTheta}_{\scriptscriptstyle T}).

\displaystyle\widehat{\varTheta}-\widehat{\varTheta}_{\scriptscriptstyle T}

\displaystyle=-(\widehat{R}+\widehat{D}^{\top}P_{\scriptscriptstyle T}\widehat{D})^{-1}[\widehat{B}^{\top}\varLambda_{\scriptscriptstyle T}+\widehat{D}^{\top}\varSigma_{\scriptscriptstyle T}(\widehat{C}+\widehat{D}\widehat{\varTheta})],

(4.8)

Since
\widehat{\varTheta}(\cdot)

for some constants

\tau

Let
P(\cdot)

\tau

), and (

For notational simplicity, let

\displaystyle{\cal A}(\cdot)

Consider the following controlled mean-field SDE over

\left\{\begin{aligned} d{\cal X}(t)&=\{{\cal A}(t){\cal X}(t)+\bar{\cal A}(t)\mathbb{E}[{\cal X}(t)]+B(t)v(t)+\bar{B}(t)\mathbb{E}[v(t)]+b(t)\}dt\\
&\hphantom{=\ }+\{{\cal C}(t){\cal X}(t)+\bar{\cal C}(t)\mathbb{E}[{\cal X}(t)]+D(t)v(t)+\bar{D}(t)\mathbb{E}[v(t)]+\sigma(t)\}dW(t),\\

(5.2)

where

.

The following result establishes the well-posedness of the SDE (

).

Proposition 5.1
.

Let
(A1)
–
(A3)
hold. For any
v(\cdot)\in\mathscr{U}[0,\tau]
, the SDE (
5.2
)
admits a unique (in the distribution sense) square integrable solution.

Proof.

Let
{\cal P}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
be the set of probability measures on
(\mathbb{R}^{n},\mathscr{B}(\mathbb{R}^{n}))

having finite second moment.
We equip
{\cal P}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
with the
L^{2}
-Wasserstein distance:

d(\nu_{1},\nu_{2})\triangleq\inf\left\{\sqrt{\mathbb{E}|\xi_{1}-\xi_{2}|^{2}}\Bigm|\xi_{i}\text{ is a random variable in $\mathbb{R}^{n}$ with }\mu_{\scriptscriptstyle\xi_{i}}=\nu_{i};~i=1,2\right\},

where
\mu_{\scriptscriptstyle\xi_{i}}
denotes the distribution of
\xi_{i}
.
Thus,
{\cal P}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
is a complete metric space.
Note that for any positive definite matrix
M,N>0
, the function
d_{\scriptscriptstyle M,N}(\cdot\,,\cdot)

defined by

\displaystyle d_{\scriptscriptstyle M,N}(\nu_{1},\nu_{2})

\displaystyle\triangleq\inf\Big\{\big[\mathbb{E}\langle M[\xi-\mathbb{E}(\xi)],\xi-\mathbb{E}(\xi)\rangle+\langle N\mathbb{E}(\xi),\mathbb{E}(\xi)\rangle\big]^{1/2}\Bigm|\xi=\xi_{1}-\xi_{2},

\displaystyle\hphantom{=\inf\bigg\lx@text@lbrace }\xi_{i}\text{ is a random variable in $\mathbb{R}^{n}$ with }\mu_{\scriptscriptstyle\xi_{i}}=\nu_{i};~i=1,2\Big\}

is also a distance on
{\cal P}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, which is equivalent to
d(\cdot\,,\cdot)
.
Denote by
{\cal X}(\cdot\,;\nu)
the solution (which is unique in the distribution sense) of

\left\{\begin{aligned} d{\cal X}(t)&=\{{\cal A}(t){\cal X}(t)+\bar{\cal A}(t)\mathbb{E}[{\cal X}(t)]+B(t)v(t)+\bar{B}(t)\mathbb{E}[v(t)]+b(t)\}dt\\
&\hphantom{=\ }+\{{\cal C}(t){\cal X}(t)+\bar{\cal C}(t)\mathbb{E}[{\cal X}(t)]+D(t)v(t)+\bar{D}(t)\mathbb{E}[v(t)]+\sigma(t)\}dW(t),\\
\mu_{\scriptscriptstyle{\cal X}(0)}&=\nu\in{\cal P}(\mathbb{R}^{n}).\end{aligned}\right.

Then we can define a mapping
{\cal L}:{\cal P}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\mapsto{\cal P}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
by

{\cal L}(\nu)\triangleq\mu_{\scriptscriptstyle X(\tau;\nu)}.

Next, we show that the
{\cal L}
is a contraction mapping.
To this end, let
\nu_{i}\in{\cal P}(\mathbb{R}^{n})

(i=1,2)
, and let
\xi_{i}
be
\mathscr{F}_{0}
-measurable
random variables with
\mu_{\scriptscriptstyle\xi_{i}}=\nu_{i}
.
Denote by
{\cal X}_{i}(\cdot)
the solution of

\left\{\begin{aligned} d{\cal X}(t)&=\{{\cal A}(t){\cal X}(t)+\bar{\cal A}(t)\mathbb{E}[{\cal X}(t)]+B(t)v(t)+\bar{B}(t)\mathbb{E}[v(t)]+b(t)\}dt\\
&\hphantom{=\ }+\{{\cal C}(t){\cal X}(t)+\bar{\cal C}(t)\mathbb{E}[{\cal X}(t)]+D(t)v(t)+\bar{D}(t)\mathbb{E}[v(t)]+\sigma(t)\}dW(t),\\
{\cal X}(0)&=\xi_{i}.\end{aligned}\right.

Then
\widehat{\cal X}(\cdot)\triangleq{\cal X}_{1}(\cdot)-{\cal X}_{2}(\cdot)
satisfies

\left\{\begin{aligned} d\widehat{\cal X}(t)&=\big\{{\cal A}(t)\widehat{\cal X}(t)+\bar{\cal A}(t)\mathbb{E}[\widehat{\cal X}(t)]\big\}dt+\big\{{\cal C}(t)\widehat{\cal X}(t)+\bar{\cal C}(t)\mathbb{E}[\widehat{\cal X}(t)]\big\}dW(t),\\
\widehat{\cal X}(0)&=\xi_{1}-\xi_{2}\equiv\hat{\xi}.\end{aligned}\right.

Clearly,
\mathbb{E}[\widehat{\cal X}(\cdot)]
and
\widehat{\cal Y}(\cdot)\triangleq\widehat{\cal X}(\cdot)-\mathbb{E}[\widehat{\cal X}(\cdot)]
satisfies

\left\{\begin{aligned} d\mathbb{E}[\widehat{\cal X}(t)]&=\widehat{\cal A}(t)\mathbb{E}[\widehat{\cal X}(t)]dt,\\
\mathbb{E}[\widehat{\cal X}(0)]&=\mathbb{E}[\hat{\xi}],\end{aligned}\right.

and

\left\{\begin{aligned} d\widehat{\cal Y}(t)&={\cal A}(t)\widehat{\cal Y}(t)dt+\big\{{\cal C}(t)\widehat{\cal Y}(t)+\widehat{\cal C}(t)\mathbb{E}[\widehat{\cal X}(t)]\big\}dW(t),\\
\widehat{\cal Y}(0)&=\hat{\xi}-\mathbb{E}[\hat{\xi}],\end{aligned}\right.

respectively. By
4.2
,
[{\cal A}(\cdot),{\cal C}(\cdot)]
and
[\widehat{\cal A}(\cdot),0]

are exponentially mean-square stable.
Thus, according to
3.2
, there exists a unique
\tau
-periodic,
uniformly positive definite function

such that

\dot{M}(t)+M(t){\cal A}(t)+{\cal A}(t)^{\top}M(t)+{\cal C}(t)^{\top}M(t){\cal C}(t)+I_{n}=0,

and there exists a unique
\tau
-periodic, uniformly positive definite function

By Itô’s rule,

\displaystyle{d\over dt}\mathbb{E}\langle M(t)\widehat{\cal Y}(t),\widehat{\cal Y}(t)\rangle

\displaystyle=-\mathbb{E}|\widehat{\cal Y}(t)|^{2}+\langle\widehat{\cal C}(t)^{\top}M(t)\widehat{\cal C}(t)\mathbb{E}[\widehat{\cal X}(t)],\mathbb{E}[\widehat{\cal X}(t)]\rangle,

\displaystyle{d\over dt}\langle N(t)\mathbb{E}[\widehat{\cal X}(t)],\mathbb{E}[\widehat{\cal X}(t)]\rangle

\displaystyle=-|\mathbb{E}[\widehat{\cal X}(t)]|^{2}-\langle\widehat{\cal C}(t)^{\top}M(t)\widehat{\cal C}(t)\mathbb{E}[\widehat{\cal X}(t)],\mathbb{E}[\widehat{\cal X}(t)]\rangle.

Since the continuous functions

and

-periodic and uniformly positive definite,

Consequently,

\displaystyle{d\over dt}\Big[\mathbb{E}\langle M(t)\widehat{\cal Y}(t),\widehat{\cal Y}(t)\rangle+\langle N(t)\mathbb{E}[\widehat{\cal X}(t)],\mathbb{E}[\widehat{\cal X}(t)]\rangle\Big]

\displaystyle\quad=-\Big[\mathbb{E}|\widehat{\cal Y}(t)|^{2}+|\mathbb{E}[\widehat{\cal X}(t)]|^{2}\Big]

\displaystyle\quad\leqslant-\beta\Big[\mathbb{E}\langle M(t)\widehat{\cal Y}(t),\widehat{\cal Y}(t)\rangle+\langle N(t)\mathbb{E}[\widehat{\cal X}(t)],\mathbb{E}[\widehat{\cal X}(t)]\rangle\Big].

That is,

is a contraction mapping with respect to the distance

Therefore, by the fixed-point theorem, the SDE (

\displaystyle 2\mathbb{E}\big\langle H_{\scriptscriptstyle T}(t)-\mathbb{E}[H_{\scriptscriptstyle T}(t)],\big(M(t)[{\cal A}_{\scriptscriptstyle T}(t)-{\cal A}(t)]+{\cal C}_{\scriptscriptstyle T}(t)^{\top}M(t)[{\cal C}_{\scriptscriptstyle T}(t)-{\cal C}(t)]\big){\cal X}^{*}_{0}(t)\big\rangle

\displaystyle\quad\leqslant\delta^{-1}\big|M(t)[{\cal A}_{\scriptscriptstyle T}(t)-{\cal A}(t)]+{\cal C}_{\scriptscriptstyle T}(t)^{\top}M(t)[{\cal C}_{\scriptscriptstyle T}(t)-{\cal C}(t)]\big|^{2}\mathbb{E}|{\cal X}^{*}_{0}(t)|^{2}

\displaystyle\hphantom{\quad\leqslant\ }+\delta\mathbb{E}|H_{\scriptscriptstyle T}(t)-\mathbb{E}[H_{\scriptscriptstyle T}(t)]|^{2}

\displaystyle\quad\leqslant\delta\mathbb{E}|H_{\scriptscriptstyle T}(t)-\mathbb{E}[H_{\scriptscriptstyle T}(t)]|^{2}+Ke^{-\lambda(T-t)}.

(6.13)

Moreover,

\mathbb{E}\big\langle M(t)[{\cal C}_{\scriptscriptstyle T}(t)-{\cal C}(t)]{\cal X}^{*}_{0}(t),[{\cal C}_{\scriptscriptstyle T}(t)-{\cal C}(t)]{\cal X}^{*}_{0}(t)\big\rangle\leqslant Ke^{-\lambda(T-t)},

(6.14)

and

\big\langle M(t)k(t),k(t)\big\rangle\leqslant K\Big[e^{-\lambda t}+e^{-\lambda(T-t)}\Big].

(6.15)

Now, set

G(t)\triangleq\mathbb{E}\big\langle M(t)\big(H_{\scriptscriptstyle T}(t)-\mathbb{E}[H_{\scriptscriptstyle T}(t)]\big),H_{\scriptscriptstyle T}(t)-\mathbb{E}[H_{\scriptscriptstyle T}(t)]\big\rangle,\quad t\in[0,T].

Combining (
6.12
)–(
6.15
) and noting (
6.10
), it follows from (
6.11
) that

\displaystyle{dG(t)\over dt}

\displaystyle\leqslant\Big[-\delta+Ke^{-\lambda(T-t)}\Big]\mathbb{E}|H_{\scriptscriptstyle T}(t)-\mathbb{E}[H_{\scriptscriptstyle T}(t)]|^{2}+K\Big[e^{-\lambda t}+e^{-\lambda(T-t)}\Big]

\displaystyle\leqslant\Big[-\delta\beta+\alpha Ke^{-\lambda(T-t)}\Big]G(t)+K\Big[e^{-\lambda t}+e^{-\lambda(T-t)}\Big].

Using the Gronwall’s inequality and recalling (
6.8
), we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}|\bar{X}_{\scriptscriptstyle T}(t)-{\cal X}^{*}(t)|^{2}

\displaystyle=|\mathbb{E}[H_{\scriptscriptstyle T}(t)]|^{2}+\mathbb{E}|H_{\scriptscriptstyle T}(t)-\mathbb{E}[H_{\scriptscriptstyle T}(t)]|^{2}

\displaystyle\leqslant|\mathbb{E}[H_{\scriptscriptstyle T}(t)]|^{2}+\alpha G(t)\leqslant K\Big[e^{-\lambda t}+e^{-\lambda(T-t)}\Big],\quad\forall t\in[0,T].

Finally, since

\displaystyle\bar{u}_{\scriptscriptstyle T}(t)-u^{*}(t)

\displaystyle=\varTheta_{\scriptscriptstyle T}(t)\Big[\bar{X}_{\scriptscriptstyle T}(t)-{\cal X}^{*}(t)\Big]+\Big[\widehat{\varTheta}_{\scriptscriptstyle T}(t)-\varTheta_{\scriptscriptstyle T}(t)\Big]\mathbb{E}\Big[\bar{X}_{\scriptscriptstyle T}(t)-{\cal X}^{*}(t)\Big]

\displaystyle\hphantom{=\ }+\Big[\varTheta_{\scriptscriptstyle T}(t)-\varTheta(t)\Big]{\cal X}^{*}_{0}(t)+\Big[\widehat{\varTheta}_{\scriptscriptstyle T}(t)-\widehat{\varTheta}(t)\Big]\mathbb{E}[{\cal X}^{*}(t)]+\Big[\phi_{\scriptscriptstyle T}(t)-v^{*}(t)\Big],

we get the desired result (
6.2
) immediately.
∎

Tori Approximation of Families of Diagonally Invariant Measures
Omri Nisan Solan, Yuval Yifrach

Abstract
Let A A be the full diagonal group in SL n ⁡ ( ℝ ) \operatorname{SL}_{n}(\mathbb{R}) . We study possible limits of Haar measures on periodic A A -orbits in the space of unimodular lattices X n X_{n} . We prove the existence of non-ergodic measures which are also weak limits of these compactly supported A A -invariant measures. In fact, given any countably many A A -invariant ergodic measures, we show that there exists a sequence of Haar measures on periodic A A -orbits such that the ergodic decomposition of its weak limit has these measures as factors with positive weight. In particular, we prove that any compactly supported A A -invariant and ergodic measure is the weak limit of the restriction of different compactly supported periodic measures to a fixed proportion of the time. In addition, for any c ∈ ( 0 , 1 ] c\in(0,1] we find a sequence of Haar measures on periodic A A orbits that converges weakly to c ​ m X n cm_{X_{n}} where m X n m_{X_{n}} denotes the Haar measure on X n X_{n} . In particular, we prove the existence of partial escape of mass for Haar measures on periodic A A orbits. These results give affirmative answers to questions posed by Shapira in [ 18 ] . Our proofs are based on a modification of Shapira’s proof in [ 18 ] and on a generalization of a construction of Cassels, as well as on effective equidistribution estimates of Hecke neighbors by Clozel, Oh and Ullmo in [ 5 ] .

Introduction

Let

\mathbb{R}^{n}

Indeed, there is a surjective map between the set of full

Denote:

\mathcal{M}(X_{n})^{A}_{e}=\{\text{ergodic }A\text{-invariant probability measures on $X_{n}$}\};

\mathcal{M}(X_{n})^{A}_{c}=\{\text{ergodic }A\text{-invariant probability measures on $X_{n}$ supported on compact orbits}\}.

We endow
\mathcal{M}(X_{n})
with the weak-
\ast

\overline{\mathcal{M}(X_{n})^{A}_{c}}
in it.
It is known that apart from measures of compact orbits,
m_{X_{n}},0\in\mathcal{M}(X_{n})

2
]

[
19

In this paper, we show that

1.1
Measures with Predetermined Ergodic Factors

The first family of measures we consider contains non-ergodic measures. Given any finitely many elements
\nu_{1},\dots,\nu_{k}\in\mathcal{M}(X_{n})^{A}_{e}
, we find a measure in
\mu\in\overline{\mathcal{M}(X_{n})^{A}_{c}}
such that the ergodic decomposition of
\mu
has positive weight on each of the
\nu_{i}
’s for
i=1,\dots,k
. In
[
18
, p.4 Q1, Q2]
, Shapira asks whether non-ergodic measures and measures with a given periodic
A
-invariant ergodic factor can be obtained as weak limits of
\mathcal{M}(X_{n})^{A}_{c}
elements. The following Theorem shows in particular that this is indeed the case:

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
(\nu_{i})_{i=1}^{N}\subseteq\mathcal{M}(X_{n})^{A}_{e}
be a possibly infinite sequence of
A
-invariant ergodic measures. Then there exists
\mu\in\overline{\mathcal{M}(X_{n})^{A}_{c}}
such that the ergodic decomposition of
\mu
has positive weights on the
\nu_{i}
’s. In particular for any
\mu\in\mathcal{M}(X_{n})^{A}_{c}
there exists
\tilde{\mu}\in\overline{\mathcal{M}(X_{n})^{A}_{c}\setminus\{\mu\}}
such that the ergodic decomposition of
\tilde{\mu}
, contains
\mu
with positive weight.

Remark 1.2

(The weights in the ergodic decomposition)
.

For a single measure
\nu_{1}
, we get an explicit lower bound on the weight, namely
\mu\geq n\left(\frac{\lfloor n/2\rfloor}{4n^{2}(n^{2}-1)}\right)^{n-1}\nu_{1}
.
Generally, the proof fixes a sequence of positive real numbers
(c_{i})_{i=1}^{N}
with
\sum_{i=1}^{N}c_{i}=1
and constructs
\mu
with
\mu\geq\left(\frac{\lfloor n/2\rfloor}{4n^{2}(n-1)^{2}(n+1)}\right)^{n-1}c_{i}^{n-1}\nu_{i}
.

1.2
Partial Escape of Mass and Entire Mass Approximations

The next measures we will find in
\overline{\mathcal{M}(X_{n})^{A}_{c}}
are measures with total mass strictly between
0
and
1
. In his paper
[
18
, p.4, Q2]
, Shapira proves that the zero measure is contained in
\overline{\mathcal{M}(X_{n})^{A}_{c}}
and poses the question of whether measures with total mass strictly between
0
and
1
also lie there.
In
[
8
]
David and Shapira find a sequence of orbits with a lower bound on the escape of mass but did not prove that the limit is not zero.
We answer Shapira’s question in the affirmative in two ways. The first is the following corollary of Theorem
1.1
:

Corollary 1.3
.

For every sequence
\nu_{1},\nu_{2},\dots,\nu_{k},\ldots\in\mathcal{M}(X_{n})^{A}_{e}
there exists
\mu\in\overline{\mathcal{M}(X_{n})^{A}_{c}}
such that
\mu(X_{n})\in(0,1)
and the ergodic decomposition of
\mu
has positive weights on
\nu_{i}
for all
i
.

The next family of measures we find inside the closure of the compactly supported periodic measures is the family
\{cm_{X_{n}}:c\in(0,1]\}
where
m_{X_{n}}
is the Haar probability measure on
X_{n}
. This family of measures is substantially different from the measure families we approximated so far since we can describe their entire ergodic decomposition (which is simply
cm_{X_{n}}
) rather than describe some of the factors and have no control over the remaining factors.
In
[
19
]
, they show that
m_{X_{n}}\in\overline{\mathcal{M}(X_{n})^{A}_{c}}
.
Here we prove a more general theorem.

Theorem 1.4
.

Let
c\in(0,1]
. Then
cm_{X_{n}}\in\overline{\mathcal{M}(X_{n})^{A}_{c}}
.

In particular, the above theorem gives another answer to Shapira’s question regarding the partial escape of mass.

Remark 1.5

(Volume of the compact orbits)
.

Every compact orbit
Ax
has a volume
\operatorname{Vol}(Ax)=\operatorname{Vol}(\mathbb{R}^{n-1}/\log\operatorname{stab}_{A}(x))
and a corresponding order

. The compact orbits we construct for the proof of Theorem
1.1
have volume
O(\log\operatorname{Disc}(R_{x}))
. As written, our proof of Theorem
1.4
doesn’t provide estimates on the volumes.
In Remark
5.13
, we argue that similarly to the proof of Theorem
1.4
, one can choose the approximating compact orbits

to have volume
O(\operatorname{Disc}(R_{x})^{c^{\prime}})
for some
c^{\prime}>0
.

The estimate of the regulator in Remark
1.5
that provides a sequence of orbits with volume polynomial in the discriminant of the corresponding order and partial escape of mass, contradicts
[
10
, Conjecture 1.11]
, which states that every such sequence of orbits must converge to an algebraic measure. The spirit of the conjecture is not disproved, and one could hope that the conjecture can be corrected by considering only partial escape of mass. We do not believe this is the case for general orbits.
Instead, we provide the following conjecture:

Conjecture 1.6
.

Let
Ax_{i}\subset X_{n}
be a sequence of compact orbits corresponding to orders
R_{i}
. Suppose that
\operatorname{Vol}(Ax_{i})>\operatorname{disc}(Ax_{i})^{\varepsilon}
for some
\varepsilon>0
. Here
\operatorname{disc}(Ax_{i})
is the discriminant of the orbit
Ax_{i}
. It is defined as in
[
10
]
.
Then
\lim_{i\to\infty}\mu_{Ax_{i}}
is a combination of countably many periodic algebraic orbits of groups
H_{j}\supsetneq A
, with total mass bounded from below as a function of

.
Moreover, if
K_{i}=R_{i}\otimes\mathbb{Q}
, and
(\operatorname{disc}(R_{i})/\operatorname{disc}K_{i})<\operatorname{disc}(R_{i})^{\delta}
for some
\delta=\delta(\varepsilon)
, then
\lim_{i\to\infty}\mu_{Ax_{i}}
is algebraic.
We suppose the same will hold for general arithmetic
G/\Gamma
with the appropriate generalization of
\operatorname{disc}(R_{i})/\operatorname{disc}K_{i}

[
10

1.3
Method of Proof

We will first show a sketch of the proof of Theorem
1.1
,
for the particular case
k=1
where we approximate a single measure
\mu
.
We will first show how to do that depending on a conjecture.
Then we will show how to overcome the need to use the conjecture.

Conjecture 1.7
.

Let
K
be a number field. For every
I\leq\mathcal{O}_{K}
, we get a measure
\mu_{I}
on a compact orbit associated to
I
(See Definition
2.6
).
The measure
\mu_{I}
depends only on the class of
I
in the class group
{\rm Cl}_{\mathcal{O}_{K}}
.
It is believed that

\displaystyle\mu_{K}:=\frac{1}{\#{\rm Cl}_{\mathcal{O}_{K}}}\sum_{I\in{\rm Cl}_{\mathcal{O}_{K}}}\mu_{I}\xrightarrow{\operatorname{disc}(K)\to\infty}m_{X_{n}},

(1.1)

where
m_{X_{n}}
is the Haar measure on
X_{n}
, and
\operatorname{disc}(K)
is the discriminant of
K
.
Moreover, we expect that
d_{\mathcal{M}^{1}(X_{n})}(\mu_{K},m_{X_{n}})=O(\operatorname{disc}(K)^{-\star})
, for
\star>0
, where
d_{\mathcal{M}^{1}(X_{n})}
is the natural metric on the space of probability measures
\mathcal{M}^{1}(X_{n})
.

In the following paragraph we will use the
\Theta
notation. In this notation, we write
f=\Theta(g)
for two functions
f,g
, if there exist constants
C_{1},C_{2}>0
such that
f<C_{1}g
and
g<C_{2}f
.

Another ingredient of the proof is a construction of a number field with very small units compared to its discriminant.
A construction of Shapira
[
18
]
following Cassels
[
4
]
is a series of number fields
K
whose unit groups are generated by elements
u\in\mathcal{O}_{K}^{\times}
such that
\log|u|=\Theta(\log{\rm disc}(K))
(which is exponentially small compared to the bound given by the class number formula:
\log|u|=O({\rm disc}(K)^{1/(n-1)})
, provided that the unit lattice is sufficiently balanced).
Applying Conjecture
1.7
to these number fields, we get that the different measures
\mu_{I}
for
I\in{\rm Cl}_{\mathcal{O}_{K}}
are equidistributed. Using this equidistribution we can approximate a generic point
x\in X_{n}
of our desired ergodic measure
\mu
, namely there is
I\in{\rm Cl}_{\mathcal{O}_{K}}
such that
d_{X_{n}}(x,x^{\prime})=O({\rm disc}^{-\star}(K))
for some
x^{\prime}\in\supp\mu_{I}
.
Hence for
r=\Theta(\log({\rm disc}(K)))
ball in
A
,
a\in B_{A}(r)
we have that
ax
is close to
ax^{\prime}\in\supp(\mu_{I})
.
On the other hand, the orbit
ax^{\prime}
is not much larger. The periods are controlled by the size of the units, which are again
\Theta(\log({\rm disc}(K)))
. This would imply that we can approximate every ball in an
A
-orbit with measures
\mu_{I}
for
I
in the class group of the special number field
K
.

To overcome the need to use Conjecture
1.7
, we prove a weaker form on a special collection of orders. We take a measure
\mu_{I}
as above and apply to it a
p
-Hecke operator
T_{p}
. On the one hand, Hecke operators are known to have well-behaved equidistribution properties (See Clozel, Oh and Ullmo
[
5
]
for an effective equidistribution result on Hecke operators and
[
2
,
1
]
for other results using equidistribution of Hecke operations applied to closed orbits). We expect to have
f(T_{p}\mu_{I},m_{X_{n}})=O(p^{-\star})
.
On the other hand, the resulting measure
T_{p}\mu_{I}
is the average of measures on compact orbits, corresponding to modules of a sub-order
R=\mathbb{Z}+p\mathcal{O}_{K}\subset\mathcal{O}_{K}
of index
[\mathcal{O}_{K}:R]=p^{n-1}
.

While this guarantees that the orbits would be equidistributed, to bound the sizes of the orbits we tweak the construction of
K
.
Not only
\mathcal{O}_{K}
should have units of logarithmic size, but also
R
. This guarantees that
T_{p}\mu_{I}
is an average of measures of logarithmically small compact orbits, and enables the proof to work without Conjecture
1.7
.

We will now describe the construction of Shapira
[
18
, Section 5.1]
, following Cassels
[
4
]
, and our modification.

Construction 1.8
.

For any tuple
\overline{m}=(m_{1},\dots,m_{n})\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}
define the polynomial
R_{\overline{m}}=\prod_{i=1}^{n}(x-m_{i})-1
. Under mild conditions on
\overline{m}
we get that
R_{\overline{m}}
is irreducible.
In the number field
\mathbb{Q}[x]/(R_{\overline{m}})
, the elements
x-m_{i}
are units.
The description of these units guarantees that they have polynomial size compared to the discriminant of the number field.

Shapira uses the structure of the units to show that the compact
A
-orbits corresponding to
\mathbb{Z}[x]/(R_{\overline{m}})
spend a high percentage of their time near the cusp (see Theorem
5.9
).

In our construction, we use a similar construction of polynomials:

Construction 1.9
.

Let
p
be a prime number.
For any tuple
\overline{m}=(m_{1},\dots,m_{n})\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}
define the polynomial
R_{\overline{m},p}=\frac{\prod_{i=1}^{n}(px-m_{i})-1}{p^{n}}
. Under some modular conditions on
\overline{m}
we get that
R_{\overline{m},p}
is integral and irreducible.
In the number field
K=\mathbb{Q}[x]/(R_{\overline{m},p})
, the elements
px-m_{i}
are units.
The description of these units guarantees that they have polynomial size compared to the discriminant of the number field, but also lies in
\mathbb{Z}+p\mathcal{O}_{K}
.

This allows us to find a small compact orbit
Ax
such that, after applying the Hecke operator to it, the new collection of compact orbits is effectively equidistributed in
X_{n}
and is composed of many small compact
A
orbits.

Remark 1.10
.

Conjecture
1.7
seems very complicated.
It was proven in case
n=2
in
[
9
]
.
A non-effective version of it is proven for
n=3
under some splitting restriction in
[
11
]
. The proof uses a deep and nontrivial number theoretic result, namely, the sub-convexity estimate of
L
functions of cubic fields.

The proof of Theorem
1.4
goes along similar lines. We start with a compact orbit
Ax_{0}
, constructed using the techniques used in
[
18
]
. As in
[
18
]
, this orbit will lie in the cusp for a density
1
proportion of its lifetime. To achieve a measure with partial escape of mass, we apply two Hecke operators. The first Hecke operator is responsible for pulling an appropriate proportion of the orbit out of the cusp. The second operator is used to get equidistribution from the section of the orbit outside of the cusp (this can be done with one operator, see Remark
5.13
).
However, this is not a single
A
-orbit. Instead, we prove by ergodicity that at least one of the
A
-orbits that constitute the Hecke operators applied to the orbit has to be almost equidistributed as well.

Remark 1.11

(Comparison to
[
2
]
and
[
19
]
)
.

Plugging
c=1
to Theorem
1.4
yields a construction identical to the one in
[
2
]
(with some irrelevant extra steps). We begin with the Hecke operator of a compact orbit and get a new collection of
A
-orbits. We obtain its equidistribution and then use ergodicity to sample one of them that equidistributes.
The construction in
[
19
]
is of the same kind, however, they show a way to sample a specific compact orbit that equidistributes, not using ergodicity in the same fashion.

1.4
Further Research

The first natural improvement of Theorem
1.1
is the full approximation.

Open Question 1.12
.

Let
\mu\in\mathcal{M}(X_{n})^{A}_{c}
. Can it be the limit measure of other measures in
\mathcal{M}(X_{n})^{A}_{c}
? In other words, is
\mu\in\overline{\mathcal{M}(X_{n})^{A}_{c}\setminus\{\mu\}}
?

It can be seen that simply improving the bounds we give in this paper cannot give a positive answer to this question, for the following reason.
If
n=3
,
Ay
is a compact orbit and
Ax
is an orbit. We can consider the set
B=\{a\in A:d_{X_{3}}(ax,Ay)<\delta\}
.
The connected components of
B
are roughly hexagons. It can be seen that two such hexagons
H_{1},H_{2}
cannot have
R
long parts of the boundaries which are
\delta R
close to one another, for some
\delta>0
and all
R>0
sufficiently large.

Focusing on a different aspect of this work, we would like to find even less trivial limits of large
A
-orbits in the following sense:

Open Question 1.13
.

What are the possible limits
\lim_{i\to\infty}\mu_{Ax_{i}}
where
Ax_{i}
is a compact orbit corresponding to an order
R_{i}
satisfying that
\operatorname{reg}(R_{i})>\operatorname{disc}(R_{i})^{\varepsilon}
?
Can it be a non-ergodic
A
-invariant probability measure?
Can it contain as an ergodic component a compact orbit uniform measure
\mu_{Ay}
?

Another possible direction for improvement is by working in different homogenous spaces.
Many questions can be asked, and here we choose to focus on a single homogenous space:

Open Question 1.14
.

Let
B
be an
n^{2}
-dimensional central simple algebra over
\mathbb{Q}
which splits over
\mathbb{R}
. Let
\mathcal{O}_{B}
be an order in
B
. Let
\Gamma=\operatorname{SL}_{1}(\mathcal{O}_{B})\subseteq\operatorname{SL}_{n}(\mathbb{R})
a lattice.
There are many compact orbits in
\operatorname{SL}_{n}(\mathbb{R})/\Gamma
, coming from degree-
n
totally real number fields
K\subset B
.
What are the possible limits of these compact orbits? Can Haar measure be obtained?
Can you extend Theorem
1.1
to this setting? Can you compute an explicit non-Haar limit measure (in contrast to Theorem
1.1
where we have control only over a small portion of the measure)?

Effective Approximations

In this section, we prove an effective approximation theorem of points in

by points of compact orbits. Formally, the goal is to prove the following lemma.

Lemma 3.1

For every compact set
\mathcal{K}\subset X_{n}

\exp(\Lambda_{k})\subseteq A

\Lambda_{k}\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n-1}_{0}

\left|\left(v_{k}^{(i)}\right)_{j}-(1-\delta_{ij}n)t_{k}\right|=O(1)
.

Remark 3.2

(3.1)

of the orbit
Ay_{k}

Definition 3.4

For every sequence of nonnegative integers

Theorem 3.5

Lemma 3.6

For every compact subset

and
p,k_{1}\leq k_{2}\leq\dots\leq k_{n},a=a_{p;k_{1},k_{2},\dots,k_{n}}\in\operatorname{SL}_{n}(\mathbb{R})
as in Definition

d_{X_{n}}\left(x^{\prime},y\right)\leq C(\mathcal{K})\left\|\left.T^{\rm F}_{a}\right|_{L^{2}_{0}(X_{n})}\right\|^{1/(n^{2}-1)}.

(3.2)

Proof.

Recall the right invariant Riemannian metric
d_{\operatorname{SL}_{n}(\mathbb{R})}
on
\operatorname{SL}_{n}(\mathbb{R})
, and its descend to
X_{n}
, the metric
d_{X_{n}}
.
Let
r_{0}<\min(\operatorname{inj}(x),\operatorname{inj}(y))
, where
\operatorname{inj}(x)
is the injectivity radius of

, that is, the maximal radius
r
such that the translation map
g\mapsto gx
is injective on

r

\operatorname{SL}_{n}(\mathbb{R})
.
Let
f_{x}=\chi_{B_{X_{n}}(x;r_{0})}
be the indicator of a radius
r_{0}
ball around
x
.
Then
\int_{X_{n}}f_{x}{\rm d}m_{X_{n}}=\operatorname{vol}(B_{\operatorname{SL}_{n}(\mathbb{R})}(I;r_{0}))=\Theta(r_{0}^{n^{2}-1})
, and a similar equality holds for
f_{y}=\chi_{B_{X_{n}}(y;r_{0})}
.
Denote by
v_{r_{0}}=\operatorname{vol}(B_{\operatorname{SL}_{n}(\mathbb{R})}(I;r_{0}))
.
Then
\tilde{f}_{x}=f_{x}-v_{r_{0}}\in L^{2}_{0}(X_{n})
and

\tilde{f}_{y}=f_{y}-v_{r_{0}}\in L^{2}_{0}(X_{n})

have norm
\|f_{x}\|^{2}=\|f_{y}\|^{2}=v_{r_{0}}(1-v_{r_{0}})^{2}+(1-v_{r_{0}})v_{r_{0}}^{2}=(1-v_{r_{0}})v_{r_{0}}
.
Consider
\left\langle\tilde{f}_{x},T^{\rm F}_{a}(\tilde{f}_{y})\right\rangle
.
On the one hand, it is at most
\left\|\left.T^{\rm F}_{a}\right|_{L^{2}_{0}(X_{n})}\right\|\|\tilde{f}_{x}\|\|\tilde{f}_{y}\|=\left\|\left.T^{\rm F}_{a}\right|_{L^{2}_{0}(X_{n})}\right\|(1-v_{r_{0}})v_{r_{0}}
.
On the other hand, consider the set:

T_{a}^{-1}(B_{X_{n}}(y;r_{0})):=\{x^{\prime}\in X_{n}:T_{a}(x^{\prime})\cap B_{X_{n}}(y;r_{0})\neq\emptyset\}

and note that
T^{\rm F}_{a}(\tilde{f}_{y})|_{(T_{a}^{-1}(B_{X_{n}}(y;r_{0})))^{c}}\equiv-v_{r_{0}}
.
Assume that
T_{a}^{-1}(B_{X_{n}}(y;r_{0}))\cap B_{X_{n}}(x;r_{0})=\emptyset

T^{\rm F}_{a}(\tilde{f}_{y})|_{B_{X_{n}}(x;r_{0})}\equiv-v_{r_{0}}
.
It follows that:

\displaystyle\left\langle\tilde{f}_{x},T^{\rm F}_{a}(\tilde{f}_{y})\right\rangle

\displaystyle=\int_{X_{n}}\tilde{f}_{x}T^{\rm F}_{a}(\tilde{f}_{y}){\rm d}m_{X_{n}}=\int_{X_{n}}f_{x}T^{\rm F}_{a}(\tilde{f}_{y}){\rm d}m_{X_{n}}=\int_{B_{X_{n}}(x;r_{0})}T^{\rm F}_{a}(\tilde{f}_{y}){\rm d}m_{X_{n}}

\displaystyle=v_{r_{0}}\cdot(-v_{r_{0}})=-v_{r_{0}}^{2}.

We deduce that

v_{r_{0}}^{2}\leq\left\|\left.T^{\rm F}_{a}\right|_{L^{2}_{0}(X_{n})}\right\|\cdot\|\tilde{f}_{x}\|\cdot\|\tilde{f}_{y}\|=\left\|\left.T^{\rm F}_{a}\right|_{L^{2}_{0}(X_{n})}\right\|(1-v_{r_{0}})v_{r_{0}},

and hence
v_{r_{0}}\leq\left\|\left.T^{\rm F}_{a}\right|_{L^{2}_{0}(X_{n})}\right\|
.
Using this logic in reverse, we deduce that if
v_{r_{0}}>\left\|\left.T^{\rm F}_{a}\right|_{L^{2}_{0}(X_{n})}\right\|
then
T_{a}^{-1}(B_{X_{n}}(y;r_{0}))\cap B_{X_{n}}(x;r_{0})\neq\emptyset
, that is, there exist

x^{\prime}=g_{0}x,y^{\prime}=g_{1}y
such that
y^{\prime}\in T_{a}(x^{\prime})
and

.
Thus
g_{0}^{-1}y^{\prime}=g_{0}^{-1}g_{1}y\in g_{0}^{-1}T_{a}(x^{\prime})=T_{a}(x)

g_{0}^{-1}y^{\prime}=g_{0}^{-1}g_{1}y

d_{X_{n}}(g_{0}^{-1}g_{1}y,y)\leq 2r_{0}
.

Altogether, we have proved that if
\left\|\left.T^{\rm F}_{a}\right|_{L^{2}_{0}(X_{n})}\right\|<v_{r_{0}}
and
r_{0}\leq\min(\operatorname{inj}(x),\operatorname{inj}(y))
then there exists
x^{\prime\prime}=g_{0}^{-1}g_{1}y
with
x^{\prime\prime}\in T_{a}(x)
and
d_{X_{n}}(x^{\prime\prime},y)\leq 2r_{0}
.
Now, let
\mathcal{K}\subset X_{n}
be a compact set. Denote the minimum of the injectivity radius on
\mathcal{K}
by

.
If
v_{r_{\mathcal{K}}}>\left\|\left.T^{\rm F}_{a}\right|_{L^{2}_{0}(X_{n})}\right\|
, then we can find
r_{0}=\Theta\left(\left\|\left.T^{\rm F}_{a}\right|_{L^{2}_{0}(X_{n})}\right\|^{1/(n^{2}-1)}\right)
with
r_{0}<r_{\mathcal{K}}
and
v_{r_{0}}>\left\|\left.T^{\rm F}_{a}\right|_{L^{2}_{0}(X_{n})}\right\|
, and the desired follows.
If
v_{r_{\mathcal{K}}}\leq\left\|\left.T^{\rm F}_{a}\right|_{L^{2}_{0}(X_{n})}\right\|
, then the desired follows for
C(\mathcal{K})=\frac{\operatorname{diam}(\mathcal{K})}{(v_{r_{\mathcal{K}}})^{1/(n^{2}-1)}}
.

3.2
Construction of special number field

Theorem 3.7
.

For every prime number
p\equiv 1\mod 2n
sufficiently big as a function of
n
, one can find a totally real number field
K
with the following properties:

(
a
)

The unit group
\mathcal{O}_{K}^{\times}
contains units
u_{1},\dots,u_{n}
with
u_{1}u_{2}\cdots u_{n}=1
.

(
b
)

One can order the real embeddings
\sigma_{1},\dots,\sigma_{n}:K\to\mathbb{R}
such that
\sigma_{i}(u_{j})>0
for all
1\leq i,j\leq n
, and

\displaystyle\log\sigma_{i}(u_{j})=\begin{cases}-2n(n-1)\log p+O(1),&\text{if }i=j,\\
2n\log p+O(1),&\text{if }i\neq j.\end{cases}

(3.3)

(
c
)

The units
u_{i}
lie in the ring
\mathbb{Z}+p\mathcal{O}_{K}
.

Proof.

Since
p\equiv 1\mod 2n
, the polynomial
x^{n}+1
has a
n
different solutions mod
p
. By Hensel’s Lemma (See
[
7
]
), it has
n
solutions mod
p^{n}
, call them
a_{0}^{\prime},\dots,a_{n-1}^{\prime}\in\{1,\dots,p^{n}-1\}
.
Let
a_{i}=a_{i}^{\prime}+2ip^{n}
for
i=0,\dots,n-1
, and note that
p^{n}+1\leq a_{i+1}-a_{i}\leq 3p^{n}
for all
0\leq i\leq n-2
.
consider now the polynomial
R(x)=\frac{(px-a_{1})(px-a_{2})\cdots(px-a_{n})-1}{p^{n}}
. It has integer coefficients since by assumption,
\sum_{i_{1}<\dots<i_{k}}a_{i_{1}}\cdots a_{i_{k}}=0\mod{p^{n}}
for all
k=1,\dots,n-1
and
a_{1}a_{2}\cdots a_{n}=(-1)^{n}\mod{p^{n}}
.

To control the real embeddings of the resulting number field, we will approximate the real roots of
R
.

By Taylor’s theorem (see
[
14
, §20.3]
) applied at
a_{i_{0}}/p
we have

R(x)=R(a_{i_{0}}/p)+R^{\prime}(a_{i_{0}}/p)(x-a_{i_{0}}/p)+\frac{1}{2}R^{\prime\prime}(x^{\prime})(x-a_{i_{0}}/p)^{2},

for some
x^{\prime}\in[a_{i_{0}}/p,x]
(this interval notation does not assume
a_{i_{0}}/p\leq x
).
Note that

R(a_{i_{0}}/p)=-\frac{1}{p^{n}},\qquad R^{\prime}(a_{i_{0}}/p)=\prod_{j\neq i_{0}}(a_{i_{0}}/p-a_{j}/p)=(-1)^{n-i_{0}}\alpha_{i_{0}}p^{n^{2}-2n+1},

for some
\alpha_{i_{0}}\geq 1
, with
\alpha_{i_{0}}=\Theta(1)
,
and for all
x^{\prime}\in[-p^{n-1},2np^{n-1}]
we have

|R^{\prime\prime}(x^{\prime})|=\left|\sum_{1\leq k<l\leq n}\prod_{j\neq k,l}(x-a_{j}/p)\right|=O(p^{n^{2}-3n+2}).

That implies that for all
x\in[-p^{n-1},2np^{n-1}]
we have

\displaystyle R(x)=-\frac{1}{p^{n}}+(-1)^{n-i_{0}}\alpha_{i_{0}}p^{n^{2}-2n+1}(x-a_{i_{0}}/p)+O(p^{n^{2}-3n+2})(x-a_{i_{0}}/p)^{2},

(3.4)

Choose
x_{i_{0}}^{\prime}
with
x_{i_{0}}^{\prime}-a_{i_{0}}/p=2\frac{(-1)^{n-i_{0}}}{p^{n^{2}-n+1}\alpha_{i_{0}}}
.
Note that
|x_{i_{0}}^{\prime}-a_{i_{0}}/p|<1
, and hence
x_{i_{0}}^{\prime}\in[-p^{n-1},2np^{n-1}]
,
Then

R(x_{i_{0}}^{\prime})=-\frac{1}{p^{n}}+(-1)^{n-i_{0}}\alpha_{i_{0}}p^{n^{2}-2n+1}(x_{i_{0}}^{\prime}-a_{i_{0}}/p)+O(p^{-n^{2}-n})=\frac{1}{p^{n}}+O(p^{-n^{2}-n}).

If
p
is sufficiently large as a function of
n
then
R(x_{i_{0}}^{\prime})>0
.
Since
R(a_{i_{0}})=-\frac{1}{p^{n}}<0
, the mean value theorem implies that there is
x_{i_{0}}\in[a_{i_{0}}/p,x_{i_{0}}^{\prime}]
with
R(x_{i_{0}})=0
.
By Eq. (
3.4
) we deduce that:

\displaystyle 0=R(x_{i_{0}})=-\frac{1}{p^{n}}+(-1)^{n-i_{0}}\alpha_{i_{0}}p^{n^{2}-2n+1}(x_{i_{0}}-a_{i_{0}}/p)+O(p^{n^{2}-3n+2})(x_{i_{0}}-a_{i_{0}}/p)^{2}.

(3.5)

This implies:

\displaystyle\frac{1}{p^{n}}

\displaystyle=(x_{i_{0}}-a_{i_{0}}/p)\left((-1)^{n-i_{0}}\alpha_{i_{0}}p^{n^{2}-2n+1}+O(p^{n^{2}-3n+2})(x_{i_{0}}-a_{i_{0}}/p)\right)

\displaystyle=(x_{i_{0}}-a_{i_{0}}/p)(-1)^{n-i_{0}}\alpha_{i_{0}}p^{n^{2}-2n+1}\left(1+O\left(p^{-n^{2}}\right)\right),

and hence,

x_{i_{0}}=a_{i_{0}}/p+\frac{(-1)^{n-i_{0}}}{p^{n^{2}-n+1}\alpha_{i_{0}}}+O\left(\frac{1}{p^{2n^{2}-n+1}}\right).

Since
|a_{i_{0}}/p-x_{i_{0}}|<1
we deduce that all

are different, and hence these are the distinct roots of
R
.
Since
|a_{i}-a_{j}|\geq p^{n}

|a_{i}/p-x_{j}|\geq p^{n-1}-1>1
.
It follows that

is irreducible, as if

, then in the ring

The number field

where

has units

. Let
u_{i}=(u_{i}^{\prime})^{2}
.
The real embeddings of

are
\sigma_{i}:\alpha\mapsto x_{i}
. They satisfy

.
Now Eq. (
3.3
) follows from the properties of

.

3.3
Application of Hecke operator to compact orbits

In this section, we introduce the following lemma, which describes how to verify that a Hecke operator splits a compact orbit into sufficiently many compact diagonal orbits.

Lemma 3.8
.

Let
K
be a totally real number field of degree
n
, let
x_{\mathcal{O}_{K}}\in X_{n}
the point with compact orbit corresponding to the
\mathbb{Z}
-module
\mathcal{O}_{K}
and let
p,k_{1}\leq\dots\leq k_{n},a=a_{p;k_{1},k_{2},\dots,k_{n}}\in\operatorname{SL}_{n}(\mathbb{R})

3.4

U\subseteq\mathcal{O}_{K}^{\times,>0}
be a subgroup and

the corresponding diagonal subgroup as in Definition

U\subseteq\mathbb{Z}+p^{k}\mathcal{O}_{K}

0\leq k_{1}\leq k_{n}\leq k
then
A_{U}\subseteq\operatorname{stab}_{A}(x^{\prime})
for every
x^{\prime}\in T_{a}(x_{\mathcal{O}_{K}})
.

Proof.

Let
u\in U
. Since
u\in\mathbb{Z}+p^{k}\mathcal{O}_{K}
, there is
m\in\mathbb{Z}
such that
u\equiv m\mod{p^{k}}
. Thus for every
\bar{b}\in\mathcal{O}_{K}/(p^{k})
we have

, that is, the multiplication by

\mathcal{O}_{K}/(p^{k})
is in fact a multiplication by a scalar.
Hence, the element

preserves
x_{\mathcal{O}_{K}}
,
p^{k}x_{\mathcal{O}_{K}}
and acts on the quotient by multiplication by the scalar
m
. This implies that
a_{u}
acts on the set of mid-groups
\{\Lambda:p^{k}x_{\mathcal{O}_{K}}\subseteq\Lambda\subseteq x_{\mathcal{O}_{K}}\}
trivially. Indeed, there is an isomorphism

\{\Lambda:p^{k}x_{\mathcal{O}_{K}}\subseteq\Lambda\subseteq x_{\mathcal{O}_{K}}\}\cong\{\bar{\Lambda}\subseteq x_{\mathcal{O}_{K}}/p^{k}x_{\mathcal{O}_{K}}\},

and
u
acts on the right-hand side as a multiplication by a scalar, which preserves all groups.
This implies that
a_{u}
preserves all Hecke neighbors, by the second construction (See Definition
3.4
).
∎

Obtaining a lower bound on the volume:

We sketch a way to perturb the above proof to obtain a lower bound on the volume of the orbits which is polynomial in the discriminant. This lower bound will come from showing that many of the

The

’s correspond to orbits of action of
\mathcal{O}_{K}^{\times}
on
\mathbb{P}((\mathbb{Z}/pq\mathbb{Z})^{n})
. To see that the orbits can be made large, one can use the number field

,
which implies that typically one of the
B_{i}
’s is very large. However, we can sketch a rigorous argument ensuring that most of the
B_{i}
’s are large. For that, let
r
be the smallest prime such that
r=1\mod 2n
and choose the
a_{i}
’s in Definition
5.7
such that

.
This choice of
a_{i}
ensures that the corresponding number field

is totally ramified at

. This can be used to show that the units in
\mathcal{O}_{K}^{\times}
generate a group of size
\Theta(r^{nm})
inside

for all

. Now we replace the Hecke operators

in the proof by

’s and therefore also the bound on the volumes.

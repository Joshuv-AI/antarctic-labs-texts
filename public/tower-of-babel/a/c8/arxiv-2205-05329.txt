1.
Introduction

In
[
16
]
, Schmidt made the following definition:

Definition 1.1
.

Let
{\mathbf{k}}
be a field and
Q\in{\mathbf{k}}[x_{1},\ldots,x_{s}]
a form, i.e. homogeneous polynomial. The Schmidt rank
\textnormal{rk}_{\mathbf{k}}(Q)
is the smallest natural number
r
such that
Q=\sum_{i=1}^{r}R_{i}S_{i}
with
R_{i},S_{i}\in{\mathbf{k}}[x_{1},\ldots,x_{s}]
forms of positive degree. If
Q
is any degree
d
polynomial, we define
\textnormal{rk}_{\mathbf{k}}(Q)
as the rank of its degree
d
part.

Note that for
l\in{\mathbf{k}}[x_{1},\ldots,x_{s}]
linear and non-constant,
\textnormal{rk}_{\mathbf{k}}(l)=\infty.

Schmidt introduced this quantity when
{\mathbf{k}}=\mathbb{Q}
and
Q
is homogeneous and proved that if
\textnormal{rk}_{\mathbb{Q}}(Q)
is sufficiently large then a certain local-global rule applies for counting the number of integer points in the set
\{x\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{s},|x|\leq P:Q(x)=0\}.
He also showed that
\textnormal{rk}_{\mathbb{C}}(Q)
is essentially equivalent to
\textnormal{codim}_{{\mathbb{C}}^{s}}(\nabla Q(x)=0),
known as the Birch rank of
Q
(we will define Birch rank more generally in the next section). Note that it follows immediately from the definition that

\textnormal{rk}_{\mathbb{C}}(Q)\leq\textnormal{rk}_{\mathbb{Q}}(Q).
The object of this paper is to prove a polynomial bound in the opposite direction, for
{\mathbf{k}}=\mathbb{Q}
and several other fields. For perfect fields
{\mathbf{k}},
inequalities bounding
\textnormal{rk}_{{\mathbf{k}}}(Q)
in terms of
\textnormal{rk}_{\bar{{\mathbf{k}}}}(Q)
are known for
d=2,3

[
5
]
and for
d=4
there is an ineffective bound when
\textnormal{char}({\mathbf{k}})\neq 2

[
10
]
. The bound we prove is quite interesting in its own right, but also has a number of immediate applications to Diophantine equations, since many results which use the Hardy-Littlewood circle method assume the Birch rank is large. Our result allows us to relax this assumption to large Schmidt rank over the base field.

We describe some direct applications of our result in the next section - counting prime solutions to Diophantine equations, counting integer solutions to polynomial equations over number fields, and an analogue for function fields.

Definition 1.2
.

We call the following fields
admissible
: number fields, finite fields and finite separable extensions of
\mathbb{F}_{q}(t).

We prove the following:

Theorem 1.3

(Schmidt rank and field extensions)
.

Let
{\mathbf{k}}
be an admissible field,
\bar{{\mathbf{k}}}
its algebraic closure, and

\textnormal{rk}_{\mathbf{k}}(Q)\leq A[\textnormal{rk}_{\bar{{\mathbf{k}}}}(Q)+1]^{B}.

We will soon give explicit values for the constants

Our result generalizes to collections of forms of the same degree, with an appropriate definition of rank.

Let

Their collective Schmidt rank is defined to be

2.
Applications

In
[
2
]
, Birch defined a geometric notion of rank which is closely related to the singular locus of the variety defined by a collection of polynomials. In many applications of the Hardy-Littlewood circle method, it’s necessary to assume that the Birch rank of the polynomials we are interested in is large.

Definition 2.1
.

Let
Q\in{\mathbf{k}}[x_{1},\ldots,x_{s}]
be a form of degree
d.
The Birch rank of
Q
is
\textnormal{rk}_{B}(Q)=\textnormal{codim}_{\mathbb{A}^{s}}(\nabla Q(x)=0).
Given a collection
Q_{1},\ldots,Q_{n}\in{\mathbf{k}}[x_{1},\ldots,x_{s}]
of forms of degree
d,
we define
S(Q_{1},\ldots,Q_{n})
to be the variety where the matrix of partial derivatives
(\partial Q_{i}/\partial x_{j})
has rank
<n.
The Birch rank of the collection is
\textnormal{rk}_{B}(Q_{1},\ldots,Q_{n})=\textnormal{codim}_{\mathbb{A}^{s}}S(Q_{1},\ldots,Q_{n}).
For a general collection of polynomials
Q_{1},\ldots,Q_{n}\in{\mathbf{k}}[x_{1},\ldots,x_{s}]
of degree
d,
we define their Birch rank in terms of the homogeneous degree
d
parts. As a matter of convention, set
\textnormal{rk}_{B}(\emptyset)=s.

It’s not difficult to show that there is an inequality

\textnormal{rk}_{B}(Q_{1},\ldots,Q_{n})\leq 2\textnormal{rk}_{\mathbf{k}}(Q_{1},\ldots,Q_{n}).

Let us discuss for a moment what happens with quadratics. For a single quadratic
Q
, there is an elementary inequality in the opposite direction

\textnormal{rk}_{\mathbf{k}}(Q)\leq\textnormal{rk}_{B}(Q).

If
{\mathbf{k}}
is algebraically closed, we can take the union of the Birch singular loci of non-trivial linear combinations
c_{1}Q_{1}+\ldots+c_{n}Q_{n},
which correspond to points of
\mathbb{P}^{n-1},
and apply the above inequality to deduce:

\textnormal{rk}_{\mathbf{k}}(Q_{1},\ldots,Q_{n})\leq\textnormal{rk}_{B}(Q_{1},\ldots,Q_{n})+n-1.

As a consequence, when
\textnormal{rk}_{\mathbf{k}}(Q_{1},\ldots,Q_{n})\geq 2n-1
we deduce that
X=\{Q_{1}=\ldots=Q_{n}=0\}
is a complete intersection of codimension
n.
For suppose
Y\subset X
has codimension
<n.
Since
\textnormal{rk}_{B}(Q_{1},\ldots,Q_{n})\geq n,
there exists a point
y\in Y
such that the Jacobian matrix has rank
n
at
y.
But then the tangent space at
y
has codimension
n,
contradicting the fact that the local codimension at
y
is
<n.

Kazhdan, Polishchuk and the first author
[
9
]
extended results of Schmidt to show that a similar inequality holds also for higher degrees. Cohen and Moshkovitz
[
3
]
proved a similar result for a single multi-linear polynomial.

Theorem 2.2

(Theorem 1.4 in
[
9
]
)
.

Assume that
d\geq 2,

{\mathbf{k}}
is algebraically closed, and
\textnormal{char}({\mathbf{k}})
doesn’t divide
d.
Then we have

\textnormal{rk}_{\mathbf{k}}(Q_{1},\ldots,Q_{n})\leq(d-1)\left[\textnormal{rk}_{B}(Q_{1},\ldots,Q_{n})+n-1\right].

In particular, if
\textnormal{rk}_{\mathbf{k}}(Q_{1},\ldots,Q_{n})\geq(d-1)(2n-1)
then

(Q_{1}=\ldots=Q_{n}=0)

is a complete intersection of codimension
n.

Corollary 2.3
.

Let
{\mathbf{k}}
be an admissible field, and
Q_{1},\ldots,Q_{n}\in{\mathbf{k}}[x_{1},\ldots,x_{s}]
forms of degree
d\geq 2,
such that
\textnormal{char}({\mathbf{k}})>d
or
\textnormal{char}({\mathbf{k}})=0.
Then there exist constants
A^{\prime}=A^{\prime}({\mathbf{k}},d),B^{\prime}=B^{\prime}({\mathbf{k}},d)
such that if
\textnormal{rk}_{\mathbf{k}}(Q_{1},\ldots,Q_{n})\geq A^{\prime}(nr)^{B^{\prime}}
then
\textnormal{rk}_{B}(Q_{1},\ldots,Q_{n})\geq r.
In particular, if
r\geq n
then
(Q_{1}=\ldots=Q_{n}=0)
is a complete intersection of codimension
n.

Proof.

If
A^{\prime},B^{\prime}
are taken sufficiently large then by theorem
1.5
we obtain

\textnormal{rk}_{\bar{{\mathbf{k}}}}(Q_{1},\ldots,Q_{n})\geq(d-1)(r+n-1).

Plugging this into theorem
2.2
proves the corollary.
∎

As we mentioned above, many applications of the Hardy-Littlewood circle method require that the Birch rank of the collection of forms be large. The above corollary which follows immediately from theorem
1.5
allows us to replace this condition by requiring the Schmidt rank to be large. We now give some examples of this.

2.1.
Prime solutions

Yamagishi
[
19
]
, extending a result of Cook and Magyar
[
4
]
, proved a local to global rule holds for counting prime solutions to systems of integral polynomial equations with sufficiently large Birch rank. Cook and Magyar conjectured that it’s sufficient to assume that the Schmidt rank over
\mathbb{Q}
is large. The conjecture was proved for polynomials of degree
\leq 3
in
[
18
]
. As a consequence of our results, we can prove their conjecture for polynomials of any degree. Let
D\geq 1
, and let
\bar{Q}=(\bar{Q}_{1},\ldots,\bar{Q}_{D})
be a system of polynomials in
\mathbb{Z}[x_{1},\ldots,x_{s}]
, where

\bar{Q}_{d}=(Q_{d,1},\ldots,Q_{d,t_{d}})
is the subsystem of degree
d
polynomials of
\bar{Q}

(1\leq d\leq D)
. Let
X=\{\bar{Q}=0\}
be the affine variety defined by
\bar{Q}.
Following Cook and Magyar, we use a different notion of rank for systems of linear polynomials. If
l_{1},\ldots,l_{n}
are linear polynomials with degree one homogeneous parts
L_{1},\ldots,L_{n}
we define
r(l_{1},\ldots,l_{n})
to be the minimum number of non-zero coefficients
in a non-trivial linear combination

\lambda_{1}L_{1}+\ldots+\lambda_{n}L_{n},

where
\boldsymbol{\lambda}=(\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{n})\in\mathbb{Q}^{n}\backslash\{\mathbf{0}\}.
Let
\Lambda
be the von Mangoldt function,
Given
x=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{s})
, we let

\Lambda(x)=\Lambda(x_{1})\ldots\Lambda(x_{s}).

Set

\mathcal{M}_{\bar{Q}}(N):=\sum_{x\in[0,N]^{s}}\Lambda(x)\ 1_{X}(x).

As an immediate consequence of combining corollary
2.3
with Yamagishi’s result we obtain:

Theorem 2.4
.

There exists a constant
\chi=\chi(D,t_{1},\ldots,t_{D})
such that if
r(\bar{Q}_{1})\geq\chi
and
\textnormal{rk}_{\mathbb{Q}}(\bar{Q}_{d})\geq\chi
for each
2\leq d\leq D
with
t_{d}>0

then there exist constants
\mathcal{C}=\mathcal{C}(\bar{Q})
, and

c>0
such that

\mathcal{M}_{\bar{Q}}(N)=\mathcal{C}(\bar{Q})\ N^{s-\sum_{d=1}^{D}dt_{d}}+O\left(\frac{N^{s-\sum_{d=1}^{D}dt_{d}}}{(\log N)^{c}}\right).

Furthermore, if the system of equations
\bar{Q}=0
has a non-singular solution in
\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{\times}
, the units of
p
-adic integers, for each prime
p
, and the system of homgeneous equations given by the leading forms has a non-singular real point in
(0,1)^{s}
, then

\mathcal{C}(\bar{Q})>0.

2.2.
Number fields

Frei and Madritsch
[
7
]

[
17

K
be a number field of degree
n
over
\mathbb{Q}
. Let
\bar{Q}
be a
system of polynomials in
s
variables over the ring of integers
\mathcal{O}

of
K
with maximal degree
D.
We assume the polynomials
to be ordered by their degrees, that is, for each
d\in\{1,\ldots,D\}
, we are given polynomials

\bar{Q}_{d}=(Q_{d,1},\ldots,Q_{d,t_{d}})\in\mathcal{O}_{K}[x_{1},\ldots,x_{s}]

of degree
d
, where
t_{d}\geq 0
with
t_{D}\geq 1
. Fix an integral ideal
\eta
of
\mathcal{O}
and a
{\mathbb{Z}}
-basis

\omega_{1},\ldots,\omega_{n}
of
\eta
. We will also consider
\omega_{1},\ldots,\omega_{n}
as an

-basis of

x=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{s})\in V^{s}
, where each
x_{i}
has the form

x_{i,1}\omega_{1}+\cdots+x_{i,n}\omega_{n}
, such that the

(x_{i,j})_{i,j}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{ns}
lie in a given box

with sides aligned to the coordinate axes of

{\mathbb{R}}^{ns}
. Given such a box
\mathcal{B}
, we define the counting function

N(P):=\left|\{x\in\eta^{s}\cap P\mathcal{B}:Q_{d,i}(x)=0\textnormal{ for all }1\leq d\leq D,1\leq i\leq t_{d}\}\right|.

Let

\Delta:=\{1\leq d\leq D:t_{d}\geq 1\}.

Let
\mathcal{D}_{0}:=0
and, for

,

\mathcal{D}_{d}:=t_{1}+2t_{2}+\cdots+dt_{d},

so that
\mathcal{D}:=\mathcal{D}_{D}

. Set

\displaystyle u_{d}

\displaystyle:=\sum_{k=d}^{D}2^{k-1}(k-1)t_{k}\quad\textnormal{ for }\quad 1\leq d\leq D+1,

\displaystyle s_{0}(d)

\displaystyle:=\mathcal{D}_{d}(2^{d-1}+u_{d+1})+u_{d+1}+\sum_{j=d+1}^{D}u_{j}t_{j}

By combining corollary

with the result of Frei and Madritsch we obtain:

Theorem 2.5

There exist constants
A(d),B(d)
such that if for all

\textnormal{rk}_{K}(\bar{Q}_{d})>A(t_{d}s_{0})^{B}
then there is a positive

\delta

3.
Reduction to multilinear polynomials and universality

In this section we reduce theorem
1.5
to a statement about multilinear forms. Let
V
be a finite dimensional vector space over
{\mathbf{k}}
and
Q\in{\mathbf{k}}[V]
a form of degree
d.
Suppose either
\textnormal{char}({\mathbf{k}})=0
or
\textnormal{char}({\mathbf{k}})>d.
Then there is an associated multilinear form
\tilde{Q}:V^{d}\to{\mathbf{k}}
given by
\tilde{Q}({\mathbf{x}}_{1},\ldots,{\mathbf{x}}_{d})=\triangle_{{\mathbf{x}}_{1}}\ldots\triangle_{{\mathbf{x}}_{d}}Q
which satisfies
Q(x)=\frac{1}{d!}\tilde{Q}(x,\ldots,x).
For multilinear forms such as this there is a finer notion of rank.

Definition 3.1
.

Let
{\mathbf{k}}
be any field,
V_{1},\ldots,V_{d}
finite dimensional vector spaces over
k,
and
P:V_{1}\times\ldots\times V_{d}\to{\mathbf{k}}
a multilinear form. We say that
P
has
partition rank

1
if there exists a partition
[d]=I\sqcup J
with
I,J\neq\emptyset
and multilinear forms
R:\prod_{i\in I}V_{i}\to k,S:\prod_{j\in J}V_{j}\to k
such that

P({\mathbf{x}})=R(({\mathbf{x}}_{i})_{i\in I})\cdot S(({\mathbf{x}}_{j})_{j\in J}).

In general the partition rank
\textnormal{prk}_{\mathbf{k}}(P)
is the smallest natural number
r
such that
P=P_{1}+\ldots+P_{r}
and
P_{i}
has partition rank
1
for all
1\leq i\leq r.

The next claim states that the partition rank of
\tilde{Q}
is essentially equivalent to the rank of
Q.

Claim 3.2
.

Let
V
be a finite dimensional vector space over
{\mathbf{k}}
and
Q\in{\mathbf{k}}[V]
a form of degree
d.
Suppose either
\textnormal{char}({\mathbf{k}})=0
or
\textnormal{char}({\mathbf{k}})>d.
then

\textnormal{rk}_{\mathbf{k}}(Q)\leq\textnormal{prk}_{\mathbf{k}}(\tilde{Q})\leq\binom{d}{\lfloor d/2\rfloor}\textnormal{rk}_{\mathbf{k}}(Q).

Proof.

For the left inequality, suppose
\tilde{Q}({\mathbf{x}})=\sum_{i=1}^{r}R({\mathbf{x}})\cdot S({\mathbf{x}})
and plug in

Q(x)=\frac{1}{d!}\tilde{Q}(x,\ldots,x)=\frac{1}{d!}\sum_{i=1}^{r}R_{i}(x,\ldots,x)\cdot S_{i}(x,\ldots,x).

For the right inequality, suppose
Q(x)=\sum_{i=1}^{r}R_{i}(x)S_{i}(x)
and take derivatives

\tilde{Q}({\mathbf{x}}_{1},\ldots,{\mathbf{x}}_{d})=\sum_{i=1}^{r}\sum_{J\subset[d],|J|=\deg R_{i}}\tilde{R_{i}}(({\mathbf{x}}_{j})_{j\in J})\tilde{S_{i}}(({\mathbf{x}}_{\mathbf{k}})_{k\in[d]\setminus J}).

∎

Partition rank is also defined for collections of multi-linear forms.

Definition 3.3
.

Let
P_{1},\ldots,P_{n}:V_{1}\times\ldots\times V_{d}\to{\mathbf{k}}
be multilinear forms. Their partition rank is

\textnormal{prk}_{\mathbf{k}}(P_{1},\ldots,P_{n})=\min_{0\neq a\in{\mathbf{k}}^{n}}\textnormal{prk}_{\mathbf{k}}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_{i}\cdot P_{i}\right).

By claim
3.2
, we can deduce theorem
1.5
from a corresponding theorem for multi-linear forms.

Theorem 3.4

(theorem
1.5
for multi-linear forms)
.

Let
{\mathbf{k}}
be an admissible field,
V_{1},\ldots,V_{d}
finite dimensional vector spaces over
{\mathbf{k}},
and
P_{1},\ldots,P_{n}:V_{1}\times\ldots\times V_{d}\to{\mathbf{k}}
multi-linear forms. There exist constants
\tilde{A}({\mathbf{k}},d),\tilde{B}({\mathbf{k}},d)
such that if
{\mathbf{k}}
is a perfect field we have

\textnormal{prk}_{\mathbf{k}}(P_{1},\ldots,P_{n})\leq\tilde{A}[n\cdot\textnormal{prk}_{\bar{{\mathbf{k}}}}(P_{1},\ldots,P_{n})+1]^{\tilde{B}},

and if
{\mathbf{k}}
is an imperfect field we have

\textnormal{prk}_{\mathbf{k}}(P_{1},\ldots,P_{n})\leq\tilde{A}[n^{1+1/d}\cdot(\textnormal{prk}_{\bar{{\mathbf{k}}}}(P_{1},\ldots,P_{n})+1)]^{\tilde{B}}.

Proof of theorem
1.5
assuming theorem
3.4
.

We will show that theorem
1.5
holds with

and

Let
{\mathbf{k}}
be an admissible field and let
Q_{1},\ldots,Q_{n}
be degree
d

\textnormal{char}({\mathbf{k}})>d
or
\textnormal{char}({\mathbf{k}})=0.
Let
\tilde{Q_{1}},\ldots,\tilde{Q_{n}}
be the associated multi-linear forms. If
{\mathbf{k}}
is perfect then by claim
3.2
and theorem
3.4

\textnormal{rk}_{\mathbf{k}}(Q_{1},\ldots,Q_{n})\leq\textnormal{prk}_{\mathbf{k}}(\tilde{Q_{1}},\ldots,\tilde{Q_{n}})\leq\tilde{A}[n\cdot\textnormal{prk}_{\bar{{\mathbf{k}}}}(\tilde{Q_{1}},\ldots,\tilde{Q_{n}})+1]^{\tilde{B}}\\
\leq\tilde{A}\left[n\cdot\binom{d}{\lfloor d/2\rfloor}\textnormal{rk}_{\bar{{\mathbf{k}}}}(Q_{1},\ldots,Q_{n})+1\right]^{\tilde{B}}\leq\tilde{A}\binom{d}{\lfloor d/2\rfloor}^{\tilde{B}}\left[n\cdot\textnormal{rk}_{\bar{{\mathbf{k}}}}(Q_{1},\ldots,Q_{n})+1\right]^{\tilde{B}}.

If
{\mathbf{k}}
is imperfect then again by claim
3.2
and theorem
3.4

\textnormal{rk}_{\mathbf{k}}(Q_{1},\ldots,Q_{n})\leq\textnormal{prk}_{\mathbf{k}}(\tilde{Q_{1}},\ldots,\tilde{Q_{n}})\leq\tilde{A}[n^{1+1/d}\cdot(\textnormal{prk}_{\bar{{\mathbf{k}}}}(\tilde{Q_{1}},\ldots,\tilde{Q_{n}})+1)]^{\tilde{B}}\\
\leq\tilde{A}[n^{1+1/d}\cdot(\binom{d}{\lfloor d/2\rfloor}\textnormal{rk}_{\bar{{\mathbf{k}}}}(Q_{1},\ldots,Q_{n})+1)]^{\tilde{B}}\leq\tilde{A}\binom{d}{\lfloor d/2\rfloor}^{\tilde{B}}\left[n^{1+1/d}\cdot(\textnormal{rk}_{\bar{{\mathbf{k}}}}(Q_{1},\ldots,Q_{n})+1)\right]^{\tilde{B}}.

∎

We prove theorem
3.4
for all of the above fields (except
\mathbb{Q}
) via a universality result, which may be of independent interest. This generalizes a universality result of Kazhdan and Ziegler appearing in
[
12
]
.

Theorem 3.5

(Universality)
.

Let
{\mathbf{k}}
be an admissible field and
R_{1},\ldots,R_{n}:({\mathbf{k}}^{t})^{d}\to{\mathbf{k}}
multilinear forms of degree
d.
There exist constants
C({\mathbf{k}},d),D({\mathbf{k}},d)
such that whenever
P_{1},\ldots,P_{n}:V_{1}\times\ldots\times V_{d}\to{\mathbf{k}}
are multilinear forms with
\textnormal{prk}_{\mathbf{k}}(P_{1},\ldots,P_{n})>C(nt^{d})^{D},
there exist linear maps
T_{i}:{\mathbf{k}}^{t}\to V_{i}
such that
P_{j}\circ(T_{1}\times\ldots\times T_{d})=R_{j}.

We now explain how theorem
3.4
follows from theorem
3.5
. Theorem
3.5
will be proved in the following sections.

Proof of theorem
3.4
assuming theorem
3.5
.

We will show that theorem
3.4
holds with
\tilde{A}=C,\tilde{B}=dD.
Supposing

\textnormal{prk}_{\mathbf{k}}(P_{1},\ldots,P_{n})>C(n^{1+1/d}\bar{r})^{dD},

we will show that
\textnormal{prk}_{\bar{{\mathbf{k}}}}(P_{1},\ldots,P_{n})\geq\bar{r}.
By theorem
3.5
with
t=n\bar{r},
there exist linear maps
T_{i}:{\mathbf{k}}^{t}\to V_{i}
such that

P_{j}\circ T=\sum_{k=(j-1)\bar{r}+1}^{j\bar{r}}\prod_{i=1}^{d}{\mathbf{x}}_{i}(k)=:D_{j}.

Clearly
\textnormal{prk}_{\bar{\mathbf{k}}}(P_{1},\ldots,P_{n})\geq\textnormal{prk}_{\bar{\mathbf{k}}}(D_{1},\ldots,D_{n}).

To show that the right hand side equals
\bar{r},
we need the following simple claim:

Claim 3.6
.

Let
{\mathbf{k}}
be a field and
P:V_{1}\times\ldots\times V_{d}\to{\mathbf{k}}
a multilinear form. The variety

Z_{P}:=\{(v_{2},\ldots,v_{d}):P(\cdot,v_{2},\ldots,v_{d})\equiv 0\}

satisfies

\textnormal{codim}_{V_{2}\times\ldots\times V_{d}}Z_{P}\leq\textnormal{prk}_{\mathbf{k}}(P).

Proof.

Suppose
P=\sum_{k=1}^{r}R_{k}(({\mathbf{x}}_{i})_{i\in I_{k}})\cdot S_{k}(({\mathbf{x}}_{j})_{j\in[d]\setminus I_{k}})
and assume without loss of generality that
1\notin I_{k}
for all
k.
Then the variety
\{R_{1}=\ldots=R_{r}=0\}
has codimension
\leq r
and is contained in
Z_{P}.

∎

Lemma 3.7
.

\textnormal{prk}_{\bar{\mathbf{k}}}(D_{1},\ldots,D_{n})=\bar{r}.

Proof.

Obviously the partition rank is
\leq\bar{r}.
On the other hand, let
D=\sum_{j=1}^{n}a_{j}D_{j}
be a non-trivial linear combination, and assume without loss of generality that
a_{1},\ldots,a_{m}\neq 0
and
a_{m+1}=\ldots=a_{n}=0
for some
m\geq 1.
We can decompose
Z_{D}
explicitly,

Z_{D}=\{\prod_{i=2}^{d}{\mathbf{x}}_{i}(k)=0:\forall k\in[m\bar{r}]\}=\bigcup_{\sigma:[m\bar{r}]\to[2,d]}\{{\mathbf{x}}_{\sigma(j)}(j)=0:\forall j\in[m\bar{r}]\}.

Z_{D}
is a finite union of linear subspaces of codimension
m\bar{r},
so
\textnormal{codim}_{V_{2}\times\ldots\times V_{d}}Z_{D}=m\bar{r}\geq\bar{r}.
By the previous claim, we get
\textnormal{prk}_{\bar{\mathbf{k}}}(D_{1},\ldots,D_{n})\geq\bar{r}.

∎

This completes the proof of theorem
3.4
for all fields in the case
n=1
and for imperfect fields in the case of general
n.
To obtain our improved bounds for perfect fields and general
n
we use the following argument which we learned from A. Polishchuk.

Proof of theorem
3.4
for perfect fields assuming the case
n=1
.

Let

\bar{r}=\textnormal{prk}_{\bar{{\mathbf{k}}}}(P_{1},\ldots,P_{n}),\mathcal{P}=\textnormal{sp}_{\bar{{\mathbf{k}}}}(P_{1},\ldots,P_{n})

Choose
0\neq P\in\mathcal{P}
with
\textnormal{rk}_{\bar{{\mathbf{k}}}}(P)=\bar{r}.
Let
\mathcal{P}^{\prime}\subset\mathcal{P}
be the
{\bar{{\mathbf{k}}}}
-subspace spanned by
P
and its Galois conjugates. By Galois descent,
\mathcal{P}^{\prime}=\textnormal{sp}_{\bar{{\mathbf{k}}}}(P^{\prime}_{1},\ldots,P^{\prime}_{m})
for some polynomials
P^{\prime}_{1},\ldots,P^{\prime}_{m}\in\textnormal{sp}_{\mathbf{k}}(P_{1},\ldots,P_{n}).
Let
\mathcal{P^{\prime}}({\mathbf{k}}):=\textnormal{sp}_{\mathbf{k}}(P^{\prime}_{1},\ldots,P^{\prime}_{m})
and choose
0\neq P^{\prime}\in\mathcal{P^{\prime}}({\mathbf{k}}).
Since
\dim\mathcal{P^{\prime}}\leq n,
there are Galois conjugates
\sigma_{1}\cdot P,\ldots,\sigma_{n}\cdot P
such that
P^{\prime}\in\textnormal{sp}_{\bar{{\mathbf{k}}}}(\sigma_{1}\cdot P,\ldots,\sigma_{n}\cdot P).
By our assumption on
P
this means
\textnormal{rk}_{\bar{{\mathbf{k}}}}(P^{\prime})\leq n\bar{r},
which by theorem
3.4
implies

\textnormal{rk}_{\mathbf{k}}(P^{\prime})\leq\tilde{A}(n\bar{r}+1)^{\tilde{B}}.

∎

∎

We now begin the proof of theorem
3.5
, which will be completed in subsequent sections. Assume without loss of generality that
V_{i}={\mathbf{k}}^{s_{i}}.
Then linear maps
T_{i}:{\mathbf{k}}^{t}\to V_{i}
are of the form
T_{i}(x)=A_{i}\cdot x
where
A_{i}\in\mathcal{M}_{s_{i}\times t}({\mathbf{k}}).
For
j_{1},\ldots,j_{d},l
the coefficient of
y^{1}_{j_{1}}\cdot\ldots\cdot y^{d}_{j_{d}}
in
P_{l}\circ(T_{1}\times\ldots\times T_{d})
is given by a multilinear form

P^{l}_{j_{1},\ldots,j_{d}}:\mathcal{M}_{s_{1}\times t}({\mathbf{k}})\times\ldots\times\mathcal{M}_{s_{d}\times t}({\mathbf{k}})\to{\mathbf{k}},

namely
P^{l}_{j_{1},\ldots,j_{d}}(A_{1},\ldots,A_{d})=\left(P_{l}\circ(A_{1}\times\ldots\times A_{d})\right)(e_{j_{1}},\ldots,e_{j_{d}}).

Claim 3.8
.

The collection of multi-linear forms thus obtained satisfies

\textnormal{prk}_{\mathbf{k}}\left((P^{l}_{j_{1},\ldots,j_{d}})_{l,j_{1},\ldots,j_{d}}\right)=\textnormal{prk}_{\mathbf{k}}(P_{1},\ldots,P_{n}).

Proof.

For a multilinear form
P=\sum_{k_{1},\ldots,k_{d}}a_{k_{1},\ldots,k_{d}}\prod_{i\in[d]}{\mathbf{x}}_{i}(k_{i})
and indices
j_{1},\ldots,j_{d}

P_{j_{1},\ldots,j_{d}}(A_{1},\ldots,A_{d})=\sum_{k_{1},\ldots,k_{d}}a_{k_{1},\ldots,k_{d}}\prod_{i\in[d]}A_{i}(k_{i},j_{i}).

Therefore,
P_{j_{1},\ldots,j_{d}}(A_{1},\ldots,A_{d})
is just
P
with relabeled variables, meaning they have the same rank. So for any
c_{1},\ldots,c_{n}
and fixed
j_{1},\ldots,j_{d}
we have

\textnormal{prk}_{\mathbf{k}}(\sum_{l}c_{l}P^{l}_{j_{1},\ldots,j_{d}})=\textnormal{prk}_{\mathbf{k}}(\sum_{l}c_{l}P_{l}).

Now let
P^{\prime}=\sum_{l,j_{1},\ldots,j_{d}}c^{l}_{j_{1},\ldots,j_{d}}P^{l}_{j_{1},\ldots,j_{d}}
be a non-trivial linear combination with, say,
c^{l}_{1,\ldots,1}\neq 0
for some
l.
Restricting to the subspace

U=\{A_{i}(k,j)=0:\forall i,k\ \forall j\neq 1\}

gives
P^{\prime}\restriction_{U}=\sum_{l}c^{l}_{1,\ldots,1}P^{l}_{1,\ldots,1}
so

\textnormal{prk}_{\mathbf{k}}(P^{\prime})\geq\textnormal{prk}_{\mathbf{k}}(\sum_{l}c^{l}_{1,\ldots,1}P^{l}_{1,\ldots,1})=\textnormal{prk}_{\mathbf{k}}(\sum_{l}c^{l}_{1,\ldots,1}P_{l})\geq\textnormal{prk}_{\mathbf{k}}(P_{1},\ldots,P_{n}).

∎

The desired equalities
P_{l}\circ(A_{1}\times\ldots\times A_{d})=R_{l}
are in fact
nt^{d}
equations of the form

(1)

P^{l}_{j_{1},\ldots,j_{d}}(A_{1},\ldots,A_{d})=r^{l}_{j_{1},\ldots,j_{d}}

and we have just proved that the collection of multi-linear forms appearing has partition rank
\textnormal{prk}_{\mathbf{k}}(P_{1},\ldots,P_{n}).
Our goal in the next sections is to show that such a system of equations has a
{\mathbf{k}}
-solution.

4.
Proof of results for finite fields and for
\mathbb{Q}

4.1.
Proof for finite fields:

We start by defining bias.

Definition 4.1
.

Let
V
be a finite dimensional vector space over a finite field
{\mathbf{k}}
and
f:V\to{\mathbf{k}}
some function. The bias of
f
is

\textnormal{bias}(f)=\left|{\mathbb{E}}_{x\in V}\chi(f(x))\right|

where
\chi:k\to{\mathbb{C}}
is some non-trivial character, and
{\mathbb{E}}_{x\in X}
denotes
\frac{1}{|X|}\sum_{x\in X}
for a finite set
X.

The bias of
f
depends also on the choice of character
\chi
but this won’t matter in what follows. The key to proving theorem
3.5
is the following theorem relating rank to bias for multilinear polynomials. The first part is due to Milicevic
[
15
]
, with a similar result proved independently by Janzer
[
8
]
, and the second to Cohen-Moshkovitz
[
3
]
.

Theorem 4.2
.

Let
{\mathbf{k}}
be a finite field and
P:V_{1}\times\ldots\times V_{d}\to{\mathbf{k}}
a multilinear form. Assume either of the following conditions holds for some
r\geq 1
:

(1)

\textnormal{prk}_{\mathbf{k}}(P)\geq\alpha r^{\beta},
for constants
\alpha(d)=2^{d^{2^{O(d^{2})}}},\beta(d)=2^{2^{O(d^{2})}}.

(2)

|{\mathbf{k}}|\geq F(r,d)
and
\textnormal{prk}_{\mathbf{k}}(P)\geq 2^{d-1}r.

Then
\textnormal{bias}(P)\leq|{\mathbf{k}}|^{-r}.

The proof of theorem
3.5
for finite fields, and hence of theorems
3.4
and
1.5
, now follows by a routine Fourier-analytic argument.

Proof.

We will show that theorem
3.5
holds for finite fields with
C=2^{d^{2^{O(d^{2})}}},D=2^{2^{O(d^{2})}}
or with
C=2^{d-1},D=1
under the additional assumption that
|{\mathbf{k}}|\geq F(nt^{d},d).

Recall that we want to show the existence of a
{\mathbf{k}}
-solution to the system of
nt^{d}
multilinear equations (
1
)

P^{l}_{j_{1},\ldots,j_{d}}(A_{1},\ldots,A_{d})=r^{l}_{j_{1},\ldots,j_{d}}.

We showed in claim
3.8
that

\textnormal{prk}_{\mathbf{k}}\left(P^{l}_{j_{1},\ldots,j_{d}}\right)_{l,j_{1},\ldots,j_{d}}=\textnormal{prk}_{\mathbf{k}}(P_{1},\ldots,P_{n}).

Applying theorem
\ref{bias-rank},
we get that any nontrivial linear combination satisfies

\textnormal{bias}\left(\sum_{l,j_{1},\ldots,j_{d}}\alpha^{l}_{j_{1},\ldots,j_{d}}P^{l}_{j_{1},\ldots,j_{d}}\right)\leq|{\mathbf{k}}|^{-nt^{d}}.

We can therefore estimate the number of solutions

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}_{A_{1},\ldots,A_{d}}\prod_{l,j_{1},\ldots,j_{d}}1_{P^{l}_{j_{1},\ldots,j_{d}}(A_{1},\ldots,A_{d})=r^{l}_{j_{1},\ldots,j_{d}}}=

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}_{\alpha^{l}_{j_{1},\ldots,j_{d}}}{\mathbb{E}}_{A_{1},\ldots,A_{d}}\chi(\sum_{l,j_{1},\ldots,j_{d}}\alpha^{l}_{j_{1},\ldots,j_{d}}(P^{l}_{j_{1},\ldots,j_{d}}(A_{1},\ldots,A_{d})-r^{l}_{j_{1},\ldots,j_{d}}))=

\displaystyle|{\mathbf{k}}|^{-nt^{d}}\left(1+\sum_{\alpha^{l}_{j_{1},\ldots,j_{d}}\neq 0}{\mathbb{E}}_{A_{1},\ldots,A_{d}}\chi(\sum_{l,j_{1},\ldots,j_{d}}\alpha^{l}_{j_{1},\ldots,j_{d}}(P^{l}_{j_{1},\ldots,j_{d}}(A_{1},\ldots,A_{d})-r^{l}_{j_{1},\ldots,j_{d}}))\right)\geq

\displaystyle|{\mathbf{k}}|^{-nt^{d}}\left(1-\sum_{\alpha^{l}_{j_{1},\ldots,j_{d}}\neq 0}\textnormal{bias}(\sum_{l,j_{1},\ldots,j_{d}}\alpha^{l}_{j_{1},\ldots,j_{d}}P^{l}_{j_{1},\ldots,j_{d}})\right)>0.

As we saw in the previous section, this proves theorem
3.4
with the constants

(1)

\tilde{A}=C=2^{d^{2^{O(d^{2})}}},\ \tilde{B}=dD=2^{2^{O(d^{2})}}.

(2)

\tilde{A}=C=2^{d-1},\ \tilde{B}=dD=d
if
|{\mathbf{k}}|\geq F(\textnormal{prk}_{\mathbf{k}}(P_{1},\ldots,P_{n}),n,d),

and theorem
1.5
with the constants

(1)

A=\tilde{A}\binom{d}{\lfloor d/2\rfloor}^{\tilde{B}}=2^{2^{O(d^{2})}},\ B=\tilde{B}=2^{2^{O(d^{2})}}.

(2)

A=\tilde{A}\binom{d}{\lfloor d/2\rfloor}^{\tilde{B}}=2^{d-1}\binom{d}{\lfloor d/2\rfloor}^{d},\ B=\tilde{B}=d
if
|{\mathbf{k}}|\geq F(\textnormal{rk}_{\mathbf{k}}(Q_{1},\ldots,Q_{n}),n,d).

∎

4.2.
Proof for
\mathbb{Q}
:

Now we turn to proving theorem
3.4
for the rationals. We prove it in the case
n=1.
The case of general
n
follows by the argument given for perfect fields in the previous section. Let
P:\mathbb{Q}^{s_{1}}\times\ldots\times\mathbb{Q}^{s_{d}}\to\mathbb{Q}
be a multi-linear form, and write
s=s_{1}+\ldots+s_{d}.
Assume without loss of generality that
P
has integer coefficients. We will show that for all but finitely many primes
p,
the reduction
P\mod p
has partition rank which is bounded from below in terms of
\textnormal{prk}_{\mathbb{Q}}(P).

Proposition 4.3
.

Let
P:\mathbb{Q}^{s_{1}}\times\ldots\times\mathbb{Q}^{s_{d}}\to\mathbb{Q}
be a multi-linear form with integer coefficients. Then for all sufficiently large primes
p
we have

\textnormal{prk}_{\mathbb{Q}}(P)\leq 2^{d-1}d\cdot\textnormal{prk}_{\mathbb{F}_{p}}(P\mod p).

We now complete the proof of theorem
3.4
and theorem
1.5
for
\mathbb{Q}
assuming proposition
4.3
.

Proof.

Suppose
\textnormal{prk}_{\mathbb{Q}}(P)\geq 4^{d-1}d\cdot\bar{r}^{d}.
By proposition
4.3
we get that for sufficiently large primes
p,
we have
\textnormal{prk}_{\mathbb{F}_{p}}(P\mod p)\geq 2^{d-1}\cdot\bar{r}^{d}.
By theorem
3.4
for sufficiently large finite fields, we conclude that for
p
sufficiently large we have
\textnormal{prk}_{\bar{\mathbb{F}_{p}}}(P\mod p)\geq\bar{r}.
By a model-theoretic result (corollary 2.2.10 in
[
14
]
), we deduce that
\textnormal{prk}_{\bar{\mathbb{Q}}}(P)\geq\bar{r}.
This completes the proof of theorem
3.4
with

\tilde{A}=4^{d-1}d,\tilde{B}=d.

By the argument given in the last section this proves theorem
1.5
with

A=\tilde{A}\binom{d}{\lfloor d/2\rfloor}^{\tilde{B}}=4^{d-1}d\binom{d}{\lfloor d/2\rfloor}^{d},\ B=\tilde{B}=d.

∎

In order to prove proposition
4.3
, we will need a simple scaling result for solutions to systems of multilinear equations.

Lemma 4.4
.

Let
\bar{Q}=Q_{1},\ldots,Q_{n}:{\mathbb{Z}}^{s_{1}}\times\ldots\times{\mathbb{Z}}^{s_{d}}\to G
be a collection of multilinear maps where
G
is some abelian group. For a positive integer
R
let

\displaystyle N_{R}(\bar{Q})

\displaystyle=|\{x\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{s}:Q_{i}(x)=0\ \forall i\in[n],0\leq x_{j}<R\ \forall j\in[s]\}|

\displaystyle N^{\prime}_{R}(\bar{Q})

\displaystyle=|\{x\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{s}:Q_{i}(x)=0\ \forall i\in[n],-R<x_{j}<R\ \forall j\in[s]\}|.

Then for any positive integer
L
we have

N_{LR}(\bar{Q})\leq L^{s}N^{\prime}_{R}(\bar{Q}),

where
s=s_{1}+\ldots+s_{d}.

Proof.

By induction on
d.
For the base case
d=1,
first note that for any
c_{i}\in G
the number of solutions to
Q_{i}(x)=c_{i}
in
[0,R)^{s}
is bounded above by
N^{\prime}_{R}(\bar{Q}).
This follows by substracting a single solution from every one of the others. Writing
\bar{R}=(R,\ldots,R),
we have

\displaystyle N_{LR}(\bar{Q})

\displaystyle=\sum_{0\leq x_{i}<LR}1_{Q_{i}(x)=0}=\sum_{\omega\in\{0,1,\ldots,L-1\}^{s}}\sum_{0\leq x_{i}<R}1_{Q_{i}(x+\omega\cdot\bar{R})=0}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\omega\in\{0,1,\ldots,L-1\}^{s}}\sum_{0\leq x_{i}<R}1_{Q_{i}(x)=-Q_{i}(\omega\cdot\bar{R})}\leq\sum_{\omega\in\{0,1,\ldots,L-1\}^{s}}N^{\prime}_{R}(\bar{Q})=L^{s}N^{\prime}_{R}(\bar{Q}).

For general
d,
we have

\displaystyle N_{LR}(\bar{Q})

\displaystyle=\sum_{0\leq{\mathbf{x}}_{1}(i)<LR}N_{LR}(\bar{Q}({\mathbf{x}}_{1},\cdot))\leq\sum_{0\leq{\mathbf{x}}_{1}(i)<LR}L^{s_{2}+\ldots+s_{d}}N^{\prime}_{R}(\bar{Q}({\mathbf{x}}_{1},\cdot))

\displaystyle=L^{s_{2}+\ldots+s_{d}}\sum_{\|{\mathbf{x}}_{2}\|_{\infty},\cdots,\|{\mathbf{x}}_{d}\|_{\infty}<R}N_{LR}(\bar{Q}(\cdot,{\mathbf{x}}_{2},\cdots,{\mathbf{x}}_{d}))

\displaystyle\leq L^{s}\sum_{\|{\mathbf{x}}_{2}\|_{\infty},\cdots,\|{\mathbf{x}}_{d}\|_{\infty}<R}N^{\prime}_{R}(\bar{Q}(\cdot,{\mathbf{x}}_{2},\cdots,{\mathbf{x}}_{d}))=L^{s}N^{\prime}_{R}(\bar{Q}),

where the first inequality follows from the inductive hypothesis and the second one follows from the base case
d=1.

∎

Proof of proposition
4.3
.

Let
s=s_{1}+\ldots+s_{d-1}.
Suppose
p
is a prime and
\textnormal{prk}_{\mathbb{F}_{p}}(P\mod p)=r.
By the inequality
\textnormal{bias}(P\mod p)\geq p^{-r},
proved in
[
11
]
, we have

|\{{\mathbf{x}}\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{s}:P({\mathbf{x}}_{1},\ldots,{\mathbf{x}}_{d-1},\cdot)=0\mod p\}|\geq p^{s-r}.

Given
0<\eta<1,
we can apply lemma
4.4
to the collection of multilinear maps

\bar{Q}=Q_{1},\ldots,Q_{s_{d}}:{\mathbb{Z}}^{s_{1}}\times\ldots\times{\mathbb{Z}}^{s_{d-1}}\to\mathbb{F}_{p},

where

Q_{i}({\mathbf{x}})=P({\mathbf{x}}_{1},\ldots,{\mathbf{x}}_{d-1},e_{i})\mod p,

to get

N_{p}(\bar{Q})\leq\lceil p^{1-\eta}\rceil^{s}N^{\prime}_{\lceil p^{\eta}\rceil}(\bar{Q})\ll_{s}p^{s(1-\eta)}N^{\prime}_{\lceil p^{\eta}\rceil}(\bar{Q}),

so

N^{\prime}_{\lceil p^{\eta}\rceil}(\bar{Q})\gg_{s}p^{\eta(s-r/\eta)}\gg_{s,\eta,r}\lceil p^{\eta}\rceil^{s-r/\eta}.

For
\eta=\frac{1}{d}
and
\|{\mathbf{x}}\|_{\infty}<\lceil p^{\eta}\rceil
we have
|P({\mathbf{x}},e_{i})|\ll p^{\frac{d-1}{d}}
so for sufficiently large
p,

P({\mathbf{x}},e_{i})=0\mod p\implies P({\mathbf{x}},e_{i})=0.

Therefore,

|\{{\mathbf{x}}\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{s}:P({\mathbf{x}},\cdot)=0,\|{\mathbf{x}}\|_{\infty}<\lceil p^{1/d}\rceil\}|\gg_{s,r}\lceil p^{1/d}\rceil^{s-dr}.

By corollary
5.7
, which will be proved in the next section, this implies
\textnormal{prk}_{\mathbb{Q}}(P)\leq 2^{d-1}dr.

∎

5.
Proof for number fields and finite separable extensions of
\mathbb{F}_{q}(t)

The obstruction to the solution to a system of equations given by multi-linear forms is the Birch singular locus.

Definition 5.1
.

Let
P:V_{1}\times\ldots\times V_{d}\to{\mathbf{k}}
be a multilinear form of degree
d>1.
The Birch singular locus is the subvariety

V_{1}\times\ldots\times V_{d-1}\supset Z_{P}=\{({\mathbf{x}}_{1},\ldots,{\mathbf{x}}_{d-1}):P({\mathbf{x}}_{1},\ldots,{\mathbf{x}}_{d-1},\cdot)=0\}.

For a collection
\mathcal{P}=(P_{1},\ldots,P_{n})
the Birch singular locus is

Z_{\mathcal{P}}=\{({\mathbf{x}}_{1},\ldots,{\mathbf{x}}_{d-1}):P_{1}({\mathbf{x}}_{1},\ldots,{\mathbf{x}}_{d-1},\cdot),\ldots,P_{n}({\mathbf{x}}_{1},\ldots,{\mathbf{x}}_{d-1},\cdot)\textnormal{ are linearly dependent}\}.

Kazhdan, Polishchuk and the first author
[
9
]
extended a result of Schmidt
[
16
]
and showed a relationship between the Birch singular locus and the Schmidt rank for a multilinear form
P
over a general field
{\mathbf{k}}.

Theorem 5.2
.

Let
g_{\mathbf{k}}(P)
denote the codimension in
V_{1}\times\ldots\times V_{d-1}
of the Zariski closure of
Z_{P}({\mathbf{k}}).
Then
\textnormal{prk}_{\mathbf{k}}(P)\leq 2^{d-1}\cdot g_{\mathbf{k}}(P).

By adapting an argument that Schmidt gave for
\mathbb{Q}
in
[
16
]
, this extends to collections of multilinear forms over some fields.

Proposition 5.3
.

Let
{\mathbf{k}}
be a number field or a finite separable extension of
\mathbb{F}_{q}(t),
and

\mathcal{P}=(P_{1},\ldots,P_{n}):{\mathbf{k}}^{s_{1}}\times\ldots\times{\mathbf{k}}^{s_{d}}\to{\mathbf{k}}

a collection of multilinear forms. Suppose for some
c_{1},\ldots,c_{n}\in{\mathbf{k}}
the system of equations
(P_{i}=c_{i})
has no solution in
{\mathbf{k}}^{s_{1}}\times\ldots\times{\mathbf{k}}^{s_{d}}.
Then

\textnormal{prk}_{\mathbf{k}}(\mathcal{P})\leq 2^{d-1}(d-1)n^{2}.

To prove proposition
5.3
we need an effective version of Noether normalization.

Lemma 5.4

(Noether normalization)
.

Let
{\mathbf{k}}
be an algebraically closed field and let
V\subset{\mathbf{k}}^{s}
be an algebraic set of codimension
t
defined by
m
homogenenous equations of degree
\leq n.
Look at matrices
M\in\mathcal{M}_{(s-t)\times s}({\mathbf{k}}).
There exists a constant
C(s,m,n)
(independent of
V
!) such that for any set
U\subset{\mathbf{k}}
of size
|U|\geq C
we can find a matrix
M
with entries in
U
for which the map
M:V\to{\mathbf{k}}^{s-t}
has finite fibers of size bounded by
C.

Proof of lemma.

Just follow the proof of linear Noether normalization, for example in
[
6
]
. Note that we need to choose the entries of
M
to avoid solutions of certain polynomial equations of degree
\leq n.

∎

This lemma gives us a useful corollary when we have a pseudo-norm at our disposal. It will be convenient to use a slightly non-standard definition.

Definition 5.5
.

Let
\mathbf{A}
be a ring. We call a function
\phi:\mathbf{A}\to{\mathbb{Z}}^{\geq 0}
a
pseudo-norm
if it satisfies the following conditions:

(1)

\phi(x+y)\leq\phi(x)+\phi(y).

(2)

\phi(xy)\ll\phi(x)\phi(y)
where the implied constant is independent of
x,y.

Given
(\mathbf{A},\phi)
as above and
R>0
we write
B_{R}(\mathbf{A}):=\{a\in\mathbf{A}:\phi(a)\leq R\}
for the ball of radius
R.
We say
(\mathbf{A},\phi)
has
linear growth
if for all
0<R<\infty
the set
B_{R}(\mathbf{A})
is finite and satisfies

|B_{CR}(\mathbf{A})|\ll_{C}|B_{R}(\mathbf{A})|
for any constant
C.

Lemma 5.6

(Point counting and dimension)
.

Let
\mathbf{A}
be an infinite integral domain equipped with a pseudo-norm
\phi
such that
(\mathbf{A},\phi)
has linear growth. Let
{\mathbf{k}}
be the fraction field of
\mathbf{A}
and
V\subset\bar{{\mathbf{k}}}^{s}
an algebraic set defined over
\mathbf{A}
by
m
homogeneous equations of degree
n.
For
R>0
set

N_{R}=|V\cap(B_{R}(\mathbf{A}))^{s}|.

Given
D>0,
there exists
R_{0}(s,m,n,D)
(but independent of
V
!) such that if for some
R>R_{0}
we have
N_{R}\geq D|B_{R}(\mathbf{A})|^{s-t}
then
\textnormal{codim}(V)\leq t.

Proof of lemma
5.6
.

Suppose, to get a contradiction, that
\textnormal{codim}(V)=t^{\prime}>t.
By infinitude of
\mathbf{A},
we can apply lemma
5.4
to get a constant
C=C(s,m,n)
and a matrix
M\in\mathcal{M}_{(s-t^{\prime})\times s}(\mathbf{A})
with entries in
B_{C}(\mathbf{A})
such that
M:V\to\bar{{\mathbf{k}}}^{s-t^{\prime}}
has fibers of size
\leq C.
This allows us to bound
N_{R}

N_{R}\leq C\cdot|M((B_{R}(\mathbf{A}))^{s})|\leq C\cdot|B_{C^{\prime}R}(\mathbf{A})|^{s-t^{\prime}}\ll_{s,m,n}|B_{R}(\mathbf{A})|^{s-t-1},

where
C^{\prime}=C^{\prime}(s,m,n).
The second and third inequalities use the fact that
\phi
is a pseudo-norm and that
(\mathbf{A},\phi)
has linear growth, respectively. If
N_{R}\geq D|B_{R}(\mathbf{A})|^{s-t}
then we combine with the above inequality to get

D\leq D^{\prime}|B_{R}(\mathbf{A})|^{-1}

where
D^{\prime}=D^{\prime}(s,m,n)>0.
By infinitude of
\mathbf{A},
this is impossible for
R\geq R_{0}(s,m,n,D)
, so we must have
\textnormal{codim}(V)\leq t.

∎

Corollary 5.7
.

Let
\mathbf{A}
be an infinite integral domain equipped with a pseudo-norm
\phi
such that
(\mathbf{A},\phi)
has linear growth. Let
{\mathbf{k}}
be the fraction field of
\mathbf{A},

P:{\mathbf{k}}^{s_{1}}\times\ldots\times{\mathbf{k}}^{s_{d}}\to{\mathbf{k}}
a multilinear form and
D
a positive constant. Write
s=s_{1}+\ldots+s_{d-1}.
Then there exists
R_{0}(s,d,D)
independent of
P
such that if we have

|Z_{P}({\mathbf{k}})\cap(B_{R}(\mathbf{A}))^{s}|\geq D|B_{R}(A)|^{s-t}

for some
R\geq R_{0}
then
\textnormal{prk}_{\mathbf{k}}(P)\leq 2^{d-1}\cdot t.

Proof.

Combine lemma
5.6
and theorem
5.2
.
∎

Corollary
5.7
is the result that we will actually use in our proof of proposition
5.3
, the other lemmas were preliminary.

Proof of proposition
5.3
.

Let
s=s_{1}+\ldots+s_{d-1}.
First we claim that our hypothesis implies
Z_{\mathcal{P}}({\mathbf{k}}^{s})={\mathbf{k}}^{s}.
Indeed, suppose this isn’t the case. Then there is some
{\mathbf{x}}\in{\mathbf{k}}^{s}\setminus Z_{\mathcal{P}}({\mathbf{k}}^{s}),
meaning
P_{i}({\mathbf{x}},\cdot)
are linearly independent and
P_{i}({\mathbf{x}},\cdot)=c_{i}
has a solution in
{\mathbf{k}}^{s_{d}}
. The proof of the proposition will be similar for both types of fields.
Number fields:
Let
\mathbf{A}
be the ring of integers of
{\mathbf{k}}.
Recalling that
\mathbf{A}\cong{\mathbb{Z}}^{m}
as
{\mathbb{Z}}
-modules, we define a pseudo-norm on
\mathbf{A}
with linear growth by

\phi(n_{1},\ldots,n_{m})=\max_{i}|n_{i}|.

Assume without loss of generality that the collection
\mathcal{P}
has coefficients in
\mathbf{A}.
Now, suppose
{\mathbf{x}}\in Z_{\mathcal{P}}({\mathbf{k}})\cap B_{R}(\mathbf{A})^{s}.
Then the matrix
M=(m_{i,j}),
where
m_{i,j}=P_{j}({\mathbf{x}}_{1},\ldots,{\mathbf{x}}_{d-1},e_{i}),
is of rank
\leq n-1
and has
\phi(m_{i,j})\ll R^{d-1}
for all
i,j.
By a standard linear algebra argument, we obtain a small vector in the kernel of
M.

Claim 5.8
.

Suppose
(\mathbf{A},\phi)
is an integral domain equipped with a pseudo-norm. Suppose
M\in M_{m\times n}(\mathbf{A})
has
\textnormal{rk}(M)\leq n-1
and
\phi(m_{i,j})\leq T
for all
i,j.
Then there exists
0\neq a\in\mathbf{A}^{n}
such that
Ma=0
and
\phi(a_{i})\ll_{n}T^{n-1}
for all
i.

Proof.

Suppose without loss of generality that the first
j\leq n-1
rows of
M
are a
{\mathbf{k}}
-basis for
\textnormal{row}(M).
We obtain a new matrix by deleting the other rows and then adding
n-j
rows of standard basis vectors to obtain an invertible
n\times n
matrix which we call
M^{\prime}.
Let
D=\det M^{\prime}.
Then Cramer’s rule gives a solution
0\neq a\in\mathbf{A}^{n}
to the equation
M^{\prime}a=(0,\ldots,0,D)
which satisfies
\phi(a_{i})\ll_{n}T^{n-1}
for all
i.

∎

Therefore, there is a nontrivial solution
a\in\mathbf{A}^{n}
to the equation
Ma=0
which satisfies
\phi(a_{i})\ll R^{(d-1)(n-1)}
for all
i.
The number of possibilities for such an
a
is
\ll R^{mn(d-1)(n-1)}.
Hence there must be some nontrivial linear combination
P=a_{1}P_{1}+\ldots+a_{n}P_{n}
with

Z_{P}({\mathbf{k}})\cap(B_{R}(\mathbf{A}))^{s}\gg R^{m[s-(d-1)n^{2}]}.

If
R
is sufficiently large, we can apply corollary
5.7
to obtain

\textnormal{prk}_{{\mathbf{k}}}(P)\leq 2^{d-1}(d-1)n^{2}.

Finite separable extensions of

\mathbb{F}_{q}(t)
: Let
\mathbf{A}
be the integral closure of
\mathbb{F}_{q}[t]
in
{\mathbf{k}}.
Then
\mathbf{A}\cong\mathbb{F}_{q}[t]^{m}
as a
\mathbb{F}_{q}[t]
-module, with the same proof as for rings of integers in number fields. Define a pseudo-norm on
\mathbb{F}_{q}[t]
with linear growth by

\phi(f)=q^{\deg(f)}
and extend it to
\mathbf{A}
by
\phi(f_{1},\ldots,f_{m})=\max_{i}\phi(f_{i}).
Assume without loss of generality that
\mathcal{P}
has coefficients in
\mathbf{A}.
From here the proof proceeds in a similar manner: If
x\in Z_{\mathcal{P}}({\mathbf{k}})\cap B_{q^{R}}(\mathbf{A})^{s}
then the matrix
M=(m_{i,j}),
where
m_{i,j}=P_{j}({\mathbf{x}}_{1},\ldots,{\mathbf{x}}_{d-1},e_{i}),
is of rank
\leq n-1
and has
\phi(m_{i,j})\ll q^{R(d-1)}
for all
i,j.
By claim
5.8
there is a nontrivial solution
a\in\mathbf{A}^{n}
to the equation
Ma=0
which satisfies
\phi(a_{i})\ll q^{R(d-1)(n-1)}
for all
i.
The number of possibilities for such an
a
is
\ll q^{mnR(d-1)(n-1)}.
Hence there must be some nontrivial linear combination
P=a_{1}P_{1}+\ldots+a_{n}P_{n}
with

Z_{P}({\mathbf{k}})\cap(B_{q^{R}}(\mathbf{A}))^{s}\gg q^{mR[s-(d-1)n^{2}]}.

If
R
is sufficiently large, we can apply corollary
5.7
to obtain

\textnormal{prk}_{{\mathbf{k}}}(P)\leq 2^{d-1}(d-1)n^{2}.

∎

Now we can finish the proof of theorem
3.5
, and hence of theorems
3.4
and
1.5
.

Proof.

We will show that theorem
3.5
holds for number fields and finite separable extensions of
\mathbb{F}_{q}(t)
with constants
C=2^{d-1}(d-1),D=2.
Suppose
P_{1},\ldots,P_{n}
satisfy
\textnormal{prk}_{\mathbf{k}}(P_{1},\ldots,P_{n})>2^{d-1}(d-1)(nt^{d})^{2}.
Recall that we proved that this implies
\textnormal{prk}_{\mathbf{k}}(P^{l}_{j_{1},\ldots,j_{d}})>2^{d-1}(d-1)(nt^{d})^{2}.
By proposition
5.3
the system of equations

P^{l}_{j_{1},\ldots,j_{d}}(A_{1},\ldots,A_{d})=r^{l}_{j_{1},\ldots,j_{d}}

from the end of section
3
has a
{\mathbf{k}}
-solution as desired. Recall that for the constants
\tilde{A},\tilde{B}
of theorem
3.4
we can take

\tilde{A}=C=2^{d-1}(d-1),\ \tilde{B}=dD=2d,

and for the constants

of theorem

A=\tilde{A}\binom{d}{\lfloor d/2\rfloor}^{\tilde{B}}=2^{d-1}(d-1)\binom{d}{\lfloor d/2\rfloor}^{2d},\ B=\tilde{B}=2d.

∎

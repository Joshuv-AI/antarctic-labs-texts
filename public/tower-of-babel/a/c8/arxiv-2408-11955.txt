κ \kappa -General-Relativity I: a Non-Commutative GR Theory with the κ \kappa -Minkowski Spacetime as its Flat Limit
Daniel Rozental, Ofek Birnholtz

Abstract
We employ a twist deformation of infinitesimal diffeomorphisms to construct a modification of General Relativity on a non-commutative spacetime extending the local κ \kappa -Minkowski geometry. This spacetime arises in Deformed Special Relativity (DSR) models, where a fundamental length scale is incorporated into Special Relativity as an effective description of quantum gravitational effects. To avoid the mathematical and physical inconsistencies associated with twisting the Poincaré group, we instead deform the dilatation-enlarged IGL(3,1) group, constructing a covariant and explicitly consistent gravitational theory (distinct from Weyl gravity). The relativistic consistency of the twisted κ \kappa -Minkowski spacetime is demonstrated, including deformed transformations and differential structures. A physically motivated Inönü–Wigner (IW) contraction procedure is suggested to enable a well-defined classical limit, addressing the correspondence issue. This framework provides a consistent foundation for a dynamical sector of DSR and allows, in future treatment, explicit computations that could advance phenomenological predictions. Keywords: Deformed Special Relativity, General Relativity, Quantum Gravity, Non-Commutative Spacetime, κ \kappa -Poincaré Group, Twist-deformations.

I.2
Overview

Our aim in the current paper is to construct a
physically consistent
theory of deformed GR, which we call
\kappa
-GR, such that it both has the
\kappa
-Minkowski spacetime as its flat limit, and enables physical predictions that can be tested.
To achieve a predictable

We then construct a twist for this enlarged group such that its covariant spacetime corresponds to

To asses what it means to pursue a

The first key conceptual subtlety lies already when considering the

This concern is sharpened by observing that while two observers agree on the spatial commutation sector

is position-dependent, so observers at different spatial locations experience different

This subtlety is well understood when considering the symmetries to be
\kappa
-Poincare (see Sec.

[

]

Specifically, for all generators
\xi\in igl(3,1)
the deformed transformations, denoted for now as

[\hat{\xi}(\hat{x}^{\mu}),\hat{x}^{\nu}]\in\mathcal{M}_{\kappa},

(7)

where

is defined by (

preserve the

This confirms that although the deformation vector always aligns with the time direction, it transforms covariantly with the observer, enabling to interpret (

-Poincaré symmetries discussed in the previous section.

In addition to showing that the
\kappa
-Minkowski algebra is closed under the deformed igl(3,1) transformations, one must ensure that the framework admits a well-defined differential geometry and a self-consistent deformed Leibniz rule.
These properties are verified explicitly in Sec.

The next ambiguity to be addressed is that of the
Correspondence Principle:
The classical limit of the deformed symmetry must recover standard GR with local Poincaré symmetry. In the paper we address this by proposing a contraction scheme of IGL(3,1) to ISO(3,1) using a dimensionless parameter
\epsilon_{RL}=\frac{\omega_{\text{local}}}{\omega_{p}}
to quantify the scale of the NC effects on a system with given frequency
\omega_{\text{local}}
. A possible additional scaling factor of the form
\left(\frac{x}{l_{\text{sys}}}\right)^{n}
may arise from Relative Locality (RL) considerations, but is left to future work
[

]
. The resulting contracted algebra IGL’(3,1) ensures that in the classical limit
\epsilon_{RL}\to 0
, no residual dilatation symmetry remains, and the theory flows to standard GR.
This contraction is reflected in the twist through the rescaled generator
D^{\prime}=\frac{1}{\alpha}D=\frac{1}{\omega_{p}}
.

The final consistency regards the
deformed gravitational theory:
The theory must admit a well-defined deformation of GR, including a consistent notion of deformed diffeomorphisms, variation principles, and conservation laws. We thus show how deformed general coordinate transformations are deformed appropriately, derive the form of the deformed Einstein equation and the Einstein–Hilbert action, and discuss the possibility for coupling matter via a deformed energy–momentum tensor, outlining some of the persisting ambiguities (especially in the definition of local conserved quantities) and approaches for resolutions and future work.

Importantly, our construction respects twisted general covariance but is
not
invariant under local scale transformations.
Although the dilatation generator
D
appears in the twist, it does not correspond to a dynamical scale symmetry as in Weyl gravity
[
56
,
57
]

D
generates only kinematic symmetry of the flat sector, and the resulting theory is not Weyl-invariant.

All in all, when we refer to a
consistent theory
in this study, we refer to one of the following:

\mathcal{A:}

Relativistic Consistency
of the deformed symmetry with the NC spacetime.

\mathcal{B:}

The
Correspondence Principle
of the theory, especially regarding its symmetry.

\mathcal{C:}

Well-defined Gravitational
theory; has a meaningful notion of deformed diffeomorphisms, variations, and conserved quantities.

V
Contraction of the Symmetry

Before developing the deformed gravity theory with
\kappa

This discrepancy implies that the classical limit of the constructed deformed GR would have an incorrect local symmetry, violating the correspondence principle, raising concerns about the physicality of the deformed GR.

In this section, in the pursuit of the
second consistency

\mathcal{B}

I.2

The proposal relies on employing the Inönü-Wigner (IW) contraction procedure to address this correspondence issue. In Subsec.
V.1

V.1
Introduction: Inönü-Wigner Contractions

In this section, we provide a brief overview of the Inönü-Wigner (IW) contractions, following closely
[
79
]
(for additional details, see
[
80
]
). In short, the IW contraction is a mathematical and physical mechanism that introduces a parameter into a given Lie algebra, for which there exists a limiting case where a different Lie algebra emerges, not isomorphic to the old one.

To begin, let us consider a Lie algebra
g
of dimension
\mathcal{N}
with a vector field basis
\{X_{a}\}_{1}^{\mathcal{N}}
. This basis satisfies the Lie bracket relations with the algebra’s structure constants:

[X_{a},X_{b}]=C_{ab}^{c}X_{c}.

(129)

Now, introduce a new basis
\{Y_{a}\}_{1}^{\mathcal{N}}
for
g
, defined as a
\epsilon
-parameterized transformation of the original basis:

Y_{a}=U(\epsilon)_{a}^{b}X_{b}.

(130)

The new basis
\{Y_{a}\}_{1}^{\mathcal{N}}
will satisfy a different Lie algebra, denoted
\tilde{g}
, with commutation relations:

[Y_{a},Y_{b}]=C(\epsilon)_{ab}^{c}Y_{c}.

(131)

The transformed structure constants are related to the original ones via:

U(\epsilon)_{a}^{d}U(\epsilon)_{b}^{c}C_{dc}^{e}U^{-1}(\epsilon)_{e}^{f}=C_{ab}^{f}(\epsilon).

(132)

To determine the nature of the transformation matrices
U(\epsilon)
, we ask that (
131
) produce a Lie algebra that is
non-isomorphic
to (
129
). Therefore, we consider transformations that are singular in some limit. Specifically, for
\epsilon=1
, we have
U(1)_{i}^{j}=\delta_{i}^{j}
, which is the identity, and for

Summary of Symmetry Contraction

In this section, we proposed a way to address the correspondence issue arising from the use of the enlarged IGL(3,1) symmetry in the
\kappa
-deformed framework by employing an Inönü–Wigner (IW) contraction scheme. This procedure ensured that, in the classical limit, the symmetry group reduces cleanly to the Poincaré group

, thereby preserving consistency with General Relativity.

The contraction was performed with respect to the vector space decomposition

, where
V_{R}\cong\text{ISO(3,1)}
is a closed and invariant subalgebra, and
V_{N}
contains the affine generators. The contracted symmetry algebra, denoted IGL

(3,1), is characterized by the generators

,

, and the rescaled dilatations

, with
\alpha_{\text{fund}}=\omega_{p}
identified as the fundamental frequency scale.

We introduced a dimensionless parameter
\epsilon_{RL}=\frac{\omega_{\text{local}}}{\omega_{p}}
, inspired by RL, to quantify the deviation from the classical regime. We noted that RL may also imply a scaling factor of the form
\left(\frac{x}{l_{\text{sys}}}\right)^{n}
, whose precise role remains to be established. In the classical limit,
\epsilon_{RL}\to 0
, and based on RL arguments, we assume
\epsilon_{RL}\leq 1
.
While
\epsilon_{RL}
arises from the physical representation of dilatation on wavefunctions (see (
154
)), the actual contraction parameter is
\alpha_{\text{fund}}=\omega_{p}
, which is physically related to
\kappa
via the Planck scale. In the classical limit

, ensuring
\epsilon_{RL}\to 0
, i.e., the symmetry contracts as IGL’(3,1)

At the quantum–Planckian scale (

), the full twisted IGL(3,1) symmetry is active. In contrast, the classical limit justifies using the contracted algebra IGL

(3,1) for
\kappa
-deformed gravity. Accordingly, the twist vector fields (Sec.
II
and
III

Finally, while this treatment ensures consistency at the level of the symmetry structure, further work is required to derive
\epsilon_{RL}
from first principles within the noncommutative theory, and in particular, to determine the form and origin of the possible scaling factor
\left(\frac{x}{l_{\text{sys}}}\right)^{n}

It is crucial to emphasize that although the twist involves the dilatation generator

Thus, although the appearance of

The resulting deformed gravitational theory respects general diffeomorphism invariance in its twisted form, but it is not invariant under local Weyl rescalings. Whether one can combine Weyl gravity with
\kappa

\mathbf{A}
is
defined by

\overset{\star}{\det}[\mathbf{A}]=\frac{\epsilon^{\mu_{1}\ldots\mu_{n}\nu_{1}\ldots\nu_{n}}}{n!}\;\mathbf{A}_{\mu_{1}\nu_{1}}\star\mathbf{A}_{\mu_{2}\nu_{2}}\star\cdots\star\mathbf{A}_{\mu_{n}\nu_{n}},

(184)

with
(\mu,\nu)\in[0,n]
.

Invariance under deformed GCTs.

We must verify that (
183
) is invariant under
deformed general coordinate transformations (GCTs) and that its variation
with respect to
\overset{\star}{g}^{\mu\nu}
reproduces
(
181
).
To do so, we follow the approach of
[
45
]
(noting that our derivation of invariance closely parallels that in
[
45
]
, albeit with a few differences), and rewrite the metric field in terms of vielbeins as:

\displaystyle g_{\mu\nu}:=\frac{1}{2}(e_{\mu}^{A}\star e_{\nu}^{B}+e_{\nu}^{B}\star e_{\mu}^{A})\tilde{\eta}_{AB},

(185)

where we have introduced a set of
vielbeins

\{e_{\alpha}^{\Gamma}\}
, with
\alpha
denoting a spacetime covariant index and
\Gamma
a local symmetry group transformation index. The symbol
\tilde{\eta}_{AB}
represents the corresponding local (flat and constant) metric.
Here, the index
\Gamma
is associated with the transformations governed by those discussed in Sec.
IV.2
, and accordingly, so is the metric
\tilde{\eta}_{AB}
. For the purposes of the current discussion, we focus solely on the covariant spacetime indices
\alpha
, and therefore defer the full specification of
\tilde{\eta}_{AB}
(beyond noting its constancy) and the transformation rules of the
\Gamma
indices to future work.

Using the linearity of (
184
) and the constancy of

\tilde{\eta}_{AB}
, we can factor out the determinant
\det(\tilde{\eta}_{AB})
, and write:

\displaystyle\sqrt{-\overset{\star}{\det}[g]}\star{\gothfamily R}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sqrt{\bigl(\overset{\star}{\det}[e_{\mu}^{A}]\bigr)^{2}\star\overset{\star}{\det}[\tilde{\eta}_{AB}]}\star{\gothfamily R}

(186)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\det[\tilde{\eta}_{AB}]\overset{\star}{\det}[e_{\mu}^{A}]\star{\gothfamily R}:=\tilde{\eta}e_{\star}\star{\gothfamily R},

where we denoted
\tilde{\eta}:=\det(\tilde{\eta}_{AB})
(a constant), and
e_{\star}:=\overset{\star}{\det}(e_{\mu}^{A})
.
Since constant factors do not affect invariance, we can drop
\tilde{\eta}
in the action. Thus, to show the invariance of (
183
), it suffices to analyze the transformation of
e_{\star}\star{\gothfamily R}
.

To begin with, from the definition of
{\gothfamily R}
in (
51
) we know it behaves as a scalar by construction (and its non-reality merely
requires adding the complex conjugate in the action; it will not change invariance properties).
For
e_{\star}
we know that each vielbein separately transforms as a vector as in (
159
); therefore, by the observation above (see discussion after (
159
)) the transformation rules of
\star
-multiplication of quantities of known transformations rules is the same as that the rules the regular multiplication has.
Regarding the transformation of
\overset{\star}{\det}(e_{\mu}^{A})=e_{\star}
, it is known that the same expression with usual products transforms as
\delta_{\xi}(\det(e_{\mu}^{A}))=-\mathcal{L}_{\xi}(\det(e_{\mu}^{A}))-\mathcal{L}_{(\partial_{\mu}\xi^{\mu})}(\det(e_{\mu}^{A}))
. Therefore, we conclude that

\displaystyle\delta_{\xi}^{\star}(e_{\star})=-\mathcal{L}^{\star}_{\xi}\!\left[\frac{\epsilon^{\mu_{1}\!\ldots}}{n!}e_{\mu_{1}}^{A_{1}}\!\star\!e_{\mu_{2}}^{A_{2}}\!\star\!\cdots\right]-\mathcal{L}^{\star}_{\xi}(e_{\star})-\mathcal{L}^{\star}_{\partial_{\mu}\xi^{\mu}}(e_{\star}),

(187)

Once we know how each term in
e_{\star}\star{\gothfamily R}
transforms, and keeping in mind the observation that
its
transformation rule will be same as the one for
e_{\star}{\gothfamily R}
, we conclude that

\displaystyle\delta^{\star}_{\xi}(e_{\star}\star{\gothfamily R})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\mathcal{L}^{\star}_{\xi}(e_{\star}\star{\gothfamily R})-\mathcal{L}^{\star}_{\partial_{\mu}\xi^{\mu}}(e_{\star}\star{\gothfamily R})

(188)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-(\partial_{\mu}\xi^{\mu})(e_{\star}\star{\gothfamily R})-\xi^{\mu}(\partial_{\mu}(e_{\star}\star{\gothfamily R}))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\partial_{\mu}[\xi^{\mu}(e_{\star}\star{\gothfamily R})],

with the action of the derivative and vector field to be taken as a
\star
-Lie derivative. The last term is what we aimed at showing: that
e_{\star}\star{\gothfamily R}
transforms to a total derivative, hence validating that
\delta_{\xi}^{\star}(\overset{\star}{S_{EH}})=0
.

Variation with respect to
\overset{\star}{g}^{\mu\nu}
.

To get the equations of motion, we follow the first formulation of gravity in the context of NC geometry as in
[
45
]
and vary the integrand in (
183
):

\displaystyle\frac{\delta}{\delta\overset{\star}{g}^{\mu\nu}}\bigl(\sqrt{-g_{\star}}\star\overset{\star}{g}^{\rho\sigma}\star{\gothfamily R}_{\rho\sigma}\bigr)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\delta\sqrt{-g_{\star}}}{\delta\overset{\star}{g}^{\mu\nu}}\star{\gothfamily R}

(189)

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\sqrt{-g_{\star}}\star\frac{\delta\overset{\star}{g}^{\rho\sigma}}{\delta\overset{\star}{g}^{\mu\nu}}\star{\gothfamily R}_{\rho\sigma}

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\sqrt{-g_{\star}}\star\overset{\star}{g}^{\rho\sigma}\star\frac{\delta{\gothfamily R}_{\rho\sigma}}{\delta\overset{\star}{g}^{\mu\nu}},

where for brevity
g_{\star}:=\overset{\star}{\det{g}}_{\mu\nu}
.
Note that since twist deformations act on space–time symmetries, not on field
variations, the usual (undeformed) Leibniz rule for

\delta(\cdots)
was applied, see
[
45
]
.
Consequently, we must evaluate the following
\star
expressions

\frac{\delta\overset{\star}{g}^{\rho\sigma}}{\delta g_{\mu\nu}},\quad\frac{\delta\sqrt{-g_{\star}}}{\delta\overset{\star}{g}^{\mu\nu}},\quad\frac{\delta{\gothfamily R}_{\rho\sigma}}{\delta\overset{\star}{g}^{\mu\nu}}.

(190)

First identity.

Following the classical treatment, from

\delta(g_{\mu\nu}\star\overset{\star}{g}^{\nu\sigma})=0
, and applying the classical Leibniz rule for
\delta
we obtain

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\delta(g_{\mu\nu})\star\overset{\star}{g}^{\nu\sigma}+g_{\mu\nu}\star\delta(\overset{\star}{g}^{\nu\sigma})

\displaystyle\Rightarrow\delta(\overset{\star}{g}^{\rho\sigma})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\overset{\star}{g}^{\mu\rho}\!\!\!\star\!\delta(g_{\mu\nu})\!\star\!\overset{\star}{g}^{\nu\sigma}=-\overset{\star}{g}^{\mu\rho}\!\!\!\star\!\overset{\star}{g}^{\nu\sigma}\!\!\!\!\star\!\delta(g_{\mu\nu}).~~~~~

(191)

using cyclicity to move the variation rightward.

Second identity.

Following the classical derivation, we first consider the variation
\delta(g_{\star})
by using (
184
) and absorbing the overall constants

\displaystyle\frac{\delta}{\delta g_{\mu\nu}}\left(\epsilon^{\mu_{1}\mu_{2}...\nu_{1}\nu_{2}...}g_{\mu_{1}\nu_{1}}\!\star\!g_{\mu_{2}\nu_{2}}\!\star\!\ldots\right)=

\displaystyle~~~~~~~~~=\epsilon^{\mu_{1}...\nu_{1}...}\left(\delta(g_{\mu\nu})\star g_{\mu_{2}\nu_{2}}\star\ldots\right.

\displaystyle~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~\left.+g_{\mu_{1}\nu_{1}}\star\delta(g_{\mu\nu})\star g_{\mu_{3}\nu_{3}}\star\ldots+\ldots\right)

\displaystyle~~~~~~~~~=g_{\star}\star\overset{\star}{g}^{\mu\nu}\star\delta(g_{\mu\nu}).

(192)

Where the classical Leibniz rule for
\delta
and cyclicity properties for each of
g_{\mu\nu}
and for moving
\delta(g)
to the right were used. By use of (
191
), we derive

\displaystyle\delta(g_{\mu\nu})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-g_{\mu\rho}\star\delta(\overset{\star}{g}^{\rho\sigma})\star g_{\nu\sigma}=-g_{\mu\rho}\star g_{\nu\sigma}\star\delta(\overset{\star}{g}^{\rho\sigma})

\displaystyle\Rightarrow\delta(g_{\star})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-g_{\star}\star g_{\rho\sigma}\delta(\overset{\star}{g}^{\rho\sigma}).

(193)

By all of these, we can now compute
\delta(\sqrt{-g_{\star}})

\displaystyle\frac{\delta(\sqrt{-g_{\star}})}{\delta\overset{\star}{g}^{\mu\nu}}=\frac{1}{2\sqrt{-g_{\star}}}\frac{\delta g_{\star}}{\delta\overset{\star}{g}^{\mu\nu}}=-\frac{1}{2}\sqrt{-g_{\star}}\star g_{\mu\nu}.

(194)

Third identity (generalized Palatini relations).

finally, we turn to the third term in (
190
), namely the variation
\delta{\gothfamily R}_{\mu\nu}
. In classical GR, the well-known
Palatini identity
provides the variation of the Ricci tensor:

\delta(R_{\mu\nu})=\nabla_{\lambda}(\delta\Gamma_{\mu\nu}^{\lambda})-\nabla_{\nu}(\delta\Gamma_{\mu\lambda}^{\lambda}).

(195)

This identity holds under the standard assumption (typically proven explicitly) that
\delta\Gamma_{\mu\nu}^{\lambda}
transforms as a rank-(1,2) tensor under spacetime diffeomorphisms. The utility of this identity is that, under metric compatibility, the last term in (
189
) can be cast schematically as
\sqrt{-g}\nabla(g^{\mu\nu}\delta\Gamma)
, which, when combined with the other terms in (
189
), becomes a total derivative and thus vanishes under suitable boundary conditions.

We aim to adopt an analogous strategy in our deformed setting by expressing
\delta{\gothfamily R}_{\mu\nu}
in terms of
\star
-covariant derivatives. Beginning from the expanded expression of the deformed Ricci tensor in (
170
) and (
178
), and using the shorthand notation (
174
), we find the variation:

\displaystyle\delta({\gothfamily R}_{\mu\nu})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\partial_{\alpha}\left(\delta S^{*\alpha}_{\beta}(\Gamma^{\beta}_{\mu\nu})\right)-\partial_{\mu}\left(\delta S^{*\alpha}_{\beta}(\Gamma_{\alpha\nu}^{\beta})\right)

(196)

\displaystyle+\left[\delta\Gamma_{\mu\nu}^{\tau}\star\Gamma_{\tau\gamma}^{\gamma}+\Gamma_{\mu\nu}^{\tau}\star\delta\Gamma_{\tau\gamma}^{\gamma}\right]

\displaystyle-\left[\delta\Gamma_{\beta\nu}^{\tau}\star\Gamma_{\mu\tau}^{\beta}+\Gamma_{\beta\nu}^{\tau}\star\delta\Gamma_{\mu\tau}^{\beta}\right]

\displaystyle+i\lambda^{\prime}\left(\delta_{\beta}^{j}\delta_{j}^{\beta}-\delta_{\mu}^{j}\delta_{j}^{\mu}\right)\left[\partial_{0}(\delta\Gamma_{\beta\nu}^{\tau})\Gamma_{\tau\mu}^{\beta}+\partial_{0}(\Gamma_{\beta\nu}^{\tau})\delta\Gamma_{\tau\mu}^{\beta}\right],

Here we used that
\delta
commutes with both derivatives and twist elements. We now define the shorthand notation:

S_{\beta}^{*\alpha}(\Gamma_{\mu\nu}^{\beta}):=\tilde{\Gamma}^{\alpha}_{\mu\nu},\quad S_{\beta}^{*\alpha}(\Gamma_{\alpha\nu}^{\beta}):=\tilde{\Gamma}^{\alpha}_{\alpha\nu},

(197)

with the index placement implied by the contraction with
\delta^{\alpha}_{\beta}
in
S_{\beta}^{*\alpha}
; see also (
70
). Using this, we rewrite (
196
) as:

\displaystyle\delta({\gothfamily R}_{\mu\nu})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\partial_{\alpha}(\delta\tilde{\Gamma}_{\mu\nu}^{\alpha})-\partial_{\mu}(\delta\tilde{\Gamma}_{\nu\alpha}^{\alpha})+\left[\delta\Gamma_{\mu\nu}^{\tau}\star\Gamma_{\tau\gamma}^{\gamma}+\Gamma_{\mu\nu}^{\tau}\star\delta\Gamma_{\tau\gamma}^{\gamma}\right]

(198)

\displaystyle-\left[\delta\Gamma_{\beta\nu}^{\tau}\star\Gamma_{\mu\tau}^{\beta}+\Gamma_{\beta\nu}^{\tau}\star\delta\Gamma_{\mu\tau}^{\beta}\right]

\displaystyle+i\lambda^{\prime}\mathcal{P}^{j}_{[\beta,\mu]}\left[\partial_{0}(\delta\Gamma_{\beta\nu}^{\tau})\Gamma_{\tau\mu}^{\beta}+\partial_{0}(\Gamma_{\beta\nu}^{\tau})\delta\Gamma_{\tau\mu}^{\beta}\right],

using the projector notation
\mathcal{P}^{j}_{[\beta,\mu]}
as defined in (
177
).

To determine whether
\delta({\gothfamily R}_{\mu\nu})
admits a representation in terms of
\overset{\star}{\nabla}(\cdot)
, we recall the general structure of the
\star
-covariant derivative acting on arbitrary tensors (cf. (
167
)):

\displaystyle\overset{\star}{\nabla}_{\beta}(T_{\mu_{1}\mu_{2}\ldots}^{\beta\nu_{1}\nu_{2}\ldots})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\partial_{\beta}(T^{\beta\nu\ldots}_{\mu\ldots})

(199)

\displaystyle+S^{\beta^{\prime}}_{\beta}(T^{\tau\nu_{1}\ldots}_{\mu_{1}\ldots})\star\Gamma^{\beta}_{\tau\beta}-S^{\beta^{\prime}}_{\beta}(T^{\beta\nu_{1}\ldots}_{\tau\mu_{2}\ldots})\star\Gamma^{\tau}_{\mu_{1}\beta^{\prime}}

\displaystyle+S^{\beta^{\prime}}_{\beta}(T^{\beta\tau\nu_{2}\ldots}_{\mu_{1}\mu_{2}\ldots})\star\Gamma^{\nu_{1}}_{\tau\beta^{\prime}}-\ldots

At zeroth order in
\lambda
, all terms in (
196
) reduce to their classical expressions, yielding:

\displaystyle\delta({\gothfamily R}_{\mu\nu}^{(0)})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\partial_{\alpha}(\delta\Gamma_{\mu\nu}^{\alpha})-\partial_{\mu}(\delta\Gamma_{\nu\alpha}^{\alpha})+\delta\Gamma_{\mu\nu}^{\tau}\Gamma_{\tau\gamma}^{\gamma}+\Gamma_{\mu\nu}^{\tau}\delta\Gamma_{\tau\gamma}^{\gamma}

(200)

\displaystyle\quad-\,\delta\Gamma_{\beta\nu}^{\tau}\Gamma_{\mu\tau}^{\beta}-\,\Gamma_{\beta\nu}^{\tau}\delta\Gamma_{\mu\tau}^{\beta},

which coincides with the classical Palatini identity (
195
), where
\delta\Gamma_{\mu\nu}^{\alpha}
is treated as a rank-(1,2) tensor and
\delta\Gamma_{\nu\alpha}^{\alpha}
as a rank-(0,1) tensor.

For the first-order-in-
\lambda
identity, let us isolate the following term, denoted
\mathbb{A}\subset\delta{\gothfamily R}_{\mu\nu}^{(1)}
, from (
198
):

\displaystyle\mathbb{A}

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle\partial_{\alpha}\delta\tilde{\Gamma}_{\mu\nu}^{\alpha}+\delta\Gamma_{\mu\nu}^{\tau}\star\Gamma_{\tau\gamma}^{\gamma}-\delta\Gamma_{\beta\nu}^{\tau}\star\Gamma_{\mu\tau}^{\beta}

(201)

\displaystyle+i\lambda^{\prime}\partial_{0}(\delta\Gamma_{\beta\nu}^{\tau})\Gamma_{\tau\mu}^{\beta}\left(\delta_{\beta}^{j}\delta_{j}^{\beta}-\delta_{\mu}^{j}\delta^{\mu}_{j}\right),

where we consider all the
\star
-products to be evaluated at first order in
\lambda^{\prime}
as prescribed in (
65
), neglecting
\mathcal{O}(1,\lambda^{n\geq 2})
terms.

Our goal is to cast
\delta{\gothfamily R}_{\mu\nu}^{(1)}
in the form of a
\star
-covariant derivative. The structure of the first term in
\mathbb{A}
suggests examining
\overset{\star}{\nabla}_{\alpha}(\delta\tilde{\Gamma}_{\mu\nu}^{\alpha})
. Using (
199
), we write:

\displaystyle\overset{\star}{\nabla}_{\alpha}(\delta\tilde{\Gamma}_{\mu\nu}^{\alpha})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\partial_{\alpha}(\delta\tilde{\Gamma}_{\mu\nu}^{\alpha})+S^{\sigma}_{\alpha}(\delta\tilde{\Gamma}_{\mu\nu}^{\tau})\star\Gamma_{\tau\alpha}^{\alpha}

(202)

\displaystyle-S^{\sigma}_{\alpha}(\delta\tilde{\Gamma}_{\tau\nu}^{\alpha})\star\Gamma_{\mu\sigma}^{\tau}-S^{\sigma}_{\alpha}(\delta\tilde{\Gamma}_{\tau\mu}^{\alpha})\star\Gamma_{\nu\sigma}^{\tau}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\partial_{\alpha}(\delta\tilde{\Gamma}_{\mu\nu}^{\alpha})+S^{\sigma}_{\alpha}(\delta\tilde{\Gamma}_{\mu\nu}^{\tau})\star\Gamma_{\tau\alpha}^{\alpha}

\displaystyle-\delta\Gamma_{\nu\tau}^{\alpha}\star\Gamma_{\mu\alpha}^{\tau}-\delta\Gamma_{\mu\tau}^{\alpha}\star\Gamma_{\nu\alpha}^{\tau},

where we used the contraction identity:

S^{\sigma}_{\alpha}(S^{*\alpha}_{\gamma}\delta\Gamma^{\gamma}_{\tau\nu})=\delta^{\sigma}_{\gamma}\delta\Gamma_{\tau\nu}^{\gamma}=\delta\Gamma_{\tau\nu}^{\sigma}.

(203)

To simplify (
202
) explicitly to
\mathcal{O}(\lambda^{\prime})
, we compute the matrix contribution

\mathbb{S}_{\mu\nu}:=S^{\sigma}_{\alpha}(\delta\tilde{\Gamma}_{\mu\nu}^{\tau})\star\Gamma_{\tau\alpha}^{\alpha}
to first order in
\lambda^{\prime}
, we find:

\displaystyle\mathbb{S}_{\mu\nu}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\delta_{\alpha}^{\sigma}+i\delta_{\alpha}^{j}\delta_{j}^{\sigma}\lambda^{\prime}\partial_{0}\right)(\delta\tilde{\Gamma}_{\mu\nu}^{\tau})\star\Gamma_{\tau\sigma}^{\sigma}

(204)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\delta_{\alpha}^{\sigma}(\delta\tilde{\Gamma}_{\mu\nu}^{\tau})\star\Gamma_{\tau\alpha}^{\alpha}+i\lambda^{\prime}\partial_{0}\delta_{\alpha}^{j}\delta_{j}^{\sigma}(\delta\tilde{\Gamma}_{\mu\nu}^{\tau})\star\Gamma_{\tau\sigma}^{\sigma}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\delta^{\tau}_{\rho}-i\lambda^{\prime}\partial_{0}\delta_{\rho}^{j}\delta_{j}^{\tau}\right)(\delta\Gamma_{\mu\nu}^{\rho})\star\Gamma_{\tau\sigma}^{\sigma}+i\lambda^{\prime}\delta_{j}^{\sigma}\delta^{j}_{\sigma}\partial_{0}(\delta\Gamma_{\mu\nu}^{\tau})\Gamma_{\tau\sigma}^{\sigma}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\delta_{\mu\nu}^{\tau}\star\Gamma_{\tau\sigma}^{\sigma}+i\lambda^{\prime}\partial_{0}(\delta\Gamma_{\mu\nu}^{\tau})\Gamma_{\tau\sigma}^{\sigma}\times\left[\delta_{\tau}^{j}\delta_{j}^{\tau}-\delta_{j}^{\sigma}\delta^{j}_{\sigma}\right].

Substituting into (
202
), we find:

\displaystyle\overset{\star}{\nabla}_{\alpha}(\delta\tilde{\Gamma}_{\mu\nu}^{\alpha})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\partial_{\alpha}(\delta\tilde{\Gamma}_{\mu\nu}^{\alpha})+\delta\Gamma_{\mu\nu}^{\tau}\star\Gamma_{\tau\sigma}^{\sigma}-\delta\Gamma_{\nu\tau}^{\sigma}\star\Gamma_{\mu\sigma}^{\tau}

(205)

\displaystyle-\delta\Gamma_{\mu\tau}^{\sigma}\star\Gamma_{\nu\sigma}^{\tau}+i\lambda^{\prime}\mathcal{P}^{j}_{[\tau,\sigma]}\partial_{0}(\delta\Gamma_{\mu\nu}^{\tau})\Gamma_{\tau\sigma}^{\sigma}.~~~~~~

Therefore, the quantity
\mathbb{A}
can be written as:

\mathbb{A}=\overset{\star}{\nabla}_{\alpha}(\delta\tilde{\Gamma}_{\mu\nu}^{\alpha})+\delta\Gamma_{\mu\tau}^{\alpha}\star\Gamma_{\nu\alpha}^{\tau}.

(206)

We now turn to the remaining terms in (
198
), denoting them as
\mathbb{B}\subset\delta{\gothfamily R}_{\mu\nu}^{(1)}
, such that
\mathbb{A}+\mathbb{B}=\delta{\gothfamily R}_{\mu\nu}^{(1)}
. To first order in
\lambda^{\prime}
:

\displaystyle\mathbb{B}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\partial_{\mu}(\delta\tilde{\Gamma}_{\nu\alpha}^{\alpha})+\Gamma_{\beta\nu}^{\tau}\star\delta\Gamma_{\mu\tau}^{\beta}-\Gamma_{\mu\nu}^{\tau}\star\delta\Gamma_{\tau\gamma}^{\gamma}

(207)

\displaystyle+i\lambda^{\prime}\partial_{0}(\Gamma_{\beta\nu}^{\tau})\delta\Gamma_{\tau\mu}^{\beta}\left(\delta_{\beta}^{j}\delta^{\beta}_{j}-\delta_{\mu}^{j}\delta^{\mu}_{j}\right)

Applying the Leibniz property
\delta(a\star b)=\delta(a)\star b+a\star\delta(b)
, we obtain:

\displaystyle\mathbb{B}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\delta\bigg[\partial_{\mu}(\tilde{\Gamma}_{\nu\alpha}^{\alpha})+\Gamma_{\beta\nu}^{\tau}\star\Gamma_{\mu\tau}^{\beta}-\Gamma_{\mu\nu}^{\tau}\star\Gamma_{\tau\gamma}^{\gamma}

(208)

\displaystyle~~~~+i\lambda^{\prime}\partial_{0}(\Gamma^{\tau}_{\beta\nu})\Gamma_{\tau\mu}^{\beta}\mathcal{P}_{[\beta,\mu]}^{j}\bigg]

\displaystyle-\left[\delta\Gamma_{\beta\nu}^{\tau}\star\Gamma_{\mu\tau}^{\beta}-\delta\Gamma_{\mu\nu}^{\tau}\star\Gamma_{\tau\gamma}^{\gamma}+i\lambda^{\prime}\partial_{0}(\delta\Gamma^{\tau}_{\beta\nu})\Gamma_{\tau\mu}^{\beta}\mathcal{P}_{[\beta,\mu]}^{j}\right].

Using (
205
), we can write:

\displaystyle\mathbb{B}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\partial_{\mu}(\delta\tilde{\Gamma}_{\nu\alpha}^{\alpha})-\delta\left[-\overset{\star}{\nabla}_{\alpha}(\Gamma_{\mu\nu}^{\alpha})+\partial_{\alpha}(\Gamma_{\mu\nu}^{\alpha})-\Gamma_{\mu\tau}^{\alpha}\star\Gamma_{\nu\alpha}^{\tau}\right]

(209)

\displaystyle+\left[-\overset{\star}{\nabla}_{\alpha}(\delta\Gamma_{\mu\nu}^{\alpha})+\partial_{\alpha}(\delta\Gamma_{\mu\nu}^{\alpha})-\delta\Gamma_{\mu\tau}^{\alpha}\star\Gamma_{\nu\alpha}^{\tau}\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\partial_{\mu}(\delta\tilde{\Gamma}_{\nu\alpha}^{\alpha})=-\overset{\star}{\nabla}_{\mu}(\delta\tilde{\Gamma}_{\alpha\nu}^{\alpha})+S_{\alpha}^{\sigma}(\delta\Gamma_{\tau\mu}^{\alpha})\star\Gamma_{\nu\sigma}^{\tau}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\overset{\star}{\nabla}_{\mu}(\delta\tilde{\Gamma}_{\alpha\nu}^{\alpha})+\delta\Gamma_{\mu\tau}^{\alpha}\star\Gamma_{\nu\alpha}^{\tau},

where we again used the contraction identity (
203
).

Finally, combining (
206
) and (
209
), we obtain the first-order deformed Palatini identity:

\displaystyle\delta{\gothfamily R}_{\mu\nu}^{(1)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathbb{A}+\mathbb{B}

(210)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\overset{\star}{\nabla}_{\alpha}(\delta\tilde{\Gamma}_{\mu\nu}^{\alpha})+\delta\Gamma_{\mu\tau}^{\alpha}\!\star\!\Gamma_{\nu\alpha}^{\tau}\!-\!\left(\overset{\star}{\nabla}_{\mu}(\delta\tilde{\Gamma}_{\alpha\nu}^{\alpha})\!+\!\delta\Gamma_{\mu\tau}^{\alpha}\!\star\!\Gamma_{\nu\alpha}^{\tau}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\boxed{\overset{\star}{\nabla}_{\alpha}(\delta\tilde{\Gamma}_{\mu\nu}^{\alpha})-\overset{\star}{\nabla}_{\mu}(\delta\tilde{\Gamma}_{\nu\alpha}^{\alpha})}

This result aligns with our goal: just as in the classical Palatini identity (
195
), we find that the deformed variation
\delta{\gothfamily R}_{\mu\nu}
—up to first order in
\lambda^{\prime}
—can be expressed as a difference of
\star
-covariant derivatives acting on appropriately defined tensors.
At this stage, it remains an open question whether such a compact
\star
-covariant form persists at higher perturbative orders in
\lambda^{\prime}
, and further investigation is required to address this point.

Nevertheless, since our interest lies in evaluating
\overset{\star}{g}\star\delta{\gothfamily R}

within an integral
, we can exploit the cyclicity of the integral under an Abelian twist to commute
\star
-factors. At zeroth order, the integral is trivial. At higher order—specifically, at order
\lambda^{\prime s}
with
s\geq 1
—we write the Ricci tensor (
170
) as:

\displaystyle{\gothfamily R}_{\mu\nu}^{(s)}

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle S^{*\alpha}_{\beta}(\partial_{[\alpha}\Gamma_{\mu]\nu}^{\beta})+\left[\delta_{\beta}^{\alpha}+\left(\sum_{n=1}^{s}\tfrac{(-i\lambda^{\prime})^{n}}{n!}\partial_{0}^{n}\right)\delta_{\beta}^{j}\delta^{\beta}_{j}\right]\times

(211)

\displaystyle\left[\delta_{\mu}^{\gamma}+\left(\sum_{r=1}^{s}\tfrac{(i\lambda^{\prime})^{r}}{r!}\partial_{0}^{r}\right)\delta_{\mu}^{j}\delta^{\gamma}_{j}\right](\Gamma^{\tau}_{\alpha\nu})\Gamma_{\gamma\tau}^{\beta}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle S^{*\alpha}_{\beta}(\partial_{[\alpha}\Gamma_{\mu]\nu}^{\beta})+\delta_{\beta}^{\alpha}\delta_{\mu}^{j}\delta^{\gamma}_{j}\tfrac{(i\lambda^{\prime})^{s}}{s!}\partial_{0}^{s}(\Gamma_{\alpha\nu}^{\tau})\Gamma_{\gamma\tau}^{\beta}

\displaystyle+\delta_{\mu}^{\gamma}\delta_{\beta}^{j}\delta_{j}^{\alpha}\tfrac{(-i\lambda^{\prime})^{s}}{s!}\partial_{0}^{s}(\Gamma^{\tau}_{\alpha\nu})\Gamma^{\beta}_{\gamma\tau}

\displaystyle+\delta_{\mu}^{j}\delta_{j}^{\gamma}\delta_{\beta}^{j}\delta^{\alpha}_{j}(i\lambda^{\prime})^{s}\partial_{0}^{s}\times\sum_{n=0}^{s}\tfrac{(-1)^{n}}{n!(s-n)!}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle S^{*\alpha}_{\beta}(\partial_{[\alpha}\Gamma_{\mu]\nu}^{\beta})+\tfrac{(i\lambda^{\prime})^{s}}{s!}\partial_{0}^{s}(\Gamma^{\tau}_{\beta\nu})\Gamma_{\tau\mu}^{\beta}\mathcal{P}_{[\beta,\mu]}^{j}(s),

where we defined the following
notation
, generalizing (
177
):

\mathcal{P}_{[\beta,\mu]}^{j}(s):=\left[\delta_{\beta}^{j}\delta_{j}^{\beta}+(-1)^{s}\delta_{\mu}^{j}\delta^{\mu}_{j}\right].

(212)

Note that in (
211
) we have discarded all terms of the form
\Gamma\star\Gamma
, which reduce to
\Gamma\Gamma
inside the integral and are thus of zeroth order in
\lambda^{\prime}
. We now express the variation of
{\gothfamily R}_{\mu\nu}
at order
s
under the integral as:

\displaystyle\delta{\gothfamily R}_{\mu\nu}^{(s)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\partial_{\alpha}(\delta\tilde{\Gamma}_{\mu\nu}^{\alpha})-\partial_{\mu}(\delta\tilde{\Gamma}_{\nu\alpha}^{\alpha})

(213)

\displaystyle+\tfrac{(i\lambda^{\prime})^{s}}{s!}\mathcal{P}_{[\beta,\mu]}^{j}(s)\left[\partial_{0}^{s}(\delta\Gamma_{\beta\nu}^{\tau})\Gamma_{\tau\mu}^{\beta}+\partial_{0}^{s}(\Gamma_{\beta\nu}^{\tau})\delta\Gamma_{\tau\mu}^{\beta}\right].

At order
s
, the analog of (
205
) simplifies considerably, as all
\Gamma\star\Gamma
terms again become zeroth-order under integration. This gives a summation structure identical to the one in (
211
), allowing us to write:

\displaystyle\overset{\star}{\nabla}_{\alpha}^{(s)}(\delta\tilde{\Gamma}_{\mu\nu}^{\alpha})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\partial_{\alpha}(\delta\tilde{\Gamma}_{\mu\nu}^{\alpha})

(214)

\displaystyle+\frac{(i\lambda^{\prime})^{s}}{s!}\partial_{0}^{s}(\delta\Gamma_{\mu\nu}^{\tau})\Gamma_{\tau\sigma}^{\sigma}\mathcal{P}_{[\tau,\sigma]}^{j}(s).

Following the same steps as in (
209
), and identifying
\overset{\star}{\nabla}_{\mu}(\delta\tilde{\Gamma}_{\alpha\nu}^{\alpha})=\partial_{\mu}(\delta\tilde{\Gamma}_{\alpha\nu}^{\alpha})
in this context (again because
\Gamma\star\Gamma
-terms are zeroth-order within the integral), we conclude that, analogously to (
210
):

\displaystyle\delta{\gothfamily R}_{\mu\nu}^{(s)}=\overset{\star}{\nabla}_{\alpha}(\delta\tilde{\Gamma}_{\mu\nu}^{\alpha})-\overset{\star}{\nabla}_{\mu}(\delta\tilde{\Gamma}_{\alpha\nu}^{\alpha})+\mathcal{O}(\star),

(215)

where
\mathcal{O}(\star)
denotes terms from (
196
) that either belong to lower orders or vanish under integration due to the cyclicity of the
\star
-product (e.g.,
\Gamma\star\Gamma\rightarrow\Gamma\Gamma
inside the integral).

An important outcome of this analysis is worth highlighting: in classical GR, the Palatini identity is essential for establishing the
local
conservation of the Einstein tensor,
\nabla G_{\mu\nu}=0
, which in turn ensures the local conservation of the energy-momentum tensor. In the NC setting, we have shown that a

The Energy–Momentum Tensor

With the deformed Einstein–Hilbert action at hand, the natural next step is to determine the corresponding deformed energy–momentum tensor (EMT) for a generic matter source. A substantial body of work already addresses this question; we shall discuss them after reviewing the relevant general expression that follows from our present framework.

General derivation.

Consider a generic deformed matter action on the non-commutative (NC) space–time

\displaystyle\overset{\star}{S}_{M}\;=\;\int d^{4}x\,\sqrt{-g_{\star}}\star\overset{\star}{\mathrm{L}}_{M},

Extensive studies of deformed EMTs in scalar

VII
Summary & Future Work

In this work, we have constructed and analyzed a deformation of General Relativity to account for a local Non-Commutative Spacetime (NCST) of the
\kappa
-Minkowski type (
3
). The motivation for introducing such an NCST arises from one of the central approaches in Quantum Gravity Phenomenology, as discussed in Sec.
I
. Studying the physical implications of such a spacetime begins with investigating its
flat sector
properties, its deformed symmetry transformations, the associated invariant quantities, and so on. A key insight in understanding such an NCST lies in a formulation of its relativistic structure, or the frame independence of physical laws. This initially originated doubt on its validity (as the relation (
3
) manifestly breaks Lorentz invariance), but eventually led to its formulation through symmetries of the
\kappa
-Poincaré group and to its physical realization via the notion of relative locality (RL), as outlined in Sec.
I
.

Our construction was based on two main observations: First, there exists a well-motivated physical and phenomenological need to generalize the
flat
NCST (
3
) to incorporate curvature induced by mass distribution; a deformed gravitational theory. Second, there now exists a solid and well-established formalism of
twist deformations
in noncommutative geometry
[
25
,
42
,
58
,
45
]
, which can be used to introduce gravity on a chosen local NCST. This formalism has already been applied to cases involving
constant
NCSTs. Essentially, this is exactly what we have pursued here: we evaluated a gravitational theory on the local NCST (
3
) using the twist formalism, which we refer to as
\kappa
-GR. However, while this may appear to be a straightforward generalization of the constant NCST case, the actual situation turned out to be more subtle.

To employ the twist formalism in constructing geometry on an NCST, the twist must belong to the symmetry group of the underlying commutative space. Yet, as discussed in Secs.
I
and
III
, there exists no twist element within the Poincaré group that can meaningfully deform spacetime into the
\kappa
-Minkowski structure. Consequently, we could not use the twist formalism to incorporate gravity with locally deformed Poincaré symmetries.

To overcome this obstacle, we followed approaches such as
[
51
,
91
]
, which address the issue by enlarging the symmetry group to include dilatations. From this extended group, a twist can be constructed that deforms commutative spacetime into the
\kappa
-Minkowski form. Accordingly, in Sec.
III
, we adopted the classical symmetry group of
local
spacetime to be IGL(3,1) (with the minimal alternative being the
\mathcal{WP}
-group), and we reviewed key properties of the associated twist that yields (
3
).

However, before proceeding with the gravitational construction, we had to reassess the physical viability of this framework. From the perspective of
flat
spacetime, several questions needed to be considered: What is the structure of the deformed symmetry group? Does it possess the same degree of
relativistic consistency
as the
\kappa
-Poincaré group? Can we still regard the NCST (
3
) as physically meaningful? These questions were the focus of Sec.
IV
, where, by following reasoning similar to previous studies on the
\kappa
-Poincaré case, we analyzed the scenario of twisted IGL(3,1) symmetry.

Specifically, we validated that the twisted symmetry, when applied to the NCST (
3
), satisfies several consistency conditions: (1) the differential one-forms are well-defined and compatible with the deformed transformations; (2) the NCST (
3
) remains closed under the action of the deformed transformations, i.e., a coordinate chart acted upon by these transformations remains within the same NCST; and (3) the spacetime (
3
), together with the deformed symmetry transformations, obeys a deformed Leibniz rule. To verify these points, we also derived explicit expressions for the full set of deformed transformations in the twisted IGL(3,1) group and their associated
\star
-coproducts.

The next concern we addressed was regarding the classical limit of the would-be constructed deformed GR with a
local
twisted IGL(3,1) symmetry group. In general, a twist deformation reduces to the associated classical symmetry when the deformation parameter (
\lambda
) vanishes. Therefore, the
form
of the equations is expected to reduce to classical GR. However, using IGL(3,1) as the pre-twisting symmetry implies a residual dilatation enlargement that persists even in the classical limit
\kappa\rightarrow\infty
. This issue was discussed in Sec.
V
, and specifically in Sec.
V.2
, where we proposed the Inönü–Wigner (IW) contraction as a suitable tool to deal with this residual symmetry.

The basic idea comes from analyzing
the vector representation of the dilatation operator and its action on a free wave, which introduces a scaling dependence on the
local
frequency of the wave (
148
). Inspecting the uncertainty relation (
149
), we noted that it naturally suggests a dimensionless small parameter
\epsilon_{RL}=\frac{\omega_{\text{local}}}{\omega_{p}}
, capturing the physical deviation from classicality. A possible additional scaling factor of the form
\left(\frac{x}{l_{\text{sys}}}\right)^{n}
may arise from RL considerations, but its form and origin are left for future work. The property of this parameter is that, based on RL, it approaches zero in the classical limit of low energies and is bounded by unity. This led us to identify the contraction parameter with
\alpha_{\text{fund}}=\omega_{p}=c/l_{p}
, analogous to the role of the speed of light in SR.

Given this, we utilized the IW contraction and performed a contraction of the dilatation enlargement controlled by the scale
\alpha_{\text{fund}}
(see (
152
) and (
154
)). This ensured that when the twist deformation is active, the usual deformation persists, and when it is turned off, the enlargement also vanishes. Technically, this involved rescaling the deformation parameter
in the twist
by
\alpha
(see (
157
)), noting that the relations (
3
) and especially (
149
) remain unaffected: they served as the motivation for introducing
\alpha_{\text{fund}}
in the first place.

Having established a well-defined local deformed symmetry with a relativistic characterization of its flat sector, which smoothly reduces to local Poincaré symmetry in the classical limit
(\kappa,\alpha\to\infty)
, we proceeded to construct a gravitational theory based on this framework. This was the subject of Sec.
VI
, where we evaluated the deformation of GR using the twist formalism on the NCST geometry with the constructed twisted symmetry as the local symmetry.

To derive a
bona fide
theory of gravitation, we first constructed the deformed diffeomorphisms in terms of deformed Lie derivatives. Using standard techniques from NCST gravity, we then evaluated the explicit form of the deformed Einstein equations in vacuum (see (
170
), (
180
)–(
182
)) and discussed associated ambiguities related to reality, and the
\star
-product ordering.

However, our goal was to show that this construction is well-defined at the level of an
action formulation
, which offers manifest symmetry properties. To that end, we introduced an ansatz for the deformed Einstein-Hilbert action (
183
), explicitly verified its global invariance under the deformed diffeomorphisms, and detailed the transformation rules for each term. We then applied the variational principle to this deformed action, generalizing classical identities to the NCST framework. This allowed us to explicitly show that the variation of (
183
) yields the deformed field equations (see (
219
)).

While constructing this variational formalism, we identified a special role for the classical
Palatini identity
, which is responsible for the local and global (covariant) conservation of the Einstein tensor in classical GR. In the NCST context, we verified a global generalization of this identity but stressed the difficulty in defining its local counterpart. This issue is not just computational and is related to the well-known ambiguities in
local conservation laws
in noncommutative geometries. Finally, we derive the general form of the deformed energy-momentum tensor using the developed deformed variational identities and discuss the related ambiguities in symmetrization and conservation.

Collecting all the pieces, we have constructed a deformation of GR with a local
\kappa
-Minkowski NCST. The local symmetry was developed and analyzed with its deformed transformation structure. The theory was built to admit a proper classical limit to GR and equipped with deformed notions of general covariance (diffeomorphisms), a deformed variational principle and action formulation, and the associated treatment of conservation laws. A summary of some of the key results is presented in Table
1
. We hope these developments will advance the understanding of deformed symmetries on
\kappa
-Minkowski spacetime and support the formulation of well-behaved deformations of gravity, leading to the possibility of computing explicit predictions in the future.

Further directions for future research include:

1.
Studying the new invariant quantities that arise from the derived deformed symmetries.

2.
Formalizing the contraction parametrization in a more rigorous manner, with a concrete definition and physical role of the parameter
\epsilon_{RL}
.

3.
Constructing explicit physical predictions, such as gravitational wave generation, through analyzing the deformation of the linearized gravitational theory.

4.
Better understanding the challenges in formulating a local deformed Palatini identity, and the relation to ambiguities in defining local conservation laws in noncommutative spacetime geometries.

On the depth of tensor products of modules
Arash Sadeghi

Abstract
. The depth of tensor product of modules over a Gorenstein local ring is studied. For finitely generated nonzero modules M M and N N over a Gorenstein local ring R R , under some assumptions on the vanishing of finite number of Tate and relative homology modules, the depth R ⁡ ( M ⊗ R N ) \depth_{R}(M\otimes_{R}N) is determined in terms of the depth R ⁡ ( M ) \depth_{R}(M) and depth R ⁡ ( N ) \depth_{R}(N) .



2010 Mathematics Subject Classification
13D07; 13D05

1.
Introduction

For finitely generated modules
M
and
N
over a commutative noetherian local ring
R
, the pair
(M,N)
is said to satisfy the
depth formula
provided

\depth R+\depth_{R}(M\otimes_{R}N)=\depth_{R}(M)+\depth_{R}(N).

The depth formula was first studied by Auslander. In
[
3
]
, he proved that the pair
(M,N)
satisfies the depth formula provided that the projective dimension of
M
is finite and
M
and
N
are Tor-independent, i.e.,
\Tor^{R}_{i}(M,N)=0
for all
i>0
.
Three decades later Huneke and Wiegand proved that the depth formula holds for Tor-independent modules over complete intersection local rings
[
11
]
.
Araya and Yoshino
[
1
]
, and Iyengar
[
14
]
independently generalized Auslander’s result for modules of finite complete intersection dimension.
The finite complete intersection dimension condition was then relaxed by Christensen and Jorgensen
[
10
]
. More precisely, they proved that the pair
(M,N)
satisfies the depth formula provided that the Gorenstein dimension of
M
is finite,
\Tor^{R}_{i}(M,N)=0
for all
i>0
and
\Tt_{i}^{R}(M,N)=0
for all
i\in\mathbb{Z}
.
Recently, Celikbas, Liang and Sadeghi established that the depth formula holds under weaker assumptions
[
7
]
:

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
M
and
N
be
R
–modules such that
M
has finite Gorenstein dimension. Then
the depth formula holds provided the following conditions hold.

(i)

\gt_{i}^{R}(M,N)=0
for all
i>0
.

(ii)

\Tt_{i}^{R}(M,N)=0
for all
i\leq 0
.

In this paper, our aim is to improve Theorem
1.1
over Gorenstein local rings; rings over which each module has finite Gorenstein dimension. Let us remark that the condition (i) in Theorem
1.1
is equivalent to the vanishing of
\gt_{i}^{R}(M,N)
for all
i=1,\ldots,d
, where
d=\dim(R)
. However the condition (ii) cannot be obtained by the vanishing of finitely many Tate homology modules
\Tt_{i}^{R}(M,N)
in general. The main feature of our result is that we require the vanishing of only finitely many Tate homology modules for the depth formula to hold. More precisely, we prove the following result; (see also Corollary
4.3
for a more general case).

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
R
be a Gorenstein local ring of dimension
d
and let
M
and
N
be
R
–modules.
Assume that the following conditions hold:

(i)

\gt_{i}^{R}(M,N)=0
for all
i=1,\ldots,d
.

(ii)

\Tt_{i}^{R}(M,N)=0
for all
i=-d,\ldots,0
.

Then
\depth_{R}(M\otimes_{R}N)+\depth R=\depth_{R}(M)+\depth_{R}(N)
, i.e., the pair
(M,N)
satisfies the depth formula.

The organization of the paper is as follows. In Section 2, we collect preliminary notions,
definitions and some known results which will be used in this paper. In Section 3, we study the depth of tensor products of modules over Cohen-Macaulay local rings.
As a consequence, in Corollary
3.5
, we obtain a bound for the depth of tensor products of modules under some conditions weaker than those in Theorem
1.2
.
In Section 4, we prove our main result.

2.
Notations and preliminary results

Throughout the paper,
R
denotes a commutative noetherian local ring and all

R
–modules are tacitly assumed to be finitely generated.

The notion of the Gorenstein dimension was introduced by Auslander
[
2
]
, and developed by Auslander and Bridger in
[
4
]
.

Definition 2.1
.

An
R
–module
M
is said to be of Gorenstein dimension zero (or totally reflexive) whenever
the canonical map
M\rightarrow M^{**}
is an isomorphism and
\Ext^{i}_{R}(M,R)=0=\Ext^{i}_{R}(M^{*},R)=0
for all
i>0
.

The Gorenstein dimension of
M
, denoted
\gd_{R}(M)
, is defined to be the infimum of all
nonnegative integers
n
such that there exists an exact sequence

0\rightarrow G_{n}\rightarrow\cdots\rightarrow G_{0}\rightarrow M\rightarrow 0,

where each
G_{i}
are totally reflexive.

For a finite presentation
P_{1}\overset{f}{\rightarrow}P_{0}\rightarrow M\rightarrow 0
of an
R
–module
M
, its transpose,
\Tr M
, is
defined as
\coker f^{*}
, where
(-)^{*}:=\Hom_{R}(-,R)
. Therefore we have the
following exact sequence:

(2.1)

0\rightarrow M^{*}\rightarrow P_{0}^{*}\rightarrow P_{1}^{*}\rightarrow\Tr M\rightarrow 0.

Note that
\Tr M
is unique up to projective equivalence.

In the following, we summarize some basic facts about Gorenstein dimension; see, for example,
[
4
]
for more details.

Theorem 2.2
.

For an
R
–module
M
, the following statements hold:

(i)

\gd_{R}(M)=0
if and only if
\gd_{R}(\Tr M)=0
;

(ii)

(Auslander-Bridger formula) If
M
has finite Gorenstein dimension, then

\gd_{R}(M)=\depth R-\depth_{R}(M).

(iii)

R
is Gorenstein if and only if
\gd_{R}(M)<\infty
for all finitely generated
R
–module
M
.

Tate (co)homology for modules of finite Gorenstein dimension was studied by Avramov and Martsinkovsky in
[
6
]
.
A complex
\mathbf{T}
of free
R
–modules is called
totally acyclic
if
\hh_{n}(\mathbf{T})=0=\hh_{n}(\Hom_{R}(\mathbf{T},R))
for all
n\in\mathbb{Z}
.
A
complete resolution
of an
R
-module
M
is a diagram

\mathbf{T}\overset{\vartheta}{\longrightarrow}\mathbf{P}\overset{\pi}{\longrightarrow}M,

where
\pi
is a projective resolution,
T
is a totally
acyclic complex,
\vartheta
is a morphism, and
\vartheta_{i}
is an isomorphism
for
i\gg 0
. An
R
-module has finite Gorenstein dimension if and only
if it has a complete resolution.

Let
M
be an
R
-module with a complete resolution
\mathbf{T}\rightarrow\mathbf{P}\rightarrow M
. For an
R
-module
N
,
Tate homology
of
M
and
N
is defined as

\widehat{\Tor}_{i}^{R}(M,N)=\hh_{i}(\mathbf{T}\otimes_{R}N)\text{ for }i\in\mathbb{Z}.

Also, for an
R
-module
N
,
Tate cohomology
of
M
and
N
is defined as

\widehat{\Ext}^{i}_{R}(M,N)=\hh^{i}(\Hom_{R}(\mathbf{T},N))\text{ for }i\in\mathbb{Z}.

By construction, there are isomorphisms

(2.2)

\widehat{\Tor}_{i}^{R}(M,N)\cong\Tor_{i}^{R}(M,N)\text{ and }\widehat{\Ext}^{i}_{R}(M,N)\cong\Ext^{i}_{R}(M,N),

for all
i>\gd_{R}(M)
.
In the following we summarize some basic properties about Tate homology which will be used throughout the paper; see
[
6
]
and
[
9
]
for more details.

Theorem 2.3
.

Let
M
and
N
be
R
–modules such that
M
has finite Gorenstein dimension. Then the following statements hold:

(i)

If either
\pd_{R}(M)<\infty
or
\pd_{R}(N)<\infty
, then
\Tt_{i}^{R}(M,N)=0
for all
i\in\mathbb{Z}
.

(ii)

If
\gd_{R}(N)<\infty
, then
\Tt_{i}^{R}(M,N)\cong\Tt_{i}^{R}(N,M)
for all
i\in\mathbb{Z}
.

(iii)

If
\gd_{R}(M)=0
, then
\widehat{\Tor}_{i}^{R}(M,N)\cong\widehat{\Ext}^{-i-1}_{R}(M^{*},N)
for all
i\in\mathbb{Z}
.

(iv)

If
N
has finite injective dimension, then
\Tt_{i}^{R}(M,N)=0
for all
i\in\mathbb{Z}
.

A sequence
\eta
of
R
-modules is called
\G
-proper
if the
induced sequence
\Hom_{R}(M,\eta)
is exact for every totally
reflexive
R
-module
M
.

A
\G
-proper resolution
of an
R
-module
M
is a resolution

\mathbf{L}\rightarrow M\rightarrow 0
by totally reflexive
R
-modules such that the augmented
resolution
\mathbf{L}^{+}
is a
\G
-proper sequence. Every module of finite Gorenstein
dimension has a
\G
-proper resolution.

Let
M
be an
R
-module with a
\G
-proper resolution
\mathbf{L}\rightarrow M\rightarrow 0
. The

\G
-relative
homology of
M
and
N
is defined as

\gt_{i}^{R}(M,N):=\hh_{i}(\mathbf{L}\otimes_{R}N)\ \text{for }i\in\mathbb{Z}.

It follows from the definition that
\gt_{i}^{R}(M,N)=0
for all
i>\gd_{R}(M)
.

Next we summarize some basic properties about relative homology which will be used throughout the paper; see
[
6
]
and
[
13
]
for more details.

Theorem 2.4
.

Let
M
and
N
be
R
–modules such that
M
has finite Gorenstein dimension. The following statements hold:

(i)

If either
\pd_{R}(M)
or
\pd_{R}(N)
is finite, then

\gt_{i}^{R}(M,N)=\Tor_{i}^{R}(M,N)\text{ for all }i\geq 0.

(ii)

There is an exact sequence:

\cdots\rightarrow\gt_{2}^{R}(M,N)\rightarrow\Tt_{1}^{R}(M,N)\rightarrow\Tor_{1}^{R}(M,N)\rightarrow\gt_{1}^{R}(M,N)\rightarrow 0.

(iii)

If
\gd_{R}(N)<\infty
, then
\gt_{i}^{R}(M,N)\cong\gt_{i}^{R}(N,M)
for all
i\geq 0
.

(iv)

If
N
has finite injective dimension, then

\gt_{i}^{R}(M,N)=\Tor_{i}^{R}(M,N)\text{ for all }i\geq 0.

The following results will be used throughout the paper.

3.
Depth formula and vanishing of Tate homology

The following lemma plays a crucial role in this paper. Before giving a proof we recall that, for
R
–modules
M
and
N
, the following sequence is exact:

(3.1)

0\rightarrow\Ext^{1}_{R}(\Tr M,N)\rightarrow M\otimes_{R}N\overset{\eta}{\rightarrow}\Hom_{R}(M^{*},N)\rightarrow\Ext^{2}_{R}(\Tr M,N)\rightarrow 0,

where
\eta
is the evaluation map
[
4
, Proposition 2.6]
.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
M
be a totally reflexive
R
–module and let
N
be an
R
–module. Assume
n
is a nonnegative integer such that

\Tt_{i}^{R}(M,N)=0
for
-n\leq i\leq 0
. Then
\depth_{R}(M\otimes_{R}N)\geq\min\{n+1,\depth_{R}(N)\}
.

Proof.

As
M^{*}=\Omega^{2}\Tr M
, by Theorem
2.3
(iii), Theorem
2.2
(i) and (
2.2
), we have the following isomorphisms

\begin{array}[]{rl}\Tt_{i}^{R}(M,N)&\cong\widehat{\Ext}^{-i-1}_{R}(M^{*},N)\\
&\cong\widehat{\Ext}^{-i+1}_{R}(\Tr M,N)\\
&\cong\Ext^{-i+1}_{R}(\Tr M,N)=0,\end{array}

for all
-n\leq i\leq 0
. If
n=0
then we get the following exact sequence:
M\otimes_{R}N\hookrightarrow\Hom_{R}(M^{*},N)
by (
3.1
.1) and the exact sequence (
3.1
).
Therefore,
\Ass_{R}(M\otimes_{R}N)\subseteq\Ass_{R}(N)
and so the assertion is clear. If
n\geq 1
then by (
3.1
.1) and the exact sequence (
3.1
) we get the following isomorphism,

(
3.1
.2)

M\otimes_{R}N\cong\Hom_{R}(M^{*},N).

Hence,
\depth_{R}(M\otimes_{R}N)=\depth_{R}(\Hom_{R}(M^{*},N))\geq\min\{2,\depth_{R}(N)\}
and so the assertion is clear for
n=1
. Now let
n>1
and let

\mathbf{P}\rightarrow M^{*}\rightarrow 0
be a free resolution of
M^{*}
.
Note that
\Ext^{i}_{R}(M^{*},N)=0
for
1\leq i\leq n-1
by (
3.1
.1). Hence, by applying the functor
\Hom_{R}(-,N)
to the free resolution of
M^{*}
, we get the
following exact sequence:

(
3.1
.3)

0\rightarrow\Hom_{R}(M^{*},N)\rightarrow\Hom_{R}(P_{0},N)\rightarrow\cdots\rightarrow\Hom_{R}(P_{n},N).

Note that
\depth_{R}(\Hom_{R}(P_{i},N))=\depth_{R}(N)
for all
i\geq 0
. It follows easily from the exact sequence (
3.1
.3) that

(
3.1
.4)

\depth_{R}(\Hom_{R}(M^{*},N))\geq\min\{n+1,\depth_{R}(N)\}.

Now the assertion follows from (
3.1
.2) and (
3.1
.4).
∎

Theorem 3.2
.

Let
R
be a Cohen-Macaulay local ring of dimension
d
and let
M
be an
R
–module of finite Gorenstein dimension and of depth
n
. Assume that
N
is an
R
–module and that the following conditions hold:

(i)

\Tt_{i}^{R}(M,N)=0
for
-n<i\leq 0
.

(ii)

\gt_{i}^{R}(M,N)=0
for
1\leq i\leq d-n
(equivalently, for all
i>0
).

Then either
\depth_{R}(M\otimes_{R}N)+\depth R=\depth_{R}(M)+\depth_{R}(N)
or

\depth_{R}(M\otimes_{R}N)\geq\depth_{R}(M)
.

Proof.

Assume that
\depth_{R}(M\otimes_{R}N)<\depth_{R}(M)
. We must prove that the pair
(M,N)
satisfies the depth formula.
Without loss of generality, we may assume that
R
is Cohen-Macaulay ring with canonical module.
Therefore, by
[
5
, Theorem A]
,
N
has a maximal Cohen-Macaulay approximation:

(
3.2
.1)

0\longrightarrow Q\longrightarrow Y\longrightarrow N\longrightarrow 0,

Where
Y
is maximal Cohen-Macaulay and
Q
has finite injective dimension. The exact sequence (
3.2
.1) induces the following long exact sequence

(
3.2
.2)

\cdots\rightarrow\Tt_{i}^{R}(M,Q)\rightarrow\Tt_{i}^{R}(M,Y)\rightarrow\Tt_{i}^{R}(M,N)\rightarrow\cdots,

of Tate homology modules by
[
9
, Proposition 2.8]
. As
\id_{R}(Q)<\infty
,
\Tt_{i}^{R}(M,Q)=0
for all
i\in\mathbb{Z}
by Theorem
2.3
(iv). Therefore,
\Tt_{i}^{R}(M,Y)\cong\Tt_{i}^{R}(M,N)
for all
i\in\mathbb{Z}
by (
3.2
.2).
In particular, by (i)

(
3.2
.3)

\Tt_{i}^{R}(M,Y)=0\text{ for }-n<i\leq 0.

As
\id_{R}(Q)<\infty
, the exact sequence (
3.2
.1) is proper and it induces the following long exact sequence

\begin{array}[]{rl}\cdots\rightarrow\gt_{1}^{R}(M,Q)\rightarrow\gt_{1}^{R}(M,Y)\rightarrow\gt_{1}^{R}(M,N)\rightarrow\\
M\otimes_{R}Q\rightarrow M\otimes_{R}Y\rightarrow M\otimes_{R}N\rightarrow 0,\end{array}

of relative homology modules by the homology version of
[
6
, Proposition 4.4]
(see also
[
6
, Remark 7.4]
). By (ii),
\gt_{1}^{R}(M,N)=0
and so we have the following exact sequence

(
3.2
.5)

0\rightarrow M\otimes_{R}Q\rightarrow M\otimes_{R}Y\rightarrow M\otimes_{R}N\rightarrow 0,

by (
3.2
.4). As
Y
is maximal Cohen-Macaulay,

(
3.2
.6)

\gt_{i}^{R}(M,Y)=0\text{ for all }i>0,

by Lemma
2.6
.
It follows from (
3.2
.4), (
3.2
.6) and (ii) that
\gt_{i}^{R}(M,Q)=0
for all
i>0
. Therefore, by Lemma
2.5
and Theorem
2.4
(iv)

(
3.2
.7)

\depth R+\depth_{R}(M\otimes_{R}Q)=\depth_{R}(M)+\depth_{R}(Q).

Now consider the following exact sequence

(
3.2
.8)

0\longrightarrow X\longrightarrow G\longrightarrow M\longrightarrow 0,

where
\pd_{R}(X)<\infty
and
\gd_{R}(G)=0
. As
X
has a finite projective dimension,
\Tt_{i}^{R}(X,Y)=0
for all
i\in\mathbb{Z}
by Theorem
2.3
(i). Therefore, the exact sequence (
3.2
.8) induces the following isomorphism
\Tt_{i}^{R}(M,Y)\cong\Tt_{i}^{R}(G,Y)
for all
i\in\mathbb{Z}
by
[
9
, Proposition 2.9]
. In particular, by (
3.2
.3) we have

(
3.2
.9)

\Tt_{i}^{R}(G,Y)=0\text{ for }-n<i\leq 0.

As
Y
is maximal Cohen-Macaulay, it follows from (
3.2
.9) and Lemma
3.1
that

(
3.2
.10)

\depth_{R}(G\otimes_{R}Y)\geq n.

As
\pd_{R}(X)<\infty
and
Y
is maximal Cohen-Macaulay,
\Tor_{i}^{R}(X,Y)=0
for all
i>0
by
[
16
, Lemma 2.2]
and so

(
3.2
.11)

\depth_{R}(X\otimes_{R}Y)=\depth_{R}(X)=n+1.

by Lemma
2.5
. As
\pd_{R}(X)<\infty
, the exact sequence (
3.2
.8) is proper
and it induces the following long exact sequence

(
3.2
.12)

0\rightarrow X\otimes_{R}Y\rightarrow G\otimes_{R}Y\rightarrow M\otimes_{R}Y\rightarrow 0.

by the homology version of
[
6
, Proposition 4.6]
and (
3.2
.6).
It follows easily from (
3.2
.10), (
3.2
.11) and the exact sequence (
3.2
,12) that

(
3.2
.13)

\depth_{R}(M\otimes_{R}Y)\geq n.

As
\depth_{R}(M\otimes_{R}N)<n
, by (
3.2
.13) and the exact sequence (
3.2
.5)

(
3.2
.14)

\depth_{R}(M\otimes_{R}N)=\depth_{R}(M\otimes_{R}Q)-1.

Note that
\depth_{R}(N)=\depth_{R}(Q)-1
. Therefore the assertion follows from (
3.2
.14) and (
3.2
.7).
∎

As a consequence we have the following result.

Corollary 3.3
.

Let
R
be a Cohen-Macaulay local ring of dimension
d
and let
M
,
N
be
R
–modules. Assume that
M
is totally reflexive and that

\Tt_{i}^{R}(M,N)=0
for
-d<i\leq 0
. Then either
M\otimes_{R}N
is maximal Cohen-Macaulay or
\depth_{R}(M\otimes_{R}N)=\depth_{R}(N)
.

Proof.

First note that
\gt_{i}^{R}(M,N)=0
for all
i>0
, because
M
is totally reflexive. Therefore, by Theorem
3.2
, either the pair
(M,N)
satisfies depth formula or

\depth_{R}(M\otimes_{R}N)\geq\depth_{R}(M)
. Now the assertion follows from Auslander-Bridger formula.
∎

Theorem 3.4
.

Let
R
be a Cohen-Macaulay local ring of dimension
d
and let
M
,
N
be
R
–modules. Assume that
M
has finite Gorenstein dimension and that the following conditions hold:

(i)

\Tt_{i}^{R}(M,N)=0
for
-d<i\leq 0
.

(ii)

\gt_{i}^{R}(M,N)=0
for all
i>0
.

Then either
\depth_{R}(M\otimes_{R}N)+\depth R=\depth_{R}(M)+\depth_{R}(N)
or

\depth_{R}(M\otimes_{R}N)\geq\depth_{R}(N)
.

Proof.

Assume that
\depth_{R}(M\otimes_{R}N)<\depth_{R}(N)
. We must prove that the pair
(M,N)
satisfies the depth formula.
Without loss of generality, we may assume that
R
is Cohen-Macaulay ring with canonical module.
Consider the following exact sequence,

(
3.4
.1)

0\longrightarrow T\longrightarrow G\longrightarrow M\longrightarrow 0,

where
G
is totally reflexive and
T
has finite projective dimension. Therefore,
\Tt_{i}^{R}(T,N)=0
for all
i\in\mathbb{Z}
by Theorem
2.3
(i) and so
the exact sequence (
3.4
.1) induces the following isomorphism

(
3.4
.2)

\Tt_{i}^{R}(G,N)\cong\Tt_{i}^{R}(M,N)\text{ for all }i\in\mathbb{Z},

by
[
9
, Proposition 2.9]
. Now consider the following exact sequence

(
3.4
.3)

0\longrightarrow N\longrightarrow I\longrightarrow L\longrightarrow 0,

where
L
is maximal Cohen-Macaulay and
I
has finite injective dimension (see
[
5
, Theorem A]
).
By Theorem
2.3
(iv),
\Tt_{i}^{R}(G,I)=0
for all
i\in\mathbb{Z}
. Hence, the exact sequence (
3.4
.3) induces the following isomorphism

(
3.4
.4)

\Tt_{i}^{R}(G,N)\cong\Tt_{i+1}^{R}(G,L)\text{ for all }i\in\mathbb{Z},

by
[
9
, Proposition 2.8]
. It follows from (i), (
3.4
.2) and (
3.4
.4) that

(
3.4
.5)

\Tt_{i}^{R}(G,L)=0\text{ for }-d+1<i\leq 1.

As

is totally reflexive,
\Tor_{1}^{R}(G,L)=0
by (
2.2
) and (
3.4
.5). Hence, the exact sequence (
3.4
.3) induces the following exact sequence:

(
3.4
.6)

0\rightarrow N\otimes_{R}G\rightarrow I\otimes_{R}G\rightarrow L\otimes_{R}G\rightarrow 0.

It follows from Lemma
3.1
and (
3.4
.5) that
\depth_{R}(L\otimes_{R}G)\geq d-1
. Therefore, by the exact sequence (
3.4
.6),

(
3.4
.7)

\depth_{R}(N\otimes_{R}G)\geq\min\{\depth_{R}(I\otimes_{R}G),d\}=\depth_{R}(I\otimes_{R}G).

As
\id_{R}(I)<\infty
and
G
is totally reflexive,
\Tor_{i}^{R}(G,I)\cong\gt_{i}^{R}(G,I)=0
for all
i>0
by Theorem
2.4
(iv) and Lemma
2.6
.
Hence, by Lemma
2.5
the pair
(G,I)
satisfies depth formula and so by Auslander-Bridger formula we have

(
3.4
.8)

\depth_{R}(I\otimes_{R}G)=\depth_{R}(I)=\depth_{R}(N).

As
\pd_{R}(T)<\infty
, the exact sequence (
3.4

\begin{array}[]{rl}\cdots\rightarrow\gt_{1}^{R}(T,N)\rightarrow\gt_{1}^{R}(G,N)\rightarrow\gt_{1}^{R}(M,N)\rightarrow\\
T\otimes_{R}N\rightarrow G\otimes_{R}N\rightarrow M\otimes_{R}N\rightarrow 0,\end{array}

of relative homology modules. By (ii) and the long exact sequence (

(
3.4
.10)

\gt_{i}^{R}(G,N)=0
for all

. Hence, it follows from (ii), Theorem
2.4
(i) and the exact sequence (

.9) that
\Tor_{i}^{R}(T,N)\cong\gt_{i}^{R}(T,N)=0
for all

. Therefore, by Lemma

\begin{array}[]{rl}\depth_{R}(T\otimes_{R}N)=\depth_{R}(N)-\pd_{R}(T)\\
=\depth_{R}(N)-\gd_{R}(M)+1.\end{array}

As
\depth_{R}(M\otimes_{R}N)<\depth_{R}(N)
, it follows from (
3.4
.7), (
3.4
.8) and the exact sequence (
3.4
.10) that

(
3.4
.12)

\depth_{R}(T\otimes_{R}N)=\depth_{R}(M\otimes_{R}N)+1.

Now the assertion follows from (
3.4
.11), (
3.4
.12) and Auslander-Bridger formula.
∎

The following is an immediate consequence of Theorem

and Theorem

.

Corollary 3.5
.

Let
R
be a Cohen-Macaulay local ring of dimension
d
and let
M
,
N
be
R
–modules. Assume that

has finite Gorenstein dimension and that the following conditions hold:

(i)

\Tt_{i}^{R}(M,N)=0
for
-d<i\leq 0
.

(ii)

\gt_{i}^{R}(M,N)=0
for all

.

Then at least one of the following holds:

\depth_{R}(M\otimes_{R}N)+\depth R=\depth_{R}(M)+\depth_{R}(N)
.

(2)

\depth_{R}(M\otimes_{R}N)\geq\max\{\depth_{R}(M),\depth_{R}(N)\}

In particular, if either

\depth_{R}(M\otimes_{R}N)=\depth_{R}(N)

.

3.5
.

4.
Main result

In order to prove our main result, Theorem
1.2
, we will prove two Theorems which are of independent interest.

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
R
be a Cohen-Macaulay local ring of dimension
d
and let
N
be an
R
–module of depth
n
. Assume that
M
is an
R
–module of finite and positive Gorenstein dimension and that the following conditions hold:

(i)

\Tt_{i}^{R}(M,N)=0
for
-n\leq i\leq 0
.

(ii)

\gt_{i}^{R}(M,N)=0
for all
i>0
.

Then
\depth_{R}(M\otimes_{R}N)+\depth R=\depth_{R}(M)+\depth_{R}(N)
.

Proof.

Without loss of generality, we may assume that
R
is Cohen-Macaulay ring with canonical module.
Consider the following exact sequence:

(
4.1
.1)

and

is totally reflexive (see
[
8
, 3.3]
). As

for all

by Theorem
2.3
(i). Therefore, the exact sequence (
4.1
.1) induces the following isomorphism
\Tt_{i}^{R}(M,N)\cong\Tt_{i+1}^{R}(X,N)
for all

. In particular,

(
4.1
.2)

\Tt_{i}^{R}(X,N)=0\text{ for }-n+1\leq i\leq 1,

by (i). It follows from (
4.1
.2) and Lemma
3.1

(
4.1
.3)

\depth_{R}(X\otimes_{R}N)\geq\depth_{R}(N)=n.

As
X
is totally reflexive, by (
4.1
.2) and (
2.2
),

(
4.1
.4)

\Tor_{1}^{R}(X,N)\cong\Tt_{1}^{R}(X,N)=0.

Therefore, the exact sequence (
4.1
.1) induces the following exact sequence:

(
4.1
.5)

0\longrightarrow M\otimes_{R}N\longrightarrow P\otimes_{R}N\longrightarrow X\otimes_{R}N\longrightarrow 0.

0\rightarrow\Omega P\rightarrow F\rightarrow P\rightarrow 0
, where
F
is free. Taking the pull-back of the two maps into

, we get the following commutative diagram

\begin{CD}&&&&&&&&\\
\ \ &&&&0&&0\\
&&&&@V{}V{}V@V{}V{}V\\
&&&&\Omega P@>{=}>{}>\Omega P\\
&&&&@V{}V{}V@V{}V{}V\\
\ \ &&0@>{}>{}>Z@>{}>{}>F@>{}>{}>X@>{}>{}>0&\\

Z

\begin{CD}&&&&&&&&\\

\gt_{1}^{R}(M,N)=0
. It follows from the above commutative diagram that

\Tor_{1}^{R}(P,N)=0.

(

(

\Tor_{i}^{R}(M,Q)\cong\gt_{i}^{R}(M,Q)=0\text{ for all }i>0.

Theorem 4.2
.

Let

be a Cohen-Macaulay local ring of dimension

Consider the following exact sequence:

(

(

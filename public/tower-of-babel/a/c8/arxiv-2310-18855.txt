Ergodic theory on coded shift spaces
Tamara Kucherenko, Martin Schmoll, Christian Wolf

Abstract
. We study ergodic-theoretic properties of coded shift spaces. A coded shift space is defined as a closure of all bi-infinite concatenations of words from a fixed countable generating set. We derive sufficient conditions for the uniqueness of measures of maximal entropy and equilibrium states of Hölder continuous potentials based on the partition of the coded shift into its concatenation set (sequences that are concatenations of generating words) and its residual set (sequences added under the closure). In this case we provide a simple explicit description of the measure of maximal entropy. We also obtain flexibility results for the entropy on the concatenation and residual sets. Finally, we prove a local structure theorem for intrinsically ergodic coded shift spaces which shows that our results apply to a larger class of coded shift spaces compared to previous works by Climenhaga [ 8 ] , Climenhaga and Thompson [ 10 , 11 ] , and Pavlov [ 25 ] .

1.
Introduction

1.1.
Motivation

It is one of the central goals in the ergodic theory of dynamical systems to identify the typical dynamics of a given system. In this context the word ‘typical’ refers to a set of points of full measure with respect to an invariant probability measure. For many classes of dynamical systems the set of invariant probability measures is rather large which raises the question which invariant measures are the natural choice to use as reference measures. Arguably the most natural reference measure choice is a measure
whose typical points carry the entire topological orbit complexity in terms of entropy. Such a measure (if it exists) is called a measure of maximal entropy. If a measure of maximal entropy exists and is unique the dynamical system is called intrinsically ergodic
[
33
,
34
]
.

In this paper, we work in the context of symbolic dynamical systems over a finite alphabet. There is a vast amount of research on the questions concerning the uniqueness of measures of maximal entropy and equilibrium states, e.g.,
[
7
,
8
,
10
,
11
,
15
,
17
,
20
,
25
]
.
We study coded shift spaces which were introduced by Blanchard and Hansel
[
4
]
as a natural generalization of transitive sofic shifts. Coded shifts include a wide range of well-known classes of symbolic systems, e.g., S-gap shifts, generalized gap shifts, and beta shifts. Our goal is to develop a fairly complete theory about the uniqueness of measures of maximal entropy and equilibrium states for Hölder continuous potentials. Roughly speaking, our classification depends on whether the entropy (or pressure) is concentrated on the concatenation set or the residual set. In the entropy case our results do not only guaranty the uniqueness of the measure of maximal entropy, but also provide an explicit formula for the values of such measure on cylinders. In particular, we obtain new information about the structure of measures of maximal entropy for such well-studied shift spaces as beta shifts and the Dyck shift.

While evaluating the entropy and pressure on the concatenation and the residual sets we also derive flexibility results concerning their entropies. Moreover, we establish a local structure theorem for intrinsically
ergodic coded shifts. From the structure theorem we conclude that our results apply to an abundance of coded shift spaces for which previous results of Climenhaga
[
8
]
, Climenhaga and Thompson
[
10
,
11
]
, and Pavlov
[
25
]
do not provide answers about the uniqueness of measures of maximal entropy and equilibrium states.

1.2.
Main results

Let
X
be a bi-infinite shift space over a finite alphabet
{\mathcal{A}}
. This means that
X
is a closed (with respect to the Tychonoff product topology), shift invariant subset of the full shift
{\mathcal{A}}^{\mathbb{Z}}
. Denote the left shift map on
X
by
\sigma
. We say
(X,\sigma)
is a coded shift space (or simply
X
is coded), if there exists a generating set
{\mathcal{G}}
which is a subset of the set of all finite words over
{\mathcal{A}}

such that
X=X({\mathcal{G}})
is the smallest shift space that contains the concatenation set
X_{\rm con}=X_{\rm con}({\mathcal{G}})
, i.e. all bi-infinite concatenations of generators. In other words,
X
is the topological closure of
X_{\rm con}
. We refer to
[
21
]
for equivalent ways to define coded shifts.
We call the set
X_{\rm res}=X_{\rm res}({\mathcal{G}})=X\setminus X_{\rm con}
the residual set of
X
. We refer to Section 3 for more details. We recall, that if the generating set is finite, then
X
is a transitive sofic shift. In particular, its theory of measures of maximal entropy and equilibrium states for Hölder continuous potentials is well-understood. Therefore, the focus of this paper is the case of infinite generating sets.

A priori, it is possible that points in
X_{\rm con}
have multiple representations as concatenations of generators. We say a coded shift
X
is uniquely representable, if there exists a generating set
{\mathcal{G}}
such that
X=X({\mathcal{G}})
and every point in
X_{\rm con}({\mathcal{G}})
has a unique representation as a concatenation of generators. In this situation we also say that
{\mathcal{G}}
uniquely represents
X_{\rm con}({\mathcal{G}})
.
A weaker condition is that
X=X({\mathcal{G}})
has the unique decipherability property, i.e., every finite word in
X
has at most one representation as a concatenation of generators. It is shown by Blanchard and Hansel
[
4
]
in 1986 that every coded shift has a generating set
{\mathcal{G}}
which has the unique decipherability property.
By using results from
[
14
]
, Béal, Perrin and Restivo
[
3
]
recently proved that every coded shift is uniquely representable.
Moreover, the proof in
[
3
]
is constructive. In particular, the proof can be used as a blueprint for a computer program which, based on the input of an arbitrary generating set
{\mathcal{G}}^{\prime}
outputs a generating set
{\mathcal{G}}
such that
{\mathcal{G}}
uniquely represents
X_{\rm con}({\mathcal{G}})
and
X({\mathcal{G}})=X({\mathcal{G}}^{\prime})
. Based on this result, it is reasonable to assume that we have a generating set which uniquely represents its concatenation set.

We remark that the set of coded shifts spans a rather broad collection of shift spaces. Indeed, every shift space
X
can be
expressed by a representation of
X
, i.e., a countable directed labeled graph such that
X
is the closure of the set of bi-infinite sequences which are associated with bi-infinite paths on the graph. Coded shifts are precisely those shift spaces which have a strongly connected representation, see
[
21
]
. It turns out that many well-known classes of shifts are coded including S-gap shifts, generalized gap shifts, transitive Sofic shifts, beta shifts, and many more, see, e.g.,
[
6
]
.

Let
X
be a shift space and let
h_{\rm top}(X)
denote the topological entropy of
\sigma|_{X}
. The variational principle for the topological entropy states that
h_{\rm top}(X)=\sup_{\mu\in\mathcal{M}_{\sigma}(X)}h_{\sigma}(\mu)
where
\mathcal{M}_{\sigma}(X)
is the space of all Borel
\sigma
-invariant probability measures on
X
endowed with the weak
∗
topology, and
h_{\sigma}(\mu)
denotes the measure-theoretic entropy of
\mu
defined in (
5
).
We say a measure
\mu\in\mathcal{M}_{\sigma}(X)
is a measure of maximal entropy for
(X,\sigma)
if
h_{\rm top}(X)=h_{\sigma}(\mu).
It follows from the expansivity of the shift map that there exists at least one (ergodic) measure of maximal entropy. However, the measure of maximal entropy is, in general, not unique
[
31
,
20
,
7
,
17
]
; if it is unique the system is called intrinsically ergodic.
Roughly speaking, if an ergodic measure
\mu
is a measure of maximal entropy its typical orbits carry the full orbit complexity in terms of entropy.

Our approach is to study the entropy on the sets
X_{\rm con}
and
X_{{\rm res}}
separately. We define
the
concatenation entropy
of
X
by

(1)

h_{\rm con}(X)=\sup\{h_{\sigma}(\mu):\mu\in\mathcal{M}_{\sigma}(X)\text{ and }\mu(X_{\rm con})=1\},

and the
residual entropy
of
X
is defined by

(2)

h_{\rm res}(X)=\sup\{h_{\sigma}(\mu):\mu\in\mathcal{M}_{\sigma}(X)\text{ and }\mu(X_{\rm res})=1\}.

We note that the concatenation entropy and residual entropy were introduced in
[
6
]
where they were called sequential entropy and limit entropy, respectively.
It is shown in Lemma
3
that
X_{\rm con}
and
X_{{\rm res}}
are Borel sets and thus
h_{\rm con}(X)
and
h_{\rm res}(X)
are well defined. Clearly,
X_{\rm con}
and
X_{\rm res}
are shift invariant sets.
Therefore,
h_{\rm top}(X)=\max\{h_{\rm con}(X),h_{\rm res}(X)\}
. Moreover, every ergodic measure of maximal entropy must put full measure either on
X_{\rm con}
or on
X_{\rm res}
. We refer to the analogous definitions of the concatenation respectively residual pressure to Section
6
.

Our first main result concerns the flexibility of the concatenation entropy. It is not too hard to see that every invariant measure which puts full measure on
X_{\rm res}
must also put full measure on a certain subshift called the limit set
X_{\rm lim}
which is the set of points
x\in X

such that every subword of
x
is a subword of some generator in
{\mathcal{G}}
(see (
8
) for the precise definition). Since the language of the concatenation set contains the language of the limit set one might heuristically expect that
h_{\rm con}(X)\geq h_{\rm res}(X)
does hold in general. The problem with this heuristic argument is that
X_{\rm con}
is not closed.
In particular, the next theorem (see Theorem
1
in the text) shows that if a fixed subshift
Z
is contained in the residual set, the concatenation entropy might still be arbitrary small.

Theorem A
.

Suppose
Z
is a transitive subshift on a finite alphabet
{\mathcal{A}}
which is not the full shift. Then there exists a generating set
{\mathcal{G}}
on the same alphabet
{\mathcal{A}}
such that for
X=X({\mathcal{G}})
the following holds:

(i)

{\mathcal{G}}
uniquely represents
X_{\rm con}
;

(ii)

Z\subset X_{\rm res}
, and if
\mu\in\mathcal{M}_{\sigma}(X)
with
\mu(X_{\rm res})=1
then
\mu(Z)=1
;

(iii)

h_{\rm con}(X)<\epsilon
.

Theorem A shows that it is not possible to obtain a general uniqueness result for measures of maximal entropy for the case
h_{\rm res}(X)>h_{\rm con}(X)
. Indeed, if the subshift
Z
in Theorem A is of positive topological entropy and has multiple measures of maximal entropy (e.g., one of the shifts constructed in
[
7
,
17
,
31
]
) then for
0<\epsilon<h_{\rm top}(Z)
the coded shift
X
also has multiple measures of maximal entropy (which are precisely the measures which maximize the entropy on
Z
).
We note here that the main difficulty is to prove property (iii). Since
X_{\rm con}
is not a subshift, estimates on word complexity cannot be applied. To overcome this difficulty we design a certain coded shift
Y
with
h_{\rm con}(Y)>h_{\rm res}(Y)
such that every invariant measure on
X
which puts full measure on the concatenation set is measure-theoretic isomorphic to a measure on the concatenation set of
Y
. This allows us to deduce (iii) from the corresponding property of
Y
.

Next we consider the case
h_{\rm res}(X)=h_{\rm con}(X)
. In this case it is possible to produce examples of coded shifts
X
with multiple measures of maximal entropy, see Examples
2
and
4
.

Finally, we consider the case
h_{\rm con}(X)>h_{\rm res}(X)
and establish the uniqueness of the measure of maximal entropy. To characterize this measure we introduce and develop in Section
4
the theory of the so-called
{\mathcal{G}}
-Bernoulli measures on a coded shifts
X
with generating set
{\mathcal{G}}
. These are probability measures which assign full measure to the concatenation set and share some similarities with Bernoulli measures which contain the unique measures of maximal entropy of full shifts. Precisely, we say that an invariant probability measure
\mu
is
{\mathcal{G}}
-Bernoulli if there exist positive real numbers
p_{g},g\in{\mathcal{G}}
with
\sum_{g\in{\mathcal{G}}}p_{g}=1
and
c>0
such that

\llbracket g_{0}\dots g_{k}\rrbracket=\frac{1}{c}\,p_{g_{0}}\cdot\ldots\cdot p_{g_{k}}

for all
g_{0},\dots,g_{k}\in{\mathcal{G}}
. Here
\llbracket g_{0}\dots g_{k}\rrbracket
denotes the
{\mathcal{G}}
-cylinder consisting of all points
x=\cdots g^{\prime}_{{-1}}.g^{\prime}_{0}\cdots g^{\prime}_{k}g^{\prime}_{{k+1}}\cdots\in X_{\rm con}
such that
g^{\prime}_{j}=g_{j}
for
j=0,\dots,k
. We refer to Section
4
for further details about
{\mathcal{G}}
-cylinders and
{\mathcal{G}}
-Bernoulli measures. With these definitions in mind we have the following result.

Theorem B
.

Let
X=X({\mathcal{G}})
be a coded shift such that
{\mathcal{G}}
uniquely represents
X_{{\rm con}}
and
h_{{\rm con}}(X)>h_{{\rm res}}(X)
.
Then
X
has a unique measure of maximal entropy
\mu_{\max}
. Moreover,
\mu_{\max}
is

{\mathcal{G}}
-Bernoulli with
p_{g}=\exp(-|g|h_{\rm top}(X))
and
c=\sum_{g\in{\mathcal{G}}}|g|\exp(-|g|h_{\rm top}(X))
.

It is important to note that Theorem B does not only establish the uniqueness of the measure of maximal entropy for a large class of shift spaces, it also provides an explicit formula for this measure. In the context of symbolic dynamics such an explicit formula for the measure of maximal entropy has, to the best of our knowledge, previously only been known for subshifts of finite type, sofic shifts, S-gap shifts
[
15
, Theorem 3.22]
, and beta shifts
[
18
]
. In the latter case the measure of maximal entropy is the lift of the Parry measure on a countable full shift.
As a consequence of Theorem B and results in
[
6
]
we obtain that the unique measure of maximal entropy of a beta shift is the
{\mathcal{G}}
-Bernoulli measure with respect to the natural generating set
{\mathcal{G}}
.
We refer to Example
3
for more details.

Furthemore, the techniques used in the proof of Theorem B can in some cases be applied to obtain an explicit formula for a measure of maximal entropy even when it is not unique. The idea is to use different adaptations of the generating set for each measure of maximal entropy of the coded shift
X
and show that the said measure is
{\mathcal{G}}
-Bernoulli for the new generating set. We showcase this approach on the Dyck shift in Example
4
. The Dyck shift is the go-to example of a coded shift where the assumptions of the uniqueness theorems in
[
8
,
25
]
, as well as Theorem B, fail: for its canonical generating set we have
X\subset X_{\rm lim}
and
h_{\rm con}(X)<h_{\rm res}(X)
. Also, the Dyck shift does have two ergodic measures of maximal entropy. Despite these pathological properties, we are able to show that both these measures are
{\mathcal{G}}
-Bernoulli with respect to slightly modified generating sets.

We recall that for transitive subshifts of finite type and Hölder continuous potentials the unique equilibrium state is a Gibbs measure (see (
7
) for the definition) which is Bernoulli
[
5
]
. It turns out that in the case of intrinsic ergodicity of a coded shift
X
, the measure of maximal entropy
\mu_{\rm max}
in Theorem B does not, in general, have the Gibbs property (see Example
1
).

As a consequence of Theorem B we obtain a formula for the topological entropy of a large class of coded shifts.
Namely, under the assumptions of Theorem
B we obtain that
h_{\rm top}(X)=\log\lambda_{\ast}
where
\lambda_{\ast}
is the unique solution of
the characteristic equation

(3)

\sum_{g\in{\mathcal{G}}}\lambda^{-|g|}=1,

see Lemma
6
.
We note that (
3
) generalizes several papers in the literature with similar formulas for the topological entropy of certain classes of coded shift spaces, e.g.,
[
2
,
6
,
9
,
13
,
22
,
25
,
29
]
. The origin of (
3
) can be traced back to the loop method for computing the entropy of a subshift of finite type developed by Peterson
[
26
]
in 1986, and, in particular, (
3
) appears in
[
25
, Theorem 1.3]
under a special condition on the coded shift space.

One consequence of the entropy formula (
3
) is that
X
is an almost-sofic shift, that is, there exists an increasing sequence of sofic shifts
X_{m}\subset X
such that
\lim_{m\to\infty}h_{\rm top}(X_{m})=h_{\rm top}(X)
. In fact, in the setting of Theorem B one can take
X_{m}=X_{m}({\mathcal{G}}(m))
where
{\mathcal{G}}(m)=\{g_{1},\dots,g_{m}\}\subset{\mathcal{G}}.
We refer to
[
26
]
for more details about almost-sofic shifts.

It is a well-known fact that transitive sofic shifts satisfy the entropy-minimality property, i.e., every proper subshift must have strictly smaller topological entropy, see, e.g.,
[
21
]
. We show that this result does not generalize to almost sofic shifts. Namely, we construct in Example
2
an almost sofic coded shift
X
which contains two disjoint proper subshifts
Z_{1}
and
Z_{2}
with
h_{\rm top}(Z_{1})=h_{\rm top}(Z_{2})=h_{\rm top}(X)
.

Recall that
X_{\rm con}
is not closed and in particular not a shift space. Therefore, Theorem B combined with the fact that
X_{\rm con}
is not a subshift
implies the following:

Corollary
.

Suppose a coded shift
X
has a measure of maximal entropy which assigns full measure to a subshift
Y\subset X
with
Y\not=X
. Then
h_{\rm con}(X)\leq h_{\rm res}(X)
for any generating set
{\mathcal{G}}
with
X=X({\mathcal{G}})
, and which uniquely represents
X_{\rm con}({\mathcal{G}})
.

We note that the corollary above is sharp. This follows from Example
2
.

We also consider the uniqueness of equilibrium states of hyperbolic Hölder continuous potentials. In particular, we show that if the entropy
h({\mathcal{G}})
of the generating set
{\mathcal{G}}
satisfies

h({\mathcal{G}})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\scriptscriptstyle\rm def}}{{=}}\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\log|\{g\in{\mathcal{G}}:|g|=n\}|=0

and the pressure of a hyperbolic Hölder continuous potential
\phi
on the concatenation set is greater than the pressure of
\phi
on the residual set, then
\phi
has a unique equilibrium state which is the lift of the invariant Gibbs measure on the associated induced system. We refer to Theorem

4
for more details.

As mentioned above, Theorem B and Theorem
4
are not the first results about unique measures of maximal entropy and equilibrium states for coded shifts. Indeed, Climenhaga
[
8
]
, Climenhaga and Thompson
[
10
,
11
]
and Pavlov
[
25
]
obtained uniqueness results under the assumption that the topological entropy (respectively pressure) of
X
exceeds the entropy of the limit set
X_{\rm lim}
. From the measure theoretical perspective this means that the concatenation entropy (respectively pressure) is greater than that of the limit set.
The approach of the above works is to generalize the classical specification-based methods of Bowen by considering weaker versions of specification.
They successfully establish and study the unique measure of maximal entropy (respectively equilibrium state) in this case.

A key difficulty for the applicability of the Climenhaga, Thompson, Pavlov
[
8
,
10
,
11
,
25
]
results is that
X_{\rm con}
and
X_{\rm lim}
are in general not disjoint. In fact, for many classes of coded shift spaces the limit set contains the concatenation set and consequently the results in
[
25
,
10
,
11
,
8
]
do not apply to obtain results about the uniqueness of entropy maximizing measures or equilibrium states.
We illustrate this in Theorem C below, which is a combination of Theorem
2
and Corollary
3
in Sections
3
and
5
, respectively. We show that for any coded shift space
X
with
h_{\rm top}(X)>h_{\rm top}(X_{\rm lim})
, there are infinitely many coded shift spaces
\widetilde{X}
”close” to
X
such that
\widetilde{X}\subset\widetilde{X}_{\lim}
holds while Theorem B still applies to
\widetilde{X}
and guarantees the uniqueness of the measure of maximal entropy. Consequently, Theorem B significantly extends the results of Climenhaga, Thompson and Pavlov.

Theorem C
.

Let
X=X({\mathcal{G}})
be a coded shift such that

{\mathcal{G}}
uniquely represents
X_{{\rm con}}
and
h_{\rm top}(X)>h_{\rm top}(X_{\rm lim})
.
Then for every
\epsilon>0
there exists a generating set
\widetilde{{\mathcal{G}}}
such that the coded shift
\widetilde{X}=X(\widetilde{\mathcal{G}})
contains
X
, and has the following properties:

(i)

\widetilde{\mathcal{G}}
uniquely represents
\widetilde{X}_{{\rm con}}
;

(ii)

\widetilde{X}\subset\widetilde{X}_{\lim}
;

(iii)

h_{{\rm res}}(X)=h_{{\rm res}}(\widetilde{X})<h_{\rm con}(\widetilde{X})
;

(iv)

h_{\rm top}(X)<h_{{\rm top}}(\widetilde{X})<h_{{\rm top}}(X)+\epsilon
.

We note that Theorem
2
is in fact a more general result than Theorem C. In some sense this theorem can be considered as a local structure result for the concatenation and residual entropy of coded shift spaces.

The paper is organized as follows. In Section 2 we review some
basic concepts from symbolic dynamics and the thermodynamic formalism.
Section 3 is devoted to the study of the concatenation and residual entropy for coded shift spaces. In particular, we present the proof of the flexibility result Theorem A and the local structure result Theorem
2
. In Section 4, we introduce and study
{\mathcal{G}}
-cylinders and so-called
{\mathcal{G}}
-Bernoulli measures for coded shift spaces. We also show that both the concatenation set and the residual set are Borel. The proof of Theorem B concerning the uniqueness of the measure of maximal entropy for coded shift spaces is presented in Section 5. In addition, several examples are constructed which emphasize various aspects of our results. Lastly, in Section 6 we derive results for the uniqueness of equilibrium states for hyperbolic Hölder continuous potentials.

2.
Preliminaries

We continue to use the notation from Section 1. We consider shift spaces over a (fixed) finite alphabet
{\mathcal{A}}
.
Let
{\mathcal{A}}^{*}=\bigcup_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}}{\mathcal{A}}^{n}
be the set of all finite words over
{\mathcal{A}}
. The length of the word
w\in{\mathcal{A}}^{*}
is denoted by
|w|
, whereas the empty word has length zero. Given
u,v\in{\mathcal{A}}^{*}
we write
uv
for the concatenation of the words
u
and
v
. For
x=(x_{i})_{i\in{\mathbb{Z}}}\in{\mathcal{A}}^{{\mathbb{Z}}}
and integers
n<m
we use the notation
x[n,m]
for the word
x_{n}\dots x_{m}
and
x[n,m)
for the word
x_{n}...x_{m-1}
. With a slight abuse of notation we also allow
n=-\infty
and
m=\infty
, i.e., we consider the rays

x(-\infty,m],x(-\infty,m),x[n,\infty]
and
x(n,\infty)
.
The cylinder of the word
w\in{\mathcal{A}}^{*}
is the subset of
{\mathcal{A}}^{{\mathbb{Z}}}
given by
[w]=\{x\in{\mathcal{A}}^{\mathbb{Z}}:x[0,|w|)=w\}
.

We briefly review some relevant definitions from the thermodynamic formalism, see
[
23
,
32
]
for detailed accounts.
Let
X
be a shift space with shift map
\sigma:X\to X
.
Endowing
X
with the
Tychonov product topology
makes
X
into a compact and metrizable topological space. In fact, the (standard) metric given by

(4)

d(x,y)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\scriptscriptstyle\rm def}}{{=}}2^{-\min\{|k|\;:\;x_{k}\neq y_{k}\}}

induces the Tychonov product topology on
X
.
We denote the set of
X
-admissible words of length
n
by
{\mathcal{L}}_{n}(X)
. We call
{\mathcal{L}}(X)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\scriptscriptstyle\rm def}}{{=}}\bigcup_{n=0}^{\infty}{\mathcal{L}}_{n}(X)
the
language of
X
.
Given
\mu\in\mathcal{M}_{\sigma}(X)
, the
measure-theoretic entropy
of
\mu
is defined by

(5)

h_{\sigma}(\mu)=\lim_{n\to\infty}-\frac{1}{n}\sum_{w\in{\mathcal{L}}(X,n)}\mu([w])\log(\mu([w])),

omitting terms with
\mu([w])=0
.
Let
\phi\in C(X,{\mathbb{R}})
. For
n\geq 1
, we define the
n
-th partition function

Z_{n}(\phi)
at
\phi
by

Z_{n}(\phi)=\sum_{w\in{\mathcal{L}}_{n}(X)}\exp\left(\sup_{x\in[w]}S_{n}\phi(x)\right),

where

S_{n}\phi(x)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\scriptscriptstyle\rm def}}{{=}}\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\phi(\sigma^{k}(x)).

We observe that the sequence
\left(\log Z_{n}(\phi)\right)_{n\geq 1}
is subadditive, see, e.g.,
[
23
]
. The
topological pressure
of
\phi
with respect to the shift space
X
is defined by

(6)

P_{\rm top}(X,\phi)=\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\log Z_{n}(\phi)=\inf\left\{\frac{1}{n}\log Z_{n}(\phi):n\geq 1\right\}.

Moreover,
h_{\rm top}(X)=P_{\rm top}(X,0)
denotes the
topological entropy
of
\sigma
.
We sometimes consider the topological pressure simultaneously on different shift spaces
X
, i.e.,
X
may be a (one-sided or two-sided) shift space over a finite or countable infinite alphabet. However, when it is clear from the context we suppress
X
in the notation of the pressure and write
P_{\rm top}(\phi)
for
P_{\rm top}(X,\phi)
.

Let
\phi\in C(X,{\mathbb{R}})
. Recall that a Borel probability measure
m
on
X
is a Gibbs measure for
\phi
if there exist constants
M>1
and
P_{m}
such that
for all
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
,
w\in{\mathcal{L}}_{n}(X)
, and
x\in[w]

(7)

M^{-1}\leq\frac{m([w])}{\exp(S_{n}\phi(x)-nP_{m})}\leq M.

Moreover, for every Gibbs measure
m
of
\phi
,
P_{m}=P_{\rm top}(\phi)
. In case the potential
\phi
is clear from the context we simply say that
m
is Gibbs. If additionally a Gibbs measure is shift-invariant, we say it is an invariant Gibbs measure.
We refer to
[
5
]
and
[
23
]
for more details.

In this paper we also make use of the measure-theoretic entropy, the topological pressure and Gibbs measures for the one-sided full shift
(Y^{+},\sigma)
and two-sided full shift
(Y,\sigma)
over a countable infinite alphabet, i.e.,
Y^{+}={\mathbb{N}}^{{\mathbb{N}}_{0}}
and
Y={\mathbb{N}}^{\mathbb{Z}}
, respectively. We note that in the countable infinite alphabet case the definitions for the measure-theoretic entropy, topological pressure and Gibbs measures are identical to the definitions (
5
), (
6
) and (
7
), see
[
23
]
for details.
We note that in the countable infinite alphabet case the quantities in (
5
) and (
6
) can be infinite. In particular, the infimum in (
6
) may be taken over a set of infinities which is infinity.

3.
Flexibility of the Residual Set

We fix an arbitrary nonempty set of finite words
{\mathcal{G}}\subset{\mathcal{A}}^{*}
. The
coded shift with the generating set

{\mathcal{G}}
is the smallest subshift of
{\mathcal{A}}^{{\mathbb{Z}}}
which contains all bi-infinite concatenations of the elements of
{\mathcal{G}}
. We denote this subshift by
X({\mathcal{G}})
. Then
X({\mathcal{G}})
can be naturally decomposed into two disjoint subsets: the
concatenation set

X_{\rm con}({\mathcal{G}})
consisting of the concatenations of the words from
{\mathcal{G}}
and the
residual set

X_{\rm res}({\mathcal{G}})
consisting of the points added to
X_{\rm con}({\mathcal{G}})
under the closure operation. More precisely, we define

\displaystyle X_{\rm con}({\mathcal{G}})

\displaystyle=\{x\in{\mathcal{A}}^{{\mathbb{Z}}}:\exists(k_{i})_{i\in{\mathbb{Z}}}\subset{\mathbb{Z}},k_{i}<k_{i+1}:x[k_{i},k_{i+1})\in{\mathcal{G}}\}

\displaystyle X_{\rm res}({\mathcal{G}})

\displaystyle=X({\mathcal{G}})\setminus X_{\rm con}({\mathcal{G}}).

We say that a generating set
{\mathcal{G}}
uniquely represents
X_{{\rm con}}
if for each
x\in X_{\rm con}({\mathcal{G}})
there is only one set of integers
\{k_{i}:i\in{\mathbb{Z}}\}
satisfying the above property.
It was shown in
[
3
]
that we can always find a
generating set
{\mathcal{G}}^{\prime}
so that
X({\mathcal{G}}^{\prime})=X({\mathcal{G}})
and
{\mathcal{G}}^{\prime}
uniquely represents
X_{{\rm con}}
. Therefore, we always assume that our generating set
{\mathcal{G}}
uniquely represents the concatenation set of the coded shift
X({\mathcal{G}})
. One might think about the set
{\mathcal{G}}
as a “countable alphabet” for
X_{{\rm con}}({\mathcal{G}})
. We use some of the analogous notations, e.g., for
W\subset{\mathcal{G}}
we write
W^{*}
for the set of all finite concatenations of the elements in
W
. Since the generating set
{\mathcal{G}}
is fixed, we frequently omit it in our notation and write
X
for
X({\mathcal{G}})
,
X_{\rm con}
for
X_{\rm con}({\mathcal{G}})
,
X_{\rm res}
for
X_{\rm res}({\mathcal{G}})
, etc.

Another important subset of
X({\mathcal{G}})
, which was considered by Climenhaga and Pavlov
[
8
,
25
]
, is the
limit set
defined by

(8)

X_{\rm lim}({\mathcal{G}})=\{x\in{\mathcal{A}}^{{\mathbb{Z}}}:\text{for any }k,\,x[-k,k]\text{ is a subword of some word in }{\mathcal{G}}\}.

Pavlov proved in
[
25
]
that
\mu(X_{\rm con}\cup X_{\rm lim})=1
for all
\mu\in\mathcal{M}_{\sigma}(X)
, and hence the concatenation set and the limit set determine the measure-theoretic dynamics on
X
. In fact, there are a number of results (e.g.,
[
8
,
10
,
25
]
) establishing various properties (including uniqueness) of measures of maximal entropy as well as equilibrium states for Hölder potentials which rely on the strict inequality between the entropy respectively pressure of
X
and
X_{\rm lim}
. However, the set
X_{\rm lim}
might be quite large in general, and even coincide with the set
X
itself. This is, for example, the case for the Dyck shift (see Example
4
), which was noted in
[
25
]
. The main motivation of the present work is to derive sufficient conditions for the uniqueness of measures of maximal entropy and equilibrium states based on the disjoint partition of the coded shift
X
into its concatenation and residual sets.

It was shown in
[
6
]
that in the case when the concatenation entropy is larger than the residual one we can compute the entropy of
X
as a limit of entropies of certain sofic subshifts. Precisely, consider any coded shift
X({\mathcal{G}})
with an infinite generating set
{\mathcal{G}}=\{g_{1},...,g_{m},\cdots\}
which uniquely represents
X_{{\rm con}}
.
Let
X_{m}
be coded shifts with generators
\{g_{1},...,g_{m}\}
.
Note that each
X_{m}
is a sofic shift since it has a finite generating set. If
h_{\rm con}(X)>h_{\rm res}(X)
then
\lim\limits_{m\to\infty}h_{\rm top}(X_{m})=h_{\rm top}(X)
. In contrast to this result we present a construction of a coded shift
X
which has any given transitive subshift as its residual set. Moreover, the entropies of
X_{m}
can be made uniformly bounded above by any given positive number, no matter how small. In fact, we prove that this number also bounds the concatenation entropy of
X
, hence providing a family of examples where a coded shift
X
satisfies
h_{\rm con}(X)\ll h_{\rm res}(X)
.

Theorem 1
.

Suppose
Z
is an irreducible subshift on a finite alphabet
{\mathcal{A}}
which is not a full shift. Let
\epsilon>0
be given. Then there exists a generating set
{\mathcal{G}}
on the same alphabet
{\mathcal{A}}
such that for
X=X({\mathcal{G}})
the following holds:

(i)

{\mathcal{G}}
uniquely represents
X_{\rm con}
;

(ii)

Z\subset X_{\rm res}
, and if
\mu\in\mathcal{M}_{\sigma}(X)
with
\mu(X_{\rm res})=1
then
\mu(Z)=1
;

(iii)

h_{\rm con}(X)<\epsilon

To prove the theorem, we build the generating set
{\mathcal{G}}
in such a way that distinct generating words can not have the same length. We show that the concatenation entropy in this case is controlled by the sequence of the cardinalities of elements in
{\mathcal{G}}
and then vary these cardinalities to achieve the
\epsilon
-bound for the entropy.
It turns out that such statement holds for any coded shift when the number of the generating words of fixed length is uniformly bounded. We establish this fact in the next lemma by constructing an isomorphism between
X_{\rm con}
and a certain generalized version of the
S
-gap shift on a large alphabet. Then we use properties of this shift to make assertions about the invariant measures on
X_{\rm con}
. As a nice by-product of this approach we obtain a formula for the concatenation entropy of a coded shift with bounded rate of growth of generating words.

The classical
S
-gap shift associated with a set
S
of non-negative integers on the alphabet
\{0,1\}
consists of strings where the number of 1’s between any two nearest 0’s must be an element of
S
. We note that the roles of 0 and 1 are reversed here compared to the convention in the literature, but it is more convenient for introducing the generalization we need. The entropy of the
S
-gap shift is
\log\lambda
where
\lambda
satisfies

(9)

\sum_{s\in S}\lambda^{-(s+1)}=1.

Although the expression (
9
) for the entropy of the
S
-gap shift was widely used, for a period of several decades there was no reference to a proof. It appears as an exercise in the monograph of Lind and Marcus
[
21
]
, which would certainly discourage one from publishing such a result. There is an incomplete proof by Spandl
[
29
]
(fixed years later in
[
15
, Corollary 3.18]
) and two alternative proofs are presented in Climenhaga’s blog
[
9
]
.

For us, the
S
-gap shift is a coded shift with generating set
\{01^{s}:s\in S\}
. The generalization we need for our purpose is the shift
Y
on the alphabet
\{0,...,d\}
associated to sets
S_{1},...,S_{d}
of non-negative integers given by the generator
\{0i^{s_{i}}:s_{i}\in S_{i},\,i=1,...,d\}
. We find its entropy by applying Pavlov’s result for coded shifts
[
25
, Theorem 1.3]
, which, in particular, gives a nice illustration of the usefulness of the theory.
It is easy to see that
Y_{\rm lim}
consists of
d
periodic points of the form
(...iii...)
, so its entropy is zero. Hence,
h_{\rm top}(Y)
satisfies
\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}c(n)e^{-nh_{\rm top}(Y)}=1
where
c(n)
is the number of words in the generator of
Y
of length
n
. We can rewrite this equation in the spirit of (
9
), so that
h_{\rm top}(Y)=\log\lambda
where
\lambda
is the unique solution of

(10)

\sum_{s\in S_{1}}\lambda^{-(s+1)}+\cdots+\sum_{s\in S_{d}}\lambda^{-(s+1)}=1.

Lemma 1
.

Let
X
be a coded shift with generator
{\mathcal{G}}
which uniquely represents
X_{{\rm con}}
. Suppose
{\mathcal{G}}
has a bounded rate of growth, i.e. there is an integer
d
such that
{\rm card}\{g\in{\mathcal{G}}:|g|=n\}\leq d
for all
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
. Then
h_{\rm con}(X)=\log\lambda
, where
\lambda
is the solution of

\sum_{g\in{\mathcal{G}}}\lambda^{-|g|}=1.

Proof.

For
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
denote by
{\mathcal{G}}(n)
the set of all generating words of length
n
,
{\mathcal{G}}(n)=\{g\in{\mathcal{G}}:|g|=n\}
and let
c(n)={\rm card}\,{\mathcal{G}}(n)
. By our assumptions these cardinalities are bounded by some constant
d
, so we adopt
d=\max\{c(n):n\in{\mathbb{N}}\}
. We define the gap-size sets
S_{i}
as

S_{i}=\{n\in{\mathbb{N}}:c(n+1)\geq i\}\text{ for }i=1,...,d.

Suppose that the generating set of
X
has
r
words of length one. We construct an isomorphism between
X
and a coded shift on the alphabet
\{0,...,d+r\}
by identifying generating words of length
n\geq 2
with
0(i)^{n-1}
where
1\leq i\leq d
and words of length 1 with
i
where
d+1\leq i\leq d+r
. Hence, we consider the coded shift
Y
generated by

\{0i^{s_{i}}:s_{i}\in S_{i},\,i=1,...,d\}\cup\{i:i=d+1,...,d+r\},

which is a slight modification of the generalized
S
-gap shift discussed above. Application of the Pavlov’s result
[
25
, Theorem 1.3]
tells us that

(11)

\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}c(n)e^{-nh_{\rm top}(Y)}=1.

Note that the residual set
Y_{\rm res}
consists of bi-infinite strings ending or beginning with an infinite
string of a single symbol
i\in\{1,...,d\}
. Any ergodic shift-invariant measure
\mu
with
\mu(Y_{\rm res})\neq 0
is supported on the periodic points of the form
(...iii...)
. This can be shown by a standard argument of partitioning the set of points ending with a string of
i
into subsets
Y_{N}=\big\{y\in Y:y[N,\infty)=0iii...\text{ for some }i\in\{1,...,d\}\big\}
. Since the sets
Y_{N}
are disjoint and each
Y_{N}=\sigma^{-N}(Y_{0})
, we see that
\mu(Y_{N})=0
. Similarly, the set of points beginning with a string of
i
must have
\mu
-measure zero as well. Therefore, for any invariant probability measure
\mu
on
Y
with
h_{\sigma}(\mu)>0
we have
\mu(Y_{\rm con})=1
.

Next we show that the topological entropy of
Y
equals the concatenation entropy of
X
by establishing an isomorphism between
X_{\rm con}
and
Y_{\rm con}
. We enumerate the words in
{\mathcal{G}}(n)
so that
{\mathcal{G}}(n)=\{g_{n,1},...,g_{n,c(n)}\}
and write
\tau(g_{n,i})=i
for
n>1
and
\tau(g_{1,i})=d+i
. Hence, for a generating word
g
its index in the set
{\mathcal{G}}(|g|)
is given by
\tau(g)
. To define the map
T:X_{\rm con}\to Y_{\rm con}
we represent each element of
X_{\rm con}
as the concatenation of the words from the generator and then replace each generating word of length 1 by
d+i

and each generating word of length greater than 1 by a block of the form
0i^{(n-1)}
, where
n
is the length of the word and
i
is its index in
{\mathcal{G}}(n)
. Precisely, for every
x\in X_{\rm con}
there is a sequence of integers
(k_{j})_{j\in{\mathbb{Z}}}
such that
x[k_{j},k_{j+1})\in{\mathcal{G}}
for all
j
. Let

T(x)=y
be so, that for
j\in{\mathbb{Z}}

y[k_{j},k_{j+1})=\begin{cases}\tau\big(x[k_{j},k_{j+1})\big),&\mbox{if $k_{j+1}-k_{j}=1$}\\
0\tau\big(x[k_{j},k_{j+1})\big)^{k_{j+1}-k_{j}-1},&\mbox{otherwise}.\end{cases}

Since
{\mathcal{G}}
uniquely represents
X_{{\rm con}}
, the sequence
(k_{j})
is unambiguously determined by
x\in X_{\rm con}
and the map
T
is well defined. It follows that
T
is one-to-one from the definition of the index map
\tau
and that
T
is onto from the construction of the gap-sets
S_{i}
. Moreover,
T(\sigma x)=\sigma T(x)
. Let
\mu_{Y}
be a measure of maximal entropy of
Y
and consider the pull-back measure
\mu_{Y}\circ T
. Then
\mu_{Y}(Y_{\rm con})=1

and the systems
(X,\sigma,\mu_{Y}\circ T))
and
(Y,\sigma,\mu_{Y})
are measure-theoretically isomorphic, hence the entropy is preserved. It follows from the fact that
\mu_{Y}\circ T(X_{\rm con})=1
that the concatenation entropy of
X
is at least the topological entropy of
Y
.
On the other hand, if there is an invariant measure
\nu
on
X
satisfying
\nu(X_{\rm con})=1
and
h_{\sigma}(\nu)>h_{\rm top}(Y)
then its push-forward
\nu\circ T^{-1}
would be an invariant measure on
Y
with entropy exceeding
h_{\rm top}(Y)
, which is a contradiction. We conclude that
h_{\rm con}(X)=h_{\rm top}(Y)
.

We now apply formula (
11
) for the entropy of
Y
and conclude that
h_{\rm con}(X)=\log\lambda
where
\lambda
must satisfy

\sum_{g\in{\mathcal{G}}}\lambda^{-|g|}=1.

∎

Suppose a coded shift
X
satisfies the assumptions of Lemma
1
and has an infinite generating set
{\mathcal{G}}
. We enumerate the elements of
{\mathcal{G}}
, so that
{\mathcal{G}}=\{g_{i}:i\in{\mathbb{N}}\}
and consider coded shifts
X_{m}
which are generated by
\{g_{1},...,g_{m}\}
. Since no elements have to be added in the closure of the concatenation part of
X_{m}
these shifts are often easier to control than
X
. It follows from the previous lemma that the topological entropy of
X_{m}
and the concatenation entropy of
X
are given by
\log\lambda_{m}
and
\log\lambda_{\ast}
respectively, where
\lambda_{m}
and
\lambda_{\ast}
satisfy

\sum_{i=1}^{m}\lambda_{m}^{-|g_{i}|}=1\text{ and }\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\lambda_{\ast}^{-|g_{i}|}=1.

We conclude that
(\lambda_{m})_{m}
is an increasing sequence with
\lim_{m\to\infty}\lambda_{m}=\lambda_{\ast}.
Thus, we obtain the following:

Corollary 1
.

Let
X
be a coded shift with generating set
{\mathcal{G}}=\{g_{i}:i\in{\mathbb{N}}\}
which uniquely represents
X_{\rm con}
and has a bounded rate of growth. Then

\lim\limits_{m\to\infty}h_{\rm top}(X_{m})=h_{\rm con}(X),

where
X_{m}
are the coded shifts generated by
\{g_{1},...,g_{m}\}
.

We are now ready to prove Theorem
1
.

Proof of Theorem
1
.

We fix a word
a
which is not in
{\mathcal{L}}(Z)
, but every proper subword of
a
is in
{\mathcal{L}}(Z)
. Such a word exists and can be chosen to have length at least two since
Z
is not a full shift. Denote by
s
the first symbol of
a
and let
\tilde{a}
be such that
s\tilde{a}=a
. Since
Z
is irreducible, for each
n
we can find a word
w_{n}\in{\mathcal{L}}(Z)
which contains all words in
{\mathcal{L}}_{n}(Z)
as subwords. Furthermore, we inductively select a sequence of words
(g_{n})_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}
in
{\mathcal{L}}(Z)
with the following properties:

(i)

g_{n}
starts with
\tilde{a}
and ends with
s
;

(ii)

g_{n}
contains
w_{n}
as a subword;

(iii)

|g_{n+1}|>|g_{n}|
.

We let
n_{k}=k\lceil\frac{1}{\epsilon}\rceil
, where
\lceil.\rceil
is the ceiling function, and define the generating set
{\mathcal{G}}=\{g_{n_{k}}:k\in{\mathbb{N}}\}
. We note that every generator
g_{n_{k}}
starts with the word
\tilde{a}
and ends with the symbol
s
. Since
a=s\tilde{a}\notin{\mathcal{L}}(Z)
it follows that no bi-infinite concatenation of generators in
{\mathcal{G}}
belongs to
Z
. Hence,
X_{\rm con}({\mathcal{G}})\cap Z=\emptyset
. On the other hand, for any
z\in Z
and any
i\in{\mathbb{N}}
,
z[-i,i]
is a subword of
g_{n_{k}}
whenever
n_{k}>2i
and hence
z\in X({\mathcal{G}})
.
It follows that
Z\subset X_{\rm res}({\mathcal{G}})
. In fact,
Z\subset X_{\rm lim}({\mathcal{G}})
, where
X_{\rm lim}({\mathcal{G}})
is defined in (
8
). Since
{\mathcal{G}}\subset{\mathcal{L}}(Z)
the opposite inclusion holds as well, so that
Z=X_{\lim}
. Recall that for any
\mu\in\mathcal{M}_{\sigma}(X)
we have
\mu(X_{{\rm con}}\cup X_{\lim})=1
. If
\mu(X_{{\rm res}})=1
then
\mu(Z)=\mu(X_{\lim})=1
.

It follows from the construction that
{\mathcal{G}}
uniquely represents
X_{{\rm con}}
since the appearances of the word
a
within
x\in X_{\rm con}
determine unambiguously the partition of
x
into a sequence of words from
{\mathcal{G}}
.
To see this we observe that dependent on the structure of the word
a
it is possible that there is a word
v
in
x
which is given by finitely many overlapping copies of
a
. However, by construction of the generators
g_{n}
the transition between generators must in this case occur in the copy of
a
on the right-hand side of the word
v
.
Hence we have established the assertions (i) and (ii).

To verify (iii) we note that the generating words of
X=X({\mathcal{G}})
have different lengths we can apply Lemma
1
with
d=1
. We obtain that
h_{\rm con}(X)=\log\lambda_{*}
where
\lambda_{*}
satisfies the equation

\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\lambda^{-|g_{n_{k}}|}=1.

To complete the proof we show that any number
\lambda
for which
\log\lambda\geq\epsilon
cannot be a solution to the above equation. Recall from our construction that
g_{n_{k}}
includes a subword
w_{n_{k}}
and hence
|g_{n_{k}}|>n_{k}\geq k/\epsilon
by the choice of the sequence
(n_{k})
.
Then
\log\lambda\geq\epsilon
implies that
\lambda^{-|g_{n_{k}}|}<e^{-k}
, so that
\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\lambda^{-|g_{i}|}<1.
We conclude that
h_{\rm con}(X)=\log\lambda_{*}<\epsilon
, which completes the proof of the theorem.
∎

We now present a local structure result for the concatenation and residual entropy of coded shift spaces.

Theorem 2
.

Let
X=X({\mathcal{G}})
be a coded shift such that

{\mathcal{G}}
uniquely represents
X_{{\rm con}}
and
h_{{\rm con}}(X)>h_{{\rm res}}(X)
.
Then for every
\epsilon>0
there exists a generating set
\widetilde{{\mathcal{G}}}={\mathcal{G}}\cup\{f_{i}:i\in{\mathbb{N}}\}
, where the
f_{i}
’s are finite words with
|f_{i}|<|f_{i+1}|
for
i\geq 1
, such that the coded shift
\widetilde{X}=X(\widetilde{\mathcal{G}})
has the following properties:

(i)

\widetilde{\mathcal{G}}
uniquely represents
\widetilde{X}_{{\rm con}}
;

(ii)

h_{{\rm res}}(X)=h_{{\rm res}}(\widetilde{X})
;

(iii)

h_{\rm top}(X)<h(\widetilde{X}_{\rm con})=h_{{\rm top}}(\widetilde{X})<h_{{\rm top}}(X)+\epsilon
;

(iv)

\widetilde{X}\subset\widetilde{X}_{\lim}
.

Moreover, it can be arranged for any given sequence
(m_{i})_{i\in{\mathbb{N}}}
of positive integers that
|f_{i}|\geq m_{i}
.

Proof.

Let
{\mathcal{G}}=\{g_{i}:i\in{\mathbb{N}}\}
be an enumeration of
{\mathcal{G}}
. We write
{\mathcal{G}}(m)=\{g_{1},\dots,g_{m}\}
. Let
X_{m}=X({\mathcal{G}}(m))
. Consider

f_{m}(\lambda)=\sum_{i=1}^{m}\lambda^{-|g_{i}|},

which are strictly positive, decreasing and analytic functions on
(1,\infty)

\lim_{\lambda\to\infty}f_{m}(\lambda)=0
.
Let
\lambda_{m}
be the unique solution of
f_{m}(\lambda)=1
and let
\lambda_{*}=\lim_{m\to\infty}\lambda_{m}
. Clearly
{\mathcal{G}}(m)
has a bounded rate of growth
and thus Lemma
1
implies
h_{\rm top}(X_{m})=\log\lambda_{m}
. Moreover, since
h_{\rm con}(X)>h_{\rm res}(X)
we may apply
[
6

and obtain

(12)

\lim_{m\to\infty}h_{\rm top}(X_{m})=\lim_{m\to\infty}\log\lambda_{m}=\log\lambda_{\ast}=h_{\rm top}(X).

Let
\epsilon>0
be given. We note that
(f_{m}^{\prime})_{m}
is a sequence of strictly negative continuous functions on
(1,\infty)
which is decreasing in

. Hence, there exists

such that
f_{m}^{\prime}(\lambda)<-c
for all
\lambda\in[\lambda_{\ast},\lambda_{\ast}+1]
and all

.
Putting these facts together shows that for every
\delta>0
there exists

such that

f_{m}(\lambda_{*}+\delta)<1-\tau
holds for all
m\in{\mathbb{N}}
.
Let
\{m_{i}\}_{i\in{\mathbb{N}}}
be a strictly increasing sequence of positive integers. By making the

larger if necessary we can assure that
\sum^{\infty}_{i=1}(\lambda_{*}+\delta)^{-m_{i}}<\tau
.
Our goal is to define
\widetilde{{\mathcal{G}}}={\mathcal{G}}\cup\{f_{i}\}
with
|f_{i}|=m_{i}
.
The additional generators
f_{i}
will be defined below.
Let
\widetilde{{\mathcal{G}}}=\{\widetilde{g}_{i}:i\in{\mathbb{N}}\}
be an enumeration of
\widetilde{{\mathcal{G}}}
and let

\widetilde{f}_{m},\widetilde{X}_{m},\widetilde{\lambda}_{m}
and
\widetilde{\lambda}_{\ast}
be defined analogously as for
X
. It follows from an elementary calculus argument that
\widetilde{f}_{m}({\lambda_{\ast}}+\delta)\leq 1
for all
m\in{\mathbb{N}}
.
Hence
\widetilde{\lambda}_{\ast}\leq\lambda_{\ast}+\delta
. By making
\delta
smaller if necessary and by further increasing the integers
m_{i}
, we can assure that

(13)

\lim_{m\to\infty}\log(\widetilde{\lambda}_{m})=\log(\widetilde{\lambda}_{\ast})<\log(\lambda_{\ast})+\epsilon.

Next we construct the additional generators
f_{i}
.
Let
u,v
be distinct words with
|u|=|v|
which do not appear in the language of

. Moreover, we assume that the periodic points

and

.

Next we inductively define a sequence of words

and generators

f_{i}=u^{2^{i}|w(i)|}w(i)v^{2^{i}|w(i)|}.

Fix

and
f_{1}
by (

{\mathcal{G}}_{i}={\mathcal{G}}\cup\{f_{1},\dots,f_{i-1}\}

to be a finite concatenation

{\mathcal{G}}_{i}

in

Moreover, we assume that the first and the last generator in

{\mathcal{G}}
.
We also assume that
w(i)

4.
Topological Structure Arising from Coding

Let
X
be a coded shift space with a generating set
{\mathcal{G}}
. For
g\in{\mathcal{G}}
we define

\displaystyle[g]

\displaystyle=\{x\in X:x=\cdots x_{-1}.x_{0}x_{1}\cdots\text{ with }x_{i}\in{\mathcal{A}}\text{ and }x_{0}x_{1}...x_{|g|-1}=g\};

\displaystyle\llbracket g\rrbracket

\displaystyle=\{x\in X:x=\cdots g_{-2}g_{-1}.g_{0}g_{1}g_{2}\cdots\text{ with }g_{i}\in{\mathcal{G}}\text{ and }g_{0}=g\}.

Hence,
[g]
is the standard cylinder set of the word
g
in
X
, whereas
\llbracket g\rrbracket
is a set of all points in
X_{\rm con}
which have
a representation as a concatenation of the elements of the generating set in which

appears starting at the zeroth coordinate. Note that
\llbracket g\rrbracket
depends on a particular choice of the generating set
{\mathcal{G}}
for

, therefore we call the sets of this type
{\mathcal{G}}
-cylinders. Assume now that
{\mathcal{G}}
uniquely represents

. We define

(17)

It follows from (
17

that

(18)

X_{\rm con}=\bigcup_{n\in{\mathbb{Z}}}\sigma^{n}(E),

however the sets on the right-hand side of (
18
) are in general not disjoint.
The next lemma provides a decomposition of

into countably many disjoint sets.

Lemma 2
.

Let
X
be a coded shift with the generating set
{\mathcal{G}}
. If
{\mathcal{G}}
uniquely represents
X_{\rm con}
then

(19)

X_{\rm con}=\bigcup_{g\in{\mathcal{G}}}\bigcup^{|g|-1}_{k=0}\sigma^{k}\llbracket g\rrbracket,

and the sets in this decomposition are pairwise disjoint.

Proof.

Since
{\mathcal{G}}
uniquely represents the elements in
X_{{\rm con}}({\mathcal{G}})
the identity (
19
) follows from the definitions of
{\mathcal{G}}
-cylinders, the set
E
and
X_{\rm con}
. Moreover, we have

\llbracket g\rrbracket\cap\llbracket h\rrbracket=\emptyset
for
g\neq h
,
g,h\in{\mathcal{G}}
and in case

\sigma^{k}\llbracket g\rrbracket\cap\llbracket h\rrbracket=\emptyset
for
1\leq k\leq|g|-1
and any

.
This follows because
\sigma^{k}\llbracket g\rrbracket\cap E=\emptyset
for

and
\llbracket h\rrbracket\subset E

so that
\sigma^{k}\llbracket g\rrbracket\cap\llbracket h\rrbracket=\emptyset
.
The cases
\sigma^{k}\llbracket g\rrbracket\cap\sigma^{l}\llbracket h\rrbracket=\emptyset
when

and

(and

if

)
are reduced to the previous case by applying
\sigma^{-\min(k,l)}
.
∎

We need an alternative representation of the set

We fix an enumeration of the elements of the generating set
{\mathcal{G}}
, i.e.,
{\mathcal{G}}=\{g_{i}:i\in{\mathbb{N}}\}
. Then we can associate with

the full shift on a countable alphabet

(20)

\pi(y)=\cdots g_{y_{-2}}g_{y_{-1}}.g_{y_{0}}g_{y_{1}}g_{y_{2}}\cdots.

. This definition of

is consistent with (

is the set of all concatenations of words from
{\mathcal{G}}
, whose entry at zero is the first letter of a generator in

. We note that

is a continuous injection.
Further note that both

and

We now show that the concatenation set, the residual set,

-cylinders as well as the set

Lemma 3

Let
X
be a coded shift with generating set
{\mathcal{G}}=\{g_{i}:i\in{\mathbb{N}}\}

uniquely represents
X_{{\rm con}}
, and let

Recall that

is a Polish space and

is a continuous injection. Therefore,

It remains to consider the
{\mathcal{G}}
-cylinder

Define

is a Polish subspace of

Applying again the Lusin-Suslin Theorem yields that

∎

does not uniquely represent

allows us only to conclude that the sets

Since we do not need this result we leave the details to the reader.

Next we extend the notion of
{\mathcal{G}}
-cylinders to finite concatenations of generators. Given

Clearly,

\llbracket g_{0}\cdots g_{k}\rrbracket=\bigcap_{i=0}^{k}\sigma^{-l_{i}}\llbracket g_{i}\rrbracket,

where

. Thus, extended

-cylinders are Borel sets follows from Lemma

For

we define

Lemma 4
.

Suppose
{\mathcal{G}}
uniquely represents
X_{\rm con}
.
For any

the set

is a countable disjoint union of shifted extended
{\mathcal{G}}
-cylinders.

Proof.

If

, then

with

and there is a word, say

,
which contains

as a subword, so that

. There are only countably many such words,
and

is contained in the shifted extended
{\mathcal{G}}
-cylinder
\sigma^{l(x)}\llbracket g_{0}\cdots g_{k}\rrbracket
where
the shift
\sigma^{l(x)}
places

so that the subword

starts at position zero.
In fact, there may be several (but finitely many) possibilities that the word

. We conclude that there

{\mathcal{G}}
-cylinders satisfying

It is easy to see that each of the shifted extended

Recall that (shifted)

-cylinders and (shifted) extended

.
We now define a class of shift invariant measures which assign full measure to

. Namely,

Therefore, in order for the measure

5.
Uniqueness of Measures of Maximal Entropy

In this section we establish the uniqueness of measures of maximal entropy for a large class of coded shift spaces. Note that the existence follows from expansivity of the system. If the generating set consists of finitely many words then the corresponding coded shift is sofic. Since the properties of equilibrium states on sofic shifts are very well understood, for simplicity of exposition we assume in the following that our generating set is countably infinite. All our results remain valid when the generating set is finite.

Indeed,

(26)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\tilde{\mu}\circ\sigma^{-1}(A)&=\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\sum_{j=0}^{|g_{i}|-1}\mu(\llbracket g_{i}\rrbracket\cap\sigma^{-(j+1)}(A))\\
&=\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\sum_{j=1}^{|g_{i}|-1}\mu(\llbracket g_{i}\rrbracket\cap\sigma^{-j}(A))+\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\mu(\llbracket g_{i}\rrbracket\cap\sigma^{-|g_{i}|}(A))\end{split}

Since
\sigma^{|g_{i}|}=\sigma_{E}
on
\llbracket g_{i}\rrbracket
and
\mu
is
\sigma_{E}
-invariant we obtain that
\mu(\llbracket g_{i}\rrbracket\cap\sigma^{-|g_{i}|}(A))=\mu(\sigma^{|g_{i}|}\llbracket g_{i}\rrbracket\cap(A))
. Recall that the sets
\sigma^{|g_{i}|}\llbracket g_{i}\rrbracket
are pairwise disjoint, which gives a decomposition
E=\bigcup_{i=1}^{\infty}\sigma^{|g_{i}|}\llbracket g_{i}\rrbracket
. It follows that

\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\mu(\llbracket g_{i}\rrbracket\cap\sigma^{-|g_{i}|}(A))=\mu(E\cap A)=\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\mu(\llbracket g_{i}\rrbracket\cap A).

Replacing the last term of (
26
) by this expression and combining the sums we see that
\tilde{\mu}\circ\sigma^{-1}(A)=\tilde{\mu}(A)
and hence
\tilde{\mu}
is
\sigma
-invariant. When
\mu\in\mathcal{M}_{\rm ind}
the quantity
\tilde{\mu}(X)=\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}|g_{i}|\mu(\llbracket g_{i}\rrbracket)
is finite so that we can normalize
\tilde{\mu}
and obtain that
{\mathbbm{l}}(\mu)=\frac{\tilde{\mu}}{\tilde{\mu}(X)}\in\mathcal{M}_{\rm con}
.

On the other hand, consider any
\nu\in\mathcal{M}_{\rm con}
and let
\mu={\mathbbm{i}}(\nu)
. Then by (
22
)

\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}|g_{i}|\mu(\llbracket g_{i}\rrbracket)=\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}|g_{i}|\frac{\nu(\llbracket g_{i}\rrbracket)}{\nu(E)}=\frac{1}{\nu(E)}\nu(X)=\frac{1}{\nu(E)}.

Using Lemma
2
and
\sigma
-invariance of
\nu
we see that

\nu(A)=\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\sum_{j=0}^{|g_{i}|-1}\mu(\sigma^{j}\llbracket g_{i}\rrbracket\cap A)=\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\sum_{j=0}^{|g_{i}|-1}\mu(\llbracket g_{i}\rrbracket\cap\sigma^{-j}(A))

for any measurable
A\subset X
. Substituting both expressions into the formula for the lift map
{\mathbbm{l}}
we obtain

{\mathbbm{l}}\circ{\mathbbm{i}}(\nu)(A)=\nu(E)\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\sum_{j=0}^{|g_{i}|-1}\frac{\nu(\llbracket g_{i}\rrbracket\cap\sigma^{-j}(A))}{\nu(E)}=\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\sum_{j=0}^{|g_{i}|-1}\mu(\sigma^{j}\llbracket g_{i}\rrbracket\cap A)=\nu(A).

∎

To deduce the existence and uniqueness of the measures which maximize the concatenation entropy we utilize the relationship (
23
) between the systems
(E,\sigma_{E})
and
(Y,\sigma)
and the ergodic theory for shifts with
countable alphabet developed in
[
23
,
27
]
. In
[
23
]
Mauldin and Urbanski proved that a Hölder potential
\psi
defined on a one-sided shift with countable alphabet
{\mathbb{N}}^{\mathbb{N}}
has a unique equilibrium state (which is also Gibbs) whenever

(27)

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\exp(\sup\psi|_{[i]})<\infty

and

(28)

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\inf(-\psi|_{[i]})\exp(\inf\psi|_{[i]})<\infty

hold, where
[i]
denotes the one-sided cylinder of
i\in{\mathbb{N}}
, i.e.
[i]
is the set of all elements of
{\mathbb{N}}^{{\mathbb{N}}}
with first coordinate
i
.
Here we say (following
[
23
]
) that a potential
\psi
on a shift with a countable alphabet is Hölder if
there are constants
C
and
\alpha
such that for any
y^{\prime}
and
y^{\prime\prime}
with
y_{0}^{\prime}=y_{0}^{\prime\prime}
we have

|\psi(y^{\prime})-\psi(y^{\prime\prime})|\leq Cd(y^{\prime},y^{\prime\prime})^{\alpha}.

Here
d(.,.)
is the standard metric on
X
defined in (
4
).
Note that the inequality must hold only when
y^{\prime}_{0}=y^{\prime\prime}_{0}
, which differs from the case of a finite alphabet. Although our shift
Y
is two-sided, our potential
\psi
will not depend on the backward history (it is constant on cylinders of length one) and hence the results from
[
23
]
are applicable in our settings.

To estimate the convergence of the above series we use the representation of the concatenation set of a coded shift as an edge shift for a connected countable directed graph
\Gamma
. The nature of this representation as well as the way to adapt relevant results from graph theory to our settings is explained in
[
25
]
(also see
[
26
]
). We do not repeat it here, but make use of the conclusions. Suppose
\Gamma
is a countable labeled directed graph associated to the coded shift
X({\mathcal{G}})
and
h_{G}(\Gamma)
is its Gurevich entropy. Then we always have
h({\mathcal{G}})\leq h_{G}(\Gamma)\leq h_{\rm top}(X)
and

(29)

\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}c(n)e^{-nh_{G}(\Gamma)}\leq 1,

where
c(n)={\rm card}\{g_{i}\in{\mathcal{G}}:|g_{i}|=n\}
and
h({\mathcal{G}})=\limsup\frac{1}{n}\log c(n)
as before.
Gurevich proved in
[
16
]
that
the existence of a Borel probability measure on the edge shift for
\Gamma
with entropy
h_{G}(\Gamma)
is equivalent to

\Gamma
being positive recurrent. According to the Vere-Jones classification
[
30
]
for connected countable directed graphs this means (in our notation) that

(30)

\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}c(n)e^{-nh_{G}(\Gamma)}=1\quad{\rm and}\quad\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}nc(n)e^{-nh_{G}(\Gamma)}<\infty.

Lemma 6
.

Let
X=X({\mathcal{G}})
be a coded shift such that
{\mathcal{G}}=\{g_{i}:i\in{\mathbb{N}}\}
uniquely represents
X_{{\rm con}}
and
h_{{\rm con}}(X)>h_{{\rm res}}(X)
. Then

\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}e^{-|g_{i}|h_{\rm top}(X)}=1\quad{\rm and}\quad\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}|g_{i}|e^{-|g_{i}|h_{\rm top}(X)}<\infty.

Proof.

Consider
f(\lambda)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}c(n)\lambda^{n}
with
c(n)={\rm card}\{g_{i}\in{\mathcal{G}}:|g_{i}|=n\}
. Note that the radius of convergence of this power series is
e^{-h({\mathcal{G}})}
and hence
f
is analytic on the interval
(-e^{-h({\mathcal{G}})},e^{-h({\mathcal{G}})})
as is its derivative
f^{\prime}(\lambda)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}nc(n)\lambda^{n-1}
.

If
h({\mathcal{G}})<h_{\rm top}(X)
then
\lambda_{\ast}=e^{-h_{\rm top}(X)}\in(-e^{-h({\mathcal{G}}))},e^{-h({\mathcal{G}}))})
and the series
\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}nc(n)e^{-nh_{\rm top}(X)}
converges. Now consider coded shifts
X_{m}
generated by
\{g\in{\mathcal{G}}:|g|\leq m\}
. It was proven in
[
6
, Proposition 28]
that whenever
h_{\rm con}(X)>h_{\rm res}(X)
we have
\lim_{m\to\infty}h_{\rm top}(X_{m})=h_{\rm top}(X)
. Since the shifts
X_{m}
have finite generating sets their topological entropies satisfy

\sum_{n=1}^{m}c(n)e^{-nh_{\rm top}(X_{m})}=1.

Letting
\lambda_{m}=e^{-h_{\rm top}(X_{m})}
we see from the above that
\lambda_{m}\to\lambda_{\ast}
and the functions
f_{m}(\lambda)=\sum_{n=1}^{m}c(n)\lambda^{n}
converge uniformly to
f(\lambda)
in the neighborhood of
\lambda_{\ast}
. Since
f_{m}(\lambda_{m})=1
, we must have
f(\lambda_{\ast})=1
as well. By the definitions of
f(\lambda)
and
\lambda_{\ast}
this means that
\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}c(n)e^{-nh_{\rm top}(X)}=1
.
If
h({\mathcal{G}})=h_{\rm top}(X)
then we must necessarily have
h_{G}(\Gamma)=h_{\rm top}(X)
. By our assumption
h_{\rm top}(X)=h_{\rm con}(X)
and hence at least one of the measures of maximal entropy on
X
assigns full measure to
X_{\rm con}
. Applying the correspondence between
X_{\rm con}
and the edge shift of
\Gamma
, we see that the assumptions of Gurevich’s theorem are satisfied. It now follows from (
30
) that
\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}c(n)e^{-nh_{\rm top}(X)}=1
and
\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}nc(n)e^{-nh_{\rm top}(X)}<\infty
.

To conclude the proof of the lemma we observe that
\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}e^{-|g_{i}|h_{\rm top}(X)}=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}c(n)e^{-nh_{\rm top}(X)}
and
\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}|g_{i}|e^{-|g_{i}|h_{\rm top}(X)}=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}nc(n)e^{-nh_{\rm top}(X)}
.
∎

Remark
.

As a consequence of Lemma
6
, (
29
), and (
30
) we obtain that when
h_{\rm con}(X)>h_{\rm res}(X)
then the topological entropy of
X
coincides with the Gurevich entropy of the countable labeled graph associated to
X
, i.e.

h_{\rm top}(X)=h_{G}(\Gamma).

We are now ready to prove Theorem B which we restate here for convenience.

Theorem 3
.

Let
X=X({\mathcal{G}})
be a coded shift such that
{\mathcal{G}}
uniquely represents
X_{{\rm con}}
and
h_{{\rm con}}(X)>h_{{\rm res}}(X)
.
Then
X
has a unique measure of maximal entropy
\mu_{\max}
, and
\mu_{\max}
is

{\mathcal{G}}
-Bernoulli with
p_{g}=\exp(-|g|h_{\rm top}(X))
and
c=\sum_{g\in{\mathcal{G}}}|g|\exp(-|g|h_{\rm top}(X))
.

Proof.

Consider the constant potential
\phi:X\rightarrow{\mathbb{R}}
given by
\phi(x)=-h_{\rm top}(X)
,
so that
P_{\rm top}(\phi)=0
. Let
\mu
be a measure of maximal entropy of
X
. Then
\mu
is an equilibrium state of
\phi
and, since
h_{{\rm con}}(X)>h_{{\rm res}}(X)
, we know that
\mu\in\mathcal{M}_{\rm con}
and
{\mathbbm{i}}(\mu)
is well defined. The corresponding induced potential
\phi_{E}:E\to{\mathbb{R}}
has the form

\phi_{E}(x)=\sum_{j=0}^{r_{E}(x)-1}\phi\circ\sigma^{j}(x).

Note that in our notation (
20
) the first return time
r_{E}(x)=|g_{y_{0}}|
where
y=\pi^{-1}(x)
. In particular, when
x\in\llbracket g_{i}\rrbracket
we have
r_{E}(x)=|g_{i}|
. Define the potential
\psi:Y\to{\mathbb{R}}
by
\psi=\phi_{E}\circ\pi
, Hence,
\psi(y)=-|g_{y_{0}}|h_{\rm top}(X)
. We first verify that if
\mu
is a measure of maximal entropy for
X
, then
\nu=(\pi^{-1})_{\ast}{\mathbbm{i}}(\mu)
is an equilibrium state for
\psi
. Since any invariant measure admits an ergodic decomposition we may assume that
\mu
is ergodic and hence assigns full measure to
X_{\rm con}
.
It immediately follows from Lemma
2
that

\int_{X}\phi\,d\mu=\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\sum_{j=0}^{|g_{i}|-1}\int_{\sigma^{j}\llbracket g_{i}\rrbracket}\phi\,d\mu=\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}\int_{\llbracket g_{i}\rrbracket}\sum_{j=0}^{|g_{i}|-1}\phi\circ\sigma^{j}\,d\mu=\mu(E)\int_{E}\phi_{E}\,d{\mathbbm{i}}(\mu).

Since by Abramov’s formula we also have
h_{\sigma}(\mu)=\mu(E)h_{\sigma_{E}}({\mathbbm{i}}(\mu))
, using the properties of
\pi
we obtain that

\displaystyle h_{\sigma}(\nu)+\int_{Y}\psi\,d\nu

\displaystyle=h_{\sigma_{E}}({\mathbbm{i}}(\mu))+\int_{E}\phi_{E}\,d{\mathbbm{i}}(\mu)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\mu(E)}\left[h_{\sigma}(\mu)+\int_{X}\phi\,d\mu\right]

\displaystyle=0.

Hence,
P_{\rm top}(\psi)\geq 0
. To show the opposite inequality we use the result from
[
23
]
that
P_{\rm top}(\psi)=\sup_{m\in{\mathbb{N}}}P_{\rm top}(\psi,Y_{m})
, where
P_{\rm top}(\psi,Y_{m})
is the topological pressure of
\psi
for the dynamical system
(Y_{m},\sigma)
with
Y_{m}=\{1,...,m\}^{\mathbb{Z}}
. Denote by
\nu_{m}
the unique equilibrium state of
\psi
on
Y_{m}
and let
\pi_{\ast}\nu_{m}
be its pushforward measure on
E
. Then

\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}|g_{i}|\pi_{\ast}\nu_{m}(\llbracket g_{i}\rrbracket)=\sum_{i=1}^{m}|g_{i}|\nu_{m}([i])<\infty,

which assures that
\pi_{\ast}(\nu_{m})\in\mathcal{M}_{\rm ind}
. By Lemma
5
the measure
\mu_{m}={\mathbbm{l}}(\pi_{\ast}\nu_{m})
is a
\sigma
-invariant probability measure on
X
for which
h_{\sigma_{E}}(\pi_{\ast}\nu_{m})=\frac{h_{\sigma}(\mu_{m})}{\mu_{m}(E)}
and
\pi_{\ast}\nu_{m}(\llbracket g_{i}\rrbracket)=\frac{\mu_{m}(\llbracket g_{i}\rrbracket)}{\mu_{m}(E)}
. Keeping in mind that
\psi(y)=-|g_{y_{0}}|h_{\rm top}(X)
and that
\pi_{\ast}
preserves the entropy of
\nu_{m}
we evaluate

\displaystyle P_{\rm top}(\psi|_{Y_{m}})

\displaystyle=h_{\sigma}(\nu_{m})+\int_{Y}\psi\,d\nu_{m}

\displaystyle=h_{\sigma}(\nu_{m})-h_{\rm top}(X)\int_{Y}|g_{y_{0}}|\,d\nu_{m}

\displaystyle=h_{\sigma}(\nu_{m})-h_{\rm top}(X)\sum_{i=1}^{m}|g_{i}|\nu_{m}([i])

\displaystyle=h_{\sigma_{E}}(\pi_{\ast}\nu_{m})-h_{\rm top}(X)\sum_{i=1}^{m}|g_{i}|\pi_{\ast}\nu_{m}(\llbracket g_{i}\rrbracket)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\mu_{m}(E)}\big(h_{\sigma}(\mu_{m})-h_{\rm top}(X)\big),

where in the last line we use the fact that
\sum_{i=1}^{m}|g_{i}|\mu_{m}(\llbracket g_{i}\rrbracket)=\mu_{m}(X)=1
.
Since
\mu_{m}
is an invariant probability measure on
X
, the variational principle implies
h_{\sigma}(\mu_{m})-h_{\rm top}(X)\leq 0
. Taking the supremum over
m\in{\mathbb{N}}
we conclude that
P_{\rm top}(\psi)=0
.

To establish the uniqueness of the measure of maximal entropy it remains to be shown that for the potential
\psi(y)=-|g_{y_{0}}|h_{\rm top}(X)
the series (
27
) and (
28
) converge. Since
\psi(y)=-|g_{i}|h_{\rm top}(X)
for all
y\in[i]
, these series are

\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}e^{-|g_{i}|h_{\rm top}(X)}\quad{\rm and}\quad\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}|g_{i}|e^{-|g_{i}|h_{\rm top}(X)}.

The second sum clearly dominates the first. The proof that the second sum is finite is given in Lemma
6
. This shows that the measure of maximal entropy for
X
is unique and we denote it by
\mu_{\rm max}
.

Next we show that
\mu_{\rm max}
is
{\mathcal{G}}
-Bernoulli. Recall that
h_{\rm con}(X)>h_{\rm res}(X)
. Therefore, Lemma
6
asserts that
h_{\rm top}(X)=\log\lambda_{\ast}
where
\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\lambda_{\ast}^{-|g_{i}|}=1
. Hence,
\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\exp(-|g_{i}|h_{\rm top}(X))=1
and we can consider the Bernoulli measure
\nu
on
Y
which assigns the mass
p_{i}=\exp(-|g_{i}|h_{\rm top}(X))
to the cylinder
[i]
. It is straight forward to check that
\nu
is an equilibrium state for
\psi
, and hence, by uniqueness,
\mu_{\rm max}
coincides with the lift of
\pi_{\ast}\nu
. Applying the formula for the lift given in Lemma
5
we see that
\mu_{\rm max}(\llbracket g_{i_{1}}...g_{i_{k}}\rrbracket)=\frac{1}{c}p_{i_{1}}\cdots p_{i_{k}}
where
c=\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}|g_{i}|\pi_{\ast}\nu(\llbracket g_{i}\rrbracket)=\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}|g_{i}|p_{i}=\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}|g_{i}|\exp(-|g_{i}|h_{\rm top}(X))
.
∎

We remark that in the last part of the proof we actually show that conditions
\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}e^{-|g_{i}|h_{\rm top}(X)}=1
and
\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}|g_{i}|e^{-|g_{i}|h_{\rm top}(X)}<\infty
imply the existence of a measure of maximal entropy
\mu\in\mathcal{M}_{\rm con}
. Moreover,
\mu
is the only such measure in
\mathcal{M}_{\rm con}
and it is ergodic and
{\mathcal{G}}
-Bernoulli. The condition
h_{\rm con}(X)>h_{\rm res}(X)
is required to guarantee that there are no measures of maximal entropy which assign full measure to
X_{\rm res}
. We obtain

Corollary 2
.

Suppose
X
is a coded shift with generating set
{\mathcal{G}}
which uniquely represents
X_{\rm con}
. Then the following are equivalent.

(i)

There exists a measure of maximal entropy
\mu
with
\mu(X_{\rm con})=1
.

(ii)

\sum_{g\in{\mathcal{G}}}e^{-|g|h_{\rm top}(X)}=1
and
\sum_{g\in{\mathcal{G}}}|g|e^{-|g|h_{\rm top}(X)}<\infty

In this case
\mu
is unique in
\mathcal{M}_{\rm con}
, and is
{\mathcal{G}}
-Bernoulli with
p_{g}=e^{-|g|h_{\rm top}(X)}
and
c=\sum_{g\in{\mathcal{G}}}|g|e^{-|g|h_{\rm top}(X)}
.

This corollary supplies a characterization of the measure of maximal entropy from
\mathcal{M}_{\rm con}
in the case when
h_{\rm con}(X)=h_{\rm res}(X)
. Moreover, it could be used even in the case
h_{\rm con}(X)<h_{\rm res}(X)
by employing another generating set specifically tailored to a particular measure of maximal entropy. We illustrate this technique on the Dyck shift in the Example
4
.

Assume that
X=X({\mathcal{G}})
is uniquely represented and
h_{{\rm con}}(X)>h_{{\rm res}}(X)
. We have shown
that
X
has a
unique
measure of maximal entropy
\mu_{\max}
. We now provide an
example which shows that this measure is, in general not a Gibbs measure.

Example 1
.

There exists an intrinsically ergodic coded shift
X({\mathcal{G}})
whose measure of maximal entropy is
{\mathcal{G}}
-Bernoulli but not Gibbs.

Proof.

We start with a strictly increasing sequence of positive integers
\{n_{i}\}_{i\in{\mathbb{N}}}
, so that

\lim_{i\to\infty}(n_{i+1}-n_{i})=\infty
. Now let
{\mathcal{G}}=\{g_{i}:\ g_{i}=0^{n_{i}}1^{n_{i}}\}
and consider
X=X({\mathcal{G}})
.
Clearly
{\mathcal{G}}
uniquely represents
X_{{\rm con}}
and

0=h_{{\rm res}}(X)<h_{{\rm con}}(X)=h_{{\rm top}}(X)<\log 2.

We know from Theorem
3
that
X
has a unique measure of maximal entropy
\mu_{\max}
which is
{\mathcal{G}}
-Bernoulli and consequently

\mu_{\max}\left(\llbracket g_{i}\rrbracket\right)=\frac{1}{c}\exp(-|g_{i}|h_{{\rm top}}(X))=\frac{1}{c}\exp(-2n_{i}h_{{\rm top}}(X)),

with a normalizing constant
c
. Now for
i\in{\mathbb{N}}
consider the words
w_{i}=0^{n_{i}+1}1^{n_{i}+1}
,
which are subwords of every
g_{j}
for all
j>i
. Moreover, there is a unique
0\leq k_{j}<|g_{j}|
such that
[w_{i}]\cap\sigma^{k_{j}}\llbracket g_{j}\rrbracket\neq\emptyset
. Since
\mu_{\rm max}
assigns full measure to
X_{\rm con}
by Lemma
2
we obtain

\displaystyle\frac{\mu_{\max}\left([w_{i}]\right)}{\exp(-2(n_{i}+1)h_{{\rm top}}(X))}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\exp(-2(n_{i}+1)h_{{\rm top}}(X))}\sum_{j>i}\mu_{\max}\left(\llbracket g_{j}\rrbracket\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{c}\exp(2(n_{i}+1)h_{{\rm top}}(X))\sum_{j>i}\exp(-2n_{j}h_{{\rm top}}(X))

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{c}\sum_{j>i}\exp(-2(n_{j}-n_{i}-1)h_{{\rm top}}(X))

Since by assumption
\{n_{i}\}
is monotonically increasing with
(n_{i+1}-n_{i})\rightarrow\infty
as
i\rightarrow\infty
, the last sum can be made as small as we wish. This violates the lower bound needed for the Gibbs property, hence

\mu_{\max}
is not Gibbs.
∎

The following example example establishes the existence of a coded shift
X=X({\mathcal{G}})
which has multiple measures of maximal entropy and satisfies
h_{{\rm con}}(X)=h_{{\rm res}}(X)
.
A similar example (with fewer properties) was given in

[
25
]
[Example 5.3]

Example 2
.

There exists a coded shift
X=X({\mathcal{G}})
such that
{\mathcal{G}}
uniquely represents
X_{\rm con}
,
h_{{\rm con}}(X)=h_{{\rm res}}(X)
, and
X
has precisely three ergodic measures of maximal entropy. Two of these ergodic measures of maximal entropy are supported on disjoint proper subshifts
Z_{1},Z_{2}\subset X
. The third ergodic measure of maximal entropy is a
{\mathcal{G}}
-Bernoulli measure on
X_{\rm con}
.

Proof.

Let
{\mathcal{A}}=\{0,1,2,3,4\}.
We will construct a coded shift
X
over the alphabet
{\mathcal{A}}
which has the desired properties.
For
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
we define

W(n)=\{vw4^{n}:\ v\in\{0,1\}^{n},w\in\{2,3\}^{n}\}\subset{\mathcal{A}}^{3n}.

Further, we define
{\mathcal{G}}=\bigcup_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}W(n)
and
W_{n}=\bigcup^{n}_{k=1}W(k)
. Let
X=X({\mathcal{G}})
and
X_{n}=X(W_{n})
. Clearly,
{\mathcal{G}}
uniquely represents
X_{{\rm con}}
.

It follows from the definitions of
X_{\rm res}
and
X_{\rm lim}
that the disjoint shift spaces
Z_{1}=\{0,1\}^{{\mathbb{Z}}}
,
Z_{2}=\{2,3\}^{{\mathbb{Z}}}
, and
Z_{3}=\{4\}^{{\mathbb{Z}}}
are contained in
X_{\rm res}\cap X_{\lim}
. Let
x\in X_{\lim}\setminus(Z_{1}\cup Z_{2}\cup Z_{3})
. Then
x
must have precisely one
transition index between rays in
Z_{1},Z_{2}
or
Z_{2},Z_{3}
. More precisely, there is
i\in\{1,2\}
and
k\in{\mathbb{Z}}
such that
x=x(-\infty,k)x[k,\infty)=uv
, where
u
is a ray in
Z_{i}
and
v
is a ray in
Z_{i+1}
.
Let
\mu\in\mathcal{M}_{\sigma}(X)
be ergodic with
\mu(X_{{\rm res}})=1
.
It follows from standard arguments (partitioning into sets with the same transition index
k
and using
\sigma
-invariance) that
\mu(X_{\lim}\setminus(Z_{1}\cup Z_{2}\cup Z_{3}))=0
. Using that by
[
25
, Lemma 4.1]
every
invariant measure assigns full measure to
X_{{\rm con}}\cup X_{\lim}

we deduce that either
\mu(Z_{1})=1
or
\mu(Z_{2})=1
or
\mu(Z_{3})=1
holds.
We conclude that there are precisely two ergodic invariant entropy maximizing probability measures among all invariant probability measures which assign full measure to
X_{{\rm res}}
. These are the
(\frac{1}{2},\frac{1}{2})
-Bernoulli measures
\mu_{1}
and
\mu_{2}
on

Z_{1}
and
Z_{2}
, respectively. Both
\mu_{1}
and
\mu_{2}
have measure-theoretic entropy
\log 2
. This shows that
h_{{\rm res}}(X)=\log 2
.

Next we show that
h_{{\rm con}}(X)=\log 2
. It follows from Lemma
1
that
h_{{\rm top}}(X_{n})=\log\lambda_{n}
, where
\lambda_{n}
is the unique solution of the equation

\sum^{n}_{k=1}2^{2k}\lambda^{-3k}=1.

Moreover,
\lambda_{n}\uparrow\lambda_{*}
as
n\to\infty
, where
\lambda_{*}
is the unique solution of

(31)

\sum^{\infty}_{k=1}2^{2k}\lambda^{-3k}=1.

A simple calculation shows that
\lambda_{*}=2
. Hence,
\lim_{n\to\infty}h_{{\rm top}}(X_{n})=\log 2
which implies
h_{{\rm con}}(X)\geq\log 2
.
Assume that
h_{{\rm con}}(X)>\log 2
. Then
[
6
, Proposition 28]

leads to the following contradiction:

\log 2=\lim_{n\to\infty}h_{{\rm top}}(X_{n})=h_{{\rm con}}(X)>\log 2.

We conclude
h_{\rm top}(X)=h_{\rm con}(X)=h_{\rm res}(X)=\log 2
. In particular,
\mu_{1}
and
\mu_{2}
are the only ergodic measures of maximal entropy which assign full measure to
X_{\rm res}
.
It remains to consider the possibility of ergodic measures of maximal entropy which assign full measure to
X_{{\rm con}}
.
For all
g\in{\mathcal{G}}
we assign
p_{g}=e^{-|g|\log 2}
to the
{\mathcal{G}}
-cylinder
\llbracket g\rrbracket
.
We define a measure
\nu_{3}
on
E
by
\nu_{3}(\llbracket g_{0}\cdots g_{k}\rrbracket)=p_{g_{0}}\cdots p_{g_{k}}
for all
g_{0},\cdots,g_{k}\in{\mathcal{G}}
. Since
h_{\rm con}(X)=h_{\rm res}(X)
we can not directly apply Theorem B.
Instead we will show directly that the lift
\mu_{3}
of
\nu_{3}
, that is,
\mu_{3}={\mathbbm{l}}\nu_{3}
is an ergodic measure of maximal entropy.

We claim that
\nu_{3}
is an invariant probability measure on
E

which can be lifted to
X_{{\rm con}}
. Indeed, since
|W(n)|=2^{2n}
we obtain

\nu_{3}(E)=\sum_{g\in G}\nu_{3}(\llbracket g\rrbracket)=\sum_{g\in G}e^{-|g|\log 2}=\sum^{\infty}_{n=1}2^{2n}e^{-3n\log 2}=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}2^{-n}=1.

Using (
23
) we observe that
\nu_{3}
is measure-theoretic isomorphic to a Bernoulli measure on the two-sided countable alphabet full-shift
Y
. This shows that
\nu_{3}
is shift invariant. Next we show that
\nu_{3}
can be lifted to the measure
\mu_{3}

on
X
by showing that the normalizing constant
c
is finite. To see this we observe that the number of elements in
{\mathcal{G}}
with length
3n
is
2^{2n}
and conclude that

c=\sum_{g\in{\mathcal{G}}}|g|p_{g}=\sum^{\infty}_{n=1}3n\cdot 2^{2n}\cdot e^{-3n\log 2}=3\sum^{\infty}_{n=1}n2^{-n}=6.

Hence
\mu_{3}
is well-defined and
\mu_{3}(E)=c^{-1}=1/6
.
Next, we calculate the measure-theoretic entropy
h_{\sigma_{E}}(\nu_{3})
. By applying the fact that
\nu_{3}
is (up to conjugacy) a Bernoulli measure we obtain

\displaystyle h_{\sigma_{E}}(\nu_{3})

\displaystyle=-\sum_{g\in{\mathcal{G}}}\nu_{3}(\llbracket g\rrbracket)\log(\nu_{3}(\llbracket g\rrbracket)

\displaystyle=-\sum_{g\in{\mathcal{G}}}e^{-|g|\log 2}\log e^{-|g|\log 2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}2^{2n}2^{-3n}3n\log 2

\displaystyle=3\log 2\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}n2^{-n}=6\log 2.

Since
\mu_{3}(E)=\frac{1}{6}
Abramov’s formula yields

h_{\sigma}(\mu_{3})=\log 2
. We conclude that
\mu_{3}
is an ergodic measure of maximal entropy on
X
and is
{\mathcal{G}}
-Bernoulli. Having established the existence of a measure of maximal entropy that assigns full measure to
X_{\rm con}
its uniqueness follows from Corollary
2
. This completes the proof.
∎

If

\beta

is not an integer.

One method to define

\Gamma_{\beta}

Given

denote the integer part of

Example 3
.

Let
\beta>1
be a non-integral real number which is not eventually periodic. Let
\widehat{X}_{\beta}
denote the coded shift with generating set

which is associated with

\widehat{X}_{\beta}
has a unique measure of maximal

\mu_{\rm max}
which is
{\mathcal{G}}_{\beta}
-Bernoulli with
p_{g}=\beta^{-|g|}
and

c=\sum_{g\in{\mathcal{G}}_{\beta}}|g|\beta^{-|g|}
. Moreover,
\mu^{+}_{\rm max}=I(\mu_{\rm max})
is the unique measure of maximal entropy of the beta shift
X_{\beta}
.

Proof.

We already know that
\widehat{X}_{\beta}
and
X_{\beta}
have unique measures of maximal entropy
\mu_{\rm max}
and
\mu^{+}_{\rm max}
, respectively. We compute

\displaystyle\sum_{g\in{\mathcal{G}}_{\beta}}e^{-|g|h_{\rm top}(X_{\beta})}

\displaystyle=\sum_{g\in{\mathcal{G}}_{\beta}}e^{-|g|\log\beta}=

\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}b_{n}e^{-n\log\beta}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{b_{n}}{\beta^{n}}=1,

and

\displaystyle\sum_{g\in{\mathcal{G}}}|g|e^{-|g|h_{\rm top}(X)}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}nb_{n}e^{-n\log\beta}=

\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{nb_{n}}{\beta^{n}}

\displaystyle\leq\lfloor\beta\rfloor\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{n}{\beta^{n}}<\infty.

This shows that condition (ii) in Corollary

is the

-Bernoulli measure with

and

c=\sum_{g\in{\mathcal{G}}_{\beta}}|g|\beta^{-|g|}
.
∎

Corollary 3

6.
Uniqueness of Equilibrium States

In this section we establish the uniqueness of equilibrium states for hyperbolic Hölder continuous potentials. Let
(X,{\mathcal{G}})
be a coded shift such that
{\mathcal{G}}
uniquely represents
X_{\rm con}
. Recall that

h({\mathcal{G}})=\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\log c(n),

where
c(n)={\rm card}\{g\in{\mathcal{G}}:|g|=n\}.
We consider the case
h({\mathcal{G}})=0
.

As before,
\mathcal{M}_{\sigma}(X)
denotes the set of all shift-invariant Borel probability measures on
X
. The topological pressure of a continuous potential
\phi:X\to{\mathbb{R}}
satisfies the well-known variational principle, i.e.

(34)

P_{\rm top}(\phi)=\sup\left\{h_{\sigma}(\mu)+\int\phi\,d\mu:\mu\in\mathcal{M}_{\sigma}(X)\right\},

see, e.g.,
[
32
]
for details. We say that
\mu\in\mathcal{M}_{\sigma}(X)
is an equilibrium state of
\phi

if
h_{\sigma}(\mu)+\int\phi\,d\mu=P_{\rm top}(\phi)
. By expansivity of the shift map,

\phi
has at least one equilibrium state. Moreover, by compactness and convexity of the space of equilibrium states there exists at least one ergodic equilibrium state.
We say
\phi
is a
hyperbolic potential
if every equilibrium state of
\phi
has non-zero entropy.

We define the
concatenation pressure
of a potential
\phi
by

(35)

P_{\rm con}(\phi)=\sup\left\{h_{\sigma}(\mu)+\int\phi\,d\mu:\mu\in\mathcal{M}_{\sigma}(X)\text{ and }\mu(X_{\rm con})=1\right\},

and the
residual pressure
of
\phi
by

(36)

P_{\rm res}(\phi)=\sup\left\{h_{\sigma}(\mu)+\int\phi\,d\mu:\mu\in\mathcal{M}_{\sigma}(X)\text{ and }\mu(X_{\rm res})=1\right\}.

We continue to use the notation from Section
5
. In particular, we denote by
\sigma_{E}:E\to E
the induced map on
E
and by
{\mathbbm{i}}:\mathcal{M}_{\rm con}\to\mathcal{M}_{\rm ind}
the bijection with inverse
{\mathbbm{l}}:\mathcal{M}_{\rm ind}\to\mathcal{M}_{\rm con}
(see Lemma
5
). Moreover, we fix an enumeration
\{g_{i}\}_{i\in{\mathbb{N}}}
of
{\mathcal{G}}
to define the map
\pi:Y\to E
(see Equation (
23
)) which conjugates the countable alphabet two-sided full shift
(Y,\sigma)
with
\sigma_{E}
.

Our main objective in this section is to prove the following result.

Theorem 4
.

Let
X=X({\mathcal{G}})
be a coded shift such that
{\mathcal{G}}
uniquely represents
X_{{\rm con}}
and
h({\mathcal{G}})=0
. Let
\phi
be a hyperbolic Hölder continuous potential on
X

with

P_{{\rm con}}(\phi)>P_{{\rm res}}(\phi)
. Then
\phi
has a unique equilibrium state
\mu_{\phi}
. Moreover,
\mu_{\phi}
is the lift of the invariant Gibbs measure for the associated potential on the two-sided countable full shift.

The proof of Theorem
4
relies on similar techniques as the proof of Theorem
3
. Therefore, we primarily discuss the differences in the proof.

From now on we assume that
\phi:X\to{\mathbb{R}}
is a Hölder continuous potential. By replacing
\phi
with
\phi-P_{\rm top}(\phi)
and noting that
\phi
and
\phi-P_{\rm top}(\phi)
have the same equilibrium states we may assume without loss of generality
P_{\rm top}(\phi)=0
.
We start with some preliminary results.

Lemma 7
.

Let
\phi
be a hyperbolic potential with
P_{\rm top}(\phi)=0
. Then there exists
\tau>0
and
N\in{\mathbb{N}}
such that

S_{n}\phi(x)<-n\tau

for all
x\in X
and all
n\geq N
.

Proof.

Suppose the statement does not hold. Then there exists a sequence
\{x(m)\}\subset X
and a strictly increasing sequence

\{l_{m}\}\subset{\mathbb{N}}
such that

\frac{1}{l_{m}}S_{l_{m}}\phi(x(m))\geq-\frac{1}{m}.

for all
m\in{\mathbb{N}}
.
Let
\mu
be a weak
∗
accumulation point of the sequence of measures

\{\mu_{m}\}
given by

\mu_{m}=\frac{1}{l_{m}}\sum^{l_{m}-1}_{k=0}\delta_{\sigma^{k}(x(m))}

Then
\mu
is
\sigma
-invariant and
\int\phi\ d\mu\geq 0
.
It follows from the variational principle that
\mu
is an equilibrium state with zero entropy. This contradicts
the assumption that
\phi
is a hyperbolic potential.
∎

Given a continuous potential
\phi:X\to{\mathbb{R}}
, analogous as in the proof of Theorem
3
we define the potential
\phi_{E}:E\rightarrow{\mathbb{R}}
by

(37)

\phi_{E}(x)=\sum^{r_{E}(x)-1}_{j=0}\phi\circ\sigma^{j}(x),

where
x=\dots g_{-2}(x)g_{-1}(x).g_{0}(x)g_{1}(x)g_{2}(x)\dots
and
r_{E}(x)=|g_{0}(x)|
. Moreover, we define the induced potential
\psi:Y\to{\mathbb{R}}
by
\psi=\phi_{E}\circ\pi
.
Recall, that the
k
-variation
\phi:X\rightarrow{\mathbb{R}}

is given by

\text{Var}_{k}(\phi):=\sup\{|\phi(x)-\phi(y)|:(x_{-k+1},...,x_{k-1})=(y_{-k+1},...,y_{k-1})\}.

Following
[
5
]
, we define

{\mathcal{F}}_{X}:=\{\phi:X\rightarrow{\mathbb{R}}:\ \text{Var}_{k}(\phi)<c\alpha^{k}\text{ for some }c>0,\ \alpha\in(0,1),\text{ all }k\in{\mathbb{N}}\}.

We use the analogous notation for the potential spaces
{\mathcal{F}}_{Y}
and
{\mathcal{F}}_{Y^{+}}
.
As a direct consequence of the definition of Hölder continuity we obtain:

Lemma 8
.

If
\phi:X\rightarrow{\mathbb{R}}
is Hölder continuous then
\phi\in{\mathcal{F}}_{X}
.

The next Lemma shows that
\phi\in{\mathcal{F}}_{X}
implies
\psi\in{\mathcal{F}}_{Y}
.
The following result is standard and we leave the proof to the reader.

Lemma 9
.

Let
X=X({\mathcal{G}})
such that
{\mathcal{G}}
uniquely represents
X_{\rm con}
.
Then
\phi\in{\mathcal{F}}_{X}
implies
\psi=\phi_{E}\circ\pi\in{\mathcal{F}}_{Y}
.

We say that
\psi,\widetilde{\psi}\in{\mathcal{F}}_{Y}
are cohomologous within the class
{\mathcal{F}}_{Y}
if there exists
u\in{\mathcal{F}}_{Y}
such that
\psi(x)=\widetilde{\psi}(x)-u(x)+u(\sigma(x))
.

We note that the results for the uniqueness of equilibrium states for countable alphabets by Mauldin and Urbanski
[
23
]
, and Sarig
[
27
]
are formulated for one-sided shift spaces. The following result allows the transition
from the two-sided to the one-sided shift with a countable alphabet.

Lemma 10
.

Let

\psi\in{\mathcal{F}}_{Y}
. Then there exists
\widetilde{\psi}\in\mathcal{F}_{Y}
cohomologous to
\psi
within the class
{\mathcal{F}}_{Y}

such that
\widetilde{\psi}(x)=\widetilde{\psi}(y)
whenever
x_{k}=y_{k}
for all
k\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}
.

Proof.

The result was first stated by Sinai
[
28
]
for subshifts of finite type over finite alphabets. A complete proof of Sinai’s result is given by Bowen
[
5
, Lemma 1.6]
. Bowen’s proof generalizes to the countable alphabet case without changes.
We only include here those details of the proof that are used later on, see

[
5
, Lemma 1.6]
for the complete proof:

Given
x\in Y
define
r(x)
by
r(x)_{k}=x_{k}
for
k\geq 0
and
r(x)_{k}=x_{0}
for
k\leq 0
. The function
u
is defined by

(38)

u(x)=\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\left(\psi(\sigma^{j}(x))-\psi(\sigma^{j}(r(x)))\right).

It follows that
u\in{\mathcal{F}}_{Y}
.
Moreover,
\widetilde{\psi}=\psi-u+u\circ\sigma
has the desired properties.
This follows from
\widetilde{\psi}=\psi(r(x))+v(x)
, where

(39)

v(x)=\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\left(\psi(\sigma^{j+1}(r(x)))-\psi(\sigma^{j}(r(\sigma(x))))\right).

∎

We note that Y. Daon presents a proof of Lemma
10
for potentials in the Walters class rather than Hölder potentials
[
12
, Theorem 3.1]
.
We further note that functions in
{\mathcal{F}}_{Y}
are not necessarily bounded since
Y
has an infinite alphabet.
For example, the unbounded function
\phi(y)=y_{0}
belongs to
{\mathcal{F}}_{Y}
.
The next result shows that the function

defined in (

) is bounded.

Corollary 4
.

Let
\psi,\widetilde{\psi}
and
u
be as in the proof of Lemma
10
. Then

u
is bounded.

Proof.

For
x\in X
and
j\geq 0
we note that
\sigma^{j}(r(x))\in\sigma^{j}(x)[-j,j]
. Therefore, the result follows from Equation (
38
) and the fact that
\psi\in{\mathcal{F}}_{Y}
.
∎

Next we obtain a version of Lemma
7
for the countable alphabet case.

Corollary 5
.

Let
\phi:X\to{\mathbb{R}}
be a hyperbolic potential with
P_{\rm top}(\phi)=0
and let
\psi=\phi_{E}\circ\pi:Y\to{\mathbb{R}}
. Let
\widetilde{\psi}:Y\to{\mathbb{R}}
be as in Lemma

.
Then there exists
\tau>0
and
N\in{\mathbb{N}}
such that for all
x\in Y
with
n=n(x)=|g_{x_{0}}|\geq N
we have
\widetilde{\psi}(x)<-n\tau
.

Proof.

The result follows from
\psi=\phi_{E}\circ\pi
, Lemma
7
and Corollary
4
.
∎

We note that the constants
\tau
and
N
in Corollary
5
do in general differ from the corresponding constants in Lemma
7
.

Given
m\in{\mathbb{N}}
we define
Y_{m}=\{1,\dots,m\}^{\mathbb{Z}}
and consider
Y_{m}
as a subshift of
Y
.
We need another auxiliary result.

Lemma 11
.

Let
\psi\in{\mathcal{F}}_{Y}
. Then
P_{\rm top}(Y,\psi)=\sup_{m\in{\mathbb{N}}}P_{\rm top}(Y_{m},\psi|_{Y_{m}}).

Proof.

The result is proven in
[

for the one-sided countable alphabet case for acceptable potentials which is a weaker condition than belonging to
{\mathcal{F}}_{Y}
. The proof in the two-sided countable alphabet case is entirely analogous.

Next we describe how to transition from the two-sided countable alphabet full shift
(Y,\sigma)
to the one-sided countable alphabet full shift
(Y^{+},\sigma)
where
Y^{+}={\mathbb{N}}^{{\mathbb{N}}_{0}}
. There is a natural bijection
b:\mathcal{M}_{\sigma}(Y)\to\mathcal{M}_{\sigma}(Y^{+})
defined by
\nu^{+}=b(\nu)
where
\nu^{+}([w])=\nu([w])
for all
w\in{\mathbb{N}}^{n},n\in{\mathbb{N}}
. Clearly,
\nu^{+}
is well-defined since invariant measures are uniquely determined by their values on cylinders. Further, Equation (
5
) shows that

(40)

h_{\sigma}(\nu)=h_{\sigma}(\nu^{+}).

Let
\psi
be a continuous potential on
Y
with
\psi(x)=\psi(y)
whenever
x_{k}=y_{k}
for all
k\geq 0
. We define a continuous potential
\psi^{+}
on
Y^{+}
by

\psi^{+}(y^{+})=\psi(y)
where
y\in Y
with
y_{k}=y^{+}_{k}
for all
k\geq 0
. It follows directly from the definitions that

(41)

\int\psi\,d\mu=\int\psi^{+}\,d\mu^{+}.

Hence,

(42)

h_{\sigma}(\nu)+\int\psi\,d\nu=h_{\sigma}(\nu^{+})+\int\psi^{+}\,d\nu^{+}.

Let
\psi
be a continuous potential on
Y
with
P_{\rm top}(Y,\psi)<\infty
. Following
[
23
]
we say
\nu\in\mathcal{M}_{\sigma}(Y)
is an equilibrium state of

if
\int-\psi\,d\nu<\infty
and
P_{\rm top}(Y,\psi)=h_{\sigma}(\nu)+\int\psi d\,\nu.
Analogously, we define equilibrium states for continuous potentials on
Y^{+}
. We have the following:

Lemma 12
.

Let
\psi:Y\to{\mathbb{R}}
be a continuous potential with
P_{\rm top}(Y,\psi)<\infty
and
\psi(x)=\psi(y)
whenever
x_{k}=y_{k}
for all

. Then
P_{\rm top}(Y,\psi)=P_{\rm top}(Y^{+},\psi^{+})
.
Moreover,
\nu\in\mathcal{M}_{\sigma}(Y)
is an equilibrium state of
\psi
if and only if
\nu^{+}=b(\nu)
is an equilibrium state of
\psi^{+}
.

Proof.

The statements follow from the definition of the topological pressure and Equation (
42
).
∎

We note that Lemma

does not imply the existence of an equilibrium state of
\psi
and
\psi^{+}
, respectively. We are now ready to present the proof of Theorem

Proof of Theorem
4
.

Let
\phi:X\to{\mathbb{R}}
be a hyperbolic potential. Without loss of generality (by replacing
\phi
with
\phi-P_{\rm top}(\phi)
) we may assume
P_{\rm top}(\phi)=0
. Let
\psi=\phi_{E}\circ\pi
be the induced potential of

. It follows from Lemmas

Finally, we discuss the Dyck shift as an example where neither the results of Climenhaga
[

]

can be applied to establish the uniqueness of equilibrium states for any Hölder continuous potential. However, our techniques can still be used to characterize the ergodic measures of maximal entropy. We recall the simple description of this shift,
given in terms of parentheses and brackets. The alphabet of cardinality four is split into two
pairs of matching left and right symbols denoted by (, ), [, ]. The Dyck shift is the smallest shift space which contains the set of bi-infinite sequences where every opening parenthesis ( must be closed by ) and every opening bracket [ must be closed by ]. Krieger
[
20

showed that the Dyck shift has topological entropy
\log 3
and admits exactly two ergodic measures of maximal entropy, both are fully supported and Bernoulli. It is known that the Dyck shift is coded. We describe below its canonical generating set
{\mathcal{G}}
and compute the exact value of

. By using an alternative coding we are able to provide a simple expression for its measures of maximal entropy.

Example 4

The Dyck shift is a coded shift with the generating set
{\mathcal{G}}=\bigcup\limits_{n=1}^{\infty}W_{n}
where the sets of words

are recursively defined by

\displaystyle W_{1}

\displaystyle=\{(\,),[\,]\}

\displaystyle W_{n}

\displaystyle=\{(w),[w]:w\in(\cup_{i=1}^{n-1}W_{i})^{*}\text{ and }|w|=2(n-1)\}.

Moreover,
{\mathcal{G}}
uniquely represents

and

. Let
{\mathcal{G}}_{1}={\mathcal{G}}\cup\{(,[\,\}
and
{\mathcal{G}}_{2}={\mathcal{G}}\cup\{\,),]\}

are
{\mathcal{G}}_{1}
- respectively
{\mathcal{G}}_{2}
-Bernoulli with

Proof.

It was noted in
[
25
, Example 5.5]
that the canonical generator
{\mathcal{G}}
uniquely represents
X_{\rm con}
. Clearly, in this case
X_{\rm lim}({\mathcal{G}})=X({\mathcal{G}})
. The residual set consists of elements from
\left({\mathcal{G}}\cup\{(,[\,\}\right)^{*}
and
\left({\mathcal{G}}\cup\{\,),]\}\right)^{*}
where at least one bracket/parenthesis is present, as well as “symmetric” concatenations
x(-\infty,k)y[k,\infty)
where

,
y\in\left({\mathcal{G}}\cup\{),]\}\right)^{*}
and the order of open parentheses and brackets between the generating words in

moving to the left is the same as the order of corresponding closed ones in
y[k,\infty)
moving to the right.

Next, we compute the entropy of the generating set
{\mathcal{G}}
. The formula

|W_{n}|=\frac{2^{n-1}(2n)!}{(2n-1)(n!)^{2}}

is derived using standard methods, which we briefly outline below since we require some of the ingredients later on. Denote by
d_{n}
the cardinality of the set
W_{n}
. It follows from the definition of
W_{n}
that

d_{n+1}=2\sum_{k=1}^{n}\sum_{i_{1}+...+i_{k}=n}d_{i_{1}}d_{i_{2}}...d_{i_{k}}.

If
d_{i_{k}}=j
for some
j\in\{1,...,n\}
we can partition the remainder
(n-j)
into
k
parts where
k
is at most
n-j
. We obtain

d_{n+1}=2\sum_{j=1}^{n}d_{j}\sum_{k=1}^{n-j}\sum_{i_{1}+...+i_{k}=n-j}d_{i_{1}}d_{i_{2}}...d_{i_{k}}=\sum_{j=1}^{n}d_{j}d_{n-j+1}.

To solve the recursive relation above we use the generating function
F(x)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}d_{n}x^{n}
. We recall that
d_{1}=2
and compute

F(x)=2x+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}d_{n+1}x^{n+1}=2x+\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\sum_{n=j}^{\infty}d_{j}x^{j}d_{n-j+1}x^{n-j+1}=2x+F(x)^{2}.

We obtain
F(x)^{2}-F(x)+2x=0
, so that
F(x)=\frac{1-\sqrt{1-8x}}{2}
(we picked the negative sign since
F(0)=0
). We use the binomial formula

\sqrt{1-8x}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^{n+1}}{4^{n}(2n-1)}\binom{2n}{n}(-8x)^{n},

and get

F(x)=\frac{1}{2}\left(1-\sqrt{1-8x}\right)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{2^{n-1}}{2n-1}\binom{2n}{n}x^{n}.

Therefore,
d_{n}=\frac{2^{n-1}}{2n-1}\binom{2n}{n}=\frac{2^{n-1}(2n)!}{(2n-1)(n!)^{2}}
, as required.

Since the length of words in
W_{n}
is
2n
, to compute the entropy of the generating set we need to evaluate
\lim\limits_{n\to\infty}\frac{\log|W_{n}|}{2n}
. We apply Stirling’s formula

. Since
\log d_{n}\approx 3n\log 2-\log 2-\log(2n-1)+\log\sqrt{4\pi n}-2\log\sqrt{2\pi n}
,
we see that

\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{\log d_{n}}{2n}=\frac{3}{2}\log 2.

Now consider the generating set
{\mathcal{G}}_{1}={\mathcal{G}}\cup\{(,[\,\}

{\mathcal{G}}

by the property of a generating word

u_{i}=v_{1}
and the corresponding closed parenthesis is
v_{m}
which is not in

. Now suppose that there is

which can be represented as two different concatenations of elements from
{\mathcal{G}}_{1}

k_{i}\leq m_{j}<k_{j+1}<m_{j+1}

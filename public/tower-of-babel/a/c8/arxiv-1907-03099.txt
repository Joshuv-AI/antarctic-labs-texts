L ∞ L^{\infty} - estimates of the solution of the Navier-Stokes equation for non-decaying initial data
Santosh Pathak

Abstract
. In this paper, we derive the main result of a paper by H-O Kreiss and Jens Lorenz from a different approach than the method proposed in their paper. More precisely, we consider the Cauchy problem for the incompressible Navier-Stokes equations in ℝ n \mathbb{R}^{n} for n ≥ 3 n\geq 3 with non-decaying initial data and derive a priori estimates of the maximum norm of all derivatives of the solution in terms of the maximum norm of the initial data. This paper is also an extension of their paper to higher dimension.



1.
Introduction

We consider the Cauchy problem of the Navier-Stokes equations in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
for
n\geq 3
:

(1.1)

\displaystyle u_{t}+u\cdot\nabla u+\nabla p=\triangle u,\hskip 15.0pt\nabla\cdot u=0,

with initial condition

(1.2)

\displaystyle u(x,0)=f(x),\hskip 15.0ptx\in\mathbb{R}^{n},

where
u=u(x,t)=(u_{1}(x,t),\cdots u_{n}(x,t))
and
p=p(x,t)
stand for the unknown velocity vector field of the fluid and its pressure, while
f=f(x)=(f_{1}(x),\cdots f_{n}(x))
is the given initial velocity vector field. In what follows, we will use the same notations for the space of vector-valued and scalar functions for convenience in writing.

There is a large literature on the existence and uniqueness of solution of the Navier-Stokes equations in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. For given initial data solutions of (1.1) and (1.2) have been constructed in various function spaces. For example, if
f\in L^{r}
for some
r
with
3\leq r<\infty
, then it is well known that there is a unique classical solution in some maximum interval of time
0\leq t<T_{f}
where
0<T_{f}\leq\infty
. But for the uniqueness of the pressure one requires
|p(x,t)|\to 0
as
|x|\to\infty
. See
[
6
]
and
[
9
]
for
r=3
and
[
1
]
for
3<r<\infty
.

If
f\in L^{\infty}
then existence of a regular solution follows from
[
2
]
. The solution is only unique if one puts some growth restrictions on the pressure as
|x|\to\infty
. A simple example of non-uniqueness is demonstrated in
[
7
]
where the velocity
u
is bounded but
|p(x,t)|\leq C|x|
. In addition, an estimate
|p(x,t)|\leq C(1+|x|^{\sigma})
with
\sigma<1
( see
[
3
]
) imply uniqueness. Also, the assumption
p\in L^{1}_{loc}(0,T;BMO)
(see
[
4
]
) implies uniqueness.

In this paper we are interested in reproving the results of a paper by H-O Kreiss and J. Lorenz (see
[
8
]
) for the initial data
f\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
for
n\geq 3
using different approach than theirs in terms of dealing with the pressure term in the Navier-Stokes equations. The approach in this paper, to prove the principal result of the Kreiss and Lorenz paper, is more “functional analytic” approach in which the role of “the Leray projector” is being implemented to get rid of the pressure term from the Navier-Stokes equations. As a consequence of that, the details and techniques in obtaining some significantly complicated results related to the pressure part in the Kreiss and Lorenz paper are being avoided which makes this paper different and simpler in that sense. At the same time, this paper is also an extension of the work by Kreiss and Lorenz to the higher space dimension whereas such generalization, in the Kreiss and Lorenz paper by their approach, seems complicated because of the non-local nature of the pressure term in the Navier-Stokes equations. Since the main source of this paper is the Kreiss and Lorenz paper, it is appropriate to give some insight of their work in this paper as well. Before we start outlining some key aspects of their paper, we introduce the following notations and will be using them throughout this paper.

\displaystyle|f|_{\infty}=\mathop{\sup}_{x}{|f(x)|}\hskip 15.0pt\text{with}\hskip 15.0pt|f(x)|^{2}=\sum_{i}f_{i}^{2}(x),

and
D^{\alpha}=D_{1}^{\alpha_{1}}\cdots D_{n}^{\alpha_{n}},D_{i}=\partial/{\partial x_{i}}\hskip 5.0pt\text{for a multiindex}\hskip 5.0pt\alpha=(\alpha_{1},\cdots,\alpha_{n})
.
In what follows, if
|\alpha|=j
, for any
j=0,1,\cdots
, then we will denote
D^{\alpha}=D_{1}^{\alpha}\cdots D_{n}^{\alpha_{n}}
by
D^{j}
. We also set

\displaystyle|\mathcal{D}^{j}u(t)|_{\infty}:=|\mathcal{D}^{j}u(\cdot,t)|_{\infty}=\mathop{\max}_{|\alpha|=j}|D^{\alpha}u(\cdot,t)|_{\infty}.

Clearly,
|\mathcal{D}^{j}u(t)|_{\infty}
measures all space derivatives of order
j
in maximum norm.

Following theorem is the main result of the paper by Kreiss and Lorenz
[
8
]
for
n=3
which is also the principal result of this paper for
n\geq 3
.

Theorem 1.1
.

Consider the Cauchy problem for the Navier-Stokes equations (1.1), (1.2), where
f\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
and
\nabla\cdot f=0
is understood in the sense of distribution. There is a constant
c_{0}>0
and for every
j=0,1,\cdots
there is a constant
K_{j}
so that

(1.3)

\displaystyle t^{j/2}|\mathcal{D}^{j}u(t)|_{\infty}\leq K_{j}|f|_{\infty}\hskip 15.0pt\text{for}\hskip 15.0pt0<t\leq\frac{c_{0}}{|f|_{\infty}^{2}}.

The constants
c_{0}
and
K_{j}
are independent of
t
and
f
.

Let us briefly discuss some key ideas of the Kreiss and Lorenz paper. Rewrite (1.1) as

\displaystyle u_{t}=\triangle u+Q

where

\displaystyle Q=-\nabla p-u\cdot\nabla u.

Applying
D^{j}
for
j\geq 0
and using
Duhamel’s principle
, one obtains

(1.4)

\displaystyle v(t)=D^{j}e^{\triangle t}f+\int_{0}^{t}e^{\triangle(t-s)}D^{j}Q(s)ds,\hskip 15.0ptv:=D^{j}u.

Roughly speaking, obtaining the desired result of the Kreiss and Lorenz paper is a twofold in view of equation (1.4): first, estimates on the solution of the heat equation. Second, estimates on the derivatives of
Q
. Also, notice in (1.4), one can move one derivative
D
to the heat semi-group and consequently requiring an estimate for
|D^{j-1}Q|_{\infty}
to estimate
|v(t)|_{\infty}
. Clearly, it is necessary to determine the pressure term
p
of the Navier-Stokes equations so that
(u,p)
solves (1.1) and (1.2); the estimate of the derivatives of
p
is being used to estimate
|D^{j-1}Q|_{\infty}
. To proceed towards obtaining the required estimates on the pressure, Kreiss and Lorenz determine the pressure from the Poisson equation

(1.5)

\displaystyle\triangle p=-\nabla\cdot(u\cdot\nabla)u

which is given by

(1.6)

\displaystyle p=\sum_{i,j}R_{i}R_{j}(u_{i}u_{j}),

where
R_{i}=(-\triangle)^{-1/2}D_{i}
is the ith Riesz transform. Since the Riesz transforms are not bounded in
L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, the pressure term
p\in L^{1}_{loc}(0,T;BMO)
where
BMO
is the space of functions of bounded mean oscillation. Because of the non-local nature of the pressure, the proof of Theorem 1.1 of the Kreiss and Lorenz paper is complicated, however. This is where the method proposed in this paper deviates significantly from the approach adopted by Kreiss and Lorenz in their paper
[
8
]
.

For the purpose of proving Theorem 1.1 for
n\geq 3
, we start by transforming moemntum equation of the Navier-Stokes equations into the abstract ordinary differential equation for
u

(1.7)

\displaystyle u_{t}=\triangle u-\mathbb{P}(u\cdot\nabla)u

by eliminating the pressure, where
\mathbb{P}
is the Leray projector defined by

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}=(\mathbb{P}_{ij})_{1\leq i,j\leq n},\hskip 15.0pt\mathbb{P}_{ij}=\delta_{ij}+R_{i}R_{j};

where
R_{i}
is as in (1.6) and
\delta_{ij}
is the Kronecker delta function. Note that the equation (1.7) is obtained from (1.1 ) by applying the Leray projector with the properties
\mathbb{P}(\nabla p)=0,\mathbb{P}(\triangle u)=\triangle u
, since
\nabla\cdot u=0
. We use the solution operator
e^{\triangle t}
of the heat equation to transform the abstract differential equation into an integral equation

(1.8)

\displaystyle u(t)=e^{\triangle t}f-\int_{0}^{t}e^{\triangle(t-s)}\mathbb{P}(u\cdot\nabla u)(s)ds\hskip 15.0ptt>0.

In a paper by Giga and others
[
5
]
for
n\geq 2
, they consider the initial data
f\in BUC(\mathbb{R}^{n})
which is the space of all bounded uniformly continuous functions or in
L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
which is the space of all essentially bounded functions, and construct a unique local in time solution of (1.8). Such solution of (1.8) is called mild solution of (1.1) and (1.2). They later proved in the same paper that such mild solution is indeed a strong solution of the Navier-Stokes equations (1.1) and (1.2) in some maximum interval of time. In addition, for essentially bounded initial data, existence and uniqueness of a solution of (1.1) and (1.2) is also proved in
[
2
]
; however, Giga and others in
[
5
]
claim that their approach is simpler than the method proposed in
[
2
]
. In the same paper by Giga and others
[
5
]
, while constructing such mild solution of (1.1) and (1.2), it requires to obtain the estimate
t^{1/2}|\nabla u|_{\infty}\leq C|f|_{\infty}
for some constant
C>0
independent of
t
and
f
in some maximum interval of time. However, such maximum norm estimates for higher order derivatives of the velocity field had not been achieved until H-O Kreiss and J. Lorenz obtained in
[
8
]
for
f\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
.

The main work of this paper will focus on deriving estimate (1.3) of Theorem 1.1 by a “different approach” in a few ways than that of the Kreiss and Lorenz paper adopts. At the same time, this paper will also demonstrate the fact, the absence of the pressure term in the transformed abstract differential equation (1.7) eliminates significant amount of work of the paper by Kreiss and Lorenz while obtaining the uniform estimates of the pressure and its derivatives. However, there are some intriguing developments in the work of this paper due to the application of the Leray projector in our “different approach”.

Major difficulty in proving Theorem 1.1 lies in the fact that the Leray projector
\mathbb{P}
is not a bounded operator in
L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, since the Riesz transforms are not bounded in this space although they are bounded in
L^{r}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
for
1<r<\infty
. To overcome the difficulty, we obtain an uniform bound on the composite operator
D^{j}e^{\triangle t}\mathbb{P}
for
j=0,1\cdots
in section 2.

This paper is organized in the following ways: In section 2 we introduce a few estimates for the solution of the heat equations and state and prove a few lemmas and a corollary which are used later. In section 3, for illustrative purpose, we introduce an analogous system and prove Theorem 3.1 which establishes result of Theorem 1.1 for the analogous system. In section 4 we prove Theorem 1.1 using the same techniques as in the proof of Theorem 3.1. Finally, in section 5, we outline some remarks on the use of the estimate (1.3) obtained in Theorem 1.1.

2.
Some Auxiliary Results

Let us consider
f\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. The solution of

\displaystyle u_{t}=\triangle u,\hskip 15.0ptu=f\hskip 15.0pt\text{at}\hskip 15.0ptt=0,

is denoted by

\displaystyle u(t):=u(\cdot,t)=e^{\triangle t}f=\theta(x,t)*f

where
\theta(t)=\theta(x,t)=1/(4\pi t)^{n/2}e^{-|x|^{2}/{4t}},t>0
is the
n
dimensional heat kernel in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
*
is the convolution operator.
It is well known that

(2.1)

\displaystyle|e^{\triangle t}f|_{\infty}

\displaystyle\leq|f|_{\infty},

(2.2)

\displaystyle|\mathcal{D}^{j}e^{\triangle t}f|_{\infty}

\displaystyle\leq C_{j}t^{-j/2}|f|_{\infty},\hskip 15.0ptt>0,\hskip 15.0ptj=1,2,\cdots

Here, and in the following
C,C_{j},c,
etc are positive constants that are independent of
t
and the initial function
f
.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
\theta(t)=\theta(x,t)
be the n-dimensional heat kernel in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Then, for every
j=1,2\cdots
and every
t>0,D^{j}\theta(t)
belongs to the Hardy space
\mathcal{H}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
and

(2.3)

\displaystyle||D^{j}\theta(t)||_{\mathcal{H}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leq C_{j}t^{-j/2}.

for some constant
C_{j}
.

Proof.

First, let us recall the definition of the Hardy space.

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})=\{u\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\hskip 15.0pts.t\hskip 15.0pt\mathop{\sup}_{s>0}|h_{s}*u|\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\}

for some Schwartz class function
h
where
h_{s}(x)=s^{-n}h(\frac{x}{s}),s>0
such that
0\leq h\leq 1
and
\int h=1
. We may endow
\mathcal{H}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
with the norm

\displaystyle||u||_{\mathcal{H}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}=||u||_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}+||\mathop{\sup}_{s>0}|h_{s}*u|||_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}.

For any
j=0,1,\cdots
, we want to prove
D^{j}\theta(t)\in{\mathcal{H}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}
, that means, it suffices to show that
\mathop{\sup}_{s>0}|h_{s}*D^{j}\theta(t)|\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. For that, we take
h(x)=\theta(x,1)
and notice

\displaystyle|h_{s}*D^{j}\theta(t)(x)|=|D^{j}\theta(t+s,x)|

so that

\displaystyle\mathop{\sup}_{s>0}|h_{s}*D^{j}\theta(t)|=|D^{j}\theta(t)|\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n}).

Finally, we arrive at

\displaystyle||D^{j}\theta(t)||_{\mathcal{H}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leq C_{j}t^{-j/2},\hskip 15.0ptt>0.

∎

Lemma 2.2
.

For any
f\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. Let
j\geq 1
, there is a constant
C_{j}
such that

(2.4)

\displaystyle|\mathcal{D}^{j}e^{\triangle t}\mathbb{P}f|_{\infty}\leq C_{j}t^{-j/2}|f|_{\infty}\hskip 15.0pt\text{for}\hskip 15.0pt0<t\leq T.

Proof.

For
1\leq i\leq n
and
t>0
, by the definition of the Leray projector, we write

\displaystyle(D^{j}e^{\triangle t}\mathbb{P}f)_{i}

\displaystyle=D^{j}e^{\triangle t}f_{i}+\sum_{l=1}^{n}D^{j}e^{\triangle t}R_{i}R_{l}f_{l}

\displaystyle=D^{j}\theta(t)*f_{i}+\sum_{l=1}^{n}D^{j}R_{i}R_{l}\theta(t)*f_{l}

\displaystyle=\sum_{l=1}^{n}(\delta_{il}+R_{i}R_{l})((D^{j}\theta(t))*f_{l})

\displaystyle=\sum_{l=1}^{n}k_{il}(t)*f_{l}

where the kernel
k_{il}(t)=(\delta_{il}+R_{i}R_{l})(D^{j}\theta(t))
. Since the Riesz transforms are bounded in
\mathcal{H}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
and
||\cdot||_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leq||\cdot||_{\mathcal{H}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}
, we have

\displaystyle||k_{il}(t)||_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}

\displaystyle\leq||k_{il}(t)||_{\mathcal{H}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}

\displaystyle\leq||D^{j}\theta(t)||_{\mathcal{H}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}.

Thus, from previous Lemma 2.1 we obtain

\displaystyle||k_{il}(t)||_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leq C_{j}t^{-j/2}\hskip 15.0pt\text{for}\hskip 15.0ptt>0.

Finally, by the Young’s inequality of convolution we estimate as

\displaystyle|(D^{j}e^{\triangle t}\mathbb{P}f)_{i}|_{\infty}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{l=1}^{n}|k_{il}*f_{l}|_{\infty}

\displaystyle\leq C||k_{il}(t)||_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}|f_{l}|_{\infty}

\displaystyle\leq C_{j}t^{-j/2}|f_{l}|_{\infty}.

Hence, Lemma 2.2 is proved.
∎

3.
Estimates For the System
u_{t}=\triangle u+D_{i}\mathbb{P}g

In this section we state and prove an analogous theorem of Theorem 1.1 for the solution of an illustrative system. For that purpose, let us recall
\mathbb{P}(u\cdot\nabla)u=\sum_{i}D_{i}\mathbb{P}(u_{i}u)
for
1\leq i\leq n
. Therefore, it is appropriate to consider the illustrative system to be

(3.1)

\displaystyle u_{t}=\triangle u+D_{i}\mathbb{P}(g(u(x,t))),\hskip 15.0ptx\in\mathbb{R}^{n},\hskip 15.0ptt\geq 0

with initial function

(3.2)

\displaystyle u(x,0)=f(x)\hskip 15.0pt\text{where}\hskip 15.0ptf\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n}).

Here
g:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}^{n}
is assumed to be quadratic in
u
. We will prove the maximum norm estimates of the derivatives of the solution of (3.1) and (3.2) by the maximum norm estimate of the initial function
f
. It is well-known that the solution is
C^{\infty}
in a maximal interval
0<t<T_{f}
where
0<T_{f}\leq\infty
.

Theorem 3.1
.

Under the assumptions on
f
and
g
mentioned above, the solution of (3.1) and (3.2) satisfies the following:

(a)

There is a constant
c_{0}>0
with

\displaystyle T_{f}>\frac{c_{0}}{|f|_{\infty}^{2}}

and

(3.3)

\displaystyle|u(t)|_{\infty}\leq 2|f|_{\infty}\hskip 15.0pt\text{for}\hskip 15.0pt0\leq t\leq\frac{c_{0}}{|f|_{\infty}^{2}}.

(b)

For every
j=1,2,\cdots,
there is a constant
K_{j}>0
with

(3.4)

\displaystyle t^{j/2}|\mathcal{D}^{j}u(t)|_{\infty}\leq K_{j}|f|_{\infty}\hskip 15.0pt\text{for}\hskip 15.0pt0<t\leq\frac{c_{0}}{|f|_{\infty}^{2}}.

The constants
c_{0}
and
K_{j}
are independent of
t
and
f
.

Proof of part (a) will be given in the following lemma, and consecutively, we will also derive the estimate (3.4) of part (b). Consider
u
as the solution of the inhomogeneous heat equation
u_{t}=\triangle u+D_{i}\mathbb{P}F
where

\displaystyle F(x,t):=g(u(x,t))\hskip 15.0pt\text{for}\hskip 15.0ptx\in\mathbb{R}^{n},\hskip 15.0pt0\leq t<T_{f}.

Since
g
is quadratic in
u
, there is a constant
C_{g}
such that we have the following:

(3.5)

\displaystyle|g(u)|\leq C_{g}|u|^{2},\hskip 15.0pt|g_{u}(u)|\leq C_{g}|u|\hskip 15.0pt\text{for all}\hskip 15.0ptu\in\mathbb{R}^{n}.

Next lemma estimates the maximum norm of
u
.

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
C_{g}
denote the constant in (3.5) and let
C
denote the constant in (2.6); set
c_{0}=\frac{1}{16C^{2}C_{g}^{2}}
. Then we have
T_{f}>c_{0}/{|f|_{\infty}^{2}}
and

(3.6)

\displaystyle|u(t)|_{\infty}<2|f|_{\infty}\hskip 15.0pt\text{ for }\hskip 15.0pt0\leq t<\frac{c_{0}}{|f|_{\infty}^{2}}.

Proof.

Suppose (3.6) does not hold, then we can find the smallest time
t_{0}
such that
|u(t_{0})|_{\infty}=2|f|_{\infty}
. Since
t_{0}
is the smallest time so we have
t_{0}<c_{0}/|f|_{\infty}^{2}
. Now by (2.1) and (2.6) we have

\displaystyle 2|f|_{\infty}

\displaystyle=|u(t_{0})|_{\infty}

\displaystyle\leq|f|_{\infty}+Ct_{0}^{1/2}\mathop{\max}_{0\leq s\leq t_{0}}|g(s)|_{\infty}

\displaystyle\leq|f|_{\infty}+CC_{g}t_{0}^{1/2}\mathop{\max}_{0\leq s\leq t_{0}}|u(s)|_{\infty}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq|f|_{\infty}+CC_{g}t_{0}^{1/2}4|f|_{\infty}^{2}.

This gives

\displaystyle 1\leq 4CC_{g}t_{0}^{1/2}|f|_{\infty},

therefore
t_{0}\geq 1/{(16C^{2}C_{g}^{2}|f|_{\infty}^{2})}=c_{0}/{|f|_{\infty}^{2}}
which is a contradiction. Therefore (3.6) must hold. The estimate
T_{f}>c_{0}/{|f|_{\infty}^{2}}
is valid since
\limsup_{t\to T_{f}}|u(t)|_{\infty}=\infty
if
T_{f}
is finite.
∎

Now we prove estimate (3.4) of Theorem 3.1 by induction on
j
. Let
j\geq 1
and assume

(3.7)

\displaystyle t^{k/2}|\mathcal{D}^{k}u(t)|_{\infty}\leq K_{k}|f|_{\infty},\hskip 15.0pt\text{for}\hskip 15.0pt0\leq t\leq\frac{c_{0}}{|f|_{\infty}^{2}}\hskip 15.0pt\text{and}\hskip 15.0pt0\leq k\leq j-1.

where
c_{0}
is the same constant as in the previous lemma.
Next, we begin by applying
D^{j}
to the equation
u_{t}=\triangle u+D_{i}\mathbb{P}g(u)
to obtain

\displaystyle v_{t}=\triangle v+D^{j+1}\mathbb{P}g(u),\hskip 15.0ptv:=D^{j}u,

\displaystyle v(t)=D^{j}e^{\triangle t}f+\int_{0}^{t}e^{\triangle(t-s)}D^{j+1}(\mathbb{P}g(u))(s)ds.

Using (2.2) we get

(3.8)

\displaystyle t^{j/2}|v(t)|_{\infty}\leq C|f|_{\infty}+t^{j/2}\biggl|\int_{0}^{t}e^{\triangle(t-s)}D^{j+1}(\mathbb{P}g(u))(s)ds\biggr|_{\infty}.

We split the integral into

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t/2}+\int_{t/2}^{t}=:I_{1}+I_{2}

and obtain

\displaystyle|I_{1}(t)|

\displaystyle=\biggl|\int_{0}^{t/2}D^{j+1}e^{\triangle(t-s)}(\mathbb{P}g(u))(s)ds\biggr|_{\infty}

\displaystyle\leq\int_{0}^{t/2}|D^{j+1}e^{\triangle(t-s)}(\mathbb{P}g(u))(s)ds|_{\infty}ds.

Using the inequality in Lemma 2.2, we get

\displaystyle|I_{1}(t)|_{\infty}

\displaystyle\leq C\int_{0}^{t/2}(t-s)^{-(j+1)/2}|g(u(s))|_{\infty}ds

\displaystyle\leq C|f|_{\infty}^{2}t^{(1-j)/2}.

The integrand in
I_{2}
has singularity at
s=t
. Therefore, we can move only one derivative from
D^{j+1}\mathbb{P}g(u)
to the heat semigroup.( If we move two or more derivatives then the singularity becomes non-integrable.) Thus, we have

\displaystyle|I_{2}(t)|_{\infty}=\biggl|-\int_{t/2}^{t}De^{\triangle(t-s)}(D^{j}\mathbb{P}g(u))(s)ds\biggr|_{\infty}.

Since the Leray projector commutes with any derivatives, therefore

\displaystyle|I_{2}(t)|_{\infty}=\bigg|-\int_{t/2}^{t}De^{\triangle(t-s)}(\mathbb{P}D^{j}g(u))(s)ds\bigg|_{\infty}.

If we use Lemma 2.2 for
j=1
, we obtain

(3.9)

\displaystyle|I_{2}(t)|_{\infty}\leq C\int_{t/2}^{t}(t-s)^{-1/2}|D^{j}g(u(s))|_{\infty}ds.

Since
g(u)
is quadratic in
u
, therefore

\displaystyle|D^{j}g(u)|_{\infty}\leq C|u|_{\infty}|\mathcal{D}^{j}u|_{\infty}+\sum_{k=1}^{j-1}|\mathcal{D}^{k}u|_{\infty}|\mathcal{D}^{j-k}u|_{\infty}.

By induction hypothesis (3.7) we obtain

(3.10)

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{j-1}|\mathcal{D}^{k}u(s)|_{\infty}|\mathcal{D}^{j-k}u(s)|_{\infty}\leq Cs^{-j/2}|f|_{\infty}^{2}.

Integral (3.9) can be estimated as below:

\displaystyle|I_{2}(t)|_{\infty}

\displaystyle\leq C\int_{t/2}^{t}(t-s)^{-1/2}\bigg(C|u(s)|_{\infty}|\mathcal{D}^{j}u(s)|_{\infty}+\sum_{k=1}^{j-1}|\mathcal{D}^{k}u(s)|_{\infty}|\mathcal{D}^{j-k}u(s)|_{\infty}\bigg)ds

\displaystyle=J_{1}+J_{2}.

Using (3.10), and since
\int_{t/2}^{t}(t-s)^{-1/2}s^{-j/2}ds=Ct^{(1-j)/2}
, where
C
is independent of
t
, we obtain
|J_{2}(t)|_{\infty}\leq C|f|_{\infty}^{2}t^{(1-j)/2}
.

For
J_{1}
, we have

\displaystyle|J_{1}(t)|_{\infty}

\displaystyle=C\int_{t/2}^{t}(t-s)^{-1/2}|u(s)|_{\infty}|\mathcal{D}^{j}u(s)|_{\infty}ds

\displaystyle\leq C|f|_{\infty}\int_{t/2}^{t}(t-s)^{-1/2}s^{-j/2}s^{j/2}|\mathcal{D}^{j}u(s)|_{\infty}ds

\displaystyle\leq C|f|_{\infty}t^{(1-j)/2}\mathop{\max}_{0\leq s\leq t}\{s^{j/2}\mathcal{D}^{j}u(s)|_{\infty}\}.

We use these bounds to bound the integral in (3.8). We have
v=D^{j}u
. Then maximizing the resulting estimate for
t^{j/2}|D^{j}u(t)|_{\infty}
over all derivatives
D^{j}
of order
j
and setting

\displaystyle\phi(t):=t^{j/2}|\mathcal{D}^{j}u(t)|_{\infty}

and from (3.8), we obtain the following estimate

\displaystyle\phi(t)\leq C|f|_{\infty}+Ct^{1/2}|f|_{\infty}^{2}+C|f|_{\infty}t^{1/2}\mathop{\max}_{0\leq s\leq t}\phi(s)\hskip 15.0pt\text{for}\hskip 15.0pt0\leq t\leq\frac{c_{0}}{|f|_{\infty}^{2}}.

Since
t^{1/2}|f|_{\infty}\leq\sqrt{c_{0}}
then
Ct^{1/2}|f|_{\infty}^{2}\leq C\sqrt{c_{0}}|f|_{\infty}
. Therefore

(3.11)

\displaystyle\phi(t)\leq C_{j}|f|_{\infty}+C_{j}|f|_{\infty}t^{1/2}\mathop{\max}_{0\leq s\leq t}\phi(s)\hskip 15.0pt\text{for}\hskip 15.0pt0\leq t\leq c_{0}/{|f|_{\infty}^{2}}.

Let us fix
C_{j}
so that the above estimate holds and set

\displaystyle c_{j}=\min\bigg\{c_{0},\frac{1}{4C_{j}^{2}}\bigg\}.

First, let us prove the following

\displaystyle\phi(t)<2C_{j}|f|_{\infty}\hskip 15.0pt\text{for}\hskip 15.0pt0\leq t<\frac{c_{j}}{|f|_{\infty}^{2}}.

Suppose there is a smallest time
t_{0}
such that
0<t_{0}<c_{j}/{|f|_{\infty}^{2}}
with
\phi(t_{0})=2C_{j}|f|_{\infty}
. Then using (3.11) we obtain

\displaystyle 2C_{j}|f|_{\infty}=\phi(t_{0})\leq C_{j}|f|_{\infty}+2C_{j}^{2}|f|_{\infty}^{2}t_{0}^{1/2},

thus

\displaystyle 1\leq 2C_{j}|f|_{\infty}t_{0}^{1/2}\hskip 15.0pt\text{gives}\hskip 15.0ptt_{0}\geq c_{j}/{|f|_{\infty}^{2}}

which contradicts the assertion. Therefore, we proved the estimate

(3.12)

\displaystyle t^{j/2}|\mathcal{D}^{j}u(t)|_{\infty}\leq 2C_{j}|f|_{\infty}\hskip 15.0pt\text{for}\hskip 15.0pt0\leq t\leq c_{j}/{|f|_{\infty}^{2}}.

If

(3.13)

\displaystyle T_{j}:=\frac{c_{j}}{|f|_{\infty}^{2}}<t\leq\frac{c_{0}}{|f|_{\infty}^{2}}=:T_{0}

then we start the corresponding estimate at
t-T_{j}
. Using Lemma 3.2, we have
|u(t-T_{j})|_{\infty}\leq 2|f|_{\infty}
and obtain

(3.14)

\displaystyle T_{j}^{j/2}|\mathcal{D}^{j}u(t)|_{\infty}\leq 4C_{j}|f|_{\infty}.

Finally, for any
t
satisfying (3.13)

\displaystyle t^{j/2}\leq T_{0}^{j/2}=\bigg(\frac{c_{0}}{c_{j}}\bigg)^{j/2}T_{j}^{j/2}

and (3.14) yield

\displaystyle t^{j/2}|\mathcal{D}^{j}u(t)|_{\infty}\leq 4C_{j}\bigg(\frac{c_{0}}{c_{j}}\bigg)^{j/2}|f|_{\infty}.

This completes the proof of Theorem 1.1.

4.
Estimates For the Navier-Stokes Equations

Recall the transformed abstract ordinary differential equation (1.7)

(4.1)

\displaystyle u_{t}=\triangle u-\mathbb{P}(u\cdot\nabla u),\hskip 15.0pt\nabla\cdot u=0

with

(4.2)

\displaystyle u(x,0)=f(x).

Solution of (4.1) and (4.2) is given by

(4.3)

\displaystyle u(t)=e^{\triangle t}f-\int_{0}^{t}e^{\triangle(t-s)}\mathbb{P}(u\cdot\nabla u)(s)ds.

Using the solution (4.3) with previous estimates (2.1),(2.2) and (2.4) we prove the following lemma.

Lemma 4.1
.

Set

(4.4)

\displaystyle V(t)=|u(t)|_{\infty}+t^{1/2}|\mathcal{D}u(t)|_{\infty},\hskip 15.0pt0<t<T(f).

There is a constant
C>0
, independent of
t
and
f
, so that

(4.5)

\displaystyle V(t)\leq C|f|_{\infty}+Ct^{1/2}\mathop{\max}_{0\leq s\leq t}{V^{2}(t)},\hskip 15.0pt0<t<T(f).

Proof.

Using estimate (2.1) of the heat equation in (4.3), we obtain

\displaystyle|u(t)|_{\infty}

\displaystyle\leq|f|_{\infty}+\bigg|\int_{0}^{t}e^{\triangle(t-s)}\mathbb{P}(u\cdot\nabla u)(s)ds\bigg|_{\infty}.

Apply identity
\mathbb{P}(u\cdot\nabla u)=\sum_{i}D_{i}\mathbb{P}(u_{i}u)
with the fact, heat semi-group commutes with
D_{i}
, then use of the inequality (2.4) in Lemma 2.2 for
j=1
to proceed

\displaystyle|u(t)|_{\infty}

\displaystyle\leq|f|_{\infty}+C\int_{0}^{t}(t-s)^{-1/2}|u(s)|_{\infty}^{2}ds

\displaystyle=|f|_{\infty}+C\int_{0}^{t}(t-s)^{-1/2}s^{-1/2}s^{1/2}|u(s)|_{\infty}^{2}ds

\displaystyle\leq|f|_{\infty}+C\mathop{\max}_{0\leq s\leq t}\{s^{1/2}|u(s)|_{\infty}^{2}\}\int_{0}^{t}(t-s)^{-1/2}s^{-1/2}ds.

Since
\int_{0}^{t}(t-s)^{-1/2}s^{-1/2}ds=C>0
which is independent of
t
, we have the following estimate

\displaystyle|u(t)|_{\infty}

\displaystyle\leq|f|_{\infty}+C\mathop{\max}_{0\leq s\leq t}\{s^{1/2}|u(s)|_{\infty}^{2}\}

(4.6)

\displaystyle|u(t)|_{\infty}

\displaystyle\leq|f|_{\infty}+Ct^{1/2}\mathop{\max}_{0\leq s\leq t}{V^{2}(s)}.

Apply
D_{i}
to (4.1) to obtain

(4.7)

\displaystyle v(t)=D_{i}e^{\triangle t}f-\int_{0}^{t}e^{\triangle(t-s)}D_{i}\mathbb{P}(u\cdot\nabla)u(s)ds.

We can estimate the integral in (4.7) using Lemma 2.2 for
j=1
in the following way:

\displaystyle\bigg|\int_{0}^{t}D_{i}e^{\triangle(t-s)}\mathbb{P}(u\cdot\nabla u)(s)ds\bigg|

\displaystyle\leq\int_{0}^{t}|D_{i}e^{\triangle(t-s)}\mathbb{P}(u\cdot\nabla u)(s)|ds

\displaystyle\leq C\int_{0}^{t}(t-s)^{-1/2}|u(s)|_{\infty}|\mathcal{D}u(s)|_{\infty}ds

\displaystyle=C\int_{0}^{t}(t-s)^{-1/2}s^{-1/2}s^{1/2}|u(s)|_{\infty}|\mathcal{D}u(s)|_{\infty}ds

\displaystyle\leq C\mathop{\max}_{0\leq s\leq t}{\{s^{1/2}|u(s)|_{\infty}|\mathcal{D}u(s)|_{\infty}\}}\int_{0}^{t}(t-s)^{-1/2}s^{-1/2}ds

\displaystyle\leq C\mathop{\max}_{0\leq s\leq t}{\{|u(s)|_{\infty}^{2}+s|\mathcal{D}u(s)|_{\infty}^{2}\}}.

Therefore, we arrive at

\displaystyle|v(t)|_{\infty}

\displaystyle\leq Ct^{-1/2}|f|_{\infty}+C\mathop{\max}_{0\leq s\leq t}{\{|u(s)|_{\infty}^{2}+s|\mathcal{D}u(s)|_{\infty}^{2}\}}

(4.8)

\displaystyle t^{1/2}|\mathcal{D}u(t)|_{\infty}

\displaystyle\leq C|f|_{\infty}+Ct^{1/2}\mathop{\max}_{0\leq s\leq t}{V^{2}(t)}.

Using (4.6) and (4.8), we have proved Lemma 4.1.
∎

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
C>0
denote the constant in estimate (4.5) and set

\displaystyle c_{0}=\frac{1}{16C^{4}}.

Then
T_{f}>c_{0}/{|f|_{\infty}^{2}}
and

(4.9)

\displaystyle|u(t)|_{\infty}+t^{1/2}|\mathcal{D}u(t)|_{\infty}<2C|f|_{\infty}\hskip 15.0pt\text{for}\hskip 15.0pt0\leq t<\frac{c_{0}}{|f|_{\infty}^{2}}.

Proof.

We prove this lemma by contradiction after recalling the definition of
V(t)
in (4.4). Suppose that (4.9) does not hold, then denote by
t_{0}
the smallest time with
V(t_{0})=2C|f|_{\infty}
. Use (4.5) to obtain

\displaystyle 2C|f|_{\infty}

\displaystyle=V(t_{0})

\displaystyle\leq C|f|_{\infty}+Ct_{0}^{1/2}4C^{2}|f|_{\infty}^{2},

thus

\displaystyle 1\leq 4C^{2}t_{0}^{1/2}|f|_{\infty}^{2},

therefore
t_{0}\geq c_{0}/|f|_{\infty}^{2}
. This contradiction proves (4.9) and
T_{f}>c_{0}/{|f|_{\infty}^{2}}
.
∎

and
j=1

5.
Remarks

We can apply estimate (1.9) of Theorem 1.1 for

(5.1)

\displaystyle\frac{c_{0}}{2|f|_{\infty}^{2}}\leq t\leq\frac{c_{0}}{|f|_{\infty}^{2}}

and obtain

(5.2)

\displaystyle|\mathcal{D}^{j}u(t)|_{\infty}\leq C_{j}|f|_{\infty}^{j+1}

in interval (5.1). Starting the estimate at
t_{0}\in[0,T_{f})
we have

(5.3)

\displaystyle|\mathcal{D}^{j}u(t_{0}+t)|_{\infty}\leq C_{j}|u(t_{0})|_{\infty}^{j+1}

for

(5.4)

\displaystyle\frac{c_{0}}{2|u(t_{0})|_{\infty}^{2}}\leq t\leq\frac{c_{0}}{|u(t_{0})|_{\infty}^{2}}.

Then, if
t_{1}
is fixed with

(5.5)

\displaystyle\frac{c_{0}}{2|f|_{\infty}^{2}}\leq t_{1}<T_{f},

we can maximize both sides of (5.3) over
0\leq t_{0}\leq t_{1}
and obtain

(5.6)

\displaystyle\max\bigg\{|\mathcal{D}^{j}u(t)|_{\infty}:\frac{c_{0}}{2|f|_{\infty}^{2}}\leq t\leq t_{1}+\tau\bigg\}\leq C_{j}\max\{|u(t)|_{\infty}^{j+1}:0\leq t\leq t_{1}\}

with

\displaystyle\tau=\frac{c_{0}}{|u(t_{1})|_{\infty}^{2}}

Estimate (5.6) says, essentially, that the maximum of the
j
-th derivatives of
u
measured by
|\mathcal{D}^{j}u|_{\infty}
, can be bounded in terms of
|u|_{\infty}^{j+1}
. Clearly, a time interval near
t=0
has to be excluded on the left-hand side of (5.6) for smoothing to become effective. The positive value of
\tau
on the left-hand side of (5.6) shows that
|u|_{\infty}^{j+1}
controls
|\mathcal{D}^{j}u|_{\infty}
for some time into the future.

As is well known, if
(u,p)
solves the Navier-Stokes equations and
\lambda>0
is any scaling parameter, then the functions
u_{\lambda},p_{\lambda}
defined by

\displaystyle u_{\lambda}(x,t)=\lambda u(\lambda x,\lambda^{2}t),\hskip 15.0ptp_{\lambda}(x,t)=\lambda^{2}p(\lambda x,\lambda^{2}t)

also solve the Navier-Stokes equations. Clearly,

\displaystyle|u_{\lambda}(t)|_{\infty}=\lambda|u(\lambda^{2}t)|_{\infty},\hskip 15.0pt|\mathcal{D}^{j}u_{\lambda}(t)|_{\infty}=\lambda^{j+1}|\mathcal{D}^{j}u(\lambda^{2}t)|_{\infty}.

Therefore,
|\mathcal{D}^{j}u|_{\infty}
and
|u|_{\infty}^{j+1}
both scale like
\lambda^{j+1}
, which is, of course, consistent with the estimate (5.6). We do not know under what assumptions
|u|_{\infty}^{j+1}
can conversely be estimated in terms of
|\mathcal{D}^{j}u|_{\infty}
.

Adaptive observer for a nonlinear system with partially unknown state matrix and delayed measurements footnoteinfo This work is supported by the Russian Science Foundation under grant 22-21-00499, https://rscf.ru/project/22-21-00499/
Olga Kozachek, Alexey Bobtsov, Nikolaev Nikolaev

Abstract
Problem of an adaptive state observer design for nonlinear system with unknown time-varying parameters and under condition of delayed measurements is considered. State observation problem was raised by many researchers (see for example Sanx et al. 2019 ). In this paper the results proposed in Bobtsov et al. 2021b , Bobtsov et al. 2021a , Bobtsov et al. 2022a , Bobtsov et al. 2022b are developed. The problem is solved under assumption that the state matrix can be represented as sum of known and unknown parts. The output vector is measured with a known constant delay. An adaptive observer which reconstructs unknown state and unknown time-varying parameter is proposed.



keywords

nonlinear system, delay, adaptive observer, parameter identification.

1
Introduction

It is well known that measurements obtained with real devices are affected by delays. It makes the design of observers and control laws for dynamical systems more complicated. Many authors explored this problem recently. For the case with linear time invariant (LTI) systems, the solution of this problem is well known
(
Fridman 2014
)
. However, in case of linear time variant and nonlinear systems it is still widely open - see the references and literature review in
Sanx et al. 2019
,
Bobtsov et al. 2021b
,
Rueda-Escobedo et al. 2020
. There are different problems related to systems with delays considered by researchers currently. For instance, in
Biroon et al. 2021
authors provide an observer-based algorithm for estimation of the unknown delay and the states of the system. A solution for the problem of state observers design for systems with constant delay was presented in
(
Guo and Mei 2020
)
and state observers for the systems with time-varying delay were proposed in
Bobtsov et al. 2021b
,
Bobtsov et al. 2021a
,
Bobtsov et al. 2022a
ets. There are many examples of real systems with delayed measurements. For example, the system considered in
Ramjug-Ballgobin et al. 2021
;
Ramjug-Ballgobin et al. 2020
is a bioreactor used for the biological treatment of wastewater. The problem of biomass regulation was solved using proportional-integral (PI) observer-based control. The slide mode controller based delayed output estimator that tracks an autonomous underwater vehicle position under time-varying disturbances and parametric variations was proposed in
Desai and Manjarekar 2021
. A discrete-time case is considered in
Sehli et al. 2021
. The authors proposed a fault estimation method for linear systems with known constant time-delay and unknown disturbances and measurement noise.

In this paper we consider a nonlinear system with unknown time-varying parameters. The unknown parameters observer is designed under condition that the output signal measurements contain a known constant delay. State observer for a nonlinear system is designed based on the obtained time-varying parameter.

Problem formulation

In this paper we consider a nonlinear system described by the following equation

\displaystyle\dot{x}(t)

\displaystyle=f(x(t),u(t))+\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}[\theta_{i}(t)Q_{i}x(t)],

(1)

\displaystyle y(t)

\displaystyle=C(t)x(\phi(t)),

(2)

where
x(t)\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
is an unknown state vector,
f(x(t),u(t))\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
C(t)\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n\times n}
are known matrices and their entries are assumed to be continuous and bouded,
Q_{i}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n\times n}
is a known matrix,
\phi(t)
is a continuous known nonnegative function which defines the measurement delay

\phi(t)=t-d,\phi(t)\geq 0,

(3)

where
d>0
is a constant delay;
\theta_{i}(t)
is an unknown time-varying sinusoidal function defined by the equation (see for example
Pyrkin et al. 2015
,
Bobtsov et al. 2011
)

\ddot{\theta_{i}}(t)=-{\omega_{i}}^{2}\theta_{i}(t),

(4)

where
\omega_{i}>0
is a constant unknown parameter.

Solving the differential equation (
4
), we can rewrite it in the following form:

\displaystyle{\theta_{i}}(t)={{a}}_{1i}\sin{\omega_{i}}t+{{a}}_{2i}\cos{\omega_{i}}t,

(5)

where
a_{1i}
and
a_{2i}
are corresponding constant unknown parameters.

Assumption 1

We suppose that
C(t)
is the identity matrix
C(t)={I}_{n\times n}
.

Assumption 2

We suppose that matrices
Q_{i}
satisfy the following conditions:

Q_{i}Q_{j}=0,

(6)

where
j\neq i
;

Q_{i}=Q_{i}^{T};

(7)

Q_{i}^{2}=kQ_{i},

(8)

where
k\in\mathbb{Z}
.

Assumption 3

We suppose that

y^{T}Q_{i}y>0

(9)

Assumption 4

We suppose that function
f(x,u)
can be represented in the following form

f(x(t),u(t))=A(y(t),u(t))x(t)+B(y(t),u(t))u(t)

(10)

where
A(y(t),u(t))\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times n};B(y(t),u(t))\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
.

According to the Assumption 4 the system (
1
), (
2
) can be rewritten in the form

\displaystyle\dot{x}(t)=A(y(t),u(t))x(t)+

\displaystyle B(y(t),u(t))u(t)+

(11)

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}[\theta_{i}(t)Q_{i}x(t)],

y(t)=C(t)x(\phi(t)).

(12)

3
Main Result

3.1
Preliminary transformations

The problem of an observer design will be solved in three steps. At first, the unknown constant parameters
\omega_{i}
will be estimated. Secondly, an observer for unknown continuous function
\theta_{i}
will be designed. At the last step, a state observer for the system (
1
), (
2
) will be designed.

Let us consider the system (
1
), (
2
) at the moment
t–d
. The equation (
1
) can be rewritten in the following way

\displaystyle\dot{x}_{d}=f_{d}+\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}[\eta_{i}Q_{i}x_{d}],

(13)

where (by virtue of the Assumption 1)

\displaystyle{x}_{d}=x(\phi(t))

\displaystyle=y,f_{d}=f(x_{d},u(\phi(t)),

(14a)

\displaystyle\eta(t)=\theta(\phi(t)).

(14b)

The new parameter
\eta_{i}(t)
is defined by an equation:

\ddot{\eta_{i}}=-{\omega_{i}}^{2}\eta_{i}.

(15)

Define function
\Psi_{j}

\Psi_{j}=y^{T}Q_{j}y=x_{d}^{T}Q_{j}x_{d},

(16)

where
j=\overline{1,n}
.

For derivative of (
16
) we have:

\dot{\Psi}_{j}=\alpha_{j}(x_{d})+[\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}\eta_{i}x_{d}^{T}Q_{i}^{T}]Q_{j}x_{d}+x_{d}^{T}Q_{j}[\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}Q_{i}x_{d}\eta_{i}],

(17)

where

\alpha_{j}(x_{d})=f_{d}^{T}Q_{j}x_{d}+x_{d}^{T}Q_{j}f_{d}

(18)

is known.

According to the Assumption 2, the equation (
17
) can be rewritten as following:

\dot{\Psi}_{j}=\alpha_{j}(x_{d})+2k\eta_{j}x_{d}^{T}Q_{j}x_{d}=\alpha_{j}(x_{d})+2k\eta_{j}\Psi_{j}.

(19)

Let us define a new variable
\xi_{j}
by the equation

\xi_{j}=\ln\Psi_{j}.

(20)

Its derivative can be written in the following form

\dot{\xi}_{j}=\frac{\dot{\Psi}_{j}}{\Psi_{j}}=\frac{\alpha_{j}({x}_{d})}{\Psi_{j}}+2k\eta_{j}.

(21)

Function
\eta_{j}
can be obtained from the previous equation:

\eta_{j}=\frac{1}{2k}(\dot{\xi}_{j}-\beta_{j}(x_{d})),

(22)

where
\beta_{j}(x_{d})=\frac{\alpha_{j}(x_{d})}{\Psi_{j}}
.

3.2
Estimation of the unknown parameter
\omega

The equation (
15
) can be rewritten for
\eta_{j}(t)
in operator form:

{p}^{2}\eta_{j}=-{\omega_{j}}^{2}\eta_{j},

(23)

where
p=d/dt
is a differential operator.

Applying an LTI filter
\frac{\lambda_{1}^{3}}{{(p+\lambda_{1})}^{3}}
, where
\lambda_{1}>0
to (
23
) we can obtain

\frac{\lambda_{1}^{3}{p}^{2}}{{(p+\lambda_{1})}^{3}}\eta_{j}=-{\omega_{j}}^{2}\frac{\lambda_{1}^{3}}{{(p+\lambda_{1})}^{3}}\eta_{j}.

(24)

Substituting
\eta_{j}(t)
from (
22
) into the previous equation, we obtain:

\frac{\lambda_{1}^{3}{p}^{2}}{{(p+\lambda_{1})}^{3}}(\dot{\xi}_{j}-\beta_{j}({x}_{d}))=-{\omega}^{2}\frac{\lambda_{1}^{3}}{{(p+\lambda_{1})}^{3}}(\dot{\xi}_{j}-\beta_{j}({x}_{d})).

(25)

The equation (
25
) can be rewritten in the linear regression form

q_{j}=\varphi_{j}v_{j},

(26)

where

q_{j}=\frac{\lambda_{1}^{3}{p}^{3}}{{(p+\lambda_{1})}^{3}}\xi_{j}+\frac{\lambda_{1}^{3}{p}^{2}}{{(p+\lambda_{1})}^{3}}\beta_{j}({x}_{d}),\\

(27)

\varphi_{j}=\frac{\lambda_{1}^{3}}{{(p+\lambda_{1})}^{3}}\beta_{j}({x}_{d})-\frac{\lambda_{1}^{3}p}{{(p+\lambda_{1})}^{3}}\xi_{j},\\

(28)

v_{j}={\omega_{j}}^{2}.

(29)

For the unknown parameter
v_{j}
estimation standard gradient algorithm
(
Ljung 1999
;
Sastry et al. 1990
)
can be used:

\dot{\hat{v}}_{j}=\gamma_{1}j\varphi_{j}(q_{j}-\varphi_{j}\hat{k}_{j}),

(30)

where
\gamma_{1}j>0
is an adaptation gain.

The unknown constant parameter
\omega_{j}
can be obtained as

\hat{\omega}_{j}=\sqrt{\left|\hat{v}_{j}\right|}.

(31)

Consider the error

\displaystyle\tilde{v}_{j}=\hat{v}_{j}-v_{j}.

(32)

Then for derivative of
\tilde{v}_{j}
we have

\displaystyle\dot{\tilde{v}}_{j}=\dot{\hat{v}}_{j}=\gamma_{1}j\varphi_{j}

\displaystyle(q_{j}-\varphi_{j}\tilde{v}_{j})=

\displaystyle\gamma_{1}j

\displaystyle\varphi_{j}^{2}{v_{j}}-\gamma_{1}j\varphi_{j}^{2}\hat{k}_{j}=-\gamma_{1}j\varphi_{j}^{2}\tilde{v}_{j}.

(33)

The solution for
\tilde{v}_{j}
takes the form

\displaystyle\tilde{v}_{j}(t)=\tilde{v}_{j0}e^{-\gamma_{1}j\int\limits_{0}^{t}\varphi_{j}^{2}d\tau}.

(34)

Then

\displaystyle\hat{v}_{j}(t)=v+\tilde{v}_{j0}e^{-\gamma_{1}j\int\limits_{0}^{t}\varphi_{j}^{2}d\tau}

(35)

and for
\hat{\omega}_{j}
we obtain

\displaystyle\hat{\omega}_{j}(t)=\sqrt{v+\tilde{v}_{j0}e^{-\gamma_{1}j\int\limits_{0}^{t}\varphi_{j}^{2}d\tau}}=\omega_{j}+\varepsilon_{j}(t),

(36)

where
\varepsilon_{j}(t)
is exponentially decaying term due to

\tilde{v}_{j0}e^{-\gamma_{1}j\int\limits_{0}^{t}\varphi_{j}^{2}d\tau}
.

3.3
Time-varying parameter observer

The solution of (
23
) is a harmonical signal. At first let us design the observer of the signal
\eta_{j}
under assumption that
\omega_{j}
is known:

\eta_{i}(t)=a_{i1}\sin(\omega_{j}\phi(t))+a_{i2}\cos(\omega_{i}\phi(t)),

(37)

where
a_{i1}
and
a_{i2}
are unknown constant parameters. Let us denote

\chi_{i}=\begin{bmatrix}\chi_{i1}\\
\chi_{i2}\end{bmatrix}=\begin{bmatrix}\sin(\omega_{i}\phi(t))\\
\cos(\omega_{i}\phi(t))\end{bmatrix}.

(38)

Now we can rewrite (
37
) in the following form:

\eta_{i}(t)=a_{i}^{\top}\chi_{i},

(39)

where
a_{i}=\begin{bmatrix}{a}_{i1}\\
{a}_{i2}\end{bmatrix}.

Substituting (
39
) to (
13
), we obtain:

\dot{{x}}_{d}=f_{d}+\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}{a_{i}}^{\top}\chi_{i}Q_{i}{x}_{d}.

(40)

Let us apply a filter
\frac{\lambda_{2}}{p+\lambda_{2}}
to previous equation. After that the initial system can be transformed into linear regression form with
2n
unknown parameters

{\mathcal{Y}}=\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}\left[a_{i1}\chi_{i1}\psi_{i}+a_{i2}\chi_{i2}\psi_{i}\right]

(41)

where

{\mathcal{Y}}=\frac{\lambda_{2}p}{p+\lambda_{2}}{x}_{d}-\frac{\lambda_{2}}{p+\lambda_{2}}f_{d}=\begin{bmatrix}{{\mathcal{Y}}}_{1}\\
\rotatebox{90.0}{$\ldots$}\\
{{\mathcal{Y}}}_{n}\end{bmatrix},

{\psi}_{i}=\begin{bmatrix}{\psi}_{i1}\\
\rotatebox{90.0}{$\ldots$}\\
{\psi}_{in}\end{bmatrix}=Q_{i}x_{d}

The linear regression model can be rewritten as a system of linear equations

\begin{cases}{\mathcal{Y}}_{1}&=\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}\left[a_{i1}\chi_{i1}\psi_{i1}+a_{i2}\chi_{i2}\psi_{i1}\right]\\
&\rotatebox{90.0}{$\ldots$}\\
{{\mathcal{Y}}}_{n}&=\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}\left[a_{i1}\chi_{i1}\psi_{in}+a_{i2}\chi_{i2}\psi_{in}\right].\end{cases}

(42)

In force of the Assumption 2 every equation of the system (
42
) can be rewritten in the following form:

{\mathcal{Y}}_{i}={\mathcal{A}}_{i}^{T}\Phi_{i}

(43)

where:

{\mathcal{A}}_{i}=a_{i}

(44)

\Phi_{i}=\begin{bmatrix}\chi_{i1}\psi_{i1}\\
\chi_{i2}\psi_{i2}\end{bmatrix}

(45)

For estimation of the unknown vector
{\mathcal{A}}_{i}
we suggest to use dynamical regression extention and mixing (DREM) technology
(
Aranovskiy et al. 2017
;
Ortega et al. 2021a
;
Bobtsov et al. 2017
)
as it was developed in
Bobtsov et al. 2021a
. The observer can be written in the following form:

\displaystyle\dot{Y}_{i}=-\lambda_{3}Y_{i}

\displaystyle+\lambda_{3}\Phi_{i}^{\top}{\mathcal{Y}}_{i},\dot{\Omega}_{i}=-\lambda_{3}\Omega_{i}+\lambda\Phi_{i}^{\top}\Phi_{i},

\displaystyle\dot{\hat{{\mathcal{A}}_{i}}}

\displaystyle=-\gamma_{2}\Delta_{i}(\Delta_{i}\hat{{\mathcal{A}}_{i}}-{\mathcal{Z}}_{i}),

(46)

with
\lambda_{3}>0
and
\gamma_{2}>0
,
\hat{{\mathcal{A}}_{i}}=\hat{a}_{i}
with the definitions

\displaystyle{\mathcal{Z}}_{i}=\mbox{adj}\{\Omega_{i}\}Y_{i},\Delta_{i}=\det\{\Omega_{i}\}.

Substituting
{\hat{a}}_{i1},{\hat{a}}_{i2}
into (
37
), we have

\hat{\eta}_{i}(t)={\hat{a}}_{i1}\sin(\omega_{i}\phi(t))+{\hat{a}}_{i2}\cos(\omega_{i}\phi(t)).

(47)

We can obtain the unknown time-varying parameter
\theta_{i}(t)
by substitution of
{\hat{a}}_{i1},{\hat{a}}_{i2}
and
\hat{\omega}_{i}
(the proof is given in
Bobtsov et al. 2022b
)

\displaystyle\hat{\theta}_{i}(t)

\displaystyle=\hat{\eta}_{i}(t+d)={\hat{a}}_{i1}\sin(\hat{\omega}_{i}t)+{\hat{a}}_{i2}\cos(\hat{\omega}_{i}t).

(48)

3.4
State observer for the system (
1
) (
2
)

Estimates of the state vector of (
1
), (
2
) can be found using generalized parameter estimation-based observer (GPEBO) technique
(
Ortega et al. 2021b
)
for systems with measurements delay from
Bobtsov et al. 2021b
. If we use the estimate of
\hat{\theta}_{i}(t)
then we have the following identification algorithm.

By force of the Assumption 4 we consider the system (
1
), (
2
) in the form

\displaystyle\dot{\xi}(t)

\displaystyle=A(t)\xi(t)+B(t)u(t)+\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}[\hat{\theta}_{i}Q_{i}]\xi(t),

(49a)

\displaystyle\dot{\Phi}_{A}(t)

\displaystyle=\left(A(t)+\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}[\hat{\theta}_{i}Q_{i}]\right)\Phi_{A}(t),\;\Phi_{A}(0)=I_{n},

(49b)

and the gradient parameter estimator

\displaystyle\dot{\hat{e}}(t)

\displaystyle=-\gamma_{3}{\mathcal{P}}(t)[{\mathcal{P}}\hat{e}(t)-{\mathcal{R}}(t)],

(50)

with
\gamma_{3}>0
.

Let us define
{\mathcal{R}}(t)
and
{\mathcal{P}}(t)
as

\displaystyle{\mathcal{R}}(t)

\displaystyle:=\mbox{adj}\{\Phi_{A}(\phi(t))\}g(t),

(51a)

\displaystyle{\mathcal{P}}(t)

\displaystyle:=\det\{\Phi_{A}(\phi(t))\},

(51b)

where
\mbox{adj}\{\cdot\}
is the adjugate matrix.

Then we define the state estimate as

\displaystyle\hat{x}(t)

\displaystyle=\xi(t){+}\Phi_{A}(t)\hat{e}_{FT},

(52a)

\displaystyle\hat{e}_{FT}

\displaystyle={1\over 1-w_{c}(t)}[\hat{e}(t)-w_{c}(t)\hat{e}(0)]

(52b)

with

\displaystyle\dot{w}(t)

\displaystyle=-\gamma{\mathcal{P}}^{2}(t)w(t),\;w(0)=1,

(53)

and
w_{c}(t)
defined via the clipping function

w_{c}(t)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{lcl}w(t)&\;\mbox{if}&w(t)\leq 1-\mu,\\
1-\mu&\;\mbox{if}&w(t)>1-\mu,\end{array}\right.

(54)

where
\mu\in(0,1)
is a designer chosen parameter.

Then
\hat{x}(t)=x(t),\;\forall t\geq t_{c},
for some
t_{c}\in(0,\infty)

{pf}

Consider error equation

\displaystyle e(t)=x(t)-\xi(t).

(55)

Then, taking into account
\hat{\theta}(t)=\theta(t)
we have

\displaystyle\dot{e}(t)=\left(A(t)-\theta(t)I_{n}\right)e(t).

(56)

The solution for
e(t)
can be found in the following form

\displaystyle e(t)=\Phi_{A}e(0),

(57)

where
e(0)=x(0)-\xi(0)
and
\Phi_{A}
is the fundamental matrix defined by equation (
49b
). For zero initial conditions in (
49a
) we have
e(0)=x(0)
.

After substitution (
57
) into (
55
) we can write

\displaystyle\Phi_{A}(t)e(0)=x(t)-\xi(t).

(58)

In (
58
) the unknown state vector
x
is used, but we can implement equation written in the following form

\displaystyle\Phi_{A}(\phi(t))e(0)=x(\phi(t))-\xi(\phi(t)),

(59)

and we obtain linear regression equation

\displaystyle g(t)=\Phi_{A}(\phi(t))e(0),

(60)

where
g(t)=x(\phi(t))-\xi(\phi(t))
.

Now we can find vector of initial conditions
(\hat{e}(0))
using gradient approach (
50
) or
(\hat{e}_{FT}(0))
using finite time algorithm (
52b
). After estimation of initial condition the state vector can be found by (
52a
).

5
Concluding Remarks

An adaptive observer for a nonlinear system (
1
), (
2
) with delayed measurements was presented. The system contains unknown time-variant parameters in the state matrix. At first we estimate constant parameters of the unknown sinusoidal function. Based on the obtained estimates we can calculate the time-varying parameters. After that we use finite time observer based on GPEBO+DREM technique to estimate unknown state vector. To demonstrate efficiency of the proposed algorithm the simulation results was presented.

High order asymptotic preserving and well-balanced schemes for the shallow water equations with source terms


Abstract
. In this study, we investigate the Shallow Water Equations incorporating source terms accounting for Manning friction and a non-flat bottom topology. Our primary focus is on developing and validating numerical schemes that serve a dual purpose: firstly, preserving all steady states within the model, and secondly, maintaining the late-time asymptotic behavior of solutions, which is governed by a diffusion equation and coincides with a long time and stiff friction limit. Our proposed approach draws inspiration from a penalization technique adopted in [Boscarino et. al, SIAM Journal on Scientific Computing, 2014] . By employing an additive implicit-explicit Runge-Kutta method, the scheme can ensure a correct asymptotic behavior for the limiting diffusion equation, without suffering from a parabolic-type time step restriction which often afflicts multiscale problems in the diffusive limit. Numerical experiments are performed to illustrate high order accuracy, asymptotic preserving, and asymptotically accurate properties of the designed schemes.

1.
Introduction

The Shallow Water Equations (SWEs) are a set of equations derived from the more comprehensive Navier-Stokes equations, which govern the behavior of fluids. This hyperbolic system finds widespread application in modeling fluid dynamics in various natural environments, including channels, rivers, and oceans.
The study of these equations carries significant practical implications, with numerous applications extending to crucial areas such as the prediction of storm surge levels, tides, and alterations in coastlines caused by events like hurricanes, among many others
[
46
,
34
,
49
,
47
,
28
]
.

In recent years, the SWEs have become more and more important in atmospheric flows, especially with different space and time scales.
In this paper, we consider the SWEs with Manning friction and a non-flat bottom topology, which can be written as

(1.1)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\partial_{t}h+\nabla\cdot{\bf q}=0,\\[8.53581pt]
\partial_{t}{\bf q}+\nabla\cdot\left(\dfrac{{\bf q}\otimes{\bf q}}{h}\right)+\nabla\left(\dfrac{g}{2}h^{2}\right)=-gh\nabla b-\gamma{\bf q},\end{array}\right.

where
h
is the depth of the water layer,
{\bf u}={\bf q}/h
is the flow velocity, which are defined on a time-space domain
(t,{\bf x})\in\mathbb{R}^{+}\times\Omega
;
g
is the gravitational constant,
b({\bf x})
is the bottom topography which is independent of time;
\otimes
denotes the Kronecker product, and
\gamma{\bf q}
models the bottom friction.
Here
\gamma=\dfrac{gk^{2}|{\bf q}|}{h^{\eta}}
with
|{\bf q}|
being the
L^{2}
norm of
{\bf q}
, and
k
is the Manning coefficient to determine the intensity of the friction exerted by the bottom of the water.
The larger the
k
is, the greater the friction exerted by the water.
\eta
is a parameter which is usually taken to be
7/3
.

To investigate the
late-time
behavior of solutions to the system (
1.1
), and following the lines of
[
13
]
, a behavior of
h
and
{\bf q}
in a long time simulation and dominant friction is considered. Such an asymptotic behavior is governed by a diffusive regime. For the convenience of analysis, we introduce a small parameter
\varepsilon
in order to scale the time
t
and the friction coefficient
k
, as follows:

(1.2)

t\leftarrow\frac{\hat{t}}{\varepsilon}\,,\qquad{k\leftarrow\frac{\hat{k}}{\varepsilon}}.\,

Then the system (
1.1
) can be rewritten as

(1.3)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\varepsilon\partial_{\hat{t}}h+\nabla\cdot{\bf q}=0,\\[8.53581pt]
\displaystyle\varepsilon\partial_{\hat{t}}{\bf q}+\nabla\cdot\left(\dfrac{{\bf q}\otimes{\bf q}}{h}\right)+\nabla\left(\dfrac{g}{2}h^{2}\right)=-gh\nabla b-\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{2}}\widehat{\gamma}{\bf q},\end{array}\right.

with
\widehat{\gamma}=\dfrac{g\hat{k}^{2}|{\bf q}|}{h^{\eta}}
.
In
[
6
]
(and the references therein), it was shown that, in the modeling of shallow water problems when the friction is particularly strong and very much dominates over the advection effects, the asymptotic regime, i.e.
\varepsilon\to 0
, associated with (
1.3
) is governed by the nonlinear diffusion equation

(1.4)

h_{t}=\nabla\cdot\left(\frac{\sqrt{h}}{\kappa(h)}\frac{\nabla h}{\sqrt{|\nabla h|}}\right).

In the next section, we show that by the Chapman-Enskog expansions of the variables
{\bf q}
and
h
in term of the parameter
\varepsilon
, we get the limiting nonlinear diffusion equation (
1.4
) when
\varepsilon\to 0
.

Now, denoting

(1.5)

\widehat{{\bf q}}=\frac{{\bf q}}{\varepsilon},

the system (
1.3
) becomes

(1.6)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\varepsilon\partial_{\hat{t}}h+\varepsilon\nabla\cdot\widehat{{\bf q}}=0,\\[8.53581pt]
\varepsilon^{2}\partial_{\hat{t}}\widehat{{\bf q}}+\varepsilon^{2}\nabla\cdot\left(\dfrac{\widehat{{\bf q}}\otimes\widehat{{\bf q}}}{h}\right)+\nabla\left(\dfrac{g}{2}h^{2}\right)=-gh\nabla b-\hat{\gamma}\widehat{{\bf q}}.\end{array}\right.

Dividing the first sub-equation of system (
1.6
) by
\varepsilon
, the second one by
\varepsilon^{2}
, and dropping the hats for clarity, then we can obtain the non-dimensional system

(1.7)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\partial_{t}h+\nabla\cdot{\bf q}=0,\\[8.53581pt]
\displaystyle\partial_{t}{\bf q}+\nabla\cdot\left(\dfrac{{\bf q}\otimes{\bf q}}{h}\right)+\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{2}}\nabla\left(\dfrac{g}{2}h^{2}\right)=-\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{2}}gh\nabla b-\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{2}}\gamma{\bf q}.\end{array}\right.

Notice that the nonlinear SWEs (
1.7
) belong to the family of hyperbolic conservation laws with stiff source terms
[
6
]
, i.e.,

(1.8)

U_{t}+\nabla\cdot F(U)=\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{2}}S(U),

where
U=(h,{\bf q})^{T}
,
\nabla\cdot F(U)=\left(\nabla\cdot{\bf q},\displaystyle\nabla\cdot\left(\frac{{\bf q}\otimes{\bf q}}{h}\right)\right)^{T}
, and
S(U)=-\left(0,\nabla\left(\dfrac{g}{2}h^{2}\right)+gh\nabla b+\gamma{\bf q}\right)^{T}
.

Solving (
1.8
) numerically is a challenging task for many reasons. Firstly, such system often admits non-trivial steady state solutions in which the source term balances the effect of the flux gradients.
There are many interesting physical phenomena, which are small perturbations near these steady states. A standard numerical scheme cannot capture this equilibrium state or small perturbations near such equilibrium state, unless a much refined mesh is adopted
[
46
,
42
,
45
]
.
A significant scheme for the SWEs with source terms is proposed by Bermudez and Vazquez
[
5
]
, which balances the gradient of pressure by source terms at the discrete level, so that the equilibrium state can be maintained in coarse mesh subdivisions.
Then many schemes are designed to preserve the steady state
[
35
,
53
,
46
,
49
,
47
,
48
,
15
,
22
,
37
,
39
,
50
,
45
,
33
]
, including finite difference, finite volume, and discontinuous Galerkin methods.
All the above works consider that the solutions are not affected by the friction, while there are many situations that the friction can not be ignored.
The additional friction term makes the well-balanced work more difficult, especially for situation that the friction is dominant.
When the system (
1.7
) reaches steady state, it becomes

(1.9)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\nabla\cdot{\bf q}=0,\\[8.53581pt]
\displaystyle\nabla\cdot\left(\dfrac{{\bf q}\otimes{\bf q}}{h}\right)+\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{2}}\nabla\left(\dfrac{g}{2}h^{2}\right)=-\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{2}}gh\nabla b-\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{2}}\gamma{\bf q}.\end{array}\right.

In case of a still water
{\bf q}=\mathbf{0}
, the system has a steady state named “lake at rest”
[
52
]
, that is

(1.10)

h({\bf x},t)+b({\bf x})=C,\qquad{\bf q}({\bf x},t)=\mathbf{0}.

For a moving water steady state
{\bf q}\neq\mathbf{0}
, it makes the design of a well-balanced scheme much more challenging. Very limited studies include traditional time discretizations
[
36
,
24
]
, or semi-implicit time discretizations
[
41
,
44
,
43
,
17
]
.
However, in most of these works, the stiff friction is caused by the vanishing of water depth
h
.
Another stiff friction is caused by a large coefficient
k

[
13
]
, which is considered in this work. Besides, for simplicity, we only consider the still water steady state (
1.10
).

Secondly, a system as (
1.8
) with a small parameter
\varepsilon
may relax to a reduced system as
\varepsilon
vanishes. Under suitable conditions
[
16
]
, the solutions of the system (
1.8
) will converge to the solutions of the limiting system, see for example (
1.4
). It is not at all obvious that a numerical scheme adopted for the solution of the system (
1.8
) will become (for given space and time discretization parameters) a consistent discretization for the limiting equation, as the small parameter
\varepsilon
vanishes. When this occurs, the scheme is said to be
asymptotic preserving
(AP). The AP scheme was proposed by S. Jin
[
30
]
and we refer the readers to the review papers
[
27
,
31
]
for more work about AP schemes.
Recently, Bulteau et. al.
[
13
]
designed an AP scheme for system (
1.7
) by using an explicit time discretization, combined with finite volume and a well-balanced modification for space discretization. The scheme has been proven to be both AP and well-balanced. However, due to an explicit time discretization, the scheme might be subject to severe time step restrictions in order to maintain stability, as the eigenvalues of the system being
\lambda_{1}={\bf u}\cdot{\bf{n}}+c/\varepsilon
and
\lambda_{2}={\bf u}\cdot{\bf{n}}-c/\varepsilon
along the normal direction
{\bf n}
, where
c=\sqrt{gh}
representing the speed of sound. Such eigenvalues lead to a time step for an explicit scheme as

(1.11)

\Delta t=\text{CFL}\frac{\Delta x}{\max(|{\bf u}|+c/\varepsilon)}\sim\varepsilon\Delta x,

where
\Delta t
is the time step size,
\Delta x
is the mesh size, and CFL is the time stability CFL number.

In the present paper, we will develop a numerical scheme to approximate the solution of (
1.7
), which accurately captures the steady state solutions and is able to restore the asymptotic regime, i.e., the scheme becomes a consistent discretization for the limiting equation (
1.4
) as
\varepsilon\to 0
(AP). Our main focus is to design a class of AP implicit-explicit (IMEX) high order finite difference weighted essentially non-oscillatory (WENO) schemes. However, the AP property only guarantees the consistency of the scheme, and in general does not guarantee the high order accuracy in the limit for
\varepsilon\ll 1
, i.e. the order of accuracy may degrade. We will show that under some assumptions, in the limit for
\varepsilon\to 0
, the scheme maintains its order of temporal accuracy, and we say that the scheme is
asymptotically accurate
(AA) for the limiting system when
\varepsilon\to 0
. To achieve high order temporal accuracy, IMEX Runge-Kutta (RK) schemes
[
8
,
7
]
represent a powerful tool for the time discretization of stiff systems of the form (
1.8
). However, directly applying IMEX RK schemes to the SWEs (
1.7
) are not very efficient. In the diffusive regime, i.e.
\varepsilon\to 0
, such schemes become consistent but explicit schemes for the diffusive limiting equation (
1.4
). Therefore, these schemes suffer from a usual parabolic time step restriction
\Delta t=O(\Delta x^{2})
, for given space and time discretization parameters
\Delta x
and
\Delta t
.
In order to avoid this parabolic time-step restriction, we introduce a new reformulation of the problem (
1.7
) based on the addition of two opposite diffusive terms in the first equation in (
1.7
), which has been introduced in
[
12
,
9
,
8
,
51
]
, and the resulting scheme is consistent and relaxed to an implicit scheme in the diffusive limit, therefore avoiding the typical parabolic restriction. This strategy is very effective. Furthermore, we will show that, under this reformulation of the system (
1.7
), the scheme converges to a high order implicit scheme in the diffusive limit (
1.4
), namely the scheme is AA.

The rest of this paper is organized as follows.
In Section
2
, we will formally derive the limiting equation for the SWEs with a non-flat topography and Manning friction. An IMEX time discretization and finite difference WENO reconstruction with well-balanced property will also be introduced. Formal analyses of AP and AA properties of the scheme are provided in Section
3
. Numerical experiments are performed in Section
4
, followed by concluding remarks in Section
5
.

2.
Diffusive limit and numerical scheme

In this section, we will first briefly review the diffusive limit for the SWEs with strong friction and a bottom topography, which leads to a nonlinear degenerate
p
-Laplacian equation. Then we will present an AP scheme, which can well capture this nonlinear diffusive limit. This approach consists of adding and subtracting the limiting nonlinear diffusive term in the water depth equation, with different time discretizations and we will show that in the diffusive limit the numerical scheme becomes an implicit time discretization for the limiting system, which can avoid a parabolic time step restriction. This technique has been developed in
[
12
,
9
,
8
]
and used in
[
38
,
51
]
.

For this system, since all terms with explicit and implicit time discretizations are seperated, we employ an additive implicit-explicit (IMEX) scheme, with a multi-stage RK method to achieve high order accuracy. In space, we adopt a high order finite difference WENO reconstruction for convective terms
[
40
,
29
]
, combined with central difference discretizations for second order derivative terms. With a careful treatment in space, the well-balanced property for a hydrostatic equilibrium state is also achieved. For our proposed scheme, we can formally prove it is AP and AA.

2.1.
Diffusive limit

Firstly, let us briefly review the limiting system for the SWEs
with friction and a non-flat bottom (
1.7
), under the diffusive scaling with (
1.2
) and (
1.5
). We denote
H=h+b
as the surface level of water, and assume the following Chapman-Enskog expansions
[
13
]

(2.1)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}h({\bf x},t)=h_{0}({\bf x},t)+\varepsilon h_{1}({\bf x},t)+\varepsilon^{2}h_{2}({\bf x},t)+\cdots,\\[8.53581pt]
{\bf q}({\bf x},t)={\bf q}_{0}({\bf x},t)+\varepsilon{\bf q}_{1}({\bf x},t)+\varepsilon^{2}{\bf q}_{2}({\bf x},t)+\cdots,\end{array}\right.

with

(2.2)

H({\bf x},t)=h_{0}({\bf x},t)+b({\bf x})+\varepsilon h_{1}({\bf x},t)+\varepsilon^{2}h_{2}({\bf x},t)+\cdots.

Here the bottom topography
b({\bf x})
is independent of time. Substituting (
2.1
) into (
1.7
), for the leading order terms, we have

(2.3a)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}h_{0}+\nabla\cdot{\bf q}_{0}=0,

(2.3b)

\displaystyle\nabla\left(\dfrac{g}{2}h_{0}^{2}\right)+gh_{0}\nabla b=-\gamma_{0}{\bf q}_{0},

with the nonlinear friction coefficient
\gamma_{0}=gk^{2}|{\bf q}_{0}|/h_{0}^{\eta}
. Denoting
H_{0}({\bf x},t)=h_{0}({\bf x},t)+b({\bf x})
, since

(2.4)

\nabla\left(\dfrac{g}{2}h_{0}^{2}\right)+gh_{0}\nabla b=gh_{0}\nabla h_{0}+gh_{0}\nabla b=gh_{0}\nabla H_{0},

with
\gamma_{0}=gk^{2}|{\bf q}_{0}|/h_{0}^{\eta}
, substituting (
2.4
) into (
2.3b
) we obtain

(2.5)

gk^{2}|{\bf q}_{0}|^{2}/h_{0}^{\eta}=|\gamma_{0}{\bf q}_{0}|=gh_{0}|\nabla H_{0}|,

which yields
|{\bf q}_{0}|=\sqrt{h_{0}^{\eta+1}|\nabla H_{0}|/k^{2}}
, and
(
2.3b
) becomes

(2.6)

{\bf q}_{0}=-\sqrt{\frac{h_{0}^{\eta+1}}{k^{2}}}\frac{\nabla H_{0}}{\sqrt{|\nabla H_{0}|}}.

Substituting (
2.6
) into (
2.3a
), it leads to the following limiting equation

(2.7)

\partial_{t}h_{0}=\nabla\cdot\left(\sqrt{\frac{h_{0}^{\eta+1}}{k^{2}}}\frac{\nabla H_{0}}{\sqrt{|\nabla H_{0}|}}\right).

This limiting equation (
2.7
) is a non-stationary
p
-Laplacian equation with
p=3/2
.
p
-Laplacian equations are degenerate parabolic equations, which arise in many physical problems
[
20
]
, and they have been studied both theoretically and numerically in several works, such as
[
1
,
26
,
32
,
19
,
21
,
18
,
23
,
4
,
3
]
, and references therein. Due to the limiting equation itself is already nonlinear and degenerate, it makes the designing of an efficient numerical scheme for (
1.7
) which can capture the diffusive limit (
2.7
) very challenging. In the following, we will propose an efficient AP scheme, which is high order both in space and in time and can well capture the corresponding limit.

2.2.
IMEX time discretization

We consider an IMEX time discretization for (
1.7
), which only uses implicit time discretizations for stiff and diffusion terms, while convective terms are discretized explicitly, so that we can achieve a large time step condition, while avoiding to solve a very complicate nonlinear system. We start with writing (
1.7
) in the following form of ordinary differential equations

(2.8)

U_{t}=F(U)+G(U),

with
U=(h,{\bf q})^{T}
and

(2.9)

F(U)=\begin{pmatrix}-\nabla\cdot{\bf q}-\mu(\varepsilon)\nabla\cdot\left(\sqrt{\frac{h^{\eta+1}}{k^{2}}}\frac{\nabla H}{\sqrt{|\nabla H|}}\right)\\
\,\\
-\nabla\cdot\left(\dfrac{{\bf q}\otimes{\bf q}}{h}\right)\end{pmatrix},\,G(U)=\begin{pmatrix}\mu(\varepsilon)\nabla\cdot\left(\sqrt{\frac{h^{\eta+1}}{k^{2}}}\frac{\nabla H}{\sqrt{|\nabla H|}}\right)\\
\,\\
-\dfrac{1}{\varepsilon^{2}}\nabla\left(\dfrac{g}{2}h^{2}\right)-\dfrac{1}{\varepsilon^{2}}gh\nabla b-\dfrac{1}{\varepsilon^{2}}\gamma{\bf q}\end{pmatrix},

where we have added and substracted a term for the equation of water depth
h
, based on the limiting equation (
2.7
). Here the weighted function
\mu(\varepsilon)
is such that
\mu:\mathbb{R}^{+}\to[0,1]
and
\mu(0)=1
. When
\varepsilon
is not small, there is no need to add and subtract the limiting term, therefore
\mu(\varepsilon)
will be small in such a hyperbolic regime, i.e.,
\mu(\varepsilon)\approx 0
. The precise choice of
\mu(\varepsilon)
depends not only on
\varepsilon
but also the mesh size or the time step as discussed in
[
8
]
, which will be specified later.
Now let us focus on the time discretization while keeping the space continuous. The spatial discretizations will be discussed in the next section.

2.2.1.
First order IMEX scheme

We begin with a first order IMEX scheme for (
2.8
) with (
2.9
). The scheme reads

(2.10a)

\displaystyle\dfrac{h^{n+1}-h^{n}}{\Delta t}+\nabla\cdot{\bf q}^{n}+\mu(\varepsilon)\nabla\cdot\left(\sqrt{\dfrac{h^{\eta+1}}{k^{2}}}\dfrac{\nabla H}{\sqrt{|\nabla H|}}\right)^{n}=\mu(\varepsilon)\nabla\cdot\left(\sqrt{\dfrac{h^{\eta+1}}{k^{2}}}\frac{\nabla H}{\sqrt{|\nabla H|}}\right)^{n+1},

(2.10b)

\displaystyle\dfrac{{\bf q}^{n+1}-{\bf q}^{n}}{\Delta t}+\nabla\cdot\left(\dfrac{{\bf q}\otimes{\bf q}}{h}\right)^{n}+\dfrac{1}{\varepsilon^{2}}\nabla\left(\dfrac{g}{2}h^{2}\right)^{n+1}=-\dfrac{1}{\varepsilon^{2}}gh^{n+1}\nabla b-\dfrac{1}{\varepsilon^{2}}\gamma^{n+1}{\bf q}^{n+1}.

Here we take the weighted function to be
\mu(\varepsilon)=e^{-\varepsilon^{2}/\Delta x}
with
\Delta x
being the mesh size
[
12
,
9
]
.
This weighted function depends on
\varepsilon
and
\Delta x
, which ensures that as
\varepsilon
goes to zero,
\mu(\varepsilon)
will approach
1
, while it approaches
0
when
\varepsilon
is of
\mathcal{O}(1)
and
\Delta x
is small enough. In this case, as
\varepsilon\rightarrow 0
, the second and third terms on the left hand side cancel with each other, and the scheme (
2.10a
) formally converges to

(2.11)

\frac{h^{n+1}-h^{n}}{\Delta t}-\nabla\cdot\left\{\sqrt{\frac{h^{\eta+1}}{k^{2}}}\frac{\nabla H}{\sqrt{|\nabla H|}}\right\}^{n+1}=0,

which is an implicit time discretization for the limting equation (
2.7
), so that a parabolic type time discretization namely
\Delta t=\mathcal{O}(\Delta x^{2})
can be avoided.

However, solving (
2.10
) we face two important issues. Let us
rewrite (
2.10a
) as

(2.12)

h^{n+1}=h_{\star}+\Delta t\mu(\varepsilon)\nabla\cdot\left\{\sqrt{\frac{h^{\eta+1}}{k^{2}}}\frac{\nabla H}{\sqrt{|\nabla H|}}\right\}^{n+1},

with

h_{\star}=h^{n}-\Delta t\left\{\nabla\cdot{\bf q}^{n}+\mu(\varepsilon)\nabla\cdot\left(\sqrt{\frac{h^{\eta+1}}{k^{2}}}\frac{\nabla H}{\sqrt{|\nabla H|}}\right)^{n}\right\}.

Since
H^{n+1}=h^{n+1}+b
, (
2.12
) is a fully nonlinear system of
h^{n+1}
, especially the diffusion coefficient depends on
\sqrt{|\nabla H|}
in the denominator. The first issue is that when the water is at or close to rest, we have
\nabla H=0
or
\nabla H
close to
0
. In this case, this nonlinear diffusion term cannot be linearized by using the approach in
[
51
]
. Instead we have to take
\nabla H
in the numerator and denominator of (
2.12
) to be at the same time level to solve a nonlinear system, in order to avoid such a singularity in the denominator. We adopt a simple Picard iteration to solve this nonlinear system as follows:

•

Starting from the initial guess
h^{n+1,0}=h^{n}
, at each iteration point, we first compute
|\nabla H|
(based on a numerical approximation), if
|\nabla H|<\delta
where
\delta
is a small value (e.g., we take
\delta=10^{-9}
in our numerical tests, unless otherwise specified), we stop the iteration and take
h^{n+1}=h^{n+1,0}=h^{n}
;

•

Otherwise, we continue the iteration by taking

(2.13)

h^{n+1,\ell+1}=h_{\star}+\Delta t\mu(\varepsilon)\nabla\cdot\left\{\frac{\left(\sqrt{\frac{h^{\eta+1}}{k^{2}}}\right)^{n+1,\ell}}{\sqrt{|\nabla H|^{n+1,\ell}}}\nabla H^{n+1,\ell+1}\right\},\quad\ell\geq 0.

The iteration is stopped when
\|h^{n+1,\ell+1}-h^{n+1,\ell}\|\leq\delta
. Here
\delta
is taken to be the same as above, and an
L^{1}
norm is taken in practice.

By taking this simple Picard iteration, as we can see, when initially the solution is at a hydrodynamic equilibrium, that is
H=\text{Const.}
, due to the iteration is stopped at the first step, such an equilibrium can be preserved.

After obtaining
h^{n+1}
, the second issue is how to update
{\bf q}^{n+1}
by (
2.10b
), since the friction coefficient
\gamma^{n+1}=\dfrac{gk^{2}|{\bf q}^{n+1}|}{(h^{n+1})^{\eta}}
also depends on
{\bf q}^{n+1}
. We first rewrite (
2.10b
) as

(2.14)

\left(1+\frac{\Delta t}{\varepsilon^{2}}\,\frac{gk^{2}|{\bf q}^{n+1}|}{(h^{n+1})^{\eta}}\right){\bf q}^{n+1}={\bf q}_{\star},

here

(2.15)

{\bf q}_{\star}={\bf q}^{n}-\Delta t\nabla\cdot\left(\frac{{\bf q}\otimes{\bf q}}{h}\right)^{n}-\frac{\Delta t}{\varepsilon^{2}}\nabla\left(\frac{g}{2}h^{2}\right)^{n+1}-\frac{\Delta t}{\varepsilon^{2}}gh^{n+1}\nabla b.

Taking an
L^{2}
norm on both sides of (
2.14
), it becomes a quadratic equation for
|{\bf q}^{n+1}|

(2.16)

\frac{\Delta t}{\varepsilon^{2}}\,\frac{gk^{2}}{(h^{n+1})^{\eta}}|{\bf q}^{n+1}|^{2}+|{\bf q}^{n+1}|-|{\bf q}_{\star}|=0.

(
2.16
) has two real roots, and the positive value is the reasonable one

\displaystyle|{\bf q}^{n+1}|=\frac{-\varepsilon^{2}+\sqrt{\varepsilon^{4}+\displaystyle\frac{4\Delta tgk^{2}}{(h^{n+1})^{\eta}}|\varepsilon^{2}{\bf q}_{\star}|}}{\displaystyle\frac{2\Delta tgk^{2}}{(h^{n+1})^{\eta}}}.

Substituting it into (
2.14
), we update
{\bf q}^{n+1}
by

(2.17)

{\bf q}^{n+1}=\frac{2\varepsilon^{2}{\bf q}_{\star}}{\varepsilon^{2}+\sqrt{\varepsilon^{4}+\displaystyle\frac{4\Delta tgk^{2}}{(h^{n+1})^{\eta}}|\varepsilon^{2}{\bf q}_{\star}|}}.

However, we note that as
\varepsilon\rightarrow 0
, numerically in (
2.17
) we face an issue of
\frac{0}{0}
, and in (
2.15
) a dividing by
\varepsilon^{2}
. Both will lead to numerical instabilities. To avoid these two stiff issues, from (
2.15
), we compute
\varepsilon^{2}{\bf q}_{\star}
into one by

\varepsilon^{2}{\bf q}_{\star}=\varepsilon^{2}{\bf q}^{n}-\varepsilon^{2}\Delta t\nabla\cdot\left(\frac{{\bf q}\otimes{\bf q}}{h}\right)^{n}-\Delta t\nabla\left(\frac{g}{2}h^{2}\right)^{n+1}-\Delta tgh^{n+1}\nabla b.

If the solution is at a hydrostatic equilibrium, namely
\nabla\left(\dfrac{1}{2}h^{2}\right)=-h\nabla b
and
{\bf q}=\mathbf{0}
,
we would expect
{\bf q}
always to be
\mathbf{0}
. However, when
\varepsilon
is small, due to round-off errors, the updating of
{\bf q}^{n+1}
by (
2.17
)
is unstable. In this case, if
|\varepsilon^{2}{\bf q}_{\star}|<\eta
and
|{\bf q}^{n}|<\eta
where
\eta
is taken to be
10^{-12}
at the round-off error level, we set
{\bf q}^{n+1}=\mathbf{0}
.

2.2.2.
High order IMEX scheme

The first order IMEX scheme (
2.10
) can be generalized to high order with an additive IMEX RK method, such procedure has been introduced in
[
8
]
.
For the additive system (
2.8
), an additive IMEX RK time discretization is characterized by the double Butcher
tableau

[
14
]
,

(2.18)

\begin{array}[]{c|c}\tilde{\mathbf{c}}&\tilde{A}\\
\hline\cr\\
&\tilde{\mathbf{b}^{T}}\end{array}\ \ \ \ \ \qquad\begin{array}[]{c|c}{\mathbf{c}}&{A}\\
\hline\cr\\
&{\mathbf{b}^{T}}\end{array},

where
\tilde{A}=(\tilde{a}_{ij})
is an
s\times s
lower triangular matrix, i.e.
\tilde{a}_{ij}=0
for
j\geq i
, leading to an explicit scheme, while
A=({a}_{ij})
is an
s\times s
matrix for an implicit scheme. A diagonally implicit RK (DIRK) scheme with
a_{ij}=0
for
j>i
is used, which is simple and efficient for implicit discretization. Other vectors are
\tilde{\mathbf{c}}=(\tilde{c}_{1},...,\tilde{c}_{s})^{T}
,
\tilde{\mathbf{b}}=(\tilde{b}_{1},...,\tilde{b}_{s})^{T}
, and
\mathbf{c}=(c_{1},...,c_{s})^{T}
,
\mathbf{b}=(b_{1},...,b_{s})^{T}
, where
\tilde{\mathbf{c}}
and
\mathbf{c}
satisfy the relation

\tilde{c}_{i}=\sum_{j=1}^{i-1}\tilde{a}_{ij}
,
c_{i}=\sum_{j=1}^{i}a_{ij}
for
i=1,...,s
.

A sketched procedure of the additive IMEX RK scheme is given as follows. Starting from
U^{n}
, for inner stages
i=1,\ldots,s
:

•

Compute the solution
U_{\star}^{(i)}
from previous known stages

(2.19)

U_{\star}^{(i)}=U^{n}+\Delta t\sum_{j=1}^{i-1}\tilde{a}_{ij}F(U^{(j)})+\Delta t\sum_{j=1}^{i-1}a_{ij}G(U^{(j)}).

•

Update
U^{(i)}
with

(2.20)

U^{(i)}=U_{\star}^{(i)}+\Delta ta_{ii}G(U^{(i)}).

After obtaining all inner stage values, we update the solution
U^{n+1}
from

(2.21)

U^{n+1}=U^{n}+\Delta t\sum_{i=1}^{s}\tilde{b}_{i}F(U^{(i)})+\Delta t\sum_{i=1}^{s}b_{i}G(U^{(i)}).

Applying (
2.19
)-(
2.21
) to (
2.8
) with (
2.9
), in components, for
U_{\star}^{(i)}
in (
2.19
), they are

(2.22a)

\displaystyle h_{\star}^{(i)}=\,

\displaystyle h^{n}-\Delta t\sum_{j=1}^{i-1}\tilde{a}_{ij}\left\{\nabla\cdot{\bf q}^{(j)}+\mu(\varepsilon)\nabla\cdot\left(\sqrt{\frac{h^{\eta+1}}{k^{2}}}\frac{\nabla H}{\sqrt{|\nabla H|}}\right)^{(j)}\right\}

\displaystyle+\Delta t\sum_{j=1}^{i-1}a_{ij}\mu(\varepsilon)\nabla\cdot\left(\sqrt{\frac{h^{\eta+1}}{k^{2}}}\frac{\nabla H}{\sqrt{|\nabla H|}}\right)^{(j)},

(2.22b)

\displaystyle{\bf q}^{(i)}_{\star}=\,

\displaystyle{\bf q}^{n}-\Delta t\sum_{j=1}^{i-1}\tilde{a}_{ij}\nabla\cdot\left(\frac{{\bf q}\otimes{\bf q}}{h}\right)^{(j)}-\Delta t\sum_{j=1}^{i-1}a_{ij}\left\{\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{2}}\nabla\left(\frac{g}{2}h^{2}\right)+\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{2}}gh\nabla b+\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{2}}\gamma{\bf q}\right\}^{(j)},

and for
U^{(i)}
in (
2.20
), they are

(2.23a)

\displaystyle h^{(i)}=h_{\star}^{(i)}+a_{ii}\Delta t\mu(\varepsilon)\nabla\cdot\left(\sqrt{\frac{h^{\eta+1}}{k^{2}}}\frac{\nabla H}{\sqrt{|\nabla H|}}\right)^{(i)},

(2.23b)

\displaystyle{\bf q}^{(i)}={\bf q}_{\star}^{(i)}-a_{ii}\frac{\Delta t}{\varepsilon^{2}}\left\{\nabla\left(\frac{g}{2}h^{2}\right)+gh\nabla b+\gamma{\bf q}\right\}^{(i)}.

The nonlinear system (
2.23a
) is also solved for
h^{(i)}
by using a simple Picard iteration, which is almost the same as the first order scheme.
The updating
{\bf q}^{(i)}
is similar to (
2.17
), here it reads

(2.24)

{\bf q}^{(i)}=\frac{2\varepsilon^{2}{\bf q}_{\star\star}^{(i)}}{\varepsilon^{2}+\sqrt{\varepsilon^{4}+\frac{4a_{ii}\Delta tgk^{2}}{(h^{(i)})^{\eta}}|\varepsilon^{2}{\bf q}_{\star\star}^{(i)}|}},

with

\varepsilon^{2}{\bf q}_{\star\star}^{(i)}=\varepsilon^{2}{\bf q}_{\star}^{(i)}-a_{ii}\Delta t\left\{\nabla\left(\frac{g}{2}h^{2}\right)+gh\nabla b\right\}^{(i)}.

For the high order IMEX RK scheme, as discussed in
[
10
,
11
]
,
a stability property is needed to ensure that the scheme is both AP and AA.
Specifically, we require that the IMEX RK scheme is
globally stiffly accurate
(GSA) with
\tilde{\mathbf{b}}^{T}={\bf e}_{s}^{T}\tilde{A}
for the explicit part of the scheme and
\mathbf{b}^{T}={\bf e}_{s}^{T}A
for the implicit one where
{\bf e}_{s}=(0,\cdots,0,1)^{T}

[
25
]
. Moreover, when the IMEX RK scheme is GSA, the numerical solution coincides with the last stage of the method, i.e.,
U^{n+1}=U^{(s)}
. From now on, we only consider GSA IMEX RK schemes.

2.3.
High order spatial discretizations

For spatial approximations of (
2.9
), we follow
[
28
]
by using a finite difference WENO reconstruction for convective terms, while a high order compact difference method for second order diffusion terms. To preserve a hydrostatic equilibrium state, a well-balanced reconstruction
[
46
]
between the gradient of pressure and bottom topography is adopted. Unlike the work
[
28
]
, we do not need to form an elliptic equation for the system (
1.7
) in the diffusive scaling.

Without loss of generality, we take the 2D problem of (
2.9
) as an example to describe our spatial approximations. 1D and higher dimensions can be discretized similarly.
Denoting
U=(h,hu,hv)^{T}
, the fluxes in (
2.9
) can be written as

\displaystyle F(U)=F_{x}(U)+F_{y}(U),

\displaystyle G(U)=G_{x}(U)+G_{y}(U),

with

\displaystyle F_{x}(U)=\left(-(hu)_{x}-\mu(\varepsilon)\left(\sqrt{\frac{h^{\eta+1}}{k^{2}}}\frac{H_{x}}{\sqrt{H_{x}+H_{y}}}\right)_{x},\,\,-(hu^{2})_{x},\,\,-(huv)_{x}\right)^{T},

\displaystyle F_{y}(U)=\left(-(hv)_{y}-\mu(\varepsilon)\left(\sqrt{\frac{h^{\eta+1}}{k^{2}}}\frac{H_{y}}{\sqrt{H_{x}+H_{y}}}\right)_{y},\,\,-(huv)_{y},\,\,-(hv^{2})_{y}\right)^{T},

\displaystyle G_{x}(U)=\left(\mu(\varepsilon)\left(\sqrt{\frac{h^{\eta+1}}{k^{2}}}\frac{H_{x}}{\sqrt{H_{x}+H_{y}}}\right)_{x},\,\,-\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{2}}\left(\frac{g}{2}h^{2}\right)_{x}-\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{2}}ghb_{x}-\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{2}}\frac{gk^{2}\sqrt{(hu)^{2}+(hv)^{2}}}{h^{\eta}}(hu),\,\,0\right)^{T},

\displaystyle G_{y}(U)=\left(\mu(\varepsilon)\left(\sqrt{\frac{h^{\eta+1}}{k^{2}}}\frac{H_{y}}{\sqrt{H_{x}+H_{y}}}\right)_{y},\,\,0,\,\,-\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{2}}\left(\frac{g}{2}h^{2}\right)_{y}-\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{2}}ghb_{y}-\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{2}}\frac{gk^{2}\sqrt{(hu)^{2}+(hv)^{2}}}{h^{\eta}}(hu)\right)^{T}.

For simplicity, we consider a rectangular domain
\Omega=[a,b]\times[c,d]
, with a uniform Cartesian partition. Let
\Delta x=\frac{b-a}{N_{x}}
and
\Delta y=\frac{d-c}{N_{y}}
be the mesh sizes along the
x
and
y
directions respectively, where
N_{x}
and
N_{y}
are the number of cells correspondingly.
The cell is denoted as
I_{ij}=[x_{i-\frac{1}{2}},x_{i+\frac{1}{2}}]\times[y_{j-\frac{1}{2}},y_{j+\frac{1}{2}}]
with
i=1,2,\ldots,N_{x}
and
j=1,2,\ldots,N_{y}
, where
a=x_{\frac{1}{2}}<x_{\frac{3}{2}}<\cdots<x_{N_{x}+\frac{1}{2}}=b
and
c=y_{\frac{1}{2}}<y_{\frac{3}{2}}<\cdots<y_{N_{y}+\frac{1}{2}}=d
.
Now we set the grid point
(x_{i},y_{j})
as
x_{i}=\frac{1}{2}(x_{i-\frac{1}{2}}+x_{i+\frac{1}{2}})
and
y_{j}=\frac{1}{2}(y_{j-\frac{1}{2}}+y_{j+\frac{1}{2}})
, located at each cell center. We denote
\omega_{i,j}
as the value at the point
(x_{i},y_{j})
, where
\omega
is
h
or
{\bf q}
.
Correspondingly
\omega^{\pm}_{i+\frac{1}{2},j}
,
\omega^{\pm}_{i,j+\frac{1}{2}}
represent cell interface values at
(x_{i+\frac{1}{2}},y_{j})
and
(x_{i},y_{j+\frac{1}{2}})
from the left and right limits, respectively. A high order finite difference WENO reconstruction will be used for those convective terms, which is done in a dimension-by-dimension way for the 2D problems:

•

WENO reconstruction with a Lax-Friedrichs flux splitting.

Here we briefly review a finite difference WENO reconstruction with a Lax-Friedrichs flux splitting for the convective terms

\nabla\cdot{\bf q}
and
\nabla\cdot\left(\frac{{\bf q}\otimes{\bf q}}{h}\right)
, we refer to
[
29
,
40
]
for more details. Taking
\nabla\cdot{\bf q}
as an example, it is in the form

(2.25)

\nabla\cdot{\bf q}=(hu)_{x}+(hv)_{y},

and we approximate them with a
k
th order finite difference reconstruction, namely

(2.26a)

\displaystyle(hu)_{x}\big|_{(x_{i},y_{j})}=\frac{1}{\Delta x}\left\{\widehat{(hu)}_{i+\frac{1}{2},j}-\widehat{(hu)}_{i-\frac{1}{2},j}\right\}+\mathcal{O}(\Delta x^{k}),

(2.26b)

\displaystyle(hv)_{y}\big|_{(x_{i},y_{j})}=\frac{1}{\Delta y}\left\{\widehat{(hv)}_{i,j+\frac{1}{2}}-\widehat{(hv)}_{i,j-\frac{1}{2}}\right\}+\mathcal{O}(\Delta y^{k}).

{\widehat{(hu)}_{i+\frac{1}{2},j}}
and
\widehat{(hv)}_{i,j+\frac{1}{2}}
are obtained by a high order finite difference WENO reconstruction.
For example,
{\widehat{(hu)}_{i+\frac{1}{2},j}}
is decomposed into the following two parts

(2.27)

\widehat{(hu)}_{i+\frac{1}{2},j}=\widehat{(hu)}_{i+\frac{1}{2},j}^{+}+\widehat{(hu)}_{i+\frac{1}{2},j}^{-}.

\widehat{(hu)}_{i+\frac{1}{2},j}^{+}
and
\widehat{(hu)}_{i+\frac{1}{2},j}^{-}
are reconstructed by using upwind and downwind information based on a Lax-Friedrichs flux splitting, with

(2.28)

(hu)_{i,j}^{\pm}=\frac{1}{2}\left\{(hu)_{i,j}\pm\Lambda H\right\},\quad\Lambda=\max\left\{|{\bf u}|+\min\{1,\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{2}}c\}\right\}.

For example, for a fifth order WENO reconstruction, the upwind flux
\widehat{(hu)}_{i+\frac{1}{2},j}^{+}
is obtained from
\{(hu)_{i-2,j}^{+},(hu)_{i-1,j}^{+},(hu)_{i,j}^{+},(hu)_{i+1,j}^{+},(hu)_{i+2,j}^{+}\}
, while the downwind flux
\widehat{(hu)}_{i+\frac{1}{2},j}^{-}
is obtained based on
\{(hu)_{i-1,j}^{-},(hu)_{i,j}^{-},(hu)_{i+1,j}^{-},(hu)_{i+2,j}^{-},(hu)_{i+3,j}^{-}\}
, respectively.
Note that here we take the surface level
H
in (
2.28
) for numerical viscosity, which in the hydrostatic state (
1.10
) will not introduce any numerical dissipation, so that can help to preserve such an equilibrium steady state.

(hv)_{i,j}^{\pm}
can be obtained similarly, and it is also similar for the flux difference approximation of
\nabla\cdot\left(\frac{{\bf q}\otimes{\bf q}}{h}\right)
. We denote such a WENO reconstruction as
\nabla_{LW}
.

•

Well-balanced WENO reconstrction.

If initially the water is at a hydrostatic state (
1.10
), such an equilibrium will be preserved for later times. Numerically it is also very important to keep such an equilibrium state. Many well-balanced methods have been proposed
[
34
,
53
,
35
,
46
,
45
]
. Here we follow
[
46
]
to obtain a well-balanced WENO reconstruction. For the system, in order to preserve
{\bf q}=\mathbf{0}
when
H
is a constant, it is important to preserve

\nabla\left(\frac{g}{2}h^{2}\right)=-gh\nabla b.

The main idea is to split the right side as

\nabla\left(\frac{g}{2}h^{2}\right)=-gh\nabla b=-gH\nabla b+\nabla\left(\frac{g}{2}b^{2}\right),

and apply the same WENO reconstruction (with exactly the same nonlinear weights) of
\nabla\left(\frac{g}{2}h^{2}\right)
to
\nabla b
and
\nabla\left(\frac{g}{2}b^{2}\right)
, so that

\nabla\left(\frac{g}{2}h^{2}\right)+gH\nabla b-\nabla\left(\frac{g}{2}b^{2}\right)=\nabla\left(\frac{g}{2}H^{2}\right)

also holds at the discrete level, when
H
is a constant.
Such a WENO reconstruction is denoted as
\nabla_{W}^{WB}
.

•

Compact central difference discretization for diffusive terms

For the diffusive terms in (
2.9
), a compact central difference discretization will be applied
[
10
,
11
,
28
]
. For example,
a fourth order central difference for
(a(x,y)H_{x})_{x}
at
(x_{i},y_{j})
takes the following form

(2.29)

\displaystyle(a(x,y)H_{x})_{x}\Big|_{(x_{i},y_{j})}=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\Delta x^{2}}{\bf a}_{i,j}\begin{pmatrix}-25/144&1/3&-1/4&1/9&-1/48\\
1/6&5/9&-1&1/3&-1/18\\
0&0&0&0&0\\
-1/18&1/3&-1&5/9&1/6\\
-1/48&1/9&-1/4&1/3&-25/144\end{pmatrix}{\bf H}^{T}_{i,j}

\displaystyle+\mathcal{O}(\Delta x^{4}),

with the two vectors being

\displaystyle{\bf a}_{i,j}

\displaystyle=(a_{i-2,j},a_{i-1,j},a_{i,j},a_{i+1,j},a_{i+2,j}),

\displaystyle{\bf H}_{i,j}

\displaystyle=(H_{i-2,j},H_{i-1,j},H_{i,j},H_{i+1,j},H_{i+2,j}),

similarly for
(a(x,y)H_{y})_{y}
along the
y
direction. The function
a(x,y)
represents the coefficient
\sqrt{\frac{h^{\eta+1}}{k^{2}}}\frac{1}{\sqrt{|\nabla H|}}
. For the gradient
|\nabla H|
in the coefficient of the diffusive terms, as no upwind information is available, so a WENO reconstruction without numerical viscosity (namely
\Lambda=0
as in (
2.28
)) is used, which we denote as
|\nabla_{W}H|
.
We note that as we observed numerically a WENO reconstruction of
|\nabla H|
is important to control numerical oscillations, especially when the solution is not smooth.

2.4.
Algorithm flowchart

Now we will summarize our proposed scheme, with high order finite difference WENO recontructions and compact central difference discretizations, coupled with high order IMEX RK schemes, which is AP and well-balanced for the SWEs with a nonflat bottom topology and Manning friction (
1.7
).
The algorithm flowchart is as follows.

(1)

Start from the time level
t^{n}
, with
U^{n}

from (

(2.30a)

\displaystyle h_{\star}^{(i)}=\,

\displaystyle+\Delta t\sum_{j=1}^{i-1}a_{ij}\mu(\varepsilon)\nabla\cdot\left(\sqrt{\frac{h^{\eta+1}}{k^{2}}}\frac{\nabla H}{\sqrt{|\nabla_{W}H|}}\right)^{(j)},

(2.30b)

\displaystyle{\bf q}^{(i)}_{\star}=\,

\displaystyle{\bf q}^{n}-\Delta t\sum_{j=1}^{i-1}\tilde{a}_{ij}\nabla_{LW}\cdot\left(\frac{{\bf q}\otimes{\bf q}}{h}\right)^{(j)}-\Delta t\sum_{j=1}^{i-1}a_{ij}\left\{\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{2}}\left[\nabla_{W}^{WB}\left(\frac{g}{2}h^{2}\right)\right.\right.

\displaystyle\left.\left.+g(h+b)\nabla_{W}^{WB}b-\nabla_{W}^{WB}\left(\frac{g}{2}b^{2}\right)\right]+\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{2}}\frac{gk^{2}|{\bf q}|}{h^{\eta}}{\bf q}\right\}^{(j)}.

(2)

Then we solve
U_{I}^{(i)}
from (
2.20
):

(a)

U_{I}^{(i)}
can be expressed as

(2.31a)

\displaystyle h^{(i)}=\,

\displaystyle h_{\star}^{(i)}+a_{ii}\mu(\varepsilon)\Delta t\nabla\cdot\left(\sqrt{\frac{h^{\eta+1}}{k^{2}}}\frac{\nabla H}{\sqrt{|\nabla_{W}H|}}\right)^{(i)},

(2.31b)

\displaystyle{\bf q}^{(i)}=\,

\displaystyle{\bf q}_{\star}^{(i)}-\Delta ta_{ii}\left\{\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{2}}\left[\nabla_{W}^{WB}\left(\frac{g}{2}h^{2}\right)+g(h+b)\nabla_{W}^{WB}b\right.\right.

\displaystyle\left.\left.-\nabla_{W}^{WB}\left(\frac{g}{2}b^{2}\right)\right]+\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{2}}\frac{gk^{2}|{\bf q}|}{(h^{\eta})}{\bf q}\right\}^{(i)}.

(b)

For the system (

from (

h^{(i),0}=h^{(i-1)}
and compute
|\nabla_{W}H^{(i),0}|

\delta

The elliptic operator
\nabla\cdot\left\{\left(\frac{\sqrt{\frac{h^{\eta+1}}{k^{2}}}}{\sqrt{|\nabla_{W}H|}}\right)^{(i),\ell}\nabla H^{(i),\ell+1}\right\}

3.
Asymptotic preserving (AP) and Asymptotically Accurate (AA)

In this section, we will formally show that the first order IMEX scheme (
2.10
) is AP, and the high order IMEX RK scheme (
2.19
)-(
2.21
) is AA.
For both properties, we only consider time discretizations, while keeping space continuous. In accordance with (
2.1
), we adopt the following Chapman-Enskog expansions:

(3.1)

h^{n}({\bf x})=h^{n}_{0}({\bf x})+\mathcal{O}(\varepsilon),\qquad{\bf q}^{n}({\bf x})={\bf q}^{n}_{0}({\bf x})+\mathcal{O}(\varepsilon),

with
H^{n}_{0}({\bf x})=h^{n}_{0}({\bf x})+b({\bf x})
, and

(3.2)

{\bf q}^{n}_{0}=-\left(\sqrt{\frac{h_{0}^{\eta+1}}{k^{2}}}\frac{\nabla H_{0}}{\sqrt{|\nabla H_{0}|}}\right)^{n}.

Here
h^{n}({\bf x})=h({\bf x},t^{n}),\,{\bf q}^{n}({\bf x})={\bf q}({\bf x},t^{n})
, and we say that the initial conditions are
well-prepared
if the condition (
3.1
) and (
3.2
) are satisfied at
n=0
.

3.1.
AP property.

For the first order IMEX scheme (
2.10
), we have

Theorem 3.1
.

As
\varepsilon\rightarrow 0
the first order IMEX scheme (
2.10
) is asymptotic preserving as long as the initial conditions are well-prepared.

Proof.

Such a proof is straightforward by using the mathematical induction.
As
\varepsilon
goes to zero, the system (
2.10
) becomes

(3.3a)

\displaystyle\frac{h_{0}^{n+1}-h_{0}^{n}}{\Delta t}+\nabla\cdot{\bf q}^{n}_{0}+\mu(0)\nabla\cdot\left(\sqrt{\frac{h_{0}^{\eta+1}}{k^{2}}}\frac{\nabla H_{0}}{\sqrt{|\nabla H_{0}|}}\right)^{n}=\mu(0)\nabla\cdot\left(\sqrt{\frac{h_{0}^{\eta+1}}{k^{2}}}\frac{\nabla H_{0}}{\sqrt{|\nabla H_{0}|}}\right)^{n+1},

(3.3b)

\displaystyle\nabla\left(\frac{g}{2}h_{0}^{2}\right)^{n+1}=-gh_{0}^{n+1}\nabla b-\frac{gk^{2}|{\bf q}_{0}^{n+1}|}{h_{0}^{\eta}}{\bf q}_{0}^{n+1}.

From (
3.3b
), following (
2.4
)-(
2.6
), we obtain

(3.4)

{\bf q}_{0}^{n+1}=-\left(\sqrt{\frac{h_{0}^{\eta+1}}{k^{2}}}\frac{\nabla H_{0}}{\sqrt{|\nabla H_{0}|}}\right)^{n+1}.

Substituting the relation of (
3.4
) at time level
t^{n}
into (
3.3a
), since
\mu(0)=1
, as
\varepsilon\to 0
we get

(3.5)

\frac{h_{0}^{n+1}-h_{0}^{n}}{\Delta t}=\nabla\cdot\left(\sqrt{\frac{h_{0}^{\eta+1}}{k^{2}}}\frac{\nabla H_{0}}{\sqrt{|\nabla H_{0}|}}\right)^{n+1},

which is a consistent implicit time discretization to the limiting equation (
2.7
). Starting from well-prepared initial conditions (
3.1
) at
n=0
, by the mathematical induction, we can see the AP property holds for all later time
t^{n}
.
∎

3.2.
AA property.

Similarly as the AP property for the first order IMEX scheme (
2.10
), here we focus on the AA analysis for high order IMEX RK schemes (
2.19
)-(
2.21
). In what follows, we recognize that the GSA condition is crucial to guarantee the AA property.

Theorem 3.2
.

Consider applying an IMEX RK scheme (
2.19
)-(
2.21
) of order
p
. Assume that the IMEX RK method is GSA and the the initial conditions are well-prepared. Let us denote by
{\bf U}^{1}({\bf x};\varepsilon)=(h^{1}({\bf x};\varepsilon),{\bf q}^{1}({\bf x};\varepsilon))^{T}
the numerical solution after one time step and
{\bf U}^{exa}({\bf x},t)=(h^{exa}({\bf x},t),{\bf q}^{exa}({\bf x},t))^{T}
the exact solution of the limiting equation (
2.7
). Then after one step, the error between the numerical and exact solution satisfies the following estimate

(3.6)

\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\,{\bf U}^{1}({\bf x};\varepsilon)={\bf U}^{exc}({\bf x},\Delta t)+\mathcal{O}(\Delta t^{p+1}),

namely, the scheme is AA.

Proof.

We prove it by using the mathematical induction for time stages. If we assume (
3.1
) and (
3.2
) hold at
t^{n}
, from the AP property of the first order IMEX scheme (
2.10
), we can get (
3.4
) and (
3.5
) hold for a time step
a_{11}\Delta t
at the first stage. Then if we assume this formal convergence holds for the
(i-1)
-th stage, we will prove it also holds for the
i
-th stage. We have:

(1)

for
U_{\star}^{(i)}
in (
2.22
), as
\varepsilon\rightarrow 0
, it becomes

\displaystyle h_{\star}^{(i)}=\,

\displaystyle h_{0}^{n}-\Delta t\sum_{j=1}^{i-1}\tilde{a}_{ij}\left\{\nabla\cdot{\bf q}_{0}^{(j)}+\mu(0)\nabla\cdot\left(\sqrt{\frac{h_{0}^{\eta+1}}{k^{2}}}\frac{\nabla H_{0}}{\sqrt{|\nabla H_{0}|}}\right)^{(j)}\right\},

\displaystyle+\Delta t\sum_{j=1}^{i-1}a_{ij}\mu(0)\nabla\cdot\left(\sqrt{\frac{h_{0}^{\eta+1}}{k^{2}}}\frac{\nabla H_{0}}{\sqrt{|\nabla H_{0}|}}\right)^{(j)},

\displaystyle{\bf q}^{(i)}_{\star}=\,

\displaystyle{\bf q}_{0}^{n}-\Delta t\sum_{j=1}^{i-1}\tilde{a}_{ij}\nabla\cdot\left(\frac{{\bf q}_{0}\otimes{\bf q}_{0}}{h_{0}}\right)^{(j)}.

Since

{\bf q}_{0}^{(j)}=-\left(\sqrt{\frac{h_{0}^{\eta+1}}{k^{2}}}\frac{\nabla H_{0}}{\sqrt{|\nabla H_{0}|}}\right)^{(j)},\quad 0\leq j\leq i-1,

and
\mu(0)=1
as
\varepsilon\to 0
,

U^{(i)}_{\star}
becomes

\displaystyle h_{\star}^{(i)}=\,

\displaystyle h_{0}^{n}+\Delta t\sum_{j=1}^{i-1}a_{ij}\nabla\cdot\left(\sqrt{\frac{h_{0}^{\eta+1}}{k^{2}}}\frac{\nabla H_{0}}{\sqrt{|\nabla H_{0}|}}\right)^{(j)},

\displaystyle{\bf q}^{(i)}_{\star}=\,

\displaystyle{\bf q}_{0}^{n}-\Delta t\sum_{j=1}^{i-1}\tilde{a}_{ij}\nabla\cdot\left(\frac{{\bf q}_{0}\otimes{\bf q}_{0}}{h_{0}}\right)^{(j)}.

(2)

for
U^{(i)}
in (
2.23
), as
\varepsilon\rightarrow 0
, first from (
2.23b
) we get

\nabla\left(\frac{g}{2}(h_{0}^{(i)})^{2}\right)+gh_{0}^{(i)}\nabla b+\gamma^{(i)}_{0}{\bf q}_{0}^{(i)}=0,

with
\gamma^{(i)}_{0}=\dfrac{gk^{2}|{\bf q}_{0}^{(i)}|}{(h_{0}^{\eta})^{(i)}}
, so that

{\bf q}_{0}^{(i)}=-\left(\sqrt{\frac{h_{0}^{\eta+1}}{k^{2}}}\frac{\nabla H_{0}}{\sqrt{|\nabla H_{0}|}}\right)^{(i)},

and (
2.23a
) becomes

h_{0}^{(i)}=h_{\star}^{(i)}+\Delta ta_{ii}\nabla\cdot\left(\sqrt{\frac{h_{0}^{\eta+1}}{k^{2}}}\frac{\nabla H_{0}}{\sqrt{|\nabla H_{0}|}}\right)^{(i)}=h_{0}^{n}+\Delta t\sum_{j=1}^{i}a_{ij}\nabla\cdot\left(\sqrt{\frac{h_{0}^{\eta+1}}{k^{2}}}\frac{\nabla H_{0}}{\sqrt{|\nabla H_{0}|}}\right)^{(j)}.

Therefore, the formal convergence also holds for the stage

.

With the inner stage

up to
s
, and given that the IMEX RK scheme is GSA, we update the solution

,
40
]

5.
Conclusion

In this paper, we have proposed a high order AP finite difference WENO scheme for the SWEs with Manning friction and a bottom topology. The scheme is proved to be AP, AA and well-balanced, which are also confirmed by one- and two-dimensional numerical tests. In this work, the diffusion limit are added as a penalization, and an additive IMEX RK method has been utilized. In the future, we may further consider a semi-implicit approach without adding the diffusion terms.

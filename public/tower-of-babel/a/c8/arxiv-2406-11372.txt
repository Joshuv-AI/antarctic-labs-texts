Universal spectrum of isolated three-body resonances
Ludovic Pricoupenko

Abstract
The exact wavefunction of an isolated three-body resonance at finite scattering length is obtained for two identical particles interacting with another one via a pairwise zero-range potential. The corresponding universal spectrum is studied as a function of the scattering length. The universality of the results is illustrated by considering a model with finite-range interactions.

I
Introduction

The search for universal solutions to the quantum three-body problem with short range interactions began with the pioneering work of Skorniakov and Ter-Martirosian (STM) on the neutron-deuteron scattering problem
[
1
]
. A series of fascinating results followed, culminating in the Efimov effect
[
2
,
3
,
4
,
5
,
6
]
. Universality means that, regardless of the actual interactions of the system that define what can be called a ’reference model’, the low-energy properties can be described using a few parameters. The reference model can therefore be replaced by a contact model defined by zero-range interactions, where for a given energy, the wavefunction is set by these few parameters. Universality happens when the dominant interaction between particles is close to the unitarity of two-body s-wave scattering, such as the neutron-neutron interaction and has led to recent predictions in nuclear physics
[
7
]
. Universality is also an important line of research in ultracold physics, which offers the possibility of tuning the scattering length and reaching unitarity with Feshbach resonances
[
8
,
9
]
. This led to the first observation of the Efimov effect
[
10
]
. Alongside dramatic experimental progress and a detailed analysis of the Efimov effect
[
11
]
, one can point to remarkable theoretical results in the three-dimensional case
[
12
,
13
,
14
,
15
,
16
,
17
,
18
]
.

Apart from the Efimov effect, which has been extensively studied, Isolated Three-Body Resonances (ITBR) close to unitarity have received less attention despite their universal character. The term ’isolated’ resonance refers to the fact that when the resonance occurs at the three-body threshold for a given scattering length, the spectrum reduces to an isolated point, whilst the Efimov effect at unitarity is characterized by an accumulation point at zero energy. The universality follows from the scale invariance of the wavefunction in a domain of short distances. The associated scaling exponent denoted in what follows by
{s}
, depends only on the configuration (angular momentum, quantum statistics and mass ratio). The Efimov effect occurs when
{s}
is imaginary whatever the short range details of the actual interactions with an infinite number of states at unitarity. In deep contrast, an ITBR may occur in all other configurations where the scaling exponent is real and positive but only for specific interactions
[
19
,
20
]
moreover there is only one shallow state at unitarity. From this perspective, Efimov states and ITBR encompass all possible universal physics for Borromean systems with short-range s-wave resonant interactions. A first result was found at unitarity and for small values of the scaling exponent
{s}
with a one-parameter spectrum law in Refs.
[
21
,
22
]
. For arbitrary
{s}
, it has been shown that the spectrum at unitarity is defined by two three-body parameters, whereas the Efimov spectrum is a one parameter law
[
23
,
24
]
.

In the present work, the universal spectrum of ITBR is derived as a function of the scattering length
{a}
for three types of systems in configurations without Efimov effect. They can consist of two identical particles (mass
{M}
) each interacting with an impurity of mass
{m}
close to a s-wave resonance (i.e. the particle-impurity scattering length
{a}
is large). They are denoted as 2FI systems when the two identical particles are fermions and 2BI systems when they are bosons (with a negligible mutual
s
wave scattering length). They can also consist of three identical interacting bosons (denoted as 3B systems) of mass
{M}
close to a s-wave resonance but with a non-zero total orbital angular momentum. The main results are as follows:
{i)}
ITBR states are expressed in terms of universal functions obtained from the exact solutions of a Lippmann Schwinger-like equation ;
{ii)}
in the limit of small detuning, the spectrum is obtained analytically for
{s\gtrsim 1.5}
; otherwise, it is deduced from an equation where only one universal parameter is evaluated numerically;
{iii)}
for
{a>0}
, the modification of the Kartavtsev Malyckh (KM) regular states of Refs.
[
16
,
17
]
, due to an ITBR is discussed;
{iv)}
the universality is tested successfully in an example of ITBR, illustrating also a way to evaluate the three-body parameters from a given spectrum.

II
Contact model

The particles of mass
{M}
labelled by
1
or
2
and the particle of mass
m
labelled by
3
, are considered in the center of mass frame. The position and wavevector of the particle
{i}
are
{\mathbf{r}_{i}}
and
{\mathbf{k}_{i}}
. There are three sets of Jacobi coordinates
{\boldsymbol{\rho}=(\boldsymbol{\rho}^{(ij)}_{1},\boldsymbol{\rho}^{(ij)}_{2})}
, each associated with a pair
(ij)
. Using the reference mass
{m_{\rm r}=mM/(M+m)}
, the coordinates associated with the pair
(23)
are the relative coordinates
{\boldsymbol{\rho}^{(23)}_{1}=\mathbf{r}_{23}}
and the position of the particle 1 with respect to this pair
{\boldsymbol{\rho}^{(23)}_{2}=\mathbf{r}_{23}\tan\theta-\mathbf{r}_{13}\sec\theta}
where
{\mathbf{r}_{ij}=\mathbf{r}_{j}-\mathbf{r}_{i}}
and
{\theta=\arcsin(m_{\rm r}/m)}
. The coordinates
{(\boldsymbol{\rho}^{(12)}_{1},\boldsymbol{\rho}^{(12)}_{2})}
[or
{(\boldsymbol{\rho}^{(31)}_{1},\boldsymbol{\rho}^{(31)}_{2})}
] are deduced from
{(\boldsymbol{\rho}^{(23)}_{1},\boldsymbol{\rho}^{(23)}_{2})}
by using a
{2\times 2}
active rotation matrix of angle
{\frac{3\pi}{4}-\frac{\theta}{2}}
[or
{(-\frac{\pi}{2}-\theta)}
]. The hyperradius
{\rho=\|\boldsymbol{\rho}\|}
that measures the relative distance between the three particles, is thus the same for the three sets. One has also the properties
{\boldsymbol{\rho}^{(ij)}_{1}=-\boldsymbol{\rho}^{(ji)}_{1}}
and
{\boldsymbol{\rho}^{(ji)}_{2}=\boldsymbol{\rho}^{(ij)}_{2}}
. More details on the Jacobi coordinates are given in Appendix
A
. The interaction between two particles
{(ij)}
is modeled by the Wigner-Bethe-Peierls contact condition when
{\rho^{(ij)}_{1}\to 0}
for a given
{\boldsymbol{\rho}^{(ij)}_{2}}
:

\langle\boldsymbol{\rho}|\Psi\rangle=\langle\boldsymbol{\rho}^{(ij)}_{2}|A_{ij}\rangle\left(\frac{1}{a}-\frac{1}{\rho^{(ij)}_{1}}\right)+O(\rho^{(ij)}_{1}).

(1)

It is convenient to introduce a statistical factor
{S}
related to the exchange symmetry:
{|A_{12}\rangle=0}
,
{|A_{23}\rangle=-|A_{13}\rangle}
with
{S=-1}
for the 2FI system;
{|A_{12}\rangle=0}
,
{|A_{13}\rangle=|A_{23}\rangle}
with
{S=1}
for the 2BI system and
{|A_{12}\rangle=|A_{13}\rangle=|A_{23}\rangle}
with
{S=2}
for the 3B system. Isotropy allows one to isolate each component of angular momentum
{\ell\hbar}
:

\langle\boldsymbol{\rho}_{2}^{(23)}|A_{23}\rangle=\langle\rho_{2}^{(23)}|\mathcal{A}_{\ell}\rangle P_{\ell}(\hat{\mathbf{e}}_{z}\cdot\boldsymbol{\rho}_{2}^{(23)}/\rho_{2}^{(23)}).

(2)

At unitarity (
{|a|=\infty}
), the contact conditions (
1
) is scale invariant so that the wavefunction is separable
[
15
]
:

\langle\boldsymbol{\rho}|\Psi\rangle{=}\frac{\langle\rho|\mathcal{A}_{\ell}\rangle}{\rho}\Phi_{\ell}\left(\frac{\boldsymbol{\rho}}{\rho}\right),

(3)

where
{\Phi_{\ell}(\boldsymbol{\rho}/\rho)}
is a normalized eigenstate of the Laplacian on the unit hypersphere with the orbital momentum
{\ell\hbar}
. The contact conditions (
1
) gives the eigenvalues
{4-\gamma^{2}}
. The possible values of
{\gamma}
are functions of the mass ratio
{M/m}
(or of
{\theta}
) and of
{\ell}

[
15
]
. Using this solution in the Schrödinger equation for
{\langle\boldsymbol{\rho}|\Psi\rangle}
, one finds that
{\rho\langle\rho|\mathcal{A}_{\ell}\rangle}
satisfies a radial 2D Schrödinger equation with an effective centrifugal barrier
{\hbar^{2}\gamma^{2}/(2m_{\rm r}\rho^{2})}
. For given
{S}
and
{M/m}
, an ITBR can exist only for
{\ell}
such that all the eigenvalues
{\gamma^{2}}
are positive. The smallest value of
{|\gamma|}
is the scaling exponent
s
which corresponds to the lowest centrifugal barrier. In a contact model, an ITBR is then characterized by the singularity
{\langle\rho|\mathcal{A}_{\ell}\rangle\propto\rho^{-1-s}}
when
{\rho\to 0}
. At unitarity the hyperradial function for a bound state of energy
{E=-{\hbar^{2}q^{2}}/{(2m_{\rm r})}}
is

\langle\rho|\mathcal{A}_{\ell}\rangle\propto\frac{K_{s}(q\rho)}{\rho}.

(4)

At finite scattering length, the function
{\rho\langle\rho|\mathcal{A}_{\ell}\rangle}
still satisfies this 2D Schrödinger equation but only for
{\rho\ll|a|}
. In physical systems, an ITBR follows from the existence of actual attractive interactions that truncate the centrifugal barrier in a region of short hyperradius
{\rho<R_{0}}
. The length
{R_{0}}
is defined such that for interparticle distances larger than
{R_{0}}
, the wavefunction of the reference model,
{\langle\boldsymbol{\rho}|\Psi^{\rm ref}\rangle}
almost coincides with
{\langle\boldsymbol{\rho}|\Psi\rangle}
. Hence, in the separability region
{R_{0}{\lesssim}\rho\ll|a|}
,
{\langle\boldsymbol{\rho}|\Psi^{\rm ref}\rangle}
factorizes as in Eq. (
3
) with
{\langle\rho|\mathcal{A}_{\ell}^{\rm ref}\rangle\simeq\langle\rho|\mathcal{A}_{\ell}\rangle}
.

To take advantage of translational invariance, it is convenient to consider the reciprocal space with the wavevectors
{(\boldsymbol{\kappa}^{(ij)}_{1},\boldsymbol{\kappa}^{(ij)}_{2})}
that conjugate
{(\boldsymbol{\rho}^{(ij)}_{1},\boldsymbol{\rho}^{(ij)}_{2})}
:

\boldsymbol{\kappa}^{(ij)}_{1}\cdot\boldsymbol{\rho}^{(ij)}_{1}+\boldsymbol{\kappa}^{(ij)}_{2}\cdot\boldsymbol{\rho}^{(ij)}_{2}=\mathbf{k}_{1}\cdot\mathbf{r}_{1}+\mathbf{k}_{2}\cdot\mathbf{r}_{2}+\mathbf{k}_{3}\cdot\mathbf{r}_{3}.

(5)

More details about these wavevectors are given in Appendix
A
. One obtains the function
{\langle k|\mathcal{A}_{\ell}\rangle}
from
{\langle\rho|\mathcal{A}_{\ell}\rangle}
in terms of the Hankel transform:

\langle k|\mathcal{A}_{\ell}\rangle=\int_{0}^{\infty}\rho^{2}j_{\ell}(k\rho)\langle\rho|\mathcal{A}_{\ell}\rangle d\rho.

(6)

Notice that in the reciprocal space, the reference state
{|A^{\rm ref}_{23}\rangle}
analogous to
{|A_{23}\rangle}
which is associated with the stationary state
{|\Psi^{\rm ref}\rangle}
of the reference model is defined by

\langle\boldsymbol{\kappa}_{2}^{(23)}|A^{\rm ref}_{23}\rangle=\frac{m_{\rm r}}{2\pi\hbar^{2}}\lim_{\kappa_{1}^{(23)}\to 0}\langle\boldsymbol{\kappa}_{1}^{(23)},\boldsymbol{\kappa}_{2}^{(23)}|V_{23}|\Psi^{\rm ref}\rangle

(7)

where
V_{23}
is the actual interaction in the reference model between particles
2
and
3
. For an angular momentum
\ell
, one has

\langle\mathbf{k}|A_{23}^{\rm ref}\rangle=\langle k|\mathcal{A}_{\ell}^{\rm ref}\rangle P_{\ell}(\hat{\mathbf{e}}_{z}\cdot\mathbf{k}/k).

(8)

III
Universal functions solutions of the STM equation

For a bound state of binding wavenumber
q
, the two-body contact conditions in Eq. (
1
) lead to the STM equation:

\langle k|\mathcal{L}_{\ell,q}^{\infty}|\mathcal{A}_{\ell}\rangle=\frac{\langle k|\mathcal{A}_{\ell}\rangle}{a},

(9)

where
{\mathcal{L}_{\ell,q}^{\infty}}
is the following operator

\langle k|\mathcal{L}_{\ell,q}^{\Lambda}|\mathcal{A}_{\ell}\rangle=\sqrt{k^{2}+q^{2}}\langle k|\mathcal{A}_{\ell}\rangle-\frac{2S(-1)^{\ell}}{\pi\sin(2\theta)}\\
\times\int_{0}^{\Lambda}\frac{k^{\prime}dk^{\prime}}{k}Q_{\ell}\left(\frac{k^{2}+k^{\prime}\,{}^{2}+q^{2}\cos^{2}\theta}{2kk^{\prime}\sin\theta}\right)\langle k^{\prime}|\mathcal{A}_{\ell}\rangle,

(10)

considered at
{\Lambda=\infty}
. A derivation of the STM equation is given in Appendix
B
. It is convenient to introduce the reduced wavenumber
{z={k}/{q}}
and the dimensionless operator
{\mathcal{L}^{\Lambda}}
obtained by substituting
{q\to 1}
and
{(k,k^{\prime})\to(z,z^{\prime})}
in Eq. (
10
). The solutions of Eq. (
9
) can be then expressed in terms of universal functions
{\langle z|\phi_{\ell,s,\tau}\rangle\equiv\langle k|\mathcal{A}_{\ell}\rangle}
that satisfy

\langle z|\mathcal{L}^{\infty}-\tau|\phi_{\ell,s,\tau}\rangle=0\quad\text{with}\quad\tau=\frac{1}{qa}<1.

(11)

The asymptotic behavior
{\langle z|\phi_{\ell,s,\tau}\rangle=O(z^{-2+s})}
when
{z\to\infty}
is obtained by considering the zero energy limit at unitarity where Eq. (
9
) is scale invariant or also from the behavior of
{\rho\langle\rho|\mathcal{A}_{l}\rangle}
when
{\rho\to 0}
. At unitarity
{(\tau=0)}
, the universal functions are obtained directly from Eqs. (
3
,
4
) and
by using the Hankel transform in Eq. (
6
). They are given in Appendix
D
. Importantly, no ITBR can be found when a cut-off is introduced in the integral of Eq. (
11
)
[
25
]
. This is explained by the underlying centrifugal barrier, which prevents Borromean states from being found if a singularity is not somehow imposed in Eq. (
11
) when
{z\to\infty}
. Instead, standard calculations are only able to recover the KM states which are regular at the three-body contact. They exist for a sufficiently small value of
{s}
and for
{a>0}
, i.e. in the presence of the shallow dimer of energy
{-\hbar^{2}/(2m_{\rm r}a^{2})}

[
16
,
17
]
.

The universal functions can be deduced from their expressions at unitarity via a Lippmann Schwinger-like equation
[
26
]
:

|\phi_{\ell,s,\tau}\rangle=|\phi_{\ell,s,0}\rangle+\lim_{\Lambda\to\infty}\frac{\tau}{\mathcal{L}^{\Lambda}-\tau}|\phi_{\ell,s,0}\rangle.

(12)

This latter equation follows from the asymptotic equivalence
{\langle z|\phi_{\ell,s,\tau}\rangle\simeq\langle z|\phi_{\ell,s,0}\rangle}
for
{z\gg 1}
which implies

\langle z|\mathcal{L}^{\infty}-\mathcal{L}^{\Lambda}|\phi_{\ell,s,\tau}\rangle\underset{\Lambda\gg 1,z}{\simeq}\langle z|\mathcal{L}^{\infty}-\mathcal{L}^{\Lambda}|\phi_{\ell,s,0}\rangle,

(13)

so that Eq. (
11
) can be written as:

(\mathcal{L}^{\Lambda}-\tau)|\phi_{\ell,s,\tau}\rangle\underset{\Lambda\gg 1}{=}\mathcal{L}^{\Lambda}|\phi_{\ell,s,0}\rangle

(14)

which gives Eq. (
12
).

IV
Universal spectrum

The spectrum can be found by using a quite general energy dependent log-derivative condition:

\frac{\partial_{\rho}[\rho\langle\rho|\mathcal{A}_{\ell}\rangle]}{\langle\rho|\mathcal{A}_{\ell}\rangle}\biggr|_{\rho=R}=-\upsilon+\upsilon^{\prime}q^{2}R^{2},

(15)

where
{R}
satisfies the condition
{qR\ll 1}
. The calculations are performed at a linear order in energy, which is mandatory when
{s>1}
, as shown in Ref.
[
23
]
. It is therefore natural to introduce the coefficient
{\upsilon^{\prime}}
in the right hand side of Eq. (
15
) as done in Ref.
[
24
]
. It will be shown in sec.
V.5
that in standard situations, the ’best’ choice of the parameters is obtained when
{\upsilon^{\prime}=0}
, so that the two relevant three-body parameters are
{(R,\upsilon)}
. The quantity
{s-\upsilon}
is the detuning from the threshold : this means that at unitarity,
{q=0}
when this detuning is zero. Now consider the expansion:

\rho\langle\rho|\mathcal{A}_{\ell}\rangle=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}c_{\ell,-s,\tau}^{(n)}(q\rho)^{n-s}-{r}_{\ell,s,\tau}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}c_{\ell,s,\tau}^{(n)}(q\rho)^{n+s}

(16)

with the choice of normalization
{c_{\ell,\pm s,\tau}^{(0)}=1}
. This series expansion is just a generalization of the expansion of the modified Bessel function
{K_{s}(q\rho)}
considered in the vicinity of
{\rho=0}
. The two sums in the right-hand-side of Eq. (
16
) are the two independent solutions of Eq. (
11
) when considered in the configuration space. The coefficient
{r_{\ell,s,\tau}}
is such that
{\lim_{\rho\to\infty}\langle\rho|\mathcal{A}_{\ell}\rangle=0}
. It fixes the balance between the irregular and regular series in Eq. (
16
) and will be denoted as the balance coefficient.The coefficients
{c_{\ell,\gamma,\tau}^{(n)}}
in Eq. (
16
) are obtained by recurrence by injecting the Hankel transform (
6
) of Eq. (
16
) in Eq. (
9
). At this stage, it is important to have in mind that
in the contact model,
{\langle\rho|\mathcal{A}_{\ell}\rangle}
coincides with the hyperradial solution of a reference model only asymptotically for
{\rho\gg R}
. In the low energy limit, Eq. (
16
) is truncated at the order
{n=2}
: an approximation compatible with the dependence of the right-hand-side of Eq. (
15
) on the energy. At this order, Eq. (
15
) gives:

\frac{1}{{a_{s}^{\star}}}-q^{2}{R_{s}^{\star}}=\frac{s\Gamma(s)^{2}r_{\ell,s,\tau}}{\pi R}\left(4q^{2}R^{2}\right)^{s},

(17)

where the generalized scattering length
{{a_{s}^{\star}}}
and the range parameter
{{R_{s}^{\star}}}
are defined by:

\displaystyle{a_{s}^{\star}}=\frac{\pi R}{s4^{s}\Gamma(s)^{2}}\left[\frac{\upsilon+s+\sum_{i=1}^{2}\frac{R^{i}}{a^{i}}\left(\upsilon+s+i\right)\frac{dc_{\ell,s,\tau}^{(i)}}{d(\tau^{i})}}{s\leftrightarrow-s}\right]

(18)

\displaystyle{R_{s}^{\star}}=\frac{sR4^{s}(s-\upsilon)\Gamma(s)^{2}}{\pi(s+\upsilon)}\left[\frac{\upsilon^{\prime}+\frac{2+\upsilon-s}{4(s-1)}}{s-\upsilon}-{s\leftrightarrow-s}\right].

(19)

The coefficients
{c_{\ell,s,\tau}^{(i)}}
are given for
{i=1,2}
in Eqs. (
113
,
114
) of the appendix
E
. Terms of order
{(qR)^{3}}
are not included, meaning that for a binding energy
{E<0}
, except if the balance coefficient
{r_{\ell,s,\tau}}
is anomalously large, the right-hand-side of Eq. (
17
) is relevant only for values of the scaling exponent
{s}
smaller than
{\frac{3}{2}}

[
27
]
. When one considers the case where
{\upsilon^{\prime}=0}
, in the limit of small detuning
{\upsilon\to s}
and also near the unitarity
{R/|a|\ll 1}
, the expression of
{a_{s}^{\star}}
can be simplified with:

\frac{1}{{a_{s}^{\star}}}\simeq\frac{s4^{s}\Gamma(s)^{2}}{\pi(\upsilon+s)}\left(\frac{\upsilon-s}{R}+\frac{c_{\ell,-s,\tau}^{(1)}}{a\tau}\right)

(20)

and for
{R_{s}^{\star}}
when
s>1
:

{R_{s}^{\star}}\simeq\frac{R4^{s-1}\Gamma(s)^{2}}{\pi(s-1)}.

(21)

Equations. (
18
,
19
) have been obtained from calculations performed up to the first order in energy. This approach is therefore similar to what is done in the effective range approximation for the two-body problem. However, this analogy is only formal and the approximation which leads to Eq. (
17
), valid in the limit of small three-body energy, has nothing to do with the actual effective range approximation performed at the two-body problem. Indeed, taking into account the effective range at the two-body level for two resonant particles modifies only the STM equation, leading to an additional term
{r_{\rm e}(k^{2}+q^{2})/2}
in the STM operator of Eq. (
10
) or equivalently, an additive term
{qr_{\rm e}(z^{2}+1)/2}
in the dimensionless operator
{\mathcal{L}^{\Lambda}}
(see for instance Ref.
[
28
]
with the substitution
{R^{\star}=-r_{\rm e}/2}
). It is assumed in the present work that the effective range is of the order of the potential radius so that this term can be neglected for
{kr_{\rm e}\ll 1}
. The solutions of this modified STM equation differ from the universal functions when the wavenumber
{k}
is larger than or of the order of
{1/r_{\rm e}}
, a region where also the universal functions differ from the reference functions
{\langle k|\mathcal{A}^{\rm ref}_{\ell}\rangle}
. The log-derivative condition is in general considered at an hyperradius
R
where also these functions differs. Nevertheless, this is not a problem because the log-derivative condition is imposed to give the correct value of the binding wavenumber
{q}
, but the reference function coincides with the reference function only for small wavenumbers
{k}

{(kR\ll 1)}
and absolutely not when
{k}
is of the order of
{1/r_{\rm e}}
. For such large values of the wavenumber the interaction potential is not negligible in the Schrödinger equation and the STM equation cannot model the actual physics.

Solutions of Eq. (
17
) are detailed in the following lines. The pure KM states labelled by
{n=1,2\dots}
are regular solutions of Eq. (
11
) i.e.
{r_{\ell,s,\tau}=\infty}
, corresponding to specific values of
{\tau=\tau_{n}}
. Their number depends on the configuration studied. Even if the following reasoning is generic, in practice it can be implemented only for a sufficiently small value of the scaling exponent such that
{r_{\ell,s,\tau}}
can be evaluated precisely. Each KM state
{(n)}
is associated with an universal branch (it depends solely on the mass ratio and the statistics) of the coefficient
{r_{\ell,s,\tau}}
considered as a function of
{\tau}
, with a vertical and positive asymptote located at
{\tau=\tau_{n}}
. For a given branch and an arbitrarily large and positive scattering length, the pure KM state is found from Eq. (
17
) with
{q=1/(a\tau_{n})}
. For a decreasing value of
{a>0}
, the KM spectrum deviates from this last law with a solution of Eq. (
17
) corresponding to a larger values of
{\tau}
on the same branch of
{r_{\ell,s,\tau}}
. Importantly, the spectrum of the shallowest KM state has an endpoint at the particle-dimer continuum threshold if Eq. (
17
) has a solution at
{q=1/a}
.

Let’s move on to the analysis of the ITBR. At unitarity, the resonance threshold
{(E=0)}
is at
{\upsilon=s}
and a shallow bound state exists when
{s-\upsilon}
is small and negative
[
23
,
24
]
. The spectrum is obtained from Eq. (
17
) by considering the branch of
{r_{\ell,s,\tau}}
starting from
{\tau=-\infty}
corresponding to the endpoint of the spectrum at the three particles continuum for the scattering length
{a=a^{-}}
. In this limit,
{r_{\ell,s,\tau}=B(-\tau)^{2s}}
, where
B
is a coefficient that can be obtained numerically (see the end of the Appendix
E
). The position of the endpoint satisfies the approximate equation:

\upsilon-s+\frac{R}{a^{-}}\frac{c_{\ell,-s,\tau}^{(1)}}{\tau}=(\upsilon+s)B\left(\frac{-R}{a^{-}}\right)^{2s}

(22)

which becomes exact in the limit of a small detuning. In this limit, one obtains

a^{-}\sim\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\frac{R}{s-\upsilon}\frac{c_{\ell,-s,\tau}^{(1)}}{\tau}&\text{for}\quad s>1/2\\
-R\left(\frac{\upsilon+s}{\upsilon-s}B\right)^{\frac{1}{2s}}&\text{for}\quad s<1/2\end{array}\right.

(23)

{s<3/2}
.

{E=E_{\rm r}-{i\Gamma}/{2}}

V.1
Separable model potential

The universality is now illustrated by using a reference model in a 2FI system with finite range interactions. The interaction
{V_{i3}}
between the fermions
{i=1,2}
and the impurity is separable:

V_{i3}=g|\chi\rangle\langle\chi|

(26)

with the form factor

\langle\boldsymbol{\kappa}_{1}^{(i3)}|\chi\rangle=\left[1+\alpha(\kappa_{1}^{(i3)}\epsilon)^{2}\right]\exp[-(\kappa_{1}^{(i3)}\epsilon)^{2}/2]

(27)

whereas the two identical fermions do not interact. The length
{\epsilon}
gives the order of magnitude of the high energy scale
{\hbar^{2}/(m_{\rm r}\epsilon^{2})}
. From Eq. (
7
), one has

\langle\boldsymbol{\kappa}_{2}^{(23)}|A^{\rm ref}\rangle=\frac{m_{\rm r}g}{2\pi\hbar^{2}}\int\frac{d^{3}\kappa^{(23)}_{1}}{(2\pi)^{3}}\langle\chi|\boldsymbol{\kappa}^{(23)}_{1}\rangle\langle\boldsymbol{\kappa}^{(23)}_{1},\boldsymbol{\kappa}_{2}^{(23)}|\Psi^{\rm ref}\rangle.

(28)

In case of bound states, the function
{\langle\mathbf{k}|A^{\rm ref}_{23}\rangle}
satisfies the STM-like equation

\frac{4\pi}{\cos\theta}\int\frac{d^{3}k^{\prime}}{(2\pi)^{3}}\frac{\langle\chi|\frac{\mathbf{k}^{\prime}+\mathbf{k}\sin\theta}{\cos\theta}\rangle\langle\frac{\mathbf{k}^{\prime}\sin\theta+\mathbf{k}}{\cos\theta}|\chi\rangle\langle\mathbf{k}^{\prime}|A^{\rm ref}_{23}\rangle}{k^{\prime 2}+k^{2}+2\mathbf{k}\cdot\mathbf{k}^{\prime}\sin\theta+q^{2}\cos^{2}\theta}\\
+\langle\mathbf{k}|A^{\rm ref}_{23}\rangle\left[-\frac{1}{a}+D(\sqrt{k^{2}+q^{2}})\right]=0

(29)

where

\displaystyle\chi(k)\equiv\langle\mathbf{k}|\chi\rangle=\left(1+\alpha\epsilon^{2}k^{2}\right)\exp(-\epsilon^{2}k^{2}/2),

(30)

\displaystyle D(k)=k\operatornamewithlimits{erfcx}(k\epsilon)+2\alpha k^{2}\epsilon\left[\frac{1}{\sqrt{\pi}}-k\epsilon\operatornamewithlimits{erfcx}(k\epsilon)\right]

\displaystyle\qquad+\alpha^{2}k^{4}\epsilon^{3}\left[k\epsilon\operatornamewithlimits{erfcx}(k\epsilon)-\frac{1}{\sqrt{\pi}}+\frac{1}{2k^{2}\epsilon^{2}\sqrt{\pi}}\right]

(31)

and
{\operatornamewithlimits{erfcx}(u)=e^{u^{2}}\operatornamewithlimits{erfc}(u)}
is the scaled complementary error function. Equation (
29
) differs from the STM equation only for wavenumbers
k
of the order of, or larger than
{1/\epsilon}
. In what follows, one considers the p-wave symmetry sector where
{\ell=1}
and
{\langle\mathbf{k}|A^{\rm ref}_{23}\rangle=(\hat{\mathbf{e}}_{\mathbf{k}}\cdot\hat{\mathbf{e}}_{z})\langle k|\mathcal{A}_{1}^{\rm ref}\rangle}

V.5
Explaining why
{\upsilon^{\prime}=0}
is the best choice in this reference model

If one considers the log-derivative of the reference function at the hyperradius
R_{0}
including a linear energy correction:

\frac{\partial_{\rho}[\rho\langle\rho|\mathcal{A}_{\ell}^{\rm ref}\rangle]}{\langle\rho|\mathcal{A}_{\ell}^{\rm ref}\rangle}\biggr|_{\rho=R_{0}}=-\upsilon_{0}+\upsilon_{0}^{\prime}q^{2}R_{0}^{2},

(33)

then assuming that
{qR_{0}\ll 1}
, the parameter
{\upsilon_{0}^{\prime}}
is directly linked with the probability occupation at short distance
[
24
]
:

\int_{\rho<R_{0}}d^{6}\rho\,\left|\langle\boldsymbol{\rho}|\Psi^{\rm ref}(E)\rangle\right|^{2}=\upsilon^{\prime}_{0}\left|R_{0}^{2}\langle\rho=R_{0}|\mathcal{A}_{\ell}^{\rm ref}\rangle\right|^{2}.

(34)

Therefore, one can expect that a good choice for the log-derivative condition in Eq. (
15
) is
{\upsilon^{\prime}=\upsilon_{0}^{\prime}}
and
{R=R_{0}}
where the radius
R_{0}
is sufficiently large to have
{\langle\rho|\mathcal{A}_{\ell}^{\rm ref}\rangle\simeq\langle\rho|\mathcal{A}_{\ell}\rangle}
when
\rho
is in the vicinity
R_{0}
(and with the choice of normalization in Eq. (
3
)). However, this identification has a sense only if the first terms of the expansion in Eq. (
16
) (at least up to
{n=2}
in the first sum of the right hand side of this equation) lead to a good approximation of the reference function. To test this issue, let us compare the high momentum behavior of
{\langle k|\mathcal{A}_{\ell}^{\rm ref}\rangle}
with the expansion of
{\langle k|\mathcal{A}_{\ell}\rangle}
in the limit
{z=k/q\gg 1}
:

\langle k|A_{\ell}\rangle=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}b_{\ell,-s,\tau}^{(n)}z^{s-2-n}-\frac{\Gamma(1+\ell+s)r_{\ell,s,\tau}}{\Gamma(1+\ell-s)}\\
\times\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}b_{\ell,s,\tau}^{(n)}z^{-s-2-n}.

(35)

The coefficient
{b_{\ell,\gamma,\tau}^{(n)}}
is proportional to
{c_{\ell,\gamma,\tau}^{(n)}}
with a contant obtained from Eq.
99
. Concerning the reference function, one then uses the ansatz:

\langle k|\mathcal{A}_{\ell}^{\rm ref}\rangle=z^{-2+s}\left[\tilde{b}_{\ell,-s,\tau}^{(0)}(z)+\frac{\tilde{b}_{\ell,-s,\tau}^{(1)}(z)}{z}+\frac{\tilde{b}_{\ell,-s,\tau}^{(2)}(z)}{z^{2}}\right]\\
+\frac{\Gamma(1+\ell+s)\tilde{r}_{1,s,\tau}(z)}{\Gamma(1+\ell-s)}z^{-2-s}\left[1+\frac{\tilde{b}_{\ell,s,\tau}^{(1)}(z)}{z}\right].

(36)

In the vicinity of a given value of
{z=k/q}
, the functions
{[\tilde{b}^{(n)}_{\ell,s,\tau}(z),\tilde{r}_{\ell,s,\tau}(z)]}
are obtained by considering them as constant coefficients (with respect to
z
) and by solving a
{5\times 5}
system obtained from Eq. (
36
) and its first four derivatives. In the region
{1\ll z\ll 1/(q\epsilon)}
, one expects that the coefficients at the plateaus coincide with those of the universal function
{[{b}_{\ell,\gamma,\tau}^{(n)},{r}_{\ell,s,\tau}]}
. In what follows, using the reference model of this section, two examples have been considered for
{\ell=1}
near the end point (at the binding wavenumber
{q=10^{-2}q_{u}}
) which is the most favorable situation to recover an equivalent behavior for the functions
{\langle k|\mathcal{A}_{1}^{\rm ref}\rangle}
and
{\langle k|\mathcal{A}_{1}\rangle}
in the limit
{k/q\gg 1}
while
{k\epsilon\ll 1}
. The degree of equivalence of the two functions in this limit has been illustrated by plotting the ratio
{\tilde{b}_{1,\pm s,\tau}(z)/b_{1,\pm s,\tau}}
in Fig.
7
for
{s=0.25}
and in Fig.
8
for
{s=1.25}
. In both figures, one observes a large plateau at large
{z}
for
{\tilde{b}^{(0)}_{1,s,\tau}(z)}
corresponding to the dominant
{z^{-2+s}}
scaling behavior. However, due to finite range effects, there is no well defined plateau for the subdominant term in
{\tilde{b}^{(2)}_{1,s,\tau}(z)}
at large momentum. However, the coefficient
{{b}^{(2)}_{1,s,\tau}}
in the expansion of the universal function for a short hyperradius, gives the dependence of the log-derivative on the energy. Therefore, the absence of plateau for
{\tilde{b}^{(2)}_{1,s,\tau}(z)}
, in this standard situation of small detuning and low energy regime, shows that it is not possible to find a value of
{R=R_{0}}
such that
{\langle\rho|\mathcal{A}_{\ell}^{\rm ref}\rangle\simeq\langle\rho|\mathcal{A}_{\ell}\rangle}
for
{qR_{0}\sim\rho q\ll 1}
and
{\upsilon_{0}^{\prime}=\upsilon^{\prime}}
. In other words, the equivalence of the two functions is not enough precise to have an equivalence between the energy dependent log-derivatives considered at a value of
{R_{0}}
satisfying
{qR_{0}\ll 1}
. When the energy of the state is varied by a small change in the inverse two-body scattering length
{1/a}
near the unitarity, the short distance behavior of
{\langle\rho|\mathcal{A}_{1}^{\rm ref}\rangle}
is marginally affected and thus from Eq. (
34

{\upsilon_{0}^{\prime}}
remains almost fixed. However, there is no reason why the parameter
{\upsilon^{\prime}}
should remain fixed in this operation.

The optimal choice
{\upsilon^{\prime}=0}
found in numerical calculations follows from the fact that for this particular value of
{\upsilon^{\prime}}
, the parameter
R
is also tightly linked to the probability occupation at small distance through the identity
[
23

\int d^{6}\rho\,\left|\langle\boldsymbol{\rho}|\Psi^{\rm ref}\rangle\right|^{2}=\int_{R<\rho}d^{6}\rho\,\left|\langle\boldsymbol{\rho}|\Psi\rangle\right|^{2}

where the tail of

(38)

remains almost fixed in a small change of the inverse scattering length
{1/a}
near the unitarity and from Eq. (
37

{R}
. This is indeed what is observed in numerical calculations with the reference model: the value
{\upsilon^{\prime}=0}
leads to the best fit of the two spectrum for fixed and appropriate values of the parameters

and
\upsilon

{1/a}
(and in particular near the endpoint at

VI
Conclusions

With regard to the possible observation of ITBR in ultracold atoms, by using for instance the rf-association techniques
[
33
,
34
]
, the 2FI system composed of two
171
Yb atoms and one caesium atom is an interesting candidate
[
23
]
. As predicted generically in Ref.
[
35
]
, an ITBR should occur for
{\ell=1}
, from the combination of an Yb-Yb optical p wave Feshbach resonance and a Yb-Cs magnetic s-wave Feshbach resonance
[
36
,
37
,
38
]
. Using other species offers the possibility of exploring the spectrum for other mass ratios
[
39
]
. Recently another analysis using Lennard-Jones potentials confirmed the existence of ITBR in 2FI systems with p-wave interactions
[
40
]
. In these examples, the two-body resonance results from a short-range attraction between the two identical fermions that allows the two-body centrifugal barrier characteristic of the p-wave to be overcome at small distance. In the three-body system, qualitatively speaking, it is this same two-body attraction that again enables the centrifugal barrier to be overcome at small hyperradius, leading to a resonance. On this basis, a similar effect is expected for two-body resonances in higher partial waves. An alternative scenario to achieve an ITBR is given by a Feshbach coupling between the three particles in an open channel and a three-body molecular state in a closed channel.

Appendix A
Jacobi coordinates

The Jacobi coordinates used in this work are a particular choice of their general definition which are remained in what follows. For a given reference mass
m_{\rm r}
, the three systems of Jacobi coordinates associated with each possible pair of particles
(ij)
are defined as follows

\boldsymbol{\rho}^{(ij)}_{1}=\sqrt{\frac{\mu_{ij}}{m_{\rm r}}}(\mathbf{r}_{j}-\mathbf{r}_{i})\ ;\ \boldsymbol{\rho}^{(ij)}_{2}=\sqrt{\frac{\mu_{ij,l}}{m_{\rm r}}}\left(\mathbf{r}_{l}-\frac{m_{i}\mathbf{r}_{i}+m_{j}\mathbf{r}_{j}}{m_{i}+m_{j}}\right)\ ;\ \boldsymbol{\rho}^{(ij)}_{3}=\frac{m_{1}\mathbf{r}_{1}+m_{2}\mathbf{r}_{2}+m_{3}\mathbf{r}_{3}}{\sqrt{m_{\rm r}(m_{1}+m_{2}+m_{3})}},

(40)

where the three integers
{(i,j,l)}
represent cyclic permutations of the triplet
{(123)}
and the reduced masses are given by

\mu_{ij}=\frac{m_{i}m_{j}}{m_{i}+m_{j}}\quad;\quad\mu_{ij,l}=\frac{m_{l}(m_{i}+m_{j})}{m_{1}+m_{2}+m_{3}}.

(41)

In what follows, when the index
(ij)
for the Jacobi coordinates
{(\boldsymbol{\rho}_{1},\boldsymbol{\rho}_{2})}
is not specified, this means that the relations remain valid whatever the choice of set of coordinates. For instance, the hyperradius in the center of mass frame
\rho=\sqrt{(\rho_{1})^{2}+(\rho_{2})^{2}}
is invariant in the choice of the pair
(ij)
:

\rho^{2}=\sum_{i=1}^{3}\frac{m_{i}}{m_{\rm r}}(\mathbf{r}_{i}-\mathbf{C})^{2},

(42)

where
{\mathbf{C}}
is the center of mass:

\mathbf{C}=\frac{m_{1}\mathbf{r}_{1}+m_{2}\mathbf{r}_{2}+m_{3}\mathbf{r}_{3}}{m_{1}+m_{2}+m_{3}}.

(43)

The three wavevectors which are the conjugate of the Jacobi coordinates are given by

\boldsymbol{\kappa}^{(ij)}_{1}=\sqrt{\frac{m_{\rm r}}{\mu_{ij}}}\frac{m_{i}\mathbf{k}_{j}-m_{j}\mathbf{k}_{i}}{m_{i}+m_{j}}\ ;\ \boldsymbol{\kappa}^{(ij)}_{2}=\sqrt{\frac{m_{\rm r}}{\mu_{ij,l}}}\frac{(m_{i}+m_{j})\mathbf{k}_{l}-m_{l}(\mathbf{k}_{i}+\mathbf{k}_{j})}{m_{1}+m_{2}+m_{3}}\ ;\ \boldsymbol{\kappa}^{(ij)}_{3}=\sqrt{\frac{m_{\rm r}}{m_{1}+m_{2}+m_{3}}}\left(\mathbf{k}_{1}+\mathbf{k}_{2}+\mathbf{k}_{3}\right).

(44)

For a given set, by construction, they are such that

\sum_{n=1}^{3}\boldsymbol{\kappa}_{n}\cdot\boldsymbol{\rho}_{n}=\sum_{n=1}^{3}\mathbf{k}_{n}\cdot\mathbf{r}_{n}.

(45)

In the center of mass frame, the hyperwavenumber
{\kappa=\sqrt{\kappa_{1}^{2}+\kappa_{2}^{2}}}
satisfies also

\kappa^{2}=\sum_{i=1}^{3}\frac{m_{\rm r}}{m_{i}}k_{i}^{2}.

(46)

In this work, one considers mass imbalanced systems where
{m_{1}=m_{2}=M}
and
{m_{3}=m}
with the mass ratio
{x=M/m}
and all the equations are derived with the choice of reference mass
m_{\rm r}=\mu_{13}=\mu_{23}
which simplifies all the expressions. In the center of mass frame, only the two vectors
{(\boldsymbol{\rho}_{1}^{(ij)},\boldsymbol{\rho}_{2}^{(ij)})}
are relevant. Introducing the relative coordinates
{\mathbf{r}_{ij}=\mathbf{r}_{j}-\mathbf{r}_{i}}
, they are given by

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\boldsymbol{\rho}^{(23)}_{1}=\mathbf{r}_{23}\\
\boldsymbol{\rho}^{(23)}_{2}=\frac{1+x}{\sqrt{1+2x}}\left(\mathbf{r}_{1}-\frac{x\mathbf{r}_{2}+\mathbf{r}_{3}}{1+x}\right)\end{array}\right.\ ;\ \left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\boldsymbol{\rho}^{(13)}_{1}=\mathbf{r}_{13}\\
\boldsymbol{\rho}^{(13)}_{2}=\frac{1+x}{\sqrt{1+2x}}\left(\mathbf{r}_{2}-\frac{x\mathbf{r}_{1}+\mathbf{r}_{3}}{1+x}\right)\end{array}\right.\ ;\ \left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\boldsymbol{\rho}^{(12)}_{1}=\sqrt{\frac{1+x}{2}}\mathbf{r}_{12}\\
\boldsymbol{\rho}^{(12)}_{2}=\sqrt{\frac{2(1+x)}{1+2x}}\left(\mathbf{r}_{3}-\frac{\mathbf{r}_{1}+\mathbf{r}_{2}}{2}\right)\end{array}\right.

(47)

and the wavevectors of the three sets are given by

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\boldsymbol{\kappa}^{(23)}_{1}=\frac{x\mathbf{k}_{3}-\mathbf{k}_{2}}{1+x}\\
\boldsymbol{\kappa}^{(23)}_{2}=\frac{\sqrt{1+2x}}{1+x}\mathbf{k}_{1}\end{array}\right.\ ;\ \left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\boldsymbol{\kappa}^{(13)}_{1}=\frac{x\mathbf{k}_{3}-\mathbf{k}_{1}}{1+x}\\
\boldsymbol{\kappa}^{(13)}_{2}=\frac{\sqrt{1+2x}}{1+x}\mathbf{k}_{2}\end{array}\right.\ ;\ \left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\boldsymbol{\kappa}^{(12)}_{1}=\frac{\mathbf{k}_{2}-\mathbf{k}_{1}}{\sqrt{2(1+x)}}\\
\boldsymbol{\kappa}^{(12)}_{2}=\sqrt{\frac{1+2x}{2(1+x)}}\mathbf{k}_{3}.\end{array}\right.

Appendix B
STM equation

Although the Skorniakov Ter-Martirosian (STM) equation is well known, a derivation is proposed in this Appendix. The stationary Schrödinger equation in the configuration space for a state of energy
{E=-\frac{\hbar^{2}q^{2}}{2m_{\rm r}}}
is:

\left(q^{2}-\Delta_{\boldsymbol{\rho}_{1}}-\Delta_{\boldsymbol{\rho}_{2}}\right)\langle\boldsymbol{\rho}|\Psi\rangle=-4\pi\sum_{i<j}\delta(\boldsymbol{\rho}^{(ij)}_{1})\langle\boldsymbol{\rho}^{(ij)}_{2}|A_{ij}\rangle.

(56)

The delta distributions in the right-hand-side of Eq. (
56
) are obtained from the action of a three dimensional Laplacian on the inverse radius singularity inherent of the Wigner-Bethe-Peierls contact condition of two interacting particles
{(ij)}
when
{\rho^{(ij)}_{1}\to 0}
, i.e.:

\langle\boldsymbol{\rho}|\Psi\rangle=\langle\boldsymbol{\rho}^{(ij)}_{2}|A_{ij}\rangle\left(\frac{1}{a}-\frac{1}{\rho^{(ij)}_{1}}\right)+O(\rho^{(ij)}_{1}).

(57)

In the reciprocal space one uses the wavevectors
{(\boldsymbol{\kappa}^{(ij)}_{1},\boldsymbol{\kappa}^{(ij)}_{2})}
which are the conjugate of
{(\boldsymbol{\rho}^{(ij)}_{1},\boldsymbol{\rho}^{(ij)}_{2})}
. For convenience, one uses the short-hand notations
{(\mathbf{p},\mathbf{k})=(\boldsymbol{\kappa}^{(23)}_{1},\boldsymbol{\kappa}^{(23)}_{2})}
,
{\mathbf{k}^{\prime}=\boldsymbol{\kappa}^{(13)}_{2}}
and
{\mathbf{k}^{\prime\prime}=\boldsymbol{\kappa}^{(12)}_{2}}

\mathbf{p}=-\mathbf{k}_{1}\sin\theta-\mathbf{k}_{2}\ ;\ \mathbf{k}=\mathbf{k}_{1}\cos\theta.

The Fourier transform of Eq. (
56

\langle\mathbf{p},\mathbf{k}|\Psi\rangle=-\frac{4\pi}{q^{2}+k^{2}+p^{2}}\left(\langle\mathbf{k}|A_{23}\rangle+\langle\mathbf{k}^{\prime}|A_{13}\rangle+\langle\mathbf{k}^{\prime\prime}|A_{12}\rangle\right).

(59)

Considering the contact condition in Eq. (
57
) for the pair
{(23)}
, one remarks that the singularity
{1/\rho_{1}^{(23)}}
is of the same type as the singularity of the two-body Green’s function of the Helmholtz equation at the imaginary wavenumber
{i\lambda}

\int\frac{d^{3}p}{(2\pi)^{3}}\frac{e^{i\mathbf{p}\cdot\boldsymbol{\rho}^{(23)}_{1}}}{p^{2}+\lambda^{2}}=\frac{\exp(-\lambda\rho^{(23)}_{1})}{4\pi\rho^{(23)}_{1}}.

(60)

The contact condition, can be then implemented in the reciprocal space in few steps. In a first step one subtracts the inverse radius singularity of
{\langle\boldsymbol{\rho}|\Psi\rangle}

{\rho^{(23)}_{1}\to 0}
and rewrites Eq. (

\int\frac{d^{3}p}{(2\pi)^{3}}\left[\langle\mathbf{p},\mathbf{k}|\Psi\rangle+\frac{4\pi\langle\mathbf{k}|A_{23}\rangle}{p^{2}+\lambda^{2}}\right]=\langle\mathbf{k}|A_{23}\rangle\left(\frac{1}{a}-\lambda\right),

{\sin 2\theta}={2S}I^{(0)}_{\ell,\gamma}

(108)

and at first up to third order in
{(1/z)}
, one obtains

\displaystyle b_{\ell,\gamma,\tau}^{(1)}=\frac{\tau I^{(0)}_{\ell,\gamma}}{I^{(0)}_{\ell,\gamma}-I^{(0)}_{\ell,1+\gamma}},

(109)

\displaystyle b_{\ell,\gamma,\tau}^{(2)}=\frac{2\tau I^{(0)}_{\ell,\gamma}b_{\ell,\gamma,\tau}^{(1)}+\frac{\cos^{2}\theta}{\sin\theta}I^{(1)}_{\ell,1+\gamma}-I^{(0)}_{\ell,\gamma}}{2\left(I^{(0)}_{\ell,\gamma}-I^{(0)}_{\ell,2+\gamma}\right)},

(110)

\displaystyle b_{\ell,\gamma,\tau}^{(3)}=\frac{2\tau I^{(0)}_{\ell,\gamma}b_{\ell,\gamma,\tau}^{(2)}+\left(\frac{\cos^{2}\theta}{\sin\theta}I^{(1)}_{\ell,2+\gamma}-I^{(0)}_{\ell,\gamma}\right)b_{\ell,\gamma,\tau}^{(1)}}{2\left(I^{(0)}_{\ell,\gamma}-I^{(0)}_{\ell,3+\gamma}\right)}.

(111)

At higher orders, for even (odd) values of
{n=2p}
(
{n=2p+1}
) ,
{b_{\ell,\gamma,\tau}^{(2p)}}
and
{\left(b_{\ell,\gamma,\tau}^{(2p+1)}/\tau\right)}
are polynoms of
{\tau^{2}}
of order
p
. One can verify that the expression of the coefficient
{b_{\ell,\gamma,0}^{(2)}}
in Eq. (
110
) coincides with the result obtained from the expansion of
{K_{s}(q\rho)}
and Eq. (
100
). For instance one has for
{\ell=0,1}
:

b_{0,\gamma,0}^{(2)}=\frac{-\gamma-2}{4}\quad;\quad b_{1,\gamma,0}^{(2)}=\frac{-\gamma(3+\gamma)}{4(1+\gamma)}.

(112)

Finally one obtains

\displaystyle c_{\ell,\gamma,\tau}^{(1)}=\frac{\tau I^{(0)}_{\ell,\gamma}\Gamma\left(1+\frac{\ell+\gamma}{2}\right)\Gamma\left(\frac{\ell-\gamma}{2}\right)}{2\left(I^{(0)}_{\ell,\gamma}-I^{(0)}_{\ell,1+\gamma}\right)\Gamma\left(\frac{1+\ell-\gamma}{2}\right)\Gamma\left(\frac{3+\ell+\gamma}{2}\right)}

(113)

\displaystyle c_{\ell,\gamma,\tau}^{(2)}=\frac{1}{4+4\gamma}+\frac{\left(\tau I^{(0)}_{\ell,\gamma}\right)^{2}}{(\ell+2+\gamma)(\ell-1-\gamma)(I^{(0)}_{\ell,\gamma}-I^{(0)}_{\ell,1+\gamma})(I^{(0)}_{\ell,\gamma}-I^{(0)}_{\ell,2+\gamma})}.

(114)

One has also from the series representation of
{K_{s}(q\rho)}
:

r_{\ell,s,0}=\frac{\pi 4^{-s}}{s\Gamma(s)^{2}\sin(\pi s)}.

(115)

Finding an analytical expression for the coefficient
{r_{\ell,s,\tau}}
away from unitarity, i.e. when
{\tau}
is not zero, remains an unsolved issue. Nevertheless, in the limit of a large and negative value of
{\tau}
, one has
{r_{\ell,s,\tau}=O(|\tau|^{2s})}
. This property plays an important role for the endpoint of the spectrum at negative scattering length. A proof can be obtained by considering the STM equation at zero energy and for a negative scattering length with the change of variable
{v=k|a|}
:

\frac{2S(-1)^{\ell}}{\pi\sin(2\theta)}\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{v^{\prime}dv^{\prime}}{v}Q_{\ell}\left(\frac{v^{2}+v^{\prime 2}}{2vv^{\prime}\sin\theta}\right)\langle v^{\prime}|\mathcal{A}_{\ell}\rangle=\langle v|\mathcal{A}_{\ell}\rangle(v+1).

(116)

In the limit of large values of
v
, one recovers the two independent solutions
{v^{-2\pm s}}
and the general solution is of the form

\langle v|\mathcal{A}_{\ell}\rangle=v^{s-2}\left(1+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}a_{\ell,-s}^{(n)}v^{-n}\right)-\frac{\Gamma(1+\ell+s)B}{\Gamma(1+\ell-s)}v^{-s-2}\left(1+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}a_{\ell,s}^{(n)}v^{-n}\right).

(117)

The expansion of Eq. (
117
) is injected in Eq. (
116
) successively for
{B=0}
and
{B=\infty}
to obtain the relations satisfied by the coefficient
{a_{\ell,-s}^{(n)}}
and
{a_{\ell,s}^{(n)}}
. Comparison with Eq. (
101
) in the limit
{\tau\to-\infty}
and
{z/|\tau|\ll 1}
permits one to show that
{a_{\ell,\pm s}^{(n)}\sim b_{\ell,\pm s,\tau}^{(n)}/|\tau|^{n}}
. Using the identity
{v=z/|\tau|}
in Eq. (
117
), and from the comparison with Eq. (
98
), one obtains
{r_{\ell,s,\tau}\sim B|\tau|^{2s}}
where
B
is a function of
{S}
,
{\theta}
and
{\ell}
.

Appendix F
Computation of the universal functions

The method used for the numerical evaluation of the universal functions and of the coefficient
{r_{\ell,s,\tau}}
when
{\tau\neq 0}
with the assumption that
{s<2}
is detailed in this Appendix. This method can be thus applied for all the ITBR of the 2FI systems when
{\ell=1}
. In the numerical analysis, the grid for the variable
{z\in\{z_{1}\dots z_{N}\}}
is defined by an exponential change of variable
{z_{n}=z_{0}e^{(n-1)h}}
where
{h\ll 1}
which permits one to sample the function on a rather large interval. In the calculations, typically from 1000 to 2000 points have been used with
{z_{0}=10^{-3}}
and
{z_{N}}
from
{10^{9}}
to
{10^{13}}
. The universal functions are discretized with

S_{n}(\tau)=z_{n}^{3/2}\langle z_{n}|\phi_{\ell,s,\tau}\rangle.

(118)

where the prefactor makes the linear system symmetrical. Using the Lippmann Schwinger-like equation, the vector
{S_{n}(\tau)}
is obtained from
{S_{n}(0)}
by means of a matrix inversion with

S_{n}(\tau)=S_{n}(0)+\delta S_{n}(\tau)\quad\text{and}\quad\delta S_{n}(\tau)=\tau\sum_{n^{\prime}}M_{n,n^{\prime}}^{-1}S_{n^{\prime}}(0)

(119)

where the matrix
{M_{n,n^{\prime}}}
is given by:

M_{n,n^{\prime}}=(\sqrt{z_{n}^{2}+1}-\tau)\delta_{n,n^{\prime}}-\frac{2hS}{\pi\sin 2\theta}(-1)^{\ell}\sqrt{z_{n}z_{n^{\prime}}}Q_{\ell}\left(\frac{z_{n}^{2}+z_{n^{\prime}}^{2}+\cos^{2}\theta}{2z_{n}z_{n^{\prime}}\sin\theta}\right).

(120)

The universal functions are then obtained numerically by

\langle z_{n}|\phi_{\ell,s,\tau}\rangle=\langle z_{n}|\phi_{\ell,s,0}\rangle+\langle z_{n}|\delta\phi_{\ell,s,\tau}\rangle\ \text{with}\ \langle z_{n}|\delta\phi_{\ell,s,\tau}\rangle=\delta S_{n}(\tau)z_{n}^{-3/2}.

(121)

Depending on the value of
s
, the dominant terms in the expansion of
{\langle z_{n}|\delta\phi_{\ell,s,\tau}\rangle}
for large
{z_{n}}
are not the same. One then uses the following ansatz for
{\langle z_{n}|\delta\phi_{\ell,s,\tau}\rangle}
:

\langle z_{n}|\delta\phi_{\ell,s,\tau}\rangle=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}z_{n}^{s-2}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{2}\frac{\delta b^{(i)}_{\ell,-s,\tau}(n)}{(z_{n})^{i}}\right)-z_{n}^{-s-2}\frac{\Gamma(1+\ell-s)}{\Gamma(1+\ell+s)}\delta r_{\ell,s,\tau}(n)\left(1+\sum_{i=1}^{2}\frac{\delta b^{(i)}_{\ell,s,\tau}(n)}{(z_{n})^{i}}\right)&\text{for}\ s\leq\frac{1}{2}\\
z_{n}^{s-2}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{3}\frac{\delta b^{(i)}_{\ell,-s,\tau}(n)}{(z_{n})^{i}}\right)-z_{n}^{-s-2}\frac{\Gamma(1+\ell-s)}{\Gamma(1+\ell+s)}\delta r_{\ell,s,\tau}(n)\left(1+\frac{\delta b^{(1)}_{\ell,s,\tau}(n)}{z_{n}}\right)&\text{for}\ \frac{3}{2}\geq s>\frac{1}{2}\\
z_{n}^{s-2}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{4}\frac{\delta b^{(i)}_{\ell,-s,\tau}(n)}{(z_{n})^{i}}\right)-z_{n}^{-s-2}\frac{\Gamma(1+\ell-s)}{\Gamma(1+\ell+s)}\delta r_{\ell,s,\tau}(n)&\text{for}\ 2>s>\frac{3}{2}.\end{array}\right.

(122)

When for large values of
{n}
, the functions
{[\delta r_{\ell,s,\tau}(n),\delta b_{\ell,\gamma,\tau}^{(i)}(n)]}
are constant, Eq. (
122
) coincides with the first five dominant terms of the expansion of the function
{\langle z|\delta\phi_{\ell,s,\tau}\rangle}
in the limit
{z\gg 1}
. For higher values of the scaling exponent, more terms in the ansatz have to be added, however the unavoidable numerical noise makes this numerical method ineffective. The functions
{[\delta r_{\ell,s,\tau}(n),\delta b_{\gamma,\tau}^{(i)}(n)]}
are obtained by solving a
{5\times 5}
linear system deduced from the expression at a given
n
of the first four discretized log-derivatives in
{\left(z\frac{d}{dz}\right)}
of the two sides of Eq. (
122
) where
{[\delta r_{\ell,s,\tau}(n),\delta b_{\ell,\gamma,\tau}^{(i)}(n)]}
are considered as constant. Precise results are found by using a discretization of the derivatives with nine points. One searches for plateaus in the functions
{[\delta r_{\ell,s,\tau}(n),\delta b_{\ell,\gamma,\tau}^{(i)}(n)]}
for a sufficiently large value of
{n}
such that
{z_{n}\gg 1}
, up to a value of
{n}
where the numerical signal is too noisy, i.e. in the far tail of the function
{\langle z_{n}|\delta\phi_{\ell,s,\tau}\rangle}
. The value
{\delta b_{\ell,\gamma,\tau}^{(i)}(n)}
(or
{\delta r_{\ell,s,\tau}(n)}
) corresponding to a minimum of slope on the plateau (whenever it exists) is denoted by
\delta b_{\ell,\gamma,\tau}^{(i),{\rm as}}
(or
{\delta r_{\ell,s,\tau}^{\rm as}}
). This numerical procedure permits a numerical evaluation of the corresponding coefficients in the expansion of
{\langle z|\phi_{\ell,s,\tau}\rangle}
at large
{z}
:

b_{\ell,\gamma,\tau}^{(i)}=b_{\ell,\gamma,0}^{(i)}+\delta b_{\ell,\gamma,\tau}^{(i),{\rm as}}\ \text{and}\quad r_{\ell,s,\tau}=r_{\ell,s,0}+\delta r_{\ell,s,\tau}^{\rm as}.

(123)

The parameter
{r_{\ell,s,\tau}}
is the only universal quantity which is obtained from a numerical study of the dimensionless STM equation. The precision of this method can be tested by considering the term
{b_{\ell,-s,\tau}^{(i)}(n)z^{s-2-n}}
corresponding to the subdominant contribution with respect to the term in
{r_{\ell,s,\tau}z^{-s-2}}
:
{\delta b_{\ell,-s,\tau}^{(1)}(n)}
for
{s<1/2}
,
{\delta b_{\ell,-s,\tau}^{(2)}(n)}
for
{1/2<s<1}
,
{\delta b_{\ell,-s,\tau}^{(3)}(n)}
for
{1<s<3/2}
…Comparing the value at the plateau with respect to the exact asymptotic value
{b_{\ell,-s,\tau}^{(n)}}
given in Appendix
E
permits one to evaluate the precision made on the value of
{r_{\ell,s,\tau}}
. Examples of computations are given in Figs.
9
-
11
. From this basis, the typical relative precision for
{r_{\ell,s,\tau}}
is of the order of
{10^{-3}}
or much smaller in the interval
{0<s<3/2}
. For larger values of
{s}
, the plateau of
{\delta r_{\ell,s,\tau}(n)}
obtained numerically is less pronounced and thus the evaluation of
{r_{\ell,s,\tau}}
is not very precise. This is not a problem for the determination of the binding energy from Eq. (
17
) where
{r_{\ell,s,\tau}}
is needed only for
{s<1.5}
.

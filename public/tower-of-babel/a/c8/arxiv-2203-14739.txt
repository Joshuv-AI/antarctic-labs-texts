Global Regular Solutions for the multi-dimensional Kuramoto-Sivashinsky equation posed on smooth domains Thanks: MSC 2010:35Q35; 35Q53. Keywords: Kuramoto-Sivashinsky equation, Global solutions; Decay in Bounded domains
N. A. Larkin

Abstract
. Initial- boundary value problems for the n-dimensional ( n n is a natural number from the interval [2,7]) Kuramoto-Sivashinsky equation posed on smooth bounded domains in ℝ n {\mathbb{R}}^{n} were considered. The existence and uniqueness of global regular solutions as well as their exponential decay have been established.



1.
Introduction

This work concerns the existence, uniqueness, regularity and exponential decay rates of solutions to initial-boundary value problems for the
n
-dimensional Kuramoto-Sivashinsky equation (KS)

\displaystyle\phi_{t}+\Delta^{2}\phi+\Delta\phi+\frac{1}{2}|\nabla\phi|^{2}=0.

(1.1)

Here
n
is a natural number from the interval [2,7],
\Delta
and
\nabla
are the Laplacian and the gradient in
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}.

In
[
10
]
, Kuramoto studied the turbulent phase waves and Sivashinsky in
[
17
]
obtained an asymptotic equation which simulated the evolution of a disturbed plane flame front. See also
[
7
]
.
Mathematical results on initial and initial- boundary value problems for one-dimensional (1.1) are presented in
[
3
,
5
,
6
,
12
,
14
,
15
,
19
,
20
]
, see references cited there for more information. In

[
4
,
5
]
, the initial value problem for the multi-dimensional (1.1) type equations has been considered.
Two-dimensional periodic problems for the K-S- equation and its modifications posed on rectangles were studied in
[
2
,
13
,
14
,
16
,
19
]
, where some results on the existence of weak solutions and nonlinear stability have been established. In
[
11
]
, initial-boundary value problems for the 3D Kuramoto-Sivashinsky equation have been studied; the existence, uniqueness and exponential decay of global regular solutions have been proved.

For
n
dimensions,
x=(x_{1},...,x_{n})
,
n=2,3,4,5,6,7
, (1.1) can be rewritten in the form of the following system:

\displaystyle(u_{j})_{t}+\Delta^{2}u_{j}+\Delta u_{j}+\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i=1}^{n}(u_{i})^{2}_{x_{j}}=0,\;j=1,...,n,

(1.2)

\displaystyle(u_{i})_{x_{j}}=(u_{j})_{x_{i}},\;j\neq i,\;\;i,j=1,...,n.

(1.3)

where
u_{j}=(\phi)_{x_{j}},\;j=1,...,n.
Let
\Omega_{n}=\prod_{i=1}^{n}(0,L_{i})
be the minimal nD parallelepiped containing a given smooth domain
\bar{D_{n}}.

First essential problem that arises while one studies either (1.1) or (1.2)-(1.3), are destabilizing effects of

\Delta u_{j};
they may be damped by dissipative terms
\Delta^{2}u_{j}
provided
D_{n}
has some specific properties. In order to understand this, we use Steklov‘s inequalities to estimate

a\|u_{j}\|^{2}\leq\|\nabla u_{j}\|^{2},\;a\|\nabla u_{j}\|^{2}\leq\|\Delta u_{j}\|^{2};\;a=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\frac{\pi^{2}}{L_{i}^{2}},\;\;j=1,...,n.

A simple analysis shows that if

1-\frac{1}{a}>0,

(1.4)

then
\Delta^{2}u_{j}
damp
\Delta u_{j}
. Naturally, here appear admissible domains where (1.4) is fulfilled, so called ”thin domains”,
where some
L_{i}
are sufficiently small while others
L_{j}
may be large
i,j=1,...,7;\;i\neq j.

Second essential problem is presence of semi-linear terms in (1.2) which are interconnected. This does not allow to obtain the first estimate independent of
u_{j}
and leads to a connection between
L_{i}
and
u_{j}(0),\;i,j=1,...,7.

Our aim in this work is to study n-dimensional initial-boundary value problems for (1.2)-(1.3) posed on smooth domains, where the existence and uniqueness of global regular solutions as well as their exponential decay of the
H^{2}(D_{n})
-norm have been established. Moreover, we obtained a ”smoothing effect” for solutions with respect to initial data. Although, cases
n=2,3
are not new, we included them for the sake of generality.

This work has the following structure: Section I is Introduction. Section 2 contains notations and auxiliary facts. In Section 3, formulation of an initial-boundary value problem in a smooth bounded domain
D_{n}
is given. The existence and uniqueness of global regular solutions, exponential decay of the
H^{2}(D_{n})
-norm and a ”smoothing effect” have been established. Section 4 contains conclusions.

3.
K-S equation posed on smooth domains

Let
\Omega_{n}
be the minimal nD-parallelepiped containing a given bounded smooth domain
\bar{D_{n}}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n},\;n=1,...,7
:

\Omega_{n}=\{x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n};x_{i}\in(0,L_{i})\},\;u_{i}=(\phi)_{x_{i}},\;i=1,...,n.

Fixing any natural
n=2,...,7,
consider in
Q_{n}=D_{n}\times(0,t)
the following initial-boundary value problem:

\displaystyle(u_{j})_{t}+\Delta^{2}u_{j}+\Delta u_{j}+\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i=1}^{n}(u^{2}_{i})_{x_{j}}=0,\;j=1,...,n,

(3.1)

\displaystyle(u_{i})_{x_{j}}=(u_{j})_{x_{i}},\;j\neq i,\;\;i,j=1,...,n;

(3.2)

\displaystyle u_{j}|_{\partial D_{n}}=\Delta u_{j}|_{\partial D_{n}}=0,\;t>0,

(3.3)

\displaystyle u_{j}(x,0)=u_{j0}(x),\;j=1,...,n,\;\;x\in D_{n}.

(3.4)

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
f\in H^{4}(D_{n})\cap H^{1}_{0}(D_{n})
and
\Delta f|_{\partial D_{n}}=0.
Then

a\|f\|^{2}\leq\|\nabla f\|^{2},\;\;a^{2}\|f\|^{2}\leq\|\Delta f\|^{2},\;\;a\|\nabla f\|^{2}\leq\|\Delta f\|^{2},

a^{2}\|\Delta f\|^{2}\leq\|\Delta^{2}f\|^{2},\;\;\|\Delta\nabla f\|^{2}\leq\|\Delta^{2}f\|\|\Delta f\|\leq\frac{1}{a}\|\Delta^{2}f\|^{2}.

where
a=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\frac{\pi^{2}}{L^{2}_{i}},\;\;\|f\|^{2}=\int_{D_{n}}f^{2}(x)dx.

Proof.

We have

\|\nabla f\|^{2}=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\|f_{x_{i}}\|^{2}.

Define

\tilde{f}(x,t)=\begin{cases}f(x,t)\text{ if }x\in D_{n};\\
0\text{ if }x\in\Omega_{n}/D_{n}.\end{cases}

(3.5)

Making use of Steklov‘s inequalities for
\tilde{f}(x,t)
and taking into account that
\|\nabla f\|=\|\nabla\tilde{f}\|,
we get

\displaystyle\|\nabla f\|^{2}\geq a\|f\|^{2},\;\text{where}\;a=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\frac{\pi^{2}}{L_{i}^{2}}.

On the other hand,

a\|f\|^{2}\leq\|\nabla f\|^{2}=-\int_{D_{n}}f\Delta fdx\leq\|\Delta f\|\|f\|.

This implies

a\|f\|\leq\|\Delta f\|\;\;\text{and}\;\;a^{2}\|f\|^{2}\leq\|\Delta f\|^{2}.

Consequently,
a\|\nabla f\|^{2}\leq\|\Delta f\|^{2}.
Similarly,

\|\Delta f\|^{2}=\int_{D_{n}}f\Delta^{2}fdx\leq\|\Delta^{2}f\|\|f\|\leq\frac{1}{a}\|\Delta^{2}f\|\|\Delta f\|.

Hence,
a\|\Delta f\|\leq\|\Delta^{2}f\|.
Moreover,

\|\Delta\nabla f\|^{2}=-\int_{D_{n}}\Delta^{2}f\Delta fdx\leq\|\Delta^{2}f\|\|\Delta f\|\leq\frac{1}{a}\|\Delta^{2}f\|^{2}.

Proof of Lemma 3.1 is complete.
∎

Remark 3.1
.

Assertions of Lemma 3.1 are true if the function
f
is replaced respectively by
u_{j},\;j=1,...,n
.

Lemma 3.2
.

In conditiions of Lemma 3.1,

\displaystyle\|f\|^{2}(t)_{H^{2}(D_{n})}\leq 3\|\Delta f\|^{2}(t),

(3.6)

\displaystyle\|f\|^{2}(t)_{H^{4}(D_{n})}\leq 5\|\Delta^{2}f\|^{2}(t),

(3.7)

\displaystyle\sup_{D_{n}}|f(x)|\leq C_{s}\|\Delta^{2}f\|,\;\text{where}\;C_{s}=5C_{n}.

(3.8)

Proof.

To prove (3.7), making use of Lemma 3.1, we find

\|f\|^{2}_{H^{4}(D_{n})}=\|f\|^{2}+\|\nabla f\|^{2}+\|\Delta f\|^{2}+\|\nabla\Delta f\|^{2}+\|\Delta^{2}f\|^{2}

\leq\Big(\frac{1}{a^{4}}+\frac{1}{a^{3}}+\frac{1}{a^{2}}+\frac{1}{a}+1\Big)\|\Delta^{2}f\|^{2}.

Since
a>1
, then (3.7) follows. Similarly, (3.6) can be proved. Moreover, taking into account Lemma 2.3, we get (3.8).
∎

Theorem 3.1

(Special basis)
.

Let
n
be a natural number from the interval [2,7];
D_{n}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
be a bounded smooth domain satisfying the Cone condition and
\Omega_{n}
be a minimal
nD
-parallelepiped containing
\bar{D_{n}}.
Let

\theta=1-\frac{1}{a}=1-\frac{1}{\sum_{i=1}^{n}\frac{\pi^{2}}{L^{2}_{i}}}>0.

(3.9)

Given

u_{j0}(D_{n})\in H^{2}(D_{n})\cap H^{1}_{0}(D_{n}),\;j=1,...,n

such that

\displaystyle\theta-\frac{2C_{s}^{2}7^{3}}{a\theta}\Big(\sum_{j=1}^{n}\|\Delta u_{j}\|^{2}(0)\Big)>0,

(3.10)

then there exists a unique global regular solution to (3.1)- (3.4):

u_{j}\in L^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{+};H^{2}(D_{n}))\cap L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{+};H^{4}(D_{n})\cap H^{1}_{0}(D_{n}));

u_{jt}\in L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{+};L^{2}(D_{n})),\;j=1,...,n.

Moreover,

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{n}\|\Delta u_{j}\|^{2}(t)\leq\Big(\sum_{j=1}^{n}\|\Delta u_{j0}\|^{2}\Big)\exp\{-a^{2}t\theta/2\}

(3.11)

and

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{n}\|\Delta u_{i}\|^{2}(t)+\int_{0}^{t}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\|\Delta^{2}u_{i}\|^{2}(\tau)d\tau\leq C\sum_{i=1}^{n}\|\Delta u_{i0}\|^{2},\;t>0.

Remark 3.2
.

In Theorem 3.1, there are two types of restrictions: the first one is pure geometrical,

1-\frac{1}{a}>0

which is needed to eliminate destabilizing effects of the terms
\Delta u_{j}
in (3.1):

\|\Delta u_{j}\|^{2}-\|\nabla u_{j}\|^{2}.

It is clear that

\lim_{L_{i}\to 0}a=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\frac{\pi^{2}}{L^{2}_{i}}=+\infty,

hence to achieve (3.9), it is possible to decrease
L_{i},\;i=1,...,n
allowing other
L_{j},\;j\neq i
to grow.

A situation with condition (3.10)
is more complicated: if initial data are not small, then it is possible either to decrease
L_{i},\;i=1,...,n,
to fulfill this condition or for fixed
L_{i},\;i=1,...,n
to decrease initial data
\|u_{j0}\|.

Proof.

It is possible to construct Galerkin‘s approximations to (3.1)-(3.4) by the following way:
let
w_{j}(x)
be eigenfunctions of the problem:

\Delta^{2}w_{j}-\lambda_{j}w_{j}=0\;\text{in}\;D_{n};\;\;w_{j}|_{\partial D_{n}}=\Delta w_{j}|_{\partial D_{n}}=0,j=1,2,....

Define

\displaystyle u^{N}_{j}(x,t)=\sum_{k=1}^{N}g_{k}^{j}(t)w_{j}(x).

Unknown functions
g_{i}^{j}(t)
satisfy the following initial problems:

\displaystyle(\frac{d}{dt}u^{N}_{j},w_{j})(t)+(\Delta^{2}u^{N}_{j},w_{j})(t)+(\Delta u^{N}_{j},w_{j})(t)

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{2}(\sum_{i=1}^{n}(u^{N}_{i})^{2}_{x_{j}},w_{j})(t)=0,

\displaystyle g_{k}^{j}(0)=g_{0k}^{j},\;\;j=1,...,n,\;\;k=1,2,....

The estimates that follow may be established on Galerkin’s approximations (see:
[
5
,
6
]
), but it is more explicitly to prove them on smooth solutions of (3.1)-(3.4).

Estimate I:

u\in L^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{+};H^{2}(D_{n})\cap H^{1}_{0}(D_{n}))\cap L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{+};H^{4}(D_{n})\cap H^{1}_{0}(D_{n}))
.

For any natural
n\in[2,7],
multiply (3.1) by
2\Delta^{2}u_{j}
to obtain

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\|\Delta u_{j}\|^{2}(t)+2\|\Delta^{2}u_{j}\|^{2}(t)+2\|\Delta^{2}u\|(t)\|\Delta u_{j}\|(t)

\displaystyle+2\sum_{i=1}^{n}(u_{i}(u_{i})_{x_{j}},\Delta^{2}u_{j})(t)=0.

(3.12)

Making use of (3.7) and Lemmas 2.3, 3.1, 3.2, we can write

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\|\Delta u_{j}\|^{2}(t)+2\theta\|\Delta^{2}u_{j}\|^{2}(t)

\displaystyle\leq 2\Big[\sum_{i=1}^{n}\sup_{D_{n}}|u_{i}(x,t)|\|\nabla u_{i}\|(t)\Big]\|\Delta^{2}u_{j}\|(t)

\displaystyle\leq 2\Big[C_{s}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\|\Delta^{2}u_{i}\|(t)\|\nabla u_{i}\|(t)\Big]\|\Delta^{2}u_{j}\|(t);j=1,...,n.

(3.13)

Summing over
j=1,...,n
and making use of Lemma 3.1, we rewrite (3.12) in the form:

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\sum_{j=1}^{n}\|\Delta u_{j}\|^{2}(t)+2\theta\sum_{j=1}^{n}\|\Delta^{2}u_{j}\|(t)

\displaystyle\leq 2C_{s}n\Big(\sum_{j=1}^{n}\|\nabla u_{j}\|(t)\Big)\Big[\sum_{j=1}^{n}\|\Delta^{2}u_{j}\|^{2}(t)\Big]

\displaystyle\leq\Big[\frac{\theta}{2}+\frac{2C_{s}^{2}n^{2}}{\theta}\Big(\sum_{j=1}^{n}\|\nabla u_{j}\|(t)\Big)^{2}\Big]\sum_{j=1}^{n}\|\Delta^{2}u_{j}\|^{2}(t)

\displaystyle\leq\Big[\frac{\theta}{2}+\frac{2C_{s}^{2}n^{3}}{\theta}\Big(\sum_{j=1}^{n}\|\nabla u_{j}\|^{2}(t)\Big)\Big]\sum_{j=1}^{n}\|\Delta^{2}u_{j}\|^{2}(t)

\displaystyle\leq\Big[\frac{\theta}{2}+\frac{2C_{s}^{2}n^{3}}{a\theta}\Big(\sum_{j=1}^{n}\|\Delta u_{j}\|^{2}(t)\Big)\Big]\sum_{j=1}^{n}\|\Delta^{2}u_{j}\|^{2}(t).

Taking this into account, we transform (3.12) in the form

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\sum_{j=1}^{n}\|\Delta u_{j}\|^{2}(t)+\frac{\theta}{2}\sum_{j=1}^{n}\|\Delta^{2}u_{j}\|^{2}(t)

\displaystyle+\Big[\theta-\frac{2C_{s}^{2}n^{3}}{a\theta}\Big(\sum_{j=1}^{n}\|\Delta u_{j}\|^{2}(t)\Big)\Big]\sum_{j=1}^{n}\|\Delta^{2}u_{j}\|^{2}(t)\leq 0.

(3.14)

Condition (3.10) and Lemma 2.4 guarantee that

\theta-\frac{2C_{s}^{2}n^{3}}{a\theta}\Big(\sum_{j=1}^{n}\|\Delta u_{j}\|^{2}(t)\Big)>0,\;\;t>0.

Hence (3.13) can be rewritten as

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\sum_{j=1}^{n}\|\Delta u_{j}\|^{2}(t)+\frac{a^{2}\theta}{2}\sum_{j=1}^{n}\|\Delta u_{j}\|^{2}(t)\leq 0.

(3.15)

Integrating, we get

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{n}\|\Delta u_{j}\|^{2}(t)\leq\sum_{j=1}^{n}\|\Delta u_{j0}\|^{2}\exp\{-a^{2}\theta t/2\}.

(3.16)

Returning to (3.22), we find

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{n}\|\Delta u_{i}\|^{2}(t)+\int_{0}^{t}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\|\Delta^{2}u_{i}\|^{2}(\tau)d\tau\leq C\sum_{i=1}^{n}\|\Delta u_{i0}\|^{2}.

(3.17)

Finally, directly from (3.1), we obtain

(u_{j})_{t}\in L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{+};L^{2}(D_{n})),\;j=1,...,n.

This completes the proof of the existence part of Theorem 3.1.

Lemma 3.3
.

A regular solution of Theorem 3.1 is uniquely defined.

Proof.

Let
u_{j}
and
v_{j}
,
j=1,...,n
be two distinct solutions to (3.1)-(3.4). Denoting
w_{j}=u_{j}-v_{j}
, we come to the following system:

\displaystyle(w_{j})_{t}+\Delta^{2}w_{j}+\Delta w_{j}+\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\Big(u^{2}_{i}-v^{2}_{i}\Big)_{x_{j}}=0,

(3.18)

\displaystyle(w_{j})_{x_{i}}=(w_{i})_{x_{j}},\;i\neq j,

(3.19)

\displaystyle w_{j}|_{\partial D_{n}}=\Delta w_{j}|_{\partial D_{n}}=0,\;t>0;

(3.20)

\displaystyle w_{j}(x,0)=0\;\text{in}\;D,\;\;j=1,...,n.

(3.21)

Multiply (3.18) by
2w_{j}
to obtain

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\|w_{j}\|^{2}(t)+2\|\Delta w_{j}\|^{2}(t)-2\|\nabla w_{j}\|^{2}(t)

\displaystyle-\sum_{i=1}^{n}\Big(\{u_{i}+v_{i}\}w_{i},(w_{j})_{x_{j}}\Big)(t)=0,\;j=1,...,n.

(3.22)

Making use of Lemmas 2.3 and 3.1, 3.2, we estimate

I=\sum_{i=1}^{n}(\{u_{i}+v_{i}\}w_{i},(w_{j})_{x_{j}})\leq\frac{\epsilon}{2}\|\nabla w_{j}\|^{2}+\frac{1}{2\epsilon}\Big(\sum_{i=1}^{n}\|\{u_{i}+v_{i}\}w_{i}\|\Big)^{2}

\leq\frac{\epsilon}{2a}\|\Delta w_{j}\|^{2}+\frac{2}{\epsilon}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\sup_{D_{n}}\{u^{2}_{i}(x,t)_{i}+v^{2}_{i}(x,t)\}\|w^{2}_{i}\|(t)

\leq\frac{\epsilon}{2a}\|\Delta w_{j}\|^{2}+\frac{4C_{s}^{2}}{\epsilon}\Big[\sum_{i=1}^{n}\{\|\Delta^{2}u_{i}\|^{2}+\|\Delta^{2}v_{i}\|^{2}\}\Big]\sum_{j=1}^{n}\|w_{j}\|^{2}.

Here
\epsilon
is an arbitrary positive number. Substituting
I
into (3.22), we get

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\|w_{j}\|^{2}(t)+(2-\frac{2}{a}-\frac{\epsilon}{2a})\|\Delta w_{j}\|^{2}(t)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{4C_{s}^{2}}{\epsilon}\Big[\sum_{i=1}^{n}\{\|\Delta^{2}u_{i}\|^{2}+\|\Delta^{2}v_{i}\|^{2}\}\Big]\sum_{j=1}^{n}\|w_{j}\|^{2}.

(3.23)

Taking
\epsilon=\frac{\theta}{2}
and summing up over
j=1,...,n,
we transform (3.23) as follows:

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\sum_{j=1}^{n}\|w_{j}\|^{2}(t)\leq C\Big[\sum_{i=1}^{n}\{\|\Delta^{2}u_{i}\|^{2}

\displaystyle+\|\Delta^{2}v_{i}\|^{2}+\|u_{i}\|^{2}(t)+\|v_{i}\|^{2}(t)\}\Big]\sum_{j=1}^{n}\|w_{j}\|^{2},\;i=1,...,n.

Since by (3.17) and Lemma 3.1,

\|\Delta^{2}u_{i}\|^{2}(t),\|\Delta^{2}v_{i}\|^{2}(t)\in L^{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{+})

and

then by Lemma 2.2,

\|w_{j}\|^{2}(t)\equiv 0\;\;j=1,...,n,\;\text{for all}\;\;t>0.

Hence

\displaystyle u_{j}(x,t)\equiv v_{j}(x,t);j=1,...,n.

The proof of Lemma 3.3, and consequently, Theorem 3.1 are complete.

4.
Conclusions

This work concerns formulation and solvability of initial-boundary value problems for the n-dimensional (
n
is a natural number from the interval [2,7],) Kuramoto-Sivashinsky system (3.1)-(3.2) posed on smooth bounded domains. Theorem 3.1 contains results on existence and uniqueness of global regular solutions as well as exponential decay of the
H^{2}(D_{n})
-norm, where
D_{n}
is a smooth bounded domain in
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
. We define a set of admissible domains, where destabilizing effects of terms
\Delta u_{j}
are damped by dissipativity of
\Delta^{2}u_{j}
due to condition (3.9). This set contains ”thin domains”, see
[
9
,
13
,
16
]
, where some dimensions of
D_{n}
are small while others may be large. Since initial- boundary value problems studied in this work do not admit the first a priori estimate independent of

Abstract
. We prove that for a compact metric space the property of having finite covering dimension is equivalent to the existence of a total order with finite snake number.



Keywords:

total order
\cdot

covering dimension
\cdot

small inductive dimension.

1.
Introduction

The following concept was introduced in
[
2
]
.
Let
M
be a metric space and let
T
be a total order on
M
(that is, for any distinct points
x,y\in M
either
x<_{T}y
or
y<_{T}x
).
Then for the ordered space
(M,T)
one calculates
the order breakpoint

\text{Br}(M,T)\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{+\infty\}
.
The value
\min_{T}\text{Br}(M,T)
is a quasi-isometry invariant for uniformly discrete metric spaces.

It was shown in
[
3
]
that a metric space with finite
Assouad-Nagata dimension admits an order
T
for which
\text{Br}(M,T)<\infty
.
It was also shown that some properties contradicting to finiteness of Assoud-Nagata dimension also forbid orders with finite
Br
,
and it was raised a question whether the existence of orders with finite
Br
is equivalent to finiteness of Assoud-Nagata dimension.

For metric spaces there are various definitions of dimension based on coverings by sets with bounded intersection multiplicity.
In order to establish similar results for
Lebesgue covering dimension
, we introduce the notion of
snake number
as an analogue of
Br
.

Definition 1
.

Let
M
be a metric space,
T
be a total order on
M
, and let
(U_{1},U_{2})
be an ordered pair of subsets of
M
.
We say that
(U_{1},U_{2})

contains a snake of length
s
if there exists a sequence of points

a_{0}<_{T}a_{1}<_{T}\dots<_{T}a_{s}

such that

U_{1}
contains the points
a_{0},a_{2},\dots,a_{2\lfloor s/2\rfloor}

and
U_{2}
contains the points

a_{1},a_{3},\dots,a_{2\lceil s/2\rceil-1}
.

Definition 2
.

Let
(M,T)
be an ordered metric space and let
x,y
be distinct points of
M
.

The snake number

\operatorname{Snake}_{T}(x,y)
is the greatest number
s
such that for any
\varepsilon>0
the pair of neighborhoods

(B_{\varepsilon}(x),B_{\varepsilon}(y))
contains a snake of length
s
.
If the greatest such number does not exist,
we say that
\operatorname{Snake}(x,y)=\infty
.

Definition 3
.

Let
M
be a metric space and
T
be a total order on
M
.
The
Snake number
of the ordered space
(M,T)
is defined as

\operatorname{Snake}(M,T):=\sup_{x,y\in M,x\neq y}\operatorname{Snake}_{T}(x,y).

The main result of this paper is

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
M
be a compact metric space.
Then the following conditions are equivalent:

(1)

the covering dimension
\dim M<\infty

(2)

there exists an order
T
on
M
such that
\operatorname{Snake}(M,T)<\infty
.

Let us specify the upper and lower bounds.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
(M,T)
be an ordered compact metric space such that
\operatorname{Snake}(M,T)\leqslant s
.
Then
\dim M\leqslant s
.

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
M
be a compact metric space with
\dim M\leqslant s
.
Then there exists an order
T
for which
\operatorname{Snake}(M,T)\leqslant 2s+1
.

One can find similarities with the following classical result.

Theorem 1.4

(
[
4
]
)
.

Let
M
be a compact metric space with covering dimension
\dim M\geq n
.
Suppose

is a continuous mapping of a closed subspace

of the Cantor set

onto the space

.
Then there exist
n+1
points
x_{0},\dots,x_{n}\in A
such that

f(x_{1})=\dots=f(x_{n})

.

(In fact, Hurewitz proved this result for small inductive dimension
\operatorname{ind}
that is equal to
\dim
for compact metric spaces).
From Theorem
1.2
one can conclude by passing to the limit the following fact

Proposition 1.5
.

Let
M
be a compact metric space with
\dim M\geq n
.
Suppose

f:A\to M
is a continuous mapping of a closed subspace
A
of
\mathbb{R}
onto the space
M
.
Then there exist
n+1
points
x_{0}<\dots<x_{n}
such that

f(x_{0})=f(x_{2})=\dots=f(x_{2\lfloor n/2\rfloor})

\operatorname{Snake}(S^{1},T^{\prime})\geqslant 2

Some basics of the topological dimension theory

Definition 4
.

Let
X
be a set and
\mathcal{A}
a family of subsets of

.
By the

\mathcal{A}
we mean the largest integer

\mathcal{A}
contains
n+1

.
A cover
\mathcal{B}
is a

of another cover
\mathcal{A}
of the same space if for every
B\in\mathcal{B}
there exists an

such that
B\subset A
.

Definition 5
.

To every metric space
M
one assigns the

of
M
, denoted by
\dim M
, which is
\infty
or an integer larger that or equal to
-1
; the definition of the function

is defined by the following conditions:

(1)

, where

if every finite open cover of the space

has a finite open refinement of order
\leqslant n
;

(2)

\dim M=n
if
\dim M\leqslant n
and
\dim M>n
, i.e. the inequality
\dim M\leqslant n-1
does not hold;

(3)

\dim M=\infty
if
\dim M>n
for
n=-1,0,1,\dots

Definition 6
.

To every metric space
M
one assigns the
small inductive dimension
of
M
, denoted by
\operatorname{ind}M
, which is
\infty
or an integer larger than or equal to -1; the definition of the function
\operatorname{ind}
is defined by the following conditions:

(1)

\operatorname{ind}M=-1
if and only if
M=\emptyset
;

(2)

\operatorname{ind}M\leqslant n
, where

, if for every point

there exists an open set

such that

and the dimension of its boundary

;

(3)

\operatorname{ind}M=n

(4)

\operatorname{ind}M=\infty

\operatorname{ind}M>0,1,2,\dots

Proof of Theorem

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
(M,T)
be an ordered metric space and let

\operatorname{Snake}(M,T)\leqslant n.

Let
M
be a disjoint union

, and suppose that

.
Then there exists an order

Proof.

For distinct points

x<_{T}y\text{ for any }y\in B_{\delta}(y_{0})\cap A.

We first show that for any

at least one of the relations
x_{0}<_{T^{\prime}}y_{0}
and
y_{0}<_{T^{\prime}}x_{0}
holds.
Denote

Y_{k}=B_{1/k}(y_{0})\cap A
.
For all sufficiently large

(X_{k},Y_{k})
and
(Y_{k},X_{k})
do not contain snakes of length
n+1
.
Consider the largest

such that there is a sequence of points

t_{1}<_{T}t_{2}<_{T}\dots<_{T}t_{m}

(i)

t_{i}
with odd

belong to
X_{k}
and all
t_{i}
with even

Y_{k}

or

(ii)

t_{i}
with odd

belong to
Y_{k}
and all
t_{i}
with even

belong to
X_{k}
.

holds.

If
t_{1}\in X_{k}
, then
t_{1}<_{T}y
for any

, otherwise the sequence

is longer.
Similarly, if
t_{1}\in Y_{k}
, then
t_{1}<_{T}x

. If the situation

holds for infinitely many

, otherwise

.

Assume that for

x_{0}<_{T^{\prime}}x_{1}<_{T^{\prime}}\dots<_{T^{\prime}}x_{m}=x_{1}

Take
\varepsilon_{0}>0
.
The set

\varepsilon_{2}<\varepsilon_{1}
. Similarly, in

.

Definition 7

Let
(M,T)
be an ordered metric space.
We call a subset

Let

\operatorname{Snake}(M,T)=s-1<\infty

Suppose

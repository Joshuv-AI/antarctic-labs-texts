Abstract




Subdifferential decomposition of 1D-regularized total variation with nonhomogeneous coefficients

Keywords: subdifferential decomposition, nonhomogeneous coefficients, quasilinear equation with singularity.

Abstract.
In this paper, we consider a convex function defined as a 1D-regularized total variation with nonhomogeneous coefficients, and prove the Main Theorem concerned with the decomposition of the subdifferential of this convex function to a weighted singular diffusion and a linear regular diffusion. The Main Theorem will be to enhance the previous regularity result for quasilinear equation with singularity, and moreover, it will be to provide some useful information in the advanced mathematical studies of grain boundary motion, based on KWC type energy.

1
Introduction

Let
\Omega:=(-L,L)\subset\mathbb{R}
be a one-dimensional spatial domain with a constant
0<L<\infty
, and let us define
H:=L^{2}(\Omega)
and
V:=H^{1}(\Omega)
. Let
0\leq\alpha\in V
and
0<\beta\in V
be fixed functions.

In this paper, we consider the following convex function on
H
:

\displaystyle\theta\in H\mapsto\Phi_{\alpha,\beta}(\theta):=V_{\alpha}(\theta)+W_{\beta}(\theta);

(1.1)

which is defined as a sum of two convex functions on
H
, defined as follows:

\displaystyle\displaystyle\theta\in H\mapsto V_{\alpha}(\theta):=\sup\left\{\begin{array}[]{l|l}\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\theta\partial_{x}\varphi\,dx,&\parbox{113.81102pt}{$\varphi\in V\cap C_{\mathrm{c}}(\Omega)$, such that $|\varphi|\leq\alpha$ on $\overline{\Omega}$}\end{array}\right\},

and

\displaystyle\displaystyle\theta\in H\mapsto W_{\beta}(\theta):=\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Omega}\beta|\partial_{x}\theta|^{2}dx,\,\mbox{ if }\theta\in V,\\[4.30554pt]
\infty,\mbox{ otherwise.}\end{array}\right.

The functional
V_{\alpha}
, defined in (
1
), is a kind of generalized total variation, so that the functional
\Phi_{\alpha,\beta}
, defined in (
1.1
), can be called a
regularized total variation
with nonhomogeneous coefficients
\alpha
and
\beta
.

On this basis, we set the goal to prove the following Main Theorem.

Main Theorem
(Decomposition of the subdifferential)
.
The subdifferential
\partial\Phi_{\alpha,\beta}
of the convex function
\Phi_{\alpha,\beta}\subset H\times H
is decomposed as follows:

\displaystyle\partial\Phi_{\alpha,\beta}=\partial V_{\alpha}+\partial W_{\beta}\mbox{ in }H\times H,

(1.5)

i.e.
\partial\Phi_{\alpha,\beta}
is represented as the sum the subdifferentials
\partial V_{\alpha}\subset H\times H
and
\partial W_{\beta}\subset H\times H
of the respective convex functions
V_{\alpha}
and
W_{\beta}
.

The equation (
1.5
) leads to the
H^{2}
-regularity of the following nonhomogeneous quasilinear equation with singularity:

\begin{cases}\displaystyle-\partial_{x}\left(\alpha(x)\frac{D\theta}{|D\theta|}+\beta(x)\partial_{x}\theta\right)=\theta^{*}\mbox{ with $\theta^{*}\in H$,}\\[6.45831pt]
\mbox{subject to the zero-Neumann type boundary condition.}\end{cases}

(1.6)

When the both
\alpha
and
\beta
are homogeneous (constants), we can obtain the
H^{2}
-regularity by using the mathematical method, developed in
[
12
]
, which is based on the general theory of PDEs (e.g.
[
10
]
). However, when
\alpha
and
\beta
are nonhomogeneous, the extra error terms brought by
\alpha
and
\beta
make it difficult to see
\theta\in H^{2}(\Omega)
in (
1.6
), by referring to the existing method. Hence, it can be said that our Main Theorem will be to enhance the previous method of
[
12
]
, and moreover, to report another variational approach based on the subdifferential.

In the meantime, the Main Theorem is motivated by the mathematical analysis of grain boundary motion, studied in
[
13
,
14
]
, and especially, the convex function
\Phi_{\alpha,\beta}
is based on the
KWC energy
, proposed by Kobayashi–Warren–Carter
[
9
]
. In this context, the variable
\theta
is the order parameter of crystalline orientation, and the nonhomogeneous coefficients
\alpha
and
\beta
are associated with another order parameter, such as the orientation order of grain in a polycrystal. In this light, our Main Theorem can be expected to provide useful information for some advanced problems that require smoothness of the system while including singularity, such as the optimal control problem governed by the KWC type model.

The proof of Main Theorem is divided in three Sections. In the next Section 2, we prepare notations and mathematical theories as the preliminaries. Additionally, in Section 3, we prove an auxiliary lemma associated with the approximating approach to the Main Theorem. Based on these, the final Section 4 is devoted to the proof of our Main Theorem.

2
Preliminaries

We begin by prescribing the assumptions and notations used throughout this paper.

Assumptions.

Throughout this paper, let
\Omega:=(-L,L)\subset\mathbb{R}
be a fixed spatial bounded domain with a constant
0<L<\infty
, and let
\Gamma:=\partial\Omega=\{-L,L\}
be the boundary of
\Omega
. Also, let
\partial_{x}
be the distributional spatial differential. On this basis, we define

\displaystyle H:=L^{2}(\Omega),

\displaystyle\,H_{\Gamma}:=\left\{\begin{array}[]{l|l}\tilde{z}&\tilde{z}:\Gamma\longrightarrow\mathbb{R}\end{array}\right\}~(\sim\mathbb{R}^{2}),\mbox{ and }V:=H^{1}(\Omega)~(\subset C(\overline{\Omega})).

Let
\alpha\in V
and
\beta\in V
be fixed functions, such that:

\min\alpha(\overline{\Omega})\geq 0,\mbox{ and }\min\beta(\overline{\Omega})>0.

(2.1)

Abstract notations.

For an abstract Banach space
X
, we denote by
|\cdot|_{X}
the norm of
X
. Let
I_{X}:X\longrightarrow X
be the identity map from
X
onto
X
. In particular, when
X
is a Hilbert space, we denote by
(\cdot,\cdot)_{X}
the inner product of
X
.

For any subset
A
of a Banach space
X
, let
\chi_{A}:X\longrightarrow\{0,1\}
be the characteristic function of
A
, i.e.:

\chi_{A}:w\in X\mapsto\chi_{A}(w):=\begin{cases}1,\mbox{ if $w\in A$,}\\[2.15277pt]
0,\mbox{ otherwise.}\end{cases}

Notations in convex analysis. (cf.
[
5
, Chapter II]
)

Let
X
be an abstract Hilbert space
X
. For a proper, lower semi-continuous (l.s.c.), and convex function
\Psi:X\longrightarrow(-\infty,\infty]
on a Hilbert space
X
, we denote by
D(\Psi)
the effective domain of
\Psi
. Also, we denote by
\partial\Psi
the subdifferential of
\Psi
. The subdifferential
\partial\Psi
corresponds to a weak differential of convex function
\Psi
, and it is known as a maximal monotone graph in the product space
X\times X
. The set
D(\partial\Psi):=\bigl\{z\in X\ |\ \partial\Psi(z)\neq\emptyset\bigr\}
is called the domain of
\partial\Psi
. We often use the notation “
[z_{0},z_{0}^{*}]\in\partial\Psi
in
X\times X
”, to mean that “
z_{0}^{*}\in\partial\Psi(z_{0})
in
X
for
z_{0}\in D(\partial\Psi)
”, by identifying the operator
\partial\Psi
with its graph in
X\times X
.

Example 1

(Examples of the subdifferential)
.

For any
\varepsilon\geq 0
, let
f^{\varepsilon}:\mathbb{R}\longrightarrow[0,\infty)
be a continuous and convex function, defined as follows:

f^{\varepsilon}:y\in\mathbb{R}\mapsto f^{\varepsilon}(y):=\sqrt{\varepsilon^{2}+|y|^{2}}\in[0,\infty).

(2.2)

When
\varepsilon>0
,
f^{\varepsilon}\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})
, and hence the subdifferential
\partial f^{\varepsilon}\subset\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}
coincides with the single-valued function of the standard differential
(f^{\varepsilon})^{\prime}\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})
, i.e.:

D(\partial f^{\varepsilon})=\mathbb{R},\mbox{ and }\partial f^{\varepsilon}(y)=(f^{\varepsilon})^{\prime}(y)=\frac{y}{\sqrt{\varepsilon^{2}+|y|^{2}}},~\mbox{for any $y\in\mathbb{R}$.}

Meanwhile, when
\varepsilon=0
, the corresponding function
f^{0}
coincides with the function of absolute value
|\cdot|:\mathbb{R}\longrightarrow[0,\infty)
. Hence, the subdifferential
\partial f^{0}
of this case coincides with the set-valued signal function
\mathop{\mathrm{Sgn}}\nolimits:\mathbb{R}\longrightarrow 2^{\mathbb{R}}
, which is defined as follows:

\displaystyle\xi\in\mathbb{R}\mapsto\mathop{\mathrm{Sgn}}\nolimits(\xi):=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\lx@intercol\displaystyle\frac{\xi}{|\xi|},\mbox{ if $\xi\neq 0$,}\hfil\lx@intercol\\[12.91663pt]
[-1,1],&\mbox{otherwise,}\end{array}\right.

i.e.:

D(\partial f^{0})=D(\partial|\cdot|)=\mathbb{R},\mbox{ and }\partial f^{0}(y)=\partial|{}\cdot{}|(y)=\mathop{\mathrm{Sgn}}\nolimits(y),\mbox{ for any $y\in\mathbb{R}$.}

Next, we mention about a notion of functional convergence, known as “Mosco-convergence”.

Definition 2.1

(Mosco-convergence: cf.
[
11
]
)
.

Let
X
be an abstract Hilbert space. Let
\Psi:X\longrightarrow(-\infty,\infty]
be a proper, l.s.c., and convex function, and let
\{\Psi_{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
be a sequence of proper, l.s.c., and convex functions
\Psi_{n}:X\longrightarrow(-\infty,\infty]
,
n=1,2,3,\dots
. Then, it is said that
\Psi_{n}\to\Psi
on
X
, in the sense of Mosco, as
n\to\infty
, iff. the following two conditions are fulfilled.

(M1) The condition of lower-bound:

\displaystyle\varliminf_{n\to\infty}\Psi_{n}(\check{w}_{n})\geq\Psi(\check{w})
, if
\check{w}\in X
,
\{\check{w}_{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}\subset X
, and
\check{w}_{n}\to\check{w}
weakly in
X
, as
n\to\infty
.

(M2) The condition of optimality:

for any
\hat{w}\in D(\Psi)
, there exists a sequence
\{\hat{w}_{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}\subset X
such that
\hat{w}_{n}\to\hat{w}
in
X
and
\Psi_{n}(\hat{w}_{n})\to\Psi(\hat{w})
, as
n\to\infty
.

Remark 1
.

Let
X
,
\Psi
, and
\{\Psi_{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
be as in Definition
2.1
. Then, the following facts hold.

(Fact 1)

(cf.
[
2
, Theorem 3.66]
) Let us assume that

\Psi_{n}\to\Psi\mbox{ on $X$, in the sense of Mosco, as $n\to\infty$,}\vskip-4.30554pt

and

\left\{~\parbox{284.52756pt}{$[w,w^{*}]\in X\times X$, ~ $[w_{n},w_{n}^{*}]\in\partial\Psi_{n}$ in $X\times X$, $n\in\mathbb{N}$,
\\
$w_{n}\to w$ in $X$, and $w_{n}^{*}\to w^{*}$ weakly in $X$, as $n\to\infty$.
}\right.

Then, it holds that:

[w,w^{*}]\in\partial\Psi\mbox{ in $X\times X$, and }\Psi_{n}(w_{n})\to\Psi(w)\mbox{, as $n\to\infty$.}

(Fact 2)

(cf.
[
6
, Lemma 4.1]
and
[
8
, Appendix]
) Let
N\in\mathbb{N}
denote a constant of dimension, and let
S\subset\mathbb{R}^{N}
be a bounded open set. Then, under the assumptions and notations as in (Fact 1), a sequence
\{\widehat{\Psi}_{n}^{S}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
of proper, l.s.c., and convex functions on
L^{2}(S;X)
, defined as:

z\in L^{2}(S;X)\mapsto\widehat{\Psi}_{n}^{S}(z):=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\lx@intercol\displaystyle\int_{S}\Psi_{n}(z(y))\,dt,\hfil\lx@intercol\\[4.30554pt]
&\mbox{ if $\Psi_{n}(z)\in L^{1}(S)$,}\\[4.30554pt]
\infty,&\mbox{ otherwise,}\end{array}\right.\mbox{for $n=1,2,3,\dots$;}

converges to a proper, l.s.c., and convex function
\widehat{\Psi}^{S}
on
L^{2}(S;X)
, defined as:

z\in L^{2}(S;X)\mapsto\widehat{\Psi}^{S}(z):=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\lx@intercol\displaystyle\int_{S}\Psi(z(y))\,dt,\mbox{ if $\Psi(z)\in L^{1}(S)$,}\hfil\lx@intercol\\[8.61108pt]
\infty,&\mbox{ otherwise;}\end{array}\right.

on
L^{2}(S;X)
, in the sense of Mosco, as
n\to\infty
.

Example 2

(Example of Mosco-convergence)
.

Let
\{f^{\varepsilon}\}_{\varepsilon\geq 0}\subset C(\mathbb{R})
be the sequence of nonexpansive convex functions, as in (
2.2
). Then, for any
\varepsilon_{0}\geq 0,f^{\varepsilon}\to f^{\varepsilon_{0}},\mbox{ uniformly on }\mathbb{R},\mbox{ as }\varepsilon\to\varepsilon_{0}
, so that:

f^{\varepsilon}\to f^{\varepsilon_{0}}\mbox{ on $\mathbb{R}$, in the sense of Mosco, as $\varepsilon\to\varepsilon_{0}$.}

Basic and specific notations.

For arbitrary
r_{0}
,
s_{0}\in[-\infty,\infty]
, we define:

r_{0}\vee s_{0}:=\max\{r_{0},s_{0}\}\ \mbox{and}\ r_{0}\wedge s_{0}:=\min\{r_{0},s_{0}\},

and in particular, we set:

[r]^{+}:=r\vee 0\ \mbox{and}\ [r]^{-}:=-(r\wedge 0),\mbox{ for any $r\in\mathbb{R}$.}

Finally, we remark on the specific functionals
V_{\alpha}:H\longrightarrow[0,\infty]
,
W_{\beta}:H\longrightarrow[0,\infty]
, and
\Phi_{\alpha,\beta}:H\longrightarrow[0,\infty]
, that are defined in (
1
), (
1
), and (
1.1
), respectively.

Remark 2
.

(cf.
[
1
,
4
]
)
The functional
V_{\alpha}
coincides with the so-called
lower semi-continuous envelope
of the following convex function:

\theta\in W^{1,1}(\Omega)\mapsto\widetilde{V}_{\alpha}(\theta):=\int_{\Omega}\alpha|\partial_{x}\theta|\,dx\in[0,\infty),

more precisely,

\displaystyle V_{\alpha}(\theta)=\inf\left\{\begin{array}[]{l|l}\displaystyle\varliminf_{i\to\infty}\widetilde{V}_{\alpha}(\tilde{\vartheta}_{i})&\parbox{113.81102pt}{$\{\tilde{\vartheta}_{i}\}_{i=1}^{\infty}\subset W^{1,1}(\Omega)$, and $\tilde{\vartheta}_{i}\to\theta$ in $H$, as $i\to\infty$}\end{array}\right\},

for any

.

In the light of (
1
) and (

), we can verify the following facts.

(Fact 3)

V_{\alpha}
is a proper, l.s.c., and convex function on

, such that:

•

the restriction

coincides with
\widetilde{V}_{\alpha}
;

•

, and

if

.

(Fact 4)

For any

, there exists

such that
\vartheta_{i}\to\theta
in

, and
\widetilde{V}_{\alpha}(\vartheta_{i})\to V_{\alpha}(\theta)
, as

.

Remark 3
.

The functional
W_{\beta}
is a proper, l.s.c., and convex function on
H
, such that
D(W_{\beta})=V
. Moreover, the subdifferential
\partial W_{\beta}\subset H\times H
is a single valued operator, such that

[\theta,\theta^{*}]\in\partial W_{\beta}\mbox{ in $H\times H$, iff. $\beta\partial_{x}\theta\in H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)$, and $\theta^{*}=-\partial_{x}(\beta\partial_{x}\theta)$ in $H$.}

Remark 4
.

Let us fix
\varepsilon\geq 0
and let
\Phi_{\alpha,\beta}^{\varepsilon}
be a function on

, defined as follows:

\displaystyle\displaystyle\Phi_{\alpha,\beta}^{\varepsilon}(\theta):=\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\alpha\sqrt{\varepsilon^{2}+|\partial_{x}\theta|^{2}}\,dx+\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Omega}\beta|\partial_{x}\theta|^{2}dx,\,\mbox{ if }\theta\in V,\\[8.61108pt]
\infty,\mbox{ otherwise.}\end{array}\right.

Under the assumption (
2.1
), the functions
\Phi^{\varepsilon}_{\alpha,\beta}
, for

, are proper, l.s.c., and convex on

. Especially, when

, the corresponding functional
\Phi^{0}_{\alpha,\beta}
coincides with the convex function

, defined in (

).

Remark 5
.

Let us fix any

, and let us define a map
\mathcal{A}^{\varepsilon}:D(\mathcal{A}^{\varepsilon})\subset H\longrightarrow H
, by putting:

\theta\in D(\mathcal{A}^{\varepsilon})\subset H\mapsto\mathcal{A}^{\varepsilon}\theta:=-\partial_{x}\bigl(\alpha(f^{\varepsilon})^{\prime}(\partial_{x}\theta)+\beta\partial_{x}\theta\bigr).

Then, by applying the standard variational technique, we can observe that:

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}^{\varepsilon}=\partial\Phi_{\alpha,\beta}^{\varepsilon}\mbox{ in }H\times H.

3
Auxiliary lemma

In this Section, we prove an auxiliary lemma which is associated with the approximating approach to the Main Theorem.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
\{\varepsilon_{m}\}_{m=1}^{\infty}\subset(0,\infty)
be arbitrary sequence such that
\varepsilon_{m}\to 0
as
m\to\infty
. Then, for the sequence
\{\Phi_{\alpha,\beta}^{\varepsilon_{m}}\}_{m=1}^{\infty}
, it holds that:

\displaystyle\Phi_{\alpha,\beta}^{\varepsilon_{m}}\to\Phi_{\alpha,\beta}\mbox{ on }H,\mbox{ in the sense of Mosco},\mbox{ as }m\to\infty.

Proof.

First, we show the lower-bound condition (M1) in Definition
2.1
.
Let
\theta\in H
and
\{\theta^{m}\}_{m=1}^{\infty}\subset H
be such that:

\displaystyle\theta^{m}\to\theta\mbox{ weakly in }H,\mbox{ as }m\to\infty.

(3.1)

Then, it is sufficient to consider only the case when
\varliminf_{m\to\infty}\Phi_{\alpha,\beta}^{\varepsilon_{m}}(\theta^{m})<\infty
, since the other case is trivial.
So, by taking a subsequence
\{m_{k}\}_{k=1}^{\infty}\subset\{m\}
, one can say that:

\displaystyle\varliminf_{m\to\infty}\Phi_{\alpha,\beta}^{\varepsilon_{m}}(\theta^{m})=\lim_{k\to\infty}\Phi_{\alpha,\beta}^{\varepsilon_{m_{k}}}(\theta^{m_{k}})<\infty.

(3.2)

With (
4
), (
3.1
), and (
3.2
) in mind, we further see that:

\displaystyle\partial_{x}\theta^{m_{k}}

\displaystyle\,\to\partial_{x}\theta\mbox{ weakly in }H,

\displaystyle\mbox{ and }\sqrt{\beta}\partial_{x}\theta^{m_{k}}

\displaystyle\,\to\sqrt{\beta}\partial_{x}\theta\mbox{ weakly in }H,\mbox{ as }k\to\infty,

(3.3)

by taking more one subsequence if necessary.
In the light of (
2.2
), (
3.1
)–(
3
), Remark
3
, weakly lower semi-continuity of
\Phi_{\alpha,\beta}
, the lower-bound condition can be verified (M1), as follows:

\displaystyle\displaystyle\varliminf_{k\to\infty}\Phi_{\alpha,\beta}^{\varepsilon_{m_{k}}}(\theta^{m_{k}})

\displaystyle\,\geq\varliminf_{k\to\infty}\Phi_{\alpha,\beta}(\theta^{m_{k}})\geq\Phi_{\alpha,\beta}(\theta).

Next, we show the optimality condition (M2) in Definition
2.1
.
Let us fix any
\theta\in D(\Phi_{\alpha,\beta})(=V)
, and let us take a sequence
\{\varphi^{k}\}_{k=1}^{\infty}\subset C^{\infty}(\overline{\Omega})
such that:

\displaystyle\varphi^{k}\to\theta\mbox{ in }V,\mbox{ and in the pointwise sense, a.e. in }\Omega,\mbox{ as }k\to\infty.

(3.4)

By (
3.4
) and Lebesgue’s dominated convergence theorem, we can configure a sequence
\{m_{k}\}_{k=0}^{\infty}\subset\mathbb{N}
such that
1=:m_{0}<m_{1}<m_{2}<\cdots<m_{k}\uparrow\infty
, as
k\to\infty
, and for any
k\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}
,

\displaystyle\displaystyle\sup_{m\geq m_{k}}\bigl|f^{\varepsilon_{m}}(\partial_{x}\varphi^{k})-|\partial_{x}\varphi^{k}|\bigr|_{L^{1}(\Omega)}<\frac{1}{2^{k}(|\alpha|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}+1)}.

(3.5)

Based on these, let us define:

\theta^{m}:=\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\varphi^{k}\mbox{ if }m_{k}\leq m<m_{k+1},\mbox{ for }k\in\mathbb{N},\\[2.84526pt]
\varphi^{1}\mbox{ if }1\leq m<m_{1},\end{array}\mbox{ for any }m\in\mathbb{N}.\right.

(3.6)

Taking into account (
3.4
)–(
3.6
) and Hölder’s inequality, we obtain that:

\displaystyle\displaystyle\bigl|

\displaystyle\,\Phi_{\alpha,\beta}^{\varepsilon_{m}}(\theta^{m})-\Phi_{\alpha,\beta}(\theta)\bigr|

\displaystyle\,\leq\left|\int_{\Omega}\bigl(\alpha f^{\varepsilon_{m}}(\partial_{x}\theta^{m})-\alpha|\partial_{x}\theta|\bigr)\,dx\right|+\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Omega}\beta\bigl||\partial_{x}\theta^{m}|^{2}-|\partial_{x}\theta|^{2}\bigr|\,dx

\displaystyle\,\leq|\alpha|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\left(\int_{\Omega}\sup_{m\geq m_{k}}\bigl|f^{\varepsilon_{m}}(\partial_{x}\varphi^{k})-|\partial_{x}\varphi^{k}|\bigr|dx+\int_{\Omega}\bigl||\partial_{x}\varphi^{k}|-|\partial_{x}\theta|\bigr|\,dx\right)

\displaystyle\,\qquad+\frac{|\beta|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}}{2}|\varphi^{k}-\theta|_{V}\left(\int_{\Omega}2(|\partial_{x}\varphi^{k}|^{2}+|\partial_{x}\theta|^{2})\,dx\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\,\leq\frac{1}{2^{k}}+|\varphi^{k}-\theta|_{V}\cdot

\displaystyle\,\qquad\cdot\left(\sqrt{2L}|\alpha|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}+\frac{|\beta|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}}{2}\left(\int_{\Omega}2(|\partial_{x}\varphi^{k}|^{2}+|\partial_{x}\theta|^{2})\,dx\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\right),

\displaystyle\,\mbox{ for any }k\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}\mbox{ and any }m\geq m_{k},

and therefore,

\displaystyle\Phi_{\alpha,\beta}^{\varepsilon_{m}}(\theta^{m})\to\Phi_{\alpha,\beta}(\theta),\mbox{ as }m\to\infty.

Thus, we conclude this lemma.
∎

4
Proof of Main Theorem

In this Section, we give the proof of Main Theorem.
Let us define a set-valued map
\mathcal{A}^{0}:D(\mathcal{A}^{0})\subset H\longrightarrow 2^{H}
, by putting:

D(\mathcal{A}^{0}):=\left\{\begin{array}[]{l|ll}\theta\in V&\begin{array}[]{lll}\lx@intercol\mbox{ there exists }\varpi^{*}\in L^{\infty}(\Omega)\mbox{ such that }\hfil\lx@intercol\\[1.07639pt]
&\bullet~\varpi^{*}\in\mathop{\mathrm{Sgn}}\nolimits(\partial_{x}\theta)\mbox{ a.e. in }\Omega\\[1.07639pt]
&\bullet~\alpha\varpi^{*}+\beta\partial_{x}\theta\in H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)\end{array}\end{array}\right\},

(4.1)

and

\displaystyle\theta

\displaystyle\,\in D(\mathcal{A}^{0})\subset H

\displaystyle\,\mapsto\mathcal{A}^{0}\theta:=\left\{\begin{array}[]{l|l}\theta^{*}\in H&\begin{array}[]{lll}\lx@intercol\theta^{*}=-\partial_{x}\bigl(\alpha\varpi^{*}+\beta\partial_{x}\theta\bigr)\mbox{ in }H,\hfil\lx@intercol\\[1.07639pt]
&\mbox{for some }\varpi^{*}\in L^{\infty}(\Omega),\mbox{ satisfying }\\[1.07639pt]
&\varpi^{*}\in\mathop{\mathrm{Sgn}}\nolimits(\partial_{x}\theta)\mbox{ a.e. in }\Omega\end{array}\end{array}\right\}.

We prove Main Theorem in accordance with the following two Steps.

Step
1
:

\mathcal{A}^{0}=\partial\Phi_{\alpha,\beta}
in
H\times H
.

Step
2
:

\partial\Phi_{\alpha,\beta}=\partial V_{\alpha}+\partial W_{\beta}
in
H\times H
.

Verification of Step
1
.

First, we show
\mathcal{A}^{0}\subset\Phi_{\alpha,\beta}
in
H\times H
.
Let us assume
\theta\in D(\mathcal{A}^{0})
and
\theta^{*}\in\mathcal{A}^{0}\theta
.
Then, by (
4
), there exists
\varpi^{*}\in L^{\infty}(\Omega)
such that:

\displaystyle\varpi^{*}\in\mathop{\mathrm{Sgn}}\nolimits(\partial_{x}\theta)\mbox{ a.e. in }\Omega\mbox{ and }\theta^{*}=-\partial_{x}(\alpha\varpi^{*}+\beta\partial_{x}\theta)\mbox{ in }H.

(4.5)

From Remark
2
, (
1
), (
4.5
), and Young’s inequality, we can compute that:

\displaystyle\displaystyle(\theta^{*},\varphi-\theta)_{H}

\displaystyle\,=\bigl(-\partial_{x}\bigl(\alpha\varpi^{*}+\beta\partial_{x}\theta\bigr),\varphi-\theta\bigr)_{H}

\displaystyle\,=\int_{\Omega}\alpha\varpi^{*}\partial_{x}(\varphi-\theta)\,dx+\int_{\Omega}\beta\,\partial_{x}\theta\,\partial_{x}(\varphi-\theta)\,dx

\displaystyle\,\leq\int_{\Omega}\alpha\bigl(|\partial_{x}\varphi|-|\partial_{x}\theta|\bigr)\,dx+\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Omega}\beta(|\partial_{x}\varphi|^{2}-|\partial_{x}\theta|^{2})\,dx

\displaystyle\,=\Phi_{\alpha,\beta}(\varphi)-\Phi_{\alpha,\beta}(\theta),\mbox{ for any }\varphi\in V.

This implies that:

\displaystyle\theta\in D(\partial\Phi_{\alpha,\beta})\mbox{ and }\theta^{*}\in\partial\Phi_{\alpha,\beta}(\theta)\mbox{ in }H.

Thus, the inclusion
\mathcal{A}^{0}\subset\partial\Phi_{\alpha,\beta}\mbox{ in }H\times H
is verified.

Next, we prove the equality
(\mathcal{A}^{0}+I_{H})H=H
.
Since, the inclusion
(\mathcal{A}^{0}+I_{H})H\subset H
is trivial, it is sufficient to prove the converse inclusion.

Let us take any
h\in H
.
Then, by Remark
5
and Minty’s theorem (cf.
[
3
, Theorem 2.2]
), we can configure a class of function
\{\theta^{\varepsilon}\}_{\varepsilon>0}\subset V
, by setting
\{\theta^{\varepsilon}:=(\mathcal{A}^{\varepsilon}+I_{H})^{-1}h\}_{\varepsilon>0}\mbox{ in }H,

i.e.

\displaystyle h-\theta^{\varepsilon}=\mathcal{A}^{\varepsilon}\theta^{\varepsilon}=\partial\Phi_{\alpha,\beta}^{\varepsilon}(\theta^{\varepsilon})\mbox{ in }H,\mbox{ for any }\varepsilon>0,

(4.6)

so that:

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\bigl(\alpha(f^{\varepsilon})^{\prime}(\partial_{x}\theta^{\varepsilon})

\displaystyle\,+\beta\partial_{x}\theta^{\varepsilon}\bigr)\partial_{x}\varphi\,dx+\int_{\Omega}\theta^{\varepsilon}\varphi\,dx

\displaystyle\,=\int_{\Omega}h\varphi\,dx,\mbox{ for any }\varphi\in V,\mbox{ and any }\varepsilon>0.

(4.7)

In the variational form (
4
), let us put
\varphi=\theta^{\varepsilon}
.
Then, with (
2.2
) and Young’s inequality in mind, we deduce that:

\displaystyle\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}|\theta^{\varepsilon}|_{H}^{2}+|\sqrt{\beta}\partial_{x}\theta^{\varepsilon}|_{H}^{2}\leq\frac{1}{2}|h|_{H}^{2},\mbox{ for any }\varepsilon>0.

(4.8)

The above (
4.8
) enable us to take a function
\theta\in V
and a sequence
\varepsilon_{1}>\varepsilon_{2}>\varepsilon_{3}>\cdots>\varepsilon_{m}\downarrow 0
, as
m\to\infty
, such that:

\displaystyle\theta^{\varepsilon_{m}}

\displaystyle\,\to\theta\mbox{ in }H,\mbox{ weakly in }V,

\displaystyle\mbox{ and }\sqrt{\beta}\partial_{x}\theta^{\varepsilon_{m}}

\displaystyle\,\to\sqrt{\beta}\partial_{x}\theta\mbox{ weakly in }H,\mbox{ as }m\to\infty.

(4.9)

In the light of Lemma
3.1
, (
4.6
), (
4
), and (Fact 1), it follows that:

\displaystyle h-\theta\in\partial\Phi_{\alpha,\beta}(\theta)\mbox{ in }H,\mbox{ and }\Phi_{\alpha,\beta}^{\varepsilon_{m}}(\theta^{\varepsilon_{m}})\to\Phi_{\alpha,\beta}(\theta),\mbox{ as }m\to\infty.

(4.10)

Also, by Remark
2
, (
2.2
), (
4
), (
4.10
), and weakly lower semi-continuity of the norm
|\cdot|_{H}
, we can compute that:

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Omega}\beta|\partial_{x}\theta|^{2}\,dx

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2}\varliminf_{m\to\infty}\int_{\Omega}\beta|\partial_{x}\theta^{\varepsilon_{m}}|^{2}\,dx\leq\frac{1}{2}\varlimsup_{m\to\infty}\int_{\Omega}\beta|\partial_{x}\theta^{\varepsilon_{m}}|^{2}\,dx

\displaystyle\leq\lim_{m\to\infty}\Phi_{\alpha,\beta}^{\varepsilon_{m}}(\theta^{\varepsilon_{m}})-\varliminf_{m\to\infty}\int_{\Omega}\alpha f^{\varepsilon_{m}}(\partial_{x}\theta^{\varepsilon_{m}})\,dx

\displaystyle\leq\Phi_{\alpha,\beta}(\theta)-\int_{\Omega}\alpha|\partial_{x}\theta|\,dx=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Omega}\beta|\partial_{x}\theta|^{2}\,dx.

(4.11)

Having in mind (
4
), (
4
), and the uniform convexity of
L^{2}
-based topologies, it is deduce that:

\sqrt{\beta}\partial_{x}\theta^{\varepsilon_{m}}\to\sqrt{\beta}\partial_{x}\theta\mbox{ in }H,\mbox{ as }m\to\infty.

(4.12)

Furthermore, by (
2.1
), (
4
), and (
4.12
), we obtain that:

\theta^{\varepsilon_{m}}\to\theta\mbox{ in }V,\mbox{ and }\partial_{x}\theta^{\varepsilon_{m}}\to\partial_{x}\theta\mbox{ in }H,\mbox{ as }m\to\infty.

(4.13)

In the meantime, by Example
1
,
\displaystyle|(f^{\varepsilon_{m}})^{\prime}(\partial_{x}\theta^{\varepsilon_{m}})|\leq 1\mbox{ a.e. in }\Omega,\mbox{ for any }m\in\mathbb{N},

and one can say

\displaystyle(f^{\varepsilon_{m}})^{\prime}(\partial_{x}\theta^{\varepsilon_{m}})\to\varpi^{*}

\displaystyle\,\mbox{ weakly-$*$ in }L^{\infty}(\Omega),\mbox{ as }m\to\infty,

\displaystyle\,\mbox{ for some }\varpi^{*}\in L^{\infty}(\Omega),

(4.14)

by taking a subsequence if necessary.

From (
1
), (
4.13
), (
4
), Example
2
, (Fact 1), and
[
5
, Proposition 2.16]
, it is inferred that:

\displaystyle\varpi^{*}\in\mathop{\mathrm{Sgn}}\nolimits(\partial_{x}\theta)\mbox{ a.e. in }\Omega.

(4.15)

On account of (
4.12
)–(
4
), letting
m\to\infty
in (
4
) yields that:

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\bigl(\alpha\varpi^{*}+\beta\partial_{x}\theta\bigr)\partial_{x}\varphi\,dx+\int_{\Omega}\theta\varphi\,dx=\int_{\Omega}h\varphi\,dx,\mbox{ for any }\varphi\in V.

(4.16)

In particular, putting
\varphi=\varphi_{0}\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
in (
4.16
), we have:

\displaystyle(h-\theta,\varphi_{0})_{H}=\int_{\Omega}\bigl(\alpha\varpi^{*}+\beta\partial_{x}\theta,\partial_{x}\varphi_{0}\bigr)\,dx,\mbox{ for any }\varphi_{0}\in H_{0}^{1}(\Omega),

which implies:

\displaystyle-\partial_{x}\bigl(\alpha\varpi^{*}+\beta\partial_{x}\theta\bigr)=h-\theta\in H,\mbox{ in }\mathscr{D}^{\prime}(\Omega).

(4.17)

In addition, we observe that:

\displaystyle\,\Bigl(\alpha\varpi^{*}+\beta\partial_{x}\theta,\psi\Bigr)_{H_{\Gamma}}=\left[\rule{-1.0pt}{12.0pt}\bigl(\alpha(x)\varpi^{*}(x)+\beta(x)\partial_{x}\theta(x)\bigr)\psi(x)\right]_{-L}^{L}

\displaystyle\,~=\int_{\Omega}\partial_{x}\bigl(\bigl(\alpha\varpi^{*}+\beta\partial_{x}\theta\bigr)[\psi]^{\mathrm{ex}}\bigr)\,dx

\displaystyle\,~=-\int_{\Omega}(h-\theta)[\psi]^{\mathrm{ex}}\,dx+\int_{\Omega}\bigl(\alpha\varpi^{*}+\beta\partial_{x}\theta\bigr)\partial_{x}[\psi]^{\mathrm{ex}}\,dx

\displaystyle\,~=0,\mbox{ for any }\psi\in H_{\Gamma}\mbox{ with any extension }[\psi]^{\mathrm{ex}}\in V.

(4.18)

(
4.17
) and (
4
) lead to:

\displaystyle\alpha\varpi^{*}+\beta\partial_{x}\theta\in H_{0}^{1}(\Omega).

(4.19)

As a consequence of (

), (

), (
4.15
), and (

), we obtain that:

\displaystyle(\mathcal{A}^{0}+I_{H})\theta=h\mbox{ in }H,\mbox{ i.e. }h\in(\mathcal{A}^{0}+I_{H})H,

and we verify
H\subset(\mathcal{A}^{0}+I_{H})H
.

Finally, the inclusion
\mathcal{A}^{0}\subset\partial\Phi_{\alpha,\beta}
in
H\times H
, and the equality

enable us to apply Minty’s theorem (cf.
[

),
and to verify that
\mathcal{A}^{0}
is a maximal monotone. Moreover, the inclusion
\mathcal{A}^{0}\subset\partial\Phi_{\alpha,\beta}
and the maximality of
\mathcal{A}^{0}
will lead to the coincidence
\mathcal{A}^{0}=\partial\Phi_{\alpha,\beta}
in
H\times H
.

Thus we finish the proof of Step 1.

Verification of Step

.

By the general theory of the convex analysis
[

, we immediately have
\partial\Phi_{\alpha,\beta}\supset\partial V_{\alpha}+\partial W_{\beta}\mbox{ in }H\times H.

So, we prove the converse inclusion:

\displaystyle\partial\Phi_{\alpha,\beta}\subset\partial V_{\alpha}+\partial W_{\beta}\mbox{ in }H\times H.

(4.20)

Let us take any

in
H\times H
, and apply the result of previous Step 1, to have a function
\varpi^{*}\in L^{\infty}(\Omega)
as in (

On this basis, we verify this Step 2, via the verifications of four Claims.

Claim
\sharp 1)
.

\theta\in H^{2}(\Omega)
and
\partial_{x}\theta\in H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)
.

For every
a\geq 0
and
b>0
, let
\rho_{(a,b)}:\mathbb{R}\longrightarrow 2^{\mathbb{R}}
be a set-valued function, defined as:

\displaystyle\rho_{(a,b)}(r):=a\mathop{\mathrm{Sgn}}\nolimits(r)+br\subset\mathbb{R},\mbox{ for any }r\in\mathbb{R},

(4.21)

and let
\rho_{(a,b)}^{*}
be the inverse of
\rho_{(a,b)}
.
Then, as is easily checked from (

) and (

),

\displaystyle\displaystyle\rho_{(a,b)}^{*}

\displaystyle\,:r\in\mathbb{R}\mapsto\frac{[r-a]^{+}-[r+a]^{-}}{b}\in\mathbb{R},

(4.22)

i.e.
(\rho_{(a,b)})^{*}
is a single-valued Lipschitz function, such that

\displaystyle\displaystyle 0\leq(\rho_{(a,b)}^{*})^{\prime}\leq\frac{1}{b}\mbox{ on }\mathbb{R},\mbox{ for every }a\geq 0\mbox{ and }b>0.

Here, from (
4.21
), (
4.22
), and Step 1, we immediately see that:

\displaystyle\tilde{\theta}:=

\displaystyle\,\rho_{(\alpha(\cdot),\beta(\cdot))}(\partial_{x}\theta)=\alpha\varpi^{*}+\beta\partial_{x}\theta\in H_{0}^{1}(\Omega),\mbox{ and }\theta^{*}=-\partial_{x}\tilde{\theta}\mbox{ in }H.

(4.23)

Therefore, having in mind (
4.22
) and (
4.23
), and applying the generalized chain rule in BV-theory
[
1
, Theorem 3.99]
, it is inferred that:

\displaystyle\displaystyle\partial_{x}\theta

\displaystyle\,=(\rho_{(\alpha(\cdot),\beta(\cdot))}^{*})(\tilde{\theta})=\frac{[\tilde{\theta}-\alpha]^{+}-[\tilde{\theta}+\alpha]^{-}}{\beta}\in H_{0}^{1}(\Omega),

\displaystyle\displaystyle\partial_{x}^{2}\theta=

\displaystyle\,\partial_{x}\left[\rule{-1.0pt}{12.0pt}\frac{[\tilde{\theta}-\alpha]^{+}-[\tilde{\theta}+\alpha]^{-}}{\beta}\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\,\frac{1}{\beta}\bigl[\rule{-1.0pt}{12.0pt}\chi_{(\alpha(\cdot),\infty)}+\chi_{(-\infty,-\alpha(\cdot))}\bigr](\tilde{\theta})\partial_{x}\tilde{\theta}-\frac{\partial_{x}\beta}{\beta^{2}}\bigl([\tilde{\theta}-\alpha]^{+}-[\tilde{\theta}+\alpha]^{-}\bigr)\in H.

Thus, Claim
\sharp 1)
is verified.

Claim
\sharp 2)
.

\beta\partial_{x}\theta\in H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)

Claim

.

\alpha\varpi^{*}\in H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)

By using (

, and the integration by part, we can observe that:

\displaystyle\alpha\varpi^{*}=\tilde{\theta}-\beta\partial_{x}\theta\in H_{0}^{1}(\Omega),

(4.24)

and

\displaystyle\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}

\displaystyle\,-\partial_{x}(\alpha\varpi^{*})(\varphi-\theta)\,dx=\int_{\Omega}\alpha\varpi^{*}\partial_{x}(\varphi-\theta)\,dx

\displaystyle\,\leq\int_{\Omega}\alpha|\partial_{x}\varphi|\,dx-\int_{\Omega}\alpha|\partial_{x}\theta|\,dx,\mbox{ for any }\varphi\in W^{1,1}(\Omega).

Next, let us take any

such that:

\displaystyle\varphi_{i}\to z\mbox{ in }H,\mbox{ and }\widetilde{V}_{\alpha}(\varphi_{i})\left(=\int_{\Omega}\alpha|\partial_{x}\varphi_{i}|\,dx\right)\to V_{\alpha}(z),\mbox{ as }i\to\infty.

Besides, putting

\displaystyle(-\partial_{x}(\alpha\varpi^{*}),

∎

5
Conclusion

In this paper, the regularized total variation functional with nonhomogeneous coefficients is considered, and it is concluded that the subdifferential of this functional is decomposed to the sum of a weighted singular diffusion and a weighted linear diffusion. The result, stated in the Main Theorem, is to guarantee the
H^{2}
-regularity of the nonhomogeneously weighted quasilinear equations. The novelty of this work is in the point that the result is obtained by means of the approximation based on Mosco-convergence, and the generalized chain rule in BV-theory
[
1
, Theorem 3.99]
. Indeed, the mathematical method adopted here is different with the traditional approach based on the PDE-theory (cf.
[
10
]
), and also, it would be a simple method to extend the result of the previous work
[
12
]
.

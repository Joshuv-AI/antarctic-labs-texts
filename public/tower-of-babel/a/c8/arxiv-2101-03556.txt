Introduction

In many areas of geometric measure theory and geometric analysis the so-called porous sets have a significant role.
The corresponding literature is so huge that we mention only the groundbreaking papers
[
4
,
27
,
16
,
9
]
,
the beautiful survey
[
17
]
, and the monograph
[
13
]
.
Roughly speaking,
S
is porous if, for any point
x\in S
, there are cubic holes located in
\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus S
arbitrary close to
x

whose diameter is comparable with the distance to the point
x
.
Under a different nomenclature, porosity was used already in 1920 by A. Denjoy
[
3
]
.
As far as we know, E. P. Dolzenko was the first who used the term ‘‘porous set’’
[
4
]
.

There are intimate connections between porosity properties of sets and their dimensions. It was proved in
[
11
]

that a set
S\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
is porous if, and only if, its Assouad dimension is strictly less than
n
.
The situation becomes more complicated in the context of the Hausdorff dimension.
The papers
[
16
,
9
,
14
]
contain results claiming that a ‘‘sufficiently strong porosity’’ of a given set
S\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}

implies existence of an appropriate upper bound on its Hausdorff dimension. However (in contrast to Assouad dimension), one can construct a
nonporous set
S\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
whose Hausdorff dimension is strictly less
than
n
.
The main goal of the present paper is to understand how the behavior of the Hausdorff contents of intersections of cubes

Q\cap S
with a given set
S\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
affects porosity-type properties of
S
.
Such sort of questions arose naturally in the study of trace problems for Sobolev spaces
[
22
]
,
[
23
]
.
This was a motivation for writing this paper.

In order to briefly describe the main results and ideas of the present paper we fix some notation and introduce basic concepts.
As usual, given
n\in\mathbb{N}
,
\mathbb{R}^{n}
denotes the linear space of all strings
x=(x_{1},...,x_{n})

of real numbers. It will be convenient to equip this space with the uniform norm, i.e.,
\mathbb{R}^{n}:=(\mathbb{R}^{n},\|\cdot\|_{\infty})
,
where
\|x\|_{\infty}:=\max\{|x_{1}|,...,|x_{n}|\}
. Furthermore, as usual, by
\mathcal{L}^{n}
we denote the classical
Lebesgue measure on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. In what follows, given a number
d\in[0,n]
and a set
E\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
, by
\mathcal{H}^{d}(E)
and
\mathcal{H}^{d}_{\infty}(E)

we will denote the
d
-Hausdorff measure and the
d
-Hausdorff content of

respectively (see the next section for the precise definitions).
Throughout the paper, the word "cube" will always mean
a closed cube in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
with sides
parallel to the coordinate axes.
Given

and

, we set
Q_{l}(x):=\prod_{i=1}^{n}[x_{i}-l,x_{i}+l]
.
In other words,

is the ball centered in

of radius

in the space
(\mathbb{R}^{n},\|\cdot\|_{\infty})
. Given a cube
Q
, we will denote by

its side length.
Given

and a cube

We recall a slightly modified modern definition of

that is
commonly used in the literature
[

]

First of all, given a nonempty set

and a parameter

we say that

is said to be

-porous if it is

(S,\tau)
-porous for some

.
The family of all

-porous

.
Finally, we say that a set

is

for all

and all

.

Recall that given

,
a closed set

(1.1)

In what follows, given

, by

we denote the class of all closed Ahlfors–David

-regular sets.

The starting point of our investigation is the following elementary but beautiful observation made by A. Jonsson

(see also Proposition 9.18 in

Note that this result was an important tool in

Let

and

(

1) Requirement

belongs to the class

(

(

(

Recent investigations related to trace problems for Sobolev-type spaces

Given a number

, a set

\mathcal{H}^{d}_{\infty}(Q_{l}(x)\cap S)\geq\lambda l^{d}\quad\text{for all}\quad x\in S\quad\text{and all}\quad l\in(0,1].

(1.2)

Since the parameter
\lambda
will play some role below, we introduce the following notation. Given

and
\lambda\in(0,1]
,
we denote by

the class of all sets

) holds. Furthermore,
we set

.

As far as we know,

-thick sets were firstly introduced by V. Rychkov in
[

]
.
Recently,

-thick sets were deeply studied in
[

]
,

The class

Indeed,

\mathcal{ADR}(d)\subset\mathcal{LCR}(d)\quad\text{for all}\quad d\in(0,n].

(1.3)

It is clear that

. If

,
inclusion (

) is strict. Indeed, it was noticed in

On the other hand,

that fail to satisfy the Ahlfors-David

and hence, fail to satisfy the

-regularity condition for any

In recent papers
[

,

,

]
it was discovered that

-thick sets
can be effectively used in the theory of traces of function spaces.

Problem A.

Suppose we are given parameters
d\in[0,n)
and
\lambda\in(0,1)
. Does there exist

\tau\in(0,1/2)
such that
\mathcal{LCR}(d,\lambda)\subset\mathcal{POR}(\tau)

Unfortunately, the answer to Problem A is negative. For example, in the case

we have

Indeed, the trivial estimate

holds does not give any useful information.

Theorem 1.1.

Given a pseudometric

\mathbb{R}^{n}

Our third main result
is an interesting interpretation of Theorem

Recall that in different topics concerning extensions of functions from a given
closed nonempty set
S\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
, the crucial role is played by the so-called
Whitney decomposition

of
\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus S

[
6

24
,
7
,

,

Recall the classical Whitney Covering Lemma
[
20
]
(see Section 6.1 therein):

There exists a countable
family
W_{S}
of closed dyadic cubes
such that:

(i)
\operatorname{int}Q\cap\operatorname{int}Q^{\prime}=\emptyset

for any

Q,Q^{\prime}\in W_{S}

such that

Q\neq Q^{\prime}
;

(ii)
\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus S=\cup\{Q:Q\in W_{S}\}
;

(iii)
l(Q)\leq\operatorname{dist}(Q,S)\leq 4l(Q)

for all

Q\in W_{S}
.

The family
W_{S}
is called
a Whitney decomposition of

\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus S
and
cubes
Q\in W_{S}
are called
Whitney cubes.

These cubes are closely and naturally related to the family of
S
-porous cubes.
Indeed, given a Whitney cube
Q=Q_{l_{\alpha}}(x_{\alpha})\in W_{S}
, one can find a cube
\widetilde{Q}:=Q_{l_{\alpha}}(\widetilde{x}_{\alpha})

whose center
\widetilde{x}_{\alpha}
is a metric projection of

to
S
such that
Q\subset 10\widetilde{Q}
.
This proves that the cube
10\widetilde{Q}
is
(S,\frac{1}{10})
-porous. Conversely, given an
(S,\tau)
-porous (for some
\tau\in(0,1)
)
cube
\widetilde{Q}
, one
can find a Whitney cube
Q
such that
Q\subset c\widetilde{Q}
for some universal constant
c\geq 1

and, furthermore,
l(Q)\approx l(\widetilde{Q})
.

For nonexpert readers we describe informally why the Whitney cubes and porous cubes are so useful in the trace
problems for the first-order
Sobolev
W_{p}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
-spaces with

. We fix a closed nonempty set

.
In the case when

and

is regular enough or in the case
when

and

is arbitrary, there are analytical tools capable of gathering
information on the behavior of a given function

from any cube
Q_{l}(x)

with

and

.
Hence, one can in some sense transfer the information from porous cubes
\widetilde{Q}
centered in

to the corresponding Whitney cubes

with comparable side lengths and
then glue it smoothly using the corresponding partition of unity related to

. This roughly and informally explains
the classical Whitney’s extension method.

Unfortunately, due to some deep analytical reasons

One cannot hope that these cubes

As a result, it is natural to consider an appropriate substitution for the role

Given a set

Given a family

Definition 2.1.

\Box

Proposition 2.2.

Let
S\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a set. Let
d\in(0,n]
,
\lambda\in(0,1]
. Then
there exists
\varepsilon_{0}>0
such that, for every
\varepsilon\in(0,\varepsilon_{0})

\overline{Q}=Q_{l}(x)

-optimal dyadic nonoverlapping

-almost covering

l(Q)\leq 2^{k(l)}\quad\hbox{for every}\quad Q\in\mathcal{Q},

The following elementary proposition will be quite useful in the sequel. It exhibits relations between
(d,\lambda)
-thick cubes
and
(d,\lambda)
-dyadically thick cubes respectively.

Proposition 2.3.

Let
d\in(0,n)
,
\lambda\in(0,1)
and let
S\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a Borel set with
\mathcal{H}^{d}_{\infty}(S)>0
. Let
Q=Q_{l}(x)
be a cube
with
l\in(0,1]
and let
k:=[-\log_{2}l]\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
. Then,

(i)
if
cQ\in\mathcal{F}_{S}(d,\lambda)
(
cQ\in\mathcal{DF}_{S}(d,\lambda)
) for some
c\geq 1
, then there
exists a dyadic cube
Q_{k,m}\in\mathcal{F}_{S}(d,\frac{\lambda}{([2c]+1)^{n}})

(
Q_{k,m}\in\mathcal{DF}_{S}(d,\frac{\lambda}{([2c]+1)^{n}})
) such that
Q_{k,m}\cap Q\neq\emptyset
;

(ii)
if
Q\notin\mathcal{F}_{S}(d,\frac{\lambda}{2^{d(j+1)}})

(
Q\in\mathcal{D}_{+}\setminus\mathcal{DF}_{S}(d,\frac{\lambda}{2^{d(j+1)}})
)
for some
j\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
, then every dyadic cube
Q_{k+j,m}\subset Q
does not belong
to
\mathcal{F}_{S}(d,\lambda)
(to
\mathcal{DF}_{S}(d,\lambda)
).

Proof.

It is clear that
l\in[2^{-k},2^{-k+1})
. Since
Q
is closed,
there are at most
([2c]+1)^{n}
dyadic cubes
Q_{k,m}
such that
Q_{k,m}\cap cQ\neq\emptyset
.

To prove the first claim we assume the contrary. Using the subadditivity property of
\mathcal{H}^{d}_{\infty}
we get

\mathcal{H}^{d}_{\infty}(cQ\cap S)\leq\sum\{\mathcal{H}^{d}_{\infty}(Q_{k,m}\cap S):Q_{k,m}\cap cQ\neq\emptyset\}<\frac{\lambda([2c]+1)^{n}}{2^{kd}([2c]+1)^{n}}\leq\lambda(l(Q))^{d}.

This contradicts the assumption that
cQ\in\mathcal{F}_{S}(d,\lambda)
.

To prove the second claim assume on the contrary that there is a dyadic cube
Q_{k+j,m}\subset Q
such that
Q_{k+j,m}\in\mathcal{F}_{S}(d,\lambda)
.
Due to monotonicity of
\mathcal{H}^{d}_{\infty}
we get by definition of the number
k

\mathcal{H}^{d}_{\infty}(Q\cap S)\geq\mathcal{H}^{d}_{\infty}(Q_{k+j,m}\cap S)\geq\frac{\lambda}{2^{(k+j)d}}\geq\frac{\lambda l^{d}}{2^{d(j+1)}}.

However, this contradicts the assumption that
Q\notin\mathcal{F}_{S}(d,\frac{\lambda}{2^{d(j+1)}})
.

The corresponding dyadic analogs of the claims can be proved similarly.
∎

3
Keystone families of cubes

The following data are
assumed to be fixed
during the whole section:

(\operatorname{\textbf{D1}})
arbitrary numbers
n\in\mathbb{N}
and
d\in(0,n]
;

(\operatorname{\textbf{D2}})
a set
S\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
with
\mathcal{H}^{d}_{\infty}(S)>0
.

Recall Definition
2
. Given
\lambda\in(0,1]
, in this section we set
\mathcal{DF}(\lambda):=\mathcal{DF}_{S}(d,\lambda)
for brevity.
In the sequel we will deal with special subfamilies of
\mathcal{DF}(\lambda)
.

Definition 3.1.

Given
\lambda\in(0,1]
, we say that a family
\mathcal{Q}
of cubes is
(d,\lambda)
-
nice for the set
S

if the following conditions hold:

(1) the family
\mathcal{Q}
is a dyadic non-overlapping
d
-almost covering of
S
;

(2)
\mathcal{Q}\subset\mathcal{DF}(\lambda)
.

The following result is a modification of Lemma 2.1 of Netrusov
[
12
]
adapted to our framework.
We present a full proof to make our paper self-contained. Furthermore, we hope that the proof will clarify the driving ideas of this section.

Lemma 3.1.

Let
\overline{Q}\in\mathcal{D}_{+}
be such that

0<\mathcal{DH}^{d}_{\infty}(\overline{Q}\cap S)<1.

(3.1)

Then, for each
\lambda\in(0,1)
, there exists a family of cubes
\widehat{\mathcal{Q}}(\lambda):=\widehat{\mathcal{Q}}(\overline{Q},\lambda)\subset\mathcal{D}
such that:

(1)

\widehat{\mathcal{Q}}(\lambda)
is
(d,\lambda)
-nice for
Q\cap S
;

(2)
for every cube
Q\in\widehat{\mathcal{Q}}(\lambda)
,

l(Q)\leq\frac{l(\overline{Q})}{2};

(3.2)

(3)
the Carleson-type packing condition,

\operatorname{H}^{d}(\widehat{\mathcal{Q}}(\lambda)|_{Q})\leq(l(Q))^{d}

(3.3)

holds for every dyadic cube
Q\subset\overline{Q}
.

Proof.

Given
\lambda\in(0,1)
, we fix
\varepsilon>0
so small that

0<\tau:=\frac{\varepsilon}{1-\lambda}<1.

(3.4)

We split the proof into several steps.

Step 1.
Recall Definition
2
. Given a dyadic cube
K\subset\overline{Q}
with

0<\mathcal{H}^{d}_{\infty}(K\cap S)<(l(K))^{d},

(3.5)

let
\mathcal{Q}(K)
be a
maximal
\varepsilon
-optimal dyadic non-overlapping
d
-almost
covering of the set
K\cap S
.
By (
3.5
) and Proposition
2
(decreasing
\varepsilon>0
if necessary) we have

l(Q)\leq\frac{l(K)}{2}\quad\hbox{for every}\quad Q\in\mathcal{Q}(K).

(3.6)

The key property of the family
\mathcal{Q}(K)
is that the Carleson-type packing condition holds true.
More precisely, by the construction and Definition
2
we have,
for every dyadic cube
Q\subset K
,

\operatorname{H}^{d}(\mathcal{Q}(K)|_{Q})\leq(l(Q))^{d}.

(3.7)

Indeed, otherwise if, for some dyadic cube
Q\subset K
, inequality (
3.7
) fails, then
we modify the family
\mathcal{Q}(K)
taking
Q
and excluding all cubes
Q^{\prime}\subset Q
,
Q^{\prime}\in\mathcal{Q}(K)
. This gives an
\varepsilon
-optimal
dyadic non-overlapping
d
-almost covering of
K\cap S
. But this contradicts the maximality of
\mathcal{Q}(K)
.

Step 2.
Given a dyadic cube
K\subset\overline{Q}
, we set

\mathcal{Q}^{1}(K):=\mathcal{Q}(K)\cap\mathcal{DF}(\lambda),\quad\widetilde{\mathcal{Q}}^{1}(K):=\mathcal{Q}(K)\setminus\mathcal{Q}^{1}(K).

Hence, using Definition
2
and the subadditivity of
\mathcal{DH}^{d}_{\infty}
we clearly have

\begin{split}&\frac{1}{1+\varepsilon}\operatorname{H}^{d}(\mathcal{Q}(K))=\frac{1}{1+\varepsilon}\operatorname{H}^{d}(\mathcal{Q}^{1}(K))+\frac{1}{1+\varepsilon}\operatorname{H}^{d}(\widetilde{\mathcal{Q}}^{1}(K))\leq\mathcal{DH}^{d}_{\infty}(K\cap S)\\
&\leq\sum\{\mathcal{DH}^{d}_{\infty}(Q\cap S):Q\in\mathcal{Q}^{1}(K)\}+\sum\{\mathcal{DH}^{d}_{\infty}(Q\cap S):Q\in\widetilde{\mathcal{Q}}^{1}(K)\}\\
&\leq\operatorname{H}^{d}(\mathcal{Q}^{1}(K))+\lambda\operatorname{H}^{d}(\widetilde{\mathcal{Q}}^{1}(K)).\end{split}

This clearly gives

\begin{split}&\varepsilon\operatorname{H}^{d}(\mathcal{Q}^{1}(K))\geq\Bigl(1-\lambda(1+\varepsilon)\Bigr)\operatorname{H}^{d}(\widetilde{\mathcal{Q}}^{1}(K)).\end{split}

(3.8)

Hence, using (
3.8
), Definition
2
and (
3.4
) we get

\begin{split}&\operatorname{H}^{d}(\widetilde{\mathcal{Q}}^{1}(K))\leq\frac{\varepsilon}{(1-\lambda)(1+\varepsilon)}\operatorname{H}^{d}(\mathcal{Q}(K))\leq\tau(l(K))^{d}.\end{split}

(3.9)

Step 3.

Suppose that we have already built,
for some
k_{0}\in\mathbb{N}
and for every
j\in\{1,...,k_{0}\}
, families of cubes

\mathcal{Q}^{j}=\mathcal{Q}^{j}(\overline{Q})
and
\widetilde{\mathcal{Q}}^{j}=\widetilde{\mathcal{Q}}^{j}(\overline{Q})
such that:

(i)
\mathcal{Q}^{1}\subset...\subset\mathcal{Q}^{k_{0}}
and
\widetilde{\mathcal{Q}}^{1}\succ...\succ\widetilde{\mathcal{Q}}^{k_{0}}
;

(ii)
\mathcal{Q}^{k_{0}}\subset\mathcal{DF}(\lambda)|_{\overline{Q}}
;

(iii)
\widetilde{\mathcal{Q}}^{k_{0}}\subset\mathcal{D}_{+}|_{\overline{Q}}\setminus\mathcal{DF}(\lambda)|_{\overline{Q}}
;

(iv) the following inequality

\operatorname{H}^{d}(\mathcal{Q}^{k_{0}}|_{Q}\cup\widetilde{\mathcal{Q}}^{k_{0}}|_{Q})\leq(l(Q))^{d}

(3.10)

holds for every dyadic cube
Q\subset\overline{Q}
;

(v) it holds

\operatorname{H}^{d}(\widetilde{\mathcal{Q}}^{k_{0}})\leq\tau^{k_{0}}(l(\overline{Q}))^{d}.

(3.11)

We recall notation and constructions of steps 1 and 2. We put

\begin{split}&\mathcal{Q}^{k_{0}+1}:=\cup\{\mathcal{Q}^{1}(Q):Q\in\widetilde{\mathcal{Q}}^{k_{0}}\}\cup\mathcal{Q}^{k_{0}}\quad\hbox{and}\\
&\widetilde{\mathcal{Q}}^{k_{0}+1}:=\cup\{\widetilde{\mathcal{Q}}^{1}(Q):Q\in\widetilde{\mathcal{Q}}^{k_{0}}\}.\end{split}

(3.12)

It is clear that conditions (i)–(iii) are satisfied with
k_{0}
replaced by
k_{0}+1
. It remains to verify that
(
3.10
) and (
3.11
) hold with
k_{0}+1
instead of
k_{0}
.
Indeed, an application of (
3.7
) with
K
replaced by
Q^{\prime}
gives, for any
Q\subset\overline{Q}
,

4
Main results

Recall that given a number
\delta>0
and a set
S\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}

the
\delta
-neighborhood of
S
is defined by the formula s

U_{\delta}(S):=\{y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:\|y-x\|_{\infty}<\delta\hbox{ for some }x\in S\}.

(4.1)

We start with the following elementary observation. We recall that the metric flor and the metric roof of a given family of sets were defined in (
2.3
).

Proposition 4.1.

Let
\overline{Q}
be an arbitrary cube in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Let
\mathcal{F}
be a family of subsets in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
such that
\overline{\mu}:=\overline{\mu}(\mathcal{F})<\frac{l(\overline{Q})}{2}
.
Then

\mathcal{L}^{n}(\cup\{F\in\mathcal{F}:F\cap\partial\overline{Q}\neq\emptyset\})\leq 2^{2n}(l(\overline{Q}))^{n-1}\overline{\mu}.

(4.2)

Proof.

Clearly, each
n
-dimensional cube has
2^{n}
facets. Given
i\in\{1,...,2^{n}\}
, we denote by
\partial^{i}\overline{Q}

the
i
th facet of the cube
\overline{Q}
. It is clear that, for any
\delta>0
, we have

U_{\delta}(\partial\overline{Q})\subset\bigcup_{i=1}^{2^{n}}U_{\delta}(\partial^{i}\overline{Q}).

(4.3)

Elementary geometrical observations give

\mathcal{L}^{n}(U_{\delta}(\partial^{i}\overline{Q}))\leq(2\delta+l(\overline{Q}))^{n-1}2\delta\quad\hbox{for each}\quad i\in\{1,...,2^{n}\}.

(4.4)

Combination of (
4.3
) and (
4.4
) allows to obtain

\mathcal{L}^{n}(U_{\delta}(\partial\overline{Q}))\leq\sum\limits_{i=1}^{2^{n}}\mathcal{L}^{n}(U_{\delta}(\partial^{i}\overline{Q}))\leq 2^{n+1}(2\delta+l(\overline{Q}))^{n-1}\delta\quad\hbox{for every}\quad\delta>0.

(4.5)

By (
2.3
) it is clear that if
F\cap\partial\overline{Q}\neq\emptyset
for some
F\in\mathcal{F}

then
F\subset U_{\overline{\mu}}(\partial Q)
. Hence,
using (
4.5
) with
\delta=\overline{\mu}
and taking into account that
\overline{\mu}\leq 2^{-1}l(\overline{Q})
we obtain the desirable estimate

\begin{split}&\mathcal{L}^{n}(\cup\{F\in\mathcal{F}:F\cap\partial Q\neq\emptyset\})\leq\mathcal{L}^{n}(U_{\overline{\mu}}(\partial\overline{Q}))\\
&\leq 2^{n+1}(2\overline{\mu}+l(\overline{Q}))^{n-1}\overline{\mu}\leq 2^{2n}(l(Q))^{n-1}\overline{\mu}.\end{split}

The proof is complete.
∎

Now we require the following auxiliary result, which can be interesting in itself.

Lemma 4.1.

Let

,

and

\overline{\delta}=\overline{\delta}(n,\overline{c},r)>0

so close to

(4.8)

,

of subsets of
\mathbb{R}^{n}

It is clear that for every set

,

By our construction it is clear that

\operatorname{H}^{d}(\mathcal{Q})=\operatorname{H}^{d}(\mathcal{F}),\quad\underline{\mu}(\mathcal{Q})=\underline{\mu},\quad\overline{\mu}(\mathcal{Q})=\overline{\mu}.

Since

,
Q\in\mathcal{Q}

\mathcal{Q}

\mathcal{Q}^{1}:=\{Q\in\mathcal{Q}:l(Q)>2^{-k^{\ast}}\tau\},\quad\mathcal{Q}^{2}:=\mathcal{Q}\setminus\mathcal{Q}^{1}.

(4.11)

Q\in\mathcal{Q}^{1}

On the other hand, since

for all

for all

.
Hence, inclusion (

[2r]+1,\quad\hbox{if}\quad Q\in\mathcal{Q}^{2}.\end{cases}

(4.12)

By (

4.11

\mathcal{L}^{n}(U_{r\delta\tau}(\operatorname{F}))\leq\theta^{n}\operatorname{H}^{n}(\mathcal{Q}^{1})+([2r]+1)^{n}\operatorname{H}^{n}(\mathcal{Q}^{2}).

(4.13)

Since

by (

) and (

\operatorname{H}^{n}(\mathcal{Q}^{2})\leq\Bigl(\frac{\tau}{2^{k^{\ast}}}\Bigr)^{n-d}\operatorname{H}^{d}(\mathcal{Q}^{2})\leq\Bigl(\frac{\tau}{2^{k^{\ast}}}\Bigr)^{n-d}\operatorname{H}^{d}(\mathcal{Q})=\Bigl(\frac{\tau}{2^{k^{\ast}}}\Bigr)^{n-d}\operatorname{H}^{d}(\mathcal{F}).

Similarly, we have the second key estimate

(4.15)

Combining (

Now we are ready to prove a relatively simple result, which however will be one of the keystones in our proof the main results of the present paper.
We believe that it can be interesting in itself.
Roughly speaking, we show that if a cube
\overline{Q}
is not
(d,\overline{\lambda})
-thick with respect to a given set
S
, then one can find
a cube
\underline{Q}\subset\overline{Q}
such that
\mathcal{H}^{d}(\underline{Q}\cap S)/(l(\underline{Q}))^{d}
is much smaller than
\overline{\lambda}
but the
side length
l(\underline{Q})
is controlled from below in a reasonable way.

Since the proof below will be quite technical, we give for the reader convenience some informal explanations of the driving ideas.
Roughly speaking, if
d\in(0,n)
and
a cube
\overline{Q}
is
(d,\overline{\lambda})
-thin,
then, for sufficiently large
k=k(d,\overline{\lambda})\in\mathbb{N}
, one can construct
a family
\mathcal{F}\subset\mathcal{D}_{k}|_{\overline{Q}}
of cardinality
\approx 2^{kn}
such that

\sum\{\mathcal{H}^{d}_{\infty}(Q\cap S):Q\in\mathcal{F}\}\leq C(n)\mathcal{H}^{d}_{\infty}(\overline{Q}\cap S)

for some universal constant
C(n)\geq 1
. Hence, if we assume that
\mathcal{H}^{d}_{\infty}(Q\cap S)
is not small for
every cube
Q\in\mathcal{F}
and if
k
is large enough, then taking into account that
d<n
, by elementary cardinality arguments we
get a contradiction with the smallness of
\mathcal{H}^{d}_{\infty}(\overline{Q}\cap S)
.
In order to construct
\mathcal{F}
one should fix a small enough
\varepsilon>0
and fix an
\varepsilon
-optimal covering

\mathcal{Q}
of
\overline{Q}\cap S
. The main technical difficulty is to split the family
\mathcal{Q}
into a ‘‘big part’’
\mathcal{Q}^{b}

and a ‘‘small part’’
\mathcal{Q}^{s}
respectively. Cubes
Q\in\mathcal{Q}^{b}
have relatively large side lengths but the Lebesgue measure
\mathcal{L}^{n}
of the union
of such cubes is relatively small. Hence, one should fix
k\in\mathbb{N}
such that
2^{-k}
is approximately equal to the minimal side length of cubes
from
\mathcal{Q}^{b}
and then select cubes from
\mathcal{D}_{k}|_{\overline{Q}}
that do not meet cubes
Q\in\mathcal{Q}^{b}
.

Theorem 4.1.

Let
d\in(0,n)
and
\overline{\lambda}\in(0,1)
. For each
c>1
, there exists a constant
\underline{\kappa}=\underline{\kappa}(\overline{\lambda},n,d,c)>0

such that, for each compact set
S\subset Q_{0,0}
, for any cube
\overline{Q}
satisfying

(4.16)

\underline{Q}\subset\overline{Q}

with the following properties:

(i)

\underline{Q}\in\mathcal{D}_{+}
and
\mathcal{H}^{d}(\underline{Q}\cap S)<(\frac{\overline{\lambda}}{c})(l(\underline{Q}))^{d}
;

(ii)

l(\underline{Q})\geq\underline{\kappa}l(\overline{Q})

Proof.

We fix an arbitrary compact set
S\subset Q_{0,0}
and a cube
\overline{Q}
satisfying (
4.16
). Without loss of generality we may assume that
\mathcal{H}^{d}_{\infty}(S)>0

\overline{\lambda}\in(0,1)

and
\overline{c}>1

(1+\varepsilon)\overline{\lambda}<1\quad\hbox{and}\quad 1-\overline{c}((1+\varepsilon)\overline{\lambda})^{\frac{n}{d}}>\frac{3}{4}(1-\overline{\lambda}^{\frac{n}{d}}).

(4.17)

We split the proof into several steps.

Step 1.
Since
\overline{Q}\notin\mathcal{F}_{S}(d,\overline{\lambda})
,
there is an
\varepsilon
-optimal

\mathcal{Q}:=\mathcal{Q}_{\varepsilon}
of the set
\overline{Q}\cap S

(4.18)

Hence, by (

4.18

\begin{split}\overline{\mu}:=\overline{\mu}(\mathcal{Q})\leq\Bigl(\operatorname{H}^{d}(\mathcal{Q})\Bigr)^{\frac{1}{d}}\leq(\overline{\lambda}(1+\varepsilon))^{\frac{1}{d}}l(\overline{Q})=:\tau<1.\end{split}

(4.19)

Step 2.

Let
\overline{\delta}=\overline{\delta}(n,\overline{c},3)>0

4
.
We set

\underline{\kappa}:=\min\Bigl\{\frac{\overline{\delta}}{3},\frac{\overline{\lambda}^{\frac{n}{d}}}{2^{2n+3}},\Bigl(\frac{(1-\overline{\lambda}^{\frac{n}{d}})}{c7^{n+1}}\Bigr)^{\frac{1}{n-d}}\Bigr\}.

(4.20)

We split the family
\mathcal{Q}
into two disjoint subfamilies. Namely, we define a ‘‘subfamily of big cubes’’ and a ‘‘subfamily of small cubes’’ of
\mathcal{Q}
respectively by letting

\mathcal{Q}^{b}_{\underline{\kappa}}:=\{Q\in\mathcal{Q}:l(Q)\geq\underline{\kappa}\tau\},\quad\mathcal{Q}^{s}_{\underline{\kappa}}:=\mathcal{Q}\setminus\mathcal{Q}^{b}_{\underline{\kappa}}.

(4.21)

We define

The main idea is to show that (
4.20
) guaranties that if

k:=[-\log_{2}(\underline{\kappa}\tau)],

(4.22)

then there are a lot of cubes from
\mathcal{D}_{k}
inside
\overline{Q}
that do not meet
F
and
\partial Q
. We set

\begin{split}&\mathcal{F}^{1}_{k}:=\{Q\in\mathcal{D}_{k}:Q\cap F\neq\emptyset\},\quad\mathcal{F}^{2}_{k}:=\{Q\in\mathcal{D}_{k}:Q\cap\partial\overline{Q}\neq\emptyset\},\\
&\mathcal{F}^{3}_{k}:=\{Q\in\mathcal{D}_{k}:Q\subset\overline{Q}\hbox{ and }Q\notin(\mathcal{F}^{1}_{k}\cup\mathcal{F}^{2}_{k})\}.\end{split}

Step 3.

By (

2^{-k}\leq 2\underline{\kappa}\tau

\begin{split}&V^{1}_{k}:=\mathcal{L}^{n}(\cup\{Q:Q\in\mathcal{F}^{1}_{k}\})\leq\mathcal{L}^{n}(U_{3\underline{\kappa}\tau}(F))\\

(4.23)

On the other hand, using Proposition

\begin{split}&V^{2}_{k}:=\mathcal{L}^{n}\Bigl(\cup\{Q:Q\in\mathcal{F}_{k}^{2}\}\Bigr)\leq 2^{2n+1}(l(\overline{Q}))^{n-1}\underline{\kappa}\tau\leq 2^{2n+1}\underline{\kappa}(l(\overline{Q}))^{n}.\end{split}

(4.24)

Using (

Hence, by definition of the Hausdorff content

If we assume that

\begin{split}&(1+\varepsilon)\overline{\lambda}(l(\overline{Q}))^{d}\geq\operatorname{H}^{d}(\mathcal{Q}^{s}_{\underline{k}})\geq\frac{1}{3^{n}}\frac{\overline{\lambda}}{c}2^{-kd}\#\mathcal{F}^{3}_{k}\\
&>\frac{\overline{\lambda}}{2c}2^{k(n-d)}\frac{(1-\overline{\lambda}^{\frac{n}{d}})}{3^{n}}(l(\overline{Q}))^{n}\geq\frac{\overline{\lambda}(1-\overline{\lambda}^{\frac{n}{d}})}{2c3^{n}}\Bigl(\frac{l(\overline{Q})}{2\underline{\kappa}\tau}\Bigr)^{n-d}(l(\overline{Q}))^{d}.\end{split}

(4.29)

Using (
4.29

Hence, using the first inequality in (

\underline{\kappa}^{n-d}>\frac{(1-\overline{\lambda}^{\frac{n}{d}})}{c7^{n+1}}.

This inequality is in contradiction with (

The following concept, which was already mentioned in the introduction, gives a natural generalisation of the concept of porous cubes.

Definition 4.1.
Given a set
S\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
, a cube
Q
, and a parameter
\gamma\in(0,1]
, we say that a set

U\subset Q
is an
(S,\gamma)
-cavity of the cube
Q
if

U\subset Q\setminus S\quad\hbox{and}\quad\mathcal{L}^{n}(U\setminus S)\geq\gamma(l(Q))^{n}.

We say that
Q
is
(S,\gamma)
-hollow if there exists an
(S,\gamma)
-cavity
U
of the cube
Q
.

We need some notation. Given numbers
d\in(0,n)
,
\lambda\in(0,1)
,

and a set

with

for each
\varkappa\in(0,1]

\begin{split}&U_{\varkappa}(Q,r):=U_{\varkappa}(Q,d,\lambda,r):=Q\setminus\Bigl(\cup\{rQ^{\prime}:Q^{\prime}\in\mathcal{DF}_{S}(d,\lambda),l(Q^{\prime})\leq\varkappa l(Q)\}\Bigr).\\
\end{split}

Let

,

and

-cavities of the cube

for all

(d,\lambda)
-nice for

with
l\in(0,1]
is such that
\overline{Q}\notin\mathcal{F}_{S}(d,\overline{\lambda})
, then the cube
\overline{Q}
is
(S,1-\overline{\lambda}^{\frac{n}{d}})
-hollow.

Indeed, by Definition
2
there is an at most countable covering
\mathcal{U}

of the set
\overline{Q}\cap S
such that

\operatorname{H}^{d}(\mathcal{U})<\overline{\lambda}l^{d}.

For each set
U\in\mathcal{U}
there is a cube
Q(U)\supset U
with
l(Q)=\operatorname{diam}U
.
It is clear that

l(Q(U))<\overline{\lambda}^{\frac{1}{d}}l\quad\hbox{for all}\quad U\in\mathcal{U}.

This gives

\operatorname{H}^{n}(\mathcal{U})<\overline{\lambda}^{\frac{n}{d}-1}l^{n-d}\operatorname{H}^{d}(\mathcal{U})\leq\overline{\lambda}^{\frac{n}{d}}l^{n}.

(4.48)

Since
\overline{\lambda}<1
, the required result follows from (
4.48
) and the subadditivity property of the Lebesgue measure
\mathcal{L}^{n}
.

It is clear that there is a huge difference between the elementary observation given above and Theorem
1
. The former observation
does not give any information about the structure of cavities in cubes whose intersection with
S
have relatively small
d
-Hausdorff content.
On the other hand, informally speaking Theorem
1
claims that the corresponding cavities in cubes are located at some ‘‘nonzero depth’’ in
\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus S

with respect to the special distance.

\Box

Now we show that Theorem
1
, which was called the first main result of the present paper admits a significant clarification in the context of
d
-thick sets.

Proof of Theroem
1
.
The case
d=0
is trivial. Hence, we assume that
d\in(0,n)
. Since
S
is
(d,\lambda)
-thick we have

S_{\varepsilon}(d,\lambda)\supset U_{\varepsilon}(S)\quad\hbox{for all}\quad\varepsilon\in(0,1].

By Theorem
1
this implies that

\overline{Q}\setminus U_{\delta l}(S)\neq\emptyset\quad\hbox{for all}\quad\delta\in(0,\overline{\delta}].

Hence, letting
\underline{\tau}:=\underline{\tau}(n,d,\overline{\lambda},\lambda)=\frac{1}{2}\overline{\delta}(n,d,\overline{\lambda},\lambda)
, and taking an arbitrary point
x^{\ast}\in\overline{Q}\setminus U_{\delta l}(S)
we obtain

Q_{\underline{\tau}}(x^{\ast})\subset\overline{Q}\setminus S.

The proof is complete.

\Box

5
Applications

In this section we present the proof of Theorem
1
. Furthermore, we introduce some new concepts, which can be of independent
interest.

The following data are assumed to be fixed during the whole section:

(
A
) arbitrary numbers
n\in\mathbb{N}
and
d\in[0,n)
;

(
B
) a compact set
S\subset Q_{0,0}
with
\lambda_{S}:=\mathcal{H}^{d}_{\infty}(S)>0
.

Recall (
2.9
) and Definition
2
. Given
\lambda\in(0,1]
we write
\mathcal{F}(\lambda)
and
\mathcal{DF}(\lambda)

instead of
\mathcal{F}_{S}(d,\lambda)
and
\mathcal{DF}_{S}(d,\lambda)
respectively.
Furthermore, given
\lambda\in(0,1]
for each
x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
we define the family

\mathcal{Q}_{x,y}(\lambda):=\{Q\ni x,y:Q\in\mathcal{F}(\lambda)\}.

Now, for each
\lambda\in(0,1]
and any
x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, we set

\widetilde{\rho}_{S,d,\lambda}(x,y):=\widetilde{\rho}_{\lambda}(x,y):=\begin{cases}\inf\{l(Q):Q\in\mathcal{Q}_{x,y}(\lambda)\},\quad x\neq y\hbox{ and }\mathcal{Q}_{x,y}(\lambda)\neq\emptyset;\\
+\infty,\quad\mathcal{Q}_{x,y}(\lambda)=\emptyset;\\
0,\quad x=y.\end{cases}

(5.1)

For any two points
x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
such that at least one of them belongs to the set
S
we put

\rho_{\lambda}(x,y):=\rho_{S,d,\lambda}(x,y):=\inf\sum\limits_{i=0}^{N-1}\widetilde{\rho}_{\lambda}(x^{i},x^{i+1}),

(5.2)

where the infimum is taken over all finite sets
\{x^{i}\}_{i=0}^{N}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
such that

x^{0}=x
and
x^{N}=y
. Finally, in the case when
x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus S
we define

\rho_{\lambda}(x,y):=\rho_{S,d,\lambda}(x,y):=\begin{cases}+\infty\quad\hbox{if}\quad\max\{\rho_{\lambda}(x,\xi),\rho_{\lambda}(y,\xi)\}=+\infty\quad\hbox{for some}\quad\xi\in S,\\
\max\{\|x-y\|_{\infty},\sup\limits_{\xi\in S}|\rho_{\lambda}(x,\xi)-\rho_{\lambda}(y,\xi)|\}\quad\hbox{otherwise}.\end{cases}

(5.3)

Recall that a pseudometric on
\mathbb{R}^{n}

is a symmetric nonnegative function
\rho:\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}\to[0,+\infty]

satisfying the triangle inequality. Given a pseudometric
\rho
on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
we will use the symbol
\mathbb{R}^{n}_{\rho}
to denote the pseudometric space
(\mathbb{R}^{n},\rho)
. By
B^{n,\rho}_{r}(x)

we will denote the closed ball centered in
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
with radius
r
(in pseudometric
\rho
), i.e.,

B^{n,\rho}_{r}(x):=\{y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:\rho(x,y)\leq r\}
.

Proposition 5.1.

For each
\lambda\in(0,1]
the function
\rho_{\lambda}:\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}\to[0,+\infty)
is a pseudometric on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
.

Proof.

The symmetry is obvious by (
5.2
) and (
5.3
). Furthermore, note that

\rho_{\lambda}(x,y)\geq\|x-y\|_{\infty}\quad\hbox{for any}\quad x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}.

Hence,
\rho_{\lambda}(x,y)=0
implies
x=y
.

It remains to verify the triangle inequality.
We fix an arbitrary triple of points
x,y,z\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
.

In the case
when
\rho_{\lambda}(x,y)=+\infty
or
\rho_{\lambda}(y,z)=+\infty
the triangle inequality is obvious.
Consider
the case
when
\rho_{\lambda}(x,y)<+\infty
and
\rho_{\lambda}(y,z)<+\infty
. We should consider
two subcases.

In
the first subcase
at least one of 3 points
x,y,z
(say
y
) belongs to the set
S
.
Given
\delta>0
, let
\{x^{i}\}_{i=0}^{N},\{x^{i}\}_{i=N+1}^{L}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be finite sets of points
such that
x^{0}=x
,
x^{N}=y
,
x^{L}=z
and

\sum\limits_{i=0}^{N-1}\widetilde{\rho}_{\lambda}(x^{i},x^{i+1})\leq\rho_{\lambda}(x,y)+\frac{\delta}{2},\quad\sum\limits_{i=N}^{L-1}\widetilde{\rho}_{\lambda}(x^{i},x^{i+1})\leq\rho_{\lambda}(y,z)+\frac{\delta}{2}.

Summing both inequalities and using (
5.2
) we obtain

\rho_{\lambda}(x,z)\leq\sum\limits_{i=0}^{L-1}\widetilde{\rho}_{\lambda}(x^{i},x^{i+1})\leq\rho_{\lambda}(x,y)+\rho_{\lambda}(y,z)+\delta.

Since
\delta>0
was chosen arbitrarily we deduce the triangle inequality for this subcase

\rho_{\lambda}(x,z)\leq\rho_{\lambda}(x,y)+\rho_{\lambda}(y,z).

(5.4)

Finally, consider
the second subcase
when
x,y,z\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus S
.
If
\|x-z\|\geq\sup_{\xi\in S}|\rho_{\lambda}(x,\xi)-\rho_{\lambda}(z,\xi)|
, by (
5.3
)
we get

\rho_{\lambda}(x,z)=\|x-z\|\leq\|x-y\|+\|y-z\|\leq\rho_{\lambda}(x,y)+\rho_{\lambda}(y,z).

(5.5)

If
\|x-z\|<\sup_{\xi\in S}|\rho_{\lambda,\varepsilon}(x,\xi)-\rho_{\lambda,\varepsilon}(z,\xi)|
by (
5.3
), we have,
for any
\xi\in S
,

\begin{split}&|\rho_{\lambda}(x,\xi)-\rho_{\lambda}(z,\xi)|\leq|\rho_{\lambda}(x,\xi)-\rho_{\lambda}(y,\xi)|+|\rho_{\lambda}(y,\xi)-\rho_{\lambda}(z,\xi)|\leq\rho_{\lambda}(x,y)+\rho_{\lambda}(y,z).\end{split}

(5.6)

Taking the supremum in (
5.6
) over all
\xi\in S
we obtain

\rho_{\lambda}(x,z)\leq\rho_{\lambda}(x,y)+\rho_{\lambda}(y,z).

(5.7)

Combining (
5.5
) with (
5.7
) we get the triangle inequality for the subcase when
x,y,z\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus S

As a result, we have proved that the triangle inequality holds for any triple of points
x,y,z\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
.

This completes the proof.

∎

Remark 5.1.

Note that if
\lambda\in(0,\lambda_{S}]
, then
Q_{0,0}\in\mathcal{DF}(\lambda)
(by assumption (
B
) and Remark
2
). Hence,
by (
5.2
), (
5.3
) it is easy to see that
\rho_{\lambda}(x,y)<+\infty
for all
x,y\in Q_{0,0}
.

\Box

Given a nonempty set
E\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus S
we define the
(d,\lambda)
-thick distance

between the sets
E\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus S
and
S
by the formula

\operatorname{D}_{\lambda}(E,S):=\operatorname{D}_{S,d,\lambda}(E,S):=\inf\{\rho_{\lambda}(x,\xi):x\in E,\xi\in S\}.

(5.8)

The following proposition gives a more simple way for computing the
(d,\lambda)
-thick distance
from a given point
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus S
to the set
S
.

Proposition 5.2.

Let
\lambda\in(0,\lambda_{S}]
. Then the following equality

\operatorname{D}_{\lambda}(x,S)=\inf\{l(Q):Q\ni x\hbox{ and $Q\in\mathcal{F}(\lambda)$}\}

(5.9)

holds for any
x\in(\frac{\lambda_{S}}{\lambda})^{\frac{1}{d}}Q_{0,0}\setminus S
.

Proof.

By Remark
2
and assumption (
B
) we have
(\frac{\lambda_{S}}{\lambda})^{\frac{1}{d}}Q_{0,0}\in\mathcal{F}(\lambda)
. Hence,

\mathcal{Q}_{x,y}(\lambda)\neq\emptyset
for each

x\in(\frac{\lambda_{S}}{\lambda})^{\frac{1}{d}}Q_{0,0}\setminus S
and
y\in S
.
As a result,
0<\rho_{\lambda}(x,y)<+\infty
for such
x
and
y
.

Now we fix an arbitrary
x\in(\frac{\lambda_{S}}{\lambda})^{\frac{1}{d}}Q_{0,0}\setminus S
. We denote the right-hand side
of (
5.9
) by
\widetilde{\operatorname{D}}_{\lambda}(x,S)
. Our aim is to show that
\operatorname{D}_{\lambda}(x,S)=\widetilde{\operatorname{D}}_{\lambda}(x,S)
.
Given
\varepsilon>0
, using (
5.2
), (
5.8
), we choose
x_{\varepsilon}\in S
and points
\{x_{i}\}_{i=0}^{N_{\varepsilon}}
in such a way that

x_{0}=x
,
x_{N_{\varepsilon}}=x_{\varepsilon}
and

\sum\limits_{i=0}^{N_{\varepsilon}-1}\widetilde{\rho}_{\lambda}(x_{i},x_{i+1})<\operatorname{D}_{\lambda}(x,S)+\varepsilon.

Hence, we clearly get

\operatorname{D}_{\lambda}(x,S)\leq\widetilde{\operatorname{D}}_{\lambda}(x,S)\leq\widetilde{\rho}_{\lambda}(x_{0},x_{1})<\operatorname{D}_{\lambda}(x,S)+\varepsilon.

Since
\varepsilon>0
can be chosen arbitrary small, we get the required equality and complete the proof.
∎

6
Examples

In the concluding section of the present paper we present elementary examples demonstrating the sharpness of
the main results.

The following example shows
that the
d
-thick condition in Theorem
1
cannot be dropped.

Example 6.1.

Let
K\subset[0,1]
be the standard middle-third Cantor set. For each
j\in\mathbb{N}
we define
K_{j}:=\{3^{-j}x:x\in K\}
. We set

S_{j}:=\bigcup_{i=0}^{2^{j}-1}\Bigl(\frac{i}{2^{j}}+K_{2j}\Bigr).

Obviously, given
j\in\mathbb{N}
, the maximal size of closed
1
-dimensional cubes

Q\subset[0,1]\setminus S_{j}
is less than
2^{-j}
.
If
d=\operatorname{ln}2/\operatorname{ln}3
we clearly have

\mathcal{H}^{d}(S_{j})\leq\frac{2^{j}}{2^{2j}}\to 0,\quad j\to\infty.

(6.1)

Finally, we define the set

S:=\bigcup_{j=0}^{\infty}\Bigl((1-\frac{1}{2^{j}})+\frac{1}{2^{j+1}}S_{j+1}\Bigr).

It follows from (
6.1
) that the set
S
is not
(d,\lambda)
-thick for any
\lambda\in(0,1)
.
On the other hand, any dyadic interval
[1-\frac{2}{2^{j}},1-\frac{1}{2^{j}}]
,
j\in\mathbb{N}
, can be
(S,\tau)
-porous only with
\tau<2^{-s}
.

\Box

Now we show that the restriction
d<n
in Theorem
1
can not be dropped.

Example 6.2.

For each
j\in\mathbb{N}
we set

S_{j}:=\bigcup_{i=0}^{2^{j}-1}[\frac{i}{2^{j}},\frac{i}{2^{j}}+\frac{1}{10}\frac{1}{2^{j}}].

(6.2)

Finally, we define the set

S:=\bigcup_{j=0}^{\infty}\Bigl((1-\frac{1}{2^{j}})+\frac{1}{2^{j+1}}S_{j+1}\Bigr).

It is easy to see that

\mathcal{H}^{1}_{\infty}([1-\frac{2}{2^{j}},1-\frac{1}{2^{j}}]\cap S)<\frac{1}{8}\frac{1}{2^{j}}\quad\hbox{for all}\quad j\in\mathbb{N}.

(6.3)

On the other hand, for any
j\in\mathbb{N}
,

\Bigl[1-\frac{2}{2^{j}},1-\frac{1}{2^{j}}\Bigr]\setminus\cup\{Q^{\prime}:l(Q^{\prime})\leq 2^{-2j}\hbox{ and $Q^{\prime}\in\mathcal{F}_{S}(1,\frac{1}{10})$}\}=\emptyset.

(6.4)

This shows that the conclusion of Theorem
1
cannot hold in the case
d=n=1
and
\lambda=\frac{1}{10}
.

\Box

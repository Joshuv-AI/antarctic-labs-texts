Abstract
. We show that the additive Borcherds lift of vector-valued non-holomorphic Eisenstein series are orthogonal non-holomorphic Eisenstein series for O ⁡ ( 2 , l ) O(2,l) . Using this we give another proof that they have a meromorphic continuation, calculate their Fourier expansion and show that they have a functional equation analogous to the classical case. Moreover, we will investigate the image of Borcherds lift and give a sufficient condition for surjectivity.



1.
Introduction

Let
L
be an even lattice of signature
(2,l)
with
l\geq 4
even. For an easier exposition we assume that
L
is maximal but the results also hold for general lattices
L
(in fact we will work with an arbitrary even lattice of signature
(b^{+},b^{-})
and then specialize to signature
(2,l)
). Let
\rho_{L}
be the Weil representation of the metaplectic group
\operatorname{Mp}_{2}(\mathbb{Z})
on the group algebra
\mathbb{C}[L^{\prime}/L]
, see
[
Bor98
]
,
[
Bru02
]
. Similarly as in
[
BK03
]
,
[
BK01
]
for
\operatorname{Re}(s)\gg 0
and
\tau=u+iv\in\mathbb{H}
we define the vector-valued non-holomorphic Eisenstein series of weight
k\in\mathbb{Z}
by

E_{k,0}(\tau,s):=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{M\in\tilde{\Gamma}_{\infty}\backslash\operatorname{Mp}_{2}(\mathbb{Z})}v^{s}\mathfrak{e}_{0}|_{k,L}M,

where
|_{k,L}
is the Petersson slash operator for the Weil representation
\rho_{L}
(in
[
BK03
]
,
[
BK01
]
the dual Weil representation
\rho_{L}^{*}
is used) and
\tilde{\Gamma}_{\infty}\subseteq\operatorname{Mp}_{2}(\mathbb{Z})
is an index
2
subgroup of the stabilizer of
\infty
. According to
[
BK03
]
the Eisenstein series has a Fourier expansion of the form

2\mathfrak{e}_{0}v^{s}+c_{k,0}(0,0,s)v^{1-s-k}+\sum_{\gamma\in L^{\prime}/L}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\in\mathbb{Z}+q(\gamma)\\
n\neq 0\end{subarray}}c_{k,0}(\gamma,n,s)\mathcal{W}_{s}(4\pi nv)\mathfrak{e}_{\gamma}(nu),

where
\mathcal{W}_{s}
is a Whittaker function and
\mathfrak{e}_{\gamma}(nu):=\mathfrak{e}_{\gamma}e^{2\pi iu}
. The Fourier expansion yields a meromorphic continuation to all
s\in\mathbb{C}
. Moreover, since
L
is maximal one has the functional equation

E_{k,0}(\tau,s)=\frac{1}{2}c_{k,0}(0,0,s)E_{k,0}(\tau,1-k-s).

In general, this functional equation involves the vector-valued non-holomorphic Eisenstein series corresponding to
\beta\in L^{\prime}/L
with
q(\beta)=0
. For a primitive isotropic
z\in L
take
z^{\prime}\in L^{\prime}
with
(z,z^{\prime})=1
. Define
K=L\cap z^{\perp}\cap z^{\prime\perp}
and let
\mathbb{H}_{l}=K\otimes\mathbb{R}+iC
, where
C
is a fixed connected component of

\{Y\in K\otimes\mathbb{R}\mid q(Y)>0\}.

Then
\mathbb{H}_{l}
is a hermitian symmetric domain and there is an index
2
subgroup
O^{+}(L\otimes\mathbb{R})\subseteq O(L\otimes\mathbb{R})
acting on
\mathbb{H}_{l}
. Write
L^{\prime}
for the dual lattice of
L
and denote by
\Gamma(L)
the kernel of the natural map
O^{+}(L)\to O(L^{\prime}/L)
. For
\operatorname{Re}(s)\gg 0
and
Z=X+iY\in\mathbb{H}_{l}
one can define the orthogonal non-holomorphic Eisenstein series of weight
\kappa=\frac{l}{2}-1+k
corresponding to the
0
-dimensional cusp
z
by

\mathcal{E}_{\kappa,z}(Z,s):=\sum_{\sigma\in\Gamma(L)_{z}\backslash\Gamma(L)}q(Y)^{s}|_{\kappa}\sigma,

where
|_{\kappa}
is the Petersson slash operator for
O^{+}(V)
and
\Gamma(L)_{z}
is the stabilizer of
z
. As in the vector-valued case we want to obtain a meromorphic continuation to all
s\in\mathbb{C}
and a functional equation. We will do this by writing the orthogonal non-holomorphic Eisenstein series as a Borcherds lift of vector-valued non-holomorphic Eisenstein series. Therefore, using the theta function

\Theta_{L}(\tau,Z)=\frac{v^{\frac{l}{2}}}{2(-2i)^{\kappa}}\sum_{\lambda\in L^{\prime}}\frac{(\lambda,Z_{L})^{\kappa}}{q(Y)^{\kappa}}\mathfrak{e}_{\lambda}(\tau q(\lambda_{Z_{L}})+\overline{\tau}q(\lambda_{Z_{L}^{\perp}}))

we define the Borcherds lift as a regularized integral

\Phi_{k,0}(Z,s):=\int_{\operatorname{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z})\backslash\mathbb{H}}^{\text{reg}}\langle E_{k,0}(\tau,s),\Theta_{L}(\tau,Z)\rangle v^{k}\mathrm{d}\mu,

see
[
Bor98
]
,
[
Bru02
]
. We will first show that this definition makes sense for
\operatorname{Re}(s)\gg 0
and that it has a meromorphic continuation to all of
s\in\mathbb{C}
. In particular, we obtain a functional equation for
\Phi_{k,0}
coming from the functional equation of the vector-valued non-holomorphic Eisenstein series
E_{k,0}
. By unfolding against the vector-valued non-holomorphic Eisenstein series one obtains

Theorem 1.1

(see Theorem
8.1
)
.

We have

\Phi_{k,0}(Z,s)=2\frac{\Gamma(s+\kappa)\zeta(2s+\kappa)}{(-2\pi i)^{\kappa}\pi^{s}}\mathcal{E}_{\kappa,z}(Z,s).

This shows, in particular, that the orthogonal non-holomorphic Eisenstein series have a meromorphic continuation to all
s\in\mathbb{C}
. Using the functional equation of vector-valued non-holomorphic Eisenstein series we obtain the functional equation for orthogonal non-holomorphic Eisenstein series

Corollary 1.2

(see Corollary
8.3
)
.

The non-holomorphic Eisenstein series
\mathcal{E}_{\kappa,z}(Z,s)
has a functional equation relating the values at
s
and
1-k-s
. More precisely, if
L
is maximal and if we write

\tilde{\varphi}_{k,0}(z,s)=\frac{\pi^{s}\Gamma(1-k-s+\kappa)\zeta(2(1-k-s)+\kappa)}{\pi^{1-k-s}\Gamma(s+\kappa)\zeta(2s+\kappa)}c_{k,0}(0,0,s),

then we have

\mathcal{E}_{\kappa,z}(Z,s)=\frac{1}{2}\tilde{\varphi}_{k,0}(z,s)\mathcal{E}_{\kappa,z}(Z,1-k-s).

By unfolding against the theta function one can calculate the Fourier expansion using the method of
[
Bor98
]
.

Theorem 1.3

(see Theorem
8.6
)
.

The non-holomorphic Eisenstein series has a Fourier expansion of the form

\mathcal{E}_{\kappa,z}=\Phi^{K}_{0,\beta}(Y/\lvert Y\rvert,s)+\sum_{\lambda\in K^{\prime}}b_{\kappa,z}(\lambda,Y,s)e(\lambda,X),

where
\Phi^{K}_{0,\beta}
is a theta lift of some Eisenstein series corresponding to the sublattice
K
. For
\lambda=0
we have

b_{\kappa,z}(0,Y,s)=2q(Y)^{s}+\tilde{\varphi}_{k,0}(z,s)q(Y)^{1-s-k}.

For the other coefficients see Theorem
8.6
.

If
L
is not maximal, we have

Theorem 1.4

(see Theorem
8.2
)
.

Assume that
L
splits two hyperbolic planes. Then every orthogonal non-holomorphic Eisenstein series corresponding to a
0
-dimensional cusp for
\Gamma(L)
is a theta lift of some vector-valued non-holomorphic Eisenstein series for the Weil representation
\rho_{L}
. In particular, they have a meromorphic continuation to all
s\in\mathbb{C}
and have a functional equation similar to the vector-valued case.

More generally, Theorem
8.2
determines the image of the additive Borcherds lift for arbitrary lattices
L
.

2.
Vector-Valued Non-Holomorphic Eisenstein Series

We will now introduce the Weil representation and vector-valued modular forms. Therefore, let
\mathbb{H}:=\{\tau=u+iv\in\mathbb{C}\mid v>0\}
be the usual upper half-plane. For
z\in\mathbb{C}
we write
e(z):=e^{2\pi iz}
and we denote by
\sqrt{z}=z^{\frac{1}{2}}
the principal branch of the square-root, i.e.
\arg(\sqrt{z})\in(-\frac{\pi}{2},\frac{\pi}{2}]
.

Definition 2.1
.

We denote by
\operatorname{Mp}_{2}(\mathbb{R})
the
metaplectic cover
of
\operatorname{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{R})
. It is realized as pairs
(M,\phi)
, where
M\in\operatorname{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{R})
and
\phi:\mathbb{H}\to\mathbb{C}
is a holomorphic square root of
\tau\mapsto c\tau+d
. The product for
(M_{1},\phi_{1}),(M_{2},\phi_{2})\in\operatorname{Mp}_{2}(\mathbb{R})
is given by

(M_{1},\phi_{1}(\tau))(M_{2},\phi_{2}(\tau)):=(M_{1}M_{2},\phi_{1}(M_{2}\tau)\phi_{2}(\tau)),

where
\left(\begin{smallmatrix}a&b\\
c&d\end{smallmatrix}\right)\tau=\frac{a\tau+d}{c\tau+d}
is the usual action of
\operatorname{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{R})
.

By
\operatorname{Mp}_{2}(\mathbb{Z})
we denote the inverse image of
\operatorname{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z})
under the covering map. It is generated by

T=\left(\begin{pmatrix}1&1\\
0&1\end{pmatrix},1\right)\quad\text{and}\quad S=\left(\begin{pmatrix}0&-1\\
1&0\end{pmatrix},\sqrt{\tau}\right).

We have the relation
S^{2}=(ST)^{3}=Z
, where
Z=\left(\left(\begin{smallmatrix}-1&0\\
0&-1\end{smallmatrix}\right),i\right)
is the standard generator of the center of
\operatorname{Mp}_{2}(\mathbb{Z})
. Furthermore, we will write
\Gamma_{\infty}:=\{\left(\begin{smallmatrix}1&n\\
0&1\end{smallmatrix}\right)\mid n\in\mathbb{Z}\}
and
\tilde{\Gamma}_{\infty}:=\{\left(\left(\begin{smallmatrix}1&n\\
0&1\end{smallmatrix}\right),1\right)\mid n\in\mathbb{Z}\}
. For an even non-degenerate lattice
L
of signature
(b^{+},b^{-})
consider the group ring
\mathbb{C}[L^{\prime}/L]
with standard basis
(\mathfrak{e}_{\gamma})_{\gamma\in L^{\prime}/L}
. For
\mathfrak{v}=\sum_{\gamma\in L^{\prime}/L}\mathfrak{v}_{\gamma}\mathfrak{e}_{\gamma}\in\mathbb{C}[L^{\prime}/L]
we write
\mathfrak{v}^{*}:=\sum_{\gamma\in L^{\prime}/L}\mathfrak{v}_{\gamma}\mathfrak{e}_{-\gamma}
. Moreover, we write
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
for the standard inner product on
\mathbb{C}[L^{\prime}/L]
which is anti-linear in the second variable. Write
\operatorname{Iso}(L^{\prime}/L)\subseteq L^{\prime}/L
for the set of isotropic elements and denote the subspace of vectors that are supported on isotropic elements by
\operatorname{Iso}(\mathbb{C}[L^{\prime}/L])
. Moreover, we introduce the notation
\mathfrak{e}_{\gamma}(\tau):=e(\tau)\mathfrak{e}_{\gamma}=e^{2\pi i\tau}\mathfrak{e}_{\gamma}
.

Definition 2.2

(
[
Bor98
]
)
.

The
Weil representation
is the unitary representation
\rho_{L}
of
\operatorname{Mp}_{2}(\mathbb{Z})
on
\mathbb{C}[L^{\prime}/L]
defined by

\rho_{L}(T)\mathfrak{e}_{\gamma}:=e(q(\gamma))\mathfrak{e}_{\gamma}\quad\text{and}\quad\rho_{L}(S)\mathfrak{e}_{\gamma}:=\frac{\sqrt{i}^{b^{-}-b^{+}}}{\sqrt{L^{\prime}/L}}\sum_{\delta\in L^{\prime}/L}e(-(\gamma,\delta))\mathfrak{e}_{\delta}.

The Weil representation factors through a finite quotient of
\operatorname{Mp}_{2}(\mathbb{Z})
.

A short calculation using orthogonality of characters shows for example

\rho_{L}(Z)\mathfrak{e}_{\gamma}=i^{b^{-}-b^{+}}\mathfrak{e}_{-\gamma}.

For a vector-valued function
f:\mathbb{H}\to\mathbb{C}[L^{\prime}/L]
we write
f_{\gamma}:\mathbb{H}\to\mathbb{C}
for its components with respect to the standard basis, i.e.
f=\sum_{\gamma\in L^{\prime}/L}f_{\gamma}\mathfrak{e}_{\gamma}
. For
k\in\frac{1}{2}\mathbb{Z}
we define the
Petersson slash operator

f\mapsto f|_{k,L}(M,\phi)
by

(f|_{k,L}(M,\phi))(\tau)=\phi(\tau)^{-2k}\rho_{L}^{-1}(M,\phi)f(M\tau).

If
f:\mathbb{H}\to\mathbb{C}[L^{\prime}/L]
is smooth and invariant under the action of
T
, i.e.
f|_{k,L}T=f
, then we have a Fourier expansion

f(\tau)=\sum_{\gamma\in L^{\prime}/L}\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}+q(\gamma)}c(\gamma,n,v)\mathfrak{e}_{\gamma}(nu).

Definition 2.3
.

A function
f:\mathbb{H}\to\mathbb{C}[L^{\prime}/L]
is said to be
modular
of weight
k
with respect to the Weil representation
\rho_{L}
if
f|_{k,L}(M,\phi)=f
for all
(M,\phi)\in\operatorname{Mp}_{2}(\mathbb{Z})
.

Assume now that
b^{+}-b^{-}
is even and let
k\in\mathbb{Z}
. Moreover, set
\kappa=\frac{b^{-}-b^{+}}{2}+k
. Write
\operatorname{Iso}(L^{\prime}/L)
for the isotropic elements in
L^{\prime}/L
and let
\beta\in\operatorname{Iso}(L^{\prime}/L)
. Similar as in
[
BK03
]
we define the
vector-valued non-holomorphic Eisenstein series
of weight
k
by

E_{k,\beta}(\tau,s)=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{M\in\tilde{\Gamma}_{\infty}\backslash\operatorname{Mp}_{2}(\mathbb{Z})}v^{s}\mathfrak{e}_{\beta}|_{k,L}M.

Observe that
[
BK03
]
consider the dual Weil representation
\rho_{L}^{*}
. For
\beta\in\operatorname{Iso}(L^{\prime}/L)
of order
N_{\beta}
and a character
\chi:(\mathbb{Z}/N_{\beta}\mathbb{Z})^{\times}\to\mathbb{C}^{\times}
we define

E_{k,\beta,\chi}(\tau,s):=\sum_{n\in(\mathbb{Z}/N_{\beta}\mathbb{Z})^{\times}}\chi(n)E_{k,n\beta}(\tau,s).

More generally, for
\mathfrak{v}\in\operatorname{Iso}(\mathbb{C}[L^{\prime}/L])
we define

\displaystyle E_{k,\mathfrak{v}}(\tau,s)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{M\in\tilde{\Gamma}_{\infty}\backslash\operatorname{Mp}_{2}(\mathbb{Z})}v^{s}\mathfrak{v}|_{k,L}M

\displaystyle=\sum_{\beta\in\operatorname{Iso}(L^{\prime}/L)}\mathfrak{v}_{\beta}E_{k,\beta}(\tau,s).

We have
E_{k,\mathfrak{v}^{*}}=(-1)^{\kappa}E_{k,\mathfrak{v}}
and a Fourier expansion of the form

\displaystyle E_{k,\mathfrak{v}}(\tau,s)

\displaystyle=(\mathfrak{v}+(-1)^{\kappa}\mathfrak{v}^{*})v^{s}+\sum_{\gamma\in\operatorname{Iso}(L^{\prime}/L)}c_{k,\mathfrak{v}}(\gamma,0,s)v^{1-s-k}

\displaystyle+\sum_{\gamma\in L^{\prime}/L}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\in\mathbb{Z}+q(\gamma)\\
n\neq 0\end{subarray}}c_{k,\mathfrak{v}}(\gamma,n,s)\mathcal{W}_{s}(4\pi nv)\mathfrak{e}_{\gamma}(nu),

where
\mathcal{W}_{s}
is the Whittaker function defined in
[
BK03
]
. For the precise coefficients see
[
BK03
]
,
[
Wil19
]
(or
[
BK01
]
,
[
Sch06
]
,
[
Sch18
]
for the holomorphic case
k>2
), we will not need them here. We will also use the notation

\displaystyle c_{k,\mathfrak{v}}(\gamma,n,s,v)

\displaystyle:=c_{k,\mathfrak{v}}(\gamma,n,s)\mathcal{W}_{s}(4\pi nv),

\displaystyle c_{k,\mathfrak{v}}(\gamma,0,s,v)

\displaystyle:=(\mathfrak{v}_{\gamma}+(-1)^{\kappa}\mathfrak{v}_{-\gamma})v^{s}+c_{k,\mathfrak{v}}(\gamma,0,s)v^{1-s-k}.

If
\kappa
is odd, the Eisenstein series for
\beta\in\operatorname{Iso}(L^{\prime}/L)
with
\beta=-\beta
vanish identically. It can be easily seen that the vector-valued Eisenstein series are
\operatorname{Mp}_{2}(\mathbb{Z})
-translates of usual scalar-valued Eisenstein series. In particular, using
[
DS05
, Section 4.10]
we obtain the meromorphic continuation of the non-holomorphic Eisenstein series. According to
[
Hej83
, Page 372]
(his Eisenstein series are given by
y^{\frac{k}{2}}E_{k,\beta}(\tau,s-\frac{k}{2})
and he considers more general representations) we have the functional equation

E_{k,\beta}(\tau,s)=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{\alpha\in\operatorname{Iso}(L^{\prime}/L)}c_{k,\beta}(\alpha,0,s)E_{k,\alpha}(\tau,1-k-s).

3.
Siegel Theta Function

Let
p
be a polynomial on
\mathbb{R}^{(b^{+},b^{-})}
which is homogeneous of degree
\kappa
in the positive definite variables and independent of the negative definite variables. For an isometry
\nu:L\otimes\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}^{(b^{+},b^{-})}
we write
\nu^{+}
and
\nu^{-}
for the inverse image of
\mathbb{R}^{(b^{+},0)}
and
\mathbb{R}^{(0,b^{-})}
. For an element
\lambda\in L\otimes\mathbb{R}
we write
\lambda_{\nu^{\pm}}
for the projection of
\lambda
onto
\nu^{\pm}
. The positive definite
majorant
associated to
\nu
is then given by
q_{\nu}(\lambda)=q(\lambda_{\nu^{+}})-q(\lambda_{\nu^{-}})
. For
\gamma\in L^{\prime}/L,\tau\in\mathbb{H}
and an isometry
\nu:L\otimes\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}^{(b^{+},b^{-})}
following
[
Bor98
]
we define the
Siegel theta function

\displaystyle\theta_{\gamma}(\tau,\alpha,\beta,\nu,p)

\displaystyle:=\sum_{\lambda\in\gamma+L}\exp\left(\frac{\Delta}{8\pi v}\right)(p)(\nu(\lambda+\beta))

\displaystyle\times e(\tau q((\lambda+\beta)_{\nu^{+}})+\overline{\tau}q((\lambda+\beta)_{\nu^{-}}-(\lambda+\beta/2,\alpha))),

where
\Delta
is the usual
Laplace operator
on
\mathbb{R}^{b^{+}+b^{-}}
and
\alpha,\beta\in L\otimes\mathbb{R}
. Moreover, we define

\displaystyle\Theta_{L}(\tau,\alpha,\beta,\nu,p)

\displaystyle:=\sum_{\gamma\in L^{\prime}/L}\theta_{\gamma}(\tau,\alpha,\beta,\nu,p)\mathfrak{e}_{\gamma}.

For
\alpha=\beta=0
we write

\displaystyle\theta_{\gamma}(\tau,\nu,p):=\theta_{\gamma}(\tau,0,0,\nu,p)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\lambda\in\gamma+L}\exp\left(-\frac{\Delta}{8\pi v}\right)(p)(\nu(\lambda))e(\tau q(\lambda_{\nu^{+}})+\overline{\tau}q(\lambda_{\nu^{-}}))

\displaystyle=\sum_{\lambda\in\gamma+L}\exp\left(-\frac{\Delta}{8\pi v}\right)(p)(\nu(\lambda))e(ivq_{\nu}(\lambda)+uq(\lambda))

and

\displaystyle\Theta_{L}(\tau,\nu,p):=\Theta_{L}(\tau,0,0,\nu,p)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\gamma\in L^{\prime}/L}\theta_{\gamma}(\tau,\nu,p)\mathfrak{e}_{\gamma}.

Using Poisson summation one obtains

Theorem 3.1

(
[
Bor98
, Theorem 4.1]
)
.

For
(M,\phi)\in\operatorname{Mp}_{2}(\mathbb{Z}),M=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}a&b\\
c&d\end{smallmatrix}\right)
we have

\Theta_{L}(M\tau,a\alpha+b\beta,c\alpha+d\beta,\nu,p)=\phi(\tau)^{b^{+}+2\kappa}\overline{\phi(\tau)}^{b^{-}}\rho_{L}(M,\phi)\Theta_{L}(\tau,\alpha,\beta,\nu,p).

In particular, for
\alpha=\beta=0
, the theta function
\Theta_{L}(\tau,\nu,p)
has weight
(\frac{b^{+}}{2}+\kappa,\frac{b^{-}}{2})
.

We will need the following growth estimate which is proven as in the classical case.

Lemma 3.2
.

The Siegel theta function satisfies

\theta_{\gamma}(\tau,\nu,p)=O(v^{-\frac{b^{+}}{2}-\kappa-\frac{b^{-}}{2}})

for
v\to 0
uniformly in
u
.

Borcherds shows in
[
Bor98
]
that the theta function can be written as a Poincaré series. We will indicate the construction, which is also given in
[
Bru02
]
. Write
\operatorname{Iso}_{0}(L)
for the primitive isotropic elements of
L
and let
z\in\operatorname{Iso}_{0}(L),z^{\prime}\in L^{\prime}
with
(z,z^{\prime})=1
. Let
N_{z}
be the level of
z
and define the lattice

K=L\cap z^{\perp}\cap z^{\prime\perp}.

Then
K
has signature
(b^{+}-1,b^{-}-1)
. For a vector
\lambda\in L\otimes\mathbb{R}
we write
\lambda_{K}
for its orthogonal projection to
K\otimes\mathbb{R}
, which is given by

\lambda_{K}=\lambda-(\lambda,z)z^{\prime}+(\lambda,z)(z^{\prime},z^{\prime})z-(\lambda,z^{\prime})z.

Let
\zeta\in L
such that
(z,\zeta)=N_{z}
and write

\zeta=\zeta_{K}+N_{z}z^{\prime}+Bz

for some
B\in\mathbb{Q}
. Then we have

L=K\oplus\mathbb{Z}\zeta+\mathbb{Z}z.

Consider the sublattice

L_{0}^{\prime}=\{\lambda\in L^{\prime}|(\lambda,z)\equiv 0\bmod{N_{z}}\}\subseteq L^{\prime}

and the projection

\pi:L_{0}^{\prime}\to K^{\prime},\lambda\mapsto\pi(\lambda)=\lambda_{K}+\frac{(\lambda,z)}{N_{z}}\zeta_{K}.

This projection induces a surjective map
L_{0}^{\prime}/L\to K^{\prime}/K
which we also denote by
\pi
and we have

L_{0}^{\prime}/L=\{\lambda\in L^{\prime}/L|(\lambda,z)\equiv 0\bmod{N_{z}}\}.

For an isometry
\nu:L\otimes\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}^{(b^{+},b^{-})}
we write
\omega^{\pm}
for the orthogonal complement of
z_{\nu^{\pm}}
in
\nu^{\pm}
. This yields a decomposition

L\otimes\mathbb{R}=\omega^{+}\oplus\mathbb{R}z_{\nu^{+}}\oplus\omega^{-}\oplus\mathbb{R}z_{\nu^{-}}

and for
\lambda\in L\otimes\mathbb{R}
we write
\lambda_{\omega^{\pm}}
for the corresponding projections of
\lambda
onto
\omega^{\pm}
. Additionally the map

\omega:L\otimes\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}^{(b^{+},b^{-})},\lambda\mapsto\nu(\lambda_{\omega^{+}}+\lambda_{\omega^{-}})

can be seen to be an isometry
K\otimes\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}^{(b^{+}-1,b^{-}-1)}
by restriction. For a homogeneous polynomial
p
as above we now define the homogeneous polynomials
p_{\omega,h}
of degree
\kappa-h
in the positive definite variables by

p(\nu(\lambda))=\sum_{h}(\lambda,z_{\nu^{+}})^{h}p_{\omega,h}(\omega(\lambda)).

We have the following

Theorem 3.3

(
[
Bor98
, Theorem 5.2]
)
.

Let
\mu=-z^{\prime}+\frac{z_{\nu^{+}}}{2z_{\nu^{+}}^{2}}+\frac{z_{\nu^{-}}}{2z_{\nu^{-}}^{2}}\in L\otimes\mathbb{R}
. Then

\displaystyle\theta_{\gamma+L}(\tau,\nu,p)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2vz_{\nu^{+}}^{2}}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}c,d\in\mathbb{Z}\\
c\equiv(\gamma,z)\bmod N_{z}\end{subarray}}\sum_{h}(-2iv)^{-h}

\displaystyle\times(c\overline{\tau}+d)^{h}e\left(-\frac{\lvert c\tau+d\rvert^{2}}{4ivz_{\nu^{+}}^{2}}-(\gamma,z^{\prime})d+q(z^{\prime})cd\right)

\displaystyle\times\theta_{K+\pi(\gamma-cz^{\prime})}(\tau,d\mu_{K},-c\mu_{K},\omega,p_{\omega,h}).

4.
Regularized Theta Lift

As before let
L
be an even lattice of signature
(b^{+},b^{-})
and
p
a polynomial on
\mathbb{R}^{(b^{+},b^{-})}
which is homogeneous of degree
\kappa
in the first
b^{+}
variables and independent of the last
b^{-}
variables (in fact the results of this and the next two sections can be generalized to include polynomials which are homogeneous also in the negative definite variables). Denote by
\Theta_{L}(\tau,\nu,p)
the corresponding theta series. Let
k=\frac{b^{+}}{2}+\kappa-\frac{b^{-}}{2}
and assume
b^{+}-b^{-}\equiv 0\bmod{2}
. For
\beta\in\operatorname{Iso}(L^{\prime}/L)
recall the vector-valued non-holomorphic Eisenstein series
E_{k,\beta}(\tau,s)
of weight
k
for the Weil representation
\rho_{L}
. Then

\langle E_{k,\beta}(\tau,s),\Theta_{L}(\tau,\nu,p)\rangle v^{\frac{b^{+}}{2}+\kappa}

is invariant under
\operatorname{Mp}_{2}(\mathbb{Z})
in
\tau
. Hence, following
[
Bor98
]
, we define

\displaystyle\Phi_{k,\beta}(\nu,p,s,t)=\lim_{T\to\infty}\int_{\mathcal{F}_{T}}\langle E_{k,\beta}(\tau,s),\Theta_{L}(\tau,\nu,p)\rangle v^{\frac{b^{+}}{2}+\kappa-t}\frac{\mathrm{d}u\mathrm{d}v}{v^{2}},

where

\mathcal{F}_{T}=\{\tau=u+iv\in\mathcal{F}\mid v\leq T\}

and set

\Phi_{k,\beta}(\nu,p,s):=\int_{\mathcal{F}}^{\text{reg}}\langle E_{k,\beta}(\tau,s),\Theta_{L}(\tau,\nu,p)\rangle v^{\frac{b^{+}}{2}+\kappa}\frac{\mathrm{d}u\mathrm{d}v}{v^{2}}:=\operatorname{CT}_{t=0}\Phi_{k,\beta}(\nu,p,s,t),

where
\operatorname{CT}_{t=0}
denotes the constant term in the Laurent expansion at
t=0
(of the meromorphic continuation). For
\mathfrak{v}\in\operatorname{Iso}(\mathbb{C}[L^{\prime}/L])
we define
\Phi_{k,\mathfrak{v}}
analogously. By linearity of the theta lift, it often suffices to consider
\Phi_{k,\beta}
for
\beta\in\operatorname{Iso}(L^{\prime}/L)
only.

Theorem 4.1
.

For an isometry
\nu:L\otimes\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}^{b^{+},b^{-}}
the regularized theta integral converges and defines a holomorphic function for
\operatorname{Re}(t)>\operatorname{Re}(s)>1
which has a meromorphic continuation in
t
to
t=0
and to all
s\in\mathbb{C}
. The possible poles come from the poles of
E_{k,\mathfrak{v}}(\tau,s)
except for finitely many simple poles at
s=1-\frac{b^{+}}{2}+n,\frac{b^{+}}{2}-n,n\in\mathbb{N}_{>0}
, which do not occur if
p
is harmonic of positive degree. Moreover, the functional equation of
E_{k,\mathfrak{v}}(\tau,s)
yields a functional equation for the lift
\Phi_{k,\mathfrak{v}}(\nu,p,s)
, i.e. we have

\Phi_{k,\mathfrak{v}}(\nu,p,s)=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{\alpha\in\operatorname{Iso}(L^{\prime}/L)}c_{k,\mathfrak{v}}(\alpha,0,s)\Phi_{k,\mathfrak{v}}(\nu,p,1-k-s).

Proof.

We have

\displaystyle\Phi_{k,\beta}(\nu,p,s,t)

\displaystyle=\lim_{T\to\infty}\int_{\mathcal{F}_{T}}\langle E_{k,\beta}(\tau,s),\Theta_{L}(\tau,\nu,p)\rangle v^{\frac{b^{+}}{2}+\kappa-t}\frac{\mathrm{d}u\mathrm{d}v}{v^{2}}

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathcal{F}_{1}}\langle E_{k,\beta}(\tau,s),\Theta_{L}(\tau,\nu,p)\rangle v^{\frac{b^{+}}{2}+\kappa-t}\frac{\mathrm{d}u\mathrm{d}v}{v^{2}}

\displaystyle+\int_{v=1}^{\infty}\int_{u=0}^{1}\langle E_{k,\beta}(\tau,s),\Theta_{L}(\tau,\nu,p)\rangle v^{\frac{b^{+}}{2}+\kappa-t}\frac{\mathrm{d}u\mathrm{d}v}{v^{2}}.

Since
\mathcal{F}_{1}
is compact and the integrand is holomorphic in
s
and
t
, the first integral converges and defines a holomorphic function for all
s,t\in\mathbb{C}
, where
E_{k,\beta}
has no pole. Therefore it is sufficient to consider the second summand, i.e.

\varphi(\nu,p,s,t)=\int_{v=1}^{\infty}\int_{u=0}^{1}\langle E_{k,\beta}(\tau,s),\Theta_{L}(\tau,\nu,p)\rangle v^{\frac{b^{+}}{2}+\kappa-t}\frac{\mathrm{d}u\mathrm{d}v}{v^{2}}.

Inserting the Fourier coefficients of
E_{k,\beta}
and
\Theta_{L}
yields

\displaystyle\varphi(\nu,p,s,t)

\displaystyle=\int_{v=1}^{\infty}\int_{u=0}^{1}\sum_{\lambda\in L^{\prime}}\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}+q(\lambda)}c_{k,\beta}(\lambda,n,s,v)\exp\left(-\frac{\Delta}{8\pi v}\right)(\overline{p})(\nu(\lambda))

\displaystyle\times\exp(-2\pi vq_{\nu}(\lambda))e\left((n-q(\lambda))u\right)v^{\frac{b^{+}}{2}+\kappa-t}\frac{\mathrm{d}u\mathrm{d}v}{v^{2}}

\displaystyle=\int_{v=1}^{\infty}\int_{u=0}^{1}\sum_{\lambda\in L^{\prime}}\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}c_{k,\beta}(\lambda,n+q(\lambda),s,v)\exp\left(-\frac{\Delta}{8\pi v}\right)(\overline{p})(\nu(\lambda))

\displaystyle\times\exp(-2\pi vq_{\nu}(\lambda))e\left(nu\right)v^{\frac{b^{+}}{2}+\kappa-t}\frac{\mathrm{d}u\mathrm{d}v}{v^{2}}.

Carrying out the integration over
u
yields

\displaystyle\int_{v=1}^{\infty}\sum_{\lambda\in L^{\prime}}c_{k,\beta}(\lambda,q(\lambda),s,v)\exp\left(-\frac{\Delta}{8\pi v}\right)(\overline{p})(\nu(\lambda))\exp(-2\pi vq_{\nu}(\lambda))v^{\frac{b^{+}}{2}+\kappa-t}\frac{\mathrm{d}v}{v^{2}}

\displaystyle=\int_{v=1}^{\infty}c_{k,\beta}(0,0,s,v)\exp\left(-\frac{\Delta}{8\pi v}\right)(\overline{p})(0)v^{\frac{b^{+}}{2}+\kappa-t}\frac{\mathrm{d}v}{v^{2}}

\displaystyle+\int_{v=1}^{\infty}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\lambda\in L^{\prime}\setminus\{0\}\\
q(\lambda)=0\end{subarray}}c_{k,\beta}(\lambda,0,s,v)\exp\left(-\frac{\Delta}{8\pi v}\right)(\overline{p})(\nu(\lambda))\exp(-2\pi vq_{\nu}(\lambda))v^{\frac{b^{+}}{2}+\kappa-t}\frac{\mathrm{d}v}{v^{2}}

\displaystyle+\int_{v=1}^{\infty}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\lambda\in L^{\prime}\setminus\{0\}\\
q(\lambda)\neq 0\end{subarray}}c_{k,\beta}(\lambda,q(\lambda),s,v)\exp\left(-\frac{\Delta}{8\pi v}\right)(\overline{p})(\nu(\lambda))\exp(-2\pi vq_{\nu}(\lambda))v^{\frac{b^{+}}{2}+\kappa-t}\frac{\mathrm{d}v}{v^{2}}.

Inserting

c_{k,\beta}(\lambda,0,s,v):=(\delta_{\beta,\lambda}+(-1)^{\kappa}\delta_{-\beta,\lambda})v^{s}+c_{k,\beta}(\lambda,0,s)v^{1-s-k}

yields for
\varphi(\nu,p,s,t)

\displaystyle 2\delta_{\beta,0}\int_{v=1}^{\infty}\exp\left(-\frac{\Delta}{8\pi v}\right)(\overline{p})(0)v^{\frac{b^{+}}{2}+\kappa+s-2-t}\mathrm{d}v

\displaystyle+c_{k,\beta}(0,0,s)\int_{v=1}^{\infty}\exp\left(-\frac{\Delta}{8\pi v}\right)(\overline{p})(0)v^{\frac{b^{+}}{2}+\kappa-s-k-1-t}\mathrm{d}v

\displaystyle+\int_{v=1}^{\infty}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\lambda\in\pm\beta+L\setminus\{0\}\\
q(\lambda)=0\end{subarray}}\exp\left(-\frac{\Delta}{8\pi v}\right)(\overline{p})(\nu(\lambda))\exp(-2\pi vq_{\nu}(\lambda))v^{\frac{b^{+}}{2}+\kappa+s-2-t}\mathrm{d}v

\displaystyle+\int_{v=1}^{\infty}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\lambda\in L^{\prime}\setminus\{0\}\\
q(\lambda)=0\end{subarray}}c_{k,\beta}(\lambda,0,s)\exp\left(-\frac{\Delta}{8\pi v}\right)(\overline{p})(\nu(\lambda))\exp(-2\pi vq_{\nu}(\lambda))v^{\frac{b^{+}}{2}+\kappa-s-k-1-t}\mathrm{d}v

\displaystyle+\int_{v=1}^{\infty}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\lambda\in L^{\prime}\setminus\{0\}\\
q(\lambda)\neq 0\end{subarray}}c_{k,\beta}(\lambda,q(\lambda),s,v)\exp\left(-\frac{\Delta}{8\pi v}\right)(\overline{p})(\nu(\lambda))\exp(-2\pi vq_{\nu}(\lambda))v^{\frac{b^{+}}{2}+\kappa-2-t}\mathrm{d}v.

The first integral converges for
\operatorname{Re}(s)<1-\frac{b^{+}}{2}+\operatorname{Re}(t)
and is equal to

\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{(\Delta^{n}\overline{p})(0)}{(-8\pi)^{n}(t-s-\frac{b^{+}}{2}+1+n)n!}.

Similarly, the second integral converges for
\operatorname{Re}(s)>\frac{b^{+}}{2}-Re(t)
and is equal to

\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{(\Delta^{n}\overline{p})(0)}{(-8\pi)^{n}(s-\frac{b^{+}}{2}+t+n)n!}.

Both are finite sums and define meromorphic functions in
s
and
t
. The possible poles match the poles described in the theorem. The sum in the third integral is a subseries of a theta function attached to the positive definite majorant
q_{\nu}
and thus the integral converges for all
s,t\in\mathbb{C}
. The convergence of the last two integrals follows from the asymptotics

c_{k,\beta}(\gamma,n,s,v)=O(e^{-2\pi\lvert n\rvert})

for
v\geq 1
as
n\to\pm\infty
.
∎

7.
Orthogonal Non-Holomorphic Eisenstein Series

From now on let
L
be an even lattice of signature
(2,l)
and let
V=L\otimes\mathbb{Q},V(\mathbb{R})=V\otimes\mathbb{R},V(\mathbb{C})=V\otimes\mathbb{C}
. Write
P(V(\mathbb{C}))
for the corresponding
projective space
. For elements
Z_{L}=X_{L}+iY_{L}\in V(\mathbb{C})\setminus\{0\}
we write
[Z_{L}]
for the canonical projection to
P(V(\mathbb{C}))
. The subset

\mathcal{K}=\{[Z_{L}]\in P(V(\mathbb{C}))\mid(Z_{L},Z_{L})=0,(Z_{L},\overline{Z_{L}})>0\}

is the hermitian symmetric domain associated to
O(V)
. It has two connected components which are interchanged by
[Z_{L}]\mapsto[\overline{Z_{L}}]
. We choose one of them and call it
\mathcal{K}^{+}
. The action of
O(V(\mathbb{R}))
on
V(\mathbb{R})
induces an action on
\mathcal{K}
. Let
O^{+}(V(\mathbb{R}))
be the subgroup which preserves the connected components of
\mathcal{K}
and let

\tilde{\mathcal{K}}^{+}=\{Z_{L}\in V(\mathbb{C})\setminus\{0\}\mid[Z_{L}]\in\mathcal{K}^{+}\}

be the preimage of
\mathcal{K}^{+}
under the projection. For
z\in\operatorname{Iso}_{0}(L)
and
z^{\prime}\in L^{\prime}
with
(z,z^{\prime})=1
write
K=L\cap z^{\perp}\cap z^{\prime\perp}
. Let
d\in\operatorname{Iso}_{0}(K)
,
d^{\prime}\in K^{\prime}
with
(d,d^{\prime})=1
and
D=K\cap d^{\perp}\cap d^{\prime\perp}
. Moreover, let
\tilde{z}=z^{\prime}-q(z^{\prime})z
and
\tilde{d}=d^{\prime}-q(d^{\prime})d
. Let
d_{3},\ldots,d_{l}
be a basis of
D
. Then
\tilde{d},d,d_{3},\ldots,d_{l}
is a basis of
K\otimes\mathbb{R}
. We define the orthogonal upper half plane as

\mathbb{H}_{l}:=\{Z=X+iY\in W(\mathbb{C})=K\otimes\mathbb{C}\mid q(Y)>0,(Y,d)>0\}.

We will write
Z=z_{1}\tilde{d}+z_{2}d+Z_{D}
with
Z_{D}\in D\otimes\mathbb{C}
and analogously for
X,Y\in W(\mathbb{R})
. For
Z\in\mathbb{H}_{l}
we define

Z_{L}:=Z-q(Z)z+\tilde{z}.

If the component
\mathcal{K}^{+}
is chosen properly, this yields a biholomorphic map
Z\mapsto[Z_{L}]
. By setting
iC=\mathbb{H}_{l}\cap i(K\otimes\mathbb{R})
we see that
\mathbb{H}_{l}=K\otimes\mathbb{R}+iC
is a
tube domain
. The action of
O^{+}(V(\mathbb{R}))
on
\mathcal{K}^{+}
then induces an action of
O^{+}(V(\mathbb{R}))
on
\mathbb{H}_{l}
. Let

j:O^{+}(V)\times\mathbb{H}_{l}\to\mathbb{C}^{\times},\quad j(\sigma,Z):=(\sigma(Z_{L}),z)

be the
factor of automorphy
, so that we have

j(\sigma,Z)(\sigma Z)_{L}=\sigma(Z_{L})

and the cocycle relation

j(\sigma_{1}\sigma_{2},Z)=j(\sigma_{1},\sigma_{2}Z)j(\sigma_{2},Z).

According to
[
Bru02
, Lemma 3.20]
we have

q(\operatorname{Im}(\sigma Z))=\frac{q(\operatorname{Im}(Z))}{\lvert j(\sigma,Z)\rvert^{2}}.

Definition 7.1
.

A function
f:\tilde{\mathcal{K}}^{+}\to\mathbb{C}
is called modular of weight
\kappa\in\mathbb{Z}
with respect to
\Gamma\subseteq\Gamma(L)
if it satisfies

(i)

f(tZ_{L})=t^{-\kappa}f(Z_{L})
for all
t\in\mathbb{C}^{\times}
.

(ii)

f(\sigma Z_{L})=f(Z_{L})
for all
\sigma\in\Gamma
.

For a modular function
f:\tilde{\mathcal{K}}^{+}\to\mathbb{C}
of weight
\kappa\in\mathbb{Z}
with respect to
\Gamma
define

f_{z}:\mathbb{H}_{l}\to\mathbb{C},\quad f_{z}(Z):=f(Z_{L})=f(Z-q(Z)z+\tilde{z}).

Then
f_{z}
satisfies

f_{z}(\sigma Z)=j(\sigma,Z)^{\kappa}f_{z}(Z)

for all
\sigma\in\Gamma
and we have a bijective correspondence between modular functions and functions with this transformation property. If we define the weight
\kappa
slash operator
f_{z}|_{\kappa}\sigma
for
\sigma\in O^{+}(V)
by

(f_{z}|_{\kappa}\sigma)(Z):=j(\sigma,Z)^{-\kappa}f_{z}(\sigma Z),

then the modular functions on
\mathbb{H}_{l}
are exactly the functions that are invariant under the slash operator for all
\sigma\in\Gamma
.

Let now
\kappa\in\mathbb{Z}
and
\lambda\in\operatorname{Iso}_{0}(L)
and recall the tube domain representation
\mathbb{H}_{l}
corresponding to a fixed cusp
z\in\operatorname{Iso}_{0}(L)
. Denote by
\Gamma(L)_{\lambda}\subseteq\Gamma(L)
the stabilizer of
\lambda
in
\Gamma(L)
and write
\sigma_{\lambda}\in O^{+}(V)
for an element satisfying
\sigma_{\lambda}\lambda=z
. Then
q(Y)^{s}|_{\kappa}\sigma_{\lambda}
has weight
\kappa
with respect to
\Gamma(L)_{\lambda}
. Hence we define the non-holomorphic Eisenstein series corresponding to the
0
-dimensional cusp
\lambda
by

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}_{\kappa,\lambda}(Z,s)

\displaystyle:=\sum_{\sigma\in\Gamma(L)_{\lambda}\backslash\Gamma(L)}q(Y)^{s}|_{\kappa}\sigma_{\lambda}\sigma

\displaystyle=\sum_{\sigma\in\Gamma(L)_{\lambda}\backslash\Gamma(L)}j(\sigma_{\lambda}\sigma,Z)^{-\kappa}\left(\frac{q(Y)}{\lvert j(\sigma_{\lambda}\sigma,Z)\rvert^{2}}\right)^{s}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\sigma\in\Gamma(L)_{\lambda}\backslash\Gamma(L)}(\lambda,\sigma(Z_{L}))^{-\kappa}\left(\frac{q(Y)}{\lvert(\lambda,\sigma(Z_{L}))\rvert^{2}}\right)^{s}

for
Z\in\mathbb{H}_{l}
and
\operatorname{Re}(s)\gg 0
. The Eisenstein series does not depend on the choice of
\sigma_{\lambda}
. We have
\Gamma(L)_{-\lambda}=\Gamma(L)_{\lambda}
and
\mathcal{E}_{\kappa,-\lambda}(Z,s)=(-1)^{\kappa}\mathcal{E}_{\kappa,\lambda}(Z,s)
.

Consider the map

\pi_{L}:\Gamma(L)\backslash\operatorname{Iso}_{0}(L)\to\Gamma(L)\backslash\operatorname{Iso}_{0}(L^{\prime})\to\operatorname{Iso}(L^{\prime}/L),

where the first arrow is given by

with
N_{z}
being the level of
z
. For
\delta\in\operatorname{Iso}(L^{\prime}/L)
we let

\mathcal{G}_{\kappa,\delta}:=\sum_{\lambda\in\pi_{L}^{-1}(\delta)}\mathcal{E}_{\kappa,\lambda},

in particular,
\mathcal{G}_{\kappa,\delta}=0
if the preimage is empty and if
\delta=-\delta
for odd
\kappa
. For
\delta\in\operatorname{Iso}(L^{\prime}/L)
of order
N_{\delta}
and
\chi
a Dirichlet character of modulus
N_{\delta}
we define

\mathcal{G}_{\kappa,\delta,\chi}:=\sum_{k\in(\mathbb{Z}/N_{\delta}\mathbb{Z})^{\times}}\chi(k)\mathcal{G}_{\kappa,k\delta}.

By orthogonality of characters, the space generated by
\mathcal{G}_{\kappa,\delta}
and the space generated by
\mathcal{G}_{\kappa,\delta,\chi}
coincide. Moreover, one easily sees, that injectivity (resp. surjectivity) of
\pi_{L}
implies that the map induced by
\delta\mapsto\mathcal{G}_{\kappa,\delta},\delta\in\operatorname{Iso}(L^{\prime}/L)
is surjective (resp. injective). The following lemmas investigate the injectivity and surjectivity of
\pi_{L}
.

Lemma 7.2
.

The map
\pi_{L}
is surjective if and only if
L
splits a hyperbolic plane.

Proof.

If
\pi_{L}
is surjective, then there is some primitive isotropic element in

of level
1
, which yields a splitting of a hyperbolic plane. Conversely, assume

L=U\oplus L_{1}=e_{1}\mathbb{Z}\oplus e_{2}\mathbb{Z}+L_{1},

and let
\delta\in L^{\prime}/L=L_{1}^{\prime}/L_{1}
be an isotropic element. Consider a preimage
\lambda\in L_{1}^{\prime}
. Then
e_{1}-q(\lambda)e_{2}+\lambda\in L^{\prime}
is primitive isotropic with image
\delta\in L^{\prime}/L
.
∎

Lemma 7.3

(
[
FH00
, Lemma 4.4]
)
.

If
L
splits two hyperbolic planes, then
\pi_{L}
is bijective.

\displaystyle z_{Z_{L}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{Y^{2}}X_{L}

\displaystyle\lvert z_{Z_{L}}\rvert

\displaystyle\mu_{K}

\displaystyle=X

\displaystyle\omega^{+}

\displaystyle=\mathbb{R}Y_{L}

Theorem 8.1

Lemma 8.4
.

For
\kappa\in\mathbb{Z},\kappa\geq 0
we have

\displaystyle\sum_{j=0}^{\lfloor\frac{\kappa}{2}\rfloor}(-1)^{j}{\kappa\choose 2j}\Gamma(1/2+j)\Gamma(1/2+s-j)

\displaystyle=2^{\kappa-2s}\pi\frac{\Gamma(1+2s-\kappa)}{\Gamma(1+s-\kappa)}

\displaystyle=\sqrt{\pi}\frac{\Gamma(\frac{1}{2}+s-\frac{\kappa}{2})\Gamma(1+s-\frac{\kappa}{2})}{\Gamma(1+s-\kappa)}.

Proof.

We first assume that
s\geq\kappa
is an integer. Then the duplication formula yields

\displaystyle\sum_{j=0}^{\lfloor\frac{\kappa}{2}\rfloor}(-1)^{j}{\kappa\choose 2j}\Gamma(1/2+j)\Gamma(1/2+(s-j))

\displaystyle=\frac{\pi\kappa!}{4^{s}}\sum_{j=0}^{\lfloor\frac{\kappa}{2}\rfloor}(-1)^{j}\frac{1}{(2j)!(\kappa-2j)!}\frac{(2j)!}{j!}\frac{(2s-2j)!}{(s-j)!}

\displaystyle=\frac{\pi\kappa!(2s-\kappa)!}{4^{s}s!}\sum_{j=0}^{\lfloor\frac{\kappa}{2}\rfloor}(-1)^{j}{s\choose s-j}{2s-2j\choose 2s-\kappa}

\displaystyle=(-1)^{\lfloor\frac{\kappa}{2}\rfloor}\frac{\pi\kappa!(2s-\kappa)!}{4^{s}s!}\sum_{j=0}^{\lfloor\frac{\kappa}{2}\rfloor}(-1)^{j}{s\choose s-\lfloor\frac{\kappa}{2}\rfloor+j}{2s-2\lfloor\frac{\kappa}{2}\rfloor+2j\choose 2s-\kappa}

\displaystyle=(-1)^{s}\frac{\pi\kappa!(2s-\kappa)!}{4^{s}s!}\sum_{j=s-\lfloor\frac{\kappa}{2}\rfloor}^{s}(-1)^{j}{s\choose j}{2j\choose 2s-\kappa}

\displaystyle=\frac{\pi\kappa!(2s-\kappa)!}{2^{2s-\kappa}s!}{s\choose{s-\kappa}}

\displaystyle=\frac{\pi(2s-\kappa)!}{2^{2s-\kappa}(s-\kappa)!}=2^{\kappa-2s}\pi\frac{\Gamma(2s-\kappa+1)}{\Gamma(s-\kappa+1)}

This shows the identity for integers
s\geq\kappa
. Now observe using
\Gamma(z+1)=z\Gamma(z)
and the duplication formula again that

\displaystyle 2^{\kappa-2s}\pi\frac{\Gamma(1+2s-\kappa)}{\Gamma(1+s-\kappa)}

\displaystyle=\sqrt{\pi}\frac{\Gamma(\frac{1}{2}+s-\frac{\kappa}{2})\Gamma(1+s-\frac{\kappa}{2})}{\Gamma(1+s-\kappa)}

\displaystyle=\Gamma(1/2+s-\kappa)p(s)

for some polynomial
p
. Similarly, the left hand side is given by

\sum_{j=0}^{\lfloor\frac{\kappa}{2}\rfloor}(-1)^{j}{\kappa\choose 2j}\Gamma(1/2+j)\Gamma(1/2+s-j)=\Gamma(1/2+s-\kappa)q(s)

for some polynomial
q
. Now we have seen that
p(s)=q(s)
for infinitely many integers and thus
p(s)=q(s)
for all
s\in\mathbb{C}
.
∎

Abstract
. Introduction

The Knizhnik–Zamolodchikov (
KZ
) differential equations are objects
of conformal field theory, representation theory, enumerative geometry, see for example
[
KZ
,
Dr
,
EFK
,
MO
,
V2
]
.
The solutions of the
KZ
equations have the form of multidimensional hypergeometric functions,
see
[
SV1
]
. In this paper we discuss the analog of hypergeometric solutions of the KZ equations considered over a
p
-adic field
instead of the field of complex numbers.

More precisely, we consider the
KZ
equations in the special case, in which the complex hypergeometric
solutions are given by the integrals of the form

(1.1)

\displaystyle I(z_{1},\dots,z_{qg+1})=\int_{C}\frac{R(t,z_{1},\dots,z_{qg+1})\,dt}{\sqrt[1/q]{(t-z_{1})\dots(t-z_{qg+1})}}

where
q,g
are positive integer parameters, and
R(t,z)
are suitable rational functions.

In this case the space of solutions of the
KZ
equations is a
qg
-dimensional complex vector space.
We also consider the
p
-adic version of the same differential
equations. We
assume that
q
is a prime number (that is a technical assumption)
and show that the
qg
-dimensional space of
local solutions of these
p
-adic

KZ
equations has a remarkable
g
-dimensional subspace of
solutions which can be
p
-adic analytically
continued as a subspace to a large domain
\mathfrak{D}_{\operatorname{KZ}}^{(m),o}

in the space where the
KZ
equations are defined, see Theorems
6.10
and
6.12
for precise statements.
This
g
-dimensional global subspace of solutions is defined as the
uniform
p
-adic limit of a

g
-dimensional space of polynomial solutions of these
KZ
equations
modulo
p^{s}
as
s\to\infty
. For
q=2
and
g=1

this construction was deduced in
[
V5
]
from the classical B. Dwork’s paper
[
Dw
]
,
see also
[
VZ1
]
. For
q=2
and any
g
the corresponding construction was developed in
[
VZ2
]
.

In
[
SV2
]
general
KZ
equations were considered over the field
{\mathbb{F}}_{p}
and their polynomial solutions
were constructed as
p
-approximations of hypergeometric integrals.
In the current paper that construction is modified to obtain
polynomial solutions modulo
p^{s}
of the
KZ
equations related to the integrals in formula (
1.1
).
The polynomial solutions are vectors of polynomials with integer coefficients. We call them
the
p^{s}
-hypergeometric solutions.
While the complex analytic integrals in (
1.1
) give the whole
qg
-dimensional space of all solutions of the
complex
KZ
equations, the
p^{s}
-hypergeometric solutions span only a
g
-dimensional subspace.
Then the
p
-adic limit of that
subspace as
s\to\infty
gives the desired globally defined subspace of solutions.

On other
p
-approximations of hypergeometric periods see
[
SV2
,
RV1
,
RV2
,
VZ1
,
VZ2
]
.

In order to prove Theorems
6.10
and
6.12
we develop
new matrix Dwork–type congruences in Section
2
.
In Section
3
we show how our Dwork–type congruences imply the uniform

p
-adic convergence of certain sequences of matrices on suitable domains of the space of their parameters.
In Section
4

we define our
KZ
equations and construct their complex holomorphic solutions.
In Section
5
we describe the
p^{s}
-hypergeometric solutions of the same equations.
In Section
6
we formulate and prove the main Theorems
6.10
and
6.12
.

This paper may be viewed as a continuation of the paper
[
VZ2
]
where the case
q=2
is developed.

2.
Dwork–type congruences

The Dwork–type congruences were originated by B. Dwork in the classical paper
[
Dw
]
.
On Dwork–type congruences see for example

[
Dw
,
Me
,
MV
,
Vl
,
VZ1
,
VZ2
]
.

In this paper
p
is an odd prime. We denote by
{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}[w^{\pm 1}]
the ring of
Laurent polynomials in variables
w
with coefficients in
{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}
.
A congruence
F(w)\equiv G(w)\pmod{p^{s}}
for two Laurent polynomials from the ring is understood
as the divisibility by
p^{s}
of all coefficients of
F(w)-\penalty G(w)
.

For a Laurent polynomial
G(w)
we define
\sigma(G(w))=G(w^{p})
.

We denote
x=(t,z)
, where
t=(t_{1},\dots,t_{r})
and
z=(z_{1},\dots,z_{n})
are two groups of variables.

2.1.
Definition of ghosts

Let
{\bf e}=(e_{1},\dots,e_{l})
be a tuple of positive integers and
\Lambda=(\Lambda_{0}(x),\Lambda_{1}(x),\dots,\Lambda_{l}(x))

a tuple of Laurent polynomials in
{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}[x^{\pm 1}]
.

Define
V_{0}(x)=\Lambda_{0}(x)
. For
s=1,\dots,l
, define

V_{s}(x)

by the recursive formula

(2.1)

\displaystyle\Lambda_{0}(x)\Lambda_{1}(x)^{p^{e_{1}}}\dots\Lambda_{s}(x)^{p^{e_{1}+\dots+e_{s}}}=V_{s}(x)+V_{s-1}(x)\Lambda_{s}(x^{p^{e_{1}+\dots+e_{s}}})+

\displaystyle\phantom{aaa}+V_{s-2}(x)\Lambda_{s-1}(x^{p^{e_{1}+\dots+e_{s-1}}})\Lambda_{s}(x^{p^{e_{1}+\dots+e_{s-1}}})^{p^{e_{s}}}+\dots+

\displaystyle\phantom{aaaaaa}+V_{0}(x)\Lambda_{1}(x^{p^{e_{1}}})\Lambda_{2}(x^{p^{e_{1}}})^{p^{e_{2}}}\cdots\Lambda_{s}(x^{p^{e_{1}}})^{p^{e_{2}+\dots+e_{s}}},

The Laurent polynomials
V_{0}(x),\dots,V_{l}(x)\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}[x^{\pm 1}]
are called
the
ghosts
associated with the tuples
{\bf e}
and
\Lambda
.

For every
0\leqslant j\leqslant s\leqslant l
, denote

\displaystyle W_{s}(x)

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle\Lambda_{0}(x)\Lambda_{1}(x)^{p^{e_{1}}}\cdots\Lambda_{s}(x)^{p^{e_{1}+\dots+e_{s}}},

\displaystyle W_{s}^{(j)}(x)

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle\Lambda_{j}(x)\Lambda_{j+1}(x)^{p^{e_{j+1}}}\cdots\Lambda_{s}(x)^{p^{e_{j+1}+\dots+e_{s}}}.

Then (
2.1
) can be formulates as

(2.2)

W_{s}(x)=V_{s}(x)+\sum_{j=1}^{s}V_{j-1}(x)W_{s}^{(j)}(x^{p^{e_{1}+\dots+e_{j}}}),

or as

(2.3)

W_{s}(x)=V_{s}(x)+\sum_{j=1}^{s}V_{j-1}(x)\sigma^{e_{1}+\dots+e_{j}}(W_{s}^{(j)}(x)).

Lemma 2.1
.

For
s=0,1,\dots,l
, we have
V_{s}(x)\equiv 0\pmod{p^{s}}
.

Proof.

In the proof we use the congruence
F(x^{p})^{p^{i-1}}\equiv F(x)^{p^{i}}\pmod{p^{i}}
valid for
i>0
.

For
s=0
we have
V_{0}(x)=\Lambda_{0}(x)
and no requirements on divisibility.
For
s=1
, we have

\displaystyle V_{1}(x)=\Lambda_{0}(x)\Lambda_{1}(x)^{p^{e_{1}}}-V_{0}(x)\Lambda_{1}(x^{p^{e_{1}}})=\Lambda_{0}(x)(\Lambda_{1}(x)^{p^{e_{1}}}-\Lambda_{1}(x^{p^{e_{1}}})),

and

(2.4)

\displaystyle\Lambda_{1}(x^{p^{e_{1}}})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\pmod{p}}}{{\equiv}}\Lambda_{1}(x^{p^{e_{1}-1}})^{p}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\pmod{p^{2}}}}{{\equiv}}\Lambda_{1}(x^{p^{e_{1}-2}})^{p^{2}}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\pmod{p^{3}}}}{{\equiv}}\dots\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\pmod{p^{e_{1}}}}}{{\equiv}}\Lambda_{1}(x)^{p^{e_{1}}}.

This proves the lemma for
s=1
.

For
s>1
the proof is by induction on
s
. Assume that the lemma is proved for all
j<s
. Then
similarly to (
2.4
) we obtain

\Lambda_{s}(x^{p^{e_{1}+\dots+e_{j}}})^{p^{e_{j+1}+\dots+e_{s}}}{\equiv}\Lambda_{s}(x)^{p^{e_{1}+\dots+e_{s}}}\pmod{p^{1+e_{j+1}+\dots+e_{s}}}

and hence

\displaystyle V_{j-1}(x)\Lambda_{s}(x^{p^{e_{1}+\dots+e_{j}}})^{p^{e_{j+1}+\dots+e_{s}}}

\displaystyle\equiv

\displaystyle V_{j-1}(x)\Lambda_{s}(x)^{p^{e_{1}+\dots+e_{s}}}\pmod{p^{j+e_{j+1}+\dots+e_{s}}}

\displaystyle\equiv

\displaystyle V_{j-1}(x)\Lambda_{s}(x)^{p^{e_{1}+\dots+e_{s}}}\pmod{p^{s}}

since
e_{i}\geqslant 1
for all
i
. Then we deduce modulo
p^{s}
:

\displaystyle V_{s}(x)

\displaystyle=W_{s-1}(x)\Lambda_{s}(x)^{p^{e_{1}+\dots+e_{s}}}-\sum_{j=1}^{s-1}V_{j-1}(x)W_{s-1}^{(j)}(x^{p^{e_{1}+\dots+e_{j}}})\Lambda_{s}(x^{p^{e_{1}+\dots+e_{j}}})^{p^{e_{j+1}+\dots+e_{s}}}

\displaystyle-V_{s-1}(x)\Lambda_{s}(x^{p^{e_{1}+\dots+e_{s}}})\equiv

\displaystyle\equiv\bigg(W_{s-1}(x)-\sum_{j=1}^{s-1}V_{j-1}(x)W_{s-1}^{(j)}(x^{p^{e_{1}+\dots+e_{j}}})-V_{s-1}(x)\bigg)\Lambda_{s}(x)^{p^{e_{1}+\dots+e_{s}}}=0,

obtaining the required statement.
∎

For a Laurent polynomial
F(t,z)
in
t,z
, let
N(F)\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{r}
be the Newton polytope of
F(t,z)
with respect to
the
t

variables only
.

Lemma 2.2
.

For
s=0,1,\dots,l
, we have

\displaystyle N(V_{s})\subset N(\Lambda_{0})+p^{e_{1}}N(\Lambda_{1})+\dots+p^{e_{1}+\dots+e_{s}}N(\Lambda_{s})\,.

Proof.

This follows from (
2.2
) by induction on
s
.
∎

2.2.
Convex polytopes

Let
\Delta=(\Delta_{0},\dots,\Delta_{l})
be a tuple of nonempty finite subsets of
{\mathbb{Z}}^{r}
of the same size

\#\Delta_{j}=g
for some positive integer
g
.

Definition 2.3
.

A tuple
(N_{0},N_{1},\dots,N_{l})
of convex polytopes in
{\mathbb{R}}^{r}

is called
(\Delta,{\bf e})
-
admissible
if for any

0\leqslant i\leqslant j<l
we have

(2.5)

\displaystyle\phantom{aaaaaa}\big(\Delta_{i}+N_{i}+p^{e_{i+1}}N_{i+1}+\dots+p^{e_{i+1}+\dots+e_{j}}N_{j}\big)\cap p^{e_{i+1}+\dots+e_{j+1}}{\mathbb{Z}}^{r}\subset p^{e_{i+1}+\dots+e_{j+1}}\Delta_{j+1}\,.

Notice that any sub-tuple
(N_{i},N_{i+1},\dots,N_{j})
of a
(\Delta,{\bf e})
-admissible
tuple
(N_{0},N_{1},\dots,N_{l})
is
(\Delta^{\prime},{\bf e}^{\prime})
-admissible where

\Delta^{\prime}=(\Delta_{i},\dots,\Delta_{j})
and
{\bf e}^{\prime}=(e_{i+1},\dots,e_{j})
.

Definition 2.4
.

A tuple
(\Lambda_{0}(t,z),\Lambda_{1}(t,z),\dots,\Lambda_{l}(t,z))
of Laurent polynomials
is called
(\Delta,{\bf e})
-
admissible

if the tuple

\big(N(\Lambda_{0}),N(\Lambda_{1}),\dots,N(\Lambda_{l})\big)
is
(\Delta,{\bf e})
-admissible.

Example
.

Let
r=1
,
n=13
,
{\bf e}=(2,2,\dots,2)
,
\Gamma=\{1,2,3,4\}\subset{\mathbb{Z}}
,

\Delta=(\Gamma,\Gamma,\dots,\Gamma)
,

N=[0,13(p^{2}-1)/3]\subset{\mathbb{R}}
,

F(t_{1},z)=\prod_{i=1}^{13}(t_{1}-z_{i})^{(p^{2}-1)/3}
. Then
the tuple
(N,N,\dots,N)
of intervals in
{\mathbb{R}}
and
the tuple of polynomials
(F(t_{1},z),F(t_{1},z),\dots,F(t_{1},z))

are
(\Delta,{\bf e})
-admissible.

2.3.
Hasse–Witt matrices

For
v\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{r}
denote by
\operatorname{Coeff}_{v}F(t,z)
the coefficient of
t^{v}
in the Laurent polynomial
F(t,z)
.
This is a Laurent polynomial in
z
.

Given
m\geqslant 1
and finite subsets
\Delta^{\prime},\Delta^{\prime\prime}\subset{\mathbb{Z}}^{r}
,
define the
Hasse–Witt matrix
of the Laurent polynomial
F(t,z)

by the formula

(2.6)

\displaystyle A(m,\Delta^{\prime},\Delta^{\prime\prime},F(t,z)):=\big(\operatorname{Coeff}_{p^{m}v-u}F(t,z)\big)_{u\in\Delta^{\prime},v\in\Delta^{\prime\prime}}\,.

Lemma 2.5
.

Let
\Lambda
be a
(\Delta,{\bf e})
-admissible tuple of Laurent polynomials in

{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}[x^{\pm 1}]={\mathbb{Z}}_{p}[t^{\pm 1},z^{\pm 1}]
.
Then for
0\leqslant s\leqslant l
we have

(i)

\displaystyle\;\;A(e_{1}+\dots+e_{s+1},\Delta_{0},\Delta_{s+1},V_{s})\equiv 0\pmod{p^{s}};

(ii)

\displaystyle\;\;A\big(e_{1}+\dots+e_{s+1},\Delta_{0},\Delta_{s+1},W_{s}\big)=

\displaystyle=A\big(e_{1},\Delta_{0},\Delta_{1},V_{0})\cdot\sigma^{e_{1}}\big(A\big(e_{2}+\dots+e_{s+1},\Delta_{1},\Delta_{s+1},W_{s}^{(1)}\big)\big)+

\displaystyle+A\big(e_{1}+e_{2},\Delta_{0},\Delta_{2},V_{1})\cdot\sigma^{e_{1}+e_{2}}\big(A\big(e_{3}+\dots+e_{s+1},\Delta_{2},\Delta_{s+1},W_{s}^{(2)}\big)\big)+\dots+

\displaystyle+A\big(e_{1}+\dots+e_{s},\Delta_{0},\Delta_{s},V_{s-1})\cdot\sigma^{e_{1}+\dots+e_{s}}\big(A\big(e_{s+1},\Delta_{s},\Delta_{s+1},W_{s}^{(s)}\big)\big)+

\displaystyle+A\big(e_{1}+\dots+e_{s+1},\Delta_{0},\Delta_{s+1},V_{s}).

Notice that all these matrices are
g\times g
-matrices.

Proof.

Part (i) follows from Lemma
2.1
. To prove (ii) consider the identity

(2.7)

\displaystyle\Lambda_{0}(t,z)\Lambda_{1}(t,z)^{p^{e_{1}}}\dots\Lambda_{s}(t,z)^{p^{e_{1}+\dots+e_{s}}}=\sum_{j=1}^{s}V_{j-1}(t,z)\Lambda_{j}(t^{p^{e_{1}+\dots+e_{j}}},z^{p^{e_{1}+\dots+e_{j}}})\times

\displaystyle\qquad\times\Lambda_{j+1}(t^{p^{e_{1}+\dots+e_{j}}},z^{p^{e_{1}+\dots+e_{j}}})^{p^{e_{j+1}}}\dots\Lambda_{s}(t^{p^{e_{1}+\dots+e_{j}}},z^{p^{e_{1}+\dots+e_{j}}})^{p^{e_{j+1}+\dots+e_{s}}}+V_{s}(t,z),

which is nothing else but (
2.1
).
Let
u\in\Delta_{0},v\in\Delta_{s+1}
. In order to calculate the coefficient of
t^{p^{e_{1}+\dots+e_{s+1}}v-u}

in the
j
-th summand on the right-hand side of (
2.7
),
we look for all pairs of vectors
w\in N(V_{j-1})
and
y\in N(\Lambda_{j}(t,z)\dots\Lambda_{s}(t,z)^{p^{e_{j+1}+\dots+e_{s+1}}})

such that

\displaystyle w+p^{e_{1}+\dots+e_{j}}y=p^{e_{1}+\dots+e_{s+1}}v-u.

Hence
u+w\in p^{e_{1}+\dots+e_{j}}{\mathbb{Z}}^{r}
. On the other hand, it follows from Lemma
2.2

that
w\in N(\Lambda_{0})+p^{e_{1}}N(\Lambda_{1})+\dots+p^{e_{1}+\dots+e_{j-1}}N(\Lambda_{j-1})
, so that

\displaystyle u+w\in\Delta_{0}+N(\Lambda_{0})+pN(\Lambda_{1})+\dots+p^{e_{1}+\dots+e_{j-1}}N(\Lambda_{j-1}).

From the
(\Delta,{\bf e})
-admissibility we deduce that

u+w=p^{e_{1}+\dots+e_{j}}\delta
for some
\delta\in\Delta_{j}
, thus

w=p^{e_{1}+\dots+e_{j}}\delta-\penalty u
,
y=p^{e_{j+1}+\dots+e_{s+1}}v-\delta
and

\displaystyle\operatorname{Coeff}_{p^{e_{1}+\dots+e_{s+1}}v-u}\big(V_{j-1}(t,z)\Lambda_{j}(t^{p^{e_{1}+\dots+e_{j}}},z^{p^{e_{1}+\dots+e_{j}}})\dots\Lambda_{s}(t^{p^{e_{1}+\dots+e_{j}}},z^{p^{e_{1}+\dots+e_{j}}})^{p^{e_{j+1}+\dots+e_{s}}}\big)=

\displaystyle\phantom{aaa}=\sum_{\delta\in\Delta_{j}}\operatorname{Coeff}_{p^{e_{1}+\dots+e_{j}}\delta-u}(V_{j-1}(t,z))\,\cdot

\displaystyle\phantom{aaaaaa}\cdot\,\sigma^{e_{1}+\dots+e_{j}}\big(\operatorname{Coeff}_{p^{e_{j+1}+\dots+e_{s+1}}v-\delta}\big(\Lambda_{j}(t,z)\Lambda_{j+1}(t,z)^{p^{e_{j+1}}}\dots\Lambda_{s}(t,z)^{p^{e_{j+1}+\dots+e_{s}}}\big)\big).

This proves (ii).
∎

2.4.
Congruences

The next results discuss congruences of the type

F_{1}(z)F_{2}(z)^{-1}\equiv G_{1}(z)G_{2}(z)^{-1}\pmod{p^{s}}
, where

F_{1},F_{2},G_{1},G_{2}
are
g\times g
matrices whose entries are Laurent polynomials in
z
.
We consider such congruences when
the determinants
\det F_{2}(z)
and
\det G_{2}(z)

are Laurent polynomials both nonzero modulo
p
.
Using Cramer’s rule we write
the entries of the inverse matrix
F_{2}(z)^{-1}
in the
form
f_{ij}(z)/\det F_{2}(z)
for
f_{ij}(z)\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}[z^{\pm 1}]
and do a similar computation for
G_{2}(z)
.
This presents the congruence
F_{1}(z)F_{2}(z)^{-1}\equiv G_{1}(z)G_{2}(z)^{-1}\pmod{p^{s}}

in the form

(2.8)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\det F_{2}(z)}\cdot F(z)\ \equiv\ \frac{1}{\det G_{2}(z)}\cdot G(z)\pmod{p^{s}}

for some
g\times g
matrices
F(z),G(z)
with entries in
{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}[z^{\pm 1}]
,
while (
2.8
) is nothing else but the congruence

F(z)\cdot\det G_{2}(z)\equiv G(z)\cdot\det F_{2}(z)\pmod{p^{s}}
.

Theorem 2.6
.

Let
(\Lambda_{0}(t,z),\Lambda_{1}(t,z),\dots,\Lambda_{l}(t,z))
be a
(\Delta,{\bf e})
-admissible
tuple of Laurent polynomials in
{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}[x^{\pm 1}]={\mathbb{Z}}_{p}[t^{\pm 1},z^{\pm 1}]
.

(i)

For
0\leqslant s\leqslant l
we have

\displaystyle A\big(e_{1}+\dots+e_{s+1},\Delta_{0},\Delta_{s+1},\Lambda_{0}(x)\Lambda_{1}(x)^{p^{e_{1}}}\cdots\Lambda_{s}(x)^{p^{e_{1}+\dots+e_{s}}}\big)\equiv

\displaystyle\equiv A\big(e_{1},\Delta_{0},\Delta_{1},\Lambda_{0}(x)\big)\cdot\sigma^{e_{1}}\big(A\big(e_{2},\Delta_{1},\Delta_{2},\Lambda_{1}(x)\big)\big)\cdots\sigma^{e_{1}+\dots+e_{s}}\big(A\big(e_{s+1},\Delta_{s},\Delta_{s+1},\Lambda_{s}(x)\big)\big)

modulo
p
.

(ii)

Assume that the determinants of the matrices
A\big(e_{i+1},\Delta_{i},\Delta_{i+1},\Lambda_{i}(t,z)\big)
,
i=0,1,\dots,l
,
are Laurent polynomials all nonzero modulo
p
.
Then for
1\leqslant s\leqslant l

the determinant of the matrix

A\big(e_{2}+\dots+e_{s+1},\Delta_{1},\Delta_{s+1},\Lambda_{1}(x)\Lambda_{2}(x)^{p^{e_{2}}}\cdots\Lambda_{s}(x)^{p^{e_{2}+\dots+e_{s}}}\big)

is a Laurent polynomial nonzero modulo
p
and
we have modulo
p^{s}

:

(2.9)

\displaystyle A\big(e_{1}+\dots+e_{s+1},\Delta_{0},\Delta_{s+1},\Lambda_{0}(x)\Lambda_{1}(x)^{p^{e_{1}}}\cdots\Lambda_{s}(x)^{p^{e_{1}+\dots+e_{s}}}\big)\cdot

\displaystyle\cdot\sigma^{e_{1}}\big(A\big(e_{2}+\dots+e_{s+1},\Delta_{1},\Delta_{s+1},\Lambda_{1}(x)\Lambda_{2}(x)^{p^{e_{2}}}\cdots\Lambda_{s}(x)^{p^{e_{2}+\dots+e_{s}}}\big)\big)^{-1}\equiv

\displaystyle\equiv A\big(e_{1}+\dots+e_{s},\Delta_{0},\Delta_{s},\Lambda_{0}(x)\Lambda_{1}(x)^{p^{e_{1}}}\cdots\Lambda_{s-1}(x)^{p^{e_{1}+\dots+e_{s-1}}}\big)\cdot

\displaystyle\cdot\sigma^{e_{1}}\big(A\big(e_{2}+\dots+e_{s},\Delta_{1},\Delta_{s},\Lambda_{1}(x)\Lambda_{2}(x)^{p^{e_{2}}}\cdots\Lambda_{s-1}(x)^{p^{e_{2}+\dots+e_{s-1}}}\big)\big)^{-1},

where in this congruence for
s=1
we
understand the second factor on the right-hand side as
the
g\times g
identity matrix, see formula (
2.10
) below.

Proof.

By Lemma
2.5
we have

\displaystyle A\big(e_{1}+\dots+e_{s+1},\Delta_{0},\Delta_{s+1},\Lambda_{0}(x)\Lambda_{1}(x)^{p^{e_{1}}}\dots\Lambda_{s}(x)^{p^{e_{1}+\dots+e_{s}}}\big)\equiv

\displaystyle\equiv A\big(e_{1},\Delta_{0},\Delta_{1},\Lambda_{0}(x))\cdot\sigma^{e_{1}}\big(A\big(e_{2}+\dots+e_{s+1},\Delta_{1},\Delta_{s+1},\Lambda_{1}(x)\Lambda_{2}(x)^{p^{e_{2}}}\dots\Lambda_{s}(x)^{p^{e_{2}+\dots+e_{s}}}\big)\big)

modulo
p
.
Iteration gives part (i) of the theorem.

If the determinants of the matrices
A\big(e_{i+1},\Delta_{i},\Delta_{i+1},\Lambda_{i}(t,z)\big)
,
i=0,1,\dots,l
,
are Laurent polynomials all nonzero modulo
p
, then part (i) implies that
the determinant

\displaystyle\det A\big(e_{2}+\dots+e_{s+1},\Delta_{1},\Delta_{s+1},\Lambda_{1}(x)\Lambda_{2}(x)^{p^{e_{2}}}\cdots\Lambda_{s}(x)^{p^{e_{2}+\dots+e_{s}}}\big)\equiv

\displaystyle\equiv\prod_{j=1}^{s}\det\sigma^{e_{2}+\dots+e_{j}}\big(A\big(e_{j+1},\Delta_{j},\Delta_{j+1},\Lambda_{j}(t,z)\big)\big)\pmod{p},

is a Laurent polynomial nonzero modulo
p
.
This proves the first statement of part (ii) of the theorem and allows us to consider the inverse matrices
in the congruence of part (ii).

We prove part (ii) by induction on
s
. For
s=1
, congruence (
2.9
)
takes the form

(2.10)

Remark
.

The results of Section
2
in the case

{\bf e}=(e_{1},\dots,e_{l})=(1,\dots,1)
and

3.
Convergence

3.1.
Unramified extensions of
{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}

We fix an algebraic closure
\overline{{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}}
of
{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}
.
For every
m
, there is a unique unramified extension of
{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}
in

\overline{{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}}
of degree
m
, denoted by
{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}^{(m)}
.
This can be obtained by attaching to
{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}
a primitive root of
1
of order
p^{m}-1
.
The norm
|\cdot|_{p}
on
{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}
extends to a norm
|\cdot|_{p}
on

{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}^{(m)}
.
Let

\displaystyle{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}^{(m)}=\{a\in{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}^{(m)}\mid|a|_{p}\leqslant 1\}

denote the ring of integers in
{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}^{(m)}
. The ring
{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}^{(m)}

has the unique maximal ideal

\displaystyle\mathbb{M}_{p}^{(m)}=\{a\in{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}^{(m)}\mid|a|_{p}<1\},

such that
\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{(m)}\big/\mathbb{M}_{p}^{(m)}
is isomorphic to the finite field

{\mathbb{F}}_{p^{m}}
.

For every
u\in{\mathbb{F}}_{p^{m}}
there is a unique
\tilde{u}\in\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{(m)}
that is a lift of
u
and such that

\tilde{u}^{p^{m}}=\tilde{u}
. The element
\tilde{u}
is called the Teichmuller lift of
u
.

3.2.
Domain
\mathfrak{D}_{B}

For
u\in{\mathbb{F}}_{p^{m}}
and
r>0
denote

\displaystyle D_{u,r}=\{a\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}^{(m)}\mid|a-\tilde{u}|_{p}<r\}\,.

We have the partition

\displaystyle{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}^{(m)}=\bigcup_{u\in{\mathbb{F}}_{p^{m}}}D_{u,1}\,.

Recall
z=(z_{1},\dots,z_{n})
. For
B(z)\in{\mathbb{Z}}[z]
, define

\displaystyle\mathfrak{D}_{B}\ =\ \{a\in({\mathbb{Z}}_{p}^{(m)})^{n}\,\mid\ |B(a)|_{p}=1\}.

Let
\bar{B}(z)
be the projection of
B(z)
to
{\mathbb{F}}_{p}[z]\subset{\mathbb{F}}_{p^{m}}[z]
.
Then
\mathfrak{D}_{B}
is the union of unit polydiscs,

\displaystyle\mathfrak{D}_{B}=\bigcup_{\begin{subarray}{c}u_{1},\dots,u_{n}\in{\mathbb{F}}_{p^{m}}\\
\bar{B}(u_{1},\dots,u_{n})\neq 0\end{subarray}}\ D_{u_{1},1}\times\dots\times D_{u_{n},1}\,.

For any
k
we have

\displaystyle\{a\in({\mathbb{Z}}_{p}^{(m)})^{n}\mid\ |B(a^{p^{k}})|_{p}=1\}

4.
KZ
equations and complex solutions

4.1.
KZ
equations

Let
{\mathfrak{g}}
be a simple Lie algebra with an invariant scalar product.
The Casimir element is
\Omega={\sum}_{i}\,h_{i}\otimes h_{i}\in{\mathfrak{g}}\otimes{\mathfrak{g}}
,
where
(h_{i})\subset{\mathfrak{g}}
is an orthonormal basis.
Let
V=\otimes_{i=1}^{n}V_{i}
be
a tensor product of
{\mathfrak{g}}
-modules,
\kappa\in{\mathbb{C}}^{\times}
a nonzero number.
The
KZ
equations
is the system of differential
equations on a
V
-valued function
I(z_{1},\dots,z_{n})
,

\displaystyle\frac{\partial I}{\partial z_{i}}\ =\ \frac{1}{\kappa}\,{\sum}_{j\neq i}\,\frac{\Omega_{i,j}}{z_{i}-z_{j}}I,\qquad i=1,\dots,n,

where
\Omega_{i,j}:V\to V
is the Casimir operator acting in the
i
th and
j
th tensor factors,
see
[
KZ
,
EFK
]
.

This system is a system of Fuchsian first order
linear differential equations.
The equations are defined on the complement in
{\mathbb{C}}^{n}
to the union of all diagonal hyperplanes.

The object of our discussion is the following particular case. Let
n,q
be positive integers.
We consider the following system of differential and algebraic equations
for a column
n
-vector
I=(I_{1},\dots,I_{n})
depending on variables
z=(z_{1},\dots,z_{n})
:

(4.1)

\displaystyle\phantom{aaa}\frac{\partial I}{\partial z_{i}}\ =\ {\frac{1}{q}}\sum_{j\neq i}\frac{\Omega_{ij}}{z_{i}-z_{j}}I,\quad i=1,\dots,n,\qquad I_{1}+\dots+I_{n}=0,

where
z=(z_{1},\dots,z_{n})
;
the
n\times n
-matrices
\Omega_{ij}
have the form

\displaystyle\Omega_{ij}\ =\ \begin{pmatrix}&\vdots^{\kern-3.41432pti}&&\vdots^{\kern-3.41432ptj}&\\
{\scriptstyle i}\cdots&{-1}&\cdots&1&\cdots\\
&\vdots&&\vdots&\\
{\scriptstyle j}\cdots&1&\cdots&-1&\cdots\\
&\vdots&&\vdots&\end{pmatrix},

and all other entries are zero.
This joint system of
differential and
algebraic equations
will be called the
system of
KZ
equations
in this paper.

For
i=1,\dots,n
denote

(4.2)

\displaystyle H_{i}(z)={\frac{1}{q}}\sum_{j\neq i}\frac{\Omega_{ij}}{z_{i}-z_{j}}\,,\qquad\nabla_{i}^{\operatorname{KZ}}=\frac{\partial}{\partial z_{i}}-H_{i}(z),\qquad i=1,\dots,n.

The linear operators
H_{i}(z)
are called the Gaudin Hamiltonians.
The
KZ
equations can be written as the system of equations,

\displaystyle\nabla_{i}^{\operatorname{KZ}}I=0,\quad i=1,\dots,n,\qquad I_{1}+\dots+I_{n}=0.

System (
4.1
) is the system of the differential
KZ
equations with
parameter
\kappa=q
associated with the Lie algebra
{\mathfrak{sl}}_{2}
and the subspace of singular vectors of weight

n-2
of the tensor power

({\mathbb{C}}^{2})^{\otimes{n}}
of two-dimensional irreducible
{\mathfrak{sl}}_{2}
-modules, up to a gauge transformation, see
this example in
[
V2
, Section 1.1]
, see also
[
V3
]
.

4.2.
Solutions over
{\mathbb{C}}

Define the
master function

\displaystyle\Phi(t,z)=(t-z_{1})^{-1/q}\dots(t-z_{n})^{-1/q}

and the column
n
-vector

(4.3)

\displaystyle I^{(C)}(z)=(I_{1},\dots,I_{n}):=\int_{C}\Big(\frac{\Phi(t,z)}{t-z_{1}},\dots,\frac{\Phi(t,z)}{t-z_{n}}\Big)dt\,,

where
C\subset{\mathbb{C}}-\{z_{1},\dots,z_{n}\}

is a contour on which the integrand takes its initial value when
t
encircles
C
.

Theorem 4.1
.

The function
I^{(C)}(z)
is a solution of system (
4.1
).

This theorem is a very particular case of the results in
[
SV1
]
.

Proof.

The theorem follows from Stokes’ theorem and the two identities:

(4.4)

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{q}\,\Big(\frac{\Phi(t,z)}{t-z_{1}}+\dots+\frac{\Phi(t,z)}{t-z_{n}}\Big)\,=\,\frac{\partial\Phi}{\partial t}(t,z)\,,

(4.5)

\displaystyle\Big(\frac{\partial}{\partial z_{i}}-\frac{1}{q}\sum_{j\neq i}\frac{\Omega_{i,j}}{z_{i}-z_{j}}\Big)\Big(\frac{\Phi(t,z)}{t-z_{1}},\dots,\frac{\Phi(t,z)}{t-z_{n}}\Big)\,=\frac{\partial\Psi^{i}}{\partial t}(t,z),

where
\Psi^{i}(t,z)
is the column
n
-vector
(0,\dots,0,-\frac{\Phi(t,z)}{t-z_{i}},0,\dots,0)
with
the nonzero element at the
i
-th place.
∎

Theorem 4.2

(cf.
[
V1
, Formula (1.3)]
)
.

All solutions of system (
4.1
) have this form.
Namely, the complex vector space of solutions of the form (
4.3
) is
(n-1)
-dimensional.

4.3.
Solutions as vectors of first derivatives

Consider the integral

\displaystyle T(z)=T^{(C)}(z)=\int_{C}\Phi(t,z)\,dt.

Then

\displaystyle I^{(C)}(z)=\,q\,\Big(\frac{\partial T^{(C)}}{\partial z_{1}},\dots,\frac{\partial T^{(C)}}{\partial z_{n}}\Big).

Denote

Then the column gradient vector

satisfies the following system of

\displaystyle\nabla_{i}^{\operatorname{KZ}}\nabla T=0,\quad i=1,\dots,n,\qquad\frac{\partial T}{\partial z_{1}}+\dots+\frac{\partial T}{\partial z_{n}}=0.

This is a system of second order linear differential equations on the function

.

5.3.

Consider the lexicographical ordering of monomials

For a nonzero Laurent polynomial

and

equals the product of the leading terms of

and

6.1.
Congruences for Hasse–Witt matrices of
KZ
equations

Let
r=1
,
n=gq+1
,
{\bf e}=(e,e,\dots)
, where
e
is defined in Section
5.1
. Let

(6.1)

\displaystyle\Gamma=\{1,\dots,g\}\subset{\mathbb{Z}},\qquad\Delta=(\Gamma,\Gamma,\dots),

\displaystyle N=[0,gp^{e}+(p^{e}-1)/q-g]\subset{\mathbb{R}}.

The infinite tuple
(N,N,\dots)
of intervals is
(\Delta,{\bf e})
-admissible, see Definition
2.3
.

Recall the polynomial

\displaystyle\Phi_{1}(t,z)=\big((t-z_{1})\dots(t-z_{n})\big)^{(p^{e}-1)/q}.

The Newton polytope of
\Phi_{1}(t,z)
with respect to variable
t
is the interval

N=[0,gp+(p-1)/q-g]
. We also have

\displaystyle\Phi_{s}(t,z)=\Phi_{1}(t,z)\cdot\Phi_{1}(t,z)^{p^{e}}\dots\Phi_{1}(t,z)^{p^{e(s-1)}}\,.

The infinite tuple
(\Phi_{1}(t,z),\Phi_{1}(t,z),\dots)
is
(\Delta,{\bf e})
-admissible, see Definition
2.4
.

For
s\geqslant 1
consider the Hasse–Witt
g\times g
matrix

\displaystyle A(\Phi_{s}(t,z)):=A(es,\Gamma,\Gamma,\Phi_{s}(t,z))=\big(\operatorname{Coeff}_{p^{es}v-u}(\Phi_{s}(t,z))\big)_{u,v=1,\dots,g}\,,

see (
2.6
). The entries of this matrix are polynomials in
z
.

Theorem 6.1
.

The determinant
\det A(\Phi_{1}(t,z))
is a homogeneous polynomial in
z
of degree

(6.2)

\displaystyle d_{\Phi}=\frac{p^{e}-1}{q}\cdot\frac{qg^{2}+2g-qg}{2}\,,

and the determinant is nonzero modulo
p
.

Proof.

Denote
A(\Phi_{1}(t,z))=:(A_{u,v}(z))_{u,v=1,\dots,g}
.

Lemma 6.2
.

The leading term of
A_{u,v}(z)
equals

\displaystyle\pm\binom{(p^{e}-1)/q}{v-u}(z_{1}z_{2}\dots z_{qg+1-qv})^{(p^{e}-1)/q}/z_{qg+1-qv}^{v-u}\,,\qquad\text{if}\;\;v\geqslant u,

\displaystyle\pm\binom{(p^{e}-1)/q}{u-v}(z_{1}z_{2}\dots z_{qg+1-qv})^{(p^{e}-1)/q}z_{qg+2-qv}^{u-v}\,,\qquad\quad\ \text{if}\;\;v\leqslant u.

For example, for
g=2
the matrix of leading terms is

(6.3)

\displaystyle\begin{pmatrix}\pm(z_{1}\dots z_{g+1})^{(p^{e}-1)/q}&\pm\binom{(p^{e}-1)/q}{1}z_{1}^{(p^{e}-1)/q}/z_{1}\\
\pm\binom{(p^{e}-1)/q}{1}(z_{1}\dots z_{q+1})^{(p^{e}-1)/q}z_{q+2}&\pm z_{1}^{(p^{e}-1)/q}\end{pmatrix}.

Proof.

The proof is by inspection.
∎

The fact that
\det A(\Phi_{1}(t,z))
is a homogeneous polynomial easily follows from
the definition of
A(\Phi_{1}(t,z))
. It is also easy to see that the leading term of the
determinant of the matrix of leading terms
of
A_{u,v}(z)
equals the product of diagonal elements,

(6.4)

\displaystyle\pm\,\prod_{v=1}^{g}(z_{1}\dots z_{qg+1-qv})^{(p^{e}-1)/q}.

This expression is not congruent to zero modulo
p
.
Counting the degree of the monomial in (
6.4
) we
obtain (
6.2
).
This proves Theorem
6.1
.
∎

Corollary 6.3
.

The infinite nondegenerate
(\Delta,{\bf e})
-admissible
tuple
(\Phi_{1}(t,z),\Phi_{1}(t,z),\dots)
satisfies the assumptions of Theorem
2.6
.
Therefore,

(i)

for
s\geqslant 1
we have

(6.5)

\displaystyle A(\Phi_{s}(t,z))\equiv A(\Phi_{1}(t,z))\cdot\sigma^{e}(A(\Phi_{1}(t,z)))\cdots\sigma^{e(s-1)}(A\big(\Phi_{1}(t,z)))\pmod{p}\,;

(ii)

for
s\geqslant 1

the determinant of the matrix
A(\Phi_{s}(t,z))

is a polynomial, which is nonzero modulo
p
, and
we have modulo
p^{s}

:

\displaystyle A(\Phi_{s+1}(t,z))\cdot\sigma^{e}(A(\Phi_{s}(t,z)))^{-1}\equiv A(\Phi_{s}(t,z))\cdot\sigma^{e}(A(\Phi_{s-1}(t,z)))^{-1},

where for
s=1
we understand the second factor on the right-hand side
as the
g\times g
identity matrix.

Proof.

The corollary follows from Theorems
6.1
and
2.6
.
∎

6.2.
Congruences for frames of solutions of
KZ
equations

Theorem 6.4
.

We have the following congruences of
n\times g
matrices.

(i)

For
s\geqslant 1
,

\displaystyle I_{s+1}(z)\cdot A(\Phi_{s+1}(t,z))^{-1}\equiv I_{s}(z)\cdot A(\Phi_{s}(t,z))^{-1}\pmod{p^{s}}\,.

(ii)

For
s\geqslant 1
and
j=1,\dots,n
,

\displaystyle\frac{\partial I_{s+1}}{\partial z_{j}}(z)\cdot A(\Phi_{s+1}(t,z))^{-1}\equiv\frac{\partial I_{s}}{\partial z_{j}}(z)\cdot A(\Phi_{s}(t,z))^{-1}\pmod{p^{s}}\,.

Proof.

Consider the first row of the Hasse–Witt matrix
A(\Phi_{s}(t,z))
,

\displaystyle\big(A_{1,1}(\Phi_{s}(t,z)),\dots,A_{1,g}(\Phi_{s}(t,z))\big),\quad A_{1,\ell}(\Phi_{s}(t,z))=\operatorname{Coeff}_{\ell p^{s}-1}(\Phi_{s}(t,z)).

For each
A_{1,\ell}(\Phi_{s}(t,z))
we view the gradient

\displaystyle\nabla A_{1,\ell}(\Phi_{s}(t,z))=\Big(\frac{\partial A_{1,\ell}(s)}{\partial z_{1}},\dots,\frac{\partial A_{1,\ell}(s)}{\partial z_{n}}\Big)

as a column
n
-vector. The resulting
n\times g
matrix of gradients

\displaystyle\nabla A(s,z):=(\nabla A_{1,1}(\Phi_{s}(t,z)),\dots,\nabla A_{1,g}(\Phi_{s}(t,z)))

is proportion to the matrix
I_{s}(z)
,
\nabla A(s,z)=\frac{1-p^{es}}{q}I_{s}(z)
.
By Theorems
2.8
and
2.9
we have
modulo
p^{s}
,

\displaystyle\nabla A(s+1,z)\cdot A(\Phi_{s+1}(t,z))^{-1}\equiv\nabla A(s,z)\cdot A(\Phi_{s}(t,z))^{-1},

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial z_{j}}\big(\nabla A(s+1,z)\big)\cdot A(\Phi_{s+1}(t,z))^{-1}\equiv\frac{\partial}{\partial z_{j}}\big(\nabla A(s,z)\big)\cdot A(\Phi_{s}(t,z))^{-1}.

These congruences imply the theorem.
∎

Corollary 6.5
.

For
s\geqslant 1
we have

\displaystyle I_{s}(z)\cdot A(\Phi_{s}(t,z))^{-1}\equiv I_{1}(z)\cdot A(\Phi_{1}(t,z))^{-1}\pmod{p}.

6.3.
Domain of convergence

By Theorem
6.1
the polynomial
\det A(\Phi_{1}(t,z))\in{\mathbb{Z}}[z]
is of degree
d_{\Phi}
and
this polynomial is nonzero modulo
p
. For a positive integer
m
define

\displaystyle\mathfrak{D}^{(m)}_{\operatorname{KZ}}=\{a\in({\mathbb{Z}}_{p}^{(m)})^{n}\mid\ |\det A(\Phi_{1}(t,a))|_{p}=1\}\,.

By Lemma
3.1
the domain

\mathfrak{D}^{(m)}_{\operatorname{KZ}}
is nonempty if
p^{m}>d_{\Phi}
.
In what follows we assume that
p^{m}>d_{\Phi}
.

We have

|\det A(\Phi_{s}(t,a))\big|_{p}=1
for
a\in\mathfrak{D}^{(m)}_{\operatorname{KZ}}
.
All entries of
A(\Phi_{s}(t,z))^{-1}
are
rational functions in
z
regular on
\mathfrak{D}^{(m)}_{\operatorname{KZ}}
.
For every
a\in\mathfrak{D}^{(m)}_{\operatorname{KZ}}
all entries of
A(\Phi_{s}(t,a))
and
A(\Phi_{s}(t,a))^{-1}

are elements of
{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}^{(m)}
.

Theorem 6.6
.

The sequence of
g\times g
matrices

\displaystyle\big(A\big(\Phi_{s}(t,z)\big)\cdot\sigma^{e}\big(A\big(\Phi_{s-1}(t,z)\big)\big)^{-1}\big)_{s\geqslant 1}\,,

whose entries are rational functions in
z
regular on
\mathfrak{D}^{(m)}_{\operatorname{KZ}}
,
uniformly converges on
\mathfrak{D}^{(m)}_{\operatorname{KZ}}
as
s\to\infty
to an analytic

g\times g
matrix which will be denoted by
\mathcal{A}(z)
.
For
a\in\mathfrak{D}^{(m)}_{\operatorname{KZ}}
we have

\displaystyle\big|\det\mathcal{A}(a)\big|_{p}=1\,

and the matrix
\mathcal{A}(a)
is invertible.

Proof.

The theorem follows from Theorem
3.6
.
∎

Theorem 6.7
.

For
i=1,\dots,n
the sequence of
g\times g
matrices

\displaystyle\Big(\Big(\frac{\partial}{\partial z_{i}}A\big(\Phi_{s}(t,z)\big)\Big)\cdot A\big(\Phi_{s}(t,z)\big)^{-1}\Big)_{s\geqslant 1}\,,

whose entries are rational functions in
z
regular on
\mathfrak{D}^{(m)}_{\operatorname{KZ}}
,
uniformly converges on
\mathfrak{D}^{(m)}_{\operatorname{KZ}}
as

to an analytic

g\times g
matrix, which will be denoted by

.

The sequence of
n\times g
matrices

\displaystyle\big(I_{s}(z)\cdot A\big(\Phi_{s}(t,z)\big)^{-1}\big)_{s\geqslant 1}\,,

whose entries are rational functions in
z
regular on
\mathfrak{D}^{(m)}_{\operatorname{KZ}}
,
uniformly converges on
\mathfrak{D}^{(m)}_{\operatorname{KZ}}
as

to an analytic

n\times g
matrix which will be denoted by
\mathcal{I}(z)
.

For

the sequence of

matrices

\displaystyle\Big(\frac{\partial I_{s}}{\partial z_{i}}(z)\cdot A\big(s,\Phi_{s}(t,z)\big)^{-1}\Big)_{s\geqslant 1}\,,

whose entries are rational functions in

.

We have

\displaystyle\frac{\partial\mathcal{I}}{\partial z_{i}}=\mathcal{I}^{(i)}-\mathcal{I}\cdot\mathcal{A}^{(i)}\,.

Proof.

The theorem follows from Theorems

,
3.8
, and Lemma

6.4.
Vector bundle
\mathcal{L}\,\to\,\mathfrak{D}^{(m),o}_{\operatorname{KZ}}

Denote

\displaystyle W=\{(I_{1},\dots,I_{n})\in({\mathbb{Q}}_{p}^{(m)})^{n}\ |\ I_{1}+\dots+I_{n}=0\}.

We consider vectors

(I_{1},\dots,I_{n})
as column vectors.
The differential operators
\nabla^{\operatorname{KZ}}_{i}
,
i=1,\dots,n
, define a connection on
the trivial bundle
W\times\mathfrak{D}^{(m)}_{\operatorname{KZ}}\to\mathfrak{D}^{(m)}_{\operatorname{KZ}}
,
called the
KZ
connection. The connection has singularities at the diagonal hyperplanes in
({\mathbb{Z}}_{p}^{(m)})^{n}

and is well-defined over

\displaystyle\mathfrak{D}^{(m),o}_{\operatorname{KZ}}=\{a=(a_{1},\dots,a_{n})\in({\mathbb{Z}}_{p}^{(m)})^{n}\mid|\det A(\Phi_{1}(t,a))|_{p}=1,\,a_{i}\neq a_{j}\,\ \forall i,j\}.

The
KZ
connection is flat,

\displaystyle\big[\nabla^{\operatorname{KZ}}_{i},\nabla^{\operatorname{KZ}}_{j}\big]=0\qquad\forall\,i,j,

see
[
EFK
]
.
The flat sections of the
KZ
connection are solutions of system (
4.1
) of
KZ
equations.

For any
a\in\mathfrak{D}^{(m)}_{\operatorname{KZ}}
let
\mathcal{L}_{a}\subset W
be the vector subspace generated by columns of the

n\times g
matrix
\mathcal{I}(a)
. Then

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}:=\bigcup\nolimits_{a\in\mathfrak{D}^{(m)}_{\operatorname{KZ}}}\,\mathcal{L}_{a}\,\to\,\mathfrak{D}^{(m)}_{\operatorname{KZ}}

is an analytic distribution of vector subspaces in the fibers of the trivial bundle

W\times\mathfrak{D}^{(m)}_{\operatorname{KZ}}\to\mathfrak{D}^{(m)}_{\operatorname{KZ}}
.

Theorem 6.10

(
[
VZ2
, Theorem 6.7]
)
.

The distribution
\mathcal{L}\,\to\,\mathfrak{D}^{(m)}_{\operatorname{KZ}}
is invariant with respect to the
KZ
connection.
In other words, if
s(z)
is a local section of
\mathcal{L}\,\to\,\mathfrak{D}^{(m)}_{\operatorname{KZ}}
, then the
sections
\nabla_{i}^{\operatorname{KZ}}s(z)
,
i=1,\dots,n
, also are sections of
\mathcal{L}\,\to\,\mathfrak{D}^{(m)}_{\operatorname{KZ}}
.

Proof.

Let
\mathcal{I}(z)=(\mathcal{I}_{1}(z),\dots,\mathcal{I}_{g}(z))
be columns of the
n\times g
matrix

\mathcal{I}(z)
.
Let
a\in\mathfrak{D}^{(m)}_{\operatorname{KZ}}
. Let
c(z)=(c_{1}(z),\dots,c_{g}(z))
be a column vector of analytic functions at
a
.
Consider a local section of the distribution
\mathcal{L}\,\to\,\mathfrak{D}^{(m)}_{\operatorname{KZ}}
,

s(z)\,=\,\sum_{j=1}^{g}\,c_{j}(z)\mathcal{I}_{j}(z)\,=:\,\mathcal{I}\cdot c
.
Then

\displaystyle\nabla^{\operatorname{KZ}}_{i}s(z)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-H_{i}\cdot\mathcal{I}\cdot c+\frac{\partial\mathcal{I}}{\partial z_{i}}\cdot c+\mathcal{I}\cdot\frac{\partial c}{\partial z_{i}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-H_{i}\cdot\mathcal{I}\cdot c+(\mathcal{I}^{(i)}-\mathcal{I}\cdot\mathcal{A}^{(i)})\cdot c+\mathcal{I}\cdot\frac{\partial c}{\partial z_{i}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-H_{i}\cdot\mathcal{I}\cdot c+(H_{i}\cdot\mathcal{I}-\mathcal{I}\cdot\mathcal{A}^{(i)})\cdot c+\mathcal{I}\cdot\frac{\partial c}{\partial z_{i}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\mathcal{I}\cdot\mathcal{A}^{(i)}\cdot c+\mathcal{I}\cdot\frac{\partial c}{\partial z_{i}}\,.

Clearly, the last expression is a local section of
\mathcal{L}\,\to\,\mathfrak{D}^{(m)}_{\operatorname{KZ}}
.
∎

Theorem 6.11
.

The function
a\mapsto\dim_{{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}^{(m)}}\mathcal{L}_{a}
is constant on

\mathfrak{D}^{(m),o}_{\operatorname{KZ}}
, in other words,

\mathcal{L}\,\to\,\mathfrak{D}^{(m)}_{\operatorname{KZ}}
is a vector bundle over
\mathfrak{D}^{(m),o}_{\operatorname{KZ}}\subset\mathfrak{D}^{(m)}_{\operatorname{KZ}}
.

The proof coincides with the proof of Theorem 6.8 in
[
VZ2
]
.

Recall that
d_{\Phi}
is the degree of the polynomial
\det A(\Phi_{1}(t,z))
and

d_{M}
is the degree of the minor defined in Lemma
5.3
.

Theorem 6.12
.

If
p^{m}>d_{\Phi}+d_{M}
,
then the analytic vector bundle
\mathcal{L}\to\mathfrak{D}^{(m),o}_{\operatorname{KZ}}
is of rank
g
.

Proof.

If
p^{m}>d_{\Phi}+d_{M}
, then the minor
M(z)
defines a function on

\mathfrak{D}^{(m),o}_{\operatorname{KZ}}
nonzero modulo
p
by Lemma
3.1
.
Then by Corollary
6.9
,
the
n\times g
matrix valued function

\mathcal{I}(z)
has a
g\times g
minor nonzero on
\mathfrak{D}^{(m)}_{\operatorname{KZ}}
.
This proves the theorem.
∎

6.5.
Remarks

6.5.1.

One may expect that the subbundle
\mathcal{L}\to\mathfrak{D}^{(m),o}_{\operatorname{KZ}}

can be extended to a rank
g
subbundle over

\mathfrak{D}^{(m)}_{\operatorname{KZ}}-\mathfrak{D}^{(m),o}_{\operatorname{KZ}}
, the union
of the diagonal hyperplanes in

\mathfrak{D}^{(m)}_{\operatorname{KZ}}
.

6.5.2.

Following Dwork we may expect that locally at any point
a\in\mathfrak{\mathfrak{}}D^{(m),o}_{\operatorname{KZ}}
, the
solutions of the KZ equations with values in
\mathcal{L}\to\mathfrak{D}^{(m),o}_{\operatorname{KZ}}

are given at
a
by power series in

z_{i}-a_{i}
,
i=1,\dots,n
, bounded in their polydiscs of convergence,
while any other local solution at
a
is given by a power series unbounded in its polydisc of convergence,
cf.
[
Dw
]
and
[
V5
, Theorem A.4]
.

6.5.3.

The KZ connection
\nabla^{\operatorname{KZ}}_{i}
,
i=1,\dots,n
, over
{\mathbb{C}}
has no nontrivial proper invariant subbundles
due to the irreducibility of its monodromy representation, see
[
Fo
, Lemma 6]
.
Thus the existence of the invariant subbundle
\mathcal{L}\to\mathfrak{D}^{(m),o}_{\operatorname{KZ}}

is a
p
-adic feature.

6.5.4.

The invariant subbundles of the KZ connection over
{\mathbb{C}}
usually are related to some additional conformal block
constructions, for example see
[
FSV
,
SV2
,
V3
,
V4
]
. Apparently our subbundle
\mathcal{L}\to\mathfrak{D}^{(m),o}_{\operatorname{KZ}}

is of a
different
p
-adic nature.

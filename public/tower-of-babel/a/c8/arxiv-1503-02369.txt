Parabolic Littlewood-Paley inequality for a class of time-dependent operators of arbitrary order, and applications to higher order stochastic PDE Thanks: The research of the second author was supported by Basic Science Research Program through the National Research Foundation of Korea(NRF) funded by the Ministry of Education, Science and Technology (20110015961)
Ildoo Kim, Kyeong-Hun Kim, Sungbin Lim

Abstract
. In this paper we prove a parabolic version of the Littlewood-Paley inequality for a class of time-dependent local and non-local operators of arbitrary order, and as an application we show this inequality gives a fundamental estimate for the L p L_{p} -theory of the stochastic partial differential equations.

1.
Introduction

The classical
Littlewood-Paley inequality says (see
[
12
]
) that for any
p\in(1,\infty)
and
f\in L_{p}(\mathbf{R}^{d})
,

\int_{\mathbf{R}^{d}}\left(\int^{\infty}_{0}|\sqrt{-\Delta}\,e^{t\Delta}f|^{2}dt\right)^{p/2}dx\leq N(p)\|f\|^{p}_{p},

(1.1)

where

e^{t\Delta}f(x):=S_{t}f=p(t,\cdot)*f(\cdot)=\frac{1}{(4\pi t)^{d/2}}\int_{\mathbf{R}^{d}}f(x-y)e^{\frac{-|y|^{2}}{4t}}dy
.
In
[
5
,
7
]
Krylov proved the following parabolic version,
in which
H
is a Hilbert space : for any
p\in[2,\infty),-\infty\leq a<b\leq\infty
,
f\in L_{p}((a,b)\times\mathbf{R}^{d},H)
,

\|(\int^{t}_{a}|(\sqrt{-\Delta}e^{(t-s)\Delta}f)(s,x)|^{2}_{H}\,ds)^{1/2}\|^{p}_{L_{p}((a,b)\times\mathbf{R}^{d})}\leq N(p)\||f|_{H}\|^{p}_{L_{p}((a,b)\times\mathbf{R}^{d})}.

(1.2)

Some related works and the significance of the parabolic Littlewood-Paley inequality in the
L_{p}
-theory of stochastic PDEs will be discussed later.

If
f=f(x)
and
H=\mathbf{R}
then by (
1.2
) with
a=0
and
b=2
,

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbf{R}^{d}}[\int^{1}_{0}|\sqrt{-\Delta}e^{s\Delta}f|^{2}ds]^{p/2}dx

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\mathbf{R}^{d}}\int^{2}_{1}[\int^{t}_{0}|\sqrt{-\Delta}e^{(t-s)\Delta}f|^{2}ds]^{p/2}dtdx\leq 2N(p)\|f\|^{p}_{L_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}.

This and the scaling
(\sqrt{-\Delta}S_{t}f(c\cdot))(x)=\sqrt{-\Delta}(cS_{c^{2}t}f)(cx)
yield (
1.1
).
Hence (
1.2
) is a generalization of (
1.1
).
Note that by putting
K_{0}(t,x)=\sqrt{-\Delta}p(t,x)
, we get
\sqrt{-\Delta}e^{(t-s)\Delta}f=K_{0}(t-s,\cdot)*f(s,\cdot)
and therefore (
1.2
) becomes

\|(\int^{t}_{a}|K_{0}(t-s,\cdot)*f(s,\cdot)(x)|^{2}_{H}ds)^{1/2}\|^{p}_{L_{p}((a,b)\times\mathbf{R}^{d})}\leq N\||f|_{H}\|^{p}_{L_{p}((a,b)\times\mathbf{R}^{d})}.

(1.3)

In this article we extend (
1.3
) to a class of time-dependent operators. For a wide class of differential operators
A(t)
with symbol
\psi(t,\xi)
, one can define the kernel

p(t,s,x)=p_{A}(t,s,x)=\mathcal{F}^{-1}(\exp(\int_{s}^{t}\psi(r,\xi))dr))(x)

so that the solution of

u_{t}=A(t)u+f,\quad u(0)=0

is given by

u=\int^{t}_{0}p(t,s,\cdot)*f(s,\cdot)ds.

We provide a classification of operators
A(t)
for which (
1.3
)
holds with formally

K_{A}(t,s,x)=\sqrt{-A(t)}p(t,s,x).

More generally, we provide sufficient conditions on measurable functions
K(t,s,x)
on
\mathbf{R}^{d+2}
so that

\|(\int^{t}_{a}|K(t,s,\cdot)*f(s,\cdot)(x)|^{2}_{H}ds)^{1/2}\|^{p}_{L_{p}((a,b)\times\mathbf{R}^{d})}\leq N\||f|_{H}\|^{p}_{L_{p}((a,b)\times\mathbf{R}^{d})}

(1.4)

holds for any
f\in C^{\infty}_{0}(\mathbf{R}^{d+1},H)
with constant
N
independent of
f,a
and
b
.
The functions
K(t,s,x)
are assumed to satisfy the conditions
described in Assumptions
2.1
and
2.2
.

For concrete examples we introduce the operators

A_{1}(t)
of
2m
-order (
m=1,2,3,\cdots
) and
A_{2}(t)
of order

\gamma\in(0,\infty)

A_{1}(t)u:=(-1)^{m-1}\sum_{|\alpha|=|\beta|=m}a^{\alpha\beta}(t)D^{\alpha+\beta}u,\quad\quad A_{2}(t)u:=-a(t)(-\Delta)^{\gamma/2}

(1.5)

where
-a(t)(-\Delta)^{\gamma/2}
is the operator with symbol
-a(t)|\xi|^{\gamma}
and the coefficients
a(t)
and
a^{\alpha\beta}(t)
are
bounded measurable in
t
and satisfy the ellipticity conditions

0<\nu<\Re[a(t)]<\nu^{-1},

and

\displaystyle\nu|\xi|^{2m}\leq\sum_{|\alpha|=|\beta|=m}\xi^{\alpha}\xi^{\beta}\Re\left[a^{\alpha\beta}(t)\right]\leq\nu^{-1}|\xi|^{2m}.

Here
\Re[z]
is the real part of
z
. Let
p_{1}(t,s,x)
and
p_{2}(t,s,x)
be the kernels related to
A_{1}(t)
and
A_{2}(t)
respectively. We prove that (
1.4
) holds with

K_{1}(t,s,x):=D^{m}p_{1}(t,s,x),\quad K_{2}(t,s,x):=(-\Delta)^{\gamma/4}p_{2}(t,s,x).

Letting the function
f

depend only on
x
, one can obtain elliptic versions of these
results. For instance, we have for any
\gamma\in(0,\infty)
and

f\in L_{p}(\mathbf{R}^{d})
,

\int_{\mathbf{R}^{d}}\left(\int^{\infty}_{0}|(-\Delta)^{\gamma/2}e^{-t(-\Delta)^{\gamma}}f|^{2}dt\right)^{p/2}dx\leq N(p,\gamma)\|f\|^{p}_{p},\quad\quad\forall\,\gamma\in(0,\infty),

which is an extension of (
1.1
), the classical (elliptic) Littlewood-Paley inequality.

Among many other examples of (
1.4
) are the product
A_{1}(t)A_{2}(t)
and
(-\Delta)^{k}\mathcal{L}_{0}(t)
(
k=0,1,2,\cdots)
, where

\mathcal{L}_{0}(t)u=\int_{\mathbf{R}^{d}}\Big(u(x+y)-u(x)-\chi(y)(\nabla u(x),y)\Big)m(t,y)\frac{dy}{|y|^{d+\gamma}},

(1.6)

\gamma\in(0,2)
,
\chi(y)=I_{\gamma>1}+I_{\gamma=1}I_{|y|\leq 1}
, and
m(t,y)\geq 0
satisfies a certain condition described in Corollary
2.8
. Note that if
m(t,y)\equiv 1
then
\mathcal{L}_{0}=-(-\Delta)^{\gamma/2}
.

One of important applications of the parabolic Littlewood-Paley
inequality is the theory of stochastic partial differential
equations of the type

du=A_{i}(\omega,t)u\,\,dt+\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}f^{k}\,\,dw^{k}_{t},\quad u(0,x)=0.

(1.7)

Here

f=(f^{1},f^{2},\cdots)
is an

\ell_{2}
-valued random function depending on
(t,x)
, and
w^{k}_{t}

are independent one-dimensional Wiener processes defined on a
probability space
(\Omega,P)
. The operators
A_{i}=A_{i}(\omega,t)

are defined in (
1.5
), but this
time we allow the coefficients
a(\omega,t)
and
a^{\alpha\beta}(\omega,t)
to depend
also on
\omega\in\Omega
. It turns out that if

f=(f^{1},f^{2},\cdots)\in L_{p}(\Omega\times(0,\infty)\times\mathbf{R}^{d},\ell_{2})

satisfies a certain measurability condition, the solutions of these
problems are given by

u_{i}(t,x)=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\int^{t}_{0}p_{i}(t,s,\cdot)*f^{k}(s,\cdot)(x)dw^{k}_{s},\quad i=1,2

(1.8)

where
p_{i}(t,s,x)
are introduced above, but
they are random due to the randomness of the coefficients.
The derivation of formula (
1.8
) can be found in

[
8
]
when
A_{i}=\Delta
, and by repeating the arguments in

[
8
]
one can derive (
1.8
) for such
A_{i}
. By
Burkholder-Davis-Gundy inequality (see
[
6
]
), we have

\displaystyle\|D^{m}u_{1}

\displaystyle(t,\cdot)\|^{p}_{L_{p}(\Omega\times(0,\infty)\times\mathbf{R}^{d})}

\displaystyle\leq N(p)\|[\int^{t}_{0}|D^{m}p_{1}(t,s,\cdot)*f(s,\cdot)(x)|^{2}_{\ell_{2}}ds]^{1/2}\|^{p}_{L_{p}(\Omega\times(0,\infty)\times\mathbf{R}^{d})}.

(1.9)

The corresponding inequality for

u_{2}
also holds with
p_{2}
and
(-\Delta)^{\gamma/4}
in place of
p_{1}
and
D^{m}
respectively.
Actually if
f
is not random, then
u_{1}
and
u_{2}
become Gaussian processes
and the reverse inequalities also hold. Thus to prove

D^{m}u_{1},\,\,(-\Delta)^{\gamma/4}u_{2}\,\,\in L_{p}(\Omega\times(0,\infty)\times\mathbf{R}^{d})

and to get a legitimate start
of the
L_{p}
-theory of stochastic PDEs of type (
1.7
), one has to
estimate the right-hand side of (
1.9
).
Obviously (
1.4
) with
K_{1}
and (
1.9
) imply

\|D^{m}u_{1}(t,\cdot)\|^{p}_{L_{p}(\Omega\times(0,\infty)\times\mathbf{R}^{d})}\leq N(p,m)\||f|_{\ell_{2}}\|^{p}_{L_{p}(\Omega\times(0,\infty)\times\mathbf{R}^{d})}.

(1.10)

Using (
1.10
) and
following the ideas in
[
8
]
, one can
construct an
L_{p}
-theory of the general
2m
-order stochastic PDEs. Similarly one can construct an
L_{p}
-theory of stochastic PDEs with the operator
A_{2}(\omega,t)
.
We acknowledge that if the coefficients
a^{\alpha\beta}
are independent of
t
then inequality (
1.10
) for high order stochastic PDEs is also introduced in
[
11
]
on the basis of
H^{\infty}
-functional calculus which is far different from our approach.
One of advantages of our approach is that no regularity condition of the coefficients with respect to time variable is required.

Below is a short description on the related works. As mentioned
above parabolic Littlewood-Paley inequality related to the Laplacian

\Delta
was first proved by Krylov in
[
5
,
7
]
. This result
is considered as a foundation of the
L_{p}
-theory of the
second-order stochastic partial differential equations. Recently the parabolic Littlewood-Paley inequality was proved for the fractional Laplacian
(-\Delta)^{\gamma/2}
,
\gamma\in(0,2)
, in
[
1
,
2
]
, and a slight extension of the result of
[
1
,
2
]
was made to the operator
\mathcal{L}_{0}(t)
in
[
10
]
and to the operator with symbol
-\phi(|\xi|^{2})
in
[
4
]
, where
\mathcal{L}_{0}(t)
is from (
1.6
) and
\phi
is a Bernstein function satisfying

c^{-1}\lambda^{\delta_{1}}\phi(t)\leq\phi(\lambda t)\leq c\lambda^{\delta_{2}}\phi(t),\quad\quad\forall\,\,\lambda,t\geq 1,

\phi(\lambda t)\leq c\lambda^{\delta_{3}}\phi(t),\quad\quad\forall\,\,\lambda,t\leq 1

with some constants
c>1
,
0<\delta_{1}\leq\delta_{2}<1
and
\delta_{3}\in(0,1]
. The operators considered in
[
1
,
2
,
4
,
10
]
are of order less than
2
, they (except
\mathcal{L}_{0}(t)
) do not depend on
t
. The novelty of this article is that it extends existing results which have been proved for lower order operators independent of
t
to the
time-dependent local and non-local operators of arbitrary order.

Next we briefly describe our approach to prove (
1.4
). We estimate the sharp function of
(\int^{t}_{a}|K(t,s,\cdot)*f(s,\cdot)(x)|^{2}_{H}ds)^{1/2}
in terms of the maximal function of
|f|_{H}
, then apply Fefferman-Stein theorem and Hardy-Littlewood maximal theorem. The operators considered in
[
1
,
2
,
4
]
are the infinitesimal generators of certain Lévy processes, and the related kernels
p(t,x)
are transition densities of these processes. Thus to estimate the sharp function of
(\int^{t}_{a}|K(t,s,\cdot)*f(s,\cdot)(x)|^{2}_{H}ds)^{1/2}
, appropriate bounds of the transition densities can be used as in
[
1
,
2
,
4
]
. But for high order operators there is no such related Lévy process and this method can not be applied. Instead, we modify the idea in
[
10
]
and make a good use of Parseval’s identity which enables us to avoid using estimates of the kernels related to the operators.

\mathbf{R}^{d}
stands for the Euclidean space of points

x=(x^{1},...,x^{d})
,
B_{r}(x):=\{y\in\mathbf{R}^{d}:|x-y|<r\}

We also use

to denote a partial derivative of order

with respect to

.
For an open set

and a nonnegative integer

. By

(resp.

(resp.

The standard

Similarly, by

We use “

By

For a Borel

For a sequence

If we write

Main results

.

The kernel

\displaystyle\int_{s}^{\infty}|\mathcal{F}\{K(t,s,\cdot)\}(\xi)|^{2}dt\leq C_{0}

(2.2)

Theorem 2.4
.

Let

. Suppose that Assumptions

Let

\mathcal{F}(A(t)u)(\xi)=\psi(t,\xi)\mathcal{F}(u)(\xi),\quad\forall\,u\in C^{\infty}_{0}(\mathbf{R}^{d}).

Define the kernel

\displaystyle p(t,s,x)=I_{0\leq s<t}\mathcal{F}^{-1}\Big(\exp\big(\int_{s}^{t}\psi(r,\xi))dr\big)\Big)(x).

(2.24)

Theorem 2.5
.

Fix
p\geq 2
and
\gamma>0
. Assume there exist constants
\nu>0

(2.25)

\displaystyle\left\|\Big(\int_{0}^{t}|\Delta^{\gamma/4}p(t,s,\cdot)\ast f(s,\cdot)(x)|_{H}^{2}~ds\Big)^{1/2}\right\|_{L_{p}(\mathbf{R}^{d+1})}\leq N\||f|_{H}\|_{L_{p}(\mathbf{R}^{d+1})},

.

For applications of Theorem

we recall the operators
A_{i}(t)
from (

A_{1}(t)u=(-1)^{m-1}\sum_{|\alpha|=|\beta|=m}a^{\alpha\beta}(t)D^{\alpha+\beta}u,\quad\quad A_{2}(t)=-a(t)(-\Delta)^{\gamma/2}

\displaystyle\nu|\xi|^{2m}\leq\sum_{|\alpha|=|\beta|=m}\xi^{\alpha}\xi^{\beta}\Re\left[a^{\alpha\beta}(\omega,t)\right]\leq\nu^{-1}|\xi|^{2m},\quad\forall\xi\in\mathbf{R}^{d}.

Denote

denote the counting measure on

Lemma 3.3
.

Let

, and

be a locally integrable and continuously differentiable function on
\mathbf{R}^{d}
. Let

and

\displaystyle\big|(f\ast v)(y)|\leq N\big(\mathbb{M}^{R_{1}+R_{2}}_{x}f^{2}(x)\big)^{1/2}\int_{R_{2}}^{\infty}(R_{1}+\rho)^{d}\Big(\int_{\partial B_{1}}\big(\nabla v(\rho w),w\big)^{2}S_{1}(dw)\Big)^{1/2}d\rho,

where
N=N(d)
.

Proof.

Since the case
d=1
is easier, we assume
d\geq 2
. Using the
polar coordinates and Fubini’s theorem we get

\displaystyle\int_{|z|>R_{2}}f(y-z)v(z)~dz

\displaystyle=\int_{R_{2}}^{\infty}\int_{\partial B_{1}}f(y-\rho w)v(\rho w)\rho^{d-1}~S_{1}(dw)d\rho

\displaystyle=\int_{\partial B_{1}}\left[\int_{R_{2}}^{\infty}v(\rho w)\left(\frac{d}{d\rho}\int_{R_{2}}^{\rho}f(y-\gamma w)\gamma^{d-1}~d\gamma\right)S_{1}(dw)\right]d\rho.

By integration by parts and the assumption on
v
, for almost all
w
,

\displaystyle\int_{R_{2}}^{\infty}v(\rho w)\left(\frac{d}{d\rho}\int_{R_{2}}^{\rho}f(y-\gamma w)\gamma^{d-1}~d\gamma\right)d\rho

\displaystyle=-\int_{R_{2}}^{\infty}(\nabla v(\rho w),w)\int_{R_{2}}^{\rho}f(y-\gamma w)\gamma^{d-1}~d\gamma d\rho.

In the above we use the fact that there exists a sequence
\rho_{n}\to\infty
, which might be dependent on
w
, so that

as

and that

is a bounded function of

By the assumption

\displaystyle\int_{B_{\rho}}f^{2}(y-z)~dz=\int_{B_{\rho}(y)}f^{2}(z)~dz

\displaystyle\leq\int_{B_{R_{1}+\rho}(x)}f^{2}(z)~dz

\displaystyle\leq N(d)(R_{1}+\rho)^{d}\mathbb{M}^{R_{1}+R_{2}}_{x}f^{2}(x).

Finally using Fubini’s theorem, Hölder’s inequality, and the assumption that

if
|z|\leq R_{2}
, we get

\displaystyle|(f\ast v)(y)|

\displaystyle\leq\Big|\int_{R_{2}}^{\infty}\int_{\partial B_{1}}(\nabla v(\rho w),w)\int_{R_{2}}^{\rho}f(y-\gamma w)\gamma^{d-1}~d\gamma S_{1}(dw)d\rho\Big|

\displaystyle\leq\int_{R_{2}}^{\infty}\Big(\int_{\partial B_{1}}\int_{R_{2}}^{\rho}\Big|(\nabla v(\rho w),w)\Big|^{2}\gamma^{d-1}d\gamma S_{1}(dw)\Big)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\hskip 113.81102pt\times\Big(\int_{\partial B_{1}}\int_{R_{2}}^{\rho}f^{2}(y-\gamma w)\gamma^{d-1}~d\gamma S_{1}(dw)\Big)^{1/2}d\rho

\displaystyle\leq\int_{R_{2}}^{\infty}\rho^{d/2}\Big(\int_{\partial B_{1}}\Big|(\nabla v(\rho w),w)\Big|^{2}S_{1}(dw)\Big)^{1/2}\Big(\int_{|z|\leq\rho}f^{2}(y-z)~dz\Big)^{1/2}d\rho

\displaystyle\leq N\big(\mathbb{M}^{R_{1}+R_{2}}_{x}f^{2}(x)\big)^{1/2}\int_{R_{2}}^{\infty}(R_{1}+\rho)^{d}\Big(\int_{\partial B_{1}}\Big|(\nabla v(\rho w),w)\big|^{2}S_{1}(dw)\Big)^{1/2}d\rho.

The lemma is proved.

Corollary 5.3
.

\displaystyle\sup_{s<t}\int_{\mathbf{R}^{d}}|x|^{\mu}|F_{1}(t,s,x)|^{2}dx<\infty,\quad\text{if}\quad\mu<\gamma+d+2;

\displaystyle\sup_{s<t}\int_{\mathbf{R}^{d}}|x|^{\mu}|F_{2}(t,s,x)|^{2}dx<\infty,\quad\text{if}\quad\mu<(\gamma+d+4);

\displaystyle\sup_{s<t}\int_{\mathbf{R}^{d}}|x|^{\mu}|F_{3}(t,s,x)|^{2}dx<\infty,\quad\text{if}\quad\mu<(3\gamma+d+2).

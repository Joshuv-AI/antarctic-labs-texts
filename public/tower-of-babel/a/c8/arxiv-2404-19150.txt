Boundedness of the p p -primary torsion of the Brauer group of products of varieties
Alexei N. Skorobogatov

Abstract
Let k k be a field finitely generated over its prime subfield. We prove that the quotient of the Brauer group of a product of varieties over k k by the sum of the images of the Brauer groups of factors has finite exponent. The bulk of the proof concerns p p -primary torsion in characteristic p p . Our approach gives a more direct proof of the boundedness of the p p -primary torsion of the Brauer group of an abelian variety, as recently proved by D’Addezio. We show that the transcendental Brauer group of a Kummer surface over k k has finite exponent, but can be infinite when k k is an infinite field of positive characteristic. This answers a question of Zarhin and the author.

The following lemma may be well known to the experts; we give a proof because we could not find it

Lemma 3.1

Let
A
be an abelian variety over an algebraically closed field
k
. Let
p
be a prime,
possibly equal to
{\rm char}(k)
. For any integer
m
the endomorphism

[m]\colon A\to A
acts on

as
m^{2}
for any

In the case

we can replace fppf cohomology by étale
cohomology. Since

as
m

{\rm H}^{2}_{\rm{\acute{e}t}}(A,\mu_{p^{n}})\cong\wedge^{2}{\rm H}^{1}_{\rm{\acute{e}t}}(A,\mu_{p^{n}})(-1)
as
m^{2}
.

Now let
p={\rm char}(k)
.
Considering the map
[p^{n}]\colon{\cal O}_{A}^{\times}\to{\cal O}_{A}^{\times}
in the fppf and étale topologies
gives rise to a canonical isomorphism
[
Ill79
, (II.5.1.4)]

{\rm H}^{i}_{\rm fppf}(A,\mu_{p^{n}})\cong{\rm H}^{i-1}_{\rm{\acute{e}t}}(A,{\cal O}_{A}^{\times}/O_{A}^{\times p^{n}}).

There is a map of étale sheaves of abelian groups

d\log\colon{\cal O}_{A}^{\times}/O_{A}^{\times p^{n}}\to W_{n}\Omega^{1}_{X}
,
see
[
Ill79
, Prop. I.3.23.2]
. By
[
Ill79
, Thm. II.1.4, (II.1.3.3)]
for each
i\geq 0
we have a
canonical isomorphism
{\rm H}^{i}_{\rm cris}(A/W_{n})\cong{\rm H}^{i}_{\rm{\acute{e}t}}(A,W_{n}\Omega_{A}^{\bullet})
.
We claim that the resulting map

is injective. We sketch the proof referring to
[

, Thm. 1.7]
for details.

The case

is stated in
[

, Remarque II.5.17 (a)]
. It is
a consequence of the following two facts:

(1) the map
{\rm H}^{0}(A,Z^{1}_{A})\to{\rm H}^{0}(A,\Omega^{1}_{A})
is surjective,

Z^{1}_{A}:={\rm{Ker}}[d\colon\Omega^{1}_{A}\to\Omega^{2}_{A}]
;

(2) the map
{\rm H}^{1}(A,Z^{1}_{A})\to{\rm H}^{2}_{\rm dR}(A/k)
induced by the natural morphism of complexes

Z^{1}_{A}[-1]\to\Omega^{\bullet}_{A}

Property (1) is true for any commutative group scheme
A
. Indeed, for invariant
vector fields
X
and

because
\omega(Y)
and
\omega(X)
are in

, and
the Lie algebra of

is abelian.

The map in (2) factors as
{\rm H}^{1}(A,Z^{1}_{A})\to{\mathbb{H}}^{2}(A,\Omega^{\geq 1}_{A})\to{\mathbb{H}}^{2}(A,\Omega^{\bullet}_{A})={\rm H}^{2}_{\rm dR}(A/k)

because for abelian varieties
the Hodge-de Rham spectral sequence degenerates at the first page, by
a theorem of Oda
[

.
The injectivity of the first map can be checked using Čech cohomology
[

The case of

follows by induction in

from the following commutative diagram with

\scriptstyle{d\log}

1-F

Proof of Theorem

A.1

For each
n
, we have a distinguished triangle in the derived category of
{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}
-modules

R\Gamma(X,{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}(1))\xrightarrow{p^{n}}R\Gamma(X,{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}(1))\to R\Gamma_{{\rm fppf}}(X,\mu_{p^{n}})

(9)

obtained from the distinguished triangles

R\Gamma_{{\rm fppf}}(X,\mu_{p^{m}})\xrightarrow{p^{n}}R\Gamma_{{\rm fppf}}(X,\mu_{p^{n+m}})\xrightarrow{}R\Gamma_{{\rm fppf}}(X,\mu_{p^{n}})

by passing to the inverse limit over all
m
. For all
i,n
the triangle (
9
) induces the short exact sequences

0\to{\rm H}^{i}(X,{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}(1))/p^{n}\to{\rm H}^{i}(X,\mu_{p^{n}})\to{\rm H}^{i+1}(X,{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}(1))[p^{n}]\to 0.

(10)

For each
i
, passing to the direct limit along the maps induced by
\mu_{p^{n}}\hookrightarrow\mu_{p^{n+1}}
we get the short exact sequence

0\to{\rm H}^{i}(X,{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}(1))\otimes_{{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}}{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}/{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}\to\varinjlim{\rm H}^{i}(X,\mu_{p^{n}})\to{\rm H}^{i+1}(X,{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}(1))\{p\}\to 0.

(11)

By Lemma
A.2
the group
{\rm H}^{i}(X,{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}(1))\otimes_{{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}}{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}/{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}
is isomorphic to
({\mathbb{Q}}_{p}/{\mathbb{Z}}_{p})^{\oplus r_{i}}
, and the group
{\rm H}^{i+1}(X,{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}(1))\{p\}
is annihilated by a power of
p
. The abelian group
{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}/{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}
is divisible, hence injective,
thus
\varinjlim{\rm H}^{i}(X,\mu_{p^{n}})
is isomorphic to the direct sum of

({\mathbb{Q}}_{p}/{\mathbb{Z}}_{p})^{\oplus r_{i}}
and
{\rm H}^{i+1}(X,{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}(1))\{p\}
.

On the other hand, for all
n
we have short exact sequences

0\to{\rm{Pic}}(X)/p^{n}\to{\rm H}^{2}(X,\mu_{p^{n}})\to{\rm{Br}}(X)[p^{n}]\to 0

(12)

which induce, after passing to the direct limit, a surjection
\varinjlim{\rm H}^{2}(X,\mu_{p^{n}})\to{\rm{Br}}(X)\{p\}
with kernel

\varinjlim{\rm{Pic}}(X)/p^{n}\cong({\mathbb{Q}}_{p}/{\mathbb{Z}}_{p})^{\rho}
.
Hence

{\rm{Br}}(X)\{p\}\cong\mathrm{Coker}(\alpha)\oplus{\rm H}^{3}(X,{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}(1))\{p\}

for some injection
\alpha\colon({\mathbb{Q}}_{p}/{\mathbb{Z}}_{p})^{\oplus\rho}\hookrightarrow({\mathbb{Q}}_{p}/{\mathbb{Z}}_{p})^{\oplus r_{2}}
, which proves the theorem.

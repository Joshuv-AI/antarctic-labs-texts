Quantitative estimate of diameter for weighted manifolds under integral curvature bounds and ε \varepsilon -range
Taku Ito

Abstract
. In this article, we extend the compactness theorems proved by Sprouse [ 11 ] and Hwang–Lee [ 2 ] to a weighted manifold under the assumption that the weighted Ricci curvature is bounded below in terms of its weight function. With the help of the ε \varepsilon -range, we treat the case that the effective dimension is at most 1 1 in addition to the case that the effective dimension is at least the dimension of the manifold. To show these theorems, we extend the segment inequality of Cheeger–Colding [ 1 ] to a weighted manifold.

1.
Introduction

One of the most fundamental theorems in Riemannian geometry is the Myers theorem
[
8
]
, which states that if an
n
-dimensional complete Riemannian manifold
(M,g)
satisfies
\mathrm{Ric}_{g}\geq(n-1)K
with
K>0
and
n\geq 2,
then
M
is compact and its diameter
\mathrm{diam}(M)
is at most
\pi/\sqrt{K}
. Moreover, its fundamental group
\pi_{1}(M)
is a finite group.
Here, we denote by
\mathrm{Ric}_{g}
the Ricci curvature of
(M,g)
. For
\kappa\in C^{\infty}(M)
,
\mathrm{Ric}_{g}\geq\kappa
means that
\mathrm{Ric}_{g}(v,v)\geq\kappa(p)g(v,v)
holds for
v\in T_{p}M
.
This theorem has been generalized by several ways. As one of these attempts, we introduce the theorems by Sprouse
[
11
]
below.

In this article, we always assume that
(M,g)
is a complete Riemannian manifold and its dimension is at least
2
. We denote by
d
and
\mathrm{vol}_{g}
the Riemannian distance function and the volume measure on
(M,g)
, respectively. We write
a_{+}=\max\{a,0\}
for
a\in\mathbb{R}
. Set
\mathbb{N}_{\geq 2}=\{\,n\in\mathbb{N}\mid n\geq 2\,\}
.
For
p\in M
and
R>0
, we denote

\displaystyle U_{p}M

\displaystyle=\{\,v\in T_{p}M\mid g(v,v)=1\,\},

\displaystyle\mathrm{Ric}_{g-}(p)

\displaystyle=\inf\{\,\mathrm{Ric}_{g}(v,v)\mid v\in U_{p}M\,\},

\displaystyle B(p,R)

\displaystyle=\{\,q\in M\mid d(p,q)<R\,\}.

Theorem 1.1

(
[
11
]
*Theorem 1.1)
.

Let
n\in\mathbb{N}_{\geq 2}
and
\eta>0
. There exists a positive constant
\delta(n,\eta)
such that if an
n
-dimensional compact Riemannian manifold
(M,g)
satisfies
\mathrm{Ric}_{g}\geq 0
and

\frac{1}{\mathrm{vol}_{g}(M)}\int_{M}\big((n-1)-\mathrm{Ric}_{g-}\big)_{+}\,d\mathrm{vol}_{g}<\delta(n,\eta)

then
\mathrm{diam}(M)<\pi+\eta
.

Theorem 1.2

(
[
11
]
*Theorem 1.2)
.

Let
n\in\mathbb{N}_{\geq 2},K\leq 0
and
R,\eta>0
. There exists a positive constant
\delta(n,K,R,\eta)
such that if an
n
-dimensional complete Riemannian manifold
(M,g)
satisfies
\mathrm{Ric}_{g}\geq(n-1)K
and

\sup_{p\in M}\frac{1}{\mathrm{vol}_{g}(B(p,R))}\int_{B(p,R)}\big((n-1)-\mathrm{Ric}_{g-}\big)_{+}\,d\mathrm{vol}_{g}<\delta(n,K,R,\eta)

then
M
is compact and
\mathrm{diam}(M)<\pi+\eta
.

Hwang–Lee
[
2
]
extended Theorems
1.1
and
1.2
to a weighted manifold having a bounded
\infty
-Ricci curvature. A
weighted manifold
is a triple
(M,g,\mu_{f})
, where
(M,g)
is an
n
-dimensional Riemannian manifold, endowed with a measure
\mu_{f}=e^{-f}d\mathrm{vol}_{g}
having smooth positive density with respect to the volume measure
\mathrm{vol}_{g}
. We call
f\in C^{\infty}(M)
the
weight function
of
\mu_{f}
.
For given
N\in(-\infty,\infty]
, the
N
-Ricci curvature
is defined for
v\in TM
by

\displaystyle\mathrm{Ric}_{N}(v,v)=\begin{cases}\mathrm{Ric}_{g}(v,v)+\mathrm{Hess}_{g}f(v,v)-\dfrac{1}{N-n}g(\nabla f,v)^{2}&\text{if }N\in\mathbb{R}\setminus\{n\},\\
\mathrm{Ric}_{g}(v,v)+\mathrm{Hess}_{g}f(v,v)&\text{if }N=\infty,\end{cases}

and for
N=n
,

\displaystyle\mathrm{Ric}_{n}(v,v)=\begin{cases}\mathrm{Ric}_{g}(v,v)&\text{if }f\text{ is a constant function},\\
-\infty&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

We call the parameter
N
the
effective dimension
. We refer to a
weighted Ricci curvature
as a generic term for the
N
-Ricci curvature with
N\in(-\infty,\infty]
. As similar as
\mathrm{Ric}_{-}
, we define
\mathrm{Ric}_{N-}:M\to\mathbb{R}
by

\mathrm{Ric}_{N-}(p)=\inf\{\,\mathrm{Ric}_{N}(v,v)\mid v\in U_{p}M\,\}.

Theorem 1.3

(
[
2
]
*Theorem 1.4)
.

Let
n\in\mathbb{N}_{\geq 2},k\geq 0
and
\eta>0
.
There exists a positive constant
\delta(n+4k,\eta)
such that if an
n
-dimensional compact weighted manifold
(M,g,\mu_{f})
satisfies
\mathrm{Ric}_{\infty}\geq 0
,
|f|\leq k
and

\frac{1}{\mu_{f}(M)}\int_{M}\big((n-1)-\mathrm{Ric}_{\infty-}\big)_{+}\,d\mu_{f}<\delta(n+4k,\eta)

then

\mathrm{diam}(M)<\left(\pi+\frac{\eta}{2}\right)\sqrt{1+\frac{8k}{(n-1)\pi}}+\frac{\eta}{2}.

Theorem 1.4

(
[
2
]
*Theorem 1.5)
.

Let
n\in\mathbb{N}_{\geq 2},k\geq 0,K\leq 0
and
R,\eta>0
. There exists a positive constant
\delta(n+4k,K,R,\eta)
such that if an
n
-dimensional complete weighted manifold
(M,g,\mu_{f})
satisfies
|f|\leq k
,
\mathrm{Ric}_{\infty}\geq(n-1)K
and

\sup_{p\in M}\frac{1}{\mu_{f}\big(B(p,R)\big)}\int_{B(p,R)}\big((n-1)-\mathrm{Ric}_{\infty-}\big)_{+}\,d\mu_{f}<\delta(n+4k,K,R,\eta)

then
M
is compact and

\mathrm{diam}(M)<\left(\pi+\frac{\eta}{2}\right)\sqrt{1+\frac{8k}{(n-1)\pi}}+\frac{\eta}{2}.

The aim of this article is to extend Theorems
1.1
–
1.4
for a weighted manifold under the assumption that the weighted curvature is bounded below in terms of its weight function. The key of the proof is the notion of the following
\varepsilon
-range introduced by Lu–Minguzzi–Ohta
[
7
]
.

Definition 1.5
.

For
n\in\mathbb{N}_{\geq 2}
and
N\in(-\infty,1]\cup[n,\infty]
,
we say that
\varepsilon\in\mathbb{R}
is in the
\varepsilon(n,N)
-range
if
\varepsilon\in\mathbb{R}
satisfies the following conditions:

\varepsilon=0\,\text{ for }N=1,\qquad|\varepsilon|<\sqrt{\frac{N-1}{N-n}}\,\text{ for }N\neq 1,n,\qquad\varepsilon\in\mathbb{R}\,\text{ for }N=n.

For
n,N
as above and
\varepsilon
in the
\varepsilon(n,N)
-range, we define the constant
c=c(n,N,\varepsilon)
by

c=\frac{1}{n-1}\left(1-\varepsilon^{2}\frac{N-n}{N-1}\right)>0

for
N\neq 1
.
If
\varepsilon=0
, then one can take
N\to 1
and set
c(1,0)=1/(n-1)
.

Notice that
|\varepsilon|=1
happens only if
N\in[n,\infty)
.
For
N\in(-\infty,1]\cup[n,\infty]
and
K\in\mathbb{R}
, Lu–Minguzzi–Ohta
[
6
]
established a curvature bound
\mathrm{Ric}_{N}\geq Ke^{4(\varepsilon-1)f/(n-1)}
on a weighted manifold
(M,g,\mu_{f})
by using the
\varepsilon(n,N)
-range. This curvature bound is a generalization of a different kind of curvature bounds
\mathrm{Ric}_{1}\geq Ke^{4f/(1-n)}
introduced by Wylie–Yeroshkin
[
12
]
and for
N\in(-\infty,1]
,
\mathrm{Ric}_{N}\geq Ke^{4f/(N-n)}
introduced by Kuwae–Li
[
4
]
. They presented several comparison theorems with each curvature bounds. Furthermore, Kuwae–Sakurai
[
5
]
also provided several comparison theorems with the
\varepsilon(n,N)
-range.

To state our theorems, we prepare the following condition.

Definition 1.6
.

Let
n\in\mathbb{N}_{\geq 2}
,
N\in(-\infty,1]\cup[n,\infty]
,
\varepsilon

in the
\varepsilon(n,N)
-range,
K\leq 0
and
b\geq a>0
.

(1)

We say that an
n
-dimensional weighted manifold
(M,g,\mu_{f})
satisfies a
(N,K,\varepsilon,a,b)
-condition
if one has

\displaystyle\mathrm{Ric}_{N}\geq Ke^{\frac{4(\varepsilon-1)}{n-1}f},\qquad a\leq e^{\frac{2(1-\varepsilon)}{n-1}f}\leq b\quad\text{on }M.

(2)

We define the constants
\widetilde{D}=\widetilde{D}(n,N,\varepsilon,a,b)
by

\displaystyle\widetilde{D}=\begin{cases}\sqrt{\dfrac{N-1}{n-1}}&\text{if }\varepsilon=1,\\[-2.84544pt]
\\
\sqrt{1+\dfrac{2}{|1-\varepsilon|\pi}\log\dfrac{b}{a}}&\text{if }\varepsilon\neq 1\text{ and }N\in[n,\infty],\\[-2.84544pt]
\\
\sqrt{\left(\dfrac{b}{a}\right)^{\lambda_{0}}+\dfrac{2}{(1-\varepsilon)\pi\lambda_{0}}\left(\left(\dfrac{b}{a}\right)^{\lambda_{0}}-1\right)}&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

Moreover, for
N\in(-\infty,1]
, we define
\lambda_{0}=\lambda_{0}(n,N,\varepsilon)
by

\lambda_{0}=\frac{1}{1-\varepsilon}\left(1-\sqrt{\frac{N-1}{N-n}}\right).

For a weighted manifold satisfying the
(N,K,\varepsilon,a,b)
-condition, we estimate its diameter quantitatively.

Theorem 1.7
.

Let
n\in\mathbb{N}_{\geq 2},N\in(-\infty,1]\cup[n,\infty]
,
\varepsilon
in the
\varepsilon(n,N)
-range,
b\geq a>0
and
H,\eta>0
.
There exists a positive constant
\delta_{1}(n,N,\varepsilon,a,b,H,\eta)
such that if an
n
-dimensional compact weighted manifold
(M,g,\mu_{f})
satisfies the
(N,0,\varepsilon,a,b)
-condition and

\frac{1}{\mu_{f}(M)}\int_{M}\big((n-1)H-\mathrm{Ric}_{N-}\big)_{+}\,d\mu_{f}\leq\delta_{1}(n,N,\varepsilon,a,b,H,\eta)

then

\mathrm{diam}(M)\leq\frac{\pi+\eta}{\sqrt{H}}\widetilde{D}(n,N,\varepsilon,a,b).

Theorem 1.8
.

Let
n\in\mathbb{N}_{\geq 2},N\in(-\infty,1]\cup[n,\infty]
and
\varepsilon
in the
\varepsilon(n,N)
-range. Take real numbers
K,a,b,H,R
and
\eta
such that
K\leq 0
and
a,b,H,R,\eta>0
with
a\leq b
. There exists a positive constant
\delta(n,N,K,\varepsilon,a,b,H,R,\eta)
such that if an
n
-dimensional complete weighted manifold
(M,g,\mu_{f})
satisfies the
(N,K,\varepsilon,a,b)
-condition and

\sup_{p\in M}\frac{1}{\mu_{f}\big(B(p,R)\big)}\int_{B(p,R)}\big((n-1)H-\mathrm{Ric}_{N-}\big)_{+}\,d\mu_{f}\leq\delta(n,N,K,\varepsilon,a,b,H,R,\eta)

then
M
is compact and

\mathrm{diam}(M)\leq\frac{\pi+\eta}{\sqrt{H}}\widetilde{D}(n,N,\varepsilon,a,b).

Remark 1.9
.

Assume that an
n
-dimensional complete weighted manifold
(M,g,\mathrm{vol}_{g})
satisfies
\mathrm{Ric}_{g}\geq(n-1)H
for
H>0
. Then
(M,g,\mathrm{vol}_{g})
satisfies the
(n,0,1,1,1)
-condition and

\sup_{p\in M}\frac{1}{\mathrm{vol}_{g}\big(B(p,R)\big)}\int_{B(p,R)}\big((n-1)H-\mathrm{Ric}_{n-}\big)_{+}\,d\mathrm{vol}_{g}=0

holds for each
R>0
. Furthermore, we find
\widetilde{D}(n,n,1,1,1)=1
. By Theorem
1.8
, we see that
\mathrm{diam}(M)\leq(\pi+\eta)/\sqrt{H}
for any
\eta>0
. Taking
\eta\to 0
, we obtain
\mathrm{diam}(M)\leq\pi/\sqrt{H}
. This means that Theorem
1.8
recovers the Myers theorem.

This article is organized as follows: In Section
2
, we recall a Bishop-type inequality, the volume comparison theorem on a weighted manifold given in
[
6
]
. Then we extend the segment inequality of Cheeger–Colding
[
1
]
to a weighted manifold under the assumption that the weighted Ricci curvature is bounded below in terms of its weight function. In Sections
3
and
4
, we prove Theorems
1.7
and
1.8
, respectively. In Sections
5
, we analyze the fundamental group of a weighted manifold satisfying the
(N,K,\varepsilon,a,b)
-condition.

2.
Preliminaries and segment inequality with
\varepsilon
-range

We recall some results by Lu–Minguzzi–Ohta
[
6
]
. Although they discussed a weighted Finsler manifold, throughout this article, we treat a weighted manifold.

2.1.
Preliminaries

Lu–Minguzzi–Ohta
[
6
]
introduced the
\varepsilon
-range and proved a volume comparison theorem for a weighted manifold
(M,g,\mu_{f})
.

For
p\in M
, let
\gamma:[0,l)\to M
be a unit speed geodesic such that
\gamma(0)=p
. Set
f_{\gamma}(t)=f\big(\gamma(t)\big)
. We denote by

\mu_{f}=e^{-f}d\mathrm{vol}_{g}={\mathcal{A}}^{f}_{\gamma}(t)dtd\theta_{n-1}=e^{-f_{\gamma}(t)}{\mathcal{A}}_{\gamma}(t)dtd\theta_{n-1}

the weighted measure in the geodesic polar coordinates along geodesics
\gamma
, where
d\theta_{n-1}
is the volume measure of the unit sphere in
T_{p}M
.

First we introduce a Bishop-type inequality.

Proposition 2.1

(
[
6
]
*Theorem 3.5)
.

Let
(M,g,\mu_{f})
be an
n
-dimensional complete weighted manifold,
N\in(-\infty,1]\cup[n,\infty]
,
\varepsilon

in the
\varepsilon(n,N)
-range and
c=c(n,N,\varepsilon)
.
For a unit speed geodesic
\gamma:[0,l)\to M
, set

h(t)=e^{-cf_{\gamma}(t)}{\mathcal{A}}_{\gamma}(t)^{c},\qquad h_{1}(\tau)=h(\varphi_{\gamma}^{-1}(\tau))

for
t\in[0,l)
and
\tau\in[0,\varphi_{\gamma}(l))
, where

\varphi_{\gamma}(t)=\int_{0}^{t}e^{\frac{2(\varepsilon-1)}{n-1}f_{\gamma}(s)}\,ds.

Then, for all
\tau\in(0,\varphi_{\gamma}(l))
,

h_{1}^{\prime\prime}(\tau)\leq-ch_{1}(\tau)\mathrm{Ric}_{N}\big((\gamma\circ\varphi_{\gamma}^{-1})^{\prime}(\tau)\big).

We define the comparison function
{\bf sn}_{\kappa}
by

{\bf sn}_{\kappa}(t)=\begin{cases}\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{\kappa}}\sin(\sqrt{\kappa}t)&\kappa>0,\\
t&\kappa=0,\\
\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{-\kappa}}\sinh(\sqrt{-\kappa}t)&\kappa<0,\end{cases}

where
t\in[0,\pi/\sqrt{\kappa}]
for
\kappa>0
and
t\in\mathbb{R}
for
\kappa\leq 0
. Notice that
{\bf sn}_{\kappa}
satisfies

\begin{cases}{\bf sn}_{\kappa}^{\prime\prime}(t)+\kappa{\bf sn}_{\kappa}(t)=0,\\
{\bf sn}_{\kappa}(0)=0,\\
{\bf sn}^{\prime}_{\kappa}(0)=1.\end{cases}

Let us recall a volume comparison theorem.

Proposition 2.2

(
[
6
]
*Theorem 3.11)
.

Let
(M,g,\mu_{f})
be an
n
-dimensional complete weighted manifold satisfying the
(N,K,\varepsilon,a,b)
-condition. Then

\dfrac{\mu_{f}\big(B(p,R)\big)}{\mu_{f}\big(B(p,r)\big)}\leq\dfrac{b}{a}\cdot\dfrac{\displaystyle\int_{0}^{R/a}{\bf sn}_{cK}(\tau)^{1/c}\,d\tau}{\displaystyle\int_{0}^{r/b}{\bf sn}_{cK}(\tau)^{1/c}\,d\tau}

holds for all
p\in M
and
0<r\leq R
.

It should be mentioned that Theorem
2.2
was originaly proved for all
K\in\mathbb{R}
in
[
6
]
*Theorem 3.11.

Remark 2.3
.

If
K=0
, we have
{\bf sn}_{0}(\tau)=\tau
, which gives

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{r}{\bf sn}(\tau)^{\frac{1}{c}}\,d\tau=\frac{c}{c+1}r^{\frac{1}{c}+1}

for
r>0
. In the setting of Theorem
2.2
for
K=0
, we find that

\dfrac{\mu_{f}\big(B(p,R)\big)}{\mu_{f}\big(B(p,r)\big)}\leq\dfrac{b}{a}\cdot\left(\frac{bR}{ar}\right)^{\frac{1}{c}+1}.

2.2.
Segment inequality with
\varepsilon
-range

The segment inequality proved by Cheeger–Colding
[
1
]
*Theorem 2.11 plays an important role in the proof of Theorems
1.1
and
1.2
. We extend this theorem as follows.

Theorem 2.4
.

Let
(M,g,\mu_{f})
be an
n
-dimensional complete weighted manifold,
N\in(-\infty,1]\cup[n,\infty]
,
\varepsilon
in the
\varepsilon(n,N)
-range and
K\in\mathbb{R}
. Assume that

\mathrm{Ric}_{N}\geq Ke^{\frac{4(\varepsilon-1)}{n-1}f}

holds.
For
i=1,2
, let
A_{i}
be bounded open subsets of
M
and W be an open subset of
M
such that, for each two points
y_{i}\in A_{i}
, any unit minimal geodesics
\gamma_{y_{1},y_{2}}
from
y_{1}
to
y_{2}
is containd in W.
Then for any non-negative integrable function
F
on
W
, we have

\displaystyle\quad\int_{A_{1}\times A_{2}}\left(\int_{0}^{d(y_{1},y_{2})}F(\gamma_{y_{1},y_{2}})\,ds\right)d\mu_{f\times f}

\displaystyle\leq C(n,c,K)\left[\mu_{f}(A_{2})\,\mathrm{diam}(A_{1})+\mu_{f}(A_{1})\,\mathrm{diam}(A_{2})\right]\int_{W}F\,d\mu_{f},

where
\mu_{f\times f}
is the product measure on
M\times M
induced by
\mu_{f}
and

C(n,c,K)=\sup_{y_{1}\in A_{1},y_{2}\in A_{2}}\left(\sup_{0<\frac{s}{2}\leq u<s\leq d(y_{1},y_{2})}\frac{{\bf sn}_{cK}\big(\varphi_{\gamma}(s)\big)^{1/c}}{{\bf sn}_{cK}\big(\varphi_{\gamma}(u)\big)^{1/c}}\right).

Proof.

Set

B=\{(y_{1},y_{2})\in A_{1}\times A_{2}\mid\mathrm{there\ exists\ a\ unique\ minimal\ geodesic\ from}\ y_{1}\ \mathrm{to}\ y_{2}\}.

Then
\mu_{f\times f}(B)=\mu_{f\times f}(A_{1}\times A_{2})
holds since the measure of a cut locus of each
y_{i}\in A_{i}
with respect to
\mu_{f}
is zero for
i=1,2
.

Define maps
E_{1},E_{2},E:A_{1}\times A_{2}\to\mathbb{R}
by

\displaystyle E_{1}(y_{1},y_{2})

\displaystyle=\int_{d(y_{1},y_{2})/2}^{d(y_{1},y_{2})}F\big(\gamma_{y_{1},y_{2}}(u)\big)\,du,

\displaystyle E_{2}(y_{1},y_{2})

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{d(y_{1},y_{2})/2}F\big(\gamma_{y_{1},y_{2}}(u)\big)\,du,

\displaystyle E(y_{1},y_{2})

\displaystyle=E_{1}(y_{1},y_{2})+E_{2}(y_{1},y_{2})=\int_{0}^{d(y_{1},y_{2})}F\big(\gamma_{y_{1},y_{2}}(u)\big)\,du.

Fix
y_{i}\in A_{i}
and
v_{i}\in U_{y_{i}}M
for
i=1,2
. Set

I(y_{i},v_{i})=\{\,s>0\mid\exp_{y_{i}}(sv_{i})\in A_{i+1},\,d\big(y_{i},\exp_{y_{i}}(sv_{i})\big)=s\,\},

where we put
A_{3}=A_{1}
. We denote by
|I(y_{1},v_{1})|
the
1
-dimensional Lebesgue measure of
I(y_{1},v_{1})
. Since
\{\,\exp_{y_{i}}(sv_{i})\mid s\in I(y_{i},v_{i})\,\}
is contained only in
A_{i+1}
, we have

|I(y_{i},v_{i})|\leq\mathrm{diam}(A_{i+1})

for
i=1,2
.

Fix
s\in I(y_{1},v_{1})
. For
\gamma(u)=\exp_{y_{1}}(uv_{1})
on
u\in[0,s)
, by Proposition
2.1
, we find that
\mathcal{A}^{f}_{\gamma}(u)/\big({\bf sn}_{cK}(\varphi_{\gamma}(u))\big)^{1/c}
is non-increasing, where
\varphi_{\gamma}(u)<\pi/\sqrt{cK}
holds if
K>0
(see
[
6
]
*Proof of Theorem 3.6). Then we find that

\frac{\mathcal{A}^{f}_{\gamma}(s)}{\mathcal{A}^{f}_{\gamma}(u)}\leq\frac{{\bf sn}_{cK}\big(\varphi_{\gamma}(s)\big)^{1/c}}{{\bf sn}_{cK}\big(\varphi_{\gamma}(u)\big)^{1/c}}\leq C(n,c,K)

for
0<s/2\leq u<s\leq T(v_{1})
, where
T(v_{1})
is the supremum of
s
such that
s\in I(y_{1},v_{1})
. This yields

\displaystyle E_{1}(y_{1},\gamma(s))\mathcal{A}^{f}_{\gamma}(s)

\displaystyle=\mathcal{A}^{f}_{\gamma}(s)\int^{s}_{s/2}F\big(\gamma(u)\big)\,du

\displaystyle\leq C(n,c,K)\int^{s}_{s/2}F\big(\gamma(u)\big)\mathcal{A}^{f}_{\gamma}(u)\,du

\displaystyle\leq C(n,c,K)\int^{T(v_{1})}_{0}F\big(\gamma(u)\big)\mathcal{A}^{f}_{\gamma}(u)\,du.

Integrating this inequality on
I(y_{1},v_{1})
implies that

\displaystyle\int_{I(y_{1},v_{1})}E_{1}(y_{1},\gamma(s))\mathcal{A}^{f}_{\gamma}(s)\,ds

\displaystyle\leq C(n,c,K)\int_{I(y_{1},v_{1})}\,ds\int_{0}^{T(v_{1})}F\big(\gamma(u)\big)\mathcal{A}^{f}_{\gamma}(u)\,du

\displaystyle\leq C(n,c,K)\,\mathrm{diam}(A_{2})\int_{0}^{T(v_{1})}F\big(\gamma(u)\big)\mathcal{A}^{f}_{\gamma}(u)\,du.

By integrating this inequality over the unit sphere in
T_{y_{1}}M
, we see that

\displaystyle\int_{A_{2}}E_{1}(y_{1},y_{2})d\mu_{f}

\displaystyle=\int_{U_{y_{1}}M}\,d\theta_{n-1}\int_{I(y_{1},v_{1})}E_{1}(y_{1},\gamma(s))\mathcal{A}^{f}_{\gamma}(s)\,ds

\displaystyle\leq C(n,c,K)\,\mathrm{diam}(A_{2})\int_{U_{y_{1}}M}\,d\theta_{n-1}\int_{0}^{T(v_{1})}F\big(\gamma(u)\big)\mathcal{A}^{f}_{\gamma}(u)\,du

\displaystyle\leq C(n,c,K)\,\mathrm{diam}(A_{2})\int_{W}F\,d\mu_{f},

where, in the third inequality, we use the fact that any unit minimal geodesics from
y_{1}\in A_{1}
to
y_{2}\in A_{2}
is containd in
W
. Therefore we have

(2.1)

\int_{A_{1}\times A_{2}}E_{1}(y_{1},y_{2})d\mu_{f\times f}\leq C(n,c,K)\,\mathrm{diam}(A_{2})\,\mu_{f}(A_{1})\int_{W}F\,d\mu_{f}.

On the other hand, substituting
t=d(y_{1},y_{2})-u
for
E_{2}(y_{1},y_{2})
yields

(2.2)

\displaystyle=\int_{A_{1}\times A_{2}}E_{1}(y_{2},y_{1})\,d\mu_{f\times f}

\displaystyle\leq C(n,c,K)\,\mathrm{diam}(A_{1})\,\mu_{f}(A_{2})\int_{W}F\,d\mu_{f}.

W=B\left(p_{1},R\right)
for
p_{1}\in M
and
r=\eta\widetilde{D}/4\sqrt{H}
for
\eta<\eta_{*}(H,R,\widetilde{D})
. We put
A_{i}=B(p_{i},r)\subset W
for
i=1,2
. The triangle inequality that for
y_{i}\in A_{i}
with
i=1,2
yields

d(y_{1},y_{2})\leq d(y_{1},p_{1})+d(p_{1},p_{2})+d(p_{2},y_{2})<R-r.

On the other hand, the distance from
y_{1}\in A_{1}
to the boundary of
W
is greater than
R-r
. This means that all unit minimal geodesics from
y_{1}\in A_{1}
to
y_{2}\in A_{2}
lie in
W
. Using Theorem
2.4
, we see that

\displaystyle\quad\inf_{(y_{1},y_{2})\in\overline{A}_{1}\times\overline{A}_{2}}\int_{0}^{d(y_{1},y_{2})}\big((n-1)H-\mathrm{Ric}_{N-}\big)_{+}(\gamma_{y_{1},y_{2}})\,ds

\displaystyle\leq 2rC(n,c,K)\left(\frac{1}{\mu_{f}(A_{1})}+\frac{1}{\mu_{f}(A_{2})}\right)\int_{B(p,R)}\big((n-1)H-\mathrm{Ric}_{N-}\big)_{+}\,d\mu_{f}.

We set

v_{cK,a}(R)=\int_{0}^{R/a}{\bf sn}_{cK}(\tau)^{1/c}\,d\tau.

Theorem
2.2
implies

\dfrac{\mu_{f}\big(B(p_{1},R)\big)}{\mu_{f}\big(B(p_{1},r)\big)}\leq\frac{b}{a}\cdot\frac{v_{cK,a}(R)}{v_{cK,b}(r)}

and Lemma
2.6
gives

C(n,c,K)\leq\widetilde{C}=\frac{{\bf sn}_{cK}\big((R-r)/a\big)^{1/c}}{{\bf sn}_{cK}\big((R-r)/b\big)^{1/c}}.

We observe that

(4.3)

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\quad\inf_{(y_{1},y_{2})\in\overline{A}_{1}\times\overline{A}_{2}}\int_{0}^{d(y_{1},y_{2})}\big((n-1)H-\mathrm{Ric}_{N-}\big)_{+}(\gamma_{y_{1},y_{2}})\,ds\\
&\leq 2rC(n,c,K)\frac{b}{a}\left(\frac{v_{cK,a}\left(R\right)}{v_{cK,b}(r)}\frac{1}{\mu_{f}\big(B(p_{1},R)\big)}+\frac{v_{cK,a}\left(2R\right)}{v_{cK,b}(r)}\frac{1}{\mu_{f}\big(B(p_{2},2R)\big)}\right)\\
&\quad\times\int_{B(p_{1},R)}\big((n-1)H-\mathrm{Ric}_{N-}\big)_{+}\,d\mu_{f}\\
&\leq 2r\widetilde{C}\frac{b}{a}\left(\frac{v_{cK,a}\left(R\right)+v_{cK,a}\left(2R\right)}{v_{cK,b}(r)}\right)\frac{1}{\mu_{f}\big(B(p_{1},R)\big)}\int_{B(p_{1},R)}\big((n-1)H-\mathrm{Ric}_{N-}\big)_{+}\,d\mu_{f},\end{split}

where we use
B(p_{1},R)\subset B(p_{2},2R)
in the second inequality.

We find a unit speed minimal geodesic
\gamma
from
y_{1}\in\overline{A}_{1}
to
y_{2}\in\overline{A}_{2}
that attains the infimum of (
4.3
).
We put
L=d(y_{1},y_{2})
. With the same argument of Theorem
1.7
, we utilize (
3.10
) and take the limit as
\lambda\to\lambda_{0}
again, we see that

\displaystyle\left.\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}\frac{d^{2}L_{i}}{ds^{2}}\right|_{s=0}

\displaystyle\leq-\frac{(n-1)HLa^{\lambda_{0}}}{2}\left(1-\frac{\pi^{2}}{HL^{2}}\widetilde{D}^{2}\right)+\frac{\eta\widetilde{D}}{2\sqrt{H}}\frac{b^{\lambda_{0}+1}\widetilde{C}}{a}\left(\frac{v_{cK,a}\left(R\right)+v_{cK,a}\left(2R\right)}{v_{cK,b}\left(\frac{\eta\widetilde{D}}{4\sqrt{H}}\right)}\right)

\displaystyle\quad\times\frac{1}{\mu_{f}\big(B(p,R)\big)}\int_{B(p,R)}\big((n-1)H-\mathrm{Ric}_{N-}\big)_{+}\,d\mu_{f}.

We set

(4.4)

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\quad\delta_{2}(n,N,K,\varepsilon,a,b,H,R,\eta)\\
&=\frac{H(n-1)(\pi+\frac{\eta}{2})}{\eta\widetilde{C}}\frac{v_{cK,b}\left(\frac{\eta\widetilde{D}}{4\sqrt{H}}\right)}{v_{cK,a}\left(R\right)+v_{cK,a}\left(2R\right)}\left(1-\frac{\pi^{2}}{\left(\pi+\frac{\eta}{2}\right)^{2}}\right)\left(\frac{a}{b}\right)^{\lambda_{0}+1}.\end{split}

If we assume that

\sup_{p\in M}\frac{1}{\mu_{f}\big(B(p,R)\big)}\int_{B(p,R)}\big((n-1)H-\mathrm{Ric}_{N-}\big)_{+}\,d\mu_{f}\leq\delta_{2}(n,N,K,\varepsilon,a,b,H,R,\eta),

then we have

\displaystyle\left.\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}\frac{d^{2}L_{i}}{ds^{2}}\right|_{s=0}

\displaystyle\leq-\frac{(n-1)HLa^{\lambda_{0}}}{2}\left(1-\frac{\pi^{2}}{HL^{2}}\widetilde{D}^{2}\right)

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{(n-1)(\pi+\frac{\eta}{2})\sqrt{H}a^{\lambda_{0}}\widetilde{D}}{2}\left(1-\frac{\pi^{2}}{(\pi+\frac{\eta}{2})^{2}}\right)

\displaystyle=-\frac{n-1}{2}\left(L-\frac{1}{\sqrt{H}}\left(\pi+\frac{\eta}{2}\right)\widetilde{D}\right)\left(Ha^{\lambda_{0}}+\frac{(n-1)\pi^{2}\widetilde{D}a^{\lambda_{0}}\sqrt{H}}{2L(\pi+\frac{\eta}{2})}\right).

Since
\gamma
is a minimal geodesic, it follows

\left.\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}\frac{d^{2}L_{i}}{ds^{2}}\right|_{s=0}\geq 0.

Therefore we obtain

L\leq\frac{1}{\sqrt{H}}\left(\pi+\frac{\eta}{2}\right)\widetilde{D}.

By the triangle inequality, we have

(4.5)

d(p_{1},p_{2})\leq L+2r\leq\frac{\pi+\eta}{\sqrt{H}}\widetilde{D}.

On the other hand, we assumed that

\frac{\pi+\eta}{\sqrt{H}}\widetilde{D}<d(p_{1},p_{2})<R-\frac{3\eta\widetilde{D}}{4\sqrt{H}},

but by (
4.5
), no geodesic starting from
p_{1}\in M
of a length greater than
(\pi+\eta)\widetilde{D}/\sqrt{H}
can be minimal. This completes the proof of the lemma.

\square

.

(3)

The case (
3

4.1
. Thus it is enough to consider the cases (
1
) and (
2

Let
R^{\prime}=R^{\prime}(\eta)>\pi\widetilde{D}/\sqrt{H}
be fixed such that
\eta<\eta_{*}(H,R^{\prime},\widetilde{D})
. For each
p\in M
, we consider the discrete subset
\{x_{i}\}\subset B(p,R^{\prime})
for
i=1,\ldots,T_{2}
such that

(see
[
10
]
*Definition 3.1 for the definition of the
R
-discrete net). We now claim:

Claim 4.2
.

Let
(M,g,\mu_{f})
be an
n
-dimensional complete weighted manifold satisfying the
(N,K,\varepsilon,a,b)
-condition. Set
T_{2}>0
as above. Then it follows

T_{2}\leq\frac{b}{a}\cdot\frac{v_{cK,b}(2R^{\prime}+R)}{v_{cK,a}\left(R/2\right)}.

Proof.

Take
i_{0}\in\{\,1,\ldots,T_{2}\,\}
such that

\mu_{f}\big(B(x_{i_{0}},R/2)\big)=\min_{1\leq i\leq T_{2}}\mu_{f}\big(B(x_{i},R/2)\big).

Since
B(x_{i},R/2)\cap B(x_{j},R/2)=\emptyset
for any

and
B(p,R^{\prime}+R)\subset B(x_{i_{0}},2R^{\prime}+R)

\displaystyle 1

\displaystyle=\frac{\mu_{f}\big(B(p,R^{\prime}+R)\big)}{\mu_{f}\big(B(p,R^{\prime}+R)\big)}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\mu_{f}\big(B(p,R^{\prime}+R)\big)}{\mu_{f}\big(B(p,R^{\prime}+R)\cap\bigcup_{i=1}^{T_{2}}B(x_{i},R/2)\big)}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\mu_{f}\big(B(p,R^{\prime}+R)\big)}{T_{2}\cdot\mu_{f}\big(B(x_{i_{0}},R/2)\big)}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\mu_{f}\big(B(x_{i_{0}},2R^{\prime}+R)\big)}{T_{2}\cdot\mu_{f}\big(B(x_{i_{0}},R/2)\big)}.

By using Theorem
2.2
, we obtain

T_{2}\leq\frac{b}{a}\cdot\frac{v_{cK,b}(2R^{\prime}+R)}{v_{cK,a}(R/2)}.

\Diamond

Then we find that, for any

,

\displaystyle\quad\frac{1}{\mu_{f}\big(B(z,R^{\prime})\big)}\int_{B(z,R^{\prime})}\big((n-1)H-\mathrm{Ric}_{N-}\big)_{+}\,d\mu_{f}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{T_{2}}{\mu_{f}\big(B(z,R^{\prime})\big)}\sup_{x_{i}\in B(z,R^{\prime})}\int_{B(x_{i},R)}\big((n-1)H-\mathrm{Ric}_{N-}\big)_{+}\,d\mu_{f}

\displaystyle\leq T_{2}\frac{b}{a}\frac{1}{\mu_{f}\big(B(z,R^{\prime}+R)\big)}\frac{v_{cK,b}(R^{\prime}+R)}{v_{cK,a}(R^{\prime})}

\displaystyle\quad\times\sup_{x_{i}\in B(z,R^{\prime})}\int_{B(x_{i},R)}\big((n-1)H-\mathrm{Ric}_{N-}\big)_{+}\,d\mu_{f}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{b^{2}}{a^{2}}\frac{v_{cK,b}(2R^{\prime}+R)}{v_{cK,a}(R/2)}\frac{v_{cK,b}(R^{\prime}+R)}{v_{cK,a}(R^{\prime})}

\displaystyle\quad\times\sup_{x_{i}\in B(z,R^{\prime})}\frac{1}{\mu_{f}\big(B(x_{i},R)\big)}\int_{B(x_{i},R)}\big((n-1)H-\mathrm{Ric}_{N-}\big)_{+}\,d\mu_{f},

where we use the estimate of

\displaystyle\quad\sup_{x\in M}\frac{1}{\mu_{f}\big(B(x,R^{\prime})\big)}\int_{B(x,R^{\prime})}\big((n-1)H-\mathrm{Ric}_{N-}\big)_{+}\,d\mu_{f}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{b^{2}}{a^{2}}\frac{v_{cK,b}(2R^{\prime}+R)}{v_{cK,a}\left(R/2\right)}\frac{v_{cK,b}(R^{\prime}+R)}{v_{cK,a}(R^{\prime})}\sup_{x\in M}\frac{1}{\mu_{f}\big(B(x,R)\big)}\int_{B(x,R)}\big((n-1)H-\mathrm{Ric}_{N-}\big)_{+}\,d\mu_{f}.

\displaystyle\quad\delta^{\prime}_{2}(n,N,K,\varepsilon,a,b,H,R,\eta)

5.
Fundamental group under integral curvature bound and

We shall show a finiteness of the fundamental group of
M
. Compared with Corollary
4.4
, we need to assume a slightly strong condition about integral curvature bound.

Corollary 5.1
.

Let
(M,g,\mu_{f})
be an
n
-dimensional complete weighted manifold satisfying the
(N,K,\varepsilon,a,b)
-condition. If there exists
H>0
,
R>\pi\tilde{D}(n,N,\varepsilon,a,b)/\sqrt{H}
such that

\sup_{p\in M}\frac{1}{\mu_{f}\big(B(p,R)\big)}\int_{B(p,R)}\big((n-1)H-\mathrm{Ric}_{N-}\big)_{+}\,d\mu_{f}<\frac{a}{b}\frac{v_{cK,b}(R)}{v_{cK,a}(3R)}\delta_{2},

\delta_{2}=\delta_{2}(n,N,K,\varepsilon,a,b,H,R,\eta_{*})
is given in (
4.4
), then the universal cover of
M
is compact, and hence
\pi_{1}(M)
is a finite group.

Proof.

We find that, by
3R/a\geq R/b
,

\frac{a}{b}\frac{v_{cK,b}(R)}{v_{cK,a}(3R)}<1.

Since
(M,g,\mu_{f})
satisfies the assumption of Corollary
4.4
, we have
\mathrm{diam}(M)<R
.

We denote by
k:\widetilde{M}\to M
the universal Riemannian covering. Set
\tilde{g}=k^{*}g
,
\tilde{f}=f\circ k
. Fix
\tilde{x}\in\widetilde{M}
.
Let
F
be the fundamental domain of
k
that contains
\tilde{x}\in\widetilde{M}
. We find that

\mu_{f}(M)=\mu_{\tilde{f}}(F),\qquad\widetilde{M}=\bigcup_{\alpha\in\Gamma}\alpha F,

where
\Gamma
is the deck transformation group of
\widetilde{M}
.
We set

T=\inf\left\{\#\Gamma_{0}\,\middle|\,B(\tilde{x},R)\subset\bigcup_{\alpha\in\Gamma_{0}}\alpha F\right\}.

Fix
w\in B(\tilde{x},R)\cap\alpha F
and
z\in\alpha F
for each
\alpha\in\Gamma_{0}
. Since

for all

, we see that

Hence this implies

\bigcup_{i=1}^{T}\alpha_{i}F\subset B(\tilde{x},3R)

and we find that

T\cdot\mu_{f}(M)=T\cdot\mu_{\tilde{f}}(F)\leq\mu_{\tilde{f}}\big(B(\tilde{x},3R)\big).

Then it follows from (
5.1
) that

\displaystyle\quad\frac{1}{\mu_{\tilde{f}}\big(B(\tilde{x},R)\big)}\int_{B(\tilde{x},R)}\big((n-1)H-\mathrm{Ric}_{N-}\big)_{+}\,d\mu_{\tilde{f}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{T}{\mu_{\tilde{f}}\big(B(\tilde{x},R)\big)}\int_{F}\big((n-1)H-\mathrm{Ric}_{N-}\big)_{+}\,d\mu_{\tilde{f}}

\displaystyle=\frac{T}{\mu_{\tilde{f}}\big(B(\tilde{x},R)\big)}\int_{M}\big((n-1)H-\mathrm{Ric}_{N-}\big)_{+}\,d\mu_{f}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{b}{a}\frac{v_{cK,b}(3R)}{v_{cK,a}(R)}\frac{T}{\mu_{\tilde{f}}\big(B(\tilde{x},3R)\big)}\int_{M}\big((n-1)H-\mathrm{Ric}_{N-}\big)_{+}\,d\mu_{f}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{b}{a}\frac{v_{cK,b}(3R)}{v_{cK,a}(R)}\frac{1}{\mu_{f}(M)}\int_{M}\big((n-1)H-\mathrm{Ric}_{N-}\big)_{+}\,d\mu_{f}.

Thus we observe that

\displaystyle\quad\sup_{\tilde{x}\in\widetilde{M}}\frac{1}{\mu_{\tilde{f}}\big(B(\tilde{x},R)\big)}\int_{B(\tilde{x},R)}\big((n-1)H-\mathrm{Ric}_{N-}\big)_{+}\,d\mu_{\tilde{f}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{b}{a}\frac{v_{cK,b}(3R)}{v_{cK,a}(R)}\frac{1}{\mu_{f}(M)}\int_{M}\big((n-1)H-\mathrm{Ric}_{N-}\big)_{+}\,d\mu_{f}

\displaystyle<\delta_{2}.

Since
(\widetilde{M},\tilde{g},\mu_{\tilde{f}})
satisfies the assumption of Corollary
4.4
,
\widetilde{M}
is compact and
\pi_{1}(M)
is a finite group.

\square

Corollary 5.2

Integral Quantization for the Discrete Cylinder
Jean Pierre Gazeau and Romain Murenzi

Abstract
. Covariant integral quantizations are based on the resolution of the identity by continuous or discrete families of normalised positive operator valued measures (POVM), which have appealing probabilistic content and which transform in a covariant way. One of their advantages is to allow to circumvent problems due to the presence of singularities in the classical models. In this paper we implement covariant integral quantizations for systems whose phase space is ℤ × 𝕊 1 \mathbb{Z}\times\,\mathbb{S}^{1} , i.e., for systems moving on the circle. The symmetry group of this phase space is the discrete & compact version of the Weyl-Heisenberg group, namely the central extension of the abelian group ℤ × SO ⁡ ( 2 ) \mathbb{Z}\times\,\mathrm{SO}(2) . In this regard, the phase space is viewed as the right coset of the group with its center. The non-trivial unitary irreducible representation of this group, as acting on L 2 ​ ( 𝕊 1 ) L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{1}) , is square integrable on the phase space. We show how to derive corresponding covariant integral quantizations from (weight) functions on the phase space and resulting resolution of the identity. As particular cases of the latter we recover quantizations with de Bièvre-del Olmo-Gonzales and Kowalski-Rembielevski-Papaloucas coherent states on the circle. Another straightforward outcome of our approach is the Mukunda Wigner transform. We also look at the specific cases of coherent states built from shifted gaussians, Von Mises, Poisson, and Fejér kernels. Applications to stellar representations are in progress.



Keywords: Covariant Weyl-Heisenberg integral quantization, discrete cylinder, coherent states, angle operator, quantum mechanics on the circle, Wigner function

Covariant integral quantizations
[
21
]
linearly transform functions (“classical observables”) on phase spaces (in a wider sense) into operators “quantum observables”) on some Hilbert spaces of “quantum states”. They are based on the resolution of the identity by continuous or discrete families of normalised positive operator valued measures (POVM) which transform in a covariant way under some symmetry group actions. In the simplest cases these symmetries are described by the Weyl-Heisenberg group (projective representations of translations in 2d dimensions), or by the affine groups (translations of a subset of variables combined with dilations of the remnant subset of bounded below variables). These quantization methods whose the origin can be traced back to Klauder, Berezin, Toepiltz, are relatively easy to manipulate when compared with geometric or deformation or other quantizations. Beyond their appealing probabilistic content, they allow to circumvent ordering problems, or those due to the presence of singularities in the classical models.

2.
(Covariant) integral quantization: a survey

Let
(X,\mu)
be a measure space and
\mathcal{H}
be a (separable) Hilbert space. An operator-valued function

(2.1)

X\ni x\mapsto\mathsf{M}(x)\ \mbox{acting in}\ \mathcal{H}\,,

resolves the identity operator
\mathbbm{1}
in
\mathcal{H}
with respect to the measure
\mu
if

(2.2)

\int_{X}\mathsf{M}(x)\,\mathrm{d}\mu(x)=\mathbbm{1}

holds in a weak sense.

Integral quantization based on Eq. (
2.2
) is the linear map of a function on
X
to an operator in
\mathcal{H}
which is defined by

(2.3)

f(x)\mapsto\int_{X}f(x)\mathsf{M}(x)\,\mathrm{d}\mu(x):=A_{f}\,,\quad 1\mapsto\mathbbm{1}\,.

If the operators
\mathsf{M}(x)
in Eq. (
2.2
)
are nonnegative and bounded, one says that they form a (normalised) positive operator-valued measure (POVM) on
X
. If they are further
unit trace-class for all
x\in X
, i.e., if the
\mathsf{M}(x)
’s are density operators, then the map

(2.4)

f(x)\mapsto\check{f}(x):=\mathrm{tr}(\mathsf{M}(x)A_{f})=\int_{X}f(x^{\prime})\,\mathrm{tr}(\mathsf{M}(x)\mathsf{M}(x^{\prime}))\,\mathrm{d}\mu(x^{\prime})

is a local averaging of the original
f(x)
(which can very singular, like a Dirac) with respect to the probability distribution on
X
,

(2.5)

x^{\prime}\mapsto\mathrm{tr}(\mathsf{M}(x)\mathsf{M}(x^{\prime}))\,.

This averaging, or semi-classical portrait of the operator
A_{f}
, is in general a regularisation, depending of course on the topological nature of the measure space
(X,\mu)
and the functional properties of the
\mathsf{M}(x)
’s.

Now, consider a set of parameters
\boldsymbol{\kappa}
and corresponding families of POVM
\mathsf{M}_{\boldsymbol{\kappa}}(x)
solving the identity

(2.6)

\int_{X}\mathsf{M}_{\boldsymbol{\kappa}}(x)\,\mathrm{d}\mu(x)=\mathbbm{1}\,,

One says that the
classical limit

f(x)
holds at
\boldsymbol{\kappa}_{0}
if

(2.7)

\check{f}_{{\boldsymbol{\kappa}}}(x):=\int_{X}f(x^{\prime})\,\mathrm{tr}(\mathsf{M}_{\boldsymbol{\kappa}}(x)\mathsf{M}_{\boldsymbol{\kappa}}(x^{\prime}))\,\mathrm{d}\mu(x^{\prime})\to f(x)\quad\mbox{as}\quad\boldsymbol{\kappa}\to\boldsymbol{\kappa}_{0}\,,

where the convergence
\check{f}\to f
is defined in the sense of a certain topology.

Otherwise said,
\mathrm{tr}(\mathsf{M}_{\boldsymbol{\kappa}}(x)\mathsf{M}_{\boldsymbol{\kappa}}(x^{\prime}))
tends to

(2.8)

\mathrm{tr}(\mathsf{M}_{\boldsymbol{\kappa}}(x)\mathsf{M}_{\boldsymbol{\kappa}}(x^{\prime}))\to\delta_{x}(x^{\prime})

where
\delta_{x}
is a Dirac measure with respect to
\mu
,

(2.9)

\int_{X}f(x^{\prime})\,\delta_{x}(x^{\prime})\,\mathrm{d}\mu(x^{\prime})=f(x)\,.

Actually, nothing guarantees the existence of such a limit on a general level. Nevertheless if the semi-classical
\check{f}_{{\boldsymbol{\kappa}}}
might appear as more realistic and more easily manageable than the original
f
, the question is to evaluate the range of acceptability of the parameters
\boldsymbol{\kappa}
.

Let us now assume that
X=G
is a Lie group with left Haar measure
{\rm d}\mu(g)
, and let
g\mapsto U(g)
be a unitary irreducible representation (UIR) of
G
in a Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}
. Let
\mathrm{M}
be a bounded self-adjoint operator on
\mathcal{H}
and let us define
g
-translations of
M
as

(2.10)

\mathrm{M}(g)=U(g)\mathrm{M}U(g)^{\dagger}\,.

Suppose that the operator

(2.11)

R:=\int_{G}\,\mathrm{M}(g)\,\mathrm{d}\mu(g)\,,

is defined in a weak sense. From the left invariance of
\mathrm{d}\mu(g)
the operator
R
commutes with all operators
U(g)
,
g\in G
, and so, from Schur’s Lemma, we have the resolution of the unity up to a constant,

(2.12)

R=c_{\mathrm{M}}\mathbbm{1}\,.

The constant
c_{\mathrm{M}}
can be found from the formula

(2.13)

c_{\mathrm{M}}=\int_{G}\,\mathrm{tr}\left(\rho_{0}\,\mathrm{M}(g)\right)\,\mathrm{d}\mu(g)\,,

where
\rho_{0}
is a given unit trace positive operator.
\rho_{0}
is chosen, if manageable, in order to make the integral convergent. Of course, it is possible that no such finite constant exists for a given
\mathrm{M}
, or worse, it can not exist for any
\mathrm{M}
(which is not the case for square integrable representations).
Now, if
c_{\mathrm{M}}
is finite and positive, the true resolution of the identity follows:

(2.14)

\int_{G}\,\mathrm{M}(g)\,\mathrm{d}\nu(g)=\mathbbm{1}\,,\quad\mathrm{d}\nu(g):=\mathrm{d}\mu(g)/c_{\mathrm{M}}\,.

For instance, in the case of a square-integrable unitary irreducible representation
U:g\mapsto U(g)
, let us pick a unit vector
|\psi\rangle
for which
c_{\mathrm{M}}=\int_{G}{\rm d}\mu(g)|\langle\psi|U(g)\psi\rangle|^{2}<\infty
, i.e
|\psi\rangle
is an admissible unit vector for
U
. With
\mathrm{M}=|\psi\rangle\langle\psi|
the resolution of the identity (
2.14
) provided by the family of states

|\psi_{g}\rangle=U(g)|\psi\rangle
reads

(2.15)

\int_{G}|\psi_{g}\rangle\langle\psi_{g}|\frac{{\rm d}\mu(g)}{c_{\mathrm{M}}}=\mathbbm{1}\,.

Vectors
|\psi_{g}\rangle
are named (generalized) coherent states for the group
G
.

The equation (
2.14
) provides an integral quantization of complex-valued functions on the group
G
as follows

(2.16)

f\mapsto A_{f}=\int_{G}\mathrm{M}(g)f(g)\frac{{\rm d}\mu(g)}{c_{\mathrm{M}}}\,.

Furthermore, this quantization is
covariant
in the sense that:

(2.17)

U(g)A_{f}U(g)^{\dagger}=A_{F}\quad\text{where}\quad F(g^{\prime})=(\mathfrak{U}(g)f)(g^{\prime})=f(g^{-1}g^{\prime})\,,

i.e.,
\mathfrak{U}(g):f\mapsto F
is the left regular representation if
f\in L^{2}(G,{\rm d}\mu(g))
.

For the group
\mathrm{H}^{\mathrm{dc}}_{1}
considered in this paper, an adaption of this material is necessary in the sense that we have to replace the group
\mathrm{H}^{\mathrm{dc}}_{1}
with its right coset
\Gamma=\mathbb{R}\backslash\mathrm{H}^{\mathrm{dc}}_{1}\sim\mathbb{Z}\times\mathrm{SO}(2)
, which amounts to replace its UIR with its projective version.

3.
Overview: Weyl operator acting on
L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{1},\mathrm{d}\gamma)

The group
\mathrm{H}^{\mathrm{dc}}_{1}
is the set of triplets
(s,m,\theta)\in\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{Z}\times\mathbb{S}^{1}
equipped with the following Weyl-Heisenberg-like multiplication:

(3.1)

(s,m,\theta)\,(s^{\prime},m^{\prime},\theta^{\prime})=\left(s+s^{\prime}+\frac{m\theta^{\prime}-m^{\prime}\theta}{2},m+m^{\prime},\theta+\theta^{\prime}\,\mathrm{mod}\,2\pi\right)\,.

The neutral element is
e=(0,0,0)
and the inverse is given by

(s,m,\theta)^{-1}=(-s,-m,-\theta\,\mathrm{mod}\,2\pi)\,.

Let us define the representation of
\mathrm{H}^{\mathrm{dc}}_{1}
acting on
L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{1},\mathrm{d}\gamma)
as:

(3.2)

({V}(s,m,\theta)\psi)(\gamma)=e^{\mathsf{i}\,s}e^{-\mathsf{i}\,\frac{m\theta}{2}}e^{\mathsf{i}\,m\gamma}\psi(\gamma-\theta)\,.

This representation is unitary and irreducible.
Due to the central parameter
s\in\mathbb{R}
, it is not square integrable on the group equipped with its semi-discrete Haar measure
\mathrm{d}(s,m,g)
, i.e., there is no
\psi\in L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{1},\mathrm{d}\gamma)
such that
\sum_{m\in\mathbb{Z}}\int_{\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{S}^{1}}\mathrm{d}s\,\mathrm{d}\theta\,|(\psi,({V}(s,m,\theta)\psi)|^{2}<\infty
. On the other hand it is square integrable on the right coset
\mathbb{R}\backslash\mathrm{H}^{\mathrm{dc}}_{1}=
of
\mathrm{H}^{\mathrm{dc}}_{1}
with its center. This right coset is identified with the phase space or discrete cylinder
\Gamma=\mathbb{Z}\times\,\mathbb{S}^{1}
.
The representation (
3.2
) induces the unitary (
\sim
Weyl) operator
U
defined on
\Gamma
by the section
s=0
on the group, i.e.,
{U}(m,\theta):={V}(0,m,\theta)
:

(3.3)

({U}(m,\theta)\psi)(\gamma)=e^{-\mathsf{i}\,\frac{m\theta}{2}}\,e^{\mathsf{i}\,m\gamma}\psi(\gamma-\theta)\,.

One can show, through the use of the Schur’s Lemma, that for any
\phi
in
L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{1},\mathrm{d}\gamma)
the family
\{{U}(m,\theta)\phi\}_{(m,\theta)\in\Gamma}
constitutes an overcomplete family
resolving the identity in the sense of Eqs. (
2.2
) or (
2.14
) or (
2.15
):

(3.4)

\sum_{m\in\mathbb{Z}}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{1}}\mathrm{d}\theta\,|{U}(m,\theta)\phi\rangle\langle{U}(m,\theta)\phi|=\mathbbm{1}\,.

This implies that for any
\phi
,
\psi
in
L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{1},\mathrm{d}\gamma)
we have:

(3.5)

\sum_{m\in\mathbb{Z}}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{1}}\mathrm{d}\theta\,|{\langle\psi|{U}(m,\theta)\phi\rangle|}^{2}={||\psi||}^{2}{||\phi||}^{2}\,.

The function
\Gamma\ni(m,\theta)\mapsto\phi_{(m,\theta)}:={U}(m,\theta)\phi
is a coherent state (CS) for the group
\mathrm{H}^{\mathrm{dc}}_{1}
in the sense given by Perelomov
[
18
]
, and the function
\phi
is then called a fiducial vector.
The interpretation of the projection of
\psi
on
\phi_{(m,\theta)}
, namely

(3.6)

\langle\,\phi_{(m,\theta)}|\psi\rangle=\int_{\mathbb{S}^{1}}\mathrm{d}\gamma\,\overline{(U(m,\theta)\phi)(\gamma)}\psi(\gamma)=\int_{\mathbb{S}^{1}}\mathrm{d}\gamma\,e^{\mathsf{i}\frac{m\theta}{2}}e^{-\mathsf{i}\,m\gamma}\overline{\phi(\gamma-\theta)}\psi(\gamma)\,,

is the phase space or momentum-angular position representation of
\psi
with respect to the family of coherent states
\phi_{(m,\theta)}
.

Let us now summarize the most important features of the above CS analysis of the elements of
L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{1},\mathrm{d}\gamma)
resulting from the resolution of the identity (
3.4
).

Proposition 3.1
.

Let
L^{2}(\Gamma,\mathrm{d}(m,\theta))
be the Hilbert space of square integrable functions on
\Gamma

For any function
\phi,\psi\in\,L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{1},\mathrm{d}\gamma)
, such that
||\phi||=1
, the map

(3.7)

•

(3.8)

\displaystyle\|\psi\|^{2}=\int_{\mathbb{S}^{1}}\mathrm{d}\gamma\,|\psi(\gamma)|^{2}=\sum_{m\in\mathbb{Z}}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{1}}\mathrm{d}\theta\,|{\langle\phi_{(m,\theta)}|\psi\rangle|}^{2}\,,

•

it can be inverted on its range:

(3.9)

•

its range is a reproducing kernel space:

(3.10)

(3.11)

(3.14)

However, one is mostly interested in considering fiducial vectors that have some “good” localization both in angular position and momentum. One way of doing this is to take the periodized of a square integrable function that has good localization properties on the real line (see

(3.20)

(\mathrm{Per}\,\phi)(x)=2\pi\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\,\phi(x+2\pi\,n)\,,

and the application of the Poisson summation formula reads:

(3.21)

where

(3.22)

\mathcal{F}[\phi](\omega)=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\mathrm{d}\,x\,e^{-\mathsf{i}\,\omega\,x}\phi(x)\,,\quad\phi(x)=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\mathrm{d}\,x\,e^{\mathsf{i}\,x\,\omega}\mathcal{F}[\phi](\omega)\,.

]
, we pick a function
\varpi
, called weight (but not necessarily positive), on the phase space
\Gamma
. We then define the operator
\mathsf{M}^{\varpi}
acting in
L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{1},\mathrm{d}\gamma)
by

(4.1)

\mathsf{M}^{\varpi}=\frac{1}{2\pi}\sum_{m\in\mathbb{Z}}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{1}}\mathrm{d}\theta\,\varpi(m,\theta)\,U(m,\theta)\,.

Proposition 4.1
.

With the assumption that the weight
\varpi
has been chosen such that the operator
\mathsf{M}^{\varpi}
is bounded:

(i)

The operator
\mathsf{M}^{\varpi}
is the integral operator:

(4.2)

(\mathsf{M}^{\varpi}\psi)(\gamma)=\int_{\mathbb{S}^{1}}\mathrm{d}\gamma\,\mathcal{M}^{\varpi}(\gamma,\gamma^{\prime})\psi(\gamma^{\prime})\,,

where the kernel
{\mathcal{M}}^{\varpi}(\gamma,\gamma^{\prime})
is given by:

(4.3)

{\mathcal{M}}^{\varpi}(\gamma,\gamma^{\prime})=\frac{1}{2\pi}\sum_{m\in\mathbb{Z}}\,\varpi(m,\gamma-\gamma^{\prime})\,e^{\mathsf{i}\frac{m(\gamma+\gamma^{\prime})}{2}}\equiv\frac{1}{2\pi}\widetilde{\varpi}_{p_{1}}\left(\frac{\gamma+\gamma^{\prime}}{2},\gamma-\gamma^{\prime}\right)\,.

Here,
\widetilde{\varpi}_{p_{1}}
is the inverse discrete Fourier transform of
\varpi
with respect to the first variable.

(ii)

The operator
\mathsf{M}^{\varpi}
is symmetric if and only the weight satisfies:

(4.4)

\displaystyle\mathsf{M}^{\varpi}={\mathsf{M}^{\varpi}}^{{\dagger}}\Leftrightarrow\overline{\varpi(-m,\gamma)}=\varpi(m,-\gamma)\,.

(iii)

The operator
\mathsf{M}^{\varpi}
is traceclass and its trace is given by

(4.5)

\mathrm{Tr}(\mathsf{M}^{\varpi})=\varpi(0,0)\,.

Proof.

(i)

The action of
\mathsf{M}^{\varpi}
on
\psi
is given by:

\displaystyle(\mathsf{M}^{\varpi}\psi)(\gamma)

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{S}^{1}}\mathrm{d}\gamma^{\prime}\left(\frac{1}{2\pi}\sum_{m\in\mathbb{Z}}\,\varpi(m,\gamma-\gamma^{\prime})e^{\mathsf{i}\frac{m(\gamma+\gamma^{\prime})}{2}}\right)\psi(\gamma^{\prime})

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{S}^{1}}\mathrm{d}\gamma^{\prime}{\mathcal{M}}^{\varpi}(\gamma,\gamma^{\prime})\psi(\gamma^{\prime})\,.

(ii)

The condition that
\mathsf{M}^{\varpi}
be symmetric implies the following condition on the kernel:

\overline{{\mathcal{M}}^{\varpi}(\gamma,\gamma^{\prime})}={\mathcal{M}}^{\varpi}(\gamma^{\prime},\gamma)

which gives:

\overline{\widetilde{\varpi}_{p_{1}}\left(\frac{\gamma+\gamma^{\prime}}{2},\gamma-\gamma^{\prime}\right)}=\widetilde{\varpi}_{p_{1}}\left(\frac{\gamma+\gamma^{\prime}}{2},-(\gamma-\gamma^{\prime}))\right).

(iii)

Therefore the trace of
\mathsf{M}^{\varpi}
corresponds the integral of the kernel over its diagonal, that is:

\int_{\mathbb{S}^{1}}\mathrm{d}\gamma\,{\mathcal{M}}^{\varpi}(\gamma,\gamma)=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{1}}\mathrm{d}\gamma\sum_{m\in\mathbb{Z}}\,\varpi(m,0)e^{\mathsf{i}\,m\gamma}=\frac{1}{2\pi}\sum_{m\in\mathbb{Z}}\,\varpi(m,0)2\pi\delta_{m\,0}=\varpi(0,0).

\square

In turn, one retrieves the weight
\varpi
from the quantization operator
\mathsf{M}^{\varpi}
through a tracing operation.

Proposition 4.2
.

The trace of the operator
{U}^{{\dagger}}(m,\theta)\mathsf{M}^{\varpi}
is given by:

(4.6)

\mathrm{Tr}[U^{{\dagger}}(m,\theta)\mathsf{M}^{\varpi}]=\varpi(m,\theta)

Proof.

To compute this trace one uses the expansion of the kernel in terms of the orthonormal Fourier basis
\left\{e_{n}(\gamma)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}e^{\mathsf{i}\,n\gamma}\right\}
as follows

\displaystyle\mathrm{Tr}[U^{{\dagger}}(m,\theta)\mathsf{M}^{\varpi}]=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\langle\,e_{n}|\,U^{{\dagger}}(m,\theta)\mathsf{M}^{\varpi}e_{n}\rangle=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\langle\,U(m,\theta)\,e_{n}|\,\mathsf{M}^{\varpi}e_{n}\rangle

\displaystyle=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{1}}d\gamma\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\,e^{\mathsf{i}\frac{m\theta}{2}}\,e^{-\mathsf{i}\,m\gamma}\,e^{-\mathsf{i}\,n\,(\gamma-\theta)}\frac{1}{2\pi}\sum_{m^{\prime}\in\mathbb{Z}}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{1}}d\theta^{\prime}\,\varpi(m^{\prime},\theta^{\prime})e^{-\mathsf{i}\frac{m^{\prime}\theta^{\prime}}{2}}e^{\mathsf{i}\,m^{\prime}\gamma}\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\,e^{\mathsf{i}\,n(\gamma-\theta^{\prime})}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{2}}\sum_{m^{\prime}\in\mathbb{Z}}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{1}}d\theta^{\prime}\,\varpi(m^{\prime},\theta^{\prime})2\pi\delta(\theta-\theta^{\prime})2\pi\delta_{m\,m^{\prime}}

\displaystyle=\varpi(m,\theta).

\square

5.
Covariant affine integral quantization from weight function

5.1.
General results

We now establish general formulae for the integral quantization issued from
a weight function
\varpi(m,\theta)
on
\Gamma=\mathbb{Z}\times\mathbb{S}^{1}
yielding the bounded self-adjoint operator
\mathsf{M}^{\varpi}
defined in (
4.1
).
This allows us to build a family of operators obtained from the Weyl operator transport of
\mathsf{M}^{\varpi}
:

(5.1)

\mathsf{M}^{\varpi}(m,\theta)=U(m,\theta)\mathsf{M}^{\varpi}U^{{\dagger}}(m,\theta)\,.

Then, the corresponding integral quantization is given by the linear map:

(5.2)

f\mapsto A^{\varpi}_{f}=\frac{1}{2\pi}\sum_{\mathbb{Z}}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{1}}\mathrm{d}\gamma\,f(m,\theta)\,\mathsf{M}^{\varpi}(m,\theta)\,.

We have the following result.

Proposition 5.1
.

A^{\varpi}_{f}
is the integral operator on
L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{1},\mathrm{d}\gamma)

(5.3)

(A^{\varpi}_{f}\psi)(\gamma)=\int_{\mathbb{S}^{1}}\mathrm{d}\gamma^{\prime}{\mathcal{A}}^{\varpi}(\gamma,\gamma^{\prime})\psi(\gamma^{\prime})\,,

and its kernel is given by

(5.4)

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}_{f}^{\varpi}(\gamma,\gamma^{\prime})=\frac{1}{2\pi}\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}F(\gamma-\gamma^{\prime},n)\varpi(n,\gamma-\gamma^{\prime})e^{\mathsf{i}\,n\frac{(\gamma+\gamma^{\prime})}{2}}\,,

where
F(\varphi,k)
is the Fourier transform of
f
with respect to the angle variable and the inverse (discrete) Fourier transform in the momentum variable,

(5.5)

\displaystyle F(\varphi,n)=\frac{1}{2\pi}\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{1}}\mathrm{d}\theta\,f(k,\theta)e^{\mathsf{i}(k\varphi-n\theta)}

Proof.

The calculation of the kernel of the integral operator
A^{\varpi}_{f}
goes through the following steps.

\displaystyle(A^{\varpi}_{f})(\psi)(\gamma)=\frac{1}{2\pi}\sum_{m\in\mathbb{Z}}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{1}}\mathrm{d}\theta\,f(m,\theta)\,\left(\mathsf{M}^{\varpi}(m,\theta)\psi\right)(\gamma)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{2}}\sum_{m\in\mathbb{Z}}\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{1}}\mathrm{d}\theta\int_{\mathbb{S}^{1}}\mathrm{d}\alpha\,f(m,\theta)\varpi(n,\alpha)\,\left(U(m,\theta)U(n,\alpha)U^{{\dagger}}(m,\theta)\psi\right)(\gamma)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{2}}\sum_{m\in\mathbb{Z}}\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{1}}\mathrm{d}\theta\int_{\mathbb{S}^{1}}\mathrm{d}\alpha\,f(m,\theta)\varpi(n,\alpha)e^{\mathsf{i}(m\alpha-n\theta)}\left(U(n,\alpha)\psi\right)(\gamma)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{2}}\sum_{m\in\mathbb{Z}}\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{1}}\mathrm{d}\theta\int_{\mathbb{S}^{1}}\mathrm{d}\alpha\,f(m,\theta)\varpi(n,\alpha)e^{\mathsf{i}(m\alpha-n\theta)}e^{-\mathsf{i}\frac{n\alpha}{2}}e^{\mathsf{i}\,n\gamma}\psi(\gamma-\alpha)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{2}}\sum_{m\in\mathbb{Z}}\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{1}}\mathrm{d}\theta\int_{\mathbb{S}^{1}}\mathrm{d}\gamma^{\prime}\,f(m,\theta)\varpi(n,\gamma-\gamma^{\prime})e^{\mathsf{i}(m(\gamma-\gamma^{\prime})-n\theta)}e^{-\mathsf{i}\frac{n(\gamma-\gamma^{\prime})}{2}}e^{\mathsf{i}\,n\gamma}\psi(\gamma^{\prime})

\displaystyle\equiv\int_{\mathbb{S}^{1}}\mathrm{d}\gamma^{\prime}\left\{\frac{1}{2\pi}\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}F(\gamma-\gamma^{\prime},n)\varpi(n,\gamma-\gamma^{\prime})e^{\mathsf{i}\,n\frac{(\gamma+\gamma^{\prime})}{2}}\right\}\psi(\gamma^{\prime})

\square

One easily checks that
f=1
gives
F(\varphi,k)=2\pi\delta(\varphi)\delta_{k\,0}
. Inserting this in
(
5.4
)
gives:

(5.6)

\mathcal{A}_{1}^{\varpi}(\gamma,\gamma^{\prime})=\frac{1}{2\pi}\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}2\pi\delta(\gamma-\gamma^{\prime})\delta_{n\,0}\varpi(n,\gamma-\gamma^{\prime})\,e^{\mathsf{i}\,n\frac{(\gamma+\gamma^{\prime})}{2}}=\delta(\gamma-\gamma^{\prime})\varpi(0,0)=\delta(\gamma-\gamma^{\prime})\\

for
\varpi(0,0)=1
.

Covariance in the sense given by Eq. (
2.17
) is easily proven in the present case.

Proposition 5.2
.

The quantization map
f\mapsto A^{\varpi}_{f}
is covariant with respect to the action of the representation
V
, that is,

(5.7)

V\,A^{\varpi}_{f}V^{{\dagger}}=\,A^{\varpi}_{\mathcal{V}f}

where
(\mathcal{V}(s,m,\theta)f)(n,\varphi)=f(n-m,\varphi-\theta)
is the induced action on the phase space.

Let us summarize the above set of results.

Proposition 5.3
.

Let
\varpi(m,\theta)
be a weight function on
\Gamma=\mathbb{Z}\times\mathbb{S}^{1}
obeying

(5.8)

\varpi(0,0)=1\ ,\quad\overline{\varpi(-m,\gamma)}=\varpi(m,-\gamma)\,,

and such that the operator
\mathsf{M}^{\varpi}
defined in (
4.1
) is bounded. It results that the operators
\mathsf{M}^{\varpi}(m,\theta)=U(m,\theta)\mathsf{M}^{\varpi}U^{{\dagger}}(m,\theta)
are bounded self-adjoint, resolve the identity

(5.9)

\frac{1}{2\pi}\sum_{\mathbb{Z}}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{1}}\mathrm{d}\theta\,\mathsf{M}^{\varpi}(m,\theta)=\mathbbm{1}

and yield a covariant integral quantization of functions (or distributions) on the phase space
\Gamma
.

In the sequel we suppose that the choice of a weight function complies with the conditions of Proposition
5.3
.

5.2.
Quantization of separable functions

As a first application, we consider the quantization of a separable function:

(5.10)

f(k,\theta)=g(k)\,h(\theta)\,.

In this case the function
F
in (
5.4
) and the integral kernel read as

(5.11)

F(\gamma-\gamma^{\prime},n)=\frac{1}{2\pi}\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{1}}\mathrm{d}\theta\,g(k)h(\theta)e^{\mathsf{i}(k(\gamma-\gamma^{\prime})-n\theta)}=\tilde{g}(\gamma-\gamma^{\prime})\hat{h}(n)\,,

(5.12)

\mathcal{A}_{gh}^{\varpi}(\gamma,\gamma^{\prime})=\tilde{g}(\gamma-\gamma^{\prime})\frac{1}{2\pi}\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\hat{h}(n)\varpi(n,\gamma-\gamma^{\prime})e^{\mathsf{i}\,n\frac{(\gamma+\gamma^{\prime})}{2}}\,.

5.3.
Quantization of functions of momentum only

The restriction of (
5.11
) and (
5.12
) to a function of momentum only,
f(m,\theta)=g(m)
, yields for the function
F
and the corresponding integral kernel:

(5.13)

F(\gamma-\gamma^{\prime},-n)=\tilde{g}(\gamma-\gamma^{\prime})\delta_{n\,0}\,,

(5.14)

\begin{split}\mathcal{A}_{g}^{\varpi}(\gamma,\gamma^{\prime})&=\frac{1}{2\pi}\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\tilde{g}(\gamma-\gamma^{\prime})\delta_{n\,0}\varpi(n,\gamma-\gamma^{\prime})e^{-\mathsf{i}\,n\frac{(\gamma-\gamma^{\prime})}{2}}\\
&=\frac{1}{2\pi}\tilde{g}(\gamma-\gamma^{\prime})\varpi(0,\gamma-\gamma^{\prime})\,,\end{split}

where
\tilde{g}(\gamma)=\sum_{m\in\mathbb{Z}}g(m)e^{\mathsf{i}\,m\gamma}
.
It results the following action of the operator
A^{\varpi}_{g}
:

(5.15)

(A^{\varpi}_{g}\psi)(\gamma)=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{1}}\mathrm{d}\gamma^{\prime}\tilde{g}(\gamma^{\prime}-\gamma)\varpi(0,\gamma-\gamma^{\prime})\psi(\gamma^{\prime})\,.

Let us give some elementary examples:

•

Angular momentum
g(m)=m
, through integrating by parts and appropriate derivability properties of the weight function,

\displaystyle(A^{\varpi}_{m}\psi)(\gamma)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\pi}\sum_{m\in\mathbb{Z}}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{1}}\mathrm{d}\gamma^{\prime}\left(\frac{\partial}{-\mathsf{i}\partial\gamma^{\prime}}{e^{\mathsf{i}\,m(\gamma-\gamma^{\prime})}}\right)\varpi(0,\gamma-\gamma^{\prime})\psi(\gamma^{\prime})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\pi}\sum_{m\in\mathbb{Z}}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{1}}\mathrm{d}\gamma^{\prime}e^{\mathsf{i}\,m(\gamma-\gamma^{\prime})}\left\{-\mathsf{i}\frac{\partial}{\partial\gamma^{\prime}}-\frac{\partial}{\partial\gamma^{\prime}}[\mathsf{i}\varpi(0,\gamma-\gamma^{\prime})]\right\}\psi(\gamma^{\prime})

\displaystyle=\left(-\varpi(0,0)\,\mathsf{i}\frac{\partial}{\partial\gamma}-\frac{\partial}{\partial\gamma}[\mathsf{i}\varpi(0,\gamma)]_{\gamma=0}\right)\psi(\gamma)=\left(-\mathsf{i}\frac{\partial}{\partial\gamma}-\frac{\partial}{\partial\gamma}[\mathsf{i}\varpi(0,\gamma)]_{\gamma=0}\right)\psi(\gamma)\,,

and so, with the definition

(5.16)

\Omega(\gamma):=\varpi(0,\gamma)\,,\quad\Omega(0)=1\,,

(5.17)

A^{\varpi}_{m}=-\mathsf{i}\frac{\partial}{\partial\gamma}-\frac{\partial}{\partial\gamma}[\mathsf{i}\varpi(0,\gamma)]_{\gamma=0}\equiv L-\Omega^{\prime}(0)\,.

Hence with a weight function obeying
\varpi(0,\gamma)]_{\gamma=0}=\Omega^{\prime}(0)=0
we retrieve the usual angular momentum operator
L=-\mathsf{i}\frac{\partial}{\partial\gamma}
.

•

Square angular momentum
g(m)=m^{2}
. Along similar methods and assumptions on the weight function, we find

\displaystyle(A^{\varpi}_{m^{2}}\psi)(\gamma)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\pi}\sum_{m\in\mathbb{Z}}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{1}}\mathrm{d}\gamma^{\prime}\left(-{\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial\gamma{{}^{\prime\,2}}}}{e^{\mathsf{i}\,m(\gamma-\gamma^{\prime})}}\right)\varpi(0,\gamma-\gamma^{\prime})\psi(\gamma^{\prime})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\pi}\sum_{m\in\mathbb{Z}}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{1}}d\gamma^{\prime}e^{\mathsf{i}\,m((\gamma-\gamma^{\prime})}\{-\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial\gamma^{\prime\,2}}[\varpi(0,\gamma-\gamma^{\prime})\psi(\gamma^{\prime})]

\displaystyle=\left(-\Omega(0)\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial\gamma^{\,2}}+2\Omega^{\prime}(0)\frac{\partial}{\partial\,\gamma}-\Omega^{\prime\prime}(0)\right)\psi(\gamma)\,.

Finally,

(5.18)

A^{\varpi}_{m^{2}}=L^{2}+2\mathsf{i}\frac{\partial}{\partial\gamma}\Omega^{\prime}(0)\,L-\Omega^{\prime\prime}(0)\,.

At this point it is valuable to compare the above quantization of functions defined on the discrete cylinder
\Gamma=\mathbb{Z}\times\mathbb{S}^{1}
with the quantization of the same functions defined on the cylinder
\mathcal{C}:=\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{S}^{1}
, for instance that one using De Bièvre or similar coherent states on the circle (see
[
16
]
and references therein). Both approaches yield similar results (up to an additive constant) for the classical momentum, i.e., the angular momentum, and its square, i.e., the kinetic energy, of a particle moving on the circle. Is it a kind of elementary illustration of Hamiltonian reduction in the sense of Marsden and Weinstein
[
26
]
?

5.4.
Quantization of function of angular position only

We now turn to the quantization of a function of the position only,
f(m,\theta)=h(\theta)
.
From

(5.19)

\tilde{f}(\gamma-\gamma^{\prime},n)=\delta(\gamma-\gamma^{\prime})\hat{h}(n)\,,

we obtain for the integral kernel:

(5.20)

\mathcal{A}_{h}^{\varpi}(\gamma,\gamma^{\prime})=2\pi\delta(\gamma-\gamma^{\prime})\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\hat{h}(n)\,\varpi(n,\gamma-\gamma^{\prime})e^{\mathsf{i}\,n\frac{(\gamma+\gamma^{\prime})}{2}}\,.

This yields the multiplication operator

(5.21)

(A^{\varpi}_{h}\psi)(\gamma)=\left(\frac{1}{2\pi}\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\hat{h}(n)\,\varpi(n,0)e^{\mathsf{i}\,n\gamma}\right)\psi(\gamma):=h^{\varpi}(\gamma)\psi(\gamma)\,.

We observe that the coefficients of Fourier series of the factor
h^{\varpi}(\gamma)
are the those of the original
h
multiplied by
\varpi(n,0)
.

6.
Semi-classical portraits

Given a function
\varpi(m,\theta)
on the phase space
\Gamma
, normalised at
\varpi(0,0)=1
, and yielding a positive unit trace operator, i.e. a density operator,
\mathsf{M}^{\varpi}
, the quantum phase space portrait of an operator
A
on
L^{2}(\Gamma,\mathrm{d}\gamma)
reads as:

(6.1)

\check{A}(m,\theta):=\mathrm{Tr}\left(A\,U(m,\theta)\,\mathsf{M}^{\varpi}U^{{\dagger}}(m,\theta)\right)=\mathrm{Tr}\left(A\,\mathsf{M}^{\varpi}(m,\theta)\right)\,.

The most interesting aspect of this notion in terms of probabilistic interpretation holds when the operator
A
is precisely the quantized version
A^{\varpi}_{f}
of a classical
f(m,\theta)
with the same function
\varpi
(actually we could define the transform with two different ones, one for the “analysis” and the other for the “reconstruction”). Then, with the use of the composition rule (
3.16
), we compute the transform:

\displaystyle f(m,\theta)

\displaystyle\mapsto\check{f}(m,\theta)\equiv\check{A}_{f}^{\varpi}(m,\theta)=\mathrm{Tr}\left(A^{\varpi}_{f}\,\mathsf{M}^{\varpi}(m,\theta)\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\pi}\sum_{m^{\prime}\in\mathbb{Z}}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{1}}\mathrm{d}\theta^{\prime}\,f(m^{\prime},\theta^{\prime})\mathrm{Tr}\left(U(m^{\prime},\theta^{\prime})\mathsf{M}^{\varpi}U^{{\dagger}}(m^{\prime},\theta^{\prime})\,U(m,\theta)\mathsf{M}^{\varpi}U^{{\dagger}}(m,\theta)\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\pi}\sum_{m^{\prime}\in\mathbb{Z}}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{1}}\mathrm{d}\theta^{\prime}\,f(m^{\prime},\theta^{\prime})\mathrm{Tr}\left(\mathsf{M}^{\varpi}\,U(m-m^{\prime},\theta-\theta^{\prime})\mathsf{M}^{\varpi}U(-(m-m^{\prime}),-(\theta-\theta^{\prime}))\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\pi}\sum_{m^{\prime}\in\mathbb{Z}}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{1}}\mathrm{d}\theta^{\prime}\,f(m-m^{\prime},\theta-\theta^{\prime})\mathrm{Tr}\left(\mathsf{M}^{\varpi}\,U(m^{\prime},\theta^{\prime})\mathsf{M}^{\varpi}U^{{\dagger}}(m^{\prime},\theta^{\prime})\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\pi}\sum_{m^{\prime}\in\mathbb{Z}}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{1}}\mathrm{d}\theta^{\prime}\,f(m-m^{\prime},\theta-\theta^{\prime})\mathrm{Tr}\left(\mathsf{M}^{\varpi}\,\mathsf{M}^{\varpi}(m^{\prime},\theta^{\prime})\right)\,.

Finally we get:

Proposition 6.1
.

The semi-classical portrait of the operator
A^{\varpi}_{f}
with respect to the weight
\varpi
is given by:

(6.2)

\check{f}(m,\theta)=\frac{1}{2\pi}\sum_{m^{\prime}\in\mathbb{Z}}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{1}}\mathrm{d}\theta^{\prime}\,f(m-m^{\prime},\theta-\theta^{\prime})\mathrm{Tr}\left(\mathsf{M}^{\varpi}(m^{\prime},\theta^{\prime})\,\mathsf{M}^{\varpi}\right)\,.

This expression, which can be viewed as a convolution on the phase space, has the meaning of an averaging of the classical
f
. The function

(6.3)

\displaystyle(m,\theta)

\displaystyle\mapsto\mathrm{Tr}\left(\mathsf{M}^{\varpi}(m,\theta)\mathsf{M}^{\varpi}\right)\,,

is a true probability distribution on
\Gamma
, i.e., is positive and with integral on
\Gamma
equal to 1, from the resolution of the identity and the fact that
\mathsf{M}^{\varpi}
is chosen as a density operator. Expression (
6.3
) is actually a kind of Husimi function.
The expression of
\mathrm{Tr}\left(\mathsf{M}^{\varpi}(m,\theta)\mathsf{M}^{\varpi}\right)
is easily derived and reads as

(6.4)

\begin{split}\mathrm{Tr}\left(\mathsf{M}^{\varpi}(m,\theta)\mathsf{M}^{\varpi}\right)&=\frac{1}{2\pi}\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\langle\,e_{n}|\left(\,\mathsf{M}^{\varpi}(m,\theta)\mathsf{M}^{\varpi}\right)\,e_{n}\rangle\\
&=\frac{1}{2\pi}\sum_{m^{\prime}\in\mathbb{Z}}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{1}}d\theta^{\prime}|\varpi(m^{\prime},\theta^{\prime})|^{2}e^{\mathsf{i}\,(m\theta^{\prime}-m^{\prime}\theta)}\,.\end{split}

Integrating this expression on
\Gamma
, we get
1
, which means that
\check{1}=1
, as expected.

7.2.
Weight related to the angular parity operator

The weight

8.
Conclusion

In this paper, we have established a covariant integral quantization for systems moving on the circle with integral momentum, i.e, for systems whose phase space is the discrete cylinder
\mathbb{Z}\times\,\mathbb{S}^{1}
.
The symmetry group of this phase space is the discrete & compact version of the Weyl-Heisenberg group, namely the central extension of the abelian group
\mathbb{Z}\times\,\mathrm{SO}(2)
, and the phase space can be viewed as the right coset of the group with its center. The existence of a non-trivial unitary irreducible representation of this group on the phase space, as acting on
L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{1})
, allowed us to derive interesting results. First, we have established the concomitant resolution of the identity and subsequent properties such as the Gabor transform on the circle and its inversion, the reproducing kernel and the fact that any square integrable function on the circle is fiducial vector. Moreover, picking a weight function on the discrete cylinder with suitable properties allows to build a bounded self-adjoint operator on
L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{1})
. The Weyl transported versions of this operator yield a resolution of the identity and the subsequent covariant quantization of functions or distributions defined on the discrete cylinder.

There are noticeable results related to the quantization of a point
(m,\theta)
in the phase space according to two standard choices of the weight.

•

With the parity weight, the quantization of the momentum is the expected angular momentum operator
L
.

(8.1)

m\mapsto\hat{m}=L\,,\quad L\psi(\gamma)=-\mathsf{i}\frac{\partial}{\partial\gamma}\psi(\gamma)\,.

while the quantization of the angle yields the multiplication operator by the angle.

(8.2)

\theta\mapsto\hat{\theta}\,,\quad\hat{\theta}\psi(\gamma)=\gamma\psi(\gamma)\,.

This is of course not acceptable. Alternatively one can quantize the periodized angle function
(\mathrm{Per}\,\mathsf{A})(\theta)
. One finds the multiplication operator defined by the same discontinuous
(\mathrm{Per}\,\mathsf{A})(\gamma)
. There is no regularisation.

•

With the coherent state weight one obtains the quantization of the momentum as the usual
L
plus an additional term, i.e., a kind of covariant derivative on the circle whose topology is now taken into account,

(8.3)

m\mapsto\hat{m}=L+\langle\,m\rangle_{|\psi|^{2}}\,,

whereas the quantization of the periodized function theta leads to its smooth regularisation,

(8.4)

(\mathrm{Per}\,\mathsf{A})(\theta)\mapsto\left[\sum_{m\in\mathbb{Z}}\hat{h}(m)\,\langle e^{-\mathsf{i}m\gamma}\rangle_{|\psi|^{2}}\,e^{\mathsf{i}\,m\gamma}\right]\phi(\gamma)\,.

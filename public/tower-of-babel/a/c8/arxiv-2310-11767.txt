Shimura curves admitting a smooth plane model
Oana Padurariu

Abstract
. We prove that there are no Shimura curves X 0 D ​ ( N ) X_{0}^{D}(N) with N N squarefree of genus g ≥ 2 g\geq 2 admitting a smooth plane model.



1.
Introduction

Modular curves and Shimura curves are central moduli spaces in number theory.
Modular curves parametrize elliptic curves equipped with level structure and Shimura curves parametrize abelian surfaces with quaternionic multiplication. Shimura curves are naturally compact, in contrast to modular curves that are compactified by adding cusps. These cusps are extremely useful in the computations of defining equations for modular curves, which lead to an impressive database of such explicit equations.
On the other hand, there is a scarcity of defining equations for Shimura curves in the literature.

We briefly recall some results
about the classification of Shimura curves. González–Rotger
[
GR06
]
provided defining equations for non-elliptic Shimura curves
X_{0}^{D}(N)
of genus one. Voight
[
Voi09
]
fully classified Shimura curves of genus at most two. Guo–Yang
[
GY17
]
gave a complete list of geometrically hyperelliptic Shimura curves
X_{0}^{D}(N)
with explicit equations. The next step in the classification of Shimura curves is finding equations for all Shimura curves admitting a smooth plane model, which is what Anni–Assaf–Lorenzo-García
[
AALG23
]
achieved for modular curves. A natural question, inspired by
[
AALG23
]
, is whether there are any Shimura curves
X_{0}^{D}(N)
admitting a smooth plane model beyond those of genus at most one. In this paper, we prove the following:

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
X_{0}^{D}(N)
be a Shimura curve for which the level
N
is squarefree. If
X_{0}^{D}(N)
admits a smooth plane model, then the genus of
X_{0}^{D}(N)
is at most one.

The proof relies on the interplay between the genus and the gonality of Shimura curves, and on computations of the number of CM points fixed by Atkin–Lehner involutions.
The paper is accompanied by code
[
Pad
]
written in Magma
[
BCP97
]
.

2.
Some background on Shimura curves

Throughout the paper,
p\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 1}
is a prime number. In this section, we recall some definitions and results about Shimura curves.

2.1.
Quaternion algebras

To define Shimura curves, one first needs to define quaternion algebras, which are extensively covered in
[
Voi21
]
. A
quaternion algebra

B
defined over
\mathbb{Q}
is a central simple
\mathbb{Q}
-algebra of dimension
4
. Alternatively,
B
is a quaternion algebra defined over
\mathbb{Q}
if and only if there are elements
i,j\in B
such that

B\simeq\mathbb{Q}\cdot 1+\mathbb{Q}\cdot i+\mathbb{Q}\cdot j+\mathbb{Q}\cdot ij

where
i^{2},j^{2}\in\mathbb{Q}^{\times}
with
ij=-ji
. An example of a quaternion algebra is
M_{2}(\mathbb{Q})
.
An
order
of
B
is a full
\mathbb{Z}
-lattice which is also a subring.

A very important invariant for a quaternion algebra is the
discriminant
. For any place
v
of
\mathbb{Q}
, we say that
B
is
ramified
at
v
if the completion
B\otimes_{\mathbb{Q}}\mathbb{Q}_{v}
is a division algebra. The set of ramified places of
B
is finite, of even cardinality, and uniquely determines the isomorphism class of a quaternion algebra. Then the discriminant
D
of
B
is defined as

D\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}v\text{ finite}\\
\text{ramified}\end{subarray}}v.

We say that
B
is
indefinite
if
B
is unramified at the archimedean place. Note that in this case
\omega(D)
is even, where

\omega(n)\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\#\{p:p|n\}.

We say that
d
is a
Hall divisor
of
n
if
d|n
and
\gcd(d,n/d)=1
. We denote this by
d\parallel n
.

2.2.
Definition of Shimura curves

Let
B
be an indefinite quaternion algebra defined over
\mathbb{Q}
of discriminant
D
and let
O
denote a maximal order. There are embeddings:

\displaystyle\iota_{\infty}

\displaystyle:B\hookrightarrow M_{2}(\mathbb{R})\simeq B\otimes_{\mathbb{Q}}\mathbb{R}

\displaystyle\iota_{p}

\displaystyle:O\hookrightarrow M_{2}(\mathbb{Z}_{p})\simeq O\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathbb{Z}_{p}

for any prime
p
not dividing
D
. We have a notion of level, similar to the case of modular curves: for any
N\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 1}
coprime to
D
, we define the
Eichler order
of level
N
to be

O_{N}\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\{x\in O\ |\ \iota_{p}(x)\equiv\begin{pmatrix}\ast&\ast\\
0&\ast\end{pmatrix}\ \text{mod}\ p^{e}\ \text{for all}\ p^{e}\parallel N\}.

Let
O_{N}^{1}
be the set of norm one elements of
O_{N}
. Then
O_{N}^{1}
acts on the upper half plane
\mathbb{H}=\{x+iy\ |\ y>0\}
via
\iota_{\infty}(O_{N}^{1})/\{\pm 1\}\leq\PSL_{2}(\mathbb{R})
by

\gamma\cdot z\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\iota_{\infty}(\gamma)\cdot z=\begin{pmatrix}a&b\\
c&d\end{pmatrix}\cdot z=\frac{az+b}{cz+d}.

Consider the Riemann surface

\left(\iota_{\infty}(O_{N}^{1})/\{\pm 1\}\right)\backslash\mathbb{H}.

If
B
has discriminant
D=1
, then this Riemann surface is the classical modular curve
Y_{0}(N).

From now on we shall assume that
B
is not isomorphic to
M_{2}(\mathbb{Q})
, or equivalently that
D>1.
By work of Shimura
[
Shi67
, Main Theorem I]
,
[
Shi70
, Theorem 2.5]
, there is an algebraic curve

X_{0}^{D}(N)

such that there is an open immersion of Riemann surfaces

\left(\iota_{\infty}(O_{N}^{1})/\{\pm 1\}\right)\backslash\mathbb{H}\hookrightarrow X_{0}^{D}(N)

which is a biregular isomorphism. The curve
X_{0}^{D}(N)
has a canonical model defined over
\mathbb{Q}

[
Shi67
, §3]
and it is called a
Shimura curve
of discriminant
D
and level
N
.

As with modular curves, Shimura curves arise naturally as a moduli problem. The curve
X_{0}^{D}(N)
parameterizes pairs
(A,\iota)
, where

•

A
is an abelian surface, and

•

\iota:O_{N}\hookrightarrow\operatorname{End}(A)
is an embedding.

We call such a pair
(A,\iota)
a
QM-surface
. It is well known that Shimura curves have no real points
[
Shi75
, Theorem 0]
,
[
Ogg85
, §3]
, i.e.

X_{0}^{D}(1)(\mathbb{R})=\emptyset.

2.3.
Atkin–Lehner involutions

As in the case of modular curves, Shimura curves also have Atkin–Lehner involutions.
Consider the normalizer group

N_{B_{>0}^{\times}}(O_{N}^{1})\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\{\alpha\in B_{>0}^{\times}\ |\ \alpha^{-1}O_{N}^{1}\alpha=O_{N}^{1}\},

where
B_{>0}^{\times}
are the units in
B
of positive norm. Elements of
N_{B_{>0}^{\times}}(O_{N}^{1})
naturally define an automorphism of the Shimura curve
X_{0}^{D}(N)
, with the scalars
\mathbb{Q}^{\times}
acting trivially. It is straightforward to check that two elements
\alpha,\beta
of
N_{B_{>0}^{\times}}(O_{N}^{1})
define the same automorphism if and only if
\alpha\beta^{-1}\in\mathbb{Q}^{\times}O_{N}^{1}.
We define the group of Atkin–Lehner involutions as follows:

W(D,N)\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}N_{B_{>0}^{\times}}(O_{N}^{1})/\mathbb{Q}^{\times}O_{N}^{1}.

We have an Atkin–Lehner involution
w_{m}
for every Hall divisor
m
of
DN
.
There is an identification

W(D,N)\simeq(\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z})^{\omega(DN)}

and we write
W(D,N)=\{w_{m}\ |\ m\parallel ND\}

[
Voi21
, Ch. 28]
.

2.4.
Genus of Shimura curves

In this subsection we recall an explicit formula for the genus of
X_{0}^{D}(N)
.
For coprime positive integers
D
and
N
such that
D
is squarefree, define

e_{k}(D,N)\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\prod_{p|D}\left(1-\left(\frac{-k}{p}\right)\right)\prod_{q\parallel N}\left(1+\left(\frac{-k}{q}\right)\right)\prod_{q^{2}|N}\nu_{p}(k),

where

\nu_{p}(k)=\begin{cases}2&\quad\text{if }\left(\frac{-k}{q}\right)=1,\\
0&\quad\text{otherwise},\\
\end{cases}

and
\left(\frac{\cdot}{\cdot}\right)

is the Kronecker quadratic symbol.

Remark 2.1
.

The above quantities have geometric meaning, for example:

•

e_{4}(D,N)
is the number of
\mathbb{Z}[i]
-CM points on
X_{0}^{D}(N)
, and also the number of order
2
elliptic points on
X_{0}^{D}(N)
,

•

e_{3}(D,N)
is the number of
\mathbb{Z}\left[\frac{1+\sqrt{-3}}{2}\right]
-CM points on
X_{0}^{D}(N)
, and also the number of order
3
elliptic points on
X_{0}^{D}(N)
.

Let
\varphi
and
\psi
be the multiplicative functions such that

\varphi(p^{k})=p^{k}-p^{k-1},\quad\psi(p^{k})=p^{k}+p^{k-1}.

Proposition 2.1
.

[
Ogg83
, p.280, 301]

For
D>1
, the genus of
X_{0}^{D}(N)
is

g(X_{0}^{D}(N))=1+\frac{\varphi(D)\psi(N)}{12}-\frac{e_{4}(D,N)}{4}-\frac{e_{3}(D,N)}{3}.

Remark 2.2
.

A Shimura curve
X_{0}^{D}(N)
with
N
squarefree is of genus zero if and only if the pair
(D,N)
is in the set:

\{(6,1),(10,1),(22,1)\}.

A Shimura curves
X_{0}^{D}(N)
with
N
squarefree is of genus one if and only if the pair
(D,N)
is in the set:

\{(14,1),(15,1),(21,1),(6,5),(10,3),(33,1),(34,1),(6,7),(46,1),(10,7),(6,13)\}.

Equations for these genus one curves and formulae for their Atkin–Lehner involutions can be found in
[
GR06
, Theorem 3.4]
.

A lower bound for the genus is given by the following:

Lemma 2.1
.

[
Sai22
, Lemma 10.5]

For
D>1
an indefinite rational quaternion discriminant and
N\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 1}
relatively prime to
D
, we have

g(X_{0}^{D}(N))>1+\frac{DN}{12}\left(\frac{1}{e^{\gamma}\log\log(DN)+\frac{3}{\log\log{6}}}\right)-\frac{7\sqrt{DN}}{3},

where
\gamma
is the Euler constant.

2.5.
Gonality of a curve

For a field
K
and a curve
C/K
, the
K
-
gonality

\operatorname{gon}_{K}(C)
is defined to be the least degree of a non-constant morphism
f:C\rightarrow\mathbb{P}^{1}
defined over
K
. For
\overline{K}
an algebraic closure of
K
, the
\overline{K}
-gonality is also called the
geometric gonality
.
We will use the following result to obtain an upper bound on the genus of
X_{0}^{D}(N)
:

Theorem 2.1
.

[
Abr96
, Theorem 1.1]

\frac{21}{200}\left(g(X_{0}^{D}(N))-1\right)\leq\operatorname{gon}_{\mathbb{C}}(X_{0}^{D}(N)).

2.6.
CM points

There is a special type of points on Shimura curve called
CM points
that play a very important rule. As Shimura curves lack cusps, Guo–Yang
[
GY17
]
used CM points to help in the computation of defining equations for all geometrically hyperelliptic Shimura curves
X_{0}^{D}(N)
. Moreover, if a point is fixed by some Atkin–Lehner involution, then it is a CM point.
This observation is one of the main ingredients in the proof of
Theorem 1.1
.

Let
K=\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-d})
be an imaginary quadratic field which admits an embedding

q:K\hookrightarrow B.

Let
R\subset K
be an order. Then
R
is said to be
optimally embedded
in
O_{N}
if we have
q(K)\cap O_{N}=q(R).

For an optimally embedded order
R
, there is exactly one fixed point
z
of the quotient
\left(\iota_{\infty}(O_{N}^{1})/\{\pm 1\}\right)\backslash\mathbb{H}
under the action of
\iota_{\infty}(q(R)).
We say that
z
is a
CM point
for
R
. The set

\operatorname{CM}(R):=\{z\in\left(\iota_{\infty}(O_{N}^{1})/\{\pm 1\}\right)\backslash\mathbb{H}\ |\ \iota_{\infty}(q(R))\cdot z=z,\ q:R\hookrightarrow B\ \text{an optimal embedding}\}

is the set of CM points for
R
. We identify the CM points on
\left(\iota_{\infty}(O_{N}^{1})/\{\pm 1\}\right)\backslash\mathbb{H}
with the corresponding points on
X_{0}^{D}(N)
.

For any
W\leq W(D,N)
, there is the natural quotient map

\pi_{W}:X_{0}^{D}(N)\longrightarrow X_{0}^{D}(N)/W.

We say a point
Q\in X_{0}^{D}(N)/W
is a
CM point
of
X_{0}^{D}(N)/W
if any of its preimages on
X_{0}^{D}(N)
is a CM point.

Remark 2.3
.

CM points on
X_{0}^{D}(N)
correspond to abelian surfaces with extra endomorphisms by the CM field
K
:

\operatorname{End}(A)\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathbb{Q}\simeq M_{2}(K).

We state an alternative description of CM points.
Recall the cover map

X_{0}^{D}(N)\to X_{0}^{D}(1).

A CM point for
R
on
X_{0}^{D}(N)
is the same as a point in the fibre over
[(A,\iota)]\in X_{0}^{D}(1)
such that

\operatorname{End}(A)\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathbb{Q}\simeq M_{2}(K)

and the ring of QM-equivariant endomorphisms of
A
is isomorphic to
R
(cf.
[
Sai22
, §2.2]
).

Theorem 2.2
.

[
Ogg83
, Section 1]

Let
N
be squarefree and
m|DN
.
The set of fixed points of
X_{0}^{D}(N)
under the involution
w_{m}
is

X_{0}^{D}(N)^{\langle w_{m}\rangle}=\begin{cases}\operatorname{CM}(\mathbb{Z}[\sqrt{-1}])\cup\operatorname{CM}(\mathbb{Z}[\sqrt{-2}])&\text{if}\ m=2,\\
\operatorname{CM}(\mathbb{Z}[\sqrt{-m}])\cup\operatorname{CM}\left(\mathbb{Z}\left[\frac{1+\sqrt{-m}}{2}\right]\right)&\text{if}\ m\equiv 3\ \text{mod}\ 4,\\
\operatorname{CM}(\mathbb{Z}[\sqrt{-m}])\ &\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

We would like to compute
\#X_{0}^{D}(N)^{\langle w_{m}\rangle}
. To this end, we need to first understand
\#CM(R)
for an order
R
.

Assume
N
is squarefree. We will use the following notation:

•

\Delta_{R}
be the discriminant of
R
,

•

f
the conductor of
R
,

•

h(R)
the class number of
R
,

•

\big(\frac{K}{p}\big)
be the Kronecker symbol,

•

\big(\frac{R}{p}\big)\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\big(\frac{K}{p}\big)
if
p\nmid f
and
1
otherwise.

The following three quantities are needed in the computation of
\#\operatorname{CM}(R)
:

D(R)\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\prod_{p|D,\ \big(\frac{R}{p}\big)=-1}p,\ \ \ N(R)\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\prod_{p|N,\ \big(\frac{R}{p}\big)=1}p,\ \ \ N^{\ast}(R)\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\prod_{p|N,\ p\nmid f,\big(\frac{R}{p}\big)=1}p.

Theorem 2.3

(
[
Ogg83
, Section 1]
,
[
Jor81
]
,
[
BD96
, Lemma 2.5]
)
.

The set
\operatorname{CM}(R)
is nonempty if and only if
\frac{DN}{D(R)N^{\ast}(R)}|\Delta_{R}
and in this case

\#\operatorname{CM}(R)=2^{\omega(D(R)N(R))}\cdot h(R).

The code that computes

and

3.
Proof of
Theorem 1.1

Assume
X_{0}^{D}(N)
is a Shimura curve with squarefree level
N
that admits a degree
d
smooth plane model over
\mathbb{Q}
. We know that the geometric gonality of a smooth plane curve of degree
d
is
d-1
, see
[
CK90
, Theorem A]
, while the genus-degree formula states that
g(X_{0}^{D}(N))=\frac{(d-1)(d-2)}{2}
. Then using
Theorem 2.1
we obtain:

\frac{21}{200}\left(\frac{(d-1)(d-2)}{2}-1\right)=\frac{21}{200}(g(X_{0}^{D}(N))-1)\leq\operatorname{gon}_{\mathbb{C}}(X_{0}^{D}(N))=d-1,

which implies that
d\leq 21
. Thus the genus of
X_{0}^{D}(N)
is at most
190
.

By Lemma
2.1
, we find that for
DN>110011
, we have
g(X_{0}^{D}(N))>190
. Therefore, we only need to consider the pairs
(D,N)
where
DN\leq 110011
is squarefree. We may consider only those pairs for which
\omega(D)
is even, as the set of ramified places has even cardinality. We further need that

g(X_{0}^{D}(N))\in\left\{\frac{(d-1)(d-2)}{2}:1\leq d\leq 21\right\}.

Thus we are left to analyze
312
pairs
(D,N)
. The discriminant
D
is at most
6990
, while the level
N
is at most
1033
.
Let
(D,N)
be such a pair, for which

g(X_{0}^{D}(N))=\frac{(d-1)(d-2)}{2}

for some
d\in\{1,\cdots,21\}
.

By
Theorem 2.2
, only CM points can be fixed by an Atkin–Lehner involution
w_{m}
. Further, by
Theorem 2.3
, one is able to compute
\#X_{0}^{D}(N)^{\langle w_{m}\rangle}
. We will combine these results with the following:

Theorem 3.1
.

[
HKKO10
, Remark 2.1 (i) and Theorem 2.2 with
n=2
]

Let
C
be a smooth plane curve of degree
d\geq 4
and
\sigma
an involution of
C
. Then the involution
\sigma
has
f=d+\frac{1-(-1)^{d}}{2}
fixed points.

If
X_{0}^{D}(N)
admits a plane model of degree
d\geq 4
, then by
Theorem 3.1
we have that:

(1)

\#X_{0}^{D}(N)^{\langle w_{m}\rangle}=d+\frac{1-(-1)^{d}}{2}\quad\text{for all }\,1<m\parallel DN.

For
298
out of the
312
candidate pairs
(D,N)
we have that
g(X_{0}^{D}(N))\geq 3
, while for the remaining
14
we have
g(X_{0}^{D}(N))\leq 1
.
Running our code
[
Pad
]
on these
298
pairs
(D,N)
we find that Property (
1
) does not hold for any of the curves with
g(X_{0}^{D}(N))\geq 3
. Thus there is no Shimura curve
X_{0}^{D}(N)
with
N
squarefree admitting a smooth plane model of degree
d\geq 4
.
In conclusion, if
X_{0}^{D}(N)
admits a smooth plane model for some
N
squarefree, then
g(X_{0}^{D}(N))\leq 1
.

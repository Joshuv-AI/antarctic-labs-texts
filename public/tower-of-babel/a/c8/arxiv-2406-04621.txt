Abstract
In this paper, we first prove that the mean-field stochastic linear quadratic (MFSLQ for short) control problem with random coefficients has a unique optimal control and derive a preliminary stochastic maximum principle to characterize this optimal control by an optimality system. However, because of the term of the form 𝔼 ⁡ [ A 1 ​ ( ⋅ ) ⊤ ​ Y ​ ( ⋅ ) ] \mathbb{E}[A_{1}(\cdot)^{\top}Y(\cdot)] in the adjoint equation, which cannot be represented in the form 𝔼 ⁡ [ A 1 ​ ( ⋅ ) ⊤ ] ​ 𝔼 ​ [ Y ⁡ ( ⋅ ) ] \mathbb{E}[A_{1}(\cdot)^{\top}]\mathbb{E}[Y(\cdot)] , we cannot solve this optimality system explicitly. To this end, we decompose the MFSLQ control problem into two problems without the mean-field terms, and one of them is a constrained problem. The constrained SLQ control problem is solved explicitly by an extended LaGrange multiplier method developed in this article.



Keywords
: Extended LaGrange multiplier method, mean-field control, linear quadratic control problem, random coefficient, Riccati equation.

Introduction

Let
(\Omega,\mathcal{F},\mathbb{F},\mathbb{P})
be a complete filtered probability space on which an

\begin{cases}dX(s)=[A(s)X(s)+A_{1}(s)\mathbb{E}X(s)+B(s)u(s)]ds\\
\quad\quad\quad\quad+[C(s)X(s)+C_{1}(s)\mathbb{E}X(s)+D(s)u(s)]dW(s),&s\in[0,T],\\
X(0)=x,\\
\end{cases}

(1.1)

where
A(\cdot),A_{1}(\cdot),C(\cdot),C_{1}(\cdot):[0,T]\times\Omega\to\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}

{\cal F}_{t}
-adapted processes, and

is an
{\cal F}_{t}
-adapted process satisfying

\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{T}|u(s)|^{2}ds\linebreak<\infty

In the above,

We define the following cost functional with random coefficients

\displaystyle J\big(u(\cdot)\big)=

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\Big\{\int_{0}^{T}\Big(\langle Q(s)X(s),X(s)\rangle+\langle Q_{1}(s)\mathbb{E}X(s),\mathbb{E}X(s)\rangle

(1.2)

\displaystyle+\langle R(s)u(s),u(s)\rangle\Big)ds+\langle GX(T),X(T)\rangle\Big\},

where

\mathcal{U}=\left\{u(\cdot):[0,T]\times\Omega\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{m}\mid u(\cdot)\text{ is ${\cal F}_{t}$-adapted and }\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{T}|u(t)|^{2}dt<\infty\right\},

Problem (MFSLQ)

Find a control
u^{\ast}(\cdot)\in\mathcal{U}

J\big(u^{\ast}(\cdot)\big)=\inf_{u(\cdot)\in\mathcal{U}}J\big(u(\cdot)\big).

The process

MFSDEs were initially used to describe physical systems involving a large number of interacting particles. The complexity in the dynamics of an SDE is reduced by replacing the interactions of all particles with their expectation.

The main contributions of this paper are as follows.
First, we establish the stochastic maximum principle (SMP for short) for Problem (MFSLQ), including the existence and uniqueness of an optimal control.
More importantly, we propose a new method for studying mean-field control problems. Namely, we decompose
the MFSLQ problem into two problems without mean-field terms. The first one is an SLQ control problem with the constraint that the expectation process is a given deterministic function, while the second is an SLQ with deterministic control process.
Finally, we solve the constrained problem by an extended LaGrange multiplier (ELM for short) method proposed in this article.
We believe that this new approach can be applied to many other mean-field problems.

Similar to Remark

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\gamma}_{0}x(\cdot)+{\gamma}_{1}{\cal L}^{-1}_{1}({\alpha}-{\cal P}x-{\cal L}_{2}{\alpha})(\cdot)+{\gamma}_{2}{\alpha}(\cdot)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left({\gamma}_{0}-{\gamma}_{1}{\cal L}^{-1}_{1}{\cal P}\right)x(\cdot)+\left({\gamma}_{2}+{\gamma}_{1}{\cal L}^{-1}_{1}(I-{\cal L}_{2})\right){\alpha}(\cdot)

\displaystyle\equiv

\displaystyle(\mathcal{P}_{2}x)(\cdot)+(\mathcal{K}_{2}\alpha)(\cdot).

This proves (

\displaystyle 0=

\displaystyle 0=

\displaystyle 0=

Lemma 4.1
.

Let (H1) and (H2) hold. Then the stochastic Riccati equation (
4.8
) admits a unique adapted solution
\big(\Sigma(\cdot),\Psi(\cdot)\big)\in L^{\infty,c}_{\mathbb{F}}(\mathbb{S}^{n})\times L^{2}_{\mathbb{F}}(\mathbb{S}^{n})

such that for some
c>0

D^{\top}\Sigma D+R\geq cI_{n},\;\quad a.e\;on\;[0,T],\;a.s.

Moreover, the linear BSDE (
4.9
) admits a unique solution

\left(\phi(\cdot),\psi(\cdot)\right)\in L^{2,c}_{\mathbb{F}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\times L^{p,2}_{\mathbb{F}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
,
\forall p\in(0,1)
.

Proof.

The existence and unqueness of the solution to the stochastic Riccati equation (
4.8
)
follows directly from Theorem 5.3 in Tang
[
24
]
. The conclusion

\big(\Sigma(\cdot),\Psi(\cdot)\big)\in L^{\infty,c}_{\mathbb{F}}(\mathbb{S}^{n})\times L^{2}_{\mathbb{F}}(\mathbb{S}^{n})
follows from the same theorem.
Further, the estimation of Theorem 5.1 in
[
24
]
implies that
\Psi(\cdot)\in L^{p,2}_{\mathbb{F}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
,
\forall\ p>0
.
Let
\big(X(\cdot),Y(\cdot),Z(\cdot)\big)
be the unique solution of (
2.9
). Define

\phi(s)=Y(s)-\Sigma(s)X(s),

and

\psi=Z-\Bigg\{\Sigma\Big(CX-DR^{-1}\left(B^{\top}Y+D^{\top}Z\right)+C_{1}\alpha\Big)+\Psi X\Bigg\}.

Applying Itô’s formula to
\phi(\cdot)
, it is easy to check that
\left(\phi(\cdot),\psi(\cdot)\right)
is a solution of (
4.9
). Also, we can check that
\big(\phi(\cdot),\psi(\cdot)\big)\in L^{2,c}_{\mathbb{F}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\times L^{p,2}_{\mathbb{F}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
,
\forall\ p\in(0,1)
which follows from

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left(\int^{T}_{0}|\Psi(s)X(s)|^{2}ds\right)^{p}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left(\sup_{s\leq T}|X(s)|^{2p}\left(\int^{T}_{0}|\Psi(s)|^{2}ds\right)^{p}\right)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left(\mathbb{E}\left(\sup_{s\leq T}|X(s)|^{2}\right)\right)^{p}\left(\mathbb{E}\left(\int^{T}_{0}|\Psi(s)|^{2}ds\right)^{p/(1-p)}\right)^{1-p}<\infty.

To prove the uniqueness of the solution to (
4.9
) and (
4.10
), let
\left(X(\cdot),\phi(\cdot),\psi(\cdot)\right)
in

\left(L^{2,c}_{\mathbb{F}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\right)^{2}\times L^{p,2}_{\mathbb{F}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
be a solution of (
4.9
) and (
4.10
). Let
Y=\Sigma X+\phi
and

Z=(I+\Sigma DR^{-1}D^{\top})^{-1}\left((\Sigma C+\Psi)X-\Sigma DR^{-1}Y+\Sigma C_{1}{\alpha}+\psi\right).

Then, for any
p\in(0,1)
,
\left(X(\cdot),Y(\cdot),Z(\cdot)\right)\in\left(L^{2,c}_{\mathbb{F}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\right)^{2}\times L^{p,2}_{\mathbb{F}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
is a solution to FBSDE (
2.9
).

To prove that
Z(\cdot)\in L^{2}_{\mathbb{F}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, we define

\tau_{k}=\inf\left\{t>0:\ \int^{t}_{0}|Z(s)|^{2}ds\geq k\right\}.

Applying Itô’s formula to
|Y(\cdot)|^{2}
, we have

\mathbb{E}|Y(T\wedge\tau_{k})|^{2}-|Y(0)|^{2}=-2\mathbb{E}\int^{T\wedge\tau_{k}}_{0}\left<Y,A^{\top}Y+C^{\top}Z+QX+{\lambda}\right>ds+\mathbb{E}\int^{T\wedge\tau_{k}}_{0}|Z|^{2}ds.

Thus,

\mathbb{E}\int^{T\wedge\tau_{k}}_{0}|Z|^{2}ds\leq\mathbb{E}|Y(T\wedge\tau_{k})|^{2}+K\mathbb{E}\int^{T\wedge\tau_{k}}_{0}\left(|Y|^{2}+|X|^{2}+|{\lambda}|^{2}\right)ds+\frac{1}{2}\mathbb{E}\int^{T\wedge\tau_{k}}_{0}|Z|^{2}ds.

Hence

\mathbb{E}\int^{T\wedge\tau_{k}}_{0}|Z|^{2}ds\leq 2\mathbb{E}|Y(T\wedge\tau_{k})|^{2}+K\mathbb{E}\int^{T\wedge\tau_{k}}_{0}\left(|Y|^{2}+|X|^{2}+|{\lambda}|^{2}\right)ds.

Taking
k\to\infty
on both sides, we obtain that

\mathbb{E}\int^{T}_{0}|Z|^{2}ds\leq 2\mathbb{E}|Y(T)|^{2}+K\mathbb{E}\int^{T}_{0}\left(|Y|^{2}+|X|^{2}+|{\lambda}|^{2}\right)ds<\infty.

Therefore,
\left(X(\cdot),Y(\cdot),Z(\cdot)\right)\in\left(L^{2,c}_{\mathbb{F}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\right)^{2}\times L^{2}_{\mathbb{F}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
is a solution to FBSDE (

). The uniqueness of
\left(\phi(\cdot),\psi(\cdot)\right)
follows from that of
\left(X(\cdot),Y(\cdot),Z(\cdot)\right)
in Lemma
2.4
.

6
Conclusion

In this paper, we studied the MFSLQ control problem with random coefficients. We first established the existence and uniqueness of the optimal control
u^{*}(\cdot)
. Further, we characterized
u^{*}(\cdot)
by an optimality system. However, this system contains terms such as
\mathbb{E}[A_{1}(\cdot)^{\top}{Y}^{*}(\cdot)]

which makes the explicit solution very difficult to obtain. To overcome this hurdle, we decompose the original MFSLQ problem into Problems
1 and 2. Problem 1 is a usual SLQ control problem with the constraint that
\mathbb{E}[X(t)]={\alpha}(t)
for all
t\in[0,T]
, where
{\alpha}(\cdot)
is a deterministic function on
[0,T]
. We introduced an ELM
{\lambda}(\cdot)
to relax the constraint. Consequently, Problem 1 is reduced to a problem
with control variables
u(\cdot)
and
{\lambda}(\cdot)
without any constraint for each fixed
{\alpha}(\cdot)
. After solving Problem 1, Problem 2 becomes a usual SLQ control problem with deterministic control variable
{\alpha}(\cdot)
.
We believe that the ELM method developed in this article can be used in other mean-field control or game problems.
For example, we can consider the
conditional mean-field LQ control problem studied by
[
20
]
and
[
17
]
with random coefficients without assuming the coefficients to be
measurable with respect to the given filtration.

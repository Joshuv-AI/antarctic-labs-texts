Congruences for Siegel modular forms of nonquadratic nebentypus mod p p
Siegfried Böcherer, Toshiyuki Kikuta

Abstract
We prove that weights of two Siegel modular forms of nonquadratic nebentypus should satisfy some congruence relations if these modular forms are congruent to each other. Applying this result, we prove that there are no mod p p singular forms of nonquadratic nebentypus. Here we consider the case where the Fourier coefficients of the modular forms are algebraic integers, and we emphasize that p p is a rational prime. Moreover, we construct some examples of mod 𝔭 \mathfrak{p} singular forms of nonquadratic nebentypus using the Eisenstein series studied by Takemori.

Introduction

Serre
[
15
]
proved that the congruence
f_{1}\equiv f_{2}
mod
p^{r}
(
f_{1}\not\equiv 0
mod
p
)
implies
k_{1}\equiv k_{2}
mod
(p-1)p^{r-1}
, where
p
is an odd prime,
f_{i}
(
i=1
,
2
) are modular forms for
{\rm SL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z})
of weights
k_{i}
.
Later, this property was extended by Katz
[
8
]
to the case of congruence subgroups of the type
\Gamma_{1}(N)
with
p\nmid N
.
In Rasmussen
[
11
]
, the case with algebraic integer Fourier coefficients is studied in detail,
and the case of the group
\Gamma_{0}(p)\cap\Gamma_{1}(N)
is also considered.

All of the above results are for the case of elliptic modular forms.
On the other hand, a generalization to the case of Siegel modular forms was given by Ichikawa
[
6
]
in an algebraic geometrical way and later given in an elementary way by the first author and Nagaoka
[
4
]
.
Here, the groups which they considered are of types
\Gamma(N)
and
\Gamma_{1}(N)
respectively (
p\nmid N
).

The first goal of this paper is to generalize the results mentioned above to the case
of Siegel modular forms
F_{i}
for a group
\Gamma_{0}(p^{m})\cap\Gamma_{1}(N)
(
m\geq 1
,
p\nmid N
) having a nebentypus characters
\chi_{i}
mod
p^{m}
.
The impetus for considering this congruence for weights in the case of nonquadratic nebentypus was to see if it could be applied to discuss the existence of a mod
p
singular modular form of nonquadratic nebentypus. We explain this more precisely.

Over
\mathbb{C}
, singular Siegel modular forms are defined by the nonvanishing of all their Fourier coefficients of maximal rank;
the properties of such modular forms were extensively studied by Freitag (see e.g.
[
5
]
);
in particular, he showed that there are no such modular
forms for
\Gamma_{0}(N)
with nonquadratic character
\chi
mod
N
.

In the works of the authors
[
1
,
2
]
on mod
p
singular forms we only considered the case of quadratic characters.
By applying our results on congruence with respect to weights as above, we find that there is no mod
p
singular form
of nonquadratic nebentypus.
Here we consider the case where the Fourier coefficients of the modular forms are algebraic integers,
and we emphasize that
p
is rational prime.
In fact, there exists a mod
\mathfrak{p}
singular modular form of nonqudratic nebentypus.
We will show that it can be constructed from the Eisenstein series of nebentypus in some cases.

2
Preliminaries

2.1
Siegel modular forms

Let
n
be a positive integer and

\mathbb{H}_{n}
the Siegel upper half space of degree
n
defined as

\mathbb{H}_{n}:=\left\{X+iY\;|\;X,\ Y\in\mathrm{Sym}_{n}(\mathbb{R}),\ Y>0\right\},

where
Y>0
means that
Y
is positive definite, and
\mathrm{Sym}_{n}(R)
is the set of symmetric matrices of size
n
with components in
R
.

The Siegel modular group
\Gamma_{n}
of degree
n
is defined by

\Gamma_{n}:=\left\{\gamma\in\mathrm{GL}_{2n}(\mathbb{Z})\;|\;{}^{t}\hskip-1.0pt\gamma J_{n}\gamma=J_{n}\right\},

where
J_{n}=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}0_{n}&-1_{n}\\
1_{n}&0_{n}\end{smallmatrix}\right)
and
0_{n}
(resp.
1_{n}
)
is the zero matrix (resp. the identify matrix) of size
n\times n
.

We define an action of
\Gamma_{n}
on
\mathbb{H}_{n}
by

\gamma Z:=(AZ+B)(CZ+D)^{-1}
for
Z\in\mathbb{H}_{n}
,
\gamma=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}A&B\\
C&D\end{smallmatrix}\right)\in\Gamma_{n}
.
For a holomorphic function
F:\mathbb{H}_{n}\longrightarrow\mathbb{C}
and a matrix
\gamma=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}A&B\\
C&D\end{smallmatrix}\right)\in\Gamma_{n}
,
we define a slash operator by

F|_{k}\;\gamma:=\det(CZ+D)^{-k}F(\gamma Z).

Let
N
be a positive integer.
In this paper,
we deal with congruence subgroups of
\Gamma_{n}
defined as

\displaystyle\Gamma_{1}(N):=\left\{\begin{pmatrix}A&B\\
C&D\end{pmatrix}\in\Gamma_{n}\>\Big|\>C\equiv 0_{n}\bmod{N},\ A\equiv D\equiv 1_{n}\bmod{N}\right\},

\displaystyle\Gamma_{0}(N):=\left\{\begin{pmatrix}A&B\\
C&D\end{pmatrix}\in\Gamma_{n}\>\Big|\>C\equiv 0_{n}\bmod{N}\right\}.

Here
A
,
B
,
C
,
D
are
n\times n
matrices.

Let
N
be coprime to
p
.
For a natural number
k
and a Dirichlet character

\chi:(\mathbb{Z}/p^{m}\mathbb{Z})^{\times}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}^{\times}
, the space

M_{k}(\Gamma_{0}^{(n)}(p^{m})\cap\Gamma_{1}^{(n)}(N),\chi)

of Siegel modular forms of weight
k
with
character
\chi
(or nebentypus) consists of all of holomorphic
functions
F:\mathbb{H}_{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
satisfying

(F|_{k}\>\gamma)(Z)=\chi(\det D)F(Z)\quad\text{for}\quad\gamma=\begin{pmatrix}A&B\\
C&D\end{pmatrix}\in\Gamma_{0}(p^{m})\cap\Gamma_{1}(N).

If
n=1
, the usual condition in the cusps should be added.
When
\chi
is a trivial character, we write simply
M^{n}_{k}(\Gamma_{0}(p^{m})\cap\Gamma_{1}(N))
for
M^{n}_{k}(\Gamma_{0}(p^{m})\cap\Gamma_{1}(N),\chi)
.

Any
F\in M_{k}(\Gamma_{0}(p^{m})\cap\Gamma_{1}(N),\chi)
has a Fourier expansion of the form

F(Z)=\sum_{0\leq T\in\Lambda_{n}}a_{F}(T)q^{T},\quad q^{T}:=e^{2\pi i{\rm tr}(TZ)},\quad Z\in\mathbb{H}_{n},

where

\Lambda_{n}:=\{T=(t_{ij})\in{\rm Sym}_{n}(\mathbb{Q})\;|\;t_{ii},\;2t_{ij}\in\mathbb{Z}\}.

We put
\Lambda_{n}^{+}:=\{T\in\Lambda_{n}\;|\;T>0\}
.

For a subring
R
of
\mathbb{C}
, let
M^{n}_{k}(\Gamma_{0}(p^{m})\cap\Gamma_{1}(N),\chi)(R)
(resp.
M^{n}_{k}(\Gamma_{0}(p^{m})\cap\Gamma_{1}(N))(R)
)
denote the
R
-module of all modular forms in
M^{n}_{k}(\Gamma_{0}(p^{m})\cap\Gamma_{1}(N),\chi)
(resp.
M^{n}_{k}(\Gamma_{0}(p^{m})\cap\Gamma_{1}(N))
)
whose Fourier coefficients are in
R
.

2.2
Congruences for modular forms

Let
K
be an algebraic number field,
{\mathcal{O}}_{K}
the ring of integers in
K
.
We define
\nu_{\mathfrak{p}}(\gamma):=e_{\mathfrak{p}}
for
\gamma\in K
, where
e_{\mathfrak{p}}\in\mathbb{Z}
is defined by

\gamma{\mathcal{O}}_{K}=\prod\mathfrak{p}^{e_{\mathfrak{p}}}
(prime ideal factorization).
Let
F
be a formal power series of the form

\displaystyle F=\sum_{T\in\Lambda_{n}}a_{F}(T)q^{T}

with
a_{F}(T)\in K
for all
T\in\Lambda_{n}
.
We define

\nu_{\mathfrak{p}}(F):=\inf\{\nu_{\mathfrak{p}}(a_{F}(T))\;|\;T\in\Lambda_{n}\}.

Let
F_{i}=\sum_{T\in\Lambda_{n}}a_{F_{i}}(T)q^{T}
(
i=1
,
2
) be two formal power series as above.
For an ideal
\mathfrak{a}=\prod\mathfrak{p}^{e_{\mathfrak{p}}}
in
{\mathcal{O}}_{K}
, suppose that
\nu_{\mathfrak{p}}(F_{i})\geq 0
for all
\mathfrak{p}
with
\mathfrak{p}\mid\mathfrak{a}
.
We write
F_{1}\equiv F_{2}
mod
\mathfrak{a}
if
\nu_{\mathfrak{p}}(F_{1}-F_{2})\geq e_{\mathfrak{p}}
for all prime ideals
\mathfrak{p}
with
\mathfrak{p}\mid\mathfrak{a}
.

Let
p
be an odd rational prime and
\mathfrak{p}
a prime ideal in
{\mathcal{O}}_{K}
such that
\mathfrak{p}\mid p{\mathcal{O}}_{K}
.
We denote by
e
the ramification index
e=e(\mathfrak{p}/p)
of
\mathfrak{p}
.
Following Rasmussen
[
11
]
, we take the Galois closure
L
of
K

and a prime ideal
\mathfrak{P}
in
{\mathcal{O}}_{L}
with
\mathfrak{P}\mid\mathfrak{p}{\mathcal{O}}_{L}
.
We put

\beta(r):=\max\left\{\Big\lceil\frac{r}{e}\Big\rceil-s-1,0\right\},

where
s:=\nu_{p}(\tilde{e})
and
\tilde{e}:=e(\mathfrak{P}/p)
is the ramification index of
\mathfrak{P}
.
We remark that
\beta(r)=r-1
for
K=\mathbb{Q}
.

Theorem 2.1

(Rasmussen
[
11
]
Theorem 2.16)
.

Let
N
be a natural number with
p\nmid N
and
F_{i}\in M_{k_{i}}^{1}(\Gamma_{0}(p)\cap\Gamma_{1}(N))({\mathcal{O}}_{K})
.
If
F_{1}\equiv F_{2}
mod
\mathfrak{p}^{r}
and
F_{1}\not\equiv 0
mod
\mathfrak{p}
, then we have

k_{1}\equiv k_{2}\bmod{(p-1)p^{\beta(r)}}.

Remark 2.2
.

Rasmussen
[
11
]
assumed
N\geq 3
.
However the statement for
N\leq 2
follows from that for
N\geq 3
, since there is
M
such that
\Gamma_{1}(N)\supset\Gamma_{1}(M)
and
M\geq 3
.

2.3
An Eisenstein series congruent to a constant

Let
p
be an odd rational prime and
\mu_{p-1}
the group of
(p-1)
th root of unity in
\mathbb{C}^{\times}
.
We consider the prime ideal factorization of
p
in the ring
\mathbb{Z}[\mu_{p-1}]
of integers in
\mathbb{Q}(\mu_{p-1})
.
We take a generator
\zeta_{p-1}
of
\mu_{p-1}
.
Let
\Phi(X)\in\mathbb{Z}[X]
be the minimal polynomial of
\zeta_{p-1}
, namely
\Phi(X)
is the cyclotomic polynomial having the root
\zeta_{p-1}
.
Then
\Phi(X)
can be decomposed in the form
\Phi(X)\equiv q_{1}(X)\cdots q_{r}(X)
mod
p
, were
r=\varphi(p-1)
and each
q_{i}(X)
is a polynomial of degree
1
satisfying
q_{i}(X)\not\equiv q_{j}(X)
mod
p
.
If we write
q_{i}(X)=X-d_{i}
with
d_{i}\in\mathbb{Z}
, then we have
d_{i}\not\equiv d_{j}
mod
p
because of
q_{i}(X)\not\equiv q_{j}(X)
mod
p
.
Then
p
is decomposed as a product of
r
prime ideals
\mathfrak{p}_{i}:=(\zeta_{p-1}-d_{i},p)
,
namely we have the perfect decomposition

p\mathbb{Z}[\mu_{p-1}]=\mathfrak{p}_{1}\cdots\mathfrak{p}_{r}=(\zeta_{p-1}-d_{1},p)\cdots(\zeta_{p-1}-d_{r},p).

For this fact, we refer to Washington
[
14
]
, p.15.

We take a prime ideal
\mathfrak{p}_{i}
from above.
We can define a character
\psi_{i}
:
(\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z})^{\times}\to\mathbb{C}^{\times}
so that

\psi_{i}(m)m\equiv 1\bmod{\mathfrak{p}_{i}}

for all
m
with
1\leq m\leq p-1
.
Note that
\psi_{i}(-1)=-1
and
\psi_{i}\neq\psi_{j}
for all
i
,
j
with
i\neq j
.
We shall say that
\psi_{i}
is the character corresponding
\mathfrak{p}_{i}
.

Let
E_{1,\psi_{i}}
be the Eisenstein series of weight
1
for
\Gamma_{0}(p)
with character
\psi_{i}
of the form

E_{1,\psi_{i}}=1-\frac{2}{B_{1,\psi_{i}}}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\left(\sum_{d\mid n}\psi_{i}(d)\right)q^{n},

where
B_{1,\psi_{i}}
is the first generalized Bernoulli number with character
\psi_{i}
described as

B_{1,\psi_{i}}=\frac{1}{p}\sum_{m=1}^{p-1}\psi_{i}(m)m.

Theorem 2.3

(Lang
[
10
]
Theorem 1.2, p.250, Rasmussen
[
11
]
, p.12)
.

We have
\nu_{\mathfrak{p}_{i}}(E_{1,\psi_{i}})\geq 0
and

E_{1,\psi_{i}}\equiv 1\mod{\mathfrak{p}_{i}}.

In other words, if we take
\gamma\in\mathbb{Z}[\mu_{p-1}]
with
\gamma\not\in\mathfrak{p}_{i}
such that

{\mathcal{E}}_{1,\psi_{i}}:=\gamma E_{1,\psi_{i}}\in M_{1}(\Gamma_{0}(p),\psi_{i})(\mathbb{Z}[\mu_{p-1}])
,
then
{\mathcal{E}}_{1,\psi_{i}}\equiv\gamma\not\equiv 0
mod
\mathfrak{p}_{i}
holds.

Remark 2.4
.

(1)

The existence of
\gamma
in the statement follows from the strong approximation theorem for the number fields.

(2)

As we will see later, we have

E_{1,\psi_{i}}\not\equiv 1\mod{p\mathbb{Z}[\mu_{p-1}]}

for any rational prime
p
with
p\geq 5
.
In other words, there exists
\mathfrak{p}_{j}
with
i\neq j
such that

E_{1,\psi_{i}}\not\equiv 1\mod{\mathfrak{p}_{j}},

and equivalently
pB_{1,\psi_{i}}\equiv 0
mod
\mathfrak{p}_{j}
.
We do not know if
\nu_{\mathfrak{p}_{j}}(E_{1,\psi_{i}})\geq 0
to begin with.

(3)

Lang
[
10
]
states that
E_{1,\psi_{i}}\equiv 1
mod
p
and
Rasmussen
[
11
]
states that
E_{1,\psi_{i}}
has a Fourier coefficients in
\mathbb{Z}[\mu_{p-1}]
.
Both statements seem to be inaccurate.

3
Main results and their proofs

3.1
Statements of main results

The first main result concerns congruences for the weights of modular forms of nonquadratic nebentypus.

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
p
be an odd rational prime and
K
a number field including
\mathbb{Q}(\mu_{p-1})
.
Let
\mathfrak{P}
be a prime ideal in
{\mathcal{O}}_{K}
with
\mathfrak{P}\mid p{\mathcal{O}}_{K}
.
For a prime ideal
\mathfrak{p}
in
\mathbb{Z}[\mu_{p-1}]
such that
\mathfrak{p}\mid p\mathbb{Z}[\mu_{p-1}]
and
\mathfrak{P}\mid\mathfrak{p}{\mathcal{O}}_{K}
,
we take the character
\psi
corresponding
\mathfrak{p}
(see Subsection
2.3
).
Let
\chi_{i}
(
i=1
,
2
) be two Dirichlet characters mod
p^{m_{i}}
with conductor
m_{i}^{\prime}
.
We put
m:=\max\{m_{1}^{\prime},m_{2}^{\prime}\}
and take
\alpha_{i}
with
0\leq\alpha_{i}\leq p-2
such that
\chi_{i}^{p^{m-1}}=\psi^{\alpha_{i}}
.
If
F_{i}\in M^{n}_{k_{i}}(\Gamma_{0}(p^{m_{i}})\cap\Gamma_{1}(N),\chi_{i})(\mathcal{O}_{K})
satisfies that
F_{1}\equiv F_{2}
mod
\mathfrak{P}^{r}
and
F_{1}\not\equiv 0
mod
\mathfrak{P}
, then we have

k_{1}\cdot p^{m-1}-\alpha_{1}\cdot p^{r-1}\equiv k_{2}\cdot p^{m-1}-\alpha_{2}\cdot p^{r-1}\bmod{(p-1)p^{\beta(r)}}.

Remark 3.2
.

The theorem above is essentially a statement about characters mod
p
.
In fact, both characters
\chi_{i}^{p^{m-1}}
(
i=1,2
) are defined mod
p
, because
\sharp(\mathbb{Z}/p^{m^{\prime}_{i}}\mathbb{Z})^{\times}=p^{m^{\prime}_{i}-1}(p-1)
and
\chi_{i}^{p^{m-1}}=(\chi_{i}^{p^{m^{\prime}_{i}-1}})^{p^{m-m^{\prime}_{i}}}
(
i=1
,
2
).
Therefore we can take
\alpha_{i}
with
0\leq\alpha_{i}\leq p-2
such that
\chi_{i}^{p^{m-1}}=\psi^{\alpha_{i}}
.

As a consequence of the above results, it can be shown that the characters are somewhat restricted when we consider congruences mod rational prime
p
.

Corollary 3.3
.

(1)

If
F_{i}\in M^{n}_{k_{i}}(\Gamma_{0}(p)\cap\Gamma_{1}(N),\chi_{i})(\mathcal{O}_{K})
satisfies that
F_{1}\equiv F_{2}
mod
p\mathcal{O}_{K}
and
F_{1}\not\equiv 0
mod
\mathfrak{p}
for some
\mathfrak{p}
with
\mathfrak{p}\mid p{\mathcal{O}}_{K}
, then we have
\chi_{1}=\chi_{2}\cdot(*/p)^{t}
for some
t\in\mathbb{Z}
. Here
(*/p)
is the unique nontrivial quadratic character mod
p
.

In particular, if
F_{1}
is of quadratic nebentypus, then
F_{2}
is of quadratic nebentaypus.

(2)

If there exist
{\mathcal{E}}_{k,\chi}\in M^{n}_{k}(\Gamma_{0}(p),\chi)(\mathcal{O}_{K})
and
\gamma\in\mathcal{O}_{K}
such that
{\mathcal{E}}_{k,\chi}\equiv\gamma\not\equiv 0
mod
p\mathcal{O}_{K}
, then
\chi
is quadratic.

Remark 3.4
.

(1)

Actually, in order to prove (1), we can replace the condition
F_{1}\equiv F_{2}
mod
p{\mathcal{O}}_{K}
by a weaker condition
F_{1}\equiv F_{2}
mod
\mathfrak{p}\mathfrak{p}^{\rho}
. Here
\rho
is the complex conjugate in
{\rm Aut}(\mathbb{C})
. See the proof in Subsection
3.3

(2)

From the assertion (2), Remark
2.4
(2) follows.

As mentioned in Introduction, the above results provide a mod
p
analogue of Freitag’s result that there are no singular forms (over
\mathbb{C}
) of nonquadratic nebentypus.
We just write down the simplest case.

For a character
\chi
mod
p
, we put
\mathbb{Q}(\chi):=\mathbb{Q}(\chi(a)\>|\>a\in\mathbb{Z})
.
Let
\mathbb{Z}[\chi]
be the ring of integers in
\mathbb{Q}(\chi)
.

Proposition 3.5
.

Let
\chi
be a character mod
p
and
F\in M_{k}^{n}(\Gamma_{0}(p),\chi)(\mathbb{Z}[\chi])
.
Assume that
F
is strongly mod
p
singular of
p
-rank

, i.e.,

,
a_{F}(T)\equiv 0
mod
p\mathbb{Z}[\chi]
for all

with

, and

mod

for some

.
Then

is a quadratic character.

Remark 3.6
.

We may as well consider mod

in

.
Then our method (see Subsection

) does not work when

Indeed, if we assume the existence of a modular form

just by multiplication by

.
To the authors’ knowledge, the existence of such

is not clear at all except degree

and

(see Theorem

and

]

For the higher degree case, in an unpublished note, Takemori constructed such

).

3.2
Proof of Theorem
3.1

We first give two reductions:

Reduction 1: Decreasing the level to achieve
\boldsymbol{m_{i}=m_{i}^{\prime}}
.

As in the proof of Section 8.2 in
[
2
]
we change
F_{i}
mod
\mathfrak{P}^{r}
to a form of level

\Gamma_{0}(p^{m_{i}^{\prime}})\cap\Gamma_{1}(N)
with same nebentypus character and with a weight
k_{i}^{\prime}

congruent to
k_{i}
modulo a multiple of
(p-1)p^{r}
.
We should keep in mind that
\beta(r)\leq r
always holds.

Reduction 2: Degree
{\boldsymbol{1}}
case is sufficient.

We may apply the method of “integral extract” from
[
4
]
.
We shortly describe the method (and the modifications necessary).
As in
[
4
]
we may associate to
F_{i}
(
i=1
,
2
) as above two elliptic modular forms

f_{i}\in M_{k_{i}}(\Gamma_{1}(p^{m_{i}}NR^{2}))({\mathcal{O}}_{K})
satisfying

f_{1}\equiv f_{2}
mod
\mathfrak{P}^{r}
.
In
[
4
]
, we ignored questions concerning nebentypus characters.
The reduction is achieved by the observation that
f_{i}
has indeed nebentypus character
\chi_{i}
on

\Gamma_{0}(p)\cap\Gamma_{1}(NR^{2})
.

We sketch the modifications necessary for the constriction of an “integral extract” which should keep track of nebentypus characters:

Proposition 3.7
.

For
F\in M_{k}^{n}(\Gamma_{0}(p^{m})\cap\Gamma_{1}(N),\chi)(K)
there exists, for any sufficiently large
R\in\mathbb{N}
an elliptic modular form
f\in M^{1}_{k}(\Gamma_{0}(p^{m})\cap\Gamma_{1}(NR^{2}),\chi)(K)
such that

•

f
is an “integral extract” of
F
, i.e., the Fourier coefficients are finite sums of Fourier coefficients of
F
,

•

\nu_{\mathfrak{p}}(F)=\nu_{\mathfrak{p}}(f)
.

Proof.

We use the construction from
[
4
]
and show that it satisfies the necessary transformation properties for
\Gamma_{0}(p^{m})\cap\Gamma_{1}(NR^{2})
.
The properties for the smaller group
\Gamma_{1}(p^{m}NR^{2})
hold anyway.
We recall from
[
4
]
that
f
is obtained (for a suitable
T_{0}
) from

F^{(R,T_{0})}=\frac{1_{n}}{R^{\frac{n(n+1)}{2}}}\sum_{J}F|_{k}\;\begin{pmatrix}1_{n}&\frac{J}{R}\\
0&1_{n}\end{pmatrix}\cdot e^{-2\pi i{\rm tr}(\frac{T_{0}\cdot J}{R})},

where
J
runs over all symmetric integral matrices mod
R
of size
n
.
Let
\left(\begin{smallmatrix}A&B\\
C&D\end{smallmatrix}\right)\in\Gamma_{0}(p^{m})\cap\Gamma_{1}^{o}(NR^{2})
.
Then, for any integral symmetric matrices
J
of size
n
we have

\begin{pmatrix}1_{n}&\frac{J}{R}\\
0&1_{n}\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}A&B\\
C&D\end{pmatrix}=X\cdot\begin{pmatrix}1_{n}&\frac{J}{R}\\
0&1_{n}\end{pmatrix}

with

X=\begin{pmatrix}A+\frac{J}{R}&-\frac{AJ}{R}-\frac{JCJ}{R^{2}}+B+\frac{JD}{R}\\
C&-\frac{CJ}{R}+D\end{pmatrix}.

Then
X\in\Gamma_{0}(p^{m})\cap\Gamma_{1}(NR^{2})
provided that

We take a prime ideal
\mathfrak{p}_{i}

from Subsection
2.3
.
Let
\omega
be the Teichmüller character on
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
.
Then an embedding from
\mathbb{Q}(\mu_{p-1})
to
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
corresponding to
\mathfrak{p}_{i}

is determined by
\sigma(\zeta_{p-1})=\omega(d_{i})
.
Let
\chi:(\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z})^{\times}\rightarrow\mathbb{Q}(\mu_{p-1})
be a character mod
p

Then we can find
\alpha

0\leq\alpha\leq p-2

\chi^{\sigma}=\omega^{\alpha}

We remark that
\chi=\psi_{i}^{-\alpha}
holds for the character
\psi_{i}
corresponding
\mathfrak{p}_{i}
,
since
\omega(d_{i})\equiv d_{i}\equiv(\psi_{i}^{-1}(d_{i}))^{\sigma}
mod
p
.

Suppose that
\chi
is nonquadratic primitive character mod
p
.
For a positive integer
k
with
k>n+1
,
let
E^{n}_{k}=\sum_{T}a_{k}(T)q^{T}
be the Eisenstein series of weight
k
and degree
n
for
\Gamma_{n}
, and
E^{n}_{k,\chi}=\sum_{T}a_{k,\chi}(T)q^{T}
the Eisenstein series of weight
k
and degree
n
for
\Gamma_{0}(p)
with character
\chi
which studied by Takemori
[
13
]
. Here we assumed that
\chi(-1)=(-1)^{k}
for
E^{n}_{k,\chi}
.
In stead of giving the direct definition of
E^{n}_{k,\chi}
, we just write down its Fourier expansion below.

Suppose that
{}^{t}UTU={\rm diag}(T^{\prime},0_{n-r})
with
T^{\prime}\in\Lambda_{r}^{+}
for some
U\in{\rm GL}_{n}(\mathbb{Z})
.
Recall from Katsurada
[
7
]
and Takemori
[

]
that

\displaystyle a_{k}(T)=2^{[(r+1)/2]}L(1-k,{\boldsymbol{1}})^{-1}\left(\prod_{i=1}^{[r/2]}L(1+2i-2k,{\boldsymbol{1}})^{-1}\right)

\displaystyle~~~~~\times\prod_{q}F_{q}(T^{\prime},q^{k-r-1})\times\begin{cases}1\quad\text{if}\ r\ \text{odd},\\
L(1+r/2-k,\chi_{T^{\prime}})\quad\text{if}\ r\ \text{even},\end{cases}

\displaystyle a_{k,\chi}(T)=2^{[(r+1)/2]}L^{(p)}(1-k,\chi)^{-1}\left(\prod_{i=1}^{[r/2]}L^{(p)}(1+2i-2k,\chi^{2})^{-1}\right)

(4.1)

\displaystyle~~~~~\times\prod_{q\neq p}F_{q}(T^{\prime},\chi(q)q^{k-r-1})\times\begin{cases}1\quad\text{if}\ r\ \text{odd},\\
L^{(p)}(1+r/2-k,\chi_{T^{\prime}}\chi)\quad\text{if}\ r\ \text{even}.\end{cases}

(4.2)

Here we put
L^{(p)}(s,\psi):=(1-\psi(p)p^{-s})L(s,\psi)
,
{\boldsymbol{1}}
is the trivial character mod
1
,
and
F_{q}(T,X)\in\mathbb{Z}[X]
is a polynomial with constant term

calculated by Katsurada
[

]
.

Let

be the Kubota–Leopoldt

-adic

Then
L_{p}(s,\psi)
is a
p
-adic meromorphic (analytic if
\psi\neq{\boldsymbol{1}}
) function on
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
satisfying

\displaystyle L_{p}(1-s,\psi)=-(1-\psi\omega^{-s}(p)p^{s-1})\frac{B_{s,\psi\omega^{-s}}}{s}\quad(s\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 1}),

(4.3)

and

Here we regard as

when

.
On these facts, see Washington

, p.57.

Then we have

\displaystyle L^{(p)}(1-s,\psi\omega^{-s})=L_{p}(1-s,\psi)

for any

.

4.1
Mod
p
-power singular forms of nonquadratic nebentypus

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
l
be a natural number and
p
a regular prime with
p>2l+1
.
We take
\alpha\in\mathbb{Z}
with
1\leq\alpha\leq p-2
(
\alpha\not\equiv 0
mod
\frac{p-1}{2}
) such that
\chi^{\sigma}=\omega^{\alpha}
.
Let
\{l_{\delta,m}\}
(
\delta=0
,
1
) be two sequences of positive integers such that
l+\frac{p-1}{2^{\delta}}
is even and

l_{\delta,m}\to\left(l,l-\alpha+\frac{p-1}{2^{\delta}}\right)\in{\boldsymbol{X}},\quad l_{\delta,m}\to\infty\quad(m\to\infty).

Then we have

\lim_{m\to\infty}(E^{n}_{l_{\delta,m},\chi})^{\sigma}=\widetilde{E}^{n}_{(l,l+(p-1)/2^{\delta})}.

Here
\widetilde{E}^{n}_{(l,l+(p-1)/2^{\delta})}
is the
p
-adic Eisenstein series studied in the authors
[
3
]
.

Proof.

We may assume that
l_{\delta,m}=l+a(m)p^{b(m)}
with
a(m)\equiv-\alpha+(p-1)/2^{\delta}
mod
p-1
,
b(m)\to\infty
(
m\to\infty
).
Recall that

\displaystyle a_{l_{\delta,m},\chi}(T)^{\sigma}=2^{[(r+1)/2]}L^{(p)}(1-l_{\delta,m},\omega^{\alpha})^{-1}\left(\prod_{i=1}^{[r/2]}L^{(p)}(1+2i-2l_{\delta,m},\omega^{2\alpha})^{-1}\right)

\displaystyle~~~~~\times\prod_{q\neq p}F_{q}(T^{\prime},\omega^{\alpha}(q)q^{l_{\delta,m}-r-1})\times\begin{cases}1\quad\text{if}\ r\ \text{odd},\\
L^{(p)}(1+r/2-l_{\delta,m},\chi_{T^{\prime}}\omega^{\alpha})\quad\text{if}\ r\ \text{even}.\end{cases}

We consider the case where
s=l_{\delta,m}
and
\psi\omega^{-s}=\omega^{\alpha}
in the formula (
4.4
).
Then we have
\psi=\omega^{\alpha+l_{\delta,m}}=\omega^{l+(p-1)/2^{\delta}}
and therefore

\displaystyle L^{(p)}(1-l_{\delta,m},\omega^{\alpha})=L_{p}(1-l_{\delta,m},\omega^{l+(p-1)/2^{\delta}}).

It follows that

\displaystyle\lim_{m\to\infty}L_{p}(1-l_{\delta,m},\omega^{l+(p-1)/2^{\delta}})=L_{p}(1-l,\omega^{l+(p-1)/2^{\delta}})=L^{(p)}\left(1-l,(q/p)^{\delta}\right).

This shows

\displaystyle\lim_{m\to\infty}L^{(p)}(1-l_{\delta,m},\omega^{\alpha})=L^{(p)}\left(1-l,(q/p)^{\delta}\right).

Similarly, we have the following
p
-adic limits.

\displaystyle\lim_{m\to\infty}L^{(p)}(1+2i-2l_{\delta,m},\omega^{2\alpha})=\lim_{m\to\infty}L_{p}(1+2i-2l_{\delta,m},\omega^{2l-2i})

\displaystyle~~~~~~~~~~~~~~=L_{p}(1+2i-2l,\omega^{2l-2i})\quad\text{for\ all\ }i\ \text{with}\ 1\leq i\leq[r/2],

\displaystyle\lim_{m\to\infty}L^{(p)}(1+r/2-l_{\delta,m},\chi_{T^{\prime}}\omega^{\alpha})=\lim_{m\to\infty}L_{p}(1+r/2-l_{\delta,m},\chi_{T^{\prime}}\omega^{l+(p-1)/2^{\delta}-r/2}),

\displaystyle~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~=L_{p}(1+r/2-l,\chi_{T^{\prime}}\omega^{l+(p-1)/2^{\delta}-r/2})\quad\text{for\ }r\ \text{even}.

Here, due to the convergence of the factor
L^{(p)}(1+2i-2l_{\delta,m},\omega^{2\alpha})^{-1}
, the condition of regularity on
p
is necessary.
In fact, if
p
is a regular prime, then
L_{p}(1+2i,\omega^{-2i})\neq 0
for all
i
with
i\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 1}

and hence

\lim_{m\to\infty}L^{(p)}(1+2i-2l_{\delta,m},\omega^{2\alpha})=L_{p}(1+2i-2l,\omega^{2l-2i})\neq 0.

This means that
L^{(p)}(1+2i-2l_{\delta,m},\omega^{2\alpha})^{-1}
converges.

We consider the factor
F_{q}(T^{\prime},\omega^{\alpha}(q)q^{l_{\delta,m}-r-1})
.
Since

\displaystyle\omega^{\alpha}(q)q^{l_{\delta,m}-r-1}

\displaystyle\equiv\omega^{\alpha}(q)q^{a(m)p^{b(m)}}q^{l-r-1}

\displaystyle\equiv\omega^{\alpha}(q)\omega^{a(m)}(q)q^{l-r-1}

\displaystyle\equiv\omega^{(p-1)/2^{\delta}}(q)q^{l-r-1}\bmod{p^{b(m)+1}}

and the constant term of
F_{q}(T^{\prime},X)
is
1
,
we have

\displaystyle F_{q}(T^{\prime},\omega^{\alpha}(q)q^{l_{\delta,m}-r-1})\equiv F_{q}(T^{\prime},\omega^{(p-1)/2^{\delta}}(q)q^{l-r-1})\bmod{p^{b(m)+1}}.

(4.5)

This shows

\displaystyle\lim_{m\to\infty}F_{q}(T^{\prime},\omega^{\alpha}(q)q^{l_{\delta,m}-r-1})=F_{q}(T^{\prime},(q/p)^{\delta}q^{l-r-1}).

Summering these formulas, we have the following.

\displaystyle\lim_{m\to\infty}a_{l_{\delta,m},\chi}(T)^{\sigma}=2^{[(r+1)/2]}L^{(p)}(1-l,(q/p)^{\delta})^{-1}\left(\prod_{i=1}^{[r/2]}L_{p}(1+2i-2l,\omega^{2l-2i})^{-1}\right)

\displaystyle~~~~~\times\prod_{q\neq p}F_{q}(T^{\prime},(q/p)^{\delta}q^{l-r-1})\times\begin{cases}1\quad\text{if}\ r\ \text{odd},\\
L_{p}(1+r/2-l,\chi_{T^{\prime}}\omega^{l+(p-1)/2^{\delta}-r/2})\quad\text{if}\ r\ \text{even}.\end{cases}

We need to check that this convergence is uniform in
T
.
The main issue is dependence on the quadratic character
\chi_{T^{\prime}}
.
If the conductor of
\chi_{T^{\prime}}\omega^{l+(p-1)/2^{\delta}-r/2}
is divisible by a prime
q
other than
p
,
then there exists a formal power series
\Phi_{T^{\prime}}(X)\in\mathbb{Z}_{p}[\![X]\!]
such that

L_{p}(s,\chi_{T^{\prime}}\omega^{l+(p-1)/2^{\delta}-r/2})=\Phi_{T^{\prime}}((1+p)^{s}-1)
(cf. Washington
[
14
]
Theorem 7.10).
By this fact and (
4.5
), we see that the convergence is uniform in
T
.

On the other hand, we take a sequence
\{k_{\delta,m}\}
of positive even integers such that
k_{\delta,m}\to(l,l+(p-1)/2^{\delta})\in{\boldsymbol{X}}
(
m\to\infty
).
We can confirm similarly that
L(1-k_{\delta,m},{\boldsymbol{1}})
,
L(1+2i-2k_{\delta,m},{\boldsymbol{1}})
,
F_{q}(T^{\prime},q^{k_{\delta,m}-r-1})
and
L(1+r/2-k_{\delta,m},\chi_{T^{\prime}})
have same
p
-adic limits respectively as above, and also that
\lim_{m\to\infty}F_{p}(T^{\prime},p^{k_{\delta,m}-r-1})=1
.
Therefore we get

\lim_{m\to\infty}(E^{n}_{l_{\delta,m},\chi})^{\sigma}=\lim_{m\to\infty}E^{n}_{k_{\delta,m}}=\widetilde{E}^{n}_{(l,l+(p-1)/2^{\delta})}.

∎

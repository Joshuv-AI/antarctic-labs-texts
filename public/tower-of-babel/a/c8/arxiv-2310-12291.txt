On the topology of conormal complexes and posets of matroids
Anastasia Nathanson, Ethan Partida

Abstract
. We introduce the poset of biflats of a matroid M M , a Lagrangian analog of the lattice of flats of M M , and study the topology of its order complex, which we call the biflats complex. This work continues the study of the Lagrangian combinatorics of matroids, which was recently initiated by work of Ardila, Denham and Huh. We show the biflats complex contains two distinguished subcomplexes: the conormal complex of M M and the simplicial join of the Bergman complexes of M M and M ⟂ M^{\perp} , the matroidal dual of M M . Our main theorems give sequences of elementary collapses of the biflats complex onto the conormal complex and the join of the Bergman complexes of M M and M ⟂ M^{\perp} . These collapses give a combinatorial proof that the biflats complex, conormal complex and the join of the Bergman complexes of M M and M ⟂ M^{\perp} are all simple homotopy equivalent. Although simple homotopy equivalent, these complexes have many different combinatorial properties. We collect and prove a list of such properties.

1.
Introduction

The Bergman fan of a matroid
M
, first defined by Ardila and Klivans
[
AK06
]
, is a simplicial fan with support equal to the tropical linear space associated to the matroid,
\trop(M)
. The work of Ardila, Denham, and Huh
[
ADH23a
]
introduces a Lagrangian analog of the well-studied Bergman fan. This fan, called the
conormal fan
of the matroid, is a simplicial fan with support equal to the product
\trop(M)\times\trop(M^{\perp})
, where
M^{\perp}
is the dual matroid of
M
. For motivation behind the descriptor “Lagrangian” see
[
ADH23a
, Sec. 1.2]
.

The conormal fan
provides an intersection-theoretic interpretation of the
h
-vector of the broken circuit complex of
M
.
Ardila, Denham and Huh used this interpretation to affirm long-standing conjectures on the log concavity of this
h
-vector posed in
[
Daw84
]
,
[
Swa03
]
, and
[
Bry10
]
.
The combinatorics of the conormal fan was further explored by Ardila, Denham and Huh in
[
ADH23
]
.
There, they give a combinatorial proof that certain intersection numbers in the Chow ring of the conormal fan
M
are the
h
-vector of the broken circuit complex of
M
.

We continue the exploration of the Lagrangian combinatorics of matroids by studying the simplicial complex associated to the conormal fan, the
conormal complex
.
Unlike the Bergman complex,
\Delta_{M}
, the conormal complex, denoted
\Delta_{M,M^{\perp}}
, is combinatorially unruly. Although Ardila, Denham, and Huh showed it to be homeomorphic to
\Delta_{M}*\Delta_{M^{\perp}}
, the simplicial join of the Bergman complexes of
M
and its dual
M^{\perp}
, both of which are order complexes of the lattice of flats of their respective matroids, we show that the conormal complex is generally
not flag
and hence cannot be the order complex of a poset.

We study the relationship between the conormal complex
\Delta_{M,M^{\perp}}
and the join of the Bergman complexes
\Delta_{M}*\Delta_{M^{\perp}}
by including both complexes as subcomplexes of a larger simplicial complex. We call this larger complex the
biflats complex
and denote it by
\Delta(\mathbf{BF}_{M,M^{\perp}})
.

Just as the Bergman complex is the order complex of the lattice of flats of a matroid, the biflats complex is the order complex of the
poset of biflats
, denoted
\mathbf{BF}_{M,M^{\perp}}
. A biflat is the Lagrangian analogue of a flat. Each biflat consists of a pair
F|G
of a flat
F
of
M
and dual flat
G
of
M^{\perp}
. We order the set of biflats by inclusion in the first component and reverse inclusion in the second component. This ordering ensures that
\Delta_{M,M^{\perp}}
and
\Delta_{M}*\Delta_{M^{\perp}}
include as subcomplexes of
\Delta(\mathbf{BF}_{M,M^{\perp}})
. However,
\mathbf{BF}_{M,M^{\perp}}
is still a combinatorially difficult object. The biflats poset
\mathbf{BF}_{M,M^{\perp}}
is not ranked and its order complex
\Delta(\mathbf{BF}_{M,M^{\perp}})
is not pure or (non-pure) shellable.

The conormal complex can be defined as a subcomplex of the biflats complex and hence naturally includes as a subcomplex. In contrast, the product of Bergman complexes
\Delta_{M}*\Delta_{M^{\perp}}
is not defined as a subcomplex of the biflats complex. We show that
\Delta_{M}*\Delta_{M^{\perp}}
is indeed isomorphic to a sub order-complex of
\Delta(\mathbf{BF}_{M,M^{\perp}})
which we call the
unmixed biflats complex
and denote by
\Delta(\mathbf{BF}_{M,M^{\perp}}^{u})
.

In our main theorems, we give sequences of elementary collapses to show that the biflats complex
is simple homotopy equivalent to both the conormal complex
and the join of the Bergman complexes of
M
and
M^{\perp}
.

Theorem 1
.

Given a matroid
M
, there is a sequence of elementary collapses, and hence a simple homotopy equivalence, between the biflats complex
\Delta(\mathbf{BF}_{M,M^{\perp}})
and
\Delta_{M}*\Delta_{M^{\perp}}
, the join of the Bergman complexes of
M
and its dual
M^{\perp}
.

Theorem 2
.

Given a matroid
M
, there is a sequence of elementary collapses, and hence a simple homotopy equivalence, between the biflats complex
\Delta(\mathbf{BF}_{M,M^{\perp}})
and the conormal complex
\Delta_{M,M^{\perp}}
.

When
M
is a uniform matroid, we will show that the conormal complex, biflats complex and unmixed biflats complex are all equal as simplicial complexes.

2.
Background

2.1.
Posets and simplicial complexes

Here, we collect a few definitions related to simplicial complexes and posets as we prepare to discuss the central objects of our work.

Given a poset
P
, its
order complex

\Delta(P)
, is the simplicial complex whose faces correspond to chains in the poset.
A simplicial complex
\Delta
is
flag
if all of its inclusion-minimal non-faces are two element sets.
Since partial orders are transitive, every order complex is flag.
For more detailed discussions, we recommend
[
Bjö95
]
and
[
Sta12
]
as references for simplicial complexes and posets, respectively.

For a face
F
of a simplicial complex
\Delta
,

•

the
link
of
F
is the simplicial complex,

\lk_{\Delta}(F)=\{G\in\Delta\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}G\cap F=\emptyset,G\cup F\in\Delta\};

•

the
open star
of
F
is the set of faces,

\str_{\Delta}(F)=\{G\in\Delta\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}F\subseteq G\};

•

the
deletion
of face
F
of
\Delta
is the simplicial complex,

\dell_{\Delta}(F)=\{G\in\Delta\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}F\not\subseteq G\}.

Note that the deletion of face
F
can be equivalently defined as
\dell_{\Delta}(F)\coloneqq\Delta\setminus\str_{\Delta}(F).
The deletion of faces is commutative; that is, for faces
F,G
in a simplicial complex
\Delta
,

\dell_{\dell_{\Delta}(F)}(G)=\dell_{\dell_{\Delta}(G)}(F)=\{H\in\Delta\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}F\not\subseteq H,G\not\subseteq H\}.

Additionally,
the
join
of two disjoint simplicial complexes
\Delta_{1}
and
\Delta_{2}
is the complex

\Delta_{1}*\Delta_{2}\coloneqq\{\sigma\cup\tau\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}\sigma\in\Delta_{1},\tau\in\Delta_{2}\}

The
ordinal sum
of posets
X\oplus Y
is the poset on the disjoint union of elements in
X,Y
such that
p\leq q
either if
p\leq q
in one of the original posets
X
or
Y
, or, if
p\in X
and
q\in Y
.

Lemma 2.1
.

If
P
is the ordinal sum of posets
X\oplus Y
, then its order complex
\Delta(P)
is the join of
\Delta(X)*\Delta(Y)
.

Lemma
2.1
follows immediately from the definition of ordinal sums and order complexes of posets.

Definition 2.1
.

A simplicial complex
\Delta
contains a
cone vertex

v
if
\Delta
can be written as
v*\Delta^{\prime}
where
\Delta^{\prime}
is a subcomplex of
\Delta
. This is equivalent to the condition that all inclusion-maximal faces of
\Delta
contain
v
.

2.2.
Simple homotopy equivalence

Our aim in this paper is to show simple homotopy equivalences between a simplicial complex and certain subcomplexes. One way to do this is by exhibiting a sequence of elementary collapses.

Definition 2.2
.

Let
\sigma
be a
k
-dimensional inclusion-maximal face of a simplicial complex
\Delta
and
\tau
be a
k-1
-dimensional face of
\Delta
. Suppose that
\tau\subset\sigma
and
\tau
is not contained in any other inclusion-maximal face of
\Delta
. Then
\tau
is a
free face
of
\sigma
and the removal of
\tau
and
\sigma
from
\Delta
is called an
elementary collapse
. A sequence of elementary collapses from a complex
\Delta
to a subcomplex
\Delta^{\prime}
shows that
\Delta
is
simple homotopy equivalent
to
\Delta^{\prime}
, which we will denote by
\Delta\simeq\Delta^{\prime}
.

Since simple homotopy equivalence is a refinement of homotopy equivalence, it is a stronger statement.
We make use of the following lemmas on elementary collapses.

Lemma 2.2
.

[
Wel99
, Lemma 2.3]

Any simplicial complex
\Delta
which has a cone vertex
v
is collapsible to
v
.

Lemma 2.3
.

[
Wel99
, Lemma 2.7]

If a simplicial complex
\Delta
has a face
F
for which
\lk_{\Delta}(F)
is collapsible to a vertex
v
in
\lk_{\Delta}(F)
, then
\Delta
is collapsible to
\dell_{\Delta}(F)
.

For any poset
P
, we use
P_{>x}
to denote the subposet that contains all elements
y\in P
such that
y>x
in
P
and
P_{<x}
to mean the subposet consisting of all
y\in P
such that
y<x
.

Lemma 2.4
.

If
P
is a poset and
x\in P
such that
P_{>x}
has a minimal element then there exists a sequence of elementary collapses from
\Delta(P)
onto
\Delta(P\setminus\{x\})
.

Proof.

Observe that
\lk_{\Delta(P)}(x)=\Delta\left(P_{>x}\right)*\Delta\left(P_{<x}\right)
. This is because we can decompose any chain
C
of
P
containing
x
as

C=C_{<x}<x<C_{>x}

where
C_{<x}
and
C_{>x}
are chains of elements less than
x
and greater than
x
, respectively. If
P_{>x}
has a minimal element, then its order complex
\Delta(P_{>x})
has a cone vertex and
\Delta\left(P_{>x}\right)*\Delta\left(P_{<x}\right)
will have a cone vertex as well. By Lemma
2.3
, this means there exists a sequence of elementary collapses from
\Delta(P)
onto
\dell_{\Delta(P)}(x)=\Delta(P\setminus\{x\})
. ∎

2.3.
Matroids and their flats

Broadly, we study order complexes defined by the data of a matroid
M
.
We will think of a matroid as a tuple
M=(E,\mathbf{F})
given by a finite ground set
E
and a collection of flats
\mathbf{F}\subseteq 2^{E}
satisfying certain axioms. When ordered by inclusion, the collection of flats
(\mathbf{F},\subseteq)
forms a geometric lattice; in an abuse of notation,
we will use
\mathbf{F}
to refer to both the lattice and the set of flats. A flat
F\in\mathbf{F}
is
proper
if
F
is not the ground set
E
, and is a
loop
if it is inclusion-minimal and is not the empty set.

The dual matroid,
M^{\perp}=(E,\mathbf{F^{\perp}}
), is a matroid defined on the same ground set with the collection of its flats, denoted as
\mathbf{F^{\perp}}
, called
dual flats
. A loop of a matroid is a
coloop
of the dual matroid. We refer the reader to
[
Oxl92
]
for the basics of matroid theory and Sections
5
and
6
of
[
Ard23
]
for a friendly introduction to the geometry of Bergman and conormal fans.

Each matroid
M
is equipped with a
rank
function
\rk_{M}\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}2^{E}\to\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
. This function has the property that, for a flat
F\in\mathbf{F}
,
\rk_{M}(F)
is equal to the largest length of chain in
\mathbf{F}
whose top element is
F
. For an arbitrary subset
S\subset E
,
\rk_{M}(S)
is equal to
\rk_{M}(F)
where
F
is a minimal flat containing
S
. The dual matroid
M^{\perp}
also has a rank function
\rk_{M^{\perp}}\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}2^{E}\to\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
. If
F
is a flat of
M
then
\rk(F)
will refer to
\rk_{M}(F)
and similarly
\rk(G)=\rk_{M^{\perp}}(G)
whenever
G
is a dual flat. We also define the corank of a flat
F
to be equal to
\cork(F)\coloneqq\rk(M)-\rk(F)
.

The rank of a matroid
\rk(M)
is the equal to the rank of the ground set
\rk_{M}(E)
and we denote this as
r
. The rank of a dual matroid
\rk_{M^{\perp}}(E)
will be denoted as
r^{\perp}
. Notably, the ranks of the matroid and its dual sum to the size of the ground set:

\rk_{M}(E)+\rk_{M^{\perp}}(E)=r+r^{\perp}=\mathinner{\!\left\lvert E\right\rvert}.

Throughout this paper, we will refer to
graphic matroids
as examples. The ground set
E
of a graphic matroid is given by the edge set of a graph. The flats of any graphic matroid
\mathbf{F}
are the edge subsets
F\subseteq E
which are transitively closed: if
F
contains a path of edges from vertex
v
to vertex
v^{\prime}
, and if
e=\{v,v^{\prime}\}\in E
, then
e\in F
. When a matroid is defined by a planar graphic matroid, its dual matroid is the matroid defined by the dual graph.

2.4.
Lagrangian analogue to flats

The Langiangian theory of matroids was started by the work of
Ardila, Denham, and Huh in
[
ADH23a
]
and further continued in
[
ADH23
]
. Most important to us is the Lagrangian analogue of a flat of a matroid.

Definition 2.4
.

Given a loopless and coloopless matroid
M
,
a
biflat

F|G
consists of a flat
F\in\mathbf{F}
and a dual flat
G\in\mathbf{F^{\perp}}
such that both are nonempty, at least one of them is proper and

F\cup G=E.

We denote the set of all biflats as
\mathbf{BF}_{M,M^{\perp}}
, dropping the subscript when the matroid is clear.

Note that the last condition in the definition above can be recharacterized by saying that
F|G
is a biflat if the closure of the complement of the flat
F
in
M^{\perp}
, denoted
\mathrm{cl}_{M^{\perp}}(F^{C})
, is contained within the dual flat
G
.
Just as flats can be ordered by inclusion, biflats can be ordered by inclusion conditions.

Definition 2.5
.

The
poset of biflats
of a matroid
M
, denoted
\mathbf{BF}_{M,M^{\perp}}
, is the partial ordering of the set of biflats of
M
by the condition that

F|G\leq F^{\prime}|G^{\prime}\text{ if and only if }F\subseteq F^{\prime}\text{ and }G\supseteq G^{\prime}.

We call a chain
\mathcal{F}|\mathcal{G}
in
\mathbf{BF}_{M,M^{\perp}}
a
bichain
.

Under this order, every biflat is preceded by
\emptyset|E
and precedes

E|\emptyset
. While these are not biflats themselves, these “pseudo-biflats” can always be attached at the beginning and end of bichains.
That is, for any bichain
\mathcal{F}|\mathcal{G}=\{F_{1}|G_{1}\leq\ldots\leq F_{\ell}|G_{\ell}\},
it is customary to assume that
F_{0}|G_{0}=\emptyset|E
and
F_{\ell+1}|G_{\ell+1}=E|\emptyset
.

Definition 2.6
.

The
biflats complex

\Delta\left(\mathbf{BF}_{M,M^{\perp}}\right)
is the order complex of the poset of biflats.

2.5.
Unmixed biflats complex

We discuss the
unmixed
biflats of a matroid. The unmixed biflats are a type of biflat first defined in
Section 4.3 of
[
ADH23
]
. We use the subposet formed by them to define the
unmixed biflats complex
.

Definition 2.7
.

A biflat
F|G
is
mixed
if both
F
is a proper flat of
M
and
G
is a proper flat of
M^{\perp}
. Otherwise, we say the biflat
F|G
is
unmixed
.
We denote the set of unmixed biflats as

\mathbf{BF}_{M,M^{\perp}}^{u}\coloneqq\{F|G\in\mathbf{BF}_{M,M^{\perp}}\colon F=E\text{ or }G=E\}.

As
\mathbf{BF}_{M,M^{\perp}}^{u}
is a subset of
\mathbf{BF}_{M,M^{\perp}}
, the set of unmixed biflats inherits a poset structure. Once again, the set and the poset share the same notation. We call the order complex of
\mathbf{BF}_{M,M^{\perp}}^{u}
the
unmixed biflats complex
and denote it by
\Delta(\mathbf{BF}_{M,M^{\perp}}^{u})
.

2.6.
Conormal complex

Lastly, we define the
conormal complex

\Delta_{M,M^{\perp}}
. This is the simplicial complex associated to the
conormal fan
. We provide a more an in-depth study of the conormal fan in Section
2.7
. We again refer to
[
ADH23a
]
and
[
ADH23
]
as sources for our definitions.

Definition 2.8
.

A
biflag
,
\mathcal{F}|\mathcal{G}
, of a matroid
M
is a bichain satisfying the restriction that

\bigcup_{F|G\in\mathcal{F}|\mathcal{G}}F\cap G\neq E.

The
conormal complex

\Delta_{M,M^{\perp}}
of a matroid is the simplicial complex whose faces are biflags of
M
.

It is shown in Proposition
2.15
of

2.7.
Fan structures

The conormal complex
\Delta_{M,M^{\perp}}
of a matroid
M
is the simplicial complex associated to the
conormal fan
of
M
, denoted
\Sigma_{M,M^{\perp}}
, which was introduced and studied by Ardila, Denham and Huh in
[
ADH23a
]
and
[
ADH23
]
. In this section, we discuss how the conormal fan interacts with the rest of this paper.

The conormal fan is a restriction of a larger fan called the
bipermutohedral fan

\Sigma_{E,E}
. In this section, we give a short introduction to both of these fans.
Later, we
use the machinery introduced here
to provide a quick proof that
\Delta_{M,M^{\perp}}
is homeomorphic to
\Delta_{M}*\Delta_{M^{\perp}}
, see Section
3
, and to motivate one of our further questions, see Section
6.1
.

Our first aim is to define the bipermutohedral fan
\Sigma_{E,E}
. The bipermutohedral fan is the simplicial fan inside the product of
tropical projective tori
whose cones correspond to biflags of
bisubsets
.

Definition 2.9
.

Given a ground set
E
, the
tropical projective torus

N_{E}
is the
(n-1)
-dimensional real vector space

\mathbb{R}^{E}/\mathbb{R}\mathbf{e}_{E},\quad\quad\mathbf{e}_{E}\coloneqq\sum_{i\in E}e_{i}.

If we relax our definition of biflat to not just consist of flats but instead any non-empty subset of the ground set, we arrive at the definition of a bisubset.

Definition 2.10
.

A
bisubset

S|T
consists of non-empty subsets
S,T\subseteq E
such that at least one of them is not equal to
E
and

S\cup T=E.

Just like biflats, we can form bichains and biflags of bisubsets. The definitions for bichains and biflags of bisubsets are identical to those given in Definitions
2.5
and
2.8
.

Following Section
2.6
of
[
ADH23a
]
, we define the bipermutohedral fan by its rays and cones. For any subset
S\subseteq E
, define the vectors
\mathbf{e}_{S}
and
\mathbf{f}_{S}
to be the image of
\sum_{i\in S}e_{i}
in the first and second copy of
N_{E}
, respectively. Then define
\mathbf{e}_{S|T}
to be the vector
(\mathbf{e}_{S},\mathbf{f}_{T})
in
N_{E}\times N_{E}
.

Definition 2.11
.

The
bipermutohedral fan

\Sigma_{E,E}
is the
(2n-2)
-dimensional simplicial fan in
N_{E}\times N_{E}
with cones

\sigma_{\mathcal{S}|\mathcal{T}}=\cone\left\{\mathbf{e}_{S|T}\right\}_{S|T\in\mathcal{S}|\mathcal{T}},\quad\text{for biflags of bisubsets }\mathcal{S}|\mathcal{T}.

The bipermutohedral fan is a rich combinatorial object with many different interpretations. For an exploration of these interpretations and their consequences, see Section
2
of
[
ADH23a
]
.

Definition 2.12
.

The tropical linear space of a matroid is defined to be

\trop(M)=\left\{z\in N_{E}\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}\min_{i\in C}(z_{i})\text{ is achieved at least twice for every circuit $C$ of $M$}\right\}\subseteq N_{E}.

Definition 2.13
.

The
conormal fan
of
M
, denoted
\Sigma_{M,M^{\perp}}
, is the intersection of
\trop(M)\times\trop(M^{\perp})
with
\Sigma_{E,E^{\perp}}
. By Proposition
3.6
of
[
ADH23a
]
,
\Sigma_{M,M^{\perp}}
is the subfan of
\Sigma_{E,E^{\perp}}
whose cones are

\sigma_{F|G}=\cone\left\{\mathbf{e}_{F|G}\right\}_{F|G\in\mathcal{F}|\mathcal{G}},\text{ for biflags of biflats $\mathcal{F}|\mathcal{G}$ of $M$.}

Remark 2.14
.

If
M
contains a loop
a\in E
then
\trop(M)
will be empty since
\{a\}
will be a circuit and thus

\trop(M)\subseteq\left\{z\in N_{E}\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}\min_{i\in\{a\}}(z_{i})\text{ is achieved at least twice.}\right\}=\emptyset.

Similarly if
M
contains a coloop,
M^{\perp}
will contain a loop and
\trop\left(M^{\perp}\right)
will be empty. Thus if
M
contains a loop or a coloop then
\trop(M)\times\trop(M^{\perp})
will be empty. Hence, we require our matroids to be loopless and coloopless to eliminate the case when the the conormal fan of
M
has empty support.

We consider one more subdivision of
\trop(M)\times\trop\left(M^{\perp}\right)
. This subdivision is the product of the
Bergman fans
of
M
and
M^{\perp}
. The Bergman fan of a matroid, first introduced in
[
AK06
]
, is a well studied fan whose support is
\trop(M)
.

Definition 2.15
.

The
Bergman fan

\Sigma_{M}
of a matroid
M
is the simplicial fan in
N_{E}
whose support is
\trop(M)
and cones are

\sigma_{\mathcal{F}}=\cone\left\{\mathbf{e}_{F}\right\}_{F\in\mathcal{F}},\text{ for flags of flats $\mathcal{F}$ of $M$.}

As
\Sigma_{M}
is a subdivision of
\trop(M)
and
\Sigma_{M^{\perp}}
is a subdivision of
\trop\left(M^{\perp}\right)
,
\Sigma_{M}\times\Sigma_{M^{\perp}}
will be a subdivision of
\trop(M)\times\trop\left(M^{\perp}\right)
.

To every simplical fan, there is an associated simplical complex whose faces are the cones of the fan. This complex is combinatorially equivalent to the complex obtained by intersecting the fan with a sphere centered around the origin. The conormal complex
\Delta_{M,M^{\perp}}
is the simplical complex associated with the conormal fan
\Sigma_{M,M^{\perp}}
while
\Delta_{M}*\Delta_{M^{\perp}}
is the simplicial complex associated with the product of Bergman fans
\Sigma_{M}\times\Sigma_{M^{\perp}}
.

\trop(M)\times\trop\left(M^{\perp}\right)

\varphi

with the unmixed biflats complex.

In the Sections
4

, we show that
\varphi
and
\psi
induce different deformation retracts of
\Delta\left(\mathbf{BF}_{M,M^{\perp}}\right)
onto
\Delta_{M,M^{\perp}}
and
\Delta_{M}*\Delta_{M^{\perp}}
by giving different sequences of elementary collapses.

Combinatorial Properties

Here we collect proofs and references for all facts in Table

, biflats complex
\Delta\left(\mathbf{BF}_{M,M^{\perp}}\right)
and unmixed biflats complex
\Delta\left(\mathbf{BF}_{M,M^{\perp}}^{u}\right)
are all equal if and only if
M
is a uniform matroid.

Lemma 3.1
.

A matroid
M
has no mixed biflats if and only if
M
is a uniform matroid.

Proof.

A matorid has no mixed biflats if and only if for every proper flat
F
and proper dual flat
G
,
F\cup G\neq E
. We can check this property by only considering inclusion maximal proper flats
F
and inclusion maximal proper dual flats
G
. By Proposition
2.1.6
of
[
Oxl92
]
, we can then write
F=E\setminus C^{\perp}
and
G=E\setminus C
where
C
and
C^{\perp}
are a circuit and a cocircuit of
M
. Hence
M
has no mixed biflats if and only if for every circuit
C
and cocircuit
C^{\perp}
,

\left(E\setminus C^{\perp}\right)\cup\left(E\setminus C\right)\neq E\iff C^{\perp}\cap C\neq\emptyset.

It is a well known exercise that a matroid is uniform if and only if every circuit intersects every cocircuit. See, for example, Exercise
3.24
of
[
Oxl14
]
.
∎

It follows from the definitions that a matroid has no mixed biflats if and only if all of its bichains are biflags. Thus we have the following characterization.

For a loopless and coloopless matroid

.

4.
Unmixed biflats complex

For this section we fix a loopless and coloopless matroid
M
on ground set
E
. We drop subscripts and let
\mathbf{BF}
refer to its poset of biflats.
We prove the first of our main theorems.

Theorem 1
.

Given a matroid
M
, there is a sequence of elementary collapses, and hence a simple homotopy equivalence, between the biflats complex
\Delta(\mathbf{BF})
and
\Delta_{M}*\Delta_{M^{\perp}}
, the join of the Bergman complexes of
M
and its dual
M^{\perp}
.

To prove Theorem
1
, we will exhibit a sequence of elementary collapses from
\Delta(\mathbf{BF})
onto
\Delta\left(\mathbf{BF}^{u}\right)
, which, by Lemma
2.5
, is isomorphic to
\Delta_{M}*\Delta_{M^{\perp}}
.
The strategy that we employ in proving this is to inductively remove mixed biflats
F|G
from the poset
\mathbf{BF}

one at a time, in a certain order, by a sequence of elementary collapses.

For a fixed flat
F_{0}\in\mathbf{F}
, we denote the set of mixed biflats which use
F_{0}
by,

\mathbf{BF}|_{F_{0}}\coloneqq\{F|G\colon F=F_{0}\text{ and }G\neq E\}\subseteq\mathbf{BF}.

A dual flat
G
can form a biflat with
F_{0}
if it contains the closure of the compliment of
F_{0}
. Thus, the poset
\mathbf{BF}|_{F_{0}}
is in bijection with the lattice of dual flats restricted to
\mathrm{cl}_{M^{\perp}}(F^{C})
. The minimal element in this subposet is
\mathrm{cl}_{M^{\perp}}(F^{C})
of
F^{C}
and the maximal element is the ground set
E
.

In the upcoming proof, we will fix a flat
F_{0}
and a number
k
and then delete all biflats
F_{0}|G
such that rank of
G
is less than
k+\rk_{M^{\perp}}(F_{0}^{C})
. As a shorthand for this operation, we write

\mathbf{BF}_{F_{0}}^{\setminus(k)}\coloneqq\mathbf{BF}\setminus\left\{F_{0}|G\colon\rk_{M^{\perp}}\left(F_{0}^{c}\right)\leq\rk_{M^{\perp}}(G)\leq\rk_{M^{\perp}}\left(F_{0}^{c}\right)+k\right\}.

We will also want to delete all mixed biflats
F|G
where the corank of
F
is less than
k
.
We write this as

\mathbf{BF}_{\setminus(k)}\coloneqq\mathbf{BF}\setminus\{F|G\colon 1\leq\cork(F)\leq k,G\neq E\}.

We aim to prove that for all
1\leq k\leq\rk(M)-1
, there is a sequence of elementary collapses from
\Delta(\mathbf{BF}_{\setminus(k-1)})
to
\Delta(\mathbf{BF}_{\setminus(k)})
. Since
\mathbf{BF}=\mathbf{BF}_{\setminus(0)}
and
\mathbf{BF}^{u}=\mathbf{BF}_{\setminus(\rk(M)-1)}
, this will allow us to inductively prove Theorem
1
. We show these elementary collapses in another inductive manner. We will take advantage of the fact that

\mathbf{BF}_{\setminus(k)}=\mathbf{BF}_{\setminus(k-1)}\setminus\bigcup_{\cork_{M}(F)=k}\mathbf{BF}\rvert_{F}.

For a fixed
k
such that
1\leq k\leq\rk(M)-1
,
let
\mathbf{SF}_{k}\subseteq\mathbf{F}
be a, possibly empty, collection of corank
k
flats.
Then let
\mathbf{SB}_{k}
be the subposet of
\mathbf{BF}_{\setminus(k-1)}
where all mixed biflats using a flat of
\mathbf{SF}_{k}
have been deleted. That is,

\displaystyle\mathbf{SB}_{k}\coloneqq

\displaystyle\mathbf{BF}_{\setminus(k-1)}\setminus\bigcup_{F\in\mathbf{SF}_{k}}\mathbf{BF}\rvert_{F}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathbf{BF}\setminus\{F|G\colon G\neq E,1\leq\cork_{M}(F)\leq k-1\text{ or }F\in\mathbf{SF}_{k}\}.

The following technical lemma will provide us leverage to give an inductive proof of Theorem
1
.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
1\leq k\leq\rk(M)-1
and
\mathbf{SF}_{k}
and
\mathbf{SB}_{k}
be defined as above. Suppose that
F_{0}
is a flat of corank
k

not
contained in
\mathbf{SF}_{k}
and let
r_{0}\coloneqq\rk_{M^{\perp}}({F_{0}}^{C})
. Then
there is a sequence of elementary collapses from
\Delta(\mathbf{SB}_{k})
onto
\Delta(\mathbf{SB}_{k}\setminus\left(\mathbf{SB}_{k}\right)\rvert_{{F_{0}}})
, the order complex created by deleting all mixed biflats of the form
{F_{0}}|G
from
\mathbf{SB}_{k}
.

Proof.

We will prove this lemma by showing that for any
0\leq\ell\leq\rk_{M^{\perp}}(E)-r_{0}-1
, there is a sequence of elementary collapses of
\Delta\left(\left(\mathbf{SB}_{k}\right)_{F_{0}}^{\setminus(\ell-1)}\right)
onto
\Delta\left(\left(\mathbf{SB}_{k}\right)_{F_{0}}^{\setminus(\ell)}\right)
.
This will prove the lemma since

\left(\mathbf{SB}_{k}\right)_{F_{0}}^{\setminus(-1)}=\mathbf{SB}_{k}\qquad\text{ and }\qquad\left(\mathbf{SB}_{k}\right)_{F_{0}}^{\setminus(\rk_{M^{\perp}}(E)-r_{0}-1)}=\mathbf{SB}_{k}\setminus\left(\mathbf{SB}_{k}\right)\rvert_{F_{0}}.

Our strategy is to
show that for all dual flats
G
with
\rk_{M^{\perp}}(G)=\ell+r_{0}
, there exists a unique minimal element in
\left(\left(\mathbf{SB}_{k}\right)_{F_{0}}^{\setminus(\ell-1)}\right)_{>{F_{0}}|G}
. Further, for any such
G
, this minimal element will be incomparable with
{F_{0}}|G^{\prime}
for any other dual flat
G^{\prime}
of the same rank as
G
. Once we do this, Lemma
2.4
will yield a sequence of elementary collapses from
\Delta\left(\left(\mathbf{SB}_{k}\right)_{F_{0}}^{\setminus(\ell-1)}\right)
onto

\Delta\left(\left(\mathbf{SB}_{k}\right)_{F_{0}}^{\setminus(\ell)}\right)
.
That is, if
G_{1},\ldots,G_{m}
are all the rank
\ell+r_{0}
dual flats such that
{F_{0}}|G_{i}
is a biflat, we can use Lemma
2.4
to construct a sequence of elementary collapses, without worrying about accidentally deleting minimal elements, to show that:

\Delta\left(\left(\mathbf{SB}_{k}\right)_{F_{0}}^{\setminus(\ell-1)}\right)\simeq\Delta\left(\left(\mathbf{SB}_{k}\right)_{F_{0}}^{\setminus(\ell-1)}\setminus\{{F_{0}}|G_{1}\}\right)\simeq\ldots\simeq\Delta\left(\left(\mathbf{SB}_{k}\right)_{F_{0}}^{\setminus(\ell-1)}\setminus\{{F_{0}}|G_{1},\ldots,{F_{0}}|G_{m}\}\right)=\Delta\left(\left(\mathbf{SB}_{k}\right)_{F_{0}}^{\setminus(\ell)}\right).

Fix an index
\ell
such that
0\leq\ell\leq\rk_{M^{\perp}}(E)-r_{0}-1
.
Now fix some dual flat
G
with
\rk_{M^{\perp}}(G)=\ell+r_{0}
and consider a biflat
H|K
in

\left(\left(\mathbf{SB}_{k}\right)_{F_{0}}^{\setminus(\ell-1)}\right)_{>{F_{0}}|G}
.
We must have that
H\supseteq{F_{0}}
and
K\subseteq G
with at least one containment being strict.
We now show that, regardless of which containment is assumed to be strict,
H=E
.

•

If
{F_{0}}\subsetneq H
, then
\cork_{M}(H)<\cork_{M}({F_{0}})
. Since
K\subseteq G\subsetneq E
, if
H
is a proper flat, then
H|K
will be a mixed biflat such that
\cork_{M}(H)<\cork_{M}({F_{0}})=k
. This means that
H|K
is not in
\mathbf{SB}_{k}
, contradicting our assumption. Hence we must have the flat
H
be the whole ground set
E
.

•

If
K\subsetneq G
,
then
\rk_{M^{\perp}}(K)<\rk_{M^{\perp}}(G)=\ell+r_{0}
. Since

H|K\in\left(\mathbf{SB}_{k}\right)_{F_{0}}^{\setminus(\ell-1)}
,
this means that
H\neq{F_{0}}
. However, now we have that
H\supsetneq{F_{0}}
and we revert to the first case.

Hence, every biflat in
\left(\left(\mathbf{SB}_{k}\right)_{F_{0}}^{\setminus(\ell-1)}\right)_{>{F_{0}}|G}

is some unmixed biflat
E|K
such that
K\subseteq G
.
Of these,
E|G
is minimal. Further,
E|G
is incomparable with
{F_{0}}|G^{\prime}
, for any other dual flat
G^{\prime}
with the same rank.
∎

We proceed to prove Theorem
1
below.

Proof.

Recall that
\mathbf{BF}_{\setminus(0)}=\mathbf{BF}
. Hence, for any flat
F
of corank
1
, we can use Lemma
4.1
to construct a sequence of elementary collapses from
\mathbf{BF}
onto
\mathbf{BF}\setminus\mathbf{BF}\rvert_{F}
. Now again applying Lemma
4.1
to all remaining flats of corank
1
, we see that
\mathbf{BF}
collapses onto
\mathbf{BF}_{\setminus(1)}
. Repeating this procedure, we can see that there is a sequence of elementary collapses from
\mathbf{BF}_{\setminus(k-1)}
onto
\mathbf{BF}_{\setminus(k)}
. Hence

\mathbf{BF}\simeq\mathbf{BF}_{\setminus(\rk(M)-1)}=\mathbf{BF}^{u}
by a sequence of elementary collapses, thus proving our theorem.
∎

5.
Conormal Complex

As in the last section, we fix
M
, a loopless and coloopless matroid on ground set
E
. We drop subscripts and let
\mathbf{BF}
refer to its poset of biflats. In this section, we set up and prove our second theorem.

Theorem 2
.

Given a matroid
M
, there is a sequence of elementary collapses, and hence a simple homotopy equivalence, between the biflats complex
\Delta(\mathbf{BF})
and the conormal complex
\Delta_{M,M^{\perp}}
.

Define
\mathbf{BC}
to be the collection of bichains which aren’t biflags. We will prove that
\Delta(\mathbf{BF})
and
\Delta_{M,M^{\perp}}
are simple homotopy equivalent by performing elementary collapses to remove all of
\mathbf{BC}
from
\Delta(\mathbf{BF})
. We do so by considering a subset
\mathbf{C}\subseteq\mathbf{BC}
and use Lemma
2.3
to collapse
\Delta(\mathbf{BF})
onto the deletions of the bichains in
\mathbf{C}
. This subset
\mathbf{C}
will contain all inclusion-minimal bichains of
\mathbf{BC}
and hence one has that

\Delta_{M,M^{\perp}}=\Delta(\mathbf{BF})\setminus\mathbf{BC}=\Delta(\mathbf{BF})\setminus\bigcup_{\mathcal{F}|\mathcal{G}\in\mathbf{C}}\str_{\Delta(\mathbf{BF})}(\mathcal{F}|\mathcal{G}).

We now define
\mathbf{C}\subset\mathbf{BC}
as the collection of bichains which don’t contain a repeated flat or dual flat.

Definition 5.1
.

Let
\mathbf{C}\subseteq\mathbf{BC}
be the subset of bichains
\mathcal{F}|\mathcal{G}
which are not biflags and no flat or dual flat appears twice in
\mathcal{F}|\mathcal{G}
. In other words,
\mathbf{C}
contains all bichains of
\mathbf{BC}
with the form

F_{1}|E\leq F_{2}|G_{2}\leq\ldots\leq F_{\ell-1}|G_{\ell-1}\leq E|G_{\ell}

where
F_{1}|E
and
E|G_{\ell}
are the only unmixed biflats and no flat
F_{i}
or dual flat
G_{i}
appears twice. We refer to
\mathbf{C}
as the set of
minimal bichains
.

In the following lemma, we will verify that
\mathbf{C}
does indeed contain all inclusion-minimal bichains of
\mathbf{BC}
.

Lemma 5.1
.

If
\mathcal{F}|\mathcal{G}
is a bichain of
\mathbf{BC}
and a flat or dual flat appears twice in
\mathcal{F}|\mathcal{G}
, then there exists some
\mathcal{F}^{\prime}|\mathcal{G}^{\prime}\in\mathbf{BC}
which is strictly contained in
\mathcal{F}|\mathcal{G}
.

Proof.

Suppose without loss of generality that
\mathcal{F}|\mathcal{G}
contains a repeated flat and
F|G,F|G^{\prime}\in\mathcal{F}|\mathcal{G}
where
G\supsetneq G^{\prime}
. Recall that
\mathcal{F}|\mathcal{G}
is not a biflag exactly when

\bigcup_{H|K\in\mathcal{F}|\mathcal{G}}H\cap K=E.

Then
F\cap G\supseteq F\cap G^{\prime}
so
\mathcal{F}|\mathcal{G}\setminus\{F|G^{\prime}\}\in\mathbf{BC}
since

\bigcup_{H|K\in\mathcal{F}|\mathcal{G}\setminus\{F|G^{\prime}\}}H\cap K=E.\qed

By Lemma
5.1
,
\mathbf{C}
contains all inclusion-minimal bichains of
\mathbf{BC}
so, as desired,

\Delta_{M,M^{\perp}}=\Delta(\mathbf{BF})\setminus\mathbf{BC}=\Delta(\mathbf{BF})\setminus\bigcup_{\mathcal{F}|\mathcal{G}\in\mathbf{C}}\str_{\Delta(\mathbf{BF})}(\mathcal{F}|\mathcal{G}).

Example 5.1
.

There are two bichains of
M_{A}
, as defined in Example
2.1
, which are not biflags:

12|E\leq E|34\quad\text{ and }\quad 12|E\leq 12|34\leq E|34.

Of these, only
12|E\leq E|34
does not contain a repeated flat or dual flat, so in this case
\mathbf{C}=\{12|E\leq E|34\}
.

We now seek to order the bichains of
\mathbf{C}
so that if
\mathcal{F}|\mathcal{G}
is the first bichain of
\mathbf{C}
then
\Delta(\mathbf{BF})
is collapsible onto
\dell_{\Delta(\mathbf{BF})}\left(\mathcal{F}|\mathcal{G}\right)
and if
\mathcal{F}^{\prime}|\mathcal{G}^{\prime}
is the second bichain of
\mathbf{C}
then
\dell_{\Delta(\mathbf{BF})}\left(\mathcal{F}|\mathcal{G}\right)
is collapsible onto
\dell_{\dell_{\Delta(\mathbf{BF})}\left(\mathcal{F}|\mathcal{G}\right)}\left(\mathcal{F}^{\prime}|\mathcal{G}^{\prime}\right)
and so on. If we can do this, we will have provided a collapsing of
\Delta(\mathbf{BF})
onto
\Delta_{M,M^{\perp}}
.
To determine the ordering of
\mathbf{C}
for which to collapse under, we construct a partial order
\preceq
on
\mathbf{C}
such that any linear extension of
\preceq
will yield the desired ordering.

Definition 5.2
.

Let

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}|\mathcal{G}=\{F_{1}|E\leq F_{2}|G_{2}\leq\dots\leq F_{\ell-1}|G_{\ell-1}\leq E|G_{\ell}\}\in\mathbf{C}\quad\text{and}

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}|\mathcal{K}=\{H_{1}|E\leq H_{2}|K_{2}\leq\dots\leq H_{m-1}|K_{m-1}\leq E|K_{m}\}\in\mathbf{C}.

We define
\preceq
to be the binary relation on minimal bichains
such that

\mathcal{F}|\mathcal{G}\preceq\mathcal{H}|\mathcal{K}

if and only if

F_{1}|E\gneq H_{1}|E\quad\text{ or }\quad\left(F_{1}|E=H_{1}|E\;\text{ and }\;F_{2}|G_{2}\lneq H_{2}|K_{2}\right)\quad\text{ or }\quad\mathcal{F}|\mathcal{G}=\mathcal{H}|\mathcal{K}.

Lemma 5.2
.

The relation
\preceq
is a partial order on the set of minimal bichains
\mathbf{C}
.

Proof.

By definition, the relation
\preceq
is reflexive.

Now we prove anti-symmetry. Suppose that
\mathcal{F}|\mathcal{G}\preceq\mathcal{H}|\mathcal{K}
. If
F_{1}|E\gneq H_{1}|E
then we cannot have
\mathcal{H}|\mathcal{K}\preceq\mathcal{F}|\mathcal{G}
since
H_{1}|E
is strictly less than
F_{1}|E
so none of our three conditions can hold. Similarly if
F_{1}|E=H_{1}|E
and
F_{2}|G_{2}\lneq H_{1}|K_{2}
, we cannot have
\mathcal{H}|\mathcal{K}\preceq\mathcal{F}|\mathcal{G}
. Thus if
\mathcal{F}|\mathcal{G}\preceq\mathcal{H}|\mathcal{K}
and
\mathcal{H}|\mathcal{K}\preceq\mathcal{F}|\mathcal{G}
we must have that
\mathcal{F}|\mathcal{G}=\mathcal{H}|\mathcal{K}
, confirming
\preceq
is anti-symmetric.

To check transitivity, let
\mathcal{F}|\mathcal{G},\mathcal{H}|\mathcal{K},\mathcal{L}|\mathcal{M}
be minimal bichains. If
\mathcal{F}|\mathcal{G}\preceq\mathcal{H}|\mathcal{K}\preceq\mathcal{L}|\mathcal{M}
, our relation ensures that

F_{1}|E\geq H_{1}|E\geq L_{1}|E\qquad\text{and}\qquad F_{2}|G_{2}\leq H_{2}|K_{2}\leq L_{2}|M_{2}.

If any of these inequalities are strict, transitivity of the ordering for biflats will imply
F_{1}|E\gneq L_{1}|E
or
F_{2}|G_{2}\lneq L_{2}|M_{2}
and thus
\mathcal{F}|\mathcal{G}\preceq\mathcal{L}|\mathcal{M}
. If all the inequalities are equalities, then the third condition will ensure
\mathcal{F}|\mathcal{G}=\mathcal{H}|\mathcal{K}=\mathcal{L}|\mathcal{M}
so
\mathcal{F}|\mathcal{G}\preceq\mathcal{L}|\mathcal{M}
.
∎

Example 5.2
.

There are
228
bichains of the matroid
M_{C}
, as defined in Example
2.1
, which are not biflags. However
\mathbf{C}
only contains nine bichains. The poset structure of the minimal bichains
(\mathbf{C},\preceq)
is given below:

\{12|E\leq E|3456\}
\{12|E\leq 1234|3456\leq E|56\}
\{1234|E\leq E|56\}
\{125|E\leq E|3456\}
\{126|E\leq E|3456\}
\{1234|E\leq E|156\}
\{1234|E\leq E|256\}
\{1234|E\leq E|3456\}
\{456|E\leq E|123\}

Fix
\preceq^{*}
a linear extension of
\preceq
. Recall that a linear extension of
\preceq
is a total ordering
\preceq^{*}
such that if
\mathcal{H}|\mathcal{K}\preceq\mathcal{F}|\mathcal{G}
then
\mathcal{H}|\mathcal{K}\preceq^{*}\mathcal{F}|\mathcal{G}
.

Definition 5.3
.

Let
\Delta_{\prec\mathcal{F}|\mathcal{G}}
be the subcomplex of
\Delta\left(\mathbf{BF}\right)
formed by deleting the open stars of all bichains
\mathcal{F}^{\prime}|\mathcal{G}^{\prime}\in\mathbf{C}
which are strictly smaller than
\mathcal{F}|\mathcal{G}
under
\preceq^{*}
. That is,

\Delta_{\prec\mathcal{F}|\mathcal{G}}\coloneqq\;\Delta(\mathbf{BF})\setminus\bigcup_{\mathcal{F}|\mathcal{G}^{\prime}\prec^{*}\mathcal{F}|\mathcal{G}}\str_{\Delta(\mathbf{BF})}\left(\mathcal{F}^{\prime}|\mathcal{G}^{\prime}\right)

We now examine the link of
\mathcal{F}|\mathcal{G}
inside of
\Delta_{\prec\mathcal{F}|\mathcal{G}}
. We first prove a technical lemma about the structure of bichains in
\Delta_{\prec\mathcal{F}|\mathcal{G}}
that contain
\mathcal{F}|\mathcal{G}
. We will then use this lemma in order to prove that the link of
\mathcal{F}|\mathcal{G}
in
\Delta_{\prec\mathcal{F}|\mathcal{G}}
has a cone vertex. Proving this, we will be able to collapse
\Delta_{\prec\mathcal{F}|\mathcal{G}}
onto
\dell_{\Delta_{\prec\mathcal{F}|\mathcal{G}}}(\mathcal{F}|\mathcal{G})
. This step will be vital in our inductive proof of Theorem
2
.

Lemma 5.3
.

Let
\mathcal{F}|\mathcal{G}
be a minimal bichain written as

\mathcal{F}|\mathcal{G}=\{F_{1}|E\leq F_{2}|G_{2}\leq\dots\leq F_{\ell-1}|G_{\ell-1}\leq E|G_{\ell}\}.

If
\mathcal{H}|\mathcal{K}
is a bichain in
\Delta_{\prec\mathcal{F}|\mathcal{G}}
containing
\mathcal{F}|\mathcal{G}
then
\mathcal{H}|\mathcal{K}
has the following structural properties:

(a)

F_{1}|E
is the largest unmixed biflat of
\mathcal{H}|\mathcal{K}
with
E
as its dual flat.

(b)

If
F_{1}|E\lneq H|K\lneq F_{2}|G_{2}
with
H|K\in\mathcal{H}|\mathcal{K}
then
H=F_{1}
or
K=G_{2}
.

Proof of (
a
).

Suppose that
\mathcal{H}|\mathcal{K}\in\Delta_{\prec\mathcal{F}|\mathcal{G}}
contains some biflat
F^{\prime}|E
where
F^{\prime}|E\gneq F_{1}|E
. This means that
F^{\prime}\supsetneq F_{1}
.

First, compare
F^{\prime}|E
and
F_{2}|G_{2}
. Since
\mathcal{F}|\mathcal{G}\in\mathbf{C}
, we know that
G_{2}\neq E
; in other words
G_{2}\subsetneq E
. It follows that
F^{\prime}|E\leq F_{2}|G_{2}
. In particular, we know that
F^{\prime}\subseteq F_{2}
. We break into two cases, the first where
F^{\prime}\subseteq F_{2}
and the second where
F^{\prime}=F_{2}
.

If
F^{\prime}\subsetneq F_{2}
, then the length
\ell
bichain

\mathcal{F}^{\prime}|\mathcal{G}^{\prime}\coloneqq\{F^{\prime}|E\leq F_{2}|G_{2}\leq F_{3}|G_{3}\leq\dots\leq F_{\ell-1}|G_{\ell-1}\leq E|G_{\ell}\}

is not a biflag since
\mathcal{F}|\mathcal{G}
is not a biflag and
F^{\prime}\cup G_{2}\supseteq F_{1}\cup G_{2}=E
.
Further the biflats of
\mathcal{F}^{\prime}|\mathcal{G}^{\prime}
have no repeated flats or dual flats since
\mathcal{F}|\mathcal{G}
was an element of
\mathbf{C}
and we assumed that
F^{\prime}\subsetneq F_{2}
.
Comparing the two bichains as elements of the poset of minimal bichains (
\mathbf{C},\prec^{*})
, we see that
F^{\prime}|E\gneq F_{1}|E
makes
\mathcal{F}^{\prime}|\mathcal{G}^{\prime}\prec^{*}\mathcal{F}|\mathcal{G}
.
We conclude that
\mathcal{F}^{\prime}|\mathcal{G}^{\prime}
is not a chain in the simplicial complex
\Delta_{\prec\mathcal{F}|\mathcal{G}}
because it is precisely in the set of bichains that have been deleted from
\mathbf{BF}
in the construction of this complex.
This contradicts our assumption of
\mathcal{H}|\mathcal{K}
being a bichain in
\Delta_{\prec\mathcal{F}|\mathcal{G}}
since
\mathcal{F}^{\prime}|\mathcal{G}^{\prime}\subseteq\mathcal{H}|\mathcal{K}
.

On the other hand, if
F^{\prime}=F_{2}
, then the length
\ell-1
bichain

\mathcal{F}^{\prime}|\mathcal{G}^{\prime}\coloneqq\{F^{\prime}|E\leq F_{3}|G_{3}\leq\ldots\leq F_{\ell-1}|G_{\ell-1}\leq E|G_{\ell}\}

is not a biflag. This is because
\mathcal{F}|\mathcal{G}
is not a biflag and

F^{\prime}\cup G_{3}=F_{2}\cup G_{3}=E.

Further, this bichain has no repeated flats or dual flats
by virtue of
\mathcal{F}|\mathcal{G}
being a minimal bichain.
As elements of
(\mathbf{C},\prec^{*})
, we have that
\mathcal{F}^{\prime}|\mathcal{G}^{\prime}\prec^{*}\mathcal{F}|\mathcal{G}

since
F^{\prime}|E\gneq F_{1}|E
.
As in the previous case, the bichain
\mathcal{F}^{\prime}|\mathcal{G}^{\prime}
cannot be an element of
\Delta_{\prec\mathcal{F}|\mathcal{G}}
.
This contradicts our starting hypothesis that
\mathcal{H}|\mathcal{K}\in\Delta_{\prec\mathcal{F}|\mathcal{G}}
.

Hence, the largest biflat unmixed in
\mathcal{H}|\mathcal{K}
must be
F_{1}|E
.
∎

Proof of (
b
).

Assume that
\mathcal{H}|\mathcal{K}
contains some biflat
H|K
such that

F_{1}|E\lneq H|K\lneq F_{2}|G_{2},\quad H\neq F_{1}\quad\text{and}\quad K\neq G_{2}.

Since
F_{1}|E\lneq H|K\lneq F_{2}|G_{2}
, we must have that
F_{1}\subsetneq H
and
K\supsetneq G_{2}
. By the first part of this lemma,
we also have that
K\subsetneq E
. We break into two cases depending on whether
H\cup G_{3}=E
or
H\cup G_{3}\neq E
.

First, suppose that
H\cup G_{3}=E
. Then
\mathcal{H}|\mathcal{K}
contains the length
\ell
bichain

\mathcal{F}^{\prime}|\mathcal{G}^{\prime}\coloneqq\{F_{1}|E\leq H|K\leq F_{3}|G_{3}\leq\ldots\leq F_{\ell-1}|G_{\ell-1}\leq E|G_{\ell}\}.

To affirm
\mathcal{F}^{\prime}|\mathcal{G}^{\prime}
is not a biflag, we check the biflats in
\mathcal{F}^{\prime}|\mathcal{G}^{\prime}
that are distinct from
\mathcal{F}|\mathcal{G}
to see that no gaps were introduced. Notice that
F_{1}\cup K\supseteq F_{1}\cup G_{2}=E
and

H\cup G_{3}=E
, thus
\mathcal{F}^{\prime}|\mathcal{G}^{\prime}
not a biflag.
Note, because
\mathcal{F}|\mathcal{G}
has no repeated flats or coflats, we have that

F_{1}\subsetneq H\subseteq F_{2}\subsetneq F_{3}\qquad\text{and}\qquad E\supsetneq K\supseteq G_{2}\supsetneq G_{3}.

This implies that the bichain
\mathcal{F}^{\prime}|\mathcal{G}^{\prime}
has no repeated flats or coflats.
As elements of
(\mathbf{C},\prec^{*})
, we have that
\mathcal{F}^{\prime}|\mathcal{G}^{\prime}\prec^{*}\mathcal{F}|\mathcal{G}
since
H|K\lneq F_{2}|G_{2}
.
It follows that
\mathcal{F}^{\prime}|\mathcal{G}^{\prime}\notin\Delta_{\prec\mathcal{F}|\mathcal{G}}
.
This contradicts our assumption that
\mathcal{H}|\mathcal{K}\in\Delta_{\prec\mathcal{F}|\mathcal{G}}
, since
\mathcal{F}^{\prime}|\mathcal{G}^{\prime}
is a sub-bichain of
\mathcal{H}|\mathcal{K}
.

H\cup G_{3}\neq E
and consider the length
\ell+1
bichain

\mathcal{F}^{\prime}|\mathcal{G}^{\prime}\coloneqq\{F_{1}|E\leq H|K\leq F_{2}|G_{2}\leq F_{3}|G_{3}\leq\ldots\leq F_{\ell-1}|G_{\ell-1}\leq E|G_{\ell}\}.

Because
\mathcal{F}^{\prime}|\mathcal{G}^{\prime}
is a sub-bichain of
\mathcal{H}|\mathcal{K}
,
\mathcal{F}^{\prime}|\mathcal{G}^{\prime}

We now check if
\mathcal{F}^{\prime}|\mathcal{G}^{\prime}
has any repeated flats or coflats. The only case remaining to check is that

. Since
\mathcal{F}|\mathcal{G}
has no gaps, we know that
F_{2}\cup G_{3}=E
. Our current assumption is that
H\cup G_{3}\neq E
, thus
F_{2}
and

Hence,
\mathcal{F}^{\prime}|\mathcal{G}^{\prime}
is an element of
\mathbf{C}
.
Once again, by our assumption,
\mathcal{F}^{\prime}|G^{\prime}\prec^{*}F|G
. This means that
\mathcal{F}^{\prime}|\mathcal{G}^{\prime}

We conclude that assuming

and

We use Lemma
5.3
to prove that the link of a minimal bichain
\mathcal{F}|\mathcal{G}
in
\Delta_{\prec\mathcal{F}|\mathcal{G}}
always contains a cone vertex. This will then let us use Lemmas
2.2
and
2.3
to collapse
\Delta_{\prec\mathcal{F}|\mathcal{G}}
onto
\dell_{\Delta_{\prec\mathcal{F}|\mathcal{G}}}\left(\mathcal{F}|\mathcal{G}\right)
.

Lemma 5.4
.

Let
\mathcal{F}|\mathcal{G}
be a minimal bichain written as

\mathcal{F}|\mathcal{G}=\{F_{1}|E\leq F_{2}|G_{2}\leq\dots\leq F_{\ell-1}|G_{\ell-1}\leq E|G_{\ell}\}.

Then the link of
\mathcal{F}|\mathcal{G}
in
\Delta_{\prec\mathcal{F}|\mathcal{G}}
contains a cone vertex.

Proof.

Because
\mathcal{F}|\mathcal{G}
is not a bichain,
F_{1}\cup G_{2}=E
and
F_{1}|G_{2}
is a biflat. Also since
\mathcal{F}|\mathcal{G}
is in
\mathbf{C}
, no flats or dual flats are repeated and
F_{1}|G_{2}\not\in\mathcal{F}|\mathcal{G}
. We prove that
F_{1}|G_{2}
is a cone vertex of
\lk_{\Delta_{\prec\mathcal{F}|\mathcal{G}}}(\mathcal{F}|\mathcal{G})
. To accomplish this, we prove that if
\mathcal{H}|\mathcal{K}
is a bichain of
\Delta_{\prec\mathcal{F}|\mathcal{G}}
and
\mathcal{F}|\mathcal{G}\subseteq\mathcal{H}|\mathcal{K}
but
F_{1}|G_{2}
isn’t a biflat of
\mathcal{H}|\mathcal{K}
, then
\mathcal{H}|\mathcal{K}\cup\{F_{1}|G_{2}\}
is a bichain in
\Delta_{\prec\mathcal{F}|\mathcal{G}}
.

Suppose that
\mathcal{H}|\mathcal{K}
is a bichain in
\Delta_{\prec\mathcal{F}|\mathcal{G}}
containing
\mathcal{F}|\mathcal{G}
but not
F_{1}|G_{2}
. As a consequence of Lemma
5.3
,
\mathcal{H}|\mathcal{K}
is forced to have the form

\dots\leq F_{1}|E\leq F_{1}|K_{1}\leq F_{1}|K_{2}\leq\dots\leq F_{1}|K_{\alpha}\leq H_{1}|G_{2}\leq H_{2}|G_{2}\leq\dots\leq H_{\beta}|G_{2}\leq F_{2}|G_{2}\leq\cdots

where

E\supsetneq K_{1}\supsetneq K_{2}\supsetneq\dots\supsetneq K_{\alpha}\supsetneq G_{2}\qquad\text{and}\qquad F_{1}\subsetneq H_{1}\subsetneq H_{2}\subsetneq\dots\subsetneq H_{\beta}\subsetneq F_{2}

are chains of dual flats and flats respectively.
Thus we can see that
\mathcal{H}|\mathcal{K}\cup\{F_{1}|G_{2}\}
is a bichain since it will have the form

\dots\leq F_{1}|E\leq F_{1}|K_{1}\leq F_{1}|K_{2}\leq\dots\leq F_{1}|K_{\alpha}\leq F_{1}|G_{2}\leq H_{1}|G_{2}\leq H_{2}|G_{2}\leq\dots\leq H_{\beta}|G_{2}\leq F_{2}|G_{2}\leq\cdots

It remains to check that
\mathcal{H}|\mathcal{K}\cup\{F_{1}|G_{2}\}
is in
\Delta_{\prec\mathcal{F}|\mathcal{G}}
. That is, we need to check that no sub-bichain of
\mathcal{H}|\mathcal{K}\cup\{F_{1}|G_{2}\}
is an element of
\mathbf{C}
which precedes
\mathcal{F}|\mathcal{G}
in
(\mathbf{C},\prec^{*})
. Suppose
\mathcal{F}^{\prime}|\mathcal{G}^{\prime}\subseteq\mathcal{H}|\mathcal{K}\cup\{F_{1}|G_{2}\}
is a minimal bichain. This bichain is either a sub-bichain of
\mathcal{H}|\mathcal{K}
or it contains
\{F_{1}|G_{2}\}
.

If
\mathcal{F}^{\prime}|G^{\prime}\subseteq\mathcal{H}|\mathcal{K}
, then
\mathcal{F}|\mathcal{G}\preceq^{*}\mathcal{F}^{\prime}|\mathcal{G}^{\prime}
by virtue of
\mathcal{H}|\mathcal{K}
being in
\Delta_{\prec\mathcal{F}|\mathcal{G}}
.
Now suppose the biflat
F_{1}|G_{2}
is in
\mathcal{F}^{\prime}|\mathcal{G}^{\prime}
. Let
F^{\prime}|E
be the unmixed biflat of
\mathcal{F}^{\prime}|\mathcal{G}^{\prime}
with
E
as its dual flat. By Lemma
5.3
we know that
F^{\prime}|E\leq F_{1}|E
. Even further, this inequality has to be strict, since otherwise
\mathcal{F}^{\prime}|\mathcal{G}^{\prime}
has a repeated flat, namely
F_{1}
. Thus,
F^{\prime}|E\lneq F_{1}|E
, which means that
\mathcal{F}|\mathcal{G}\preceq^{*}\mathcal{F}^{\prime}|\mathcal{G}^{\prime}
.
∎

6.
Further Questions

6.1.
“Global” deformation retracts

Recall that the conormal fan
\Sigma_{M,M^{\perp}}
is the restriction of the bipermutohedral fan
\Sigma_{E,E}
to
\trop(M)\times\trop\left(M^{\perp}\right)
. The bipermutohedral fan has an associated simplicial complex
\Delta_{E,E}
which will contain all conormal complexes as induced subcomplexes. This complex, which we call the
bipermutohedral complex
, will have faces given by biflags of bisubsets.

A
bisubset

S|T
is a pair of non-empty subsets
S,T\subset E
such that
S\cup T=E
and
S\cap T\neq E
. Like in Definition
2.6
, we can define the simplicial complex
\Delta\left(\mathbf{BS}_{E,E}\right)
whose faces are given by bichains of bisubsets. This simplicial complex contains the bipermutohedral complex as a subcomplex.

The fan structure of the product of Bergman fans
\Sigma_{M}\times\Sigma_{M^{\perp}}
on
\trop(M)\times\trop\left(M^{\perp}\right)
is the restriction of a larger fan, namely the product of permutohedral fans
\Sigma_{E}\times\Sigma_{E}
, see
[
AK06
]
. As in Lemma
2.5
, the complex
\Delta_{E}*\Delta_{E}
associated to
\Sigma_{E}\times\Sigma_{E}
will be isomorphic to a subcomplex of
\Delta\left(\mathbf{BS}_{E,E}\right)
.

We can thus recreate the diagram at the end of Section
2.7
for these larger “global” objects:

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{N_{E}\times N_{E}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\Sigma_{E,E}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\Sigma_{E}\times\Sigma_{E}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\Delta_{E,E}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\Delta(\mathbf{BS}_{E,E})}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\Delta_{E}*\Delta_{E}}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\varphi}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\psi}

It is unknown to the authors if the maps
\varphi
and
\psi
induce homotopy equivalences. Unlike the Bergman case, where
\Delta_{E}
is the Bergman complex of
U_{n,n}
, none of these “global” complexes are related to a single matroid. Thus the proofs we give in Sections
4
and
5
do not immediately carry over.

Are
\Delta_{E,E}
and
\Delta_{E}*\Delta_{E}
homotopic to
\Delta\left(\mathbf{BS}_{E,E}\right)

6.3.
\Aut(M)
-equivariant homotopy equivalences

For simplicial complexes and posets equipped with a
G
-action there exists a notion of
G
-equivariant homotopy equivalence. See Section
2.2
of
[
HH13
]
and
[
TW91
]
for the relevant definitions and tools for simplicial complexes and posets. As the simplicial complexes we construct only rely on matroidal information, they are all equipped with an

\Aut(M)
-equivariant homotopy equivalences.

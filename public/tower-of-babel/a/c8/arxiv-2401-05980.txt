Boundary determination of coefficients appearing in a perturbed weighted p p -Laplace equation
Nitesh Kumar, Tanmay Sarkar and Manmohan Vashisth

Abstract
. We study an inverse boundary value problem associated with p p -Laplacian which is further perturbed by a linear second order term, defined on a bounded set Ω \Omega in ℝ n , n ≥ 2 \mathbb{R}^{n},n\geq 2 . We recover the coefficients at the boundary from the boundary measurements which are given by the Dirichlet to Neumann map. Our approach relies on the appropriate asymptotic expansion of the solution and it allows one to recover the coefficients pointwise. Furthermore, by considering the localized Dirichlet-to-Neumann map around a boundary point, we provide a procedure to reconstruct the normal derivative of the coefficients at that boundary point.

Primary 35R30, 35J60.

1.
Introduction

In this paper, we consider the following weighted
p
-Laplace equation which is perturbed by a linear term

\begin{cases}\nabla\cdot(\sigma(x)\nabla u(x)+\gamma(x)|\nabla u(x)|^{p-2}\nabla u(x))=0,&\quad x\in~\Omega,\\
u(x)=f(x),&\quad x\in\partial\Omega,\end{cases}

(1.1)

where the domain
\Omega\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n},n\geq 2
is a bounded open set with smooth boundary
\partial\Omega
. Throughout the paper, we assume that the exponent
p
satisfies
1<p<\infty
. Furthermore, we consider the coefficients
\sigma
and
\gamma
are positive functions with the regularity
\sigma\in C^{\infty}(\overline{\Omega})
and
\gamma\in L^{\infty}(\Omega)
respectively. Moreover, the coefficients
\sigma
and
\gamma
are assumed to satisfy the following conditions:

\begin{cases}0<\lambda<\sigma(x)<\lambda^{-1},\\
0<m_{1}<\gamma(x)<m_{1}^{-1},\end{cases}

where
\lambda
, and
m_{1}
are positive constants. The Dirichlet data
f
is considered as follows:

f\in W^{1/2,2}(\partial\Omega)
whenever
p\in(1,2)
, and

f\in W^{1-1/p,p}(\partial\Omega)
whenever
p\in(2,\infty)
.

The nonlinear Dirichlet to Neumann map (DN-map) is defined by

\Lambda_{\sigma,\gamma}(f)=(\sigma(x)+\gamma(x)|\nabla u|^{p-2})\partial_{\nu}u|_{\partial\Omega},

(1.2)

where
u
is the unique solution of equation (
1.1
) with the boundary value
f
. Throughout the paper, we assume that
\nu
is the outer unit normal to
\partial\Omega
.

In case of
p=2
, the equation(
1.1
) becomes a linear conductivity equation and there are several results related to boundary determination of conductivity coefficients appearing in the linear equation. For instance, in
[
15
,
26
]
the smooth conductivity and all its derivatives at the boundary are recovered from the boundary measurements of the solution. The authors in
[
15
,
26
]
proved the uniqueness of conductivity together with its derivatives at the boundary of domain. Later
Nachman in
[
20
]
recovered the coefficient at the boundary by assuming that
\gamma\in W^{1,m}(\Omega),~m>n
. Under the assumption
m>n/2
, Nachman in
[
20
]
also recovered the first normal derivative of
\gamma\in W^{2,m}(\Omega)
.
Furthermore, Brown
[
3
]
described a procedure for recovering
\gamma|_{\partial\Omega}
in a pointwise manner by assuming
\gamma\in L^{\infty}(\Omega)
. For the conductivity equation, Nakamura and Tanuma
[
21
,
22
]
provided a formula for reconstructing the conductivity and its higher order derivatives from the localized DN-map.
Moreover, there are several local boundary determination results, for instance, one can refer to
[
1
,
13
,
23
]
and references therein.

Motivated by the applications in image processing, fluid mechanics and modeling of sand-piles, Calderón-type inverse problems for the
p
-Laplace equation are studied by several authors. For instance, Salo and Zhong
[
24
]
studied the boundary uniqueness problem associated with
p
-Laplace equation for
n\geq 2
.
Brander in
[
2
]
recovered the gradient of
\gamma
at the boundary using the Rellich’s type identity. Furthermore, Cârstea and Kar in
[
4
]
considered the weighted perturbed
p
-Laplace equation and provided a procedure for reconstruction of the coefficients in the interior of the domain using the complex geometric optics solution for
n\geq 3
. However, up to our knowledge, the boundary determination problem for weighted perturbed
p
-Laplace equation concerning the coefficient
\gamma
has not been studied in the literature.

In this paper, we will give a procedure for recovery of the coefficients at boundary of the domain. To do this, we use the
\epsilon
-expansion of solution which varies according to the choice of
p
. The approach of using the
\epsilon
-expansion or linearization for solving the inverse problems for nonlinear elliptic equations have been used by several authors, see for example
[
4
,
5
,
6
,
7
,
9
,
10
,
11
,
12
,
14
,
16
,
17
,
19
]
. Motivated by
[
4
,
5
,
12
]
our idea is to find an appropriate integral identity, from which reconstruction of the coefficient
\gamma
at the boundary of the domain is possible. Using the approach used in
[
5
]
we derived the integral identity by constructing a sequence of explicit functions which are solutions of conductivity equation. Such solutions can be used to extract the Taylor series of the coefficient
\gamma
at the boundary point from the knowledge of
\Lambda_{\sigma,\gamma}
.

We can now state our boundary determination results.

Theorem 1.1
.

(Reconstruction of coefficients)

Suppose
p>1
with
p\neq 2
and
\Omega\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n},n\geq 2
is a bounded domain with smooth boundary
\partial\Omega
. Furthermore, assume that the coefficients are continuous on
\overline{\Omega}
and bounded below by a positive constant.
Then the coefficients
\sigma
and
\gamma
in (
1.1
) can be reconstructed at the boundary from the knowledge of given DN-map
\Lambda_{\sigma,\gamma}
.

Theorem 1.2
.

(Reconstruction of normal derivatives of coefficients at the boundary)

Assume that
p\in(1,\infty)\backslash\{2\}
and for
n\geq 2
,
\Omega\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
is a bounded domain with smooth boundary
\partial\Omega
. Suppose
\sigma\in C^{\infty}(\overline{\Omega})
and
D^{\alpha^{\prime}}_{x^{\prime}}D^{\alpha_{n}}_{x_{n}}\gamma
is continuous around
x_{0}
for any multi-index
(\alpha^{\prime},\alpha_{n})
such that
|\alpha^{\prime}|+2\alpha_{n}\leq 2
, where
x=(x^{\prime},x_{n}),~x^{\prime}\in\mathbb{R}^{n-1}
.
Then the normal derivative of the coefficients can be evaluated at the boundary point of
\Omega
from the knowledge of given DN-map
\Lambda_{\sigma,\gamma}
associated to (
1.1
).

The paper is organized as follows: we state the well-posedness results of (
1.1
) along with corresponding
\epsilon-
expansion of the solution in section
2
which further includes the epsilon expansion of Dirichlet to Neumann map. In section
3
, we provide a detailed proof for the reconstruction of the coefficient at the boundary of the domain (cf. Theorem
1.1
). We construct a few approximate solutions using the localized Dirichlet data to extract the normal derivative of
\gamma
and obtain a reconstruction formula in Section
4
.

2.
\varepsilon
-expansion of the solution for perturbed
p
-Laplacian problem

In this section, we state the existence of the solution and corresponding energy estimates for the boundary value problem (BVP) (
1.1
). Based on the range of
p
, we have weak solution and strong solution of the BVP (
1.1
) corresponding to sufficiently small boundary data. We remark that such boundary data are considered since we require the resulting estimates while analyzing the boundary determination problem. More precisely, we shall use the weak solution and strong solution of BVP
1.1
while reconstructing the scalar coefficient
\gamma
at the boundary.
The following Proposition
2.1
can be proved by defining suitable energy functionals and subsequently, considering the minimizing sequence (for details, we refer to
[
5
, Section 2]
).

Proposition 2.1

(Weak solutions)
.

(i)

Assume that
1<p<2
and
f\in W^{1/2,2}(\partial\Omega)
. Then the BVP (
1.1
) has a unique solution

u\in W^{1/2,2}(\Omega)\cap W^{1,p}(\Omega),

and it satisfies the following estimate

\|u\|_{W^{1,2}(\Omega)}\leq C\|f\|_{W^{1/2,2}(\partial\Omega)},

where positive constant
C
does not depend on the boundary data
f
.

(ii)

Assume that
2<p<\infty
and
f\in W^{1-{1/p},p}(\partial\Omega)
. Then the BVP (
1.1
) has a unique solution

u\in W^{1/2,2}(\Omega)\cap W^{1,p}(\Omega),

along with the stability estimate

\|u\|_{W^{1,p}(\Omega)}\leq C\|f\|_{W^{1-{1/p},p}(\partial\Omega)},

where
C=C(\Omega)>0
.

Proposition
2.1
can be further improved in case of
p>2
. In fact, there exists a strong solution for the BVP (
1.1
).
By defining an appropriate operator and using the Sobolev embedding results and finally, demonstrating the fixed point of contraction mapping, the existence of the strong solution can be established. For detailed proof of the following Proposition
2.2
, we refer to
[
5
, Theorem 2.2]
.

Proposition 2.2

(Strong solution)
.

Assume that
p>2
and
m\in\mathbb{R}
such that
m>n
. Furthermore, let
f\in W^{2-{1/m},m}(\partial\Omega)
and there holds

\displaystyle\|f\|_{W^{2-{1/m},m}(\partial\Omega)}\leq M,

for some
M>0
.
Then there exists a unique solution for BVP (
1.1
)

u\in W^{2,m}(\Omega).

Moreover, the solution
u
satisfies

\|u\|_{W^{2,m}(\Omega)}\leq C\|f\|_{W^{2-{1/m},m}(\partial\Omega)},

(2.1)

where constant
C>0
is depending on the choice of
p
, domain
\Omega
and coefficients
\sigma
, and
\gamma
.

Based on the well-posedness results, described in Proposition
2.1
and Proposition
2.2
, we define the nonlinear DN-map in a weak sense as follows:

\displaystyle\langle\Lambda_{\sigma,\gamma}(f),w|_{\partial\Omega}\rangle=\int_{\Omega}(\sigma(x)+\gamma(x)|\nabla u|^{p-2})\nabla u\cdot\overline{\nabla w}~dx,

(2.2)

where
w\in W^{2,m}(\Omega)\cap W^{1,2}(\Omega)
. Afterwards, we look into the
\varepsilon
-expansion of the solution for the BVP (
1.1
). Since the expansion depends on the range of
p
, we carry out the analysis via two cases.

2.1.
Case-1:
p>2

We look for
\varepsilon
-expansion of the solution of (
1.1
) for
p>2
based on the existence of strong solution mentioned in Proposition
2.2
.

Let us consider the following BVP

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\nabla\cdot(\sigma(x)\nabla u_{\varepsilon}(x)+\gamma(x)|\nabla u_{\varepsilon}(x)|^{p-2}\nabla u_{\varepsilon}(x))=0,&\quad x\in~\Omega,\\
u_{\varepsilon}(x)=\varepsilon f(x),&\quad x\in\partial\Omega.\\
\end{cases}

(2.3)

From the stability estimate (
2.1
), we obtain

\displaystyle\|\varepsilon^{-1}u_{\varepsilon}\|_{W^{2,m}(\Omega)}\leq C\|f\|_{W^{2-1/m,m}(\partial\Omega)}.

(2.4)

As a consequence, we consider the following
\varepsilon
-expansion

\displaystyle u_{\varepsilon}=\varepsilon u_{0}+\varepsilon^{p-1}v,

(2.5)

where
u_{0}\in W^{2,m}(\Omega)
and
v\in W^{2,m}(\Omega)
will be determined by substituting (
2.5
) in the BVP (
2.3
).
We obtain that
u_{0}
satisfies the following BVP

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\nabla\cdot(\sigma(x)\nabla u_{0}(x))=0,&\quad x\in\Omega,\\
u_{0}(x)=f(x),&\quad x\in\partial\Omega,\end{cases}

(2.6)

and eventually,
v
satisfies the BVP

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\nabla\cdot(\sigma(x)\nabla v)+\varepsilon^{1-p}\nabla\cdot(\gamma(x)|\nabla u_{\varepsilon}|^{p-2}\nabla u_{\varepsilon})=0,&\quad x\in~\Omega,\\
v(x)=0&\quad x\in\partial\Omega.\\
\end{cases}

(2.7)

2.2.
Case-2:
1<p<2
.

For
p\in(1,2)
,
\varepsilon
-expansion of the solution of BVP (
1.1
) is a consequence of existence of a weak solution of (
1.1
). Since
p\in(1,2)
, then we get a sublinear term instead of a nonlinear term. Therefore, for arbitrary small
\varepsilon>0
, we will take the Dirichlet boundary data as
\varepsilon^{-1}f
in (
1.1
).

Let us consider

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\nabla\cdot(\sigma(x)\nabla u_{\varepsilon}+\gamma(x)|\nabla u_{\varepsilon}|^{p-2}\nabla u_{\varepsilon})=0,&\quad x\in~\Omega,\\
u_{\varepsilon}(x)=\varepsilon^{-1}f(x),&\quad x\in\partial\Omega.\\
\end{cases}

(2.8)

The well-posedness of above BVP (
2.8
) is assured by the Proposition
2.1
along with the stability estimate

\|\varepsilon u_{\varepsilon}\|_{W^{1,2}(\Omega)}\leq C\|f\|_{W^{1/2,2}(\partial\Omega)}.

(2.9)

By taking into account the estimate (
2.9
), we consider the following expansion:

\displaystyle u_{\varepsilon}=\varepsilon^{-1}u_{0}+\varepsilon^{1-p}v,

(2.10)

where
u_{0}\in W^{1,2}(\Omega)
and
v\in W^{1,2}(\Omega)
will be determined by substituting (
2.10
) in the BVP (
2.8
).
It is straightforward to observe that
u_{0}
satisfies the following BVP

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\nabla\cdot(\sigma\nabla u_{0})=0,&\quad x\in\Omega,\\
u_{0}(x)=f(x),&\quad x\in\partial\Omega,\end{cases}

(2.11)

and
v
satisfies the BVP

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\nabla\cdot(\sigma(x)\nabla v)+\varepsilon^{p-1}\nabla\cdot(\gamma(x)|\nabla u_{\varepsilon}|^{p-2}\nabla u_{\varepsilon})=0,&\quad x\in~\Omega,\\
v(x)=0,&\quad x\in\partial\Omega.\\
\end{cases}

(2.12)

2.3.
\varepsilon
-expansion of DN-map

In order to recover the parameter
\gamma
at the boundary, we require the
\varepsilon
-expansion of the DN-map described in (
2.2
). We carry out the following analysis motivated by Cârstea et al.
[
5
]
in which the reconstruction of
\gamma
in the interior of domain in
\mathbb{R}^{n}

(n\geq 3)
is described.
The following estimate will be instrumental for our analysis: for
p>2
,

\displaystyle\big||\eta|^{p-2}\eta-|\beta|^{p-2}\beta\big|\leq C(|\eta|+|\beta|)^{p-2}|\eta-\beta|,

(2.13)

where
C>0
and
\eta,\beta\in\mathbb{C}^{n}
(for detailed proof, one can look into
[
24
]
). Let us consider
w\in W^{2,m}(\Omega)\cap W^{1,2}(\Omega)
. Using the estimate (
2.13
), we obtain

\begin{split}\Big|\int_{\Omega}&\gamma(x)(|\nabla u_{\varepsilon}|^{p-2}\nabla u_{\varepsilon}-\varepsilon^{p-1}|\nabla u_{0}|^{p-2}\nabla u_{0})\cdot\overline{\nabla w}~dx\Big|\\
&\leq C\varepsilon^{2p-3}\int_{\Omega}(|\varepsilon^{-1}\nabla u_{\varepsilon}|^{p-2}+|\nabla u_{0}|^{p-2})|\nabla v||\nabla w|~dx=\mathcal{O}(\varepsilon^{2p-3}).\end{split}

(2.14)

With the help of expansion (
2.5
) and above estimate (
2.14
), we get

\displaystyle\langle\Lambda_{\sigma,\gamma}(\varepsilon f),w\big|_{\partial\Omega}\rangle

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}(\sigma(x)+\gamma(x)|\nabla u_{\varepsilon}|^{p-2})\nabla u_{\varepsilon}\cdot\overline{\nabla w}~dx

\displaystyle=\varepsilon\int_{\Omega}\sigma(x)\nabla u_{0}\cdot\overline{\nabla w}~dx~+~\varepsilon^{p-1}\int_{\Omega}\sigma(x)\nabla v\cdot\overline{\nabla w}~dx

\displaystyle\qquad+\varepsilon^{p-1}\int_{\Omega}\gamma(x)|\nabla u_{0}|^{p-2}\nabla u_{0}\cdot\overline{\nabla w}~dx~+~\mathcal{O}(\varepsilon^{2p-3}).

Subsequently, by considering the limit
\varepsilon
tending to zero, we have

\begin{split}\lim_{\varepsilon\rightarrow 0}\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\langle\Lambda_{\sigma,\gamma}(\varepsilon f),w\big|_{\partial\Omega}\rangle&=\int_{\Omega}\sigma(x)\nabla u_{0}\cdot\overline{\nabla w}~dx\\
&=\langle\Lambda_{\sigma}(f),w\big|_{\partial\Omega}\rangle,\end{split}

(2.15)

where
\Lambda_{\sigma}(f)
is the DN-map associated to the Dirichlet BVP (
2.6
).

For
p\in(1,2)
, in order to recover the parameter
\gamma
at the boundary, we need to consider the
\varepsilon
-expansion of the DN-map using
u_{\varepsilon}
described in (
2.10
).
As in the earlier case, we start with
w\in W^{1,2}(\Omega)
. Using the estimate (
2.13
), we obtain

\begin{split}\Big|\int_{\Omega}&\gamma(x)(|\nabla u_{\varepsilon}|^{p-2}\nabla u_{\varepsilon}-\varepsilon^{1-p}|\nabla u_{0}|^{p-2}\nabla u_{0})\cdot\overline{\nabla w}~dx\Big|\\
&\leq C\varepsilon^{3-2p}\int_{\Omega}(|\varepsilon\nabla u_{\varepsilon}|^{p-2}+|\nabla u_{0}|^{p-2})|\nabla v||\nabla w|~dx=\mathcal{O}(\varepsilon^{3-2p}).\end{split}

(2.16)

With the help of expansion (
2.10
) and above estimate (
2.16
), we get

\displaystyle\langle\Lambda_{\sigma,\gamma}(\varepsilon^{-1}f),w\big|_{\partial\Omega}\rangle

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}(\sigma(x)+\gamma(x)|\nabla u_{\varepsilon}|^{p-2})\nabla u_{\varepsilon}\cdot\overline{\nabla w}~dx

\displaystyle=\varepsilon^{-1}\int_{\Omega}\sigma(x)\nabla u_{0}\cdot\overline{\nabla w}~dx~+~\varepsilon^{1-p}\int_{\Omega}\sigma(x)\nabla v\cdot\overline{\nabla w}~dx

\displaystyle\qquad+\varepsilon^{1-p}\int_{\Omega}\gamma(x)|\nabla u_{0}|^{p-2}\nabla u_{0}\cdot\overline{\nabla w}~dx~+~\mathcal{O}(\varepsilon^{3-2p}).

By considering the DN-map
\Lambda_{\sigma}(f)
associated with the Dirichlet BVP (
2.11
), we have

\begin{split}\lim_{\varepsilon\rightarrow 0}\varepsilon\langle\Lambda_{\sigma,\gamma}(\varepsilon^{-1}f),w\big|_{\partial\Omega}\rangle&=\int_{\Omega}\sigma(x)\nabla u_{0}\cdot\overline{\nabla w}~dx\\
&=\langle\Lambda_{\sigma}(f),w\big|_{\partial\Omega}\rangle.\end{split}

(2.17)

Hence we conclude that the DN-map
\Lambda_{\sigma}(f)
is known from the knowledge of given DN-map
\Lambda_{\sigma,\gamma}
, for all values of
p
under consideration.

3.
Proof for the Theorem
1.1

In this section, we provide the reconstruction procedure of the coefficients
\sigma
, and
\gamma
.
In order to prove the Theorem
1.1
, we shall consider two separate cases based on the range of
p
.

3.1.
Reconstruction of
\sigma

For the reconstruction of
\sigma
from the knowledge of the DN-map
\Lambda_{\sigma}(f)
associated to BVP (
2.6
), one can refer to
[
20
, Theorem 5.1]
.

3.2.
Reconstruction of
\gamma

Using the
\varepsilon
-expansion of the DN-map, we will give a procedure for the recovery of the parameter
\gamma
at the boundary
\partial\Omega
. For this, our idea is to find a suitable integral identity which is known from the knowledge of the DN-map. By the asymptotic expansion of the solution along with appropriate choice of sequence of solutions of conductivity equation, the desired reconstruction of
\gamma
at the boundary of
\Omega
is carried out.

3.2.1.
Case:
p>2

Let us consider
w\in{W}^{2,m}(\Omega)
such that it satisfies

\displaystyle\nabla\cdot(\sigma\nabla w)=0\quad\text{ on }\Omega.

(3.1)

By multiplying equation (
2.3
) with
w
and integrating the resulting equation over
\Omega
, we get

\int_{\Omega}w(\nabla\cdot(\sigma\nabla u_{\varepsilon}+\gamma|\nabla u_{\varepsilon}|^{p-2}\nabla u_{\varepsilon}))~~dx=0

and it further yields the following equation by using the integration by parts

\int_{\Omega}\nabla w\cdot(\sigma\nabla u_{\varepsilon}+\gamma|\nabla u_{\varepsilon}|^{p-2}\nabla u_{\varepsilon})~dx=\int_{\partial\Omega}w(\sigma+\gamma|\nabla u_{\varepsilon}|^{p-2})\partial_{\nu}u_{\varepsilon}~dS.

Using (
3.1
), the above expression becomes

\int_{\Omega}\nabla w\cdot(\gamma|\nabla u_{\varepsilon}|^{p-2}\nabla u_{\varepsilon})~dx=\int_{\partial\Omega}w(\sigma+\gamma|\nabla u_{\varepsilon}|^{p-2})\partial_{\nu}u_{\varepsilon}~dS-\varepsilon\langle\Lambda_{\sigma}(f),w|_{\partial\Omega}\rangle.

(3.2)

We observe that the right hand side terms of (
3.2
) are known by the prescribed DN-maps
\Lambda_{\sigma,\gamma}
and
\Lambda_{\sigma}
respectively. As a result, the left hand side of (
3.2
) is known to us. Let us define

\displaystyle K^{*}

\displaystyle:=\int_{\Omega}\nabla w\cdot(\gamma|\nabla u_{\varepsilon}|^{p-2}\nabla u_{\varepsilon})~dx.

The term
K^{*}
is known from the knowledge of the DN-map associated with perturbed
p
-Laplacian problem.

We can further rewrite
K^{*}
as

\displaystyle K^{*}=\int_{\Omega}\nabla w\cdot(\gamma|\nabla u_{\varepsilon}|^{p-2}\nabla u_{\varepsilon})~dx

\displaystyle-\varepsilon^{p-1}~\int_{\Omega}\nabla w\cdot(\gamma|\nabla u_{0}|^{p-2}\nabla u_{0})~dx

\displaystyle+\varepsilon^{p-1}\int_{\Omega}\nabla w\cdot(\gamma|\nabla u_{0}|^{p-2}\nabla u_{0})~dx.

By taking into account the estimate (
2.14
), we deduce that

\displaystyle K^{*}

\displaystyle=\varepsilon^{p-1}\int_{\Omega}\nabla w\cdot(\gamma|\nabla u_{0}|^{p-2}\nabla u_{0})~dx+\mathcal{O}(\varepsilon^{2p-3}).

Further, we define
I(u_{0},w)
as follows:

\displaystyle I(u_{0},w):=\int_{\Omega}\nabla w\cdot(\gamma|\nabla u_{0}|^{p-2}\nabla u_{0})~dx.

(3.3)

As a consequence, we have

\displaystyle K^{*}=\varepsilon^{p-1}I(u_{0},w)+\mathcal{O}(\varepsilon^{2p-3}),

(3.4)

where
w\in W^{2,m}(\Omega)
and
u_{0}
satisfies the BVP (
2.6
). Furthermore, we observe that

\displaystyle I(u_{0},w)=\lim_{\varepsilon\rightarrow 0^{+}}\varepsilon^{1-p}\Big(K^{*}-\mathcal{O}(\varepsilon^{2p-3})\Big)

and subsequently,
I(u_{0},w)
is known to us.

Let us choose the functions
u_{1},u_{2},u_{3}\in{C}^{\infty}(\Omega)
such that

\displaystyle\nabla\cdot(\sigma\nabla u_{j})=0,\quad j=1,2,3.

(3.5)

In addition, we assume that
u_{1}
is real valued and
\nabla u_{1}
does not have any zeros.
Since
I(u_{0},w)
is known for any
w\in W^{2,m}(\Omega)
. In particular
I(u_{0},u_{3})
is known.
By defining
J_{1}
and
J_{2}
as follows:

\displaystyle J_{1}(u_{1},u_{2},u_{3}):

\displaystyle=\frac{2}{p-2}\partial_{\bar{z}}[I(u_{1}+z\overline{u_{2}},u_{3})]\Big|_{z=0}

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}\gamma|\nabla u_{1}|^{p-4}(\nabla u_{1}\cdot\nabla u_{2})(\nabla u_{1}\cdot\nabla u_{3})~dx,

\displaystyle J_{2}(u_{1},u_{2},u_{3}):

\displaystyle=\frac{2}{p-2}\partial_{z}[I(u_{1}+zu_{2},u_{3})]\Big|_{z=0}

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}\gamma|\nabla u_{1}|^{p-4}[(\overline{\nabla u_{1}}\cdot\nabla u_{2})(\nabla u_{1}\cdot\nabla u_{3})+\frac{2}{p-2}|\nabla u_{1}|^{2}(\nabla u_{2}\cdot\nabla u_{3})]~dx,

we conclude that the functionals
J_{1}(u_{1},u_{2},u_{3})
and
J_{2}(u_{1},u_{2},u_{3})
will be known to us.
Furthermore, we define

\displaystyle\beta_{u_{1}}(x):=\gamma|\nabla u_{1}(x)|^{p-2}

(3.6)

and

\displaystyle K(u_{1},u_{2},u_{3}):=\frac{p-2}{2}[J_{2}(u_{1},u_{2},u_{3})-J_{1}(u_{1},u_{2},u_{3})].

Hence
K
can be represented as

K(u_{1},u_{2},u_{3})=\int_{\Omega}\beta_{u_{1}}(\nabla u_{2}\cdot\nabla u_{3})~dx.

For each choice of
u_{2}
and
u_{3}
satisfying (
3.5
), the term
K(u_{1},u_{2},u_{3})
is known. Since,
\sigma
is real valued, therefore by choosing
u_{3}=\overline{u_{2}}
, we find that

K(u_{1},u_{2},\overline{u_{2}})=\int_{\Omega}\beta_{u_{1}}\nabla u_{2}\cdot\overline{\nabla u_{2}}~dx,

(3.7)

is known to us.

Let
x_{0}\in\partial\Omega
be arbitrary, but fixed. From the knowledge of
K(u_{1},u_{2},\overline{u_{2}})
in (
3.7
), we will recover
\beta_{u_{1}}
at
x_{0}
. By using (
3.6
), we recover
\gamma(x_{0})
from
\beta_{u_{1}}(x_{0})
. Following the similar approach presented in
[
24
,
3
]
, we can construct a sequence of solutions
\{u_{M}\}
satisfying the conductivity equation (
3.5
) with coefficient frozen at the boundary point
x_{0}
. Suppose
\rho
is a
C^{1}
function and we choose
\zeta
as a unit tangent vector to
\partial\Omega
at
x_{0}
such that

\displaystyle\zeta\cdot\nabla\rho(x_{0})=0,\qquad|\zeta|=|\nabla\rho(x_{0})|.

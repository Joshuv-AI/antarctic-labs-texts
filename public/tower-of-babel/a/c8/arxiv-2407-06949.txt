Decay estimates for a class of Dunkl wave equations
Cheng Luo, Shyam Swarup Mondal, Manli Song

Abstract
. Let Δ κ \Delta_{\kappa} be the Dunkl Laplacian on ℝ n \mathbb{R}^{n} and ϕ : ℝ + → ℝ \phi:\mathbb{R}^{+}\to\mathbb{R} is a smooth function. The aim of this manuscript is twofold. First, we study the decay estimate for a class of dispersive semigroup of the form e i ​ t ​ ϕ ​ ( − Δ κ ) e^{it\phi(\sqrt{-\Delta_{\kappa}})} . We overcome the difficulty arising from the non-homogeneousity of ϕ \phi by frequency localization. As applications, in the next part of the paper, we establish Strichartz estimates for some concrete wave equations associated with the Dunkl Laplacian Δ k , \Delta_{k}, which corresponds to ϕ ⁡ ( r ) = r , r 2 , r 2 + r 4 , 1 + r 2 , 1 + r 4 \phi(r)=r,r^{2},r^{2}+r^{4},\sqrt{1+r^{2}},\sqrt{1+r^{4}} , and r μ , 0 < μ ≤ 2 , μ ≠ 1 r^{\mu},0<\mu\leq 2,\mu\neq 1 . 1.
Introduction

The main aim of this paper is to investigate decay estimates for a class of dispersive equations of the form

\begin{cases}i\partial_{t}u+\phi(\sqrt{-\Delta_{\kappa}})u=f,\\
u|_{t=0}=u_{0},\end{cases}

(1.1)

where
\Delta_{\kappa}
is the Dunkl Laplacian on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
f:\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{C}
, and
\phi:\mathbb{R}^{+}\to\mathbb{R}
is a smooth function that satisfies some growth condition.

Dispersive inequalities for evolution equations, such as the Schrödinger and wave equations play a significant role in the study of semilinear and quasilinear problems, which naturally appear in several physical applications. Proving dispersion means estimating the decay in time on the evolution group associated with the free equation. In recent times, dispersive properties of evolution equations have become a crucial tool in the study of a variety of questions, including local and global existence for nonlinear equations, well-posedness of Cauchy problems
for nonlinear equations in Sobolev spaces of low order, scattering theory, and many others. In many circumstances, the essential step in proving this time decay is based on the application of a stationary phase theorem on an (approximate) representation of the solution. Dispersion phenomena, when combined with an abstract functional analysis argument, famously known as the
TT^{*}
-argument (see
[
35
]
), offer a range of estimates involving space-time Lebesgue norms. These inequalities also known as Strichartz estimates, have evolved into a fundamental and amazing tool in the study of nonlinear partial differential equations over the last few decades, see
[
8
]
and references therein.

Strichartz estimates also be interpreted as Fourier restriction estimates, which have a vital role in classical harmonic analysis and are closely linked to arithmetic combinatorics. Considerable attention has been devoted by several researchers to deriving Strichartz estimates in different frameworks. For example, in the Euclidean setting, these estimates have been proved for several dispersive equations, such as the wave equation and Schrödinger equation, for instance, see the pioneering works
[
25
,
35
,
51
]
. The theory of Strichartz estimates has also been extended in the non-Euclidean frameworks.
For example, see
[

,

,

,

,

,

,

,

,

,

]
for Strichartz estimates on H-type groups and more generality of step

stratified Lie groups,
[

,

,

,

]
for hyperbolic spaces,
[

]
for compact Riemannian manifolds, and
[

One of the essential ingredients in the derivation of Strichartz estimates is the dispersive estimates. Our aim of this paper is to establish a decay estimate for a class of dispersive equations as follows:

\begin{cases}i\partial_{t}u+\phi(\sqrt{-\Delta_{\kappa}})u=f,\\
u|_{t=0}=u_{0},\end{cases}

(1.2)

where
\Delta_{\kappa}
is the Dunkl Laplacian on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
f:\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{C}
, and
\phi:\mathbb{R}^{+}\to\mathbb{R}
is a smooth function satisfying

(C1) There exists
m_{1}>0
such that for any
\alpha\geqslant 2
and
\alpha\in\mathbb{N}
,

|\phi^{\prime}(r)|\sim r^{m_{1}-1},\quad|\phi^{(\alpha)}(r)|\lesssim r^{m_{1}-\alpha},\quad r\geqslant 1.

(C2) There exists
m_{2}>0
such that for any
\alpha\geqslant 2
and
\alpha\in\mathbb{N}
,

|\phi^{\prime}(r)|\sim r^{m_{2}-1},\quad|\phi^{(\alpha)}(r)|\lesssim r^{m_{2}-\alpha},\quad 0<r<1.

(C3) There exists
\alpha_{1}>0
such that

|\phi^{\prime\prime}(r)|\sim r^{\alpha_{1}-2},\quad r\geqslant 1.

(C4) There exists
\alpha_{2}>0
such that

|\phi^{\prime\prime}(r)|\sim r^{\alpha_{2}-2},\quad 0<r<1.

Here we use a Fourier multiplier notation to define
\phi(\sqrt{-\Delta_{\kappa}})
as in the following form

\phi(\sqrt{-\Delta_{\kappa}})f=\mathcal{F}_{\kappa}^{-1}\phi(|\xi|)\mathcal{F}_{\kappa}f,

where
\mathcal{F}_{\kappa}
denotes Dunkl transform, defined in (
2.3
), Section
2
. Notice that the conditions (C1) and (C3) represent the homogeneous order of
\phi
in high frequency, and the conditions (C2) and (C4) reflect the homogeneous order of
\phi
in low frequency. If
\phi
satisfies conditions (C1) and (C3), then
\alpha_{1}\leq m_{1}
. Similarly, if
\phi
satisfies (C2) and (C4), then
\alpha_{2}\geq m_{2}
.

Here, we want to emphasize that several dispersive wave equations associated with Dunkl Laplacian reduced to type (
1.2
).
For instance, the Dunkl Schrödinger equation corresponds to
\phi(r)=r^{2}
, the Dunkl wave equation corresponds to
\phi(r)=r
, the fractional Dunkl Schrödinger equation corresponds to
\phi(r)=r^{\mu}
,
0<\mu<2,\mu\neq 1
, the fourth-order Dunkl Schrödinger equation corresponds to
\phi(r)=r^{2}+r^{4}
, the beam equation
\phi(r)=\sqrt{1+r^{4}}
, and the Klein-Gordon equation corresponds to
\phi(r)=\sqrt{1+r^{2}}
, among others.

Dunkl operators are differential-difference operators that generalize the standard partial derivatives. These operators were introduced by Charles Dunkl
[
19
]
using a finite reflection group and a parameter function on its root system. Applications of Dunkl operators in mathematics and mathematical physics are numerous. For example, they play an important role in the integration of quantum many-body problems of the Calogero-Moser-Sutherland type and are employed in the study of probabilistic processes. Additionally, these operators have had a lasting impression on the study of special functions in one and multiple variables as well as orthogonal polynomials, see
[
21
]
.

For
\kappa=0,
as the the Dunkl Laplacian
\Delta_{\kappa}
reduces to usual Laplacian
\Delta
on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, the system (
1.2
) reduces to

\begin{cases}i\partial_{t}u+\phi(\sqrt{-\Delta})u=f,\\
u|_{t=0}=u_{0}.\end{cases}

(1.3)

In 1977, Strichartz
[
51
]
derived the priori estimates for the solution to (
1.3
) in the space-time norm using the classical Fourier restriction theorem of Stein and Tomas
[
49
,
54
,
55
]
. However, later, it was improved by Ginibre-Velo
[
25
]
using a standard duality argument along with the dispersive estimate of the form

\displaystyle\left\|e^{it\phi(\sqrt{-\Delta})}u_{0}\right\|_{X}\lesssim|t|^{-\theta}\left\|u_{0}\right\|_{X^{*}},

(1.4)

where
X^{*}
is the dual space of
X
. In 2008, Guo-Peng-Wang
[
28
]
used a unified way to study the decay for a class of dispersive semigroup
e^{it\phi(\sqrt{-\Delta})}
on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
for the system (
1.3
). They assumed that
\phi:\mathbb{R}^{+}\to\mathbb{R}
is smooth satisfying (C1)-(C4) and obtained several decay estimates in time for the dispersive semigroup
e^{it\phi(\sqrt{-\Delta})}
by introducing Littlewood–Paley projector operator, see
[
28
, Theorem 1]
.

Motivated by the recent works, in this paper, we aim to establish the decay estimate for a class of dispersive equations associated with Dunkl Laplacian (
1.2
). Note that, if
\phi
is a homogeneous function of order
m
, namely,
\phi(\lambda r)=

\lambda^{m}\phi(r)
for all
\lambda>0
, the dispersive estimate can be easily obtained by a theorem of Littman and dyadic decomposition. However, things become complicated when the function
\phi
is not homogeneous because the scaling constants can not be effectively separated from the time. Since the phases we consider are not necessarily homogeneous or weakly dispersive, to overcome the difficulty, we use a Littlewood-Paley decomposition (see
[
28
]
) to more accurately capture the
difference between the low and the high frequencies in different scales and catch the possible need for extra regularity.

Let
S_{0}
and
\tilde{\Delta}_{j}
be the Littlewood-Paley decomposition related to the Dunkl Laplacian
\Delta_{\kappa}
and
N=2\gamma+n
(see Section
2
for detailed definition). Then the main results of this paper are as follows:

Theorem 1.1
.

Assume
\phi:\mathbb{R}^{+}\to\mathbb{R}
is smooth and
U_{t}=e^{it\phi(\sqrt{-\Delta_{\kappa}})}
. Then the subsequent outcomes are valid.

(A)

For
j\geq 0
, assume that
\phi
satisfies (C1), then

\|U_{t}\tilde{\Delta}_{j}u_{0}\|_{L_{\kappa}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\lesssim|t|^{-\theta}2^{j\left(N-m_{1}\theta\right)}\|u_{0}\|_{L_{\kappa}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})},\,0\leq\theta\leq\frac{N-1}{2}.

(1.5)

In addition, if
\phi
satisfies (C3), then

\|U_{t}\tilde{\Delta}_{j}u_{0}\|_{L_{\kappa}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\lesssim|t|^{-\frac{N-1+\theta}{2}}2^{j\left(N-\frac{m_{1}(N-1+\theta)}{2}-\frac{\theta(\alpha_{1}-m_{1})}{2}\right)}\|u_{0}\|_{L_{\kappa}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})},\,0\leq\theta\leq 1.

(1.6)

(B)

For
j<0
, suppose
\phi
satisfies (C2), then

\|U_{t}\tilde{\Delta}_{j}u_{0}\|_{L_{\kappa}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\lesssim|t|^{-\theta}2^{j\left(N-m_{2}\theta\right)}\|u_{0}\|_{L_{\kappa}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})},\,0\leq\theta\leq\frac{N-1}{2}.

In addition, if
\phi
satisfies (C4), then

\|U_{t}\tilde{\Delta}_{j}u_{0}\|_{L_{\kappa}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\lesssim|t|^{-\frac{N-1+\theta}{2}}2^{j\left(N-\frac{m_{2}(N-1+\theta)}{2}-\frac{\theta(\alpha_{2}-m_{2})}{2}\right)}\|u_{0}\|_{L_{\kappa}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})},\,0\leq\theta\leq 1.

(C)

If
\phi
satisfies (C2), then

\|U_{t}S_{0}u_{0}\|_{L_{\kappa}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\lesssim(1+|t|)^{-\theta}\|u_{0}\|_{L_{\kappa}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})},\,\theta=\min\left(\frac{N}{m_{2}},\frac{N-1}{2}\right).

(1.7)

In addition, if
\phi
satisfies (C4) and
\alpha_{2}=m_{2}
, then

\|U_{t}S_{0}u_{0}\|_{L_{\kappa}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\lesssim(1+|t|)^{-\theta}\|u_{0}\|_{L_{\kappa}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})},\,\theta=\min\left(\frac{N}{m_{2}},\frac{N}{2}\right).

(1.8)

We prove the above result in Section
3
. The main difficulty in the setup of Dunkl is that we don’t have an explicit expression for the Dunkl transform compared to the classical Fourier transform. In this paper, we used Littlewood-Paley decomposition to better understand the regularity is needed at low and high frequencies in order to get a time decay. We also use the fact that Dunkl transform for radial functions can be expressed in terms of integrals of Bessel functions. In order to estimate these integrals, we rely on Van der Corput’s Lemma and the decay properties of the Bessel functions.

Using the decay estimates in Theorem
1.1
,
we establish Strichartz estimates for the Dunkl Schrödinger equation (corresponds to
\phi(r)=r^{2}
), the Dunkl wave equation (corresponds to
\phi(r)=r
), the fractional Dunkl Schrödinger equation (corresponds to
\phi(r)=r^{\mu}
),
0<\mu<2,\mu\neq 1
, the fourth-order Dunkl Schrödinger equation (corresponds to
\phi(r)=r^{2}+r^{4}
), the beam equation (corresponds to
\phi(r)=\sqrt{1+r^{4}}
), and the Klein-Gordon equation (corresponds to
\phi(r)=\sqrt{1+r^{2}}
). We also prove the global well-posedness for the nonlinear Klein-Gordon equation and beam equation for small initial data.

There are many situations where dispersion phenomena fail, for instance in compact manifolds, on some domains, on the Heisenberg group. However, in some cases, Strichartz estimates (in weak forms) or smoothing properties can be established using other approaches that are not based on dispersive inequality techniques. The Fourier restriction theorem and Kato smoothing effect techniques stand out among them.
Recently, the authors in

2.1.
Dunkl operators

The basic ingredient in the theory of Dunkl operators are root systems and finite reflection groups. We start this section by the definition of root system.

Let
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
denotes the standard Euclidean scalar product in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. For
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, we denote
|x|
as
|x|=\langle x,x\rangle^{1/2}
.
For
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\backslash\{0\},
we denote
r_{\alpha}
as the reflection with respect to the hyperplane
\langle\alpha\rangle^{\perp}
, orthogonal to
\alpha
and is defined by

r_{\alpha}(x):=x-2\frac{\langle\alpha,x\rangle}{|\alpha|^{2}}\alpha,\quad x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}.

A finite set
\mathcal{R}
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}\backslash\{0\}
is said to be a root system if the following holds:

(1)

r_{\alpha}(\mathcal{R})=\mathcal{R}
for all
\alpha\in\mathcal{R}
,

(2)

\mathcal{R}\cap\mathbb{R}\alpha=\{\pm\alpha\}
for all
\alpha\in\mathcal{R}
.

For a given root system
\mathcal{R}
, the subgroup
G\subset O(n,\mathbb{R})
generated by the reflections
\left\{r_{\alpha}\mid\alpha\in\mathcal{R}\right\}
is called the finite Coxeter group associated with
\mathcal{R}
. The dimension of
span\mathcal{R}
is called the rank of
\mathcal{R}
. For a detailed study on the theory of finite reflection groups, we refer to
[
30
]
. Let
\mathcal{R}^{+}:=\{\alpha\in\mathcal{R}:\langle\alpha,\beta\rangle>0\}
for some
\beta\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\backslash\bigcup_{\alpha\in\mathcal{R}}\langle\alpha\rangle^{\perp}
, be a fix positive root system.

Some typical examples of such a system are the Weyl groups such as the symmetric group
S_{n}
for the type
A_{n-1}
root system and the hyperoctahedral group for the type
B_{n}
root system. In addition,
H_{3},H_{4}
(icosahedral groups) and,
I_{2}(n)
(symmetry group of the regular
n
-gon) are also the Coxeter groups.

A multiplicity function for
G
is a function
\kappa:\mathcal{R}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
which is constant on
G
-orbits. Setting
\kappa_{\alpha}:=\kappa(\alpha)
for
\alpha\in\mathcal{R},
from the definition of
G
-invariant, we have
\kappa_{g\alpha}=\kappa_{\alpha}
for all
g\in G
. We say
\kappa
is non-negative if
\kappa_{\alpha}\geq 0
for all
\alpha\in\mathcal{R}
. The
\mathbb{C}
-vector space of non-negative multiplicity functions on
\mathcal{R}
is denoted by
\mathcal{K}^{+}
. Let us denote
\gamma
as
\gamma=\gamma(\kappa):=\sum\limits_{\alpha\in\mathcal{R}^{+}}\kappa(\alpha)
. For convenience, we denote
N=2\gamma+n
throughout the paper.

For
\xi\in\mathbb{C}^{n}
and
\kappa\in\mathcal{K}^{+},
Dunkl in 1989 introduced a family of first-order differential-difference operators
T_{\xi}:=T_{\xi}(\kappa)
by

\displaystyle T_{\xi}(\kappa)f(x):=\partial_{\xi}f(x)+\sum_{\alpha\in\mathcal{R}^{+}}\kappa_{\alpha}\langle\alpha,\xi\rangle\frac{f(x)-f\left(r_{\alpha}x\right)}{\langle\alpha,x\rangle},\quad f\in C^{1}\left(\mathbb{R}^{n}\right),

(2.1)

where
\partial_{\xi}
denotes the directional derivative corresponding to
\xi
. The operator
T_{\xi}
defined in (
2.1
), formally known as Dunkl operator and is one of the most important developments in the theory of special functions associated with root systems
[
18
]
. They commute pairwise and are skew-symmetric with respect to the
G
-invariant measure
h_{\kappa}(x)dx
, where the weight function

h_{\kappa}(x):=\prod\limits_{\alpha\in\mathcal{R}^{+}}|\langle\alpha,x\rangle|^{2\kappa_{\alpha}}

is of
homogeneous degree
2\gamma
. Thanks to the
G
-invariance of the multiplicity function, the definition of the Dunkl operator is independent of the choice of the positive subsystem
\mathcal{R}^{+}
. In
[
19
]
, it is shown that for any
\kappa\in\mathcal{K}^{+}
, there is a unique linear isomorphism
V_{\kappa}
(Dunkl’s intertwining operator) on the space
\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
of polynomials on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
such that

(1)

V_{\kappa}\left(\mathcal{P}_{m}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\right)=\mathcal{P}_{m}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
for all
m\in\mathbb{N}
,

(2)

V_{\kappa}|_{\mathcal{P}_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}=id
,

(3)

T_{\xi}(\kappa)V_{\kappa}=V_{\kappa}\partial_{\xi}
,

where
\mathcal{P}_{m}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
denotes the space of homogeneous polynomials of degree
m
.

For any finite reflection group
G
and any
k\in\mathcal{K}^{+}
, Rösler in
[
44
]
proved that there exists a unique positive Radon probability measure
\rho_{x}^{\kappa}
on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
such that

V_{\kappa}f(x)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}f(\xi)d\rho_{x}^{\kappa}(\xi).

(2.2)

The measure
\rho_{x}^{k}
depends on
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
and its support is contained in the ball
B\left(\|x\|\right):=\{\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:\|\xi\|\leq\|x\|\}
. In view of the Laplace type representation (
2.2
), Dunkl’s intertwining operator
V_{\kappa}
can be extended to a larger class of function spaces.

Let
\{\xi_{1},\xi_{2},\cdots,\xi_{n}\}
be an orthonormal basis of
(\mathbb{R}^{n},\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle)
. Then the Dunkl Laplacian operator
\Delta_{\kappa}
is defined as

\Delta_{\kappa}=\sum_{j=1}^{n}T^{2}_{\xi_{j}}(\kappa).

The definition of
\Delta_{\kappa}
is independent of the choice of the orthonormal basis of
\mathbb{R}^{n}.
In fact, one can see that the operator
\Delta_{\kappa}
also can be expressed as

\Delta_{\kappa}f(x)=\Delta f(x)+\sum_{\alpha\in\mathcal{R}^{+}}\kappa_{\alpha}\left\{\frac{2\langle\nabla f(x),\alpha\rangle}{\langle\alpha,x\rangle}-|\alpha|^{2}\frac{f(x)-f(r_{\alpha}x)}{\langle\alpha,x\rangle^{2}}\right\},\quad f\in C^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n}),

where
\nabla
and
\Delta
are the usual gradiant and Laplacian operator on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, respectively. Observe that, for
\kappa\equiv 0
, the Dunkl Laplacian operator
\Delta_{\kappa}
reduces to the classical Euclidean Laplacian
\Delta
.

For every
y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, the system

\begin{cases}T_{\xi}u(x,y)=\langle y,\xi\rangle\,u(x,y),\quad\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{n},\\
u(0,y)=1,\end{cases}

admits a unique analytic solution on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, which we denote as
K(x,y)
and is known as the Dunkl kernel in general. The kernel has a unique holomorphic extension to
\mathbb{C}^{n}\times\mathbb{C}^{n}
and is given by

K(x,y)=V_{\kappa}(e^{\langle\cdot,y\rangle})(x)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}e^{\langle\xi,y\rangle}d\rho_{x}^{\kappa}(\xi),\quad\forall x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{n},

where the positive Radon probability measure
\rho_{x}^{k}
depends on
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
and its support is contained in the ball
B\left(\|x\|\right):=\{\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:\|\xi\|\leq\|x\|\}
. When
\kappa\equiv 0
, the Dunkl kernel
K(x,y)
reduces to the exponential
e^{\langle x,y\rangle}
. For
x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
z,w\in\mathbb{C}^{n}
, the Dunkl kernel satisfies the following properties:

(i)

K(z,w)=K(w,z)
and
K(\lambda z,w)=K(z,\lambda w),\quad\lambda\in\mathbb{C}
.

(ii)

For any
\nu\in\mathbb{N}^{n}
, we have

|D_{z}^{\nu}K(x,z)|\leq|x|^{|\nu|}e^{|\operatorname{Re}(z)||x|},

where
D_{z}^{\nu}=\frac{\partial^{|\nu|}}{\partial{z_{1}}^{\nu_{1}}\cdots{z_{n}}^{\nu_{n}}}
and
|\nu|=\nu_{1}+\cdots+\nu_{n}
. In particular, for any
x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, we have

|K(x,iy)|\leq 1.

(iii)

K(ix,y)=\overline{K(-ix,y)}
and
K(gx,gy)=K(x,y),\quad g\in G
.

2.2.
The Dunkl transform

For
1\leq p<\infty
, let
L_{\kappa}^{p}\left(\mathbb{R}^{n}\right)
be the space of
L^{p}
-functions on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
with respect to the weight
h_{\kappa}(x)
with the
L_{\kappa}^{p}\left(\mathbb{R}^{n}\right)
-norm

\|f\|_{L_{\kappa}^{p}\left(\mathbb{R}^{n}\right)}=\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|f(x)|^{p}h_{\kappa}(x)dx\right)^{\frac{1}{p}},

and
\|f\|_{L_{\kappa}^{\infty}\left(\mathbb{R}^{n}\right)}=ess\sup\limits_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}|f(x)|
.
The Dunkl transform of
f\in L_{\kappa}^{1}\left(\mathbb{R}^{n}\right)
is defined by

\mathcal{F}_{\kappa}f(y)=\frac{1}{c_{\kappa}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}f(x)K(x,-iy)h_{\kappa}(x)dx,

(2.3)

where
c_{\kappa}
is the Mehta-type constant defined by

c_{\kappa}=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}e^{-\frac{|x|^{2}}{2}}h_{\kappa}(x)dx.

When
\kappa\equiv 0
, the Dunkl transform coincides with the classical Fourier transform. The Dunkl transform plays the same role as the Fourier transform in classical Fourier analysis and enjoys properties similar to those of the classical Fourier transform. With
N=2\gamma+n
, here we list some basic properties of the Dunkl transform (see
[
20
,
32
,
16
]
).

(i)

For all
f\in L_{\kappa}^{1}\left(\mathbb{R}^{n}\right)
, we have

\|\mathcal{F}_{\kappa}f\|_{L_{\kappa}^{\infty}\left(\mathbb{R}^{n}\right)}\leq\frac{1}{c_{\kappa}}\|f\|_{L_{\kappa}^{1}\left(\mathbb{R}^{n}\right)}.

(ii)

For any function
f
in the Schwartz space
\mathcal{S}\left(\mathbb{R}^{n}\right)
, we have

\mathcal{F}_{\kappa}(T_{\xi}f)(y)=i\langle\xi,y\rangle\mathcal{F}_{\kappa}f(y),\quad y,\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{n}.

In particular, it follows that

\mathcal{F}_{\kappa}(\Delta_{\kappa}f)(y)=-|y|^{2}\mathcal{F}_{\kappa}f(y),\quad y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}.

(iii)

For all
f\in L_{\kappa}^{1}\left(\mathbb{R}^{n}\right)
, we have

\mathcal{F}_{\kappa}(f(\cdot/\lambda))(y)=\lambda^{N}\mathcal{F}_{\kappa}f(\lambda y),\quad y\in\mathbb{R}^{n},

for all
\lambda>0
.

(iv)

The Dunkl transform
\mathcal{F}_{\kappa}
is a homeomorphism of the Schwartz space
\mathcal{S}\left(\mathbb{R}^{n}\right)
and its inverse is given by
\mathcal{F}_{\kappa}^{-1}g(x)=\mathcal{F}_{\kappa}g(-x),
for all
g\in\mathcal{S}\left(\mathbb{R}^{n}\right)
. In addition, for all
f\in\mathcal{S}\left(\mathbb{R}^{n}\right)
, it satisfies

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|f(x)|^{2}h_{\kappa}(x)dx=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|\mathcal{F}_{\kappa}f(y)|^{2}h_{\kappa}(y)dy.

In particular, the Dunkl transform extends to an isometric isomorphism on
L_{\kappa}^{2}\left(\mathbb{R}^{n}\right)
. The Dunkl transform can be extended to the space of tempered distributions
\mathcal{S}^{\prime}\left(\mathbb{R}^{n}\right)
and is also a homeomorphism of
\mathcal{S}^{\prime}\left(\mathbb{R}^{n}\right)
.

(v)

For every
f\in L_{\kappa}^{1}\left(\mathbb{R}^{n}\right)
such that
\mathcal{F}_{\kappa}f\in L_{\kappa}^{1}\left(\mathbb{R}^{n}\right)
, we have the inversion formula

f(x)=\frac{1}{c_{\kappa}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\mathcal{F}_{\kappa}f(y)K(ix,y)h_{\kappa}(y)dy,\quad a.e.\;x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}.

(vi)

If
f
is a radial function in
L_{\kappa}^{1}\left(\mathbb{R}^{n}\right)
such that
f(x)=\tilde{f}(|x|)
, then

\mathcal{F}_{\kappa}f(y)=\frac{1}{\Gamma(N/2)}\int_{0}^{\infty}\tilde{f}(r)\frac{J_{\frac{N-2}{2}}(r|y|)}{\left(r|y|\right)^{\frac{N-2}{2}}}r^{N-1}dr,

(2.4)

where
J_{\nu}
denotes the Bessel function of order
\nu>-\frac{1}{2}
.

2.3.
Dunkl convolution operator

For given
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, the Dunkl translation operator
f\mapsto\tau_{x}f
is defined on
\mathcal{S}\left(\mathbb{R}^{n}\right)
by

\mathcal{F}_{\kappa}(\tau_{x}f)(y)=K(x,iy)\mathcal{F}_{\kappa}f(y),\quad y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}.

At the moment, the explicit formula of the Dunkl translation operator is known only in two cases. One is when
f(x)=\tilde{f}(|x|)
is a continuous radial function in
L_{k}^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, the Dunkl translation operator is represented by (see
[
44
,
15
]
)

\tau_{x}f(y)=V_{\kappa}[\tilde{f}\left(\sqrt{|y|^{2}+|x|^{2}-2\langle y,\cdot\rangle}\right)](x)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\tilde{f}\left(\sqrt{|y|^{2}+|x|^{2}-2\langle y,\xi\rangle}\right)d\rho^{\kappa}_{x}(\xi).

The Dunkl translation operator can also be extended to the space of tempered distributions
\mathcal{S}^{\prime}\left(\mathbb{R}^{n}\right)
. It turns out to be rather difficult to extend important results in classical Fourier analysis to the setting of the Dunkl transform in general. One of the difficulties comes from the fact that the Dunkl translation operator is not positive in
general. In fact, even
L_{\kappa}^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
-boundedness of
\tau_{x}
is not established in general. On the other hand, when
f
is a radial function in
L_{\kappa}^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
,
1\leq p\leq\infty
, the following holds (see
[
26
,
52
]
)

\|\tau_{x}f\|_{L_{\kappa}^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leq\|f\|_{L_{\kappa}^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}.

Using the Dunkl translation operator, we define the Dunkl convolution of functions
f,g\in\mathcal{S}\left(\mathbb{R}^{n}\right)
by

f*_{\kappa}g(x)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\tau_{x}f(-y)g(y)h_{\kappa}(y)dy,\quad x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}.

The Dunkl convolution satisfies the following properties (see
[
52
]
).

(1)

\mathcal{F}_{\kappa}(f*_{\kappa}g)=\mathcal{F}_{\kappa}(f)\mathcal{F}_{\kappa}(g)
and
f*_{\kappa}g=g*_{\kappa}f
.

(2)

Young’s inequality: Let
1\leq p,q,r\leq\infty
such that
1+\frac{1}{r}=\frac{1}{p}+\frac{1}{q}
. If
f\in L^{p}_{\kappa}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
and
g
is a radial function of
L_{\kappa}^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, then
f*_{\kappa}g\in L_{\kappa}^{r}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
and we have

\|f*_{\kappa}g\|_{L_{\kappa}^{r}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leq\|f\|_{L_{\kappa}^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\|g\|_{L_{\kappa}^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}.

(2.5)

2.4.
Dunkl Besov spaces

In this subsection, we recall the definition of the Dunkl Besov spaces using Littlewood-Paley decomposition related to the Dunkl Laplacian
\Delta_{\kappa}
(see
[
34
,
38
]
). Dunkl-Besov spaces will be a convenient tool to prove many of our results.

Fix a non-increasing smooth function
R
on
[0,+\infty)
such that
R=1
in
[0,1/2]
and
R=0
in
[1,+\infty)
. Let
\psi(r)=R(r/2)-R(r)
and
0\leq\psi\leq 1
such that
\hbox{supp\,}\psi\subseteq C_{0}=[1/2,2]
. Moreover, we observe that

\displaystyle R(r)+\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\psi(2^{-j}r)

\displaystyle=1,\quad r\geq 0,

\displaystyle\underset{j\in\mathbb{Z}}{\sum}\psi(2^{-j}r)

\displaystyle=1,\quad r>0.

For
u\in\mathcal{S}^{\prime}\left(\mathbb{R}^{n}\right)
, we define the Littlewood-Paley decomposition related to the Dunkl Laplacian by

S_{0}u=R(\sqrt{-\Delta_{\kappa}})u=\mathcal{F}_{\kappa}^{-1}\left(R(|\cdot|)\mathcal{F}_{\kappa}u\right)
and
\tilde{\Delta}_{j}u=\psi(2^{-j}\sqrt{-\Delta_{\kappa}})u=\mathcal{F}_{\kappa}^{-1}\left(\psi(2^{-j}|\cdot|)\mathcal{F}_{\kappa}u\right),
for all
j\in\mathbb{Z}
. Moreover, we have

u=S_{0}u+\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\tilde{\Delta}_{j}u\;\in\;\mathcal{S}^{\prime}\left(\mathbb{R}^{n}\right).

Moreover, we also have

S_{0}u=S_{0}(\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\tilde{\Delta}_{j})u=\sum_{j\leq 0}S_{0}\tilde{\Delta}_{j}u.

In addition, from Bernstein’s inequalities
[
34
]
, for any
\sigma\in\mathbb{R}
,
j\in\mathbb{Z}
,
1\leq p,q\leq\infty
and
u\in\mathcal{S}^{\prime}\left(\mathbb{R}^{n}\right)
, we have

\displaystyle\left\|(-\Delta_{\kappa})^{\frac{\sigma}{2}}\tilde{\Delta}_{j}u\right\|_{L_{\kappa}^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}

\displaystyle\lesssim 2^{j\sigma}\|\tilde{\Delta}_{j}u\|_{L_{\kappa}^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})},

(2.6)

\displaystyle\left\|\tilde{\Delta}_{j}u\right\|_{L_{\kappa}^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}

\displaystyle\lesssim 2^{jN\left(\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{q}\right)}\|\tilde{\Delta}_{j}u\|_{L_{\kappa}^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}.

(2.7)

For
1\leq p,q\leq\infty
and
s\in\mathbb{R}
, the Dunkl Besov space
B^{s,\kappa}_{p,q}
is defined as the set of tempered distributions
u\in\mathcal{S}^{\prime}\left(\mathbb{R}^{n}\right)
such that
\|u\|_{B^{s,\kappa}_{p,q}}<\infty
with

\|u\|_{B^{s,\kappa}_{p,q}}=\|S_{0}u\|_{L_{\kappa}^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}+\begin{cases}\left(\sum\limits_{j=0}^{\infty}2^{jsq}\|\tilde{\Delta}_{j}u\|_{L_{\kappa}^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{q}\right)^{\frac{1}{q}},\quad 1\leq q<\infty\\
\sup_{j\geq 0}2^{js}\|\tilde{\Delta}_{j}u\|_{L_{\kappa}^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\quad,\quad q=\infty.\end{cases}

Also, for
1\leq p\leq\infty
and
s\in\mathbb{R}
, the Dunkl Sobolev space
H_{p}^{s,\kappa}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
is
defined as the set of tempered distributions
u\in\mathcal{S}^{\prime}\left(\mathbb{R}^{n}\right)
such that
\mathcal{F}^{-1}_{\kappa}\left((1+|\cdot|^{2})^{\frac{s}{2}}\mathcal{F}_{\kappa}u\right)\in L_{\kappa}^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
with the norm

\|u\|_{H_{p}^{s,\kappa}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}=\|\mathcal{F}^{-1}_{\kappa}\left((1+|\cdot|^{2})^{\frac{s}{2}}\mathcal{F}_{\kappa}u\right)\|_{L_{\kappa}^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}.

Throughout the paper, we denote
H^{s,\kappa}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
for
H_{2}^{s,\kappa}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. Moreover, we have the equivalent property that

u\in H^{s,\kappa}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\Leftrightarrow u\in B^{s,\kappa}_{2,2}(\mathbb{R}^{n}).

Now we list some important properties related to the Besov space
B^{s,\kappa}_{p,q}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
in the following proposition.

Proposition 2.1
.

[
34
]
Let
p,q\in[1,\infty]
and
s\in\mathbb{R}
. Then

(1)

The space
B^{s,\kappa}_{p,q}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
is a Banach space with the norm
||\cdot||_{B^{s,\kappa}_{p,q}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}
.

(2)

The definition of
B^{s,k}_{p,q}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
does not depend on the choice of the function
R
in the Littlewood-Paley decomposition.

(3)

The dual space of
B^{s,\kappa}_{p,q}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
is
B^{-s,\kappa}_{p^{\prime},q^{\prime}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
;

(4)

For any
\sigma>0
, we have the continuous inclusion
B^{s+\sigma,\kappa}_{p,q}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\subseteq B^{s,\kappa}_{p,q}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
.

(5)

Let
u\in\mathcal{S}^{\prime}\left(\mathbb{R}^{n}\right)
and
\sigma\in\mathbb{R}
, then
u\in B^{s,\kappa}_{p,q}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
if and only if
(-\Delta_{\kappa})^{\sigma/2}u\in B^{s-\sigma,\kappa}_{p,q}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
.

(6)

For any
1\leq p_{1}\leq p_{2}\leq\infty
,
1\leq q_{1}\leq q_{2}\leq\infty,
and
s_{1}\leq s_{2}\in\mathbb{R}
such that
s_{1}-\frac{N}{p_{1}}=s_{2}-\frac{N}{p_{2}}
, we also have the continuous inclusion

B^{s_{1},\kappa}_{p_{1},q_{1}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\subseteq B^{s_{2},\kappa}_{p_{2},q_{2}}(\mathbb{R}^{n}).

(7)

For all
p\in[2,\infty]
, we have the continuous inclusion
B^{0,\kappa}_{p,2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\subseteq L_{\kappa}^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
.

(8)

B^{0,\kappa}_{2,2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})=L_{\kappa}^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
.

2.5.
Technical Lemmas

In this subsection, we recall several important properties to prove the main result of this paper. From the Dunkl transform and Young’s inequality, the
L_{\kappa}^{1}-L_{\kappa}^{\infty}
decay estimates are reduced to a list of oscillatory integrals related to Bessel functions. In order to estimate the oscillatory integrals, we recall the stationary phase lemmas and the properties of Bessel functions. We first recall the van der Corput lemma.

Lemma 2.2

(Van der Corput lemma
[
50
]
)
.

Let
g\in C^{\infty}([a,b])
be real-valued such that
|g^{\prime\prime}(x)|\geq\delta
for any
x\in[a,b]
with
\delta>0
. Then for any function
\psi\in C^{\infty}([a,b])
, there exists a constant
C
(does not depend on
\delta,a,b,g
or
\psi
) such that

\left|\int_{a}^{b}e^{{ig(x)}}\psi(x)\,dx\right|\leq C{\delta^{-1/2}}\left(\|\psi\|_{\infty}+\|\psi^{\prime}\|_{1}\right).

Let
J_{\nu}
be the Bessel function of order
\nu>-\frac{1}{2}
, defined as

J_{\nu}(r)=\frac{(\frac{r}{2})^{\nu}}{\Gamma(\nu+\frac{1}{2})\pi^{\frac{1}{2}}}\int_{-1}^{1}e^{ir\tau}(1-\tau^{2})^{\nu-\frac{1}{2}}d\tau.

We list some properties of
J_{\nu}
in the following lemma.

Lemma 2.3

(see
[
27
]
)
.

For
r>0
and
\nu>-\frac{1}{2}
, we have

\displaystyle(1)J_{\nu}(r)\leq C_{\nu}r^{\nu},

(2.8)

\displaystyle(2)\frac{d}{dr}\left(r^{-\nu}J_{\nu}(r)\right)=-r^{-\nu}J_{\nu+1}(r),

(2.9)

\displaystyle(3)J_{\nu}(r)\leq C_{\nu}r^{-\frac{1}{2}},

(2.10)

\displaystyle(4)J_{\nu}(r)=\frac{(\frac{r}{2})^{\nu}}{\Gamma(\nu+\frac{1}{2})\Gamma(\frac{1}{2})}\left[ie^{-ir}\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-rt}(t^{2}+2it)^{\nu-\frac{1}{2}}dt-ie^{ir}\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-rt}(t^{2}-2it)^{\nu-\frac{1}{2}}dt\right].

(2.11)

Remark 2.4
.

From (
2.8
) and (
2.9
) of Lemma
2.3
, we can easily obtain that for any
0\leq s\leq 2
and
\beta\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
,

\left|\frac{d^{\beta}}{dr^{\beta}}\left(\psi(r)\frac{J_{\frac{N-2}{2}}(rs)}{(rs)^{\frac{N-2}{2}}}r^{N-1}\right)\right|\leq C_{\beta},

(2.12)

where
\psi
is the function in Section
2.4
.
Again from (
2.11
), we have the identity

\frac{J_{\frac{N-2}{2}}(r)}{r^{\frac{N-2}{2}}}=C\left(e^{ir}h(r)+e^{-ir}\overline{h(r)}\right),

(2.13)

where

h(r)=-i\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-rt}(t^{2}-2it)^{\frac{N-3}{2}}dt.

Moreover, integrating by parts (if necessary) and using Van der Corput’s Lemma, for any
\beta\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
, one can get

\left|\frac{d^{\beta}}{dr^{\beta}}h(r)\right|\leq C_{\beta}(1+r)^{-\frac{N-1}{2}-\beta},

(2.14)

for all
\beta\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
. Thus, from (
2.14
), for any
s>2
and
\beta\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
, we have

\left|\frac{d^{\beta}}{dr^{\beta}}\left(\psi(r)h(rs)r^{N-1}\right)\right|\leq C_{\beta}s^{-\frac{N-1}{2}}.

(2.15)

We also exploit the following estimates, which can be easily proved by comparing the sums with the corresponding integrals.

Lemma 2.5
.

Fix
\beta>0
. There exists
C_{\beta}>0
such that for any
A>0
, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z},2^{j}\leq A}2^{j\beta}

\displaystyle\leq C_{\beta}A^{\beta},

\displaystyle\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z},2^{j}>A}2^{-j\beta}

\displaystyle\leq C_{\beta}A^{-\beta}.

Finally, we apply the following duality arguments (see
[
25
]
) to prove the Strichartz estimates for some concrete equations related to the Dunkl Laplacian.

Lemma 2.6
.

Let
H
be a Hilbert space,
X
be a Banach space,
X^{*}
is the dual of
X
, and
D
be a vector space densely contained in
X
. Let
A\in\mathcal{L}_{a}(D,H)
and
A^{*}\in\mathcal{L}_{a}(H,D_{a}^{*})
be its adjoint operator, defined by

\langle A^{*}v,f\rangle_{D}=\langle v,Af\rangle_{H},\quad\forall f\in D,\quad\forall v\in H,

where
\mathcal{L}_{a}(Y,Z)
is the space of linear maps from a vector space
Y
to a vector space
Z
,
D^{*}_{a}
is the algebraic dual of
D
,
\langle\varphi,f\rangle_{D}
is the pairing between
D^{*}_{a}
and
D
, and
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{H}
is the scalar product in
H
. Then the following three conditions are equivalent:

(1)

There exists
0\leqslant a\leqslant\infty
such that for all
f\in D
,

\|Af\|\leqslant a\|f\|_{X}.

(2)

\mathfrak{R}(A^{*})\subset X^{*}
and there exists
0\leqslant a\leqslant\infty
, such that for all
v\in H
,

\|A^{*}v\|_{X^{*}}\leqslant a\|v\|.

(3)

\mathfrak{R}(A^{*}A)\subset X^{*}
,and there exists
a
,
0\leqslant a\leqslant\infty
, such that for all
f\in D
,

\|A^{*}Af\|_{X^{*}}\leqslant a^{2}\|f\|_{X},

where
\|\cdot\|
denote the norm in
H
.

The constant
a
is the same in all three parts. If one of those conditions is satisfied, the operators
A
and
A^{*}A
extend by continuity to bounded operators from
X
to
H
and from
X
to
X^{*}
, respectively.

Lemma 2.7
.

Let
H
,
D
and two triplets
(X_{i},A_{i},a_{i}),i=1,2
, satisfy any of the conditions of Lemma
2.6
. Then for all choices of
i,j=1,2
,
\mathfrak{R}(A_{i}^{*}A_{j})\subset X_{i}^{*}
, and for all
f\in D
, we have

\|A_{i}^{*}A_{j}f\|_{X_{i}^{*}}\leqslant a_{i}a_{j}\|f\|_{X_{j}}.

Lemma 2.8
.

Let
H
be a Hilbert space,
I
be an interval of
\mathbb{R}
,
X\subset\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(I\times\mathbb{R}^{n})
be a Banach space which is stable under time restriction, and let
X
and

satisfy (any of) the conditions of Lemma
2.6
. Then the operator

is a bounded operator from

to

and from

to

.

3.
Proof of main results

This section is devoted to proving Theorem
1.1
, i.e., decay estimate for a class of
dispersive semigroup
e^{it\phi(\sqrt{-\Delta_{\kappa}})}
on

Proof of Theorem
1.1
.

In order to prove part
(A)
first, we note that

U_{t}\tilde{\Delta}_{j}u_{0}=\mathcal{F}_{\kappa}^{-1}\left(e^{it\phi(|\cdot|)}\psi(2^{-j}|\cdot|)\mathcal{F}_{\kappa}u_{0}\right)=\mathcal{F}_{\kappa}^{-1}\left(e^{it\phi(|\cdot|)}\psi(2^{-j}|\cdot|)\right)*_{\kappa}u_{0}.

(3.1)

It follows from Young’s inequality (
2.5
) that

\|U_{t}\tilde{\Delta}_{j}u_{0}\|_{L_{\kappa}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leq\|\mathcal{F}_{\kappa}^{-1}\left(e^{it\phi(|\cdot|)}\psi(2^{-j}|\cdot|)\right)\|_{L_{\kappa}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\|u_{0}\|_{L_{\kappa}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}.

Now our aim is to estimate
I_{j}(x)=\mathcal{F}_{\kappa}^{-1}\left(e^{it\phi(|\cdot|)}\psi(2^{-j}|\cdot|)\right)(x)
. From the Dunkl transform formula of a radial function (
2.4
), we have

\displaystyle I_{j}(x)

\displaystyle=\mathcal{F}_{\kappa}^{-1}\left(e^{it\phi(|\cdot|)}\psi(2^{-j}|\cdot|)\right)(x)

(3.2)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\Gamma(N/2)}\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{it\phi(r)}\psi(2^{-j}r)\frac{J_{\frac{N-2}{2}}(r|x|)}{\left(r|x|\right)^{\frac{N-2}{2}}}r^{N-1}dr

\displaystyle=\frac{{2^{jN}}}{\Gamma(N/2)}\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{it\phi(2^{j}r)}\psi(r)\frac{J_{\frac{N-2}{2}}(2^{j}r|x|)}{\left(2^{j}r|x|\right)^{\frac{N-2}{2}}}r^{N-1}dr

\displaystyle=II_{j}(2^{j}s),

where
s=|x|
and

II_{j}(s)=\frac{2^{jN}}{\Gamma(N/2)}\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{it\phi(2^{j}r)}\psi(r)\frac{J_{\frac{N-2}{2}}(rs)}{\left(rs\right)^{\frac{N-2}{2}}}r^{N-1}dr.

We will continue the discussion in two cases. First, we use the vanishing property at the origin (
2.8
) and the recurring property for the Bessel functions (
2.9
). On the other hand, we apply the decay property of the Bessel function (
2.13
).

Case 1.

s\leq 2
:
By denoting
D_{r}=\frac{1}{i2^{j}t\phi^{\prime}(2^{j}r)}\frac{d}{dr}
, we notice that

D_{r}\left(e^{it\phi(2^{j}r)}\right)=e^{it\phi(2^{j}r)},\;D_{r}^{*}f=\frac{i}{2^{j}t}\frac{d}{dr}\left(\frac{1}{\phi^{\prime}(2^{j}r)}f\right).

For
\beta\in\mathbb{N}
and
r\in[1/2,2]
, it follows from the hypothesis (C1) that

\frac{d^{\beta}}{dr^{\beta}}\left(\frac{1}{\phi^{\prime}(2^{j}r)}\right)\leq C_{\beta}2^{-j(m_{1}-1)}.

(3.3)

Using integration by part, we have

\displaystyle II_{j}(s)

\displaystyle=\frac{{2^{jN}}}{\Gamma(N/2)}\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{it\phi(2^{j}r)}\psi(r)\frac{J_{\frac{N-2}{2}}(rs)}{\left(rs\right)^{\frac{N-2}{2}}}r^{N-1}dr

\displaystyle=\frac{{2^{jN}}}{\Gamma(N/2)}\int_{0}^{\infty}D_{r}\left(e^{it\phi(2^{2j}r)}\right)\psi(r)\frac{J_{\frac{N-2}{2}}(rs)}{\left(rs\right)^{\frac{N-2}{2}}}r^{N-1}dr

\displaystyle=\frac{{2^{jN}}}{\Gamma(N/2)}\cdot\frac{i}{2^{j}t}\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{it\phi(2^{j}r)}\frac{d}{dr}\left(\frac{1}{\phi^{\prime}(2^{j}r)}\psi(r)\frac{J_{\frac{N-2}{2}}(rs)}{\left(rs\right)^{\frac{N-2}{2}}}r^{N-1}\right)dr

\displaystyle=\frac{{2^{jN}}}{\Gamma(N/2)}\cdot\left(\frac{i}{2^{j}t}\right)^{Q}\sum_{\ell=0}^{Q}\sum_{(\beta_{1},\beta_{2},\cdots,\beta_{Q})\in\Lambda_{Q}^{\ell}}C_{Q,\ell}\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{it\phi(2^{j}r)}

\displaystyle\times\prod_{i=1}^{Q}\frac{d^{\beta_{i}}}{dr^{\beta_{i}}}\left(\frac{1}{\phi^{\prime}(2^{j}r)}\right)\frac{d^{Q-\ell}}{dr^{Q-\ell}}\left(\psi(r)\frac{J_{\frac{N-2}{2}}(rs)}{\left(rs\right)^{\frac{N-2}{2}}}r^{N-1}\right)\,dr,

for any
Q\in\mathbb{N}
, where
\Lambda_{Q}^{\ell}=\{(\beta_{1},\beta_{2},\cdots,\beta_{Q})\in\{0,1,2,\cdots,\ell\}^{Q}:\sum\limits_{i=1}^{Q}\beta_{i}=\ell\}
. Now using the recurring property for the Bessel functions (

2.8

|II_{j}(s)|\leq C_{Q,N,k}|t|^{-Q}2^{j(N-m_{1}Q)},

(3.4)

for any

|II_{j}(s)|\lesssim|t|^{-\theta}2^{j(N-m_{1}\theta)}.

(3.5)

This completes the proof of (A) in this case.

Case 2.

s>2
. In this case, we shall apply the decay property of Bessel functions.
From the identity (
2.13
), we can write

\displaystyle II_{j}(s)

\displaystyle=C2^{jN}\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{it\phi(2^{j}r)}\psi(r)\left(e^{irs}h(rs)+e^{-irs}\overline{h(rs)}\right)r^{N-1}\,dr

4.1.
Wave equation

First, we consider the Dunkl-wave equation as

\begin{cases}\partial_{t}^{2}u-\Delta_{\kappa}u=g,\\
u|_{t=0}=u_{0},\\
\partial_{t}u|_{t=0}=u_{1}.\end{cases}

(4.2)

By Duhamel’s principle, the solution is formally given by

u(t)=\frac{d{\mathcal{W}}_{t}}{dt}u_{0}+{\mathcal{W}}_{t}u_{1}-\int_{0}^{t}{\mathcal{W}}_{t-\tau}g(\tau,\cdot)\,d\tau,

where

\mathcal{W}_{t}=\frac{\sin(t\sqrt{-\Delta_{\kappa}})}{\sqrt{-\Delta_{\kappa}}},\quad\frac{d\mathcal{W}_{t}}{dt}=\cos(t\sqrt{-\Delta_{\kappa}}).

This reduces to the semigroup
W_{t}=e^{it\sqrt{-\Delta_{\kappa}}}
, which corresponds to the case
\phi(r)=r
. Thus
\phi
satisfies (C1) and (C2) with
m_{1}=m_{2}=1
. Using Theorem
1.1
, we obtain the following decay estimate for the wave operator.

Proposition 4.4
.

Assume
2\leq p\leq\infty
,
1\leq q\leq\infty
,
\delta=\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{p}
and
2s\leq-(N+1)\delta
, then we have

\|W_{t}f\|_{B_{p,q}^{s,\kappa}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\lesssim k(t)\|f\|_{B_{p^{\prime},q}^{-s,\kappa}(\mathbb{R}^{n})},\quad k(t)=\begin{cases}|t|^{-2\max(s+N\delta,0)},\,&|t|\leq 1,\\
|t|^{-(N-1)\delta},\,&|t|\geq 1.\end{cases}

Proof.

First, we prove the case
|t|\geq 1
. It follows from (
1.7
) of Theorem
1.1
by taking
\theta=\frac{N-1}{2}
and Plancherel’s identity that

\displaystyle\|W_{t}S_{0}f\|_{L_{\kappa}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}

\displaystyle\lesssim|t|^{-\frac{N-1}{2}}\|S_{0}f\|_{L_{\kappa}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})},

\displaystyle\|W_{t}S_{0}f\|_{L_{\kappa}^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}

\displaystyle\leq\|S_{0}f\|_{L_{\kappa}^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}.

From (
1.5
) of Theorem
1.1
by taking
\theta=\frac{N-1}{2}
and Plancherel’s identity, for
j\geq 0
, we have

\displaystyle\left\|W_{t}\tilde{\Delta}_{j}f\right\|_{L_{\kappa}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}

\displaystyle\lesssim|t|^{-\frac{N-1}{2}}2^{j\frac{N+1}{2}}\|\tilde{\Delta}_{j}f\|_{L_{\kappa}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})},

\displaystyle\left\|W_{t}\tilde{\Delta}_{j}f\right\|_{L_{\kappa}^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}

\displaystyle\leq\|\tilde{\Delta}_{j}f\|_{L_{\kappa}^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}.

Applying the Riesz-Thorin interpolation theorem, for any
j\geq 0
, we have

\displaystyle\|W_{t}S_{0}f\|_{L_{\kappa}^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}

\displaystyle\lesssim|t|^{-(N-1)\delta}\|S_{0}f\|_{L_{\kappa}^{p^{\prime}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})},

\displaystyle\|W_{t}\tilde{\Delta}_{j}f\|_{L_{\kappa}^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}

\displaystyle\lesssim|t|^{-(N-1)\delta}2^{j(N+1)\delta}\|\tilde{\Delta}_{j}f\|_{L_{\kappa}^{p^{\prime}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leq|t|^{-(N-1)\delta}2^{-2js}\|\tilde{\Delta}_{j}f\|_{L_{\kappa}^{p^{\prime}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})},

where in the last inequality we used the fact that
2s\leq-(N+1)\delta
and
\delta=\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{p}
. Therefore

\|W_{t}f\|_{B_{p,q}^{s,\kappa}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\lesssim|t|^{-(N-1)\delta}\|f\|_{B_{p^{\prime},q}^{-s,\kappa}(\mathbb{R}^{n})},\,|t|\geq 1.

(4.3)

Next, we consider the case
|t|\leq 1
. Putting
\theta\geq 0
in (
1.7
) of Theorem
1.1
and applying Plancherel’s identity and Riesz-Thorin interpolation theorem, we get

\|W_{t}S_{0}f\|_{L_{\kappa}^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\lesssim\|S_{0}f\|_{L_{\kappa}^{p^{\prime}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}.

Again for
j\geq 0

1.5
) of Theorem
1.1
with Plancherel’s identity and Riesz-Thorin interpolation theorem, we obtain

\|W_{t}\tilde{\Delta}_{j}f\|_{L_{\kappa}^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\lesssim|t|^{-2\theta\delta}2^{2j(N-\theta)\delta}\|\tilde{\Delta}_{j}f\|_{L_{\kappa}^{p^{\prime}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})},

for

Therefore

If

(4.4)

for

\tilde{p}_{i},r_{i}\in[2,\infty]
and

\frac{2}{r_{1}}+\frac{N-1}{\tilde{p}_{1}}=\frac{N-1}{2},\;\;\frac{2}{r_{2}}+\frac{N-1}{\tilde{p}_{2}}\leq\frac{N-1}{2},~~\text{ and }~~s_{i}=-\frac{N+1}{2}\left(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{\tilde{p}_{i}}\right),\;i=1,2,

except for
(N,\tilde{p}_{i},{r}_{i})=(3,\infty,2)
. If
p_{i},r_{i}\in[2,\infty],i=1,2
with the sole exception of the pair
(N,p_{1},r_{1},p_{2},r_{2})=(3,\infty,2,3,2)
satisfies the condition

\frac{1}{r_{1}}+\frac{N}{p_{1}}=\frac{N}{2}-1,\;\;\frac{2}{r_{1}}+\frac{N-1}{p_{1}}\leq\frac{N-1}{2}~~\text{ and }~~\frac{1}{r_{2}}+\frac{N}{p_{2}}\leq\frac{N}{2},

then we the following relations

s_{1}+1\leq 0,\;\;s_{1}+1-\frac{N}{\tilde{p}_{1}}=0-\frac{N}{p_{1}},

and

-s_{2}\geq 0,\;\;-s_{2}-\frac{N}{\tilde{p}_{2}}=0-\frac{N}{p_{2}}.

Therefore, using the embedding relation between the Besov space and Lebesgue space in Proposition
2.1
, we obtain

B_{\tilde{p}_{1},2}^{s_{1}+1,\kappa}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\subseteq L^{p_{1}}_{\kappa}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
and
L_{\kappa}^{p_{2}^{\prime}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\subseteq B_{\tilde{p}^{\prime}_{2},2}^{-s_{2},\kappa}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
and this completes our desired result.
∎

4.2.
Fractional Schrödinger equation

Consider the fractional Dunkl-Schrödinger equation (
0<\mu\leq 2
and
\mu\neq 1
)

\begin{cases}i\partial_{t}u+(-\Delta_{\kappa})^{\frac{\mu}{2}}u=g,\\
u|_{t=0}=u_{0}.\end{cases}

(4.5)

By Duhamel’s principle, the solution is formally given by

u(t)=\mathcal{S}^{\mu}_{t}u_{0}-i\int_{0}^{t}\mathcal{S}^{\mu}_{t-\tau}g(\tau,\cdot)\,d\tau,

where
\mathcal{S}^{\mu}_{t}=e^{it(-\Delta_{\kappa})^{\frac{\mu}{2}}}
and it corresponds to the case when
\phi(r)=r^{\mu}
. By a simple calculation, we see that

\phi^{\prime}(r)=\mu r^{\mu-1},\quad\phi^{\prime\prime}(r)=\mu(\mu-1)r^{\mu-2}.

This shows that
\phi
satisfies (C1)-(C4) with
m_{1}=\alpha_{1}=m_{2}=\alpha_{2}=\mu
. Using Theorem
1.1
, we obtain the following decay estimate for
\mathcal{S}^{\mu}_{t}

Proposition 4.8
.

Let
0<\mu\leq 2
and
\mu\neq 1
. Assume
2\leq p\leq\infty
,
1\leq q\leq\infty
,
\delta=\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{p}

\|\mathcal{S}^{\mu}_{t}f\|_{B_{p,q}^{s,\kappa}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\lesssim k(t)\|f\|_{B_{p^{\prime},q}^{-s,\kappa}(\mathbb{R}^{n})},\quad k(t)=\begin{cases}|t|^{-\frac{2}{\mu}\max(s+N\delta,0)},\,&|t|\leq 1,\\
|t|^{-N\delta},\,&|t|\geq 1.\end{cases}

Proof.

First, we prove the case
|t|\geq 1
. From (
1.8
) of Theorem
1.1
with
\theta=\frac{N}{2}
, Plancherel’s identity yields

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{S}^{\mu}_{t}S_{0}f\|_{L_{\kappa}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}

\displaystyle\lesssim|t|^{-\frac{N}{2}}\|S_{0}f\|_{L_{\kappa}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})},

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{S}^{\mu}_{t}S_{0}f\|_{L_{\kappa}^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}

\displaystyle\leq\|S_{0}f\|_{L_{\kappa}^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}.

Again from (
1.6
) of Theorem
1.1
by taking
\theta=1
, for
j\geq 0
, Plancherel’s identity gives

\displaystyle\left\|\mathcal{S}^{\mu}_{t}\tilde{\Delta}_{j}f\right\|_{L_{\kappa}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}

\displaystyle\lesssim|t|^{-\frac{N}{2}}2^{jN(1-\frac{\mu}{2})}\|\tilde{\Delta}_{j}f\|_{L_{\kappa}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})},

\displaystyle\left\|\mathcal{S}^{\mu}_{t}\tilde{\Delta}_{j}f\right\|_{L_{\kappa}^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}

\displaystyle\leq\|\tilde{\Delta}_{j}f\|_{L_{\kappa}^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}.

Now applying the Riesz-Thorin interpolation theorem, for any
j\geq 0
, we obtain

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{S}^{\mu}_{t}S_{0}f\|_{L_{\kappa}^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}

\displaystyle\lesssim|t|^{-N\delta}\|S_{0}f\|_{L_{\kappa}^{p^{\prime}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})},

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{S}^{\mu}_{t}\tilde{\Delta}_{j}f\|_{L_{\kappa}^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}

\displaystyle\lesssim|t|^{-N\delta}2^{2jN\left(1-\frac{\mu}{2}\right)\delta}\|\tilde{\Delta}_{j}f\|_{L_{\kappa}^{p^{\prime}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leq|t|^{-N\delta}2^{-2js}\|\tilde{\Delta}_{j}f\|_{L_{\kappa}^{p^{\prime}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}.

This implies that

\|\mathcal{S}^{\mu}_{t}f\|_{B_{p,q}^{s,\kappa}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\lesssim|t|^{-N\delta}\|f\|_{B_{p^{\prime},q}^{-s,\kappa}(\mathbb{R}^{n})},\,|t|\geq 1.

Next, we discuss the case
|t|\leq 1
. For
\theta=0
in (
1.8
) of Theorem
1.1
, applying
\theta=0
, Plancherel’s identity and Riesz-Thorin interpolation theorem, we get

\|\mathcal{S}^{\mu}_{t}S_{0}f\|_{L_{\kappa}^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\lesssim\|S_{0}f\|_{L_{\kappa}^{p^{\prime}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}.

For
j\geq 0
, it follows from (
1.5
)
and (
1.6
) of Theorem
1.1
, Plancherel’s identity and Riesz-Thorin interpolation theorem that for
0\leq\theta\leq\frac{N}{2}
, we have

\|\mathcal{S}^{\mu}_{t}\tilde{\Delta}_{j}f\|_{L_{\kappa}^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\lesssim|t|^{-2\theta\delta}2^{2j(N-\theta\mu)\delta}\|\tilde{\Delta}_{j}f\|_{L_{\kappa}^{p^{\prime}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}.

This implies that

2^{js}\|\mathcal{S}^{\mu}_{t}\tilde{\Delta}_{j}f\|_{L_{\kappa}^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\lesssim|t|^{-2\theta\delta}2^{2j(s+(N-\theta\mu)\delta)}2^{-js}\|\tilde{\Delta}_{j}f\|_{L_{\kappa}^{p^{\prime}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}.

If

and

\|\mathcal{S}^{\mu}_{t}f\|_{B_{p,q}^{s,\kappa}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\lesssim|t|^{-\frac{2}{\mu}\max(s+N\delta,0)}\|f\|_{B_{p^{\prime},q}^{-s,\kappa}(\mathbb{R}^{n})},\,|t|\leq 1,

which completes the proof of the proposition.
∎

4.3.
Fourth-order Schrödinger equation

Consider the fourth-order Schrödinger equation

\begin{cases}i\partial_{t}u+\Delta_{\kappa}^{2}u-\Delta_{\kappa}u=g,\\
u|_{t=0}=u_{0}.\end{cases}

(4.9)

By Duhamel’s principle, the solution is formally given by

u(t)=\mathcal{U}_{t}u_{0}-i\int_{0}^{t}\mathcal{U}_{t-\tau}g(\tau,\cdot)\,d\tau,

where
\mathcal{U}_{t}=e^{it(\Delta_{\kappa}^{2}-\Delta_{\kappa})}
and it corresponds to the case when
\phi(r)=r^{4}+r^{2}
. By a simple calculation, we see that

\phi^{\prime}(r)=4r^{3}+2r,\quad\phi^{\prime\prime}(r)=12r^{2}+2.

Thus
\phi
satisfies (C1)–(C4) with
m_{1}=\alpha_{1}=4
,
m_{2}=\alpha_{2}=2
. Using Theorem
1.1
, we obtain the following decay estimate for

Proposition 4.13
.

Assume
2\leq p\leq\infty
,
1\leq q\leq\infty
,

and

\|\mathcal{U}_{t}f\|_{B_{p,q}^{s,\kappa}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\lesssim k(t)\|f\|_{B_{p^{\prime},q}^{-s,\kappa}(\mathbb{R}^{n})},\quad k(t)=\begin{cases}|t|^{-\frac{1}{2}\max(s+N\delta,0)},\,&|t|\leq 1,\\
|t|^{-N\delta},\,&|t|\geq 1.\end{cases}

Proof.

First, we prove the case
|t|\geq 1
. For
\theta=\frac{N}{2}
in (
1.8
) of Theorem
1.1
, Plancherel’s identity gives

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{U}_{t}S_{0}f\|_{L_{\kappa}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}

\displaystyle\lesssim|t|^{-\frac{N}{2}}\|S_{0}f\|_{L_{\kappa}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})},

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{U}_{t}S_{0}f\|_{L_{\kappa}^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}

\displaystyle\leq\|S_{0}f\|_{L_{\kappa}^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}.

Applying Riesz-Thorin interpolation theorem, we have

\|\mathcal{U}_{t}S_{0}f\|_{L_{\kappa}^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\lesssim|t|^{-N\delta}\|S_{0}f\|_{L_{\kappa}^{p^{\prime}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}.

(4.10)

Again for
j\geq 0
, it follows from (
1.5
) and (
1.6
) of Theorem
1.1
that

\|\mathcal{U}_{t}\tilde{\Delta}_{j}f|_{L_{\kappa}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\lesssim|t|^{-\theta}2^{j\left(N-4\theta\right)}\|\tilde{\Delta}_{j}f\|_{L_{\kappa}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})},\,0\leq\theta\leq\frac{N}{2}.

(4.11)

Taking
\theta=\frac{N}{2}
in (
4.11
), combined with Plancherel’s identity and Riesz-Thorin interpolation theorem, it indicates

\|\mathcal{U}_{t}\tilde{\Delta}_{j}f\|_{L_{\kappa}^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\lesssim|t|^{-N\delta}2^{-2jN\delta}\|\tilde{\Delta}_{j}f\|_{L_{\kappa}^{p^{\prime}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leq|t|^{-N\delta}2^{-2js}\|\tilde{\Delta}_{j}f\|_{L_{\kappa}^{p^{\prime}}},

(4.12)

for any
j\geq 0
. From (
4.10
) and (
4.12
), we obtain our desired result of the proposition in this case.

Next, we discuss the case
|t|\leq 1
. For
\theta=0
in (
1.7
) of Theorem
1.1
, Plancherel’s identity and Riesz-Thorin interpolation theorem gives

\|\mathcal{U}_{t}S_{0}f\|_{L^{p}_{\kappa}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\lesssim\|S_{0}f\|_{L^{p^{\prime}}_{\kappa}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}.

Again for
j\geq 0
, from (
4.11

, we obtain

\|\mathcal{U}_{t}\tilde{\Delta}_{j}f\|_{L^{p}_{\kappa}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\lesssim|t|^{-2\theta\delta}2^{2j(N-4\theta)\delta}\|\tilde{\Delta}_{j}f\|_{L^{p^{\prime}}_{\kappa}(\mathbb{R}^{n})},

which reduces to

2^{js}\|\mathcal{U}_{t}\tilde{\Delta}_{j}f\|_{L^{p}_{\kappa}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\lesssim|t|^{-2\theta\delta}2^{2j\left(s+(N-4\theta)\delta\right)}2^{-js}\|\tilde{\Delta}_{j}f\|_{L^{p^{\prime}}_{\kappa}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}.

If

and hence

\|\mathcal{U}_{t}f\|_{B_{p,q}^{s,\kappa}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\lesssim|t|^{-\frac{1}{2}\max(s+N\delta,0)}\|f\|_{B_{p,q}^{-s,\kappa}(\mathbb{R}^{n})},\,|t|\leq 1.

4.4.
Klein-Gordon equation

Moreover, we consider the Klein-Gordon equation

\begin{cases}\partial_{t}^{2}u-\Delta_{\kappa}u+u=g,\\
u|_{t=0}=u_{0},\\
\partial_{t}u|_{t=0}=u_{1}.\end{cases}

(4.13)

u(t)=\frac{d\mathcal{K}_{t}}{dt}u_{0}+\mathcal{K}_{t}u_{1}-\int_{0}^{t}\mathcal{K}_{t-\tau}g(\tau,\cdot)\,d\tau,

where

K_{t}=e^{it\sqrt{I-\Delta_{\kappa}}}
, which corresponds to the case when
\phi(r)=\sqrt{1+r^{2}}
.

\phi^{\prime}(r)=r(1+r^{2})^{-\frac{1}{2}},\;\phi^{\prime\prime}(r)=(1+r^{2})^{-\frac{3}{2}}.

This shows that
\phi
satisfies (C1)–(C4) with
m_{1}=1,\alpha_{1}=-1,
and
m_{2}=\alpha_{2}=2
. Using Theorem
1.1
, we obtain the following decay estimate for

Proposition 4.14
.

Assume
2\leq p\leq\infty
,

, and

1.6

\|B_{t}S_{0}f\|_{L_{\kappa}^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\lesssim|t|^{-\frac{N\delta}{2}}\|S_{0}f\|_{L_{\kappa}^{p^{\prime}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}.

1.1
that

\|B_{t}\tilde{\Delta}_{j}f\|_{L_{\kappa}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\lesssim|t|^{-\theta}2^{j\left(N-2\theta\right)}\|\tilde{\Delta}_{j}f\|_{L_{\kappa}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})},\,0\leq\theta\leq\frac{N}{2}.

(4.16)

Taking
\theta=\frac{N}{2}
in (
4.16
), combined with Plancherel’s identity and Riesz-Thorin interpolation theorem, we obtain

\|B_{t}\tilde{\Delta}_{j}f\|_{L_{\kappa}^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\lesssim|t|^{-N\delta}\|\tilde{\Delta}_{j}f\|_{L_{\kappa}^{p^{\prime}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leq|t|^{-\frac{N\delta}{2}}2^{-2js}\|\tilde{\Delta}_{j}f\|_{L_{\kappa}^{p^{\prime}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})},

(4.17)

for any
j\geq 0
. From (
4.15
) and (
4.17
), we get our desired result.

Next, we consider the case
|t|<1
. By taking
\theta=0
in (
1.8
) of Theorem
1.1
along with Plancherel’s identity and Riesz-Thorin interpolation theorem, we have

\|B_{t}S_{0}f\|_{L_{\kappa}^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\lesssim\|S_{0}f\|_{L_{\kappa}^{p^{\prime}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}.

For
j\geq 0
, it follows from (
4.16
), Plancherel’s identity, and Riesz-Thorin interpolation theorem, for
0\leq\theta\leq\frac{N}{2}
, we have

\|B_{t}\tilde{\Delta}_{j}f\|_{L_{\kappa}^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\lesssim|t|^{-2\theta\delta}2^{2j\left(N-2\theta\right)\delta}\|\tilde{\Delta}_{j}f\|_{L_{\kappa}^{p^{\prime}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})},

and thus

2^{js}\|B_{t}\tilde{\Delta}_{j}f\|_{L_{\kappa}^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\lesssim|t|^{-2\theta\delta}2^{2j\left(s+\left(N-2\theta\right)\delta\right)}2^{-js}\|\tilde{\Delta}_{j}f\|_{L_{\kappa}^{p^{\prime}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}.

If
-N\delta\leq s\leq 0
, we can choose
0\leq\theta\leq\frac{N}{2}
such that
s+N\delta=2\theta\delta
. If
s<-N\delta
, we can choose
\theta=0
. Thus, we have

2^{js}\|B_{t}\tilde{\Delta}_{j}f\|_{L_{\kappa}^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\lesssim|t|^{-\max(s+N\delta,0)}2^{-js}\|\tilde{\Delta}_{j}f\|_{L_{\kappa}^{p^{\prime}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})},

which completes the proof of the proposition.
∎

5.
Well-posedness results for nonlinear Klein-Gordon and beam equations

Using the decay estimates for the nonlinear Klein-Gordon and beam equation with the different decay rates between
|t|>1
and
|t|\leq 1
in the previous section, we prove the global well-posedness results for nonlinear problems. First, we consider the Cauchy problem for the nonlinear Klein-Gordon equation

\begin{cases}\partial_{t}^{2}u-\Delta_{\kappa}u+u=F(u),\\
u|_{t=0}=u_{0},\\
\partial_{t}u|_{t=0}=u_{1},\end{cases}

(5.1)

with a power-like nonlinearity
F(u)
satisfies

F(0)=0,\quad|F(u)-F(v)|\leq C(|u|^{\alpha}+|v|^{\alpha})|u-v|

(5.2)

for some
\alpha>0
, where
C
is a positive constant.
By Duhamel’s principle, the solution of (
5.1
) is formally given by

u=\frac{d\mathcal{K}_{t}}{dt}u_{0}+\mathcal{K}_{t}u_{1}-\int_{0}^{t}\mathcal{K}_{t-\tau}F(u)\,d\tau,

where
\mathcal{K}_{t}=\frac{\sin(t\sqrt{I-\Delta_{\kappa}})}{\sqrt{I-\Delta_{\kappa}}}
. Similarly, as in the Laplacian in Euclidean space, we find the critical index as

\alpha_{K}(N)=\frac{2-N+\sqrt{N^{2}+12N+4}}{2N}.

Using the classical fixed point argument, we obtain the global well-posedness theorem for the nonlinear Klein-Gordon equation.

Theorem 5.1
.

Let
\alpha_{K}(N)<\alpha<\frac{4}{N}
,
\sigma(\alpha)=\frac{\alpha(N+2)}{2(\alpha+2)}
, and
(u_{0},u_{1})\in H^{\sigma(\alpha),\kappa}_{\frac{\alpha+2}{\alpha+1}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\times H^{\sigma(\alpha)-1,\kappa}_{\frac{\alpha+2}{\alpha+1}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
with sufficient small norm. Then the equation (
5.1
) has a unique solution

u\in C(\mathbb{R},H^{\frac{\alpha}{\alpha+2},\kappa}(\mathbb{R}^{n}))\cap L^{\alpha+1}(\mathbb{R},L_{\kappa}^{\alpha+2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})).

Proof.

By Proposition
2.1
and
4.14
, we have

\displaystyle\|\frac{d\mathcal{K}_{t}}{dt}u_{0}\|_{L^{\alpha+2}_{k}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}

\displaystyle\leq\|\frac{d\mathcal{K}_{t}}{dt}u_{0}\|_{B^{0,\kappa}_{\alpha+2,2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\lesssim k(t)\|u_{0}\|_{B^{\sigma(\alpha),\kappa}_{\frac{\alpha+2}{\alpha+1},2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\lesssim k(t)\|u_{0}\|_{H^{\sigma(\alpha),\kappa}_{\frac{\alpha+2}{\alpha+1}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})},

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{K}_{t}u_{1}\|_{L^{\alpha+2}_{k}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}

\displaystyle\leq\|\mathcal{K}_{t}u_{1}\|_{B^{0,\kappa}_{\alpha+2,2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\lesssim k(t)\|u_{1}\|_{B^{\sigma(\alpha)-1,\kappa}_{\frac{\alpha+2}{\alpha+1},2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\lesssim k(t)\|u_{1}\|_{H^{\sigma(\alpha)-1,\kappa}_{\frac{\alpha+2}{\alpha+1}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})},

(5.3)

where

k(t)=\begin{cases}|t|^{-\max(\frac{\alpha(N-2)}{2(\alpha+2)},0)},\,&|t|\leq 1,\\
|t|^{-\frac{\alpha N}{2(\alpha+2)}},\,&|t|\geq 1.\end{cases}

For
\alpha_{K}(N)<\alpha<\frac{4}{N}
, we notice that

(\alpha+1)\frac{\alpha(N-2)}{2(\alpha+2)}<1,\;\;(\alpha+1)\frac{\alpha N}{2(\alpha+2)}>1,\;\;\text{and }\;\sigma(\alpha)<1.

This implies that
k\in L^{\alpha+1}(\mathbb{R})
and

\displaystyle\|\frac{d\mathcal{K}_{t}}{dt}u_{0}\|_{L^{\alpha+1}(\mathbb{R},L^{\alpha+2}_{k}(\mathbb{R}^{n}))}

\displaystyle\leq C\|u_{0}\|_{H^{\sigma(\alpha),\kappa}_{\frac{\alpha+2}{\alpha+1}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})},

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{K}_{t}u_{1}\|_{L^{\alpha+1}(\mathbb{R},L^{\alpha+2}_{k}(\mathbb{R}^{n}))}

\displaystyle\leq C\|u_{1}\|_{H^{\sigma(\alpha)-1,\kappa}_{\frac{\alpha+2}{\alpha+1}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}.

Therefore, from (
5.3
), Proposition
2.1
, and Young’s inequality, it follows that

\displaystyle\left\|\int_{0}^{t}\mathcal{K}_{t-\tau}F(u)\,d\tau\right\|_{L^{\alpha+1}(\mathbb{R},L^{\alpha+2}_{k}(\mathbb{R}^{n}))}

\displaystyle\leq\left\|\int_{0}^{t}\|\mathcal{K}_{t-\tau}F(u)\|_{L^{\alpha+2}_{k}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\,d\tau\right\|_{L^{\alpha+1}(\mathbb{R})}

\displaystyle\leq C\left\|\int_{0}^{t}k(t-\tau)\|F(u)\|_{H^{\sigma(\alpha)-1,\kappa}_{\frac{\alpha+2}{\alpha+1}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\,d\tau\right\|_{L^{\alpha+1}(\mathbb{R})}

\displaystyle\leq C\left\|\int_{0}^{t}k(t-\tau)\|F(u)\|_{L_{k}^{\frac{\alpha+2}{\alpha+1}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\,d\tau\right\|_{L^{\alpha+1}(\mathbb{R})}

\displaystyle\leq C\|k\|_{L^{\alpha+1}(\mathbb{R})}\|F(u)\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R},L_{k}^{\frac{\alpha+2}{\alpha+1}}(\mathbb{R}^{n}))}

\displaystyle\leq C\left\||u|^{\alpha+1}\right\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R},L_{k}^{\frac{\alpha+2}{\alpha+1}}(\mathbb{R}^{n}))}

\displaystyle=C\|u\|^{\alpha+1}_{L^{\alpha+1}(\mathbb{R},L_{k}^{\alpha+2}(\mathbb{R}^{n}))}.

Taking
M=2C(\|u_{0}\|_{H^{\sigma(\alpha),\kappa}_{\frac{\alpha+2}{\alpha+1}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}+\|u_{1}\|_{H^{\sigma(\alpha)-1,\kappa}_{\frac{\alpha+2}{\alpha+1}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})})
, we set the evolution space
X
as

X=\{u\in L^{\alpha+1}(\mathbb{R},L_{k}^{\alpha+2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})):\|u\|_{L^{\alpha+1}(\mathbb{R},L_{k}^{\alpha+2}(\mathbb{R}^{n}))}\leq M\}.

We say that a function
u
is a
mild solution
to (
5.1
) if
u
is a fixed point for the integral operator

\mathcal{T}:u\in X\mapsto\mathcal{T}u(t,x):=\frac{d\mathcal{K}_{t}}{dt}u_{0}+A_{t}u_{1}-\int_{0}^{t}\mathcal{K}_{t-\tau}F(u)\,d\tau.

We prove the global-in-time existence and uniqueness of small data Sobolev solutions of low regularity to the Cauchy problem (
5.1
) by finding a unique fixed point to the operator
\mathcal{T}
. It means that there exists a unique global solution
u
to the equation
\mathcal{T}u=u\in X
, which also gives the solution to (
5.1
). In order to prove that the operator
\mathcal{T}
has a uniquely determined fixed point, we use Banach’s fixed point argument with respect to the norm on
X
as defined above.

Now for any
u,v\in X
, we have

\|\mathcal{T}u\|_{L^{\alpha+1}(\mathbb{R},L_{k}^{\alpha+2}(\mathbb{R}^{n}))}\leq\frac{M}{2}+CM^{\alpha+1},

and

\displaystyle\quad\|\mathcal{T}u-\mathcal{T}v\|_{L^{\alpha+1}(\mathbb{R},L_{k}^{\alpha+2}(\mathbb{R}^{n}))}

\displaystyle=\left\|\int_{0}^{t}\mathcal{K}_{t-\tau}\left(F(u)-F(v)\right)\,d\tau\right\|_{L^{\alpha+1}(\mathbb{R},L_{k}^{\alpha+2}(\mathbb{R}^{n}))}

\displaystyle\leq C\left\|F(u)-F(v)\right\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R},L_{k}^{\frac{\alpha+2}{\alpha+1}}(\mathbb{R}^{n}))}

\displaystyle\leq C\left\|(|u|^{\alpha}+|v|^{\alpha})|u-v|\right\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R},L_{k}^{\frac{\alpha+2}{\alpha+1}}(\mathbb{R}^{n}))}

\displaystyle=C\left\|\left\|(|u|^{\alpha}+|v|^{\alpha})|u-v|\right\|_{L_{k}^{\frac{\alpha+2}{\alpha+1}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\right\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R})}

\displaystyle\leq C\left\|\left\||u|^{\alpha}+|v|^{\alpha}\right\|_{L_{k}^{\frac{\alpha+2}{\alpha}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\left\|u-v\right\|_{L_{k}^{\alpha+2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\right\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R})}

\displaystyle\leq C\left\||u|^{\alpha}+|v|^{\alpha}\right\|_{L^{\frac{\alpha+1}{\alpha}}(\mathbb{R},L_{k}^{\frac{\alpha+2}{\alpha}}(\mathbb{R}^{n}))}\left\|u-v\right\|_{L^{\alpha+1}(\mathbb{R},L_{k}^{\alpha+2}(\mathbb{R}^{n}))}

\displaystyle\leq C\left(\left\||u|^{\alpha}\right\|_{L^{\frac{\alpha+1}{\alpha}}(\mathbb{R},L_{k}^{\frac{\alpha+2}{\alpha}}(\mathbb{R}^{n}))}+\left\||v|^{\alpha}\right\|_{L^{\frac{\alpha+1}{\alpha}}(\mathbb{R},L_{k}^{\frac{\alpha+2}{\alpha}}(\mathbb{R}^{n}))}\right)\left\|u-v\right\|_{L^{\alpha+1}(\mathbb{R},L_{k}^{\alpha+2}(\mathbb{R}^{n}))}

\displaystyle=C\left(\|u\|_{L^{\alpha+1}(\mathbb{R},L_{k}^{\alpha+2}(\mathbb{R}^{n}))}^{\alpha}+\|v\|_{L^{\alpha+1}(\mathbb{R},L_{k}^{\alpha+2}(\mathbb{R}^{n}))}^{\alpha}\right)\left\|u-v\right\|_{L^{\alpha+1}(\mathbb{R},L_{k}^{\alpha+2}(\mathbb{R}^{n}))}

\displaystyle\leq 2CM^{\alpha}\left\|u-v\right\|_{L^{\alpha+1}(\mathbb{R},L_{k}^{\alpha+2}(\mathbb{R}^{n}))}.

Notice that, for
CM^{\alpha}<\frac{1}{2}
,
\mathcal{T}:X\to X
is a constraction mapping. Applying Banach’s fixed point theorem, there exists a global small data Sobolev solution
u
of the equation
u=\mathcal{T}u
in
X
, which also gives the solution to the equation (
5.1
). In addition, by the embedding
H^{\sigma(\alpha),\kappa}_{\frac{\alpha+2}{\alpha+1}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\subseteq H^{\frac{\alpha}{\alpha+2},\kappa}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
and
H^{\sigma(\alpha)-1,\kappa}_{\frac{\alpha+2}{\alpha+1}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\subseteq H^{-\frac{2}{\alpha+2},\kappa}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, we have

\displaystyle\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R},H^{\frac{\alpha}{\alpha+2},\kappa}(\mathbb{R}^{n}))}

\displaystyle\leq\|\frac{d\mathcal{K}_{t}}{dt}u_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R},H^{\frac{\alpha}{\alpha+2},\kappa}(\mathbb{R}^{n}))}+\|\mathcal{K}_{t}u_{1}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R},H^{\frac{\alpha}{\alpha+2},\kappa}(\mathbb{R}^{n}))}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad+\|\int_{0}^{t}\mathcal{K}_{t-\tau}F(u)\,d\tau\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R},H^{\frac{\alpha}{\alpha+2},\kappa}(\mathbb{R}^{n}))}

\displaystyle\leq\|u_{0}\|_{H^{\frac{\alpha}{\alpha+2},\kappa}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}+\|u_{1}\|_{H^{-\frac{2}{\alpha+2},\kappa}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}+\|F(u)\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R},H^{-\frac{2}{\alpha+2},\kappa}(\mathbb{R}^{n}))}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|u_{0}\|_{H^{\sigma(\alpha),\kappa}_{\frac{\alpha+2}{\alpha+1}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}+\|u_{1}\|_{H^{\sigma(\alpha)-1,\kappa}_{\frac{\alpha+2}{\alpha+1}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}+\|F(u)\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R},H^{\sigma(\alpha)-1,\kappa}_{\frac{\alpha+2}{\alpha+1}})}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|u_{0}\|_{H^{\sigma(\alpha),\kappa}_{\frac{\alpha+2}{\alpha+1}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}+\|u_{1}\|_{H^{\sigma(\alpha)-1,\kappa}_{\frac{\alpha+2}{\alpha+1}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}+\||u|^{\alpha+1}\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R},L_{k}^{\frac{\alpha+2}{\alpha+1}}(\mathbb{R}^{n}))}

\displaystyle=\|u_{0}\|_{H^{\sigma(\alpha),\kappa}_{\frac{\alpha+2}{\alpha+1}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}+\|u_{1}\|_{H^{\sigma(\alpha)-1,\kappa}_{\frac{\alpha+2}{\alpha+1}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}+\|u\|^{\alpha}_{L^{\alpha+1}(\mathbb{R},L_{k}^{\alpha+2}(\mathbb{R}^{n}))},

which indicates that the unique solution also belongs to
L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R},H^{\frac{\alpha}{\alpha+2},\kappa}(\mathbb{R}^{n}))
.
∎

Argued similarly as in the nonlinear Klein-Gordon equation (
5.1
), we also study the Cauchy problem for the nonlinear beam equation

\begin{cases}\partial_{t}^{2}u+\Delta_{\kappa}^{2}u+u=F(u),\\
u|_{t=0}=u_{0},\\
\partial_{t}u|_{t=0}=u_{1}.\end{cases}

(5.4)

By Duhamel’s principle, the solution is formally given by

u(t)=\frac{d\mathcal{B}_{t}}{dt}u_{0}+\mathcal{B}_{t}u_{1}-\int_{0}^{t}\mathcal{B}_{t-\tau}F(u)\,d\tau.

(5.5)

We also find the critical index

\alpha_{B}(N)=\frac{4-N+\sqrt{N^{2}+24N+16}}{2N}.

The following result is about the global well-posedness for the nonlinear beam equation (
5.5
).

Theorem 5.2
.

Let
\alpha_{B}(N)<\alpha<\frac{8}{N}
,
\sigma(\alpha)=\frac{\alpha N}{\alpha+2}-\frac{2}{\alpha+1}
,
\sigma(\alpha)<s\leq 2
,
s_{0}=s-\frac{\alpha N}{2(\alpha+2)}
and
(u_{0},u_{1})\in H^{s,\kappa}_{\frac{\alpha+2}{\alpha+1}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\times H^{s-2,\kappa}_{\frac{\alpha+2}{\alpha+1}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
with sufficient small norm. Then the equation (
5.5
) has a unique solution

u\in C(\mathbb{R},H^{s_{0},\kappa}(\mathbb{R}^{n}))\cap L^{\alpha+1}(\mathbb{R},L_{\kappa}^{\alpha+2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})).

Proof.

From Proposition
2.1
and
4.15
, we have

\displaystyle\|\frac{d\mathcal{B}_{t}}{dt}u_{0}\|_{L^{\alpha+2}_{k}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}

\displaystyle\leq\|\frac{d\mathcal{B}_{t}}{dt}u_{0}\|_{B^{0,\kappa}_{\alpha+2,2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\lesssim k(t)\|u_{0}\|_{B^{s,\kappa}_{\frac{\alpha+2}{\alpha+1},2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\lesssim k(t)\|u_{0}\|_{H^{s,\kappa}_{\frac{\alpha+2}{\alpha+1}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})},

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{B}_{t}u_{1}\|_{L^{\alpha+2}_{k}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}

\displaystyle\leq\|\mathcal{B}_{t}u_{1}\|_{B^{0,\kappa}_{\alpha+2,2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\lesssim k(t)\|u_{1}\|_{B^{s-2,\kappa}_{\frac{\alpha+2}{\alpha+1},2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\lesssim k(t)\|u_{1}\|_{H^{s-2,\kappa}_{\frac{\alpha+2}{\alpha+1}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})},

(5.6)

where

k(t)=\begin{cases}|t|^{-\max\left(\frac{\alpha N}{2(\alpha+2)}-\frac{s}{2},0\right)},\,&|t|\leq 1,\\
|t|^{-\frac{\alpha N}{4(\alpha+2)}},\,&|t|\geq 1.\end{cases}

For
\alpha_{B}(N)<\alpha<\frac{8}{N}
and
\sigma(\alpha)<s\leq 2
, we see that

(\alpha+1)\left(\frac{\alpha N}{2(\alpha+2)}-\frac{s}{2}\right)<1\quad\text{and}\quad(\alpha+1)\frac{\alpha N}{4(\alpha+2)}>1,

which implies that
k\in L^{\alpha+1}(\mathbb{R})
and

\displaystyle\|\frac{d\mathcal{B}_{t}}{dt}u_{0}\|_{L^{\alpha+1}(\mathbb{R},L^{\alpha+2}_{k}(\mathbb{R}^{n}))}

\displaystyle\leq C\|u_{0}\|_{H^{s,\kappa}_{\frac{\alpha+2}{\alpha+1}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})},

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{B}_{t}u_{1}\|_{L^{\alpha+1}(\mathbb{R},L^{\alpha+2}_{k}(\mathbb{R}^{n}))}

\displaystyle\leq C\|u_{1}\|_{H^{s-2,\kappa}_{\frac{\alpha+2}{\alpha+1}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}.

Therefore, from (
5.6
), Proposition
2.1
, and Young’s inequality, it follows that

\displaystyle\left\|\int_{0}^{t}\mathcal{B}_{t-\tau}F(u)\,d\tau\right\|_{L^{\alpha+1}(\mathbb{R},L^{\alpha+2}_{k}(\mathbb{R}^{n}))}

\displaystyle\leq\left\|\int_{0}^{t}\|\mathcal{B}_{t-\tau}F(u)\|_{L^{\alpha+2}_{k}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\,d\tau\right\|_{L^{\alpha+1}(\mathbb{R})}

\displaystyle\leq C\left\|\int_{0}^{t}k(t-\tau)\|F(u)\|_{H^{s-2,\kappa}_{\frac{\alpha+2}{\alpha+1}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\,d\tau\right\|_{L^{\alpha+1}(\mathbb{R})}

\displaystyle\leq C\left\|\int_{0}^{t}k(t-\tau)\|F(u)\|_{L_{k}^{\frac{\alpha+2}{\alpha+1}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\,d\tau\right\|_{L^{\alpha+1}(\mathbb{R})}

\displaystyle\leq C\|k\|_{L^{\alpha+1}(\mathbb{R})}\|F(u)\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R},L_{k}^{\frac{\alpha+2}{\alpha+1}}(\mathbb{R}^{n}))}

\displaystyle\leq C\left\||u|^{\alpha+1}\right\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R},L_{k}^{\frac{\alpha+2}{\alpha+1}}(\mathbb{R}^{n}))}

\displaystyle=C\|u\|^{\alpha+1}_{L^{\alpha+1}(\mathbb{R},L_{k}^{\alpha+2}(\mathbb{R}^{n}))}.

Taking
M=2C(\|u_{0}\|_{H^{s,\kappa}_{\frac{\alpha+2}{\alpha+1}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}+\|u_{1}\|_{H^{s-2,\kappa}_{\frac{\alpha+2}{\alpha+1}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})})
, we set the evolution spaces

X=\{u\in L^{\alpha+1}(\mathbb{R},L_{k}^{\alpha+2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})):\|u\|_{L^{\alpha+1}(\mathbb{R},L_{k}^{\alpha+2}(\mathbb{R}^{n}))}\leq M\}.

Define the mapping

\mathcal{T}:u\mapsto\frac{d\mathcal{B}_{t}}{dt}u_{0}+\mathcal{B}_{t}u_{1}-\int_{0}^{t}\mathcal{B}_{t-\tau}F(u)\,d\tau,\;\forall u\in X.

Then for any
u,v\in X
, we have

\|\mathcal{T}u\|_{L^{\alpha+1}(\mathbb{R},L_{k}^{\alpha+2}(\mathbb{R}^{n}))}\leq\frac{M}{2}+CM^{\alpha+1}.

Thus

\displaystyle\quad\|\mathcal{T}u-\mathcal{T}v\|_{L^{\alpha+1}(\mathbb{R},L_{k}^{\alpha+2}(\mathbb{R}^{n}))}

\displaystyle=\left\|\int_{0}^{t}\mathcal{B}_{t-\tau}\left(F(u)-F(v)\right)\,d\tau\right\|_{L^{\alpha+1}(\mathbb{R},L_{k}^{\alpha+2}(\mathbb{R}^{n}))}

\displaystyle\leq C\left\|F(u)-F(v)\right\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R},L_{k}^{\frac{\alpha+2}{\alpha+1}}(\mathbb{R}^{n}))}

\displaystyle\leq C\left\|(|u|^{\alpha}+|v|^{\alpha})|u-v|\right\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R},L_{k}^{\frac{\alpha+2}{\alpha+1}}(\mathbb{R}^{n}))}

\displaystyle=C\left\|\left\|(|u|^{\alpha}+|v|^{\alpha})|u-v|\right\|_{L_{k}^{\frac{\alpha+2}{\alpha+1}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\right\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R})}

\displaystyle\leq C\left\|\left\||u|^{\alpha}+|v|^{\alpha}\right\|_{L_{k}^{\frac{\alpha+2}{\alpha}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\left\|u-v\right\|_{L_{k}^{\alpha+2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\right\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R})}

\displaystyle\leq C\left\||u|^{\alpha}+|v|^{\alpha}\right\|_{L^{\frac{\alpha+1}{\alpha}}(\mathbb{R},L_{k}^{\frac{\alpha+2}{\alpha}}(\mathbb{R}^{n}))}\left\|u-v\right\|_{L^{\alpha+1}(\mathbb{R},L_{k}^{\alpha+2}(\mathbb{R}^{n}))}

\displaystyle\leq C\left(\left\||u|^{\alpha}\right\|_{L^{\frac{\alpha+1}{\alpha}}(\mathbb{R},L_{k}^{\frac{\alpha+2}{\alpha}}(\mathbb{R}^{n}))}+\left\||v|^{\alpha}\right\|_{L^{\frac{\alpha+1}{\alpha}}(\mathbb{R},L_{k}^{\frac{\alpha+2}{\alpha}}(\mathbb{R}^{n}))}\right)\left\|u-v\right\|_{L^{\alpha+1}(\mathbb{R},L_{k}^{\alpha+2}(\mathbb{R}^{n}))}

\displaystyle=C\left(\|u\|_{L^{\alpha+1}(\mathbb{R},L_{k}^{\alpha+2}(\mathbb{R}^{n}))}^{\alpha}+\|v\|_{L^{\alpha+1}(\mathbb{R},L_{k}^{\alpha+2}(\mathbb{R}^{n}))}^{\alpha}\right)\left\|u-v\right\|_{L^{\alpha+1}(\mathbb{R},L_{k}^{\alpha+2}(\mathbb{R}^{n}))}

\displaystyle\leq 2CM^{\alpha}\left\|u-v\right\|_{L^{\alpha+1}(\mathbb{R},L_{k}^{\alpha+2}(\mathbb{R}^{n}))}.

Notice that, for
CM^{\alpha}<\frac{1}{2}
,
\mathcal{T}:X\to X
is a constraction mapping. Therefore, applying Banach’s fixed point theorem, the equation (
5.4
) has a unique
solution
u\in X
. In addition, by the embedding
H^{s,\kappa}_{\frac{\alpha+2}{\alpha+1}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\subseteq H^{s_{0},\kappa}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
and
H^{s-2,\kappa}_{\frac{\alpha+2}{\alpha+1}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\subseteq H^{s_{0}-2,\kappa}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, we have

\displaystyle\quad\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R},H^{s_{0},\kappa}(\mathbb{R}^{n}))}

\displaystyle\leq\|\frac{d\mathcal{B}_{t}}{dt}u_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R},H^{s_{0},\kappa}(\mathbb{R}^{n}))}+\|\mathcal{B}_{t}u_{1}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R},H^{s_{0},\kappa}(\mathbb{R}^{n}))}+\|\int_{0}^{t}\mathcal{B}_{t-\tau}F(u)\,d\tau\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R},H^{s_{0},\kappa}(\mathbb{R}^{n}))}

\displaystyle\leq\|u_{0}\|_{H^{s_{0},\kappa}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}+\|u_{1}\|_{H^{s_{0}-2,\kappa}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}+\|F(u)\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R},H^{s_{0}-2,\kappa})}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|u_{0}\|_{H^{s,\kappa}_{\frac{\alpha+2}{\alpha+1}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}+\|u_{1}\|_{H^{s-2,\kappa}_{\frac{\alpha+2}{\alpha+1}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}+\|F(u)\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R},H^{s-2,\kappa}_{\frac{\alpha+2}{\alpha+1}}(\mathbb{R}^{n}))}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|u_{0}\|_{H^{s,\kappa}_{\frac{\alpha+2}{\alpha+1}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}+\|u_{1}\|_{H^{s-2,\kappa}_{\frac{\alpha+2}{\alpha+1}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}+\||u|^{\alpha+1}\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R},L_{k}^{\frac{\alpha+2}{\alpha+1}}(\mathbb{R}^{n}))}

\displaystyle=\|u_{0}\|_{H^{s,\kappa}_{\frac{\alpha+2}{\alpha+1}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}+\|u_{1}\|_{H^{s-2,\kappa}_{\frac{\alpha+2}{\alpha+1}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}+\|u\|^{\alpha}_{L^{\alpha+1}(\mathbb{R},L_{k}^{\alpha+2}(\mathbb{R}^{n}))},

which indicates that the unique solution also belongs to
L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R},H^{s_{0},\kappa}(\mathbb{R}^{n}))
.
∎

Lower bounds on the least common multiple of a polynomial sequence and its radical

Abstract
. 1.
Introduction

The study of the least common multiple of an integer polynomial sequence goes back to Chebychev, who observed that
\log{\mathrm{lcm}}\left(1,2,,\ldots,N\right)\sim N
is equivalent to the prime number theorem and gave lower and upper bounds for this quantity. This problem later inspired a more general problem of studying the least common multiple of polynomial sequences. For a linear polynomial
f(x)=qx+a,\ k,a\in{\mathbb{Z}},\ q>0,q+a>0
it was observed by Bateman and Kalb
[
BKS02
]
, that

\textrm{log lcm}\left(f\left(1\right),f(2),\ldots,f\left(N\right)\right)\sim c_{f}N
as
N\to\infty
, where
c_{f}=\frac{q}{\varphi(q)}\sum_{1\leq m\leq q,(m,q)=1}\frac{1}{m}
, which is a consequence of the prime number theorem for arithmetic progressions.

Cilleruelo
[
Cil11
]
studied the quantity

(1.1)

L_{f}(N)=\textrm{lcm}\left(f\left(1\right),f(2),...,f\left(N\right)\right)

for an irreducible polynomial
f\in{\mathbb{Z}}[x]
of degree
d\geq 2
. In the case
d=2
he proved the asymptotic
\log L_{f}(N)\sim N\log N
and made the following

Conjecture 1.1
.

Let
f\in{\mathbb{Z}}[x]
be a fixed irreducible polynomial of degree
d\geq 2
. Then

(1.2)

\log L_{f}(N)\sim\left(d-1\right)N\log N

as
N\to\infty
.

Convention.
Throughout the rest of the paper in all asymptotic notation the polynomial
f
is assumed fixed, while the parameter
N
is taken to infinity.

At present there are no known examples of polynomials of degree
d>2
for which the conjecture is known to hold, nor is it known whether such a polynomial even exists. Cilleruelo’s argument also shows the predicted upper bound, i.e. if
f\in\mathbb{Z}[X]
is an irreducible polynomial of degree
d\geq 2
, then

\log L_{f}(N)\lesssim(d-1)N\log N.

Maynard and Rudnick
[
MR21
]
provided a lower bound of the correct order of magnitude:

(1.3)

\log L_{f}(N)\gtrsim\frac{1}{d}N\log N.

Sah
[
Sah20
]
improved this lower bound to

(1.4)

\log L_{f}(N)\gtrsim N\log N.

He also studied the radical of the least common multiple

\ell_{f}(N)=\mathrm{rad}(L_{f}(N))=\prod_{p|f(1)f(2)\ldots f(N)\atop{\mathrm{prime}}}p

and made the following conjecture:

Conjecture 1.2
.

Let
f\in{\mathbb{Z}}[x],\,\deg f=d\geq 2
be irreducible. Then

\log\ell_{f}(N)\sim\log L_{f}(N)\sim(d-1)N\log N.

He proved the following lower bound:

(1.5)

\log\ell_{f}(N)\gtrsim\frac{2}{d}N\log N.

We note that conditional on the ABC conjecture one can show that
\log\ell_{f}(N)\sim\log L_{f}(N)
, see
[
EL23
, Remark 6.2]
.

Rudnick and Zehavi
[
RZ20
]
established an averaged form of (
1.2
) with
f
varying in a suitable range which depends on
N
. Leumi
[
Leu21
]
, as well as the author and Landsberg
[
EL23
]
studied a function field analog of the problem. In the latter work the analog of Conjecture
1.1
was established for a special class of polynomials (which includes many polynomials of degree
d>2
) and it was shown that the analogs of conjectures
1.1
and
1.2
are equivalent. Technau
[
Tec24
]
derived improved lower bounds on
L_{f}(N),\ell_{f}(N)
for even polynomials (i.e.
f(x)=f(-x)
) and a further improved lower bound on
\ell_{f}(N)
for the cyclotomic polynomial
f=x^{2^{k}}+1
.

In the present work we slightly improve the bound (
1.5
) and give further improved lower bounds on
L_{f}(N),\ell_{f}(N)
for polynomials of special form. Our first main result is the following

Theorem 1
.

Let
f\in{\mathbb{Z}}[x],\,\deg f=d\geq 3
be irreducible. Then

(1.6)

\log\ell_{f}(N)\gtrsim\frac{d-1}{V_{d}}N\log N,

where

(1.7)

V_{d}=\sum_{k=1}^{\min(\lfloor 3d/4\rfloor,d-2)}(d-k)=\left[\begin{array}[]{ll}2,&d=3,\\
5,&d=4,\\
{\lfloor\frac{3d}{4}\rfloor\left(d-\frac{\lfloor\frac{3d}{4}\rfloor+1}{2}\right)},&d\geq 5.\end{array}\right.

The constant in (
1.6
) is bigger than the constant in (
1.5
) for all
d\geq 3
. For large
d
it is asymptotic to
\frac{32}{15}\cdot\frac{1}{d}
.

Before stating our next main result, we need the following

Definition 1.3
.

A non-empty finite subset
S=\{\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{n}\}\subset\overline{{\mathbb{Q}}}
is called
potent
if there exist positive integers
a_{1},\ldots,a_{n}
such that
\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_{i}\alpha_{i}\in{\mathbb{Z}}
.

Example 1.4
.

Let
h\in{\mathbb{Z}}[x],\,\deg h\geq 2
be a polynomial,
a\in\overline{{\mathbb{Q}}}
. Then the set of roots of
h(x)-a
in
{\overline{{\mathbb{Q}}}}
is potent.
Indeed write
h=\sum_{i=0}^{s}h_{i}x^{i},\,h_{s}\neq 0
and let
\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{k}
be the distinct roots of
h(x)-a
with
\alpha_{i}
having multiplicity
a_{i}
. Then by Vieta’s formula
\sum_{i=1}^{k}|h_{s}a_{i}|\alpha_{i}=\pm h_{s-1}\in{\mathbb{Z}}
.

Our second main result is the following

Theorem 2
.

Let
f\in{\mathbb{Z}}[x],\,\deg f=d\geq 4
be irreducible and assume that its set of roots
S
in
\overline{{\mathbb{Q}}}
can be partitioned into (non-empty) potent subsets
S_{1},\ldots,S_{r}
. Then

(i)

\log L_{f}(N)\gtrsim\frac{d-1}{d-r}N\log N.

(ii)

\log\ell_{f}(N)\gtrsim\frac{d-1}{V_{d,r}}N\log N,

where

(1.8)

V_{d,r}=\sum_{k=1}^{\min(\lfloor 3d/4\rfloor,d-2)}(d-\max(k,r))

(note that
V_{d,r}<V_{d}
whenever
r>1
).

Corollary 1.5
.

Let
f=g\circ h
be irreducible with
g,h\in{\mathbb{Z}}[x],\,\deg g=r,\,\deg h=s,\,\deg f=d=rs,\,r,s\geq 2
. Then

\log L_{f}(N)\gtrsim\frac{d-1}{d-r}N\log N.

Proof.

Let
\beta_{1},\ldots,\beta_{r}\in\overline{{\mathbb{Q}}}
be the roots of
g
. Taking
R_{i}=\{\alpha\in{\overline{{\mathbb{Q}}}}:\,h(\alpha)=\beta_{i}\}
(
1\leq i\leq r
) we obtain the partition required in Theorem
2
(by Example
1.4
applied to
h(x)-\beta_{i}
each
R_{i}
is potent).
∎

The special case
h(x)=x^{2}
of the above corollary (i.e. when
f(x)=f(-x)
is even) was independently obtained by Technau
[
Tec24
]
, with a slightly worse constant in the
\ell_{f}(N)
bound. The quadratic case
\deg h=2
gives the biggest increase in the constant compared to (
1.4
), namely we obtain the constant
\frac{2(d-1)}{d}
instead of
1
.

In the case of a decomposable polynomial
f
the bound in Theorem 2(ii) can be refined further.

Theorem 3
.

Let
f=g\circ h
be irreducible with
g,h\in{\mathbb{Z}}[x],\,\deg g=r,\,\deg h=s,\,\deg f=d=rs,\,r,s\geq 2
. Then

\log\ell_{f}(N)\gtrsim\frac{d-1}{W_{d,r}}N\log N,

where

(1.9)

W_{d,r}=\sum_{k=1}^{\min(\lfloor 3d/4\rfloor,d-2)}\max(d-kr,r-\lfloor kr/d\rfloor)

(note that
W_{d,r}<V_{d,r}
whenever
r,s\geq 2
).

In the special case
\deg h=\deg g=d^{1/2}
the constant in Theorem
3
grows asymptotically like
\frac{32}{31}\cdot\frac{1}{d^{1/2}}
for large
d
, compared with
\frac{32}{15}\cdot\frac{1}{d}
in Theorem
1
.

Our final main result strengthens the bound in Theorem 1 for polynomials with a small Galois group.

Theorem 4
.

Let
f\in{\mathbb{Z}}[x],\deg f=d\geq 3
be irreducible,
S\subset\overline{{\mathbb{Q}}}
its set of roots,
G_{f}
its Galois group,
e=|G_{f}|
. Suppose that one can partition
S
into (non-empty) potent subsets
S_{1},\ldots,S_{r}
where
r
satisfies

\dim\langle S\cup\{1\}\rangle_{\mathbb{Q}}=d+1-r,

here
\langle\cdot\rangle_{\mathbb{Q}}
denotes the linear span over
{\mathbb{Q}}
(note that since the
S_{i}
are potent and disjoint, each contributes a new linear dependency on
S\cup\{1\}
and a priori
\dim\langle S\cup\{1\}\rangle_{\mathbb{Q}}\leq d+1-r
).
Then

\log\ell_{f}(N)\gtrsim\frac{d-1}{U_{d,e}}N\log N,

where

(1.10)

U_{d,e}=\sum_{k=1}^{\min(d-2,\lfloor 3d/4\rfloor)}\min\left(d-k,\bigg\lceil\frac{e}{k}\bigg\rceil-1\right).

Remark 1.6
.

We have
U_{d,e}<V_{d}
iff
\lceil e/k\rceil-1<d-k
for some
1\leq k\leq\min(d-2,\lfloor 3d/4\rfloor)
. In this case the constant in Theorem
4
is bigger than in (
1.6
). In particular this happens whenever
e=d\geq 4
. However there is no improvement if
e>d^{2}
, so the order of the Galois group of
f
needs to be at most quadratic in
d
to obtain an improvement over Theorem
1
.

Corollary 1.7
.

Let
f\in{\mathbb{Z}}[x],\,\deg f=d\geq 3
be an irreducible polynomial with roots
\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{d}\in{\overline{{\mathbb{Q}}}}
such that

(a)

{\mathbb{Q}}(\alpha_{1})={\mathbb{Q}}(\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{n})
.

(b)

\dim\langle 1,\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{d}\rangle_{{\mathbb{Q}}}=d
.

Then

\log\ell_{f}(N)\gtrsim\frac{d-1}{U_{d}}N\log N,

where

U_{d}=U_{d,d}=\sum_{k=1}^{\min(d-2,\lfloor 3d/4\rfloor)}(\lceil d/k\rceil-1).

Proof.

Condition
(a)
is equivalent to
e=|G_{f}|=d
, the set
\{\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{n}\}
is potent by Example
1.4
. We may apply Theorem
4
with the partition
S=S_{1}
and obtain the assertion.
∎

Remark 1.8
.

Condition (b) in Corollary
1.7
typically holds, it says that there are no linear relations between the roots of
f
and 1 other than the one imposed by Vieta’s formula.

Remark 1.9
.

The first few values of
U_{d}
are
U_{3}=2,U_{4}=4,U_{5}=7,U_{6}=9,U_{7}=13,U_{8}=15,U_{9}=18,U_{10}=21
. For large
d
we have
U_{d}\sim d\log d
, so the constant in Corollary
1.7
grows like
1/\log d
as
d\to\infty
, compared with
32/15d
in Theorem
1
. The actual predicted constant is
d-1
.

Corollary 1.10
.

Let
\Phi_{m}(x)
be the
m
-th cyclotomic polynomial, with
m=p^{k}n
,
p
prime,
n
squarefree. Denote
d=\deg\Phi_{m}=\varphi(m)
and assume
d\geq 3
. Then

\log\ell_{\Phi_{m}}(N)\gtrsim\frac{d-1}{U_{d}}N\log N.

Proof.

Denote by
S
the set of roots of
\Phi_{m}
, i.e. the primitive
m
-th roots of unity and let
\zeta\in S
be one such root. Note that
{\mathbb{Q}}(\zeta)={\mathbb{Q}}(S)
and
|{\mathrm{Gal}}(\Phi_{m}/{\mathbb{Q}})|=d=\varphi(m)
.

If
m
is itself squarefree then by
[
Joh85
, p.1]

S
is a basis of
{\mathbb{Q}}(\zeta)
and therefore
\dim\langle S\cup\{1\}\rangle_{\mathbb{Q}}=d
(since
S
is potent
S\cup\{1\}
is linearly dependent. In fact
\sum_{\alpha\in S}\alpha=\mu(m)
, where
\mu
is the Möbius function). Hence Corollary
1.7
applies, giving the assertion.

Now assume that
m
is not squarefree, so we may assume
m=p^{k}n
,
k\geq 2
,
(p,n)=1
.
Denote

\{a_{1},\ldots,a_{d/p}\}=\{1\leq a<m/p:(a,m/p)=1\}

and set

S_{i}=\{\zeta^{a_{i}},\zeta^{a_{i}+m/p},\zeta^{a_{i}+2m/p}+\ldots\zeta^{a_{i}+(p-1)m/p}\},\quad 1\leq i\leq d/p.

Then
S_{1},\ldots,S_{r},\,r=d/p
is a partition of
S
and each
S_{i}
is potent since
\sum_{\alpha\in S_{i}}\alpha=0
. Finally by
[
Joh85
, p.1]
we have
\dim\langle S\rangle_{\mathbb{Q}}=(p-1)d/p
and
1\not\in\langle S\rangle_{\mathbb{Q}}
because
\mathrm{tr}_{{\mathbb{Q}}(\zeta)/{\mathbb{Q}}}(\alpha)=\sum_{\beta\in S}\beta=\sum_{i=1}^{d/p}\sum_{\beta\in S_{i}}\beta=0
for any
\alpha\in S
, while
\mathrm{tr}_{{\mathbb{Q}}(\zeta)/{\mathbb{Q}}}(1)\neq 0
. Hence
\dim\langle S\cup\{1\}\rangle_{\mathbb{Q}}=(p-1)d/p+1=d-r+1
, so Theorem
4
applies with
e=d
and we obtain the assertion of the corollary.
∎

Remark 1.11
.

It the special case
m=2^{k}
the sharper bound
\log\ell_{\Phi_{m}}(N)\gtrsim\frac{2^{k-1}-1}{(k-1)2^{k-2}}N\log N
was obtained in
[
Tec24
]
by a different method.

.

In the setup of Theorem

, if additionally the set of roots of

This applies in particular to the cyclotomic polynomial
\Phi_{m}
where

2.
General strategy and proof of Theorem
1

The usual approach to estimating
L_{f}(N)
and
\ell_{f}(N)
is to consider their prime factorization and separately estimate the contribution of small primes
p=O(N)
and large primes (not falling in this range). The contribution of small primes can be estimated precisely using the Chebotarev density theorem (in fact even weaker results on the distribution of prime ideals suffice), but understanding the contribution of large primes is a significant challenge. The approach to lower bounds taken in
[
MR21
,
Sah20
]
as well as here is to bound the number of values
f(1),\ldots,f(N)
that a given large prime can divide, with or without counting multiplicity. Our new results come from giving improved bounds of this type for polynomials of special form. An additional ingredient we use for the lower bounds on
\ell_{f}(N)
are results of Hooley
[
Hoo67
]
and Browning
[
Bro11
]
from their work on power-free values of polynomials.

Proposition 2.1
.

Let
f\in{\mathbb{Z}}[x],\deg f=d\geq 2
be an irreducible polynomial. Suppose there exist constants
D=D(f)>0,\delta=\delta(f)>0
such that for any sufficiently large
N\geq N_{0}(f)
and any prime
p>DN
,
p
divides at most
\delta
of the values
f(1),f(2),\ldots,f(N)
. Then

L_{f}(N)\gtrsim\frac{d-1}{\delta}N\log N.

Proof.

This is proved in
[
Sah20
, Proof of Theorem 1.3]
for the special case
D=d|f_{d}|+1,\,\delta=d-1
, but the proof works essentially verbatim for any
D,\delta>0
.∎

For the analogous proposition for
\ell_{f}(N)
we need to recall the results of Hooley and Browning which were key steps in their work on power-free values of polynomials.

Proposition 2.2
.

Let
f\in{\mathbb{Z}}[x],\deg d\geq 3
be an irreducible polynomial.
The number of values among
f(1),\ldots,f(N)
which are divisible by
p^{\min(d-1,\lfloor 3d/4\rfloor+1)}
for some prime
p>N
is
o(N)
.

Proof.

It follows from
[
Hoo67
, Equation (15)]
that the number of values among
f(1),\ldots,f(N)
divisible by
p^{d-1}
for a prime
p>N
is
o(N)
. The same statement with
p^{\lfloor 3d/4\rfloor+1}
in place of
p^{d-1}
follows from the bound established in
[
Bro11
, §3]
on the quantity
E(\xi)
defined in
[
Bro11
, Equation (9)]
.
∎

Proposition 2.3
.

Let
f\in{\mathbb{Z}}[x],\,\deg f=d\geq 3
be an irreducible polynomial. Suppose there exist constants
D=D(f)>0,\delta_{k}=\delta_{k}(f)\geq 0\,(1\leq k\leq d)
such that for any sufficiently large
N\geq N_{0}(f)
and any prime
p>DN
,
p^{k}
divides at most
\delta_{k}
of the values
f(1),\ldots,f(N)
. Then

\ell_{f}(N)\gtrsim\frac{d-1}{V}N\log N,

where

(2.1)

V=\sum_{k=1}^{\min(d-2,\lfloor 3d/4\rfloor)}\delta_{k}.

Proof.

The same assertion but with the constant
\frac{d-1}{\sum_{k=1}^{d}\delta_{k}}
is essentially established in
[
Sah20
, Proof of Theorem 1.4]
. The main new ingredient introduced here is Proposition
2.2
.
We denote by
\sum_{p},\prod_{p}
sums and products over primes respectively. By
v_{p}(n)
we denote the multiplicity of
p
in the factorization of
n
. Denote
Q(N)=\prod_{n=1}^{N}f(n)
and

Q_{1}(N)=\prod_{p\atop{p\leq DN}}p^{v_{p}(f(n))},\quad Q_{L}(N)=\prod_{p\atop{p>DN}}p^{v_{p}(f(n))},\quad\ell_{L}(N)=\prod_{p|Q(N)\atop{p>DN}}p.

We have
Q(N)=Q_{1}(N)Q_{L}(N)
and
\ell_{f}(N)\geq\ell_{L}(N)
. In
[
Sah20
]
the quantity
Q_{1}(N)
is further decomposed as
Q_{1}(N)=Q_{S}(N)Q_{LI}(N)
(products over small and linear-sized primes) and it is shown that
\log Q_{S}\sim N\log N

[
Sah20
, Proposition 2.1]
and
\log Q_{LI}(N)=O(N)

[
Sah20
, Proposition 3.1]
. Since
\log Q(N)\sim dN\log N
we obtain

\log Q_{L}(N)=\log\frac{Q(N)}{Q_{S}(N)Q_{LI}(N)}\sim(d-1)N\log N.

Now fix
0<\epsilon<1
and assume WLOG that
D\geq 1
. By Proposition
2.2
, for
N
sufficiently large at most
\epsilon N
of the values
f(1),\ldots,f(N)
are divisible by the
m
-th power of any prime
p>DN
, where
m=\min(d-1,\lfloor 3d/4\rfloor+1)
, while at most
\delta_{k}
are divisible by the
k
-th power of such a prime for each
1\leq k\leq d
.
Thus
v_{p}(f(n))\leq V
(where
V
is given by (
2.1
)) for all but
\epsilon N
of
1\leq n\leq N
and
v_{p}(f(n))\leq\delta=\sum_{k=1}^{d}\delta_{k}
for all of them (taking
D
large enough we may assume WLOG that
p^{d+1}>|f(N)|
for any
p>DN
, and then
p^{d+1}\nmid f(n)
for any
1\leq n\leq N
). Hence (using that
\log p<(d+1)\log N
if
p|Q(N)
and
N
is sufficiently large)

(d-1)N\log N\sim\log Q_{L}(N)=\sum_{p|Q(N)\atop{p>DN}}v_{p}(f(n))\log p\leq\epsilon N\delta(d+1)\log N+\sum_{p|Q(N)\atop{p>DN}}V\log p\\
=\epsilon\delta(d+1)N\log N+V\log\ell_{L}(N).

Taking
\epsilon\to 0
shows that

\log\ell_{f}(N)\geq\log\ell_{L}(N)\gtrsim\frac{d-1}{V}N\log N,

proving the assertion.
∎

The next lemma is a generalization of
[
Sah20
, Lemma 4.1]
.

Lemma 2.4
.

Let
f=\sum_{i=0}^{d}f_{i}x^{i}\in{\mathbb{Z}}[x],\deg f=d\geq 2
be a fixed polynomial. Denote
D=d|f_{d}|+1
. There exists a constant
M=M(f)\in{\mathbb{N}}
, such that for any
N\geq M
and integers
1\leq k\leq d-1
,
a\in{\mathbb{Z}}
,
q>(DN)^{k}
, there do not exist integers
M\leq n_{1}<n_{2}<\ldots<n_{d-k+1}\leq N
such that:

(a)

f(n_{i})\equiv a\pmod{q}
for all
1\leq i\leq d-k+1
,

(b)

(q,n_{i}-n_{j})=1
for all
1\leq i<j\leq d-k+1
.

Proof.

Assume that
1\leq n_{1}<n_{2}<\ldots<n_{d-k+1}\leq N
satisfy properties (a),(b) from the statement of the lemma. Denote

(2.2)

A=\sum_{i=1}^{d-k+1}\frac{f(n_{i})-a}{\displaystyle\prod_{1\leq j\leq d-k+1\atop{j\neq i}}(n_{i}-n_{j})}=\sum_{i=1}^{d-k+1}\frac{f(n_{i})}{\displaystyle\prod_{1\leq j\leq d-k+1\atop{j\neq i}}(n_{i}-n_{j})},

with the last equality stemming from the well-known identity

\sum_{i=1}^{d-k+1}\frac{1}{\displaystyle\prod_{1\leq j\leq d-k+1\atop{j\neq i}}(n_{i}-n_{j})}=0,

which can be obtained by considering the Lagrange interpolation
L(x)
of the constant 1 at the points

Lemma 3.1

|N_{K/{\mathbb{Q}}}(\gamma)|\leq f_{d}^{|G_{f}|}(((tN)^{1/t}+1)C+(tN)^{1/t})^{|G_{f}|}\leq(DN)^{\frac{|G_{f}|}{t}}

if we take
D\geq f_{d}^{t}(2C+1)^{t}t
.
There are two cases to consider.

\gamma\neq 0
.
In this case we have

0<|N_{K/{\mathbb{Q}}}(\gamma)|<(DN)^{\frac{|G_{f}|}{t}}.

Now since
p^{k}|N_{K/{\mathbb{Q}}}(\gamma)

and
p>DN
, we must have
t<\frac{|G_{f}|}{k}
, proving the assertion of the lemma.

\gamma=0
.
Here we need to use the assumptions (from the statement of Theorem
4
) that each
S_{i}
is potent and

\dim\langle S\cup\{1\}\rangle=d+1-r
. Throughout the rest of the proof all spans (denoted by
\langle\cdot\rangle
) and linear dependencies are over
{\mathbb{Q}}
.

First we claim that we must have
S_{i}\subset\{\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{t}\}
for some
i
. Otherwise there exists
\beta_{i}\in S_{i}\setminus\{\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{t}\}
for each
1\leq i\leq r
and since
S_{i}
is potent
\beta_{i}\in\langle S_{i}\setminus\{\beta_{i}\}\rangle
, so we have

\langle S\cup\{1\}\rangle=\langle\bigcup(S_{i}\setminus\{\beta_{i}\})\cup\{1\}\rangle
, with the set in the latter span containing
\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{t}
. But by (
5.1
) and the assumption
\gamma=0
we have
\sum_{i=1}^{t}a_{i}\alpha_{i}=u
and thus
\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{t},1
are linearly dependent over
{\mathbb{Q}}
, so

\dim\langle S\cup\{1\}\rangle=\dim\Big\langle\bigcup_{i=1}^{r}(S_{i}\setminus\{\beta_{i}\})\cup\{1\}\Big\rangle\leq\Bigl|\bigcup_{i=1}^{r}(S_{i}\setminus\{\beta_{i}\})\cup\{1\}\Bigr|-1\leq d-r,

a contradiction to the assumption
\dim\langle S\cup\{1\}\rangle=d+1-r
. This establishes our claim that
S_{i}\subset\{\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{t}\}
for some
i
.

Now suppose
S_{i}=\{\alpha_{j_{1}},\ldots\alpha_{j_{s}}\}
.
Then since
S_{i}
is potent we have
\sum_{l=1}^{s}b_{l}\alpha_{j_{l}}=w\in{\mathbb{Z}}
for some
0<b_{l}\in{\mathbb{Z}}
, and hence
\sum_{l=1}^{s}b_{l}\bar{\alpha}_{j_{l}}=\bar{w}
. Since
\bar{\alpha}_{j_{l}}=\bar{n}_{j_{l}}
we obtain
\sum_{l=1}^{s}b_{j_{l}}n_{j_{i}}-w\equiv 0\pmod{p}
, which is impossible if
D\geq\sum_{l=1}^{s}b_{j_{l}}+1
,
p>DN
and
N
is sufficiently large, just as in the proof of Lemma
3.2
(here we need to use the assumption that
f
does not have integer roots). We conclude that the assertion of the lemma holds with
D=\max\left(f_{d}^{t}(2C+1)^{t}t,\sum_{l=1}^{s}b_{l}+1\right)
.

∎

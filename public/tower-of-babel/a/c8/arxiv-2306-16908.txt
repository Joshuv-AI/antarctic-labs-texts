Approximate controllability of a 2D linear system related to the motion of two fluids with surface tension
Sébastien Court

Abstract
We consider a coupled system of partial differential equations describing the interactions between a closed free interface and two viscous incompressible fluids. The fluids are assumed to satisfy the incompressible Navier-Stokes equations in time-dependent domains that are determined by the free interface. The mean curvature of the interface induces a surface tension force that creates a jump of the Cauchy stress tensor on both sides. It influences the behavior of the surrounding fluids, and therefore the deformation of this interface via the equality of velocities. In dimension 2, the steady states correspond to immobile interfaces that are circles with all the same volume. Considering small displacements of steady states, we are lead to consider a linearized version of this system. We prove that the latter is approximately controllable to a given steady state for any time T > 0 T>0 by the means of additional surface tension type forces, provided that the radius of the circle of reference does not coincide with a scaled zero of the Bessel function of first kind.



Keywords:
Navier-Stokes equations, Free boundary, Surface tension, Approximate Controllability.

Introduction

Topics related to the study of the motion and shape of soap bubbles is getting more and more attention, as they have applications in the design of medical treatments and food products for example. Their motion is realized via surface tension, by interaction with surrounding fluids. The question of their stability is a major issue, and the Kolmogorov theory predicts that a soap bubble does not burst as long as the ratio between the inertia forces and the surface tension forces does not reach a power of a critical diameter, quantified by Hinze
[
Hin55
]
. At intermediate Reynolds number, this question is delicate as the inertia forces play a non-negligible role, leading for example to bouncing effects
[
Cou19
, Fig. 11]
. In order to circumvent this limitation on the diameter, one can also try to design additional surface tension forces that control and guarantee the stability of the soap bubble. In practice the realization of this type of controls can be realized in different ways, like for example surfactants
[
FS00
,
Ran+12
]
, or electrochemical effects
[
Kha+14
,
EKD16
]
. In the present paper, we propose sufficient conditions on the diameter of a 2-dimensional soap bubble for being approximately controllable to a circle, without bound constraint on this diameter.

2
On the steady states

Consider a reference configuration given by a circle
\Gamma
of radius
r_{s}>0
, splitting the domain
\Omega
into
\Omega^{+}
and
\Omega^{-}
, such that
\Gamma\cap\partial\Omega=\emptyset
, we define the following class of displacements:

\mathcal{C}:=\left\{Z_{s}\in\mathbf{H}^{1/2}(\Gamma)\text{ such that }(\mathrm{Id}+Z_{s})(\Gamma)\text{ is a circle of radius $r_{s}$ and }(\mathrm{Id}+Z_{s})(\Gamma)\cap\partial\Omega=\emptyset\right\}.

Note that the set
\mathcal{C}
contains rotations and certain translations, as well as diffeomorphisms of the circle
\Gamma
. Let us show that the set
\mathcal{C}
corresponds to steady deformations
\mathrm{Id}+Z_{s}
. The steady states
(u_{s}^{+},p_{s}^{+},u_{s}^{-},p_{s}^{-},Z_{s})
define
\Gamma_{s}:=(\mathrm{Id}+Z_{s})(\Gamma)
with curvature denoted by
\kappa_{s}
,which splits the domain
\Omega
into
\Omega^{+}_{s}
and
\Omega_{s}^{-}
, and satisfy the following system

\displaystyle\rho^{\pm}(u_{s}^{\pm}\cdot\nabla)u_{s}^{\pm}-\divg\sigma^{\pm}(u_{s}^{\pm},p_{s}^{\pm})=0\quad\text{and}\quad\divg u_{s}^{\pm}=0

\displaystyle\text{in }\Omega_{s}^{\pm},

(3a)

\displaystyle u_{s}^{+}=0

\displaystyle\text{on }\partial\Omega,

(3b)

\displaystyle u_{s}^{+}=u_{s}^{-}=0\quad\text{and}\quad-\left[\sigma(u_{s},p_{s})\right]n_{s}=\mu\kappa_{s}n_{s}

\displaystyle\text{on }\Gamma_{s}.

(3c)

We prove that locally the steady states are trivial states, where the steady displacements
Z_{s}
lie in
\mathcal{C}
.

Proposition 2.1
.

There exists a constant
C_{s}>0
depending only on
\nu^{\pm}
,
\rho^{\pm}
and
\Omega
, such that the only velocities solution of system (
3
) in the set
\{(u_{s}^{+},u_{s}^{-})\in\mathbf{H}^{1}(\Omega_{s}^{+})\times\mathbf{H}^{1}(\Omega_{s}^{-})|\ \|u^{\pm}_{s}\|_{\mathbf{L}^{2}(\Omega)}\leq C_{s}\}
is
u^{\pm}_{s}=0
. This corresponds to a configuration where
\Gamma_{s}
is a circle, and the pressures
p_{s}^{\pm}
are constant on both sides of
\Gamma_{s}
.

Proof.

Recall that
(v\cdot\nabla)v=\divg(v\otimes v)
when
\divg v=0
. From (
3a
), we deduce by integration by parts

\nu^{\pm}\|\nabla u_{s}^{\pm}\|^{2}_{[\mathbf{L}^{2}(\Omega_{s}^{\pm})]^{2}}=\rho^{\pm}\int_{\Omega}(u^{\pm}_{s}\otimes u^{\pm}_{s}):\nabla u^{\pm}_{s}\mathrm{d}\Omega_{s}^{\pm}.

We estimate the right-hand-side with the Hölder’s inequality and the continuous embedding
\mathbf{H}^{1/2}(\Omega_{s}^{\pm})\hookrightarrow\mathbf{L}^{4}(\Omega_{s}^{\pm})
valid in dimension 2:

\int_{\Omega}(u_{s}^{\pm}\otimes u_{s}^{\pm}):\nabla u_{s}^{\pm}\mathrm{d}\Omega_{s}^{\pm}\leq\|\nabla u_{s}^{\pm}\|_{[\mathbf{L}^{2}(\Omega_{s}^{\pm})]^{2}}\|u_{s}^{\pm}\|^{2}_{\mathbf{L}^{4}(\Omega_{s}^{\pm})}\leq C\|\nabla u_{s}^{\pm}\|_{[\mathbf{L}^{2}(\Omega_{s}^{\pm})]^{2}}\|u_{s}^{\pm}\|^{2}_{\mathbf{H}^{1/2}(\Omega_{s}^{\pm})}.

Since we have the interpolation
\mathbf{H}^{1/2}(\Omega_{s}^{\pm})=\left[\mathbf{L}^{2}(\Omega_{s}^{\pm});\mathbf{H}^{1}(\Omega_{s}^{\pm})\right]_{1/2}
, this yields

\nu^{\pm}\|\nabla u_{s}^{\pm}\|^{2}_{[\mathbf{L}^{2}(\Omega_{s}^{\pm})]^{2}}\leq\rho^{\pm}C\|\nabla u_{s}^{\pm}\|_{[\mathbf{L}^{2}(\Omega_{s}^{\pm})]^{2}}\|u_{s}^{\pm}\|_{\mathbf{L}^{2}(\Omega_{s}^{\pm})}\|u_{s}^{\pm}\|_{\mathbf{H}^{1}(\Omega_{s}^{\pm})}\leq\rho^{\pm}C\|u_{s}^{\pm}\|_{\mathbf{L}^{2}(\Omega_{s}^{\pm})}\|u_{s}^{\pm}\|^{2}_{\mathbf{H}^{1}(\Omega_{s}^{\pm})}.

Using the Poincaré inequality in
\Omega_{s}^{\pm}
, namely
\|u_{s}^{\pm}\|_{\mathbf{H}^{1}(\Omega_{s}^{\pm})}\leq C_{\mathcal{P}}\|\nabla u_{s}^{\pm}\|_{[\mathbf{L}^{2}(\Omega_{s}^{\pm})]^{2}}
, as
{u_{s}}^{\pm}_{|\partial\Omega_{s}^{\pm}}=0
, we deduce

\nu^{\pm}\|u_{s}^{\pm}\|^{2}_{\mathbf{H}^{1}(\Omega_{s}^{\pm})}\leq\rho^{\pm}CC_{\mathcal{P}}\|u_{s}^{\pm}\|_{\mathbf{L}^{2}(\Omega_{s}^{\pm})}\|u_{s}^{\pm}\|^{2}_{\mathbf{H}^{1}(\Omega_{s}^{\pm})}\leq\rho^{\pm}CC_{\mathcal{P}}C_{s}\|u_{s}^{\pm}\|^{2}_{\mathbf{H}^{1}(\Omega_{s}^{\pm})}.

By choosing
C_{s}<\displaystyle\frac{\nu^{\pm}}{\rho^{\pm}CC_{\mathcal{P}}}
, we obtain necessarily
\|u_{s}^{\pm}\|_{\mathbf{H}^{1}(\Omega_{s}^{\pm})}=0
. Hence
u_{s}^{\pm}=0
almost everywhere in
\Omega_{s}^{\pm}
, and so
\nabla p_{s}^{\pm}=0
in
\Omega_{s}^{\pm}
. The pressure is thus constant on both sides of the bubble-soap, and the transmission condition (
3c
) reduces to the so-called Young-Laplace equation, that is
\kappa_{s}=[p_{s}]/\mu
is constant. So
\Gamma_{s}
is a circle enclosing the same volume as
\Gamma
(because of incompressibility), and the displacements
Z_{s}
describe the set
\mathcal{C}
, completing the proof.
∎

Therefore system (
1
) is linearized around
(u_{s}^{+},p_{s}^{+},u_{s}^{-},p_{s}^{-},Z_{s})
such that
u_{s}^{\pm}=0
,
\nabla p_{s}^{\pm}=0
and
Z_{s}\in\mathcal{C}
.

3
On the linearization

In order to linearize system (
1
), the method commonly used consists in defining a change of variable (as an extension of mapping
X
), introduce a change of unknowns and rewrite the equations (
1a
)-(
1c
) in reference domains
\Omega^{\pm}
and
\Gamma
. Next, introduce the displacement
Z=X-X_{s}
, where
X_{s}-\mathrm{Id}\in\mathcal{C}
corresponds to a desired steady state, and linearize the corresponding transformed system by considering

small. Referring to
[
Cou22
]

\begin{array}[]{rcl}\displaystyle\rho^{\pm}\frac{\partial u^{\pm}}{\partial t}-\divg\sigma^{\pm}(u^{\pm},p^{\pm})=\rho^{\pm}f^{\pm}\quad\text{ and }\quad\divg u^{\pm}=0&&\text{in }\Omega^{\pm}\times(0,T),\\
u^{+}=0&&\text{on }\partial\Omega\times(0,T),\\
u^{+}=u^{-}=\displaystyle\frac{\partial Z}{\partial t}\quad\text{ and }\quad-\left[\sigma(u,p)\right]n=\mu\divg_{\Gamma}(\nabla^{n}_{\Gamma}Z)+g&&\text{on }\Gamma\times(0,T),\\[5.0pt]

\nabla^{n}_{\Gamma}Z:=(n\otimes n)\nabla_{\Gamma}Z

3.1
The kernel of

and the lack of coercivity

The kernel of the operator
Z\mapsto\nabla^{n}_{\Gamma}Z=(n\otimes n)\nabla_{\Gamma}Z
which appears in the linearization of

conditions the unique continuation argument used in the proof of approximate controllability (section

(where

. Several examples of such displacements are represented in Figure

Therefore, in the proof of the approximate controllability result in section

3.2
Considering the Laplace-Beltrami operator

In order to define a linear operator whose the kernel is reduced to trivial displacements, we need to regularize the operator
\divg_{\Gamma}\nabla^{n}_{\Gamma}
. One possibility consists in considering a prior control
g_{0}
, in a form of a feedback operator, as it is done in
[
Cou22
]
, as
g_{0}=\divg((\tau\otimes\tau)\nabla_{\Gamma}Z)
, dealing with the tangential gradient of

only, so that the resulting linear operator corresponds to

. Note that in
[

), it is actually the Laplace-Beltrami operator which is considered as operator representing linear version of the surface tension operator. Therefore, from now, we propose to rather deal with system (
2
), involving the Laplace-Beltrami operator. Alternatively, one could also keep the degenerate operator

, consider a reachable set that excludes the elements of its kernel, and thus approximate controllability would be obtained for system (
2
) up to elements of the kernel of

.

For system (
2
), we will assume in the rest of the paper that for all

f^{\pm}\in\mathrm{L}^{2}(0,T;\mathbf{L}^{2}(\Omega^{\pm})),\quad g\in\mathrm{L}^{2}(0,T;\mathbf{H}^{1/2}(\Gamma))\cap\mathrm{H}^{1/4}(0,T;\mathbf{L}^{2}(\Gamma)),

there exists a unique solution
(u^{+},u^{-},Z)
, satisfying

\displaystyle u^{\pm}\in\mathrm{L}^{2}(0,T;\mathbf{H}^{2}(\Omega^{\pm}))\cap\mathrm{H}^{1}(0,T;\mathbf{L}^{2}(\Omega^{\pm})),\quad\displaystyle\frac{\partial Z}{\partial t}\in\mathrm{L}^{2}(0,T;\mathbf{H}^{3/2}(\Gamma))\cap\mathrm{H}^{1}(0,T;\mathbf{H}^{-1/2}(\Gamma)).

This parabolic regularity result is given in
[

Approximate controllability

Assume now

and

Definition 4.1
.

Define the reachable set for system (

\mathcal{R}(T)=\left\{(u^{\pm}(T),Z(T))\in\mathbf{H}^{1}(\Omega^{\pm})\times\mathbf{H}^{1/2}(\Gamma)/\mathbb{R}^{2}\mid(u^{+},u^{-},Z)\text{ satisfies~\eqref{sysmain}, }g\in\mathrm{L}^{2}(0,T;\mathbf{H}^{1/2}(\Gamma))\right\}.

We say that system (

\mathbf{H}^{1}(\Omega^{+})\times\mathbf{H}^{1}(\Omega^{-})\times\mathbf{H}^{1/2}(\Gamma)/\mathbb{R}^{2}

Note that the displacements are considered up to translations of

satisfies the following Bessel type equation:

r^{2}\frac{\partial^{2}v_{\theta}}{\partial r^{2}}+r\frac{\partial v_{\theta}}{\partial r}+\left(\frac{\lambda^{-}}{\nu^{-}}r^{2}-1\right)v_{\theta}=0.

Via the change of variable
\tilde{r}=\displaystyle\sqrt{\frac{\lambda^{-}}{\nu^{-}}}r
, we deduce that there exists
\alpha>0
such that

v_{\theta}(r,\theta)=\alpha J_{1}\left(\sqrt{\frac{\lambda^{-}}{\nu^{-}}}r\right),

where
J_{1}
is the Bessel function of the first kind given by
\displaystyle J_{1}(x)=\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^{m}}{m!(m+1)!}\left(\frac{x}{2}\right)^{2m+1}
.
From the boundary condition given in (
8c
), we have
v_{\theta}(r_{s},\theta)=0
, and consequently, if
r_{s}
does not lie in the set introduced in (
5
), then necessarily
\alpha=0
and thus
v_{\theta}\equiv 0
, and so
v^{-}\equiv 0
. From there system (
7
) reduces to

\begin{array}[]{rcl}-\divg\sigma^{+}(v^{+},q^{+})=\lambda^{+}v^{+}\quad\text{and}\quad\divg v^{+}=0&&\text{in }\Omega^{+},\\[5.0pt]
v^{+}=0&&\text{on }\partial\Omega^{+},\\[5.0pt]
\sigma^{+}(v^{+},q^{+})n=q^{-}n&&\text{on }\Gamma,\end{array}

where
q^{-}
is constant. The continuation argument of
[
FL96
]
applies in order to deduce that
v^{+}\equiv 0
too. Thus
\displaystyle\dot{\phi}^{\pm}=\frac{\partial\phi^{\pm}}{\partial t}\equiv 0
, that is
\phi^{\pm}
are constant in time, and then system (
6
) reduces to

\begin{array}[]{rcl}-\divg\sigma^{\pm}(\phi_{T}^{\pm},\psi_{T}^{\pm})=0\quad\text{and}\quad\divg\phi_{T}^{\pm}=0&&\text{in }\Omega^{\pm},\\[5.0pt]
\phi_{T}^{\pm}=0\quad\text{and}\quad-\left[\sigma(\phi_{T},\psi_{T})\right]n=\mu\Delta_{\Gamma}Z_{T}&&\text{on }\Gamma.\end{array}

An energy estimate shows that

\nu^{+}\int_{\Omega^{+}}|\varepsilon(\phi_{T}^{+})|^{2}\mathrm{d}\Omega^{+}+\nu^{-}\int_{\Omega^{+}}|\varepsilon(\phi_{T}^{-})|^{2}\mathrm{d}\Omega^{-}+\mu\int_{\Gamma}|\nabla_{\Gamma}Z_{T}|^{2}\mathrm{d}\Gamma=0,

(9)

and yields
\varepsilon(\Phi_{T}^{\pm})\equiv 0
. From
[
Tem83
, page 18]
, there exists
h^{\pm}\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
and
\omega^{\pm}\in\mathbb{R}
such that the functions
\phi^{\pm}_{T}
are reduced to
\phi^{\pm}_{T}(y)=h^{\pm}+\omega^{\pm}y^{\perp}
, for
y\in\Omega^{\pm}
,
and since
\phi_{T}^{\pm}=0
on
\Gamma
, we have
\phi_{T}^{\pm}\equiv 0
. Finally, from (
9
) we deduce that
\nabla_{\Gamma}Z_{T}=0
, meaning that
Z_{T}\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
is a translation, in particular
Z_{T}=0
in
\mathbf{H}^{1/2}(\Gamma)/\mathbb{R}^{2}
, which completes the proof.
∎

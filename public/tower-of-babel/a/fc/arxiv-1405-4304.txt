Cores of Dirichlet forms related to Random Matrix Theory
Hirofumi Osada, Hideki Tanemura

Abstract
We prove the sets of polynomials on configuration spaces are cores of Dirichlet forms describing interacting Brownian motion in infinite dimensions. Typical examples of these stochastic dynamics are Dyson’s Brownian motion and Airy interacting Brownian motion. Both particle systems have logarithmic interaction potentials, and naturally arise from random matrix theory. The results of the present paper will be used in a forth coming paper to prove the identity of the infinite-dimensional stochastic dynamics related to the random matrix theories constructed by apparently different methods: the method of space-time correlation functions and that of stochastic analysis.

Random matrices Dyson’s model interacting Brownian motions Airy random point fields logarithmic potentials Dirichlet forms infinite-particle systems

Introduction.

In random matrix theory, one of the main issues is to clarify
the distribution of the eigenvalues and
its asymptotic behavior as the size of the matrices goes to infinity.
The prototypes of random matrices are Gaussian ensembles,
divided into three classes and
called Gaussian orthogonal/unitary/symplectic ensembles (GOE/GUE/GSE),
according to their invariance under conjugation by orthogonal/unitary/symplectic groups.

The eigenvalue distributions of Gaussian random matrices
of
N\times N
size are then given as

\check{m}_{\beta}^{N}(d{\mbox{\boldmath$x$}}_{N})=\frac{1}{Z}h_{N}({\mbox{\boldmath$x$}}_{N})^{\beta}e^{-\frac{\beta}{4}|{\mbox{\boldmath$x$}}_{N}|^{2}}d{\mbox{\boldmath$x$}}_{N},

where
d{\mbox{\boldmath$x$}}_{n}=dx_{1}dx_{2}\cdots dx_{N}
,
{\mbox{\boldmath$x$}}_{N}=(x_{i})\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
, and

\displaystyle{h_{N}({\mbox{\boldmath$x$}}_{N})=\prod_{i<j}^{N}|x_{i}-x_{j}|}.

Here and after
Z
denotes the normalizing constant.
The GOE, GUE, and GSE correspond to inverse temperature

\beta=1,2
and
4
, respectively
[
9
,
2
]
.

The celebrated Wigner’s theorem asserts that the empirical measure

(1.1)

\displaystyle\displaystyle{\frac{1}{N}\sum_{j=1}^{N}\delta_{{x_{j}}/{\sqrt{N}}}}.

of the eigenvalues under the distribution
\check{m}_{\beta}^{N}(d{\mbox{\boldmath$x$}}_{N})
converges to the semicircle law

\varsigma(x)dx
as
N\to\infty

[
2
,
9
]
, where

\varsigma(x)dx
is the probability on
\mathbb{R}

such that

(1.2)

\displaystyle\varsigma(x)dx=\frac{1}{2\pi}\sqrt{4-x^{2}}{\bf 1}_{[-2,2]}(x)dx.

For a countable subset
\{x_{n}\}
, we call the
\sigma
-finite measure

\xi=\sum_{n}\delta_{x_{n}}
a configuration if it becomes a Radon measure.
The set of all configurations on
\mathbb{R}
is a Polish space equipped with the vague topology, and is called the configuration space over
\mathbb{R}
.
We call
\xi
unlabeled particles, and
{\mbox{\boldmath$x$}}=(x_{1},x_{2},\ldots)
labeled particles.

Note that for
N
-particle systems, there exists an obvious bijection between the distribution of the unlabeled
N
-particles and the
symmetric
distribution of the labeled
N
-particles, where
N\in\mathbb{N}
.
We note that this is not the case for infinite particle systems.

For a given distribution
\mu
of
N
-unlabeled particles,
we denote by
\check{\mu}
the symmetric density of the associated

N
-labeled particles in the sequel.

To examine the behavior of the distribution of the configuration

\xi=\sum_{i=1}^{N}\delta_{x_{j}}
under

,

The latter corresponds to the scaling such that

, and the distribution of

under

\check{\mu}_{{\sf soft},\beta}^{N}(d{\mbox{\boldmath$y$}}_{N})=\frac{1}{Z}h_{N}({\mbox{\boldmath$y$}}_{N})^{\beta}e^{\frac{-\beta}{4N^{1/3}}|{\mbox{\boldmath$y$}}_{N}-2N^{2/3}{\bf 1}_{N}|^{2}}d{\mbox{\boldmath$y$}}_{N},

where

.

Let

is the determinantal random point field

Let

Let

An element
\xi

.
A function

For a local function

The infinite sequence given by

.
For given

, the right hand side is a permutation invariant function, and the square field

Main results.

We call a function

.

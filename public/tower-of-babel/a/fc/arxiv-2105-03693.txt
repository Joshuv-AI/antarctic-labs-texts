Abstract
We study the connections between the notions of combinatorial discrepancy and graph degeneracy. In particular, we prove that the maximum discrepancy over all subgraphs H H of a graph G G of the neighborhood set system of H H is sandwiched between Ω ⁡ ( log ⁡ deg ⁡ ( G ) ) \Omega\hskip 0.5pt(\log\mathrm{deg}(G)) and 𝒪 ⁡ ( deg ⁡ ( G ) ) \mathcal{O}\hskip 0.5pt(\mathrm{deg}(G)) , where deg ⁡ ( G ) \mathrm{deg}(G) denotes the degeneracy of G G . We extend this result to inequalities relating weak coloring numbers and discrepancy of graph powers and deduce a new characterization of bounded expansion classes. Then, we switch to a model theoretical point of view, introduce pointer structures, and study their relations to graph classes with bounded expansion. We deduce that a monotone class of graphs has bounded expansion if and only if all the set systems definable in this class have bounded hereditary discrepancy. Using known bounds on the VC-density of set systems definable in nowhere dense classes we also give a characterization of nowhere dense classes in terms of discrepancy. As consequences of our results, we obtain a corollary on the discrepancy of neighborhood set systems of edge colored graphs, a polynomial-time algorithm to compute ε \varepsilon -approximations of size 𝒪 ⁡ ( 1 / ε ) \mathcal{O}\hskip 0.5pt(1/\varepsilon) for set systems definable in bounded expansion classes, an application to clique coloring, and even the non-existence of a quantifier elimination scheme for nowhere dense classes.

Introduction and some motivating examples

Discrepancy theory emerged from the study of the irregularities of
statistical distributions and number sequences. It developed and
became a central tool in computational geometry. Two decades ago,
Matoušek
[
27
]
initiated the study of

combinatorial discrepancy
, which became a significant subject
in its own right. The combinatorial discrepancy measures the
inevitable irregularities of set systems and the inherent difficulty
to approximate them. (We refer to Section
2
for the
formal definitions of the concepts considered in this paper.)

Discrepancy theory offers powerful tools and techniques with many
applications in computational geometry, probabilistic algorithms, derandomization,
communication complexity, searching, machine learning,
pseudorandomness, optimization, computer
graphics, and more. Central notions in this theory are also the well
known notions of VC-dimension,
\varepsilon
-nets and

\varepsilon
-approximations, the latter corresponding to the expected
properties of a pseudorandom set. We refer the reader to the
textbooks
[
9
,
12
,
28
]
for a presentation of the discrepancy method
and some of its applications.

A structural theory of classes of sparse graphs emerged recently,
which is based on the study of densities of shallow minors, generalized
coloring numbers, and constrained orientations
[
35
]
. In
this setting, two central notions are those of
classes with
bounded expansion
, which generalize classes excluding a topological
minor, and
nowhere dense classes
, which generalize classes
locally excluding a topological minor. These classes have strong
algorithmic and structural properties. In particular, in a nowhere dense class
it can be checked in almost linear time whether a first-order formula
is satisfied in a given graph from the
class
[
19
]
. This last example is only
one among others that witness a strong connection between sparsity
theory and first-order logic. We refer the interested reader to

[
35
]
for a comprehensive study of these classes.

The aim of this paper is to establish a bridge between discrepancy
theory and sparsity theory through a study of the combinatorial
discrepancy of set systems defined from sparse classes of graphs by
means of first-order formulas and further to give new
characterizations of degeneracy, bounded expansion, and nowhere denseness in terms of
discrepancy of definable set systems. Before proceeding, we take time
for a few motivating examples.

Preliminaries and statement of the results

2.1
Combinatorial discrepancy

Let
(U,\mathscr{S})
be a
set system
, where
\mathscr{S}
is a collection of
subsets of the
ground set

U
. When the ground set is clear
from the context, we refer to the set system as
\mathscr{S}
. The

discrepancy
of a mapping
\chi\colon U\rightarrow\{-1,1\}
on a
set
S\in\mathscr{S}
is

\mathrm{disc}_{\chi}(S)=\bigl|\sum_{v\in S}\chi(v)\bigr|
;
the
discrepancy
of
\chi
on
\mathscr{S}
is the maximum of

\mathrm{disc}_{\chi}(S)
over all
S\in\mathscr{S}
, that is,

\mathrm{disc}_{\chi}(\mathscr{S})=\max_{S\in\mathscr{S}}\mathrm{disc}_{\chi}(S)
.
The
(combinatorial) discrepancy
of
\mathscr{S}
is the minimum
discrepancy of a mapping
\chi\colon U\rightarrow\{-1,1\}
on
\mathscr{S}
,
that is,

\mathrm{disc}(\mathscr{S})=\min_{\chi:U\rightarrow\{-1,1\}}\ \max_{S\in\mathscr{S}}\ \biggl|\sum_{v\in S}\chi(v)\biggr|.

Thus, the combinatorial discrepancy of a set system measures how balanced a

2
-coloring of this system can be.

It is usual to consider bounds for the discrepancy of a set system

(U,\mathscr{S})
in terms of
n=\bigl|\bigcup\mathscr{S}\bigr|
(and sometimes in terms of
m=|\mathscr{S}|
). For
instance, by a celebrated result of Spencer
[
41
]
, the
discrepancy of a set system is in
\mathcal{O}\hskip 0.5pt(\sqrt{n\log(m/n)})
, and in the case
where
m=n
we have
\mathrm{disc}(\mathscr{S})\leq 6\sqrt{n}
. This latter bound is
tight up to the constant. Another important result is the Theorem of
Beck and Fiala
[
4
]
.

Theorem 2.1

(Beck-Fiala Theorem)
.

The discrepancy of a set system with
degree
at most
t

(that is, each element lies in at most
t
sets) is less than

2t
.

This theorem was subsequently improved by Bednarchak and Helm

[
5
]
, who gave an upper bound of
2d-3
for

d\geq 3
. Then Bukh
[
6
]
proved that the upper
bound can be decreased to
2d-\log^{*}d
for sufficiently large
d
.

Many of the techniques to derive upper bounds for the discrepancy are
non-constructive and it is difficult to efficiently find optimal
discrepancy colorings. Given a set system on
n
elements and

m\in\mathcal{O}\hskip 0.5pt(n)
sets, it is
\mathsf{NP}
-hard to distinguish whether the system
has discrepancy
0
or

\Omega\hskip 0.5pt(\sqrt{n})

[
8
]
. In
particular, under the assumption
{\mathsf{P}}\neq{\mathsf{NP}}
, one cannot compute a
function
\chi
whose discrepancy on the set system is within factor

o\hskip 0.5pt(\sqrt{n})
of the discrepancy of the
system. However, in polynomial time one can compute a coloring
\chi

with discrepancy
\mathcal{O}\hskip 0.5pt(\sqrt{n})

[
24
]
.
Also, the proof of the Beck-Fiala Theorem is constructive, and gives a
polynomial time deterministic algorithm to compute a coloring
\chi

with discrepancy smaller than twice the degree of the set system.

A standard example of set systems with high discrepancy is given by the following.

Example 1

(Sylvester’s example)
.

Sylvester inductively constructed Hadamard matrices
H_{\hskip-0.7ptp}
of order
2^{p}
for every non-negative integer
p
as follows:

H_{0}=\begin{pmatrix}[r]1\end{pmatrix}
,
H_{1}=\begin{pmatrix}[r]1&1\\
1&-1\end{pmatrix}
, and for
p\geq 1

H_{\hskip-0.7ptp+1}=\begin{pmatrix}[r]H_{p}&H_{p}\\
H_{p}&-H_{p}\end{pmatrix}=H_{1}\otimes H_{\hskip-0.7ptp}
, where
\otimes
denotes the Kronecker product of matrices.
Let

\mathscr{S}_{\hskip-0.7ptp}=\bigl\{\{j\colon(H_{\hskip-0.7ptp})_{i,j}=1\}\colon 1\leq i\leq 2^{p}\bigr\}.

Then

\mathrm{disc}(\mathscr{S}_{\hskip-0.7ptp})=\Omega\hskip 0.5pt(\sqrt{n}),

where
n=|\bigcup\mathscr{S}_{\hskip-0.7ptp}|
.

Unfortunately, the discrepancy is known to be a
fragile
notion,
as witnessed by the following standard example (see
e.g.
[
3
]
): let
\mathscr{S}=\{S_{1},\dots,S_{m}\}
be a
set system with ground set
U
and let
\mathscr{S}^{\prime}=\{S_{1}^{\prime},\dots,S_{m}^{\prime}\}

be a copy of
\mathscr{S}
with ground set
U^{\prime}
disjoint from
U
. Then
the set system
\mathscr{S}^{\prime\prime}=\{S_{1}\cup S_{1}^{\prime}\dots,S_{m}\cup S_{m}^{\prime}\}
has always
discrepancy zero, independent of the discrepancy of
\mathscr{S}
.A more robust notion is the
hereditary discrepancy
of a set
system
(U,\mathscr{S})
, defined as

\mathrm{herdisc}(\mathscr{S})=\max_{U^{\prime}\subseteq U}\mathrm{disc}(\mathscr{S}|_{U^{\prime}})
,
where
\mathscr{S}|_{U^{\prime}}
denotes the set system
\{S\cap U^{\prime}:S\penalty\ \in\penalty\ \mathscr{S}\}
. Moreover, bounding the hereditary discrepancy allows to
bound the sizes of
\varepsilon
-nets and
\varepsilon
-approximations

(what is not the case for discrepancy). For this reason, we focus on
hereditary discrepancy in this paper. Note that the bound in the
Beck-Fiala Theorem applies to the hereditary discrepancy as well, as
considering the trace over a subset of the universe does not increase
the degree of a set system.

2.2
Sparse graph classes.

We consider finite, simple and undirected graphs. For a graph
G
we
write
V(G)
for its vertex set and
E(G)
for its edge set. We denote
by
\delta(G)
the
minimum degree
of
G
, by
\overline{\rm d}(G)
the

average degree
of
G
, and by
\omega(G)
the

of

We denote by

the

of

the set of all the vertices adjacent to

in

\mathscr{S}^{E}(G)=\{N_{G}(v):v\in V(G)\}
.

We write

(resp.

is a subgraph (resp. an induced subgraph) of

. A graph

-degenerate is the

\deg(G)
of

.Hence,
\deg(G)=\max_{H\subseteq_{i}G}\delta(H)
.

A class

if it is closed under

if it is closed under taking
induced subgraphs. A class

are

-degenerate.

The

of a graph

is the graph

obtained by subdividing every edge of

r

\leq

r

of a graph

\leq

2r
-subdivision of

is a subgraph of
G
. We denote by

the set of all the topological minors of

r
,
and define

\widetilde{\nabla}_{r}(G)=\max\{|E(H)|/|V(H)|:H\in G\,\widetilde{\triangledown}\,r\}
.
The two key notions in the theory of sparsity
[
35
]
are the

bounded expansion
and
nowhere denseness
,
defined as follows.

Definition 2.1
.

A class
\mathscr{C}
of graphs has

if there
exists a function
f\colon\mathbb{N}\rightarrow\mathbb{N}
with

\forall G\in\mathscr{C}\ \ \forall H\in G\,\widetilde{\triangledown}\,r\quad\overline{\rm d}(H)\leq f(r).

(1)

Example 2
.

All proper minor-closed classes of graphs and all classes of graphs
with bounded maximum degree have bounded expansion.

Definition 2.2
.

A class
\mathscr{C}
of graphs is
nowhere dense
if there
exists a function
f\colon\mathbb{N}\rightarrow\mathbb{N}
with

\forall G\in\mathscr{C}\ \ \forall H\in G\,\widetilde{\triangledown}\,r\quad\omega(H)\leq f(r).

(2)

Note that every class with bounded expansion is nowhere dense but the
converse does not hold.

Example 3
.

The class of all graphs
G
whose girth exceeds the maximum degree
is nowhere dense but does not have bounded expansion.

Bounded expansion and nowhere dense classes enjoy numerous
characterizations and applications (see
[
35
]
).
Among
others, let us mention the characterizations based on
p
-centered
colorings
[
33
]
and on (generalized) weak coloring numbers
[
43
]
.

The
depth
of a rooted forest is the maximum number of vertices in a path linking a root to a leaf. The

{\rm td}(G)
of a graph

is the minimum height of a rooted forest

such that adjacent vertices in
G
belong to a path of

linking a root to a leaf. For a positive integer

, a
p
-treedepth coloring
of a graph

is a vertex coloring such that every
k\leq p
color classes induce a subgraph with treedepth at most
k
. Closely related is the notion of a
p
-centered coloring
, which is a coloring of the vertices such that every connected subgraph with at most
p
colors on the vertices contains some uniquely colored vertex. It is easily checked that every
(p+1)
-centered coloring is a

-treedepth coloring (see
[
32
,
35
]
).

Theorem 2.2

(Nešetřil, Ossona de Mendez
[
33
]
)
.

A class
\mathscr{C}
of graphs has bounded expansion if and only if there exists a function
f\colon\mathbb{N}\rightarrow\mathbb{N}
such that every graph

admits, for each positive integer

, a
p
-centered coloring with at most

We recall the definition of the generalized coloring
numbers, which have been introduced by Kierstead and Yang

[
23
]
as a generalization of the so-called coloring

be a graph and let

be a linear ordering
of

. We say that a vertex

-reachable

) linking

is the minimum

. The

is defined as the minimum

.

Theorem 2.3

(Zhu,
[
43
]
)
.

A class
\mathscr{C}
of graphs has bounded expansion if and only if

such that for every graph

and every positive

.

Note that nowhere dense classes can also be characterized in terms of

to first-order definable set

2.3
Model theory and definable set systems.

In order to establish a bridge between an approach of discrepancy
based on structural graph theory and one based on (finite) model
theory, we consider (from
Section

5
onward) set systems defined
from graphs by means of first-order formulas. As a motivating example among others,
note that first-order logic is the foundation of query languages over relational databases
(see e.g.
[
25
]
). In this original setting, queries
are formulas and query answers form definable set systems.

For simplicity, we focus on (vertex
colored) graphs, and on structures with only unary predicates and
unary functions (which we call
pointer structures
). However,
all definitions extend to general structures in a straightforward
way. We use standard notation from graph theory
[
11
]
and
finite model theory
[
25
]
, and we refer to the
referenced textbooks for all undefined notation.

Recall that a
signature

\sigma
is a set of relation and function symbols with an attached arity. A
\sigma
-structure

\mathbf{M}
is defined by its
domain

M
(which is a set), and an interpretation of the symbols in
\sigma
as actual relations and functions on
M
.
The
Gaifman graph
of a structure
\mathbf{M}
is the graph with vertex set
M
, where two (distinct) vertices
u,v\in M
are adjacent if they belong together to a tuple of a relation (
k
-ary functions being interpreted as
(k+1)
-ary relations).

The
quantifier rank
of a first-order formula
\varphi
is the maximum number of nested quantifiers in
\varphi
.
For example, the quantifier rank of the formula

\exists x\,\bigl((\exists y\,(P(x,y)\vee(\forall z\,R(x,yz)))\bigr)\wedge(\forall t\,Z(x,t))
is three.
We say that two
\sigma
-structures
\mathbf{M}
and
\mathbf{M}^{\prime}
are
q
-equivalent
if they satisfy the same sentences with quantifier rank at most
q
.

Let
G
be a graph with adjacency relation
E(x,y)
. A set

S\subseteq V(G)^{d}
is
definable
in
G

with parameters

if there exists a partitioned formula
\varphi(\bar{x};\bar{y})
(with

\bar{x}=(x_{1},\dots,x_{d})
and
\bar{y}=(y_{1},\dots,y_{\ell})
) and an

\ell
-tuple
\bar{b}=(b_{1},\dots,b_{\ell})
of vertices of
G
such that
a
d
-tuple
\bar{v}=(v_{1},\dots,v_{d})
belongs to
S
if and only if

G\models\varphi(\bar{v},\bar{b})
. For
\bar{b}\in V(G)^{\ell}
, let

\varphi(G,\bar{b})=\{\bar{v}\in V(G)^{d}:G\models\varphi(\bar{v},\bar{b})\}
be
the subset of
d
-tuples of vertices of
G
defined by
\varphi
for the
parameter
\bar{b}
. The
set system defined by
\varphi(\bar{x};\bar{y})
on
G
is the set system

(V(G)^{|\bar{x}|},\mathscr{S}^{\varphi}(G))
, where

\mathscr{S}^{\varphi}(G)=\{\varphi(G,\bar{b}):\bar{b}\in V(G)^{|\bar{y}|}\}
. In
particular, the neighborhood set system
\mathscr{S}^{E}(G)
of a graph
G
is
defined by the partitioned formula
E(x;y)
.

Let

on

\{\rho(G)\}
, where

. More generally, for a finite set

(with same

2.4

The Vapnik–Chervonenkis dimension (VC-dimension) is a classical

U

(U,\mathscr{S})
is

shattered
by
\mathscr{S}
if
\mathscr{S}|_{X}
contains all the subsets
of
X
. The
VC-dimension
of
\mathscr{S}
is the maximum size of a
subset shattered by
\mathscr{S}
. Classes
\mathscr{C}
of graphs with the property
that for all first-order formulas

\mathscr{S}^{\varphi}(G)

play a key role in model theory: they are called

dependent classes

[
39
]
.
Classes whose monotone closure is dependent are exactly nowhere dense
classes
[
1
]
.

A more precise description of the complexity of a set system
\mathscr{S}
is
given by its
(primal) shatter function

\pi_{\mathscr{S}}
, which is defined as
follows: for an integer
m
, we define
\pi_{\mathscr{S}}(m)
to be the maximum
over all subsets
X\subseteq U
of size
m
of the number of distinct
sets in the collection
\mathscr{S}|_{X}
. By the famous Sauer-Shelah

[
37
,
38
,
42
]
we
have
\pi_{\mathscr{S}}(k)\in\mathcal{O}\hskip 0.5pt(m^{d})
if the VC-dimension of
\mathscr{S}
is at
most
d
.

A strong connection between these notions and combinatorial
discrepancy is given by the next fundamental result, which is known to
be optimal
[
2
]
.

Theorem 2.4

(Matoušek
[
26
]
and Matoušek, Welzl, and Wernisch
[
29
]
)
.

Let
\mathscr{S}
be a set system, let
n
be the size of the ground set of

\mathscr{S}
, and let
d,C
be constants, such that
\pi_{\mathscr{S}}(m)\leq Cm^{d}
for
all
m\leq n
. Then the discrepancy of
\mathscr{S}
is bounded
by

\mathrm{disc}(\mathscr{S})=\mathcal{O}\bigl(n^{1/2-1/2d}\bigr),\text{ if }d>1,\text{ and }\mathrm{disc}(\mathscr{S})=\mathcal{O}\bigl(\log^{5/2}n\bigr),\text{ if }d=1.

Each set system
\mathscr{S}
with ground set
U
can be represented by its
incidence graph

with parts
U
and
\mathscr{S}
, where
u\in U
is adjacent to
S\in\mathscr{S}
if

.
The
dual
of a set system
\mathscr{S}
with ground set
U
is the set system

\mathscr{S}^{\ast}
, whose incidence graph is obtained from the incidence graph of
\mathscr{S}
by exchanging the parts. (Note that this corresponds to the notion of hypergraph duality.)

The
dual shatter function

of a set system
\mathscr{S}
is defined as the primal shatter function of the dual set system
\mathscr{S}^{\ast}
.
The discrepancy of a set system can also be bounded in terms of the dual shatter function (where the bound is also tight
[
26
]
). As noticed by Matoušek
[
26
]
, the next theorem follows from a slight modification of the bound obtained by Matoušek, Welzl, and Wernisch

]
for the discrepancy of set systems defined by half-spaces, by using (in the abstract setting) the packing lemma due to Haussler
[
20
]
, instead of the elementary lemma concerning the volume of an

-ball in an arrangement of hyperplanes due to Chazelle and Welzl.

Theorem 2.5
.

Let
\mathscr{S}
be a set system, let
n
be the size of the ground set of

\mathscr{S}
, and let
d,C
be constants, such that
\pi_{\mathscr{S}}^{*}(m)\leq Cm^{d}
for
all
m\leq n
. Then the discrepancy of
\mathscr{S}
is bounded
by

\mathrm{disc}(\mathscr{S})=\mathcal{O}\bigl(n^{1/2-1/2d}\,\sqrt{\log n}\bigr).

Bounded expansion and nowhere dense classes
can be characterized by shatter functions:

(Pilipczuk, Siebertz,
Toruńczyk
[
36
]
)
.

Let
\mathscr{C}
be a monotone class of graphs.
Then

1.

the class
\mathscr{C}
has bounded expansion if and only if for every

\varphi(\bar{x};\bar{y})
there exists a constant

with

.

From this result, one can derive a bound on the hereditary discrepancy
of sets systems definable in nowhere dense and bounded expansion

established in

; in the bounded expansion case, our
result will establish a constant upper bound, clearly improving the

and
2.6
.

3
Discrepancy and Degeneracy

In this section, we relate the degeneracy of a graph
G
to the discrepancy of
the neighborhood set system
\mathscr{S}^{E}(G)
of
G
. Our main
result is the following theorem, which directly follows from

Lemmas

3.2
and
3.1
proved below.

Theorem 3.1
.

For every graph
G
we have

\frac{\log_{2}(\pi\deg(G))}{4}-2\leq\max_{H\subseteq G}\mathrm{disc}(\mathscr{S}^{E}(H))<3\deg(G).

(3)

About the upper bound

The bipartite graph
G
with a part of size
n
and a part of size

\binom{n}{d}
corresponding to all possible neighborhoods of size
d

in the part of size
n
is such that

\max_{H\subseteq G}\mathrm{disc}(\mathscr{S}^{E}(H))\geq d=\deg(G)
. Hence, the
upper bound of
Theorem

3.1
is tight up to a constant
factor.

About the lower bound

As Spencer’s bound
\mathrm{disc}(\mathscr{S})\leq 6\sqrt{n}
for every set system

\mathscr{S}
on a ground set of size
n
and
\mathcal{O}\hskip 0.5pt(n)
many sets is known to
be tight up to a constant factor (see
Example

4
), we have

\max_{H\subseteq K_{n}}\mathrm{disc}(\mathscr{S}^{E}(H))\in\Omega\hskip 0.5pt(\deg(K_{n})^{1/2})
. It is possible that the lower
bound given in
Theorem

3.1
might be improved to

\Omega\hskip 0.5pt(\deg(G)^{c})
for some positive constant
c
.

About the maximum over subgraphs

In the statement, we consider
\max_{H\subseteq G}\mathrm{disc}(\mathscr{S}^{E}(H))
,
which is a monotone form of the discrepancy. Instead of

\mathrm{disc}(\mathscr{S}^{E}(H))
we could have considered

\mathrm{herdisc}(\mathscr{S}^{E}(H))
. However, this would have led to the same result,
as for every graph
G
we have

\max_{H\subseteq G}\mathrm{herdisc}(\mathscr{S}^{E}(H))=\max_{H\subseteq G}\mathrm{disc}(\mathscr{S}^{E}(H)).

Indeed, as
\mathrm{herdisc}(\mathscr{S}^{E}(H))\geq\mathrm{disc}(\mathscr{S}^{E}(H))
we get that

\max_{H\subseteq G}\mathrm{herdisc}(\mathscr{S}^{E}(H))
is at least

\max_{H\subseteq G}\mathrm{disc}(\mathscr{S}^{E}(H))
. For the reverse inequality, it
will be sufficient to prove

\mathrm{herdisc}(\mathscr{S}^{E}(G))\leq\max_{H\subseteq G}\mathrm{disc}(\mathscr{S}^{E}(H))
. Let

V^{\prime}\subseteq V(G)
be such that

\mathrm{herdisc}(\mathscr{S}^{E}(G))=\mathrm{disc}(\mathscr{S}^{E}(G)|_{V^{\prime}})
, let
W\subseteq V(G)
be
the set of all neighbors in
G
of some vertex in
V^{\prime}
, and let
H
be
the subgraph of
G
with vertex set
V^{\prime}\cup W
, where we keep all the
edges of
G
with some endpoint in
V^{\prime}
. Then

\mathscr{S}^{E}(G)|_{V^{\prime}}\subseteq\mathscr{S}^{E}(H)
, thus

\mathrm{herdisc}(\mathscr{S}^{E}(G))\leq\mathrm{disc}(\mathscr{S}^{E}(H))
.

We now prove that the discrepancy of the neighborhood set system is
linearly bounded by the degeneracy.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
G
be a graph. Then

\mathrm{herdisc}(\mathscr{S}^{E}(G))<3\deg(G)
.

Proof.

Let
d=\deg(G)
and let
\vec{G}
be an orientation of
G
with
maximum out-degree
d
. Let
N^{-}(v)
and
N^{+}(v)
denote,
respectively, the in-neighborhood and the out-neighborhood of a
vertex
v
. Consider the set system
\mathscr{S}_{1}=\{N^{-}(v):v\in V(G)\}
. Every vertex
v
in
V(G)
belongs to at most
d
sets in

\mathscr{S}_{1}
, for if
v\in N^{-}(u)
, then
u\in N^{+}(v)
. According to the
Beck-Fiala Theorem, for every subset
X
of vertices there exists a
function
\chi\colon X\to\{-1,1\}
such that for every vertex
v

we have
\Bigl|\sum_{u\in N^{-}(v)\cap X}\chi(u)\Bigr|<2d
. As

|N^{+}(v)\cap X|\leq d
we have

\Bigl|\sum_{u\in N_{G}(v)\cap X}\chi(u)\Bigr|<3d
. Hence we have

\mathrm{disc}(\mathscr{S}^{E}(G)_{|X})<3\deg(G)
.
∎

Using Bukh’s improvement

\rhd
Claim 1
.

Let

be positive integers and let

\sum_{k=0}^{a}\binom{a}{k}\,\binom{b}{k}\leq\binom{2s}{s}.

5
A model theoretic approach to degeneracy: pointer structures

A
pointer structure
is a unary structure, that is, a structure with a (possibly infinite) signature
\sigma
that consists of unary relation and unary function symbols. It will be convenient to
assume that the function symbols in
\sigma
are the elements of a monoid
{\mathcal{F}}_{\sigma}
. We fix the signature
\sigma
and we will consider only
\sigma
-structures
satisfying the theory
T_{\sigma}
that consists of the sentences

\forall x\,f(g(x))=h(x)
, where
f
and
g
range over
{\mathcal{F}}_{\sigma}
and
h=f\mathop{\circ}g
. Note that this is not really a restriction, but rather a way to simplify the notations.

Observation 5.1
.

For every
d
-degenerate graph
G
there is a pointer structure
\mathbf{M}
with
d
unary functions such that
\mathscr{S}^{E}(G)=\mathscr{S}^{\eta}(\mathbf{M})
, where
\eta(x,y)
is the quantifier-free formula

\eta(x,y):=\neg(x=y)\wedge\bigvee_{i=1}^{d}\bigl((f_{i}(x)=y)\vee(f_{i}(y)=x)\bigr).

Proof.

If
G
is
d
-degenerate, then
G
has an orientation
\vec{G}
with maximum out-degree
d
.
Consider such an orientation
\vec{G}
of
G
and an arbitrary labeling of the arcs of
\vec{G}
by integers between
1
and
d
such that the arcs with the same origin have pairwise different labels.
We define
\mathbf{M}
as the pointer structure with domain
V(G)
such that for every vertex
v
and every integer
i\in[d]
we have
f_{i}(v)=w
if
(v,w)
is an arc of
\vec{G}
with label
i
, and
f_{i}(v)=v
if no arc with origin
v
has label
i
. That
G\models E(u,v)
is equivalent to
\mathbf{M}\models\eta(u,v)
is straightforward.
∎

For a pointer structure
\mathbf{M}
(with signature
\sigma
) and a subset
A
of the domain of
\mathbf{M}
, we define the pointer structure
\mathbf{M}[A]

weakly induced
by
A
on
\mathbf{M}
as the pointer structure with domain
A
(and signature
\sigma
), such that for every relation symbol

P\in\sigma
, every function symbol
f\in\sigma
, and all distinct
u
and
v
in
A
, we have

\displaystyle\mathbf{M}[A]\models P(u)

\displaystyle\iff\mathbf{M}\models P(u)

\displaystyle\mathbf{M}[A]\models f(u)=v

\displaystyle\iff\mathbf{M}\models f(u)=v

(It follows that if
u\in A
and the image by
f
of
u
in
\mathbf{M}
is not in
A
, then
u
is a fixed point of
f
in
\mathbf{M}[A]
.)
Note that the Gaifman graph of
\mathbf{M}[A]
is the subgraph of the Gaifman graph of
\mathbf{M}
induced by
A
.

5.1
Hereditary discrepancy of QF-definable sets systems

The goal of this section is to prove the following theorem.

Theorem 5.1
.

For every finite set

\Phi=\{\varphi_{1}(\bar{x};\bar{y}),\dots,\varphi_{p}(\bar{x};\bar{y})\}
of quantifier free partitioned
\sigma
-formulas (with same
|\bar{x}|
but possibly different
|\bar{y}|
) there exists a
constant
C_{\Phi}
such that for every
\sigma
-structure
\mathbf{M}

we have

\mathrm{herdisc}(\mathscr{S}^{\Phi}(\mathbf{M}))\leq C_{\Phi}.

The proof goes by the following key steps. One of the building blocks is the Beck-Fiala Theorem. However, we cannot directly apply this result as it requires set systems to have bounded degree. We generalize the theorem to set systems that are Boolean combinations of set systems with bounded degree (Lemmas
5.1
and
5.2
).
Finally, we prove that we can decompose set systems defined by quantifier free formulas into Boolean combinations of set systems that have bounded degree (Lemma
5.3
), which concludes the proof of
Theorem

5.1
.

The two following lemmas extend the Beck-Fiala Theorem to handle
set systems built from sets in a set system of bounded degree.

Lemma 5.1
.

Let
(U,\mathscr{S}_{0})
be a set system of degree at most
t
.
Then the set system
(U,\mathscr{S}_{1})
, where
\mathscr{S}_{1}
is the collection of all intersections of sets in
\mathscr{S}_{0}
(with the convention that
U
is the empty intersection) has degree at most
2^{t}
.

Proof.

As each
v\in U
belongs to at most
t
sets
S_{1},\dots,S_{t}
in
\mathscr{S}_{0}
, it belongs to at most
2^{t}
sets in
\mathscr{S}_{1}
, namely the sets of the form
\bigcap_{i\in I}S_{i}
, for
I\subseteq[t]
. Thus the set system
(U,\mathscr{S}_{1})
has degree at most
2^{t}
.
∎

Lemma 5.2
.

Let
(U,\mathscr{S}_{1})
and
(U,\mathscr{S})
be two set systems over the same ground set
U
such that
\mathscr{S}_{1}
is closed under taking intersections (including
U
, the empty intersection) and such that
every set in
\mathscr{S}
is a Boolean combination of at most
k
sets from
\mathscr{S}_{1}
.
Then
\mathrm{herdisc}(\mathscr{S})\leq 4^{k}\,\mathrm{herdisc}(\mathscr{S}_{1})
.

Proof.

Let
S\in\mathscr{S}
. By assumption,
S
is a Boolean combination of at most
k
sets
S_{1},\dots,S_{k}
in
\mathscr{S}_{1}
. This Boolean combination can be written as a disjoint union of at most
2^{k}
terms of the form
T_{I}=\bigcap_{i\in I}S_{i}\cap\bigcap_{j\in[k]\setminus I}(U\setminus S_{j})
, where
I\subseteq[k]
. Let
\mathbb{I}_{Y}\colon U\rightarrow\{0,1\}
denote the indicator function of a subset
Y
of
U
. For a subset
J
of
[k]
, let
S_{J}=\bigcap_{j\in J}S_{j}
. Note that
S_{J}\in\mathscr{S}_{1}
. Then we have

\mathbb{I}_{T_{I}}=\mathbb{I}_{S_{I}}\cdot\prod_{j\in[k]\setminus I}(\mathbb{I}_{U}-\mathbb{I}_{S_{j}})=\sum_{J\subseteq[k]\setminus I}(-1)^{|J|}\,\mathbb{I}_{S_{I\cup J}}.

To see this, consider some
u\in U
. Assume first that
u
does not appear in any of the sets
S_{j}
for
j\in[k]\setminus I
. Then the only term in the sum with
\mathbb{I}_{S_{I\cup J}}(u)\neq 0
is that with
J=\emptyset
, so we obtain exactly
I_{S_{I}}
. Otherwise, the set
K=\{j\in[k]\setminus I:u\in S_{j}\}
is non-empty and
u\in S_{J}
if and only if
J\subseteq K
. Thus we count
u
exactly

\sum_{J\subseteq K}(-1)^{|J|}=\sum_{i=0}^{|K|}\binom{|K|}{i}(-1)^{i}=(1-1)^{|K|}=0
times by the binomial theorem.

Hence, for any
X\subseteq U
we have

|\chi(T_{I}\cap X)|\leq\sum_{J\subseteq[k]\setminus I}|\chi(S_{I\cup J}\cap X)|\leq 2^{k}\cdot\mathrm{herdisc}(\mathscr{S}_{1})
.
As
\chi(S\cap X)
is the sum of at most
2^{k}
terms of the form

\chi(T_{I}\cap X)
we deduce
\mathrm{herdisc}(\mathscr{S})\leq 4^{k}\,\mathrm{herdisc}(\mathscr{S}_{1})
.
∎

We now turn to Lemma
5.3
, which is the main ingredient from the logical point of view.

Lemma 5.3
.

Let
\varphi(\bar{x};\bar{y})
be a quantifier-free
\sigma
-formula.
There exists a finite set

\Psi=\{\rho_{i}(\bar{x}):1\leq i\leq k\}\cup\{\psi_{j}(\bar{x};\bar{z}):1\leq j\leq\ell\}

of quantifier-free
\sigma
-formulas (with same
|\bar{x}|
, but possibly different
|\bar{z}|
for the formulas
\psi_{j}
)
where
each
\psi_{j}(\bar{x},\bar{z})
has the form

\psi_{j}(\bar{x},\bar{z}):=\bigwedge_{r=1}^{|\bar{z}|}\bigl(f_{\alpha_{j,r}}(x_{i_{j,r}})=z_{r}\bigr)

and there is an
integer
p
such that
for every
\sigma
-structure
\mathbf{M}
,

\mathscr{S}^{\varphi}(\mathbf{M})
is a Boolean combination of

\mathscr{S}^{\Psi}(\mathbf{M})
, and
\mathscr{S}^{\Psi}(\mathbf{M})

.

Note that we do not translate

into an equivalent formula.
An example for the construction can be found below the proof and it may be
helpful to consider it in parallel while reading the proof.

Proof.

The formula
\varphi(\bar{x};\bar{y})
is equivalent
(modulo

) to a Boolean combination of

\zeta(\bar{x};\bar{y}):=\rho(\bar{x})\wedge\eta(\bar{y})\wedge\bigwedge_{(f,g,i,j)\in\mathcal{F}}f(x_{i})=g(y_{j}),

where
\mathcal{F}
is a finite subset of

{\mathcal{F}}_{\sigma}\times{\mathcal{F}}_{\sigma}\times[|\bar{x}|]\times[|\bar{y}|]
.
This is because we may assume that
\varphi
uses no composition of

is a monoid, and because every atomic formula can either use a unary relation symbol (and then is in
\rho
or
\eta
), or express equality of two terms. If both terms are of the form

(resp.

\rho
(resp. in
\eta

.
Moreover, we can assume that each pair

.
Then for every structure
\mathbf{M}
and every tuple

we have that

is a Boolean combination of sets

.

Now for every

of the Boolean combination, we add

and
\psi
to
\Psi
, where we define
\bar{z}
and

from

by replacing each appearing

by a new variable

.
Note that
\bar{z}
may be longer than
\bar{y}
, however its length is at most the number of conjuncts in the big conjunction in

.
Then
\psi
has the required form.
Moreover, for every tuple
\bar{b}
, we can define an assignment
\bar{c}
of

(let

) such that

\zeta(\mathbf{M};\bar{b})=\begin{cases}\rho(\mathbf{M})\cap\psi(\mathbf{M};\bar{c})&\text{if }\mathbf{M}\models\eta(\bar{b})\\

Finally, observe that for every tuple

, there do not exist two distinct tuples

with

. If

of the conjunction,

.
Hence the degree of each

is

. Obviously, the degree of each

(as this set system contains

As we have

is at most

.
∎

5.2
Pointer structures and set-defined graphs

Pointer structures are intrinsically related to set-defined classes.
Recall that the
age
of an infinite graph is the class of all its finite induced subgraphs. A class
\mathscr{C}
(of finite graphs) is
set-defined
if
it is included in the age of an (infinite) graph definable in
\mathbb{N}
(seen as a model of an infinite set in the empty language)
[
22
]
.
In other words,
\mathscr{C}
is set defined if there exists a partitioned formula
\varphi(\bar{x};\bar{y})
with
|\bar{x}|=|\bar{y}|=d
such that every graph in
\mathscr{C}
is an induced subgraph of the graph with vertex set
\mathbb{N}^{d}
in which
\bar{u}
and
\bar{v}
are adjacent if and only if
\mathbb{N}\models\varphi(\bar{u},\bar{v})
. Set-defined classes have bounded VC-dimension (they are actually both semi-algebraic and stable).
Set-defined classes of graphs generalize classes of degenerate graphs.
Indeed, it is proved in
[
22
]
that
a class is degenerate if and only if it is
biclique-free
(i.e. excludes some biclique as a subgraph) and set-defined.

Lemma 5.4
.

If a set system is definable by a quantifier free formula in a pointer structure, then it is included in the neighborhood set system of a set-defined graph. Conversely, every neighborhood set system of a set-defined graph is included in a set system definable by a quantifier free formula in a pointer structure.

Proof.

Let
q(\bar{x};\bar{y})
be a quantifier-free formula in the language of pointer structures.
We can assume that the formula
q
contains no composition of functions (as we assumed that the functions in the signature form a monoid). Let
f_{1},\dots,f_{a}
(resp.
P_{1},\dots,P_{b}
) be the functions (resp. the predicates) appearing in
q
.
Let
\mathbf{M}
be a pointer structure with universe
M
. Without loss of
generality we assume
M=\{2,\ldots,|M|+2\}
. We will define
\mathbf{M}
on the
subset
X=\{0,1\}\cup M
of
\mathbb{N}
, where we will define a bipartite graph
such that the neighborhoods of the elements of one part correspond to the sets defined by
q
on
\mathbf{M}
.

We associate to each element
u
of
M
the tuples

\displaystyle\Lambda_{0}(u)

\displaystyle=(0,u,f_{1}(u),\dots,f_{a}(u),0,p_{1}(u),\dots,p_{b}(u))\text{ and }

\displaystyle\Lambda_{1}(u)

\displaystyle=(1,u,f_{1}(u),\dots,f_{a}(u),0,p_{1}(u),\dots,p_{b}(u)),

where
p_{i}(u)=1
if
\mathbf{M}\models P_{i}(u)
, and
p_{i}(u)=0
otherwise.
The tuples
\Lambda_{0}(u)
will belong to one part of the bipartite
graph and the tuples
\Lambda_{1}(v)
will belong to the other part. We
use the first coordinate to distinguish the parts.

Then it is easily checked that the formula
q(\bar{x};\bar{y})
is equivalent to a quantifier free formula
Q(\bar{s},\bar{t})
, where
\bar{s}
consists of all the tuples
\Lambda_{0}(x_{i})
and
\bar{t}
consists of all the tuples
\Lambda_{1}(y_{j})
. For
example, to check the atomic formula
f_{i}(x_{1})=g_{j}(y_{2})
we need to check
that the
(i+2)
nd coordinate of
s_{1}
is equal to the

(j+2)
nd coordinate of
t_{2}
(the
i+2
nd coordinate of
s_{1}
, which is
the tuple
\Lambda_{0}(x_{1})
, contains the value
f_{i}(x_{1})
and analogously,
the
j+2
nd coordinate of
t_{2}
, which is
the tuple
\Lambda_{1}(y_{2})
, contains the value
f_{j}(y_{2})
). Similarly,

\mathbf{M}\models P_{i}(x_{1})
if and only if the coordinate
a+3+i
of

s_{1}
contains
1
. We use the
0
in coordinate
a+3
to test this, by
checking if the coordinate
a+3
of
s_{1}
is equal to the coordinate
a+3+i

of
s_{1}
.

The set system defined by
q
on
\mathbf{M}
is the neighborhood set system of an induced subgraph of the bipartite graph defined on
X
(as
a subset of
\mathbb{N}
) by
\bar{s}
adjacent to
\bar{t}
if
X\models Q(\bar{s};\bar{t})
.

Conversely, assume that
\varphi(\bar{x};\bar{y})
is a first-order formula (in the empty language). We may assume
|\bar{x}|=|\bar{y}|=d>1
, as we can repeat a coordinate and pad to get
|\bar{x}|=|\bar{y}|>1
if needed. We may also assume that
\varphi
is quantifier free (as the theory of infinite sets has quantifier elimination, see
[
21
]
). Let
G
be a
finite induced subgraph of the graph defined by
\varphi
on
\mathbb{N}
. Let
A

be the set of vertices of

, which is a finite subset of
\mathbb{N}^{d}
.
Let
\mathbf{M}
be the pointer structure with domain

, where

is the set of all elements of
\mathbb{N}
appearing in a tuple
of

, and where

is defined by:

if
\bar{v}\in A

if

An important application of the notion of discrepancy is that of an

\left|\frac{|A\cap S|}{|A|}-\frac{|S|}{|U|}\right|\leq\varepsilon

Upper bounds for the sizes of

Matoušek, Welzl, and Wernisch proved

In particular, every set system

-approximation

9
Concluding Remarks

As mentioned in
Section

5.2
, the set systems definable by a quantifier-free formula in pointer structures are basically the same as the neighborhood set systems of set-defined graphs.

A
k
-copy
of a graph
G
is the graph obtained from
k
copies of
G
by adding an edge between any two clone vertices.
A
simple (first-order) interpretation

\mathsf{I}
is a pair of formulas
(\eta(x,y),\nu(x))
; it maps a (possibly colored) graph
G
into the graph
\mathsf{I}(G)
with vertex set
\nu(G)=\{v\in G\colon G\models\nu(v)\}
and edge set
\eta(G)\cap\nu(G)^{2}=\{\{u,v\}\in\nu(G)^{2}\colon G\models\eta(u,v)\vee\eta(v,u)\}
(where the formulas
\eta
and
\nu
can use the colors).
A
(first-order) transduction

\mathsf{T}
is a pair
(k,\mathsf{I})
, where
k
is an integer and
\mathsf{I}
is a simple interpretation; it maps a graph
G
to the class of all graphs that can be obtained from
G
by computing a
k
-copy, then coloring this
k
-copy in an arbitrary way, then applying the transduction
\mathsf{I}
. We refer the interested reader to
[
16
,
31
]
for a discussion on first-order transductions.

For a monotone class
\mathscr{C}
, it follows from
[
22
]
and
Theorem

6.2
that the four following properties are equivalent:

1.

\mathscr{C}
has bounded expansion;

2.

every transduction of
\mathscr{C}
is set-defined;

3.

every transduction of
\mathscr{C}
is linearly
\chi
-bounded;

4.

every set system definable in
\mathscr{C}
has bounded hereditary discrepancy.

The equivalence between the first three items is known to hold for biclique-free classes of graphs.
We conjecture that this extends to the equivalence with the last item.

Conjecture 6
.

A biclique-free class of graphs has bounded expansion if and only if every set system definable in
\mathscr{C}
has bounded hereditary discrepancy.

An intriguing related problem is the following, for which a positive answer would imply
6
.

Problem 7
.

Is it true that for every non-degenerate biclique-free class
\mathscr{C}
of graphs there exists a transduction
\mathsf{T}
such that

\mathsf{T}(\mathscr{C})
includes a monotone non-degenerate class of graphs?

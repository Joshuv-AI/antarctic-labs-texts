Analytical computation of boundary integrals for the Helmholtz equation in three dimensions
Nail A. Gumerov, Ramani Duraiswami

Abstract
A key issue in the solution of partial differential equations via integral equation methods is the evaluation of possibly singular integrals involving the Green’s function and its derivatives multiplied by simple functions over discretized representations of the boundary. For the Helmholtz equation, while many authors use numerical quadrature to evaluate these boundary integrals, we present analytical expressions for such integrals over flat polygons in the form of infinite series. These can be efficiently truncated based on the accurate error bounds, which is key to their integration in methods such as the Fast Multipole Method.

Given surface
S

\displaystyle L_{k}\left[\sigma\right]\left(\mathbf{r}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{S}\sigma\left(\mathbf{r}^{\prime}\right)G_{k}\left(\mathbf{r},\mathbf{r}^{\prime}\right)dS\left(\mathbf{r}^{\prime}\right),

(1)

\displaystyle M_{k}\left[\mu\right]\left(\mathbf{r}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{S}\mu\left(\mathbf{r}^{\prime}\right)\frac{\partial G_{k}\left(\mathbf{r},\mathbf{r}^{\prime}\right)}{\partial n\left(\mathbf{r}^{\prime}\right)}dS\left(\mathbf{r}^{\prime}\right),

\displaystyle\mathbf{L}_{k}^{\prime}\left[\sigma\right]\left(\mathbf{r}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\nabla\int_{S}\sigma\left(\mathbf{r}^{\prime}\right)G_{k}\left(\mathbf{r},\mathbf{r}^{\prime}\right)dS\left(\mathbf{r}^{\prime}\right),

\displaystyle\mathbf{M}_{k}^{\prime}\left[\mu\right]\left(\mathbf{r}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\nabla\int_{S}\mu\left(\mathbf{r}^{\prime}\right)\frac{\partial G_{k}\left(\mathbf{r},\mathbf{r}^{\prime}\right)}{\partial n\left(\mathbf{r}^{\prime}\right)}dS\left(\mathbf{r}^{\prime}\right).

where
\sigma
and
\mu
are the single and double layer densities,
\mathbf{n}
is the normal to the surface, and
G_{k}\left(\mathbf{r},\mathbf{r}^{\prime}\right)
is the free-space Green function for given wavenumber

G_{k}\left(\mathbf{r},\mathbf{r}^{\prime}\right)=\frac{e^{ik\left|\mathbf{r}-\mathbf{r}^{\prime}\right|}}{4\pi\left|\mathbf{r}-\mathbf{r}^{\prime}\right|}.

(2)

1.1
Computation of boundary integrals

In center panel collocation with the boundary discretized by triangles, the integrals are approximated by
N
integrals over flat patches (e.g.,
triangles
\Delta_{l}
) centered at
\mathbf{r}_{l}^{(c)}
,

\displaystyle L_{k}\left[\sigma\right]\left(\mathbf{r}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{l^{\prime}=1}^{N}\int_{\Delta_{l^{\prime}}}\sigma\left(\mathbf{r}^{\prime}\right)G_{k}\left(\mathbf{r},\mathbf{r}^{\prime}\right)dS\left(\mathbf{r}^{\prime}\right)\approx\sum_{l^{\prime}=1}^{N}L_{ll^{\prime}}\sigma_{l^{\prime}},

(3)

\displaystyle\sigma_{l^{\prime}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sigma\left(\mathbf{r}_{l^{\prime}}^{(c)}\right),\quad L_{ll^{\prime}}=\int_{\Delta_{l^{\prime}}}G_{k}\left(\mathbf{r},\mathbf{r}^{\prime}\right)dS\left(\mathbf{r}^{\prime}\right).

Similar expressions can be written for other boundary integrals. For a flat
patch
\Delta
with vertices (
\mathbf{r}_{1},\mathbf{...},\mathbf{r}_{n})

defining the polygon as a right-hand oriented contour
C
with normal
\mathbf{n}
(see Fig.
1
), we need to compute the integrals

\displaystyle L_{k\Delta}\left(\mathbf{r}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\Delta}G_{k}\left(\mathbf{r,r}^{\prime}\right)dS\left(\mathbf{r}^{\prime}\right),\quad M_{k\Delta}\left(\mathbf{r}\right)=\int_{\Delta}\mathbf{n}\cdot\nabla_{\mathbf{r}^{\prime}}G_{k}\left(\mathbf{r}^{\prime}\mathbf{,r}\right)dS\left(\mathbf{r}^{\prime}\right),

(4)

\displaystyle\mathbf{L}_{k\Delta}^{\prime}\left(\mathbf{r}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\nabla_{\mathbf{r}}\int_{\Delta}G_{k}\left(\mathbf{r}^{\prime}\mathbf{,r}\right)dS\left(\mathbf{r}^{\prime}\right),\quad\mathbf{M}_{k\Delta}^{\prime}\left(\mathbf{r}\right)=\nabla_{\mathbf{r}}\int_{\Delta}\mathbf{n}\cdot\nabla_{\mathbf{r}^{\prime}}G_{k}\left(\mathbf{r}^{\prime}\mathbf{,r}\right)dS\left(\mathbf{r}^{\prime}\right).

Note that

\displaystyle G_{k}\left(\mathbf{r,r}^{\prime}\right)=\nabla_{s}^{\prime}\cdot\left[\mathbf{\rho}f_{k}\left(\rho;h\right)\right],\quad f_{k}\left(\rho;h\right)=\frac{e^{ikr}-e^{ik\left|h\right|}}{4\pi ik\rho^{2}},

(5)

\displaystyle h=\mathbf{n\cdot r,\hskip 11.49994pt\rho=r}^{\prime}-\mathbf{r+n}h,\quad r=\left|\mathbf{r}-\mathbf{r}^{\prime}\right|=\sqrt{\rho^{2}+h^{2}},\quad\rho=\left|\mathbf{\rho}\right|,

where
\nabla_{s}^{\prime}
is the surface gradient operator with respect
to
\mathbf{r}^{\prime}
and
h
is the projection of
\mathbf{r}
to the
normal. Using the Gauss divergence theorem, we obtain

L_{k\Delta}\left(\mathbf{r}\right)=\sum_{j=1}^{n}I_{j},\quad I_{j}=\int_{C_{j}}\left(\mathbf{n}_{j}^{\prime}\cdot\mathbf{\rho}\right)f_{k}\left(\rho;h\right)dC,

(6)

where
C_{j}
are line segments constituting the triangle sides and
\mathbf{n}_{j}^{\prime}\mathbf{\ }
are the normals to these segments outward to
the triangle. Expressions for
I_{j}
can be simplified to

\displaystyle I_{j}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle H\left(l_{j}-x^{\prime},y^{\prime},z^{\prime}\right)-H\left(-x^{\prime},y^{\prime},z^{\prime}\right),\quad j=1,2,3,

(7)

\displaystyle H\left(x,y^{\prime},z^{\prime}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-z^{\prime}\int f\left(\rho;y^{\prime}\right)dx=\frac{-z^{\prime}}{4\pi ik}\int\frac{e^{ikr}-e^{iky^{\prime}}}{x^{2}+z^{\prime 2}}dx,\quad r=\sqrt{x^{2}+y^{\prime 2}+z^{\prime 2}},\quad y^{\prime}=\left|h\right|,

where
l_{j}
is the length of segment
C_{j}
,
\left(x^{\prime},y^{\prime},z^{\prime}\right)
are the coordinates of the evaluation
point
\mathbf{r}
in the local right-hand oriented Euclidean reference
frame centered at the triangle vertex, which
x
-axis (basis vector
\mathbf{i}_{x}^{\prime}
) is directed along the segment, and the other basis
vectors are
\mathbf{i}_{y}^{\prime}=\mathbf{n}
,
\mathbf{i}_{z}^{\prime}=\mathbf{n}_{j}^{\prime}
. Expressions for the surface integrals can be
obtained by summation of the line integrals:

\displaystyle L_{k\Delta}\left(\mathbf{r}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{n}I_{j}\left(x_{j}^{\prime},h,z_{j}^{\prime}\right),\quad I_{j}\left(x^{\prime},y^{\prime},z^{\prime}\right)=H\left(l_{j}-x^{\prime},y^{\prime},z^{\prime}\right)-H\left(-x^{\prime},y^{\prime},z^{\prime}\right),

(8)

\displaystyle\mathbf{L}_{k\Delta}^{\prime}\left(\mathbf{r}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{n}\mathbf{I}_{j}^{\prime}\left(x_{j}^{\prime},h,z_{j}^{\prime}\right),\quad\mathbf{I}_{j}^{\prime}\left(x^{\prime},y^{\prime},z^{\prime}\right)=\mathbf{H}^{\prime}\left(l_{j}-x^{\prime},y^{\prime},z^{\prime}\right)-\mathbf{H}^{\prime}\left(-x^{\prime},y^{\prime},z^{\prime}\right),\quad

\displaystyle M_{k\Delta}\left(\mathbf{r}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{n}J_{j}\left(x_{j}^{\prime},h,z_{j}^{\prime}\right),\quad J_{j}\left(x^{\prime},y^{\prime},z^{\prime}\right)=K\left(l_{j}-x^{\prime},y^{\prime},z^{\prime}\right)-K\left(-x^{\prime},y^{\prime},z^{\prime}\right),

\displaystyle\mathbf{M}_{k\Delta}^{\prime}\left(\mathbf{r}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{n}\mathbf{J}_{j}^{\prime}\left(x_{j}^{\prime},h,z_{j}^{\prime}\right),\quad\mathbf{J}_{j}^{\prime}\left(x^{\prime},y^{\prime},z^{\prime}\right)=\mathbf{K}^{\prime}\left(l_{j}-x^{\prime},y^{\prime},z^{\prime}\right)-\mathbf{K}^{\prime}\left(-x^{\prime},y^{\prime},z^{\prime}\right),

where

\mathbf{H}^{\prime}=-\mathbf{i}_{x}^{\prime}\frac{\partial H}{\partial x}+\mathbf{i}_{y}^{\prime}\frac{\partial H}{\partial y^{\prime}}+\mathbf{i}_{z}^{\prime}\frac{\partial H}{\partial z^{\prime}}\mathbf{,\hskip 11.49994pt}K=-\frac{\partial H}{\partial y^{\prime}},\quad\mathbf{K}^{\prime}=\mathbf{i}_{x}^{\prime}\frac{\partial^{2}H}{\partial x\partial y^{\prime}}-\mathbf{i}_{y}^{\prime}\frac{\partial^{2}H}{\partial y^{\prime 2}}-\mathbf{i}_{z}^{\prime}\frac{\partial^{2}H}{\partial z^{\prime}\partial y^{\prime}}.

(9)

The above expressions are obtained for
h\geqslant 0
. For negative
h
the
following symmetry relations can be applied

\displaystyle I_{j}\left(x^{\prime},-y^{\prime},z^{\prime}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle H\left(l_{j}-x^{\prime},y^{\prime},z^{\prime}\right)-H\left(-x^{\prime},y^{\prime},z^{\prime}\right),

(10)

\displaystyle\mathbf{I}_{j}^{\prime}\left(x^{\prime},-y^{\prime},z^{\prime}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\widehat{\mathbf{H}}^{\prime}\left(l_{j}-x^{\prime},y^{\prime},z^{\prime}\right)-\widehat{\mathbf{H}}^{\prime}\left(-x^{\prime},y^{\prime},z^{\prime}\right),\quad

\displaystyle J_{j}\left(x^{\prime},-y^{\prime},z^{\prime}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\widehat{K}\left(l_{j}-x^{\prime},y^{\prime},z^{\prime}\right)-\widehat{K}\left(-x^{\prime},y^{\prime},z^{\prime}\right),\quad

\displaystyle\mathbf{J}_{j}^{\prime}\left(x^{\prime},-y^{\prime},z^{\prime}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\widehat{\mathbf{K}}^{\prime}\left(l_{j}-x^{\prime},y^{\prime},z^{\prime}\right)-\widehat{\mathbf{K}}^{\prime}\left(-x^{\prime},y^{\prime},z^{\prime}\right),\quad

where

\widehat{\mathbf{H}}^{\prime}=-\mathbf{i}_{x}^{\prime}\frac{\partial H}{\partial x}-\mathbf{i}_{y}^{\prime}\frac{\partial H}{\partial y^{\prime}}+\mathbf{i}_{z}^{\prime}\frac{\partial H}{\partial z^{\prime}}\mathbf{,\hskip 11.49994pt}\widehat{K}=\frac{\partial H}{\partial y^{\prime}},\quad\widehat{\mathbf{K}}^{\prime}=-\mathbf{i}_{x}^{\prime}\frac{\partial^{2}H}{\partial x\partial y^{\prime}}-\mathbf{i}_{y}^{\prime}\frac{\partial^{2}H}{\partial y^{\prime 2}}+\mathbf{i}_{z}^{\prime}\frac{\partial^{2}H}{\partial z^{\prime}\partial y^{\prime}}.

(11)

1.1.1
Primitives

The use of the BEM with center panel collocation presumes that the triangle
size is much smaller than the wavelength, so
k\left|\mathbf{r}-\mathbf{r}_{j}\right|\ll\pi.
The triangle inequality then shows

k\left|r-r_{0}\right|=k\left|\left|\mathbf{y}-\mathbf{x}\right|-\left|\mathbf{y}-\mathbf{x}_{j}\right|\right|\leqslant k\left|\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{x}_{j}\right|\ll\pi,\quad r_{0}=\sqrt{x^{\prime 2}+y^{\prime 2}+z^{\prime 2}}.

(12)

For any integration point, we can use the following truncated expansion of
the exponent

e^{ikr}=e^{ikr_{0}}e^{ik\left(r-r_{0}\right)}=e^{ikr_{0}}\sum_{m=0}^{p-1}\frac{\left(ik\right)^{m}}{m!}\left(r-r_{0}\right)^{m}+O\left(\frac{1}{p!}\max\left(k^{p}\left|r-r_{0}\right|^{p}\right)\right).

(13)

Using the binomial formula for
\left(r-r_{0}\right)^{m}
, substituting
expansion into Eq. (
7
), and neglecting the truncation error term, we
obtain

H\left(x,y^{\prime},z^{\prime}\right)=\frac{1}{4\pi ik}\left[e^{iky^{\prime}}k_{0}\left(x,y^{\prime},z^{\prime}\right)-e^{ikr_{0}}\sum_{l=0}^{p-1}A_{l}^{(p)}k_{l}\left(x,y^{\prime},z^{\prime}\right)\right],

(14)

A_{l}^{(p)}=\frac{\left(ik\right)^{l}}{l!}a_{p-l}\left(-ikr_{0}\right),\quad a_{l}\left(\xi\right)=\sum_{m=0}^{l-1}\frac{\xi^{m}}{m!}.\quad l=0,...,p-1,

(15)

and the functions

k_{m}\left(x,y^{\prime},z^{\prime}\right)=z^{\prime}\int\frac{r^{m}}{x^{2}+z^{\prime 2}}dx,\quad r=\sqrt{x^{2}+y^{\prime 2}+z^{\prime 2}},\quad m=0,\pm 1,\pm 2,...,

(16)

can be recursively computed as described in the Appendix.

Computation of the derivatives of the single and double layer potentials
requires derivatives of the primitives with respect to any of their three
arguments. This can be done using a similar method, with the only notice,
that, first, the derivative should be computed using integral form (
7
)
and then truncated expansion (
13
) should be inserted there. Dropping
these derivations we present only the final results, which can be written as
follows (for all functions arguments are
\left(x,y^{\prime},z^{\prime}\right)
).

\displaystyle H

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{ik}\left(R-S\right),\quad\frac{\partial H}{\partial x}=-z^{\prime}T,\quad\frac{\partial H}{\partial y^{\prime}}=R-V,\quad\frac{\partial H}{\partial z^{\prime}}=xT-U,

(17)

\displaystyle\frac{\partial^{2}H}{\partial x\partial y^{\prime}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-z^{\prime}P,\quad\frac{\partial^{2}H}{\partial y^{\prime 2}}=-k^{2}H+z^{\prime}Q,\quad\frac{\partial^{2}H}{\partial z^{\prime}\partial y^{\prime}}=xP-y^{\prime}Q,

\displaystyle P

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{4\pi}\frac{1}{x^{2}+z^{\prime 2}}\left(\frac{y^{\prime}}{r}e^{ikr}-e^{iky^{\prime}}\right),\quad R=\frac{1}{4\pi}e^{iky^{\prime}}k_{0},\quad T=\frac{1}{4\pi ik}\frac{e^{ikr}-e^{iky^{\prime}}}{x^{2}+z^{\prime 2}},

(18)

\displaystyle S

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{4\pi}e^{ikr_{0}}\sum_{l=0}^{p-1}A_{l}^{(p)}k_{l},\quad U=\frac{1}{4\pi}e^{ikr_{0}}\sum_{l=0}^{p-1}A_{l}^{(p)}i_{l-1},\quad V=\frac{y^{\prime}}{4\pi}e^{ikr_{0}}\sum_{l=0}^{p-1}A_{l}^{(p)}k_{l-1},

\displaystyle Q

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{4\pi}e^{ikr_{0}}\sum_{l=0}^{p-1}A_{l}^{(p)}\left(iki_{l-2}-i_{l-3}\right),\quad

where functions

i_{m}\left(x;a\right)=\int r^{m}dx,\quad r=\sqrt{x^{2}+a^{2}},\quad a^{2}=y^{\prime 2}+z^{\prime 2},\quad m=0,\pm 1,\pm 2,...,

(19)

can be recursively computed as described in the Appendix.

We note now that in computation of primitives some cases with removable or
non-removable (strong) singularities can appear. Strong singularities appear
only for the cases when the evaluation point belongs to contour
C
, in
which case, indeed the hypersingular integral blows up (in fact,
cancellation of these singularities happens when summing up integrals over
neighbor elements; such situation is typical for evaluation of integrals
which exist in a sense of principal Cauchy value, but blow up if the
integration bounds coincide with singular points). However, this case is
never encountered in the center panel collocation method, as the center of
the element never belongs to its edge. Field points also are non-singular
since they do not belong to the boundary. However, such singularities should
be avoided in evaluation of spatial gradients of the double layer potential
(e.g. evaluation point should not approach the boundary closer than some
small distance consistent with the flat constant panel discretization errors.

Note that all singularities occur only if
z^{\prime}=0
. In this case,
however,

H=\frac{\partial H}{\partial x}=\frac{\partial H}{\partial y^{\prime}}=\frac{\partial^{2}H}{\partial x\partial y^{\prime}}=\frac{\partial^{2}H}{\partial y^{\prime 2}}=0,

(20)

so these functions should be set to zero immediately. Other derivatives are
regular and computable if
x\neq 0
and
y^{\prime}\neq 0.
Strong singular
case corresponds to
y^{\prime}=0
and
x=0
, which should be avoided as
the program should avoid computation of integrals at edge evaluation points
where
y^{\prime}=0
and
x=0
appears on the integration path. Formulae
for limiting cases
x=0
and
y\neq 0
and
x\neq 0
and
y=0
can be found
straightforward from the above analytical expressions.

In case
k\rightarrow 0
the Green function of the Helmholtz equation
smoothly transits to the Green function of the Laplace equation. In this
case the series related toe the expansions of the exponents disappear and
all primitives can be expressed via elementary functions:

\displaystyle H

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{y^{\prime}k_{0}+k_{1}}{4\pi},\quad\frac{\partial H}{\partial x}=-\frac{z^{\prime}}{4\pi}\frac{r-y^{\prime}}{x^{2}+z^{\prime 2}},\quad\frac{\partial H}{\partial y^{\prime}}=\frac{k_{0}-y^{\prime}k_{-1}}{4\pi},

(21)

\displaystyle\frac{\partial H}{\partial z^{\prime}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{x}{4\pi}\frac{r-y^{\prime}}{x^{2}+z^{\prime 2}}-\frac{i_{-1}}{4\pi},\quad\frac{\partial^{2}H}{\partial x\partial y^{\prime}}=-\frac{z^{\prime}}{4\pi}\frac{1}{\left(x^{2}+z^{\prime 2}\right)}\left(\frac{y^{\prime}}{r}-1\right),

\displaystyle\frac{\partial^{2}H}{\partial y^{\prime 2}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{z^{\prime}i_{-3}}{4\pi},\quad\frac{\partial^{2}H}{\partial z^{\prime}\partial y^{\prime}}=\frac{x}{4\pi}\frac{1}{\left(x^{2}+z^{\prime 2}\right)}\left(\frac{y^{\prime}}{r}-1\right)+\frac{y^{\prime}i_{-3}}{4\pi}.

These expressions are also useful for understanding of the singularities in
general case
k\neq 0
, as these singularities are caused by singularities
of the Laplacian kernel can be efficiently computed as described in the
Appendix.

Appendix A
Elementary integral computations

A.1
Computation of integrals
i_{m}

In this appendix we show how integrals

i_{m}\left(x;a\right)=\int r^{m}dx,\quad r=\sqrt{x^{2}+a^{2}},\quad a^{2}=y^{\prime 2}+z^{\prime 2},\quad m=0,\pm 1,\pm 2,...

(22)

can be computed analytically. For small values of even and odd
m
these
integrals can be computed

\displaystyle i_{0}\left(x;a\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int dx=x,\quad i_{-1}\left(x;a\right)=\int r^{-1}dx=\ln\left|r+x\right|,

(23)

\displaystyle i_{-2}\left(x;a\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int r^{-2}dx=\frac{1}{a}\arctan\frac{x}{a},\quad i_{-3}\left(x;a\right)=\int r^{-3}dx=\frac{x}{a^{2}r}.

We have the recurrence:

\displaystyle i_{m+2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int r^{m+2}dx=xr^{m+2}-\left(m+2\right)\int x^{2}r^{m}dx

(24)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle xr^{m+2}-\left(m+2\right)i_{m+2}+(m+2)a^{2}i_{m}.

i_{m+2}=\frac{xr^{m+2}}{m+3}+\frac{m+2}{m+3}a^{2}i_{m},\quad m\neq-3.

(25)

The special case
m=-3
is not so important, since we know for
i_{-1}\left(x;a\right).
We need integral values for
m\geqslant-3
, and have explicit
expressions for all required non-positive
m
and we can recursively find
all positive
m
starting the recurrence from
m=0
for even
m
and from
m=-1
for odd
m
. For even
m=2n
we can evaluate the integrals as series
explicitly using the binomial expansion

\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!i_{2n}\left(x;a\right)=\int r^{2n}dx=\int\left(x^{2}+a^{2}\right)^{n}dx=\sum_{l=0}^{n}\frac{n!}{l!(n-l)!}a^{2(n-l)}\int x^{2l}dx=\sum_{l=0}^{n}\frac{n!a^{2(n-l)}x^{2l+1}}{l!(n-l)!\left(2l+1\right)}.

(26)

For odd
m=2n+1
we have the recurrence

i_{2n+1}=\frac{1}{2n+2}\left[xr^{2n+1}+\left(2n+1\right)a^{2}i_{2n-1}\right].

(27)

Expanding this down to
i_{-1}
we obtain

i_{2n+1}\left(x;a\right)=x\sum_{l=0}^{n}\frac{2^{2l}\left(2n+2\right)!(l!)^{2}}{2^{2n+2}\left(2l+1\right)!\left[\left(n+1\right)!\right]^{2}}r^{2l+1}a^{2(n-l)}+\frac{\left(2n+2\right)!}{2^{2n+2}\left[\left(n+1\right)!\right]^{2}}a^{2n+2}\ln\left|r+x\right|.

(28)

Note that primitives
i_{m}\left(x;a\right)
may have singularities only
if
a=0.
In such cases we have

i_{m}\left(x;0\right)=\frac{x\left|x\right|^{m}}{m+1},\quad m\neq-1,\quad i_{-1}\left(x;0\right)=\mbox{sgn}(x)\ln\left|x\right|.

(29)

This shows that all functions with negative index are singular at
x=0
and
this should be accounted for in formulae involving them.

A.2
Computation of integrals
k_{m}

In this appendix we show how integrals

k_{m}\left(x;y^{\prime},z^{\prime}\right)=z^{\prime}\int\frac{r^{m}}{x^{2}+z^{\prime 2}}dx,\quad r=\sqrt{x^{2}+y^{\prime 2}+z^{\prime 2}},\quad m=0,\pm 1,\pm 2,...

(30)

can be computed analytically. Note, that for
y^{\prime}=0
these reduce to
the integrals
i_{m}
:

k_{m}\left(x;0,z^{\prime}\right)=z^{\prime}\int\frac{r^{m}}{r^{2}}dx=z^{\prime}i_{m-2}\left(x;\left|z\right|\right).

(31)

So we only need consider the case
y^{\prime}\neq 0.
We derive recurrences
for all needed
k_{m}
and find initial values to use them. We have

\displaystyle k_{m+2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle z^{\prime}\int\frac{r^{m+2}}{x^{2}+z^{\prime 2}}dx=z^{\prime}\int\frac{\left(x^{2}+z^{\prime 2}\right)+y^{\prime 2}}{x^{2}+z^{\prime 2}}\rho^{m}dx

(32)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle z^{\prime}i_{m}\left(x;\sqrt{y^{\prime 2}+z^{\prime 2}}\right)+y^{\prime 2}k_{m}.

With known expressions for the initial values (odd and even), all integrals
can be computed recursively. For this we have

\displaystyle k_{0}\left(x;y^{\prime},z^{\prime}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{z^{\prime}}{\left|z^{\prime}\right|}\arctan\frac{x}{\left|z^{\prime}\right|}=\mbox{sgn}(z^{\prime})\arctan\frac{x}{\left|z^{\prime}\right|}

(33)

\displaystyle k_{1}\left(x;y^{\prime},z^{\prime}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{y^{\prime}z^{\prime}}{\left|z^{\prime}\right|}\arctan\frac{y^{\prime}x}{\left|z^{\prime}\right|r}+z^{\prime}\ln\left|r+x\right|=y^{\prime}\mbox{sgn}(z^{\prime})\arctan\frac{y^{\prime}x}{\left|z^{\prime}\right|r}+z^{\prime}\ln\left|r+x\right|.

For the boundary integrals we need only one negative value
m=-1.
We can
find this value from the same recurrence and known
k_{1}:

\displaystyle k_{-1}\left(x;y^{\prime},z^{\prime}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{y^{\prime 2}}\left[k_{1}\left(x;y^{\prime},z^{\prime}\right)-z^{\prime}i_{-1}\left(x;\sqrt{y^{\prime 2}+z^{\prime 2}}\right)\right]

(34)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{z^{\prime}}{y^{\prime}\left|z^{\prime}\right|}\arctan\frac{y^{\prime}x}{\left|z^{\prime}\right|r}=\frac{\mbox{sgn}(z^{\prime})}{y^{\prime}}\arctan\frac{y^{\prime}x}{\left|z^{\prime}\right|r}.

Note that
y^{\prime}k_{-1}\left(x;y^{\prime},z^{\prime}\right)

entering the primitive expressions are not singular even when
z^{\prime}

and
y^{\prime}
approaches zero, while depend on the path (ratio
y^{\prime}/z^{\prime}
).

Comprehensive study of light axial vector mesons with the presence of triangle singularity
Meng-Chuan Du 1,2 , and Qiang Zhao 1,2

Abstract
We present a systematic study of the productions and decays of light axial vector mesons with J P ​ C = 1 + ⁣ ± J^{PC}=1^{+\pm} in charmonium decays. In the quark model scenario, the two axial vector nonets are connected with each other by the Gell-Mann-Okubo mass relation through the mixing between two K 1 K_{1} states (i.e. K 1 ​ ( 1270 ) K_{1}(1270) and K 1 ​ ( 1400 ) K_{1}(1400) ) with configurations of P 1 3 {}^{3}P_{1} and P 1 1 {}^{1}P_{1} . The mixing angles between f 1 f_{1} and f 1 ′ f_{1}^{\prime} (i.e. f 1 ​ ( 1285 ) f_{1}(1285) and f 1 ​ ( 1420 ) f_{1}(1420) ), and between h 1 h_{1} and h 1 ′ h_{1}^{\prime} (i.e. h 1 ​ ( 1170 ) h_{1}(1170) and h 1 ​ ( 1415 ) h_{1}(1415) ) can be reliably constrained. We then introduce the intermediate K ∗ ​ K ¯ + c . c . K^{*}\bar{K}+c.c. meson loop transitions in the description of the productions and decays of these axial vector mesons. The presence of the nearby S S -wave K ∗ ​ K ¯ + c . c . K^{*}\bar{K}+c.c. to which these axial vector mesons have strong coupling strengths, turns out to be crucial for understanding many puzzling questions related to their productions and decays. This is because that the S S -wave K ∗ ​ K ¯ + c . c . K^{*}\bar{K}+c.c. rescatterings by the kaon exchange satisfy the triangle singularity (TS) condition in some of these cases and the TS mechanism can introduce special interference effects in the exclusive decays of these light axial vector mesons.

Introduction

The constituent quark model has proved to be successful on the description of many qualitative features of low-lying hadrons. However, it also shows that it is challenging for us to provide a quantitative prescription for the hadron spectroscopy due to the complexity of non-perturbative QCD. A long-standing puzzling case is the properties of the lowest-lying positive parity vectors, namely, the axial vector mesons. With many unanswered mysterious questions on their productions and decays it seems that a consistent picture for the axial vector nonets as the first orbital excitation states in the light meson spectroscopy is still far from broadly accepted.

\displaystyle ig_{h_{1}\rho\pi}i^{2}g_{\rho\pi\pi}\epsilon_{\mu}\frac{i}{s_{bd}-m_{\rho}^{2}+im_{\rho}\Gamma_{\rho}}i[(-1+\frac{s_{b}-s_{d}}{s_{bd}})p_{b}^{\mu}+(1+\frac{s_{b}-s_{d}}{s_{bd}})p_{d}^{\mu}],

where the momenta of
\pi^{+}
,
\pi^{0}

\pi^{-}

Note that the following relation holds,

g_{h_{1}\rho\pi}\equiv\sqrt{2}g_{BVP}\sin{\alpha_{h}}.

(70)

It suggests that another relation is needed in order to determine

and

in the
1^{+-}
sector. In principle, with the data for

\displaystyle g_{h_{1}^{\prime}\rho\pi}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sqrt{2}g_{BVP}\cos{\alpha_{h}}=g_{h_{1}\rho\pi}\cot{\alpha_{h}}\ ,

(71)

\displaystyle g_{h_{1}^{\prime}K^{*}\bar{K}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle g_{BVP}\left(\frac{\sqrt{2}}{2}\cos{\alpha_{h}}-\sin{\alpha_{h}}\right)

(72)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}g_{h_{1}\rho\pi}(\cot{\alpha_{h}}-\sqrt{2})\ .

\Gamma_{h_{1}^{\prime}\to K^{*}\bar{K}\to K\bar{K}\pi}\simeq 6\Gamma_{h_{1}^{\prime}\to K^{*}\bar{K}\to K^{+}K^{-}\pi^{0}}=55.9\ \text{MeV}.

(74)

III
Production and decay of axial vector mesons with
J^{PC}=1^{++}

III.1
Key issues

In our previous study
Wu:2012pg
it was shown that the axial vector meson
f_{1}^{\prime}
should have contributions to the isospin-violating decay of
J/\psi\to\gamma+3\pi
which was measured by BESIII

. The observable of the angular distribution of the pion recoiling the

should contain a non-negligible
S
-wave contribution apart from the dominant
P
-wave from the intermediate
\eta
resonances. Different from the treatment of Ref.
Wu:2012pg
where an arbitrary coupling strength was determined by fitting the angular distribution, we will quantify the production and decay of both
f_{1}
and
f_{1}^{\prime}
in the
J/\psi
radiative decays into
\gamma\eta\pi\pi
,
\gamma K\bar{K}\pi
and
\gamma+3\pi
with the presence of the TS mechanism.

In Ref.
APFB-2017
a preliminary result was reported and it showed that the
f_{1}(1420)
as a pole structure should account for the
S
-wave enhancements observed in the invariant mass spectra of
\eta\pi\pi
and
K\bar{K}\pi
in
J/\psi\to\eta\pi\pi
and
K\bar{K}\pi

Debastiani:2016xgg
that the

enhancement was not a genuine state and it could be produced by the TS mechanism due to the
f_{1}(1285)
pole. This is an interesting scenario since it, on the one hand, shows that the TS mechanism plays an non-negligible role in various processes indeed. And on the other hand, it also raises questions on the structure of
f_{1}(1420)
. In Refs.
Roca:2005nm
;
Roca:2006tr
a systematic study of the
S
-wave vector and peudoscalar meson interactions led to dynamically generated pole structures which can be associated with the PDG listed states, i.e.
b_{1}(1235),\ h_{1}(1170),\ h_{1}(1380),\ a_{1}(1260)
, and
f_{1}(1285)
. It was also mentioned there that the
f_{1}(1420)
could not be accommodated in their scheme. In Ref.
Debastiani:2016xgg
the
f_{1}(1420)
was proposed to be an enhanced structure by the TS mechanism which was originated from the
f_{1}(1285)
as a dynamically generated state by the
K^{*}\bar{K}+c.c.

S
-wave interaction. The authors of Ref.
Debastiani:2016xgg
obtained the lineshape of the invariant mass by fitting the data from WA102
Barberis:1998by
with an arbitrary strength undetermined. Although this seems to be consistent with the result of Ref.
Roca:2005nm
, it would leave crucial questions in the understanding the overall axial vector spectrum. In particular, by treating
f_{1}(1285)
as a dynamically generated state due to the
S
-wave
K^{*}\bar{K}+c.c.
interaction would bring conflicts to some of those experimental observables for
f_{1}^{\prime}
which fit quite well the flavor singlet and octet mixing pattern
Barberis:1998by
;
Close:1997nm
;
Patrignani:2016xqp
.

To proceed, we first point out that the dominant decay mode of
f_{1}(1285)
is
\eta\pi\pi
with
B.R.(f_{1}\to\eta\pi\pi)=(52.4^{+1.9}_{-2.2})\%
, among which
B.R.(f_{1}\to a_{0}(980)\pi)=(36\pm 7)\%

Patrignani:2016xqp
. Note that the branching ratio
B.R.(f_{1}\to a_{0}(980)\pi)=(36\pm 7)\%
does not include the contributions from the
a_{0}(980)\to K\bar{K}

Patrignani:2016xqp
. It means that the real value should be larger than this and it explicitly indicates the dominant mode of
f_{1}\to a_{0}(980)\pi
. In contrast, the branching ratio for
f_{1}\to K\bar{K}\pi
is
B.R.(f_{1}\to K\bar{K}\pi)=(9.0\pm 0.4)\%
and the intermediate decay mode of
f_{1}\to K^{*}\bar{K}+c.c.
has not been observed. Although the phase space would limit the partial width of
f_{1}\to K^{*}\bar{K}+c.c.
,
it suggests that the
f_{1}
coupling to
K^{*}\bar{K}
does not necessarily to be strong. This actually jeopardizes the conclusion of Ref.
Debastiani:2016xgg
where it has been assumed that the
K^{*}\bar{K}+c.c.
decay channel had saturated the
K\bar{K}\pi
mode for
f_{1}(1285)
. Another caveat in Ref.
Debastiani:2016xgg
is that the invariant mass spectrum measured by WA102 cannot constrain the relative coupling strength for the production of
f_{1}
and
f_{1}^{\prime}
and this will be correlated with the couplings for their decays into exclusive channels (Note that their mixing angle is also a variable to be determined). These remind us of a coherent study of both
f_{1}
and
f_{1}^{\prime}
in the exclusive process of the
J/\psi
radiative decays where both the production and decay mechanisms can be properly evaluated.

For the coherent study of the axial vector mesons with
C=1^{++}
the following observations can be itemized:

•

The available experimental results suggest that the
f_{1}^{\prime}
dominantly decays into the
K\bar{K}\pi
final state via
K^{*}\bar{K}+c.c.
and its decays into
\eta\pi\pi
is much suppressed.

•

The
f_{1}
dominantly decays into
\eta\pi\pi
via the intermediate
a_{0}\pi
process and its decay into
K\bar{K}\pi
is also via the
a_{0}\pi
.

•

The mixing between
f_{1}
and
f_{1}^{\prime}
will affect both production and decay processes.

•

The
S
-wave coupling to
K^{*}\bar{K}+c.c.
introduces the effects from the TS mechanism which needs to be coherently investigated for both
f_{1}
and
f_{1}^{\prime}
in exclusive processes.

•

The
S
-wave coupling to

implies the TS enhancement in the isospin-1 channel which refers to the
a_{1}(1420)

III.2
Productions of the
C=+1
axial vector mesons in charmonium decays

The isoscalar
f_{1}
and
f_{1}^{\prime}
can be produced in the
J/\psi
radiative decays. In their three-body decays, e.g.
f_{1}\to\eta\pi\pi
and
K\bar{K}\pi
, both states can contribute. It thus requires a coherent study of these two states in each channel. We parametrize the production amplitudes using the following effective Lagrangian for
J/\psi\to\gamma A
(here,
A
denotes
f_{1}
or
f_{1}^{\prime}
):

\displaystyle L_{J/\psi\gamma A}=g_{A}\epsilon_{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}\partial^{\mu}\psi^{\nu}\gamma^{\rho}A^{\sigma}\ ,

(75)

which gives the amplitude

\displaystyle M_{J/\psi\gamma A}=g_{A}\epsilon_{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}p_{\psi}^{\mu}\epsilon_{\psi}^{\nu}\epsilon_{\gamma}^{\rho}\epsilon_{A}^{\sigma}\ ,

(76)

where
\epsilon_{\psi}^{\nu}
,
\epsilon_{\gamma}^{\rho}
, and
\epsilon_{A}^{\sigma}
are the polarization vectors for
J/\psi
, the photon and the axial vector meson, respectively. The production strength for
f_{1}
and
f_{1}^{\prime}
in the
J/\psi
radiative decays can be related to each other in the SU(3) flavor symmetry limit:

\displaystyle\frac{g_{f}^{\prime}}{g_{f}}=\frac{\langle f_{1}^{\prime}|\hat{H}_{\gamma}|J/\psi\rangle}{\langle f_{1}|\hat{H}_{\gamma}|J/\psi\rangle}=\frac{\sqrt{2}\cos{\alpha_{f}}-\sin{\alpha_{f}}}{\sqrt{2}\sin{\alpha_{f}}+\cos{\alpha_{f}}},

(77)

where we have assumed that
\langle(s\bar{s})_{1^{++}}|\hat{H}_{\gamma}|J/\psi\rangle=\langle(u\bar{u})_{1^{++}}|\hat{H}_{\gamma}|J/\psi\rangle=\langle(d\bar{d})_{1^{++}}|\hat{H}_{\gamma}|J/\psi\rangle
. Namely, we neglect the SU(3) symmetry breaking with the couplings for the light pair creations. Then, the amplitude of
J/\psi\to\gamma(f_{1}+f_{1}^{\prime})\to\gamma ABC
can be parametrized as

\displaystyle M_{J/\psi\to\gamma(f_{1}+f_{1}^{\prime})\to\gamma ABC}=M_{J/\psi\gamma f_{1}}\left(\frac{i}{D_{f_{1}}}M_{f_{1}\to ABC}+\frac{g_{f}^{\prime}}{g_{f}}\frac{i}{D_{f_{1}^{\prime}}}M_{f_{1}^{\prime}\to ABC}\right),

(78)

where
M_{f_{1}\to ABC}
and
M_{f_{1}^{\prime}\to ABC}
are the decay amplitudes for
f_{1}
and
f_{1}^{\prime}
, respectively, and will be explicitly calculated in the next subsection.

For the convenience of calculation, we define
M_{f_{1}^{\prime}\to ABC}=\sum_{i}M_{f_{1}^{\prime}\to ABC}^{(i)}
and
M_{f_{1}^{\prime}\to ABC}^{(i)}=\chi_{i}M_{f_{1}\to ABC}^{(i)}
, where
i
denotes the
i
-th intermediate state. Then, the spectrum can be expressed as the following form:

\displaystyle\frac{d\Gamma}{d\sqrt{s}}=\frac{2s}{\pi}\Gamma_{J/\psi\to\gamma f_{1}}\frac{1}{2\sqrt{s}}\int d\Phi_{ABC}\frac{1}{3}|\sum_{i}\left(\frac{i}{D_{f_{1}}}+\frac{\chi_{i}g_{f}^{\prime}}{g_{f}}\frac{i}{D_{f_{1}^{\prime}}}\right)M_{f_{1}\to ABC}^{(i)}|^{2},

(79)

where the
\Phi_{ABC}
is the phase space for the three-body
ABC
in
J/\psi\to\gamma ABC
; Functions
D_{f_{1}}
and
D_{f_{1}^{\prime}}
are the inverse propagators of
f_{1}
and
f_{1}^{\prime}
, respectively, i.e.

\displaystyle D_{f_{1}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle s-m_{f_{1}}^{2}+im_{f_{1}}\Gamma_{f_{1}},

(80)

\displaystyle D_{f_{1}^{\prime}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle s-m_{f_{1}^{\prime}}^{2}+im_{f_{1}^{\prime}}\Gamma_{f_{1}^{\prime}}(s),

(81)

where
\Gamma_{f_{1}}=22.7\ \text{MeV}
and
\Gamma_{f_{1}^{\prime}}=\Gamma_{f_{1}^{\prime}\to K\bar{K}\pi}+\Gamma_{f_{1}^{\prime}\to a_{0}\pi\to\eta\pi\pi}
, are adopted.

Different from the production of
f_{1}/f_{1}^{\prime}
the production of
a_{1}
in the
J/\psi
radiative decays will be suppressed due to the isospin. To produce
a_{1}
in the
J/\psi
radiative decays it needs to go through the final state photon radiation. This is relatively suppressed by the additional gluon exchange in
c\bar{c}
annihilations. In
e^{+}e^{-}
annihilations the ideal process to probe the production of

is via
\chi_{c1}\to\pi a_{1}(1260)/a_{1}(1420)\to 4\pi
, where
\chi_{c1}
can be copiously produced through
\psi(3686)\to\gamma\chi_{c1}
. For the production of

it can be described by the following Lagrangian:

L_{\chi AP}=ig_{a}\epsilon_{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}\partial^{\mu}\chi_{c1}^{\nu}\partial^{\rho}A^{\sigma}P\ ,

(82)

where
g_{a}
is the coupling strength for producing a pair of
(q\bar{q})_{1^{++}}
and
(q\bar{q})_{0^{-+}}
in the
\chi_{c1}

In principle,

can be determined by the partial decay width for

decays into final stable particles, e.g.

spectrum in the analysis which can be described as

\displaystyle\frac{d\Gamma}{d\sqrt{s}}\sim\frac{2s}{\pi}\frac{\Gamma_{a_{1}\to ABC}(s)}{(s-m_{a_{1}(1260)})^{2}+s\Gamma_{a_{1}}(s)},

(83)

where the

represents the energy-dependent width for

or
a_{1}^{-}\to f_{0}\pi^{-}\to\pi^{+}\pi^{-}\pi^{0}

The energy-dependent total width

is estimated by

\Gamma_{a_{1}}(s)\simeq\Gamma_{a_{1}\to\rho\pi\ (S-\text{wave})}/B.R.(a_{1}\to\rho\pi\ (S-\text{wave}))\ .

(84)

Note that both the total width and partial decay width

are far from well established for

. The amplitude of
b_{1}^{+}\to K^{+}\bar{K}^{*0}\to K^{+}K^{-}\pi^{+}
(Fig.
6
(a)) reads

M_{b_{1}^{+}\to K^{+}K^{-}\pi^{+}}^{tree}=ig_{b_{1}^{+}\bar{K}^{*0}K^{+}}ig_{\bar{K}^{0}K^{-}\pi^{+}}\epsilon_{b_{1}\mu}\frac{i(-g^{\mu\nu}+\frac{p_{ab}^{\mu}p_{ab}^{\nu}}{s_{ab}})}{D_{\bar{K}^{*0}}}i(p_{a}-p_{b})_{\nu},

(124)

where
p_{ab}=p_{a}+p_{b}
is the momentum of
\bar{K}^{*0}
.

Similarly, the amplitude of
b_{1}^{+}\to\phi\pi^{+}\to K^{+}K^{-}\pi^{+}
(Fig.
6
(b)) can be obtained

\displaystyle M_{b_{1}^{+}\to K^{+}K^{-}\pi^{+}}^{tri}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2ig_{b_{1}^{+}\bar{K}^{*0}K^{+}}ig_{\bar{K}^{*0}K^{-}\pi^{+}}ig_{\phi K^{+}K^{-}}ig_{\phi K^{+}K^{-}}\epsilon_{b_{1}\mu}\tilde{I}^{\mu\alpha}\frac{i(-g^{\alpha\beta}+\frac{p_{c}^{\alpha}p_{c}^{\beta}}{p_{c}^{2}})}{p_{c}^{2}-m_{\phi}^{2}+im_{\phi}\Gamma_{\phi}}i(p_{a}-p_{d})_{\beta}

(125)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle ig_{b_{1}^{+}\bar{K}^{*0}K^{+}}ig_{\bar{K}^{*0}K^{-}\pi^{+}}ig_{\phi K^{+}K^{-}}ig_{\phi K^{+}K^{-}}\epsilon_{b_{1}\mu}(\tilde{I}_{n}^{\mu\alpha}+\tilde{I}_{c}^{\mu\alpha})\frac{i(-g^{\alpha\beta}+\frac{p_{c}^{\alpha}p_{c}^{\beta}}{p_{c}^{2}})}{p_{c}^{2}-m_{\phi}^{2}+im_{\phi}\Gamma_{\phi}}i(p_{a}-p_{d})_{\beta}\ ,

where the momenta of the
\pi^{+}
,
\phi
,
K^{+}
and
K^{-}
mesons are labeled by
p_{b}
,
p_{c}
,
p_{a}
and
p_{d}
, respectively;
\tilde{I}_{\mu\alpha}
is the typical loop function for the triangle loop, i.e.

\displaystyle\tilde{I}^{\mu\alpha}\equiv-i\int\frac{d^{4}q}{(2\pi)^{4}}\frac{(-g^{\mu\nu}+\frac{q^{\mu}q^{\nu}}{q^{2}})(q-2p_{b})_{\nu}(p_{c}+2p_{b}-2q)_{\alpha}F(q^{2})}{(q-m_{1}^{2}+im_{1}\Gamma_{1})((q-p_{b})^{2}-m_{2}^{2})((p_{0}-q)^{2}-m_{3}^{2})},

(126)

which is a tensor integral and will be contracted by the polarization vectors of
b_{1}
and
\phi
. Considering the slightly different masses between the charged and neutral intermediate states due to the isospin symmetry breaking, we note the charged and neutral loop integral functions by
\tilde{I}_{c}^{\mu\alpha}
and
\tilde{I}_{n}^{\mu\alpha}
, respectively.

In these amplitudes the unknown coupling
g_{b_{1}^{+}\bar{K}^{*0}K^{+}}
can be calculated by two ways based on the SU(3) symmetry. On the one hand, it can be connected to the decay of
b_{1}^{+}\to\omega\pi^{+}
where
g_{b_{1}^{+}\omega\pi^{+}}
can be extracted by assuming that
b_{1}\to\omega\pi
exhausts its total width. The partial width for
b_{1}^{+}\to\omega\pi^{+}
reads

\Gamma(b_{1}^{+}\to\omega\pi^{+})=\frac{|\vec{p}_{\omega}|}{8\pi m_{b_{1}}^{2}}\frac{g_{b_{1}^{+}\omega\pi^{+}}^{2}}{3}(2+\frac{(m_{b_{1}}^{2}+m_{\omega}^{2}-m_{\pi}^{2})^{2}}{4m_{b_{1}}^{2}m_{\omega}^{2}}),

(127)

where the
|\vec{p}_{\omega}|
refers to the momentum of
\omega
in the rest frame of
b_{1}
.

On the other hand,
g_{b_{1}^{+}\bar{K}^{*0}K^{+}}
can be calculated by
g_{BVP}
, which is obtained by calculating
h_{1}\to\rho\pi
. In the SU(3) limit, these two methods should give the same result. The numerical results show that there is about
10\%
discrepancy between these two methods, which is acceptable. The discrepancy may results from the following aspects:

•

The SU(3) flavour symmetry only approximately holds.

•

The estimation based on
g_{h_{1}\rho\pi}
is correlated with the determination of the
h_{1}
-
h_{1}^{\prime}
mixing. Thus, the mixing angle may introduce uncertainties to the coupling constant.

•

The
b_{1}\to\omega\pi
process contains non-negligible
D
-wave contributions, which is not considered in the above estimation.

However, we regard that the
10\%
discrepancy is an indication of self-consistency in the determination of the
h_{1}
-
h_{1}^{\prime}
mixing angle. So we adopt
g_{BVP}
extracted from
h_{1}\to\rho\pi
in the final numerical calculations.

h_{1}^{\prime}\to\phi\pi\to K\bar{K}\pi

The triangle loop transition in Fig.
7
provides an isospin breaking mechanism for
h_{1}^{\prime}\to\phi\pi\to K\bar{K}\pi
. The loop integral has the same form as Eq. (
126
), except that the charged and neutral loops must cancel due to the isospin breaking. Similar to the case of the isospin-breaking decay of
f_{1}^{\prime}\to 3\pi
, the unequal masses between the intermediate charged and neutral
K
(or
K^{*}
) mesons will lead to residue amplitudes after the cancellation.

The production of
h_{1}^{\prime}
in
J/\psi\to h_{1}^{\prime}\eta
can be described the same way as that for
J/\psi\to b_{1}\pi
in the previous subsection. Here, we consider the decay of
h_{1}^{\prime}\to\phi\pi
for which the amplitude can be written as:

\displaystyle M_{h_{1}^{\prime}\to K^{+}K^{-}\pi^{0}}^{tri}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle ig_{h_{1}^{\prime}K^{+}\bar{K}^{*-}}ig_{\bar{K}^{*-}K^{-}\pi^{0}}ig_{\phi K^{+}K^{-}}ig_{\phi K^{+}K^{-}}\epsilon_{h_{1}^{\prime}\mu}(\tilde{I}_{n}^{\mu\alpha}-\tilde{I}_{c}^{\mu\alpha})\frac{i(-g^{\alpha\beta}+\frac{p_{c}^{\alpha}p_{c}^{\beta}}{p_{c}^{2}})}{p_{c}^{2}-m_{\phi}^{2}+im_{\phi}\Gamma_{\phi}}i(p_{a}-p_{d})_{\beta}\ ,

(128)

which is to be compared with Eq. (
125
) and the loop function,
\tilde{I}^{\mu\alpha}
is the same as Eq. (
126
). The inverse propagator for
h_{1}^{\prime}
is

D_{h_{1}^{\prime}}(s)=s-m_{h_{1}^{\prime}}^{2}+im_{h_{1}^{\prime}}\Gamma_{h_{1}^{\prime}}(s),

(129)

with

\Gamma_{h_{1}^{\prime}}(s)\simeq\Gamma_{h_{1}^{\prime}\to K^{*}\bar{K}\to K\bar{K}\pi}(s)\ ,

(130)

where we have assumed that the
K^{*}\bar{K}
channel is dominant in the
h_{1}^{\prime}
decays. As shown by Eq. (
74
), this is a reasonable approximation concerning the poor experimental status of
h_{1}
and
h_{1}^{\prime}
.

V.1.1
Parameters

The parameters in this study include the vertex coupling constants and a cut-off energy for the loop integrals.
For the positive
C
-parity channel the key parameters are
g_{f_{1}^{\prime}K^{*}\bar{K}}
and
g_{f_{1}^{\prime}a_{0}\pi}
. They will determine the relative strengths between the
K\bar{K}\pi
and
\eta\pi\pi
channel and the sum of these two partial widths will nearly saturate the total width. Also, by assuming that both couplings,
g_{f_{1}^{\prime}a_{0}\pi}
and
g_{a_{1}f_{0}\pi}
, are via the non-strange component in the wave functions of
f_{1}^{\prime}
and
a_{1}
, they can be expressed by the bare
g_{ASP}
coupling.

At this moment the experimental data do not allow a precise determination of these parameters. Our strategy for the parameter determinations is as follows: Since the
K\bar{K}\pi
channel is dominant in the
f_{1}^{\prime}
decay we assume that this channel accounts for a partial width of 50 MeV such that we can describe the available data best. This determines
g_{f_{1}^{\prime}K^{*}\bar{K}}=2.17
. In the SU(3) flavor symmetry the coupling
g_{f_{1}K^{*}\bar{K}}=1.24
and
g_{a_{1}K^{*}\bar{K}}=2.04
can then be determined. Notice that the couplings
g_{f_{0}K^{+}K^{-}}
and
g_{a_{0}K\bar{K}}=2.24
are relatively well established quantities, and
g_{K^{*}K\pi}=3.2
can be determined by experimental data for
K^{*}\to K\pi
. In Table

V.1.2
Partial decay widths

In Tables
4
and
5
we list the calculated partial decay widths for

and

, respectively, and compare them with the available data. For the decays of

, it shows that the partial decay widths for both states are consistent with the experimental measurements

. Some specific points can be learned:

•

The decay of
f_{1}\to\eta\pi\pi
is a dominant channel due to the large tree diagram contribution, i.e.
f_{1}\to a_{0}\pi\to\eta\pi\pi
. In contrast, the triangle loop contribution is larger than the tree process in
f_{1}^{\prime}\to\eta\pi\pi
. The reason is because of the different couplings in these two channels, i.e.
g_{f_{1}a_{0}\pi}>>g_{f_{1}^{\prime}a_{0}\pi}
and
g_{f_{1}K^{*}\bar{K}}<<g_{f_{1}^{\prime}K^{*}\bar{K}}
(see Table
3
).

•

The decay of
f_{1}\to K\bar{K}\pi
is suppressed by the phase space. In contrast, this is the dominant channel for
f_{1}^{\prime}
. The main contribution is from the tree diagram transition of
f_{1}^{\prime}\to K^{*}\bar{K}+c.c.\to K\bar{K}\pi
. Note that the mass of
f_{1}^{\prime}
is located within the kinematic of the TS condition. As a consequence, the contributions from the triangle diagram are larger than the tree process in the transitions involving
a_{0}\pi
. This also enhances the isospin-breaking effects in
f_{1}^{\prime}\to 3\pi
than the tree-level
a_{0}-f_{0}
mixing contribution.

•

The isospin-breaking decays, in
f_{1}
and
f_{1}^{\prime}\to 3\pi
, can be illustrated by the following ratios, respectively,

\displaystyle R_{f_{1}\to 3\pi}

\displaystyle\equiv

\displaystyle\frac{B.R.(f_{1}\to f_{0}\pi\to\pi^{+}\pi^{-}\pi^{0})}{B.R.(f_{1}\to a_{0}\pi\to\eta\pi^{0}\pi^{0})}=\frac{3\times 0.01}{9.7}=0.31\%\ ,

\displaystyle R_{f_{1}^{\prime}\to 3\pi}

\displaystyle\equiv

\displaystyle\frac{B.R.(f_{1}^{\prime}\to f_{0}\pi\to\pi^{+}\pi^{-}\pi^{0})}{B.R.(f_{1}^{\prime}\to a_{0}\pi\to\eta\pi^{0}\pi^{0})}=\frac{3\times 0.16}{10}=4.8\%.

(131)

-

mixing and contributions from the triangle diagram are compatible. Due to the low mass of

VI
Conclusions

In this work we present a comprehensive study of the
C=\pm 1
light axial vector mesons. We first discuss the mixing angles between
f_{1}
and
f_{1}^{\prime}
(i.e.
f_{1}(1285)
and
f_{1}(1420)
), and between
h_{1}
and
h_{1}^{\prime}
(i.e.
h_{1}(1170)
and
h_{1}(1415)
). These two angles can be related to the mixing between
K_{1}(1270)
and
K_{1}(1400)
through the Gell-Mann-Okubo mass relation. We then introduce the intermediate
K^{*}\bar{K}+c.c.
meson loop transitions in the description of the productions and decays of these axial vector mesons. The presence of the nearby
S
-wave
K^{*}\bar{K}+c.c.
to which these axial vector mesons have strong couplings, turns out to be crucial for understanding many puzzling questions related to their productions and decays. This is because that the
K^{*}\bar{K}+c.c.
rescatterings by the kaon exchange satisfy the triangle singularity condition. Therefore, the TS mechanism can introduce special interference effects in the exclusive decays of these light axial vector mesons.

In the
C=+1
sector the main decay channels of the axial vectors are
K\bar{K}\pi
and
\eta\pi\pi
for
f_{1}
and
f_{1}^{\prime}
, and
3\pi
for
a_{1}(1260)
. We show that a combined study of these processes by taking into account of the TS mechanism provides clear evidences for the assignment of
f_{1}
and
f_{1}^{\prime}
as the mixing states of the flavor singlet and octet. Although
f_{1}
can produce an enhancement around 1.4 GeV due to the TS mechanism, without the inclusion of
f_{1}^{\prime}
as a genuine state and the SU(3) partner of
f_{1}
, it is impossible to understand its productions and decays in both
K\bar{K}\pi
and
\eta\pi\pi
channels. We also show that the TS mechanism accounts for the
a_{1}(1420)
enhancement which is originated from the strong
a_{1}(1260)
coupling to the
K^{*}\bar{K}+c.c.
threshold. The same mechanism also accounts for the relatively large isospin breaking effects in
f_{1}^{\prime}\to 3\pi
except that the charged and neutral triangle loop amplitudes have a destructive interfering phase. The combined analysis of these channels provides a self-consistent check of the underlying dynamics.

In the
C=-1
sector the experimental information is still sparse. We choose to investigate two special processes, i.e.
J/\psi\to b_{1}\pi\to\phi\pi^{+}\pi^{-}
and
J/\psi\to h_{1}^{\prime}\eta\to\phi\pi\eta\to K\bar{K}\pi\eta
, in order to highlight the role played by the TS mechanism. In the first process the decay of
b_{1}\to\phi\pi
is the OZI-rule suppressed. Thus, the tree-level transition is highly suppressed. The triangle diagram actually provides an OZI-evading mechanism which will produce abnormal lineshapes in both
K\bar{K}
and
\phi\pi
invariant mass spectra. In particular, a clear enhancement at about 1.39 GeV is predicted in the
\phi\pi
invariant mass spectrum due to the TS mechanism. The second process is an isospin-breaking decay for
h_{1}^{\prime}\to\phi\pi
. It shows that the TS mechanism provides a leading source of the isospin-breaking effects. It is interesting to notice that whether or not to include the
h_{1}^{\prime}
pole does not cause drastic differences in the lineshapes of the
\phi\pi
invariant mass spectrum. However, the absolute values of the branching ratios in these two scenarios will be very different. Without the inclusion of the
h_{1}^{\prime}
pole, the branching ratio of
J/\psi\to K^{*}\bar{K}\eta+c.c.\to\phi\pi\eta
will be much smaller than that of
J/\psi\to h_{1}^{\prime}\eta\to\phi\pi\eta
. These predictions can be searched for in experiment at BESIII.

In brief, the productions and decays of these two sets of axial vector mesons have provided important evidences for the role played by the TS mechanism. This combined analysis has helped clarify some crucial issues concerning their identifications and classifications.

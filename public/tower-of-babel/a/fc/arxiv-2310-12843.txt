Local behavior of critical points of isotropic Gaussian random fields
Paul Marriott, Weinan Qi, Yi Shen

Abstract
In this paper we examine isotropic Gaussian random fields defined on ℝ N \mathbb{R}^{N} satisfying certain conditions. Specifically, we investigate the type of a critical point situated within a small vicinity of another critical point, with both points surpassing a given threshold. It is shown that the Hessian of the random field at such a critical point is equally likely to have a positive or negative determinant. Furthermore, as the threshold tends to infinity, almost all the critical points above the threshold are local maxima and the saddle points with index N − 1 N-1 . Consequently, we conclude that the closely paired critical points above a high threshold must comprise one local maximum and one saddle point with index N − 1 N-1 .

Introduction

Let
X=\{X(\bm{t}),\bm{t}\in\mathbb{R}^{N}\}
be an isotropic Gaussian random field defined on
R^{N},N=1,2,...
. The critical points of
X
are the points at which its gradient vanishes. They are naturally classified into different types such as local maxima, saddle points of different kinds, and local minima. This paper explores the local interactions between critical points for which the values of
X
exceed a threshold
u
. More specifically, we pose and address the following question:

When two critical points surpassing
u
are situated in close proximity to one another, what types can they belong to?

The motivation for studying the critical points of Gaussian random fields largely stems from random topology, an emerging area of probability that deals with the topological features of random objects, such as random sets or random graphs. Its application in statistics is known as topological data analysis, and has also garnered significant research interest. For an overview of this field, readers can refer to
[
2
]
or
[
10
]
. An important mathematical tool employed in these areas is Morse theory, which suggests that topological information related to the homology groups of a set can be derived from the critical points of a function defined on that set, provided that the function meets certain non-degeneracy conditions
[
9
]
. As a result, understanding the behavior of critical points, such as their locations and heights, proves highly beneficial.

It has long been believed that for a stationary Gaussian random field satisfying some mild conditions, the locations of critical points exceeding
u
will converge to a (homogeneous) Poisson point process as
u
tends to infinity
[
1
]
. Putting its proof aside, another issue when applying this so-called Poisson clumping heuristic is that in real-world scenarios, it is not feasible for
u
to truly tend to infinity. Consequently, it is often necessary to consider the deviation from the Poisson limit, which is due to the interactions between the critical points, as Poisson limit corresponds to the independent case. For example, if the covariance is positive and a critical point above a high threshold already exists at a particular location, the value of the random field will be elevated around that point, conditional on this event. This could intuitively increase the chance of a point in the affected area to be a critical point exceeding the threshold. This is what pushes us to understand the behavior of the critical points in close vicinity of another critical point surpassing a threshold.

The remaining sections of this paper are structured as follows. In Chapter
2
we introduce the basic settings and notations. Chapter
3
consists of a detailed analysis of the covariance of the random field and its second derivatives. Using these findings, Chapter
4
explores the behavior of two critical points when their distance approaches 0, and shows that in this case, the determinants of their Hessian matrices must have opposite signs. Chapter
5
further reveals that as the threshold
u
tends to infinity, only the local maxima and the saddle points with index
N-1
will remain. Therefore, a pair of closely situated critical points of a Gaussian random field above a high threshold predominantly comprises one local maximum and one saddle point with index
N-1
.

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{6}\rho^{(1)}(0)+\frac{5}{18}\rho^{(1)}(0)^{2}\rho^{(2)}(0)^{-2}\rho^{(3)}(0).

Proof.

Since the proof is long and mainly consists of heavy computation, we include it in Appendix
A
.
∎

From Lemma
2.5
, we see that the elements of
\bm{\Sigma}_{\bm{u}}(r)
are at least two times continuously differentiable in
r
at 0.
Intuitively, one may also expect that the diagonal matrix
\bm{\Lambda}_{\bm{u}}(r)
of its ordered eigenvalues and the corresponding matrix
\bm{P}_{\bm{u}}(r)
consisting of its eigenvectors to have the similar properties. In particular, when
\rho(x)
,
x\geq 0
is real analytic on a neighborhood of 0, we can simply follow the proof of Lemma
2.5
to show that
\bm{\Sigma}_{\bm{u}}(r)
is also real analytic on that neighborhood. Then by the main theorem in
[
8
]
, both the eigenvalues and the eigenvectors of
\bm{\Sigma}_{\bm{u}}(r)
can be parameterized real analytically on a neighborhood of 0. As for the ordering of these eigenvalues, by noting the fact that zeros of a real analytic function indexed by
\mathbb{R}
are isolated (see, for example, Corollary 1.2.5 in
[
7
]
), we can also show that
\bm{\Lambda}_{\bm{u}}(r)
and
\bm{P}_{\bm{u}}(r)
can be both real analytic on a neighborhood of 0.

However, the above result relies on the assumption that
\rho
is real analytic, which is not guaranteed even when
\rho
is infinitely differentiable. Thus, the following condition can be regarded as a generalization of real analyticity of
\rho
on a neighborhood of 0, such that both the ordered eigenvalues and the corresponding eigenvectors of
\bm{\Sigma}_{\bm{u}}(r)
can change smoothly enough under perturbation.

Definition 2.6
.

(Perturbation Condition)
Let
X
be qualified, and let
\bm{\Sigma}(\bm{t})
,
\bm{P}(\bm{t})
and
\bm{\Lambda}(\bm{t})
,
\bm{t}\in\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\{\bm{0}_{N}\}
be the matrices as defined above. Then
X
is said to be
qualified under perturbation
if there exists a version of
\bm{P}(\bm{t})
and
\bm{\Lambda}(\bm{t})
, such that
for any direction
\bm{u}=(u_{1},\dots,u_{N})^{T}\in\mathbb{S}^{N-1}
,

1.

both
\bm{P}_{\bm{u}}(0):=\lim_{r\downarrow 0}\bm{P}_{\bm{u}}(r)
and
\bm{\Lambda}_{\bm{u}}(0):=\lim_{r\downarrow 0}\bm{\Lambda}_{\bm{u}}(r)
exist;

2.

there exists a constant
\delta_{pc}\in(0,\delta_{\rho}]
such that
\bm{P}_{\bm{u}}(r)
and
\bm{\Lambda}_{\bm{u}}(r)
are both continuous on
r\in[0,\delta_{pc}]
;

3.

\bm{P}_{\bm{u}}(r)=\bm{P}_{\bm{u},0}+\bm{P}_{\bm{u},1}r+o(r)
as
r\to 0
for some
\bm{P}_{\bm{u},0}
,
\bm{P}_{\bm{u},1}\in\mathbb{R}^{L\times L}
;

4.

\bm{\Lambda}_{\bm{u}}(r)=\bm{\Lambda}_{\bm{u},0}+\bm{\Lambda}_{\bm{u},1}r+\bm{\Lambda}_{\bm{u},2}r^{2}+o(r^{2})
as
r\to 0
,
where
\bm{\Lambda}_{\bm{u},j}
,
j=0,1,2
are all
L\times L
real-valued diagonal matrices. More specifically, this is equivalent to

\lambda_{\bm{u},i}(r)=\lambda_{\bm{u},i,0}+\lambda_{\bm{u},i,1}r+\lambda_{\bm{u},i,2}r^{2}+o(r^{2}),

where
\lambda_{\bm{u},i,j}\in\mathbb{R}
for
i=1,\dots,L
and
j=0,1,2
, such that

\bm{\Lambda}_{\bm{u},0}:={\rm diag}\,(\lambda_{\bm{u},i,0},i=1,\dots,L)
,
\bm{\Lambda}_{\bm{u},1}:={\rm diag}\,(\lambda_{\bm{u},i,1},i=1,\dots,L)
, and
\bm{\Lambda}_{\bm{u},2}:={\rm diag}\,(\lambda_{\bm{u},i,2},i=1,\dots,L)
.

In the following sections,
if
X
is qualified under perturbation, then
\bm{P}(\bm{t})
and
\bm{\Lambda}(\bm{t})
are selected to be a version satisfying all the four conditions in Definition
2.6
by default.

Remark 2.7
.

Let
X
be qualified under perturbation and
\bm{u}\in\mathbb{S}^{N-1}
.
Then by Definition
2.6
,

\bm{A}_{\bm{u}}(r)=\bm{P}_{\bm{u}}(r)\bm{\Lambda}_{\bm{u}}^{1/2}(r)
,
r\geq 0
is also continuous on
r\in[0,\delta_{pc}]
and there exist
\bm{A}_{\bm{u},0},\bm{A}_{\bm{u},1/2},\bm{A}_{\bm{u},1}\in\mathbb{R}^{L\times L}
such that

\bm{A}_{\bm{u}}(r)=\bm{A}_{\bm{u},0}+\bm{A}_{\bm{u},1/2}r^{1/2}+\bm{A}_{\bm{u},1}r+o(r)

as
r\to 0
. In particular, we have
\bm{A}_{\bm{u},0}=\bm{A}_{\bm{u}}(0)
.

In addition, by the continuities of
\bm{\Sigma}_{\bm{u}}(r)
,
\bm{A}_{\bm{u}}(r)
,
\bm{P}_{\bm{u}}(r)
and
\bm{\Lambda}_{\bm{u}}(r)
on
r\in[0,\delta_{pc}]
,
it is easy to see

(i)

\bm{P}_{\bm{u},0}
is orthogonal;

(ii)

\bm{\Sigma}_{\bm{u},0}
has the eigen-decomposition

\bm{\Sigma}_{\bm{u},0}=\bm{P}_{\bm{u},0}\bm{\Lambda}_{\bm{u},0}\bm{P}_{\bm{u},0}^{T};

(iii)

\bm{A}_{\bm{u},0}=\bm{P}_{\bm{u},0}\bm{\Lambda}_{\bm{u},0}^{1/2}
.

3
Covariance Structure

In this section, we have a detailed analysis on
\bm{\Sigma}_{\bm{u}}(r),\bm{A}_{\bm{u}}(r),\bm{P}_{\bm{u}}(r)\text{ and }\bm{\Lambda}_{\bm{u}}(r)
, especially when
r\to 0
.

3.1
General Covariance Structure

The following lemma collects some useful results from Lemma
2.5
and Definition
2.6
.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
X
be qualified under perturbation and
\bm{u}\in\mathbb{S}^{N-1}
.
Then for any
1\leq i\leq L
, we have

(i)

(\bm{P}_{\bm{u},0}^{(i)})^{T}\bm{P}_{\bm{u},1}^{(i)}=0
;

(ii)

\lambda_{\bm{u},i,1}=0
, i.e.,
\bm{\Lambda}_{\bm{u},1}=\bm{0}_{L\times L}
;

(iii)

if
\lambda_{\bm{u},i,0}=0
, then
\lambda_{\bm{u},i,2}=(\bm{P}_{\bm{u},0}^{(i)})^{T}\bm{\Sigma}_{\bm{u},2}\bm{P}_{\bm{u},0}^{(i)}
;

(iv)

\bm{\Sigma}_{\bm{u},0}\bm{P}_{\bm{u},1}^{(i)}=\lambda_{\bm{u},i,0}\bm{P}_{\bm{u},1}^{(i)}
.

Proof.

By Definition
2.6
and the orthogonality of
\bm{P}_{\bm{u}}(r)
for any
r\geq 0
, we have

\displaystyle\bm{I}_{L}

\displaystyle=\bm{P}_{\bm{u}}(r)^{T}\bm{P}_{\bm{u}}(r)

\displaystyle=\left(\bm{P}_{\bm{u},0}+\bm{P}_{\bm{u},1}r+o(r)\right)^{T}\left(\bm{P}_{\bm{u},0}+\bm{P}_{\bm{u},1}r+o(r)\right)

\displaystyle=\bm{I}_{L}+\left(\bm{P}_{\bm{u},0}^{T}\bm{P}_{\bm{u},1}+\bm{P}_{\bm{u},1}^{T}\bm{P}_{\bm{u},0}\right)r+o(r),

which implies

\bm{P}_{\bm{u},0}^{T}\bm{P}_{\bm{u},1}+\bm{P}_{\bm{u},1}^{T}\bm{P}_{\bm{u},0}=\bm{0}_{L\times L}.

Thus, for any
1\leq i\leq L
,

(\bm{P}_{\bm{u},0}^{(i)})^{T}\bm{P}_{\bm{u},1}^{(i)}=\frac{1}{2}\left(\bm{P}_{\bm{u},0}^{T}\bm{P}_{\bm{u},1}+\bm{P}_{\bm{u},1}^{T}\bm{P}_{\bm{u},0}\right)[i,i]=0.

For (ii), note that

\displaystyle\bm{\Sigma}_{\bm{u}}(r)\bm{P}_{\bm{u}}(r)^{(i)}

\displaystyle=\left(\bm{\Sigma}_{\bm{u},0}+\bm{\Sigma}_{\bm{u},2}r^{2}+o(r^{2})\right)\left(\bm{P}_{\bm{u},0}^{(i)}+\bm{P}_{\bm{u},1}^{(i)}r+o(r)\right)

\displaystyle=\bm{\Sigma}_{\bm{u},0}\bm{P}_{\bm{u},0}^{(i)}+\left(\bm{\Sigma}_{\bm{u},0}\bm{P}_{\bm{u},1}^{(i)}\right)r+o(r)

and

\displaystyle\lambda_{\bm{u},i}(r)\bm{P}_{\bm{u}}(r)^{(i)}

\displaystyle=\left(\lambda_{\bm{u},i,0}+\lambda_{\bm{u},i,1}r+\lambda_{\bm{u},i,2}r^{2}+o(r^{2})\right)\left(\bm{P}_{\bm{u},0}^{(i)}+\bm{P}_{\bm{u},1}^{(i)}r+o(r)\right)

(18)

\displaystyle=\lambda_{\bm{u},i,0}\bm{P}_{\bm{u},0}^{(i)}+\left(\lambda_{\bm{u},i,0}\bm{P}_{\bm{u},1}^{(i)}+\lambda_{\bm{u},i,1}\bm{P}_{\bm{u},0}^{(i)}\right)r+o(r).

Then by
\bm{\Sigma}_{\bm{u}}(r)\bm{P}_{\bm{u}}(r)^{(i)}=\lambda_{\bm{u},i}(r)\bm{P}_{\bm{u}}(r)^{(i)}
, we get

\lambda_{\bm{u},i,0}\bm{P}_{\bm{u},1}^{(i)}+\lambda_{\bm{u},i,1}\bm{P}_{\bm{u},0}^{(i)}=\bm{\Sigma}_{\bm{u},0}\bm{P}_{\bm{u},1}^{(i)}.

(19)

By left-multiplying
(\bm{P}_{\bm{u},0}^{(i)})^{T}
on the both sides of (
19
), (i) of this lemma, (i) and (ii) in Remark
2.7
, and the symmetry of
\bm{\Sigma}_{\bm{u},0}
, we have for any
1\leq i\leq L
,

\lambda_{\bm{u},i,1}=\left(\bm{P}_{\bm{u},0}^{(i)}\right)^{T}\bm{\Sigma}_{\bm{u},0}\bm{P}_{\bm{u},1}^{(i)}=\left(\bm{P}_{\bm{u},1}^{(i)}\right)^{T}\bm{\Sigma}_{\bm{u},0}\bm{P}_{\bm{u},0}^{(i)}=\lambda_{\bm{u},i,0}\left(\bm{P}_{\bm{u},1}^{(i)}\right)^{T}\bm{P}_{\bm{u},0}^{(i)}=0.

For (iii), by (ii) and
\lambda_{\bm{u},i,0}=0
, (
18
) becomes

\displaystyle\lambda_{\bm{u},i}(r)\bm{P}_{\bm{u}}(r)^{(i)}

\displaystyle=\left(\lambda_{\bm{u},i,2}r^{2}+o(r^{2})\right)\left(\bm{P}_{\bm{u},0}^{(i)}+\bm{P}_{\bm{u},1}^{(i)}r+o(r)\right)

\displaystyle=\lambda_{\bm{u},i,2}\bm{P}_{\bm{u},0}^{(i)}r^{2}+o(r^{2}).

Similarly as in (ii), by left-multiplying both sides of
\bm{\Sigma}_{\bm{u}}(r)\bm{P}_{\bm{u}}(r)^{(i)}=\lambda_{\bm{u},i}(r)\bm{P}_{\bm{u}}(r)^{(i)}
by
\left(\bm{P}_{\bm{u},0}^{(i)}\right)^{T}
and comparing the coefficients for
r^{2}
, we have

\lambda_{\bm{u},i,2}=\left(\bm{P}_{\bm{u},0}^{(i)}\right)^{T}\bm{\Sigma}_{\bm{u},2}\bm{P}_{\bm{u},0}^{(i)}.

Finally, taking (ii) into (
19
) yields (iv).
∎

Remark 3.2
.

Let
X

, it is easy to see the matrix

(20)

For any

,
1\leq k\leq N

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
X
be qualified under perturbation and
\bm{u}\in\mathbb{S}^{N-1}
. Then

(i)

if
\bm{M}\in\mathbb{R}^{N\times N}
is the
N
-th order matriculation of a vector
\bm{a}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
(

\bm{H}(\bm{u})\bm{a}=\bm{M}\bm{u};

(ii)

\bm{\Sigma}_{\bm{u},0}\bm{H}^{T}(\bm{u})=\bm{0}_{L\times N}
;

(iii)

\bm{H}(\bm{u})\bm{A}_{\bm{u}}(r)^{(i)}=\bm{0}_{N}+o(r)
as
r\to 0
for any
1\leq i\leq{\rm Rank}\,(\bm{\Sigma}_{\bm{u},0})
.

Proof.

One can directly check (i) by (
21
). As for (ii), note that the expressions in (
14
) and (
16
) are both symmetric in
i_{1}
and
j_{1}
.
As a result, for any

Consequently, one can varify that for any

Then by (i) of this lemma and
\bm{\Sigma}_{\bm{u},0}=\bm{\Sigma}_{\bm{u},0}^{T}

(\bm{\Sigma}_{\bm{u},0})_{(i)}\bm{H}^{T}(\bm{u})=\left(\bm{H}(\bm{u})(\bm{\Sigma}_{\bm{u},0})^{(i)}\right)^{T}=\left({\rm Matri}\,\left((\bm{\Sigma}_{\bm{u},0})^{(i)}\right)\bm{u}\right)^{T}=\bm{0}_{N}^{T}.

As for (iii), by (ii) of this lemma, we have

\bm{H}(\bm{u})\bm{A}_{\bm{u},0}=\bm{0}_{N\times L}.

(22)

Then
by Remark
3.2
, it suffices to show that for any

,

\bm{H}(\bm{u})\bm{A}_{\bm{u},1}^{(i)}=\bm{0}_{N}.

.
By (iii) in Remark
2.7
and (

), we have for any

\bm{H}(\bm{u})\bm{P}_{\bm{u},0}^{(i)}=\lambda_{\bm{u},i,0}^{-1/2}\bm{H}(\bm{u})\bm{A}_{\bm{u},0}^{(i)}=\bm{0}_{N}.

),

) is immediate, and hence completes the proof.

The following is our first main result.

Theorem 4.3
.

Let

be qualified under perturbation. Then for any

Intuitively, when a critical point is very close to another critical point with unknown type, the determinant of its Hessian is equally likely to be positive or negative.

for some

\displaystyle f\left(\bm{M}^{v^{*}}(0),\cdots,\bm{M}^{v^{*}}(0),\widetilde{\bm{M}}^{v_{0}}(0)\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\sigma\in\Pi_{N}}(-1)^{{\rm sign}\,(\sigma)}{\rm det}\,\left(\bm{M}^{v^{*}}(0)^{(\sigma(1))},\cdots,\bm{M}^{v^{*}}(0)^{(\sigma(N-1))},\widetilde{\bm{M}}^{v_{0}}(0)^{(\sigma(N))}\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\sigma\in\Pi_{N}}(-1)^{{\rm sign}\,(\sigma)}\widetilde{\bm{M}}^{v_{0}}(0)[N,\sigma(N)]

\displaystyle\rm{\displaystyle det}\,\left(\left(\bm{M}^{v^{*}}(0)^{(\sigma(1))},\cdots,\bm{M}^{v^{*}}(0)^{(\sigma(N-1))}\right)[1:(N-1),1:(N-1)]\right)

\displaystyle=(N-1)!\sum_{j=1}^{N}(-1)^{N-j}\widetilde{\bm{M}}^{v_{0}}(0)[N,j]

\displaystyle\rm{\displaystyle det}\,\left(\left(\bm{M}^{v^{*}}(0)^{(1)},\cdots,\widehat{\bm{M}^{v^{*}}(0)}^{(j)},\cdots,\bm{M}^{v^{*}}(0)^{(N)}\right)[1:(N-1),1:(N-1)]\right)

\displaystyle=(N-1)!{\rm det}\,\begin{pmatrix}\bm{M}^{v^{*}}(0)_{(1)}\\
\vdots\\
\bm{M}^{v^{*}}(0)_{(N-1)}\\
\widetilde{\bm{M}}^{v_{0}}(0)_{(N)}\\
\end{pmatrix},

where the second equality follows from the fact that
\bm{M}^{v^{*}}(0)_{(N)}=\bm{0}_{N}
(see (
44
)),
and the last equality is given by Laplace’s expansion.

Now suppose

f(\bm{M}^{v^{*}}(0),\cdots,\bm{M}^{v^{*}}(0),\widetilde{\bm{M}}^{v_{0}}(0))=0

for any integer
L-N\leq v_{0}\leq L-1
,
then

{\rm det}\,\begin{pmatrix}\bm{M}^{v^{*}}(0)_{(1)}\\
\vdots\\
\bm{M}^{v^{*}}(0)_{(N-1)}\\
\widetilde{\bm{M}}^{v_{0}}(0)_{(N)}\\
\end{pmatrix}=0,

which, together with (
44

\widetilde{\bm{M}}^{v_{0}}(0)[N,N]=\bm{u}_{0}^{T}\widetilde{\bm{M}}^{v_{0}}(0)\bm{u}_{0}=0.

Then by (i) of Lemma
3.3
, we have for any
L-N\leq v_{0}\leq L-1
,

\bm{u}_{0}^{T}\bm{H}(\bm{u}_{0})\bm{P}_{0}^{(v_{0})}=\bm{u}_{0}^{T}\widetilde{\bm{M}}^{v_{0}}(0)\bm{u}_{0}=0.

Note that
(\bm{u}_{0}^{T}\bm{H}(\bm{u}_{0}))^{T}=(\bm{H}(\bm{u}_{0})_{(N)})^{T}
is non-zero, and by (ii) of Lemma
3.3
, it is in the zero space of
\bm{\Sigma}_{0}
, which is expanded by
\bm{P}_{0}^{(L-N)},\dots,\bm{P}_{0}^{(L)}
. Thus, the above equality implies
(\bm{H}(\bm{u}_{0})_{(N)})^{T}
and
\bm{P}_{0}^{(L)}
must be linearly dependent. Then
by Lemma
3.7
, we get
\bm{H}(\bm{u}_{0})_{(N)}
and
(0,\dots,0,1,-1)\in\mathbb{R}^{L}

which contradicts (
21
). This completes the proof.

Lemma 4.6

For (i), let

\bm{y}^{\prime}=(y_{1},\dots,y_{L-N-1},-y_{L-N},\dots,-y_{L})

\bm{A}_{(L)}(0)\bm{y}>u\text{ and }h_{0}(\bm{y})>0.

\bm{A}(0)^{(i)}=0

Definition 4.7
.

For any
n\geq 1
, a function
g:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}
is said to be
regular
if it can be written in the form:

g(\bm{y})=|\alpha(\bm{y})|\exp(-\beta(\bm{y})),

where
\alpha(\bm{y})
is a non-zero polynomial of
y_{1},\dots,y_{n}
and
\beta(\bm{y})=\bm{y}^{T}\bm{\Sigma}\bm{y}
for some positive-definite matrix
\bm{\Sigma}\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}
.

Remark 4.8
.

It is easy to check that a regular function on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
must be non-negative, bounded and integrable. Moreover, the integral of a regular function on a non-empty open set must be positive.

Let
X
be qualified under perturbation.
For any
r\geq 0
and
\bm{y}\in\mathbb{R}^{L}
,
define

g_{r}(\bm{y}):=\left|h_{r}(\bm{y})\right|p_{L}(\bm{y}),

(55)

where
h_{r}(\bm{y})
is as defined in (
46
)-(
49
) and

p_{L}(\bm{y}):=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{L/2}}\exp\left(-\frac{1}{2}\bm{y}^{T}\bm{y}\right).

(56)

For any
r\geq 0
, since
h_{r}(\bm{y})
is a nonzero polynomial of
y_{1},\dots,y_{L}
,
g_{r}(\bm{y})
is a regular function of
\bm{y}\in\mathbb{R}^{L}
. In addition, since
\bm{A}(r)
is continuous on
r\in[0,\delta_{pc}]
(where
\delta_{pc}
is as defined in Definition

), all the coefficients of the polynomial

are continuous, and then uniformly bounded on

.
Then by the triangle inequality, there exists a constant

such that for any

Thus, for any

and

g_{r}(\bm{y})\leq C\sum_{v_{1},\dots,v_{N}\in\{1,\dots,L\}}\left|y_{v_{1}}\cdots y_{v_{N}}\right|p_{L}(\bm{y}),

(58)

where the right-hand side is a finite sum of regular functions.

Note that (ii) of Lemma

.

Corollary 5.6
.

\lim_{u\to\infty}\lim_{\|\bm{t}\|\to 0}\frac{f_{u,N}(\bm{t})}{f_{u,N-1}(\bm{t})+f_{u,N}(\bm{t})}=\frac{1}{2}.

Since we are conditioning on having a critical point at the origin with unknown index, the ratio in Corollary

\bm{\Sigma}(\bm{t})=\bm{V}_{11}(\bm{t})-\bm{V}_{12}(\bm{t})\bm{V}_{22}^{-1}(\bm{t})\bm{V}_{12}^{T}(\bm{t}).

A.1
The Blocked Covariance Matrix

For convenience, we adopt the following notations for
\bm{t}\in B(\bm{0}_{N},\delta_{\rho})\setminus\{\bm{0}_{N}\}
:

1.

\bm{G}_{00}(\bm{t}):={\rm Cov}\,[X(\bm{0}),X(\bm{t})]=R(\bm{t})
;

2.

\bm{G}_{01}(\bm{t}):={\rm Cov}\,[X(\bm{0}),\triangledown X(\bm{t})]=-{\rm Cov}\,[\triangledown X(\bm{0}),X(\bm{t})]=(R_{1}(\bm{t}),\dots,R_{N}(\bm{t}))
;

3.

\bm{G}_{20}(\bm{t}):={\rm Cov}\,[\triangledown^{2}X(\bm{0}),X(\bm{t})]={\rm Cov}\,[X(\bm{0}),\triangledown^{2}X(\bm{t})]\in\mathbb{R}^{(L-2)\times 1}
;

4.

\bm{G}_{21}(\bm{t}):={\rm Cov}\,[\triangledown^{2}X(\bm{0}),\triangledown X(\bm{t})]=-{\rm Cov}\,[\triangledown X(\bm{0}),\triangledown^{2}X(\bm{t})]\in\mathbb{R}^{(L-2)\times N}
;

5.

\bm{G}_{22}(\bm{t}):={\rm Cov}\,[\triangledown^{2}X(\bm{0}),\triangledown^{2}X(\bm{t})]\in\mathbb{R}^{(L-2)\times(L-2)}
.

Their relationships with the covariance function
R
are given by Lemma
2.2
.
In particular, by (
2
)-(
5
), we have

1.

\bm{G}_{00}(\bm{0}):={\rm Cov}\,[X(\bm{0}),X(\bm{0})]=R(\bm{0})
;

2.

\bm{G}_{01}(\bm{0}):={\rm Cov}\,[X(\bm{0}),\triangledown X(\bm{0})]=(R_{1}(\bm{0}),\dots,R_{N}(\bm{0}))=\bm{0}_{1\times N}
;

3.

\bm{G}_{20}(\bm{0}):={\rm Cov}\,[\triangledown^{2}X(\bm{0}),X(\bm{0})]\in\mathbb{R}^{(L-2)\times 1}
;

4.

\bm{G}_{21}(\bm{0}):={\rm Cov}\,[\triangledown^{2}X(\bm{0}),\triangledown X(\bm{0})]=\bm{0}_{(L-2)\times N}
;

5.

\bm{G}_{22}(\bm{0}):={\rm Cov}\,[\triangledown^{2}X(\bm{0}),\triangledown^{2}X(\bm{0})]\in\mathbb{R}^{(L-2)\times(L-2)}
.

Immediately, we have

\bm{V}_{11}(\bm{t})=\begin{pmatrix}\bm{G}_{22}(\bm{0})&\bm{G}_{20}(\bm{0})&\bm{G}_{20}(\bm{t})\\
\bm{G}_{20}^{T}(\bm{0})&\bm{G}_{00}(\bm{0})&\bm{G}_{00}(\bm{t})\\
\bm{G}_{20}^{T}(\bm{t})&\bm{G}_{00}^{T}(\bm{t})&\bm{G}_{00}(\bm{0})\end{pmatrix}

(91)

and

\bm{V}_{12}(\bm{t})=\begin{pmatrix}-\bm{G}_{21}(\bm{0})&-\bm{G}_{21}(\bm{t})\\
-\bm{G}_{01}(\bm{0})&-\bm{G}_{01}(\bm{t})\\
\bm{G}_{01}(\bm{t})&-\bm{G}_{01}(\bm{0})\end{pmatrix}\\
=\begin{pmatrix}\bm{0}_{(L-2)\times N}&-\bm{G}_{21}(\bm{t})\\
\bm{0}_{1\times N}&-\bm{G}_{01}(\bm{t})\\
\bm{G}_{01}(\bm{t})&\bm{0}_{1\times N}\end{pmatrix}.

(92)

As for
\bm{V}_{22}^{-1}(\bm{t})
,
by (
3
), we have

{\rm Cov}\,[\triangledown X(\bm{0}),\triangledown X(\bm{0})]=-2\rho^{(1)}(0)\bm{I}_{N}

and

{\rm Cov}\,[\triangledown X(\bm{t}),\triangledown X(\bm{0})]=-2\rho^{(1)}(\|\bm{t}\|^{2})\bm{I}_{N}-4\rho^{(2)}(\|\bm{t}\|^{2})\bm{t}\bm{t}^{T}.

Recall that by (
7
),
\rho^{(1)}(0)<0
. We can define

k_{1}(\bm{t}):=\frac{\rho^{(1)}(\|\bm{t}\|^{2})}{\rho^{(1)}(0)}\text{ and }k_{2}(\bm{t}):=\frac{2\rho^{(2)}(\|\bm{t}\|^{2})}{\rho^{(1)}(0)},

for any
\bm{t}\in B(\bm{0}_{N},\delta_{\rho})\setminus\{\bm{0}_{N}\}
. Thus,

\bm{V}_{22}(\bm{t})=-2\rho^{(1)}(0)\begin{pmatrix}\bm{I}_{N}&k_{1}(\bm{t})\bm{I}_{N}+k_{2}(\bm{t})\bm{t}\bm{t}^{T}\\
k_{1}(\bm{t})\bm{I}_{N}+k_{2}(\bm{t})\bm{t}\bm{t}^{T}&\bm{I}_{N}\end{pmatrix}

and then

\bm{V}_{22}^{-1}(\bm{t})=-\frac{1}{2\rho^{(1)}(0)}\begin{pmatrix}\bm{I}_{N}&k_{1}(\bm{t})\bm{I}_{N}+k_{2}(\bm{t})\bm{t}\bm{t}^{T}\\
k_{1}(\bm{t})\bm{I}_{N}+k_{2}(\bm{t})\bm{t}\bm{t}^{T}&\bm{I}_{N}\end{pmatrix}^{-1}.

To further calculate
\bm{V}_{22}^{-1}(\bm{t})
, we need the following two facts which can be easily checked:

1.

for any symmetric matrix
\bm{B}\in\mathbb{R}^{N\times N}
such that
\bm{I}_{N}-\bm{B}^{2}
is invertible,

\begin{pmatrix}\bm{I}_{N}&\bm{B}\\
\bm{B}&\bm{I}_{N}\end{pmatrix}^{-1}=\begin{pmatrix}(\bm{I}_{N}-\bm{B}^{2})^{-1}&-\bm{B}(\bm{I}_{N}-\bm{B}^{2})^{-1}\\
-(\bm{I}_{N}-\bm{B}^{2})^{-1}\bm{B}&(\bm{I}_{N}-\bm{B}^{2})^{-1}\end{pmatrix};

2.

(The Sherman–Morrison Formula) for any
\bm{w},\bm{v}\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
such that
1+\bm{v}^{T}\bm{w}\neq 0
,

(\bm{I}_{N}+\bm{w}\bm{v}^{T})^{-1}=\bm{I}_{N}-\frac{\bm{w}\bm{v}^{T}}{1+\bm{v}^{T}\bm{w}}.

By taking

\bm{B}=k_{1}(\bm{t})\bm{I}_{N}+k_{2}(\bm{t})\bm{t}\bm{t}^{T}
in Fact 1,
we get

\displaystyle\bm{I}_{N}-\bm{B}^{2}

\displaystyle=\left(1-k_{1}^{2}(\bm{t})\right)\left(\bm{I}_{N}-k_{3}(\bm{t})\bm{t}\bm{t}^{T}\right),

(93)

where by letting
k_{*}(\bm{t}):=k_{1}(\bm{t})+k_{2}(\bm{t})\|\bm{t}\|^{2}
,

k_{3}(\bm{t}):=\frac{2k_{1}(\bm{t})k_{2}(\bm{t})+k_{2}^{2}(\bm{t})\|\bm{t}\|^{2}}{1-k_{1}^{2}(\bm{t})}=\frac{k_{2}(\bm{t})(k_{1}(\bm{t})+k_{*}(\bm{t}))}{1-k_{1}^{2}(\bm{t})}.

By (
11
),

1-k_{1}^{2}(\bm{t})=\frac{(\rho^{(1)}(0))^{2}-(\rho^{(1)}(\|\bm{t}\|^{2}))^{2}}{(\rho^{(1)}(0))^{2}}>0.

(94)

Thus,
k_{3}(\bm{t})
is well-defined for any
\bm{t}\in B(\bm{0}_{N},\delta_{\rho})\setminus\{\bm{0}_{N}\}
. To apply Fact 2 to the right-hand side of (
93
), we still need to show

1-k_{3}(\bm{t})\|\bm{t}\|^{2}\neq 0
for any
\bm{t}\in B(\bm{0}_{N},\delta_{\rho})\setminus\{\bm{0}_{N}\}
. One can easily check that

\left(1-k_{3}(\bm{t})\|\bm{t}\|^{2}\right)\left(1-k_{1}^{2}(\bm{t})\right)=1-k_{*}^{2}(\bm{t}).

Taking
\bm{t}^{\prime}:=(t_{1}^{\prime},t_{2}^{\prime},\dots,t_{N}^{\prime})=(0,\dots,0,\|\bm{t}\|)
and by (
10
), we have

|k_{*}(\bm{t})|=|k_{*}(\bm{t}^{\prime})|=\left|k_{1}(\bm{t}^{\prime})+\|\bm{t}\|^{2}k_{2}(\bm{t}^{\prime})\right|=\left|\frac{\rho^{(1)}(\|\bm{t}^{\prime}\|^{2})+2t_{N}^{\prime 2}\rho^{(2)}(\|\bm{t}^{\prime}\|^{2})}{\rho^{(1)}(0)}\right|<1.

Thus, for any
\bm{t}\in B(\bm{0}_{N},\delta_{\rho})\setminus\{\bm{0}_{N}\}
,

1-k_{3}(\bm{t})\|\bm{t}\|^{2}=\frac{1-k_{*}^{2}(\bm{t})}{1-k_{1}^{2}(\bm{t})}>0.

(95)

Then by Fact 2,
we have

\displaystyle(\bm{I}_{N}-\bm{B}^{2})^{-1}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{1-k_{1}^{2}(\bm{t})}\left(\bm{I}_{N}+\frac{k_{3}(\bm{t})\bm{t}\bm{t}^{T}}{1-k_{3}(\bm{t})\|\bm{t}\|^{2}}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{1-k_{1}^{2}(\bm{t})}\left(\bm{I}_{N}+k_{4}(\bm{t})\bm{t}\bm{t}^{T}\right),

where

k_{4}(\bm{t}):=\frac{k_{3}(\bm{t})}{1-k_{3}(\bm{t})\|\bm{t}\|^{2}}=\frac{\left(1-k_{1}^{2}(\bm{t})\right)k_{3}(\bm{t})}{1-k_{*}^{2}(\bm{t})}=\frac{k_{2}(\bm{t})(k_{1}(\bm{t})+k_{*}(\bm{t}))}{1-k_{*}^{2}(\bm{t})}.

Then

\displaystyle(\bm{I}_{N}-\bm{B}^{2})^{-1}\bm{B}=\bm{B}(\bm{I}_{N}-\bm{B}^{2})^{-1}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{1-k_{1}^{2}(\bm{t})}\left(\bm{I}_{N}+k_{4}(\bm{t})\bm{t}\bm{t}^{T}\right)\left(k_{1}(\bm{t})\bm{I}_{N}+k_{2}(\bm{t})\bm{t}\bm{t}^{T}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{1-k_{1}^{2}(\bm{t})}\left(k_{1}(\bm{t})\bm{I}_{N}+\left(k_{2}(\bm{t})+k_{1}(\bm{t})k_{4}(\bm{t})+k_{2}(\bm{t})k_{4}(\bm{t})\|\bm{t}\|^{2}\right)\bm{t}\bm{t}^{T}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{1-k_{1}^{2}(\bm{t})}\left(k_{1}(\bm{t})\bm{I}_{N}+k_{5}(\bm{t})\bm{t}\bm{t}^{T}\right),

where

\displaystyle k_{5}(\bm{t}):

\displaystyle=k_{2}(\bm{t})+k_{1}(\bm{t})k_{4}(\bm{t})+k_{2}(\bm{t})k_{4}(\bm{t})\|\bm{t}\|^{2}

\displaystyle=k_{2}(\bm{t})+k_{*}(\bm{t})k_{4}(\bm{t})

\displaystyle=\frac{k_{2}(\bm{t})(1+k_{1}(\bm{t})k_{*}(\bm{t}))}{1-k_{*}^{2}(\bm{t})}.

It is easy to see
k_{i}(\bm{t})
,
i=1,2,\dots,5
are all well-defined for any
\bm{t}\in B(\bm{0}_{N},\delta_{\rho})\setminus\{\bm{0}_{N}\}
.

In summary, for any
\bm{t}\in B(\bm{0}_{N},\delta_{\rho})\setminus\{\bm{0}_{N}\}
, we have

\displaystyle-2\rho^{(1)}(0)\bm{V}_{22}^{-1}(\bm{t})

(96)

\displaystyle=\begin{pmatrix}\frac{1}{1-k_{1}^{2}(\bm{t})}\bm{I}_{N}&-\frac{k_{1}(\bm{t})}{1-k_{1}^{2}(\bm{t})}\bm{I}_{N}\\
-\frac{k_{1}(\bm{t})}{1-k_{1}^{2}(\bm{t})}\bm{I}_{N}&\frac{1}{1-k_{1}^{2}(\bm{t})}\bm{I}_{N}\\
\end{pmatrix}+\begin{pmatrix}\frac{k_{4}(\bm{t})}{1-k_{1}^{2}(\bm{t})}\bm{t}\bm{t}^{T}&-\frac{k_{5}(\bm{t})}{1-k_{1}^{2}(\bm{t})}\bm{t}\bm{t}^{T}\\
-\frac{k_{5}(\bm{t})}{1-k_{1}^{2}(\bm{t})}\bm{t}\bm{t}^{T}&\frac{k_{4}(\bm{t})}{1-k_{1}^{2}(\bm{t})}\bm{t}\bm{t}^{T}\\
\end{pmatrix}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{1-k_{1}^{2}(\bm{t})}\begin{pmatrix}\bm{I}_{N}&-k_{1}(\bm{t})\bm{I}_{N}\\
-k_{1}(\bm{t})\bm{I}_{N}&\bm{I}_{N}\\
\end{pmatrix}+\frac{1}{1-k_{1}^{2}(\bm{t})}\begin{pmatrix}k_{4}(\bm{t})\bm{t}\bm{t}^{T}&-k_{5}(\bm{t})\bm{t}\bm{t}^{T}\\
-k_{5}(\bm{t})\bm{t}\bm{t}^{T}&k_{4}(\bm{t})\bm{t}\bm{t}^{T}\\
\end{pmatrix},

where

k_{1}(\bm{t})=\frac{\rho^{(1)}(\|\bm{t}\|^{2})}{\rho^{(1)}(0)},~~k_{2}(\bm{t})=\frac{2\rho^{(2)}(\|\bm{t}\|^{2})}{\rho^{(1)}(0)},~~k_{*}(\bm{t})=k_{1}(\bm{t})+k_{2}(\bm{t})\|\bm{t}\|^{2},

k_{4}(\bm{t})=\frac{k_{2}(\bm{t})(k_{1}(\bm{t})+k_{*}(\bm{t}))}{1-k_{*}^{2}(\bm{t})}\text{ and }k_{5}(\bm{t})=\frac{k_{2}(\bm{t})(1+k_{1}(\bm{t})k_{*}(\bm{t}))}{1-k_{*}^{2}(\bm{t})}.

Here one should note that

\displaystyle k_{5}(\bm{t})-k_{4}(\bm{t})

\displaystyle=\frac{k_{2}(\bm{t})(1-k_{1}(\bm{t}))(1-k_{*}(\bm{t}))}{1-k_{*}^{2}(\bm{t})}

(97)

\displaystyle=\frac{k_{2}(\bm{t})(1-k_{1}(\bm{t}))}{1+k_{*}(\bm{t})}

\displaystyle\to 0\text{ as $\|\bm{t}\|\to 0$.}

In consequence, by (
91
), (
92
) and (
96
),

\displaystyle-2\rho^{(1)}(0)(1-k_{1}^{2}(\bm{t}))\bm{V}_{12}(\bm{t})\bm{V}^{-1}_{22}(\bm{t})\bm{V}_{12}^{T}(\bm{t})

\displaystyle=\begin{pmatrix}\bm{0}_{(L-2)\times N}&-\bm{G}_{21}(\bm{t})\\
\bm{0}_{1\times N}&-\bm{G}_{01}(\bm{t})\\
\bm{G}_{01}(\bm{t})&\bm{0}_{1\times N}\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}\bm{I}_{N}&-k_{1}(\bm{t})\bm{I}_{N}\\
-k_{1}(\bm{t})\bm{I}_{N}&\bm{I}_{N}\\
\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}\bm{0}_{N\times(L-2)}&\bm{0}_{N\times 1}&\bm{G}_{01}^{T}(\bm{t})\\
-\bm{G}_{21}^{T}(\bm{t})&-\bm{G}_{01}^{T}(\bm{t})&\bm{0}_{N\times 1}\end{pmatrix}

\displaystyle+\begin{pmatrix}\bm{0}_{(L-2)\times N}&-\bm{G}_{21}(\bm{t})\\
\bm{0}_{1\times N}&-\bm{G}_{01}(\bm{t})\\
\bm{G}_{01}(\bm{t})&\bm{0}_{1\times N}\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}k_{4}(\bm{t})\bm{t}\bm{t}^{T}&-k_{5}(\bm{t})\bm{t}\bm{t}^{T}\\
-k_{5}(\bm{t})\bm{t}\bm{t}^{T}&k_{4}(\bm{t})\bm{t}\bm{t}^{T}\\
\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}\bm{0}_{N\times(L-2)}&\bm{0}_{N\times 1}&\bm{G}_{01}^{T}(\bm{t})\\
-\bm{G}_{21}^{T}(\bm{t})&-\bm{G}_{01}^{T}(\bm{t})&\bm{0}_{N\times 1}\end{pmatrix}

\displaystyle=\begin{pmatrix}\bm{G}_{21}(\bm{t})\bm{G}_{21}^{T}(\bm{t})&\bm{G}_{21}(\bm{t})\bm{G}_{01}^{T}(\bm{t})&k_{1}(\bm{t})\bm{G}_{21}(\bm{t})\bm{G}_{01}^{T}(\bm{t})\\

\displaystyle+\begin{pmatrix}k_{4}(\bm{t})\bm{G}_{21}(\bm{t})\bm{t}\bm{t}^{T}\bm{G}_{21}^{T}(\bm{t})&k_{4}(\bm{t})\bm{G}_{21}(\bm{t})\bm{t}\bm{t}^{T}\bm{G}_{01}^{T}(\bm{t})&k_{5}(\bm{t})\bm{G}_{21}(\bm{t})\bm{t}\bm{t}^{T}\bm{G}_{01}^{T}(\bm{t})\\
k_{4}(\bm{t})\bm{G}_{01}(\bm{t})\bm{t}\bm{t}^{T}\bm{G}_{21}^{T}(\bm{t})&k_{4}(\bm{t})\bm{G}_{01}(\bm{t})\bm{t}\bm{t}^{T}\bm{G}_{01}^{T}(\bm{t})&k_{5}(\bm{t})\bm{G}_{01}(\bm{t})\bm{t}\bm{t}^{T}\bm{G}_{01}^{T}(\bm{t})\\
k_{5}(\bm{t})\bm{G}_{01}(\bm{t})\bm{t}\bm{t}^{T}\bm{G}_{21}^{T}(\bm{t})&k_{5}(\bm{t})\bm{G}_{01}(\bm{t})\bm{t}\bm{t}^{T}\bm{G}_{01}^{T}(\bm{t})&k_{4}(\bm{t})\bm{G}_{01}(\bm{t})\bm{t}\bm{t}^{T}\bm{G}_{01}^{T}(\bm{t})\end{pmatrix},

and then the blocked version of
\bm{\Sigma}(\bm{t})
is given by

\displaystyle\bm{\Sigma}(\bm{t})=\bm{V}_{11}(\bm{t})-\bm{V}_{12}(\bm{t})\bm{V}^{-1}_{22}(\bm{t})\bm{V}_{12}^{T}(\bm{t})

(98)

\displaystyle=\begin{pmatrix}\bm{G}_{22}(\bm{0})&\bm{G}_{20}(\bm{0})&\bm{G}_{20}(\bm{t})\\

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{2\rho^{(1)}(0)}\frac{1}{1-k_{1}^{2}(\bm{t})}\begin{pmatrix}k_{4}(\bm{t})\bm{G}_{21}(\bm{t})\bm{t}\bm{t}^{T}\bm{G}_{21}^{T}(\bm{t})&k_{4}(\bm{t})\bm{G}_{21}(\bm{t})\bm{t}\bm{t}^{T}\bm{G}_{01}^{T}(\bm{t})&k_{5}(\bm{t})\bm{G}_{21}(\bm{t})\bm{t}\bm{t}^{T}\bm{G}_{01}^{T}(\bm{t})\\

A.2
Asymptotic Expansions of the Coefficients

Note that by Condition (3) in Definition
2.3
, we have

\rho(x)=\rho(0)+\rho^{(1)}(0)x+\frac{1}{2}\rho^{(2)}(0)x^{2}+\frac{1}{6}\rho^{(3)}(0)x^{3}+o(x^{3})\text{ as $x\downarrow 0$.}

In this part, we would like to use the above expansion to expand

(i)

\frac{\|\bm{t}\|^{2}}{2\rho^{(1)}(0)(1-k_{1}^{2}(\bm{t}))}
,

(ii)

\frac{k_{4}(\bm{t})\|\bm{t}\|^{2}}{2\rho^{(1)}(0)(1-k_{1}^{2}(\bm{t}))}
,

(iii)

\frac{k_{1}(\bm{t})\|\bm{t}\|^{2}}{2\rho^{(1)}(0)(1-k_{1}^{2}(\bm{t}))}
,

(iv)

\frac{k_{5}(\bm{t})\|\bm{t}\|^{2}}{2\rho^{(1)}(0)(1-k_{1}^{2}(\bm{t}))}
.

For (i),
note that

\displaystyle\left(\rho^{(1)}(0)+\rho^{(1)}(\|\bm{t}\|^{2})\right)^{-1}

\displaystyle=\left(\rho^{(1)}(0)+\rho^{(1)}(0)\right)^{-1}-\left(\rho^{(1)}(0)+\rho^{(1)}(0)\right)^{-2}\rho^{(2)}(0)\|\bm{t}\|^{2}+o(\|\bm{t}\|^{2})

\displaystyle=\left(2\rho^{(1)}(0)\right)^{-1}-\left(2\rho^{(1)}(0)\right)^{-2}\rho^{(2)}(0)\|\bm{t}\|^{2}+o(\|\bm{t}\|^{2})

and

\displaystyle\|\bm{t}\|^{2}\left(\rho^{(1)}(0)-\rho^{(1)}(\|\bm{t}\|^{2})\right)^{-1}

1.2.
Divisor-bounded multiplicative functions

Let

1.3.1.
Rankin-Selberg estimates for
GL_{m}
in typical short intervals

Let
f
be a fixed even weight
k\geq 2
, level
1
primitive, Hecke-normalized holomorphic cusp form without complex multiplication, and write its Fourier expansion at
\infty

f(z)=\sum_{n\geq 1}\lambda_{f}(n)n^{\frac{k-1}{2}}e(nz),\quad\text{Im}(z)>0,

with
\lambda_{f}(1)=1
. Set also

g_{f}(n):=\sum_{d^{2}|n}|\lambda_{f}(n/d^{2})|^{2}.

By the Hecke relation

\lambda_{f}(m)\lambda_{f}(n)=\sum_{d|(m,n)}\lambda_{f}\left(\frac{mn}{d^{2}}\right),\quad m,n\in\mathbb{N},

|\lambda_{f}|^{2}
and thus also
g_{f}
are multiplicative functions.
Deligne showed that
|\lambda_{f}(p)|\leq 2
for all primes
p
, and in general
|\lambda_{f}(n)|^{2}\leq d(n)^{2}
. Thus
|\lambda_{f}|^{2}
is bounded by a power of a divisor-function, and the same can be shown for
g_{f}
.

The classical Rankin-Selberg method shows
[
18
, Sec. 14.9]
that asymptotic formulae

\displaystyle\frac{1}{X}\sum_{n\leq X}|\lambda_{f}(n)|^{2}=c_{f}+O(X^{-2/5}),\quad\quad\frac{1}{X}\sum_{n\leq X}g_{f}(n)=d_{f}+O(X^{-2/5}),

hold as
X\rightarrow\infty
, where
c_{f},d_{f}>0
are constants depending on
f
. The Rankin-Selberg problem is equivalent to asking for an improvement of the error term
X^{-2/5}
in both of these estimates, but this is not our point of interest here.

One can ask whether the above asymptotic formulae continue to hold in short intervals. Ivić
[
17
]
considered
the variance of the error term in short interval averages. Specifically, he showed
[
17
, Cor. 2]
on the Lindelöf hypothesis that

(5)

\frac{1}{X}\int_{X}^{2X}\left(\frac{1}{h}\sum_{x<n\leq x+h}g_{f}(n)-d_{f}\right)^{2}dx=o(1),

as long as
h\geq X^{2/5-\varepsilon}
, albeit with a power-saving error term in the latter range.

At the expense of the quality of the error term, we obtain the following improvement in the range where (
5
) holds.

Corollary 1.1
.

Let
10\leq h_{0}\leq X/(10\log X)
and set
h:=h_{0}\log X
. Then there is a constant
\theta>0
such that

\frac{1}{X}\int_{X}^{2X}\left(\frac{1}{h}\sum_{x<n\leq x+h}|\lambda_{f}(n)|^{2}-c_{f}\right)^{2}dx\ll\frac{\log\log h_{0}}{\log h_{0}}+\frac{\log\log X}{(\log X)^{\theta}}.

The same estimate holds when
|\lambda_{f}|^{2}
and
c_{f}
are replaced by
g_{f}
and

, respectively.

This corollary might appear surprising, given our currently incomplete understanding of the shifted convolution problem

\sum_{X<n\leq 2X}|\lambda_{f}(n)|^{2}|\lambda_{f}(n+r)|^{2},\quad\quad 1\leq|r|\leq h.

Actually, our proof of Corollary

, Deligne’s theorem and the prime number theorem for Rankin-Selberg

1.3.3.
Hooley’s
\Delta
-function in short intervals

The distribution of divisors of a typical positive integer is a topic of classical interest, and a source of many difficult problems.
Given an integer
n\in\mathbb{N}
, let

\mathcal{D}_{n}(v):=\frac{1}{d(n)}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}d|n\\
d\leq e^{v}\end{subarray}}1,\quad\quad\text{ for }v\in\mathbb{R}.

This is a distribution function on the divisors of
n
.
A concentration function for
\mathcal{D}_{n}(v)
, in the sense of probability theory, can be given by

Q(n):=\max_{u\in\mathbb{R}}|\mathcal{D}_{n}(u+1)-\mathcal{D}_{n}(u)|=\max_{u\in\mathbb{R}}\frac{1}{d(n)}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}d|n\\
e^{u}<d\leq e^{u+1}\end{subarray}}1.

Hooley

15

considered the unnormalized variant

\Delta

0\leq\Delta(n)\leq d(n)
, but one seeks more refined data about this function. For example, Erdős
[
2
]
conjectured in 1948 that, except on a set of natural density 0,
\Delta(n)>1
.

Many authors have investigated the average and almost sure behaviour of
\Delta

[
21
]
proved Erdős’ conjecture in a quantitative form. A significant portion of Hall and Tenenbaum’s book
[
12
]
is devoted to the
\Delta

[
11
]
). For a partial survey of these results, see
[
40
]
.

Much less has been done concerning the local behaviour of the
\Delta
-function. To the author’s knowledge the only result about its short interval behaviour was worked out in the setting of polynomials over a finite field by Gorodetsky
[

By relating

Let

,

In the sequel we will require control over various averages of multiplicative functions

First, we will require some general pointwise estimates for prime power values of

In preparation, define

P(s):=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\geq 1\\
p^{k}||n\Rightarrow p^{k}\leq X\end{subarray}}\frac{f(n)}{n^{s}}=\prod_{p\leq X}\left(1+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}k\geq 1\\
p^{k}\leq X\end{subarray}}\frac{f(p^{k})}{p^{ks}}\right),\quad\text{Re}(s)>1.

Wherever
P
is non-zero we may also write the logarithmic derivative Dirichlet series

-\frac{P^{\prime}}{P}(s)=\sum_{n\geq 1}\frac{\Lambda_{f}(n)}{n^{s}}.

Lemma 3.1
.

Suppose
f:\mathbb{N}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
is multiplicative and satisfies
|f(n)|\leq d_{B}(n)^{C}
for all
n\leq X
.

a) For any prime power
p^{\nu}\leq X
we have

|f(p^{\nu})|\ll_{B,C}\begin{cases}\left(\frac{5}{4}\right)^{\nu}\left(1+\frac{\nu}{B-1}\right)^{(B-1)C},&\text{ if $B>1$,}\\
1&\text{ if $B=1$.}\end{cases}

b) For any
\eta\in[0,1/2)
we have

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}p^{\nu}\leq X\\
\nu\geq 2\end{subarray}}\frac{|f(p^{\nu})|}{p^{(1-\eta)\nu}}\ll_{\eta,B,C}1.

c)
\Lambda_{f}(n)=0
unless
n=p^{\nu}
for some prime power
p^{\nu}
. In particular, if
p^{\nu}\leq X
we have
|\Lambda_{f}(p)|\leq B\log p
when
\nu=1
and otherwise
|\Lambda_{f}(p^{\nu})|\ll_{\varepsilon,B,C}p^{\varepsilon\nu}
.

Proof.

a) If
B=1
then the claim is obvious since
d_{B}\equiv 1
. Thus, we may assume that
B>1
. We may also assume that
\nu
is large relative to
B,C
, for otherwise the estimate is trivial for a suitably large implicit constant.

Given these assumptions, observe that by Stirling’s approximation,

\displaystyle|f(p^{\nu})|

\displaystyle\leq d_{B}(p^{\nu})^{C}=\binom{\nu+B-1}{\nu}^{C}\ll_{B,C}\left(\frac{\sqrt{2\pi(\nu+B-1)}}{2\pi\sqrt{\nu(B-1)}}\left(1+\frac{B-1}{\nu}\right)^{\nu}\cdot\left(1+\frac{\nu}{B-1}\right)^{B-1}\right)^{C}

\displaystyle\ll_{B,C}\left(\frac{5}{4}\right)^{\nu}\left(1+\frac{\nu}{B-1}\right)^{C(B-1)},

provided that
\nu
is large enough that
\left(1+\frac{B-1}{\nu}\right)^{C}\leq\frac{5}{4}
. This proves a).

b) Let
\delta:=\frac{1}{2}-\eta
. For each
2\leq p\leq X
we have
p^{1/2}>5/4
, and thus by a),

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\nu\geq 2:\\
p^{\nu}\leq X\end{subarray}}\frac{|f(p^{\nu})|}{p^{(1-\eta)\nu}}\ll_{B,C}\sum_{\nu\geq 2}\left(1+\frac{\nu}{B-1}\right)^{BC}\left(\frac{5}{4p^{1/2+\delta}}\right)^{\nu}\ll_{B,C,\delta}\sum_{\nu\geq 2}\left(\frac{5}{4p^{(1+\delta)/2}}\right)^{\nu}\ll p^{-1-\delta}.

We deduce b) upon summing over
p\leq X
.

c) We begin by giving an expression for
\Lambda_{f}(p^{\nu})
.

In light of a), we may deduce that there is
\sigma=\sigma(B,C)>1
such that when
\text{Re}(s)\geq\sigma
,

(12)

\left|\sum_{p^{\nu}\leq X}\frac{f(p^{\nu})}{p^{\nu s}}\right|\leq\frac{1}{2}\text{ for all $2\leq p\leq X$.}

It follows from the Euler product representation of
P(s)
that
P(s)\neq 0
in the half-plane
\text{Re}(s)\geq\sigma
. Thus,
-P^{\prime}(s)/P(s)
is also analytic in this half-plane.

Integrating
-P^{\prime}/P
term-by-term from
s
to
\infty
along a line contained in the half-plane
\text{Re}(s)\geq\sigma
, we deduce that

\sum_{n\geq 1}\frac{\Lambda_{f}(n)}{n^{s}\log n}=\log P(s)=\sum_{p\leq X}\log\left(1+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\nu\geq 1\\
p^{\nu}\leq X\end{subarray}}\frac{f(p^{\nu})}{p^{\nu s}}\right).

Given (
12
), we obtain the Taylor expansion

\displaystyle\sum_{n\geq 1}\frac{\Lambda_{f}(n)}{n^{s}\log n}

\displaystyle=\sum_{p\leq X}\sum_{k\geq 1}\frac{(-1)^{k-1}}{k}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\nu_{1},\ldots,\nu_{k}\geq 1\\
p^{\nu_{i}}\leq X\forall i\end{subarray}}\frac{f(p^{\nu_{1}})\cdots f(p^{\nu_{k}})}{p^{(\nu_{1}+\cdots+\nu_{k})s}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}p^{\nu}\\
p\leq X\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{p^{\nu s}\log p^{\nu}}\left(\log p^{\nu}\cdot\sum_{1\leq k\leq\nu}\frac{(-1)^{k-1}}{k}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\nu_{1}+\cdots+\nu_{k}=\nu\\
\nu_{1},\ldots,\nu_{k}\geq 1\\
p^{\nu_{i}}\leq X\forall i\end{subarray}}\prod_{1\leq i\leq k}f(p^{\nu_{i}})\right).

By the identity theorem for Dirichlet series, we thus find that
\Lambda_{f}(n)=0
unless

for some prime power

with

, in which case

(13)

\Lambda_{f}(p^{\nu})=\log p^{\nu}\cdot\sum_{1\leq k\leq\nu}\frac{(-1)^{k-1}}{k}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\nu_{1}+\cdot+\nu_{k}=\nu\\
\nu_{1},\ldots,\nu_{k}\geq 1\\
p^{\nu_{i}}\leq X\forall i\end{subarray}}\prod_{1\leq i\leq k}f(p^{\nu_{i}}).

When
\nu=1
we get the expression
\Lambda_{f}(p)=f(p)\log p
, so that

.

For

and the triangle inequality in (

Proof of Theorem
1.4

Let
f
be a primitive, Hecke-normalized holomorphic cusp form without complex multiplication of fixed even weight
k\geq 2
and level 1. Write the Fourier expansion of
f
at
\infty
as

f(z)=\sum_{n\geq 1}\lambda_{f}(n)n^{\frac{k-1}{2}}e(nz),\quad\quad\text{Im}(z)>0,

where
\{\lambda_{f}(n)\}_{n}
is the sequence of normalized Fourier coefficients with
\lambda_{f}(1)=1
. As noted in Section
1.3.2

\lambda_{f}
is a multiplicative function.
Deligne’s proof of the Ramanujan conjecture for
f
shows that
|\lambda_{f}(p)|\leq 2
and
|\lambda_{f}(n)|\leq d(n)
for all primes
p
and positive integers
n
. Moreover, the quantiative Sato-Tate theorem of Thorner
[
44
]
, which is based on the deep results of Newton and Thorne
[
30
]
, shows that for any
[a,b]\subseteq[-2,2]
,

(26)

|\{p\leq X:\lambda_{f}(p)\in[a,b]\}|=\left(\frac{1}{\pi}\int_{a}^{b}\sqrt{1-(v/2)^{2}}dv\right)\int_{2}^{X}\frac{dt}{\log t}+O\left(\frac{X\log(k\log X)}{(\log X)^{3/2}}\right).

Recall that

c_{\alpha}=\frac{2^{\alpha}}{\sqrt{\pi}}\frac{\Gamma\left(\frac{\alpha+1}{2}\right)}{\Gamma(\alpha/2+2)}.

Using this data, we will prove the following.

Proposition 5.1
.

Let
\alpha>0
. There is a constant
\delta=\delta(\alpha)>0
such that
|\lambda_{f}|^{\alpha}\in\mathcal{M}(X;c_{\alpha},2^{\alpha},2;1/2-\varepsilon,\delta)
.

Using Proposition
5.1
we will be able to apply Theorem
1.7
in order to derive Corollary
1.1
.

We will check that
|\lambda_{f}|^{\alpha}
satisfies the required hypotheses in the following lemmas.

Lemma 5.2
.

For any
2\leq z<w
we have

\sum_{z<p\leq w}\frac{|\lambda_{f}(p)|^{\alpha}}{p}=c_{\alpha}\sum_{z<p\leq w}\frac{1}{p}+O((\log z)^{-1/2+o(1)}).

Similarly, we have

\sum_{p\leq X}\frac{|\lambda_{f}(p)|^{2\alpha}}{p}=c_{2\alpha}\log\log X+O(1).

Proof.

Let
\beta\in\{\alpha,2\alpha\}
. By partial summation,

\sum_{z<p\leq w}\frac{|\lambda_{f}(p)|^{\beta}}{p}=\int_{0}^{2}u^{\beta}d\{\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}z<p\leq w\\
|\lambda_{f}(p)|\leq u\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{p}\}=2^{\beta}\sum_{z<p\leq w}\frac{1}{p}-\beta\int_{0}^{2}\left(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}z<p\leq w\\
|\lambda_{f}(p)|\leq u\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{p}\right)u^{\beta-1}du.

Fix
u\in(0,2]
, and let
I_{u}:=[0,u]\cup[-u,0]
so that
|\lambda_{f}(p)|\leq u
if and only if
\lambda_{f}(p)\in I_{u}
. By partial summation and (
26
),

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}z<p\leq w\\
\lambda_{f}(p)\in I_{u}\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{p}

\displaystyle=\frac{2}{\pi}\int_{0}^{u}\sqrt{1-(v/2)^{2}}dv\cdot\int_{z}^{w}\frac{dy}{y\log y}+O\left(\frac{\log\log z}{(\log z)^{3/2}}+\int_{z}^{w}\frac{\log\log y\,dy}{y(\log y)^{3/2}}\right)

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{2}{\pi}\int_{0}^{u}\sqrt{1-(v/2)^{2}}dv\right)\log(\log w/\log z)+O((\log z)^{-1/2+o(1)}).

Multiplying the main term by
\beta u^{\beta-1}
and integrating in
u
, we obtain
I_{\beta}\log(\log w/\log z)+O(1/\log z)
, where

\displaystyle I_{\beta}

\displaystyle:=\frac{2\beta}{\pi}\int_{0}^{2}u^{\beta-1}\int_{0}^{u}\sqrt{1-(v/2)^{2}}dvdu=\frac{2\beta}{\pi}\int_{0}^{2}\left(\int_{v}^{2}u^{\beta-1}du\right)\sqrt{1-(v/2)^{2}}dv

\displaystyle=\frac{2^{\beta+1}}{\pi}\int_{0}^{2}(1-(v/2)^{\beta})\sqrt{1-(v/2)^{2}}dv=2^{\beta}-\frac{2^{\beta+1}}{\pi}\int_{0}^{2}(v/2)^{\beta}\sqrt{1-(v/2)^{2}}dv.

Making the change of variables
t:=(v/2)^{2}
, we find that

2^{\beta}-I_{\beta}=\frac{2^{\beta+1}}{\pi}\int_{0}^{1}t^{(\beta-1)/2}(1-t)^{1/2}dt=\frac{2^{\beta+1}}{\pi}\frac{\Gamma\left(\frac{\beta+1}{2}\right)\Gamma(3/2)}{\Gamma(\beta/2+2)}=\frac{2^{\beta}}{\sqrt{\pi}}\frac{\Gamma\left(\frac{\beta+1}{2}\right)}{\Gamma(\beta/2+2)}=c_{\beta}.

It follows, therefore, that

\displaystyle\sum_{z<p\leq w}\frac{|\lambda_{f}(p)|^{\alpha}}{p}

\displaystyle=I_{\alpha}\sum_{z<p\leq w}\frac{1}{p}+O((\log z)^{-1/2+o(1)})=c_{\alpha}\sum_{z<p\leq w}\frac{1}{p}+O((\log z)^{-1/2+o(1)}),

\displaystyle\sum_{z<p\leq w}\frac{|\lambda_{f}(p)|^{2\alpha}}{p}

\displaystyle=I_{2\alpha}\sum_{z<p\leq w}\frac{1}{p}+O((\log z)^{-1/2+o(1)})=c_{2\alpha}\sum_{z<p\leq w}\frac{1}{p}+O((\log z)^{-1/2+o(1)}),

and both claims follow.
∎

To obtain uniform lower bound estimates for
\rho(|\lambda_{f}|^{\alpha},n^{it};X)^{2}
we will need some control over the product
|\lambda_{f}(p)|^{\alpha}(1-\cos(t\log p))
, on average over
p
. In some ranges of
t
this is furnished by the following lemma.

Lemma 5.3
.

Let
|t|\geq 1
. There is a constant
c=c(\alpha)>0
such that if
Y\geq(|t|+3)^{2}
and
Y
is sufficiently large then

\sum_{Y<p\leq 2Y}|\lambda_{f}(p)|^{\alpha}|1-p^{it}|^{2}\geq c\frac{Y}{\log Y}.

Proof.

We adapt an argument due to Goldfeld and Li
[
4
, Lem. 12.12 and 12.15]
and Humphries
[
16
, Lem. 2.1]
. Let
\eta\in(0,1/2)
be a parameter to be chosen later. By the prime number theorem for Rankin-Selberg
L
-functions
[
31
, Thm. 2]
we have

\sum_{Y<p\leq 2Y}|\lambda_{f}(p)|^{2}\log p=(1+o(1))Y.

Since
|\lambda_{f}(p)|^{2}\leq 4
for all
p
, invoking the usual prime number theorem we obtain

\displaystyle(1+o(1))Y

\displaystyle\leq\eta^{2}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}Y<p\leq 2Y\\
|\lambda_{f}(p)|\leq\eta\end{subarray}}\log p+4(\log 2Y)|\{Y<p\leq 2Y:|\lambda_{f}(p)|>\eta\}|

\displaystyle\leq(\eta^{2}+o(1))Y+4(\log 2Y)|\{Y<p\leq 2Y:|\lambda_{f}(p)|>\eta\}|,

which rearranges as

(27)

|\{Y<p\leq 2Y:|\lambda_{f}(p)|>\eta\}|\geq\left(\frac{1-\eta^{2}}{4}-o(1)\right)\frac{Y}{\log Y}.

Next, we estimate the cardinality

\displaystyle|\{Y<p\leq 2Y:|1-p^{it}|\leq\eta\}|

\displaystyle=|\{Y<p\leq 2Y:|\sin((t\log p)/2)|\leq\eta/2\}|.

Set
\beta:=\sin^{-1}(\eta/2)/\pi\in[0,1/2]
, Whenever
\sin(t\log p/2)\in[-\eta/2,\eta/2]
there is
m\in\mathbb{Z}
such that
(t\log p)/2\in[\pi(m-\beta),\pi(m+\beta)]
. By Jordan’s inequality,
\beta\leq\frac{1}{2}\sin(\pi\beta)=\frac{\eta}{4}
, and we see that

\displaystyle|\{Y<p\leq 2Y:|1-p^{it}|\leq\eta\}|\leq|\{Y<p\leq 2Y:\|(t\log p)/(2\pi)\|\leq\eta/4\}|,

where
\|t\|:=\min_{m\in\mathbb{Z}}|t-m|
. Splitting up the primes
Y<p\leq 2Y
according to the nearest integer
m
to
(t\log p)/(2\pi)
, the latter may be bounded above as

\leq\sum_{\frac{|t|\log Y+\eta/4}{2\pi}\leq m\leq\frac{|t|\log(2Y)-\eta/4}{2\pi}}\left(\pi\left(e^{\frac{2\pi m+\eta/4}{|t|}}\right)-\pi\left(e^{\frac{2\pi m-\eta/4}{|t|}}\right)\right).

For each
m
we have
e^{\frac{2\pi m+\eta/4}{|t|}}\leq\left(1+\frac{\eta}{2|t|}\right)e^{\frac{2\pi m-\eta/4}{|t|}}
.
By the Brun-Titchmarsh theorem we get, uniformly over all
m
in the sum,

\pi\left(e^{\frac{2\pi m+\eta/4}{|t|}}\right)-\pi\left(e^{\frac{2\pi m-\eta/4}{|t|}}\right)\leq\frac{\eta}{|t|}\frac{e^{2\pi(m-\eta/4)/|t|}}{2\pi(m-\eta/4)/|t|-\log(|t|/\eta)}\leq\eta\frac{2Y}{|t|(\log Y-(\log Y)/2)}\leq 8\eta\frac{Y}{|t|\log Y},

for
Y
large enough. Since there are
\leq 1+|t|(\log(2Y)-\log Y)/(2\pi)\leq 2|t|
integers in the range of summation, we obtain

(28)

|\{Y<p\leq 2Y:|1-p^{it}|\leq\eta\}|\leq 16\eta\frac{Y}{\log Y}.

We deduce from (
27
) and (
28
) that

\displaystyle|\{Y<p\leq 2Y:|\lambda_{f}(p)|>\eta\text{ and }|1-p^{it}|>\eta\}|

\displaystyle\geq|\{Y<p\leq 2Y:|\lambda_{f}(p)|>\eta\}|-|\{Y<p\leq 2Y:|1-p^{it}|\leq\eta\}|

\displaystyle\geq\max\left\{0,\frac{1-64\eta-\eta^{2}}{4}-o(1)\right\}\cdot\frac{Y}{\log Y}.

Selecting
\eta=\frac{1}{128}
, we get that if
Y
is sufficiently large,

|\{Y<p\leq 2Y:|\lambda_{f}(p)|>\eta\text{ and }|1-p^{it}|>\eta\}|\geq\frac{Y}{10\log Y}.

Finally, we obtain that

\sum_{Y<p\leq 2Y}|\lambda_{f}(p)|^{\alpha}|1-p^{it}|^{2}>\eta^{2+\alpha}|\{Y<p\leq 2Y:|\lambda_{f}(p)|>\eta\text{ and }|1-p^{it}|>\eta\}|\geq\frac{\eta^{2+\alpha}}{10}\cdot\frac{Y}{\log Y},

which proves the claim with
c:=2^{-10(2+\alpha)}
.
∎

We now obtain lower bounds for
\rho(|\lambda_{f}|^{\alpha},n^{it};X)^{2}
for all
|t|\leq X
.

Lemma 5.4
.

There is a
\delta=\delta(\alpha)>0
such that whenever
|t|\leq X
we have

\sum_{p\leq X}\frac{|\lambda_{f}(p)|^{\alpha}(1-\cos(t\log p))}{p}\geq\delta\min\{\log\log X,\log(1+|t|\log X)\}-O(1).

Moreover, we may select
t_{0}(|\lambda_{f}|^{2},X)=0
.

Proof.

We may assume that
X
is sufficiently large, else the estimate given is trivial. When
|t|\leq 1/\log X
the claim is vacuous. Thus, we may focus on the case
1/\log X<|t|\leq X
. We consider the ranges
1/\log X<|t|\leq 1
,
1<|t|\leq\log X
and
\log X<|t|\leq X
separately.
Throughout we will introduce an auxiliary parameter
2\leq Y\leq X
, chosen case by case, and use the inequality

\sum_{p\leq X}\frac{|\lambda_{f}(p)|^{\alpha}(1-\cos(t\log p)}{p}\geq\sum_{Y\leq p\leq X}\frac{|\lambda_{f}(p)|^{\alpha}(1-\cos(t\log p))}{p}.

Suppose first that
1/\log X<|t|\leq 1
. Let
Y:=e^{1/|t|}
and write

R(X):=\sum_{p\leq X}|\lambda_{f}(p)|^{\alpha}-c_{\alpha}\pi(X),

so that by arguments analogous to those of Lemma
5.2
,
R(X)\ll X/(\log X)^{3/2-o(1)}
. By partial summation and Lemma
5.2
,

\displaystyle\sum_{Y\leq p\leq X}\frac{|\lambda_{f}(p)|^{\alpha}\cos(t\log p)}{p}

\displaystyle=c_{\alpha}\int_{Y}^{X}\frac{\cos(t\log u)}{u}\frac{du}{\log u}+O\left(\frac{1}{\log Y}\right)-\int_{Y}^{X}R(u)\left(\cos(t\log u)-t\sin(t\log u)\right)\frac{du}{u^{2}}

\displaystyle=c_{\alpha}\int_{1}^{|t|\log X}\cos(tv/|t|)\frac{dv}{v}+O\left((1+|t|)(\log Y)^{-1/2+o(1)}\right)\ll 1,

the bound in the last step arising from setting
v:=|t|\log u
and integrating by parts. Thus, in light of Lemma
5.2

\sum_{Y<p\leq X}\frac{|\lambda_{f}(p)|^{\alpha}(1-\cos(t\log p))}{p}=c_{\alpha}\log(1+|t|\log X)-O(1).

Next, we consider the intermediate range
1<|t|\leq\log X
. Here, we set
Y:=(10\log X)^{2}
, employ a dyadic decomposition and apply Lemma
5.3
to obtain, when
X
is large enough,

\displaystyle\sum_{p\leq X}\frac{|\lambda_{f}(p)|^{\alpha}(1-\cos(t\log p))}{p}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{2}\sum_{Y<2^{j}\leq X/2}2^{-(j+1)}\sum_{2^{j}<p\leq 2^{j+1}}|\lambda_{f}(p)|^{\alpha}|1-p^{it}|^{2}\geq\frac{c}{4\log 2}\sum_{Y<2^{j}\leq X/2}\frac{1}{j}

\displaystyle=\frac{c}{4\log 2}\log\left(\frac{\log X}{\log Y}\right)-O(1)\geq\frac{c}{8\log 2}\log\log X-O(1),

for some
c=c(\alpha)>0
.

Finally, assume that
\log X\leq|t|\leq X
. In this case, put
Y:=\exp\left((\log X)^{2/3+\varepsilon}\right)
, where
\varepsilon>0
is small. Let
m\geq 1
be an integer parameter to be chosen later. By Hölder’s inequality,

\displaystyle\left|\sum_{Y<p\leq X}\frac{|\lambda_{f}(p)|^{\alpha}\cos(t\log p)}{p}\right|\leq\left(\sum_{Y<p\leq X}\frac{|\lambda_{f}(p)|^{\frac{2\alpha m}{2m-1}}}{p}\right)^{1-1/2m}\cdot\left(\sum_{Y<p\leq X}\frac{\cos(t\log p)^{2m}}{p}\right)^{1/2m}.

We can bound
|\lambda_{f}(p)|^{2\alpha m/(2m-1)}\leq|\lambda_{f}(p)|^{\alpha}2^{\alpha/(2m-1)}
, so that by Lemma
5.2
the first sum is

\leq 2^{\alpha/(2m)}c_{\alpha}^{1-1/(2m)}(\log(\log X/\log Y))^{1-1/2m}+O_{m}(1).

Now, we can write

\cos(t\log p)^{2m}=2^{-2m}(p^{it}+p^{-it})^{2m}=2^{-2m}\binom{2m}{m}+2^{-2m}\sum_{0\leq j\leq m-1}\binom{2m}{j}(p^{2i(m-j)t}+p^{-2i(m-j)t}).

By the zero-free region of the Riemann zeta function (see e.g.,
[
25
, Lem. 2]
) we have

\max_{1\leq|l|\leq m}\left|\sum_{Y<p\leq X}\frac{1}{p^{1+ilt}}\right|\ll\frac{\log X}{1+|t|}+(\log X)^{-10}\ll 1.

It follows that

\sum_{Y<p\leq X}\frac{\cos(t\log p)^{2m}}{p}=2^{-2m}\binom{2m}{m}\log(\log X/\log Y)+O_{m}(1),

and therefore in sum we have

\left|\sum_{Y<p\leq X}\frac{|\lambda_{f}(p)|^{\alpha}\cos(t\log p)}{p}\right|\leq c_{\alpha}\left(\frac{2^{\alpha}}{c_{\alpha}}\right)^{1/(2m)}\left(2^{-2m}\binom{2m}{m}\right)^{1/2m}\log\left(\frac{\log X}{\log Y}\right)+O_{m}(1).

Using the bounds
\sqrt{2\pi n}(n/e)^{n}\leq n!\leq 2\sqrt{2\pi n}(n/e)^{n}
, valid for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
(see e.g.,
[
32
]
), we get that

\left(\frac{2^{\alpha}}{c_{\alpha}}\right)^{1/(2m)}\left(2^{-2m}\binom{2m}{m}\right)^{1/(2m)}\leq\left(\frac{2^{\alpha+1}}{c_{\alpha}\sqrt{\pi m}}\right)^{1/(2m)},

and thus taking

, we obtain

\left|\sum_{Y<p\leq X}\frac{|\lambda_{f}(p)|^{\alpha}\cos(t\log p)}{p}\right|\leq 2^{-1/(2m)}c_{\alpha}\log(\log X/\log Y)+O(1),

for
\eta\in(0,1)
. We thus obtain in this case that

\sum_{p\leq X}\frac{|\lambda_{f}(p)|^{\alpha}(1-\cos(t\log p))}{p}\geq c_{\alpha}(1-2^{-1/(2m)})\left(\frac{1}{3}-\varepsilon\right)\log\log X-O(1).

Combining our estimates from each of these ranges and putting
\delta:=c_{\alpha}\min\{c/(8\log 2),\frac{1}{4}(1-2^{-1/(2m)})\}

\sum_{p\leq X}\frac{|\lambda_{f}(p)|^{\alpha}(1-\cos(t\log p))}{p}\geq\delta\min\{\log\log X,\log(1+|t|\log X)\}-O(1).

This completes the proof of the lower bound.

Finally, note that
\rho(|\lambda_{f}|^{\alpha},1;X)=0

is a minimizer of

. This completes the proof.

\displaystyle\ll\left(\left(\frac{\log\log h_{0}}{\log h_{0}}\right)^{c_{\alpha}}+\frac{\log\log X}{(\log X)^{\theta}}\right)(\log X)^{2(c_{\alpha}-1)},

where

where

.
Thus, changing

by a constant factor, we deduce that

∎

Lemma 5.6

for some absolute constant

The second follows immediately from the first statement by partial summation.

5.3.
Proof of Corollary
1.6

Recall that

\Delta(n):=\max_{u\in\mathbb{R}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}d|n\\
e^{u}\leq d<e^{u+1}\end{subarray}}1.

Given
\theta\in\mathbb{R}
, write also

d(n,\theta):=\sum_{d|n}d^{i\theta}.

This is clearly a multiplicative function, with
d(n)=d(n,0)
. In probabilistic terms, it is also
d(n)
times the characteristic function of the distribution function

\mathcal{D}_{n}(v):=\frac{1}{d(n)}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}d|n\\
d\leq e^{v}\end{subarray}}1,\quad v\in\mathbb{R}.

introduced in Section
1.3
.
The following general bounds for concentration functions in terms of characteristic functions allow us to relate
\Delta(n)
with integral averages of
d(n,\theta)
.

Lemma 5.7
.

There are constants
c_{2}>c_{1}>0
such that, uniformly in
n\in\mathbb{N}
,

c_{1}\frac{1}{d(n)}\int_{0}^{1}|d(n,\theta)|^{2}d\theta\leq\Delta(n)\leq c_{2}\int_{0}^{1}|d(n,\theta)|d\theta.

Proof.

This is a special case of
[
12
, Lem. 30.2]
.
∎

By Lemma
5.7
we find that for any
x\in[X/2,X]
and
10\leq h\leq X
,

(29)

\sum_{x-h<n\leq x}\Delta(n)\gg\int_{0}^{1}\sum_{x-h<n\leq x}\frac{|d(n,\theta)|^{2}}{d(n)}d\theta.

For
\theta\in\mathbb{R}
and
n\in\mathbb{N}
we write
f_{\theta}(n):=|d(n,\theta)|^{2}/d(n)
.

Corollary 5.8
.

Let
\theta\in(1/\log X,1]
. Let
10\leq h_{0}\leq X/\log X
and put
h:=h_{0}(\theta^{-1}\log X)^{1/2}
. Then there is a constant
\kappa_{1,2}>0
such that for any
0<\kappa<\kappa_{1,2}
,

\frac{2}{X}\int_{X/2}^{X}\left|\frac{1}{h}\sum_{x-h<n\leq x}f_{\theta}(n)-\frac{2}{X}\sum_{X/2<n\leq X}f_{\theta}(n)\right|^{2}dx\ll\left(\frac{\log\log h_{0}}{\log h_{0}}+\frac{\log\log X}{(\log X)^{\kappa}}\right),

The implicit constant is independent of
\theta
.

To this end, we prove that
f_{\theta}\in\mathcal{M}(X;1,2,1)
for all
1/\log X<\theta\leq 1
, which is the purpose of the following lemmas.

Lemma 5.9
.

Let
\theta\in(0,1]
, and let
\beta:=\min\{1/\theta,\log X\}
. Then

\sum_{p\leq X}\frac{f_{\theta}(p)}{p}=\log(\beta\log X)+O(1).

Similarly,

H(f_{\theta};X)\asymp(\beta\log X)^{1/2}.

Proof.

Observe that for each
p
,

f_{\theta}(p)=\frac{1}{2}|1+p^{i\theta}|^{2}=1+\cos(\theta\log p).

Put
Y:=\min\{X,\exp(1/\theta)\}=e^{\beta}
. For
p\leq Y
we have
\cos(\theta\log p)=1+O(\theta^{2}(\log p)^{2})
, so that by the prime number theorem,

\sum_{p\leq Y}\frac{f_{\theta}(p)}{p}=\sum_{p\leq Y}\frac{2}{p}+O\left(\theta^{2}\sum_{p\leq e^{1/\theta}}\frac{(\log p)^{2}}{p}\right)=2\log(\min\{1/\theta,\log X\})+O(1).

This proves the first claim if
0\leq\theta\leq 1/\log X
, so assume now that
1/\log X<\theta\leq 1
. By partial summation and the prime number theorem, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{Y<p\leq X}\frac{1+\cos(\theta\log p)}{p}=\int_{1}^{\theta\log X}(1+\cos v)\frac{dv}{v}+O(1)

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi}(1+\cos u)du\right)\log(\theta\log X)+O(1)

\displaystyle=\log(\theta\log X)+O(1).

We thus deduce that

\sum_{p\leq X}\frac{f_{\theta}(p)}{p}=2\log(1/\theta)+\log(\theta\log X)=\log(\theta^{-1}\log X),

and the first claim follows for all
\frac{1}{\log X}<\theta\leq 1
as well.

For the second claim, we simply note that

H(f_{\theta};X)\asymp\exp\left(\sum_{p\leq X}\frac{(f_{\theta}(p)-1)^{2}}{p}\right)=\exp\left(\sum_{p\leq X}\frac{\cos(\theta\log p)^{2}}{p}\right).

A similar partial summation argument shows that

\displaystyle\sum_{p\leq X}\frac{\cos(\theta\log p)^{2}}{p}

\displaystyle=\log(\min\{1/\theta,\log X\})+\left(\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi}(\cos u)^{2}du\right)\log(1+\theta\log X)+O(1)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\log(\min\{\log X,1/\theta\}\log X)+O(1),

and the claim follows.
∎

Lemma 5.10
.

Let
\theta\in(0,1]
. Let
2\leq z\leq w\leq X
. Then

\sum_{z<p\leq w}\frac{f_{\theta}(p)}{p}\geq\sum_{z<p\leq w}\frac{1}{p}+O(1/\log z).

Proof.

Set
Y:=\min\{X,e^{1/\theta}\}
once again. If
w\leq Y
then
\cos(\theta\log p)\geq\cos(1)\geq 0
for all
z<p\leq w
, and thus

\sum_{z<p\leq w}\frac{f_{\theta}(p)}{p}\geq\sum_{z<p\leq w}\frac{1}{p}+O(1/\log z).

On the other hand, if
Y\leq z
(so that
\theta>1/\log X
) then by the same partial summation argument as in Lemma
5.9
we find that

\sum_{z<p\leq w}\frac{f_{\theta}(p)}{p}=\left(\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi}(1+\cos u)du\right)\log(\log w/\log z)+O(1/\log z)=\sum_{z<p\leq w}\frac{1}{p}+O(1/\log z).

Finally, suppose
z<Y<w
. In this case, we split the interval into the segments
(z,Y]
and
(Y,w]
and apply the arguments in each of the previous two cases to obtain

\sum_{z<p\leq w}\frac{f_{\theta}(p)}{p}\geq\sum_{z<p\leq Y}\frac{1}{p}+\sum_{Y<p\leq w}\frac{1}{p}+O(1/\log z)\geq\sum_{z<p\leq w}\frac{1}{p}+O(1/\log z),

as claimed.
∎

Proof of Corollary
5.8
.

Let
h=h_{0}(\theta^{-1}\log X)
. Note that
|d(n;\theta)|^{2}/d(n)\leq d(n)
uniformly over

for all
\theta\in(1/\log X,1]

. Since

∎

Proof of Corollary

\frac{2}{X}\sum_{X/2<n\leq X}\frac{|d(n,\theta)|^{2}}{d(n)}\geq\frac{2}{X}\sum_{X/2<n\leq X}\frac{\mu^{2}(n)|d(n,\theta)|^{2}}{d(n)}=|\zeta(1+i\theta)|H_{\theta}(1)+O(|\theta^{3/2}|/\sqrt{\log X}),

(31)

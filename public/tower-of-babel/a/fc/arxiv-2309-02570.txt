Time consistency of dynamic risk measures and dynamic performance measures generated by distortion functions
Tomasz R. Bielecki, Igor Cialenco, Hao Liu

Abstract




Abstract
:

The aim of this work is to study risk measures generated by distortion functions in a dynamic discrete time setup, and to investigate the corresponding dynamic coherent acceptability indices (DCAIs) generated by families of such risk measures. First we show that conditional version of Choquet integrals indeed are dynamic coherent risk measures (DCRMs), and also introduce the class of dynamic weighted value at risk measures. We prove that these two classes of risk measures coincides. In the spirit of robust representations theorem for DCAIs, we establish some relevant properties of families of DCRMs generated by distortion functions, and then define and study the corresponding DCAIs. Second, we study the time consistency of DCRMs and DCAIs generated by distortion functions. In particular, we prove that such DCRMs are sub-martingale time consistent, but they are not super-martingale time consistent. We also show that DCRMs generated by distortion functions are not weakly acceptance time consistent. We also present several widely used classes of distortion functions and derive some new representations of these distortions.

This manuscript is also complimented with a technical appendix, where we collect a series of results and proofs. In particular, we prove that the dynamic risk measures generated by regular distortion functions are indeed dynamic coherent risk measures. Similarly, we provide the detailed proof that the acceptability indices generated by distortion functions are dynamic coherent acceptability indices. We also present some properties of conditional quantiles, conditional
\var
and conditional
\avar
.

Keywords:

dynamic risk measures, dynamic acceptability indices, time consistency, distortion functions, weighted value at risk, average value at risk, low invariant risk measures, MINVAR, MAXVAR, MINMAXVAR, MAXMINVAR, Choquet integral

Introduction

The aim of this paper is to study various types of time consistency of a class of Dynamic Coherent Risk Measures (DCRMs) that are generated by distortion functions, as well as time consistency of the corresponding Dynamic Coherent Acceptability Indices (DCAIs).

Risk and performance measures have been staying at the core of finance and insurance industries, successfully used as tools for computing the regulatory capital requirement, in wealth management, as well as in pricing and hedging complex derivatives through a nonlinear setup. In the seminal paper by Artzner et al
[
ADEH99
]
, the authors proposed a systematic approach in studying coherent risk measures (CRM), in a static (one period time) framework, as real valued functions acting on random P&Ls, that satisfies a set of desired axioms. Namely, with
L^{\infty}
denoting the space of (essentially) bounded random variables on some probability space
(\Omega,\mathscr{F},\mathbb{P})
,
a static coherent risk measure is a function
\rho:L^{\infty}\rightarrow[-\infty,\infty]
that is monotone decreasing, cash-additive, sub-additive and positive homogeneous (see Section
3
for details). It was shown that such functions admit different representations, usually derived in the context of convex analysis and duality theory. There exists a vast literature dedicated to extensions of coherent risk measures theory to various degrees.

In the static setup, the natural pathway was to impose a different, usually smaller, set of axioms or to considering a larger spaces on which these functions are defined. We refer the reader to
[
DK13
]
and references therein for a detailed overview of general theory of risk measures (convex, monetary, quasi-convex, etc). One important class of coherent risk measures consists of the coherent risk measures generated by distortion functions, introduced in
[
FS04
]
, that take the form

\rho^{\psi}(X)=-\int_{\mathbb{R}}y\dif\psi(\mathbb{P}(X\leq y)),

(1.1)

where the distortion function
\psi:[0,1]\rightarrow[0,1]
is non-decreasing and
\psi(0)=0
,
\psi(1)=1
. The name distortion is apparent - the function
\psi
distorts the tails of the distribution of the random variable
X
, which in the context of risk management corresponds to distorting of the distribution of the large or extreme losses and gains. Clearly, if
\psi(x)=x
, then
\rho^{\psi}
is the negative of the (linear) expectation, hence no distortions. Respectively,
\rho^{\psi}_{t}
with any other distortion function will actually distort the distribution of the P&L tails. We also note that from mathematical point of view
\rho^{\psi}
is closely related to Choquet integrals. The general structure of this class of risk measures is well studied in the static case, originated with the eminent Kusoka result on law-invariant convex risk measures
[
Kus01
]
; see also
[
Sha13
]
and
[
FS04
, Chapter 4]
. In particular, it was proved that the class of (static) law-invariant, comonotone coherent risk measures coincides with the class of coherent risk measures generated by distortion functions. Moreover, this class of risk measures corresponds to the so-called weighted value of risk (
\wvar
) measures; see, for instance,
[
Che06
]
for a comprehensive study of these measures in static case and their applications to risk management. We also mention
[
BPV22
]
for a discussion of distortion risk measures in the context of model uncertainty. Using a similar axiomatic approach, in
[
CM09
]
the authors introduce a class of performance measures, called Coherent Acceptability Indices (CAI), as functions
\alpha:L^{\infty}\to[0,+\infty]
, that are monotone increasing, scale invariant and quasi-concave. Under some technical continuity assumptions, it can be shown that any CAI
\alpha
can be characterized by a family of CRMs
\{\bar{\rho}^{x},\ x\geq 0\}
. In particular, each
\bar{\rho}^{x}
can be generated by a distortion function. Applications of these types of performance measures go beyond the risk management, and were successfully applied, for example, in portfolio management and pricing of derivatives (cf.
[
MS16
]
and references therein).

Another significant avenue of research is focused on extending the risk and performance measures to a dynamic setup, either in discrete or continuous time framework. Naturally, a dynamic risk or performance measure should take into account the flow of information, which can be achieved by considering the conditional (in probability sense) versions of the corresponding properties such as monotonicity, positive homogeneity, cash-additivity, scale invariance, quasi-concavity, etc. Alternatively, one can ‘condition’ at each time
t
a given representation of a CRM or CAI, and then study its properties. However, we emphasize that a dynamic risk measure is not just a sequence of conditional static risk measures adapted to the underlying filtration. Additionally, one must address the issue of measuring riskiness and/or holding preferences consistently over time. This is typically achieved by imposing an additional axiom called time consistency. There is an extensive literature on DCRMs (see survey
[
AP11
]
), and more recently on DCAIs, as well as time consistency in decision making in general. With very few exceptions, for dynamic risk measures, the strong form of time consistency was invoked: if
\rho_{t+1}(X)=\rho_{t+1}(Y)
, then
\rho_{t}(X)=\rho_{t}(Y)
.
In addition to admitting a reasonable economic interpretation, using properties of DCRM, this form of time consistency can be written as a recursive relationship
\rho_{t}(X)=\rho_{t}(-\rho_{t+1}(X))
, which can be conveniently used in stochastic control problems with DCRM criteria to derive the Bellman equations, or to link dynamic risk measures to BSDEs or BS
\Delta
Es. On the other hand, there are many other forms of time consistency, some suitable for some classes of measures or applications, while others in principle can not be satisfied by some risk or performance measures. For example, as shown in
[
BCZ14
,
BCDK16
]
, a DCAI can never be strong time consistent. From decision making point of view, time consistency is at the heart of the inter-temporal problems. We refer the readers to the survey
[
BCP17
]
, where the authors give a comprehensive study of various types of time consistency for both dynamic risk and performance measures, in a discrete time setup, based on the unified approach to time-consistency introduced in
[
BCP18
]
.

With this at hand, one may ask: what is the dynamic counterpart of
\rho^{\psi}
, and of DCAI generated by distortion functions? What forms of time consistency do these measures satisfy? This paper is the first attempt to investigate these questions. In Section
2
we start introducing the notations, and give some preliminary results about distortion functions. In Section
3
, we define and study DCRMs and DCAIs generated by distortion functions. It is tempting, to use representation (
1.1
), and define the dynamic
\rho^{\psi}
as follows. Let
(\Omega,\mathscr{F},\{\mathscr{F}_{t}\}_{t\in\mathcal{T}},\mathbb{P})
be a filtered probability space, where the increasing collection of
\sigma
-algebras
\mathscr{F}_{t}
,
t\in\mathcal{T}
, models the flow of information accumulated through time, and take
X\in L^{\infty}(\mathscr{F}_{T})
, that models the terminal P&L of a given investment. Denote by
\bar{L}^{0}(\mathscr{F}_{t})
the set of all
\mathscr{F}_{t}
-measurable random variables taking values in
[-\infty,+\infty]
. Define the risk measure
\rho_{t}^{\psi}:L^{\infty}(\mathscr{F}_{T})\to\bar{L}^{0}(\mathscr{F}_{t})
, as

\rho_{t}^{\psi}(X)=-\int_{\mathbb{R}}y\dif\psi(\mathbb{P}(X\leq y\;|\;\mathscr{F}_{t})).

(1.2)

We prove that
\rho^{\psi}_{t}
indeed satisfies the axioms of DCRM. Also here we extend the notion of Weighted Value at Risk (
\wvar
) to the dynamic setup, and establish the correspondence between DCRM generated by distortions and the dynamic
\wvar
. Also here, we present the definition and properties of DCAIs generated by families of distortion functions. Section
3.1
is dedicated to four important examples of distortion functions widely used in risk management, MINVAR, MAXVAR, MINMAXVAR and MAXMINVAR
[
CM09
]
. For distortion functions corresponding to MINVAR and MAXVAR we derive some new representations. Time consistency - the central topic of this paper - is investigated in Section
4
. We first note that thanks to
[
KS09
]
, for any
\psi
different from identity,
\rho^{\psi}
is not strongly time consistent, and hence majority of existing theoretical results on DCRM do not apply. We prove that
\rho^{\psi}
is sub-martingale time consistent, but it is not super-martingale time consistent (see Section
4.1.3
). Moreover, we show that DCRMs generated by distortion functions are not even weakly acceptance time consistent; Section
4.1.4
. In Section
4.2
we study the time consistency of DCAIs. We collect in the Appendix
A
some auxiliary definitions and results.

Finally we note that for the sake of brevity, the proofs of some results are deferred to the Supplement
B
. Although these results are new and important, in the authors opinions their proofs are standard, albeit lengthy and sometimes technical, and hence presented as a technical online supplement.

Preliminaries

Let
T
be a fixed and finite time horizon, and let
\mathcal{T}:=\{0,1,...,T\}
. We consider a filtered probability space
(\Omega,\mathscr{F},\{\mathscr{F}_{t}\}_{t\in\mathcal{T}},\mathbb{P})
, with
\mathscr{F}_{0}=\{\emptyset,\Omega\}
and
\mathscr{F}=\mathscr{F}_{T}
. Throughout, we will use the notations
L^{\infty}:=L^{\infty}(\Omega,\mathscr{F},\mathbb{P})
,
L^{\infty}_{t}:=L^{\infty}(\Omega,\mathscr{F}_{t},\mathbb{P})
,
t\in\mathcal{T}
, and
L^{\infty}_{t,+}
the set of all non-negative random variables in
L^{\infty}_{t}
, for
t\in\mathcal{T}
. As usual, all equalities and inequalities will be understood in
\mathbb{P}
-almost surely sense unless otherwise stated. The set of all probability measures on
[0,1]
is denoted by
\mathcal{P}[0,1]
.

We recall that for any real-valued random variable
X:(\Omega,\mathscr{F})\rightarrow(\mathbb{R},\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{R}))
there exists a
regular conditional distribution
of
X
given the
\sigma
-algebra
\mathscr{F}_{t}
. That is, there exits a null set
N^{X}_{t}\in\mathscr{F}_{t}
, such that for any
\omega\in\Omega\backslash N^{X}_{t}
and any
B\in\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{R})
, we have that
\mathbb{P}(X\in B|\mathscr{F}_{t})(\omega)
is a distribution function (cf.
[
Kle13
, Theorem 8.29]
). In what follows, conditional probabilities will be understood in this sense, and since the set
N^{X}:={\cup}_{t\in\mathcal{T}}N^{X}_{t}
is also a null set, and we will use it conveniently instead of
N_{t}
, for all
t\in\mathcal{T}
.

Definition 2.1
.

A non-decreasing mapping
\psi:[0,1]\rightarrow[0,1]
is called a
distortion function
if
\psi(0)=0
,
\psi(1)=1
.
A distortion function is
regular
if it is concave and continuous. The set of regular distortion functions will be denoted by
\Upsilon
.

Next result gives a connection between concave and continuous distortion functions, further clarifying the notion of regular distortion function.

Lemma 2.2
.

For any concave distortion function
\psi
except the identity function, we have that
\psi(x)>x
for any
x\in(0,1)
. Moreover, any concave distortion
\psi
is continuous on
(0,1]
.

The proof of Lemma
2.2
is deferred to the Supplement
B
.

In view of this result, a concave distortion function which is also continuous at
x=0
is regular. Unless otherwise stated,
in this work we consider only regular distortion functions.

We will be also using a particular type of distortion functions given by

\psi_{\mu}(y):=\int_{[0,y]}\int_{(z,1]}\frac{1}{s}\mu(\dif s)\dif z,\quad y\in[0,1],

(2.1)

for some
\mu\in\mathcal{P}[0,1]
. We refer the reader to
[
Che06
,
FS04
]
for properties of this class of distortions.

Remark 2.3
.

It can be shown (cf.
[
FS04
, Lemma 4.63]
) that (
2.1
) establishes a one-to-one correspondence between probability measures
\mu\in\mathcal{P}[0,1]
and concave distortion functions
\psi
. Moreover,
\psi_{\mu}
is continuous on
[0,1]
if and only if
\mu(\{0\})=0
. Given a concave distortion function
\psi
, the probability measure
\mu
such that
\psi_{\mu}=\psi
has the induced distribution function
F
on
\left([0,1],\mathcal{B}([0,1])\right)
given by

\displaystyle\begin{split}F(y)=\begin{cases}0,&y=0\\
\psi(y)-y\left(\psi_{+}\right)^{\prime}(y),&0<y<1\\
1,&y=1,\end{cases}\end{split}

(2.2)

where
\psi_{+}^{\prime}
is the right derivative. In particular, if
\left(\psi_{+}\right)^{\prime}(1-)>0
,
F
is discontinuous at point
1
with jump size
\left(\psi_{+}\right)^{\prime}(1-)
.

3
DCRM and DCAI generated by distortion functions

In this section we will introduce classes of dynamic risk measures and dynamic performance measures generated by distortion functions, and investigate various convenient representations of such measures.

In the context of risk and performance measures, the elements
X\in L^{\infty}
should be viewed as terminal discounted Profit and Losses (P&L) of a financial position or portfolio.
A risk measure is meant to determine the riskiness of such positions, measured in the same currency units as
X
.

Definition 3.1
.

A
Dynamic Coherent Risk Measure
(DCRM) is a function
\rho:\mathcal{T}\times L^{\infty}\times\Omega\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
that is adapted, normalized, local, cash-additive, monotone decreasing, sub-additive and positive homogeneous (see Appendix
A
for details).

Usually, an additional property called time consistency is imposed on DCRMs. The importance of time consistency property is imperative - it relates the agent’s preferences through time in a consistent way. One of the main goals of this paper is to investigate this property for a large class of DCRMs and DCAIs; see Section
4
. We refer the reader to
[
AP11
]
for a servey of time consistency of DCRMs, and more recently to the survey
[
BCP17
]
, where the authors give a comprehensive study of various types of time consistency for both dynamic risk measures and performance measures, in a discrete time setup.

Since the pioneering works
[
ADEH97
,
ADEH99
]
, considerable body of literature was devoted to describe DCRMs and other similar class of functions satisfying certain set of desired axioms under names of convex risk measures, monetary risk measures, acceptability indices, as well as their dynamic counterparts; cf.
[
DK13
,
BCDK16
]
for some general results an literature review. Standard results in this field would be dual representations sometimes also called robust representations. For example, it can be proved that in a static setup,
\mathcal{T}=\{0,1\}
, a coherent risk measure admits the following representation

\displaystyle\rho(X)=-\inf_{\mathbb{Q}\in\mathcal{Q}}\mathbb{E}_{\mathbb{Q}}(X),

(3.1)

where
\mathcal{Q}
is a nonempty set of probability measures, absolutely continuous with respect to
\mathbb{P}
. Different sets
\mathcal{Q}
would generated different risk measures. The robust representation (
3.1
), in addition to being useful for theoretical developments, also gives alternative interpretations of risk measures. For example, the set of probabilities
\mathcal{Q}
can be viewed as plausible scenarios, in which case, the value of
\rho(X)
is equal to the worst expected P&L under possible scenarios. On the other hand, using (
3.1
) for efficient computations could be challenging, unless the extreme measure
\mathbb{Q}
is known. In many applications, one would usually use risk measures given by explicit analytical formulas. One large and important class of such risk measures are DCRMs generated by distortion functions, given by

\rho^{\psi}(X)=-\int_{\mathbb{R}}y\dif\psi(\mathbb{P}(X\leq y)).

(3.2)

It is clear that
\rho^{\psi}_{t}
with
\psi(x)=x
becomes the negative of (linear) expectation, and for any other
\psi\in\Upsilon
the distribution of the tails of the random variable
X
will be distorted, which in the context of risk management corresponds to distorting distribution of the large or extreme losses and gains. The general structure of this class of risk measures
in the static setup
is well studied, originated with the eminent Kusoka result on law-invariant convex risk measures
[
Kus01
]
; see also
[
Sha13
]
and
[
FS04
, Chapter 4]
. In particular, it was proved that the class of (static) law-invariant, comonotone coherent risk measures coincides with the class of coherent risk measures generated by distortion functions. Moreover, it is known that this class of risk measures corresponds to the so-called weighted value of risk (
\wvar
) measures; see, for instance,
[
Che06
]
for a comprehensive study of these measures in static case and their applications to risk management. However, to the best of our knowledge, the dynamic counterpart of (
3.2
) is not formally studied, and first goal of this paper is to fill this gap.

Next, we introduce the key object in this paper, that, as seen below, corresponds to the dynamic version of (
1.2
). For any
\psi\in\Upsilon,X\in L^{\infty}
and
t\in\mathcal{T}
, we define

\displaystyle\rho_{t}^{\psi}(X):=\int_{[0,\infty)}\psi\left(\mathbb{P}(-X>y\;|\;\mathscr{F}_{t})\right)\dif y+\int_{(-\infty,0)}\left[\psi(\mathbb{P}(-X>y\;|\;\mathscr{F}_{t}))-1\right]\dif y,

(3.3)

which can be viewed as the conditional Choquet integral
[
FS04
, Section 4.6]
.

Proposition 3.2
.

For any
\psi\in\Upsilon
, the mapping
\rho^{\psi}
is a law-invariant DCRM.

Proof.

Law-invariance of
\rho_{t}^{\psi}
is clear.
Verification of properties of DCRM follow by somewhat standard but lengthy arguments. The main technical difficulties are related to the measurability issues and giving sense to conditional quantities through regular conditional distributions. The detailed proof is deferred to the Supplement
B

\rho^{\psi}
the
DCRM generated by the distortion function

\psi

\rho_{t}^{\psi}(X)=-\int_{\mathbb{R}}y\dif\psi(\mathbb{P}(X\leq y\;|\;\mathscr{F}_{t})).

(3.4)

Here we briefly extend some of the relevant results to the dynamic setup.

Definition 3.3
.

Given a probability measure
\mu\in\mathcal{P}[0,1]
, the
dynamic weighted value at risk
(dWV@R) is defined as

\displaystyle\dwvar^{\mu}_{t}(X):=\int_{(0,1]}\avar_{\alpha}(X\;|\;\mathscr{F}_{t})\mu(\dif\alpha),\qquad X\in L^{\infty},\ t\in\mathcal{T},

(3.5)

where
\avar_{\alpha}
is the conditional average value at risk.

The conditional average value at risk is defined by analogy to the regular average value at risk through the conditional quantiles or value at risk.
The delicate part is to give proper probabilistic meaning to these quantities, which we briefly present in Appendix
A

B

Next result gives alternative representation of
\dwvar^{\mu}(X)
by means of conditional quantile function. Please refer to Definition A.1 in the Appendix 1 for the definition of
q^{+}_{z}(X\;|\;\mathscr{F}_{t})

Lemma 3.4
.

Let
\mu\in\mathcal{P}([0,1])
and denote by
\psi_{\mu,+}^{{}^{\prime}}
the right hand derivative of
\psi_{\mu}
. Then,

\displaystyle\dwvar^{\mu}_{t}(X)(\omega)

\displaystyle=-\int_{(0,1)}q^{+}_{z}(X\;|\;\mathscr{F}_{t})(\omega)\psi_{\mu,+}^{{}^{\prime}}(z)\dif z,

(3.6)

for any
X\in L^{\infty}
,
t\in\mathcal{T}
and
\omega\in\Omega\backslash N^{X}
.

Proof.

In view of (
A.2
) we have

\displaystyle\dwvar^{\mu}_{t}(X)(\omega)

\displaystyle=-\int_{(0,1]}\frac{1}{\alpha}\int_{(0,\alpha)}q^{+}_{z}(X\;|\;\mathscr{F}_{t})(\omega)\dif z\mu(\dif\alpha)

\displaystyle=-\int_{(0,1)}q^{+}_{z}(X\;|\;\mathscr{F}_{t})(\omega)\int_{(0,1]}\frac{1}{\alpha}\mathbbm{1}_{z<\alpha}\mu(\dif\alpha)\dif z

\displaystyle=-\int_{(0,1)}q^{+}_{z}(X\;|\;\mathscr{F}_{t})(\omega)\psi_{\mu,+}^{{}^{\prime}}(z)\dif z.

∎

Next, we prove that
\dwvar^{\mu}
is a DCRM generated by a distortion function.

Theorem 3.5
.

Let
\mu
be a given probability measure on
[0,1]
. Then, for any
X\in L^{\infty}
,
t\in\mathcal{T}
, and
\omega\in\Omega\backslash N^{X}

\dwvar^{\mu}_{t}(X)(\omega)=\rho^{\psi_{\mu}}_{t}(X)(\omega).

(3.7)

Hence,
\dwvar^{\mu}(X)
is a law-invariant DCRM.

Proof.

First we consider
X\geq 0
. By Lemma
3.4
and the definition of conditional upper quantile, we have

\displaystyle\dwvar^{\mu}_{t}(X)(\omega)

\displaystyle=-\int_{(0,1)}\sup\{y\in\mathbb{R}\;|\;\mathbb{P}(X<y\;|\;\mathscr{F}_{t})(\omega)\leq z\}\psi_{\mu,+}^{{}^{\prime}}(z)\dif z

\displaystyle=-\int_{(0,1)}\int_{[0,\infty)}\mathbbm{1}_{\mathbb{P}(X<y\;|\;\mathscr{F}_{t})(\omega)\leq z}dy\psi_{\mu,+}^{{}^{\prime}}(z)\dif z

\displaystyle=-\int_{[0,\infty)}\int_{(0,1)}\mathbbm{1}_{\mathbb{P}(X<y\;|\;\mathscr{F}_{t})(\omega)\leq z}\psi_{\mu,+}^{{}^{\prime}}(z)\dif z\dif y

\displaystyle=-\int_{[0,\infty)}\left[1-\psi\left(\mathbb{P}(X<y\;|\;\mathscr{F}_{t})(\omega)\right)\right]\dif y

\displaystyle=\int_{(-\infty,0)}\left[\psi\left(\mathbb{P}(-X>y\;|\;\mathscr{F}_{t})(\omega)\right)-1\right]\dif y

\displaystyle=\rho_{t}^{\psi_{\mu}}(X)(\omega).

If

, we consider

, where

On the other hand, by (

\dwvar^{\mu}_{t}(X^{\prime})=\dwvar^{\mu}_{t}(X)-C.

The notion of static Coherent Acceptability Index (CAI) was introduced in
[
CM06
]
, in the same axiomatic spirit of CRM, aiming to generalize the known performance measures such as Sharpe ratio, gain to loss ratio, risk adjusted return on capital, etc. The dynamic coherent acceptability indices (DCAIs), in a discrete time setup, were first introduced and studied in
[
BCZ14
]
, and consequently in
[
BCIR13
,
BCC15
,
BCDK16
,
BBN14
]
.

Definition 3.7
.

A DCAI is a function
\alpha:\mathcal{T}\times L^{\infty}\times\Omega\rightarrow[0,\infty]
that is adapted, local, quasi-concave, monotone increasing and scale invariant.

Similar to DCRM, we study time consistency of DCAI separately, in next section. Under some technical continuity assumptions, it was proved that a DCAI
\alpha_{t}
can be uniquely identified with a family
\rho^{x}_{t},\ x\in[0,+\infty)
, of DCRMs. We focus our attention on the case when
\rho^{x},x\geq 0
, are generated by distortion functions. This is motivated, in particular, by the extensive use of the static CAIs generated by distortion function in various applications from finance (cf.
[
MS16
]
).

Let us introduce some definitions and discuss some properties of families of distortion functions and the corresponding DCRMs.
In what follows, we assume that the underlying probability space is atomless.

Definition 3.8
.

A family, indexed by
x>0
, of distortion functions
(\psi_{x})_{x>0}
, is called increasing, if
\psi_{x_{1}}(y)\leq\psi_{x_{2}}(y)
for any
y\in[0,1]
and
0<x_{1}\leq x_{2}
.
Respectively, it is called right continuous, if
\lim\limits_{{z\rightarrow x^{+}}}\psi_{z}(y)=\psi_{x}(y)
for all
y\in[0,1]
and
x>0
.

Definition 3.9
.

A family, indexed by
x>0
, of DCRMs
(\rho^{\psi_{x}})_{x>0}
, is called increasing, if
\rho^{\psi_{x_{1}}}_{t}(X)\leq\rho^{\psi_{x_{2}}}_{t}(X)
for any
X\in L^{\infty}
,
t\in\mathcal{T}
and
0<x_{1}\leq x_{2}
.
Respectively, it is called right continuous, if
\lim\limits_{{z\rightarrow x^{+}}}\rho^{\psi_{z}}_{t}(X)=\rho^{\psi_{x}}_{t}(X)
for all
X\in L^{\infty}
,
t\in\mathcal{T}
and
x>0
.

Lemma 3.10
.

A family of DCRMs
(\rho^{\psi_{x}})_{x>0}
is increasing if and only if
(\psi_{x})_{x>0}
is an increasing family of regular distortion functions.
Moreover, a family of DCRMs
(\rho^{\psi_{x}})_{x>0}
is right continuous if and only if
(\psi_{x})_{x>0}
is a right continuous family of regular distortion functions.

The proof of Lemma
3.10
can be found in accompanying supplement.

Remark 3.11
.

Several notes are in order:

•

3.1
Families of DCRMs

In this section we give several important examples of families of DCRMs that generate DCAIs. These examples are based on families of distortion functions introduced in
[
CM09
]
, that lead to the so-called MINVAR, MAXVAR, MAXMINVAR and MINMAXVAR risk measures. As already mentioned, the corresponding CAIs were successfully used, in a static setup, in a series of papers and monographs devoted to conic finance
[
MS16
,
MS11b
,
MS11a
]
. Here we will present the dynamic counterpart of these measures.

3.1.1
Dynamic MINVAR

Consider the following family of regular distortion functions

\psi_{x}^{\mathrm{MINVAR}}(y)=1-(1-y)^{x+1},\quad x\in\mathbb{R}_{+},\ y\in[0,1].

Clearly this is an increasing and continuous family of distortions. As next result shows, the probability measure
\mu
from (
2.1
) can be computed explicitly for these examples.

Proposition 3.13
.

Given
x\in\mathbb{R}_{+}
, let
\mu\in\mathcal{P}[0,1]
be such that
\psi_{\mu}=\psi_{x}^{\mathrm{MINVAR}}
. Then,
\mu
follows
\mathrm{Beta}(2,x)
distribution.

Proof.

Let
F
denote the distribution function of
\mu
. By (
2.2
), for any
y\in[0,1]
, we have

\displaystyle F(y)

\displaystyle=1-(1-y)^{x+1}-(x+1)y(1-y)^{x}=1-(1-y)^{x}-xy(1-y)^{x}

\displaystyle=1-(1-y)^{x}-xy(1-y)^{x}=I_{y}(1,x)-xy(1-y)^{x}

\displaystyle=I_{y}(1,x)-\frac{y(1-y)^{x}}{B(1,x)}=I_{y}(2,x),

where
I_{y}(2,x)
is the regularized incomplete beta function and
B(1,x)=\frac{\Gamma(1)\Gamma(x)}{\Gamma(1+x)}=\frac{1}{x}
.
Note that
F
is exactly the cumulative distribution function of the beta distribution
\mathrm{Beta}(2,x)
.
∎

Similar to
[
CM09
]
, we provide an intuitive interpretation of
\rho_{t}^{\psi_{x}^{\mathrm{MINVAR}}}
.
Namely, for any
x\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
and
t\in\mathcal{T}
, it holds that

\displaystyle\rho^{\psi_{x}^{\mathrm{MINVAR}}}_{t}(X)=-\mathbb{E}[Y\;|\;\mathscr{F}_{t}],\quad\textrm{ with }\ Y\stackrel{{\scriptstyle d}}{{=}}\min\left\{X_{1},\ldots,X_{x+1}\right\},

where
X_{1},\ldots,X_{x+1}
are identically distributed as
X
and mutually conditionally independent given
\mathscr{F}_{t}
.
Indeed,

\displaystyle\begin{split}-\mathbb{E}[Y\;|\;\mathscr{F}_{t}]&=-\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}yd\mathbb{P}(Y\leq y\;|\;\mathscr{F}_{t})=-\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}yd(1-\mathbb{P}(\min\left\{X_{1},\ldots,X_{x+1}\right\}>y\;|\;\mathscr{F}_{t}))\\
&=-\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}yd(1-\prod_{i=1}^{x+1}\mathbb{P}(X_{i}>y\;|\;\mathscr{F}_{t}))=-\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}yd(1-(1-F_{X\;|\;\mathscr{F}_{t}}(y))^{x+1})\\
&=-\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}yd\psi_{x}^{MINVAR}\left(F_{X\;|\;\mathscr{F}_{t}}(y)\right)=\rho^{\psi_{x}^{MINVAR}}_{t}(X).\end{split}

The DAI generated by
\rho^{\psi_{x}^{\mathrm{MINVAR}}}
is denoted by
\alpha^{\mathrm{MINVAR}}
.

3.1.2
Dynamic MAXVAR

The DCRM called dynamic MAXVAR is generated by the following family of distortions

\psi_{x}^{MAXVAR}(y)=y^{\frac{1}{x+1}},\quad x\in\mathbb{R}_{+},\ y\in[0,1],

with the corresponding
\mu\in\mathcal{P}[0,1]
from (
2.1
) given in the next result.

Proposition 3.14
.

For a fixed
x\in\mathbb{R}_{+}
, let
\mu
be the probability measure such that
\psi_{\mu}=\psi_{x}^{\mathrm{MAXVAR}}
. Then,

\mu=\frac{x}{x+1}\hat{\mu}+\frac{1}{x+1}\delta_{1},

where
\hat{\mu}
follows the power with density
f_{\widehat{\mu}}(y)\propto y^{-\frac{x}{x+1}}
, and
\delta_{1}
is the Dirac measure at
1
.

Proof.

By (
2.2
), the distribution function of
\mu
is given by

F(y)=\begin{cases}0,&y=0\\
\frac{x}{x+1}y^{\frac{1}{x+1}},&0<y<1\\
1,&y=1.\end{cases}

Note that
F
is discontinuous at
1
since,

\displaystyle\mu(\{1\})=\left(\psi_{x}^{\mathrm{MAXVAR}}\right)^{\prime}(1-)=\frac{1}{x+1}y^{\frac{1}{x+1}-1}|_{y=1-}=\frac{1}{x+1}.

This completes the proof.
∎

Next, we will show that for fixed
x\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
and
t\in\mathcal{T}
,

\rho^{\psi_{x}^{\mathrm{MAXVAR}}}_{t}(X)=-\mathbb{E}[Y\;|\;\mathscr{F}_{t}],\quad\textrm{with}\ X\stackrel{{\scriptstyle d}}{{=}}\max\left\{Y_{1},\ldots,Y_{x+1}\right\},

where
Y_{1},\ldots,Y_{x+1}
are identically distributed as
Y
and mutually conditionally independent given
\mathscr{F}_{t}
. This, in particular, justifies the name
\mathrm{MAXVAR}
; see also
[
CM09
]
. Indeed, since

\begin{split}F_{X|\mathscr{F}_{t}}(y)&=\mathbb{P}(\max\left\{Y_{1},\ldots,Y_{x+1}\right\}\leq y\;|\;\mathscr{F}_{t})=\mathbb{P}(Y_{1}\leq y,\ldots,Y_{x+1}\leq y\;|\;\mathscr{F}_{t})\\
&=\prod_{i=1}^{x+1}\mathbb{P}(Y_{i}\leq y\;|\;\mathscr{F}_{t})=(F_{Y|\mathscr{F}_{t}}(y))^{x+1},\end{split}

we have that
F_{Y|\mathscr{F}_{t}}(y)=(F_{X|\mathscr{F}_{t}}(y))^{\frac{1}{x+1}}=\psi_{x}^{\mathrm{MAXVAR}}(F_{X|\mathscr{F}_{t}}(y))
. Hence,

\displaystyle-\mathbb{E}[Y\;|\;\mathscr{F}_{t}]

\displaystyle=-\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}yd\mathbb{P}(Y\leq y\;|\;\mathscr{F}_{t})=-\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}yd\psi_{x}^{\mathrm{MAXVAR}}\left(F_{X|\mathscr{F}_{t}}(y)\right)

\displaystyle=\rho^{\psi_{x}^{\mathrm{MAXVAR}}}_{t}(X).

3.1.3
Dynamic MAXMINVAR and MINMAXVAR

Similarly to the previous examples, one can elevate the MAXMINVAR and MINMAXVAR defined in

(3.9)

where
X_{1},\ldots,X_{x+1}
are identically distributed as

and

are identically distributed as

,
and both sequences are mutually conditionally independent given

where

and mutually conditionally independent given

Finally, we want to mention that

4
Time consistency of measures generated by distortion functions

Time consistency in decision making is a large topic with many existing fundamental results. We refer to the survey
[
BCP17
]
, where the authors present a comprehensive literature review and discuss different frameworks to study time consistency for risk or performance measures that admit numerical representations. Here, we will use the approach to time consistency developed in
[
BCP18
]
. For convenience, we list the relevant forms of time consistency in Appendix
A
. We assume the setup of Section
3
, and in particular we consider an atomless filtered probability space.

4.1
Time consistency of
\rho^{\psi}

We recall that if
\psi=\textrm{Id}
is the identity function, then
\rho_{t}^{\psi}(X)=\mathbb{E}(-X\;|\;\mathscr{F}_{t})
. This extreme case naturally satisfies all forms of time consistency. Therefore, in this section we omit this trivial case, and unless otherwise specified, we assume that
\psi\neq\textrm{Id}
.

As already mentioned,
\rho^{\psi}
is never strong time consistent (i.e. recurrent), except for
\psi
identity, but as we will show, it satisfies weaker forms of time consistency.

4.1.1
Sub-martingale and weakly rejection time consistency

Theorem 4.1
.

For any
\psi\in\Upsilon
, the DCRM
\rho^{\psi}
is sub-martingale time consistent.

Proof.

Let
t,s\in\mathcal{T}
such that
t\leq s
. Then, first by Fubini’s theorem and then by Jensen’s inequality, we deduce

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}(\rho_{s}^{\psi}(X)\;|\;\mathscr{F}_{t})

\displaystyle=\int_{[0,\infty)}\mathbb{E}(\psi(\mathbb{P}(-X>y|\mathscr{F}_{s}))|\mathscr{F}_{t})\dif y+\int_{(-\infty,0)}\mathbb{E}([\psi(\mathbb{P}(-X>y|\mathscr{F}_{s}))-1]|\mathscr{F}_{t})\dif y

\displaystyle\leq\int_{[0,\infty)}\psi(\mathbb{P}(-X>y\;|\;\mathscr{F}_{t}))\dif y+\int_{(-\infty,0)}[\psi(\mathbb{P}(-X>y\;|\;\mathscr{F}_{t}))-1]\dif y

\displaystyle=\rho_{t}^{\psi}(X).

The proof is complete.
∎

In view of
[
BCP17
, Proposition 5.4]
, weak time consistency is one of the weakest forms of time consistency, in the sense that it is implied by any time consistency generated by a projective update rule, and in particular weak rejection time consistency is implied by the sub-martingale time consistency. Thus, Proposition
4.1
implies that
\rho^{\psi}
is weakly rejection time consistent.

4.1.4
Weak acceptance time consistency

In this section we show that, in general, DCRMs generated by distortion functions are not even weakly acceptance time consistent, in the sense of the following result.

Theorem 4.6
.

For any probability measure
\mu\in\mathcal{P}[0,1]\setminus\{\delta_{1}\}
such that
\psi_{\mu}
is absolutely continuous, there exists a filtered probability space, such that
\rho^{\psi_{\mu}}
is not weakly acceptance time consistent.

The proof of Theorem
4.6
follows from a series of lemmas below.

In view of Remark

, Theorem

is equivalent to the statement that, for any absolutely continuous regular distortion

Lemma 4.7

For any probability measure
\mu\in\mathcal{P}([0,1])\setminus\mathcal{P}^{\prime}

\psi_{\mu}\left(\int_{(0,1)}z\psi_{\mu}^{{}^{\prime}}(z)dz\right)+\int_{(0,1)}z\psi_{\mu}^{{}^{\prime}}(z)dz-1<0.

Proof.

Let

\displaystyle m_{\mu}=1-\int_{(0,1)}\psi_{\mu}(z)dz.

(4.3)

With this at hand, (

\displaystyle\psi_{\mu}\left(m_{\mu}\right)+m_{\mu}-1<0,

We first prove a mild version of (

\psi_{\mu}\left(m_{\mu}\right)+m_{\mu}-1\leq 0.

(4.5)

\displaystyle=\int_{(0,1]}\frac{1}{s}\int_{(0,1]}\mathbbm{1}_{z<\min\{m_{\mu},s\}}\dif z\mu(ds)+m_{\mu}-1

\displaystyle=\int_{(0,\frac{1}{a+1}]}\mu(\dif s)+\frac{1}{a+1}\int_{(\frac{1}{a+1},1]}\frac{1}{s}\mu(\dif s)+\frac{1}{a+1}-1

\displaystyle=\frac{a-1}{a}+\frac{1}{a+1}\frac{1}{a}+\frac{1}{a+1}-1

\displaystyle=0.

This completes the proof.

\square

In

A.1
Conditional quantiles,
\var
and
\avar

The conditional versions of qualtiles, value at risk (
\var
) and average value at risk (
\avar
), are defined naturally by using ‘probabilistic conditioning’ of the corresponding regular (or static) notions. Most of the properties are also expected to hold after ‘conditioning’. While morally this is true, the difficulties are hidden in technical details related to measurability and well-definiteness of these quantities. Here, we define these objects, and state some of their properties, while the details are deferred to the Supplement
B
.

Definition A.1
.

For any
\alpha\in(0,1)
, the conditional upper and lower
\alpha
-quantile of
X\in L^{\infty}
with respect to
\sigma
-field
\mathscr{F}_{t}
are defined as

\displaystyle q_{\alpha}^{+}(X\;|\;\mathscr{F}_{t})=\esssup\{m\in L^{\infty}_{t}\;|\;\mathbb{P}(X\leq m\;|\;\mathscr{F}_{t})\leq\alpha\},

\displaystyle q_{\alpha}^{-}(X\;|\;\mathscr{F}_{t})=\essinf\{m\in L^{\infty}_{t}\;|\;\mathbb{P}(X\leq m\;|\;\mathscr{F}_{t})\geq\alpha\}.

It can be shown that the upper and lower quantile admit the representations

\displaystyle q_{\alpha}^{+}(X\;|\;\mathscr{F}_{t})(\omega)

\displaystyle=\sup\{x\in\mathbb{R}\;|\;\mathbb{P}(X\leq x\;|\;\mathscr{F}_{t})(\omega)\leq\alpha\}

\displaystyle=\sup\{x\in\mathbb{R}\;|\;\mathbb{P}(X<x\;|\;\mathscr{F}_{t})(\omega)\leq\alpha\}

\displaystyle q_{\alpha}^{-}(X\;|\;\mathscr{F}_{t})(\omega)

\displaystyle=\inf\{x\in\mathbb{R}\;|\;\mathbb{P}(X\leq x\;|\;\mathscr{F}_{t})(\omega)\geq\alpha\}

\displaystyle=\inf\{x\in\mathbb{R}\;|\;\mathbb{P}(X<x\;|\;\mathscr{F}_{t})(\omega)\geq\alpha\},

\displaystyle q^{+}_{\alpha}(X\;|\;\mathscr{F}_{t})(\omega)

\displaystyle=-q^{-}_{1-\alpha}(-X\;|\;\mathscr{F}_{t})(\omega).

The conditional
\var
, similar to its static counterpart, is defined in terms of conditional
\alpha
-quantile function.

Definition A.2
.

For fixed
\alpha\in(0,1)
and
X\in L^{\infty}
, conditional
\var
at level
\alpha
with respect to
\sigma
-field
\mathscr{F}_{t}
is defined as,

\displaystyle\var_{\alpha}(X\;|\;\mathscr{F}_{t}):=-q_{\alpha}^{+}(X\;|\;\mathscr{F}_{t})=\essinf\{m\in L^{\infty}_{t}\;|\;\mathbb{P}(X+m<0\;|\;\mathscr{F}_{t})\leq\alpha\}.

(A.1)

From financial point of view,
\var_{\alpha}(X|\mathscr{F}_{t})
can be viewed as the smallest amount of capital which, if added to the position
X
at time
t
, implies that (conditional) probability that the secured position
X+m
will yield losses is below the level
\alpha
. Clearly, the
\var
does not capture the size or distribution of the losses beyond the quantile. To overcome this, the notion of average value at risk is introduced.

Definition A.3
.

The conditional Average Value at Risk at level
\alpha\in(0,1]
of a position
X\in L^{\infty}
is given by

\displaystyle\avar_{\alpha}(X\;|\;\mathscr{F}_{t}):=\frac{1}{\alpha}\int_{(0,\alpha)}\var_{z}(X\;|\;\mathscr{F}_{t})\dif z=-\frac{1}{\alpha}\int_{(0,\alpha)}q_{z}^{+}(X\;|\;\mathscr{F}_{t})\dif z.

(A.2)

Note that
q_{z}^{+}(X\;|\;\mathscr{F}_{t})
is monotone increasing with respect to
z
. Since monotonicity implies Borel measurability,
z\mapsto q_{z}^{+}(X\;|\;\mathscr{F}_{t})
is Borel measurable. Thus, the integral in (
A.2
) is well-defined.

A.2
Notions of Time Consistency

In
[
BCP17
,
BCP18
]
the authors introduced a general framework for studying time consistency of local and monotone (increasing) functions
f:\mathcal{T}\times L^{\infty}\times\Omega\to\mathbb{R}
.
In particular, the notion of weakly/middle and rejection or acceptance time consistency, super/sub-martingale time consistency, and strong time consistency were introduced. The time consistency property and its economic interpretation stays at the foundation of the inter-temporal decision making theory. It is beyond the scope of this work to discuss the subtle differences between different forms of time consistency, and we refer the reader to the survey
[
BCP17
]
and references therein. Here, for the sake of completeness, but also to avoid confusions between acceptance and rejection time consistency depending on monotonicity, we simply list some of these notions relevant to the objects we study.

A DCRM
\rho
is:

•

strong
time consistent if
\rho_{s}(X)=\rho_{s}(Y)\Rightarrow\rho_{t}(X)=\rho_{t}(Y)
;

•

sub-martingale
time consistent if

\rho_{t}(X)\geq\mathbb{E}\left[\rho_{s}(X)\;|\;\mathscr{F}_{t}\right]
;

•

middle rejection
time consistent if,
\rho_{s}(X)\geq\rho_{s}(Y)\Rightarrow\rho_{t}(X)\geq\rho_{t}(Y)
, for any
Y\in L^{\infty}_{s}
;

•

super-martingale
time consistent if

\rho_{t}(X)\leq\mathbb{E}\left[\rho_{s}(X)\;|\;\mathscr{F}_{t}\right]
;

•

weak acceptance
time consistent if
\rho_{s}(X)\leq 0\Rightarrow\rho_{t}(X)\leq 0,

for any
X,Y\in L^{\infty}
,
s,t\in\mathcal{T}
, such that
s>t
.

A DCAI
\alpha
is:

•

weakly rejection
time consistent if,

\alpha_{s}\left(X\right)\leq m_{t}\Rightarrow\alpha_{t}\left(X\right)\leq m_{t}
;

•

weakly acceptance
time consistent if,

\alpha_{s}\left(X\right)\geq m_{t}\Rightarrow\alpha_{t}\left(X\right)\geq m_{t},

for any
X\in L^{\infty}
,
s,t\in\mathcal{T}
with
s>t
and non-negative
m_{t}\in\mathscr{F}_{t}
.

We start with a result on distortion functions.

Lemma B.1
.

For any concave distortion
\psi
except the identity function, we have that
\psi(x)>x
for any
x\in(0,1)
. Moreover, any concave distortion
\psi
is continuous on
(0,1]
.

Proof.

Since
\psi
is concave on
[0,1]
, then
\psi(x)\geq x
for any
x\in[0,1]
. Otherwise suppose there exists
x^{\prime}\in[0,1]
such that
\psi(x^{\prime})<x^{\prime}
. Take
x=1
,
y=0
and
\alpha=x^{\prime}
, then we get,

\psi(\alpha x+(1-\alpha)y)=\psi(x^{\prime})<x^{\prime}=\alpha\psi(x)+(1-\alpha)\psi(y),

which contradicts that
\psi
is concave on
[0,1]
.

Since
\psi
cannot be identity function, thus there exists
m\in(0,1)
, such that
\psi(m)>m
.

Now we prove the lemma by contradiction. Suppose there exists
z\in(0,1)
, such that
\psi(z)\leq z
. Since
\psi(x)\geq x
for any
x\in[0,1]
, then
\psi(z)=z
. First assume
m<z
. We will show that
\psi
is not concave on
[0,1]
, i.e.

\displaystyle\exists\ x,y\in[0,1],\ \exists\ \alpha\in[0,1],\ \text{such that}\ \psi(\alpha x+(1-\alpha)y)<\alpha\psi(x)+(1-\alpha)\psi(y).

(B.1)

Take
x=m
,
y=1
,
\alpha=\frac{1-z}{1-m}
. Then we obtain

\displaystyle\psi(\alpha x+(1-\alpha)y)=\psi\left(\frac{1-z}{1-m}m+\frac{z-m}{1-m}\right)=\psi(z)=z.

Since
z=\frac{1-z}{1-m}m+\frac{z-m}{1-m}
, it follows that,

\displaystyle\psi(\alpha x+(1-\alpha)y)=\frac{(1-z)m}{1-m}+\frac{z-m}{1-m}<\frac{(1-z)\psi(m)}{1-m}+\frac{z-m}{1-m}=\alpha\psi(x)+(1-\alpha)\psi(y).

This leads to (
B.1
), and thus
\psi
is not concave. The case
m>z
, is treated similarly, take
x=0
,
y=m
,
\alpha=1-\frac{z}{m}
, and obtain (
B.1
).

To prove the second part, we show that
\psi
is continuous on
(0,1)
. By
[
FS04
, Proposition A.4 (a)]
, concavity of
\psi
on
[0,1]
implies that
\psi
is locally Lipschitz continuous on
(0,1)
.
So for each point
r\in(0,1)
, there exists a neighbourhood
O_{r}
of
r
, for any
x,y\in O_{r}
, there exists
M_{r}\in\mathbb{R}_{+}
, such that
\frac{|\psi(x)-\psi(y)|}{|x-y|}\leq M_{r}
. Take
y=r
, then
|\psi(x)-\psi(r)|\leq M_{r}|x-r|
. Therefore
\psi(x)\rightarrow\psi(r)
as
x\rightarrow r
, which means that
\psi
is continuous at
r
. Since
r
is arbitrary on
(0,1)
,
\psi
is continuous on
(0,1)
.

Next for the continuity at point 1, if
\psi
is identity function, then obviously it is continuous at 1. For other distortion
\psi
, we use proof by contradiction. Suppose
\psi
is not continuous at point 1, since
\psi(1)=1
and
\psi
is increasing, assume
\psi(1-)=a<1
. Take arbitrary
y\in[a,1)
, since
\psi
is increasing,
\psi(y)\leq\psi(1-)=a\leq y
. This is in contradiction to
\psi(y)>y
for any
y\in(0,1)
.
This concludes the proof.
∎

Lemma B.2
.

Let
A_{1},\ldots,A_{n}\in\mathscr{F}_{T}
be a (disjoint) partition of
\Omega
and put
I:=\{1,...,n\}
. For given
t\in\mathcal{T}
,
\psi\in\Upsilon
, and
\omega\in\Omega\backslash N^{X}
, construct the function
Q^{\omega}
on
\mathscr{G}:=\sigma(A_{1},\ldots,A_{n})
generated by

Q^{\omega}[\bigcup_{k\in J\subseteq I}A_{k}]:=\sum_{k\in J\subseteq I}[\psi(\mathbb{P}(B_{k}\;|\;\mathscr{F}_{t})(\omega))-\psi(\mathbb{P}(B_{k-1}\;|\;\mathscr{F}_{t})(\omega))],

(B.2)

where
B_{0}:=\emptyset,\ B_{k}:=\bigcup_{j=1}^{k}A_{j}
,
k\in I
. Then,
Q^{\omega}
is a probability measure on
(\Omega,\mathscr{G})
.

Proof.

Clearly
Q^{\omega}[\emptyset]=0
, and

\displaystyle Q^{\omega}[\Omega]

\displaystyle=Q^{\omega}[\bigcup_{k\in I}A_{k}]=\sum_{k\in I}[\psi(\mathbb{P}(B_{k}\;|\;\mathscr{F}_{t})(\omega))-\psi(\mathbb{P}(B_{k-1}\;|\;\mathscr{F}_{t})(\omega))]

\displaystyle=\psi(\mathbb{P}(B_{n}\;|\;\mathscr{F}_{t})(\omega))-\psi(\mathbb{P}(B_{0}\;|\;\mathscr{F}_{t})(\omega))=1.

Since the
\mathcal{G}
is generated by a finite partition,

-additivity of

Q^{\omega}[\bigcup_{k\in J\subseteq I}A_{k}]=\sum_{k\in J\subseteq I}[\psi(\mathbb{P}(B_{k}\;|\;\mathscr{F}_{t})(\omega))-\psi(\mathbb{P}(B_{k-1}\;|\;\mathscr{F}_{t})(\omega))]=\sum_{k\in J\subseteq I}Q^{\omega}[A_{k}].

Thus,

is a probability measure, and this concludes the proof.

Lemma B.3
.

Given
\psi\in\Upsilon
and
t\in\mathcal{T}
, for any
\omega\in\Omega\backslash N^{X}
, let
Q^{\omega}
be the probability measure given by (
B.2
). Then, for any non-negative random variable
X=\sum_{i=1}^{n}x_{i}\mathbbm{1}_{A_{i}}
we have

\sum_{i=1}^{n}-x_{i}Q^{\omega}[A_{i}]\leq\rho_{t}^{\psi}(X)(\omega).

(B.3)

Proof.

First we will show that if
x_{n}\geq\ldots\geq x_{1}\geq x_{0}:=0
, then
\rho_{t}^{\psi}(X)(\omega)=\sum_{i=1}^{n}-x_{i}Q^{\omega}(A_{i})
, i.e. (
B.3
) becomes equality. With
B_{k},\ k\in I
, defined in Lemma
B.2
, we notice that

\{-X>y\}=\begin{cases}B_{n},&y<-x_{n}\\
B_{n-1},&-x_{n}\leq y<-x_{{n}-1}\\
\ldots\\
B_{1},&-x_{2}\leq y<-x_{1}\\
B_{0},&-x_{1}\leq y.\end{cases}

This, consequently implies that

\displaystyle\rho_{t}^{\psi}(X)(\omega)

\displaystyle=\int_{(-\infty,0)}[\psi(\mathbb{P}(-X>y\;|\;\mathscr{F}_{t})(\omega))-1]\dif y

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}[\psi(\mathbb{P}(B_{i}\;|\;\mathscr{F}_{t})(\omega))-1](x_{i+1}-x_{i})

\displaystyle=-Q^{\omega}(A_{n})(x_{n}-x_{n-1})-\sum_{i=n-1}^{n}Q^{\omega}(A_{i})(x_{n-1}-x_{n-2})-\ldots

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad-\sum_{i=2}^{n}Q^{\omega}(A_{i})(x_{2}-x_{1})-\sum_{i=1}^{n}Q^{\omega}(A_{i})x_{1}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{n}-x_{i}Q^{\omega}(A_{i}).

Second, we prove that
\rho_{t}^{\psi}(X)(\omega)\geq\sum_{k=1}^{n}-x_{k}Q^{\omega}(A_{k})
for arbitrary non-negative step function
X
. Note that, any permutation
\sigma
of
\{1,\ldots,n\}
induces a probability
Q_{\sigma}^{\omega}
by applying Lemma
B.2
to the relabeled partition
A_{\sigma(1)}\ldots,A_{\sigma(n)}
. Let
\sigma
be the permutation of
I
such that
x_{\sigma(1)}\leq\ldots\leq x_{\sigma(n)}
, then in view of the above, we have

\rho_{t}^{\psi}(X)({\omega})=\sum_{k=1}^{n}-x_{\sigma(k)}Q^{\omega}_{\sigma}(A_{\sigma(k)}).

Hence, the assertion will follow if we can prove that

\sum_{k=1}^{n}-x_{\sigma(k)}Q^{\omega}_{\sigma}(A_{\sigma(k)})\geq\sum_{k=1}^{n}-x_{k}Q^{\omega}(A_{k}).

(B.4)

We claim that (
B.4
) will follow if we can prove that

\sum_{k=1}^{n}-x_{\tau(k)}Q^{\omega}_{\tau}(A_{\tau(k)})\geq\sum_{k=1}^{n}-x_{k}Q^{\omega}(A_{k}),

(B.5)

where
\tau
is the transposition of arbitrary, but fixed, two neighboring indices
i
and
i+1
where
x_{i}\geq x_{i+1}
, such that
x_{\tau(i)}\leq x_{\tau(i+1)}
; operation
\tau
only affects the positions
i
and
i+1
, with all other terms remaining terms unchanged. Let us show that indeed (
B.5
) implies (
B.4
). If the values of
X
are not arranged in increasing order, then there exists at least one pair of neighboring indices with decreasing order, so that we can apply
m
finitely many times neighboring swapping operation
\tau
until we get the permutation
\sigma
, and at each step we relabel
X
. At
j
-th step, we swap two reverse-order
neighboring indices of
X_{\tau}^{j-1}
which is the relabeled sequence from step
j-1
. Then, we get that
X^{j}_{\tau}=\sum_{k=1}^{n}x_{\tau(k)}^{j-1}A^{j-1}_{\tau(k)}
, and by (
B.5
), we deduce that

\mathbb{E}^{Q^{\omega}_{j}}(-X^{j}_{\tau})=\sum_{k=1}^{n}-x^{j-1}_{\tau(k)}Q^{\omega}_{j}(A^{j-1}_{\tau(k)})\geq\sum_{k=1}^{n}-x^{j-1}_{k}Q^{\omega}_{j-1}(A^{j-1}_{k})=\mathbb{E}^{Q^{\omega}_{j-1}}(-X^{j-1}_{\tau}).

(B.6)

where
Q^{\omega}_{j}
and
Q^{\omega}_{j-1}
correspond to
Q^{\omega}_{\tau}
and
Q^{\omega}
in (
B.5
) respectively. Taking
j=m
, we get
X^{m}_{\tau}
being the same as
X_{\sigma}
since both are the relabeled
X
in increasing order. Moreover,
Q^{\omega}_{\sigma}
and
Q^{\omega}_{m}
are the same since both are constructed based on the same sequence of sets, so that

\mathbb{E}^{Q^{\omega}_{\sigma}}(-X_{\sigma})=\mathbb{E}^{Q^{\omega}_{m}}(-X^{m}_{\tau}).

(B.7)

By applying (
B.6
) inductively, we have

\mathbb{E}^{Q^{\omega}_{m}}(-X^{m}_{\tau})\geq\mathbb{E}^{Q^{\omega}_{m-1}}(-X^{m-1}_{\tau})\geq\ldots\geq\mathbb{E}^{Q^{\omega}_{1}}(-X^{1}_{\tau})\geq\mathbb{E}^{Q^{\omega}}(-X).

(B.8)

Combining (
B.7
) and (
B.8
), then (
B.4
) follows, and thus (
B.5
) implies (
B.4
).

Finally, it remains to prove (
B.5
). Recall that
\tau(i)=i+1
,
\tau(i+1)=i
and
\tau(k)=k
for all other
k
. Thus,

\displaystyle-\sum_{k=1}^{n}x_{\tau(k)}Q_{\tau}^{\omega}(A_{\tau(k)})+\sum_{k=1}^{n}x_{k}Q^{\omega}(A_{k})

\displaystyle=-x_{\tau(i)}Q_{\tau}^{\omega}\left[A_{\tau(i)}\right]-x_{\tau(i+1)}Q^{\omega}_{\tau}\left[A_{\tau(i+1)}\right]

(B.9)

\displaystyle\qquad+x_{i}Q^{\omega}\left[A_{i}\right]+x_{i+1}Q^{\omega}\left[A_{i+1}\right]

(B.10)

\displaystyle=-x_{i+1}Q^{\omega}_{\tau}\left[A_{i+1}\right]-x_{i}Q^{\omega}_{\tau}\left[A_{i}\right]

(B.11)

\displaystyle\qquad+(x_{i}Q^{\omega}\left[A_{i}\right]+x_{i+1}Q^{\omega}\left[A_{i+1}\right])

(B.12)

\displaystyle=-x_{i}\left(Q^{\omega}_{\tau}\left[A_{i}\right]-Q^{\omega}\left[A_{i}\right]\right)-x_{i+1}\left(Q^{\omega}_{\tau}\left[A_{i+1}\right]-Q^{\omega}\left[A_{i+1}\right]\right).

(B.13)

To compute
Q^{\omega}_{\tau}\left(A_{k}\right)
, let us introduce

B_{0}^{\tau}:=\emptyset\quad\text{ and }\quad B_{k}^{\tau}:=\bigcup_{j=1}^{k}A_{\tau(j)},\quad k=1,\ldots,n.

(B.14)

We note that
B_{i}^{\tau}=\bigcup_{j=1}^{i-1}A_{j}\bigcup A_{i+1}
and
B_{k}^{\tau}=B_{k}
, for

. Hence,

\begin{split}Q_{\tau}^{\omega}\left[A_{i}\right]+Q^{\omega}_{\tau}\left[A_{i+1}\right]&=Q^{\omega}_{\tau}\left[A_{\tau(i+1)}\right]+Q^{\omega}_{\tau}\left[A_{\tau(i)}\right]\\
&=\psi(\mathbb{P}(B_{i+1}^{\tau}\;|\;\mathscr{F}_{t})(\omega))-\psi(\mathbb{P}(B_{i}^{\tau}\;|\;\mathscr{F}_{t})(\omega))\\
&\qquad+\psi(\mathbb{P}(B_{i}^{\tau}\;|\;\mathscr{F}_{t})(\omega))-\psi(\mathbb{P}(B_{i-1}^{\tau}\;|\;\mathscr{F}_{t})(\omega))\\
&=\psi(\mathbb{P}(B_{i+1}^{\tau}\;|\;\mathscr{F}_{t})(\omega))-\psi(\mathbb{P}(B_{i-1}^{\tau}\;|\;\mathscr{F}_{t})(\omega))\\
&=\psi(\mathbb{P}(B_{i+1}\;|\;\mathscr{F}_{t})(\omega))-\psi(\mathbb{P}(B_{i-1}\;|\;\mathscr{F}_{t})(\omega))\\
&=Q^{\omega}\left[A_{i}\right]+Q^{\omega}\left[A_{i+1}\right].\end{split}

(B.15)

Moreover,
B_{i}^{\tau}\cap B_{i}=B_{i-1}
,
B_{i}^{\tau}\cup B_{i}=B_{i+1}
and we have,

\begin{split}\mathbb{P}(B_{i}\;|\;\mathscr{F}_{t})(\omega)&-\mathbb{P}(B_{i-1}\;|\;\mathscr{F}_{t})(\omega)=\mathbb{P}(-X>-x_{i+1}\;|\;\mathscr{F}_{t})(\omega)-\mathbb{P}(-X>-x_{i}\;|\;\mathscr{F}_{t})(\omega)\\
&=\mathbb{P}(-X>-x_{i+2}\;|\;\mathscr{F}_{t})(\omega)-\mathbb{P}(\{-X>-x_{i}\}\cup\{-x_{i+1}\geq-X>-x_{i+2}\}\;|\;\mathscr{F}_{t})(\omega)\\
&=\mathbb{P}(B_{i+1}\;|\;\mathscr{F}_{t})(\omega)-\mathbb{P}(B_{i}^{\tau}\;|\;\mathscr{F}_{t})(\omega).\end{split}

(B.16)

Since
\psi
is concave, we deduce

\frac{\psi(\mathbb{P}(B_{i+1}\;|\;\mathscr{F}_{t})(\omega))-\psi(\mathbb{P}(B_{i}^{\tau}\;|\;\mathscr{F}_{t})(\omega))}{\mathbb{P}(B_{i+1}\;|\;\mathscr{F}_{t})(\omega)-\mathbb{P}(B_{i}^{\tau}\;|\;\mathscr{F}_{t})(\omega)}\leq\frac{\psi(\mathbb{P}(B_{i}\;|\;\mathscr{F}_{t})(\omega))-\psi(\mathbb{P}(B_{i-1}\;|\;\mathscr{F}_{t})(\omega))}{\mathbb{P}(B_{i}\;|\;\mathscr{F}_{t})(\omega)-\mathbb{P}(B_{i-1}\;|\;\mathscr{F}_{t})(\omega)},

and invoking (
B.16
), we obtain

\psi(\mathbb{P}(B_{i+1}\;|\;\mathscr{F}_{t})(\omega))-\psi(\mathbb{P}(B_{i}^{\tau}\;|\;\mathscr{F}_{t})(\omega))\leq\psi(\mathbb{P}(B_{i}\;|\;\mathscr{F}_{t})(\omega))-\psi(\mathbb{P}(B_{i-1}\;|\;\mathscr{F}_{t})(\omega)).

Thus,

\begin{split}Q^{\omega}\left[A_{i+1}\right]&=\psi(\mathbb{P}(B_{i+1}\;|\;\mathscr{F}_{t})(\omega))-\psi(\mathbb{P}(B_{i}\;|\;\mathscr{F}_{t})(\omega))\\
&\leq\psi(\mathbb{P}(B_{i}^{\tau}\;|\;\mathscr{F}_{t})(\omega))-\psi(\mathbb{P}(B_{i-1}^{\tau}\;|\;\mathscr{F}_{t})(\omega))\\
&=Q^{\omega}_{\tau}\left[A_{\tau(i)}\right]\\
&=Q^{\omega}_{\tau}\left[A_{i+1}\right].\end{split}

(B.17)

By (
B.15
) and (
B.17
), we have
Q^{\omega}\left[A_{i+1}\right]\leq Q^{\omega}_{\tau}\left[A_{i+1}\right]\hskip 5.69054pt
and
Q^{\omega}\left[A_{i}\right]\geq Q^{\omega}_{\tau}\left[A_{i}\right]
. Moreover,

Q^{\omega}\left[A_{i}\right]-Q^{\omega}_{\tau}\left[A_{i}\right]=Q^{\omega}_{\tau}\left[A_{i+1}\right]-Q^{\omega}\left[A_{i+1}\right].

Due to our assumption
x_{i}\geq x_{i+1}
, and thus

\displaystyle-\sum_{k=1}^{n}x_{\tau(k)}Q^{\omega}_{\tau}(A_{\tau(k)})+\sum_{k=1}^{n}x_{k}Q^{\omega}(A_{k})

\displaystyle=-x_{i}\left(Q^{\omega}_{\tau}\left[A_{i}\right]-Q^{\omega}\left[A_{i}\right]\right)-x_{i+1}\left(Q^{\omega}_{\tau}\left[A_{i+1}\right]-Q^{\omega}\left[A_{i+1}\right]\right)

\displaystyle=(x_{i}-x_{i+1})\left(Q^{\omega}_{\tau}\left[A_{i+1}\right]-Q^{\omega}\left[A_{i+1}\right]\right)\geq 0.

B.1
On DCRMs

For any
\psi,X\in L^{\infty}
and
t\in\mathcal{T}
, we define

\displaystyle\rho_{t}^{\psi}(X):=\int_{[0,\infty)}\psi\left(\mathbb{P}(-X>y\;|\;\mathscr{F}_{t})\right)dy+\int_{(-\infty,0)}\left[\psi(\mathbb{P}(-X>y\;|\;\mathscr{F}_{t}))-1\right]\dif y.

(B.18)

Definition B.4
.

A
Dynamic Coherent Risk Measure
(DCRM) is a function
\rho:\mathcal{T}\times L^{\infty}\times\Omega\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
that is adapted, normalized, local, cash-additive, monotone decreasing, sub-additive and positive homogeneous.

Proposition B.5
.

The mapping
\rho^{\psi}
is a DCRM.

Proof.

First we show that (
B.18
) is well-defined, that is:

1.

For any
t\in\mathcal{T}
,
X\in L^{\infty}
and
y\in\mathbb{R}
, the mapping
y\mapsto\psi(\mathbb{P}(-X>y\;|\;\mathscr{F}_{t})(\omega))
is Borel measurable, and thus the integrals in (
B.18
) are well-defined;

2.

\rho_{t}^{\psi}(X)\in L^{\infty}
, for all
t\in\mathcal{T}
,
X\in L^{\infty}
.

Since for any
\omega\in\Omega\backslash N^{X}
,
\mathbb{P}(-X>y\;|\;\mathscr{F}_{t})(\omega)
is a distribution function, then it is Borel measurable with respect to
y
. Consequently,
\psi
being continuous, preserves measurability, and (i) is proved. Next take
a
and
b
to be the essential infimum and the essential supremum of
X
, respectively, and without loss of generality, assume that
a<0<b
. Then,

\displaystyle\rho_{t}^{\psi}(X)(\omega)

\displaystyle=\int_{[0,\infty)}\psi\left(\mathbb{P}(-X>y\;|\;\mathscr{F}_{t})\right)\dif y+\int_{(-\infty,0)}\left[\psi(\mathbb{P}(-X>y\;|\;\mathscr{F}_{t}))-1\right]\dif y

\displaystyle=\int_{[0,-a]}\psi\left(\mathbb{P}(-X>y\;|\;\mathscr{F}_{t})\right)\dif y+\int_{[-b,0)}\left[\psi(\mathbb{P}(-X>y\;|\;\mathscr{F}_{t}))-1\right]\dif y

\displaystyle\in[-b,-a],

which means that
\rho_{t}^{\omega}
is bounded and (ii) is proved.

Next we will show that
\rho^{\psi}
satisfies all DCRM properties, that is, adapted, normalized, monotone, local, cash-additive, monotone decreasing, sub-additive and positive homogeneous.

Adaptiveness.

For any
\omega\in\Omega\backslash N^{X}
and
y\in\mathbb{R}
,
\mathbb{P}(-X>y\;|\;\mathscr{F}_{t})(\omega)
is a regular conditional distribution, and hence it is
\mathscr{F}_{t}\bigotimes\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{R})
joint measurable. Continuity of
\psi
preserves joint measurability, by Tonelli theorem,
\omega\mapsto\rho_{t}^{\psi}(X)(\omega)
is
\mathscr{F}_{t}
-measurable.

Normalization.

\displaystyle\rho_{t}^{\psi}(0)=\int_{[0,\infty)}\psi\left(\mathbb{P}(0>y\;|\;\mathscr{F}_{t})\right)\dif y+\int_{(-\infty,0)}\left[\psi(\mathbb{P}(0>y\;|\;\mathscr{F}_{t}))-1\right]\dif y=0.

Locality.
For any
\omega\in\Omega\backslash N^{X}
and
A\in\mathscr{F}_{t}
, note that,

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}(-\mathbbm{1}_{A}X>y\;|\;\mathscr{F}_{t})(\omega)=\begin{cases}\mathbb{P}(A\cap\{-X>y\}\;|\;\mathscr{F}_{t})(\omega),&y\in[0,\infty)\\
\mathbb{P}(\{A\cap\{-X>y\}\}\cup A^{c}\;|\;\mathscr{F}_{t})(\omega),&y\in(-\infty,0).\end{cases}

Denote by
J_{1}=\mathbbm{1}_{A}(\omega)\rho_{t}^{\psi}(\mathbbm{1}_{A}X)(\omega)
and we will show that
J_{1}=\mathbbm{1}_{A}(\omega)\rho_{t}^{\psi}(X)(\omega)
.

\displaystyle J_{1}

\displaystyle=\mathbbm{1}_{A}(\omega)\int_{[0,\infty)}\psi(\mathbb{P}(-\mathbbm{1}_{A}X>y\;|\;\mathscr{F}_{t})(\omega))\dif y

\displaystyle\qquad+\mathbbm{1}_{A}(\omega)\int_{(-\infty,0)}[\psi(\mathbb{P}(-\mathbbm{1}_{A}X>y\;|\;\mathscr{F}_{t})(\omega))-1]\dif y

\displaystyle=\mathbbm{1}_{A}(\omega)\int_{[0,\infty)}\psi(\mathbb{P}(A\cap\{-X>y\}\;|\;\mathscr{F}_{t})(\omega))\dif y

\displaystyle\qquad+\mathbbm{1}_{A}(\omega)\int_{(-\infty,0)}[\psi(\mathbb{P}(\{A\cap\{-X>y\}\}\cup A^{c}\;|\;\mathscr{F}_{t})(\omega))-1]\dif y

\displaystyle=\mathbbm{1}_{A}(\omega)\int_{[0,\infty)}\psi(\mathbb{E}(\mathbbm{1}_{A}\mathbbm{1}_{-X>y}\;|\;\mathscr{F}_{t})(\omega))\dif y

\displaystyle\qquad+\mathbbm{1}_{A}(\omega)\int_{(-\infty,0)}[\psi(\mathbb{E}(\mathbbm{1}_{A}\mathbbm{1}_{-X>y}+\mathbbm{1}_{A^{c}}\;|\;\mathscr{F}_{t})(\omega))-1]\dif y.

Then, since
\psi(0)=0
and
\psi(1)=1
, we can move
\mathbbm{1}_{A}
outside of
\psi
and continue the proof as following,

\displaystyle J_{1}

\displaystyle=\mathbbm{1}_{A}(\omega)\int_{[0,\infty)}\mathbbm{1}_{A}(\omega)\psi(\mathbb{P}(-X>y\;|\;\mathscr{F}_{t})(\omega))\dif y

\displaystyle\qquad+\mathbbm{1}_{A}(\omega)\int_{(-\infty,0)}[\mathbbm{1}_{A}(\omega)\psi(\mathbb{P}(-X>y\;|\;\mathscr{F}_{t})(\omega))+\mathbbm{1}_{A^{c}}(\omega)-1]\dif y

\displaystyle=\mathbbm{1}_{A}(\omega)\int_{[0,\infty)}\psi(\mathbb{P}(-X>y\;|\;\mathscr{F}_{t})(\omega))\dif y

\displaystyle\qquad+\mathbbm{1}_{A}(\omega)\int_{(-\infty,0)}[\mathbbm{1}_{A}(\omega)\psi(\mathbb{P}(-X>y\;|\;\mathscr{F}_{t})(\omega))-\mathbbm{1}_{A}(\omega)]\dif y

\displaystyle=\mathbbm{1}_{A}(\omega)\int_{[0,\infty)}\psi(\mathbb{P}(-X>y\;|\;\mathscr{F}_{t})(\omega))\dif y

\displaystyle\qquad+\mathbbm{1}_{A}(\omega)\int_{(-\infty,0)}[\psi(\mathbb{P}(-X>y\;|\;\mathscr{F}_{t})(\omega))-1]\dif y

\displaystyle=\mathbbm{1}_{A}(\omega)\rho_{t}^{\psi}(X)(\omega).

Cash Additivity.
We first prove cash additivity for
m\in L^{\infty}_{t,+}
. For any
m\in L^{\infty}_{t,+}
, there exists a sequence of step functions,

m^{n}:=\sum_{i=1}^{N_{n}}y_{i}\mathbbm{1}_{A_{i}},\hskip 8.53581pty_{i}\geq 0,\hskip 8.53581ptA_{i}\in\mathscr{F}_{t},

increasingly converge to
m
in
L^{\infty}
, where
N_{n}
is a constant and
A_{i}\cap A_{j}=\emptyset
, for
i\neq j
. For any
X\in L^{\infty}
, with fixed
y\in\mathbb{R}
,
\{X+m^{n}>y\}
is an increasing sequence of sets converging to
\{X+m>y\}
. Note that for any
n\in\mathbb{N}
, there exists a null set
N^{X,m^{n},t}\in\mathscr{F}_{t}
, such that for any
\omega\in\Omega\backslash N^{X,m^{n},t}
,
\mathbb{P}(X+m^{n}>y\;|\;\mathscr{F}_{t})(\omega)
is a regular distribution.

Let
M=\bigcup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\bigcup_{t\in\mathcal{T}}N^{X,m^{n},t}\bigcup N^{X,m}
, which is a null set as a countable union of null sets. For any
\omega\in\Omega\backslash M
, by continuity of probability,

\mathbb{P}(X+m^{n}>y\;|\;\mathscr{F}_{t})(\omega)\nearrow\mathbb{P}(X+m>y\;|\;\mathscr{F}_{t})(\omega),\quad n\to\infty.

By left-continuity of
\psi
, as
n\to\infty
,

\psi(\mathbb{P}(X+m^{n}>y\;|\;\mathscr{F}_{t})(\omega))\nearrow\psi(\mathbb{P}(X+m>y\;|\;\mathscr{F}_{t})(\omega)).

Then by monotone convergence theorem, as
n\to\infty
,

\int_{[0,\infty)}\psi(\mathbb{P}(X+m^{n}>y\;|\;\mathscr{F}_{t})(\omega))\dif y\rightarrow\int_{[0,\infty)}\psi(\mathbb{P}(X+m>y\;|\;\mathscr{F}_{t})(\omega))\dif y.\\

(B.19)

Note that for any
n\in\mathbb{N}
and
y\in\mathbb{R}_{-}
,

\left|\psi(\mathbb{P}(X+m^{n}>y\;|\;\mathscr{F}_{t})(\omega))-1\right|\leq|\psi(\mathbb{P}(X>y\;|\;\mathscr{F}_{t})(\omega))-1|,

and the latter is integrable on
(-\infty,0)
. By dominated convergence theorem, as
n\to\infty
,

\int_{(-\infty,0)}[\psi(\mathbb{P}(X+m^{n}>y\;|\;\mathscr{F}_{t})(\omega))-1]\dif y\rightarrow\int_{(-\infty,0)}[\psi(\mathbb{P}(X+m>y\;|\;\mathscr{F}_{t})(\omega))-1]\dif y.

(B.20)

Combining (
B.19
) and (
B.20
), and obtain

\rho_{t}^{\psi}(X+m^{n})(\omega)\rightarrow\rho_{t}^{\psi}(X+m)(\omega),\quad n\to\infty.

(B.21)

It is enough to show that,

\rho_{t}^{\psi}(X+m^{n})(\omega)=\rho_{t}^{\psi}(X)(\omega)-m^{n}(\omega),

(B.22)

since this combined with (
B.21
) imply the desired equality
\rho_{t}^{\psi}(X+m)(\omega)=\rho_{t}^{\psi}(X)(\omega)-m(\omega)
. Let us prove next (
B.22
), for which we put
J_{2}:=\rho_{t}^{\psi}(X+m^{n})
. Then,

\displaystyle J_{2}

\displaystyle=\int_{[0,\infty)}\psi(\mathbb{P}(-X-m^{n}>y\;|\;\mathscr{F}_{t}))\dif y+\int_{(-\infty,0)}\left[\psi(\mathbb{P}(-X-m^{n}>y\;|\;\mathscr{F}_{t}))-1\right]\dif y

\displaystyle=\int_{[0,\infty)}\psi\left(\mathbb{P}\left(-X-\sum_{i=1}^{N_{n}}y_{i}\mathbbm{1}_{A_{i}}>y\;|\;\mathscr{F}_{t}\right)\right)\dif y

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad+\int_{(-\infty,0)}\left[\psi\left(\mathbb{P}\left(-X-\sum_{i=1}^{N_{n}}y_{i}1_{A_{i}}>y\;|\;\mathscr{F}_{t}\right)\right)-1\right]\dif y

\displaystyle=\int_{[0,\infty)}\psi\left(\mathbb{P}\left(-X>\sum_{i=1}^{N_{n}}(y+y_{i})\mathbbm{1}_{A_{i}}\;|\;\mathscr{F}_{t}\right)\right)\dif y

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad+\int_{(-\infty,0)}\left[\psi\left(\mathbb{P}\left(-X>\sum_{i=1}^{N_{n}}(y+y_{i})\mathbbm{1}_{A_{i}}\;|\;\mathscr{F}_{t}\right)\right)-1\right]\dif y

\displaystyle=\int_{[0,\infty)}\psi\left(\mathbb{E}\left(\mathbbm{1}_{-X>\sum_{i=1}^{N_{n}}(y+y_{i})\mathbbm{1}_{A_{i}}}\sum_{i=1}^{N_{n}}\mathbbm{1}_{A_{i}}\;|\;\mathscr{F}_{t}\right)\right)\dif y

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad+\int_{(-\infty,0)}\left[\psi\left(\mathbb{E}\left(\mathbbm{1}_{-X>\sum_{i=1}^{N_{n}}(y+y_{i})\mathbbm{1}_{A_{i}}}\sum_{i=1}^{N_{n}}\mathbbm{1}_{A_{i}}\;|\;\mathscr{F}_{t}\right)\right)-1\right]\dif y

\displaystyle=\int_{[0,\infty)}\psi\left(\mathbb{E}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{N_{n}}\mathbbm{1}_{-X>(y+y_{i})}\mathbbm{1}_{A_{i}}\;|\;\mathscr{F}_{t}\right)\right)\dif y

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad+\int_{(-\infty,0)}\left[\psi\left(\mathbb{E}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{N_{n}}\mathbbm{1}_{-X>(y+y_{i})}\mathbbm{1}_{A_{i}}\;|\;\mathscr{F}_{t}\right)\right)-1\right]\dif y

\displaystyle=\int_{[0,\infty)}\psi\left(\sum_{i=1}^{N_{n}}\mathbbm{1}_{A_{i}}\mathbb{E}\left(\mathbbm{1}_{-X>(y+y_{i})}\;|\;\mathscr{F}_{t}\right)\right)\dif y

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad+\int_{(-\infty,0)}\left[\psi\left(\sum_{i=1}^{N_{n}}\mathbbm{1}_{A_{i}}\mathbb{E}\left(\mathbbm{1}_{-X>(y+y_{i})}\;|\;\mathscr{F}_{t}\right)\right)-1\right]\dif y

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{N_{n}}\mathbbm{1}_{A_{i}}\int_{[0,\infty)}\psi\left(\mathbb{P}\left(-X>(y+y_{i})\;|\;\mathscr{F}_{t}\right)\right)\dif y

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad+\sum_{i=1}^{N_{n}}\mathbbm{1}_{A_{i}}\int_{(-\infty,0)}\left[\psi\left(\mathbb{P}\left(-X>(y+y_{i})\;|\;\mathscr{F}_{t}\right)\right)-1\right]\dif y.

Take
z_{i}=y+y_{i}
, and since
y_{i}>0
, we continue the proof as follows,

\displaystyle J_{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=0}^{N_{n}}\mathbbm{1}_{A_{i}}\left\{\int_{[y_{i},\infty)}\psi(\mathbb{P}(-X>z_{i}\;|\;\mathscr{F}_{t}))\dif z_{i}+\int_{(-\infty,y_{i})}[\psi(\mathbb{P}(-X>z_{i}\;|\;\mathscr{F}_{t}))-1]\dif z_{i}\right\}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=0}^{N_{n}}\mathbbm{1}_{A_{i}}\left\{\int_{[y_{i},\infty)}\psi(\mathbb{P}(-X>z_{i}\;|\;\mathscr{F}_{t}))\dif z_{i}+\int_{[0,y_{i})}\left[\psi(\mathbb{P}(-X>z_{i}\;|\;\mathscr{F}_{t}))-1\right]\dif z_{i}\right\}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad+\sum_{i=0}^{N_{n}}\mathbbm{1}_{A_{i}}\left\{\int_{(-\infty,0)}[\psi(\mathbb{P}(-X>z_{i}\;|\;\mathscr{F}_{t}))-1]\dif z_{i}\right\}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=0}^{N_{n}}\mathbbm{1}_{A_{i}}\left\{\int_{[0,\infty)}\psi(\mathbb{P}(-X>z_{i}\;|\;\mathscr{F}_{t}))\dif z_{i}+\int_{(-\infty,0)}\left[\psi(\mathbb{P}(-X>z_{i}\;|\;\mathscr{F}_{t}))-1\right]\dif z_{i}\right\}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad-\sum_{i=0}^{N_{n}}y_{i}\mathbbm{1}_{A_{i}}

\displaystyle=\int_{[0,\infty)}\psi(\mathbb{P}(-X>z\;|\;\mathscr{F}_{t}))\dif z+\int_{(-\infty,0)}\left[\psi(\mathbb{P}(-X>z\;|\;\mathscr{F}_{t}))-1\right]\dif z-m^{n}

\displaystyle=\rho_{t}^{\psi}(X)-m^{n}.

Thus, (
B.22
) is proved.

For general
m\in L^{\infty}
, denote by
M
its essential supremum, and let let
\hat{m}=m+M\geq 0
. By cash additivity of
\rho_{t}^{\psi}
for non-negative
\hat{m}
,

\rho_{t}^{\psi}(X+\hat{m})(\omega)=\rho_{t}^{\psi}(X)(\omega)-\hat{m}(\omega)=\rho_{t}^{\psi}(X)(\omega)-m(\omega)-M.

(B.23)

Also by cash additivity of
\rho_{t}^{\psi}
for constant
M
, i.e.
\rho_{t}^{\psi}(X+M)(\omega)=\rho_{t}^{\psi}(X)(\omega)-M
, we have

\rho_{t}^{\psi}(X+\hat{m})(\omega)=\rho_{t}^{\psi}(X+m+M)(\omega)=\rho_{t}^{\psi}(X+m)(\omega)-M.

(B.24)

Combine (
B.23
) and (
B.24
), and deduce that
\rho_{t}^{\psi}(X+m)(\omega)=\rho_{t}^{\psi}(X)(\omega)-m(\omega)
. Thus, cash-additivity is established.

Monotonicity.
For any
X,Y\in L^{\infty}
with
X\leq Y
, we have for any
y\in\mathbb{R}
,
\mathbb{P}(-X>y\;|\;\mathscr{F}_{t})\geq\mathbb{P}(-Y>y\;|\;\mathscr{F}_{t})
. Since
\psi
is increasing,
\psi(\mathbb{P}(-X>y\;|\;\mathscr{F}_{t}))\geq\psi(\mathbb{P}(-Y>y\;|\;\mathscr{F}_{t}))
. Then we have,

\displaystyle\rho_{t}^{\psi}(X)

\displaystyle=\int_{[0,\infty)}\psi(\mathbb{P}(-X>y\;|\;\mathscr{F}_{t}))\dif y+\int_{(-\infty,0)}[\psi(\mathbb{P}(-X>y\;|\;\mathscr{F}_{t}))-1]\dif y

\displaystyle\geq\int_{[0,\infty)}\psi(\mathbb{P}(-Y>y\;|\;\mathscr{F}_{t}))\dif y+\int_{(-\infty,0)}[\psi(\mathbb{P}(-Y>y\;|\;\mathscr{F}_{t}))-1]\dif y=\rho_{t}^{\psi}(Y).

Sub-additivity.
This part of the proof is inspired by
[
FS04
, Lemma 4.92]
.

First, we observe that
\rho_{t}^{\psi}
is Lipschitz continuous with respect to the
L^{\infty}
norm, i.e. for given
X
,
Y\in L^{\infty}
,

\|\rho_{t}^{\psi}(X)-\rho_{t}^{\psi}(Y)\|_{\infty}\leq\|X-Y\|_{\infty}.

(B.25)

Indeed, since
X\leq Y+\|X-Y\|_{\infty}
, by monotonicity of
\rho_{t}^{\psi}
, we have

\rho_{t}^{\psi}(X)\geq\rho_{t}^{\psi}(Y+\|X-Y\|_{\infty})
, and according to cash additivity of
\rho_{t}^{\psi}
,
\rho_{t}^{\psi}(X)\geq\rho_{t}^{\psi}(Y)-\|X-Y\|_{\infty}.
Reversing the roles of
X
and
Y
yields (
B.25
).

In view of Lemma
B.3
, given
\psi\in\Upsilon
and
t\in\mathcal{T}
, for any
\omega\in\Omega\backslash N^{X}
, we build the probability measure
Q^{\omega}
, such that for any non-negative step function
X=\sum_{i=1}^{n}x_{i}\mathbbm{1}_{A_{i}}
,

\sum_{i=1}^{n}-x_{i}Q^{\omega}[A_{i}]\leq\rho_{t}^{\psi}(X)(\omega).

In particular, the equality holds if
x_{1}\leq\ldots\leq x_{n}
. Now if
X
is not non-negative, take
\hat{X}=X+M\geq 0
, where
M=\esssup X
. By cash additivity of
\rho_{t}^{\psi}
,

\rho_{t}^{\psi}(\hat{X})(\omega)=\rho_{t}^{\psi}(X+M)(\omega)=\rho_{t}^{\psi}(X)(\omega)-M.

Since
\hat{X}\geq 0
,

\rho_{t}^{\psi}(\hat{X})(\omega)\geq\sum_{i=1}^{n}[-x_{i}-N]Q^{\omega}(A_{i})=\sum_{i=1}^{n}-x_{i}Q^{\omega}(A_{i})-M.

Then we have,

\rho_{t}^{\psi}(X)(\omega)\geq\sum_{i=1}^{n}-x_{i}Q^{\omega}(A_{i}).

and therefore Lemma
B.3
holds true all step functions.

Now back to the proof of sub additivity of
\rho_{t}^{\psi}
. Given
X
,
Y\in L^{\infty}
, there exist two sequences of step functions
X^{n}
and
Y^{n}
converging to
X
and
Y
respectively in
L^{\infty}
. Then, by (
B.25
), to prove sub-additivity, it is enough to show that, for any
\omega\in\Omega\backslash N^{X}
,

\rho_{t}^{\psi}(X^{n}+Y^{n})(\omega)\leq\rho_{t}^{\psi}(X^{n})(\omega)+\rho_{t}^{\psi}(Y^{n})(\omega).

(B.26)

For fixed
n
, let
A_{1},\ldots,A_{m}
be the (disjoint) partition of underlying probability space
\Omega
, such that
X^{n}=\sum_{i=1}^{m}x_{i}\mathbbm{1}_{A_{i}}
and
Y^{n}=\sum_{i=1}^{m}y_{i}\mathbbm{1}_{A_{i}}
, and assume that the indices
i=1,\ldots,m
are arranged such that
x_{1}+y_{1}\leq\ldots\leq x_{m}+y_{m}
. In view of generalized version of Lemma
B.3
, for any
\omega\in\Omega\backslash N^{X}
, one can construct a probability
Q^{\omega}
, such that,

\rho_{t}^{\psi}(X^{n}+Y^{n})(\omega)=\sum_{i=1}^{m}(-x_{i}-y_{i})Q^{\omega}[A_{i}]=\sum_{i=1}^{m}-x_{i}Q^{\omega}[A_{i}]-y_{i}Q^{\omega}[A_{i}].

and

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{m}-x_{i}Q^{\omega}[A_{i}]\leq\rho_{t}^{\psi}(X^{n})(\omega),\quad\sum_{i=1}^{m}-y_{i}Q^{\omega}[A_{i}]\leq\rho_{t}^{\psi}(Y^{n})(\omega).

In conclusion,

\rho_{t}^{\psi}(X^{n}+Y^{n})(\omega)=\sum_{i=1}^{m}-x_{i}Q^{\omega}[A_{i}]-y_{i}Q^{\omega}[A_{i}]\leq\rho_{t}^{\psi}(X^{n})(\omega)+\rho_{t}^{\psi}(Y^{n})(\omega),

and thus (
B.26
) is proved.

Positive Homogeneity.

For any
\lambda\in L^{\infty}_{t,+}
, there exists a sequence of step functions,

\lambda^{n}:=\sum_{i=1}^{N_{n}}y_{i}\mathbbm{1}_{A_{i}},\hskip 8.53581pty_{i}\geq 0,\hskip 8.53581ptA_{i}\in\mathscr{F}_{t},

increasing and convergent to
\lambda
in
L^{\infty}
, where
N_{n}
is a constant and
A_{i}
are disjoint. Note that for any
n\in\mathbb{N}
, there exists a null set
N^{X,\lambda^{n},t}\in\mathscr{F}_{t}
, such that for any
\omega\in\Omega\backslash N^{X,\lambda^{n},t}
,
\mathbb{P}(\lambda^{n}X>y\;|\;\mathscr{F}_{t})(\omega)
is a regular distribution.

Let
M=\bigcup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\bigcup_{t\in\mathcal{T}}N^{X,\lambda^{n},t}\bigcup N^{X,\lambda}
, since
M
is a countable union of null sets, it is still a null set. For any
X\in L^{\infty}
and fixed
y\in[0,\infty)
,
\{\lambda^{n}X>y\}
is an increasing sequence of sets. For any
\omega\in\Omega\backslash M
, by continuity of probability for increasing sequence of sets,

\mathbb{P}(\lambda^{n}X>y\;|\;\mathscr{F}_{t})(\omega)\nearrow\mathbb{P}(\lambda X>y\;|\;\mathscr{F}_{t})(\omega),\quad n\to\infty.

By continuity of
\psi
, as
n\to\infty
,

\psi(\mathbb{P}(\lambda^{n}X>y\;|\;\mathscr{F}_{t})(\omega))\nearrow\psi(\mathbb{P}(\lambda X>y\;|\;\mathscr{F}_{t})(\omega)).

In view of the monotone convergence theorem,

\int_{0}^{\infty}\psi(\mathbb{P}(\lambda^{n}X>y\;|\;\mathscr{F}_{t})(\omega))\dif y\xrightarrow[n\to\infty]{}\int_{0}^{\infty}\psi(\mathbb{P}(\lambda X>y\;|\;\mathscr{F}_{t})(\omega))\dif y.

(B.27)

Similarly, one can show that

\int_{-\infty}^{0}[\psi(\mathbb{P}(\lambda^{n}X>y\;|\;\mathscr{F}_{t})(\omega))-1]\dif y\xrightarrow[n\to\infty]{}\int_{-\infty}^{0}[\psi(\mathbb{P}(\lambda X>y\;|\;\mathscr{F}_{t})(\omega))-1]\dif y.

(B.28)

Combining (
B.27
) and (
B.28
), we obtain,

\rho_{t}^{\psi}(\lambda^{n}X)(\omega)\xrightarrow[n\to\infty]{}\rho_{t}^{\psi}(\lambda X)(\omega).

From here, the desired equality
\rho_{t}^{\psi}(\lambda X)(\omega)=\lambda\rho_{t}^{\psi}(X)(\omega)
will follow once we show that

\rho_{t}^{\psi}(\lambda^{n}X)(\omega)=\lambda^{n}\rho_{t}^{\psi}(X)(\omega),

which we prove next.

\displaystyle\rho_{t}^{\psi}(\lambda^{n}X)

\displaystyle=\int_{[0,\infty)}\psi(\mathbb{P}(-\lambda^{n}X>y\;|\;\mathscr{F}_{t}))\dif y+\int_{(-\infty,0)}[\psi\left(\mathbb{P}\left(-\lambda^{n}X>y\;|\;\mathscr{F}_{t}\right)\right)-1]\dif y

\displaystyle=\int_{[0,\infty)}\psi\left(\mathbb{P}\left(-\sum_{i=1}^{N_{n}}y_{i}\mathbbm{1}_{A_{i}}X>y\;|\;\mathscr{F}_{t}\right)\right)\dif y

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad+\int_{(-\infty,0)}\left[\psi\left(\mathbb{P}\left(-\sum_{i=1}^{N_{n}}y_{i}\mathbbm{1}_{A_{i}}X>y\;|\;\mathscr{F}_{t}\right)\right)-1\right]\dif y

\displaystyle=\int_{[0,\infty)}\psi\left(\mathbb{P}\left(-X>\sum_{i=1}^{N_{n}}(y/y_{i})\mathbbm{1}_{A_{i}}\;|\;\mathscr{F}_{t}\right)\right)\dif y

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad+\int_{(-\infty,0)}\left[\psi\left(\mathbb{P}\left(-X>\sum_{i=1}^{N_{n}}(y/y_{i})\mathbbm{1}_{A_{i}}\;|\;\mathscr{F}_{t}\right)\right)-1\right]\dif y

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}\psi\left(\mathbb{E}\left(\mathbbm{1}_{-X>\sum_{i=1}^{N_{n}}(y/y_{i})\mathbbm{1}_{A_{i}}}\sum_{i=1}^{N_{n}}\mathbbm{1}_{A_{i}}\;|\;\mathscr{F}_{t}\right)\right)\dif y

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad+\int_{-\infty}^{0}\left[\psi\left(\mathbb{E}\left(\mathbbm{1}_{-X>\sum_{i=1}^{N_{n}}(y/y_{i})\mathbbm{1}_{A_{i}}}\sum_{i=1}^{N_{n}}\mathbbm{1}_{A_{i}}\;|\;\mathscr{F}_{t}\right)\right)-1\right]\dif y

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}\psi\left(\mathbb{E}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{N_{n}}\mathbbm{1}_{-X>y/y_{i}}\mathbbm{1}_{A_{i}}\;|\;\mathscr{F}_{t}\right)\right)\dif y

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad+\int_{-\infty}^{0}\left[\psi\left(\mathbb{E}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{N_{n}}\mathbbm{1}_{-X>y/y_{i}}\mathbbm{1}_{A_{i}}\;|\;\mathscr{F}_{t}\right)\right)-1\right]\dif y

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}\psi\left(\sum_{i=1}^{N_{n}}\mathbbm{1}_{A_{i}}\mathbb{E}\left(\mathbbm{1}_{-X>y/y_{i}}\;|\;\mathscr{F}_{t}\right)\right)\dif y

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad+\int_{-\infty}^{0}\left[\psi\left(\sum_{i=1}^{N_{n}}\mathbbm{1}_{A_{i}}\mathbb{E}\left(\mathbbm{1}_{-X>y/y_{i}}\;|\;\mathscr{F}_{t}\right)\right)-1\right]\dif y

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{N_{n}}\mathbbm{1}_{A_{i}}\int_{0}^{\infty}\psi\left(\mathbb{P}\left(-X>y/y_{i}\;|\;\mathscr{F}_{t}\right)\right)\dif y

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad+\sum_{i=1}^{N_{n}}\mathbbm{1}_{A_{i}}\int_{-\infty}^{0}[\psi\left(\mathbb{P}\left(-X>y/y_{i})\;|\;\mathscr{F}_{t}\right)\right)-1]\dif y

\displaystyle\overset{z_{i}=y/y_{i}}{=}\sum_{i=1}^{N_{n}}\mathbbm{1}_{A_{i}}y_{i}\Big\{\int_{0}^{\infty}\psi\left(\mathbb{P}\left(-X>z_{i}\;|\;\mathscr{F}_{t}\right)\right)\dif z_{i}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad+\int_{-\infty}^{0}[\psi\left(\mathbb{P}\left(-X>z_{i}\;|\;\mathscr{F}_{t}\right)\right)-1]\dif z_{i}\Big\}

\displaystyle=\lambda^{n}\rho_{t}^{\psi}(X).

Note that in the third equality, we assume
y_{i}>0
for all
i\in I
, otherwise just omit the terms in the sums with
y_{j}=0
.

The proof is complete.

Next results gives a convenient representation of the DCRMs generated by distortion functions.

Lemma B.6
.

For any
\psi\in\Upsilon
, the following representation holds

\rho_{t}^{\psi}(X)=-\int_{\mathbb{R}}y\dif\psi(\mathbb{P}(X\leq y\;|\;\mathscr{F}_{t})).

(B.29)

Proof.

In the space
BV
of functions with bounded variation, consider the set

NBV:=\{F\in BV\;|\;F\hskip 5.69054pt\text{is right continuous and}\hskip 5.69054ptF(-\infty)=0\}.

It can be shown (cf.
[
Fol99
, Theorem 3.36, and Exercise 34(b), p.108]
) that if
F,G\in NBV
and there are no points in
[a,b]
where
F
and
G
are both discontinuous, then,

\int_{[a,b]}F(y)\dif G(y)=F(b)G(b)-F(a-)G(a-)-\int_{[a,b]}G(y)\dif F(y).

Let
a
and
b
be the essential infimum and the essential supremum of
X
respectively and assume that
a<0<b
. Notice that (potential) discontinuities in the distribution function
F_{X}
are at most countable, and hence the Lebesgue integrals below are not affected. For any
\omega\in\Omega\backslash N^{X}
, we deduce

\displaystyle\rho_{t}^{\psi}(X)(\omega)

\displaystyle=\int_{[0,-a]}\psi(\mathbb{P}(-X>y\;|\;\mathscr{F}_{t})(\omega))\dif y+\int_{[-b,0)}(\psi(\mathbb{P}(-X>y\;|\;\mathscr{F}_{t})(\omega))-1)\dif y

\displaystyle=\int_{[0,-a]}\psi(\mathbb{P}(X<-y\;|\;\mathscr{F}_{t})(\omega))\dif y+\int_{[-b,0)}\left(\psi(\mathbb{P}(X<-y\;|\;\mathscr{F}_{t})(\omega))-1\right)\dif y

\displaystyle=\int_{[0,-a]}\psi(\mathbb{P}(X\leq-y\;|\;\mathscr{F}_{t})(\omega))\dif y+\int_{[-b,0)}\left(\psi(\mathbb{P}(X\leq-y\;|\;\mathscr{F}_{t})(\omega))-1\right)\dif y

\displaystyle=\int_{[a,0]}\psi(\mathbb{P}(X\leq z\;|\;\mathscr{F}_{t})(\omega))\dif z+\int_{(0,b]}\left(\psi(\mathbb{P}(X\leq z\;|\;\mathscr{F}_{t})(\omega))\right)\dif z-b

\displaystyle=\psi(\mathbb{P}(X\leq z\;|\;\mathscr{F}_{t})(\omega))z|_{a-}^{0}-\int_{[a,0]}z\dif\psi(\mathbb{P}(X\leq z\;|\;\mathscr{F}_{t})(\omega))

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad+\psi(\mathbb{P}(X\leq z\;|\;\mathscr{F}_{t})(\omega))z|_{0}^{b}-\int_{(0,b]}z\dif\left(\psi(\mathbb{P}(X\leq z\;|\;\mathscr{F}_{t})(\omega))\right)-b

\displaystyle=-\int_{[a,0]}z\dif\psi(\mathbb{P}(X\leq z\;|\;\mathscr{F}_{t})(\omega))-\int_{(0,b]}\dif\left(\psi(\mathbb{P}(X\leq z\;|\;\mathscr{F}_{t})(\omega))\right)

\displaystyle=-\int_{[a,b]}z\dif\psi(\mathbb{P}(X\leq z\;|\;\mathscr{F}_{t})(\omega)).

∎

Remark B.7
.

We note that left continuity of
\psi
is sufficient to prove positive homogeneity of
\rho^{\psi}_{t}
, however the right continuity of
\psi
is not only needed for proving cash additivity and positive homogeneity of
\rho^{\psi}_{t}
, but also serves as a sufficient condition for representation (
3.4
) to hold.

B.2
On DCAIs

In this section we present some fundamental properties on DCAIs. Throughout this section we assume additionally the probability space is atomless. One of the reasons to invoke atomless property is due to the fact that a probability space supports random variables with continuous distribution if and only if such probability space is atomless; cf.
[
FS04
, Proposition A.27]
.

We start with several results about families of distortion functions and the corresponding DCRMs.

Lemma B.8
.

A family of DCRMs
(\rho^{\psi_{x}})_{x>0}
is increasing if and only if
(\psi_{x})_{x>0}
is an increasing family of distortion functions.
Moreover, a family of DCRMs
(\rho^{\psi_{x}})_{x>0}
is right continuous if and only if
(\psi_{x})_{x>0}
is a right continuous family of distortion functions.

Proof.

We start with the first part of the assertion on monotonicity.

(
\Leftarrow
) Using Choquet representation (
3.3
) of
\rho_{t}^{\psi_{x}}
, for any
X\in L^{\infty}
,
\omega\in\Omega\backslash N^{X}
,
t\in\mathcal{T}
and
0<x_{1}\leq x_{2}
, we get

\displaystyle\rho^{\psi_{x_{2}}}_{t}(X)(\omega)-

\displaystyle\rho^{\psi_{x_{1}}}_{t}(X)(\omega)=\int_{[0,\infty)}\left[\psi_{x_{2}}(\mathbb{P}(-X>y\;|\;\mathscr{F}_{t})(\omega))-\psi_{x_{1}}(\mathbb{P}(-X>y\;|\;\mathscr{F}_{t})(\omega))\right]\dif y

\displaystyle+\int_{(-\infty,0)}\left[\psi_{x_{2}}(\mathbb{P}(-X>y\;|\;\mathscr{F}_{t})(\omega))-\psi_{x_{1}}(\mathbb{P}(-X>y\;|\;\mathscr{F}_{t})(\omega))\right]\dif y\geq 0.

(
\Rightarrow
) Proof by contradiction. Assume that there exist
0<x_{1}\leq x_{2}
and
y^{\star}\in[0,1]
, such that
\psi_{x_{1}}(y^{\star})>\psi_{x_{2}}(y^{\star})
. Since the probability space is atomless, we take
X=1
with probability
y^{\star}
and
X=2
with probability
1-y^{\star}
. When
t=0
,

\displaystyle\rho^{\psi_{x_{2}}}_{0}(X)-\rho^{\psi_{x_{1}}}_{0}(X)

\displaystyle=\int_{[0,\infty)}\left[\psi_{x_{2}}(\mathbb{P}(-X>y))-\psi_{x_{1}}(\mathbb{P}(-X>y))\right]\dif y

\displaystyle\qquad+\int_{(-\infty,0)}\left[\psi_{x_{2}}(\mathbb{P}(-X>y))-\psi_{x_{1}}(\mathbb{P}(-X>y))\right]\dif y

\displaystyle=\int_{[-2,-1)}\left[\psi_{x_{2}}(\mathbb{P}(-X>y))-\psi_{x_{1}}(\mathbb{P}(-X>y))\right]\dif y

\displaystyle=\psi_{x_{2}}(y^{\star})-\psi_{x_{1}}(y^{\star})<0,

which contradicts the fact that
(\rho^{\psi_{x}})_{x>0}
is an increasing family of DCRMs.

Next we focus on the second part of the lemma about the right continuity.

(
\Leftarrow
) Let
a
and
b
be the essential infimum and the essential supremum of
X
respectively and assume that
a<b
. For any
z>x
and
\omega\in\Omega\backslash N^{X}
,

\displaystyle\rho^{\psi_{z}}_{t}(X)(\omega)

\displaystyle=\int_{[0,\infty)}\psi_{z}(\mathbb{P}(-X>y\;|\;\mathscr{F}_{t})(\omega))\dif y+\int_{(-\infty,0)}\left[\psi_{z}(\mathbb{P}(-X>y\;|\;\mathscr{F}_{t})(\omega))-1\right]\dif y

\displaystyle=\int_{[0,-a)}\psi_{z}(\mathbb{P}(-X>y\;|\;\mathscr{F}_{t})(\omega))\dif y+\int_{(-b,0)}\left[\psi_{z}(\mathbb{P}(-X>y\;|\;\mathscr{F}_{t})(\omega))-1\right]\dif y.

For any
y\in[0,-a)
,
|\psi_{z}(\mathbb{P}(-X>y|\mathscr{F}_{t})(\omega))|\leq 1
, and thus

\int_{[0,-a)}|\psi_{z}(\mathbb{P}(-X>y|\mathscr{F}_{t})(\omega))|\dif y\leq\int_{[0,-a)}1\dif y=a.

Similarly, for any
y\in(-b,0)
,
|\psi_{z}(\mathbb{P}(-X>y|\mathscr{F}_{t})(\omega))-1|\leq 1
, then

\int_{(-b,0)}|\psi_{z}(\mathbb{P}(-X>y|\mathscr{F}_{t})(\omega))-1|\dif y\leq b.

Since
(\psi_{x})_{x>0}
is a right continuous family of distortion functions, then by the dominated convergence theorem,

\displaystyle\lim_{z\rightarrow x^{+}}\int_{[0,-a)}\psi_{z}(\mathbb{P}(-X>y|\mathscr{F}_{t})(\omega))\dif y=\int_{[0,-a)}\psi_{x}(\mathbb{P}(-X>y|\mathscr{F}_{t})(\omega))\dif y,

\displaystyle\lim_{z\rightarrow x^{+}}\int_{(-b,0)}[\psi_{z}(\mathbb{P}(-X>y|\mathscr{F}_{t})(\omega))-1]\dif y=\int_{(-b,0)}[\psi_{x}(\mathbb{P}(-X>y|\mathscr{F}_{t})(\omega))-1]\dif y.

Combining the above two identities, we have,

\lim_{z\rightarrow x^{+}}\rho^{\psi_{z}}_{t}(X)(\omega)=\rho^{\psi_{x}}_{t}(X)(\omega).

(
\Rightarrow
) Proof by contradiction. Assume that there exists
x_{0}>0
and
y^{\star}\in[0,1]
, such that
\lim\limits_{{z\rightarrow x_{0}^{+}}}\psi_{z}(y^{\star})\neq\psi_{x_{0}}(y^{\star})
. Since the probability space is atomless, we take
X=1
with probability
y^{\star}
and
X=2
with probability
1-y^{\star}
. When
t=0
,

\displaystyle\lim_{z\rightarrow{x_{0}}^{+}}\rho^{\psi_{z}}_{0}(X)

\displaystyle=\lim_{z\rightarrow x_{0}^{+}}\left[\int_{[0,\infty)}\psi_{z}(\mathbb{P}(-X>y))+\int_{(-\infty,0)}\left[\psi_{z}(\mathbb{P}(-X>y))-1\right]\dif y\right]

\displaystyle=\lim_{z\rightarrow x_{0}^{+}}\int_{[-2,-1)}\left[\psi_{z}(\mathbb{P}(-X>y))-1\right]\dif y

\displaystyle=\lim_{z\rightarrow x_{0}^{+}}\psi_{z}(y^{\star})-1

\displaystyle\neq\psi_{x_{0}}(y^{\star})-1

\displaystyle=\rho^{\psi_{x_{0}}}_{0}(X),

which contradicts the fact that
(\rho^{\psi_{x}})_{x>0}
is a right continuous family of DCRMs.

This completes the proof.
∎

Remark B.9
.

The assumption at the beginning of this section that the probability space is atomless is important in Lemma
3.10
to ensure that
X=1
with probability
y^{\star}
exists. If atomless property does not hold, then generally speaking the ‘only if’ directions in Lemma
3.10
does not hold true, as Example
B.10
and Example
B.11
below show. However, it is worth mentioning that a probability space with atoms may still support Lemma
3.10
for random variables with distributions not affected by atoms.

Example B.10
.

We will show that, generally speaking, an increasing family of DCRMs
(\rho^{\psi_{x}})_{x>0}
does not imply that the corresponding family of distortions
(\psi_{x})_{x>0}
is increasing.

Let
\mathcal{T}:=\{0,1\}
, and consider the filtered probability space
(\Omega,\mathscr{F},\{\mathscr{F}_{t}\}_{t\in\mathcal{T}},\mathbb{P})
, with
\Omega=\{\omega_{1},\omega_{2}\}
,
\mathscr{F}_{0}=\{\emptyset,\Omega\}
,
\mathscr{F}_{1}=\mathscr{F}=2^{\Omega}
and
\mathbb{P}(\omega_{1})=\mathbb{P}(\omega_{2})=\frac{1}{2}
.

Suppose
m>0>n
, and let
X=m
with probability
\frac{1}{2}
and
X=n
with probability
\frac{1}{2}
. For any
0<x_{1}\leq x_{2}
and
X\in L^{\infty}
,
\rho^{\psi_{x_{2}}}_{1}(X)-\rho^{\psi_{x_{1}}}_{1}(X)=-X+X=0
. On the other hand,

\displaystyle\rho^{\psi_{x_{2}}}_{0}(X)-\rho^{\psi_{x_{1}}}_{0}(X)

\displaystyle=\int_{[0,\infty)}\left[\psi_{x_{2}}(\mathbb{P}(-X>y))-\psi_{x_{1}}(\mathbb{P}(-X>y))\right]\dif y

\displaystyle\qquad+\int_{(-\infty,0)}\left[\psi_{x_{2}}(\mathbb{P}(-X>y))-\psi_{x_{1}}(\mathbb{P}(-X>y))\right]\dif y

\displaystyle=\int_{[0,-n)}\left[\psi_{x_{2}}(\mathbb{P}(-X>y))-\psi_{x_{1}}(\mathbb{P}(-X>y))\right]\dif y

\displaystyle\qquad+\int_{(-m,0)}\left[\psi_{x_{2}}(\mathbb{P}(-X>y))-\psi_{x_{1}}(\mathbb{P}(-X>y))\right]\dif y

\displaystyle=(m-n)\left(\psi_{x_{2}}\left(\frac{1}{2}\right)-\psi_{x_{1}}\left(\frac{1}{2}\right)\right).

Thus,
(\rho^{\psi_{x}})_{x>0}
is an increasing family of DCRMs, as long as
\psi_{x_{1}}(\frac{1}{2})\leq\psi_{x_{2}}(\frac{1}{2})
, for any
0<x_{1}\leq x_{2}
,
Clearly, one can take
[y_{1},y_{2}]\subset[0,1]
with
y_{1}>\frac{1}{2}
, such that
\psi_{x_{1}}(y)>\psi_{x_{2}}(y)
for all
y\in(y_{1},y_{2})
and
\psi_{x_{1}}(y)\leq\psi_{x_{2}}(y)
on remaining interval, which makes
(\psi_{x})_{x>0}
not an increasing family of distortion functions.

Example B.11
.

Consider the same probability space as in Example
B.10
.
We will show that a right continuous family of DCRMs
(\rho^{\psi_{x}})_{x>0}
does not imply that
(\psi_{x})_{x>0}
is a right continuous family of distortion functions.

Suppose
m>0>n
, and let
X=m
with probability
\frac{1}{2}
, and
X=n
with probability
\frac{1}{2}
. For any
x>0
and
X\in L^{\infty}
,
\lim\limits_{z\rightarrow x^{+}}\rho^{\psi_{z}}_{1}(X)-\rho^{\psi_{x}}_{1}(X)=-X+X=0
, while

\displaystyle\lim_{z\rightarrow{x}^{+}}\rho^{\psi_{z}}_{0}(X)

\displaystyle=\lim_{z\rightarrow x^{+}}\left[\int_{[0,\infty)}\psi_{z}(\mathbb{P}(-X>y))+\int_{(-\infty,0)}\left[\psi_{z}(\mathbb{P}(-X>y))-1\right]dy\right]

\displaystyle=\lim_{z\rightarrow x^{+}}\left[\int_{[0,-n)}\psi_{z}(\mathbb{P}(-X>y))dy+\int_{(-m,0)}\left[\psi_{z}(\mathbb{P}(-X>y))-1\right]dy\right]

\displaystyle=(m-n)\lim_{z\rightarrow x^{+}}\psi_{z}\left(\frac{1}{2}\right)-m.

Hence, as long as for any
x>0
,
\lim\limits_{z\rightarrow x^{+}}\psi_{z}(\frac{1}{2})=\psi_{x}(\frac{1}{2})
, we have
(\rho^{\psi_{x}})_{x>0}
is a right continuous family of DCRMs. Obviously, by taking
\psi_{x},x>0
, such that
\lim\limits_{z\rightarrow x^{+}}\psi_{z}(y)\neq\psi_{x}(y)
for any
y\neq\frac{1}{2}
, implies that
(\psi_{x})_{x>0}
is not a right continuous family of distortion functions.

We are ready to present the main result of this section.

Proposition B.12
.

The mapping
\alpha_{t}^{\Psi}
is a DCAI, that is
\alpha_{t}^{\Psi}
is adapted, local, quasi-concave, monotone increasing and scale invariant.

Proof.

The proof of this result can be obtained by using robust representations results for DCAIs (cf.
[
BBN14
]
or
[
BCDK16
]
). For the sake of completeness, we present here an independent proof by verifying directly the corresponding properties of DCAI.

Adaptiveness.

Consider the set
A^{x}:=\{\omega\in\Omega\;|\;\alpha_{t}^{\Psi}(X)(\omega)\geq x\}
, where
x\in\mathbb{R}_{+}
,
t\in\mathcal{T}
and
X\in L^{\infty}
are fixed. We will show
A^{x}\in\mathscr{F}_{t}
.
Let

\displaystyle B^{x}=\{\omega\in\Omega\;|\;\rho_{t}^{\psi_{x}}(X)(\omega)\leq 0\}.

(B.30)

First we will prove that
A^{x}=B^{x}
. Indeed, for any
\omega\in\{\rho_{t}^{\psi_{x}}(X)\leq 0\}
, we have
x\in\left\{z\in\mathbb{R}_{+}\;|\;\rho_{t}^{\psi_{z}}(X)(\omega)\leq 0\right\}
. Thus,

\displaystyle\alpha_{t}^{\Psi}(X)(\omega)=\sup\left\{z\in\mathbb{R}_{+}\;|\;\rho_{t}^{\psi_{z}}(X)(\omega)\leq 0\right\}\geq x,

(B.31)

and hence
B^{x}\subset A^{x}
.

Next we show that
A^{x}\subset B^{x}
. By (
B.31
),

\omega\in A^{x}\Rightarrow\sup\left\{z\in\mathbb{R}_{+}\;|\;\rho_{t}^{\psi_{z}}(X)(\omega)\leq 0\right\}\geq x.

For any
y<x
, we also have
\sup\left\{z\in\mathbb{R}_{+}\;|\;\rho_{t}^{\psi_{z}}(X)(\omega)\leq 0\right\}>y
, which implies that
\rho_{t}^{\psi_{y}}(X)(\omega)\leq 0
.
Therefore, to show
\omega\in B^{x}
, since
\rho_{t}^{\psi_{x}}(X)(\omega)\leq 0
, it suffices to show that

\displaystyle\lim_{y\uparrow x}\rho_{t}^{\psi_{y}}(X)(\omega)=\rho_{t}^{\psi_{x}}(X)(\omega).

(B.32)

To prove the latter, recall that
\left(\psi_{x}\right)_{x\in\mathbb{R}_{+}}
is a family of continuous distortions pointwise increasing
in
x
, and thus by the monotone convergence theorem, we obtain,

\displaystyle\lim_{y\uparrow x}\int_{0}^{\infty}\psi_{y}(\mathbb{P}(-X>z\;|\;\mathscr{F}_{t})(\omega))\dif z=\int_{0}^{\infty}\psi_{x}(\mathbb{P}(-X>z\;|\;\mathscr{F}_{t})(\omega))\dif z.

(B.33)

Then since,

\lim_{y\uparrow x}\left(\psi_{y}(\mathbb{P}(-X>z\;|\;\mathscr{F}_{t})(\omega))-1\right)=\psi_{x}(\mathbb{P}(-X>z\;|\;\mathscr{F}_{t})(\omega))-1,

and for any
z\in(-\infty,0)
and
y\in\mathbb{R}_{+}
, we have that

|\psi_{y}(\mathbb{P}(-X>z\;|\;\mathscr{F}_{t})(\omega))-1|\leq 1.

Again, by the dominated convergence theorem,

\displaystyle\hskip-31.2982pt\lim_{y\uparrow x}\int^{0}_{-\infty}[\psi_{y}(\mathbb{P}(-X>z\;|\;\mathscr{F}_{t})(\omega))-1]\dif z=\int^{0}_{-\infty}[\psi_{x}(\mathbb{P}(-X>z\;|\;\mathscr{F}_{t})(\omega))-1]\dif z.

(B.34)

With (
B.33
) and (
B.34
) at hand, (
B.32
) follows at once, and thus

A^{x}=B^{x}

(B.35)

is proved.
Measurability of
\rho_{t}^{\psi_{x}}(X)
is verified in the proof of Proposition
3.2
, which implies that
A^{x}\in\mathscr{F}_{t}
.

Locality.

For any
t\in\mathcal{T}
,
X\in L^{\infty}
,
A\in\mathscr{F}_{t}
and
\omega\in\Omega\backslash N^{X}
,

\displaystyle 1_{A}(\omega)\alpha_{t}^{\Psi}(X)(\omega)

\displaystyle=1_{A}(\omega)\sup\Big\{x\in\mathbb{R}_{+}\;|\;\int_{0}^{\infty}\psi_{x}(\mathbb{P}(-X>y\;|\;\mathscr{F}_{t})(\omega))\dif y

\displaystyle\qquad+\int^{0}_{-\infty}[\psi_{x}(\mathbb{P}(-X>y\;|\;\mathscr{F}_{t})(\omega))-1]\dif y\leq 0\Big\}

\displaystyle=1_{A}(\omega)\sup\Big\{x\in\mathbb{R}_{+}\;|\;1_{A}(\omega)\int_{0}^{\infty}\psi_{x}(\mathbb{P}(-X>y\;|\;\mathscr{F}_{t})(\omega))\dif y

\displaystyle\qquad+1_{A}(\omega)\int^{0}_{-\infty}[\psi_{x}(\mathbb{P}(-X>y\;|\;\mathscr{F}_{t})(\omega))-1]\dif y\leq 0\Big\}

\displaystyle=1_{A}(\omega)\sup\Big\{x\in\mathbb{R}_{+}\;|\;\int_{0}^{\infty}\psi_{x}(\mathbb{P}(-1_{A}X>y\;|\;\mathscr{F}_{t})(\omega))\dif y

\displaystyle\qquad+\int^{0}_{-\infty}[\psi_{x}(\mathbb{P}(-1_{A}X>y\;|\;\mathscr{F}_{t})(\omega))-1]\dif y\leq 0\Big\}

\displaystyle=1_{A}(\omega)\alpha^{\Psi}_{t}(1_{A}X)(\omega).

Hence locality of
\alpha_{t}^{\Psi}
is established.

Quasi-concavity.

For any
t\in\mathcal{T}
,
X,Y\in L^{\infty}
and
\omega\in\Omega\backslash N^{X}
. Let
m>0
be some
\mathscr{F}_{t}
-measurable random variable such that
\alpha^{\Psi}_{t}(X)(\omega)\geq m(\omega)
and
\alpha^{\Psi}_{t}\left(Y\right)(\omega)\geq m(\omega)
.

Fix one such
\omega
and denote
m^{\star}=m(\omega)
. By (
B.35
), we have that
\alpha^{\Psi}_{t}(X)(\omega)\geq m^{\star}
and
\alpha^{\Psi}_{t}\left(Y\right)(\omega)\geq m^{\star}
imply that

\rho_{t}^{\psi_{m(\omega)}}(X)(\omega)=\rho_{t}^{\psi_{m^{\star}}}(X)(\omega)\leq 0,

\rho_{t}^{\psi_{m(\omega)}}(Y)(\omega)=\rho_{t}^{\psi_{m^{\star}}}(Y)(\omega)\leq 0,

respectively. Since sublinearity implies convexity, then by sublinearity of
\rho^{\psi}_{t}
,

\displaystyle\rho_{t}^{\psi_{m^{\star}}}\left(\lambda X+(1-\lambda)Y\right)(\omega)\leq\lambda\rho_{t}^{\psi_{m^{\star}}}(X)(\omega)+(1-\lambda)\rho_{t}^{\psi_{m^{\star}}}(Y)(\omega)\leq 0.

(B.36)

By (
B.35
) again, we show (
B.36
) is equivalent to
\alpha^{\Psi}_{t}\left(\lambda X+(1-\lambda)Y\right)(\omega)\geq m^{\star}
.
Therefore, quasi-concavity holds.

Monotonicity.
For any
t\in\mathcal{T}
,
X,Y\in L^{\infty}
with
X\leq Y
and
\omega\in\Omega\backslash N^{X}
. For any
\mathscr{F}_{t}
-measurable random variable
m>0
, such that
\alpha_{t}^{\Psi}(X)(\omega)>m(\omega)
, we have
\rho_{t}^{\psi_{m(\omega)}}(X)(\omega)\leq 0
. By monotonicity of
\rho^{\psi}_{t}
,
\rho_{t}^{\psi_{m(\omega)}}(Y)(\omega)\leq\rho_{t}^{\psi_{m(\omega)}}(X)(\omega)\leq 0
.
Then,
\alpha_{t}^{\Psi}(Y)(\omega)\geq m(\omega)
, and hence
\alpha_{t}^{\Psi}(Y)(\omega)\geq\alpha_{t}^{\Psi}(X)(\omega)
.

Scale Invariance.

For any
t\in\mathcal{T}
,
X\in L^{\infty}
,
\lambda\in L^{\infty}_{t}
with
\lambda>0
, and
\omega\in\Omega\backslash N^{X}
, we have that,

\alpha_{t}^{\Psi}(\lambda X)(\omega)=\sup\left\{x\in\mathbb{R}_{+}\;|\;\rho_{t}^{\psi_{x}}(\lambda X)(\omega)\leq 0\right\}.

In view of the positive homogeneity of
\rho^{\psi}_{t}
,

\alpha_{t}^{\Psi}(\lambda X)(\omega)=\sup\left\{x\in\mathbb{R}_{+}\;|\;\lambda\rho_{t}^{\psi_{x}}(X)(\omega)\leq 0\right\}.

Since
\lambda>0
, then
\lambda\rho_{t}^{\psi_{x}}(X)\leq 0
is equivalent to
\rho_{t}^{\psi_{x}}(X)\leq 0
. Hence,
\alpha_{t}^{\Psi}(\lambda X)=\alpha_{t}^{\Psi}(X)
.

The proof is complete.

∎

B.3
On conditional quantiles

The conditional versions of qualtiles are defined naturally using ‘probabilistic conditioning’ of the corresponding regular (or static) notions. Same hold true for most properties. While morally this is true, the difficulties are hidden in technical details related to measurably and well-definiteness of these objects. Here we present some results related to that. We also refer to
[
BCF18
]
for some relevant discussion on this topic.

Definition B.13
.

For any
\alpha\in(0,1)
, the conditional upper and lower
\alpha
-quantile of
X\in L^{\infty}
with respect to
\sigma
-field
\mathscr{F}_{t}
are defined as,

\displaystyle q_{\alpha}^{+}(X\;|\;\mathscr{F}_{t})=\esssup\{m\in L^{\infty}_{t}\;|\;\mathbb{P}(X\leq m\;|\;\mathscr{F}_{t})\leq\alpha\},

\displaystyle q_{\alpha}^{-}(X\;|\;\mathscr{F}_{t})=\essinf\{m\in L^{\infty}_{t}\;|\;\mathbb{P}(X\leq m\;|\;\mathscr{F}_{t})\geq\alpha\}.

Below wWe will give other representations of the conditional quantile functions, and we start with an auxiliary result.

Lemma B.14
.

For any
X\in L^{\infty},\ t\in\mathcal{T},\ a\in L^{\infty}_{t}
, and for fixed
\omega^{\prime}\in\Omega
,

\displaystyle P(X\leq a\;|\;\mathscr{F}_{t})(\omega^{\prime})=\mathbb{P}(X\leq a(\omega^{\prime})\;|\;\mathscr{F}_{t})(\omega^{\prime}).

(B.37)

Proof.

For fixed
\omega^{\prime}\in\Omega
,

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}(X\leq a\;|\;\mathscr{F}_{t})(\omega^{\prime})

\displaystyle=\mathbbm{1}_{a=a(\omega^{\prime})}\mathbb{P}(X\leq a\;|\;\mathscr{F}_{t})=\mathbbm{1}_{a=a(\omega^{\prime})}\mathbb{E}(\mathbbm{1}_{X\leq a}\;|\;\mathscr{F}_{t})

\displaystyle=\mathbbm{1}_{a=a(\omega^{\prime})}\mathbb{E}(\mathbbm{1}_{a=a(\omega^{\prime})}\mathbbm{1}_{X\leq a}\;|\;\mathscr{F}_{t})=\mathbbm{1}_{a=a(\omega^{\prime})}\mathbb{E}(\mathbbm{1}_{X\leq a(\omega^{\prime})}\;|\;\mathscr{F}_{t})

\displaystyle=\mathbb{P}(X\leq a(\omega^{\prime})\;|\;\mathscr{F}_{t})(\omega^{\prime}).

∎

Lemma B.15
.

For any
X\in L^{\infty}
,
t\in\mathcal{T}
,
\alpha\in(0,1)
and
\omega\in\Omega\backslash N^{X}
, the conditional lower and upper
\alpha
-quantile admit the representation

\displaystyle q_{\alpha}^{+}(X\;|\;\mathscr{F}_{t})(\omega)

\displaystyle=\sup\{x\in\mathbb{R}\;|\;\mathbb{P}(X\leq x\;|\;\mathscr{F}_{t})(\omega)\leq\alpha\},

\displaystyle q_{\alpha}^{-}(X\;|\;\mathscr{F}_{t})(\omega)

\displaystyle=\inf\{x\in\mathbb{R}\;|\;\mathbb{P}(X\leq x\;|\;\mathscr{F}_{t})(\omega)\geq\alpha\}.

Proof.

We will prove the first identity, and the second identity about
q_{\alpha}^{-}(X\;|\;\mathscr{F}_{t})
can be proved similarly. For simplicity, denote by
m^{*}:=q_{\alpha}^{+}(X\;|\;\mathscr{F}_{t})
and
x^{*}(\omega):=\sup\{x\in\mathbb{R}\;|\;\mathbb{P}(X\leq x\;|\;\mathscr{F}_{t})(\omega)\leq\alpha\}
. We will show that the set
\{m^{*}\neq x^{*}\}\cap(\Omega\setminus N^{X})
is an empty set.
We proceed by splitting
\{m^{*}\neq x^{*}\}
into two sets
\{m^{*}<x^{*}\}
and
\{m^{*}>x^{*}\}
.

First assume that
\{m^{*}<x^{*}\}\cap(\Omega\setminus N^{X})\neq\emptyset
. Then, there exist
M_{1}\in\mathscr{F}_{t}
and
a\in L^{\infty}_{t}
, such that on non-empty set
M_{1}\backslash N^{X}
,

m^{*}<a<x^{*}.

(B.38)

Since
a<x^{*}
on
M_{1}\backslash N^{X}
, we have for fixed
\omega^{\prime}\in M_{1}\backslash N^{X}
,

a(\omega^{\prime})<x^{*}(\omega^{\prime})=\sup\{x\in\mathbb{R}\;|\;\mathbb{P}(X\leq x\;|\;\mathscr{F}_{t})(\omega^{\prime})\leq\alpha\}.

Thus,
\mathbb{P}(X\leq a(\omega^{\prime})\;|\;\mathscr{F}_{t})(\omega^{\prime})\leq\alpha
, which by by Lemma
B.15
becomes,

\mathbb{P}(X\leq a\;|\;\mathscr{F}_{t})(\omega^{\prime})\leq\alpha.
Since
\omega^{\prime}
can be chosen arbitrarily in
M_{1}\backslash N^{X}
, we have,

\mathbb{P}(X\leq a\;|\;\mathscr{F}_{t})\mathbbm{1}_{M_{1}\backslash N^{X}}\leq\alpha.

By locality of conditional probability,
\mathbb{P}(X\leq a\mathbbm{1}_{M_{1}\backslash N^{X}}\;|\;\mathscr{F}_{t})\leq\alpha,
which by the definition of
q_{\alpha}^{+}(X\;|\;\mathscr{F}_{t})
implies that
a\mathbbm{1}_{M_{1}\backslash N^{X}}\leq m^{*}
. This contradicts (
B.38
), and thus
\{m^{*}<x^{*}\}=\emptyset
on
\Omega\backslash N^{X}
.

Next we prove that
\{m^{*}>x^{*}\}\cap(\Omega\backslash N^{X})
is also an empty. Assume this is not true. Then, there exist
M_{2}\in\mathscr{F}_{t}
and
b\in L^{\infty}_{t}
, such that on non-empty set
M_{2}\backslash N^{X}
,

\displaystyle x^{*}<b<m^{*}.

(B.39)

Since
x^{*}<b
on
M_{2}\backslash N^{X}
, for fixed
\omega^{\prime}\in M_{2}\backslash N^{X}
,

\displaystyle b(\omega^{\prime})>x^{*}(\omega^{\prime})=\sup\{x\in\mathbb{R}\;|\;\mathbb{P}(X\leq x\;|\;\mathscr{F}_{t})(\omega^{\prime})\leq\alpha\}.

Thus,
\mathbb{P}(X\leq b(\omega^{\prime})\;|\;\mathscr{F}_{t})(\omega^{\prime})>\alpha
, and by Lemma
B.15
,
\mathbb{P}(X\leq b\;|\;\mathscr{F}_{t})(\omega^{\prime})>\alpha.
Since
\omega^{\prime}
can be chosen arbitrarily in
M_{2}\backslash N^{X}
, we have on
M_{2}\backslash N^{X}
, that
\mathbb{P}(X\leq b\;|\;\mathscr{F}_{t})>\alpha
. By the definition of
q_{\alpha}^{+}(X\;|\;\mathscr{F}_{t})
, we have that
b>m^{*}
on
M_{2}\backslash N^{X}
, which contradicts (
B.39
).

The proof is complete.
∎

As a consequence of Lemma
B.15
, one can show that (see also
[
FS04
, Section 4.4]
for unconditional case)

\displaystyle q_{\alpha}^{+}(X\;|\;\mathscr{F}_{t})(\omega)

\displaystyle=\sup\{x\in\mathbb{R}\;|\;\mathbb{P}(X<x\;|\;\mathscr{F}_{t})(\omega)\leq\alpha\}.

\displaystyle q_{\alpha}^{-}(X\;|\;\mathscr{F}_{t})(\omega)

\displaystyle=\inf\{x\in\mathbb{R}\;|\;\mathbb{P}(X<x\;|\;\mathscr{F}_{t})(\omega)\geq\alpha\}.

For brefity, we show the first identity only. Let
a=\sup\{x\in\mathbb{R}\;|\;\mathbb{P}(X<x\;|\;\mathscr{F}_{t})(\omega)\leq\alpha\}
and
b=\sup\{x\in\mathbb{R}\;|\;\mathbb{P}(X\leq x\;|\;\mathscr{F}_{t})(\omega)\leq\alpha\}
. Then, since

\left\{x\in\mathbb{R}\;|\;\mathbb{P}(X<x\;|\;\mathscr{F}_{t})(\omega)\leq\alpha\right\}\supset\left\{x\in\mathbb{R}\;|\;\mathbb{P}(X\leq x\;|\;\mathscr{F}_{t})(\omega)\leq\alpha\right\},

we have
a\geq b
. To show that
a\leq b
, we proceed by contradiction. Assume that
a>b
, then there exists
s_{0},s_{1}\in\mathbb{R}
, such that
b<s_{0}<s_{1}<a
. The inequality
s_{1}<a
implies that
\mathbb{P}(X<s_{1}\;|\;\mathscr{F}_{t})(\omega)\leq\alpha
, and
b<s_{0}
implies that
\mathbb{P}(X\leq s_{0}\;|\;\mathscr{F}_{t})(\omega)>\alpha
. Consequently, we get

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}(X<s_{1}\;|\;\mathscr{F}_{t})(\omega)\leq\alpha<\mathbb{P}(X\leq s_{0}\;|\;\mathscr{F}_{t})(\omega).

(B.40)

On the other hand, by
[
BCF18
, (A.4) and (A.5)]
, since
s_{0}<s_{1}
, we have that
\mathbb{P}(X\leq s_{0}\;|\;\mathscr{F}_{t})(\omega)\leq\mathbb{P}(X<s_{1}\;|\;\mathscr{F}_{t})(\omega)\leq\alpha
, which contradicts (
B.40
).

Finally, we present a result that relates lower conditional quantile to upper conditional quantile.

Lemma B.16
.

For any
X\in L^{\infty},\ \omega\in\Omega\backslash N^{X}
,

\displaystyle q^{+}_{\alpha}(X\;|\;\mathscr{F}_{t})(\omega)=-q^{-}_{1-\alpha}(-X\;|\;\mathscr{F}_{t})(\omega).

Proof.

By Lemma
B.15
, for any
\omega\in\Omega\backslash N^{\prime}
,

\displaystyle q^{+}_{\alpha}(X\;|\;\mathscr{F}_{t})(\omega)

\displaystyle=\sup\{x\in\mathbb{R}\;|\;\mathbb{P}(X<x\;|\;\mathscr{F}_{t})(\omega)\leq\alpha\}=-\inf\{-x\in\mathbb{R}\;|\;\mathbb{P}(X<x\;|\;\mathscr{F}_{t})(\omega)\leq\alpha\}

\displaystyle=-\inf\{x\in\mathbb{R}\;|\;\mathbb{P}(X<-x\;|\;\mathscr{F}_{t})(\omega)\leq\alpha\}

\displaystyle=-\inf\{x\in\mathbb{R}\;|\;\mathbb{P}(-X>x\;|\;\mathscr{F}_{t})(\omega)\leq\alpha\}

\displaystyle=-\inf\{x\in\mathbb{R}\;|\;1-\mathbb{P}(-X\leq x\;|\;\mathscr{F}_{t})(\omega)\leq\alpha\}

\displaystyle=-\inf\{x\in\mathbb{R}\;|\;\mathbb{P}(-X\leq x\;|\;\mathscr{F}_{t})(\omega)\geq 1-\alpha\}

\displaystyle=-q^{-}_{1-\alpha}(-X\;|\;\mathscr{F}_{t})(\omega).

∎

Lemma B.17
.

For any
X\in L^{\infty}
,
t\in\mathcal{T}
and
\omega\in\Omega\backslash N^{X}
,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[X\;|\;\mathscr{F}_{t}](\omega)=\int_{(0,1)}q^{-}_{1-z}(X\;|\;\mathscr{F}_{t})(\omega)\dif z.

Proof.

By
[
Kal06
, Theorem 5.4]
, fix two measurable spaces
S
and
T
, a
\sigma
-field
\mathscr{F}\subset\mathcal{A}
, and a random element
\xi
in
S
such that
\mathbb{P}[\xi\in\cdot\;|\;\mathscr{F}]
has a regular version
\nu
. Further consider an
\mathscr{F}
-measurable random element
\eta
in
T
and a measurable function
f
on
S\times T
with
\mathbb{E}|f(\xi,\eta)|<\infty
. Then, for any
\omega\in\Omega\backslash N^{\xi}
,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[f(\xi,\eta)\;|\;\mathscr{F}](\omega)=\int\nu(\dif s)(\omega)f(s,\eta)(\omega).

(B.41)

Note that in our settings, there is only one variable
X
which plays the role of
\xi
in (
B.41
). Since
X\in L^{\infty}
, we only consider
X\geq 0
, otherwise take
X^{\prime}=X+C
, where
C:=\esssup X
. Thus we have,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[X\;|\;\mathscr{F}_{t}](\omega)

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}}xd\mathbb{P}(X\leq x\;|\;\mathscr{F}_{t})(\omega)=\int_{[0,C]}x\dif\mathbb{P}(X\leq x\;|\;\mathscr{F}_{t})(\omega)

\displaystyle=x\mathbb{P}(X\leq x\;|\;\mathscr{F}_{t})(\omega)|^{C}_{0-}-\int_{[0,C]}\mathbb{P}(X\leq x\;|\;\mathscr{F}_{t})(\omega)\dif x

\displaystyle=C-\int_{[0,C]}\left[1-\mathbb{P}(X>x\;|\;\mathscr{F}_{t})(\omega)\right]\dif x=\int_{[0,C]}\mathbb{P}(X>x\;|\;\mathscr{F}_{t})(\omega)\dif x

\displaystyle=\int_{[0,C]}\int_{(0,1)}\mathbbm{1}_{z<\mathbb{P}(X>x\;|\;\mathscr{F}_{t})(\omega)}\dif z\dif x=\int_{(0,1)}\int_{[0,C]}\mathbbm{1}_{z<\mathbb{P}(X>x\;|\;\mathscr{F}_{t})(\omega)}\dif x\dif z

\displaystyle=\int_{(0,1)}\int_{[0,C]}\mathbbm{1}_{\mathbb{P}(X\leq x\;|\;\mathscr{F}_{t})(\omega)<1-z}\dif x\dif z,

Next, we will consider the continuous part and the discrete part of
X
separately. For fixed
z\in(0,1)
, let
x^{\prime}=\inf\{x\in\mathbb{R}\;|\;\mathbb{P}(X\leq x\;|\;\mathscr{F}_{t})(\omega)\geq 1-z\}
.

If
X
is continuous at
x^{\prime}
, then
\mathbb{P}(X=x^{\prime}\;|\;\mathscr{F}_{t})(\omega)=0
, we have
\mathbb{P}(X\leq x^{\prime}\;|\;\mathscr{F}_{t})(\omega)=1-z
, and

\displaystyle\int_{[0,C]}\mathbbm{1}_{\mathbb{P}(X\leq x\;|\;\mathscr{F}_{t})(\omega)<1-z}\dif x

\displaystyle=C-\int_{[0,C]}\mathbbm{1}_{\mathbb{P}(X\leq x\;|\;\mathscr{F}_{t})(\omega)\geq 1-z}\dif x

\displaystyle=C-\int_{[x^{\prime},C]}\mathbbm{1}_{\mathbb{P}(X\leq x\;|\;\mathscr{F}_{t})(\omega)\geq 1-z}\dif x

\displaystyle=C-\int_{[x^{\prime},C]}\mathbbm{1}\dif x=x^{\prime}.

If
X
is discrete at
x^{\prime}
, then
\mathbb{P}(X=x^{\prime}\;|\;\mathscr{F}_{t})(\omega)>0
. For those
z^{\prime}
such that
\mathbb{P}(X\leq x^{\prime}-\;|\;\mathscr{F}_{t})(\omega)<1-z^{\prime}\leq\mathbb{P}(X\leq x^{\prime}\;|\;\mathscr{F}_{t})(\omega)
, we deduce

\displaystyle\int_{[0,C]}\mathbbm{1}_{\mathbb{P}(X\leq x\;|\;\mathscr{F}_{t})(\omega)<1-z}\dif x

\displaystyle=C-\int_{[0,C]}\mathbbm{1}_{\mathbb{P}(X\leq x\;|\;\mathscr{F}_{t})(\omega)\geq 1-z}\dif x

\displaystyle=C-\int_{[x^{\prime},C]}\mathbbm{1}_{\mathbb{P}(X\leq x\;|\;\mathscr{F}_{t})(\omega)\geq 1-z}\dif x

\displaystyle=C-\int_{[x^{\prime},C]}\mathbbm{1}dx=x^{\prime}.

Thus for fixed
z\in(0,1)
, and any
X
, we have

\int_{[0,C]}\mathbbm{1}_{\mathbb{P}(X\leq x\;|\;\mathscr{F}_{t})(\omega)<1-z}\dif x=\inf\{x\in\mathbb{R}\;|\;\mathbb{P}(X\leq x\;|\;\mathscr{F}_{t})(\omega)\geq 1-z\},

and we continue

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[X\;|\;\mathscr{F}_{t}](\omega)

\displaystyle=\int_{(0,1)}\int_{[0,C]}\mathbbm{1}_{\mathbb{P}(X\leq x\;|\;\mathscr{F}_{t})(\omega)<1-z}\dif x\dif z

\displaystyle=\int_{(0,1)}\inf\{x\in\mathbb{R}\;|\;\mathbb{P}(X\leq x\;|\;\mathscr{F}_{t})(\omega)\geq 1-z\}\dif z

\displaystyle=\int_{(0,1)}q^{-}_{1-z}(X\;|\;\mathscr{F}_{t})(\omega)\dif z.

∎

B.4
On
\var
and
\avar

The conditional
\var
, similar to its static counterpart, is defined in terms of conditional
\alpha
-quantile function.

Definition B.18
.

For fixed
\alpha\in(0,1)
and
X\in L^{\infty}
, conditional
\var
at level
\alpha
with respect to
\sigma
-field
\mathscr{F}_{t}
is defined as,

\displaystyle\var_{\alpha}(X\;|\;\mathscr{F}_{t}):=-q_{\alpha}^{+}(X\;|\;\mathscr{F}_{t})=\essinf\{m\in L^{\infty}_{t}\;|\;\mathbb{P}(X+m<0\;|\;\mathscr{F}_{t})\leq\alpha\}.

(B.42)

From financial point of view,
\var_{\alpha}(X|\mathscr{F}_{t})
can be viewed as the smallest amount of capital, which, if added to the position
X
at time
t
, will yield a secured position
X+m
that encounters losses with a (conditional) probability below the level
\alpha
. Clearly, the
\var_{\alpha}
does not capture the size or the distribution of the losses beyond the
\alpha
-quantile. To overcome this, the notion of average value at risk is introduced.

Definition B.19
.

The conditional Average Value at Risk at level
\alpha\in(0,1]
of a position
X\in L^{\infty}
is given by

\displaystyle\avar_{\alpha}(X\;|\;\mathscr{F}_{t}):=\frac{1}{\alpha}\int_{(0,\alpha)}\var_{z}(X\;|\;\mathscr{F}_{t})dz=-\frac{1}{\alpha}\int_{(0,\alpha)}q_{z}^{+}(X\;|\;\mathscr{F}_{t})dz.

(B.43)

Note that
q_{z}^{+}(X\;|\;\mathscr{F}_{t})
is monotone increasing with respect to
z
. Since monotonicity implies Borel measurability,
z\mapsto q_{z}^{+}(X\;|\;\mathscr{F}_{t})
is Borel measurable. Thus, the integral in (
B.43
) is well-defined.

In the existing literature, the
\avar
sometimes is defined through the so-called robust or dual representations; cf.
[
BCF18
]
for the conditional case. Next results shows that these definitions are equivalent.

Lemma B.20
.

For any
X\in L^{\infty}
and
\alpha\in(0,1]
,

\avar_{\alpha}(X\;|\;\mathscr{F}_{t})=\esssup\{\mathbb{E}[-XZ\;|\;\mathscr{F}_{t}]\;|\;Z\in\mathscr{F},0\leq Z\leq 1/\alpha,\mathbb{E}(Z\;|\;\mathscr{F}_{t})=1\}.

(B.44)

Moreover, a maximizer
Z^{*}
in the right hand side of (
B.44
) exists, and it is given by

Z_{\alpha}^{*}=\frac{1}{\alpha}\left(\mathbbm{1}_{X<q_{\alpha}^{\pm}(X\;|\;\mathscr{F}_{t})}+\varepsilon\mathbbm{1}_{X=q_{\alpha}^{\pm}(X\;|\;\mathscr{F}_{t})}\right),

(B.45)

here

\displaystyle\varepsilon=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}0,&\mathbb{P}(X=q_{\alpha}^{\pm}(X\;|\;\mathscr{F}_{t})\;|\;\mathscr{F}_{t})=0,\\
\frac{\alpha-\mathbb{P}(X<q_{\alpha}^{\pm}(X\;|\;\mathscr{F}_{t})\;|\;\mathscr{F}_{t})}{\mathbb{P}(X=q_{\alpha}^{\pm}(X\;|\;\mathscr{F}_{t})\;|\;\mathscr{F}_{t})},&\text{\ otherwise}.\end{array}\right.

Proof.

In view of (
B.43
), it is enough to show that,

-\frac{1}{\alpha}\int_{(0,\alpha)}q^{+}_{z}(X\;|\;\mathscr{F}_{t})(\omega)\dif z=\mathbb{E}[-XZ_{\alpha}^{*}\;|\;\mathscr{F}_{t}](\omega).

(B.46)

Let
L=-\frac{1}{\alpha}\int_{(0,\alpha)}q^{+}_{z}(X\;|\;\mathscr{F}_{t})(\omega)\dif z
. Then,

\begin{split}L&=\frac{1}{\alpha}\left[\int_{(0,\alpha)}q^{+}_{\alpha}(X\;|\;\mathscr{F}_{t})(\omega)-q^{+}_{z}(X\;|\;\mathscr{F}_{t})(\omega)\dif z\right]-q^{+}_{\alpha}(X\;|\;\mathscr{F}_{t})(\omega)\\
&=\frac{1}{\alpha}\left[\int_{(0,1)}\left(q^{+}_{\alpha}\left(X\;|\;\mathscr{F}_{t}\right)(\omega)-q^{+}_{z}\left(X\;|\;\mathscr{F}_{t}\right)(\omega)\right)^{+}\dif z\right]-q^{+}_{\alpha}(X\;|\;\mathscr{F}_{t})(\omega).\end{split}

(B.47)

Next we prove an intermediary equality

\mathbb{E}\left[\left(q^{+}_{\alpha}\left(X\;|\;\mathcal{F}_{t}\right)-X\right)^{+}\;|\;\mathcal{F}_{t}\right](\omega)=\int_{(0,1)}\left(q^{+}_{\alpha}\left(X\;|\;\mathcal{F}_{t}\right)(\omega)-q^{+}_{z}\left(X\;|\;\mathcal{F}_{t}\right)(\omega)\right)^{+}\dif z.

(B.48)

By Lemma
B.17

\begin{split}\mathbb{E}\big[\left(q^{+}_{\alpha}(X\;|\;\mathcal{F}_{t})-X\right)^{+}&\;|\;\mathcal{F}_{t}\big](\omega)=\int_{(0,1)}q^{-}_{1-z}\left(\left(q^{+}_{\alpha}(X\;|\;\mathcal{F}_{t})-X\right)^{+}\;|\;\mathcal{F}_{t}\right)(\omega)\dif z\\
&=\int_{(0,1)}\sup\{x\in\mathbb{R}\;|\;\mathbb{P}\left((q^{+}_{\alpha}(X\;|\;\mathcal{F}_{t})-X)^{+}<x\;|\;\mathcal{F}_{t}\right)(\omega)<1-z\}\dif z.\end{split}

(B.49)

Since

\mathbb{P}(X^{+}<x)=\mathbb{P}(\max\{0,X\}<x)=\mathbb{P}(0<x,X<x)=\begin{cases}0,&x\geq 0,\\
\mathbb{P}(X<x),&x>0,\end{cases}

we have that

\displaystyle\sup\{x\in\mathbb{R}\;|\;\mathbb{P}\left(\left(q^{+}_{\alpha}(X\;|\;\mathcal{F}_{t})-X\right)^{+}<x\;|\;\mathcal{F}_{t}\right)(\omega)<1-z\}

(B.50)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad=\sup\{x>0\;|\;\mathbb{P}\left(q^{+}_{\alpha}(X\;|\;\mathcal{F}_{t})-X<x\;|\;\mathcal{F}_{t}\right)(\omega)<1-z\}.

(B.51)

Then, we combine (
B.49
) with (
B.50
), and obtain

\mathbb{E}\left[\left(q^{+}_{\alpha}(X\;|\;\mathcal{F}_{t})-X\right)^{+}\;|\;\mathcal{F}_{t}\right](\omega)=\int_{(0,1)}\sup\{x>0\;|\;\mathbb{P}\left(q^{+}_{\alpha}(X\;|\;\mathcal{F}_{t})-X<x\;|\;\mathcal{F}_{t}\right)(\omega)<1-z\}\dif z.

Using this, to show (
B.48
), it is sufficient to show that for any
z\in(0,1)
,

\sup\{x>0\;|\;\mathbb{P}\left(q^{+}_{\alpha}(X\;|\;\mathcal{F}_{t})-X<x\;|\;\mathcal{F}_{t}\right)(\omega)<1-z\}\left(q^{+}_{\alpha}(X\;|\;\mathcal{F}_{t})(\omega)-q^{+}_{z}(X\;|\;\mathcal{F}_{t})(\omega)\right)^{+},

(B.52)

which we prove next. Denote by
J:=\sup\{x>0\;|\;\mathbb{P}\left(q^{+}_{\alpha}(X\;|\;\mathcal{F}_{t})-X<x\;|\;\mathcal{F}_{t}\right)(\omega)<1-z\}
. Then,

\displaystyle J

\displaystyle=\sup\{x>0\;|\;\mathbb{P}\left(-X<-q^{+}_{\alpha}(X\;|\;\mathcal{F}_{t})(\omega)+x\;|\;\mathcal{F}_{t}\right)(\omega)<1-z\}

\displaystyle=\sup\{x>0\;|\;\mathbb{P}\left(X>q^{+}_{\alpha}(X\;|\;\mathcal{F}_{t})(\omega)-x\;|\;\mathcal{F}_{t}\right)(\omega)<1-z\}

\displaystyle=\sup\{x>0\;|\;\mathbb{P}\left(X\leq q^{+}_{\alpha}(X\;|\;\mathcal{F}_{t})(\omega)-x\;|\;\mathcal{F}_{t}\right)(\omega)>z\}.

Let
y:=q^{+}_{\alpha}(X\;|\;\mathcal{F}_{t})(\omega)-x
, and we continue

\displaystyle J

\displaystyle=\sup\{q^{+}_{\alpha}(X\;|\;\mathcal{F}_{t})(\omega)-y>0\;|\;\mathbb{P}\left(X\leq y\;|\;\mathcal{F}_{t}\right)(\omega)>z\}

\displaystyle=q^{+}_{\alpha}(X\;|\;\mathcal{F}_{t})(\omega)+\sup\{-y>-q^{+}_{\alpha}(X\;|\;\mathcal{F}_{t})(\omega)\;|\;\mathbb{P}\left(X\leq y\;|\;\mathcal{F}_{t}\right)(\omega)>z\}

\displaystyle=q^{+}_{\alpha}(X\;|\;\mathcal{F}_{t})(\omega)-\inf\{y<q^{+}_{\alpha}(X\;|\;\mathcal{F}_{t})(\omega)\;|\;\mathbb{P}\left(X\leq y\;|\;\mathcal{F}_{t}\right)(\omega)>z\}

\displaystyle=q^{+}_{\alpha}(X\;|\;\mathcal{F}_{t})(\omega)-\min\left\{q^{+}_{\alpha}(X\;|\;\mathcal{F}_{t})(\omega),\inf\left\{y\in\mathbb{R}\;|\;\mathbb{P}\left(X\leq y\;|\;\mathcal{F}_{t}\right)(\omega)>z\right\}\right\}

\displaystyle=q^{+}_{\alpha}(X\;|\;\mathcal{F}_{t})(\omega)+\max\left\{-q^{+}_{\alpha}(X\;|\;\mathcal{F}_{t})(\omega),-\inf\left\{y\in\mathbb{R}\;|\;\mathbb{P}\left(X\leq y\;|\;\mathcal{F}_{t}\right)(\omega)>z\right\}\right\}

\displaystyle=\max\left\{0,q^{+}_{\alpha}(X\;|\;\mathcal{F}_{t})(\omega)-q^{+}_{z}(X\;|\;\mathcal{F}_{t})(\omega)\right\}

\displaystyle=\left(q^{+}_{\alpha}(X\;|\;\mathcal{F}_{t})(\omega)-q^{+}_{z}(X\;|\;\mathcal{F}_{t})(\omega)\right)^{+},

and thus (
B.52
), is proved, and consequently (
B.48
) is established.

Then by (
B.47
) and (
B.48
), we get,

\displaystyle L

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\alpha}\mathbb{E}\left[\left(q^{+}_{\alpha}(X\;|\;\mathcal{F}_{t})-X\right)^{+}\;|\;\mathcal{F}_{t}\right](\omega)-q^{+}_{\alpha}(X\;|\;\mathcal{F}_{t})(\omega)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\alpha}\mathbb{E}\left[\left(q^{+}_{\alpha}(X\;|\;\mathcal{F}_{t})-X\right)\mathbbm{1}_{X<q^{+}_{\alpha}(X\;|\;\mathcal{F}_{t})}\;|\;\mathcal{F}_{t}\right](\omega)-q^{+}_{\alpha}(X\;|\;\mathcal{F}_{t})(\omega)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\alpha}q^{+}_{\alpha}\left(X\;|\;\mathcal{F}_{t}\right)(\omega)\mathbb{P}\left(X<q_{\alpha}^{+}(X\;|\;\mathcal{F}_{t})\;|\;\mathcal{F}_{t}\right)(\omega)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad+\frac{1}{\alpha}\mathbb{E}\left[-X\mathbbm{1}_{X<q^{+}_{\alpha}(X\;|\;\mathcal{F}_{t})}\;|\;\mathcal{F}_{t}\right](\omega)-q^{+}_{\alpha}(X\;|\;\mathcal{F}_{t})(\omega)

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{\alpha}q^{+}_{\alpha}\left(X\;|\;\mathcal{F}_{t}\right)(\omega)\left[\alpha-\mathbb{P}\left(X<q_{\alpha}^{+}(X\;|\;\mathcal{F}_{t})\;|\;\mathcal{F}_{t}\right)(\omega)\right]

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad+\frac{1}{\alpha}\mathbb{E}\left[-X\mathbbm{1}_{X<q^{+}_{\alpha}(X\;|\;\mathcal{F}_{t})}\;|\;\mathcal{F}_{t}\right](\omega)

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{\alpha}q_{\alpha}^{+}(X\;|\;\mathcal{F}_{t})(\omega)\mathbb{E}\left[\frac{\alpha-\mathbb{P}(X<q_{\alpha}^{+}(X\;|\;\mathcal{F}_{t})\;|\;\mathcal{F}_{t})}{\mathbb{P}(X=q_{\alpha}^{+}(X\;|\;\mathcal{F}_{t})\;|\;\mathcal{F}_{t})}\mathbbm{1}_{X=q^{+}_{\alpha}(X\;|\;\mathcal{F}_{t})}\;|\;\mathcal{F}_{t}\right](\omega)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad+\frac{1}{\alpha}\mathbb{E}\left[-X\mathbbm{1}_{X<q^{+}_{\alpha}(X\;|\;\mathcal{F}_{t})}\;|\;\mathcal{F}_{t}\right](\omega)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\alpha}\mathbb{E}\left[-q_{\alpha}^{+}(X\;|\;\mathcal{F}_{t})\frac{\alpha-\mathbb{P}(X<q_{\alpha}^{+}(X\;|\;\mathcal{F}_{t})\;|\;\mathcal{F}_{t})}{\mathbb{P}(X=q_{\alpha}^{+}(X\;|\;\mathcal{F}_{t})\;|\;\mathcal{F}_{t})}\mathbbm{1}_{X=q^{+}_{\alpha}(X\;|\;\mathcal{F}_{t})}\;|\;\mathcal{F}_{t}\right](\omega)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad+\frac{1}{\alpha}\mathbb{E}\left[-X\mathbbm{1}_{X<q^{+}_{\alpha}(X\;|\;\mathcal{F}_{t})}\;|\;\mathcal{F}_{t}\right](\omega)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\alpha}\mathbb{E}\left[-X\frac{\alpha-\mathbb{P}(X<q_{\alpha}^{+}(X\;|\;\mathcal{F}_{t})\;|\;\mathcal{F}_{t})}{\mathbb{P}(X=q_{\alpha}^{+}(X\;|\;\mathcal{F}_{t})\;|\;\mathcal{F}_{t})}\mathbbm{1}_{X=q^{+}_{\alpha}(X\;|\;\mathcal{F}_{t})}\;|\;\mathcal{F}_{t}\right](\omega)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad+\frac{1}{\alpha}\mathbb{E}\left[-X\mathbbm{1}_{X<q^{+}_{\alpha}(X\;|\;\mathcal{F}_{t})}\;|\;\mathcal{F}_{t}\right](\omega)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\alpha}\mathbb{E}\left[-X\varepsilon\mathbbm{1}_{X=q^{+}_{\alpha}(X\;|\;\mathcal{F}_{t})}\;|\;\mathcal{F}_{t}\right](\omega)+\frac{1}{\alpha}\mathbb{E}\left[-X\mathbbm{1}_{X<q^{+}_{\alpha}(X\;|\;\mathcal{F}_{t})}\;|\;\mathcal{F}_{t}\right](\omega)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}[-XZ_{\alpha}^{*}\;|\;\mathcal{F}_{t}](\omega),

where
Z_{\alpha}^{*}
is given as (
B.45
). This is exactly (
B.46
), and the proof is complete.
∎

Codes from Goppa codes
Chunlei Liu

Abstract
The Fröbenius acts on a Goppa code whose structure polynomial has coefficients in the symbol field. Its fixed codewords form a subcode. Deleting the redundance caused by repetition, we obtain a new code. We call this code a reduced Goppa code. It is proved that, as classical Goppa codes, the reduced Goppa codes approach to the Gilbert-Varshamov bound. It is also proved that, if the characteristic of the symbol field is small, the decoding time for a reduced Goppa code of codeword length n n is O ⁡ ( n 2 ​ log ν ​ n ) O(n^{2}\log^{\nu}n) in operations of the symbol field. Here the constant ν \nu is determined by the performance of polynomial factorisation algorithms. Recall that the decoding time for classical Goppa codes of codeword length n n is O ⁡ ( n 2 ​ log ν ​ n ) O(n^{2}\log^{\nu}n) in operations of a field whose size is about the size of the set of locations. By comparison, reduced Goppa codes can be decoded much quicker that classical Goppa codes.



Key words
: Reed-Solomon codes, BCH codes, Goppa codes, Gilbert-Varshamov Bound, Berlekamp-Massey algorithm.

1
INTRODUCTION

Reed-Solomon codes
[
RS
]
, BCH codes
[
BC
]
,
[
Ho
]
and Goppa codes
[
Go
]
are widely used error correcting codes due to their fast decoding algorithms based on the Berlekamp-Massey algorithm
[
Be68
,
Ma
]
. We shall construct new codes from Goppa codes. These new codes will have a faster decoding algorithm and have performances competent to the classical Goppa codes.

We recall the notion of Goppa codes in a sense as narrow as possible. As any linear code, a Goppa code has a Galois field
{\rm GF}(q)
as its symbol field. To ensure quick decoding, we require
q
to be a power of a small prime number. Its location field is an extension of
{\rm GF}(q)
, say
{\rm GF}(q^{m})
. Correspondingly, its set of locations is
{\mathbb{Z}}/(q^{m}-1)
, which is canonically isomorphic to the multiplicative group
{\rm GF}(q^{m})^{\times}
. To ensure a lower bound for its minimal distance, it has a designed distance
\delta
. To get a code of good performance, it has a structure polynomial
g
of degree
\delta-1
having no roots in
{\rm GF}(q^{m})^{\times}
. For simplicity, we require
g
to have coefficients in
{\rm GF}(q)
. The Goppa code with these parameters is denoted as
\Gamma(g,\delta,m,q)
.
Its codewords satisfy the congruence

\sum_{i=0}^{q^{m}-2}\frac{c_{i}}{x-\beta^{i}}\equiv 0({\rm mod}g(x)),

where
\beta
is a fixed primitive element of
{\rm GF}(q^{m})
.

The Fröbenius of the Galois group of
{\rm GF}(q^{m})
over
{\rm GF}(q)
acts on
\Gamma(g,\delta,m,q)
. Its fixed codewords form a subcode
\Gamma^{\rm F}(g,\delta,m,q)
. Deleting the redundance caused by repetition, we obtain a new code
{\rm R}\Gamma(g,\delta,m,q)
. We call this code a reduced Goppa code. The precise construction of reduced Goppa codes is given in Definition
2.7
. It is proved that the reduced Goppa codes approach to the Gilbert-Varshamov bound (see Theorem
4.5
). It is also proved that the decoding time for a reduced Goppa code of codeword length
n
is
O(n^{2}\log^{\nu}n)
in
q
-ary operations (see Theorem
5.15
). Here the constant
\nu
is determined by the performance of polynomial factorisation algorithms. Recall that decoding the classical Goppa codes of codeword length
n=q^{m}-1
requires
O(n^{2}\log^{\nu}n)
in
q^{m}
-ary operations. By comparison, reduced Goppa codes can be decoded much quicker than classical Goppa codes.

2
Construction

In this section we introduce the action of the Fröbenius on Goppa codes, and give the construction of reduced Goppa codes in Definition
2.7
.

Definition 2.1

The Fröbenius
{\rm Frob}
on the group
{\mathbb{Z}}/(q^{m}-1)
is a permutation on
{\mathbb{Z}}/(q^{m}-1)
defined by the formula:

{\rm Frob}(i+(q^{m}-1){\mathbb{Z}})=qi+(q^{m}-1){\mathbb{Z}}.

Definition 2.2

The Fröbenius
{\rm Frob}
on the space
{\rm GF}(q)^{{\mathbb{Z}}/(q^{m}-1)}
is a permutation on
{\rm GF}(q)^{{\mathbb{Z}}/(q^{m}-1)}
defined by the formula:

{\rm Frob}(r)_{i}=r_{q^{m-1}i}.

Lemma 2.3

The Goppa code
\Gamma(g,\delta,m,q)
is stable under the Fröbenius.

Proof.
Let
c\in\Gamma(g,\delta,m,q)
.
That is

\sum_{i=0}^{q^{m}-2}\frac{c_{i}}{x-\beta^{i}}\equiv 0({\rm mod}g(x)).

As
g
has coefficients in
{\rm GF}(q)
, we infer

\sum_{i=0}^{q^{m}-2}\frac{c_{i}}{x-\beta^{qi}}\equiv 0({\rm mod}g(x)).

Then

\displaystyle\sum_{i=0}^{q^{m}-2}\frac{{\rm Frob}(c)_{i}}{x-\beta^{i}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{i=0}^{q^{m}-2}\frac{c_{q^{m-1}i}}{x-\beta^{i}}({\rm mod}g(x))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{i=0}^{q^{m}-2}\frac{c_{i}}{x-\beta^{qi}}({\rm mod}g(x))

\displaystyle\equiv

\displaystyle 0({\rm mod}g(x)).

That is,
{\rm Frob}(c)\in\Gamma(g,\delta,m,q)
.
The lemma is proved.

Definition 2.4

The subcode of
{\Gamma}(g,\delta,m,q)
formed of Fröbenius fixed codewords is the code

\Gamma^{\rm F}(g,\delta,m,q)=\{c\in{\Gamma}(g,\delta,m,q)\mid{\rm Frob}(c)=c\}.

Definition 2.5

The set of orbits of
{\mathbb{Z}}/(q^{m}-1)
under the Fröbenius is denoted as
O(m,q)
.

Definition 2.6

Let
c\in\Gamma^{\rm F}(g,\delta,m,q)\}
. For
o\in O(m,q)
, we define

c_{o}=c_{i},\ \forall i\in o.

Definition 2.7

The code

{\rm R}\Gamma(g,\delta,m,q)=\{(c_{o})_{o\in O(m,q)}\mid c\in\Gamma^{\rm F}(g,\delta,m,q)\}

is called a reduced Goppa code.

We see that
{\rm R}\Gamma(g,\delta,m,q)
is obtained from
{\Gamma}^{\rm F}(g,\delta,m,q)
by deleting redundance caused by repetition, and, as a space over

{\rm GF}(q)
, is canonically isomorphic to
\Gamma^{\rm F}(g,\delta,m,q)
.

3
Lower bounds on minimal distance and dimension

In this section we give lower bounds for the minimal distance and dimension of reduced Goppa codes. The main results are Theorems
3.1
and
3.6
.

Theorem 3.1
(distance bound)

{\rm dist}{\rm R}\Gamma(g,\delta,m,q)\geq\frac{1}{m}{\rm dist}\Gamma^{\rm F}(g,\delta,m,q)\geq\frac{\delta}{m}.

Proof.
Let
c\in{\rm R}\Gamma(g,\delta,m,q)
be of minimal distance
d
. For
i\in{\mathbb{Z}}/(q^{m}-1)
,
define

c_{i}=c_{o},\ i\in o.

Then
(c_{i})_{i\in{\mathbb{Z}}/(q^{m}-1)}\in\Gamma^{\rm F}(g,\delta,m,q)
, and

w(c_{i})_{i\in{\mathbb{Z}}/(q^{m}-1)}\leq md.

It follows that

{\rm dist}\Gamma^{\rm F}(g,\delta,m,q)\leq md\leq m\cdot{\rm dist}{\rm R}\Gamma(g,\delta,m,q).

The theorem now follows.

Lemma 3.2

If
g(x)=\sum_{i=0}^{\delta-1}g_{i}x^{i}
, then the polynomials

\sum_{i=1}^{\delta-1-j}g_{i+j}x^{i},\ j=0,1,\cdots,\delta-2

are linearly independent in
{\rm GF}(q)[x]/(g(x))
.

Proof.

This follows from the fact that

(\sum_{i=1}^{\delta-1-j}g_{i+j}x^{i})_{j=0,1,\cdots,\delta-2}
is equal to

(x^{\delta-1},x^{\delta-2},\cdots,x)\left(\begin{array}[]{cccc}g_{\delta-1}&0&0&0\\
g_{\delta-2}&g_{\delta-1}&0&0\\
\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&0\\
g_{1}&g_{2}&\cdots&g_{\delta-1}\\
\end{array}\right)

Lemma 3.3
(codeword equations)

c\in\Gamma(g,\delta,m,q)
if and only if

\sum_{i=0}^{q^{m}-2}\frac{c_{i}}{g(\beta^{i})}\beta^{ij}=0,\ j=0,1,\cdots,\delta-2.

Proof.
We have

\displaystyle\sum_{i=0}^{q^{m}-2}\frac{g(x)-g(\beta^{i})}{g(\beta^{i})}\frac{c_{i}}{x-\beta^{i}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{i=0}^{q^{m}-2}\frac{c_{i}}{g(\beta^{i})}\sum_{u=1}^{\delta-1}g_{u}\frac{x^{u}-\beta^{iu}}{x-\beta^{i}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{i=0}^{q^{m}-2}\frac{c_{i}}{g(\beta^{i})}\sum_{u=1}^{\delta-1}g_{u}\sum_{j=0}^{u-1}x^{u-j}\beta^{ij}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{i=0}^{q^{m}-2}\frac{c_{i}}{g(\beta^{i})}\sum_{j=0}^{\delta-2}\beta^{ij}\sum_{u=j+1}^{\delta-1}g_{u}x^{u-j}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{j=0}^{\delta-2}\left(\sum_{i=0}^{q^{m}-2}c_{i}\frac{\beta^{ij}}{g(\beta^{i})}\right)\sum_{i=1}^{\delta-1-j}g_{i+j}x^{i}.

By the last lemma, the polynomials

\sum_{i=1}^{\delta-1-j}g_{i+j}x^{i},\ j=0,1,\cdots,\delta-2

are linearly independent.
It follows that

\displaystyle\sum_{i=0}^{q^{m}-2}\frac{c_{i}}{g(\beta^{i})}\beta^{ij}=0,\ j=0,1,\cdots,\delta-2

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow

\displaystyle\sum_{i=0}^{q^{m}-2}\frac{g(x)-g(\beta^{i})}{g(\beta^{i})}\frac{c_{i}}{x-\beta^{i}}\equiv 0({\rm mod}g(x))

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow

\displaystyle\sum_{i=0}^{q^{m}-2}\frac{c_{i}}{x-\beta^{i}}\equiv 0({\rm mod}g(x))

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow

\displaystyle c\in\Gamma(g,\delta,m,q).

The lemma is proved.

Lemma 3.4
(codeword congruence)

c\in\Gamma(g,\delta,m,q)

if and only if

\sum_{i=0}^{q^{m}-2}\frac{c_{i}}{g(\beta^{i})}\frac{1}{1-\beta^{i}x}\equiv 0({\rm mod}x^{\delta-1}).

Proof.

We have

\displaystyle\sum_{i=0}^{q^{m}-2}\frac{c_{i}}{g(\beta^{i})}\frac{1}{1-\beta^{i}x}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{i=0}^{q^{m}-2}\frac{c_{i}}{g(\beta^{i})}\sum_{j=0}^{+\infty}\beta^{ij}x^{j}

\displaystyle\equiv

\displaystyle\sum_{j=0}^{\delta-2}x^{j}\sum_{i=0}^{q^{m}-2}\frac{c_{i}}{g(\beta^{i})}\beta^{ij}({\rm mod}x^{\delta-1}).

Thus

\displaystyle\sum_{i=0}^{q^{m}-2}\frac{c_{i}}{g(\beta^{i})}\frac{1}{1-\beta^{i}x}\equiv 0({\rm mod}x^{\delta-1})

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow

\displaystyle\sum_{j=0}^{\delta-2}x^{j}\sum_{i=0}^{q^{m}-2}\frac{c_{i}}{g(\beta^{i})}\beta^{ij}\equiv 0({\rm mod}x^{\delta-1})

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow

\displaystyle\sum_{i=0}^{q^{m}-2}\frac{c_{i}}{g(\beta^{i})}\beta^{ij}=0,\ j=0,\cdots,\delta-2

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow

\displaystyle c\in\Gamma(g,\delta,m,q),

where the last equivalence follows from the last lemma.
The lemma is proved.

Lemma 3.5
(codeword equations)

(c_{o})_{o\in O(m,q)}\in{\rm R}\Gamma(g,\delta,m,q)

if and only if

\sum_{o\in O(m,q)}c_{o}\sum_{i\in o}\frac{\beta^{ij}}{g(\beta^{i})}=0,\ j=0,1,\cdots,\delta-2,

if and only if

\sum_{o\in O(m,q)}c_{o}\sum_{i\in o}\frac{1}{g(\beta^{i})(1-\beta^{i}x)}\equiv 0({\rm mod}x^{\delta-1}).

Proof.
Let

(c_{o})_{o\in O(m,q)}\in{\rm GF}(q)^{O(m,q)}
. For
i\in{\mathbb{Z}}/(q^{m}-1)
,
define

c_{i}=c_{o},\ i\in o.

Then

\sum_{i=0}^{q^{m}-2}\frac{c_{i}}{g(\beta^{i})}\beta^{ij}=\sum_{o\in O(m,q)}c_{o}\sum_{i\in o}\frac{\beta^{ij}}{g(\beta^{i})},\ j=0,1,\cdots,\delta-2.

It follows that

\displaystyle(c_{o})_{o\in O(m,q)}\in{\rm R}\Gamma(g,\delta,m,q)

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow

\displaystyle(c_{i})_{i\in{\mathbb{Z}}/(q^{m}-1)}\in\Gamma^{\rm F}(g,\delta,m,q)

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow

\displaystyle\sum_{i=0}^{q^{m}-2}\frac{c_{i}}{g(\beta^{i})}\beta^{ij}=0,\ j=0,1,\cdots,\delta-2

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow

\displaystyle\sum_{o\in O(m,q)}c_{o}\sum_{i\in o}\frac{\beta^{ij}}{g(\beta^{i})}=0,\ j=0,1,\cdots,\delta-2.

Similarly,
c\in\Gamma(g,\delta,m,q)
if and only if

\sum_{i=0}^{q^{m}-2}\frac{c_{i}}{g(\beta^{i})}\frac{1}{1-\beta^{i}x}\equiv 0({\rm mod}x^{\delta-1}).

The lemma is proved.

Theorem 3.6
(dimension bound)

{\rm dim}{\rm R}\Gamma(g,\delta,m,q)\geq|O(m,q)|-\delta+1.

Proof.
This follows from the last lemma as well as the fact that

\sum_{i\in o}\frac{\beta^{ij}}{g(\beta^{i})}\in{\rm GF}(q).

4
The Gilbert-Varshamov bound

In this section we prove Theorem
4.5
, which says that the reduced Goppa codes approach the Gilbert-Varshamov bound as classical Goppa codes
[
Be73
]
.

Definition 4.1
(Entropy)

The
q
-ary entropy function on
(0,1)
is defined by the formula

H_{q}(x)=x\log_{q}(q-1)-x\log_{q}x-(1-x)\log_{q}(1-x).

Lemma 4.2
(Entropy lemma)

If

\frac{d}{n}\leq\frac{q-1}{q}
, then

H_{q}(\frac{d}{n})\geq\frac{1}{n}\log_{q}\sum_{i=0}^{d}{n\choose i}(q-1)^{i}.

Proof.
By assumption, we have
\frac{d}{n-d}\leq q-1.
Thus, if
0\leq i\leq d
, we have

\displaystyle q^{-nH_{q}(\frac{d}{n})}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(q-1)^{-d}(\frac{d}{n})^{d}(\frac{n-d}{n})^{n-d}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle(q-1)^{-i}(\frac{d}{n})^{i}(\frac{n-d}{n})^{n-i}

It follows that

\displaystyle q^{-nH_{q}(\frac{d}{n})}\sum_{i=0}^{d}{n\choose i}(q-1)^{i}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sum_{i=0}^{d}{n\choose i}(\frac{d}{n})^{i}(\frac{n-d}{n})^{n-i}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 1.

The lemma now follows.

Definition 4.3

The set of monic irreducible polynomials over
{\rm GF}(q)
is denoted as

{\rm Irr}(\delta-1,q)
.

Lemma 4.4

\displaystyle\#\{g\in{\rm Irr}(\delta-1,q)\mid{\rm dist}({\rm R}\Gamma(g,\delta,m,q))\leq d\}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{md}{\delta-1}\sum_{i=0}^{d}{|O(m,q)|\choose i}(q-1)^{i}.

Proof.
We have

\displaystyle\#\{g\in{\rm Irr}(\delta-1,q)\mid c\in{\rm R}\Gamma(g,\delta,m,q)\}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\#\{g\in{\rm Irr}(\delta-1,q)\mid\sum_{i=0}^{q^{m}-2}\frac{c_{i}}{x-\beta^{i}}\equiv 0({\rm mod}g(x))\}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{m\cdot{\rm wt}(c)}{\delta-1}.

It follows that

\displaystyle\#\{g\in{\rm Irr}(\delta-1,q)\mid{\rm dist}({\rm R}\Gamma(g,\delta,m,q))\leq d\}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{d}\sum_{{\rm wt}(c)=i}\#\{g\in{\rm Irr}(\delta-1,q)\mid c\in{\rm R}\Gamma(g,\delta,m,q)\}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{md}{\delta-1}\sum_{i=0}^{d}{|O(m,q)|\choose i}(q-1)^{i}.

The lemma is proved.

Theorem 4.5
(Asymptotic Gibert-Varshamov bound)

Let
q
be any prime power. Let
\varepsilon>0
. Then there is a tuple
(g,\delta,m)
such that
{\rm R}\Gamma(g,\delta,m,q)
makes sense, such that

\frac{{\rm dist}{\rm R}\Gamma(g,\delta,m,q)}{|O(m,q)|}\leq\frac{q-1}{q},

and such that

H_{q}(\frac{{\rm dist}{\rm R}\Gamma(g,\delta,m,q)}{|O(m,q)|})+\frac{{\rm dim}{\rm R}\Gamma(g,\delta,m,q)}{|O(m,q)|}\geq 1-\varepsilon.

Proof.

First, we choose
N>0
such that, for all
m>N
, and for all

\delta
with

\frac{q^{m}}{m^{2}}\leq\delta\leq\frac{q^{m}}{m}+q^{\frac{m}{2}+1},

we have

\frac{\log_{q}(1-q^{-\frac{\delta-1}{2}}-q^{-\delta+1})+\log_{q}(\delta-1)-m-\log_{q}m}{|O(m,q)|}\geq-\varepsilon.

Second, we choose
m
and

\delta
such that
m>N
, such that
\delta-1
is prime to
m
,
and such that

\frac{q^{m}}{m^{2}}\leq\delta\leq\frac{q-1}{q}\cdot|O(m,q)|.

Third, we choose
d
to be the largest integer such that

\frac{md}{\delta-1}\sum_{i=0}^{d}{|O(m,q)|\choose i}(q-1)^{i}<q^{\delta-1}-q^{\frac{\delta-1}{2}}-1,

which implies that

\#\{g\in{\rm Irr}(\delta-1,q)\mid{\rm dist}({\rm R}\Gamma(g,\delta,m,q))\leq d\}<q^{\delta-1}-q^{\frac{\delta-1}{2}}-1<\#{\rm Irr}(\delta-1,q).

Fourth, we choose
g\in{\rm Irr}(\delta-1,q)
such that

g\not\in\{g\in{\rm Irr}(\delta-1,q)\mid{\rm dist}({\rm R}\Gamma(g,\delta,m,q))\leq d\}.

As
\delta-1
is prime to
m
,
g
has no roots in
{\rm GF}(q^{m})^{\times}
.
Thus
{\rm R}\Gamma(g,\delta,m,q)
makes sense, and

D={\rm dist}({\rm R}\Gamma(g,\delta,m,q))>d.

With these chosen parameters, we have

\displaystyle\frac{{\rm dist}({\rm R}\Gamma(g,\delta,m,q))}{|O(m,q)|}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{|O(m,q)|-{\rm dim}{\rm R}\Gamma(g,\delta,m,q)+1}{|O(m,q)|}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{\delta}{|O(m,q)|}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{q-1}{q},

where the first inequality follows from the singleton bound, and the second inequality follows from our bound for the dimensions of reduced Goppa codes.
Moreover,

\displaystyle H_{q}(\frac{{\rm dist}{\rm R}\Gamma(g,\delta,m,q)}{|O(m,q)|})

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{|O(m,q)|}\log_{q}\sum_{i=0}^{D}{|O(m,q)|\choose i}(q-1)^{i}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\frac{\log_{q}(q^{\delta-1}-q^{\frac{\delta-1}{2}}-1)+\log_{q}(\delta-1)-\log_{q}(mD)}{|O(m,q)|}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\frac{\log_{q}(q^{\delta-1}-q^{\frac{\delta-1}{2}}-1)+\log_{q}(\delta-1)-m-\log_{q}(m)}{|O(m,q)|}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\frac{\delta-1}{|O(m,q)|}-\varepsilon

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\frac{|O(m,q)|-{\rm dim}{\rm R}\Gamma(g,\delta,m,q)}{|O(m,q)|}-\varepsilon.

The theorem is proved.

5
Decoding

In this section we describe the decoding algorithm for reduced Goppa codes.
The main result is Theorem
5.15
.

Definition 5.1

Let
r\in{\rm GF}(q)^{O(m,q)}
.
If there is a codeword
c\in{\rm R}\Gamma(g,\delta,m,q)
such that

\sum_{o\in O(m,q):r_{o}\neq c_{o}}|o|\leq\frac{\delta-1}{2},

then it is called a B-M correctable word for
{\rm R}\Gamma(g,\delta,m,q)
.

Definition 5.2

If
r\in{\rm GF}(q)^{O(m,q)}

is a B-M correctable word for
{\rm R}\Gamma(g,\delta,m,q)
, then the error location set of
r
is

E=\{o\in O(m,q)\mid r_{o}\neq c_{o}\},

and the error of
r

is
e=r-c
.

Definition 5.3

If
r\in{\rm GF}(q)^{O(m,q)}

is a B-M correctable word for
{\rm R}\Gamma(g,\delta,m,q)
, then

S_{j}=\sum_{o\in O(m,q)}r_{o}\sum_{i\in o}\frac{\beta^{ij}}{g(\beta^{i})},\ j=0,1,\cdots,\delta-2.

are called syndromes of
r
.

Lemma 5.4

If
r\in{\rm GF}(q)^{O(m,q)}

is a B-M correctable word for
{\rm R}\Gamma(g,\delta,m,q)
, then
its syndromes can be calculated out within

O(n\delta)
time in
q
-ary field operations, where
n
is the length of
r
.

Proof.
This follows from the fact that

\sum_{i\in o}\frac{\beta^{ij}}{g(\beta^{i})}\in{\rm GF}(q),\ j=0,1,\cdots,\delta-2,

which can be calculated out in pre-computation.

Lemma 5.5
(syndrome congruence)

If
r\in{\rm GF}(q)^{O(m,q)}

is a B-M correctable word for
{\rm R}\Gamma(g,\delta,m,q)
, then

\sum_{j=0}^{\delta-2}S_{j}x^{j}\equiv\sum_{o\in O(m,q)}e_{o}\sum_{i\in o}\frac{1}{g(\beta^{i})(1-\beta^{i}x)}({\rm mod}x^{\delta-1}).

Proof.
We have

\displaystyle\sum_{j=0}^{\delta-2}S_{j}x^{j}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{j=0}^{\delta-2}x^{j}\sum_{o\in O(m,q)}r_{o}\sum_{i\in o}\frac{\beta^{ij}}{g(\beta^{i})}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{o\in O(m,q)}r_{o}\sum_{i\in o}\frac{\sum_{j=0}^{\delta-2}x^{j}\beta^{ij}}{g(\beta^{i})}

\displaystyle\equiv

\displaystyle\sum_{o\in O(m,q)}r_{o}\sum_{i\in o}\frac{1}{g(\beta^{i})(1-\beta^{i}x)}({\rm mod}x^{\delta-1}).

The lemma now follows from the equality
r=c+e
and the codeword equation

\sum_{o\in O(m,q)}c_{o}\sum_{i\in o}\frac{1}{g(\beta^{i})(1-\beta^{i}x)}\equiv 0({\rm mod}x^{\delta-1}).

Definition 5.6

If
r\in{\rm GF}(q)^{O(m,q)}

is a B-M correctable word for
{\rm R}\Gamma(g,\delta,m,q)
, then the polynomial pair
(\omega(x),\sigma(x))
such that
\deg\omega(x)<\deg\sigma(x)\leq\frac{\delta-1}{2}
, and

\frac{\omega(x)}{\sigma(x)}\equiv\sum_{j=0}^{\delta-2}S_{j}x^{j}({\rm mod}x^{\delta-1})

is called a B-M pair of
r
.

Lemma 5.7

If
r\in{\rm GF}(q)^{O(m,q)}

is a B-M correctable word for
{\rm R}\Gamma(g,\delta,m,q)
, then, after its syndromes have been calculated out, its B-M pair
(\omega(x),\sigma(x))
can be calculated out within

O(\delta^{2})
time in
q
-ary field operations.

Proof.
This follows from the time estimation for the well-known Berlekamp-Massey algorithm, as well as the fact that

S_{j}\in{\rm GF}(q),\ j=0,\cdots,\delta-2.

Definition 5.8

The location polynomial of
o\in O(m,q)
is the irreducible polynomial

p_{o}(x)=\prod_{i\in o}(x-\beta^{-i}).

Lemma 5.9
(syndrome equation)

If
r\in{\rm GF}(q)^{O(m,q)}

is a B-M correctable word for
{\rm R}\Gamma(g,\delta,m,q)
, then

\frac{\omega(x)}{\sigma(x)}=\sum_{o\in O(m,q)}e_{o}\sum_{i\in o}\frac{1}{g(\beta^{i})(1-\beta^{i}x)}.

Proof.
By the syndrome congruence, as well as the definition of B-M pair, we have

\displaystyle\frac{\omega(x)}{\sigma(x)}

\displaystyle\equiv

\displaystyle\sum_{j=0}^{\delta-2}S_{j}x^{j}({\rm mod}x^{\delta-1})

\displaystyle\equiv

\displaystyle\sum_{o\in O(m,q)}e_{o}\sum_{i\in o}\frac{1}{g(\beta^{i})(1-\beta^{i}x)}({\rm mod}x^{\delta-1}).

Comparing degrees of related polynomials, we see that

The lemma is proved.

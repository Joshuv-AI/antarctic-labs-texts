Convexity and transport for isentropic Euler equations on a sphere
Gordon Blower

Abstract
. The paper considers the Euler system of PDE on a smooth compact Riemannian manifold of positive curvature without boundary, and the sphere 𝕊 2 {\mathbb{S}}^{2} in particular. The paper interprets the Euler equations as a transport problem for the fluid density under dynamics governed by the gradient of the internal energy of the fluid. The paper develops the notion of transport cost in the tangent bundle, and compares its properties with the Wasserstein transportation cost on the manifold. There are applications to the discrete approximation to the Euler equations in the style of Gangbo and Wesdickenberg ( Comm. Partial Diff. Equations 34 (2009), 1041-1073), except that the analysis is heavily dependent upon the curvature of the underlying manifold. The internal energy is assumed to satisfy convexity conditions that allow analysis via Φ \Phi -entropy entropy-production inequalities, and the results apply to the power law ρ γ \rho^{\gamma} where 1 < γ < 3 / 2 1<\gamma<3/2 , which includes the case of a diatomic gas. The paper proves existence of weak solutions of the continuity equation, and gives a sufficient condition for existence of weak solutions to the acceleration equation.

(ii) The notion of interpolation between measures is considered in section 4 of

3.
Convexity and Wasserstein distance

In this section, we are concerned with how curvature of

affects curvature of
{\mathcal{W}}^{2}(M)
as a metric space, and begin by considering a functional from (

Definition 3.1
.

For a bijection

, and
\Phi_{t}(x)=\exp_{x}(tv(x))

\displaystyle E[T]={\frac{1}{2}}\int_{{\mathbb{S}}^{2}}d(T(x),x)^{2}f(x)m(dx),

Proposition 3.2
.

(i) Then the first outer variation is

(3.3)

\displaystyle\langle\delta_{o}{E}[T],v\rangle=-\int_{{\mathbb{S}}^{2}}{\frac{d(T(x),x)}{\sin d(T(x),x)}}x\cdot v(T(x))f(x)m(dx),

(ii) and
({d^{2}}/{dt^{2}})_{t=0}E[\Phi_{t}\circ T]\geq 0

Proof.

(i) Let

be the inverse of

\displaystyle\zeta(y)={\frac{\|\zeta\|}{\sin\|\zeta\|}}\bigl(T^{*}(y)-(T^{*}(y)\cdot y)y\bigr).

\displaystyle\cos d(\Phi_{t}(y),T^{*}(y))

\displaystyle=\cos d(y,T^{*}(y))\cos d(y,\Phi_{t}(y))

\displaystyle\quad+\sin d(y,T^{*}(y))\sin d(y,\Phi_{t}(y)){\frac{v(y)\cdot\zeta(y)}{\|v(y)\|\|\zeta(y)\|}},

hence

\displaystyle\Bigl({\frac{d}{dt}}\Bigr)_{t=0}d(\Phi_{t}(y),T^{*}(y))=-{\frac{v(y)\cdot\zeta(y)}{\|v(y)\|\|\zeta(y)\|}}\Bigl({\frac{d}{dt}}\Bigr)_{t=0}d(\Phi_{t}(y),y)=-{\frac{v(y)\cdot\zeta(y)}{\|\zeta(y)\|}}.

\displaystyle\langle\delta_{o}{E}[T],v\rangle

\displaystyle=\Bigl({\frac{d}{dt}}\Bigr)_{t=0}{\frac{1}{2}}\int_{{\mathbb{S}}^{2}}d(\Phi_{t}\circ T(x),x)^{2}f(x)m(dx)

\displaystyle=\Bigl({\frac{d}{dt}}\Bigr)_{t=0}{\frac{1}{2}}\int_{{\mathbb{S}}^{2}}d(\Phi_{t}(y),T^{*}(y)))^{2}\rho(y)m(dy)

\displaystyle=-\int_{{\mathbb{S}}^{2}}d(y,T^{*}(y)){\frac{v(y)\cdot\zeta(y)}{\|\zeta(y)\|}}\rho(y)m(dy)

\displaystyle=-\int_{{\mathbb{S}}^{2}}v(T(x))\cdot\zeta(T(x))f(x)m(dx),

so from (

(3.8)

\displaystyle\langle\delta_{o}{E}[T],v\rangle=-\int_{{\mathbb{S}}^{2}}v(T(x))\cdot{\frac{d(T(x),x)}{\sin d(T(x),x)}}\bigl(x-(x\cdot T(x))T(x)\bigr)f(x)m(dx)

and

(ii) Likewise, the second derivative is

\displaystyle\Bigl(

\displaystyle{\frac{d^{2}}{dt^{2}}}\Bigr)_{t=0}{\frac{1}{2}}\int_{{\mathbb{S}}^{2}}d(\Phi_{t}\circ T(x),x)^{2}f(x)m(dx)

\displaystyle=\int_{{\mathbb{S}}^{2}}\Bigl({\frac{\|\zeta(T(x))\|}{\tan\|\zeta(T(x))\|}}\Bigl(\|v(T(x))\|^{2}-{\frac{(v(T(x))\cdot\zeta(T(x)))^{2}}{\|\zeta(T(x))\|^{2}}}\Bigr)

(3.9)

\displaystyle\quad+{\frac{(v(T(x))\cdot\zeta(T(x)))^{2}}{\|\zeta(T(x))\|^{2}}}\Bigr)f(x)m(dx),

and is evidently nonnegative. In the proof of Theorem

∎

Now we consider triples
\{f,\rho_{0},\rho_{1}\}
of probability density functions, where

[

{\mathcal{W}}^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
with base point, and we have the added complication of the curvature of
M
. Given
y,x_{0},x_{1}\in M

\gamma^{(0)}(t)=\exp_{y}(t\xi_{0})
from

to
x_{0}=\gamma^{(0)}(1),
and likewise a geodesic
\gamma^{(1)}(t)=\exp_{y}(t\xi_{1})
from
y=\gamma^{(1)}(0)
to
x_{1}=\gamma^{(1)}(1).
There are two natural routes from

to
x_{1}

(1) let
\gamma^{(2)}
be a geodesic from
x_{0}
to
x_{1}

(2) let
F(s,t)=\exp_{y}(t(1-s)\xi_{0}+ts\xi_{1})

x_{0}
to
x_{1}
.

Let
T_{0}:M\rightarrow M
and
T_{1}:M\rightarrow M
be optimal transport maps such that
\rho_{0}=T_{0}\sharp f
and
\rho_{1}=T_{1}\sharp f
, so each

gives a triple
\{y,T_{0}(y),T_{1}(y)\}
. There are correspondingly two natural ways of interpolating between
\rho_{0}
and
\rho_{1}
according to whether we use (1) optimal transport from
\rho_{0}
to
\rho_{1}
, or (2) Jacobi fields. When

and

F(s,t)=\exp_{x}(t(1-s)\nabla\phi_{0}(y)+st\nabla\phi_{1}(y)).

For an interpolation as in (2), we have the following result.

Proposition 3.3
.

(i) For
M
satisfying the geometrical hypotheses
1.5
, there exists
\kappa_{M}>0
such that given probability density functions
\rho_{0},\rho_{1},f
on
M
, there exists a continuous path
(\rho_{s})_{s\in[0,1]}
in
{\mathcal{W}}^{2}(M)
from
\rho_{0}
to
\rho_{1}
such that

(3.10)

\displaystyle(1-s)W_{2}^{2}(\rho_{0},f)+sW_{2}^{2}(\rho_{1},f)\geq W_{2}^{2}(\rho_{s},f)+\kappa_{M}s(1-s)W_{2}^{2}(\rho_{0},\rho_{1})\qquad(s\in[0,1]).

(ii) In particular,
\kappa_{{\mathbb{S}}^{2}}=4/\pi^{2}
.

Proof.

In the case of
{\mathbb{S}}^{2}
, let
y=\exp_{x}\xi
and
z=\exp_{x}\eta
where
\xi,\eta\in T_{s}{\mathbb{S}}^{2}
have angle
\theta
between them; applying the cosine rule to the spherical triangle
\triangle xyz
, we
have

\cos d(y,z)=\cos\|\xi\|\cos\|\eta\|+\sin\|\xi\|\sin\|\eta\|\cos\theta

and we deduce by trigonometric identities that

\displaystyle 2\sin^{2}{\frac{d(y,z)}{2}}

\displaystyle=8\sin^{2}{\frac{\|\xi\|-\|\eta\|}{4}}\cos^{2}{\frac{\|\xi\|-\|\eta\|}{4}}+2\sin^{2}{\frac{\theta}{2}}\sin\|\xi\|\sin\|\eta\|,

(3.11)

\displaystyle\leq{\frac{1}{2}}\bigl(\|\xi\|-\|\eta\|\bigr)^{2}+2\|\xi\|\|\eta\|\sin^{2}{\frac{\theta}{2}}

in which

. By simple estimates, we deduce that

(3.12)

\displaystyle d(y,z)^{2}\leq{\frac{\pi^{2}}{4}}\|\xi-\eta\|^{2}.

The general case is based upon a similar idea. Let
-\phi_{0}
and
-\phi_{1}
be
d^{2}/2
concave functions on
M
, and suppose that
T_{0}(x)=\exp_{x}\nabla\phi_{0}
and
T_{1}(x)=\exp_{x}\nabla\phi_{1}(x)
are optimal transport maps such that
\rho_{0}=T_{0}\sharp f
and
\rho_{1}=T_{1}\sharp f
. Then it is natural to introduce
T_{s}(x)=\exp_{x}((1-s)\nabla\phi_{0}(x)+s\nabla\phi_{1}(x))
and
\rho_{s}=T_{s}\sharp f
to interpolate between these; we do not assert that
T_{s}
is an optimal transport map. One can construct a family of geodesics
t\mapsto F(s,t)
all starting at
x
, given by

F(s,t)=\exp_{x}(t(1-s)\nabla\phi_{0}(x)+st\nabla\phi_{1}(x)),

and determine
T_{s}(x)
by Jacobi’s equation on page 366 of
[
29
]
; the curve
s\mapsto F(s,1)
from
T_{0}(x)
to
T_{1}(x)
is not necessarily a geodesic. Alternatively, one can find the minimizer of

\inf_{x}\bigl\{d(x,y)^{2}/2+(1-s)\phi_{0}(x)+s\phi_{1}(x)\bigr\}

since the infimum is attained for

such that

, and

d^{2}(T_{s}(x),x)/2=\|(1-s)\nabla\phi_{0}(x)+s\nabla\phi_{1}(x)\|^{2}/2.

We have

(1-s)\|\nabla\phi_{0}\|^{2}+s\|\nabla\phi_{1}\|^{2}-\|(1-s)\nabla\phi_{0}(x)+s\nabla\phi_{1}(x)\|^{2}=s(1-s)\|\nabla\phi_{0}-\nabla\phi_{1}\|^{2}.

then

\displaystyle(

\displaystyle 1-s)W_{2}^{2}(\rho_{0},f)+sW_{2}^{2}(\rho_{1},f)

\displaystyle=(1-s)\int_{M}\|\nabla\phi_{0}(x)\|^{2}f(x)m(dx)+s\int_{M}\|\nabla\phi_{1}(x)\|^{2}f(x)m(dx)

\displaystyle\geq\int_{M}d^{2}(T_{s}(x),x)f(x)m(dx)/2+s(1-s)\int_{M}\|\nabla\phi_{0}(x)-\nabla\phi_{1}(x)\|^{2}f(x)m(dx)

(3.13)

\displaystyle\geq W_{2}^{2}(\rho_{s},f)+s(1-s)\int_{M}\|\nabla\phi_{0}(x)-\nabla\phi_{1}(x)\|^{2}f(x)m(dx).

With
\theta
denoting the angle between
\nabla\phi_{0}
and
\nabla\phi_{1}
, as measured with respect to the Riemannian metric on

, we have by
[
29
]
14.1

\|\nabla\phi_{0}-\nabla\phi_{1}\|^{2}=(\|\nabla\phi_{0}\|-\|\nabla\phi_{1}\|)^{2}+4\|\nabla\phi_{0}\|\|\nabla\phi_{1}\|\sin^{2}(\theta/2),

where with
\kappa_{x}
the Gaussian curvature at
x
,

d\Bigl(\exp_{x}{\frac{t\nabla\phi_{0}(x)}{\|\nabla\phi_{0}(x)\|}},\exp_{x}{\frac{t\nabla\phi_{1}(x)}{\|\nabla\phi_{1}(x)\|}}\Bigr)^{2}=4\Bigl(\sin^{2}{\frac{\theta}{2}}\Bigr)\Bigl(t^{2}-{\frac{\kappa_{x}t^{4}\cos^{2}(\theta/2)}{3}}+O(t^{6})\Bigr)\quad(t\rightarrow 0+),

so with
t=\|\nabla\phi_{0}(x)\|
and
s=\|\nabla\phi_{1}(x)\|
, we assume without loss of generality that
t\leq s
, so have

\displaystyle d\Bigl(

\displaystyle\exp_{x}\nabla\phi_{0}(x),\exp_{x}\nabla\phi_{1}(x)\Bigr)^{2}

\displaystyle\leq 2d\Bigl(\exp_{x}{\frac{t\nabla\phi_{0}(x)}{\|\nabla\phi_{0}(x)\|}},\exp_{x}{\frac{t\nabla\phi_{1}(x)}{\|\nabla\phi_{1}(x)\|}}\Bigr)^{2}+2d\Bigl(\exp_{x}{\frac{t\nabla\phi_{1}(x)}{\|\nabla\phi_{1}(x)\|}},\exp_{x}{\frac{s\nabla\phi_{1}(x)}{\|\nabla\phi_{1}(x)\|}}\Bigr)^{2}

\displaystyle\leq 8\Bigl(\sin{\frac{\theta}{2}}\Bigr)^{2}\Bigl(t^{2}-{\frac{\kappa_{x}t^{4}\cos^{2}(\theta/2)}{3}}+O(t^{6})\Bigr)+2\Bigl(\|\nabla\phi_{0}(x)\|-\|\nabla\phi_{1}(x)\|\Bigr)^{2}

\displaystyle\leq(2+O(t^{2}))\|\nabla\phi_{0}(x)-\nabla\phi_{1}(x)\|^{2}

(3.14)

\displaystyle\leq C\|\nabla\phi_{0}(x)-\nabla\phi_{1}(x)\|^{2},

for come uniform
C>0
on
M
. Finally, we have

\displaystyle W_{2}^{2}(\rho_{0},\rho_{1})

\displaystyle\leq\int_{M}d\Bigl(\exp_{x}\nabla\phi_{0}(x),\exp_{x}\nabla\phi_{1}(x)\Bigr)^{2}f(x)m(dx)

(3.15)

\displaystyle\leq C\int_{M}\|\nabla\phi_{0}(x)-\nabla\phi_{1}(x)\|^{2}f(x)m(dx).

For
M={\mathbb{S}}^{2}
, the choice of

is given by (

).
∎

In Proposition

, we have shown that the graph of

for

lies below an upward pointing parabola that intersects at

; we have not quite shown that

is convex; compare with

4.
Convexity of internal energy

The internal energy for density
\rho
is
{\mathcal{U}}(\rho)=\int_{M}\Theta(\rho(x))\rho(x)m(dx)
, and in this section we consider the convexity of
{\mathcal{U}}
under transportation when
\Theta
satisfies the following conditions.

Definition 4.1
.

(Convexity Hypotheses)
We assume that
\Theta:(0,\infty)\rightarrow{\mathbb{R}}
is four times continuously differentiable, and that

(i)
C_{\infty}:

r\mapsto\Theta(e^{r})
is strictly increasing and convex;

(ii)
\Theta(x)\rightarrow 0
as
x\rightarrow 0+
and
\Theta(x)\rightarrow\infty
as
x\rightarrow\infty
;

(iii)
\Theta
satisfies
(\Delta_{2})
, in the sense of Orlicz’s theory, so there exists
K_{2}>0
such that
\Theta(2x)\leq K_{2}\Theta(x)
for all
x>0
.

The conditions (i), (ii) and (iii) imply that
\Theta_{1}(x)=x\Theta^{\prime}(x)+\Theta(x)
is positive and increasing. For application to (
5.10
), we later impose the additional conditions:

(iv)
x\Theta_{1}^{\prime}(x)
is increasing;

(v)
-1/(x\Theta^{\prime}_{1}(x)^{2})
is convex.

Examples 4.1
(1) For
1<\gamma<3/2
,
\Theta(x)=x^{\gamma-1}
satisfies all conditions (i)-(v). This choice is physically relevant since for
\rho
the density of a diatomic gas in
{\mathbb{R}}^{3}
, the specific heat capacity of air at constant pressure divided by the specific heat capacity at constant volume is approximately
\gamma=\alpha+1=7/5
. For a diatomic gas in
{\mathbb{R}}^{3}
, the assumption
\rho^{1/2}\in H^{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{3})
ensures that
\rho_{0}\in L^{\gamma}({\mathbb{R}}^{3}).

(2) For
\gamma>0
, the powers
\Theta(r)=r^{\gamma-1}
satisfy (i)-(iii). For a monatomic gas, the ratio of specific heats is
\gamma=5/3
.

(3) Also,
\Theta(r)=\log r
satisfies (i) but not (ii). The expression
\rho\Theta(\rho)=\rho\log\rho
is the integrand that is involved in the thermodynamic entropy of
\rho
. (The notion of
\Phi
-entropy is related to but different from this.)

(4) Proposition
4.2
applies to
\Theta(x)=\log(1+x)
and
\Theta(x)=x^{\alpha}
for
\alpha>0
. However, it does not apply to
\Theta(x)=\sqrt{\log(1+x)}
, as discussed in
[
5
]

(5) Under conditions (i) and (ii), the function
U:[0,\infty)\rightarrow{\mathbb{R}}
given by
U(x)=x\Theta(x)
satisfies
U(0)=0
,
U(x)
is convex and
r\mapsto e^{r}U(e^{-r})
is convex, so
U
belongs to Villani’s class
{\mathcal{DC}}_{\infty}
of
[
29
]
; also
U
is increasing and
U(x)\geq 0
,
U(x)\rightarrow\infty
as
x\rightarrow\infty
, so
U
is an Orlicz function, and gives rise to an Orlicz function space. The duality is most conveniently expressed in terms of
\Theta_{1}
and its inverse function
\chi
. If
\Theta
satisfies (iii) then
U
also satisfies (iii). We provide details in Proposition
4.2
and Remark
4.3
(iv).

Proposition 4.2
.

Suppose that
\Theta(e^{r})
is strictly increasing and convex, with
\Theta(e^{r})\rightarrow\infty
as
r\rightarrow\infty
and
\Theta(e^{r})\rightarrow 0
as
r\rightarrow-\infty
.

(i) Then the internal energy satisfies

(4.1)

\displaystyle\int_{M}\rho(x)\Theta(\rho(x))m(dx)\leq 2\Theta(4)+2\int_{0}^{\infty}\Theta\Bigl({\frac{2}{m\{x:\rho(x)>\lambda\}}}\Bigr)m\{x:\rho(x)>\lambda\}\,d\lambda.

(ii) Suppose that
f\in{\mathcal{W}}^{2}(M)
satisfies
\int_{M}f(x)\Theta(f(x))m(dx)<K
for some
K>1
. Then the set of probability density functions

(4.2)

\displaystyle\Omega_{K}=\Bigl\{\rho\in L^{1}(M):\int_{M}\rho dm=1;W_{2}^{2}(\rho,f)+\int_{M}\rho(x)\Theta(\rho(x))m(dx)\leq K\Bigr\}

is closed, convex and weakly sequentially compact in
L^{1}(M)
.

(iii) The functional
E:\Omega_{K}\rightarrow{\mathbb{R}}

(4.3)

\displaystyle{\mathcal{E}}(\rho;f)=W_{2}^{2}(\rho,f)+\int_{M}\rho(x)\Theta(\rho(x))m(dx)

attains its infimum at a unique point
\rho_{1}\in\Omega_{K}
.

(iv) If
x\Theta(x)
and its Legendre transform satisfy the
\Delta_{2}
condition, then
x\Theta(x)
determines an Orlicz norm such that
\Omega_{K}
is compact for the weak topology.

Proof.

(i) Let
\rho
be a probability density function with respect to a positive Radon measure
m
on
M
. Then
\rho
has median
\mu_{\rho}
such that
\mu_{\rho}m\{x:\rho(x)>\mu_{\rho}\}=\mu_{\rho}/2\leq 1
by Chebyshev’s inequality. Also

\displaystyle\int_{M}\rho(x)\Theta(\rho(x))m(dx)

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}(\Theta(\lambda)+\lambda\Theta^{\prime}(\lambda))m\{x:\rho(x)>\lambda\}\,d\lambda

(4.4)

\displaystyle\leq 2\int_{0}^{\infty}\Theta(2\lambda)m\{x:\rho(x)>\lambda\}\,d\lambda;

and we split this integral as the sum of

(4.5)

\displaystyle 2\int_{\mu_{\rho}}^{\infty}\Theta(2\lambda)m\{x:\rho(x)>\lambda\}\,d\lambda\leq 2\int_{\mu_{\rho}}^{\infty}\Theta\Bigl({\frac{2}{m\{x:\rho(x)>\lambda\}}}\Bigr)m\{x:\rho(x)>\lambda\}\,d\lambda,

by Chebyshev’s inequality, and

(4.6)

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\mu_{\rho}}\Theta(2\lambda)m\{x:\rho(x)>\lambda\}\,d\lambda\leq\Theta(2\mu_{\rho})\int_{\mu_{\rho}}^{\infty}m\{x:\rho(x)>\lambda\}d\lambda\leq\Theta(4).

(ii) For
\rho\in\Omega_{K}
, we have

\int_{M}d(x,x_{0})^{2}\rho(x)m(dx)\leq\int_{M}d(x,x_{0})^{2}f(x)m(x)+K=K_{0}+K,

and
\int_{M}\rho(x)\Theta(\rho(x))m(dx)\leq K
for all
\rho\in\Omega_{K}
. Also, for
\rho_{0},\rho_{1}\in\Omega_{K}
let
\pi_{0}
be an optimal transport plan taking
f
to
\rho_{0}
, and
\pi_{1}
an optimal transport plan taking
f
to
\rho_{1}.
Then for
0<t<1
, we introduce
\rho_{t}=(1-t)\rho_{0}+t\rho_{1}\in{\hbox{Prob}}(M)
, and we have

W_{2}(\rho_{t},f)^{2}\leq(1-t)W_{2}(\rho_{0},f)+tW_{2}(\rho_{1},f),

since
(1-t)\pi_{0}+t\pi_{1}
is a transport plan taking
f
to
\rho_{t}
. Also

\int_{M}\rho_{t}(x)\Theta(\rho_{t}(x))m(dx)\leq(1-t)\int_{M}\rho_{0}(x)\Theta(\rho_{0}(x))m(dx)+t\int_{M}\rho_{1}(x)\Theta(\rho_{1}(x))m(dx)

since
r\Theta(r)
is strictly convex. Hence
\Omega_{K}
is convex.

Given any sequence
(p_{j})_{j=1}^{\infty}
in
\Omega_{K}
, we have

\Theta(R)\int_{[p_{j}(x)>R]}p_{j}(x)m(dx)\leq\int p_{j}(x)\Theta(p_{j}(x))m(dx)\leq K

and

R^{2}\int_{\{x\in M:d(x,x_{0})>R\}}p_{j}(x)m(dx)\leq\int_{M}d(x,x_{0})^{2}p_{j}(x)m(dx)\leq K+K_{0}

so by the criterion of
[
17
]
page 292 there exists a weakly convergent subsequence, with limit
p_{\infty}\in L^{1}
and
p_{\infty}\in{\hbox{Prob}}(M)
. By Fatou’s Lemma, we have

\displaystyle W_{2}^{2}(p_{\infty},f)

\displaystyle+\int_{M}p_{\infty}(x)\Theta(p_{\infty}(x))m(dx)

(4.7)

\displaystyle\leq\lim\inf_{j\rightarrow\infty}\Bigl(W_{2}^{2}(p_{j},f)+\int_{M}p_{j}(x)\Theta(p_{j}(x))m(dx)\Bigr)\leq K,

so
p_{\infty}\in\Omega_{K}
. Hence
\Omega_{K}
is weakly sequentially compact and closed in the weak topology.

(iii) This is a marginal entropy transport problem in the sense of E8 of
[
22
]
. Note that
f\in\Omega_{K}
, so the problem is feasible. The functional
E
is nonnegative and weakly lower semicontinuous on the bounded convex and weakly compact set
\Omega_{K}
. Let
E_{0}=\inf\{{\mathcal{E}}(\rho;f):\rho\in\Omega_{K}\}
, and choose
(p_{j})_{j=2}^{\infty}\in\Omega_{K}
such that
{\mathcal{E}}(p_{j};f)\rightarrow E_{0}
as

. Then there exists a weakly convergent subsequence, converging to
\rho_{1}\in\Omega_{K}
. Then by lower semi continuity, we have
E_{0}\leq{\mathcal{E}}(\rho_{1};f)\leq\lim\inf_{j\rightarrow\infty}{\mathcal{E}}(p_{j};f)=E_{0}
; hence
E
attains its infimum at
\rho_{1}
.

Suppose that
E
attains the infimum at
\rho_{0},\rho_{1}\in\Omega_{K}
and
V=\{x:\rho_{0}(x)\neq\rho_{1}(x)\}
has
m(V)>0
. Then
x\Theta(x)
is strictly convex, which implies

\displaystyle\int_{M}

\displaystyle\rho_{t}(x)\Theta(\rho_{t}(x))m(dx)

(4.8)

\displaystyle={\frac{1}{2}}\int_{{\mathbb{S}}^{2}}\|\nabla q_{h}(y)\|^{2}\rho_{h}(y)m(dy)+{\mathcal{U}}(\rho_{h})

\displaystyle\leq{\frac{1}{2}}\int_{{\mathbb{S}}^{2}}\|\nabla\phi_{0}(x)\|^{2}f(x)m(dx)+\int_{{\mathbb{S}}^{2}}\Theta(\rho_{0}(x))\rho_{0}(x)m(dx)

\displaystyle\quad+{\frac{h^{2}}{2}}\int_{{\mathbb{S}}^{2}}\|\nabla\phi_{h}(x)\|^{2}f(x)m(dx)-h\int_{{\mathbb{S}}^{2}}\nabla\phi_{0}(x)\cdot\nabla\phi_{h}(x)f(x)m(dx)

\displaystyle\quad+\int_{{\mathbb{S}}^{2}}f(x)\nabla\Theta_{1}(\rho_{h}(x))\cdot(-h\nabla\phi_{0}(x)+h^{2}\nabla\phi_{h}(x))m(dx)

\displaystyle\leq{\frac{1}{2}}\int_{{\mathbb{S}}^{2}}\|\nabla q_{0}(x)\|^{2}\rho_{0}(x)m(dx)+\int_{{\mathbb{S}}^{2}}\Theta(\rho_{0}(x))\rho_{0}(x)m(dx)

(7.14)

\displaystyle\quad-{\frac{h^{2}}{2}}\int_{{\mathbb{S}}^{2}}\rho_{h}(x)\|\nabla(\Theta_{1}\circ\rho_{h})(x))\|^{2}m(dx),

hence the result. Thus we have dissipation of energy, as required by (
1.4
).
∎

8.
Weak solutions of the Euler equations

In this section we use the discrete time algorithm with
h\rightarrow 0
to obtain weak solutions to the Euler equations. Both the continuity equation and the acceleration equation involve
\rho
, so we use the approximation procedure of the preceding section to create a discrete-time approximation, which we then convert into a
2
-absolutely continuous path
[0,\tau]\rightarrow{\mathcal{W}}^{2}({\mathbb{S}}^{2}):

t\mapsto p_{t}
; then we solve the associated ODE to generate a flow in

2
-absolutely continuous path
[0,\tau]\rightarrow{\mathcal{W}}^{1}({\mathbb{S}}^{2}):

t\mapsto\rho(\cdot,t)
. Due to lack of Lipschitz continuity, there is an extra approximation step, where we smooth the densities in the space variable. We are able to establish existence of a weak solution of the Euler continuity equation in this way.

Regimes of the lateral van der Waals force in the presence of dielectrics
Lucas Queiroz, Edson C. M. Nogueira, Danilo T. Alves

Abstract
In a recent paper, it was shown that, under the action of the lateral van der Waals (vdW) force due to a perfectly conducting corrugated surface, a neutral anisotropic polarizable particle in vacuum can be attracted not only to the nearest corrugation peak, but also to a valley, or an intermediate point between a peak and a valley, with such behaviors called peak, valley and intermediate regimes, respectively. In the present paper, we investigate how these regimes are affected by the consideration of two non-dispersive semi-infinite dielectrics ϵ 1 \epsilon_{1} and ϵ 2 \epsilon_{2} , separated by a corrugated interface. Specifically, we study the vdW interaction between a neutral anisotropic polarizable particle, embedded in the dielectric ϵ 2 \epsilon_{2} , and the dielectric ϵ 1 \epsilon_{1} . We show that when ϵ 2 < ϵ 1 \epsilon_{2}<\epsilon_{1} the peak, valley and intermediate regimes have, unless numerical factors, behaviors similar to those found for the situation where the particle is in vacuum and interacting with a conducting medium. For the case ϵ 2 > ϵ 1 \epsilon_{2}>\epsilon_{1} , one might expect a mere permute between the peak and valley regimes, in comparison to the case ϵ 2 < ϵ 1 \epsilon_{2}<\epsilon_{1} . Surprisingly, we find that when ϵ 2 > ϵ 1 \epsilon_{2}>\epsilon_{1} the regimes exhibit a very different and nontrivial behavior. Moreover, we show that similar regimes arise in the classical case involving a neutral polarized particle. The description of how the peak, valley and intermediate regimes are affected by the presence of dielectrics may be relevant for a better understanding of the interaction between anisotropic particles and corrugated surfaces.

Nogueira et al. 2021
, which is related to the interaction between the dipole in vacuum and a grounded conducting corrugated surface, and is given by

U_{\text{cond}}^{(1)}(\mathbf{r}_{0})=-\sum_{i,j}\frac{d_{i}d_{j}}{64\pi\epsilon_{0}z_{0}^{4}}\int\frac{d^{2}{\bf q}}{(2\pi)^{2}}\tilde{h}({\bf q})e^{i{\bf q}\cdot{\bf r}_{0\parallel}}\mathcal{I}_{ij}^{\text{cond}}\left(\mathbf{q}z_{0}\right),

(35)

where
\tilde{h}({\bf q})
is the Fourier representation of
h(\textbf{r}_{\parallel})
, and the functions
\mathcal{I}_{ij}^{\text{cond}}=\mathcal{I}_{ji}^{\text{cond}}
are defined as

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}_{xx}^{\text{cond}}(\mathbf{q}z_{0})

\displaystyle=\frac{3}{8}q^{2}z_{0}^{2}[qz_{0}K_{3}(qz_{0})-q_{x}^{2}z_{0}^{2}K_{2}(qz_{0})],

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}_{yy}^{\text{cond}}(\mathbf{q}z_{0})

\displaystyle=\frac{3}{8}q^{2}z_{0}^{2}[qz_{0}K_{3}(qz_{0})-q_{y}^{2}z_{0}^{2}K_{2}(qz_{0})],

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}_{zz}^{\text{cond}}(\mathbf{q}z_{0})

\displaystyle=[2q^{2}z_{0}^{2}+\frac{3}{8}q^{4}z_{0}^{4}]K_{2}(qz_{0})+\frac{1}{4}q^{3}z_{0}^{3}K_{3}(qz_{0}),

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}_{xy}^{\text{cond}}(\mathbf{q}z_{0})

\displaystyle=-\frac{3}{8}q_{x}q_{y}q^{2}z_{0}^{4}K_{2}(qz_{0}),

(36)

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}_{xz}^{\text{cond}}(\mathbf{q}z_{0})

\displaystyle=iq_{x}q^{2}z_{0}^{3}[K_{2}(qz_{0})-\frac{3}{8}qz_{0}K_{3}(qz_{0})],

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}_{yz}^{\text{cond}}(\mathbf{q}z_{0})

\displaystyle=iq_{y}q^{2}z_{0}^{3}[K_{2}(qz_{0})-\frac{3}{8}qz_{0}K_{3}(qz_{0})],

where
K_{2}
and
K_{3}
are modified Bessel functions of the second kind.
The second term,
U_{\text{diel}}^{(1)}
, in Eq. (
34
), is a correction in the interaction energy which arises specifically from the consideration of a dielectric as a corrugated surface.
We also write
U_{\text{diel}}^{(1)}
in terms of
\tilde{h}({\bf q})
, so that, it is given by

\displaystyle U_{\text{diel}}^{(1)}(\mathbf{r}_{0})

\displaystyle=-\frac{(\epsilon_{1}-\epsilon_{2})}{(\epsilon_{1}+\epsilon_{2})^{2}}\sum_{i,j}\frac{d_{i}d_{j}}{64\pi\epsilon_{0}z_{0}^{4}}\int\frac{d^{2}\mathbf{q}}{(2\pi)^{2}}\tilde{h}(\mathbf{q})

\displaystyle\times e^{i\mathbf{q}\cdot{\bf r}_{0\parallel}}\mathcal{I}_{ij}^{\text{diel}}(\mathbf{q}z_{0}),

(37)

where the functions
\mathcal{I}_{ij}^{\text{diel}}=\mathcal{I}_{ji}^{\text{diel}}
are defined as

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}_{xx}^{\text{diel}}(\mathbf{q}z_{0})=

\displaystyle(4q_{x}^{2}z_{0}^{2}+3q^{2}z_{0}^{2}+\frac{3}{8}q_{x}^{2}q^{2}z_{0}^{4})K_{2}(qz_{0})

\displaystyle-(q_{x}^{2}qz_{0}^{3}+\frac{3}{8}q^{3}z_{0}^{3})K_{3}(qz_{0}),

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}_{yy}^{\text{diel}}(\mathbf{q}z_{0})=

\displaystyle(4q_{y}^{2}z_{0}^{2}+3q^{2}z_{0}^{2}+\frac{3}{8}q_{y}^{2}q^{2}z_{0}^{4})K_{2}(qz_{0})

\displaystyle-(q_{y}^{2}qz_{0}^{3}+\frac{3}{8}q^{3}z_{0}^{3})K_{3}(qz_{0}),

(38)

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}_{zz}^{\text{diel}}(\mathbf{q}z_{0})=

\displaystyle\frac{3}{4}q^{3}z_{0}^{3}K_{3}(qz_{0})-\frac{3}{8}q^{4}z_{0}^{4}K_{2}(qz_{0}),

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}_{xy}^{\text{diel}}(\mathbf{q}z_{0})=

\displaystyle q_{x}q_{y}z_{0}^{2}[(4+\frac{3}{8}q^{2}z_{0}^{2})K_{2}(qz_{0})-qz_{0}K_{3}(qz_{0})],

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}_{xz}^{\text{diel}}(\mathbf{q}z_{0})=

\displaystyle iq_{x}q^{2}z_{0}^{3}[\frac{3}{8}qz_{0}K_{3}(qz_{0})-2K_{2}(qz_{0})],

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}_{yz}^{\text{diel}}(\mathbf{q}z_{0})=

\displaystyle iq_{y}q^{2}z_{0}^{3}[\frac{3}{8}qz_{0}K_{3}(qz_{0})-2K_{2}(qz_{0})].

Substituting Eqs. (
35
) and (
37
) in Eq. (
34
), we write
U_{\text{cla}}^{(1)}
as

\displaystyle U_{\text{cla}}^{(1)}(\mathbf{r}_{0})=

\displaystyle-\frac{\epsilon_{1}^{2}(1-\frac{\epsilon_{2}}{\epsilon_{1}})}{\epsilon_{2}\left(\epsilon_{1}+\epsilon_{2}\right)^{2}}\sum_{i,j}\frac{d_{i}d_{j}}{64\pi\epsilon_{0}z_{0}^{4}}\int\frac{d^{2}\mathbf{q}}{\left(2\pi\right)^{2}}\tilde{h}\left(\mathbf{q}\right)

\displaystyle\times e^{i\mathbf{q}\cdot{\bf r}_{0\parallel}}\left[\mathcal{I}_{ij}^{\text{cond}}\left(\mathbf{q}z_{0}\right)+\frac{\epsilon_{2}}{\epsilon_{1}}\mathcal{I}_{ij}^{\text{diel}}\left(\mathbf{q}z_{0}\right)\right].

(39)

Making
\epsilon_{1}\to\infty
and
\epsilon_{2}=1
in Eq. (
39
),
one recovers
U_{\text{cla}}^{(1)}=U_{\text{cond}}^{(1)}
, obtained in Ref.
Nogueira et al. 2021
.

Let us investigate the case of a sinusoidal corrugated surface with amplitude
a
and corrugation period
\lambda
, which is described by
h(x)=a\cos(kx)
, where
k=2\pi/\lambda
and
a\ll z_{0}
.
Then, the integrals in Eq. (
39
) can be analytically solved
Gradshteyn and Ryzhik 2007
;
Watson 1944
, resulting in

\displaystyle U_{\text{cla}}^{(1)}(\mathbf{r}_{0})=-\frac{\epsilon_{1}^{2}}{\epsilon_{2}(\epsilon_{1}+\epsilon_{2})^{2}}

\displaystyle\frac{3aA(\frac{\epsilon_{2}}{\epsilon_{1}},d_{i}d_{j},kz_{0})}{512\pi\epsilon_{0}z_{0}^{4}}

\displaystyle\times\cos

\displaystyle\left[kx_{0}-\delta\left(\frac{\epsilon_{2}}{\epsilon_{1}},d_{i}d_{j},kz_{0}\right)\right],

(40)

where
\delta
is a nontrivial phase function defined by

\sin(\delta)=\frac{B(\frac{\epsilon_{2}}{\epsilon_{1}},d_{i}d_{j},kz_{0})}{A(\frac{\epsilon_{2}}{\epsilon_{1}},d_{i}d_{j},kz_{0})},\;\;\;\cos(\delta)=\frac{C(\frac{\epsilon_{2}}{\epsilon_{1}},d_{i}d_{j},kz_{0})}{A(\frac{\epsilon_{2}}{\epsilon_{1}},d_{i}d_{j},kz_{0})},

(41)

with
A=\sqrt{B^{2}+C^{2}}
,

\displaystyle B\left(\frac{\epsilon_{2}}{\epsilon_{1}},d_{i}d_{j},kz_{0}\right)=

\displaystyle-2d_{x}d_{z}\left(1-\frac{\epsilon_{2}}{\epsilon_{1}}\right)

\displaystyle\times[\mathcal{R}_{xz}^{\text{cond}}(kz_{0})+\frac{\epsilon_{2}}{\epsilon_{1}}\mathcal{R}_{xz}^{\text{diel}}(kz_{0})],

(42)

\displaystyle C\left(\frac{\epsilon_{2}}{\epsilon_{1}},d_{i}d_{j},kz_{0}\right)=

\displaystyle\left(1-\frac{\epsilon_{2}}{\epsilon_{1}}\right)\sum_{i}d_{i}^{2}

\displaystyle\times[\mathcal{R}_{ii}^{\text{cond}}\left(kz_{0}\right)+\frac{\epsilon_{2}}{\epsilon_{1}}\mathcal{R}_{ii}^{\text{diel}}\left(kz_{0}\right)],

(43)

the functions
{\cal R}_{ij}^{\text{cond}}(kz_{0})
are defined by:

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}_{xx}^{\text{cond}}\left(u\right)

\displaystyle=u^{3}K_{3}\left(u\right)-u^{4}K_{2}\left(u\right);

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}_{yy}^{\text{cond}}\left(u\right)

\displaystyle=u^{3}K_{3}\left(u\right);

(44)

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}_{zz}^{\text{cond}}\left(u\right)

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{16}{3}u^{2}+u^{4}\right)K_{2}\left(u\right)+\frac{2}{3}u^{3}K_{3}\left(u\right);

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}_{xz}^{\text{cond}}\left(u\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{8}{3}u^{3}K_{2}\left(u\right)-u^{4}K_{3}\left(u\right),

and the functions
{\cal R}_{ij}^{\text{diel}}(kz_{0})
are defined by:

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}_{xx}^{\text{diel}}\left(u\right)

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{56}{3}u^{2}+u^{4}\right)K_{2}\left(u\right)-\frac{11}{3}u^{3}K_{3}\left(u\right);

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}_{yy}^{\text{diel}}\left(u\right)

\displaystyle=8u^{2}K_{2}\left(u\right)-u^{3}K_{3}\left(u\right);

(45)

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}_{zz}^{\text{diel}}\left(u\right)

\displaystyle=2u^{3}K_{3}\left(u\right)-u^{4}K_{2}\left(u\right);

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}_{xz}^{\text{diel}}\left(u\right)

\displaystyle=u^{4}K_{3}\left(u\right)-\frac{16}{3}u^{3}K_{2}\left(u\right).

IV
Van der Waals interaction

Let us consider a neutral polarizable particle, put at
{\bf r}_{0}=x_{0}\hat{{\bf x}}+y_{0}\hat{{\bf y}}+z_{0}\hat{{\bf z}}

(z_{0}>0)
, embedded in a dielectric
\epsilon_{2}
, as illustrated in Fig.
1
(with
\textbf{r}^{\prime}=\textbf{r}_{0}
).
The vdW interaction
U_{\text{vdW}}
between this particle and a dielectric
\epsilon_{1}
[Fig.
1
] can be written as
U_{\text{vdW}}\approx U^{(0)}_{\text{vdW}}+U^{(1)}_{\text{vdW}}
.
The first term
U^{(0)}_{\text{vdW}}
, which can be written as

U_{\text{vdW}}^{(0)}(z_{0})=-\frac{1}{\epsilon_{2}}\left(\frac{\epsilon_{1}-\epsilon_{2}}{\epsilon_{1}+\epsilon_{2}}\right)\frac{\langle\hat{d}_{x}^{2}\rangle+\langle\hat{d}_{y}^{2}\rangle+2\langle\hat{d}_{z}^{2}\rangle}{64\pi\epsilon_{0}z_{0}^{3}},

(49)

is the vdW interaction energy between the particle and the dielectric
\epsilon_{1}
, for the case of a planar interface, as illustrated in Fig.
1
.
In Eq. (
49
), one can see that, when
\epsilon_{2}<\epsilon_{1}
, one has the particle attracted towards the planar interface, whereas if
\epsilon_{2}>\epsilon_{1}
, it occurs a repulsion and a reduction of the magnitude of the force.
The second term,
U^{(1)}_{\text{vdW}}
, which is the first-order correction to
U^{(0)}_{\text{vdW}}
due to the corrugation in the interface between
\epsilon_{1}
and
\epsilon_{2}
, is obtained by substituting
G^{(1)}
, given by Eq. (
28
) [with
{\cal G}^{(1)}
given by Eqs. (
22
)-(
25
)], in Eq. (
32
), obtaining

\displaystyle U_{\text{vdW}}^{(1)}(\mathbf{r}_{0})=

\displaystyle-\frac{\epsilon_{1}^{2}(1-\frac{\epsilon_{2}}{\epsilon_{1}})}{\epsilon_{2}\left(\epsilon_{1}+\epsilon_{2}\right)^{2}}\sum_{i,j}\frac{\langle\hat{d}_{i}\hat{d}_{j}\rangle}{64\pi\epsilon_{0}z_{0}^{4}}\int\frac{d^{2}\mathbf{q}}{\left(2\pi\right)^{2}}\tilde{h}\left(\mathbf{q}\right)

\displaystyle\times e^{i\mathbf{q}\cdot{\bf r}_{0\parallel}}\left[\mathcal{I}_{ij}^{\text{cond}}\left(\mathbf{q}z_{0}\right)+\frac{\epsilon_{2}}{\epsilon_{1}}\mathcal{I}_{ij}^{\text{diel}}\left(\mathbf{q}z_{0}\right)\right],

(50)

where

\langle\hat{d}_{i}\hat{d}_{j}\rangle=\frac{\hbar}{\pi}\int_{0}^{\infty}d\xi\,\alpha_{ij}\left(i\xi,\epsilon_{2}\right),

with
\alpha_{ij}
being the components of the polarizability tensor
Buhmann 2012a
;
Buhmann 2012b
.
The dependence of the polarizability on
\epsilon_{2}
, comes from the fact that this property is modified when the particle is embedded in a dielectric medium
Fiedler et al. 2017
.
Here, we assume that, in a first approximation, the polarization tensor is given by
\alpha_{ij}\left(i\xi,\epsilon_{2}\right)\approx f(\epsilon_{2})\alpha_{ij}^{0}\left(i\xi\right)
, where
\alpha_{ij}^{0}\left(i\xi\right)
is the vacuum polarizability, and
f(\epsilon_{2})>0
is a function which carries all the dependence of the polarizability on
\epsilon_{2}
.

Let us investigate the case of a sinusoidal corrugated surface with amplitude
a
and corrugation period
\lambda
, which is described by
h(x)=a\cos(kx)
, where
k=2\pi/\lambda
and
a\ll z_{0}
.
Then, Eq. (
50
) leads to

\displaystyle U_{\text{vdW}}^{(1)}(\mathbf{r}_{0})=-\frac{\epsilon_{1}^{2}}{\epsilon_{2}\left(\epsilon_{1}+\epsilon_{2}\right)^{2}}

\displaystyle\frac{3aA(\frac{\epsilon_{2}}{\epsilon_{1}},\langle\hat{d}_{i}\hat{d}_{j}\rangle,kz_{0})}{512\pi\epsilon_{0}z_{0}^{4}}

\displaystyle\times\cos

\displaystyle\left[kx_{0}-\delta\left(\frac{\epsilon_{2}}{\epsilon_{1}},\langle\hat{d}_{i}\hat{d}_{j}\rangle,kz_{0}\right)\right],

(51)

where
A(\frac{\epsilon_{2}}{\epsilon_{1}},\langle\hat{d}_{i}\hat{d}_{j}\rangle,kz_{0})
and
\delta(\frac{\epsilon_{2}}{\epsilon_{1}},\langle\hat{d}_{i}\hat{d}_{j}\rangle,kz_{0})

are obtained by replacing

{d}_{i}{d}_{j}\rightarrow\langle\hat{d}_{i}\hat{d}_{j}\rangle
in Eqs. (
41
)-(
43
).
Similar to the classical case, taking the limit
\lambda/z_{0}\to\infty
, one obtains

U_{\text{vdW}}^{(1)}\to-a\cos\left(kx_{0}\right)\frac{3}{z_{0}}\frac{1}{\epsilon_{2}}\left(\frac{\epsilon_{1}-\epsilon_{2}}{\epsilon_{1}+\epsilon_{2}}\right)\frac{\langle\hat{d}_{x}^{2}\rangle+\langle\hat{d}_{y}^{2}\rangle+2\langle\hat{d}_{z}^{2}\rangle}{64\pi\epsilon_{0}z_{0}^{3}},\\

so that, one can also write

U_{\text{vdW}}^{(1)}\to-h(x_{0})\frac{dU_{\text{vdW}}^{(0)}(z_{0})}{dz_{0}}.

(52)

which is characteristic of the PFA
Dalvit et al. 2008b
.
One can see that this limit eliminates the presence of the phase function
\delta
, so that, the intermediate regime and the transitions between peak and valley regimes can not be predicted if this approximation is used.

Considering isotropic particles, we have
\langle\hat{d}_{x}\hat{d}_{z}\rangle=0
and
\langle\hat{d}^{2}_{x}\rangle=\langle\hat{d}^{2}_{y}\rangle=\langle\hat{d}^{2}_{z}\rangle=\langle\hat{d}^{2}\rangle>0
, which leads to
B(\langle\hat{d}_{i}\hat{d}_{j}\rangle,kz_{0})=0
and
C=\left(1-\frac{\epsilon_{2}}{\epsilon_{1}}\right)\langle\hat{d}^{2}\rangle\sum_{i}[\mathcal{R}_{ii}^{\text{cond}}\left(kz_{0}\right)+\frac{\epsilon_{2}}{\epsilon_{1}}\mathcal{R}_{ii}^{\text{diel}}\left(kz_{0}\right)]
.
Thus, the occurrence of peak and valley regimes depending on
\lambda/z_{0}
and
{\epsilon_{2}}/{\epsilon_{1}}
is shown in Fig.
8
.
It is important to remark that, the sign of the function
C
is not affected by
\langle\hat{d}^{2}\rangle
, which means the behavior illustrated in Fig.
8
is valid to any isotropic particle.
In this figure, one can note that, for
{\epsilon_{2}}/{\epsilon_{1}}<1
, an isotropic particle, under the action of the lateral vdW force, can only be attracted by the corrugation peaks.
Otherwise, for
{\epsilon_{2}}/{\epsilon_{1}}>1
, one can see that a transition between peak and valley regime can occur, and we highlight the value
\lambda/z_{0}\approx 0.864

{\epsilon_{2}}/{\epsilon_{1}}
that makes the peak regime possible.
The existence of two regimes when
{\epsilon_{2}}/{\epsilon_{1}}>1

V
Final remarks

We studied how the peak, valley and intermediate regimes for the lateral vdW force are affected by the consideration of two non-dispersive semi-infinite dielectrics,
\epsilon_{1}
and
\epsilon_{2}
, separated by a corrugated interface.
Our main result is given by Eq. (
50
), which, applied to a sinusoidal corrugation [Eq. (
51
)], showed that when
\epsilon_{2}/\epsilon_{1}<1

these regimes have, unless numerical factors, behaviors similar to those found for the
situation discussed in Ref.
Nogueira et al. 2021
, where an anisotropic particle is in vacuum and interacting with a perfectly conducting surface.
For the case
\epsilon_{2}/\epsilon_{1}>1
,
we showed that the differences from
\epsilon_{2}/\epsilon_{1}<1
are far beyond of a mere permute between the peak and valley regimes.
For instance, when the particle is oriented in
xy
-plane, enhancing the ratio
\epsilon_{2}/\epsilon_{1}
, one has a change of the peak regimes: from the particle oriented around
\phi=0,\pi
to
\phi=\pi/2,3\pi/2
.
As another example, we can have situations of almost complete elimination of the peak regime.
Moreover, we showed a remarkable existence of the peak and valley regimes for an isotropic particle when
{\epsilon_{2}}/{\epsilon_{1}}>1
(whereas for
{\epsilon_{2}}/{\epsilon_{1}}<1
, the existence of these regimes demands anisotropy).

In the classical context, involving a particle presenting a permanent electric dipole moment, our main result is given by Eq. (
39
), with the application to a sinusoidal corrugation [Eq. (
40
)] providing the description of the peak, valley and intermediate regimes in this context.
Eqs. (
40
) and (
51
) provide, in classical and quantum domains, respectively, a first estimate about the non-trivial behavior of the peak, valley and intermediate regimes in the presence of dielectric media.

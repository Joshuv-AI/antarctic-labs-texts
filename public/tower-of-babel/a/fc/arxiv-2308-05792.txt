Commuting operations factorise
Renato Renner, Ramona Wolf

Abstract
. Consider two agents, Alice and Bob, each of whom takes a quantum input, operates on a shared quantum system K K , and produces a quantum output. Alice and Bob’s operations may commute, in the sense that the joint input-output behaviour is independent of the order in which they access K K . Here we ask whether this commutation property implies that K K can be split into two factors on which Alice and Bob act separately. The question can be regarded as a “fully quantum” generalisation of a problem posed by Tsirelson, who considered the case where Alice and Bob’s inputs and outputs are classical. In this case, the answer is negative in general, but it is known that a factorisation exists in finite dimensions. Here we show the same holds in the fully quantum case, i.e., commuting operations factorise, provided that all input systems are finite-dimensional.

Preliminaries

This section collects definitions, notation and theorems that are used in the proofs of the paper.

Notation 2.1
.

We label Hilbert spaces with capital letters
H

\rho_{H}\coloneqq\tr_{K}(\rho_{HK})

is the partial trace over

Notation 2.2
.

We denote by
\mathrm{id}_{H}
the identity operator on
H
. For
H

\,\overline{\mathrm{id}}_{H}
is the normalised maximally mixed state on

Notation 2.3
.

We write
\mathcal{M}:H\to K
or
\mathcal{M}_{H\to K}
to indicate that a completely positive (CP) map
\mathcal{M}
goes from a system

to a system

. For a CP map
\mathcal{M}_{H\to KR}

\mathcal{M}_{H\to K}\coloneqq\mathrm{tr}_{R}\circ\mathcal{M}_{H\to KR}
. We usually omit identity maps, i.e.,
\mathcal{M}_{H\to K}(\rho_{HR})\coloneqq\left(\mathcal{M}_{H\to K}\otimes\mathcal{I}_{R}\right)(\rho_{HR})
.

Remark 2.4
.

A CP map
\mathcal{M}:H\to K
is trace-preserving (TP) if and only if
\tr(\mathcal{M}(W_{H}))=\mathrm{tr}(W_{H})
holds for any trace-class operator
W_{H}
. This may also be written as

(2)

\displaystyle\tr_{K}\circ\mathcal{M}_{H\to K}=\tr_{H}.

Note also that, if
\mathcal{M}
has the Kraus representation
\mathcal{M}:\,W_{H}\mapsto\sum_{z}E_{z}W_{H}E_{z}^{*}
, then the TP property is equivalent to
\sum_{z}E_{z}^{*}E_{z}=\mathrm{id}_{H}
.

Similarly,
\mathcal{M}
is trace non-increasing if and only if
\tr(\mathcal{M}(W_{H}))\leq\mathrm{tr}(W_{H})
for any trace-class operator
W_{H}\geq 0
or, equivalently, if the Kraus operators satisfy
\sum_{z}E_{z}^{*}E_{z}\leq\mathrm{id}_{H}
. Note that this also implies the operator inequality

(3)

\displaystyle\tr_{K}\circ\mathcal{M}_{H\to K}(W_{HR}))\leq\tr_{H}(W_{HR})\qquad\forall\,W_{HR}\geq 0.

Notation 2.5
.

For any state
\rho_{H}
, we can define a CPTP map from

Appendix A
On the Choi-Jamiołkowski isomorphism

Let
\psi_{H\tilde{H}}=\smash{\lvert\psi\rangle\!\langle\psi\rvert_{H\tilde{H}}}
be a maximally entangled state between a finite-dimensional space
H
and an isomorphic space
\tilde{H}
, which we keep fixed for the following discussion. According to
2.10
, we may choose an orthonormal basis
\smash{\{\lvert i\rangle_{H}\}_{i\in\{1,\ldots,d\}}}
of
H
, which then induces an orthonormal basis
\{\lvert i\rangle_{\tilde{H}}\}_{i\in\{1,\ldots d\}}
on
\tilde{H}
such that

(203)

\lvert\psi\rangle_{H\tilde{H}}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{d}}\sum_{i}\lvert i\rangle_{H}\otimes\lvert i\rangle_{\tilde{H}},

where
d\coloneqq\dim(H)=\dim(\tilde{H})
.

Definition A.1
.

The Choi-Jamiołkowski (C.-J.) operator of a CP map
\mathcal{M}:H\to K
is defined as

(204)

\rho_{K\tilde{H}}\coloneqq\mathcal{M}(\psi_{H\tilde{H}}).

Lemma A.2
.

Let
\rho_{K\tilde{H}}
be the C.-J. operator of a CP map
\mathcal{M}:H\to K
. Then for all operators
W_{H}
on
H
,

(205)

\mathcal{M}(W_{H})=d^{2}\,\mathrm{tr}_{\tilde{H}}\Big(\mathrm{tr}_{H}\big(W_{H}\psi_{H\tilde{H}}\big)\rho_{K\tilde{H}}\Big).

Proof.

Using
A.1
and
eq.

203
, we can verify the claim by a direct calculation (states with the same colour yield a
\delta
-function of the corresponding labels):

(206)

\displaystyle d^{2}\,\mathrm{tr}_{\tilde{H}}

\displaystyle\Big(\mathrm{tr}_{H}\big(W_{H}\psi_{H\tilde{H}}\big)\rho_{K\tilde{H}})\Big)

(207)

\displaystyle=d^{2}\,\mathrm{tr}_{\tilde{H}}\Big(\mathrm{tr}_{H}\big(W_{H}\psi_{H\tilde{H}}\big)\mathcal{M}(\psi_{H\tilde{H}})\Big)

(208)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i,i^{\prime},j,j^{\prime}}\mathrm{tr}_{\tilde{H}}\Big(\mathrm{tr}_{H}\big(W_{H}(\lvert i\rangle_{H}\lvert i\rangle_{\tilde{H}}\langle i^{\prime}\rvert_{H}\langle i^{\prime}\rvert_{\tilde{H}})\big)\mathcal{M}(\lvert j\rangle_{H}\lvert j\rangle_{\tilde{H}}\langle j^{\prime}\rvert_{H}\langle j^{\prime}\rvert_{\tilde{H}})\Big)

(209)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k,\tilde{k}}\sum_{i,i^{\prime},j,j^{\prime}}{\color[rgb]{0,0.5742,0.1719}\langle\tilde{k}\rvert_{\tilde{H}}}\Big(\langle k\rvert_{H}\big(W_{H}(\lvert i\rangle_{H}{\color[rgb]{0,0.5742,0.1719}\lvert i\rangle_{\tilde{H}}}{\color[rgb]{1,0.5078,0}\langle i^{\prime}\rvert_{H}}{\color[rgb]{0.0547,0.3164,0.6563}\langle i^{\prime}\rvert_{\tilde{H}}}){\color[rgb]{1,0.5,0}\lvert k\rangle_{H}}\big)\mathcal{M}(\lvert j\rangle_{H}{\color[rgb]{0.0547,0.3164,0.6563}\lvert j\rangle_{\tilde{H}}}\langle j^{\prime}\rvert_{H}{\color[rgb]{1,0.0742,0}\langle j^{\prime}\rvert_{\tilde{H}}})\Big){\color[rgb]{1,0.0742,0}\lvert\tilde{k}\rangle_{\tilde{H}}}

(210)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i,j}\langle j\rvert_{H}W_{H}\lvert i\rangle_{H}\mathcal{M}(\lvert j\rangle_{H}\langle i\rvert_{H})

(211)

\displaystyle=\mathcal{M}\Big(\sum_{i,j}\lvert j\rangle_{H}\langle j\rvert_{H}W_{H}\lvert i\rangle_{H}\langle i\rvert_{H}\Big)

(212)

\displaystyle=\mathcal{M}\big(W_{H}\big).

∎

Lemma A.3
.

A CP map
\mathcal{M}:H\to K
is trace non-increasing if and only if its corresponding C.-J. operator
\rho_{K\tilde{H}}
fulfils
\rho_{\tilde{H}}\leq\,\overline{\mathrm{id}}_{\tilde{H}}
. It is trace-preserving if and only if
\rho_{\tilde{H}}=\,\overline{\mathrm{id}}_{\tilde{H}}
.

Proof.

Let

(213)

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}(W_{H})=\sum_{z}E_{z}W_{H}E_{z}^{*}

be the Kraus representation of
\mathcal{M}
with Kraus operators
E_{z}
. We then have

(214)

\displaystyle\rho_{\tilde{H}}=\mathrm{tr}_{K}\bigl(\mathcal{M}(\psi_{H\tilde{H}})\bigr)

\displaystyle=\sum_{z}\mathrm{tr}_{K}\bigl((E_{z}\otimes\mathrm{id}_{\tilde{H}})\psi_{H\tilde{H}}(E_{z}^{*}\otimes\mathrm{id}_{\tilde{H}})\bigr)

(215)

\displaystyle=\sum_{z}\mathrm{tr}_{H}\bigl(\psi_{H\tilde{H}}(E_{z}^{*}E_{z}\otimes\mathrm{id}_{\tilde{H}})\bigr)

(216)

\displaystyle=\mathrm{tr}_{H}\bigl(\psi_{H\tilde{H}}\sum_{z}E_{z}^{*}E_{z}\otimes\mathrm{id}_{\tilde{H}}\bigr),

where we used the cyclicity of the trace. Because, for trace non-increasing maps,
\sum_{x}E_{x}^{*}E_{x}\leq\id_{H}
, we find

(217)

\displaystyle\rho_{\tilde{H}}\leq\mathrm{tr}_{H}\bigl(\psi_{H\tilde{H}}(\mathrm{id}_{H}\otimes\mathrm{id}_{\tilde{H}})\bigr)=\mathrm{tr}_{H}\bigl(\psi_{H\tilde{H}}\bigr)=\,\overline{\mathrm{id}}_{\tilde{H}}.

The inequality becomes an equality if
\mathcal{M}
is trace-preserving.

To show the other direction, suppose
\rho_{\tilde{H}}\leq\,\overline{\mathrm{id}}_{\tilde{H}}
. Then, using
A.2
,

(218)

\displaystyle\mathrm{tr}_{K}\big(\mathcal{M}(W_{H})\big)

\displaystyle=d^{2}\,\mathrm{tr}_{K}\Big[\mathrm{tr}_{\tilde{H}}\Big(\mathrm{tr}_{H}(W_{H}\psi_{H\tilde{H}})\rho_{K\tilde{H}}\Big)\Big]

(219)

\displaystyle=d^{2}\,\mathrm{tr}_{\tilde{H}}\Big(\mathrm{tr}_{H}(W_{H}\psi_{H\tilde{H}})\underbrace{\mathrm{tr}_{K}(\rho_{K\tilde{H}})}_{\leq\,\overline{\mathrm{id}}_{\tilde{H}}}\Big)

(220)

\displaystyle\leq d\,\mathrm{tr}_{\tilde{H}}\big(\mathrm{tr}_{H}(W_{H}\psi_{H\tilde{H}})\big)

(221)

\displaystyle=d\,\mathrm{tr}_{H}\big(W_{H}\underbrace{\mathrm{tr}_{\tilde{H}}(\psi_{H\tilde{H}}}_{=\,\overline{\mathrm{id}}_{H}})\big)

(222)

\displaystyle=\mathrm{tr}_{H}(W_{H}).

The inequality in
eq.

220
becomes an equality if
\rho_{\tilde{H}}=\,\overline{\mathrm{id}}_{\tilde{H}}
.
∎

Lemma A.4
.

A CP map
\mathcal{M}:H_{A}\otimes H_{B}\to K_{A}\otimes K_{B}
, with
H_{A}\otimes H_{B}
finite-dimensional, has product form
\mathcal{M}^{(A)}\otimes\mathcal{M}^{(B)}
, where
\mathcal{M}^{(A)}:H_{A}\to K_{A}
and
\mathcal{M}^{(B)}:H_{B}\to K_{B}
if and only if its C.-J. operator
\rho_{K_{A}K_{B}\tilde{H}_{A}\tilde{H}_{B}}
has product form
\rho_{K_{A}\tilde{H}_{A}}\otimes\rho_{K_{B}\tilde{H}_{B}}
.

Proof.

According to
2.10
, the entangled state
\lvert\psi\rangle_{H\tilde{H}}
used for the C.-J. isomorphism can be decomposed as

(223)

\displaystyle\lvert\psi\rangle_{H_{A}H_{B}\tilde{H}_{A}\tilde{H}_{B}}=\lvert\psi\rangle_{H_{A}\tilde{H}_{A}}\otimes\lvert\psi\rangle_{H_{B}\tilde{H}_{B}},

where
\lvert\psi\rangle_{H_{A}\tilde{H}_{A}}
and
\lvert\psi\rangle_{H_{B}\tilde{H}_{B}}
are maximally entangled states on the respective subsystems. Suppose now that
\mathcal{M}=\mathcal{M}^{(A)}\otimes\mathcal{M}^{(B)}
. The corresponding C.-J. operator is then given by

(224)

\displaystyle\rho_{K_{A}K_{B}\tilde{H}_{A}\tilde{H}_{B}}

\displaystyle=\mathcal{M}\big(\psi_{H_{A}H_{B}\tilde{H}_{A}\tilde{H}_{B}}\big)

(225)

\displaystyle=\mathcal{M}^{(A)}\otimes\mathcal{M}^{(B)}\big(\psi_{H_{A}\tilde{H}_{A}}\otimes\psi_{H_{B}\tilde{H}_{B}}\big)

(226)

\displaystyle=\mathcal{M}^{(A)}\big(\psi_{H_{A}\tilde{H}_{A}}\big)\otimes\mathcal{M}^{(B)}\big(\psi_{H_{B}\tilde{H}_{B}}\big)

(227)

\displaystyle\eqqcolon\rho_{K_{A}\tilde{H}_{A}}\otimes\rho_{K_{B}\tilde{H}_{B}},

hence it has product form.

For the other direction, suppose that the C.-J. operator is of the form
\rho_{K_{A}K_{B}\tilde{H}_{A}\tilde{H}_{B}}=\rho_{K_{A}\tilde{H}_{A}}\otimes\rho_{K_{B}\tilde{H}_{B}}
. The corresponding map is then given by

(228)

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}(W_{H_{A}H_{B}})

(229)

\displaystyle=d^{2}\,\mathrm{tr}_{\tilde{H}_{A}\tilde{H}_{B}}\Big(\mathrm{tr}_{H_{A}H_{B}}\big(W_{H_{A}H_{B}}\psi_{H_{A}H_{B}\tilde{H}_{A}\tilde{H}_{B}}\big)\rho_{K_{A}K_{B}\tilde{H}_{A}\tilde{H}_{B}}\Big)

(230)

\displaystyle=(\dim(H_{A})\dim(H_{B}))^{2}\,\mathrm{tr}_{\tilde{H}_{A}\tilde{H}_{B}}\Big(\mathrm{tr}_{H_{A}H_{B}}\big(W_{H_{A}H_{B}}\psi_{H_{A}\tilde{H}_{A}}\otimes\psi_{H_{B}\tilde{H}_{B}}\big)\rho_{K_{A}\tilde{H}_{A}}\otimes\rho_{K_{B}\tilde{H}_{B}}\Big)

(231)

\displaystyle=\Big(\dim(H_{A})^{2}\tr_{\tilde{H}_{A}H_{A}}\big(\psi_{H_{A}\tilde{H}_{A}}\rho_{K_{A}\tilde{H}_{A}}\big)\otimes\dim(H_{B})^{2}\tr_{\tilde{H}_{B}H_{B}}\big(\psi_{H_{B}\tilde{H}_{B}}\rho_{K_{B}\tilde{H}_{B}}\big)\Big)(W_{H_{A}H_{B}})

(232)

\displaystyle\eqqcolon\big(\mathcal{M}^{(A)}\otimes\mathcal{M}^{(B)}\big)(W_{H_{A}H_{B}}),

hence it has product form.
∎

Appendix B
Necessity of the unitality condition

In
3.1
, the unitality condition (Condition
(ii)
; see also the slightly weaker requirement mentioned in
3.3
), is necessary (see
3.6
). The same condition also occurs in
4.1
. Its necessity can be illustrated with the following example of maps for
4.1
.

Let
A
,
B
, and
H=(I,J,K)
be classical random variables, where
I
,
J
, and
K
take values
i,j\in\{0,1\}
and
k\in\{0,1\}^{2}\cup\{\perp\}
. Define the maps
{\mathcal{X}}:H\to A\otimes H
and
{\mathcal{Y}}:H\to B\otimes H
as follows.

(233)

\displaystyle{\mathcal{X}}:\hskip 10.0pt

\displaystyle\text{if }k=\perp\text{ then }a\in_{R}\{0,1\};(k_{1},k_{2})\coloneqq(a,i)

(234)

\displaystyle\text{else }a\coloneqq k_{1}\oplus(i\cdot k_{2})

(235)

\displaystyle{\mathcal{Y}}:\hskip 10.0pt

\displaystyle\text{if }k=\perp\text{ then }b\in_{R}\{0,1\};(k_{1},k_{2})\coloneqq(b,j)

(236)

\displaystyle\text{else }b\coloneqq k_{1}\oplus(j\cdot k_{2})

where
a\in_{R}\{0,1\}
means that
a
is chosen uniformly at random.

These maps fulfil all conditions in
4.1
except for the unitality condition: Firstly, they are CP and TP since they are defined in terms of functions of variables. Furthermore, it is clear from their definitions that
\mathrm{tr}_{H}\circ{\mathcal{X}}
and
\mathrm{tr}_{H}\circ{\mathcal{Y}}
are independent of
J
and
I
, respectively, i.e., Condition
(iii)
holds. Finally, they commute: This is obvious for the input
(i,j,k\neq\perp)
since in this case, the maps do not change the value of
k
. For
(i,j,k=\perp)
, the output on
A
and
B
of
{\mathcal{Y}}\circ{\mathcal{X}}
is
a=r
and
b=r\oplus(j\cdot i)
, where
r
is a uniform random bit. Hence,
a
and
b
are both uniformly random bits with the correlation
a\oplus b=i\cdot j
. On the other hand, the output of
{\mathcal{X}}\circ{\mathcal{Y}}
is
b=r
and
a=r\oplus(i\cdot j)
. Again, this means that
a
and
b
are both uniform random bits with the correlation
a\oplus b=i\cdot j
. Thus, the probability distribution
\mathrm{Pr}_{AB|IJK}(\cdot,\cdot|i,j,\perp)
is identical for
{\mathcal{Y}}\circ{\mathcal{X}}
and
{\mathcal{X}}\circ{\mathcal{Y}}
, hence the maps satisfy Condition
(i)
of
4.1
.

However,
\mathrm{tr}_{A}\circ{\mathcal{X}}
is not unital, i.e., uniform distributions are not mapped to uniform distributions. This can be seen from the fact that the output on
K
is never
k=\perp
, hence the probability of
k=\perp
is zero. In particular, the probability distribution of the output
K
is never uniform. By symmetry,
\mathrm{tr}_{B}\circ{\mathcal{Y}}
is not unital, either. Hence, Condition
(ii)
is violated.

The question is now whether it is still possible to find a CPTP map
\mathcal{D}
such that
\mathrm{tr}_{H}\circ{\mathcal{Y}}\circ{\mathcal{X}}
can be written as
(\overline{\mathcal{X}}\otimes\overline{\mathcal{Y}})\circ\mathcal{D}
as in
4.1
, even though the unitality condition is not fulfilled. This is equivalent to asking whether the input-output behaviour described above can be generated with a setup as depicted on the right-hand side of
fig.

5
. Note that this setup corresponds to that of a CHSH game (i.e., a Bell test), where Alice and Bob each have local inputs
I
and
J
, respectively, and outputs
A
and
B
. Furthermore, each of them has access to one part of a bipartite quantum system
K\otimes K
, which may, for example, be prepared in a maximally entangled state. Here, Alice’s output
A
is independent of Bob’s input
J
, and Bob’s output
B
is independent of Alice’s input
I
.

Crucially, the input-output behaviour defined by
\mathrm{Pr}_{AB|IJK}
for
k=\perp
corresponds to a PR box
[
PR94
]
, whose characteristics is that it always fulfils the winning condition,
a\oplus b=i\cdot j
, of the CHSH game. However, from
[
Tsi80
]
we know that this condition can only be fulfilled with a probability
\approx 85\%
while
4.1
would imply that it is fulfilled with certainty if it were applicable.

We conclude that the unitality condition is necessary for
4.1
, i.e., it cannot be dropped without replacement. Since the corollary is an implication of
3.1
, this also shows that the unitality condition is necessary for
3.1
, i.e., the claim of the theorem would be wrong if Condition
(ii)
were dropped—a fact that also follows from our converse statement,
3.6
.

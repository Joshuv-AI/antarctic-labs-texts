1.
Introduction

We denote the Gaussian on
\mathbb{R}^{k}
by

d\gamma(x)=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{\frac{k}{2}}}e^{-|x|^{2}/2}\,dx,

where
|x|
is the Euclidean norm of
x=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{k})\in\mathbb{R}^{k}
. For
p>0
we set

\|f\|_{p}:=\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{k}}|f|^{p}\,d\gamma\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}

for all Borel measurable functions
f:\mathbb{R}^{k}\mapsto\mathbb{C}
. Given a multiindex
\alpha=(\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{k})\in(\mathbb{Z}_{+})^{k}
we define probabilist’s Hermite polynomials on
\mathbb{R}^{k}
as follows

H_{\alpha}(x)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{k}}(x+iy)^{\alpha}d\gamma(y),

where
w^{\alpha}:=w_{1}^{\alpha_{1}}\ldots w_{k}^{\alpha_{k}}
for any
w=(w_{1},\ldots,w_{k})\in\mathbb{C}^{k}
.
The family
\{H_{\alpha}\}_{\alpha\in(\mathbb{Z}_{+})^{k}}
forms an orthogonal basis in
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{k},d\gamma)
equipped with the natural innear product structure.

Any polynomial
f:\mathbb{R}^{k}\to\mathbb{C}
admits a representation

(1.1)

f(x)=\sum_{|\alpha|\leq\deg f}c_{\alpha}H_{\alpha}(x),

for some
c_{\alpha}\in\mathbb{C}
, where
|\alpha|=\alpha_{1}+\ldots+\alpha_{k}
. For any complex number
z\in\mathbb{C}
,
|z|\leq 1
, Mahler transform
T_{z}
is an operator that acts on
f
as

T_{z}f(x)=\sum_{|\alpha|\leq\deg f}z^{|\alpha|}c_{\alpha}H_{\alpha}(x).

Clearly
T_{0}f(x)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{k}}fd\gamma
, and
T_{1}f(x)=f(x)
. When
z=r
,
r\in[-1,1]
, the operator

T_{r}f(x)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{k}}f(xr+\sqrt{1-r^{2}}y)d\gamma(y)

is called the noise operator (or Ornstein–Uhlenbeck semigroup when parametried by
r=e^{-t}
). Perhaps one of the most fundamental results in Gauss space is the hypercontractivity due to Bonami
[
2
]
, Nelson
[
12
]
, and Gross
[
5
]
which says that for any
p\geq 1
we have

(1.2)

\displaystyle\|T_{r}f\|_{(p-1)/r^{2}+1}\leq\|f\|_{p}\quad\text{holds for all}\quad r\in[-1,1].

Recently
[
7
]
Hariya showed that for any
r\in(0,1]

(1.3)

\displaystyle r^{2}\log\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{k}}\exp\left(\frac{T_{r}h}{r^{2}}\right)d\gamma\right)\leq\log\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{k}}\exp(h)d\gamma\right)

holds for any Borel measurable
h:\mathbb{R}^{k}\mapsto\mathbb{R}
with
\|h\|_{1}+\|e^{h}\|_{1}<\infty
. Moreover, using stochastic calculus Hariya unified both of the statements (
1.2
) and (
1.3
) into one compact and elegant inequality.

(Hariya

satisfy the growth condition (
4.1
) then

F\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{k}}P(|T_{z}f|)d\gamma\right)\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{k}}F(P(|f|))d\gamma

holds for all polynomials
f:\mathbb{R}^{k}\mapsto\mathbb{C}
, and all
k\geq 1
if and only if the local condition

(2.15)

\displaystyle\left|(1-z^{2})\left(\varphi^{\prime}(s)-1\right)-z^{2}\frac{e^{\varphi(s)+s}}{h(P(e^{s}))}\right|\leq(1-|z|^{2})\left(\varphi^{\prime}(s)+1\right)-|z|^{2}\frac{e^{\varphi(s)+s}}{h(P(e^{s}))}

holds for all
s\in\mathbb{R}
.

Example 1.
If we let
\varphi(t)=(p-1)t
, and
h(t)=\frac{p}{q-p}t
, where
1<p<q<\infty
, then clearly
F
and
P
satisfy the growth condition (
4.1
),
h(P(e^{s}))=\frac{e^{sp}}{q-p}
, and we obtain that (
2.15
) reduces to (
2.2
).

Example 2
. Let again
\varphi(t)=(p-1)t,p>1
, and
h(t)=\alpha t+\log(1+t)
with
\alpha>0
. Then clearly
F
and
P
satisfy the growth condition (
4.1
).
We have

\displaystyle\frac{tQ^{\prime\prime}(t)}{Q^{\prime}(t)}=\frac{p}{\alpha+\frac{p\log(1+t^{p}/p)}{t^{p}}}+p-1.

Thus
\sup_{t\in(0,\infty)}\frac{tQ^{\prime\prime}(t)}{Q^{\prime}(t)}=\frac{p}{\alpha}+p-1
, and hence Corollary
1
holds with

\displaystyle r^{*}=\min\left\{\sqrt{p-1},\frac{1}{\sqrt{\frac{p}{\alpha}+p-1}}\right\}.

More sophisticated examples can be created by considering
\varphi(t)=\beta t-\log(1+t^{2}),\beta>1
, and
h(t)=\alpha t+t^{\lambda}
with
\alpha>0
and
\lambda\in(0,1)
.

3.
The construction of
C(s)

To explain the appearence of the map
C(s)
for simplicity let
k=1
, and let us rewrite the inequality (
1.7
) as follows

(3.1)

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}F(P(|T_{z}f(\xi)|))\leq F(\mathbb{E}P(|f(\xi)|)),

where
\xi\sim N(0,1)
is the standard Gaussian random variable, and
F=Q\circ P^{-1}
. By the central limit theorem we may replace
\xi
by

\displaystyle\xi_{m}:=\frac{\varepsilon_{1}+\ldots+\varepsilon_{m}}{\sqrt{m}},

where
\varepsilon_{1},\ldots,\varepsilon_{m}
are i.i.d. symmetric Bernoulli
\pm 1
random variables, and ask whether a “stronger inequality”, i.e., (
3.1
) holds with
\xi_{m}
instead of
\xi
. Now notice that we can think of
f(\xi_{m})
as a function on the Hamming cube
\{-1,1\}^{m}
, namely

\displaystyle g(\varepsilon_{1},\ldots,\varepsilon_{m})=f\left(\frac{\varepsilon_{1}+\ldots+\varepsilon_{m}}{\sqrt{m}}\right).

Any function
g:\{-1,1\}^{m}\mapsto\mathbb{C}
admits Fourier–Wash series representaiton

(3.2)

\displaystyle g(\varepsilon)=\sum_{S\subset\{1,\ldots,m\}}\widehat{g}(S)W_{S}(\varepsilon),\quad\text{where}\quad W_{S}(\varepsilon)=\prod_{j\in S}\varepsilon_{j},

where
\varepsilon=(\varepsilon_{1},\ldots,\varepsilon_{m}),
and
\widehat{g}(S)=\mathbb{E}g(\varepsilon)W_{S}(\varepsilon)
.

An important part is to understand how
T_{z}f(\xi_{m})
is related to the right hand side of (
3.2
). Let
f(t)=\sum_{\ell=0}^{N}c_{\ell}H_{\ell}(t)
. Beckner’s lemma
[
1
]
says that for any integer
\ell\geq 0
, we have

\displaystyle H_{\ell}\left(\frac{\varepsilon_{1}+\ldots+\varepsilon_{m}}{\sqrt{m}}\right)=\frac{\ell!}{m^{\ell/2}}\sum_{|S|=\ell,\,S\subset\{1,\ldots,n\}}W_{S}(\varepsilon)+L_{m}

where
L_{m}
are
lower order terms
which converge to zero in distribution. Thus

\displaystyle g(\varepsilon_{1},\ldots,\varepsilon_{n})=f(\xi_{m})=\sum_{S\subset\{1,\ldots,m\},\,|S|\leq N}\frac{|S|!}{m^{|S|/2}}c_{|S|}W_{S}(\varepsilon)+\tilde{L}_{m},

and also

\displaystyle T_{z}f(\xi_{m})=\sum_{\ell=0}^{N}z^{\ell}c_{\ell}H_{\ell}(\xi_{m})=\sum_{S\subset\{1,\ldots,m\},\,|S|\leq N}z^{|S|}\frac{|S|!}{m^{|S|/2}}c_{|S|}W_{S}(\varepsilon)+K_{m},

where
K_{m},\tilde{L}_{m}
are some other lower order terms. If we ignore the lower order terms
\tilde{L}_{m}
and
K_{m}
and set them to be zero (at least we hope that this is the case in the limit
m\to\infty
after taking the expectation), and we denote
a_{S}:=\frac{|S|!}{m^{|S|/2}}c_{|S|}
, then we may wonder under what condiitons on
F,P
and
z
the following
discrete version
of (
3.1
) holds true

(3.3)

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}F\big(P\big(|\sum_{S\subset\{1,\ldots,m\}}z^{|S|}a_{S}W_{S}(\varepsilon)|\big)\big)\leq F\big(\mathbb{E}P\big(|\sum_{S\subset\{1,\ldots,m\}}a_{S}W_{S}(\varepsilon)|\big)\big).

If we denote
\tilde{g}(\varepsilon)=\sum_{S\subset\{1,\ldots,m\}}a_{S}W_{S}(\varepsilon)
, and extend the domain of the
polynomial

\tilde{g}
in a natural way to
\mathbb{C}^{m}
we can rewrite (
3.3
) as follows

(3.4)

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}F\big(P\big(|\tilde{g}(z\varepsilon_{1},\ldots,z\varepsilon_{m})|\big)\big)\leq F\big(\mathbb{E}P\big(|\tilde{g}(\varepsilon_{1},\ldots,\varepsilon_{m})|\big)\big).

The inequality (
3.4
) for
m=1
is what is called
two point-inequality

(3.5)

\displaystyle\frac{F(P(|a+bz|))+F(P(|a-bz|))}{2}\leq F\left(\frac{P(|a+b|)+P(|a-b|)}{2}\right)

holds for all
a,b\in\mathbb{C}
. The verification of (
3.5
) for a given
F
,
P
, and
z\in\mathbb{C}
, can be a difficult techincal problem, see for example, Weissler’s conjecture
[
8
]
where the two-point inequality (
3.5
) was solved in the case
F(t)=t
and
P(t)=t^{p}
.

An important observation is that if we let
a=1
, and
b=\omega\delta
, where
\omega\in\mathbb{C}
is arbitrary and
\delta\in\mathbb{R}
, then after writing Taylor’s formula for both sides of the two-point inequality (
3.5
) at point
\delta=0
we will see that the first order terms will cancel each other. The comparison of the second order terms in (
3.5
) and then optimization over all
w\in\mathbb{C}
gives the local condition (
1.6
) in Theorem
1
. So there is a hope that in the limit
m\to\infty
only the local condition will be needed, which happens to be true in addition with (
1.5
).

The condition (
1.5
) allows us to induct (
3.5
) and obtain (
3.3
). Indeed, as explained in Section
1
, the condition (
1.5
) implies (in fact is equiavlent to under some mild assumptions on
F
) that

(3.6)

\displaystyle\mathfrak{M}_{F}(h)=F^{-1}\left(\int_{\Omega}F(h)d\mu\right)\quad\text{is convex}

as a functional acting on nonnegative functions
h:\Omega\mapsto[0,\infty)
, where
d\mu
is a probability measure on the probability space
(\Omega,d\mu)
equipped with Borel
\sigma
-algebra. Therefore, we have

\displaystyle F^{-1}(\mathbb{E}F\big(P\big(|\tilde{g}(z\varepsilon_{1},\ldots,z\varepsilon_{m})|\big)\big))=F^{-1}(\mathbb{E}_{m-1}\mathbb{E}^{1}F\big(P\big(|\tilde{g}(z\varepsilon_{1},\ldots,z\varepsilon_{m})|\big)\big))

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle(\ref{two-pp})}}{{\leq}}F^{-1}(\mathbb{E}_{m-1}F\big(\mathbb{E}^{1}P\big(|\tilde{g}(\varepsilon_{1},\ldots,z\varepsilon_{m})|\big)\big))\stackrel{{\scriptstyle(\ref{polya1})}}{{\leq}}\mathbb{E}^{1}F^{-1}(\mathbb{E}_{m-1}F\big(P\big(|\tilde{g}(\varepsilon_{1},\ldots,z\varepsilon_{m})|\big)\big))

(3.7)

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathrm{induction}}}{{\leq}}\mathbb{E}P(|\tilde{g}(\varepsilon)|).

Here
\mathbb{E}_{n-1}
and
\mathbb{E}^{1}
take the average in the last
n-1
and the first variables correspondingly.

The inductive argument (
3.7
) shows that if the two-point inequality (
3.5
) holds, and also the functional
\mathfrak{M}_{F}(\cdot)
is convex then the discrete map

(3.8)

\displaystyle\varphi(k)=\mathbb{E}^{k}F^{-1}(\mathbb{E}_{n-k}F(P(|\tilde{g}(\varepsilon_{1},\ldots,\varepsilon_{k},z\varepsilon_{k+1},\ldots,z\varepsilon_{m})|)))

(3.9)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}^{k}F^{-1}(\mathbb{E}_{n-k}Q(|\tilde{g}(\varepsilon_{1},\ldots,\varepsilon_{k},z\varepsilon_{k+1},\ldots,z\varepsilon_{m})|))

is nondecreasing for
0\leq k\leq m
. If we choose
k=k(m)
so that
\lim_{m\to\infty}k/m=s\in[0,1]
then we expect the discrete map (
3.8
) to converge to

\displaystyle r(s)=\int_{\mathbb{R}}F^{-1}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}}Q\Big(\Big|\int_{\mathbb{R}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}R(\sqrt{s}(u+iv)+z\sqrt{1-s}(x+iy))\,d\gamma(v)\,d\gamma(y)\Big|\Big)\,d\gamma(x)\right)\,d\gamma(u),

where
R(t)=\sum_{\ell=0}^{N}a_{\ell}t^{\ell}
. The passage from the discrete map
\varphi(k)
to continuous map
r(s)
is a bit nontrivial, and it was proved in
[
9
]
for functions
Q(t)=t^{q}
and
F(t)=t^{q/p}
. Monotonicity of
\varphi(k)
implies that
r(s)
is nondecreasing. Since
r(0)=F^{-1}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}}Q(|T_{z}f|)d\gamma\right)
and
r(1)=\int_{\mathbb{R}}F^{-1}(Q(|f|))d\gamma=\int_{\mathbb{R}}P(|f|)d\gamma
(see Section
4
) we recover (
1.7
).

One can ingore all the heuristics used in this reasoning and try to directly show
r^{\prime}(s)\geq 0
. The map
C(s)
introduced in Section
4
is similar to
r(s)
but different,

\displaystyle C(s)=\int_{\mathbb{R}}F\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}}P\Big(\Big|\int_{\mathbb{R}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}R(\sqrt{s}(u+iv)+z\sqrt{1-s}(x+iy))\,d\gamma(v)\,d\gamma(y)\Big|\Big)\,d\gamma(u)\right)\,d\gamma(x),

for example, our flow
C(s)
interpolates (
1.7
) in a
slightly different way, namely, we have
C(0)=\int_{\mathbb{R}}F(P(|T_{z}f|))d\gamma
and
C(1)=F\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}}P(|f|)d\gamma\right)
. It remains to extend the argument from
\mathbb{R}
to
\mathbb{R}^{k}
and hope that
C^{\prime}(s)\geq 0
. Surprisingly, this turns out to be the case with the smoothened version of
C(s)
, under the assumptions (
1.5
) and (
1.6
).

4.
The proof of Theorem
1

Let
B\in C^{2}((0,\infty))
. We say
B
satisfies growth condition if there exists
N,M>0
such that

(4.1)

\displaystyle|B^{\prime\prime}(t)|<\exp(\log^{N}(t+N))

holds for all
t\geq M
. Clearly by integrating the inequality (
4.1
) we obtain similar bounds (perhaps with different constants
N
and
M
) for
|B(t)|
and
|B^{\prime}(t)|
too.

Lemma 1
.

Let nonnegative
F,M\in C([0,\infty))\cap C^{2}((0,\infty))
satisfy growth condition (
4.1
), and be such that
F^{\prime},M^{\prime}>0
on
(0,\infty)
. Assume

\displaystyle F^{\prime\prime}(x)>0\quad\text{and}\quad x\mapsto F^{\prime}(x)/F^{\prime\prime}(x)\quad\text{is concave on}\quad(0,\infty).

If the local condition

(4.2)

\displaystyle-\frac{F^{\prime\prime}(M(x))}{F^{\prime}(M(x))}(M^{\prime}(x)\Re(zw))^{2}+M^{\prime\prime}(x)((\Re w)^{2}-(\Re zw)^{2})+\frac{1-|z|^{2}}{2}\frac{M^{\prime}(x)}{x}|w|^{2}\geq 0

holds for all
x>0
, all
w\in\mathbb{C}
, and some
z\in\mathbb{C},|z|\leq 1
, then

(4.3)

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{k}}F(M(|T_{z}f|^{2}))d\gamma\leq F\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{k}}M(|f|^{2})d\gamma\right)

holds for all
k\geq 1
and all polynomials
f:\mathbb{R}^{k}\mapsto\mathbb{C}
.

Proof.

Pick any
\varepsilon>0
and consider
\tilde{M}(x)=M(x+\varepsilon)
. The main reason we introduce
\tilde{M}
is to avoid issues with the values
M^{\prime}(0)
,
M^{\prime\prime}(0)
being not defined, and the fact that
M(0)
can be zero which can make
F^{\prime}(M(0))
and
F^{\prime\prime}(M(0))
undefined. However, for the first time of reading the proof the reader should ignore these techincal difficulties and think
\varepsilon=0
, i.e.,
\tilde{M}=M
.

Notice that
\tilde{M}>M(\varepsilon)>0
, and
\tilde{M}
satisfies growth condition (
4.1
). Also notice that
\tilde{M}
satisfies the local condition (
4.2
). Indeed, it suffices to apply the inequality (
4.2
) at point
x+\varepsilon
instead of
x
, and use the inequality

\frac{1-|z|^{2}}{2}\frac{M^{\prime}(x+\varepsilon)}{x+\varepsilon}|w|^{2}\leq\frac{1-|z|^{2}}{2}\frac{\tilde{M}^{\prime}(x)}{x}|w|^{2}.

Let
d\gamma^{(s)}
denote the Gaussian measure on
\mathbb{R}^{k}
of variance
s>0
by

d\gamma^{(s)}(x)=\frac{1}{(2\pi s)^{\frac{k}{2}}}e^{-|x|^{2}/2s}\,dx.

Let
f(x)=\sum_{|\alpha|\leq\mathrm{deg}(f)}c_{\alpha}H_{\alpha}(x)
, and let

\displaystyle\ell(x)=\sum_{|\alpha|\leq\deg f}c_{\alpha}x_{1}^{\alpha_{1}}\ldots x_{k}^{\alpha_{k}}

Next, define

g(x,u,s)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{k}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{k}}\ell((u+iv)+z(x+iy))\,d\gamma^{(s)}(v)\,d\gamma^{(1-s)}(y),

and consider
C(s):[0,1]\to\mathbb{R}
given by

C(s)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{k}}F\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{k}}\tilde{M}(|g(x,u,s)|^{2})\,d\gamma^{(s)}(u)\right)\,d\gamma^{(1-s)}(x).

Note that the inequality (
4.3
) can be rewritten as
C(0)\leq C(1)
. To see this, let us make a change of variables and write

g(x,u,s)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{k}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{k}}\ell((u+i\sqrt{s}v)+z(x+i\sqrt{1-s}y))\,d\gamma(v)\,d\gamma(y).

Substitute
g(x,u,s)
into the definition of
C(s)
and write

\displaystyle C(s)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{k}}F\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{k}}\tilde{M}\Big(\Big|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{k}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{k}}\ell(\sqrt{s}(u+iv)+z\sqrt{1-s}(x+iy))\,d\gamma(v)\,d\gamma(y)\Big|^{2}\Big)\,d\gamma(u)\right)\,d\gamma(x).

Notice that

\displaystyle T_{z}f(x)

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{k}}\ell(z(x+iy))\,d\gamma(y),

\displaystyle f(u)

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{k}}\ell(u+iv)\,d\gamma(v),

and so,

\displaystyle C(0)

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{k}}F\left(\tilde{M}\Big(\Big|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{k}}\ell(z(x+iy))\,d\gamma(y)\Big|^{2}\Big)\right)\,d\gamma(x)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{k}}F(\tilde{M}(|T_{z}f|^{2}))\,d\gamma,

and

\displaystyle C(1)=F\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{k}}\tilde{M}\Big(\Big|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{k}}\ell(u+iv)\,d\gamma(v)\Big|^{2}\Big)\,d\gamma(u)\right)=F\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{k}}\tilde{M}(|f|^{2})\,d\gamma\right).

We claim
C^{\prime}(s)\geq 0
. Denote by
I_{s}B
the heat flow of
B:\mathbb{R}^{k}\mapsto\mathbb{C}
evaluated at zero, namely

(4.4)

I_{s}B=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{k}}B\,d\gamma^{(s)}.

Note that by the heat equation we have

(4.5)

\frac{d}{ds}I_{s}B=\frac{1}{2}I_{s}(\Delta_{x}B).

For what follows, we need to indicate the variable in which the flow
I_{s}
is applied. For instance, in (
4.5
), we will denote it as
I_{s}^{x}
instead of
I_{s}
. With this notation we have

\displaystyle C^{\prime}(s)

\displaystyle=\frac{d}{ds}\left(I_{1-s}^{x}F(I_{s}^{u}\tilde{M}(|g(x,u,s)|^{2}))\right)

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{2}I_{1-s}^{x}\Delta_{x}[F(I_{s}^{u}\tilde{M}(|g(x,u,s)|^{2}))]+I_{1-s}^{x}\frac{d}{ds}F(I_{s}^{u}\tilde{M}(|g(x,u,s)|^{2})).

Denote

A=I_{s}^{u}\tilde{M}(|g(x,u,s)|^{2}),

and let us shortly write
g:=g(x,u,s)
. We have

\displaystyle C^{\prime}(s)=I_{1-s}^{x}\left[-\frac{1}{2}\Delta_{x}[F(A))]+F^{\prime}(A))I_{s}^{u}\left[\frac{1}{2}\Delta_{u}\tilde{M}(|g|^{2})+\frac{d}{ds}\tilde{M}(|g|^{2})\right]\right].

Set
g_{j}
to be the partial derivative of
g
with respect to
u_{j}
. This derivative indicates that we first differentiate the polynomial
\ell
in the
j
-th coordinate and then apply the flow
I_{1-s}^{y}I_{s}^{v}
. Our goal is to express the quantity
C^{\prime}(s)
in terms of
g_{j}

as much as it is possible
, because later we can treat the variables
g_{j}
as being arbitrary and it will help us to better see why
C^{\prime}(s)
has a definite sign.

We have

\displaystyle g(x,u,s)_{x_{j}}

\displaystyle=zg_{j},

\displaystyle(|g|^{2})_{u_{j}}

\displaystyle=(g\overline{g})_{u_{j}}=g_{i}\bar{g}+g\bar{g_{j}}=2\Re(g_{j}\bar{g}),

\displaystyle(|g|^{2})_{x_{j}}

\displaystyle=(g\overline{g})_{x_{j}}=g_{x_{j}}\bar{g}+g\bar{g}_{x_{j}}=zg_{j}\bar{g}+g\bar{z}\bar{g}_{j}=2\Re(zg_{j}\bar{g}),

\displaystyle(|g|^{2})_{u_{j}u_{j}}

\displaystyle=g_{jj}\overline{g}+g\overline{g_{jj}}+2|g_{j}|^{2},

\displaystyle(|g|^{2})_{x_{j}x_{j}}

\displaystyle=g_{x_{j}x_{j}}\bar{g}+g\overline{g_{x_{j}x_{j}}}+2g_{x_{j}}\bar{g}_{x_{j}}=z^{2}g_{jj}\overline{g}+g\overline{z}^{2}\overline{g_{jj}}+2|z|^{2}|g_{j}|^{2}.

We also compute,

\displaystyle\frac{d}{ds}g

\displaystyle=\frac{d}{ds}I_{1-s}^{y}I_{s}^{v}\ell((u+iv)+z(x+iy))

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{2}I_{1-s}^{y}I_{s}^{v}\Delta_{y}\ell((u+iv)+z(x+iy))+\frac{1}{2}I_{1-s}^{y}I_{s}^{v}\Delta_{v}\ell((u+iv)+z(x+iy))

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{j=1}^{k}\left(-(iz)^{2}g_{jj}+i^{2}g_{jj}\right)=\frac{z^{2}-1}{2}\sum_{j=1}^{k}g_{jj},

and therefore we have

\displaystyle\frac{d}{ds}\tilde{M}(|g|^{2})

\displaystyle=\tilde{M}^{\prime}(|g|^{2})(|g|^{2})_{s}=\tilde{M}^{\prime}(|g|^{2})\left(\bar{g}\frac{d}{ds}g+g\frac{d}{ds}\bar{g}\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{k}\tilde{M}^{\prime}(|g|^{2})\left(\frac{z^{2}-1}{2}\bar{g}g_{jj}+\frac{\bar{z}^{2}-1}{2}g\bar{g}_{jj}\right).

Thus

\displaystyle C^{\prime}(s)=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\sum_{j=1}^{k}I_{1-s}^{x}\left[-\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial x_{j}^{2}}F(A)+F^{\prime}(A)I_{s}^{u}\Big[\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial u_{j}^{2}}\tilde{M}(|g|^{2})+\tilde{M}^{\prime}(|g|^{2})[(z^{2}-1)\bar{g}g_{jj}+(\bar{z}^{2}-1)g\bar{g}_{jj}]\Big]\right].

Let us denote by
\mathscr{L}:=\mathscr{L}(x,s)
the function inside the square brackets above. We will show that
\mathscr{L}\geq 0
. We have

\displaystyle\mathscr{L}=

\displaystyle-F^{\prime\prime}(A)(I_{s}^{u}\tilde{M}^{\prime}(|g|^{2})(|g|^{2})_{x_{j}})^{2}-F^{\prime}(A)I_{s}^{u}\tilde{M}^{\prime\prime}(|g|^{2})((|g|^{2})_{x_{j}})^{2}-F^{\prime}(A)I_{s}^{u}\tilde{M}^{\prime}(|g|^{2})(|g|^{2})_{x_{j}x_{j}}

\displaystyle+F^{\prime}(A)I_{s}^{u}\big[\tilde{M}^{\prime\prime}(|g|^{2})((|g|^{2})_{u_{j}})^{2}+\tilde{M}^{\prime}(|g|^{2})[(|g|^{2})_{u_{j}u_{j}}+(z^{2}-1)\bar{g}g_{jj}+(\bar{z}^{2}-1)g\bar{g}_{jj}]\big],

so,

\displaystyle\mathscr{L}=

\displaystyle-F^{\prime\prime}(A)(I_{s}^{u}\tilde{M}^{\prime}(|g|^{2})(|g|^{2})_{x_{j}})^{2}+F^{\prime}(A)I_{s}^{u}\tilde{M}^{\prime\prime}(|g|^{2})\Big[((|g|^{2})_{u_{j}})^{2}-((|g|^{2})_{x_{i}})^{2}\Big]+

\displaystyle+F^{\prime}(A)I_{s}^{u}\tilde{M}^{\prime}(|g|^{2})\Big[(|g|^{2})_{u_{j}u_{j}}-(|g|^{2})_{x_{j}x_{j}}+(z^{2}-1)\bar{g}g_{jj}+(\bar{z}^{2}-1)g\bar{g}_{jj}\Big].

Note that

\displaystyle(|g|^{2})_{u_{j}u_{j}}-(|g|^{2})_{x_{j}x_{j}}=g_{jj}\overline{g}(1-z^{2})+g\bar{g}_{jj}(1-\bar{z}^{2})+2|g_{j}|^{2}(1-|z|^{2}),

Miraculously the second derivatives
g_{jj}
cancel each other, and we get

(4.6)

\displaystyle\mathscr{L}

\displaystyle=-4F^{\prime\prime}(A)(I_{s}^{u}\tilde{M}^{\prime}(|g|^{2})\Re(zg_{j}\overline{g}))^{2}

\displaystyle+F^{\prime}(A)I_{s}^{u}\left(4\tilde{M}^{\prime\prime}(|g|^{2})\Big[(\Re(g_{j}\overline{g}))^{2}-(\Re(zg_{j}\overline{g}))^{2}\Big]+2(1-|z|^{2})\tilde{M}^{\prime}(|g|^{2})|g_{j}|^{2}\right).

Since
F^{\prime}(A)>0
it is enough to show
\frac{\mathscr{L}}{F^{\prime}(A)}\geq 0
. Recall that
A=I_{s}^{u}\tilde{M}(|g|^{2})
,
x\mapsto\frac{F^{\prime}(x)}{F^{\prime\prime}(x)}
is concave on
(0,\infty)
, and the map
(x,y)\mapsto\frac{y^{2}}{x}
is convex on
\mathbb{R}\times(0,\infty)
. Therefore we have

\displaystyle-4\frac{F^{\prime\prime}(A)}{F^{\prime}(A)}(I_{s}^{u}\tilde{M}^{\prime}(|g|^{2})\Re(zg_{j}\overline{g}))^{2}=-4\frac{(I_{s}^{u}\tilde{M}^{\prime}(|g|^{2})\Re(zg_{j}\overline{g}))^{2}}{\frac{F^{\prime}(I_{s}^{u}\tilde{M}(|g|^{2}))}{F^{\prime\prime}(I_{s}^{u}\tilde{M}(|g|^{2}))}}

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathrm{Jensen}}}{{\geq}}-4\frac{(I_{s}^{u}\tilde{M}^{\prime}(|g|^{2})\Re(zg_{j}\overline{g}))^{2}}{I_{s}^{u}\left(\frac{F^{\prime}(\tilde{M}(|g|^{2}))}{F^{\prime\prime}(\tilde{M}(|g|^{2}))}\right)}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathrm{Jensen}}}{{\geq}}-4I_{s}^{u}\left(\frac{(\tilde{M}^{\prime}(|g|^{2})\Re(zg_{j}\overline{g}))^{2}}{\frac{F^{\prime}(\tilde{M}(|g|^{2}))}{F^{\prime\prime}(\tilde{M}(|g|^{2}))}}\right).

Remark 2
.

Notice that the inequality

\displaystyle-4\frac{F^{\prime\prime}(A)}{F^{\prime}(A)}(I_{s}^{u}\tilde{M}^{\prime}(|g|^{2})\Re(zg_{j}\overline{g}))^{2}\geq-4I_{s}^{u}\left(\frac{F^{\prime\prime}(\tilde{M}(|g|^{2}))(\tilde{M}^{\prime}(|g|^{2})\Re(zg_{j}\overline{g}))^{2}}{F^{\prime}(\tilde{M}(|g|^{2}))}\right)

also follows from Jensen’s inequality provided that

is convex on
(0,\infty)\times\mathbb{R}
.

Thus we have
\frac{\mathscr{L}}{F^{\prime}(A)}\geq 4I_{s}^{u}\Psi
, where

\displaystyle\Psi

\displaystyle=-(\tilde{M}^{\prime}(|g|^{2})\Re(zg_{j}\overline{g}))^{2}\frac{F^{\prime\prime}(\tilde{M}(|g|^{2}))}{F^{\prime}(\tilde{M}(|g|^{2}))}+\tilde{M}^{\prime\prime}(|g|^{2})\Big[(\Re(g_{j}\overline{g}))^{2}-(\Re(zg_{j}\overline{g}))^{2}\Big]

\displaystyle+\frac{1-|z|^{2}}{2}\tilde{M}^{\prime}(|g|^{2})|g_{j}|^{2}.

If
|g|=0
then
\Psi=\frac{1-|z|^{2}}{2}\tilde{M}^{\prime}(0)|g_{j}|^{2}\geq 0
. Otherwise we denote
|g|^{2}=x>0
and
w=g_{j}\overline{g}\in\mathbb{C}
then (
4.2
) implies
\Psi\geq 0
finishing the proof of
C^{\prime}(s)\geq 0
.

Recall that the condition
C^{\prime}(s)\geq 0
for
s\in(0,1)
implies
C(0)\leq C(1)
, i.e.,

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{k}}F(M(|T_{z}f|^{2}+\varepsilon))d\gamma\leq F\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{k}}M(|f|^{2}+\varepsilon)d\gamma\right)

holds for all
\varepsilon>0
. As
\varepsilon\to 0
by continuity we have
M(|f|^{2}+\varepsilon)\to M(|f|^{2})
and
F(M(|T_{z}f|^{2}+\varepsilon))\to F(M(|T_{z}f|^{2}))
everywhere, therefore, the fact that
F
and

satisfy growth condition (
4.1
) together with Lebesgue dominated convergence theorem completes the proof of Lemma

.
∎

Before we proceed to the next lemma, we would like to point out the following

Proposition 1
.

If
F\in C^{4}((0,\infty))
and
F^{\prime},F^{\prime\prime}>0
on
(0,\infty)
, then the map

(4.7)

\displaystyle t\mapsto\frac{F^{\prime}(t)}{F^{\prime\prime}(t)}\quad\text{is concave on}\quad(0,\infty)

if and only if the map

(4.8)

\displaystyle B(t,y)\mapsto\frac{y^{2}F^{\prime\prime}(t)}{F^{\prime}(t)}\quad\text{is convex on}\quad(0,\infty)\times\mathbb{R}.

Proof.

Indeed, (
4.8
) holds if and only
\mathrm{det}(\mathrm{Hess}(B))\geq 0
. We have

\displaystyle\mathrm{det}(\mathrm{Hess}(B))

\displaystyle=2y^{2}\left(\frac{F^{\prime\prime}(t)}{F^{\prime}(t)}\frac{d^{2}}{dt^{2}}\frac{F^{\prime\prime}(t)}{F^{\prime}(t)}-2\left(\frac{d}{dt}\frac{F^{\prime\prime}(t)}{F^{\prime}(t)}\right)^{2}\right)

\displaystyle\frac{2y^{2}}{(F^{(1)})^{3}}\left(F^{(1)}F^{(2)}F^{(4)}+(F^{(2)})^{2}F^{(3)}-2F^{(1)}(F^{(3)})^{2}\right),

where
F^{(j)}
denotes
j
’th derivative of
F(t)
with respect to
t
. Also notice that

\displaystyle\frac{d^{2}}{dt^{2}}\frac{F^{\prime}(t)}{F^{\prime\prime}(t)}=-\frac{1}{(F^{(2)})^{3}}(F^{(1)}F^{(2)}F^{(4)}+(F^{(2)})^{2}F^{(3)}-2F^{(1)}(F^{(3)})^{2}).

Thus the inequality
\mathrm{det}(\mathrm{Hess}(B))\geq 0
is the same as
\frac{d^{2}}{dt^{2}}\frac{F^{\prime}(t)}{F^{\prime\prime}(t)}\leq 0
finishing the proof of the proposition.
∎

Lemma 2
.

Let nonnegative
Q,P\in C^{2}((0,\infty))\cap C([0,\infty))
be such that
Q^{\prime},P^{\prime}>0
on
(0,\infty)
, and both

and

satisfy growth condition (

). If either

\displaystyle F^{\prime\prime}(x)>0\quad\text{and}\quad x\mapsto F^{\prime}(x)/F^{\prime\prime}(x)\quad\text{is concave on}\quad(0,\infty),

or
F^{\prime\prime}\equiv 0
on
(0,\infty)
, and the local condition

(4.9)

\displaystyle|wz|^{2}-(\Re\,wz)^{2}+\frac{xQ^{\prime\prime}(x)}{Q^{\prime}(x)}(\Re\,wz)^{2}\leq|w|^{2}-(\Re\,w)^{2}+\frac{xP^{\prime\prime}(x)}{P^{\prime}(x)}(\Re\,w)^{2}

, all

, and some

,

, then we have

(4.10)

\displaystyle Q^{-1}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}Q(|T_{z}f|)d\gamma\right)\leq P^{-1}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}P(|f|)d\gamma\right).

holds for all polynomials
f:\mathbb{R}^{k}\mapsto\mathbb{C}
, and all

.

Proof.

Let us refwrite (
4.10
) as follows

(4.11)

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{k}}F(M(|T_{z}f|^{2}))d\gamma\leq F\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{k}}M(|f|^{2})d\gamma\right),

where

, and

. By Lemma
1
the inequality (

and

satisfy the conditions in Lemma

. All conditions trivially hold except one needs to check the local condition (

). We claim that (

). Indeed, we have

\displaystyle M^{\prime}(x)=\frac{1}{2}\frac{P^{\prime}(\sqrt{x})}{x^{1/2}},

\displaystyle M^{\prime\prime}(x)=P^{\prime\prime}(\sqrt{x})\frac{1}{4x}-P^{\prime}(\sqrt{x})\frac{1}{4x^{3/2}},

\displaystyle F^{\prime}(x)=\frac{Q^{\prime}(P^{-1}(x))}{P^{\prime}(P^{-1}(x))},

\displaystyle\frac{F^{\prime\prime}(M(x))}{F^{\prime}(M(x))}(M^{\prime}(x))^{2}=\left(\frac{Q^{\prime\prime}(\sqrt{x})}{P^{\prime}(\sqrt{x})Q^{\prime}(\sqrt{x})}-\frac{P^{\prime\prime}(\sqrt{x})}{(P^{\prime}(\sqrt{x}))^{2}}\right)(M^{\prime}(x))^{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{4x}\left(\frac{Q^{\prime\prime}(\sqrt{x})P^{\prime}(\sqrt{x})}{Q^{\prime}(\sqrt{x})}-P^{\prime\prime}(\sqrt{x})\right),

(4.12)

Lemma 3
.

The local condition (
4.9
) holds if and only if

\displaystyle\left|\left(\frac{xQ^{\prime\prime}(x)}{Q^{\prime}(x)}-1\right)z^{2}-\left(\frac{xP^{\prime\prime}(x)}{P^{\prime}(x)}-1\right)\right|\leq\left(\frac{xP^{\prime\prime}(x)}{P^{\prime}(x)}+1\right)-\left(\frac{xQ^{\prime\prime}(x)}{Q^{\prime}(x)}+1\right)|z|^{2}

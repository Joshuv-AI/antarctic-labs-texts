Abstract
. Error estimates of cubic interpolated pseudo-particle scheme (CIP scheme) for the one-dimensional advection equation with periodic boundary conditions are presented. The CIP scheme is a semi-Lagrangian method involving the piecewise cubic Hermite interpolation. Although it is numerically known that the space-time accuracy of the scheme is third order, its rigorous proof remains an open problem. In this paper, denoting the spatial and temporal mesh sizes by h h and Δ ​ t \Delta t respectively, we prove an error estimate O ⁡ ( Δ ​ t 3 + h 4 Δ ​ t ) O(\Delta t^{3}+\frac{h^{4}}{\Delta t}) in L 2 L^{2} norm theoretically, which justifies the above-mentioned prediction if h = O ⁡ ( Δ ​ t ) h=O(\Delta t) . The proof is based on properties of the interpolation operator; the most important one is that it behaves as the L 2 L^{2} projection for the second-order derivatives. We remark that the same strategy perfectly works as well to address an error estimate for the semi-Lagrangian method with the cubic spline interpolation.

1.
Introduction

Cubic interpolated pseudo-particle scheme (CIP scheme) is a numerical method for hyperbolic partial differential equations.
The scheme was first presented by Takewaki et al.
[
17
]
,
and has been applied to various partial differential equations
including hydrodynamic equations
[
18
,
24
]
,
KdV equations
[
23
]
,
and multi-dimensional shallow water equations
[
21
]
.
Numerical results indicate that
the CIP scheme for the one-dimensional advection equation
has third-order accuracy in time and space
[
25
]
.
Since the CIP scheme is an explicit method,
it is relatively easy to implement and
it requires shorter computational time.

We briefly introduce the CIP scheme.
Let us consider the one-dimensional advection equation with a constant velocity
u

(1.1)

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\partial_{t}\varphi(x,t)+u\partial_{x}\varphi(x,t)=0,&(x,t)\in\mathbb{T}\times[0,T],\\
\varphi(x,0)=\varphi_{0}(x),&x\in\mathbb{T},\end{cases}

where
\mathbb{T}=\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z}
stands for a interval
[0,1]
with periodic boundary conditions,
while we will address more general cases in which
u
depends on
x
and
t
in this paper.
We set time steps
0=t^{0}<t^{1}<\cdots<t^{N}=T
, time step sizes
\Delta t_{n}=t^{n+1}-t^{n}

and a spatial grid
0=x_{0}<x_{1}<\cdots<x_{M}=1
.
We denote an approximation of
\varphi(\cdot,t^{n})
by
\varphi_{h}^{n}
.
The CIP scheme for (
1.1
) is described as follows:

(1.2)

\displaystyle\varphi_{h}^{0}=I_{h}^{3}\varphi_{0},

(1.3)

\displaystyle\varphi_{h}^{n+1}=I_{h}^{3}(\varphi_{h}^{n}(\cdot-u\Delta t_{n})),\quad n=0,1,\ldots,N-1,

where
I_{h}^{3}:C^{1}(\mathbb{T})\to C^{1}(\mathbb{T})
is a cubic Hermite interpolation operator defined in Subsection
3.1
. As shown in (
1.3
), the CIP scheme for the advection equation with a constant velocity is repetition of the translation
\varphi_{h}^{n}\mapsto\varphi_{h}^{n}(\cdot-u\Delta t_{n})
and the cubic Hermite interpolation
\varphi_{h}^{n}(\cdot-u\Delta t_{n})\mapsto I_{h}^{3}(\varphi_{h}^{n}(\cdot-u\Delta t_{n}))
.

We note that the CIP method above differs from the semi-Lagrangian method with cubic Hermite interpolation proposed by
[
9
]
, in the way to compute first-order derivatives of functions to be interpolated. In fact, the former obtains them by computing the differentiated form of the solution formula of the PDE, whereas the latter approximates them by a finite difference method.

Nara–Takaki
[
10
]
studied the stability of the CIP scheme for the one-dimensional advection equation with a constant velocity. Tanaka et al.
[
20
]
analyzed the stability of a scheme in which functions of discrete variables in the CIP scheme are replaced by functions of continuous variables. They proved that these schemes are not stable in
L^{2}
, while the
L^{2}
norm of the approximation of
\varphi(\cdot,t^{n})
is bounded by the
H^{1}
norm of the initial value
\varphi_{0}
. In these studies, where the von Neumann stability analysis is applied, the conditions that the spatial grid is uniform and that
u
is constant are imposed. Tanaka
[
19
]
proved convergence of the scheme with functions of continuous variables without rates. Furthermore, Besse
[
2
]
conducted convergence analysis of a semi-Lagrangian scheme using the cubic Hermite interpolation on a uniform mesh for the one-dimensional Vlasov–Poisson equation, which exploits a similar strategy to the CIP scheme for the advection equation.

The primary challenge in deriving
L^{2}
error estimates for the CIP scheme stems from the fact that the cubic Hermite interpolation operator is unbounded in
L^{2}(\mathbb{T})
. In fact, if a mesh is given, one can easily construct a function having an arbitrarily small
L^{2}
norm, whose Hermite interpolation is a non-zero constant (see Proposition
3.2
). In order to overcome this issue, we introduce a weighted
H^{2}
norm defined in Subsection
3.2
, which recovers the
L^{2}
norm in the limit where meshes are refined suitably. We see that operator
I_{h}^{3}
is stable with respect to the weighted
H^{2}
norm, which enables us to prove
L^{2}
convergence of the CIP scheme, even on non-uniform meshes. Since we obtain error bounds in the weighted
H^{2}
norm, we also have error estimates in
H^{1}
and
H^{2}
. However, they are suboptimal compared with what numerical experiments suggest.

In the context of geophysical fluid dynamics, various kinds of semi-Lagrangian methods have attracted much attention
[
5
,
11
,
16
]
. Among them, the semi-Lagrangian methods with spline interpolation is known as a less dispersive and less diffusive scheme
[
12
]
. Ferretti–Mehrenberger
[
7
]
investigated the stability of the semi-Lagrangian schemes with spline or symmetric Lagrange interpolation on uniform meshes for advection equations by regarding them as Lagrange–Galerkin schemes. In addition, the stability and convergence analyses of semi-Lagrangian schemes with spline or symmetric Lagrange interpolation for Vlasov–Poisson system are conducted in
[
1
]
. However, it seems to be non-trivial to apply their strategies on the cases with non-uniform meshes. On the other hand, our approach is applicable for non-uniform meshes. Since the cubic spline interpolation has a similar property to the cubic Hermite interpolation, we can obtain error estimates with respect to the weighted
H^{2}
norm for the scheme with spline interpolation in the same way as for the CIP scheme.

It is also known that the CIP scheme has low phase errors
[
22
]
(see
[
13
]
for a general discussion on phase errors). Although we do not provide theoretical analyses of phase errors, we examine through numerical experiments that the phase error of the CIP scheme is significantly smaller than those of semi-Lagrangian schemes with spline or symmetric Lagrange interpolation.

The paper is organized as follows. In Section
2
, we introduce the CIP scheme for the one-dimensional advection equation. In Section
3
, we examine the stability and convergence of the CIP scheme theoretically. In Section
4
, we employ the same approach to the semi-Lagrangian method with the cubic spline interpolation. In Section
5
, we show some numerical results on norm errors and phase errors.

2.
CIP methods for one-dimensional advection equations

2.1.
One-dimensional advection equations

We consider the one-dimensional advection equation

(2.1)

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\displaystyle\partial_{t}\varphi(x,t)+u(x,t)\partial_{x}\varphi(x,t)=0,&(x,t)\in\mathbb{T}\times[0,T],\\
\varphi(x,0)=\varphi_{0}(x),&x\in\mathbb{T},\end{cases}

where the velocity
u(x,t)
and the initial value
\varphi_{0}(x)
are given functions.

We assume that
u
is smooth. Let
\xi:[0,T]\times\mathbb{T}\times[0,T]\to\mathbb{T}
be the characteristic curve of (
2.1
), that is,
\xi(s;x,t)
is defined as the solution of

(2.2)

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\displaystyle\frac{d}{ds}\xi(s;x,t)=u(\xi(s;x,t),s),&s\in[0,T],\\
\xi(t;x,t)=x\end{cases}

for
(x,t)\in\mathbb{T}\times[0,T]
. By differentiating (
2.2
) with respect to
x
, we see that
\xi_{x}(s;x,t)
is the solution of

(2.3)

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\displaystyle\frac{d}{ds}\xi_{x}(s;x,t)=u_{x}(\xi(s;x,t),s)\xi_{x}(s;x,t),&s\in[0,T],\\
\xi_{x}(t;x,t)=1.\end{cases}

Therefore we have an explicit expression of
\xi_{x}
as

(2.4)

\displaystyle\xi_{x}(s;x,t)=\exp\left(\int_{t}^{s}u_{x}(\xi(\tau;x,t),\tau)d\tau\right).

We replace
t
and
x
in the first equation in (
2.1
) with
s
and
\xi(s;x,t)
respectively to obtain

(2.5)

\displaystyle\partial_{s}\varphi(\xi(s;x,t),s)+u(\xi(s;x,t),s)\cdot\partial_{x}\varphi(\xi(s;x,t),s)=0.

Combining with (
2.2
), we obtain

(2.6)

\displaystyle\partial_{s}\varphi(\xi(s;x,t),t)+\partial_{s}\xi(s;x,t)\cdot\partial_{x}\varphi(\xi(s;x,t),s)=0,

which implies

(2.7)

\displaystyle\frac{d}{ds}\left[\varphi(\xi(s;x,t),s)\right]=0.

By using the second equation of (
2.2
), we have

(2.8)

\displaystyle\varphi(x,t)

\displaystyle=\varphi(\xi(s;x,t),s)

and

(2.9)

\displaystyle\varphi_{x}(x,t)

\displaystyle=\xi_{x}(s;x,t)\cdot\varphi_{x}(\xi(s;x,t),s)

for any
x\in\mathbb{T}
and
t,s\in[0,T]
.
Let us set time steps
0=t^{0}<t^{1}<\cdots<t^{N}=T
and define
\xi^{n}\coloneqq\xi(t^{n};\cdot,t^{n+1})
.
Equations (
2.8
) and (
2.9
) imply

(2.10)

\displaystyle\varphi(x,t^{n+1})

\displaystyle=\varphi(\xi^{n}(x),t^{n})

and

(2.11)

\displaystyle\varphi_{x}(x,t^{n+1})

\displaystyle=\xi_{x}^{n}(x)\cdot\varphi_{x}(\xi^{n}(x),t^{n}),

where
\varphi^{n}=\varphi(\cdot,t^{n})
.

2.2.
CIP scheme for the advection equation

In this subsection, we introduce the CIP scheme for (
2.1
).
We set a spatial mesh
0=x_{0}<x_{1}<\cdots<x_{M}=1
. Let

(2.12)

\displaystyle\Delta t_{n}=t^{n+1}-t^{n},\quad\Delta t=\max_{0\leq n\leq N-1}\Delta t_{n},\quad\Delta t^{\prime}=\min_{0\leq n\leq N-1}\Delta t_{n},

(2.13)

\displaystyle h_{j}=x_{j+1}-x_{j},\quad h=\max_{0\leq j\leq M-1}h_{j}.

In this paper, we always suppose
\Delta t<1
.
We define the function space of piecewise cubic functions of class
C^{1}
by

(2.14)

\displaystyle V_{h}^{1,3}=\left\{v\in C^{1}(\mathbb{T})\mathrel{}\middle|\mathrel{}v|_{[x_{j},x_{j+1}]}\in\mathbb{P}_{3}([x_{j},x_{j+1}]),\quad j=0,\dots,M-1\right\},

where
\mathbb{P}_{3}([x_{j},x_{j+1}])
is the set of all polynomials of degree at most
3
on the interval
[x_{j},x_{j+1}]
.

Let
F_{j}^{n}
and
G_{j}^{n}
denote the approximations of
\varphi(t^{n},x_{j})
and
\varphi_{x}(t^{n},x_{j})
, respectively.
We are now ready to describe the concrete procedure of the CIP scheme as follows.

(1)

Discretize the initial value as

(2.15)

\displaystyle F_{j}^{0}=\varphi_{0}(x_{j}),\quad G_{j}^{0}=D\varphi_{0}(x_{j}),\quad j=0,1,\ldots,M-1,

where
D
denotes
\frac{d}{dx}
.

(2)

Assume that we have already obtained
\{F_{j}^{n}\}_{j=0}^{M-1}
and
\{G_{j}^{n}\}_{j=0}^{M-1}
for some
n\in\{0,1,\ldots,N-1\}
.
We denote the approximations of
\xi^{n}(x_{j})
and
\xi_{x}^{n}(x_{j})
by
X_{0,j}^{n}
and
X_{1,j}^{n}
respectively.
We compute (
2.2
) and (
2.3
) approximately using the third-order Runge–Kutta method as follows:

(2.16)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\bm{y}_{j}&=(x_{j},1)\\
\bm{k}_{j,1}^{n}&=\bm{u}\left(\bm{y}_{j},t^{n+1}\right),\\
\bm{k}_{j,2}^{n}&=\bm{u}\left(\bm{y}_{j}-\frac{\Delta t_{n}}{2}\bm{k}_{j,1}^{n},t^{n+1}-\frac{\Delta t_{n}}{2}\right),\\
\bm{k}_{j,3}^{n}&=\bm{u}\left(\bm{y}_{j}-\Delta t_{n}\left(-\bm{k}_{j,1}^{n}+2\bm{k}_{j,2}^{n}\right),t^{n+1}-\Delta t_{n}\right),\\
(X_{0,j}^{n},X_{1,j}^{n})&=\bm{y}_{j}-\Delta t_{n}\left(\frac{1}{6}\bm{k}_{j,1}^{n}+\frac{4}{6}\bm{k}_{j,2}^{n}+\frac{1}{6}\bm{k}_{j,3}^{n}\right),\end{split}

where
\bm{u}:\mathbb{T}\times\mathbb{R}\times[0,T]\to\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}
is defined by

(2.17)

\displaystyle\bm{u}(y_{0},y_{1},t)\coloneqq(u(y_{0},t),y_{1}u_{x}(y_{0},t)).

Here
\bm{k}_{j,1}^{n}
,
\bm{k}_{j,2}^{n}
and
\bm{k}_{j,3}^{n}
are auxiliary variables of the Runge–Kutta method.

(3)

Define a function
\varphi_{h}^{n}\in V_{h}^{1,3}
such that
\varphi_{h}^{n}(x_{j})=F_{j}^{n}
and
D\varphi_{h}^{n}(x_{j})=G_{j}^{n}
for
j=0,\dots,M-1
(i.e., the cubic Hermite interpolation). We compute (
2.10
) and (
2.11
) approximately

(2.18)

\displaystyle F_{j}^{n+1}=\varphi_{h}^{n}(X_{0,j}^{n}),

(2.19)

\displaystyle G_{j}^{n+1}=X_{1,j}^{n}\cdot D\varphi_{h}^{n}(X_{0,j}^{n})

for
j=0,1,\dots,M-1
.

3.
Mathematical analysis of the CIP scheme

3.1.
Preliminaries

In the previous section, we explained the procedure of the CIP scheme in terms of grid functions, but it is difficult to handle with this form. Therefore, as a preparation for discussions of stability and convergence, we express the CIP scheme with the help of functions on
\mathbb{T}
instead of the discrete functions on the grids.

We introduce the continuous version of (
2.16
). For
n=0,1,\ldots,N-1
, let
X^{n}:\mathbb{T}\to\mathbb{T}
be defined by

(3.1)

\displaystyle\begin{split}k_{1}^{n}(x)&=u(x,t^{n+1}),\\
k_{2}^{n}(x)&=u\left(x-\frac{\Delta t_{n}}{2}k_{1}^{n}(x),t^{n+1}-\frac{\Delta t_{n}}{2}\right),\\
k_{3}^{n}(x)&=u\left(x-\Delta t_{n}\left(-k_{1}^{n}(x)+2k_{2}^{n}(x)\right),t^{n+1}-\Delta t_{n}\right),\\
X^{n}(x)&=x-\Delta t_{n}\left(\frac{1}{6}k_{1}^{n}(x)+\frac{4}{6}k_{2}^{n}(x)+\frac{1}{6}k_{3}^{n}(x)\right).\end{split}

Moreover, we define the cubic Hermite interpolation operator
I_{h}^{3}:C^{1}(\mathbb{T})\rightarrow V_{h}^{1,3}
by

(3.2)

\displaystyle(I_{h}^{3}g)(x_{j})=g(x_{j}),\quad D(I_{h}^{3}g)(x_{j})=g_{x}(x_{j}),\quad j=0,\dots,M-1.

Then, as presented in the following lemma, we can equivalently rewrite the CIP scheme, originally formulated in terms of grid-based functions in Section
2
, as a standard function-based relation.

Lemma 3.1
.

The numerical solutions
\{\varphi_{h}^{n}\}_{n=0}^{N}
defined in Subsection
2.2
satisfy

(3.3)

\displaystyle\varphi_{h}^{0}=I_{h}^{3}\varphi_{0},

(3.4)

\displaystyle\varphi_{h}^{n+1}=I_{h}^{3}\left(\varphi_{h}^{n}\circ X^{n}\right)

for
n=0,1,\ldots,N-1
.

Proof.

Equation (
3.3
) is obvious. To prove (
3.4
), it is enough to show that for any
n=0,1,\ldots,N-1
and
j=0,1,\ldots,M-1

(3.5)

\displaystyle\varphi_{h}^{n+1}(x_{j})=\varphi_{h}^{n}(X^{n}(x_{j})),

(3.6)

\displaystyle D\varphi_{h}^{n+1}(x_{j})=\left.\frac{d}{dx}\varphi_{h}^{n}(X^{n}(x))\right|_{x=x_{j}}.

We define vector-valued functions
\bm{k}_{p}^{n}(x)=(k_{p}^{n}(x),Dk_{p}^{n}(x))
for
p=1,2,3
and
\bm{y}(x)=(x,1)
, where
k_{p}^{n}
is defined in (
3.1
).
Then we have

(3.7)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\bm{k}_{1}^{n}(x)&=\bm{u}(\bm{y}(x),t^{n+1}),\\
\bm{k}_{2}^{n}(x)&=\bm{u}\left(\bm{y}(x)-\frac{\Delta t_{n}}{2}\bm{k}_{1}^{n}(x),t^{n+1}-\frac{\Delta t_{n}}{2}\right),\\
\bm{k}_{3}^{n}(x)&=\bm{u}\left(\bm{y}(x)-\Delta t_{n}\left(-\bm{k}_{1}^{n}(x)+2\bm{k}_{2}^{n}(x)\right),t^{n+1}-\Delta t_{n}\right),\\
(X^{n}(x),X_{x}^{n}(x))&=\bm{y}(x)-\Delta t_{n}\left(\frac{1}{6}\bm{k}_{1}^{n}(x)+\frac{4}{6}\bm{k}_{2}^{n}(x)+\frac{1}{6}\bm{k}_{3}^{n}(x)\right),\end{split}

where
\bm{u}
is defined by (
2.17
).
Here the first components of (
3.7
) are identical to (
3.1
), and the second components are the first-order derivatives of (
3.1
).
We substitute
x=x_{j}
in (
3.7
) and then compare (
2.16
) and (
3.7
) to obtain

(3.8)

\displaystyle X^{n}(x_{j})=X_{0,j}^{n},

(3.9)

\displaystyle X_{x}^{n}(x_{j})=X_{1,j}^{n}.

From (
2.18
) and (
3.8
), we have (
3.5
).
Furthermore, from (
3.8
) and (
3.9
), we have

(3.10)

\displaystyle\left.\frac{d}{dx}\varphi_{h}^{n}(X^{n}(x))\right|_{x=x_{j}}

\displaystyle=X_{x}^{n}(x_{j})\cdot(D\varphi_{h}^{n})(X^{n}(x_{j}))

(3.11)

\displaystyle=X_{1,j}^{n}\cdot(D\varphi_{h}^{n})(X_{0,j}^{n}).

Combining with (
2.18
), we obtain (
3.6
).
∎

3.2.
Stability analysis

This subsection is devoted to discussion about the stability of the CIP scheme described in Lemma
3.1
.

We use the Lebesgue space
L^{p}(\mathbb{T})
, the Sobolev space
H^{m}(\mathbb{T})
and
W^{m,\infty}(\mathbb{T})
, and denote their norms by
\left\|\cdot\right\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{T})}
,
\left\|\cdot\right\|_{H^{m}(\mathbb{T})}
and
\left\|\cdot\right\|_{W^{m,\infty}(\mathbb{T})}
respectively. We use the semi-norm
\left|\cdot\right|_{H^{m}(\mathbb{T})}
defined by

(3.12)

\displaystyle\left|g\right|_{H^{m}(\mathbb{T})}=\left\|D^{m}g\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T})},\quad g\in H^{m}(\mathbb{T}).

As we already mentioned in Section
1
, one of the reason why stability analysis of the CIP scheme is difficult is that the cubic Hermite interpolation operator is unstable in
L^{p}
(
1<p<\infty
) in the sense of the next proposition.

Proposition 3.2
.

Suppose that the spatial grid
\{x_{j}\}_{j=0}^{M-1}
is fixed. For
p\in(1,\infty)
, the cubic Hermite interpolation operator
I_{h}^{3}
is not bounded in
L^{p}(\mathbb{T})
, that is,

(3.13)

\displaystyle\sup_{v\in C^{1}(\mathbb{T})}\frac{\left\|I_{h}^{3}v\right\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{T})}}{\left\|v\right\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{T})}}=+\infty.

Proof.

Let
v\in C^{1}(\mathbb{T})
satisfy

(3.14)

\displaystyle v(x_{j})=1,\quad Dv(x_{j})=0\quad(j=0,\ldots,M-1),

(3.15)

\displaystyle 0\leq v(x)<1\quad(x\not\in\{x_{0},x_{1}\ldots,x_{M-1}\}),

and
v_{n}(x)=v(x)^{n}
for
n\in\{1,2,\ldots\}
.
Since
v_{n}(x_{j})=1
and
Dv_{n}(x_{j})=0
for any
j
, we have
I_{h}^{3}v_{n}\equiv 1
and
\left\|I_{h}^{3}v_{n}\right\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{T})}=1
for any
n
.
On the other hand, since
v_{n}\to 0
almost everywhere on
\mathbb{T}
and
|v_{n}(x)|\leq 1
for
x\in\mathbb{T}
, we have
\left\|v_{n}\right\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{T})}\to 0
as
n\to\infty
by the bounded convergence theorem.
Therefore we obtain (
3.13
).
∎

Thus, we introduce another norm depending on the mesh size

(3.16)

\displaystyle\left\|g\right\|_{H_{h,\Delta t}^{2}(\mathbb{T})}=\left(\left\|g\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T})}^{2}+\frac{h^{4}}{\Delta t}\left|g\right|_{H^{2}(\mathbb{T})}^{2}\right)^{1/2},

which we call weighted
H^{2}
norm in this paper.
As we will see in Lemma
3.6
, the cubic Hermite interpolation operator is stable with respect to the weighted
H^{2}
norm.

Now we prepare two assumptions:

Assumption 1
.

There exists a positive constant
C_{1}
such that
h^{4}\leq C_{1}\Delta t
.

Assumption 2
.

The temporal mesh is quasi-uniform, that is, there exists a positive constant
C_{2}
such that
\Delta t\leq C_{2}\Delta t^{\prime}
. Here we recall that
\Delta t=\max_{0\leq n\leq N-1}\Delta t_{n}
and
\Delta t^{\prime}=\min_{0\leq n\leq N-1}\Delta t_{n}
.

Our aim in this subsection is to prove the following lemma concerning the stability of the CIP scheme, whose proof will be addressed in the end of this subsection.

Lemma 3.3
.

Suppose that
u_{xx}\in C(\mathbb{T})
. Under Assumptions
1
and
2
, the CIP scheme is stable with respect to the weighted
H^{2}
norm, that is, there exists a positive constant
C
such that for any
g\in H^{2}(\mathbb{T})

(3.17)

\displaystyle\left\|I_{h}^{3}(g\circ X^{n})\right\|_{H_{h,\Delta t}^{2}(\mathbb{T})}\leq(1+C\Delta t_{n})\left\|g\right\|_{H_{h,\Delta t}^{2}(\mathbb{T})}

when
\Delta t
is sufficiently small.

We remark that a numerical solution
\varphi_{h}^{n}
is in
H^{2}(\mathbb{T})
since
V_{h}^{1,3}\subset H^{2}(T)
.

The time evolution of the CIP scheme in
[t^{n},t^{n+1}]
consists of two mappings from
H^{2}(\mathbb{T})
to
H^{2}(\mathbb{T})
as follows:

(3.18)

\displaystyle\varphi_{h}^{n}\mapsto\varphi_{h}^{n}\circ X^{n}\mapsto I_{h}^{3}\left(\varphi_{h}^{n}\circ X^{n}\right)=\varphi_{h}^{n+1}.

In what follows, we prove stabilities with respect to the weighted
H^{2}
norm of these two steps. Before we consider stability of the cubic Hermite interpolation operator, we recall some of its properties.

In the next lemma, the error estimate of the cubic Hermite interpolation is provided.

Lemma 3.4

(Theorem 2 of
[
3
]
, Corollary 3.1 of
[
8
]
)
.

There exists a positive constant
C
such that

(3.19)

\displaystyle\left\|g-I_{h}^{3}g\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T})}

\displaystyle\leq Ch^{4}\left|g\right|_{H^{4}(\mathbb{T})},

\displaystyle g

\displaystyle\in H^{4}(\mathbb{T}),

(3.20)

\displaystyle\left|g-I_{h}^{3}g\right|_{H^{2}(\mathbb{T})}

\displaystyle\leq Ch^{2}\left|g\right|_{H^{4}(\mathbb{T})},

\displaystyle g

\displaystyle\in H^{4}(\mathbb{T}),

(3.21)

\displaystyle\left\|g-I_{h}^{3}g\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T})}

\displaystyle\leq Ch^{2}\left|g-I_{h}^{3}g\right|_{H^{2}(\mathbb{T})},

\displaystyle g

\displaystyle\in H^{2}(\mathbb{T}).

The following lemma states that the cubic Hermite interpolation operator behaves as the
L^{2}
projection for second-order derivatives.

Lemma 3.5

(Lemma 3.2 of
[
8
]
)
.

For any
f,g\in H^{2}(\mathbb{T})
, the following equality holds:

(3.22)

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{T}}D^{2}\left(f(x)-I_{h}^{3}f(x)\right)\cdot D^{2}\left(I_{h}^{3}g\right)(x)dx=0,

which implies

(3.23)

\displaystyle\left|f-I_{h}^{3}f+I_{h}^{3}g\right|_{H^{2}(\mathbb{T})}^{2}=\left|f-I_{h}^{3}f\right|_{H^{2}(\mathbb{T})}^{2}+\left|I_{h}^{3}g\right|_{S^{2}(\mathbb{T})}^{2}.

Using these two lemmas, we can deduce the stability of the second mapping in (
3.18
), namely, the stability of the cubic Hermite interpolation operator with respect to the weighted
H^{2}
norm.

Lemma 3.6
.

There exists a positive constant
C
such that

(3.24)

\displaystyle\left\|I_{h}^{3}g\right\|_{H_{h,\Delta t}^{2}(\mathbb{T})}\leq(1+C\Delta t)\left\|g\right\|_{H_{h,\Delta t}^{2}(\mathbb{T})},\quad g\in H^{2}(\mathbb{T}).

Furthermore, under Assumption
2
, there exists a positive constant
C
such that

(3.25)

\displaystyle\left\|I_{h}^{3}g\right\|_{H_{h,\Delta t}^{2}(\mathbb{T})}\leq(1+C\Delta t_{n})\left\|g\right\|_{H_{h,\Delta t}^{2}(\mathbb{T})},\quad g\in H^{2}(\mathbb{T}).

Proof.

The triangle inequality and the inequality of arithmetic and geometric means give

(3.26)

\displaystyle\left(a+b\right)^{2}\leq(1+d)a^{2}+\left(1+\frac{1}{d}\right)b^{2},\quad a,b\in\mathbb{R},\quad d>0.

By the definition of the weighted
H^{2}
norm and (
3.26
), we have

(3.27)

\displaystyle\left\|I_{h}^{3}g\right\|_{H_{h,\Delta t}^{2}(\mathbb{T})}^{2}

\displaystyle=\left\|g+(I_{h}^{3}g-g)\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T})}^{2}+\frac{h^{4}}{\Delta t}\left|I_{h}^{3}g\right|_{H^{2}(\mathbb{T})}^{2}

(3.28)

\displaystyle\leq(1+\Delta t)\left\|g\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T})}^{2}+\left(1+\frac{1}{\Delta t}\right)\left\|I_{h}^{3}g-g\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T})}^{2}+\frac{h^{4}}{\Delta t}\left|I_{h}^{3}g\right|_{H^{2}(\mathbb{T})}^{2}.

Using (
3.21
), we have

(3.29)

\displaystyle\left\|I_{h}^{3}g\right\|_{H_{h,\Delta t}^{2}(\mathbb{T})}^{2}

(3.30)

\displaystyle\leq(1+\Delta t)\left\|I_{h}^{3}g\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T})}^{2}+h^{4}\left(1+\frac{1}{\Delta t}\right)\left|I_{h}^{3}g-g\right|_{H^{2}(\mathbb{T})}^{2}+\frac{h^{4}}{\Delta t}\left|I_{h}^{3}g\right|_{H^{2}(\mathbb{T})}^{2}

(3.31)

\displaystyle\leq(1+\Delta t)\left\|I_{h}^{3}g\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T})}^{2}+h^{4}\left(1+\frac{1}{\Delta t}\right)\left(\left|I_{h}^{3}g-g\right|_{H^{2}(\mathbb{T})}^{2}+\left|I_{h}^{3}g\right|_{H^{2}(\mathbb{T})}^{2}\right).

Using (
3.23
), we have

(3.32)

\displaystyle\left\|I_{h}^{3}g\right\|_{H_{h,\Delta t}^{2}(\mathbb{T})}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq(1+\Delta t)\left\|I_{h}^{3}g\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T})}^{2}+(1+\Delta t)\frac{h^{4}}{\Delta t}\left|g\right|_{H^{2}(\mathbb{T})}^{2}

If we suppose the time steps are quasi-uniform, (
3.25
) follows from (
3.24
).
∎

Lemma 3.7
.

If
u_{xx}\in C(\mathbb{T}\times[0,T])

We will give the proof of (

Let

\displaystyle\tilde{\bm{u}}\left(\bm{\xi},s\right)=(u(\xi_{0},s),\xi_{1}u_{x}(\xi_{0},s),(\xi_{1})^{2}u_{xx}(\xi_{0},s)+\xi_{2}u_{x}(\xi_{0},s)),

(3.37)

\displaystyle\tilde{\bm{k}}_{1}^{n}(x)

Lemma 3.8
.

If
u_{xx}\in C(\mathbb{T}\times[0,T])
, there exists a positive constant
C

\displaystyle{\frac{1}{2}\leq}\frac{1}{1+C\Delta t_{n}}\leq\left|\xi^{n}\right|_{W^{1,\infty}(\mathbb{T})}\leq 1+C\Delta t_{n}\leq\frac{3}{2},

(3.43)

\displaystyle\left|\xi^{n}\right|_{W^{2,\infty}(\mathbb{T})}\leq C\Delta t_{n}

is sufficiently small.

Proof.

By (
2.4

(3.44)

\displaystyle\exp\left(-\left\|u_{x}\right\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}\times[0,T])}\Delta t_{n}\right)\leq\xi_{x}^{n}(x)\leq\exp\left(\left\|u_{x}\right\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}\times[0,T])}\Delta t_{n}\right),

from which (

Differentiate (

(3.45)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\xi_{xx}(s;x,t)&=\xi_{x}(s;x,t)\int_{t}^{s}u_{xx}(\xi(\tau;x,t),\tau)\xi_{x}(\tau;x,t)d\tau,\end{split}

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\xi_{xxx}(s;x,t)\\

\displaystyle|\xi_{xx}^{n}(x)|\leq\left\|\xi_{x}\right\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T})}^{2}\left\|u_{xx}\right\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}\times[0,T])}\Delta t_{n},

∎

u_{xx}\in C(\mathbb{T}\times[0,T])

3.50

Lemma 3.10
.

If
u_{xx}\in C(\mathbb{T}\times[0,T])
and Assumption
1
hold, there exists a positive constant
C
such that for

(3.53)

\displaystyle\left\|g\circ X^{n}\right\|_{H_{h,\Delta t}^{2}(\mathbb{T})}\leq(1+C\Delta t_{n})\left\|g\right\|_{H_{h,\Delta t}^{2}(\mathbb{T})}

Proof.

Firstly, we consider
\left\|g\circ X^{n}\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T})}
. From (
3.49
), we see that
X^{n}:\mathbb{T}\to\mathbb{T}
is a bijection. By a change of variables

(3.54)

\displaystyle\left\|g\circ X^{n}\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T})}^{2}

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{T}}g(X^{n}(x))^{2}dx

(3.55)

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\mathbb{T}}\frac{g(y)^{2}}{X_{x}^{n}\circ(X^{n})^{-1}(y)}dy.

3.49

(3.56)

\displaystyle\left\|g\circ X^{n}\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T})}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq(1+C\Delta t_{n})\int_{\mathbb{T}}g(y)^{2}dy

\displaystyle=(1+C\Delta t_{n})\left\|g\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T})}^{2}.

Next, we consider
\left|\left(g\circ X^{n}\right)\right|_{H^{2}(\mathbb{T})}

By Lemma
3.9

(3.58)

\displaystyle\left|g\circ X^{n}\right|_{H^{2}(\mathbb{T})}

\displaystyle=\left\|X_{xx}^{n}\cdot g_{x}\circ X^{n}+(X_{x}^{n})^{2}\cdot g_{xx}\circ X^{n}\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T})}

\displaystyle\leq\left\|X_{xx}^{n}\right\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T})}\left\|g_{x}\circ X^{n}\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T})}+\left\|X_{x}^{n}\right\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T})}^{2}\left\|g_{xx}\circ X^{n}\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T})}

\displaystyle\leq C\Delta t_{n}\left\|(g_{x})\circ X^{n}\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T})}+(1+C\Delta t_{n})\left\|(g_{xx})\circ X^{n}\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T})}.

Applying (
3.26
) to (

(3.61)

\displaystyle\left|g\circ X^{n}\right|_{H^{2}(\mathbb{T})}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\left(1+\frac{1}{\Delta t_{n}}\right)\cdot(C\Delta t_{n})^{2}\left\|g_{x}\circ X^{n}\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T})}^{2}

(3.62)

\displaystyle\quad+\left(1+\Delta t_{n}\right)\cdot(1+C\Delta t_{n})^{2}\left\|g_{xx}\circ X^{n}\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T})}^{2}

(3.63)

\displaystyle\leq C\Delta t_{n}\left\|g_{x}\circ X^{n}\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T})}^{2}+(1+C\Delta t_{n})\left\|g_{xx}\circ X^{n}\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T})}^{2}.

Using (
3.57
), we have

(3.64)

\displaystyle\left|g\circ X^{n}\right|_{H^{2}(\mathbb{T})}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq C\Delta t_{n}\left|g\right|_{H^{1}(\mathbb{T})}^{2}+(1+C\Delta t_{n})\left|g\right|_{H^{2}(\mathbb{T})}^{2}.

(3.65)

\displaystyle\left\|g_{x}\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T})}^{2}

\displaystyle=-\int_{\mathbb{T}}g(x)\cdot g_{xx}(x)dx\leq\frac{1}{2}\left(\left\|g\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T})}^{2}+\left|g\right|_{H^{2}(\mathbb{T})}^{2}\right).

(3.66)

\displaystyle\left|g\circ X^{n}\right|_{H^{2}(\mathbb{T})}^{2}\leq C\Delta t_{n}\left\|g\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T})}^{2}+(1+C\Delta t_{n})\left|g\right|_{H^{2}(\mathbb{T})}^{2}.

Combining (

) and (

(3.67)

\displaystyle\left\|g\circ X^{n}\right\|_{H_{h,\Delta t}^{2}(\mathbb{T})}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\left(1+C(\Delta t_{n}+h^{4})\right)\left\|g\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T})}^{2}+\frac{h^{4}}{\Delta t}(1+C\Delta t_{n})\left|g\right|_{H^{2}(\mathbb{T})}^{2}.

1
, we have

(3.68)

\displaystyle\left\|g\circ X^{n}\right\|_{H_{h,\Delta t}^{2}(\mathbb{T})}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq(1+C\Delta t_{n})\left(\left\|g\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T})}^{2}+\frac{h^{4}}{\Delta t}\left|g\right|_{H^{2}(\mathbb{T})}^{2}\right)

(3.69)

\displaystyle=(1+C\Delta t_{n})\left\|g\right\|_{H_{h,\Delta t}^{2}(\mathbb{T})}^{2},

which implies the desired inequality.

3.3.
Convergence analysis

For
n_{1},n_{2}\in\{0,1,\ldots,N\}
,
We define
\left\|\cdot\right\|_{l^{2}(n_{1},n_{2};H^{4})}
by

(3.70)

\displaystyle\left\|g\right\|_{l^{2}(n_{1},n_{2};H^{4})}\coloneqq\left(\sum_{n=n_{1}}^{n_{2}}\Delta t_{n}\|g(\cdot,t^{n})\|_{H^{4}(\mathbb{T})}^{2}\right)^{1/2}

for

, and denote
\left\|\cdot\right\|_{l^{2}(0,N;H^{4}(\mathbb{T}))}
by
\left\|\cdot\right\|_{l^{2}(H^{4})}
.

Assumption 3
.

\varphi\in C([0,T];H^{4}(\mathbb{T}))
.

We give one of the main results of this paper, which states that the CIP scheme has third-order accuracy in time and space when
h=O(\Delta t)
.

Theorem 3.11
.

Suppose
u_{xx}\in C^{3}(\mathbb{T})
. Under Assumptions
1
,
2
and
3
, there exists a positive constant
C
depending on
T
and
u

such that

(3.71)

\displaystyle\left\|\varphi^{n}-\varphi_{h}^{n}\right\|_{H_{h,\Delta t}^{2}(\mathbb{T})}\leq C\left(\Delta t^{3}+\frac{h^{4}}{\Delta t}\right){\left\|\varphi\right\|_{l^{2}(H^{4})}},\quad n=0,1,\ldots,N

when
\Delta t
is sufficiently small.

In order to prove Theorem
3.11
, we show some lemmas which are necessary for estimates of truncation errors.

Let us estimate errors between a function composed with
\xi^{n}
and one composed with
X^{n}
. For a related result considering the Dirichlet boundary condition, see Theorem 3.1 of
[

]

Lemma 3.12
.

Suppose
u_{xx}\in C^{3}(\mathbb{T})
. there exists a positive constant
C
such that for any
g\in H^{3}(\mathbb{T})

(3.72)

\displaystyle\left\|g\circ\xi^{n}-g\circ X^{n}\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T})}\leq C\Delta t_{n}^{4}\left\|g\right\|_{H^{1}(\mathbb{T})},

(3.73)

\displaystyle\left|g\circ\xi^{n}-g\circ X^{n}\right|_{H^{1}(\mathbb{T})}\leq C\Delta t_{n}^{4}\left(\left|g\right|_{H^{1}(\mathbb{T})}+\left|g\right|_{H^{2}(\mathbb{T})}\right),

(3.74)

\displaystyle\left|g\circ\xi^{n}-g\circ X^{n}\right|_{H^{2}(\mathbb{T})}\leq C\Delta t_{n}^{4}\left(\left|g\right|_{H^{1}(\mathbb{T})}+\left|g\right|_{H^{2}(\mathbb{T})}+\left|g\right|_{H^{3}(\mathbb{T})}\right)

for
n=0,1,\ldots,N
when
\Delta t
is sufficiently small.

Proof.

Firstly, we evaluate
\left\|g\circ\xi^{n}-g\circ X^{n}\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T})}
.
By the fundamental theorem of calculus, we have

(3.75)

\displaystyle\left\|g\circ\xi^{n}-g\circ X^{n}\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T})}^{2}

(3.76)

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{T}}\left|g(\xi^{n}(x))-g(\xi^{n}(x)-X^{n}(x)+X^{n}(x))\right|^{2}dx

(3.77)

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{T}}\left|\int_{0}^{X^{n}(x)-\xi^{n}(x)}g_{x}(\xi^{n}(x)+y)dy\right|^{2}dx

(3.78)

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{T}}\left|\int_{0}^{1}(X^{n}(x)-\xi^{n}(x))g_{x}(\xi^{n}(x)+(X^{n}(x)-\xi^{n}(x))z)dz\right|^{2}dx

(3.79)

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{T}}\left|(X^{n}(x)-\xi^{n}(x))\right|^{2}\left|\int_{0}^{1}g_{x}(\xi^{n}(x)+(X^{n}(x)-\xi^{n}(x))z)dz\right|^{2}dx.

The Cauchy–Schwarz inequality gives

(3.80)

\displaystyle\left\|g\circ\xi^{n}-g\circ X^{n}\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T})}^{2}

(3.81)

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\mathbb{T}}\Big|(X^{n}(x)-\xi^{n}(x))\Big|^{2}\int_{0}^{1}\Big|g_{x}(\xi^{n}(x)+(X^{n}(x)-\xi^{n}(x))z)\Big|^{2}dzdx.

Using (
3.35
), we have

(3.82)

\displaystyle\left\|g\circ\xi^{n}-g\circ X^{n}\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T})}^{2}

(3.83)

\displaystyle\leq C\Delta t^{8}\int_{0}^{1}\int_{\mathbb{T}}\Big|g_{x}(\xi^{n}(x)+(X^{n}(x)-\xi^{n}(x))z)\Big|^{2}dxdz.

Let
f(x;z)\coloneqq\xi^{n}(x)+(X^{n}(x)-\xi^{n}(x))z
. By (
3.42
) and (
3.49
), for fixed
z\in[0,1]
,
f(\cdot;z):\mathbb{T}\to\mathbb{T}
is a bijection and
\frac{1}{2}\leq\partial_{x}f(x;z)\leq\frac{3}{2}
. Therefore, by a change of variables
x\mapsto w=f(x;z)
, (
3.83
) becomes

(3.84)

\displaystyle\left\|g\circ\xi^{n}-g\circ X^{n}\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T})}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq C\Delta t^{8}\int_{0}^{1}\int_{\mathbb{T}}2\Big|g_{x}(w)\Big|^{2}dwdz

(3.85)

\displaystyle=C\Delta t^{8}\left\|g_{x}\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T})}^{2},

which implies (
3.72
).

Secondly, we estimate
\left|g\circ\xi^{n}-g\circ X^{n}\right|_{H^{1}(\mathbb{T})}
.
By the triangle inequality, we have

(3.86)

\displaystyle\left|g\circ\xi^{n}-g\circ X^{n}\right|_{H^{1}(\mathbb{T})}

(3.87)

\displaystyle=\left\|g_{x}\circ\xi^{n}\cdot\xi_{x}^{n}-g_{x}\circ X^{n}\cdot X_{x}^{n}\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T})}

(3.88)

\displaystyle\leq\left\|g_{x}\circ\xi^{n}\cdot\xi_{x}^{n}-g_{x}\circ X^{n}\cdot\xi_{x}^{n}\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T})}+\left\|g_{x}\circ X^{n}\cdot\xi_{x}^{n}-g_{x}\circ X^{n}\cdot X_{x}^{n}\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T})}

(3.89)

\displaystyle=\left\|g_{x}\circ\xi^{n}-g_{x}\circ X^{n}\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T})}\left\|\xi_{x}^{n}\right\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T})}+\left\|g_{x}\circ X^{n}\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T})}\left\|\xi_{x}^{n}-X_{x}^{n}\right\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T})}.

By (
3.34
), (
3.42
), (
3.57
) and (
3.72
), we have

(3.90)

\displaystyle\left|g\circ\xi^{n}-g\circ X^{n}\right|_{H^{1}(\mathbb{T})}\leq C\Delta t_{n}^{4}\left|g\right|_{H^{2}(\mathbb{T})}+C\Delta t_{n}^{4}\left|g\right|_{H^{1}(\mathbb{T})}.

Therefore, we obtain (
3.73
).

Lastly, we estimate
\left|g\circ\xi^{n}-g\circ X^{n}\right|_{H^{2}(\mathbb{T})}
. The triangle inequality gives

(3.91)

\displaystyle\left|g\circ\xi^{n}-g\circ X^{n}\right|_{H^{2}(\mathbb{T})}

(3.92)

\displaystyle\leq\left\|D(g_{x}\circ\xi^{n}\cdot\xi_{x}^{n}-g_{x}\circ X^{n}\cdot\xi_{x}^{n})\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T})}

(3.93)

\displaystyle\quad+\left\|D(g_{x}\circ X^{n}\cdot\xi_{x}^{n}-g_{x}\circ X^{n}\cdot X_{x}^{n})\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T})}.

The first term in the right-hand side of (
3.93
) is evaluated as

(3.94)

\displaystyle\left\|D(g_{x}\circ\xi^{n}\cdot\xi_{x}^{n}-g_{x}\circ X^{n}\cdot\xi_{x}^{n})\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T})}

(3.95)

\displaystyle\leq\left|g_{x}\circ\xi^{n}-g_{x}\circ X^{n}\right|_{H^{1}(\mathbb{T})}\left|\xi^{n}\right|_{W^{1,\infty}(\mathbb{T})}+\left\|g_{x}\circ\xi^{n}-g_{x}\circ X^{n}\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T})}\left|\xi^{n}\right|_{W^{2,\infty}(\mathbb{T})}.

By Lemma
3.8
, (
3.72
) and (
3.73
),

4.1.

We introduce a semi-Lagrangian method with the cubic spline interpolation

Let

5.2.
Phase errors

In this subsection, we demonstrate that the CIP scheme has a smaller phase error than other finite difference schemes.

For the one-dimensional advection equation with a constant velocity (
1.1
), we consider one-step methods with a uniform grid
x_{j}=j/M
(
0\leq j\leq M-1
) and
t^{n}=nT/N
(
0\leq n\leq N
) for positive integers
M
and
N
. Suppose that the numerical approximation of
\varphi(t^{n})
is denoted by
\varphi_{h}^{n}
.

The discrete Fourier transform of a grid function
v=\{v_{j}\}_{j=0}^{M-1}
is defined by

(5.4)

\displaystyle\mathscr{F}(v)[k]=\sum_{j=0}^{M-1}v_{j}\exp(-2\pi ikx_{j})

for
k\in\mathbb{Z}
, where we denote the imaginary unit by
i
.

Definition 5.1
.

(phase shift)
For
k\in\mathbb{N}
, Let
\varphi_{h}^{1}
denote the numerical approximation of
\varphi(\Delta t)
with the initial value
\varphi_{0}(x)=\exp(2\pi kx)
.
Then, we define the phase shift by
\theta_{k}=-\arg\left(\mathscr{F}(\varphi_{h}^{1})[k]\right)
.

We remark that the phase shift of the exact solution is
2\pi\mu kh
, where
\mu=\frac{u\Delta t}{h}
is the CFL number (cf. Section 3 of
[
13
]
).

In Figure
1
, we show the phase shift of the CIP scheme, the semi-Lagrangian scheme with spline interpolation, the semi-Lagrangian scheme with symmetric Lagrange interpolation
[
6
]
, and the first-order upwind scheme.
The error of phase shift in the CIP scheme is significantly smaller than that of other schemes, especially when
kh
is close to
1/2
.

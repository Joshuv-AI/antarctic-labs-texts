Abstract
. We study the generic volume rigidity of ( d − 1 ) (d-1) -dimensional simplicial complexes in ℝ d − 1 \mathbb{R}^{d-1} , and show that the volume rigidity of a complex can be identified in terms of its exterior shifting. In addition, we establish the volume rigidity of triangulations of several 2 2 -dimensional surfaces and prove that, in all dimensions > 1 >1 , volume rigidity is not characterized by a corresponding hypergraph sparsity property.

Introduction

Let

n

(d-1)

It is easy to show that the continuous and infinitesimal versions coincide for generic embeddings, as is the case for graph rigidity

Volume Rigidity.

The signed volume of a
(d-1)
-face
\sigma=\{v_{1},\dots,v_{d}\}\in K
with respect to
\mathbf{p}
is given by the determinant of the
d\times d
matrix

M_{\mathbf{p},\sigma}=\begin{pmatrix}\mathbf{p}(v_{1})&\dots&\mathbf{p}(v_{d})\\
1&\dots&1\end{pmatrix}.

Observe that , for every
1\leq i\leq d-1,~1\leq j\leq d
, the derivative of the signed volume
\det M_{\mathbf{p},\sigma}
with respect to the
i
-th coordinate of
\mathbf{p}(v_{j})
is given by the cofactor
C_{i,j}(M_{\mathbf{p},\sigma})
— that is, the determinant of the submatrix obtained by removing the
i
-th row and
j
-th column multiplied by
(-1)^{i+j}
.

The

of the pair

is a

(d-1)
-faces of

(d-1)
rows. The column vector

corresponding to a
(d-1)
-face

is defined by

(\mathbf{v}_{\sigma})_{v_{i},j}=C_{i,j}(M_{\mathbf{p},\sigma})~,~i\in[d],~j\in[d-1],

and

elsewhere. Here
(\mathbf{v}_{\sigma})_{v_{i},j}
denotes the

-th coordinate of
\mathbf{v}_{\sigma}
in the block of
v_{i}
. In words,
\mathfrak{V}
is the Jacobian of the function
\mathbf{p}\mapsto(\det M_{\mathbf{p},\sigma})_{\sigma\in K}
, viewing
\mathbf{p}
as a
(d-1)n
-dimensional vector.

This matrix was introduced in
[
LP22
, Appendix A]
along with the description of a

(d^{2}-d-1)
-subspace of the left kernel of

, arising from the volume-preserving transformations of

Definition 1.1

An

for a generic

Our main result is a characterization of volume rigidity in the setting of Kalai’s exterior shifting.

Theorem 1.2

Fix

In the

Every triangulation of the

Basic properties of

We start by briefly exploring some useful properties of the complex

Given a partially ordered set (poset)

Claim 2.1
.

Let
(P,<)
be a poset and

of

P_{<_{l},x}=P_{<,x}

Proof.

View the sets

as posets with the partial order induced by

are smaller than those in

.
∎

We will mainly work with the partial order

on the power set of
[n]
and denote the set of its linear extensions by

Claim 2.2

\Delta^{p}(K)=\bigcup_{<_{l}\in\mathcal{L}}\Delta^{l}(K)

Proof.

On the one hand, if

for some

then

is not spanned by

, which contains the vector set

. Therefore, by the definition of

in (

Volume rigidity and
\Delta^{p}(\cdot)

We are now ready to prove Theorem
1.2
. We denote
\sigma_{0}=\{1,3,...,d,n\}

Lemma 2.4

The image of

Proof.

The fact that

for

and

which actually completes the proof by the rank-nullity theorem since
(1+(n-d)(d-1))+(d^{2}-d-1)=n(d-1).

First, for every

and

consider the vector defined by setting
m_{i}=f_{j}
and
m_{i^{\prime}}=0
for every

. Then,

\psi(m_{2},...,m_{d})=f_{[d]\setminus\{i\}}\wedge f_{j}=0

since

. This amounts to

The linear independence of these

.

Identify the vertices of

\displaystyle\psi(e_{i,v})

\displaystyle=f_{[d]\setminus\{i\}}\wedge e_{v}

\displaystyle=(-1)^{d-i}f_{1}\wedge\dots\wedge f_{i-1}\wedge e_{v}\wedge f_{i+1}\wedge\dots\wedge f_{d}

\displaystyle=(-1)^{d-i+d-1}f_{2}\wedge\dots\wedge f_{i-1}\wedge e_{v}\wedge f_{i+1}\wedge\dots\wedge f_{d}\wedge f_{1}.

Let

Proof of Corollary

\{1,2,\ldots,d-2,d+1,d+2\}\nleq_{p}\{1,3,4,\ldots,d,n\}

•

If

•

5.
Concluding remarks

We end up with some related open problems.
An obvious one is to extend Corollary
1.3
to include all surface triangulations.

Conjecture 5.1
.

Every triangulation of a compact connected surface without boundary, minus a single triangle, is volume-rigid.

The problem we face in applying Fogelsanger’s decomposition
[
Fog88
]
(see also
[
CJT22
, Sec.3.3]
) to volume rigidity of surfaces is that the pieces in the decomposition include triangle faces not existing in the original triangulation, and thus the gluing lemmas we could prove, e.g. Lemma
3.3
, are not strong enough to settle Conjecture
5.1
.

It is known that for every triangulation
K
of the
2
-sphere on
n
vertices, minus a single triangle, its exterior shifting
\Delta^{\rm{lex}}(K)=\Delta^{p}(K)
consists exactly of the triangle
13n
and all the triangles that are smaller than it in the lex-order, and their subsets. This is a sufficient condition for volume rigidity by Theorem
1.2
. The following conjecture deals with a higher-dimensional counterpart of this fact.

Conjecture 5.2
.

For every
d\geq 3
, every triangulation
K
of the
(d-1)
-sphere minus a single
(d-1)
-simplex is volume rigid.

It is also natural to ask whether the stronger property of
\{1,3,4,\ldots,d,n\}\in\Delta^{\rm{lex}}(K)
holds true. This is known, and tight, for stacked spheres
[
Mur07
]
(also
[
Nev07
, Example 2.1.8]
).
Let us remark that the conclusion
\{1,3,4,\ldots,d,n\}\in\Delta^{s}(K)
for Kalai’s symmetric shifting operator
\Delta^{s}(\cdot)
is equivalent to the hard-Lefschetz isomorphism from degree
1
to degree
d-1
in a generic Artinian reduction of the Stanley-Reisner ring of
K
over the field of reals; the later isomorphism was proved recently by Adiprasito
[
Adi18
]
.

Back to general complexes,

Problem 5.3
.

For every dimension, find a combinatorial characterization of the corresponding volume-rigidity matroid.

The combinatorial characterization problem is important for the
d
-rigidity matroid (and is open for
d\geq 3
). The
d
-rigidity of a graph
G
on
n
vertices is equivalent to

\{d,n\}\in\Delta^{s}(G)
. In view of this fact, we ask:

Problem 5.4
.

Define a version of symmetric shifting
\Delta^{sp}(\cdot)
and find a matroid on
\binom{[n]}{d}
such that its bases
K
are exactly those satisfying
\Delta^{sp}(K)=\{\tau:\tau\leq_{p}\{1,3,4,\ldots,d,n\}\}
.

An additional direction to explore is the volume rigidity of a
(d-1)
-dimensional simplicial complex
K
in
\mathbb{R}^{d^{\prime}}
for
d^{\prime}\geq d-1
. That is, let
\mathbf{p}:V(K)\to\mathbb{R}^{d^{\prime}}
be generic, and ask whether there is a non-trivial motion of the vertices that preserves all the volumes of

’s
(d-1)
-dimensional simplices in
\mathbb{R}^{d^{\prime}}
. The case

, and the case

is the volume rigidity notion we study in this paper. Several natural questions on the remaining cases

arise: what are the trivial motions in this setting? Is there a characterization of

-volume rigidity in

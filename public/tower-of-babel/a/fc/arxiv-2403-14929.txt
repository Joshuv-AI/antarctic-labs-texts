Four-body Semileptonic Decays B → D ∗ ​ P ​ ℓ + ​ ν ℓ B\to D^{*}P\ell^{+}\nu_{\ell} with the SU(3) Flavor Symmetry
Meng-Yuan Wan, Yuan-Guo Xu † , Qi-Lin Jia, Yue-Xin Liu, Yi-Jie Zhang College of Physics and Communication Electronics, JiangXi Normal University, NanChang, JiangXi 330022, China † Corresponding author. E-mail addresses:yuanguoxu@jxnu.edu.cn

Abstract
We present a complete study of the B → D ∗ ​ P ​ ℓ + ​ ν ℓ ​ ( ℓ = e , μ , τ ) B\to D^{*}P\ell^{+}\nu_{\ell}\penalty\ (\ell=e,\mu,\tau) decays with the non-resonant, the charmed axial vector resonant and the charmed tensor resonant contributions by using the SU(3) flavor symmetry. Relevant amplitude relations between different decay modes are obtained by the SU(3) flavor symmetry. We then predict non-measured branching ratios of the B → D ∗ ​ P ​ ℓ + ​ ν ℓ B\to D^{*}P\ell^{+}\nu_{\ell} decays with the non-resonant and the charmed resonant contributions by using present experimental data of the B → D ∗ ​ P ​ ℓ ′ ⁣ + ​ ν ℓ ′ ​ ( ℓ ′ = e , μ ) B\to D^{*}P\ell^{\prime+}\nu_{\ell^{\prime}}\penalty\ (\ell^{\prime}=e,\mu) decays within 2 ​ σ 2\sigma errors. We have found that B 0 , + → D ∗ ​ η ​ ℓ + ​ ν ℓ B^{0,+}\to D^{*}\eta\ell^{+}\nu_{\ell} , B 0 , + → D ∗ ​ η ′ ​ ℓ + ​ ν ℓ B^{0,+}\to D^{*}\eta^{\prime}\ell^{+}\nu_{\ell} , B s 0 → D s ∗ ​ η ​ ℓ + ​ ν ℓ B^{0}_{s}\to D^{*}_{s}\eta\ell^{+}\nu_{\ell} , B s 0 → D s ∗ ​ η ′ ​ ℓ + ​ ν ℓ B^{0}_{s}\to D^{*}_{s}\eta^{\prime}\ell^{+}\nu_{\ell} and B 0 , + → D s ∗ ​ K ​ ℓ + ​ ν ℓ B^{0,+}\to D^{*}_{s}K\ell^{+}\nu_{\ell} decays only receive non-resonant contributions. Decays B s 0 → D s ∗ ⁣ − ​ π 0 ​ ℓ + ​ ν ℓ B^{0}_{s}\to D_{s}^{*-}\pi^{0}\ell^{+}\nu_{\ell} only receive the D s ​ 1 ′ D^{\prime}_{s1} resonant contributions. Other decays receive all three kinds of contributions, and three kinds of contributions are important in most of decays.

Introduction

Decays

decays.
And the predictions of the four-body semileptonic decays
B\to D^{*}P\ell^{+}\nu_{\ell}
can be tested by LHC and Belle-II in the near future.

For the semileptonic decays, since leptons do not participate in the strong interaction, the weak and strong dynamics
can be separated, the theoretical description of the semileptonic decays are relatively simple. Moreover, the experimental backgrounds of the semileptonic decays are cleaner than ones of the hadronic decays.
The hadronic transition form factors, which are crucial for testing the theoretical calculations of the involved strong interaction, contain all the strong dynamics in the initial and final hadrons.
The analytic structure of the
B\to D^{*}P
form factors is more complicated than for the
B\to D^{*}
or
B\to P
form factors, as similarly analysed in Ref.
Feldmann:2018kqr

in the

Shi:2017pgh
;
Shi:2020rkz

the light-cone sum rules
Sekihara:2015iha
;
Cheng:2017smj

Hambrock:2015aor

Boer:2016iez

of hadronic interactions, the evaluations of the form factors are difficult and often plugged with large uncertainties.

In the absence of reliable calculations, the symmetry analysis can provide very useful information about the decays.
SU(3) flavor symmetry, which is independent of the detailed dynamics, provides us an opportunity to relate different decay modes,
nevertheless, it cannot determine the sizes of the amplitudes or the form factors by itself.
However, if experimental data are enough, one may use the data to extract the form factors.
Even though it is only an approximate symmetry due to up, down, and strange quarks having different masses, it still gives some valuable information about the decays.

II.1
Decay amplitudes

The four-body semileptonic decays
B\rightarrow D^{*}P\ell^{+}\nu_{\ell}
are generated by
\bar{b}\rightarrow\bar{c}\ell^{+}\nu_{\ell}
transition, and the effective Hamiltonian is

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}_{eff}(\bar{b}\rightarrow\bar{c}\ell^{+}\nu_{\ell})=\frac{G_{F}}{\sqrt{2}}V_{cb}\bar{b}\gamma^{\mu}(1-\gamma_{5})\bar{c}\penalty\ \bar{\nu_{\ell}}\gamma_{\mu}(1-\gamma_{5})\ell,

(1)

where
G_{F}
is the Fermi constant,
V_{cb}
is the CKM matrix element. Decay amplitudes of the
B\rightarrow D^{*}P\ell^{+}\nu_{\ell}
decays can be written as

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \mathcal{A}(B\rightarrow D^{*}P\ell^{+}\nu_{\ell})&=\langle D^{*}(k_{1})P(k_{2})\ell^{+}(q_{1})\nu_{\ell}(q_{2})|\mathcal{H}_{eff}(\bar{b}\rightarrow\bar{c}\ell^{+}\nu_{\ell})|B(p_{B})\rangle\\
&=\frac{G_{F}}{\sqrt{2}}V_{cb}L_{\mu}H^{\mu},\end{aligned}

(2)

where
L_{\mu}=\bar{\nu_{\ell}}\gamma_{\mu}(1-\gamma_{5})\ell
is the leptonic charged current, and
H^{\mu}=\langle D^{*}(k_{1})P(k_{2})|\bar{c}\gamma^{\mu}(1-\gamma_{5})\bar{b}|B(p_{B})\rangle
is the hadronic matrix element.

Usually, the hadronic matrix element
H^{\mu}
can be obtained in terms of the form factors of the
B\rightarrow D^{*}P
. Similar to
B\to PP
form factors given in Ref.
Boer:2016iez
,
the
B\rightarrow D^{*}P
form factors are defined by

\displaystyle\langle D^{*}(k_{1})P(k_{2})|\bar{c}\gamma^{\mu}\bar{b}|B(p_{B})\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle iF_{\perp}\frac{1}{\sqrt{k^{2}}}q^{\mu}_{\perp},

(3)

\displaystyle-\langle D^{*}(k_{1})P(k_{2})|\bar{c}\gamma^{\mu}\bar{b}|B(p_{B})\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle F_{t}\frac{q^{\mu}}{\sqrt{q^{2}}}+F_{0}\frac{2\sqrt{q^{2}}}{\sqrt{\lambda}}k^{\mu}_{0}+F_{\parallel}\frac{1}{\sqrt{k^{2}}}\bar{k}^{\mu}_{\parallel},

(4)

where
k\equiv k_{1}+k_{2}
,
q\equiv q_{1}+q_{2}
,
\lambda=\lambda(m_{D^{*}}^{2},q^{2},k^{2})
with
\lambda(a,b,c)=a^{2}+b^{2}+c^{2}-2ab-2bc-2ac
, and
F_{0},F_{t},F_{\perp},F_{\parallel}
are the form factors of
B\rightarrow D^{*}P
. In addition,
q^{\mu}_{\perp}
,
k^{\mu}_{0}
and
\bar{k}^{\mu}_{\parallel}
are defined in Ref.
Boer:2016iez
.

The differential branching ratios of the nonresonant
B\rightarrow D^{*}P\ell^{+}\nu_{\ell}
decays can be written as

\displaystyle\frac{d\mathcal{B}(B\rightarrow D^{*}P\ell^{+}\nu_{\ell})_{N}}{dq^{2}dk^{2}}=\frac{1}{2}\tau_{B}|\mathcal{N}|^{2}\beta_{\ell}(3-\beta_{\ell})|H_{N}|^{2},

(5)

with

\displaystyle{|\mathcal{N}|}^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle G^{2}_{F}{|V_{cb}|}^{2}\frac{\beta_{\ell}q^{2}\sqrt{\lambda}}{3\times 2^{10}\pi^{5}m^{3}_{B}}\,,\quad\text{with}\quad\beta_{\ell}=1-\frac{m^{2}_{\ell}}{q^{2}}\,.

\displaystyle|H_{N}|^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle|F_{0}|^{2}+\frac{2}{3}(|F_{\parallel}|^{2}+|F_{\perp}|^{2})+\frac{3m_{\ell}^{2}}{q^{2}(3-\beta_{\ell})}|F_{t}|^{2},

(6)

where
\tau_{M}
(
m_{M}
) is lifetime(mass) of
M
particle. The ranges of integration are given by
(m_{D^{*}}+m_{P})^{2}\leq k^{2}\leq(m_{B}-m_{\ell})^{2}
and
m_{\ell}^{2}\leq q^{2}\leq(m_{B}-\sqrt{k^{2}})^{2}
.

The calculations of the
F_{0},F_{t},F_{\perp},F_{\parallel}
form factors are difficult. If we ignore
|F_{t}|^{2}
term since it is proportional to
m_{\ell}^{2}
and it is small when
\ell=e,\mu
,
|H_{N}|^{2}
is only include the hadronic part.
Noted that although
|F_{t}|^{2}
term might be large when
\ell=\tau
, it is difficult to estimate its contribution in this work, so we still ignore it.
Then
|H_{N}|^{2}
, which only includes hadronic part, can be related by the SU(3) flavor symmetry/breaking of
u
,
d
,
s
quarks.

Except for the non-resonant
B\to D^{*}P\ell^{+}\nu_{\ell}
decays, the
B\rightarrow D^{*}P\ell^{+}\nu_{\ell}
decay with the
D^{**}
resonant state is also studied, where
D^{**}
is an lightest excited charmed meson.
There are the four lightest excited charmed meson states,
D^{**}=(D_{0},D^{\prime}_{1},D_{1},D^{*}_{2})
or
(D_{s0},D^{\prime}_{s1},D_{s1},D^{*}_{s2})
. The spin-0 states
D_{0}
and
D_{s0}
can only decay to
DP

Belle:2022yzd
,
so we will study
D^{\prime}_{1}
,
D_{1}
and
D^{*}_{2}
decay to
D^{*}P
in this work.

In the case of the decay widths of the resonances are very narrow, the resonant decay branching ratios respect a simple factorization relation

\mathcal{B}(B\rightarrow D^{**}\ell^{+}\nu_{\ell},D^{**}\rightarrow D^{*}P)=\mathcal{B}(B\rightarrow D^{**}\ell^{+}\nu_{\ell})\times\mathcal{B}(D^{**}\rightarrow D^{*}P),

(36)

and this result is also a good approximation for wider resonances. Above Eq. (
36
) will be used in our analysis for resonant
B\rightarrow D^{**}(\rightarrow D^{*}P)\ell^{+}\nu_{\ell}
decays.
Relevant
\mathcal{B}(B\rightarrow D^{**}\ell^{+}\nu_{\ell})
and
\mathcal{B}(D^{**}\rightarrow D^{*}P)
also can be obtained by the SU(3) flavor symmetry.

III.1.1
B\to D_{1}/D^{\prime}_{1}\ell^{+}\nu_{\ell}
decays

Similar to
B\to V\ell^{+}\nu_{\ell}
decays, the decay amplitudes of the
B\to A\ell^{+}\nu_{\ell}
decays with
A=D_{1}/D^{\prime}_{1}
can be written as

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}(B\rightarrow A\ell^{+}\nu_{\ell})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{G_{F}}{\sqrt{2}}V_{cb}\sum_{mm^{\prime}}g_{mm^{\prime}}L^{\lambda_{\ell}\lambda_{\nu}}_{m}H^{\lambda_{A}}_{m^{\prime}},

(37)

with

\displaystyle L^{\lambda_{\ell}\lambda_{\nu}}_{m}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\epsilon_{\alpha}(m)\bar{\nu_{\ell}}\gamma^{\alpha}(1-\gamma_{5})\ell,

(38)

\displaystyle H^{\lambda_{A}}_{m^{\prime}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\epsilon^{*}_{\beta}(m^{\prime})\langle{A}(p,\varepsilon^{*})|\bar{c}\gamma^{\beta}(1-\gamma_{5})b|B(p_{B})\rangle,

(39)

where the particle helicities
\lambda_{A}=0,\pm 1
,
\lambda_{\ell}=\pm\frac{1}{2}
and
\lambda_{\nu}=+\frac{1}{2}
, as well as
\epsilon_{\mu}(m)
is the polarization vectors of the virtual
W
with
m=0,t,\pm 1
,
and
\varepsilon^{*}
is the polarization vectors of
A
meson.
Hadronic matrix element can be parameterized by the
B\to A
form factors
Cheng:2017pcq
;
Cheng:2003sm

\displaystyle\left<A(p,\varepsilon^{*})\left|\bar{c}\gamma_{\mu}(1-\gamma_{5})b\right|B(p_{B})\right>

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{2iA(q^{\prime 2})}{m_{B}-m_{A}}\epsilon_{\mu\nu\alpha\beta}\varepsilon^{*\nu}p^{\alpha}_{B}p^{\beta}

(40)

\displaystyle-\left[\varepsilon^{*}_{\mu}(m_{B}-m_{A})V^{A}_{1}(q^{\prime 2})-(p_{B}+p)_{\mu}(\varepsilon^{*}.p_{B})\frac{V^{A}_{2}(q^{\prime 2})}{m_{B}-m_{A}}\right]

\displaystyle+q^{\prime}_{\mu}(\varepsilon^{*}.p_{B})\frac{2m_{A}}{q^{\prime 2}}[V^{A}_{3}(q^{\prime 2})-V^{A}_{0}(q^{\prime 2})],

with
q^{\prime}=p_{B}-p
. Then the hadronic helicity
amplitudes can be written as
Sun:2011ssd
;
Li:2009tx
;
Colangelo:2019axi
;
Verma:2011yw

\displaystyle H^{A}_{\pm}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(m_{B}-m_{A})V^{A}_{1}(q^{\prime 2})\mp\frac{2m_{B}|\vec{p}_{c}|}{(m_{B}-m_{A})}A(q^{\prime 2}),

(41)

\displaystyle H^{A}_{0}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2m_{A}\sqrt{q^{\prime 2}}}\left[(m_{B}^{2}-m_{A}^{2}-q^{\prime 2})(m_{B}-m_{A})V^{A}_{1}(q^{\prime 2})-\frac{4m_{B}^{2}|\vec{p}_{c}|^{2}}{m_{B}-m_{A}}V^{A}_{2}(q^{\prime 2})\right],

(42)

\displaystyle H^{A}_{t}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{2m_{B}|\vec{p}_{c}|}{\sqrt{q^{\prime 2}}}V^{A}_{0}(q^{\prime 2}),

(43)

where
|\vec{p}_{c}|\equiv\frac{\sqrt{\lambda}}{2m_{B}}
with
\lambda=m_{B}^{4}+m_{A}^{4}+q^{\prime 4}-2m_{B}^{2}m_{A}^{2}-2q^{\prime 2}m^{2}_{B}-2q^{\prime 2}m^{2}_{A}
.

The differential branching ratios are
Ivanov:2019nqd

\frac{d\mathcal{B}(B\to A\ell^{+}\nu_{\ell})}{dq^{\prime 2}}=\frac{\tau_{B}G_{F}^{2}|V_{cb}|^{2}\lambda^{1/2}q^{\prime 2}}{24(2\pi)^{3}m_{B}^{3}}\left(1-\frac{m_{\ell}^{2}}{q^{\prime 2}}\right)^{2}\left(1+\frac{m_{\ell}^{2}}{2q^{\prime 2}}\right){\cal H}_{\rm total}(B\to A\ell^{+}\nu_{\ell}),

(44)

with

\displaystyle{\cal H}_{\rm total}(B\to A\ell^{+}\nu_{\ell})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Big(\big|H^{A}_{+}\big|^{2}+\big|H^{A}_{-}\big|^{2}+\big|H^{A}_{0}\big|^{2}\Big)+\frac{3m_{\ell}^{2}}{2q^{\prime 2}}\Big/\left(1+\frac{m_{\ell}^{2}}{2q^{\prime 2}}\right)\big|H^{A}_{t}\big|^{2},

(45)

where
m_{\ell}^{2}\leq q^{\prime 2}\leq(m_{B}-m_{A})^{2}
.
Then one can obtain the branching ratios by the Eq. (
44
) and the form factors. Usually, it is difficult to calculate the form factors, and their results depend on the different approaches.

IV
Global analysis

In this section, we will analyse the numerical results of
\mathcal{B}(B\to D^{*}P\ell^{+}\nu_{\ell})
given in Tab.
2
.
As given in Tab.
2
, we can see that
\mathcal{B}(B\to D^{*}P\tau^{+}\nu_{\tau})
with the non-resonant and the charmed axial vector resonant contributions are much smaller than corresponding
\mathcal{B}(B\to D^{*}P\ell^{\prime+}\nu_{\ell^{\prime}})

since the former small phase spaces.

We can see that
B^{0,+}\to D^{*}\eta\ell^{+}\nu_{\ell}
,
B^{0,+}\to D^{*}\eta^{\prime}\ell^{+}\nu_{\ell}
,

B^{0}_{s}\to D^{*}_{s}\eta\ell^{+}\nu_{\ell}
,
B^{0}_{s}\to D^{*}_{s}\eta^{\prime}\ell^{+}\nu_{\ell}
and
B^{0,+}\to D^{*}_{s}K\ell^{+}\nu_{\ell}
decays
only receive the non-resonant contributions. Under the SU(3) flavor symmetry,
\mathcal{B}(B^{0}_{s}\to D_{s}^{*-}\pi^{0}\ell^{+}\nu_{\ell})_{N}=0
, and
\mathcal{B}(B^{0}_{s}\to D_{s}^{*-}\pi^{0}\ell^{+}\nu_{\ell})_{all}

only include the
D^{\prime-}_{s1}
resonant contributions.

Other
B\to D^{*}P\ell^{+}\nu_{\ell}
decays receive the contributions of the non-resonant
states, the charmed axial vector resonant states and the charmed tensor resonant
states. In the
\ell=e,\mu
decays, the charmed axial vector resonant contributions are largest. But in the
\ell=\tau
decays, both the charmed axial vector and tensor resonant contributions are important.
We sum all three kinds of contributions as
\mathcal{B}_{all}
(for the non-resonant branching ratios, they are taken in the ranges of 0 to their maximal values listed in the second column of Tab.
2
.), after considering the experimental bounds of
\mathcal{B}(B^{+}\to D^{*-}\pi^{+}\ell^{\prime+}\nu_{\ell^{\prime}})_{all}
in Eq. (
25
)
and
\mathcal{B}(B^{0}\to D^{*0}\pi^{+}\ell^{\prime+}\nu_{\ell^{\prime}})_{all}
in Eq. (
29
), the results of
\mathcal{B}_{all}
are listed in the last column of Tab.
2
.

Noted that the interference terms between non-resonant, the axial vector resonant and the tensor resonant contributions exist, but they have not been considered in this work,
and they might not be ignored if all three kinds of contributions or two kinds of contributions are important in the decays. And they will be studied in our succeeding work.

V
Summary

In this paper, we have studied the
B\to D^{*}P\ell^{+}\nu_{\ell}
decay processes with the non-resonant, the charmed axial vector resonant and the charmed tensor resonant contributions
by using the SU(3) flavor symmetry.
The amplitude relations of the non-resonant
B\to D^{*}P\ell^{+}\nu_{\ell}
decays, the semileptonic
B\to D^{**}\ell^{+}\nu_{\ell}
decays and the non-leptonic
D^{**}\to D^{*}P
decays have been obtained by the SU(3) flavor symmetry.
And then using relevant experimental data of the
B\to D^{*}P\ell^{\prime+}\nu_{\ell^{\prime}}
decays, we have presented a theoretical
analysis of these decays. Our main results can be summarized as follows.

We have found that
B^{0,+}\to D^{*}\eta\ell^{+}\nu_{\ell}
,
B^{0,+}\to D^{*}\eta^{\prime}\ell^{+}\nu_{\ell}
,
B^{0}_{s}\to D^{*}_{s}\eta\ell^{+}\nu_{\ell}
,
B^{0}_{s}\to D^{*}_{s}\eta^{\prime}\ell^{+}\nu_{\ell}
and
B^{0,+}\to D^{*}_{s}K\ell^{+}\nu_{\ell}
decays only receive
non-resonant contributions. Decays
B^{0}_{s}\to D_{s}^{*-}\pi^{0}\ell^{+}\nu_{\ell}
only receive the
D^{\prime}_{s1}
resonant contributions, and the latter contributions might be larger than the former.
Other decays receive the non-resonant, the charmed axial vector resonant and the charmed tensor resonant contributions, all three kinds of contributions are important in most of decays.
Many branching ratios have been predicted on the order of
\mathcal{O}(10^{-3})
, and they might be measured in future experiments.

Although only approximate predictions can be obtained by the SU(3) flavor symmetry, they are still useful for understanding these decays.
So far, our predictions under the SU(3) flavor symmetry are quite agree with present experimental data.
Our predictions could be tested in future experiments, such as LHCb and Belle II.

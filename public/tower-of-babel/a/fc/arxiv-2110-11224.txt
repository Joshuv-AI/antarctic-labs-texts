On restriction of exponential sums to hypersurfaces with zero curvature Thanks: The author is partially supported by the NSF grant DMS-2055156 Thanks: AMS subject classification: Primary 42A45, Secondary 11L07
Ciprian Demeter

Abstract
. We prove essentially sharp bounds for the L p L^{p} restriction of weighted Gauss sums to monomial curves. Getting the L 2 L^{2} upper bound combines the T ​ T ∗ TT^{*} method for matrices with the first and second derivative test for exponential sums. The matching lower bound follows via constructive interference on short blocks of integers, near the critical point of the phase function. This method is used to make the broader point that restriction to hypersurfaces is really sensitive to curvature. Our results here complement those in [ 4 ] .



1.
introduction

The paper
[
4
]
is concerned with proving essentially scale-independent (by that we mean estimates with at most
N^{\epsilon}
losses)
L^{p}
bounds for a variety of weighted exponential sums restricted to hypersurfaces. The key assumption in
[
4
]
is that hypersurfaces have nonzero Gaussian curvature. This property was exploited in our arguments by means of the decay of the Fourier transform of the corresponding surface measures.

The simplest instance is provided by the weighted Gauss sums. We write
e(z)=e^{2\pi iz}
.

Theorem 1.1

(
[
4
]
)
.

Assume
\phi:[-1,1]\to{\mathbb{R}}
is
C^{2}
and satisfies
\inf_{x\in[-1,1]}|\phi^{\prime\prime}(x)|>0
. Then for each
a_{n}\in{\mathbb{C}}
we have

\|\sum_{n=-N}^{N}a_{n}e(nx+n^{2}\phi(x))\|_{L^{2}(-1,1)}\lesssim\|a_{n}\|_{2}.

For all the examples discussed in
[
4
]
, it was not clear whether nonzero curvature was an artifact of the proof or a necessary condition. Here, we prove the latter is the case. For the example of weighted Gauss sums restricted to monomial curves, we can in fact be very precise.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
k\geq 3
. Let
\mathcal{B}_{N,k}
the smallest constant such that
the inequality

\|\sum_{n=-N}^{N}a_{n}e(nx-n^{2}x^{k})\|_{L^{2}(-1,1)}\leq\mathcal{B}_{N,k}\|a_{n}\|_{2}

holds for arbitrary
a_{n}\in{\mathbb{C}}
. Then
N^{\frac{k-2}{6(k-1)}}\lesssim\mathcal{B}_{N,k}{\;\lessapprox}\;N^{\frac{k-2}{6(k-1)}}
.

The notation
{\;\lessapprox}\;
stands for logarithmic losses,
(\log N)^{O(1)}
in the scale parameter
N
.

The main idea for getting lower bounds is described in the next section. We construct coefficients
a_{n}
that realize constructive interference for our weighted Gauss sums on a “large” set of values for
x
. Standard computations show that the constant coefficient case
a_{n}=1
is well behaved, in the sense that

\|\sum_{n=-N}^{N}e(nx-n^{2}x^{k})\|_{L^{2}(-1,1)}\lesssim_{\epsilon}N^{\frac{1}{2}+\epsilon}.

Our optimal construction will involve quadratically modulated coefficients. They arise while doing Taylor expansions near the critical point of the phase function. Similar scale-dependent lower bounds in higher dimensions are explored in Section
4
.

The matching upper bound in Theorem
1.2
is obtained in Section
3
. The
TT^{*}
method is combined with stationary phase analysis and derivative tests for exponential sums.

The numerology in Theorem
1.2
is interesting in a few ways. The sharp bound at the endpoint
L^{2}
is sensitive to the exponent
k\geq 2
, but the sharp bound at the critical exponent
p=4
is not. See Theorem
4.8
. Also, both the lower and upper bounds in
L^{2}
are more difficult to obtain than the ones in
L^{4}
. This is unlike the typical examples in the literature.

The result in Theorem
1.2
can also be related to the following (essentially equivalent reformulation of the) periodic Schrödinger maximal operator conjecture. For each measurable function
\phi:[0,1]\to[0,1]
, it is expected that the following estimate holds

\|\sum_{n=-N}^{N}a_{n}e(nx+n^{2}\phi(x))\|_{L^{2}(0,1)}\lesssim_{\epsilon}N^{\frac{1}{4}+\epsilon}\|a_{n}\|_{2}.

See
[
1
]
for recent progress and the history of this conjecture.

2.
Lower bounds

Throughout the rest of the paper we will write either
A=o(B)
or
A\ll B
to denote the fact that
A\leq cB
for some small enough absolute constant
c>0
, that depends on
k
. The notation
A\lesssim B
or
A=O(B)
will refer to the scenario when
A\leq CB
for some potentially large, but still universal, constant
C
.

Theorem 2.1
.

Assume
\phi\in C^{k+1}(-1,1)
for some
k\geq 3
. Assume there is
x_{0}\in(-1,1)
such that

\phi^{\prime}(x_{0})=\phi^{\prime\prime}(x_{0})=\ldots=\phi^{(k-1)}(x_{0})=0

\phi^{(k)}(x_{0})\not=0.

Then for each
N
there exists
a_{n}\in{\mathbb{C}}
such that for each
p\geq 2

\|\sum_{n=-N}^{N}a_{n}e(nx-n^{2}\phi(x))\|_{L^{p}(-1,1)}\gtrsim N^{\frac{2k-1}{6(k-1)}-\frac{k+1}{3p(k-1)}}\|a_{n}\|_{2},

with an implicit constant depending on
\phi
, but independent of
N
.

Proof.

Due to modulation invariance, we may assume
x_{0}=0
, at the expense of replacing the integration domain with a smaller interval
(-\delta,\delta)
. We will also normalize such that
\phi(x)={x^{k}}+O(x^{k+1})
. Indeed, since the sum in
n
is over a symmetric interval, it will not matter whether
\phi^{(k)}(0)
is positive or negative. Let
x_{N}\sim\frac{1}{N^{\frac{1}{k-1}}}
be such that
\phi^{\prime}(x_{N})=\frac{1}{N}
. Such a point exists since
\phi^{\prime}(x)=kx^{k-1}+O(x^{k})
.

We apply Taylor’s formula to
\psi(x,n)=nx-n^{2}\phi(x)
near the point
(x_{N},\frac{N}{2})
. If
x\in I_{1}=[x_{N}-\Delta,x_{N}+\Delta]
and
n\in I_{2}=[\frac{N}{2}-M,\frac{N}{2}+M]
then

\displaystyle\psi(x,n)

\displaystyle=\psi(x_{N},\frac{N}{2})+\psi_{x}(x_{N},\frac{N}{2})(x-x_{N})+\psi_{n}(x_{N},\frac{N}{2})(n-\frac{N}{2})

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{2}\psi_{xx}(x_{N},\frac{N}{2})(x-x_{N})^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\psi_{nn}(x_{N},\frac{N}{2})(n-\frac{N}{2})^{2}

\displaystyle+\psi_{xn}(x_{N},\frac{N}{2})(x-x_{N})(n-\frac{N}{2})

\displaystyle+O(\|\psi_{xxx}\|_{L^{\infty}(I_{1}\times I_{2})})\Delta^{3}+O(\|\psi_{xnn}\|_{L^{\infty}(I_{1}\times I_{2})})\Delta M^{2}+O(\|\psi_{xxn}\|_{L^{\infty}(I_{1}\times I_{2})})\Delta^{2}M.

The terms involving
\psi
,
\psi_{x}
and
\psi_{xx}
are independent of
n
, so they are annihilated by the absolute value. The terms involving
\psi_{n}
and
\psi_{nn}

are independent of
x
. They disappear from the computations once we choose

a_{n}=e(-\psi_{n}(x_{N},\frac{N}{2})(n-\frac{N}{2})-\frac{1}{2}\psi_{nn}(x_{N},\frac{N}{2})(n-\frac{N}{2})^{2}).

(1)

We would like to make the cubic terms
o(1)
. We choose
\Delta=o(\frac{1}{N^{\frac{k+1}{3(k-1)}}})
,
M=N^{\frac{2k-1}{3(k-1)}}
. Note that our choice of
x_{N}
implies that

\psi_{xn}(x_{N},\frac{N}{2})=0.

Note also that
x\sim x_{N}
whenever
x\in I_{1}
. Thus

\|\psi_{xnn}\|_{L^{\infty}(I_{1}\times I_{2})}\lesssim\frac{1}{N},\;\;\;\text{and }\;\;\|\psi_{xnn}\|_{L^{\infty}(I_{1}\times I_{2})}\Delta M^{2}=o(1).

Similarly,

\|\psi_{xxn}\|_{L^{\infty}(I_{1}\times I_{2})}\lesssim\frac{N}{N^{\frac{k-2}{k-1}}},\;\;\;\text{and }\;\;\|\psi_{xxn}\|_{L^{\infty}(I_{1}\times I_{2})}\Delta^{2}M=o(1)

\|\psi_{xxx}\|_{L^{\infty}(I_{1}\times I_{2})}\lesssim\frac{N^{2}}{N^{\frac{k-3}{k-1}}},\;\;\;\text{and }\;\;\|\psi_{xxx}\|_{L^{\infty}(I_{1}\times I_{2})}\Delta^{3}=o(1).

Let
a_{n}
be as in (
1
) for
|n-\frac{N}{2}|\leq M
, and
a_{n}=0
otherwise. So
\|a_{n}\|_{2}\sim M^{1/2}
. We have for
|x-x_{N}|\leq\Delta

|\sum_{n=-N}^{N}a_{n}e(nx-n^{2}\phi(x))|\sim M,

thus

\int_{-\delta}^{\delta}|\sum_{n=-N}^{N}a_{n}e(nx-n^{2}\phi(x))|^{p}dx\gtrsim M^{p}\Delta.

It now suffices to note that
M^{1/2}\Delta^{1/p}\sim N^{\frac{2k-1}{6(k-1)}-\frac{k+1}{3p(k-1)}}
.

∎

3.
L^{2}
upper bounds

Fix
k\geq 3
. It suffices to prove that

\|\sum_{C_{k}N\leq n\leq N}a_{n}e(nx-n^{2}\frac{x^{k}}{k})\|_{L^{2}(0,1)}{\;\lessapprox}\;N^{\frac{k-2}{6(k-1)}}\|a_{n}\|_{2}.

where
C_{k}<1
is close to 1, depending on
k
. Working with
x^{k}/k
versus
x^{k}
simplifies some of the numerology, but has no other relevance to the argument. The reduction to a fixed dyadic block costs us a
\log N
. In particular, all future computations will involve
n,m\sim N
. The estimate

\|\sum_{-N\leq n\leq-1}a_{n}e(nx-n^{2}\frac{x^{k}}{k})\|_{L^{2}(0,1)}{\;\lessapprox}\;\|a_{n}\|_{2}

is an immediate consequence of Lemma
3.2
and Lemma
5.3
, as it will become clear in a moment. The advantage here is that there are no critical points in
[0,1]
.

Let
\phi(x)=\phi_{n,m}(x)=(n-m)[x-(n+m)\frac{x^{k}}{k}]
, with
n\not=m
. This phase function has only one critical point in
[0,1]
, at
x_{0}=\frac{1}{(n+m)^{\frac{1}{k-1}}}
. Let
\theta_{n,m}
and
\eta
be smooth functions such that

1_{[0,1]}(x)\leq\theta_{n,m}(x)+\eta((n+m)^{\frac{1}{k-1}}(x-x_{0}))\leq 1_{[-x_{0}/2,2]}(x).

In order to facilitate the forthcoming oscillatory integral estimate, we require
\eta
to be supported in the interval
[-\delta,\delta]
, and to be equal to 1 on
[-\delta/2,\delta/2]
, where
\delta=o(1)
is small enough depending only on
k
.

We write

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}|\sum_{C_{k}N\leq n\leq N}

\displaystyle a_{n}e(nx-n^{2}\frac{x^{k}}{k})|^{2}dx\leq

\displaystyle\sum_{C_{k}N\leq n,m\leq N}a_{n}\overline{a_{m}}\int e((n-m)x-(n^{2}-m^{2})\frac{x^{k}}{k})\theta_{n,m}(x)dx

\displaystyle+\sum_{C_{k}N\leq n,m\leq N}a_{n}\overline{a_{m}}\int e((n-m)x-(n^{2}-m^{2})\frac{x^{k}}{k})\eta((n+m)^{\frac{1}{k-1}}(x-x_{0}))dx.

The next two lemmas clarify the asymptotic nature of the integrals in the two sums.

Lemma 3.1

(Stationary regime)
.

If
|n-m|\gtrsim N^{\frac{1}{k-1}}
we have for some
C_{sta}\in{\mathbb{C}}
independent of
n,m

\int e(\phi(x))\eta((n+m)^{\frac{1}{k-1}}(x-x_{0}))dx=O(\frac{1}{|m-n|})+\begin{cases}\frac{\overline{C_{sta}}}{|n-m|^{\frac{1}{2}}|n+m|^{\frac{1}{2(k-1)}}}e(\frac{(k-1)(n-m)}{k(n+m)^{\frac{1}{k-1}}}),\;\;m<n\\
\\
\frac{{C_{sta}}}{|n-m|^{\frac{1}{2}}|n+m|^{\frac{1}{2(k-1)}}}e(\frac{(k-1)(n-m)}{k(n+m)^{\frac{1}{k-1}}}),\;\;m>n\end{cases}.

If
|n-m|\lesssim N^{\frac{1}{k-1}}
we have

|\int e(\phi(x))\eta((n+m)^{\frac{1}{k-1}}(x-x_{0}))dx|\lesssim\frac{1}{N^{\frac{1}{k-1}}}.

Proof.

Use Taylor expansion near
x_{0}
to write

\phi(x)=\frac{(k-1)(n-m)}{k(n+m)^{\frac{1}{k-1}}}-\frac{n^{2}-m^{2}}{k(n+m)^{\frac{k}{k-1}}}\sum_{j=2}^{k}{k\choose j}[(n+m)^{\frac{1}{k-1}}(x-x_{0})]^{j}.

Changing variables
(n+m)^{\frac{1}{k-1}}(x-x_{0})=t
we find that the integral equals

e(\frac{(k-1)(n-m)}{k(n+m)^{\frac{1}{k-1}}})\frac{1}{(n+m)^{\frac{1}{k-1}}}\int e(\lambda P(t))\eta(t)dt,

where
\lambda=\frac{m^{2}-n^{2}}{(n+m)^{\frac{k}{k-1}}}
,
P(t)=\frac{1}{k}\sum_{j=2}^{k}{k\choose j}t^{j}
. When
|\lambda|\lesssim 1
, we use the fact that the integral is
O(1)
.

Let us now assume that
\lambda\gtrsim 1
, so we have
m>n
.
Note that
P(0)=P^{\prime}(0)=0
and
P^{\prime\prime}(0)\not=0
. We may thus invoke Proposition 3 (see also Remarks 1.3.4) in Chapter 8 from
[
8
]
to write, with some
C_{sta}\in{\mathbb{C}}
independent of
\lambda

\int e(\lambda P(t))\eta(t)dt=\frac{C_{sta}}{|\lambda|^{1/2}}+O(|\lambda|^{-1}).

Note also that when
\lambda
is negative,
\int e(\lambda P(t))\eta(t)dt
is the complex conjugate of
\int e(-\lambda P(t))\eta(t)dt
. The proof is now complete.

∎

Lemma 3.2

(Non-stationary regime)
.

If
n\not=m
we have

|\int e(\phi(x))\theta_{n,m}(x)dx|\lesssim\frac{1}{|n-m|}.

Proof.

To simplify notation, let us write
\theta=\theta_{n,m}
. Since
\phi^{\prime}
is nonzero on the support of
\theta
, we can use integration by parts to write

|\int e(\phi(x))\theta(x)dx|\leq\int|\frac{\theta^{\prime}(x)}{\phi^{\prime}(x)}|dx+\int|\frac{\theta(x)\phi^{\prime\prime}(x)}{(\phi^{\prime}(x))^{2}}|dx.

The support of
\theta
is inside the union of
I_{1}=[-x_{0}/2,x_{0}(1-\frac{\delta}{2})]
and
I_{2}=[x_{0}(1+\frac{\delta}{2}),2]
.
The measure of
I_{1}
is
O(x_{0})
and we have
\|\theta^{\prime}\|_{L^{\infty}(I_{1})}=O(x_{0}^{-1})
. Also,
|\phi^{\prime}(x)|\sim|n-m|
for
x\in I_{1}
. These show that
\int_{I_{1}}|\frac{\theta^{\prime}(x)}{\phi^{\prime}(x)}|dx=O(\frac{1}{|n-m|})
.
A similar argument shows that
\int_{I_{1}}|\frac{\theta(x)\phi^{\prime\prime}(x)}{(\phi^{\prime}(x))^{2}}|dx\lesssim\frac{1}{|n-m|}
, since
|\phi^{\prime\prime}(x)|\lesssim\frac{|n-m|}{x_{0}}
on
I_{1}
.

On the other hand, on
I_{2}
we have
\|\theta^{\prime}\|_{L^{\infty}(I_{2})}=O(x_{0}^{-1})
,
|\phi^{\prime}(x)|\sim N|n-m|x^{k-1}

and
|\phi^{\prime\prime}(x)|\sim N|n-m|x^{k-2}
. We conclude by writing

\int_{I_{2}}|\frac{\theta^{\prime}(x)}{\phi^{\prime}(x)}|dx\lesssim\frac{1}{N|n-m|x_{0}}\int_{I_{2}}\frac{1}{x^{k-1}}dx\lesssim\frac{1}{|n-m|},

\int_{I_{2}}|\frac{\theta(x)\phi^{\prime\prime}(x)}{(\phi^{\prime}(x))^{2}}|dx\lesssim\frac{1}{N|n-m|}\int_{I_{2}}x^{-k}dx\lesssim\frac{1}{|n-m|}.

∎

The bound

\int|\sum_{C_{k}N\leq n\leq N}a_{n}e(nx-n^{2}\frac{x^{k}}{k})|^{2}\theta(x)dx\lesssim(\log N)\|a_{n}\|_{2}^{2}

is a consequence of Lemma
3.2
and Lemma
5.3
.

It remains to prove the bound

\int|\sum_{CN\leq n\leq N}a_{n}e(nx-n^{2}\frac{x^{k}}{k})|^{2}\eta((n+m)^{\frac{1}{k-1}}(x-x_{0}))dx{\;\lessapprox}\;N^{\frac{k-2}{3(k-1)}}\|a_{n}\|_{2}^{2}.

By invoking Lemma
3.1
and Lemma
5.3
(to deal with the error term
O(\frac{1}{|m-n|})
), it suffices to prove that

|\sum_{C_{k}N\leq n,m\leq N}a_{n}\overline{a_{m}}c_{n,m}|{\;\lessapprox}\;N^{\frac{k-2}{3(k-1)}}\|a_{n}\|_{2}^{2},

(2)

where

c_{n,m}=\begin{cases}\frac{\overline{C_{sta}}}{|n-m|^{\frac{1}{2}}|n+m|^{\frac{1}{2(k-1)}}}\;e(\frac{(k-1)(n-m)}{k(n+m)^{\frac{1}{k-1}}}),\;\text{if}\;|n-m|\geq N^{\frac{1}{k-1}}\text{ and }m<n\\
\\
\frac{{C_{sta}}}{|n-m|^{\frac{1}{2}}|n+m|^{\frac{1}{2(k-1)}}}\;e(\frac{(k-1)(n-m)}{k(n+m)^{\frac{1}{k-1}}}),\;\text{if}\;|n-m|\geq N^{\frac{1}{k-1}}\text{ and }m>n\\
\\
0,\;\text{otherwise}\end{cases}.

We first note that Lemma
5.3
is not strong enough to give (
2
), as for each
n\sim N
we have

\sum_{m:\;C_{k}N\leq m\leq N\atop{|n-m|\geq N^{\frac{1}{k-1}}}}\frac{1}{|n-m|^{\frac{1}{2}}|n+m|^{\frac{1}{2(k-1)}}}\sim N^{\frac{k-2}{2(k-1)}}.

To get the stronger result we need to exploit the oscillatory nature of
c_{n,m}
, and Lemma
5.4
will prove the right tool for this purpose. It is immediate that
C=(c_{n,m})

is Hermitian. We need to estimate the coefficients

d_{n,m}=\sum_{x}c_{n,x}{c_{x,m}}.

Given
C_{k}N\leq n<m\leq N
, consider the function
f=f_{n,m}
defined for
C_{k}N\leq x\leq N

f(x)=\frac{k-1}{k}(\frac{x-n}{(x+n)^{\frac{1}{k-1}}}-\frac{x-m}{(x+m)^{\frac{1}{k-1}}}).

We start by proving two auxiliary lemmas regarding the derivatives of
f
. The coefficient
\frac{k-1}{k}
will be irrelevant for our computations.

Lemma 3.3
.

We have for each
C_{k}N\leq x\leq N

|f^{\prime\prime}(x)|\sim\frac{|m-n|}{N^{\frac{2k-1}{k-1}}}.

Proof.

We compute

f^{\prime}(x)=\frac{1}{k}\left[\frac{(k-2)x+kn}{(x+n)^{\frac{k}{k-1}}}-\frac{(k-2)x+km}{(x+m)^{\frac{k}{k-1}}}\right],

(3)

f^{\prime\prime}(x)=\frac{1}{k(k-1)}\left[\frac{(2-k)x+(2-3k)n}{(x+n)^{\frac{2k-1}{k-1}}}-\frac{(2-k)x+(2-3k)m}{(x+m)^{\frac{2k-1}{k-1}}}\right].

Define

g_{x}(t)=\frac{(2-k)x+(2-3k)t}{(x+t)^{\frac{2k-1}{k-1}}}

and note that

g_{x}^{\prime}(t)=\frac{-k^{2}x+tk(3k-2)}{(k-1)(x+t)^{\frac{3k-2}{k-1}}}.

Note that
k^{2}<k(3k-2)
when
k\geq 2
. Choosing
C_{k}
sufficiently close to 1 insures that for
C_{k}N\leq x,t\leq N

we have

g_{x}^{\prime}(t)\sim N^{-\frac{2k-1}{k-1}}.

The desired estimate now follows from the Mean Value Theorem.

∎

Lemma 3.4
.

If
|x-n|\ll m-n
or
|x-m|\ll m-n
then

|f^{\prime}(x)|\sim\frac{(m-n)^{2}}{N^{\frac{2k-1}{k-1}}}.

(4)

Proof.

We start by pointing out that the restriction on
x
being close to either
n
or
m
is necessary. Indeed, since
f(n)=f(m)
,
f
must have a critical point in the interval
(n,m)
. The lemma says that this point is somewhere in the middle.

Let

h_{x}(t)=\frac{(k-2)x+kt}{(x+t)^{\frac{k}{k-1}}}.

Then

h_{x}^{\prime}(t)=\frac{k}{k-1}\times\frac{x-t}{(x+t)^{\frac{2k-1}{k-1}}}.

Since
h_{x}^{\prime}
can be very small (for example
h_{x}^{\prime}(x)=0
), the Mean Value Theorem is not useful in estimating (
3
). Instead, we first use Taylor’s formula with cubic error terms to estimate
|f^{\prime}(n)|
and
|f^{\prime}(m)|
. More precisely,

f^{\prime}(m)=h_{m}(n)-h_{m}(m)=\frac{1}{2}h_{m}^{\prime\prime}(m)(n-m)^{2}+O(\|h_{m}^{\prime\prime\prime}\|_{L^{\infty}[C_{k}N,N]})(m-n)^{3}.

(5)

We compute

h_{x}^{\prime\prime}(t)=\frac{k}{(k-1)^{2}}\times\frac{-kx+(3k-2)t}{(x+t)^{\frac{3k-2}{k-1}}},

h_{x}^{\prime\prime\prime}(t)=\frac{k(2k-1)(3k-2)}{(k-1)^{3}}\times\frac{x-t}{(x+t)^{\frac{4k-3}{k-1}}}.

The relevant things for us are
h_{m}^{\prime\prime}(m)\sim\frac{1}{N^{\frac{2k-1}{k-1}}}
and
\|h_{m}^{\prime\prime\prime}\|_{L^{\infty}[C_{k}N,N]}\lesssim\frac{1}{N^{\frac{3k-2}{k-1}}}
. By choosing
C_{k}
sufficiently close to 1 we can guarantee that
m-n=o(N).
This in turn makes the cubic error term in (
5
) much smaller than the quadratic term, showing that

|f^{\prime}(m)|\sim\frac{(m-n)^{2}}{N^{\frac{2k-1}{k-1}}}.

Finally, using Lemma
3.3
and the Mean Value Theorem we find that

|f^{\prime}(x)-f^{\prime}(m)|\sim|x-m|\frac{m-n}{N^{\frac{2k-1}{k-1}}}.

This quantity is
o(\frac{(m-n)^{2}}{N^{\frac{2k-1}{k-1}}})
when
|x-m|\ll m-n
, showing that
|f^{\prime}(x)|\sim\frac{(m-n)^{2}}{N^{\frac{2k-1}{k-1}}}.
A similar argument works when
x
is near
n
.

∎

We are now ready to estimate the coefficients
d_{n,m}
. We start with the easy regime where there are no cancellations.

Proposition 3.5
.

Assume
|n-m|\leq N^{\frac{2k-1}{3(k-1)}}
. Then
|d_{n,m}|{\;\lessapprox}\;\frac{1}{N^{\frac{1}{k-1}}}
.

Proof.

Triangle inequality leads to the estimate

|d_{n,m}|\lesssim\frac{1}{N^{\frac{1}{k-1}}}\sum_{x\sim N\atop{x\not=n,m}}\frac{1}{\sqrt{|n-x|}}\frac{1}{\sqrt{|m-x|}}.

When
|x-\frac{n+m}{2}|\lesssim|m-n|
, we use that
\max\{|x-n|,|x-m|\}\gtrsim|n-m|
. When
|x-\frac{n+m}{2}|\gg|m-n|
we use that
|x-n|,|x-m|\sim|x-\frac{n+m}{2}|
.

∎

Before we move to discuss the next regime, let us pause to understand the sharpness in the estimate from Proposition
3.5
. Let us assume
|n-m|\sim N^{\frac{2k-1}{3(k-1)}}
. Let
x_{0}\in(n,m)
be the critical point of
f=f_{n,m}
. Then, using Lemma
3.3
we write for each
|x-x_{0}|\ll m-n

f(x)=f(x_{0})+O(\frac{m-n}{N^{\frac{2k-1}{k-1}}})(x-x_{0})^{2}=f(x_{0})+o(1).

It follows that when evaluating the sum coming from such
x
(as part of the whole sum defining
d_{n,m}
), there is no cancellation. More precisely

|\sum_{|x-x_{0}|\ll m-n}\frac{1}{\sqrt{|(n-x)(m-x)|}}\frac{1}{[(n+x)(m+x)]^{\frac{1}{2(k-1)}}}e(f(x))|

\sim\sum_{|x-x_{0}|\ll m-n}\frac{1}{\sqrt{|(n-x)(m-x)|}}\frac{1}{[(n+x)(m+x)]^{\frac{1}{2(k-1)}}}\sim\frac{1}{N^{\frac{1}{k-1}}}.

We will next see that cancellations occur when
m
and
n
are further apart.

Proposition 3.6
.

If
|n-m|\geq N^{\frac{2k-1}{3(k-1)}}
we have

|d_{n,m}|\lesssim\frac{N^{\frac{2k-3}{2(k-1)}}}{|n-m|^{3/2}}
.

Proof.

We may assume that
n<m
. We distinguish four regimes. Let
I_{1}
,
I_{2}
be intervals of length
o(m-n)
centered at
n
and
m
respectively, such that (
4
) holds. Let
J_{1}
,
J_{2}
be intervals of smaller length
o(\frac{N^{\frac{2k-1}{k-1}}}{|n-m|^{2}})
centered at
n
and
m
respectively. Note that
J_{1}\subset I_{1}
and
J_{2}\subset I_{2}
.

The choice of
J_{1}
and
J_{2}
is motivated by the fact that there is no cancellation for the sums of
e(f(x))
when
x
is restricted to these intervals. Indeed, Lemma
3.4
shows that when
x\in J_{1}

|f(x)-f(n)|\sim\frac{|J_{1}|(m-n)^{2}}{N^{\frac{2k-1}{k-1}}}\ll 1.

As a general rule throughout the forthcoming argument, we will use the fact that the sequence

w_{x}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{|(n-x)(m-x)|}}\frac{1}{[(n+x)(m+x)]^{\frac{1}{2(k-1)}}}

changes monotonicity
O(1)
many times in the interval
[C_{k}N,N]
. Thus, for each interval
I
we have that
\|w_{x}\|_{V^{1}(I)}\lesssim\|w_{x}\|_{l^{\infty}(I)}
.

The reader will notice that the difference between
C_{sta}
and
\overline{C_{sta}}
in the definition on
c_{n,m}
does not affect the argument.

1. Let
x
be in the core of
[n,m]
, more precisely
x\in R_{1}=[n,m]\setminus(I_{1}\cup I_{2})
. This interval contains the critical point of
f
, so the first derivative test is not applicable. However, we may use the second derivative test, Theorem
5.2
to estimate for each
I\subset R_{1}

|\sum_{x\in I}e(f(x))|\lesssim\lambda^{1/2}|I|+\lambda^{-1/2},

where, according to Lemma
3.3
we have
\lambda\sim\frac{m-n}{N^{\frac{2k-1}{k-1}}}
. The term
\lambda^{-1/2}
dominates since
|I|\leq m-n\lesssim\lambda^{-1}
. Moreover,
\|w_{x}\|_{l^{\infty}(R_{1})}\sim\frac{1}{N^{\frac{1}{k-1}}(m-n)}
. Combining this with Lemma
5.5
we are led to the desired estimate

|\sum_{x\in R_{1}}w_{x}e(f(x))|\lesssim\lambda^{-1/2}\|w_{x}\|_{l^{\infty}(R_{1})}\sim\frac{N^{\frac{2k-3}{2(k-1)}}}{|n-m|^{3/2}}.

2. An identical argument works for
R_{2}=[C_{k}N,N]\setminus(I_{1}\cup I_{2}\cup[n,m])
. The term
\lambda^{-1/2}
still dominates and we have the same upper bound for
\|w_{x}\|_{l^{\infty}(R_{2})}
as before. While we could do better in this regime by using the first derivative test, the previous upper bound is good enough for our purposes.

3. Let us now restrict
x
to one of the four intervals comprising the set
R_{3}=(I_{1}\setminus J_{1})\cup(I_{2}\setminus J_{2})
. Note first that the
l^{\infty}
norm of the weights gets larger in this regime, due to the proximity of
x
to either
n
or
m

\|w_{x}\|_{l^{\infty}(R_{3})}\sim\frac{1}{N^{\frac{1}{k-1}}\sqrt{m-n}\sqrt{|J_{1}|}}\sim\frac{\sqrt{m-n}}{N^{\frac{2k+1}{2(k-1)}}}.

An application of the second derivative test would lead to the bound

|\sum_{x\in R_{3}}w_{x}e(f(x))|\lesssim\frac{\sqrt{m-n}}{N^{\frac{2k+1}{2(k-1)}}}\lambda^{-1/2}\sim\frac{1}{N^{\frac{1}{k-1}}}.

However, this is significantly worse than what we need to prove. To achieve the desired result, we use the first derivative test

|\sum_{x\in R_{3}}w_{x}e(f(x))|\lesssim\frac{\sqrt{m-n}}{N^{\frac{2k+1}{2(k-1)}}}\times\frac{N^{\frac{2k-1}{k-1}}}{(m-n)^{2}}\sim\frac{N^{\frac{2k-3}{2(k-1)}}}{(m-n)^{3/2}}.

4. When
x\in R_{4}=J_{1}\cup J_{2}
, the sharp bound follows using the triangle inequality

|\sum_{x\in R_{4}}w_{x}e(f(x))|\lesssim\sum_{x\in R_{4}}w_{x}\sim\frac{1}{N^{\frac{1}{k-1}}\sqrt{m-n}}\sum_{1\leq p\ll\frac{N^{\frac{2k-1}{k-1}}}{|n-m|^{2}}}\frac{1}{\sqrt{p}}\sim\frac{N^{\frac{2k-3}{2(k-1)}}}{(m-n)^{3/2}}.

∎

We are finally in position to prove (
2
). For each
n\in[C_{k}N,N]
, we use Lemma
3.5
and
3.6
to write

\displaystyle\sum_{C_{k}N\leq m\leq N}|d_{n,m}|

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{m:\;|m-n|\leq N^{\frac{2k-1}{3(k-1)}}}|d_{n,m}|+\sum_{m:\;|m-n|\geq N^{\frac{2k-1}{3(k-1)}}}|d_{n,m}|

\displaystyle{\;\lessapprox}\;N^{\frac{2k-1}{3(k-1)}-\frac{1}{k-1}}+{N^{\frac{2k-3}{2(k-1)}}}\sum_{N^{\frac{2k-1}{3(k-1)}}\leq p\lesssim N}\frac{1}{p^{3/2}}

\displaystyle\sim N^{\frac{2(k-2)}{3(k-1)}}.

We combine this estimate with Lemma
5.4
to write

\displaystyle\sum_{C_{k}N\leq n,m\leq N}a_{n}\overline{a_{m}}c_{n,m}\leq(\max_{n}\sum_{m}|d_{n,m}|)^{1/2}\|a_{n}\|_{2}^{2}\lesssim N^{\frac{k-2}{3(k-1)}}\|a_{n}\|_{2}^{2}.

Remark 3.7
.

It seems possible that the upper bound (in addition to the already mentioned lower bound) in Theorem
1.2
would hold for all
C^{k+1}
functions with finitely many zeros of
\phi^{\prime}
, all of order
k-1
. To keep things concise, we have decided to not pursue this line of investigation. However, there is a simple argument for
k=3

\phi^{\prime}(0)=\phi^{\prime\prime}(0)=0
and
\phi^{\prime\prime\prime}(0)\not=0
, so that
\phi(x)=\frac{\phi^{\prime\prime\prime}(0)}{3!}x^{3}+O(x^{4})
. We may assume 0 is the only critical point of

The estimate in the nonstationary regime

\|\sum_{n\sim N}a_{n}e(nx+n^{2}\phi(x))\|_{L^{2}(|x|\gg N^{-1/2})}{\;\lessapprox}\;\|a_{n}\|_{2}

is an immediate consequence of the fact that the critical point of

\phi_{n,m}(x)=(n-m)[x+(n+m)\phi(x)]

lies in the interval
|x|\lesssim N^{-1/2}
when

. Note further that we have

\sum_{n=1}^{N}\|w_{n+1}(x)-w_{n}(x)\|_{L^{\infty}(|x|\lesssim N^{-1/2})}\lesssim 1,

(6)

where
w_{n}(x)=e(n^{2}(\phi(x)-\frac{\phi^{\prime\prime\prime}(0)}{3!}x^{3}))
. Thus, using summation by parts and Cauchy–Schwarz we have

\|\sum_{n=1}^{N}a_{n}e(nx+n^{2}\phi(x))\|_{L^{2}(|x|\lesssim N^{-1/2})}^{2}\lesssim

\int_{|x|\lesssim N^{-1/2}}(\sum_{m=1}^{N}|w_{m+1}(x)-w_{m}(x)|)(\sum_{m=1}^{N}|w_{m+1}(x)-w_{m}(x)||\sum_{n=1}^{m}a_{n}e(nx+n^{2}\frac{\phi^{\prime\prime\prime}(0)}{6}x^{3})|^{2})dx\lesssim

\|\sum_{m=1}^{N}|w_{m+1}(x)-w_{m}(x)|\|_{L^{\infty}(|x|\lesssim\frac{1}{N^{1/2}})}\int_{|x|\lesssim\frac{1}{N^{1/2}}}\sum_{m=1}^{N}|w_{m+1}(x)-w_{m}(x)||\sum_{n=1}^{m}a_{n}e(nx+n^{2}\frac{\phi^{\prime\prime\prime}(0)}{6}x^{3})|^{2}dx\lesssim

(\sum_{m=1}^{N}\|w_{m+1}(x)-w_{m}(x)\|_{L^{\infty}(|x|\lesssim N^{-1/2})})^{2}\max_{m\leq N}\int_{|x|\lesssim N^{-1/2}}|\sum_{n=1}^{m}a_{n}e(nx+n^{2}\frac{\phi^{\prime\prime\prime}(0)}{6}x^{3})|^{2}dx.

This is
{\;\lessapprox}\;N^{1/6}\|a_{n}\|_{2}^{2}
, due to Theorem
1.2
and (
6
).

The two dimensional analogue of Theorem

Theorem 4.1
.

Assume
\phi:[-1,1]^{2}\to{\mathbb{R}}
is

and has nonzero Hessian

\inf_{(x,y)\in[-1,1]^{2}}|\phi_{xx}(x,y)\phi_{yy}(x,y)-\phi_{xy}^{2}(x,y)|>0.

Then for each

\|\sum_{n,m=-N}^{N}a_{n,m}e(nx+my+(n^{2}+m^{2})\phi(x,y))\|_{L^{2}([-1,1]^{2})}{\;\lessapprox}\;\|a_{n,m}\|_{2}.

Let us show that the result may fail for the zero curvature cylinder
\phi(x,y)=-x^{3}
.

Theorem 4.2
.

For each
N
there is a choice of coefficients
a_{n,m}
such that

\|\sum_{n,m=-N}^{N}a_{n,m}e(nx+my-(n^{2}+m^{2})x^{3})\|_{L^{2}([-1,1]^{2})}\gtrsim N^{\frac{1}{12}}\|a_{n,m}\|_{2}.

Proof.

Write the phase as

(nx-n^{2}x^{3})+(mx-m^{2}x^{3})+(m-\frac{N}{2})(y-x)+\frac{N(y-x)}{2}.

The proof of Theorem
2.1
shows that for appropriate functions
f_{1},f_{2}
and for
|n-\frac{N}{2}|,|m-\frac{N}{2}|\ll N^{5/6}
,
|x-\frac{1}{\sqrt{3N}}|\ll\frac{1}{N^{2/3}}
we have

nx-n^{2}x^{3}=f_{1}(n)+f_{2}(x)+o(1),

mx-m^{2}x^{3}=f_{1}(m)+f_{2}(x)+o(1).

Let
a_{n,m}=e(-f_{1}(n)-f_{1}(m))
if
|n-\frac{N}{2}|,|m-\frac{N}{2}|\ll N^{5/6}
, and
a_{n,m}=0
otherwise.
It follows that we have constructive interference

|\sum_{n,m=-N}^{N}a_{n,m}e(nx+my-(n^{2}+m^{2})x^{3})|\sim N^{5/3}

on the set
S=\{(x,y):\;|x-\frac{1}{\sqrt{3N}}|\ll\frac{1}{N^{2/3}},\;|y-x|\ll N^{-5/6}\}
. The result is now immediate.

∎

Theorem 4.5
.

Assume
\phi:[-1,1]^{2}\to{\mathbb{R}}
has nonzero Hessian. Then for each

we have

\|\sum_{n=-N}^{N}a_{n}e(nx+n^{2}y+n^{3}\phi(x,y))\|_{L^{6}([-1,1]^{2})}\lesssim_{\epsilon}N^{\epsilon}\|a_{n}\|_{2}.

(8)

produces constructive interference near the origin (

The question left open in

Theorem 4.6
.

For each
N
there is a sequence
a_{n}
such that

\|\sum_{n=-N}^{N}a_{n}e(nx+n^{2}y-n^{3}(y+x^{3})^{3})\|_{L^{6}([-1,1]^{2})}\gtrsim N^{1/36}\|a_{n}\|_{2}.

Proof.

With the change of variables
u=x
,

, we have

\|\sum_{n=-N}^{N}a_{n}e(nx+n^{2}y-n^{3}(y+x^{3})^{3})\|_{L^{6}([-1,1]^{2})}\geq

\|\sum_{n=-N}^{N}a_{n}e((nu-n^{2}u^{3})+(n^{2}v-n^{3}v^{3}))\|_{L^{6}([-1/2,1/2]^{2})}.

Recall that if
|n-\frac{N}{2}|\ll N^{5/6}
and
|u-\frac{1}{\sqrt{3N}}|\ll N^{-2/3}
we have

nu-n^{2}u^{3}=f_{1}(n)+f_{2}(u)+o(1).

A similar argument will show that with
v_{N}=\frac{2}{3\sqrt{N}}
we have

\theta(v,n)=n^{2}v-n^{3}v^{3}+n^{3}v_{N}^{3}=g_{1}(n)+g_{2}(v)+o(1)

whenever
(v,n)\in J_{1}\times J_{2}
defined by
|v-v_{N}|\ll\delta=N^{-1-\frac{2}{3}}
and
|n-\frac{N}{2}|\ll M=N^{5/6}
. This follows as before from Taylor’s formula with cubic error terms, since

\theta_{vn}(v_{N},\frac{N}{2})=0,

\|\theta_{vnn}\|_{L^{\infty}(J_{1}\times J_{2})}\lesssim 1,\;\;\;\text{and }\;\;\|\theta_{vnn}\|_{L^{\infty}(J_{1}\times J_{2})}\delta M^{2}=o(1),

\|\theta_{vvn}\|_{L^{\infty}(J_{1}\times J_{2})}\lesssim N^{3/2},\;\;\;\text{and }\;\;\|\theta_{vvn}\|_{L^{\infty}(J_{1}\times J_{2})}\delta^{2}M=o(1),

\|\theta_{vvv}\|_{L^{\infty}(J_{1}\times J_{2})}\lesssim N^{3},\;\;\;\text{and }\;\;\|\theta_{vvv}\|_{L^{\infty}(J_{1}\times J_{2})}\delta^{3}=o(1),

and

\|\theta_{nnn}\|_{L^{\infty}(J_{1}\times J_{2})}\lesssim\max_{v\in J_{1}}|v^{3}-v_{N}^{3}|\sim\frac{1}{N^{8/3}},\;\;\;\text{and }\;\;\|\theta_{nnn}\|_{L^{\infty}(J_{1}\times J_{2})}M^{3}=o(1).

It follows that for an appropriate choice of
a_{n}
restricted to
|n-\frac{N}{2}|\ll N^{5/6}
we have

\int_{|u-\frac{1}{\sqrt{3N}}|\ll N^{-2/3}\atop{|v-\frac{2}{3\sqrt{N}}|\ll N^{-5/3}}}|\sum_{n=-N}^{N}a_{n}e((nu-n^{2}u^{3})+(n^{2}v-n^{3}v^{3}))|^{6}dudv\sim\frac{N^{5}}{N^{7/3}}\sim N^{1/6}\|a_{n}\|_{2}^{6}.

∎

\|\sum_{n=-N}^{N}a_{n}(n^{2}y+n^{3}y^{2})\|_{L^{7}([-1,1]^{2})}\lesssim_{\epsilon}N^{\frac{3}{14}+\epsilon}\|a_{n}\|_{2}.

4.5
provides a positive answer in three dimensions to the following conjecture proposed in

Conjecture 4.7
.

If
\Sigma

Theorem 4.8
.

Let

Apart from

Proof.

When

1.1
with the trivial
L^{\infty}

.

Let us now focus on

2.1
when

|x|\leq\frac{1}{N}
when

.

1.2
and the trivial
L^{\infty}
bound, it suffices to prove the

estimate

\|\sum_{n=-N}^{N}a_{n}e(nx-n^{2}x^{k})\|_{L^{4}(-1,1)}\lesssim_{\epsilon}N^{\frac{1}{4}+\epsilon}\|a_{n}\|_{2}.

The argument in Section

shows that

\|\sum_{n=-N}^{N}a_{n}e(nx-n^{2}x^{k})\|_{L^{2}(|x|\gg N^{-\frac{1}{k-1}})}{\;\lessapprox}\;\|a_{n}\|_{2}.

\|\sup_{|t|\lesssim 1/N}|\sum_{n=-N}^{N}a_{n}e(nx+n^{2}t)|\|_{L^{4}(-1,1)}\lesssim_{\epsilon}N^{\frac{1}{4}+\epsilon}\|a_{n}\|_{2},

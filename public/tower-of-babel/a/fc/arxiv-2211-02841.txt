The generalized inverses of tensors via the C-product
Hongwei Jin, Shumin Xu, Hongjie Jiang, Xiaoji Liu

Abstract
This paper studies the issues about the generalized inverses of tensors under the C-product. The aim of this paper is threefold. Firstly, this paper present the definition of the Moore-Penrose inverse, Drazin inverse of tensors under the C-product. Moreover, the inverse along a tensor is also introduced. Secondly, this paper gives some other expressions of the generalized inverses of tensors by using several decomposition forms of tensors. Finally, the algorithms for computing the Moore-Penrose inverse, Drazin inverse of tensors and the inverse along a tensor are established. Keywords : C-product, Tensors, Moore-Penrose, Drazin inverse, Inverse along a tensor AMS classification: 15A18, 15A69.

Introduction

In recent years, the studies of tensors or the multidimensional array have become more popular. A complex tensor can be regarded as a multidimensional array of data, which takes the form
\mathcal{A}=(a_{{i_{1}}\ldots{i_{p}}})\in\mathbbmss{C}^{n_{1}\times n_{2}\times\cdots\times n_{p}}
. The order of a tensor is the number of dimensions which is also called ways or modes. Therefore, the well-known vectors and matrices are called first-order tensors and second-order tensors. This paper studies the third-order tensors.

This paper is organized as follows. In Section 2, we give the terms and symbols needed to be used in this paper. Then, we introduce the C-product of two tensors and some properties of it. In Section 3, we firstly define the Moore-Penrose inverse of tensors via the C-product. Then, we provide some decompositions of the tensor, including C-SVD, C-QR decomposition, C-Schur decomposition, C-full rank decomposition, C-QDR decomposition and C-HS decomposition. Furthermore, we use these decompositions to give the expressions for the Moore-Penrose inverse of tensors. In Section 4, we study the Drazin inverse of the tensor under the C-product. This part gives the definition and a few properties for the Drazin inverse of tensors, and provide several expressions for the Drazin inverse of tensors. In Section 5, we define the inverse along a tensor under the C-product. Some expressions of the class of the inverse are obtained. Moreover, an algorithm for computing the inverse along a tensor is built.
In the last section, we establish an application on higher-order Markov Chains concerning the group inverse of the tensor.

L(\mathcal{A})=\mathcal{A}\times_{3}\mathbf{M}\Leftrightarrow L(\mathcal{A})_{(3)}=\mathbf{M}\mathcal{A}_{(3)}

by (
4
) and (
5
). Then,

L(\mathcal{A})_{(3)}=\left[\begin{array}[]{cccccccccccccc}\mathcal{L}_{111}&\mathcal{L}_{211}&\cdots&\mathcal{L}_{n_{1}11}&0&\mathcal{L}_{221}&\cdots&\mathcal{L}_{n_{1}21}&0&0&\mathcal{L}_{331}&\cdots&\mathcal{L}_{n_{1}31}&\cdots\\
\mathcal{L}_{112}&\mathcal{L}_{212}&\cdots&\mathcal{L}_{n_{1}12}&0&\mathcal{L}_{222}&\cdots&\mathcal{L}_{n_{1}22}&0&0&\mathcal{L}_{332}&\cdots&\mathcal{L}_{n_{1}32}&\cdots\\
\vdots&\vdots&&\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&&\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&&\vdots\\
\mathcal{L}_{11n_{3}}&\mathcal{L}_{21n_{3}}&\cdots&\mathcal{L}_{n_{1}1n_{3}}&0&\mathcal{L}_{22n_{3}}&\cdots&\mathcal{L}_{n_{1}2n_{3}}&0&0&\mathcal{L}_{33n_{3}}&\cdots&\mathcal{L}_{n_{1}3n_{3}}&\cdots\\
\end{array}\right],

which implies
L(\mathcal{A})
is an F-lower tensor.
∎

3
The Moore-Penrose inverse of tensors under the C-product

In this part, we will give some expressions of the Moore-Penrose inverse by using the C-SVD, C-QR decomposition, C-Schur decomposition, C-full rank decomposition, C-QDR decomposition and C-HS decomposition. Then, we establish an algorithm to compute the Moore-Penrose inverse based on the C-SVD of a tensor
\mathcal{A}
.

3.1
The expressions of the Moore-Penrose inverse of tensors

Definition 3.1
.

Let
\mathcal{A}\in\mathbbmss{C}^{n_{1}\times n_{2}\times n_{3}}
. If there exists a tensor
\mathcal{X}\in\mathbbmss{C}^{n_{2}\times n_{1}\times n_{3}}
such that

\mathcal{A}*_{c}\mathcal{X}*_{c}\mathcal{A}=\mathcal{A},\qquad\mathcal{X}*_{c}\mathcal{A}*_{c}\mathcal{X}=\mathcal{X},\qquad(\mathcal{A}*_{c}\mathcal{X})^{H}=\mathcal{A}*_{c}\mathcal{X},\qquad(\mathcal{X}*_{c}\mathcal{A})^{H}=\mathcal{X}*_{c}\mathcal{A},

(7)

then
\mathcal{X}
is called the
Moore-Penrose inverse
of the tensor
\mathcal{A}
and is denoted by
\mathcal{A}^{\dagger}
.

For
\mathcal{A}\in\mathbbmss{C}^{n_{1}\times n_{2}\times n_{3}}
, denote
\mathcal{A}{\{i,j,\ldots,k\}}

the set of all
\mathcal{X}\in\mathbbmss{C}^{n_{2}\times n_{1}\times n_{3}}
which satisfy equations (
i
), (
j
),
\ldots
, (
k
) of
(\ref{mp})
. In this case,
\mathcal{X}
is a
\{i,j,\ldots,k\}
-inverse.

Theorem 3.1
.

[
25
]

Let
\mathcal{A}\in\mathbbmss{C}^{n_{1}\times n_{2}\times n_{3}}
. Then there exist
unitary tensors
\mathcal{U}\in\mathbbmss{C}^{n_{1}\times n_{1}\times n_{3}}
and
\mathcal{V}\in\mathbbmss{C}^{n_{2}\times n_{2}\times n_{3}}

such that

\mathcal{A}=\mathcal{U}*_{c}\mathcal{S}*_{c}\mathcal{V}^{H},

where
\mathcal{S}
is an
n_{1}\times n_{2}\times n_{3}
F-diagonal tensor. We call this decomposition the C-SVD of
\mathcal{A}
.

Theorem 3.2
.

The Moore-Penrose inverse of an arbitrary tensor
\mathcal{A}\in\mathbbmss{C}^{n_{1}\times n_{2}\times n_{3}}
exists and is unique.

Proof.

By Lemma
2.1
, one has

(\mathbf{C}_{n_{3}}\otimes\mathbf{I}_{n_{1}})\matt(\mathcal{A})(\mathbf{C}^{-1}_{n_{3}}\otimes\mathbf{I}_{n_{2}})=\left[\begin{array}[]{cccc}L(\mathcal{A})^{(1)}&&&\\
&L(\mathcal{A})^{(2)}&&\\
&&\ddots&\\
&&&L(\mathcal{A})^{(n_{3})}\\
\end{array}\right].

Let
L(\mathcal{A})^{(i)}=\mathbf{U}_{i}\mathbf{\Sigma}_{i}\mathbf{V}^{H}_{i}
be the singular value decomposition of
L(\mathcal{A})^{(i)}
,
i=1,...,n_{3}
. Thus, we have

\displaystyle(\mathbf{C}_{n_{3}}\otimes\mathbf{I}_{n_{1}})\matt(\mathcal{A})(\mathbf{C}^{-1}_{n_{3}}\otimes\mathbf{I}_{n_{2}})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left[\begin{array}[]{cccc}L(\mathcal{A})^{(1)}&&&\\
&L(\mathcal{A})^{(2)}&&\\
&&\ddots&\\
&&&L(\mathcal{A})^{(n_{3})}\\
\end{array}\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left[\begin{array}[]{cccc}\mathbf{U}_{1}\mathbf{\Sigma}_{1}\mathbf{V}^{H}_{1}&&&\\
&\mathbf{U}_{2}\mathbf{\Sigma}_{2}\mathbf{V}^{H}_{2}&&\\
&&\ddots&\\
&&&\mathbf{U}_{n_{3}}\mathbf{\Sigma}_{n_{3}}\mathbf{V}^{H}_{n_{3}}\\
\end{array}\right].

For each

\mathbf{\Sigma}_{i}=\left[\begin{array}[]{ccccccc}\sigma^{i}_{1}&&&&&&\\
&\ddots&&&&&\\
&&&\sigma^{i}_{r_{i}}&&&\\
&&&&0&&\\
&&&&&\ddots&\\
&&&&&&0\\
\end{array}\right],

\sigma^{i}_{j},j=1,2,...,{r_{i}}
,
r_{i}=rank(L(\mathcal{A})^{(i)})
are singular values of
L(\mathcal{A})^{(i)}
. We define the matrices
\mathbf{R}_{i}
,
i=1,...,n_{3}
, as

\mathbf{R}_{i}=\left[\begin{array}[]{ccccccc}\frac{1}{\sigma^{i}_{1}}&&&&&&\\
&\ddots&&&&&\\
&&&\frac{1}{\sigma^{i}_{r_{i}}}&&&\\
&&&&0&&\\
&&&&&\ddots&\\
&&&&&&0\\
\end{array}\right].

Observe that
\mathbf{R}_{i}=\mathbf{\Sigma}_{i}^{\dagger}
for
i=1,\ldots,n_{3}
. Let

\mathbf{X}_{i}=\mathbf{V}_{i}\mathbf{R}_{i}\mathbf{U}_{i}^{H}
for
i=1,\ldots,n_{3}
. Now, we have

\begin{bmatrix}\mathbf{X}_{1}&&\\
&\ddots&\\
&&\mathbf{X}_{n_{3}}\\
\end{bmatrix}=\begin{bmatrix}\mathbf{V}_{1}&&\\
&\ddots&\\
&&\mathbf{V}_{n_{3}}\\
\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}\mathbf{R}_{1}&&\\
&\ddots&\\
&&\mathbf{R}_{n_{3}}\\
\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}\mathbf{U}^{H}_{1}&&\\
&\ddots&\\
&&\mathbf{U}^{H}_{n_{3}}\\
\end{bmatrix}.

Thus,

\displaystyle\ten((\mathbf{C}^{-1}_{n_{3}}\otimes\mathbf{I}_{n_{2}})\begin{bmatrix}\mathbf{X}_{1}&&\\
&\ddots&\\
&&\mathbf{X}_{n_{3}}\\
\end{bmatrix}(\mathbf{C}_{n_{3}}\otimes\mathbf{I}_{n_{1}}))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\ten((\mathbf{C}^{-1}_{n_{3}}\otimes\mathbf{I}_{n_{2}})\begin{bmatrix}\mathbf{V}_{1}&&\\
&\ddots&\\
&&\mathbf{V}_{n_{3}}\\
\end{bmatrix}(\mathbf{C}_{n_{3}}\otimes\mathbf{I}_{n_{1}}))

\displaystyle\times

\displaystyle\ten((\mathbf{C}^{-1}_{n_{3}}\otimes\mathbf{I}_{n_{2}})\begin{bmatrix}\mathbf{R}_{1}&&\\
&\ddots&\\
&&\mathbf{R}_{n_{3}}\\
\end{bmatrix}(\mathbf{C}_{n_{3}}\otimes\mathbf{I}_{n_{1}}))

\displaystyle\times

\displaystyle\ten((\mathbf{C}^{-1}_{n_{3}}\otimes\mathbf{I}_{n_{2}})\begin{bmatrix}\mathbf{U}^{H}_{1}&&\\
&\ddots&\\
&&\mathbf{U}^{H}_{n_{3}}\\
\end{bmatrix}(\mathbf{C}_{n_{3}}\otimes\mathbf{I}_{n_{1}})),

that is
\mathcal{X}=\mathcal{V}*_{c}\mathcal{R}*_{c}\mathcal{U}^{H}
. It is easy to check that
\mathcal{X}
satisfies
(\ref{mp})
, which means the Moore-Penrose inverse of a tensor
\mathcal{A}
exists.

On the other hand, suppose
\mathcal{X}_{1}
and
\mathcal{X}_{2}
both are the solutions of
(\ref{mp})
. Then, we have

\displaystyle\mathcal{X}_{1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathcal{X}_{1}*_{c}\mathcal{A}*_{c}\mathcal{X}_{1}=\mathcal{X}_{1}*_{c}(\mathcal{A}*_{c}\mathcal{X}_{2}*_{c}\mathcal{A})*_{c}\mathcal{X}_{1}=\mathcal{X}_{1}*_{c}(\mathcal{A}*_{c}\mathcal{X}_{2})^{H}*_{c}(\mathcal{A}*_{c}\mathcal{X}_{1})^{H}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathcal{X}_{1}*_{c}(\mathcal{A}*_{c}\mathcal{X}_{1}*_{c}\mathcal{A}*_{c}\mathcal{X}_{2})^{H}=\mathcal{X}_{1}*_{c}(\mathcal{A}*_{c}\mathcal{X}_{2})^{H}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathcal{X}_{1}*_{c}\mathcal{A}*_{c}\mathcal{X}_{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathcal{X}_{1}*_{c}(\mathcal{A}*_{c}\mathcal{X}_{2}*_{c}\mathcal{A})*_{c}\mathcal{X}_{2}=(\mathcal{X}_{1}*_{c}\mathcal{A})^{H}*_{c}(\mathcal{X}_{2}*_{c}\mathcal{A})^{H}*_{c}\mathcal{X}_{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(\mathcal{X}_{2}*_{c}\mathcal{A}*_{c}\mathcal{X}_{1}*_{c}\mathcal{A})^{H}*_{c}\mathcal{X}_{2}=(\mathcal{X}_{2}*_{c}\mathcal{A})^{H}*_{c}\mathcal{X}_{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathcal{X}_{2}*_{c}\mathcal{A}*_{c}\mathcal{X}_{2}=\mathcal{X}_{2}.

Therefore, the Moore-Penrose inverse of
\mathcal{A}
is unique.
∎

Theorem 3.3
.

Let
\mathcal{A}\in\mathbbmss{C}^{n_{1}\times n_{2}\times n_{3}}
and
\mathcal{A}=\mathcal{U}*_{c}\mathcal{S}*_{c}\mathcal{V}^{H}
be the C-SVD of
\mathcal{A}
.
Then,

\mathcal{A}^{\dagger}=\mathcal{V}*_{c}\mathcal{S}^{\dagger}*_{c}\mathcal{U}^{H}.

Proof.

It is easy to check that
\mathcal{V}*_{c}\mathcal{S}^{\dagger}*_{c}\mathcal{U}^{H}
holds for the four equations of
(\ref{mp})
.
∎

Theorem 3.4
.

Let
\mathcal{A}\in\mathbbmss{C}^{n_{1}\times n_{2}\times n_{3}}
. Then there exist
a unitary tensor
\mathcal{Q}\in\mathbbmss{C}^{n_{1}\times n_{1}\times n_{3}}
and an F-upper tensor
\mathcal{R}\in\mathbbmss{C}^{n_{1}\times n_{2}\times n_{3}}

such that

\mathcal{A}=\mathcal{Q}*_{c}\mathcal{R},

which is called the C-QR decomposition of
\mathcal{A}
.

Proof.

Let
\widehat{\mathcal{A}}=L(\mathcal{A})
,
\widehat{\mathcal{Q}}=L(\mathcal{Q})
and
\widehat{\mathcal{R}}=L(\mathcal{R})
. Suppose
\widehat{\mathcal{A}}^{(i)}=\mathbf{Q}_{i}\mathbf{R}_{i}=\widehat{\mathcal{Q}}^{(i)}\widehat{\mathcal{R}}^{(i)}
,
i=1,2,...,n_{3}
, are the QR decomposition of
\widehat{\mathcal{A}}^{(i)}
. Hence,
\mathcal{A}=\mathcal{Q}*_{c}\mathcal{R}
. Furthermore, one has
L(\mathcal{Q}*_{c}\mathcal{Q}^{H})=L(\mathcal{Q})\triangle L(\mathcal{Q}^{H})
. Thus,

L(\mathcal{Q})^{(i)}L(\mathcal{Q}^{H})^{(i)}=\widehat{\mathcal{Q}}^{(i)}(\widehat{\mathcal{Q}}^{(i)})^{H}=\mathbf{I}_{n_{1}}=L(\mathcal{I})^{(i)},\ i=1,2,...,n_{3}.

This implies
\mathcal{Q}*_{c}\mathcal{Q}^{H}=\mathcal{I}
, that is
\mathcal{Q}
is a unitary tensor. On the other hand,
\mathbf{R}_{i}
are upper triangular matrices and so are
\widehat{\mathcal{R}}^{(i)}
. This implies
\mathcal{R}
is an F-upper tensor.
∎

.

\mathcal{A}\in\mathbbmss{C}^{n_{1}\times n_{2}\times n_{3}}

.
∎

Theorem 3.6
.

Let
\mathcal{A}\in\mathbbmss{C}^{n\times n\times n_{3}}
. Then there exist

\mathcal{Q}\in\mathbbmss{C}^{n\times n\times n_{3}}
and an F-upper tensor
\mathcal{T}\in\mathbbmss{C}^{n\times n\times n_{3}}

such that

\mathcal{A}
.

Let
\widehat{\mathcal{A}}=L(\mathcal{A})
,
\widehat{\mathcal{Q}}=L(\mathcal{Q})
and
\widehat{\mathcal{T}}=L(\mathcal{T})
. Suppose

,
i=1,2,...,n_{3}
, are the Schur decomposition of
\widehat{\mathcal{A}}^{(i)}
. Thus,
\mathcal{A}=\mathcal{Q}^{H}*_{c}\mathcal{T}*_{c}\mathcal{Q}
. By the proof of Theorem
3.4
,
\mathcal{Q}
is a unitary tensor. On the other hand,
\mathbf{T}_{i}
are upper triangular matrices and so are
\widehat{\mathcal{T}}^{(i)}
. This implies
\mathcal{T}
is an F-upper tensor.
∎

Theorem 3.7
.

Let
\mathcal{A}\in\mathbbmss{C}^{n\times n\times n_{3}}
and
\mathcal{A}=\mathcal{Q}^{H}*_{c}\mathcal{T}*_{c}\mathcal{Q}
be the C-Schur decomposition of
\mathcal{A}
.
Then,

\mathcal{A}^{\dagger}=\mathcal{Q}^{H}*_{c}\mathcal{T}^{\dagger}*_{c}\mathcal{Q}.

Proof.

Now, we will check that
\mathcal{Q}^{H}*_{c}\mathcal{T}^{\dagger}*_{c}\mathcal{Q}
holds for the four equations of
(\ref{mp})
. Let
\mathcal{X}=\mathcal{Q}^{H}*_{c}\mathcal{T}^{\dagger}*_{c}\mathcal{Q}
, we will have

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}*_{c}\mathcal{X}*_{c}\mathcal{A}=\mathcal{Q}^{H}*_{c}\mathcal{T}*_{c}\mathcal{Q}*_{c}\mathcal{Q}^{H}*_{c}\mathcal{T}^{\dagger}*_{c}\mathcal{Q}*_{c}\mathcal{Q}^{H}*_{c}\mathcal{T}*_{c}\mathcal{Q}=\mathcal{Q}^{H}*_{c}\mathcal{T}*_{c}\mathcal{T}^{\dagger}*_{c}\mathcal{T}*_{c}\mathcal{Q}=\mathcal{Q}^{H}*_{c}\mathcal{T}*_{c}\mathcal{Q}=\mathcal{A},

\displaystyle\mathcal{X}*_{c}\mathcal{A}*_{c}\mathcal{X}=\mathcal{Q}^{H}*_{c}\mathcal{T}^{\dagger}*_{c}\mathcal{Q}*_{c}\mathcal{Q}^{H}*_{c}\mathcal{T}*_{c}\mathcal{Q}*_{c}\mathcal{Q}^{H}*_{c}\mathcal{T}^{\dagger}*_{c}\mathcal{Q}=\mathcal{Q}^{H}*_{c}\mathcal{T}^{\dagger}*_{c}\mathcal{T}*_{c}\mathcal{T}^{\dagger}*_{c}\mathcal{Q}=\mathcal{Q}^{H}*_{c}\mathcal{T}^{\dagger}*_{c}\mathcal{Q}=\mathcal{X},

\displaystyle(\mathcal{A}*_{c}\mathcal{X})^{H}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(\mathcal{Q}^{H}*_{c}\mathcal{T}*_{c}\mathcal{Q}*_{c}\mathcal{Q}^{H}*_{c}\mathcal{T}^{\dagger}*_{c}\mathcal{Q})^{H}=(\mathcal{Q}^{H}*_{c}\mathcal{T}*_{c}\mathcal{T}^{\dagger}*_{c}\mathcal{Q})^{H}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathcal{Q}^{H}*_{c}\mathcal{T}*_{c}\mathcal{T}^{\dagger}*_{c}\mathcal{Q}=\mathcal{Q}^{H}*_{c}\mathcal{T}*_{c}\mathcal{Q}*_{c}\mathcal{Q}^{H}*_{c}\mathcal{T}^{\dagger}*_{c}\mathcal{Q}=\mathcal{A}*_{c}\mathcal{X}

and

\displaystyle(\mathcal{X}*_{c}\mathcal{A})^{H}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(\mathcal{Q}^{H}*_{c}\mathcal{T}^{\dagger}*_{c}\mathcal{Q}*_{c}\mathcal{Q}^{H}*_{c}\mathcal{T}*_{c}\mathcal{Q})^{H}=(\mathcal{Q}^{H}*_{c}\mathcal{T}^{\dagger}*_{c}\mathcal{T}*_{c}\mathcal{Q})^{H}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathcal{Q}^{H}*_{c}\mathcal{T}^{\dagger}*_{c}\mathcal{T}*_{c}\mathcal{Q}=\mathcal{Q}^{H}*_{c}\mathcal{T}^{\dagger}*_{c}\mathcal{Q}*_{c}\mathcal{Q}^{H}*_{c}\mathcal{T}*_{c}\mathcal{Q}=\mathcal{X}*_{c}\mathcal{A}.

∎

From now on, we denote

\DCT(\matt(\mathcal{A}))=(\mathbf{C}_{n_{3}}\otimes\mathbf{I}_{n_{1}})\matt(\mathcal{A})(\mathbf{C}^{-1}_{n_{3}}\otimes\mathbf{I}_{n_{2}})=\left[\begin{array}[]{cccc}L(\mathcal{A})^{(1)}&&&\\
&L(\mathcal{A})^{(2)}&&\\
&&\ddots&\\
&&&L(\mathcal{A})^{(n_{3})}\\
\end{array}\right],

and

\ten(\IDCT(\left[\begin{array}[]{cccc}L(\mathcal{A})^{(1)}&&&\\
&L(\mathcal{A})^{(2)}&&\\
&&\ddots&\\
&&&L(\mathcal{A})^{(n_{3})}\\
\end{array}\right]))=\mathcal{A}.

In the following, we give the full rank decomposition of the tensor. Notice that not all the tensors have the full rank decomposition we defined.

Definition 3.2
.

Let
\mathcal{A}\in\mathbbmss{C}^{n_{1}\times n_{2}\times n_{3}}
. If
\mathcal{A}
can be decomposed into

\mathcal{A}=\mathcal{M}*_{c}\mathcal{N},

where

\mathcal{M}=\ten(\IDCT(\left[\begin{array}[]{ccc}\mathbf{M}_{1}&&\\
&\ddots&\\
&&\mathbf{M}_{n_{3}}\\
\end{array}\right]))\in\mathbbmss{C}^{n_{1}\times r\times n_{3}},\ \mathbf{M}_{i}\in\mathbbmss{C}^{n_{1}\times r}_{r},\ i=1,2,...,n_{3}

and

\mathcal{N}=\ten(\IDCT(\left[\begin{array}[]{ccc}\mathbf{N}_{1}&&\\
&\ddots&\\
&&\mathbf{N}_{n_{3}}\\
\end{array}\right]))\in\mathbbmss{C}^{r\times n_{2}\times n_{3}},\ \mathbf{N}_{i}\in\mathbbmss{C}^{r\times n_{2}}_{r},\ i=1,2,...,n_{3},

.

Note:

Let
\widehat{\mathcal{A}}=L(\mathcal{A})
,
\widehat{\mathcal{M}}=L(\mathcal{M})
and
\widehat{\mathcal{N}}=L(\mathcal{N})
. Suppose
\widehat{\mathcal{A}}^{(i)}=\mathbf{M}_{i}\mathbf{N}_{i}=\widehat{\mathcal{M}}^{(i)}\widehat{\mathcal{N}}^{(i)}

. We deduce when

,

, one has the decomposition of the definition established.
\Box

Theorem 3.8
.

Let
\mathcal{A}\in\mathbbmss{C}^{n_{1}\times n_{2}\times n_{3}}
. Suppose
\mathcal{A}
has the C-full rank decomposition
\mathcal{A}=\mathcal{M}*_{c}\mathcal{N}
.
Then,

\mathcal{A}^{\dagger}=\mathcal{N}^{H}*_{c}(\mathcal{M}^{H}*_{c}\mathcal{A}*_{c}\mathcal{N}^{H})^{-1}*_{c}\mathcal{M}^{H}.

We will check that
\mathcal{N}^{H}*_{c}(\mathcal{M}^{H}*_{c}\mathcal{A}*_{c}\mathcal{N}^{H})^{-1}*_{c}\mathcal{M}^{H}
holds for the four equations of
(\ref{mp})
. Let
\mathcal{X}=\mathcal{N}^{H}*_{c}(\mathcal{M}^{H}*_{c}\mathcal{A}*_{c}\mathcal{N}^{H})^{-1}*_{c}\mathcal{M}^{H}
. Then, we have

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}*_{c}\mathcal{X}*_{c}\mathcal{A}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}*_{c}\mathcal{N}*_{c}\mathcal{N}^{H}*_{c}(\mathcal{M}^{H}*_{c}\mathcal{A}*_{c}\mathcal{N}^{H})^{-1}*_{c}\mathcal{M}^{H}*_{c}\mathcal{M}*_{c}\mathcal{N}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}*_{c}\mathcal{N}*_{c}\mathcal{N}^{H}*_{c}(\mathcal{M}^{H}*_{c}\mathcal{M}*_{c}\mathcal{N}*_{c}\mathcal{N}^{H})^{-1}*_{c}\mathcal{M}^{H}*_{c}\mathcal{M}*_{c}\mathcal{N}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}*_{c}\mathcal{N}*_{c}\mathcal{N}^{H}*_{c}(\mathcal{N}*_{c}\mathcal{N}^{H})^{-1}*_{c}(\mathcal{M}^{H}*_{c}\mathcal{M})^{-1}*_{c}\mathcal{M}^{H}*_{c}\mathcal{M}*_{c}\mathcal{N}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}*_{c}\mathcal{N}=\mathcal{A},

\displaystyle\mathcal{X}*_{c}\mathcal{A}*_{c}\mathcal{X}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathcal{N}^{H}*_{c}(\mathcal{M}^{H}*_{c}\mathcal{A}*_{c}\mathcal{N}^{H})^{-1}*_{c}\mathcal{M}^{H}*_{c}\mathcal{A}*_{c}\mathcal{N}^{H}*_{c}(\mathcal{M}^{H}*_{c}\mathcal{A}*_{c}\mathcal{N}^{H})^{-1}*_{c}\mathcal{M}^{H}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathcal{N}^{H}*_{c}(\mathcal{M}^{H}*_{c}\mathcal{A}*_{c}\mathcal{N}^{H})^{-1}\mathcal{M}^{H}=\mathcal{X},

\displaystyle(\mathcal{A}*_{c}\mathcal{X})^{H}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle[\mathcal{M}*_{c}\mathcal{N}*_{c}\mathcal{N}^{H}*_{c}(\mathcal{M}^{H}*_{c}\mathcal{A}*_{c}\mathcal{N}^{H})^{-1}*_{c}\mathcal{M}^{H}]^{H}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle[\mathcal{M}*_{c}\mathcal{N}*_{c}\mathcal{N}^{H}*_{c}(\mathcal{N}*_{c}\mathcal{N}^{H})^{-1}*_{c}(\mathcal{M}^{H}*_{c}\mathcal{M})^{-1}*_{c}\mathcal{M}^{H}]^{H}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}*_{c}(\mathcal{M}^{H}*_{c}\mathcal{M})^{-1}*_{c}\mathcal{M}^{H}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}*_{c}\mathcal{N}*_{c}\mathcal{N}^{H}*_{c}(\mathcal{N}*_{c}\mathcal{N}^{H})^{-1}*_{c}(\mathcal{M}^{H}*_{c}\mathcal{M})^{-1}*_{c}\mathcal{M}^{H}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}*_{c}\mathcal{N}*_{c}\mathcal{N}^{H}*_{c}(\mathcal{M}^{H}*_{c}\mathcal{A}*_{c}\mathcal{N}^{H})^{-1}*_{c}\mathcal{M}^{H}=\mathcal{A}*_{c}\mathcal{X}

and

\displaystyle(\mathcal{X}*_{c}\mathcal{A})^{H}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle[\mathcal{N}^{H}*_{c}(\mathcal{M}^{H}*_{c}\mathcal{A}*_{c}\mathcal{N}^{H})^{-1}*_{c}\mathcal{M}^{H}*_{c}\mathcal{M}*_{c}\mathcal{N}]^{H}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle[\mathcal{N}^{H}*_{c}(\mathcal{N}*_{c}\mathcal{N}^{H})^{-1}*_{c}(\mathcal{M}^{H}*_{c}\mathcal{M})^{-1}*_{c}\mathcal{M}^{H}*_{c}\mathcal{M}*_{c}\mathcal{N}]^{H}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathcal{N}^{H}*_{c}(\mathcal{N}*_{c}\mathcal{N}^{H})^{-1}*_{c}\mathcal{N}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathcal{N}^{H}*_{c}(\mathcal{N}*_{c}\mathcal{N}^{H})^{-1}*_{c}(\mathcal{M}^{H}*_{c}\mathcal{M})^{-1}*_{c}\mathcal{M}^{H}*_{c}\mathcal{M}*_{c}\mathcal{N}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathcal{N}^{H}*_{c}(\mathcal{M}^{H}*_{c}\mathcal{A}*_{c}\mathcal{N}^{H})^{-1}*_{c}\mathcal{M}^{H}*_{c}\mathcal{M}*_{c}\mathcal{N}=\mathcal{X}*_{c}\mathcal{A}.

Definition 3.3
.

Let
\mathcal{A}\in\mathbbmss{C}^{n_{1}\times n_{2}\times n_{3}}
. If
\mathcal{A}
can be decomposed as

\mathcal{A}=\mathcal{Q}*_{c}\mathcal{D}*_{c}\mathcal{R},

where

\mathcal{Q}=\ten(\IDCT(\left[\begin{array}[]{ccc}\mathbf{Q}_{1}&&\\
&\ddots&\\
&&\mathbf{Q}_{n_{3}}\\
\end{array}\right]))\in\mathbbmss{C}^{n_{1}\times r\times n_{3}},\ \mathbf{Q}_{i}\in\mathbbmss{C}^{n_{1}\times r}_{r},\ i=1,2,...,n_{3},

\mathcal{D}\in\mathbbmss{C}^{r\times r\times n_{3}}
is an invertible F-diagonal tensor and

\mathcal{R}=\ten(\IDCT(\left[\begin{array}[]{ccc}\mathbf{R}_{1}&&\\

\mathcal{A}^{D}=\mathcal{A}^{k}*_{c}(\mathcal{A}^{2k+1})^{{\dagger}}*_{c}\mathcal{A}^{k}.

Proof.

By the definition of the Drazin inverse, one has

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}^{k}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}^{k+1}*_{c}\mathcal{A}^{D}=\mathcal{A}^{k+2}*_{c}(\mathcal{A}^{D})^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\cdots

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}^{2k}*_{c}(\mathcal{A}^{D})^{k}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}^{2k+1}*_{c}(\mathcal{A}^{D})^{k+1}.

Let
\mathcal{X}=\mathcal{A}^{k}*_{c}(\mathcal{A}^{2k+1})^{(1)}*_{c}\mathcal{A}^{k}
. Therefore, we have

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}^{k+1}*_{c}\mathcal{X}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}^{k+1}*_{c}\mathcal{A}^{k}*_{c}(\mathcal{A}^{2k+1})^{(1)}*_{c}\mathcal{A}^{k}=\mathcal{A}^{2k+1}*_{c}(\mathcal{A}^{2k+1})^{(1)}*_{c}\mathcal{A}^{2k+1}*_{c}(\mathcal{A}^{D})^{k+1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}^{2k+1}*_{c}(\mathcal{A}^{D})^{k+1}=\mathcal{A}^{k},

\displaystyle\mathcal{X}*_{c}\mathcal{A}*_{c}\mathcal{X}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}^{k}*_{c}(\mathcal{A}^{2k+1})^{(1)}*_{c}\mathcal{A}^{k}*_{c}\mathcal{A}*_{c}\mathcal{A}^{k}*_{c}(\mathcal{A}^{2k+1})^{(1)}*_{c}\mathcal{A}^{k}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}^{k}*_{c}(\mathcal{A}^{2k+1})^{(1)}*_{c}\mathcal{A}^{k}=\mathcal{X}.

Moreover,

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}*_{c}\mathcal{X}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}*_{c}\mathcal{A}^{k}*_{c}(\mathcal{A}^{2k+1})^{(1)}*_{c}\mathcal{A}^{k}=\mathcal{A}*_{c}\mathcal{A}^{2k}*_{c}(\mathcal{A}^{D})^{k}*_{c}(\mathcal{A}^{2k+1})^{(1)}*_{c}\mathcal{A}^{2k+1}*_{c}(\mathcal{A}^{D})^{k+1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(\mathcal{A}^{D})^{k}*_{c}\mathcal{A}^{2k+1}*_{c}(\mathcal{A}^{2k+1})^{(1)}*_{c}\mathcal{A}^{2k+1}*_{c}(\mathcal{A}^{D})^{k+1}=(\mathcal{A}^{D})^{k}*_{c}\mathcal{A}^{2k+1}*_{c}(\mathcal{A}^{D})^{k+1},

and

\displaystyle\mathcal{X}*_{c}\mathcal{A}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}^{k}*_{c}(\mathcal{A}^{2k+1})^{(1)}*_{c}\mathcal{A}^{k+1}=\mathcal{A}^{2k+1}*_{c}(\mathcal{A}^{D})^{k+1}*_{c}(\mathcal{A}^{2k+1})^{(1)}*_{c}\mathcal{A}*_{c}\mathcal{A}^{2k}*_{c}(\mathcal{A}^{D})^{k}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(\mathcal{A}^{D})^{k+1}*_{c}\mathcal{A}^{2k+1}*_{c}(\mathcal{A}^{2k+1})^{(1)}*_{c}\mathcal{A}^{2k+1}*_{c}(\mathcal{A}^{D})^{k}=(\mathcal{A}^{D})^{k+1}*_{c}\mathcal{A}^{2k+1}*_{c}(\mathcal{A}^{D})^{k}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(\mathcal{A}^{D})^{k}*_{c}\mathcal{A}^{2k+1}*_{c}(\mathcal{A}^{D})^{k+1},

which implies
\mathcal{A}*_{c}\mathcal{X}=\mathcal{X}*_{c}\mathcal{A}
. Thus, we obtain

\mathcal{A}^{D}=\mathcal{A}^{k}*_{c}(\mathcal{A}^{2k+1})^{(1)}*_{c}\mathcal{A}^{k}
. By taking
(\mathcal{A}^{2k+1})^{{\dagger}}
for
(\mathcal{A}^{2k+1})^{(1)}
,
we have

\mathcal{A}^{D}=\mathcal{A}^{k}*_{c}(\mathcal{A}^{2k+1})^{{\dagger}}*_{c}\mathcal{A}^{k}
.
∎

Theorem 4.2
.

Let
\mathcal{A}\in\mathbbmss{C}^{n_{1}\times n_{1}\times n_{3}}
and
\ind(\mathcal{A})=k
. Suppose
\mathcal{A}^{k}
has the C-QDR decomposition
\mathcal{A}^{k}=\mathcal{Q}*_{c}\mathcal{D}*_{c}\mathcal{R}
.
Then,

\mathcal{A}^{D}=\mathcal{Q}*_{c}(\mathcal{R}*_{c}\mathcal{A}*_{c}\mathcal{Q})^{-1}*_{c}\mathcal{R}.

Proof.

Let

\displaystyle\DCT(\matt(\mathcal{A}))=\left[\begin{array}[]{cccc}L(\mathcal{A})^{(1)}&&&\\
&L(\mathcal{A})^{(2)}&&\\
&&\ddots&\\
&&&L(\mathcal{A})^{(n_{3})}\\
\end{array}\right].

Since
\mathcal{A}^{k}=\mathcal{Q}*_{c}\mathcal{D}*_{c}\mathcal{R}
is the C-QDR decomposition, we conclude that
(L(\mathcal{A})^{(i)})^{k}=\mathbf{Q}_{i}\mathbf{D}_{i}\mathbf{R}_{i}
,
\mathbf{Q}_{i}\in\mathbbmss{C}^{n_{1}\times r}_{r}
,
\mathbf{D}_{i}\in\mathbbmss{C}^{r\times r}_{r}
,
\mathbf{R}_{i}\in\mathbbmss{C}^{r\times n_{2}}_{r}
,
i=1,2,...,n_{3}
are the QDR decomposition of
(L(\mathcal{A})^{(i)})^{k}
. Notice that
(L(\mathcal{A})^{(i)})^{k}=(\mathbf{Q}_{i}\mathbf{D}_{i})\mathbf{R}_{i}=\mathbf{Q}_{i}(\mathbf{D}_{i}\mathbf{R}_{i})
are full rank decomposition of
(L(\mathcal{A})^{(i)})^{k}
. By
[
31
, Theorem 2.1]
, we have
\mathbf{R}_{i}L(\mathcal{A})^{(i)}\mathbf{Q}_{i}\mathbf{D}_{i}
and

\mathbf{D}_{i}\mathbf{R}_{i}L(\mathcal{A})^{(i)}\mathbf{Q}_{i}
,
i=1,2,...,n_{3}
are invertible. So are

\mathcal{R}*_{c}\mathcal{A}*_{c}\mathcal{Q}*_{c}\mathcal{D}
and
\mathcal{D}*_{c}\mathcal{R}*_{c}\mathcal{A}*_{c}\mathcal{Q}
. Hence, we have
\mathcal{R}*_{c}\mathcal{A}*_{c}\mathcal{Q}
is invertible.

On the other hand, by
[
32
]
, we conclude that

(\mathbf{Q}_{i}\mathbf{D}_{i}\mathbf{R}_{i}L(\mathcal{A})^{(i)}\mathbf{Q}_{i}\mathbf{D}_{i}\mathbf{R}_{i})^{\dagger}=(\mathbf{D}_{i}\mathbf{R}_{i})^{\dagger}(\mathbf{R}_{i}L(\mathcal{A})^{(i)}\mathbf{Q}_{i})^{-1}(\mathbf{Q}_{i}\mathbf{D}_{i})^{\dagger},\ \ i=1,2,...,n_{3}

due to
\mathbf{R}_{i}L(\mathcal{A})^{(i)}\mathbf{Q}_{i}
are invertible,
\mathbf{D}_{i}\mathbf{R}_{i}
are full row rank and
\mathbf{Q}_{i}\mathbf{D}_{i}
are full column rank. Therefore,

(\mathcal{Q}*_{c}\mathcal{D}*_{c}\mathcal{R}*_{c}\mathcal{A}*_{c}\mathcal{Q}*_{c}\mathcal{D}*_{c}\mathcal{R})^{{\dagger}}=(\mathcal{D}*_{c}\mathcal{R})^{{\dagger}}*_{c}(\mathcal{R}*_{c}\mathcal{A}*_{c}\mathcal{Q})^{-1}*_{c}(\mathcal{Q}*_{c}\mathcal{D})^{\dagger}.

By Theorem
4.1
, we have

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}^{D}=\mathcal{A}^{k}*_{c}(\mathcal{A}^{2k+1})^{{\dagger}}*_{c}\mathcal{A}^{k}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}^{k}*_{c}(\mathcal{A}^{k}*_{c}\mathcal{A}*_{c}\mathcal{A}^{k})^{{\dagger}}*_{c}\mathcal{A}^{k}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathcal{Q}*_{c}\mathcal{D}*_{c}\mathcal{R}*_{c}(\mathcal{Q}*_{c}\mathcal{D}*_{c}\mathcal{R}*_{c}\mathcal{A}*_{c}\mathcal{Q}*_{c}\mathcal{D}*_{c}\mathcal{R})^{{\dagger}}*_{c}\mathcal{Q}*_{c}\mathcal{D}*_{c}\mathcal{R}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathcal{Q}*_{c}\mathcal{D}*_{c}\mathcal{R}*_{c}(\mathcal{D}*_{c}\mathcal{R})^{{\dagger}}*_{c}(\mathcal{R}*_{c}\mathcal{A}*_{c}\mathcal{Q})^{-1}*_{c}(\mathcal{Q}*_{c}\mathcal{D})^{\dagger}*_{c}\mathcal{Q}*_{c}\mathcal{D}*_{c}\mathcal{R}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathcal{Q}*_{c}(\mathcal{R}*_{c}\mathcal{A}*_{c}\mathcal{Q})^{-1}*_{c}\mathcal{R}.

∎

Let

\DCT(\matt(\mathcal{P}))=\begin{bmatrix}L(\mathcal{P})^{(1)}&&\\
&\ddots&\\

A higher order Markov chain is an extension of a finite Markov chain, in which the stochastic
process
X_{0},X_{1},\cdots
with values in
\{1,2,\cdots,n\}
, has the transition probabilities

0\leq\mathcal{P}_{i_{1}i_{2}i_{3}}=\prob(X_{t}=i_{1}\mid X_{t-1}=i_{2},X_{t-2}=i_{3})\leq 1,

where
\sum\limits_{j=1}^{n}L(\mathcal{P})^{(i)}_{jk}=1
,
i=1,...,n
,
1\leq k\leq n
. We call the tensor

\mathcal{P}
a
transition tensor
.

Let
F
be a subset of
\mathbbmss{R}
and let
\{X_{t}:t\in F\}
be a set of random variables. If
F
is
countable and if the range of each
X_{t}
is the same finite set, then the chain is said to be
a
finite Markov chain
. Let us denote
\{G_{1},\ldots,G_{m}\}
the range of any
X_{t}
.

It is useful to have in mind that
X_{k}
is the outcome of the chain on the
k
th step.
The probability of
X_{k}
being in state
G_{j}
provided that
X_{k-1}
was in state
G_{i}
is

L(\mathcal{P})^{(i)}_{jk}(s)=\prob(X_{s}=G_{k}|X_{s-1}=G_{j})
,
i=1,\ldots,n
. These probabilities are said to be the

one-step transition probabilities
. If each of the one-step transition probabilities does not
depend on
s
(does not depend on time), i.e.,
L(\mathcal{P})^{(i)}_{jk}(s)=\mathcal{P}_{jki}
, for any
s=1,2,\ldots
,

i=1,\ldots,n
, then we say that the chain is
homogeneous
.

In the sequel, we will focus our attention to finite homogeneous Markov chains and will simply write
‘Markov chain’ or ‘chain’ to denote a finite homogeneous Markov chain.

An
ergodic set

\Omega
is a set of states in which every state of
\Omega
is accessible
from any other state of
\Omega
and, in addition, no state outside
\Omega
is accessible from any state
of
\Omega
.

A
transient
set
\Omega
is a set whose elements are states in which every state of
\Omega

is accessible from every other state of
\Omega
, but some state outside
\Omega
is accessible from
each state of
\Omega
.

A Markov chain is
ergodic
if the transition tensor of the chain is irreducible, or equivalently,
the states of this chain form a single ergodic set. An ergodic chain is
regular
if its transition
tensor
\mathcal{P}
has the following property: exists a natural number
k
such that
\mathcal{P}^{k}>0
.

A state is
absorbing
if the chain enters in this state, it can never be left. A chain is an

absorbing chain
if it has at least one absorbing state and, in addition, from every state of
this chain it is possible to enter in an absorbing state (but not necessarily in one step).
See
[
35
]
for details.

Theorem 6.1
.

If
\mathcal{P}\in\mathbbmss{R}^{n\times n\times n}
is any transition tensor and if
\mathcal{A}=\mathcal{I}-\mathcal{P}
, then
\mathcal{A}^{\#}

exists.

Proof.

Let

\DCT(\matt(\mathcal{P}))=\begin{bmatrix}L(\mathcal{P})^{(1)}&&\\
&\ddots&\\
&&L(\mathcal{P})^{(n)}\end{bmatrix}.

Since
\mathcal{A}=\mathcal{I}-\mathcal{P}
, then

\DCT(\matt(\mathcal{A}))=\begin{bmatrix}L(\mathcal{A})^{(1)}&&\\
&\ddots&\\
&&L(\mathcal{A})^{(n)}\end{bmatrix}=\begin{bmatrix}L(\mathcal{I})^{(1)}-L(\mathcal{P})^{(1)}&&\\
&\ddots&\\
&&L(\mathcal{I})^{(n)}-L(\mathcal{P})^{(n)}\end{bmatrix}.

Hence, we have
L(\mathcal{A})^{(i)}=L(\mathcal{I})^{(i)}-L(\mathcal{P})^{(i)}
for
i=1,2,\ldots,n
. By using
[
35
, Theorem 8.2.1]
, one has

\ind(L(\mathcal{A})^{(i)})=1
, which implies that the group inverse of
L(\mathcal{A})^{(i)}
exists. Then the group inverse of
\mathcal{A}
exists.
∎

Theorem 6.2
.

Let
\mathcal{P}\in\mathbbmss{R}^{n\times n\times n}
be the transition tensor of a chain and let
\mathcal{A}=\mathcal{I}-\mathcal{P}
. Then

\mathcal{I}-\mathcal{A}*_{c}\mathcal{A}^{\#}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\lim\limits_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\mathcal{I}+\mathcal{P}+\mathcal{P}^{2}+\cdots+\mathcal{P}^{n-1}}{n},\ \text{for every transition tensor}\ \mathcal{P}\\
\lim\limits_{n\rightarrow\infty}(\alpha\mathcal{I}+(1-\alpha)\mathcal{P}),\ \text{for every transition tensor}\ \mathcal{P}\ \text{and}\ 0<\alpha<1\\
\lim\limits_{n\rightarrow\infty}\mathcal{P}^{n},\ \text{for every regular chain}\\
\lim\limits_{n\rightarrow\infty}\mathcal{P}^{n},\ \text{for every absorbing chain.}\end{array}\right.

Proof.

Let

\DCT(\matt(\mathcal{A}))=\begin{bmatrix}L(\mathcal{A})^{(1)}&&\\
&\ddots&\\
&&L(\mathcal{A})^{(n)}\end{bmatrix}.

Since
\mathcal{A}=\mathcal{I}-\mathcal{P}
, by Theorem
6.1
, the group inverse of
\mathcal{A}
exists. Hence,

\DCT(\matt(\mathcal{A}^{\#}))=\begin{bmatrix}(L(\mathcal{A})^{(1)})^{\#}&&\\
&\ddots&\\
&&(L(\mathcal{A})^{(n)})^{\#}\end{bmatrix}.

Now, it is easy to see

\DCT(\matt(\mathcal{I}-\mathcal{A}*_{c}\mathcal{A}^{\#}))=\begin{bmatrix}L(\mathcal{I})^{(1)}-(L(\mathcal{A})^{(1)})(L(\mathcal{A})^{(1)})^{\#}&&\\
&\ddots&\\
&&L(\mathcal{I})^{(n)}-(L(\mathcal{A})^{(n)})(L(\mathcal{A})^{(n)})^{\#}\end{bmatrix}.

Denote
L(\mathcal{I})^{(i)}=\overline{\mathbf{I}}_{i}
,
L(\mathcal{A})^{(i)}=\overline{\mathbf{A}}_{i}
and
L(\mathcal{P})^{(i)}=\overline{\mathbf{P}}_{i}
. By using
[
35
, Theorem 8.2.2]
, we have

\overline{\mathbf{I}}_{i}-\overline{\mathbf{A}}_{i}\overline{\mathbf{A}}_{i}^{\#}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\lim\limits_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\overline{\mathbf{I}}_{i}+\overline{\mathbf{P}}_{i}+\overline{\mathbf{P}}_{i}^{2}+\cdots+\overline{\mathbf{P}}_{i}^{n-1}}{n},\ \text{for every transition matrix}\ \overline{\mathbf{P}}_{i}\\
\lim\limits_{n\rightarrow\infty}(\alpha\overline{\mathbf{I}}_{i}+(1-\alpha)\overline{\mathbf{P}}_{i}),\ \text{for every transition matrix}\ \overline{\mathbf{P}}_{i}\ \text{and}\ 0<\alpha<1\\
\lim\limits_{n\rightarrow\infty}{\overline{\mathbf{P}}_{i}}^{n},\ \text{for every regular chain}\\
\lim\limits_{n\rightarrow\infty}{\overline{\mathbf{P}}_{i}}^{n},\ \text{for every absorbing chain,}\ i=1,\cdots,n,\end{array}\right.

which implies that

\mathcal{I}-\mathcal{A}*_{c}\mathcal{A}^{\#}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\lim\limits_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\mathcal{I}+\mathcal{P}+\mathcal{P}^{2}+\cdots+\mathcal{P}^{n-1}}{n},\ \text{for every transition tensor}\ \mathcal{P}\\
\lim\limits_{n\rightarrow\infty}(\alpha\mathcal{I}+(1-\alpha)\mathcal{P}),\ \text{for every transition tensor}\ \mathcal{P}\ \text{and}\ 0<\alpha<1\\
\lim\limits_{n\rightarrow\infty}\mathcal{P}^{n},\ \text{for every regular chain}\\
\lim\limits_{n\rightarrow\infty}\mathcal{P}^{n},\ \text{for every absorbing chain.}\end{array}\right.

∎

Populations of Solutions to Cyclotomic Bethe Equations
Alexander VARCHENKO † and Charles A.S. YOUNG ‡

Abstract
We study solutions of the Bethe Ansatz equations for the cyclotomic Gaudin model of [Vicedo B., Young C.A.S., arXiv:1409.6937]. We give two interpretations of such solutions: as critical points of a cyclotomic master function, and as critical points with cyclotomic symmetry of a certain “extended” master function. In finite types, this yields a correspondence between the Bethe eigenvectors and eigenvalues of the cyclotomic Gaudin model and those of an “extended” non-cyclotomic Gaudin model. We proceed to define populations of solutions to the cyclotomic Bethe equations, in the sense of [Mukhin E., Varchenko A., Commun. Contemp. Math. 6 (2004), 111–163, math.QA/0209017], for diagram automorphisms of Kac–Moody Lie algebras. In the case of type A with the diagram automorphism, we associate to each population a vector space of quasi-polynomials with specified ramification conditions. This vector space is equipped with a ℤ 2 {\mathbb{Z}}_{2} -gradation and a non-degenerate bilinear form which is (skew-)symmetric on the even (resp. odd) graded subspace. We show that the population of cyclotomic critical points is isomorphic to the variety of isotropic full flags in this space.

Introduction

Let
{\mathfrak{g}}
be a complex Kac–Moody Lie algebra and
\sigma\colon{\mathfrak{g}}\to{\mathfrak{g}}
an automorphism of order
M\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 1}
. Let
\omega\in{\mathbb{C}}^{\times}
be a primitive
M
th root of unity. We may choose a Cartan subalgebra
{\mathfrak{h}}\subset{\mathfrak{g}}
such that
\sigma({\mathfrak{h}})={\mathfrak{h}}
. We have the canonical pairing
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle\colon{\mathfrak{h}}^{*}\otimes{\mathfrak{h}}\to{\mathbb{C}}
, and the simple roots
\alpha_{i}\in{\mathfrak{h}}^{*}
and coroots
\alpha^{\vee}_{i}\in{\mathfrak{h}}
, where
i
runs over the set
I
of nodes of the Dynkin diagram.

Consider the following system of equations in
m\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 0}
variables
\bm{t}=(t_{1},\dots,t_{m})\in{\mathbb{C}}^{m}
and labels
\bm{\mathsf{c}}=({\mathsf{c}}(1),\dots,{\mathsf{c}}(m))\in I^{m}
:

\displaystyle 0=\sum_{k=0}^{M-1}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\frac{\big\langle\sigma^{k}\Lambda_{i},\alpha^{\vee}_{{\mathsf{c}}(j)}\big\rangle}{t_{j}-\omega^{k}z_{i}}-\sum_{k=0}^{M-1}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}i=1\\
i\neq j\end{subarray}}^{m}\frac{\big\langle\sigma^{k}\alpha_{{\mathsf{c}}(i)},\alpha^{\vee}_{{\mathsf{c}}(j)}\big\rangle}{t_{j}-\omega^{r}t_{i}}

\displaystyle\hphantom{0=}{}+\frac{1}{t_{j}}\left(\big\langle\Lambda_{0},\alpha^{\vee}_{{\mathsf{c}}(j)}\big\rangle-\sum_{k=1}^{M-1}\frac{\big\langle\sigma^{k}\alpha_{{\mathsf{c}}(j)},\alpha_{{\mathsf{c}}(j)}^{\vee}\big\rangle}{1-\omega^{k}}\right),\qquad j=1,\dots,m,

(1.1)

where
\Lambda_{0},\Lambda_{1},\dots,\Lambda_{N}\in{\mathfrak{h}}^{*}
are weights (with
\sigma\Lambda_{0}=\Lambda_{0}
) and
z_{1},\dots,z_{N}
are non-zero points in the complex plane whose orbits, under the action of the cyclic group
\omega^{\mathbb{Z}}
, are pairwise disjoint.

When
\sigma=\operatorname{id}
,
\omega=1
and
\Lambda_{0}=0
, these equations reduce to the following well-known set of equations in mathematical physics:

\displaystyle 0=\sum_{i=0}^{N}\frac{\big\langle\Lambda_{i},\alpha^{\vee}_{{\mathsf{c}}(j)}\big\rangle}{t_{j}-z_{i}}-\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}i=1\\
i\neq j\end{subarray}}^{m}\frac{\big\langle\alpha_{{\mathsf{c}}(i)},\alpha^{\vee}_{{\mathsf{c}}(j)}\big\rangle}{t_{j}-t_{i}},\qquad j=1,\dots,m.

(1.2)

These are the equations for critical points of the
master functions

[
19
]
which appear in the integral expressions for hypergeometric solutions to the Knizhnik–Zamolodchikov (KZ) equations. They are also the
Bethe equations
of the quantum Gaudin model
[
1
,
5
,
18
]
.

The equations (
1.1
) were introduced (for simple
{\mathfrak{g}}
) in the study of cyclotomic generalizations of the Gaudin model
[
26
,
27
]
– see also
[
3
,
21
,
22
]
– as we recall in Section
3
below. Let us call them the
cyclotomic Bethe equations
. (Cyclotomic generalizations of the KZ equations were studied in
[
2
,
4
]
, and appear in, in particular, the representation theory of cyclotomic Hecke algebras
[
23
]
.)

It is natural to ask whether the cyclotomic Bethe equations (
1.1
) can be interpreted as the equations for critical points of some master function. In the present paper we begin by giving
two different such interpretations.
First, they are indeed the critical point equations for a
cyclotomic master function
, which we write down in (
2.2
). But they are also the equations for critical points with cyclotomic – more precisely
S_{m}\ltimes({\mathbb{Z}}/M{\mathbb{Z}})^{m}
– symmetry of what we call an
extended master function
, (
2.8
).

Recall that a master function is specified by a
weighted arrangement of hyperplanes
: that is, by a finite collection
{\mathcal{C}}
of affine hyperplanes in a complex affine space of finite dimension, together with an assignment of a number
a(H)\in{\mathbb{C}}
to each hyperplane
H\in{\mathcal{C}}
. Indeed, for each
H\in{\mathcal{C}}
, let
\ell_{H}=0
be an affine equation for
H
; then the master function is
\Phi=\sum\limits_{H\in{\mathcal{C}}}a(H)\log\ell_{H}
.

The cyclotomic master function corresponds to a hyperplane arrangement in
{\mathbb{C}}^{m}
whose hyperplanes include
t_{i}=\omega^{k}t_{j}
,
1\leq i<j\leq m
, for each
k\in{\mathbb{Z}}/M{\mathbb{Z}}
. By contrast, the extended master function corresponds to a hyperplane arrangement in
{\mathbb{C}}^{mM}
, but has only those hyperplanes corresponding to the type
A
root system, i.e.,
t_{i}=t_{j}
,
1\leq i<j\leq mM
, etc.
Because the extended master function is a master function of this standard form, its critical point equations are the Bethe equations for a certain standard (i.e., non-cyclotomic) Gaudin model, which we call the
extended Gaudin model
. This observation leads to our first result: a correspondence between the spectrum of the cyclotomic Gaudin model and a “cyclotomic” part of the spectrum of the extended Gaudin model. See Theorem
3.5
.

Solutions to the Bethe equations (
1.2
) form families called
populations
. Populations were first introduced in
[
15
,
20
]
, where a
generation procedure
was given which produces families of new solutions to the Bethe equations starting from a given solution. A population is then defined to be the Zariski closure of the set of all solutions to the Bethe equations obtained by repeated application of this generation procedure, starting from a given solution.
It is known that if
{\mathfrak{g}}
is simple then every population is isomorphic to the flag variety of the Langlands dual Lie algebra
{}^{L}{\mathfrak{g}}
. This was shown in
[
15
]
for types
A
,
B
,
C
and in all finite types in
[
6
,
16
]
. (A population can also be understood as the variety of Miura opers with a given underlying oper; see
[
6
,
16
]
.)

In the present work our main goal is to initiate the study of
cyclotomic populations
: populations of solutions to the equations (
1.1
).

We formulate in Section
4
a definition of cyclotomic populations for
{\mathfrak{g}}
a general Kac–Moody Lie algebra and
\sigma
any diagram automorphism of
{\mathfrak{g}}
satisfying the
linking condition
. (We also place certain restrictions on the weight
\Lambda_{0}
; see Section
4.1
.) The linking condition
[
7
]
states that, for every node
i\in I
, the restriction of the Dynkin diagram to the orbit
\sigma^{\mathbb{Z}}(i)
consists either of disconnected nodes (in which case
i
has
linking number
L_{i}=1
), or of a number of disconnected copies of the
{\mathrm{A}}_{2}
Dynkin diagram (in which case
i
has
linking number
L_{i}=2
). What the linking condition ensures is that it is possible to “fold” the Dynkin diagram by the automorphism
\sigma
. See Section
2.3
and
[
7
]
.

In Section
4
we define the cyclotomic population to be the Zariski closure of the set of all cyclotomic critical points obtained by repeated application of a certain “cyclotomic generation procedure”, starting from a given cyclotomic critical point. So the key ingredient is this generation procedure. Let us describe it, in outline. There is an “elementary cyclotomic generation” step associated to each orbit
\sigma^{\mathbb{Z}}(i)
. There are two cases:
L_{i}=1
and
L_{i}=2
.

First, suppose
i\in I
is a node with linking number
L_{i}=1
.
A critical point
(\bm{t},\bm{\mathsf{c}})
is represented by a tuple of polynomials,
\bm{y}=(y_{i}(x))_{i\in I}
, where the roots of the polynomial
y_{i}(x)
,
i\in I
, are the Bethe variables
t_{s}
of “colour”
i
, i.e., those such that
{\mathsf{c}}(s)=i
. Following
[
15
]
, one defines a function of
x
,

\displaystyle y_{i}^{(i)}(x;c):=y_{i}(x)\int^{x}\xi^{\langle\Lambda_{0},\alpha^{\vee}_{i}\rangle}T_{i}(\xi)\prod_{j\in I}y_{j}(\xi)^{-\langle\alpha_{j},\alpha^{\vee}_{i}\rangle}d\xi+cy_{i}(x),

(1.3)

depending on a parameter
c\in{\mathbb{C}}
.
Here
T_{i}(x)
,
i\in I
, are certain functions encoding the “frame” data, i.e., the points
z_{1},\dots,z_{N}
and the weights
\Lambda_{1},\dots,\Lambda_{N}
; see (
4.5
).
The Bethe equations ensure that
y_{i}^{(i)}(x;c)
is in fact a polynomial, and moreover that if we consider the new tuple
\bm{y}^{(i)}(c)
in which
y_{i}(x)
is replaced by
y_{i}^{(i)}(x;c)
, then for almost all values of
c
this new tuple again represents a solution to the Bethe equations. Call the replacement
\bm{y}\mapsto\bm{y}^{(i)}(c)

elementary generation in direction
i
.
Now suppose the initial tuple
\bm{y}
represents a cyclotomic point. That means

\displaystyle y_{\sigma j}(\omega x)\simeq y_{j}(x),\qquad j\in I;

see Lemma
4.5
. Since the orbit
\sigma^{\mathbb{Z}}(i)
consists of disconnected nodes of the Dynkin diagram, the operations of elementary generation in the directions
\sigma^{\mathbb{Z}}(i)
commute. By performing each of them once, in any order, we can arrange to arrive at a new cyclotomic point. See Theorem
4.6
.

Next, suppose
i\in I
is a node with linking number
L_{i}=2
. Then for every copy of the
{\mathrm{A}}_{2}
diagram, with nodes say
j
and
{\bar{\jmath}}
, one must perform the sequence of generation steps
j
,
{\bar{\jmath}}
,
j
. Doing this for each copy of
{\mathrm{A}}_{2}
in turn, in any order, we can arrange to arrive at a new cyclotomic point. See Theorem
4.20
.

When
L_{i}=2
there is a subtlety coming from our assumptions about the weight at the origin,
\Lambda_{0}
. Throughout Section
4
, motivated by
[
26
]
, we assume that
\langle\Lambda_{0},\alpha^{\vee}_{i}\rangle
is non-integral when
L_{i}=2
. That means that the expression (
1.3
) develops a branch point at the origin. The upshot is that at certain intermediate steps, the weight at the origin is shifted to
{\mathsf{s}}_{i}\cdot\Lambda_{0}
, before eventually being shifted back to
\Lambda_{0}
. See Proposition
4.10
and compare
[
17
]
.

In either case,
L_{i}=1
or
L_{i}=2
, we write
\bm{y}^{(i,\sigma)}(c)
for the tuple of polynomials representing the new cyclotomic critical point. It depends on a single parameter
c
. The replacement
\bm{y}\mapsto\bm{y}^{(i,\sigma)}(c)
is the elementary cyclotomic generation, in the direction of the orbit
\sigma^{\mathbb{Z}}(i)
.

To a critical point
(\bm{t},\bm{\mathsf{c}})
represented by a tuple of polynomials
\bm{y}
one can associate a weight
\Lambda_{\infty}
. See (
2.4
) and (
4.10
). For fixed
\Lambda_{0},\Lambda_{1},\dots,\Lambda_{N}
, we may regard
\Lambda_{\infty}
as encoding the number of roots
t_{s}
of each “colour”
i\in I
, i.e., the degrees of the polynomials
y_{i}(x)
. It is known that
\Lambda_{\infty}(\bm{y}^{(i)}(c))
is equal either to
\Lambda_{\infty}(\bm{y})
or to
{\mathsf{s}}_{i}\cdot\Lambda_{\infty}(\bm{y})
, where
{\mathsf{s}}_{i}\cdot{}
denotes the shifted action of the Weyl reflection in root
\alpha_{i}
. See
[
15
]
.
We have an analogous statement in the cyclotomic case. Namely, there is a “folded” Weyl group
W^{\sigma}
with generators
{\mathsf{s}}_{i}^{\sigma}
. See Section
2.3
. And we show that
\Lambda_{\infty}(\bm{y}^{(i,\sigma)}(c))
is equal either to
\Lambda_{\infty}(\bm{y})
or to
{\mathsf{s}}_{i}^{\sigma}\cdot\Lambda_{\infty}(\bm{y})
. For the precise statement see Theorems
4.6
and
4.20
.

We proceed in Section
5
to treat in detail the case of type A with the diagram automorphism.

Recall first from
[
15
]
the structure of populations in type
A_{R}
,
R\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 1}
, for the master functions associated to marked points
z_{1},\dots,z_{N}
and integral dominant weights
\Lambda_{1},\dots,\Lambda_{N}
. In that setting, every population of critical points is isomorphic to a variety of full flags in a certain
(R+1)
-dimensional vector space
{\mathcal{K}}
of polynomials. The ramification points of
{\mathcal{K}}
are
z_{1},\dots,z_{N}
and
\infty
, and the ramification data at these points are specified by the weights
\Lambda_{1},\dots,\Lambda_{N}
and an integral dominant weight
\tilde{\Lambda}_{\infty}
.
Given a full flag
{\mathcal{F}}=\{0=F_{0}\subset F_{1}\subset F_{2}\subset\dots\subset F_{R+1}={\mathcal{K}}\}
in
{\mathcal{K}}
, pick any basis
(u_{i}(x))_{i=1}^{R+1}
of polynomials adjusted to this flag, i.e., such that
F_{k}=\operatorname{span}_{\mathbb{C}}(u_{1}(x),\dots,u_{k}(x))
. Then define a tuple of functions
\bm{y}^{\mathcal{F}}=(y^{\mathcal{F}}_{k}(x))_{k=1}^{R}
by

\displaystyle y^{\mathcal{F}}_{k}(x)=\operatorname{Wr}(u_{1}(x),\dots,u_{k}(x))/\big(T_{1}^{k-1}(x)T_{2}^{k-2}(x)\dots T_{k-1}(x)\big),

where – as in (
1.3
) above – the
(T_{i}(x))_{i=1}^{R}
are functions encoding the “frame” data
z_{1},\dots,z_{N}
and
\Lambda_{1},\dots,\Lambda_{N}
, and where
\operatorname{Wr}(u_{1}(x),\dots,u_{k}(x))
denotes the Wronskian determinant. The ramification properties of
{\mathcal{K}}
ensure that the
y^{\mathcal{F}}_{k}(x)
are in fact polynomials. Moreover the map
{\mathcal{F}}\mapsto\bm{y}^{\mathcal{F}}
is an isomorphism of varieties from the variety of full flags in
{\mathcal{K}}
to the population associated with
{\mathcal{K}}
.
The space
{\mathcal{K}}
is the kernel of a certain linear differential operator
{\mathcal{D}}
of order
R+1
(essentially a type
A
oper). This operator
{\mathcal{D}}
can be defined in terms of the
(T_{i}(x))_{i=1}^{R}
together with the polynomials
(y_{i}(x))_{i=1}^{R}
of (any) point in the population. (See Section
5.4
.)

Now let us discuss how the picture changes in our present setting.
For us, the weight at the origin
\Lambda_{0}
need not be integer dominant. We assume it satisfies weaker assumptions given in (
5.1
). These assumptions mean that we are led to consider vector spaces
{\mathcal{K}}
of
quasi-
polynomials: that is, polynomials in
x^{\frac{1}{2}}
. The local behaviour of these quasi-polynomials near the origin is encoded in
\Lambda_{0}
. The remaining ramification points are
z_{1},\dots,z_{N}
,
-z_{1},\dots,-z_{N}
, and
\infty
. See Definition
5.2
.

The space of quasi-polynomials
{\mathcal{K}}
admits a natural
{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}
gradation
{\mathcal{K}}={\mathcal{K}}_{\textup{O}}\oplus{\mathcal{K}}_{\textup{Sp}}
. We call flags which respect this gradation
decomposable
. Decomposable full flags are classified by their
type
; see Section
5.3
. In particular the flags
{\mathcal{F}}\in F\!L_{S}({\mathcal{K}})
of a certain preferred type
S
, (
5.9
), are sent to polynomials under the map
{\mathcal{F}}\mapsto\bm{y}^{\mathcal{F}}
. This map of varieties
F\!L_{S}({\mathcal{K}})\to{\mathbb{P}}({\mathbb{C}}[x])^{R}
is an isomorphism onto its image. The
cyclotomic population
is then the set of cyclotomic tuples in this image, i.e., the set of tuples
\bm{y}^{\mathcal{F}}
,
{\mathcal{F}}\in F\!L_{S}({\mathcal{K}})
, such that
y_{i}(x)\simeq y_{R+1-i}(-x)
,
i=1,\dots,R
. The question is: which flags in
F\!L_{S}({\mathcal{K}})
map to cyclotomic tuples?

To answer this question we introduce the notion of a
cyclotomically self-dual space of quasi-polynomials
. The space
{\mathcal{K}}
has a natural dual space
{\mathcal{K}}^{\dagger}
of quasi-polynomials – see Section
5.5
– and we say
{\mathcal{K}}
is cyclotomically self-dual if for all
v(x)\in{\mathcal{K}}
,
v(-x)\in{\mathcal{K}}^{\dagger}
. (Compare the very similar notion of a self-dual space of polynomials in
[
15
]
.)
We show that a sufficient condition for
{\mathcal{K}}
to be cyclotomically self-dual is that there exists at least one full flag
{\mathcal{F}}
in
{\mathcal{K}}
such that
\bm{y}^{\mathcal{F}}
is cyclotomic (Theorem
5.14
).
If
{\mathcal{K}}
is cyclotomically self-dual then it admits a canonical non-degenerate bilinear form
B
. We show that, for all full flags
{\mathcal{F}}
in
{\mathcal{K}}
, the tuple
\bm{y}^{\mathcal{F}}
is cyclotomic if and only if
{\mathcal{F}}
is isotropic with respect to
B
(Theorem
5.17
).

Therefore the cyclotomic population is isomorphic to the variety
F\!L_{S}^{\perp}({\mathcal{K}})
of isotropic flags of type
S
in
{\mathcal{K}}
. The bilinear form
B
is symmetric on
{\mathcal{K}}_{\textup{O}}
and skew-symmetric on
{\mathcal{K}}_{\textup{Sp}}
, and these subspaces are mutually orthogonal with respect to
B
(Theorem
5.23
). Hence this variety
F\!L_{S}^{\perp}({\mathcal{K}})
is isomorphic to the direct product of spaces of isotropic flags
F\!L^{\perp}({\mathcal{K}}_{\textup{Sp}})\times F\!L^{\perp}({\mathcal{K}}_{\textup{O}})
.

2
Master functions and cyclotomic symmetry

2.1
Kac–Moody algebras

Let
I
be a finite set of indices and
A=(a_{i,j})_{i,j\in I}
a generalized Cartan matrix, i.e.,
a_{i,i}=2
and
a_{i,j}\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\leq 0}
whenever
i\neq j
, with
a_{i,j}=0
if and only if
a_{j,i}=0
.
Let
{\mathfrak{g}}:={\mathfrak{g}}(A)
be the corresponding complex Kac–Moody Lie algebra
[
11
, Section 1]
,
{\mathfrak{h}}\subset{\mathfrak{g}}
a Cartan subalgebra, and

\displaystyle{\mathfrak{g}}={\mathfrak{n}}_{-}\oplus{\mathfrak{h}}\oplus{\mathfrak{n}}_{+}

a triangular decomposition.
Let
\alpha_{i}\in{\mathfrak{h}}^{*}
,
\alpha^{\vee}_{i}\in{\mathfrak{h}}
,
i\in I
be collections of simple roots and coroots respectively. We have
\dim{\mathfrak{h}}=|I|+\dim\ker A=2|I|-\operatorname{rank}A
. By definition,

\displaystyle\langle\alpha_{i},\alpha^{\vee}_{j}\rangle=a_{j,i},

where
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle\colon{\mathfrak{h}}^{*}\otimes{\mathfrak{h}}\to{\mathbb{C}}
is the canonical pairing.

We assume that
A
is symmetrizable, i.e., there exists a diagonal matrix
D=\operatorname{diag}(d_{i})_{i\in I}
, whose entries are coprime positive integers, such that the matrix
B=DA
is symmetric. Let
(\cdot,\cdot)
be the associated symmetric bilinear form on
{\mathfrak{h}}^{*}
. We have
(\alpha_{i},\alpha_{j})=d_{i}a_{i,j}
and

\displaystyle\langle\lambda,\alpha^{\vee}_{i}\rangle=2(\lambda,\alpha_{i})/(\alpha_{i},\alpha_{i})\qquad\text{for all $\lambda\in{\mathfrak{h}}^{*}$}.

The form
(\cdot,\cdot)
is non-degenerate. Therefore it gives an identification
{\mathfrak{h}}\cong_{\mathbb{C}}{\mathfrak{h}}^{*}
and hence a non-degenerate symmetric bilinear form on
{\mathfrak{h}}
which we also write as
(\cdot,\cdot)
.

Let
{\mathcal{P}}:=\{\lambda\in{\mathfrak{h}}^{*}\colon\langle\lambda,\alpha^{\vee}_{i}\rangle\in{\mathbb{Z}}\}
be the integral weight lattice and
{\mathcal{P}}_{+}:=\{\lambda\in{\mathfrak{h}}^{*}\colon\langle\lambda,\alpha^{\vee}_{i}\rangle\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 0}\}
the set of dominant integral weights.

Let
W\subset\operatorname{End}({\mathfrak{h}}^{*})
be the Weyl group. It is generated by the reflections
{\mathsf{s}}_{i}
,
i\in I
, given by

\displaystyle{\mathsf{s}}_{i}(\lambda):=\lambda-\langle\lambda,\alpha^{\vee}_{i}\rangle\alpha_{i},\qquad\lambda\in{\mathfrak{h}}^{*}.

Let
\rho\in{\mathfrak{h}}^{*}
be a vector such that
\langle\rho,\alpha^{\vee}_{i}\rangle=1
for
i\in I
.
We use
\cdot
to denote the
shifted action
of the Weyl group, i.e.,

\displaystyle{\mathsf{s}}\cdot\lambda:=w(\lambda+\rho)-\rho,\qquad{\mathsf{s}}\in W,\quad\lambda\in{\mathfrak{h}}^{*}.

2.2
Diagram automorphism

Suppose
\sigma
is an automorphism of the Dynkin diagram
[
11
, Section 4.7]
of
A
. That is,
\sigma
is a permutation of the index set
I
such that

\displaystyle a_{\sigma i,\sigma j}=a_{i,j}.

Let
M
be the order of
\sigma
and let
\omega\in{\mathbb{C}}^{\times}
be a primitive
M
th root of unity.

To such a permutation is associated a
diagram automorphism

{\mathfrak{g}}\to{\mathfrak{g}}
of the Kac–Moody Lie algebra
[
7
]
, which we shall also write as
\sigma
. We have

\displaystyle\sigma E_{i}=E_{\sigma i},\qquad\sigma F_{i}=F_{\sigma i},\qquad\sigma\alpha^{\vee}_{i}=\alpha^{\vee}_{\sigma i},\qquad i\in I,

where
E_{i}\in{\mathfrak{n}}
,
F_{i}\in{\mathfrak{n}}^{-}
,
i\in I
, are a set of Chevalley generators of
[{\mathfrak{g}},{\mathfrak{g}}]
. This defines
\sigma
on the derived subalgebra
[{\mathfrak{g}},{\mathfrak{g}}]
of
{\mathfrak{g}}
. For the action of
\sigma
on the derivations, i.e., on a complement of
[{\mathfrak{g}},{\mathfrak{g}}]
in
{\mathfrak{g}}
, see
[
7
, Section 3.2]
. This action may be chosen to ensure that
\sigma\colon{\mathfrak{g}}\to{\mathfrak{g}}
has order
M
and respects the bilinear form
(\cdot,\cdot)
on
{\mathfrak{h}}
:

\displaystyle(\sigma X,\sigma Y)=(X,Y)\qquad\text{for all}\quad X,Y\in{\mathfrak{h}}.

The action of
\sigma
on
{\mathfrak{h}}^{*}
is defined by
\sigma\lambda:=\lambda\circ\sigma^{-1}
so that
\langle\sigma\lambda,\sigma X\rangle=\langle\lambda,X\rangle
for all
\lambda\in{\mathfrak{h}}^{*}
,
X\in{\mathfrak{h}}
. Note that then
\sigma\alpha_{i}=\alpha_{\sigma i}
for all
i\in I
.

Let
{\mathfrak{g}}^{\sigma}\subset{\mathfrak{g}}

be the Lie subalgebra of elements invariant under
\sigma
. We have

\displaystyle{\mathfrak{g}}^{\sigma}={\mathfrak{n}}_{-}^{\sigma}\oplus{\mathfrak{h}}^{\sigma}\oplus{\mathfrak{n}}_{+}^{\sigma}

with
{\mathfrak{n}}_{\pm}^{\sigma}={\mathfrak{g}}^{\sigma}\cap{\mathfrak{n}}_{\pm}
and
{\mathfrak{h}}^{\sigma}={\mathfrak{g}}^{\sigma}\cap{\mathfrak{h}}
.

2.3
The linking condition and the folded diagram

(2.3)

for

For any

\displaystyle=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\bm{n}\in P_{m,N}\\
(k_{1},\dots,k_{m})\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{M}^{m}\end{subarray}}\bigotimes_{i=1}^{N}\frac{\check{\sigma}^{k_{n^{i}_{1}}}\big(F_{c(n^{i}_{1})}\big)\check{\sigma}^{k_{n^{i}_{2}}}\big(F_{c(n^{i}_{2})}\big)\cdots\check{\sigma}^{k_{n^{i}_{p_{i}-1}}}\big(F_{c(n^{i}_{p_{i}-1})}\big)\check{\sigma}^{k_{n^{i}_{p_{i}}}}\big(F_{c(n^{i}_{p_{i}})}\big)\mathsf{v}_{\Lambda_{i}}}{\big(\omega^{k_{n^{i}_{1}}}w_{n^{i}_{1}}-\omega^{k_{n^{i}_{2}}}w_{n^{i}_{2}}\big)\cdots\big(\omega^{k_{n^{i}_{p_{i}-1}}}w_{n^{i}_{p_{i}-1}}-\omega^{k_{n^{i}_{p_{i}}}}w_{n^{i}_{p_{i}}}\big)\big(\omega^{k_{n^{i}_{p_{i}}}}w_{n^{i}_{p_{i}}}-z_{i}\big)},

where
\check{\sigma}(X):=\omega\sigma(X)
.

(It is an interesting open problem to determine under what circumstances the vector
\psi_{\bm{t}}
is non-zero.)

On the other hand, consider the (usual) quadratic Gaudin Hamiltonians in the special case (
2.7
). We refer to this situation as the
extended Gaudin model
, and write
\tilde{\mathcal{H}}^{(i)}
as
\mathcal{H}^{(i)}_{\rm ext}
. Note that

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}^{(i)}_{\rm ext}\in\operatorname{End}\left(M_{\Lambda_{0}}\otimes\bigotimes_{k=0}^{M-1}\bigotimes_{i=1}^{N}M_{\sigma^{k}\Lambda_{i}}\right),\qquad i=0,1,\dots,NM.

(3.8)

The following is then a corollary of Theorem
3.1
.

Corollary 3.4
.

To any critical point of the cyclotomic master function, i.e., to any solution
\bm{t}
to the equations (
2.3
), there corresponds a simultaneous eigenvector of the linear operators
\mathcal{H}^{(i)}_{\rm ext}
,
i=0,1,\dots,nM
, such that
\mathcal{H}^{(0)}_{\rm ext}
has eigenvalue zero and, for each
k=0,\dots,M-1
and
i=1,\dots,N
, the eigenvalue of
\mathcal{H}^{(k+Mi)}_{\rm ext}
is given by
\omega^{-k}E_{i}
with
E_{i}
as in (
3.6
).

Proof.

Let
\bm{t}
be the corresponding (by Lemma
2.9
) cyclotomic critical point of the extended master function
\widehat{\Phi}
. Then the result is a special case of Theorem
3.1
, by substituting (
2.7
) and (
2.11
) into (
3.1
). (To see that
\mathcal{H}^{(0)}_{\rm ext}
has eigenvalue zero note that

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{N}\sum_{s=0}^{M-1}\frac{(\Lambda_{0},\sigma^{s}\Lambda_{i})}{0-\omega^{s}z_{i}}-\sum_{j=1}^{m}\sum_{s=0}^{M-1}\frac{(\Lambda_{0},\sigma^{s}\alpha_{{\mathsf{c}}(j)})}{0-\omega^{s}t_{j}}=0,

because
\sum\limits_{s=0}^{M-1}\omega^{-s}\sigma^{-s}\Lambda_{0}=\Lambda_{0}\sum\limits_{s=0}^{M-1}\omega^{-s}=0
since
\sigma\Lambda_{0}=\Lambda_{0}
and
M>1
.)
∎

In summary, we have the following observation.

Theorem 3.5
.

To any critical point of the cyclotomic master function
there corresponds both a simultaneous eigenvector (
3.7
) of the Hamiltonians
\mathcal{H}^{(i)}
of the cyclotomic Gaudin model and a simultaneous eigenvector (
3.2
) of the Hamiltonians
\mathcal{H}^{(i)}_{\rm ext}
of the extended Gaudin model,
i=1,\dots,n
, with the corresponding eigenvalues equal and in both cases being given by (
3.6
).

Remark 3.6
.

The operators
\mathcal{H}^{(i)}
and
\mathcal{H}^{(i)}_{\rm ext}
are acting in different spaces, (
3.4
) and (
3.8
) respectively. It would be interesting to relate these operators by some means independent of the Bethe ansatz.

4
Cyclotomic generation procedure

In
[
15
,
20
]
a procedure was introduced which generates new critical points of master functions starting from a given initial critical point. There is an “elementary generation” step associated to each
i\in I
. The Zariski closure of the collection of all critical points obtained by recursively applying elementary generations in all possible ways is called the “population” to which the initial critical point belongs.

The extended master functions, (
2.8
) above, are master functions of the standard form (unlike the cyclotomic master functions (
2.2
)). Modulo subtleties coming from the fact that the weight
\Lambda_{0}
at the origin need not be dominant integral, that means the generation procedure can be applied.

In this section we describe this generation procedure and go on to show how, given a cyclotomic critical point, one can obtain new cyclotomic critical points by applying the elementary generation steps in certain carefully chosen combinations. The resulting collections of cyclotomic critical points will be called “cyclotomic populations”.

4.1
Conditions on
\bm{\Lambda_{0}}

In the remainder of the paper we assume that
\sigma
is a diagram automorphism obeying the linking condition (
2.1
). That means for each
i\in I
, either
L_{i}=1
or
L_{i}=2
.

In addition, in this section, Section
4
, we place the following conditions on the weight
\Lambda_{0}\in{\mathfrak{h}}^{\sigma,*}
.

For each
i\in I
such that
L_{i}=1
, we suppose that

\displaystyle\langle\Lambda_{0},\alpha_{i}^{\vee}\rangle\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 0}

(4.1)

and

\displaystyle\langle\Lambda_{0},\alpha_{i}^{\vee}\rangle+1\equiv 0\mod M/M_{i}.

(4.2)

For each
i\in I
such that
L_{i}=2
, we suppose that

\displaystyle 2\langle\Lambda_{0},\alpha^{\vee}_{i}\rangle+1\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 0}.

(4.3)

Remark 4.1
.

One can verify that these conditions are satisfied by the weight
\Lambda_{0}
of (
3.5
) in the case of diagram automorphisms of finite-type Dynkin diagrams. Our assumptions on
\Lambda_{0}
in the treatment of type
A_{R}
in Section
5
below are weaker.

4.2
Tuples of polynomials

To any pair
(\bm{t};\bm{\mathsf{c}})
with
\bm{t}\in{\mathbb{C}}^{\tilde{m}}
and
\bm{\mathsf{c}}\in I^{\tilde{m}}
, we may associate a tuple of polynomials
{\bm{y}}=(y_{1}(x),\dots,y_{r}(x))
, given by

\displaystyle y_{i}(x):=\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}j=1\\
{\mathsf{c}}(j)=i\end{subarray}}^{\tilde{m}}(x-t_{j}),\qquad i\in I.

(4.4)

We say that this tuple
\bm{y}

represents the pair
(\bm{t};\bm{\mathsf{c}})
.
We consider each coordinate
y_{i}(x)
only up to multiplication by a non-zero complex number, since we are only concerned with their zeros. So the tuple
\bm{y}
defines a point in the direct product
{\mathbb{P}}({\mathbb{C}}[x])^{|I|}
of
|I|
copies of the projective space
{\mathbb{P}}({\mathbb{C}}[x])
, where
{\mathbb{C}}[x]
is the vector space of complex polynomials in
x
.

Conversely, given any
\bm{y}\in{\mathbb{P}}({\mathbb{C}}[x])^{|I|}
we may extract the pair
(\bm{t};\bm{\mathsf{c}})\in{\mathbb{C}}^{\tilde{m}}\times I^{\tilde{m}}
such that (
4.4
) holds. This pair is unique up to permutation by an element of
S_{\tilde{m}}
; see (
2.10
).

Define
T_{i}(x)
,
i\in I
, to be

\displaystyle T_{i}(x):=\prod_{s=1}^{N}\prod_{k=0}^{M-1}\big(x-\omega^{k}z_{s}\big)^{\langle\sigma^{k}\Lambda_{s},\alpha^{\vee}_{i}\rangle}.

(4.5)

We say that a tuple of polynomials
{\bm{y}}=(y_{i}(x))_{i\in I}\in{\mathbb{P}}({\mathbb{C}}[x])^{|I|}
is
generic
(
with respect to
(T_{i}(x))_{i\in I})
if for each
i\in I
,

y_{i}(x)
has no root in common with
T_{i}(x)
, or with any
y_{j}(x)
,
j\in I{\setminus}\{i\}
, such that
\langle\alpha_{j},\alpha^{\vee}_{i}\rangle\neq 0
.

Note that if
\bm{y}
represents a critical point of the extended master function
\widehat{\Phi}(\bm{t};\bm{\mathsf{c}};\bm{z};\bm{\Lambda})
, (
2.8
), i.e., its roots obey (
2.9
), then the tuple
\bm{y}
must be generic. (Indeed, if (
2.9
) holds then in particular each summand on the left hand side of (
2.9
) must have non-zero denominator. By definition that implies that the corresponding tuple is generic.)

4.3
Elementary generation: the
\bm{L_{i}=1}
case

Throughout this subsection, we suppose
i\in I
is such that
L_{i}=1
. That means that the simple roots
\alpha_{\sigma^{k}i}
,
i=1,\dots,M_{i}
, are mutually orthogonal. Equivalently it means that the reflections
{\mathsf{s}}_{\sigma^{k}i}\in W
,
i=1,\dots,M_{i}
, are mutually commuting.

Let
y_{i}^{(i)}(x)
be of the form

\displaystyle y_{i}^{(i)}(x)=y_{i}(x)\int^{x}\xi^{\langle\Lambda_{0},\alpha^{\vee}_{i}\rangle}T_{i}(\xi)\prod_{j\in I}y_{j}(\xi)^{-\langle\alpha_{j},\alpha^{\vee}_{i}\rangle}d\xi,

(4.6)

so that
y_{i}^{(i)}(x)
is a solution to the equation

\displaystyle\operatorname{Wr}(y_{i}(x),y_{i}^{(i)}(x))=x^{\langle\Lambda_{0},\alpha^{\vee}_{i}\rangle}T_{i}(x)\prod_{j\in I{\setminus}\{i\}}y_{j}(x)^{-\langle\alpha_{j},\alpha^{\vee}_{i}\rangle},

(4.7)

where
\operatorname{Wr}(f(x),g(x)):=f(x)g^{\prime}(x)-f^{\prime}(x)g(x)
denotes the Wronskian determinant.

Proposition 4.2
.

If
\bm{y}
represents a critical point then
y_{i}^{(i)}(x)
is a polynomial.

Proof.

We have
\langle\Lambda_{0},\alpha^{\vee}_{i}\rangle\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 0}
as in (
4.1
), and for each
s\in\{1,\dots,N\}
,
\Lambda_{s}
is integral dominant so
\langle\Lambda_{s},\alpha^{\vee}_{i}\rangle\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 0}
. So the integrand is a rational function with poles at most at the points
t_{p}
,
p\in\{1,\dots,m\}
, for which
{\mathsf{c}}(p)=i
. Consider such a point
t_{p}
.
Note that

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial x}\log x^{\langle\Lambda_{0},\alpha^{\vee}_{i}\rangle}T_{i}(x)(x-t_{p})^{2}\prod_{j\in I}y_{j}(x)^{-\langle\alpha_{j},\alpha^{\vee}_{i}\rangle}

\displaystyle\qquad{}=\sum_{k=0}^{M-1}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\frac{\langle\sigma^{k}\Lambda_{i},\alpha^{\vee}_{{\mathsf{c}}(p)}\rangle}{x-\omega^{k}z_{i}}+\frac{\langle\Lambda_{0},\alpha^{\vee}_{{\mathsf{c}}(p)}\rangle}{x}-\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}i=1\\
i\neq p\end{subarray}}^{\tilde{m}}\frac{\langle\alpha_{{\mathsf{c}}(i)},\alpha_{{\mathsf{c}}(p)}\rangle}{x-t_{i}}.

(4.8)

This vanishes at
x=t_{p}
by virtue of the critical point equations (
2.9
). It follows that the residue of the integrand at
t_{p}
vanishes: indeed, this residue is

\displaystyle\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial x}x^{\langle\Lambda_{0},\alpha^{\vee}_{i}\rangle}T_{i}(x)(x-t_{p})^{2}\prod_{j\in I}y_{j}(x)^{-\langle\alpha_{j},\alpha^{\vee}_{i}\rangle}\right)_{x=t_{p}},

which vanishes if (
4.8
) vanishes.
This shows that
y_{i}^{(i)}(x)
is an entire function. It is of polynomial growth for large
x
. Therefore it is a polynomial.
∎

If
y_{i}^{(i)}(x)
is any solution to (
4.7
) then so too is
y_{i}^{(i)}(x)+cy_{i}(x)
for any
c\in{\mathbb{C}}
.

Thus, given any tuple
\bm{y}
representing a critical point we have,
for each value of a parameter
c\in{\mathbb{C}}
, a new tuple of polynomials
\bm{y}^{(i)}
, obtained from the tuple
\bm{y}
by replacing
y_{i}(x)
with
y_{i}^{(i)}(x)+cy_{i}(x)
.
We say
\bm{y}^{(i)}
is obtained from
\bm{y}
by
generation in the
i
th direction
, and we call
\bm{y}^{(i)}
the
immediate descendant of
\bm{y}
in the
i
th direction
.

Proposition 4.3

(
[
15
]
)
.

The tuple of polynomials
\bm{y}^{(i)}
is generic for almost all
c
.
If
\bm{y}^{(i)}
is generic then it represents a critical point.

The tuples
\bm{y}^{(i)}
describe a projective line in
{\mathbb{P}}({\mathbb{C}}[x])^{|I|}
.
It will be useful to have the following specific parameterization of this line.
There exists a unique solution
y_{i}^{(i)}(x)
to the equation (
4.7
), call it
y_{i}^{(i)}(x;0)
, such that the coefficient of
x^{\deg y_{i}}
in
y^{(i)}_{i}(x;0)
is zero. Let us define

\displaystyle y_{i}^{(i)}(x;c):=y^{(i)}_{i}(x;0)+cy_{i}(x),

(4.9)

and define
\bm{y}^{(i)}(c)\in{\mathbb{P}}({\mathbb{C}}[x])^{|I|}
to be the tuple obtained from the tuple
\bm{y}
by replacing
y_{i}(x)
with
y^{(i)}_{i}(x;0)+cy_{i}(x)
.

We say generation in the
i
th direction is
degree-increasing
if
\deg y_{i}^{(i)}>\deg y_{i}
for almost all
c
.

Recall that there is a weight at infinity,
\Lambda_{\infty}
, associated to any critical point. For the critical point represented by
\bm{y}
this weight is, cf. (
2.4
),

\displaystyle\Lambda_{\infty}(\bm{y})=\Lambda_{0}+\sum_{s=1}^{N}\sum_{k=0}^{M-1}\sigma^{k}\Lambda_{s}-\sum_{j\in I}\alpha_{j}\deg y_{j}.

(4.10)

For fixed
\Lambda_{0},\Lambda_{1},\dots,\Lambda_{N}
we can think of
\Lambda_{\infty}
as encoding the degrees of the polynomials
y_{j}
.
Note that
\deg y_{i}^{(i)}(x;0)=\deg y_{i}+\langle\Lambda_{\infty},\alpha_{i}^{\vee}\rangle+1
. It follows that the weight at infinity of
\bm{y}^{(i)}(0)
is

\displaystyle\Lambda_{\infty}-\alpha_{i}\big(\langle\Lambda_{\infty},\alpha^{\vee}_{i}\rangle+1\big)=\Lambda_{\infty}-\langle\Lambda_{\infty}+\rho,\alpha^{\vee}_{i}\rangle\alpha_{i}={\mathsf{s}}_{i}\cdot\Lambda_{\infty}.

This establishes the following lemma.

Lemma 4.4
.

Generation in the
i
th direction
(
with
L_{i}=1)
is degree-increasing if and only if
\Lambda_{\infty}
is
i
-dominant, i.e.,
\langle\Lambda_{\infty},\alpha^{\vee}_{i}\rangle\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 0}
.

If generation in the
i
th direction is degree-increasing, then the weight at infinity associated with the critical point represented by
\bm{y}_{i}^{(i)}(c)
is
{\mathsf{s}}_{i}\cdot\Lambda_{\infty}
.
Otherwise it is
\Lambda_{\infty}
for all
c\neq 0

(
and
{\mathsf{s}}_{i}\cdot\Lambda_{\infty}
for
c=0)
.

4.4
Cyclotomic generation: the
\bm{L_{i}=1}
case

We continue to suppose that
i
is such that
L_{i}=1
.

If
\bm{y}
represents a cyclotomic point then its immediate descendant
\bm{y}^{(i)}
in the
i
th direction generically does not. However if, starting from a cyclotomic critical point, we successively generate in
each
of the directions
\sigma^{k}i
,
k=1,\dots,M_{i}
, in turn, in any order, then we can arrange to arrive at a (new) cyclotomic critical point. This is the content of Theorem
4.6
below.

Let
\simeq
denote equality up to a constant (independent of
x
) nonzero factor. Recall the definition (
2.11
) of a cyclotomic point.

Lemma 4.5
.

A tuple of polynomials
\bm{y}
represents a cyclotomic point if and only if

\displaystyle y_{\sigma j}(\omega x)\simeq y_{j}(x)

for all
j\in I
.
If
y_{j}(x)
and
y_{\sigma j}(x)
share the same leading coefficient for all
j\in I
, then the tuple
\bm{y}
represents a cyclotomic point if and only if

\displaystyle y_{\sigma j}(\omega x)=\omega^{\deg y_{j}}y_{j}(x)

for all
j\in I
.

For the rest of this subsection, we suppose
\bm{y}
represents a cyclotomic critical point. Hence in particular
\sigma\Lambda_{\infty}=\Lambda_{\infty}
.
Let
y^{(i)}_{i}(x;c)=y^{(i)}_{i,0}(x)+cy_{i}(x)
be as in (
4.9
). (So
y^{(i)}_{i}(x;c)
is a parameterization of the space of solutions to (
4.7
).) Define
\bm{y}^{(i,\sigma)}(c)
to be the tuple of polynomials given by

\displaystyle y^{(i,\sigma)}_{\sigma^{k}i}\big(\omega^{k}x;c\big):=\omega^{k\deg y^{(i)}_{i}}y^{(i)}_{i}(x;c),\qquad k=0,1,\dots,M_{i}-1,

and
y^{(i,\sigma)}_{j}(x;c)=y_{j}(x)
for
j\in I{\setminus}\sigma^{\mathbb{Z}}i
.
Recall
{\mathsf{s}}_{i}^{\sigma}
from Lemma
2.5
.

Theorem 4.6
.

For almost all
c\in{\mathbb{C}}
, the tuple
\bm{y}^{(i,\sigma)}(c)
represents a cyclotomic critical point. The exceptional values of
c
form a finite subset of
{\mathbb{C}}
.

The weight at infinity of
\bm{y}^{(i,\sigma)}(c)
is
{\mathsf{s}}^{\sigma}_{i}\cdot\Lambda_{\infty}
if
\langle\Lambda_{\infty},\alpha^{\vee,\sigma}_{i}\rangle\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 0}
. Otherwise it is
\Lambda_{\infty}
for all
c\neq 0
, and
{\mathsf{s}}^{\sigma}_{i}\cdot\Lambda_{\infty}
for
c=0
.

Proof.

First let us show that
\bm{y}^{(i,\sigma)}
represents a cyclotomic point for all
c\in{\mathbb{C}}
. Comparing our definition of
\bm{y}^{(i,\sigma)}
with the criterion in Lemma
4.5
, one sees that it is enough to check that

\displaystyle y^{(i)}_{i}\big(\omega^{M_{i}}x;c\big)=\omega^{M_{i}\deg y^{(i)}_{i}}y^{(i)}_{i}(x;c).

Inspecting (
4.6
), we see that this equality holds for all
c\in{\mathbb{C}}
if and only if

\displaystyle\omega^{M_{i}\langle\Lambda_{\infty}+\rho,\alpha_{i}^{\vee}\rangle}=1.

(4.11)

But now, given (
4.10
) and the assumption that
\Lambda_{s}
,
s=1,\dots,n
are integral, the following lemma implies that (
4.11
) holds if and only if we impose the condition (
4.2
) on
\Lambda_{0}
.

Lemma 4.7
.

Suppose
\Lambda\in{\mathfrak{h}}^{*}
is an integral weight. Then, for any
j\in I
,

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{M-1}\langle\sigma^{k}\Lambda,\alpha^{\vee}_{j}\rangle M_{j}\equiv 0\mod M.

Proof.

We have

\displaystyle\left\langle\sum_{k=0}^{M-1}\sigma^{k}\Lambda,\alpha^{\vee}_{j}\right\rangle M_{j}=\left\langle\Lambda,\sum_{k=0}^{M-1}\sigma^{-k}\alpha^{\vee}_{j}\right\rangle M_{j}

\displaystyle\hphantom{\left\langle\sum_{k=0}^{M-1} \sigma^k\Lambda,\alpha^\vee_j \right\rangle M_j }{}=\left\langle\Lambda,\frac{M}{M_{j}}\sum_{k=0}^{M_{j}-1}\alpha^{\vee}_{j}\right\rangle M_{j}=M\left\langle\Lambda,\sum_{k=0}^{M_{j}-1}\alpha^{\vee}_{j}\right\rangle\in M{\mathbb{Z}}.

∎

Now we show that
\bm{y}^{(i,\sigma)}
represents a critical point for all but finitely many
c\in{\mathbb{C}}
.
Note that from definition (
4.5
) we have

\displaystyle T_{\sigma j}(\omega x)=\omega^{\big\langle\sum\limits_{s=1}^{N}\sum\limits_{k=0}^{M-1}\sigma^{k}\Lambda_{s},\alpha^{\vee}_{j}\big\rangle}T_{j}(x),\qquad j\in I.

(4.12)

Hence, in view of (
4.10
),

\displaystyle x^{\langle\Lambda_{0},\alpha^{\vee}_{\sigma i}\rangle}T_{\sigma i}(\omega x)\prod_{j\in I}y_{j}(\omega x)^{-\langle\alpha_{j},\alpha^{\vee}_{\sigma i}\rangle}=x^{\langle\Lambda_{0},\alpha^{\vee}_{i}\rangle}T_{\sigma i}(\omega x)\prod_{j\in I}y_{\sigma j}(\omega x)^{-\langle\alpha_{\sigma j},\alpha^{\vee}_{\sigma i}\rangle}

\displaystyle\hphantom{x^{\langle\Lambda_0,\alpha^\vee_{\sigma i}\rangle} T_{\sigma i}(\omega x) \prod_{j\in I} y_j(\omega x)^{-\langle\alpha_j,\alpha^\vee_{\sigma i}\rangle}}{}=\omega^{\langle\Lambda_{\infty},\alpha^{\vee}_{i}\rangle}\left(x^{\langle\Lambda_{0},\alpha^{\vee}_{i}\rangle}T_{i}(x)\prod_{j\in I}y_{j}(x)^{-\langle\alpha_{j},\alpha^{\vee}_{i}\rangle}\right).

Note also that since
L_{i}=1
, no node
j
in the orbit of
i
is linked by an edge of the Dynkin diagram to
i
. That is, no
y_{j}
for
j
in the orbit of
i
appears on the right of (
4.7
).
Hence, for
k=1,\dots,M_{i}-1
,
y^{(i,\sigma)}_{\sigma^{k}i}(x;c)
obeys the equation

\displaystyle\operatorname{Wr}(y_{\sigma^{k}i}(x),y^{(i,\sigma)}_{\sigma^{k}i}(x;c))=x^{\langle\Lambda_{0},\alpha^{\vee}_{\sigma^{k}i}\rangle}T_{\sigma^{k}i}(x)\prod_{j\in I{\setminus}\{\sigma^{k}i\}}y_{j}(x)^{-\langle\alpha_{j},\alpha^{\vee}_{\sigma^{k}i}\rangle}.

and the tuple
\bm{y}^{(i,\sigma)}
is indeed the result of generating in each of the directions
i,\sigma i,\dots,\sigma^{M_{i}-1}i
(in any order).
It follows from Proposition
4.3
that
\bm{y}^{(i,\sigma)}
is generic for almost all
c
, and represents a critical point whenever it is generic.

The statements about the weight at infinity follow from Lemma
4.4
and Section
2.3
.
This completes the proof of Theorem
4.6
.
∎

4.5
Elementary generation: the
\bm{L_{i}=2}
case

For this subsection we suppose that
i\in I
is such that
L_{i}=2
. That implies
M_{i}
is even and the restriction of the Dynkin diagram to the nodes
\sigma^{\mathbb{Z}}i
consists of
\frac{M_{i}}{2}\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 1}
disconnected copies of the Dynkin diagram of type
{\mathrm{A}}_{2}
, as sketched below:

\sigma^{0}i
\sigma^{0}{\bar{\imath}}
\sigma^{1}i
\sigma^{1}{\bar{\imath}}
\sigma^{2}i
\sigma^{2}{\bar{\imath}}
\sigma^{M_{i}/2-1}i
\sigma^{M_{i}/2-1}{\bar{\imath}}

Here, for brevity, we write
{\bar{\imath}}:=\sigma^{M_{i}/2}i
.

Remark 4.8
.

Among finite and affine types, only the case
M_{i}/2=1
occurs.

We define
y_{i}^{(i)}(x)
by

\displaystyle y_{i}^{(i)}(x):=y_{i}(x)x^{-{\langle\Lambda_{0},\alpha^{\vee}_{i}\rangle}-1}\int_{0}^{x}\xi^{{\langle\Lambda_{0},\alpha^{\vee}_{i}\rangle}}T_{i}(\xi)\prod_{j\in I}y_{j}(\xi)^{-\langle\alpha_{j},\alpha^{\vee}_{i}\rangle}d\xi.

Here the limits
\int_{0}^{x}
mean that
y^{(i)}_{i}(x)

is holomorphic at
x=0
. This condition defines the integral uniquely, since
\langle\Lambda_{0},\alpha^{\vee}_{i}\rangle\notin{\mathbb{Z}}
by our assumption (
4.3
).

Proposition 4.9
.

If
\bm{y}
represents a critical point then
y^{(i)}_{i}(x)
is a polynomial. It has degree
\deg y^{(i)}_{i}=\deg y_{i}+\langle\Lambda_{\infty}-\Lambda_{0},\alpha^{\vee}_{i}\rangle
.

Proof.

The proof is as for Proposition
4.2
.
∎

Let
\bm{y}^{(i)}=(y^{(i)}_{j}(x))_{j\in I}
be the tuple of polynomials whose
i
th component
y^{(i)}_{i}(x)
is as above, and whose remaining components are the same as those of
\bm{y}
, i.e.,

\displaystyle y^{(i)}_{j}(x)=y_{j}(x)\qquad\text{ for all }\quad j\in I{\setminus}\{i\}.

Let
(\bm{t}^{(i)};\bm{\mathsf{c}}^{(i)})
denote the pair represented by this tuple in the sense of Section
4.2
. It turns out that
\bm{t}^{(i)}
is not in general a critical point of the extended master function
\widehat{\Phi}(\bm{t}^{(i)};\bm{\mathsf{c}}^{(i)};\bm{z};\bm{\Lambda})
, i.e., it does not in general obey the equations (
2.9
). Instead, the following result gives the analogous collection of equations that it does obey, provided
\bm{y}^{(i)}
is generic.

Proposition 4.10
.

If
\bm{y}
represents a critical point
and

\displaystyle(-1)^{\langle\Lambda_{\infty},\alpha^{\vee}_{i}+\alpha^{\vee}_{\bar{\imath}}\rangle}=(-1)^{\langle\Lambda_{0},\alpha^{\vee}_{i}+\alpha^{\vee}_{\bar{\imath}}\rangle}=(-1)^{2\langle\Lambda_{0},\alpha^{\vee}_{i}\rangle}=-1,

using
\sigma\Lambda_{0}=\Lambda_{0}
and the property (
4.1
).
∎

Lemma 4.22
.

For all but finitely many
c\in{\mathbb{C}}
,
y^{(i,{\bar{\imath}},i)}_{\bar{\imath}}(x;c)
and
y^{(i,{\bar{\imath}},i)}_{\bar{\imath}}(-x;c)
have no root in common.

Proof.

Recall
y^{(i,{\bar{\imath}},i)}_{\bar{\imath}}(x;c)=y^{({\bar{\imath}},i)}_{\bar{\imath}}(x;c)
. Consider the leading behaviour in small
c
. As
c\to 0
,
\deg y_{\bar{\imath}}
of the roots of
y^{({\bar{\imath}},i)}_{\bar{\imath}}(x;c)
tend to the
\deg y_{\bar{\imath}}
roots of
y_{\bar{\imath}}(x)
. By the assumption that
\bm{y}
was generic and cyclotomic, none of these are roots of
y_{\bar{\imath}}(-x)\simeq y_{i}(x)
.

Recall (
4.19
) and the fact that
\langle\Lambda_{\infty}+\rho,\alpha^{\vee}_{i}+\alpha^{\vee}_{\bar{\imath}}\rangle
is odd, by the assumption (
4.3
).

If
\langle\Lambda_{\infty}+\rho,\alpha^{\vee}_{i}+\alpha^{\vee}_{\bar{\imath}}\rangle<0
, then these are all the roots of
y^{({\bar{\imath}},i)}_{\bar{\imath}}(x;c)
.

If
\langle\Lambda_{\infty}+\rho,\alpha^{\vee}_{i}+\alpha^{\vee}_{\bar{\imath}}\rangle\not<0
then the remaining

\langle\Lambda_{\infty}+\rho,\alpha^{\vee}_{i}+\alpha^{\vee}_{\bar{\imath}}\rangle>0
roots of
y^{({\bar{\imath}},i)}_{\bar{\imath}}(x;c)
tend to the roots of the equation
cx^{\langle\Lambda_{\infty}+\rho,\alpha^{\vee}_{i}+\alpha^{\vee}_{\bar{\imath}}\rangle}+1=0
. This limiting set of roots multiplied by
-1
does not intersect itself. This implies the lemma.
∎

5.2
Vector spaces of quasi-polynomials

Let

\displaystyle{\tilde{T}}_{i}(x)=x^{\langle\Lambda_{0},\alpha^{\vee}_{i}\rangle}\prod_{s=1}^{N}(x-z_{s})^{\langle\Lambda_{s},\alpha^{\vee}_{i}\rangle}(x+z_{s})^{\langle\Lambda_{R+1-s},\alpha^{\vee}_{i}\rangle},\qquad i\in I.

Thus
{\tilde{T}}_{i}(x)=x^{\langle\Lambda_{0},\alpha^{\vee}_{i}\rangle}T_{i}(x)
with
T_{i}(x)
as in (
4.5
).

In view of (
5.1
),
{\tilde{T}}_{i}(x)\in{\mathbb{C}}[x]
for all
i\notin\{p,R+1-p\}
. If
0<p<R+1-p<R
then
{\tilde{T}}_{p}(x)
and
{\tilde{T}}_{R+1-p}(x)
belong to
x^{-\frac{1}{2}}{\mathbb{C}}[x]
. If
p=R+1-p
then
{\tilde{T}}_{p}(x)\in{\mathbb{C}}[x]
.

We define the
degree
,
\deg p
, of a Laurent polynomial
p(x)\in{\mathbb{C}}[x^{\pm\frac{1}{2}}]
to be the leading power of
x
(for large
x
) that appears in
p(x)
with non-zero coefficient.

We will call any polynomial in
x^{\frac{1}{2}}
a
quasi-polynomial
.

A vector space
V\subset{\mathbb{C}}\big[x^{\frac{1}{2}}\big]
of quasi-polynomials is
decomposable
if

\displaystyle V=V\cap{\mathbb{C}}[x]\oplus V\cap x^{\frac{1}{2}}{\mathbb{C}}[x].

A tuple of quasi-polynomials is
decomposable
if each element lies in either
{\mathbb{C}}[x]
or
x^{\frac{1}{2}}{\mathbb{C}}[x]
. In particular, a
decomposable basis
of a decomposable vector space
V\subset{\mathbb{C}}\big[x^{\frac{1}{2}}\big]
is one in which each basis vector lies in either
{\mathbb{C}}[x]
or
x^{\frac{1}{2}}{\mathbb{C}}[x]
.

Define the
divided Wronksian determinant
of quasi-polynomials
u_{1},\dots,u_{k}\in{\mathbb{C}}\big[x^{\frac{1}{2}}\big]
by

\displaystyle\operatorname{Wr}^{\dagger}(u_{1},\dots,u_{k}):=\frac{\operatorname{Wr}(u_{1},\dots,u_{k})}{{\tilde{T}}_{1}^{k-1}{\tilde{T}}_{2}^{k-2}\cdots{\tilde{T}}_{k-1}},\qquad\operatorname{Wr}(u_{1},\dots,u_{k}):=\det\left(\frac{d^{j-1}u_{i}}{dx^{j-1}}\right)_{i,j=1}^{k},

for
k=1,\dots,R+1
.

Define

\displaystyle\Lambda:=\Lambda_{0}+\sum_{s=1}^{N}(\Lambda_{s}+\sigma\Lambda_{s}),

(5.2)

and suppose
\tilde{\Lambda}_{\infty}\in{\mathfrak{h}}^{*}
is a dominant weight such that
\Lambda-\tilde{\Lambda}_{\infty}=\sum\limits_{i\in I}k_{i}\alpha_{i}
for some
k_{i}\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 0}
.
Such a weight defines numbers
d_{1},\dots,d_{R+1}\in{\mathbb{Z}}/2
,
0\leq d_{1}<\dots<d_{R+1}
,
by

\displaystyle d_{1}:=\langle\Lambda-\tilde{\Lambda}_{\infty},\epsilon_{1}\rangle,\qquad d_{k}:=\langle\Lambda-({\mathsf{s}}_{1}\cdots{\mathsf{s}}_{k-1})\cdot\tilde{\Lambda}_{\infty},\epsilon_{1}\rangle,\qquad k=2,\dots,R+1.

(5.3)

Lemma 5.1
.

We have

\displaystyle d_{k}=d_{1}+\langle\tilde{\Lambda}_{\infty}+\rho,\alpha^{\vee}_{1}+\dots+\alpha^{\vee}_{k-1}\rangle,\qquad k=2,\dots,R+1.

(5.4)

Hence, for all
p>0
,
d_{1},\dots,d_{p}
and
d_{R+2-p},\dots,d_{R+1}
are integers while
d_{p+1},\dots,d_{R+1-p}
are half odd integers, i.e., have the form
m+\frac{1}{2}
for
m\in{\mathbb{Z}}
. If
p=0
then
d_{1},\dots,d_{R+1}
are all integers.

Proof.

We have
d_{k}-d_{1}=\langle\tilde{\Lambda}_{\infty}+\rho-({\mathsf{s}}_{1}\cdots{\mathsf{s}}_{k-1})(\tilde{\Lambda}_{\infty}+\rho),\epsilon_{1}\rangle=\langle\tilde{\Lambda}_{\infty}+\rho,\epsilon_{1}-({\mathsf{s}}_{k-1}\cdots{\mathsf{s}}_{1})\epsilon_{1}\rangle=\langle\tilde{\Lambda}_{\infty}+\rho,\epsilon_{1}-\epsilon_{k}\rangle
and hence (
5.4
).
∎

Definition 5.2
.

We say a vector space of quasi-polynomials
{\mathcal{K}}\!\subset\!{\mathbb{C}}\big[x^{\frac{1}{2}}\big]
has
frame

\tilde{T}_{1},\dots,\tilde{T}_{R};\tilde{\Lambda}_{\infty}
if the following conditions hold:

(i)

There is a basis
(u_{k}(x))_{k=1}^{R+1}
of
{\mathcal{K}}
such that
\deg u_{k}=d_{k}
for each
k=1,\dots,R+1
.

(ii)

For any
z\in{\mathbb{C}}{\setminus}\{0\}
and

,
k=1,\dots,R+1
, the divided Wronskian
\operatorname{Wr}^{\dagger}(v_{1}
,
\dots,v_{k})
is regular at
z
, and moreover,
\operatorname{Wr}^{\dagger}(v_{1},\dots,v_{k})
is nonzero at
z
for suitable
v_{1},\dots,v_{k}
.

(iii)

For all
v_{1},\dots,v_{k}\in{\mathcal{K}}
,
k=1,\dots,R+1
, the divided Wronskian
\operatorname{Wr}^{\dagger}(v_{1},\dots,v_{k})
has at
x=0
an expansion of the form
\sum\limits_{m\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 0}/2}a_{m}x^{m}
and moreover this expansion has nonzero
a_{0}
for suitable
v_{1},\dots,v_{k}
.

In the remainder of this section,
{\mathcal{K}}
will denote a decomposable vector space of quasi-polynomials with frame
\tilde{T}_{1},\dots,\tilde{T}_{R};\tilde{\Lambda}_{\infty}
.

Conditions (
ii
) and (

) specify the

of
{\mathcal{K}}
at every point

. Condition (

) specifies the ramification conditions at

. See
[
15
, Section 5.5]
. The degrees

will be called the

at infinity

Note that conditions (

) and (

) together imply in particular that

has no

. That is, there is no

such that

for all

. They also imply the following important lemma.

Lemma 5.3
.

For all
v_{1},\dots,v_{k}\in{\mathcal{K}}
,
k=1,\dots,R+1
, the divided Wronskian
\operatorname{Wr}^{\dagger}(v_{1},\dots,v_{k})
is a quasi-polynomial.

Since

is decomposable it follows from condition (

admits a decomposable basis

such that

for each

.
We call any such basis a

Lemma 5.4
.

Any two special bases
(u_{k})_{k=1}^{R+1}
and
(u^{\prime}_{k})_{k=1}^{R+1}
are related by a triangular change of basis,
u^{\prime}_{k}=\sum\limits_{j\leq k}a_{kj}u_{j}
, such that
a_{kj}=0
whenever
d_{k}-d_{j}\notin{\mathbb{Z}}
.

Lemma 5.5
.

Let
m\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 1}
. Let
n_{1},\dots,n_{m}
be non-negative integers. Then

\displaystyle\operatorname{Wr}(x^{n_{1}},\dots,x^{n_{m}})=x^{\sum\limits_{i=1}^{m}n_{i}-\frac{m(m-1)}{2}}\prod_{1\leq j<i\leq m}(n_{i}-n_{j}).

Lemma 5.6
.

Let
(u_{i}(x))_{i=1}^{R+1}
be a special basis of
{\mathcal{K}}
. Then

\displaystyle\operatorname{Wr}^{\dagger}(u_{1},\dots,u_{R+1})=\prod_{1\leq j<i\leq R+1}(d_{i}-d_{j}).

Proof.

By Lemma
5.3
,
\operatorname{Wr}^{\dagger}(u_{1},\dots,u_{R+1})\in{\mathbb{C}}\big[x^{\frac{1}{2}}\big]
. We must show that it has degree zero and compute the constant term.
From the condition that
\Lambda-\tilde{\Lambda}_{\infty}\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 0}[\alpha_{i}]_{i\in I}
it follows that

0=\langle\Lambda-\tilde{\Lambda}_{\infty},-(R+1)\epsilon_{1}+R\alpha^{\vee}_{1}+(R-1)\alpha^{\vee}_{2}+\dots+2\alpha^{\vee}_{R-1}+\alpha^{\vee}_{R}\rangle

and therefore

\displaystyle(R+1)d_{1}=\langle\Lambda-\tilde{\Lambda}_{\infty},R\alpha^{\vee}_{1}+(R-1)\alpha^{\vee}_{2}+\dots+2\alpha^{\vee}_{R-1}+\alpha^{\vee}_{R}\rangle.

(5.5)

Then (
5.4
) implies

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{R+1}d_{i}-\frac{(R+1)R}{2}=\langle\Lambda,R\alpha^{\vee}_{1}+(R-1)\alpha^{\vee}_{2}+\dots+2\alpha^{\vee}_{R-1}+\alpha^{\vee}_{R}\rangle.

(5.6)

The result follows by Lemma

.
∎

Corollary 5.7
.

\operatorname{Wr}^{\dagger}(v_{1},\dots,v_{R+1})
is a constant
(
independent of

for all

.

Let
(u_{k})_{k=1}^{R+1}
be a special basis of

. Introduce the subspaces

\displaystyle{\mathcal{K}}_{\textup{Sp}}:=\operatorname{span}_{\mathbb{C}}(u_{1},\dots,u_{p})\oplus\operatorname{span}_{\mathbb{C}}(u_{R+2-p},\dots,u_{R+1}),

\displaystyle{\mathcal{K}}_{\textup{O}}:=\operatorname{span}_{\mathbb{C}}(u_{p+1},\dots,u_{R+1-p}),

so that

\displaystyle{\mathcal{K}}={\mathcal{K}}_{\textup{Sp}}\oplus{\mathcal{K}}_{\textup{O}}.

By Lemma
5.4
, these definitions of do not depend on the choice of special basis
(u_{k})_{k=1}^{R+1}
.
By Lemma

we have that, whenever

, then

\displaystyle{\mathcal{K}}_{\textup{Sp}}={\mathcal{K}}\cap{\mathbb{C}}[x],\qquad{\mathcal{K}}_{\textup{O}}={\mathcal{K}}\cap x^{\frac{1}{2}}{\mathbb{C}}[x].

(5.7)

Exceptionally, when

, we have

,

.

Given a decomposable subspace

, we write

\displaystyle\operatorname{sdim}V:=(\dim V\cap{\mathcal{K}}_{\textup{Sp}}|\dim V\cap{\mathcal{K}}_{\textup{O}}).

5.3
Flags in
\bm{{\mathcal{K}}}

Let
F\!L({\mathcal{K}})
denote the
space of full (i.e.,

-step) flags in
{\mathcal{K}}
.

We say an

{\mathcal{F}}=\{0=F_{0}\subset F_{1}\subset F_{2}\subset\dots\subset F_{r}={\mathcal{K}}\}
in
{\mathcal{K}}
is
decomposable
if each
F_{k}
is decomposable.

The space of decomposable full flags in
{\mathcal{K}}
has
\binom{R+1}{2p}
connected components.
These connected components are labeled by
2p
-element subsets
Q\subset\{1,\dots,R+1\}
. Define
F\!L_{Q}({\mathcal{K}})
to be the subset consisting of the flags
{\mathcal{F}}=\{0=F_{0}\subset F_{1}\subset\dots\subset F_{R+1}={\mathcal{K}}\}
such that for each
k
,

\displaystyle\operatorname{sdim}F_{k}-\operatorname{sdim}F_{k-1}=\begin{cases}(1|0)&\text{if}\quad k\in Q,\\
(0|1)&\text{if}\quad k\notin Q.\end{cases}

We call elements of
F\!L_{Q}({\mathcal{K}})

flags of type
Q
.

For each
Q
the variety
F\!L_{Q}({\mathcal{K}})
is isomorphic to the direct product of full flag spaces
F\!L({\mathcal{K}}_{\textup{Sp}})\times F\!L({\mathcal{K}}_{\textup{O}})
. The isomorphism

\displaystyle\eta_{Q}\colon\ F\!L({\mathcal{K}}_{\textup{Sp}})\times F\!L({\mathcal{K}}_{\textup{O}})\to F\!L_{Q}({\mathcal{K}})

(5.8)

sends a pair of flags
F_{1,+}\subset\dots\subset F_{2p,+}
,
F_{1,-}\subset\dots\subset F_{R+1-2p,-}
to the flag
F_{1}\subset\dots\subset F_{R}
, where

F_{k}=F_{k_{1},+}\oplus F_{k_{2},-}
,
k_{1}=|Q\cap\{1,\dots,k\}|
,
k_{2}=k-k_{1}
.

Call a
2p
-element subset
Q\subset\{1,\dots,R+1\}

symmetric
if
Q
is
invariant with respect to the involution
k\mapsto R+2-k
.
In particular, the following subset
S
is symmetric

\displaystyle S:=\{1,\dots,p,R+2-p,\dots,R+1\}.

(5.9)

If
(u_{k})_{k=1}^{R+1}
is a special basis of
{\mathcal{K}}
then the full flag
{\mathcal{F}}=\{0=F_{0}\subset F_{1}\subset F_{2}\subset\dots\subset F_{R+1}={\mathcal{K}}\}
defined by

\displaystyle F_{k}=\operatorname{span}_{\mathbb{C}}(u_{1},\dots,u_{k}),\qquad k=1,\dots,R+1,

(5.10)

belongs to
F\!L_{S}({\mathcal{K}})
. By Lemma
5.4
this flag is independent of the choice of special basis.

To any full flag
{\mathcal{F}}=\{0\subset F_{1}\subset F_{2}\subset\dots\subset F_{R+1}={\mathcal{K}}\}
in

one can associate a tuple
\bm{y}^{\mathcal{F}}=(y_{i}(x))_{i=1}^{R}\in{\mathbb{P}}\big({\mathbb{C}}\big[x^{\frac{1}{2}}\big]\big)^{R}
. Namely, let
(u^{\mathcal{F}}_{k}(x))_{k=1}^{R+1}
be any basis of
{\mathcal{K}}
such that

\displaystyle F_{k}=\operatorname{span}_{\mathbb{C}}\big(u^{\mathcal{F}}_{1},\dots,u^{\mathcal{F}}_{k}\big),\qquad k=1,\dots,R+1.

(we say such a basis is
adjusted to

{\mathcal{F}}
)
and then let

\displaystyle y^{\mathcal{F}}_{k}:=\operatorname{Wr}^{\dagger}\big(u^{\mathcal{F}}_{1},\dots,u^{\mathcal{F}}_{k}\big),\qquad k=1,\dots,R.

(5.11)

By Lemma
5.3
, these are quasi-polynomials.

We have the shifted action of the Weyl group of type
A_{R}
on weights as in Section
2.1
. The weight at infinity
\Lambda_{\infty}(\bm{y}^{\mathcal{F}})
, as in (
4.10
), belongs to the shifted Weyl orbit of
\tilde{\Lambda}_{\infty}

[
15
, Section 3.6]
. It is equal to
\tilde{\Lambda}_{\infty}
if and only if
{\mathcal{F}}
is the flag given in (

).

The map
{\mathcal{F}}\mapsto\bm{y}^{\mathcal{F}}
defines a morphism of varieties,

\displaystyle\beta\colon\ F\!L({\mathcal{K}})\to{\mathbb{P}}\big({\mathbb{C}}\big[x^{1/2}\big]\big)^{R}.

This morphism
\beta
defines an isomorphism of

onto its image, as in Lemmas 5.14–5.16 of

]
.

Lemma 5.8
.

The image
\beta(F\!L_{S}({\mathcal{K}}))
of the variety of flags of type
S
lies in
{\mathbb{P}}({\mathbb{C}}[x])^{R}
, i.e., consists of tuples of polynomials.

Proof.

In the exceptional case
p=0
no fractional powers are present at all and the result is clear. Suppose

.
Let
{\mathcal{F}}\in F\!L_{S}({\mathcal{K}})
and let
(u_{k}^{\mathcal{F}})_{k=1}^{R+1}
be a basis of
{\mathcal{K}}
adjusted to
{\mathcal{F}}
. By inspection one sees that because

,
\operatorname{Wr}(u^{\mathcal{F}}_{1},\dots,u^{\mathcal{F}}_{k})
lies in

(resp.

) for precisely those

lies in
{\mathbb{C}}[x^{\pm 1}]
(resp.

). For each

.

Lemma 5.9
.

The tuple
\beta({\mathcal{F}})=\bm{y}^{\mathcal{F}}
is decomposable if and only if
{\mathcal{F}}
is a decomposable flag.
If
{\mathcal{F}}
is a decomposable flag of type

then

\displaystyle y_{k}^{\mathcal{F}}\in\begin{cases}\phantom{x^{\frac{1}{2}}}{\mathbb{C}}[x]&\text{if}\quad\left|S\triangle Q\cap\{1,\dots,k\}\right|\in 2{\mathbb{Z}},\\

.
In particular

.

5.4
Fundamental differential operator and the recovery theorem

To any given a tuple
\bm{y}=(y_{i}(x))_{i=1}^{R}\in{\mathbb{P}}\big({\mathbb{C}}\big[x^{\frac{1}{2}}\big]\big)^{R}
of quasi-polynomials, we may associate a differential operator
{\mathcal{D}}(\bm{y})
, defined by

\displaystyle{\mathcal{D}}(\bm{y}):=\left(\partial-\log^{\prime}\frac{{\tilde{T}}_{1}{\tilde{T}}_{2}\cdots{\tilde{T}}_{R}}{y_{R}}\right)\left(\partial-\log^{\prime}\frac{y_{R}{\tilde{T}}_{1}{\tilde{T}}_{2}\cdots{\tilde{T}}_{R-1}}{y_{R-1}}\right)\cdots

\displaystyle\hphantom{{\mathcal{D}}(\bm{y}) =}{}\times\left(\partial-\log^{\prime}\frac{y_{2}{\tilde{T}}_{1}}{y_{1}}\right)\big(\partial-\log^{\prime}y_{1}\big)=\overset{\longrightarrow}{\prod_{i=0}^{R}}\left(\partial-\log^{\prime}\frac{y_{R+1-i}\prod\limits_{j=1}^{R-i}{\tilde{T}}_{j}}{y_{R-i}}\right),

with the understanding that
y_{0}=y_{R+1}=1
. Here
\partial:=\partial/\partial x
and
\log^{\prime}f:=f^{\prime}(x)/f(x)
.

Theorem 5.10

(
[
15
, Lemma 5.6]
)
.

Let
\bm{y}\in\beta(F\!L({\mathcal{K}}))
. Then
{\mathcal{K}}=\ker{\mathcal{D}}
.

5.5
The dual space
\bm{{\mathcal{K}}^{\dagger}}

Let
{\mathcal{K}}^{\dagger}
be the complex vector space

\displaystyle{\mathcal{K}}^{\dagger}:=\operatorname{span}_{\mathbb{C}}\big\{{\operatorname{Wr}}^{\dagger}(v_{1},\dots,v_{R})\colon v_{1},\dots,v_{R}\in{\mathcal{K}}\big\}\subset{\mathbb{C}}\big[x^{\frac{1}{2}}\big].

The space
{\mathcal{K}}^{\dagger}
is a space of quasi-polynomials by Lemma
5.3
.
The spaces
{\mathcal{K}}^{\dagger}
and
{\mathcal{K}}
are dual with respect to the pairing

\displaystyle(\cdot,\cdot)\colon\ {\mathcal{K}}^{\dagger}\times{\mathcal{K}}\to{\mathbb{C}}

defined by

\displaystyle\big(v_{1},\operatorname{Wr}^{\dagger}(v_{2},\dots,v_{R+1})\big):=\operatorname{Wr}^{\dagger}(v_{1},v_{2},\dots,v_{R+1}).

Given any basis
(u_{i}(x))_{i=1}^{R+1}
of
{\mathcal{K}}
there is a basis
(W_{i}(x))_{i=1}^{R+1}
of
{\mathcal{K}}^{\dagger}
defined by

\displaystyle W_{i}:=\operatorname{Wr}^{\dagger}(u_{1},\dots,\widehat{u}_{i},\dots,u_{R+1})\in{\mathcal{K}}^{\dagger},\qquad i=1,\dots,R+1,

where
\widehat{u}_{i}
denotes omission. We have

\displaystyle(u_{i},W_{j})=0\qquad\text{if}\quad i\neq j,\qquad(u_{i},W_{i})\neq 0.

Let
d^{\dagger}_{1}>\dots>d^{\dagger}_{R+1}
be the numbers given by

\displaystyle d^{\dagger}_{R+1}:=-\langle\Lambda-\tilde{\Lambda}_{\infty},\epsilon_{R+1}\rangle,\qquad d^{\dagger}_{k}:=-\langle\Lambda-({\mathsf{s}}_{R}\cdots{\mathsf{s}}_{k})\cdot\tilde{\Lambda}_{\infty},\epsilon_{R+1}\rangle,\qquad k=1,\dots,R,

cf. (
5.3
). We have

\displaystyle d^{\dagger}_{k}=d^{\dagger}_{R+1}+\langle\tilde{\Lambda}_{\infty}+\rho,\alpha^{\vee}_{k}+\dots+\alpha^{\vee}_{R}\rangle,\qquad k=1,\dots,R,

(5.12)

by an argument as for Lemma
5.1
.

Lemma 5.11
.

Let
(u_{i}(x))_{i=1}^{R+1}
be a special basis of
{\mathcal{K}}
. Then
\deg W_{k}=d^{\dagger}_{k}
,
k=1,\dots,R+1
, and the basis
(W_{k})_{k=1}^{R+1}
is decomposable.

Proof.

From
\Lambda-\tilde{\Lambda}_{\infty}\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 0}[\alpha_{i}]_{i\in I}
we have

0=\langle\Lambda-\tilde{\Lambda}_{\infty},(R+1)\epsilon_{R+1}+\alpha^{\vee}_{1}+2\alpha^{\vee}_{2}+\dots+(R-1)\alpha^{\vee}_{R-1}+R\alpha^{\vee}_{R}\rangle
, and hence

\displaystyle(R+1)d^{\dagger}_{R+1}=\langle\Lambda-\tilde{\Lambda}_{\infty},\alpha^{\vee}_{1}+2\alpha^{\vee}_{2}+\dots+R\alpha^{\vee}_{R}\rangle.

(5.13)

Now

\displaystyle\deg W_{R+1}=\deg\operatorname{Wr}^{\dagger}(u_{1},\dots,u_{R})=\sum_{i=1}^{R}d_{i}-\frac{R(R-1)}{2}-\langle\Lambda,(R-1)\alpha^{\vee}_{1}+\dots+\alpha^{\vee}_{R-1}\rangle

\displaystyle\hphantom{\deg W_{R+1}}{}=Rd_{1}+\langle\tilde{\Lambda}_{\infty}+\rho,(R-1)\alpha^{\vee}_{1}+\dots+\alpha^{\vee}_{R-1}\rangle-\frac{R(R-1)}{2}

\displaystyle\hphantom{\deg W_{R+1}=}{}-\langle\Lambda,(R-1)\alpha^{\vee}_{1}+\dots+\alpha^{\vee}_{R-1}\rangle

\displaystyle\hphantom{\deg W_{R+1}}{}=Rd_{1}+\langle\tilde{\Lambda}_{\infty}-\Lambda,(R-1)\alpha^{\vee}_{1}+\dots+\alpha^{\vee}_{R-1}\rangle,

where we used (
5.4
). Hence, using (
5.5
), we have

\displaystyle\begin{split}&(R+1)\deg W_{R+1}=R\langle\Lambda-\tilde{\Lambda}_{\infty},R\alpha^{\vee}_{1}+(R-1)\alpha^{\vee}_{2}+\dots+2\alpha^{\vee}_{R-1}+\alpha^{\vee}_{R}\rangle\\
&\hphantom{(R+1)\deg W_{R+1}=}{}-(R+1)\langle\Lambda-\tilde{\Lambda}_{\infty},(R-1)\alpha^{\vee}_{1}+\dots+\alpha^{\vee}_{R-1}\rangle\\
&\hphantom{(R+1)\deg W_{R+1}}{}=\langle\Lambda-\tilde{\Lambda}_{\infty},\alpha^{\vee}_{1}+2\alpha^{\vee}_{2}+\dots+R\alpha^{\vee}_{R}\rangle\end{split}

since
R(R+1-k)-(R+1)(R-k)=k
.
Comparing this with (
5.13
) we see that
d^{\dagger}_{R+1}=\deg W_{R+1}
.
Then for the remaining
W_{k}
, we note that
\deg W_{R+1}-\deg W_{k}=d_{k}-d_{R+1}
for
k=1,\dots,R
.
And by (
5.4
) and (
5.12
),

\displaystyle d_{k}-d_{R+1}=-\langle\tilde{\Lambda}_{\infty}+\rho,\alpha^{\vee}_{k}+\dots+\alpha^{\vee}_{R}\rangle=d^{\dagger}_{R+1}-d^{\dagger}_{k}.

Thus
d^{\dagger}_{k}=\deg W_{k}
for
k=1,\dots,R+1
. Finally, since the basis
(u_{k})_{k=1}^{R+1}
is decomposable and each
{\tilde{T}}_{k}
lies in either
{\mathbb{C}}[x^{\pm 1}]
or
x^{\frac{1}{2}}{\mathbb{C}}[x^{\pm 1}]
, it follows that
(W_{k})_{k=1}^{R+1}
is decomposable.
∎

5.6
Cyclotomic points and cyclotomic self-duality

Let us fix
(-1)^{m}:=e^{m\pi i}
for
m\in{\mathbb{Z}}/2
.
Then given a monomial
q(x)=x^{m}
,
m\in{\mathbb{Z}}/2
, we define
q(-x):=(-1)^{m}x^{m}
. We extend the transformation
q(x)\mapsto q(-x)
to Laurent polynomials in
x^{\frac{1}{2}}
by linearity.

We say that
{\mathcal{K}}
is
cyclotomically self-dual
if

\displaystyle u(x)\in{\mathcal{K}}\ \Leftrightarrow\ u(-x)\in{\mathcal{K}}^{\dagger}.

Lemma 5.12
.

If
{\mathcal{K}}
is cyclotomically self-dual then

\displaystyle d_{k}+d_{R+2-k}=R+\langle\Lambda,\alpha^{\vee}_{1}+\dots+\alpha^{\vee}_{R}\rangle,\qquad k=1,\dots,R+1.

Proof.

If
{\mathcal{K}}
is cyclotomically self-dual then we must have
d_{k}=d^{\dagger}_{R+2-k}
,
k=1,\dots,R+1
.
Comparing (
5.4
) and (
5.12
) we see that this implies that

\displaystyle\langle\tilde{\Lambda}_{\infty}+\rho,\alpha^{\vee}_{1}+\dots\alpha^{\vee}_{k}\rangle=d_{k+1}-d_{1}=d^{\dagger}_{R+1-k}-d^{\dagger}_{R+1}

\displaystyle\hphantom{\langle\tilde{\Lambda}_\infty+ \rho, \alpha^\vee_1 + \ldots\alpha^\vee_k \rangle}{}=\langle\tilde{\Lambda}_{\infty}+\rho,\alpha^{\vee}_{R+1-k}+\dots+\alpha^{\vee}_{R}\rangle,\qquad k=1,\dots,R,

and hence

\displaystyle\langle\tilde{\Lambda}_{\infty},\alpha^{\vee}_{k}\rangle=\langle\tilde{\Lambda}_{\infty},\alpha^{\vee}_{R+1-k}\rangle,\qquad k=1,\dots,R.

Therefore

\displaystyle d_{k}+d_{R+2-k}=2d_{1}+\langle\tilde{\Lambda}_{\infty}+\rho,\alpha^{\vee}_{1}+\dots+\alpha^{\vee}_{R}\rangle,\qquad k=1,\dots,R+1,

and so, because the right-hand side here does not depend on
k
,

\displaystyle d_{k}+d_{R+2-k}=\frac{2}{R+1}\sum_{j=1}^{R+1}d_{j},\qquad k=1,\dots,R+1.

(5.14)

Recall (
5.6
) and the definition (
5.2
) of
\Lambda
. Using now the fact that
\langle\Lambda,\alpha^{\vee}_{i}\rangle=\langle\Lambda,\alpha^{\vee}_{R+1-i}\rangle
,
i=1,\dots,R
, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{R+1}d_{j}-\frac{(R+1)R}{2}=\frac{R+1}{2}\langle\Lambda,\alpha^{\vee}_{1}+\dots+\alpha^{\vee}_{R}\rangle.

Thus, given (
5.14
), we have the result.
∎

If
{\mathcal{K}}
is cyclotomically self-dual then there is a non-degenerate bilinear form
B
on
{\mathcal{K}}
defined by

\displaystyle B(u(x),v(x)):=(u(x),v(-x)),

i.e.,

\displaystyle B(u,v)=\operatorname{Wr}^{\dagger}(u,v_{1},\dots,v_{R}),\qquad\text{where}\quad v(-x)=\operatorname{Wr}^{\dagger}(v_{1},\dots,v_{R}).

Let us call a tuple of quasi-polynomials
\bm{y}\in{\mathbb{P}}\big({\mathbb{C}}\big[x^{\frac{1}{2}}\big]\big)^{R}

cyclotomic
if

\displaystyle y_{k}(-x)\simeq y_{R+1-k}(x),\qquad k=1,\dots,R.

Proposition 5.13
.

Let
{\mathcal{F}}\in F\!L({\mathcal{K}})
. If the tuple
\beta({\mathcal{F}})\in{\mathbb{P}}\big({\mathbb{C}}\big[x^{\frac{1}{2}}\big]\big)^{R}
is cyclotomic then
{\mathcal{F}}
is a decomposable flag.

Proof.

Let
\bm{y}^{\mathcal{F}}=\beta({\mathcal{F}})
. To prove that
{\mathcal{F}}
is decomposable it is enough to show that each entry
y^{\mathcal{F}}_{k}
of this tuple lies in
{\mathbb{C}}[x]
or in
x^{1/2}{\mathbb{C}}[x]
. For each
k=1,\dots,R
we have
y^{\mathcal{F}}_{k}(x)=x^{\frac{1}{2}}a_{k}(x)+b_{k}(x)
for some polynomials
a_{k}(x)
and
b_{k}(x)
in
x
. If
\bm{y}^{\mathcal{F}}
is cyclotomic then
y_{R+1-k}(x)\simeq y^{\mathcal{F}}_{k}(-x)=(-1)^{\frac{1}{2}}x^{\frac{1}{2}}a_{k}(-x)+b_{k}(-x)
for each
k
. That is,
a_{R+1-k}(x)=(-1)^{\frac{1}{2}}c_{k}a_{k}(-x)
and
b_{R+1-k}(x)=c_{k}b_{k}(-x)
for some non-zero constants
c_{k}
. But that means

\displaystyle a_{k}(x)=(-1)^{\frac{1}{2}}c_{R+1-k}a_{R+1-k}(-x)=-c_{R+1-k}c_{k}a_{k}(x),

\displaystyle b_{k}(x)=c_{R+1-k}b_{R+1-k}(-x)=+c_{R+1-k}c_{k}b_{k}(x),

from which we conclude that for each
k
at least one of
a_{k}(x)
and
b_{k}(x)
must vanish.
∎

Theorem 5.14
.

Suppose
\beta(F\!L({\mathcal{K}}))
contains a cyclotomic tuple.
Then
{\mathcal{K}}
is cyclotomically self-dual.

Proof.

We shall need the following identity among Wronskian determinants.

Lemma 5.15

(
[
15
]
)
.

Given integers
0\leq k\leq s
and functions
f_{1},\dots,f_{s+1}
, we have

\displaystyle\operatorname{Wr}\big(\operatorname{Wr}(f_{1},\dots,f_{s-k},\dots,f_{s},\widehat{f}_{s+1}),\operatorname{Wr}(f_{1},\dots,f_{s-k},\dots,\widehat{f}_{s},f_{s+1}),\dots,

\displaystyle\qquad\operatorname{Wr}(f_{1},\dots,f_{s-k},\widehat{f}_{s-k+1},\dots,f_{s+1})\big)=\operatorname{Wr}(f_{1},\dots,f_{s-k})\big(\operatorname{Wr}(f_{1},\dots,f_{s+1})\big)^{k},

where
\widehat{f}
denotes omission.

To prove Theorem
5.14
we argue as for Theorem 6.8 in
[
15
]
.
Let
{\mathcal{F}}\in F\!L({\mathcal{K}})
be a full flag in
{\mathcal{K}}
and
(u_{i}(x))_{i=1}^{R+1}
a basis of
{\mathcal{K}}
adjusted to this flag.
Let
\bm{y}=\bm{y}^{\mathcal{F}}
be the corresponding tuple of quasi-polynomials as in (
5.11
), and
(W_{i}(x))_{i=1}^{R+1}
the corresponding basis of
{\mathcal{K}}^{\dagger}
as in (
5.5
).
Then Theorem
5.14
follows from the case
k=R+1

Lemma 5.16
.

If
\bm{y}
is cyclotomic then

\displaystyle\operatorname{span}_{\mathbb{C}}(u_{1}(-x),\dots,u_{k}(-x))=\operatorname{span}_{\mathbb{C}}(W_{R+1},W_{R},\dots,W_{R+2-k}),\qquad k=1,\dots,R+1.

Proof.

Let us prove the lemma by induction on

. For
k=1
we have

\displaystyle u_{1}(-x)=y_{1}(-x)\simeq y_{R}(x)=\operatorname{Wr}^{\dagger}(u_{1},\dots,u_{R})=W_{R+1}

as required. Assume the statement holds for all values up to some

\displaystyle\operatorname{Wr}(u_{1}(-x),\dots,u_{k}(-x),W_{R+1-k})\simeq\operatorname{Wr}(u_{1}(-x),\dots,u_{k}(-x),u_{k+1}(-x)).

(5.15)

Indeed, (
5.15
) is an inhomogeneous differential equation in
W_{R+1-k}(x)
and if it holds then it must be that
W_{R+1-k}(x)
is proportional to
u_{k+1}(-x)
modulo

, which is sufficient given the inductive assumption.

By the inductive assumption, we have

\displaystyle\operatorname{Wr}(u_{1}(-x),\dots,u_{k}(-x),W_{R+1-k})

\displaystyle\qquad{}\simeq\operatorname{Wr}(W_{R+1},W_{R},\dots,W_{R+1-k+1},W_{R+1-k})

\displaystyle\qquad{}=\operatorname{Wr}\big(\operatorname{Wr}(u_{1},\dots,u_{R+1-k-1},\dots,u_{R},\widehat{u}_{R+1}),

\displaystyle\qquad\quad{}\ \operatorname{Wr}(u_{1},\dots,u_{R+1-k-1},\dots,\widehat{u}_{R},u_{R+1}),\dots,

\displaystyle\qquad\quad{}\ \operatorname{Wr}(u_{1},\dots,u_{R+1-k-1},\widehat{u}_{R+1-k},\dots,u_{R+1})\big)\big/\big({\tilde{T}}_{1}^{R-1}{\tilde{T}}_{2}^{R+1-3}\cdots{\tilde{T}}_{R-1}^{1}\big)^{k+1}

\displaystyle\qquad{}=\operatorname{Wr}(u_{1},\dots,u_{R-k})(\operatorname{Wr}(u_{1},\dots,u_{R+1}))^{k}\big/\big({\tilde{T}}_{1}^{R-1}{\tilde{T}}_{2}^{R+1-3}\cdots{\tilde{T}}_{R-1}^{1}\big)^{k+1},

the final equality by Lemma
5.15
.
Since
\operatorname{Wr}^{\dagger}(u_{1},\dots,u_{R+1})=\operatorname{Wr}(u_{1},\dots,u_{R+1})/{\tilde{T}}_{1}^{R}{\tilde{T}}_{2}^{R-1}\cdots{\tilde{T}}_{R}^{1}
is a nonzero constant by Lemma
5.6
we therefore have

\displaystyle\operatorname{Wr}(u_{1}(-x),\dots,u_{k}(-x),W_{R+1-k})\simeq\operatorname{Wr}(u_{1},\dots,u_{R-k})\frac{\big({\tilde{T}}_{1}^{R}\cdots{\tilde{T}}_{R}^{1}\big)^{k}}{\big({\tilde{T}}_{1}^{R-1}\cdots{\tilde{T}}_{R-1}\big)^{k+1}}

\displaystyle\hphantom{\operatorname{Wr}(u_1(-x),\ldots,u_k(-x),W_{R+1-k}) }{}=\frac{\operatorname{Wr}(u_{1},\dots,u_{R-k})}{{\tilde{T}}_{1}^{R+1-k-2}\cdots{\tilde{T}}_{R+1-k-2}^{1}}{\tilde{T}}_{R}^{k}\cdots{\tilde{T}}_{R+1-k}^{1}.

Now we may use again the fact that
\bm{y}
is cyclotomic, so
y_{k}(-x)\simeq y_{R+1-k}(x)
.
In view of (
5.11
), that implies

\displaystyle\frac{\operatorname{Wr}(u_{1},\dots,u_{R-k})}{{\tilde{T}}_{1}^{R+1-k-2}\cdots{\tilde{T}}_{R+1-k-2}^{1}}\simeq\frac{\operatorname{Wr}(u_{1}(-x),\dots,u_{k+1}(-x))}{{\tilde{T}}_{1}^{k}(-x)\cdots{\tilde{T}}_{k}^{1}(-x)}.

Recall that
{\tilde{T}}_{R+1-k}(x)\simeq{\tilde{T}}_{k}(-x)
. Hence we have indeed that

\displaystyle\operatorname{Wr}(u_{1}(-x),\dots,u_{k}(-x),W_{R+1-k})\simeq\operatorname{Wr}(u_{1}(-x),\dots,u_{k+1}(-x)),

as required.
∎

This completes the proof of Theorem
5.14
.
∎

Given a subspace
U\subset{\mathcal{K}}
, let

\displaystyle U^{\perp}:=\{v\in{\mathcal{K}}\colon B(u,v)=0\text{ for all }u\in U\}

denote its orthogonal complement in
{\mathcal{K}}
with respect to the bilinear form
B
. Recall that a full flag
{\mathcal{F}}=\{0=F_{0}\subset F_{1}\subset F_{2}\subset\dots\subset F_{R}\subset F_{R+1}={\mathcal{K}}\}\in F\!L({\mathcal{K}})
is called
isotropic
with respect to
B
if
F_{k}=F^{\perp}_{R+1-k}
for
k=1,\dots,R
.

Theorem 5.17
.

Suppose
{\mathcal{K}}
is cyclotomically self-dual. A full flag
{\mathcal{F}}\in F\!L({\mathcal{K}})
is isotropic if and only if the associated tuple
\bm{y}^{\mathcal{F}}
is cyclotomic.

Proof.

Let
(u_{i}(x))_{i=1}^{R+1}
be a basis of
{\mathcal{K}}
adjusted to
{\mathcal{F}}
, so that we have (
5.11
).

For the “only if” direction, suppose
\bm{y}^{\mathcal{F}}
is cyclotomic.
By Lemma
5.16
,

\displaystyle F_{k}=\operatorname{span}_{\mathbb{C}}(u_{1},\dots,u_{k})=\operatorname{span}_{\mathbb{C}}(W_{R+1}(-x),\dots,W_{R+2-k}(-x)).

We also have
F_{R+1-k}^{\perp}=\operatorname{span}_{\mathbb{C}}(u_{1},\dots,u_{R+1-k})^{\perp}=\operatorname{span}_{\mathbb{C}}(W_{R+1}(-x),\dots,W_{R+2-k}(-x))
by (

). Therefore

.

For the “if” direction, suppose
{\mathcal{F}}=\{F_{k}\}
is isotropic. Since

, and given (

), we have two bases for

, namely
(u_{1},\dots,u_{k})
and

. So to prove that

.

If

\displaystyle\operatorname{span}_{\mathbb{C}}(u_{1}(-x),\dots,u_{k}(-x))=\operatorname{span}_{\mathbb{C}}(W_{R+1},W_{R},\dots,W_{R+2-k}),\qquad k=1,\dots,R+1,

.
∎

5.7
Witt bases and the symmetries of the bilinear form
\bm{B}

We say that
(r_{k})_{k=1}^{R+1}
is a
Witt basis
of
the cyclotomically self-dual space
{\mathcal{K}}
if

\displaystyle\operatorname{Wr}^{\dagger}(r_{1},\dots,\widehat{r}_{k},\dots,r_{R+1})\simeq r_{R+2-k}(-x),\qquad k=1,\dots,R+1.

(5.17)

The following lemma gives a useful alternative characterization of Witt bases.

Lemma 5.20
.

The basis
(r_{k})_{k=1}^{R+1}
is a Witt basis if and only if

\displaystyle B(r_{i},r_{j})=0\qquad\text{whenever}\quad i+j\neq R+2.

(5.18)

Proof.

Suppose
(u_{k})_{k=1}^{R+1}
is a basis of
{\mathcal{K}}
and let
(W_{k})_{k=1}^{R+1}
be as in (
5.5
). Then
(W_{i}(x))_{i=1}^{R+1}
and
(u_{i}(-x))_{i=1}^{R+1}
are two bases of
{\mathcal{K}}^{\dagger}
and so
u_{i}(-x)=\sum\limits_{j=1}^{R+1}C_{ij}W_{j}(x)
, for some invertible matrix
C_{ij}
. We have
B(u_{i},u_{j})=\sum\limits_{k=1}^{R+1}C_{jk}\operatorname{Wr}^{\dagger}(u_{i},u_{1},u_{2},\dots,\widehat{u}_{k},\dots,u_{R+1})=(-1)^{i-1}C_{ji}\operatorname{Wr}^{\dagger}(u_{1},\dots,u_{R+1})
. Hence (
5.17
) is equivalent to (
5.18
).
∎

Theorem 5.21
.

Every cyclotomically self-dual space
{\mathcal{K}}
has a special basis
(r_{k})_{k=1}^{R+1}
which is also Witt basis, and in which in fact

\displaystyle\operatorname{Wr}^{\dagger}(r_{1},\dots,\widehat{r}_{k},\dots,r_{R+1})=(-1)^{-\deg r_{R+2-k}}r_{R+2-k}(-x),\qquad k=1,\dots,R+1.

(5.19)

Proof.

Let
(u_{k}(x))_{k=1}^{R+1}
be a special basis of
{\mathcal{K}}
. We may suppose that the
u_{k}(x)
all have leading coefficient 1. Let
(W_{k}(x))_{k=1}^{R+1}
be the basis of
{\mathcal{K}}^{\dagger}
as in (
5.5
). By Lemma
5.11
,
\deg W_{k}=d^{\dagger}_{k}
.
By Lemma
5.5
, we have

\displaystyle W_{k}=\operatorname{Wr}^{\dagger}(u_{1},\dots,\widehat{u}_{k},\dots,u_{R+1})=D_{k}x^{d^{\dagger}_{k}}+\cdots,

where the ellipsis indicates terms of lower degree in
x
and where

\displaystyle D_{k}:=\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq j<i\leq R+1\\
i\neq k,\,j\neq k\end{subarray}}(d_{i}-d_{j}),\qquad k=1,\dots,R+1.

Since
{\mathcal{K}}
is cyclotomically self-dual we must have

\displaystyle d_{k}=d^{\dagger}_{R+2-k},\qquad k=1,\dots,R+1,

and

\displaystyle W_{k}=\operatorname{Wr}^{\dagger}(u_{1},\dots,\hat{u}_{k},\dots,u_{R+1})=D_{k}(-1)^{-d_{R+2-k}}u_{R+2-k}(-x)+\cdots.

Now from (
5.4
) we have

\displaystyle d_{k}-d_{l}=d_{R+2-l}-d_{R+2-k},\qquad 1\leq k<l\leq R+1,

using which one verifies that

\displaystyle D_{k}=D_{R+2-k},\qquad k=1,\dots,R+1.

Given this equality, if we set

\displaystyle q_{k}:=u_{k}D_{k}^{\frac{1}{2}}\prod_{j=1}^{R+1}D_{j}^{-\frac{1}{2R-2}}

then we have

\displaystyle\operatorname{Wr}^{\dagger}(q_{1},\dots,\widehat{q}_{k},\dots,q_{R+1})=(-1)^{-d_{R+2-k}}q_{R+2-k}(-x)+\cdots.

In this way, we arrive at

\displaystyle\operatorname{Wr}^{\dagger}(q_{1},\dots,\widehat{q}_{k},\dots,q_{R+1})

\displaystyle\qquad{}=(-1)^{-d_{R+2-k}}q_{R+2-k}(-x)+\sum_{j=2}^{R+1}q_{R+2-j}(-x)c^{j}_{k},\qquad k=1,\dots,R+1,

(5.20)

for some constants
c^{j}_{k}
. That is, we have

\displaystyle\operatorname{Wr}^{\dagger}(q_{1},\dots,q_{R-1},q_{R},\widehat{q}_{R+1})=(-1)^{-d_{1}}q_{1}(-x),

\displaystyle\operatorname{Wr}^{\dagger}(q_{1},\dots,q_{R-1},\widehat{q}_{R},q_{R+1})=(-1)^{-d_{2}}q_{2}(-x)+c_{R}^{1}q_{1}(-x),

Lemma 5.36

On local existence of solutions for nonlinear systems of Cauchy-Riemann operator of any order in the plane
Yifei Pan

Abstract
We prove a general local existence theorem for nonlinear systems of Cauchy-Riemann operator of any order in one complex variable with initial values at a given point, which is a counterpart of local existence of ODE.

In this paper we consider local existence of solutions for nonlinear partial differential systems of Cauchy-Riemann operator in dimension two from point view of complex analysis.

First we introduce some simple notations. Let
D
denote the closed disk
\{z\in\mathbb{C}\mid|z|\leq R\}
. Let
m
be a positive integer. For each
k
:
1\leq k\leq m
, we denote
\mathcal{D}^{k}v

as a vector in
\mathbb{C}^{2^{k}}
with entries
\partial^{\mu}\bar{\partial}^{\nu}v
where
\mu+\nu=k
where
v
is a complex valued function on
D
. Here we use the standard complex derivatives for partial derivatives as

\partial=\frac{1}{2}(\frac{\partial}{\partial x}-i\frac{\partial}{\partial y}),\mbox{ }\bar{\partial}=\frac{1}{2}(\frac{\partial}{\partial x}+i\frac{\partial}{\partial y}),

where
z=x+iy
.
Let
u
be a map from
D
to
\mathbb{C}^{n}
with
u=(u^{1},...,u^{n})
. We denote
\mathcal{D}^{k}u
as
(\mathcal{D}^{k}u^{1},...,\mathcal{D}^{k}u^{n})
, which is a vector in
\mathbb{C}^{2^{k}n}
.

Let
\Omega
be the set

\Omega=D\times\mathbb{C}^{n}\times\mathbb{C}^{2n}\times\cdot\cdot\cdot\times\mathbb{C}^{2^{m-1}n},

with coordinates as
(z,\eta_{0},\eta_{1},...,\eta_{m-1})
.

The following is the main result of this paper, which can be considered as an analogue for systems of ordinary differential equations with initial conditions.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
a:\Omega\to\mathbb{C}^{n}
be any function of class
C^{1+\alpha}
. Let
\mu,\nu
be nonnegative integers such that
\mu+\nu=m\geq 1
, and
c_{i,\bar{j}}
be any vector in
\mathbb{C}^{n}
for
i+j\leq m-1
. Then the system of Cauchy-Riemann operators with initial values at the origin:

\displaystyle\partial^{\mu}\bar{\partial}^{\nu}u(z)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle a(z,u,\mathcal{D}^{1}u,...,\mathcal{D}^{m-1}u)

\displaystyle\partial^{i}\bar{\partial}^{j}u(0)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle c_{i,\bar{j}},\mbox{ for }i+j\leq m-1

has (infinitely many) solutions
u:D\to\mathbb{C}^{n}
of class
C^{m+\alpha}
in
D
for sufficiently small values of
R
.

As a special case we state the following

Corollary 1.2
.

Let
a:D\times\mathbb{C}^{n}\to\mathbb{C}^{n}
be any function of class
C^{1+\alpha}
. Let
c
be any vector in
\mathbb{C}^{n}
.
Then the following nonlinear first odrer partial differential system of Cauchy-Riemnn operator

\displaystyle\bar{\partial}u(z)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle a(z,u(z))

\displaystyle u(0)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle c

has (infinitely many) solutions
u:D\to\mathbb{C}^{n}
of class
C^{1+\alpha}
in
D
for sufficiently small values of
R
.

In order to prove Theorem 1.1, it suffices to prove the case of zero initial values. The general case of initial values can be converted easily. The whole paper is devoted to give a proof for the case of zero initial values.

Finally, The main idea and method have been applied to higher dimensions of elliptic operators [PZ], PY].

1.1
Examples of no solutions-Mizohata equation

After a famous example of Lewy [L] in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
, Mizohata [M] considered, in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
, the following equation

\frac{\partial u}{\partial x}+ix\frac{\partial u}{\partial y}=F(x,y).

It was proved in [M] that there is a smooth function
F
for which the above equation has no solution near the origin. Converting the equation to complex one, one has the following equation

\displaystyle\bar{\partial}u=\frac{1}{1+\mbox{Re}z}F(z,\bar{z})-\frac{1-\mbox{Re}z}{1+\mbox{Re}z}\partial u.

(1)

According to the notation as Theorem 1.1, we have

a(z,\eta_{0},\eta)=\frac{1}{1+\mbox{Re}z}F(z,\bar{z})-\frac{1-\mbox{Re}z}{1+\mbox{Re}z}\eta,

whence

\partial_{\eta}(0)=-1,\bar{\partial}_{\eta}a(0)=0.

Therefore the condition (1) is not met for Theorem 1.1. Taking
\partial^{\mu}\bar{\partial}^{\nu-1}
on both side, we have

\partial^{\mu}\bar{\partial}^{\nu}u=-\frac{1-\mbox{Re}z}{1+\mbox{Re}z}\partial^{\mu+1}\bar{\partial}^{\nu-1}u+\cdot\cdot\cdot+\partial^{\mu}\bar{\partial}^{\nu-1}\left\{\frac{1}{1+\mbox{Re}z}F(z,\bar{z})\right\}.

This is a differential equation of any order which has no solutions locally at the origin.

1.2
Osserman’s theorem

We remark that it is classical that the equation
\Delta u=e^{2u}
has no solutions in the whole plane
\mathbb{C}
due to Ahlfors [A], and local existence puts constrain on the radius of existence due to Osserman [O]. Therefore, the vanishing condition in Theorem B can’t be dropped, and the existence of radius in Theorem A can’t be arbitrarily large. In fact, solve a solution
u
by Theorem A, of

\Delta u=e^{2u},u(0)=a,\nabla u(0)=b

for
|z|\leq R
. Then by [O], we have
R\leq 2e^{-a}
, which goes to zero if
a\to\infty
.

Also we can solve the following

\bar{\partial}u=u^{2}

specifically to get

u(z)=\frac{1}{\varphi(z)+\bar{z}}

where
\varphi(z)
is an entire function. From this, we can see the radius of existence of the solution can be any radius
R
.
On the other hand, we can solve

\bar{\partial}u=e^{u}

to get

u(z)=\ln(\varphi(z)+\bar{z})

where we can define a branch cut so that
u
is well-defined.

2
Function spaces and their norms

2.1
Hölder space

Let
C^{\alpha}(D)
be the set of all functions
f
on
D
for which

H_{\alpha}[f]=\sup\left\{{\frac{|f(z)-f(z^{\prime})|}{|z-z^{\prime}|^{\alpha}}\bigg|z,z^{\prime}\in\mathrm{D}}\right\}

is finite. Let
C^{k}(D)
be the set of all function
f
on
D
whose
k^{\textup{th}}
order partial derivatives exist and are continuous,
k
an integer,
k\geq 0
.

C^{k+\alpha}(D)
is the set of all functions
f
on
D
whose
k^{\textup{th}}
order partial derivatives exist and belong to
C^{\alpha}(D)
.

The symbol
|f|
or
|f|_{\mathrm{D}}
denotes
\textup{sup}_{z\in D}|f(z)|
.
For
f\in C^{\alpha}(D)
we define

\|f\|=|f|+(2R)^{\alpha}H_{\alpha}[f].

The set of
n
-tuples
f=(f_{1},...,f_{n})
of functions (vector functions or maps) of
C^{\alpha}(D)
is denoted by
[C^{\alpha}(D)]^{n}
, and
H_{\alpha}[f]
is defined as the maximum of
H_{\alpha}[f_{i}](i=1,..,n)
. In a similar fashion we define
|f|_{A}=\sup_{z\in A}|f(z)|
for functions and vector functions, and write
|f|
when the domain is understood. Finally, in this paper throughout, the norm of
\mathbb{C}^{N}
is taken as
|v|=\max|v_{j}|
.

The following lemma is well-known; for a proof see ([NW], 7.1b).

Lemma 2.1
.

The function
\|\cdot\cdot\cdot\|
defined on
C^{\alpha}(D)
is a norm, with respect to which
C^{\alpha}(D)
is a Banach algebra:
\|fg\|\leq\|f\|\|g\|
.

The following simple lemma is to be used multiple times throughout the paper.

Lemma 2.2
.

If
f\in C^{k+\alpha}(D)
, then

|f(z^{\prime})-\sum_{l=0}^{k}\frac{1}{l!}\sum_{i+j=l}\partial^{i}\bar{\partial}^{j}f(z)(z^{\prime}-z)^{i}{(\bar{z}^{\prime}-\bar{z})}^{j}|\leq\bigg\{\sum_{i+j=k}H_{\alpha}[\partial^{i}\bar{\partial}^{j}f]\bigg\}|z^{\prime}-z|^{k+\alpha}.

Proof.

Expanding at
z
, we have the formula

\displaystyle f(z^{\prime})-\sum_{l=0}^{k-1}\frac{1}{l!}\sum_{i+j=l}\partial^{i}\bar{\partial}^{j}f(z)(z^{\prime}-z)^{i}{(\bar{z}^{\prime}-\bar{z})}^{j}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}\int_{0}^{t_{k-1}}\cdot\cdot\cdot\int_{0}^{t_{1}}\left\{\frac{d^{k}}{dt^{k}}f(tz^{\prime}+(1-t)z)\right\}dtdt_{1}\cdot\cdot\cdot dt_{k-1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}\int_{0}^{t_{k-1}}\cdot\cdot\cdot\int_{0}^{t_{1}}\left\{\sum_{i+j=k}\partial^{i}\bar{\partial}^{j}f(tz^{\prime}+(1-t)z)(z^{\prime}-z)^{i}{(\bar{z}^{\prime}-\bar{z})}^{j}\right\}dtdt_{1}\cdot\cdot\cdot dt_{k-1}.

Hence, by subtracting kth term
\frac{1}{k!}\sum_{i+j=k}\partial^{i}\bar{\partial}^{j}f(z)(z^{\prime}-z)^{i}{(\bar{z}^{\prime}-\bar{z})}^{j}
from both sides, we have

f(z^{\prime})-\sum_{l=0}^{k}\frac{1}{l!}\sum_{i+j=l}\partial^{i}\bar{\partial}^{j}f(z)(z^{\prime}-z)^{i}{(\bar{z}^{\prime}-\bar{z})}^{j}

=\int_{0}^{1}\int_{0}^{t_{k-1}}\cdot\cdot\cdot\int_{0}^{t_{1}}\left\{\sum_{i+j=k}\{\partial^{i}\bar{\partial}^{j}f(tz^{\prime}+(1-t)z)-\partial^{i}\bar{\partial}^{j}f(z)\}(z^{\prime}-z)^{i}{(\bar{z}^{\prime}-\bar{z})}^{j}\right\}dtdt_{1}\cdot\cdot\cdot dt_{k-1}

Thus we have,

|f(z^{\prime})-\sum_{l=0}^{k}\frac{1}{l!}\sum_{i+j=l}\partial^{i}\bar{\partial}^{j}f(z)(z^{\prime}-z)^{i}{(\bar{z}^{\prime}-\bar{z})}^{j}|

\leq\sum_{i+j=k}H_{\alpha}[\partial^{i}\bar{\partial}^{j}f]|z^{\prime}-z|^{k+\alpha}.

This completes the proof.
∎

2.2
Function spaces with vanishing order at the origin

Our idea of solving differential equations of order
m
is to look for solutions that vanish up to
m-1
order at the origin; this way the norm estimate of the function space to be considered later is made possible using only
m
th order derivatives.
The general case of initial values can be converted to the special case of zero initial values. We denote for
k\geq 1
,
C_{0}^{k+\alpha}(D)
the set of all functions in
C^{k+\alpha}(D)
whose derivatives vanish up to order
k-1
at the origin. Specifically

C_{0}^{k+\alpha}(D)=\{f\in C^{k+\alpha}(D)\big|\partial^{i}\bar{\partial}^{j}f(0)=0,i+j\leq k-1\}.

We now define functions
\|\cdot\cdot\cdot\|^{(k)}
on
C^{k+\alpha}(D)
inductively. On
C^{1+\alpha}(D)
we define, following [NW],

\|f\|^{(1)}=\max\{\|\partial f\|,\|\bar{\partial}f\|\}.

For
k\geq 2
, we define

\|f\|^{(k)}=\max\{\|\partial f\|^{(k-1)},\|\bar{\partial}f\|^{(k-1)}\}.

Obviously, we have the definition
\|\cdot\cdot\cdot\|^{(k)}
in terms of
\|\cdot\cdot\cdot\|
:

\|f\|^{(k)}=\max_{i+j=k}\{\|\partial^{i}\bar{\partial}^{j}f\|\}.

We point out that the function
\|\cdot\cdot\cdot\|^{(k)}
on
C^{k+\alpha}(D)
is not a norm since
\|f\|^{(k)}=0
if and only if
f
is
a polynomial of degree at most
k-1
. However it becomes norm when restricted to the subspace
C_{0}^{k+\alpha}(D)
, which is to be proved below. In this paper, we denote
C
as a constant independent of
R
varying from line to line. First we obtain some useful estimates.

Lemma 2.3
.

If
f\in C_{0}^{k+\alpha}(D)
, then

\|f\|\leq CR^{k}\|f\|^{(k)}.

Proof.

Let
f\in C_{0}^{k+\alpha}(D)
, then

\displaystyle f(z)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}\int_{0}^{t_{k-1}}\cdot\cdot\cdot\int_{0}^{t_{1}}\left\{\frac{d^{k}}{dt^{k}}f(tz)\right\}dtdt_{1}\cdot\cdot\cdot dt_{k-1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}\int_{0}^{t_{k-1}}\cdot\cdot\cdot\int_{0}^{t_{1}}\left\{\sum_{i+j=k}\partial^{i}\bar{\partial}^{j}f(tz)z^{i}\bar{z}^{j}\right\}dtdt_{1}\cdot\cdot\cdot dt_{k-1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{i+j=k}\left\{\int_{0}^{1}\int_{0}^{t_{k-1}}\cdot\cdot\cdot\int_{0}^{t_{1}}\partial^{i}\bar{\partial}^{j}f(tz)dtdt_{1}\cdot\cdot\cdot dt_{k-1}\right\}z^{i}\bar{z}^{j}.

Applying norm inequality, we obtain

\displaystyle\|f\|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sum_{i+j=k}\frac{1}{k!}\|\partial^{i}\bar{\partial}^{j}f\|\|z^{i}\bar{z}^{j}\|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sum_{i+j=k}\frac{1}{k!}\|\partial^{i}\bar{\partial}^{j}f\|\|z\|^{k}\leq CR^{k}\|f\|^{(k)},

where we have used
\|z\|=3R
, which is easily verified.
∎

Lemma 2.4
.

If
f\in C_{0}^{m+\alpha}(D)
, then, for
l\leq m
,

\|f\|^{(l)}\leq CR^{m-l}\|f\|^{(m)}.

Proof.

Let
f\in C_{0}^{m+\alpha}(D)
. If
i+j=l
, then
\partial^{i}\bar{\partial}^{j}f\in C_{0}^{m-l+\alpha}(D)
. By Lemma 2.3, we have

\|\partial^{i}\bar{\partial}^{j}f\|\leq CR^{m-l}\|\partial^{i}\bar{\partial}^{j}f\|^{(m-l)}\leq CR^{m-l}\|f\|^{(m)}.

∎

Lemma 2.5
.

The function space
C_{0}^{k+\alpha}(D)
equipped with the function
\|\cdot\cdot\cdot\|^{(k)}
is a Banach space.

Proof.

First define
|||f|||=\sum_{i+j=0}^{k}\|\partial^{i}\bar{\partial}^{j}f\|
. It is well known that
C^{k+\alpha}(D)
equipped with the norm
|||\cdot|||
is a Banach space. By Lemma 2.4 the norm
\|\cdot\cdot\cdot\|^{(k)}
for
C_{0}^{k+\alpha}(D)
is equivalent to the norm
|||\cdot|||
of
C^{k+\alpha}(D)
. Note that
C_{0}^{k+\alpha}(D)
is a closed subspace of
C^{k+\alpha}(D)
and is therefore a Banch space with norm
\|\cdot\cdot\cdot\|^{(k)}
.
∎

3.1
Basic definitions and properties

The operators are defined for integrable functions on

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{C}\frac{f(\zeta)d\zeta}{\zeta-z},\hskip 28.45274ptS_{b}f(z)=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{C}\frac{f(\zeta)d\bar{\zeta}}{\zeta-z},

Using polar coordinates, we see that

We also define related operators:

\displaystyle|Tf|\leq 4R|f|.

More generally, if

The fundamental property between operators

Lemma 3.1

If

T\bar{\partial}f=f-Sf\mbox{ on }\mbox{}\mathrm{Int}(D),

Lemma 3.2

and

3.2
A new high order integral operator

For
k\geq 0

{}^{k+2}\overline{T}f=\overline{{}^{k+2}T(\bar{f})}

More generally, if
\Delta

Lemma 3.3

Let

I=^{k+1}T_{\delta}f(z)-^{k+1}T_{\delta}f(z^{\prime})-\frac{k!}{2\pi i}\int_{D\setminus\delta}\bigg\{\frac{f(\zeta)-P_{k-1}(\zeta,z)}{(\zeta-z)^{k+1}}-\frac{f(\zeta)-P_{k-1}(\zeta,z^{\prime})}{(\zeta-z^{\prime})^{k+1}}\bigg\}d\bar{\zeta}\wedge d\zeta.

We write by Lemma 2.2 that

f(\zeta)=P_{k-1}(\zeta,z)+\frac{1}{k!}\sum_{i+j=k}\partial^{i}\bar{\partial}^{j}f(z)(\zeta-z)^{i}(\bar{\zeta}-\bar{z})^{j}+E_{k}(\zeta,z)

where

|E_{k}(\zeta,z)|\leq\frac{1}{k!}\bigg\{\sum_{i+j=k}H_{\alpha}[\partial^{i}\bar{\partial}^{j}f]\bigg\}|\zeta-z|^{k+\alpha}.

We now consider the terms in order. First we need the following, using Lemma 3.6 and Corollary 3.5,

{}^{k+1}T_{\delta}f(z)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{-k!}{2\pi i}\int_{\delta}\frac{f(\zeta)-P_{k-1}(\zeta,z)}{(\zeta-z)^{k+1}}d\bar{\zeta}\wedge d\zeta

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{-1}{2\pi i}\sum_{i+j=k}\partial^{i}\bar{\partial}^{j}f(z)\int_{\delta}\frac{(\zeta-z)^{i}(\bar{\zeta}-\bar{z})^{j}}{(\zeta-z)^{k+1}}d\bar{\zeta}\wedge d\zeta

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\frac{-k!}{2\pi i}\int_{\delta}\frac{E_{k}(\zeta,z)}{(\zeta-z)^{k+1}}d\bar{\zeta}\wedge d\zeta

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\partial^{k}f(z)\frac{-1}{2\pi i}\int_{\delta}\frac{1}{(\zeta-z)}d\bar{\zeta}\wedge d\zeta+\frac{-k}{2\pi i}\int_{\delta}\frac{E_{k}(\zeta,z)}{(\zeta-z)^{k+1}}d\bar{\zeta}\wedge d\zeta

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\partial^{k}f(z)(\bar{z}-\frac{\bar{z}+\bar{z}^{\prime}}{2})+\frac{-k}{2\pi i}\int_{\delta}\frac{E_{k}(\zeta,z)}{(\zeta-z)^{k+1}}d\bar{\zeta}\wedge d\zeta

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\partial^{k}f(z)(\bar{z}-\bar{z}^{\prime})+I_{3}.

Similarly we have

{}^{k+1}T_{\delta}f(z^{\prime})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\partial^{k}f(z^{\prime})(\bar{z}^{\prime}-\bar{z})+\frac{-k}{2\pi i}\int_{\delta}\frac{E_{k}(\zeta,z^{\prime})}{(\zeta-z^{\prime})^{k+1}}d\bar{\zeta}\wedge d\zeta

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\partial^{k}f(z^{\prime})(\bar{z}^{\prime}-\bar{z})+I_{3}^{\prime}.

Now we estimate
I_{3},I_{3}^{\prime}
. It follows

|I_{3}|=\bigg|\frac{-k}{2\pi i}\int_{\delta}\frac{E_{k}(\zeta,z)}{(\zeta-z)^{k+1}}d\bar{\zeta}\wedge d\zeta\bigg|\leq C\sum_{i+j=k}H_{\alpha}[\partial^{i}\bar{\partial}^{j}f]\int_{0}^{2\pi}\int_{0}^{r_{1}(\theta)}\frac{r^{k+\alpha}2rdrd\theta}{r^{k+1}}

where
r_{1}(\theta)
is the distance from
z
to the boundary of
\delta
in direction
\theta
; note
r_{1}(\theta)\leq 2\rho
. Continuing the computation, we have

|I_{3}|\leq C\sum_{i+j=k}H_{\alpha}[\partial^{i}\bar{\partial}^{j}f](2\rho)^{1+\alpha}

which approaches
0
as
\rho\to 0
. The same estimate holds true for
I_{3}^{\prime}
. Thus we have

\displaystyle|^{k+1}T_{\delta}f(z)-^{k+1}T_{\delta}f(z^{\prime})-\partial^{k}f(z)(\bar{z}-\bar{z}^{\prime})|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle|I_{3}|+|I_{3}^{\prime}|+\bigg|\frac{1}{2}\partial^{k}f(z)(\bar{z}-\bar{z}^{\prime})-\frac{1}{2}\partial^{k}f(z^{\prime})(\bar{z}^{\prime}-\bar{z})-\partial^{k}f(z)(\bar{z}-\bar{z}^{\prime})\bigg|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle|I_{3}|+|I_{3}^{\prime}|+\frac{1}{2}H_{\alpha}[\partial^{k}f]|z-z^{\prime}|^{1+\alpha}.

On the other hand, to continue to estimate, we have

\displaystyle\int_{D\setminus\delta}\bigg\{\frac{f(\zeta)-P_{k-1}(\zeta,z)}{(\zeta-z)^{k+1}}-\frac{f(\zeta)-P_{k-1}(\zeta,z^{\prime})}{(\zeta-z^{\prime})^{k+1}}\bigg\}d\bar{\zeta}\wedge d\zeta

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{D\setminus\delta}f(\zeta)\bigg(\frac{1}{(\zeta-z)^{k+1}}-\frac{1}{(\zeta-z^{\prime})^{k+1}}\bigg)d\bar{\zeta}\wedge d\zeta-\int_{D\setminus\delta}\frac{P_{k-1}(\zeta,z)}{(\zeta-z)^{k+1}}d\bar{\zeta}\wedge d\zeta

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\int_{D\setminus\delta}\frac{P_{k-1}(\zeta,z^{\prime})}{(\zeta-z^{\prime})^{k+1}}d\bar{\zeta}\wedge d\zeta

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle I_{4}+I_{5}+I_{6}.

We need the following lemma to proceed.

Lemma 3.8
.

If
m\geq 2,n\geq 0
, then

\int_{D\setminus\delta}\frac{(\bar{\zeta}-\bar{z})^{n}}{(\zeta-z)^{m}}d\bar{\zeta}\wedge d\zeta=\int_{D\setminus\delta}\frac{(\bar{\zeta}-\bar{z}^{\prime})^{n}}{(\zeta-z^{\prime})^{m}}d\bar{\zeta}\wedge d\zeta=0.

Actually more is true:

\int_{D\setminus\delta}\frac{(\bar{\zeta}-\bar{s})^{n}}{(\zeta-s)^{m}}d\bar{\zeta}\wedge d\zeta=0

for any
s\in\delta
.

Proof.

We have

\int_{D\setminus\delta}\frac{(\bar{\zeta}-\bar{z})^{n}}{(\zeta-z)^{m}}d\bar{\zeta}\wedge d\zeta=\frac{1}{(m-1)!}\frac{d^{m-1}}{dw^{m-1}}\int_{D\setminus\delta}\frac{(\bar{\zeta}-\bar{z})^{n}}{\zeta-w}d\bar{\zeta}\wedge d\zeta\bigg|_{w=z},

=\frac{1}{(m-1)!}\frac{d^{m-1}}{dw^{m-1}}\{\int_{D}\frac{(\bar{\zeta}-\bar{z})^{n}}{\zeta-w}d\bar{\zeta}\wedge d\zeta-\int_{\delta}\frac{(\bar{\zeta}-\bar{z})^{n}}{\zeta-w}d\bar{\zeta}\wedge d\zeta\}\bigg|_{w=z},

which is zero by applying Lemma 3.4.
∎

By Lemma 3.8, we have

I_{5}=I_{6}=0.

Now we only have to estimate
I_{4}
. Indeed,

I_{4}=-(k+1)\int_{z^{\prime}}^{z}\bigg(\int_{D\setminus\delta}\frac{f(\zeta)}{(\zeta-w)^{k+2}}d\bar{\zeta}\wedge d\zeta\bigg)dw

where the integration with respect to
w
is along the straight line segment from
z^{\prime}
to
z
. Now it suffices to estimate

\displaystyle\int_{D\setminus\delta}\frac{f(\zeta)}{(\zeta-w)^{k+2}}d\bar{\zeta}\wedge d\zeta-\int_{D}\frac{f(\zeta)-P_{k}(\zeta,z)}{(\zeta-z)^{k+2}}d\bar{\zeta}\wedge d\zeta

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{D\setminus\delta}\frac{f(\zeta)}{(\zeta-w)^{k+2}}d\bar{\zeta}\wedge d\zeta-\int_{D\setminus\delta}\frac{f(\zeta)-P_{k}(\zeta,z)}{(\zeta-z)^{k+2}}d\bar{\zeta}\wedge d\zeta

\displaystyle-

\displaystyle\int_{\delta}\frac{f(\zeta)-P_{k}(\zeta,z)}{(\zeta-z)^{k+2}}d\bar{\zeta}\wedge d\zeta

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{D\setminus\delta}f(\zeta)\bigg\{\frac{1}{(\zeta-w)^{k+2}}-\frac{1}{(\zeta-z)^{k+2}}\bigg\}d\bar{\zeta}\wedge d\zeta-\int_{\delta}\frac{f(\zeta)-P_{k}(\zeta,z)}{(\zeta-z)^{k+2}}d\bar{\zeta}\wedge d\zeta

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle I_{7}+I_{8}.

Here we have used Lemma 3.8 to conclude

\int_{D\setminus\delta}\frac{P_{k}(\zeta,z)}{(\zeta-z)^{k+2}}d\bar{\zeta}\wedge d\zeta=0.

Now we have

\displaystyle I_{7}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{D\setminus\delta}f(\zeta)\bigg\{\frac{1}{(\zeta-w)^{k+2}}-\frac{1}{(\zeta-z)^{k+2}}\bigg\}d\bar{\zeta}\wedge d\zeta

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(k+2)\int_{D\setminus\delta}f(\zeta)\int_{w}^{z}\frac{ds}{(\zeta-s)^{k+3}}d\bar{\zeta}\wedge d\zeta

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(k+2)\int_{w}^{z}ds\int_{D\setminus\delta}\frac{f(\zeta)-P_{k}(\zeta,s)}{(\zeta-s)^{k+3}}d\bar{\zeta}\wedge d\zeta

where we have used that

\int_{D\setminus\delta}\frac{P_{k}(\zeta,s)}{(\zeta-s)^{k+3}}d\bar{\zeta}\wedge d\zeta=0,

for
s\in\delta
by Lemma 3.8. Now

\displaystyle|I_{7}|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C|w-z|\bigg|\int_{D\setminus\delta}\frac{f(\zeta)-P_{k}(\zeta,s)}{(\zeta-s)^{k+3}}d\bar{\zeta}\wedge d\zeta\bigg|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C|w-z|\sum_{i+j=k}H_{\alpha}[\partial^{i}\bar{\partial}^{j}f]2\pi\int_{\rho/2}^{2R}\frac{r^{k+\alpha}2rdr}{r^{k+3}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle C|w-z|\sum_{i+j=k}H_{\alpha}[\partial^{i}\bar{\partial}^{j}f]2\pi\frac{1}{1-\alpha}((\rho/2)^{\alpha-1}-(2R)^{\alpha-1})

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\sum_{i+j=k}H_{\alpha}[\partial^{i}\bar{\partial}^{j}f]\rho^{\alpha}.

In the last inequality we have used that
|w-z|\leq|z-z^{\prime}|=\rho.

We have thus shown that

{}^{k+1}Tf(z)-^{k+1}Tf(z^{\prime})=^{k+2}Tf(z)(z-z^{\prime})+\partial^{k}f(z)(\bar{z}-\bar{z}^{\prime})+O(|z-z^{\prime}|^{1+\alpha})

as
z^{\prime}\to z
. Thus
{}^{k+1}Tf
has a total differential and
\partial(^{k+1}Tf)=^{k+2}Tf
.
∎

In what follows, we will show that

Lemma 3.9
.

Let
f\in C^{k+\alpha}(D)
. Then

|^{k+2}Tf|\leq CR^{\alpha}\sum_{i+j=k}H_{\alpha}[\partial^{i}\bar{\partial}^{j}f].

Proof.

We compute, using Lemma 2.2,

\displaystyle|^{k+2}Tf(z)|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{(k+1)!}{2\pi}\bigg|\int_{D}\frac{f(\zeta)-P_{k}(\zeta,z)}{(\zeta-z)^{k+2}}d\bar{\zeta}\wedge d\zeta\bigg|,

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\sum_{i+j=k}H_{\alpha}[\partial^{i}\bar{\partial}^{j}f]\int_{0}^{2\pi}\int_{0}^{2R}\frac{r^{k+\alpha}2rdrd\theta}{r^{k+2}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle CR^{\alpha}\sum_{i+j=k}H_{\alpha}[\partial^{i}\bar{\partial}^{j}f].

∎

The following is well known [NW, K]

Lemma 3.10
.

If
f\in C^{1+\alpha}(D)
, then

{}^{2}Tf=T(\partial f)-S_{b}(f).

Lemma 3.11
.

If
f\in C^{k+\alpha}(D)
, then for
k\geq 1

{}^{k+2}Tf=^{2}T(\partial^{k}f)-\sum_{i=1}^{k}\partial^{i}S_{b}(\partial^{k-i}f).

Proof.

By Lemma 3.10, we have

{}^{2}Tf=T(\partial f)-S_{b}(f).

Taking derivative, we have

\partial^{2}Tf=\partial T(\partial f)-\partial S_{b}(f),

which is equivalent to

{}^{3}Tf=^{2}T(\partial f)-\partial S_{b}(f).

A mathematical induction finishes the proof.
∎

Lemma 3.12
.

If
f\in C^{k+\alpha}(D)
, then

H_{\alpha}[\partial^{k}S_{b}f]\leq C\sum_{i+j=k}H_{\alpha}[\partial^{i}\bar{\partial}^{j}f].

Proof.

We notice that

\partial^{k}S_{b}f(z)=\frac{k!}{2\pi i}\int_{C}\frac{f(\zeta)}{(\zeta-z)^{k+1}}d\bar{\zeta},

and
S_{b}f(z)
is holomorphic in
\mathrm{Int}(D)
.
First we need the following

\int_{C}\frac{P_{k}(\zeta,w)}{(\zeta-w)^{k+2}}d\bar{\zeta}=0,

for
w\in\mathrm{Int}(D)
, and where
P_{k}(\zeta,w)
is the Taylor expansion of
f(\zeta)
of order
k
at
w
. Indeed,

\int_{C}\frac{P_{k}(\zeta,w)}{(\zeta-w)^{k+2}}d\bar{\zeta}=\sum_{l=0}^{k}\frac{1}{l!}\sum_{i+j=l}\partial^{i}\bar{\partial}^{j}f(w)\int_{C}\frac{(\zeta-w)^{i}(\bar{\zeta}-\bar{w})^{j}}{(\zeta-w)^{k+2}}d\bar{\zeta}.

It suffices to prove the following

\int_{C}\frac{(\bar{\zeta}-\bar{w})^{n}}{(\zeta-w)^{m+2}}d\bar{\zeta}=0

for
m,n\geq 0
. This is equivalent to prove that

\int_{C}\frac{\bar{\zeta}^{n}}{(\zeta-w)^{m+2}}d\bar{\zeta}=0

for
m,n\geq 0
. This is equivalent to prove that

\int_{C}\frac{1}{\zeta^{n+2}(\zeta-w)^{m+2}}d\zeta=0,

which is true by the residue theorem. Now we are ready to estimate.

\displaystyle\frac{2\pi i}{k!}(\partial^{k}S_{b}f(z)-\partial^{k}S_{b}f(z^{\prime}))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{C}f(\zeta)\bigg[\frac{1}{(\zeta-z)^{k+1}}-\frac{1}{(\zeta-z^{\prime})^{k+1}}\bigg]d\bar{\zeta}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{k+1}\int_{C}f(\zeta)\int_{z}^{z^{\prime}}\frac{dw}{(\zeta-w)^{k+2}}d\bar{\zeta}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{k+1}\int_{z}^{z^{\prime}}dw\int_{C}\frac{f(\zeta)}{(\zeta-w)^{k+2}}d\bar{\zeta}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{k+1}\int_{z}^{z^{\prime}}dw\int_{C}\frac{f(\zeta)-P_{k}(\zeta,w)}{(\zeta-w)^{k+2}}d\bar{\zeta}.

In order to apply estimates in [NW], we convert integral to be on unit circle. Setting
w=R\omega
and
\zeta=R\eta
, this becomes

\frac{1}{(k+1)R^{k}}\int_{z/R}^{z^{\prime}/R}d\omega\int_{|\eta|=1}\frac{f(R\eta)-P_{k}(R\eta,R\omega)}{(\eta-\omega)^{k+2}}d\bar{\eta}

where the line integral is taken on the shorter segment of the circle through
z/R
and
z^{\prime}/R
and orthogonal to the unit circle(see [NW] (6.2a)).
Further, setting
\tau=(\eta-\omega)/(1-\bar{\omega}\eta)
, we have

\eta=\frac{\tau+\omega}{1+\bar{\omega}\tau},\mbox{ }\eta-\omega=\frac{\tau(1-\omega\bar{\omega})}{1+\tau\bar{\omega}},\mbox{ }d\eta=\frac{(1-\omega\bar{\omega})}{(1+\tau\bar{\omega})^{2}}d\tau.

Hence

\displaystyle\frac{2\pi}{k!}

\displaystyle|\partial^{k}S_{b}f(z)-\partial^{k}S_{b}f(z^{\prime})|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{C}{(R^{k}}\int_{z/R}^{z^{\prime}/R}|d\omega|\int_{|\eta|=1}\bigg|\frac{f(R\eta)-P_{k}(R\eta,R\omega)}{(\eta-\omega)^{k+2}}\bigg||d\bar{\eta}|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{C}{R^{k}}\int_{z/R}^{z^{\prime}/R}|d\omega|\int_{|\eta|=1}\frac{|f(R\frac{\tau+\omega}{1+\bar{\omega}\tau})-P_{k}(R\frac{\tau+\omega}{1+\bar{\omega}\tau},R\omega)||1+\tau\bar{\omega}|^{k}}{(1-\omega\bar{\omega})^{k+1}}|d\eta|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{C}{R^{k}}\sum_{i+j=k}H_{\alpha}[\partial^{i}\bar{\partial}^{j}f]\int_{z/R}^{z^{\prime}/R}|d\omega|\int_{|\eta|=1}\frac{|R\frac{\tau+\omega}{1+\bar{\omega}\tau}-R\omega|^{k+\alpha}|1+\tau\bar{\omega}|^{k}}{(1-\omega\bar{\omega})^{k+1}}|d\eta|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle CR^{\alpha}\sum_{i+j=k}H_{\alpha}[\partial^{i}\bar{\partial}^{j}f]\int_{z/R}^{z^{\prime}/R}\frac{|d\omega|}{(1-\omega\bar{\omega})^{1-\alpha}}\int_{|\tau|=1}\frac{|d\tau|}{|1+\bar{\omega}\tau|^{\alpha}}.

According to [NW](6.2a and p473), one has

\int_{|\tau|=1}\frac{|d\tau|}{|1+\bar{\omega}\tau|^{\alpha}}\leq\frac{4\pi}{1-\alpha},

\int_{z/R}^{z^{\prime}/R}\frac{|d\omega|}{(1-\omega\bar{\omega})^{1-\alpha}}\leq\frac{2}{\alpha}|z-z^{\prime}|^{\alpha}\frac{1}{R^{\alpha}}.

Hence

H_{\alpha}[\partial^{k}S_{b}f]\leq C\sum_{i+j=k}H_{\alpha}[\partial^{i}\bar{\partial}^{j}f].

∎

The following is the key result to be used to prove existence of solutions in what follows.

Theorem 3.13
.

If
f\in C^{k+\alpha}(D)
, then

H_{\alpha}[^{k+2}Tf]\leq C\sum_{i+j=k}H_{\alpha}[\partial^{i}\bar{\partial}^{j}f],

H_{\alpha}[^{k+2}\overline{T}f]\leq C\sum_{i+j=k}H_{\alpha}[\partial^{i}\bar{\partial}^{j}f].

Proof.

By Lemma 3.11, and applying Lemma 3.12, we have

{}^{k+2}Tf

\displaystyle=

{}^{2}T(\partial^{k}f)-\sum_{i=1}^{k}\partial^{i}S_{b}(\partial^{k-i}f).

\displaystyle H_{\alpha}[^{k+2}Tf]

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle H_{\alpha}[^{2}T(\partial^{k}f)]+\sum_{i=1}^{k}H_{\alpha}[\partial^{i}S_{b}(\partial^{k-i}f)]

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle CH_{\alpha}[\partial^{k}f]+C\left\{\sum_{i=1}^{k}\frac{1}{i}\sum_{p+q=i}H_{\alpha}[\partial^{p}\bar{\partial}^{q}\partial^{k-i}f]\right\}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\sum_{i+j=k}H_{\alpha}[\partial^{i}\bar{\partial}^{j}f].

∎

Lemma 3.14
.

If
h\in C^{k+\alpha}(D)

(k\geq 0)
, then

\|Th\|^{(k+1)}\leq C\|h\|^{(k)},

\|\overline{T}h\|^{(k+1)}\leq C\|h\|^{(k)}.

Proof.

Let
i+j=k+1
. If
j\geq 1
, then
\partial^{i}\bar{\partial}^{j}Th=\partial^{i}\bar{\partial}^{j-1}h
.
Otherwise,
\partial^{k+1}Th=^{k+2}Th
. We have by Lemma 3.9, 3.13

\displaystyle\|^{k+2}Th\|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle|^{k+2}Th|+(2R)^{\alpha}H_{\alpha}[^{k+2}Th]

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle CR^{\alpha}\sum_{i+j=k}H_{\alpha}[\partial^{i}\bar{\partial}^{j}h]+(2R)^{\alpha}C\sum_{i+j=k}H_{\alpha}[\partial^{i}\bar{\partial}^{j}h]

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle(CR^{\alpha}+(2R)^{\alpha}C)\sum_{i+j=k}H_{\alpha}[\partial^{i}\bar{\partial}^{j}h]

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\|h\|^{(k)}.

In the last inequality we have used that
H_{\alpha}[\partial^{i}\bar{\partial}^{j}h]\leq(2R)^{-\alpha}\|h\|^{(k)}
for
i+j=k
.
The proof is now complete.
∎

The following is the main result of this section.

Theorem 3.15
.

If
h\in C^{\alpha}(D)
and
\mu+\nu=m
, then

\|T^{\nu}\overline{T}^{\mu}h\|^{(m)}\leq C\|h\|.

Proof.

Since, if
\nu\geq 1
,

\|T^{\nu}\overline{T}^{\mu}h\|^{(m)}=\|T(T^{\nu-1}\overline{T}^{\mu}h)\|^{(m-1+1)},

which is less than, by invoking Lemma 3.14

C\|T^{\nu-1}\overline{T}^{\mu}h\|^{(m-1)},

which is less than, by repeating the argument,

This gives the proof of the theorem.
∎

4
A High order integral operator

In order to prove the existence of solutions of system with arbitrary initial values we will first solve the equation with zero initial values at the origin. Namely, we are to solve the system of equations

\displaystyle\partial^{\mu}\bar{\partial}^{\nu}u(z)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle a(z,u(z),\mathcal{D}^{1}u(z),...,\mathcal{D}^{m-1}u(z))

(2)

with zero initial values at the origin:

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}^{k}u(0)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 0,\mbox{ for }0\leq k\leq m-1

(3)

Every solution
u
of (1) satisfies the following integral equation, by Lemma 3.2,

\displaystyle u(z)=\psi(z)+T^{\nu}\overline{T}^{\mu}a(\zeta,u,\mathcal{D}^{1}u,...,\mathcal{D}^{m-1}u)(z),

(4)

where
\partial^{\mu}\bar{\partial}^{\nu}\psi(z)=0
.
In what follows, we will introduce function spaces that are Banach with norms to reflect the initial values condition, and
will modify the equation (3) to fit the defined Banach space and to apply fixed point theorem to produce the needed solutions. In doing so, we will take a full advantage of
two parameters: the radius
R
of
D
and the radius
\gamma
of a closed ball in the Banach space where the solutions are sought from.

4.1
Iteration procedures

For the sake of generality we consider a Banach space
\mathbf{B}(R)
depending on a positive parameter
R
.
R
will be less than some fixed positive quantity.
The norm on
\mathbf{B}(R)
is denoted by
\|\cdot\cdot\cdot\|
; it may also depend on
R
.
The following deals with a map
\mathbf{\Theta}:\mathbf{A}(R,\gamma)\to\mathbf{B}(R)
, where
\mathbf{A}(R,\gamma)(\gamma>0)
is a closed subset of
\mathbf{B}(R)
defined as

\mathbf{A}(R,\gamma)=\{\Omega|\|\Omega\|\leq\gamma\}
. Here we actually have a family of such maps with two parameters
R,\gamma
.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
\mathbf{\Theta}:\mathbf{A}(R,\gamma)\to\mathbf{B}(R)
, and
and let
\delta(R,\gamma)
and
\eta(R,\gamma)
exist such that for all
\Omega,\Omega^{\prime}\in\mathbf{A}(R,\gamma)

\|\mathbf{\Theta}(\Omega^{\prime})-\mathbf{\Theta}(\Omega)\|\leq\delta(R,\gamma)\|\Omega^{\prime}-\Omega\|,

\|\mathbf{\Theta}(\Omega)\|\leq\eta(R,\gamma).

If there exist
R_{0}>0,\gamma_{0}>0
such that
\delta(R,\gamma_{0})\leq 3/4
and
\eta(R,\gamma_{0})\leq\frac{\gamma_{0}}{2}
for
R\leq R_{0}
. Let
\psi\in\mathbf{B}(R)
be such that

\|\psi\|\leq\frac{\gamma_{0}}{2}
. Then the following equation

\Omega=\psi+\mathbf{\Theta}(\Omega)

has a unique solution in
\mathbf{A}(R,\gamma_{0})
for
R\leq R_{0}
. The solution
\Omega
is the limit of the sequence

\displaystyle\Omega_{N+1}=\psi+\mathbf{\Theta}(\Omega_{N})\mbox{ for }N=1,2,...

where
\Omega_{1}\in\mathbf{A}(R,\gamma_{0})
.

4.2
Integral equations on Banach spaces

Here we fix the Banach space to work on.
Let
\mathbf{B}(R)
be
[C^{m+\alpha}_{0}(D)]^{n}
, which is, by Lemma 2.6 a Banach space with norm
||\cdot\cdot\cdot||^{(m)}
defined as follows:
if
f=(f_{1},...,f_{n})\in\mathbf{B}(R)
, then
\|f\|^{(m)}=\max_{1\leq i\leq n}\|f_{i}\|^{(m)}
.
Let
\gamma
be a positive number, and we consider

\mathbf{A}(R,\gamma)=\{f\in\mathbf{B}(R)|\|f\|^{(m)}\leq\gamma\}.

We note that
\mathbf{A}(R,\gamma)
is a closed subset of
\mathbf{B}(R)
.
Now we assume
u=(u^{1},...,u^{n})
and
a=(a^{1},...,a^{n})
. According to (1) , we consider equations

u^{i}=\psi^{i}+T^{\nu}\overline{T}^{\mu}a^{i}(z,u,\mathcal{D}^{1}u,...,\mathcal{D}^{m-1}u)

where
\psi=(\psi^{1},...,\psi^{n})
is such that
\partial^{\mu}\bar{\partial}^{\nu}\partial\psi=0
.
To simplify the notation we define

\omega^{i}(f)=T^{\nu}\overline{T}^{\mu}a^{i}(z,f,\mathcal{D}^{1}f,...,\mathcal{D}^{m-1}f)

for all
f\in\mathbf{B}(R)
and
i=1,2,...,n
.
To assure the zero initial satisfied, we consider

\displaystyle\mathbf{\Theta}^{i}(f)(\zeta)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\omega^{i}(f)(\zeta)-\sum_{p=0}^{m-1}\frac{1}{p!}\sum_{k+l=p}[\partial^{k}\bar{\partial}^{l}\omega^{i}(f)](0)\zeta^{k}\bar{\zeta}^{l}.

(5)

We first notice that
T,\overline{T}
map
C^{\alpha}(\mathrm{D})\to C^{1+\alpha}(D)
, and it follows, by the construction (1),
\mathbf{\Theta}^{i}(f)(\zeta)\in C^{m+\alpha}_{0}(D)
for
f\in\mathbf{B}(R)
. Thus we define a map from
\mathbf{B}(R)
to
\mathbf{B}(R)
.

\displaystyle\mathbf{\Theta}

Proof of Theorem 1.1

5.1
Case of zero initial values

First, we note from (14) that for each
\gamma
,
\lim_{\mathrm{R}\to 0}\delta(R,\gamma)=0
.
Let
\eta(R,\gamma)=C|a(0)|+CA(R,\gamma)R+C\delta(R,\gamma)
from (16).
Now we choose
\gamma_{0}
so large that
\frac{\gamma_{0}}{4}>C|a(0)|
. Then we choose
R
sufficiently small that

CA(R,\gamma)R+C\delta(R,\gamma_{0})\leq\frac{\gamma_{0}}{4}
.
As a result, we have

\eta(R,\gamma_{0})\leq\frac{\gamma_{0}}{2}.

If necessary, we choose
R
further so that

\delta(R,\gamma_{0})<3/4.

Let
\psi(z)
be a homogenous polynomial map of degree
m
such that
\|\psi\|\leq\frac{\gamma_{0}}{2}
. Then
we can apply Lemma 4.1 to

u=\psi+\mathbf{\Theta}(u)

on
A(R,\gamma_{0})
to get the desired unique solution that vanish up to order
m-1
at the origin.
\psi
be arbitrary, we have obtained infintely many solutions.

5.2
General initial values

To get the general case, we consider a new system,
p(z)=(p^{1},...,p^{n})
with
\partial^{i}\bar{\partial}^{j}p(0)=c_{i,\bar{j}}
.

\partial^{\mu}\bar{\partial}^{\nu}\tilde{u}=a(z,\tilde{u}+p(z),\mathcal{D}^{1}\tilde{u}+\mathcal{D}^{1}p(z),...,\mathcal{D}^{m-1}\tilde{u}+\mathcal{D}^{m-1}p(z)).

This can be written as

\partial^{\mu}\bar{\partial}^{\nu}\tilde{u}=b(z,\tilde{u},\mathcal{D}^{1}\tilde{u},...,\mathcal{D}^{m-1}\tilde{u}).

We can solve
\tilde{u}
as just proved so that
\tilde{u}
vanishes up to order
m-1
at the origin.
Then
u=\tilde{u}+p(z)
solves the original equation with desired property of derivatives at the origin since
\partial^{\mu}\bar{\partial}^{\nu}\tilde{u}=\partial^{\mu}\bar{\partial}^{\nu}u
. The proof is complete.

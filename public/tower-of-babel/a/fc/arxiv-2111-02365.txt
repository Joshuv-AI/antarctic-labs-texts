The Schur Expansion of Characteristic Polynomials and Random Matrices
Taro Kimura & Edward A. Mazenc

Abstract
We develop a new framework to compute the exact correlators of characteristic polynomials, and their inverses, in random matrix theory. Our results hold for general potentials and incorporate the effects of an external source. In matrix model realizations of string theory, these correspond to correlation functions of exponentiated “(anti-)branes” in a given background of “momentum branes”. Our method relies on expanding the (inverse) determinants in terms of Schur polynomials, then re-summing their expectation values over the allowed representations of the symmetric group. Beyond unifying previous, seemingly disparate calculations, this powerful technique immediately delivers two new results: 1) the full finite N N answer for the correlator of inverse determinant insertions in the presence of a matrix source, and 2) access to an interesting, novel regime M > N M>N , where the number of inverse determinant insertions M M exceeds the size of the matrix N N .

2.1
Schur Polynomial Average

r_{\alpha}\neq r_{\beta}\end{subarray}}\det_{1\leq\alpha,\beta\leq M}\left[z_{\alpha}^{M-N-r_{\beta}-1}\right]\det_{1\leq\alpha,\beta\leq M}\quantity[ \braket{P_{N - \alpha}}{x^{-M+r_\beta+N}} ]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\tilde{\mathcal{Z}}_{N-M}/\tilde{\mathcal{Z}}_{N}}{\Delta_{M}(Z)}\det_{1\leq\alpha,\beta\leq M}\left[\sum_{r=0}^{\infty}z_{\alpha}^{M-N-r-1}\innerproduct{P_{N-\beta}}{x^{N - M + r}}\right]

(2.27)

where we used a discrete equivalent of the Andréif–Heine identity to trade the sum of products of determinants for a determinant of the sum of products.

We can now explicitly perform the sum over
r
, and recognize it as the geometric series expansion
(z_{\alpha}-x)^{-1}=\sum_{r=0}^{\infty}z^{-r-1}x^{-r}
. This leaves us with

\expectationvalue{\prod_{\alpha=1}^{M} \det(z_{\alpha}-X)^{-1}}=\frac{\tilde{\mathcal{Z}}_{N-M}/\tilde{\mathcal{Z}}_{N}}{\Delta_{M}(Z)}\det\left[z_{\alpha}^{M-N}\int\frac{\differential{x}}{2\pi}e^{-V(x)}P_{N-\beta}(x)\frac{x^{N-M}}{z_{\alpha}-x}\right]

(2.28)

To recover the more familiar expression, first rewrite

\frac{x^{N-M}}{z-x}=\frac{z^{N-M}}{z-x}-\frac{z^{N-m}-x^{N-m}}{z-x}=\frac{z^{N-M}}{z-x}+\mathcal{O}(x^{N-M-1})

(2.29)

and note that, by the orthogonality,

\int\frac{\differential{x}}{2\pi}e^{-V(x)}P_{N-\beta}(x)\mathcal{O}(x^{N-(M+1)})=0.

(2.30)

Recalling the definition of the Hilbert transform (
1.5
), this gives us our final expression

\displaystyle\expectationvalue{\prod_{\alpha=1}^{M} \det(z_{\alpha}-X)^{-1}}

\displaystyle=\frac{\tilde{\mathcal{Z}}_{N-M}/\tilde{\mathcal{Z}}_{N}}{\Delta_{M}(Z)}\det_{1\leq\alpha,\beta\leq M}\left[\int\frac{\differential{x}}{2\pi}e^{-V(x)}\frac{P_{N-\beta}(x)}{z_{\alpha}-x}\right]

\displaystyle=\frac{\tilde{\mathcal{Z}}_{N-M}/\tilde{\mathcal{Z}}_{N}}{\Delta_{M}(Z)}\det_{1\leq\alpha,\beta\leq M}\left[\tilde{P}_{N-\beta}(z_{\alpha})\right]

(2.31)

which reproduces the classic formula for inverse characteristic polynomials in terms of a determinant over Hilbert-transforms of the orthogonal polynomials, as advertised in Eq.(
1.4
).
This shows a different (and also simpler) derivation of the inverse characteristic polynomial formula, originally found in
[
5
]
.

The Case with
M>N

Let us now consider the opposite situation
M>N
.
In this case, a different manipulation is necessary in the Cauchy sum formula (
2.23
).
We now have the condition
\ell(\lambda)\leq\operatorname{min}(N,M)=N
, so that
\lambda_{\alpha}=0
for all
\alpha>N
.

While there are no immediate simplification to be made to our expression for
\expectationvalue{s_{\lambda}(X)}
, we may instead exploit the following block matrix structure for the
M
-variable Schur polynomial (
2.24
),

\displaystyle\frac{s_{\lambda}(Z^{-1})}{\det(Z)^{N}}=\frac{1}{\Delta_{M}(Z)}\det_{\begin{subarray}{c}i=1,\ldots,N\\
\alpha=1,\ldots,M\\
a=1,\ldots,M-N\end{subarray}}\begin{bmatrix}z_{\alpha}^{-\lambda_{i}+i-(N+1)}\\
z_{\alpha}^{a-1}\end{bmatrix}.

(2.32)

Thus, the inverse characteristic polynomial average admits the following expansion over partitions,

\displaystyle\expectationvalue{ \prod_{\alpha=1}^M \det(z_\alpha- X)^{-1} }

\displaystyle=\frac{\tilde{\mathcal{Z}}_{N}^{-1}}{\Delta_{M}(Z)}\sum_{0\leq\lambda_{N}\leq\cdots\leq\lambda_{1}\leq\infty}\det_{\begin{subarray}{c}i=1,\ldots,N\\
\alpha=1,\ldots,M\\
a=1,\ldots,M-N\end{subarray}}\begin{bmatrix}z_{\alpha}^{-\lambda_{i}+i-(N+1)}\\
z_{\alpha}^{a-1}\end{bmatrix}\det_{1\leq i,j\leq N}\quantity[ \braket{p_{N-i}}{x^{\lambda_j + N - j}} ].

(2.33)

To perform the summation over the partition, we reintroduce
r_{i}=\lambda_{i}+N-i
obeying the strictly decreasing condition
r_{1}>r_{2}>\cdots>r_{N}\geq 0
.
We can use a slight generalization of the discrete Andréif--Heine identity accommodating the determinant of different size matrices
5
5

5

This is easiest to see by expanding the determinants using the Levi-Civita symbol.
to obtain

\displaystyle\sum_{0\leq\lambda_{N}\leq\cdots\leq\lambda_{1}\leq\infty}\det_{\begin{subarray}{c}i=1,\ldots,N\\
\alpha=1,\ldots,M\\
a=1,\ldots,M-N\end{subarray}}\begin{bmatrix}z_{\alpha}^{-\lambda_{i}+i-(N+1)}\\
z_{\alpha}^{a-1}\end{bmatrix}\det_{1\leq i,j\leq N}\quantity[ \braket{p_{N-i}}{x^{\lambda_j + N - j}} ]

\displaystyle=\det_{\begin{subarray}{c}i=1,\ldots,N\\
\alpha=1,\ldots,M\\
a=1,\ldots,M-N\end{subarray}}\begin{bmatrix}\displaystyle\sum_{r=0}^{\infty}z_{\alpha}^{-r-1}\innerproduct{p_{N-i}}{x^r}\\
z_{\alpha}^{a-1}\end{bmatrix}=\det_{\begin{subarray}{c}i=1,\ldots,N\\
\alpha=1,\ldots,M\\
a=1,\ldots,M-N\end{subarray}}\begin{bmatrix}\tilde{p}_{N-i}(z_{\alpha})\\
p_{a-1}(z_{\alpha})\end{bmatrix},

(2.34)

where we define the Hilbert transform of the monic polynomial,

\displaystyle\tilde{p}_{k}(z)=\int\frac{\differential{x}}{2\pi}e^{-V(x)}\frac{p_{k}(x)}{z-x}.

(2.35)

We have also used the invariance of the determinant under linear transformation to convert the monomial
z_{\alpha}^{a-1}
to an arbitrary monic polynomial
p_{a-1}(z_{\alpha})
.
This gives us the following determinantal formula for the inverse characteristic polynomial in the case of
M>N
,

\displaystyle\expectationvalue{ \prod_{\alpha=1}^M \det(z_\alpha- X)^{-1} }=\frac{\tilde{\mathcal{Z}}_{N}^{-1}}{\Delta_{M}(Z)}\det_{\begin{subarray}{c}i=1,\ldots,N\\
\alpha=1,\ldots,M\\
a=1,\ldots,M-N\end{subarray}}\begin{bmatrix}\tilde{p}_{N-i}(z_{\alpha})\\
p_{a-1}(z_{\alpha})\end{bmatrix}.

(2.36)

To the best of our knowledge, the inverse characteristic polynomial formula for
M>N
has not appeared before in the literature. It represents one of the main results of this paper.
We have thus seen how our analysis based on the Schur polynomial expansion provides a systematic method to calculate various averages of the characteristic polynomials, and their inverses.

3
Generalizing to Non-Zero Source

3.1
Schur Polynomial Average with Source

In order to calculate the characteristic polynomials with the source term, we first consider the (non-normalized) Schur polynomial average in the presence of an external matrix source,

\displaystyle\expectationvalue{s_\lambda(X)}_{A}=\int\differential{X}e^{-\Tr V(X)+i\Tr AX}s_{\lambda}(X)\,.

(3.1)

To evaluate this average, we use the Harish-Chandra–Itzykson–Zuber (HCIZ) formula
[
16
]

6
6

6

We remark

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\operatorname{Vol}U(N)}\int_{U(N)}\differential{U}e^{i\Tr XUYU^{-1}}\ \xrightarrow{X\to\mathbbm{1}_{N}}\ \frac{1}{\operatorname{Vol}U(N)}\int_{U(N)}\differential{U}e^{i\Tr Y}=e^{i\Tr Y}.

(3.2)

This fixes the normalization of the integral.

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\operatorname{Vol}U(N)}\int_{U(N)}\differential{U}e^{i\Tr XUYU^{-1}}=\frac{\Gamma_{2}(N+1)(-i)^{N(N-1)/2}}{\Delta_{N}(X)\Delta_{N}(Y)}\det_{1\leq i,j\leq N}\quantity[ e^{i x_i y_i} ],

(3.3)

which yields

\displaystyle\expectationvalue{s_\lambda(X)}_{A}

\displaystyle=\frac{C_{N}}{\Delta_{N}(A)}\frac{1}{N!}\int\prod_{i=1}^{N}\frac{\differential{x}_{i}}{2\pi}e^{-V(x_{i})}\det_{1\leq i,j\leq N}\quantity[ e^{i\sa_i x_j} ]\det_{1\leq i,j\leq N}\quantity[ x_i^{\lambda_j + N - j} ]

\displaystyle=\frac{C_{N}}{\Delta_{N}(A)}\det_{1\leq i,j\leq N}\quantity[ \braket{ e^{i\sa_i x} }{x^{\lambda_j + N - j}} ].

(3.4)

with
(\mathsf{a}_{i})_{i=1,\ldots,N}
the eigenvalues of
A
and the normalization constant

\displaystyle C_{N}=\operatorname{Vol}U(N)\times\Gamma_{2}(N+1)\times(-i)^{N(N-1)/2}=(2\pi)^{N(N+1)/2}(-i)^{N(N-1)/2}.

(3.5)

We use this result in the following to compute the characteristic polynomial, and its inverse, with non-zero source.

3.2
Characteristic Polynomial Average with Source

We first compute the expectation value of multiple determinant insertions, in the presence of source.
In this Section, we reproduce the results of
[
13
]
, in particular its Eq. (3.10).

Applying the Schur polynomial expansion together with the formula (
3.4
), we obtain

\displaystyle\expectationvalue{\prod_{\alpha=1}^N \det(z_\alpha- X)}_{A}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\Delta_{M}(Z)}\sum_{\lambda\subseteq(M^{N})}(-1)^{|\lambda|}\det_{1\leq\alpha,\beta\leq M}\quantity[z_\alpha^{N - \lambda_\beta^\text{T} + \beta- 1} ]\expectationvalue{s_\lambda(X)}_{A}

\displaystyle=\frac{C_{N}}{\Delta_{M}(Z)\Delta_{N}(A)}\sum_{\lambda\subseteq(M^{N})}(-1)^{|\lambda|}\det_{1\leq\alpha,\beta\leq M}\quantity[ z_\alpha^{N - \lambda_\beta^\text{T} + \beta- 1} ]\det_{1\leq i,j\leq N}\left[\innerproduct{e^{i\sa_{i}x}}{x^{N+\lambda_{j}-j}}\right].

(3.6)

We can again recognize this as the minor expansion on the first
M
rows of the following
(N+M)\times(N+M)
matrix,

\displaystyle\sum_{\lambda\subseteq(M^{N})}(-1)^{|\lambda|}\det_{1\leq\alpha,\beta\leq M}\quantity[ z_\alpha^{N - \lambda_\beta^\text{T} + \beta- 1} ]\det_{1\leq i,j\leq N}\quantity[ \braket{e^{i\sa_{i}x}}{x^{\lambda_j + N - j}} ]

\displaystyle=\det_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq\alpha,\beta\leq M\\
1\leq i,j\leq N\end{subarray}}\begin{bmatrix}z_{\alpha}^{N+M-\beta}&z_{\alpha}^{N-j}\\
\innerproduct{e^{i\sa_{i}x}}{x^{N + M - \beta}}&\innerproduct{e^{i\sa_{i}x}}{x^{N - j}}\end{bmatrix}=\det_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq\alpha,\beta\leq M\\
1\leq i,j\leq N\end{subarray}}\begin{bmatrix}p_{N+M-\beta}(z_{\alpha})&p_{N-j}(z_{\alpha})\\
\innerproduct{e^{i\sa_{i}x}}{p_{N + M - \beta}}&\innerproduct{e^{i\sa_{i}x}}{p_{N - j}}\end{bmatrix}\,.

(3.7)

where in going to the last equality, we again used the linearity of the inner-product and the invariance of the determinant under linear transformations on its rows to trade
x^{N+M-\beta}\rightarrow p_{N+M-\beta}(x)
.
We can thus write the final result as

\displaystyle\expectationvalue{\prod_{\alpha=1}^N \det(z_\alpha- X)}_{A}

\displaystyle=\frac{C_{N}}{\Delta_{M}(Z)\Delta_{N}(A)}\det_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq\alpha,\beta\leq M\\
1\leq i,j\leq N\end{subarray}}\begin{bmatrix}p_{N+M-\beta}(z_{\alpha})&p_{N-j}(z_{\alpha})\\
Q_{N+M-\beta}(\mathsf{a}_{i})&Q_{N-j}(\mathsf{a}_{i})\end{bmatrix}\,

(3.8)

where we borrowed the notation of
[
13
]

Q_{k}(\mathsf{a})=\innerproduct{e^{i\sa x}}{p_{k}}=\int\frac{\differential{x}}{2\pi}e^{-V(x)+i\mathsf{a}x}p_{k}(x).

(3.9)

This shows the formula presented in (
1.10
).

3.3
Inverse Characteristic Polynomial Average with Source

We now wish to generalize our calculation to the inverse characteristic polynomial with non-zero source

\expectationvalue{\prod_{\alpha=1}^{M} \det(z_{\alpha}-X)^{-1}}_{A}=\int\differential{X}e^{-\Tr V(x)+i\Tr AX}\prod_{\alpha=1}^{M}\frac{1}{\det(z_{\alpha}-X)}.

(3.10)

This computation is the main technical result of this paper.

With the expectation value of
s_{\lambda}(x)
in the presence of the source
A
as in (
3.4
), we use the Cauchy sum formula to expand the inverse determinants in terms of Schur polynomials.
We begin with the case
M<N
. This means
\lambda_{i}=0
for
i>M
.
Then, we obtain a block matrix structure similar in spirit to the case without the source term (
2.25
),

\displaystyle\expectationvalue{s_\lambda(X)}_{A}

\displaystyle=\frac{C_{N}}{\Delta_{N}(A)}\det_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq\alpha,\beta\leq M\\
M+1\leq a,b\leq N\end{subarray}}\begin{bmatrix}\innerproduct{e^{i\sa_\alpha x}}{x^{\lambda_\beta+ N - \beta}}&\innerproduct{e^{i\sa_\alpha x}}{p_{N - b}}\\
\innerproduct{e^{i\sa_a x}}{x^{\lambda_\beta+ N - \beta}}&\innerproduct{e^{i\sa_a x}}{p_{N - b}}\end{bmatrix}.

(3.11)

None of the blocks in the above matrix vanish. Hence, the determinant no longer factorizes into a determinant of the
M\times M
and
(N-M)\times(N-M)
diagonal blocks. This is the main complication relative to the case without source.

Using the same notation as in Section
2.3
, we now want to perform the following sum over representations

\displaystyle\frac{1}{M!}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}0\leq r_{1},...,r_{M}\leq\infty\\
r_{\alpha}\neq r_{\beta}\end{subarray}}\det_{1\leq\alpha,\beta\leq M}\left[z_{\alpha}^{M-N-r_{\beta}-1}\right]\det_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq\alpha,\beta\leq M\\
M+1\leq a,b\leq N\end{subarray}}\begin{bmatrix}\innerproduct{e^{i\sa_\alpha x}}{x^{\lambda_\beta+ N - \beta}}&\innerproduct{e^{i\sa_\alpha x}}{p_{N - b}}\\
\innerproduct{e^{i\sa_a x}}{x^{\lambda_\beta+ N - \beta}}&\innerproduct{e^{i\sa_a x}}{p_{N - b}}\end{bmatrix}.

(3.12)

After yet another application of the generalized discrete Andréif–Heine identity and performing the geometric sum, we arrive at

\displaystyle\expectationvalue{\prod_{\alpha=1}^{M} \det(z_{\alpha}-X)^{-1}}_{A}=

\displaystyle\frac{C_{N}}{\Delta_{N}(A)\Delta_{M}(Z)}\det_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq\alpha,\beta\leq M\\
M+1\leq a,b\leq N\end{subarray}}\begin{bmatrix}\innerproduct{e^{i\sa_\alpha}}{ \frac{(x/z_{\beta})^{N-M}}{z_\beta- x} }&\innerproduct{e^{i\sa_\alpha}}{ p_{N - b} }\\[5.0pt]
\innerproduct{e^{i\sa_a}}{ \frac{(x/z_{\beta})^{N-M}}{z_\beta- x} }&\innerproduct{e^{i\sa_a}}{ p_{N - b} }\end{bmatrix}.

(3.13)

The final simplification arises by noting that we can rewrite

z^{M-N}\left(\frac{x^{N-M}}{z-x}\right)=\frac{1}{z-x}+\sum_{s=0}^{N-M-1}\frac{x^{s}}{z^{s+1}},

(3.14)

so that the first
M
columns can be decomposed as

\displaystyle z_{\beta}^{M-N}\innerproduct{e^{i\sa_i x}}{\frac{x^{N-M}}{z_\beta- x}}

\displaystyle=\innerproduct{e^{i\sa_i x}}{\frac{1}{z_\beta- x}}+\sum_{b=M+1}^{N}z_{\beta}^{-N+b-1}\innerproduct{e^{i\sa_i x}}{x^{N-b}}.

(3.15)

The determinant is insensitive to this linear superposing of the other
N-M
columns, so we can drop these other terms. Our final result therefore reads, as advertised:

\displaystyle\expectationvalue{\prod_{\alpha=1}^{M} \det(z_{\alpha} -X)^{-1} }_{A}=

\displaystyle\frac{C_{N}}{\Delta_{N}(A)\Delta_{M}(Z)}\det_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq\alpha,\beta\leq M\\
M+1\leq a,b\leq N\end{subarray}}\begin{bmatrix}\tilde{Q}(\mathsf{a}_{\alpha},z_{\beta})&Q_{N-b}(\mathsf{a}_{\alpha})\\
\tilde{Q}(\mathsf{a}_{a},z_{\beta})&Q_{N-b}(\mathsf{a}_{a})\end{bmatrix}

(3.16)

with the new function
\tilde{Q}(\mathsf{a},z)
defined as in Eq. (
1.12
).
The structure of this result agrees with a particular limit of a supermatrix model studied by one of the authors in
[
17
]
(see the
M=p=0
limit of Eq. (3.3) in that paper). The normalization could not be computed there due to the use of a supermatrix generalization of the HCIZ angular integral.

3.3.1
Zero Source Limit

We can further check our formula by considering the limit of vanishing source,
A\rightarrow 0
. This limit is not completely straightforward because of Van-der-Monde determinant
\Delta_{N}(A)
appearing in the denominator. We therefore need to expand the numerator to
\mathcal{O}(\mathsf{a}_{i}^{N-1})
.
Begin by writing (the unusual labeling of
k
in the Taylor expansion will be useful below)

\displaystyle\tilde{Q}(\mathsf{a}_{i},z_{\beta})=

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{N}\frac{1}{(N-k)!}\tilde{Q}^{(N-k)}(0,z_{\beta})\mathsf{a}_{i}^{N-k}+\mathcal{O}(\mathsf{a}_{i}^{N}),

(3.17a)

\displaystyle Q_{N-b}(\mathsf{a}_{i})=

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{N}\frac{1}{(N-k)!}Q^{(N-k)}_{N-b}(0)\mathsf{a}_{i}^{N-k}+\mathcal{O}(\mathsf{a}_{i}^{N}),

(3.17b)

where

\displaystyle\tilde{Q}^{(k)}(0,z_{\beta})=

\displaystyle\left.\frac{\partial^{k}\tilde{Q}(\mathsf{a}_{i},z_{\beta})}{\partial\mathsf{a}_{i}^{k}}\right|_{\mathsf{a}_{i}=0}=i^{k}\int\frac{\differential{x}}{2\pi}e^{-V(x)}\frac{x^{k}}{z_{\beta}-x},

(3.18a)

\displaystyle Q^{(k)}_{N-b}(0)=

\displaystyle\left.\frac{\partial^{k}Q_{N-b}(\mathsf{a}_{i})}{\partial\mathsf{a}_{i}^{k}}\right|_{\mathsf{a}_{i}=0}=i^{k}\int\frac{\differential{x}}{2\pi}e^{-V(x)}x^{k}p_{N-b}(x).

(3.18b)

Interpreting the sum in the Taylor expansions of Eq. (
3.17
) as matrix multiplication, and using
\det(AB)=\det(A)\det(B)
, we arrive at the following expansion for the determinant

\displaystyle\det_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq\alpha,\beta\leq M\\
M+1\leq a,b\leq N\end{subarray}}\begin{bmatrix}\tilde{Q}(\mathsf{a}_{\alpha},z_{\beta})&Q_{N-b}(\mathsf{a}_{\alpha})\\
\tilde{Q}(\mathsf{a}_{a},z_{\beta})&Q_{N-b}(\mathsf{a}_{a})\end{bmatrix}

\displaystyle=\det_{1\leq i,j\leq N}\left[\frac{1}{(N-j)!}\mathsf{a}_{i}^{N-j}\right]\det_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq\alpha,\beta\leq M\\
M+1\leq a,b\leq N\end{subarray}}\begin{bmatrix}\tilde{Q}^{(N-\alpha)}(0,z_{\beta})&Q^{(N-\alpha)}_{N-b}(0)\\
\tilde{Q}^{(N-a)}(0,z_{\beta})&Q^{(N-a)}_{N-b}(0)\end{bmatrix}+\mathcal{O}(\mathsf{a}_{i}^{N}).

(3.19)

The upper right block
Q_{N-b}^{(N-\alpha)}(0)
vanishes by orthogonality if we specialize the monic polynomial
p_{k}(x)\to P_{k}(x)
, so that the second determinant factorizes into a determinant of each diagonal block. With the Van-der-Monde determinant written as
\Delta_{N}(A)=\det_{1\leq i,j\leq N}(\mathsf{a}_{i}^{N-j})
, and using the invariance of the determinant under linear transformation of the rows, we can simplify the above to

\displaystyle\frac{\tilde{\mathcal{Z}}_{N-M}}{\Gamma_{2}(N+1)(-i)^{N(N-1)/2}}\Delta_{N}(A)\det_{1\leq\alpha,\beta\leq M}\left[\int\frac{\differential{x}}{2\pi}e^{-V(x)}\frac{P_{N-\alpha}(x)}{z_{\beta}-x}\right]+\mathcal{O}(\mathsf{a}_{i}^{N})

(3.20)

where
\tilde{\mathcal{Z}}_{N-M}=\prod_{a=M+1}^{N}h_{N-a}
comes from evaluating the determinant of lower right block. It is trivial now to take the limit
\mathsf{a}_{i}\rightarrow 0
and recuperate the result of Section
2.3
(recalling the relative factor of
\mathcal{Z}_{N}
used in the normalization there).

3.3.2
The Case with
M>N

So far, we have assumed the condition
M\leq N
in the computation.
We now consider the opposite situation
M>N
, and calculate the inverse characteristic polynomial average.

In this case, we similarly apply the determinantal formula (
2.32
), and put to use the Schur polynomial average with source (
3.4
).
We then obtain

\displaystyle\expectationvalue{\prod_{\alpha=1}^{M} \det(z_{\alpha}-X)^{-1}}_{A}

\displaystyle=\frac{C_{N}}{\Delta_{M}(Z)\Delta_{N}(A)}\sum_{0\leq\lambda_{N}\leq\cdots\leq\lambda_{1}\leq\infty}\det_{\begin{subarray}{c}i=1,\ldots,N\\
\alpha=1,\ldots,M\\
a=1,\ldots,M-N\end{subarray}}\begin{bmatrix}z_{\alpha}^{-\lambda_{i}+i-(N+1)}\\
z_{\alpha}^{a-1}\end{bmatrix}\det_{1\leq i,j\leq N}\quantity[ \braket{ e^{i\sa_i x} }{x^{\lambda_j + N - j}} ].

(3.21)

Introducing
r_{i}=\lambda_{i}+N-i
obeying
r_{1}>r_{2}>\cdots>r_{N}\geq 0
as before, we can again use the generalized discrete Andréif–Heine identity to write

\displaystyle\sum_{0\leq\lambda_{N}\leq\cdots\leq\lambda_{1}\leq\infty}\det_{\begin{subarray}{c}i=1,\ldots,N\\
\alpha=1,\ldots,M\\
a=1,\ldots,M-N\end{subarray}}\begin{bmatrix}z_{\alpha}^{-\lambda_{i}+i-(N+1)}\\
z_{\alpha}^{a-1}\end{bmatrix}\det_{1\leq i,j\leq N}\quantity[ \braket{ e^{i\sa_i x} }{x^{\lambda_j + N - j}} ]

\displaystyle=\det_{\begin{subarray}{c}i=1,\ldots,N\\
\alpha=1,\ldots,M\\
a=1,\ldots,M-N\end{subarray}}\begin{bmatrix}\displaystyle\sum_{r=0}^{\infty}z_{\alpha}^{-r-1}\innerproduct{e^{i\sa_i x}}{x^r}\\
z_{\alpha}^{a-1}\end{bmatrix}=\det_{\begin{subarray}{c}i=1,\ldots,N\\
\alpha=1,\ldots,M\\
a=1,\ldots,M-N\end{subarray}}\begin{bmatrix}\tilde{Q}(\mathsf{a}_{i},z_{\alpha})\\
p_{a-1}(z_{\alpha})\end{bmatrix}.

(3.22)

To convert
z_{\alpha}^{a-1}
to
p_{a-1}(z_{\alpha})
in the last equality, we have used the invariance of the determinant under linear transformation.
Hence, we arrive at the inverse characteristic polynomial formula for
M>N
,

\displaystyle\expectationvalue{\prod_{\alpha=1}^{M} \det(z_{\alpha}-X)^{-1}}_{A}=\frac{C_{N}}{\Delta_{M}(Z)\Delta_{N}(A)}\det_{\begin{subarray}{c}i=1,\ldots,N\\
\alpha=1,\ldots,M\\
a=1,\ldots,M-N\end{subarray}}\begin{bmatrix}\tilde{Q}(\mathsf{a}_{i},z_{\alpha})\\
p_{a-1}(z_{\alpha})\end{bmatrix}.

(3.23)

Zero Source Limit

The zero source limit
A\to 0
of the formula (

3.3.1
.
Applying the Taylor expansion for the function
\tilde{Q}(\mathsf{a}_{i},z_{\alpha})
with respect to the source (

), we straightforwardly obtain

\displaystyle\lim_{A\to 0}\expectationvalue{\prod_{\alpha=1}^{M} \det(z_{\alpha}-X)^{-1}}_{A}=\frac{\operatorname{Vol}U(N)}{\Delta_{M}(Z)}\det_{\begin{subarray}{c}i=1,\ldots,N\\

a=1,\ldots,M-N\end{subarray}}\begin{bmatrix}\tilde{p}_{N-i}(z_{\alpha})\\
p_{a-1}(z_{\alpha})\end{bmatrix}.

(3.24)

This reproduces the previous result (
2.36
), again remembering the relative normalization constant
\mathcal{Z}_{N}
(
2.12
).

3.3.3
Characteristic Polynomial/Source Duality

The characteristic polynomial with the source shows an interesting duality under
Z\leftrightarrow A

[
4
,
18
,
13
]
.
We demonstrate this duality using the formula obtained in this paper.

Let us focus on the Gaussian case for simplicity,
V(x)=x^{2}/2
.
Then,
P_{k}
and
Q_{k}
are essentially equivalent to each other in this case.
Furthermore, we have

\displaystyle e^{\frac{1}{2}\mathsf{a}^{2}}\tilde{Q}(\mathsf{a},z)

\displaystyle=\int\frac{\differential{x}}{2\pi}\frac{e^{-\frac{1}{2}(x-i\mathsf{a})^{2}}}{z-x}=\int\frac{\differential{x}}{2\pi}\frac{e^{-\frac{1}{2}x^{2}}}{z-i\mathsf{a}-x}=\int\frac{\differential{x}}{2\pi}\frac{e^{-\frac{1}{2}(x+z)^{2}}}{-i\mathsf{a}-x}=e^{-\frac{1}{2}z^{2}}\tilde{Q}(iz,-i\mathsf{a}).

(3.25)

Comparing the two expressions for the inverse characteristic polynomial (
3.16
) and (
3.23
), we explicitly see the duality
Z\leftrightarrow A
in the Gaussian case. This duality had been derived for the Gaussian case via an integrating in-out-in-out procedure, rewriting the inverse determinants in terms of a bosonic integral (see section 4.2 of
[
13
]
).

4
Discussion

Eigenvalue “Fermi Sea” Picture and the
M>N
Regime for Inverse Determinant Correlators

The
M>N

String Theory Perspective on the Schur Expansion of Characteristic Polynomials

Matrix models provide one of the most concrete ways to study open/closed string duality. In particular, we can explicitly implement the idea of “closing up holes” on the open string worldsheet, replacing them with a superposition of closed string vertex operators. On the matrix side, this corresponds simply to rewriting the determinant operator as
\det(z-X)=\exp\Tr\log(z-X)
and Taylor expanding for large
z
:

\prod_{\alpha=1}^{M}\det(z_{\alpha}-X)=\det_{M\times M}(Z)\exp\quantity( -\sum_{k=1}^{\infty} t_{k} \Tr X^{k} )

(4.1)

with

t_{k}=\frac{1}{k}\Tr_{M}(Z^{-k}).

(4.2)

The
t_{k}
’s are the so-called Miwa variables, and play the role of couplings for the closed string worldsheet “vertex operators”
\Tr X^{k}
. They play a crucial role in the Kontsevich duality
[
20
]
. Eq. (
4.2
) shows exactly how the insertion of D-branes can equivalently be re-expressed in terms of a new closed string background.

One interesting take on the Schur expansion of the characteristic polynomial is as a decomposition of the brane insertions in terms of an orthogonal set of multi-closed-string ‘‘operators’’.

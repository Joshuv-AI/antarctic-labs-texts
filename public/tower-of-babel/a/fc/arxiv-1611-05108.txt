1.
Introduction

Denote by
\mathbb{C}_{n\times n}
the set of
n\times n
complex matrices and
\mathbb{P}_{n}\subset\mathbb{C}_{n\times n}
the set of
n\times n
positive definite matrices.
For
A\in\mathbb{C}_{n\times n}
, we denote by
A^{*}
and
|A|=(A^{*}A)^{\frac{1}{2}}
the conjugate transpose and the positive semidefinite part of
A
, respectively. Given
n\times n
Hermitian matrices
A
and
B
,
A\leq B
means that
B-A
is positive semidefinite.

For
x=(x_{1},x_{2},\dots,x_{n})
,
y=(y_{1},y_{2},\dots,y_{n})\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, let
x^{\downarrow}=(x_{[1]},x_{[2]},\dots,x_{[n]})
denote a rearrangement of the components of
x
such that
x_{[1]}\geq x_{[2]}\geq\cdots\geq x_{[n]}
. The notation
x\leq y
means that
x_{[i]}\leq y_{[i]},\,\,i=1,\ldots,n.
We say that
x
is weakly majorized by
y
, denoted by
x\prec_{w}y
, if
\sum_{j=1}^{k}x_{[j]}\leq\sum_{j=1}^{k}y_{[j]}
for all
1\leq k\leq n
. We say that
x
is majorized by
y
, denoted by
x\prec y
, if
x\prec_{w}y
and
\sum_{j=1}^{n}x_{j}=\sum_{j=1}^{n}y_{j}
.

Let
\mathbb{R}_{+}
denote the set of all positive real numbers and
\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}=(\mathbb{R}_{+})^{n}
. Given
x,y\in(\mathbb{R}_{+})^{n}
, we say that
x
is weakly log-majorized by
y
, written as
x\prec_{w\,\log}y
, if
\prod_{i=1}^{k}x_{[i]}\leq\prod_{i=1}^{k}y_{[i]}
, for
k=1,\ldots,n
;
x
is log-majorized by
y
, denoted by
x\prec_{\log}y
, if
x\prec_{w\,\log}y
and
\prod_{j=1}^{n}x_{j}=\prod_{j=1}^{n}y_{j}
.

Let
A\in\mathbb{P}_{n}
. Denote by
\lambda(A)=(\lambda_{1}(A),\dots,\lambda_{n}(A))\in\mathbb{R}_{+}^{n}
the vector of eigenvalues of
A
and we may arrange the eigenvalues in non-increasing order
\lambda_{1}(A)\geq\cdots\geq\lambda_{n}(A)
.

Matic
[
5
, Theorem 1.1]
proved the following determinantal inequality. Zhang
[
8
]
and Choi
[
2
]
gave two different proofs, respectively. We state the theorem using Choi’s version.

Theorem 1.1
.

(Matic
[
5
]
)
Let
C\in\mathbb{P}_{n}
and
D=D_{1}\oplus\dots\oplus D_{k}
, where
D_{1}\in\mathbb{P}_{n_{1}},\dots,D_{k}\in\mathbb{P}_{n_{k}}
with
n_{1}+\cdots+n_{k}=n
. Partition
C
according to
(n_{1},\dots,n_{k})
so that
{\mbox{Diag}\,}C=C_{1}\oplus\dots\oplus C_{k}
in which
{\mbox{Diag}\,}C
is the main block diagonal of
C
. Then

(1.1)

\det(I_{n_{1}}+C_{1}^{-1}D_{1})\cdots\det(I_{n_{k}}+C_{k}^{-1}D_{k})\leq\det(I_{n}+C^{-1}D).

In this paper we obtain the following weak log majorization result where
C
and
D
are given as in Theorem
1.1
:

(1.2)

\lambda(C^{-1}_{1}D_{1}\oplus\cdots\oplus C^{-1}_{k}D_{k})\prec_{w\,\log}\lambda(C^{-1}D).

We note that (
1.2
) does not hold if one replaces the vectors of eigenvalues by the vectors of singular values.

We would like to point out that (
1.2
) is more fundamental than (
1.1
). In order to see that we will first derive the following determinantal inequality as an application of (
1.2
):

(1.3)

\det(I_{n_{1}}+(C_{1}^{-1}D_{1})^{p})\cdots\det(I_{n_{k}}+(C_{k}^{-1}D_{k})^{p})\leq\det(I_{n}+(C^{-1}D)^{p}),\quad p\geq 0,

which is evidently a generalization of (
1.1
).
We will show by example that (
1.3
) is not true when
p<0
.

By looking at (
1.3
) as a generalization of (
1.1
), one might ask whether the following two possible generalizations of (
1.1
) are true or not:

(1.4)

\det(I_{n_{1}}+|C_{1}^{-1}D_{1}|^{p})\cdots\det(I_{n_{k}}+|C_{k}^{-1}D_{k}|^{p})\leq\det(I_{n}+|C^{-1}D|^{p}),\quad p\geq 0,

and

(1.5)

\det(I_{n_{1}}+C_{1}^{-p}D_{1}^{p})\cdots\det(I_{n_{k}}+C_{k}^{-p}D_{k}^{p})\leq\det(I_{n}+C^{-p}D^{p}),\quad p\geq 0.

Both answers are negative and we will provide a counterexample for both inequalities.

Choi
[
2
, Theorem 2]
obtained the following determinantal inequality:

Theorem 1.2
.

(Choi
[
2
]
) Let
A_{i}\in\mathbb{P}_{n},\,i=1,\dots,m
, and
{\mbox{Diag}\,}A_{i}=A_{i}^{(1)}\oplus\cdots\oplus A_{i}^{(k)}
,
where
A_{i}^{(j)}\in\mathbb{P}_{n_{j}}
for
i=1,\dots,m
,
j=1,\ldots,k
.

(1.6)

\det\left(\sum_{i=1}^{m}(A_{i}^{(1)})^{-1}\right)\cdots\det\left(\sum_{i=1}^{m}(A_{i}^{(k)})^{-1}\right)\leq\det\left(\sum_{i=1}^{m}A_{i}^{-1}\right).

We present a weak majorization inequality which is complementary to (
1.6
) and pose a weak log majorization open problem.

2.
Weak log majorization and generalization of Matic’s determinantal inequality

Regarding (
1.3
), one may ask whether
(C^{-1}D)^{p}
is well defined or not when
p\in\mathbb{R}
. The question makes sense as we know that
X^{p}
is not necessarily defined for a general matrix
X\in\mathbb{C}_{n\times n}
. However, we can define
X^{p}

when
X
is hyperbolic, i.e.,
X
is diagonalizable with positive eigenvalues.
Let
\mathbb{H}_{n}
be the set of all hyperbolic matrices in
\mathbb{C}_{n\times n}
and
\mathbb{D}_{n}
be the set of all diagonalizable matrices in
\mathbb{C}_{n\times n}
with real eigenvalues.
We claim that the exponential map
\exp:\mathbb{D}_{n}\to\mathbb{H}_{n}
is bijective. It is surjective since each
X\in\mathbb{H}_{n}
can be written as
X=SDS^{-1}
for some nonsingular
S
, where
D={\mbox{diag}\,}(e^{\lambda_{1}},\dots,e^{\lambda_{n}})
and
\lambda_{1},\dots,\lambda_{n}\in\mathbb{R}
. Set
Y:=S(\log D)S^{-1}
,
where
\log D:={\mbox{diag}\,}(\lambda_{1},\dots,\lambda_{n})
. Note that

e^{Y}=e^{S(\log D)S^{-1}}=S(e^{\log D})S^{-1}=SDS^{-1}=X.

We are going to show that
\exp
is injective.
Let
Y_{1},Y_{2}\in\mathbb{D}_{n}
such that
e^{Y_{1}}=e^{Y_{2}}
. Let
P_{1}D_{1}P_{1}^{-1}=Y_{1}
and
P_{2}D_{2}P_{2}^{-1}=Y_{2}
, where
D_{1}
and
D_{2}
are diagonal with diagonal entries arranged in non-increasing order. Taking exponentials of both sides yields
P_{1}e^{D_{1}}P_{1}^{-1}=P_{2}e^{D_{2}}P_{2}^{-1}
and thus

D_{1}=D_{2}=D
, say, by spectrum consideration. Write
D:=d_{1}I_{n_{1}}\oplus\cdots\oplus d_{k}I_{n_{k}}
, where
d_{1}>\cdots>d_{k}
. Set
Q=P_{1}^{-1}P_{2}
. So

e^{D}Q=Qe^{D}
and hence
Q=Q_{1}\oplus\cdots\oplus Q_{k}
, where
Q_{i}\in\mathbb{C}_{n_{i}\times n_{i}}
. Thus
DQ=QD
, i.e.,

DP_{1}^{-1}P_{2}=P_{1}^{-1}P_{2}D
, which implies that
Y_{1}=Y_{2}
. So we conclude that the map
\exp:\mathbb{D}_{n}\to\mathbb{H}_{n}
is bijective. Now given
X\in\mathbb{H}_{n}
,
define
X^{p}:=e^{pY}
,
p\in\mathbb{R}
, where
Y
is the unique matrix in
\mathbb{D}_{n}
such that
X=e^{Y}
. Explicitly,
if we write
X=S{\mbox{diag}\,}(\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{n})S^{-1}
for some nonsingular
S\in\mathbb{C}_{n\times n}
, where
\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{n}\in\mathbb{R}_{+}
are the eigenvalues of
X
in any order, then

X^{p}:=S{\mbox{diag}\,}(\xi_{1}^{p},\dots,\xi_{n}^{p})S^{-1}
since
Y=S{\mbox{diag}\,}(\log\xi_{1},\dots,\log\xi_{n})S^{-1}
.

Let us get back to the well-definedness of
(C^{-1}D)^{p}
,
p\in\mathbb{R}
, regarding (
1.3
). Note that
C^{-1},D\in\mathbb{P}_{n}
.
Given
A,B\in\mathbb{P}_{n}
, the product
AB
is hyperbolic though it may not be in
\mathbb{P}_{n}
. It is because that
AB
is similar to
B^{1/2}ABB^{-1/2}=B^{1/2}AB^{1/2}\in\mathbb{P}_{n}
, which is unitarily similar to a positive diagonal matrix. Hence
AB=P{\mbox{diag}\,}(\gamma_{1},\dots,\gamma_{n})P^{-1}
, for some nonsingular
P\in\mathbb{C}_{n\times n}
, where
\gamma_{1},\dots,\gamma_{n}\in\mathbb{R}_{+}
so that
(AB)^{p}=P{\mbox{diag}\,}(\gamma_{1}^{p},\dots,\gamma_{n}^{p})P^{-1}
,
p\in\mathbb{R}
.

Now we give our main result as follows.

Theorem 2.1
.

Under the conditions as in Theorem
1.1
, we have

(2.1)

\lambda(C^{-1}_{1}D_{1}\oplus\cdots\oplus C^{-1}_{k}D_{k})\prec_{w\,\log}\lambda(C^{-1}D).

Proof.

It suffices to prove the case when
D=I_{n}
:

(2.2)

\displaystyle\lambda(C_{1}^{-1}\oplus\cdots\oplus C_{k}^{-1})\prec_{w\,\log}\lambda(C^{-1}).

The reason is that

\lambda(C^{-1}D)=\lambda((D^{-1/2}CD^{-1/2})^{-1})

and

{\mbox{Diag}\,}(D^{-1/2}CD^{-1/2})=(D_{1}^{-1/2}C_{1}D_{1}^{-1/2})\oplus\cdots\oplus(D_{k}^{-1/2}C_{k}D_{k}^{-1/2}).

Thus
(
2.1
) is equivalent to

\lambda(({\mbox{Diag}\,}(D^{-1/2}CD^{-1/2}))^{-1})\prec_{w\,\log}\lambda((D^{-1/2}CD^{-1/2})^{-1})

so it is sufficient to show (
2.2
).
By a result of Ky Fan
[
4
, p.308]
we have

(2.3)

\displaystyle\lambda(C_{1}\oplus\dots\oplus C_{k})\prec\lambda(C).

It is known that
[
4
, p.165]
for any convex function
f:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
, if
x\prec y
, where
x=(x_{1},x_{2},\ldots,x_{n}),y=(y_{1},y_{2},\ldots,y_{n})\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, then

(f(x_{1}),f(x_{2}),\ldots,f(x_{n}))\prec_{w}(f(y_{1}),f(y_{2}),\ldots,f(y_{n})).

Since
f(x)=x^{-p}
, where
p>0
, is convex on
(0,+\infty)
, by (
2.3
), we have

(2.4)

\displaystyle\lambda(C_{1}^{-p}\oplus\cdots\oplus C_{k}^{-p})\prec_{w}\lambda(C^{-p}),\quad p>0.

For
m
positive numbers
a_{1},\dots,a_{m}
, their power mean of order
r
is defined by

M_{r}(a):=\left(\frac{1}{m}\sum_{i=1}^{m}a_{i}^{r}\right)^{\frac{1}{r}}

and it is known that
\lim_{r\to 0^{+}}M_{r}(a)=(\prod_{i=1}^{m}a_{i})^{\frac{1}{m}}
(see
[
3
, p.15]
). Applying this to (
2.4
) leads to

\lambda(C^{-1}_{1}\oplus\cdots\oplus C^{-1}_{k})\prec_{w\,\log}\lambda(C^{-1}),

i.e., (
2.2
).
Thus, we complete the proof.
∎

2.1
) may not be true if
D\in\mathbb{P}_{n}
is not in diagonal block form, where

{\mbox{Diag}\,}D=D_{1}\oplus\dots\oplus D_{k}
, and
D_{1}\in\mathbb{P}_{n_{1}},\dots,D_{k}\in\mathbb{P}_{n_{k}}
with
n_{1}+\cdots+n_{k}=n
.

Example 2.2
.

Let

C=\begin{pmatrix}14&8&9&8\\
8&12&7&7\\
9&7&10&8\\
8&7&8&8\end{pmatrix},\quad C_{1}=\begin{pmatrix}14&8\\
8&12\end{pmatrix},\quad C_{2}=\begin{pmatrix}10&8\\
8&8\end{pmatrix},

D=\begin{pmatrix}11&12&6&11\\
12&16&7&12\\
6&7&5&6\\
11&12&6&14\end{pmatrix},\quad D_{1}=\begin{pmatrix}11&12\\
12&16\end{pmatrix},\quad D_{2}=\begin{pmatrix}5&6\\
6&14\end{pmatrix}.

By Matlab,

\displaystyle\lambda(C^{-1}D)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(4.8921,1.0664,0.3433,0.1772),

\displaystyle\lambda(C_{1}^{-1}D_{1})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(1.3488,0.2281),

\displaystyle\lambda(C_{2}^{-1}D_{2})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(4.8080,0.4420).

So
\lambda(C_{1}^{-1}D_{1}\oplus C_{2}^{-1}D_{2})\not\prec_{w\,\log}\lambda(C^{-1}D)
by considering the products of the first two largest entries of both sides.

Remark 2.3
.

Remark 2.4
.

Taking

in Theorem

\prod_{i=m}^{n}\lambda_{i}(C)\leq\prod_{i=m}^{n}\lambda_{i}(C_{1}\oplus\cdots\oplus C_{k}),\,\,\,m=1,\ldots,n.

(

Remark 2.5
.

Let
C\in\mathbb{P}_{n}
be given as in Theorem
1.1
and
D\in\mathbb{C}_{n\times n}
be positive semidefinite with
{\mbox{Diag}\,}D=D_{1}\oplus\cdots\oplus D_{k}
. Wang, Xi, and Zhang

proved

\lambda(C_{1}^{-1}D_{1}\oplus 0)\leq\lambda(C^{-1}D).

In Example
2.2
, one can see that
(\lambda(C_{i}^{-1}D_{i}),0,0)\leq\lambda(C^{-1}D)
,

. Theorem

Next we give an application of Theorem
2.1
as follows.

Theorem 2.6
.

Under the conditions as in Theorem
1.1
, we have

(2.6)

\det(I_{n_{1}}+(C_{1}^{-1}D_{1})^{p})\cdots\det(I_{n_{k}}+(C_{k}^{-1}D_{k})^{p})\leq\det(I_{n}+(C^{-1}D)^{p}),\quad p\geq 0.

Proof.

When
p=0
, it is trivial so we may assume
p>0
.
By Theorem
2.1
, we have

(2.7)

\log\lambda(C^{-1}_{1}D_{1}\oplus\cdots\oplus C^{-1}_{k}D_{k})\prec_{w}\log\lambda(C^{-1}D).

Let us recall a known result
[
4
, p.167]
for any increasing convex function
f:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
. If
x\prec_{w}y
, where
x=(x_{1},x_{2},\ldots,x_{n}),y=(y_{1},y_{2},\ldots,y_{n})\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, then

(f(x_{1}),f(x_{2}),\ldots,f(x_{n}))\prec_{w}(f(y_{1}),f(y_{2}),\ldots,f(y_{n})).

Since the function
f(x)=\log(1+e^{px})
, where
p>0
, is an increasing convex function, applying the function to (
2.7
) gives

\sum_{i=1}^{n}\log(1+(\lambda_{i}(C^{-1}_{1}D_{1}\oplus\cdots\oplus C^{-1}_{k}D_{k}))^{p})\leq\sum_{i=1}^{n}\log(1+(\lambda_{i}(C^{-1}D))^{p}),

i.e.,

\sum_{i=1}^{n}\log(1+\lambda_{i}((C^{-1}_{1}D_{1})^{p}\oplus\cdots\oplus(C^{-1}_{k}D_{k})^{p}))\leq\sum_{i=1}^{n}\log(1+\lambda_{i}((C^{-1}D)^{p})).

The desired result follows by taking exponential on both sides.
∎

\displaystyle\det(D_{1}^{-2}+C_{1}^{-2})\cdots\det(D_{k}^{-2}+C_{k}^{-2})\cdot(\det D)^{2}.

Since (
2.8
) is false, (
1.5
) is also invalid.

Remark 2.11
.

Since (
1.4
) is not true, the vectors of eigenvalues cannot be replaced by the vectors of singular values in (
2.1
) from the proof in Theorem
2.6
. In other words,

s(C^{-1}_{1}D_{1}\oplus\cdots\oplus C^{-1}_{k}D_{k})\not\prec_{w\,\log}s(C^{-1}D),

where
C,D,C_{i},D_{i},i=1,\ldots,k
, are given as in Theorem
1.1
.

3.
Weak majorization complementary to Choi’s determinantal inequality

Let us first recall a result from
[
7
, Theorem 7.13]
.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
A\in\mathbb{C}_{n\times n}
be positive semidefinite and let
[A]
be the principal submatrix of
A
corresponding to some fixed rows and columns. Assuming that the inverses involved exist, we have

[A]^{-1}\leq[A^{-1}].

Theorem 3.2
.

Under the conditions as in Theorem
1.2
, we have

(3.1)

\displaystyle\lambda\left(\sum_{i=1}^{m}(A_{i}^{(1)})^{-1}\oplus\cdots\oplus\sum_{i=1}^{m}(A_{i}^{(k)})^{-1}\right)^{p}

\displaystyle\prec_{w}

\displaystyle\lambda\left(\sum_{i=1}^{m}A_{i}^{-1}\right)^{p},\quad p\geq 1.

Proof.

By (
2.3
), we have

(3.2)

\displaystyle\lambda\left(\sum_{i=1}^{m}(A_{i}^{-1})^{(1)}\oplus\cdots\oplus\sum_{i=1}^{m}(A_{i}^{-1})^{(k)}\right)\prec\lambda\left(\sum_{i=1}^{m}A_{i}^{-1}\right).

By Lemma
3.1
and Weyl’s monotonicity theorem
[
1
, p.63]
, we have

(3.3)

\displaystyle\lambda\left(\sum_{i=1}^{m}(A_{i}^{(1)})^{-1}\oplus\cdots\oplus\sum_{i=1}^{m}(A_{i}^{(k)})^{-1}\right)\leq\lambda\left(\sum_{i=1}^{m}(A_{i}^{-1})^{(1)}\oplus\cdots\oplus\sum_{i=1}^{m}(A_{i}^{-1})^{(k)}\right).

Now (
3.2
) and (
3.3
) lead to

\displaystyle\lambda\left(\sum_{i=1}^{m}(A_{i}^{(1)})^{-1}\oplus\cdots\oplus\sum_{i=1}^{m}(A_{i}^{(k)})^{-1}\right)\prec_{w}\lambda\left(\sum_{i=1}^{m}A_{i}^{-1}\right).

The desired result follows by the fact that
f(x)=x^{p},\,p>1,
is increasing and convex on
(0,+\infty)
.
∎

Remark 3.3
.

Choi’s determinantal inequality (
1.6
) is equivalent to the following:

\det\left(\sum_{i=1}^{m}(A_{i}^{(1)})^{-1}\oplus\cdots\oplus\sum_{i=1}^{m}(A_{i}^{(k)})^{-1}\right)^{p}\leq\det\left(\sum_{i=1}^{m}A_{i}^{-1}\right)^{p},\quad p>0.

Thus, Theorem
3.2
is a complement to Theorem
1.2
.

It is natural to ask whether (
3.1
) holds for
0<p<1
? By Theorem
3.2
and the proof of Theorem
2.1
, this question is equivalent to the following weak log majorization question:

Question 3.4
.

Under the conditions as in Theorem
1.2
,

(3.4)

\lambda\left(\sum_{i=1}^{m}(A_{i}^{(1)})^{-1}\oplus\cdots\oplus\sum_{i=1}^{m}(A_{i}^{(k)})^{-1}\right)\prec_{w\log}\lambda\left(\sum_{i=1}^{m}A_{i}^{-1}\right)?

When
n=2,k=2,n_{1}=n_{2}=1
, (
3.4
) holds by (
1.6
) and (
3.1
). The other cases are open.
We would like to point out that we performed computer experiments and the outcomes are consistent with the weak log majorization given in (
3.4
).

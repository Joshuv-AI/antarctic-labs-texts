Continua and persistence of periodic orbits in ensembles of oscillators Journal: Nonlinearity
R Ronge, M A Zaks, T Pereira

Abstract
Certain systems of coupled identical oscillators like the Kuramoto-Sakaguchi or the active rotator model possess the remarkable property of being Watanabe-Strogatz integrable. We prove that such systems, which couple via a global order parameter, feature a normally attracting invariant manifold that is foliated by periodic orbits. This allows us to study the asymptotic dynamics of general ensembles of identical oscillators by applying averaging theory. For the active rotator model, perturbations result in only finitely many persisting orbits, one of them giving rise to splay state dynamics. This sheds some light on the persistence and typical behavior of splay states previously observed.



Keywords
: Watanabe-Strogatz integrability, phase oscillators, active rotator, normal hyperbolicity

Introduction

Studies of synchronization phenomena in ensembles of self-sustained oscillators or excitable elements have found numerous applications, from physics
[
1
,
2
]
over engineering
[
3
,
4
]
and neurobiology
[

6
]
to social science
[

. For systems of

elements, an important class of phase models is of the form

\displaystyle\dot{\phi}_{j}=f(Z)\rme^{\rmi\phi_{j}}+g(Z)+\bar{f}(Z)\rme^{-\rmi\phi_{j}}

(1)

where
\phi_{j}\in\mathbb{S}^{1}:=\mathbb{R}/2\pi\mathbb{Z}

. Hence every element

couples to the rest of the ensemble via

[

]
as well as the active rotator model by Shinomoto and Kuramoto

belong to this class.

Systems of the form (

by Watanabe and Strogatz, showing that these systems possess

WS-integrability has lead to the understanding of multiple phenomena in ensembles of identically driven oscillators such as Chimera states
[

]
, classification of attractors
[
15
]
, and asymptotic stability of periodic two-cluster states
[
16
,
17

WS-integrable systems can also feature splay states, which are of special interest because of their specific spatio-temporal symmetry
[
18
,
19

and are potential candidates for attractors for systems of type (
1

Equation (

[
20
,
21
]
. Its Möbius group symmetry and subsequent partial integrability is the result of neglecting higher modes, which would otherwise break this symmetry. It remains an open problem as to how the WS-framework can be applied to general settings.

In this work, we focus on models of the type

\eqalign{\dot{\phi}_{j}=f(Z)\rme^{\rmi\phi_{j}}+g(Z)+\bar{f}(Z)\rme^{-\rmi\phi_{j}}+\epsilon h(\phi_{j})\\
h(\phi_{j})=\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}a_{n}\sin n\phi_{j}+b_{n}\cos n\phi_{j}}

(2)

where the symmetry of (
1
) is broken by introducing higher harmonics in the

starts with the second modes since any zeroth or first mode perturbations can be absorbed by

Our motivation comes from the classic model of coupled identical active rotators

In particular, this model is closely related to systems of coupled theta-neurons
[
22
,
23
]
. Here, when we speak of an

The classic active rotator model is a first order phase reduced description for general systems of coupled

24
]
. The question arises what happens to the continuous family of periodic orbits from
[
25

if we break the symmetry of (
3

3

generalized
active rotators

26

instead, for which the equations of motion read

\eqalign{\dot{\phi}_{j}=\omega-\sin\phi_{j}+\epsilon h(\phi_{j})+\frac{\kappa}{N}\sum_{k=1}^{N}\sin(\phi_{k}-\phi_{j})\cr h(\phi_{j})=\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}a_{n}\sin n\phi_{j}+b_{n}\cos n\phi_{j}}

(4)

2
Informal statements of main results

We first investigate the geometry of the continuum in section
4
which results in theorem
4.12
and proposition
4.13
. Below, we give an informal statement for the special case of the active rotator model (
3
), discussed in section
6
.

Theorem

(Informal statement for active rotators)
.

Consider a system of classic active rotators (
3
) with
\left|\omega\right|<1
. For
\kappa<\kappa_{0}=-\sqrt{1-\omega^{2}}
and sufficiently large
N
, the system possesses a continuous family of periodic orbits where the union over these orbits forms a
normally attracting invariant manifold
(NAIM). One of the orbits of this continuum features splay state dynamics.

The orbits of the continuum can be parameterized by
N-3
functionally independent cross-ratios
\boldsymbol{\lambda}
which are preserved under the flow of (
1
), see
[
13
]
. The splay state corresponds thereby to special value
\boldsymbol{\lambda}=\boldsymbol{\lambda^{*}}
, defined below. On introducing symmetry-breaking perturbations in the on-site dynamics in (
2
), the periodic orbits of the family generally vanish while the NAIM persists. Averaging the resulting perturbation term in the now time-dependent cross-ratios over an orbit of the unperturbed system results in an averaged system for which hyperbolic fixed points correspond to hyperbolic periodic orbits of the perturbed system. This leads to our second main result theorem
5.1
for which we again give an informal version below.

Theorem
.

The averaged dynamics of the cross-ratios possess a fixed point at
\boldsymbol{\lambda^{*}}
, corresponding to the splay state in the unperturbed system.

Whether
\boldsymbol{\lambda^{*}}
is hyperbolic depends on the specific setup of the model (
2
). However, we conjecture that splay state orbits are generically hyperbolic.

In section
6
, we apply our results to the active rotator models where we first
determine the critical coupling strength
\kappa_{0}
of (
3
) below which the NAIM-forming continuum arises and apply the averaging principle to the dynamics on the NAIM and conduct numerical experiments where we find that
\boldsymbol{\lambda^{*}}
is hyperbolic for the averaged dynamics so that the splay state is robust in (
4
).

Strategy:

We outline the main steps to prove our main theorems
4.12
and
5.1
.:

Step 1:
Since we are dealing with identically driven units, we can
assume the elements to be in (strict) cyclic order which is preserved by the flow of (
1
). In lemma
4.8
, we show that there exists a diffeomorphism from the space
\mathbb{T}^{N}_{\mathrm{ordered}}
of phases
\boldsymbol{\phi}\in\mathbb{T}^{N}
in strict cyclic order to the space of Möbius group parameters and the
N-3
conserved cross-ratios of (
1
) which allows to write down the dynamics of
\boldsymbol{\phi}(t)
in terms of these new coordinates.

Step 2:
We show that there exists an open set of cross-ratios, for which the equations of motion for the system (
1
) in WS-variables can be approximated in
C^{1}
-norm by truncating higher-order terms in
N
. We then proceed in theorem
4.12
by showing that there exists a family of periodic orbits for this truncated system, whose union possesses the desired NAIM structure by virtue of the persistence theorems for hyperbolic orbits
[
29
]
.

Step 3:
The dynamics on the NAIM describe the asymptotic dynamics of the perturbed system (
2
). For any smooth
h
, applying the method of averaging to the slowly-varying cross-ratio components of the vector field on the NAIM, we define an averaged perturbation function
\boldsymbol{F}_{h}
which governs the averaged dynamics in
\boldsymbol{\lambda}
. Hyperbolic fixed points of this system determine persistent periodic orbits for the perturbed system (
2
).

Step 4:
Using cyclic permutation properties of the cross-ratios, we show in theorem
5.1
for
N=4
that
\boldsymbol{F}_{h}
vanishes for smooth choices of
h
for the cross-ratios
\boldsymbol{\lambda^{*}}
that correspond to the splay state. Afterwards, we show that this statement holds true for general
N\geq 4
by summing over the group action of cyclic permutations.

3
Preliminaries

3.1
Normally attracting invariant manifolds

Our definition of a normally attracting invariant manifold (NAIM) is according to
[
30
]
.

Definition 3.1

(Normally attracting invariant manifold)
.

Let
r\geq 1
and
\dot{\boldsymbol{x}}=\boldsymbol{f}(\boldsymbol{x})
with
\boldsymbol{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
\boldsymbol{f}\in C^{r}
be a dynamical system with flow
\Phi:\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}^{n}
. A given
C^{r}
-submanifold
\mathcal{M}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
is then called an attracting invariant manifold of this system if it fulfills the following three criteria:

1.

\mathcal{M}
is invariant under the flow, i. e.,
\Phi^{t}(\mathcal{M})=\mathcal{M}

\forall t\in\mathbb{R}
.

2.

There exists a continuous splitting

T_{\mathcal{M}}\mathbb{R}^{n}=T\mathcal{M}\oplus\mathcal{N}

of the tangent bundle
T\mathbb{R}^{n}
, restricted to
\mathcal{M}
, into the tangent bundle
T\mathcal{M}
and a normal bundle
\mathcal{N}
with continuous projections
\pi_{\mathcal{M}}
and
\pi_{\mathcal{N}}
. This splitting is invariant under the linearized flow
\mathrm{D}\Phi^{t}=\mathrm{D}\Phi^{t}_{\mathcal{M}}\oplus\mathrm{D}\Phi^{t}_{\mathcal{N}}
.

3.

There exist real numbers
a
and
b
with
a<r\,b\leq 0
, and
C>0
such that the following exponential growth conditions hold on
T_{\mathcal{M}}\mathbb{R}^{n}
:

\displaystyle\forall t\leq 0,(\boldsymbol{x},\boldsymbol{\nu})\in T\mathcal{M}:

\displaystyle\left|\left|\mathrm{D}\Phi^{t}_{\mathcal{M}}(\boldsymbol{x})\cdot\boldsymbol{\nu}\right|\right|

\displaystyle\leq C\,\rme^{bt}\left|\left|\boldsymbol{\nu}\right|\right|

\displaystyle\forall t\geq 0,(\boldsymbol{x},\boldsymbol{\nu})\in\mathcal{N}:

\displaystyle\left|\left|\mathrm{D}\Phi^{t}_{\mathcal{N}}(\boldsymbol{x})\cdot\boldsymbol{\nu}\right|\right|

\displaystyle\leq C\,\rme^{at}\left|\left|\boldsymbol{\nu}\right|\right|.

A key feature of NAIMs is that they persist under small perturbations of
\boldsymbol{f}
, as is stated in the following theorem, cf. Theorem. 4.1
[
31
]
.

Theorem 3.2

(Persistence of NAIMs)
.

Let
\mathcal{M}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a compact attracting invariant manifold of the system
\dot{\boldsymbol{x}}=\boldsymbol{f}(\boldsymbol{x})
. Then, there exists an
\epsilon>0
such that for any vector field
\boldsymbol{\tilde{f}}
with
\left|\left|\boldsymbol{\tilde{f}}-\boldsymbol{f}\right|\right|_{C^{1}}\leq\epsilon
, there exists a unique invariant
C^{r}
-manifold
\tilde{\mathcal{M}}
for
\boldsymbol{\tilde{f}}
that is diffeomorphic to
\mathcal{M}
, normally attracting, and
\mathcal{O}\left(\left|\left|\boldsymbol{\tilde{f}}-\boldsymbol{f}\right|\right|_{C^{1}}\right)
-close to
\mathcal{M}
.

We close this section with the following remarks.

Remarks
.

1.

If not stated otherwise, we employ the Euclidean norm
\left|\left|\cdot\right|\right|
and its induced norm.

2.

The persistence theorem
3.2
for NAIMs also holds for manifolds with boundary as long as they are
overflowing invariant
. In our case, the NAIMs have a boundary which is generally not overflowing invariant. This problem can be overcome by modifying
\boldsymbol{f}
in a neighborhood of the boundary accordingly.

3.

We use Landau’s big-
\mathcal{O}
notation for order functions, i. e.,
f(\epsilon)=\mathcal{O}(g(\epsilon))
if there exist constants
K\geq 0
and
\epsilon_{0}\geq 0
such that
0\leq\left|f(\epsilon)\right|\leq K\left|g(\epsilon)\right|
for all
0\leq\epsilon\leq\epsilon_{0}
.

3.2
The averaging method

To investigate the asymptotic dynamics of (
2
), we employ averaging theory. This approach relies on Theorem 7.9 in
[
32
]
on averaging over periodic orbits, stated below.

Theorem 3.3
.

Consider the system

\eqalign{\dot{\boldsymbol{x}}=\epsilon\;\!\boldsymbol{F}(\boldsymbol{x},\psi)+\epsilon^{2}\boldsymbol{F}_{2}(\boldsymbol{x},\psi,\epsilon)\cr\dot{\psi}=\Omega(\boldsymbol{x})+\epsilon\,G(\boldsymbol{x},\psi,\epsilon)}

(5)

where
\boldsymbol{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
\psi\in\mathbb{S}^{1}
and assume that the function
\Omega(\boldsymbol{x})
is bounded away from zero, i. e.,
\Omega(\boldsymbol{x})>c>0
for some number
c
and all
\boldsymbol{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
. If the averaged system

\dot{\boldsymbol{y}}=\epsilon\;\!\boldsymbol{\hat{F}}(\boldsymbol{y})

with

\boldsymbol{\hat{F}}(\boldsymbol{y}):=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\boldsymbol{F}(\boldsymbol{y},\psi)\,\mathrm{d}\psi

possesses a hyperbolic fixed point
\boldsymbol{y}_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
\epsilon>0
is sufficiently small, (
5
) possesses a periodic solution
t\mapsto\left(\boldsymbol{x}(t),\psi(t)\right)
of the same stability type as
\boldsymbol{y}_{0}
.

3.3
Watanabe-Strogatz integrability

Watanabe and Strogatz showed that a class of systems of
N
identically driven phase variables possess
N-3
conserved quantities
[
12
]
. Later on, this finding was explained in a broader geometrical context in terms of Möbius transformations
[
13
]
. More specific, WS-integrable systems can be brought to the form

\dot{\phi}_{j}=f\rme^{\rmi\phi_{j}}+g+\bar{f}\rme^{-\rmi\phi_{j}}

(6)

with smooth functions
f:\mathbb{T}^{N}\to\mathbb{C}
and
g:\mathbb{T}^{N}\to\mathbb{R}
where
\mathbb{T}^{N}:=(\mathbb{S}^{1})^{N}
denotes the
N
-dimensional torus and a bar denotes the complex conjugate. For short, we write
\boldsymbol{\phi}:=(\phi_{1},\dots,\phi_{N})\in\mathbb{T}^{N}
.

The dynamics of the
\phi_{j}(t)
in (
6
) can be written as

\displaystyle\rme^{\rmi\phi_{j}(t)}=G_{\alpha(t),\psi(t)}\left(\rme^{\rmi\theta_{j}}\right),\quad j=1,\dots,N

(7)

where

G_{\alpha,\psi}\left(z\right):=\frac{\alpha+\rme^{\rmi\psi}z}{1+\bar{\alpha}\rme^{\rmi\psi}z}

for
z\in\mathbb{C}
with parameters
\psi\in\mathbb{S}^{1}
and
\alpha\in\mathbb{D}:=\left\{z\in\mathbb{C}\;;\,\left|z\right|<1\right\}
.
G_{\alpha,\psi}
is an element of the Möbius group

\mathcal{G}:=\left\{G_{\alpha,\psi}:\mathbb{D}\to\mathbb{D}\;;\,(\alpha,\psi)\in\mathbb{D}\times\mathbb{S}^{1}\right\}.

where
\mathbb{D}:=\left\{z\in\mathbb{C}\;;\,\left|z\right|<1\right\}
denotes the complex unit disk. Generally, the Möbius group is the group of biholomorphic automorphisms of the augmented complex plane
\hat{\mathbb{C}}:=\mathbb{C}\cup\{\infty\}

[
28
]
. In the context of WS-theory, one considers the subgroup
\mathcal{G}
of biholomorphic automorphisms of
\mathbb{D}
. By analytic continuation, these transformations map the boundary
\partial\mathbb{D}
bijectively onto itself. In accordance to the literature on WS-theory we call
\mathcal{G}

the
Möbius group.

Equation (
7
) means that
\mathcal{G}
acts diagonally on
\mathbb{T}^{N}
. Setting in accordance to the literature for any
\boldsymbol{\theta}=(\theta_{1},\dots,\theta_{N})\in\mathbb{T}^{N}

\rme^{\rmi\boldsymbol{\theta}}:=\left(\rme^{\rmi\theta_{1}},\dots,\rme^{\rmi\theta_{N}}\right)

we write in a slight abuse of notation

G_{\alpha,\psi}\left(\rme^{\rmi\boldsymbol{\theta}}\right):=\left(G_{\alpha,\psi}\left(\rme^{\rmi\theta_{1}}\right),\dots,G_{\alpha,\psi}\left(\rme^{\rmi\theta_{N}}\right)\right).

(8)

The time evolution of the group parameters
\alpha
and
\psi
is then given by

\eqalign{\dot{\alpha}=\rmi\left(f\alpha^{2}+g\alpha+\bar{f}\right)\\
\dot{\psi}=f\alpha+g+\bar{f}\bar{\alpha}}

(9)

where
f
and
g
now depend implicitly on
\alpha
,
\psi
, and
\boldsymbol{\theta}
by virtue of (
7
).

4
NAIMs for WS-integrable systems

The aim of this section is to establish a way in which the dynamics of a systems of identical phase variables
\boldsymbol{\phi}
can be written in terms of the Möbius group parameters and the cross-ratios. The main result of this section is thus lemma
4.8
on the existence of a diffeomorphism between the set of all
\boldsymbol{\phi}\in\mathbb{T}^{N}
in cyclic order and the space
\mathbb{D}\times\mathbb{S}^{1}\times V
where
V
is the
(N-3)
-dimensional space of conserved quantities.

4.1
Watanabe-Strogatz variables

Since all units are identically driven, the order of phases on
\mathbb{S}^{1}
is preserved under the flow of (
1
). We restrict our attention without loss of generality to the case where the
\phi_{j}
are in (strict) cyclic order. This gives rise to the following definition.

Definition 4.1
.

For fixed
N
, the space
\mathbb{T}^{N}_{\mathrm{ordered}}\subset\mathbb{T}^{N}
is defined as

\mathbb{T}^{N}_{\mathrm{ordered}}:=\left\{\boldsymbol{\theta}\in\mathbb{T}^{N}\;;\,\theta_{1}<\dots<\theta_{N}<\theta_{1}+2\pi\right\}.

The (diagonal) group action (
8
) of the Möbius group
\mathcal{G}
induces the following equivalence relation on
\mathbb{T}^{N}_{\mathrm{ordered}}
.

Definition 4.2
.

Any two points
\boldsymbol{\vartheta},\boldsymbol{\theta}\in\mathbb{T}^{N}_{\mathrm{ordered}}
are equivalent if and only if there exists a
G_{\alpha,\psi}\in\mathcal{G}
such that
\rme^{\rmi\boldsymbol{\vartheta}}=G_{\alpha,\psi}\left(\rme^{\rmi\boldsymbol{\theta}}\right)
in which case we write
\boldsymbol{\vartheta}\sim\boldsymbol{\theta}
.

That this is an equivalence relation is established below.

Proposition 4.3
.

The relation
\sim
is an equivalence relation on
\mathbb{T}^{N}_{\mathrm{ordered}}
.

Proof.

This follows immediately from the group properties of
\mathcal{G}

[
28
]
: Consider any three points
\boldsymbol{\vartheta}\sim\boldsymbol{\theta}\sim\boldsymbol{\phi}\in\mathbb{T}^{N}_{\mathrm{ordered}}
. Then, (i) the identity map
G_{0,0}\in\mathcal{G}
guarantees that
\boldsymbol{\theta}\sim\boldsymbol{\theta}
, (ii) there exists a
G_{\alpha,\psi}\in\mathcal{G}
such that
\rme^{\rmi\boldsymbol{\vartheta}}=G_{\alpha,\psi}\left(\rme^{\rmi\boldsymbol{\theta}}\right)
. The inverse
G_{\alpha,\psi}^{-1}\in\mathcal{G}
fulfills
\rme^{\rmi\boldsymbol{\theta}}=G_{\alpha,\psi}^{-1}\left(\rme^{\rmi\boldsymbol{\vartheta}}\right)
and
\boldsymbol{\theta}\sim\boldsymbol{\vartheta}
. (iii) there exists a
G_{\beta,\chi}\in\mathcal{G}
with
\rme^{\rmi\boldsymbol{\theta}}=G_{\beta,\chi}\left(\rme^{\rmi\boldsymbol{\phi}}\right)
and
\rme^{\rmi\boldsymbol{\vartheta}}=G_{\alpha,\psi}\circ G_{\beta,\chi}\left(\rme^{\rmi\boldsymbol{\phi}}\right)
where
G_{\alpha,\psi}\circ G_{\beta,\chi}\in\mathcal{G}
so that
\boldsymbol{\vartheta}\sim\boldsymbol{\phi}
.
∎

The resulting partition of
\mathbb{T}^{N}_{\mathrm{ordered}}
in equivalence classes is invariant under the flow of (
6
) according to (
7
). We write

[\boldsymbol{\theta}]:=\left\{\boldsymbol{\vartheta}\in\mathbb{T}^{N}_{\mathrm{ordered}}\;;\,\exists G_{\alpha,\psi}\in\mathcal{G}:\rme^{\rmi\boldsymbol{\vartheta}}=G_{\alpha,\psi}\left(\rme^{\rmi\boldsymbol{\theta}}\right)\right\}

for its equivalence classes.

The real-valued so-called
cross-ratios

\Lambda_{p,q,r,s}(\boldsymbol{\theta}):=\frac{\big(\rme^{\rmi\theta_{p}}-\rme^{\rmi\theta_{s}}\big)\big(\rme^{\rmi\theta_{q}}-\rme^{\rmi\theta_{r}}\big)}{\big(\rme^{\rmi\theta_{q}}-\rme^{\rmi\theta_{s}}\big)\big(\rme^{\rmi\theta_{p}}-\rme^{\rmi\theta_{r}}\big)}

with
p,q,r,s=1,\dots,N
are invariant under arbitrary Möbius transformations
[
28
]
and are thus in particular conserved under the flow of (
6
). It was shown in
[
13
]
, that all cross-ratios can be written in terms of
N-3
functionally independent ones. To choose such a set, we make the following definition.

Definition 4.4
.

Let the set
V\subset\mathbb{R}^{N-3}
be defined as

V:=\left\{\boldsymbol{\lambda}\in(0,1)^{N-3}\;;\,1>\lambda_{1}>\dots>\lambda_{N-3}>0\right\}.

The cross-ratio function
\boldsymbol{\Lambda}:\mathbb{T}^{N}_{\mathrm{ordered}}\to V
is defined by

\eqalign{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}(\boldsymbol{\theta})&:=\left(\Lambda_{1}(\boldsymbol{\theta}),\dots,\Lambda_{N-3}(\boldsymbol{\theta})\right)\\
\Lambda_{k}(\boldsymbol{\theta})&:=\Lambda_{1,2,3,k+3}(\boldsymbol{\theta})=\frac{\big(\rme^{\rmi\theta_{1}}-\rme^{\rmi\theta_{k+3}}\big)\big(\rme^{\rmi\theta_{2}}-\rme^{\rmi\theta_{3}}\big)}{\big(\rme^{\rmi\theta_{2}}-\rme^{\rmi\theta_{k+3}}\big)\big(\rme^{\rmi\theta_{1}}-\rme^{\rmi\theta_{3}}\big)}}

(10)

with
k=1,\dots,N-3
.

The
N-3
components
\Lambda_{k}
are functionally independent. We note some important properties for the spaces
\mathbb{T}^{N}_{\mathrm{ordered}}
and
V
as well as the cross-ratio function
\boldsymbol{\Lambda}
below.

Lemma 4.5
.

The following two assertions hold:

1.

The set
\mathbb{T}^{N}_{\mathrm{ordered}}
is invariant under the action of
\mathcal{G}
.

2.

The cross-ratio (
10
) defines a smooth function
\boldsymbol{\Lambda}:\mathbb{T}^{N}_{\mathrm{ordered}}\to V
.

Proof.

The first assertion follows from the fact that all Möbius transformations
G_{\alpha,\psi}\in\mathcal{G}
are orientation preserving
[
28
]
. Let
\boldsymbol{\theta}\in\mathbb{T}^{N}_{\mathrm{ordered}}
and
\rme^{\rmi\boldsymbol{\vartheta}}=G_{\alpha,\psi}\left(\rme^{\rmi\boldsymbol{\theta}}\right)
. By preservation of orientation, we have
\boldsymbol{\vartheta}\in\mathbb{T}^{N}_{\mathrm{ordered}}
.

The second assertion follows from the fact that all derivatives of
\Lambda_{k}
exist because all
\theta_{j}
are distinct and that

\displaystyle\mathrm{D}_{\theta_{k+3}}\Lambda_{k}(\boldsymbol{\theta})=\frac{1}{2}\frac{\sin\frac{\theta_{1}-\theta_{2}}{2}\sin\frac{\theta_{2}-\theta_{3}}{2}}{\sin\frac{\theta_{1}-\theta_{3}}{2}\sin^{2}\frac{\theta_{2}-\theta_{k+3}}{2}}<0,\qquad

\displaystyle k=1,\dots,N-3

so that each
\Lambda_{k}
is strictly monotonically decreasing in
\theta_{k+3}
on
\mathbb{T}^{N}_{\mathrm{ordered}}
. In particular, we find
\Lambda_{k}(\boldsymbol{\theta})>\Lambda_{k+1}(\boldsymbol{\theta})
on
\mathbb{T}^{N}_{\mathrm{ordered}}
. Additionally, we find that
\lim_{\theta_{k+3}\downarrow\theta_{3}}\Lambda_{k}(\boldsymbol{\theta})=1
and
\lim_{\theta_{k+3}\uparrow\theta_{1}+2\pi}\Lambda_{k}(\boldsymbol{\theta})=0
so that
\boldsymbol{\Lambda}
indeed maps
\mathbb{T}^{N}_{\mathrm{ordered}}
to
V
.
∎

As a next step, we define a coordinate system on
\mathbb{T}^{N}_{\mathrm{ordered}}
in terms of
\alpha
,
\psi
and the cross-ratios
\boldsymbol{\lambda}\in V
. We start with the following lemma.

Lemma 4.6
.

For any two
\boldsymbol{\vartheta},\boldsymbol{\theta}\in\mathbb{T}^{N}_{\mathrm{ordered}}
, we have

\boldsymbol{\vartheta}\sim\boldsymbol{\theta}\Leftrightarrow\boldsymbol{\Lambda}(\boldsymbol{\vartheta})=\boldsymbol{\Lambda}(\boldsymbol{\theta})

and in particular, we have

[\boldsymbol{\theta}]=\left\{\boldsymbol{\vartheta}\in\mathbb{T}^{N}_{\mathrm{ordered}}\;;\,\boldsymbol{\Lambda}(\boldsymbol{\vartheta})=\boldsymbol{\Lambda}(\boldsymbol{\theta})\right\},

i. e., the equivalence classes
[\boldsymbol{\theta}]
are exactly the level sets of the cross-ratio function
\boldsymbol{\Lambda}
.

Proof.

(
\Rightarrow
): Since
\boldsymbol{\vartheta}\sim\boldsymbol{\theta}
, there exists a
G_{\alpha,\psi}\in\mathcal{G}
with
\rme^{\rmi\boldsymbol{\vartheta}}=G_{\alpha,\psi}\left(\rme^{\rmi\boldsymbol{\theta}}\right)
. Then
\boldsymbol{\Lambda}(\boldsymbol{\vartheta})=\boldsymbol{\Lambda}(\boldsymbol{\theta})
follows the invariance of cross-ratios under arbitrary Möbius transformations.

(
\Leftarrow
): Let
\boldsymbol{\Lambda}(\boldsymbol{\vartheta})=\boldsymbol{\Lambda}(\boldsymbol{\theta})
for some
\boldsymbol{\vartheta},\boldsymbol{\theta}\in\mathbb{T}^{N}_{\mathrm{ordered}}
. We construct a suitable
G_{\alpha,\psi}\in\mathcal{G}
for which
G_{\alpha,\psi}\left(\rme^{\rmi\boldsymbol{\theta}}\right)=\rme^{\rmi\boldsymbol{\vartheta}}
holds. Consider the two general Möbius transformations
\mu_{\boldsymbol{\theta}}:\hat{\mathbb{C}}\to\hat{\mathbb{C}}
and
\mu_{\boldsymbol{\vartheta}}:\hat{\mathbb{C}}\to\hat{\mathbb{C}}
with

\displaystyle\mu_{\boldsymbol{\theta}}(z)

\displaystyle:=\frac{\Big(z-\rme^{\rmi\theta_{1}}\Big)\Big(\rme^{\rmi\theta_{2}}-\rme^{\rmi\theta_{3}}\Big)}{\Big(z-\rme^{\rmi\theta_{2}}\Big)\Big(\rme^{\rmi\theta_{1}}-\rme^{\rmi\theta_{3}}\Big)},\qquad\mu_{\boldsymbol{\vartheta}}(z)

\displaystyle:=\frac{\Big(z-\rme^{\rmi\vartheta_{1}}\Big)\Big(\rme^{\rmi\vartheta_{2}}-\rme^{\rmi\vartheta_{3}}\Big)}{\Big(z-\rme^{\rmi\vartheta_{2}}\Big)\Big(\rme^{\rmi\vartheta_{1}}-\rme^{\rmi\vartheta_{3}}\Big)}.

These maps fulfill

\displaystyle\mu_{\boldsymbol{\theta}}\left(\rme^{\rmi\theta_{1}}\right)=\mu_{\boldsymbol{\vartheta}}\left(\rme^{\rmi\vartheta_{1}}\right)=0,\qquad\mu_{\boldsymbol{\theta}}\left(\rme^{\rmi\theta_{2}}\right)=\mu_{\boldsymbol{\vartheta}}\left(\rme^{\rmi\vartheta_{2}}\right)=\infty,\qquad\mu_{\boldsymbol{\theta}}\left(\rme^{\rmi\theta_{3}}\right)=\mu_{\boldsymbol{\vartheta}}\left(\rme^{\rmi\vartheta_{3}}\right)=1

so that the function
G=\mu_{\boldsymbol{\vartheta}}^{-1}\circ\mu_{\boldsymbol{\theta}}
maps
\rme^{\rmi\theta_{k}}
to
\rme^{\rmi\vartheta_{k}}
for
k=1,2,3
. In particular,
G
is a bijective conformal map from
\partial\mathbb{D}
to
\partial\mathbb{D}
. It is also orientation preserving since
\rme^{\rmi\theta_{1}},\rme^{\rmi\theta_{2}},\rme^{\rmi\theta_{3}}\in\partial\mathbb{D}
and
\rme^{\rmi\vartheta_{1}},\rme^{\rmi\vartheta_{2}},\rme^{\rmi\vartheta_{3}}\in\partial\mathbb{D}
are in the same cyclic order by assumption.
G
is therefore an element of
\mathcal{G}
. Our assumption
\Lambda_{k}(\boldsymbol{\theta})=\Lambda_{k}(\boldsymbol{\vartheta})
implies that

\mu_{\boldsymbol{\theta}}\big(\rme^{\rmi\theta_{k+3}}\big)=\Lambda_{k}(\boldsymbol{\theta})=\Lambda_{k}(\boldsymbol{\vartheta})=\mu_{\boldsymbol{\vartheta}}\big(\rme^{\rmi\vartheta_{k+3}}\big).

and
G\left(\rme^{\rmi\theta_{k}}\right)=\rme^{\rmi\vartheta_{k}}
holds also for all
k=4,\dots,N
. We conclude that
G\left(\rme^{\rmi\boldsymbol{\theta}}\right)=\rme^{\rmi\boldsymbol{\vartheta}}
which finishes the proof.
∎

Lemma
4.6
implies that we can parameterize the partition of
\mathbb{T}^{N}_{\mathrm{ordered}}
in equivalence classes

via the cross-ratios
\boldsymbol{\lambda}\in V

[\boldsymbol{\theta}]\equiv\mathcal{L}_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}(\boldsymbol{\Lambda}):=\left\{\boldsymbol{\vartheta}\in\mathbb{T}^{N}_{\mathrm{ordered}}\;;\,\boldsymbol{\Lambda}(\boldsymbol{\vartheta})=\boldsymbol{\lambda}\right\}.

where
\boldsymbol{\lambda}=\boldsymbol{\Lambda}(\boldsymbol{\theta})
. Each
\boldsymbol{\theta}\in\mathbb{T}^{N}_{\mathrm{ordered}}
is an element of the level set
\mathcal{L}_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}(\boldsymbol{\Lambda})
with
\boldsymbol{\lambda}=\boldsymbol{\Lambda}(\boldsymbol{\theta})
. We have to introduce a suitable coordinate system on each set
\mathcal{L}_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}(\boldsymbol{\Lambda})

The parameters
\alpha
and

-\rmi\,\mathrm{Log\;}\frac{\rme^{\frac{2\pi\rmi}{N}}\left(\lambda_{k-3}+\lambda_{k-3}\,\rme^{\frac{2\pi\rmi}{N}}-1\right)}{-\lambda_{k-3}+(1-\lambda_{k-3})\,\rme^{\frac{2\pi\rmi}{N}}}&for $k=4,\dots,N$.}}

its WS-coordinates
\left(\alpha,\psi,\boldsymbol{\lambda}\right)

via the relation

\rme^{\rmi\boldsymbol{\theta}}=G_{\alpha,\psi}\left(\rme^{\rmi\boldsymbol{\Theta}(\boldsymbol{\lambda})}\right)

where

determines in
which

\mathcal{L}_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}(\boldsymbol{\Lambda})

\alpha

Lemma 4.7
.

The map

, i. e.,

\boldsymbol{\Theta}(\boldsymbol{\lambda})\in\mathcal{L}_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}(\boldsymbol{\Lambda})

\boldsymbol{\Theta}

) solves the equation

for

We state the final result of this section on the existence of the well-defined WS-coordinate system on
\mathbb{T}^{N}_{\mathrm{ordered}}
in the following proposition.

Proposition 4.8
.

The map
\boldsymbol{m}:\mathbb{D}\times\mathbb{S}^{1}\times V\to\mathbb{T}^{N}_{\mathrm{ordered}}
with

\boldsymbol{m}(\alpha,\psi,\boldsymbol{\lambda}):=-\rmi\,\mathrm{Log\;}G_{\alpha,\psi}\left(\rme^{\rmi\boldsymbol{\Theta}(\boldsymbol{\lambda})}\right)=-\rmi\,\mathrm{Log\;}\frac{\alpha+\rme^{\rmi\psi}\rme^{\rmi\boldsymbol{\Theta}(\boldsymbol{\lambda})}}{1+\bar{\alpha}\,\rme^{\rmi\psi}\rme^{\rmi\boldsymbol{\Theta}(\boldsymbol{\lambda})}}

is a smooth diffeomorphism.

Proof.

As a composition of the smooth maps
(\alpha,\psi,\boldsymbol{\theta})\mapsto G_{\alpha,\psi}(\rme^{\rmi\boldsymbol{\theta}})
,
\boldsymbol{\Theta}
, and the
\mathrm{Log\;}
-function,
\boldsymbol{m}
is smooth. We show that
\boldsymbol{m}
is also bijective and
\mathrm{D}\boldsymbol{m}
has full rank.

Injectivity of
\boldsymbol{m}
: Suppose that for
(\alpha,\psi,\boldsymbol{\lambda}),(\alpha^{\prime},\psi^{\prime},\boldsymbol{\lambda}^{\prime})\in\mathbb{D}\times\mathbb{S}^{1}\times V
the equality
\boldsymbol{m}(\alpha,\psi,\boldsymbol{\lambda})=\boldsymbol{m}(\alpha^{\prime},\psi^{\prime},\boldsymbol{\lambda}^{\prime})
holds. We find

\displaystyle-\rmi\,\mathrm{Log\;}G_{\alpha,\psi}\left(\rme^{\rmi\boldsymbol{\Theta}(\boldsymbol{\lambda})}\right)=-\rmi\,\mathrm{Log\;}G_{\alpha^{\prime},\psi^{\prime}}\left(\rme^{\rmi\boldsymbol{\Theta}(\boldsymbol{\lambda}^{\prime})}\right)

\displaystyle\Rightarrow\rme^{\rmi\boldsymbol{\Theta}(\boldsymbol{\lambda})}=G_{\alpha,\psi}^{-1}\circ G_{\alpha^{\prime},\psi^{\prime}}\left(\rme^{\rmi\boldsymbol{\Theta}(\boldsymbol{\lambda}^{\prime})}\right).

By lemma
4.6
, this implies

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{\Theta}(\boldsymbol{\lambda})\in[\boldsymbol{\Theta}(\boldsymbol{\lambda}^{\prime})]=\mathcal{L}_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}^{\prime}}(\boldsymbol{\Lambda})\quad\Rightarrow\quad\boldsymbol{\lambda}=\boldsymbol{\Lambda}\circ\boldsymbol{\Theta}(\boldsymbol{\lambda})=\boldsymbol{\lambda}^{\prime}.

Further,
\boldsymbol{\Theta}(\boldsymbol{\lambda})
always possesses at least three distinct coordinates (e.g.
\Theta_{1}
,
\Theta_{2}
, and
\Theta_{3}
). But since a Möbius map is uniquely defined by the images of three distinct points, we have
G_{\alpha,\psi}\left(\rme^{\rmi\boldsymbol{\Theta}(\boldsymbol{\lambda})}\right)=G_{\alpha^{\prime},\psi^{\prime}}\left(\rme^{\rmi\boldsymbol{\Theta}(\boldsymbol{\lambda})}\right)\Leftrightarrow G_{\alpha,\psi}=G_{\alpha^{\prime},\psi^{\prime}}\Leftrightarrow(\alpha,\psi)=(\alpha^{\prime},\psi^{\prime})
and
(\alpha,\psi,\boldsymbol{\lambda})=(\alpha^{\prime},\psi^{\prime},\boldsymbol{\lambda}^{\prime})
.

Surjectivity of
\boldsymbol{m}
: For any
\boldsymbol{\theta}\in\mathbb{T}^{N}_{\mathrm{ordered}}
, the cross-ratios
\boldsymbol{\lambda}=\boldsymbol{\Lambda}(\boldsymbol{\theta})\in V
are well defined. Since
\boldsymbol{\Theta}(\boldsymbol{\lambda})
and
\boldsymbol{\theta}
are both elements of
\mathcal{L}_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}(\boldsymbol{\Lambda})=[\boldsymbol{\Theta}(\boldsymbol{\lambda})]=[\boldsymbol{\theta}]
, there exists a
G_{\alpha,\psi}\in\mathcal{G}
with
G_{\alpha,\psi}\left(\rme^{\rmi\boldsymbol{\Theta}(\boldsymbol{\lambda})}\right)=\rme^{\rmi\boldsymbol{\theta}}
. But this implies the existence of an
(\alpha,\psi,\boldsymbol{\lambda})\in\mathbb{D}\times\mathbb{S}^{1}\times V
which is mapped by
\boldsymbol{m}
to
\boldsymbol{\theta}
.

At last, we show that the derivative
\mathrm{D}\boldsymbol{m}
has full rank everywhere. For each
x\in\{\alpha,\bar{\alpha},\psi,\lambda_{1},\dots,\lambda_{N-3}\}
, let
\mathrm{D}_{x}\boldsymbol{m}=(\mathrm{D}_{x}\boldsymbol{m}_{1},\dots,\mathrm{D}_{x}\boldsymbol{m}_{N})^{T}
denote the respective column of
\mathrm{D}\boldsymbol{m}=\left[\mathrm{D}_{\alpha}\boldsymbol{m},\mathrm{D}_{\bar{\alpha}}\boldsymbol{m},\mathrm{D}_{\psi}\boldsymbol{m},\mathrm{D}_{\lambda_{1}}\boldsymbol{m},\dots,\mathrm{D}_{\lambda_{N-3}}\boldsymbol{m}\right]
.
From the identity

\boldsymbol{\Lambda}(\boldsymbol{m}(\alpha,\psi,\boldsymbol{\lambda}))=\boldsymbol{\lambda}

we note first that
\mathrm{D}_{\boldsymbol{\theta}}\boldsymbol{\Lambda}\cdot\mathrm{D}_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}\boldsymbol{m}=\mathrm{id}_{N-3}
or more explicitly

\pmatrix{\mathrm{D}_{\theta_{1}}\Lambda_{1}&\dots&\mathrm{D}_{\theta_{N}}\Lambda_{1}\cr\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\cr\mathrm{D}_{\theta_{1}}\Lambda_{N-3}&\dots&\mathrm{D}_{\theta_{N}}\Lambda_{N-3}}\cdot\pmatrix{\mathrm{D}_{\lambda_{1}}m_{1}&\dots&\mathrm{D}_{\lambda_{N-3}}m_{1}\cr\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\cr\mathrm{D}_{\lambda_{1}}m_{N}&\dots&\mathrm{D}_{\lambda_{N-3}}m_{N}}=\mathrm{id}_{N-3}

(14)

where
\mathrm{id}_{N-3}
is the
(N-3)
-dimensional identity matrix. Thus,
\mathrm{D}_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}\boldsymbol{m}
has a left inverse
\mathrm{D}_{\boldsymbol{\theta}}\boldsymbol{\Lambda}
and therefore has in particular full column rank.

Secondly, we have

\displaystyle\mathrm{D}_{\boldsymbol{\theta}}\boldsymbol{\Lambda}\cdot\mathrm{D}_{\alpha}\boldsymbol{m}=\mathrm{D}_{\boldsymbol{\theta}}\boldsymbol{\Lambda}\cdot\mathrm{D}_{\bar{\alpha}}\boldsymbol{m}=\mathrm{D}_{\boldsymbol{\theta}}\boldsymbol{\Lambda}\cdot\mathrm{D}_{\psi}\boldsymbol{m}=0

(15)

so the column vectors
\mathrm{D}_{\alpha}\boldsymbol{m}
,
\mathrm{D}_{\bar{\alpha}}\boldsymbol{m}
, and
\mathrm{D}_{\psi}\boldsymbol{m}
are orthogonal to the column vectors
\mathrm{D}_{\theta_{j}}\boldsymbol{\Lambda}
and thus linearly independent of the column vectors
\mathrm{D}_{\lambda_{k}}\boldsymbol{m}
: if any linear combination

\boldsymbol{y}=a\,\mathrm{D}_{\alpha}\boldsymbol{m}+b\,\mathrm{D}_{\bar{\alpha}}\boldsymbol{m}+c\,\mathrm{D}_{\psi}\boldsymbol{m}

was in the span of the
\mathrm{D}_{\lambda_{k}}\boldsymbol{m}
, i. e., if we would have

\boldsymbol{y}=\sum_{j=1}^{N-3}c_{j}\mathrm{D}_{\lambda_{j}}\boldsymbol{m}

with
c_{k}\neq 0
for some
k\in\{1,\dots,N-3\}
, we would find

\displaystyle 0=\mathrm{D}_{\boldsymbol{\theta}}\Lambda_{k}\cdot\boldsymbol{y}=\sum_{j=1}^{N-3}c_{j}\mathrm{D}_{\boldsymbol{\theta}}\Lambda_{k}\cdot\mathrm{D}_{\lambda_{j}}\boldsymbol{m}=c_{k}

where the first equality follows from (
15
) and the last from (
14
). This contradicts the assumption that
c_{k}\neq 0
.

The three vectors
\mathrm{D}_{\alpha}\boldsymbol{m}
,
\mathrm{D}_{\bar{\alpha}}\boldsymbol{m}
, and
\mathrm{D}_{\psi}\boldsymbol{m}
are linearly independent. To see this, consider the
(N-3)\times 3
matrix
A=\left[\mathrm{D}_{\alpha}\boldsymbol{m},\mathrm{D}_{\bar{\alpha}}\boldsymbol{m},\mathrm{D}_{\psi}\boldsymbol{m}\right]
with
\mathrm{rank\;}A\leq 3
. For any matrix, its rank is equal to the largest order square submatrix with nonzero determinant
[
33
]
. Let the submatrix
\hat{A}
consist of the first three components of each column of
A
. It is of the form

\displaystyle\hat{A}=\pmatrix{\frac{\displaystyle\rmi}{\displaystyle\rme^{\rmi\psi}-\alpha}&\frac{\displaystyle\rmi\rme^{\rmi\psi}}{\displaystyle\bar{\alpha}\rme^{\rmi\psi}-1}&\frac{\displaystyle\rme^{\rmi\psi}(\left|\alpha\right|^{2}-1)}{\displaystyle(\rme^{\rmi\psi}-\alpha)(\bar{\alpha}\rme^{\rmi\psi}-1)}\cr\frac{\displaystyle\rmi}{\displaystyle\rme^{\rmi\psi}\zeta_{N}-\alpha}&\frac{\displaystyle\rmi\rme^{\rmi\psi}\zeta_{N}}{\displaystyle\bar{\alpha}\rme^{\rmi\psi}\zeta_{N}-1}&\frac{\displaystyle\rme^{\rmi\psi}\zeta_{N}(\left|\alpha\right|^{2}-1)}{\displaystyle(\rme^{\rmi\psi}\zeta_{N}-\alpha)(\bar{\alpha}\rme^{\rmi\psi}\zeta_{N}-1)}\cr\frac{\displaystyle\rmi}{\displaystyle\rme^{\rmi\psi}\zeta_{N}^{2}-\alpha}&\frac{\displaystyle\rmi\rme^{\rmi\psi}\zeta_{N}^{2}}{\displaystyle\bar{\alpha}\rme^{\rmi\psi}\zeta_{N}^{2}-1}&\frac{\displaystyle\rme^{\rmi\psi}\zeta_{N}^{2}(\left|\alpha\right|^{2}-1)}{\displaystyle(\rme^{\rmi\psi}\zeta_{N}^{2}-\alpha)(\bar{\alpha}\rme^{\rmi\psi}\zeta_{N}^{2}-1)}},

where we set
\zeta_{N}=\exp(2\pi\rmi/N)
and has the determinant

\det\hat{A}=\frac{X}{Y_{1}Y_{2}}

where

\displaystyle X=-(1-\zeta_{N})^{3}(1+\zeta_{N})(1-\left|\alpha\right|^{2})\rme^{3\rmi\psi}\zeta_{N}

\displaystyle Y_{1}=(\alpha-\rme^{\rmi\psi})(\alpha-\rme^{\rmi\psi}\zeta_{N})(\alpha-\rme^{\rmi\psi}\zeta_{N}^{2})

\displaystyle Y_{2}=(1-\bar{\alpha}\rme^{\rmi\psi})(1-\bar{\alpha}\rme^{\rmi\psi}\zeta_{N})(1-\bar{\alpha}\rme^{\rmi\psi}\zeta_{N}^{2}).

This determinant is nonvanishing: By the triangle inequality, we have

\displaystyle\left|Y_{1}\right|\leq(1+\left|\alpha\right|)^{3}\qquad\textrm{and}\qquad\left|Y_{2}\right|\leq(1+\left|\alpha\right|)^{3}

while for
N\geq 3
and with
\left|1\pm\rme^{\rmi\theta}\right|=\sqrt{2}\sqrt{1\pm\cos\theta}
and
1-\cos\theta\geq\theta^{2}/8
for all
\theta\in\left[-\frac{2\pi}{3},\frac{2\pi}{3}\right]
, we have

\displaystyle\left|X\right|=\left|1-\rme^{\frac{2\pi\rmi}{N}}\right|^{3}\left|1+\rme^{\frac{2\pi\rmi}{N}}\right|\left(1-\left|\alpha\right|^{2}\right)\geq\left(\frac{\pi}{N}\right)^{3}\left(1-\left|\alpha\right|^{2}\right)

where in the third line we also used
1+\cos\theta\geq 1/2
for all
\theta\in\left[-\frac{2\pi}{3},\frac{2\pi}{3}\right]
. This implies

\left|\det\hat{A}\right|\geq\left(\frac{\pi}{N}\right)^{3}\frac{1-\left|\alpha\right|^{2}}{(1+\left|\alpha\right|)^{6}}>0

so that
\hat{A}
has full rank 3 and thus
\mathrm{rank\;}A=3
. Hence,
\mathrm{D}_{\alpha}\boldsymbol{m}
,
\mathrm{D}_{\bar{\alpha}}\boldsymbol{m}
, and
\mathrm{D}_{\psi}\boldsymbol{m}
are linearly independent. From the inverse function theorem for smooth functions, since
\boldsymbol{m}
is smooth, so is its inverse
[
34
]
. This finishes the proof.
∎

\displaystyle\dot{\alpha}

4.2
The level set of uniform distributions

On the level set

16

and solve the resulting equations. Under the right conditions, this yields a family of periodic orbits whose union forms a NAIM. Interpreting the truncated terms as

The key observation for systems of type (
1
) is that on the level set
\mathcal{L}_{\boldsymbol{\lambda^{*}}}(\boldsymbol{\Lambda})
the order parameter
Z
can be approximated by
\alpha
if
N
is sufficiently large
[
35
,
14
]
. Since
f
and
g
in (

, replacing
Z
by
\alpha
decouples (

) from

which simplifies the problem of finding periodic solution of (

C^{1}
-norm. The main result of this section is an estimate of
Z
and its partial derivatives for large
N
, which is used to estimate the error from truncating higher order terms in
N
in (
16
). We start by writing
Z
in terms of
\alpha
,
\psi
, and
\boldsymbol{\lambda}
. We first define the auxiliary function
Z_{\boldsymbol{\theta}}:\mathbb{D}\times\mathbb{S}^{1}\times\mathbb{T}^{N}\to\mathbb{D}
with

\displaystyle Z_{\boldsymbol{\theta}}(\alpha,\psi,\boldsymbol{\theta})

\displaystyle:=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{j=1}^{N}\frac{\alpha+\rme^{\rmi\psi}\rme^{\rmi\theta_{j}}}{1+\bar{\alpha}\,\rme^{\rmi\psi}\rme^{\rmi\theta_{j}}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{j=1}^{N}(\alpha+\rme^{\rmi\psi}\rme^{\rmi\theta_{j}})\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}(-\bar{\alpha}\,\rme^{\rmi\psi}\rme^{\rmi\theta_{j}})^{k}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\alpha(-\bar{\alpha}\,\rme^{\rmi\psi})^{k}\frac{1}{N}\sum_{j=1}^{N}\rme^{\rmi k\theta_{j}}+\rme^{\rmi\psi}(-\bar{\alpha}\,\rme^{\rmi\psi})^{k}\frac{1}{N}\sum_{j=1}^{N}\rme^{\rmi(k+1)\theta_{j}}.

Introducing the symbols

we arrive at the general expression

\displaystyle Z_{\boldsymbol{\theta}}(\alpha,\psi,\boldsymbol{\theta})

\displaystyle=\alpha\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}(-\bar{\alpha}\,\rme^{\rmi\psi})^{k}\left\langle\rme^{\rmi k\boldsymbol{\theta}}\right\rangle+\rme^{\rmi\psi}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}(-\bar{\alpha}\,\rme^{\rmi\psi})^{k}\left\langle\rme^{\rmi(k+1)\boldsymbol{\theta}}\right\rangle.

From this, we can define

by setting
\boldsymbol{\theta}=\boldsymbol{\Theta}(\boldsymbol{\lambda})
in the equation above so that we arrive at

\eqalign{Z(\alpha,\psi,\boldsymbol{\lambda})&:=Z_{\boldsymbol{\theta}}(\alpha,\psi,\boldsymbol{\Theta}(\boldsymbol{\lambda}))\\

(17)

For
\boldsymbol{\lambda}=\boldsymbol{\lambda^{*}}
, the symbol

and

can be written in simple form as shown next.

Lemma 4.9
.

Recall that
\boldsymbol{\theta^{*}}=\boldsymbol{\Theta}(\boldsymbol{\lambda^{*}})
is defined by
\theta^{*}_{j}=-\pi+2\pi(j-1)/N
with
j=1,\dots,N
. For
\boldsymbol{\theta^{*}}
, we have

\left\langle\rme^{\rmi k\boldsymbol{\theta^{*}}}\right\rangle=\cases{(-1)^{k}&if $k\in N\mathbb{Z}$\cr 0&else.}

Proof.

Let
k\in N\mathbb{Z}
, i. e.,
k=Nl
for some integer
l
. Then

\rme^{\rmi k\theta^{*}_{j}}=\rme^{-\rmi\pi k+\frac{2\pi\rmi}{N}Nl(j-1)}=\rme^{-\rmi\pi k}=(-1)^{k}

for all
j
and
\left\langle\rme^{\rmi k\boldsymbol{\theta^{*}}}\right\rangle=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{j=1}^{N}(-1)^{k}=(-1)^{k}
. On the other hand, let
k\notin N\mathbb{Z}
. We use the well known identity
\sum_{j=1}^{N}z^{j-1}=\frac{z^{N}-1}{z-1}
for any

which yields

\displaystyle\left\langle\rme^{\rmi k\boldsymbol{\theta^{*}}}\right\rangle=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{j=1}^{N}\rme^{-\rmi\pi k+\frac{2\pi\rmi}{N}(j-1)k}=0

∎

Each level set

is diffeomorphic to the space

so that we identify

with

Consider a smooth function

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{F}:\mathbb{D}\times\mathbb{S}^{1}\times V

\displaystyle\to\mathbb{R}^{n}.

The derivative of its restriction

to the level set

Let

\mathbb{D}_{r}:=\left\{\alpha\in\mathbb{C}\;;\,|\alpha|<r\right\}.

The following lemma gives an estimate to leading order in

for

on the level set
\mathcal{L}_{\boldsymbol{\lambda^{*}}}(\boldsymbol{\Lambda})
of uniform distributions.

Lemma 4.10
.

Consider
\overline{\mathbb{D}}_{r}
for any
0<r<1
and let

\displaystyle\eta:(\alpha,\psi,\boldsymbol{\lambda})

\displaystyle\mapsto Z(\alpha,\psi,\boldsymbol{\lambda})-\alpha\qquad\textrm{on}\qquad\mathbb{D}\times\mathbb{S}^{1}\times V

\displaystyle\to\mathbb{C}

Then,

\displaystyle\sup_{(\alpha,\psi)\in\overline{\mathbb{D}}_{r}\times\mathbb{S}^{1}}\left|\eta(\alpha,\psi,\boldsymbol{\lambda^{*}})\right|=\mathcal{O}(r^{N-1})

(18)

\displaystyle\sup_{(\alpha,\psi)\in\overline{\mathbb{D}}_{r}\times\mathbb{S}^{1}}\left|\left|\tilde{\mathrm{D}}\eta(\alpha,\psi,\boldsymbol{\lambda^{*}})\right|\right|=\mathcal{O}(Nr^{N-2}).

(19)

Proof.

We start with (
18
). From (
17
),
\boldsymbol{\theta^{*}}=\boldsymbol{\Theta}(\boldsymbol{\lambda^{*}})
, and lemma
4.9
, we infer

\displaystyle\eta(\alpha,\psi,\boldsymbol{\lambda^{*}})

\displaystyle=-\left(1-\left|\alpha\right|^{2}\right)\,\rme^{\rmi\psi}\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}(\bar{\alpha}\,\rme^{\rmi\psi})^{kN-1}

\displaystyle=-\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\bar{\alpha}^{kN-1}\rme^{\rmi kN\psi}+\alpha\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\bar{\alpha}^{kN}\rme^{\rmi kN\psi}

yielding

\displaystyle\sup_{(\alpha,\psi)\in\overline{\mathbb{D}}_{r}\times\mathbb{S}^{1}}\left|\eta(\alpha,\psi,\boldsymbol{\lambda^{*}})\right|

\displaystyle=\sup_{(\alpha,\psi)\in\overline{\mathbb{D}}_{r}\times\mathbb{S}^{1}}\left|\left(1-\left|\alpha\right|^{2}\right)\,\rme^{\rmi\psi}\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}(\bar{\alpha}\,\rme^{\rmi\psi})^{kN-1}\right|

\displaystyle\leq\sup_{(\alpha,\psi)\in\overline{\mathbb{D}}_{r}\times\mathbb{S}^{1}}\left(1-\left|\alpha\right|^{2}\right)\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\left|\bar{\alpha}\,\rme^{\rmi\psi}\right|^{kN-1}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}r^{kN-1}=\mathcal{O}(r^{N-1}).

so that (
18
) holds.

To prove (
19
), we first compute the entries of
\tilde{\mathrm{D}}\eta(\alpha,\psi,\boldsymbol{\lambda^{*}})
to leading order in
N
. From this, we can expand
\left|\left|\tilde{\mathrm{D}}\eta(\alpha,\psi,\boldsymbol{\lambda^{*}})\right|\right|
to leading order. For each
x\in\{\alpha,\bar{\alpha},\psi\}
, we write in a slight abuse of notation
\mathrm{D}_{x}\eta=\mathrm{Re\;}\mathrm{D}_{x}\eta+\rmi\,\mathrm{Im\;}\mathrm{D}_{x}\eta
for the respective column
(\mathrm{Re\;}\mathrm{D}_{x}\eta,\mathrm{Im\;}\mathrm{D}_{x}\eta)^{T}
of
\mathrm{D}\eta
.

For the first column of
\mathrm{D}\eta(\alpha,\psi,\boldsymbol{\lambda^{*}})
, we find

\displaystyle\mathrm{D}_{\alpha}\eta(\alpha,\psi,\boldsymbol{\lambda^{*}})

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}(\bar{\alpha}\,\rme^{\rmi\psi})^{kN}

\displaystyle\left|\mathrm{D}_{\alpha}\eta(\alpha,\psi,\boldsymbol{\lambda^{*}})\right|

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\left|\alpha\right|^{kN}\leq\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}r^{kN}

for all
(\alpha,\psi)\in\overline{\mathbb{D}}_{r}\times\mathbb{S}^{1}
and thus

\displaystyle\sup_{(\alpha,\psi)\in\overline{\mathbb{D}}_{r}\times\mathbb{S}^{1}}\left|\mathrm{Re\;}\mathrm{D}_{\alpha}\eta(\alpha,\psi,\boldsymbol{\lambda^{*}})\right|

\displaystyle=\mathcal{O}(r^{N})

\displaystyle\sup_{(\alpha,\psi)\in\overline{\mathbb{D}}_{r}\times\mathbb{S}^{1}}\left|\mathrm{Im\;}\mathrm{D}_{\alpha}\eta(\alpha,\psi,\boldsymbol{\lambda^{*}})\right|

\displaystyle=\mathcal{O}(r^{N}).

For the second column, we find

\displaystyle\mathrm{D}_{\bar{\alpha}}\eta(\alpha,\psi,\boldsymbol{\lambda^{*}})

\displaystyle=-\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}(kN-1)\,\bar{\alpha}^{kN-2}\,\rme^{\rmi kN\psi}+\alpha\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}kN\,\bar{\alpha}^{kN-1}\,\rme^{\rmi kN\psi}

\displaystyle\left|\mathrm{D}_{\bar{\alpha}}\eta(\alpha,\psi,\boldsymbol{\lambda^{*}})\right|

\displaystyle\leq\;\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\left[(kN-1)\left|\alpha\right|^{kN-2}+kN\left|\alpha\right|^{kN}\right]

\displaystyle\leq\;\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\left[(kN-1)\,r^{kN-2}+kN\,r^{kN}\right]

for all
(\alpha,\psi)\in\overline{\mathbb{D}}_{r}\times\mathbb{S}^{1}
and

\displaystyle\sup_{(\alpha,\psi)\in\overline{\mathbb{D}}_{r}\times\mathbb{S}^{1}}\left|\mathrm{Re\;}\mathrm{D}_{\bar{\alpha}}\eta(\alpha,\psi,\boldsymbol{\lambda^{*}})\right|

\displaystyle=\mathcal{O}(Nr^{N-2})

\displaystyle\sup_{(\alpha,\psi)\in\overline{\mathbb{D}}_{r}\times\mathbb{S}^{1}}\left|\mathrm{Im\;}\mathrm{D}_{\bar{\alpha}}\eta(\alpha,\psi,\boldsymbol{\lambda^{*}})\right|

\displaystyle=\mathcal{O}(Nr^{N-2}).

Finally, we have

\displaystyle\mathrm{D}_{\psi}\eta(\alpha,\psi,\boldsymbol{\lambda^{*}})

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\rmi kN\big[-\bar{\alpha}^{kN-1}+\alpha\bar{\alpha}^{kN}\big]\rme^{\rmi kN\psi}

\displaystyle\left|\mathrm{D}_{\psi}\eta(\alpha,\psi,\boldsymbol{\lambda^{*}})\right|

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}kN\big[\left|\alpha\right|^{kN-1}+\left|\alpha\right|^{kN+1}\big]

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}kN\big[r^{kN-1}+r^{kN+1}\big]

for all
(\alpha,\psi)\in\overline{\mathbb{D}}_{r}\times\mathbb{S}^{1}
so that

\displaystyle\sup_{(\alpha,\psi)\in\overline{\mathbb{D}}_{r}\times\mathbb{S}^{1}}\left|\mathrm{Re\;}\mathrm{D}_{\psi}\eta(\alpha,\psi,\boldsymbol{\lambda^{*}})\right|

\displaystyle=\mathcal{O}(Nr^{N-1})

\displaystyle\sup_{(\alpha,\psi)\in\overline{\mathbb{D}}_{r}\times\mathbb{S}^{1}}\left|\mathrm{Im\;}\mathrm{D}_{\psi}\eta(\alpha,\psi,\boldsymbol{\lambda^{*}})\right|

\displaystyle=\mathcal{O}(Nr^{N-1}).

Writing

\left|\left|A\right|\right|_{1}=\max_{1\leq j\leq n}\sum_{i=1}^{m}\left|A_{ij}\right|

for the induced 1-norm for any
m\times n

A
and using the inequality

for its Euclidean norm, we arrive at

\displaystyle\left|\left|\tilde{\mathrm{D}}\eta(\alpha,\psi,\boldsymbol{\lambda^{*}})\right|\right|\leq\sqrt{3}\max_{x\in\{\alpha,\bar{\alpha},\psi\}}\left(\left|\mathrm{Re\;}\tilde{\mathrm{D}}_{x}\eta(\alpha,\psi,\boldsymbol{\lambda^{*}})\right|+\left|\mathrm{Im\;}\tilde{\mathrm{D}}_{x}\eta(\alpha,\psi,\boldsymbol{\lambda^{*}})\right|\right)

and hence

\left|\left|\tilde{\mathrm{D}}\eta(\alpha,\psi,\boldsymbol{\lambda^{*}})\right|\right|=\mathcal{O}(Nr^{N-2}),

uniformly for all
(\alpha,\psi)\in\overline{\mathbb{D}}_{r}\times\mathbb{S}^{1}
which proves (

).
∎

Lemma 4.11
.

Let

and consider the vector field

\displaystyle<\varepsilon

Proof.

Step 1:
Let

with

\eqalign{F(Z)&=\int_{0}^{1}\eta\cdot\mathrm{D}_{z}F|_{z=\alpha+t\eta}+\bar{\eta}\cdot\mathrm{D}_{\bar{z}}F|_{z=\alpha+t\eta}\,\mathrm{d}t\\
\left|F(Z)\right|&\leq\left|\eta\right|\cdot\int_{0}^{1}\left|\mathrm{D}_{z}F\right|_{z=\alpha+t\eta}+\left|\mathrm{D}_{\bar{z}}F\right|_{z=\alpha+t\eta}\,\mathrm{d}t.}

(21)

Since
F
is smooth and
\overline{\mathbb{D}}_{r}
is compact, there then exist constants
M_{1},M_{2}>0
such that
\left|\mathrm{D}_{z}F\right|<M_{1}
and
\left|\mathrm{D}_{\bar{z}}F\right|<M_{2}
for all

so that in particular, the integral above is of order

and

\left|F(Z(\alpha,\psi,\boldsymbol{\lambda^{*}}))\right|=\mathcal{O}(\left|\eta(\alpha,\psi,\boldsymbol{\lambda^{*}})\right|)=\mathcal{O}(r^{N-1})

for all
(\alpha,\psi)\in\overline{\mathbb{D}}_{r}\times\mathbb{S}^{1}
by lemma
4.10

\displaystyle\mathrm{D}_{x}F(Z)=

\displaystyle\quad\int_{0}^{1}\mathrm{D}_{z}\mathrm{D}_{z}F|_{z=\alpha+t\eta}\,\mathrm{D}_{x}(\alpha+t\eta)\,\mathrm{d}t\cdot\eta

\displaystyle+\int_{0}^{1}\mathrm{D}_{\bar{z}}\mathrm{D}_{z}F|_{z=\alpha+t\eta}\,\mathrm{D}_{x}(\bar{\alpha}+t\bar{\eta})\,\mathrm{d}t\cdot\eta

\displaystyle+\int_{0}^{1}\mathrm{D}_{z}\mathrm{D}_{\bar{z}}F|_{z=\alpha+t\eta}\,\mathrm{D}_{x}(\alpha+t\eta)\,\mathrm{d}t\cdot\bar{\eta}

\displaystyle+\int_{0}^{1}\mathrm{D}_{\bar{z}}\mathrm{D}_{\bar{z}}F|_{z=\alpha+t\eta}\,\mathrm{D}_{x}(\bar{\alpha}+t\bar{\eta})\,\mathrm{d}t\cdot\bar{\eta}

\displaystyle+\int_{0}^{1}\mathrm{D}_{z}F|_{\alpha+t\eta}\,\mathrm{d}t\cdot\mathrm{D}_{x}\eta+\int_{0}^{1}\mathrm{D}_{\bar{z}}F|_{z=\alpha+t\eta}\,\mathrm{d}t\cdot\mathrm{D}_{x}\bar{\eta}

so that by the same argument as above and again with lemma
4.10

\left|\mathrm{D}_{x}(\alpha+t\eta(\alpha,\psi,\boldsymbol{\lambda^{*}}))\right|=\mathcal{O}(1)+\mathcal{O}(Nr^{N-2})=\mathcal{O}(1)

\displaystyle\left|\mathrm{D}_{x}F(Z(\alpha,\psi,\boldsymbol{\lambda^{*}}))\right|

\displaystyle\leq\left|\eta\right|\cdot\int_{0}^{1}\Big[\left|\mathrm{D}_{z}\mathrm{D}_{z}F\right|\!\,_{z=\alpha+t\eta}\cdot\left|\mathrm{D}_{x}(\alpha+t\eta)\right|+

\displaystyle\hskip 49.79231pt+\left|\mathrm{D}_{\bar{z}}\mathrm{D}_{z}F\right|\!\,_{z=\alpha+t\eta}\cdot\left|\mathrm{D}_{x}(\bar{\alpha}+t\bar{\eta})\right|+

\displaystyle\hskip 49.79231pt+\left|\mathrm{D}_{z}\mathrm{D}_{\bar{z}}F\right|\!\,_{z=\alpha+t\eta}\cdot\left|\mathrm{D}_{x}(\alpha+t\eta)\right|+

\displaystyle\hskip 49.79231pt+\left|\mathrm{D}_{\bar{z}}\mathrm{D}_{\bar{z}}F\right|\!\,_{z=\alpha+t\eta}\cdot\left|\mathrm{D}_{x}(\bar{\alpha}+t\bar{\eta})\right|\Big]\,\mathrm{d}t\;+

\displaystyle\hskip 49.79231pt+\left|\mathrm{D}_{x}\eta\right|\cdot\int_{0}^{1}\left|\mathrm{D}_{z}F\right|\!\,_{z=\alpha+t\eta}+\left|\mathrm{D}_{\bar{z}}F\right|\!\,_{z=\alpha+t\eta}\,\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle=\mathcal{O}(\left|\eta(\alpha,\psi,\boldsymbol{\lambda^{*}})\right|)+\mathcal{O}(\left|\mathrm{D}_{x}\eta(\alpha,\psi,\boldsymbol{\lambda^{*}})\right|)=\mathcal{O}(Nr^{N-2}).

Step 2:
Since
\boldsymbol{Y}(\alpha,\psi)

for all

. By smoothness of

, it follows that for each

Proof.

Lemma 6.1

We solve (

We show that for any

Using (

7
Conclusion and outlook

Families of periodic orbits are a common occurrence in Watanabe-Strogatz integrable systems. For the case that all involved common fields only depend on the Kuramoto order parameter, we showed that the union of these orbits further possesses the structure of a normally attracting invariant manifold and that one of the orbits is a splay state. To determine which of the periodic orbits survive for the perturbed system, we developed a criterion by applying the method of averaging to the dynamics on the NAIM.

One of the orbits that formed the invariant manifold of the unperturbed system features splay state dynamics due to equivariance under cyclic permutations. We showed that averaging over the splay state orbit of the unperturbed system yields a fixed point in the averaged dynamics for the perturbed system. Whether it also hyperbolic depends on the specific setup of the perturbed system and in particular on the choice for the perturbation function.

Applying our results to the active rotator model by Shinomoto and Kuramoto, our observations are two-fold: (i) We determined the critical coupling strength below which the model possesses the family of periodic orbits. (ii) Applying the averaging principle to the perturbed or generalized active rotator model, numerical evidence indicates that averaging over the unperturbed splay state indeed results in a hyperbolic fixed point for the averaged dynamics and thus the splay state is generically hyperbolic for ensembles of identical generalized active rotators. It also illustrates the versatility of the proposed procedure as an easy-to-implement tool to study perturbations to WS-integrable systems.

In restricting attention to the case of solely
Z
-dependent common fields
f
and
g
, we excluded, e. g., systems of identically driven elements with external forcing
[
38
]
or ensembles that consist of subpopulations
[
39
]
, both of which are WS-integrable. The question thus arises whether, and if, how the results, presented here, may be generalized to such and other WS-integrable systems.

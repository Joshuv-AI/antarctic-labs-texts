dimorphic properties of Bernoulli random variable
Taekyun Kim, DAE SAN KIM, Hyunseok Lee, Seong-Ho Park

Abstract
. The aim of this paper is to study a dimorphic property associated with two different sums of identically independent Bernoulli random variables having two different families of probability mass functions. In addition, we give two expressions on sums of products of degenerate Stirling numbers of the second kind and Stirling numbers of the first kind connected with those two different sums of identically independent Bernoulli random variables.



2010 Mathematics Subject Classification
11B73; 60G50

1.
Introduction

It is well known that Bernoulli random variable is the discrete random variable which takes the value 1 with probability
p
and the value 0 with probability
1-p
, where
0\leq p\leq 1
.
In this paper, we study a dimorphic property (see Theorem 2) associated with two different sums of identically independent Bernoulli random variables having two different families of probability mass functions.

Further, we give two expressions on sums of products of degenerate Stirling numbers of the second kind and Stirling numbers of the first kind in connection with those two different sums of identically independent Bernoulli random variables. In fact, one is expressed in terms of the expectation of a random variable associated with one sum of identically independent Bernoulli random variables (see Theorem 3) and the other in terms of an integral involving the other sum of identically independent Bernoulli random variables (see Theorem 4). In the rest of this section, we recall some facts that are needed throughout this paper.

For any
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
, the degenerate exponential function is defined as

(1)

e_{\lambda}^{x}(t)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}(x)_{n,\lambda}\frac{t^{n}}{n!},\quad(\mathrm{see}\ [7,8,9,10,11,12]),

where
(x)_{0,\lambda}=1,\ (x)_{n,\lambda}=x(x-\lambda)\big(x-(n-1)\lambda\big),\ (n\geq 1)
.

When
x=1
, for simplicity we write
e_{\lambda}(t)=e_{\lambda}^{1}(t)
. Note that
\displaystyle\lim_{\lambda\rightarrow 0}e_{\lambda}^{x}(t)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{x^{n}}{n!}t^{n}=e^{xt}\displaystyle
.

The Stirling numbers of the first kind are defined by

(2)

(x)_{n}=\sum_{l=0}^{n}S_{1}(n,l)x^{l},\quad(n\geq 0),\quad(\mathrm{see}\ [1,3,4,14]),

where
(x)_{0}=1,\ (x)_{n}=x(x-1)\cdots(x-n+1),\ (n\geq 1)
.
As the inversion formula of (
2
), the Stirling numbers of the second kind are defined by

(3)

x^{n}=\sum_{l=0}^{n}S_{2}(n,l)(x)_{l},\quad(n\geq 0),\quad(\mathrm{see}\ [14,15,16,17,18]).

Moreover, in [8] the degenerate Stirling numbers of the second kind are defined as

(4)

(x)_{n,\lambda}=\sum_{l=0}^{n}S_{2,\lambda}(n,l)(x)_{l},\quad(n\geq 0).

Note that
\displaystyle\lim_{\lambda\rightarrow 0}S_{2,\lambda}(n,l)=S_{2}(n,l),\ (n,l\geq 0)\displaystyle
. From (
4
), we note that

(5)

\frac{1}{k!}\big(e_{\lambda}(t)-1\big)^{k}=\sum_{n=k}^{\infty}S_{2,\lambda}(n,k)\frac{t^{n}}{n!},\quad(k\geq 0),\quad(\mathrm{see}\ [6,8,9]).

Let
X
be a discrete random variable with probability mass function
p(j)=P\{X=j\},\ (j=1,2,\dots)
. Then the expectation of
X
is defined by

(6)

E[X^{n}]=\sum_{i=1}^{n}i^{n}p(i),\quad(n\in\mathbb{N}),\quad(\mathrm{see}\ [1,2,17]).

It is known that that the variance of
X
is given by

(7)

\sigma^{2}=\mathrm{Var}(X)=E[X^{2}]-\big(E[X]\big)^{2},\quad(\mathrm{see}\ [17]).

Let
(X_{j})_{1\leq j\leq n}
be identically independent Bernoulli random variables such that
X_{j}
has the probability of success
\frac{1}{j},\ (j=1,2,\dots,n)
. That is,

(8)

X_{j}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{cc}1,&\textrm{if success,}\\
0,&\textrm{otherwise,}\end{array}\right.

with
P\{X_{j}=1\}=\frac{1}{j},\ P\{X_{j}=0\}=1-P\{X_{j}=1\}
, where
j=1,2,3,\dots,n
, (see [2,5,13,17,18]).

Let us assume that the random variable
Y_{n}
is defined by

(9)

Y_{n}=X_{1}+X_{2}+\cdots+X_{n}=\sum_{i=1}^{n}X_{i}.

From (
6
), (
7
) and (
9
), we note that

(10)

\mu_{n}=E[Y_{n}]=\sum_{j=1}^{n}E[X_{j}]=\sum_{j=1}^{n}\frac{1}{j},

and

(11)

\sigma_{n}^{2}=E[Y_{n}^{2}]-\big(E[Y_{n}]\big)^{2}=\sum_{j=1}^{n}\frac{1}{j}\bigg(1-\frac{1}{j}\bigg).

2.
Dimorphic properties of Bernoulli random variables

In this section, we show the dimorphic property (see Theorem 2) associated with the two different sums
Y_{n}=\sum_{j=1}^{n}X_{j}
(see (
9
)) and
Z_{n,\lambda}(\alpha)=\sum_{j=1}^{n}X_{j,\lambda}(\alpha)
(see (
13
)) of the identically independent Bernoulli random variables
X_{j}
, with the probability of success
\frac{1}{j}
, and those
X_{j,\lambda}(\alpha)
, with the probability of success
\frac{\alpha}{\lambda j+\alpha}
.

For
\alpha>0\ (\alpha\in\mathbb{R})
, and
\lambda\in(0,1)
, let
\big(X_{j,\lambda}(\alpha)\big)_{1\leq j\leq n}
be identically independent Bernoulli random variables such that
X_{j,\lambda}(\alpha)
has the probability of success
\frac{\alpha}{\lambda j+\alpha},\ (j=1,2,\dots,n)
.

That is,

(12)

X_{j,\lambda}(\alpha)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{cc}1,&\textrm{if success,}\\
0&\textrm{otherwise,}\end{array}\right.

with
P\{X_{j,\lambda}(\alpha)=1\}=\frac{\alpha}{\alpha+\lambda j},\ P\{X_{j,\lambda}(\alpha)=0\}=1-P\{X_{j,\lambda}(\alpha)=1\}
.

Let us assume that
Z_{n,\lambda}(\alpha)
is defined by

(13)

Z_{n,\lambda}(\alpha)=X_{1,\lambda}(\alpha)+X_{2,\lambda}(\alpha)+\cdots+X_{n,\lambda}(\alpha)=\sum_{i=1}^{n}X_{i,\lambda}(\alpha).

From (
6
), (
7
) and (
13
), we have

(14)

\mu_{n,\lambda}(\alpha)=E\big[Z_{n,\lambda}(\alpha)\big]=\sum_{j=1}^{n}\frac{\alpha}{\alpha+\lambda j},

and

(15)

\displaystyle\sigma_{n}^{2}(\alpha)\ =\ \mathrm{Var}\big(Z_{n,\lambda}(\alpha)\big)\ =\ E\big[Z_{n,\lambda}^{2}(\alpha)\big]-\Big(E\big[Z_{n,\lambda}(\alpha)\big]\Big)^{2}

\displaystyle=\ \sum_{j=1}^{n}E\big[X_{j,\lambda}^{2}(\alpha)\big]+2\sum_{i<j}E\big[X_{j,\lambda}(\alpha)\big]E\big[X_{i,\lambda}(\alpha)\big]

\displaystyle\quad-\bigg\{\sum_{j=1}^{n}\bigg(\frac{\alpha}{\alpha+\lambda j}\bigg)^{2}+2\sum_{i<j}\bigg(\frac{\alpha}{\alpha+\lambda j}\bigg)\bigg(\frac{\alpha}{\alpha+\lambda i}\bigg)\bigg\}

\displaystyle=\ \sum_{j=1}^{n}\frac{\alpha}{\alpha+\lambda j}+2\sum_{i<j}\frac{\alpha^{2}}{(\alpha+\lambda j)(\alpha+\lambda i)}-\sum_{j=1}^{n}\bigg(\frac{\alpha}{\alpha+\lambda j}\bigg)^{2}-2\sum_{i<j}\frac{\alpha^{2}}{(\alpha+\lambda j)(\alpha+\lambda i)}

\displaystyle=\ \sum_{j=1}^{n}\frac{\alpha}{\alpha+\lambda j}\bigg(1-\frac{\alpha}{\alpha+\lambda j}\bigg).

Lemma 1
.

For
n\geq 1
, let
\mu_{n,\lambda}(\alpha)
be the mean of
Z_{n,\lambda}(\alpha)
, and let
\sigma_{n,\lambda}^{2}
be the variance of
Z_{n,\lambda}(\alpha)
. Then we have

\mu_{n,\lambda}(\alpha)=\sum_{j=1}^{n}\frac{\alpha}{\alpha+\lambda j},

and

\sigma_{n,\lambda}^{2}(\alpha)=\sum_{j=1}^{n}\frac{\alpha}{\alpha+\lambda j}\bigg(1-\frac{\alpha}{\alpha+\lambda j}\bigg).

From (
8
), we have

(16)

\displaystyle E\big[(1+\frac{z}{\lambda})^{X_{j}}\big]

\displaystyle=\ (1+\frac{z}{\lambda})P\{X_{j}=1\}+P\{X_{j}=0\}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{j}+\frac{z}{\lambda j}+1-\frac{1}{j}=1+\frac{z}{\lambda j}.

By (
16
), we get

(17)

\displaystyle E\Big[(1+\frac{z}{\lambda})^{Y_{n}}\Big]

\displaystyle=E\Big[(1+\frac{z}{\lambda})^{\sum_{j=1}^{n}X_{j}}\Big]

\displaystyle=\ \prod_{j=1}^{n}E\big[(1+\frac{z}{\lambda})^{X_{j}}\big]=\prod_{j=1}^{n}\bigg(1+\frac{z}{\lambda j}\bigg).

On the other hand,

(18)

E\Big[z^{Z_{n,\lambda}(\alpha)}\Big]=E\Big[z^{\sum_{j=1}^{n}X_{j,\lambda}(\alpha)}\Big]=\prod_{j=1}^{n}E\Big[z^{X_{j,\lambda}(\alpha)}\Big].

Note that

(19)

\displaystyle E\Big[z^{X_{j,\lambda}(\alpha)}\Big]\

\displaystyle=\ zP\{X_{j,\lambda}(\alpha)=1\}+P\{X_{j,\lambda}(\alpha)=0\}

\displaystyle=\ \frac{\alpha z}{\alpha+\lambda j}+1-\frac{\alpha}{\alpha+\lambda j}

\displaystyle=\ \frac{\alpha z+\lambda j}{\alpha+\lambda j},\quad(j=1,2,\dots,n).

By (
18
) and (
19
), we get

(20)

E\Big[z^{Z_{n,\lambda}(\alpha)}\Big]=\prod_{j=1}^{n}\bigg(\frac{\alpha z+\lambda j}{\alpha+\lambda j}\bigg).

From (
17
) and (
20
), we note that

(21)

\displaystyle E\Big[(1+\frac{\alpha}{\lambda})^{Y_{n}}\Big]E\Big[z^{Z_{n,\lambda}(\alpha)}\Big]

\displaystyle\quad=\ \prod_{j=1}^{n}\bigg(1+\frac{\alpha}{\lambda j}\bigg)\prod_{j=1}^{n}\bigg(\frac{\alpha z+\lambda j}{\alpha+\lambda j}\bigg)

\displaystyle\quad=\ \prod_{j=1}^{n}\bigg(\frac{\alpha z+\lambda j}{\lambda j}\bigg)=\ \prod_{j=1}^{n}\bigg(1+\frac{\alpha z}{\lambda j}\bigg).

By (
8
), we easily get

(22)

E\bigg[\bigg(1+\frac{\alpha z}{\lambda}\bigg)^{Y_{n}}\bigg]=\prod_{j=1}^{n}E\bigg[\bigg(1+\frac{\alpha z}{\lambda}\bigg)^{X_{j}}\bigg]=\prod_{j=1}^{n}\bigg(1+\frac{\alpha z}{\lambda j}\bigg).

Therefore, by (
21
) and (
22
), we obtain the following theorem.

Theorem 2
.

For
n\in\mathbb{N}
, we have

E\Big[(1+\frac{\alpha}{\lambda})^{Y_{n}}\Big]E\Big[z^{Z_{n,\lambda}(\alpha)}\Big]=E\bigg[\bigg(1+\frac{\alpha z}{\lambda}\bigg)^{Y_{n}}\bigg].

3.
Applications to sums of products of degenerate Stirling numbers of the second kind and Stirling numbers of the first kind

Here, as an application of the dimorphic property in Theorem 2, we derive two different expressions on the sum
\sum_{m=l}^{n}S_{2,\lambda}(n+1,m+1)S_{1}(m+1,l+1)
, one involving
Y_{n}
(see Theorem 3) and the other involving
Z_{n,\lambda}(\alpha)
(see Theorem 4).

From (
2
) and (
4
), we note that

(23)

\displaystyle(x)_{n+1,\lambda}\

\displaystyle=\ \sum_{m=0}^{n+1}S_{2,\lambda}(n+1,m)(x)_{m}\ =\ \sum_{m=1}^{n+1}S_{2,\lambda}(n+1,m)(x)_{m}

\displaystyle=\ \sum_{m=0}^{n}S_{2,\lambda}(n+1,m+1)(x)_{m+1}

\displaystyle=\ x\sum_{m=0}^{n}S_{2,\lambda}(n+1,m+1)\sum_{l=0}^{m}S_{1}(m+1,l+1)x^{l}

\displaystyle=\ x\sum_{l=0}^{n}\bigg(\sum_{m=l}^{n}S_{2,\lambda}(n+1,m+1)S_{1}(m+1,l+1)\bigg)x^{l}.

By (
1
) and (
17
), we easily get

(24)

\displaystyle(x)_{n+1,\lambda}\

\displaystyle=\ x(x-\lambda)(x-2\lambda)\cdots(x-n\lambda)

\displaystyle=\ n!\lambda^{n}(-1)^{n}x\bigg(1-\frac{x}{\lambda}\bigg)\bigg(1-\frac{x}{\lambda 2}\bigg)\cdots\bigg(1-\frac{x}{\lambda n}\bigg)

\displaystyle=\ n!(-1)^{n}\lambda^{n}x\prod_{j=1}^{n}\bigg(1-\frac{x}{\lambda j}\bigg)

\displaystyle=\ n!(-1)^{n}\lambda^{n}xE\bigg[\bigg(1-\frac{x}{\lambda})^{Y_{n}}\bigg].

From (
23
) and (
24
), we note that

(25)

\displaystyle z\sum_{l=0}^{n}\bigg(\sum_{m=l}^{n}S_{2,\lambda}(n+1,m+1)S_{1}(m+1,l+1)\bigg)z^{l}

\displaystyle\quad=n!(-1)^{n}\lambda^{n}zE\bigg[\bigg(1-\frac{z}{\lambda}\bigg)^{Y_{n}}\bigg]

\displaystyle\quad=n!(-1)^{n}\lambda^{n}z\sum_{l=0}^{n}(-1)^{l}E\bigg[\binom{Y_{n}}{l}\bigg]\bigg(\frac{z}{\lambda}\bigg)^{l}

\displaystyle\quad=z\sum_{l=0}^{n}(-1)^{n-l}\lambda^{n-l}n!E\bigg[\binom{Y_{n}}{l}\bigg]z^{l}.

By comparing the coefficients on both sides of (
25
), we obtain the following theorem.

Theorem 3
.

For
0\leq l\leq n
, we have

\sum_{m=l}^{n}S_{2,\lambda}(n+1,m+1)S_{1}(m+1,l+1)=(-1)^{n-l}\lambda^{n-l}n!E\bigg[\binom{Y_{n}}{l}\bigg].

As
Y_{n}
is taking integer values between
0
and
n
, we see that

(26)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}E\Bigg[\bigg(1+\frac{\alpha}{\lambda}e^{i\theta}\bigg)^{Y_{n}}\Bigg]e^{-im\theta}d\theta\

\displaystyle=\ \frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}\sum_{j=0}^{n}E\bigg[\binom{Y_{n}}{j}\bigg]\bigg(\frac{\alpha}{\lambda}\bigg)^{j}e^{ji\theta}e^{-im\theta}d\theta

\displaystyle=\ \sum_{j=0}^{n}E\bigg[\binom{Y_{n}}{j}\bigg]\frac{\alpha^{j}}{2\pi}\lambda^{-j}\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}e^{i\theta(j-m)}d\theta

\displaystyle=\lambda^{-m}\alpha^{m}E\bigg[\binom{Y_{n}}{m}\bigg],

where
i=\sqrt{-1}
.

From Theorem 2 and (
26
), we have

(27)

\displaystyle\lambda^{-l}\alpha^{l}E\bigg[\binom{Y_{n}}{l}\bigg]=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}E\bigg[\bigg(1+\frac{\alpha}{\lambda}e^{i\theta}\bigg)^{Y_{n}}\bigg]e^{-il\theta}d\theta

\displaystyle\quad=\ \frac{1}{2\pi}E\Big[(1+\frac{\alpha}{\lambda})^{Y_{n}}\Big]\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}E\Big[e^{i\theta Z_{n,\lambda}(\alpha)}\Big]e^{-il\theta}d\theta

\displaystyle\quad=\ \prod_{j=1}^{n}\bigg(1+\frac{\alpha}{\lambda j}\bigg)\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}E\Big[e^{i\theta(Z_{n,\lambda}(\alpha)-l)}\Big]d\theta

\displaystyle\quad=\ \frac{\Gamma(1+\frac{\alpha}{\lambda})(1+\frac{\alpha}{\lambda})(2+\frac{\alpha}{\lambda})\cdots(n+\frac{\alpha}{\lambda})}{n!\Gamma(1+\frac{\alpha}{\lambda})}\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}E\Big[e^{i\theta(Z_{n,\lambda}(\alpha)-l)}\Big]d\theta

\displaystyle\quad=\frac{\Gamma(n+\frac{\alpha}{\lambda}+1)}{n!\Gamma(1+\frac{\alpha}{\lambda})}\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}E\Big[e^{i\theta(Z_{n,\lambda}(\alpha)-l)}\Big]d\theta.

By Theorem 3 and (
27
), we get

(28)

\displaystyle\sum_{m=l}^{n}S_{2,\lambda}(n+1,m+1)S_{1}(m+1,l+1)\ =\ (-1)^{n-l}\lambda^{n-l}n!E\bigg[\binom{Y_{n}}{l}\bigg]

\displaystyle\quad=\ (-1)^{n-l}\lambda^{n}\frac{\Gamma(n+\frac{\alpha}{\lambda}+1)}{\alpha^{l}\Gamma(1+\frac{\alpha}{\lambda})}\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}\Big[e^{i\theta(Z_{n,\lambda}(\alpha)-l)}\Big]d\theta

\displaystyle\quad=\ (-1)^{n-l}\lambda^{n+1}\frac{\Gamma(n+\frac{\alpha}{\lambda}+1)}{\alpha^{l+1}\Gamma(\frac{\alpha}{\lambda})}\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}\Big[e^{i\theta(Z_{n,\lambda}(\alpha)-l)}\Big]d\theta.

Therefore, by (
28
), we obtain the following theorem.

Theorem 4
.

For
0\leq l\leq n
,
\alpha>0(\alpha\in\mathbb{R})
,
\lambda\in(0,1)
, and
i=\sqrt{-1}
, we have

\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}\Big[e^{i\theta(Z_{n,\lambda}(\alpha)-l)}\Big]d\theta=(-1)^{n-l}\frac{\alpha^{l+1}}{\lambda^{n+1}}\frac{\Gamma(\frac{\alpha}{\lambda})}{\Gamma(n+\frac{\alpha}{\lambda}+1)}\sum_{m=l}^{n}S_{2,\lambda}(n+1,m+1)S_{1}(m+1,l+1).

4.
Conclusion

Let
(X_{j})_{1\leq j\leq n}
be identically independent Bernoulli random variables such that
X_{j}
has the probability of success
\frac{1}{j},\ (j=1,2,\dots,n)
, with
Y_{n}=X_{1}+X_{2}+\cdots+X_{n}
. Let
\big(X_{j,\lambda}(\alpha)\big)_{1\leq j\leq n}
be identically independent Bernoulli random variables such that
X_{j,\lambda}(\alpha)
has the probability of success
\frac{\alpha}{\alpha+\lambda j},\ (j=1,2,\dots,n)
, with
Z_{n,\lambda}(\alpha)=X_{1,\lambda}(\alpha)+X_{2,\lambda}(\alpha)+\cdots+X_{n,\lambda}(\alpha)
. Here
\alpha
is a positive real number and
\lambda
is a real number with
0<\lambda<1
.

Then we showed in Theorem 2 the dimorphic property:

E\Big[(1+\frac{\alpha}{\lambda})^{Y_{n}}\Big]E\Big[z^{Z_{n,\lambda}(\alpha)}\Big]=E\bigg[\bigg(1+\frac{\alpha z}{\lambda}\bigg)^{Y_{n}}\bigg].

Further, we derived two different expressions on the sum
\sum_{m=l}^{n}S_{2,\lambda}(n+1,m+1)S_{1}(m+1,l+1)
in connection with
Y_{n}
and
Z_{n,\lambda}(\alpha)
. Indeed, we derived the following:

\displaystyle\sum_{m=l}^{n}S_{2,\lambda}(n+1,m+1)S_{1}(m+1,l+1)\ =\ (-1)^{n-l}\lambda^{n-l}n!E\bigg[\binom{Y_{n}}{l}\bigg]

\displaystyle\quad=\ (-1)^{n-l}\lambda^{n+1}\frac{\Gamma(n+\frac{\alpha}{\lambda}+1)}{\alpha^{l+1}\Gamma(\frac{\alpha}{\lambda})}\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}\Big[e^{i\theta(Z_{n,\lambda}(\alpha)-l)}\Big]d\theta.

There are various ways of studying special numbers and polynomials, to mention a few, generating functions, combinatorial methods, probability theory,
p
-adic analysis, umbral calculus, differential equations, special functions and analytic number theory.
In recent years, we have had lively interests in the study of various degenerate versions of special numbers and polynomials with those diverse tools.
As a result of such explorations, we came up with, for example, the degenerate Stirling numbers which are degenerate versions of the ordinary Stirling numbers and appear in many different contexts.
The novelty of this paper is that we obtained two different expressions on sums of products of degenerate Stirling numbers of the second kind and Stirling numbers of the first kind in connection with two different sums of identically independent Bernoulli random variables.
This is one example of our efforts in the applications of probability theory to the study of some special numbers and polynomials and also of degenerate versions of those numbers and polynomials.

We would like to continue to find many applications of probability theory to the study of some special numbers and polynomials and also of their degenerate versions. More generally, it is one of our future projects to continue to explore various degenerate versions of many special polynomials and numbers with aforementioned tools.

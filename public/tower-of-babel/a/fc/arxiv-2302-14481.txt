Dumont–Thomas complement numeration systems for ℤ \mathbb{Z}
Sébastien Labbé, Jana Lepšová

Abstract
. We extend the well-known Dumont–Thomas numeration systems to ℤ \mathbb{Z} using an approach inspired by the two’s complement numeration system. Integers in ℤ \mathbb{Z} are canonically represented by a finite word (starting with 𝟶 \mathtt{0} when nonnegative and with 𝟷 \mathtt{1} when negative). The systems are based on two-sided periodic points of substitutions as opposed to the right-sided fixed points. For every periodic point of a substitution, we construct an automaton which returns the letter at position n ∈ ℤ n\in\mathbb{Z} of the periodic point when fed with the representation of n n in the corresponding numeration system. The numeration system naturally extends to ℤ d \mathbb{Z}^{d} . We give an equivalent characterization of the numeration system in terms of a total order on a regular language. Lastly, using particular periodic points, we recover the well-known two’s complement numeration system and the Fibonacci analogue of the two’s complement numeration system.

The extension of the Dumont–Thomas

]
.

The main results of this contribution are Theorem

,

(Proposition

)
and similarly for the

The need for extending the theory of numeration systems based on substitutions from

2.
Preliminaries

An
alphabet

A
is a finite set and its elements

are called
\emph{letters}
.
A

.
When it is more convenient, we denote the

by

.
The

is denoted by

.
The set of all finite words over the alphabet

is denoted by

and the set of all nonempty words over the alphabet

.
We define the concatenation

as the following binary operation:

\odot:A^{*}\times A^{*}\to A^{*},u\odot v\mapsto uv.

The set
A^{*}
with the concatenation as operation
forms a monoid with
\varepsilon
as the neutral element.

A

over
A
is a map
\eta:A^{*}\to A^{*}

such that
\eta(u\odot v)=\eta(u)\odot\eta(v)
for all words
u,v\in A^{*}
.
A

\eta:A^{*}\to A^{*}
is a morphism such that

\eta(a)\in A^{+}
is nonempty
for every
a\in A

and there exists
a\in A
such that
a
is

growing
, that is,

\lim_{k\to+\infty}|\eta^{k}(a)|=+\infty
.
A morphism
\eta
is said

if
there exists
k\in\mathbb{N}
such that
for every
a,b\in A

the letter
a
appears in
\eta^{k}(b)
.
A morphism
\eta
is said

-uniform

|\eta(a)|=d
for every letter
a\in A
.

We call
u_{0}u_{1}u_{2}\cdots\in A^{\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}}
a

and
\cdots u_{-3}u_{-2}u_{-1}\in A^{{\mathbb{Z}_{<0}}}
a
left-infinite word
.
We call

a

and
we separate by a vertical bar its elements

and

to indicate the origin, i.e.,

.

Substitutions can be applied naturally to two-sided words

by setting

\eta(\dots u_{-3}u_{-2}u_{-1}|u_{0}u_{1}u_{2}\cdots)=\cdots\eta(u_{-3})\eta(u_{-2})\eta(u_{-1})|\eta(u_{0})\eta(u_{1})\eta(u_{2})\cdots.

Let
\mathbb{D}\in\{\mathbb{Z},{\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}},{\mathbb{Z}_{<0}}\}
.
A word

is called a

\eta

if there exists an integer

\eta^{p}(u)=u
,

The minimum integer

A periodic point with period

\eta
.
The set of periodic points of

Since we are mostly interested in two-sided words in this contribution,

If

,

If the seed letters of a two-sided periodic point are growing, then the

More precisely,

,

is a period of

.

Let
u=u_{0}u_{1}\cdots\in\Per_{\mathbb{N}}(\eta)
with a seed

.
The previous terminology is inspired by
[
CS01a
]
,
where a prefix-suffix automaton is associated with

.
However, for our goal an automaton associated with
\eta

as in
[

]
is sufficient.
Let
\mathcal{D}
denote the alphabet

The set

is a monoid for the operation

The deterministic finite automaton with output (DFAO)
associated to the substitution

The transition function

,
and, for every

We let

Definition 3.1

Let

\eta

is a prefix of

\eta(a)

As done in

Dumont and Thomas proved the following result, which we rewrite in our notation.

Theorem 3.2
.

[

Let

For every integer

The proof of the above theorem was based on the following lemmas,

.

[

Let

Lemma 3.6
.

Let

Lemma 3.12
.

.

Proof.

Let

(m_{i},a_{i})_{i=0,\dots,p-1}

.
Thus

\tail_{\eta,p,x}(n)=|m_{p-1}|\odot|m_{p-2}|\odot\ldots\odot|m_{0}|

\tail_{\eta,p,x}(n^{\prime})=|m^{\prime}_{p-1}|\odot|m^{\prime}_{p-2}|\odot\ldots\odot|m^{\prime}_{0}|

(i)

, then

\tail_{\eta,p,x}(n)=\tail_{\eta,p,x}(n^{\prime})
.
Conversely, if
\tail_{\eta,p,x}(n)=\tail_{\eta,p,x}(n^{\prime})
, then

since both are prefixes

and

and

(ii)

|m_{p-1}|\odot|m_{p-2}|\odot\ldots\odot|m_{0}|<_{lex}|m^{\prime}_{p-1}|\odot|m^{\prime}_{p-2}|\odot\ldots\odot|m^{\prime}_{0}|

Then there exists an integer
\ell

such that

,

|m_{j}|=|m^{\prime}_{j}|
for every integer

and

.

and

for every

.
Thus

and

of the same letter.

if

.

\displaystyle n-n^{\prime}

Then

. If

4.

\mathbb{Z}
based on periodic points

In this section, we prove extensions of

to right-infinite and left-infinite periodic

\mathbb{Z}

associated to any two-sided periodic point with growing seed of a substitution.

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
\eta:A^{*}\to A^{*}
be a substitution with growing letter
a\in A
.
Let

such that

.
Let
p\geq 1
be a period of
u
.
For every integer
n\geq 1
,
there exists a unique integer

such that
p
divides
k

and a unique sequence
(m_{i},a_{i})_{i=0,\dots,k-1}
such that

(i)

this sequence is
a
-admissible and

m_{k-1}m_{k-2}\cdots m_{k-p}\neq\varepsilon
,

(ii)

u_{0}u_{1}\cdots u_{n-1}=\eta^{k-1}(m_{k-1})\eta^{k-2}(m_{k-2})\cdots\eta^{0}(m_{0})
.

Proof.

Since
u
is a periodic point of period
p
, we have
that
u_{0}=a
is a prefix of
\eta^{p}(a)
.
Also, since
a
is growing, we have that
\eta^{p}(a)\in aA^{+}
.
Thus
(|\eta^{p\ell}(a)|)_{\ell\in\mathbb{N}}
is a strictly increasing sequence
starting with value 1 when
\ell=0
.
Let
n\geq 1
be an integer.
There exists a unique integer

\ell\geq 1
such that

|\eta^{p(\ell-1)}(a)|\leq n<|\eta^{p\ell}(a)|
.
Let
k=p\ell
so that we have

(2)

|\eta^{k-p}(a)|\leq n<|\eta^{k}(a)|.

The word

is thus a proper prefix
of
\eta^{k}(a)
.
From Lemma
3.9
,
there exists a unique

(m_{i},a_{i})_{i=0,\dots,k-1}
such that

Assume by contradiction that
m_{k-1}m_{k-2}\cdots m_{k-p}=\varepsilon
.
Then

and
from Lemma
3.3
, we have

\displaystyle n

\displaystyle=|m|=\textstyle\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}|\eta^{j}(m_{j})|=\textstyle\sum_{j=0}^{k-p-1}|\eta^{j}(m_{j})|

\displaystyle<|\eta^{k-p-1}(m_{k-p-1}a_{k-p-1})|\leq|\eta^{k-p-1}(\eta(a_{k-p}))|=|\eta^{k-p}(a)|,

a contradiction with (

).
Thus

.
∎

Lemma 4.9
.

Let
\eta:A^{*}\to A^{*}
be a substitution and

be a two-sided periodic point with growing seed.

be the period of

.

Let

Let

be a substitution and

be a two-sided periodic point with growing seed

\quad\delta(\texttt{start},\mathtt{1})=s_{-1}=u_{-1}

,

and every

,
it holds that

\delta(c,i)=d
if and only if
\eta(c)=w
and
w_{i}=d
.

A theorem of Cobham

says that
a sequence

is

.

Theorem 6.1
.

Let
\eta:A^{*}\to A^{*}
be a substitution and

u\in\Per(\eta)
be a two-sided periodic point with growing seed

s=u_{-1}|u_{0}
.
Then for every
n\in\mathbb{Z}

u_{n}=\mathcal{A}_{\eta,s}(\rep_{u}(n)).

Proof.

If
n\in\{0,-1\}
then by definition
we have

u_{n}=s_{n}=\mathcal{A}_{\eta,s}(\rep_{u}(n))
.

Let
n\in\mathbb{Z}\setminus\{0,-1\}
.
Induction hypothesis: for every
m\in\mathbb{Z}
such that
|m|<|n|

it holds that
x_{m}=\mathcal{A}_{\eta,s}(\rep_{u}(m))
.
Let
q
be the
u
-quotient and
r
the
u
-remainder
of
n
. As
|q|<|n|
,
q
fulfills the induction hypothesis, i.e.,

u_{q}=\mathcal{A}_{\eta,s}(\rep_{u}(q))
.
Let
p\geq 1
be the period of
u
.
From Lemma
4.9
we have

u_{n}=\eta^{p}(u_{q})[r]
and

\rep_{u}(n)=\rep_{u}(q)\odot\tail_{\eta,p,u_{q}}(r)
.
Using Lemma
3.11
and
Equation (

), we have

\displaystyle u_{n}

\displaystyle=\eta^{p}(u_{q})[r]=\mathcal{A}_{\eta,u_{q}}(\tail_{\eta,p,u_{q}}(r))

\displaystyle=\mathcal{A}_{\eta,s}(\rep_{u}(q)\odot\tail_{\eta,p,u_{q}}(r))=\mathcal{A}_{\eta,s}(\rep_{u}(n)).\qed

7.
Numeration systems for
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
based on periodic points

A numeration system for
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
can be deduced from the numeration system for

\mathbb{Z}
based on a periodic point. Since not all integers are represented by words
of the same length, we propose here a way to pad them to a common length.

Let
\eta:A^{*}\to A^{*}
be a substitution and
u\in\Per(\eta)
with period
p\geq 1

and growing seed.
Let
{\mathtt{W}_{\min}}
and
{\mathtt{W}_{\max}}
be the following minimum and the maximum element
under the
\tail
map with particular parameters:

\displaystyle{\mathtt{W}_{\min}}

\displaystyle=\tail_{\eta,p,u_{0}}(0)=\mathtt{0}^{p},

\displaystyle{\mathtt{W}_{\max}}

\displaystyle=\tail_{\eta,p,u_{-1}}(|\eta^{p}(u_{-1})|-1).

The words
{\mathtt{W}_{\min}}
and
{\mathtt{W}_{\max}}
play the role of neutral words in the
numeration system as illustrated in the next lemma.
Below the words
{\mathtt{W}_{\min}}
and
{\mathtt{W}_{\max}}
are concatenated with others words
from
\mathcal{D}^{*}
using the binary operation
\odot
,
which is not explicitly written to avoid heavy notation.

Lemma 7.1
.

Let
\eta:A^{*}\to A^{*}
be a substitution and

u\in\Per(\eta)
be a two-sided periodic point with growing seed

s=u_{-1}|u_{0}
.
Let
w\in\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{A}_{\eta,s})
. Then

\mathcal{A}_{\eta,s}(w)=\begin{cases}\mathcal{A}_{\eta,s}(\mathtt{0}({\mathtt{W}_{\min}})^{i}v),&\text{ if }w=\mathtt{0}v,\\
\mathcal{A}_{\eta,s}(\mathtt{1}({\mathtt{W}_{\max}})^{i}v),&\text{ if }w=\mathtt{1}v,\end{cases}

for every integer
i\geq 0
.

Proof.

Let
i\geq 0
be an integer
and
p\geq 1
be the period of
u
.
Let
w\in\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{A}_{\eta,s})
.

Suppose that
w
starts with letter
\mathtt{0}
.
Let
v\in\mathcal{D}^{*}
such that
w=\mathtt{0}v
.
We have
\mathcal{A}_{\eta,u_{0}}(\mathtt{0}^{p})=u_{0}
.
Thus
\mathcal{A}_{\eta,s}(\mathtt{0}({\mathtt{W}_{\min}})^{i})=u_{0}
.
From Equation (
7
)
and Equation (
8
) we obtain

\mathcal{A}_{\eta,s}(w)=\mathcal{A}_{\eta,s}(\mathtt{0}v)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{eq:automata-relations}}}{{=}}\mathcal{A}_{\eta,u_{0}}(v)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{eq:automata-relations-2}}}{{=}}\mathcal{A}_{\eta,s}(\mathtt{0}({\mathtt{W}_{\min}})^{i}v).

Suppose that
w
starts with letter
\mathtt{1}
.
Let
v\in\mathcal{D}^{*}
such that
w=\mathtt{1}v
.
We have
\mathcal{A}_{\eta,u_{-1}}({\mathtt{W}_{\max}})=u_{-1}
.
Thus
\mathcal{A}_{\eta,s}(\mathtt{1}({\mathtt{W}_{\max}})^{i})=u_{-1}
.
From Equation (
7
)
and Equation (
8
) we obtain

\mathcal{A}_{\eta,s}(w)=\mathcal{A}_{\eta,s}(\mathtt{1}v)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{eq:automata-relations}}}{{=}}\mathcal{A}_{\eta,u_{-1}}(v)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{eq:automata-relations-2}}}{{=}}\mathcal{A}_{\eta,s}(\mathtt{1}({\mathtt{W}_{\max}})^{i}v).\qed

It is useful to pad words to a certain length using neutral words as follows
using a pad function.
Let
s=u_{-1}|u_{0}
.
Let
w\in\mathcal{L}_{\ell p+1}(\mathcal{A}_{\eta,s})
for some
\ell\in\mathbb{N}
.
Let

such that
t\geq|w|
and

.
We define

\pad_{t}(w)=\begin{cases}\mathtt{0}({\mathtt{W}_{\min}})^{m}v,&\text{ if }w=\mathtt{0}v;\\
\mathtt{1}({\mathtt{W}_{\max}})^{m}v,&\text{ if }w=\mathtt{1}v,\end{cases}

where
m=(t-|w|)/p
.
The padding map can be used to pad words so that they all
have the same length. This allows us to represent coordinates in

\mathbb{Z}^{d}
in dimension
d\geq 1
.

Definition 7.2

(Numeration system for
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
)
.

Let

be a substitution and

For every

\rep_{u}({\boldsymbol{n}})=\left(\begin{array}[]{c}\pad_{t}(\rep_{u_{1}}(n_{1}))\\
\pad_{t}(\rep_{u_{2}}(n_{2}))\\
\dots\\
\pad_{t}(\rep_{u_{d}}(n_{d}))\end{array}\right)\in\{\mathtt{0},\mathtt{1}\}^{d}(\mathcal{D}^{d})^{*},

where
t=\max\{|\rep_{u_{i}}(n_{i})|\colon 1\leq i\leq d\}
.

8.
A Total Order

In this section, we define a total order on
\{\mathtt{0},\mathtt{1}\}\mathcal{D}^{*}:=\{\mathtt{0},\mathtt{1}\}\odot\mathcal{D}^{*}

and we show that
\rep_{u}
is increasing with respect to this order.

The radix order on a language
L\subset\mathcal{D}^{*}
is a total order
(L,<_{rad})

such that
u<_{rad}v
if and only if

|u|<|v|
or
|u|=|v|
and
u<_{lex}v
,
where
<_{lex}
denotes the lexicographic order.
For example, over the alphabet
\{\mathtt{0},\mathtt{1}\}
, the minimum elements for the radix order are:

\varepsilon<_{rad}\mathtt{0}<_{rad}\mathtt{1}<_{rad}\mathtt{0}\mathtt{0}<_{rad}\mathtt{0}\mathtt{1}<_{rad}\mathtt{1}\mathtt{0}<_{rad}\mathtt{1}\mathtt{1}<_{rad}\mathtt{0}\mathtt{0}\mathtt{0}<_{rad}\mathtt{0}\mathtt{0}\mathtt{1}<_{rad}\cdots.

We define the reversed-radix order as a total order such that
u<_{rev}v
if and only if

|u|>|v|
or
|u|=|v|
and
u<_{lex}v
.
For example, over the alphabet
\{\mathtt{0},\mathtt{1}\}
,
the maximum elements for the reverse-radix order are:

\cdots<_{rev}\mathtt{1}\mathtt{1}\mathtt{0}<_{rev}\mathtt{1}\mathtt{1}\mathtt{1}<_{rev}\mathtt{0}\mathtt{0}<_{rev}\mathtt{0}\mathtt{1}<_{rev}\mathtt{1}\mathtt{0}<_{rev}\mathtt{1}\mathtt{1}<_{rev}\mathtt{0}<_{rev}\mathtt{1}<_{rad}\varepsilon.

Let us stress that the reversed-radix order behaves in the same manner
as the radix order on the words of the same length.
Also if
L
has infinite cardinality, then
L
has no maximal element for the radix order
and has no minimum element for the reverse-radix order.

The radix order and the reverse-radix order can be used jointly to define a
total order on a language with no minimum nor maximum element. Below, we use the first letter
of a word in
\{\mathtt{0},\mathtt{1}\}\mathcal{D}^{*}
to split the two cases.

Definition 8.1

(total order
\prec
)
.

For every
u,v\in\{\mathtt{0},\mathtt{1}\}\mathcal{D}^{*}
,
we define
u\prec v
if and only if

•

u\in\mathtt{1}\mathcal{D}^{*}
and
v\in\mathtt{0}\mathcal{D}^{*}
, or

•

u,v\in\mathtt{0}\mathcal{D}^{*}
and
u<_{rad}v
, or

•

u,v\in\mathtt{1}\mathcal{D}^{*}
and
u<_{rev}v
.

Thus, if
\mathcal{D}=\{\mathtt{0},\mathtt{1}\}
, we get

\cdots\prec\mathtt{1}\mathtt{0}\mathtt{0}\prec\mathtt{1}\mathtt{0}\mathtt{1}\prec\mathtt{1}\mathtt{1}\mathtt{0}\prec\mathtt{1}\mathtt{1}\mathtt{1}\prec\mathtt{1}\mathtt{0}\prec\mathtt{1}\mathtt{1}\prec\mathtt{1}\prec\mathtt{0}\prec\mathtt{0}\mathtt{0}\prec\mathtt{0}\mathtt{1}\prec\mathtt{0}\mathtt{0}\mathtt{0}\prec\mathtt{0}\mathtt{0}\mathtt{1}\prec\mathtt{0}\mathtt{1}\mathtt{0}\prec\mathtt{0}\mathtt{1}\mathtt{1}\prec\cdots.

The total order
\prec
makes sense with respect to Dumont–Thomas complement numeration
systems for
\mathbb{Z}
because of the following result.

Proposition 8.2
.

Let
\eta:A^{*}\to A^{*}
be a substitution and

u\in\Per(\eta)
be a two-sided periodic point with growing seed.
The map

\rep_{u}:\mathbb{Z}\to\{\mathtt{0},\mathtt{1}\}\mathcal{D}^{*}

is increasing with respect to the order
\prec
on
\{\mathtt{0},\mathtt{1}\}\mathcal{D}^{*}
.

Proof.

Let
n,n^{\prime}\in\mathbb{Z}
be two integers such that
n<n^{\prime}
.

Assume that
n<0\leq n^{\prime}
.
Then
\rep_{u}(n)\in\mathtt{1}\mathcal{D}^{*}

and
\rep_{u}(n^{\prime})\in\mathtt{0}\mathcal{D}^{*}

so that

\rep_{u}(n)\prec\rep_{u}(n^{\prime})
.

Assume that
0\leq n<n^{\prime}
.
Then
\rep_{u}(n)\in\mathtt{0}\mathcal{D}^{*}

and
\rep_{u}(n^{\prime})\in\mathtt{0}\mathcal{D}^{*}
.
The case
|\rep_{u}(n)|>|\rep_{u}(n^{\prime})|
is impossible.
Indeed, suppose that
|\rep_{u}(n)|=k+1
and
|\rep_{u}(n^{\prime})|=k^{\prime}+1

for some integers
k
and
k^{\prime}
.
If
|\rep_{u}(n)|>|\rep_{u}(n^{\prime})|
, then
k-p\geq k^{\prime}
,
where
p
is the period of
u
.
From Equation (
2
), we have

n^{\prime}<|\eta^{k^{\prime}}(a)|\leq|\eta^{k-p}(a)|\leq n,

a contradiction.
If
|\rep_{u}(n)|<|\rep_{u}(n^{\prime})|
,
then
\rep_{u}(n)\prec\rep_{u}(n^{\prime})
.
Suppose now that
|\rep_{u}(n)|=|\rep_{u}(n^{\prime})|=k+1

for some integer
k
.
From Lemma
3.12
,
we have

\rep_{u}(n)=\mathtt{0}\odot\tail_{\eta,k,u_{0}}(n)<_{lex}\mathtt{0}\odot\tail_{\eta,k,u_{0}}(n^{\prime})=\rep_{u}(n^{\prime}).

Thus
\rep_{u}(n)\prec\rep_{u}(n^{\prime})
.

Assume that
n<n^{\prime}<0
.
Then
\rep_{u}(n)\in\mathtt{1}\mathcal{D}^{*}

and
\rep_{u}(n^{\prime})\in\mathtt{1}\mathcal{D}^{*}
.
The case
|\rep_{u}(n)|<|\rep_{u}(n^{\prime})|
is impossible.
Indeed, suppose that
|\rep_{u}(n)|=k+1
and
|\rep_{u}(n^{\prime})|=k^{\prime}+1

for some integers
k
and
k^{\prime}
.
If
|\rep_{u}(n)|<|\rep_{u}(n^{\prime})|
, then
k^{\prime}-p\geq k
,
where
p
is the period of
u
.
From Equation (
4
), we have

n^{\prime}<-|\eta^{k^{\prime}-p}(b)|\leq-|\eta^{k}(b)|\leq n,

a contradiction.
If
|\rep_{u}(n)|>|\rep_{u}(n^{\prime})|
,
then
\rep_{u}(n)\prec\rep_{u}(n^{\prime})
.
Suppose that
|\rep_{u}(n)|=|\rep_{u}(n^{\prime})|=k+1

for some integer
k
.
From Lemma
3.12
,
we have

\rep_{u}(n)=\mathtt{1}\odot\tail_{\eta,k,u_{-1}}(n)<_{lex}\mathtt{1}\odot\tail_{\eta,k,u_{-1}}(n^{\prime})=\rep_{u}(n^{\prime}).

Thus
\rep_{u}(n)\prec\rep_{u}(n^{\prime})
.
∎

It follows from Proposition
8.2

that
\rep_{u}:\mathbb{Z}\to\{\mathtt{0},\mathtt{1}\}\mathcal{D}^{*}
is injective.
Therefore it is a bijection onto its image.
The next result describes the image of the map
\rep_{u}
.

Lemma 8.3
.

Let
\eta:A^{*}\to A^{*}
be a substitution and

u\in\Per(\eta)
be a two-sided periodic point with growing seed
s=u_{-1}|u_{0}
.
Let
p\geq 1
be the period of
u
.
Then

\rep_{u}(\mathbb{Z})=\bigcup_{\ell\in\mathbb{N}}\mathcal{L}_{\ell p+1}(\mathcal{A}_{\eta,s})\setminus\{\mathtt{0}{\mathtt{W}_{\min}},\mathtt{1}{\mathtt{W}_{\max}}\}\mathcal{D}^{*}.

Proof.

(\subseteq)
.
It follows from Theorem
6.1
that

\rep_{u}(\mathbb{Z})\subset\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{A}_{\eta,s})
.
Also for every
n\in\mathbb{Z}
,
\rep_{u}(n)
is a word of length
\ell p+1

for some
\ell\in\mathbb{N}
.
Thus
\rep_{u}(\mathbb{Z})\subset\bigcup_{\ell\in\mathbb{N}}\mathcal{L}_{\ell p+1}(\mathcal{A}_{\eta,s})
.
It remains to show that

\rep_{u}(\mathbb{Z})\cap\{\mathtt{0}{\mathtt{W}_{\min}},\mathtt{1}{\mathtt{W}_{\max}}\}\mathcal{D}^{*}=\varnothing
.
Suppose by contradiction that there exists
n\in\mathbb{Z}
such that

\rep_{u}(n)\in\mathtt{0}{\mathtt{W}_{\min}}\mathcal{D}^{*}
.
We have

\rep_{u}(n)=\mathtt{0}\odot|m_{k-1}|\odot|m_{k-2}|\odot\ldots\odot|m_{0}|
,
where
k=\ell p
.
Then

|m_{k-1}|\odot\ldots\odot|m_{k-p}|=\mathtt{0}^{p}
, which implies

m_{k-1}m_{k-2}\ldots m_{k-p}=\varepsilon
, thus contradicting
Theorem
4.1
.
On the other hand, suppose by contradiction that there exists
n\in\mathbb{Z}
such that

\rep_{u}(n)\in\mathtt{1}{\mathtt{W}_{\max}}\mathcal{D}^{*}
.
We have

\rep_{u}(n)=\mathtt{1}\odot|m_{k-1}|\odot|m_{k-2}|\odot\ldots\odot|m_{0}|
.
Then

|m_{k-1}|\odot\ldots\odot|m_{k-p}|=\tail_{\eta,p,u_{-1}}(|\eta^{p}(u_{-1})|-1)
,
which implies

\eta^{p-1}(m_{k-1})\eta^{p-2}(m_{k-2})\cdots\eta^{0}(m_{k-p})

is the prefix of
\eta^{p}(u_{-1})
of length
|\eta^{p}(u_{-1})|-1
,
a contradiction with
Theorem
4.2
.

(\supseteq)
.
Let
\ell\in\mathbb{N}
and
k=\ell p
.
Let
v=v_{k-1}\cdots v_{0}

such that
\mathtt{d}\odot v\in\mathcal{L}_{\ell p+1}(\mathcal{A}_{\eta,s})\setminus\{\mathtt{0}{\mathtt{W}_{\min}},\mathtt{1}{\mathtt{W}_{\max}}\}\mathcal{D}^{*}
.

Suppose that
\mathtt{d}=\mathtt{0}
.
We have
v\in\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{A}_{\eta,u_{0}})
.
From Lemma
3.8
,
there exists a
u_{0}
-admissible sequence

(m_{i},a_{i})_{i=0,\dots,k-1}
such that

|m_{i}|=v_{i}
for every
i=0,\dots,k-1
.
Let
n=\sum_{i=0}^{k-1}|\eta^{i}(m_{i})|
.
From Theorem
4.1

and using
v\notin{\mathtt{W}_{\min}}\mathcal{D}^{*}
,
we have
\rep_{u}(n)=\mathtt{0}\odot v
.
Thus
\mathtt{d}\odot v\in\rep_{u}(\mathbb{Z})
.

Suppose that
\mathtt{d}=\mathtt{1}
.
We have
v\in\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{A}_{\eta,u_{-1}})
.
From Lemma
3.8
,
there exists a
u_{-1}
-admissible sequence

(m_{i},a_{i})_{i=0,\dots,k-1}
such that

|m_{i}|=v_{i}
for every
i=0,\dots,k-1
.
Let
n=-|\eta^{k}(u_{-1})|+\sum_{i=0}^{k-1}|\eta^{i}(m_{i})|
.
From Theorem
4.2
,
and using
v\notin{\mathtt{W}_{\max}}\mathcal{D}^{*}
,
we have
\rep_{u}(n)=\mathtt{1}\odot v
.
Thus
\mathtt{d}\odot v\in\rep_{u}(\mathbb{Z})
.
∎

Theorem 8.4
.

Let
\eta:A^{*}\to A^{*}
be a substitution and

.
Let

Let

The following items are equivalent:

•

,

•

is increasing with respect to

, its image is

and

.

Proof.

Suppose that

.

is increasing from Proposition

.
Its image was computed in Lemma

,
Also,
f(0)=\mathtt{0}
from Definition
4.3
.

Let
f:\mathbb{Z}\to\{\mathtt{0},\mathtt{1}\}\mathcal{D}^{*}
.
Suppose
f
is increasing, its image is

.

satisfies the same properties.

such that

,
we conclude that

.
∎

9.2.
Fibonacci analogue of the two’s complement numeration system

In what follows, the Fibonacci sequence

(F_{n})_{n\geq 0}
,

F_{n}=F_{n-1}+F_{n-2}
, for all
n\geq 2
,
is defined with the initial conditions

F_{0}=1
,
F_{1}=2
.
We denote
\mathcal{D}=\{\mathtt{0},\mathtt{1}\}
.

In
[
LL23
]
, a Fibonacci analogue of the two’s complement numeration
system for nonnegative and negative integers was defined
from the value map
\val_{\mathcal{F}c}:\mathcal{D}^{*}\to\mathbb{Z}

by
\val_{\mathcal{F}c}(w)=\sum_{i=0}^{k-1}w_{i}F_{i}-w_{k-1}F_{k}

for every binary word
w=w_{k-1}\cdots w_{0}\in\mathcal{D}^{k}
.
It is an analog of the two’s complement value map
\val_{2c}
,
using Fibonacci numbers instead of powers of 2.
It was proved in
[
LL23
]
that
for every
n\in\mathbb{Z}
there exists a
unique odd-length word

w\in L=\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{D}\mathcal{D})^{*}\setminus\left(\mathcal{D}^{*}11\mathcal{D}^{*}\cup 000\mathcal{D}^{*}\cup 101\mathcal{D}^{*}\right)

such that
n=\val_{\mathcal{F}c}(w)
.
It defines the map

\rep_{\mathcal{F}c}:\mathbb{Z}\to L
by the rule
n\mapsto w
.

We show that the Dumont–Thomas complement numeration system obtained from the two-sided Fibonacci
word is the Fibonacci analogue of the two’s complement numeration system
introduced in
[
LL23
]
.

Proposition 9.2
.

Let
\varphi:a\mapsto ab,b\mapsto a
be the Fibonacci substitution
and let
\gamma\in\Per(\varphi)
be the periodic point of period 2 with
seed

.
Then
\rep_{\gamma}
is the Fibonacci analogue of the two’s complement
numeration system, that is,
\rep_{\gamma}=\rep_{\mathcal{F}c}
.

Proof.

From Proposition
8.2
,

\rep_{\gamma}:\mathbb{Z}\to\{\mathtt{0},\mathtt{1}\}\mathcal{D}^{*}

is an increasing map with respect to the order
\prec
.
Thus, it is an increasing bijection
\mathbb{Z}\to\rep_{\gamma}(\mathbb{Z})
.
Denote

.

8.3
, we have

\displaystyle\rep_{\gamma}(\mathbb{Z})

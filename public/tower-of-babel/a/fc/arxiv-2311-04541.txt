Second-order Perturbative OTOC of Anharmonic Oscillators
Wung-Hong Huang*

Abstract




Abstract

The out-of-time-order correlator (OTOC) of simple harmonic oscillator with extra anharmonic (quartic) interaction are calculated by the second quantization method. We obtain the analytic formulas of spectrum, Fock space states and matrix elements of coordinate to the second order of anharmonic interaction. These relations clearly reveal the property that the correction of the interaction is proportional to the quantum number of the energy level, and shows the enhancement for some physical quantities. The analytic results explicitly show that the OTOC is raising in the quadratic power law at early times. Then, we use the formulas to do numerical summation to calculate the OTOC, which shows that, at late times, while the first-order perturbative OTOC is oscillating as that in a simple harmonic oscillator, the second-order perturbative OTOC saturates to a constant value. We compare it with
2\langle x^{2}\rangle_{T}\langle p^{2}\rangle_{T}
, which is associated with quantum chaotic behavior in systems that exhibit chaos, and discuss the validity of the second-order perturbation.

OTOC in Quantum Theory

2.1
Quantum Mechanic Approach to OTOC

We first review the Hashimoto’s method of calculating OTOC in quantum mechanic model
[
23
]
. For a general time-independent Hamiltonian:
H=H(x_{1},....x_{n},p_{1},....p_{n})
the function of OTOC is defined by

\displaystyle C_{T}(t)=-\langle[x(t),p(0)]^{2}\rangle_{T}

(2.1)

where
\langle{\cal O)}\rangle\ \equiv tr(e^{-\beta H}\,{\cal O)})/tre^{-\beta H}
. Using energy eigenstates
|n\rangle
, defined by

\displaystyle H|n\rangle=E_{n}|n\rangle~~~~~~~~~

(2.2)

as the basis of the Hilbert space, then

\displaystyle\boxed{C_{T}(t)={1\over Z}\sum_{n}e^{-\beta E_{n}}\,c_{n}(t)},~~~c_{n}(t)\equiv-\langle n|[x(t),p(0)]^{2}|n\rangle

(2.3)

To calculate
c_{n}(t)
we can insert the complete set
\sum_{m}|m\rangle\langle m|=1
to find an useful formula

\displaystyle\boxed{c_{n}(t)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\sum_{m}\langle n|[x(t),p(0)]|m\rangle\langle m|[x(t),p(0)]|n\rangle\ \boxed{=\sum_{m}(ib_{nm})(ib_{nm})^{*}}~

(2.4)

\displaystyle b_{nm}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-i\langle n|[x(t),p(0)]|m\rangle,~~b_{nm}^{*}=b_{mn}

(2.5)

To proceed, we substituting a relation
x(t)=e^{iHt/\hbar}\,x\,e^{-iHt/\hbar}
and inserting the completeness relation again, and finally obtain

\displaystyle b_{nm}

\displaystyle\equiv

\displaystyle-i\langle n|x(t),p(0)|m\rangle+i\langle n|p(0)x(t),|m\rangle=-i\langle n|e^{iHt/\hbar}\,x\,e^{-iHt/\hbar}p(0)|m\rangle+i\langle n|p(0)e^{iHt/\hbar}\,x\,e^{-iHt/\hbar}|m\rangle

(2.6)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-i\sum_{k}\langle n|e^{i\ Ht/\hbar}\,x\,e^{-iHt/\hbar}|k\rangle\langle k|p(0)|m\rangle+i\sum_{k}<n|p(0)|k\rangle\langle k|e^{iHt/\hbar}\,x\,e^{-iHt/\hbar}|m\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-i\sum_{k}\Big(e^{iE_{nk}t/\hbar}x_{nk}p_{km}-e^{iE_{km}t/\hbar}p_{nk}x_{km}\Big)

where

\displaystyle E_{nm}=E_{n}-E_{m},~~~x_{nm}=\langle n|x|m\rangle,~~p_{nm}=\langle n|p|m\rangle~~~~

(2.7)

For a Hamiltonian

Simple Harmonic oscillator : Wavefunction Method

Applying above relations and following definitions

to SHO, then, the Hamiltonian

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\hbar\omega\over 2}\Big(aa^{\dagger}+a^{\dagger}a\Big)=\hbar\omega\Big(a^{\dagger}a+\frac{1}{2}\Big)

\displaystyle H|n\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle E_{n}|n\rangle,~~E_{n}=\hbar\omega\left(n+\frac{1}{2}\right),~~~E_{nm}=\hbar\omega(n-m)

Basic relations

\displaystyle\equiv

\displaystyle\langle n|x|m\rangle=\sqrt{\hbar\over 2M\omega}\Big(\sqrt{m}\ \delta_{n,m-1}+\sqrt{m+1}\ \delta_{n,m+1}\Big)

which exactly reproduces (

3.1
Perturbative Energy and State : Formulas

We consider the anharmonic oscillators with quartic interaction

\displaystyle H

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle({p^{2}\over 2M}+{M\omega^{2}\over 2}x^{2})+g{x^{4}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\hbar\omega\Big(a^{\dagger}a+\frac{1}{2}\Big)+{g}\ \Big({\hbar\over 2M\omega}\Big)^{2}(a^{\dagger}+a)^{4}=H^{(0)}+gV

which has a well-known unperturbed solution

\displaystyle H^{(0)}|n^{(0)}\rangle=E^{(0)}_{n}|n^{(0)}\rangle=\hbar\omega\Big(n^{(0)}+{1\over 2}\Big)|n^{(0)}\rangle

(3.2)

The second-order perturbation formulas in quantum mechanics textbook are

\displaystyle{E_{n}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle E^{(0)}_{n}+g\ \langle n^{(0)}|V|n^{(0)}\rangle+g^{2}\sum_{k\neq m}{|\langle k^{(0)}|V|n^{(0)}\rangle|^{2}\over E^{(0)}_{n}-E^{(0)}_{k}}+{\cal O}(g^{3})

(3.3)

\displaystyle{|n\rangle}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle|n^{(0)}\rangle+g\sum_{k\neq n}|k^{(0)}\rangle{\langle k^{(0)}|V|n^{(0)}\rangle\over E^{(0)}_{n}-E^{(0)}_{k}}-{1\over 2}g^{2}\ |n^{(0)}\rangle\sum_{k\neq n}{|\langle k^{(0)}|V|n^{(0)}\rangle|^{2}\over(E^{(0)}_{n}-E^{(0)}_{k})^{2}}

(3.4)

\displaystyle-g^{2}\sum_{k\neq n}|k^{(0)}\rangle{\langle k^{(0)}|V|n^{(0)}\rangle\langle n^{(0)}|V|n^{(0)}\rangle\over(E^{(0)}_{n}-E^{(0)}_{k})^{2}}+g^{2}\sum_{k\neq n}\sum_{\ell\neq n}|k^{(0)}\rangle{\langle k^{(0)}|V|\ell^{(0)}\rangle\over E^{(0)}_{n}-E^{(0)}_{k}}{\langle\ell^{(0)}|V|n^{(0)}\rangle\over E^{(0)}_{n}-E^{(0)}_{\ell}}

\displaystyle+{\cal O}(g^{3})

In the rest of this section we will use above formulas to calculate:

\widetilde{E}
.

2. Perturbative state
\widetilde{|n\rangle}
.

3. Perturbative matrix elements
\widetilde{x_{mn}}

Using these analytic results we can explicitly show that the OTOC is raising in quadratic power law form at early times. Then, we use the formulas to do numerical summation to calculate the OTOC in the next section.

3.2
Perturbative Energy
\widetilde{E}
: Model Calculations

Using above formulas we can calculate the corrections to state and energy by extra potential
V
. From now on we change the notation
\{n^{(0)},E^{(0)},|n^{(0)}\rangle\}\to\{n,E,|n\rangle\}
and notations
\{\widetilde{n},\ \widetilde{E},\ \widetilde{|n\rangle}\}
are used to represent the perturbed quantities. We use the unit :
\hbar=\omega=M=1
and keep the coupling strength
g
as a only free parameter.

A crucial quantity we need, after calculation, is

\displaystyle\langle k|V|n\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\sqrt{n-3}\sqrt{n-2}\sqrt{n-1}\sqrt{n}}{4}\ \delta_{k,n-4}+\frac{\sqrt{n-1}\sqrt{n}\ (2n-1)}{2}\ \delta_{k,n-2}+\frac{3(2n(n+1)+1)}{4}\ \delta_{k,n}

(3.5)

\displaystyle+\frac{\sqrt{n+1}\sqrt{n+2}\ (2n+3)}{2}\ \delta_{k,n+2}+\frac{\sqrt{n+1}\sqrt{n+2}\sqrt{n+3}\sqrt{n+4}}{4}\ \delta_{k,n+4}

Using above result the perturbative energy
\widetilde{E_{n}}
becomes

\displaystyle\widetilde{E_{n}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Big(n+{1\over 2}\Big)+\frac{3g(2n(n+1)+1)}{4}-{g^{2}(2n+1)(17n(n+1)+21)\over 8}+{\cal O}(g^{3})~~

(3.6)

\displaystyle\widetilde{E_{nm}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\widetilde{E_{n}}-\widetilde{E_{m}}

(3.7)

This relation shows an interesting property of “enhancement” which we mention it in below.

In the case of
n>1
above relation leads to

\displaystyle\widetilde{E_{n}}

\displaystyle\approx

\displaystyle\alpha_{0}n+\alpha_{1}gn^{2}+\alpha_{2}g^{2}n^{3}=n\Big(\alpha_{0}+\alpha_{1}\cdot(gn)+\alpha_{2}\cdot(gn)^{2}\Big)~~~~~

(3.8)

and we see that, for example, for small value of

the value of

is larger then
1
if the mode of state

. This leads to a general property of “enhancement” that no matter how small the value of coupling strength
g
, some perturbative quantities could be very large in higher energy levels. Therefore, to make the perturbation reliable, we should keep only low-energy-level systems.

This then limits us to consider systems at low energy temperatures, since the higher energy level states will be suppressed by the Boltzmann factor. The problem of valid of perturbation will be discussed in a later section.

6
Discusions

In this paper we use the method which was set up by Hashimoto recently in
[
23
,
24
,
25
]
to study the OTOC in the quantum harmonic oscillator with extra anharmonic (quartic) interaction. In contrast to the previous studies we calculate the OTOC by second quantization method in perturbative approximation, which can give a few useful analytical formulas.

Some interesting properties could be read from the analytical formulas of perturbative results. For example, using the analytical formulas we clearly see that the interactions can enhance the correlation to very large values over time and is proportional to the energy level. This lead to the rapidly rising characteristic of the initial time of OTOC. The behavior that at the early times OTOC is raising in the quadratic power law is demonstrated without numerical calculations.

On the other hand, the perturbation method of the second quantization method also has some disadvantages, which we mention below :

\bullet
First, the coupling strength shall be small. Therefore, some properties which appear only in strong coupling cannot be studied. This is the general constrain in the perturbation method.

\bullet
Secondly, in the wave function method, the Hamiltonian has to solved step by step to find the solutions of each energy level
n
. The associated microcanonic OTOC is then calculated. Therefore, we can know the properties of the solved levels only up to finite
n
. On the other hand, in the second quantization method, we can know the physical properties of any level
n
, for example
\widetilde{E_{n}}
, and can find the associated microcanonic OTOC. However, as discussed in the previous subsection: Valid of Perturbation, for each coupling
g
there is an associated cutoff level
n_{\text{cut}}
and the computed properties are reliable only for the levels
n<n_{\text{cut}}
. So, essentially only the property of levels up to the finite
n_{\text{cut}}
are useful.

Finally, we mention two further studies relevant to our approach.

\bullet
First, the OTOC of non-linearly coupled oscillators was studied in an interesting paper
[
24
]
. The properties of the early-time exponential growth of OTOC and saturation property at late times, which is shown in systems of quantum chaos, were found. The first-order perturbation investigation in our unpublished paper note
[
44
]
, however did not find either of them. It is expected that the second-order perturbation could exhibit both characteristics. Note that the coupled oscillator has two coordinate, (x,y), and has two associated quantum level number, (
n_{x}
,
n_{y}
), as shown in
[
44
]
. Thus the second-order study of coupled oscillators will be more complicated. However, once we have a few useful analytical formulas, then as has shown in this paper, we can find the analytic property at early times and then get the Lyapunov in an efficient way. It is interesting to study the problem.

\bullet
Secondly, the problem of many-body chaos at weak coupling had been investigated in several years ago by Stanford
[
45
]
, in which the system of matrix
\Phi^{4}
theory was studied. In a recent paper, Kolganov and Trunin study in detail the classical and quantum butterfly effects in related theories
[
46
]
. Since that the interacting scalar field theory could be transformed to a system of coupled oscillators, see for example
[
47
]
, it is interesting to study the problems along the prescription of this paper.

A note on continuous entropy
Roberto Longo

Abstract
Von Neumann entropy has a natural extension to the case of an arbitrary semifinite von Neumann algebra, as was considered by I. E. Segal. We relate this entropy to the relative entropy and show that the entropy increase for an inclusion of von Neumann factors is bounded by the logarithm of the Jones index. The bound is optimal if the factors are infinite dimensional.



1
Introduction

In recent years, there has been much interest in applications of entropy to quantum field theory. For example, a novel argument
[
2
]
for the Zamolodchikov
c
-theorem concerning the irreversibility of renormalization group flow in 2 spacetime dimensions made use of the formal notion of the von Neumann entropy of the density matrix of a quantum field reduced to a local region in spacetime, a double cone. It is difficult to put this argument on a rigorous basis because in quantum field theory the algebra of a double cone region is actually a von Neumann algebra of Type III, and notions such as density matrix and von Neumann entropy are not available for such algebras.

In the physics literature, the non-existence of a notion of entropy for an algebra of Type III is described by saying that in quantum field theory, the entropy of a double cone region (or of any local region in spacetime) is ultraviolet divergent. The nature of the ultraviolet divergence depends on the spacetime dimension. In two dimensions, the divergence is only logarithmic. The argument in
[
2
]
relies on this and involves considering linear combinations of entropies for different double cone regions from which the divergences cancel. Defining rigorously finite linear combination of entropies might be one way to put the argument in
[
2
]
, and others somewhat like it, on a rigorous basis. This would require considerations beyond the von Neumann algebra structure, since the assertion that the ultraviolet divergence of the entropy is logarithmic is special to 2 spacetime dimensions.

Here we will consider a somewhat similar but much simpler situation in which a renormalized notion of entropy is available.
Entropy for an algebra
{\mathcal{A}}
of Type II
1
was first discussed long ago by I. E. Segal
[
19
]
. Segal noted that for a state of
{\mathcal{A}}
, a fairly natural notion of entropy can be defined, with the unusual property that there is a (normalized) state of
{\mathcal{A}}
with maximum entropy, namely the tracial state
\tau
, and no state of minimum entropy. Entropy is then defined to vanish for the tracial state, and therefore, to be negative (or equal to
-\infty
in some cases) for other states.
To see the interpretation of this entropy in terms of renormalization, consider a hyperfinite Type II
1
algebra
{\mathcal{A}}
, which is the large
i
limit of a family of matrix algebras
{\mathcal{M}}_{i}
of dimension
n_{i}^{2}
. The maximum entropy state of
{\mathcal{M}}_{i}
is the tracial state
\tau_{i}
,
with von Neumann entropy
S_{\mathrm{vN}}(\tau_{i})=\log n_{i}
. In the limit
i\to\infty
,
\tau_{i}
converges to
\tau
. If

\varphi_{i}
is a family of states of
{\mathcal{M}}_{i}
that converge for large
i
to a state
\varphi
of
{\mathcal{A}}
, the Type II
1
entropy
S(\varphi)

can be defined as
\lim_{i\to\infty}(S_{\mathrm{vN}}(\varphi_{i})-S_{\mathrm{vN}}(\tau_{i}))=\lim_{i\to\infty}(S_{\mathrm{vN}}(\varphi_{i})-\log n_{i})
. With this definition, it is clear that
\tau
is the maximum entropy state of
{\mathcal{A}}
and has entropy 0. The subtraction of the divergent part
\log n_{i}
is necessary to ensure the existence of a large
i
limit, and makes clear the analogy with renormalized entropy as studied in the physics literature.

A renormalized entropy can also be defined for an algebra
{\mathcal{A}}
of Type II
∞
, with the difference that in this case, because there is no canonical normalization of a tracial weight of
{\mathcal{A}}
, entropy is really only naturally defined up to an additive constant, the same for all states.
At first sight, one might think that because the algebras of local regions in quantum field theory are of Type III, entropy of a Type II algebra would have little application in physics. In fact, this thought is probably one reason that entropy of an algebra of Type II has been relatively little-studied.
However, Type II algebras can appear in black hole physics
[
24
]
and also in certain random matrix models
[
21
]
, so there is indeed some physical motivation to study entropy for Type II.

In this article, we observe that entropy for a state
\varphi
of a Type II algebra can be interpreted as a relative entropy
S(\varphi|\!|\tau)
.
This implies that in a trace-preserving inclusion of Type II algebras
{\mathcal{B}}\subset{\mathcal{A}}
, the entropy of any state increases, cf.
[
19
]
. Our main result is a bound on the entropy increase in terms of the Jones index
[{\mathcal{A}}:{\mathcal{B}}]
.

Vaughan Jones made fundamental contributions in multiple areas of mathematics and mathematical physics. He always was extremely interested in applications of operator algebras to physics. The Jones index of a subfactor was an important tool in his discoveries in von Neumann algebra theory, mathematical physics, and knot theory. We hope therefore that the modest contribution to the theory of the Jones index that we make here would have pleased Vaughan, and we are happy to dedicate this article to his memory.

2
Noncommutative probability spaces

Let
(X,\mu)
be a probability space and
f\in L^{1}(X,\mu)
, with
f>0
almost everywhere, and
\intop\nolimits_{X}fd\mu=1
.
Define the entropy
S(f)
of the random variable
f
by

S(f)=-\intop\nolimits_{X}f\log fd\mu=\intop\nolimits_{X}f\log f^{-1}d\mu\,.

Lemma 2.1
.

S(f)\leq 0
.

Note that
-\log
is a convex function, thus

-\log\Big(\intop\nolimits_{X}f^{-1}d\nu\Big)\leq-\intop\nolimits_{X}\log f^{-1}d\nu

by Jensen inequality, for every probability measure
\nu
on
X
. Therefore, setting
d\nu=fd\mu
, we have

S(f)=\intop\nolimits_{X}f\log f^{-1}d\mu=\intop\nolimits_{X}\log f^{-1}d\nu\leq\log\Big(\intop\nolimits_{X}f^{-1}d\nu\Big)=\log\Big(\intop\nolimits_{X}1d\mu\Big)=0\,.

(1)

\square

Note that

S(f)=-S(\nu|\!|\mu)

where
S(\nu|\!|\mu)
is the relative entropy of the between the states
\mu
and
\nu
on the von Neumann algebra
L^{\infty}(X,\mu)
, indeed

S(\nu|\!|\mu)=\intop\nolimits(\log f-\log 1)d\nu=\intop\nolimits f\log fd\mu\,.

Let now
{\mathcal{A}}
be a finite von Neumann algebra, thus there exists a normal faithful trace
\tau
(
\tau
is unique if
{\mathcal{A}}
is a factor once
\tau
is normalised with
\tau(1)=1
). If
\varphi
is a normal faithful state of
{\mathcal{A}}
, there exists a positive, non singular operator
\rho
affiliated with
{\mathcal{A}}
such that

\varphi(x)=\tau(\rho x)\,,\quad x\in{\mathcal{A}}\,,

(2)

thus
\tau(\rho)=1
.

We define the
entropy

S_{\tau}(\varphi)
of
\varphi
w.r.t.
\tau
as

S_{\tau}(\varphi)=-\tau(\rho\log\rho)\,.

Setting
\tau_{0}=\frac{1}{\tau(1)}\tau
, we have
\varphi=\tau_{0}(\rho_{0}\cdot)
with
\rho_{0}=\tau(1)\rho
, therefore

S_{\tau_{0}}(\varphi)=-\tau_{0}(\rho_{0}\log\rho_{0})=S_{\tau}(\rho)-\tau(1)\log\tau(1)

so we can assume that
\tau
is normalised with
\tau(1)=1
.

Proposition 2.2
.

If
\tau
is normalised, we have

S_{\tau}(\varphi)\leq 0\,,

possibly
S_{\tau}(\varphi)=-\infty
, and
S_{\tau}(\varphi)=0
iff
\varphi=\tau
.

By considering the von Neumann algebra generated by
\rho
, the statement follows by (
1
). Alternatively, the statement is a consequence of the following proposition.

\square

Let
{\mathcal{A}}
be an arbitrary von Neumann algebra and
\varphi,\psi
normal, positive, faithful linear functionals on
{\mathcal{A}}
.
Araki’s relative entropy
[
1
]
is defined by

S(\varphi|\!|\psi)=-(\xi_{\varphi},\log\Delta_{\xi_{\psi},\xi_{\varphi}}\xi_{\varphi})\,,

(3)

where
\xi_{\varphi},\xi_{\psi}
are any cyclic vector representatives of
\varphi,\psi
on the underlying Hilbert space
{\mathcal{H}}
(we may assume that
{\mathcal{A}}
is in a standard form).
Here
\Delta_{\xi_{\varphi},\xi_{\psi}}
is the relative modular operator between
\varphi
and
\psi

[
20
]
, i.e.
\Delta_{\xi_{\varphi},\xi_{\psi}}={\rm S}^{*}_{\xi_{\varphi},\xi_{\psi}}{\rm S}_{\xi_{\varphi},\xi_{\psi}}
with
{\rm S}_{\xi_{\varphi},\xi_{\psi}}
the closure of the map
x\xi_{\psi}\mapsto x^{*}\xi_{\varphi}
,
x\in{\mathcal{A}}
. The right hand side of (
3
) is well defined for all
\varphi,\psi
by

S(\varphi|\!|\psi)=\intop\nolimits_{0}^{\infty}\log s\,d(\xi_{\varphi},e_{s}\xi_{\varphi})\,

with
\Delta_{\xi_{\psi},\xi_{\varphi}}=\intop\nolimits_{0}^{\infty}s\,de_{s}
the spectral resolution of
\Delta_{\xi_{\psi},\xi_{\varphi}}
. Indeed

S(\varphi|\!|\psi)\geq\varphi(1)\big(\log\varphi(1)-\log\psi(1)\big)\,,

possibly
S(\varphi|\!|\psi)=+\infty
. Recall also that, if
\varphi
is a state, then

S(\varphi|\!|\lambda\psi)=S(\varphi|\!|\psi)-\log\lambda\,,\quad\lambda>0\,.

(4)

Note that
S(\varphi|\!|\psi)
can be easily defined also if
\varphi,\psi
are not faithful, see
[
17
]
; for simplicity we mostly consider the faithful case.

Proposition 2.3
.

Let
\tau
be a normal faithful trace on
{\mathcal{A}}
. Then

S_{\tau}(\varphi)=-S(\varphi|\!|\tau)\,,

where
S(\varphi|\!|\tau)
is Araki’s relative entropy (
3
) between
\tau
and
\varphi
on
{\mathcal{A}}
.

The relative modular operator
\Delta_{\xi_{\tau}\xi_{\varphi}}
(w.r.t. vector representatives
\xi_{\varphi},\xi_{\tau}
of
\varphi,\tau
in the natural cone) is equal to
\rho^{-1}
. Therefore

S(\varphi|\!|\tau)=-(\xi_{\varphi},\log\Delta_{\xi_{\tau},\xi_{\varphi}}\xi_{\varphi})=(\xi_{\varphi},\log\rho\,\xi_{\varphi})=\varphi(\log\rho)=\tau(\rho\log\rho)=-S_{\tau}(\varphi)\,.

\square

Due to the above proposition, the entropy
S_{\tau}
depends on the choice of the tracial state
\tau
. However, if
{\mathcal{A}}
is a type II
1
factor, the tracial state is unique.

Since the relative entropy is monotone, we infer that the
S_{\tau}
is monotone.

Corollary 2.4
.

If
{\mathcal{B}}\subset{\mathcal{A}}
is a von Neumann subalgebra and
\tau
a normal faithful trace on
{\mathcal{A}}
. Then

S_{\tau}(\varphi|_{\mathcal{B}})\geq S_{\tau}(\varphi)\,,

where
S_{\tau}(\varphi|_{\mathcal{B}})
is the entropy of the restriction of
\varphi
to
{\mathcal{B}}
w.r.t.
\tau|_{\mathcal{B}}
.

S_{\tau}(\varphi|_{\mathcal{B}})=-S(\varphi|_{\mathcal{B}}|\!|\tau|_{\mathcal{B}})\geq-S(\varphi|\!|\tau)=S_{\tau}(\varphi)\,.

\square

We have the additivity of
S_{\tau}
.

Proposition 2.5
.

Let
{\mathcal{A}}_{i}
be von Neumann algebras with tracial normal faithful states
\tau_{i}
,
i=1,2
, and
\tau=\tau_{1}\otimes\tau_{2}
the trace on
{\mathcal{A}}={\mathcal{A}}_{1}\otimes{\mathcal{A}}_{2}
. Then

S_{\tau}(\varphi_{1}\otimes\varphi_{2})=S_{\tau_{1}}(\varphi_{1})+S_{\tau_{2}}(\varphi_{2})

for any normal faithful states
\varphi_{i}
on
{\mathcal{A}}_{i}
.

S_{\tau}(\varphi_{1}\otimes\varphi_{2})=S(\varphi_{1}\otimes\varphi_{2}|\!|\tau_{1}\otimes\tau_{2})=S(\varphi_{1}|\!|\tau_{1})+S(\varphi_{2}|\!|\tau_{2})=S_{\tau_{1}}(\varphi_{1})+S_{\tau_{2}}(\varphi_{2})\,.

\square

Suppose now
{\mathcal{M}}_{n}
is type
I_{n}
factor, namely
{\mathcal{M}}_{n}
is the
n\times n
matrix algebra. Let
\Tr
be the trace on
{\mathcal{M}}_{n}
, thus
\Tr(1)=n
and

\Tr=n\tau\,,

with
\tau
the normalised trace. With
\varphi
a state on
{\mathcal{M}}_{n}
, the von Neumann entropy of
\varphi
is defined by

S_{\rm vN}(\varphi)=-\Tr(\sigma\log\sigma)\,,

with
\sigma
the density matrix associated with
\varphi
, namely

\varphi(x)=\Tr(\sigma x)\,,\quad x\in{\mathcal{M}}_{n}\,,

(5)

Note that the von Neumann entropy on type I factor is not monotone.

We now compare
S_{\tau}(\varphi)
with
S_{\rm vN}(\varphi)
. With
\rho
and
\sigma
as in (
2
) and (
5
), clearly we have

n\sigma=\rho

thus the following holds as particular case of (
4
).

Proposition 2.6
.

If
\tau
is normalised, we have

S_{\tau}(\varphi)=S_{\rm vN}(\varphi)-\log n\,.

We have

S_{\rm vN}(\varphi)=-\Tr(\sigma\log\sigma)=-n\tau\Big(\frac{\rho}{n}\log\frac{\rho}{n}\Big)=-\tau\Big(\rho\log\frac{\rho}{n}\Big)\\
=-\tau(\rho\log\rho)+\log n=S_{\tau}(\varphi)+\log n\,.

\square

As a consequence, the entropy
S_{\tau}(\varphi)
on
{\mathcal{M}}_{n}
satisfies the bound

-\log n\leq S_{\tau}(\varphi)\leq 0\,;

(6)

S_{\tau}(\varphi)=0
iff
\varphi
is the tracial state
\tau
, while
S_{\tau}(\varphi)=-\log n
iff
\varphi
is a pure state.

Now, the von Neumann entropy is additive:

S_{\rm vN}(\varphi_{1}\otimes\varphi_{2})=S_{\rm vN}(\varphi_{1})+S_{\rm vN}(\varphi_{2})

therefore, if
\varphi_{1}
is a state on
{\mathcal{A}}
and
\varphi_{2}
is a state on
{\mathcal{M}}_{n}
, we have

S_{\tau}(\varphi_{1}\otimes\varphi_{2})=S_{\tau}(\varphi_{1})+S_{\tau}(\varphi_{2})=S_{\tau}(\varphi_{1})+S_{\rm vN}(\varphi_{2})-\log n\,.

Let
{\mathcal{A}}_{1},{\mathcal{A}}_{2}
be von Neumann algebras
\varphi
a normal faithful state on
{\mathcal{A}}={\mathcal{A}}_{1}\otimes{\mathcal{A}}_{2}
and
\psi_{k}
a faithful normal state on
{\mathcal{A}}_{k}
,
k=1,2
. Recall the subadditivity property of the relative entropy
[
17
, Cor. 5.21]
:

S(\varphi|\!|\psi_{1}\otimes\psi_{2})\geq S(\varphi|_{{\mathcal{A}}_{1}}|\!|\psi_{1})+S(\varphi|_{{\mathcal{A}}_{2}}|\!|\psi_{2})\,.

Proposition 2.7
.

Let
{\mathcal{A}}
be a finite von Neumann algebra with faithful normal tracial state
\tau
. If
\varphi
is a faithful state on
{\mathcal{A}}\otimes{\mathcal{M}}_{n}
, then

S_{\tau}(\varphi|_{\mathcal{A}})+S_{\rm vN}(\varphi|_{{\mathcal{M}}_{n}})-S_{\tau}(\varphi)\geq\log n\,.

The equality occurs iff
\varphi=\varphi|_{\mathcal{A}}\otimes\varphi|_{{\mathcal{M}}_{n}}
.

We have

S_{\tau}(\varphi)=-S(\varphi|\!|\tau)=-S(\varphi|\!|\tau_{\mathcal{A}}\otimes\tau_{{\mathcal{M}}_{n}})\leq-S(\varphi|_{\mathcal{A}}|\!|\tau_{\mathcal{A}})-S(\varphi|_{{\mathcal{M}}_{n}}|\!|\tau_{{\mathcal{M}}_{n}})=\\
S_{\tau}(\varphi|_{\mathcal{A}})+S_{\tau}(\varphi|_{{\mathcal{M}}_{n}})=S_{\tau}(\varphi|_{\mathcal{A}})+S_{\rm vN}(\varphi|_{{\mathcal{M}}_{n}})-\log n\,.

\square

3
A bound for the entropy increase

Let
{\mathcal{B}}\subset{\mathcal{A}}
be an inclusion of von Neumann algebras and let
\tau
be a faithful normal tracial state on
{\mathcal{A}}
. In case
{\mathcal{A}}
,
{\mathcal{B}}
are factors, V. Jones defined the index
[{\mathcal{A}}:{\mathcal{B}}]
as the ratio of Murray and von Neumann’s coupling constants. In the more general non factor case,
Pimsner and Popa
[
18
]
gave a probabilistic definition of the index
[{\mathcal{A}}:{\mathcal{B}}]_{\varepsilon}
, with
\varepsilon:{\mathcal{A}}\to{\mathcal{B}}
the trace preserving expectation:

[{\mathcal{A}}:{\mathcal{B}}]_{\varepsilon}=\lambda^{-1}\,,

(7)

with
\lambda\geq 0
the best constant such that

\varepsilon(x)\geq\lambda x

(8)

for all positive
x\in{\mathcal{A}}
. The above inequality is called the Pimsner-Popa inequality.

If
{\mathcal{A}}
and
{\mathcal{B}}
are II
1
factors and
\varepsilon
is the unique trace preserving conditional expectation
\varepsilon:{\mathcal{A}}\to{\mathcal{B}}
, then

[{\mathcal{A}}:{\mathcal{B}}]_{\varepsilon}=\text{Jones index of ${\mathcal{B}}\subset{\mathcal{A}}$}\,.

We shall later comment on the case
{\mathcal{A}}
is not of type II
1
.

Let now
{\mathcal{B}}\subset{\mathcal{A}}
be an arbitrary inclusion of von Neumann algebras with a normal faithful conditional expectation
\varepsilon:{\mathcal{A}}\to{\mathcal{B}}
.
Given normal states
\varphi
and
\psi
on
{\mathcal{A}}
, recall the formula
[
17
, Thm. 5.15]
for the relative entropy

S(\varphi|\!|\psi\cdot\varepsilon)=S(\varphi|_{\mathcal{B}}|\!|\psi|_{\mathcal{B}})+S(\varphi|\!|\varphi\cdot\varepsilon)\,.

(9)

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
{\mathcal{B}}\subset{\mathcal{A}}
be an inclusion of von Neumann algebras and
\varepsilon:{\mathcal{A}}\to{\mathcal{B}}
a finite index normal conditional expectation. Then

S(\varphi|\!|\varphi\cdot\varepsilon)\leq\log[{\mathcal{A}}:{\mathcal{B}}]_{\varepsilon}

for every faithful normal state of
{\mathcal{A}}
.

As
\varepsilon(x)\geq\lambda x
, for all positive
x\in{\mathcal{A}}
, with
\lambda
the inverse of the index, we have

\varphi\cdot\varepsilon\geq\lambda\varphi

and this implies

S(\varphi|\!|\varphi\cdot\varepsilon)\leq S(\varphi|\!|\lambda\varphi)=-\log\lambda\,,

(10)

where the first inequality follows by Corollary
4.3
and the equality
S(\varphi|\!|\lambda\varphi)=-\log\lambda
is a particular case of (
4
).

\square

Proposition 3.2
.

Let
{\mathcal{B}}\subset{\mathcal{A}}
be an inclusion of von Neumann algebras,
\tau
a finite normal faithful trace on
{\mathcal{A}}
and
\varepsilon:{\mathcal{A}}\to{\mathcal{B}}
the trace preserving expectation. For every normal faithful state
\varphi
on
{\mathcal{A}}
,
we have

S_{\tau|_{\mathcal{B}}}(\varphi|_{\mathcal{B}})-S_{\tau}(\varphi)\leq\log[{\mathcal{A}}:{\mathcal{B}}]_{\varepsilon}\,,

(11)

where
[{\mathcal{A}}:{\mathcal{B}}]_{\varepsilon}
is the index w.r.t.
\varepsilon
.

Taking
\psi=\tau
in formula (
9
), we have

S(\varphi|\!|\tau)=S(\varphi|_{\mathcal{B}}|\!|\tau|_{\mathcal{B}})+S(\varphi|\!|\varphi\cdot\varepsilon)\,,

(12)

namely

S_{\tau|_{\mathcal{B}}}(\varphi|_{\mathcal{B}})-S_{\tau}(\varphi)=S(\varphi|\!|\varphi\cdot\varepsilon)\,.

As by Lemma
3.1

S(\varphi|\!|\varphi\cdot\varepsilon)\leq S(\varphi|\!|\lambda\varphi)=-\log\lambda=\log[{\mathcal{A}}:{\mathcal{B}}]_{\varepsilon}\,,

the bound (
11
) follows.

\square

It is known that if
{\mathcal{A}}
is infinite-dimensional, then the quantity
[{\mathcal{A}}:{\mathcal{B}}]_{\varepsilon}
as we have defined is
equal to the Jones index
[{\mathcal{A}}:{\mathcal{B}}]
. For finite-dimensional
{\mathcal{A}}
, this is actually not the case. For example, if

{\mathcal{A}}={\mathcal{M}}_{n}
,
{\mathcal{B}}={\mathbb{C}}
, then
[{\mathcal{A}}:{\mathcal{B}}]=n^{2}
but
[{\mathcal{A}}:{\mathcal{B}}]_{\varepsilon}=n
.
The definition of
[{\mathcal{A}}:{\mathcal{B}}]_{\varepsilon}
can be modified as follows to coincide with the Jones index
[{\mathcal{A}}:{\mathcal{B}}]
in all cases:
[{\mathcal{A}}:{\mathcal{B}}]
is the inverse of the largest constant
\lambda
such that
\varepsilon-\lambda\!\cdot\!{\rm id}
is completely positive. However, except in the case that
{\mathcal{A}}
is finite-dimensional,
\varepsilon-\lambda\!\cdot\!{\rm id}
is completely positive if and only if it is positive, and this refinement is unnecessary.

We conclude by providing a bound as in Prop.
3.2
for the increase of
S(\tau|\!|\varphi)
.
Note that, with
{\mathcal{A}}
,
\tau
,
\varphi
as in (
2
), we have

S(\tau|\!|\varphi)=-\tau(\log\rho)\geq 0\,,

similarly as in (
1
).

Proposition 3.3
.

With the notations in Prop.
3.2
, we hav

S(\tau|\!|\varphi)-S(\tau|_{\mathcal{B}}|\!|\varphi|_{\mathcal{B}})\leq\log[{\mathcal{A}}:{\mathcal{B}}]_{\varepsilon}\,.

Clearly

\varphi|_{\mathcal{B}}=\tau\big(\varepsilon(\rho)\,\cdot\,\big)\,,

and
\varepsilon(\rho)\geq\lambda\rho
by the Pimsner-Popa inequality. So

\log\big(\varepsilon(\rho)\big)\geq\log(\lambda\rho)=\log\rho+\log\lambda

because the logarithm is an operator monotone function. Therefore

S(\tau|\!|\varphi)-S(\tau|_{\mathcal{B}}|\!|\varphi|_{\mathcal{B}})=-\tau\big(\log(\varepsilon(\rho)\big)+\tau(\log\rho)\leq-\log\lambda=\log[{\mathcal{A}}:{\mathcal{B}}]_{\varepsilon}\,.

\square

It would be to interesting estimate
\sup_{\varphi}S(\varphi\cdot\varepsilon|\!|\varphi)
too.

Note that, by the argument in the proof of Theorem
5.4
, if
{\mathcal{A}}_{i}
and
{\mathcal{B}}_{i}
are increasing sequences of matrix subalgebras of dimension
n_{i}^{2}
and
m_{i}^{2}
such that
\cup_{i}{\mathcal{A}}_{i}
,
\cup_{i}{\mathcal{B}}_{i}
are weakly dense in
{\mathcal{A}}
,
{\mathcal{B}}
, then

S_{\tau|_{{\mathcal{B}}}}(\varphi|_{{\mathcal{B}}})-S_{\tau}(\varphi)=\lim_{i}\big(S_{\tau_{i}|_{{\mathcal{B}}_{i}}}(\varphi_{i}|_{{\mathcal{B}}_{i}})-S_{\tau_{i}}(\varphi_{i})\big)=\lim_{i}\big(S_{\rm vN}(\varphi_{i}|_{{\mathcal{B}}_{i}})-S_{\rm vN}(\varphi_{i})+\log(n_{i}/m_{i})\big)\,.

Furthermore, if
{\mathcal{B}}_{i}\subset{\mathcal{A}}_{i}
and
\varepsilon_{{\mathcal{B}}_{i+1}}\varepsilon_{{\mathcal{A}}_{i}}=\varepsilon_{{\mathcal{B}}_{i}}
, where
\varepsilon_{{\mathcal{A}}_{i}}
,
\varepsilon_{{\mathcal{B}}_{i}}
denote the trace preserving expectations onto
{\mathcal{A}}_{i}
,
{\mathcal{B}}_{i}
, then by
[
18
, Prop. 2.6]
we have

[{\mathcal{A}}:{\mathcal{B}}]_{\varepsilon}=\lim_{i}[{\mathcal{A}}_{i}:{\mathcal{B}}_{i}]=\lim_{i}n^{2}_{i}/m^{2}_{i}\,,

therefore

S_{\tau|_{{\mathcal{B}}}}(\varphi|_{{\mathcal{B}}})-S_{\tau}(\varphi)=\lim_{i}\big(S_{\rm vN}(\varphi_{i}|_{{\mathcal{B}}_{i}})-S_{\rm vN}(\varphi_{i})\big)+\frac{1}{2}\log[{\mathcal{A}}:{\mathcal{B}}]_{\varepsilon}\,.

4
State/weight relative entropy

Let
{\mathcal{A}}
be a von Neumann algebra and
\varphi,\psi
positive, normal, linear functionals on
{\mathcal{A}}
. We recall Kosaki’s variational formula
[
11
]
. Fix any
∗
-strongly dense linear subspace
V
of
{\mathcal{A}}
containing the identity. Then

S(\varphi|\!|\psi)=\sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\sup_{x\in\mathfrak{V}}\left\{\varphi(1)\log n-\intop\nolimits_{1/n}^{\infty}\big(\varphi(y(t)^{*}y(t))+t^{-1}\psi(x(t)x(t)^{*})\Big)\frac{dt}{t}\right\}\,,

(13)

where
\mathfrak{V}
is the set of all step functions
x:(1/n,\infty)\to V
with finite range, and
x(t)+y(t)=1
.
The advantage of Kosaki’s formula is that it has all main properties built in it.

Let now
\varphi
be a positive, normal, faithful linear functional on
{\mathcal{A}}
and
\psi
a normal, faithful, semifinite weight on
{\mathcal{A}}
, see
[
20
, Chapter VII]
(semifinite means that the definition domain of
\psi
is weakly dense in
{\mathcal{A}}
). We may assume that
{\mathcal{A}}
acts standardly on the GNS Hilbert space
{\mathcal{H}}_{\varphi}
of
\varphi
. Let
\varphi^{\prime}
be the normal, faithful, positive linear functional on the commutant
{\mathcal{A}}^{\prime}
of
{\mathcal{A}}
given by

\varphi^{\prime}=(\xi_{\varphi},\cdot\,\xi_{\varphi})\,,

(14)

where
\xi_{\varphi}\in{\mathcal{H}}_{\varphi}
is the GNS vector. Let
d\psi/d\varphi^{\prime}
be Connes’ spatial derivative between
\psi
and
\varphi^{\prime}

[
3
]
.

We define the
relative entropy between
\varphi
and
\psi
by

S(\varphi|\!|\psi)=-(\xi_{\varphi},\log(d\psi/d\varphi^{\prime})\,\xi_{\varphi})\,,

(15)

provided the above formula is well defined; this is the case, in particular, if
\xi_{\varphi}
belongs to the domain of
\log(d\psi/d\varphi^{\prime})
. More generally, let
d\psi/d\varphi^{\prime}=\intop\nolimits_{0}^{\infty}s\,d{e_{s}}
be the spectral resolution of
d\psi/d\varphi^{\prime}
, then

S(\varphi|\!|\psi)=-\intop\nolimits_{0}^{\infty}\log s\,d(\xi_{\varphi},e_{s}\xi_{\varphi})

(16)

provided either the positive or the negative part of
\log s
belongs to

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
{\mathcal{A}}
be a von Neumann algebra,
\varphi
a positive, normal, faithful linear functional on
{\mathcal{A}}

{\mathcal{A}}

are well defined,
then

\psi_{1}\leq\psi_{2}\implies S(\varphi|\!|\psi_{1})\geq S(\varphi|\!|\psi_{2})\,.

We have
\psi_{1}\leq\psi_{2}\implies d\psi_{1}/d\varphi^{\prime}\leq d\psi_{2}/d\varphi^{\prime}

[
20
, Prop. 3.10]
. On the other hand,

\psi_{1}\leq\psi_{2}\implies d\varphi^{\prime}/d\psi_{1}\geq d\varphi^{\prime}/d\psi_{2}\implies\log(d\varphi^{\prime}/d\psi_{1})\geq\log(d\varphi^{\prime}/d\psi_{2})

(\xi,\log(d\varphi^{\prime}/d\psi_{1}\xi)\geq(\xi,\log(d\varphi^{\prime}/d\psi_{2}\xi)

and
\log(d\varphi^{\prime}/d\psi_{2})

\square

.

5
The bound in the semifinite case

Let
{\mathcal{A}}
be a von Neumann algebra and
\tau
a normal, faithful, semifinite trace on
{\mathcal{A}}
. With

a faithful, normal state on
{\mathcal{A}}

\rho

(density matrix) such that

\varphi(x)=\tau(\rho^{1/2}x\rho^{1/2})

The entropy

is defined by

S_{\tau}(\varphi)=-\tau(\rho\log\rho)\,,

(19)

provided

is well defined with

\tau(x_{+})<\infty
or
-\tau(x_{-})<\infty
, where

is not defined for every normal state
\varphi
. We shall say that

S_{\tau}(f)
is finite if both

and
\tau(x_{-})
are finite, namely
\tau(|\rho\log\rho|)<\infty
.

Note that, even if

is a type II
∞

depends on the choice of the trace

\tau
, due to the relation

S_{\lambda\tau}(\varphi)=S_{\tau}(\varphi)+\log\lambda\,,\quad\lambda>0\,.

(20)

The case
\tau
is unbounded shows important differences with the case
\tau
is bounded and
the notion of entropy
S_{\tau}(\varphi)
needs care. If
{\mathcal{A}}=L^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}},dt)
and
\tau
is the Lebesgue integral, the state

is given by the integral with a positive density function

and

is the

of

introduced by Shannon. The differential entropy is neither positive nor negative definite. Moreover, it is not the limit of the discrete entropy under a discrete approximation, indeed one needs a logarithmic rescaling, see
[
4
, Chapter 8]

\square

Theorem 5.4
.

Let
{\mathcal{A}}
be a von Neumann algebra with a normal, faithful, semifinite trace
\tau
,

a von Neumann subalgebra such that
\tau|_{\mathcal{B}}
is semifinite and
\varepsilon:{\mathcal{A}}\to{\mathcal{B}}
the trace preserving expectation. If
\varphi
is a normal faithful state on
{\mathcal{A}}
such that
S_{\tau}(\varphi)
is finite, then

S_{\tau}(\varphi|_{\mathcal{B}})
is finite too and we have

S_{\tau}(\varphi|_{\mathcal{B}})-S_{\tau}(\varphi)\leq\log[{\mathcal{A}}:{\mathcal{B}}]_{\varepsilon}\,,

(22)

where
[{\mathcal{A}}:{\mathcal{B}}]_{\varepsilon}
is the index w.r.t.
\varepsilon
and
S_{\tau}(\varphi|_{\mathcal{B}})\equiv S_{\tau|_{\mathcal{B}}}(\varphi|_{\mathcal{B}})
.

Fix the state
\varphi
.
By Lemma
5.1
, there exists a sequence of positive linear functionals
\psi_{k}
that give a bounded entropy approximation for
\tau
w.r.t.
\varphi

Thus
S(\varphi|\!|\psi_{k})\searrow S(\varphi|\!|\psi)
and

is finite for large

S(\varphi|_{\mathcal{B}}|\!|\psi_{k}|_{\mathcal{B}})
is finite too for large

Now,
\psi_{k}(x)\nearrow\tau(x)
for all positive

, therefore
\psi_{k}(\varepsilon(x))\nearrow\tau(\varepsilon(x))=\tau(x)
for all positive

is finite for large

By Lemma

\square

We end up this section by pointing out that the entropy of a state in a semifinite factor
{\mathcal{A}}
depends only on the approximate inner equivalence. Namely,

\varphi_{1}\sim\varphi_{2}\implies S_{\tau}(\varphi_{1})=S_{\tau}(\varphi_{2})\,,

where

means that the norm closed orbit by inner automorphisms in the predual

of

generated by

and

are the same; namely
\varphi_{2}
belongs to the norm closure of

, where
{\hbox{\rm Ad}}u
denotes the inner automorphism of
{\mathcal{A}}
implemented by the unitary

.
This follows because
\varphi_{1}\sim\varphi_{2}
iff
the trace spectral density on the spectral family of the density matrices
\rho_{i}
of
\varphi_{i}
coincide,
i=1,2
,
[
9
, Lemma 4.3]
. Thus,
in this case, if
\rho_{i}=\intop\nolimits_{0}^{\infty}\lambda de_{i,\lambda}
is the spectral resolution of
\varphi_{i}
, we have
\tau(e_{1,\lambda})=\tau(e_{2,\lambda})
so that

-\tau(\rho_{i}\log\rho_{i})=-\intop\nolimits_{0}^{\infty}\lambda\log\lambda\,d\tau(e_{i,\lambda})

is independent of

(assuming the entropy is well defined).

\varphi\cdot\varepsilon=\varphi
; therefore
S_{\mathcal{A}}(\varphi|\!|\varphi\cdot\varepsilon)=0
.
With
\xi_{\varphi}
and
\varphi^{\prime}
as above, eq. (
24
) gives

S_{{\mathcal{B}}^{\prime}}(\varphi^{\prime}|\!|\varphi^{\prime}\cdot\varepsilon^{\prime})=\log[{\mathcal{A}}:{\mathcal{B}}]_{\varepsilon}

showing that the bound
\log[{\mathcal{A}}:{\mathcal{B}}]_{\varepsilon}=\log[{\mathcal{A}}^{\prime}:{\mathcal{B}}^{\prime}]_{\varepsilon^{\prime}}
in (
25
) is optimal for the dual inclusion
{\mathcal{A}}^{\prime}\subset{\mathcal{B}}^{\prime}
with dual expectation
\varepsilon^{\prime}
. So the proposition follows by Lemma
6.1
.

\square

In order eq. (
24
) to hold,
{\mathcal{A}}
was assumed to be in standard form.
If
{\mathcal{A}}
is finite dimensional, (
25
) does not hold in general with
[{\mathcal{A}}:{\mathcal{B}}]_{\varepsilon}
defined in (
23
) according to Kosaki. Indeed, we have
S_{\mathcal{A}}(\varphi|\!|\varphi\cdot\varepsilon)\leq-\log\lambda
, with
\lambda
the Pimsner-Popa bound in (
8
), and in this case
\lambda^{-1}
may be strictly less than Kosaki’s index.

Let
{\mathcal{A}}
be a semifinite factor and
{\mathcal{B}}\subset{\mathcal{A}}
a subfactor. If the index
{\mathcal{B}}\subset{\mathcal{A}}
is finite (w.r.t. some expectation), then
{\mathcal{B}}
is semifinite too. In this case, the trace
\tau
of
{\mathcal{A}}
has a semifinite restriction to
{\mathcal{B}}
and
[{\mathcal{A}}:{\mathcal{B}}]_{\varepsilon}<\infty
, with
\varepsilon
the
\tau
-preserving expectation, see (
21
) and
[
14
]
.

Corollary 6.3
.

Let
{\mathcal{A}}
be a semi-finite, infinite dimensional factor. If
{\mathcal{B}}\subset{\mathcal{A}}
is a finite index subfactor and
\varepsilon:{\mathcal{A}}\to{\mathcal{B}}
the expectation preserving the trace
\tau
, then

\sup_{\varphi}\big\{S_{\tau}(\varphi|_{\mathcal{B}})-S_{\tau}(\varphi)\big\}=\log[{\mathcal{A}}:{\mathcal{B}}]_{\varepsilon}\,,

where the supremum is taken over all normal states
\varphi
of
{\mathcal{A}}
such that
S_{\tau}(\varphi)
and
S_{\tau}(\varphi|_{\mathcal{B}})
are finite.

Suppose first that the trace
\tau
is bounded. Then eq. (
12
) in the proof of Prop.
3.2
shows that
S_{\tau}(\varphi|_{\mathcal{B}})-S_{\tau}(\varphi)=\log[{\mathcal{A}}:{\mathcal{B}}]_{\varepsilon}

if
\varphi
is a maximum point in eq. (
25
).

If
\tau
is unbounded, we consider again a state
\varphi
of
{\mathcal{A}}
such that
S_{\mathcal{A}}(\varphi|\!|\varphi\cdot\varepsilon)=\log[{\mathcal{A}}:{\mathcal{B}}]_{\varepsilon}
.
We take a sequence of bounded entropy approximation functionals
\psi_{k}
for
\tau
w.r.t.
\varphi
with
\psi_{k}\cdot\varepsilon=\psi_{k}
as in the proof of Theorem
5.4
.
As
S(\varphi|\!|\psi_{k})
is finite, also
S(\varphi_{\mathcal{B}}|\!|\psi_{k}|_{\mathcal{B}})

is finite by Lemma
4.4
. Therefore
\psi_{k}|_{\mathcal{B}}
is a sequence of bounded entropy approximation for
\tau|_{\mathcal{B}}
w.r.t.
\psi_{k}|_{\mathcal{B}}
.
By Lemma
4.2
, we so have

S(\varphi|\!|\psi_{k})\to S(\varphi|\!|\tau)\,,\quad S(\varphi|_{\mathcal{B}}|\!|\psi_{k}|_{\mathcal{B}})\to S(\varphi|_{\mathcal{B}}|\!|\tau|_{\mathcal{B}})\,.

By eq. (
9
), we have

S(\varphi|\!|\psi_{k})-S(\varphi|_{\mathcal{B}}|\!|\psi_{k}|_{\mathcal{B}})=S(\varphi|\!|\psi_{k}\cdot\varepsilon)-S(\varphi|_{\mathcal{B}}|\!|\psi_{k}|_{\mathcal{B}})=S(\varphi|\!|\varphi\cdot\varepsilon)=\log[{\mathcal{A}}:{\mathcal{B}}]_{\varepsilon}\,,

thus

S(\varphi|\!|\tau)-S(\varphi|_{\mathcal{B}}|\!|\tau|_{\mathcal{B}})=\lim_{k}\big(S(\varphi|\!|\psi_{k})-S(\varphi|_{\mathcal{B}}|\!|\psi_{k}|_{\mathcal{B}})\big)=\log[{\mathcal{A}}:{\mathcal{B}}]_{\varepsilon}\,,

that is
S_{\tau}(\varphi|_{\mathcal{B}})-S_{\tau}(\varphi)=\log[{\mathcal{A}}:{\mathcal{B}}]_{\varepsilon}
due to the identity (
17
).

\square

More generally, let
{\mathcal{B}}\subset{\mathcal{A}}
be an inclusion of von Neumann algebras with a finite index expectation
\varepsilon:{\mathcal{A}}\to{\mathcal{B}}
. We shall say that
\varepsilon
has scalar index
[{\mathcal{A}}:{\mathcal{B}}]_{\varepsilon}
if eq. (
23
) holds for a scalar
[{\mathcal{A}}:{\mathcal{B}}]_{\varepsilon}
. This is the case if the centers of
{\mathcal{A}}
and
{\mathcal{B}}
are finite dimensional and have trivial intersection, with
\varepsilon
the minimal expectation
[
6
]
.

The identity (
24
) still holds in this case, by the same proof. It follows that Corollary
6.3
remains true if
{\mathcal{B}}\subset{\mathcal{A}}
is an inclusion of properly infinite von Neumann algebras which has finite scalar index
[{\mathcal{A}}:{\mathcal{B}}]_{\varepsilon}
.

7
Further comments

Structures in the physical literature (see
[
5
,
7
]
) suggest to consider the entropy relative to a linear subspace, not only relative to an algebra. We consider such a notion and a few comments.

Let
{\mathcal{A}}
be a von Neumann algebra and
\varphi,\psi
positive, normal, linear functionals on
{\mathcal{A}}
. Given a linear subspace
V\subset{\mathcal{A}}
containing the identity, we set

S_{V}(\varphi|\!|\psi)=\sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\sup_{x\in\mathfrak{V}}\left\{\varphi(1)\log n-\intop\nolimits_{1/n}^{\infty}\big(\varphi(y(t)^{*}y(t))+t^{-1}\psi(x(t)x(t)^{*})\Big)\frac{dt}{t}\right\}\,,

(26)

where
\mathfrak{V}
is the set of all step functions
x:(0,\infty)\to V
with finite range, and
x(t)+y(t)=1
.

If
V
is
∗
strongly dense in
{\mathcal{A}}
, this is of course Kosaki’s formula (
13
) for the relative entropy; namely

S_{V}(\varphi|\!|\psi)=S_{\mathcal{A}}(\varphi|\!|\psi)\,.

We list the following basic properties of
S_{V}
, whose proof is immediate.
{\mathcal{A}}
is a von Neumann algebra,
\varphi,\psi,\phi
normal, positive linear functionals on
{\mathcal{A}}
and
V\subset{\mathcal{A}}
a unital linear space.

a)

\phi\leq\psi
implies
S_{V}(\varphi|\!|\phi)\geq S_{V}(\varphi|\!|\psi)
.

b)

S_{\bar{V}}(\varphi|\!|\psi)=S_{V}(\varphi|\!|\psi)
with
\bar{V}
the
∗
strong closure of
V
.

c)

Monotonicity. If
W\subset V
is a unital linear subspaces, then
S_{W}(\varphi|\!|\psi)\leq S_{V}(\varphi|\!|\psi)
.

d)

Martingale convergence. Let
V_{i}\subset{\mathcal{A}}
be an increasing net of unital linear subspaces with
V\equiv\cup_{i}V_{i}
. Then
S_{V_{i}}(\varphi|\!|\psi)\nearrow S_{V}(\varphi|\!|\psi)
.

If now
\varphi
is a positive, normal, linear functional and
\psi
a normal, semifinite, faithful weight on
{\mathcal{A}}
, we set

S_{V}(\varphi|\!|\psi)=\inf_{\phi\leq\psi}S_{V}(\varphi|\!|\phi)\,,

(27)

where the infimum is taken over the set
{\mathcal{P}}_{\psi}
of all positive, normal, linear functionals
\phi
on
{\mathcal{A}}
such that
\phi\leq\psi
. We recall that

\psi(x)=\sup\big\{\phi(x):\phi\in{\mathcal{P}}_{\psi}\big\}\,,\quad\text{for all positive}\ x\in{\mathcal{A}}\,,

[
20
, Thm. 1.11]
.

Suppose that
\tau
is a semifinite, faithful normal trace on
{\mathcal{A}}
and
\rho
is the density matrix of
\varphi
w.r.t.
\tau
. Recall that

S_{\tau}(\varphi)=-S_{\mathcal{A}}(\varphi|\!|\tau)\,,

(28)

provided the
S_{\tau}(\varphi)=-\tau(\rho\log\rho)
is well defined. We may define
S_{\tau}(\varphi)
for all states by the above formula with the right hand side given by (
27
) with
V={\mathcal{A}}
.

If
V\subset{\mathcal{A}}
is a linear subspace containing the identity as above, we then set

S_{\tau,V}(\varphi)\equiv-S_{V}(\varphi|\!|\tau)\,.

If
W\subset V
is a unital linear subspace, it follows from
c)
above that

W\subset V\implies S_{V}(\varphi|\!|\tau)\leq S_{W}(\varphi|\!|\tau)\,,

therefore the monotonicity property holds for
S_{\tau,V}(\varphi)
:

S_{\tau,V}(\varphi)\leq S_{\tau,W}(\varphi)\,,

in particular
S_{\tau,{\mathcal{A}}}(\varphi)\leq S_{\tau,V}(\varphi)
.

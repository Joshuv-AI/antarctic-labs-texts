Scattered wavefield in the stochastic homogenization regime
[

Abstract
In the context of providing a mathematical framework for the propagation of ultrasound waves in a random multiscale medium, we consider the scattering of classical waves (modeled by a divergence form scalar Helmholtz equation) by a bounded object with a random composite micro-structure embedded in an unbounded homogeneous background medium. Using quantitative stochastic homogenization techniques, we provide asymptotic expansions of the scattered field in the background medium with respect to a scaling parameter describing the spatial random oscillations of the micro-structure. Introducing a boundary layer corrector to compensate the breakdown of stationarity assumptions at the boundary of the scattering medium, we prove quantitative L 2 L^{2} - and H 1 H^{1} - error estimates for the asymptotic first-order expansion. The theoretical results are supported by numerical experiments.

Introduction and context

The emergence of quantitative medical imaging techniques that can map the numerical value of a physical parameter in a biological tissue constitutes a major shift of paradigm for the theory of inverse problems. Imaging modalities are now expected not only to produce images that are anatomically accurate (structural images) but also stably and quantitatively reconstruct parameters of interest that can help discriminate pathological states.

Medical ultrasound imaging is a powerful, safe, portable and cheap imaging modality that is used in countless physical exams. Ultrasonic pulses (in the MHz range) are transmitted into the region of interest and the images are obtained by numerically backpropagating the echoes generated by the tissues and recorded on a receiver array. Each tissue and its pathological state will be characterized by a distinct type of speckle on the image.

The technique relies on the fact that most soft tissues have a mass density and compressibility close to those of water (and ultrasonic waves travel in these tissues almost as in water) yet have echogenic properties that can be explained by the presence of acoustic heterogeneities of characteristic size much smaller than the wavelength, see
[
1
]
.

The quantification of these echogenic properties (known in the literature as
backscattering coefficient estimation

[
2
]
) relied until now on the introduction of an
ultrasonic reflectivity

[
3
]
and approximations of the scattered field derived under a set of restrictive hypotheses that do not hold in many practical situations (usually assumptions of the low scatterer concentration, single scattering regime, strictly homogeneous mass density in the medium, uniformity of the excitation beam …
[
4
]
). Recently, using a formal approach based on a separation of scale in the scattering process, Aubry
\&
al. have recently obtained spectacular results in quantitative speed of sound imaging on experimental data
[
5
]
.

In this paper, we aim at providing a mathematical framework for the propagation of ultrasound waves in random multiscale media. Using the tools of
stochastic homogenization
, we provide a mathematical model for the acoustic properties of a soft tissue as well as
quantitative
asymptotic expansions of the scattered field with respect to the scale of the acoustic heterogeneities in the medium.

Main contribution

In this paper, we are interested in the scattering of classical waves by a bounded object with a random composite micro-structure embedded in an unbounded homogeneous background medium. The problem considered is modeled by a divergence form scalar Helmholtz equation with discontinuous rapidly oscillating (at some scale
\varepsilon

\fint_{\square_{0}}\sigma_{i,jm}=\fint_{\square_{0}}\beta_{i}=0.

(20)

Furthermore,
\sigma_{i}
is skew-symmetric and verifies a.s.

\nabla\cdot\sigma_{i}=q_{i}

(21)

where for
i,j\in[\![1,d]\!]
,

(\nabla\cdot\sigma_{i})_{j}:=\sum_{m=1}^{d}\partial_{m}\sigma_{i,jm}

(22)

and
\beta
verifies a.s.

\nabla\cdot\beta=n-n^{hom}.

(23)

(b)

\nabla\beta_{i}
and
\nabla\sigma_{i,jm}
are stationary, have finite second moments and vanishing expectation.

\sigma
is the classical extended corrector in stochastic homogenization of the operator
-\nabla\cdot a\nabla
and can be found for example in
[
26
, Lemma 1]
. The well-posedness of

is proven in the exact same manner.

We can now write the problem verified by
Z_{\varepsilon}
.

Lemma 3

(Two-scale error)
.

Z_{\varepsilon}:=u_{\varepsilon}-w_{\varepsilon}
is a.s. the unique solution in
H^{1}(B_{R}\setminus\overline{D})~\times~H^{1}(D)
of:

\left\{\begin{aligned} &-\Delta Z_{\varepsilon}-k^{2}n_{0}Z_{\varepsilon}=0&&\textrm{in}\,B_{R}\setminus\bar{D},\\
&-\nabla\cdot a_{\varepsilon}\nabla Z_{\varepsilon}-k^{2}n_{\varepsilon}Z_{\varepsilon}=\nabla\cdot F_{\varepsilon}+k^{2}G_{\varepsilon}&&\textrm{in}\,D,\\
&Z_{\varepsilon}^{-}-Z_{\varepsilon}^{+}=\varepsilon u_{1,\varepsilon}&&\textrm{on}\,\partial D,\\
&\nabla Z_{\varepsilon}^{-}\cdot\nu-a_{\varepsilon}\nabla Z_{\varepsilon}^{+}\cdot\nu\\
&\hskip 28.45274pt=F_{\varepsilon}\cdot\nu+\varepsilon\sum_{i=1}^{d}\left(\nabla\cdot(\sigma_{i}^{\varepsilon}\partial_{i}u_{0})^{+}\right)\cdot\nu-k^{2}\varepsilon(\beta^{\varepsilon}u_{0})^{+}\cdot\nu&&\textrm{on}\,\partial D,\\
&\nabla Z_{\varepsilon}\cdot\nu=\Lambda(Z_{\varepsilon})&&\textrm{on}\,\partial B_{R},\end{aligned}\right.

(24)

where
F_{\varepsilon}\in H^{1}(D)
,
G_{\varepsilon}\in H^{1}(D)
are defined as follows

F_{\varepsilon}:=\varepsilon\sum_{i=1}^{d}(a_{\varepsilon}\phi_{i}^{\varepsilon}-\sigma_{i}^{\varepsilon})\nabla(\partial_{i}u_{0})+\varepsilon k^{2}\beta^{\varepsilon}u_{0},

(25)

G_{\varepsilon}:=\varepsilon\sum_{i=1}^{d}(n_{\varepsilon}\phi_{i}^{\varepsilon}-\beta_{i}^{\varepsilon})\partial_{i}u_{0}.

(26)

Here,
\phi^{\varepsilon}
denotes
\phi^{\varepsilon}(\cdot):=\phi(\frac{\cdot}{\varepsilon})
.
\beta_{i}^{\varepsilon}
and
\sigma_{i}^{\varepsilon}
are defined similarly from
\beta_{i}
and
\sigma_{i}
and for the rest of the paper. Note that we have then

\nabla\phi^{\varepsilon}(\cdot)=\frac{1}{\varepsilon}(\nabla\phi)(\frac{\cdot}{\varepsilon}).

Proof.

Let us first derive the problem satisfied by
Z_{\varepsilon}
before proving well-posedness. In
B_{R}\setminus\overline{D}
and on
\partial B_{R}
,
u_{\varepsilon}
and
w_{\varepsilon}
verify the same equation and so does
Z_{\varepsilon}
.
Using the equation (
12
) for
u_{\varepsilon}
and equation (
13
) for
u_{0}
, we have moreover

\begin{split}-\nabla\cdot&(a_{\varepsilon}\nabla Z_{\varepsilon})-k^{2}n_{\varepsilon}Z_{\varepsilon}\\
&=-\nabla\cdot(a^{hom}\nabla u_{0})-k^{2}n^{hom}u_{0}+\nabla\cdot\left(a_{\varepsilon}(\nabla u_{0}+\varepsilon\nabla u_{1,\varepsilon})\right)+k^{2}n_{\varepsilon}(u_{0}+\varepsilon u_{1,\varepsilon})\quad\textrm{in }D.\end{split}

(27)

By the definition of the extended corrector of Definition
6
,

\begin{split}-k^{2}n^{hom}&u_{0}+k^{2}n_{\varepsilon}(u_{0}+\varepsilon u_{1,\varepsilon})\\
&=k^{2}(n_{\varepsilon}-n^{hom})u_{0}+\varepsilon k^{2}n_{\varepsilon}\sum_{i=1}^{d}\phi_{i}^{\varepsilon}\partial_{i}u_{0}\\
&=k^{2}(\nabla\cdot\beta)(\frac{\cdot}{\varepsilon})u_{0}+\varepsilon k^{2}n_{\varepsilon}\sum_{i=1}^{d}\phi_{i}^{\varepsilon}\partial_{i}u_{0}\\
&=k^{2}\varepsilon\nabla\cdot(\beta^{\varepsilon}u_{0})+\varepsilon k^{2}\sum_{i=1}^{d}-\beta^{\varepsilon}_{i}\partial_{i}u_{0}+n_{\varepsilon}\phi_{i}^{\varepsilon}\partial_{i}u_{0}\\
&=k^{2}G_{\varepsilon}+\varepsilon k^{2}\nabla\cdot(\beta^{\varepsilon}u_{0}).\end{split}

(28)

By the skew-symmetry of
\sigma_{i}
,
i\in[\![1,d]\!]
, for all
x\in D
,

\begin{split}\nabla\cdot\left(\sum_{i=1}^{d}(\nabla\cdot\sigma_{i})(\frac{x}{\varepsilon})\partial_{i}u_{0}(x)\right)&=\nabla\cdot(\varepsilon\sum_{i=1}^{d}(\nabla\cdot\sigma_{i}^{\varepsilon}(x))\partial_{i}u_{0}(x))\\
&=\varepsilon\sum_{i,j,m=1}^{d}\partial_{j}((\partial_{m}\sigma_{i,jm}^{\varepsilon}(x))\partial_{i}u_{0}(x))\\
&=\varepsilon\sum_{i,j,m=1}^{d}\partial_{jm}(\sigma_{i,jm}^{\varepsilon}(x)\partial_{i}u_{0}(x))-\partial_{j}(\sigma_{i,jm}^{\varepsilon}(x)(\partial_{im}u_{0}(x)))\\
&=-\varepsilon\sum_{i,j,m=1}^{d}\partial_{j}(\sigma_{i,jm}^{\varepsilon}(x)(\partial_{im}u_{0}(x)))\\
&=-\varepsilon\nabla\cdot\left(\sum_{i=1}^{d}\sigma_{i}^{\varepsilon}(x)\nabla\partial_{i}u_{0}(x)\right).\end{split}

(29)

Therefore, we obtain

\begin{split}-\nabla\cdot&(a^{hom}\nabla u_{0})+\nabla\cdot(a_{\varepsilon}(\nabla u_{0}+\varepsilon\nabla u_{1,\varepsilon}))\\
&=\sum_{i=1}^{d}\nabla\cdot\biggl(\biggl((a(e_{i}+\nabla\phi_{i})-a^{hom}e_{i}\biggr)(\frac{\cdot}{\varepsilon})\partial_{i}u_{0}\biggr)+\varepsilon\nabla\cdot\left(a_{\varepsilon}\phi_{i}^{\varepsilon}\nabla(\partial_{i}u_{0})\right)\\
&=\sum_{i=1}^{d}\nabla\cdot\left((\nabla\cdot\sigma_{i})(\frac{\cdot}{\varepsilon})\partial_{i}u_{0}\right)+\varepsilon\nabla\cdot\left(a_{\varepsilon}\phi_{i}^{\varepsilon}\nabla(\partial_{i}u_{0})\right)\\
&=\nabla\cdot\left(\varepsilon\sum_{i=1}^{d}\left((a_{\varepsilon}\phi_{i}^{\varepsilon}-\sigma_{i}^{\varepsilon})\nabla\partial_{i}u_{0}\right)\right)\\
&=\nabla\cdot F_{\varepsilon}-\varepsilon k^{2}\nabla\cdot(\beta^{\varepsilon}u_{0}).\end{split}

(30)

By combining (
28
) and (
30
), we obtain the error satisfied inside
D
.

Using the jump conditions of
u_{\varepsilon}
and
u_{0}
across
\partial D
, one gets:

Z_{\varepsilon}^{-}-Z_{\varepsilon}^{+}=\varepsilon u_{1,\varepsilon},

(31)

and the flux jump:

\begin{split}\nabla&Z_{\varepsilon}^{-}\cdot\nu-a_{\varepsilon}\nabla Z_{\varepsilon}^{+}\cdot\nu\\
&=\sum_{i=1}^{d}((a_{\varepsilon}(e_{i}+\nabla\phi_{i}^{\varepsilon})-a^{hom}e_{i})\partial_{i}u_{0})^{+}\cdot\nu+\varepsilon(a_{\varepsilon}\phi_{i}^{\varepsilon}\nabla(\partial_{i}u_{0}))^{+}\cdot\nu\\
&=\sum_{i=1}^{d}(\varepsilon(\nabla\cdot\sigma_{i}^{\varepsilon})\partial_{i}u_{0})^{+}\cdot\nu+\varepsilon(a_{\varepsilon}\phi_{i}^{\varepsilon}\nabla\partial_{i}u_{0})^{+}\cdot\nu\\
&=\varepsilon\sum_{i=1}^{d}((a_{\varepsilon}\phi_{i}^{\varepsilon}-\sigma_{i}^{\varepsilon})\nabla(\partial_{i}u_{0}))^{+}\cdot\nu+\nabla\cdot(\sigma_{i}^{\varepsilon}\partial_{i}u_{0})^{+}\cdot\nu\\
&=F_{\varepsilon}\cdot\nu+\varepsilon\sum_{i=1}^{d}\nabla\cdot(\sigma_{i}^{\varepsilon}\partial_{i}u_{0})^{+}\cdot\nu-k^{2}\varepsilon(\beta^{\varepsilon}u_{0})^{+}\cdot\nu.\\
\end{split}

(32)

The well-posedness of (
24
) is a direct consequence of Proposition
14
. Since a.s. for
i,j,k\in[\![1,d]\!]
,
\phi_{i}
,
\beta_{i}
,
\sigma_{i,jk}\in H^{1}_{loc}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
then
\phi_{i}
,
\beta_{i}
,
\sigma_{i,jk}\in H^{1}(D)
. Moreover,
u_{0}\in H^{2}(D)
and
u_{1,\varepsilon}\in H^{1}(D)
. Therefore,
F_{\varepsilon}
,
G_{\varepsilon}\in H^{1}(D)
,
{u_{1,\varepsilon}}_{|\partial D}\in H^{\frac{1}{2}}(\partial D)
and
F_{\varepsilon}\cdot\nu+\varepsilon\sum_{i=1}^{d}\nabla\cdot(\sigma_{i}^{\varepsilon}\partial_{i}u_{0})^{+}\cdot\nu-k^{2}\varepsilon(\beta^{\varepsilon}u_{0})^{+}\cdot\nu\in H^{-\frac{1}{2}}(\partial D)
, and we can apply Proposition
14
.
∎

As it is customary in homogenization in the presence of boundary since

Definition 7

(Boundary corrector)

Let

be the a.s. unique solution in

\left\{\begin{aligned} &-\Delta v_{\varepsilon}-k^{2}n_{0}v_{\varepsilon}=0&&\textrm{in}\,B_{R}\setminus\overline{D},\\

Proposition 4

A.s.

\left\{\begin{aligned} &-\nabla\cdot a_{\varepsilon}\nabla(Z_{\varepsilon}-v_{\varepsilon})-k^{2}n_{\varepsilon}(Z_{\varepsilon}-v_{\varepsilon})=\nabla\cdot F_{\varepsilon}+k^{2}G_{\varepsilon}&&\textrm{in}\,B_{R},\\
&\nabla(Z_{\varepsilon}-v_{\varepsilon})\cdot\nu=\Lambda(Z_{\varepsilon}-v_{\varepsilon})&&\textrm{on}\,\partial B_{R}.\\
\end{aligned}\right.

(35)

Once again, we apply the Proposition

Proposition 5

(Corrector bounds)
.

Under the mixing Hypothesis
1
,

(a)

(

There exists an a.s. finite (non-stationary) random field

such that for all

\left(\fint_{\square_{x}}|\phi|^{2}+|\sigma|^{2}+|\beta|^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\leq\mathcal{C}(x)\mu_{d}(|x|),

where for all

\mu_{d}(y)=\left\{\begin{aligned} &\sqrt{y}&&\textrm{if }d=1,\\
&|\log(2+y)|^{\frac{1}{2}}&&\textrm{if }d=2,\\
&1&&\textrm{if }d=3.\end{aligned}\right.

(37)

(b)

(Corrector bound in average):

Furthermore, for all

,

satisfies the following stochastic integrability

\mathbb{E}[\exp(\frac{1}{C}\mathcal{C}(y)^{\gamma})]\leq 2

for some constant

,

,

,

,

,
a_{S}
and exponent

and the exponential decay rate of
\pi
.

(c)

(Mean-value property):

There exists a stationary
\frac{1}{8}
-Lipschitz continuous random field

(the so-called minimal radius) satisfying (
38
) such that for all

\int_{B_{\ell}(x)}|\nabla\phi|^{2}\lesssim(\ell+r_{*}(x))^{d}.

(39)

For any bounded domain

with squares of size
\varepsilon

as the set of centers of those squares,

We prove the following estimate for the two-scale expansion error with the boundary layer in

Proposition 6

(

- convergence of the two-scale expansion with the boundary corrector)

Let

{u_{0}\in H^{1}(B_{R})}

Remark 4

Note that

Proof.

By (

It then suffices to bound a.s. the norm of each corrector on the right hand side by

We prove the bound for

(

(46)

In the rest of the article
\widehat{\chi_{\varepsilon}}
denotes a random variable satisfying the stochastic integrability (
38

Proof.

Thanks to Proposition
6
, we only need to estimate the norm of
v_{\varepsilon}
in

We lift the trace jump of

across

by considering

\tilde{v}_{\varepsilon}:=v_{\varepsilon}-\varepsilon\eta_{\varepsilon}u_{1,\varepsilon}
where

is a smooth cutoff satisfying for all

0\leq\eta_{\varepsilon}(x)\leq 1,\hskip 5.69046pt\eta_{\varepsilon}=0\hskip 5.69046pt\textrm{in }D_{2\varepsilon\mu_{d}(\frac{1}{\varepsilon})},\hskip 5.69046pt\eta_{\varepsilon}=1\,\textrm{in }B_{R}\setminus\overline{D_{\varepsilon\mu_{d}(\frac{1}{\varepsilon})}},\hskip 5.69046pt|\nabla\eta_{\varepsilon}(x)|\lesssim\frac{1}{\varepsilon\mu_{d}(\frac{1}{\varepsilon})},

, since
\eta_{\varepsilon}u_{1,\varepsilon}\in H^{1}(D)

is a.s. the unique solution in

to

\left\{\begin{aligned} &-\Delta\tilde{v}_{\varepsilon}-k^{2}n_{0}\tilde{v}_{\varepsilon}=0\qquad&&\textrm{in}\,B_{R}\setminus\overline{D},\\
&-\nabla\cdot a_{\varepsilon}\nabla\tilde{v}_{\varepsilon}-k^{2}n_{\varepsilon}\tilde{v}_{\varepsilon}=\varepsilon\nabla\cdot a_{\varepsilon}\nabla(\eta_{\varepsilon}u_{1,\varepsilon})+\varepsilon k^{2}n_{\varepsilon}(\eta_{\varepsilon}u_{1,\varepsilon})\qquad&&\textrm{in}\,D,\\
&\nabla\tilde{v}_{\varepsilon}^{-}\cdot\nu-a_{\varepsilon}\nabla\tilde{v}_{\varepsilon}^{+}\cdot\nu=a_{\varepsilon}\nabla(\eta_{\varepsilon}u_{1,\varepsilon})\cdot\nu\\

&\nabla\tilde{v}_{\varepsilon}\cdot\nu=\Lambda(\tilde{v}_{\varepsilon})\qquad&&\textrm{on}\,\partial B_{R}.\\
\end{aligned}\right.

(47)

\sum_{i=1}^{d}\nabla\cdot(\sigma_{i}^{\varepsilon}\partial_{i}u_{0})^{+})\cdot\nu-k^{2}\varepsilon(\beta^{\varepsilon}u_{0})^{+}\cdot\nu=\sum_{i=1}^{d}\nabla\cdot(\sigma_{i}^{\varepsilon}\partial_{i}u_{0}\eta_{\varepsilon})^{+})\cdot\nu-k^{2}\varepsilon(\beta^{\varepsilon}u_{0}\eta_{\varepsilon})^{+}\cdot\nu.

&\hskip 56.9055pt-k^{2}\varepsilon\nabla\cdot(\beta^{\varepsilon}u_{0}\eta_{\varepsilon})\overline{w}+k^{2}\varepsilon\beta^{\varepsilon}u_{0}\eta_{\varepsilon}\cdot\nabla\overline{w},\end{split}

(49)

where

denotes the conjugate of

and
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{H^{-\frac{1}{2}}(\partial B_{R}),H^{\frac{1}{2}}(\partial B_{R})}
denotes the duality product
H^{-\frac{1}{2}}(\partial B_{R}),H^{\frac{1}{2}}(\partial B_{R})
. By the coercivity of the sesquilinear form, we then obtain

\begin{split}\left\|\tilde{v}_{\varepsilon}\right\|_{H^{1}(B_{R})}&\lesssim\varepsilon\left\|\eta_{\varepsilon}u_{1,\varepsilon}\right\|_{H^{1}(D)}+\varepsilon\left\|\sum_{i=1}^{d}\nabla\cdot(\sigma_{i}^{\varepsilon}\partial_{i}u_{0}\eta_{\varepsilon})\right\|_{L^{2}(D)}\\
&\hskip 28.45274pt+\varepsilon\left\|\nabla\cdot(\beta^{\varepsilon}u_{0}\eta_{\varepsilon})\right\|_{L^{2}(D)}+\varepsilon\left\|\beta^{\varepsilon}u_{0}\eta_{\varepsilon}\right\|_{L^{2}(D)}.\\
\end{split}

(50)

Let
\mathcal{S}_{\eta_{\varepsilon}}:=\textrm{supp}(\eta_{\varepsilon})

4
Asymptotic expansion of the scattered field

4.1
Main result

The convergence estimates that we established in the previous section provide an asymptotic expansion of the field at order
\varepsilon
. Outside
D
,
u_{\varepsilon}
is approximated at first-order by
u_{0}
according to Proposition
7
. Physically,
u_{0}
corresponds to the wave that interacts with the effective medium of parameters
a^{hom}
and
n^{hom}
. It depends on the distribution of the scatterers as
a^{hom}
does but it is deterministic and thus is not characteristic of one realization in a given medium. In the context of ultrasounds the measurements are usually done using the same sensor array that transmits the plane wave excitation (ultrasonic transducers can be used as transmitters and as receivers). So
u_{0}
contains only the contribution from the boundary
\partial D
while we would like to characterize the speckle field generated by the small heterogeneities.
We are then interested in this section in obtaining the next order term in the expansion of the field outside of
D
.

We introduce
G_{0}
the Green function associated to the homogenized problem (
13
)
i.e.

G_{0}
verifies in
\mathcal{D}^{\prime}(B_{R})
for all
y\in B_{R}
,

\left\{\begin{aligned} &-\Delta G_{0}(\cdot,y)-k^{2}n_{0}G_{0}(\cdot,y)=\delta(\cdot-y)&&\textrm{in}\,B_{R}\setminus\overline{D},\\
&-\nabla\cdot\left(a^{hom}\nabla G_{0}(\cdot,y)\right)-k^{2}n^{hom}G_{0}(\cdot,y)=\delta(\cdot-y)&&\textrm{in}\,D,\\
&G_{0}(\cdot,y)^{-}=G_{0}(\cdot,y)^{+}&&\textrm{on}\,\partial D,\\
&\nabla G_{0}(\cdot,y)^{-}\cdot\nu-a^{hom}\nabla G_{0}(\cdot,y)^{+}\cdot\nu=0&&\textrm{on}\,\partial D,\\
&\nabla G_{0}\cdot\nu=\Lambda(G_{0})&&\textrm{on}\,\partial B_{R}.\\
\end{aligned}\right.

(77)

For all
\alpha>0
, we define
D^{\alpha}:=\{x\in B_{R}\,|\,dist(x,D)<\alpha\}
. For
z\in B_{R}\setminus\overline{D^{\alpha}}
,
u_{\varepsilon}
verifies the following Lippman-Schwinger equation

\begin{split}u_{\varepsilon}(z)=u_{0}(z)+\int_{D}&(a^{hom}-a_{\varepsilon}(x))\nabla u_{\varepsilon}(x)\cdot\nabla G_{0}(x,z)\mathrm{d}x\\
&-k^{2}\int_{D}(n^{hom}-n_{\varepsilon}(x))u_{\varepsilon}(x)G_{0}(x,z)\mathrm{d}x.\end{split}

(78)

We make use of the asymptotic expansion of
u_{\varepsilon}
in
H^{1}(D)
and obtain for all
{z\in B_{R}\setminus\overline{D^{\alpha}}}

\begin{split}u_{\varepsilon}(z)=u_{0}(z)+\int_{D}&\sum_{i=1}^{d}\biggl(a^{hom}-a_{\varepsilon}(x)(e_{i}+\varepsilon\nabla\phi_{i}^{\varepsilon}(x)\biggr)\partial_{i}u_{0}(x)\cdot\nabla G_{0}(x,z)\mathrm{d}x\\
&-k^{2}\int_{D}(n^{hom}-n_{\varepsilon}(x))u_{0}(x)G_{0}(x,z)\mathrm{d}x+R^{\varepsilon}(z),\end{split}

(79)

where

\begin{split}R^{\varepsilon}(z):=\int_{D}&(a^{hom}-a_{\varepsilon}(x)\left(\nabla u_{\varepsilon}(x)-\sum_{i=1}^{d}(e_{i}+\varepsilon\nabla\phi_{i}^{\varepsilon}(x))\partial_{i}u_{0}(x)\right)\cdot\nabla G_{0}(x,z)\mathrm{d}x\\
&-k^{2}\int_{D}(n^{hom}-n_{\varepsilon}(x))(u_{\varepsilon}(x)-u_{0}(x))G_{0}(x,z)\mathrm{d}x.\end{split}

(80)

Using the strong convergence estimates established in Proposition
7
and Proposition
8
leads to

\mathbb{E}[\left\|R^{\varepsilon}(z)\right\|_{L^{2}(B_{R}\setminus\overline{D^{\alpha}})}]\lesssim\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{2}}\mu_{d}(\frac{1}{\varepsilon})^{\frac{1}{2}},

which is not sufficient since
u_{\varepsilon}-u_{0}
is of order
\varepsilon
in
L^{2}(B_{R}\setminus\overline{D})
. We thus need to estimate more sharply the weak convergence of the two quantities
(a^{hom}-a_{\varepsilon}(x))\left(\nabla u_{\varepsilon}-\sum_{i=1}^{d}(e_{i}+\varepsilon\nabla\phi_{i}^{\varepsilon})\partial_{i}u_{0}\right)
and
(n^{hom}-n_{\varepsilon})(u_{\varepsilon}-u_{0})
.

In
[
17
]
and
[
16
]
, the authors study the fluctuations of
\nabla u_{\varepsilon}
in the context of the Poisson equation in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
. They prove that the fluctuations of
\nabla u_{\varepsilon}
and
a_{\varepsilon}\nabla u_{\varepsilon}
can be recovered from the fluctuations of the commutator
\Xi\in L^{2}_{loc}(\mathbb{R}^{d})^{d}
defined by:

\forall i\in[\![1,d]\!],\,\Xi_{i}:=(a-a^{hom})(e_{i}+\nabla\phi_{i}).

(81)

This leads to estimate

\begin{split}\mathcal{R}^{\varepsilon}:=\varepsilon^{-\frac{d}{2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}(a_{\varepsilon}-a^{hom})\nabla u_{\varepsilon}\cdot g-\sum_{i=1}^{d}\Xi_{i}(\frac{\cdot}{\varepsilon})\partial_{i}u_{0}\cdot g\end{split}

(82)

for all
g\in\mathcal{C}^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{d})^{d}
. They show that, for all
g\in\mathcal{C}^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{d})^{d}

\begin{split}\textrm{Var}[\mathcal{R}^{\varepsilon}]^{\frac{1}{2}}&\lesssim\varepsilon\mu_{d}(\frac{1}{\varepsilon}).\end{split}

(83)

We extend this result to our situation where the Poisson equation is replaced by the Helmholtz equation leading to a second term in
R^{\varepsilon}
and where we have to take into account the boundary of
D
as the support of
G_{0}
is not compactly supported in
D
. We deal with this last point in a similar manner as in Section

by introducing the appropriate boundary layer. However the rate of convergence is now
1/2
order smaller.

Our main result is stated in the following theorem.

Theorem 10

(Pointwise convergence of
R^{\varepsilon}
)

12
),

13

77

Proof of Lemma

.

(a) First note that for

,

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\mathbbm{1}_{B_{t}(x)}(y)\mathrm{d}x=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\mathbbm{1}_{B_{t}(y)}(x)\mathrm{d}x=Ct^{d},

where

depends only on

.

We have also for

,

Let
U\in L^{1}(D)
and
t>0
. By Fubini’s theorem,

\begin{split}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left(\int_{B_{t}(x)\cap D}|U|\right)\mathrm{d}x&=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\int_{D}|U(y)|\mathbbm{1}_{B_{t}(x)}(y)\mathrm{d}y\mathrm{d}x\\

&\leq Ct^{d}\int_{D}|U(y)|\mathrm{d}y\\
&\leq Ct^{d}\left(\int_{D}|U|\right).\end{split}

(181)

(b) Similarly,

\begin{split}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\rho_{T}(x)^{\alpha}&\left(\int_{B_{t}(x)\cap D}|U|\right)^{2}\mathrm{d}x\\

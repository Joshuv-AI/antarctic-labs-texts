A spectral Erdős-Sós theorem
Sebastian Cioabă, Dheer Noal Desai, Michael Tait

Abstract
The famous Erdős-Sós conjecture states that every graph of average degree more than t − 1 t-1 must contain every tree on t + 1 t+1 vertices. In this paper, we study a spectral version of this conjecture. For n > k n>k , let S n , k S_{n,k} be the join of a clique on k k vertices with an independent set of n − k n-k vertices and denote by S n , k + S_{n,k}^{+} the graph obtained from S n , k S_{n,k} by adding one edge. We show that for fixed k ≥ 2 k\geq 2 and sufficiently large n n , if a graph on n n vertices has adjacency spectral radius at least as large as S n , k S_{n,k} and is not isomorphic to S n , k S_{n,k} , then it contains all trees on 2 ​ k + 2 2k+2 vertices. Similarly, if a sufficiently large graph has spectral radius at least as large as S n , k + S_{n,k}^{+} , then it either contains all trees on 2 ​ k + 3 2k+3 vertices or is isomorphic to S n , k + S_{n,k}^{+} . This answers a two-part conjecture of Nikiforov affirmatively.



1
Introduction

For a fixed graph
F
, the
Turán number
for
F
is denoted by
\mathrm{ex}(n,F)
and is the maximum number of edges an
n
vertex
F
-free graph may have. The celebrated Erdős-Stone-Simonovits theorem
[
11
,
12
]
determines an asymptotic formula for
\mathrm{ex}(n,F)
for any
F
with chromatic number at least
3
. On the other hand, determining even the order of magnitude of the Turán number for most bipartite graphs is a notoriously difficult problem.

The particular class of bipartite Turán problems of interest to us in this paper is the class of trees. Given a tree
T
with
t+1
vertices, considering disjoint copies of
K_{t}
shows that
\mathrm{ex}(n,T)\geq\left\lfloor\frac{n}{t}\right\rfloor\binom{t}{2}
. On the other hand, a standard graph theory exercise shows that
\mathrm{ex}(n,T)\leq n(t-1)
. Note the multiplicative factor of
2
between these bounds. The Erdős-Sós conjecture predicts that the lower bound is correct for all trees.

Conjecture 1.1

(Erdős and Sós 1963 (see
[
10
]
))
.

Any graph of average degree larger than
t-1
contains all trees on
t+1
vertices.

There are many partial results towards this conjecture. For example, the conjecture is true if one additionally forbids
C_{4}
(see
[
26
]
which improved the result from
[
3
]
that the conjecture holds in host graphs of girth
5
), in the case that
t
is large compared to
n

[
15
,
28
,
31
,
33
]
, when the tree has diameter
4

[
21
]
. An asymptotic solution for sufficiently many vertices was announced by Ajtai-Komlós-Simonovits-Szemerédi (c.f.
[
14
, pp. 224-227]
). Many other partial results towards the conjecture have appeared and we will not try to list them all.

In this paper we consider a spectral version of this conjecture. Given a graph
G
, let
\lambda=\lambda(G)
denote the spectral radius of its adjacency matrix. Maximizing
\lambda
over a fixed family of graphs is known as a
Brualdi-Solheid problem

[
4
]
. Many results on Brualdi-Solheid type problems have been obtained over the years, for example
[
1
,
2
,
9
,
13
,
25
,
29
,
30
]
. A Brualdi-Solheid problem inspired by Turán numbers is to ask to maximize
\lambda
over the family of
n
-vertex
F
-free graphs. Indeed, this was proposed to be systematically investigated by Nikiforov in
[
24
]
.

We define
\mathrm{spex}(n,F)
to be the maximum adjacency spectral radius that an
n
-vertex graph not containing a copy of
F
may have. Determining
\mathrm{spex}(n,F)
for various graphs
F
has been of significant interest since being introduced, for example
[
5
,
6
,
7
,
8
,
17
,
20
,
22
,
23
,
27
,
32
,
34
,
35
,
36
]
. One motivation for studying this parameter is that because the spectral radius of a graph is an upper bound for its average degree, any upper bound on
\mathrm{spex}(n,F)
also gives an upper bound on
\mathrm{ex}(n,F)
via the inequality

\mathrm{ex}(n,F)\leq\frac{n}{2}\mathrm{spex}(n,F).

Several classical extremal graph theory results have spectral strengthenings through this inequality, for example Turán’s theorem is implied by Nikiforov’s result
[
22
]
on
\mathrm{spex}(n,K_{r+1})
. In this paper we study
\mathrm{spex}(n,T)
when
T
is a tree. Note that disjoint copies of
K_{t}
show that
\mathrm{spex}(n,T)\geq t-1
if
T
is a tree with
t+1
vertices. However, we can do much better than this. For
n>k
, let
S_{n,k}
be the join of a clique on
k
vertices with an independent set of
n-k
vertices and denote by
S_{n,k}^{+}
the graph obtained from
S_{n,k}
by adding one edge. If a tree has at least
k+1
vertices in each side of its bipartition then it is not a subgraph of
S_{n,k}
. Therefore, a path on
2k+2
vertices is not a subgraph of
S_{n,k}
and a path on
2k+3
vertices is not a subgraph of
S_{n,k}^{+}
. Nikiforov
[
24
]
showed that in fact
S_{n,k}
and
S_{n,k}^{+}
have the largest spectral radii over all
n
-vertex graphs forbidding paths on
2k+2
and
2k+3
vertices respectively (see Lemma
3.4
for the value of
\lambda(G_{n,k})
). In the same paper, it was conjectured that these graphs are spectral extremal for the set of all trees on
2k+2
or
2k+3
vertices, yielding the following spectral version of the Erdős-Sós conjecture.

Conjecture 1.2

(Nikiforov 2010)
.

Let
k\geq 2
and
G
be a graph of sufficiently large
n
.

(a) if
\lambda_{1}(G)\geq\lambda_{1}(S_{n,k})
then
G
contains all trees of order
2k+2
unless
G=S_{n,k}
;

(b) if
\lambda_{1}(G)\geq\lambda_{1}(S_{n,k}^{+})
then
G
contains all trees of order
2k+3
unless
G=S_{n,k}^{+}
.

Partial results towards this conjecture were given in
[
16
,
18
,
19
]
for trees of diameter at most
4
and other special cases. In this paper we prove Conjecture
1.2
. The proof builds on our work
[
5
]
in which we determined
\mathrm{spex}(n,C_{2\ell})
for sufficiently large
n
.

Organization and Notation

For
k\geq 2
, let
\mathcal{T}_{k}
denote the set of all trees on
2k+2
vertices and
\mathcal{T}_{k}^{\prime}
denote the set of all trees on
2k+3
vertices. We use
\mathcal{G}_{n,k}
to denote the set of graphs on
n
vertices not containing at least one tree
T\in\mathcal{T}_{k}
on
2k+2
vertices. Let
G_{n,k}
be a graph with maximum spectral radius among all graphs in
\mathcal{G}_{n,k}
. Similarly, we use
\mathcal{G}_{n,k}^{\prime}
to denote the set of graphs on
n
vertices not containing at least one tree
T\in\mathcal{T}_{k}^{\prime}
on
2k+3
vertices, and
G_{n,k}^{\prime}
to denote a graph with maximum spectral radius among all graphs in
\mathcal{G}_{n,k}^{\prime}
.

Let
\mathrm{x}
be the scaled Perron vector whose maximum entry is equal to
1
and let
z
be a vertex having Perron entry
\mathrm{x}_{z}=1
. We define positive constants
\eta,\epsilon
and
\alpha
satisfying

\displaystyle\begin{split}\eta&<\left\{\frac{1}{10k},\frac{1}{(2k+2)(16k^{2})}\cdot\left(16k^{2}-1-\frac{4(16k^{3}-1)}{5k}\right)\right\}\\
\epsilon&<\min\left\{\eta,\frac{\eta}{2},\frac{1}{8k^{3}},\frac{\eta}{32k^{3}+2}\right\}\\
\alpha&<\min\left\{\eta,\frac{\epsilon^{2}}{22k}\right\}.\end{split}

(1)

We leave the redundant conditions in these inequalities so that when we reference this choice of constants it is clear what we are using. Let
L
be the set of vertices in
V(G_{n,k})
having “large” weights and
S=V\setminus L
be the vertices of “small” weights,

L:=\{v\in V|\mathrm{x}_{v}\geq\alpha\},S:=\{v\in V|\mathrm{x}_{v}<\alpha\}.

Also, let
M\supset L
be the set of vertices of “not too small” a weight:

M:=\{v\in V|\mathrm{x}_{v}\geq\alpha/3\}.

Denote by
L^{\prime}\subset L
the following subset of vertices of “very large” weights:

L^{\prime}:=\{v\in L|\mathrm{x}_{v}\geq\eta\}.

Finally, let
N_{i}(u)
be the vertices at distance
i
from some vertex
u\in V
, and
L_{i}(u),S_{i}(u)
and
M_{i}(u)
be the sets of vertices in
L,S
and
M
respectively, at distance
i
from
u
. If the vertex is unambiguous from context, we will use
L_{i}
,
S_{i},
and
M_{i}
instead.

In Section
3
we give some background graph theory lemmas that we will need and prove preliminary lemmas on the eigenvalues and eigenvectors of our extremal graphs. In Section
4
we progressively refine the structure of our extremal graphs. Using these results, we prove Conjecture
1.2
in Section
5
.

3
Lemmas from spectral and extremal graph theory

Lemma 3.1
.

For a non-negative symmetric matrix
B
, a non-negative non-zero vector
\mathbf{\mathrm{y}}
and a positive constant
c
, if
B\mathbf{\mathrm{y}}\geq c\mathbf{\mathrm{y}}
entrywise, then
\lambda(B)\geq c
.

Proof.

Assume that
B\mathbf{\mathrm{y}}\geq c\mathbf{\mathrm{y}}
entrywise, with the same assumptions for
B,\mathbf{\mathrm{y}}
and
c
as in the statement of the theorem. Then
\mathbf{\mathrm{y}}^{T}B\mathbf{\mathrm{y}}\geq\mathbf{\mathrm{y}}^{T}c\mathbf{\mathrm{y}}
and
\lambda(B)\geq\dfrac{\mathbf{\mathrm{y}}^{T}B\mathbf{\mathrm{y}}}{\mathbf{\mathrm{y}}^{T}\mathbf{\mathrm{y}}}\geq c
.
∎

Lemma 3.2
.

For any tree
T_{t}
on
t
vertices,

\frac{1}{2}(t-2)n\leq\mathrm{ex}(n,T_{t})\leq(t-2)n.

(2)

Lemma 3.3
.

For
m\geq 2
, the complete bipartite graph
K_{\left\lfloor\frac{m}{2}\right\rfloor,m-1}
contains all trees on
m
vertices.

Proof.

Let
T_{m}
be a tree with
m
vertices. We show that
T_{m}
is contained in
K_{\left\lfloor\frac{m}{2}\right\rfloor,m-1}
. The tree
T_{m}
is bipartite with the smaller part having at least one vertex and at most
\left\lfloor\frac{m}{2}\right\rfloor
vertices, while the larger part has at least
\left\lceil\frac{m}{2}\right\rceil
vertices and at most
m-1
vertices. If we use a one-to-one map to send the vertices of
T_{m}
into
K_{\left\lfloor\frac{m}{2}\right\rfloor,m-1}
so that the smaller part of
T_{m}
maps into the part on
\left\lfloor\frac{m}{2}\right\rfloor
vertices and the larger part of
T_{m}
maps into the other part of the complete bipartite graph, then we can see that
T_{m}\subset K_{\left\lfloor\frac{m}{2}\right\rfloor,m-1}

∎

Lemma 3.4
.

\sqrt{kn}\leq\dfrac{k-1+\sqrt{(k-1)^{2}+4k(n-k)}}{2}\leq\lambda(G_{n,k})\leq\lambda(G_{n,k}^{\prime})<\sqrt{(4k+2)n}\leq\sqrt{5kn}
.

Proof.

We get the lower bound from the spectral radius of
S_{n,k}
. The upper bound is obtained by using the second-degree eigenvalue-eigenvector equation and
e(G_{n,k}^{\prime})\leq(2k+1)n
(from Lemma
3.2
):

\begin{split}\lambda^{2}&\leq\max_{v\in V}\left\{\sum_{u\sim v}A^{2}_{v,u}\right\}=\max_{v\in V}\left\{\sum_{u\sim v}d(u)\right\}\\
&=\max_{v\in V}\left\{d(v)+2e(N_{1}(v))+e(N_{1}(v),N_{2}(v))\right\}\\
&\leq 2e(G_{n,k}^{\prime})\leq(4k+2)n\leq 5kn,\end{split}

(3)

which finishes our proof.
∎

Lemma 3.5
.

|L|\leq\dfrac{5\sqrt{kn}}{\alpha}
and
|M|\leq\dfrac{15\sqrt{kn}}{\alpha}
.

Proof.

For any
v\in V
, we have that

\lambda\mathrm{x}_{v}=\sum_{u\sim v}\mathrm{x}_{u}\leq d(v).

(4)

Combining this with the lower bound in Lemma
3.4
gives
\sqrt{kn}\mathrm{x}_{v}\leq\lambda\mathrm{x}_{v}\leq d(v)
. Summing over all vertices
v\in L
gives

|L|\sqrt{kn}\alpha\leq\sum_{v\in L}d(v)\leq\sum_{v\in V}d(v)\leq 2e(G_{n,k}^{\prime})\leq 2\max_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{k}^{\prime}}\{\mathrm{ex}(n,T)\}\leq(4k+2)n\leq 5kn.

(5)

Thus,
|L|\leq\dfrac{5\sqrt{kn}}{\alpha}
. A similar argument shows that
|M|\leq\dfrac{15\sqrt{kn}}{\alpha}
.∎

Lemma 3.6
.

The graphs
G_{n,k}
and
G_{n,k}^{\prime}
are connected and for any vertex
v\in V(G^{\prime}_{n,k})
(or
V(G_{n,k})
), we have that

\mathrm{x}_{v}\geq\dfrac{1}{\lambda(G_{n,k})}>\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{5kn}}.

Proof.

For brevity, we prove this for
G_{n,k}^{\prime}
, the proof for
G_{n,k}
being identical. If
G_{n,k}^{\prime}
has
r\geq 2
components:
C_{1},\ldots,C_{r}
, then let
C_{1}
be a component containing
z
such that
\lambda(G_{n,k}^{\prime})=\lambda(C_{1})
.
By applying (
4
) for the vertex
z
, we have that
d(z)\geq\lambda(G_{n,k}^{\prime})>\sqrt{kn}
.
Say
u
is some vertex in another component. We modify the graph
G_{n,k}^{\prime}
by deleting all the edges adjacent to
u
and adding the edge
uz
, to obtain the graph
\hat{G}_{n,k}
. As
\lambda(C_{1})=\lambda(G_{n,k}^{\prime})
, this modification strictly increases the spectral radius. Moreover, we claim that
\hat{G}_{n,k}
does not contain any new trees in
\mathcal{T}^{\prime}_{k}
which were not contained in
G_{n,k}^{\prime}
. Assume to the contrary that a new tree
T\in\mathcal{T}_{k}^{\prime}
is created due to the modification and that there are no isomorphic copies of
T
in
G_{n,k}^{\prime}
. Then
u
is a leaf of
T
and is adjacent only to the vertex
z
.
Now
T\in\mathcal{T}^{\prime}_{k}
is a graph on
2k+3
vertices and
d(z)\geq\sqrt{kn}
. So,
z
would have already been adjacent in
G^{\prime}_{n,k}
to at least
\sqrt{kn}-(2k+1)
vertices not in the vertex set of
T
, thus an isomorphic copy of
T
is already present in
G^{\prime}_{n,k}
, a contradiction.

The statement follows from (
4
) for any vertex
v
adjacent to
z
. Now consider some vertex
v\in V
that is not adjacent to
z
such that
\mathrm{x}_{v}<\dfrac{1}{\lambda(G_{n,k}^{\prime})}
. As above, we have
d(z)\geq\sqrt{kn}
. Modifying the graph
G_{n,k}^{\prime}
in the same way it was done above, by deleting the edges adjacent to
v
in
G_{n,k}^{\prime}
and adding the edge
vz
, strictly increases the spectral radius by considering the Rayleigh quotient. For the same reason as before, we also do not create any new
T\in\mathcal{T}_{k}^{\prime}
which had no isomorphic copies in
G_{n,k}^{\prime}
already. ∎

4
Structural results for extremal graphs

All the lemmas in this section apply to both
G_{n,k}
and
G_{n,k}^{\prime}
. For brevity, we write all the proofs for
G_{n,k}^{\prime}
, but the same results for
G_{n,k}
follow by replacing
G_{n,k}^{\prime}
with
G_{n,k}
in the proof. First we show that the degree of any vertex in
L
is linear and hence
L
has constant size.

Lemma 4.1
.

Any vertex
v\in L
has degree
d(v)\geq\dfrac{\alpha}{10(4k+3)}n
. Moreover,
|L|\leq\dfrac{500}{\alpha}k^{2}
.

Proof.

Assume to the contrary that there exists a vertex
v\in L
of degree
d(v)<\dfrac{\alpha}{10(4k+3)}n
. Let
\mathrm{x}_{v}=c\geq\alpha
. The second degree eigenvector-eigenvalue equation with respect to the vertex
v
gives that

\begin{split}knc\leq\lambda^{2}c=\lambda^{2}\mathrm{x}_{v}=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}u\sim v\\
w\sim u\end{subarray}}\mathrm{x}_{w}&\leq d(v)c+2e(N_{1}(v))+\sum_{u\sim v}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}w\sim u,\\
w\in N_{2}(v)\end{subarray}}\mathrm{x}_{w}\\
&\leq(4k+3)d(v)+\sum_{u\sim v}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}w\sim u,\\
w\in M_{2}(v)\end{subarray}}\mathrm{x}_{w}+\sum_{u\sim v}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}w\sim u,\\
w\in N_{2}\setminus M_{2}(v)\end{subarray}}\mathrm{x}_{w},\end{split}

(6)

where the last inequality follows from Lemma
3.2
.
Since
d(v)<\dfrac{\alpha}{10(4k+3)}n
by our assumption and
c\geq\alpha
, we have

(k-0.1)nc<\sum_{u\sim v}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}w\sim u,\\
w\in M_{2}(v)\end{subarray}}\mathrm{x}_{w}+\sum_{u\sim v}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}w\sim u,\\
w\in N_{2}\setminus M_{2}(v)\end{subarray}}\mathrm{x}_{w}.

(7)

From
d(v)<\frac{\alpha}{10(4k+3)}
and Lemma
3.5
, we get that

\sum_{u\sim v}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}w\sim u,\\
w\in M_{2}(v)\end{subarray}}\mathrm{x}_{w}\leq e(N_{1}(v),M_{2}(v))\leq(2k+1)(d(v)+|M|)<(2k+1)\left(\dfrac{\alpha}{10(4k+3)}n+\dfrac{15\sqrt{kn}}{\alpha}\right).

For
n
sufficiently large, we have that

(2k+1)\left(\dfrac{\alpha}{10(4k+3)}n+\dfrac{15\sqrt{kn}}{\alpha}\right)\leq.9n\alpha\leq.9nc,

and consequently using Lemma
3.2
,

(k-1)nc<\sum_{u\sim v}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}w\sim u,\\
w\in N_{2}\setminus M_{2}(v)\end{subarray}}\mathrm{x}_{w}\leq e(N_{1}(v),N_{2}\setminus M_{2}(v))\dfrac{\alpha}{3}\leq(2k-1)n\dfrac{\alpha}{3}.

This is a contradiction because

and
c\geq\alpha
. Hence,
d(v)\geq\dfrac{\alpha}{10(4k+3)}n
for all

.
Thus,

(2k+1)n\geq e(G^{\prime}_{n,k})\geq\dfrac{1}{2}\sum_{v\in L}d(v)\geq|L|\dfrac{\alpha}{20(4k+3)}n,

so
|L|\leq\dfrac{20(2k+1)(4k+3)}{\alpha}\leq\dfrac{500}{\alpha}k^{2}
.
∎

We will now refine our arguments in the proof above to improve degree estimates for the vertices in

Lemma 4.2
.

If
v
is a vertex in
L^{\prime}
with
\mathrm{x}_{v}=c

Proof.

Our proof is again by contradiction. The second degree eigenvalue-eigenvector with respect to the vertex

\begin{split}knc&\leq\lambda^{2}c=\sum_{u\sim v}\sum_{w\sim u}\mathrm{x}_{w}=d(v)c+\sum_{u\sim v}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}w\sim u,\\
w\neq v\end{subarray}}\mathrm{x}_{w}\\
&\leq d(v)c+\sum_{u\in S_{1}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}w\sim u\\
w\in L_{1}\cup L_{2}\end{subarray}}\mathrm{x}_{w}+2e(S_{1})\alpha+2e(L)+e(L_{1},S_{1})\alpha+e(N_{1},S_{2})\alpha.\end{split}

(8)

The observation that any subgraph of

intersect and hence
L^{\prime}\subset\{z\}\cup N_{1}(z)\cup N_{2}(z)
. We need the following inequalities for the number of edges from the neighborhoods of vertices with Perron weight close to the maximum.

Lemma 4.3
.

For
z
the vertex with
\mathbf{\mathrm{x}}_{z}=1
, we have
(1-\epsilon)kn\leq e(S_{1},\{z\}\cup L_{1}\cup L_{2})\leq(k+\epsilon)n
.

Proof.

To obtain the lower bound, we use (
9
). Given
\mathrm{x}_{z}=1
, we have

kn(1)\leq d(z)+e(S_{1},L_{1}\cup L_{2})+\frac{\epsilon^{2}n}{2}.

Since
e(S_{1},\{z\}\cup L_{1}\cup L_{2})=e(S_{1},L_{1}\cup L_{2})+d(z)
, the lower bound follows as
\frac{\epsilon^{2}n}{2}<k\epsilon n
.

To obtain the upper bound, we assume toward a contradiction that for the vertex
z
, we have
e(S_{1},\{z\}\cup L_{1}\cup L_{2})>(k+\epsilon)n
. We will obtain a contradiction via Lemma
3.3
by showing that
K_{k+1,2k+2}\subset G_{n,k}^{\prime}
. For this we prove the following claim.

Claim 4.3.1
.

Let
\delta:=\frac{\epsilon\alpha}{500k^{2}}
. With respect to the vertex
z
there are at least
\delta n
vertices inside
S_{1}
with degree
k
or more in
G_{n,k}^{\prime}[S_{1},L_{1}\cup L_{2}]
.

Proof.

Assume to the contrary that at most
\delta n
vertices in
S_{1}
have degree at least
k
in
G_{n,k}^{\prime}[S_{1},L_{1}\cup L_{2}]
. Then
e(S_{1},L_{1}\cup L_{2})<(k-1)|S_{1}|+|L|\delta n\leq(k-1)n+\epsilon n
, because
|S_{1}|\leq n
and by Lemma
4.1
. This contradicts our assumption that
e(S_{1},\{z\}\cup L_{1}\cup L_{2})>(k+\epsilon)n
.
∎

Hence, there is a subset
D\subset S_{1}
with at least
\delta n
vertices such that every vertex in
D
has degree at least
k
in
G_{n,k}^{\prime}[S_{1},L_{1}\cup L_{2}]
. Since there are only at most
\binom{|L|}{k}\leq\binom{500k^{2}/\alpha}{k}
options for every vertex in
D
to choose a set of
k
neighbors from, we have that there exists some set of
k
vertices in
L_{1}\cup L_{2}\setminus\{z\}
having a common neighborhood with at least
\delta n/\binom{|L|}{k}\geq\delta n/\binom{500k^{2}/\alpha}{k}=\frac{\epsilon\alpha}{500k^{2}}n/\binom{500k^{2}/\alpha}{k}\geq 2k+2
vertices. Thus,
K_{k,2k+2}\subset G_{n,k}^{\prime}[S_{1},L_{1}\cup L_{2}]
and
K_{k+1,2k+2}\subset G_{n,k}^{\prime}[S_{1},L_{1}\cup L_{2}\cup\{z\}]
, a contradiction by Lemma
3.3
. Hence
e(S_{1},\{z\}\cup L_{1}\cup L_{2})\leq(k+\epsilon)n
.
∎

Next we show that all the vertices in
L^{\prime}
in fact have Perron weight close to the maximum.

Lemma 4.4
.

For all vertices
v\in L^{\prime}
, we have

d(v)\geq\left(1-\frac{1}{8k^{3}}\right)n
and
\mathbf{\mathrm{x}}_{v}\geq 1-\frac{1}{16k^{3}}
. Moreover,
|L^{\prime}|=k
.

Proof.

Suppose we are able to show that
\mathbf{\mathrm{x}}_{v}\geq 1-\frac{1}{16k^{3}}
, then using Lemma
4.2
and by (
1
) we have that

d(v)\geq\left(1-\frac{1}{8k^{3}}\right)n
.
Then because every vertex
v\in L^{\prime}
has
d(v)\geq\left(1-\frac{1}{8k^{3}}\right)n
, we must have that
|L^{\prime}|\leq k
else
G_{n,k}^{\prime}[S_{1},L^{\prime}]
contains a
K_{k+1,2k+2}
, a contradiction by Lemma
3.3
.

Next, if
|L^{\prime}|\leq k-1
then using Lemma
4.3
and (
9
) applied to the vertex
z
we have

kn\leq\lambda^{2}\leq e(S_{1},L^{\prime}\setminus L)+e(S_{1},L_{1}\cup L_{2})\eta+\frac{\epsilon n^{2}}{2}\leq(k-1)n+(k+\epsilon)n\eta+\frac{\epsilon^{2}n}{2}<kn

where the last inequality holds by (
1
) and gives the contradiction. Thus
|L^{\prime}|=k
. Thus, all we need to show is that
\mathbf{\mathrm{x}}_{v}\geq 1-\frac{1}{16k^{3}}
for any
v\in L^{\prime}
.

Now towards a contradiction assume that there is some vertex in
v\in L^{\prime}
such that
\mathbf{\mathrm{x}}_{v}<1-\frac{1}{16k^{3}}
.
Then refining (
9
) with respect to the vertex
z
we have that

\begin{split}kn&\leq\lambda^{2}<e(S_{1},L_{1}(z)\cup L_{2}(z)\setminus\{v\})+|N_{1}(z)\cap N_{1}(v)|\mathbf{\mathrm{x}}_{v}+\frac{\epsilon^{2}n}{2}\\
&<(k+\epsilon)n-|S_{1}(z)\cap N_{1}(v)|+|N_{1}(z)\cap N_{1}(v)|\left(1-\frac{1}{16k^{3}}\right)+\frac{\epsilon^{2}n}{2}\\
&=kn+\epsilon n+|L_{1}(z)\cap N_{1}(v)|-|N_{1}(z)\cap N_{1}(v)|\frac{1}{16k^{3}}+\frac{\epsilon^{2}n}{2}.\end{split}

(12)

Thus, using Lemma
4.1
we have
\frac{|N_{1}(z)\cap N_{1}(v)|}{16k^{3}}<\epsilon n+\frac{\epsilon^{2}n}{2}+|L|\leq 2\epsilon n
.

But,
v\in L^{\prime}
, thus
\mathbf{\mathrm{x}}_{v}\geq\eta
and
d(v)\geq\left(\eta-\epsilon\right)n
, and so
|N_{1}(v)\cap N_{1}(v)|\geq\left(\eta-2\epsilon\right)n>32k^{3}\epsilon n
by (
1
), a contradiction.
∎

Now we have
|L^{\prime}|=k
and every vertex in
L^{\prime}
has degree at least
\left(1-\frac{1}{8k^{3}}\right)n
. Thus, the common neighborhood of vertices in
L^{\prime}
has at least
\left(1-\frac{1}{8k^{2}}\right)n
vertices. Let
R
denote the set of vertices in this common neighborhood. Let
E
be the set of remaining “exceptional” vertices not in
L^{\prime}
or
R
. Thus
|E|\leq\frac{n}{8k^{2}}
. We will now show that
E=\emptyset
and thus
G_{n,k}^{\prime}
contains a large complete bipartite subgraph
K_{k,n-k}
.

Lemma 4.5
.

For any vertex
v\in V(G_{n,k}^{\prime})
, the Perron weight in the neighborhood of
v
satisfies
\sum_{w\sim v}\mathbf{\mathrm{x}}_{w}\geq k-\frac{1}{16k^{2}}
.

Proof.

Clearly if
v\in L^{\prime}
, we have

\sum_{w\sim v}\mathbf{\mathrm{x}}_{w}=\lambda\mathbf{\mathrm{x}}_{v}\geq\lambda\left(1-\epsilon\right)\geq k-\frac{1}{16k^{2}}.

If
v\in R
, then

\sum_{w\sim v}\mathbf{\mathrm{x}}_{w}\geq\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}w\sim v\\
w\in L^{\prime}\end{subarray}}\mathbf{\mathrm{x}}_{w}\geq k\left(1-\frac{1}{16k^{3}}\right)=k-\frac{1}{16k^{2}}.

Finally, let
v\in E
. If
\sum_{w\sim u}\mathbf{\mathrm{x}}_{w}<k-\frac{1}{16k^{2}}
, consider the graph
H
obtained from
G_{n,k}^{\prime}
by deleting all edges adjacent to
v
and adding the edges
uv
for all
u\in L^{\prime}
. Now since
\sum_{w\sim v}\mathbf{\mathrm{x}}_{w}<k-\frac{1}{16k^{2}}
we have that
x^{T}A(H)x>x^{T}A(G_{n,k}^{\prime})x
, and so by the Rayleigh principle
\lambda(H)>\lambda(G_{n,k}^{\prime})
. However, there are no new trees
T\in\mathcal{T}^{\prime}_{k}
that have isomorphic copies in
H
but no isomorphic copies in
G_{n,k}^{\prime}
. To see this, assume to the contrary that
T
is a new tree having an isomorphic copy in
H
but not in
G_{n,k}^{\prime}
. Then
T
has
2k+3
vertices
v=v_{1},v_{2},\ldots v_{2k+3}
and
v
has at most
k
neighbors in
T
all of which lie in
L^{\prime}
. Now since the common neighborhood of vertices in
L^{\prime}
has at least
\left(1-\frac{1}{8k^{2}}\right)n>2k+2
vertices,
T
must already have an isomorphic copy in
G_{n,k}^{\prime}
, a contradiction.
∎

Let
K_{a,b}^{+},K_{a,b}^{p},
and
K_{a,b}^{m}
denote the graphs obtained from the complete bipartite graph
K_{a,b}
by adding into the part of size
b
an edge
K_{2}
, a path
P_{3}
on
3
vertices, and a matching with two edges
K_{2}\cup K_{2}
, respectively.

Lemma 4.6
.

The graph
K_{k,2k+1}^{+}:=K_{k}\vee\left((2k-1)K_{1}\cup K_{2}\right)
contains all trees in
\mathcal{T}_{k}
and the graphs
K_{k,2k+2}^{p}:=K_{k}\vee\left((2k-1)K_{1}\cup P_{3}\right)
and
K_{k,2k+2}^{m}:=K_{k}\vee\left((2k-2)K_{1}\cup 2K_{2}\right)
contain all trees in
\mathcal{T}_{k}^{\prime}
.

Proof.

First we prove that
K_{k,2k+1}^{+}
contains all trees in
\mathcal{T}_{k}
. Say
T
is a tree in
\mathcal{T}_{k}
. The tree
T
is a bipartite graph which has smallest part of size less than or equal to
k+1
. If the smallest part has size less than
k+1
then we are done, as
T\subset K_{k,2k+1}
. Now if instead
T
has both parts of size
k+1
we let
v
be a leaf of
T
. Then
T-\{v\}
is a tree with
2k+1
vertices with one part of size
k
and the other of size
k+1
. Therefore,
T-\{v\}\subset K_{k,k+1}
and
T\subset K_{k,k+2}^{+}\subset K_{k,2k+1}^{+}
.

Next, in case
T\in\mathcal{T}_{k}^{\prime}
then the tree
T
is a bipartite graph whose smaller part has at most
k+1
vertices. In case the smaller part of
T
has at most
k
vertices, then
T\subset K_{k,2k+2}
which is contained in both
K_{k,2k+2}^{p}
and
K_{k,2k+2}^{m}
, and we are done. Now suppose
T
has a smaller part of size
k+1
and a larger part of size
k+2
. Then if there is a leaf in the smaller part, then by the same argument used for trees in
\mathcal{T}_{k}
we can show that
T\subset K_{k,k+3}^{+}\subset K_{k,2k+2}^{+}
which is contained in both
K_{k,2k+2}^{p}
and
K_{k,2k+2}^{m}
. If the smaller part has no leaves then every vertex in the smaller part has degree at least
2
and since the sum of their degrees cannot be more than
2k+2
, we must have that each vertex in the smaller part of
T
has degree
2
. Let
v
be one such vertex of degree
2
that lies in the smaller part. Then
T-\{v\}\subset K_{k,k+2}
and
T\subset K_{k,k+3}^{p}\subset K_{k,2k+2}^{p}
.
Finally, let
l_{1}
and
l_{2}
be two leaves that lie in the larger part. Clearly they cannot have a common neighbor in the smaller part as
2k+3>3
and
T
is connected. Thus,
T-\{l_{1},l_{2}\}\subset K_{k+1,k}
and
T\subset K_{k,k+3}^{m}\subset K_{k,2k+2}^{m}
where we are now including
l_{1}
and
l_{2}
into the formerly smaller part.
∎

It follows from Lemma
4.6
that
e(R)\leq 1
. Moreover, any vertex in
E
is adjacent to at most
2k+2
vertices in
R
, else
K_{k+1,2k+2}\subset G_{n,k}^{\prime}
a contradiction by Lemma
3.3
. Finally, any vertex in
E
is adjacent to at most
k-1
vertices in
L^{\prime}
by the definition of
E
.

Lemma 4.7
.

The set
E
is empty and
G_{n,k}^{\prime}
contains the complete bipartite graph
K_{k,n-k}
.

Proof.

Assume to the contrary that
E\neq\emptyset
. Recall that any vertex
r\in R
satisfies
\mathbf{\mathrm{x}}_{r}<\eta
. Therefore, any vertex
v\in E
must satisfy

\sum_{u\sim v}\mathbf{\mathrm{x}}_{u}=\lambda\mathbf{\mathrm{x}}_{v}=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}u\sim v\\
u\in L^{\prime}\cup R\end{subarray}}\mathbf{\mathrm{x}}_{u}+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}u\sim v\\
u\in E\end{subarray}}\mathbf{\mathrm{x}}_{u}\leq k-1+\left(2k+2\right)\eta+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}u\sim v\\
u\in E\end{subarray}}\mathbf{\mathrm{x}}_{u}.

Combining this with Lemma
4.5
gives

\begin{split}\frac{\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}u\sim v\\
u\in E\end{subarray}}\mathbf{\mathrm{x}}_{u}}{\lambda\mathbf{\mathrm{x}}_{v}}\geq\frac{\lambda\mathbf{\mathrm{x}}_{v}-(k-1)-(2k+1)\eta}{\lambda\mathbf{\mathrm{x}}_{v}}\geq 1-\dfrac{(k-1)+(2k+2)\eta}{k-\frac{1}{16k^{2}}}\geq\frac{4}{5k},\end{split}

(13)

where the last inequality follows from (
1
). Now consider the matrix
B=A(G_{n,k}^{\prime}[E])
and vector
y:=\mathbf{\mathrm{x}}_{|_{E}}
(the restriction of the vector
\mathbf{\mathrm{x}}
to the set
E
). We see that for any vertex
v\in E

B\mathbf{\mathrm{y}}_{v}=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}u\sim v\\
u\in E\end{subarray}}\mathbf{\mathrm{x}}_{u}\geq\frac{4}{5k}\lambda\mathbf{\mathrm{x}}_{v}=\frac{4}{5k}\lambda\mathbf{\mathrm{y}}_{v}.

Hence, by Lemma
3.1
, we have that
\lambda(B)\geq\frac{4}{5k}\lambda\geq\frac{4}{5}\sqrt{\frac{n}{k}}
. This is a contradiction to Lemma
3.4
which gives
\lambda(B)\leq\sqrt{5k|E|}\leq\sqrt{5k\frac{n}{8k^{2}}}=\sqrt{\frac{5n}{8k}}
, else
E
contains all trees in
\mathcal{T}_{k}^{\prime}
.
∎

5
Proof of Conjecture
1.2

It follows from Lemma
4.7
that both
G_{n,k}
and
G_{n,k}^{\prime}
contain the complete bipartite graph
K_{k,n-k}
, where the part on
k
vertices is the set
L^{\prime}
and the part on
n-k
vertices is the set
R
. By Lemma
4.6
we have that
e(R)=0
in
G_{n,k}
and
e(R)\leq 1
in
G_{n,k}^{\prime}
. Hence
G_{n,k}\subset S_{n,k}
and
G_{n,k}^{\prime}\subset S_{n,k}^{+}
. Since adding more edges will only increase the spectral radius, we see that the spectral radius is maximized if the vertices of
L^{\prime}
induce a clique
K_{k}
and the number of edges in
R
is as large as possible. Thus
G_{n,k}\cong S_{n,k}
and
G_{n,k}^{\prime}\cong S_{n,k}^{+}

∎

6
Conclusion

In this paper we proved Conjecture
1.2
, a spectral version of the Erdős-Sós conjecture. We would like however to highlight an important difference between the classical and spectral settings. If the Erdős-Sós conjecture is true, then when
t
divides
n
, disjoint copies of
K_{t}
show that the conjecture is best possible. Furthermore, this extremal construction does not contain any tree on
t+1
vertices. On the other hand,
S_{n,k}
and
S_{n,k}^{+}
contain many trees on
2k+2
and
2k+3
vertices (and more). Therefore, it would be interesting to study
\mathrm{spex}(n,T)
for various specific trees
T
, and we leave this as an open problem.

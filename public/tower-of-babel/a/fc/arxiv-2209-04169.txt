Introduction

A fusion category
\mathcal{C}

\mathbb{C}

Unlike the classification of fusion categories by Frobenius-Perron dimensions, however, we even don’t know the structures of fusion categories with a prime global dimension.

Recently, some progresses are made in topics related to the classifications of spherical fusion categories by their global dimensions. In

2.1
Fusion categories and dimensions

Let
\mathcal{C}
be a fusion category,
\mathcal{O}(\mathcal{C})
the set of isomorphism classes of simple objects of a fusion category
\mathcal{C}
. Then the Frobenius-Perron homomorphism
\text{FPdim}(-):\text{Gr}(\mathcal{C})\to\mathbb{C}
is the unique ring homomorphism such that
\text{FPdim}(X)\geq 1
is an algebraic integer,
\forall X\in\mathcal{O}(\mathcal{C})
,
\text{FPdim}(X)
is called the Frobenius-Perron dimension of the object
X
, and the sum

\displaystyle\text{FPdim}(\mathcal{C}):=\sum_{X\in\mathcal{O}(\mathcal{C})}\text{FPdim}(X)^{2}

is defined as the Frobenius-Perron dimension of
\mathcal{C}
.

A fusion category
\mathcal{C}
is weakly integral if
\text{FPdim}(\mathcal{C})\in\mathbb{Z}
,
\mathcal{C}
is integral if
\text{FPdim}(X)\in\mathbb{Z}
for all
X\in\mathcal{O}(\mathcal{C})
. We use
\mathcal{C}_{\text{int}}
to denote the maximal integral fusion subcategory of
\mathcal{C}
. The adjoint subcategory
\mathcal{C}_{\text{ad}}
of a weakly integral fusion category is always integral
[
10
]
.
A simple object of
\mathcal{C}
is called invertible if
X\otimes X^{*}=I
, the unit object, equivalently,
\text{FPdim}(X)=1
. A fusion category
\mathcal{C}
is pointed if
\mathcal{O}(\mathcal{C})
is a finite group, where the group multiplication is induced by the tensor product
\otimes
. In the following, we use
\mathcal{C}_{\text{pt}}
to denote the maximal pointed subcategory of
\mathcal{C}
, that is, the fusion subcategory generated by invertible simple objects of
\mathcal{C}
. And we say a fusion category
\mathcal{C}
is non-pointed if
\mathcal{C}_{\text{pt}}\neq\mathcal{C}
. In addition, two fusion categories
\mathcal{C}
and
\mathcal{D}
are Grothendieck equivalent if
\text{Gr}(\mathcal{C})\cong\text{Gr}(\mathcal{D})
as fusion rings.

Let
\mathcal{C}
be a spherical fusion category
\mathcal{C}
with spherical structure
j
, which is a natural isomorphism
j=\{j_{X}|j_{X}:X\overset{\sim}{\to}X^{**},X\in\mathcal{C}\}
such that
\dim_{j}(X)=\dim_{j}(X^{*})
,
\dim_{j}(X)
is called the quantum (or, categorical) dimension of
X
determined by
j
, where
\dim_{j}(X)
is defined as the (categorical) trace of
\text{id}_{X}
, that is,

\displaystyle\dim_{j}(X)=\text{Tr}(\text{id}_{X}):=\text{ev}_{X}\circ(j_{X}\circ\text{id}_{X})\otimes\text{id}_{X^{*}}\circ\text{coev}_{X},

where
(X^{*},\text{ev}_{X},\text{coev}_{X})
is the dual object of
X
and we suppress the associativity and unit constraints of
\mathcal{C}
. We define the global (or, categorical) dimension of the fusion category
\mathcal{C}
as

\displaystyle\dim(\mathcal{C}):=\sum_{X\in\mathcal{O}(\mathcal{C})}\dim_{j}(X)^{2},

the global dimension
\dim(\mathcal{C})
is independent of the choice of the spherical structure of
\mathcal{C}
and
\dim_{j}(-)
induces a homomorphism from
\text{Gr}(\mathcal{C})
to
\mathbb{C}

[
10
, Proposition 4.7.12]
.

Given an arbitrary spherical fusion category
\mathcal{C}
, we can consider the twist (or Galois conjugate)
\mathcal{C}^{\sigma}
of
\mathcal{C}
, where
\sigma\in\text{Gal}(\overline{\mathbb{Q}}/\mathbb{Q})
and
\overline{\mathbb{Q}}
is the algebraic closure of
\mathbb{Q}
. More precisely,
\mathcal{C}^{\sigma}
is a fusion category with the same monoidal functor
\otimes
as
\mathcal{C}
and the associator of
\mathcal{C}^{\sigma}
is obtained by composing the one of
\mathcal{C}
with automorphism
\sigma
. Moreover,
\text{dim}(\mathcal{C}^{\sigma})=\sigma(\text{dim}(\mathcal{C}))
.
A fusion category
\mathcal{C}
is said to be pseudo-unitary if
\text{dim}(\mathcal{C})=\text{FPdim}(\mathcal{C})
. For more properties of global dimension, we refer the readers to references
[
10
,
11
,
22
]
. In this paper, we will fix a spherical structure
j
and denote
\dim_{j}(-)
by
\dim(-)
.

2.2
Formal codegrees of fusion categories

Let
\mathcal{C}
be a fusion category, then the complexified ring
\text{Gr}(\mathcal{C})\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathbb{C}
is semisimple. Given an irreducible representation
\chi
of
\text{Gr}(\mathcal{C})\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathbb{C}
, the element

\displaystyle\alpha_{\chi}=\sum_{X\in\mathcal{O}(\mathcal{C})}\text{tr}_{\chi}(X)X^{*}

is central, where
\text{tr}_{\chi}
is the ordinary trace function on the representation
\chi
, moreover
\chi^{\prime}(\alpha_{\chi})=0
if
\chi\ncong\chi^{\prime}
and
f_{\chi}:=\chi(\alpha_{\chi})
is a positive algebraic integer
[
14
]
,
f_{\chi}
is called a formal codegree of fusion category
\mathcal{C}

[
20
]
. For example,
\text{FPdim}(\mathcal{C})
and
\dim(\mathcal{C})
are formal codegrees of
\mathcal{C}
.

It was showed that
\frac{\dim(\mathcal{C})}{f_{\chi}}
is always a totally positive algebraic integer

[
20
, Corollary 2.14]
and the set of formal codegrees of
\mathcal{C}
satisfy the following equation
[
20
, Proposition 2.10]

\displaystyle\sum_{\chi}\frac{\chi(1)}{f_{\chi}}=1.

(1)

If
\mathcal{C}\ncong\text{Vec}
, then
f_{\chi}>\sqrt{\frac{2\text{rank}(\mathcal{C})}{\text{rank}(\mathcal{C})+1}}\geq\sqrt{\frac{4}{3}}
for all irreducible representations
\chi

[
22
, Theorem 4.2.1]
. Moreover,
\sigma(\dim(\mathcal{C}))>\frac{4\sqrt{3}}{5}
if the non-trivial fusion category
\mathcal{C}
is not a Galois conjugate of
\mathcal{C}(\mathfrak{sl}_{2},3)_{\text{ad}}

[
22
, Proposition A.1.1]
, where
\sigma\in\text{Gal}(\overline{\mathbb{Q}}/\mathbb{Q})
.

Let
\alpha
be an algebraic integer with the minimal polynomial
g(x)=x^{n}+a_{1}x^{n-1}+\cdots+a_{n-1}x+a_{n}
, then
\alpha
is called a
d
-number if
(a_{n})^{j}
divides
(a_{j})^{n}
for all
1\leq j\leq n

[
20
, Definition 1.1]
, see
[
20
, Lemma 2.7]
for more equivalent conditions about
d
-numbers. The formal codegrees of a fusion category are
d
-numbers
[
20
, Theorem 1.2]
, for example. In addition, the Frobenius-Perron dimensions and quantum dimensions of objects, and formal codegrees of a fusion category are cyclotomic algebraic integers
[
11
, Corollary 8.53]
. Hence, in order to determine whether a totally positive algebraic integer
\alpha
is a formal codegree of a fusion category
\mathcal{C}
, we can use the Program GAP to test whether
\alpha
is a
d
-number and the Galois group of the minimal polynomial of
\alpha
is abelian, this is called the
d
-number tests and cyclotomic test
[
20
]
.

2.3
Modular fusion categories and representations of
\text{SL}(2,\mathbb{Z})

Let
\mathcal{C}
be a braided fusion category with braiding
c
and
\mathcal{D}
a fusion subcategory of
\mathcal{C}
. Then the centralizer of
\mathcal{D}
in
\mathcal{C}
is the following fusion subcategory

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}_{\mathcal{C}}^{\prime}={\{X\in\mathcal{C}|c_{Y,X}c_{X,Y}=\text{id}_{X\otimes Y},\forall Y\in\mathcal{D}}\}.

We call
\mathcal{C}^{\prime}:=\mathcal{C}_{\mathcal{C}}^{\prime}
the Müger center of
\mathcal{C}

[
15
]
.

Let
\mathcal{C}
be a braided spherical (i.e., pre-modular) fusion category with ribbon structure
\theta
, the matrices
S=(S_{X,Y})
and
T=(\delta_{X,Y}\theta_{X})
are called the
S
-matrix and
T
-matrix of
\mathcal{C}
, respectively, where
S_{X,Y}=\text{Tr}(c_{Y,X}c_{X,Y})
,
X,Y\in\mathcal{O}(\mathcal{C})
.
A modular fusion category is a pre-modular fusion category such that the
S
-matrix
S
is non-degenerate, equivalently
\mathcal{C}^{\prime}=\text{Vec}

[
7
,
10
,
15
]
. Moreover, the
S
-matrix a modular fusion category
\mathcal{C}
determines the multiplication of the Grothendieck ring
\text{Gr}(\mathcal{C})
by the famous Verlinde formula
[
10
]
, i.e., for
X,Y,Z\in\mathcal{O}(\mathcal{C})
,

\displaystyle N_{X,Y}^{Z}:=\dim_{\mathbb{C}}(\text{Hom}(X\otimes Y,Z))=\sum_{W\in\mathcal{O}(\mathcal{C})}\frac{S_{X,W}S_{Y,W}S_{Z^{*},W}}{\dim(W)}.

(2)

Moreover, given a modular fusion category
\mathcal{C}
, the Verlinde formula (
2
) also implies that the set of ring homomorphism from
\text{Gr}(\mathcal{C})
to
\mathbb{C}
is in bijective correspondence with
\mathcal{O}(\mathcal{C})

[
10
]
. Explicitly, let
X\in\mathcal{O}(\mathcal{C})
, then
h_{X}(Y):=\frac{S_{X,Y}}{\dim(X)}
defines a ring homomorphism from
\text{Gr}(\mathcal{C})
to
\mathbb{C}
,
\forall Y\in\mathcal{O}(\mathcal{C})
; notice that the set of formal codegrees of
\mathcal{C}
is
\{\frac{\dim(\mathcal{C})}{\dim(X)^{2}}|X\in\mathcal{O}(\mathcal{C})\}
due to the Verlinde formula (
2
). Since
\sigma\circ h_{X}(-)
is also a ring homomorphism of
\text{Gr}(\mathcal{C})
, where
\sigma\in\text{Gal}(\overline{\mathbb{Q}}/\mathbb{Q})
, there is a unique simple object
Y
such that
\sigma\circ h_{X}(-)=h_{Y}(-)
. Hence, there is a unique permutation
\hat{\sigma}
of
\mathcal{O}(\mathcal{C})
such that
\hat{\sigma}(X)=Y
and
\sigma\circ h_{X}(-)=h_{\hat{\sigma}(X)}(-)
. We call the subset

\displaystyle\mathcal{O}_{X}(\mathcal{C}):=\{Y\in\mathcal{O}(\mathcal{C})|Y=\hat{\sigma}(X)~\text{for some $\sigma\in\text{Gal}(\overline{\mathbb{Q}}/\mathbb{Q})$}\}

the Galois orbit of the simple object
X
. When
\mathcal{O}_{I}(\mathcal{C})=\mathcal{O}(\mathcal{C})
, then
\mathcal{C}
is said to be transitive; transitive modular fusion categories are classified explicitly
[
16
, Theorem II]
.

The modular data
(S,T)
of a modular fusion category
\mathcal{C}
is also connected closely with the congruence representations of the modular group
\text{SL}(2,\mathbb{Z})

[
3
,
4
,
6
]
, which is generated by
\mathfrak{s}=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}0&1\\
-1&0\\
\end{array}\right)
and
\mathfrak{t}=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}1&1\\
0&1\\
\end{array}\right)
with relations
\mathfrak{s}^{4}=1
and
(\mathfrak{s}\mathfrak{t})^{3}=\mathfrak{s}^{2}
. Explicitly, the morphism
\mathfrak{s}\mapsto\frac{1}{\sqrt{\dim(\mathcal{C})}}S
,
\mathfrak{t}\mapsto T
defines a projective representation of
\text{SL}(2,\mathbb{Z})

[
10
, Theorem 8.16.1]
, where
\sqrt{\dim(\mathcal{C})}
is the positive square root of
\dim(\mathcal{C})
.

Let
\tau^{\pm}(\mathcal{C}):=\sum_{X\in\mathcal{O}(\mathcal{C})}\dim(X)^{2}\theta_{X}^{\pm}

be the Gauss sums of
\mathcal{C}

[
7
,
10
]

. It follows from

]
that there always exists a third root
\xi
of

such that

and

of
\text{SL}(2,\mathbb{Z})
of level
N

\ker(\rho_{\mathcal{C}})
is a congruence subgroup of level

. Moreover,
\mathbb{Q}(S)\subseteq\mathbb{Q}(T)\subseteq\mathbb{Q}(t)

[

, the normalized ribbon structure of the simple object

\forall X\in\mathcal{O}(\mathcal{C})

\sigma^{2}(t_{X})=t_{\hat{\sigma}(X)}
,

Let

Remark 2.1
.

Let
\mathcal{C}
be a non-trivial modular fusion category with
N(\dim(\mathcal{C}))=p^{N}
, where
p
is an odd prime. Then there exists a simple object
X
such that
p
divides
\text{ord}(t_{X})
.

Indeed, if
t_{Y}=1
for all
Y\in\mathcal{O}(\mathcal{C})
, then
\xi=1
as
t_{I}=1/\xi=1
, so
\theta_{Y}=1

(\forall Y\in\mathcal{O}(\mathcal{C}))
. Notice that the balancing equation
[
10
]
then implies that
s_{X,Y}=\dim(X)\dim(Y)
, particularly the
S
-matrix of
\mathcal{C}
can’t be non-degenerate, it is a contradiction.

Moreover, let
X
be the simple object of
\mathcal{C}
such that
\text{ord}(t_{X})=p^{n}
is maximal. Then

\displaystyle\text{rank}(\mathcal{C})\geq\frac{p^{n-1}(p-1)}{2}.

By the Galois symmetry of modular fusion categories
[
6
]
, we have
\sigma^{2}(t_{X})=t_{\hat{\sigma}(X)}
, then

\displaystyle\text{Gal}(\mathbb{Q}(t_{X})/\mathbb{Q})\cdot t_{X}={\{t_{\hat{\sigma}(X)}=\sigma^{2}(t_{X})|\sigma\in\text{Gal}(\mathbb{Q}(t_{X})/\mathbb{Q})}\},

hence the number of Galois conjugates of

is greater or equal to the order of the following subgroup

\displaystyle\text{Gal}(\mathbb{Q}(t_{X})/\mathbb{Q})^{2}:={\{\sigma^{2}|\sigma\in\text{Gal}(\mathbb{Q}(t_{X})/\mathbb{Q})}\}

of
\text{Gal}(\mathbb{Q}(t_{X})/\mathbb{Q})
. It follows immediately that the order of
\text{Gal}(\mathbb{Q}(t_{X})/\mathbb{Q})^{2}
is exactly
\frac{p^{n-1}(p-1)}{2}
, since
\text{Gal}(\mathbb{Q}(t_{X})/\mathbb{Q})
is a cyclic group of order
p^{n-1}(p-1)
.

Throughout this paper, we always use

Proof.

Since

does not divides
\dim(\mathcal{C})
. Hence, all formal codegrees of
\mathcal{C}
are Galois conjugates of
\dim(\mathcal{C})

[

, Theorem 4.4]

\mathcal{C}
is a modular fusion category, formal codegrees of

of
\mathcal{C}

\displaystyle\frac{\dim(\mathcal{C})}{\dim(X)^{2}}=\sigma(\dim(\mathcal{C}))=\sigma\left(\frac{\dim(\mathcal{C})}{\dim(I)^{2}}\right)=\frac{\dim(\mathcal{C})}{\dim(\hat{\sigma}(I))^{2}},

[

]

.

as modular fusion category, where

\displaystyle\dim(\mathcal{C}(\mathfrak{sl}_{2},p-2)_{\text{ad}})=\frac{p}{4\text{sin}^{2}(\pi/p)},~N\left(\frac{p}{4\text{sin}^{2}(\pi/p)}\right)=p^{(p-3)/2},

Lemma 3.3

is braided tensor equivalent to a Galois conjugate of one of the following modular fusion categories

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\mathcal{C}(\mathfrak{sl}_{2},3)_{\text{ad}}\boxtimes\mathcal{C}(\mathfrak{sl}_{2},3)^{\sigma}_{\text{ad}}\boxtimes\mathcal{C}(\mathbb{Z}_{5},\eta),&\\
\mathcal{C}(\mathfrak{sl}_{2},3)_{\text{ad}}\boxtimes\mathcal{C}(\mathfrak{sl}_{2},3)_{\text{ad}}\boxtimes\mathcal{C}(\mathfrak{sl}_{2},3)^{\sigma}_{\text{ad}}\boxtimes\mathcal{C}(\mathfrak{sl}_{2},3)^{\sigma}_{\text{ad}},&\end{array}\right.&\hbox{if $p=5;$}\\
\mathcal{C}(\mathfrak{sl}_{2},5)_{\text{ad}}\boxtimes\mathcal{C}(\mathfrak{sl}_{2},5)^{\tau}_{\text{ad}}\boxtimes\mathcal{C}(\mathfrak{sl}_{2},5)^{\tau^{2}}_{\text{ad}},&\hbox{if $p=7;$}\\
\mathcal{C}(\mathfrak{sl}_{2},9)_{\text{ad}}\boxtimes\mathcal{C}(\mathfrak{so}_{5},\frac{5}{2})_{\text{ad}}^{\nu}&\hbox{if $p=11$.}\end{array}\right.

where
\sigma\in\text{Gal}(\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{5})/\mathbb{Q})
such that
\sigma(\zeta_{5})=\zeta_{5}^{2}
,
\tau\in\text{Gal}(\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{7})/\mathbb{Q})
such that
\tau(\zeta_{7})=\zeta_{7}^{2}
and
\nu\in\text{Gal}(\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{11})/\mathbb{Q})
such that
\nu(\zeta_{11})=\zeta_{11}^{9}
.

This completes the classification of non-simple modular fusion categories of global dimension
p^{2}
. In addition, it is worth to note that
\mathcal{C}(\mathfrak{so}_{5},\frac{3}{2})_{\text{ad}}
and its Galois conjugates are simple modular fusion categories of global dimension
9
.

Question 4.16
.

Let
p>3
be a prime. Is there a simple modular fusion category
\mathcal{C}
of global dimension
p^{2}
?

Moreover, let
\mathcal{D}
be a spherical fusion category of global dimension
p
, then its Drinfeld center
\mathcal{Z}(\mathcal{D})
is a modular fusion category of global dimension
p^{2}
. Therefore, a negative answer to Question
4.16
will also result in a complete classification of spherical fusion categories of prime global dimensions.

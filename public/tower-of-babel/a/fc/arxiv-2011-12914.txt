1.
Introduction

Let
V
be a finite dimensional vector space over a field
F
.
Let
G\subset GL(V)
be a finite group and
S(V)
the symmetric algebra of
V
.

G
acts as a finite group of automorphisms on
S(V)
.
Let
S(V)^{G}=\{x\in S(V)|g(x)=x,\forall g\in G\}
be the ring of
G
-invariants.
The determination of the polynomial property of
S(V)^{G}
is an old problem.
The basic result is the following.
We refer to
[
1
]
,
[
17
]
for an historical account.

Theorem
.

(Shephard-Todd-Chevalley-Serre).
Suppose in addition that
(p,|G|)=1
, where
p=\Char F
. Then
S(V)^{G}
is a polynomial ring if and only if
G
is generated by pseudo-reflections.

Serre also proved the following implications, where no restrictions on
p
are assumed.

Theorem
.

[
18
]
,
[
3
, V, §6, exercise 8]
.
Suppose
S(V)^{G}
is a polynomial ring.
Then
G
is generated by pseudo-reflections and
S(V)^{G_{U}}
is a polynomial ring for each subspace
U\subset V^{*}
, where
G_{U}=\{g\in G|g(u)=u,\forall u\in U\}
.

The second implication was firstly proved by R. Steinberg
[
19
]
in case
F=\mathbb{C}
.
This theorem suggests a starting point in answering the polynomiality question in the modular case, that is if
p
divides
|G|
.
The following result by Nakajima was the first positive result.

Theorem
.

[
16
]
.
Suppose
G\subset GL(V)
is a finite
p
-group, where
\dim_{F}V=n
is finite, and
F=\mathbb{F}_{p}
is the prime field.
Then
S(V)^{G}
is a polynomial ring if and only if:

There is a basis of
V

\{z_{1},...,z_{n}\}
with
g(z_{1})=z_{1}
,
(g-1)(z_{i})\in Fz_{1}+\cdots+Fz_{i-1}
,
i=2,...,n
, for each
g\in G
such that:

|G-\orbit(z_{1})|\cdots|G-\orbit(z_{n})|=|G|.

Actually the ”if” part always implies the polynomiality of
S(V)^{G}
with no restriction on
F
.
The necessity of the condition is only granted when
F=\mathbb{F}_{p}
.
As such this theorem has no relation to Serre’s result.

Extending and completing earlier results of Nakajima, Kemper-Malle established,using classification theorems for finite irreducible groups, the following result.

Theorem
.

(
[
12
, Main Theorem]
and the corrections in
[
5
, p. 119]
).
Suppose
G\subset GL(V)
is a finite group, where
V
is a finite dimensional irreducible
G
-module.
Then
S(V)^{G}
is a polynomial ring if and only if:

(1)

S(V)^{G_{U}}
is a polynomial ring for each subspace
U\subset V^{*}
with
\dim_{F}U\geq 1
, and

(2)

G
is generated by pseudo-reflections.

Actually one merely needs assuming
\dim_{F}U=1
in item
(1)
.
However, in spite of a reduction to an indecomposable
V
, Kemper-Malle refrained from stating the following conjecture.

Conjecture C
.

Suppose
G\subset GL(V)
is a finite group, where
V
is finite dimensional.
Then
S(V)^{G}
is a polynomial ring if and only if:

\indent(C1)

S(V)^{G_{U}}
is a polynomial ring for each subspace
U\subset V^{*}
with
\dim_{F}U=1
, and

\indent(C2)

G
is generated by pseudo-reflections.

Our result, in case of
\dim_{F}V\geq 4
, initiated from the trivial observation that Conjecture C holds trivially for a
p
-group

.
Indeed the action of

V^{*}
can always be simultaneously triangulized, in particular there exists

Therefore

This suggests, in the spirit of Conjecture C, that for a finite
p
-group, there may exist a weaker assumption on subspaces of

, e.g. on the set
\{U|U\subset V^{*}
,
\dim_{F}U=2\}
.
Indeed our main result is of this nature:

Theorem B
.

Suppose
G\subset GL(V)
is a finite
p
-group, where
p=\Char F
,
\dim_{F}V\geq 4
. Then
S(V)^{G}
is a polynomial ring if and only if:

(1)

S(V)^{G_{U}}

\dim_{F}U=2

G_{U}=\{g\in G|g(u)=u

(2)

Moreover

(3)

\dim(
non-smooth locus of

\leq 1

So Theorem B has a singularity theory flavour. Note that the assumption

One may be puzzled by the omission of the condition ”

The simple answer is that conditions B(1) and B(2) actually imply it.
This is a consequence of a result of Kemper

Another seemingly puzzling question is the appearance of the Cohen-Macaulay assumption in Theorem B(2), as it neither appears in Kemper-Malle
[

In fact the Cohen-Macaulay property is a consequence of condition (C1).

In case of
\dim_{F}V=3

Theorem A
.

Suppose
G\subset GL(V)
is a finite
p
-group, where
\dim_{F}V=3

p=\Char F

Then
S(V)^{G}
is a polynomial ring if and only if
G
is generated by transvections.

The proof of Theorem A is computational.
By a standard result
G
has the following presentations of matrices
G\subset\begin{bmatrix}1&F&F\\
0&1&F\\
0&0&1\end{bmatrix}
,
namely
V
has a basis
\{v_{1},v_{2},v_{3}\}
with
g(v_{1})=v_{1}
,
(g-1)(v_{2})\in Fv_{1}
,
(g-1)(v_{3})\in Fv_{1}+Fv_{2}
, for each
g\in G

The simpler cases
G\subset\begin{bmatrix}1&F&F\\
0&1&0\\
0&0&1\end{bmatrix}

and
G\subset\begin{bmatrix}1&0&F\\
0&1&F\\
0&0&1\end{bmatrix}

are handled by earlier results of Nakajima.
The remaining case is more involved and the end result

The following is a sketch of the proof of Theorem B, if

is perfect (Theorem
2.15
).
It is done by a sequence of hypersurface sections reducing the problem, after

steps to a
3

Theorem A cannot be used directly, but provides us with three elements which are a system of parameters (s.o.p.).
The homogenous lifts in
S(V)^{G}
of these three elements together with the homogenous lifts in
S(V)^{G}
of the elements involved in the definition of each hypersurface section, form a homogenous s.o.p. with their degree’s product being equal to
|G|
.
This implies that
S(V)^{G}
is generated by these
n
lifts and consequently is a polynomial ring.
The general case requires some further considerations about the non-smooth locus.
A simplified proof in case
dim_{F}V=4
is presented in the appendix.

We end with two
\dim_{F}V=4

In the first one

In the second example, we have, for the same group

It is a well-known classical result that for a a finite

p

p=\Char F>0

F=F^{p}
.
Let

(in
n

(1)

v\in A^{G}\cap V
;

(2)

x_{i}

\deg x_{i}=p^{e_{i}}
,

, where

,

, and
\{v,v_{2},...,v_{n}\}
is a basis of
V
;

(3)

e_{1}\leq e_{2}\leq\cdots\leq e_{n}
.

Then there exist homogenous elements

\deg y_{i}=p^{e_{i}}

,
i=2,...,n
, having the following properties:

(4)

(g-1)(y_{2})\in F[v^{p^{e_{1}}}]_{+}

,

\{v,u_{2},...,u_{n}\}
is a triangulating basis for the action of
G
on
V
;

Proof:

\{x_{2},...,x_{n}\}=\cup_{r=1}^{l}\{x_{i_{r-1}+1},...,x_{i_{r}}\}

l

Suppose
(4)

, as well as

\begin{split}(g-1)(x_{i})&=\sum_{j=s+1}^{t}b_{ij}(g)x_{j}+x,\\
&\text{ where }b_{ij}(g)\in F,\text{ }x\in F[v^{p^{e_{1}}},x_{2},...,x_{s}]_{+},\text{ for each }g\in G.\end{split}

(v^{p^{e_{1}}},x_{2},...,x_{s})
, the prime graded ideal generated by

in
F[v^{p^{e_{1}}},x_{2},...,x_{t}]

G
-stable.
Consequently
G
acts by graded automorphisms on

F[v^{p^{e_{1}}},x_{2},...,x_{t}]/(v^{p^{e_{l}}},x_{2},...,x_{s})=F[\bar{x}_{s+1},...,\bar{x}_{t}]

2.2
) a linear action on

(t-s)
-dimensional
F
-subspace of the polynomial ring

.
It implies, since
G
is a
p
-group, the existence of a
W
-basis
\{z_{s+1},...,z_{t}\}
which triangulates the action of
G
on
W
. Thus:

(2.3)

(g-1)(z_{s+1})=0,\text{ }(g-1)(z_{i})=\sum_{j=s+1}^{i-1}a_{ij}(g)z_{j},\text{ }a_{ij}(g)\in F,\text{ }s+2\leq i\leq t.

.

.

y_{i}-u_{i}^{p^{e_{i}}}\in v^{p^{e_{1}}}S(V)

.

.

.

(g-1)(u_{i})^{p^{e_{i}}}\in(v,u_{2},...,u_{i-1})

F[w_{1},y,X_{i+1}]=S(V)^{<\sigma_{i+1},...,\sigma_{1},\tau_{1},...,\tau_{m},\epsilon_{1},...,\epsilon_{n}>}
.
Hence
S(V)^{T}=F[w_{1},y,X]
, where
X:=X_{n}
.
\Box

We finally arrive at the main result of this section.

Theorem 3.3
.

Suppose
T\subset GL(V)
is a finite transvection group,
dim_{F}V=3
and
Fw_{1}\subset W=Fw_{2}+Fw_{1}\subset V
is a flag of
T
-modules.
Then
S(V)^{T}
is a polynomial ring.
Moreover
S(V)^{T}=F[w_{1},y,X]
, with
y\in S(W)
, if
T\not\subset\begin{bmatrix}1&0&F\\
0&1&F\\
0&0&1\end{bmatrix}
,

\begin{bmatrix}1&F&F\\
0&1&0\\
0&0&1\end{bmatrix}
.

Proof:

Let
\theta\in T
be a transvection.
Then
\theta(w_{1})=w_{1}
.
Also
\theta_{|W}
is a transvection on
W
(or
1_{W}
) implying that
\theta_{|W}\in\begin{bmatrix}1&F\\
0&1\end{bmatrix}
(with respect to the basis
\{w_{2},w_{1}\}
).
Since
det\theta=1
it follows that
\theta\in\begin{bmatrix}1&F&F\\
0&1&F\\
0&0&1\end{bmatrix}
, with respect to the basis
\{v,w_{2},w_{1}\}
where
v\in V-W
.
Since
rank(\theta-1_{V})=1
, it follows that either
\theta\in\begin{bmatrix}1&F&F\\
0&1&0\\
0&0&1\end{bmatrix}
or
\theta\in\begin{bmatrix}1&0&F\\
0&1&F\\
0&0&1\end{bmatrix}
.
Therefore each transvection generator of
T
has the form of either (1), (2) or (3) of Proposition
3.2
.

If
\sigma_{r}=\cdots=\sigma_{1}=1
, then by Proposition
2.9

S(V)^{T}
is a polynomial ring.
Similarly if
\tau_{1}=\cdots=\tau_{m}=1
, then by Proposition
2.7

S(V)^{T}
is again a polynomial ring.
So we may assume that
T
is generated by transvections as described in Proposition
3.2
.

Keeping the notation of Proposition
3.2
, then since
(H,H)
is a finite subgroup of
(T,T)\subseteq\begin{bmatrix}1&0&F\\
0&1&0\\
0&0&1\end{bmatrix}

it follows that the generators of
(H,H)
can be added to
<\epsilon_{1},...,\epsilon_{n}>
, if they are not already there, so condition (4) of Proposition
3.2
can be arranged.
Finally condition (5) of Proposition
3.2
can also be arranged by dropping any redundant generator.
Thus all the requirements of Proposition
3.2
are in place, and
S(V)^{T}
is therefore a polynomial ring.
\Box

The next result is a direct consequence.

Theorem 3.4
.

Let
T\subset GL(V)
be a finite
p
-group which is generated by transvections, where
p=\Char F>0
and
\dim_{F}V=3
.
Then
S(V)^{T}
is a polynomial ring.

Proof:

By a standard result for
p
-groups, there is a flag of
T
-modules
Fw_{1}\subset W=Fw_{2}+Fw_{1}\subset V
.
Therefore Theorem
3.3
is applicable.
\Box

The proof of Theorem
2.15
when
\dim_{F}V=4
,
F=F^{p}

We assume that
S(V)^{G}
is Cohen-Macaulay and satisfies Serre’s
R_{2}

G
is generated by transvections by Lemma
2.5
.
By a standard result there is a non-zero element
v_{1}\in V
with
g(v_{1})=v_{1}
, for each
g\in G
.

Let
H_{1}:=\{g\in G|(g-1)(V)\subseteq Fv_{1}\}
,
V_{2}:=V/Fv_{1}
.
Then since
H_{1}=\ker(G\rightarrow G_{|V_{2}})
,
H_{1}
is normal in
G
and
G/H_{1}:=G_{2}
acts faithfully and linearly on
V_{2}
.
It is a consequence of Proposition
2.9
that
S(V)^{H_{1}}=F[v_{1},x_{2},x_{3},x_{4}]
is a polynomial ring, where
\{x_{2},x_{3},x_{4}\}
are homogenous elements,
\deg x_{i}=p^{f_{i}}
, and
x_{i}-u_{i}^{p^{f_{i}}}\in v_{1}S(V)
,
u_{i}\in V
,
i=2,3,4
.
Consequently by Proposition
2.14

|H_{1}|=p^{f_{2}}\cdot p^{f_{2}}\cdot p^{f_{4}}
.

Since
S(V_{2})\supset F[\bar{u}_{2}^{p^{f_{2}}},\bar{u}_{3}^{p^{f_{3}}},\bar{u}_{4}^{p^{f_{4}}}]=S(V)^{H_{1}}/v_{1}S(V)^{H_{1}}
and the latter is a polynomial ring in
3

\{\bar{u}_{2},\bar{u}_{3},\bar{u}_{4}\}
is a basis of
V_{2}
.
This also shows that
G_{2}
acts faithfully on
S(V)^{H_{1}}/v_{1}S(V)^{H_{1}}
.
Indeed if
g\in G
with
(g-1)(S(V)^{H_{1}})\subseteq v_{1}S(V)^{H_{1}}
, then
(g-1)(u_{i})^{p^{f_{i}}}\in v_{1}S(V)
and since the latter is prime ideal
(g-1)(u_{i})\in v_{1}S(V)\cap V=Fv_{1}
, implying, since
\{v_{1},u_{2},u_{3},u_{4}\}
is a basis of
V
, that
g\in H_{1}
. Consequently by Proposition
2.13
, using the Cohen-Macaulay and
R_{2}
properties, we have that
S(V)^{G}/(v_{1})=[S(V)^{H_{1}}/v_{1}S(V)^{H_{1}}]^{G_{2}}
.

We may assume that
f_{1}\leq f_{2}\leq f_{3}
.
In order to exploit the
3
-dimensional result of
S(V_{2})^{G_{2}}
(Theorem A),
\{\bar{u}_{2},\bar{u}_{3},\bar{u}_{4}\}
should have been a triangulating basis for the action of
G_{2}
on
V_{2}
.
We therefore change, by Proposition
2.11
, the generators
\{x_{2},x_{3},x_{4}\}
into
\{y_{2},y_{3},y_{4}\}
having the properties:

(1)

S(V)^{H_{1}}=F[v_{1},y_{2},y_{3},y_{4}]
,
\deg y_{i}=p^{f_{i}}
,
y_{i}-w_{i}^{p^{f_{i}}}\in v_{1}S(V)
,
i=2,3,4
, and

(2)

(g-1)(y_{2})\in Fv_{1}^{p^{f_{2}}}
,
(g-1)(y_{i})\in F[v_{1},y_{2},...,y_{i-1}]_{+}
,
i=3,4
,
\forall g\in G
.

Hence
(g-1)(\bar{w}_{2})^{p^{f_{2}}}=0
,
(g-1)(\bar{w}_{i})^{p^{f_{i}}}\in(\bar{w}_{2},...,\bar{w}_{i-1})S(V_{2})
,
i=3,4
,
\forall g\in G_{2}
.
Since the latter is a prime ideal in
S(V_{2})
, we have by degree consideration:

g(\bar{w}_{2})=\bar{w}_{2},\text{ }(g-1)(\bar{w}_{i})\in(\bar{w}_{2},...,\bar{w}_{i-1})S(V_{2})\cap V_{2}=F\bar{w}_{2}+\cdots+F\bar{w}_{i-1},\text{ }i=3,4,\text{ }\forall g\in G_{2}.

G_{2}
is generated by transvections by Lemma
2.6
.
We can use now the
3
-dimensional result (Theorem A) according to the following separation into cases, where all matrices are given with respect to the
G_{2}
-triangulating basis
\{\bar{w}_{2},\bar{w}_{3},\bar{w}_{4}\}
of

.

\textbf{1: }G_{2}\subset\begin{bmatrix}1&0&F\\
0&1&F\\
0&0&1\end{bmatrix},\textbf{ 2: }G_{2}\subset\begin{bmatrix}1&F&F\\

Case 1:
This case is translated into
(g-1)(\bar{w}_{4})

(g-1)(\bar{w}_{3})\in F\bar{w}_{2}

g(\bar{w}_{2})=\bar{w}_{2}

S(V)^{G}/(v_{1})=F[\bar{y}_{2},\bar{y}_{3},\bar{y}_{4}]^{G_{2}}=F[\bar{w}_{2}^{p^{f_{2}}},\bar{w}_{3}^{p^{f_{3}}},\bar{w}_{4}^{p^{f_{4}}}]^{G_{2}}=F[\bar{w}_{2}^{p^{f_{2}}},y,z].

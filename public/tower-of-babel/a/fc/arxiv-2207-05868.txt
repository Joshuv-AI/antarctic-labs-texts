Approximation algorithms for job scheduling with block-type conflict graphs
Hanna Furmańczyk, Tytus Pikies, Inka Sokołowska, Krzysztof Turowski

Abstract
The problem of scheduling jobs on parallel machines (identical, uniform, or unrelated), under incompatibility relation modeled as a block graph, under the makespan optimality criterion, is considered in this paper. No two jobs that are in the relation (equivalently in the same block) may be scheduled on the same machine in this model. The presented model stems from a well-established line of research combining scheduling theory with methods relevant to graph coloring. Recently, cluster graphs and their extensions like block graphs were given additional attention. We complement hardness results provided by other researchers for block graphs by providing approximation algorithms. In particular, we provide a 2 2 -approximation algorithm for P | G = block graph | C max P|G=\textit{block graph}|C_{\max} and a PTAS for the case when the jobs are unit time in addition. In the case of uniform machines, we analyze two cases. The first one is when the number of blocks is bounded, i.e. Q | G = k-block graph | C max Q|G=\textit{{k}-block graph}|C_{\max} . For this case, we provide a PTAS, improving upon results presented by D. Page and R. Solis-Oba. The improvement is two-fold: we allow richer graph structure, and we allow the number of machine speeds to be part of the input. Due to strong NP-hardness of Q | G = 2-clique graph | C max Q|G=\textit{{2}-clique graph}|C_{\max} , the result establishes the approximation status of Q | G = k-block graph | C max Q|G=\textit{{k}-block graph}|C_{\max} . The PTAS might be of independent interest because the problem is tightly related to the NUMERICAL k k -DIMENSIONAL MATCHING WITH TARGET SUMS problem. The second case that we analyze is when the number of blocks is arbitrary, but the number of cut-vertices is bounded and jobs are of unit time. In this case, we present an exact algorithm. In addition, we present an FPTAS for graphs with bounded treewidth and a bounded number of unrelated machines. The paper ends with extensive tests of the selected algorithms.

block graphs, bounded treewidth graphs, scheduling, identical machines, uniform machines, unrelated machines, incompatibility/conflict graph

The sources of wealth in many current societies are skills and knowledge of the employees.
To increase welfare, obstacles blocking utilization of the skills and potential should be removed.
Imagine that there is some corporation lacking in the fields of inclusivity and diversity; hence, there is a huge risk that some talents are wasted.
To remedy the situation, the corporation decided to hire a consulting firm, at which we work.
At our disposal there is a group of inclusivity officers who can be assigned to perform group trainings.
The corporation has a hierarchical structure: a person can have some co-workers, subordinates, and a superior.
We like to assign the employees to the instructors in a way that no instructor will train persons that are in a direct business relationship.
This is due to the fact, that it will be good to avoid mixing the inclusivity problems with business hierarchy – the hierarchy might interfere with learning.
Also, providing a more confidential environment is valuable.
How to perform the training to minimize the time of solving the inclusivity problems, if:

1.

all the officers have equal skills and the persons require a similar amount of training,

2.

the employees require a different amount of training,

3.

the skills of the officers are diverse, but can be grouped within teams,

4.

some employees have special needs and some officers can more efficiently train them?

We consider the problem of makespan minimization for job scheduling on parallel machines with a conflict graph.
Formally, an instance of the problem is characterized by

The set of vertices of

Due to the diverse machine environment, we define the

•

Identical machines

A schedule is a function

Task scheduling problems can often be expressed in the language of graph coloring.

In the paper, we focus on block graphs since this class of graphs best reflects the hierarchical structure in corporations which is of great importance in our application.

Let us return to the inclusivity problem presented earlier, and observe that it can be well modeled in the presented framework as a scheduling problem on block graphs:

1.

a training of the person can be modeled as a job,

2.

an instructor can be modeled as a machine,

3.

a hierarchy of employees can be modeled as a block graph:
a team has usually one leader, but each person in a team can be a leader for another team, lower in the hierarchy, hence a team can be represented as a block,

4.

the criterion of solving the problems as soon as possible can be modeled by
C_{\max}
.

This is only one sample application of scheduling in the presented model.
The model with other classes of graphs has many applications like scheduling jobs on a system with unstable power supply

Pikies et al., 2022

Also, for an earlier review of possible use cases see

Previous work and our results

We refer the reader to standard textbooks for other notions of graph theory, scheduling theory, and approximation theory e.g.
Brucker, 2006
;
Diestel, 2005
;
Ausiello et al., 1999
;
Cygan et al., 2015
for an overview of the respective topics. For more information about approximation schemes classes (

,

,

) see also
Epstein and Sgall, 2004
;

;

.

Graham provided one of the first analyses of a scheduling problem, proving that the list scheduling is a constant approximation ratio algorithm for
P||C_{\max}

Graham, 1966
.
It is well-known that
P2||C_{\max}
problem is equivalent to the NP-hard
PARTITION
problem
Garey and Johnson, 1979
.
However, it was also proved that
Q||C_{\max}
(and therefore
P||C_{\max}
as well) admits a polynomial time approximation scheme (PTAS)
Hochbaum and Shmoys, 1988
and for
Rm||C_{\max}
there exists a fully polynomial time approximation scheme (FPTAS)
Horowitz and Sahni, 1976
.
Moreover,
Q|p_{j}=1|C_{\max}
can be solved optimally in
\textnormal{O}(\min\{n+m\log{m},n\log{m}\})
time
Dessouky et al., 1990
.
For the most general case, namely
R||C_{\max}
, a
(2-\frac{1}{m})
-approximation algorithm was provided in
Shchepin and Vakhania, 2005
.
On the other hand, it was proved that there is no polynomial algorithm with an approximation ratio better than
\frac{3}{2}
, unless
\textnormal{P}=\textnormal{NP}

Lenstra et al., 1990
.

The idea of incompatibility relation and conflict graph as understood in this paper was introduced by Bodlaender and Jansen in
Bodlaender and Jansen, 1993
, and first significantly developed by Bodlaender et al. in
Bodlaender et al., 1994
.
These authors developed a
2
-approximation algorithm for
P|G=bipartite|C_{\max}
running in
\textnormal{O}(n\log{m}
) time, and showed that there cannot exist a polynomial-time approximation algorithm for approximating the optimum makespan with worst-case ratio
2-\varepsilon
for any fixed
\varepsilon>0
unless
\textnormal{P}=\textnormal{NP}
, while for the special case of
P|G=tree|C_{\max}
there exists an algorithm in said running time finding a solution with length at most
5/3
times the length of the optimum makespan.
They also showed that PTAS for the problems
P2|G|C_{\max}
and
Pm|\textrm{tw}(G)\leq k|C_{\max}
– and we note that the latter result is especially relevant for this paper since
Pm|G=\textit{block graph}|C_{\max}
is a subproblem of
Pm|\textrm{tw}(G)\leq m|C_{\max}
.

Clearly, each forest is also a block graph with every block corresponding to an edge. As
Pikies et al., 2022
noted, the problem
P|G,p_{j}=1|C_{\max}
is closely tied to
MUTUAL EXCLUSION SCHEDULING
problem, in which we are looking for a schedule so that no two jobs are connected by an edge in
G
are executed at the same time.
Thus, from a polynomial time optimal algorithm for
MUTUAL EXCLUSION SCHEDULING
problem, presented in
Baker and Coffman Jr., 1996
, they inferred that
P|G=\textit{forest},p_{j}=1|C_{\max}
can be solved optimality in polynomial time.

For the above results and throughout this paper there is assumed a customary notion that the number of jobs and machines are encoded in unary form, and that a schedule is a function from tasks to machines. Additionally, our encoding of choice for a block graph as a part of an input instance is a block-cut tree, defined above (see
Figure

1
).
However, let us point out that there exists also another approach to encoding input instance and solution which results in a different boundary between easy and hard problems. In
Mallek and Boudhar, 2022
the authors assumed that the number of jobs and machines is encoded in binary (and the information about the conflict graph is provided in some concise form), and thus by reduction from
PARTITION
problem they proved that
P2|G=\textit{forest},p_{j}=1|C_{\max}
is NP-hard, but still if we applied a pseudopolynomial algorithm for
PARTITION
we would get an algorithm with running time
\textnormal{O}(nm)
. We also note that the same difference occurred before for
Q|G=\textit{complete 2-partite},p_{j}=1|C_{\max}
problem: on the one hand in
Pikies et al., 2022
it was proved that it admits a
\textnormal{O}(mn^{3}\log(mn))
algorithm when number of jobs and machines is encoded in unary form, but on the other hand
Mallek et al., 2019
showed that this problem is NP-hard, when
n
and
m
are encoded in binary, and the conflict graph is encoded in
\textnormal{O}(poly(\log{n}))
bits.

Let us denote by
cliques
a graph consisting of disjoint cliques and by
k-clique graph
a
cliques
with at most
k
connected components.
It is natural to view
block graph
and
k-block graph
as a generalization of
cliques
and
k-clique graph
, respectively.
An EPTAS for
P|G=\textit{cliques}|C_{\max}
was provided by
Grage et al., 2019
, improving upon the PTAS given by Das and Wiese
Das and Wiese, 2017
.
In
Page and Solis-Oba, 2020
it was proved that
Q|G=\textit{cliques},p_{j}=1,\mathcal{M}_{j}|C_{\max}
(where
\mathcal{M}_{j}
denotes that we additionally restrict the subset of machines available for each job) can be solved in polynomial time, while
Q|\textit{{2}-clique graph}|C_{\max}
is strongly NP-hard; the hardness result can be easily extended to any fixed number of cliques.
The case of
R|G=\textit{cliques}|C_{\max}
has also been studied in
Das and Wiese, 2017
.
In that paper, it was shown that there is no constant approximation algorithm in the general case and even in some more restricted cases.

Finally, let us turn our attention to the equitable coloring problem.
It was proved that this problem is strongly NP-hard for block graphs
Gomes et al., 2019
, and as a direct consequence the problem
P|G=\textit{block graph},p_{j}=1|C_{\max}
is strongly NP-hard as well.

Against this background, we focus our attention on the conflict graph being a block graph and we consider the problem of task scheduling with such a conflict graph in different variants.
As it was mentioned above, already the problem of unit-time task scheduling on identical machines with the conflict block graph is strongly NP-hard, thus we mostly focus on finding good approximation algorithms for hard problems.

The organization of the paper is as follows:
we start in
Section

2
with scheduling on identical machines.
First, we give a
2
-approximation algorithm for the general case with
\textnormal{O}(n\log{m})
running time.
Then, we consider the narrow case
P|G=\textit{block graph},p_{j}=1|C_{\max}\leq k
and develop a polynomial time algorithm for it.
These two algorithms, in turn, are important subprocedures in a PTAS for
P|G=\textit{block graph},p_{j}=1|C_{\max}
.
In
Section

3
we turn our attention to the uniform machines. We analyze two cases: when the number of blocks is bounded and when this number is arbitrary, but the number of cut-vertices is bounded and jobs are of unit time. In the former case we provide a PTAS and in the latter one we devise an exact algorithm running in polynomial time. Note that the first mentioned result in a sense is the best possible, due to the strong NP-hardness of the problem
Page and Solis-Oba, 2020
.
In
Section

4
we consider unrelated machines and we provide an FPTAS for graphs with bounded treewidth and a bounded number of machines, which also captures a class of block graphs with bounded clique number.
Finally, in
Section

5
we present the results of extensive tests of our algorithms.

Thus, our approximation algorithms and schemes extend the knowledge in the area of job scheduling with conflict graphs.

.

.

Observe that the algorithm can be implemented in
\textnormal{O}(n\log{m})

At each

Then, we retrieve the first

\textnormal{O}(|B|\log{m})

Finally, we assign the jobs to the machines in the proper order and re-add the machines with updated loads to the heap in
\textnormal{O}(|B|\log{m})

Since

Lemma 2.13
.

Let an instance of
P|G=\textit{block graph},p_{j}=1|C_{\max}
with
n\geq 1
jobs, where
n=d(m-1)+r
for some
d\geq 0
and
r\in\{0,\ldots,m-2\}
, and
m\geq 2
machines be given.
The greedy algorithm returns a schedule, in which there are assigned at most
\lceil n/(m-1)\rceil
jobs to every machine.
Moreover, if
r=0
, then the constructed schedule has at least one machine with a load strictly less than
\lceil n/(m-1)\rceil
; otherwise, it has at least
m-r
such machines.

Proof.

The proof is by induction on the number of blocks.
For a graph having exactly one block the theorem holds: we assign at most one job to every machine, and clearly
\lceil n/(m-1)\rceil\geq 1
.
It is also easy to verify that if
r=0
then it has to be the case
n=m-1
for the problem to be feasible, thus there is one machine with a load less than
1
.
Similarly, the cases for
n\leq m-2
and
n=m
hold.

Now, assume that the theorem holds for all graphs composed of
k
blocks, and consider any block graph
G
composed of
k+1
blocks.
Let us fix a subgraph
G^{\prime}
of
G
on
n^{\prime}
vertices including only the first
k
blocks according to its block-cut tree pre-order traversal.
Let
n^{\prime}=d^{\prime}(m-1)+r^{\prime}
be the number of the vertices of
G^{\prime}
.
By induction assumption the greedy algorithm for
G^{\prime}
returns a schedule whose total makespan does not exceed
\lceil n^{\prime}/(m-1)\rceil
.

We schedule the remaining jobs from the last block according to the greedy algorithm, assigning at most one job to each machine.

If
r^{\prime}=0
then it is clear that the limit on the number of jobs increased by at least one since
\lceil n/(m-1)\rceil\geq d^{\prime}+1=\lceil n^{\prime}/(m-1)\rceil+1
. Thus the extended schedule satisfies the total makespan condition.
By considering separately cases
n-n^{\prime}\leq m-2
,
n-n^{\prime}=m-1
, and
n-n^{\prime}=m
we can verify directly that we have the required number of machines that are processing less than
\lceil n/(m-1)\rceil
jobs.

•

In the first case we have at most
r=n-n^{\prime}
machines where the new jobs are assigned.
Hence only these machines can have
\lceil n/(m-1)\rceil
jobs in the extended schedule. Thus
m-r
machines have to be loaded with less than
\lceil n/(m-1)\rceil
jobs.

•

In the second case
r=r^{\prime}=0
holds and clearly there is at least one machine with a load less than
\lceil n/(m-1)\rceil
.

•

The last case means that a clique on
m
vertices is added – the
(k+1)
-th block has no common vertex with other
k
blocks.
However, this means that the new upper limit is
\lceil n/(m-1)\rceil=\lceil n^{\prime}/(m-1)\rceil+2
and hence there are
m
machines with a load less than
\lceil n/(m-1)\rceil
, in fact.

For
r^{\prime}>0
we can split the proof into four cases.
In each case, by induction, there are at least
m-r^{\prime}
machines processing less than
\lceil n^{\prime}/(m-1)\rceil
jobs.
However, due to the fact that the blocks may have a common cut-vertex, perhaps one of them is not available for scheduling.

•

If
r^{\prime}+n-n^{\prime}<m-1
, or equivalently
n-n^{\prime}<m-r-1
, then all jobs of
G-G^{\prime}
can be scheduled on machines processing less than
\lceil n^{\prime}/(m-1)\rceil
jobs.
In the end
m-r^{\prime}-(n-n^{\prime})=m-r
machines process less than
\lceil n/(m-1)\rceil
jobs.

•

If
r^{\prime}+n-n^{\prime}=m-1
then we still schedule the jobs of
G-G^{\prime}
on the machines processing less than
\lceil n^{\prime}/(m-1)\rceil
jobs.
After extending the schedule there remains at least one machine with a load less than
\lceil n^{\prime}/(m-1)\rceil
.

•

If
m\leq r^{\prime}+n-n^{\prime}\leq 2m-3
then
r=r^{\prime}+(n-n^{\prime})-(m-1)
and, by induction, there are at most
r^{\prime}
machines processing
\lceil n^{\prime}/(m-1)\rceil
jobs.
Consider the case that one of the machines processing less than
\lceil n^{\prime}/(m-1)\rceil
cannot be scheduled upon.
Moreover, let
n-n^{\prime}>m-r^{\prime}-1

(we skip other subcases because they are similar and easier to prove).
Observe that no more than
r^{\prime}-(n-n^{\prime}-(m-r^{\prime}-1))
machines were processing
\lceil n^{\prime}/(m-1)\rceil
jobs and are processing
\lceil n/(m-1)\rceil
jobs now.
Hence, there will be at least
r^{\prime}-(n-n^{\prime}-(m-r^{\prime}-1))+(m-r^{\prime}-1)+1
machines that are processing less than
\lceil n/(m-1)\rceil
.
By a simple transformation we obtain the induction thesis.

•

if
r^{\prime}+n-n^{\prime}\geq 2m-2
then it has to be the case
r=0
,
n-n^{\prime}=m
, and
r^{\prime}=m-2
.
Hence, again the added block is a clique on
m
vertices.
By induction, there were at least
1
machine processing less than
\lceil n^{\prime}/(m-1)\rceil
jobs.
Clearly, at least this machine is processing less than
\lceil n/(m-1)\rceil
in the extended schedule.

In the first two cases
\lceil n/(m-1)\rceil=\lceil n^{\prime}/(m-1)\rceil
, but the jobs are assigned only to machines that are processing less than
\lceil n^{\prime}/(m-1)\rceil
jobs.
The machines are guaranteed to exist, by induction.
In the other cases
\lceil n/(m-1)\rceil>\lceil n^{\prime}/(m-1)\rceil
.
Thus by assigning at most one additional job to every machine we cannot violate the total makespan condition.

We verified that both the total makespan condition and the condition on the number of machines processing less than
\lceil n/(m-1)\rceil
jobs are fulfilled.
Therefore we see that extending the scheduling for the last block is always possible which concludes the proof of the induction step.
∎

Theorem 2.14
.

There exists a PTAS for
P|G=\textit{block graph},p_{j}=1|C_{\max}
.

Proof.

First let us assume that there exists a schedule with
C_{\max}\leq\frac{2}{\varepsilon}
.
If this is the case, from
Theorem

2.12
, it follows that we can find an optimal solution using
Algorithm

5
in time
\textnormal{O}(n^{2}(2/\varepsilon)^{3}m^{14/\varepsilon+8})
.

Next let us suppose that
m\leq\frac{2}{\varepsilon}+1
.
In this case we can use PTAS for
Pm|\textrm{tw}(G)\leq m|C_{\max}
from
Bodlaender et al., 1994
, i.e.
(1+\delta)
-approximate algorithm running in time
\textnormal{O}(\delta^{-m}n^{m+1}m^{2m})
.
For
\delta=\frac{1}{n+1}
it is guaranteed to return the optimal solution in time
\textnormal{O}(n^{2/\/\varepsilon+1}n^{2/\/\varepsilon+2}(2/\/\varepsilon+1)^{{4/\/\varepsilon}+2})
.

Finally, if
m>\frac{2}{\varepsilon}+1
and we did not find any optimal solution with
C^{OPT}_{\max}\leq\frac{2}{\varepsilon}
then we apply
Algorithm

1
.
From
Lemma

2.13
we infer that

\displaystyle C_{\max}

\displaystyle<\frac{n}{m-1}+1<\frac{n}{m}\left(1+\frac{1}{m-1}\right)+C^{OPT}_{\max}\cdot\frac{\varepsilon}{2}<\frac{n}{m}\cdot(1+\frac{\varepsilon}{2})+C^{OPT}_{\max}\cdot\frac{\varepsilon}{2}\leq C^{OPT}_{\max}\left(1+\varepsilon\right).

In the last inequality we used a simple lower bound
C^{OPT}_{\max}\geq\left\lceil\frac{n}{m}\right\rceil
.
∎

3.1
Bounded number of cut-vertices, unit time jobs

To find an optimal solution for the problem
Q|G=\textit{block graph},\textit{cut}(G)\leq k,p_{j}=1|C_{\max}
, we combine three ideas: binary search over all possible objective values, exhaustive search over all possible valid assignment of all cut-vertices to machines (i.e. assigning adjacent cut-vertices to different machines), and solving an auxiliary flow problem.

To simplify the problem we concentrate on the subproblem in which we guessed the value

The core part of our algorithm for
Rm|\textrm{tw}(G)\leq k|C_{\max}

Theorem 4.1
.

Algorithm

9
combined with binary search is an FPTAS for
Rm|\textrm{tw}(G)\leq k|C_{\max}
problem.
It runs in total time equal to
\textnormal{O}(n\log(np_{max})\cdot m^{3k+3}\cdot\lceil\frac{n}{\varepsilon}\rceil^{2m})
.

Proof.

The algorithm will perform a binary search on the value of
C_{\max}
and in each turn it will check for feasibility. For an initial upper bound of
C_{\max}
let us take
np_{max}
.

Note, that the maximum possible difference between the initial and rounded processing times is equal to
n\cdot\frac{\varepsilon C^{OPT}_{\max}}{n}=\varepsilon C^{OPT}_{\max}
.

To prove the correctness of the algorithm, it is sufficient to note that on the one hand, if it does not return
NO
, then it always returns a valid schedule.

On the other hand, it is easy to note that every partial schedule
s\in S(B)
:

1.

assigns all jobs included in a subtree of
T
rooted in
B
,

2.

satisfies all incompatibility conditions between jobs in the subtree of
T
rooted in
B
,

3.

has a total makespan at most
C
.

Moreover, by construction we ensure that we include schedules with all possible current loads of all machines and assignments of jobs to machines in the current bag
B
.

Thus, if we consider an optimal schedule, then it appears ultimately in
S(r)
as long as
C\geq(1+\varepsilon)C^{OPT}_{\max}
– or it can be excluded at some point during trimming, but then there remains another, equivalent schedule in terms of current loads and assignments of jobs from the current bag, which eventually will provide some equivalent complete schedule in terms of loads with rounded processing times.

To compute the time complexity, let us first consider how large any set
S(B)
can be.
We can have at most
\lceil\frac{n}{\varepsilon}\rceil
different loads for each machine, thus
\lceil\frac{n}{\varepsilon}\rceil^{m}
different loads in total.
Additionally, there are at most
m^{k+1}
assignments of jobs from
B
to the machines.
Thus, after trimming we are left with at most one schedule per unique machine load or assignment of jobs, so
|S(B)|\leq\lceil n/\varepsilon\rceil^{m}m^{k+1}
.
Moreover,
|S^{\prime}|\leq m^{k+1}
,
thus
|S^{\prime}\times S(B_{1})\times S(B_{2})|\leq\lceil n/\varepsilon\rceil^{2m}m^{3k+3}
.
For each triple it takes
\textnormal{O}(k^{2})
time to check if the jobs are scheduled consistently, and
\textnormal{O}(m)
time to merge the schedules and check their new total makespan.

Since there are
\textnormal{O}(n)
bags to process and
\textnormal{O}(\log(np_{max}))
iterations of binary search, it completes the proof.
∎

Setup

The testing consists of the following steps:

Partition Generation

First, we provide two values:
n_{tasks}
(number of tasks
n
) and
n_{parts}
(number of blocks), to obtain the sizes of the blocks in the desired block graphs.
Precisely, we generate a random partition of
n_{tasks}+n_{parts}-1
into
n_{parts}
parts.
We apply a modified version of the well-known uniform random integer partitioning algorithm from
(
Nijenhuis and Wilf, 1978
, p. 72–76)
.
We modified the algorithm, to generate a partition into given number of parts, and to ensure that all the values in the partition are within arbitrary predefined bounds
2
and
m
.
The number to be partitioned and the bounds were chosen to generate a collection of
n
jobs, represented by connected graph, which can be scheduled on
m
machines.

Block Graph Generation

Due to our best knowledge, there does not exist a predefined, established, algorithm for random block graph generation.
Due to this, we propose the following method, which consists of the 2 phases.
First, the sizes of the parts are randomly shuffled; and a clique of the size given by the first part size is created.
After this, iteratively until all other part sizes are considered, the following steps are performed:

1.

A next part size
s
in the shuffled partition is picked.

2.

One of existing blocks, denoted as
B
, is chosen, uniformly at random and one of its vertices, denoted as
v
, is chosen, also uniformly at random.

3.

A new block
B^{\prime}
consisting of
v
and
s-1
new vertices is constructed.
That is, we connect a new clique consisting of
s-1
new vertices to
v
, thus forming a block of size
s
.

By this construction we obtain a (rooted) cut-tree structure representing the generated graphs.

Algorithm Application

Finally, the respective scheduling algorithms are applied.
In particular, we implemented:
Algorithm

1
(
Greedy
),
Algorithm

5
(
Exact-Cmax
), a version of
Algorithm

9
suitable for the identical machines (

); and
Algorithm

6
(

In addition, we implemented the PTAS for

from
Theorem

2.14
, which effectively consists of the first three algorithms (

Empirical results on
Tree-Width

When testing this algorithm we have observed that, in quite a few cases, the algorithm could not be applied at all due to the high memory requirements.
In particular for even

jobs, the program started to consume more than

One idea to make it applicable to a larger data, is to better exploit the simplicity of the structure of block graphs and to prepare an
FPTAS
specially tailored to this class.
More precisely, to exploit the fact that for identical machines in many places we do not have to carry about loads of particular machines, only about loads at all.
This means that one could apply
Exact-Cmax
to this case as well, provided that its complexity is upper bounded by the number of distinct colorings that appear during the processing.
Obviously, the maximum cardinality of a color class has size potentially up to
n
(a good heuristic is to use

produced by

here instead).
However, the set of distinct colorings would be mostly empty, the total number of vectors still would be
\textnormal{O}(n^{poly(m)})
–polynomial one, due to the bound on the number of machines.
Hence, if one prepare data structures in a way that they are sensitive to the number of different coloring that are stored, it should be possible to apply the algorithms as well.
We have tested the proposed application of
Exact-Cmax
, together with the heuristic approach, due to the fact that it is simply equivalent to running
Exact-Cmax
with the bound on
C_{\max}
given by greedy algorithm.
As we can see in

However, when the number of machines grows, also the number of equivalent loads of the machines grows, which means that the savings will grow as well.
This proves, that an approach tailored to the studied structure is more efficient, than a general approach.

This means that, to make the algorithm more practical, a more approximation oriented approach shall be considered.

By

There is fundamental difference between
YES

NO

To answer
YES

NO

Also due to this, for
b_{\min}

However, the assignments of cut vertices are verified with respect to guessed

On the other hand, due to more more prevalent

Interestingly, the maximum average number of valid assignments checked was for

We think that a deeper theoretical analysis and more measurements are needed to characterize the behaviour of the algorithm.
In particular an analysis how much more costly in time terms is
NO

YES

If a difference is huge, then perhaps instead of bisection it would be better to from some point iterate over candidates for
C_{\max}
starting from the biggest one.

In our example, for
m=4
,
m=6
,
m=8

1.5\%
,
13.5\%
,
36.3\%
of assignments were valid, respectively.
Hence, a proposed heuristics is to use greedy assignment / branch-and-bound algorithm to filter out more assignment of cut-vertices.
A assignment of even some cut-vertices gives a lower bound on the length of any schedule consistent with this assignment.
Hence, if it not better than the best
C_{\max}
found so far, there is no point in even considering this assignment any further.
Moreover, the assignment has to be consistent with the incompatibility relation.
There is not point to continue generating the assignments if a part of the assignment is not compatible with the relation.

6
Open problems

Although we have explored the status of scheduling problems with conflict block graphs to some extent, there are still some related problems remaining to be solved.
For example, we are interested in decreasing an approximation factor of an algorithm for
P|G=\textit{block graph}|C_{\max}
or proving some inapproximability results.
It is also interesting to know whether there exists a PTAS for
Q|G=\textit{block graph},p_{j}=1|C_{\max}
or any constant factor approximation algorithm for
Q|G=\textit{block graph}|C_{\max}
.
Other results in the area that is the subject of this paper would be also very welcomed.

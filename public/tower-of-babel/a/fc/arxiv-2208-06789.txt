Entanglement, CPT and neutral kaons
Antonio Di Domenico

Abstract
Direct tests of T \mathrm{T} , CP \mathrm{CP} , CPT \mathrm{CPT} symmetries in transitions using entangled neutral kaons produced at a ϕ \phi -factory are briefly reviewed.

2
Direct test of
discrete symmetries with neutral kaons

In order
to implement direct tests of
\mathrm{T}
,
\mathrm{CP}
,
\mathrm{CPT}
symmetries in transitions, the preparation of the initial kaon state
is performed exploiting the
entanglement of
\mathrm{K^{0}}\mathrm{\bar{K}^{0}}
pairs produced at a
\phi
-factory.
[
1
,
2
,
3
]

In this way, taken a kaon transition process as a reference,
the exchange of

in
and
out
states
required for a genuine
test
involving an anti-unitary transformation implied by time-reversal,
can be easily implemented.
In fact,
the initial kaon pair produced in
\phi\rightarrow\mathrm{K^{0}}\mathrm{\bar{K}^{0}}
decays
can be rewritten in terms of any pair of orthogonal states:

\displaystyle|i\rangle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\{|\mathrm{K^{0}}\rangle|\mathrm{\bar{K}^{0}}\rangle-|\mathrm{\bar{K}^{0}}\rangle|\mathrm{K^{0}}\rangle\}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\{|\mathrm{K_{+}}\rangle|\mathrm{K_{-}}\rangle-|\mathrm{K_{-}}\rangle|\mathrm{K_{+}}\rangle\}~.

(1)

Here
the states
|\mathrm{K_{-}}\rangle
,
|\mathrm{K_{+}}\rangle
are defined as
the states which cannot decay into pure
\mathrm{CP}=\pm 1
final states,

\pi\pi

or
3\pi^{0}
, respectively.
[
2
,
3
]

The condition of orthogonality
\langle\mathrm{K_{-}}|\mathrm{K_{+}}\rangle=0
,
corresponds to assume negligible direct
\mathrm{CP}
(or
\mathrm{CPT}
) violation contributions, while
the
\Delta S=\Delta Q
rule is also assumed, so that the two flavor orthogonal eigenstates
|\mathrm{K^{0}}\rangle
and
|\mathrm{\bar{K}^{0}}\rangle
are identified by the charge of the lepton in semileptonic decays.

Thus, exploiting the perfect anticorrelation of the states implied
by Eq. (
1
),
it is possible to have a
“flavor-tag”or a
“
\mathrm{CP}
-tag”,
i.e. to infer the flavor (
\mathrm{K^{0}}
or
\mathrm{\bar{K}^{0}}
) or the
\mathrm{CP}
(
\mathrm{K_{+}}
or
\mathrm{K_{-}}
) state
of the still alive kaon by observing a specific flavor decay (
\pi^{+}\ell^{-}\nu

or
\pi^{-}\ell^{+}\bar{\nu}
) or CP decay (
\pi\pi
or
3\pi^{0}
) of the other (and first decaying) kaon in the pair.
Then the decay of the surviving kaon into a semileptonic (
\ell^{+}
or
\ell^{-}
),
\pi\pi
or
3\pi^{0}
final state, filter the kaon final state as a flavor or
\mathrm{CP}
state.

In this way one can identify
a reference transition (e.g.
\mathrm{K^{0}}\to\mathrm{K_{-}}
)
and its symmetry conjugate (e.g. the
\mathrm{CPT}
-conjugated
\mathrm{K_{-}}\to\mathrm{\bar{K}^{0}}
),
and directly compare them
through
the
corresponding
ratios of probabilities.
The observable ratios
for the various symmetry tests can be defined as follows:
[
6
]

\displaystyle R_{2,\mathrm{T}}^{\rm{exp}}(\Delta t)

\displaystyle\equiv

\displaystyle\frac{I(\ell^{-},3\pi^{0};\Delta t)}{I(\pi\pi,\ell^{+};\Delta t)}\cdot\frac{1}{D_{\mathrm{CPT}}}

(2)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(1-4\Re\epsilon+4\Re x_{+}+4\Re y\right)

\displaystyle\times\left|1+\left(2\epsilon+\epsilon^{\prime}_{3\pi^{0}}+\epsilon^{\prime}_{\pi\pi}\right)e^{-i(\lambda_{S}-\lambda_{L})\Delta t}\right|^{2}~,

\displaystyle R_{4,\mathrm{T}}^{\rm{exp}}(\Delta t)

\displaystyle\equiv

\displaystyle\frac{I(\ell^{+},3\pi^{0};\Delta t)}{I(\pi\pi,\ell^{-};\Delta t)}\cdot\frac{1}{D_{\mathrm{CPT}}}

(3)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(1+4\Re\epsilon+4\Re x_{+}-4\Re y\right)

\displaystyle\times\left|1-\left(2\epsilon+\epsilon^{\prime}_{3\pi^{0}}+\epsilon^{\prime}_{\pi\pi}\right)e^{-i(\lambda_{S}-\lambda_{L})\Delta t}\right|^{2}~,

\displaystyle R_{2,\mathrm{CP}}^{\rm{exp}}(\Delta t)

\displaystyle\equiv

\displaystyle\frac{I(\ell^{-},3\pi^{0};\Delta t)}{I(\ell^{+},3\pi^{0};\Delta t)}

(4)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(1-4\Re\epsilon_{S}-4\Re x_{-}+4\Re y\right)

\displaystyle\times\left|1+2\left(\epsilon_{S}+\epsilon^{\prime}_{3\pi^{0}}\right)e^{-i(\lambda_{S}-\lambda_{L})\Delta t}\right|^{2}~,

\displaystyle R_{4,\mathrm{CP}}^{\rm{exp}}(\Delta t)

\displaystyle\equiv

\displaystyle\frac{I(\pi\pi,\ell^{+};\Delta t)}{I(\pi\pi,\ell^{-};\Delta t)}

(5)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(1+4\Re\epsilon_{L}-4\Re x_{-}-4\Re y\right)

\displaystyle\times\left|1-2\left(\epsilon_{L}+\epsilon^{\prime}_{\pi\pi}\right)e^{-i(\lambda_{S}-\lambda_{L})\Delta t}\right|^{2}~,

\displaystyle R_{2,\mathrm{CPT}}^{\rm{exp}}(\Delta t)

\displaystyle\equiv

\displaystyle\frac{I(\ell^{-},3\pi^{0};\Delta t)}{I(\pi\pi,\ell^{-};\Delta t)}\cdot\frac{1}{D_{\mathrm{CPT}}}

(6)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(1-4\Re\delta+4\Re x_{+}-4\Re x_{-}\right)

\displaystyle\times\left|1+\left(2\delta+\epsilon^{\prime}_{3\pi^{0}}-\epsilon^{\prime}_{\pi\pi}\right)e^{-i(\lambda_{S}-\lambda_{L})\Delta t}\right|^{2}~,

\displaystyle R_{4,\mathrm{CPT}}^{\rm{exp}}(\Delta t)

\displaystyle\equiv

\displaystyle\frac{I(\ell^{+},3\pi^{0};\Delta t)}{I(\pi\pi,\ell^{+};\Delta t)}\cdot\frac{1}{D_{\mathrm{CPT}}}

(7)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(1+4\Re\delta+4\Re x_{+}+4\Re x_{-}\right)

\displaystyle\times\left|1-\left(2\delta+\epsilon^{\prime}_{3\pi^{0}}-\epsilon^{\prime}_{\pi\pi}\right)e^{-i(\lambda_{S}-\lambda_{L})\Delta t}\right|^{2}~,

The Pebble-Relation Comonad in Finite Model Theory
Yoàv Montacute ORCID:0000-0001-9814-7323, Nihil Shah ORCID:0000-0003-2844-0828

Abstract
. The pebbling comonad, introduced by Abramsky, Dawar and Wang, provides a categorical interpretation for the k k -pebble games from finite model theory. The coKleisli category of the pebbling comonad specifies equivalences under different fragments and extensions of infinitary k k -variable logic. Moreover, the coalgebras over this pebbling comonad characterise treewidth and correspond to tree decompositions. In this paper we introduce the pebble-relation comonad, which characterises pathwidth and whose coalgebras correspond to path decompositions. We further show that the existence of a coKleisli morphism in this comonad is equivalent to truth preservation in the restricted conjunction fragment of k k -variable infinitary logic. We do this using Dalmau’s pebble-relation game and an equivalent all-in-one pebble game. We then provide a similar treatment to the corresponding coKleisli isomorphisms via a bijective version of the all-in-one pebble game. Finally, we show as a consequence a new Lovász-type theorem relating pathwidth to the restricted conjunction fragment of k k -variable infinitary logic with counting quantifiers.

1.
Introduction

Model theory is a field in which mathematical structures are not seen as they really are, i.e. up to isomorphism, but through the fuzzy reflection imposed by their definability in some logic
\mathscr{J}
.
Namely, given two structures over the same signature
\mathcal{A}
and
\mathcal{B}
, model theory is concerned with equivalence under the relation

\mathcal{A}\equiv^{\mathscr{J}}\mathcal{B}=\forall\phi\in\mathscr{J},\;\mathcal{A}\models\phi\Leftrightarrow\mathcal{B}\models\phi.

Historically, a central theme in model theory has been to find syntax-free characterisations of these equivalences.
This is exemplified by the Keisler-Shelah theorem
[
She71
]
for first-order logic.
These equivalences are also characterised by model-comparison, or Spoiler-Duplicator games.
Spoiler-Duplicator games are graded by a (typically finite) ordinal that corresponds to a grading of some syntactic resource.
For example, the
k
-pebble game introduced by Immerman
[
Imm82
]
characterises equivalence in infinitary logic graded by the number of variables
k
.

Abramsky, Dawar and Wang
[
ADW17
]
provided this game, and two similar variants, with a categorical interpretation in terms of morphisms which involve the pebbling comonad
\mathbb{P}_{k}
.
Since then similar game comonads were discovered for the Ehrenfeucht-Fraïssé games
[
AS18
]
, modal-bisimulation games
[
AS18
]
, games for guarded
logics
[
AM21
]
and games for finite-variable logics with generalised
quantifiers
[
CD21
]
.
In all of these cases the coalgebras over the game comonad correspond to decompositions of structures such as tree decompositions of width
<k
and forest covers of height
\leq k
, in the cases of the pebbling comonad and the Ehrenfeucht-Fraïssé comonad respectively.
As an immediate corollary of these correspondences, we obtain alternative and novel definitions for associated graph parameters such as treewidth in the case of the pebbling comonad and tree-depth in the case of the Ehrenfeucht-Fraïssé comonad.

Abramsky et al.
[
ADW17
]
proved that the coKleisli morphisms associated with
\mathbb{P}_{k}
correspond to Duplicator’s winning strategies in the one-sided
k
-pebble game.
This one-sided game, introduced by Kolaitis and Vardi
[
KV90
]
, was used to study expressivity in
Datalog
, preservation of existential positive sentences of
k
-variable logic
\exists^{+}\mathscr{L}_{\infty}
and
P
-tractable constraint satisfaction problems.

Dalmau
[
Dal05
]
developed an analogous one-sided pebble-relation game that he used to study expressivity in linear
Datalog
, preservation of a restricted conjunction fragment of existential positive
k
-variable logic
{\exists^{+}{\curlywedge}\mathscr{L}^{k}}
and
NL
-tractable constraint satisfaction problems.
Further, Abramsky et al.
[
ADW17
]
demonstrated that, for the pebbling comonad, the coKleisli isomorphism associated with
\mathbb{P}_{k}
corresponds to Hella’s bijective variant of the
k
-pebble game
[
Hel96
]
. However, the bijective version of the pebble-relation game has not been explored.

In this paper we widen the domain of these game comonads by introducing the pebble-relation comonad
\mathbb{PR}_{k}
, where the coalgebras correspond to path decompositions of width
<k
.
This yields a new definition for pathwidth.
Moreover, just as coKleisli morphisms of
\mathbb{P}_{k}
correspond to Duplicator’s winning strategies in the one-sided
k
-pebble game, we show that coKleisli morphisms of
\mathbb{PR}_{k}
correspond to Duplicator’s winning strategies in Dalmau’s
k
-pebble relation game
[
Dal05
]
.
We do this by introducing an equivalent game which, as will be detailed later, is much more suitable for our purpose.
This game is called the
all-in-one
k
-pebble game
and the existence of a winning strategy for Duplicator in this game is equivalent to truth preservation in the restricted conjunction fragment of
k
-variable infinitary logic.
Inspired by the isomorphism result for
\mathbb{P}_{k}
, we define a bijective variant of this game.
Consequently, the coKleisli isomorphism allows us to obtain a new characterisation of equivalence in the, heretofore unexplored, restricted conjunction fragment of
k
-variable infinitary logic with counting quantifiers.
It is worth mentioning that equivalence in this fragment may yield a new isomorphism approximation method
[
WL68
]
, similarly to how equivalences in
k+1
-variable counting logic can be algorithmically interpreted as graphs which are indistinguishable by the polynomial time
k
-Weisfeiler-Lehman isomorphism approximation
method
[
Gro17
]
.

As one immediate consequence of this comonadic interpretation, we will demonstrate that equivalence in the restricted conjunction fragment of
k
-variable logic with counting quantifiers is equivalent to counting homomorphisms from structures of pathwidth
<k
.
This applies and expands the theoretical picture established in a recent paper by Dawar, Jakl and Reggio
[
DJR21
]
regarding a categorical Lovász-type theorem.
It is also intertwined with the growing interest in the relationship between homomorphism counting and pathwidth as discussed for example in Dell, Grohe and Ratten
[
DGR18
]
.

2.2.
Category theory

We assume familiarity with the standard category-theoretic notions of category, functor and natural transformation.
Given a
category
\mathfrak{C}
, we denote the class of its objects by
\mathfrak{C}_{0}
and the class of its morphisms by
\mathfrak{C}_{1}
.
We define the notion of a relative comonad which weakens the endofunctor requirement of the standard comonad. The relative comonad is the dual of the
relative monad
introduced in
[
ACU14
]
.
Given a functor
J:\mathfrak{J}\rightarrow\mathfrak{C}
, a
relative comonad on
J
is a triple
(\mathbb{T},\varepsilon,(\cdot)^{*})
, where
\mathbb{T}:\mathfrak{J}_{0}\rightarrow\mathfrak{C}_{0}
is an object mapping,
\varepsilon_{X}:\mathbb{T}X\rightarrow JX\in\mathfrak{C}_{1}

X\in\mathfrak{J}_{0}
and

is a coextension map, for each
f:\mathbb{T}X\rightarrow JY\in\mathfrak{C}_{1}
, satisfying the following equations:

\varepsilon_{X}^{*}=\mathsf{id}_{\mathbb{T}X};\qquad\varepsilon\circ f^{*}=f;\qquad(g\circ f^{*})^{*}=g^{*}\circ f^{*}.

These equations allow us to extend the object mapping
\mathbb{T}
to a functor, where
\mathbb{T}f=(Jf\circ\varepsilon_{X})^{*}
for
f:X\rightarrow Y
.
For every relative comonad
(\mathbb{T},\varepsilon,(\cdot)^{*})
over
J:\mathfrak{J}\rightarrow\mathfrak{C}
, we can define an associated
coKleisli category
, denoted by
\mathcal{K}(\mathbb{T})
, where

•

\mathcal{K}(\mathbb{T})_{0}
is the same as the class of objects
\mathfrak{J}_{0}
;

•

\mathcal{K}(\mathbb{T})_{1}
are morphisms of type
f:\mathbb{T}X\rightarrow JY\in\mathfrak{C}_{1}
;

•

The composition
g\circ_{\mathcal{K}}f:\mathbb{T}X\rightarrow JZ
of two morphisms
f:\mathbb{T}X\rightarrow JY
and
g:\mathbb{T}Y\rightarrow JZ
is given by

\mathbb{T}X\xrightarrow{f^{*}}\mathbb{T}Y\xrightarrow{g}JZ;

•

The identity morphisms are given by the counit

\varepsilon_{X}:\mathbb{T}X\rightarrow JX.

The ordinary notion of a
comonad in coKleisli form

[
Man76
]
and the corresponding Kleisli category can be recovered when

and

.
Observe that given a comonad
(\mathbb{T}:\mathfrak{C}\rightarrow\mathfrak{C},\varepsilon,(\cdot)^{*})
and a functor

, the functor

, i.e.

.

Given an ordinary comonad in coKleisli form, we can define a comultiplication morphism

\mathbb{T}\delta_{X}\circ\delta_{X}=\delta_{\mathbb{T}X}\circ\delta_{X};\qquad\mathbb{T}\varepsilon_{X}\circ\delta_{X}=\varepsilon_{\mathbb{T}X}\circ\delta_{X}=\mathsf{id}_{\mathbb{T}X}.

The triple

Given a comonad

2.4.
Logical fragments

We are mainly concerned with fragments of infinitary logic.
The infinitary logic
\mathscr{L}_{\infty}
has the standard syntax and semantics of first-order logic, but where disjunctions and conjunctions are allowed to be taken over arbitrary sets of formulas.
We denote formulas with free variables among
\vec{x}=(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})
by
\phi(\vec{x})
.
If
\mathcal{A}
is a
\sigma
-structure,
\vec{a}\in A^{n}
,
\phi(\vec{x})\in\mathscr{L}_{\infty}
and
\mathcal{A},\vec{a}
satisfies
\phi(\vec{x})
, then we write
\mathcal{A},\vec{a}\models\phi(\vec{x})
.

For vectors of variables and elements
\vec{x}=(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})
, we write
[\vec{x}]=\{x_{1},\dots,x_{n}\}
for the underlying
support
of the vector.
The infinitary logic can be graded into
k
-variable fragments, denoted by
\mathscr{L}^{k}
, where formulas contain at most
k
-many variables.

For every logic
\mathscr{J}
considered throughout the paper, we will also be interested in two variants.
The first variant is the existential positive fragment
\exists^{+}\mathscr{J}
, where we only consider formulas constructed using existential quantifiers, disjunctions, conjunctions and atomic formulas.
The second variant involves a restricted conjunction.
A
restricted conjunction
is a conjunction of the form
\bigwedge\Psi
, where
\Psi
is a set of formulas satisfying the following condition:

(R)

At most one formula
\psi\in\Psi
with quantifiers is not a sentence.

The main goal of this paper is to study the
restricted conjunction logics

{\exists^{+}{\curlywedge}\mathscr{L}^{k}}
and
{\#{\curlywedge}\mathscr{L}^{k}}
(Section
5.2
).

Given two
\sigma
-structures
\mathcal{A}
and
\mathcal{B}
, if for all sentences
\phi\in\mathscr{J}(\sigma)
,
\mathcal{A}\models\phi
implies
\mathcal{B}\models\phi
, then we write
\mathcal{A}\Rrightarrow^{\mathscr{J}}\mathcal{B}
.
If
\mathcal{A}\Rrightarrow^{\mathscr{J}}\mathcal{B}
and
\mathcal{B}\Rrightarrow^{\mathscr{J}}\mathcal{A}
, then we write
\mathcal{A}\equiv^{\mathscr{J}}\mathcal{B}
.
For logics
\mathscr{J}
closed under negation, we have that
\mathcal{A}\Rrightarrow^{\mathscr{J}}\mathcal{B}
implies
\mathcal{A}\equiv^{\mathscr{J}}\mathcal{B}
.

2.5.
Spoiler-Duplicator games

The relations
\Rrightarrow^{\mathscr{J}}
and
\equiv^{\mathscr{J}}
for specific choices of
\mathscr{J}
are characterised, in a syntax-free fashion, by Spoiler-Duplicator games (also called model-comparison games or Ehrenfeucht-Fraïssé style games).
In each game, we consider two
\sigma
-structures,
\mathcal{A}
and
\mathcal{B}
, and two players: Spoiler, who tries to show that two structures are different under
\mathscr{J}
; and Duplicator, who tries to show the two structures are the same under
\mathscr{J}
(i.e.
\mathcal{A}\equiv^{\mathscr{J}}\mathcal{B}
).
The standard Ehrenfeucht-Fraïssé game appears in many introductory texts on model theory (see e.g.
[
Lib04
]
).
The model-comparison games we are interested in are modified versions of the one-sided
k
-pebble game
[
Imm82
]
and the bijective
k
-pebble game
[
Hel96
]
.
Each game is played in a number of rounds.

For the one-sided
k
-pebble game
\exists^{+}\textbf{Peb}_{k}(\mathcal{A},\mathcal{B})
, which characterises
\Rrightarrow^{\mathscr{L}^{k}}
, both Spoiler and Duplicator have a set of
k
pebbles. At each
n
-th round such that
n\in\omega
:

•

Spoiler places a pebble
p_{n}\in[\textsf{k}]
on an element

. If the pebble
p_{n}\in[\textsf{k}]
is already placed on an element of
A
, Spoiler moves the pebble from that element to the chosen element;

•

Duplicator places the pebble
p_{n}\in[\textsf{k}]
on an element
b_{n}\in B
.

At the end of the
n
-th round, we have a pair of sequences
s=[(p_{1},a_{1}),\dots,(p_{n},a_{n})]
and
t=[(p_{1},b_{1}),\dots,(p_{n},b_{n})]
.
For every
p
in the set
\textsf{pebbles}(s)=\{p_{1},\dots,p_{n}\}
of pebbles appearing in
s
, let
a^{p}=\textsf{last}_{p}(s)
and
b^{p}=\textsf{last}_{p}(t)
be the last elements pebbled with
p
in
s
and

respectively.
If the relation
\gamma_{n}=\{(a^{p},b^{p})\mid p\in\textsf{pebbles}(s)\}
is a partial homomorphism from
\mathcal{A}
to
\mathcal{B}
, then Duplicator wins the
n
-th round of the
k
-pebble game.
1
1

1

When Spoiler (resp. Duplicator) makes a choice that results in her winning a round, we often say that this choice is a
winning move
for her.

Duplicator has a winning strategy in
\exists^{+}\textbf{Peb}_{k}(\mathcal{A},\mathcal{B})
if for every round
n\in\omega
, and for every move by Spoiler in the
n
-th round, Duplicator has a winning move.

For the bijective
k
-pebble game

, Spoiler wins automatically if there is no bijection between
A
and
B
. At each
n
-th round such that

:

•

Spoiler chooses a pebble

;

•

Duplicator responds with a bijection

;

•

Spoiler places the pebble

. Duplicator places the pebble

.

Duplicator wins the

Capturing these games as constructions on the category of relational structures has been the underlying theme of the research program motivating this paper.

2.6.
Spoiler-Duplicator game comonads

The larger research program motivating this paper is the discovery of Spoiler-Duplicator game comonads
\mathbb{C}_{k}
associated with logics
\mathscr{J}
graded by a resource
k
.
Though these Spoiler-Duplicator game comonads have no general definition that applies to all cases, different indexed families of comonads
\mathbb{C}_{k}
over
\mathfrak{R}(\sigma)
exhibit the same pattern of results:
the morphism power theorem, the isomorphism power theorem and the coalgebra characterisation theorem
[
ADW17
,
AS18
,
CD21
,
AM21
]
.
In this section, we will review the general schema for each of these results.

Morphism and Isomorphism Power Theorems

Given a logic
\mathscr{J}_{k}
, graded by some syntactic resource
k
(e.g. number of variables, quantifier rank and modal depth) and corresponding model-comparison game
\textbf{G}_{k}
, the Spoiler-Duplicator comonad associated with
\textbf{G}_{k}
is an indexed family of comonads
\mathbb{C}_{k}
over
\mathfrak{R}(\sigma)

[
AS18
]
.
We can then leverage the coKleisli category
\mathcal{K}(\mathbb{C}_{k})
associated with
\mathbb{C}_{k}
to capture the relation
\Rrightarrow^{\exists^{+}\mathscr{J}_{k}}
of the sentences in the existential positive fragment of
\mathscr{J}_{k}
limited by the resource
k
.

Theorem 1

(Morphism Power Theorem)
.

For all
\sigma
-structures
\mathcal{A}
and
\mathcal{B}
, the following are equivalent:

(1)

Duplicator has a winning strategy in
\exists^{+}\textbf{G}_{k}(\mathcal{A},\mathcal{B})
.

(2)

\mathcal{A}\Rrightarrow^{\exists^{+}\mathscr{J}_{k}}\mathcal{B}
.

(3)

There exists a coKleisli morphism
f:\mathbb{C}_{k}\mathcal{A}\rightarrow\mathcal{B}
.

For example, in the case of the pebbling comonad
\mathbb{P}_{k}
, a coKleisli morphism
\mathbb{P}_{k}\mathcal{A}\rightarrow\mathcal{B}
corresponds to a winning strategy for Duplicator in
\exists^{+}\textbf{Peb}_{k}(\mathcal{A},\mathcal{B})
and characterises the relation
\Rrightarrow^{\exists^{+}\mathscr{L}^{k}}

[
ADW17
]
.
The indexing of
\mathbb{C}_{k}
models resources in the corresponding game.
Namely, for
k\leq l
, there is a comonad inclusion
\mathbb{C}_{k}\hookrightarrow\mathbb{C}_{l}
.
Interpreting the inclusion in terms of the game corresponds to the fact that Spoiler playing with
k
resources is a special case of Spoiler playing with
l\geq k
resources.
This means that the smaller the
k
is, the easier it is to find morphisms of type
\mathbb{C}_{k}\mathcal{A}\rightarrow\mathcal{B}
.

Ostensibly, the asymmetry of a coKleisli morphism means that only the “forth” aspect of the game
\textbf{G}_{k}
(the game
\exists^{+}\textbf{G}_{k}
) can be captured by this comonadic approach.
A natural candidate for capturing the symmetric game would be to consider the symmetric relation of coKleisli isomorphism, i.e. there exist morphisms
f:\mathbb{C}_{k}\mathcal{A}\rightarrow\mathcal{B}
and
g:\mathbb{C}_{k}\mathcal{B}\rightarrow\mathcal{A}
such that
g\circ_{\mathcal{K}}f=\varepsilon_{\mathcal{A}}
and
f\circ_{\mathcal{K}}g=\varepsilon_{\mathcal{B}}
.
However, the isomorphisms in
\mathcal{K}(\mathbb{C}_{k})
characterise the equivalence relation
\equiv^{\#\mathscr{J}_{k}}
for the logic
\mathscr{J}_{k}
extended with counting quantifiers. This results in a logic stronger than
\mathscr{J}_{k}
.

Theorem 2

(Isomorphism Power Theorem)
.

For all finite
\sigma
-structures
\mathcal{A}
and
\mathcal{B}
, the following are equivalent:

(1)

Duplicator has a winning strategy in
\#\textbf{G}_{k}(\mathcal{A},\mathcal{B})
.

(2)

\mathcal{A}\equiv^{\#\mathscr{J}_{k}}\mathcal{B}
.

(3)

There exists a coKleisli isomorphism
f:\mathbb{C}_{k}\mathcal{A}\rightarrow\mathcal{B}
.

For example, in the case of the pebbling comonad
\mathbb{P}_{k}
, a coKleisli isomorphism
\mathbb{P}_{k}\mathcal{A}\rightarrow\mathcal{B}
corresponds to a winning strategy for Duplicator in
\#\textbf{Peb}_{k}(\mathcal{A},\mathcal{B})
and characterises the relation
\equiv^{\#\mathscr{L}^{k}}

[
ADW17
]
.

Coalgebras and Adjunctions

A natural inquiry regarding the comonads
\mathbb{C}_{k}
is to investigate the category of coalgebras
\mathcal{EM}(\mathbb{C}_{k})
.
It turns out that for all the cases of
\mathbb{C}_{k}
constructed from some game
\textbf{G}_{k}
, the coalgebras
\mathcal{A}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}_{k}\mathcal{A}
correspond to forest covers of structures
\mathcal{A}
.

Define a
forest cover for
\mathcal{A}
to be a forest
(S,\leq)
, where
A\subseteq S
, such that if
a\frown a^{\prime}\in\mathcal{A}
, i.e.
a
and
a^{\prime}
are related in some tuple of
\mathcal{A}
, then
a\leq a^{\prime}
or
a^{\prime}\leq a
. In this paper, we assume that all forest covers are
tight
forest covers, i.e. where
S=A
.

Theorem 3

(coalgebra characterisation)
.

The following statements are equivalent:

(1)

\mathcal{A}
has a forest cover of parameter
\leq k
.

(2)

There exists a coalgebra
\alpha:\mathcal{A}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}_{k}\mathcal{A}
.

For every game comonad
\mathbb{C}_{k}
, we define the
\mathbb{C}
-coalgebra number of
\mathcal{A}
to be the least
k
such that there exists a coalgebra
\mathcal{A}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}_{k}\mathcal{A}
. We denote it by
\kappa^{\mathbb{C}}(\mathcal{A})
.
This definition together with the coalgebra characterisation theorems allow us to obtain new alternative definitions for various combinatorial invariants of relational structures.

For example, in the case of the Ehrenfeucht-Fraïssé comonad
\mathbb{E}_{k}
, a coalgebra over
\mathcal{A}
corresponds to a forest cover of
\mathcal{A}
with height
\leq k
.
Consequently,
\kappa^{\mathbb{E}}(\mathcal{A})
is equal to the tree-depth of
\mathcal{A}

[
AS18
]
.
Moreover, in the case of the pebbling comonad, a coalgebra over
\mathcal{A}
corresponds to a forest cover over
\mathcal{A}
with an additional pebbling function
p:A\rightarrow[\textsf{k}]
that encodes the data of a tree decomposition for
\mathcal{A}
of width
<k
. Consequently,
\kappa^{\mathbb{P}}(\mathcal{A})
corresponds to the treewidth of
\mathcal{A}

[
ADW17
]
.

The bijective correspondence between forest covers of a certain type and coalgebras can be extended to isomorphisms of the respective categories.
In the language of adjunctions, for every
\mathbb{C}_{k}
, we can form the category of forest-ordered
\sigma
-structures
\mathfrak{T}^{\mathbb{C}_{k}}(\sigma)
consisting of

•

\mathfrak{T}^{\mathbb{C}_{k}}(\sigma)_{0}
are pairs
(\mathcal{A},\leq)
, where
\mathcal{A}\in\mathfrak{R}(\sigma)_{0}
and
\leq
forest-orders the universe
A
of
\mathcal{A}
such that the following condition holds:

(E) if
a\frown a^{\prime}\in\mathcal{A}
, then
a\leq a^{\prime}
or
a^{\prime}\leq a
.

•

\mathfrak{T}^{\mathbb{C}_{k}}(\sigma)_{1}
are
\sigma
-morphisms
f:\mathcal{A}\rightarrow\mathcal{B}\in\mathfrak{R}(\sigma)_{1}
that preserve roots and the covering relation of
\leq
. That is,
f
preserves immediate successors in
\leq
.

•

The identity and composition are inherited from
\mathfrak{R}(\sigma)
.

Evidently, there exists a forgetful functor
U_{k}:\mathfrak{T}^{\mathbb{C}_{k}}(\sigma)\rightarrow\mathfrak{R}(\sigma)
.
We may view
\mathbb{C}_{k}
as the comonad arising from constructing a functor
F_{k}:\mathfrak{R}(\sigma)\rightarrow\mathfrak{T}^{\mathbb{C}_{k}}(\sigma)
that is right adjoint to
U_{k}
and where the adjunction
U_{k}\dashv F_{k}
is comonadic.
That is,
\mathfrak{T}^{\mathbb{C}_{k}}(\sigma)
and
\mathcal{EM}(\mathbb{C}_{k})
are equivalent categories.

For example, the category of coalgebras for the Ehrenfeucht-Fraïssé comonad is equivalent to the category of forest covers of height
\leq k

[
AS21
]
.
Similarly, the category of coalgebras
\mathcal{EM}(\mathbb{P}_{k})
for the pebbling comonad is equivalent to the category of
k
-pebble forest covers
[
AS21
]
.

For each of the result schemata (morphism power theorem, isomorphism power theorem and coalgebra characterisation theorem), we mentioned the corresponding result for the pebbling comonad.
However, we would like to stress that analogous results hold for all of the comonads previously discussed in the literature
[
ADW17
,
AS18
,
AM21
,
CD21
]
. Obtaining these results from a general definition is an active topic of research.

3.
Pebble-Relation Comonad

The pebble-relation comonad, which captures pathwidth, is closely related to the pebbling comonad
(\mathbb{P}_{k},\varepsilon,()^{*})
that is used to define treewidth
[
ADW17
,
AS18
]
.
Given a
\sigma
-structure
\mathcal{A}
, we define the universe of
\mathbb{P}_{k}\mathcal{A}
as
([\textsf{k}]\times A)^{+}
.
The counit morphism
\varepsilon_{\mathcal{A}}:\mathbb{P}_{k}\mathcal{A}\rightarrow\mathcal{A}
is defined as the second component of the last element of the sequence, i.e.
\varepsilon_{\mathcal{A}}[(p_{1},a_{1}),\dots,(p_{n},a_{n})]=a_{n}
.
The coextension of a morphism

is defined as

f^{*}[(p_{1},a_{1}),\dots,(p_{n},a_{n})]=[(p_{1},b_{1}),\dots,(p_{n},b_{n})],

where
b_{i}=f[(p_{1},a_{1}),\dots,(p_{i},a_{i})]
, for all
i\in[\textsf{n}]
.

To define the
\sigma
-structure on
\mathbb{P}_{k}\mathcal{A}
, we will need to introduce some new notation.
The mapping
\pi_{A}:\mathbb{P}_{k}A\rightarrow[\textsf{k}]
is defined as the first component of the last element of the sequence, i.e.
\pi_{A}[(p_{1},a_{1}),\dots,(p_{n},a_{n})]=p_{n}
.
Suppose
R\in\sigma
is an
m
-ary relation. Then
R^{\mathbb{P}_{k}\mathcal{A}}(s_{1},\dots,s_{m})
iff

(1)

\forall i,j\in[\textsf{m}],s_{i}\sqsubseteq s_{j}
or
s_{j}\sqsubseteq s_{i}
; (
pairwise comparability
)

(2)

If
s_{i}\sqsubseteq s_{j}
, then
\pi_{A}(s_{i})
is not in
(s_{i},s_{j}]
,

and similarly for
s_{j}\sqsubseteq s_{i}
; (
active pebble
)

(3)

R^{\mathcal{A}}(\varepsilon_{\mathcal{A}}(s_{1}),\dots,\varepsilon_{\mathcal{A}}(s_{m}))
. (
compatibility
)

The elements of
\mathbb{P}_{k}\mathcal{A}
can be seen as the set of Spoiler plays in the
k
-pebble game on the structure
\mathcal{A}
.
The additional active pebble condition models how Spoiler’s choice of
k
pebble placements amounts to moving a
k
-sized window of variables that are assigned to elements in the structure
\mathcal{A}
.
When working with
\mathbb{P}_{k}\mathcal{A}
, as before, we will use
\textsf{pebbles}(s)
to denote the set of pebbles appearing in
s
,
\textsf{last}_{p}(s)
to denote the last element
a\in\mathcal{A}
in the sequence
s
pebbled by
p\in\textsf{pebbles}(s)
.
The set of
active elements of
s
is defined as
\textsf{Active}(s)=\{\textsf{last}_{p}(s)\mid p\in\textsf{pebbles}(s)\}
.
Given two sequences
s\in\mathbb{P}_{k}A
and
t\in\mathbb{P}_{k}B
, we define the relation of pebbled elements
\gamma_{s,t}=\{(\textsf{last}_{p}(s),\textsf{last}_{p}(t))\}\subseteq A\times B
.

We now introduce our main construction, which is the family of comonads
(\mathbb{PR}_{k},\varepsilon,()^{*})
, for every
k\in\omega
over
\mathfrak{R}(\sigma)
.
Given a
\sigma
-structure
\mathcal{A}
, we define the universe of
\mathbb{PR}_{k}\mathcal{A}
as

\mathbb{PR}_{k}A=\{([(p_{1},a_{1}),\dots,(p_{n},a_{n})],i)\},

where
(p_{j},a_{j})\in[\textsf{k}]\times A
and
i\in[\textsf{n}]
.
Intuitively,
\mathbb{PR}_{k}A
is the set of Spoiler plays in the
k
-pebble game paired with an index denoting a move of the play.
The counit morphism
\varepsilon_{\mathcal{A}}:\mathbb{PR}_{k}\mathcal{A}\rightarrow\mathcal{A}
is defined as
\varepsilon_{\mathcal{A}}([(p_{1},a_{1}),\dots,(p_{n},a_{n})],i)=a_{i}
.
The coextension of a morphism
f:\mathbb{PR}_{k}\mathcal{A}\rightarrow\mathcal{B}
is defined as

f^{*}([(p_{1},a_{1}),\dots,(p_{n},a_{n})],i)=([(p_{1},b_{1}),\dots,(p_{n},b_{n})],i),

where
b_{j}=f([(p_{1},a_{1}),\dots,(p_{n},a_{n})],j)
for all
j\in[\textsf{n}]
.

We can interpret the relations on
\mathbb{PR}_{k}\mathcal{A}
in a similar manner to the interpretations given for
\mathbb{P}_{k}\mathcal{A}
:
Suppose
R\in\sigma
is an
m
-ary relation. Then
R^{\mathbb{PR}_{k}\mathcal{A}}((s_{1},i_{1}),\dots,(s_{m},i_{m}))
iff

(1)

\forall j\in[\textsf{m}],s_{j}=s

)

(2)

\pi_{A}(s,i_{j})
does not appear in

)

(3)

R^{\mathcal{A}}(\varepsilon_{\mathcal{A}}(s,i_{1}),\dots,\varepsilon_{\mathcal{A}}(s,i_{m}))

compatibility
)

where

and
\pi_{A}:A\rightarrow[\textsf{k}]
is defined as

\pi_{A}([(p_{1},a_{1}),\dots,(p_{n},a_{n})],i)=p_{i}.

The active pebble and compatibility conditions play a similar role as in the definition of
\mathbb{P}_{k}\mathcal{A}
.
As we will see in Section

4.
Coalgebras and Path Decompositions

We return to the initial motivation for constructing the pebble-relation comonad.
That is, to give a categorical definition for the combinatorial parameter of pathwidth.
There are many different characterisations of pathwidth.
The original definition, introduced by Robertson and Seymour in
[
RS83
]
, is in terms of path decompositions of a structure
\mathcal{A}
.
We will show that coalgebras
\mathcal{A}\rightarrow\mathbb{PR}_{k}\mathcal{A}
correspond to path decompositions of
\mathcal{A}
of width
<k
.
Given the relationship between
\mathbb{PR}_{k}
and
\mathbb{P}_{k}
, our proof is very similar to the one given in
[
ADW17
,
AS21
]
demonstrating that coalgebras over the pebbling comonad correspond to tree decompositions.
Intuitively, a sequence of pebbled elements with a common root forms a tree, so the mapping
f:A\rightarrow\mathbb{P}_{k}A
associates with
a\in A
a node in that tree.
By contrast, a mapping
f:A\rightarrow\mathbb{PR}_{k}A
associates with an element
a\in A
a full path of pebbled elements and index into that path.
It is this intuition that explains why coalgebras of
\mathbb{P}_{k}
correspond to tree decompositions, while coalgebras of
\mathbb{PR}_{k}
correspond to path decompositions.

{defi}

[path decomposition]
Given a finite
\sigma
-structure
\mathcal{A}
, a

\mathcal{A}
is a triple
(X,\leq_{X},\lambda)
, where
(X,\leq_{X})
is a linearly ordered set and
\lambda:X\rightarrow\wp(A)
is a function satisfying the following conditions:

(PD1)

For every
a\in A
, there exists
x\in X
such that
a\in\lambda(x)
.

(PD2)

If
a\frown a^{\prime}\in\mathcal{A}
, then
a,a^{\prime}\in\lambda(x)
for some
x\in X
.

(PD3)

For all
y\in[x,x^{\prime}]
,
\lambda(x)\cap\lambda(x^{\prime})\subseteq\lambda(y)
, where
[x,x^{\prime}]
is an interval with respect to
\leq_{X}
.

The
width of a path decomposition

(X,\leq_{X},\lambda)
for
\mathcal{A}
is given by

.

{defi}
[pathwidth]
The pathwidth of a
\sigma
-structure

has a path decomposition of width
k
.

-pebble linear forest cover for
\mathcal{A}
is a tuple
(\mathcal{F},p)
, where
\mathcal{F}=\{(S_{i},\leq_{i})\}_{i\in I}
is a partition of

into (possibly infinitely many) linearly ordered subsets and

is a pebbling function such that the following conditions hold:

(FC1)

If

, then there exists

such that

.

(FC2)

and

, then for all

,

.

We aim to show that the existence of a path decomposition
(X,\leq_{X},\lambda)
of width
<k
for a finite
\mathcal{A}
is equivalent to the existence of a
k
-pebble linear forest cover for
\mathcal{A}
.
In order to do so, we need to define a pebbling function

.
We accomplish this by defining functions
\tau_{x}:\lambda(x)\rightarrow[\textsf{k}]
on the subset associated with a node

in the path decomposition.
If we define these functions in a consistent manner, then they can be ‘glued’ together to obtain

.
We use the definition below to identify consistent families of
\tau_{x}
.

{defi}
[
k
-pebbling section family]
Given a path decomposition
(X,\leq_{X},\lambda)
of width
<k
for
\mathcal{A}
, we define a
k
-pebbling section family for
(X,\leq_{X},\lambda)
as a family of functions
\{\tau_{x}:\lambda(x)\rightarrow[\textsf{k}]\}
indexed by
x\in X
, such that the following conditions hold:

(1)

(Locally-injective) For every
x\in X
,
\tau_{x}
is an injective function.

(2)

(Glueability) For every
x,x^{\prime}\in X
,

\tau_{x}|_{\lambda(x)\cap\lambda(x^{\prime})}=\tau_{x^{\prime}}|_{\lambda(x)\cap\lambda(x^{\prime})}.

We show that every path decomposition has a pebbling section family.

Lemma 6
.

If
(X,\leq_{X},\lambda)
is a path decomposition of width
<k
, then
(X,\leq_{X},\lambda)
has a
k
-pebbling section family
\{\tau_{x}\}_{x\in X}
.

Proof 4.1
.

By induction on the linear order
\leq_{X}
for every
x\in X
, we construct a
k
-pebbling section family
\{\tau_{z}\}_{z\in{\downarrow x}}
for
{(Y,\leq_{Y},\lambda|_{Y})}
,
where
Y={\downarrow x}
and
\leq_{Y}={\leq_{X}}\cap(Y\times Y)
.

Base Case: Suppose

is the
\leq_{X}
-least element.
By definition

, the cardinality of
\lambda(r)
is
\leq k
, thus we can enumerate the elements via an injective function
\tau_{r}:\lambda(r)\rightarrow[\textsf{k}]
.
By construction,
\tau_{r}
is injective, so
\{\tau_{r}\}
is locally-injective.
Glueability follows trivially as every
x\in{\downarrow r}
is equal to

.

Inductive Step: Let
x^{\prime}
be the immediate
\leq_{X}
-successor of
x
.
By the induction hypothesis, there exists a
k
-pebbling section family
\{\tau_{y}\}_{y\in{\downarrow x}}
.
Let

denote the subset of ‘new’ elements

for every

.

Claim:
For every

.
We show that this claim holds: By

\lambda(y)\cap\lambda(x^{\prime})\subseteq\lambda(z)
.
In particular, since

,
\lambda(y)\cap\lambda(x^{\prime})\subseteq\lambda(x)
.
Therefore,
\lambda(y)\cap\lambda(x^{\prime})\subseteq\lambda(x)\cap\lambda(x^{\prime})
.

From the claim and the definition of

, we have that
\lambda(x^{\prime})=(\lambda(x)\cap\lambda(x^{\prime}))\sqcup V_{x^{\prime}}
, where

denotes a disjoint union.
This allows us to define

by cases on each of these parts.
Fix an injective function

enumerating

.
Let

enumerate the elements of
[\textsf{k}]
not in the image of

.
Define

as

\tau_{x^{\prime}}(a)=\begin{cases}\tau_{x}(a)&\text{if $a\in\lambda(x)\cap\lambda(x^{\prime})$};\\
i_{j}&\text{if $a\in V_{x^{\prime}}$ and $\upsilon_{x^{\prime}}(a)=j$}.\end{cases}

Injectivity of

follows from the injectivity of
\tau_{x}|_{\lambda(x)\cap\lambda(x^{\prime})}
and
\upsilon_{x^{\prime}}
.
To verify glueability, it suffices to check that
\tau_{x^{\prime}}|_{\lambda(y)\cap\lambda(x^{\prime})}=\tau_{y}|_{\lambda(y)\cap\lambda(x^{\prime})}
for

.
Since

is a
k
-pebbling section family, for all

,
\tau_{y}|_{\lambda(y)\cap\lambda(x)}=\tau_{x}|_{\lambda(y)\cap\lambda(x)}
.
By construction,
\tau_{x}|_{\lambda(x)\cap\lambda(x^{\prime})}=\tau_{x^{\prime}}|_{\lambda(x)\cap\lambda(x^{\prime})}
.
By the claim, we have that
\lambda(y)\cap\lambda(x^{\prime})\subseteq\lambda(x)\cap\lambda(x^{\prime})
.
Therefore,
\tau_{y}|_{\lambda(y)\cap\lambda(x^{\prime})}=\tau_{x^{\prime}}|_{\lambda(y)\cap\lambda(x^{\prime})}
.

5.
Morphism and Isomorphism Power Theorems

While the construction of
\mathbb{PR}_{k}
is motivated by capturing pathwidth as coalgebras, the other Spoiler-Duplicator game comonads

, explored in previous work

In this section we prove a morphism power theorem for

, showing that coKleisli morphisms
\mathbb{PR}_{k}\mathcal{A}\rightarrow\mathcal{B}
correspond to Duplicator’s winning strategies in a one-sided pebble-relation game.
This game characterises preservation of sentences in the existential positive
k
-variable logic with restricted conjunctions

We then prove an isomorphism power theorem for

which works analogously by showing that coKleisli isomorphisms

correspond to Duplicator’s winning strategies in a new type of pebble game which will be described in detail. We conclude the section with an ensuing corollary of the exhibited results.

The proof of this theorem follows similar lines to the analogous proof for the bijective Ehrenfeucht-Fraïssé game given by

We define the logic
{\#{\curlywedge}\mathscr{L}^{k}}
where the equivalence relation
\equiv_{{\#{\curlywedge}\mathscr{L}^{k}}}
is characterised by Duplicator winning strategies in
\#\textbf{PPeb}_{k}(\mathcal{A},\mathcal{B})

In order to define our logic
{\#{\curlywedge}\mathscr{L}^{k}}

{\exists{\curlywedge}\mathscr{L}^{k}}(Z)

This logic is the same as
{\exists^{+}{\curlywedge}\mathscr{L}^{k}}

We also allow negations on atomic formulas in
{\exists{\curlywedge}\mathscr{L}^{k}}(Z)
and require that every existential quantifier
\exists x_{j}
is guarded with an equality

equating a ordinary variable
x_{j}
with a walk pointer variable
z_{i}
.
Explicitly, the formulas of
{\exists{\curlywedge}\mathscr{L}^{k}}(Z)
can be defined recursively as follows:

\psi(\vec{x},\vec{z})::=R(x_{i_{1}},\dots,x_{i_{r}})\;|\;\neg p\;|\;\bigwedge\Psi\;|\;\bigvee\Psi\;|\;\exists x_{j}(x_{j}=z_{i}\wedge\psi(\vec{x^{\prime}},\vec{z}^{\prime})),

where

is an

\{x_{i_{1}},\dots,x_{i_{r}}\}\in[\vec{x}]\subseteq\{x_{1},\dots,x_{k}\}
,

is an atomic formula,
\bigwedge\Psi
is a conjunction of formulas
\psi(\vec{x},\vec{z})
satisfying
(R)
,
\bigvee\Psi
is a disjunction of formulas
\psi(\vec{x},\vec{z})
,
x_{j}\in[\vec{x^{\prime}}]
and
z_{i}\notin\vec{z}^{\prime}
.

We can now define sentences of
{\#{\curlywedge}\mathscr{L}^{k}}
as disjunctions of formulas that involve counting tuples of

Explicitly, the sentence of
{\#{\curlywedge}\mathscr{L}^{k}}
can be defined recursively as follows:

\phi::=\bigvee\Phi\;|\;\exists^{j}(z_{i_{1}},\dots,z_{i_{m}})\psi(z_{i_{1}},\dots,z_{i_{m}}),

where
\Phi
is a collection of sentences
\phi
,

and
\psi(z_{i_{1}},\dots,z_{i_{m}})\in{\exists{\curlywedge}\mathscr{L}^{k}}(Z)
.

Observe that in the syntax of the
\exists^{j}\vec{z}
quantifier every free variable in the bound formula
\psi(\vec{z})\in{\exists{\curlywedge}\mathscr{L}^{k}}(Z)
is from the sort
Z
of walk pointer variables.
We define the semantics of the operation above as

•

\mathcal{A}\vDash\exists^{j}(z_{i_{1}},\dots,z_{i_{m}})\psi(z_{i_{1}},\dots,z_{i_{m}})
iff there exist exactly

\vec{a}=(a_{1},\dots,a_{m})
such that
\mathcal{A},\vec{a}\vDash\psi(z_{i_{1}},\dots,z_{i_{m}})
.

The quantifier
\exists^{j}(z_{i_{1}},\dots,z_{i_{m}})
in the logic
{\#{\curlywedge}\mathscr{L}^{k}}
is inspired by a similar

[

.

A formula of
\varphi(\vec{x})\in{\exists^{+}{\curlywedge}\mathscr{L}^{k}}
is primitive if it contains no disjunctive subformula and every restricted conjunction subformula
\bigvee\Psi
does not contain a sentence.
From definition of primitive formula and the condition
(R)

\bigvee\Psi

contains only literals and at most one quantified formula with a free variable.
We similarly can define primitive formulas of
{\exists{\curlywedge}\mathscr{L}^{k}}(Z)
.
Thus, we extend this definition to

5.3.
Lovász-type theorem for pathwidth

In one of his seminal papers, Lovász
[
Lov67
]
proved that two finite
\sigma
-structures
\mathcal{A}
and
\mathcal{B}
are isomorphic if and only if for all finite
\sigma
-structures
\mathcal{C}
, the number of homomorphisms from
\mathcal{C}
to
\mathcal{A}
is the same as the number of homomorphisms from
\mathcal{C}
to
\mathcal{B}
.
Following this result, weaker notions of equivalence have been found to have an analogous characterisation
[
Dvo09
,
Gro20
,
MR20
]
.
These equivalences involve restricting the class of test structures
\mathcal{C}
.
For instance, Dvořák
[
Dvo09
]
showed that counting homomorphisms from finite
\sigma
-structures of treewidth
<k
yields equivalence in
k
-variable logic with counting quantifiers.

Counting homomorphisms from graphs of treewidth
<k
also has a linear algebraic characterisation in terms of existence of a non-negative real solution to a certain system of linear
equations
[
DGR18
]
.

It was shown in a later work by Grohe, Rattan and Seppelt
[
GRS22
]
that removing this non-negativity constraint yields a homomorphism counting result from graphs of pathwidth
<k
.
In their work they used techniques from representation theory.
By contrast, we use techniques from category theory to obtain an analogue of Dvořák’s result providing a logical characterisation of homomorphism counting from
\sigma
-structures with pathwidth
<k
.

In recent work, Dawar, Jakl and Reggio
[
DJR21
]
introduced a generalisation of Lovász-type theorems for comonads over
\mathfrak{R}(\sigma)
.
In this section we utilise this generalisation in order to show that counting homomorphisms from finite
\sigma
-structures of pathwidth
<k
yields equivalence in restricted conjunction
k
-variable logic with counting quantifiers.

We begin by introducing the categorical generalisation for Lovász-type theorems

Let

Conclusion

The results we exhibited extend the growing list of Spoiler-Duplicator game comonads that unify particular model-comparison games with combinatorial invariants of relational structures.
This approach has applications in reformulations of Rossman’s homomorphism preservation theorem

Abstract
. The maximal rank n n which admits a non-almost regular m m -gonal form is 4 4 . In this article, for any given ( a 1 , a 2 , a 3 , a 4 ) ∈ ℕ 4 (a_{1},a_{2},a_{3},a_{4})\in{\mathbb{N}}^{4} and sufficiently large m m , we completely determine whether the m m -gonal form ⟨ a 1 , a 2 , a 3 , a 4 ⟩ m \left<a_{1},a_{2},a_{3},a_{4}\right>_{m} is almost regular or not. And we show that for any m ≥ 3 m\geq 3 , there are infinitely many non-almost regular m m -gonal forms.



1.
Introduction

The
m
-gonal number
which is defined as a total number of dots to constitute a regular
m
-gon is one of the classical objects in the number theory.
More precisely, we call the total number of dots of regular
m
-gon with
x
dots for each side

(1.1)

P_{m}(x):=\frac{m-2}{2}(x^{2}-x)+x

as
x
-th
m
-gonal number
.
One of the most famous stories about
m
-gonal number is a Fermat’s Conjecture which states that every positive integer may be written as a sum of at most
m

m
-gonal numbers.
This conjecture was resolved by Lagrange, Gauss, and Cauchy for
m=4
,
m=3
, and
m\geq 3
, respectively.
As a generalization of Gauss’s work, Liouville classified the
3
-tuples
(a_{1},a_{2},a_{3})\in{\mathbb{N}}^{3}
for which
every positive integer can be written as the weighted sum of triangular numbers
a_{1}P_{3}(x_{1})+a_{2}P_{3}(x_{2})+a_{3}P_{3}(x_{3})
.
And as a generalization of Lagrange’s work, Ramanujan classified the
4
-tuples
(a_{1},a_{2},a_{3},a_{4})\in{\mathbb{N}}^{4}
for which
every positive integer can be written as the weighted sum of square numbers
a_{1}P_{4}(x_{1})+a_{2}P_{4}(x_{2})+a_{3}P_{4}(x_{3})+a_{4}P_{4}(x_{4})
, but one mistake was found later in the Ramanujan’s list.

We call a weighted sum of
m
-gonal numbers

(1.2)

F_{m}(\mathbf{x})=a_{1}P_{m}(x_{1})+\cdots+a_{n}P_{m}(x_{n})

where
(a_{1},\cdots,a_{n})\in{\mathbb{N}}^{n}
as
m
-gonal form
.
We simply write the
m
-gonal form
a_{1}P_{m}(x_{1})+\cdots+a_{n}P_{m}(x_{n})
as
\left<a_{1},\cdots,a_{n}\right>_{m}
.
But exceptionally we adopt a notation
\left<a_{1},\cdots,a_{n}\right>
for the square (or
4
-gonal) form (i.e., diagonal quadratic form) instead of
\left<a_{1},\cdots,a_{n}\right>_{4}
true to the tradition of the theory of quadratic form.

By directly following the definition of original
m
-gonal number, we may catch that only non-negative rational integer
x
would be admitted in (
1.1
).
But recently, as a generalized
m
-gonal number version, we often admit negative rational integer
x
too.
For
N\in{\mathbb{N}}
, if the diophantine equation

(1.3)

F_{m}(\mathbf{x})=N

has an integer solution
\mathbf{x}\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}
(in sense of the generalized
m
-gonal number) (resp. a non-negative integer solution
\mathbf{x}\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}^{n}
(in sense of the original
m
-gonal number)), then we say that the
m
-gonal form
F_{m}(\mathbf{x})

(globally) represents

N
over
{\mathbb{Z}}
(resp. over
{\mathbb{N}}_{0}
).
Undoubtedly, a representation of an integer
N
over
{\mathbb{N}}_{0}
implies a representation of
N
over
{\mathbb{Z}}
by an
m
-gonal form.
As is well known, the problem of completely classifying every positive integer
N
which is represented by arbitrary given
m
-gonal form
F_{m}(\mathbf{x})
is not easy in general.
In an effort to approach the problem,
we suggest the reduced congruence equation

(1.4)

F_{m}(\mathbf{x})\equiv N\pmod{r}.

When the congruence equation (
1.4
) has an integer solution
\mathbf{x}\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}
(or equivalently, has a non-negative integer solution
\mathbf{x}\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}^{n}
) for every
r\in{\mathbb{Z}}
, we say that
F_{m}(\mathbf{x})

locally represents

N
.
The solvability of congruence equation

F_{m}(\mathbf{x})\equiv N\pmod{p^{\alpha}}

only for every prime
p
and natural number
{\alpha}\in{\mathbb{N}}

would be enough to derive the local representability of
N
by
F_{m}(\mathbf{x})
in virtue of the Chinese Remainder Theorem.
Note that for a prime
p
,
the congruence equation
F_{m}(\mathbf{x})\equiv N\pmod{p^{\alpha}}

has an integer solution
\mathbf{x}\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}
for every natural number
{\alpha}\in{\mathbb{N}}

if and only if the equation
F_{m}(\mathbf{x})=N
has a
p
-adic integer solution
\mathbf{x}\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}^{n}
.
When the equation
F_{m}(\mathbf{x})=N
has a
p
-adic integer solution
\mathbf{x}\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}^{n}
, we say that
F_{m}(\mathbf{x})
represents
N
over
{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}
.
Obviously, the (global) representability implies the local representability, but the converse does not hold in general.
Especially, when the converse also holds, i.e., an
m
-gonal form
F_{m}(\mathbf{x})
represents every positive integer
N
which is locally represented by
F_{m}(\mathbf{x})
over
{\mathbb{Z}}
(resp. over
{\mathbb{N}}_{0}
), we say that
F_{m}(\mathbf{x})
is
regular over
{\mathbb{Z}}
(resp. over
{\mathbb{N}}_{0}
)
.
When
F_{m}(\mathbf{x})
represents every positive integer
N
which is locally represented by
F_{m}(\mathbf{x})
but finitely many over
{\mathbb{Z}}
(resp. over
{\mathbb{N}}_{0}
), we say that
F_{m}(\mathbf{x})
is
almost regular over
{\mathbb{Z}}
(resp. over
{\mathbb{N}}_{0}
)
.

The concept of

was firstly introduced by Dickson in

]
.
In

In

And they claimed the regularity of all of them but 22.

And Lemke Oliver

The finiteness of primitive regular ternary triangular forms (i.e.,

and all the finitely many primitive regular ternary triangular forms was determined by Kim and Oh

As an improvement of the result in

]
, Chan and Ricci

]
.
As another version’s improvement of the work, in

]
, He and Kane showed the finiteness of primitive regular ternary polygonal forms (i.e.,

-gonal forms for arbitrary

Which implies that there are only finitely many

-gonal form of rank

.
In

, Kim and the author showed that

which admits a regular

-gonal form of rank

.
Moreover in
[

, they completely determined the type of regular

-gonal forms of rank

for all

and
m\geq 28
with

Now let us turn to our main subject which is almost regular form in this article.
By Theorem 4.9 (1) in
[

]
, every

-gonal form of rank

is almost regular over

, i.e.,

-gonal form of rank

.
But such the nice local-to-global principle is not guaranteed when the rank is less than 5 in general.
There is non-almost regular

-gonal form of rank

-gonal form over

.
In this short article, we mainly look into the non-almost regular

-gonal forms of rank
4
.
The following two theorems are the main goal in this article.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
a_{1},a_{2},a_{3},a_{4}\in{\mathbb{N}}
be given.

(1)

If the quaternary quadratic form
\left<a_{1},a_{2},a_{3},a_{4}\right>\otimes{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}
is isotropic for every prime
p
, then
the quaternary
m
-gonal form
\left<a_{1},a_{2},a_{3},a_{4}\right>_{m}
is almost regular over
{\mathbb{Z}}
for any
m\geq 3
.

(2)

Otherwise, define

a non-empty set of primes.

For any
m\geq 3
satisfying

(1.5)

\begin{cases}p|m-2&\text{for all $p\in T\setminus\{2\}$}\\
m\not\equiv 0\pmod{4}&\text{if }2\in T,\end{cases}

the
m
-gonal form
\left<a_{1},a_{2},a_{3},a_{4}\right>_{m}
is almost regular over
{\mathbb{Z}}
.

For every sufficiently large

which is not satisfying (

), the
m
-gonal form
\left<a_{1},a_{2},a_{3},a_{4}\right>_{m}
is not almost regular over

(so also over

).

Theorem 1.2
.

For any

, there are infinitely many primitive quaternary

-gonal forms which are not almost regular over
{\mathbb{Z}}
(so also over
{\mathbb{N}}_{0}
).

Remark 1.3
.

By using Theorem
1.1
, for arbitrary given

-tuple
(a_{1},a_{2},a_{3},a_{4})\in{\mathbb{N}}^{4}
and any sufficiently large

-gonal form
\left<a_{1},a_{2},a_{3},a_{4}\right>_{m}

2.
Preliminaries

First of all, we may get an easy but important observation that

N=a_{1}P_{m}(x_{1})+\cdots+a_{n}P_{m}(x_{n})

if and only if

8(m-2)N+(m-4)^{2}(a_{1}+\cdots+a_{n})=\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_{i}(2(m-2)x_{i}-(m-4))^{2}.

Which allows transfer the issue of the representation by
m
-gonal form to the issue of the representation by diagonal quadratic form with a congruence condition.
By using the following proposition, one may effectively determine the representability of integer by an
m
-gonal form over non-archimedean local ring with help of known results in the quadratic form theory over non-archimedean local ring.

Proposition 2.1
.

Let
F_{m}(\mathbf{x})=a_{1}P_{m}(x_{1})+\cdots+a_{n}P_{m}(x_{n})
be a primitive (i.e., satisfying
(a_{1},\cdots,a_{n})=1
)
m
-gonal form.

(1)

When
p
is an odd prime with
p|m-2
,
F_{m}(\mathbf{x})
is universal over
{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}
.

(2)

When
m\not\equiv 0\pmod{4}
,
F_{m}(\mathbf{x})
is universal over
{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}
.

(3)

When
p
is an odd prime with
(p,m-2)=1
, an integer
N
is represented by
F_{m}(\mathbf{x})
over
{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}
if and only if the integer
8(m-2)N+(a_{1}+\cdots+a_{n})(m-4)^{2}
is represented by the diagonal quadratic form
\left<a_{1},\cdots,a_{n}\right>
over
{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}
.

(4)

When
m\equiv 0\pmod{4}
, an integer
N
is represented by
F_{m}(\mathbf{x})
over
{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}
if and only if the integer
\frac{m-2}{2}N+(a_{1}+\cdots+a_{n})\left(\frac{m-4}{4}\right)^{2}
is represented by the diagonal quadratic form
\left<a_{1},\cdots,a_{n}\right>
over
{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}
.

Proof.

See Proposition 3.1 in
[
14
]
.

Remark 2.2
.

Now, we may need understanding the behavior of representation by a diagonal quadratic form which is anisotropic over a non-archimedean local ring.

(1)

First, we consider a quaternary quadratic form which is anisotropic over a non-dyadic (i.e., for an odd prime
p
) local ring
{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}
.
A quaternary anisotropic diagonal quadratic form over

(2)

A primitive quaternary anisotropic diagonal quadratic form over dyadic

\left<a_{1}2^{2r_{1}},a_{2}2^{2r_{2}},a_{3}2^{2r_{3}},a_{4}2^{2r_{4}}\right>

.
One may get that for a quadratic form
\left<a_{1},a_{2},a_{3},a_{4}\right>

{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}

\text{ord}_{2}(a_{1}x_{1}^{2}+a_{2}x_{2}^{2}+a_{3}x_{3}^{2}+a_{4}x_{4}^{2})\geq r+2

\left<a_{1},a_{2},a_{3},a_{4}\right>_{m}
over
{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}
for any
N\equiv r(p)\pmod{p^{\text{ord}_{p}(4p)}}
.
By combining the above arguments with the Chinese Remainder Theorem, we may take a residue
r
in
{\mathbb{Z}}/\left(\prod\limits_{p\in T\setminus\{q\}}p^{\text{ord}_{p}(4p)}\right)\cdot\left(q^{\max\limits_{1\leq i\leq 4}\text{ord}_{q}(64a_{i})}\right){\mathbb{Z}}

satisfying that for any integer
N
with

N\equiv r\pmod{\left(\prod\limits_{p\in T^{\prime}\setminus\{q\}}p^{\text{ord}_{p}(4p)}\right)\cdot\left(q^{\max\limits_{1\leq i\leq 4}\text{ord}_{q}(64a_{i})}\right)},

the followings

\begin{cases}\text{$N$ is locally represented by $\left<a_{1},a_{2},a_{3},a_{4}\right>_{m}$}\\
\text{$8(m-2)N+(m-4)^{2}(a_{1}+a_{2}+a_{3}+a_{4})\equiv 0\pmod{q^{\max\limits_{1\leq i\leq 4}\text{ord}_{q}(64a_{i})}}$}\end{cases}

hold.
So we may take a positive integer
N_{0}
in the interval
[a_{1}+a_{2}+a_{3}+a_{4}+1,m-4]
such that
N_{0}
is locally represented by
\left<a_{1},a_{2},a_{3},a_{4}\right>_{m}
and

8(m-2)N_{0}+(m-4)^{2}(a_{1}+a_{2}+a_{3}+a_{4})\equiv 0\pmod{q^{\max\limits_{1\leq i\leq 4}\text{ord}_{q}(64a_{i})}}.

But since the smallest
m
-gonal number is
m-3
except
0
and
1
, the integer
N_{0}
in the interval
[a_{1}+a_{2}+a_{3}+a_{4}+1,m-4]
would not be (globally)
represented by
\left<a_{1},a_{2},a_{3},a_{4}\right>_{m}
over
{\mathbb{Z}}
(and so over
{\mathbb{N}}_{0}
too).
And then we may get infinitely many positive integers
N_{n}
which are represented by
\left<a_{1},a_{2},a_{3},a_{4}\right>_{m}
locally, but not globally
where

8(m-2)N_{n}+(m-4)^{2}(a_{1}+a_{2}+a_{3}+a_{4})=q^{2kn}(8(m-2)N_{0}+(m-4)^{2}(a_{1}+a_{2}+a_{3}+a_{4}))
for
k\in{\mathbb{N}}
satisfying

(3.1)

\begin{cases}q^{k}\equiv 1\pmod{8(m-2)}&\text{when }q\text{ is an odd prime}\\
q^{k}\equiv 1\pmod{\frac{m-2}{2}}&\text{when }q=2.\end{cases}

For a contradiction, we suppose that
N_{n}
is represented by
\left<a_{1},a_{2},a_{3},a_{4}\right>_{m}
over
{\mathbb{Z}}
, i.e.,

8(m-2)N_{n}+(m-4)^{2}(a_{1}+a_{2}+a_{3}+a_{4})=\sum_{i=1}^{4}a_{i}(2(m-2)x_{i}-(m-4))^{2}

for some
(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3},x_{4})\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{4}
.
Since

8(m-2)N_{n}+(m-4)^{2}(a_{1}+a_{2}+a_{3}+a_{4})\equiv 0\pmod{q^{2kn+{\max\limits_{1\leq i\leq 4}\{\text{ord}_{q}(64a_{i})\}}}}

and
\left<a_{1},a_{2},a_{3},a_{4}\right>\otimes{\mathbb{Z}}_{q}
is anisotropic,
we have that

2(m-2)x_{i}-(m-4)\equiv 0\pmod{q^{kn+\text{ord}_{q}(4)}}

by Remark
2.2
.
From our choice of
k\in{\mathbb{N}}
satisfying (
3.1
), we have that

\frac{2(m-2)x_{i}-(m-4)}{q^{kn}}=2(m-2)y_{i}-(m-4)

for some
y_{i}\in{\mathbb{Z}}
.
And then we obtain a contradiction that
N_{0}=a_{1}P_{m}(y_{1})+a_{2}P_{m}(y_{2})+a_{3}P_{m}(y_{3})+a_{4}P_{m}(y_{4})
.
This completes the proof.

∎

Proof of Theorem
1.2
.

Let
p
be an odd prime with
(p,m-2)=1
.
We may take an odd prime
q_{1}
which is a quadratic residue modulo
p
and an odd prime
q_{2}(\not=q_{1})
for which
-q_{2}
is a quadratic non-residue modulo
p
.
Note that it would be possible to take infinitely many such odd primes
p,q_{1}
and
q_{2}
.
Now we show that the
m
-gonal form

\left<q_{1},q_{2},q_{1}p,q_{2}p\right>_{m}

is not almost regular over
{\mathbb{Z}}
(so also over
{\mathbb{N}}_{0}
).
By Proposition
2.1
, we may see that
\left<q_{1},q_{2},q_{1}p,q_{2}p\right>_{m}
is locally universal since the quaternary quadratic form
\left<q_{1},q_{2},q_{1}p,q_{2}p\right>
is locally universal.
But the
m
-gonal form
\left<q_{1},q_{2},q_{1}p,q_{2}p\right>_{m}
does not (globally) represent the infinitely many positive integers

N_{n}=\frac{p^{2kn}\{8(m-2)+(m-4)^{2}(q_{1}+q_{2}+q_{1}p+q_{2}p)\}-(m-4)^{2}(q_{1}+q_{2}+q_{1}p+q_{2}p)}{8(m-2)}

where

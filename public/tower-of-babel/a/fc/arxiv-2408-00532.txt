Large deviations for the maximum of a reducible two-type branching Brownian motion
Hui He

Abstract
. We consider a two-type reducible branching Brownian motion, defined as a particle system on the real line in which particles of two types move according to independent Brownian motions and create offspring at a constant rate. Particles of type 1 1 can give birth to particles of types 1 1 and 2 2 , but particles of type 2 2 only give births to descendants of type 2 2 . Under some specific conditions, Belloum and Mallein in [ 5 ] showed that the maximum position M t M_{t} of all particles alive at time t t , suitably centered by a deterministic function m t m_{t} , converge weakly. In this work, we are interested in the decay rate of the following upper large deviation probability, as t → ∞ t\rightarrow\infty , ℙ ⁡ ( M t ≥ θ ​ m t ) , θ > 1 . \mathbb{P}(M_{t}\geq\theta m_{t}),\quad\theta>1. We shall show that the decay rate function exhibits phase transitions depending on certain relations between θ \theta , the variance of the underlying Brownian motion and the branching rate.

1.
Introduction and main results

Branching Brownian motion (BBM) is a spatial branching process of great interests in recent years. On the one hand, its connection with the partial differential Fisher-Kolmogorov-Petrovsky-Piskunov (F-KPP) equation brings out many studies on both probabilistic and analytic sides, see for instance,
[
1
,
18
,
10
,
16
,
3
]
. On the other hand, it is the fundamental model to understand the BBM-universality class which includes the 2-dim Gaussian free field
[
8
,
9
]
and 2-dim cover times
[
12
]
, etc.

A one-dimensional standard binary branching Brownian motion is a continuous-time particle system on the real line which can be constructed as follows. It starts with one individual located at the origin at time
0
that moves according to a Brownian motion with variance
\sigma^{2}>0
. After an independent exponential time of parameter
\beta
, the initial particle dies and gives birth to 2 children that start at the position their parent occupied at its death. These
2
children then move according to independent Brownian motions with variance
\sigma^{2}
and give birth independently to their own children at rate
\beta
. The particle system keeps evolving in this fashion for all time.

In this work, we shall study the two-type reducible branching Brownian motion. This is a particle system on the real line in which particles possess a type in addition with their position. Particles of type
1
move according to Brownian motions with variance
\sigma^{2}
and branch at rate
\beta
into two children of type
1
. Additionally, they give birth to particles of type
2
at rate
\alpha
. Particles of type
2
move according to Brownian motions with variance
\sigma_{2}^{2}
and branch at rate
\beta_{2}
, but cannot give birth to descendants of type
1
. Up to a dilation of time and space, in this work we shall always assume that
\beta_{2}=\sigma_{2}=1
. This model has been studied extensively by many authors; see
[
7
,
14
,
15
,
17
]
.

With abuse of notation, for all
t\geq 0
, we denote by
{\mathcal{N}}_{t}
the collection of individuals alive at time
t
for both the standard BBM and the two-type reducible BBM. And denote by
{\mathcal{N}}_{t}^{1}
and
{\mathcal{N}}_{t}^{2}
the set of particles of type 1 and type 2 at time
t
, respectively. For any
u\in{\mathcal{N}}(t)
and
s\leq t
, let
X^{u}_{s}
denote the position at time
s
of the individual
u
or its ancestor alive at that time. The maximum of the branching Brownian motion at time
t
is defined as

\displaystyle M_{t}:=\max\{X^{u}_{t}:u\in{\mathcal{N}}(t)\}.

To distinguish the two-type reducible BBM and the standard BBM, we shall regard the standard BBM as
(\sigma^{2},\beta)
-BBM to emphasize the variance of the Brownian motion and the branching rate. We also denote by
\mathbb{P}_{\sigma^{2},\beta}
its law and write
\bar{X}^{u}_{s}
for the position at time
s
of the individual
u
of a
(\sigma^{2},\beta)
-BBM.
For
(\sigma^{2},\beta)
-BBM, the asymptotic behaviour of

\bar{M}_{t}:=\max\{\bar{X}_{t}^{u}:u\in{\mathcal{N}}(t)\},

as
t\to\infty
, has been subjected to intense study, partly due to its link with the F-KPP reaction-diffusion equation, defined as

(1)

\partial_{t}u(t,x)=\frac{1}{2}\partial^{2}_{x}u(t,x)+(1-u(t,x))-(1-u(t,x))^{2},\quad t\geq 0,\,x\in{\mathbb{R}}.

Precisely, McKean
[
18
]
showed that

\mathbb{P}_{\sigma^{2},1}(\bar{M}_{t}\geq\sigma x)=\bar{u}(t,x),

where the function
(t,x)\mapsto\bar{u}(t,x)
is the unique solution of (
1
) with initial condition
\bar{u}(0,x)=\mathbf{1}_{\{x<0\}}
. With the help of (
1
), Bramson
[
10
]
proved that for a
(\sigma^{2},\beta)
-BBM,

\bar{M}_{t}-\sqrt{2\sigma^{2}\beta}t+\frac{3}{2\sqrt{2\beta/\sigma^{2}}}\ln t,

converges weakly, as
t\rightarrow\infty
. In particular, uniformly in
x\in\mathbb{R}
, as
t\rightarrow\infty
,

(2)

\displaystyle 1-\bar{u}(t,\bar{m}_{t}+x)=\mathbb{P}_{\sigma^{2},1}\left(\frac{\bar{M}_{t}}{\sigma}-\sqrt{2}t+\frac{3}{2\sqrt{2}}\ln t\leq x\right)\longrightarrow\bar{\omega}(x),

where
\bar{\omega}
is the travelling wave solution of the F-KPP equation at speed
\sqrt{2}
, which satisfies

\frac{1}{2}\bar{\omega}^{\prime\prime}+\sqrt{2}\bar{\omega}^{\prime}-\bar{\omega}(1-\bar{\omega})=0.

Lalley and Sellke
[
16
]
characterized
\bar{\omega}
in terms of a random shift of the Gumbel distribution by introducing the so-called derivative martingale.

Parallel results of (
2
) for the two-type reducible BBM were obtained in
[
5
]
. To state their results, we need to decompose the state space
(\beta,\sigma^{2})\in{\mathbb{R}}_{+}^{2}
into three regions (see Figure 1 below for the phase diagram):

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}_{\text{I}}

\displaystyle=\left\{\left(\beta,\sigma^{2}\right):\sigma^{2}>\frac{\mathbb{1}_{\{\beta\leq 1\}}}{\beta}+\mathbb{1}_{\{\beta>1\}}\frac{\beta}{2\beta-1}\right\},

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}_{\text{II}}

\displaystyle=\left\{\left(\beta,\sigma^{2}\right):\sigma^{2}<\frac{\mathbb{1}_{\{\beta\leq 1\}}}{\beta}+\mathbb{1}_{\{\beta>1\}}(2-\beta)\right\},

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}_{\text{III}}

\displaystyle=\left\{\left(\beta,\sigma^{2}\right):\sigma^{2}+\beta>2\text{ and }\sigma^{2}<\frac{\beta}{2\beta-1}\right\}.

Phase diagram of the two-type reducible BBM

From Belloum and Mallein
[

]
, we have:

M_{t}-m_{t}

converges weakly, as

, where

\displaystyle m_{t}=\begin{cases}\sqrt{2\sigma^{2}\beta}t-\frac{3}{2\sqrt{2\beta/\sigma^{2}}}\ln t,\quad&(\beta,\sigma^{2})\in\mathcal{C}_{\text{I}},\cr\sqrt{2}t-\frac{3}{2\sqrt{2}}\ln t,\quad&(\beta,\sigma^{2})\in\mathcal{C}_{\text{II}},\cr\frac{\sigma^{2}-\beta}{\sqrt{2\left(1-\sigma^{2}\right)(\beta-1)}}t,\quad&(\beta,\sigma^{2})\in\mathcal{C}_{\text{III}}.\end{cases}

From above, we see that if
\left(\beta,\sigma^{2}\right)\in\mathcal{C}_{I}
, the speed of the two-type reducible BBM is
\sqrt{2\beta\sigma^{2}}
, which is the same as particle s of type
1
alone, ignoring births of particles of type
2
. Conversely, if
\left(\beta,\sigma^{2}\right)\in\mathcal{C}_{\text{II}}
, then the speed of the process is
\sqrt{2}
, equal to the speed of a single BBM of particles of type
2
. Finally, if
\left(\beta,\sigma^{2}\right)\in\mathcal{C}_{\text{III}}
, the speed of the process is a mixture of the long-time asymptotic of the processes of particles of type
1
and
2
, which is larger than
\max\left(\sqrt{2},\sqrt{2\beta\sigma^{2}}\right)
.

We remark here that before the work of Belloum and Mallein
[
5
]
, Biggins’ work
[

was the first to consider the maximum of multi-type reducible branching random walks. After the work of Belloum and Mallein
[

does not cover all the possible values for

[

studied the case of
\beta=\sigma^{2}=1

In this article, we study the large deviation probabilities for the maximum of a two-type reducible BBM. That is the convergence rate of the following probability

\mathbb{P}(M_{t}>\theta m_{t}),\quad\theta>1,

as
t\rightarrow\infty
. We divide the main result of our article into three theorems, one for each area the pair
\left(\beta,\sigma^{2}\right)
belongs to.

Theorem 1.1
.

Assume
\left(\beta,\sigma^{2}\right)\in\mathcal{C}_{\text{I}}
. Then
the following limit exists for any
\theta>1
,

\lim_{t\to\infty}\frac{1}{t}\ln\mathbb{P}\left(M_{t}\geq\sqrt{2\beta\sigma^{2}\theta^{2}}\cdot t\right)=A,

where if
\sigma^{2}\geq 1
, then

A=\beta\left(1-\theta^{2}\right),

and if
\beta>1
and
\sigma^{2}<1
, then we have

A=\begin{cases}\beta\left(1-\theta^{2}\right),&\theta^{2}\leq\frac{(\beta-1)\sigma^{2}}{\left(1-\sigma^{2}\right)\beta},\\
1-\theta^{2}\beta\sigma^{2},&\theta^{2}\geq\frac{(\beta-1)}{\left(1-\sigma^{2}\right)\sigma^{2}\beta},\\
\frac{-2\sqrt{\beta(\beta-1)\left(1-\sigma^{2}\right)}\sigma\theta+\beta-\sigma^{2}}{1-\sigma^{2}},&\frac{(\beta-1)\sigma^{2}}{\left(1-\sigma^{2}\right)\beta}<\theta^{2}<\frac{(\beta-1)}{\left(1-\sigma^{2}\right)\sigma^{2}\beta}.\end{cases}

Theorem 1.2
.

Assume
\left(\beta,\sigma^{2}\right)\in\mathcal{C}_{\text{II}}
. Then the following limit exists for any
\theta>1
,

\lim_{t\to\infty}\frac{1}{t}\ln\mathbb{P}\left(M_{t}\geq\sqrt{2}\theta t\right)=:A,

where if
\sigma^{2}\leq 1
, we have

A=1-\theta^{2},

and if
\beta\leq 1,\,\sigma^{2}>1
, we have

A=\begin{cases}1-\theta^{2},&\theta^{2}\leq\frac{(1-\beta)}{\left(\sigma^{2}-1\right)},\cr\beta-\frac{\theta^{2}}{\sigma^{2},}&\theta^{2}\geq\frac{(1-\beta)}{\left(\sigma^{2}-1\right)}\sigma^{4},\cr\frac{2\sqrt{(1-\beta)\left(\sigma^{2}-1\right)}\theta+\beta-\sigma^{2}}{1-\sigma^{2}},&\frac{(1-\beta)}{\left(\sigma^{2}-1\right)}<\theta^{2}<\frac{(1-\beta)}{\left(\sigma^{2}-1\right)}\sigma^{4}.\end{cases}

Theorem 1.3
.

Assume
\left(\beta,\sigma^{2}\right)\in\mathcal{C}_{\text{III}}
. Then for any
\theta>1
, we have

\lim_{t\to\infty}\frac{1}{t}\ln\mathbb{P}\left(M_{t}\geq\xi\theta t\right)=\begin{cases}1-\frac{\xi^{2}\theta^{2}}{2},&\theta^{2}\geq\frac{2(\beta-1)}{1-\sigma^{2}}\frac{1}{\xi^{2}},\\

where

Notice that
f_{2}^{\prime}(u_{0})=0
. This corresponds to the case that

\beta<1,1<\sigma^{2}<\frac{1}{\beta}
, and

\frac{(1-\beta)}{\left(\sigma^{2}-1\right)}<\theta^{2}<\frac{(1-\beta)}{\left(\sigma^{2}-1\right)}\sigma^{4}.

We also recall that in this case

0<u_{0}=\frac{\sqrt{\frac{\sigma^{2}-1}{1-\beta}}\cdot\theta-1}{\sigma^{2}-1}<1

and

f_{2}(u_{0})=\frac{-2\sqrt{(1-\beta)\left(\sigma^{2}-1\right)}\theta-\beta+\sigma^{2}}{\sigma^{2}-1}.

Choose

x_{0}=\frac{\sqrt{2}\theta u_{0}\sigma^{2}}{1-u_{0}+u_{0}\sigma^{2}},\quad x_{t}=x_{0}t.

One can check that
x_{0}<\sqrt{2}\theta
since
u_{0}>0.

Then,

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left(\exists\,u\in{\mathcal{N}}_{t}^{2}:X_{t}^{u}\geqslant\sqrt{2}\theta t\right)

\displaystyle\geq\int_{0}^{1}\left(1-e^{-\alpha\bar{u}\left(t-u_{0}t-s,\sqrt{2}\theta t-x_{0}t\right)}\right)\mathbb{P}\left(|{\mathcal{N}}_{u_{0}t+s}^{1}(x_{t})|\geq 1\right){\rm d}s.

We also notice that

\frac{\left(\sqrt{2}\theta-x_{0}\right)^{2}}{2\left(1-u_{0}\right)^{2}}>1,

since

\sigma^{2}<\frac{1}{\beta}<2-\beta,\quad\text{for}\quad\beta<1.

Then applying (
3
) again, we have

\displaystyle\liminf_{t\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{t}\ln\bar{u}\left(t-u_{0}t-s,\sqrt{2}\theta t-x_{0}t\right)\geq\left(1-\frac{\left(\sqrt{2}\theta-x_{0}\right)^{2}}{2\left(1-u_{0}\right)^{2}}\right)\left(1-u_{0}\right),

uniform for
s\in[0,1]
and

\lim_{t\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{t}\ln\mathbb{P}\left(|{\mathcal{N}}_{u_{0}t+s}^{1}(x_{t})|\geq 1\right)=\beta u_{0}-\frac{x_{0}^{2}}{2\sigma^{2}u_{0}},

uniform for
s\in[0,1]
. Then one has

(52)

\displaystyle\liminf_{t\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{t}\ln\mathbb{P}\left(\exists\,u\in{\mathcal{N}}_{t}^{2}:X_{t}^{u}\geqslant\sqrt{2}\theta t\right)

(53)

\displaystyle\geq\beta u_{0}-\frac{x_{0}^{2}}{2\sigma^{2}u_{0}}+\left(1-\frac{\left(\sqrt{2}\theta-x_{0}\right)^{2}}{2\left(1-u_{0}\right)^{2}}\right)\left(1-u_{0}\right)

(54)

\displaystyle=(\beta-1)u_{0}+1-\frac{\theta^{2}}{\left(\sigma^{2}-1\right)u_{0}+1}

(55)

\displaystyle=\frac{-2\sqrt{(1-\beta)\left(\sigma^{2}-1\right)}\theta-\beta+\sigma^{2}}{\sigma^{2}-1}.

This proves Theorem
1.2
for the above case.

5.
Proof of Theorem
1.3

In this section, we always assume that

(\beta,\sigma^{2})\in\mathcal{C}_{\text{III}}=\left\{\left(\beta,\sigma^{2}\right):\sigma^{2}+\beta>2\text{ and }\sigma^{2}<\frac{\beta}{2\beta-1}\right\}.

Recall
\xi=\frac{\sigma^{2}-\beta}{\sqrt{2(1-\sigma^{2})(\beta-1)}}.

The arguments are also exactly the same as those in Section
4
. One just replace
\theta
by
\frac{\xi\theta}{\sqrt{2}}
. We give only an outline here.

5.1.
Upper bound

Proof.

First, by union bound, one has

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left(M_{t}\geqslant\xi\theta t\right)

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle 2\max\left(e^{\beta t}\mathbb{P}\left(\sigma B_{t}\geqslant\xi\theta t\right),\alpha\int_{0}^{t}e^{\beta s+(t-s)}\mathbb{P}\left(\sigma B_{s}+B_{t}-B_{s}\geqslant\xi\theta t\right){\rm d}s\right)

\displaystyle=:

\displaystyle 2\max\left(\text{I},\,\text{II}\right).

According to (
5
), we have

\text{I}\leq O(1)\cdot\frac{1}{\sqrt{t}}\cdot e^{\beta t}\cdot e^{-\frac{\xi^{2}\theta^{2}t^{2}}{2\sigma^{2}t}}=\frac{O(1)}{\sqrt{t}}\cdot e^{\left(\beta-\frac{\xi^{2}\theta^{2}}{2\sigma^{2}}\right)t}

and

\text{II}\leq\alpha\int_{0}^{t}e^{\beta s+(t-s)}\cdot\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi\left(\sigma^{2}s+t-s\right)}}\cdot e^{-\frac{\xi^{2}\theta^{2}t^{2}}{2\left(\sigma^{2}s+t-s\right)}}{\rm d}s.\\

For
II
, set

(56)

f_{3}(u)=(\beta-1)u+1-\frac{\xi^{2}\theta^{2}}{2((\left.\sigma^{2}-1\right)u+1)},\quad u\in(0,1).

Then

f_{3}^{\prime}(u)=\beta-1+\frac{\xi^{2}\left(\sigma^{2}-1\right)\theta^{2}}{2\left[\left(\sigma^{2}-1\right)u+1\right]^{2}}.

and

f_{3}(u_{0})=\frac{\sqrt{2}(1-\beta)-\theta^{2}\left(\sigma^{2}-\beta\right)^{2}-1}{\sqrt{2}\left(\sigma^{2}-1\right)}+1.

Since
\left(\beta,\sigma^{2}\right)\in\mathcal{C}_{\text{III}}
, then
\beta>1,\sigma^{2}<1
and hence
u\mapsto f_{3}^{\prime}(u)
is decreasing. We have three cases.

If
\theta^{2}\geq\frac{2(\beta-1)}{1-\sigma^{2}}\frac{1}{\xi^{2}}
, then
f_{3}^{\prime}(0)\leq 0
. Thus
\max_{0\leq u\leq 1}f_{3}(u)=f_{3}(0)
and hence

(57)

\limsup_{t\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{t}\ln\mathbb{P}\left(M_{t}\geq\xi\theta t\right)\leq f_{3}(0)=1-\frac{\xi^{2}\theta^{2}}{2}.

If
\theta^{2}\leq\frac{2(\beta-1)}{1-\sigma^{2}}\frac{\sigma^{4}}{\xi^{2}}
, then
f_{3}^{\prime}(1)\geq 0
. Thus
\max_{0\leq u\leq 1}f_{3}(u)=f_{3}(1)
and hence

(58)

\limsup_{t\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{t}\ln\mathbb{P}\left(M_{t}\geq\xi\theta t\right)\leq f_{3}(1)=\beta-\frac{\xi^{2}\theta^{2}}{2\sigma^{2}}.

If
\frac{2(\beta-1)}{1-\sigma^{2}}\frac{\sigma^{4}}{\xi^{2}}<\theta^{2}<\frac{2(\beta-1)}{1-\sigma^{2}}\frac{1}{\xi^{2}}
, then
f_{3}^{\prime}(0)>0
and
f_{3}^{\prime}(1)<0
. Thus
\max_{0\leq u\leq 1}f_{3}(u)=f_{3}(u_{0})
with
f_{3}^{\prime}(u_{0})=0
yields
that

(59)

u_{0}=\frac{\xi\theta\sqrt{\frac{1-\sigma^{2}}{2(\beta-1)}}-1}{\sigma^{2}-1}

and

(60)

\limsup_{t\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{t}\ln\mathbb{P}\left(M_{t}\geq\xi\theta t\right)\leq f_{3}(u_{0})=\frac{\sqrt{2}(1-\beta)-\theta^{2}\left(\sigma^{2}-\beta\right)^{2}-1}{\sqrt{2}\left(\sigma^{2}-1\right)}+1.

∎

5.2.
Lower bound for Theorem
1.3

Notice that

(61)

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left(M_{t}\geqslant\xi\theta t\right)\geqslant\max\left\{\mathbb{P}\left(\exists\,u\in{\mathcal{N}}_{t}^{1}:X_{t}^{u}\geqslant\xi\theta t\right),\,\mathbb{P}\left(\exists\,u\in{\mathcal{N}}_{t}^{2}:X_{t}^{u}\geqslant\xi\theta t\right)\right\}.

First, from (
3
), we have

(62)

\displaystyle\lim_{t\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{t}\ln\mathbb{P}\left(\exists\,u\in{\mathcal{N}}_{t}^{1}:X_{t}^{u}\geqslant\xi\theta t\right)

\displaystyle=\lim_{t\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{t}\ln\mathbb{P}_{\sigma^{2},\beta}\left(\bar{M}_{t}\geqslant\xi\theta t\right)

(63)

\displaystyle=\beta-\frac{\xi^{2}\theta^{2}}{2\sigma^{2}}.

By similar reasoning as (
29
) and (
32
), we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left(\exists\,u\in{\mathcal{N}}_{t}^{2}:X_{t}^{u}\geqslant\xi\theta t\right)

\displaystyle\geq\int_{I_{t}}1-\mathbb{E}\left[\exp\left\{-\alpha\sum_{u\in{\mathcal{N}}_{s}^{1}(x_{t})}\bar{u}\left(t-s,\xi\theta t-x_{t}\right)\right\}\right]{\rm d}s,

where
I_{t}\subset[0,t]
is an interval of length
1
,
x_{t}\in{\mathbb{R}}
is a deterministic function of
t
.
Both
I_{t}
and
x_{t}
will be determined later from case to case. Recall from (
56
) that

f_{3}(u)=(\beta-1)u+1-\frac{\xi^{2}\theta^{2}}{2((\left.\sigma^{2}-1\right)u+1)},\quad u\in(0,1).

5.2.1.
The case of
\max_{0\leq u\leq 1}f_{3}(u)=f_{3}(0)
.

This corresponds to the cases that

\theta^{2}\geq\frac{2(\beta-1)}{1-\sigma^{2}}\frac{1}{\xi^{2}}
. Choose

I_{t}=[\varepsilon t,\varepsilon t+1],\quad x_{t}=-\varepsilon t,

for
\varepsilon>0
small enough. Then

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left(\exists\,u\in{\mathcal{N}}_{t}^{2}:X_{t}^{u}\geqslant\xi\theta t\right)

\displaystyle\geq\int_{0}^{1}\left(1-\exp\left\{-\alpha\bar{u}\left(t-\varepsilon t-s,\xi\theta t+\varepsilon t\right)\right\}\right)\mathbb{P}_{\sigma^{2},\beta}\left(\bar{M}_{\varepsilon t+s}\geq-\varepsilon t\right){\rm d}s.

Then applying (
3
), we have

\lim_{\varepsilon\rightarrow 0}\liminf_{t\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{t}\ln\bar{u}\left(t-s,\xi\theta t+\varepsilon t\right)\geq 1-\frac{\xi^{2}\theta^{2}}{2}.

We further notice that

\mathbb{P}_{\sigma^{2},\beta}\left(\bar{M}_{\varepsilon t+s}\geq-\varepsilon t\right)\rightarrow 1,\quad\text{as }t\rightarrow\infty.

Then applying Jensen’s inequality and Fatou’s lemma, one has

\displaystyle\liminf_{t\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{t}\ln\mathbb{P}\left(\exists\,u\in{\mathcal{N}}_{t}^{2}:X_{t}^{u}\geqslant\sqrt{2}\theta t\right)

\displaystyle\geq 1-\frac{\xi^{2}\theta^{2}}{2}.

This proves Theorem
1.3
for the cases
\theta^{2}\geq\frac{2(\beta-1)}{1-\sigma^{2}}\frac{1}{\xi^{2}}
.

5.2.2.
The case of
\max_{0\leq u\leq 1}f_{3}(u)=f_{3}(1)
.

This case corresponds to the case

\theta^{2}\leq\frac{2(\beta-1)}{1-\sigma^{2}}\frac{\sigma^{4}}{\xi^{2}}
. Similarly as before, (
61
) and (
62
) yield
Theorem
1.3
for this case.

5.2.3.
The case of
\max_{0\leq u\leq 1}f_{3}(u)=f_{3}(u_{0})
for
0<u_{0}<1
.

Notice that
f_{3}^{\prime}(u_{0})=0
. This corresponds to the case

\frac{2(\beta-1)}{1-\sigma^{2}}\frac{\sigma^{4}}{\xi^{2}}<\theta^{2}<\frac{2(\beta-1)}{1-\sigma^{2}}\frac{1}{\xi^{2}}
. Choose

x_{0}=\frac{\xi\theta u_{0}\sigma^{2}}{1-u_{0}+u_{0}\sigma^{2}},\quad x_{t}=x_{0}t.

Obviously
x_{0}<\xi\theta
, since
u_{0}>0.

Then

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left(\exists\,u\in{\mathcal{N}}_{t}^{2}:X_{t}^{u}\geqslant\xi\theta t\right)

\displaystyle\geq\int_{0}^{1}\left(1-e^{-\alpha\bar{u}\left(t-u_{0}t-s,\,\xi\theta t-x_{0}t\right)}\right)\mathbb{P}\left(|{\mathcal{N}}_{u_{0}t+s}^{1}(x_{t})|\geq 1\right){\rm d}s.

We also notice that

\frac{\left(\xi\theta-x_{0}\right)^{2}}{2\left(1-u_{0}\right)^{2}}>1.

Then applying (
3
) again, we have

\displaystyle\liminf_{t\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{t}\ln\bar{u}\left(t-u_{0}t-s,\xi\theta t-x_{0}t\right)\geq\left(1-\frac{\left(\xi\theta-x_{0}\right)^{2}}{2\left(1-u_{0}\right)^{2}}\right)\left(1-u_{0}\right),

uniform for
s\in[0,1]
and

\lim_{t\rightarrow\infty}\mathbb{P}\left(|{\mathcal{N}}_{u_{0}t+s}^{1}(x_{t})|\geq 1\right)=\beta u_{0}-\frac{x_{0}^{2}}{2\sigma^{2}u_{0}},

uniform for
s\in[0,1]
. Then one has

(64)

\displaystyle\liminf_{t\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{t}\ln\mathbb{P}\left(\exists\,u\in{\mathcal{N}}_{t}^{2}:X_{t}^{u}\geqslant\xi\theta t\right)=f_{3}(u_{0}).

This proves Theorem
1.3
for the above case. We are done.

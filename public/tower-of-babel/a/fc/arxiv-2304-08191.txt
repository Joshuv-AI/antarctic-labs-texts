A study on Prompt Design, Advantages and Limitations of ChatGPT for Deep Learning Program Repair Conference: arXiv; April 2023; Earth
Jialun Cao, Meiziniu Li, Ming Wen, Shing-Chi Cheung

Abstract
. ChatGPT has revolutionized many research and industrial fields. ChatGPT has shown great potential in software engineering to boost various traditional tasks such as program repair, code understanding, and code generation. However, whether automatic program repair (APR) applies to deep learning (DL) programs is still unknown. DL programs, whose decision logic is not explicitly encoded in the source code, have posed unique challenges to APR. While to repair DL programs, an APR approach needs to not only parse the source code syntactically but also needs to understand the code intention. With the best prior work, the performance of fault localization is still far less than satisfactory (only about 30%). Therefore, in this paper, we explore ChatGPT’s capability for DL program repair by asking three research questions. (1) Can ChatGPT debug DL programs effectively? (2) How can ChatGPT’s repair performance be improved by prompting? (3) In which way can dialogue help facilitate the repair? On top of that, we categorize the common aspects useful for prompt design for DL program repair. Also, we propose various prompt templates to facilitate the performance and summarize the advantages and disadvantages of ChatGPT’s abilities such as detecting bad code smell, code refactoring, and detecting API misuse/deprecation.



1.
Introduction

Large language models (LLMs) such as ChatGPT have been receiving increasing attention due to their remarkable effectiveness in performing a wide range of tasks.

Recent advances in large language models (LLMs) have triggered various studies
(
Fan
et al. 2023
;
Xia
et al. 2022
;
Zeng et al. 2022
)
examining the use of these models for software development tasks.
These studies analyze the capability of LLMs in program repair
(
Fan
et al. 2023
;
Xia
et al. 2022
)
, code understanding, and code generation
(
Zeng et al. 2022
)
. Their findings indicate that LLMs significantly outperform state-of-the-art techniques in program understanding
(
Zeng et al. 2022
)
and program repair
(
Xia
et al. 2022
;
Fan
et al. 2023
)
.
Furthermore, a recent study
(
Sobania
et al. 2023
)
evaluates the program repair performance of ChatGPT on a benchmark consisting of 40 textbook Python programs (
e.g.
, breadth-first-search, Hanoi, LCS-length).
The evaluation shows that ChatGPT achieves competitive performance
with state-of-the-art techniques. Furthermore, their preliminary attempt indicates that by taking advantage of prompting, the performance of ChatGPT can be further improved.

In this paper, we study the dual perspectives (i.e., pros and cons) in the use of LLMs to debug and repair deep learning (DL) programs. We base our study on ChatGPT because of its impact, performance, and availability among comparable LLMs.
In addition, we put our focus on repairing DL programs for two main reasons.
First, unlike conventional programs, a DL program manifests its functionality by guiding the training of the resulting deep neural network (DNN)
(
Cao
et al. 2022
)
. This means the decision logic is not directly encoded in the source code. Thus, to repair the DL program, ChatGPT needs not only to parse the source code syntactically but also to understand the intention of DL programs to repair or optimize the resulting DNNs.
Besides the differences in paradigms, our study differs from prior studies
(
Fan
et al. 2023
;
Xia
et al. 2022
;
Zeng et al. 2022
;
Sobania
et al. 2023
)
in the selection of program subjects with significantly more dependencies (6 times more libraries) and functionalities (more than the doubled number of lines). See Section
2.1
for more details.
Our study aims to answer three research questions.

RQ1. Can ChatGPT debug the DL program effectively?

This RQ is designed to understand ChatGPT’s performance in DL program debugging as compared with that of two state-of-the-art techniques. In particular, we split the debugging task into three steps,
i.e.
, fault detection, fault localization, and program repair. For evaluation, we quantify the number of faulty programs detected (fault detection ability), the number of faults localized (fault localization ability), and the number of faults repaired (program repair ability), respectively. As such, the pros and cons of ChatGPT could be better appreciated.

RQ2. How can ChatGPT’s repair performance be improved by prompting?

As pointed out by several recent studies
(
Nashid
et al. 2023
;
Sobania
et al. 2023
;
Liu et al. 2023
)
,

prompt engineering
can help LLMs provide better answers in various applications and research topics.
However,
what information should be included in the prompt
remains unexplored. To answer this question, we turn to real-world queries raised by developers by analyzing and categorizing their questions. On top of our quantitative analysis, we propose an enhanced prompt template. Finally, we utilize this enhanced prompt template on the same benchmark to reveal its effectiveness.

RQ3. In which way can dialogue help facilitate the repair?

Since ChatGPT is designed to interact with users, whether and how performing dialogue could help to facilitate the program repair is unknown. Thus, we explore whether the hints of fault locations and incorrect repair could further improve the performance.

On top of that, we summarize the pros and cons of ChatGPT’s ability during our study. To highlight the results, throughout the paper, we use three icons to annotate the pros (

), cons (

), and features (

) of utilizing ChatGPT in debugging DL programs.
For example, the deprecated API in DL libraries could affect the automatic DL program repair because ChatGPT fails to recognize the parameter names used in the old version. We also notice that, although the code refactoring could help simplify the code, some refactoring (
e.g.
, variable renaming) could lead to program crashes due to inconsistent variable names. In addition, with the help of dialogue feature of ChatGPT, the program repair ability could be improved, yet meanwhile, with the input sequence increases, catastrophic forgetting or misunderstanding may happen to impede further improvements.

The contributions of this work are summarized as follows:

•

We conduct a study of the DL program debugging (
i.e.
, fault detection, fault localization and program repair) ability of ChatGPT. The study complements prior works using buggy programs with more complicated functionalities and more dependencies.

•

We categorize the common aspects useful for prompt design and propose enhanced prompt templates that are more effective for DL program repair.

•

We summarize the characteristics of the programs repaired by ChatGPT. Our findings indicate that the program could be better repaired by providing more code intention. Also, ChatGPT is good at handling API misuse and deprecation, while it is short in default parameter identification and handling.

2.
Study Design

2.1.
Benchmark

To evaluate the automatic DL program repair ability of ChatGPT, we use a benchmark released by a recent study
(
Cao
et al. 2022
)
, which contains 58 buggy DL programs collected from StackOverflow and Github.
In particular,
each buggy DL program contains up to five types of faults, including inappropriate hyperparameter settings, incorrect data preprocessing, and incorrect model architectures.
In the evaluation, we use the buggy programs collected from StackOverflow, because the programs in GitHub exceed the maximum input tokens.

Recent studies
(
Hu et al. 2019
;
Sobania
et al. 2023
)
explore the program repair ability of ChatGPT on conventional programs using different benchmarks
(
Hu et al. 2019
;
Lin
et al. 2017
)
.
Table
1
lists the differences between these benchmarks.
The two benchmarks consist of Python/Java programs implementing fundamental functionalities such as sorting algorithms.
Each program’s average number of lines ranges from 13 to 22 and implements one functionality.
For comparison, in this paper, we use the benchmark
(
Cao
et al. 2022
)
in which every program contains about 46 lines on average, implementing several functionalities including data preprocessing, DL model construction, model training, and evaluation.
Furthermore, programs in the prior two benchmarks involve few dependencies, while the benchmark in this paper involves more than 6 dependencies for each program, including TensorFlow, Keras, and PyTorch.
We hope our study could complement prior work by exploring ChatGPT’s repair ability on more complicated programs and exploring the capabilities of handling dependency issues
(
e.g.
, software updates and dependency conflicts).

2.2.
Baselines

In the study, we consider two baselines: AutoTrainer
(
Zhang
et al. 2021
)
and DeepFD
(
Cao
et al. 2022
)
. AutoTrainer
(
Zhang
et al. 2021
)
identifies the training problems, such as dying reLU and oscillating loss during the training, and repairs the identified training problems using predefined repairing strategies. It iterates strategies until the training problem disappears or the maximum number of repairing is reached.
DeepFD
(
Cao
et al. 2022
)
monitors the changes in runtime features (e.g., loss, gradient, and accuracy) during training and classifies the training process into predefined types of faults. Since DeepFD does not support program repair, we compare only its fault detection and localization abilities.

2.3.
ChatGPT Setup

We build a pipeline for our experiments using the latest ChatGPT API based on the
gpt-3.5-turbo
model released by OpenAI.
Due to the non-deterministic nature of ChatGPT, for each query, we run five independent requests by starting a new conversation every time on the API. We record all prompts and their corresponding answers. We make them publicly available for review.

2.4.
Evaluation Metrics

We adopt the evaluation metrics following existing works
(
Zhang
et al. 2021
;
Wardat
et al. 2021
;
Cao
et al. 2022
;
Wardat
et al. 2022
)
.
In the fault detection evaluation, we consider an alarm raised by an approach correct if it is made on a buggy program, regardless of whether the root cause has been identified.
Due to the stochastic nature of ChatGPT, for each buggy program, we repeat the fault detection request five times, each with a new conversation.
If the majority (
\geq 3
) of replies correctly report fault existence, we denote the result as a
Yes
; otherwise,
No
.
A fault localization is considered correct if the fault reported is the same as the one marked
in the benchmark
(
Cao
et al. 2022
)
. Since there could be more than one fault in a buggy program, we record the number of faults reported by each approach. For ChatGPT, we put down the number of faults reported each round, and we use the best result among the five rounds for comparison.
In the repair evaluation, a repair
is considered correct if the concerned fault is repaired in the same way as that in the benchmark.
Note that we examine whether a repair is heading in the correct direction for the fault concerning incorrect training epochs.
For example, for a buggy program, the training epoch is repaired from 1 to 50 in the benchmark, while ChatGPT repairs it to 20.
Although the repaired values of epochs are different, we consider it a correct repair since the repairing strategies (
i.e.
, increasing the epoch) adopted are the same.

Finding 1
:
Using

DL program faults detected by ChatGPT broadly fall into three types: syntactic faults, functional faults, and code smell. In the following, we discuss how ChatGPT provides answers for these fault types.
(1)
Syntactic Faults
. ChatGPT usually
reports whether a program is syntactically correct at the beginning of the reply. A program that contains a syntactic fault (
e.g.
, undefined variables, missing importing dependencies) can prevent ChatGPT from detecting the other types of faults.
(2)
Functional Faults
. This type of fault usually causes the program fails to perform its intended functions correctly. To detect such faults, ChatGPT needs to understand the code intention, then detect and repair the fault
when the

implementation differs from the code intention
as understood by ChatGPT.
For example, if a DL program first processes the labels using
one-hot
encoding (
i.e.
, the pre-process indicates that it is a dataset for classification problem), then uses
mean_absolute_error
(which is inappropriate for the classification problem) to calculate the loss.
In such cases, there is a gap between the implementation and the functionality as ChatGPT understands, so it reports a functionality fault.
Another interesting finding is that ChatGPT has learned to detect missing or redundant implementation. For example, it suggests adding codes to visualize the training process or removing the redundant print statement.
(3)
Code Smell
. Code smells do not necessarily result in functional errors. They are more likely poor coding practices. ChatGPT is able to suggest possible ways to improve the program’s quality (
e.g.
, code refactoring) or performance (
e.g.
, replacing self-implemented methods with the available APIs).

Finding 2
:

Three types of faults that are observed could be detected by ChatGPT, including
syntactic faults
,
functional faults

4.
RQ2. Impact of Prompt

The experimental results of ChatGPT in RQ1 are less than satisfactory, we are then motivated to design a better prompt to improve the program repair performance utilizing the power of ChatGPT.

4.1.
Study of Real-world Questions

As revealed in RQ1, providing more information, such as code intention, is desired for prompt design.
Yet,
what information should be included in the prompt?

To better explore this question, we turn to the queries raised by real developers.
In particular, we scraped the texts (code blocks are excluded) of those cases from StackOverflow, and then carefully categorized the descriptions into seven types of information as follows via manual analysis.

Symptom
clarifies the user intention towards repairing. It allows ChatGPT to better understand the symptom that users want to repair (
e.g.
, syntax, dependency, or performance error). The common symptoms include abnormal outputs (
e.g.
, NaN, zero output), unsatisfactory results (
e.g.
, low accuracy, huge loss), over-fitting, or slow convergence.

Task
explains the design purpose of the code. Both loss functions
categorical_crossentropy
and
mean_absolute_

error
are syntactically correct in DL programs. While given the context that the program is designed for classification problems, the first loss function is more desirable than the latter one.
The common tasks include classification problems (
e.g.
, multi-label or binary classification) and regression problems.

Dataset
describes the characteristics of the input data (features and labels). Obtaining the dataset’s knowledge could help ChatGPT to gain a better understanding of the code intention. For example, if ChatGPT knows the input data are images, it has a higher chance of identifying the proper data preprocessing method, loss function, etc. In addition, input data range also affects the way of data preprocessing. For example, knowing the input values ranging from 0 to 255, it is more likely that ChatGPT could point out a normalization is needed before training. Generally, the description regarding the dataset includes the type of training data (
e.g.
, images), the distribution of data (
e.g.
, a sequence of sine waves), positive/negative values, the dimension of training data, and the number of labels. It is also the case where the dataset is well-known, such as MNIST and Iris. In that case, specifying the dataset name is informative and helpful.

Version/Dependency
specifies the version of libraries that are used. Since the libraries are evolving rapidly, specifying the version of libraries may help to reproduce the code correctly and to get the proper API recommendation. The versions of Python, the versions of DL libraries (
e.g.
, PyTorch, Tensorflow), and the version of the backends are commonly seen in StackOverflow.

Attempted fixes
are those fixes that have been tried by Developers but failed to achieve the desired goals. The attempted fixes help to avoid ineffective repair strategies.

Hyperparameter
and
Model Architecture
provide a brief introduction to the network and the training strategies. While this information could be easily identified in code, cases regarding such information are rare.

We visualize the statistics of each type of information in Figure
1
. Clear that the description of
symptom
is observed in almost all cases (33/34). Followed by the
task
(26/34) and
dataset
(24/34) descriptions.
The remaining information is observed occasionally.

Finding 6
:

There are mainly seven types of query information that are commonly seen in real-world forums. Among them, the description regarding
symptom
,
task
, and
dataset
are the most commonly observed ones.

7.2.
Failing-Test-Guided Prompt

A recent study
(
Nashid
et al. 2023
)
utilizes the failing test as hints to improve the repair ability. However, this study only focuses on conventional programs while the decision logic of DL programs is not directly encoded in the source code. We are thus motivated to explore whether failing tests could also help DL program repair.
We take an instance from StackOverflow
(
to use keras for
XOR 2023
)

as an example. This case contains four faults in the program, while using all the above prompts (PT-B, PT-E), there are at most 1 fault that can be correctly repaired. After providing the hint of fault locations, there is still one fault unrepaired. We then try whether the information of failing tests could complete the repair.

In particular, this case trains a DNN to emulate the XOR problem,
we first train the repaired program returned by ChatGPT. With the hints of fault locations, ChatGPT successfully repaired three faults. We execute the program and test the trained DNN against the test set. A failing test appears,
i.e.
, given the input
[1,1]
, the DNN outputs
[9.9999845e-01]
(
\approx

[1]
), while the expected output is
[0]
. We then integrate such failing information to ChatGPT by saying “
The repair is wrong. For the input
[1, 1]
, the output should be
[0]
instead of
[1]
. Please repair it again.
” ChatGPT apologizes and then adds a rounding step before the output. We continue asking it to output a number that is
close
to
[1]
, but ChatGPT still cannot repair the fault. It indicates that for DL program repair, providing the failing test cases may not be helpful.

Finding 12:

Providing
an individual failing test to ChatGPT may not help DL program repair
because the DL programs do not explicitly write the decision logic of the resulting deep neural networks.
Besides, ChatGPT is not able to execute the program at the current stage, even though a patch is provided by ChatGPT, a validation step is still needed.

8.2.
Deep Learning Program Repair

To debug and repair DL programs, various approaches have been proposed.
AutoTrainer
(
Zhang
et al. 2021
)
proposed an automatic approach to detect and fix the training problems in DNN programs at runtime. It particularly focuses on detecting and repairing five common training problems: gradient vanish, gradient explode, dying ReLU, oscillating loss, and slow convergence. It encapsulates and automates the detecting and repairing process by dynamic monitoring. Amazon SageMaker Debugger
(
Rauschmayr et al. 2021
)
and UMLAUT
(
Schoop
et al. 2021
)
provide a set of built-in heuristics to debug faults in DNN models during training.
DeepLocalize
(
Wardat
et al. 2021
)
, the first fault localization approach for DNN models (such as incorrect learning rate or inappropriate loss function). It locates in layers where the symptom happens instead of where the fault’s root cause resides. DeepFD
(
Cao
et al. 2022
)
and DeepDiagnosis
(
Wardat
et al. 2022
)
diagnose and localize faults in DL programs. DeepDiagnosis also suggests actionable advice on how to repair.

9.
Threats of Validity

To avoid the difference between the ChatGPT website interface and the latest API, we repeat the experiment of RQ1 using the interface (Mar 14, 2023 Version).
The experiment results only show a slight difference. The evaluation results are released for the reference. Besides, ChatGPT’s replies are carried out manually, which may be subjective and misunderstandings. To alleviate this threat, the cases used in the paper come along with StackOverflow post IDs. Also, all the prompts and replies could be traced in our released repository.
Finally, since ChatGPT is trained on a large corpus of text data from the Internet (
e.g.
, books, articles, open-source code repositories), it is possible that there is a data leakage problem,
i.e.
, the cases in the benchmark were included in its training set, so the output is generated due to memorization.
However, we observe that ChatGPT does not perform well with various prompt templates. It indicates it has not memorized the cases in the benchmark. This observation may partially alleviate the concern regarding such threat.

Conclusion

In this paper, we study ChatGPT’s ability in DL program debugging (
i.e.
, fault detection, fault localization, and program repair). In particular, we explore the impact of prompts on the debugging performance and propose a prompt template to improve the performance. In addition, we explored how far ChatGPT’s dialogue feature can push the performance forward. On top of the experiment, we summarize the pros and cons of ChatGPT’s reply and point out the possible directions where ChatGPT could help to facilitate the SE community.

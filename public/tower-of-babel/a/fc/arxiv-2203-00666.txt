Temporal Increments of the KPZ equation with general initial data
Sayan Das

Abstract
. We consider the Cole-Hopf solution of the (1+1)-dimensional KPZ equation ℋ f ​ ( t , x ) \mathcal{H}^{f}(t,x) started with initial data f f . In this article, we study the sample path properties of the KPZ temporal process ℋ t f := ℋ f ​ ( t , 0 ) \mathcal{H}_{t}^{f}:=\mathcal{H}^{f}(t,0) . 1.
Introduction

In this article we study the local sample path properties of the
KPZ
(
KPZ
) equation. KPZ equation is a stochastic partial differential equation formally given by

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\mathcal{H}=\tfrac{1}{2}\partial_{xx}\mathcal{H}+\tfrac{1}{2}(\partial_{x}\mathcal{H})^{2}+\xi,\qquad\mathcal{H}=\mathcal{H}(t,x),\quad(t,x)\in[0,\infty)\times\mathbb{R}

where
\xi=\xi(t,x)
is the Gaussian space-time white noise. Introduced in
[
43
]
, the KPZ equation is a popular model for interfaces of random growth. However, the equation is ill-posed due to the presence of the non-linear term
(\partial_{x}\mathcal{H})^{2}
. To make sense of the equation one considers
\mathcal{Z}:=e^{\mathcal{H}}
which formally solves the
SHE
(
SHE
) with multiplicative noise:

(1.1)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\mathcal{Z}=\tfrac{1}{2}\partial_{xx}\mathcal{Z}+\xi\mathcal{Z},\qquad\mathcal{Z}=\mathcal{Z}(t,x),\quad(t,x)\in[0,\infty)\times\mathbb{R}.

The
SHE
is known to be well-posed and has a well-developed solution theory based on Itô integral and chaos expansion
[
53
,
2
,
50
,
16
]
. In particular, (
1.1
) with the initial data
\mathcal{Z}(0,x)=g(x)
can be rewritten in Duhamel form as

(1.2)

\displaystyle\mathcal{Z}(t,x)=\int_{\mathbb{R}}p_{t}(x-y)g(y)\,\mathrm{d}y+\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}}p_{t-s}(x-y)\mathcal{Z}(s,y)\xi(\mathrm{d}s,\mathrm{d}y).

Here
p_{t}(x):=(2\pi t)^{-1/2}\exp(-x^{2}/(2t))
is the standard heat kernel. There exists a unique mild solution for the above equation with the initial data
\mathcal{Z}(0,x)=g(x)
independent of the noise
\xi
and satisfying

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}\left[\sup_{A\subset[-n,n]}\int_{A}g(x)dx\right]\leq\mathrm{C}e^{\mathrm{C}n}}

for some constant
C>0
and for all
n\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
.

For a function-valued initial data
\mathcal{Z}(0,x)\geq 0
that is not identically zero, it is known

\mathcal{Z}(t,x)>0
for all
t>0
and
x\in\mathbb{R}
almost surely
[
46
]
. Thus
\mathcal{H}:=\log\mathcal{Z}
is well defined and is termed as the Cole-Hopf solution of the KPZ equation. This notion of solution coincides with other notions of solutions, such as regularity structures
[
35
,
36
]
, paracontrolled distributions
[
27
,
32
]
, and energy solutions
[
28
,
33
]
, under suitable assumptions.

An often considered initial data is to start the
SHE
from a Dirac delta at the origin, i.e.,

(1.3)

\displaystyle\mathcal{Z}^{\mathbf{nw}}(0,x):=\delta_{0}(x)

which is referred to as
the narrow wedge initial data for the KPZ equation
\mathcal{H}^{\mathbf{nw}}
. For such an initial data,
[
24
]
established the positivity for
\mathcal{Z}^{\mathbf{nw}}(t,x)
so that the
Cole-Hopf solution
\mathcal{H}^{\mathbf{nw}}:=\log\mathcal{Z}^{\mathbf{nw}}
is well-defined.

In this paper, we consider a general class of initial data for the KPZ equation which we now describe.

Definition 1.1
.

Fix
\mathfrak{p}=(\theta,\delta,\lambda,\kappa,M)\in(0,\infty)^{5}
. The
\mathbf{Hyp}_{1}(\mathfrak{p})
class is a class of possible initial data for the KPZ equation consisting of:

(1)

Narrow wedge initial data corresponding to delta initial data (
1.3
) for
SHE
.

(2)

Brownian initial data: The two sided Brownian motion
B(x)
independent from the space-time white noise
\xi
.

(3)

Measurable deterministic functions
f:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}\cup\{-\infty\}
satisfying the following two conditions:

(a)

f(x)\leq\lambda(1+|x|)^{2-\delta}
,

(b)

there exist a subinterval
\mathcal{I}\subset[-M,M]
with
|\mathcal{I}|=\theta
such that
f(y)\geq-\kappa
for all
y\in\mathcal{I}
.

We consider the
SHE

\mathcal{Z}^{f}(t,x)
(
1.1
) started from initial data
e^{f}
where
f
belong to the above class and define the KPZ equation with initial data
f
via the Cole-Hopf transform
\mathcal{H}^{f}(t,x):=\log\mathcal{Z}^{f}(t,x)
. We consider the
KPZ temporal process

\mathcal{H}^{f}(t,0)
as a function of time with spatial point fixed at
0
. We use the abbreviated notation
\mathcal{Z}_{t}^{f}(x):=\mathcal{Z}^{f}(t,x)
,
\mathcal{Z}_{t}^{f}:=\mathcal{Z}_{t}^{f}(0)
,
\mathcal{H}_{t}^{f}(x):=\mathcal{H}^{f}(t,x)
, and
\mathcal{H}_{t}^{f}:=\mathcal{H}_{t}^{f}(0)
. Our main result, which is stated below, shows that the KPZ temporal process
\mathcal{H}_{t}^{f}
can be approximated by a fractional Brownian motion at a local level uniformly over compact sets of
(0,\infty)
.

Theorem 1.2
.

Fix
\mathfrak{p}\in(0,\infty)^{5}
,
k\geq 2
,
T>1
, and
\beta\in(0,\frac{2}{5})
. Consider any
f
from the
\mathbf{Hyp}_{1}(\mathfrak{p})
class defined in Definition
1.1
. One can construct a probability space consisting of the KPZ temporal process
\mathcal{H}_{t}^{f}
started with
f
, a fractional Brownian motion
\mathfrak{B}_{t}
with Hurst parameter
\frac{1}{4}
(defined in Definition
3.1
), such that the process:
\{\mathcal{H}_{t}^{f}-(2/\pi)^{\frac{1}{4}}\mathfrak{B}_{t}:t\in[T^{-1},T]\}
is locally Hölder continuous with exponent
\beta
and furthermore

\mathbb{E}\left[\big[\mathcal{H}_{t}^{f}-(2/\pi)^{\frac{1}{4}}\mathfrak{B}_{t}\big]_{C^{0,\beta}[T^{-1},T]}^{k}\right]<\infty.

Here
\big[f\big]_{C^{0,\beta}[a,b]}
denotes the Hölder coefficient of
f
which is defined as

\displaystyle\big[f\big]_{C^{0,\beta}[a,b]}:=\sup_{x\neq y\in[a,b]}\frac{|f(x)-f(y)|}{|x-y|^{\beta}}.

Remark 1.3
.

In plain words, Theorem
1.2
shows that one can put
\mathcal{H}_{t}^{f}
,
\mathfrak{B}_{t}
in a common probability space such that
{\mathcal{H}_{t}^{f}-(2/\pi)^{\frac{1}{4}}\mathfrak{B}_{t}}
on any compact interval of
(0,\infty)
is Hölder continuous with Hölder exponent less than
\frac{2}{5}
. Since
\mathfrak{B}_{t}
is itself a Hölder continuous process with Hölder exponent less than
\frac{1}{4}
, Theorem
1.2
entails that
\mathcal{H}_{t}^{f}
is almost surely Hölder continuous with Hölder exponent being less than
\frac{1}{4}
as well. This recovers the result from
[
2
, Theorem 2.2]
(see also
[
53
]
) which shows that the solution to the
SHE
(for a wide class of functional initial data including delta initial data) as a process in time is almost surely Hölder continuous with Hölder exponents less than
\frac{1}{4}
.

The sharp comparison in Theorem
1.2
allows us to obtain several sample path properties of the KPZ temporal process
\mathcal{H}_{t}^{f}
as a consequence. To state the results, we recall the notion of
\alpha
-variation of a function. Given a function
g:[s,t]\to\mathbb{R}
and
\alpha>0
, define the
\alpha
-variation of
g
at scale
\varepsilon
by

\displaystyle\mathcal{V}_{\alpha,\varepsilon}(g):=\sum_{u\in[s+\varepsilon,t]\cap\varepsilon\mathbb{Z}}|g(u)-g(u-\varepsilon)|^{\alpha}.

With this definition in place, we are now ready to state the theorem regarding the temporal increments of the KPZ equation for initial data drawn from
\mathbf{Hyp}_{1}(\mathfrak{p})
.

Theorem 1.4
.

Fix
\mathfrak{p}\in(0,\infty)^{5}
and consider any
f
from the
\mathbf{Hyp}_{1}(\mathfrak{p})
class defined in Definition
1.1
. Consider the KPZ temporal process
\mathcal{H}_{t}^{f}
started from
f
. The following holds:

(1)

Temporal increments of
\mathcal{H}^{f}
are asymptotically normal. For
0<t_{1}<t_{2}<\cdots<t_{k}<\infty
, as
\varepsilon\downarrow 0
,

\displaystyle\big((\pi/2)^{\frac{1}{4}}\varepsilon^{-\frac{1}{4}}[\mathcal{H}_{t_{i}+\varepsilon}^{f}-\mathcal{H}_{t_{i}}^{f}]\big)_{i=1}^{k}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle d}}{{\to}}\mathcal{N}_{k}(\mathbf{0},\mathbf{I}_{k}).

where
\mathcal{N}_{k}(\mathbf{0},\mathbf{I}_{k})
is a standard Gaussian
k
-vector with mean
\mathbf{0}
and variance-covariance matrix
\mathbf{I}_{k}
.

(2)

\mathcal{H}^{f}
has quartic variation in the sense that for any
[s,t]\subset(0,\infty)
, we have

\displaystyle\mathcal{V}_{4,\varepsilon}(\mathcal{H}^{f}\mid_{[s,t]})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle p}}{{\to}}\tfrac{6}{\pi}(t-s).

(3)

For any
t>0
with probability
1
we have

\displaystyle\limsup_{\varepsilon\downarrow 0}\frac{\mathcal{H}_{t+\varepsilon}^{f}-\mathcal{H}_{t}^{f}}{\varepsilon^{1/4}\sqrt{\log\log(1/\varepsilon)}}=\left(\frac{8}{\pi}\right)^{1/4}.

(4)

With probability
1
we have

\displaystyle\limsup_{\varepsilon\downarrow 0}\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{4}}\sqrt{\log(1/\varepsilon)}}\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq s<t\leq 2\\
|t-s|\leq\varepsilon\end{subarray}}|\mathcal{H}_{t}^{f}-\mathcal{H}_{s}^{f}|=\left(\frac{8}{\pi}\right)^{1/4}.

(5)

Set

\displaystyle E(\alpha):=\left\{t\in[1,2]:\limsup_{\varepsilon\downarrow 0}\frac{|\mathcal{H}_{t+\varepsilon}^{f}-\mathcal{H}_{t}^{f}|}{\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{4}}\sqrt{\log(1/\varepsilon)}}\geq\alpha\left(\frac{8}{\pi}\right)^{\frac{1}{4}}\right\}.

We have
\dim_{H}E(\alpha)=1-\alpha^{2}
almost surely where
\dim_{H}
denotes the Hausdorff dimension.

1.1.
Proof Idea

In this section, we illustrate the main ideas that go behind the proof of Theorem
1.2
. The proof strategy is to first establish a similar temporal approximation at the level of the Stochastic Heat Equation and then update it to the KPZ equation level using tail estimates for the KPZ equation. Our approximation for the
SHE
holds for a wider class of initial data, namely the
\mathbf{Hyp}_{2}(\mathfrak{q})
class (see Definition
2.1
for details).

Let us consider
SHE

\mathcal{Z}^{f}(t,x)
started from initial data
f
drawn from
\mathbf{Hyp}_{2}(\mathfrak{q})
class (see Definition
2.1
). We first study the temporal differences of the
SHE
. Using (
1.2
) for each
t,\varepsilon>0
, one has

(1.4)

\displaystyle\mathcal{Z}^{f}(t+\varepsilon,0)-\mathcal{Z}^{f}(t,0)

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}}[p_{t+\varepsilon}(y)-p_{t}(y)]e^{f(y)}\mathrm{d}y

\displaystyle+\int_{t}^{t+\varepsilon}\int_{\mathbb{R}}p_{t+\varepsilon-s}(y)\mathcal{Z}^{f}(s,y)\xi(\mathrm{d}s,\mathrm{d}y)

\displaystyle+\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}}[p_{t+\varepsilon-s}(y)-p_{t-s}(y)]\mathcal{Z}^{f}(s,y)\xi(\mathrm{d}s,\mathrm{d}y).

Assuming
\varepsilon
is small enough, we seek to find a good approximation for the r.h.s. of above equation. We now give a heuristic idea of how to arrive at a good approximation. We remark that the following approach was outlined in
[
44
]
.

For the class of initial data considered here, the first term on the r.h.s. of (
1.4
) can be shown to be of the order
O(\varepsilon)
which turns out to be much more regular than the other two terms. Thus this term can be ignored.

Let us consider the third term in r.h.s. of (
1.4
). Note that the heat kernel
p_{t}(x)
is singular near
t=0
and for small enough
t
,
p_{t}(y)\mathrm{d}y\approx\delta_{0}(\mathrm{d}y)
. As long as
\mathcal{Z}^{f}(s,y)
is nice enough, one expects

\int_{0}^{t^{\prime}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}[p_{t+\varepsilon-s}(y)-p_{t-s}(y)]\mathcal{Z}^{f}(s,y)\xi(\mathrm{d}s,\mathrm{d}y)

is regular enough for
t^{\prime}<t
. Hence the main contribution in the third term on r.h.s. of (
1.4
) comes when
s
is close to
t
. For
s
close to
t
, the essential contribution of
[p_{t+\varepsilon-s}(y)-p_{t-s}(y)]
comes from
y=0
. Similarly in the second term, as
s
is already close to
t
, again the contribution of
p_{t+\varepsilon-s}(y)
comes when
y
is close to zero. Thus naively we may replace
\mathcal{Z}^{f}(s,y)
by
\mathcal{Z}^{f}(t,0)
in the second and third terms. We then expect the quantity on the r.h.s. of (
1.4
) is close to

\displaystyle\mathcal{Z}^{f}(t,0)\cdot\left[\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}}[p_{t+\varepsilon-s}(y)-p_{t-s}(y)]\xi(\mathrm{d}s,\mathrm{d}y)+\int_{t}^{t+\varepsilon}\int_{\mathbb{R}}p_{t+\varepsilon-s}(y)\xi(\mathrm{d}s,\mathrm{d}y)\right],

which is same as

\displaystyle\mathcal{Z}^{f}(t,0)\cdot\left[\mathcal{V}_{t+\varepsilon}(0)-\mathcal{V}_{t}(0)\right],

where

\displaystyle\mathcal{V}_{t}(x):=\int_{(0,t)\times\mathbb{R}}p_{t-s}(y-x)\xi(\mathrm{d}s,\mathrm{d}y)

with the same underlying space-time white noise
\xi(t,x)
.
\mathcal{V}_{t}(x)
solves the linear stochastic heat equation
\partial_{t}\mathcal{V}=\frac{1}{2}\partial_{xx}\mathcal{V}+\xi
with
\mathcal{V}_{0}(x)\equiv 0
. By Proposition 2.2 in
[
44
]
(
\alpha=2
in their statement), one can find a fractional Brownian motion
\mathfrak{B}_{t}
with Hurst parameter
\frac{1}{4}
and another process
R_{t}
on the same probability space as
\mathcal{V}_{t}
such that

\mathcal{V}_{t}=(2/\pi)^{\frac{1}{4}}\mathfrak{B}_{t}+R_{t}.

Finally, the process
R_{t}
is known to be much more regular and can be ignored. Thus the above approach leads to the following approximation theorem.

Theorem 1.5
.

Fix
\mathfrak{q}=(\mathfrak{q}_{k})_{k=1}^{\infty}\in((0,\infty)\times(0,1])^{\mathbb{N}}
and fix any
f\in\mathbf{Hyp}_{2}(\mathfrak{q})
defined in Definition
2.1
. Consider the
SHE
defined in (
1.1
) with initial data
e^{f}
. Fix
T>1
and
k\geq 2
and
\beta\in(0,\frac{2}{5})
. There exists a fractional Brownian motion
\mathfrak{B}_{t}
with Hurst parameter
\frac{1}{4}
on the same probability space and constants
\mathrm{C}=\mathrm{C}(\beta,\mathfrak{q}_{k},k,T)>0
and
\varepsilon_{0}=\varepsilon_{0}(\beta,\mathfrak{q}_{k},k,T)>0
such that for all
\varepsilon\in(0,\varepsilon_{0})
we have

(1.5)

\displaystyle\sup_{t\in[T^{-1},T]}\mathbb{E}\left[|\mathcal{Z}_{t+\varepsilon}^{f}-\mathcal{Z}_{t}^{f}-(2/\pi)^{1/4}\mathcal{Z}_{t}^{f}(\mathfrak{B}_{t+\varepsilon}-\mathfrak{B}_{t})|^{k}\right]\leq\mathrm{C}\varepsilon^{k\beta}.

We mention that this result appears in
[
44
]
for flat initial data
\mathcal{Z}(0,x)\equiv\mbox{Const}
. Once Theorem
1.5
is established, a similar approximation theorem for the KPZ equation is established using sharp tail estimates for the KPZ equation that holds for general initial data. These sharp tail estimates are proven in
[
7
]
and holds for all initial data in
\mathbf{Hyp}_{2}(\mathfrak{q})
class.

To make the above argument rigorous, one requires uniform moment estimates as well as spatial and temporal regularity moment estimates for the SHE. Such estimates are well known for flat initial data (Lemma 3.1 in
[
44
]
). One of the key technical achievements of this paper is to extend these moment estimates to general initial data. Given any
T>1
, we are interested in estimating moments of three types:

•

\mathbb{E}[|\mathcal{Z}_{t}^{f}(y)|^{k}]
for all
t\in(0,T]
and
y\in\mathbb{R}.

•

\mathbb{E}[|\mathcal{Z}_{t}^{f}(y)-\mathcal{Z}_{t}^{f}(0)|^{k}]
for all
t\in[T^{-1},T]
and
|y|
small enough.

•

\mathbb{E}[|\mathcal{Z}_{s}^{f}(0)-\mathcal{Z}_{t}^{f}(0)|^{k}]
for all
s,t\in[T^{-1},T]
.

For the first point, we show that the logarithm of the moments is of the form
(1+|y|)^{2-\delta}
. For the next two points, we produce regularity estimates. We show that the expressions in the second and the third points above are bounded by
|y|^{k\theta}
(for any
\theta\in(0,\frac{1}{2})
) and
|s-t|^{k/4}
respectively.

We first prove the uniform moment estimate and spatial regularity estimate for narrow wedge data using the moment estimates and tail estimates from
[
7
]
and
[
8
]
respectively. We then upgrade it to general initial data via the
convolution formula
that relates the spatial process of
\mathcal{Z}^{f}(t,\cdot)
with that of
\mathcal{Z}^{\mathbf{nw},y}(t,\cdot)
started with initial data
\delta_{y}(x)
.

Proposition 1.6

(Convolution formula)
.

For any functional initial data in
\mathbf{Hyp}_{2}(\mathfrak{q})
, one has

\mathcal{Z}_{t}^{f}(x)=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\mathcal{Z}_{t}^{\mathbf{nw},y}(x)e^{f(y)}\mathrm{d}y,

where
\mathcal{Z}_{t}^{\mathbf{nw},y}(x)
denotes the
SHE
started with initial
\delta_{y}(x)
. Furthermore for each fixed
y
and
t>0
,

(1.6)

\displaystyle\mathcal{Z}_{t}^{\mathbf{nw},y}(x)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle d}}{{=}}\mathcal{Z}_{t}^{\mathbf{nw}}(x-y)

as processes in
x
.

The proof of the above proposition is immediate from the chaos expansion for the
SHE
and hence skipped. Owing to Proposition
1.6
, we see that the spatial process
\mathcal{Z}_{t}^{f}(\cdot)
is equal in distribution to
\int_{\mathbb{R}}\mathcal{Z}_{t}^{\mathbf{nw}}(\cdot-y)e^{f(y)}\mathrm{d}y
. The latter quantity is a function of
\mathcal{Z}_{t}^{\mathbf{nw}}
and the initial data
f
only. Thus, inserting spatial regularity estimates for
\mathcal{Z}_{t}^{\mathbf{nw}}
in the second term leads to spatial regularity estimates for
\mathcal{Z}_{t}^{f}
(see Section
2.2
for details).

Although the temporal difference
\mathcal{Z}_{s}^{f}-\mathcal{Z}_{t}^{f}
can be written as

\int_{\mathbb{R}}(\mathcal{Z}_{s}^{\mathbf{nw},y}(0)-\mathcal{Z}_{t}^{\mathbf{nw},y}(0))e^{f(y)}\mathrm{d}y,

the quantity
\mathcal{Z}_{s}^{\mathbf{nw},y}(0)-\mathcal{Z}_{t}^{\mathbf{nw},y}(0)
can not be viewed as a function of a
single
narrow wedge solution of SHE (as (
1.6
) holds for each fixed
t>0
only). Thus the convolution formula alone is not very helpful in obtaining temporal regularity estimates. To derive the temporal regularity estimate, we rely on the chaos expansion instead and use the uniform moment estimates for general initial data.

Our main results about the KPZ temporal process fit into the broader endeavor of understanding the local structure and multifractal properties of stochastic systems. Studying fractal properties of stochastic partial differential equations (SPDEs) is an active area of research for the last five-six decades. The main focus of a vast majority of such works resided in the study of the large peaks of the SPDEs with multiplicative noise

Local structure of the

[
44

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\mathcal{Z}=\tfrac{1}{2}\Delta_{\alpha/2}\mathcal{Z}+\sigma(\mathcal{Z})\xi,\qquad\mathcal{Z}(0,x)\equiv 1,

It was shown that locally

looks like a fixed multiple of fractional Brownian motion of Hurst parameter

2.
Moment estimates for Stochastic Heat Equation

Before commencing with the analysis, let us mention a few pieces of notation that we will use throughout this paper. Given a function (possibly random)
f:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}\cup\{-\infty\}
, we use
\mathcal{Z}^{f}(t,x)
or
\mathcal{Z}_{t}^{f}(x)
to denote the solution to the
SHE
started from the initial data
e^{f}
. For narrow wedge initial data (i.e.,
\delta_{0}(x)
initial data for the
SHE
), we denote it as
\mathcal{Z}^{\mathbf{nw}}(t,x)
or
\mathcal{Z}^{\mathbf{nw}}_{t}(x)
. When
x=0
, we will often use the shorthand notations
\mathcal{Z}_{t}^{f}:=\mathcal{Z}_{t}^{f}(0)
and
\mathcal{Z}_{t}^{\mathbf{nw}}:=\mathcal{Z}_{t}^{\mathbf{nw}}(0)
. We use analogous notations for the KPZ equation as well. We use
\mathrm{C}=\mathrm{C}(x,y,z,\ldots)>0
to denote a generic deterministic positive finite
constant that may change from line to line, but dependent on the designated variables
x,y,z,\ldots
.

In this section, we prove moment estimates for the
SHE
(
1.1
), for a wide class of initial data which we now introduce.

Definition 2.1
.

Fix any
\mathfrak{q}=(\mathfrak{q}_{k})_{k=1}^{\infty}=(\lambda_{k},\delta_{k})_{k=1}^{\infty}\in((0,\infty)\times(0,1])^{\mathbb{N}}
. The
\mathbf{Hyp}_{2}(\mathfrak{q})
class is a class of possible initial data for the KPZ equation consisting of:

(1)

Narrow wedge initial data corresponding to delta initial data (
1.3
) for
SHE
.

(2)

Consider any measurable (possibly random) function
f:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}\cup\{-\infty\}
. For each
k\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
, we set

(2.1)

\displaystyle{M_{k}^{f}(x):=\mathbb{E}[e^{kf(x)}]},

with the convention that
\mathbb{E}[e^{kf(x)}]=e^{kf(x)}
when
f
is non-random.
f
belongs to the class
\mathbf{Hyp}_{2}(\mathfrak{q})
if for each
k\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
and for all
x\in\mathbb{R}
we have

(2.2)

\displaystyle\log M_{k}^{f}(x)\leq\lambda_{k}(1+|x|^{2-\delta_{k}}).

This class of initial data is much larger than the
\mathbf{Hyp}_{1}(\mathfrak{p})
class introduced in Definition
1.1
. We record this fact in the following lemma.

Lemma 2.2
.

Given
\mathfrak{p}\in(0,\infty)^{5}
, there exists
\mathfrak{q}\in((0,\infty)\times(0,1])^{\mathbb{N}}
such that

\mathbf{Hyp}_{1}(\mathfrak{p})\subset\mathbf{Hyp}_{2}(\mathfrak{q}).

Proof.

Fix any
\mathfrak{p}=(\theta,\delta,\lambda,\kappa,M)\in(0,\infty)^{5}
. We set
\lambda_{k}=\max\{2k\lambda,\frac{k^{2}}{2}\}
,
\delta_{k}=\min(1,\delta)
. Consider
\mathfrak{q}:=(\lambda_{k},\delta_{k})_{k=1}^{\infty}\in((0,\infty)\times(0,1])^{\mathbb{N}}
. Clearly for any deterministic function
f\in\mathbf{Hyp}_{1}(\mathfrak{p})
, following the condition (3) in Definition
1.1
and (
2.1
), we have
\log M_{k}^{f}(x)=kf(x)\leq k\lambda(1+|x|)^{2-\delta}
. We have

(1+|x|)^{2-\delta}\leq(1+|x|)^{\max(1,2-\delta)}\leq 2\cdot[1+|x|^{2-\delta_{k}}].

Thus
f\in\mathbf{Hyp}_{2}(\mathfrak{q})
. For Brownian initial data as
\mathbb{E}[e^{kB(x)}]=e^{k^{2}|x|/2}
, we have
{\log}M_{k}^{f}(x)\leq\frac{1}{2}k^{2}|x|\leq\lambda_{k}(1+|x|^{2-\delta_{k}})
. Hence
\mathbf{Hyp}_{1}(\mathfrak{p})\subset\mathbf{Hyp}_{2}(\mathfrak{q})
.
∎

The rest of this section is devoted to proving several kinds of moment estimates for the
SHE
. In Section
2.1
, we establish uniform moment estimates in Proposition
2.3
. In Section
2.2
and Section
2.3
, we produce spatial and temporal regularity estimates in Proposition
2.4
and Proposition
2.6
respectively. For Proposition
2.3
and Proposition
2.4
, we first prove our estimates for the narrow wedge case in Propositions
-nw
and
-nw
respectively. Then we upgrade it to general initial data by convolution formula (Proposition
1.6
).

2.1.
Uniform moment estimates

In this subsection we provide growth estimates for the moments of the
SHE
started from
f\in\mathbf{Hyp}_{2}(\mathfrak{q})
.

Proposition 2.3
.

Fix
\mathfrak{q}\in((0,\infty)\times(0,1])^{\mathbb{N}}
,
T>0
and
k\geq 2
. There exists a constant
\Delta=\Delta(\mathfrak{q}_{k},k,T)>0
such that for all
s\in[0,T]
, for all functional
f\in\mathbf{Hyp}_{2}(\mathfrak{q})
and
y\in\mathbb{R}
we have

(2.3)

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[[\mathcal{Z}^{f}_{s}(y)]^{k}\right]\leq\Delta e^{\Delta|y|^{2-\delta_{k}}}.

Note that the above proposition does not cover the narrow wedge initial data case. For that initial data we have the following moment estimate.

Proposition
2.3
-nw
.

Fix
T>0
and
k\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
. Consider the
SHE

\mathcal{Z}^{\mathbf{nw}}_{t}(x)
started from delta initial data (
1.3
). There exists a constant
\mathrm{C}=\mathrm{C}(k,T)>0
such that for all
s\in(0,T]
and
y\in\mathbb{R}
we have

(2.4)

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[(2\pi s)^{k/2}[\mathcal{Z}^{\mathbf{nw}}_{s}(y)]^{k}\exp\left(\tfrac{ky^{2}}{2s}\right)\right]\leq\mathrm{C}<\infty.

Proof.

By Proposition 1.4 in
[
1
]
we have
\mathcal{Z}^{\mathbf{nw}}(s,y)e^{\frac{y^{2}}{2s}}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle d}}{{=}}\mathcal{Z}^{\mathbf{nw}}(s,0)
. It thus suffices to prove

(2.5)

\displaystyle\sup_{s\in(0,T]}(2\pi s)^{k/2}\mathbb{E}\left[[\mathcal{Z}^{\mathbf{nw}}_{s}(0)]^{k}\right]\leq\mathrm{C}<\infty.

Precise moment estimates for
\mathcal{Z}^{\mathbf{nw}}_{s}(0)
are available in the literature; see Lemma 4.1 in
[
7
]
for example. Indeed, Lemma 4.1 in
[
7
]
shows that there exists
\mathrm{C}=\mathrm{C}(k,T)>0
such that
\mathbb{E}\left[[\mathcal{Z}^{\mathbf{nw}}_{s}(0)]^{k}\right]\leq\mathrm{C}
for
s\in[\pi,T]
and
\mathbb{E}\left[[\mathcal{Z}^{\mathbf{nw}}_{s}(0)]^{k}\right]\leq\mathrm{C}s^{-k/2}
for
s\in(0,\pi]
. These two estimates together verifies (
2.5
).
∎

In order to extend our moment estimate from narrow wedge initial data to functional initial data we rely on the convolution formula (Proposition
1.6
).

Proof of Proposition
2.3
.

To get the moment bounds for general initial data, we use Proposition
1.6
and a clever Hölder trick inspired from
[
29
]
(see Eq (2.2)). Using Proposition
1.6
we have

(2.6)

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[\mathcal{Z}_{s}^{f}(y)^{k}]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\left[\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}}\mathcal{Z}_{s}^{\mathbf{nw},z}(y)e^{f(z)}\mathrm{d}z\right)^{k}\right]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\left[\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}}e^{-\frac{(y-z)^{2}}{4s}}e^{\frac{(y-z)^{2}}{4s}}\mathcal{Z}_{s}^{\mathbf{nw},z}(y)e^{f(z)}\mathrm{d}z\right)^{k}\right].

We now apply Hölder inequality to the terms
e^{-\frac{(y-z)^{2}}{4s}}
and
e^{\frac{(y-z)^{2}}{4s}}\mathcal{Z}_{s}^{\mathbf{nw},z}(y)e^{f(z)}
with Hölder exponent
k
and
\ell=\frac{k}{k-1}
to get

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[\mathcal{Z}_{s}^{f}(y)^{k}]

\displaystyle\leq\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}}e^{-\frac{\ell(y-z)^{2}}{4s}}dz\right)^{\frac{k}{\ell}}\mathbb{E}\left[\int_{\mathbb{R}}e^{\frac{k(y-z)^{2}}{4s}}[\mathcal{Z}_{s}^{\mathbf{nw},z}(y)]^{k}e^{kf(z)}\mathrm{d}z\right]

(2.7)

\displaystyle=\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}}e^{-\frac{\ell(y-z)^{2}}{4s}}dz\right)^{k-1}\int_{\mathbb{R}}e^{\frac{k(y-z)^{2}}{4s}}M_{k}^{f}(z)\mathbb{E}[\mathcal{Z}_{s}^{\mathbf{nw},z}(y)^{k}]\mathrm{d}z

(2.8)

\displaystyle=\left(\tfrac{4s\pi}{\ell}\right)^{\frac{k-1}{2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}e^{\frac{k(y-z)^{2}}{4s}}M_{k}^{f}(z)\mathbb{E}[\mathcal{Z}_{s}^{\mathbf{nw},z}(y)^{k}]\mathrm{d}z

where
M_{k}^{f}(x)
is defined in (
2.1
). Note that the equality in (
2.7
) follows by interchanging the expectation and integral (as the integrand is nonnegative) and utilizing the fact that the initial data
f
is independent of the underlying space-time white noise. Using (
1.6
) and applying Proposition
-nw
we have

(2.9)

r.h.s. of (
2.8
)

\displaystyle\leq\mathrm{C}\tfrac{1}{(2\pi s)^{1/2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}M_{k}^{f}(z)\exp\left({-\tfrac{k(y-z)^{2}}{4s}}\right)\mathrm{d}z

where
\mathrm{C}>0
depends on
k,T
. Using assumption (
2.2
) followed by a change of variable
u\mapsto\sqrt{k}(z-y)/\sqrt{2s}
we have

r.h.s. of (
2.9
)

\displaystyle=\mathrm{C}\tfrac{1}{(2\pi s)^{1/2}}\sqrt{\tfrac{2s}{k}}e^{\lambda_{k}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\exp\left(\lambda_{k}|u\sqrt{\tfrac{2s}{k}}+{y}|^{2-\delta_{k}}-\tfrac{u^{2}}{2}\right)\mathrm{d}u

\displaystyle\leq\mathrm{C}e^{\lambda_{k}}e^{\Delta|y|^{2-\delta_{k}}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\exp\left(\Delta|u|^{2-\delta_{k}}-\tfrac{u^{2}}{2}\right)\mathrm{d}u,

where in the last line we used the fact that one can choose
\Delta>0
depending on
\lambda_{k}
and
T
such that
\lambda_{k}|u\sqrt{\frac{2s}{k}}+y|^{2-\delta_{k}}\leq\Delta(|u|^{2-\delta_{k}}+|y|^{2-\delta_{k}})
for all
s\in[0,T]
and for all
u,y\in\mathbb{R}
.
Clearly the above integral is uniformly bounded by a constant depending on
\Delta
and
\delta_{k}
. Thus adjusting the constant
\Delta
we get the desired bound.
∎

2.2.
Spatial Regularity estimate

In this subsection we prove regularity estimates for the moments of
|\mathcal{Z}_{t}^{f}(x)-\mathcal{Z}_{t}^{f}(0)|
. As before we rely on the convolution formula to bypass narrow wedge results to functional data. For proving regularity estimates for narrow wedge data, we use tail estimates for differences of height functions in the scaled KPZ equation noted in
[
8
]
. Towards this end, for narrow wedge initial data, we introduce the
1:2:3
scaled version of the KPZ equation:

(2.10)

\displaystyle\mathfrak{h}_{t}(x):=\frac{\log\mathcal{Z}_{t}^{\mathbf{nw}}(t^{2/3}x)+\frac{t}{24}}{t^{1/3}}.

The above
1:2:3
scaling of fluctuations, space, and time are the characteristics of the models in KPZ universality class. Being an integrable model,
\mathfrak{h}_{t}(x)
itself enjoys several properties. It admits exact one-point distribution formula and moment formulas
[
1
]
. Furthermore, it can be viewed as the top curve of the KPZ line ensemble that enjoys a type of Brownian Gibbs property
[
9
]
. Exploiting such a rich structure several fine estimates for
\mathfrak{h}_{t}(x)
are obtained
[
7
]
,
[
8
]
, and
[
18
]
. We utilize them to obtain moment estimates in our setting.

Proposition 2.4
.

Fix
\mathfrak{q}\in((0,\infty)\times(0,1])^{\mathbb{N}}
,
T>1
,
\theta\in(0,\frac{1}{2})
and
k\geq 2
. There exists a constant
\mathrm{C}=\mathrm{C}(\mathfrak{q}_{k},k,T,\theta)>0
such that for all functional
f\in\mathbf{Hyp}_{2}(\mathfrak{q})
we have

\displaystyle\sup_{t\in[T^{-1},T],0<|x|\leq\frac{1}{\mathrm{C}}}\mathbb{E}\left[\frac{|\mathcal{Z}_{t}^{f}(x)-\mathcal{Z}_{t}^{f}(0)|^{k}}{|x|^{k\theta}}\right]=\mathrm{C}<\infty.

Proposition
2.4
-nw
.

Fix
T>1
and
k\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
. There exists a constant
\mathrm{C}=\mathrm{C}(k,T)>0
such that for all
y\in\mathbb{R}
we have

\displaystyle\sup_{t\in[T^{-1},T],x\neq 0}\mathbb{E}\left[\frac{|\mathcal{Z}_{t}^{\mathbf{nw}}(x+y)-\mathcal{Z}_{t}^{\mathbf{nw}}(y)|^{k}}{|x|^{k/2}}\right]=\mathrm{C}<\infty.

We use tail estimates of
\mathfrak{h}_{t}(x)
and its spatial or temporal increments and convert them to moment estimates for
\mathcal{Z}_{t}^{\mathbf{nw}}(y)
via the following Lemma.

Lemma 2.5
.

Let
X:\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
be a stochastic process. Fix
M>1
and let
I
be an interval of
\mathbb{R}
. Suppose for all
s,t\in I
with
|t-s|<1
and for all
m>0
we have

(2.11)

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left(|X(s,t)|\geq m|s-t|^{\beta}\right)\leq\mathrm{C}\exp\left(-\tfrac{1}{\mathrm{C}}m^{\alpha}\right)

for some constant
\mathrm{C}>0
,
\beta>0
and
\alpha>1
. Then for all
k\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
we have

(2.12)

\displaystyle\sup_{p\in[M^{-1},M]}\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}s\neq t\in I\\
|t-s|\leq 1\end{subarray}}\mathbb{E}\left[\frac{\left|e^{pX(s,t)}-1\right|^{k}}{|s-t|^{k\beta}}\right]=R(\alpha,\beta,\mathrm{C},k,M)<\infty.

We first complete the proof of Proposition
-nw
assuming Lemma
2.5
.

Proof of Proposition
-nw
.

Fix
T>1
,
t\in[T^{-1},T]
and
x,y\in\mathbb{R}
with
x\neq 0
. By Proposition
-nw
, Proposition
-nw
follows for all
x
bounded away from
0
. Thus we may assume
|x|\leq T^{-2/3}
and set
u=t^{-2/3}x
and
v=t^{-2/3}y
. As
t\in[T^{-1},T]
, we have
|u|\leq T^{2/3}|x|\leq 1
. Using the scaling in (
2.10
), we have the following relation:

\displaystyle\frac{\mathcal{Z}_{t}^{\mathbf{nw}}(x+y)e^{\frac{(x+y)^{2}}{2t}}}{\mathcal{Z}_{t}^{\mathbf{nw}}(y)e^{\frac{y^{2}}{2t}}}=\exp\bigg(t^{1/3}\big(\mathfrak{h}_{t}(u+v)+\tfrac{(u+v)^{2}}{2}-\mathfrak{h}_{t}(v)-\tfrac{v^{2}}{2}\big)\bigg).

We introduce the parabola in the above equation as
\mathfrak{h}_{t}(w)+\frac{w^{2}}{2}
is stationary in
w
and one has tail estimates for the two-point difference of the process
\mathfrak{h}_{t}(w)+\frac{w^{2}}{2}
. Let us temporarily set

\displaystyle A:=\mathcal{Z}_{t}^{\mathbf{nw}}(x+y)e^{\frac{(x+y)^{2}-y^{2}}{2t}}=\mathcal{Z}_{t}^{\mathbf{nw}}(y)\exp\bigg(t^{1/3}\big(\mathfrak{h}_{t}(u+v)+\tfrac{(u+v)^{2}}{2}-\mathfrak{h}_{t}(v)-\tfrac{v^{2}}{2}\big)\bigg).

In view of the above equality, we have the following decomposition:

(2.13)

\displaystyle\frac{\mathcal{Z}_{t}^{\mathbf{nw}}(x+y)-\mathcal{Z}_{t}^{\mathbf{nw}}(y)}{\sqrt{|x|}}

\displaystyle=\frac{\mathcal{Z}_{t}^{\mathbf{nw}}(x+y)-A}{\sqrt{|x|}}+\frac{A-\mathcal{Z}_{t}^{\mathbf{nw}}(y)}{\sqrt{|x|}}

\displaystyle=\frac{\mathcal{Z}_{t}^{\mathbf{nw}}(x+y)\big[1-e^{\frac{(x+y)^{2}-y^{2}}{2t}}\big]}{\sqrt{x}}

\displaystyle+\mathcal{Z}_{t}^{\mathbf{nw}}(y)\frac{\left[e^{t^{1/3}[\mathfrak{h}_{t}(u+v)+\frac{(u+v)^{2}}{2}-\mathfrak{h}_{t}(v)-\frac{v^{2}}{2}]}-1\right]}{\sqrt{|x|}}.

As
|a+b|^{k}\leq 2^{k-1}(|a|^{k}+|b|^{k})
, it suffices to provide bounds for the
k
-th moment for each of the two terms in r.h.s. of (
2.13
) separately. As the second term is a product of two terms, using
|cd|^{k}\leq|c|^{2k}+|d|^{2k}
, it is enough to provide moment bounds for
\mathcal{Z}_{t}^{\mathbf{nw}}(y)
and
\left[e^{t^{1/3}[\mathfrak{h}_{t}(u+v)+\frac{(u+v)^{2}}{2}-\mathfrak{h}_{t}(v)-\frac{v^{2}}{2}]}-1\right]
separately. Now Proposition
-nw
ensures all moments of
\mathcal{Z}_{t}^{\mathbf{nw}}(y)
are uniformly bounded. On the other hand, By Theorem 1.3 in
[
8
]
for all
v\in\mathbb{R}
and
u\in[-1,1]
we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}(|\mathfrak{h}_{t}(u+v)+\tfrac{(u+v)^{2}}{2}-\mathfrak{h}_{t}(v)-\tfrac{v^{2}}{2}|\geq m\sqrt{|u|})\leq\mathrm{C}\exp\left(-\tfrac{1}{\mathrm{C}}m^{3/2}\right),

where the constant
\mathrm{C}>0
depends only on
T
. Since
u=t^{-2/3}x
and
t\in[T^{-1},T]
. Using the above estimate, in view of Lemma
2.5
, we get that

\displaystyle\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}t\in[T^{-1},T]\\
0<|x|\leq T^{-2/3},v\in\mathbb{R}\end{subarray}}\mathbb{E}\left[\frac{|e^{t^{1/3}[\mathfrak{h}_{t}(t^{-2/3}x+v)+\frac{(t^{-2/3}x+v)^{2}}{2}-\mathfrak{h}_{t}(v)-\frac{v^{2}}{2}]}-1|^{2k}}{|x|^{k}}\right]<\infty.

This leads to a uniform moment bound for the second term in (
2.13
). For the first term, using Proposition
-nw
we have

\displaystyle\big|1-e^{\frac{(x+y)^{2}-y^{2}}{2t}}\big|^{k}|x|^{-k/2}\mathbb{E}[\mathcal{Z}_{t}^{\mathbf{nw}}(x+y)^{k}]

\displaystyle\leq\mathrm{C}|x|^{-k/2}\big|1-e^{\frac{(x+y)^{2}-y^{2}}{2t}}\big|^{k}e^{-\frac{k(x+y)^{2}}{2t}}

\displaystyle=\mathrm{C}\bigg[|x|^{-1/2}\big|e^{-\frac{(x+y)^{2}}{2t}}-e^{-\frac{y^{2}}{2t}}\big|\bigg]^{k}

\displaystyle\leq\mathrm{C}\bigg[|x|^{1/2}\cdot\sup_{y\in\mathbb{R},t\in[T^{-1},T]}\big|\partial_{y}(e^{-\frac{y^{2}}{2t}})\big|\bigg]^{k},

where the last inequality follows by applying the mean value theorem and then taking supremum over the derivative. The last term is uniformly bounded over
0<x\leq T^{-2/3}\leq 1
(as
T\geq 1
). This completes the proof.
∎

Proof of Lemma
2.5
.

Due to (
2.11
), for all
p\in[M^{-1},M]
, for all
m>0
, and
s\neq t\in I

we have

\mathbb{P}\left(|pX(s,t)|\leq m|s-t|^{\beta}\right)\leq\mathrm{C}\exp\left(-\tfrac{1}{\mathrm{C}}m^{3/2}p^{-3/2}\right)\leq\widetilde{\mathrm{C}}\exp\left(-\tfrac{1}{\widetilde{\mathrm{C}}}m^{3/2}\right)

where
\widetilde{\mathrm{C}}=M^{3/2}\mathrm{C}
. Thus it is enough to show (
2.12
) assuming
p=1
. Toward this end, we will show

\frac{e^{X(s,t)}-1}{|s-t|^{\beta}}

has good enough upper and lower tail decay uniform in
s,t\in I
with
0<|t-s|<1
. Assume
m\geq 2
. For the upper tail using (
2.11
) we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left(e^{X(s,t)}-1\geq m{|s-t|^{\beta}}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{P}\left(|X(s,t)|\geq\log(1+m|s-t|^{\beta})\right)

(2.14)

\displaystyle\leq\mathrm{C}\exp\left(-\tfrac{1}{\mathrm{C}}|t-s|^{-\alpha\beta}{\log^{\alpha}(1+m|t-s|^{\beta})}\right).

Note that for
u\geq 1
and
|t-s|\leq 1
, we have

\frac{|t-s|^{\beta}}{1+u|t-s|^{\beta}}\geq\frac{|t-s|^{\beta}}{2u}
. Integrating both sides w.r.t.
u
from
1
to
m
leads to

\log(1+m|t-s|^{\beta})\geq\tfrac{1}{2}|t-s|^{\beta}\log m+\log(1+|t-s|^{\beta})\geq\tfrac{1}{2}|t-s|^{\beta}\log m.

Thus,
\mbox{r.h.s.~of \eqref{e:a11}}\leq\mathrm{C}\exp\left(-\frac{1}{2^{\alpha}\mathrm{C}}\log^{\alpha}m\right)
.

For the lower tail observe that if
m|s-t|^{\beta}\geq 1
, then
e^{X(s,t)}-1>-m|s-t|^{\beta}
. Thus we may assume
m|s-t|^{\beta}<1
. Take
u\geq 1
with
u|s-t|^{\beta}<1
. We have
\frac{{|s-t|^{\beta}}}{1-u{|s-t|^{\beta}}}\geq\frac{{|s-t|^{\beta}}}{u}
. Integrating both sides w.r.t.
u
from
1
to
m
leads to

-\log(1-m\sqrt{|s-t|^{\beta}})\geq\sqrt{|s-t|^{\beta}}\log m-\log(1-|s-t|^{\beta})\geq|s-t|^{\beta}\log m.

Using this inequality and (
2.11
) we get

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left(e^{X(s,t)}-1\leq-m{|s-t|^{\beta}}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{P}\left(|X(s,t)|\geq-\log(1-m|s-t|^{\beta})\right)

\displaystyle\leq\mathrm{C}\exp\left(-\tfrac{1}{\mathrm{C}}|t-s|^{-\alpha\beta}{[-\log(1-m|t-s|^{\beta})]^{\alpha}}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\exp\left(-\tfrac{1}{\mathrm{C}}\log^{\alpha}m\right).

As
\alpha>1
, both the upper and lower tail decay faster than any polynomial. This proves (
2.12
).
∎

Proof of Proposition
2.4
.

Fix any
f\in\mathbf{Hyp}_{2}(\mathfrak{q})
. Fix
|x|\leq\frac{1}{2}
. By convolution formula (Proposition
1.6
) and the elementary inequality
|\int g(y)\mathrm{d}y|\leq\int|g(y)|\mathrm{d}y
we have

\displaystyle|\mathcal{Z}_{t}^{f}(x)-\mathcal{Z}_{t}^{f}(0)|

\displaystyle=\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}}e^{f(y)}[\mathcal{Z}_{t}^{\mathbf{nw},y}(x)-\mathcal{Z}_{t}^{\mathbf{nw},y}(0)]\mathrm{d}y\right|

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}}e^{f(y)}\left|\mathcal{Z}_{t}^{\mathbf{nw},y}(x)-\mathcal{Z}_{t}^{\mathbf{nw},y}(0)\right|\mathrm{d}y.

Similar to (
2.6
) and (
2.8
), we first rewrite the above integrand
e^{f(y)}\left|\mathcal{Z}_{t}^{\mathbf{nw},y}(x)-\mathcal{Z}_{t}^{\mathbf{nw},y}(0)\right|
as product of
e^{\frac{y^{2}}{4t}}e^{f(y)}\left|\mathcal{Z}_{t}^{\mathbf{nw},y}(x)-\mathcal{Z}_{t}^{\mathbf{nw},y}(0)\right|
and
e^{-\frac{y^{2}}{4t}}
. Then apply Hölder inequality with exponents
k
and
\ell=\frac{k}{k-1}
to get

(2.15)

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\left|\mathcal{Z}_{t}^{f}(x)-\mathcal{Z}_{t}^{f}(0)\right|^{k}\right]\leq\left(\tfrac{4s\pi}{\ell}\right)^{\frac{k-1}{2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}e^{\frac{ky^{2}}{4t}}M_{k}^{f}(y)\mathbb{E}\big[|\mathcal{Z}_{t}^{\mathbf{nw},y}(x)-\mathcal{Z}_{t}^{\mathbf{nw},y}(0)|^{k}\big]\,\mathrm{d}y,

where
M_{k}^{f}(x)
is defined in (
2.1
). Using (
1.6
) and (
2.2
) we have

(2.16)

r.h.s. of (
2.15
)

\displaystyle\leq\mathrm{C}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\exp\left(\tfrac{ky^{2}}{4t}+\lambda_{k}(1+|y|^{2-\delta_{k}})\right)\mathbb{E}\big[|\mathcal{Z}_{t}^{\mathbf{nw}}(x+y)-\mathcal{Z}_{t}^{\mathbf{nw}}(y)|^{k}\big]\mathrm{d}y.

By spatial regularity estimate from Proposition
-nw
we have

(2.17)

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\big[|\mathcal{Z}_{t}^{\mathbf{nw}}(x+y)-\mathcal{Z}_{t}^{\mathbf{nw}}(y)|^{k}\big]\leq\mathrm{C}\cdot|x|^{k/2}.

On the other hand, by moment estimate from Proposition
-nw
we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\big[|\mathcal{Z}_{t}^{\mathbf{nw}}(x+y)-\mathcal{Z}_{t}^{\mathbf{nw}}(y)|^{k}\big]

\displaystyle\leq 2^{k-1}\left[\mathbb{E}\big[|\mathcal{Z}_{t}^{\mathbf{nw}}(x+y)|^{k}\big]+\mathbb{E}\big[|\mathcal{Z}_{t}^{\mathbf{nw}}(y)|^{k}\big]\right]

\displaystyle\leq\mathrm{C}\left[\exp(-\tfrac{k(x+y)^{2}}{2t})+\exp(-\tfrac{ky^{2}}{2t})\right]

where the constant
\mathrm{C}>0
depends only on
k,T
. Here we crucially used the fact that
t
has a lower bound, i.e.,
t\geq T^{-1}
to derive the constant
\mathrm{C}
free of
t
. Now as
|x|\leq\frac{1}{2}
, we may choose
y_{0}(\mathfrak{q}_{k},k,T)>0
, such that for all
|y|\geq y_{0}
and for all
t\in[T^{-1},T]
we have

\exp(-\tfrac{k(x+y)^{2}}{2t})+\exp(-\tfrac{ky^{2}}{2t})\leq\mathrm{C}\exp(-\lambda_{k}(1+|y|^{2-\delta_{k}})-\tfrac{ky^{2}}{4t}-\tfrac{ky^{2}}{8t})

for some
\mathrm{C}
depending on
\mathfrak{q}_{k},k,T
. Thus combining this estimate with (
2.17
), we have that for all
|y|\geq y_{0}
and
|x|\leq\frac{1}{2}
,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\big[|\mathcal{Z}_{t}^{\mathbf{nw}}(x+y)-\mathcal{Z}_{t}^{\mathbf{nw}}(y)|^{k}\big]\leq\mathrm{C}\cdot\min\left\{|x|^{k/2},\exp\left(-\lambda_{k}(1+|y|^{2-\delta_{k}})-\tfrac{ky^{2}}{4t}-\tfrac{ky^{2}}{8t}\right)\right\}.

Thus,

\displaystyle\mbox{r.h.s.~of \eqref{3.5.1}}\leq\mathrm{C}\int_{-y_{0}}^{y_{0}}|x|^{k/2}e^{\lambda_{k}|y|^{2-\delta_{k}}}\,\mathrm{d}y+\mathrm{C}\int_{|y|\geq y_{0}}\min\left\{|x|^{k/2}e^{\lambda_{k}|y|^{2-\delta_{k}}},e^{-\tfrac{ky^{2}}{8T}}\right\}\,\mathrm{d}y.

The first term is indeed bounded by
\mathrm{C}\cdot|x|^{k/2}
. For the second term, we set
y_{1}=\sqrt{2T\log(1/x)}
and observe

\displaystyle\int_{|y|\geq y_{1}}\min\left\{|x|^{k/2}e^{\lambda_{k}|y|^{2-\delta_{k}}},e^{-\tfrac{ky^{2}}{8T}}\right\}\,\mathrm{d}y\leq\int_{|y|\geq y_{1}}e^{-\tfrac{ky^{2}}{8T}}\,\mathrm{d}y\leq\mathrm{C}|x|^{k},

\displaystyle\int_{|y|\leq y_{1}}\min\left\{|x|^{k/2}e^{\lambda_{k}|y|^{2-\delta_{k}}},e^{-\tfrac{ky^{2}}{8T}}\right\}\,\mathrm{d}y\leq 2y_{1}|x|^{k/2}e^{\lambda_{k}|y_{1}|^{2-\delta_{k}}}\leq\mathrm{C}|x|^{k\theta}.

The last inequality is true for all small enough
x
depending on
\lambda_{k}
,
\delta_{k}
,
\theta
, and
T
. This is due to the fact
y_{1}
and
e^{\lambda_{k}|y_{1}|^{2-\delta_{k}}}
are both sub-polynomial order. This completes the proof.
∎

2.3.
Temporal regularity estimate

The main goal of this section is to prove the following proposition that provides temporal regularity estimates for the
SHE
started from initial data
f
in
\mathbf{Hyp}_{2}(\mathfrak{q})
class defined in Definition
2.1
.

Proposition 2.6
.

Fix
\mathfrak{q}\in((0,\infty)\times(0,1])^{\mathbb{N}}
,
T>1
and
k\geq 2
. There exists a constant
\mathrm{C}=\mathrm{C}(\mathfrak{q}_{k},k,T)>0
such that for all
f\in\mathbf{Hyp}_{2}(\mathfrak{q})
we have

\displaystyle\sup_{s\neq t\in[T^{-1},T]}\mathbb{E}\left[\frac{|\mathcal{Z}_{s}^{f}(0)-\mathcal{Z}_{t}^{f}(0)|^{k}}{|s-t|^{k/4}}\right]=\mathrm{C}<\infty.

The proof of Proposition
2.6
relies on some heat kernel estimates which we record below.

Lemma 2.7
.

Fix
\theta\in(0,\frac{1}{2})
and
\Delta,\delta_{k}>0
, one can find constants
\rho_{0}=\rho_{0}(\theta,\Delta,\delta_{k})>0
and
\mathrm{C}=\mathrm{C}(\theta,\Delta,\delta_{k})>0
such that for all
\rho\in(0,\rho_{0})
we have

(2.18)

\displaystyle\qquad\int_{0}^{\rho}\int_{\mathbb{R}}p^{2}_{r}(y)\min\{|y|^{2\theta},e^{\Delta|y|^{2-\delta_{k}}}\}\,\mathrm{d}y\,\mathrm{d}r\leq\mathrm{C}\rho^{\frac{1}{2}+\theta}.

Additionally we also have

(2.19)

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\rho}\int_{\mathbb{R}}p^{2}_{r}(y)e^{\Delta|y|^{2-\delta_{k}}}\,\mathrm{d}y\,\mathrm{d}r\leq\mathrm{C}\rho^{\frac{1}{2}},

for all
\rho\in(0,\rho_{0})
where now
\rho_{0}
and
\mathrm{C}
can be chosen free of
\theta
.

Lemma 2.8
.

Fix
\mathfrak{q}\in((0,\infty)\times(0,1])^{\mathbb{N}}
,
T>1
, and
a\in[\frac{1}{2},\infty]
. There exist constants
\varepsilon_{0}(\mathfrak{q}_{k},k,T)>0
and
\mathrm{C}(\mathfrak{q}_{k},k,T)>0
, such that for all
\varepsilon\leq\varepsilon_{0}
and for all
f\in\mathbf{Hyp}_{2}(\mathfrak{q})
we have

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t-\varepsilon^{a}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}[p_{t+\varepsilon-s}(x)-p_{t-s}(x)]^{2}\big[1+\mathbb{E}[\mathcal{Z}_{s}^{f}(x)^{k}]^{\frac{2}{k}}\big]\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}s\leq\mathrm{C}\cdot\varepsilon^{2-\frac{3}{2}\min(a,1)}.

Besides the above lemmas, proof of Proposition
2.6
also utilizes Burkholder-Davis-Gundy (BDG) inequality
[
15
, Lemma 2.3]
. Since we will make repeated use of this inequality throughout, we recall it here for readers’ convenience.

Theorem 2.9

(BDG inequality)
.

For every
t\geq 0
, let
\mathcal{F}_{t}^{0}
denote the sigma-algebra generated by every Wiener
integral of the form

\int_{(0,t)\times\mathbb{R}}\psi_{s}(y)\xi(\mathrm{d}s,\mathrm{d}y)

as
\psi
ranges over all elements of
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+}\times\mathbb{R})
. We complete every such sigma-algebra, and make the filtration

\{\mathcal{F}_{t}\}_{t\geq 0}
right continuous in order to obtain the “Brownian filtration”
\mathcal{F}
that corresponds to the white noise
\xi
.
Let
\Psi:=\{\Psi_{t}(x)\}_{t\geq 0,x\in\mathbb{R}}
be a predictable random field with respect to
\mathcal{F}
. Then, for every real number
k\in[2,\infty)
, we have the following inequality:

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\left|\int_{(0,t)\times\mathbb{R}}\Psi_{s}(y)\xi(\mathrm{d}s,\mathrm{d}y)\right|^{k}\right]^{\frac{2}{k}}\leq 4k\int_{(0,t)\times\mathbb{R}}\mathbb{E}\left[\left|\Psi_{s}(y)\right|^{k}\right]^{\frac{2}{k}}\,\mathrm{d}s\,\mathrm{d}y.

Let us now first complete the proof of Proposition
2.6
assuming Lemmas
2.7
and
2.8
.

Proof of Proposition
2.6
.

By Proposition
2.3
and
-nw
, Proposition
2.6
holds for
s,t
with
|s-t|
bounded away from zero. So we assume
s>t
and
s-t
is small enough. Set
s=t+\varepsilon
and from (
1.2
) observe that

(2.20)

\displaystyle\mathcal{Z}_{t+\varepsilon}^{f}(0)-\mathcal{Z}_{t}^{f}(0)

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}}[p_{t+\varepsilon}(y)-p_{t}(y)]e^{f(y)}\,\mathrm{d}y+\int_{t}^{t+\varepsilon}\int_{\mathbb{R}}p_{t+\varepsilon-s}(y)\mathcal{Z}_{s}^{f}(y)\xi(\mathrm{d}s,\mathrm{d}y)

\displaystyle+\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}}[p_{t+\varepsilon-s}(y)-p_{t-s}(y)]\mathcal{Z}_{s}^{f}(y)\xi(\mathrm{d}s,\mathrm{d}y).

Our goal is to control the moments for each term appearing in r.h.s. of (
2.20
).
For the first term, we claim that

(2.21)

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\bigg[\big|\int_{\mathbb{R}}\left[p_{t+\varepsilon}(y)-p_{t}(y)\right]e^{f(y)}\mathrm{d}y\big|^{k}\bigg]\leq\mathrm{C}\cdot\varepsilon^{k}

where
\mathrm{C}
depends on
k,T,\lambda_{k}
and
\delta_{k}
. Towards this end, we first note that

\displaystyle\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}}[p_{t+\varepsilon}(y)-p_{t}(y)]e^{f(y)}\mathrm{d}y\right|\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}}|p_{t+\varepsilon}(y)-p_{t}(y)|e^{f(y)}\mathrm{d}y.

Writing
|p_{t+\varepsilon}(y)-p_{t}(y)|e^{f(y)}
as product of
e^{\frac{y^{2}}{8T}}|p_{t+\varepsilon}(y)-p_{t}(y)|e^{f(y)}
and
e^{-\frac{y^{2}}{8T}}
, and applying Hölder inequality with exponent
k
and
\ell=\frac{k}{k-1}
we get

\displaystyle\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}}|p_{t+\varepsilon}(y)-p_{t}(y)|e^{f(y)}\mathrm{d}y\right)^{k}\leq\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}}e^{-\frac{\ell y^{2}}{8T}}\right)^{k-1}\int_{\mathbb{R}}e^{\frac{ky^{2}}{8T}}|p_{t+\varepsilon}(y)-p_{t}(y)|^{k}e^{kf(y)}\mathrm{d}y.

Hence we have

(2.22)

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\bigg[\big|\int_{\mathbb{R}}\left[p_{t+\varepsilon}(y)-p_{t}(y)\right]e^{f(y)}\mathrm{d}y\big|^{k}\bigg]\leq\mathrm{C}\int_{\mathbb{R}}e^{\frac{ky^{2}}{8T}}|p_{t+\varepsilon}(y)-p_{t}(y)|^{k}\mathbb{E}[e^{kf(y)}]\mathrm{d}y,

where
\mathrm{C}
depends on
k,T
. But by mean value theorem we get that

\displaystyle e^{\frac{ky^{2}}{8T}}|p_{t+\varepsilon}(y)-p_{t}(y)|^{k}\mathbb{E}[e^{kf(y)}]

\displaystyle\leq\varepsilon^{k}\cdot\sup_{t\in[T^{-1},T+1]}e^{\frac{ky^{2}}{8T}}M_{k}^{f}(y)|\partial_{t}p_{t}(y)|^{k}

\displaystyle\leq\mathrm{C}\varepsilon^{k}\cdot e^{\frac{ky^{2}}{8T}}e^{\lambda_{k}(1+|y|^{2-\delta_{k}})}y^{2}e^{-\frac{ky^{2}}{2T}},

where we have used the condition (
2.2
) in the last inequality above. Integrating both sides of the above equation over
y\in\mathbb{R}
, we get that r.h.s. of (
2.22
) is at most
\mathrm{C}\cdot\varepsilon^{k}
where
\mathrm{C}
depends on
k,T,\lambda_{k}
and
\delta_{k}
. This verifies (
2.21
).

To compute the moments of the second and third term in r.h.s. of (
2.20
), we apply BDG inequality (Theorem
2.9
) on each of the above terms to get

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\left|\int_{t}^{t+\varepsilon}\int_{\mathbb{R}}p_{t+\varepsilon-s}(y)\mathcal{Z}_{s}^{f}(y)\xi(\mathrm{d}s,\mathrm{d}y)\right|^{k}\right]^{\frac{2}{k}}

\displaystyle\hskip 85.35826pt\leq\mathrm{C}\int_{t}^{t+\varepsilon}\int_{\mathbb{R}}p_{t+\varepsilon-s}(y)^{2}\mathbb{E}[\mathcal{Z}_{s}^{f}(y)^{k}]^{\frac{2}{k}}\mathrm{d}y\,\mathrm{d}s,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\left|\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}}[p_{t+\varepsilon-s}(y)-p_{t-s}(y)]\mathcal{Z}_{s}^{f}(y)\xi(\mathrm{d}s,\mathrm{d}y)\right|^{k}\right]^{\frac{2}{k}}

\displaystyle\hskip 85.35826pt\leq\mathrm{C}\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}}[p_{t+\varepsilon-s}(y)-p_{t-s}(y)]^{2}\mathbb{E}[\mathcal{Z}_{s}^{f}(y)^{k}]^{\frac{2}{k}}\mathrm{d}y\,\mathrm{d}s.

In view of the above moment estimates along with (
2.21
), leads to moment estimates for the temporal difference in view of the identity in (
2.20
). Indeed, using the elementary inequalities
|a+b+c|^{k}\leq 3^{k-1}(|a|^{k}+|b|^{k}+|c|^{k})
and
|x+y+z|^{\frac{2}{k}}\leq|x|^{\frac{2}{k}}+|y|^{\frac{2}{k}}+|z|^{\frac{2}{k}}
for
k\geq 2
we get that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\big|\mathcal{Z}_{t+\varepsilon}^{f}(0)-\mathcal{Z}_{t}^{f}(0)\big|^{k}\right]^{\frac{2}{k}}

\displaystyle\leq\mathrm{C}\cdot\varepsilon^{k}+\mathrm{C}\int_{t}^{t+\varepsilon}\int_{\mathbb{R}}p_{t+\varepsilon-s}(y)^{2}\mathbb{E}[\mathcal{Z}_{s}^{f}(y)^{k}]^{\frac{2}{k}}\mathrm{d}y\,\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle\hskip 56.9055pt+\mathrm{C}\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}}[p_{t+\varepsilon-s}(y)-p_{t-s}(y)]^{2}\mathbb{E}[\mathcal{Z}_{s}^{f}(y)^{k}]^{\frac{2}{k}}\mathrm{d}y\,\mathrm{d}s.

By the moment estimates from Proposition
-nw
and Proposition
2.3
we see that the first double integral above is at most
\int_{0}^{\varepsilon}\int_{\mathbb{R}}p_{r}^{2}(y)e^{\Delta|y|^{2-\delta}}\,\mathrm{d}y\,\mathrm{d}r
which is at most
\mathrm{C}\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{2}}
by Lemma
2.7
with
\rho=\varepsilon
. The second double integral above is at most
\mathrm{C}\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{2}}
as well from Lemma
2.8
with
a=\infty
. This completes the proof. ∎

Proof of Lemma
2.7
.

Suppose
c_{0}
be such that
|y|^{2\theta}\leq e^{\Delta|y|^{2-\delta_{k}}}
for all
|y|\leq c_{0}
. We separate the
y
integral into two parts depending on
|y|\leq c_{0}
and
|y|\geq c_{0}
. Making a change of variable

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\rho}\int_{|y|\leq c_{0}}p^{2}_{r}(y)|y|^{2\theta}\,\mathrm{d}y\,\mathrm{d}r=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{\rho}r^{\theta-\frac{1}{2}}\int_{|x|\leq c_{0}/\sqrt{r}}e^{-x^{2}}|x|^{2\theta}\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}r\leq\mathrm{C}\int_{0}^{\rho}r^{\theta-\frac{1}{2}}\,\mathrm{d}r\leq\mathrm{C}\rho^{\frac{1}{2}+\theta}.

For the
|y|\geq c_{0}
integral, we can choose
\rho_{0}
depending on

and other parameters so that the following two conditions hold:

•

e^{-\frac{y^{2}}{r}}\leq r^{2}e^{-\frac{y^{2}}{2r}}
for all

and
|y|\geq c_{0}
, and

•

\frac{y^{2}}{4r}\geq\Delta|y|^{2-\delta_{k}}
for all
|y|\geq c_{0}
and

.

Then for all

we have

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\rho}\int_{|y|\geq c_{0}}p^{2}_{r}(y)e^{\Delta|y|^{2-\delta_{k}}}\,\mathrm{d}y\,\mathrm{d}r

\displaystyle=\tfrac{1}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{\rho}\tfrac{1}{r}\int_{|y|\geq c_{0}}e^{-\frac{y^{2}}{r}}e^{\Delta|y|^{2-\delta_{k}}}\,\mathrm{d}y\,\mathrm{d}r

\displaystyle\leq\tfrac{1}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{\rho}r\int_{|y|\geq c_{0}}e^{-\frac{y^{2}}{4r}}\,\mathrm{d}y\,\mathrm{d}r\leq\mathrm{C}\int_{0}^{\rho}r^{\frac{3}{2}}\,\mathrm{d}r\leq\mathrm{C}\rho^{\frac{5}{2}}.

This proves (
2.18
). For (
2.19
) we proceed in the exact same manner. Here we choose

. Then

integral is now bounded above by

. The other integral can be bounded exactly as before. This completes the proof.
∎

Proof of Lemma
2.8
.

Define

\displaystyle\Upsilon(B):=\int_{B}\int_{\mathbb{R}}[p_{t+\varepsilon-s}(y)-p_{t-s}(y)]^{2}\big[1+\mathbb{E}[\mathcal{Z}_{s}^{f}(y)^{k}]^{\frac{2}{k}}\big]\,\mathrm{d}y\,\mathrm{d}s.

We prove Lemma
2.8
in two steps. In Step 1 we show that
\Upsilon((0,t-\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{4}}))\leq\mathrm{C}\varepsilon^{5/4}.
Then in Step 2, we argue

\Upsilon([t-\varepsilon^{1/4},t-\varepsilon^{a}))\leq\mathrm{C}\varepsilon^{2-\frac{3}{2}\min(a,1)}.
Since
\Upsilon((0,t-\varepsilon^{a}))=\Upsilon((0,t-\varepsilon^{1/4}))+\Upsilon([t-\varepsilon^{1/4},t-\varepsilon^{a}))
, we get the desired bound.

Step 1
.
For narrow wedge initial data applying Proposition
-nw
we get

(2.23)

\displaystyle\Upsilon((0,t-\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{4}}))

\displaystyle\leq\mathrm{C}\int_{0}^{t-\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{4}}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}[p_{t+\varepsilon-s}(y)-p_{t-s}(y)]^{2}(1+s^{-1}e^{-y^{2}/s})\mathrm{d}y\,\mathrm{d}s,

whereas for functional
f\in\mathbf{Hyp}_{2}(\mathfrak{q})
applying Proposition
2.3
we get

(2.24)

\displaystyle\Upsilon((0,t-\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{4}}))

\displaystyle\leq\mathrm{C}\int_{0}^{t-\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{4}}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}[p_{t+\varepsilon-s}(y)-p_{t-s}(y)]^{2}e^{2\Delta|y|^{2-\delta_{k}}}\mathrm{d}y\,\mathrm{d}s,

for some
\Delta(\mathfrak{q}_{k},k,T)>0
.
Note that
t-s\geq\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{4}}
. Set

\displaystyle\chi:=\sup_{y\in\mathbb{R},r\in[\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{4}},T]}|p_{r+\varepsilon}(y)-p_{r}(y)|e^{\Delta|y|^{2-\delta_{k}}+|y|}.

We claim that

. As

, assuming the claim we see that

\displaystyle\mbox{r.h.s.~of \eqref{bdg4}}\leq\mathrm{C}\cdot\chi^{2}\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{R}}[e^{-2|y|}+s^{-1}e^{-y^{2}/s-2|y|}]\,\mathrm{d}y\,\mathrm{d}s\leq\mathrm{C}\cdot\varepsilon^{\frac{5}{4}},

\displaystyle\mbox{r.h.s.~of \eqref{bdg5}}\leq\mathrm{C}\cdot\chi^{2}\int_{0}^{T}\,\mathrm{d}s\int_{\mathbb{R}}e^{-2|y|}\,\mathrm{d}y\leq\mathrm{C}\cdot\varepsilon^{\frac{5}{4}},

which proves that
\Upsilon((0,t-\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{4}}))\leq\mathrm{C}\varepsilon^{\frac{5}{4}}
. Hence it suffices to prove the claim. Towards this end, we rely on mean value theorem. Observe that

\displaystyle e^{\Delta|y|^{2-\delta_{k}}+|y|}\partial_{r}p_{r}(y)=\tfrac{1}{\sqrt{8\pi}}r^{-3/2}\left[\tfrac{1}{r}y^{2}e^{-\frac{y^{2}}{4r}}-e^{-\frac{y^{2}}{4r}}\right]e^{-\frac{y^{2}}{4r}+\Delta|y|^{2-\delta_{k}}+|y|}.

Clearly
|\tfrac{1}{r}y^{2}e^{-\frac{y^{2}}{4r}}-e^{-\frac{y^{2}}{4r}}|
is uniformly bounded as
y
varies in
\mathbb{R}
and
r
varies in
(0,\infty)
. Furthermore

\sup_{y\in\mathbb{R},r\in(0,T+1]}\exp\left(-\tfrac{y^{2}}{4r}+\Delta|y|^{2-\delta_{k}}+|y|\right)\leq\sup_{y\in\mathbb{R}}\ \exp\left(-\tfrac{y^{2}}{4T+4}+\Delta|y|^{2-\delta_{k}}+|y|\right)\leq\mathrm{C}<\infty.

Thus for all
r\in(0,T+1]
and
y\in\mathbb{R}
we have
|e^{\Delta|y|^{2-\delta_{k}}+|y|}\partial_{r}p_{r}(y)|\leq\mathrm{C}r^{-3/2}
. Hence

\chi=\sup_{y\in\mathbb{R},r\in[\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{4}},T]}|p_{r+\varepsilon}(y)-p_{r}(y)|e^{\Delta|y|^{2-\delta_{k}}+|y|}\leq\varepsilon\cdot\sup_{y\in\mathbb{R},r\in[\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{4}},T+1]}e^{\Delta|y|^{2-\delta_{k}}+|y|}|\partial_{r}p_{r}(y)|\leq\mathrm{C}\cdot\varepsilon^{\frac{5}{8}}.

This completes our work for this step.

Step 2.
In this step we prove that
\Upsilon([t-\varepsilon^{1/4},t-\varepsilon^{a}))\leq\mathrm{C}\varepsilon^{2-\frac{3}{2}\min(a,1)}.
We may choose
\varepsilon
small enough so that

. Hence the range of integration is bounded away from zero. We may thus combine the estimates from Proposition
-nw
and Proposition
2.3
to conclude that for all
f\in\mathbf{Hyp}_{2}(\mathfrak{q})
we have

\displaystyle\Upsilon([t-\varepsilon^{1/4},t-\varepsilon^{a}))\leq\mathrm{C}\int_{\varepsilon^{a}}^{\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{4}}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}[p_{r+\varepsilon}(y)-p_{r}(y)]^{2}e^{2\Delta|y|^{2-\delta_{k}}}\mathrm{d}y\,\mathrm{d}r,

for some
C,\Delta>0
depending on
\mathfrak{q},k,T
(Note that this
\Delta
might be different from the one appearing in (
2.3
) as we have combined it with the estimate from (
2.4
)). We divide the
y
-integral into two parts base on
|y|\leq 1
and
|y|\geq 1
,

\displaystyle\mathrm{C}\int_{\varepsilon^{a}}^{\varepsilon^{1/4}}\int_{|y|\leq 1}[p_{r+\varepsilon}(y)-p_{r}(y)]^{2}e^{2\Delta|y|^{2-\delta_{k}}}\mathrm{d}y\,\mathrm{d}r

\displaystyle\leq\mathrm{C}e^{2\Delta}\int_{\varepsilon^{a}}^{\varepsilon^{1/4}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}[p_{r+\varepsilon}(y)-p_{r}(y)]^{2}\mathrm{d}y\,\mathrm{d}r

\displaystyle=\tfrac{\mathrm{C}e^{2\Delta}}{2\sqrt{\pi}}\int_{\varepsilon^{a}}^{\varepsilon^{1/4}}\left[\tfrac{1}{\sqrt{r+\varepsilon}}+\tfrac{1}{\sqrt{r}}-\tfrac{2}{\sqrt{r+\frac{\varepsilon}{2}}}\right]\,\mathrm{d}r

\displaystyle=\tfrac{\mathrm{C}e^{2\Delta}}{\sqrt{\pi}}[f(\varepsilon^{1/4})-f(\varepsilon^{a})],

where

\displaystyle f(r)

\displaystyle:=\sqrt{r+\varepsilon}+\sqrt{r}-2\sqrt{r+\tfrac{\varepsilon}{2}}

\displaystyle=(\sqrt{r+\varepsilon}-\sqrt{r+\tfrac{\varepsilon}{2}})+(\sqrt{r}-\sqrt{r+\tfrac{\varepsilon}{2}})

\displaystyle=\frac{\varepsilon/2}{\sqrt{r+\varepsilon}+\sqrt{r+\tfrac{\varepsilon}{2}}}-\frac{\varepsilon/2}{\sqrt{r}+\sqrt{r+\tfrac{\varepsilon}{2}}}

\displaystyle=(\varepsilon/2)\frac{\sqrt{r}-\sqrt{r+\varepsilon}}{(\sqrt{r+\varepsilon}+\sqrt{r+\tfrac{\varepsilon}{2}})(\sqrt{r}+\sqrt{r+\tfrac{\varepsilon}{2}})}

\displaystyle=-\tfrac{1}{2}\varepsilon^{2}\left[(\sqrt{r}+\sqrt{r+\varepsilon})(\sqrt{r}+\sqrt{r+\tfrac{\varepsilon}{2}})(\sqrt{r+\varepsilon}+\sqrt{r+\tfrac{\varepsilon}{2}})\right]^{-1}.

is negative and decreasing. Furthermore,
|f(r)|\leq\sqrt{2}\varepsilon^{2}(r+\varepsilon)^{-3/2}\leq\sqrt{2}\varepsilon^{2}\min(r,\varepsilon)^{3/2}
. Hence

|f(\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{4}})-f(\varepsilon^{a})|\leq|f(\varepsilon^{a})|\leq\sqrt{2}\varepsilon^{2}\min(\varepsilon^{a},\varepsilon)^{3/2}\leq\mathrm{C}\varepsilon^{2-\frac{3}{2}\min(a,1)}.

We now turn towards the other integral:
|y|\geq 1
region. Since
|y|\geq 1
, we may choose
\varepsilon_{0}=\varepsilon_{0}(k,\Delta,\delta_{k})>0
so that the following two conditions hold for all

:

•

e^{-\tfrac{y^{2}}{r+\varepsilon}}\leq\varepsilon^{2}e^{-\tfrac{y^{2}}{2r+2\varepsilon}}
for all
r\in(0,\varepsilon^{1/4}]
, and
|y|\geq 1
.

•

\frac{y^{2}}{4r+4\varepsilon}\geq 2\Delta|y|^{2-\delta_{k}}
for all
r\in(0,\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{4}}]
, and
|y|\geq 1
.

Assuming
\varepsilon\leq\varepsilon_{0}
, the above condition forces

p_{r+\varepsilon}(y)^{2}e^{2\Delta|y|^{2-\delta_{k}}}\leq\mathrm{C}\varepsilon^{2}(r+\varepsilon)^{-1/2}p_{r+\varepsilon}(y/2),\qquad p_{r}(y)^{2}e^{2\Delta|y|^{2-\delta_{k}}}\leq\mathrm{C}\varepsilon^{2}r^{-1/2}p_{r}(y/2).

Using
[p_{r+\varepsilon}(y)-p_{r}(y)]^{2}\leq p_{r+\varepsilon}(y)^{2}+p_{r}(y)^{2}
we thus get that

[p_{r+\varepsilon}(y)-p_{r}(y)]^{2}e^{2\Delta|y|^{2-\delta_{k}}}\leq\mathrm{C}\varepsilon^{2}[(r+\varepsilon)^{-1/2}p_{r+\varepsilon}(y/2)+r^{-1/2}p_{r}(y/2)].

Integrating both sides of the above equation over
|y|\geq 1
and
r\in[\varepsilon^{a},\varepsilon^{1/4}]
we get

\displaystyle\int_{\varepsilon^{a}}^{\varepsilon^{1/4}}\int_{|y|\geq 1}[p_{r+\varepsilon}(y)-p_{r}(y)]^{2}e^{2\Delta|y|^{2-\delta_{k}}}\mathrm{d}y\,\mathrm{d}r

\displaystyle\leq\mathrm{C}\varepsilon^{2}\int_{\varepsilon^{a}}^{\varepsilon^{1/4}}\int_{|y|\geq 1}[(r+\varepsilon)^{-1/2}p_{r+\varepsilon}(y/2)+r^{-1/2}p_{r}(y/2)]\mathrm{d}y\,\mathrm{d}r

\displaystyle\leq\mathrm{C}\varepsilon^{2}\int_{\varepsilon^{a}}^{\varepsilon^{1/4}}[(r+\varepsilon)^{-1/2}+r^{-1/2}]\,\mathrm{d}r

\displaystyle\leq\mathrm{C}\varepsilon^{2}\int_{0}^{\varepsilon^{1/4}}r^{-\frac{1}{2}}\,\mathrm{d}r\leq\mathrm{C}\varepsilon^{2+1/8}.

Combining the above estimate along with the estimate for the
\{|y|\leq 1\}
integral, we get
\Upsilon([t-\varepsilon^{1/4},t-\varepsilon^{a}))\leq\mathrm{C}\varepsilon^{2-\frac{3}{2}\min(a,1)},
completing the proof.
∎

\mathfrak{p}\in(0,\infty)^{5}
. Consider the scaled KPZ equation

\mathfrak{h}_{t}^{f}:=t^{-1/3}(\mathcal{H}_{2t}^{f}+\tfrac{t}{12}-\tfrac{2}{3}\log(2t)).

Since
t\in[T^{-1},T]
and we allow
\mathrm{C}
to be dependent on
T
, it is suffices to show
\mathbb{P}(|\mathfrak{h}_{t}^{f}|\geq s)\leq\mathrm{C}\exp(-\tfrac{1}{\mathrm{C}}s^{3/2})
. This is essentially proved in
[
7
]
for narrow wedge initial data, Brownian initial data, and for certain functional initial data belonging to
\mathbf{Hyp}
class in their Definition 1.1. Their
\mathbf{Hyp}
class is adapted to the KPZ fixed point scaling and consists of conditions on
g(y):=t^{-1/3}f((2t)^{2/3}y)
. We briefly explain how our
\mathbf{Hyp}_{1}(\mathfrak{p})
class is contained in their class of initial data. Assume
f
to be functional initial data. Recall the condition
3a
from Definition
1.1
. As
t\in[T^{-1},T]
and
\delta>0
, one can get a constant
A=A(\lambda,\delta,T)
so that

g(y)\leq\lambda t^{-1/3}(1+|(2t)^{2/3}y|)^{2-\delta}\leq A+\tfrac{1}{2\cdot 2^{2/3}}y^{2}.

This verifies Eq. (1.2) in
[
7
]
for
C\mapsto A
and
\nu\mapsto\frac{1}{2}
.
Furthermore due to condition
3b
, there must exist an interval
\mathcal{I}\subset[-(2T)^{2/3}M,(2T)^{2/3}M]
with size at least
(2T)^{2/3}\theta
where
g(y)\geq-\kappa T^{-1/3}
. This verifies Eq. (1.3) in
[
7
]
for
\theta\mapsto(2T)^{2/3}\theta,\kappa\mapsto\kappa T^{-1/3},
and
M\mapsto(2T)^{2/3}M.

Thus,
g\in\mathbf{Hyp}(A,\frac{1}{2},(2T)^{2/3}M,(2T)^{2/3}\theta,-\kappa T^{-1/3})
according to Definition 1.1 in
[
7
]
. Since we allow
\mathrm{C}
to be dependent on
T
, Theorem 1.2 and Theorem 1.4 in
[
7
]
leads to the desired conclusion.
∎

Proof of Theorem
1.2
.

Fix
\mathfrak{p}\in(0,\infty)^{5}
and fix any
f\in\mathbf{Hyp}_{1}(\mathfrak{p})
. For simplicity, we drop the
f
from the notation and write
\mathcal{H}_{t}
and
\mathcal{Z}_{t}
for
\mathcal{H}_{t}^{f}
and
\mathcal{Z}_{t}^{f}
respectively. We obtain a
\mathfrak{q}\in((0,\infty)\times(0,1])^{\mathbb{N}}
from Lemma
2.2
so that
\mathbf{Hyp}_{1}(\mathfrak{p})\subset\mathbf{Hyp}_{2}(\mathfrak{q})
. Given any
k\geq 2
,
\beta\in(0,\frac{2}{5})
and
T>1
we will show that there exists a probability space containing
\mathcal{H}_{t}
as well as a fractional Brownian motion
\mathfrak{B}_{t}
with index
\frac{1}{4}
and a constant
\mathrm{C}>0
depending on
k,\beta,T
such that

(4.1)

\displaystyle\sup_{s,t\in[T^{-1},T]}\mathbb{E}\left[\left|\mathcal{H}_{s}-\mathcal{H}_{t}-(2/\pi)^{1/4}(\mathfrak{B}_{s}-\mathfrak{B}_{t})\right|^{k}\right]\leq\mathrm{C}|s-t|^{k\beta}.

Then Theorem
1.2
follows from Kolmogorov’s continuity theorem.

Note that the one point exponential tail estimates from Proposition
4.1
ensures moments of
\mathbb{E}[|\mathcal{H}_{s}|^{k}+|\mathcal{H}_{t}|^{k}]
are uniformly bounded as
s,t
varies on
[T^{-1},T]
. By properties of fractional Brownian motion,
\mathbb{E}[|\mathfrak{B}_{s}-\mathfrak{B}_{t}|^{k}]
is uniformly bounded as
s,t
varies on
[T^{-1},T]
. Thus by Minkowski’s inequality the supremum on the left-hand side of (
4.1
) is finite. Thus, whenever
|s-t|
is bounded away from zero, (
4.1
) follows by choosing
\mathrm{C}
large enough. So, without loss of generality we may assume
s=t+\varepsilon
with
\varepsilon\in(0,\varepsilon_{0})
where
\varepsilon_{0}
is small enough. We divide the rest of the proof into two steps.

Step 1.
In this step we claim that

(4.2)

\displaystyle\sup_{t\in[T^{-1},T]}\varepsilon^{-\frac{k}{4}}\mathbb{E}[\left|\mathcal{H}_{t+\varepsilon}-\mathcal{H}_{t}|^{k}\right]<\infty,

for all
\varepsilon\leq\varepsilon_{0}
where
\varepsilon_{0}=\varepsilon_{0}(\beta,\mathfrak{q},k,T)>0
is chosen from Theorem
1.5
. Observe that

|x|^{k}\leq(e^{|x|}-1)^{k}\leq|e^{-x}-1|^{k}+|e^{x}-1|^{k}.

Take
x=\mathcal{H}_{t+\varepsilon}-\mathcal{H}_{t}
and then divide the aforementioned inequality by
\varepsilon^{\frac{k}{4}}
to get

\displaystyle\varepsilon^{-\frac{k}{4}}|\mathcal{H}_{t+\varepsilon}-\mathcal{H}_{t}|^{k}

\displaystyle\leq\mathcal{Z}_{t+\varepsilon}^{-k}\cdot\varepsilon^{-\frac{k}{4}}|\mathcal{Z}_{t+\varepsilon}-\mathcal{Z}_{t}|^{k}+[\mathcal{Z}_{t}]^{-k}\cdot\varepsilon^{-\frac{k}{4}}|\mathcal{Z}_{t+\varepsilon}-\mathcal{Z}_{t}|^{k}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2}\left([\mathcal{Z}_{t+\varepsilon}]^{-2k}+\varepsilon^{-\frac{k}{2}}|\mathcal{Z}_{t+\varepsilon}-\mathcal{Z}_{t}|^{2k}\right)+\frac{1}{2}\left([\mathcal{Z}_{t}]^{-2k}+\varepsilon^{-\frac{k}{2}}|\mathcal{Z}_{t+\varepsilon}-\mathcal{Z}_{t}|^{2k}\right)

(4.3)

\displaystyle=\varepsilon^{-\frac{k}{2}}|\mathcal{Z}_{t+\varepsilon}-\mathcal{Z}_{t}|^{2k}+\frac{1}{2}\left([\mathcal{Z}_{t+\varepsilon}]^{-2k}+[\mathcal{Z}_{t}]^{-2k}\right),

where the inequality in the second line is an application of the
ab\leq\frac{1}{2}(a^{2}+b^{2})
inequality applied with
a=\mathcal{Z}_{t+\varepsilon}^{-k}
and
b=\varepsilon^{-\frac{k}{4}}|\mathcal{Z}_{t+\varepsilon}-\mathcal{Z}_{t}|^{k}
and with
a=\mathcal{Z}_{t}^{-k}
and
b=\varepsilon^{-\frac{k}{4}}|\mathcal{Z}_{t+\varepsilon}-\mathcal{Z}_{t}|^{k}
. Set

(4.4)

\displaystyle\Theta_{t,\varepsilon}:=\mathcal{Z}_{t+\varepsilon}-\mathcal{Z}_{t}-(2/\pi)^{1/4}\mathcal{Z}_{t}(\mathfrak{B}_{t+\varepsilon}-\mathfrak{B}_{t}),

so that
\mathcal{Z}_{t+\varepsilon}-\mathcal{Z}_{t}=\Theta_{t,\varepsilon}+(2/\pi)^{\frac{1}{4}}\mathcal{Z}_{t}(\mathfrak{B}_{t+\varepsilon}-\mathfrak{B}_{t})
. Using the elementary inequality
|a+b|^{2k}\leq 2^{2k-1}(a^{2k}+b^{2k})
with
a=\Theta_{t,\varepsilon}
and
b=(2/\pi)^{1/4}\mathcal{Z}_{t}(\mathfrak{B}_{t+\varepsilon}-\mathfrak{B}_{t})
we get that

\displaystyle|\mathcal{Z}_{t+\varepsilon}-\mathcal{Z}_{t}|^{2k}\leq\mathrm{C}\left[|\Theta_{t,\varepsilon}|^{2k}+\mathcal{Z}_{t}^{2k}|\mathfrak{B}_{t+\varepsilon}-\mathfrak{B}_{t}|^{2k}\right].

Plugging this inequality back in r.h.s. of (
4.3
) we get

r.h.s. of (
4.3
)

\displaystyle\leq\mathrm{C}\varepsilon^{-\frac{k}{2}}\left[|\Theta_{t,\varepsilon}|^{2k}+\mathcal{Z}_{t}^{2k}|\mathfrak{B}_{t+\varepsilon}-\mathfrak{B}_{t}|^{2k}\right]+\frac{1}{2}\left(\mathcal{Z}_{t+\varepsilon}^{-2k}+\mathcal{Z}_{t}^{-2k}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\mathrm{C}\varepsilon^{-\frac{k}{2}}|\Theta_{t,\varepsilon}|^{2k}+\mathrm{C}\left[\mathcal{Z}_{t}^{4k}+\varepsilon^{-k}|\mathfrak{B}_{t+\varepsilon}-\mathfrak{B}_{t}|^{4k}\right]+\frac{1}{2}\left(\mathcal{Z}_{t+\varepsilon}^{-2k}+\mathcal{Z}_{t}^{-2k}\right)

where the last inequality is another application of
ab\leq\frac{1}{2}(a^{2}+b^{2})
inequality with
a=\mathcal{Z}_{t}^{2k}
and
b=\varepsilon^{-k/2}|\mathfrak{B}_{t+\varepsilon}-\mathfrak{B}_{t}|^{2k}
. Taking expectations and utilizing (
1.5
) with
\beta=\frac{1}{3}<\frac{2}{5}
(for
\varepsilon\leq\varepsilon_{0}
) and the fact that
\varepsilon^{-\frac{1}{4}}(\mathfrak{B}_{t+\varepsilon}-\mathfrak{B}_{t})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle d}}{{=}}Z\sim N(0,1)
we get

\displaystyle\sup_{t\in[T^{-1},T]}\varepsilon^{-\frac{k}{4}}\mathbb{E}[\left|\mathcal{H}_{t+\varepsilon}-\mathcal{H}_{t}|^{k}\right]\leq\mathrm{C}\left[\varepsilon^{-\frac{k}{2}+\frac{2k}{3}}+\mathbb{E}[|\mathcal{Z}_{t}|^{4k}]+\sup_{t\in[T^{-1},T+1]}\mathbb{E}\left([\mathcal{Z}_{t}]^{-2k}+[\mathcal{Z}_{t}]^{4k}\right)\right],

for all
\varepsilon\in(0,\varepsilon_{0})
and for some constant
\mathrm{C}>0
depending on
\mathfrak{q}_{k},k,T
. The moments of
\mathcal{Z}_{t}
(both negative and positive) can be bounded uniformly on
t\in[T^{-1},T]
using the uniform one point upper and lower tail bounds for
\mathcal{H}_{t}=\log\mathcal{Z}_{t}
for
t\in[T^{-1},T]
from Proposition
4.1
. Thus the right hand side of above equation is finite uniformly in
\varepsilon\in(0,\varepsilon_{0})
. This completes our work in this step.

Step 2.
In this step we show that

(4.5)

\displaystyle\sup_{t\in[T^{-1},T]}\varepsilon^{-\frac{k}{2}}\mathbb{E}\left[\left|\frac{\mathcal{Z}_{t+\varepsilon}}{\mathcal{Z}_{t}}-1-\mathcal{H}_{t+\varepsilon}+\mathcal{H}_{t}\right|^{k}\right]<\infty,

for all
\varepsilon\leq\varepsilon_{0}
where
\varepsilon_{0}=\varepsilon_{0}(\beta,\mathfrak{q},2k,T)>0
is chosen from Theorem
1.5
. Assuming (
4.5
), proof of (
4.1
) for
s=t+\varepsilon
can be shown as follows.

First observe that

(4.6)

\displaystyle\varepsilon^{-\beta}\left[\mathcal{H}_{t+\varepsilon}-\mathcal{H}_{t}-(2/\pi)^{\frac{1}{4}}(\mathfrak{B}_{t+\varepsilon}-\mathfrak{B}_{t})\right]

\displaystyle=\varepsilon^{-\beta}\left[\mathcal{H}_{t+\varepsilon}-\mathcal{H}_{t}-\frac{\mathcal{Z}_{t+\varepsilon}}{\mathcal{Z}_{t}}+1\right]+\varepsilon^{-\beta}\left[\frac{\mathcal{Z}_{t+\varepsilon}}{\mathcal{Z}_{t}}-1-(2/\pi)^{\frac{1}{4}}(\mathfrak{B}_{t+\varepsilon}-\mathfrak{B}_{t})\right]

\displaystyle=\varepsilon^{-\beta}\left[\mathcal{H}_{t+\varepsilon}-\mathcal{H}_{t}-\frac{\mathcal{Z}_{t+\varepsilon}}{\mathcal{Z}_{t}}+1\right]+\mathcal{Z}_{t}^{-1}\cdot\varepsilon^{-\beta}\Theta_{t,\varepsilon}

where
\Theta_{t,\varepsilon}
is defined in (
4.4
). Applying the elementary inequality
|a+bc|^{k}\leq 2^{k-1}|a|^{k}+2^{k-2}b^{2k}+2^{k-2}c^{2k}
with

\displaystyle a=\varepsilon^{-\beta}\left[\mathcal{H}_{t+\varepsilon}-\mathcal{H}_{t}-\frac{\mathcal{Z}_{t+\varepsilon}}{\mathcal{Z}_{t}}+1\right],\quad b=\mathcal{Z}_{t}^{-1},\quad c=\varepsilon^{-\beta}\Theta_{t,\varepsilon},

in view of the identity in (
4.6
), we get that

\displaystyle\varepsilon^{-k\beta}\mathbb{E}\left[\big|\mathcal{H}_{t+\varepsilon}-\mathcal{H}_{t}-(2/\pi)^{1/4}(\mathfrak{B}_{t+\varepsilon}-\mathfrak{B}_{t})\big|^{k}\right]

\displaystyle\leq\mathrm{C}\varepsilon^{-k\beta}\mathbb{E}\left[\big|\frac{\mathcal{Z}_{t+\varepsilon}}{\mathcal{Z}_{t}}-1-\mathcal{H}_{t+\varepsilon}+\mathcal{H}_{t}\big|^{k}\right]

\displaystyle\hskip 28.45274pt+\mathrm{C}\mathbb{E}[\mathcal{Z}_{t}^{-2k}]+\mathrm{C}\varepsilon^{-2k\beta}\mathbb{E}[|\Theta_{t,\varepsilon}|^{2k}].

Each of the three expectations appearing on r.h.s. of the above equation is uniformly bounded by (
4.5
), tail estimates from Proposition
4.1
and Theorem
1.5
respectively. This verifies (
4.1
) modulo (
4.5
). Hence it suffices to show (
4.5
). Towards this end, first observe that

|e^{x}-1-x|^{k}\leq x^{2k}e^{k|x|}\leq x^{2k}(e^{-x}+e^{x})^{k}.

Take
x=\mathcal{H}_{t+\varepsilon}-\mathcal{H}_{t}
and then divide the aforementioned inequality by
\varepsilon^{\frac{k}{2}}
to get

(4.7)

\displaystyle\varepsilon^{-\frac{k}{2}}\left|\frac{\mathcal{Z}_{t+\varepsilon}}{\mathcal{Z}_{t}}-1-\mathcal{H}_{t+\varepsilon}+\mathcal{H}_{t}\right|^{k}

\displaystyle\leq\varepsilon^{-\frac{k}{2}}\left|\mathcal{H}_{t+\varepsilon}-\mathcal{H}_{t}\right|^{2k}\left|\frac{\mathcal{Z}_{t}}{\mathcal{Z}_{t+\varepsilon}}+\frac{\mathcal{Z}_{t+\varepsilon}}{\mathcal{Z}_{t}}\right|^{k}

\displaystyle\leq\varepsilon^{-\frac{k}{2}}\left|\mathcal{H}_{t+\varepsilon}-\mathcal{H}_{t}\right|^{2k}\mathcal{Z}_{t+\varepsilon}^{-k}\mathcal{Z}_{t}^{-k}|\mathcal{Z}_{t}^{2}+\mathcal{Z}_{t+\varepsilon}^{2}|^{k}.

Using the AM-GM inequality we may bound the product of above four terms by sum of fourth powers of each term. This gives us

r.h.s. of (
4.7
)

\displaystyle\leq\mathrm{C}\left[\varepsilon^{-2k}\left|\mathcal{H}_{t+\varepsilon}-\mathcal{H}_{t}\right|^{8k}+\mathcal{Z}_{t+\varepsilon}^{-4k}+\mathcal{Z}_{t}^{-4k}+|\mathcal{Z}_{t}^{2}+\mathcal{Z}_{t+\varepsilon}^{2}|^{4k}\right]

\displaystyle\leq\mathrm{C}\left[\varepsilon^{-2k}\left|\mathcal{H}_{t+\varepsilon}-\mathcal{H}_{t}\right|^{8k}+\mathcal{Z}_{t+\varepsilon}^{-4k}+\mathcal{Z}_{t}^{-4k}+\mathcal{Z}_{t}^{8k}+\mathcal{Z}_{t+\varepsilon}^{8k}\right],

where the last inequality follows by noting
|a^{2}+b^{2}|^{4k}\leq 2^{4k-1}(a^{8k}+b^{8k})
(with
a=\mathcal{Z}_{t}^{2}
and
b=\mathcal{Z}_{t+\varepsilon}^{2})
.
The expectation of the first term on the right hand side of the above equation is uniformly bounded via (
4.2
). The moments of
\mathcal{Z}_{r}
(both negative and positive) can be bounded uniformly on
r\in[T^{-1},T]
using the uniform one point upper and lower tail bounds for
\mathcal{H}_{r}=\log\mathcal{Z}_{r}
for
r\in[T^{-1},T]
from Proposition
4.1
. Thus the expectation of the right hand side of the above equation is bounded uniformly in
\varepsilon\in(0,\varepsilon_{0})
and
t\in[T^{-1},T]
. This completes the proof.
∎

Proof of Theorem
1.4
.

We appeal to Theorem
1.2
to get a probability space containing the KPZ temporal process
\mathcal{H}_{t}^{f}
and fractional Brownian motion
\mathfrak{B}_{t}
such that
\mathcal{H}_{t}^{f}-(2/\pi)^{1/4}\mathfrak{B}_{t}
is
\frac{2}{5}^{-}
Hölder continuous. Hence it is enough to understand the increment properties of
\mathfrak{B}_{t}
instead. Using (
3.1
) it follows that for
t_{i}\neq t_{j}
the pairwise covariance

\varepsilon^{-1/2}\operatorname{Cov}(\mathfrak{B}_{t_{i}+\varepsilon}-\mathfrak{B}_{t_{i}},\mathfrak{B}_{t_{j}+\varepsilon}-\mathfrak{B}_{t_{j}})\to 0

as
\varepsilon\downarrow 0
. This leads to the proof of Part (1) of Theorem
1.4
. The quartic variation and the Law of iterated logarithm for
\mathfrak{B}_{t}
follows from Eq 1 in
[
31
]
(see also
[
51
]
) and Theorem 1.1 (C) in
[
47
]
respectively. Modulus of continuity and Hausdorff dimension for
\mathfrak{B}_{t}
follows from Theorem 2.1 and Theorem 5.3 in
[
42
]
.
∎

A new approach for stability analysis of 1 − 1- D wave equation with time delay
Shijie Zhou, Hongyinping Feng, Zhiqiang Wang

Abstract




I
Introduction

The time delay is ubiquitous in many engineering control systems. Both
actuator delay
and sensor delay may change not only the performance
but also cause damages to the stability of the systems.
Since the time delay is an infinite-dimensional dynamics itself, the
time delay in observation and control presents great
challenge in distributed parameter control systems
Logemann
.
It is well known that a small
time delay in a stabilizing boundary output feedback could destabilize the system

Datko86
;
Datko88
;
Datko93
. This implies that
some controllers of PDEs may become practically not implementable in the
presence of the time delay.
It is therefore very important to consider the time delay
in the process of the controller designs.

In the past a few decades, many efforts have been made for the stabilization and stability analysis of PDEs
with time delay. The seminal result on feedback stabilization for one-dimensional wave equation was considered in
Datko86

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}z_{tt}(x,t)=z_{xx}(x,t),x\in(0,1),t>0,\\
z(0,t)=0,\\
z_{x}(1,t)=u(t),t>0,\\
y(t)=z_{t}(1,t),t>0,\end{array}\right.

(1)

where
u(t)
is the control input and
y(t)
the output. When the time delay is absent, the stabilization of (
1
) is trivial
that a propositional feedback
u(t)=-kz_{t}(1,t)
with
k>0
can stabilize exponentially the system.
However, this feedback is not robust to the input delay. In other word,
system

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}z_{tt}(x,t)=z_{xx}(x,t),x\in(0,1),t>0,\\
z(0,t)=0,\\
z_{x}(1,t)=-kz_{t}(1,t-\tau),k>0,t\geq 0\end{array}\right.

(2)

is always unstable as indicated in
Datko86
, no matter how small the time delay
\tau
is. A typical study was first made in
XuGQ2006delay
where stabilization for
system (
1
) with input delay was considered by
regarding the time delay as
a dynamics represented by one-dimensional transport equation.
The controller in
XuGQ2006delay
is
split into two parts:
u(t)=-k\mu z_{t}(1,t)-k(1-\mu)z_{t}(1,t-\tau)
and
the time delay free part cannot be zero. The same approach was also used in
Sage1
;
Sage2
.
The backstepping method is an another powerful tool to compensate the
time delay, which has been applied to finite-dimensional systems with actuator and sensor delays
in
Krstic2008scl
. In monograph

(
Krsticdelaybook
, Chapter 19)
, stabilization for an anti-stable wave equation
with input delay was realized by the backstepping method.
Although the time delay can be compensated in the infinite-dimensional actuator,
the controller, as a full state feedback, was very complicated in
(
Krsticdelaybook
, Chapter 19)
.
When the output is suffered from a time delay, an observer/predictor
was proposed in
GuoXuCZDelay2012
, which is systematical
and has been extended recently to the abstract systems in

MeiZD
and
GuoMEiZDTAC
. But in general, the
convergence in
MeiZD
;
GuoMEiZDTAC
is valid only for smooth initial states.
Although the convergence of
GuoXuCZDelay2012
is true for general initial states,
the controller seems still not straightforward. When
the input delay equals even multiples of the wave propagation time, the system can be
stabilized exponentially by direct feedback discussed in
Wangdelay2011
.
In
Gugat
, a specific time delay in boundary observation can be used to stabilize the wave equation.
In
Gugat2
, a switching delay feedback can be used to stabilize a vibrating string.
In
FSIAM2
and
FengTAC
, a non-collocated feedback was
proposed to cope with the output delay being equal to one.

In this paper, we
consider
a new controller to stabilize system (
1
)
with input delay. By considering the time delay dynamic as a first order transport equation,
the problem is converted into boundary control of a cascaded PDE system. By virtue of
boundary stabilization for first order hyperbolic systems, we are able to design the
feedback control.
The idea was inspired by
(
WangZQ2013
, Lemma 3.5)
where
the following system of transport equation

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\tau w_{t}(x,t)+w_{x}(x,t)=0,\\
w(0,t)=kw(1,t)+{(k-1)\mu}\int_{0}^{1}w(x,t){\rm d}x\end{array}\right.

(3)

is exponentially stable
in the state space
L^{2}(0,1)
if and only if
|k|<1
and
\mu>-1
.
This inspires us that the boundary feedback
u(t)=-c_{1}w(1,t)-c_{2}\int_{0}^{1}w(x,t){\rm d}x
with
c_{1},c_{2}\in\mathbb{R}

may compensate the time delay.

Enlightened by
Wangdelay2011
, we use the semigroup approach to explain the well-posedness of the system and the Riesz basis approach to get the dynamical behavior of the system in terms of vibrating frequencies.
The major contribution of our paper is to develop a new method for spectral analysis.
We derive sufficient and necessary conditions for the feedback gain and time delay which guarantee the exponential stability of the closed-loop system. Comparing with similar conditions developed in
Wangdelay2011
,
we get
the explicit term of the stability region of
c
for different values of
\tau
and from this
we easily obtain the shrink of the stability region as
\tau
increases.
Another main advantage of the proposed method lies in the
investigation of the robustness to a small perturbation in time delay in high frequencies.
Actually, we prove that any small perturbation of
\epsilon\in\mathbb{R}
in time delay will excite a high frequency mode (i.e., a mode with frequency on the order of
\mathcal{O}(\dfrac{1}{|\epsilon|})
as
\epsilon\to 0
).
In this way, we
give an intrisic mathematical interpretation of the destabilizing effect of arbitrarily small time delays and hence verify the judgement (or conjecture) given in
(
Datko86
, Page 5, remark)
. This gives a mathematical explanation why numerical experiments usually do not demonstrate the non-robustness when a small perturbation is added to the time delay (See Remark
12
).

We proceed as follows. In Section
II
, we present the model formulation, well-posedness of the closed-loop system and fundamental spectral analysis. In Section
III
, we derive sufficient and necessary conditions for the feedback gain and time delay which guarantee the exponential stability of the closed-loop system. In Section
IV
, we investigate the robustness to a small perturbation in time delay in high frequencies. In Section
, we present some numerical simulations for illustration.

II
Preliminaries

II.1
Model formulation

We consider the
stabilization of the following wave equation with time delay in the control:

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}z_{tt}(x,t)=z_{xx}(x,t),\\
z(0,t)=0,\\
z_{x}(1,t)=u(t-\tau),\\
y(t)=z_{t}(1,t),{t\geq 0,}\end{array}\right.

(4)

where
u(t)
is the control input,
y(t)
is the output, and
\tau
is the
time delay. For notational simplicity, we omit in equations hereafter the
obvious domains for both time
t
and spatial variable
x
when
there is no confusion.
Set
w(x,t)=u(t-\tau x)
. Then, the time delay system
(
4
) is written as

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}z_{tt}(x,t)=z_{xx}(x,t),\\
z(0,t)=0,\\
z_{x}(1,t)=w(1,t),\\
\tau w_{t}(x,t)+w_{x}(x,t)=0,\\
w(0,t)=u(t),\\
y(t)=z_{t}(1,t),\end{array}\right.

(5)

which is a cascaded PDE system without explicitly the time delay.
Inspired from (
3
), a feedback control is designed as

\left.\begin{array}[]{l}u(t)=-c_{1}w(1,t)-c_{2}z_{t}(1,t),\end{array}\right.

(6)

where
c_{1},c_{2}\in\mathbb{R}
are tuning parameters. Since we only consider the
output feedback, the integral term
\int_{0}^{1}w(x,t){\rm d}x
in (
3
) is ignored.
The first term of (
6
) is damper for the transport equation
and the second term is a direct proportional feedback for wave equation without time delay. When
c_{1}=0
, the system becomes (
2
).
Under the feedback (
6
), the closed-loop of system (
5
) reads

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}z_{tt}(x,t)=z_{xx}(x,t),\\
z(0,t)=0,\\
z_{x}(1,t)=w(1,t),\\
\tau w_{t}(x,t)+w_{x}(x,t)=0,\\
w(0,t)=kz_{t}(1,t).\end{array}\right.

(7)

II.2
Well-posedness of system (
7
)

We consider system (
7
) in the state space

\mathcal{X}=H^{1}_{L}(0,1)\times(L^{2}(0,1))^{2},

(8)

where

H^{1}_{L}(0,1)=\{f\in H^{1}(0,1)\ |\ f(0)=0\}
. The inner product in
\mathcal{X}
is defined by

\langle(f_{1},g_{1},h_{1}),(f_{2},g_{2},h_{2})\rangle_{\mathcal{X}}=\int_{0}^{1}[f_{1}^{\prime}(x)\overline{f_{2}^{\prime}(x)}+g_{1}(x)\overline{g_{2}(x)}+h_{1}(x)\overline{h_{2}(x)}]{\rm d}x

(9)

for
(f_{i},g_{i},h_{i})\in\mathcal{X},\ i=1,2
.
System (
7
) can be written as an evolutionary equation in
\mathcal{X}
:

\dfrac{\rm d}{{\rm d}t}(z(\cdot,t),z_{t}(\cdot,t),w(\cdot,t))=\mathscr{A}(z(\cdot,t),z_{t}(\cdot,t),w(\cdot,t)),

(10)

where the operator
\mathscr{A}:D(\mathscr{A})\subset\mathcal{X}\to\mathcal{X}
is defined by

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\mathscr{A}(f,g,h)=(g,f^{\prime\prime},-\tau^{-1}h^{\prime}),\forall(f,g,h)\in D(\mathscr{A}),\\
D(\mathscr{A})=\left\{(f,g,h)\ |\ f\in H^{2}(0,1),g,h\in H^{1}(0,1),f(0)=g(0)=0,\right.\\
\hskip 56.9055pt\left.f^{\prime}(1)=h(1),\ h(0)=-c_{1}h(1)-c_{2}g(1)\right\}.\end{array}\right.

(11)

Lemma 1

Let the operator
\mathscr{A}
be defined by (
11
). Then
\mathscr{A}^{-1}
exists and is compact. Hence,
\sigma(\mathscr{A})
consists of isolated eigenvalues multiplicity only.

Proof
For any
(\hat{f},\hat{g},\hat{h})\in\mathcal{X}
, we solve the equation
\mathscr{A}(f,g,h)=(\hat{f},\hat{g},\hat{h})
to get

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}g=\hat{f},\\
f(x)=f^{\prime}(1)x-\int_{0}^{x}\int_{\alpha}^{1}\hat{g}(s){\rm d}s,\ f^{\prime}(1)=-\frac{1}{1+c}\left(c\hat{f}(1)+\tau\int_{0}^{1}\hat{h}(s){\rm d}s\right),\\
h(x)=h(0)-\tau\int_{0}^{x}\hat{h}(s){\rm d}s,\ h(0)=\frac{c}{1+c}\left(\tau\int_{0}^{1}\hat{h}(s){\rm d}s-\hat{f}(1)\right),\end{array}\right.

(12)

which implies, by the Sobolev trace-embedding, that
\mathscr{A}^{-1}

exists and is compact in
\mathcal{X}
. So

\sigma(\mathscr{A})
consists of isolated eigenvalues of finite algebraic multiplicity.

Theorem 2

Suppose that

where

(13)

For

II.3
Spectral analysis

Let us now consider the eigenvalue problem of
\mathscr{A}
.

Let us now consider the eigenvalue problem of
\mathscr{A}
,
\mathscr{A}(f,g,h)=\lambda(f,g,h)
, then

g=\lambda f
and

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}f^{\prime\prime}=\lambda^{2}f,h^{\prime}=-\tau\lambda h,\\
f(0)=0,f^{\prime}(1)=h(1),\\
h(0)=-c_{1}h(1)-c_{2}\lambda f(1).\end{array}\right.

(23)

When
\lambda=0
, the solution of (
23
) is found to be

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}f(x)=bx,\\
h(x)=b,\end{array}\right.

(24)

where the constant
b
satisfies that
b=-c_{1}b
. Thus, the equation has a nontrivial solution if and only if
c_{1}=-1
. When
c_{1}=-1
, the corresponding eigenfunction

(f_{0},g_{0},h_{0})
is given by

f_{0}(x)=x,~~g_{0}(x)=1,~~h_{0}(x)=1.

When
\lambda\neq 0
, the solution of (
23
) is found to be

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}f(x)=a{\rm e}^{\lambda x}-a{\rm e}^{-\lambda x}=2a\sinh\lambda x,\\
h(x)=b{\rm e}^{-\tau\lambda x},\end{array}\right.

(25)

where
a
and
b
are constants that satisfy

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}c_{2}\lambda\left({\rm e}^{\lambda}-{\rm e}^{-\lambda}\right)a+\left(1+c_{1}{\rm e}^{-\tau\lambda}\right)b=0,\\
\lambda\left({\rm e}^{\lambda}+{\rm e}^{-\lambda}\right)a-{\rm e}^{-\tau\lambda}b=0.\end{array}\right.

(26)

The characteristic determinant of (
26
)
is

\begin{array}[]{l}\Delta(\lambda)=\left|\begin{array}[]{cc}c_{2}\lambda\left({\rm e}^{\lambda}-{\rm e}^{-\lambda}\right)&1+c_{1}{\rm e}^{-\tau\lambda}\\
\lambda\left({\rm e}^{\lambda}+{\rm e}^{-\lambda}\right)&-{\rm e}^{-\tau\lambda}\end{array}\right|=-\lambda\cosh\lambda(1+c_{1}{\rm e}^{-\lambda\tau})-c_{2}\lambda\sinh\lambda{\rm e}^{-\lambda\tau}.\end{array}

(27)

Thus, the equation has a nontrival solution if and only if

\tilde{\Delta}(\lambda)\triangleq-\cosh\lambda(1+c_{1}{\rm e}^{-\lambda\tau})-c_{2}\sinh\lambda{\rm e}^{-\lambda\tau}=0.

(28)

Furthermore, we observe that when
c_{1}=-1
,
\lambda=0
is also a root for
\tilde{\Delta}(\lambda)=0
, then we conclude that

\sigma({\mathscr{A}})=\sigma_{p}(\mathscr{A})=\{\lambda\in\mathbb{C}|\tilde{\Delta}(\lambda)=0\}.

Therefore, each
\lambda\in\sigma(\mathscr{A})\backslash\{0\}
is geometrically simple, and the corresponding eigenfunction

(f_{\lambda},g_{\lambda},h_{\lambda})
is given by

f_{\lambda}(x)={\rm e}^{-\tau\lambda}\sinh\lambda x,~~g_{\lambda}(x)=\lambda{\rm e}^{-\tau\lambda}\sinh\lambda x,~~h_{\lambda}(x)=\lambda\cosh\lambda{\rm e}^{-\tau\lambda x}.

For
\lambda=0,c_{1}=-1
, the corresponding eigenfunction

(f_{0},g_{0},h_{0})
is given by

f_{0}(x)=x,~~g_{0}(x)=1,~~h_{0}(x)=1.

For any
\lambda\in\rho(\mathscr{A})
, we have following lemma on the expression of the resolvent operator.

Wedge-Lifted Codes
Jabari Hastings, Amy Kanne, Ray Li, Mary Wootters

Abstract
We define wedge-lifted codes , a variant of lifted codes, and we study their locality properties. We show that (taking the trace of) wedge-lifted codes yields binary codes with the t t -disjoint repair property ( t t -DRGP). When t = N 1 / 2 ​ d t=N^{1/2d} , where N N is the block length of the code and d ≥ 2 d\geq 2 is any integer, our codes give improved trade-offs between redundancy and locality among binary codes.

Introduction

In this work, we define and study
Wedge-Lifted Codes
, and show they yield improved binary error correcting codes for a notion of locality known as the
t
-disjoint repair group property.

An
error correcting code
(or simply
code
)
\mathcal{C}\subset\Sigma^{N}
is a set of strings of a fixed
length

N
over an
alphabet

\Sigma
.
If
\Sigma=\{0,1\}
,
\mathcal{C}
is called a
binary

We measure the quality of a code by the

, denoted
K^{\perp}
, defined as
K^{\perp}=N-K
, where
K=\log_{|\Sigma|}|\mathcal{C}|
is the
dimension
of the code.
It is desirable for codes to be larger, or equivalently to have less redundancy.

In this work we are interested in constructing better (less redundant) binary codes with
locality
.
There are several notions of locality in this literature, but informally a code exhibits locality if we can correct one or a small number of erasures by looking only
locally at a few other symbols of the codeword.
In this work we construct codes with a notion of locality known as the
t
-disjoint repair group property (
t
-DRGP).

Definition
\thetheorem
.

A code
\mathcal{C}\subseteq\Sigma^{N}
has the
t-
disjoint repair group property
(
t
-DRGP) if for every
i\in[N]
, there is a collection of
t
disjoint subsets
S_{1},\ldots,S_{t}\subseteq[N]\setminus\{i\}
and functions
f_{1},\ldots,f_{t}
so that for all
c\in\mathcal{C}
and
j\in[t]
,
f_{j}\left(c|_{S_{j}}\right)=c_{i}
.

Codes with the
t
-DRGP are motivated by distributed storage, where one desires efficient recovery from a few erasures (see surveys
[
RSG+13
,
Ska18
]
).
Codes with the
t
-DRGP are also relevant to
private information retrieval
(PIR) in cryptography, as all codes (with a systematic encoding) with the
t
-DRGP also form
(t+1)
-PIR
codes, a notion defined in
[
FVY15
]
for
(t+1)
-server private information retrieval. Further, when
t=\Omega(N)
is large, codes with the
t
-DRGP are equivalent to
locally correctable codes

[
KT00
,
Woo10
]
.

Previously the best constructions for codes with the disjoint repair group property were given by lifted multiplicity codes
[
LW19
,
HPP+20
]
(see also
[
Wu15
]
), but these codes have very large alphabet sizes, which is undesirable from the perspective of distributed storage and PIR.
Hence, a natural question, explicitly asked in
[
LW19
]
, is, what are the best constructions of
binary
codes with the
t
-DRGP?
Our work makes progress on this question by giving new constructions of binary codes with the
t
-DRGP with the best known redundancy for some values of
t
.

{theorem}

For positive integers

and infinitely many

t=N^{1/2d}

N

.
As all codes with the

Our Wedge-Lifted Codes have relatively small alphabet size (compared to previous work using lifted multiplicity codes, for example), and so we are able to use them to obtain good binary codes by taking the coordinate-wise trace over

It is an interesting question whether the construction of

Other notions of locality

The
t
-DRGP is closely related to other notions of locality.
Any linear code with the
t
-DRGP is by definition a
(t+1)
-private information retrieval (PIR) code.
In PIR codes, one only needs to recover the information symbols, rather than all codeword symbols
[
FVY15
,
BE16
,
AY19
]
.
Our constructions are linear and therefore immediately give constructions of
(t+1)
-PIR codes.
The
t
-DRGP is also closely related to
batch codes
, which generalize PIR codes
[
IKOS04
,
RSDG16
,
AY19
,
HPPV20
]
.
The
t
-DRGP is also related to
locally correctable codes
(LCCs)
[
GKS13
,
KSY14
,
HOW15
,
KMRS16
]
and
locally decodable codes
(LDCs)
[
KT00
,
Efr12
]
.
Any code of length
N
has the
\Omega(N)
-DRGP if and only if it is a constant query LCC, and any code of length
N
is an
\Omega(N)
-PIR code if and only if it is a constant-query LDC.
This equivalence between LCCs/LDCs and disjoint repair groups was in fact used to prove lower bounds for LCCs/LDCs
[
KT00
,
Woo10
]
.
Towards applications in distributed storage, a generalization of the

-DRGP called

have also been studied
[

,
RPDV14
,
TB14
,
TBF16
]
, where the disjoint repair groups are additionally constrained to have a bounded size.

Our approach

The best known constructions of codes
[
GKS13
,
FGW17
,
LW19
,
HPP+20
]
with the DRGP leverage an idea called
lifting

[
GKS13
]
.
The basic idea of lifting is to improve the Reed-Muller Code, which has some locality properties, by relaxing the construction so that the locality properties are preserved but so that the redundancy decreases.
To illustrate lifting and how we adapt the idea to our work, we assume for the rest of the discussion that we are working with bivariate polynomial codes over a field
\mathbb{F}_{q}
of characteristic 2.
In this way, codewords can be viewed not only as elements of
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{q^{2}}
, but also as (evaluations of) bivariate polynomials in
\mathbb{F}_{q}[X,Y]
of degree at most
q-1
in each variable.
While lifted codes can be analyzed over larger field characteristics, lifted codes seem to obtain the best redundancy over fields characteristic 2, so we focus our attention on characteristic 2.

The bivariate Reed-Muller Code over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
obtained by evaluating polynomials of total degree
\leq q-2
has length
N=q^{2}
, has redundancy roughly
N/2
, and has the
\sqrt{N}
disjoint repair group property, because each line forms a repair group and there are
\sqrt{N}
lines in
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{2}
passing through each point.
Indeed, each line forms a repair group because the line-restriction of any bivariate polynomial with total degree at most
q-2
is a univariate polynomial of degree at most
q-2
, and thus the sum of the evaluations along any line is 0.
Guo Kopparty and Sudan
[
GKS13
]
show that, perhaps surprisingly, if we remove the total degree condition but still require lines to form repair groups, we can decrease the redundancy of the code from
\Omega(N)
to
N^{0.792}
(if the field
\mathbb{F}_{q}
has characteristic 2).

To obtain codes with the
t
-DRGP for values of
t
other than
\sqrt{N}
, variations of this idea have been proposed.
In
[
FGW17
]
, the authors define
partially lifted codes
, requiring that only
some
, rather than all, lines in
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{2}
form repair groups, and show this gives an improved construction for
t=N^{1/4}
.
The works of
[
Wu15
,
LW19
,
PV19
,
HPP+20
]
adapt
[
GKS13
]
by adding derivatives to the construction to obtain
lifted multiplicity codes
, and show these give the best known constructions for the DRGP for all values of
t\in(1,N^{0.99})
(but with a large alphabet).
In
[
LMM+20
]
, the authors consider another variant,
Hermitian lifted codes
where the polynomials are evaluated on a Hermitian curve rather than on all of
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{2}
.

In this work, we propose another method of adapting the construction of
[
GKS13
]
to other values of
t
, which we call
wedge-lifts
.
This method avoids the large alphabets of lifted multiplicity codes
[
Wu15
,
LW19
,
PV19
,
HPP+20
]
, improves on the partially lifted codes of
[
FGW17
]
, and additionally gives the best constructions of codes with the
t
-DRGP for
t=N^{1/2d}
for integers
d\geq 2
.
Roughly, the idea of wedge-lifts is that, instead of requiring that the repair groups are lines, we take the repair groups to be sets of lines (“wedges”) through a point.
For a line to form a repair group in the ordinary lifted code, we need the sum of the evaluations along the line to be 0.
For a wedge to form a repair group in the wedge-lifted code, we require the sum of all the points in the wedge to be 0.
If the wedges have odd size (the field has characteristic 2), then each “wedge parity check” is the sum of “line parity checks”, and thus the ordinary lifted code is a subset of the wedge-lifted code.
We then show that, if the wedges are formed by lines lying in a coset of some subgroup
H\leq\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\times}
of the multiplicative group of
\mathbb{F}_{q}
, then the codes have redundancy at most
t\sqrt{N}
, matching the constructions in
[
FVY15
]
.
Furthermore, for certain choices of the subgroup
H
, we can analyze the redundancy of the construction more precisely, giving the improved bounds on the redundancy in Theorem
1
.
We are only able to precisely analyze wedge-lifted codes for some choices of the subgroup
H
, and we leave it to future work to give better bounds on the redundancy for other subgroups
H
.

Our constructions of wedge-lifted codes are
q
-ary codes. In order to construct binary codes, as stated in Theorem
1
, we take the (coordinate-wise)
trace
of the code. This technique of taking the trace, which is implicit in
[
GKS13
]
, saves a
\log(q)
multiplicative factor in the redundancy over a simpler technique of reducing the alphabet size, which is used in
[
AY19
,
LW19
,
HPP+20
]
.

Preliminaries

In this section, we introduce the background and notation we will use throughout the paper.

2.1

Notation and basic definitions

Let
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
denote the finite field of order
q
and let
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{\times}
denote its multiplicative subgroup. We study linear codes
\mathcal{C}\subseteq{\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{N}
of block length
N
over an alphabet of size
q
. Throughout this paper, we assume that
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
has characteristic
2
and write
q=2^{\ell}
.

We need the following tools to reason about the binary representations of integers.

Definition
\thetheorem
.

Let
x,y
be two non-negative integers with binary representations
x=\overline{x_{\ell-1}\dotsm x_{0}},y=\overline{y_{\ell-1}\dotsm y_{0}}
. For each
j\in\{0,\ldots,\ell-1\}
, let
a_{j}=\max(x_{j},y_{j})
and
b_{j}=\min(x_{j},y_{j})
. Then, define the bitwise-OR
\vee
and bitwise-AND
\wedge
of
x
and
y
as follows

x\vee y=\overline{a_{\ell-1}\dotsm a_{0}}

x\wedge y=\overline{b_{\ell-1}\dotsm b_{0}}

Definition
\thetheorem
.

For non-negative integers
x
and
y
with binary representations (possibly with leading zeros)
x=\overline{x_{\ell-1}\dotsm x_{0}},y=\overline{y_{\ell-1}\dotsm y_{0}}
, respectively, we say that
x

lies in the
2
-shadow
of
y
, denoted
x\leq_{2}y
, if
x_{i}\leq y_{i}
for all
i\in\{0,\dots,\ell-1\}
.

We are interested in
2
-shadows because of Lucas’s theorem.

{theorem}
[Lucas’s Theorem]
Let
p
be a prime and
x=\overline{x_{\ell-1}\dotsm x_{0}},y=\overline{y_{\ell-1}\dotsm y_{0}}
be written in base
p
. Then,

\binom{x}{y}=\prod_{i=0}^{\ell-1}\binom{x_{i}}{y_{i}}\mod p

In the case where
p=2
, then
\binom{x}{y}=1\mod p
if and only if
y\leq_{2}x
.

The codes
\mathcal{C}
we consider are polynomial evaluation codes.
For a polynomial
P\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q}[X_{1},\dots,X_{m}]
, we write its corresponding codeword as

\mathrm{eval}_{q}(P)=\left<P(x_{1},\dots x_{m})\right>_{(x_{1},\dots,x_{m})\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{m}}.

Above, we assume some fixed order on the elements of
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{m}
. In this paper, we focus on when
m
is
1
or
2
. We are concerned with the restriction of bivariate polynomials to lines.

Definition
\thetheorem
.

For a line
L:{\mathbb{F}}_{q}\to{\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{2}
with
L(T)=(L_{1}(T),L_{2}(T))
and a polynomial
P:{\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{2}\to{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
, we define the restriction of
P
on
L
, denoted
P|_{L}
, to be the unique polynomial of degree at most
q-1
so that
P|_{L}(T)=P(L_{1}(T),L_{2}(T))
.

2.2

Trace codes

Given a linear code
\mathcal{C}\subseteq{\mathbb{F}}_{p^{\ell}}^{N}
, it is sometimes desirable to construct another code
\mathcal{C}^{\prime}\subseteq{\mathbb{F}}_{p}^{N}
over a smaller alphabet that maintains some properties of
\mathcal{C}
. We briefly describe a way to create such a code using the trace function,
\mathrm{tr}_{p}
.

Definition
\thetheorem
.

Let
\mathrm{tr}_{p}:{\mathbb{F}}_{p^{\ell}}\to{\mathbb{F}}_{p}
be the trace function

\mathrm{tr}_{p}(\alpha)=\sum_{i=0}^{\ell-1}\alpha^{p^{i}}

We can extend
\mathrm{tr}_{p}:{\mathbb{F}}_{p^{\ell}}^{N}\to{\mathbb{F}}_{p}^{N}
by defining

\mathrm{tr}_{p}(v)=(\mathrm{tr}_{p}(v_{1}),\ldots,\mathrm{tr}_{p}(v_{N}))\in{\mathbb{F}}_{p}^{N}

We can further extend it to codes
\mathcal{C}\subseteq{\mathbb{F}}_{p^{\ell}}^{N}
by taking the the trace of every vector in the code:

\mathrm{tr}_{p}(\mathcal{C})=\{\mathrm{tr}_{p}(v):v\in\mathcal{C}\}\subseteq{\mathbb{F}}_{p}^{N}

Note that
\mathrm{tr}_{p}
is a
{\mathbb{F}}_{p}
-linear map. Hence, if
\mathcal{C}
is a linear code, then
\mathrm{tr}_{p}(\mathcal{C})
is also a linear code. We can bound the rate of
\mathrm{tr}_{p}(\mathcal{C})
in terms of the rate of
\mathcal{C}
using the following corollary of Delsarte’s theorem.

{theorem}
[Follows from Delsarte’s theorem (see, e.g.,
[
Sti09
]
)]
For any
{\mathbb{F}}_{p^{\ell}}
-linear code
\mathcal{C}\subseteq{\mathbb{F}}_{p^{\ell}}^{N}
,

\dim\mathcal{C}\leq\dim\mathrm{tr}_{p}(\mathcal{C})\leq\ell\cdot\dim\mathcal{C}

Wedge-lifted codes

In this section, we define and analyze wedge-lifted codes. As mentioned above, we focus on bivariate codes, although as remarked above we believe our work could be extended to more variables. To that end, from now on we use
\mathrm{eval}_{q}
denote the bivariate evaluation map:

Definition
\thetheorem
.

Let
\mathrm{eval}_{q}:{\mathbb{F}}_{q}[X,Y]\to{\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{q^{2}}
be the evaluation map of the polynomial at all of
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{2}
, namely
\mathrm{eval}_{q}(P):=(P(\textbf{x}))_{\textbf{x}\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{2}}

In Section
3.1
, we give the formal definition of wedge-lifted codes. In Section
3.2
, we analyze wedge-lifted codes in a special case where the construction arises from cosets of
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\times}
. Finally in Section
3.3
, we further set parameters to fix particular coset decompositions, culminating in our main theorem of the section, Theorem
3.3
.

3.1

Definition of wedge-lifted codes

Finally we are ready to define wedges, wedge-restrictions, and wedge-lifted codes.

Definition
\thetheorem

(Wedge)
.

For a point
\vec{p}=(x,y)\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{2}
and a set
H\subseteq{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
, we define the

wedge
through
\vec{p}
formed by
H
, denoted
W_{H,\vec{p}}
, as the set of affine lines passing through
\vec{p}
whose slope is in
H
,

W_{H,\vec{p}}=\left\{(L_{1}(T),L_{2}(T))\;\middle|\;\begin{array}[]{c}L_{1}(T)=T\ \text{and for some }\alpha\in H,\\
L_{2}(T)=\alpha(T-x)+y\end{array}\right\}.

Definition
\thetheorem

(Wedge restriction)
.

For a wedge
W_{H,\vec{p}}
and polynomial
P\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q}[X,Y]
, we define the
wedge restriction
of
P
to the wedge
W_{H,\vec{p}}
, denoted
P|_{W_{H,\vec{p}}}
, to be the sum of the restrictions of
P
to each line in the wedge,

P|_{W_{H,\vec{p}}}=\sum_{L\in W_{H,\vec{p}}}P|_{L}=\sum_{\alpha\in H}\sum_{T\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}P(T,\alpha(T-x)+y).

Note that when
W_{H,\vec{p}}
consists of an odd number of lines and the field has characteristic
2
, the wedge-restriction of
P
is equivalent to the sum of
P
’s evaluations of each point in the wedge,

\displaystyle P|_{W_{H,\vec{p}}}=\sum_{{(x,y)}\in W_{H,\vec{p}}}P(x,y).

Definition
\thetheorem

(Wedge-lifted codes)
.

Let
\mathcal{H}
be a collection of disjoint subsets of
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
, with each
H\in\mathcal{H}
having odd size. The
(\mathcal{H},q)

wedge-lifted code
is a code
\mathcal{C}
over alphabet
\Sigma={\mathbb{F}}_{q}
of length
q^{2}
given by

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}=\left\{\mathrm{eval}_{q}(P)\;\middle|\;\begin{array}[]{c}P\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q}[X,Y]\ \text{and},\ \text{for any}\ H\in\mathcal{H},\\
\text{and any}\ \vec{p}\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{2},P|_{W_{H,\vec{p}}}=0\end{array}\right\}.

Following the approach of previous work
[
GKS13
,
FGW17
,
LW19
,
PV19
,
HPP+20
]
, we show that wedge-lifted codes contain the evaluations of polynomials that lie in the span of “good” monomials. Informally, a monomial is
(\mathcal{H},q)
-good if it restricts nicely to all the wedges we can construct with
\mathcal{H}
.

Definition
\thetheorem

(
(\mathcal{H},q)
-good monomials)
.

Let
\mathcal{H}
be a collection of disjoint subsets of
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
.
We say that a monomial
P(X,Y)=X^{a}Y^{b}
with
0\leq a,b\leq q-1
is
(\mathcal{H},q)
-
good
if for every
H\in\mathcal{H}
and every
\vec{p}\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{2}
,
P|_{W_{H,\vec{p}}}=0
, and say it is
(\mathcal{H},q)
-
bad
otherwise.

By definition, the evaluations of all good monomials lie within our wedge-lifted codes. Furthermore, monomials
X^{a}Y^{b}
with
a,b\leq q-1
form a basis for polynomials of degree at most
q-1
, which are in bijection with
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{q^{2}}
through the
\mathrm{eval}_{q}
map. Therefore, we can obtain a lower bound on the rate of our code by finding a lower bound on the number of good monomials.

Observation
\thetheorem
.

For any
(\mathcal{H},q)
wedge-lifted code
\mathcal{C}
, the redundancy of
\mathcal{C}
is at most the number of
(\mathcal{H},q)
-bad monomials.

Moreover, the number of disjoint repair groups for a wedge-lifted code is straightforward to compute.

Proposition
\thetheorem
.

Any
(\mathcal{H},q)
wedge-lifted code
\mathcal{C}
has
|\mathcal{H}|
disjoint repair groups.

Proof.

For any

(3)

\displaystyle P|_{W_{gH,\vec{p}}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\alpha\in H}\binom{b}{q-1-a}(g\alpha)^{-a}(y-g\alpha x)^{a+b-(q-1)}

3.3

Instantiations

In this section, we continue to assume that
\mathcal{H}
is a collection of cosets

for some natural numbers

and
\ell^{\prime}
, and each coset in

has order

.

We make use of two observations. The first is that finding the difference between the integers

and
b
, where
a
lies in the 2-shadow of

, is as simple as taking the difference between corresponding bits.

Observation
\thetheorem
.

Let
a
and
b
be nonnegative integers with binary representations
\overline{a_{\ell-1}\dotsm a_{0}}
and
\overline{b_{\ell-1}\dotsm b_{0}}
respectively. If
a\leq_{2}b
, then the integer
c=b-a
has binary representation
\overline{c_{\ell-1}\dotsm c_{0}}
where
c_{i}=b_{i}-a_{i}
for every integer
i\in\{0,\dots,\ell-1\}
.

The second is that multiples of
(q-1)/(q^{1/d}-1)
that are less than
q
have repeating bit patterns.

Observation
\thetheorem
.

Let
q=2^{\ell^{\prime}d}
for some natural numbers
\ell^{\prime}
and
d
. Then any nonnegative integer
a<q
with binary representation
\overline{a_{\ell^{\prime}d-1}\dotsm a_{0}}
is a multiple of

if and only if
\overline{a_{\ell^{\prime}-1}\dotsm a_{0}}=\overline{a_{2\ell^{\prime}-1}\dotsm a_{\ell^{\prime}}}=\dots=\overline{a_{d\ell^{\prime}-1}\dotsm a_{(d-1)\ell^{\prime}}}
.

{lemma}

Let
q=2^{\ell^{\prime}d}
for some natural numbers
\ell^{\prime}
and
d
. Let
H\leq{\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{\times}
be a subgroup of order
|H|=(q-1)/(q^{1/d}-1)
and
\mathcal{H}
be the collection of cosets
gH
.
Then any monomial
X^{a}Y^{b}
with
0\leq a,b\leq q-1
is
(\mathcal{H},q)
-bad if and only if both of the following conditions hold:

1.

a\vee b=q-1

2.

For every
r,s\in\{0,1\dots,d-1\}
, there does not exist

such that

and
a_{r\ell^{\prime}+j}=b_{s\ell^{\prime}+j}=0
.

Proof.

We first prove the forward direction. If
X^{a}Y^{b}
is
(\mathcal{H},q)
-bad, then there exists

such that

. Since

and

and
i_{s\ell^{\prime}+j}=0
.
Note that

and

together imply that
a_{r\ell^{\prime}+j}=1
. Note also that

and

together imply that
a_{s\ell^{\prime}+j}=1
. Hence, it is never the case that
b_{r\ell^{\prime}+j}=a_{s\ell^{\prime}+j}=1
and
a_{r\ell^{\prime}+j}=b_{s\ell^{\prime}+j}=0
.

We now show that the converse also holds. Suppose that for every
r,s\in\{0,1\dots,d-1\}
, there does not exist

such that
b_{r\ell^{\prime}+j}=a_{s\ell^{\prime}+j}=1
and
a_{r\ell^{\prime}+j}=b_{s\ell^{\prime}+j}=0
. Then we can construct a whole number

such that

. For each

we assign the values of

according to the following rules:

1.

If

, then let

.

2.

If there exist

.

Note that our construction ensures that

. Note also that for every

and every

, which implies that

. It remains to show that

. We need not focus on the bits in

that are zero, as those will satisfy the

r\in\{0,1,\dots,d-1\}
and

such that

and

. If such

and there exists

Trace of Wedge-Lifted Codes

In this section, we take the trace of our codes in Theorem
3.3
and set parameters, in order to prove our main theorem, Theorem

We begin with a lemma that says that we can take the coordinate-wise trace of a code over a field of characteristic two without hurting the redundancy or locality.

{lemma}

Let
\mathcal{C}\subseteq{\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{q^{2}}
be a
(\mathcal{H},q)
-wedge-lifted code. Then, there exists a binary code
\mathcal{C}^{\prime}\subseteq{\mathbb{F}}_{2}^{q^{2}}

Proof.

For any polynomial
P
such that
\mathrm{eval}(P)\in\mathcal{C}
,
H\in\mathcal{H}
, and

. Therefore, using the fact that
\mathrm{tr}_{2}
commutes with addition,

\mathrm{tr}_{2}(0)=\mathrm{tr}_{2}\left(\sum_{\alpha\in H}\sum_{T\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}P(T,\alpha(T-x)+y)\right)=\sum_{\alpha\in H}\sum_{T\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}\mathrm{tr}_{2}(P(T,\alpha(T-x)+y))

Therefore, if we view the code
\mathrm{tr}_{2}(\mathcal{C})
as a code over
{\mathbb{F}}_{2}^{q^{2}}
with coordinates indexed by
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{2}
, any index
\vec{p}
of a codeword
\mathrm{eval}(\mathrm{tr}_{2}\circ P)
of
\mathrm{tr}_{2}(\mathcal{C})
can be repaired by summing over the lines whose slopes are in
H
. So,
\mathrm{tr}_{2}(\mathcal{C})
and
\mathcal{C}
both have
|\mathcal{H}|
disjoint repair groups.

Furthermore, Theorem
2.2
states that
\dim(\mathrm{tr}_{2}(\mathcal{C}))\geq\dim(\mathcal{C})
, so because these codes have the same length,
\mathrm{tr}_{2}(\mathcal{C})
has the same or lower redundancy as
\mathcal{C}
.
∎

Finally, we are in a position to prove Theorem

Conclusion

In this paper, we introduced
wedge-lifted codes
, which give an improved construction of binary codes with the
t
-DRGP for several
t\leq\sqrt{N}
.
We conclude with some open questions.

1.

For all
\alpha\in(0,1/2)
with
t=N^{\alpha}
, are there binary codes with the
t
-DRGP and redundancy
O(t^{1-\varepsilon}\sqrt{N})
for some
\varepsilon>0
, possibly (but ideally not) depending on
\alpha
?
Our work shows this is true for
\alpha=1/2d
when
d
is any positive integer.
The work of
[
LW19
]
showed this is true (with an absolute
\varepsilon=0.425
) for nonbinary codes.

2.

Can one improve
[
RV16
,
Woo16
]
to prove better lower bounds on the redudancy of codes with the
t
-DRGP?

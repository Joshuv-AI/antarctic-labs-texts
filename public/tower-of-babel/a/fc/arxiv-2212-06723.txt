Essential norms of pointwise multipliers in the non-algebraic setting
Tomasz Kiwerski, Jakub Tomaszewski

Abstract
. Motivated by some recent results, but also referring to recognized classics, we compute the essential norm and the weak essential norm of multiplication operators acting between two distinct Köthe spaces both defined over the same σ \sigma -finite measure space. A by-product of the technology we have developed here are some applications to Banach sequence spaces related to decreasing functions and to Banach spaces of analytic functions on the unit disc, in particular, Hardy spaces. We will close our work with some specific examples illustrating the previously obtained results including Musielak–Orlicz sequence spaces, in particular, Nakano sequence spaces, as well as Lorentz and Marcinkiewicz sequence spaces.

10

3.3.
Essential norms

Denote by
\mathscr{L}
the class of all operators, which we
always
assumed to be linear and bounded, between all Banach spaces.
For two Banach spaces, say
X
and
Y
, let
\mathscr{L}(X,Y)
be the set of all operators from
X
into

. More generally, for

\mathscr{I}\subset\mathscr{L}
any two Banach spaces

and
Y
we set
\mathscr{I}(X,Y)\coloneqq\mathscr{I}\cap\mathscr{L}(X,Y)
.
If the need arises, we will shorten the symbols
\mathscr{L}(X,X)
and
\mathscr{I}(X,X)
to
\mathscr{L}(X)
and, respectively,
\mathscr{I}(X)
.
Following Pietsch
[

]
(see also
[
DJP01
]
) we recall that a class

is called a
operator ideal
(in the sense of Pietsch)
if the following conditions are satisfied:

\bullet

for any one-dimensional Banach space
X
the identity operator
\text{id}_{X}\colon X\rightarrow X
belongs to
\mathscr{I}
,

\bullet

for all Banach spaces, say
X
and
Y
, the set
\mathscr{I}(X,Y)
is a linear subspace of
\mathscr{L}(X,Y)
,

\bullet

for any four Banach spaces, say

,
X
,
Y
and
Z

UTS
belongs to
\mathscr{I}(W,Z)

whenever

belongs to
\mathscr{L}(Y,Z)
,
T
to
\mathscr{I}(X,Y)
, and
U
to
\mathscr{L}(W,X)
.

An operator ideal
\mathscr{I}
is said to be a

provided
\mathscr{I}(X,Y)
is closed in

for any two Banach
spaces
X
and
Y
. Some very well-known classes of operator ideals, like compact operators, weakly compact operators, strictly singular operators
or completely continuous operators are examples of closed operator ideals.

For any closed operator ideal
\mathscr{I}
and any operator, say

, acting between two Banach spaces

and
Y
by the
essential
norm
\left\lVert T\colon X\rightarrow Y\right\rVert_{\mathscr{I}}
of

with respect to an operator ideal

we mean

(3.3)

\left\lVert T\colon X\rightarrow Y\right\rVert_{\mathscr{I}}\coloneqq\left\lVert T\colon X\rightarrow Y\right\rVert_{{\mathscr{L}}(X,Y)/\mathscr{I}(X,Y)}=\inf\{\left\lVert T-J\right\rVert_{X\rightarrow Y}\colon J\in\mathscr{I}(X,Y)\}

(cf. with the definitions given in
[
Tyl95
, p. 96]
and
[
Lef09
, Definition 0.3]
). The last expression on the right in (
3.3
)
is, of course, nothing else but a natural quotient norm on the space

, that is to say,

\left\lVert T\colon X\rightarrow Y\right\rVert_{{\mathscr{L}}(X,Y)/\mathscr{I}(X,Y)}=\text{dist}_{\mathscr{L}(X,Y)}(T\colon X\rightarrow Y,\mathscr{I}(X,Y)).

The above definition, in its two most common versions, looks as follows

\bullet

the quotient algebra
{\mathscr{L}}(X,Y)/{\mathscr{K}}(X,Y)
, where
{\mathscr{K}}(X,Y)
denote the ideal of compact operators from
X
to
Y
,
is called the

and the corresponding quotient norm

(or, a bit more concisely,

of

;

\bullet

alike, the quotient algebra

, where

denote the ideal of weakly compact operators
acting between
X
and
Y
, is called the

and the corresponding quotient norm
\left\lVert T\colon X\rightarrow Y\right\rVert_{{\mathscr{L}}(X,Y)/\mathscr{W}(X,Y)}

(briefly, just
\left\lVert T\colon X\rightarrow Y\right\rVert_{w}

of
T
.

Clearly, an operator
T
acting between
X
and
Y
belongs to the closed operator ideal

if, and only if,

\left\lVert T\colon X\rightarrow Y\right\rVert_{{\mathscr{L}}(X,Y)/\mathscr{I}(X,Y)}=0.

\left\{r_{n}\right\}_{n=1}^{\infty}

and symmetrically distributed random variables on the unit interval taking the values
\pm 1
. In more familiar form, the
n^{\text{th}}
standard Rademacher
r_{n}

with
n\in\mathbb{N}
is just a function

(3.4)

r_{n}(t)\coloneqq\sign(\sin 2^{n}\pi t),

where
0<t<1
(see
[
AK06
, Definition 6.2.1, p. 132]
and
[
LT77
, p. 24]
; Astashkin’s book
[
Ast20
]
seems to be an
inexhaustible source of information about Rademacher’s functions).

However, for some reasons, which will become clear later, the object we really want to construct here is a family of functions that shares
the most important properties of Rademacher system, but can be defined on
any

\sigma
-finite measure space.
Since on a finite measure space
(\Omega,\Sigma,\mu)
Rademachers are nothing else but a sequence of independent random variables
each of which takes values
\pm 1
with probability
\mu(\Omega)/2
, so the construction of Rademachers on any finite and
non-atomic measure space should present no difficulties (cf.
[
JL06
, p. 224]
). With a little patience while gluing the appropriate
fragments together we can do the same for every
\sigma
-finite measure space. Unfortunately, handling with such a construction turned out
to be quite difficult to us. Therefore, we propose the following, easier-to-work, one.

Construction 3.22

(Abstract Rademachers)
.

Let
(\Omega,\Sigma,\mu)
be a non-atomic
\sigma
-finite measure space and let
\{\Omega_{N}\}_{N=1}^{\infty}

be a decomposition of
\Omega
into countable family of pairwise disjoint sets of measure not exceeding one. Further,
for every
N\in\mathbb{N}
, let
T_{N}\colon\Omega_{N}\rightarrow(0,\mu(\Omega_{N}))
be a measure-preserving transformation
the existence of which is guaranteed by Ryff’s theorem (see
[
BS88
, Theorem 7.5, p. 82]
). Put

r^{\Omega_{N}}_{n}(\omega)\coloneqq(r_{n}\circ T_{N})(\omega)\quad\text{ for }\quad\omega\in\Omega_{N},

where, as usual, the symbol
r_{n}
stands for the
n^{\text{th}}
standard Rademacher (like in (
3.4
) above).
Then, for
n\in\mathbb{N}
, by the
n^{\text{th}}
abstract Rademacher
on
\Omega
we will understand the function

r^{\Omega}_{n}\colon\Omega\rightarrow\{\pm 1\}
defined as

r^{\Omega}_{n}(\omega)\coloneqq\sum_{N=1}^{\infty}r^{\Omega_{N}}_{n}(\omega)\quad\text{ for }\quad\omega\in\Omega.

\blacktriangle

With such a definition at hand, it is not difficult to prove the following, essentially technical, lemma which seems to be well-known
in its basic version (that is, when
\Omega
is just a unit interval or a semi-axis with the Lebesgue measure; see, for example,
[
AK06
, Lemma 6.3.2, p. 143]

and
[
Kub14
, Lemma 3.2]
).

Lemma 3.23
.

Let
(\Omega,\Sigma,\mu)
be a non-atomic complete and
\sigma
-finite measure space. Then for any function, say
f
,
from
L_{1}(\Omega,\Sigma,\mu)
we have

\lim\limits_{n\rightarrow\infty}\int_{\Omega}f(\omega)r^{\Omega}_{n}(\omega)d\mu(\omega)=0.

Proof.

Take
f\in L_{1}(\Omega,\Sigma,\mu)
and let
\{\Omega_{N}\}_{N=1}^{\infty}
be like in Construction
3.22
.

First, we claim that for any
N\in\mathbb{N}
we have

(3.5)

\lim\limits_{n\rightarrow\infty}\int_{\Omega}f(\omega)r^{\Omega_{N}}_{n}(\omega)d\mu(\omega)=0.

Indeed,

\displaystyle\lim\limits_{n\rightarrow\infty}\int_{\Omega}f(\omega)r^{\Omega_{N}}_{n}(\omega)d\mu(\omega)

\displaystyle=\lim\limits_{n\rightarrow\infty}\int_{\Omega_{N}}f(\omega)\left(r_{n}\circ T_{N}\right)(\omega)d\mu(\omega)

\displaystyle=\lim\limits_{n\rightarrow\infty}\int_{0}^{\mu\left(\Omega_{N}\right)}\left(f\circ T_{N}^{-1}\right)(t)r_{n}(t)dm(t)=0,

where the last equality follows from, for example,
[
Kub14
, Lemma 3.2]
. This proves the claim.

Next, fix
\varepsilon>0
. Since the space
L_{1}(\Omega,\Sigma,\mu)
is order continuous (see
[
BS88
, Theorem 5.5, p. 27]
),
so there is a finite sequence of natural numbers, say
\{N_{j}\}_{j=1}^{M}
, such that

\int_{\Omega\setminus\bigcup\limits_{j=0}^{M}\Omega_{N_{j}}}\left\lvert f(\omega)\right\rvert d\mu(\omega)<\frac{\varepsilon}{2}.

Now, using (
3.5
), for every
j=1,2,...,M
we can find
n_{j}\in\mathbb{N}
such that

\left\lvert\int_{\Omega}f(\omega)r^{\Omega_{N_{j}}}_{n}(\omega)d\mu(\omega)\right\rvert<\frac{\varepsilon}{2M}\quad\text{ for }\quad n\geqslant n_{j}.

Finally, for all
n\geqslant\max\{n_{1},n_{2},...,n_{M}\}
, we have

\displaystyle\left\lvert\int_{\Omega}f(\omega)r^{\Omega}_{n}(\omega)d\mu(\omega)\right\rvert

\displaystyle=\left\lvert\sum\limits_{j=0}^{M}\int_{\Omega_{N_{j}}}f(\omega)r^{\Omega}_{n}(\omega)d\mu(\omega)+\int_{\Omega\setminus\bigcup\limits_{j=0}^{M}\Omega_{N_{j}}}f(\omega)r^{\Omega}_{n}(\omega)d\mu(\omega)\right\rvert

\displaystyle\leqslant\sum\limits_{j=0}^{M}\left\lvert\int_{\Omega_{N_{j}}}f(\omega)r^{\Omega_{N_{j}}}_{n}(\omega)d\mu(\omega)\right\rvert+\int_{\Omega\setminus\bigcup\limits_{j=0}^{M}\Omega_{N_{j}}}\left\lvert f(\omega)\right\rvert d\mu(\omega)<\varepsilon.

In view of
\varepsilon
’s arbitrariness this finishes the proof.
∎

4.
Main results

This section is a main part of our work. Since the title of this paragraph does not leave much room for guesswork,
so let us stop at the following remark, which we will use repeatedly without making any explicit reference to it.
In view of

M_{\lambda}=M_{e^{i\varphi}\left\lvert\lambda\right\rvert}=M_{e^{i\varphi}}M_{\left\lvert\lambda\right\rvert},

the multiplication operator
M_{\lambda}
belongs to some operator ideal, say

, if, and only if, the same can be said about the

4.1.
Essential norms

For the sake of completeness, let us remind that an operator acting from one Banach space to another is
called
compact
provided it carries bounded sets to relatively compact one.

What we want to do now is to calculate the essential norm of the multiplication operator
M_{\lambda}
which acts between two Köthe function spaces
X
and
Y
.
Roughly speaking, we will show that
M_{\lambda}\colon X\rightarrow Y
is as far from being compact as it can be.

Theorem 4.1

(Essential norm of multiplication operators between Köthe function spaces)
.

Let
X
and
Y
be two Köthe function spaces with the Fatou property both defined on the same
\sigma
-finite
measure space
(\Omega,\Sigma,\mu)
. Suppose that either the space
X
is separable or the space
Y
is reflexive.
Then

\left\lVert M_{\lambda}\colon X\rightarrow Y\right\rVert_{ess}=\left\lVert\lambda\right\rVert_{M(X,Y)}=\left\lVert M_{\lambda}\colon X\rightarrow Y\right\rVert.

In particular, there are no non-trivial compact multiplication operators acting from
X
into
Y
.

Proof.

Of course, we can immediately assume that the space
M(X,Y)
is non-trivial, because otherwise there is nothing to prove. What’s more,
the inequality

\left\lVert M_{\lambda}\colon X\rightarrow Y\right\rVert_{ess}\leqslant\left\lVert M_{\lambda}\right\rVert_{X\rightarrow Y}=\left\lVert\lambda\right\rVert_{M(X,Y)}

is true for obvious reasons, so there is noting else but to turn to the proof of a more interesting inequality.

First case.

Let us start with the case when the space
X
is separable. Fix
\varepsilon>0
and take
\lambda\in M(X,Y)
. It follows from the definition
of the norm in the space
M(X,Y)
that there exists
f\in\text{Ball}(X)
with
\left\lVert\lambda f\right\rVert_{Y}\geqslant\left\lVert\lambda\right\rVert_{M(X,Y)}-\varepsilon
.
Moreover, it is also clear that

\left\lVert\lambda fr^{\Omega}_{n}\right\rVert_{Y}=\left\lVert\lambda f\right\rVert_{Y}\geqslant\left\lVert\lambda\right\rVert_{M(X,Y)}-\varepsilon,

where
r^{\Omega}_{n}
denote the
n^{\text{th}}
abstract Rademacher on
\Omega
(see Construction
3.22
for details).
Now, for any compact operator, say
K
, acting from
X
into
Y
, we have

\displaystyle\left\lVert M_{\lambda}-K\right\rVert_{X\rightarrow Y}

\displaystyle\geqslant\left\lVert(M_{\lambda}-K)(fr^{\Omega}_{n})\right\rVert_{Y}

\displaystyle\geqslant\left\lvert\left\lVert\lambda fr^{\Omega}_{n}\right\rVert_{Y}-\left\lVert K(fr^{\Omega}_{n})\right\rVert_{Y}\right\rvert,

We claim that the sequence
\left\{fr^{\Omega}_{n}\right\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
is weakly null. To see this, take
g\in X^{\times}

(due to our assumptions the space
X
is order continuous, thus we have the equality
X^{*}=X^{\times}
; see, for example,

[
BS88
, Theorem 4.1, p. 20]
). Since
fg\in L_{1}(\Omega,\Sigma,\mu)
, so it is enough to apply Lemma
3.23
to conclude that

\lim\limits_{n\rightarrow\infty}\left\lvert\langle fr^{\Omega}_{n},g\rangle\right\rvert=\lim\limits_{n\rightarrow\infty}\left\lvert\int_{\Omega}(fg)r^{\Omega}_{n}d\mu\right\rvert=0

and this proves our claim. Consequently, since every compact operator is
a fortiori
completely continuous,
so
\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\left\lVert K(fr^{\Omega}_{n})\right\rVert_{Y}=0
. But now, using the above inequalities, we get

\left\lVert M_{\lambda}-K\right\rVert_{X\rightarrow Y}\geqslant\left\lVert\lambda\right\rVert_{M(X,Y)}-\varepsilon.

This concludes the proof in the first case.

Second case.

Now, let us move on to the second case where we assume that the space
Y
is reflexive. It follows from Schauder’s theorem that an operator, say
T
, acting
between Banach spaces is compact if, and only if, its adjoint
T^{*}
is compact too (see, for example,
[
AK06
, A.4, p. 329]
).
Hence, we can conclude
13
13

13

Note that in general there is no hope for an equality in
(
4.1
) (see
[
AJS80
, Example 1]
; cf.
[
AJS80
, p. 160]
).
that

(4.1)

\left\lVert M_{\lambda}\colon X\rightarrow Y\right\rVert_{ess}\geqslant\left\lVert M_{\lambda}^{*}\colon Y^{*}\rightarrow X^{*}\right\rVert_{ess},

where
M_{\lambda}^{*}
is the adjoint of the multiplication operator
M_{\lambda}
.
Next, we will need the following observation.

Claim
\spadesuit
.

Let
W
and

be two Köthe function space both defined on the same
\sigma

.
Further, let

be the multiplication operator with the symbol

Suppose the space

is separable. Then the following diagram commutes

\textstyle{Z^{*}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{M_{\lambda}^{*}}
\textstyle{W^{*}}

\scriptstyle{L}

\textstyle{W^{\times}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
id

where
L\colon Z^{\times}\rightarrow Z^{*}
is the natural isometric isomorphism between the Köthe dual
Z^{\times}

and the topological dual
Z^{*}
(see below for details), and
M_{\lambda}\colon Z^{\times}\rightarrow W^{\times}
is the Köthe
adjoint
14
14

14

Recall that for a bounded operator
T
acting between two Köthe spaces

Y

different measure spaces its

(or

T^{\times}\colon Y^{\times}\rightarrow X^{\times}

\langle x,T^{\times}(y^{*})\rangle=\langle T(x),y^{*}\rangle\quad\text{ for all }\quad x\in X\quad\text{ and }\quad y^{*}\in Y^{\times}.

.

Proof of Claim
\spadesuit
.

It is clear that each function

induces a bounded linear functional

L_{g}\colon f\rightsquigarrow L_{g}(f)\coloneqq\int_{\Omega}fgd\mu

Z

L\colon g\rightsquigarrow\left[f\rightsquigarrow L_{g}(f)\right]\quad\text{ for }\quad f\in Z

Therefore, for

\displaystyle\langle f,M_{\lambda}^{*}(L(g))\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle M_{\lambda}(f),L(g)\rangle

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}M_{\lambda}(f)gd\mu

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}fM_{\lambda}(g)d\mu

\displaystyle=\langle f,M_{\lambda}(g)\rangle.

This ends the proof of Claim

.

\blacksquare

Now, it follows from the preceding Claim

that the adjoint operator
M_{\lambda}^{*}\colon Y^{*}\rightarrow X^{*}

M_{\lambda}\colon Y^{\times}\rightarrow X^{\times}
. Thus, we have

\displaystyle\left\lVert M_{\lambda}\colon X\rightarrow Y\right\rVert_{ess}

\displaystyle\geqslant\left\lVert M_{\lambda}\colon Y^{\times}\rightarrow X^{\times}\right\rVert_{ess}\quad(\text{using Claim\penalty\ $\spadesuit$ together with \eqref{inequality: ess norm of T and adjoint T}})

\displaystyle=\left\lVert\lambda\right\rVert_{M(Y^{\times},X^{\times})}\quad(\text{using the first part of this proof})

\displaystyle=\left\lVert\lambda\right\rVert_{M(X^{\times\times},Y^{\times\times})}

Since it is straightforward to see that
M(X,X)\equiv L_{\infty}
, so one immediate consequence of Theorem
4.1
is the following

Corollary 4.2
.

Let
X
be a separable Köthe function space with the Fatou property. Then

\left\lVert M_{\lambda}\colon X\circlearrowleft\right\rVert_{ess}=\left\lVert\lambda\right\rVert_{L_{\infty}}=\left\lVert M_{\lambda}\colon X\circlearrowleft\right\rVert.

In particular, there are no non-trivial compact multiplication operators acting from
X
into itself.

Let us turn our attention to the case of Köthe spaces defined over purely atomic measure spaces.
Here the situation is completely different, because, any symbol which is eventually zero induces a compact multiplication operator.

Theorem 4.3

(Essential norm of multiplication operators between Köthe sequence spaces)
.

Let
X
and
Y
be two Köthe sequence spaces with the Fatou property. Suppose that either the space
X
is reflexive
or the space
Y
is separable. Then

\left\lVert M_{\lambda}\colon X\rightarrow Y\right\rVert_{ess}=\lim\limits_{n\rightarrow\infty}\left\lVert\sum_{i=n}^{\infty}\lambda_{i}e_{i}\right\rVert_{M(X,Y)}.

In particular, the multiplication operator
M_{\lambda}\colon X\rightarrow Y
is compact if, and only if,
\lambda\in M(X,Y)_{o}
.

Proof.

In order to show the first of the two inequalities, take
\lambda=\{\lambda_{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
from
M(X,Y)
.
Let
\text{Proj}_{n}\colon Y\rightarrow Y
be the projection onto first
n
coordinates. We have

\displaystyle\left\lVert M_{\lambda}\colon X\rightarrow Y\right\rVert_{ess}

\displaystyle=\inf\Big\{\left\lVert M_{\lambda}-K\right\rVert_{X\rightarrow Y}\colon K\in{\mathscr{K}}(X,Y)\Big\}

\displaystyle\leqslant\left\lVert M_{\lambda}-\text{Proj}_{n}M_{\lambda}\right\rVert_{X\rightarrow Y}

\displaystyle=\left\lVert\sum_{i=n}^{\infty}\lambda_{i}e_{i}\right\rVert_{M(X,Y)},

where to get the inequality we have used the plain fact that finite rank operators acting between Banach spaces are always compact.
In consequence, we conclude
15
15

15

It seems worth emphasizing that inequality (
4.2
) holds without any additional assumptions on
X
and
Y
.

that

(4.2)

\left\lVert M_{\lambda}\colon X\rightarrow Y\right\rVert_{ess}\leqslant\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\left\lVert\sum_{i=n}^{\infty}\lambda_{i}e_{i}\right\rVert_{M(X,Y)}.

Now, let us turn to the proof of the second inequality. We will consider both situations mentioned in the formulation of the theorem separately.

First case
. Suppose that the space
X
is reflexive. Fix
\varepsilon>0
. Due to the definition of norm in the space
M(X,Y)
we infer
that for any
n\in\mathbb{N}
we can find a sequence, say
x^{(n)}=\left\{x^{(n)}_{i}\right\}_{i=1}^{\infty}
, in
\text{Ball}_{X}
such that

(4.3)

\left\lVert\sum_{i=n}^{\infty}\lambda_{i}x^{(n)}_{i}e_{i}\right\rVert_{Y}\geqslant\left\lVert\sum_{i=n}^{\infty}\lambda_{i}e_{i}\right\rVert_{M(X,Y)}-\varepsilon.

Clearly, without losing generality, we can assume that
\supp\left(x^{(n)}\right)\subset\{n,n+1,...\}
for
n\in\mathbb{N}
.
In view of (
4.3
) and the ideal property of the space
Y
this means that

(4.4)

\left\lVert\lambda x^{(n)}\right\rVert_{Y}\geqslant\left\lVert\sum_{i=n}^{\infty}\lambda_{i}e_{i}\right\rVert_{M(X,Y)}-\varepsilon\quad\text{ for every }\quad n\in\mathbb{N}.

Next, we claim that the sequence
\left\{x^{(n)}\right\}_{n=1}^{\infty}\subset\text{Ball}(X)
is weakly null.
Indeed, since the space
X
is reflexive, so the space
X^{\times}
is order continuous and
X^{*}\equiv X^{\times}

(keeping in mind that the space
X
has the Fatou property we refer to
[
BS88
, Corollaries 4.3 and 4.4, p. 23]
).
Therefore, for any
x^{*}=\{x^{*}_{i}\}_{i=1}^{\infty}
from
X^{\times}
, we have

\displaystyle\left\lvert\langle x^{(n)},x^{*}\rangle\right\rvert

\displaystyle=\left\lvert\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}x^{(n)}_{i}x^{*}_{i}\right\rvert

\displaystyle=\left\lvert\sum_{i=n}^{\infty}x^{(n)}_{i}x^{*}_{i}\right\rvert

\displaystyle\leqslant\left\lVert\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}x^{(n)}_{i}e_{i}\right\rVert_{X}\left\lVert\sum_{i=n}^{\infty}x^{*}_{i}e_{i}\right\rVert_{X^{\times}}\to 0

as
n
goes to infinity.
Now, for any compact operator
K
acting between
X
and
Y
, we have

\displaystyle\left\lVert M_{\lambda}-K\right\rVert_{X\rightarrow Y}

\displaystyle\geqslant\left\lVert(M_{\lambda}-K)(x^{(n)})\right\rVert_{Y}

\displaystyle\geqslant\left\lVert\lambda x^{(n)}\right\rVert_{Y}-\left\lVert Kx^{(n)}\right\rVert_{Y},

where the last inequality follows from the reverse triangle inequality. Thus, remembering about inequality (
4.4
),

\left\lVert M_{\lambda}-K\right\rVert_{X\rightarrow Y}\geqslant\left\lVert\sum_{i=n}^{\infty}\lambda_{i}e_{i}\right\rVert_{M(X,Y)}-\left\lVert Kx^{(n)}\right\rVert_{Y}-\varepsilon.

Going further, since every compact operator is
a fortiori
completely continuous and, as we have shown above, the sequence

\left\{x^{(n)}\right\}_{n=1}^{\infty}\subset\text{Ball}_{X}
is weakly null, so
\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\left\lVert Kx^{(n)}\right\rVert_{Y}=0
.
In consequence, we have

\left\lVert M_{\lambda}-K\right\rVert_{X\rightarrow Y}\geqslant\lim\limits_{n\rightarrow\infty}\left\lVert\sum_{i=n}^{\infty}\lambda_{i}e_{i}\right\rVert_{M(X,Y)}-\varepsilon

and this, due to
\varepsilon
’s arbitrariness, ends the proof in the first case.

Second case
. Suppose that the space
Y
is separable. Again, fix
\varepsilon>0
. Let
\left\{x^{(n)}\right\}_{n=1}^{\infty}\subset\text{Ball}_{X}

be the sequence constructed above. Further, let
K\colon X\rightarrow Y
be a compact operator. Since the set
\left\{Kx^{(n)}\right\}_{n=1}^{\infty}

is relatively compact in
Y
, so it has an accumulation point, say

.
This means that there is a subsequence
\left\{x^{(n(k))}\right\}_{k=1}^{\infty}

\left\{x^{(n)}\right\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
with

(4.5)

\left\lVert Kx^{(n(k))}-y_{a}\right\rVert_{Y}<\varepsilon\quad\text{ for all }\quad k\in\mathbb{N}\quad\text{ big enough}.

Therefore, using again the reverse triangle inequality,

\displaystyle\left\lVert M_{\lambda}-K\right\rVert_{X\rightarrow Y}

\displaystyle\geqslant\left\lVert(M_{\lambda}-K)(x^{(n(k))})\right\rVert_{Y}

\displaystyle\geqslant\left\lVert M_{\lambda}x^{(n(k))}-a\right\rVert_{Y}-\left\lVert Kx^{(n(k))}-a\right\rVert_{Y}.

However, the above inequality in tandem with (
4.5
) implies that for sufficiently big
k\in\mathbb{N}
, we have

(4.6)

\left\lVert M_{\lambda}-K\right\rVert_{X\rightarrow Y}\geqslant\left\lVert M_{\lambda}x^{(n(k))}-a\right\rVert_{Y}-\varepsilon.

On the other hand, recalling that
\supp\left(x^{(n)}\right)\subset\{n,n+1,...\}
for
n\in\mathbb{N}
, we get

\displaystyle\left\lVert M_{\lambda}x^{(n(k))}-a\right\rVert_{Y}

\displaystyle=\left\lVert\lambda x^{(n(k))}-a\right\rVert_{Y}

\displaystyle\geqslant\left\lVert\sum_{i=n(k)}^{\infty}\left[\lambda_{i}x^{(n(k))}_{i}-a_{i}\right]e_{i}\right\rVert_{Y}

\displaystyle\geqslant\left\lVert\sum_{i=n(k)}^{\infty}\lambda_{i}x^{(n)}_{i}e_{i}\right\rVert_{Y}-\left\lVert\sum_{i=n(k)}^{\infty}a_{i}e_{i}\right\rVert_{Y}

\displaystyle\geqslant\left\lVert\sum_{i=n(k)}^{\infty}\lambda_{i}e_{i}\right\rVert_{M(X,Y)}-2\varepsilon,

where the last inequality follows from inequality (
4.4
) together with the fact that the space
Y
is order continuous.
Finally, by combining the just obtained inequality with (
4.6
),

\left\lVert M_{\lambda}-K\right\rVert_{X\rightarrow Y}\geqslant\left\lVert\sum_{i=n(k)}^{\infty}\lambda_{i}e_{i}\right\rVert_{M(X,Y)}-3\varepsilon.

This, due to the arbitrariness of
K
and the ideal property of the space
M(X,Y)
, gives

\left\lVert M_{\lambda}\colon X\rightarrow Y\right\rVert_{ess}\geqslant\lim\limits_{n\rightarrow\infty}\left\lVert\sum_{i=n}^{\infty}\lambda_{i}e_{i}\right\rVert_{M(X,Y)}-3\varepsilon.

This proves what we wanted.
∎

In the algebraic setting, the above Theorem
4.3
reduces to the following

Corollary 4.4
.

Let
X
be a separable Köthe sequence space with the Fatou property. Then

\left\lVert M_{\lambda}\colon X\circlearrowleft\right\rVert_{ess}=\limsup\limits_{n\rightarrow\infty}\left\lvert\lambda_{n}\right\rvert.

In particular, the multiplication operator
M_{\lambda}\colon X\circlearrowleft
is compact if, and only if,
\lambda\in c_{0}
.

Proof.

Since
M(X,X)\equiv\ell_{\infty}
, so using Theorem

we immediately get

\left\lVert M_{\lambda}\colon X\circlearrowleft\right\rVert_{ess}=\lim\limits_{n\rightarrow\infty}\left\lVert\sum_{i=n}^{\infty}\lambda_{i}e_{i}\right\rVert_{\ell_{\infty}}=\lim\limits_{n\rightarrow\infty}\left(\sup\limits_{i\geqslant n}\left\lvert\lambda_{i}\right\rvert\right)=\limsup\limits_{n\rightarrow\infty}\left\lvert\lambda_{n}\right\rvert.

Theorem 4.5

(Essential norm of multiplication operators between Köthe spaces)
.

Let
X

(\Omega,\Sigma,\mu)

Further, let

Suppose that one of the spaces

\left\lVert M_{\lambda}\colon X\rightarrow Y\right\rVert_{ess}\approx\max\left\{\left\lVert M_{\lambda}\colon X(\Omega_{c})\rightarrow Y(\Omega_{c})\right\rVert_{ess},\left\lVert M_{\lambda}\colon X(\Omega_{a})\rightarrow Y(\Omega_{a})\right\rVert_{ess}\right\}

Proof.

Without loss of generality, we can assume that the space
M(X,Y)
is non-trivial.
Moreover, to make things easier, let us assume that every atom in
\Omega_{a}
is equivalent to a singleton.
Then, since
\Omega=\Omega_{c}\sqcup\Omega_{a}

restricted to
\Omega_{a}
is nothing else but the weighted sum of countably many Dirac measures.
In other words,
\mu(A)=\mu_{c}(A)+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}w_{n}\delta_{a_{n}}(A)
for
A\in\Sigma
,
where
w_{n}
’s are positive real numbers and
a_{n}
’s are atoms in
\Omega_{a}
.

Take
\lambda\in M(X,Y)
.
To show the first inequality let
\{K_{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
be a sequence of compact operators defined as

K_{n}\colon X\rightarrow Y,\quad K_{n}\colon f\rightsquigarrow\left[\omega\rightsquigarrow\lambda(\omega)f(\omega)\chi_{\{a_{1},a_{2},...,a_{n-1}\}}(\omega)\right],

where
n\geqslant 2
. We have

\displaystyle\left\lVert M_{\lambda}\colon X\rightarrow Y\right\rVert_{ess}

\displaystyle\leqslant\left\lVert M_{\lambda}-K_{n}\right\rVert_{X\rightarrow Y}

\displaystyle\leqslant\left\lVert M_{\lambda}-K_{n}\right\rVert_{X(\Omega_{c})\rightarrow Y(\Omega_{c})}+\left\lVert M_{\lambda}-K_{n}\right\rVert_{X(\Omega_{a})\rightarrow Y(\Omega_{a})}

\displaystyle=\left\lVert M_{\lambda}\right\rVert_{X(\Omega_{c})\rightarrow Y(\Omega_{c})}+\left\lVert\text{Proj}_{n}M_{\lambda}\right\rVert_{X(\Omega_{a})\rightarrow Y(\Omega_{a})}.

Now, it follows from Theorem
4.1
and Theorem
4.3
that

\left\lVert M_{\lambda}\colon X(\Omega_{c})\rightarrow Y(\Omega_{c})\right\rVert_{ess}=\left\lVert M_{\lambda}\right\rVert_{X(\Omega_{c})\rightarrow Y(\Omega_{c})},

and, respectively,

\left\lVert M_{\lambda}\colon X(\Omega_{a})\rightarrow Y(\Omega_{a})\right\rVert_{ess}=\left\lVert\text{Proj}_{n}M_{\lambda}\right\rVert_{X(\Omega_{a})\rightarrow Y(\Omega_{a})}.

In consequence, we have

\left\lVert M_{\lambda}\colon X\rightarrow Y\right\rVert_{ess}\leqslant 2\max\left\{\left\lVert M_{\lambda}\colon X(\Omega_{c})\rightarrow Y(\Omega_{c})\right\rVert_{ess},\left\lVert M_{\lambda}\colon X(\Omega_{a})\rightarrow Y(\Omega_{a})\right\rVert_{ess}\right\}.

To prove the second inequality, let us first define two operators

\text{Proj}_{\bullet}^{X}\colon X\rightarrow X(\Omega_{\bullet}),\quad\text{Proj}_{\bullet}^{X}\colon f\rightsquigarrow\left[\omega\rightsquigarrow f(\omega)\chi_{\Omega_{\bullet}}(\omega)\right]

and

\text{Proj}_{\bullet}^{Y}\colon Y\rightarrow Y(\Omega_{\bullet}),\quad\text{Proj}_{\bullet}^{Y}\colon f\rightsquigarrow\left[\omega\rightsquigarrow f(\omega)\chi_{\Omega_{\bullet}}(\omega)\right],

where
\bullet\in\{a,c\}
.
It is straightforward to see that
\text{Proj}_{\bullet}^{X}
and
\text{Proj}_{\bullet}^{Y}
are the norm one projections
onto
X(\Omega_{\bullet})
and, respectively,
Y(\Omega_{\bullet})
.
Now, we have

\displaystyle\left\lVert M_{\lambda}\colon X(\Omega_{\bullet})\rightarrow Y(\Omega_{\bullet})\right\rVert_{ess}

\displaystyle=\inf\left\{\left\lVert M_{\lambda}-K\right\rVert_{X(\Omega_{\bullet})\rightarrow Y(\Omega_{\bullet})}\colon K\in\mathscr{K}(X(\Omega_{\bullet}),Y(\Omega_{\bullet}))\right\}

\displaystyle=\inf\left\{\left\lVert\text{Proj}_{\bullet}^{Y}(M_{\lambda}-K)\text{Proj}_{\bullet}^{X}\right\rVert_{X\rightarrow Y}\colon K\in\mathscr{K}(X,Y)\right\}

\displaystyle\leqslant\inf\left\{\left\lVert M_{\lambda}-K\right\rVert_{X\rightarrow Y}\colon K\in\mathscr{K}(X,Y)\right\}

\displaystyle=\left\lVert M_{\lambda}\colon X\rightarrow Y\right\rVert_{ess}.

Thus,

\max\left\{\left\lVert M_{\lambda}\colon X(\Omega_{c})\rightarrow Y(\Omega_{c})\right\rVert_{ess},\left\lVert M_{\lambda}\colon X(\Omega_{a})\rightarrow Y(\Omega_{a})\right\rVert_{ess}\right\}\leqslant\left\lVert M_{\lambda}\colon X\rightarrow Y\right\rVert_{ess}.

This ends the proof.
∎

4.2.
Measures of non-compactness

Inspired by de Pagter and Schep’s approach taken in
[
PS88
]
, in this section we want to look at Theorem
4.3

from measures of non-compactness’s perspective.

Let
D
be a bounded subset of a Banach space
X
. Recall, that the
Hausdorff measure of non-compactness
\gamma(D)
of
D
is defined to be
the infimum over all
\varepsilon
’s such that
D
can be covered with a finite number of balls with radii
\varepsilon>0
.
Or, what comes to the same thing,

\gamma(D)=\inf\left\{\varepsilon>0\colon D\subset K+\varepsilon\text{Ball}_{X}\text{ with }K\text{ compact}\right\}.

The
measure of non-compactness
of an operator
T\colon X\rightarrow Y
is also denoted by
\gamma
and is given by

\gamma(T\colon X\rightarrow Y)\coloneqq\gamma(T\text{Ball}_{X}).

The functional
\gamma
induces a norm on the quotient
\mathscr{L}(X,Y)/\mathscr{K}(X,Y)
.
However, this norm need not be complete in general (see
[
AT87
, Theorem 2.5]
).
We refer to
[
BG79
]
,
[
Dil87
]
,
[
LS71
]
and
[
PS88
]
for more.

We say that
X
has the
\delta
-approximation property
(briefly, the
\delta
-AP
) whenever for each compact subset, say
D
,
of
X
and for each
\varepsilon>0
, there is a finite rank operator
T\colon X\rightarrow Y
with
\left\lVert T\right\rVert_{X\rightarrow Y}\leqslant\delta

so that
\left\lVert Tx-x\right\rVert_{X\rightarrow Y}\leqslant\varepsilon
for every
x\in D
.
Furthermore,
X
has the
metric approximation property
(briefly, the
MAP
), if it has the
1
-AP
.
Similarly, we say that
X
has the
\delta
-compact approximation property
(briefly, the
\delta
-CAP
),
if in the definition of
\delta
-AP
a finite rank operator is replaced with a compact operator.
Moreover,
X
has the
metric compact approximation property
(briefly, the
MCAP
), if it has the
1
-CAP
.

Remark 4.6
.

Every Banach space with a Schauder basis has the
\delta
-AP
for some
\delta\geqslant 1
(that is to say, the
bounded approximation property BAP
).
Clearly, since a finite rank operators are compact, so the
\delta
-CAP
is formally weaker that the
\delta
-AP
.
Moreover, most Banach spaces are know to have the
\delta
-CAP
for some
\delta\geqslant 1
(that is to say, the
bounded compact approximation property BCAP
).
However, because of Enflo’s example, there exists a Banach space without
BCAP
(see
[
Enf73
]
; see also
[
LT77
, Theorem 1.e.7]
).

\blacktriangle

The main result of this section is the following

Theorem 4.7
.

Let
X
be a Köthe sequence spaces and let
Y
be a rearrangement invariant sequence space. Then

\left\lVert M_{\lambda}\colon X\rightarrow Y\right\rVert_{ess}=\lim\limits_{n\rightarrow\infty}\left\lVert\sum_{i=n}^{\infty}\lambda_{i}e_{i}\right\rVert_{M(X,Y)}.

Proof.

It is straightforward to see that

(4.7)

\gamma(M_{\lambda}\colon X\rightarrow Y)\leqslant\left\lVert M_{\lambda}\colon X\rightarrow Y\right\rVert_{ess}.

Indeed, for any compact operator
K\colon X\rightarrow Y
, it is crystal clear that

\gamma(M_{\lambda}\text{Ball}_{X})=\gamma\left((M_{\lambda}+K)\text{Ball}_{X}\right)\leqslant\left\lVert(M_{\lambda}+K)\text{Ball}_{X}\right\rVert_{Y}\leqslant\left\lVert M_{\lambda}+K\right\rVert_{X\rightarrow Y}.

Moreover, it is a particular part of Lebow and Schechter’s result
[
LS71
, Theorem 3.6]

(later supplemented by Astala and Tylli
[
AT87
, Theorems 2.2 and 2.3]
) that

(4.8)

\left\lVert M_{\lambda}\colon X\rightarrow Y\right\rVert_{ess}\leqslant\delta\gamma(M_{\lambda}\colon X\rightarrow Y)

provided
Y
has the
\delta
-CAP
.
Since, due to our assumptions, the space
Y
is a rearrangement invariant, so it follows from Rao’s result (see
[
Rao70
, Theorem 4.6]
and
[
Rao73
]
)
that
Y
has the
MAP
.
Consequently,
Y
has the
MCAP
, that is,
1
-CAP
.
Then (
4.7
) together with (
4.8
) implies that

(4.9)

\left\lVert M_{\lambda}\colon X\rightarrow Y\right\rVert_{ess}=\gamma(M_{\lambda}\colon X\rightarrow Y).

(It is probably worth mentioning that up this point the above proof goes for any operator
T\colon X\rightarrow Y
,
where
X
and
Y
are Banach spaces such that
Y
has the
MCAP
.) Putting things together, we have

\displaystyle\left\lVert M_{\lambda}\colon X\rightarrow Y\right\rVert_{ess}

\displaystyle=\gamma(M_{\lambda}\colon X\rightarrow Y)

\displaystyle=\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\sup\left\{\left\lVert\sum_{i=n}^{\infty}y_{i}e_{i}\right\rVert_{Y}\colon y=\{y_{i}\}_{i=1}^{\infty}\in M_{\lambda}\text{Ball}_{X}\right\}

\displaystyle=\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\sup\left\{\left\lVert\sum_{i=n}^{\infty}\lambda_{i}x_{i}e_{i}\right\rVert_{Y}\colon x=\{x_{i}\}_{i=1}^{\infty}\in\text{Ball}_{X}\right\}

\displaystyle=\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\left\lVert\sum_{i=n}^{\infty}\lambda_{i}e_{i}\right\rVert_{M(X,Y)},

where the first equality follows from (
4.9
),
while the second equality is a consequence of Banaś and Goebel’s formula
[
BG79
, Theorem 6.1.1, p. 23]

along with the fact that the space
Y
has the ideal property.
∎

4.4.
Weak essential norms

Recall that an operator acting between two Banach spaces is

if it takes bounded subset to relatively weakly compact one.
Equivalently, thanks to the Eberlein–Šmulian theorem, an operator is weakly compact if, and only if, every bounded sequence admits a subsequence
whose image is weakly convergent.

As we saw earlier (see Theorem

function spaces. It is natural, then, to weaken this notion and ask, for example, about weakly compact multipliers.

But, before we proceed to the formulation of the next result, let us remind of a concept that, in a sense, will play one of the main role here.

\bullet

Halperin’s
D
-type Hölder–Rogers inequality (see
[
Hal53
]
),

\bullet

Sawyer’s duality formula (see
[

]
),

\bullet

Halperin’s and Sinnamon’s level functions (see

Let us start with the following preparatory result.

Lemma 5.1

(Separable part of Tandori sequence spaces)
.

Let
X
be a Köthe sequence space. Then

(\widetilde{X})_{o}=\widetilde{X_{o}}.

Proof.

Let us start by recalling the well-known fact:
Let
Z
be a Köthe sequence space. Then

(
\spadesuit
)

z=\{z_{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}\in Z\textit{ belongs to the ideal }Z_{o}\textit{ if, and only if, }\lim\limits_{n\rightarrow\infty}\left\lVert z\chi_{\{n,n+1,...\}}\right\rVert_{Z}=0.

If we keep in mind (
\spadesuit
), the rest of the proof basically follows from the following simple-to-see inequality

(5.1)

\hbox{\hskip 55.55573pt\hskip-26.9017pt\hbox{$x\chi_{\{n,n+1,...\}}$}\hskip-26.9017pt\hskip-55.55573pt\raisebox{-3.69446pt}{\hbox{\scalebox{1}[1.5]{$\mathchoice{\scalebox{2.4}[1]{$\displaystyle\widetilde{\phantom{\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;}}$}}{\scalebox{2.4}[1]{$\textstyle\widetilde{\phantom{\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;}}$}}{\scalebox{2.4}[1]{$\scriptstyle\widetilde{\phantom{\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;}}$}}{\scalebox{2.4}[1]{$\scriptscriptstyle\widetilde{\phantom{\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;}}$}}$}}}\hskip-55.55573pt\hskip 55.55573pt}=\widetilde{x}\chi_{\{n,n+1,...\}}\quad\text{ for all }\quad n\in\mathbb{N}.

In fact, after imposing the norm
\left\lVert\bullet\right\rVert_{X}
on both sides of (
5.1
), we get

(5.2)

\left\lVert x\chi_{\{n,n+1,...\}}\right\rVert_{\widetilde{X}}=\left\lVert\hbox{\hskip 55.55573pt\hskip-26.9017pt\hbox{$x\chi_{\{n,n+1,...\}}$}\hskip-26.9017pt\hskip-55.55573pt\raisebox{-3.69446pt}{\hbox{\scalebox{1}[1.5]{$\mathchoice{\scalebox{2.4}[1]{$\displaystyle\widetilde{\phantom{\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;}}$}}{\scalebox{2.4}[1]{$\textstyle\widetilde{\phantom{\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;}}$}}{\scalebox{2.4}[1]{$\scriptstyle\widetilde{\phantom{\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;}}$}}{\scalebox{2.4}[1]{$\scriptscriptstyle\widetilde{\phantom{\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;}}$}}$}}}\hskip-55.55573pt\hskip 55.55573pt}\right\rVert_{X}=\left\lVert\widetilde{x}\chi_{\{n,n+1,...\}}\right\rVert_{X}\quad\text{ for all }\quad n\in\mathbb{N}.

Now, if
x\in(\widetilde{X})_{o}
, then it follows from (
\spadesuit
) that
\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\left\lVert x\chi_{\{n,n+1,...\}}\right\rVert_{\widetilde{X}}=0
.
Therefore, in view of (
5.2
), also
\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\left\lVert\widetilde{x}\chi_{\{n,n+1,...\}}\right\rVert_{X}=0
.
But this exactly means that
\widetilde{x}\in X_{o}
. Consequently,
x\in\widetilde{X_{o}}
and we have the inclusion
(\widetilde{X})_{o}\subset\widetilde{X_{o}}
.
On the other hand, take
x\in\widetilde{X_{o}}
. Then
\widetilde{x}\in X_{o}
, so
\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\left\lVert\widetilde{x}\chi_{\{n,n+1,...\}}\right\rVert_{X}=0
.
However, using again (
5.2
), we see that
\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\left\lVert x\chi_{\{n,n+1,...\}}\right\rVert_{\widetilde{X}}=0
as well.
This gives us that
x\in(\widetilde{X})_{o}
and, in consequence,
(\widetilde{X})_{o}\supset\widetilde{X_{o}}
.
The proof is complete.
∎

Later, it will be necessary to use some of the results obtained by the authors in
[
KT24
]
.
For this reason, let us briefly recall the following

Construction 5.2
.

Let
X
be a rearrangement invariant sequence space.
With
X
we can associate a Köthe sequence space
\mathbf{E}(X)
defined as a vector space

\mathbf{E}(X)\coloneqq\left\{x=\{x_{n}\}_{n=0}^{\infty}\colon\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}x_{n}\chi_{\Delta_{n}}\in X\right\}

and equipped with the norm
\left\lVert x\right\rVert_{\mathbf{E}(X)}\coloneqq\left\lVert\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}x_{n}\chi_{\Delta_{n}}\right\rVert_{X}
.
Here,
\Delta_{j}
is the
n^{\text{th}}
dyadic interval, that is,
\Delta_{n}\coloneqq\{2^{n},2^{n}+1,2^{n+1}-1\}
for
n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
.

\blacktriangle

Interestingly, the above construction goes back to Kalton
[
Kal93
]
.
However, in
[
KT24
]
it was used to obtain an abstract variant of the so-called Grosse-Erdmann’s blocking technique (see
[
GE98
]
).

Now we are ready to present and also prove the main result of this section.

Theorem 5.3

(Essential norm of multipliers acting between abstract Cesàro and Tandori sequence spaces)
.

Let
X
and
Y
be two rearrangement invariant sequence spaces with the Fatou property.
Suppose that either the space
X
is reflexive or the space
Y
is separable.
Further, suppose that either the space
{\bf E}(Y)
factorizes though
{\bf E}(X)
, that is,

{\bf E}(Y)={\bf E}(X)\odot M({\bf E}(X),{\bf E}(Y)),

or that both spaces
X
and
Y
are strongly separated, that is,

\alpha_{X}>\beta_{Y}.

Then, we have

\left\lVert M_{\lambda}\colon\widetilde{X}\rightarrow\widetilde{Y}\right\rVert_{ess}\approx\lim\limits_{n\rightarrow\infty}\left\lVert\widetilde{\lambda}\chi_{\{n,n+1,...\}}\right\rVert_{M(X,Y)}.

Additionally, if Hardy’s operator
\mathscr{H}
is bounded on both spaces
X
and
Y
, then also

\left\lVert M_{\lambda}\colon CX\rightarrow CY\right\rVert_{ess}\approx\lim\limits_{n\rightarrow\infty}\left\lVert\widetilde{\lambda}\chi_{\{n,n+1,...\}}\right\rVert_{M(X,Y)}.

Moreover, both of the above estimates depend on
X
and
Y
only.
In particular, the multiplication operator
M_{\lambda}\colon\begin{array}[]{l}CX\rightarrow CY\\
\;\;\>\widetilde{X}\rightarrow\widetilde{Y}\end{array}

is compact if, and only if, the function
\widetilde{\lambda}=\left\{\widetilde{\lambda_{n}}\right\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
belongs to the ideal
M(X,Y)_{o}
.

Proof.

As a warm-up, let us explain the following, not particularly difficult issues, that will be very useful later.

(a) Due to our assumption about the boundedness of Hardy’s operator
\mathscr{H}
on
X
and
Y
, both spaces
CX
and
CY
are non-trivial
(this easily follows from the definition of the Cesàro space construction, however, this statement is also a form of Hardy’s inequality;
see
[
Ben96
, p. 1]
and
[
KMP07
, pp. 19–21 and 147–159]
).

(b) Let
Z
be a Köthe sequence space with the Fatou property. Then both spaces
CZ
and
\widetilde{Z}
have the Fatou property
(see, for example,
[
LM15
, Theorem 1(d)]
; although, formally, the proof given in
[
LM15
]
covers only Köthe function spaces,
it works just as well for their sequence counterparts).

(c) Both spaces
\widetilde{Z}
and
CZ
are order continuous provided
Z
is an order continuous Köthe sequence space
(this is a direct consequence of Lemma
5.1
and a nearly direct consequence of Lemma 11 in
[
KT17
]
;
cf. the proof of Proposition 5.1 in
[
KKT22
]
).

(d) Lastly, we claim that

Claim
\diamondsuit
.

Let
Z
be a reflexive rearrangement invariant sequence space with the Fatou property.
Suppose that the Hardy’s operator
\mathscr{H}
is bounded on
Z
.
Then both spaces
\widetilde{Z}
and
CZ
are reflexive.

Proof of Claim
\diamondsuit

Both spaces are related to each other through duality, so we will limit ourselves to the proof in the case of the Cesàro space construction.

Since the space
Z
is reflexive and has the Fatou property, so it follows from
[
BS88
, Corollary 4.4, p. 23]
that both spaces
Z
and
Z^{\times}

are order continuous. Because of (b) and, one more time,
[
BS88
, Corollary 4.4, p. 23]
, to complete the proof it is enough to show that the spaces

CZ
and
(CZ)^{\times}
are order continuous. Now, in the first case, that is, when we consider
CZ
, it results immediately from (c),
while in the second case, that is, when we consider
(CZ)^{\times}
, things are a bit more complicated.
Here, using the Leśnik and Maligranda duality results from
[
LM15
]
, we have

(CX)^{\times}=\widetilde{X^{\times}}

(see
[
LM15
, Theorem 6]
). But, with Lemma
5.1
in mind, we also have

\left((CX)^{\times}\right)_{o}=\left(\widetilde{X^{\times}}\right)_{o}=\widetilde{(X^{\times})_{o}}=\widetilde{X^{\times}}=(CX)^{\times}.

This means that also the space
(CX)^{\times}
is order continuous. That is all we needed to finish the proof of Claim
\diamondsuit
.

\blacksquare

Now, the essential part of the proof is based on the following three steps.

First step.
Due to Theorem 6.a.3 from
[
KT24
]
(see also
[
KLM19
, Theorem 6]
and
[
Ben96
, pp. 67–77]
), we have

(5.3)

M(CX,CY)=\widetilde{M(X,Y)}

and

(5.4)

M(\widetilde{X},\widetilde{Y})=\widetilde{M(X,Y)}.

In consequence,

\left\lVert M_{\lambda}\colon\begin{array}[]{l}CX\rightarrow CY\\
\;\;\>\widetilde{X}\rightarrow\widetilde{Y}\end{array}\right\rVert\approx\left\lVert\lambda\right\rVert_{\widetilde{M(X,Y)}}.

Second step.
In view of first step, (a), (b), (c) and (d) there is noting to prevent us from using Theorem
4.3
.
Thus, we get the formula

\left\lVert M_{\lambda}\colon CX\rightarrow CY\right\rVert_{ess}\approx\lim\limits_{n\rightarrow\infty}\left\lVert\lambda\chi_{\{n,n+1,...\}}\right\rVert_{\widetilde{M(X,Y)}}.

Moreover, this means that the multiplication operator
M_{\lambda}\colon\begin{array}[]{l}CX\rightarrow CY\\
\;\;\>\widetilde{X}\rightarrow\widetilde{Y}\end{array}

is compact if, and only if,
\lambda\in\left(\widetilde{M(X,Y)}\right)_{o}
.
However, as shown in Lemma
5.1
,

\left(\widetilde{M(X,Y)}\right)_{o}=\widetilde{M(X,Y)_{o}}.

Therefore,

\lambda\in\left(\widetilde{M(X,Y)}\right)_{o}\quad\text{ if, and only if, }\quad\lambda\in\widetilde{M(X,Y)_{o}}.

The latter clearly means that
\widetilde{\lambda}\in M(X,Y)_{o}
.

Third step.
Finally, using the formula (
5.2
) form the proof of Lemma
5.1
, we have

\left\lVert\lambda\chi_{\{n,n+1,...\}}\right\rVert_{\widetilde{M(X,Y)}}=\left\lVert\hbox{\hskip 55.55573pt\hskip-26.96075pt\hbox{$\lambda\chi_{\{n,n+1,...\}}$}\hskip-26.96075pt\hskip-55.55573pt\raisebox{-1.05556pt}{\hbox{\scalebox{1}[1.5]{$\mathchoice{\scalebox{2.4}[1]{$\displaystyle\widetilde{\phantom{\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;}}$}}{\scalebox{2.4}[1]{$\textstyle\widetilde{\phantom{\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;}}$}}{\scalebox{2.4}[1]{$\scriptstyle\widetilde{\phantom{\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;}}$}}{\scalebox{2.4}[1]{$\scriptscriptstyle\widetilde{\phantom{\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;}}$}}$}}}\hskip-55.55573pt\hskip 55.55573pt}\right\rVert_{M(X,Y)}=\left\lVert\widetilde{\lambda}\chi_{\{n,n+1,...\}}\right\rVert_{M(X,Y)}.

∎

Algebraic situation is, of course, much simpler.

Corollary 5.4
.

Let
X
be a separable Köthe sequence space with the Fatou property. Then

\left\lVert M_{\lambda}\colon\begin{array}[]{l}CX\circlearrowleft\\
\;\,\widetilde{X}\circlearrowleft\end{array}\right\rVert_{ess}=\limsup\limits_{n\rightarrow\infty}\left\lvert\lambda_{n}\right\rvert.

In particular, the multiplication operator
M_{\lambda}\colon\begin{array}[]{l}CX\circlearrowleft\\
\;\,\widetilde{X}\circlearrowleft\end{array}

is compact if, and only if,
\lambda\in c_{0}
.

Proof.

This result is an easy consequence of Corollary
4.4
and (c) from the proof of Theorem
5.3
.
Indeed, since
M(CX,CX)\equiv\ell_{\infty}
and the space
CX
is order continuous (this follows from our assumptions together with
[
LM16
, Lemma 1]
;
cf.
[
KKT22
, Proposition 5.1]
), so

\left\lVert M_{\lambda}\colon CX\circlearrowleft\right\rVert_{ess}=\lim\limits_{n\rightarrow\infty}\left\lVert\lambda\chi_{\{n,n+1,...\}}\right\rVert_{\ell_{\infty}}=\lim\limits_{n\rightarrow\infty}\left(\sup\limits_{m\geqslant n}\left\lvert\lambda_{m}\right\rvert\right)=\limsup\limits_{n\rightarrow\infty}\left\lvert\lambda_{n}\right\rvert.

The part concerning Tandori spaces is proved in a completely analogous way.
∎

Let us close this section with two examples concerning the compact multipliers between Cesáro–Orlicz sequence spaces.
For a Young function
M
, by the
Cesáro–Orlicz sequence space
ces_{M}
we understand here the space
C\ell_{M}
,
where
\ell_{M}
is the well-known Orlicz sequence space.

Example 5.5
.

Let
M
and
N
be two Young functions such that either
M
or
N
and its Young conjugate
N^{*}

satisfy the
\Delta_{2}
-condition for small arguments (see
[
Mal83
]
for details).
Suppose that
\alpha_{\ell_{M}}>1
and
\alpha_{\ell_{N}}>1
(equivalently, we can use the so-called
Matuszewska–Orlicz indices
\alpha_{M}
and
\alpha_{N}
here; see
[
LT79
, Proposition 2.b.5, p. 139]
).
Further, suppose that
M(\ell_{M},\ell_{N})\neq\ell_{\infty}
.
Then, due to
[
KT24
, Example 6.b.2]
, we have

(5.5)

M(ces_{M},ces_{N})=\widetilde{\ell_{N\ominus M}}.

Here, the function
N\ominus M
is the so-called generalized Young conjugate of
M
with respect to
N
, that is,

(N\ominus M)(t)\coloneqq\sup_{t>0}\left\{N(ts)-M(s)\right\}
(see
[
DR00
]
and
[
LT21
]
).
In consequence, using Theorem
5.3
, we see that the multiplication operator

M_{\lambda}\colon ces_{M}\rightarrow ces_{N}
is compact if, and only if,
\widetilde{\lambda}\in(\ell_{N\ominus M})_{o}
.
Moreover, it follows from
[
Mal83
, Theorem 3.3(a), p. 17]
, that the ideal
(\ell_{N\ominus M})_{o}
coincide
with the space of finite elements in
\ell_{N\ominus M}
, that is,

(\ell_{N\ominus M})_{o}=\left\{x=\{x_{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}\colon\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}(N\ominus M)(\eta x_{n})<\infty\text{ for all }\eta>0\right\}.

A more classical situation is as follows. Let
1<p<q<\infty
with
1/r=1/p-1/q
.
Then, using (
5.5
) (see also
[
Ben96
, p. 69]
and
[
KT24
, Corollary 6.b.4]
), it is straightforward to see that

M(ces_{q},ces_{p})=\widetilde{\ell_{r}}.

Therefore, the multiplication operator
M_{\lambda}\colon ces_{q}\rightarrow ces_{p}
is compact if, and only if,
\widetilde{\lambda}\in\ell_{r}
.

\blacktriangle

Example 5.6
.

Let
M
and
N
be two Young functions exactly as in Example
5.5

except that now we assume that
M(\ell_{M},\ell_{N})=\ell_{\infty}
(or, which is one thing,
\ell_{M}\hookrightarrow\ell_{N}
).
Again, using
[
KT24
, Example 6.b.1]
, we have

(5.6)

M(ces_{M},ces_{N})=\ell_{\infty}(w_{M\to N}),

where
w_{M\to N}(n)\coloneqq M^{-1}(1/n)/N^{-1}(1/n)
for
n\in\mathbb{N}
.
Therefore, this time, the multiplication operator
M_{\lambda}\colon ces_{M}\rightarrow ces_{N}
is compact if, and only if,

\left\{\frac{M^{-1}(1/n)}{N^{-1}(1/n)}\lambda_{n}\right\}_{n=1}^{\infty}\in c_{0}.

In particular, for
1<p<q<\infty
, we have

(5.7)

M(ces_{p},ces_{q})=\ell_{\infty}\left(w_{p\to q}\right),

where
w_{p\to q}=n^{1/q-1/p}
for
n\in\mathbb{N}
, so the multiplication operator
M_{\lambda}\colon ces_{p}\rightarrow ces_{q}

is compact if, and only if,
\left\{n^{1/q-1/p}\lambda_{n}\right\}_{n=1}^{\infty}\in c_{0}
.
In this way, we recreated Albanese, Bonet and Ricker’s result from
[
ABR18
]
(precisely, see
[
ABR18
, Proposition 2.2]
).

\blacktriangle

Remark 5.7
.

Let
1<p<q<\infty
with

Using once again (

) (that is, Theorem 6.a.3 from
[

]
) in tandem with

5.5
and

,
it is straightforward to see that the multiplication operators

and

and

\blacktriangle

5.2.
Abstract Cesàro function spaces

Let
X
is a Köthe function space defined on the unit interval
(0,1)
or the semi-axis
(0,\infty)

both equipped with the usual Lebesgue measure.
Then, the
continuous Hardy operator

\mathscr{H}
is defined by

\mathscr{H}\colon f\rightsquigarrow\left[x\rightsquigarrow\frac{1}{x}\int_{0}^{x}f(t)dt\right]

and the corresponding
abstract Cesàro function space
CX
, is defined as the vector space of all real-valued measurable
functions, say
f
, such that
\mathscr{H}(\left\lvert f\right\rvert)\in X
, equipped with the norm
\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{CX}\coloneqq\left\lVert\mathscr{H}(\left\lvert f\right\rvert)\right\rVert_{X}
.

Here, we do not really need more than the above definition, but the isomorphic and isometric structure of the Cesàro function spaces
has been the subject of extensive research and has many non-trivial results to offer (see, for example,
[
ALM19
]
,
[
CR16
]
,
[
DS07
]
,

[
KKM21
]
,
[
KKT22
]
,
[
KMS07
]
,
[
KT17
]
,
[
KT24
]
,
[
LM15
]
,
[
LM16
]
and
[
MS06
]
; see also a survey
[
AM14
]
and references therein).

Let us get to the point (for unexplained notation, we refer directly to
[
KT24
]
).

Theorem 5.8

(Essential norm of multiplication operators between abstract Cesàro function spaces)
.

Let
X
and
Y
be two Köthe function spaces with the Fatou property both defined on the semi-axis
(0,\infty)
equipped with the Lebesgue measure.
Suppose that either the space
X
is separable or the space
Y
is reflexive.
Further, suppose that either the space
{\bf E}(Y)
factorizes though
{\bf E}(X)
, that is,

{\bf E}(Y)={\bf E}(X)\odot M({\bf E}(X),{\bf E}(Y)),

or that both spaces
X
and
Y
are strongly separated, that is,

\alpha_{X}>\beta_{Y}.

Then, we have

\left\lVert M_{\lambda}\colon CX\rightarrow CY\right\rVert_{ess}\approx\left\lVert\lambda\right\rVert_{\widetilde{M(X,Y)}}=\left\lVert M_{\lambda}\colon CX\rightarrow CY\right\rVert

and the above estimate depend on
X
and
Y
only.
Therefore, in particular, there is a lack of compact multiplication operators acting between two abstract Cesàro function spaces.

Proof.

The proof of this fact is to mimic the argument used in the proof of Theorem
5.3
. And so:

(
\alpha
) Since Hardy’s operator
\mathscr{H}
is bounded on both spaces
X
and
Y
, so it follows from the optimal domain’s point of view
that the spaces
CX
and
CY
are both non-trivial. Note, however, that the boundedness of Hardy’s operator
\mathscr{H}
is in general not
necessary for a non-triviality of the Cesàro function space (see Example 3.3(d) and Example 3.3(f) in
[
KKM21
]
).

(
\beta
) As in the sequence case, the Fatou property and order continuity property are inherited by the corresponding Cesàro function space
(here we need
[
LM15
, Lemma 1(d)]
and
[
KT17
, Lemma 11]
, respectively). Note also that the abstract Cesàro function spaces
are
never
reflexive, because, for example, they contain a complemented copy of
L_{1}(0,1)
(see
[
KKM21
, Lemma 4.1]
).

(
\gamma
) In view of the two previous steps, we can now use Theorem
4.1
to conclude that

\left\lVert M_{\lambda}\colon CX\rightarrow CY\right\rVert_{ess}\approx\left\lVert\lambda\right\rVert_{M(CX,CY)}=\left\lVert M_{\lambda}\colon CX\rightarrow CY\right\rVert.

(
\delta
) Finally, due to our assumptions and
[
KT24
, Theorem 6.a.3]
(see also
[
KLM19
, Theorem 6]
), we have

M(CX,CY)=\widetilde{M(X,Y)}.

All this together with Theorem
4.1
completes the proof.
∎

Let
1<p\leqslant\infty
.
Recall, that the
classical Cesàro function spaces
Ces_{p}
is defined as
Ces_{p}\coloneqq CL_{p}
.
It was proved by Al Alam, Gaillard, Habib, Lefèvre and Maalouf in
[
AGH+18
]
that the essential norm
of the multiplier
M_{\lambda}\colon Ces_{p}(0,1)\rightarrow Ces_{p}(0,1)
is precisely equal to sup-norm of the symbol
\lambda
(see
[
AGH+18
, Theorem 5.2]
).
Thus, for the sake of completeness, let us also formulate the following result.

Corollary 5.9
.

Let
X
be a separable Köthe function spaces with the Fatou property defined either on the semi-axis
(0,\infty)

or the unit interval
(0,1)
equipped with the Lebesgue measure. Then

\left\lVert M_{\lambda}\colon CX\circlearrowleft\right\rVert_{ess}=\left\lVert\lambda\right\rVert_{L_{\infty}}=\left\lVert M_{\lambda}\colon CX\circlearrowleft\right\rVert.

In particular, there are no non-trivial compact multiplication operators acting from
CX
into itself.

Proof.

To begin with, recall that
M(CX,CX)\equiv L_{\infty}
. Moreover, it follows from
[
KT17
, Lemma 11]
and, respectively,
[
LM15
, Lemma 1(d)]

that if
X
is order continuous or, respectively, has the Fatou property, then the same can be said about the space
CX
. Now, this result is an immediate
consequence of Corollary
4.2
.
∎

5.3.
Banach spaces of analytic functions

In this subsection, we will improve and complete the main result from Mleczko’s paper
[
Mle09
]
(precisely, see
[
Mle09
, Theorem 2.5]
).
However, before we do that, we need short preliminaries to clarify our notation.

Let
\mathscr{H}(\mathbb{D})
be the space of all analytic functions on the unit disc
\mathbb{D}\coloneqq\{z\in\mathbb{C}\colon\left\lvert z\right\rvert<1\}
.
Further, let
E
be a Köthe sequence space. With a linear subspace, say
H(\mathbb{D})
, of
\mathscr{H}(\mathbb{D})
we can associate
the space

\widehat{H}(\mathbb{D})\coloneqq\left\{\left\{\widehat{f}(n)\right\}_{n=0}^{\infty}\colon\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\widehat{f}(n)\chi_{n}\in H(\mathbb{D})\right\}

of Taylor’s coefficients of functions from
H(\mathbb{D})
, where
\chi_{n}(z)=z^{n}
for
z\in\mathbb{D}
and
n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
.
Then with any sequence
\lambda=\{\lambda_{n}\}_{n=0}^{\infty}
from
M(\widehat{H}(\mathbb{D}),E)
we can associate its
Fourier multiplier operator

\mathscr{M}_{\lambda}\colon H(\mathbb{D})\rightarrow E
defined by

\mathscr{M}_{\lambda}\colon f\rightsquigarrow\left\{\lambda_{n}\widehat{f}(n)\right\}_{n=0}^{\infty},

where
f=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\widehat{f}(n)\chi_{n}\in H(\mathbb{D})
for
n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
.

Let
X
be a Köthe space defined on the unit circle
\mathbb{T}\coloneqq\{z\in\mathbb{C}\colon\left\lvert z\right\rvert=1\}

equipped with the normalized Lebesgue measure
dm(t)=\left\lvert dt\right\rvert/2\pi
.
Then the
abstract Hardy space
H[X]
is defined as a vector space

H[X]\coloneqq\left\{f\in X\colon\widehat{f}(n)=0\text{ for all }n<0\right\}

furnished with the norm inherited from
X
. Equivalently, via convolution with the Poisson kernel, we can treat the Hardy space
H[X]

as a space of analytic functions on the unit disc
\mathbb{D}
. In particular, the space
H[L_{p}]
with
1\leqslant p\leqslant\infty

is nothing else, but the
classical Hardy space

H_{p}
. For much more information about the theory of Hardy spaces we refer to Duren’s monograph
[
Dur76
]
.

We are ready to prove the promised result.

Theorem 5.10

(Essential norms of Fourier multipliers)
.

Let
H(\mathbb{D})
be a Banach space of analytic function on the unit disc
\mathbb{D}
intermediate between
H_{\infty}(\mathbb{D})

and
H_{2}(\mathbb{D})
. Moreover, let
E
be a separable Köthe sequence space with the Fatou property. Then

\left\lVert\mathscr{M}_{\lambda}\colon H(\mathbb{D})\rightarrow E\right\rVert_{ess}=\lim\limits_{n\rightarrow\infty}\left\lVert\sum_{i=n}^{\infty}\lambda_{i}e_{i}\right\rVert_{M(\ell_{2},E)}.

In particular, the Fourier multiplier
\mathscr{M}_{\lambda}\colon H(\mathbb{D})\rightarrow E
is compact if, and only if,
\lambda\in M(\ell_{2},E)_{o}
.

Proof.

The proof basically consists of two steps.

First step
. First, we need to transfer the situation to our
“ideal”
word. This can be done using the so-called solid envelopes
together with Anderson and Shield’s results from
[
AS76
]
. More precisely, thanks to our assumption that

H_{\infty}(\mathbb{D})\hookrightarrow H(\mathbb{D})\hookrightarrow H_{2}(\mathbb{D})

and, moreover, knowing that the solid envelope of
H_{\infty}(\mathbb{D})
and
H_{2}(\mathbb{D})
is
\ell_{2}

(the harder part of this result is essentially due to S. Kisliakov; see
[
Mle09
, Proposition 1.1]
), it is not difficult to see that the following diagram commutes

\textstyle{H(\mathbb{D})\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
id
\scriptstyle{\mathscr{M}_{\lambda}}
\textstyle{E}
\textstyle{H_{2}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\mathscr{F}}
\textstyle{\ell_{2}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{M_{\lambda}}

Here
\lambda\in\widehat{H}(\mathbb{D})
and
\mathscr{F}(f)\coloneqq\left\{\widehat{f}(n)\right\}_{n=0}^{\infty}
for
f\in H(\mathbb{D})

(see
[
Mle09
, Corollary 1.2]
). But this clearly means that

\mathscr{M}(H(\mathbb{D}),E)=M(\ell_{2},E).

Second step
. Since we assumed that the space
E
is separable, so to finish the proof it is enough to apply Theorem
4.3
.
∎

(About Theorem

)

Let

Suppose that

Corollary 5.12

(P. Mleczko, 2009)

Let
H(\mathbb{D})
be a Banach space of analytic function on the unit disc
\mathbb{D}
intermediate between
H_{\infty}(\mathbb{D})

and
H_{2}(\mathbb{D})
. Moreover, let
E
be a separable Köthe sequence space with the Fatou property intermediate between

\ell_{2}

. Then the Fourier multiplier

\limsup_{n\rightarrow\infty}\left\lvert\lambda_{n}\right\rvert=0.

With the extra assumption that

\displaystyle\lim\limits_{n\rightarrow\infty}\left\lVert\sum_{i=n}^{\infty}\lambda_{i}e_{i}\right\rVert_{M(\ell_{2},E)}

\displaystyle=\lim\limits_{n\rightarrow\infty}\left\lVert\sum_{i=n}^{\infty}\lambda_{i}e_{i}\right\rVert_{\ell_{\infty}}

\displaystyle=\lim\limits_{n\rightarrow\infty}\left(\sup_{m\geqslant n}\left\lvert\lambda_{m}\right\rvert\right)

\displaystyle=\limsup_{n\rightarrow\infty}\left\lvert\lambda_{n}\right\rvert.

6.
Arithmetic, remarks and examples

Theorem
4.3
provides a simple recipe to check whether
the pointwise multiplier
M_{\lambda}\colon X\rightarrow Y
is compact.
Namely, it is enough to first calculate the space of pointwise multipliers
M(X,Y)
and, then,
find its separable part, that is, the ideal
M(X,Y)_{o}
.
The functions belonging to
M(X,Y)_{o}
are exactly those symbols that generate compact multipliers.

In general, however, the description of
M(X,Y)_{o}
is impossible.
The main reason for this is the observation that the space
M(X,Y)
depends on the
“relative”

position of the spaces
X
and
Y
. For instance,
M(\ell_{1},\ell_{\infty})\equiv\ell_{\infty}

and
M(\ell_{\infty},\ell_{1})\equiv\ell_{1}
.
Anyhow, even when
X
and
Y
belong to some class of rearrangement invariant spaces, like, for example,
Orlicz spaces, the description of the space
M(X,Y)
in the language of
X
and
Y
is a difficult task
with only piecemeal solutions scattered throughout the existing literature.
Actually, the example of Orlicz spaces is an exceptionally appropriate example here.
After many partial results and almost half a century of struggle, this problem has only recently been
put to rest by Djakov and Ramanujan in
[
DR00
]
, and Leśnik and the second-named author in
[
LT17
]

(we refer to
[
LT17
]
for a more detailed discussion).

That said, from this point on, we will focus on some important classes of rearrangement invariant sequence
spaces like, for example, Orlicz sequence spaces, Lorentz sequence spaces and Marcinkiewicz sequence spaces.
After the discussion on Musielak–Orlicz sequence spaces, our goal is to prove Theorem
6.4
.

En route
to this result we will show a series of lemmas (see Lemma
6.5
, Lemma
6.6

and Lemma
6.7
) that are of independent interest.

6.1.
A few thoughts about Musielak–Orlicz sequence spaces

Let
\{M_{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
be a sequence of Orlicz functions
25
25

25

By an
Orlicz function
M\colon[0,\infty)\rightarrow[0,\infty)

we understand a convex and strictly increasing function vanishing at zero. We decided to use only the Orlicz functions here instead of the more
general Young functions (which can
“jump”
to infinity) to avoid some unnecessary complications.
.
Recall, that the
Musielak–Orlicz sequence space

\ell_{\{M_{n}\}}
is a vector space of all sequences
x=\{x_{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}

with
\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}M_{n}(\left\lvert x_{n}\right\rvert/\varrho)<\infty
for some
\varrho>0
, equipped with the
Luxemburg–Nakano norm

\left\lVert x\right\rVert_{\ell_{\{M_{n}\}}}\coloneqq\inf\left\{\varrho>0\colon\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}M_{n}\left(\frac{x_{n}}{\varrho}\right)\leqslant 1\right\}.

This general construction includes two very important types of spaces.
On the one hand, the
Nakano sequence spaces
\ell_{\{p_{n}\}}
(or
variable exponent Lebesgue–Riesz sequence spaces
),
which are nothing else but Musielak–Orlicz sequence spaces
\ell_{\{M_{n}\}}
with all generating functions
M_{n}
being just power functions,
that is,
M_{n}(t)=t^{p_{n}}
for some
p_{n}\geqslant 1
.
On the other, the
Orlicz sequence spaces
\ell_{M}
are Musielak–Orlicz sequence spaces
\ell_{\{M_{n}\}}
with
“constant”

sequence of Orlicz functions
\{M_{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
, that is, with
M_{n}=M
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
.

We refer to the book of Cruz-Uribe and Fiorenza
[
CUF13
]
, the monograph of Lindenstrauss and Tzafriri
[
LT77
, Chapter 4]

and the book of Maligranda
[
Mal89
]
for much more information about variable Lebesgue spaces, Musielak–Orlicz sequence spaces and Orlicz spaces.

Remark 6.1

(About compact multipliers between Musielak–Orlicz sequence spaces)
.

Here we want to roughly explain the general technology needed to describe compact multipliers
acting between
\ell_{\{M_{n}\}}
and
\ell_{\{N_{n}\}}
.

It follows from
[
LT21
]
that the space of pointwise multipliers between two Musielak–Orlicz
sequence spaces is another Musielak–Orlicz sequence space.
In our setting, using Theorem 9 from
[
LT21
]
, we have

M\left(\ell_{\{M_{n}\}},\ell_{\{N_{n}\}}\right)=\ell_{\{N_{n}\ominus M_{n}\}},

where

(N_{n}\ominus M_{n})(t)\coloneqq\sup\limits_{0\leqslant s\leqslant 1}[N_{n}(ts)-M_{n}(s)]\quad\text{ for }\quad n\in\mathbb{N}

is the
generalized Young conjugate
of
M_{n}
with respect to
N_{n}
.

Next, Kolwicz and Panfil in
[
KP12
]
gave a complete description of a separable part of the Musielak–Orlicz space
(in fact, they allowed for an even more general construction).
Precisely, it can be deduced from Theorem 11 in
[
KP12
]
that a sequence
x=\{x_{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}

has an order continuous norm in
\ell_{\{M_{n}\}}
if, and only if, the sequence of Orlicz functions
\{M_{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}

satisfies the
\Delta_{2}(x)
-condition
(see
[
KP12
, Definition 1]
).

The rest is essentially a matter of collecting existing results. Thanks to Theorem
4.3
,
the multiplication operator
M_{\lambda}\colon\ell_{\{M_{n}\}}\rightarrow\ell_{\{N_{n}\}}
is compact if, and only if,
the sequence
\{N_{n}\ominus M_{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
satisfies the
\Delta_{2}(\lambda)
-condition.

\blacktriangle

Even though the necessary calculations from Remark
6.1
can be done quite easily in a concrete situation,
the entire method can hardly be called effective (let alone the case when we allow the more general Young functions in place of Orlicz functions).
Anyway, sacrificing a little generality on the altar of clarity, we have the following

Theorem 6.2

(Compact multipliers between Nakano sequence spaces)
.

Let
\{p_{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
and
\{q_{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
be two sequences of real numbers with
1\leqslant p_{n}\leqslant q_{n}<\infty

for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
. Suppose that either

1<\inf\limits_{n\in\mathbb{N}}p_{n}\leqslant\sup\limits_{n\in\mathbb{N}}p_{n}<\infty

or

\sup\limits_{n\in\mathbb{N}}q_{n}<\infty.

Then the multiplication operator
M_{\lambda}\colon\ell_{\{q_{n}\}}\rightarrow\ell_{\{p_{n}\}}
is compact if, and only if,

\forall\ell>1\colon\lim\limits_{k\rightarrow\infty}\sum_{\left\{n\in\supp(\lambda)\colon r_{n}>\frac{\log k}{\log\ell}\right\}}\left(\left\lvert\lambda_{n}\right\rvert\ell\right)^{r_{n}}=0,

where
1/r_{n}=1/q_{n}-1/p_{n}
for
n\in\mathbb{N}
.

Proof.

To make things clear, let us divide the proof into a few short steps.

First step.
It is part of folklore that the Nakano space
\ell_{\{s_{n}\}}
is order continuous if, and only if,
\sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}s_{n}<\infty
.
In consequence, using duality and
[
BS88
, Corollary 4.4, p. 23]
, the space
\ell_{\{s_{n}\}}
is reflexive if, and only if,
\inf_{n\in\mathbb{N}}s_{n}>1

and
\sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}s_{n}<\infty
.

Second step.
Suppose that
1\leqslant p_{n}\leqslant q_{n}<\infty
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
.
Let
1/r_{n}=1/q_{n}-1/p_{n}
for
n\in\mathbb{N}
. Then, it follows from
[
LT21
, Theorem 9]

(see also Corollary 10 in
[
LT21
]
) that

M\left(\ell_{\{p_{n}\}},\ell_{\{q_{n}\}}\right)=\ell_{\{q_{n}\ominus p_{n}\}}=\ell_{\{r_{n}\}}.

Third step.
Next, due to
[
KP12
, Theorem 11]
, the sequence
x=\{x_{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
belongs to the separable part of the
Musielak–Orlicz sequence space
\ell_{\{M_{n}\}}
if, and only if, for each
\ell>1
, we have

\lim_{k\rightarrow\infty}\left\lVert\sum_{n\in A_{k}^{\ell}}M_{n}\left(\ell x_{n}\right)e_{n}\right\rVert_{\ell_{1}}=0.

Here,
A_{k}^{\ell}\coloneqq\left\{n\in\supp(x)\colon M_{n}(\ell x_{n})>kM_{n}(x_{n})\right\}
.
However, in our situation, we need to apply the above criterion to the Nakano sequence space
\ell_{\{r_{n}\}}
.
Thus, after some simple calculations, it is easy to see that the sequence
x=\{x_{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}

belongs to the ideal
(\ell_{\{r_{n}\}})_{o}
if, and only if,

\lim_{k\rightarrow\infty}\left\lVert\sum_{n\in A_{k}^{\ell}}\left(\ell x_{n}\right)^{r_{n}}e_{n}\right\rVert_{\ell_{1}}=0.

Here,

A_{k}^{\ell}=\left\{n\in\supp(x)\colon r_{n}>\frac{\log k}{\log\ell}\right\}.

Fourth step.
Now, to complete the proof, it is enough to use the previous steps and Theorem
4.3
.
∎

6.2.
Lorentz and Marcinkiewicz’s setting

Let
\varphi
be a positive, increasing and concave function on
(0,\infty)
.
By the
Lorentz sequence space
\lambda_{\varphi}
and the
Marcinkiewicz sequence space
m_{\varphi}
,
we understand the spaces of all sequences
x=\{x_{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
for which the following norms

\left\lVert x\right\rVert_{\lambda_{\varphi}}\coloneqq\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}x_{n}^{\star}(\varphi(n+1)-\varphi(n))

and, respectively,

\left\lVert x\right\rVert_{m_{\varphi}}\coloneqq\sup\limits_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\frac{\varphi(n)}{n}\sum_{k=1}^{n}x_{k}^{\star}

are finite. Here,
x^{\star}=\{x_{n}^{\star}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
is the decreasing rearrangement of the sequence
x=\{x_{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
.
In the case when
\varphi(n)=n^{1/p}
with
1<p<\infty
, the corresponding Marcinkiewicz sequence space
m_{\varphi}

is denoted by
\ell_{p,\infty}
and is sometimes called the
weak
\ell_{p}
-space
.

Remark 6.3

(Separable part of
\lambda_{\varphi}
and
m_{\varphi}
)
.

It is well-known that the Lorentz sequence space
\lambda_{\varphi}
is separable if, and only if,
\varphi(\infty)=\infty

(see, for example,
[
KPS82
, Lemma 5.1, p. 110]
; cf.
[
Kol16
, Corollary 4.20]
together with the note on p. 820 about the sequence case).
Note also that if
\varphi(\infty)<\infty
, then
\lambda_{\varphi}=\ell_{\infty}
. Thus, in this case,
(\lambda_{\varphi})_{o}=c_{0}
.
On the other hand, the Marcinkiewicz sequence spaces
m_{\varphi}
are never separable (see, for example,
[
Kol16
, Remark 4.4(ii)]
).
However, the separable part
(m_{\varphi})_{o}
of
m_{\varphi}
can be described precisely in the following way

(m_{\varphi})_{o}=\left\{x=\{x_{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}\in m_{\varphi}\colon\lim\limits_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\varphi(n)}{n}\sum_{k=1}^{n}x_{k}^{\star}=0\right\}

(see
[
KPS82
, pp. 115–116]
). Similarly, if
\varphi(\infty)<\infty
, then
m_{\varphi}=\ell_{\infty}
and
(m_{\varphi})_{o}=c_{0}
.

\blacktriangle

After this short reminder, the main result of this section is as follows.

Theorem 6.4

(Compact multipliers between Lorentz and Marcinkiewicz sequence spaces)
.

Let
\varphi
and
\psi
be two positive, increasing and concave functions on
(0,\infty)
.
Further, let
X
and
Y
be two rearrangement invariant sequence spaces with the Fatou property.

(i)

Suppose that
\delta_{\varphi}<\infty
. Then the multiplier

M_{\lambda}\colon X\rightarrow\lambda_{\varphi}
is compact if, and only if,
either
\left\{\frac{\varphi(n)}{n}\lambda_{n}^{\star}\right\}_{n=1}^{\infty}\in\left(X^{\times}\right)_{o}
provided

\left\{\varphi(n)/n\right\}_{n=1}^{\infty}\notin X^{\times}
, or
\{\lambda_{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}\in c_{0}

provided
\left\{\varphi(n)/n\right\}_{n=1}^{\infty}\in\left(X^{\times}\right)_{o}
.

(ii)

Suppose that
\gamma_{\varphi}>1
. Then the multiplier

M_{\lambda}\colon m_{\varphi}\rightarrow Y
is compact if, and only if, either

\left\{\lambda_{n}^{\star}/\varphi(n)\right\}_{n=1}^{\infty}\in Y_{o}
, provided

\left\{1/\varphi(n)\right\}_{n=1}^{\infty}\notin Y
, or
\{\lambda_{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}\in c_{0}
,
provided
\left\{1/\varphi(n)\right\}_{n=1}^{\infty}\notin Y
.

In particular,

(iii)

if
\limsup_{n\rightarrow\infty}\psi(n)/\varphi(n)=\infty

and
\gamma_{\varphi}>1
, then the multiplier
M_{\lambda}\colon m_{\varphi}\rightarrow\lambda_{\psi}

is compact if, and only if,
\left\{\frac{\psi(n+1)-\psi(n)}{\varphi(n)}\lambda_{n}^{\star}\right\}_{n=1}^{\infty}\in\ell_{1}
,

(iv)

if
\limsup_{n\rightarrow\infty}\varphi(n)/\psi(n)=\infty
and
\delta_{\varphi}<\infty
,
then the multiplier
M_{\lambda}\colon\lambda_{\psi}\rightarrow\lambda_{\varphi}

is compact if, and only if,
\left\{\frac{\varphi(n)}{\psi(n)}\lambda_{n}^{\star}\right\}_{n=1}^{\infty}\in c_{0}
.

Before we give the proof of Theorem
6.4
,
we need some preparatory lemmas that seem interesting in themselves.

Lemma 6.5
.

Let
X
and
Y
be two rearrangement invariant sequence spaces with the Fatou property.
Then, up to the equivalence of norms, the space
M(X,Y)
is rearrangement invariant as well.

Proof.

To see this, take the function
x=\{x_{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
from
M(X,Y)
. For convenience, let us consider two cases separately.

First case.
Suppose that
x^{\star}(\infty)>0
.
This clearly implies that
\ell_{\infty}\hookrightarrow M(X,Y)
. Moreover, if
F\hookrightarrow G
, then it is straightforward to
see that
M(E,F)\hookrightarrow M(E,G)
. Note also that
M(E,\ell_{\infty})\hookrightarrow\ell_{\infty}
. Indeed, if an unbounded
function, say
z=\{z_{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
, belonged to
M(E,\ell_{\infty})
, then
z_{n}=ze_{n}\in\ell_{\infty}
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
,
which is impossible. Thus, combining all these three facts together and remembering that
Y\hookrightarrow\ell_{\infty}

(see
[
BS88
, Corollary 6.7, p. 78]
), we get

\ell_{\infty}\hookrightarrow M(X,Y)\hookrightarrow M(X,\ell_{\infty})\hookrightarrow\ell_{\infty}.

In consequence,
M(X,Y)=\ell_{\infty}
.

Second case.
Suppose that
x^{\star}(\infty)=0
. Here we will, more or less, follow the argument given in
[
KLM12
, Theorem 2.2]
.
Let
y=\{y_{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
be any function that is equi-measurable with
x
. Then, due to
[
KPS82
, Lemma 2.1, p. 60]
,
for any
\varepsilon>0
there exists a measure-preserving transformation
T_{\varepsilon}
such that

\left\lVert x-y\circ T_{\varepsilon}\right\rVert_{\ell_{1}}<\varepsilon.

Thus, since
\ell_{1}\overset{c}{\hookrightarrow}Y
with
c=\varphi_{Y}(1)
and
X\overset{C}{\hookrightarrow}\ell_{\infty}
with
C=1/\varphi_{X}(1)

(see
[
BS88
, pp. 77–78]
), so for all functions
z=\{z_{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
from
\text{Ball}_{X}
, we have

\displaystyle\left\lVert yz\right\rVert_{Y}

\displaystyle=\left\lVert(y\circ T_{\varepsilon})(z\circ T_{\varepsilon})\right\rVert_{Y}

\displaystyle\leqslant\left\lVert x(z\circ T_{\varepsilon})\right\rVert_{Y}+\left\lVert\left(y\circ T_{\varepsilon}-x\right)(z\circ T_{\varepsilon})\right\rVert_{Y}

\displaystyle\leqslant\left\lVert x\right\rVert_{M(X,Y)}+\left\lVert\left(y\circ T_{\varepsilon}-x\right)(z\circ T_{\varepsilon})\right\rVert_{F}

\displaystyle\leqslant\left\lVert x\right\rVert_{M(X,Y)}+\left\lVert z\circ T_{\varepsilon}\right\rVert_{\ell_{\infty}}\left\lVert y\circ T_{\varepsilon}-x\right\rVert_{Y}

\displaystyle\leqslant\left\lVert x\right\rVert_{M(X,Y)}+\frac{\varphi_{Y}(1)}{\varphi_{X}(1)}\left\lVert z\right\rVert_{X}\left\lVert y\circ T_{\varepsilon}-x\right\rVert_{\ell_{1}}

\displaystyle<\left\lVert x\right\rVert_{M(X,Y)}+\frac{\varphi_{Y}(1)}{\varphi_{X}(1)}\varepsilon.

Due to
\varepsilon
’s arbitrariness we conclude that

\left\lVert y\right\rVert_{M(X,Y)}\leqslant\left\lVert x\right\rVert_{M(X,Y)}.

To prove the reverse inequality, it is enough to swap the roles of
x
and
y
.
∎

Lemma 6.6
.

Let
X
and
Y
be two rearrangement invariant sequence spaces with the Fatou property.
Further, let
\varphi
be a positive, increasing and concave function on
(0,\infty)
. Then

(i)

M(m_{\varphi},Y)=\left[Y\left(1/\varphi\right)\right]^{\bigstar}
provided
\gamma_{\varphi}>1
,

(ii)

M(X,\lambda_{\varphi})=\left[X^{\times}\left(\varphi(t)/t\right)\right]^{\bigstar}
provided
\delta_{\varphi}<\infty
.

Proof.

(i)
. Without loss of generality, we can assume that
m_{\varphi}\neq\ell_{\infty}
or, which is the same thing,
that the function
\varphi
is unbounded. Otherwise, the sequence
\{\varphi(n)\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
is equivalent to a constant
sequence and we have the following identifications

M(m_{\varphi},Y)=M(\ell_{\infty},Y)=Y=Y\left(\frac{1}{\varphi}\right)=\left[Y\left(\frac{1}{\varphi}\right)\right]^{\bigstar}.

We will now consider two cases.

First case.
Suppose that
\left\{1/\varphi(n)\right\}_{n=1}^{\infty}\in Y
.
Then, it is clear, that
\ell_{\infty}\hookrightarrow Y\left(1/\varphi\right)
.
Moreover, for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
, we have

\frac{\varphi_{Y}(n)}{\varphi(n)}x_{n}^{\star}=\varphi_{Y}(n)\left[\frac{x_{n}^{\star}}{\varphi(n)}\right]^{\star}\leqslant\frac{\varphi_{Y}(n)}{n}\sum_{k=1}^{n}\frac{x_{k}^{\star}}{\varphi(k)},

where the first equality follows from a simple fact that the product of two decreasing functions is decreasing,
and the second inequality is due to
[
BS88
, Proposition 3.2, p. 52]
.
Thus, in view of the embedding
Y\hookrightarrow m_{\varphi_{Y}}
(see
[
BS88
, Proposition 5.1, p. 70]
), we have

\ell_{\infty}\equiv\ell_{\infty}^{\bigstar}\hookrightarrow\left[Y\left(\frac{1}{\varphi}\right)\right]^{\bigstar}\hookrightarrow\left[m_{\varphi_{Y}}\left(\frac{1}{\varphi}\right)\right]^{\bigstar}\hookrightarrow\left[\ell_{\infty}\left(\frac{\varphi_{Y}}{\varphi}\right)\right]^{\bigstar}.

Now, since
\sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\varphi_{Y}(n)/\varphi(n)<\infty
(otherwise, the sequence
\left\{1/\varphi(n)\right\}_{n=1}^{\infty}

does not belongs to
\left[\ell_{\infty}(\varphi_{Y})\right]^{\bigstar}
, which is impossible), so

\left[\ell_{\infty}\left(\frac{\varphi_{Y}}{\varphi}\right)\right]^{\bigstar}\hookrightarrow[\ell_{\infty}]^{\bigstar}\equiv\ell_{\infty}.

This means that

(6.1)

\left[Y\left(\frac{1}{\varphi}\right)\right]^{\bigstar}=\ell_{\infty}.

On the other hand, since
\left\{1/\varphi(n)\right\}_{n=1}^{\infty}\in Y
, so
\ell_{\infty}(\varphi)\hookrightarrow Y
.
Thus, remembering that
\gamma_{\varphi}>1
and using
[
KPS82
, Theorem 5.3, p. 115]
, we have

m_{\varphi}=\left[\ell_{\infty}(\varphi)\right]^{\bigstar}\hookrightarrow Y^{\bigstar}\equiv Y.

This imply that
\ell_{\infty}\hookrightarrow M(m_{\varphi},Y)
.
Moreover, due to Lemma
6.5
, the space
M(m_{\varphi},Y)
is rearrangement invariant,
so
M(m_{\varphi},Y)\hookrightarrow\ell_{\infty}
. In consequence,

(6.2)

M(m_{\varphi},Y)=\ell_{\infty}.

Putting (
6.1
) together with (
6.2
), we get

M(m_{\varphi},Y)=\left[Y\left(\frac{1}{\varphi}\right)\right]^{\bigstar}.

This is what we wanted.

Second case.
This time, let us assume that
\left\{1/\varphi(n)\right\}_{n=1}^{\infty}\notin Y
.
Again, our plan is to show that

(6.3)

M(m_{\varphi},Y)=\left[Y\left(\frac{1}{\varphi}\right)\right]^{\bigstar}.

To do this, we will need the following result whose proof we essentially borrow from
[
KLM14
, p. 653]
.

Claim
\blacktriangle
.

Let
F
be a rearrangement invariant sequence space.
Further, let
\varphi
be a positive, increasing and concave function on
(0,\infty)
with
\gamma_{\varphi}>1
.
Then, we have

\left\lVert x\right\rVert_{M(m_{\varphi},F)}=\sup\limits_{T}\left\lVert\frac{x}{\varphi\circ T}\right\rVert_{F},

where the supremum is taken over all measure-preserving transformations
T
.

Proof of Claim
\blacktriangle

Take
x\in M(m_{\varphi},F)
and let
T
be a measure preserving transformation.
Since,
\left(1/(\varphi\circ T)\right)^{\star}=1/\varphi\in\ell_{\infty}(\varphi)

and
m_{\varphi}=[\ell_{\infty}(\varphi)]^{\bigstar}
,
so
1/(\varphi\circ T)\in m_{\varphi}
.
In consequence,
x/(\varphi\circ T)\in F
and

\left\lVert\frac{x}{\varphi\circ T}\right\rVert_{F}\leqslant\left\lVert x\right\rVert_{M(m_{\varphi},F)}.

Taking the supremum over all measure-preserving transformations
T
, we have

\sup\limits_{T}\left\lVert\frac{x}{\varphi\circ T}\right\rVert_{F}\leqslant\left\lVert x\right\rVert_{M(m_{\varphi},F)}.

To prove the reverse inequality, suppose that
x/(\varphi\circ T)\in Y
for any measure-preserving transformation
T
.
Take
z\in\text{Ball}_{m_{\varphi}}
. As we already explained above, we can assume that
m_{\varphi}\neq\ell_{\infty}
.
Thus,
m_{\varphi}\hookrightarrow c_{0}
and, consequently,
z^{*}(\infty)=0
.
Moreover, Ryff’s theorem guarantee the existence of a measure-preserving transformation, say
T_{0}
,
such that
\left\lvert z\right\rvert=z^{\star}\circ T_{0}
(see
[
BS88
, Theorem 7.5, p. 82]
).
Putting these facts together, we get

\left\lvert xz\right\rvert=\left\lvert x\right\rvert(z^{\star}\circ T_{0})\leqslant\frac{\left\lvert x\right\rvert}{\varphi\circ T_{0}}\in F.

This means that
x\in M(m_{\varphi},Y)
and, moreover,

\left\lVert x\right\rVert_{M(m_{\varphi},F)}\leqslant\sup\limits_{T}\left\lVert\frac{x}{\varphi\circ T}\right\rVert_{F}.

The proof is finished.

\blacksquare

Now we are ready to show (
6.3
).

Embedding
M(m_{\varphi},Y)\hookrightarrow\left[Y\left(1/\varphi\right)\right]^{\bigstar}
.

Take
x\in M(m_{\varphi},Y)
. In view of Claim
\blacktriangle
, it is enough to show that

\left\lVert\frac{x^{\star}}{\varphi}\right\rVert_{Y}=\left\lVert\frac{x^{\star}\circ T}{\varphi\circ T}\right\rVert_{Y}\leqslant\sup\limits_{T}\left\lVert\frac{x}{\varphi\circ T}\right\rVert_{Y}.

However, once we know that there is a measure-preserving transformation, say
T_{0}
, such that
\left\lvert x\right\rvert=x^{\star}\circ T_{0}
,
the above inequality is basically obvious. So our goal for now is to show that
x^{\star}(\infty)=0
. Then, a simple application
of Ryff’s theorem will complete the argument. To do this, recall that we assumed that
\left\{1/\varphi(n)\right\}_{n=1}^{\infty}\notin Y
.
Thus

\ell_{\infty}(\varphi)\not\hookrightarrow Y

and, consequently,

(6.4)

m_{\varphi}=\left[\ell_{\infty}(\varphi)\right]^{\bigstar}\not\hookrightarrow Y^{\bigstar}=Y.

We claim that

(6.5)

\ell_{\infty}\not\hookrightarrow M(m_{\varphi},Y).

Indeed, otherwise, due to the fact that the space
M(m_{\varphi},Y)
is rearrangement invariant (see Lemma
6.5
),
we would have that
M(m_{\varphi},Y)=\ell_{\infty}
. This in turn, however, would mean that
m_{\varphi}\hookrightarrow Y
.
This clearly contradicts (
6.4
), so (
6.5
) follows.
Thus, using once again the fact that the space
M(m_{\varphi},Y)
is rearrangement invariant, we have

M(m_{\varphi},Y)\hookrightarrow c_{0}.

This is exactly what we needed.

Embedding
\left[Y\left(1/\varphi\right)\right]^{\bigstar}\hookrightarrow M(m_{\varphi},Y)
.

Take
x\in\left[Y\left(1/\varphi\right)\right]^{\bigstar}
and let
T
be a measure-preserving transformation.
Applying
[
KPS82
, Property
10^{\circ}
, p. 67]
and remembering that the function
t\rightsquigarrow 1/\varphi(t)

is decreasing, we have

\displaystyle\left[\frac{x}{\varphi\circ T}\right]^{\star}

\displaystyle\leqslant D_{2}x^{\star}\,D_{2}\left[\frac{1}{\varphi\circ T}\right]^{\star}

\displaystyle=D_{2}x^{\star}\,D_{2}\left[\frac{1}{\varphi}\right]

\displaystyle=D_{2}\left[\frac{x^{*}}{\varphi}\right].

Moreover, since the space
Y
is rearrangement invariant, so

\displaystyle\left\lVert\frac{x}{\varphi\circ T}\right\rVert_{Y}

\displaystyle=\left\lVert\frac{x}{\varphi\circ T}\right\rVert_{Y^{\bigstar}}

\displaystyle\leqslant\left\lVert D_{2}\right\rVert_{Y\rightarrow Y}\left\lVert\frac{x^{\star}}{\varphi}\right\rVert_{Y}

\displaystyle\leqslant 2\left\lVert x\right\rVert_{\left[Y\left(1/\varphi\right)\right]^{\bigstar}},

where the last inequality is essentially due to the Calderón–Mityagin interpolation theorem (see
[
LT79
, p. 130]
; cf.
[
KPS82
, pp. 96–98]
).
Taking the supremum over all measure-preserving mappings ends the proof of (
6.3
).

(ii)
This follows by duality. Indeed, remembering about our assumptions, we have

\displaystyle M\left(X,\lambda_{\varphi}\right)

\displaystyle=M\left((\lambda_{\varphi})^{\times},X^{\times}\right)\quad\text{(using \cite[cite]{[\@@bibref{}{KLM12}{}{}, Property\penalty\ (vii), p.\penalty\ 879]})}

\displaystyle=M\left(m_{t/\varphi(t)},X^{\times}\right)\quad\text{(using \cite[cite]{[\@@bibref{}{KPS82}{}{}, Theorem\penalty\ 5.2, p.\penalty\ 112]})}

\displaystyle=\left[X^{\times}\left(\frac{\varphi(t)}{t}\right)\right]^{\bigstar}\quad\text{(by (i) proved above)}.

∎

Next, we need a lemma in the spirit of Kolwicz’s results from
[
Kol16
]
(see, for example, Theorem 3.9 ibidem).

Lemma 6.7
.

Let
X
be a rearrangement invariant sequence space with the Fatou property.
Further, let
w
be a positive and decreasing function on
(0,\infty)
such that
w\notin X
.
Suppose that up to an equivalent norm the space
[X(w)]^{\bigstar}
is a Banach space. Then

[X(w)^{\bigstar}]_{o}=\left[X_{o}(w)\right]^{\bigstar}.

Proof.

In order not to overload the notation, we will omit some parentheses here and there. This should not lead to misunderstandings.
Moreover, it is more or less obvious that
X(w)_{o}=X_{o}(w)
, so we only need to show that

(6.6)

[X(w)_{o}]^{\bigstar}=[X(w)^{\bigstar}]_{o}.

We will justify both inclusions separately.

First inclusion
. Fix
\varepsilon>0
. To show that

(6.7)

[X(w)_{o}]^{\bigstar}\subset[X(w)^{\bigstar}]_{o},

take the sequence
x=\{x_{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
from
[X(w)_{o}]^{\bigstar}
.
This means that the sequence
x^{\star}=\{x^{\star}_{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
belongs to
X(w)_{o}
.
Since the space
X(w)^{\bigstar}
is rearrangement invariant, so in order to prove the inclusion (
6.7
),
it is enough to show that
x^{\star}\in[X(w)^{\bigstar}]_{o}
(see
[
KKT22
, Theorem 3.6]
).
Clearly,

\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\left\lVert\sum_{i=n}^{\infty}x^{\star}_{i}e_{i}\right\rVert_{X(w)}=0,

so there is
N\in\mathbb{N}
with

\left\lVert\sum_{i=n}^{\infty}x^{\star}_{i}e_{i}\right\rVert_{X(w)}<\varepsilon\quad\text{ for }\quad n\geqslant N.

Next, let us note that
x^{\star}(\infty)=0
. In fact, if this were not the case, then
\ell_{\infty}\hookrightarrow X(w)
.
However, since the function
w
is decreasing, this would mean that
w\in X
, which is impossible.
Therefore, we can find
M\in\mathbb{N}
such that

x^{\star}_{m}\leqslant\frac{\varepsilon}{\left\lVert\sum_{i=1}^{N}e_{i}\right\rVert_{X(w)}}\quad\text{ for }\quad m\geqslant M.

In consequence, for
n\geqslant\max\{M,N\}
, we have

\displaystyle\left\lVert\sum_{i=n}^{\infty}x^{\star}_{i}e_{i}\right\rVert_{X(w)^{\bigstar}}

\displaystyle\leqslant\left\lVert\left(\sum_{i=M}^{\infty}x^{\star}_{i}e_{i}\right)^{\star}\right\rVert_{X(w)}

\displaystyle\leqslant\left\lVert\sum_{i=1}^{N}x^{\star}_{M}e_{i}\right\rVert_{X(w)}+\left\lVert\sum_{i=N+1}^{\infty}x^{\star}_{i}e_{i}\right\rVert_{X(w)}<2\varepsilon.

Thus
x^{\star}\in[X(w)^{\bigstar}]_{o}
and this completes the proof of (
6.7
).

Second inclusion
. Take
x=\{x_{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
from
[X(w)^{\bigstar}]_{o}
. We will show that

(6.8)

[X(w)^{\bigstar}]_{o}\subset[X(w)_{o}]^{\bigstar}.

Once again, since the space
X(w)^{\bigstar}
is rearrangement invariant, so in view of
[
KKT22
, Theorem 3.6]

the fact that
x\in[X(w)^{\bigstar}]_{o}
is equivalent to
x^{*}\in[X(w)^{\bigstar}]_{o}
.
Thus, in order to prove (
6.8
), we need to show that
x^{*}\in X(w)_{o}
, that is,
that for any sequence
\left\{x^{(n)}\right\}_{n=1}^{\infty}\subset X(w)
with
0\leqslant x^{(n)}\leqslant x^{\star}

and
x^{(n)}\downarrow 0
we have
\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\left\lVert x^{(n)}\right\rVert_{X(w)}=0
. Take such a sequence
\left\{x^{(n)}\right\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
.
Since
[X(w)^{\bigstar}]_{o}\subset[\ell_{\infty}]_{o}=c_{0}
, so
x^{\star}(\infty)=0
.
Thus
\left(x^{(n)}\right)^{\star}\downarrow 0
and, trivially,
0\leqslant\left(x^{(n)}\right)^{\star}\leqslant x^{\star}
.
This means that
\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\left\lVert\left(x^{(n)}\right)^{\star}\right\rVert_{X(w)}=0
.
In consequence, we get

\displaystyle\left\lVert x^{(n)}\right\rVert_{X(w)}

\displaystyle=\left\lVert(x^{(n)}w)^{\star}\right\rVert_{X}\quad\text{(because $X$ is rearrangement invariant)}

\displaystyle\leqslant\left\lVert D_{2}(x^{(n)})^{\star}\,D_{2}w^{\star}\right\rVert_{X}\quad\text{(using \cite[cite]{[\@@bibref{}{KPS82}{}{}, Property\penalty\ $10^{\circ}$, p.\penalty\ 67]})}

\displaystyle=\left\lVert D_{2}\left[(x^{(n)})^{\star}w^{\star}\right]\right\rVert_{X}

\displaystyle\leqslant\left\lVert D_{2}\right\rVert_{X\rightarrow X}\left\lVert(x^{(n)})^{\star}w^{\star}\right\rVert_{X}\quad\text{(because $D_{2}$ is bounded on $X$)}

\displaystyle=\left\lVert D_{2}\right\rVert_{X\rightarrow X}\left\lVert(x^{(n)})^{\star}w\right\rVert_{X}\quad\text{(since the function $w$ is decreasing)}

\displaystyle=\left\lVert D_{2}\right\rVert_{X\rightarrow X}\left\lVert x^{(n)}\right\rVert_{[X(w)]^{\bigstar}}\to 0

as
n
goes to infinity. The proof is finished.
∎

Digression 6.8

(About Lemma
6.7
)
.

What seems quite surprising is that our proof is rather elementary, in the sense, that it use neither the concept of the so-called
OC^{\bigstar}
-point
or the machinery developed in
[
Kol16
]
. Actually, the first-named author learned from Kolwicz another, slightly more general,
proof which directly uses some ideas from
[
Kol16
]
. However, let us put it in the drawer for now.

\blacktriangle

Remark 6.9
.

As we mentioned earlier, the symmetrization of a Köthe space is in general only a quasi-Banach space (see
[
KR09
, Lemma 1.4]
; cf.
[
KLM19
, Corollary 1]
).
However, there is a rather simple criterion to determine whether the space
[X(1/\phi)]^{\bigstar}
, where
X
is a rearrangement invariant sequence space
and
\phi
is a non-decreasing and concave function on
(0,\infty)
, is a Banach space up to an equivalent norm. Indeed, suppose that

\frac{1}{\beta_{X}}<1-\frac{1}{\gamma_{\phi}}.

Then the discrete Hardy operator
\mathscr{H}
is bounded on the space
X(1/\phi)
(see
[
Mal83
, Theorem 1]
;
see also a note on Pavlov’s result on p. 127 in
[
KMP07
]
).
In consequence, the functional
{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\bullet\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}\colon[X(1/\phi)]^{\bigstar}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
defined as

x\rightsquigarrow{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|x\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}\coloneqq\left\lVert\frac{x^{\star\star}}{\phi}\right\rVert_{X},

where
x^{\star\star}
denote the
maximal function
of
x^{\star}
, that is,

x^{\star\star}\coloneqq\left\{\frac{1}{n}\sum_{k=1}^{n}x^{\star}_{k}\right\}_{n=1}^{\infty}

(see
[
BS88
, Definition 3.1, p. 52]
), defines on the space
[X(1/\phi)]^{\bigstar}
an equivalent norm.
In fact, in view of
[
BS88
, p. 54]
the triangle inequality is clear.
Moreover, for any
x\in[X(1/\phi)]^{\bigstar}
we have the following inequalities

\left\lVert\frac{x^{\star}}{\phi}\right\rVert_{X}\leqslant\left\lVert\frac{x^{\star\star}}{\phi}\right\rVert_{X}={\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|x\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}\leqslant\left\lVert\mathscr{H}\right\rVert_{[X(1/\phi)]^{\bigstar}\rightarrow[X(1/\phi)]^{\bigstar}}\left\lVert\frac{x^{\star}}{\phi}\right\rVert_{X},

which means that

\left\lVert x\right\rVert_{[X(1/\phi)]^{\bigstar}}\approx{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|x\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}.

\blacktriangle

After all this preparations, we are finally in the position to provide the promised proof of Theorem
6.4
.

Proof of Theorem
6.4
.

(i)
Under the assumption that
\left\{\varphi(n)/n\right\}_{n=1}^{\infty}\notin X^{\times}
,
we have the following equivalences

\displaystyle M_{\lambda}\colon X\rightarrow\lambda_{\varphi}\quad\text{ is compact }\quad

\displaystyle\Longleftrightarrow\quad\lambda\in M(X,\lambda_{\varphi})_{o}\quad\text{(using Theorem\penalty\ \ref{thm: ess norm ri})}

\displaystyle\Longleftrightarrow\quad\lambda\in\left[\left[X^{\times}\left(\frac{\varphi(t)}{t}\right)\right]^{\bigstar}\right]_{o}\quad\text{(by Lemma\penalty\ \ref{LEMMA: a'la KLM12/14}(ii))}

\displaystyle\Longleftrightarrow\quad\lambda\in\left[\left(X^{\times}\right)_{o}\left(\frac{\varphi(t)}{t}\right)\right]^{\bigstar}\quad\text{(by Lemma\penalty\ \ref{LEMMA: a'la Kolwicz OC})}

\displaystyle\Longleftrightarrow\quad\left\{\frac{\varphi(n)}{n}\lambda_{n}^{\star}\right\}_{n=1}^{\infty}\in\left(X^{\times}\right)_{o}.

Now, suppose that
\left\{\varphi(n)/n\right\}_{n=1}^{\infty}\in X^{\times}
.
Then the first part of the proof of Lemma
6.6
together with duality results from

[
KPS82
, Theorem 5.2, p. 112]
teaches us that

M(X,\lambda_{\varphi})=M\left((\lambda_{\varphi})^{\times},X^{\times}\right)=M(m_{t/\varphi(t)},X^{\times})=\ell_{\infty}.

Therefore, applying Theorem
4.7
one more time, we see immediately that

M_{\lambda}\colon X\rightarrow\lambda_{\varphi}\quad\text{ is compact if, and only if, }\quad\lambda\in(\ell_{\infty})_{o}=c_{0}.

(ii)
Suppose that
\left\{1/\varphi(n)\right\}_{n=1}^{\infty}\notin Y
. We have

\displaystyle M_{\lambda}\colon m_{\varphi}\rightarrow Y\quad\text{ is compact }\quad

\displaystyle\Longleftrightarrow\quad\lambda\in M(m_{\varphi},Y)_{o}\quad\text{(using Theorem\penalty\ \ref{thm: ess norm ri})}

\displaystyle\Longleftrightarrow\quad\lambda\in\left[\left[Y\left(\frac{1}{\varphi}\right)\right]^{\bigstar}\right]_{o}\quad\text{(by Lemma\penalty\ \ref{LEMMA: a'la KLM12/14}(i))}

\displaystyle\Longleftrightarrow\quad\lambda\in\left[Y_{o}\left(\frac{1}{\varphi}\right)\right]^{\bigstar}\quad\text{(by Lemma\penalty\ \ref{LEMMA: a'la Kolwicz OC})}

\displaystyle\Longleftrightarrow\quad\left\{\frac{1}{\varphi(n)}\lambda_{n}^{\star}\right\}_{n=1}^{\infty}\in Y_{o}.

Now, suppose that
\left\{1/\varphi(n)\right\}_{n=1}^{\infty}\in Y
. Then, due to the first part of the proof of Lemma
6.6
,

M(m_{\varphi},Y)=\ell_{\infty}.

Thus, we have

M_{\lambda}\colon m_{\varphi}\rightarrow Y\quad\text{ is compact if, and only if, }\quad\lambda\in(\ell_{\infty})_{o}=c_{0}.

(iii)
Suppose that
\limsup_{n\rightarrow\infty}\psi(n)/\varphi(n)=\infty
. Then, in view of the embedding
\lambda_{\psi}\hookrightarrow m_{\psi}

(see
[
BS88
, Proposition 5.9, p. 70]
), it is straightforward to see that if
\limsup_{n\rightarrow\infty}\psi(n)/\varphi(n)=\infty
,
then
\left\{1/\varphi(n)\right\}_{n=1}^{\infty}\notin m_{\psi}
. In consequence,
\left\{1/\varphi(n)\right\}_{n=1}^{\infty}\notin\lambda_{\psi}
.
We have

\displaystyle M(m_{\varphi},\lambda_{\psi})

\displaystyle=\left[\lambda_{\psi}\left(\frac{1}{\varphi}\right)\right]^{\bigstar}\quad\text{(by Lemma\penalty\ \ref{LEMMA: a'la KLM12/14}(i))}

\displaystyle=\left[\left[\ell_{1}(\Delta\psi)\right]^{\bigstar}\left(\frac{1}{\varphi}\right)\right]^{\bigstar}\quad\text{(because $\lambda_{\phi}\equiv[\ell_{1}(\Delta\phi)]^{\bigstar}$, $\Delta\phi(n)\coloneqq\phi(n+1)-\phi(n)$)}

\displaystyle=\left[\ell_{1}(\Delta\psi)\left(\frac{1}{\varphi}\right)\right]^{\bigstar}\quad\text{(in view of the equality $\left(\frac{x^{\star}}{\varphi}\right)^{\star}=\frac{x^{\star}}{\varphi}$)}

\displaystyle=\left[\ell_{1}\left(\frac{\Delta\psi}{\varphi}\right)\right]^{\bigstar},

where
\Delta\psi(n)\coloneqq\psi(n+1)-\psi(n)
for
n\in\mathbb{N}
. Since the sequence
\left\{\frac{\psi(n+1)-\psi(n)}{\varphi(n)}\right\}_{n=1}^{\infty}

is decreasing and
\ell_{1}
is separable, so applying Lemma
6.7
, we get

\left[\left[\ell_{1}\left(\frac{\Delta\psi}{\varphi}\right)\right]^{\bigstar}\right]_{o}=\left[(\ell_{1})_{o}\left(\frac{\Delta\psi}{\varphi}\right)\right]^{\bigstar}=\left[\ell_{1}\left(\frac{\Delta\psi}{\varphi}\right)\right]^{\bigstar}.

Let us make it clear that the use of Lemma
6.7
was completely justified in this situation, because

\left\{\Delta\psi(n)/\varphi(n)\right\}_{n=1}^{\infty}\notin\ell_{1}
. In fact, if this were not the case, then

\left\{1/\varphi(n)\right\}_{n=1}^{\infty}\in\lambda_{\psi}
which, due to our assumptions, is impossible.
All this means is that the sequence
\lambda=\{\lambda_{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
belongs to
M(m_{\varphi},\lambda_{\psi})_{o}
if,
and only if, the sequence
\left\{\frac{\psi(n+1)-\psi(n)}{\varphi(n)}\lambda_{n}^{*}\right\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
belongs to
\ell_{1}
.
Since the space
\lambda_{\psi}
is order continuous (see Remark
6.3
),
so the rest follows from Theorem
4.3
.

(iv)
Suppose that
\limsup_{n\rightarrow\infty}\varphi(n)/\psi(n)=\infty
. Using the known equality between the fundamental
functions of the space
X
and the fundamental function of its Köthe dual
X^{\times}
(see
[
BS88
, Theorem 5.2, p. 66]
), that is,

\varphi_{X}(n)\varphi_{X^{\times}}(n)=n\quad\text{ for }\quad n\in\mathbb{N},

and arguing as at the beginning of the proof of (iii), it is clear that if
\limsup_{n\rightarrow\infty}\varphi(n)/\psi(n)=\infty
,
then
\left\{\varphi(n)/n\right\}_{n=1}^{\infty}\notin(\lambda_{\psi})^{\times}=m_{t/\psi(t)}
.
In consequence, we have

\displaystyle M(\lambda_{\psi},\lambda_{\varphi})

\displaystyle=\left[\left(\lambda_{\psi}\right)^{\times}\left(\frac{\varphi(t)}{t}\right)\right]^{\bigstar}\quad\text{(by Lemma\penalty\ \ref{LEMMA: a'la KLM12/14}(ii))}

\displaystyle=\left[m_{t/\psi(t)}\left(\frac{\varphi(t)}{t}\right)\right]^{\bigstar}\quad\text{(using \cite[cite]{[\@@bibref{}{KPS82}{}{}, Theorem\penalty\ 5.2, p.\penalty\ 112]})}

\displaystyle=\left[\left[\ell_{\infty}\left(\frac{t}{\psi(t)}\right)\right]^{\bigstar}\left(\frac{\varphi(t)}{t}\right)\right]^{\bigstar}\quad\text{(because $m_{\phi}=[\ell_{\infty}(\phi)]^{\bigstar}$ provided $\gamma_{\phi}>1$)}.

Next, keeping in mind that the function
t\rightsquigarrow\varphi(t)/t
is decreasing and using the obvious fact that the product of
two decreasing functions is still decreasing, we get

\left[\left[\ell_{\infty}\left(\frac{t}{\psi(t)}\right)\right]^{\bigstar}\left(\frac{\varphi(t)}{t}\right)\right]^{\bigstar}=\left[\ell_{\infty}\left(\frac{t}{\psi(t)}\right)\left(\frac{\varphi(t)}{t}\right)\right]^{\bigstar}=\left[\ell_{\infty}\left(\frac{\varphi}{\psi}\right)\right]^{\bigstar}.

Now, since
\left\{\varphi(n)/\psi(n)\right\}_{n=1}^{\infty}\notin\ell_{\infty}

(otherwise,
\left\{\varphi(n)/n\right\}_{n=1}^{\infty}\in m_{t/\psi(t)}
and this is contrary to our assumptions),
so we can use Lemma
6.7
and, consequently, we get

\left[\left[\ell_{\infty}\left(\frac{\varphi}{\psi}\right)\right]^{\bigstar}\right]_{o}=\left[(\ell_{\infty})_{o}\left(\frac{\varphi}{\psi}\right)\right]^{\bigstar}=\left[c_{0}\left(\frac{\varphi}{\psi}\right)\right]^{\bigstar}.

Hence it is clear that the sequence
\lambda=\{\lambda_{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
belongs to the space
M(\lambda_{\psi},\lambda_{\varphi})_{o}
if, and only if,
the sequence
\left\{\frac{\varphi(n)}{\psi(n)}\lambda_{n}^{\star}\right\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
belongs to
c_{0}
. The use of Theorem
4.3

together with the information that the space
\lambda_{\varphi}
is order continuous (again, see Remark
6.3
) completes the proof.

\blacksquare

7.
Open ends

Let us list and discuss some problems which arise from this paper.

7.1.
Things that should not be

We believe that the assumptions about separability and reflexivity present in Theorem
4.1

and Theorem
4.3
are unnecessary or, at least, can be significantly weakened. This belief
is clearly supported by Theorem
4.7
(which, however, relies on some heavy machinery) and Schep’s results from
[
Sch22

One counter-example to the factorization problem

Let
(\Omega,\Sigma,\mu)
be a
\sigma
-finite measure space.
Celebrated Lozanovskiĭ’s result says that each function

from
L_{1}(\Omega)
can be written as a pointwise product of two functions,
say
g
and
h
, where the first one belongs to a Köthe space
X
and the second one comes from its Köthe dual
X^{\times}

in such a way that

\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{L_{1}(\Omega)}\approx\left\lVert g\right\rVert_{X}\left\lVert h\right\rVert_{X^{\times}}

(see
[
Loz69
, Theorem 6]
; cf.
[
Mal89
, p. 185]
). Using the language of the arithmetic of Köthe spaces we can
express this result in compact, but equivalent, form as

X\odot X^{\times}=X\odot M(X,L_{1}(\Omega))=L_{1}(\Omega).

From here, however, the short road leads to the much more general question if for a given pair of Köthe spaces, say
X
and
Y
,
the following equality holds

X\odot M(X,Y)=Y?

We will refer to this kind of question as the
factorization problem
.

Remark A.1
.

It is not particularly difficult to find a pair of Köthe sequence spaces for which the factorization problem has a negative solution.
For example, let
X
and
Y
be two Köthe spaces such that
X\neq Y
, but
M(X,Y)=L_{\infty}(\Omega)
. Then

X\odot M(X,Y)=X\odot L_{\infty}(\Omega)=X\neq Y,

that is,
X
does not factorize through
Y
. As a more concrete illustration, take
1<p<\infty
and notice that

\ell_{p}\odot M(\ell_{p},\ell_{p,\infty})=\ell_{p}\odot\ell_{\infty}=\ell_{p}\subsetneqq\ell_{p,\infty}.

This clearly means that
\ell_{p}
does not factorize through
\ell_{p,\infty}
. On the other hand, using duality argument, we can also show that

\displaystyle\ell_{p,1}\odot M(\ell_{p,1},\ell_{p})

\displaystyle=\ell_{p,1}\odot M((\ell_{p})^{\times},(\ell_{p,1})^{\times})

\displaystyle=\ell_{p,1}\odot M(\ell_{q},\ell_{q,\infty})

\displaystyle=\ell_{p,1}\odot\ell_{\infty}

\displaystyle=\ell_{p,1}\subsetneqq\ell_{p},

where
1/p+1/q=1
. Consequently, also
\ell_{p,1}
does not factorize through
\ell_{p}
.

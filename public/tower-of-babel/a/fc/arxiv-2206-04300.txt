Cone-Restricted Information Theory
Ian George, Eric Chitambar

Abstract
The max-relative entropy and the conditional min-entropy it induces have become central to one-shot and zero-error quantum information theory. Both may be expressed in terms of a conic program over the positive semidefinite cone. Recently, it was shown that the same conic program over the separable cone admits an operational interpretation in terms of communicating classical information over a quantum channel. In this work, we generalize this framework to determine which results in quantum information theory rely upon the positive semidefinite cone and which can be generalized. We show that the standard asymptotic equipartition properties break down if the cone never approximates the positive semidefinite cone. This shows that such altered measures characterize different tasks than the traditional quantities even asymptotically. We present parallel results for the extended conditional min-entropy, which requires extending the notion of k-superpositive channels to superchannels. We also show that the near-equivalence of the smoothed max and Hartley entropies breaks down in this setting. However, we show for classical-quantum states, the separable cone is sufficient to re-cover the asymptotic theory, thereby drawing a strong distinction between the fully and partial quantum settings. We also present operational uses of this framework. We first show the cone restricted min-entropy of a Choi operator captures a measure of entanglement-assisted noiseless classical communication using restricted measurements. We show that quantum majorization results naturally generalize to other cones. As a novel example, we introduce a new min-entropy-like quantity that captures the quantum majorization of quantum channels in terms of bistochastic pre-processing. Lastly, we relate this framework to general conic norms and their non-additivity. Throughout this work we emphasize the introduced measures’ relationship to general convex resource theories. In particular, we look at both resource theories that capture locality and resource theories of coherence/Abelian symmetries.

Introduction

Quantum information theory is perhaps best set apart from its classical subtheory in the variety of resources it supplies for information processing tasks
[
1
]
, such as entanglement and coherence. A number of new information measures also emerge in the quantum setting due to both the non-commutative aspect of quantum mechanics as well as the plethora of ways that quantum resources can be transformed in the one-shot setting
[
2
,
3
]
.
As such, the quantum information theorist is always interested in a better understanding of the interplay between the use of a resource for an information processing task, the ability to convert between resources, the measures that capture resourcefulness, and the operational interpretations of said measures.

At an even more abstract level, the goal of the information theorist is to characterize operational tasks in terms of (entropic) measures. What characterizations can be established depends on the theory, e.g. the existence of a single-letter characterization of classical capacity when restricting to classical channels is not, at least currently, known to extend for quantum information theory because it includes quantum channels and entangled states
[
4
,
5
]
. It is therefore interesting to find manners of determining where characterizations may break down or change as the physical resources considered in information theory are restricted. A refined delineation between quantum and classical information theory, which is one way to interpret many of the results of this work, may be viewed as one motivation for the altered measures we consider.

The interplay between resources, operational tasks, and entropic measures may be seen by considering
quantum majorization
. Broadly speaking, quantum majorization describes when zero-error convertibility of one quantum object to another is feasible using a given family of allowed transformations. When the two objects belong to some relevant set, this feasibility in conversion induces a partial order on the set. Quantum majorization is analogous to the well-known notion of vector majorization, which is said to hold when one real vector can be converted to another by some stochastic channel. More generally, one can consider a majorization relation between classical channels defined by the convertibility of one to another under post-processing conversion, as formulated by Blackwell
[
6
,
7
]
.
A quantum notion of ”Blackwellian” majorization for quantum channels was obtained in
[
8
]
. Similarly, a notion of quantum majorization on bipartite states via local processing,
\sigma_{AB}\to\sigma_{AC}
using
\Phi_{B\to C}
, was obtained in
[
9
]
, and a notion of converting bipartite channels was obtained in
[
10
]
and subsequently improved in
[
11
,
12
]
. Beyond the obvious operational interest of these quantum versions, it is worth remarking that in each case it is shown that one element of the set majorizes the other if and only if some family of relations involving the conditional min-entropy holds.

The relation of majorization to the min-entropy is interesting for multiple reasons. First, the min-entropy has operational interpretations in terms of singlet fraction and guessing probability
[
13
]
. As such, if a form of quantum majorization relates to conditions on the min-entropy, it also relates to conditions on the entanglement or distinguishability. Second, while the min-entropy is a one-shot quantity, when smoothed its regularization is known to converge to the conditional von Neumann entropy for identically and independently distributed (i.i.d.) inputs as the number of copies tends to infinity. This is known as the (fully quantum) asymptotic equipartition property (AEP) and is crucial for recovering asymptotic behaviour from one-shot behaviour. However, the smooth min-entropy AEP may be shown to be in a sense inherited from the smoothed max-relative divergence
[
2
]
. At the same time, the max-relative divergence itself is of interest not only because it induces the min-entropy, but because the behaviour of its regularization in the limit for i.i.d. inputs, analogous to the smooth min-entropy AEP, quantifies the error exponent in quantum Stein’s lemma to first-order.

As such, entropic measures, resource conversion, and information-theoretic tasks are strongly related at a formal level. These relations have only grown more complicated recently as a variation on the min-entropy was found to admit an operational interpretation in terms of classical communication over a quantum channel
[
16
]
. This variation is obtained by taking the optimization program corresponding to the min-entropy and restricting it’s feasible region to the set of separable operators. For this reason, it is called the
separable min-entropy
. It follows that the separable min-entropy is always an upper bound on the standard min-entropy. Furthermore, it was shown that, unlike the min-entropy, the separable min-entropy is a non-additive measure in general. These properties call into question the behaviour of the regularized separable min-entropy for i.i.d. states in the limit of many copies in
both
the smoothed and unsmoothed cases. As the separable min-entropy already admits an operational interpretation, it is not obvious that other cones contained in the positive cone could not induce min-entropies with operational interpretations as well.
Moreover, arguably, it is not clear the positive cone and standard min-entropy should be uniquely central in information theory given the existence of an operational interpretation of the separable min-entropy.

It is this train of thought which this work aims to resolve. First, we introduce the notion of the max-relative entropy defined over closed convex cones which we call
restricted max-relative entropies
. We use these to define the
restricted min-entropies
. We then use this framework to show if we consider sequences of cones that do not asymptotically become equivalent to the positive semidefinite cone, then the smoothed regularized quantities do not converge to the relative and conditional entropy respectively. These results show tasks characterized by the restricted measures do not even asymptotically become equivalent to the unrestricted case. In particular, this means these measures cannot characterize traditional fully quantum results even to first order. We refer to such results as ‘anti-standard AEP’ results as, while they don’t tell us what the regularized quantity converges to, they tell us it doesn’t converge to the traditional measure. These anti-standard AEPs strongly rely on the continuously growing viewpoint of thinking of min-entropy-like quantities in terms of support functions
[
10
,
17
]
. Furthermore, as we will see, this approach is not only information-theoretically interesting, but deeply related to resource theories. This is largely because many resource theories can be formulated in terms of convex sets
[
18
]
, although we note our results also apply to any resource theory whose underlying resource has an
extensive
nature to it
[
19
]
, as defined more precisely in Section
4
.

Moreover, by defining a restricted max-relative entropy in terms of the restricted min-entropy, we are able to show that in the previously stated regime, the restricted max-entropy’s smoothed regularization won’t converge to the conditional entropy even for classical states. However, we show we can construct a restricted Hartley entropy that does work properly on classical states. This exemplifies a strong gap between the restricted Hartley and restricted max-entropy that is known to not exist for the positive semidefinite cone
[
20
]
. All of these points taken together further cement the positive semidefinite cone as central to fully quantum information theory much in the same way that complete positivity being captured by the positive semidefinite cone under the Choi-Jamiolkowski isomorphism does.

Furthermore, by first considering symmetries, we show that if we only consider classical-quantum (CQ) states, then the classical-quantum cone and its ordering is sufficient for establishing known equivalences of entropic and operational definitions at least for point-to-point quantum information theory. Moreover, the separable cone is the minimal cone under the demand that the cone is invariant under local unitaries, and so in some sense captures its own privileged position. Specifically, the Löwner order is only necessary for establishing certain equivalences of entropic and operational definitions for
fully quantum
information theory.

To exemplify the generality of this method, we extend the anti-standard AEP to the recently introduced extended min-entropy
H^{\mathrm{ext}}_{\min}(B|A)_{\Phi}
for a bipartite channel
\Phi:A_{0}B_{0}\to A_{1}B_{1}

[
10
]
, which has also been recently extended to the general cone case
[
12
]
. In doing so, we contribute to the entanglement theory for supermaps by expanding on
[
21
]
and introducing the ‘flipped’ extended min-entropy
H^{\mathrm{ext}}_{\min}(A|B)_{\Phi}
, again making use of the characterization of min-entropy-like quantities as support functions.

Having resolved the general framework and the breakdown of asymptotic results, we turn our attention to operational interpretations of the cone-restricted theory. First, we show that every restricted min-entropy for a convex closed cone that is invariant under local unitaries is captured by an entanglement-assisted communication game with restricted measurements (See Figure
2
). We refer to these as cone-restricted communication values as they fit into the framework of communication value
[
16
]
. This resolves an open problem from
[
16
]
.

Figure 2:

Bob then measures the entire state, but is restricted to measurements in the cone. The unnormalized success probability that Bob gets Alice’s message is given by the restricted min-entropy.

Second, we show a notion of quantum majorization of channels in terms of (possibly restricted) sets of bistochastic pre-processing in terms of a novel restricted min-entropy evaluated on the Choi matrix. In doing so, we stress the connection between majorization results and the viewpoint that min-entropic quantities are related to support functions.

Finally, we discuss further refinements of the behaviour of the restricted max-relative entropy and its relation to general conic norms. Specifically, we show for any cone captured by (an Abelian) symmetry and operators restricted to the cone, one can recover all the standard properties of the max-relative entropy and min-entropy. We however show this does not—at least obviously—extend to the case of cones which capture a notion of entanglement. We also show in what cases one can reduce the determination of the restricted max-relative entropy and min-entropy to a cone norm—a property that always holds when the cone is the positive semidefinite cone
[
22
]
. Regardless, we show that even when one can reduce the problem to determining the cone norm of an operator, things are not straightforward by considering the Werner states. As the total work is lengthy, we summarize the results below.

Unsurprisingly, convex geometry is integral to this work, and we briefly review the topic here. While this summary is sufficient, we refer the reader to

A set

Given a finite-dimensional real inner product space

(3)

2.2
Generalized Entropies

While the von Neumann entropy has been shown to be very successful for capturing the fundamental limits of asymptotically vanishing-error quantum information processing, there has been great progress in expanding beyond this regime by considering generalized entropies. Of particular interest for this work will be the max-relative entropy or max-divergence, which for
P,Q\in\mathrm{Pos}(A)
is given by

\displaystyle D_{\max}(P||Q)

\displaystyle:=\inf\{\alpha:P\leq 2^{\alpha}Q\}\ .

(7)

This measure has various nice properties, a few of which we now note. The max-divergence is known to be the smallest generalization of the classical max-divergence that is both additive and satisfies contractivity under CPTP maps
[
2
, Prop. 4.3]
. It is known to be a limit of the Sandwiched Rényi Divergences that have found great use recently:
D_{\max}(P||Q)=\lim_{\alpha\to\infty}\widetilde{D}_{\alpha}(P||Q)
where

\displaystyle\widetilde{D}_{\alpha}(P||Q)=\frac{1}{\alpha-1}\log\frac{\|Q^{\frac{1-\alpha}{2\alpha}}PQ^{\frac{1-\alpha}{2\alpha}}\|_{\alpha}^{\alpha}}{\Tr(P)}\ .

(8)

D_{\max}
also induces the min-entropy
H_{\min}(A|B)
by following the standard rule for inducing an entropy from a divergence
[
2
]
:

\displaystyle H_{\min}(A|B)_{\rho}:=\sup_{\sigma_{B}\in\mathrm{D}(B)}-D_{\max}(\rho_{AB}||I_{A}\otimes\sigma_{B})\ ,

(9)

where
I_{A}
is the identity on
A
.

Furthermore,
H_{\min}(A|B)
is well-known to satisfy a particularly nice duality property: for
\ket{\psi}\in A\otimes B\otimes C
,

\displaystyle H_{\min}(A|B)_{\rho_{AB}}=-H_{\max}(A|C)_{\rho_{AC}}\ ,

(10)

where
H_{\max}
is the entropy induced by
\widetilde{D}_{\min}(P||Q):=\lim_{n\to\infty}\widetilde{D}_{\alpha}(P||Q)
. We pre-emptively note there is an alternative max-entropy known as the quantum Hartley entropy, denoted
H_{0}(A|B)
, but under smoothing it is known to be logarithmically (in the smoothing parameter) equivalent
[
23
]
.

2.2.1
Recovering Asymptotic Results - Smoothed Entropies

These aforementioned measures are useful for the non-asymptotic setting as already noted. One would hope that under some setting, one could recover the asymptotic results. To do this, smoothed entropies were introduced
[
26
]
. In particular,

\displaystyle H^{\varepsilon}_{\min}(A:B)_{\rho}:=\max_{\widetilde{\rho}\in\mathscr{B}^{\varepsilon}(\rho)}H_{\min}(A|B)_{\widetilde{\rho}}\ ,

(11)

where
\mathscr{B}^{\varepsilon}(\rho):=\{\widetilde{\rho}:\Tr[\widetilde{\rho}]\leq 1,P(\rho,\widetilde{\rho})\leq\varepsilon\}

P(\cdot,\cdot)

\Delta(\rho,\sigma)\leq P(\rho,\sigma)\leq\sqrt{2\Delta(\rho,\sigma)}\ ,

where
\rho,\sigma
are sub-normalized states and
\Delta(\rho,\sigma)=\frac{1}{2}\{\|\rho-\sigma\|_{1}+|\Tr[\rho-\sigma]|\}
[
2
]

Using these definitions, it has been shown

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\left[\frac{1}{n}H^{\varepsilon}_{\min}(A^{n}|B^{n})_{\rho^{\otimes n}}\right]=H(A|B)_{\rho}\,,

(12)

and the same result for
H_{\max}^{\varepsilon}:=\min_{\widetilde{\rho}\in\mathscr{B}^{\varepsilon}(\rho)}H_{\max}(A|B)
.
This property is known as the fully quantum asymptotic equipartition property (FQAEP, AEP for short or when clear). This is because it allows one to recover asymptotic results in terms of standard entropies from the non-asymptotic setting under the i.i.d. assumption. This in a sense extends the asymptotic equipartition property that gives rise to the typical set used to derive asymptotic results, which is why it is given this name.
The FQAEP was first shown in the partially quantum setting by Renner
[
26
]
and then generalized to the fully quantum case in
[
27
]
. Since then, there have been various refinements in terms of second-order corrections and generalizing beyond i.i.d. structure. These AEPs have allowed for the unification of one-shot characterizations of many tasks
[
20
,
28
,
29
]
, and so the AEP property for the min-entropy is of interest in and of itself.

AEP of One-Shot Divergences and Stein’s Lemma

Given the success of one-shot entropies, further smoothed divergence measures were introduced
[
22
,
30
]
. In particular for our purposes, the smoothed max-relative entropy and one-shot hypothesis testing entropies:

\displaystyle D^{\varepsilon}_{\max}(\rho||\sigma):=\min_{\widetilde{\rho}\in\mathscr{B}^{\varepsilon}(\rho)}D_{\max}(\widetilde{\rho}||\sigma)

(13)

\displaystyle\exp(-D_{h}^{\ve}(P||Q))=\text{min.}

\displaystyle\langle P,X\rangle

(14)

\displaystyle\text{s.t.}

\displaystyle\langle Q,X\rangle\geq 1-\varepsilon

\displaystyle 0\leq X\leq I\ .

The latter of these two is named the hypothesis testing divergence because when
P
and
Q
in (
14
) are quantum states
\rho
and
\sigma
, then it determines the minimal error of the second kind,
\beta_{1}
, under the condition that the error of the first kind is bounded by
\varepsilon
,
\alpha_{1}\leq\varepsilon
, in hypothesis testing. That is, it captures asymmetric one-shot hypothesis testing exactly. Moreover, it may be used to recover the traditional (quantum) Stein’s lemma, which we state for completeness.

Proposition 2

(Stein’s Lemma
[
31
,
32
]
)
.

For hypotheses
\rho^{\otimes n}
and
\sigma^{\otimes n}
respectively and tolerated error of the first kind
\varepsilon
, denote the error of the second kind by
\beta^{\varepsilon}_{n}
. It holds that

-\lim_{n\to\infty}\left[\frac{1}{n}\log(\beta_{n}^{\ve})\right]=D(\rho||\sigma)\ .

As noted in
[
30
]
, we can recover Stein’s lemma directly from the operational interpretation of
\exp(-D^{\ve}_{h}(\rho||\sigma))
along with its AEP:

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\left[\frac{1}{n}D^{\varepsilon}_{h}(\rho^{\otimes n}||\sigma^{\otimes n})\right]=D(\rho||\sigma)\quad\forall\varepsilon\in(0,1)\ .

In fact, a second-order of Stein’s lemma follows from a second-order AEP for
D^{\varepsilon}_{h}
as determined in
[
28
]
.

While the smoothed max-divergence does not directly characterize hypothesis testing like the hypothesis testing divergence, it still can characterize Stein’s lemma
to first-order
. A simple way of seeing this is that the smooth max divergence’s AEP has the same behaviour:

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\left[\frac{1}{n}D^{\varepsilon}_{\max}(\rho^{\otimes n}||\sigma^{\otimes n})\right]=D(\rho||\sigma)\quad\forall\varepsilon\in(0,1)\ .

(15)

That
D^{\varepsilon}_{\max}
does not characterize hypothesis testing to second-order follows from
D^{\varepsilon}_{h}\neq D_{\max}^{\varepsilon}
.

An alternative way of seeing why
D^{\varepsilon}_{\max}
characterizes hypothesis testing to first-order follows from being able to bound the hypothesis testing divergence by the smooth max divergence. Specifically,
[
28
]
shows that for
0<\delta<\varepsilon<1
and any quantum states
\rho,\sigma
,

\displaystyle D^{\sqrt{1-\varepsilon+\delta}}_{\max}(\rho^{\otimes n}||\sigma^{\otimes n})+O(\log(\mrm{poly}(n)))\leq

\displaystyle D^{\varepsilon-\delta}_{h}(\rho^{\otimes n}||\sigma^{\otimes n})

\displaystyle\hskip 7.11317pt\leq D^{\sqrt{1-\varepsilon}}_{\max}(\rho^{\otimes n}||\sigma^{\otimes n})+O(\log(\mrm{poly}(n)))\ .

We can in fact abstract this above point. Any smoothed-divergence-like quantity
\mathcal{D}^{\varepsilon}
whose regularized limit converges to the relative entropy must to first-order characterize hypothesis testing. This is first because it’s regularized quantity converges to the same result as the Stein’s lemma. Second it is because it implies there are functions that are asymptotically sublinear in the copies of the state
n
for bounding the distance from
D^{\varepsilon}_{h}(\rho^{\otimes n}||\sigma^{\otimes n})
. We highlight this point as it will give us an information-theoretic operational lens for considering our cone-restricted max-divergence.

Proposition 3
.

Any smoothed divergence-like quantity
\mathcal{D}^{\varepsilon}(\cdot||\cdot)
that for all quantum states
\rho,\sigma
and
\varepsilon\in(0,1)
satisfies

\lim_{n\to\infty}\left[\frac{1}{n}\mathcal{D}^{\varepsilon}(\rho^{\otimes n}||\sigma^{\otimes n})\right]=D(\rho||\sigma)

characterizes hypothesis testing to first-order, i.e. characterizes Stein’s Lemma to first order.

In summary, the limits given in Eqns. (
12
) and (
15
) are clearly of great import to quantum information theory. One major result of this work is to establish under what settings these limits cease to hold as we modify the measure and how this relates to characterizing information-theoretic tasks and resource theories.

2.3.1
Resource Theory of Bounded Schmidt Number

Recall that given a pure state
\ket{\psi}\in A\otimes B
, the Schmidt rank of
\ket{\psi}
, denoted
\mathrm{SR}(\ket{\psi})
is the size of the smallest finite alphabet
\Sigma
such that there exist sets of pure state
\{\ket{\phi}_{i}\}_{i\in\Sigma}\subset A
,
\{\ket{\gamma}_{i}\}_{i\in\Sigma}\subset B
and probability distribution

, such that
\ket{\psi}=\sum_{i=1}^{|\Sigma|}\sqrt{p(i)}\ket{\phi_{i}}\otimes\ket{\gamma_{i}}
. The Schmidt number is the generalization to mixed states. That is, given
\rho\in\mathrm{D}(A\otimes B)
, the Schmidt number of
\rho
, denoted
\mathrm{SN}(\rho)
is the smallest
k
such that there exists a finite alphabet
\Sigma
such that
\rho=\sum_{i=1}^{|\Sigma|}p(i)|\psi_{i}\rangle\langle\psi_{i}|
where
p\in\mathcal{P}(\Sigma)
and
\mathrm{SR}(\psi_{i})\leq k
for all
i\in\Sigma
. As we will need cones, it would be useful to have the relevant cone which we now define. Following
[
24
]
, we refer to this cone as the ‘Entanglement Rank
r
Cone’.

Definition 1
.

An operator
X\in\mathrm{Pos}(A\otimes B)
is contained in the Entanglement Rank
r
Cone,
\mathrm{Ent}_{r}(A:B)
, if and only if there exists finite alphabet
\Sigma
and
\{Z_{i}\}_{i=1}^{|\Sigma|}\subset\mathrm{L}(A,B)
such that

X=\sum_{i\in\Sigma}\mathrm{vec}(Z_{i})\mathrm{vec}(Z_{i})^{\ast}

where
\rank(Z_{i})\leq r
for all
i\in\Sigma
.

A few remarks on the definition are in order. First, note that
\mathrm{Ent}_{r}(A:B)
is clearly a cone as this property won’t change under scaling by a non-negative value. Second, this is the cone which pertains to density matrices with Schmidt number bounded by
r
. There are many ways to see this, but one is to recall that the rank of a matrix is the tensor rank of the matrix, so by linearity of the vec mapping, (the renormalized)
\mathrm{vec}(Z_{i})
has Schmidt rank of at most
r
. This observation further implies
\mathrm{Ent}_{r}(A:B)\supset\mathrm{Ent}_{r-1}(A:B)
for
r\geq 2
with extreme cases
\mathrm{Ent}_{n}(A:B)=\mathrm{Pos}(A\otimes B)
where
n=\min\{d_{A},d_{B}\}
and
\mathrm{Ent}_{1}=\mathrm{Sep}(A:B)
where we recall
X\in\mathrm{Sep}(A:B)
if and only if
X=\sum_{i=1}^{|\Sigma|}P_{i}\otimes Q_{i}
where
\{P_{i}\}_{i=1}^{|\Sigma|}\subset\mathrm{Pos}(A)
,
\{Q_{i}\}_{i=1}^{|\Sigma|}\subset\mathrm{Pos}(B)
. Therefore, we can see the entanglement rank cones parameterized by
r
as interpolating between the separable cone and the positive semidefinite cone in terms of the amount of allowed entanglement. Lastly, we note that
\mathrm{Ent}_{r}(A:B)
is closed as follows. The set of pure states with Schmidt number
\leq r
is known to be compact
[
33
]
and it is clear that
\mathrm{Ent}_{r}(A:B)\cap\mathrm{D}
is the convex hull of the set of pure states with Schmidt number
\leq r
. As the convex hull of a compact set is compact,
\mathrm{Ent}_{r}(A:B)\cap\mathrm{D}
is compact. As the cone generated by a compact set not including zero is closed, by Proposition
1
, we conclude
\mathrm{Ent}_{r}(A:B)
is closed.

In terms of a proper resource theory, we only need a few general properties. First recall that the maximally entangled state
\ket{\tau}_{A\widetilde{A}}:=\frac{1}{\sqrt{d}}\sum_{i\in[d]}\ket{ii}_{A\widetilde{A}}
is considered the maximal resource in bipartite entanglement theory. We will denote

. However, if we only care about its dimension, we will denote it

[

\displaystyle\mathrm{supp}_{\tau_{d}}(X)\leq\frac{r}{d}\Tr[X]\ .

(16)

Moreover, this holds for any state that is local-unitarily equivalent to

2.3.2
Resource Theory of Coherence

A resource theory with somewhat similar structure to that of entanglement theory is that of coherence theory. While a very rich theory, we will only need the following results.

Definition 3
.

Let
A\cong\mathbb{C}^{d}
. An operator
X\in\mathcal{I}(A)
,i.e. incoherent with respect to a preferred orthonormal basis
\{\ket{i}\}_{i\in[d]}

X=\sum_{i\in[d]}\alpha_{i}|i\rangle\langle i|
where
\alpha_{i}\geq 0
for all

.

We define the maximally coherent state for
A\cong\mathbb{C}^{|\Sigma|}
by
\ket{\varsigma_{d}}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{d}}\sum_{i\in[d]}\ket{i}
. If we wish to refer to the density matrix form, we denote it
\varsigma_{d}\equiv|\varsigma_{d}\rangle\langle\varsigma_{d}|

It would be convenient for our purposes to be able to interpolate between the positive semidefinite cone and the fully incoherent cone
\mathcal{I}
in the same manner that the set of cones
\mathrm{Ent}_{r}
do for entanglement. For these purposes, we introduce the following cones.

Definition 4
.

Let
A\cong\mathbb{C}^{d}
. An operator

, i.e. have incoherence rank
r
with respect to a preferred orthonormal basis
\{\ket{i}\}_{i\in[d]}

\Sigma

\sum_{j\in\Sigma}\alpha_{j}\Pi_{j}

\{\Pi_{j}\}_{j\in\Sigma}
are incoherent projectors,
\Pi_{j}=\sum_{i\in S_{j}}\outerproduct{i}{i}

\max_{j}\rank(\Pi_{j})\leq r
.

Note these cones are clearly convex and that
\mathcal{I}(A)=\mathcal{I}_{1}(A)
,
\mathcal{I}_{d}=\mathrm{Pos}(A)
, and
\mathcal{I}_{r}(A)\supset\mathcal{I}_{r-1}(A)
for

, so we get the interpolation we wanted. It is also easy to see they are closed. consider
\mathcal{I}_{r}\cap\mathrm{D}
, which it is straightforward to see is the convex hull of the incoherent projectors excluding the zero matrix. For
A\cong\mathbb{C}^{d}
, there are a total of

non-zero incoherent projectors as there are
2^{d}-1
non-empty subsets of

\mathcal{I}_{r}\cap\mathrm{D}

\mathcal{I}_{r}
is closed.

We do note the above set of cones doesn’t restrict what subspaces are incoherent. We may conclude the cones restrict the strength of the coherence, not the subspace size.

Proposition 6
.

(Distance from
\varsigma_{d}

Let

\mathcal{I}_{P_{[d]}}

Proof.

Simply using the definitions,
Note

\langle\varsigma_{d},\Pi_{j}\rangle=d^{-1}\sum_{i\in S_{j}}|\bra{j}\ket{\varsigma_{d}}|^{2}=d_{1}^{-1}|S_{j}|\ ,

from which it follows

\displaystyle\langle\varsigma_{d},X\rangle=\sum_{j}\alpha_{j}\langle\varsigma_{d},\Pi_{j}\rangle\leq d^{-1}\sum_{j}\alpha_{j}|S_{j}|\leq\frac{r}{d}\Tr[X]\ ,

where the bound is by assumption on the bound on the rank of
\Pi_{i}
.
∎

Resource Theory of (Abelian) Asymmetry

An alternative approach to interpolating would be to simply think of sequences of cones with bounded subspace sizes. To do so, we can introduce the following definition.

Definition 6
.

Let
A\cong\mathbb{C}^{d}
. Let
\mathbb{P}:=\{\Pi_{i}\}_{i}
be a set of mutually orthogonal projectors that sum to the identity on

if and only if

for all

\mathcal{I}_{\mathbb{P}}
is clearly a convex cone and for the trivial choice

In comparison to
\mathcal{I}_{r}

is clearly convex. Therefore by Proposition

be the relevant projectors in Definition

3
Cone-Restricted Entropies

With all of this addressed, we now introduce our framework. At a high level the idea is to use that many one-shot entropies may be expressed as semidefinite programs and then consider sufficiently well-behaved closed convex cones that are subsets of the positive semidefinite cone. Of primary interest for this work will be the max-relative entropy and the min-entropy. We note there have been works that have had a similar approach, but these works are ultimately distinct in both form and what results are proven from them. We elaborate on this once we have introduced the restricted min-entropy.

3.1
The Restricted Max Relative Entropy

In the case of
P,Q\in\mathrm{Pos}(A)
such that
P\ll Q
, we can express
D_{\max}
in terms of a semidefinite program:

D_{\max}(P||Q)=\log\left(\min\{\gamma:P\leq\gamma Q\}\right)\ ,

where we have just used that
\gamma=2^{\alpha}
from the original definition (
7
). This can be seen as the dual program of a semidefinite program by considering the standard form of (
5
) for the choices
\mathcal{K}^{\ast}\to\mathrm{Pos}(A)
,
W\to\mathbb{R}
,
\phi^{\ast}(\gamma)=\gamma Q
,
y=\gamma
,
a=P
, and
b=1
.
We can then immediately abstract this idea to alternative cones.

Definition 7
.

For
P,Q\in\mathrm{Pos}(A)
and
\mathcal{K}\subseteq\mathrm{Pos}(A)
, the
\mathcal{K}
-restricted max-relative entropy is

D^{\mathcal{K}}_{\max}(P||Q):=\log\left(\inf\{\gamma:P\preceq_{\mathcal{K}^{\ast}}\gamma Q\}\right)\ .

Clearly this recovers
D_{\max}
for the choice of
\mathcal{K}=\mathrm{Pos}
. Moreover, we have the following straightforward relation between various cones, which implies
D_{\max}
achieves the highest value of all choices
\mathcal{K}\subset\mathrm{Pos}(A)
.

Proposition 7
.

Consider cones
\mathcal{K}_{1}\subseteq\mathcal{K}_{2}\subseteq\mathrm{Pos}(A)
. Let
P,Q\in\mathrm{Pos}(A)
. Then
D_{\max}^{\mathcal{K}_{1}}(P||Q)\leq D_{\max}^{\mathcal{K}_{2}}(P||Q)
.

Proof.

As
\mathcal{K}_{1}\subseteq\mathcal{K}_{2}
,
\mathcal{K}^{\ast}_{1}\supseteq\mathcal{K}^{\ast}_{2}
. As
P\preceq_{\mathcal{K}^{\ast}}\gamma Q\Leftrightarrow\gamma Q-P\in\mathcal{K}^{\ast}
, it follows the optimal
\gamma
for
D^{\mathcal{K}_{2}}(P||Q)
is always feasible for
D^{\mathcal{K}_{1}}_{\max}(P||Q)
. This completes the proof.
∎

It also allows us to express it in terms of a conic program when operator dominance is satisfied.

Proposition 8
.

Let
\mathcal{K}
be a closed, convex cone. Let
P\ll Q
where
P,Q\in\mathrm{Pos}(A)
. Then
\exp(D_{\max}^{\cK}(P||Q))
has the following primal and dual conic programs

\displaystyle\emph{Primal:}

\displaystyle\hskip 2.84526pt\max\{\langle P,X\rangle\;:\;\langle Q,X\rangle\leq 1\;\&\;X\in\mathcal{K}\}

(18)

\displaystyle\emph{Dual:}

\displaystyle\hskip 2.84526pt\min\{\gamma:\gamma Q\succeq_{\mathcal{K}^{\ast}}P\;\&\;\gamma\in\mathbb{R}\}

(19)

where we have trivially relaxed the equality in the primal problem.

Proof.

That they are maximizations/minimizations follows from the cone being closed and convex. The dual problem follows from the definition of
D^{\mathcal{K}}_{\max}
. The primal problem follows from noting that if
\phi^{*}(\lambda):=\lambda Q
, then
\phi(M)=\langle M,Q\rangle
.
∎

It is standard to prove strong duality for conic programs. Indeed, the above programs satisfy this, but it is easiest to see by first showing that
D^{\mathcal{K}}_{\max}
will in general satisfy the property of normalization.

Proposition 9

(Normalization)
.

Let
\mathcal{K}\subseteq\mathrm{Pos}
be closed and convex. Then

D^{\mathcal{K}}_{\max}(aP||bQ)=D^{\mathcal{K}}_{\max}(P||Q)+\log(a)-\log(b)\ .

Proof.

To see this, just consider the primal problem. The objective function and constraint are both linear. Therefore to obtain the optimum of
D^{\mathcal{K}}(aP||bQ)
, one can solve for the optimizer of the conic program for
D^{\mathcal{K}}(P||Q)
,
X^{\star}
. Let the optimal value of the conic program be
\alpha^{*}
. Then by linearity the optimizer of the conic program for
D^{\mathcal{K}}(aP||bQ)
is
b^{-1}X^{\star}
which obtains an optimal value
\frac{a}{b}\alpha^{*}
. As the relative entropy is the
\log
of the conic program, we have

\displaystyle D^{\mathcal{K}}_{\max}(aP||bQ)=\log(\frac{a}{b}\alpha^{*})=D^{\mathcal{K}}_{\max}(P||Q)+\log(a)-\log(b)\ ,

which completes the proof.
∎

Using this it is now easy to prove strong duality under the assumption that the identity is contained in the relative interior of the cone.

Proposition 10

(Strong Duality)
.

Let
P,Q\in\mathrm{Pos}(A)
such that
P\ll Q
. Let
\mathcal{K}\subseteq\mathrm{Pos}(A)
such that
I^{A}\in\mathrm{Relint}(\mathcal{K})
. Let
\alpha,\beta
be the values obtained by (
18
), (
19
) respectively. Then
\alpha=\beta
, i.e. strong duality holds.

Proof.

By the normalization property, it suffices to prove strong duality in the case
\hat{P},\hat{Q}\in\mathrm{D}(A)
where
\hat{P}=\Tr[P]^{-1}P
and likewise for
\hat{Q}
. We also assume we have not trivially relaxed the primal constraint. Then the choice
X=I^{A}
in the primal is strictly feasible by assumption. We know
\mathrm{Pos}\subseteq\mathcal{K}^{*}
by definition of dual cone and the assumption
\mathcal{K}\subseteq\mathrm{Pos}
. As
P\ll Q
, there must exist
\gamma
such that
\gamma Q-P\in\mathrm{Pos}\subset\mathcal{K}^{*}
. Thus the primal is strictly feasible and the dual is feasible, so strong duality holds by Slater’s condition for strong duality
[
25

4.1
The Anti-Standard Max-Divergence AEP

In this subsection we show sufficient conditions for breaking that the AEP for the cone-restricted max-divergence converges to the relative entropy. This allows us to give sufficient conditions for when the measure cannot characterize hypothesis testing of quantum states even to first order given Proposition
3
. In doing so, we reveal necessary conditions for fully quantum hypothesis testing. At the same time, we can instead think of the intuition as arising from resource theories. Specifically, what will be crucial in this section will be that the support of pure states within the cone is bounded from a target state outside of the cone. Interpreting the cone as induced by free states, we can think of this as the requirement that the free states are sufficiently bounded away from a maximally resourceful state under the inner product. We can then think of the intuition as being that when the free states are sufficiently bounded away from a maximal resource, the induced measure can’t behave like the un-restricted measure i.e. the max-relative entropy
D_{\max}
. This in turn means the fundamental limits of fully quantum information theory cannot be achieved in the restricted theory. We begin by capturing these ideas in the one-shot zero-error setting.

Proposition 13
.

Let
\mathcal{K}\subsetneq\mathrm{Pos}(A)
be a closed, convex cone such that
I\in\mathrm{Relint}(\mathcal{K})
. Let
\psi\in\mathrm{D}(A)
be a pure state (i.e.
\Tr[\psi^{2}]=1
) and
\psi\not\in\mathcal{K}
. It follows

D^{\mathcal{K}}_{\max}(\psi||\pi)<D(\psi||\pi)=D_{\max}(\psi||\pi)\ ,

where
\pi:=d_{A}^{-1}I_{A}
.

Proof.

Then

D(\psi||\pi)=\Tr[\psi\log\psi]-\Tr[\psi\log\pi]=\log(d_{A})\ .

As
I\in\mathrm{Relint}(\mathcal{K})
, we can use the primal problem (
18
):

\displaystyle D^{\mathcal{K}}_{\max}(\psi||\pi)=

\displaystyle\log(\max\{ \bra{\psi}X\ket{\psi} : \Tr[X] \leq d_{A} \, , \, X \in\cK\})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\log(d_{A} \max\{ \bra{\psi}\widehat{X}\ket{\psi} : \widehat{X} \in\Density\cap\cK\})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\log(d_{A})+\log(\max\{\bra{\psi}\widehat{X}\ket{\psi} : \widehat{X} \in\Density\cap\cK\})

\displaystyle<

\displaystyle\log(d_{A})\ ,

where the first equality is using the conic program (
18
) with the observations
\Tr[\psi X]=\bra{\psi}X\ket{\psi}
and
\Tr[\pi X]=d_{A}^{-1}\Tr[X]
, the second is because the optimal
X^{\star}
has trace of
d_{A}
since
\mathcal{K}
is a cone, and if
X^{\star}
is the optimizer, then
X^{\star}=d_{A}\widehat{X}
where
\widehat{X}\in\mathrm{D}\cap\mathcal{K}
, the third equality is a standard property of logarithms, and the strict inequality is because, by assumption
\psi\not\in\mathcal{K}
and is a closed cone, so
\max\{\bra{\psi}\widehat{X}\ket{\psi}:\widehat{X}\in\mathrm{D}\cap\mathcal{K}\}<1
and the logarithm must be strictly negative. In the case
\mathcal{K}=\mathrm{Pos}
, then the above derivation shows
D_{\max}(\psi||\pi)=\log(d_{A})
, so this completes the proof.
∎

First, we again stress that if we think of
\mathcal{K}
as being induced by free states
\mathcal{F}
, the above result shows the measure of a resourceful state from the maximally mixed (resource-less) state will differ between
D^{\mathcal{K}}_{\max}
and
D_{\max}
. Moreover, this is because the resourceful state’s overlap with any free state is necessarily lower than overlap over the unrestricted set of density matrices, which contains the resourceful state itself. Furthermore, note that the above result shows that for pure states outside of the cone
\mathcal{K}
, not only is
D^{\mathcal{K}}_{\max}<D_{\max}
, but it is less than the standard relative entropy
D
. This is a strong subversion as
D<D_{\max}
always holds, and this property may be viewed as the reason why the measure cannot characterize hypothesis testing even to first-order. Moreover, we note a standard way to prove that
D\leq D_{\max}
is using that
\log
is an operator monotone. It follows that the previous proposition implies, as we might expect,
\log
does not have an equivalent property under any further restricted conic ordering. We can in fact further show that for restricted cones, we cannot satisfy the canonical requirements of a divergence.

Corollary 4
.

Let
\mathcal{K}\subsetneq\mathrm{Pos}(A)
be a closed, convex cone such that
I\in\mathrm{Relint}(\mathcal{K})
.
Then
D^{\mathcal{K}}_{\max}
neither satisfies isometric invariance nor contractivity under CPTP maps (data-processing). That is,
D^{\mathcal{K}}_{\max}
is not a quantum Rényi entropy.

Proof.

As
\mathcal{K}\subsetneq\mathrm{Pos}(A)
is closed and convex, there must be a pure state
\psi\not\in\mathcal{K}
as cone of the convex hull of all pure states generates
\mathrm{Pos}(A)
. Therefore, let
\phi\in\mathrm{D}(A)
be pure and
\psi\not\in\mathcal{K}
. Let
U
be any unitary such that
U:\ket{\psi}\to\ket{\phi}
, where such a unitary will always exist.

\displaystyle D_{\max}^{\mathcal{K}}(\psi||I)<2\log(d)=

\displaystyle D^{\mathcal{K}}_{\max}(\phi||I)=D(U\psi U^{\ast}||UIU^{\ast})\ ,

where the first inequality is by the previous proposition.
∎

Given these two previous points, we might now ask if the max-divergence AEP (
15
) can hold for alternative cones, where, following (
13
),
D_{\max}^{\varepsilon,\mathcal{K}}(\rho||\sigma)=\min_{\widetilde{\rho}\in\mathscr{B}^{\varepsilon}(\rho)}D_{\max}^{\mathcal{K}}(\widetilde{\rho}||\sigma)
. Here we first show this in our most general mathematical form where all we are concerned about is the support of the cone on some target state. The advantage of this generality is that it will allow us to show how strongly dependent Stein’s lemma is on the positive cone by considering the measure. However, the general mathematical result again has meaning in terms of resource theories. In a resource theory you have some property that defines your free states
\mathcal{F}
on some Hilbert space
A
. Generally this property is well-defined on
n-
fold copies of the space. To be a physically well-defined theory, you would want that no matter how many copies you have, the free states are bounded away from the maximal resourceful state. Then taking the resource-theoretic interpretation of the cone as induced by the free states, and viewing the aforementioned ‘target state’ as a maximally resourceful state, the following theorem says if your resource theory is stable under copies, then the AEP does not converge to the relative entropy and thus cannot characterize hypothesis testing given Stein’s lemma.

Theorem 1
.

Consider a sequence of closed convex cones
\mathcal{K}^{(n)}\subset\mathrm{Pos}(A^{\otimes n})
. Let
\psi^{\otimes n}\not\in\mathcal{K}^{(n)}
for all
n\geq n_{0}
for some
n_{0}\in\mathbb{N}
, and
I^{A}\in\mathrm{Relint}(\mathcal{K}^{(n^{\prime})})
for all
n^{\prime}\geq n_{0}^{\prime}
. Then for all
\varepsilon\in[0,1]

\lim_{n\to\infty}\left\{\frac{1}{n}D^{\varepsilon,\mathcal{K}}_{\max}(\psi^{\otimes n}||\pi^{\otimes n})\right\}~<~D(\psi||\pi)\

if and only if
\mathrm{supp}_{\mathcal{K}^{(n)}\cap\mathrm{D}(A^{\otimes n})}(\psi^{\otimes n})\leq f(n)=O(c^{n})
where
c<1
.

Proof.

We first prove that the support condition implies the traditional AEP for max-divergence breaks down for these states. We will show
\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}D^{\mathcal{K}^{(n)}}_{\max}(\psi^{\otimes n}||\pi^{\otimes n})<D(\psi||\pi)
. Since smoothing the conic max-relative entropy only decreases it (
20
), it suffices to prove for
\varepsilon=0
as it would imply it for any larger
\varepsilon
. Then we consider

\displaystyle D^{\mathcal{K}^{(n)}}_{\max}(\psi^{\otimes n}||\pi^{\otimes n})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\log(\max\{ \bra{\psi}X\ket{\psi} : \Tr[X] \leq d^{n}_{A} \, , \, X \in\cK\})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\log(d^{n}_{A} \max\{ \bra{\psi^{\otimes n}}\widehat{X}\ket{\psi^{\otimes n}} : \widehat{X} \in\Density\cap\cK^{(n)} \})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle n\log(d_{A})+\log(\max\{\bra{\psi^{\otimes n}}\widehat{X}\ket{\psi^{\otimes n}} : \widehat{X} \in\Density\cap\cK^{(n)} \})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle n\log(d_{A})+\log(f(n))\ .

Therefore,

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\left[\frac{1}{n}D^{\mathcal{K}^{(n)}}_{\max}(\psi^{\otimes n})\right]=\log(d_{A})+\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{\log(f(n))}{n}=

\displaystyle\log(d_{A})+\log(c)

\displaystyle<

\displaystyle\log(d_{A})=D(\psi||\pi)\ ,

where we used the assumption
f(n)=O(c^{n})
where
c<1
. Note that smoothing only aggravates this problem.
This completes the first direction.

To prove the second direction, we prove the contrapositive. If
f(n)=2^{o(n)}
, i.e. is sub-exponential, then
\lim_{n\to\infty}1/n\log f(n)\to 0
, so the AEP between
\psi
and
\pi
would hold in this setting. Note that smoothing would preserve the
f(n)
bound.

As
f(n)\leq 1
for all
n
, the only option yet considered is
\lim_{n\to\infty}f(n)=1
, in which case
\lim_{n\to\infty}1/n\log f(n)=0
and the AEP holds. In the case of smoothing, if this limiting behaviour holds, the AEP holds. This completes the proof.
∎

Before applying Theorem

is the set of free states. Effectively this means either the state

This first captures a notion of the necessary cone for a max-divergence-like quantity to characterize the fully quantum Stein’s lemma. It also makes it clear that the the above result applies to resource theories whose free states are properly bounded away from their maximally resourceful state.

Corollary 5

We focus on the set of cones

\displaystyle\mathrm{supp}_{\mathcal{I}_{f(n)}\cap\mathrm{D}(A^{\otimes n})}\langle\varsigma_{d}^{\otimes n},\sigma_{K}\rangle\leq\frac{f(n)}{d^{n}_{A}}\ .

As long as

. Note that for any

This shows you can in effect exponentially increase any non-trivial fraction of the total initial space and allow for that much coherence, without the regularized quantity converging to the relative entropy. Thus, the sequence of cone-restricted max-divergences converge to the relative entropy, unless you had considered the entire space, i.e. the positive cone. It follows by Proposition

This shows that if a sequence of cones

can be contained by incoherent cones that don’t sufficiently contain their maximally coherent state, Stein’s lemma cannot be characterized by

∎

Without loss of generality, assume

For clarity, we assume

Note the entanglement rank cones have been chosen as they are physically interesting and capture the rate at which we increase entanglement, but they treat all maximally entangled states on equal footing. We can in fact construct a similar result to prioritize an arbitrary choice of maximally entangled.

Corollary 7
.

A sequence of cones
\{\mathcal{K}^{(n)}(A^{n}:B^{n})\}
only characterizes the Quantum Stein lemma to first-order if asymptotically it contains all states that are local-unitary equivalent to the canonical maximally entangled state.

Proof.

Fix a state that is local-unitarily equivalent to the maximally entangled state and denote it

For any

and every density matrix is positive,

over the closed interval

\widetilde{\mathcal{F}}_{n}
is a convex, compact set for each

is compact and does not include

\mathcal{I}
is in it’s relative interior so long as
\delta_{n}\leq 1-d^{-n}
for all
n
as that is small enough that
\mathcal{K}^{(n)}\supseteq\mathrm{Ent}_{1}(A:B)
by (

\mathrm{Relint}(\mathcal{K}):=\{x\in\mathcal{K}:\exists\varepsilon>0:\mathcal{N}_{\varepsilon}(x)\cap\mathrm{Aff}(\mathcal{K})\subset\mathcal{K}\}\ ,

we know there is an epsilon ball of separable states around the identity
[

]

contains the separable states, so the appropriate choice of epsilon results in the intersection being contained in
\mathcal{K}^{(n)}
. Thus we have cones that satisfy constraints to apply Theorem
1
. Therefore as long as
\limsup_{n}\delta_{n}<1
, the regularized quantity does not converge to the relative entropy and so by Proposition
3
, Quantum Stein’s lemma cannot be characterized by this sequence of cones.

Now first note this only considered a single choice of maximally entangled state, so it’s much stronger than the previous entanglement rank result as, for example, the set will include the rest of the maximally entangled state basis under local unitaries
(U\otimes W)
as they are mutually orthogonal. Finally, the above shows that if a sequence of cones
\{\mathcal{K}^{(n)}\}_{n}
can be contained by

for a choice of
\widetilde{\tau}_{d}
and appropriate choices of

, then the Stein’s lemma cannot be characterized by this sequence of measures. It follows for Stein’s lemma to hold, that must not be the case for any choice of
\widetilde{\tau}_{d}
which completes the proof.
∎

4.2
Anti-Standard Min-Entropy AEP

In the previous section we showed general conditions such that the convergence of the regularized cone-restricted max-divergence is not the relative entropy and thus cannot characterize hypothesis testing even to first-order. In particular, recalling the normalization property (Proposition
9
), we have shown this by showing the regularized

does not converge to

when
\rho
is maximally coherent and the cone is
\mathcal{I}_{r}
.
Noting that
H(A)_{\rho}=-D(\rho||I)
and
H_{\min}(A)_{\rho}=-\log(\supp_{\Density(A)}(\rho))

[

, one might suspect that we have inadvertently also shown that

does not satisfy an AEP in a general setting. Indeed, this follows from (

so that the cone
\mathcal{K}(A\otimes B)
is really

. The focus of this section is showing this to be the case of the restricted min-entropy in the general conditional case. While this may seem uninteresting as we have broken the unconditional case, the conditional case is fundamental in terms of entanglement theory, whereas in the unconditional case the coherence resource seems the fundamental choice. Following the same presentation as the previous section, we first show how the one-shot case breaks down for general cones, then we present sufficient conditions for an AEP to breakdown.

We note as explained in Section
3.3
, there is an asymmetry for min-entropy that is captured in difference of the type of support function. Formally, by the same proof as for Proposition
12
, we have for

\displaystyle\exp(-H_{\min}^{\cK}(B|A)_{P})=d_{B}\underset{\Psi^{\ast}\in\mathrm{CPU}(B,A):J_{\Psi}\in\mathcal{K}}{\max}\langle(\Psi\otimes\textrm{id}_{B})(P),\tau_{d_{B}}\rangle\ ,

which is less natural to work with. However, we can use the support function definition of

directly, to see how the AEP function behaves. This partially resolves an open direction noted in
[
17
]
, though the cones we focus on are more specific.

We first present explicit examples of where there exists a gap between

,

and
H^{\mathcal{K}}_{\min}
in the same fashion as for
D_{\max}^{\mathcal{K}}
. This will hold under the rather minimal assumption that

does not contain all pure states

where

is a unitary. It also makes clear the general idea of when the AEP would fail.

Proposition 14
.

Let
\mathcal{K}\subsetneq\mathrm{Pos}(A\otimes B)
be closed and convex and that
d_{A}^{-1}I^{AB}\in\mathrm{Relint}(\mathcal{K})
. Let
\psi:=(I^{A}\otimes U)\tau_{d}(I^{A}\otimes U^{\ast})\not\in\mathcal{K}\subset\mathrm{Pos}(A:B)
where

is a unitary. Then there exists
\rho
such that

H^{\mathcal{K}}_{\min}(A|B)_{\rho}>H(A|B)_{\rho}=H_{\min}(A|B)_{\rho}\ .

Proof.

We are going to show this for
\rho=\psi
. By Proposition
12
, we want to maximize
\langle\textrm{id}_{A}\otimes\Psi(\psi),\tau_{d}\rangle
over CPTNI
\Psi
such that
J_{\Psi^{*}}\in\mathcal{K}
. Clearly the unique optimal choice of map is
\Psi:=U^{\ast}(\cdot)U
as this will achieve the value of
1
and mapping
\psi
to any other state will achieve
<1
, though possibly to arbitrary precision. We therefore focus on this map and the arbitrary precision will be resolved by the closed-ness of the cone.
The dual map of this is
U(\cdot)U^{\ast}
whose Choi operator is
d(I_{A}\otimes U)(\tau_{d})(I_{A}\otimes U)^{\ast}=d\psi
. By assumption,
\psi
is not in the cone so neither is

. Therefore the Choi operator of the dual map of the optimal choice is not in the cone. Since the cone is closed one also cannot be arbitrarily approaching this point. We therefore may conclude the optimal

is the negative log of this optimized inner product scaled by

(Proposition

H^{\mathcal{K}}_{\min}(A|B)_{\psi}>\log(d_{A})=H(A|B)_{\psi}=H_{\min}(A|B)_{\psi}
where we have used the strict monotonicity of the logarithm and that a straightforward calculation will show
H(A|B)_{\psi}=H(A)_{\pi}=\log(d_{A})
.
∎

We now show the same property for

Proposition 15
.

Let
\mathcal{K}\subsetneq\mathrm{Pos}(A\otimes B)

d_{A}^{-1}I^{AB}\in\mathrm{Relint}(\mathcal{K})

Proof.

Let

. Then,

\displaystyle\langle J_{\Psi},J_{\mathcal{U}}\rangle=d^{2}\langle(\textrm{id}_{A}\otimes\mathcal{U}^{\ast}\circ\Psi)(\tau),\tau\rangle<d^{2}\ ,

where we used the definition of the Choi operator, that the identity map is self-adjoint,
\mathcal{U}^{\ast}\circ\Psi
cannot be the identity map by assumption. Noting
H_{\min}(B|A)_{J_{\textrm{id}}}=H(B|A)_{J_{\textrm{id}}}=-2\log(d)

∎

Remark 1
.

If we had taken (

We note the proposition and corollary above imply that for any cone to achieve the same value as the min-entropy in even simple cases,

Theorem 2
.

Consider a sequence of cones
\mathcal{K}^{(n)}((A\otimes A^{\prime})^{\otimes n})

\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}H^{\mathcal{K}^{(n)}}_{\min}(A^{n}|B^{n})_{\tau^{\otimes n}}>H(A|B)_{\tau}

Local CP Map invariance of Free Resources Sufficient for anti-standard min-entropy AEP—

While the above is useful for exemplifying that entanglement is the property mathematically captured by a fully quantum AEP, if we wished to interpret this in terms of resource theories, this is not as insightful. This is because normally a resource theory is defined on a space and then one considers many copies of states on that space. In other words, the cones in a generalized resource theory might be best thought of as probabilistic mixtures of many copies of some free resource, as we will soon formalize. It is a natural question to ask what property must a generalized resource theory satisfy for these induced cones to lead to an anti-standard AEP. Here we will show it suffices for the free resources to be closed under local CP maps and not contain the maximally entangled state. To do this, we will need the notion of completely free-resource-forcing channel.

Definition 8
.

Given a set of free states
\mathcal{F}
that can be well-defined for any Hilbert space, a channel
\Phi\in\mathrm{C}(A,B)
is completely resource-breaking (CRB) if
(\textrm{id}_{A}\otimes\Phi)(\rho_{RA})\in\mathcal{F}(RB)
for all
\rho\in\mathrm{D}(RA)
.

First we remark that CRB channels are not the same as resource-destroying maps
[
38
]
. First, resource-destroying maps do not consider an ancillary space. Second, a map is resource-destroying only if any free state is mapped to itself. In contrast, the map that throws out the input and prepares a fixed free state is likely to be CRB, e.g. if one thinks of separable states as being the free states. Having established this, we show that for resource theories that capture locality, the CRB channels play an important algebraic role in the resource theory.

Proposition 16
.

Consider a resource theory with a set of free states
\mathcal{F}
that is well-defined for any Hilbert space and stable under local CP maps. Then
\Phi\in\mathrm{C}(A,B)
is a completely resource-breaking channel if and only if
J_{\Phi}\in\mathcal{K}_{\mathcal{F}}
.

Proof.

(
\Rightarrow
) Let
\Phi\in\mathrm{C}(A,B)
be a CRB channel. Then
J_{\Phi}=d_{A}(\textrm{id}_{A}\otimes\Phi)(\tau_{AA^{\prime}})=d_{A}\sigma_{AB}\in\mathcal{K}_{\mathcal{F}}
where we have used the definition of CRB channel.

(
\Leftarrow
) Let
J_{\Phi}\in\mathcal{K}_{\mathcal{F}}
. Then define

d_{A}\rho^{AB}:=(\textrm{id}_{A}\otimes\Phi)(\Phi^{+})=J_{\Phi}\in\mathcal{K}_{\mathcal{F}}\ .

Recall that given
\sigma_{A}
,
\psi_{AA^{\prime}}=d_{A}^{-1}\sqrt{\sigma_{A}}\Phi^{+}\sqrt{\sigma_{A}}
is a purification of
\sigma_{A}
. It follows that for any
\sigma_{A}
,

d_{A}(\textrm{id}_{A}\otimes\Phi)(\psi_{AA^{\prime}})=d_{A}\sqrt{\sigma_{A}}J_{\Phi}\sqrt{\sigma_{A}}\in\mathcal{K}_{\mathcal{F}}\ ,

where the containment follows from the assumption
\mathcal{F}
is local CP map invariant. As there is no restriction on
\sigma_{A}
, we have for any pure state
\psi_{AA^{\prime}}
, the above equation holds. Therefore, by linearity,
(\textrm{id}_{A}\otimes\Phi)(\rho_{AA^{\prime}})\in\mathcal{F}
for all
\rho_{AA^{\prime}}
.

To extend to arbitrary
\textrm{id}_{R}
, note that for any
R
it suffices to prove it for pure states
\psi_{RA}
due to linearity. By isometric equivalence of purifications,
\ket{\psi}_{RA^{\prime}}=(V_{A\to R}\otimes\textrm{id}_{A})\ket{\psi}_{AA^{\prime}}
, which again results in a local CP map. This completes the proof.
∎

As noted, in a resource theory, you normally define the free states on a single copy of the (multipartite) space and then use many copies, e.g. your resource is
\sigma^{\otimes n}
where
\sigma\in\mathcal{F}\subset\mathrm{D}(A\otimes B)
. You could then in principle use probabilistic mixtures of such a state, i.e. your states are the convex hull . Thus for
n
copies the natural set to consider is

\mathcal{F}_{n}:=\mathrm{conv}(\{\sigma^{\otimes n}:\sigma\in\mathcal{F}\})\ .

Then we can generate the cone from this. We now combine the above result with Theorem
1
to get the following, which in effect says you won’t satisfy an AEP with the cone unless the maximal resource is contained in the free states.

Corollary 9
.

For a resource theory with free states
\mathcal{F}\subset\mathrm{D}(A\otimes B)
stable under local CP maps, the AEP does not hold unless
\tau\in\mathcal{F}
, i.e. the maximally entangled state is included.

Proof.

If
\tau\not\in\mathcal{F}
, there exists
\delta\in(0,1)
such that
\max_{\sigma\in\mathcal{F}}\langle\sigma,\tau\rangle\leq\delta
. Note this will also be true if you swap the spaces and take the transpose as
\tau
is invariant under both of these operations. This is useful for our purposes as these operations take
J_{\Psi}
to
J_{\Psi^{\ast}}
. Define
\widetilde{\mathcal{F}}_{n}:=\{{\mathrm{SWAP}(\sigma^{\otimes n})}^{\mathrm{T}}:\sigma\in\mathcal{F}\}
.
It follows

\max_{\sigma^{n}\in\widetilde{\mathcal{F}}_{n}}\langle\sigma^{n},\tau^{\otimes n}\rangle=\max_{\nu}\int_{\sigma\in\widetilde{\mathcal{F}}}\langle\sigma^{\otimes n},\tau^{\otimes n}\rangle d\nu(\sigma)\leq\delta^{n}\ ,

where
\nu
is optimized over the space of probability measures over
\widetilde{\mathcal{F}}
and the final equality follows by linearity. Therefore, by Proposition
16
,
\mathrm{supp}_{\sigma\in\mathrm{cone}(\widetilde{\mathcal{F}}_{n})\cap\widetilde{\mathrm{D}}}\langle\sigma,\tau^{\otimes n}\rangle\leq\delta^{n}
. As
\widetilde{\mathcal{F}}_{n}
corresponds to swapping the spaces and taking the complex conjugate, this is the space of adjoint maps. Thus we can apply Theorem
2
without needing to assume
J_{\Psi^{\ast}}\in\mathcal{K}
to break the AEP as
\delta^{n}\to 0
since
\delta<1
. We can do an identical argument for
\mathcal{F}_{n}
to apply Theorem
3
. This completes the proof.
∎

Coherence anti-standard min-entropy AEP

While in this setting entanglement does seem to be the fundamental property, it may be of interest that we can present a coherence version of the anti-standard AEP, since we were able to present an entanglement version of breaking the cone-restricted max-divergence AEP. To do this, we do note that certain incoherent operations have algebraic structure that imply properties of their Choi matrices in terms of entanglement rank.

Proposition 17
.

A quantum channel
\Psi\in\mathrm{C}(A,B)
is classical-to-classical in a preferred basis if and only if
J_{\Psi}\in\mathcal{I}(A\otimes B)
. Moreover, this implies
J_{\Psi^{\ast}}\in\mathcal{I}(A\otimes B)

Therefore, as

Restricted Min- and Max-Entropy Duality

One well known property of the standard min- and max-entropy mentioned in the background is that they satisfy a special duality: for pure states
\rho_{ABC}
,
H_{\min}(A|B)=-H_{\max}(A|C)

[

be invariant under local unitaries under both spaces. We will call such a cone

Definition 9

(Restricted Max-Entropy)
.

Consider

of it. Let

\omega\in\mathrm{D}(C)\end{subarray}}\,F^{2}(\Phi(\rho_{ABC}),\tau_{AA^{\prime}}\otimes\omega_{C})\ .

We first note that the definition is global in the sense that to even define the measure required reference to the purified space
B
.
This differs than in the standard min-entropy, i.e. the

Symmetries, Data-Processing Inequalities, and Special Cases

To prove that the separable cone is sufficient for partially quantum information theory, it will be useful to consider the general theory of symmetries and data processing inequality for smoothed and unsmoothed restricted min-entropy, where we recall
H_{\min}^{\mathcal{K},\varepsilon}(A|B)_{\rho}=\underset{\widetilde{\rho}\in\mathcal{B}^{\varepsilon}(\rho)}{\max}H^{\mathcal{K}}_{\min}(A|B)_{\widetilde{\rho}}
.

Recalling Corollary
4
shows restricted relative entropy does not satisfy a data-processing inequality, we generalize
[

in the straightforward manner to get the general conic data-processing settings.

Proposition 22
.

Consider
\mathcal{K}\subseteq\mathrm{Pos}
. Given
\Phi:\mathrm{Pos}(A\otimes B)\to\mathrm{Pos}(A^{\prime}\otimes B^{\prime})
such that
\Phi^{\ast}:\mathcal{K}(A^{\prime}:B^{\prime})\to\mathcal{K}(A:B)
, then
D_{\max}^{\mathcal{K}}(\Phi(P)||\Phi(Q))\leq D_{\max}^{\mathcal{K}}(P||Q)
for all
P,Q\in\mathrm{Pos}(A\otimes B)
. Moreover, if there exists
\phi:\mathrm{Pos}(B)\to\mathrm{Pos}(B^{\prime})
that is trace-preserving such that
\Phi(I_{A}\otimes\sigma_{B})\preceq_{\mathcal{K}{\ast}}I_{A^{\prime}}\otimes\phi(\sigma_{B})
for all
\sigma_{B}\in\mathrm{D}(B)
. Then
H^{\mathcal{K}}_{\min}(A|B)_{P}\leq H^{\mathcal{K}}_{\min}(A|B)_{\Phi(P)}
for all
P\in\mathrm{Pos}(A\otimes B)

Proof.

We begin with the
D_{\max}^{\mathcal{K}}
case. Recall
D_{\max}^{\mathcal{K}}(P||Q)=\log\inf\{\gamma:\gamma Q-P\in\mathcal{K}^{\ast}\}
. Recall by definition of dual cone,
X\in\mathcal{K}^{\ast}(A:B)
iff
\langle X,Y\rangle\geq 0
for all
Y\in\mathcal{K}(A:B)
. It follows, if
X\in\mathcal{K}^{\ast}(A:B)
and any
Y\in\mathcal{K}(A^{\prime}:B^{\prime})
,
\langle\Phi(X),Y\rangle=\langle X,\Phi^{\ast}(Y)\rangle\geq 0
where the last inequality is by the assumption
\Phi^{\ast}:\mathcal{K}(A^{\prime}:B^{\prime})\to\mathcal{K}(A:B)
. Thus
\Phi:\mathcal{K}^{\ast}(A:B)\to\mathcal{K}^{\ast}(A^{\prime}:B^{\prime})
. Consider any feasible
\gamma
for
D_{\max}^{\mathcal{K}}(P||Q)
. Then we have

\displaystyle 0\preceq_{\mathcal{K}}^{\ast}\Phi(\gamma Q-P)=\gamma\Phi(Q)-\Phi(P)\ .

Therefore any feasible
\gamma
for
D_{\max}^{\mathcal{K}}(P||Q)
is feasible for
D_{\max}^{\mathcal{K}}(\Phi(P)||\Phi(Q))
. As we are considering an infimum, this completes the proof of the
D_{\max}^{\mathcal{K}}
case.

For the restricted min-entropy case, recall that

\displaystyle H^{\mathcal{K}}_{\min}(A|B)_{\rho}:=-\min\{\lambda\in\mathbb{R}:2^{\lambda}I_{A}\otimes\sigma_{B}-\rho\succeq_{\mathcal{K}^{\ast}}0\,\&\,\sigma_{B}\in\mathrm{D}(B)\}\ .

Consider any feasible point
(\lambda,\sigma_{B})
for
H^{\mathcal{K}}_{\min}(A|B)_{\rho}
, then we have

\displaystyle 0\preceq_{\mathcal{K}^{\ast}}\Phi(2^{\lambda}I_{A}\otimes\sigma_{B}-\rho)=2^{\lambda}\Phi(I_{A}\otimes\sigma_{B})-\Phi(\rho)\preceq_{\mathcal{K}^{\ast}}2^{\lambda}I_{A^{\prime}}\otimes\phi(\sigma_{B})-\Phi(\rho),

where in the second generalized inequality, we used our assumption on the existence of
\phi
.Thus, for any feasible
(\lambda,\sigma_{B})
for
H^{\mathcal{K}}_{\min}(A|B)_{\rho}
,
(\lambda,\phi(\sigma_{B}))
is feasible
H^{\mathcal{K}}_{\min}(A^{\prime}|B^{\prime})_{\Phi(\rho)}
. Thus the optimal
\lambda
for determining
H^{\mathcal{K}}_{\min}(A^{\prime}|B^{\prime})_{\Phi(\rho)}
is at least as small as that for
H^{\mathcal{K}}_{\min}(A|B)_{\Phi(\rho)}
. Taking into account the minus sign proves
H^{\mathcal{K}}_{\min}(A^{\prime}|B^{\prime})_{\Phi(\rho)}\geq H^{\mathcal{K}}_{\min}(A|B)_{\rho}
.
∎

We now want to consider the special case of states with symmetries. As introduced in the background, a ‘symmetry’ of a state
\rho
means
U\rho U^{\ast}=\rho
where
U
is some unitary. If you take all the symmetries of a given state, they form a group. Given the group
G
of symmetries of a state
\rho
, define the projection/twirling map
\mathcal{G}(\cdot)=\int U\cdot U^{\ast}dU
where
dU
is the Haar measure for the relevant group. Clearly
\mathcal{G}(\rho)=\rho
.

Symmetries are useful for two reasons: first, they are relevant throughout physics and determine coherence resource theories. Second, they can take complicated problems and reduce them to be more manageable. In particular, this has been useful for simplifying entanglement measures in certain cases
[
40
]
, and as we are interested in entanglement rank cones, one might expect this to be helpful here as well.

Remark 2
.

We note a more general class of operators of similar form are mixed unitary channels where the difference is the unitaries need not make up a group. In effect, when a mixed unitary channel is not composed of a group of unitaries that are integrated according to the Haar measure, the channel won’t be self-adjoint, which means moving it onto the observable doesn’t restrict the relevant state space. Hence we do not consider such channels here.

We now prove a general abstract theorem and then consider specific cases.

Theorem 4
.

Let
\rho_{AB}
be invariant under a projection channel
\mathcal{G}
. Let
\mathcal{G}
satisfy data-processing for
H_{\min}^{\mathcal{K}}(A|B)_{\rho}
where
\mathcal{K}\subset\mathrm{Pos}(A\otimes B)
. Then without loss of generality, when determining
H^{\mathcal{K},\varepsilon}_{\min}(A|B)_{\rho}
, one can restrict to optimizing over
\{\widetilde{\rho}\in\mathcal{B}^{\varepsilon}(\rho):\mathcal{G}(\widetilde{\rho})=\widetilde{\rho}\}
.

Proof.

Since
\rho
is invariant under
\mathcal{G}
, if
\widetilde{\rho}\in\mathcal{B}^{\varepsilon}(\rho)
, then
\mathcal{G}(\rho)\in\mathcal{B}^{\varepsilon}(\rho)
as purified distance monotonically decreases under CPTNI maps. Since, by assumption,
H^{\mathcal{K}}_{\min}
only increases under the action of
\mathcal{G}
, the optimizer must be in the post-twirled set. Noting
\mathcal{G}
is a linear (and thus continuous) map and
\mathcal{B}^{\varepsilon}(\rho)
is compact, the restricted set is compact. Thus we can restrict ourselves to this set and achieve the same value.
∎

We now present the less general but primarily relevant setting.

.

\displaystyle\mathcal{G}(I_{A}\otimes\sigma_{B})=\int(U_{A}I_{A}U^{\ast}_{A}\otimes U_{B}\sigma U_{B}^{\ast})\,dU=I_{A}\otimes\int U_{B}\sigma U_{B}^{\ast}\,dU\ ,

Proposition 24
.

Any pinching channel can be written as a mixed unitary channel where the unitaries form a group, i.e. a pinching channel is a specific type of symmetry.

Proof.

Suppose
|\Sigma|=r
. Define the unitary operators

U_{k}=\sum_{x=0}^{r-1}e^{ixk/(2\pi r)}\Pi_{x}\qquad\text{for $k=0,\cdots,r-1$}.

Then

\displaystyle\frac{1}{r}\sum_{k=0}^{r-1}U_{k}\rho U_{k}^{\dagger}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{r}\sum_{x,x^{\prime}=0}^{r-1}\sum_{k=0}^{r-1}e^{i(x-x^{\prime})k/(2\pi r)}\Pi_{x}\rho\Pi_{x^{\prime}}\}=\sum_{x=0}^{r-1}\Pi_{x}\rho\Pi_{x}.

This is clearly a group as
k=0
results in the identity operator,
U_{k}U_{k^{\prime}}=U_{k+k^{\prime}}
, and
U_{r-k}
is the inverse of
U_{k}
for any
k\in\{0,...,r-1\}
.
∎

Corollary 13

If any register of
\rho_{ABCD}
satisfies a pinching invariance, then we can restrict to smoothed states in its ball that satisfy said invariance.

Separable Cone Sufficient for Classical-Quantum Information Theory

Corollary

We now extend this point to a sufficient number of common entropic quantities to convince ourselves that we have recovered at least point-to-point classical-quantum information theory.

Corollary

The Hartley entropy may be written as

\displaystyle\exp(H_{0,\max}(A|B))=\sup_{\sigma\in\mathrm{D}(B)}\Tr(\Pi_{\rho}(\I_{A} \otimes\sigma_{B}))=\|\Tr_{A}(\Pi_{\rho})\|_{\infty}\ ,

where
\Pi_{\rho}

Note that
\Pi_{\rho}
may be viewed as the projection onto the positive eigenspace of

\succeq_{\mathcal{K}}

\{\Pi^{\mathcal{K}}\succeq\Pi_{\rho}\}:=\{\Pi\in\mathrm{Proj}\cap\mathcal{K}:\Pi\succeq\Pi_{\rho}\}\ .

We then use this set to define the conic Hartley entropy.

\displaystyle\exp(H^{\cK}_{0,\max}(A|B)):=\underset{\Pi\in\{\Pi^{\mathcal{K}}\succeq\Pi_{\rho}\}}{\min}\|\tr_{A}(\Pi_{AB})\|_{\infty}\ .

(30)

This is the definition we want as we now argue by showing that it satisfies desirable properties.
To do this, we first introduce the

Definition 10

Fix a basis of

This cone is clearly generated by the CQ quantum states, which is why we call it the CQ cone. Moreover, note that by definition of restricted Hartley entropy it is always an upper bound on
H_{0,\max}^{\mathrm{Sep}}

\displaystyle H_{0,\max}(A|B)_{\rho}=-H_{\min}^{\downarrow}(A|C)_{\rho}\ ,

Next we prove an unsurprising but special property of the CQ-Hartley entropy.

Proposition 26
.

Let
\rho_{AB}
be an arbitrary state. Then

H_{0,\max}^{\mathrm{CQ}}(A|B)_{\rho}=H_{0,\max}(A|B)_{(\mathcal{G}\otimes\textrm{id})(\rho)}\ ,

where
\mathcal{G}
is dephasing according to the
\mathrm{CQ}

Proof.

Define the map
\Phi_{x^{\prime}}(\cdot):=(\bra{x'}\otimes I_{B})\cdot(\ket{x'}\otimes I_{B})
for all
x^{\prime}\in\mathcal{X}

p(x)\rho_{B}^{x}:=\Phi_{x}(\rho_{AB})

then let

be the zero matrix. Let
\Pi_{\rho^{x}}
be the projector onto the support of
\rho_{B}^{x}

\Pi_{\rho^{x}}-\rho^{x}_{B}\succeq 0

\Pi_{\rho^{x}}\succ\widetilde{\Pi}_{x}

\widetilde{\Pi}_{x}-\rho_{B}^{x}\not\succeq 0

\Phi_{x^{\prime}}(\sum_{x}\outerproduct{x}{x}\otimes\widetilde{\Pi}_{x}-\rho_{AB})=\outerproduct{x'}{x'}\otimes(\widetilde{\Pi}_{x^{\prime}}-\rho_{B}^{x^{\prime}})\not\succeq 0

\mathrm{CQ}
cone as removing anything from it will become infeasible. Moreover,
\sum_{x}\outerproduct{x}{x}\otimes\Pi_{\rho^{x}}
is the projector onto the support of

Lemma 5

Let
\rho_{XBC}
be pure where it is classical on

H_{0,\max}^{\mathrm{CQ},\varepsilon}(X|B)_{\rho}=-H_{\min}^{\downarrow,\varepsilon}(X|C)_{\rho}

Proof.

We effectively follow the proof of duality for smooth entropies from

As already noted, in the relevant setting both spaces are invariant under local embeddings, so we may assume the spaces

and

\displaystyle H_{0,\max}^{\mathrm{CQ},\varepsilon}(X|B)_{\rho}

\displaystyle=\min_{\widetilde{\rho}\in\mathscr{B}^{\varepsilon}_{\ast}(\rho_{XBC})}H_{0,\max}(X|B)_{(\mathcal{G}\otimes\textrm{id}_{BC})(\widetilde{\rho})}

\displaystyle=\min_{\widetilde{\rho}\in\mathscr{B}^{\varepsilon}_{\ast}(\rho_{XBC})}-H_{\min}^{\downarrow}(X|C)_{(\mathcal{G}\otimes\textrm{id}_{BC})(\widetilde{\rho})}

\displaystyle\geq\min_{\widetilde{\rho}\in\mathscr{B}^{\varepsilon}(\rho_{XC})}-H_{\min}^{\downarrow}(X|C)_{(\mathcal{G}\otimes\textrm{id}_{C})(\widetilde{\rho})}

\displaystyle=-H_{\min}^{\downarrow,\varepsilon}(X|C)_{\rho}\ ,

where the second equality uses the duality (

For the other direction, note by DPI of max-relative entropy and the purified distance,

without loss of generality. Therefore it’s the same idea in backwards, but we provide it for completeness.

\displaystyle-H_{\min}^{\downarrow,\varepsilon}(X|C)_{\rho}

\displaystyle=-\max_{\widetilde{\rho}\in\mathscr{B}^{\varepsilon}_{\ast}(\rho_{XBC})}H_{\min}^{\downarrow}(X|C)_{(\mathcal{G}\otimes\textrm{id}_{BC})(\widetilde{\rho})}

\displaystyle=\min_{\widetilde{\rho}\in\mathscr{B}^{\varepsilon}_{\ast}(\rho_{XBC})}H_{0,\max}(X|B)_{(\mathcal{G}\otimes\textrm{id})(\rho)}

\displaystyle=\min_{\widetilde{\rho}\in\mathscr{B}^{\varepsilon}_{\ast}(\rho_{XBC})}H_{0,\max}^{\mathrm{CQ}}(X|B)_{(\mathcal{G}\otimes\textrm{id})(\rho)}

This completes the proof.

Corollary 16
.

As we have chosen to define them so as to recover the proper measure when
\mathcal{K}=\mathrm{Pos}

H_{0,\max}^{\mathcal{K}}
and

asymptotically does not hold when one considers a sequence of cones

\mathcal{K}^{(n)}\subseteq\mathrm{Ent}_{f(n)}(A^{n}:B^{n})

Using Corollary

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\left[\frac{1}{n}H^{\mathcal{K}^{(n)}}_{\max}(X^{n}|Z^{n})\right]<

\displaystyle H(X|Z)_{\pi_{X}\otimes\outerproduct{z}{z}}=\lim_{n\to\infty}\left[\frac{1}{n}H^{\mathcal{K}^{(n)}}_{0,\max}(X^{n}|Z^{n})\right]\ ,

What the above tells us is there is an interesting thing that happens. In proper quantum information theory it is often sufficient to restrict ourselves to Sandwiched Rényi divergences, which include

because we can approximate

In case it is not clear, we convince ourselves the reason this worked out is because
H_{0,\max}^{\mathrm{CQ}}
can characterize the optimal zero-error compression for classical data with quantum side information
[
41
]

p_{XY}
where Alice (sender) has
X
and Bob (receiver) has

, he only needs to distinguish between

where
\mathcal{X}_{|y}
is the support of

, i.e. the values
X
can take conditioned on
Y=y
.
Then if there is no error, clearly Alice needs an alphabet
\mathcal{Z}
such that
|\mathcal{Z}|=\max_{y}|\mathcal{X}_{|y}|
so long as they have a pre-agreed upon codebook. Then Alice only needs to send
\log|\mathcal{Z}|
bits. So we verify that this measure captures that. As explained, the optimizer is
\Pi_{p_{XY}}
if one writes

as a density matrix, because for a CQ state,
H_{0,\max}^{\mathrm{CQ}}=H_{0,\max}
. Then a direct calculation determines:

\displaystyle\Pi_{p_{XY}}=

\displaystyle\sum_{x,y}\mathds{1}\{p(x,y)\geq 0\}E_{x,x}\otimes E_{y,y}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{y}E_{y,y}\otimes\sum_{x}\mathds{1}\{p(x,y)\geq 0\}E_{x,x}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{y}E_{y,y}\otimes|\mathcal{X}_{|y}|E_{x,x}\ ,

where

is the indicator function for event

\|\Tr_{X}(\Pi_{p_{XY}})\|_{\infty}=\|\sum_{y}|\mathcal{X}_{|y}|E_{y,y}\|_{\infty}=\max_{y}|\mathcal{X}_{|y}|\ ,

which is exactly what we wanted.

Generalizing to Other Measures

To fully justify the claim that a framework for classical-quantum states only requires the CQ or separable cone, we briefly touch on other useful entropic measures in the restricted framework.

The information spectrum method of Han and Verdú
[
42
,
43
]
is a common tool for classical information theory and was generalized to the quantum setting in
[
28
]
. Much like the quantum Hartley entropy, it is defined in terms of a projector onto a positive eigenspace, albeit a more elaborate one. Given
\rho\in\mathrm{D}_{\leq}(A)
,
Q\in\mathrm{Pos}(A)
, the standard
\varepsilon
-information spectrum
[
28
]
is defined as

D^{\varepsilon}_{s}(\rho||Q):=\sup\{\gamma\in\mathbb{R}\,:\,\langle\rho,\{\rho\preceq 2^{\gamma}Q\}\rangle\leq\varepsilon\}\ ,

where
\{\rho\preceq 2^{\gamma}Q\}
is the projector onto the positive eigenspace of
(2^{\gamma}Q-\rho)
. Note in general this is distinct from the projector onto its support which is why it has distinct notation.

To extend this to generalized cones, we follow what we did for the Hartley entropy and define the set of projectors contained in the cone that also dominate this relevant projector:

\displaystyle\{\Pi^{\mathcal{K}}\succeq\{\rho\preceq 2^{\gamma}Q\}\}:=\{\Pi\in\mathrm{Proj}\,\cap\,\mathcal{K}:\Pi\succeq\{\rho\preceq 2^{\gamma}Q\}\}\ .

The
\varepsilon,\mathcal{K}
-information spectrum divergence may then be defined as

\displaystyle D_{s}^{\varepsilon,\mathcal{K}}(\rho||Q)=\sup\{\gamma\in\mathbb{R}|\underset{\Pi\in\{\Pi^{\mathcal{K}}\succeq\{\rho\preceq 2^{\gamma}Q\}\}}{\min}\langle\rho,\Pi\rangle\leq\varepsilon\}\ .

This is a min-max and so clearly is not particularly feasible to deal with in general. Just as for the Hartley entropy (Proposition
25
), in the case
\mathcal{K}=\mathrm{Pos}
, the optimal
\Pi
is the projector we want to recover as by definition of the set we are minimizing over, any other projector must be of the form
X+\{2^{\gamma}\sigma\succeq\rho\}
where
X\geq 0
, so we have

\displaystyle\langle\rho,X+\{2^{\gamma}\sigma\succeq\rho\}\rangle=\langle\rho,\{2^{\gamma}\sigma\succeq\rho\}\rangle+\langle\rho,X\rangle\geq\langle\rho,\{2^{\gamma}\sigma\succeq\rho\}\rangle\ .

By the same argument as above, if
\mathcal{K}=\mathrm{Sep}
and
both

\rho
and
Q
are classical-quantum, then the optimizer is the same as in the unrestricted case. Moreover it is easy to show the unrestricted case reduces to the classical notion of information spectrum if
\rho
and
Q
classical. Therefore, the
\varepsilon,\mathrm{Sep}
-information spectrum divergence at least mathematically captures the partially quantum setting.

Given the information spectrum divergence’s close relationship to the hypothesis testing divergence, we note the
\varepsilon,\mathcal{K}
-hypothesis testing divergence may be defined using (
14
) with the additional restriction that
X\in\mathcal{K}
. This restricted entropy was studied in great detail in
[
37
]
. For our focus on arguing the separable cone captures partially quantum information theory, we state the following unsurprising proposition that in this setting an AEP holds for the cone-restricted hypothesis-testing divergence, which is one way of proving the quantum Stein lemma holds for CQ states
with the separable cone.

Proposition 28
.

D^{\varepsilon,\mathrm{Sep}}_{h}(P||Q)=D^{\varepsilon}_{h}(P||Q)
if both
P,Q
are CQ with respect to the same partition. Moreover, in this setting an AEP holds.

Proof.

Following the same idea as in the proof of Corollary
12
, since
P
or
Q
is CQ, without loss of generality the optimal decision function
X
is CQ and thus in the separable cone. Since many copies of a CQ state is still CQ, the AEP will hold.
∎

While one cannot prove that all partially quantum information theory is captured by the separable cone without a, presumably never-ending, exhaustive set of proofs for each operational setting, we have shown that the separable cone is sufficient for the common entropic measures on classical-quantum states and even is able to recover the asymptotic results via AEPs.

As a final remark, we note that for both partially quantum and fully classical information theory, one does not even need the separable cone. Rather, under the assumption that classical information theory has a preferred basis, it is clear that the cone that is incoherent in the preferred bases is sufficient for partially quantum and classical information theory as one would expect. This is ultimately formalized in Theorem

Consider the sets of transformations
\mathrm{T}(A_{0},A_{1})

With all of this established, we can discuss the asymptotic equipartition property. To date, an operational interpretation of

For these purposes we first introduce the notion of entanglement rank for CP maps which interpolate between the well-known space of separable CP maps and arbitrary bipartite CP maps.

Definition 11
.

Theorem 8
.

Consider a sequence of cones
\mathcal{K}^{(n)}((AB)^{\otimes n})

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\left[\frac{1}{n}H^{\mathrm{ext},\mathcal{K},\varepsilon}_{\min}(B|A)_{\textrm{id}_{AB}^{\otimes n}}\right]\neq\lim_{n\to\infty}\left[\frac{1}{n}H^{\mathrm{ext},\varepsilon}_{\min}(B|A)_{\textrm{id}_{AB}^{\otimes n}}\right]

\widehat{D}((AB)^{\otimes n}):=\{(d_{A_{1}}d_{B_{0}})^{-n}\mathbf{J}_{\Theta}^{A^{n}B^{n}}:\Theta\in\mathbb{S}^{\mathcal{K}}\}

Proof.

We focus on the case
\varepsilon=0

35

\displaystyle\frac{1}{n}\left[H_{\min}^{\mathrm{ext},\mathcal{K}}(B^{n}|A^{n})_{\textrm{id}_{AB}^{\otimes n}}\right]

\displaystyle\frac{1}{n}\left[-\log\left((d_{A}d_{B_{0}})^{n}\max_{\rho\in\widehat{D}^{\mathcal{K}}((AB)^{\otimes n})}\langle\rho,\tau_{A_{0}B_{0}}^{\otimes n}\rangle\right)\right]

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle-\log(d_{A}d_{B_{0}})-\frac{1}{n}\log f(n)

It follows if

where

Corollary 18
.

An AEP cannot hold for

Proof.

If
\mathcal{K}^{(n)}=\mathrm{Ent}_{f(n)}

\displaystyle\forall\{\omega^{C^{\prime}}_{i}\}\in\mathrm{D}(C^{\prime}),\hskip 8.53581pt\underset{\mathcal{E}\in\mathrm{CU}^{\mathcal{K}}(C^{\prime},B)}{\max}\sum_{i}\Tr[P_{i}^{B}\otimes\mathcal{E}(\omega_{i}^{C^{\prime}})\tau^{BB^{\prime}}]\geq\sum_{i}\Tr[R^{A}_{i}\otimes\omega_{i}^{C^{\prime}}\tau^{AA^{\prime}}]\ .

\displaystyle\forall\{\omega^{C^{\prime}}_{i}\}\in\mathrm{D}(C^{\prime}),\hskip 14.22636pt\exp(-H_{\min}^{\cK,\uparrow}(B|C')_{\Omega})\geq\exp(-H^{\cK,\uparrow}_{\min}(A|C')_{\Omega})

(51)

is necessary and sufficient. This completes the equivalence of Items 1 and 2. It follows that

\displaystyle\forall\{\omega^{C^{\prime}}_{i}\}\quad H_{\min}^{\mathcal{K}}(B|C^{\prime})_{\Omega}\leq H^{\mathcal{K}}_{\min}(A|C^{\prime})_{\Omega}

is a sufficient condition because it implies (

satisfies data processing for any cone because

11
Norms, Additivity, and Restricted Entropies

At this point we have seen the general framework of conic information theory and its ability to capture different operational tasks as a function of the cone. We have also seen that for cones that in a specific sense don’t asymptotically approximate the positive semidefinite cone, the asymptotic equipartition properties, and the relevant operational interpretations, cease to hold. A remaining question would then be under what conditions do the conic measures behave like the standard (
\mathcal{K}=\mathrm{Pos}
) case. Since we have already seen that any pure state outside of the cone will result in
D_{\max}^{\mathcal{K}}
behaving differently than
D_{\max}
(Proposition
13
), the strongest result we could hope for is that
P,Q\in\mathcal{K}
,
D_{\max}^{\mathcal{K}}(P||Q)=D_{\max}(P||Q)
.

Even less demanding, we might hope that
D_{\max}^{\mathcal{K}}(P||Q)
is additive. The additive property is not straightforward in general. This can be seen as follows: Consider the ‘natural’ sequence of cones for the
n-
fold space, in the symmetry case where
\mathcal{K}^{(1)}=\{X\in\mathrm{Pos}:\mathcal{G}(X)=X\}
where
\mathcal{G}
is the twirling map, then
\mathcal{K}^{(n)}=\{X\in\mathrm{Pos}:\mathcal{G}^{\otimes n}(X)=X\}
, and in the entanglement rank cone case
\mathcal{K}^{(1)}=\mathrm{Ent}_{k}(A:B)
, so
\mathcal{K}^{(n)}=\mathrm{Ent}_{n\cdot k}(A^{n}:B^{n})
. In both cases it is clear that the primal problems for
\exp(D^{\cK}_{\max}(P||Q))
and
\exp(-H^{\cK}_{\min}(A|B))
(Eqns. (
18
) and (
22
) respectively) are multiplicative by taking the
n-
fold optimizer. This implies
D_{\max}^{\mathcal{K}}(P^{\otimes n}||Q^{\otimes n})\geq nD_{\max}(P||Q)
,
H_{\min}^{\mathcal{K}}(A^{n}|B^{n})_{P^{\otimes n}}\geq nH_{\min}^{\mathcal{K}}(A|B)_{P}
. When
\mathcal{K}=\mathrm{Pos}
, these are known to hold with equality because one can use the
n-
fold dual optimizer as well
[
20
]
. However, it is known for a general
P
,
\exp(-H^{\Sep}_{\min}(A|B)_{P})
can have the optimizer change. This results in the communication value
\text{cv}^{\mathrm{Sep}}
being non-multiplicative
[
16
]
.

In this section, we explore both of these questions by connecting the conic program framework to conic norms. We see that in effect for cones that capture symmetries, all of the properties hold so long as
P,Q\in\mathcal{K}
. In contrast, for entanglement cones we can prove additivity of
D_{\max}^{\mathrm{Ent}_{k}}
under significantly restricted conditions on
Q
.

Definition 15
.

Let
X\in\mathrm{L}(A)
. Let
\mathcal{K}\subset\mathrm{Pos}
be a closed, convex cone such that
\mathrm{span}(\mathcal{K})=\mathrm{L}(A)
. Then the
\mathcal{K}
-operator norm is

\displaystyle\|X\|_{\mathcal{K}}:=\max_{\rho\in\mathcal{K}}\{|\langle X,\rho\rangle|:\Tr(\rho)=1\}\ ,

where the maximization follows from
\mathcal{K}
being closed and convex guarantees the
\rho\in\mathrm{D}(A)\cap\mathcal{K}
being a compact set.

We note these norms were studied in
[
45
,
47
]
as well as in a sense in
[
17
]
. It is easy to see that in the proof of Proposition
13
, we were in fact converting problems about
\mathcal{K}
-restricted entropic quantities into a problem of
\mathcal{K}
-norms. We now explain why this is the case.

Proposition 31
.

(
[
45
, Proposition 5.2.4]
)
Let
X\in\mathrm{Pos}(A)
. Then
\gamma I^{A}-X\in\mathcal{K}^{\ast}
if and only if
\|X\|_{\mathcal{K}}\leq\gamma
.

Proof.

By definition of dual cone,
\gamma I^{A}-X\in\mathcal{K}^{\ast}
if and only if
\langle\gamma I^{A}-X,K\rangle\geq 0
for all
K\in\mathcal{K}
. Note since it is a cone we can restrict just to
K\in\mathcal{K}
such that
\Tr[K]=1
, so it is if and only if
\gamma-\langle X,\rho\rangle
for all
\rho\in\mathcal{K}\cap\mathrm{D}(A)
, which is true if and only if
\gamma\geq\|X\|_{\mathcal{K}}
.
∎

From this it is straightforward to show the conic norms satisfy the following conic program
[
45
]
.

Proposition 32

(
[
45
]
)
.

For
X\in Pos(A)
,
\|X\|_{\mathcal{K}}
is given by

\displaystyle\emph{Primal:}

\displaystyle\hskip 2.84526pt\max\{\langle\rho,X\rangle\;:\;\Tr(\rho)\leq 1\;\&\;X\in\mathcal{K}\}

(52)

\displaystyle\emph{Dual:}

\displaystyle\hskip 2.84526pt\min\{\gamma:\gamma I\geq Y+X\;\&\;Y\in\mathcal{K}^{\ast}\}

(53)

It is worth remarking for a general cone
\mathcal{K}
it is not clear that it would be multiplicative over tensor products, unlike the standard operator norm. In particular, for
\mathcal{K}=\mathrm{Ent}_{k}
, these are known as the Operator Schmidt Norms
[

]
, denoted
\|\cdot\|_{S(k)}
. For consistency with the literature, we will use this wording throughout this sections. Moreover, in the case that
\mathcal{K}=\mathrm{Sep}(A:B)
, the primal problem given above is in fact the intermediary term
\Lambda^{2}(X)
where the geometric measure of entanglement (GME) is given by
G(X)=-\log(\Lambda^{2}(X))
. It is well known that
G(X)
is not in general additive, or equivalently that
\Lambda^{2}(X)
is not in general multiplicative, over tensor products. It also follows that one might expect various properties known to hold for the GME might generalize for the operator schmidt norms. Indeed, we can obtain a significant generalization of the additivity of GME for states that are elementwise non-negative in the computational basis from
[

]

Theorem 12
.

Let
\rho\in\mathrm{D}(A_{1}\otimes B_{1})
be elementwise non-negative (in the computational basis). Let
\sigma\in\mathrm{D}(A_{2}\otimes B_{2})
be arbitrary. Then for any

\|\rho\otimes\sigma\|_{\mathrm{Ent}_{r}(A^{2}_{1}:B^{2}_{1})}=\|\rho\|_{\mathrm{Sep}(A_{1}:B_{1})}\|\sigma\|_{\mathrm{Ent}_{r}(A_{2}:B_{2})}\ .

The proof is contained in the Appendix. We note that in the original work
[
48
]
, they in effect consider a generalized set of norms that also considers multipartite entanglement. As we are focused on bipartite entanglement throughout, we have don’t provide such a generalization, though for the right choice of cone it ought to be straightforward.

It is then also clear by (
19
) that
D_{\max}^{\mathcal{K}}(P^{A}||I^{A})=\log\|P\|_{\mathcal{K}}
for
P\in\mathrm{Pos}(A)
. It follows that our choice of
\pi
was fortuitous to make use of this. More generally, we have the following proposition to simplify calculations.

Proposition 33
.

Let
\mathcal{K}\subset\mathrm{Pos}(A)
be closed and convex. Let
P,Q\in\mathrm{Pos}(A)
. Consider the CP map
\Phi(\cdot):=Q^{-1/2}\cdot Q^{-1/2}
where
{\cdot}^{-1/2}
is the Moore-Penrose psuedo-inverse. If
\Phi:\mathcal{K}(A)\to\mathcal{K}(A)
, then
D_{\max}^{\mathcal{K}}(P||Q)=\log\|Q^{-1/2}PQ^{-1/2}\|_{\mathcal{K}}
.

Proof.

Note
\Phi
is self-dual. Therefore, if
\Phi:\mathcal{K}\to\mathcal{K}
, then
\Phi^{\ast}:\mathcal{K}\to\mathcal{K}
. As such, if
2^{\gamma}Q-P\in\mathcal{K}
, then
\mathcal{K}^{\ast}\ni\Phi(\gamma Q-P)=2^{\gamma}I-Q^{-1/2}PQ^{-1/2}
. By minimizing over
\gamma
, one obtains
\log\|Q^{-1/2}PQ^{-1/2}\|_{\mathcal{K}}
.
∎

We note in the case that
\mathcal{K}=\mathrm{Pos}
, this is a well known result where it is always the case as
Q^{-1/2}\cdot Q^{-1/2}
is completely positive and
\|\cdot\|_{\mathrm{Pos}}=\|\cdot\|_{\infty}
. The difficulty is when
\mathcal{K}\subsetneq\mathrm{Pos}
as the entanglement properties of neither
Q^{-1/2}
nor
Q^{-1/2}PQ^{-1/2}
are straightforward. Regardless, it allows us to see certain cases where the problem is (more) tractable.

Cones Pertaining to (Abelian) Symmetry

The first case shows that if the relevant property and cone is a symmetry, then we recover everything for which we could hope. In effect, this is not surprising as satisfying a symmetry implies commuting operators, but it does provide evidence it could be the case more generally.

Theorem 13
.

Let
\mathcal{K}\subseteq\mathrm{Pos}(A)
such that
X\in\mathcal{K}
if and only if
\mathcal{G}(X)=X
where
\mathcal{G}
is a twirling map. Let
P,Q\in\mathcal{K}
. Then

1.

D_{\max}^{\mathcal{K}}(P||Q)=D_{\max}(P||Q)
.

2.

If
\mathcal{K}^{(n)}(A^{\otimes n}):=\{X\in\mathrm{Pos}(A^{\otimes n}):\mathcal{G}^{\otimes n}(X)=X\}
,
D_{\max}^{\mathcal{K}^{(n)}}(P^{\otimes n}||Q^{\otimes n})=nD_{\max}(P||Q)

3.

(AEP) For
\varepsilon\in(0,1)
,

\lim_{n\to\infty}\left[\frac{1}{n}D_{\max}^{\mathcal{K},\varepsilon}(P^{\otimes n}||Q^{\otimes n})\right]=D(P||Q)\ .

4.

If
A=A\otimes B
and the cone is defined by a local symmetry, then

H_{\min}^{\mathcal{K}}(A|B)_{P}=H_{\min}(\mathcal{K})
and for
\varepsilon\in(0,1),

\lim_{n\to\infty}\left[\frac{1}{n}H^{\mathcal{K},\varepsilon}_{\min}(A^{n}|B^{n})_{P}\right]=H(A|B)_{P}\ .

Proof.

Any
X\in\mathcal{K}
may be written as
X=\sum_{k}\alpha_{k}\Pi_{k}
where
\{\Pi_{k}\}_{k}
are the minimal projectors, which are the extreme points of the Hermitian matrices under the twirling. It’s then clear that
Q^{-1/2}\cdot Q^{-1/2}:\mathcal{K}\to\mathcal{K}
. Moreover, the maximum eigenvalue of
Q^{-1/2}PQ^{-1/2}
will be an eigenvector of one of the projectors. Therefore,
\|Q^{-1/2}PQ^{-1/2}\|_{\mathcal{K}}=\|Q^{-1/2}PQ^{-1/2}\|_{\infty}
. This completes the first item. The second item is just noting the
n-
fold case uses the
n-
fold minimal projectors. Lastly, the third item is as follows. Recall
D_{\max}^{\mathcal{K},\varepsilon}(\rho||\sigma)=\underset{\widetilde{\rho}\in\mathscr{B}^{\varepsilon}(\rho)}{\min}D^{\mathcal{K}}_{\max}(\widetilde{\rho}||\sigma)
. We can convert
P,Q
to density matrices by the normalization property (Prop.
9
). Then note
\mathcal{G}^{\otimes n}(\widetilde{\rho})\in\mathscr{B}^{\varepsilon}(\rho^{\otimes n})
by data processing. By data-processing (Proposition
22
), we can conclude the optimal for smoothed is in this twirled set for each
n
both in
\mathcal{K}^{(n)}
and
\mathcal{K}^{(n)}=\mathrm{Pos}
, so they must be the same by Item 1. As the regularized smoothed max-relative entropy converges to
D(\rho||\sigma)

[
2
]
, this completes the proof of Item 3. Item 4 follows from Corollary
12
.
∎

Entanglement Rank Cones - Operator Schmidt Norms

In contrast to the previous setting, for the entanglement rank cones

whose norms are referred to as the operator Schmidt norms and denoted as

-norms in
[

, nothing is well behaved. The first issue is that the structure to guarantee a CP map

Proposition 34
.

Let
P\in\mathrm{Pos}(A\otimes B)
. Let

. Then for

,

D_{\max}^{\mathrm{Ent}_{kn}}(P^{\otimes n}||Q^{\otimes n})=\log\|(Q^{-1/2}PQ^{-1/2})^{\otimes n}\|_{S(kn)}\ .

Proof.

If
\rank(Q)=k
, then
\rank({Q^{\otimes n}}^{-1/2})=nk
. Therefore, by Proposition

{Q^{\otimes n}}^{-1/2}P^{\otimes n}{Q^{\otimes n}}^{-1/2}=(Q^{-1/2}PQ^{-1/2})^{\otimes n}\in\mathrm{Ent}_{kn}(A:B)

∎

First note that for the above proof to be non-trivial,
P\ll Q
, which means
\rank(P)\leq k
as well. Therefore, one can see this is quite restrictive, though one might expect it is significantly more general if

were to generally imply

. Beyond this, note we have not been able to prove additivity. This is because we would need, for the

in (

{Y^{\star}}^{\otimes 2}-Y^{\star}\otimes X-X\otimes Y^{\star}\in\mathcal{K}^{\ast}

. This seems unlikely in general. We can however prove additivity holds in the special case of
\mathcal{K}=\mathrm{PPT}

Proposition 35
.

D^{\mathrm{PPT}}_{\max}(P||Q)
is given by
\log(\alpha)
where
\alpha
is given by

Primal

\displaystyle\text{maximize:}

\displaystyle\langle P,X\rangle

(54)

\displaystyle\text{subject to:}

\displaystyle\langle Q,X\rangle\leq 1

\displaystyle X,X^{\Gamma}\in\mathrm{Pos}(A)\ ,

Dual

\displaystyle\text{minimize:}

\displaystyle\gamma

(55)

\displaystyle\text{subject to:}

\displaystyle\gamma Q\geq P+Y^{\Gamma}

\displaystyle Y\geq 0\ ,

where

is the partial transpose map.

We refer the reader to the Appendix for the derivation. This then leads to additivity result in this special case.

Theorem 14
.

Let
P,P^{\prime},Q,Q^{\prime}\in\mathrm{PPT}(A:B)
. Then,

D_{\max}^{\mathrm{PPT}}(P\otimes P^{\prime}||Q\otimes Q^{\prime})=D_{\max}^{\mathrm{PPT}}(P||Q)+D_{\max}^{\mathrm{PPT}}(P^{\prime}||Q^{\prime})\ .

Proof.

Let
\alpha,\gamma,X,(\gamma,Y)
and
\alpha^{\prime},X^{\prime},(\gamma^{\prime},Y^{\prime})
be the optimal value, primal optimizer, and dual optimizer for

and

respectively. Then
\langle Q\otimes Q^{\prime},X\otimes X^{\prime}\rangle=1
and
\langle P\otimes P^{\prime},X\otimes X^{\prime}\rangle=\alpha\cdot\alpha^{\prime}
. Note this SDP has strong duality (given we proved the equivalent conic program did earlier), so it suffices to prove we can get the same optimal value for the dual program.
Now note

\displaystyle\gamma Q\otimes\gamma^{\prime}Q^{\prime}\succeq

\displaystyle(P+Y^{\Gamma})\otimes(P^{\prime}+Y^{\prime\Gamma})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle P\otimes P^{\prime}+P\otimes Y^{\prime\Gamma}+Y^{\Gamma}\otimes P^{\prime}+Y^{\Gamma}\otimes Y^{\prime\Gamma}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle P\otimes P^{\prime}+\Gamma^{BB^{\prime}}(\overline{P}\otimes\overline{P^{\prime}}+\overline{P}\otimes Y^{\prime}+Y\otimes\overline{P^{\prime}}+Y\otimes Y^{\prime})\ ,

where the inequality is by assumption of them being optimizers and the last equality is by defining

and using if you have fixed bases of

for PPT, then the basis for
BB^{\prime}

\Gamma^{BB^{\prime}}

\gamma\cdot\gamma^{\prime}
is a dual optimal value. As

, we have proven the SDP is multiplicative and so

is additive (as it is the

of the SDP).
∎

11.1
Special Case: Werner States

As the operator Schmidt norms are difficult to handle in general, in this section we investigate how they behave for a class of operators that are manageable: the Werner states.

The Werner states of fixed local dimension
d
may be parameterized in the following two ways

\displaystyle\rho_{\lambda}

\displaystyle:=\frac{2\lambda}{d(d+1)}\Pi_{+}+\frac{2(1-\lambda)}{d(d-1)}\Pi_{-}\,,\lambda\in[0,1]

(56)

\displaystyle\rho_{\alpha}

\displaystyle:=\frac{1}{d(d-\alpha)}(I^{AB}-\alpha\mathbb{F})\,,\alpha\in[-1,1]\ ,

(57)

where
\Pi_{+},\Pi_{-}
are the symmetric and anti-symmetric projectors respectively,
\mathbb{F}
is the swap operator, and they may be related by
\Pi_{+}=\frac{1}{2}(I^{AB}+\mathbb{F})
. The two parameterizations may be related by the conversions

\displaystyle\alpha=\frac{(1-2\lambda)d+1}{1-2\lambda+d}\quad\quad\lambda=\frac{(1+d)(1-\alpha)}{2(d-\alpha)}\ .

(58)

These are known to be entangled for
\lambda\in[0,1/2)
, or equivalently
\alpha\in(1/d,1]
.

As one might expect from their ability to be decomposed into symmetric and asymmetric projectors, these are the states that invariant under the local symmetry
\mathcal{U}(\cdot):=\int(U\otimes U)\cdot(U\otimes U)^{\ast}\,dU
where
dU
is the Haar measure. This is often referred to as being
UU
-invariant. These states are known to be excellent for admitting non-additivity and non-multiplicativity results. In
[
48
]
it was shown their geometric measure of entanglement is super-multiplicative for
n=2
and the same was shown for the communication value,
\text{cv}^{\mathrm{Sep}}
, of the Werner-Holevo channels
\mathcal{W}_{\lambda}
whose Choi operators are given by
d\rho_{\lambda}
in
[
16
]
. This is equivalent to the non-additivity of
H_{\min}^{\mathrm{Sep}}
for the Choi operators of the Werner-Holevo channels by (
10
). Here we expand on these properties.

First we show the non-multiplicativity of the Werner-Holevo channels for
\text{cv}^{\mathrm{Sep}}
is in fact a property of the non-additivity of the non-multiplicativity of the
S(1)
norm.

Proposition 37
.

Let
k,n\in\mathbb{N}
and
\mathcal{K}=\mathrm{Ent}_{k}(A^{n}:B^{n})
. Then

\displaystyle H_{\min}^{\mathcal{K}}(A^{n}|B^{n})_{\rho^{\otimes n}_{\alpha}}=

\displaystyle-D^{\mathcal{K}}_{\max}(\rho_{\alpha}^{\otimes n}||I_{A^{n}}\otimes\pi_{B^{n}})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\log\|\rho_{\alpha}^{\otimes n}\|_{\mathrm{Ent}_{k}(A^{n}:B^{n})}+n\log(d)\ .

Proof.

As
\mathrm{Ent}_{k}(A^{n}:B^{n})
is invariant under local maps, by the data-processing inequality (
22
),

\displaystyle D_{\max}^{\mathrm{Ent}_{k}}(\rho^{\otimes n}_{\alpha}||I\otimes\sigma)\geq

\displaystyle D_{\max}^{\mathrm{Ent}_{k}}(\mathcal{U}^{\otimes n}(\rho^{\otimes n}_{\alpha})||\,\mathcal{U}^{\otimes n}(I\otimes\sigma))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle D^{\mathrm{Ent}_{k}}_{\max}(\rho_{\alpha}^{\otimes n}||I\otimes\int U\sigma U^{\ast}\,dU)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle D^{\mathrm{Ent}_{k}}_{\max}(\rho_{\alpha}^{\otimes n}||I\otimes\pi)\ ,

where the
\sigma
was arbitrary. Recalling that

H^{\mathrm{Ent}_{k}}_{\min}(A^{n}|B^{n})_{\rho_{\alpha}^{\otimes n}}=\max_{\sigma\in\mathrm{D}(B^{n})}-D_{\max}^{\mathrm{Ent}_{k}}(\rho^{\otimes n}_{\alpha}||I\otimes\sigma)\ ,

it follows that

H^{\mathrm{Ent}_{k}}_{\min}(A^{n}|B^{n})_{\rho_{\alpha}^{\otimes n}}=-D^{\mathrm{Ent}_{k}}_{\max}(\rho^{\otimes n}_{\alpha}||I\otimes\pi)\ .

Using that
I\otimes\pi=\frac{1}{d^{n}}(I\otimes I)
along with the normalization property (Proposition
9
) along with
D_{\max}^{\mathcal{K}}(X||I)=\|X\|_{\mathcal{K}}
gets the second equality.
∎

This simplifies things as all we need to do is determine
\|\rho^{\otimes n}_{\alpha}\|_{\mathrm{Ent}_{k}(A^{n}:B^{n})}
. In general, this is not known. However, it is known that the space of operators that are
UU-
invariant is the same as the space of PPT operators with the same invariance
[
40
]
. Therefore, using the primal problem (
52
) we ultimately obtain the following linear program:

Proposition 38
.

\|\rho_{\lambda}^{\otimes 2}\|_{\mathrm{Ent}_{1}(A^{2}_{1}:B^{2}_{1})}
is given by the linear program:

\displaystyle\max.\;

\displaystyle\;\lambda^{2}w+\lambda(1-\lambda)(x+y)+(1-\lambda)^{2}z

\displaystyle\mathrm{s.t.}\;

\displaystyle\;d_{+}(w+y)\geq d_{-}(x+z)

\displaystyle\;d_{+}(w+x)\geq d_{-}(y+z)

\displaystyle\;d_{+}^{2}w+d_{-}^{2}z\geq d_{+}d_{-}(x+y)

\displaystyle\;\left[d_{+}^{2}w+d_{+}d_{-}(x+y)+d_{-}^{2}z\right]=\frac{4}{d^{2}}

\displaystyle\;w,x,y,z\geq 0\ ,

where
d_{+}\equiv d+1
,
d_{-}\equiv d-1
, and we note it can be reparameterized to
\rho_{\alpha}
using (

Something in effect equivalent was derived in

]

that the Werner states have a simple expression for their entanglement rank norm:

\|\rho_{\alpha}\|_{\mathrm{Ent}_{k}(A:B)}=\begin{cases}\frac{1+\min\{\alpha,0\}}{d(d-\alpha)}&k=1\\
\frac{1+|\alpha|}{d(d-\alpha)}&2\leq k\leq n\ .\end{cases}

(59)

Combining these two propositions, we are able to plot tight bounds of the non-multiplicativity of the

Conclusion

In this work we have investigated the general structure of one-shot information theory measures when we consider the optimization programs for cones other than the positive semidefinite cone. In doing so, we have even further cemented the importance of the positive semidefinite cone and the Löwner order. We have done this by showing that for restricted cones the asymptotic equipartition properties cease to hold and this effects what tasks they can properly capture, e.g. failing to capture hypothesis testing or successfully capturing minimal-error decoding. Moreover, we have captured this breakdown in terms of resources, in particular coherence and entanglement. We have also shown the separable cone, the minimally cone invariant under local unitaries, is sufficent for information theory over CQ states. This exemplifies the strong distinction between fully quantum and partially quantum tasks that has been observed in the literature.

After we explored the measures behaviour with respect to recovering i.i.d. limits, we established operational interpretations of the cone restricted entropic quantities. First, we established that the restricted min-entropy captures a notion of entanglement-assisted classical communication over a quantum channel under restricted measurements. Second, we introduced a further restricted min-entropic-like quantity that captures the ability to convert one channel to another via pre-processing. This also exemplifies how other majorization results may be generalized to restricted classes of transformations.

Finally, we introduced the relationship between restricted max-relative entropy and the generalized cone operator norm. In doing so, we showed for an Abelian symmetry, the cone restricted to said Abelian symmetry behaves like the max-relative entropy on states that satisfy the symmetry. We showed for entanglement rank cones, the same property is unclear. It would be interesting and possibly enlightening to know if such a property does hold or not for these entanglement rank cones and why.

One further way to extend this work would be to extend it into the category-theoretic language of
[
17
]
. It is possible further generality and refinements could be achieved in that framework. Moreover, as noted in the main text,
[
17
]
only considered
H^{\mathcal{K}}_{\min}(B|A)
, so extending
H_{\min}^{\mathcal{K}}(A|B)
into a category-theoretic framework would be new in and of itself.

Beyond this, we note a tangential avenue of research pertaining to superchannels given this work. First, we introduced the flipped extended min-entropy
H^{\mathrm{ext}}_{\min}(A|B)
, but we did not present an operational interpretation beyond it’s generalization of singlet fraction. It could be interesting to establish one. We also proved an anti-standard AEP for both forms of extended min-entropy, but what asymptotic measure an extended min-entropy AEP would converge to has not yet been established. Establishing this would also be of interest. Lastly, in proving our anti-standard AEP for extended min-entropy, we established the notion of
k-
entanglement-breaking supermaps. It is likely that there is more to be said about this general class of supermaps.

B_{k}=\sum_{i^{\prime},j^{\prime}}\beta_{i^{\prime},j^{\prime}}^{k}E_{i^{\prime},j^{\prime}}
, where we will drop the
k
index for simplicity.
Then, we see

\displaystyle\mathrm{vec}(B^{\ast}_{k})\mathrm{vec}(A_{k}^{\ast})^{\ast}=

\displaystyle\sum_{i,j,i^{\prime},j^{\prime}}\overline{\beta}_{i^{\prime},j^{\prime}}\alpha_{i,j}E_{j^{\prime},j}\otimes E_{i^{\prime},i}

\displaystyle\xrightarrow{SWAP}

\displaystyle\sum_{i,j,i^{\prime},j^{\prime}}\alpha_{i,j}\overline{\beta}_{i^{\prime},j^{\prime}}E_{i^{\prime},i}\otimes E_{j^{\prime},j}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\sum_{i,j,i^{\prime},j^{\prime}}\alpha_{i,j}\overline{\beta}_{i^{\prime},j^{\prime}}E_{i,i^{\prime}}\otimes E_{j,j^{\prime}}\right)^{\mathrm{T}}=\left(\mathrm{vec}(A_{k})\mathrm{vec}(B_{k})^{\ast}\right)^{\mathrm{T}}\ .

By linearity of the SWAP and transpose, summing over
k
completes the proof.
∎

Proof of Theorem
12
.

First note that for arbitrary
X\in\mathrm{Herm}(A\otimes B)
,

\displaystyle\|X\|_{\mathrm{Ent}_{k}}=\sup\{|\bra{w}X\ket{v}|:\mathrm{SR}(\ket{w}),\mathrm{SR}(\ket{v})\leq k\}\ ,

which we will make use of later in the proof.
Now let
\rho\in\mathrm{D}(A_{1}\otimes B_{1})
be non-negative (in the computational basis). Let
\sigma\in\mathrm{D}(A_{2}\otimes B_{2})
be arbitrary. It follows
\rho\otimes\sigma\in\mathrm{Pos}(A^{2}_{1}\otimes B^{2}_{1})
. Therefore, by the primal problem for the conic norm of a positive semidefinite operator (
52
) and that a convex optimization problem is maximized by an extreme point of the set being optimized over, without loss of generality, let
\ket{\psi^{\star}}
be the optimizer of
\|\rho\otimes\sigma\|_{\mathrm{Ent}_{r}(A^{2}_{1}:B^{2}_{1})}
. It follows
\mathrm{SR}(\ket{\psi^{\star}})\leq r
. It follows,

\displaystyle\ket{\psi^{\star}}=\sum_{i\in[r]}\alpha_{i}\ket{\varphi_{i}}_{A^{2}_{1}}\otimes\ket{\wt{\varphi}_{i}}_{B^{2}_{1}}=\sum_{j,k}a_{j}b_{k}\ket{j,k}_{A^{2}_{1}}\otimes\left(\sum_{i\in[r]}\alpha_{i}\ket{\psi^{i}_{j}}_{B_{1}}\ket{\phi^{i}_{k}}_{B_{2}}\right)\ ,

where
a_{j},b_{k}\geq 0
for all of their indices and
\sum_{i}|\alpha_{i}|^{2}=\sum_{j}a_{j}^{2}=\sum_{k}b_{k}^{2}=1
and
\ket{\psi^{i}_{j}},\ket{\phi^{i}_{k}}
are normalized states. In the first line we have used the definition of Schmidt rank to decompose the state into product states, and in the second we have decomposed the halves of the product states so that the
A
spaces are expressed in the computational basis and the coefficients are all real by absorbing any phase into the definitions of
\ket{\psi^{i}_{j}},\ket{\phi^{i}_{k}}
.

Using this decomposition, we obtain

\displaystyle\|\rho\otimes\sigma\|_{\mathrm{Ent}_{r}}=

\displaystyle\bra{\psi^{\star}}\rho\otimes\sigma\ket{\psi^{\star}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Big|\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}j,k,\overline{j},\overline{k}\end{subarray}}a_{j}b_{k}a_{\overline{j}}b_{\overline{j}}\bra{j,k}\rho\ket{\ol{j},\ol{k}}\cdot\sum_{i,\overline{i}\in[r]}\overline{\alpha}_{i}\alpha_{\overline{i}}\bra{\psi^{i}_{j}}\bra{\phi^{i}_{k}}\sigma\ket{\psi^{\ol{i}}_{\ol{j}}}\ket{\phi^{\ol{i}}_{\ol{k}}}\Big|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}j,k,\overline{j},\overline{k}\end{subarray}}a_{j}b_{k}a_{\overline{j}}b_{\overline{j}}\bra{j,k}\rho\ket{\ol{j},\ol{k}}\cdot\Big|\sum_{i,\overline{i}\in[r]}\overline{\alpha}_{i}\alpha_{\overline{i}}\bra{\psi^{i}_{j}}\bra{\phi^{i}_{k}}\sigma\ket{\psi^{\ol{i}}_{\ol{j}}}\ket{\phi^{\ol{i}}_{\ol{k}}}\Big|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}j,k,\overline{j},\overline{k}\end{subarray}}a_{j}b_{k}a_{\overline{j}}b_{\overline{j}}\bra{j,k}\rho\ket{\ol{j},\ol{k}}\right)\|\sigma\|_{\mathrm{Ent}_{r}(A_{2}:B_{2})}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\|\rho\|_{\mathrm{Ent}_{1}(A_{1}:B_{1})}\|\sigma\|_{\mathrm{Ent}_{r}(A_{2}:B_{2})}\ ,

where the third equality uses that
\rho
is non-negative, the first inequality is noting that
\sum_{i\in[r]}\alpha_{i}\ket{\psi^{i}_{j}}\ket{\phi^{i}_{k}}
is a quantum state with Schmidt rank
\leq r
, so by the
\|\cdot\|_{\mathrm{Ent}_{r}}
for Hermitian matrices, it’s upper bounded by the norm. The last inequality is noting that
v:=\sum_{j,k}a_{j}b_{k}\bra{j,k}
is also a separable quantum state, so by the conic program for the norm, we have the bound.

Moreover, note

\displaystyle\|\rho\otimes\sigma\|_{\mathrm{Ent}_{r}(A^{2}_{1}:B^{2}_{1})}\geq

\displaystyle\|\rho\|_{\mathrm{Ent}_{r}(A_{1}:B_{1})}\|\sigma\|_{\mathrm{Ent}_{r}(A_{2}:B_{2})}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\|\rho\|_{\mathrm{Ent}_{1}(A_{1}:B_{1})}\|\sigma\|_{\mathrm{Ent}_{r}(A_{2}:B_{2})}\ ,

where the first inequality is because from the primal problem (
52
), it’s clear the entanglement rank norm is always at least multiplicative and the second inequality is because the entanglement rank norms monotonically increase in
r
. Combining the two directions of inequalities forces an inequality and completes the proof.
∎

Proof of Proposition

We start from (the equality version of) Eqn. (

Proof of Proposition
38
.

First note by (
52
) (relabeling
\rho
with
\sigma
to avoid confusion),

\langle\rho_{\lambda}^{\otimes 2},\sigma\rangle=\langle\rho_{\lambda}^{\otimes 2},\mathcal{U}^{\otimes 2}(\sigma)\rangle\ ,

where

is the twirling map and it’s a self-adjoint map. As this twirling can be applied without loss of generality, the optimal
\sigma
will be invariant under such twirling. Thus we have

\sigma=w\Pi_{+}^{\otimes 2}+x\Pi_{+}\otimes\Pi_{-}+y\Pi_{-}\otimes\Pi_{+}+z\Pi_{-}^{\otimes 2}\ .

For this to be a positive operator, it follows
w,x,y,z\geq 0
. Moreover, to be unit trace, we require

\left[(d+1)^{2}w+(x+y)(d+1)(d-1)+(d-1)^{2}z\right]=\frac{4}{d^{2}}\ ,

where we have used
\Tr[\Pi_{+}]=\binom{n+1}{2}

\Tr[\Pi_{-}]=\binom{n}{2}

56

\langle\rho_{\lambda}^{\otimes 2},\sigma\rangle\rangle=\lambda^{2}w+\lambda(1-\lambda)(x+y)+(1-\lambda)^{2}z\ .

\displaystyle 4d^{2}\sigma^{\Gamma}=

\displaystyle+\left\{(w+x)(d+1)-(y+z)(d-1)\right\}\phi^{+}\otimes\phi^{\perp}

\displaystyle+\Big\{w(d+1)^{2}+z(d-1)^{2}-(x+y)(d-1)(d+1)\Big\}{\phi^{+}}^{\otimes 2}\ .

Noting these are mutually orthogonal subspaces and that the factor of

is irrelevant for the positivity of these coefficients results, we have four constraints. Noting that

Proposition 44
.

For
\lambda\in[0,1]
, define
\kappa_{\lambda}=\lambda\log\left[\frac{\lambda}{1-\lambda}\frac{d-1}{d+1}\right]+\log\left[\frac{2(1-\lambda)}{d(d-1)}\right]
. Then

\kappa_{\lambda}+\log(d)\geq D(\sigma_{\lambda}||I_{A}\otimes\omega)\geq\kappa_{\lambda}

where the upper bound is saturated if
\omega=\frac{1}{d}I_{B}
, and the lower bound is saturated if
\omega
is pure.

Proof.

First note
D(\sigma_{\lambda}||I_{A}\otimes\omega)=\Tr[\sigma_{\lambda}\log\sigma_{\lambda}]-\Tr[\sigma_{\lambda}I_{A}\otimes\log\omega]
, where we have used that
\log(\I_{A} \otimes P)=\log(I_{A})\otimes P+I_{A}\otimes\log P=I_{A}\otimes\log P
for positive definite
P
. We focus on each term separately.

\displaystyle\Tr[\sigma_{\lambda}\log\sigma_{\lambda}]=

\displaystyle\lambda\alpha_{0}\Tr[\Pi_{0}\log(\lambda\alpha_{0} \Pi_{0})]+(1-\lambda)\alpha_{1}\Tr[\Pi_{1}\log((1-\lambda) \alpha_{1} \Pi_{1})]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lambda\alpha_{0}\log(\lambda\alpha_0)\alpha_{0}^{-1}+(1-\lambda)\alpha_{1}\log((1-\lambda)\alpha_1)\alpha_{1}^{-1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lambda\log(\lambda\alpha_{0})+\log((1-\lambda)\alpha_{1})-\lambda\log((1-\lambda)\alpha_{1})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lambda\log(\frac{\lambda}{1-\lambda} \frac{\alpha_{0}}{\alpha_{1}})+\log((1-\lambda)(\alpha_{1}))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\log\left[\frac{\lambda}{1-\lambda}\frac{d-1}{d+1}\right]+\log\left[\frac{2(1-\lambda)}{d(d-1)}\right]\ ,

where the first equality is just expanding the definition of
\sigma_{\lambda}
and throwing out the terms which have disjoint supports, the second is that its the same projector up to some factor so the trace is that factor times the rank of the projector, the third equality is just cancelling factors, the fourth inequality is combining terms, the fifth equality is simplifying the constants.

Now we consider the second term:

\displaystyle\Tr[\sigma_{\lambda}I_{A}\otimes\log\omega]=

\displaystyle 1/d\Tr[I_{B}\log\omega]=1/d\Tr[\log\omega]\ ,

where we have used the partial trace adjoint and that
\Tr_{A}(\sigma_{\lambda})=\frac{1}{n}I_{B}
for the first equality. Now note

\displaystyle\max_{\omega\in\mathrm{D}(B)}D(\sigma_{\lambda}||I_{A}\otimes\omega)

\displaystyle=\text{term 1}+\frac{1}{d}\max_{\omega\in\mathrm{D}(B)}-\Tr[\log\omega]

\displaystyle=\text{term 1}-\frac{1}{d}\min_{p\in\mathcal{P}(|B|)}\sum_{i}\log(p_{i})\ ,

where that last optimization problem is achieved by
p_{i}=|B|^{-1}\,\forall i
, i.e. the maximally mixed state. Note
-\frac{1}{d}\Tr[\log 1/dI_{B}]=\log d
. Note if we consider minimizing
D
, then we maximize over that last term, which must be non-positive, so the optimal is achieved by
p_{i}=1
for some choice of
i
which is equivalent to
\omega
being pure, which completes the proof.
∎

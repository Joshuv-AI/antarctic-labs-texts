Diabatic Representation of Exotic Hadrons in the Dynamical Diquark Model
Richard F. Lebed, Steven R. Martinez

Abstract
We apply the diabatic formalism, an extension of the adiabatic approximation inherent to the Born-Oppenheimer (BO) approach of atomic physics, to the problem of mixing between exotic multiquark hadrons and their nearby di-hadron thresholds. The unperturbed BO eigenstates are obtained using the dynamical diquark model, while the diabatic calculation introduces a mixing potential between these states and the threshold states. We solve the resulting coupled Schrödinger equations numerically for hidden-charm tetraquarks of both open and closed strangeness to obtain physical mass eigenvalues, and explore the di-hadron state content and spatial extent of the eigenstates. As an explicit example, X ⁡ ( 3872 ) X(3872) emerges with a dominant D 0 ​ D ¯ ∗ 0 D^{0}\bar{D}^{*0} component, but also contains a considerable diquark-antidiquark component that can contribute significantly to its radiative decay widths, and this component also generates a full multiplet of other diquark-based exotic hadrons to be compared with experiment.

One formalism that has predicted a specific spectrum of multiquark

dynamical diquark model

-

rather to persist as components of a single multiquark state
connected by a color flux tube. Since each diquark is a color
triplet containing a heavy quark, the same potentials that are
computed on the lattice to describe heavy quarkonium and its hybrids
can be imported into Schrödinger equations, which are solved

\delta^{\vphantom{1}}{\bar{\delta}}

procedure has been performed both for the multiplet average

;
Giron and Lebed 2020a
;
Giron and Lebed 2020b
;
Giron et al. 2021

{\bar{\delta}}

with a color-triplet triquark
{\bar{\theta}}\!\equiv\![\bar{Q}_{\bar{\bf 3}}(q_{1}q_{2})_{\bar{\bf 3}}]_{\bf 3}

This treatment of obtaining eigenstates of heavy (quasi-)particles
at separation
r
connected by a static potential manifests the
well-known Born-Oppenheimer (BO) approximation
Born and Oppenheimer 1927

from atomic physics. Intrinsic to the approximation is the
assumption that the light degrees of freedom (d.o.f.) of the state
adjust instantly to changes in the configuration
\Gamma
of the
heavy sources (an
adiabatic
approximation), and that the
eigenstates of the potential
V_{\Gamma}(r)
thus derived from each
such configuration
\Gamma
change gradually with
V_{\Gamma}(r)
.
However, when the value of
V_{\Gamma}(r)
crosses the energy of a
di-hadron threshold, the physical eigenstates undergo a rapid level
crossing between a predominantly
\delta^{\vphantom{1}}{\bar{\delta}}
state and a predominantly
di-hadron state. The mixing of configurations induces

diabatic
changes to the system, and solving for its new
eigenstates requires a generalization beyond the strict BO limiting
case.

Such a diabatic formalism has been extensively developed in atomic
physics, and in recent years it has been standardized into textbook
form
Baer 2006
. This approach provides a specific,
nonperturbative method for incorporating the mixing of
coupled-channel contributions from di-hadron states into the
calculations, and we briefly review the relevant formalism below.
The diabatic formalism was first applied to heavy-quark systems quite
recently in Ref.
Bruschini and González 2020
; in that case, the analysis
examined mixing of di-hadron thresholds with conventional
quarkonium, rather than with the 4-quark
\delta^{\vphantom{1}}{\bar{\delta}}
states of the
dynamical diquark model that are used here.

The initial study of Ref.
Bruschini and González 2020
specifically
considers states lying below or just above a di-hadron threshold. In
this work we adopt the same restriction, specifically to study the
lightest hidden-charm exotics of valence quark content

c\bar{c}q\bar{q}^{\prime}
(where
q^{(\prime)}
are
u
or
d

quarks),
c\bar{c}s\bar{s}
, and
c\bar{c}q\bar{s}
. “Lightest” in
this sense means the members of the (positive-parity) ground-state BO
multiplet
\Sigma^{+}_{g}(1S)

corresponding flavor contents include
X(3872)
,
Y(4140)
, and

Z_{cs}(4000)
, respectively. Of course, the same methods can be
applied as well to orbitally excited multiplets such as

\Sigma^{+}_{g}(1P)
[containing,
e.g.
,
Y(4220)
], hidden-bottom
[
e.g.
,
Z_{b}(10610)
] states, and fully charmed

c\bar{c}c\bar{c}

Here we focus on identifying the
\delta^{\vphantom{1}}
-
{\bar{\delta}}
and meson-meson content

III
Diabatic Mixing Formalism

In this work, we begin with the same construction as in the original
dynamical diquark model
Giron et al. 2019
. That is, one separates
the light d.o.f. from the heavy d.o.f. by writing the Hamiltonian
as

H=K_{\rm heavy}+H_{\rm light}=\frac{\mathbf{p}^{2}}{2\mu_{\rm heavy}}+H_{\rm light},

(5)

such that the Schrödinger equation now reads

\left(\frac{\mathbf{p}^{2}}{2\mu_{\rm heavy}}+H_{\rm light}-E\right)|\psi\rangle=0.

(6)

Under the current analysis, “light field” refers to either just the
glue fields (in the case of a
\delta^{\vphantom{1}}
-
{\bar{\delta}}
state) or both glue and
exchanged light-quark fields (in the case of the meson-meson states).

We now implement the Ansatz that the states defined in
Sec.
II
may appreciably mix with nearby meson-meson
thresholds sharing the same
J^{PC}
quantum numbers, but assume that
the two types of states are clearly distinguishable away from the
thresholds. Thus, one must determine and solve the multi-channel
Schrödinger equation connecting the
\delta^{\vphantom{1}}{\bar{\delta}}
states to such
threshold states. We closely follow the work of
Ref.
Bruschini and González 2020
, which carries out this process using
conventional quarkonium rather than
\delta^{\vphantom{1}}{\bar{\delta}}
states. Applying the
diabatic expansion to the eigenstates of said Schrödinger
equation yields
Bruschini and González 2020

|\psi\rangle=\sum_{i}\int d\mathbf{r}^{\prime}\tilde{\psi}_{i}(\mathbf{r}^{\prime},\mathbf{r}_{0})\>|\mathbf{r}^{\prime}\rangle\>|\xi_{i}(\mathbf{r}_{0})\rangle,

(7)

where
\mathbf{r}^{\prime}
denotes the separation of the heavy sources,

\mathbf{r}_{0}
is a freely set fiducial parameter, and

|\xi_{i}\rangle
are eigenstates of the light-field Hamiltonian.
Inserting Eq. (
7
) into Eq. (
6
) and applying

\langle\xi_{j}(\mathbf{r}_{0})|
on the left-hand side produces

\sum_{i}\left[-\frac{\hbar^{2}}{2\mu_{i}}\delta^{\vphantom{1}}_{ij}\nabla^{2}+V_{ji}(\mathbf{r,r_{0}})-E\delta^{\vphantom{1}}_{ji}\right]\tilde{\psi}_{i}(\mathbf{r,r_{0}})=0,

(8)

with the
diabatic potential matrix
defined as

V_{ji}(\mathbf{r,r_{0}})\equiv\langle\xi_{j}(\mathbf{r}_{0})|H_{\rm light}|\xi_{i}(\mathbf{r}_{0})\rangle.

(9)

We identify the
i\!=\!0
term with
\delta^{\vphantom{1}}{\bar{\delta}}
states, and

i\!>\!0
terms with meson-meson states.

This result may be written more compactly in matrix notation as

[\text{K}+\text{V}(\mathbf{r})]\mathbf{\Psi}(\mathbf{r})=\emph{E}\,\mathbf{\Psi}(\mathbf{r})\,,

(10)

with the parameter
\mathbf{r}_{0}
implicit. Neglecting interactions
between distinct meson-meson components, as is done in analogous
lattice-QCD studies
Bulava et al. 2019
, the potential matrix then
becomes

\text{V}=\begin{pmatrix}V_{\delta^{\vphantom{1}}{\bar{\delta}}}(\mathbf{r})&V_{\rm mix}^{(1)}(\mathbf{r})&\cdots&V_{\rm mix\vphantom{\overline{M}_{2}}}^{(N)}(\mathbf{r})\\
V_{\rm mix}^{(1)}(\mathbf{r})&V_{M_{1}\overline{M}_{2}}^{(1)}(\mathbf{r})&&\\
\vdots&&\ddots\\
V_{\rm mix\vphantom{\overline{M}_{2}}}^{(N)}(\mathbf{r})&&&V_{M_{1}\overline{M}_{2}}^{(N)}(\mathbf{r})\\
\end{pmatrix},

(11)

with the kinetic-energy operator expressed as

\text{K}=\begin{pmatrix}-\frac{\hbar^{2}}{2\mu_{\delta^{\vphantom{1}}{\bar{\delta}}}}&&&\\
&-\frac{\hbar^{2}}{2\mu^{(1)}_{M_{1}\overline{M}_{2}}}&&\\
&&\ddots&\\
&&&-\frac{\hbar^{2}}{2\mu^{(N)}_{M_{1}\overline{M}_{2}}}\\
\end{pmatrix}\nabla^{2},

(12)

where omitted elements are zeroes. Inspection of the
i>0
diagonal
elements leads to the identification of

V_{M_{1}\overline{M}_{2}}^{(i)}(\mathbf{r})
as simply being the energy
associated with that of the pure free
i^{\rm th}
meson-meson state.
That is,

V_{M_{1}\overline{M}_{2}}^{(i)}(\mathbf{r})=T_{M_{1}\overline{M}_{2}}\,,

(13)

where

T_{M_{1}\overline{M}_{2}}\equiv m^{\vphantom{\dagger}}_{M_{1}}\!+m_{\overline{M}_{2}}\,.

(14)

For
S
-wave
\delta^{\vphantom{1}}{\bar{\delta}}
states, we identify

V_{\delta^{\vphantom{1}}{\bar{\delta}}}(\mathbf{r})
as the uncoupled
\Sigma^{+}_{g}

potential
Lebed 2017
, with parameters that are calculated
on the lattice
Berwein et al. 2015
. Thus, we parametrize

V_{\delta^{\vphantom{1}}{\bar{\delta}}}(r)=-\frac{\alpha}{r}+\sigma r+V_{0}+m_{\delta^{\vphantom{1}}}+m_{{\bar{\delta}}}\,,

(15)

where
\alpha,\sigma,
and
V_{0}
are
0.053~\text{GeV$\cdot$fm},1.097~\text{GeV/fm},
and
-0.180~\text{GeV}
, respectively. For
each flavor sector, one also requires values for the
\delta^{\vphantom{1}},{\bar{\delta}}

masses, and a corresponding list of di-hadron thresholds with
matching
J^{PC}
quantum numbers must be identified. One then needs
only to determine the appropriate form for the mixing potentials.
Reference
Bruschini and González 2020

we also adopt here. Explicitly,

|V_{\rm mix}^{(i)}(r)|=\frac{\Delta}{2}\exp\!\left\{-\frac{1}{2}\frac{\left[V^{\vphantom{\dagger}}_{\delta^{\vphantom{1}}{\bar{\delta}}}(r)-T_{M_{1}\overline{M}_{2}}^{(i)}\right]^{2}}{(\rho\sigma)^{2}}\right\},

(16)

where
\sigma
is the same string-tension parameter as in the
Cornell-like potential of Eq. (
15
).
\Delta
is a free
parameter with units of energy indicating the strength of the mixing,
and
\rho
is the radial scale for the level
crossing
Bruschini and González 2020
.

It is a useful first exercise to consider this procedure for a single
threshold. The diabatic-potential matrix then reads

\text{V}(r)=\begin{pmatrix}V^{\vphantom{\dagger}}_{\delta^{\vphantom{1}}{\bar{\delta}}}(r)&V^{\vphantom{\dagger}}_{\rm mix}(r)\\
V^{\vphantom{\dagger}}_{\rm mix}(r)&T_{M_{1}\overline{M}_{2}}\end{pmatrix},

(17)

where we have replaced
\mathbf{r}
with its magnitude, since all
relevant potentials at this stage depend solely upon the diquark
separation distance. The corresponding single-threshold
kinetic-energy operator becomes

\text{K}=\begin{pmatrix}-\frac{\hbar^{2}}{2\mu_{\delta^{\vphantom{1}}{\bar{\delta}}}}&\\
&-\frac{\hbar^{2}}{2\mu_{M_{1}\overline{M}_{2}}}\end{pmatrix}\nabla^{2}.

(18)

The diabatic-potential matrix eigenvalues [denoted
V_{-}(r)
and

V_{+}(r)
] and corresponding eigenvectors [

and

|\xi_{+}(r)\rangle
] may be directly related to the
\delta^{\vphantom{1}}{\bar{\delta}}
and

\text{R}(r)=\begin{pmatrix}\ \ \cos\theta(r)&\sin\theta(r)\\
-\sin\theta(r)&\cos\theta(r)\end{pmatrix},

(19)

such that

\text{R}(r)\text{V}(r)\text{R}^{\dagger}(r)=\text{diag}\left\{V_{-}(r),V_{+}(r)\right\}\,.

(20)

Here,
\theta
is identified as the mixing angle between the

\delta^{\vphantom{1}}{\bar{\delta}}
and meson-meson terms. Since
\text{R}(r)
diagonalizes

\text{V}(r)
, one can deduce an expression for
\theta
in terms of
its matrix elements

:

\theta(r)=\frac{1}{2}\arctan\left(\frac{2V_{\rm mix}(r)}{T_{M_{1}\overline{M}_{2}}-V^{\vphantom{\dagger}}_{\delta^{\vphantom{1}}{\bar{\delta}}}(r)}\right).

(21)

The original form of Eq. (

24

explored in Ref.

Gens et al. 2021

V
Conclusions

The introduction into a diquark-based model—in this paper, the
variant known as the dynamical diquark model—of effects caused by
the proximity of some of its eigenstates to di-hadron thresholds
creates a model for multiquark exotic hadrons that combines the best
features of both diquark and hadron-molecular models. One universal
model can then incorporate exotic candidates that lie quite close to
such thresholds, but which still maintain a close physical connection
to other exotic candidates with no obvious di-hadron interpretation.

Coupled-channel calculations between bound states and thresholds have
of course been carried out many times in the past, originally in the
context of atomic and molecular physics, but more recently for
exotic-hadron candidates as well. The so-called diabatic approach
used here earns distinction as the natural (and rigorous)
generalization of the Born-Oppenheimer approximation used to compute
the unperturbed spectrum of heavy-heavy systems such as quarkonium
and its hybrid excitations, and in this work for the first time, the
diquark-antidiquark states of the dynamical diquark model.

The precise functional form for the mixing potential between
unperturbed states and thresholds used here is phenomenological in
origin, but it is motivated by lattice-QCD results involving the
breaking of the color flux tube between the heavy sources. Clearly,
advances in lattice simulations can be incorporated into improved
future calculations.

We find, using parameters from the most recent analysis in the
dynamical diquark model, that the famous
X(3872)
can naturally
contain a dominant component of
D^{0}\bar{D}^{*0}
and yet originate
as the unique isosinglet
1^{++}
member of the lightest multiplet of
hidden-charm diquark-antidiquark states. Masses of all the remaining
members of the multiplet are then predicted, along with their
di-hadron content generated by nearby thresholds. Since the original
model has been extended to study hidden-charm, hidden-strangeness and
open-strange states as well, we also present calculations for those
flavor sectors. General results include the observation that

S
-wave thresholds always produce larger effects than
D
-wave
thresholds, even if the latter are substantially closer to the
mass eigenvalue, and that coincident di-hadron thresholds with
different arrangements of the light flavors in the hadrons have
comparable effects.

We also use our results on state content to calculate the radiative
decay widths for
X(3872)\!\to\!\gamma J/\psi
and

\gamma\psi(2S)
, which provides crucial information on the
short-distance components of
X(3872)
, since its di-hadron component
is spatially much larger than that of its diquark-antidiquark
component. Interestingly, we obtain a
\gamma\psi(2S)
width much
smaller than the current measured value, due not just to the fact
that the compact component amounts to only about 10% of the full
state, but also that the overlap of the
1S
diquark-antidiquark and

2S

c\bar{c}
states is small. We conclude that obtaining the full
radiative width may require including a comparable contribution from
the diffuse di-hadron component, or even from a
\chi_{c1}(2P)

component (which can also be included in the diabatic formalism).

These initial calculations, while quite encouraging, remain quite
incomplete. First, no spin- or flavor-dependent effects (besides
explicit differences in the diquark mass) have been incorporated into
the unperturbed side of this calculation. The Hamiltonian for the
full dynamical diquark model contains spin-spin and spin-isospin
operators that, for example, distinguish masses of
X(3872)
,

Z_{c}(3900)
, and
Z_{c}(4020)
. In this calculation, the
only mass splittings arise from the explicit differences of

D^{(*)}_{(s)}
masses. One major future research thrust is the
inclusion of explicit fine-structure effects into the initial
unperturbed states.

A second possible improvement involves the treatment of the
threshold contribution. Di-hadron molecular models have been alluded
to several times in this paper, but the actual treatment of the
di-hadron state uncoupled from the diquark-antidiquark state,
according to Eq. (
14
), is simply that of two free
hadrons. Should it be desirable to regard the pair as forming a true
di-hadron molecule, then directly replacing Eq. (
14
)
with a mildly attractive
e.g.
, meson-exchange) potential wouldbe straightforward in this formalism.

Finally, the diabatic formalism as presented here strictly only
applies to states either below or not too far above significant
di-hadron thresholds. Some of the exotic candidates [
e.g.
,

Z_{c}(4430)
] lie rather far from relevant thresholds, and
consequently have large decay widths. Such broad resonances should
properly be treated as poles in di-hadron scattering amplitudes.
However, the diabatic formalism has been developed, in the case of
mixing with conventional quarkonium, to include the calculation of
strong decay widths
Bruschini and González 2021a
and di-hadron scattering
amplitudes
Bruschini and González 2021b
. These methods can be
immediately adapted to the case of mixing with diquark-antidiquark
states, and will also be incorporated into future work.

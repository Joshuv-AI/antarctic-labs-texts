A new multi-partite entanglement measure and its holographic dual Preprint: TIFR/TH/22-34
Abhijit Gadde, Vineeth Krishna, Trakshu Sharma

Abstract
In this letter we define a natural generalization of the von Neumann entropy to multiple parties that is symmetric with respect to all the parties. We call this measure multi-entropy. We show that for conformal field theories with holographic duals, the multi-entropy is computed by the area of an appropriate “soap-film” anchored on the boundary. We conjecture the quantum version of this prescription that takes into account the sub-leading corrections in G N G_{N} .



I
Introduction

In recent years, quantum information theoretic notions such as entanglement entropy
Calabrese and Cardy 2004
;
Ryu and Takayanagi 2006
;
Hubeny et al. 2007
;
Casini et al. 2011
;
Dong 2014
;
Engelhardt and Wall 2015
have been immensely useful in shedding light on some of the important questions pertaining to quantum gravity .
In particular, it has helped understand holographic encoding of the gravitational Hilbert space and in turn, the Black-hole entropy
Bekenstein 1973
;
Hawking 1975
and the information paradox. However, with the exception of reflected entropy
Dutta and Faulkner 2021
and negativity
Calabrese et al. 2012
;
Kusuki et al. 2019
, almost all the discussion has been centered on the entanglement between two parties i.e. on bi-partite entanglement. It is expected that knowing multi-partite entanglement structure in quantum gravity would refine our understanding of the holographic encoding of gravitational Hilbert space in conformal field theory Hilbert space. To this end, we introduce a multi-partite entanglement measure and its holographic dual.

II
The Measure

Let
|\Psi\rangle\in\otimes_{{\mathtt{a}}={1}}^{\mathtt{q}}{\cal H}_{\mathtt{a}}
be a quantum state of a
{\mathtt{q}}
-party system. Let
{\rm dim}\,{\mathcal{H}}_{\mathtt{a}}=d_{\mathtt{a}}
and
|\alpha_{\mathtt{a}}\rangle,\,\alpha_{\mathtt{a}}\in\{1,\ldots,d_{\mathtt{a}}\}
be its orthonormal basis. In
|\alpha_{\mathtt{a}}\rangle
basis, the state
|\Psi\rangle
is given as

\displaystyle|\Psi\rangle=\sum_{\alpha_{1}=1}^{d_{1}}\ldots\sum_{\alpha_{\mathtt{q}}=1}^{d_{\mathtt{q}}}\,\psi_{\alpha_{1}\ldots\alpha_{\mathtt{q}}}|\alpha_{1}\rangle\otimes\ldots\otimes|\alpha_{\mathtt{q}}\rangle.

(1)

A
{\mathtt{q}}
-party entanglement measure is the information contained in the wavefunction
\psi
(and its complex conjugate) that is invariant under “local unitary transformations”. They are products of unitary operations
{\mathcal{U}}_{\mathtt{a}}\in U(d_{\mathtt{a}})
performed in individual system
{\mathtt{a}}
. Naturally, such a measure is obtained by contracting
\alpha_{\mathtt{a}}
indices for all
{\mathtt{a}}
. Now
\alpha_{\mathtt{a}}
is a fundamental index on
\psi
and an anti-fundamental index of
\bar{\psi}
. Hence, in order to construct invariants we need an equal number of
\psi
’s and
\bar{\psi}
’s. We will call this number the replica number
n
. We index the replicas by the superscript
(i)
. In particular, the Hilbert space
{\mathcal{H}}_{a}
of the
i
-th replica is denoted as
{\mathcal{H}}_{\mathtt{a}}^{(i)}
and the associated basis as
|\alpha_{\mathtt{a}}^{(i)}\rangle
. The wavefunction of the
i
-th replica is then
\psi_{\alpha_{1}^{(i)}\ldots\alpha_{\mathtt{q}}^{(i)}}
and similarly its conjugate is
\bar{\psi}^{\alpha_{1}^{(i)}\ldots\alpha_{\mathtt{q}}^{(i)}}
.

II.1
Bi-partite

Let us consider the following bi-partite entanglement measure with a fixed replica number
n

\displaystyle{\mathcal{Z}}_{n}\equiv{\rm Tr}(\rho^{n}),\quad{\rm where}\quad\rho_{\alpha^{(1)}_{1}}^{\alpha^{(2)}_{1}}\equiv\psi_{\alpha^{(1)}_{1}\alpha^{(1)}_{2}}\bar{\psi}^{\alpha^{(2)}_{1}\alpha^{(1)}_{2}}.

(2)

Here
\rho
is the density matrix for party
{1}
. With this notation, the squared norm of
|\Psi\rangle
is
{\mathcal{Z}}_{1}
. This measure is related to the familiar
n
-th Renyi entropy
S_{n}
as

\displaystyle S_{n}=\frac{1}{1-n}{\rm Log}({\mathcal{Z}}_{n}/{\mathcal{Z}}_{1}^{n}).

(3)

Let us reformulate this measure using permutations acting on replicas. This will be useful while dealing with entanglement in quantum field theories using twist operators.

\displaystyle{\mathcal{Z}}_{n}=(\psi_{\alpha^{(1)}_{1}\alpha^{(1)}_{2}}\psi_{\alpha^{(2)}_{1}\alpha^{(2)}_{2}}\ldots)(\bar{\psi}^{\alpha^{(1)}_{1}\alpha^{(\sigma\cdot 1)}_{2}}\bar{\psi}^{\alpha^{(2)}_{1}\alpha^{(\sigma\cdot 2)}_{2}}\ldots)

(4)

where
\sigma
is a cyclic permutation element acting in the space of replicas
i=1,\ldots,n
of the second party i.e.
\sigma(\alpha_{2}^{(i)})=\alpha_{2}^{(i+1)}
with
\alpha_{2}^{(n+1)}\equiv\alpha_{2}^{(1)}
. Because this permutation acts only on the replica copies of party-
{2}
, we denote it as
\sigma_{2}
.
A priori, it would seem that we should specify two permutation elements
(\sigma_{1},\sigma_{2})
, the first one acting on replicas of party-
{1}
and the other acting on replicas of party-
{2}
to get the most general index contraction, i.e.

\displaystyle(\psi_{\alpha^{(1)}_{1}\alpha^{(1)}_{2}}\psi_{\alpha^{(2)}_{1}\alpha^{(2)}_{2}}\ldots)(\bar{\psi}^{\alpha^{(\sigma_{1}\cdot 1)}_{1}\alpha^{(\sigma_{2}\cdot 1)}_{2}}\bar{\psi}^{\alpha^{(\sigma_{1}\cdot 2)}_{1}\alpha^{(\sigma_{2}\cdot 2)}_{2}}\ldots)

(5)

However, we can always relabel replicas of
\bar{\psi}
so that one of the
\sigma
’s, say
\sigma_{1}
, is brought to
{\rm id}
form. In other words,
(\sigma_{1},\sigma_{2})
and
(\sigma_{1}g,\sigma_{2}g),g\in{\mathbb{S}}_{n}
yield the same measure. We express this as an equivalence relation

\displaystyle(\sigma_{1},\sigma_{2})\sim(\sigma_{1},\sigma_{2})\,g

(6)

There is yet another equivalence, namely under simultaneous relabeling of replica copies of
both

\psi
’s and
\bar{\psi}
’s. Such a relabeling leads to conjugation of both
\sigma_{\mathtt{a}}
by some
h\in{\mathbb{S}}_{n}
. We express this as the equivalence relation

\displaystyle(\sigma_{1},\sigma_{2})\sim h^{-1}\,(\sigma_{1},\sigma_{2})\,h.

(7)

If we use equation (
6
) to set
\sigma_{1}={\rm id}
, then thanks to equation (
7
), the bi-partite entanglement measure depends only on the conjugacy class of
\sigma_{2}
. Conjugacy class in
{\mathbb{S}}_{n}
is specified by cycle decomposition
\{p_{k}\}
where
p_{k}
is the number of
k
-cycles such that
\sum_{k}k\,p_{k}=n
. For this class, it is easy to see that the resulting measure (
5
) is

\displaystyle\prod_{k}\,({\rm Tr}(\rho^{k}))^{p_{k}}=\prod_{k}\,({\mathcal{Z}}_{k})^{p_{k}}.

(8)

This shows that the ring of bi-partite measures is generated by
{\mathcal{Z}}_{k}
’s.

II.2
Multi-partite

Following this discussion, we can write a general multi-partite measure by giving permutation elements
(\sigma_{1},\ldots,\sigma_{\mathtt{q}})
with equivalence relations

\displaystyle(\sigma_{1},\ldots,\sigma_{\mathtt{q}})

\displaystyle\sim(\sigma_{1},\ldots,\sigma_{\mathtt{q}})\cdot g

(9)

\displaystyle(\sigma_{1},\ldots,\sigma_{\mathtt{q}})

\displaystyle\sim h^{-1}\,(\sigma_{1},\ldots,\sigma_{\mathtt{q}})\,h.

(10)

Unlike the bi-partite case, where Renyi entropies are the only independent measures of entanglement, for the case of three or higher number of parties, the number of measures increase exponentially or faster with the replica number
n
. In this letter, we will not concern ourselves with general
{\mathtt{q}}
-party measures but rather focus on a particular family of measures labeled by an integer that is symmetric under the exchange of all the parties.

As in the bi-partite case, equation (
9
) can be used to set
\sigma_{1}={\rm id}
. But this “gauge fixing” obscures the symmetry between all the parties as it treats party-
{1}
differently from others. So we will not use the gauge freedom (
9
) and (
10
) just yet and specify all
\sigma_{\mathtt{a}}
’s. We will do so with
n^{{\mathtt{q}}}
replicas. We index the replicas with a
{\mathtt{q}}
-dimensional index vector
(i_{1},\ldots,i_{\mathtt{q}})
where each
i_{\mathtt{a}}=\{1,\ldots,n\}
. The permutation element
\sigma_{\mathtt{a}}
is defined as the cyclic element acting only on
i_{\mathtt{a}}
:

\displaystyle\sigma_{\mathtt{a}}\cdot(\ldots,i_{\mathtt{a}},\ldots)=(\ldots,i_{\mathtt{a}}+1,\ldots),\quad{\mathtt{a}}=1,\ldots,{\mathtt{q}}.

(11)

As an element of the permutation group
{\mathbb{S}}_{n^{{\mathtt{q}}}}
, its conjugacy class is
p_{n}=n^{{\mathtt{q}}-1}
with all other
p_{k}=0
. It is also clear that this measure is symmetric in all the
{\mathtt{q}}
parties.

Now we use the gauge freedom (
9
) with
g=\sigma_{1}^{-1}
. This gives us an equivalent set of permutation elements

\displaystyle({\rm id},\sigma_{2}\sigma_{1}^{-1},\ldots,\sigma_{\mathtt{q}}\sigma_{1}^{-1}).

(12)

Note that the action of any of these permutations on
(i_{1},\ldots,i_{\mathtt{q}})
keeps the sum
\sum_{\mathtt{a}}\,i_{\mathtt{a}}
invariant. As a result, their action on
n^{{\mathtt{q}}}
replicas splits into
n
orbits of
n^{{\mathtt{q}}-1}
elements with
\sum_{\mathtt{a}}i_{\mathtt{a}}={\rm constant}({\rm mod}\,n)
. Each orbit gives rise to the same invariant of
\psi
’s and
\bar{\psi}
’s.
It is convenient to work with a single orbit, say with
\sum_{\mathtt{a}}i_{\mathtt{a}}=0({\rm mod}\,n)
. For this orbit, we can use the relabeling freedom (
10
) to set the replica index
i_{1}
to
1
. With this gauge fixing, we get a convenient presentation of the rest of the permutation elements
(\sigma_{2}\sigma_{1}^{-1},\ldots,\sigma_{\mathtt{q}}\sigma_{1}^{-1})\equiv(\hat{\sigma}_{2},\ldots,\hat{\sigma}_{\mathtt{q}})
.

\displaystyle\hat{\sigma}_{\mathtt{a}}\cdot(\ldots,i_{\mathtt{a}},\ldots)=(\ldots,i_{\mathtt{a}}+1,\ldots),\quad{\mathtt{a}}={2},\ldots,{\mathtt{q}}.

(13)

We have denoted the gauge fixed permutation elements as
\hat{\sigma}_{\mathtt{a}}
to avoid confusion with un-gauge fixed permutation elements
\sigma_{\mathtt{a}}
.
The index set in equation (
13
) starts from
i_{2}
because
i_{1}
has already been set to
1
. We denote the measure defined by
\hat{\sigma}_{\mathtt{a}}
elements in (
13
) as
{\mathcal{Z}}_{n}^{({\mathtt{q}})}
and define Renyi multi-entropy
S_{n}^{({\mathtt{q}})}
as

\displaystyle S^{({\mathtt{q}})}_{n}\equiv\frac{1}{1-n}{\rm Log}\,({\mathcal{Z}}_{n}^{({\mathtt{q}})}/({\mathcal{Z}}_{1}^{({\mathtt{q}})})^{n^{{\mathtt{q}}-1}}).

(14)

Here the factor
({\mathcal{Z}}_{1}^{({\mathtt{q}})})^{n^{{\mathtt{q}}-1}}
serves to normalize the state.
This is the family of
{\mathtt{q}}
-partite measures that we are interested in. Let us pause for a moment to consider properties of the set
\{\hat{\sigma}_{\mathtt{a}}:{\mathtt{a}}={2},\ldots,{\mathtt{q}}\}
.

•

\hat{\sigma}_{\mathtt{a}}
’s have the same equivalence class given by the cycle
p_{n}=n^{{\mathtt{q}}-2}
with all other
p_{k}=0
.

•

\hat{\sigma}_{\mathtt{a}}^{-1}\hat{\sigma}_{\mathtt{b}}
for
{\mathtt{a}}\neq{\mathtt{b}}
also have the same equivalence class.

•

Together,
\hat{\sigma}_{\mathtt{a}}
s generate the subgroup
{\mathbb{Z}}_{n}^{\otimes{{\mathtt{q}}-1}}
of the permutation group.

These observations will be important in the future discussions.

In a way, fixing
\hat{\sigma}_{1}={\rm id}
has a very natural interpretation. If we consider a single
\psi
and a single
\bar{\psi}
with
i_{1}
index contraction, we get the density matrix on
{\mathcal{H}}_{2}\otimes\ldots\otimes{\mathcal{H}}_{\mathtt{q}}
. This density matrix has the index structure

\displaystyle\rho_{\alpha_{2}^{(i_{2})}\ldots\alpha_{\mathtt{q}}^{(i_{\mathtt{q}})}}^{\alpha_{2}^{(\hat{\sigma}_{2}\cdot i_{2})}\ldots\alpha_{\mathtt{q}}^{(\hat{\sigma}_{\mathtt{q}}\cdot i_{\mathtt{q}})}}.

(15)

This means the gauge fixed permutations
\hat{\sigma}_{\mathtt{a}}
’s describe index contractions of this density matrix. Let us see this with two examples, for
{\mathtt{q}}=2
,

\displaystyle{\mathcal{Z}}^{({2})}_{n}=\sum\prod_{i_{2}}\,\rho_{\alpha_{2}^{(i_{2})}}^{\alpha_{2}^{(\hat{\sigma}_{2}\cdot i_{2})}}=\sum\rho_{\alpha_{2}^{(1)}}^{\alpha_{2}^{(\hat{\sigma}_{2}\cdot 1)}}\rho_{\alpha_{2}^{(2)}}^{\alpha_{2}^{(\hat{\sigma}_{2}\cdot 2)}}\ldots\rho_{\alpha_{2}^{(n)}}^{\alpha_{2}^{(\hat{\sigma}_{2}\cdot n)}}.

Here sum is over all repeated indices.
With
\hat{\sigma}_{2}
being a cyclic element as specified by equation (
13
), this is nothing but
{\mathcal{Z}}_{n}
defined earlier. So we have showed
S_{n}^{({2})}=S_{n}
. Graphically, denoting
\rho
as in figure
1
, we get the graphical representation of
S^{({2})}_{n}
as a circular lattice of
\rho
’s of length
n
as in figure
1
. Each directed link in this lattice represents index contraction from a fundamental index to an anti-fundamental index. The advantage of this graphical notation is that it admits straightforward extension to higher number of parties.

and the index contraction that gives

.

For instance, in the case of
{3}
-parties, we denote the density matrix as in figure
2
. Then
{\mathcal{Z}}^{({3})}_{n}
is the index contraction given by the toric lattice of
\rho
’s with both sides being of length
n
. This is shown in figure
2
. Similarly,
{\mathcal{Z}}^{({\mathtt{q}})}_{n}
is the index contraction given by
({\mathtt{q}}-1)
-dimensional toric lattice with all sides being of length
n
.

Arguably the most important measure of bi-partite entanglement, the von-Neumann entropy
S
, is obtained as a limit

\displaystyle S={\rm lim}_{n\to{1}}\,S_{n}.

(16)

The Renyi multi-entropy
S^{({\mathtt{q}})}_{n}
is defined such that the limit

\displaystyle S^{({\mathtt{q}})}={\rm lim}_{n\to{1}}\,S^{({\mathtt{q}})}_{n},

(17)

called
the
multi-entropy, has a number of nice properties.

1.

It is symmetric in all the parties.

2.

For
{\mathtt{q}}=2
, it reduces to the von-Neumann entropy.

3.

It admits a convenient holographic description.

Properties
1
and
2
are obvious from the discussion so far. In the rest of the paper, we will discuss multi-entropy for holographic theories and describe what we exactly mean by property
3
. The multi-entropy can also be computed from
{{\mathcal{Z}}}_{n}^{({\mathtt{q}})}
using the formula

\displaystyle S^{({\mathtt{q}})}=-\partial_{n}{\rm log}\Big({\mathcal{Z}}_{n}^{({\mathtt{q}})}/({\mathcal{Z}}_{1}^{({\mathtt{q}})})^{n^{{\mathtt{q}}-1}}\Big)|_{n=1}.

(18)

We will use this formula and equation (
17
) interchangeably.

II.3
A note on analytic continuation

We have defined the multi-entropy as a limit of the Renyi multi-entropy in equation (
17
). This requires analytic continuation of
S_{n}^{({\mathtt{q}})}
away from integer
n
. One may rightly wonder whether such an analytic continuation exists and under what conditions is it unique. The issue of existence was recently emphasized in
Penington et al. 2022
.
The uniqueness issue is usually tackled using the Carlson’s theorem. It says

•

A function
f(z)
that is analytic for
{\rm Re}(z)>0
and satisfies

\displaystyle f(|z|)\leq Ae^{c|z|},\quad f(iy)\leq Ae^{\pi y}

(19)

for some real constants
A
and
c
for all
z\in{\mathbb{C}}
and
y\in{\mathbb{R}}
and vanishes for any non-negative integer must be identically zero.

In order to argue for the uniqueness of the analytic continuation (if it exists) we must look for analytic functions obeying the analyticity and boundedness conditions required by the Carlson’s theorem, henceforth called the Carlson conditions. The problem of constructing such a function from its values at non-negative integers or “input data” was considered in a paper by Regge and Viano
Regge and Viano 1962
. There this problem was motivated from the theory of complex angular momentum. The authors obtain a solution that is even amenable to numerical analysis. However, it is not clear whether their proposed analytic continuation obeys the Carlson conditions. This question was expounded upon in the paper
Albino et al. 1964
. In this paper, authors propose an elaborate set of conditions on the input data that is necessary for the Carlson conditions to hold
1
1

1

We would like to thank Geoff Penington to bring this paper to our attention.
. We are currently exploring if these conditions satisfied Renyi multi-entropies of qubit states. If these conditions hold for general quantum states then the multi-entropy would be unambiguously defined. For the purposes of this paper, we will define multi-entropy only for those states whose Renyi multi-entropy admit a manifest analytic continuation. As we will see below, this is the case for holographic states.

II.4
Example

Although the multi-entropy is amenable to holographic computation as we will see shortly, it is difficult to compute
for finite dimensional quantum systems. This is because multi-entropy is defined only via the replica trick and, in general, it is difficult to compute
{\mathcal{Z}}_{n}^{({\mathtt{q}})}
as an analytic function of
n
. However, for a special class of states that we call generalized GHZ states, the multi-entropy is readily computed. Consider
|\Psi\rangle\in\otimes_{{\mathtt{a}}=1}^{\mathtt{q}}{\mathcal{H}}_{\mathtt{a}}
with
{\rm dim}\,{\mathcal{H}}_{{\mathtt{a}}}=d
. The generalized GHZ state is defined as

\displaystyle|\Psi\rangle_{\rm GHZ}=\sum_{i}\lambda_{i}|i\rangle\otimes\ldots\otimes|i\rangle.

(20)

Here
|i\rangle
is an orthonormal basis in
{\mathcal{H}}_{{\mathtt{a}}}
for all
{\mathtt{a}}
. The state is normalized so
\sum_{i}|\lambda_{i}|^{2}=1
.
It is easy to compute
{\mathcal{Z}}_{n}^{({\mathtt{q}})}
because the same index
i
runs through all the contractions. It contributes
|\lambda_{i}|^{2n^{{\mathtt{q}}-1}}
. Then the
{\mathtt{q}}
-Renyi entropy is

\displaystyle S_{n}^{({\mathtt{q}})}=\frac{1}{1-n}{\rm log}\Big(\sum_{i}|\lambda_{i}|^{2n^{{\mathtt{q}}-1}}\Big).

(21)

Taking the
n\to 1
limit,

\displaystyle S^{({\mathtt{q}})}=(1-{\mathtt{q}})\sum_{i}|\lambda_{i}|^{2}{\rm log}|\lambda_{i}|^{2}.

(22)

A bi-partite state can always be taken to the generalized GHZ form via Schmidt decomposition. Then
|\lambda_{i}|^{2}
are the eigenvalues of the density matrix. It is clear that
S^{({\mathtt{q}})}
agrees with the von-Neumann entropy for
{\mathtt{q}}=2
. We believe that it is extremely important to develop techniques to compute multi-entropy for general states to understand its quantum information theoretic properties.

II.5
Distinguishing isospectral density matrices

\rho_{12}
and
\sigma_{12}
on two qubits such that the spectrum of
\rho_{12},\rho_{1}
and
\rho_{2}
is identical to that of
\sigma_{12},\sigma_{1}
and
\sigma_{2}
respectively. Such density matrices are known as isospectral. This example of the isospectral pair is borrowed from

. The isospectral property of these density matrices guarantees that no bi-partite measure of entanglement can distinguish between these states. However, as we will show, all the Renyi multi-entropies for these two states are different.

(23)

The non-zero eigenvalues of
\rho_{12},\rho_{1},\rho_{2}
(and also for
\sigma_{12},\sigma_{1},\sigma_{2}
) are
\frac{1}{3},\frac{2}{3}
. The values of the first few Renyi multi-entropies are

S^{(3)}_{3}

is obtained by tracing out one party in the pure W-state

and

III
Multi-entropy from Holography

In this section, we will discuss computation of multi-entropy in a
D
-dimensional conformal field theory
{\cal T}
. Let the state
|\Psi\rangle
be defined on a time symmetric Cauchy slice
{\mathcal{R}}
of a
D
-manifold
{\mathcal{M}}
. It is given by a Euclidean path integral on the half-space
{\mathcal{M}}_{\Psi}
such that
\partial{\mathcal{M}}_{\Psi}={\mathcal{R}}
. The dual bra
\langle\Psi|
is constructed by Euclidean path integral on the other half
{\mathcal{M}}_{\bar{\Psi}}
. It is obtained from
{\mathcal{M}}_{\Psi}
by reflecting across
{\mathcal{R}}
. The squared norm of
\Psi
is the partition function
{\mathcal{Z}}_{\mathcal{M}}
on
{\mathcal{M}}
.
Let us decompose
{\mathcal{R}}
into
{\mathtt{q}}
number of disjoint regions
{\mathcal{R}}_{\mathtt{a}}
, such that
\cup_{\mathtt{a}}{\mathcal{R}}_{\mathtt{a}}={\mathcal{R}}
. Let the Hilbert space on region
{\mathcal{R}}_{\mathtt{a}}
be
{\mathcal{H}}_{\mathtt{a}}
.
We are interested in computing multi-entropy of the state
|\Psi\rangle
under the decomposition
\otimes_{\mathtt{a}}{\mathcal{H}}_{\mathtt{a}}
. For theories that admit a weakly coupled gravity dual, this problem can be addressed holographically.

The replica trick
Calabrese and Cardy 2004
involves working with the tensor product theory
{\cal T}^{\otimes n^{{\mathtt{q}}-1}}
on
{\mathcal{M}}
. This theory has the discrete symmetry
{\mathbb{S}}_{n^{{\mathtt{q}}-1}}
. It admits co-dimension
2
twist defects labeled by elements of this permutation group.
For every pair of regions
({\mathcal{R}}_{\mathtt{a}},{\mathcal{R}}_{\mathtt{b}})
that share a boundary, we insert the twist operator
{{\mathcal{O}}}_{\hat{\sigma}_{\mathtt{a}}^{-1}\hat{\sigma}_{{\mathtt{b}}}}
on the common boundary. The measure
{\mathcal{Z}}_{n}^{({\mathtt{q}})}
is then given by the correlation function of these twist operators.
Let us denote the resulting replicated manifold as
{\cal M}_{n}
. The correlation function of twist operators is the partition function
{\mathcal{Z}}_{{\mathcal{M}}_{n}}
on
{\mathcal{M}}_{n}
.
Using (
14
) we have

\displaystyle S^{{\mathtt{q}}}_{n}=\frac{1}{1-n}{\rm Log}({\mathcal{Z}}_{{\mathcal{M}}_{n}}/({\mathcal{Z}}_{\mathcal{M}})^{n^{{\mathtt{q}}-1}}).

(24)

Following
Lewkowycz and Maldacena 2013
, we will proceed to analyze this problem holographically. Let
{\mathcal{B}}_{n}
be dominant the gravity solutions such that
\partial{\mathcal{B}}_{n}={\mathcal{M}}_{n}
. The manifold
{\mathcal{B}}_{n}
is a smooth manifold e.g. in the case of Einstein gravity with negative cosmological constant, it is of constant negative curvature. The holographic dictionary gives

\displaystyle{\rm Log}\,{\mathcal{Z}}_{{\mathcal{M}}_{n}}=-{\mathcal{S}}_{\rm grav}({\mathcal{B}}_{n}).

(25)

Here
{\mathcal{S}}_{\rm grav}({\cal X})
is the gravitational action evaluated on the solution
{\cal X}
.
For
n=1
, this gives
{\rm Log}\,{\mathcal{Z}}_{{\mathcal{M}}}=-{\mathcal{S}}_{\rm grav}({\mathcal{B}}),{\mathcal{B}}\equiv{\mathcal{B}}_{1}
.

The background fields on the manifold
{\mathcal{M}}_{n}
enjoy a replica symmetry. This is the symmetry generated by the permutation elements
\hat{\sigma}_{\mathtt{a}}^{-1}\hat{\sigma}_{\mathtt{b}}
associated to all the twist operators. As remarked earlier, this group is
{\mathbb{Z}}_{n}^{\otimes{{\mathtt{q}}-1}}
. Following
Lewkowycz and Maldacena 2013
, we will assume that the dominant bulk solution
{\mathcal{B}}_{n}
enjoys this symmetry.
The solution
{\mathcal{B}}_{n}
consists of co-dimension
2
loci that are invariant under certain subgroups of the replica symmetry group. Some of these loci, called “external”, are anchored at the fixed points on the boundary (these are locations of twist operator insertions on
{\mathcal{M}}
) while the rest are “internal”. Let us denote the loci that are anchored at the fixed points corresponding to the twist operator
{\mathcal{O}}_{g}
as
{\mathcal{L}}_{g}
. They are invariant under the
{\mathbb{Z}}_{n}
subgroup generated by
g
. Generically, two
{\mathcal{L}}
s can merge to form a different
{\mathcal{L}}
. Merging obeys the algebra
{\mathcal{L}}_{g_{1}}\cdot{\mathcal{L}}_{g_{2}}\to{\mathcal{L}}_{g_{1}g_{2}}
.
The internal loci come about because of such merging.
Below we will assume that

1.

Every fixed point locus is of the form
{\mathcal{L}}_{\hat{\sigma}_{\mathtt{a}}^{-1}\hat{\sigma}_{\mathtt{b}}}
.

We will justify this assumption shortly. Thanks to the special property of our permutation elements
\hat{\sigma}_{\mathtt{a}}
stated below equation (
14
), this means that any locus is invariant under
some

{\mathbb{Z}}_{n}
subgroup of the replica symmetry. Every locus
{\mathcal{L}}_{g}
appears in groups of

IV
Generalizations

In this section we will comment on the generalizations of our soap-film prescription in various directions.

IV.1
Covariant prescription

The covariant generalization of the Ryu-Takayanagi formula was presented in
Hubeny et al. 2007
. It was argued there that the entanglement entropy is given by area of the extremal surface rather than the minimal one. This prescription was later rephrased as a maximin prescription in
Wall 2014
, where the surface in question is obtained first by minimizing the area on some achronal slice
\Sigma
and then by maximizing the area with respect to the variation of
\Sigma
. In case there are multiple extremal solutions, we pick the one with the minimum area as that corresponds to the most dominant solution.

The soap-film prescription proposed here admits a natural covariant generalization along the lines of
Hubeny et al. 2007
and
Wall 2014
. We conjecture that the covariant multi-entropy is obtained as the area of the minimal extremal soap-film rather than the globally minimal one and that this choice is equivalent to the one obtained by the maximin prescription of
Wall 2014
.

IV.2
Higher derivative gravity

For higher derivative gravitational theories, we do expect a non-trivial contribution localized at the higher co-dimensional meeting loci
{\widetilde{\mathcal{L}}}^{(k)}
’s because the dimensional analysis argument presented in section
III
ceases to be valid as
\ell_{\rm scale}
can be provided by the inverse mass-scale of the higher derivative corrections.
It would be interesting to compute these terms in general higher derivative theory of gravity.

IV.3
Quantum corrections

Quantum correction to the multi-entropy can be found using the replica trick in the bulk.
We consider
n^{{\mathtt{q}}-1}
replica copies of the orbifold
\widetilde{\mathcal{B}}_{n}
and insert the bulk twist operators
{\mathcal{V}}_{g}
at
\widetilde{\mathcal{L}}_{g}
. This has the effect of reversing the orbifold and give back the geometry
{\mathcal{B}}_{n}
. The partition function on the replicated manifold is then simply the partition function
{\mathcal{Z}}_{n}^{({\mathtt{q}})}
. This is the partition function that goes into the calculation of the multi-entropy. If the replica trick was performed on the bulk geometry
{\mathcal{B}}
, it would have given the bulk multi-entropy
S^{({\mathtt{q}})}_{\rm bulk}({\mathcal{W}})
corresponding to the chamber decomposition
{\mathcal{C}}_{a}
directly, however, because the replica trick was performed on
\widetilde{\mathcal{B}}_{n}
, it is not obvious that what we get is
S^{({\mathtt{q}})}_{\rm bulk}({\mathcal{W}})
.
This situation is similar to the bi-partite case
Faulkner et al. 2013
(see also
Barrella et al. 2013
). There, the difference between the two quantities is captured by changing the classical solution by
{\mathcal{O}}(G_{N})
to account for the one loop expectation value of the stress tensor. This changes the area and hence the entanglement entropy by
{\mathcal{O}}(1)
. We expect a similar formula to give the sub-leading correction to the multi-entropy

\displaystyle S^{({\mathtt{q}})}=\frac{\langle\hat{A}({\mathcal{W}})\rangle}{4G_{N}}+S_{\rm bulk}^{({\mathtt{q}})}({\mathcal{W}})+{\rm c.t.}.

(31)

Here
\hat{A}({\mathcal{W}})
is the area operator of the soap film and
{\rm c.t.}
are the counter-terms that render
S_{\rm bulk}^{({\mathtt{q}})}({\mathcal{W}})
finite.

Following
Engelhardt and Wall 2015
,
Dong and Lewkowycz 2018
we conjecture a formula that is valid to all orders in
1/G_{N}
perturbation theory: Multi-entropy is given
by the above formula but
{\mathcal{W}}
is not the ordinary area extremizing soap-film but rather the “quantum extremal soap-film” i.e. the soap film that extremizes the combination

\displaystyle\frac{A({\mathcal{W}})}{4G_{N}}+S_{\rm bulk}^{({\mathtt{q}})}({\mathcal{W}}).

(32)

In analogy with the bi-partite case, we call this prescription the quantum extremal soap-film prescription.

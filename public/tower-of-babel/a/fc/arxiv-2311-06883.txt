Abstract
Evidence for the singly Cabibbo suppressed decay Λ c + → p ​ π 0 \Lambda_{c}^{+}\to p\pi^{0} is reported for the first time with a statistical significance of 3.7 ​ σ 3.7\sigma based on 6.0 fb − 1 \mathrm{fb}^{-1} of e + ​ e − e^{+}e^{-} collision data collected at center-of-mass energies between 4.600 and 4.843 GeV with the BESIII detector at the BEPCII collider. The absolute branching fraction of Λ c + → p ​ π 0 \Lambda_{c}^{+}\to p\pi^{0} is measured to be ( 1.56 − 0.58 + 0.72 ± 0.20 ) × 10 − 4 (1.56^{+0.72}_{-0.58}\pm 0.20)\times 10^{-4} . Combining with the branching fraction of Λ c + → n ​ π + \Lambda_{c}^{+}\to n\pi^{+} , ( 6.6 ± 1.3 ) × 10 − 4 (6.6\pm 1.3)\times 10^{-4} , the ratio of the branching fractions of Λ c + → n ​ π + \Lambda_{c}^{+}\to n\pi^{+} and Λ c + → p ​ π 0 \Lambda_{c}^{+}\to p\pi^{0} is calculated to be 3.2 − 1.2 + 2.2 3.2^{+2.2}_{-1.2} . As an important input for the theoretical models describing the decay mechanisms of charmed baryons, our result indicates that the non-factorizable contributions play an essential role and their interference with the factorizable contributions should not be significant. In addition, the absolute branching fraction of Λ c + → p ​ η \Lambda_{c}^{+}\to p\eta is measured to be ( 1.63 ± 0.31 stat ± 0.11 syst ) × 10 − 3 (1.63\pm 0.31_{\rm stat}\pm 0.11_{\rm syst})\times 10^{-3} .



The hadronic weak decays of the
\Lambda_{c}^{+}
baryon have attracted strong theoretical interest.
However, till now there is no reliable phenomenological model which could clarify the complicated underlying dynamics of charmed baryon decays.
The hadronic decay amplitudes of charmed baryons generally consist of factorizable and non-factorizable contributions.
Unlike charmed mesons, the non-factorizable contribution is not negligible compared to the factorizable ones since the
W
-exchange process is no longer subject to helicity and color suppression
Cheng et al. 2018
;
Cheng 2015
.
This makes the corresponding theoretical calculation more complicated.
Moreover, the interference between the
W
-emission and exchange contributions
remain unclear despite of the extensive studies by various phenomenological models.
Therefore, experimentally investigating the non-leptonic weak hadronic decays of
\Lambda_{c}^{+}
are highly desired, in which the two-body singly Cabibbo suppressed (SCS) decays,
\Lambda^{+}_{c}\to n\pi^{+}
,
\Lambda_{c}^{+}\to p\pi^{0}
, and
\Lambda_{c}^{+}\to p\eta

\Lambda^{+}_{c}\to n\pi^{+}
and
\Lambda_{c}^{+}\to p\pi^{0}
receive the external and color-suppressed internal
W
-emission contributions, respectively,
together with the non-factorizable
W
-exchange ones.
In addition, the ratio of their branching fractions (BFs) is expected to be less sensitive to the input parameters in the phenomenological models, due to the significant cancellation of the corresponding correlated uncertainties, and is therefore effective to test the different models.

\Lambda^{+}_{c}\to n\pi^{+}
, which is measured by BESIII in 2022
Ablikim et al. 2022a
.
However, the updated prediction on BF of
\Lambda_{c}^{+}\to p\pi^{0}
, (
1.3\pm 0.7)\times 10^{-4}
,
still appears to be greater than the upper limit measured by Belle,
<8.0\times 10^{-5}
, despite the large uncertainty.
Additionally, by combining the results from Belle
Li et al. 2021
and BESIII
Ablikim et al. 2022a
, the ratio of the BFs between
\Lambda^{+}_{c}\to n\pi^{+}
and
\Lambda_{c}^{+}\to p\pi^{0}
is calculated to be greater than 7.2 at the 90% confidence level.
This result unexpectedly conflicts with most theoretical predictions,
such as 2.0
with the SU(3)
f
symmetry
Geng et al. 2018
;
Sharma and Verma 1997
;
Lü et al. 2016
,
4.7 with the SU(3)
f
symmetry including the contribution from
\mathcal{O}(6)
and
\mathcal{O}(\overline{15})

Geng et al. 2019
,
4.5
from the constituent quark model
Uppal et al. 1994
, and 3.5 from the dynamical calculation based on the
pole model and current algebra
Cheng et al. 2018
.
Very recently, Ref.
Zhong et al. 2023
calculated the BF of
\Lambda_{c}^{+}\to p\pi^{0}
to be
(0.51_{-0.61}^{+0.59})\times 10^{-4}

or
(0.16\pm 0.09)\times 10^{-4}
,
by considering SU(3) broken or SU(3) respected effect, respectively,
which is consistent with Belle’s result
and lower than previous SU(3)
f
predictions.
To clarify the aforementioned contradictions, searching for
\Lambda_{c}^{+}\to p\pi^{0}
signal is crucial.
Moreover, the study of
\Lambda_{c}^{+}\to p\pi^{0}
will definitely contribute to the interpretation of the non-factorizable contributions and their interferences with the factorizable components.

In this Letter, the first evidence of the SCS decay
\Lambda_{c}^{+}\to p\pi^{0}
is reported using
e^{+}e^{-}
collision data, collected by the BESIII detector at ten center-of-mass (c.m.) energies between 4.600 and 4.843 GeV, corresponding to a total integrated luminosity of 6.0 fb
-1

Ablikim et al. 2022b
;
Ablikim et al. 2022c
.
These large data samples collected just above the
\Lambda_{c}^{+}\bar{\Lambda}_{c}^{-}
production threshold provide a clean environment and an excellent opportunity to search for
\Lambda_{c}^{+}\to p\pi^{0}
with the double-tag approach.
In addition, the decay
\Lambda_{c}^{+}\to p\eta
is also measured with the same approach to provide a validation.
Throughout this Letter, charge-conjugate modes are implicitly included.

The design and performance of the BESIII detector are described in detail in Ref.
Ablikim et al. 2010a
.
Simulated event samples are produced with a
geant4
-based
Agostinelli et al. 2003
Monte Carlo (MC) package, which includes the geometric description
Huang et al. 2022
of the BESIII detector and the detector response.
Signal MC samples of
e^{+}e^{-}\to\Lambda_{c}^{+}\bar{\Lambda}_{c}^{-}
, with
\bar{\Lambda}_{c}^{-}
decaying to nine specific tag modes (as described below and listed in Table
1
),
together with
\Lambda_{c}^{+}
decaying to
p\pi^{0}
or
p\eta
, are used to determine the detection efficiencies. They are generated by
kkmc
including the effects of initial-state radiation (ISR) and the beam energy spread.
To estimate backgrounds, inclusive MC samples, which consist of
\Lambda^{+}_{c}\bar{\Lambda}^{-}_{c}
events,
D_{(s)}^{(*)}
production, ISR production of vector charmonium(-like) states, Bhabha scattering,
\mu^{+}\mu^{-}
,
\tau^{+}\tau^{-}
,
\gamma\gamma
events and other inclusive hadronic processes incorporated in
kkmc

Jadach et al. 2001
, are generated.
Subsequent decays of all intermediate states are modeled with
evtgen

Lange 2001
;
Ping 2008
using the BFs either taken from the Particle Data Group
Workman et al. 2022
, when available, or modeled with
lundcharm

Chen et al. 2000
;
Yang et al. 2014
.
Final state radiation from charged final state particles is incorporated using the
photos
package
Richter-Was 1993
.

This work is carried out by the double-tag (DT) method.
First, we select a data sample of the process
e^{+}e^{-}\rightarrow\Lambda_{c}^{+}\bar{\Lambda}_{c}^{-}
, called the single-tag (ST) sample, by tagging a
\bar{\Lambda}_{c}^{-}
baryon with one of the nine exclusive hadronic decay modes, as listed in the first column of Table
1
.
Then, we search for the signal decays
\Lambda_{c}^{+}\to p\pi^{0}
and
\Lambda_{c}^{+}\to p\eta
in the system recoiling against the ST
\bar{\Lambda}_{c}^{-}
candidates, referred to as the DT sample hereafter.

Charged tracks, photon candidates as well as the intermediate
\pi^{0}
,
K_{S}^{0}
,
\bar{\Lambda}
, and
\bar{\Sigma}^{0}
states are selected and reconstructed using the same criteria described in detail in Ref.
Ablikim et al. 2022a
.
In addition, the kaon candidate is now required to have a particle identification (PID) probability to be a kaon greater than 0.0005, in order to suppress background with pions misidentified as kaons.
PID for charged tracks combines measurements of the energy deposited in
the MDC,
dE/dx
, and the flight time from the TOF to form likelihoods
L(h)

(
h=p,K,\pi
) for each hadron hypothesis.
Two variables, the energy difference,
\Delta E=E_{\bar{\Lambda}_{c}^{-}}-E_{\mathrm{beam}}
, and the beam energy constrained mass
M_{\mathrm{BC}}=\sqrt{E_{\mathrm{beam}}^{2}/c^{4}-|\vec{p}_{\bar{\Lambda}^{-}_{c}}|^{2}/c^{2}}
, are adopted to identify the ST
\bar{\Lambda}_{c}^{-}
candidates, where
E_{\mathrm{beam}}
is the beam energy,
E_{\bar{\Lambda}_{c}^{-}}
and
\vec{p}_{\bar{\Lambda}^{-}_{c}}
are the energy and momentum of the
\bar{\Lambda}_{c}^{-}
candidate, respectively.
The tagged candidates are selected with the minimum
\left|\Delta E\right|
among all the candidates, and are required to satisfy the
\Delta E
requirements listed in the second column of Table
1
.
To avoid cross feed from the other ST modes, the same requirements as in Ref.
Ablikim et al. 2018
are applied for the
\bar{\Lambda}_{c}^{-}\to\bar{p}K_{S}^{0}\pi^{0}
,
\bar{\Lambda}_{c}^{-}\to\bar{p}K_{S}^{0}\pi^{+}\pi^{-}
and
\bar{\Lambda}\pi^{-}\pi^{+}\pi^{-}
modes.

The ST yields are extracted by performing unbinned maximum likelihood fits to the corresponding
M_{\mathrm{BC}}
distributions in the range (2.2,
E_{\mathrm{beam}}
)
\mathrm{GeV}/c^{2}
.
In the fit, the signal shape is modeled by the
M_{\mathrm{BC}}
spectrum extracted from the signal MC sample convolved with a Gaussian function to compensate the resolution difference between the data and the MC simulation.
The background is described by an ARGUS function
Albrecht et al. 1990
with the endpoint parameter fixed to the corresponding
E_{\mathrm{beam}}
.
The detection efficiencies of ST candidates are estimated with MC samples of
e^{+}e^{-}\to\Lambda_{c}^{+}\bar{\Lambda}_{c}^{-}
with
\bar{\Lambda}_{c}^{-}
decaying to one of the nine tag modes and
\Lambda_{c}^{+}
decaying generically to all possible final states.
The ST yields and efficiencies at
\sqrt{s}=4.682
GeV are shown in Table
1
. The results, including the ST yields and efficiencies for the other nine c.m. energies, and the fits for all c.m. energies are summarized in Supplemental Material
BES
.
The total ST yield for all the ten c.m. energies is
119398\pm 413_{\rm stat}
events.

10

The uncertainty associated with DT efficiencies,

Barlow

\Lambda

\Lambda_{c}^{+}\to pK^{-}\pi^{+}\pi^{0}
and
\Lambda_{c}^{+}\to\Lambda\pi^{+}\pi^{0}

The uncertainty due to the signal MC model is taken as 0.4%
Ablikim et al. 2023
.
Uncertainties from the BFs of

and

\Lambda_{c}^{+}\to p\pi^{0}

\pm 1\sigma

For
\Lambda_{c}^{+}\to p\eta
and

In summary, based on
e^{+}e^{-}
collision data samples with a total integrated luminosity of 6.0
\mathrm{fb}^{-1}
collected at c.m. energies between 4.600 and 4.843 GeV with the BESIII detector, the SCS decays
\Lambda_{c}^{+}\to p\pi^{0}
and
\Lambda_{c}^{+}\to p\eta
are measured using a DT method.
Evidence is obtained for the decay
\Lambda_{c}^{+}\to p\pi^{0}
, with a statistical significance of
3.7\sigma
and a decay BF of
(1.56^{+0.72}_{-0.58}\pm 0.20)\times 10^{-4}
.
The BF of
\Lambda_{c}^{+}\to p\eta
is measured as
(1.63\pm 0.31_{\rm stat}\pm 0.11_{\rm syst})\times 10^{-3}
, consistent with previous results
Ablikim et al. 2017
;
Ablikim et al. 2023
;
Li et al. 2021
.
The
\mathcal{B}(\Lambda_{c}^{+}\to p\pi^{0})
result distinctly exceeds the upper limit measured by Belle experiment.
Taking
\mathcal{B}(\Lambda^{+}_{c}\to n\pi^{+})=(6.6\pm 1.3)\times 10^{-4}

Ablikim et al. 2022a
, the ratio of the BFs between
\Lambda^{+}_{c}\to n\pi^{+}
and
\Lambda_{c}^{+}\to p\pi^{0}
is calculated to be
3.2^{+2.2}_{-1.2}
, where
3.2
denotes the most probable value, and the upper and lower errors covers the 68% confidence interval.
This value is obtained by generating toy MC samples and forming a Probability Density Function of the ratio. The detailed method is given in the Supplemental Material
BES
.
This ratio is consistent with majority of phenomenological predictions
Sharma and Verma 1997
;
Lü et al. 2016
;
Uppal et al. 1994
;
Cheng et al. 2018
;
Geng et al. 2018
;
Geng et al. 2019
. More importantly, not only the ratio but also the individual BFs of

\Lambda_{c}^{+}\to p\pi^{0}
and
\Lambda^{+}_{c}\to n\pi^{+}
agree with the calculation of SU(3)
f
symmetry including the
contributions from both
\mathcal{O}(6)
and
\mathcal{O}(\overline{15})

Geng et al. 2019
,
which indicates the non-factorizable contributions play an essential role in these two decays
and their interference with the factorizable contributions should not be significant.
The comparisons between the measurement results with Belle’s upper limit and phenomenological predictions are shown in Fig.
3

Operadic Dold-Kan correspondence and mapping spaces between enriched operads
Hoang Truong

Abstract
. According to the Dold-Kan correspondence, the normalization functor determines a categorical equivalence between simplicial abelian groups and non-negatively graded chain complexes. Nevertheless, the same statement is no longer true when extending to the context of algebras over an operad. In spite of this, the existence of the Dold-Kan correspondence for operadic algebras can still be realized from a homotopy-theoretic perspective. This was indeed observed for A ∞ \sA_{\infty} -algebras by Schwede-Shipley, and for E ∞ \E_{\infty} -algebras by Mandell, and more generally, for algebras over any Σ \Sigma -cofibrant operad due to White-Yau. In the first part of the paper, we shall prove further that the normalization functor induces an equivalence between the ∞ \infty -categories of operads with non-fixed sets of colors, in the Dwyer-Kan homotopy theory framework. Subsequently, we delve into investigating the mapping spaces between enriched operads by relating them to mapping spaces between operadic bimodules via nice fiber sequences. The validity of the main statements is examined first for simplicial operads, and then for connective dg operads due to the operadic Dold-Kan correspondence.

Background and notations

2.1.
Operads and modules over an operad

2.2.
Operadic model structures

We now recall the homotopy theory of algebras over an operad and the homotopy theory of enriched operads themselves.

\mathcal{S}

. Let
\mathcal{P}
be a

on

. By convention, we will say that
\mathcal{P}
is

-

]
, Fresse
[

]

is cofibrantly generated and such that the monoidal unit

-enriched operad, denoted

Remark 2.8

In a cofibrantly generated semi-model category, not all cofibrations can be expressed as the retract of a

, but only those cofibrations whose domain is cofibrant. In particular, every cofibrant object can be expressed as the retract of a

, the unit map

is a weak equivalence.

Proof.

This observation is well known within the realm of (full) model categories. The same argument can be applied without any impediment.
∎

For
\mathcal{C}\in\Cat(\mathcal{S})
an
\mathcal{S}
-enriched category, the
homotopy category
of
\mathcal{C}
, denoted
\Ho(\mathcal{C})
, is the ordinary category whose objects are the same as those of
\mathcal{C}
and such that, for
x,y\in\Ob(\mathcal{C})
, we have

\textrm{Hom}_{\Ho(\mathcal{C})}(x,y):=\textrm{Hom}_{\Ho(\mathcal{S})}(1_{\mathcal{S}},\Map_{\mathcal{C}}(x,y)).

By convention, the
homotopy category of an operad

\mathcal{P}
is

\Ho(\mathcal{P}):=\Ho(\mathcal{P}_{1})

(see Remark
2.5
for notation). Moreover, a map
f:\mathcal{P}\rightarrow\mathcal{Q}
in
\Op(\mathcal{S})
is called a
levelwise weak equivalence
(resp.
fibration, trivial fibration
, etc) if for every sequence
(c_{1},\cdots,c_{n};c)
of colors of
\mathcal{P}
, the induced map

\mathcal{P}(c_{1},\cdots,c_{n};c)\longrightarrow\mathcal{Q}(f(c_{1}),\cdots,f(c_{n});f(c))

is a weak equivalence (resp. fibration, trivial fibration, etc) in
\mathcal{S}
. In what follows, we recall the two model structures on enriched operads, according to Caviglia’s
[
19
]
.

Definition 2.10
.

A map
f:\mathcal{P}\rightarrow\mathcal{Q}
in
\Op(\mathcal{S})
is called a
Dwyer-Kan equivalence
if it is a levelwise weak equivalence and such that the induced functor

\Ho(f):\Ho(\mathcal{P})\longrightarrow\Ho(\mathcal{Q})

between homotopy categories is essentially surjective.

Definition 2.11
.

The
Dwyer-Kan model structure on

\Op(\mathcal{S})
is the one whose weak equivalences are the Dwyer-Kan equivalences and whose trivial fibrations are the levelwise trivial fibrations surjective on colors.

Definition 2.12
.

The
canonical model structure on

\Op(\mathcal{S})
is the one whose fibrant objects are the levelwise fibrant operads and whose trivial fibrations are the same as those of the Dwyer-Kan model structure.

Remark 2.13
.

The two model structures on
\Op(\mathcal{S})
are a natural generalization of the corresponding model structure on
\Cat(\mathcal{S})
. For more details, the Dwyer-Kan model structure on
\Cat(\mathcal{S})
is developed in
[
20
,
21
]
, and can be thought of as a generalization of the Dwyer-Kan homotopy theory of simplicial categories introduced by Bergner (
[
22
]
). On the other hand, the canonical model structure on
\Cat(\mathcal{S})
is established by Berger-Moerdijk
[
23
]
, as another approach to the homotopy theory of enriched categories. Nevertheless, in practice the Dwyer-Kan and canonical model structures usually coincide (cf. [
[
19
]
,
\lx@sectionsign
8]).

Remark 2.14
.

A map
f
in
\Op(\mathcal{S})
is a Dwyer-Kan equivalence if and only if it is a levelwise weak equivalence and such that its underlying map in
\Cat(\mathcal{S})
is a Dwyer-Kan equivalence. On other hand, as originally introduced in
[
19
]
,
f
is a
canonical fibration
(i.e. a fibration with respect to the canonical model structure) if and only if it is a levelwise fibration and such that its underlying map in
\Cat(\mathcal{S})
is a canonical fibration.

Examples 2.15
.

We discuss the three base categories that we are concerned with in this paper. Let
k
be a commutative ring.

(i) The first one is
\Set_{\Delta}
the category of simplicial sets, equipped with the Cartesian monoidal product and with the classical (Kan-Quillen) model structure. The transferred model structure on
\Alg_{\mathcal{P}}(\Set_{\Delta})
exists for every operad
\mathcal{P}
. Moreover, both the Dwyer-Kan and canonical model structures on

exist and, in fact, coincide with each other.

(ii) One of the main interests in this paper is
\sMod(\textbf{k})
the category of simplicial

and with the model structure transferred from the classical model structure on
\Set_{\Delta}
. As well as the case above, all the model category structures of interest are available and, in addition, the two model structures on
\Op(\sMod(\textbf{k}))
coincide.

(iii) Another main interest is the category
\mathcal{C}_{\geqslant 0}(\textbf{k})
of connective dg
k
-modules, equipped with the usual tensor product over

and with the projective model structure. In general, for an operad
\mathcal{O}
in

exists only as a semi-model category, provided

). We would guess that the Dwyer-Kan homotopy theory on

3.1.
Dold-Kan correspondence of operadic algebras

In this subsection, we shall present a proof of Theorem

As usual, we let
\sN

denote the inverse of
\sN
, as parts of the Dold-Kan correspondence. Let

be a unital ring and let

and

be the simplicial right and left
R
-modules, respectively. Recall that the
Alexander-Whitney map

\AW:\sN(A\otimes_{R}B)\longrightarrow\sN A\otimes_{R}\sN B

and the

\nabla:\sN A\otimes_{R}\sN B\longrightarrow\sN(A\otimes_{R}B)

form the homotopy inverses of each other. For more details, we refer the readers to, e.g., [
[

Remark 3.1
.

Let
K
and
L
be connective dg right and left
R
-modules, respectively. There is an induced natural map

(3.1)

\Gamma(K\otimes_{R}L)\longrightarrow\Gamma(K)\otimes_{R}\Gamma(L)

given by the adjoint of the composed map

K\otimes_{R}L\longrightarrow\sN\Gamma(K)\otimes_{R}\sN\Gamma(L)\overset{\nabla}{\longrightarrow}\sN(\Gamma(K)\otimes_{R}\Gamma(L))

where the first map is the tensor product over
R
of the unit maps of
\Gamma\dashv\sN
. This composed map is in particular a weak equivalence. Thus, the map (
3.1
) is as well a weak equivalence, because the functor
\Gamma
sends every weak equivalence to a weak equivalence.

Now let
k
be a fixed commutative ring. The tensor product over
k
will be abbreviated as
``\otimes
”.

Let
A
and
B
be two simplicial
k
-modules. The maps

\nabla:\sN A\otimes\sN B\longrightarrow\sN(A\otimes B)\,\;\text{and}\;\AW:\sN(A\otimes B)\longrightarrow\sN A\otimes\sN B

respectively equip the functor
\sN:\sMod_{\textbf{k}}\longrightarrow\mathcal{C}_{\geqslant 0}(\textbf{k})
with a lax and an oplax monoidal structure. Note that while the lax monoidal structure of
\sN
is symmetric, however, its oplax monoidal structure is not so (cf. [
[
3
]
, §2.3]). In particular, the canonical lax monoidal structure of
\Gamma:\mathcal{C}_{\geqslant 0}(\textbf{k})\longrightarrow\sMod_{\textbf{k}}
, that is induced by
\AW
, is not symmetric.

Due to the symmetricity of the lax monoidal structure of
\sN:\sMod_{\textbf{k}}\longrightarrow\mathcal{C}_{\geqslant 0}(\textbf{k})
, the lifted functor
\sN_{C}:\Coll_{C}(\sMod_{\textbf{k}})\longrightarrow\Coll_{C}(\mathcal{C}_{\geqslant 0}(\textbf{k}))
admits an extended lax monoidal structure

\sN_{C}(X)\circ\sN_{C}(Y)\longrightarrow\sN_{C}(X\circ Y)

for
X,Y\in\Coll_{C}(\sMod_{\textbf{k}})
. As a consequence,
\sN_{C}
descends to a functor

(3.2)

\sN_{C}:\Op_{C}(\sMod_{\textbf{k}})\longrightarrow\Op_{C}(\mathcal{C}_{\geqslant 0}(\textbf{k}))

between
C
-colored operads.

Remark 3.2
.

On other hand, the functor
\Gamma:\mathcal{C}_{\geqslant 0}(\textbf{k})\longrightarrow\sMod_{\textbf{k}}
does not admit any canonical symmetric lax monoidal structure. Consequently, in general, one can not produce
\sMod_{\textbf{k}}
-enriched operads just by applying
\Gamma
to the
\mathcal{C}_{\geqslant 0}(\textbf{k})
-enriched operads levelwise.

Despite that inconvenience, we can give a description of the left adjoint of (
3.2

3.2

]

We complete the proof by observing that the composition of the two right Quillen equivalences obtained above is exactly the functor

. ∎

In [

,

Corollary 3.9
.

Let
\mathcal{P}
be a levelwise cofibrant operad in

\sN^{ib}_{\mathcal{P}}:\IbMod(\mathcal{P})\longrightarrow\IbMod(\sN\mathcal{P})

Remark 3.10
.

According to the loc.cit again, the category of left (resp. right)

Here is the corollary that is of most interest.

Corollary 3.11
.

The normalization functor induces a right Quillen equivalence

3.2.
Dold-Kan correspondence of enriched operads

In this subsection, we shall give a proof of the statement
1.4
. To this end, we first need to show that applying the normalization functor levelwise determines a right adjoint functor
\Op(\sMod_{\textbf{k}})\longrightarrow\Op(\mathcal{C}_{\geqslant 0}(\textbf{k}))
.

For the latter, it is needed to understand how the Grothendieck construction is compatible with adjunctions. Let
\mathcal{F}:\mathcal{C}^{\op}\longrightarrow\Cat
be a contravariant functor on a given category
\mathcal{C}
, and valued in the category of small categories
\Cat
. For each map
\alpha:c\rightarrow d
in
\mathcal{C}
, we will write
\alpha^{*}:\mathcal{F}(d)\longrightarrow\mathcal{F}(c)
standing for the functor corresponding to
\alpha
through
\mathcal{F}
. As usual, we denote by
\int_{\mathcal{C}}\mathcal{F}
the (contravariant) Grothendieck construction of
\mathcal{F}
.

Construction 3.15
.

Suppose we are given two functors
\mathcal{F},\mathcal{G}:\mathcal{C}^{\op}\longrightarrow\Cat
and suppose that for every object
c\in\Ob(\mathcal{C})
, there is an adjunction

\rL_{c}:\mathcal{F}(c)\mathrel{\vbox{\halign{\hfil$#$\hfil\cr\scriptstyle\cr\kern 0.43057pt\cr\relbar\joinrel\rightharpoonup\cr\kern-1.29167pt\cr\begin{picture}\roundcap\polyline(0,0)(1,0)
\polyline(0.5,0)(0.5,1)
\end{picture}\cr\leftharpoondown\joinrel\relbar\cr\scriptstyle\cr}}}\mathcal{G}(c):\rR_{c}

such that the collection of right adjoints
\{\rR_{c}\}_{c\in\mathcal{C}}
forms a natural transformation from
\mathcal{G}
to
\mathcal{F}
. We establish two
integrated functors

\rL:\int_{\mathcal{C}}\mathcal{F}\longrightarrow\int_{\mathcal{C}}\mathcal{G}\;\;\text{and}\;\;\rR:\int_{\mathcal{C}}\mathcal{G}\longrightarrow\int_{\mathcal{C}}\mathcal{F}

as follows.

\bullet
Recall that an object of
\int_{\mathcal{C}}\mathcal{F}
is the choice of a pair
(c,X)
with
c\in\Ob(\mathcal{C})
and with
X
being an object of
\mathcal{F}(c)
. The functor
\rL
is given on objects by sending
(c,X)
to the pair
(c,\rL_{c}X)
. The functor
\rR
is defined on objects in a similar way.

\bullet
A map
(c,A)\longrightarrow(d,B)
in
\int_{\mathcal{C}}\mathcal{G}
is by definition the choice of a pair
(\alpha,f)
with
\alpha:c\rightarrow d
being a map in
\mathcal{C}
and
f:A\longrightarrow\alpha^{*}B
a map in
\mathcal{G}(c)
. We define
\rR(\alpha,f)
to be the pair

\left(\alpha,\;\rR_{c}(A)\overset{\rR_{c}(f)}{\xrightarrow{\hskip 28.45274pt}}\rR_{c}\alpha^{*}(B)\overset{\cong}{\longrightarrow}\alpha^{*}\rR_{d}(B)\right).

(The latter isomorphism is due to the assumption that
\{\rR_{c}\}_{c\in\mathcal{C}}
forms a natural transformation from
\mathcal{G}
to
\mathcal{F}
). Now let
(\beta,g):(c,X)\longrightarrow(d,Y)
be a map in
\int_{\mathcal{C}}\mathcal{F}
. We define
\rL(\beta,g)
to be the pair
(\beta,\widetilde{g})
in which
\widetilde{g}
is given by the adjoint of the composed map

X\overset{g}{\longrightarrow}\beta^{*}Y\overset{\beta^{*}(u_{Y})}{\xrightarrow{\hskip 28.45274pt}}\beta^{*}\rR_{d}\rL_{d}(Y)\overset{\cong}{\longrightarrow}\rR_{c}\beta^{*}\rL_{d}(Y)

where
u_{Y}
signifies a unit map of the adjunction
\rL_{d}\dashv\rR_{d}
.

The main interest in this construction is as follows.

Proposition 3.16
.

The obtained integrated functors form an adjunction between the Grothendieck constructions:

\rL:\int_{\mathcal{C}}\mathcal{F}\;\mathrel{\vbox{\halign{\hfil$#$\hfil\cr\scriptstyle\cr\kern 0.43057pt\cr\relbar\joinrel\rightharpoonup\cr\kern-1.29167pt\cr\begin{picture}\roundcap\polyline(0,0)(1,0)
\polyline(0.5,0)(0.5,1)
\end{picture}\cr\leftharpoondown\joinrel\relbar\cr\scriptstyle\cr}}}\;\int_{\mathcal{C}}\mathcal{G}:\rR\,.

Proof.

The proof is straightforward using the definition and basic properties of adjoint functors.
∎

Let us now apply Construction
3.15
to the collection of adjunctions

\{\mathcal{L}_{C}:\Op_{C}(\mathcal{C}_{\geqslant 0}(\textbf{k}))\mathrel{\vbox{\halign{\hfil$#$\hfil\cr\scriptstyle\cr\kern 0.43057pt\cr\relbar\joinrel\rightharpoonup\cr\kern-1.29167pt\cr\begin{picture}\roundcap\polyline(0,0)(1,0)
\polyline(0.5,0)(0.5,1)
\end{picture}\cr\leftharpoondown\joinrel\relbar\cr\scriptstyle\cr}}}\Op_{C}(\sMod_{\textbf{k}}):\sN_{C}\}_{C\in\Sets}.

Here we note that since the functors
\sN_{C}
’s are given by applying the normalization functor levelwise, they indeed commute with the changing-colors functors. Thus by Proposition
3.16
, we obtain an integrated adjunction between the categories of enriched operads:

\mathcal{L}:\Op(\mathcal{C}_{\geqslant 0}(\textbf{k}))\mathrel{\vbox{\halign{\hfil$#$\hfil\cr\scriptstyle\cr\kern 0.43057pt\cr\relbar\joinrel\rightharpoonup\cr\kern-1.29167pt\cr\begin{picture}\roundcap\polyline(0,0)(1,0)
\polyline(0.5,0)(0.5,1)
\end{picture}\cr\leftharpoondown\joinrel\relbar\cr\scriptstyle\cr}}}\Op(\sMod_{\textbf{k}}):\sN.

Lemma 3.17
.

The functor
\sN:\Op(\sMod_{\textbf{k}})\longrightarrow\Op(\mathcal{C}_{\geqslant 0}(\textbf{k}))
preserves and detects the Dwyer-Kan equivalences.

Proof.

According to [
[
9
]
,
\lx@sectionsign
5], the Dwyer-Kan model structure on
\Cat(\sMod_{\textbf{k}})
can be obtained by transferring that model structure on
\Cat(\mathcal{C}_{\geqslant 0}(\textbf{k}))
along the normalization functor. In particular, a map
f
in
\Cat(\sMod_{\textbf{k}})
is a Dwyer-Kan equivalence if and only if
\sN(f)
is one. We complete the proof by combining the latter fact with Remark
2.14
, and along with the fact that the functor
\sN
preserves and detects the levelwise weak equivalences.
∎

In order to state the main theorem, we will regard
\Op(\sMod_{\textbf{k}})
and
\Op(\mathcal{C}_{\geqslant 0}(\textbf{k}))
as
relative categories
with weak equivalences being precisely the Dwyer-Kan equivalences. To avoid confusion, we will write
\Op^{\prime}(\sMod_{\textbf{k}})
and
\Op^{\prime}(\mathcal{C}_{\geqslant 0}(\textbf{k}))
standing for the corresponding relative categories. Since
\sN
preserves Dwyer-Kan equivalences, it determines a
relative functor
from
\Op^{\prime}(\sMod_{\textbf{k}})
to
\Op^{\prime}(\mathcal{C}_{\geqslant 0}(\textbf{k}))
.

Theorem 3.18
.

The functor
\sN:\Op^{\prime}(\sMod_{\textbf{k}})\longrightarrow\Op^{\prime}(\mathcal{C}_{\geqslant 0}(\textbf{k}))
is an equivalence of relative categories, meaning that it induces an equivalence between the underlying
\infty
-categories (or simplicial localizations).

The proof of this theorem will require a more technical lemma. Let
\mathcal{O}
be a
C
-colored operad in
\mathcal{C}_{\geqslant 0}(\textbf{k})
. We will say that
\mathcal{O}
is
relative cofibrant
if it is cofibrant as an object of
\Op_{C}(\mathcal{C}_{\geqslant 0}(\textbf{k}))
with respect to the transferred semi-model structure.

Lemma 3.19
.

The functor
\mathcal{L}
preserves Dwyer-Kan equivalences between relative cofibrant operads.

Proof.

Let
f:\mathcal{O}\longrightarrow\mathcal{O}^{\prime}
be any Dwyer-Kan equivalence between relative cofibrant objects. Since
\sN
detects Dwyer-Kan equivalences (see Lemma
3.17
), the proof will be completed after showing that the map
\sN\mathcal{L}(f):\sN\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{O})\longrightarrow\sN\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{O}^{\prime})
is one. By construction, the unit map
\mathcal{O}\longrightarrow\sN\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{O})
agrees with the unit
\mathcal{O}\longrightarrow\sN_{C}\mathcal{L}_{C}(\mathcal{O})
of the adjunction
\mathcal{L}_{C}\dashv\sN_{C}
. By Corollary
3.11
, the latter is a weak equivalence in
\Op_{C}(\mathcal{C}_{\geqslant 0}(\textbf{k}))
and in particular, a Dwyer-Kan equivalence. Thus by naturality, the two maps
\sN\mathcal{L}(f)
and
f
are related via a pair of Dwyer-Kan equivalences and hence,
\sN\mathcal{L}(f)
is indeed a Dwyer-Kan equivalence.
∎

Remark 3.20
.

When the Dwyer-Kan model structure on
\Op(\mathcal{C}_{\geqslant 0}(\textbf{k}))
is available, the class of relative cofibrant operads is precisely that of cofibrant objects in
\Op(\mathcal{C}_{\geqslant 0}(\textbf{k}))
(cf.
[
14
]
, Observation 4.1.1(ii)). In this situation, the statement of Lemma
3.19
becomes evident because
\mathcal{L}
is then a left Quillen functor.

For each set
C
, there exists a canonical functorial cofibrant replacement
Q_{C}
on the category
\Op_{C}(\mathcal{C}_{\geqslant 0}(\textbf{k}))
which is natural in
``C
”. We can then integrate the collection
\{Q_{C}\,|\,C\in\Sets\}
into a single
functorial relative cofibrant replacement:

Q:\Op(\mathcal{C}_{\geqslant 0}(\textbf{k}))\longrightarrow\Op(\mathcal{C}_{\geqslant 0}(\textbf{k})).

We denote by
\mathbb{L}\mathcal{L}:=\mathcal{L}\circ Q
and refer to it the
relative left derived functor
of
\mathcal{L}
.

Proof of Theorem
3.18
.

Due to Lemma
3.19
, the functor
\mathbb{L}\mathcal{L}
determines a functor between relative categories:

\mathbb{L}\mathcal{L}:\Op^{\prime}(\mathcal{C}_{\geqslant 0}(\textbf{k}))\longrightarrow\Op^{\prime}(\sMod_{\textbf{k}}).

It now suffices to argue that the composed functor
\mathbb{L}\mathcal{L}\circ\sN
is weakly equivalent to the identity functor
\Id_{\Op^{\prime}(\sMod_{\textbf{k}})}
, and that
\sN\circ\,\mathbb{L}\mathcal{L}
is related to the corresponding identity via a chain of natural Dwyer-Kan equivalences. These follow from the fact that the (co)unit of
\mathcal{L}\dashv\sN
is simply given by the (co)units of the adjunctions
\mathcal{L}_{C}\dashv\sN_{C}
for
C\in\Sets
, that the functor
\sN_{C}
preserves and detects weak equivalences, and that
\mathcal{L}_{C}\dashv\sN_{C}
is a Quillen equivalence for every
C
.
∎

Remark 3.21
.

Assume that the Dwyer-Kan model structure on
\Op(\mathcal{C}_{\geqslant 0}(\textbf{k}))
exists. Then it automatically coincides with the canonical model structure, according to [
[
19
]
, Proposition 4.25] and the fact that these two model structures have the same trivial fibrations. Therefore, a map in
\Op(\mathcal{C}_{\geqslant 0}(\textbf{k}))
is a Dwyer-Kan fibration if and only if it is a levelwise fibration such that its underlying map in
\Cat(\mathcal{C}_{\geqslant 0}(\textbf{k}))
is a Dwyer-Kan fibration (see also Remark
2.14
). The same statement holds for Dwyer-Kan fibrations in
\Op(\sMod_{\textbf{k}})
as well. We hence deduce that the functor
\sN
preserves and detects the Dwyer-Kan fibrations, similarly as in Lemma
3.17
.

Corollary 3.22
.

Suppose that the Dwyer-Kan model structure on the category
\Op(\mathcal{C}_{\geqslant 0}(\textbf{k}))
exists. Then
\mathcal{L}\dashv\sN
forms a Quillen equivalence.

Proof.

First, the adjunction
\mathcal{L}\dashv\sN
forms a Quillen adjunction by Lemma
3.17
and by Remark
3.21
. Theorem
3.18
proves further that
\mathcal{L}\dashv\sN
is a Quillen equivalence as expected.
∎

4.
Mapping spaces of operads in relation to operadic bimodules

In this section, we will let
\mathcal{S}
be a fixed symmetric monoidal category and let
\mathcal{P}
be a
C
-colored operad in
\mathcal{S}
. To avoid confusion, we will write

\sqcup\;,\;\bigsqcup\;\text{and}\;\coprod

standing for the coproduct operations (and also, pushouts) in the categories
\BMod(\mathcal{P})
,
\Op_{C}(\mathcal{S})
and
\Op(\mathcal{S})
, respectively.

4.1.
The first fiber sequence

First, let us consider the case where the set of colors is fixed.

We are recalling a hypothesis introduced by Dwyer-Hess, that has played a very active role in their work
[
12
]
. Recall by definition that a
pointed
\mathcal{P}
-bimodule
is a
\mathcal{P}
-bimodule
M
endowed with a map
\mathcal{I}_{C}\longrightarrow M
of
C
-collections. We will let
\BMod(\mathcal{P})^{*}
denote the category of such objects with morphisms being the basepoint-preserving maps.

Remark 4.1
.

Note that
\mathcal{P}
itself is an object of
\BMod(\mathcal{P})^{*}
with the base point given by the unit of
\mathcal{P}
. Notice also that
\BMod(\mathcal{P})^{*}
agrees with the under-category
\BMod(\mathcal{P})_{\mathcal{P}\circ\mathcal{P}/}
where
\mathcal{P}\circ\mathcal{P}
represents the free
\mathcal{P}
-bimodule generated by
\mathcal{I}_{C}
.

Let
f+g:\mathcal{P}\bigsqcup\mathcal{P}\longrightarrow\mathcal{Q}
be a map in
\Op_{C}(\mathcal{S})
. Then
\mathcal{Q}
inherits a
\mathcal{P}
-bimodule structure with the left (resp. right)
\mathcal{P}
-action induced by
f
(resp.
g
). From this, we obtain a restriction functor

\Op_{C}(\mathcal{S})_{\mathcal{P}\bigsqcup\mathcal{P}/}\longrightarrow\BMod(\mathcal{P})^{*},

which admits a left adjoint, denoted by
\E
. Observe that the functor
\E
sends
\mathcal{P}
to itself
\mathcal{P}\in\Op_{C}(\mathcal{S})_{\mathcal{P}\bigsqcup\mathcal{P}/}
equipped with the fold map
\Id+\Id:\mathcal{P}\bigsqcup\mathcal{P}\longrightarrow\mathcal{P}
.

We suppose further that
\mathcal{S}
is a symmetric monoidal model category satisfying the assumptions as in Theorem
2.6
.

Definition 4.2
.

(
Goodness hypothesis
)
The operad
\mathcal{P}\in\Op_{C}(\mathcal{S})
is said to be
good
if the left derived functor
\mathbb{L}\E:\BMod(\mathcal{P})^{*}\longrightarrow\Op_{C}(\mathcal{S})_{\mathcal{P}\bigsqcup\mathcal{P}/}
sends
\mathcal{P}
to itself
\mathcal{P}
. Moreover, the base category
\mathcal{S}
is
good
if every cofibrant operad in
\mathcal{S}
is good.

Remark 4.3
.

Considering operads as monoid objects in the monoidal category of
C
-collections, we can see that the
goodness hypothesis
is a particular case of what Dwyer and Hess refer to as the stability of
distinguished objects
with respect to the functor
\E
(cf. [
[
12
]
,
\lx@sectionsign
3.4]).

We give the following statement as an illustration of how that hypothesis can be applied in practice.

Theorem 4.4
.

Suppose that
\mathcal{P}\in\Op_{C}(\mathcal{S})
is a good cofibrant operad and suppose given a map
f:\mathcal{P}\longrightarrow\mathcal{O}
in
\Op_{C}(\mathcal{S})
. We also regard
\mathcal{O}
as a
\mathcal{P}
-bimodule with the structure induced by
f
. Then there is a fiber sequence of spaces

(4.1)

\Omega\Map^{\h}_{\Op_{C}(\mathcal{S})}(\mathcal{P},\mathcal{O})\longrightarrow\Map^{\h}_{\BMod(\mathcal{P})}(\mathcal{P},\mathcal{O})\longrightarrow\underset{c\in C}{\prod}\Map^{\h}_{\mathcal{S}}(1_{\mathcal{S}},\mathcal{O}(c;c))

in which the loop space
\Omega\Map^{\h}_{\Op_{C}(\mathcal{S})}(\mathcal{P},\mathcal{O})
is taken at the base point
f
.

Remark 4.5
.

This theorem is in fact a version of Theorem 3.11 of the loc.cit. Nonetheless, we shall provide an alternative proof that is somewhat more direct.

Proof of Theorem
4.4
.

First, observe that there is a commutative square of left Quillen functors

(4.2)

\textstyle{\BMod(\mathcal{P})_{\mathcal{P}\circ\mathcal{P}/}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\mu_{!}}
\scriptstyle{\E}
\textstyle{\BMod(\mathcal{P})_{\mathcal{P}/}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\overline{\E}}
\textstyle{\Op_{C}(\mathcal{S})_{\mathcal{P}\bigsqcup\mathcal{P}/}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\;\;(\Id+\Id)_{!}}
\textstyle{\Op_{C}(\mathcal{S})_{\mathcal{P}/}}

where
\overline{\E}
denotes the left adjoint to the corresponding restriction functor, the functor
\mu_{!}
is induced by the composition
\mu:\mathcal{P}\circ\mathcal{P}\longrightarrow\mathcal{P}
regarded as a map in
\BMod(\mathcal{P})
, and
(\Id+\Id)_{!}
refers to the functor induced by the fold map
\mathcal{P}\bigsqcup\mathcal{P}\longrightarrow\mathcal{P}
. To verify the commutativity, it suffices to observe that the associated square of right adjoints is commutative.

Let us take a factorization
\mathcal{P}\circ\mathcal{P}\overset{\varphi}{\longrightarrow}\mathcal{P}^{b}\overset{\simeq}{\longrightarrow}\mathcal{P}
in
\BMod(\mathcal{P})
of
\mu
into a cofibration followed by a weak equivalence. In particular,
\varphi
exhibits
\mathcal{P}^{b}
as a cofibrant model for
\mathcal{P}\in\BMod(\mathcal{P})^{*}
. Since
\mathcal{P}
is good and cofibrant, it implies that the derived image of
\mathcal{P}^{b}\in\BMod(\mathcal{P})^{*}
through the composed functor
(\Id+\Id)_{!}\circ\E
is a model for
\Sigma(\mathcal{P}\bigsqcup\mathcal{P})
, i.e. the homotopy pushout of the fold map
\mathcal{P}\bigsqcup\mathcal{P}\longrightarrow\mathcal{P}
with itself. In other direction, the derived image of
\mathcal{P}^{b}
under
\mu_{!}
is the pushout
\mathcal{P}\underset{\mathcal{P}\circ\mathcal{P}}{\sqcup}\mathcal{P}^{b}=:B_{\mathcal{P}}
. Combined with the commutativity of (
4.2
), those observations prove that the derived image of
B_{\mathcal{P}}\in\BMod(\mathcal{P})_{\mathcal{P}/}
through
\overline{\E}
is given by
\Sigma(\mathcal{P}\bigsqcup\mathcal{P})\in\Op_{C}(\mathcal{S})_{\mathcal{P}/}
.

Let us now consider the following coCartesian square in
\BMod(\mathcal{P})
:

(4.3)

\textstyle{\mathcal{P}\sqcup(\mathcal{P}\circ\mathcal{P})\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\;\;\Id\sqcup\varphi}
\scriptstyle{\Id+\mu}
\textstyle{\mathcal{P}\sqcup\mathcal{P}^{b}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{\mathcal{P}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{B_{\mathcal{P}}}

Note that, when considered as a coCartesian square in
\BMod(\mathcal{P})_{\mathcal{P}/}
, this square is in fact homotopy coCartesian due to the fact that
\varphi:\mathcal{P}\circ\mathcal{P}\longrightarrow\mathcal{P}^{b}
is a cofibration between cofibrant
\mathcal{P}
-bimodules. Consider
\mathcal{O}
as an object of
\BMod(\mathcal{P})_{\mathcal{P}/}
. The square (
4.3
) yields a fiber sequence:

\Map^{\h}_{\BMod(\mathcal{P})_{\mathcal{P}/}}(B_{\mathcal{P}},\mathcal{O})\longrightarrow\Map^{\h}_{\BMod(\mathcal{P})_{\mathcal{P}/}}(\mathcal{P}\sqcup\mathcal{P}^{b},\mathcal{O})\longrightarrow\Map^{\h}_{\BMod(\mathcal{P})_{\mathcal{P}/}}(\mathcal{P}\sqcup(\mathcal{P}\circ\mathcal{P}),\mathcal{O}).

We shall complete the proof by showing each of these three spaces, in the order given, is naturally weakly equivalent to the respective space in the sequence (
4.1
). First, argue that there exists a chain of weak equivalences:

\Map^{\h}_{\BMod(\mathcal{P})_{\mathcal{P}/}}(B_{\mathcal{P}},\mathcal{O})\simeq\Map^{\h}_{\Op_{C}(\mathcal{S})_{\mathcal{P}/}}(\Sigma(\mathcal{P}\bigsqcup\mathcal{P}),\mathcal{O})\simeq\Omega\Map^{\h}_{\Op_{C}(\mathcal{S})_{\mathcal{P}/}}(\mathcal{P}\bigsqcup\mathcal{P},\mathcal{O}).

Indeed, the first weak equivalence follows from the second paragraph, while the other is straightforward. Moreover, by adjunction we have a weak equivalence

\Map^{\h}_{\Op_{C}(\mathcal{S})_{\mathcal{P}/}}(\mathcal{P}\bigsqcup\mathcal{P},\mathcal{O})\simeq\Map^{\h}_{\Op_{C}(\mathcal{S})}(\mathcal{P},\mathcal{O}).

We thus obtain a weak equivalence
\Map^{\h}_{\BMod(\mathcal{P})_{\mathcal{P}/}}(B_{\mathcal{P}},\mathcal{O})\simeq\Omega\Map^{\h}_{\Op_{C}(\mathcal{S})}(\mathcal{P},\mathcal{O})
. By adjunction again, we obtain the following chains of weak equivalences:

\Map^{\h}_{\BMod(\mathcal{P})_{\mathcal{P}/}}(\mathcal{P}\sqcup\mathcal{P}^{b},\mathcal{O})\simeq\Map^{\h}_{\BMod(\mathcal{P})}(\mathcal{P}^{b},\mathcal{O})\simeq\Map^{\h}_{\BMod(\mathcal{P})}(\mathcal{P},\mathcal{O}),\;\text{and}

\Map^{\h}_{\BMod(\mathcal{P})_{\mathcal{P}/}}(\mathcal{P}\sqcup(\mathcal{P}\circ\mathcal{P}),\mathcal{O})\simeq\Map^{\h}_{\BMod(\mathcal{P})}(\mathcal{P}\circ\mathcal{P},\mathcal{O})\simeq\Map^{\h}_{\Coll_{C}(\mathcal{S})}(\mathcal{I}_{C},\mathcal{O}).

Finally, notice that the latter space is weakly equivalent to
\underset{c\in C}{\prod}\Map^{\h}_{\mathcal{S}}(1_{\mathcal{S}},\mathcal{O}(c;c))
.
∎

Remark 4.6
.

According to the proof above, the map

\Map^{\h}_{\BMod(\mathcal{P})}(\mathcal{P},\mathcal{O})\longrightarrow\underset{c\in C}{\prod}\Map^{\h}_{\mathcal{S}}(1_{\mathcal{S}},\mathcal{O}(c;c))

is weakly equivalent to the map
\Map^{\h}_{\BMod(\mathcal{P})}(\mathcal{P},\mathcal{O})\longrightarrow\Map^{\h}_{\BMod(\mathcal{P})}(\mathcal{P}\circ\mathcal{P},\mathcal{O})
that is induced by the composition
\mu:\mathcal{P}\circ\mathcal{P}\longrightarrow\mathcal{P}
.

Proposition 4.9

For any commutative ring

Proof.

Let
\mathcal{P}\in\Op_{C}(\mathcal{C}_{\geqslant 0}(\textbf{k}))

\mathcal{C}_{\geqslant 0}(\textbf{k})

\textstyle{\BMod(\mathcal{P})^{*}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\E}
\textstyle{\BMod(\mathcal{L}_{C}\mathcal{P})^{*}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}

\mathcal{S}

Notation 4.15

When

Example 4.18

More generally, suppose that
\mathcal{S}
comes equipped with a weak monoidal Quillen adjunction

Theorem 4.20

There is a homotopy Cartesian square of spaces of the form

\textstyle{\Map^{\h}_{\Op_{C}(\mathcal{S})}(\mathcal{P},f^{*}\mathcal{Q})\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}

in which the bottom horizontal map is given by inserting, for each

, the object

into

Proof.

Without loss of generality, we may assume that
\mathcal{P}\in\Op_{C}(\mathcal{S})
is cofibrant. Since the initial
C

\mathcal{I}_{C}
is cofibrant as an object of
\Op(\mathcal{S})
, it implies that
\mathcal{P}
is as well cofibrant within

(cf.

\textstyle{\mathcal{I}_{C}\coprod\mathcal{I}_{C}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{\mathcal{I}_{C}\coprod\mathcal{P}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{\mathcal{I}_{C}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}

Due to the discussion above, this square is automatically homotopy coCartesian when considered as a square in
\Op(\mathcal{S})_{\mathcal{I}_{C}/}
. We also regard
\mathcal{Q}
as an object of
\Op(\mathcal{S})_{\mathcal{I}_{C}/}
via the map
\eta_{f}:\mathcal{I}_{C}\longrightarrow\mathcal{Q}
that is determined uniquely by

. Now, the above square yields a homotopy Cartesian square of spaces

\textstyle{\Map^{\h}_{\Op(\mathcal{S})_{\mathcal{I}_{C}/}}(\mathcal{P},\mathcal{Q})\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{\Map^{\h}_{\Op(\mathcal{S})}(\mathcal{P},\mathcal{Q})\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{\{\eta_{f}\}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}

(In fact, a generalization of this holds true in any
under
\infty
-category
, according to [
[
20
]
, 5.5.5.12] with the opposite convention). We shall complete the proof by showing the existence of natural weak equivalences:

\Map^{\h}_{\Op(\mathcal{S})_{\mathcal{I}_{C}/}}(\mathcal{P},\mathcal{Q})\simeq\Map^{\h}_{\Op_{C}(\mathcal{S})}(\mathcal{P},f^{*}\mathcal{Q})\;\;\text{and}\;\;\Map^{\h}_{\Op(\mathcal{S})}(\mathcal{I}_{C},\mathcal{Q})\simeq\underset{C}{\prod}\cor(\mathcal{Q}_{1}).

) with the fact that

as expected.

For the second one, we will make use of the Quillen adjunction
\Cat(\mathcal{S})\mathrel{\vbox{\halign{\hfil$#$\hfil\cr\scriptstyle\cr\kern 0.43057pt\cr\relbar\joinrel\rightharpoonup\cr\kern-1.29167pt\cr\begin{picture}\roundcap\polyline(0,0)(1,0)

(see Remark

can be identified with a category in
\mathcal{S}
exhibited as the

\Map^{\h}_{\Op(\mathcal{S})}(\mathcal{I}_{C},\mathcal{Q})\simeq\Map^{\h}_{\Cat(\mathcal{S})}(\mathcal{I}_{C},\mathcal{Q}_{1})\simeq\underset{C}{\prod}\Map^{\h}_{\Cat(\mathcal{S})}([0]_{\mathcal{S}},\mathcal{Q}_{1})\,\overset{\defi}{=}\,\underset{C}{\prod}\cor(\mathcal{Q}_{1}).

. As above, suppose we are given a map

in
\Op(\mathcal{C}_{\geqslant 0}(\textbf{k}))
. According to Example

is weakly equivalent to the
Eilenberg–MacLane space

in which as usual

signifies the group of units. Thus, in this case we obtain two fiber sequences

\Omega\Map^{\h}_{\Op_{C}(\mathcal{S})}(\mathcal{P},f^{*}\mathcal{Q})\longrightarrow\Map^{\h}_{\BMod(\mathcal{P})}(\mathcal{P},f^{*}\mathcal{Q})\longrightarrow\underset{C}{\prod}\,\textbf{k}\,,\;\text{and}

\Map^{\h}_{\Op_{C}(\mathcal{S})}(\mathcal{P},f^{*}\mathcal{Q})\longrightarrow\Map^{\h}_{\Op(\mathcal{S})}(\mathcal{P},\mathcal{Q})\longrightarrow\underset{C}{\prod}\,K(\textbf{k}^{\times},1)

respectively according to Theorems
4.4
and
4.20
.

Example 4.23

We will now revisit our examination of the general base category

, i.e. the operad in

whose colors are chosen from the collection of objects in

Suppose we are given a collection

\textstyle{\Map^{\h}_{\Op_{C}(\mathcal{S})}(\mathcal{P},\textrm{End}_{A})\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}

4.3.
Double loop spaces of mapping spaces between enriched operads

In this last subsection, we shall provide a proof of the statement
1.9
. As in the previous subsection, we will assume that the base category
\mathcal{S}
satisfies the assumptions as in Theorem
2.6
and such that the Dwyer-Kan homotopy theory on
\Op(\mathcal{S})
exists (at least) as a semi-model category. Moreover, we will let
\mathcal{P}\in\Op_{C}(\mathcal{S})
be a cofibrant operad satisfying the goodness hypothesis (see Definition
4.2
).

Additionally, we will assume that
\mathcal{S}
satisfies Lurie’s
invertibility hypothesis
[
[
20
]
, Definition A.3.2.12]. Roughly speaking, this hypothesis requires that, for any category
\mathcal{C}\in\Cat(\mathcal{S})
containing a morphism
f
, localizing
\mathcal{C}
at
f
does not change the homotopy type of
\mathcal{C}
as long as
f
is an isomorphism in
\Ho(\mathcal{C})
.

Remark 4.25
.

The invertibility hypothesis is in fact popular in practice, as discussed in [
[
14
]
,
\lx@sectionsign
3]. In particular, all the base categories of Examples
2.15
satisfy that hypothesis.

Now suppose we are given a map
f:\mathcal{P}\longrightarrow\mathcal{O}
in
\Op(\mathcal{S})
. As usual,
f
induces a map
\mathcal{P}\longrightarrow f^{*}\mathcal{O}
in
\Op_{C}(\mathcal{S})
. We also regard this latter map as a map in
\BMod(\mathcal{P})
.

Theorem 4.26
.

There is a natural weak equivalence

\Omega^{2}\Map^{\h}_{\Op(\mathcal{S})}(\mathcal{P},\mathcal{O})\simeq\Omega\Map^{\h}_{\BMod(\mathcal{P})}(\mathcal{P},f^{*}\mathcal{O})

in which the double loop space on the left is taken at the base point
f
, and while the loop space on the right is taken at the map
\mathcal{P}\longrightarrow f^{*}\mathcal{O}
.

Our proof will leverage an approach analogous to the one we used in proving [
[
14
]
, Proposition 5.2.5]. Let us first pick up several notations from the loc.cit.

We let
[1]_{\mathcal{S}}
denote the category with objects
0,1
and with the mapping spaces
\Map_{[1]_{\mathcal{S}}}(0,1)=\Map_{[1]_{\mathcal{S}}}(0,0)=\Map_{[1]_{\mathcal{S}}}(1,1)=1_{\mathcal{S}}
and
\Map_{[1]_{\mathcal{S}}}(1,0)=\emptyset
. Next, denote by
[1]_{\mathcal{S}}^{\sim}
the category which is the same as the previous one except that
\Map_{[1]^{\sim}_{\mathcal{S}}}(1,0)=1_{\mathcal{S}}
. Take a factorization

[1]_{\mathcal{S}}\longrightarrow\mathcal{E}\overset{\simeq}{\longrightarrow}[1]_{\mathcal{S}}^{\sim}

of the canonical map
[1]_{\mathcal{S}}\longrightarrow[1]_{\mathcal{S}}^{\sim}
into a cofibration followed by a trivial fibration. We now obtain a sequence of maps

[0]_{\mathcal{S}}\coprod[0]_{\mathcal{S}}\longrightarrow[1]_{\mathcal{S}}\longrightarrow\mathcal{E}\overset{\simeq}{\longrightarrow}[1]_{\mathcal{S}}^{\sim}\overset{\simeq}{\longrightarrow}[0]_{\mathcal{S}}

such that the first two maps are cofibrations, while the others are weak equivalences.

We will write
-\otimes-
for the levelwise tensor product of categories in
\mathcal{S}
. For more details, the tensor product
\mathcal{C}\otimes\mathcal{D}
of two categories
\mathcal{C}
and
\mathcal{D}
has as set of objects
\Ob(\mathcal{C}\otimes\mathcal{D}):=\Ob(\mathcal{C})\times\Ob(\mathcal{D})
and such that for every
c,c^{\prime}\in\Ob(\mathcal{C})
and
d,d^{\prime}\in\Ob(\mathcal{D})
we have

\Map_{\mathcal{C}\otimes\mathcal{D}}((c,d),(c^{\prime},d^{\prime})):=\Map_{\mathcal{C}}(c,c^{\prime})\otimes\Map_{\mathcal{D}}(d,d^{\prime}).

Now applying
\mathcal{I}_{C}\otimes(-)
(where
\mathcal{I}_{C}
is viewed as an
\mathcal{S}
-enriched category) to the above sequence, we obtain another sequence of maps in
\Cat(\mathcal{S})
:

(4.13)

\mathcal{I}_{C}\coprod\mathcal{I}_{C}\longrightarrow\mathcal{I}_{C}\otimes[1]_{\mathcal{S}}\longrightarrow\mathcal{I}_{C}\otimes\mathcal{E}\overset{\simeq}{\longrightarrow}\mathcal{I}_{C}\otimes[1]_{\mathcal{S}}^{\sim}\overset{\simeq}{\longrightarrow}\mathcal{I}_{C}.

Observe that the last two maps are weak equivalences, while the others are again cofibrations because
\mathcal{I}_{C}
is a discrete category.

We will be particularly concerned with the following objects in
\Op(\mathcal{S})_{\mathcal{P}//\mathcal{P}}
:

\mathcal{A}:=(\mathcal{P}\coprod\mathcal{P})\underset{\mathcal{I}_{C}\coprod\mathcal{I}_{C}}{\coprod}\mathcal{I}_{C}\otimes[1]_{\mathcal{S}}\;\;\;\text{and}\;\;\;\mathcal{B}:=(\mathcal{P}\coprod\mathcal{P})\underset{\mathcal{I}_{C}\coprod\mathcal{I}_{C}}{\coprod}\mathcal{I}_{C}\otimes\mathcal{E}\;.

Note first that each of these two pushouts represents the corresponding homotopy pushout, due to the two cofibrations of (
4.13
) and along with the cofibrancy of
\mathcal{P}
. In other words, the two objects
\mathcal{A}
and
\mathcal{B}
have the right homotopy type.

We regard
\Op(\mathcal{S})_{\mathcal{P}//\mathcal{P}}
as a pointed model category with
\mathcal{P}
as the base point, so that we may define the suspension functor
\Sigma(-)
on this category.

Lemma 4.27
.

Let
\varphi:\mathcal{A}\longrightarrow\mathcal{B}
be the map induced by the map
[1]_{\mathcal{S}}\longrightarrow\mathcal{E}
. Then the induced map
\Sigma(\varphi):\Sigma(\mathcal{A})\longrightarrow\Sigma(\mathcal{B})
is a weak equivalence in
\Op(\mathcal{S})_{\mathcal{P}//\mathcal{P}}
.

Proof.

In fact, this claim can be derived from the proof of [
[
14
]
, Lemma 5.2.10]. For readers’ convenience, we still present here a detailed proof. Observe first that the map
\varphi
can be equivalently rewritten as the map

\overline{\mathcal{A}}\;\underset{\mathcal{I}_{C}}{\coprod}\mathcal{P}\longrightarrow\overline{\mathcal{B}}\;\underset{\mathcal{I}_{C}}{\coprod}\mathcal{P}

in which
\overline{\mathcal{A}}:=\mathcal{P}\underset{\mathcal{I}_{C}}{\coprod}(\mathcal{I}_{C}\otimes[1]_{\mathcal{S}})
and
\overline{\mathcal{B}}:=\mathcal{P}\underset{\mathcal{I}_{C}}{\coprod}(\mathcal{I}_{C}\otimes\mathcal{E})
. Accordingly,
\varphi
is a homotopy cobase change in
\Op(\mathcal{S})_{\mathcal{P}//\mathcal{P}}
of the map
\overline{\varphi}:\overline{\mathcal{A}}\longrightarrow\overline{\mathcal{B}}
. Thus we just need to prove that the map
\Sigma(\overline{\varphi})
is a weak equivalence. Moreover, note that
\overline{\varphi}
coincides with the (derived) image of the map
\psi:\mathcal{I}_{C}\otimes[1]_{\mathcal{S}}\longrightarrow\mathcal{I}_{C}\otimes\mathcal{E}
through the left Quillen functor
\eta_{!}:\Op(\mathcal{S})_{\mathcal{I}_{C}//\mathcal{I}_{C}}\longrightarrow\Op(\mathcal{S})_{\mathcal{P}//\mathcal{P}}
that is induced by the unit map
\mathcal{I}_{C}\longrightarrow\mathcal{P}
. Therefore, it will suffice to verify that
\Sigma(\psi)
is a weak equivalence (in
\Cat(\mathcal{S})_{\mathcal{I}_{C}//\mathcal{I}_{C}}
). By the weak equivalence
\mathcal{I}_{C}\otimes\mathcal{E}\overset{\simeq}{\longrightarrow}\mathcal{I}_{C}
, we just need to show that the fold map

\mathcal{I}_{C}\otimes\mathcal{E}\underset{\mathcal{I}_{C}\otimes[1]_{\mathcal{S}}}{\coprod}\mathcal{I}_{C}\otimes\mathcal{E}\longrightarrow\mathcal{I}_{C}\otimes\mathcal{E}

is a weak equivalence. But this is equivalent to saying that the fold map
\mathcal{E}\underset{[1]_{\mathcal{S}}}{\coprod}\mathcal{E}\longrightarrow\mathcal{E}
is a weak equivalence, which immediately follows from the invertibility hypothesis on
\mathcal{S}
.
∎

Remark 4.28
.

Notice that there is a weak equivalence
\overline{\mathcal{B}}\overset{\simeq}{\longrightarrow}\mathcal{P}
that follows from the weak equivalence
\mathcal{I}_{C}\otimes\mathcal{E}\overset{\simeq}{\longrightarrow}\mathcal{I}_{C}
. This implies the existence of a weak equivalence
\mathcal{B}\overset{\simeq}{\longrightarrow}\mathcal{P}\underset{\mathcal{I}_{C}}{\coprod}\mathcal{P}
in
\Op(\mathcal{S})_{\mathcal{P}//\mathcal{P}}
. We now obtain a sequence of maps
\mathcal{A}\longrightarrow\mathcal{B}\overset{\simeq}{\longrightarrow}\mathcal{P}\underset{\mathcal{I}_{C}}{\coprod}\mathcal{P}
, each of which becomes a weak equivalence after taking suspension:

\Sigma(\mathcal{A})\overset{\simeq}{\longrightarrow}\Sigma(\mathcal{B})\overset{\simeq}{\longrightarrow}\Sigma(\mathcal{P}\underset{\mathcal{I}_{C}}{\coprod}\mathcal{P}).

In light of this, we will regard
\Sigma(\mathcal{A})
and
\Sigma(\mathcal{B})
as two convenient models for the suspension
\Sigma(\mathcal{P}\underset{\mathcal{I}_{C}}{\coprod}\mathcal{P})
.

Remark 4.29
.

We will be also interested in the following objects in
\Op(\mathcal{S})_{\mathcal{P}//\mathcal{P}}
:

\mathcal{U}:=\mathcal{P}\underset{\mathcal{I}_{C}\coprod\mathcal{I}_{C}}{\coprod}\mathcal{I}_{C}\otimes[1]_{\mathcal{S}}\;\;\;\text{and}\;\;\;\mathcal{V}:=\mathcal{P}\underset{\mathcal{I}_{C}\coprod\mathcal{I}_{C}}{\coprod}\mathcal{I}_{C}\otimes\mathcal{E}\;.

As above, these two objects have already the right homotopy type. Additionally, there is a canonical map
\rho:\mathcal{U}\longrightarrow\mathcal{V}
that is induced by the map
[1]_{\mathcal{S}}\longrightarrow\mathcal{E}
. Moreover, a similar argument to that in the above proof shows that the map

\Sigma(\rho):\Sigma(\mathcal{U})\overset{\simeq}{\longrightarrow}\Sigma(\mathcal{V})

is a weak equivalence as well.

We will be in position to prove the main theorem of this subsection after a more comprehensive discussion below.

By adjunctions, we have a chain of weak equivalences

\Map^{\h}_{\Op_{C}(\mathcal{S})}(\mathcal{P},f^{*}\mathcal{O})\,\simeq\,\Map^{\h}_{\Op_{C}(\mathcal{S})_{\mathcal{P}/}}(\mathcal{P}\bigsqcup\mathcal{P},f^{*}\mathcal{O})\,\simeq\,\Map^{\h}_{\Op(\mathcal{S})_{\mathcal{P}/}}(\mathcal{P}\underset{\mathcal{I}_{C}}{\coprod}\mathcal{P},\mathcal{O}).

Here we made use of the fact that the coproduct
\mathcal{P}\bigsqcup\mathcal{P}\in\Op_{C}(\mathcal{S})
, when considered as an object of
\Op(\mathcal{S})
, agrees with the pushout
\mathcal{P}\underset{\mathcal{I}_{C}}{\coprod}\mathcal{P}\in\Op(\mathcal{S})
. In the same fashion, we may obtain that

\underset{c\in C}{\prod}\Map^{\h}_{\mathcal{S}}(1_{\mathcal{S}},f^{*}\mathcal{O}(c;c))\cong\Map^{\h}_{\Op_{C}(\mathcal{S})}(\Free(\mathcal{I}_{C}),f^{*}\mathcal{O})\simeq\Map^{\h}_{\Op(\mathcal{S})_{\mathcal{P}/}}(\mathcal{P}\underset{\mathcal{I}_{C}}{\coprod}\Free(\mathcal{I}_{C}),\mathcal{O})

in which
\Free(\mathcal{I}_{C})
signifies the free
C
-colored operad generated by the
C
-collection
\mathcal{I}_{C}
. Thus we may rewrite the fiber sequence (
4.1
) for the map
\mathcal{P}\longrightarrow f^{*}\mathcal{O}
as

\Omega\Map^{\h}_{\Op(\mathcal{S})_{\mathcal{P}/}}(\mathcal{P}\underset{\mathcal{I}_{C}}{\coprod}\mathcal{P},\mathcal{O})\longrightarrow\Map^{\h}_{\BMod(\mathcal{P})}(\mathcal{P},f^{*}\mathcal{O})\longrightarrow\Map^{\h}_{\Op(\mathcal{S})_{\mathcal{P}/}}(\mathcal{P}\underset{\mathcal{I}_{C}}{\coprod}\Free(\mathcal{I}_{C}),\mathcal{O}).

On other hand, the fiber sequence (
4.10
) can be equivalently rewritten as

\Map^{\h}_{\Op(\mathcal{S})_{\mathcal{P}/}}(\mathcal{P}\underset{\mathcal{I}_{C}}{\coprod}\mathcal{P},\mathcal{O})\longrightarrow\Map^{\h}_{\Op(\mathcal{S})}(\mathcal{P},\mathcal{O})\longrightarrow\Map^{\h}_{\Op(\mathcal{S})_{\mathcal{P}/}}(\mathcal{P}\coprod\mathcal{I}_{C},\mathcal{O}).

Consequently, we obtain two fiber sequences as follows

\Omega\Map^{\h}_{\BMod(\mathcal{P})}(\mathcal{P},f^{*}\mathcal{O})\longrightarrow\Omega\Map^{\h}_{\Op(\mathcal{S})_{\mathcal{P}/}}(\mathcal{P}\underset{\mathcal{I}_{C}}{\coprod}\Free(\mathcal{I}_{C}),\mathcal{O})\overset{\alpha}{\longrightarrow}\Omega\Map^{\h}_{\Op(\mathcal{S})_{\mathcal{P}/}}(\mathcal{P}\underset{\mathcal{I}_{C}}{\coprod}\mathcal{P},\mathcal{O})

\Omega^{2}\Map^{\h}_{\Op(\mathcal{S})}(\mathcal{P},\mathcal{O})\longrightarrow\Omega^{2}\Map^{\h}_{\Op(\mathcal{S})_{\mathcal{P}/}}(\mathcal{P}\coprod\mathcal{I}_{C},\mathcal{O})\overset{\beta}{\longrightarrow}\Omega\Map^{\h}_{\Op(\mathcal{S})_{\mathcal{P}/}}(\mathcal{P}\underset{\mathcal{I}_{C}}{\coprod}\mathcal{P},\mathcal{O}).

For the proof below, the key step is to find convenient models for the two maps
\alpha
and
\beta
, and additionally, to show how they can be related via a pair of natural weak equivalences.

Proof of Theorem
4.26
.

Notice first that, when considered as an object of
\Op(\mathcal{S})
, the operad
\Free(\mathcal{I}_{C})
can be modeled by

\Free(\mathcal{I}_{C})\,\cong\,\mathcal{I}_{C}\,\underset{\mathcal{I}_{C}\coprod\mathcal{I}_{C}}{\coprod}\,\mathcal{I}_{C}\otimes[1]_{\mathcal{S}}\in\Op(\mathcal{S}).

Due to this, we obtain an identification

(4.14)

\mathcal{P}\underset{\mathcal{I}_{C}}{\coprod}\Free(\mathcal{I}_{C})\cong\mathcal{P}\underset{\mathcal{I}_{C}\coprod\mathcal{I}_{C}}{\coprod}\mathcal{I}_{C}\otimes[1]_{\mathcal{S}}\;\overset{\defi}{=}\;\mathcal{U}.

Next, because of the fact that
\mathcal{P}\coprod\mathcal{I}_{C}
agrees with the (derived) image of
\mathcal{I}_{C}\coprod\mathcal{I}_{C}
through the functor
\eta_{!}:\Op(\mathcal{S})_{\mathcal{I}_{C}//\mathcal{I}_{C}}\longrightarrow\Op(\mathcal{S})_{\mathcal{P}//\mathcal{P}}
, we obtain that

(4.15)

\Sigma(\mathcal{P}\coprod\mathcal{I}_{C})\simeq\eta_{!}\Sigma(\mathcal{I}_{C}\coprod\mathcal{I}_{C})\simeq\eta_{!}(\mathcal{I}_{C}\underset{\mathcal{I}_{C}\coprod\mathcal{I}_{C}}{\coprod}\mathcal{I}_{C}\otimes\mathcal{E})\cong\mathcal{P}\underset{\mathcal{I}_{C}\coprod\mathcal{I}_{C}}{\coprod}\mathcal{I}_{C}\otimes\mathcal{E}\;\overset{\defi}{=}\;\mathcal{V}.

Let us now consider the following commutative square in
\Op(\mathcal{S})_{\mathcal{P}/}
:

(4.16)

\textstyle{\Sigma(\mathcal{A})\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\Sigma(\overline{\alpha})}
\scriptstyle{\Sigma(\varphi)}
\scriptstyle{\simeq}
\textstyle{\Sigma(\mathcal{U})\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\simeq}
\scriptstyle{\Sigma(\rho)}
\textstyle{\Sigma(\mathcal{B})\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\Sigma(\overline{\beta})}
\textstyle{\Sigma(\mathcal{V})\;}

such that the maps
\overline{\alpha}:\mathcal{A}\longrightarrow\mathcal{U}
and
\overline{\beta}:\mathcal{B}\longrightarrow\mathcal{V}
are both induced by the fold map
\mathcal{P}\coprod\mathcal{P}\longrightarrow\mathcal{P}
, and the vertical maps are all weak equivalences as indicated earlier. The map
\Sigma(\overline{\alpha})
induces a map between derived mapping spaces

\Map^{\h}_{\Op(\mathcal{S})_{\mathcal{P}/}}(\Sigma(\mathcal{U}),\mathcal{O})\longrightarrow\Map^{\h}_{\Op(\mathcal{S})_{\mathcal{P}/}}(\Sigma(\mathcal{A}),\mathcal{O})

whose domain is weakly equivalent to
\Omega\Map^{\h}_{\Op(\mathcal{S})_{\mathcal{P}/}}(\mathcal{P}\underset{\mathcal{I}_{C}}{\coprod}\Free(\mathcal{I}_{C}),\mathcal{O})
due to (
4.14
), and whose codomain is weakly equivalent to
\Omega\Map^{\h}_{\Op(\mathcal{S})_{\mathcal{P}/}}(\mathcal{P}\underset{\mathcal{I}_{C}}{\coprod}\mathcal{P},\mathcal{O})
by Remark
4.28
. Moreover, the above map stands as a natural model for the map
\alpha
. In addition, we may show that the induced map

\Map^{\h}_{\Op(\mathcal{S})_{\mathcal{P}/}}(\Sigma(\mathcal{V}),\mathcal{O})\longrightarrow\Map^{\h}_{\Op(\mathcal{S})_{\mathcal{P}/}}(\Sigma(\mathcal{B}),\mathcal{O})

is weakly equivalent to the map
\beta
, according to (
4.15
) and Remark
4.28
again. In summary, we now obtain a commutative square

\textstyle{\Map^{\h}_{\Op(\mathcal{S})_{\mathcal{P}/}}(\Sigma(\mathcal{V}),\mathcal{O})\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\simeq}
\textstyle{\Map^{\h}_{\Op(\mathcal{S})_{\mathcal{P}/}}(\Sigma(\mathcal{B}),\mathcal{O})\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\simeq}
\textstyle{\Map^{\h}_{\Op(\mathcal{S})_{\mathcal{P}/}}(\Sigma(\mathcal{U}),\mathcal{O})\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{\Map^{\h}_{\Op(\mathcal{S})_{\mathcal{P}/}}(\Sigma(\mathcal{A}),\mathcal{O})\;}

such that the top and bottom horizontal maps are respectively weakly equivalent to

and
\alpha
, and while the two vertical maps are all weak equivalences. Thus we get a natural weak equivalence between homotopy fibers

\Omega^{2}\Map^{\h}_{\Op(\mathcal{S})}(\mathcal{P},\mathcal{O})\overset{\simeq}{\longrightarrow}\Omega\Map^{\h}_{\BMod(\mathcal{P})}(\mathcal{P},f^{*}\mathcal{O})

as expected.
∎

Remark 4.30
.

As in Lemma

holds for every

Example 4.31
.

Let us apply Theorem

to the case where

, i.e. the initial single-colored operad in

, and where

Now let

This assertion is in general not a trivial one, even though it could be verified somehow more directly.

Example 4.32

Continuing from the above example, let us proceed by considering a model category

Example 4.33
.

We may use Theorem
4.26
to investigate mapping spaces between
\infty
-categories (or simplicial categories, equivalently). Suppose we are given a functor

\infty
-categories. We will write

to denote the

-category of spaces. We may think of the category

\mathcal{C}
-bimodules. Then we may regard

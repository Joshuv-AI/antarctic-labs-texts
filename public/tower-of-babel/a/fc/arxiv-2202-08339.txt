Dimensions on Lattice Ordered Abelian Groups and Model Theory of Modules over Prüfer Domains. Thanks: The author was supported by PRIN 2017-Mathematical Logic: models, sets, computability.
Lorna Gregory

Abstract
. We prove a transfer theorem which, when combined with the Jaffard-Kaplansky-Ohm Theorem, allows results in model theory of modules over Bézout domains to be translated into results over Prüfer domains via their value groups. Extending work of Puninski and Toffalori, we show that the extended positive cone of the value group of a Prüfer domain has m-dimension if and only if its lattice of pp- 1 1 -formulae has breadth (equivalently width) and that these dimensions are equal. Further, we show that the existence of these dimensions is equivalent to the lattice of pp- 1 1 -formulae having m-dimension (and hence to its Ziegler spectrum having Cantor-Bendixson rank) and the non-existence of superdecomposable pure-injective modules. Finally, we give a best possible upper bound for the m-dimension of the pp- 1 1 -lattice of a Prüfer domain in terms of the m-dimension of the extended positive cone of its value group and show that all ordinals which are not of the form λ + 1 \lambda+1 for λ \lambda a limit ordinal occur as the m-dimension of the pp- 1 1 -lattice of a Bézout domain.

It is often the case that results in model theory of modules over Bézout domains can be proved, with sometimes significantly more effort, for Prüfer domains. The first main contribution of this paper is to prove a transfer theorem which allows results in model theory of modules over Bézout domains to be translated into results over Prüfer domains.

A Bézout domain is an integral domain whose finitely generated ideals are principal and a Prüfer domain is an integral domain such that all its localisations at maximal ideals are valuation domains. Bézout domains are the non-Noetherian analogues of principal ideal domains and Prüfer domains are non-Noetherian analogues of Dedekind domains.
Many classically important rings are Bézout or Prüfer domains. They include Dedekind domains and hence rings of integers of number fields; the ring of complex entire functions
[

, Thm. 9]
and the ring of algebraic integers
[

; the ring of integer valued polynomials with rational coefficients
[

Remark 2.1

Let

(2)

\bigwedge_{i=1}xA_{i}=0+B_{i}|x\leq xC=0+D|x

(3)

C\subseteq\sum_{i=1}^{n}(CD:B_{i})\cap A_{i}+CD

Proof.

Prest’s duality implies that

and

are equivalent. We now show that

and

are equivalent. Let

-module. For any finitely generated ideal

if and only if

for all indecomposable pure-injective modules and all indecomposable pure-injective

By

for a prime ideals

The following remark is easy to show for valuation rings and hence follows for arithmetical rings using

Remark 2.6
.

Let

Lemma 2.7
.

Let
R
be an arithmetical ring and let

be finitely generated ideals. Then

if and only if

. This is further equivalent to

Proof.

Let

. Then

\sum_{i=1}^{n}b_{i}R|x\wedge x0=0+R|x\wedge xA=0.

So, by

,

For any ring

, it is easy to see that

implies

. Suppose

. Then, since

C\subseteq(AB+CD:A)=(AB:A)+(CD:A)=(B:A)+(0:A)+(CD:A)=(B:A)+(CD:A).

The first equality follows from

and the second two follow from
2.6
.

Now suppose that

is a Prüfer domain and

. Then, by
1.1
,
R=(AC:AC)\subseteq(AB:AC)+(CD:AC)
. If

or

then

. So suppose that

. By

and

. Therefore
(B:C)+(D:A)=R
.

Now suppose that

It follows from

Definition 2.9

(1)

Let

(1)

Let

We are ready to prove our transfer theorem.
The theorem has essentially already been established. We just need to translate what we have shown into the value group.

Theorem 2.17
.

Let
R,S

\ell

\widehat{t}:\texttt{Filters}(\Gamma(R)_{\infty}^{+})\rightarrow\texttt{Filters}(\Gamma(S)_{\infty}^{+}),\ \ \ \mathcal{F}\mapsto\{A\in\Gamma(S)^{+}\ |\ t(A)\in\mathcal{F}\}\cup\{\infty\}

then

.

We now prove by ordinal induction that for all

if and only if

. Since

for all

, it’s enough to show that for all

,

if and only if

. The base case,

\sim_{\alpha}
and
C_{\alpha}
. Suppose

and that we have proved the statement for

if and only if
\mathcal{L}\text{-}\dim[0/\sim_{\beta},c/\sim_{\beta}]=0
. By the induction hypothesis,

if and only if

if and only if

.

The statement
(i)
is a direct consequence of the main statement. For
(ii)
, first suppose
\Gamma_{\infty}^{+}
has
\mathcal{L}
-dimension
\alpha
. Then
\mathcal{L}\text{-}\dim(\Gamma/C_{\mathcal{L},\alpha})_{\infty}^{+}=0
. Thus, for all

,

. Therefore

. For the converse, suppose
\mathcal{L}\text{-}\dim\Gamma_{\infty}^{+}=\infty

(\Gamma/C_{\mathcal{L},\alpha})_{\infty}^{+}\cong\Gamma_{\infty}^{+}/\sim_{\mathcal{L},\alpha}
for all

6.
Calculating the breadth

Throughout, unless otherwise stated,
R
is a Bézout domain.
The main purpose of this section is to show that the breadth of

\Gamma(R)_{\infty}^{+}
when

, which implies the statement for Bézout domains. We then use our transfer theorem

to extend the result to Prüfer domains.

Proposition 6.1
.

Let
R
be a Bézout domain which is not a field and let
S:=S(R)
. The surjective lattice homomorphism

\pi_{S}:\textnormal{pp}_{R}^{1}\rightarrow\textnormal{pp}_{R_{S}}^{1}

induced by restriction of scalars along the epimorphism

is such that for all
\varphi,\psi\in\textnormal{pp}_{R}^{1}
,
\pi_{S}(\varphi)=\pi_{S}(\psi)
if and only if the interval
[\varphi\wedge\psi,\varphi+\psi]
has breadth
0
.

In order to prove the proposition we need to show:

(1)

If
[\psi,\varphi]
is totally ordered then
[\pi_{S}(\psi),\pi_{S}(\varphi)]
is the trivial interval.

(2)

If
\psi\leq\varphi
and
\pi_{S}(\varphi)=\pi_{S}(\psi)
then
[\psi,\varphi]
has has breadth
0
.

Remark 6.2
.

Let
L
be a modular lattice,
a_{1},\ldots,a_{n}\in L
and
b_{1},\ldots,b_{m}\in L
. If
[(\bigsqcup_{i=1}^{n}a_{i})\sqcap\bigsqcap_{j=1}^{m}b_{j},\bigsqcup_{i=1}^{n}a_{i}]
is a chain then
[b_{j}\sqcap a_{i},a_{i}]
is a chain for all
1\leq i\leq n
and
1\leq j\leq m
.

Lemma 6.3
.

Suppose
R
is not a field. Let

with
g,h\neq 0
be such that
aR+dR=R
. If the interval

[(awg|x+xh=0)\wedge g|x\wedge xdwh=0,g|x\wedge xdwh=0],

is trivial then
w
is a unit and if it is a chain then either
w
is a unit of

or
w
is irreducible.

Proof.

The first statement follows from
2.7
because
(dwhR:h)+(awgR:g)=w(dR+aR)
.

The condition
aR+dR=R
implies that either
a\neq 0
or

. We will prove the second claim of the lemma under the assumption that
a\neq 0
(Prest’s duality interchanges this case with the case where

).

Suppose that
w
is not a unit and
w
is not irreducible. There exists non-units

with

such that

. Let
\varphi
be

and
\psi
be
auvg|x+xh=0
. Let
\varphi_{1}
be
avg|x+xh=0
and let
\varphi_{2}
be
auvg|x+xvh=0
.

Note that
\varphi_{1},\varphi_{2}\geq\psi
. Hence it is enough to show that
\varphi_{1}\wedge\varphi
and
\varphi_{2}\wedge\varphi
are incomparable elements of
\textnormal{pp}^{1}_{R}
.
Using the distributivity of

, it is easy to see that

and

avg|x\wedge xduvh=0\nleq\varphi_{2}

Let

. In

. Now

Claim 2:

Let

. In

By claim

Lemma 6.5
.

Let

. Then

. Therefore, for all

is a unit and hence

is equivalent to

, or,

. So we may assume

Proposition 6.6

N\in\left(\nicefrac{{x=x}}{{c|x}}\right)

Corollary 7.12
.

Let
R
be a Bézout domain which is not a field. If
N\in\textnormal{Zg}(R)
is isolated then there exists

irreducible such that

. In particular,
\textnormal{Zg}(R_{S})\subseteq\textnormal{Zg}(R)^{(1)}
.

Recall that the foundation rank

of an element

is defined inductively as follows:
\textnormal{fdrk\,}a=0
if and only if there are no elements

, and, for any ordinal

Proposition 7.13
.

Suppose that
\Gamma(R)_{\infty}^{+}
has m-dimension. The CB rank of

is less than or equal to the foundation rank of
(\textnormal{rk}\textnormal{Ass}N,\textnormal{rk}\textnormal{Div}N)
in
\text{Ord}^{2}

We show that for all

, if

then

then
\textnormal{rk}\textnormal{Ass}N=\textnormal{rk}\textnormal{Div}N=0
. By

,

\textnormal{Zg}(R)

\textnormal{Zg}(R)^{(\beta)}
. By

and

,

for an admissible pair
(I,J)
of weakly prime ideals with

and

. Let

(respectively

) be such that

and

(respectively

and

), and, such that for all weakly prime

\textnormal{rk}K^{\#}\geq\textnormal{rk}I^{\#}
(respectively
\textnormal{rk}K^{\#}\geq\textnormal{rk}J^{\#}

and

(respectively

and

(respectively

We now show that if

then it is isolated by

\mathcal{W}:=\left(\nicefrac{{b|x\wedge xac=0}}{{xa=0+bd|x}}\right)

in

The upper bound for the CB rank of the Ziegler spectrum in the previous proposition also holds for Prüfer domains by
2.17
. By
6.9
, the m-dimension of
\Gamma(R)_{\infty}^{+}
is a lower bound for the m-dimension of
\textnormal{pp}_{R}^{1}
. Hence, by
3.7
, also a lower bound for
\textnormal{CBrank}(\textnormal{Zg}(R))
.

Corollary 7.14
.

Let
R
be a Prüfer domain. If
\Gamma(R)_{\infty}^{+}
has m-dimension
\alpha
then

\alpha\leq\textnormal{CBrank}(\textnormal{Zg}(R))\leq\alpha\cdot 2.

We now reinterpret a result of Puninski, which computes the CB ranks of Ziegler spectra of valuation domains, to show that the upper bound in the previous corollary is the best possible bound on the CB rank of
\textnormal{Zg}(R)
in terms of the m-dimension of
\Gamma(R)_{\infty}^{+}
.

Let
S
be a ring. For each ordinal
\alpha
, we define an ideal
J(\alpha)(S)\lhd S
by ordinal induction. Define
J(0)(S)
to be the Jacobson radical of
S
and
J(\alpha+1)(S):=\bigcap_{n\in\mathbb{N}}J(\alpha)(S)^{n}
. If
\beta
is a limit ordinal then let
J(\beta)(S):=\bigcap_{\alpha<\beta}J(\alpha)(S)
.

Theorem 7.15
.

[
Pun99
, 2.1 & 3.6]

Let
V
be a valuation domain. The Ziegler spectrum of
V
has CB rank if and only if there exists an ordinal
\alpha
such that
J(\alpha)(V)=0
. Moreover, the CB rank of
\textnormal{Zg}(V)
is
\alpha\cdot 2
if and only if
\alpha
is the least ordinal such that
J(\alpha)(V)=0
.

We will show that the least ordinal
\alpha
such that
J(\alpha)(V)=0
is equal to
\textnormal{mdim}\Gamma(V)_{\infty}^{+}
. In order to do this we need a lemma.

Lemma 7.16
.

Let
V
be a valuation domain and
\alpha\in\textnormal{Ord}
. Then
J(\alpha)(V)
is prime and
J(\alpha+1)(V)=J(1)(V_{J(\alpha)(V)})
as subsets of the field of fractions of
V
.

Proof.

The intersection of a set of prime ideals of a valuation domain
V
is a prime ideal and,
[
FS01
, Ch II 1.3(c)]
, for any ideal
I\lhd V
,
\bigcap_{n\in\mathbb{N}}I^{n}
is prime. Therefore
J(\alpha)(V)
is prime for all
\alpha\in\textnormal{Ord}
. Since
V
is a valuation domain, for all prime ideals
\mathfrak{p}\lhd V
,
\mathfrak{p}V_{\mathfrak{p}}=\mathfrak{p}
as subsets of the field of fractions of
V
. Therefore for all
\alpha\in\textnormal{Ord}
,

J(\alpha+1)(V)=\bigcap_{n\in\mathbb{N}}J(\alpha)(V)^{n}=\bigcap_{n\in\mathbb{N}}(J(\alpha)(V)V_{J(\alpha)(V)})^{n}=J(1)(V_{J(\alpha)(V)}).

∎

Corollary 7.17
.

Let
V
be a valuation domain such that
\Gamma(V)_{\infty}^{+}
has m-dimension
\alpha
. Then
\textnormal{Zg}(V)
has CB rank
\alpha\cdot 2
.

Proof.

We show that for all
\alpha\in\textnormal{Ord}
,
S_{\alpha}(V)=V\backslash J(\alpha)(V)
. The result then follows from Puninski’s result, since, by
5.4
, the m-dimension of
\Gamma(V)_{\infty}^{+}
is the least ordinal
\alpha
such that
S_{\alpha}(V)=V\backslash\{0\}
.

By definition,
S_{0}(V)
is the set of units of
V
and, since
V
is local,
J(0)(V)
is the set of non-units of
V
. So
S_{0}(V)=V\backslash J(0)(V)
.

If
J(0)(V)^{2}=J(0)(V)
then either
J(0)(V)=0
or
J(0)(V)
is not finitely generated. In either case,
J(0)(V)=J(1)(V)
and
S_{0}(V)=S_{1}(V)
since
V
has no irreducible elements. Therefore
V\backslash J(1)(V)=S_{1}(V)
.

If
J(0)(V)^{2}\neq J(0)(V)
then
J(0)(V)=cV
for some non-zero
c\in V
. Since
J(0)(V)
is maximal,
c
is irreducible. Then
a\notin J(1)(V)
if and only if
a\in V\backslash c^{n}V
for some
n\in\mathbb{N}
if and only if
a=c^{m}u
for some
m\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
and
u
a unit. Since
J(0)(V)
is maximal,
c
is the unique irreducible element of
V
and hence
a\in S_{1}(V)
if and only if
a=uc^{m}
for some
m\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
and unit
u
. Therefore
V\backslash J(1)(V)=S_{1}(V)
.

So we have proved that
S_{\alpha}(V)=V\backslash J(\alpha)(V)
for
\alpha=1
. We now proceed by ordinal induction.
It follows from the definitions of
J(\alpha)(V)
and
S_{\alpha}(V)
that if
\lambda
is a limit ordinal and
S_{\alpha}(V)=V\backslash J(\alpha)(V)
for all
\alpha<\lambda
then
S_{\lambda}(V)=V\backslash J(\lambda)(V)
.

Suppose
S_{\alpha}(V)=V\backslash J(\alpha)(V)
. Then

S_{\alpha+1}(V)=S_{1}(V_{S_{\alpha}(V)})\cap V=[V_{S_{\alpha}(V)}\backslash J(1)(V_{S_{\alpha}(V)})]\cap V=V\backslash J(\alpha+1)(V).

The first equality is by definition of
S_{\alpha+1}(V)
, the second follows from the claim with
\alpha=1
and the third follows from
7.16
.
∎

We now calculate the CB rank of
\textnormal{Zg}(R)
in the extreme opposite case, that is, when
R
is a Prüfer domain of Krull dimension
1
. The first case is straight forward.

Remark 7.18
.

Let
R
be a Prüfer domain. If
\textnormal{mdim}\Gamma(R)_{\infty}^{+}=1
then
R
is a Dedekind domain which is not a field. Hence, if
\textnormal{mdim}\Gamma(R)_{\infty}^{+}=1
then
\textnormal{Zg}(R)
has CB rank
2
.

Proof.

We just need to show that
R
is Noetherian. Since
\textnormal{mdim}\Gamma(R)_{\infty}^{+}=1
,
\Gamma(R)
is generated by elements
a\in\Gamma(R)^{+}
such that
[0,a]
is a simple interval. Thus, every multiplication prime filter of
\Gamma(R)_{\infty}^{+}
is either
\{\infty\}
or contains such an element. In the second case, the filter is of the form
\{b\in\Gamma(R)_{\infty}^{+}\ |\ b\geq a\}
. It follows that all prime ideals of
R
are finitely generated and hence that
R
is Noetherian. It is well known, see for instance
[
Pre09
, 5.2.4]
, that the Ziegler spectra of Dedekind domains have CB rank
2
.
∎

Remark 7.19
.

If
R
is a Prüfer domain of Krull dimension
1
such that
\Gamma(R)^{+}_{\infty}
has m-dimension then for all maximal ideals
\mathfrak{m}\lhd R
,
R_{\mathfrak{m}}
is a discrete valuation domain.

Proof.

Since
\Gamma(R_{\mathfrak{m}})_{\infty}^{+}
is a quotient of
\Gamma(R)_{\infty}^{+}
, it has m-dimension and hence
\mathfrak{m}R_{\mathfrak{m}}
is a principal ideal. Therefore, since
R
has Krull dimension
1
,
R_{\mathfrak{m}}
is a discrete valuation domain.
∎

Lemma 7.20
.

Let
R
be a Prüfer domain of Krull dimension
1
and such that
\Gamma(R)^{+}_{\infty}
has m-dimension. For all
N\in\textnormal{Zg}(R)
, either

(1)

\textnormal{Ass}N=\textnormal{Div}N
;

(2)

\textnormal{Ass}N=0
and hence
N=N(0,\textnormal{Div}N)
; or

(3)

\textnormal{Div}N=0
and hence
N=N(\textnormal{Ass}N,0)
.

Proof.

Since
\textnormal{Ass}N,\textnormal{Div}N
are prime ideals and
\textnormal{Ass}N\cup\textnormal{Div}N=\textnormal{Att}N
is a prime ideal, it follows directly from the assumption that
R
has Krull dimension
1
that either
\textnormal{Ass}N=\textnormal{Div}N
,
\textnormal{Ass}N=0
or
\textnormal{Div}N=0
.

Suppose
N
is an indecomposable pure-injective
R
-module and
\textnormal{Ass}N=0
. Then
N=N(0,I)
. Now either
\textnormal{Div}N=0
or
\mathfrak{m}:=\textnormal{Div}N
is a maximal ideal. In the first case
I=0
. In the second case, since
R_{\mathfrak{m}}
is a discrete valuation domain, all non-zero ideals of
R_{\mathfrak{m}}
are of the form
c^{n}R_{\mathfrak{m}}
for some
c\in R
. Therefore
I=c^{n}R_{\mathfrak{m}}\cap R
. It is easy to check that
(I:c^{n-1})=cR_{\mathfrak{m}}\cap R=\mathfrak{m}
. Therefore, by
2.14
,
N(0,I)\cong N(0,\mathfrak{m})
as required.
∎

For each ordinal
\alpha
with
\text{m-dim}\Gamma(R)_{\infty}^{+}\geq\alpha+1
, define

\Delta_{\alpha}(R):=\{N(\mathfrak{m},0),N(0,\mathfrak{m})\ |\ \mathfrak{m}\lhd R\text{ is a maximal ideal and }\textnormal{rk}\,\mathfrak{m}=\alpha\}.

The next remark follows from
7.4
and
7.7
.

Remark 7.21
.

Let
R
be a Bézout domain of Krull dimension
1
. For all ordinals
\alpha
with
\textnormal{mdim}\Gamma(R)_{\infty}^{+}\geq\alpha+1
, viewing
\textnormal{Zg}(R_{S_{\alpha}})
as a subspace of
\textnormal{Zg}(R)
,

\Delta_{\alpha}(R)=\Delta_{0}(R_{S_{\alpha}}).

We now compute the first two Cantor-Bendixson derivatives of
\textnormal{Zg}(R)
. This will form the base case for an inductive description of the higher Cantor-Bendixson derivatives in
7.23
.

Lemma 7.22
.

Let
R
be a Bézout domain of Krull dimension
1
and such that
\Gamma(R)_{\infty}^{+}
has m-dimension.

(1)_{0,0}

If
\textnormal{mdim}\Gamma(R)_{\infty}^{+}\geq 1
then

\textnormal{Zg}(R)^{(1)}=\textnormal{Zg}(R_{S_{1}})\cup\Delta_{0}(R).

(1)_{0,1}

If
\textnormal{mdim}\Gamma(R)_{\infty}^{+}\geq 2
then
\textnormal{Zg}(R)^{(2)}=\textnormal{Zg}(R_{S_{1}})^{(1)}
.

Proof.

(1)_{0,0}

By
7.11
,
N\in\textnormal{Zg}(R)
is isolated if and only if
\textnormal{rk}\textnormal{Ass}N=\textnormal{rk}\textnormal{Div}N=0
. By
7.7
,

if and only if
\textnormal{rk}\textnormal{Ass}N>0
and
\textnormal{rk}\textnormal{Div}N>0

,
\textnormal{rk}0>0
. So, by
7.20
,
N\in\textnormal{Zg}(R)^{(1)}\backslash\textnormal{Zg}(R_{S_{1}})
if and only if

or
N=N(\mathfrak{m},0)
for some maximal ideal
\mathfrak{m}
with
\textnormal{rk}\mathfrak{m}=0
.

(1)_{0,1}

Using
(1)_{0,0}
, it is enough to show that all points in
\Delta_{0}(R)
are isolated in
\textnormal{Zg}(R)^{(1)}
and that if a point is isolated in
\textnormal{Zg}(R_{S_{1}})
then it is isolated in
\textnormal{Zg}(R)^{(1)}
.

Suppose
\mathfrak{m}\lhd R
is a maximal ideal with
\textnormal{rk}\mathfrak{m}=0
. There exists

such that

. Now

if and only if

and hence

. Therefore, by

,

if and only if either

or

. So

isolates

in
\textnormal{Zg}(R)^{(1)}
.

Now suppose that
N\in\textnormal{Zg}(R_{S_{1}})
is isolated in
\textnormal{Zg}(R_{S_{1}})
. Since
\textnormal{mdim}\Gamma(R)_{\infty}^{+}\geq 2
,
\textnormal{mdim}\Gamma(R_{S_{1}})_{\infty}^{+}\geq 1
and hence
R_{S_{1}}
is not a field. By

,
\textnormal{Ass}N=\textnormal{Div}N=cR_{S_{1}}\cap R
for some

which is irreducible as an element of

. Let
\mathcal{U}
be an open subset of

such that

For all

, either
\textnormal{Ass}M=0
or
\textnormal{Div}M=0
. So, in particular, either

or

. Therefore

\mathcal{W}\cap\textnormal{Zg}(R)^{(1)}=\{N\}
.
∎

We will now compute the Cantor-Bendixson derivatives of
\textnormal{Zg}(R)
.

Proposition 7.23
.

Let
R
be a Bézout domain of Krull dimension
1
such that
\Gamma(R)_{\infty}^{+}
has m-dimension.
Let
\lambda
be a limit ordinal and
n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
.

(0)_{\lambda}

If
\textnormal{mdim}\Gamma(R)_{\infty}^{+}\geq\lambda
then
\textnormal{Zg}(R)^{(\lambda)}=\textnormal{Zg}(R_{S_{\lambda}})
.

(1)_{\lambda,n}

If
\textnormal{mdim}\Gamma(R)_{\infty}^{+}\geq\lambda+n+1
then

\textnormal{Zg}(R)^{(\lambda+n+1)}=\textnormal{Zg}(R_{S_{\lambda+n}})^{(1)}=\textnormal{Zg}(R_{S_{\lambda+n+1}})\cup\Delta_{\lambda+n}(R).

Proof.

We prove the proposition by induction on
\lambda
. First we prove the base case
\lambda=0
. The statement
(0)_{0}
is trivial. The first equality of
(1)_{0,0}
is trivial and we have already proved the second equality in
7.22
. For all
n\in\mathbb{N}
, the second equality in
(1)_{0,n}
follows from the first using
(1)_{0,0}
because
\textnormal{mdim}\Gamma(R)_{\infty}^{+}\geq n+1
implies
\textnormal{mdim}\Gamma(R_{S_{\lambda+n}})_{\infty}^{+}\geq 1
and hence

\textnormal{Zg}(R_{S_{\lambda+n}})^{(1)}=\textnormal{Zg}(R_{S_{\lambda+n+1}})\cup\Delta_{0}(R_{S_{\lambda+n}})=\textnormal{Zg}(R_{S_{\lambda+n+1}})\cup\Delta_{\lambda+n}(R).

The second equality follows from
7.21
.
So we just need to prove the first inequality in
(1)_{0,n}
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
. We have already proved the first equality of
(1)_{0,1}
in
7.22
. Suppose that
(1)_{0,n}
is true and
\textnormal{mdim}\Gamma(R)_{\infty}^{+}\geq(n+1)+1
.
Then

\textnormal{Zg}(R)^{((n+1)+1)}=(\textnormal{Zg}(R)^{(n+1)})^{\prime}=(\textnormal{Zg}(R_{S_{n}})^{(1)})^{\prime}=\textnormal{Zg}(R_{S_{n}})^{(1+1)}=\textnormal{Zg}(R_{S_{n+1}})^{(1)}.

The second equality is an application of
(1)_{0,n}
and the forth equality is an application of
(1)_{0,1}
applied to
R_{S_{n}}
(note that
\textnormal{mdim}\Gamma(R_{S_{n}})_{\infty}^{+}\geq 1+1
). So, by induction on
n
we have proved the first equality in
(1)_{0,n}
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
and we have seen that this implies the second equality in
(1)_{0,n}
. This completes the base case.

We now prove
(0)_{\lambda}
and
(1)_{\lambda,n}
for all limit ordinals
\lambda
and
n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
by induction on
\lambda
. Let
\lambda
be a limit ordinal and suppose we have shown
(0)_{\alpha}
,
(1)_{\alpha,n}
for all limit ordinals
\alpha<\lambda
and
n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
.
In order to prove
(0)_{\lambda}
, suppose
\textnormal{mdim}\Gamma(R)_{\infty}^{+}\geq\lambda
. We split into two cases.

Case 1:

\lambda=\gamma+\omega
for some limit ordinal
\gamma
.

Using
(1)_{\gamma,n}
for
n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
,

\textnormal{Zg}(R)^{(\lambda)}=\bigcap_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\textnormal{Zg}(R)^{(\gamma+n+1)}=\bigcap_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\textnormal{Zg}(R_{S_{\gamma+n}})^{(1)}\subseteq\textnormal{Zg}(R_{S_{\lambda}}).

We have already seen that if
\textnormal{mdim}\Gamma(R)_{\infty}^{+}\geq 1
then
\textnormal{Zg}(R_{S})\subseteq\textnormal{Zg}(R)^{(1)}
. Therefore, since
\textnormal{mdim}\Gamma(R_{S_{\gamma+n}})\geq 1
,
\textnormal{Zg}(R_{S_{\gamma+n+1}})\subseteq\textnormal{Zg}(R_{S_{\gamma+n}})^{(1)}
. Hence
\textnormal{Zg}(R_{S_{\lambda}})\subseteq\textnormal{Zg}(R_{S_{\gamma+n}})^{(n)}
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
. So the final inclusion displayed above is actually an equality as required.

Case 2:
The limit ordinals
\beta<\lambda
are cofinal in
\lambda
.

By the induction hypothesis,

\textnormal{Zg}(R)^{(\lambda)}=\bigcap_{\beta<\lambda}\textnormal{Zg}(R)^{(\beta)}=\bigcap_{\beta<\lambda}\textnormal{Zg}(R_{S_{\beta}})=\textnormal{Zg}(R_{S_{\lambda}}).

This proves
(0)_{\lambda}
.

In order to prove
(1)_{\lambda,n}
, suppose
\textnormal{mdim}\Gamma(R)_{\infty}^{+}\geq\lambda+n+1
. By
(0)_{\lambda}
,
\textnormal{Zg}(R)^{(\lambda+n+1)}=\textnormal{Zg}(R_{S_{\lambda}})^{(n+1)}
. Moreover
\textnormal{mdim}\Gamma(R_{S_{\lambda}})_{\infty}^{+}\geq n+1
. So
(1)_{0,n}
implies

\textnormal{Zg}(R_{S_{\lambda}})^{(n+1)}=\textnormal{Zg}(R_{S_{\lambda+n}})^{(1)}=\textnormal{Zg}(R_{S_{\lambda+n+1}})\cup\Delta_{n}(R_{S_{\lambda}})=\textnormal{Zg}(R_{S_{\lambda+n+1}})\cup\Delta_{\lambda+n}(R).

So we have proved
(1)_{\lambda,n}
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
.
∎

Theorem 7.24
.

Let
R
be a Prüfer domain of Krull dimension
1
. If
\Gamma(R)_{\infty}^{+}
has m-dimension
\alpha
then the CB rank of
\textnormal{Zg}(R)
is
\alpha
if
\alpha
is a limit ordinal and
\alpha+1
otherwise.

Proof.

By
2.17
, combined with the Jaffard-Kaplansky-Ohm theorem, it is enough to prove the result for Bézout domains.
Suppose
\alpha
is a limit ordinal. Then, by
7.23
,
\textnormal{Zg}(R)^{(\alpha)}=\textnormal{Zg}(R_{S_{\alpha}})
. Since
\Gamma(R)_{\infty}^{+}
has m-dimension
\alpha
,
R_{S_{\alpha}}
is a field and hence
\textnormal{Zg}(R_{S_{\alpha}})
has just one point. Therefore
\textnormal{Zg}(R)
has CB rank
\alpha
.
Suppose
\alpha=\lambda+n+1
where
n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
and
\lambda
is a limit ordinal. Then, by
7.23
,
\textnormal{Zg}(R)^{(\lambda+n+1)}=\textnormal{Zg}(R_{S_{\lambda+n}})^{(1)}
. Since
\Gamma(R)_{\infty}^{+}
has m-dimension
\alpha
,
\Gamma(R_{S_{\lambda+n}})_{\infty}^{+}

1
. So, by
7.18
,
\textnormal{Zg}(R_{S_{\lambda+n}})^{(1)}
has CB rank

∎

A Bézout domain has Krull dimension

if and only if
\Gamma
is non-trivial and all multiplication prime filters of

are maximal. In the next subsection, for each ordinal

, we construct an

-group

, we show that for each ordinal

.

Corollary 7.25
.

For all ordinals

.

8.
Lattice ordered abelian groups of continuous functions

We consider the
\ell
-group
C(X,\mathbb{Z})
of continuous functions from a Boolean space
X
to
\mathbb{Z}
equipped with the discrete topology. We show that all multiplication prime filters of
C(X,\mathbb{Z})^{+}
are maximal. Thus any Bézout domain with value group
C(X,\mathbb{Z})
has Krull dimension
1
. We show that
X
has CB rank
\alpha
if and only if
C(X,\mathbb{Z})^{+}_{\infty}
has m-dimension
\alpha+1
. In order to construct an
\ell
-group
\Gamma
such that all multiplication prime filters of
\Gamma^{+}
are maximal and
\Gamma_{\infty}^{+}
has specified m-dimension
\alpha
, we take a Boolean space with CB rank
\alpha
and take
\Gamma
to be the convex
\ell
-subgroup of
C(X,\mathbb{Z})
of all
f\in C(X,\mathbb{Z})
with
f(x)=0
for all
x\in X
of CB rank
\alpha
.

A space is
Boolean
if it is Hausdorff, compact and totally disconnected. Recall that the clopen sets of a Boolean space form a basis of open sets. There is a close connection between Boolean algebras and Boolean spaces given by Stone’s duality theorem which gives a contravariant equivalence of categories between the category of Boolean spaces with continuous maps and the category of Boolean algebras. On objects this duality takes a Boolean space
X
to
\mathcal{K}(X)
its Boolean algebra of clopen sets. In the other direction, it takes a Boolean algebra
B
to
\text{Prim}(B)
which is the topological space with points the prime filters of
B
and basis of open sets of the form
V(b):=\{p\in\text{Prim}(B)\ |\ b\in p\}
. The map

X\rightarrow\textnormal{Prim}(\mathcal{K}(X))\ \ \ x\mapsto\mathcal{P}_{x}:=\{K\in\mathcal{K}(X)\ |\ x\in K\}

is a homeomorphism with inverse given by taking a prime filter
\mathcal{F}\in\textnormal{Prim}(\mathcal{K}(X))
to the unique point of
X
in
\bigcap_{\mathcal{U}\in\mathcal{F}}\mathcal{U}
.

For
f\in C(X,\mathbb{Z})
, define

\textnormal{Supp}f:=\{x\in X\ |\ f(x)\neq 0\}.

Remark 8.1
.

(1)

For all
f\in C(X,\mathbb{Z})
, the image of
f
is finite and for each
n\in\mathbb{Z}
,
f^{-1}(n)
is clopen. In particular,
\textnormal{Supp}f
is clopen.

(2)

If
W_{1},\ldots,W_{l}
are a partition of
X
into clopen sets and
a_{1},\ldots,a_{l}\in\mathbb{Z}
then the function
f
defined by
f(x):=a_{i}
for all
x\in W_{i}
is continuous.

(3)

If
K
is a clopen subset of
X
then define

\chi_{K}(x):=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}1,&\hbox{if $x\in K$;}\\
0,&\hbox{otherwise.}\end{array}\right.

The function
\chi_{K}
is continuous.

Definition 8.2
.

For
f\in C(X,\mathbb{Z})^{+}
, define
f^{\prime}\in C(X,\mathbb{Z})
by setting
f^{\prime}(x)=f(x)
if
f(x)\leq 1
and
f^{\prime}(x)=1
otherwise.

It follows from
8.1
that
f^{\prime}
is continuous. Note that, if
f\in C(X,\mathbb{Z})^{+}
then
\textnormal{Supp}f^{\prime}=\textnormal{Supp}f
. Hence, if
f,g\in C(X,\mathbb{Z})^{+}
and
\textnormal{Supp}f=\textnormal{Supp}g
then
f^{\prime}=g^{\prime}
.

We record some properties of
f^{\prime}
and
Supp
which follow easily from their definitions.

Remark 8.3
.

Let
f,g\in C(X,\mathbb{Z})^{+}
. Then

(1)

there exists
n\in\mathbb{N}
such that
f^{\prime}\leq f\leq nf^{\prime}
;

(2)

\textnormal{Supp}f^{\prime}\subseteq\textnormal{Supp}g^{\prime}
if and only if
f^{\prime}\leq g^{\prime}
;

(3)

\textnormal{Supp}(f\wedge g)=\textnormal{Supp}(f)\cap\textnormal{Supp}(g)
; and

(4)

\textnormal{Supp}(f\vee g)=\textnormal{Supp}(f)\cup\textnormal{Supp}(g)
.

Lemma 8.4
.

The map

\mathcal{F}\mapsto B(\mathcal{F}):=\{\textnormal{Supp}f\ |\ f\in\mathcal{F}\}

is a bijection between multiplication prime filters of
C(X,\mathbb{Z})^{+}
and prime filters of
\mathcal{K}(X)
. In particular, the multiplication prime filters of
C(X,\mathbb{Z})^{+}
are exactly the filters of the form

\mathcal{F}_{x}:=\{f\in C(X,\mathbb{Z})^{+}\ |\ f(x)>0\}

where
x\in X
. Moreover, they are all maximal.

Proof.

We start by showing that if
\mathcal{F}
is a multiplication prime filter of
C(X,\mathbb{Z})^{+}
then
B(\mathcal{F})
is a prime filter of
\mathcal{K}(X)
. Since for all
f\in C(X,\mathbb{Z})^{+}
,
f\in\mathcal{F}
if and only if
f^{\prime}\in\mathcal{F}
and
\textnormal{Supp}f=\textnormal{Supp}f^{\prime}
,

B(\mathcal{F}):=\{\textnormal{Supp}f^{\prime}\ |\ f^{\prime}\in\mathcal{F}\}.

So, it follows from
8.3

(2)
and
(3)
that
B(\mathcal{F})
is a filter. If
\textnormal{Supp}f=\emptyset
then
f=0
. Therefore
B(\mathcal{F})
is a proper filter. It now follows from
8.3

(4)
that
B(\mathcal{F})
is a prime filter.

That
\mathcal{F}_{x}
is a multiplication prime filter follows easily from it’s definition. Hence, it follows from Stone duality that the map is surjective. In order to show that the map is injective, suppose that
\mathcal{F}
is a multiplication prime filter of
C(X,\mathbb{Z})^{+}
and that
f\in C(X,\mathbb{Z})^{+}
with
\textnormal{Supp}f\in B(\mathcal{F})
. There exists
g\in\mathcal{F}
such that
\textnormal{Supp}g=\textnormal{Supp}f
. Therefore
\textnormal{Supp}f^{\prime}=\textnormal{Supp}g^{\prime}
and hence
f^{\prime}=g^{\prime}
. So
g^{\prime}=f^{\prime}\in\mathcal{F}
and hence
f\in\mathcal{F}
as required.
∎

In this section we are only interested in m-dimension. So we will write
C_{\alpha}
for the subgroup
C_{\mathbbm{2},\alpha}(C(X,\mathbb{Z}))
of
C(X,\mathbb{Z})
.

Lemma 8.5
.

If
f\in C(X,\mathbb{Z})^{+}
is such that
[0,f]
is a simple interval then there exists an isolated point
y\in X
such that
f(y)=1
and
f(x)=0
for all
x\neq y
. In particular
C_{1}
is the set of
f\in C(X,\mathbb{Z})
with
\textnormal{Supp}f\subseteq X\backslash X^{(1)}
.

Proof.

Suppose that
f\in C(X,\mathbb{Z})^{+}
is such that
[0,f]
is a simple interval.
Then
f\geq f^{\prime}>0
. So
f=f^{\prime}
. Suppose that
\textnormal{Supp}f
contains two distinct elements
x,y
. Since
\mathcal{K}(X)
is a basis and
X
is Hausdorff, there exists a clopen set
V\subseteq\textnormal{Supp}f
such that
x\in V
and
y\notin V
. Then
\chi_{V}<f
because
V
is a proper subset of
\textnormal{Supp}f
and
0<\chi_{V}
because
V\neq\emptyset
. Thus, if
[0,f]
is a simple interval then
\textnormal{Supp}f
is a one point clopen set
\{y\}
,
f(y)=1
and
f(x)=0
for all
x\neq y
.

If
f\in C_{1}
then, by
4.1
,
f=\sum_{i=1}^{m}f_{i}-\sum_{i=m+1}^{n}f_{i}
where
f_{i}\in C(X,\mathbb{Z})^{+}
and
[0,f_{i}]
and is a simple interval for
1\leq i\leq n
. It follows from the first part of the lemma that
\textnormal{Supp}f\subseteq\bigcup_{i=1}^{n}\textnormal{Supp}f_{i}
is a subset of
X\backslash X^{(1)}
. Conversely, suppose
\textnormal{Supp}f\subseteq X\backslash X^{(1)}
. Then
\textnormal{Supp}f
contains only isolated points. So, since
\textnormal{Supp}f
is compact it is finite. Let
\textnormal{Supp}f=\{x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}\}
. For each
1\leq i\leq n
, define
f_{i}\in C^{+}(X,\mathbb{Z})
by setting
f_{i}(x_{i})=1
and
f_{i}(x)=0
for all
x\neq x_{i}
. Now each
f_{i}\in C_{1}
and there exists
m_{i}\in\mathbb{Z}
for
1\leq i\leq n
such that
f=\sum_{i=1}^{n}m_{i}f_{i}
. So
f\in C_{1}
as required.
∎

Remark 8.6
.

Let
X
a Boolean space and
Y
a closed subset. The map

C(X,\mathbb{Z})\rightarrow C(Y,\mathbb{Z}),\ \ \ f\mapsto f|_{Y},

induced by restriction to
Y
, is a surjective homomorphism of
\ell
-groups.

Proof.

It is easy to see that the map is an
\ell
-group homomorphism. So we just need to show it is surjective.

Note
Y
is Boolean. By Stone duality and since epimorphisms in the category of Boolean algebras are surjective, the map taking
K\in\mathcal{K}(X)
to
K\cap Y\in\mathcal{K}(Y)
is surjective (see
[
Kop89
, 7.6 (b)]
for a direct proof). It follows easily that any partition of
Y
into clopen sets is the intersection with
Y
of a partition of
X
into clopen sets.

Take
f\in C(Y,\mathbb{Z})
. Let
f(Y)=\{a_{1},\ldots,a_{n}\}
and let
Y_{i}=f^{-1}(a_{i})
for
1\leq i\leq n
. By the argument in the previous paragraph, there exists clopen sets
X_{i}
for
1\leq i\leq n
which partition
X
and such that
X_{i}\cap Y=Y_{i}
. Define
g\in C(X,\mathbb{Z})
by setting
g(x)=a_{i}
for
x\in X_{i}
. Then
g
is continuous and
g|_{Y}=f
as required.
∎

Lemma 8.7
.

The restriction map

f\in C(X,\mathbb{Z})\mapsto f|_{X^{(1)}}\in C(X^{(1)},\mathbb{Z})

is surjective and has kernel
C_{1}
. Therefore
C(X,\mathbb{Z})/C_{1}
is isomorphic to
C(X^{(1)},\mathbb{Z})
.

Proof.

The restriction map from
C(X,\mathbb{Z})
to
C(X^{(1)},\mathbb{Z})
is a surjective
\ell
-group homomorphism since
X^{(1)}
is closed by
8.6
.

For
f\in C(X,\mathbb{Z})
,
f|_{X^{(1)}}=0
if and only if
\textnormal{Supp}f\subseteq X\backslash X^{(1)}
. So, by
8.5
, the kernel of the restriction map from
C(X,\mathbb{Z})
to
C(X^{(1)},\mathbb{Z})
is
C_{1}
.
∎

Lemma 8.8
.

For all ordinals
\alpha
,
C(X^{(\alpha)},\mathbb{Z})\cong C(X,\mathbb{Z})/C_{\alpha}
. In particular, if
X
has CB rank
\beta
then
C(X,\mathbb{Z})_{\infty}^{+}
has m-dimension
\beta+1
.

Proof.

We show, by induction on ordinals, that the restriction map
f\mapsto f|_{X^{(\alpha)}}
has kernel
C_{\alpha}
. Since
X^{(\alpha)}
is closed, this map is surjective and hence this will prove the first claim.

For
\alpha=0
,
C(X^{(\alpha)},\mathbb{Z})=C(X,\mathbb{Z})
and
C_{0}=\{0\}
. The successor step follows from
8.7
.

Suppose
\lambda
is a limit ordinal. Then
f\in C_{\lambda}
if and only if
f\in C_{\alpha}
for some
\alpha<\lambda
. Therefore
f\in C_{\lambda}
if and only if
f|_{X^{(\alpha)}}=0
for some
\alpha<\lambda
. Thus
f\in C_{\lambda}
implies
f|_{X^{(\lambda)}}=0
. Conversely, suppose
f|_{X^{(\lambda)}}=0
. Then
\textnormal{Supp}f\subseteq\bigcup_{\alpha<\lambda}(X\backslash X^{(\alpha)})
. Since
\textnormal{Supp}f
is closed and
X
is compact,
\textnormal{Supp}f
is compact. Therefore
\textnormal{Supp}f\subseteq X\backslash X^{(\alpha)}
for some
\alpha<\lambda
. Hence
f|_{X^{(\alpha)}}=0
for some
\alpha<\lambda
. Thus
f\in C_{\alpha}
for some
\alpha<\lambda
as required.

By
4.7
, the m-dimension of
C(X,\mathbb{Z})_{\infty}^{+}
is the least ordinal
\alpha
such that
C_{\alpha}=C(X,\mathbb{Z})
. So, the m-dimension of
C(X,\mathbb{Z})_{\infty}^{+}
is the least ordinal
\alpha
such that
C(X,\mathbb{Z})/C_{\alpha}=\{0\}
. Therefore, the m-dimension of
C(X,\mathbb{Z})_{\infty}^{+}
is the least ordinal
\alpha
such that
X^{(\alpha)}=\emptyset
. Since
X
is compact,
\alpha=\beta+1
for some ordinal
\beta
and the CB rank of
X
is
\beta
as required.
∎

Unfortunately this means that the m-dimension of
C(X,\mathbb{Z})_{\infty}^{+}
is never a limit ordinal. The problem is that, since
X
is compact, if
X
has CB rank
\alpha
then
X^{(\alpha)}
is non-empty.

Definition 8.9
.

Let
X
be a Boolean space of CB rank
\alpha
. Define

(1)

C^{-}(X,\mathbb{Z})
to be the
\ell
-subgroup of
f\in C(X,\mathbb{Z})
such that
f(x)=0
for all
x\in X^{(\alpha)}
, and

(2)

\mathcal{K}^{-}(X)
be the lattice of clopen sets of
X
which do not contain any point of CB rank
\alpha
.

Note that
\mathcal{K}^{-}(X)
is unlikely to be a bounded lattice.

Lemma 8.10
.

The prime filters of
\mathcal{K}^{-}(X)
are of the form

\mathcal{P}^{-}_{x}:=\{K\in\mathcal{K}^{-}(X)\ |\ x\in K\}

where
x\in X\backslash X^{(\alpha)}
.
Moreover, they are all maximal as filters in
\mathcal{K}^{-}(X)
.

Proof.

Since
\mathcal{K}^{-}(X)
is distributive, the complement of a prime filter is an ideal. Let
\mathcal{P}
be a prime filter in
\mathcal{K}^{-}(X)
. Let
\mathcal{P}^{\prime}
be the filter generated by
\mathcal{P}
in
\mathcal{K}(X)
. Then
\mathcal{P}^{\prime}\cap\mathcal{K}^{-}(X)=\mathcal{P}
. Let
\mathcal{I}
be
\mathcal{K}^{-}(X)\backslash\mathcal{P}
. Since
\mathcal{K}^{-}(X)
is an ideal of
\mathcal{K}(X)
,
\mathcal{I}
is an ideal of
\mathcal{K}(X)
. Therefore
\mathcal{P}^{\prime}\cap\mathcal{I}\subseteq\mathcal{P}\cap\mathcal{I}=\emptyset
. One can now use Zorn’s lemma to produce a prime filter
\mathcal{F}
in
\mathcal{K}(X)
such that
\mathcal{F}\supseteq\mathcal{P}
and
\mathcal{F}\cap\mathcal{I}=\emptyset
. So
\mathcal{F}\cap\mathcal{K}^{-}(X)=\mathcal{P}
. By Stone duality, there exists
x\in X
such that
\mathcal{F}
is the set of
K\in\mathcal{K}(X)
such that
x\in K
. If
x\in X^{(\alpha)}
then
\mathcal{F}\cap\mathcal{K}^{-}(X)=\emptyset
. So
x\in X\backslash X^{(\alpha)}
as required.

It remains to show that if
x,y\in X\backslash X^{(\alpha)}
are non-equal then
\mathcal{F}^{-}_{x}
and
\mathcal{F}^{-}_{y}
are incomparable. The set of points of CB rank
\alpha
is closed. So, since
\mathcal{K}(X)
is a basis for
X
, there exists
U\in\mathcal{K}(X)
such that
x,y\in U
and
U\subseteq X\backslash X^{(\alpha)}
. Since
X
is Hausdorff, there exists
K\in\mathcal{K}(X)
such that
x\in K
and
y\notin K
. Then
x\in K\cap U
and
y\notin K\cap U
. Thus
K\cap U\in\mathcal{F}^{-}_{x}
and
K\cap U\notin\mathcal{F}^{-}_{y}
. Therefore
\mathcal{F}^{-}_{x}
is not a subset of
\mathcal{F}^{-}_{y}
. Symmetrically, one can show that
\mathcal{F}^{-}_{y}
is not a subset of
\mathcal{F}^{-}_{x}
. So
\mathcal{F}^{-}_{x}
is maximal for all
x\in X\backslash X^{(\alpha)}
.
∎

We show that every multiplication prime filter of
C^{-}(X,\mathbb{Z})^{+}
is the intersection of a multiplication prime filter of
C(X,\mathbb{Z})
with
C^{-}(X,\mathbb{Z})^{+}
.

Lemma 8.11
.

Let
X
be a Boolean space of CB rank
\alpha
. The map

\mathcal{F}\mapsto B(\mathcal{F}):=\{\textnormal{Supp}f\ |\ f\in\mathcal{F}\}

is a bijection between multiplication prime filters of
C^{-}(X,\mathbb{Z})^{+}
and prime filters of
\mathcal{K}^{-}(X)
. In particular, the multiplication prime filters of
C^{-}(X,\mathbb{Z})^{+}
are exactly the filters of the form

\mathcal{F}^{-}_{x}:=\{f\in C^{-}(X,\mathbb{Z})^{+}\ |\ f(x)>0\}

where
x\in X\backslash X^{(\alpha)}
. Moreover, they are all maximal.

Proof.

Note that
f\in C^{-}(X,\mathbb{Z})
if and only if
f^{\prime}\in C^{-}(X,\mathbb{Z})
. That
B(\mathcal{F})
is a prime filter of
\mathcal{K}^{-}(X)
for
\mathcal{F}
a multiplication prime filter of
C^{-}(X,\mathbb{Z})
and that the map is injective follows exactly as in
8.4
. That the map is surjective and the rest of the lemma follows from
8.11
.
∎

Lemma 8.12
.

Let
X
be a Boolean space with CB rank
\alpha
. Then
C^{-}(X,\mathbb{Z})_{\infty}^{+}
has m-dimension
\alpha
.

Proof.

For
\alpha\in\textnormal{Ord}
, let
C_{\alpha}^{-}
denote the subgroup
C_{\mathbbm{2},\alpha}(C^{-}(X,\mathbb{Z}))
of
C^{-}(X,\mathbb{Z})
. It is easy to see that
C^{-}(X,\mathbb{Z})
is a convex subgroup of
C(X,\mathbb{Z})
. Thus, for all
f\in C^{-}(X,\mathbb{Z})^{+}
,
[0,f]
is simple in
C^{-}(X,\mathbb{Z})
if and only if it is simple in
C(X,\mathbb{Z})
. Thus, by
4.1
,
C_{1}\cap C^{-}(X,\mathbb{Z})=C_{1}^{-}
. So it follows from
8.7
that the restriction map

f\in C^{-}(X,\mathbb{Z})\mapsto f|_{X^{(1)}}\in C^{-}(X^{(1)},\mathbb{Z})

is surjective with kernel
C_{1}^{-}
. As in
8.8
for
C(X,\mathbb{Z})
, it follows by ordinal induction that
C^{-}(X^{(\beta)},\mathbb{Z})
is isomorphic to
C^{-}(X,\mathbb{Z})/C^{-}_{\beta}
. Therefore, by
4.7
, the m-dimension of
C^{-}(X,\mathbb{Z})_{\infty}^{+}
is the least ordinal
\beta
such that
C^{-}(X^{(\beta)},\mathbb{Z})=\{0\}
. For any non-empty Boolean space
Y
with CB rank,
C^{-}(Y,\mathbb{Z})=\{0\}
if and only if
Y
has CB rank
0
. Thus
C^{-}(X^{(\beta)},\mathbb{Z})=\{0\}
if and only if
\beta
is the CB rank of
X
. So, the CB rank of
X
is equal to the m-dimension of
C^{-}(X,\mathbb{Z})_{\infty}^{+}
.
∎

Proposition 8.13
.

For each ordinal
\alpha
, there exists a Bézout domain
R
with Krull dimension
1
such that
\Gamma(R)_{\infty}^{+}
has m-dimension
\alpha
.

Proof.

Let
X
be a Boolean space with CB rank
\alpha
. That such a space exists is well known; for instance, see
[
MP60
, 4.5]
, where it is shown that the ordinal
\omega^{\alpha}+1
with the interval topology has CB rank
\alpha
. By the Jaffard-Kaplansky-Ohm theorem, there exists a Bézout domain
R
with
\Gamma(R)\cong C^{-}(X,\mathbb{Z})

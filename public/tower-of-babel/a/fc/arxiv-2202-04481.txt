Minimax Rate-Distortion
Adeel Mahmood, Aaron B. Wagner

Abstract
We show the existence of variable-rate rate-distortion codes that meet the disortion constraint almost surely and are minimax, i.e., strongly, universal with respect to an unknown source distribution and a distortion measure that is revealed only to the encoder and only at runtime. If we only require minimax universality with respect to the source distribution and not the distortion measure, then we provide an achievable O ~ ​ ( 1 / n ) \tilde{O}(1/\sqrt{n}) redundancy rate, which we show is optimal. This is in contrast to prior work on universal lossy compression, which provides O ⁡ ( log ⁡ n / n ) O(\log n/n) redundancy guarantees for weakly universal codes under various regularity conditions. We show that either eliminating the regularity conditions or upgrading to strong universality while keeping these regularity conditions entails an inevitable increase in the redundancy to O ~ ​ ( 1 / n ) \tilde{O}(1/\sqrt{n}) . Our construction involves random coding with non-i.i.d. codewords and a zero-rate uncoded transmission scheme. The proof uses exact asymptotics from large deviations, acceptance-rejection sampling, and the VC dimension of distortion measures.

Most existing works on universal lossy compression rely on random code constructions

be quite involved, and it requires various technical conditions.
In contrast, we use a random code construction which relies on acceptance-rejection

type-theoretic methods. The codebook is generated from

Our approach obviates the need for the
technical conditions alluded to earlier. It also has the added advantage that it can readily accommodate universality over the distortion
measure, albeit with the modification discussed next.

\displaystyle\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\begin{cases}=1&\text{ if }Q^{t,d,\rho}(k)>0\\
\leq 1&\text{ if }Q^{t,d,\rho}(k)=0\end{cases}

(\ref{evll})
separately and then add them together later. Using the same definition of the constant
V_{1}
in
(\ref{u1u2consts})
, we have from
(\ref{aleenanida})
that

\displaystyle\frac{1}{n}\ln\mathbb{E}_{c}[i_{J(X^{n})}]

\displaystyle\leq R(t(X^{n}),d,\rho)+\frac{K-3/4}{2}\frac{\ln n}{n}+\frac{2\min\left(\ln(K),\ln(J)\right)}{d\,n^{5/8}}\rho_{\max}+\frac{V_{1}}{n}

(91)

and

\displaystyle\frac{2}{n}\ln\left(\log\mathbb{E}_{c}[i_{J(X^{n})}]+1\right)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{2}{n}\ln\left(\frac{1}{\ln 2}\left(nR(t(X^{n}),d,\rho)+\frac{K-3/4}{2}\ln n+2\frac{\min\left(\ln(K),\ln(J)\right)}{d}\rho_{\max}n^{3/8}+V_{1}+\ln(2)\right)\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{2}{n}\ln\left(nR(t(X^{n}),d,\rho)+\frac{K-3/4}{2}\ln n+2\frac{\min\left(\ln(K),\ln(J)\right)}{d}\rho_{\max}n^{3/8}+V_{1}+\ln(2)\right)-\frac{2\ln\ln(2)}{n}.

(92)

We now use the bounds in
(\ref{nidah201})
and
(\ref{nidah6})
in
(\ref{evll})
. Since
R(t,d,\rho)\leq\ln(K)
, it is easy to see that there exist an integer
\mathcal{Z}
and a constant
\mathcal{G}
such that for
n\geq\mathcal{Z}
, we have

\displaystyle\frac{\ln(2)}{n}\mathbb{E}_{p}\left[\mathbb{E}_{c}\left[l(f_{n}(\phi_{n}(X^{n},\rho)))\right]\right]

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{E}_{p}\left[R(T,d,\rho)\right]+\frac{K+13/4}{2}\frac{\ln n}{n}+\frac{2\min\left(\ln(K),\ln(J)\right)}{d\,n^{5/8}}\rho_{\max}+\mathcal{G}\frac{\ln\ln n}{n}.

(93)

Note that
\mathcal{Z}
and
\mathcal{G}
are independent of
t
and
\rho
.

So far, we have constructed random non-prefix and prefix
d^{\prime}
-semifaithful codes with expected rates upper bounded by
(\ref{subskarisko})
and
(\ref{balahi})
, respectively. We now use post-correction to obtain
d
-semifaithful codes. Let
y^{n}
be the reconstruction sequence corresponding to
x^{n}
such that

\displaystyle\rho_{n}(x^{n},y^{n})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\rho(x_{i},y_{i})\leq d^{\prime}.

For any integer
M<n
, let
\{l_{m}\}_{m=1}^{M}
be a sequence indexing the
M
highest distortion letter pairs
(x_{i},y_{i})
, i.e.,

\displaystyle\rho(x_{l_{1}},y_{l_{1}})\geq\rho(x_{l_{2}},y_{l_{2}})\geq\cdots\geq\rho(x_{l_{M}},y_{l_{M}})\geq\rho(x_{i},y_{i})\,\,\,\,\forall i\notin\{l_{m}\}_{m=1}^{M}.

We replace

M=\left\lceil\frac{2\rho_{\max}}{d}n^{3/8}\right\rceil

symbols, namely
y_{l_{1}},y_{l_{2}},\ldots,y_{l_{M}}
, in
y^{n}
to obtain another sequence
\hat{y}^{n}
which satisfies
\rho_{n}(x^{n},\hat{y}^{n})\leq d
. From the assumption in
(\ref{dist_assump})
, we can choose the replacement symbols
\hat{y}_{l_{1}},\hat{y}_{l_{2}},\ldots,\hat{y}_{l_{M}}
such that
\rho(x_{l_{m}},\hat{y}_{l_{m}})=0
for all
1\leq m\leq M
. The post-corrected sequence
\hat{y}^{n}
reproduces
x^{n}
within distortion
d
since

\displaystyle\rho_{n}(x^{n},\hat{y}^{n})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i\in\{l_{m}\}_{m=1}^{M}}\rho(x_{i},y_{i})+\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i\notin\{l_{m}\}_{m=1}^{M}}\rho(x_{i},y_{i})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i\notin\{l_{m}\}_{m=1}^{M}}\rho(x_{i},y_{i})

\displaystyle=\frac{n-M}{n}\frac{1}{n-M}\sum_{i\notin\{l_{m}\}_{m=1}^{M}}\rho(x_{i},y_{i})

\displaystyle\leq\frac{n-M}{n}\left(d+\frac{2\rho_{\max}}{n^{5/8}}\right)

\displaystyle\leq d.

The encoder will need at most

\displaystyle M\left(\log(n)+\min\left(\log(K),\log(J)\right)+2\right)

(94)

bits using fixed-length encoding to convey this
“post-correction”
information. Note that for the non-prefix
d^{\prime}
-semifaithful coding scheme from before, this
“post-correction”
information needs to be sent before the variable length encoding of the reconstruction sequence. The rate increment from the post-correction bits in
(\ref{opwxz})
is upper bounded by

\displaystyle\,\frac{\ln(2)}{n}\left(M\left(\log(n)+\min\left(\log(K),\log(J)\right)+2\right)\right)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\ln(n)}{n}+\ln(n)\frac{2\rho_{\max}}{d\,n^{5/8}}+\frac{\min\left(\ln(K),\ln(J)\right)}{n}+\min\left(\ln(K),\ln(J)\right)\frac{2\rho_{\max}}{d\,n^{5/8}}+\frac{\ln(4)}{n}+\frac{4\rho_{\max}}{d\,n^{5/8}}\ln(2).

(95)

Adding
(\ref{rate_incr})
to
(\ref{subskarisko})
and
(\ref{balahi})
establishes the results of Theorems
1
and
2
, respectively.

(96)

Let

(97)

Denoting the probability law associated with the random code

\displaystyle\,\,\,\,\mathbb{P}_{c}\left(l(f_{n}(\Phi_{n}(x^{n},\rho)))>\frac{1}{\ln(2)}\left(nR(t,d,\rho)+\ln(2)+\frac{K-3/4}{2}\ln n+\frac{2n^{3/8}\min\left(\ln(K),\ln(J)\right)}{d}\rho_{\max}+V_{1}+\gamma_{n}\ln\ln n\right)\right)

\displaystyle\leq(1-g_{t,\rho})^{\exp\left(n\,R(t,d,\rho)+\frac{2\rho_{\max}\min\left(\ln(K),\ln(J)\right)}{d}n^{3/8}+\frac{K-3/4}{2}\ln n+V_{1}+\gamma_{n}\ln\ln n\right)-1}

where the inequality

above uses the inequality

. Now if we let

\displaystyle\,\,\,\,\mathbb{P}_{t,c}\left(l(f_{n}(\Phi_{n}(X_{t}^{n},\rho)))>\frac{1}{\ln(2)}\left(nR(t,d,\rho)+\ln(2)+\frac{K-3/4}{2}\ln n+\mbox{}\right.\right.

\displaystyle\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\left.\left.\frac{2n^{3/8}\min\left(\ln(K),\ln(J)\right)}{d}\rho_{\max}+V_{1}+\gamma_{n}\ln\ln n\right)\right)\leq\frac{e}{n^{J^{2}K^{2}+J-1}},

(100)

where the last inequality above follows from

\displaystyle\,\,\,\,\mathbb{P}_{T,c}\left(\bigcup_{t\in\mathcal{P}_{n}(A)}\left\{l(f_{n}(\Phi_{n}(X_{t}^{n},\rho)))>\frac{1}{\ln(2)}\left(nR(t,d,\rho)+\ln(2)+\frac{K-3/4}{2}\ln n+\mbox{}\right.\right.\right.

The above result proves that for large enough

As we will show later, this result implies the existence of a deterministic

-semifaithful code which has uniformly good performance over all types. However, our goal is to prove the existence of a deterministic
d^{\prime}
-semifaithful code in the universal distortion framework, i.e., one which has uniformly good performance over all types as well as all distortion measures. Since the set of distortion measures

fails. Instead, we invoke the fact that the space of distortion measures

can be partitioned into a polynomial number of equivalence classes. For full explanation, we refer the reader to
[
22

. Here, we merely state and use the following proposition which is a straightforward corollary of
[
22
, Proposition 1]
:

Proposition 2

For a given blocklength
n
and distortion level
d
, there are
\xi\leq(n+1)^{J^{2}K^{2}-1}+1
equivalence classes of
\mathcal{D}
, denoted by
[\mathcal{D}]_{\rho_{1}}
,
[\mathcal{D}]_{\rho_{2}}
, …,
[\mathcal{D}]_{\rho_{\xi}}
, where
\rho_{1},\rho_{2},\ldots,\rho_{\xi}
are arbitrarily chosen representative distortion measures. A
d
-semifaithful code
\tilde{C}_{n}
with respect to a distortion measure
\rho
is also
d
-semifaithful with respect to all distortion measures
\rho^{\prime}\in[\mathcal{D}]_{\rho}
in the same equivalence class.

We will make the choice of representative distortion measures
\rho_{1},\rho_{2},\ldots,\rho_{\xi}
be a function of the type
t
. For every type
t
and every equivalence class
[\mathcal{D}]_{\rho_{i}}
, we choose the representative distortion measure
\rho_{i}^{t}\in[\mathcal{D}]_{\rho_{i}}
which satisfies

\displaystyle R(t,d,\rho_{i}^{t})\leq R(t,d,\rho)

(103)

for all
\rho\in[\mathcal{D}]_{\rho_{i}}
. Henceforth, the representative distortion measures, chosen differently for each type, will be
\rho_{1}^{t},\rho_{2}^{t},\ldots,\rho^{t}_{\xi}
.

Now applying the union bound over the types and distortion measures gives

\displaystyle\,\,\,\,\mathbb{P}_{T,c}\left(\bigcup_{i=1}^{\xi}\bigcup_{t\in\mathcal{P}_{n}(A)}\left\{l(f_{n}(\Phi_{n}(X_{t}^{n},\rho_{i}^{t})))>\frac{1}{\ln(2)}\left(nR(t,d,\rho_{i}^{t})+\ln(2)+\frac{K-3/4}{2}\ln n+V_{1}+\mbox{}\right.\right.\right.

\displaystyle\left.\left.\left.\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\frac{2n^{3/8}\min\left(\ln(K),\ln(J)\right)}{d}\rho_{\max}+\gamma_{n}\ln\ln n\right)\right\}\right)\leq\left((n+1)^{J^{2}K^{2}-1}+1\right)(n+1)^{J-1}\frac{e}{n^{J^{2}K^{2}+J-1}}\to 0\text{ as }n\to\infty.

(104)

Also note that

\displaystyle\,\,\,\,\,\,\mathbb{P}_{T,c}\left(\bigcup_{i=1}^{\xi}\bigcup_{t\in\mathcal{P}_{n}(A)}\left\{l(f_{n}(\Phi_{n}(X_{t}^{n},\rho_{i}^{t})))>\frac{1}{\ln(2)}\left(nR(t,d,\rho_{i}^{t})+\ln(2)+\frac{K-3/4}{2}\ln n+\mbox{}\right.\right.\right.

\displaystyle\left.\left.\left.\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\frac{2n^{3/8}\min\left(\ln(K),\ln(J)\right)}{d}\rho_{\max}+V_{1}+\gamma_{n}\ln\ln n\right)\right\}\right)

(105)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}_{T,c}\left[\mathds{1}\left(\bigcup_{i=1}^{\xi}\bigcup_{t\in\mathcal{P}_{n}(A)}\left\{l(f_{n}(\Phi_{n}(X_{t}^{n},\rho_{i}^{t})))>\frac{1}{\ln(2)}\left(nR(t,d,\rho_{i}^{t})+\ln(2)+\frac{K-3/4}{2}\ln n+\mbox{}\right.\right.\right.\right.

\displaystyle\left.\left.\left.\left.\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\frac{2n^{3/8}\min\left(\ln(K),\ln(J)\right)}{d}\rho_{\max}+V_{1}+\gamma_{n}\ln\ln n\right)\right\}\right)\right]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}_{c}\left[\mathbb{E}_{T}\left[\mathds{1}\left(\bigcup_{i=1}^{\xi}\bigcup_{t\in\mathcal{P}_{n}(A)}\left\{l(f_{n}(\Phi_{n}(X_{t}^{n},\rho_{i}^{t})))>\frac{1}{\ln(2)}\left(nR(t,d,\rho_{i}^{t})+\ln(2)+\frac{K-3/4}{2}\ln n+\mbox{}\right.\right.\right.\right.\right.

\displaystyle\left.\left.\left.\left.\left.\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\frac{2n^{3/8}\min\left(\ln(K),\ln(J)\right)}{d}\rho_{\max}+V_{1}+\gamma_{n}\ln\ln n\right)\right\}\right)\Bigg|\Phi_{n}\right]\right]

\displaystyle\leq\left((n+1)^{J^{2}K^{2}-1}+1\right)\left((n+1)^{J-1}\right)\frac{e}{n^{J^{2}K^{2}+J-1}}.

(106)

The above inequality implies that that there exists a deterministic
d^{\prime}
-quantizer
\phi_{n}
such that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{T}\left[\mathds{1}\left(\bigcup_{i=1}^{\xi}\bigcup_{t\in\mathcal{P}_{n}(A)}\left\{l(f_{n}(\Phi_{n}(X_{t}^{n},\rho_{i}^{t})))>\frac{1}{\ln(2)}\left(nR(t,d,\rho_{i}^{t})+\ln(2)+\frac{K-3/4}{2}\ln n+\mbox{}\right.\right.\right.\right.

\displaystyle\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\leq\left((n+1)^{J^{2}K^{2}-1}+1\right)\left((n+1)^{J-1}\right)\frac{e}{n^{J^{2}K^{2}+J-1}}.

(107)

This in turn implies that

\displaystyle\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\mathbb{P}_{T}\left(\bigcup_{i=1}^{\xi}\bigcup_{t\in\mathcal{P}_{n}(A)}\left\{l(f_{n}(\Phi_{n}(X_{t}^{n},\rho_{i}^{t})))>\frac{1}{\ln(2)}\left(nR(t,d,\rho_{i}^{t})+\ln(2)+\frac{K-3/4}{2}\ln n+\mbox{}\right.\right.\right.

\displaystyle l(f_{n}(\phi_{n}(x^{n},\rho_{i}^{t})))

\displaystyle\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\left.\left((n+1)^{J^{2}K^{2}-1}+1\right)\left((n+1)^{J-1}\right)\frac{e}{n^{J^{2}K^{2}+J-1}}\left(\ln K+\frac{\ln(2)}{n}\right)+\frac{\ln(2)}{n}\right]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}_{p}\left[R(T,d,\rho_{i}^{T})\right]+\frac{K-3/4}{2}\frac{\ln n}{n}+\frac{2\min\left(\ln(K),\ln(J)\right)}{d\,n^{5/8}}\rho_{\max}+\frac{V_{1}+\ln(2)}{n}+\gamma_{n}\frac{\ln\ln n}{n}+

\displaystyle\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\left((n+1)^{J^{2}K^{2}-1}+1\right)\left((n+1)^{J-1}\right)\frac{e}{n^{J^{2}K^{2}+J-1}}\left(\ln K+\frac{\ln(2)}{n}\right)+\frac{\ln(2)}{n}

\displaystyle\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\left((n+1)^{J^{2}K^{2}-1}+1\right)\left((n+1)^{J-1}\right)\frac{e}{n^{J^{2}K^{2}+J-1}}\left(\ln K+\frac{\ln(2)}{n}\right)+\frac{\ln(2)}{n}.

In equality

is distributed uniformly over the type class

Finally, we use post-correction to make the code
d
-semifaithful. This post-correction was described in the proof of Theorem 1 (Section

and

as

with respect to the metric defined in

. Since

\rho(p,W)
in both

\displaystyle\limsup_{m\to\infty}R(p^{(m)},d,\rho^{(m)})\leq R(p^{*},d,\rho^{*}).

(118)

On the other hand, let

achieve the minimum in the definition of

. Let
\{m_{l}\}
be a subsequence such that
Q^{(m_{l})}\to Q
for some
Q
and

\displaystyle\lim_{l\to\infty}R(p^{(m_{l})},d,\rho^{(m_{l})})=\liminf_{m\to\infty}R(p^{(m)},d,\rho^{(m)}).

\displaystyle 0=R(p^{*},d,\rho^{*})\leq\liminf_{m\to\infty}R(p^{(m)},d,\rho^{(m)}).

(119)

, then for sufficiently large

\rho^{(m)}(p^{(m)},Q^{(m)})=d

is strictly decreasing in
d
for

. Now since

\displaystyle\lim_{l\to\infty}\rho^{(m_{l})}(p^{(m_{l})},Q^{(m_{l})})=\rho^{*}(p^{*},Q)=d,

we obtain

\displaystyle R(p^{*},d,\rho^{*})

\displaystyle\leq I(p^{*},Q)

\displaystyle=\lim_{l\to\infty}I(p^{(m_{l})},Q^{(m_{l})})

\displaystyle=\liminf_{m\to\infty}R(p^{(m)},d,\rho^{(m)}).

(120)

(\ref{batman1})

(\ref{bat22})

\displaystyle n

\displaystyle\geq\left(\frac{(C_{1})^{2}}{3(\rho_{\max})^{2}}\right)^{\frac{1}{2\alpha-1}}.

Note that the lower bounds in
(\ref{chebyshevwala})
and
(\ref{nlargeenough})
hold uniformly for all
t\in\mathcal{P}_{n}(A)
and
\rho\in\mathcal{D}
. Taking the minimum of
(\ref{chebyshevwala})
and
(\ref{nlargeenough})
gives the result of Lemma
9
.

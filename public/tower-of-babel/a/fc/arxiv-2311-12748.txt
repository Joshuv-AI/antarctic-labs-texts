Characterization of the upper Assouad codimension
Josh Kline, Antti V. Vähäkangas

Abstract
. We present a new notion, the upper Aikawa codimension, and establish its equivalence with the upper Assouad codimension in a metric space with a doubling measure. To achieve this result, we first prove variant of a local fractional Hardy inequality.



2020 Mathematics Subject Classification
Primary 28A75; Secondary 26D10, 46E35

1.
Introduction

The focus of this note is a variation of the Assouad dimension, specifically, the upper Assouad codimension. Alongside its dual counterpart, known as the lower Assouad codimension, these concepts were introduced in
[
10
]
for a metric space with a doubling measure. Their applications extend for instance to Hardy inequalities
[
7
,
12
]
, with distance measured to an irregular set, and density properties of compactly supported smooth functions in fractional Sobolev spaces
[
5
]
. For more comprehensive treatments of Assouad dimensions, additional information can be found in
[
13
]
and the monographs
[
9
,
11
]
.

Another notion of dimension, defined through the integral condition

\mathchoice{\mathop{\kern 1.99997pt\vrule width=6.00006pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.49997pt\kern-8.00003pt\intop}\nolimits_{\kern-3.27773ptB(z,R)}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{B(z,R)}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{B(z,R)}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{B(z,R)}}\frac{1}{\operatorname{dist}(y,E)^{\alpha}}\,dy\leq CR^{-\alpha}

was employed by Aikawa in
[
1
]
to investigate quasiadditivity properties of Riesz capacity; we also refer to
[
2
]
.
The following result from
[
14
, Theorem 5.1]
establishes
a connection between these notions of dimensions:
The lower Assouad codimension (refer to Definition
2.1
) of a set
E\subset X
is the supremum of those
\alpha\geq 0
for which
E
satisfies the lower Aikawa condition (Definition
2.2
).
Consequently, the lower Assouad codimension
\lcodima(E)
can be referred to as the lower Aikawa codimension of
E
.

The aim of this note is to prove an analogous characterization for the upper Assouad codimension (see Definition
2.5
), which is the dual concept of the lower Assouad codimension. More specifically, when
E\subset X
, we prove that
\ucodima(E)
is the infimum of all
\alpha>0
for which
E
satisfies a novel upper Aikawa condition (Definition
2.6
).
This main result is formulated in Theorem
5.3
, and to our knowledge, it is new even in
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
equipped with the Euclidean distance and the Lebesgue measure.

The main challenge concerning the proof of Theorem
5.3
lies in establishing the inequality
\ucodima(E)\leq\inf A
; here,
A
denotes the set of exponents
\alpha>0
for which
E
satisfies the upper Aikawa condition.
This inequality is deduced from a local Hardy inequality, the fractional version of which we prove under the assumption that
E
satisfies the upper Aikawa condition with exponent
\alpha
(refer to Section
4
).
In Section
3
we lay the groundwork for the local Hardy inequality by employing a truncation technique from
[
16
]
on the set
E
. We also utilize the fractional variant
[
8
]
of the Maz’ya truncation method
[
17
, p. 110]
in the proof.

2.
Notation and Preliminaries

We assume throughout this paper that
X=(X,d,\mu)
is
a metric measure space (with at least two points), where

\mu
is a Borel measure supported on
X
such that

0<\mu(B)<\infty
for
all (open) balls

B=B(x,r):=\{y\in X:d(x,y)<r\}

with
x\in X
and
r>0
.
We also assume throughout
that
\mu
is
doubling
; that is, there is a constant
c_{\mu}>1
such that
whenever
x\in X
and
r>0
, we have

(1)

\mu(B(x,2r))\leq c_{\mu}\,\mu(B(x,r)).

By iterating the doubling condition (
1
), we can find constants
s=\log_{2}c_{\mu}>0
and
C>0
such that

(2)

\frac{\mu(B(x,r))}{\mu(B(x,R))}\geq C\Bigl(\frac{r}{R}\Bigr)^{s}

whenever
x\in X
and
0<r\leq R<\operatorname{diam}(X)
; see
[
3
, Lemma 3.3]
.

The closure of a set
E\subset X
is denoted by
{\mathchoice{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\displaystyle E}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{E}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\textstyle E}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{E}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\scriptstyle E}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{E}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\scriptscriptstyle E}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{E}}}
.
We use
\operatorname{dist}(x,E)=\inf\{d(x,y)\,:\,y\in E\}
to denote the distance from a point
x\in X
to a nonempty set
E\subset X
.
If
E\subset X
, then
\mathbf{1}_{E}

denotes the characteristic function of
E
; that is,
\mathbf{1}_{E}(x)=1
if
x\in E

and
\mathbf{1}_{E}(x)=0
if
x\in X\setminus E
.
We abbreviate
dy=d\mu(y)
in the integrals and
use the following familiar notation

u_{A}=\mathchoice{\mathop{\kern 1.99997pt\vrule width=6.00006pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.49997pt\kern-8.00003pt\intop}\nolimits_{\kern-3.27773ptA}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{A}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{A}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{A}}u(y)\,dy=\frac{1}{\mu(A)}\int_{A}u(y)\,d\mu(y)

for the integral average of
u\in L^{1}(A)
over a measurable set
A\subset X

with
0<\mu(A)<\infty
.
The set of all Lipschitz functions
u:X\to{\mathbb{R}}
is denoted by
\operatorname{Lip}(X)
.
We denote
{\mathbb{N}}=\{1,2,3,\ldots\}
and
{\mathbb{N}}_{0}={\mathbb{N}}\cup\{0\}
.

2.1.
Lower Assouad codimension and lower Aikawa condition

In order to motivate and provide context for our novel
upper Aikawa condition in Section
2.2
, we first recall
the lower Assouad codimension

[
10
]
and its
relation to the lower Aikawa condition
[
14
]
.

When
E\subset X
and
r>0
, the
open
r
-neighbourhood of
E

is the set

E_{r}=\{x\in X\,:\,\operatorname{dist}(x,E)<r\}.

Observe that
E_{r}={\mathchoice{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\displaystyle E}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{E}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\textstyle E}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{E}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\scriptstyle E}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{E}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\scriptscriptstyle E}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{E}}}_{r}
. That is, the

r
-neighbourhoods of
E
and its closure coincide.

Definition 2.1
.

The
lower Assouad codimension
of a nonempty set
E\subset X
,
denoted by
\lcodima(E)
, is
the supremum of all
Q\geq 0
for
which there exists a constant
C>0
such that

(3)

\frac{\mu(E_{r}\cap B(z,R))}{\mu(B(z,R))}\leq C\Bigl(\frac{r}{R}\Bigr)^{Q}

for every
z\in E
and all
0<r<R<\operatorname{diam}(E)
.
If
\operatorname{diam}(E)=0
, then
the restriction
R<\operatorname{diam}(E)
is replaced
with
R<\operatorname{diam}(X)
.

Definition 2.2
.

Suppose
E\subset X
is a nonempty set and
\alpha\geq 0
. Assume that there exists a constant
C>0

such that

(4)

\mathchoice{\mathop{\kern 1.99997pt\vrule width=6.00006pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.49997pt\kern-8.00003pt\intop}\nolimits_{\kern-3.27773ptB(z,R)}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{B(z,R)}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{B(z,R)}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{B(z,R)}}\frac{1}{\operatorname{dist}(y,E)^{\alpha}}\,dy\leq CR^{-\alpha}

for every
z\in E
and
0<R<\operatorname{diam}(E)
. If
\operatorname{diam}(E)=0
, then the
restriction
R<\operatorname{diam}(E)
is replaced with
R<\operatorname{diam}(X)
.
Then we say that
E
satisfies the
lower Aikawa condition with exponent
\alpha
.

Observe that (
4
) always holds for
\alpha=0
.
The following result is
[
14
, Theorem 5.1]
.

Theorem 2.3
.

Let
E\subset X
be a nonempty set. Then
\lcodima(E)

is the supremum of all
\alpha\geq 0
for which
E
satisfies
the lower Aikawa condition with exponent
\alpha
.

Our definition of the upper Aikawa condition, see Definition
2.6
below, is
motivated by condition (B) of the following Theorem
2.4
.

We recall that a set
E\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
is
porous
if there exists a constant
C>0
such that
for every
x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
and
r>0
there exists
y\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
satisfying
B(y,Cr)\subset B(x,r)\setminus E
.

Theorem 2.4
.

Consider
X={\mathbb{R}}^{n}
equipped with the
Euclidean distance and the Lebesgue measure denoted by
\mu
.
Let
0\leq\alpha<n
and
E\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{n}

be a nonempty set. Let us consider the following conditions:

(A)

E
satisfies
the lower Aikawa condition with exponent
\alpha
.

(B)

For every
\varepsilon>0
, there exists
\delta>0
such that

\mathchoice{\mathop{\kern 1.99997pt\vrule width=6.00006pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.49997pt\kern-8.00003pt\intop}\nolimits_{\kern-3.27773ptB(z,R)}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{B(z,R)}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{B(z,R)}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{B(z,R)}}\frac{\mathbf{1}_{K}(y)}{\operatorname{dist}(y,E)^{\alpha}}\,dy\leq\varepsilon R^{-\alpha}

whenever
z\in E
,
R>0
and
K\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{n}

is a Lebesgue measurable set such that

\mu(K\cap B(z,R))\leq\delta\mu(B(z,R))\,.

Condition (A) implies (B). Conversely, if
E
is porous and
\operatorname{dist}(\cdot,E)^{-\alpha}
is locally integrable, then
(B) implies (A).

Proof.

Let
w(y)=\operatorname{dist}(y,E)^{-\alpha}
for every
y\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
.
Let us assume that condition (A) holds and fix
\varepsilon>0
.
By
[
11
, Lemma 10.4]
, we may
further assume that (
4
) holds for all
R>0

with a possibly different constant
C_{1}>0
.
Then from
[
11
, Theorem 10.9]
, we
conclude that
w
belongs to the Muckenhoupt class
A_{\infty}
. By applying one of the well-known characterizations of
A_{\infty}
weights
[
19
]
, we infer that there
exists
\delta>0
as follows.
Fix
z\in E
,
R>0
and a Lebesgue measurable set
K\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{n}

such that
\mu(K\cap B(z,R))\leq\delta\mu(B(z,R))
.
Then

\displaystyle\int_{B(z,R)}\frac{\mathbf{1}_{K}(y)}{\operatorname{dist}(y,E)^{\alpha}}\,dy

\displaystyle=\int_{K\cap B(z,R)}w(y)\,dy\leq\frac{\varepsilon}{C_{1}}\int_{B(z,R)}w(y)\,dy

\displaystyle=\frac{\varepsilon}{C_{1}}\int_{B(z,R)}\frac{1}{\operatorname{dist}(y,E)^{\alpha}}\,dy\leq\varepsilon R^{-\alpha}\mu(B(z,R))\,,

where the last step follows from (
4
).
Condition (B) follows from this.

Conversely, assume that (B) holds, the set
E
is porous and
w
is locally integrable. Denote
w(A)=\int_{A}w(y)\,dy
for measurable sets
A\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
.
We show that
w
belongs to Muckenhoupt
A_{\infty}
class of weights. Since
E
is porous, condition (A) then follows by applying
[
11
, Theorem 10.15]
.
For this purpose, we let
\varepsilon>0
.
By
[
19
]
, it suffices to find a
\delta>0
such that

w(K)\leq\varepsilon w(B(x,r))
whenever

B(x,r)\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
is a ball and
K\subset B(x,r)

is a measurable set such that
\mu(K)\leq\delta\mu(B(x,r))
.
We choose
\delta>0
such that
condition (B) holds for the pair
\varepsilon/4^{n}>0
and
\delta>0
.
Clearly, we may further assume that
4^{\alpha}\delta<\varepsilon
.

First we assume that
2r\leq\operatorname{dist}(x,E)
. Observe
that
\operatorname{dist}(x,E)/2\leq\operatorname{dist}(y,E)\leq 2\operatorname{dist}(x,E)
for every
y\in B(x,r)
, and therefore

(5)

\begin{split}\int_{B(x,r)}\frac{\mathbf{1}_{K}(y)}{\operatorname{dist}(y,E)^{\alpha}}\,dy&\leq 2^{\alpha}\operatorname{dist}(x,E)^{-\alpha}\mu(K)\\
&\leq(2\operatorname{dist}(x,E))^{-\alpha}4^{\alpha}\delta\mu(B(x,r))\leq{\varepsilon}\int_{B(x,r)}\frac{1}{\operatorname{dist}(y,E)^{\alpha}}\,dy\,.\end{split}

Next we consider the case, where
2r>\operatorname{dist}(x,E)
. Write
R=2r
and
choose
z\in E
such that
x\in B(z,R)
. Observe
that
B(x,r)\subset B(z,2R)
and

\mu(K\cap B(z,2R))\leq\mu(K)\leq\delta\mu(B(x,r))\leq\delta\mu(B(z,2R))\,.

Condition (B) and the fact that
\mu
is the Lebesgue measure imply that

(6)

\begin{split}\int_{B(x,r)}\frac{\mathbf{1}_{K}(y)}{\operatorname{dist}(y,E)^{\alpha}}\,dy&\leq\int_{B(z,2R)}\frac{\mathbf{1}_{K}(y)}{\operatorname{dist}(y,E)^{\alpha}}\,dy\leq\frac{\varepsilon}{4^{n}}(2R)^{-\alpha}\mu(B(z,2R))\\
&\leq\varepsilon(3r)^{-\alpha}\mu(B(x,r))\leq{\varepsilon}\int_{B(x,r)}\frac{1}{\operatorname{dist}(y,E)^{\alpha}}\,dy\,.\end{split}

In any case, by combining inequalities (
5
) and (
6
), we find that

w(K)=w(K\cap B(x,r))\leq\varepsilon w(B(x,r))\,,

as claimed.
∎

2.2.
Upper Assouad codimension and upper Aikawa condition

We first recall the upper Assouad codimension from

[
10
]
.

Definition 2.5
.

The
upper Assouad codimension
of a nonempty set
E\subset X
,
denoted by
\ucodima(E)
, is
the infimum of all
Q\geq 0
for
which there exists a constant
c>0
such that

(7)

\frac{\mu(E_{r}\cap B(z,R))}{\mu(B(z,R))}\geq c\Bigl(\frac{r}{R}\Bigr)^{Q}

for every
z\in E
and all
0<r<R<\operatorname{diam}(E)
.
If
\operatorname{diam}(E)=0
, then
the restriction
R<\operatorname{diam}(E)
is replaced
with
R<\operatorname{diam}(X)
.

Observe from Inequality (
2
)
that
\ucodima(E)\leq\log_{2}c_{\mu}
for every nonempty
E\subset X
.

In this note, we introduce the following concept that we call
an upper Aikawa condition. This terminology is motivated
by Theorem
2.4
.

Definition 2.6
.

Suppose
E\subset X
is a nonempty set and
\alpha>0
. Assume that, for every
\varepsilon>0
, there exists

\delta>0
such that

\mathchoice{\mathop{\kern 1.99997pt\vrule width=6.00006pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.49997pt\kern-8.00003pt\intop}\nolimits_{\kern-3.27773ptB(z,R)}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{B(z,R)}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{B(z,R)}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{B(z,R)}}\frac{\mathbf{1}_{K}(y)}{\operatorname{dist}(y,E)^{\alpha}}\,dy\geq\frac{R^{-\alpha}}{\varepsilon}

whenever
z\in E
,
0<R<\operatorname{diam}(E)
and
K\subset X
is a Borel set that satisfies the condition

\mu(B(z,R)\setminus K)\leq\delta\mu(B(z,R))\,.

If
\operatorname{diam}(E)=0
, then the restriction
R<\operatorname{diam}(E)
is replaced
with
R<\operatorname{diam}(X)
.
Then we say that
E
satisfies the
upper Aikawa condition with exponent
\alpha
.

3.
Truncation of the set

In order to prove the local Hardy inequality in Section
4
,
we need to truncate a given closed set
E
to a ball in a manner which preserves big pieces of the original set
E
. Therefore, we use
the following method of truncation from
[
16
]
, see also

[
18
,
4
,
15
]
.
The proofs of Lemma
3.1
and Lemma

can be found from
[

]
; see also
[

for the case

.

Lemma 3.1
.

Assume that
E\subset X
is a closed set and let
z\in E
and
r>0
.
Let
F_{0}=E\cap{\mathchoice{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\displaystyle B(z,\frac{r}{2})}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{B(z,\frac{r}{2})}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\textstyle B(z,\frac{r}{2})}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{B(z,\frac{r}{2})}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\scriptstyle B(z,\frac{r}{2})}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{B(z,\frac{r}{2})}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\scriptscriptstyle B(z,\frac{r}{2})}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{B(z,\frac{r}{2})}}}
and define recursively

F_{j}=\bigcup_{x\in F_{j-1}}E\cap{\mathchoice{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\displaystyle B(x,2^{-j-1}r)}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{B(x,2^{-j-1}r)}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\textstyle B(x,2^{-j-1}r)}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{B(x,2^{-j-1}r)}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\scriptstyle B(x,2^{-j-1}r)}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{B(x,2^{-j-1}r)}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\scriptscriptstyle B(x,2^{-j-1}r)}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{B(x,2^{-j-1}r)}}}\,,\qquad\text{ for every }j\in{\mathbb{N}}\,.

Let
F=\overline{\bigcup_{j\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}}F_{j}}
.
Then

z\in F\subset E\cap{\mathchoice{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\displaystyle B(z,r)}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{B(z,r)}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\textstyle B(z,r)}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{B(z,r)}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\scriptstyle B(z,r)}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{B(z,r)}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\scriptscriptstyle B(z,r)}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{B(z,r)}}}

for every

The truncated set

.

Let

.

By truncating the set

the following holefilling lemma. This is an adaptation of

[

, Lemma 5.3]
, see also
[

, Lemma 7.27]
.

Lemma 3.4
.

Let
1\leq p<\infty
,
0<s<1

and assume that
E
,
B(z,r)
and
F
are as in Lemma
3.1
.
Suppose that
\sigma\geq 1
and
\kappa\geq 2
, and
u\in\operatorname{Lip}(X)
satisfies
u=0
in
F
. Then

(9)

\begin{split}&\int_{B(z,\sigma\kappa r)\setminus F}\frac{\lvert u(x)\rvert^{p}}{\operatorname{dist}(x,F)^{sp}}\,dx\\
&\qquad\leq C_{1}\int_{B(z,\sigma\kappa r)}\int_{B(z,\sigma\kappa r)}\frac{\lvert u(x)-u(y)\rvert^{p}}{d(x,y)^{sp}\,\mu(B(x,d(x,y)))}\,dy\,dx+C_{2}\int_{B(z,\kappa r)\setminus F}\frac{\lvert u(x)\rvert^{p}}{\operatorname{dist}(x,F)^{sp}}\,dx\,,\end{split}

where
C_{1}=C(s,c_{\mu},p,\sigma,\kappa)
and
C_{2}=1+3^{p}\kappa^{sp}C(c_{\mu},\sigma)
.

Proof.

Since
F\subset{\mathchoice{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\displaystyle B(z,r)}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{B(z,r)}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\textstyle B(z,r)}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{B(z,r)}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\scriptstyle B(z,r)}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{B(z,r)}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\scriptscriptstyle B(z,r)}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{B(z,r)}}}
and
\kappa\geq 2
, we have

\displaystyle\int_{(B(z,\sigma\kappa r)\setminus F)\setminus B(z,\kappa r)}\frac{\lvert u(x)\rvert^{p}}{\operatorname{dist}(x,F)^{sp}}\,dx\leq r^{-sp}\int_{B(z,\sigma\kappa r)}\lvert u(x)\rvert^{p}\,dx

\displaystyle\qquad\leq 3^{p}r^{-sp}\biggl(\int_{B(z,\sigma\kappa r)}\bigl\lvert u(x)-u_{B(z,\sigma\kappa r)}\bigr\rvert^{p}\,dx

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad+\mu(B(z,\sigma\kappa r))\bigl\lvert u_{B(z,\sigma\kappa r)}-u_{B(z,\kappa r)}\bigr\rvert^{p}+\mu(B(z,\sigma\kappa r))\bigl\lvert u_{B(z,\kappa r)}\bigr\rvert^{p}\biggr).

By applying the Hölder’s inequality and the fractional
(s,p,p,p)
-Poincaré inequality from
[
6
, Lemma 2.2]
, we get

\displaystyle 3^{p}r^{-sp}\biggl(\int_{B(z,\sigma\kappa r)}\bigl\lvert u(x)-u_{B(z,\sigma\kappa r)}\bigr\rvert^{p}\,dx+\mu(B(z,\sigma\kappa r))\bigl\lvert u_{B(z,\sigma\kappa r)}-u_{B(z,\kappa r)}\bigr\rvert^{p}\biggr)

\displaystyle\qquad\leq C(c_{\mu},\sigma,p)r^{-sp}\int_{B(z,\sigma\kappa r)}\bigl\lvert u(x)-u_{B(z,\sigma\kappa r)}\bigr\rvert^{p}\,dx

\displaystyle\qquad\leq C(s,c_{\mu},p,\sigma,\kappa)\int_{B(z,\sigma\kappa r)}\int_{B(z,\sigma\kappa r)}\frac{\lvert u(x)-u(y)\rvert^{p}}{d(x,y)^{sp}\,\mu(B(x,d(x,y)))}\,dy\,dx.

On the other hand, since
u=0
in
F
, we obtain

\displaystyle 3^{p}r^{-sp}\mu(B(z,\sigma\kappa r))\bigl\lvert u_{B(z,\kappa r)}\bigr\rvert^{p}

\displaystyle\leq 3^{p}C(c_{\mu},\sigma)r^{-sp}\int_{B(z,\kappa r)\setminus F}\lvert u(x)\rvert^{p}\,dx

\displaystyle\leq 3^{p}\kappa^{sp}C(c_{\mu},\sigma)\int_{B(z,\kappa r)\setminus F}\frac{\lvert u(x)\rvert^{p}}{\operatorname{dist}(x,F)^{sp}}\,dx\,.

Since

\displaystyle\int_{B(z,\sigma\kappa r)\setminus F}\frac{\lvert u(x)\rvert^{p}}{\operatorname{dist}(x,F)^{sp}}\,dx=\int_{B(z,\kappa r)\setminus F}\frac{\lvert u(x)\rvert^{p}}{\operatorname{dist}(x,F)^{sp}}\,dx+\int_{(B(z,\sigma\kappa r)\setminus F)\setminus B(z,\kappa r)}\frac{\lvert u(x)\rvert^{p}}{\operatorname{dist}(x,F)^{sp}}\,dx\,,

the claim follows from the above estimates.
∎

4.
Local Hardy inequality

We show
that the upper Aikawa condition implies a local variant of the Hardy inequality.
The proof combines various techniques. For instance, we borrow the following
fractional variant of the Maz’ya truncation method from
[
8
]
.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
u\in\operatorname{Lip}(X)
. For every
k\in{\mathbb{Z}}
, define
a function
u_{k}:X\to[0,1]
by

u_{k}(x)=\begin{cases}1,&\text{if $|u(x)|\geq 2^{k+1}$,}\\
\frac{|u(x)|}{2^{k}}-1,&\text{if $2^{k}<|u(x)|<2^{k+1}$,}\\
0,&\text{if $|u(x)|\leq 2^{k}$.}\end{cases}

Then there exists a constant
C=C(p)
such that

\displaystyle\sum_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}}2^{kp}\int_{B(z,R)}\int_{B(z,R)}

\displaystyle\frac{\lvert u_{k}(x)-u_{k}(y)\rvert^{p}}{d(x,y)^{sp}\,\mu(B(x,d(x,y)))}\,dy\,dx

\displaystyle\qquad\leq C\int_{B(z,R)}\int_{B(z,R)}\frac{\lvert u(x)-u(y)\rvert^{p}}{d(x,y)^{sp}\,\mu(B(x,d(x,y)))}\,dy\,dx

whenever
z\in X
and
R>0
.

Proof.

For every
i\in{\mathbb{Z}}
we
denote

E_{i}=\{x\in B(z,R):|u(x)|>2^{i}\}\quad\text{and}\quad A_{i}=E_{i}\setminus E_{i+1}\,.

Fix
k\in{\mathbb{Z}}
and observe that

(10)

\begin{split}B(z,R)&=\{x\in B(z,R)\,:\,0\leq\lvert u(x)\rvert<\infty\}\\
&=\underbrace{\{x\in B(z,R)\,:\,u(x)=0\}}_{=:N}\cup\bigcup_{i\in{\mathbb{Z}}}A_{i}\\
&=N\cup\bigcup_{i<k}A_{i}\cup A_{k}\cup\bigcup_{i>k}A_{i}\,.\end{split}

By using the decomposition (
10
) and combining terms, we obtain that

(11)

\begin{split}&\int_{B(z,R)}\int_{B(z,R)}\frac{\lvert u_{k}(x)-u_{k}(y)\rvert^{p}}{d(x,y)^{sp}\,\mu(B(x,d(x,y)))}\,dy\,dx\\
&\qquad\leq\bigg\{\sum_{i\leq k}\sum_{j\geq k}\int_{A_{i}}\int_{A_{j}}+\sum_{j\geq k}\int_{N}\int_{A_{j}}\bigg\}\frac{\lvert u_{k}(x)-u_{k}(y)\rvert^{p}}{d(x,y)^{sp}\,\mu(B(x,d(x,y)))}\,dy\,dx\\
&\qquad\qquad+\bigg\{\sum_{i\geq k}\sum_{j\leq k}\int_{A_{i}}\int_{A_{j}}+\sum_{i\geq k}\int_{A_{i}}\int_{N}\bigg\}\frac{\lvert u_{k}(x)-u_{k}(y)\rvert^{p}}{d(x,y)^{sp}\,\mu(B(x,d(x,y)))}\,dy\,dx\,.\end{split}

We observe that
|u_{k}(x)-u_{k}(y)|\leq 2^{-k}|u(x)-u(y)|
. Moreover, if
x\in A_{i}
and
y\in A_{j}
,
where
i+2\leq j
, then

|u(x)-u(y)|\geq|u(y)|-|u(x)|\geq 2^{j-1}
,
hence
|u_{k}(x)-u_{k}(y)|\leq 1\leq 2\cdot 2^{-j}|u(x)-u(y)|
.
Therefore,

|u_{k}(x)-u_{k}(y)|\leq 2\cdot 2^{-j}|u(x)-u(y)|,\quad(x,y)\in A_{i}\times A_{j}\,,

whenever
i\leq k\leq j
.
Observe also that
\sum_{k=i}^{j}2^{(k-j)p}<\sum_{k=-\infty}^{j}2^{(k-j)p}\leq\frac{1}{1-2^{-p}}
. Hence,

\displaystyle\sum_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}}2^{kp}\sum_{i\leq k}\sum_{j\geq k}

\displaystyle\int_{A_{i}}\int_{A_{j}}\frac{\lvert u_{k}(x)-u_{k}(y)\rvert^{p}}{d(x,y)^{sp}\,\mu(B(x,d(x,y)))}\,dy\,dx

\displaystyle\leq 2^{p}\sum_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}}\sum_{i\leq k}\sum_{j\geq k}2^{(k-j)p}\int_{A_{i}}\int_{A_{j}}\frac{\lvert u(x)-u(y)\rvert^{p}}{d(x,y)^{sp}\,\mu(B(x,d(x,y)))}\,dy\,dx

\displaystyle\leq 2^{p}\sum_{i\in{\mathbb{Z}}}\sum_{j\in{\mathbb{Z}}}\sum_{i\leq k\leq j}2^{(k-j)p}\int_{A_{i}}\int_{A_{j}}\frac{\lvert u(x)-u(y)\rvert^{p}}{d(x,y)^{sp}\,\mu(B(x,d(x,y)))}\,dy\,dx

\displaystyle\leq\frac{2^{p}}{1-2^{-p}}\int_{B(z,R)}\int_{B(z,R)}\frac{\lvert u(x)-u(y)\rvert^{p}}{d(x,y)^{sp}\,\mu(B(x,d(x,y)))}\,dy\,dx.

A similar argument shows that

\displaystyle\sum_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}}2^{kp}\sum_{j\geq k}\int_{N}\int_{A_{j}}\frac{\lvert u_{k}(x)-u_{k}(y)\rvert^{p}}{d(x,y)^{sp}\,\mu(B(x,d(x,y)))}\,dy\,dx

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\leq 2^{p}\sum_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}}\sum_{j\geq k}2^{(k-j)p}\int_{N}\int_{A_{j}}\frac{\lvert u(x)-u(y)\rvert^{p}}{d(x,y)^{sp}\,\mu(B(x,d(x,y)))}\,dy\,dx

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\leq\frac{2^{p}}{1-2^{-p}}\int_{B(z,R)}\int_{B(z,R)}\frac{\lvert u(x)-u(y)\rvert^{p}}{d(x,y)^{sp}\,\mu(B(x,d(x,y)))}\,dy\,dx\,.

Fubini’s theorem and similar estimates for the two remaining series in (
11
) shows that

\displaystyle\sum_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}}2^{kp}\int_{B(z,R)}\int_{B(z,R)}\frac{\lvert u_{k}(x)-u_{k}(y)\rvert^{p}}{d(x,y)^{sp}\,\mu(B(x,d(x,y)))}\,dy\,dx

\displaystyle\qquad\leq\frac{2^{p+2}}{1-2^{-p}}\int_{B(z,R)}\int_{B(z,R)}\frac{\lvert u(x)-u(y)\rvert^{p}}{d(x,y)^{sp}\,\mu(B(x,d(x,y)))}\,dy\,dx\,.

Thus, we obtain the desired inequality with
C=\frac{2^{p+2}}{1-2^{-p}}
.
∎

The following theorem gives a local fractional Hardy inequality
for sets satisfying the upper Aikawa condition with suitable exponent.

Theorem 4.2
.

Let
1<q<p<\infty
and
0<s<1
.
Suppose that a closed set
E\subset X
satisfies the
upper Aikawa condition with exponent
\alpha=sq
.
Assume that
B(z,r)
and
F
are as in Lemma
3.1
, with either
0<r<\operatorname{diam}(E)
if

\operatorname{diam}(E)>0
, or
0<r<\operatorname{diam}(X)
if
\operatorname{diam}(E)=0
.
Then there exists a constant
C>0
independent of
z
and
r
such that

\int_{B(z,8r)\setminus F}\frac{\lvert u(x)\rvert^{p}}{\operatorname{dist}(x,F)^{sp}}\,dx\leq C\int_{B(z,8r)}\int_{B(z,8r)}\frac{\lvert u(x)-u(y)\rvert^{p}}{d(x,y)^{sp}\,\mu(B(x,d(x,y)))}\,dy\,dx

for all
u\in\operatorname{Lip}(X)
such that
u=0
in
F
.

Proof.

Let

u\in\operatorname{Lip}(X)
such that
u=0
in
F
.
By considering the Lipschitz functions

\max\{0,\lvert u(x)\rvert-\tau\}\,,\qquad\tau>0\,,

and applying Fatou’s lemma, we may assume that
u=0
in
F_{\tau}
for some

\tau>0
, where
F_{\tau}
is the
\tau
-neighborhood of
F
.
For
k\in{\mathbb{Z}}
, we
denote

E_{k}=\{x\in B(z,8r):|u(x)|>2^{k}\}

and we define a function
u_{k}:X\to[0,1]
by

u_{k}(x)=\begin{cases}1,&\text{if $|u(x)|\geq 2^{k+1}$,}\\
\frac{|u(x)|}{2^{k}}-1,&\text{if $2^{k}<|u(x)|<2^{k+1}$,}\\
0,&\text{if $|u(x)|\leq 2^{k}$.}\end{cases}

Then
u_{k}\in\operatorname{Lip}(X)
and it satisfies
u_{k}=1
on
E_{k+1}
and
u_{k}=0
on
B(z,8r)\setminus E_{k}\supset F
.

We estimate

(12)

\begin{split}\int_{B(z,8r)\setminus F}\frac{\lvert u(x)\rvert^{p}}{\operatorname{dist}(x,F)^{sp}}\,dx&\leq\sum_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}}2^{(k+3)p}\int_{E_{k+2}\setminus E_{k+3}}\frac{1}{\operatorname{dist}(x,F)^{sp}}\,dx\\
&\leq\sum_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}}2^{(k+3)p}\int_{E_{k+2}}\frac{1}{\operatorname{dist}(x,F)^{sp}}\,dx\\
&\leq\sum_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}}2^{(k+3)p}\int_{B(z,8r)\setminus F}\frac{u_{k+1}(x)^{p}}{\operatorname{dist}(x,F)^{sp}}\,dx\,.\end{split}

Fix
k\in{\mathbb{Z}}
. By the
5r
-covering lemma
[
3
, Lemma 1.7]
, there exists a cover of
B(z,2r)\setminus F

by balls
B_{i}=B(x_{i},r_{i})
,
i\in{\mathbb{N}}
, such that

\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\mathbf{1}_{B_{i}}\leq C(c_{\mu})
and

x_{i}\in B(z,2r)\setminus F
and
r_{i}=\operatorname{dist}(x_{i},F)/2

for every
i\in{\mathbb{N}}
.
We first estimate the integral over the smaller set
B(z,2r)\setminus F
as follows

(13)

\begin{split}\int_{B(z,2r)\setminus F}\frac{u_{k+1}(x)^{p}}{\operatorname{dist}(x,F)^{sp}}\,dx&\leq\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\int_{B(z,2r)\cap B_{i}}\frac{u_{k+1}(x)^{p}}{\operatorname{dist}(x,F)^{sp}}\,dx\\
&\leq\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}r_{i}^{-sp}\int_{B(z,2r)\cap B_{i}}u_{k+1}(x)^{p}\,dx\,.\end{split}

For every
x\in X
we write

\displaystyle g_{k}(x)

\displaystyle=\mathbf{1}_{B(z,8r)}(x)\left(\int_{B(z,8r)}\frac{\lvert u_{k}(x)-u_{k}(y)\rvert^{p}}{d(x,y)^{sp}\,\mu(B(x,d(x,y)))}\,dy\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}\,,

\displaystyle h_{k}(x)

\displaystyle=\mathbf{1}_{B(z,8r)\setminus F}(x)\frac{u_{k-1}(x)}{\operatorname{dist}(x,F)^{s}}\,.

Fix
i\in{\mathbb{N}}
and fix a point
x\in B(z,2r)\cap B_{i}
.
We aim to show that

(14)

u_{k+1}(x)^{p}\leq C(\delta,s,p,c_{\mu})r_{i}^{sp}\left(M(g_{k}^{q})(x)\right)^{p/q}+\varepsilon^{p/q}r_{i}^{sp}(M(h_{k}^{q})(x))^{p/q}\,,

where
\varepsilon>0
and
\delta>0
are as in the statement
of Lemma
3.3
for
\alpha=sq
. We will choose these parameters later.
Clearly, we may assume that
u_{k+1}(x)\not=0
.
Fix
m\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}
such that

2^{-m}r\leq\operatorname{dist}(x_{i},F)<2^{-m+1}r
. Write
B_{i}^{*}=B(x_{i},2^{-m+2}r)
. Observe
that
x\in B_{i}\subset B_{i}^{*}
.

There are two cases:

1.
(u_{k})_{B_{i}^{*}}<1-\delta
.
Since
u_{k+1}(x)\not=0
and
x\in B(z,2r)
, we have

x\in E_{k+1}
and
u_{k}(x)=1
. Therefore

(15)

1=\frac{1}{\delta}(1-(1-\delta))<\frac{1}{\delta}(u_{k}(x)-(u_{k})_{B_{i}^{*}})=\frac{1}{\delta}\lvert u_{k}(x)-(u_{k})_{B_{i}^{*}}\rvert\,.

Denote
B^{\prime}_{j}=B(x,2^{-j-m}r)
for each
j\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}
. We estimate

\displaystyle\lvert u_{k}(x)-(u_{k})_{B_{i}^{*}}\rvert\leq\lvert u_{k}(x)-(u_{k})_{B^{\prime}_{0}}\rvert+\lvert(u_{k})_{B^{\prime}_{0}}-(u_{k})_{B_{i}^{*}}\rvert.

Observe that
x\in B_{j}^{\prime}\subset B_{0}^{\prime}\subset B_{i}^{*}\subset B(z,8r)
for all
j\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}
.
Hence, by the
(s,1,q,p)
-Poincaré inequality
[
6
, Lemma 2.2]

\displaystyle\leq\mathchoice{\mathop{\kern 1.99997pt\vrule width=6.00006pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.49997pt\kern-8.00003pt\intop}\nolimits_{\kern-3.27773ptB(z,R)}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{B(z,R)}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{B(z,R)}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{B(z,R)}}\mathbf{1}_{E_{r}\cap K}(y)\,dy

\displaystyle\leq r^{\alpha}\mathchoice{\mathop{\kern 1.99997pt\vrule width=6.00006pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.49997pt\kern-8.00003pt\intop}\nolimits_{\kern-3.27773ptB(z,R)}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{B(z,R)}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{B(z,R)}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{B(z,R)}}\frac{\mathbf{1}_{E_{r}\cap K}(y)}{\operatorname{dist}(y,E)^{\alpha}}\,dy\leq\left(\frac{r}{R}\right)^{\alpha}R^{\alpha}\mathchoice{\mathop{\kern 1.99997pt\vrule width=6.00006pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.49997pt\kern-8.00003pt\intop}\nolimits_{\kern-3.27773ptB(z,R)}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{B(z,R)}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{B(z,R)}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{B(z,R)}}\frac{\mathbf{1}_{K}(y)}{\operatorname{dist}(y,E)^{\alpha}}\,dy

All in all, we get

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\leq\frac{c}{2}\theta^{Q-\alpha}=\frac{c}{2}\Bigl(\frac{r}{R}\Bigr)^{Q-\alpha}\leq R^{\alpha}\mathchoice{\mathop{\kern 1.99997pt\vrule width=6.00006pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.49997pt\kern-8.00003pt\intop}\nolimits_{\kern-3.27773ptB(z,R)}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{B(z,R)}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{B(z,R)}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{B(z,R)}}\frac{\mathbf{1}_{K}(y)}{\operatorname{dist}(y,E)^{\alpha}}\,dy\,,

which shows that
E
satisfies the upper Aikawa condition with exponent
\alpha
.
∎

Lemma 5.2
.

Let

1<q<p<\infty
and
0<s<1
. Assume that a set
E\subset X
satisfies the upper Aikawa condition with exponent

\alpha=sq
.
Then

\ucodima(E)\leq sp
.

Proof.

We first show that the closure
{\mathchoice{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\displaystyle E}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{E}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\textstyle E}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{E}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\scriptstyle E}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{E}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\scriptscriptstyle E}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{E}}}
satisfies
the upper Aikawa condition with exponent
\alpha=sq
.
This is clear if
\operatorname{diam}(E)=0
, so we can
assume that
\operatorname{diam}(E)>0
.
We let
\varepsilon>0

and denote
\varepsilon^{\prime}=\varepsilon/c_{\mu}^{2}>0
.
Let
\delta^{\prime}>0
be such that
E
satisfies
the upper Aikawa condition with the pair
\varepsilon^{\prime}
and
\delta^{\prime}
.
Choose
\delta=\delta^{\prime}/c_{\mu}^{2}
.
Let
z\in{\mathchoice{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\displaystyle E}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{E}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\textstyle E}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{E}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\scriptstyle E}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{E}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\scriptscriptstyle E}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{E}}}
,
0<R<\operatorname{diam}({\mathchoice{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\displaystyle E}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{E}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\textstyle E}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{E}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\scriptstyle E}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{E}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\scriptscriptstyle E}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{E}}})
and assume
that a Borel set
K\subset X
satisfies the condition

\mu(B(z,R)\setminus K)\leq\delta\mu(B(z,R))\,.

Fix a point
w\in B(z,R/2)\cap E
. Then
w\in E
,
0<R/2<\operatorname{diam}(E)
and

\displaystyle\mu(B(w,R/2)\setminus K)

\displaystyle\leq\mu(B(z,R)\setminus K)\leq\delta\mu(B(z,R))

\displaystyle\leq c_{\mu}^{2}\delta\mu(B(w,R/2))=\delta^{\prime}\mu(B(w,R/2))\,.

Therefore

R^{-\alpha}\leq(R/2)^{-\alpha}\leq\varepsilon^{\prime}\mathchoice{\mathop{\kern 1.99997pt\vrule width=6.00006pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.49997pt\kern-8.00003pt\intop}\nolimits_{\kern-3.27773ptB(w,R/2)}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{B(w,R/2)}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{B(w,R/2)}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{B(w,R/2)}}\frac{\mathbf{1}_{K}(y)}{\operatorname{dist}(y,E)^{\alpha}}\,dy\leq\varepsilon^{\prime}c_{\mu}^{2}\mathchoice{\mathop{\kern 1.99997pt\vrule width=6.00006pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.49997pt\kern-8.00003pt\intop}\nolimits_{\kern-3.27773ptB(z,R)}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{B(z,R)}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{B(z,R)}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{B(z,R)}}\frac{\mathbf{1}_{K}(y)}{\operatorname{dist}(y,{\mathchoice{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\displaystyle E}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{E}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\textstyle E}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{E}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\scriptstyle E}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{E}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\scriptscriptstyle E}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{E}}})^{\alpha}}\,dy

Since
\varepsilon^{\prime}c_{\mu}^{2}=\varepsilon
, the closure
{\mathchoice{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\displaystyle E}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{E}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\textstyle E}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{E}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\scriptstyle E}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{E}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\scriptscriptstyle E}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{E}}}
satisfies the upper Aikawa condition
with exponent
\alpha=sq
.

Next we adapt the proof of
[
6
, Theorem 5.3]
.
Fix
z\in E\subset{\mathchoice{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\displaystyle E}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{E}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\textstyle E}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{E}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\scriptstyle E}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{E}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\scriptscriptstyle E}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{E}}}
. We also fix either
0<r<R<\operatorname{diam}(E)

if
\operatorname{diam}(E)>0
, or
0<r<R<\operatorname{diam}(X)
if
\operatorname{diam}(E)=0
.
It suffices to show that

(19)

\frac{\mu(E_{r}\cap B(z,R))}{\mu(B(z,R))}\geq c\Bigl(\frac{r}{R}\Bigr)^{sp},

where the constant
c>0
is independent of
z
,
r
and
R
.

If
\mu(E_{r}\cap B(z,R))\geq\frac{1}{2}\mu(B(z,R))
, the claim is clear since
\bigl(\frac{r}{R}\bigr)^{sp}\leq 1
. Thus we may assume in the sequel that

\mu(E_{r}\cap B(z,R))<\tfrac{1}{2}\mu(B(z,R))
, whence

(20)

\mu(B(z,R)\setminus E_{r})\geq\tfrac{1}{2}\mu(B(z,R))>0.

We define a Lipschitz function
u\in\operatorname{Lip}(X)
by

u(x)=\min\{1,4r^{-1}\operatorname{dist}(x,E)\}\,,\qquad x\in X.

Then
u=0
in
{\mathchoice{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\displaystyle E}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{E}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\textstyle E}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{E}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\scriptstyle E}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{E}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\scriptscriptstyle E}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{E}}}
.
Since
u=1
in
X\setminus E_{r}
, we obtain

(21)

\begin{split}R^{-sp}\int_{B(z,R)}\lvert u(x)\rvert^{p}\,dx&\geq R^{-sp}\int_{B(z,R)\setminus E_{r}}\lvert u(x)\rvert^{p}\,dx\\
&=R^{-sp}\mu(B(z,R)\setminus E_{r})\geq\tfrac{1}{2}R^{-sp}\mu(B(z,R)),\end{split}

where the last step follows from (
20
).

Assume that
F\subset{\mathchoice{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\displaystyle E}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{E}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\textstyle E}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{E}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\scriptstyle E}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{E}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\scriptscriptstyle E}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{E}}}
is as in Lemma
3.1

applied with the closed set
{\mathchoice{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\displaystyle E}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{E}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\textstyle E}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{E}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\scriptstyle E}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{E}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\scriptscriptstyle E}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{E}}}
and the ball
B(z,R/8)
. Then
z\in F
and
u=0
in
F
. Using inequalities (
21
) and
Theorem
4.2
with the closed set
{\mathchoice{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\displaystyle E}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{E}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\textstyle E}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{E}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\scriptstyle E}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{E}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\scriptscriptstyle E}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{E}}}
, we get

(22)

\begin{split}C_{1}\int_{B(z,R)}\int_{B(z,R)}\frac{\lvert u(x)-u(y)\rvert^{p}}{d(x,y)^{sp}\,\mu(B(x,d(x,y)))}\,dy\,dx&\geq 2\int_{B(z,R)\setminus F}\frac{\lvert u(x)\rvert^{p}}{\operatorname{dist}(x,F)^{sp}}\,dx\\
&\geq 2R^{-sp}\int_{B(z,R)}\lvert u(x)\rvert^{p}\,dx\\
&\geq R^{-sp}\mu(B(z,R))\,,\end{split}

where the constant
C_{1}
is independent of
z
,
r
and
R
.
To prove the claim (
19
), it hence suffices to show that

(23)

\int_{B(z,R)}\int_{B(z,R)}\frac{\lvert u(x)-u(y)\rvert^{p}}{d(x,y)^{sp}\,\mu(B(x,d(x,y)))}\,dy\,dx\leq C_{2}r^{-sp}\mu(E_{r}\cap B(z,R))\,,

where the constant
C_{2}
is independent of
z
,
r
and
R
.
This estimate follows by arguing as in the proof of
[
6
, Theorem 5.3]
; we omit the somewhat
lengthy argument.
∎

Theorem 5.3
.

Let
E\subset X
be a nonempty set. Then
\ucodima(E)

is the infimum of all
\alpha>0
for which
E
satisfies
the upper Aikawa condition with exponent
\alpha
.

Proof.

Denote by
A
the set of all
\alpha>0
for which
E
satisfies
the upper Aikawa condition with exponent
\alpha
. Lemma
5.1
shows that

A\not=\emptyset
, and more specifically
(\ucodima(E),\infty)\subset A
. Therefore
\inf A\leq\ucodima(E)
. Conversely,
assume that
\alpha\in A
so that
E
satisfies the upper Aikawa condition with exponent
\alpha>0
. Choose
1<q<\infty
and
0<s<1
such that
\alpha=sq
. Fix

p>q
. Lemma
5.2
implies that

\ucodima(E)\leq sp
. Since
p>q
was arbitrary, it follows that

\ucodima(E)\leq sq=\alpha
. Since
\alpha\in A
was arbitrary, we find that

\ucodima(E)\leq\inf A
.
∎

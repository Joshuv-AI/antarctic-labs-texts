Finite group actions on dg categories and Hochschild homology
Ville Nordström

Abstract
We prove a decomposition of the Hochschild homology groups of the equivariant dg category 𝒞 G \mathscr{C}^{G} associated to a small dg category 𝒞 \mathscr{C} with direct sums on which a finite group G G acts. When the ground field is ℂ \mathbb{C} this decomposition is related to a categorical action of Rep ​ ( G ) \text{Rep}(G) on 𝒞 G \mathscr{C}^{G} and the resulting action of the representation ring R ℂ ​ ( G ) R_{\mathbb{C}}(G) on H ​ H ∙ ​ ( 𝒞 G ) HH_{\bullet}(\mathscr{C}^{G}) .

Introduction

Let
\mathscr{C}
be a dg category over a field of characteristic zero and
G
. If

is a finite group which acts on
\mathscr{C}
via autoequivalences

we can consider the dg category

of equivariant objects in

is the module category of a dg algebra

then it follows from
[
6
]
(
[
2
]
in the case of an ordinary algebra) that

\displaystyle HH_{\bullet}(\mathscr{C}^{G})\cong\bigoplus_{[g]}HH_{\bullet}(\mathscr{C};\rho_{g})^{C(g)}

(1)

where

denotes the conjugacy class of
g
and
C(g)
is the centralizer of
g
in
G
. In this note we prove that this decomposition holds assuming only that
\mathscr{C}
is a small dg category that admits finite direct sums. To our knowledge this has not appeared in the literature. Even in situations where this decomposition was already known we believe proving it directly in the dg category setting is illuminating. The projection and inclusion maps

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces HH_{\bullet}(\mathscr{C}^{G})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces HH_{\bullet}(\mathscr{C};\rho_{g})^{C(g)}}

associated to this decomposition arise very naturally in the dg category setting; they are induced by two dg functors

\mathscr{C}^{G}\overset{For}{\to}\mathscr{C},\quad\mathscr{C}\overset{S}{\to}\mathscr{C}^{G}

and natural transformations

S\circ\rho_{g}\cong S,\ \rho_{g}\circ For\cong For.

Moreover, in the dg category setting this decomposition has another interesting explanation when the ground field is
\mathbb{C}
. Namely, we explain how the representation ring of
G
over
\mathbb{C}
,
R_{\mathbb{C}}(G)
acts on
HH_{\bullet}(\mathscr{C}^{G})
. We then prove that the decomposition above matches up with the decomposition of the
R_{\mathbb{C}}(G)
-module

HH_{\bullet}(\mathscr{C}^{G})=\bigoplus_{[g]}\varphi_{[g]}\cdot HH_{\bullet}(\mathscr{C}^{G})

where
\varphi_{[g]}\in R_{\mathbb{C}}(G)

denotes the indicator function on the conjugacy class of

.

Finally we note that as a consequence of the decomposition (
1
) above we have that for any small dg category which admits finite sums,
\text{Sym}^{n}(HH_{\bullet}(\mathscr{C}))
appears naturally as a summand in
HH_{\bullet}(\text{Sym}^{n}(\mathscr{C}))
. This is related to Conjecture 3.24 in
[
1
]
.

In
[
4
]
, Perry establishes a decomposition analogous to (
1
) for Hochschild cohomology also using the functors
For
and
S
mentioned above. Apart from that however our approaches differ in the following way. They use adjoint properties of these functors and the description of Hochschild cohomology as a derived hom between bimodules. In general one cannot describe Hochschild homology as derived hom between bimodules so another approach is necessary. We use instead that Hochschild homology is functorial with respect to dg functors, something which is not true for cohomology, to produce the projections and inclusions associated to the decomposition (

1.1
Summary of paper

In section 2 we introduce group actions on dg categories. In section 3.1 we recall some basic facts about dg categories. In section 3.2 we recall the definition of Hochschild homology of dg categories with values in an endofunctor and prove a few simple lemmas. In section 3.3 we recall the Künneth formula for Hochschild homology. Although the Künneth formula is well known, we could not find a proof which both provides an explicit formula and works at the level of generality that we need so we include a proof as well. Finally in section 4.1 we state and prove our main result and in section 4.2 we describe an application of it.

1.2
Conventions

All vector spaces will be over a fixed ground field
k
of characteristic zero. Sometimes we will need
k=\mathbb{C}
and will then make this clear.

All functors between dg categories will be dg functors. By a natural transformation between two dg functors
F,F^{\prime}:\mathscr{C}\to\mathscr{B}
we will always mean a closed degree zero natural transformations. An equivalence
F:\mathscr{C}\to\mathscr{B}
between dg categories will mean a dg functor that admits an inverse
F^{-1}:\mathscr{B}\to\mathscr{C}
such that
F\circ F^{-1}\cong\mathrm{id}_{\mathscr{B}}
and
F^{-1}\circ F\cong\mathrm{id}_{\mathscr{C}}
and these natural isomorphisms are required to be degree zero cycles.

Our convention on matrices is that they act on the left. So when we describe a morphism in some category between objects with specified direct sum decompositions by a matrix, the columns will be indexed by the summands of the source and the rows by the summands of the target.

2
Finite group actions on dg categories

When talking about an action of a finite group
G
on a dg category
\mathscr{C}
there are different levels of strictness that one can require. Here is the definition that we will work with.

Definition 1
.

An action of
G
on
\mathscr{C}
is the data of an autoequivalence
\rho_{g}:\mathscr{C}\overset{\sim}{\to}\mathscr{C}
for every
g\in G
, natural isomorphisms
\theta_{g,g^{\prime}}:\rho_{g}\circ\rho_{g^{\prime}}\overset{\sim}{\implies}\rho_{g^{\prime}g}
and
\eta:\rho_{e}\overset{\sim}{\implies}\mathrm{id}_{\mathscr{C}}
and these should satisfy the following two conditions.

i) For each
g\in G
and

we have

(\theta_{e,g})_{c}=\eta_{\rho_{g}(c)}:\rho_{e}\rho_{g}(c)\to\rho_{g}(c)

and

(\theta_{g,e})_{c}=\rho_{g}(\eta_{c}):\rho_{g}\rho_{e}(c)\to\rho_{g}(c).

ii) For all
g,h,k\in G
we have commutative diagrams

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\rho_{g}\rho_{h}\rho_{k}(c)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\rho_{g}\rho_{kh}(c)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\rho_{hg}\rho_{k}(c)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\rho_{khg}(c)}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\rho_{g}((\theta_{h,k})_{c})}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(\theta_{g,h})_{\rho_{k}(c)}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(\theta_{g,kh})_{c}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(\theta_{hg,k})_{c}}

Remark 2
.

One could also consider a more lax notion of group action where each group element acts via a quasi equivalence and the equalities of morphisms in the two conditions above are replaced by specified homotopies. However, we don’t know of interesting examples where such group actions arise. The type of group action we defined above on the other hand does arise. For example if
G
acts on a smooth variety

then we get a group action in the above sense on the dg category of perfect complexes
\text{perf}(X)
or on the dg category of matrix factorizations

of some function

by pulling back vector bundles.

Whenever we have a group action as above we can consider the category of
G
-equivariant objects in
\mathscr{C}
.

Hochschild homology of dg categories

3.1
Some background on dg categories

Here we give a brief summary of dg categories and their modules. We follow section 2.1 of
[
5
]
.

A right module over dg category
\mathscr{C}
is a dg functor
M:\mathscr{C}^{op}\to\underline{\text{Com}}(k)
where the later is the dg category whose objects are chain complexes of vector spaces and whose morphism complexes are defined in the usual way. Similarly we define a left module as functors
\mathscr{C}\to\underline{\text{Com}}(k)
. The collection of right (resp. left) dg modules themselves form a dg category in a natural way which we denote
mod-\mathscr{C}
(resp.
\mathscr{C}-mod
). For any
c\in\mathsf{Ob}(\mathscr{C})
we denote by
h_{c}
the right module represented by
c
. The category
Z^{0}(mod-\mathscr{C})
has the structure of an exact category where a sequence of modules

M\to M^{\prime\prime}\to M

is defined to be exact if for any
c\in\mathsf{Ob}(\mathscr{C})

both

M(c)\to M^{\prime\prime}(c)\to M^{\prime}(c)

is an exact sequence of complexes and

H(M(c))\to H(M^{\prime\prime}(c))\to H(M^{\prime}(c))

is an exact sequence of graded vector spaces.

Lemma 6
.

If
V
is any chain complex of vector spaces, and

then the functor

Proof.

Consider a diagram

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces X}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces V\otimes h_{c}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces M.}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\pi}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\varphi}

The map

\hat{\varphi}\in\text{Hom}_{Z^{0}(mod-\mathscr{C})}(h_{c},\text{Hom}_{k}^{\bullet}(V,M(-))=\text{Hom}_{Ch(k)}(V,M(c))

where
\text{Hom}_{Ch(k)}(A,B)
denotes chain maps between complexes of vector spaces. Because

and

are both surjective the induced map

\text{Hom}_{Ch(k)}(V,X(c))\to\text{Hom}_{Ch(k)}(V,M(c))

∎

If
\mathscr{C}
and
\mathscr{B}
are two dg categories we can form their tensor product

When defining composition of morphisms in

A right module over

For any

We denote by

the left

There is a standard way to resolve

3.2
Hochschild homology

We first define the standard complex which we will use to compute Hochschild homology

Definition 7
.

Let
\mathscr{C}
be a small dg category and let
F:\mathscr{C}\to\mathscr{C}
be an endofunctor. Set

C_{n}(\mathscr{C};F):=\bigoplus_{c_{0},...,c_{n}\in\mathscr{C}}\mathscr{C}(c_{1},F(c_{0}))\otimes\mathscr{C}(c_{2},c_{1})\otimes...\otimes\mathscr{C}(c_{0},c_{n}).

There is a differential
d_{2}:C_{n}(\mathscr{C};F)\to C_{n-1}(\mathscr{C};F)
defined by

\displaystyle d_{2}\big(a_{0}[a_{1}|\cdots|a_{n}]\big)

\displaystyle=a_{0}a_{1}[a_{2}|\cdots|a_{n}]

\displaystyle+\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}(-1)^{i}a_{0}[\cdots|a_{i}a_{i+1}|\cdots]

\displaystyle+(-1)^{n+|a_{n}|(|a_{0}|+...+|a_{n-1}|)}F(a_{n})a_{0}[a_{1}|\cdots|a_{n-1}].

Each
C_{n}(\mathscr{C};F)
is itself a cohomologically graded chain complex
(C_{n}(\mathscr{C};F)^{\bullet},d_{1})
. The differentials
d_{1}
and
d_{2}
commute and therefore we get a total complex (cohomologically graded) by altering the sign of
d_{1}
by

on

whose

th term for

is

\bigoplus_{n-m=k}C_{m}(\mathscr{C};F)^{n}.

The homology of this complex is the Hochschild homology of
\mathscr{C}
with values in
F
and it is denoted
HH_{\bullet}(\mathscr{C};F)
.

.

Different sign conventions appear for the Hochschild differential but they all give rise to isomorphic chain complexes and so we use this one as it is the least complicated. This sign convention appears for example in

]

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathscr{C}(c_{1},F(c_{0}))\otimes...\otimes\mathscr{C}(c_{0},c_{n})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathscr{C}^{\prime}(\phi(c_{1}),F^{\prime}\circ\phi(c_{0}))\otimes...\otimes\mathscr{C}^{\prime}(\phi(c_{0}),\phi(c_{n}))}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces f_{0}[f_{1}|\cdots|f_{n}]}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\epsilon_{c_{0}}\phi(f_{0})\big[\phi(f_{1})|\cdots|\phi(f_{n})\big].}

We will refer to the data of such a

3.3
Künneth isomorphism

In this section we give a proof of the Künneth isomorphism for Hochschild homology and check that it is compatible with the
S_{2}
action.
Given two dg categories
\mathscr{C}
and
\mathscr{B}
we define

Sh:C_{\bullet}(\mathscr{C})\otimes C_{\bullet}(\mathscr{B})\to C_{\bullet}(\mathscr{C}\otimes\mathscr{B})

by the formula

\displaystyle f_{0}[f_{1}|\cdots|f_{k}]\otimes g_{0}[g_{1}|\cdots|g_{l}]\mapsto\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad

\displaystyle\sum_{\sigma\in(k,l)-shuffles}(-1)^{|\sigma|+\epsilon}f_{0}\otimes g_{0}[\cdots|f_{i}\otimes 1|\cdots|1\otimes g_{j}|\cdots|f_{i+1}\otimes 1|\cdots|1\otimes g_{j}|\cdots].

The sign in the formula above is given by

\epsilon=\sum|g_{t}||f_{s}|

where the sum is over all pairs
(f_{s},g_{t})
so that
f_{s}
appears after
g_{t}
.

Proposition 12
.

The map
Sh
defined above induces an isomorphism

HH_{\bullet}(\mathscr{C})\otimes HH_{\bullet}(\mathscr{B})\to HH_{\bullet}(\mathscr{C}\otimes\mathscr{B}).

When
\mathscr{C}=\mathscr{B}
this isomorphism commutes with the
S_{2}
action.

Before we prove the proposition we recall some facts about Hochschild homology. Just like for algebras Hochschild homology can be defined in a ”basis independent” way as
\Delta_{\mathscr{C}}^{r}\overset{L}{\otimes}\Delta_{\mathscr{C}}^{l}
. As we show in the lemma below, the standard complex is then obtained by computing this derived tensor product using the bar resolution of
\Delta_{\mathscr{C}}^{l}
described in section
3.1
.

Lemma 13
.

For each n we have an isomorphism of chain complexes

C_{n}(\mathscr{C})\cong\Delta_{\mathscr{C}}^{r}\otimes_{\mathscr{C}\otimes\mathscr{C}^{op}}\text{Bar}^{n}(\mathscr{C}).

Moreover, this isomorphism is compatible with the Hochschild and Bar differentials and so it gives an isomorphism

C_{\bullet}(\mathscr{C})\cong Tot^{\oplus}(\Delta_{\mathscr{C}}^{r}\otimes_{\mathscr{C}\otimes\mathscr{C}^{op}}Bar^{\bullet}(\mathscr{C})).

Proof.

The complex on the right is the cokernel of

\bigoplus_{(x,y),(x^{\prime},y^{\prime})}\mathscr{C}(x,y)\otimes(\mathscr{C}(y^{\prime},y)\otimes\mathscr{C}(x,x^{\prime}))\otimes\text{Bar}^{n}(\mathscr{C})(y^{\prime},x^{\prime})\to\bigoplus_{(x,y)}\mathscr{C}(x,y)\otimes\text{Bar}^{n}(y,x)

f\otimes(\alpha\otimes\beta)\otimes B\mapsto(-1)^{|\alpha||f|}\alpha f\beta\otimes B-(-1)^{(|B|+|\beta|)|\alpha|}f\otimes\beta B\alpha.

The isomorphism is given induced by

\bigoplus_{(x,y)}\mathscr{C}(x,y)\otimes\text{Bar}^{n}(y,x)\to C_{n}(\mathscr{C})

f\otimes b_{0}|b_{1}|\cdots|b_{n+1}\mapsto(-1)^{|b_{n+1}|(|b_{0}|+...+|b_{n}|+|f|)}b_{n+1}fb_{0}[b_{1}|\cdots b_{n}]

and its inverse sends
a_{0}[a_{1}|\cdots|a_{n}]
to the image of
a_{0}\otimes 1|a_{1}|...|a_{n}|1
in the cokernel.
∎

Now we again consider an action of a finite group

The assignment

on

Proof.

We have

\displaystyle(\rho_{h},C_{h,g})\circ(\rho_{h^{\prime}},C_{h^{\prime},g})=(\rho_{h}\circ\rho_{h^{\prime}},(C_{h,g})_{\rho_{h^{\prime}}}\circ\rho_{h}(C_{h^{\prime},g})).

The tensor product functor

\varphi_{[g]}HH_{\bullet}(\mathscr{C}^{G})\subset HH_{\bullet}(\mathscr{C}^{G})

4.1
The case of symmetric powers

Now for any dg category
\mathscr{C}
we can define its tensor powers. Recall that by definition
\mathscr{C}^{\otimes n}
is the dg category with objects
x_{\bullet}=(x_{1},x_{2},...,x_{n})
with each
x_{i}\in\mathsf{Ob}(\mathscr{C})
. The homomorphism spaces are given by
Hom_{\mathscr{C}^{\otimes n}}(x_{\bullet},y_{\bullet}):=\mathscr{C}(x_{1},y_{1})\otimes\mathscr{C}(x_{2},y_{2})\otimes...\otimes\mathscr{C}(x_{n},y_{n})
.
There is a right action of
S^{n}
on
\mathscr{C}
defined on objects by
\sigma(x_{1},...,x_{n})=(x_{\sigma(1)},...,x_{\sigma(n)}).
This action is strict in the strongest possible sense. All the higher categorical data is trivial,
\theta_{\sigma,\tau}=\mathrm{id}
,
\eta=\mathrm{id}
. Let
\widehat{\mathscr{C}^{\otimes n}}
be the pre triangulated hull of
\mathscr{C}^{\otimes n}
and note that
S_{n}
still acts on
\widehat{\mathscr{C}^{\otimes n}}
. If we set
\text{Sym}^{n}(\mathscr{C})=(\widehat{\mathscr{C}^{\otimes n}})^{S_{n}}
then the theorem tells us

HH_{\bullet}(\text{Sym}^{n}(\mathscr{C}))=\bigoplus_{[\sigma]\in S_{n}/\sim}HH_{\bullet}\big(\widehat{\mathscr{C}^{\otimes n}},\rho_{\sigma}\big)^{C(\sigma)}.

In particular it contains one summand (the one corresponding to the identity permutation)

HH_{\bullet}\big(\widehat{\mathscr{C}^{\otimes n}}\big)^{S_{n}}\cong HH_{\bullet}(\mathscr{C}^{\otimes n})^{S_{n}}\cong(HH_{\bullet}(\mathscr{C})^{\otimes n})^{S_{n}}=\text{Sym}^{n}(HH_{\bullet}(\mathscr{C}))

where the second isomorphism is the Künneth isomorphism (which is an isomorphism of
S_{n}
-modules). We get the following corollary.

Corollary 17
.

If
\mathscr{C}
is a small dg category then
HH_{\bullet}(\text{Sym}^{n}(\mathscr{C}))
contains naturally
\text{Sym}^{n}(HH_{\bullet}(\mathscr{C}))
as a summand.

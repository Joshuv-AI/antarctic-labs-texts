Abstract
. The association of subordination and special functions is used to find sharp estimates on the parameter β \beta such that the analytic function p ⁡ ( z ) p(z) is subordinate to certain functions having positive real part whenever p ⁡ ( z ) + β ​ z ​ p ′ ​ ( z ) p(z)+\beta zp^{\prime}(z) is subordinate to the Janowski function. Further, when the traditional approach of solving higher order differential subordination implications failed, the concept of admissibility is employed to establish certain second and third order differential subordination relations between the analytic function p p and the functions associated with right half plane. As a sequel, we demonstrated the starlikeness of various well-known analytic functions as well.

Let

The theory of subordination developed by Miller and Mocanu

We denote the class of

Let
\Omega
and
\Delta
be the improper sets in the complex plane and
\psi:\mathbb{C}^{3}\times\mathbb{D}\to\mathbb{D}
be the function.
The search of finding the existence of the smallest set
\Delta\subset\mathbb{C}
and the largest set
\Omega\subset\mathbb{C}
such that the implication

\psi(p(z),zp^{\prime}(z),z^{2}p^{\prime\prime}(z);z)\subset\Omega\implies p(z)\subset\Delta;z\in\mathbb{D}

holds, gave rise to the concept of admissibility conditions.
Several authors have been implementing this approach in the subordination theory to find sufficient conditions for starlikeness, convexity, boundedness and other geometric properties.

In the first theorem of this paper, we use the theory of hypergeometric functions to obtain sharp estimates on
\beta
so that whenever
p(z)+\beta zp^{\prime}(z)
is subordinate to
Janowski
function implies
p(z)
is subordinate to certain functions with positive real part. Subordination relations for the particular cases are demonstrated graphically. As a consequence, sufficient conditions for certain type of starlikeness of the function
f\in\mathcal{A}
are also derived. Let us recall some preliminaries in order to obtain our required bounds.
The admissibility conditions are the key tool which is used to solve some second and third order differential subordination relations. This is achieved by extending the pre existing literature on admissibility conditions for the
exponential
function.

Definition 1.1
.

(Gauss Hypergeometric Function):

For
|z|<1
and parameters
a,b\in\mathbb{C}
and
c\notin\{0\cup\mathbb{Z}_{-}\}
, the hypergeometric function
{}_{2}F_{1}(a,b;c;z)
is defined by the convergent power series

F(a,b;c;z)={}_{2}F_{1}(a,b;c;z)=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{(a)_{k}(b)_{k}}{(c)_{k}k!}z^{k}.

(1.1)

The function
F(a,b;c;z)
is analytic in
\mathbb{C}
and is one of the solutions of the differential equation

z(1-z)y^{\prime\prime}+[c-(a+b+1)z]y^{\prime}-aby=0

at
z=0
.
The differential of the function
F(a,b;c;z)
satisfies the relation

\frac{\partial F(a,b;c;z)}{\partial z}=\frac{ab}{c}F(a+1,b+1;c+1;z).

Definition 1.2
.

(Order of Starlikeness):

For an analytic function
f
, its order of starlikeness with respect to zero is defined as follows:

\sigma(f):=\inf_{z\in\mathbb{D}}\operatorname{Re}\left(\frac{zf^{\prime}(z)}{f(z)}\right)\in[-\infty,1]

In the proof of our first result, the following results are used:

Lemma 1.3
.

[
23
, Theorem 3.4h, p.132]

Let
q:\mathbb{D}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
be analytic and
\psi
and
v
be analytic in a domain
U\supseteq q(\mathbb{D})
with
\psi(w)\neq 0
whenever
w\in q(\mathbb{D})
. Set

Q(z):=zq^{\prime}(z)\psi(q(z))\quad\text{and}\quad h(z):=v(q(z))+Q(z),z\in\mathbb{D}.

Suppose that

(i)

either
h(z)
is convex, or
Q(z)
is starlike univalent in
\mathbb{D}
and

(ii)

\operatorname{Re}\left(\frac{zh^{\prime}(z)}{Q(z)}\right)>0,z\in\mathbb{D}
.

If
p
is analytic in
\mathbb{D}
, with
p(0)=q(0)
,
p(\mathbb{D})\subset U
and

v(p(z))+zp^{\prime}(z)\psi(p(z))\prec v(q(z))+zq^{\prime}(z)\psi(q(z))

then
p\prec q
, and
q
is the best dominant.

Theorem 1.4
.

[
15
, Theorem 1(a)]

Let
a,b
and
c
be non-zero real numbers such that
0<a\leq b\leq c
. Then,

1-\frac{ab}{b+c}\leq\sigma(zF(a,b;c;z))\leq 1-\frac{ab}{2c}.

Proof.

Let the analytic function

q_{\beta}(z):=\frac{A-B}{\beta+1}z\left(F(1,1+\frac{1}{\beta};2+\frac{1}{\beta};-Bz)\right)+\frac{\beta}{\beta+1}+\frac{1}{\beta+1}

For

\psi(w):=\beta
.
Let

\displaystyle Q(z)

\displaystyle=zq_{\beta}^{\prime}(z)\psi(q_{\beta}(z))

\displaystyle=\beta zq_{\beta}^{\prime}(z)

\displaystyle=\beta z\biggr[\frac{A-B}{\beta+1}\left(F(1,1+\frac{1}{\beta};2+\frac{1}{\beta};-Bz)\right)+\frac{A-B}{2\beta+1}z\left(F(2,2+\frac{1}{\beta};3+\frac{1}{\beta};-Bz)\right)\biggr].

From the hypergeometric functions

Since

\sigma\left(zF(2,2+\frac{1}{\beta};3+\frac{1}{\beta};-Bz)\right)\geq 1-\frac{2+4\beta}{2+5\beta}=\frac{\beta}{2+5\beta}>0.

Therefore, the function

Also, the function

Since

Further, using the Lemma

The study of univalent funtions and differential subordination is a very vast domain and it is difficult to obtain all the subordination implications following the same methods. A great deal of research is being done in this direction to explore different ways to solve higher order differential subordination relations. Miller and Mocanu in their monograph mentioned the concept of admissible functions. Recently, authors in
[
29
]
studied first and second order differential subordination using this approach.

Definition 2.4
.

Consider the analytic functions having Taylor series expansion

Then,

denote the class of all such functions for some

and fixed integer

Definition 2.5
.

[

Analogous result for the second order differential subordination is stated as below:

Theorem 2.10

denote an analytic function with

Proof.

Set
\Omega=\{w\in\mathbb{C}:|\log w|<1\}
and define the function

\displaystyle|\psi(r,s,t;z)-1|

\displaystyle=|\alpha s+t|

\alpha z^{3}p^{\prime\prime\prime}(z)+\beta z^{2}p^{\prime\prime}(z)+\gamma zp^{\prime}(z)\prec\phi_{SG}(z)
, where

\gamma\geq\begin{cases}\beta_{0}e+\alpha+\beta^{2}/8\alpha,&\text{when }\beta\leq 4\alpha\\
\beta_{0}e-\alpha+\beta,&\text{when }\beta\geq 4\alpha.\end{cases}

(ii)

\alpha\left(z^{3}p^{\prime\prime\prime}(z)/zp^{\prime}(z)\right)+\beta\left(zp^{\prime}(z)/p(z)+1\right)\prec\phi_{SG}(z)
, where

\begin{cases}\beta\geq\beta_{0}+\alpha+\beta^{2}/8\alpha,&\text{when }\beta\leq 4\alpha\\
\alpha\geq\beta_{0},&\text{when }\beta\geq 4\alpha.\end{cases}

Proof.

Let
\Omega=\{w\in\mathbb{C}:\left|\log\left(w/(2-w)\right)\right|<1\}
be the domain.

(i)

Define the function
\psi(r,s,t,u;z)=\alpha u+\beta t+\gamma s
.
Using
[
29
, Lemma 2.7]
, which says
\left|\log\left(z/(2-z)\right)\right|\geq 1
if and only if
|z|\geq\beta_{0}
, we have

\displaystyle|\psi(r,s,t,u;z)|

\displaystyle=|\alpha u+\beta t+\gamma s|

\displaystyle=|s|\left|\alpha\frac{u}{s}+\beta\left(\frac{t}{s}+1\right)+\gamma-\beta\right|

\displaystyle\geq e^{-1}\left|\alpha\frac{u}{s}+\beta\left(\frac{t}{s}+1\right)+\gamma-\beta\right|

\displaystyle\geq e^{-1}\left\{\alpha\operatorname{Re}\frac{u}{s}+\beta\operatorname{Re}\left(\frac{t}{s}+1\right)+\gamma-\beta\right\}

\displaystyle\geq e^{-1}(\alpha\cos 2\theta+\beta(1+\cos\theta)+\gamma-\beta).

For
x=\cos\theta
, let us consider
g(x):=\alpha(2x^{2}-1)+\beta(1+x)+\gamma-\beta
.

Case 1:

\beta\leq 4\alpha
. Observe that
g^{\prime}(x)=0
if
x=-\beta/4\alpha
. Since
the minimum value of the function
g
occurs at
x=-\beta/4\alpha
, we have

|\psi(r,s,t,u;z)|\geq e^{-1}\left(\gamma-\alpha-\beta^{2}/8\alpha\right)\geq\beta_{0}.

Hence, the required result follows from Theorem (
2.13
).

Case 2:

\beta>4\alpha
. Since
-\beta/(4\alpha)\leq-1
,
g^{\prime}(x)\neq 0
, it follows that
the function
g
is increasing and thus,

|\psi(r,s,t,u;z)|\geq e^{-1}(\alpha-\beta+\gamma)\geq\beta_{0}

and hence, the result follows from Theorem (
2.13
).

(ii)

Consider the function
\psi(r,s,t,u;z)=\alpha\left(u/s\right)+\beta\left(s/r+1\right)
.
Using
[
29
, Lemma 2.7]
, which says
\left|\log\left(z/(2-z)\right)\right|\geq 1
if and only if
|z|\geq\beta_{0}
, we have

\displaystyle|\psi(r,s,t,u;z)|

\displaystyle=\left|\alpha\left(\frac{u}{s}\right)+\beta\left(\frac{s}{r}+1\right)\right|

\displaystyle\geq\beta+\beta\operatorname{Re}\left(\frac{s}{r}\right)+\alpha\operatorname{Re}\left(\frac{u}{s}\right)

\displaystyle\geq\beta(1+\cos\theta)+\alpha\cos 2\theta.

For
x=\cos\theta
, the function
m(x):=\beta(1+x)+\alpha(2x^{2}-1)
will attain its minimum value in the following two cases:

Case 1:

\beta\leq 4\alpha
. Note that the function
m
attains its minimum value at
x=-\beta/4\alpha
and thus, we have

|\psi(r,s,t,u;z)|\geq\beta-\alpha-\beta^{2}/8\alpha\geq\beta_{0}.

Henceforth, the desired result follows from Theorem (
2.13
).

Case 2:

\beta>4\alpha
. Then
-\beta/(4\alpha)<-1
. Thus, the function
m(x)
is increasing and

|\psi(r,s,t,u;z)|\geq\alpha\geq\beta_{0}

and hence, the result follows from Theorem (
2.13
).

∎

Corollary 2.17
.

As an application, we obtain sufficient conditions for the function
f\in\mathcal{A}
to be exponential starlike in
\mathbb{D}
by substituting
p(z)=zf^{\prime}(z)/f(z)
.

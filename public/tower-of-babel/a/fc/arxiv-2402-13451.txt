On the Folklore set and Dirichlet spectrum for matrices
Mumtaz Hussain, Johannes Schleischitz, Benjamin Ward

Abstract
. We study the Folklore set of Dirichlet improvable matrices in ℝ m × n \mathbb{R}^{m\times n} which are neither singular nor badly approximable. We prove the non-emptiness for all positive integer pairs m , n m,n apart from { m , n } = { 1 , 1 } \{m,n\}=\{1,1\} and { m , n } = { 2 , 3 } \{m,n\}=\{2,3\} in a constructive manner. For a wide range of integer pairs ( m , n ) (m,n) we construct subsets of the Folklore set with an exact prescribed Dirichlet constant (in some right neighbourhood of 0 0 ). This enables us to provide information on the Dirichlet Spectrum of matrices. The key technique of our construction is to build first vectors of a given Diophantine type, and then to show that most ‘liftings’ to matrices will preserve this Diophantine type. This is a variant of a method introduced by Moshchevitin for uniform approximation. Our technique is often also applicable to arbitrary norms. As a corollary we obtain lower bounds on the Hausdorff dimension of these sets. These statements complement previous results of the middle-named author (Selecta Math. 2023), Beresnevich et. al. (Adv. Math. 2023), and Das et. al. (Adv. Math. 2024).



Keywords
: Dirichlet spectrum, Folklore set, Uniform approximation, Singular sets, linear forms, Hausdorff dimension.

Math Subject Classification 2010: 11J13, 11J82, 11J83, 11K60

1.
Uniform approximation for Systems of linear forms and arbitrary norms

Let
n,m\in\mathbb{N}
, and let
\|\cdot\|_{1}
be any norm on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
\|\cdot\|_{2}
any norm on
\mathbb{R}^{m}
. For a given real
m\times n
matrix
\Omega\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times n}

and a parameter
t\geq 1
define the piecewise constant approximation function

\psi_{\Omega}^{m\times n}(\|\cdot\|_{1},\|\cdot\|_{2},t):=\underset{1\leq\|\boldsymbol{\hat{b}}\|_{1}\leq t}{\min_{\boldsymbol{b}=(\hat{\boldsymbol{b}},\tilde{\boldsymbol{b}})\in\mathbb{Z}^{n+m}:}}\left\|\Omega\cdot\widehat{\boldsymbol{b}}+\tilde{\boldsymbol{b}}\right\|_{2}=\underset{1\leq\|\boldsymbol{\hat{b}}\|_{1}\leq t}{\min_{\boldsymbol{b}=(\hat{\boldsymbol{b}},\tilde{\boldsymbol{b}})\in\mathbb{Z}^{n+m}:}}\left\|\sum_{i=1}^{n}\Omega_{i}b_{i}+\tilde{\boldsymbol{b}}\right\|_{2},

where
\Omega_{i}
denotes the
i
th column of the matrix
\Omega
, and

(1)

\hat{\boldsymbol{b}}=(b_{1},\dots,b_{n}),\qquad\tilde{\boldsymbol{b}}=(b_{n+1},\dots,b_{n+m}),\qquad\boldsymbol{b}=(\hat{\boldsymbol{b}},\tilde{\boldsymbol{b}})

interpreted as column vectors. The column vectors
\boldsymbol{b}
for which the minimum is attained for some
t
, are often called best approximations of
\Omega
. See works of Lagarias
[
22
,
23
]
and the survey articles
[
26
,
11
]
for the history and further details on best approximations in dimensions greater than
1
.

Define the Dirichlet constant of a matrix as

\Theta^{m\times n}(\|\cdot\|_{1},\|\cdot\|_{2},\Omega)=\limsup_{t\to\infty}\;t^{n/m}\psi_{\Omega}^{m\times n}(\|\cdot\|_{1},\|\cdot\|_{2},t),

and further let

\widetilde{\Theta}^{m\times n}(\|\cdot\|_{1},\|\cdot\|_{2},\Omega)=\liminf_{t\to\infty}\;t^{n/m}\psi_{\Omega}^{m\times n}(\|\cdot\|_{1},\|\cdot\|_{2},t).

The values
\Theta^{m\times n}
and
\widetilde{\Theta}^{m\times n}

compare the approximation quality with
the standard Dirichlet function
t^{-n/m}
in the uniform and ordinary settings, respectively. By equivalence of norms and Dirichlet’s Theorem, for each pair
(m,n)
and each pair of norms
\|\cdot\|_{1},\|\cdot\|_{2}
we can derive the constants

(2)

D=D^{m\times n}(\|\cdot\|_{1},\|\cdot\|_{2}):=\sup_{\Omega\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times n}}\Theta^{m\times n}(\|\cdot\|_{1},\|\cdot\|_{2},\Omega)<\infty.

For
\|\cdot\|_{1}
and
\|\cdot\|_{2}
the standard maximum norms, we have
D=1
, with the upper estimate coming from Minkowski’s Theorem for systems of linear forms. This value is attained for Lebesgue almost all matrices by well-known metrical results. For any pair
(m,n)
and norms
\|\cdot\|_{1},\|\cdot\|_{2}
we follow
[
2
]
and define the
Dirichlet Spectrum
to be the set

(3)

\mathcal{D}_{m,n}(\|\cdot\|_{1},\|\cdot\|_{2}):=\left\{\Theta^{m\times n}(\|\cdot\|_{1},\|\cdot\|_{2},\Omega):\Omega\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times n}\right\}\subseteq[0,D]\,.

When the norms are clear we will omit them from notation. For results on the Dirichlet spectrum with respect to the maximum norm see
[
31
, Appendix]
and more completely
[
30
]
. In
[
30
]
it is proven that the spectrum is the entire unit interval in the dual setting with the max norm,
i.e.
\mathcal{D}_{1,n}(\|\cdot\|_{\infty})=[0,1]
. For results on the Dirichlet spectrum in the simultaneous approximation setting for various norms on
\mathbb{R}^{2}
see the recent articles
[
1
,
20
]
for the current state of the art. In short, it was shown in
[
20
, Corollary 1.2]
that the upper bound
D^{2\times 1}(|\cdot|,\|\cdot\|_{2})
defined in (
2
) is an accumulation point for any norm on
\mathbb{R}^{2}
, and in
[
1
]
it is proven that for any arbitrary norm the Dirichlet spectrum contains an interval starting at the origin, see
[
1
, Corollary 1]
. Both of these results are for simultaneous approximation on
\mathbb{R}^{2}
. We should stress that results for specific norms were known prior to the statements of
[
1
,
20
]
, see for example
[
2
,
3
,
31
]
. See §
1.1
for a very recent result on the Dirichlet Spectrum.

The Dirichlet constant can also be used to categorize matrices into certain well-known sets in Diophantine approximation. For
0\leq c\leq D
, define the following sets

\displaystyle Di_{m,n}(\|\cdot\|_{1},\|\cdot\|_{2},c)

\displaystyle:=\left\{\Omega\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times n}:\Theta^{m\times n}(\|\cdot\|_{1},\|\cdot\|_{2},\Omega)\leq c\right\},

\displaystyle DI_{m,n}(\|\cdot\|_{1},\|\cdot\|_{2},c)

\displaystyle:=\left\{\Omega\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times n}:\Theta^{m\times n}(\|\cdot\|_{1},\|\cdot\|_{2},\Omega)=c\right\}.

The set
DI_{m,n}(\|\cdot\|_{1},\|\cdot\|_{2},c)
denotes the set of points that have exact Dirichlet constant
c
. Note that trivially
DI_{m,n}(\|\cdot\|_{1},\|\cdot\|_{2},c)=\emptyset
if
c\not\in\mathcal{D}^{m\times n}(\|\cdot\|_{1},\|\cdot\|_{2})
. The set
Di_{m,n}(\|\cdot\|_{1},\|\cdot\|_{2},c)
represents the set of
c
-Dirichlet improvable matrices with respect to norms

\|\cdot\|_{1},\|\cdot\|_{2}
. The more widely known set of
Dirichlet improvable matrices
is denoted as

Di_{m,n}(\|\cdot\|_{1},\|\cdot\|_{2}):=\left\{\Omega\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times n}:\Theta^{m\times n}(\|\cdot\|_{1},\|\cdot\|_{2},\Omega)<D\right\}=\bigcup_{0\;<\;c\;<\;D}Di_{m,n}(\|\cdot\|_{1},\|\cdot\|_{2},c).\\

Note that
DI_{m,n}(\|\cdot\|_{1},\|\cdot\|_{2},c)\subseteq Di_{m,n}(\|\cdot\|_{1},\|\cdot\|_{2},c)\subseteq Di_{m,n}(\|\cdot\|_{1},\|\cdot\|_{2})
. When it it clear from context what norms are being used we will drop the notation. As well as the set of Dirichlet improvable matrices one can use the Dirichlet constant (the uniform and asymptotic one) to characterize the set of
Singular matrices
and the set of
Badly approximable matrices
. Define

\displaystyle Sing_{m,n}

\displaystyle:=DI_{m,n}(\|\cdot\|_{1},\|\cdot\|_{2},0)=\bigcap_{0\;<\;c\;<\;D}Di_{m,n}(\|\cdot\|_{1},\|\cdot\|_{2},c),

\displaystyle Bad_{m,n}

\displaystyle:=\left\{\Omega\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times n}:\widetilde{\Theta}^{m\times n}(\|\cdot\|_{1},\|\cdot\|_{2},\Omega)>0\right\}.

Note that the set of Singular and Badly approximable matrices do not depend on norms, by the equivalence of norms. Moreover it follows from Mahler’s Dual Convex Body Theory that

Bad_{m,n}=Bad_{n,m},\quad\text{ and }\quad Sing_{m,n}=Sing_{n,m}.

Trivially, for any
0<c<D
we have that
Sing_{m,n}\subseteq Di_{m,n}(c)\subseteq Di_{m,n}
, and for
Bad_{m,n}
we have the relations

Bad_{m,n}\subseteq Di_{m,n}\quad\text{ and }\quad Bad_{m,n}\cap Sing_{m,n}=\emptyset\,.

The left was shown by Davenport and Schmidt
[
15
, Theorem 2]
and the right is obvious. The sets
Bad_{m,n}
and
Sing_{m,n}
are non-empty, as was classically shown by Jarnik and Khintchine (see
[
19
]
for the case of singular vectors). Furthermore, it is well known that all of the sets highlighted above are Lebesgue nullsets. This can be proven through a variety of methods such as the Dani Correspondence principle and ergodicity, or geometric measure theory, see for example
[
6
]
. So to give any meaningful notion of size to these sets we consider the Hausdorff dimension, denoted
\dim_{H}
. See
[
16
]
for the definition and properties of
\dim_{H}
. The following results have been proven:

\displaystyle\dim_{H}Bad_{m,n}

\displaystyle=mn\,,\;\;\text{for $m=n=1$ in \cite[cite]{[\@@bibref{}{Jarnik3}{}{}]} and for general $m,n$ in \cite[cite]{[\@@bibref{}{schmidt1960,schmidtsystemoflinearforms}{}{}]}},

\displaystyle\dim_{H}Di_{m,n}

\displaystyle=mn\,,\text{ \cite[cite]{[\@@bibref{}{schmidt1960,schmidtsystemoflinearforms}{}{}]} combined with \cite[cite]{[\@@bibref{}{DavenportSchmidt3}{}{}, Theorem 2]}},

\displaystyle\dim_{H}Sing_{m,n}

\displaystyle=mn\left(1-\frac{1}{m+n}\right)\,,\;\;\begin{array}[]{c}\text{for $m=2,n=1$ in \cite[cite]{[\@@bibref{}{Cheung}{}{}]}, for $m\geq 2,n=1$ in \cite[cite]{[\@@bibref{}{CheungChevallier}{}{}]},}\\
\text{and for general $m,n$ in~\cite[cite]{[\@@bibref{}{Dasetal}{}{}, Theorem~1.1]}}.\end{array}

The dimensions of
Di_{m,n}(\|\cdot\|_{1},\|\cdot\|_{2},c)
are not yet established. In the case of maximum norms,
m\geq 2,n=1
, and any constant
C>0
, for
c>0
sufficiently small and for any real number
t>m
the bounds

\tfrac{m^{2}}{m+1}+c^{t}\leq\dim_{H}Di_{m,1}(c)\leq\tfrac{m^{2}}{m+1}+C\cdot c^{m/2}

were proven in
[
10
, Theorem 1.3]
. See also
[
10
, Corollary 6.12]
for an improvement on the lower bound.
The reader will notice that the dimension results for
Sing_{m,n}
and
Di_{m,n}(c)
hold only for
\min\{m,n\}\geq 2
. For
m=n=1
it was proven by Khintchine
[
19
]
that
Sing_{1,1}=\mathbb{Q}
,
in fact
Di_{1,1}(c)=\mathbb{Q}
for any
c<\frac{1}{2}
.
By combining this with
[
15
, Theorem 1]
, we obtain

Di_{1,1}=\mathbb{Q}\cup Bad_{1,1}.

That is, any point in the set of one dimensional Dirichlet improvable points is either Badly approximable or Singular (i.e. rational in this case). In higher dimensions much less was known, until the recent paper of Beresnevich et. al.
[
7
]
, who showed that, for
n\geq 2
, the
Folklore set

FS_{m,1}:=Di_{m,1}\backslash(Bad_{m,1}\cup Sing_{m,1})\,

has a continuum of points. They mention that this question was first asked in
[
36
]
(see the remark immediately following
[
36
, Theorem 4.6]
) and furthermore conjectured that, for
n\geq 2
, we should have
\dim_{H}FS_{m,1}=m
, see
[
7
, Problem 3.1]
. Given this setup we define the Folklore set on
m\times n
matrices with arbitrary norms as

FS_{m,n}(\|\cdot\|_{1},\|\cdot\|_{2})=Di_{m,n}(\|\cdot\|_{1},\|\cdot\|_{2})\backslash\left(Bad_{m,n}\cup Sing_{m,n}\right)

and, for any
0<c<D
, the smaller set

FS_{m,n}(\|\cdot\|_{1},\|\cdot\|_{2},c)=DI_{m,n}(\|\cdot\|_{1},\|\cdot\|_{2},c)\backslash Bad_{m,n}.

When the norms are the maximum norms we denote the sets by
FS_{m,n}
and
FS_{m,n}(c)
respectively. Again, it was conjectured that the set
FS_{m,n}(\|\cdot\|_{1},\|\cdot\|_{2})
has full Hausdorff dimension
mn

[
7
, Problem 4.1]
. See §
1.1
for more results on the Folklore set. In this article, we prove a range of results that extend the theorems given in
[
7
]
in two different ways:

•

In Section
2
we begin by considering the Folklore set in the dual approximation setting (
m=1
) and add an additional condition that the point has some specific Dirichlet constant
c
. That is, we consider the set
FS_{1,n}(\|\cdot\|_{1},\|\cdot\|_{2},c)
. We prove this set has Hausdorff dimension at least
n-2
for all constants
c
in some right neighbourhood of
0
. In particular the Dirichlet spectrum
\mathcal{D}_{m,n}
in such a setting contains a proper interval
starting at
0
.

•

In Section
3
we consider the Folklore set in the setting of systems of linear forms. In almost all cases of
m,n
, we prove non-emptiness (Theorem
3.1
) and in most of these cases positive Hausdorff dimension
(Theorems
3.3
,
3.4
,
3.6
,
3.7
). Again, in several cases we can describe any small enough positive exact Dirichlet constant, even when considering a setup with arbitrary norms.

Prior to stating our metrical results we give the following lemma, which is a simple consequence of German’s transference principle
[
17
, Theorem 7]
.

Lemma 1.1
.

Given
m,n
, there exists
\delta=\delta_{m,n}>0
so that
if
\Omega\in FS_{m,n}(\|\cdot\|_{1},\|\cdot\|_{2},c)
for some
0<c<\delta
, then its transpose
\Omega^{T}
satisfies
\Omega^{T}\in FS_{n,m}(\|\cdot\|_{2},\|\cdot\|_{1})
.

We will provide a proof at the end in Section
9
. Note when considering the transpose we lose control over the precise Dirichlet constant.

1.1.
A note on recent advances

This area of research has recently gained a significant amount of interest. In particular, since the preparation of this article there has been two remarkable results within this area that improve upon several aspects of some of our results. We highlight these below and where relevant within the article.

•

The article of Das, Fishman, Simmons and Urbanski on the Variational principle on the Parametric Geometry of Numbers
[
13
]
is a breakthrough piece of work that takes recent work of Roy
[

]
and Schmidt and Summerer
[

]
and provides a general framework to calculate Hausdorff dimension formula. The Folklore set described above is such a set that fits into their very general framework. We note that the previous iteration of the variational principle had not been immediately applicable to the Folklore set, thus not yet implying

That is, the set is at least as large as the set of Singular matrices, in terms of Hausdorff dimension. At the time of preparing this manuscript the authors were not aware of the recent advances in
[

]
. Our proof technique is different and more constructive in nature, but the Hausdorff dimension lower bounds of

]
do not, is a Hausdorff dimension bound on the set

. That is, we can prescribe exact Dirichlet constants, even for arbitrary norms, in certain cases. See for example our Theorem

below. We have attempted to make clear throughout where the bound (

‣
1.1
) improve on our statements and where our results are new. Note that the conjectured (full) dimension still remains elusive.

In a recent remarkable preprint of Agin and Weiss

Arbitrary norms

Given any norm

3.
The Folklore set for a system of linear forms

3.1.
Two fundamental results for maximum norm

satisfies
\boldsymbol{\zeta}\in Di_{n}(c)\setminus Bad_{n}

Proof.

Let us write
\boldsymbol{\xi}^{\ast}=(\xi_{1},\xi_{2},1)
and similarly

\boldsymbol{\zeta}^{\ast}=(\zeta_{1},\ldots,\zeta_{n},1)
. Denote
\boldsymbol{e}_{i}=(0,0\ldots,0,1,0,\ldots,0)
the

of
\mathbb{R}^{n+1}

Our claim follows from the fact that

(12)

\psi_{\boldsymbol{\zeta}}^{1\times n}(t)\leq\psi_{\boldsymbol{\xi}}^{1\times 2}(t),\qquad t>0.

Indeed, any

\boldsymbol{b}=(b_{1},b_{2},b_{3})\in\mathbb{Z}^{3}
with respect to
\boldsymbol{\xi}
that realizes
\psi_{\boldsymbol{\xi}}(t)
. Then the blown-up vector

\tilde{\boldsymbol{b}}:=b_{1}\boldsymbol{e}_{1}+b_{2}\boldsymbol{e}_{2}+b_{3}\boldsymbol{e}_{n+1}=(b_{1},b_{2},0,\ldots,0,b_{3})\in\mathbb{Z}^{n+1}

so the minimum over all integer points defining
\psi_{\boldsymbol{\zeta}}(t)
cannot be larger. Trivially (
11
) and (
12
) implies

\liminf_{t\to\infty}t^{n}\psi_{\boldsymbol{\zeta}}^{1\times n}(t)=0\,,

\boldsymbol{\zeta}\not\in Bad_{n}

∎

Theorem 5.2

Proof of Theorem

Remark 17

Notice that in the above calculation, we simply use

Proposition 2

Let

Assume

Writing

∎

In the sequel denote by

As indicated before, we apply Proposition

Theorems
3.3
admits a rather easy proof, as a consequence of the following simple lemma. We consider maximum norms on both
\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
\mathbb{R}^{m}

and simply write
\psi^{m\times n}_{\Omega}(t)

for
\psi^{m\times n}_{\Omega}(\|\cdot\|_{\infty},\|\cdot\|_{\infty},t)
.

Lemma 8.1
.

Let
\Omega\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times n}
be a block matrix
consisting of rectangular blocks
\Omega_{1},\ldots,\Omega_{k}

of sizes
(m_{j},n_{j}),1\leq j\leq k

along the diagonal,
m=\sum m_{j}
,
n=\sum n_{j}
.
Then

\psi_{\Omega}^{m\times n}(t)=\min_{1\leq j\leq k}\psi_{\Omega_{j}}^{m_{j}\times n_{j}}(t),\qquad t>0.

Proof.

First observe that by the form of
\Omega
, the problem
decomposes into
k
decoupled linear form approximation problems.
Fix
t
and let
\boldsymbol{b}_{1},\ldots,\boldsymbol{b}_{k}
in
\mathbb{Z}^{m_{j}+n_{j}}
be the best approximation integer vectors
inducing
\psi_{\Omega_{j}}^{m_{j}\times n_{j}}(t)
for
1\leq j\leq k
, i.e.
\boldsymbol{b}=\boldsymbol{b}_{j}

minimizes
\|\Omega_{j}^{\ast}\boldsymbol{b}\|_{\infty}=\|\Omega_{j}\hat{\boldsymbol{b}}-\tilde{\boldsymbol{b}}\|_{\infty}
among integer vectors
\boldsymbol{b}
with
0<\|\boldsymbol{b}\|_{\infty}\leq t
.
The inequality

\psi_{\Omega}^{m\times n}(t)\leq\min_{1\leq j\leq k}\psi_{\Omega_{j}}^{m_{j}\times n_{j}}(t)

follows by taking the
integer vector
\boldsymbol{b}\in\mathbb{Z}^{m+n}
with entries of
\boldsymbol{b}_{j_{0}}
at the according places, with
j_{0}

inducing the minimum of
\|\Omega_{j}^{\ast}\boldsymbol{b}_{j}\|_{\infty}
over
j
, and
0
entries at all other places. By choice of the maximum norm, this will have the same norm

\|\Omega^{\ast}\cdot\boldsymbol{b}\|_{\infty}=\|\Omega_{j_{0}}^{\ast}\cdot\boldsymbol{b}_{j_{0}}\|_{\infty}=\psi_{\Omega_{j_{0}}}^{m_{j_{0}}\times n_{j_{0}}}(t)=\min_{1\leq j\leq k}\psi_{\Omega_{j}}^{m_{j}\times n_{j}}(t).

Conversely, since some coordinate of the best approximation vector for given
t
is not
0
and the system is decoupled and we use maximum norm,
we must have

\psi_{\Omega}^{m\times n}(t)\geq\min_{1\leq j\leq k}\psi_{\Omega_{j}}^{m_{j}\times n_{j}}(t).

∎

Proof of Lemma
1.1

For matrix
\Omega\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times n}
and real numbers
A,B\in\mathbb{R}_{+}
define the convex bodies

\displaystyle M_{A,B}(\Omega)

\displaystyle:=\left\{\boldsymbol{z}\in\mathbb{R}^{n+m}:\max_{1\leq j\leq m}\left|\sum_{i=1}^{n}\Omega_{j,i}z_{i}+z_{n+j}\right|\leq A,\quad\max_{1\leq i\leq n}|z_{i}|\leq B\right\},

\displaystyle\widehat{M}_{A,B}(\Omega)

\displaystyle:=\left\{\boldsymbol{z}\in\mathbb{R}^{n+m}:\max_{1\leq i\leq n}\left|z_{i}-\sum_{j=1}^{m}\Omega_{i,j}z_{n+j}\right|\leq B,\quad\max_{1\leq j\leq m}|z_{n+j}|\leq A\right\}.

The following theorem proven by German is crucial.

Theorem 9.1
.

[
17
, Theorem 7]

Let
\Omega\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times n}
and
A,B\in\mathbb{R}_{+}
. If

M_{A,B}(\Omega)\cap\mathbb{Z}^{n+m}\backslash\{\boldsymbol{0}\}\neq\emptyset\,,

then

\widehat{M}_{A^{*},B^{*}}(\Omega)\cap\mathbb{Z}^{n+m}\backslash\{\boldsymbol{0}\}\neq\emptyset

for

A^{*}=C(A^{n}B^{1-n})^{\frac{1}{n+m-1}}\quad\text{ and }\quad B^{*}=C(A^{1-m}B^{m})^{\frac{1}{n+m-1}},

with
C>0
constant dependent only on
n
and
m
.

Proof of Lemma
1.1
.

Since
\Omega\in FS_{m,n}(\|\cdot\|_{1},\|\cdot\|_{2},c)\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{m\times n}
we have

\Theta^{m\times n}(\|\cdot\|_{1},\|\cdot\|_{2},\Omega)=c
. By the equivalence of norms we have that the same is true up to some constant, and so

\Theta^{m\times n}(\|\cdot\|_{\infty},\|\cdot\|_{\infty},\Omega)\asymp c\,.

Hence, for all sufficiently large
t\in\mathbb{R}
and some
\rho>0
there is an integer

\boldsymbol{b}\in M_{\rho t^{-n/m},t}(\Omega)\cap\mathbb{Z}^{n+m}\backslash\{\textbf{0}\}\,.

By Theorem
9.1
, for all sufficiently large
u\in\mathbb{R}
there exists integer

\boldsymbol{b}\in\widehat{M}_{u,\delta u^{-m/n}}(\Omega)\cap\mathbb{Z}^{n+m}\backslash\{\textbf{0}\}\,,\quad\text{ for }\quad\delta=C^{\tfrac{m+n}{n}}\rho^{\tfrac{m}{n(n+m-1)}}.

Hence
\Omega^{T}\in Di_{n,m}(\delta)
, and by equivalence of norms there exists some
\delta^{*}>0
such that
\Omega^{T}\in Di_{n,m}(\|\cdot\|_{2},\|\cdot\|_{1},\delta^{*})
.
If
D^{*}=D^{n\times m}(\|\cdot\|_{1},\|\cdot\|_{2})
is the Dirichlet constant from (
2
) for the transpose problem,
for small enough
\delta
and thus
\delta^{*}<D^{*}
this will induce a Dirichlet
improvable matrix
\Omega^{T}
.
Conversely, for any
0<\gamma<c
there exists an unbounded sequence of
t\in\mathbb{R}
for which

\widehat{M}_{t,\gamma t^{-n/m}}(\Omega^{T})\cap\mathbb{Z}^{n+m}\backslash\{\textbf{0}\}=\emptyset\,,

otherwise
\Omega\in Di_{m,n}(\gamma)
, which is clearly false since
\Omega\in DI_{m,n}(c)
. Applying the contrapositive of Theorem
9.1
we obtain that there exist certain arbitrarily large
u\in\mathbb{R}
and a constant
C^{*}>0
for which

M_{C^{*}u^{-n/m},u}(\Omega^{T})\cap\mathbb{Z}^{n+m}\backslash\{\textbf{0}\}=\emptyset\,.

Hence
\Omega^{T}\not\in Sing_{n,m}
. Furthermore, one can similarly show that
\Omega^{T}\not\in Bad_{n,m}
, and so

\Omega^{T}\in Di_{n,m}(\|\cdot\|_{2},\|\cdot\|_{1},\delta^{*})\backslash(Sing_{n,m}\cup Bad_{n,m})\subseteq FS_{n,m}(\|\cdot\|_{2},\|\cdot\|_{1}).

∎

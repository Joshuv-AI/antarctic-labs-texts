Non-compact inaudibility of weak symmetry and commutativity via generalized Heisenberg groups
Teresa Arias-Marco, José Manuel Fernández-Barroso

Abstract
Two Riemannian manifolds are said to be isospectral if there exists a unitary operator which intertwines their Laplace-Beltrami operator. In this paper, we prove in the non-compact setting the inaudibility of the weak symmetry property and the commutative property using an isospectral pair of 23 dimensional generalized Heisenberg groups.



Keywords:
Laplace operator; Isospectral Riemannian manifolds; Weakly-symmetric spaces; Commutative spaces; g.o. spaces

MSC2020:

58J53; 53C25; 58J50.

1
Introduction

Locally symmetric spaces are Riemannian manifolds whose local geodesic symmetries are local isometries.
Symmetric-like
spaces are Riemannian manifolds with a geometric or algebraic property that locally symmetric spaces satisfy. Weakly symmetric and commutative spaces are examples of symmetric-like spaces. Selberg in
[
15
]
introduced the notion of
weakly symmetric
spaces as those on which any two points can be interchanged by an isometry. In the same paper, Selberg proved that the algebra of all invariant differential operators is commutative in every weakly symmetric space, these spaces are known as
commutative
spaces.

Kaplan, in
[
10
]
, developed examples of non locally symmetric Riemannian manifolds, namely
generalized Heisenberg groups
. The study of symmetric-like properties in generalized Heisenberg groups has been studied by many authors (e.g.
[
14
]
,
[
13
]
,
[
5
]
,
[
4
]
).

Two Riemannian manifolds,
M
and
M^{\prime}
are said to be
isospectral
if there exists a unitary operator
T:L^{2}(M^{\prime})\to L^{2}(M)
which intertwines their Laplacians,
\Delta
and
\Delta^{\prime}
, respectively, this is such that
T\circ\Delta^{\prime}=\Delta\circ T
. If
M
and
M^{\prime}
are compact Riemannian manifolds, the definition of being isospectral is equivalent to the fact that both manifolds have the same spectrum. In particular, Gordon and Wilson in
[
9
]
studied the isospectrality between pairs of generalized Heisenberg groups. Szabó in
[
17
]
introduced a new example of isospectral closed Riemannian manifolds which can be seen as compact submanifolds of generalized Heisenberg groups. Szabó’s examples were also introduced independently by Gordon, Gornet, Schueth, Webb and Wilson in
[
8
]
, using a different approach.

A geometric property is said to be
inaudible
, or it cannot be heard, when one can find isospectral Riemannian manifolds such that one of them satisfies that property and the other does not. In
[
1
]
, the authors proved the inaudibility of some symmetric-like properties using Szabó’s examples. In this paper, we rely on the work done in
[
9
]
, and we use a pair of isospectral generalized Heisenberg groups of dimension 23 to develop our main result:

The property of being weakly symmetric and the property of being commutative are inaudible properties on non-compact Riemannian manifolds.

In Section
2
, we treat the isospectrality between generalized Heisenberg groups. For that, we first study the isospectrality between compact 2-step nilmanifolds (i.e. quotients of 2-step nilpotent Lie groups) whose coverings are generalized Heisenberg groups. Finally, in Section
3
, we remember the main results concerning the study of symmetric-like properties on generalized Heisenberg groups, and we prove our main result.

2
Isospectrality on generalized Heisenberg groups

A well studied kind of manifolds are generalized Heisenberg groups, which are particular examples of 2-step nilpotent Lie groups. These manifolds were introduced by Kaplan in
[
10
]
(for a more detailed information, consult
[
5
]
). In this section we show how they are constructed, and how they are classified according to their dimension.

Let consider
\mathfrak{v}
and
\mathfrak{z}
real vector spaces which are orthogonal with respect to an inner product
g
, and a linear map
j:\mathfrak{z}\to\mathfrak{so(v)}
. Let denote by
\mathfrak{n}
the real vector space
\mathfrak{v}\oplus\mathfrak{z}
. Using this
j
map, one can define a Lie bracket
[\cdot,\cdot]
on
\mathfrak{n}
by

g\left([X,Y],Z\right)=g\left(j_{Z}X,Y\right),

(1)

for each
X,Y\in\mathfrak{v}
and each
Z\in\mathfrak{z}
. This Lie bracket makes
\mathfrak{n}
a 2-step nilpotent Lie algebra, this is
[\mathfrak{n},\mathfrak{n}]\subset\mathfrak{z}
and
[\mathfrak{n},\mathfrak{z}]=0
. Thus,
\mathfrak{z}
is also known as the center of
\mathfrak{n}
. If we need to distinguish the Lie algebra or the Lie bracket depending on the
j
map, we will denote it by
\mathfrak{n}(j)
and
[\cdot,\cdot]^{j}
, respectively.

N(j)
is the 2-step nilpotent Lie group whose Lie algebra is
\mathfrak{n}(j)
, and together with the metric induced by the inner product
g
, that we denote it again by
g
, the pair
(N(j),g)
is a Riemannian manifold. The usual Lie group exponential map
\exp^{j}:\mathfrak{n}(j)\to N(j)
which satisfies the Cambell–Baker–Hausdorff formula

\exp^{j}(X)\cdot\exp^{j}(Y)=\exp^{j}\left(X+Y+\frac{1}{2}[X,Y]^{j}\right),

is a diffeomorphism due to
N(j)
is simply-connected and 2-step nilpotent. We will denote
\exp
instead of
\exp^{j}
, if there is no confusion with
j
.

A 2-step nilpotent Lie algebra,
\mathfrak{n}
, is said to be a
generalized Heisenberg algebra
when, additionally,
j
satisfies the following relation

j_{Z}^{2}=-\|Z\|^{2}\textup{Id}_{\mathfrak{v}},

(2)

for every
Z\in\mathfrak{z}
. In that case, its connected, simply-connected 2-step nilpotent Lie group,
N(j)
, is a
generalized Heisenberg group
, and
j
is a map of Heisenberg type.

Generalized Heisenberg algebras,
\mathfrak{n}=\mathfrak{v}\oplus\mathfrak{z}
, can be classified using the classification of representations of Clifford algebras over the real vector space
\mathfrak{z}
attached to a negative definite quadratic form
-\|\mathfrak{z}\|^{2}
. The classification can be found in
[
2
]
, but we include it here:

i)

If
\dim\mathfrak{z}\not\equiv 3\mod 4
, then the Clifford algebra
Cl(\mathfrak{z})
has a unique irreducible Clifford module
\mathfrak{v}_{0}
up to equivalence, and the generalized Heisenberg algebra is obtained up to isomorphism, taking the direct sum of
p
times the irreducible Clifford module, this is,
\mathfrak{v}\cong(\mathfrak{v}_{0})^{p},p\geq 1.

ii)

If
\dim\mathfrak{z}\equiv 3\mod 4
, then the Clifford algebra
Cl(\mathfrak{z})
has two non-equivalent irreducible Clifford modules
\mathfrak{v}_{1}
and
\mathfrak{v}_{2}
, up to equivalence. The generalized Heisenberg algebras are obtained up to isomorphism, taking
\mathfrak{v}\cong(\mathfrak{v}_{1})^{p}\oplus(\mathfrak{v}_{2})^{q}
, with
p\geq 0,q\geq 0,p+q\geq 1
, which is isomorphic to
\mathfrak{v}\cong(\mathfrak{v}_{1})^{q}\oplus(\mathfrak{v}_{2})^{p}
due to
\mathfrak{v}_{1}
and
\mathfrak{v}_{2}
have the same dimension. In this case, we name the generalized Heisenberg algebras as
\mathfrak{n}_{p,q}
.

With this notation,
\mathfrak{v}
is said to be
isotypic
if its Clifford module structure is irreducible. When
\dim\mathfrak{z}\not\equiv 3\mod 4
,
\mathfrak{v}
is trivially isotypic. If
\dim\mathfrak{z}\equiv 3\mod 4
,
\mathfrak{v}
is isotypic when
p=0
or
q=0
, with
p+q\geq 1
.

Note that, in the case when
\dim\mathfrak{z}\equiv 3\mod 4
, if we fix non-negative integers
(p_{1},q_{1})
and
(p_{2},q_{2})
such that
p_{1}+q_{1}=p_{2}+q_{2}
, then we obtain generalized Heisenberg algebras of the same dimension,
\mathfrak{n}_{p_{1},q_{1}}
and
\mathfrak{n}_{p_{2},q_{2}}
. These algebras are isomorphic if and only if
(p_{2},q_{2})=\{(p_{1},q_{1}),(q_{1},p_{1})\}
.

Now, we want to find a pair of isospectral and non-isometric 2-step nilmanifolds whose Riemannian coverings are generalized Heisenberg groups. We also show the isospectrality between those Riemannian coverings which are non-compact.

Following Wilson
[
18
]
and Gordon
[
6
]
, we say that a Riemannian manifold is a 2-step
nilmanifold
if it is a quotient of a simply-connected 2-step nilpotent Lie group
N(j)
by a possible trivial discrete subgroup
\Gamma(j)
, together with a left invariant Riemannian metric
g
. We denote it by
N^{j}=(N(j)/\Gamma(j),g)
where
\Gamma(j)=\exp^{j}(\mathcal{M}+\mathcal{L})
with
\mathcal{M}
and
\mathcal{L}
cocompact lattices in
\mathfrak{v}
and
\mathfrak{z}
, respectively.

Wilson
[
18
]
proved that two simply-connected Riemannian 2-step nilmanifolds are isometric if and only if the associated metric Lie algebras are isomorphic. According to the classification of the generalized Heisenberg algebras in terms of the attached Clifford algebra, there are up to isomorphisms one generalized Heisenberg algebra in each dimension when
\dim\mathfrak{z}\not\equiv 3\mod 4
, and there are two Lie algebras when
\dim\mathfrak{z}\equiv 3\mod 4
. We will center our attention in the case when
\dim\mathfrak{z}\equiv 3\mod 4
.

Gordon introduced in
[
6
]
the following map in order to work with the isotypic and the non isotypic representation of
\mathfrak{v}
at the same time. Let
A
denotes the quaternion numbers
\mathbb{H}
or the octonion numbers
\mathbb{O}
. Suppose that
\mathfrak{v}=(A)^{p}\oplus(A)^{q},p,q\geq 0,p+q\geq 1,p,q\in\mathbb{N}
, and the center is the pure elements of
A
,
\mathfrak{z}=A^{*}
. The map
j^{(p,q)}:\mathfrak{z}\to\mathfrak{so(v)}
is given by

j^{(p,q)}_{Z}(X_{1},\dots,X_{p},X_{p+1},\dots,X_{p+q})=(Z\cdot X_{1},\dots,Z\cdot X_{p},X_{p+1}\cdot Z,\dots,X_{p+q}\cdot Z),

(3)

where
Z\in\mathfrak{z},X_{i}\in A,i=1,\dots,p+q
, and
\cdot
is the usual multiplication in
A
. This map is always of Heisenberg type independently of the values of
p
and
q
.

We denote by
\mathfrak{n}(p,q)
the generalized Heisenberg algebra
\mathfrak{n}_{p,q}(j^{(p,q)})
, by
N(p,q)
the generalized Heisenberg group
\exp(\mathfrak{n}(p,q))
, and by
\mathcal{M}_{p,q}+\mathcal{L}
the lattice in
\mathfrak{n}(p,q)
spanned by its integer standard basis. The 2-step nilmanifolds of Heisenberg type obtained by taking the cocompact quotient of
N(p,q)
by
\Gamma_{p,q}:=\exp(\mathcal{M}_{p,q}+\mathcal{L})
, with its left invariant Riemannian metric, are denoted by
N^{p,q}
. With this notation, Gordon proved in
[
6
]
the following result.

Theorem 2.1
.

N^{p,q}
and
N^{p^{\prime},q^{\prime}}
are isospectral if
p+q=p^{\prime}+q^{\prime}
. Moreover, they are locally isometric if and only if
(p^{\prime},q^{\prime})=\{(p,q),(q,p)\}
.

Remark 2.2
.

This result allows us to find pairs of isospectral 2-step nilmanifolds constructed using generalized Heisenberg groups. One possible situation in the case
\dim\mathfrak{z}\equiv 3\mod 4
is

\textstyle{N(p,q)\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{N(p+q,0)\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{N^{p,q}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}

where

and

with

\textstyle{N^{p,q}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}

\displaystyle=\frac{\partial f}{\partial X}(\sigma_{(p,q)}^{\alpha}(X),Z)\cdot f\left(j^{(p+q,0)}_{\iota(Z^{\alpha})}\left(\sigma_{(p,q)}^{\alpha}(X),Z\right)\right)

\displaystyle=\sigma_{(p,q)*}^{\alpha}\left(\frac{\partial f}{\partial X}(X,Z)\right)\cdot f\left(\sigma_{(p,q)}^{\alpha}\left(j^{(p,q)}_{\iota(Z^{\alpha})}X\right),Z\right)

\displaystyle=\sigma_{(p,q)*}^{\alpha}\left(\frac{\partial f}{\partial X}(X,Z)\right)\cdot\sigma_{(p,q)*}^{\alpha}f\left(j^{(p,q)}_{\iota(Z^{\alpha})}X,Z\right)

\displaystyle=\sigma_{(p,q)*}^{\alpha}\left(\frac{\partial f}{\partial X}(X,Z)\cdot f\left(j^{(p,q)}_{\iota(Z^{\alpha})}X,Z\right)\right)

\displaystyle=\sigma_{(p,q)*}^{\alpha}\left(\left(j^{(p,q)}_{\iota(Z^{\alpha})}X\right)\bullet f(X,Z)\right),

for every
f(X,Z)\in L^{2}_{\mathbb{C}^{\alpha}}(N(p,q)/\Gamma)
.

Now, due to
\sigma_{(p,q)*}^{\alpha}
acts separately for each
\alpha
, it leaves each subspace
L^{2}_{\mathbb{C}^{\alpha}}(N(p,q)/\Gamma)
invariant. Thus, we can define

\sigma_{(p,q)*}:=\bigoplus_{\alpha}\sigma_{(p,q)*}^{\alpha}:L^{2}_{\mathbb{C}}(N(p,q)/\Gamma)\to L^{2}_{\mathbb{C}}(N(p+q,0)/\Gamma),

such that

\sigma_{(p,q)*}\circ\left(j^{(p,q)}_{\xi}(X)\right)\bullet=\left(j^{(p+q,0)}_{\xi}(X)\right)\bullet\circ\sigma_{(p,q)*}.

Therefore,
\sigma_{(p,q)*}
intertwines the Laplacians of
N(p,q)/\Gamma
and
N(p+q,0)/\Gamma
, this is

\sigma_{(p,q)*}\circ\tilde{\Delta}^{(p,q)}=\tilde{\Delta}^{(p+q,0)}\circ\sigma_{(p,q)*}.

Let consider
T:L^{2}(N(p,q))\to L^{2}(N(p+q,0))
given by

T=\mathcal{F}\circ\sigma_{(p,q)*}\circ\mathcal{F}^{-1}.

Then, for each
f\in L^{2}(N(p,q)/\Gamma)
, it is satisfied

\displaystyle T\circ\Delta^{(p,q)}(\mathcal{F}(f))

\displaystyle=\mathcal{F}\circ\sigma_{(p,q)*}\circ\mathcal{F}^{-1}(\Delta^{(p,q)}(\mathcal{F}(f)))=\mathcal{F}\circ\sigma_{(p,q)*}\circ\mathcal{F}^{-1}(\mathcal{F}(\tilde{\Delta}^{(p,q)}f))

\displaystyle=\mathcal{F}\circ\sigma_{(p,q)*}(\tilde{\Delta}^{(p,q)}f)=\mathcal{F}(\tilde{\Delta}^{(p+q,0)}\circ\sigma_{(p,q)*}(f))

\displaystyle=\Delta^{(p+q,0)}(\mathcal{F}(\sigma_{(p,q)*}(f)))=\Delta^{(p+q,0)}(\mathcal{F}\circ\sigma_{(p,q)*}\circ\mathcal{F}^{-1}(\mathcal{F}(f)))

\displaystyle=\Delta^{(p+q,0)}(\mathcal{F}\circ\sigma_{(p,q)*}\circ\mathcal{F}^{-1})(\mathcal{F}(f))=\Delta^{(p+q,0)}\circ T(\mathcal{F}(f)).

Equivalently,

T\circ\Delta^{(p,q)}=\Delta^{(p+q,0)}\circ T.

∎

3
Main results and conclusions

Generalized Heisenberg groups provide examples of Riemannian manifolds with different geometric properties depending on their dimension.

Let
(M,g)
be a Riemannian manifold,
\nabla
its Levi–Civita connection and the curvature tensor of
M
,

R(X,Y)=[\nabla_{X},\nabla_{Y}]-\nabla_{[X,Y]},\quad X,Y\in\mathfrak{X}(M).

Fixing a point
p\in M
and a vector
v\in T_{p}M
, let denote by
\gamma_{v}
the geodesic starting in
p
with initial direction
v
. Then, a
local geodesic symmetry
is the diffeomorphism
s_{p}
such that it fixes the point
p
and involves the vector of the geodesics in a neighborhood of the point, this is
s_{p}\circ\gamma_{v}=\gamma_{-v}
.

A Riemannian manifold is said to be
locally symmetric
if the local geodesic symmetries are in addition isometries. Moreover, Selberg in
[
15
]
introduced the notion of
weak symmetry
. These spaces are those on which any two points can be interchanged by an isometry. Berndt, Prüfer and Vanhecke proved in
[
3
]
that weak symmetry is equivalent to
ray symmetry
introduced by Szabó in
[
16
]
.

Selberg proved in
[
15
]
that the algebra of all invariant differential operators of a weakly symmetric space is commutative. This fact points out a new definition, a
commutative
space is a Riemannian space whose algebra of all invariant differential operators are commutative. Now, a natural question arises:
Is any commutative Riemannian manifold a weakly symmetric space?
This question was answered negatively by Lauret in
[
12
]
, where he found examples of commutative spaces which are not weakly symmetric.

Berndt, Ricci and Vanhecke in
[
4
]
distinguished between weakly symmetric spaces in the
broad sense
, this is the original definition of Selberg, and weakly symmetric spaces in the
narrow sense
, where the two points interchanging isometries must belong to the identity component of the group of isometries.

Weak symmetry and commutativity were studied in generalized Heisenberg groups by Bernd, Ricci and Vanhecke in
[
4
]
, and by Ricci in
[
13
]
, respectively. These authors classified the generalized Heisenberg groups satisfying these properties in terms of their dimension.

Theorem 3.1

(Ricci,
[
13
]
)
.

A generalized Heisenberg group is commutative if and only if

1.

\dim\mathfrak{z}=1,2
or 3,

2.

\dim\mathfrak{z}=5
and
\dim\mathfrak{v}=8
,

3.

\dim\mathfrak{z}=6
and
\dim\mathfrak{v}=8
,

4.

\dim\mathfrak{z}=7
and:

i)

\dim\mathfrak{v}=8
,

ii)

\dim\mathfrak{v}=16
, with
\mathfrak{v}
isotypic, this is
\mathfrak{n}=\mathfrak{n}_{2,0}\cong\mathfrak{n}_{0,2}
.

Theorem 3.2

(Berndt, Ricci, Vanhecke,
[
4
]
)
.

A generalized Heisenberg group is weakly symmetric in the broad sense if and only if it is commutative. Moreover, every weakly symmetric in the broad sense generalized Heisenberg group is weakly symmetric in the narrow sense except the case when
\dim\mathfrak{z}=1
.

Following Kowalski and Vanhecke
[
11
]
, a
g.o. space
is a Riemannian manifold
(M,g)
whose geodesics are orbits of a one-parameter subgroup of the group of the isometries of
M
. Generalized Heisenberg groups which are g.o. spaces were studied and classified by Riehm in
[
14
]
.

Theorem 3.3

(Riehm,
[
14
]
)
.

A generalized Heisenberg group is a Riemannian g.o. space if and only if

1.

\dim\mathfrak{z}=1,2
or 3,

2.

\dim\mathfrak{z}=5
and
\dim\mathfrak{v}=8
,

3.

\dim\mathfrak{z}=6
and
\dim\mathfrak{v}=8
,

4.

\dim\mathfrak{z}=7
and:

i)

\dim\mathfrak{v}=8
,

ii)

\dim\mathfrak{v}=16
, with
\mathfrak{v}
isotypic, this is
\mathfrak{n}=\mathfrak{n}_{2,0}\cong\mathfrak{n}_{0,2}
,

iii)

\dim\mathfrak{v}=24
, with
\mathfrak{v}
isotypic, this is
\mathfrak{n}=\mathfrak{n}_{3,0}\cong\mathfrak{n}_{0,3}
.

Note that, generalized Heisenberg groups provide an example of g.o. space which is not weakly symmetric nor commutative, namely the generalized Heisenberg group with
\dim\mathfrak{z}=7
,
\dim\mathfrak{v}=24
and
\mathfrak{v}
isotypic.

In order to prove the main results, we combine Proposition
2.3
with the classifications of generalized Heisenberg groups presented in Theorem
3.1
, Theorem
3.3
, and Theorem
3.2
.

Proposition 3.4
.

One cannot hear if a non-compact Riemannian manifold is commutative.

Proof.

By Proposition
2.3
, the 23-dimensional non-compact generalized Heisenberg groups
N(2,0)
and
N(1,1)
with 7-dimensional center are isospectral. The classification of generalized Heisenberg groups which are commutative spaces, Theorem
3.1
, establishes that
N(2,0)
is a commutative space while
N(1,1)
is not a commutative space.
∎

The next result was already proved by Gordon in
[
7
]
using a result by Gordon and Wilson in
[
9
]
. Summarizing, its proof is as the previous one, but using Theorem
3.3
.

Proposition 3.5
.

One cannot hear if a non-compact Riemannian manifold is a g.o. space.

Now, using again the same pair of isospectral manifolds together with Theorem
3.2
, we obtain the result about the weak symmetric property.

Proposition 3.6
.

One cannot hear if a non-compact Riemannian manifold is weakly symmetric, neither in the broad sense nor in the narrow sense.

These three propositions point out the inaudibility of being a g.o. space, being a commutative space, and being a weakly symmetric space in the non-compact case using
N(2,0)
and
N(1,1)
with 7-dimensional center. However, we cannot guarantee if their compact quotients,
N^{2,0}
and
N^{1,1}
, inherit the same global properties, and it is still open the audibility problem of these properties in the compact setting. We can only guarantee that
N^{2,0}
is weakly locally symmetric while
N^{1,1}
is not. This fact proves the inaudibility in the compact case of the weak local symmetry in dimension 23. Nevertheless, this local property was already proved to be inaudible by the first author and Schueth in
[
1
]
, using a pair of 10-dimensional closed isospectral manifolds introduced by Szabó in
[
17
]
.

The main results proved in this paper add two new properties to the list of inaudible Riemannian properties, being a weakly symmetric space and being a commutative space, only in the non-compact case. So, it is natural to ask what happen in the compact case. This is:

Can one find examples of isospectral closed Riemannian manifolds proving the inaudibility of being weakly symmetric, commutative or a g.o. space in the compact case?

Therefore, it is important to find new examples of isospectral non-isometric pairs of compact Riemannian manifolds.

We would also like to remark that using the above mentioned classifications, we can only guarantee the inaudibility in the non-compact case of the weakly symmetric and the commutative properties in dimension 23. Moreover, using Theorem
3.3
and working in the same way as in the proof of Proposition
3.5
, but using the 31-dimensional non-compact generalized Heisenberg groups
N(3,0)
and
N(2,1)
with 7-dimensional center, we also obtain the inaudibility in the non-compact case of the g.o. property in dimension 31. This means, we can only guarantee the inaudibility of the g.o. property in the non-compact case in dimension 23 and 31. Then, a second question arises:

Can one find examples of isospectral Riemannian manifolds in another dimensions which prove the inaudibility of the same symmetric-like properties?

We hope to answer these questions in forthcoming papers using new examples of closed isospectral manifolds developed by the authors.

Anomalous production of massive gauge boson pairs at muon colliders
Brad Abbott, Aram Apyan, Bianca Azartash-Namin, Veena Balakrishnan, Jeffrey Berryhill, Shih-Chieh Hsu, Sergo Jindariani, Mayuri Prabhakar Kawale, Elham E Khoda, Ryan Parsons, Alexander Schuy, Michael Strauss, John Stupak, Connor Waits

Abstract
The prospects of searches for anomalous production of hadronically decaying weak boson pairs at proposed high-energy muon colliders are reported. Muon-muon collision events are simulated at s = 6 \sqrt{s}=6 , 10, and 30 TeV, corresponding to an integrated luminosity of 4 4 , 10 10 , and 10 10 ab -1 , respectively. Simulated μ ​ μ → WW + ν ​ ν / μ ​ μ \mu\mu\rightarrow\rm{W}\rm{W}+\nu\nu/\mu\mu events are used to set expected constraints on the structure of quartic vector boson interactions in the framework of a dimension-8 effective field theory. Similarly, μ ​ μ → WW / ZZ + ν ​ ν \mu\mu\rightarrow\rm{W}\rm{W}/\rm{Z}\rm{Z}+\nu\nu events are used to report constraints on the product of the cross section and branching fraction for vector boson fusion production of a heavy neutral Higgs boson decaying to weak boson pairs. These results are interpreted in the context of the Georgi–Machacek model.



I
Introduction

Vector boson scattering (VBS) processes probe the structure of the triple and quartic electroweak (EW) gauge boson self-interactions
Lee et al. 1977a
;
Lee et al. 1977b
. Deviations of measurements with respect to the Standard Model (SM) predictions could indicate the presence of anomalous quartic gauge couplings (aQGCs)
Éboli et al. 2006
;
Almeida et al. 2020
. Measurements of VBS processes provide a unique insight into the EW symmetry breaking mechanism as the unitarity of the tree-level amplitude of the longitudinally polarized VBS at high energies is restored by a Higgs boson
Lee et al. 1977a
;
Lee et al. 1977b
. New physics models predict enhancements in VBS processes through extended Higgs sectors or modifications of the Higgs boson couplings to
\rm{W}
and
\rm{Z}
bosons
Espriu and Yencho 2013
;
Chang et al. 2013
.

A multi-TeV muon collider (
\mu^{+}\mu^{-}
)
Accettura et al. 2023
is a “high-luminosity weak boson collider”
Costantini et al. 2020
and provides a great opportunity to study VBS processes. A comprehensive physics case for a future high-energy muon collider, with center of mass energies from 1 to 100 TeV, is reported in Ref.
Al Ali et al. 2022
. A muon collider has considerable advantages compared to proposed linear and circular electron-positron (
e^{+}e^{-}
)
Bambade et al. 2019
;
Charles et al. 2018
;
Abada et al. 2019a
;
Dong et al. 2018
with larger collision energy and luminosity reach. In addition, a muon collider has a relatively clean environment compared to circular proton-proton (
\rm{pp}
) machines
Abada et al. 2019b
;
CEP 2018
and the total energy of the muon is available in a collision in contrast to the dissociation of the composite proton. However, compared to
e^{+}e^{-}
colliders, the effects of backgrounds induced by the muon beam decays, referred to as “beam-induced background,” are important and need to be studied in detail
Bartosik et al. 2020
.

This paper focuses on the prospects of aQGC searches using events with hadronically decaying
\rm{W}^{\pm}\rm{W}^{\mp}
boson pairs. The studies are performed in the
\mu\mu\rightarrow\rm{W}\rm{W}+\nu\nu/\mu\mu
channels, where the
\rm{W}
boson pair is produced in association with two neutrinos or two muons, respectively. Figure
1
shows representative Feynman diagrams involving quartic vertices for the
\rm{W}\rm{W}\nu\nu
(left) and
\rm{W}\rm{W}\mu\mu

Éboli et al. 2006
. The
S0

L

Éboli et al. 2006
. The
\rm{W}\rm{W}\nu\nu
and
\rm{W}\rm{W}\mu\mu

Prospects of searches for a heavy neutral Higgs boson produced in association with two neutrinos and decaying to
\rm{W}\rm{W}
or
\rm{Z}\rm{Z}
boson pairs are also reported in this paper. In particular, the Georgi–Machacek (GM) model
Georgi and Machacek 1985
;
Englert et al. 2013a
;
Englert et al. 2013b
with both real and complex triplets is considered. In the GM model the tree-level ratio of the
\rm{W}
and
\rm{Z}
boson masses is protected against large radiative corrections and the physical scalar states transform as multiplets under a global custodial symmetry. The model contains a fermiophobic fiveplet, a fermiophilic triplet, and two singlets, one of which is identified as the 125 GeV SM-like Higgs boson.

The fiveplet physical states, collectively referred to as
\rm{H}_{5}
, consist of a neutral Higgs boson in addition to singly and doubly charged Higgs bosons, which are degenerate in mass (denoted as
m_{\rm{H}_{5}}
). Only the neutral fiveplet state is considered here. The
H5plane
benchmark, where the triplet states are heavier than the fiveplet states, is used
de Florian et al. 2016
. In this benchmark, the
\rm{H}_{5}
states are produced primarily via vector boson fusion (VBF) and the production cross section is proportional to the parameter
s_{\mathrm{H}}
, characterizing the contribution of the isotriplet scalar fields to the masses of the W and Z bosons. The
\mu\mu\rightarrow\rm{H}_{5}\nu\nu
production mode is considered where the
\rm{H}_{5}
boson is assumed to decay to
\rm{V}\rm{V}
, where
\rm{V}
is a
\rm{W}
or
\rm{Z}
boson
Zaro and Logan 2015
;
de Florian et al. 2016
, yielding a
\rm{V}\rm{V}\nu\nu
final state.

Measurements of VBS processes at the CERN LHC by the ATLAS and CMS Collaborations have reported constraints on aQGCs in the framework of dimension-8 effective field theory (EFT) operators

Khachatryan et al. 2015

;
Tumasyan et al. 2021
. Prospects for aQGC searches using the scattering of W and Z bosons at the High-Luminosity LHC (HL-LHC) and High-Energy LHC (HE-LHC) are reported in Ref.
Dainese et al. 2019

II
Event simulation

The
MadGraph5
_aMC@NLO 3.1.1
Frederix and Frixione 2012
;
Alwall et al. 2014
and
Whizard
3
Moretti et al. 2001
;
Kilian et al. 2011
Monte Carlo (MC) event generators are used to simulate the signal and background contributions. The aQGC processes are simulated using
MadGraph5
_aMC@NLO at leading order (LO). The contributions of the amplitude of the interference between the EFT operators and the SM (referred to as the interference term) are simulated separately from the contributions involving only EFT operators (referred to as the quadratic term). More details can be found in Ref.
ATLAS
Collaboration 2023
. The
\rm{H}_{5}
signal processes are simulated using
MadGraph5
_aMC@NLO at LO for the mass range from 0.5 TeV to 3 TeV using the
H5plane
benchmark. The
s_{\mathrm{H}}
values are set to 0.5 for masses up to 0.8 TeV and 0.25 for higher masses to be compatible with present constraints
de Florian et al. 2016
. The SM
\rm{W}\rm{W}\nu\nu
and
\rm{W}\rm{W}\mu\mu
background processes are simulated with
MadGraph5
_aMC@NLO. These SM processes are also simulated with
Whizard
at LO and good agreement is seen with
MadGraph5
_aMC@NLO predictions.

Other background processes contributing to the
\rm{W}\rm{W}\nu\nu
channel are simulated following Ref.
Fleper et al. 2017
. The
\rm{W}\rm{Z}\mu\nu
,
\rm{Z}\rm{Z}\mu\mu
,
\rm{W}\rm{W}\mu\mu
, and
\rm{W}\rm{W}\rm{Z}(\rightarrow\!\nu\nu)
processes are simulated using
Whizard
. The initial state radiation of beam particles as implemented in
Whizard
is included in the simulation.

The parton showering and hadronization are simulated using
Pythia
8.306
Sjöstrand et al. 2015
. Detector effects are simulated using
Delphes
3.5
de Favereau et al. 2014
with a generic muon collider detector description. The effects of beam-induced background are not considered in this description. Muons and electrons are reconstructed with an absolute pseudorapidity up to 2.5. Jets are clustered from the reconstructed stable particles (except electrons and muons) using
FastJet

Cacciari et al. 2012
with the
Valencia
algorithm
Boronat et al. 2015
. Inclusive clustering with a distance parameter of
R=1
is performed.

III
Event selection

Events are selected targeting hadronically decaying
\rm{W}\rm{W}
and
\rm{Z}\rm{Z}
boson pairs with a large invariant mass. The jets are required to have transverse momenta (
p_{\mathrm{T}}
) greater than 100 GeV and be relatively central, with
|\cos\theta|<0.8
, where
\theta
is the angle of the jet with respect to the beam axis. The jet with the highest
p_{\mathrm{T}}
is called the “leading jet” and the jet with the second-highest
p_{\mathrm{T}}
the “subleading jet.” The leading and subleading jets are each required to have a mass greater than 40 GeV.

The
\rm{W}\rm{W}\nu\nu
and
\rm{Z}\rm{Z}\nu\nu
channels are targeted by vetoing events with a reconstructed electron or muon with momentum greater than
3
GeV. This requirement significantly reduces the
\rm{W}\rm{W}\mu\mu
and
\rm{Z}\rm{Z}\mu\mu
background contributions in these channels, which could be reduced even further with a detector with better forward muon coverage. As these channels contain two neutrinos in the final state, the events are also required to have a missing mass (
m_{\mathrm{miss}}
) greater than
200
GeV. The
m_{\mathrm{miss}}
is defined as

m_{\mathrm{miss}}=\sqrt{(\sqrt{s}-E_{\rm{V}\rm{V}})^{2}-|\vec{p}_{\rm{V}\rm{V}}|^{2}},

(1)

where
E_{\rm{V}\rm{V}}
and
\vec{p}_{\rm{V}\rm{V}}
are the energy and momentum of the
\rm{V}
boson pair. This requirement removes events where neutrinos are produced from
\rm{Z}
boson decays and reduces the contributions of the s-channel
\rm{W}\rm{W}
and two-jet processes.

The
\rm{W}\rm{W}\mu\mu
channel is targeted by requiring two oppositely charged muons with momenta greater than
0.5
GeV and
|\cos\theta|<0.99
. The largely dominant background contribution is the SM production of
\rm{W}\rm{W}\mu\mu
where the final state muons tend to be very forward. The mass of the dimuon pair is required to be greater than 106 GeV to reduce the contribution of events where the muons are produced from
\rm{Z}
boson decays.

Results

The selected events are used to constrain aQGC parameters in an EFT framework. Statistical analysis of the event yields is performed separately in the
\rm{W}\rm{W}\nu\nu
and
\rm{W}\rm{W}\mu\mu
channels with a fit to the invariant mass distribution of the two leading jets, which typically correspond to the pair of
\rm{W}
bosons, denoted
m_{\rm{W}\rm{W}}
. The distributions of
m_{\rm{W}\rm{W}}
after the event selection at each of the different center-of-mass energies are shown in Fig.
2
. The expected
95\%
confidence level (CL) lower and upper limits on the aQGC parameters
f/\Lambda^{4}
, where
f
is the Wilson coefficient of the given operator and
\Lambda
is the energy scale of new physics, are derived from Wilk’s theorem
Wilks 1938
assuming that the profile likelihood test statistic is
\chi^{2}
distributed
Cowan et al. 2011a
. No nuisance parameters corresponding to systematic uncertainties are included in the fits.

The EFT framework is not a complete model and the presence of nonzero aQGCs will violate tree-level unitarity at sufficiently high energy
Almeida et al. 2020

1.4.0

2

channel contributions to the zeroth partial wave are considered and the smallest unitarity bound is chosen. Generally, for 6 and 10 TeV collider options, unitarity violation occurs around or above the collider center-of-mass energy.
On the other hand, the expected limits at

30
TeV for many of the operators.

V
Summary

Prospects of searches for anomalous production of heavy gauge boson pairs at future high-energy muon colliders are reported. Muon-muon collision events are simulated at
\sqrt{s}=6
, 10, and 30 TeV corresponding to an integrated luminosity of
4
,
10
, and
10
ab
-1
, respectively. The simulated events are used to study the
\rm{W}\rm{W}\nu\nu
and
\rm{W}\rm{W}\mu\mu
channels with the
\rm{W}
bosons decaying hadronically. Constraints on the quartic vector boson interactions in the framework of dimension-8 effective field theory operators are obtained with stringent expected limits set on the EFT operators
S0
,
S1
,
M0
,
M1
,
M7
,
T0
,
T1
,
T2
,
T6
, and
T7
. Depending on the operator, the limits are better by more than one or two orders of magnitude compared to the expected limits at the HL-LHC and HE-LHC. The
\rm{W}\rm{W}\nu\nu
and
\rm{Z}\rm{Z}\nu\nu
channels are also used to report expected constraints on the product of the cross section and branching fraction for vector boson fusion production of a heavy neutral Higgs boson as a function of mass from 0.5 to 3 TeV. These results are interpreted in the context of the Georgi–Machacek model and show significantly more stringent constraints compared to the LHC results with more than an order of magnitude better sensitivity for
m_{\rm{H}_{5}}
values greater than 1 TeV.

Relativistic hydrodynamics with spin in the presence of electromagnetic fields Journal: Nuclear Physics A
Rajeev Singh, Masoud Shokri, S. M. A. Tabatabaee Mehr

Abstract
We extend the classical phase-space distribution function to include the spin and electromagnetic fields coupling and derive the modified constitutive relations for charge current, energy-momentum tensor, and spin tensor. Because of the coupling, the new tensors receive corrections to their perfect fluid counterparts and make the background and spin fluid equations of motion communicate with each other. We investigate special cases which are relevant in high-energy heavy-ion collisions, including baryon free matter and large mass limit. Using Bjorken symmetries, we find that spin polarization increases with increasing magnetic field for an initially positive baryon chemical potential. The corrections derived in this framework may help to explain the splitting observed in Lambda hyperons spin polarization measurements.

Conventions

We use the mostly-minus Minkowski metric signature which reads
g_{\mu\nu}={\rm diag}\left(+1,-1,-1,-1\right)
. As a result, the fluid four-velocity is normalized as
U^{\mu}U_{\mu}=1
. The operator
\Delta^{\mu\nu}=g^{\mu\nu}-U^{\mu}U^{\nu}
projects tensors on the space transverse to
U^{\mu}
. A tensor
M_{\mu\nu}
can be decomposed into a symmetric
M_{(\mu\nu)}\equiv\frac{1}{2}\left(M_{\mu\nu}+M_{\nu\mu}\right)
and an asymmetric
M_{[\mu\nu]}\equiv\frac{1}{2}\left(M_{\mu\nu}-M_{\nu\mu}\right)
part. We denote Levi-Civita symbol as
\epsilon^{\alpha\beta\gamma\delta}
which is totally antisymmetric and use the convention of
\epsilon^{0123}=-\epsilon_{0123}=1
. Euclidean three-vectors are denoted with boldface, like
\mathbf{B}
, as opposed to four-vectors. For the scalar and Frobenius product we use the notation
a\cdot b\equiv a^{\mu}b_{\nu}
and
A:B\equiv A^{\mu\nu}B_{\mu\nu}
, respectively. Throughout the paper we assume natural units
i.e.

c=\hbar=k_{B}~=1
, unless stated otherwise.

3
One-particle distribution function: coupling spin to EM fields

In this section we extend the classical phase-space spin distribution function
[
28
,
33
]
by introducing a term coupling the spin to the external EM fields, in a way suggested in Ref.
[
12
]
.

For the classical treatment of particles having spin one half and mass
m
, phase-space distribution function with spin reads
[
28
,
33
]

f^{\pm}_{0}(x,p,s)=f^{\pm}_{0}(x,p)\exp[\frac{1}{2}\,\omega(x):s(p)]\,,

(1)

where
\omega_{\alpha\beta}(x)
is the spin polarization tensor and
s^{\alpha\beta}(p)
represents the particle internal angular momentum expressed with spin four-vector
s^{\alpha}
and four-momentum
p^{\alpha}
as

\displaystyle s^{\alpha\beta}=\frac{1}{m}\epsilon^{\alpha\beta\gamma\delta}p_{\gamma}s_{\delta}\,.

(2)

In Eq. (
1
),
f_{0}^{\pm}(x,p)=\exp[-p\cdot\beta(x)\pm\xi(x)]
is the Jüttner distribution, with
\xi(x)
being the ratio of the baryon chemical potential
\mu(x)
over temperature
T(x)
,
\xi=\mu/T
, and
\beta_{\mu}(x)
is the ratio of the fluid four-velocity
U_{\mu}(x)
to temperature,
\beta_{\mu}=U_{\mu}/T
.
Note that, the classical distribution function (
1
) is valid only for the case of local collisions between particles, however, it can be extended to include nonlocal effects through the gradients of
f^{\pm}_{0}(x,p,s)

[
28
]
.

We generalize the phase-space distribution function (
1
) to a case of interaction between the particle magnetic moment and the external EM field by introducing the modified distribution function in the form

f^{\pm}_{\rm s}(x,p,s)=f^{\pm}_{\rm 0}(x,p,s)\exp\left[\mp\alpha_{M}(x)F(x):s\right],

(3)

where
F_{\mu\nu}
is the Faraday tensor expressed in terms of electric
E_{\mu}
and magnetic
B_{\mu}
four-vectors as

F_{\mu\nu}=E_{\mu}U_{\nu}-E_{\nu}U_{\mu}+\epsilon_{\mu\nu\alpha\beta}U^{\alpha}B^{\beta},

(4)

with

E^{\mu}\equiv F^{\mu\nu}U_{\nu}\,,\qquad B^{\mu}\equiv\frac{1}{2}\epsilon^{\mu\nu\alpha\beta}F_{\nu\alpha}U_{\beta}\,.

(5)

In Eq. (
3
),
\alpha_{M}=\mu_{M}/T
where
\mu_{M}=g_{Q}\mu_{N}
is the magnetic moment of the quasiparticles and
\mu_{N}
being the nuclear magneton. In this work, for simplicity, we assume that the quasiparticles are
\Lambda
hyperons, with
g_{\Lambda}=-0.6138\pm 0.0047

[
94
]
. However, it should be mentioned that, a more realistic setup needs multiple quark-like quasiparticles, with constitutive masses, see for example
[
31
]
.
Keeping in mind the smallness of the amplitude of spin polarization in measurements
[
17
]
,
we assume the small polarization limit
[
29
]
,
\omega_{\alpha\beta}\ll 1
.
We also assume weak EM fields
eB\ll M^{2}
, with
M
being a typical energy scale relevant to the system in consideration. If the quasiparticles are assumed to be electrically neutral Lambda hyperons, then
M\sim m_{\Lambda}
. The aforementioned assumptions allow Eq. (
3
) to be approximated as

\displaystyle f^{\pm}_{\rm s}(x,p,s)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle f_{0}^{\pm}(x,p)\left(1+\frac{1}{2}\omega:s\right)\left(1\mp\alpha_{M}F:s\right),~~~~

(6)

where
f_{\rm s}^{+}(f_{\rm s}^{-})
represents the distribution function for particles (antiparticles).
Similarly to the Faraday tensor, the spin polarization tensor
\omega_{\alpha\beta}
in Eq. (
6
) can be decomposed with respect to the fluid four-velocity as
[
32
]

\omega_{\mu\nu}=\kappa_{\mu}U_{\nu}-\kappa_{\nu}U_{\mu}+\epsilon_{\mu\nu\alpha\beta}U^{\alpha}\omega^{\beta},

(7)

where the four-vectors
\kappa_{\mu}
and
\omega_{\mu}
,

\kappa_{\mu}=\omega_{\mu\alpha}U^{\alpha},\quad\omega_{\mu}=\frac{1}{2}\epsilon_{\mu\alpha\beta\gamma}\omega^{\alpha\beta}U^{\gamma},

(8)

are orthogonal to
U^{\mu}

[
32
]
.
The above conditions leave
\kappa_{\mu}
and
\omega_{\mu}
with three degrees of freedom each, constituting together the same number of degrees of freedom as
\omega_{\mu\nu}
.
These four-vectors can be written in terms of three space-like orthonormal vectors
X^{\mu}
,
Y^{\mu}
, and
Z^{\mu}
which span the plane transverse to
U^{\mu}
. These vectors, together with
U^{\mu}
, form a basis satisfying
[
29
,
32
]

U\cdot U=1\,,\quad X\cdot X=Y\cdot Y=Z\cdot Z=-1\,.

(9)

Consequently,

\displaystyle\kappa^{\alpha}

\displaystyle=C_{\kappa X}X^{\alpha}+C_{\kappa Y}Y^{\alpha}+C_{\kappa Z}Z^{\alpha}\,,

(10)

\displaystyle\omega^{\alpha}

\displaystyle=C_{\omega X}X^{\alpha}+C_{\omega Y}Y^{\alpha}+C_{\omega Z}Z^{\alpha}\,.

(11)

Here,
C_{\boldsymbol{\kappa}}=(C_{\kappa X},C_{\kappa Y},C_{\kappa Z})
, and

C_{\boldsymbol{\omega}}=(C_{\omega X},C_{\omega Y},C_{\omega Z})
will be referred as the spin polarization components.

4
Constitutive relations in the presence of EM fields

In the following we derive the constitutive relations for the baryon charge current, energy-momentum tensor, and spin tensor from the distribution function (
6
) introduced in the previous section.

4.1
Charge current

The baryon charge current is the first moment of the modified distribution function (
6
)
[
28
,
27
]

N^{\lambda}=\!\int\!\mathrm{dP}~\mathrm{dS}\,\,p^{\lambda}\,\left[f^{+}_{\rm s}(x,p,s)\!-\!f^{-}_{\rm s}(x,p,s)\right],

(12)

where the invariant integration measures for momentum
\mathrm{dP}
and spin
\mathrm{dS}
are defined, respectively, as
[
28
]

\displaystyle\mathrm{dP}

\displaystyle=\frac{\mathrm{d}^{3}p}{(2\pi)^{3}E_{p}}\,,

\displaystyle\mathrm{dS}

\displaystyle=\frac{m}{\pi{\mathfrak{s}}}\,\mathrm{d}^{4}s~\delta(s\cdot s+{{\mathfrak{s}}}^{2})~\delta(p\cdot s)\,.

(13)

Here
E_{p}
is the particle energy and

\mathfrak{s}^{2}
is the length of the spin vector, which for spin half particles equals 3/4
[
28
]
.

Similarly to the current of a dissipative charged fluid
[
95
]
,
N^{\lambda}
can be decomposed as

N^{\lambda}={\cal N}U^{\lambda}+N_{\perp}^{\lambda}=\left({\cal N}_{\rm PF}+{\cal N}_{\rm EM}\right)U^{\lambda}+N_{\perp}^{\lambda}\,,

(14)

where
{\cal N}_{\rm PF}
is the perfect fluid baryon charge density
[
32
]

\displaystyle{\cal N}_{\rm PF}=4\,\sinh(\xi)\,{\cal N}_{(0)}\,.

(15)

The factor
4\,\sinh(\xi)
accounts for spin and particle-antiparticle degeneracies, and
{\cal N}_{(0)}

is number density of the spinless neutral classical massive particles
[
1
]

{\cal N}_{(0)}=gT^{3}\,z^{2}K_{2}(z)\,,

(16)

with
g=1/(2\pi^{2})
,

z=m/T
and
K_{n}
being
n^{th}

modified Bessel function of second kind.
In Eq. (
14
),
{\cal N}_{\rm EM}
is the charge density modification and
N_{\perp}^{\lambda}
is the transverse current, both due to spin-EM coupling. They read

\displaystyle{\cal N}_{\rm EM}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\alpha_{M}\cosh(\xi){\cal N}_{(0)}\epsilon^{\beta\gamma\nu\mu}\omega_{\beta\gamma}U_{\mu}B_{\nu}\,,

\displaystyle N_{\perp}^{\lambda}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\alpha_{M}\cosh(\xi)\mathcal{A}_{3}\big(U^{\lambda}F^{\beta\gamma}+6U^{\lambda}U^{[\beta}E^{\gamma]}

(17)

\displaystyle-U^{[\beta}F^{\gamma]\lambda}-g^{\lambda[\beta}E^{\gamma]}\big)\omega_{\beta\gamma}\,,

where

{\cal A}_{3}=-\left(2\left({\cal E}_{(0)}+{\cal P}_{(0)}\right)\right)/(T\,z^{2})\,

[
25
,
28
,
27
,
32
]
with
{\cal P}_{(0)}
, and
{\cal E}_{(0)}
denoting the pressure, and energy density of the spinless and neutral classical massive particles, defined as
[
1
]

{\cal P}_{(0)}={\cal N}_{(0)}T\,,\qquad{\cal E}_{(0)}=g\,z^{3}T^{4}K_{1}(z)+3{\cal P}_{(0)}\,,

(18)

respectively.

The conservation of charge current,
\partial_{\mu}N^{\mu}=0
, leads to the first equation of motion

\hbox{\hskip 3.75pt\hskip-3.75pt\hbox{$\mathcal{N}$}\hskip-3.75pt\hskip-4.16663pt\raisebox{7.83331pt}{\hbox{$\mkern 2.0mu\bullet$}}\hskip-4.16663pt\hskip 3.75pt}_{\rm PF}+\hbox{\hskip 3.75pt\hskip-3.75pt\hbox{$\mathcal{N}$}\hskip-3.75pt\hskip-4.16663pt\raisebox{7.83331pt}{\hbox{$\mkern 2.0mu\bullet$}}\hskip-4.16663pt\hskip 3.75pt}_{\rm EM}+\left(\mathcal{N}_{\rm PF}+\mathcal{N}_{\rm EM}\right)\theta_{U}=-\partial\cdot N_{\perp}\,,

(19)

wherein
\hbox{\hskip 3.75pt\hskip-3.75pt\hbox{$\cdots$}\hskip-3.75pt\hskip-4.16663pt\raisebox{8.0pt}{\hbox{$\mkern 2.0mu\bullet$}}\hskip-4.16663pt\hskip 3.75pt}\equiv U\cdot\partial\,{\cdots}
denotes the co-moving temporal derivative
[
96
]
and
\theta_{U}\equiv\partial\cdot U
is the expansion scalar. If the quasi-particles carry a electric charge
q
, the electric current is

J^{\mu}=q\,N^{\mu}\,,

(20)

resulting in a “back-reaction” of spin-EM coupling with EM fields via Maxwell equations. Since we are taking Lambda hyperons, which are electrically neutral, as the quasiparticles of the fluid the electric current vanishes
[
97
]
.

4.2
Energy-momentum tensor

The energy-momentum tensor is the second moment of the distribution function (
6
), namely
[
28
,
27
]

\displaystyle T^{\mu\nu}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int\mathrm{dP}~\mathrm{dS}\,\,p^{\mu}p^{\nu}\,\left[f^{+}_{\rm s}(x,p,s)+f^{-}_{\rm s}(x,p,s)\right].~~

(21)

Performing the spin integration followed by the momentum integration one obtains

T^{\mu\nu}={\cal E}U^{\mu}U^{\nu}-{\cal P}\Delta^{\mu\nu}+{\cal Q}^{\mu}U^{\nu}+{\cal Q}^{\nu}U^{\mu}+{\cal T}^{\mu\nu}\,.

(22)

Here
{\cal E}
is the modified energy density

\displaystyle{\cal E}

\displaystyle\equiv

\displaystyle U_{\mu}U_{\nu}T^{\mu\nu}=\mathcal{E}_{\rm PF}+\mathcal{E}_{\rm EM}\,,

(23)

with

\displaystyle{\cal E}_{\rm PF}

\displaystyle=4\cosh(\xi){\cal E}_{(0)}\,,

(24)

\displaystyle{\cal E}_{\rm EM}

\displaystyle=\alpha_{M}\sinh(\xi)\bigg\{{\cal E}_{(0)}\,\omega:F\,

\displaystyle+2\Big[\left(I_{40}^{(0)}+I_{41}^{(0)}\right)\kappa\cdot E-2I_{41}^{(0)}\omega\cdot B\Big]\bigg\}\,,

and
{\cal P}
is the modified pressure

\displaystyle{\cal P}

\displaystyle\equiv

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{3}\Delta:T=\mathcal{P}_{\rm PF}+\mathcal{P}_{\rm EM}\,,

(25)

with

\displaystyle{\cal P}_{\rm PF}

\displaystyle=4\cosh(\xi)\,{\cal P}_{(0)}\,,

(26)

\displaystyle{\cal P}_{\rm EM}

\displaystyle=\alpha_{M}\sinh(\xi)\bigg\{{\cal P}_{(0)}\,\omega:F

\displaystyle-2\Big[\left(I_{41}^{(0)}+\frac{5}{3}I_{42}^{(0)}\right)\kappa\cdot E-\frac{10}{3}I_{42}^{(0)}\omega\cdot B\Big]\bigg\}.

In Eq. (
22
),
{\cal Q}^{\mu}
is a transverse vector current that resembles the heat current, and
{\cal T}^{\mu\nu}
is a transverse traceless tensor similar to the stress tensor of a dissipative fluid
[
95
]
given by

\displaystyle{\cal Q}^{\mu}

\displaystyle\equiv

\displaystyle\Delta^{\mu}_{\hphantom{\alpha}\alpha}U_{\beta}T^{\alpha\beta}

(27)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2\,\alpha_{M}\sinh(\FUG)I_{41}^{(0)}\epsilon^{\mu\nu\alpha\beta}\,U_{\nu}\left(E_{\alpha}\omega_{\beta}-B_{\alpha}\kappa_{\beta}\right),

\displaystyle{\cal T}^{\mu\nu}

\displaystyle\equiv

\displaystyle\Delta^{\mu\nu}_{\alpha\beta}\,T^{\alpha\beta}

(28)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 4\,\alpha_{M}\sinh(\FUG)I_{42}^{(0)}\Big(E^{(\mu}\kappa^{\nu)}+B^{(\mu}\omega^{\nu)}

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{3}\Delta^{\mu\nu}\left(\kappa\cdot E+\omega\cdot B\right)\Big)\,.

where
\Delta^{\mu\nu}_{\alpha\beta}\equiv(1/2)\left[\Delta^{\mu}_{\hphantom{\alpha}\alpha}\Delta^{\nu}_{\hphantom{\alpha}\beta}+\Delta^{\nu}_{\hphantom{\alpha}\alpha}\Delta^{\mu}_{\hphantom{\alpha}\beta}-(2/3)\Delta^{\mu\nu}\Delta_{\alpha\beta}\right]
.
The thermodynamic integrals
I_{nq}^{(r)}
appearing in the equations above are defined in
A
.

As usual, the conservation of energy and momentum,
\partial_{\nu}T^{\mu\nu}=F^{\mu\sigma}J_{\sigma}
, can be decomposed into longitudinal and transverse parts with respect to
U
. The longitudinal one, the so-called energy equation, reads

\displaystyle\hbox{\hskip 2.6389pt\hskip-2.6389pt\hbox{${\cal E}$}\hskip-2.6389pt\hskip-4.16663pt\raisebox{7.83331pt}{\hbox{$\mkern 2.0mu\bullet$}}\hskip-4.16663pt\hskip 2.6389pt}+({\cal E}+{\cal P})\,\theta_{U}=-qE\cdot{N_{\bot}}+\hbox{\hskip 3.95901pt\hskip-3.95901pt\hbox{$U$}\hskip-3.95901pt\hskip-4.16663pt\raisebox{7.83331pt}{\hbox{$\mkern 2.0mu\bullet$}}\hskip-4.16663pt\hskip 3.95901pt}\cdot\mathcal{Q}-\nabla\cdot\mathcal{Q}+\frac{1}{2}\,\mathcal{T}:\sigma\,,

(29)

where
\nabla_{\mu}\equiv\partial_{\mu}-U_{\mu}U^{\nu}\partial_{\nu}
and

\sigma^{\mu\nu}\equiv\Delta^{\mu\nu}_{\alpha\beta}\nabla^{\alpha}U^{\beta}
.
In the direction transverse to
U
we have

\displaystyle(\mathcal{E}+\mathcal{P})\,\hbox{\hskip 3.95901pt\hskip-3.95901pt\hbox{$U$}\hskip-3.95901pt\hskip-4.16663pt\raisebox{7.83331pt}{\hbox{$\mkern 2.0mu\bullet$}}\hskip-4.16663pt\hskip 3.95901pt}^{\mu}=\nabla^{\mu}\mathcal{P}+q\left({\cal N}E^{\mu}+\epsilon^{\mu\alpha\beta\sigma}B_{\alpha}{N_{\bot}}_{\beta}\,U_{\sigma}\right)

(30)

\displaystyle\hskip 14.22636pt-\Big(\hbox{\hskip 3.95901pt\hskip-3.95901pt\hbox{$U$}\hskip-3.95901pt\hskip-4.16663pt\raisebox{7.83331pt}{\hbox{$\mkern 2.0mu\bullet$}}\hskip-4.16663pt\hskip 3.95901pt}\cdot\mathcal{Q}+\frac{1}{2}\,\mathcal{T}:\sigma\Big)U^{\mu}-\partial_{\alpha}\mathcal{T}^{\mu\alpha}-\hbox{\hskip 4.08334pt\hskip-4.08334pt\hbox{${\cal Q}$}\hskip-4.08334pt\hskip-4.16663pt\raisebox{7.83331pt}{\hbox{$\mkern 2.0mu\bullet$}}\hskip-4.16663pt\hskip 4.08334pt}^{\mu}-\partial_{\alpha}\left(U^{\alpha}{\cal Q}^{\mu}\right).

4.3
Spin tensor

The spin tensor is defined as
[
28
]

S^{\lambda,\mu\nu}=\int\mathrm{dP}~\mathrm{dS}\,\,p^{\lambda}\,s^{\mu\nu}\left[f^{+}_{\rm s}(x,p,s)+f^{-}_{\rm s}(x,p,s)\right].

(31)

Plugging the distribution function (
6
) into the above equation and integrating over the spin and momenta gives

S^{\lambda,\mu\nu}=S^{\lambda,\mu\nu}_{\rm PF}-2\alpha_{M}\tanh(\xi)S^{\lambda,\mu\nu}_{\rm EM}\,,

(32)

where
[
28
,
27
,
29
,
32
]

\displaystyle S^{\alpha,\beta\gamma}_{\rm PF}

\displaystyle=\cosh(\xi)\Big[{\cal A}_{1}\,U^{\alpha}\,\omega^{\beta\gamma}+{\cal A}_{2}\,U^{\alpha}\,U^{[\beta}\,\omega^{\gamma]}_{\hphantom{\alpha}\delta}\,U^{\delta}

\displaystyle+{\cal A}_{3}\,\left(U^{[\beta}\,\omega^{\gamma]\alpha}+g^{\alpha[\beta}\,\omega^{\gamma]}_{\hphantom{\alpha}\delta}\,U^{\delta}\right)\Big]\,,

\displaystyle S^{\alpha,\beta\gamma}_{\rm EM}

\displaystyle=\cosh(\xi)\Big[U^{\alpha}{\cal A}_{1}\,F^{\beta\gamma}+{\cal A}_{2}\,U^{\alpha}U^{[\beta}\,F^{\gamma]}_{\hphantom{\alpha}\hphantom{\alpha}\delta}\,U^{\delta}

\displaystyle+{\cal A}_{3}\,\left(U^{[\beta}\,F^{\gamma]\alpha}+g^{\alpha[\beta}\,F^{\gamma]}_{\hphantom{\alpha}\hphantom{\alpha}\delta}\,U^{\delta}\right)\Big],

(33)

with the thermodynamic coefficients defined as

{\cal A}_{1}={\cal N}_{(0)}-{\cal A}_{3}\,,\quad{\cal A}_{2}=2\left[{\cal A}_{1}-2{\cal A}_{3}\right]\,.

(34)

As the phase-space distribution function (
6
) is valid only for the local collisions of the particles, it suggests that the orbital contribution
(L^{\alpha,\beta\gamma})
, in the total angular momentum
(J^{\alpha,\beta\gamma})

J^{\alpha,\beta\gamma}=L^{\alpha,\beta\gamma}+S^{\alpha,\beta\gamma}=x^{\beta}\,T^{\alpha\gamma}-x^{\gamma}\,T^{\alpha\beta}+S^{\alpha,\beta\gamma}\,,

(35)

can be eliminated and spin
(S^{\alpha,\beta\gamma})
can be conserved independently
[
28
]
.
Here, the energy-momentum tensor (
21
) is, by definition, symmetric. However, it may have antisymmetric contribution if we include nonlocal collisional effects
[
73
,
72
]
, which are neglected in the current study for simplicity.

Thus, neglecting nonlocal collisions we observe that the spin tensor is a conserved current

\partial_{\alpha}\,S^{\alpha,\beta\gamma}=0\,.

(36)

From above we get six equations of motion for the six spin polarization components (
C_{\boldsymbol{\kappa}}
and
C_{\boldsymbol{\omega}}
) which, in general, are coupled to each other.

5.1
Baryon-free system

In ultra-relativistic heavy-ion collisions, the QGP can be considered approximately as baryon-free matter
[
3
]
. In the absence of the baryon chemical potential, i.e.,
\mu=0
, the spin-EM coupling can only affect the charge current sector.
The spin-EM coupling results in a non-vanishing EM component of the number density
{\cal N}_{\rm EM}
and transverse current
N_{\bot}^{\mu}
in Eq. (
14
). Furthermore, the heat current
\mathcal{Q}^{\mu}
(
27
) and stress tensor
\mathcal{T}^{\mu\nu}
(
28
) vanish, and the energy-momentum tensor is reduced to its perfect fluid form

T^{\mu\nu}={\cal E}_{\rm PF}U^{\mu}U^{\nu}-{\cal P}_{\rm PF}\Delta^{\mu\nu}\,.

(37)

Similarly, the spin tensor (
32
) reduces to

S^{\alpha,\beta\gamma}_{(\mu=0)}=U^{\alpha}\left[{\cal A}_{1}\omega^{\beta\gamma}+{\cal A}_{2}U^{[\beta}\kappa^{\gamma]}\right]+{\cal A}_{3}\left[U^{[\beta}\omega^{\gamma]\alpha}+g^{\alpha[\beta}\kappa^{\gamma]}\right]

(38)

with no spin-EM coupling terms.

5.2
Large mass limit

Another special case is when the constituents of the fluid, say hyperons, have masses much larger than the temperature, i.e.
z=m/T\gg 1
. Then, we can neglect
{\cal A}_{3}
in Eq. (
33
), and the electric field no longer appears in the spin tensor (
32
), giving

S^{\alpha,\beta\gamma}_{z\gg 1}=\cosh(\xi){\cal N}_{(0)}U^{\alpha}\epsilon^{\beta\gamma\mu\nu}U_{\mu}\left(\omega_{\nu}-2\alpha_{M}\tanh(\xi)B_{\nu}\right).

(39)

0+1D: Bjorken flow

Bjorken symmetries simplify equations of motion significantly;
the hydrodynamic variables become functions of proper time
\tau=\sqrt{t^{2}-z^{2}}
alone, and energy-momentum conservation in the transverse direction
(
30

is trivially satisfied.
Furthermore, the heat current
{\cal Q}^{\mu}
(
27

{\cal T}^{\mu\nu}

28

Yet, even in this situation, the equations of motion of background and spin are highly coupled due to spin-EM contribution.
The fluid flow is affected by the spin dynamics, which is different from the previous studies
[
29
,
30
,
31
]

We start with the baryon charge conservation (
19
). The transverse current trivially satisfies
\partial_{\mu}N_{\bot}^{\mu}=0
due to Bjorken symmetries. Therefore the charge conservation reduces to an equation formally similar to that of Bjorken perfect fluid
[
29
]

\derivative{\cal N}{\tau}+\frac{\cal N}{\tau}=0\,.

(40)

For simplicity, we assume uncharged particles and the ideal MHD limit. Therefore, the profile of the magnetic field is
[
98
]

B^{\mu}=\alpha{\cal B}_{0}\frac{\tau_{0}}{\tau}Y^{\mu}\,,

(41)

with
{\cal B}_{0}=(1/e)\,m_{\pi}^{2}
, with
m_{\pi}
being the Pion’s mass, and
\alpha
being a positive dimensionless number. At the initial proper time
\tau_{0}
the initial magnetic field is
B_{0}=\alpha{\cal B}_{0}
. Setting
\alpha=0
switches off the magnetic field. Here,
Y^{\mu}=(0,0,1,0)
.
Due to the magnetic field profile adopted herein, Eq. (
41

C_{\omega Y}
will be coupled to the EM fields, which is indeed the case shown below.

The spin-EM coupling occurs through the term
F:\omega=-2B_{0}C_{\omega Y}\tau_{0}/\tau
, which gives rise to

\displaystyle{\cal N}

\displaystyle={\cal{N}}_{\rm PF}+2\,gT^{3}\,\alpha_{M}\,B_{0}\,C_{\omega Y}\,\frac{\tau_{0}}{\tau}\cosh(\xi)\Big[z^{2}\,K_{2}(z)

\displaystyle+2\,z\,K_{1}(z)+2\,\left(K_{2}(z)+3K_{3}(z)\right)\Big]\,,

(42)

where

is given by Eq. (
15

Here
T

\xi

are unknown functions of
\tau

The next equation is the energy equation which reads

\derivative{\cE}{\tau}+\frac{{\cal E}+{\cal P}}{\tau}=0\,,

(43)

where

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}

\displaystyle=gT^{4}\,z^{2}\left(z\,K_{1}(z)+3\,\,K_{3}(z)\right)

\displaystyle\times 2\left(2\cosh(\xi)+\alpha_{M}\,B_{0}\,C_{\omega Y}\,\frac{\tau_{0}}{\tau}\sinh(\xi)\right),

(44)

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}

\displaystyle={\cal{N}}_{\rm PF}T\left(\coth{(\xi)}+\alpha_{M}\,B_{0}\,C_{\omega Y}\,\frac{\tau_{0}}{2\tau}\right).

(45)

7
Summary

In this work, we introduced a spin-EM coupling term into the classical phase-space distribution function and derived the charge current, energy-momentum tensor, and spin tensor. In contrast to previous studies
[
29
,
30
,
31
]
, the background and spin equations of motion are highly coupled due to the spin-EM coupling. We also mentioned certain special cases relevant to ultra-relativistic heavy-ion collisions. Lastly, we examined our formalism with Bjorken flow for four different sets of initial temperature and baryon chemical potential and found that with increasing collisional energy, the amplitude of spin component decreases in the low collision energy regime, while it increases otherwise.
With some further considerations, the presented formalism may be relevant in explaining the observed
\Lambda-\bar{\Lambda}
spin polarization splitting. In particular, it is required to consider more realistic setups, which is the focus of our future investigation.

Grid-Forming Hybrid Angle Control: Behavior, Stability, Variants and Verification
Ali Tayyebi, Denis Vettoretti, Adolfo Anta, Florian Dörfler

Abstract
This work explores the stability, behavior, variants, and a chil ( chil ) verification of the recently proposed gfm ( gfm ) hac ( hac ). We revisit the foundation of gfm hac , and highlight its behavioral properties in relation to the conventional sm ( sm ). Next, we introduce the required complementary controls to be combined with the hac to realize a gfm behavior. The characterization of the analytical operating point and nonlinear energy-based stability analysis of a grid-connected converter under the hac is presented. Further, we consider various output filter configurations and derive an approximation for the original control proposal. Moreover, we provide details on the integration of gfm hac into a complex converter control architecture and introduce several variants of the standard hac . Finally, the performance of gfm hac is verified by several test scenarios in a chil setup to test its behavior against real-world effect such as noise and delays.

III-C

Nonlinear energy-based stability analysis

The system (

A more comprehensive nonlinear analysis approach is to associate a so-called

wrt

(24)

\displaystyle\dfrac{\text{{d}}{V(\hat{x})}}{\text{{d}}t}

\displaystyle=\left(\dfrac{c_{1}}{2}\sin\dfrac{\hat{\delta}}{2}\right)\dfrac{\text{{d}}{\hat{\delta}}}{\text{{d}}t}+2\left(\left(c_{2}\hat{\zeta}\right)\dfrac{\text{{d}}{\hat{\zeta}}}{\text{{d}}t}+\left(c_{3}\hat{v}_{\text{{dc}}}\right)\dfrac{\text{{d}}{\hat{v}_{\text{{dc}}}}}{\text{{d}}t}+\left(c_{4}\hat{i}_{\text{{d}}}\right)\dfrac{\text{{d}}{\hat{i}_{\text{{d}}}}}{\text{{d}}t}+\left(c_{5}\hat{i}_{\text{{q}}}\right)\dfrac{\text{{d}}{\hat{i}_{\text{{q}}}}}{\text{{d}}t}\right),

V-A

Controller-hardware-in-the-loop verification approach

To verify the proposed

The utilized hardware benchmark is depicted in Figure
7

The control cards receive the dc voltage and ac voltage and current as analog signals from Opal-RT, and generate the PWM signals to be sent back to the real-time simulator as digital signals. Switching frequency for the inverter is set to 5kHz, which is the same rate as for the execution of the controller in the control card. The grid-connected converter model and the dc source controller are executed in Opal-RT, using Time-Stamped Bridges to model the inverter IGBTs

The testbed architecture is represented in Figure

Given that the control cards possess two cores, data recording occurs in an online manner, bypassing memory limitations in the control card. Our setup allows us to measure relevant internal signals from the controllers such as frequency and filtered ac power. By means of configuration files, the tests of interest, set-points, and models to be used are defined. The tests are completely automatized, including the flashing of the control cards, building the grid models for the Opal-RT, synchronization of the cards and the real-time simulator, and finally the retrieval of all data of interest.

VI
Conclusion

In this work, we discussed the behavioral properties of the

Unknown Truths and Unknowable Truths
Jie Fan

Abstract
Notions of unknown truths and unknowable truths are important in formal epistemology, which are related to each other in e.g. Fitch’s paradox of knowability. Although there have been some logical research on the notion of unknown truths and some philosophical discussion on the two notions, there seems to be no logical research on unknowable truths. In this paper, we propose a logic of unknowable truths, investigate the logical properties of unknown truths and unknowable truths, which includes the similarities of the two notions and the relationship between the two notions, and axiomatize this logic.



Keywords: unkown truths, unknowable truths, axiomatization, Fitch’s paradox of knowability

1
Introduction

This article investigates notions of unknown truths and unknowable truths. A proposition is an
unknown truth
, if it is true but unknown; a proposition is an
unknowable truth
, if it is true but unknowable. The two notions are related to each other in e.g. Fitch’s paradox of knowability, which states that if there is an unknown truth, then there is an unknowable truth.

The notion of unknown truths is important in formal epistemology. For example, it is related to verification thesis, which says that all truths can be known. By the thesis and two uncontroversial principles of knowledge, it follows from the notion that all truths are actually known. In this way, the notion gives rise to a well-known counterexample to the verification thesis. This is the so-called ‘Fitch’s paradox of knowability’. To take another example: the notion gives us an important type of Moore sentences, which is in turn crucial to Moore’s paradox, saying that one cannot claim the paradoxical sentence “
p
but I do not know it”. A well-known result is that such a Moore sentence is unsuccessful and self-refuting
[
17
,
10
]
.

The notion of unknowable truths is crucial in the dispute of realists and anti-realists. Anti-realists holds the aforementioned verificationist thesis, which states that all truths can be known, thereby denying the very possibility of unknowable truths. In contrast, realists believe that there are parts of reality, representable in some conceptually accessible language, that it is impossible for any agent ever to know. As guessed in
[
19
, p. 119]
, “Perhaps, in truths that cannot be known lies ‘the mystery at the heart of things’.”

Note that the notion of unknowable truths also gives an important type of Moore sentences, since one cannot claim the paradoxical sentence “
p
is true but I cannot know it”. We can also show that such a Moore sentence is unsuccessful and self-refuting.

Some researchers in the literature discuss about whether unknowable truths exist or not. For example, Edgington
[
8
]
admits that there are
unknowable truths in the sense of Fitch’s argument. Horsten
[
18
]
gives an interesting example of unknowable truths, by using a revision of the Gödel sentence.
Cook
[
5
]
argues that even if it is impossible for it to be known of any particular sentence
that it is both true and unknowable, we may still know that there are
unknowable truths.

However, it is shown in
[

]
to be unsuccessful since the argument depends on a paradoxical reasoning.
Besides, it is argued in
[

that there are unknowable truths, that is, necessary limits to knowledge. As we will show below, under our interpretation of knowability, there are indeed unknowable truths. Thus our interpretation is in line with the aforementioned philosophical discussion.

In the literature, there have been several interpretations for the notion of knowability. For instance, in
[
20
,
3
]
, knowability means ‘known after an announcement’; in
[
6
]
, knowability means the existence of proofs; in
[

, knowability means capabilities to know; in
[

]
, it is interpreted as a capacity-knowledge modality ‘it can be known that’; in
[
23
]
, knowability means ‘known after an information update’; whereas in
[

]
, knowability means ‘ dynamically possible knowledge of what was true before the update of the epistemic state of the agent’. In this paper, we follow

to interpret ‘

\varphi
is known after some truthful public announcement’, so that ‘
\varphi
is an unknowable truth’ is interpreted as ‘
\varphi
is true and after any truthful public announcement,
\varphi
is unknown’.

Unknowable truths (and unknown truths) is a subjective concept, since it is possible that a proposition is an unknowable truth (an unknown truth, resp.) for an agent but not for another. For instance, consider the true proposition “it is raining but Ann does not know it”. This proposition is an unknowable truth (an unknown truth, resp.) for Ann, but is
not
an unknowable truth (an unknown truth, resp.) for another agent Bob, who may be aware of Ann’s ignorance. Therefore we move to the multiagent cases.

Although there have been some logical research on the notion of unknown truths (see e.g.
[
14
,
11
]
), and also some philosophical discussion on the two notions
[

, there seems to be no logical research on unknowable truths. For instance, there have been no axiomatization on the notion of unknowable truths. For another example, it is unnoticed that there are many similarities and relationship between the notions of unknown truths and unknowable truths. In this paper, we propose a logic of unknowable truths,
investigate the logical properties of unknown truths and unknowable truths, which include the similarities and relationship between the two notions, and axiomatize the logic of unknowable truths.

2
Syntax and Semantics

Throughout the paper, we let
P
be the denumerable set of propositional variables, and let
I
be a finite set of agents.

Definition 1
.

Where
p\in\textbf{P}
,
i\in\textbf{I}
, the language of the logic of unknowable truths, denoted
LUT
, is defined recursively as follows:

\begin{array}[]{lll}\textbf{LUT}&\varphi::=&p\mid\neg\varphi\mid(\varphi\land\varphi)\mid\textit{K}_{i}\varphi\mid[\varphi]\varphi\mid U_{i}\varphi\\
\end{array}

\textit{K}_{i}\varphi
is read “agent
i
knows that
\varphi
”,
[\psi]\varphi
is read “after every truthfully public announcement of
\psi
, it is the case that
\varphi
”, and
U_{i}\varphi
is read “
\varphi
is an unknowable truth for
i
”. Other connectives are defined as usual; in particular,
\bullet_{i}\varphi
, read “
\varphi
is an unknown truth”, abbreviates
\varphi\land\neg\textit{K}_{i}\varphi
, and
\langle\psi\rangle\varphi
abbreviates
\neg[\psi]\neg\varphi
.
Without the construct
U_{i}\varphi
, we obtain public announcement logic
PAL
; without the construct
[\varphi]\varphi
further, we obtain epistemic logic
EL
.
The language
LUT
is interpreted on models.

Definition 2
.

A
model

\mathcal{M}
is a triple
\langle S,\{R_{i}\mid i\in\textbf{I}\},V\rangle
, where
S
is a nonempty set of states, each
R_{i}
is the accessibility relation for
i
, and
V
is a valuation. We assume that every
R_{i}
is reflexive.
2
2

2

Note that here for simplicity, we only consider the minimal restriction of knowledge. We can also consider extra properties of knowledge, for instance, transitivity, Euclidicity, etc., in which cases we add the corresponding axioms for knowledge in the axiomatization.
A
frame
is a model without valuations. A
pointed model
is a pair of a model and a state in the model.

Definition 3
.

Given a model
\mathcal{M}=\langle S,\{R_{i}\mid i\in\textbf{I}\},V\rangle
, the semantics of
LUT
is defined recursively as follows.

\begin{array}[]{|lll|}\hline\cr\mathcal{M},s\vDash p&\iff&s\in V(p)\\
\mathcal{M},s\vDash\neg\varphi&\iff&\mathcal{M},s\nvDash\varphi\\
\mathcal{M},s\vDash\varphi\land\psi&\iff&\mathcal{M},s\vDash\varphi\text{ and }\mathcal{M},s\vDash\psi\\
\mathcal{M},s\vDash\textit{K}_{i}\varphi&\iff&\text{for all }t,\text{ if }sR_{i}t,\text{ then }\mathcal{M},t\vDash\varphi\\
\mathcal{M},s\vDash[\psi]\varphi&\iff&\text{if }\mathcal{M},s\vDash\psi\text{ then }\mathcal{M}|_{\psi},s\vDash\varphi\\
\mathcal{M},s\vDash U_{i}\varphi&\iff&\mathcal{M},s\vDash\varphi\text{ and for all }\psi\in\textbf{EL},\mathcal{M},s\vDash{[\psi]\neg\textit{K}_{i}}\varphi\\
\hline\cr\end{array}

where
\mathcal{M}|_{\psi}
is the model restriction of
\mathcal{M}
to
\psi
-states, and the restriction of announced formula in the semantics of
U_{i}
is to avoid circularity. We say that
\varphi
is
valid over a frame
F
, if for all models
\mathcal{M}
based on
F
and all states
s
, we have
\mathcal{M},s\vDash\varphi
. We say that
\varphi
is
valid
, denoted
\vDash\varphi
, if it is valid over all (reflexive) frames. We say that
\varphi
is
satisfiable
, if there is a pointed model
(\mathcal{M},s)
such that
\mathcal{M},s\vDash\varphi
.

Intuitively,
U_{i}\varphi
means that
\varphi
is true and unknowable for
i
; in more details,
\varphi
is true and after each truthfully public announcement,
\varphi
is unknown to
i
.

One may compute the semantics of
\bullet_{i}
and
\langle\psi\rangle\varphi
as follows:

\begin{array}[]{lll}\mathcal{M},s\vDash\bullet_{i}\varphi&\iff&\mathcal{M},s\vDash\varphi\text{ and for some }t,sR_{i}t\text{ and }\mathcal{M},t\nvDash\varphi\\
\mathcal{M},s\vDash\langle\psi\rangle\varphi&\iff&\mathcal{M},s\vDash\psi\text{ and }\mathcal{M}|_{\psi},s\vDash\varphi.\\
\end{array}

3
Logical properties

In this section, we investigate logical properties of unknown truths and unknowable truths.

3.1
Similarities

First, we can summarize some similarities between the logical properties of unknown truths and the logical properties of unknowable truths in the following diagram.

\begin{array}[]{|c||c|c|}\hline\cr&\text{Unknown Truths}&\text{Unknowable Truths}\\
\hline\cr&\bullet_{i}\varphi\to\varphi&U_{i}\varphi\to\varphi\\
&\bullet_{i}(\varphi\to\psi)\to(\bullet_{i}\varphi\to\bullet_{i}\psi)&U_{i}(\varphi\to\psi)\to(U_{i}\varphi\to U_{i}\psi)\\
&\bullet_{i}\varphi\land\bullet_{i}\psi\to\bullet_{i}(\varphi\land\psi)&U_{i}\varphi\land U_{i}\psi\to U_{i}(\varphi\land\psi)\\
&\bullet_{i}\varphi\to\bullet_{i}{\bullet_{i}\varphi}&U_{i}\varphi\to U_{i}U_{i}\varphi\\
\text{validities}&\bullet_{i}\varphi\leftrightarrow\bullet_{i}{\bullet_{i}\varphi}&U_{i}\varphi\leftrightarrow U_{i}U_{i}\varphi\\
&\neg\textit{K}_{i}{\bullet_{i}\varphi}&\neg\textit{K}_{i}U_{i}\varphi\\
&\neg{\bullet_{i}\textit{K}_{i}\varphi}~(\text{transitive})&\neg U_{i}\textit{K}_{i}\varphi~(\text{transitive})\\
&\neg{\bullet_{i}\neg\textit{K}_{i}\varphi}~(\text{Euclidean})&\neg U_{i}\neg\textit{K}_{i}\varphi~(\text{Euclidean})\\
&[\bullet_{i}p]\neg{\bullet_{i}p}&[U_{i}p]\neg U_{i}p\\
\hline\cr\hline\cr\text{invalidities}&\neg{\bullet_{i}\varphi}\to\bullet_{i}\neg{\bullet_{i}\varphi}&\neg U_{i}\varphi\to U_{i}\neg U_{i}\varphi\\
\hline\cr\end{array}

In what follows, we take some of the validities and invalidities as examples, and leave others to the reader.

Proposition 4
.

\vDash U_{i}(\varphi\to\psi)\to(U_{i}\varphi\to U_{i}\psi)
.

Proof.

Let
\mathcal{M}=\langle S,\{R_{i}\mid i\in\textbf{I}\},V\rangle
be a model and
s\in S
. Suppose that
\mathcal{M},s\vDash U_{i}(\varphi\to\psi)
and
\mathcal{M},s\vDash U_{i}\varphi
, to show that
\mathcal{M},s\vDash U_{i}\psi
. By supposition, we have that
\mathcal{M},s\vDash\varphi
and for all
\chi\in\textbf{EL}
,
\mathcal{M},s\vDash[\chi]\neg\textit{K}_{i}\varphi
, and that
\mathcal{M},s\vDash\varphi\to\psi
and for all
\chi\in\textbf{EL}
,
\mathcal{M},s\vDash[\chi]\neg\textit{K}_{i}(\varphi\to\psi)
. From
\mathcal{M},s\vDash\varphi
and
\mathcal{M},s\vDash\varphi\to\psi
, it follows that
\mathcal{M},s\vDash\psi
. From for all
\chi\in\textbf{EL}
,
\mathcal{M},s\vDash[\chi]\neg\textit{K}_{i}(\varphi\to\psi)
, we can derive that for all
\chi\in\textbf{EL}
,
\mathcal{M},s\vDash[\chi]\neg\textit{K}_{i}\psi
. Therefore,
\mathcal{M},s\vDash U_{i}\psi
.
∎

Proposition 5
.

\vDash U_{i}\varphi\land U_{i}\psi\to U_{i}(\varphi\land\psi)
.

Proof.

Let
\mathcal{M}=\langle S,\{R_{i}\mid i\in\textbf{I}\},V\rangle
be a model and
s\in S
. Suppose that
\mathcal{M},s\vDash U_{i}\varphi
and
\mathcal{M},s\vDash U_{i}\psi
, to show that
\mathcal{M},s\vDash U_{i}(\varphi\land\psi)
. By supposition,
\mathcal{M},s\vDash\varphi
and for all
\chi\in\textbf{EL}
,
\mathcal{M},s\vDash[\chi]\neg\textit{K}_{i}\varphi
, and
\mathcal{M},s\vDash\psi
and for all
\chi\in\textbf{EL}
,
\mathcal{M},s\vDash[\chi]\neg\textit{K}_{i}\psi
. It follows that
\mathcal{M},s\vDash\varphi\land\psi
and for all
\chi\in\textbf{EL}
,
\mathcal{M},s\vDash[\chi]\neg\textit{K}_{i}(\varphi\land\psi)
, and therefore
\mathcal{M},s\vDash U_{i}(\varphi\land\psi)
.
∎

Intuitively, one cannot know the unknowable truths. This is because unknowable truths are themselves unknowable truths, as shown below. One can show that it is equivalent to the statement that it is impossible for it to be known of any particular sentence
that it is both true and unknowable, where the latter is argued by Cook
[
5
]
.

Proposition 6
.

\vDash U_{i}\varphi\to U_{i}U_{i}\varphi
, and thus
\vDash U_{i}\varphi\leftrightarrow U_{i}U_{i}\varphi
.

Proof.

Let
\mathcal{M}=\langle S,\{R_{i}\mid i\in\textbf{I}\},V\rangle
be a model and
s\in S
. Suppose that
\mathcal{M},s\vDash U_{i}\varphi
, to show that
\mathcal{M},s\vDash U_{i}U_{i}\varphi
. By supposition, it suffices to show that for all
\psi\in\textbf{EL}
,
\mathcal{M},s\vDash[\psi]\neg\textit{K}_{i}U_{i}\varphi
.

If not, then for some
\psi\in\textbf{EL}
we have
\mathcal{M},s\vDash\langle\psi\rangle\textit{K}_{i}U_{i}\varphi
, thus
\mathcal{M}|_{\psi},s\vDash\textit{K}_{i}U_{i}\varphi
. From this it follows that for all
t
such that
sR_{i}t
,
\mathcal{M}|_{\psi},t\vDash U_{i}\varphi
, and then
\mathcal{M}|_{\psi},t\vDash\varphi
, and for all
\chi\in\textbf{EL}
,
\mathcal{M}|_{\psi},t\vDash[\chi]\neg\textit{K}_{i}\varphi
, which implies that
\mathcal{M}|_{\psi},t\vDash\neg\textit{K}_{i}\varphi
. Since
sR_{i}s
, we obtain
\mathcal{M}|_{\psi},s\vDash\neg\textit{K}_{i}\varphi
. On the other hand, since for all
t
such that
sR_{i}t
we have
\mathcal{M}|_{\psi},t\vDash\varphi
, we infer that
\mathcal{M}|_{\psi},s\vDash\textit{K}_{i}\varphi
: a contradiction.

Based on the above analysis, we conclude that
\mathcal{M},s\vDash U_{i}U_{i}\varphi
, as desired.
∎

Intuitively, one cannot know the unknowable truths, since otherwise one would know the truths that cannot be known, which is impossible. In other words, unknowable truths are necessary limits to knowledge. This follows immediately from the result below.

Proposition 7
.

\vDash\neg\textit{K}_{i}U_{i}\varphi
.

Proof.

Suppose, for a contradiction, that there is a model
\mathcal{M}=\langle S,\{R_{i}\mid i\in\textbf{I}\},V\rangle
and
s\in S
such that
\mathcal{M},s\vDash\textit{K}_{i}U_{i}\varphi
. Then for all
t
, if
sR_{i}t
then
\mathcal{M},t\vDash U_{i}\varphi
, thus
\mathcal{M},t\vDash\varphi
and for all
\psi\in\textbf{EL}
,
\mathcal{M},t\vDash[\psi]\neg\textit{K}_{i}\varphi
. On one hand, we can obtain that
\mathcal{M},s\vDash\textit{K}_{i}\varphi
; on the other hand, by letting
t
be
s
and
\psi
be
\top
, we infer that
\mathcal{M},s\vDash\neg\textit{K}_{i}\varphi
: a contradiction.
∎

Remark 8
.

If we assume the property of transitivity, then
\neg U_{i}\textit{K}_{i}\varphi
is valid, which says that all knowledge is not an unknowable truth. The proof goes as follows.
Assume, for a contradiction, that there is a model
\mathcal{M}=\langle S,\{R_{i}\mid i\in\textbf{I}\},V\rangle
and a state
s\in S
such that
\mathcal{M},s\vDash U_{i}\textit{K}_{i}\varphi
. Then
\mathcal{M},s\vDash\textit{K}_{i}\varphi
, which implies that
\mathcal{M},s\vDash\textit{K}_{i}\textit{K}_{i}\varphi
due to transitivity. We have also that for all
\psi\in\textbf{EL}
,
\mathcal{M},s\vDash[\psi]\neg\textit{K}_{i}\textit{K}_{i}\varphi
. By letting
\psi
be
\top
, we infer that
\mathcal{M},s\vDash\neg\textit{K}_{i}\textit{K}_{i}\varphi
. Contradiction. Similarly, if we assume the property of Euclidicity, we can show that
\neg U_{i}\neg\textit{K}_{i}\varphi
is valid, which says that all non-knowledge is not an unknowable truth. Similar results apply to the case for the notion of unknown truths:
\neg{\bullet_{i}\textit{K}_{i}\varphi}
is valid over transitive frames, and
\neg{\bullet_{i}\neg\textit{K}_{i}\varphi}
is valid over Euclidean frames.

The following states that
not
all non-unknowable-truths are themselves unknowable truths.

Proposition 9
.

\nvDash\neg U_{i}\varphi\to U_{i}\neg U_{i}\varphi
.

Proof.

We show that
\nvDash\neg U_{i}\top\to U_{i}\neg U_{i}\top
. First, it should be clear that
\vDash\neg U_{i}\top
, since there is an announcement (say
\top
) such that after that the agent knows
\top
. However,
\nvDash U_{i}\neg U_{i}\top
, since validities are always knowable truths, thus not unknowable truths (we will defer the statement to Prop.
17
).
∎

As mentioned in the introduction, Moore sentences such as
\bullet_{i}p
and
U_{i}p
are unsuccessful and self-refuting. It is shown in
[
10
]
that
\bullet_{i}p
is unsuccessful and self-refuting. Here we show that
U_{i}p
is also unsuccessful and self-refuting.

Proposition 10
.

\vDash[U_{i}p]\neg U_{i}p
, and thus
\nvDash[U_{i}p]U_{i}p
.

Proof.

Suppose that
\mathcal{M},s\vDash U_{i}p
, to show that
\mathcal{M}|_{U_{i}p},s\nvDash U_{i}p
. By supposition,
\mathcal{M},s\vDash p
. Thus
\mathcal{M}|_{U_{i}p}\subseteq\mathcal{M}|_{p}
, and then
\mathcal{M}|_{U_{i}p}|_{U_{i}p}\subseteq\mathcal{M}|_{p}|_{U_{i}p}
. Since
\mathcal{M}|_{p},s\vDash\textit{K}_{i}p
, we obtain that
\mathcal{M}|_{p},s\nvDash U_{i}p
, that is,
s\notin\mathcal{M}|_{p}|_{U_{i}p}
. It then follows that
s\notin\mathcal{M}|_{U_{i}p}|_{U_{i}p}
, and therefore
\mathcal{M}|_{U_{i}p},s\nvDash U_{i}p
.
∎

One may ask if there are any differences between the logical properties of unknown truths and the logical properties of unknowable truths. The answer is positive. We have seen from
[
10
]
that
\bullet_{i}(\varphi\land\psi)\to\bullet_{i}\varphi\lor\bullet_{i}\psi
is valid. In contrast,
U_{i}(\varphi\land\psi)\to U_{i}\varphi\vee U_{i}\psi
is invalid.

.

\nvDash U_{i}(\varphi\land\psi)\to U_{i}\varphi\lor U_{i}\psi

3.2
Interactions

In what follows, we study the interactions between unknown truths and unknowable truths. First, every unknowable truth is an unknown truth, but not vise versa. This indicates that the notion of unknowable truths is stronger than that of unknown truths.

Proposition 12
.

\vDash U_{i}\varphi\to\bullet_{i}\varphi
, and
\nvDash\bullet_{i}\varphi\to U_{i}\varphi
.

Proof.

Let
\mathcal{M}=\langle S,R,V\rangle
be a model and

Axiomatization and Soundness

In this section, we will give a proof system for
LUT
and show its soundness. For this, we need a notion of
admissible form
. This notion is originated from
[
15
, pp. 55–56]
, which is called ‘necessity forms’ in
[
2
,
3
,
1
,
4
]
.

Definition 18

(Admissible forms)
.

Let
\varphi\in\textbf{LUT}
and
i\in\textbf{I}
. Admissible forms
\eta(\sharp)
are defined recursively in the following.

\begin{array}[]{ll}\eta(\sharp)::=&\sharp\mid\varphi\to\eta(\sharp)\mid{\textit{K}_{i}}\eta(\sharp)\mid[\varphi]\eta(\sharp)\\
\end{array}

Note that
\sharp
is a placeholder, not a formula. By replacing
\sharp
in an admissible form
\eta(\sharp)
with a formula
\chi
, we obtain a formula
\eta(\psi)
, defined as follows.

\begin{array}[]{lll}\sharp(\chi)&=&\chi\\
(\varphi\to\eta(\sharp))(\chi)&=&\varphi\to\eta(\chi)\\
{\textit{K}_{i}}\eta(\sharp)(\chi)&=&\textit{K}_{i}\eta(\chi)\\
([\varphi]\eta(\sharp))(\chi)&=&[\varphi]\eta(\chi)\\
\end{array}

With the previous preparation, we now axiomatize the logic of unknowable truths
LUT
.

Definition 19
.

The proof system
\mathbb{LUT}
consists of the following axioms and inference rules.

\begin{array}[]{ll}\text{PL}&\text{All instances of tautologies}\\
\text{K}&\textit{K}_{i}(\varphi\to\psi)\to(\textit{K}_{i}\varphi\to\textit{K}_{i}\psi)\\
\text{KA}&[\chi](\varphi\to\psi)\to([\chi]\varphi\to[\chi]\psi)\\
\text{T}&\textit{K}_{i}\varphi\to\varphi\\
\text{AP}&[\psi]p\leftrightarrow(\psi\to p)\\
\text{AN}&[\psi]\neg\varphi\leftrightarrow(\psi\to\neg[\psi]\varphi)\\
\text{AC}&[\psi](\varphi\land\chi)\leftrightarrow([\psi]\varphi\land[\psi]\chi)\\
\text{AK}&[\psi]\textit{K}_{i}\varphi\leftrightarrow(\psi\to\textit{K}_{i}[\psi]\varphi)\\
\text{AA}&[\psi][\chi]\varphi\leftrightarrow[\psi\land[\psi]\chi]\varphi\\
\text{AU}&U_{i}\varphi\to\varphi\land[\psi]\neg\textit{K}_{i}\varphi,\text{ where }\psi\in\textbf{EL}\\
\text{MP}&\dfrac{\varphi~~~\varphi\to\psi}{\psi}\\
\text{GEN}&\dfrac{\varphi}{\textit{K}_{i}\varphi}\\
\text{GENA}&\dfrac{\varphi}{[\chi]\varphi}\\
\text{RU}&\dfrac{\eta(\varphi\land[\psi]\neg\textit{K}_{i}\varphi)\text{ for all }\psi\in\textbf{EL}}{\eta(U_{i}\varphi)}\\
\end{array}

The novel thing is the axiom AU and inference rule RU, which together characterize the semantics of
U_{i}
in a certain way: AU says that if
\varphi
is an unknowable truth for
i
, then
\varphi
is true and after every truthfully public announcement,
\varphi
is unknown to
i
; RU roughly says that if
\varphi
is true but unknown to
i
after every truthfully public announcement, then
\varphi
is an unknowable truth for
i
.
Other axioms and inference rules are familiar from public announcement logic, cf. e.g.
[
22
]
.

We say a formula
\varphi
is a
theorem
of
\mathbb{LUT}
, or
\varphi
is
provable
in
\mathbb{LUT}
, notation:
\vdash\varphi
, if
\varphi
is either an instantiation of some axiom, or obtained from axioms with an application of some inference rule. Let
Thm
be the set of all theorems of
\mathbb{LUT}
.

Proposition 20
.

\mathbb{LUT}
is sound with respect to the class of all frames.

Proof.

We only consider the axiom AU and the inference rule RU. The validity of axiom AC is straightforward by semantics. For the soundness of RU, we need to show that if
\vDash\eta(\varphi\land[\psi]\neg\textit{K}_{i}\varphi)\text{ for all }\psi\in\textbf{EL}
, then
\vDash\eta(U_{i}\varphi)
. To do this, we show the following stronger result:

(\star)
for all
(\mathcal{M},s)
, if
\mathcal{M},s\vDash\eta(\varphi\land[\psi]\neg\textit{K}_{i}\varphi)\text{ for all }\psi\in\textbf{EL}
, then
\mathcal{M},s\vDash\eta(U_{i}\varphi)
.

The proof proceeds by induction on the structure of admissible forms. The base case and the inductive cases
\varphi\to\eta(\sharp)
and
\textit{K}_{i}\eta(\sharp)
and
[\varphi]\eta(\sharp)
follow from the semantics and the induction hypothesis.
∎

To demonstrate the completeness of

(2) for all

As mentioned before, in the proof of the truth lemma below, we need to use a formula-based complexity measure to ‘reduce’ the hard case
U_{i}\varphi
to the easy-to-fix case
\varphi\land[\psi]\neg\textit{K}_{i}\varphi
(where
\psi\in\textbf{EL}
). Now we introduce the notion of complexity, which combines the notions of size and
U
-depth.

Definition 27

(Size,
U
-depth, Complexity)
.

The
complexity
of a formula consists of two aspects: size and
U
-depth, which are defined as follows.

The
size
of a formula
\varphi
, denoted
S(\varphi)
, is a positive natural number, defined recursively as follows.

\begin{array}[]{lll}S(p)&=&1\\
S(\neg\varphi)&=&S(\varphi)+1\\
S(\varphi\land\psi)&=&S(\varphi)+S(\psi)+1\\
S(\textit{K}_{i}\varphi)&=&S(\varphi)+1\\
S([\varphi]\psi)&=&(5+S(\varphi))\cdot S(\psi)\\
S(U_{i}\varphi)&=&S(\varphi)+1\\
\end{array}

The
U
-depth
of a formula
\varphi
, denoted
d_{U}(\varphi)
, is a natural number, defined recursively as follows.

\begin{array}[]{lll}d_{U}(p)&=&0\\
d_{U}(\neg\varphi)&=&d_{U}(\varphi)\\
d_{U}(\varphi\land\psi)&=&max\{d_{U}(\varphi),d_{U}(\psi)\}\\
d_{U}(\textit{K}_{i}\varphi)&=&d_{U}(\varphi)\\
d_{U}([\varphi]\psi)&=&d_{U}(\varphi)+d_{U}(\psi)\\
d_{U}(U_{i}\varphi)&=&d_{U}(\varphi)+1\\
\end{array}

We say that
\varphi
is
less complex than

\psi
, denoted
c(\varphi)<c(\psi)
, if either
d_{U}(\varphi)<d_{U}(\psi)
, or
d_{U}(\varphi)=d_{U}(\psi)
and
S(\varphi)<S(\psi)
.

One may easily check that
S(\varphi\to\psi)=S(\varphi)+S(\psi)+3
and
d_{U}(\varphi\to\psi)=max\{d_{U}(\varphi),d_{U}(\psi)\}
. Moreover,
d_{U}(\varphi)=0
for all
\varphi\in\textbf{EL}
.

Note that our definition of
S(\varphi\land\psi)
is different from that in
[
4
,
21
,
9
]
. The reason of so defining is that, for us, it is more suitable for the intuition of the size of a conjunction. Also note that the number 5 in the definition of
S([\varphi]\psi)
is the least natural number that gives us the following properties (more precisely, (4) and (5) in Prop.
28
), in contrast to the least natural number 4 in the complexity measure for public announcement logic in
[
22
, Chap. 7]
.

Proposition 28
.

(1)

c(\psi)<c(\neg\psi)

(2)

c(\psi)<c(\psi\land\chi)
,
c(\chi)<c(\psi\land\chi)
.

(3)

c(\psi)<c(\textit{K}_{i}\psi)

(4)

c(\psi\to p)<c([\psi]p)

(5)

c(\psi\to\neg[\psi]\chi)<c([\psi]\neg\chi)

(6)

c([\psi]\chi_{1})<c([\psi](\chi_{1}\land\chi_{2}))
,
c([\psi]\chi_{2})<c([\psi](\chi_{1}\land\chi_{2}))

(7)

c(\psi\to{\textit{K}_{i}[\psi]\chi})<c({[\psi]\textit{K}_{i}}\chi)

(8)

c([\psi\land[\psi]\chi]\delta)<c([\psi][\chi]\delta)

(9)

c([\psi]\chi)<c([\psi]U_{i}\chi)
,
c([\psi]{[\delta]\neg\textit{K}_{i}}\chi)<c([\psi]U_{i}\chi)
, where
\delta\in\textbf{EL}
.

(10)

c(\psi)<c(U_{i}\psi)
,
c({[\chi]\neg\textit{K}_{i}}\psi)<c(U_{i}\psi)
, where
\chi\in\textbf{EL}
.

Proof.

We show some of them as examples.

(4) One may easily verify that
d_{U}(\psi\to p)=d_{U}(\psi)=d_{U}([\psi]p)
. Moreover,
S(\psi\to p)=S(\psi)+S(p)+3=S(\psi)+4<5+S(\psi)=S([\psi]p)
. Thus
c(\psi\to p)<c([\psi]p)
.

(5) One may easily verify that
d_{U}(\psi\to\neg[\psi]\chi)=d_{U}(\psi)+d_{U}(\chi)=d_{U}([\psi]\neg\chi)
. Moreover,
S(\psi\to\neg[\psi]\chi)=S(\psi)+5\cdot S(\chi)+S(\psi)\cdot S(\chi)+4<S(\psi)+5\cdot S(\chi)+S(\psi)\cdot S(\chi)+5=S([\psi]\neg\chi)
. Therefore,
c(\psi\to\neg[\psi]\chi)<c([\psi]\neg\chi)
.

(7) One may easily verify that
d_{U}(\psi\to{\textit{K}_{i}[\psi]}\chi)=d_{U}(\psi)+d_{U}(\chi)=d_{U}({[\psi]\textit{K}_{i}}\chi)
. Moreover,
S(\psi\to{\textit{K}_{i}[\psi]}\chi)=S(\psi)+5\cdot S(\chi)+S(\psi)\cdot S(\chi)+4<S(\psi)+5\cdot S(\chi)+S(\psi)\cdot S(\chi)+5=S({[\psi]\textit{K}_{i}}\chi)
. Therefore,
c(\psi\to{\textit{K}_{i}[\psi]}\chi)<c({[\psi]\textit{K}_{i}}\chi)
.

(8) One may easily verify that
d_{U}([\psi\land[\psi]\chi]\delta)=d_{U}(\psi)+d_{U}(\chi)+d_{U}(\delta)=d_{U}([\psi][\chi]\delta)
. Moreover,
S([\psi\land[\psi]\chi]\delta)=6\cdot S(\delta)+S(\psi)\cdot S(\delta)+5\cdot S(\chi)\cdot S(\delta)+S(\psi)\cdot S(\chi)\cdot S(\delta)
, and
S([\psi][\chi]\delta)=25\cdot S(\delta)+5\cdot S(\psi)\cdot S(\delta)+5\cdot S(\chi)\cdot S(\delta)+S(\psi)\cdot S(\chi)\cdot S(\delta)
, thus
S([\psi\land[\psi]\chi]\delta)<S([\psi][\chi]\delta)
. Therefore,
c([\psi\land[\psi]\chi]\delta)<c([\psi][\chi]\delta)
.

(10) Since
d_{U}(\psi)<d_{U}(\psi)+1=d_{U}(U_{i}\psi)
, we obtain
c(\psi)<c(U_{i}\psi)
. Also, one may verify that
d_{U}({[\chi]\neg\textit{K}_{i}}\psi)=d_{U}(\psi)<d_{U}(\psi)+1=d_{U}(U_{i}\psi)
, thus
c({[\chi]\neg\textit{K}_{i}}\psi)<c(U_{i}\psi)
.
∎

Now we are ready to show the truth lemma.

Lemma 29
.

For all
s\in S^{c}
, for all
\varphi\in\textbf{LUT}
, we have

\mathcal{M}^{c},s\vDash\varphi\text{ iff }\varphi\in s.

Proof.

By induction on the complexity of
\varphi
. Recall that the notion of complexity is given in Def.
27
.

•

\varphi=p\in\textbf{P}
. Straightforward by the definition of
V^{c}
.

•

\varphi=\neg\psi
. By the item (1) of Prop.
28
,
c(\psi)<c(\varphi)
. Then by induction hypothesis, we have that
\mathcal{M}^{c},s\vDash\psi
iff
\psi\in s
. Thus
\mathcal{M}^{c},s\vDash\varphi
iff
\mathcal{M}^{c},s\nvDash\psi
iff
\psi\notin s
iff
\varphi\in s
.

•

\varphi=\psi\land\chi
. By the item (2) of Prop.
28
,
c(\psi),c(\chi)<c(\varphi)
. Then by induction hypothesis,
\mathcal{M}^{c},s\vDash\psi
iff
\psi\in s
, and
\mathcal{M}^{c},s\vDash\chi
iff
\chi\in s
. Therefore,
\mathcal{M}^{c},s\vDash\varphi
iff (
\mathcal{M}^{c},s\vDash\psi
and
\mathcal{M}^{c},s\vDash\chi
) iff (
\psi\in s
and
\chi\in s
) iff
\varphi\in s
.

\varphi=\textit{K}_{i}\psi

28
,
c(\psi)<c(\varphi)
. Then we have the following equivalences.

\begin{array}[]{lll}\mathcal{M}^{c},s\vDash\textit{K}_{i}\psi&\iff&\text{for all }t\in S^{c},\text{ if }sR^{c}_{i}t,\text{ then }\mathcal{M}^{c},t\vDash\psi\\
&\iff&\text{for all }t\in S^{c},\text{ if }sR^{c}_{i}t,\text{ then }\psi\in t\\
&\iff&\textit{K}_{i}\psi\in s,\\
\end{array}

where the penultimate equivalence holds by induction hypothesis, whereas the last one has been shown in Prop.
26
.

•

\varphi=[\psi]p

\begin{array}[]{lll}\mathcal{M}^{c},s\vDash[\psi]p&\iff&\mathcal{M}^{c},s\vDash\psi\to p\\

\varphi=[\psi](\chi_{1}\land\chi_{2})

\begin{array}[]{lll}\mathcal{M}^{c},s\vDash[\psi](\chi_{1}\land\chi_{2})&\iff&\mathcal{M}^{c},s\vDash[\psi]\chi_{1}\text{ and }\mathcal{M}^{c},s\vDash[\psi]\chi_{2}\\

It is now a standard exercise to show the following result.

Theorem 30
.

\mathbb{LUT}
is sound and complete with respect to the class of all frames. That is, if
\vDash\varphi
, then
\vdash\varphi
.

With the completeness in hand, we can give a syntactic proof for Fitch’s paradox of knowability, which is much simpler than those in the literature, e.g. in

19
]
.

Proposition 31
.

If
\vdash\neg U_{i}\varphi
for all
\varphi
, then
\vdash\neg{\bullet_{i}\varphi}
for all
\varphi
.

Proof.

Suppose that
\vdash\neg U_{i}\varphi
for all
\varphi
, then
\vdash\neg U_{i}{\bullet_{i}\varphi}
. Then by Prop.
13
and Thm.
30
,
\vdash\bullet_{i}\varphi\to U_{i}{\bullet_{i}\varphi}
, and therefore
\vdash\neg{\bullet_{i}\varphi}
for all
\varphi
.
∎

6
Conclusion and Discussion

In this paper, we proposed a logic of unknowable truths, investigated the logical properties of unknown truths and the logical properties of unknowable truths, which includes the similarities and relationship between the two notions, and finally axiomatized the logic of unknowable truths.

So far we have seen that the semantics of unknowable truths depends on that of propositional knowledge. However, we have mainly focused on the relationship between unknowable truths and unknown truths, instead of between unknowable truths and propositional knowledge, and the notion of unknown truths is weaker than that of propositional knowledge. So there seems to be some asymmetry between the semantics of unknowable truths and our concerns.
Then a natural question is: is there any other semantics for unknowable truths which relates the notion to that of unknown truths more directly/properly? Our answer is positive. We will introduce this new semantics in the future work.

For another future work, a natural extension would be to add propositional quantifiers. This can increase the expressive power of the current logic. For instance, we can express Fitch’s paradox of knowability in the new language as follows:
\forall p\neg U_{i}p\to\forall p\neg{\bullet_{i}p}
, or equivalently,
\exists p{\bullet_{i}p}\to\exists pU_{i}p
.

On Numbers of Semistable Representations of Quivers over Finite Fields
Jiuzhao Hua

Abstract
In this paper we prove an identity in terms of generating functions which enables us to calculate the numbers of isomorphism classes of absolutely indecomposable semistable representations of quivers over finite fields.



1
Introduction

We are interested in counting rational points of quiver moduli spaces. For the origin and fundamental properties of quiver moduli, their connections with
Algebraic Geometry, Quantum Groups and Kac Moody algebras, we refer the reader to King
[
4
]
and Reineke

[
6
]
[
8
]
. We adopt all terminologies and notations from Deng & Hua
[
1
]
.

Given a positive integer
n
and
an
n\times n
matrix
C=[a_{ij}]
where
a_{ij}
’s are non-negative integers, let
\Gamma
be the quiver associated to
C
. Thus
\Gamma
has
n
vertices
\{1,2,\cdots,n\}

and there are
a_{ij}
arrows from
i
to
j
. Let
\mathbb{F}_{q}
be a finite field with
q
elements where
q
is a prime power and

\text{Rep}(\alpha,\mathbb{F}_{q})
be the set of all representations of
\Gamma
over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
with dimension
\alpha
, and

R(\alpha,q)=|\text{Rep}(\alpha,\mathbb{F}_{q})|
. Thus

R(\alpha,q)=q^{\sum_{1\leq i,j\leq n}a_{ij}\alpha_{i}\alpha_{j}}\text{ for }\alpha=(\alpha_{1},\cdots,\alpha_{n})\in\mathbb{N}^{n}.

Recall from Reineke
[
6
]
, each stability function

\theta:\mathbb{Z}^{n}\to\mathbb{Z}
gives rise to a slope function:

\mu(\alpha):=\frac{\theta(\alpha)}{ht(\alpha)},\text{ where }ht(\alpha)=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\alpha_{i}\text{ for }\alpha=(\alpha_{1},\cdots,\alpha_{n})\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}\backslash\{0\}.

The slope of a representation
M
is defined as
\mu(M):=\mu(\dim M)
.
A representation
M
is called semistable (resp. stable) if for any non-zero proper subrepresentation
U
of
M
, we have
\mu(U)\leq\mu(M)
(resp.
\mu(U)<\mu(M)
).

For a fixed rational number
\mu\in\mathbb{Q}
, let
\Delta_{\mu}=\{\alpha\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}:\theta(\alpha)=\mu\cdot ht(\alpha)\}
and

\Delta_{\mu}^{+}=\Delta_{\mu}\cap\mathbb{N}^{n}
. The set of all semistable representations of
\Gamma
over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
with slope
\mu
forms an abelian category.
Let
\textup{Rep}_{\mu}^{ss}(\alpha,\mathbb{F}_{q})
be the set of all semistable representations of
\Gamma
over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
with dimension
\alpha
and slope
\mu
,
and
R_{\mu}^{ss}(\alpha,q)=|\textup{Rep}_{\mu}^{ss}(\alpha,\mathbb{F}_{q})|
. Then

R_{\mu}^{ss}(\alpha,q)=0
if
\alpha\notin\Delta_{\mu}^{+}
.
Recall that the Euler form on
\Gamma
is defined by:

\langle\alpha,\beta\rangle:=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\alpha_{i}\beta_{i}-\sum_{i,j=1}^{n}a_{ij}\alpha_{i}\beta_{j}\text{ for }\alpha,\beta\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}.

Then, for
\alpha\in\Delta_{\mu}^{+}
,
R_{\mu}^{ss}(\alpha,q)
is given by the following formula which is due to Reineke
[
6
]
:

\frac{R_{\mu}^{ss}(\alpha,q)}{|\text{GL}(\alpha,\mathbb{F}_{q})|}=\sum_{\alpha^{*}}(-1)^{s-1}q^{-\sum_{i<j}\langle\alpha^{j},\alpha^{i}\rangle}\prod_{i=1}^{s}\frac{R(\alpha^{i},q)}{|\text{GL}(\alpha^{i},\mathbb{F}_{q})|},

(1)

where the sum runs over all tuples
\alpha^{*}=(\alpha^{1},\cdots,\alpha^{s})
of non-zero dimension vectors such that
\alpha=\sum_{i=1}^{s}\alpha_{i}
and

\mu(\sum_{j=1}^{i}\alpha^{j})>\mu(\alpha)
for
i=1,\cdots,s-1
. Since
|\text{GL}(n,\mathbb{F}_{q})|=\prod_{i=0}^{n-1}(q^{n}-q^{i})
,

R_{\mu}^{ss}(\alpha,q)
is explicitly computable.

Let

A_{\mu}^{ss}(\alpha,q)
(resp.
A_{\mu}^{s}(\alpha,q)
) be the number of isomorphism classes of semistable (resp. stable) representations
of
\Gamma
over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
with dimension
\alpha
and slope
\mu
. Thus
A_{\mu}^{ss}(\alpha,q)=A_{\mu}^{s}(\alpha,q)=0
if

\alpha\notin\Delta_{\mu}^{+}
.
It is known by Reineke
[
7
]
that
A_{\mu}^{s}(\alpha,q)
is of polynomial count, i.e., there exists a polynomial
f(x)\in\mathbb{Q}[x]
such that

A_{\mu}^{s}(\alpha,q^{r})=f(q^{r})
for all
r\in\mathbb{N}
. The same is true for
A_{\mu}^{ss}(\alpha,q)
. Reineke
[
7
]
further asserts that

A_{\mu}^{s}(\alpha,q)\in\mathbb{Z}[q]
.

The ring of formal power series
R=\mathbb{Q}(q)[[X_{1},\cdots,X_{n}]]
is equipped with a structure of
\lambda
-ring in terms of Adams operator:

\psi_{k}(f(q,X_{1},\cdots,X_{n}))=f(q^{k},X_{1}^{k},\cdots,X_{n}^{k}).

Let
\mathfrak{m}
be the unique maximal ideal of the ring
R
. We define the map
\text{Exp}:\mathfrak{m}\to 1+\mathfrak{m}
as follows:

\text{Exp}(f)=\exp\Big(\sum_{k\geq 1}\frac{1}{k}\psi_{k}(f)\Big).

This map has an inverse
\text{Log}:1+\mathfrak{m}\to\mathfrak{m}
(see Getzler
[
2
]
) given by:

\text{Log}(f)=\sum_{k\geq 1}\frac{\mu(k)}{k}\psi_{k}(\log(f)),

where
\mu
is the classical Möbius function.

A_{\mu}^{s}(\alpha,q)
can be calculated by the following identity due to Mozgovoy and Reineke
[
5
]
:

\Big(\sum_{\alpha\in\Delta_{\mu}^{+}}\frac{R_{\mu}^{ss}(\alpha,q)}{|\text{GL}(\alpha,\mathbb{F}_{q})|}X^{\alpha}\Big)\circ\text{Exp}\Big(\frac{1}{1-q}\sum_{\alpha\in\Delta_{\mu}^{+}\backslash\{0\}}\!A_{\mu}^{s}(\alpha,q)X^{\alpha}\Big)=1,

(2)

in the algebra
\mathbb{Q}(q)^{tw}[[X_{1},\cdots,X_{n}]]
.

The aim of this paper is to derive a formula for
A_{\mu}^{ss}(\alpha,q)
in the same spirit as Hua
[
3
]
and Deng & Hua
[
1
]
.
In Section 2, we prove that any representation with a nilpotent endomorphism has a special filtration such that the sum of its composition factors is isomorphic to
the core of the representation. It follows that the representation is semistable if and only if its core is semistable. We then derive a formula for the number of
representations fixed by a conjugacy class of
\textup{GL}(\alpha,\mathbb{F}_{q})
. The main result of this paper Theorem
3.1
is stated in Section 3, followed by
a conjectural statement regarding
A_{\mu}^{ss}(\alpha,q)
.

2
Filtrations induced by nilpotent endomorphisms

For a partition
\lambda=(\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2},\cdots,\lambda_{r})\in\mathcal{P}
, where
\lambda_{1}\geq\lambda_{2}\geq\cdots\geq\lambda_{r}
, let

J_{\lambda}=\left[\begin{array}[]{cccc}J_{\lambda_{1}}&0&\dots&0\\
0&\!\!\!\!J_{\lambda_{2}}&\dots&0\\
\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
0&0&\dots&\!\!\!\!J_{\lambda_{r}}\end{array}\right],\text{ where }J_{\lambda_{i}}=\left[\begin{array}[]{ccccc}0&1&0&\dots&0\\
0&0&1&\dots&0\\
\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
0&0&0&\dots&1\\
0&0&0&\dots&0\end{array}\right]_{\lambda_{i}\times\lambda_{i}}.

Thus any nilpotent matrix over a field is conjugate to
J_{\lambda}
for some
\lambda\in\mathcal{P}
.
The type-U matrices and their cores are introduced in Deng & Hua
[
1
]
. Any representation
\sigma
with a nilpotent endomorphism has a core, denoted by
\sigma_{0}
.

Theorem 2.1

(Turnbull & Aitken
[
9
]
)
.

Let
\lambda=(\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2},\dots,\lambda_{s})
and
\mu=(\mu_{1},\mu_{2},\dots,\mu_{t})
be two partitions, then any matrix
U
over a field
k
that satisfies

J_{\lambda}U=UJ_{\mu}
can be written as an
s\times t
block matrix in the following form:

\left[\begin{array}[]{cccc}U_{11}&U_{12}&\dots&U_{1t}\\
U_{21}&U_{22}&\dots&U_{2t}\\
\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
U_{s1}&U_{s2}&\dots&U_{st}\end{array}\right],

where each submatrix
U_{ij}
is a type-U matrix over
k
of order
\lambda_{i}\times\mu_{j}
for all
(i,j)
where
1\leq i\leq s
and
1\leq j\leq t
.

Theorem 2.2
.

Given
\tau=(\tau_{1},\cdots,\tau_{n})\in\mathcal{P}^{n}
, let
\alpha=(|\tau_{1}|,\cdots,|\tau_{n}|)\in\mathbb{N}^{n}
and
g=(I+J_{\tau_{1}},\cdots,I+J_{\tau_{n}})\in\textup{GL}(\alpha,\mathbb{F}_{q})
, and

X_{g}=\left\{\sigma\in\textup{Rep}(\alpha,\mathbb{F}_{q}):g\cdot\sigma=\sigma\right\}
the set of representations fixed by
g
.
For any
\sigma\in X_{g}
, let
M
be the module of the path algebra
\mathbb{F}_{q}\Gamma
defined by
\sigma
, then

M
admits a filtration of submodules in the following form:

\begin{array}[]{rrrrrrrrrr}0=M_{0,0}\subseteq M_{1,r}\subseteq&M_{1,r-1}\subseteq&\!\!\!\!\cdots\subseteq&\!\!\!\!M_{1,i}\subseteq&\!\!\!\!\cdots\subseteq&\!\!\!\!M_{1,2}\subseteq&\!\!\!\!M_{1,1}\subseteq\\

\vdots&\vdots&\\
M_{r-1,r}\subseteq&\!\!\!\!M_{r-1,r-1}\subseteq\\
M_{r,r}=&M\\
\end{array}

where

is the maximal part in the partitions
\tau_{1},\cdots,\tau_{n}
,
such that

1.

M_{i,s}/M_{i,s+1}\cong M_{1,s}/M_{1,s+1}
and
\dim M_{1,s}/M_{1,s+1}=d_{\tau}^{\,\underline{s}}
for

and
i\leq s\leq r-1
,
where
d_{\tau}^{\,\underline{s}}=(m_{\tau_{1}}^{\underline{s}},\cdots,m_{\tau_{n}}^{\underline{s}})
is the multiplicity vector of order

induced by
\tau
as defined in Deng & Hua
[
1
]
.

2.

M_{i,r}/M_{i-1,i-1}\cong M_{1,r}
and
\dim M_{1,r}=d_{\tau}^{\,\underline{r}}
for
1\leq i\leq r
.

3.

\bigoplus_{s=1}^{r}(M_{1,s}/M_{1,s+1})^{s}\cong\sigma_{0}
, where
\sigma_{0}
is the core of
\sigma
, assuming that
M_{1,r+1}=0
.

First note that
\sigma\in X_{g}
if and only if
(J_{\tau_{1}},\cdots,J_{\tau_{n}})
is an endomorphism of
\sigma

Lemma 2.1
.

Let

be a short exact sequence of non-zero representations of
\Gamma
over a field

.
Then we have

\mu(Y)\leq\mu(Z)
if and only if
\mu(X)\leq\mu(Z)

2.

\mu(X)<\mu(Y)
if and only if
\mu(Y)<\mu(Z)
if and only if
\mu(X)<\mu(Z)

Lemma 2.2
.

Let

be a short exact sequence of non-zero representations of
\Gamma
over a field
k

. Then
Y
is semistable if and only if

and

are semistable.

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
0\to X\to Y\to Z\to 0
be a short exact sequence of non-zero representations of
\Gamma
over a field
k
.
If
\mu(X)\leq\mu(Y)
and
\mu(Z)\leq\mu(Y)
, then
\mu(X)=\mu(Y)=\mu(Z)
.

Proof.

\mu(X)\leq\mu(Y)
implies that
\mu(Y)\leq\mu(Z)

Corollary 2.1
.

Let
\tau,\sigma,M
as in Theorem
2.2
. Then

\mu

Proof.

The second part is trivial because

M_{1,s}/M_{1,s+1}
is zero or semistable with slope
\mu
for all

is semistable with slope
\mu
because the category of semistable representations with

\mu
is closed under extension.

Now suppose that

is semistable and
\mu(M)=\mu
. We have the following short exact sequence:

0\to M_{r-1,r-1}\to M\to M_{1,r}\to 0.

and hence

3
Generating functions for absolutely indecomposables

We state the main result of this paper and the necessary definitions, the proofs are analogous to Hua
[
3
]
and Deng & Hua
[
1
]
, and are omitted here.

Definition 3.1
.

For
\tau=(\tau_{1},\cdots,\tau_{n})\in\mathcal{P}^{n}
, let
X^{|\tau|}=X_{1}^{|\tau_{1}|}\cdots X_{n}^{|\tau_{n}|}
.
We define a formal power series in
\mathbb{Q}(q)[[X_{1},\cdots,X_{n}]]
as follows:

P(q,X_{1},\cdots,X_{n})=\sum_{\tau\in\mathcal{P}^{n}}\!\frac{q^{\sum_{1\leq i,j\leq n}\!a_{ij}(|\tau_{i},\tau_{j}|)}\!\prod_{s\geq 1}\!R_{\mu}^{ss}(d_{\tau}^{\,\underline{s}},q)}{\prod_{1\leq i\leq n}\!q^{\langle\tau_{i},\tau_{i}\rangle}b_{\tau_{i}}\!(q^{-1})}X^{|\tau|},

(3)

where the “inner product”
\langle\lambda,\mu\rangle
and the function
b_{\lambda}(q)
for
\lambda,\mu\in\mathcal{P}
are defined in Deng & Hua
[
1
]
.
Note that
((0),\cdots,(0))\in\mathcal{P}^{n}
gives rise to a term equal to
1
in the sum.

An equivalent form of
P(q,X_{1},\cdots,X_{n})
is as follows:

P(q,X_{1},\cdots,X_{n})=1+\sum_{\alpha^{*}}\!\Big(\prod_{s\geq 1}\frac{q^{\langle\alpha^{s},\alpha^{s}\rangle}}{q^{\langle\beta^{s},\beta^{s}\rangle}}\frac{R_{\mu}^{ss}(\alpha^{s},q)}{|\text{GL}(\alpha^{s},q)|}X^{s\alpha^{s}}\Big),

(4)

where the sum runs over all tuples of dimension vectors
\alpha^{*}=(\alpha^{1},\alpha^{2},\cdots,\alpha^{r})
such that
r\geq 1
,
\alpha^{i}\in\mathbb{N}^{n}
for
1\leq i\leq r
and
\alpha^{r}\neq 0
,
and
\beta^{s}=\sum_{i\geq s}\alpha^{i}
for
1\leq s\leq r
, and
\langle-,-\rangle
is the Euler form associated to quiver
\Gamma
.

Definition 3.2
.

We define rational functions
H(\alpha,q)\in\mathbb{Q}(q)
for all
\alpha\in\mathbb{N}^{n}\backslash\{0\}
as follows:

\log\left(P(q,X_{1},\cdots,X_{n})\right)=\sum_{\alpha\in\mathbb{N}^{n}\backslash\{0\}}\!H(\alpha,q)X^{\alpha},

where
\log
is the formal logarithm, i.e.,
\log(1+x)=\sum_{i\geq 1}(-1)^{i-1}x^{i}/i
.

Theorem 3.1
.

The following identity holds for all
\alpha\in\mathbb{N}^{n}\backslash\{0\}
:

A_{\mu}^{ss}(\alpha,q)=(q-1)\sum_{d\,|\,\bar{\alpha}}\frac{\mu(d)}{d}H\Big(\frac{\alpha}{d},q^{d}\Big),

where the sum runs over all divisors of
\bar{\alpha}:=\gcd(\alpha_{1},\cdots,\alpha_{n})
.

Theorem
3.1
implies that
A_{\mu}^{ss}(\alpha,q)
is a rational function in
q
. Since
A_{\mu}^{ss}(\alpha,q)
takes integer value for any prime power
q
,
it must be a polynomial function in
q
, i.e.,
A_{\mu}^{ss}(\alpha,q)\in\mathbb{Q}[q]
.
Theorem
3.1
is equivalent to the following identity in the formal power series ring
\mathbb{Q}(q)[[X_{1},\cdots,X_{n}]]
:

P(q,X_{1},\cdots,X_{n})=\,\,\text{Exp}\Big(\frac{1}{q-1}\sum_{\alpha\in\Delta_{\mu}^{+}\backslash\{0\}}\!A_{\mu}^{ss}(\alpha,q)X^{\alpha}\Big).

(5)

Reineke
[
7
]
conjectured that the polynomial
A_{\mu}^{s}(\alpha,q)
possesses some kind of positivity property,
A_{\mu}^{s}(\alpha,q)\in\mathbb{N}[q-1]

to be precise. This conjecture is still open. We conjecture that the polynomial
A_{\mu}^{ss}(\alpha,q)
also possesses a positivity property in the sense that
A_{\mu}^{ss}(\alpha,q)\in\mathbb{N}[q]
.
It is conceivable that the constant term of
A_{\mu}^{ss}(\alpha,q)
carries some interesting geometric meanings similar to the Kac conjecture.

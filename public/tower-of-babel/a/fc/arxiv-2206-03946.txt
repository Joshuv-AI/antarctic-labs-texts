Sampling at twice the Nyquist rate in two frequency bins guarantees uniqueness in Gabor phase retrieval
Matthias Wellershoff

Abstract
We show that bandlimited signals can be uniquely recovered (up to a constant global phase factor) from Gabor transform magnitudes sampled at twice the Nyquist rate in two frequency bins.

Introduction

We consider the recovery of square-integrable signals
f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
from the magnitude of their
Gabor transforms

\mathcal{G}f(x,\omega):=\sqrt[4]{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}}f(t)\mathrm{e}^{-\pi(t-x)^{2}}\mathrm{e}^{-2\pi\mathrm{i}t\omega},\qquad(x,\omega)\in\mathbb{R}^{2},

(1)

also called the
Gabor phase retrieval problem
. Specifically, we are interested in questions related to the reconstruction of
f
from
\left\lvert\mathcal{G}f\right\rvert
measured on lattices
1
1

1

A
lattice

\Lambda\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
is a discrete subset of the time-frequency plane that can be written as
\Lambda=L\mathbb{R}^{k}
, where
L\in\mathbb{R}^{2\times k}
is a matrix with linearly independent columns and
k\in\{1,2\}
.

\Lambda\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
, which we refer to as
sampled Gabor phase retrieval
.

The first uniqueness result for sampled Gabor phase retrieval was presented in
alaifari2021uniqueness
where it is shown that real-valued, bandlimited, square-integrable signals
f
with bandwidth
B>0
can be recovered (up to a constant global sign factor) from
\left\lvert\mathcal{G}f\right\rvert
sampled on
\tfrac{1}{4B}\mathbb{Z}\times\{0\}
. Just two months later, the paper
grohs2023injectivity
appeared in which it is revealed that the real-valuedness assumption from
alaifari2021uniqueness
can be dropped: specifically, the bandlimited square-integrable signals
f
whose Fourier transform is in
L^{4}
can be recovered (up to a constant global phase factor) from
\left\lvert\mathcal{G}f\right\rvert
sampled on
\tfrac{1}{4B}\mathbb{Z}\times\mathbb{Z}
. Another two months later, the paper
alaifari2021phase
appeared, showing that the bandlimitedness assumption cannot be dropped. This is achieved by constructing two square-integrable functions that do not agree (up to a constant global phase factor) but whose Gabor transform magnitudes agree on
\Lambda
, where
\Lambda\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
is a general lattice in the time-frequency plane.

Finally, we mention the recent preprint
wellershoff2021injectivity
in which results from
grohs2023injectivity
are extended to show that all bandlimited square-integrable signals
f
can be recovered (up to a constant global phase factor) from
\left\lvert\mathcal{G}f\right\rvert
sampled on
\tfrac{1}{4B}\mathbb{Z}\times\mathbb{N}
. In this paper, we further strengthen this result and prove that in fact bandlimited square-integrable signals
f
, with bandwidth
B>0
, can be recovered (up to a constant global phase factor) from
\left\lvert\mathcal{G}f\right\rvert
sampled on
\tfrac{1}{4B}\mathbb{Z}\times\{\omega_{0},\omega_{1}\}
, where
\omega_{0}\neq\omega_{1}
. As
\tfrac{1}{4B}
is exactly twice the Nyquist rate, we therefore show that sampling at twice the Nyquist rate in two frequency bins guarantees uniqueness in Gabor phase retrieval as advertised in the title. We point out that a similar result was already known for the Cauchy wavelet transform
alaifari2020phase
: more precisely, sampling at twice the Nyquist rate at two scales guarantees uniqueness in Cauchy wavelet phase retrieval.

Remark 1
.

The original motivation for this paper stems from a resemblance of the result in
alaifari2021uniqueness
on Gabor sign retrieval with the work in
balan2006signal
on finite-dimensional sign retrieval: in the prior, it is shown that sampling at twice the Nyquist rate in a single frequency bin guarantees uniqueness in Gabor sign retrieval while, in the latter, it is shown that
2n-1
generic measurement vectors are necessary and sufficient for uniqueness in finite-dimensional sign retrieval. As it is also known that on the order of
4n
generic measurement vectors are necessary and sufficient for uniqueness in finite-dimensional phase retrieval
balan2006signal
;
heinosaari2013quantum
, it seems natural to ask whether sampling at four times the Nyquist rate in one frequency bin or at twice the Nyquist rate in two frequency bins would guarantee uniqueness in Gabor phase retrieval. The former is clearly untrue as can be seen from considering
f,g\in\mathrm{PW}_{B}^{2}
real-valued and

\left\lvert\mathcal{G}(f+\mathrm{i}g)\right\rvert=\left\lvert\mathcal{G}(f-\mathrm{i}g)\right\rvert\mbox{ on }\mathbb{R}\times\{0\}.

The latter is shown to be true in this paper.

Notation

We denote the normalised Gaussian by
\phi(t)=\sqrt[4]{2}\exp(-\pi t^{2})
, where
t\in\mathbb{R}
. For
-\infty<a<b<\infty
and
f\in L^{p}(\mathbb{R})
, with
p\in[1,\infty)
, we write
\operatorname{supp}f\subset[a,b]
if
f(t)=0
, for a.e.
t\not\in[a,b]
. Moreover, we define the families of
translation
and
modulation
operators
(\operatorname{T}_{x})_{x\in\mathbb{R}}:L^{p}(\mathbb{R})\to L^{p}(\mathbb{R})
and
(\operatorname{M}_{\omega})_{\omega\in\mathbb{R}}:L^{p}(\mathbb{R})\to L^{p}(\mathbb{R})
by

\operatorname{T}_{x}f(t):=f(t-x),\qquad\operatorname{M}_{\omega}f(t):=f(t)\mathrm{e}^{2\pi\mathrm{i}t\omega}.

We furthermore use the convention

\mathcal{F}f(\xi)=\int_{\mathbb{R}}f(t)\mathrm{e}^{-2\pi\mathrm{i}t\xi}\,\mathrm{d}t,\qquad\xi\in\mathbb{R},

for the Fourier transform of
f\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R})\cup L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
and note that the Fourier transform of the normalised Gaussian is the normalised Gaussian itself, i.e.
\mathcal{F}\phi=\phi
. Additionally, as is usual in the phase retrieval literature, we will introduce an equivalence relation on the set of complex-valued functions via

f\sim g:\iff\exists\alpha\in\mathbb{R}:f=\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\alpha}g

and say that
f
and
g

agree up to global phase
if
f\sim g
. The space of polynomials with complex argument and complex coefficients is denoted by
\mathbb{C}[z]
. Similarly, the subspace of degree
n\in\mathbb{N}
polynomials in
\mathbb{C}[z]
is denoted by
\mathbb{C}_{n}[z]
. Finally, for an entire function
F:\mathbb{C}\to\mathbb{C}
, we denote its zero set by
Z(F)\subset\mathbb{C}
and define a function
m_{F}:\mathbb{C}\to\mathbb{N}_{0}
which assigns the multiplicity of
z
as a zero of
F
to every
z\in\mathbb{C}
. Note that we use the convention
m_{F}(z)=0
, for
z\not\in Z(F)
.

Definitions and basic notions

We will work with the
Paley–Wiener spaces
of bandlimited functions

\mathrm{PW}_{B}^{p}:=\{f\in L^{p}(\mathbb{R})\,;\,\operatorname{supp}(\mathcal{F}f)\subset[-B,B]\},\qquad p\in[1,\infty],

where
B>0
. With this definition, it is well known that
\mathrm{PW}_{B}^{1}\subset\mathrm{PW}_{B}^{2}
. It turns out to be useful to consider the
Bargmann transform
of square-integrable signals
f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
given by

\mathcal{B}f(z):=\sqrt[4]{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}}f(t)\mathrm{e}^{2\pi tz-\pi t^{2}-\tfrac{\pi}{2}z^{2}}\,\mathrm{d}t,\qquad z\in\mathbb{C}.

One of the two main reasons for this is that the Bargmann transform and the Gabor transform (as defined in equation (
1
)) are related via the formula
(
grochenig2001foundations
, Proposition 3.4.1 on p. 54)

\mathcal{G}f(x,-\omega)=\mathrm{e}^{\pi\mathrm{i}x\omega}\mathcal{B}f(x+\mathrm{i}\omega)\mathrm{e}^{-\tfrac{\pi}{2}\left(x^{2}+\omega^{2}\right)},\qquad(x,\omega)\in\mathbb{R}^{2}.

The other main reason is that the Bargmann transform of a square-integrable signal is an entire function of finite order. More precisely, we define the
order
of an entire function
F:\mathbb{C}\to\mathbb{C}
to be

\rho:=\limsup_{r\to\infty}\frac{\log(\log(\sup_{\left\lvert z\right\rvert=r}\left\lvert F(z)\right\rvert))}{\log r}

and say that
F
is of
finite order
if
\rho<\infty
. For
F=\mathcal{B}f
, with
f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
, one can use
(
grochenig2001foundations
, Theorem 3.4.2 on p. 54)
to show that
\rho\leq 2
: we say that
\mathcal{B}f
is of
second-order
.

Preliminaries

Our goal is to recover a bandlimited function
f
with bandwidth
B
from the magnitudes of its Gabor transform
\left\lvert\mathcal{G}f\right\rvert
sampled on the set
\tfrac{1}{4B}\mathbb{Z}\times\{\omega_{0},\omega_{1}\}
, where
\omega_{0}\neq\omega_{1}
. In order to do this, we follow a three-step procedure.

1.

We note that
\left\lvert\mathcal{G}f(\cdot,\omega)\right\rvert^{2}
is bandlimited, for all
\omega\in\mathbb{R}
, and use the Nyquist–Shannon sampling theorem to recover
\left\lvert\mathcal{G}f\right\rvert^{2}
on
\mathbb{R}\times\{\omega_{0},\omega_{1}\}
.

2.

Relating this to the Bargmann transform of
f
shows that it suffices to analyse the recovery of a second-order entire function from magnitude measurements on two parallel lines. In this direction, we show that a second-order entire function is either uniquely determined (up to global phase) by its magnitude on two parallel lines or it has infinitely many evenly spaced zeroes.

3.

Finally, we make use of the bandlimitedness of
f
again and show that the Bargmann transform of
f
can only have infinitely many evenly spaced zeroes if
f=0
.

Let us start with the realisation of item 1. We note that the following lemma already follows from the considerations in
wellershoff2021injectivity
. A considerably simpler proof based on a different convention for the Paley–Wiener spaces is given here.

Lemma 1
.

Let
B>0
,
\omega\in\mathbb{R}
and
f\in\mathrm{PW}_{B}^{2}
. Then,
x\mapsto\left\lvert\mathcal{G}f(x,\omega)\right\rvert^{2}\in\mathrm{PW}_{2B}^{2}
.

Proof.

We have
x\mapsto\mathrm{e}^{2\pi\mathrm{i}x\omega}\mathcal{G}f(x,\omega)\in\mathrm{PW}_{B}^{2}
since it is the (inverse) Fourier transform of
\mathcal{F}f\cdot\operatorname{T}_{\omega}\phi\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
which satisfies
\supp(\mathcal{F}f\cdot\operatorname{T}_{\omega}\phi)\subset[-B,B]

(
grochenig2001foundations
, Equation (3.5) on p. 39)
. Therefore,
x\mapsto\left\lvert\mathcal{G}f(x,\omega)\right\rvert^{2}\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R})
and applying the Fourier convolution theorem to

x\mapsto\left\lvert\mathcal{G}f(x,\omega)\right\rvert^{2}=\mathcal{G}f(x,\omega)\overline{\mathcal{G}f(x,\omega)}

shows that
x\mapsto\left\lvert\mathcal{G}f(x,\omega)\right\rvert^{2}\in\mathrm{PW}_{2B}^{1}\subset\mathrm{PW}_{2B}^{2}
.
∎

Next, we move on to item 2 of our three-step procedure: the analysis of the recovery of a second-order entire function from magnitude measurements on two parallel lines. Interestingly, it can be shown that it is impossible to reconstruct a finite order entire function (up to global phase) from magnitude information on two parallel lines
wellershoff2022phase
. It is therefore also impossible to recover a square-integrable signal from Gabor phase retrieval measurements on two parallel lines; a fact which has been used to construct the counterexamples to Gabor phase retrieval in
alaifari2021phase
. We are not considering general square-integrable signals here however and the bandlimitedness assumption turns out to be sufficient to exclude counterexamples.

Let us start by realising that fixing the magnitude of an entire function on two parallel lines enforces a periodicity in its zeroes. More precisely, we show that, if two entire functions
F
and
G
have magnitudes that agree on
\mathbb{R}\cup(\mathbb{R}+\mathrm{i}\tau)
, then
m_{F}-m_{G}
is
(2\mathrm{i}\tau)
-periodic.

Lemma 2
.

Let
\tau>0
and let
F,G\in\mathbb{C}\to\mathbb{C}
be two entire functions such that
\left\lvert F\right\rvert=\left\lvert G\right\rvert
on
\mathbb{R}\cup(\mathbb{R}+\mathrm{i}\tau)
. Then,

m_{F}(z+2\mathrm{i}\tau)-m_{G}(z+2\mathrm{i}\tau)=m_{F}(z)-m_{G}(z),\qquad z\in\mathbb{C}.

Proof.

Let
z\in\mathbb{C}
denote an arbitrary complex number. According to
(
mcdonald2004phase
, Proposition 1 on p. 261)
,
\left\lvert F\right\rvert=\left\lvert G\right\rvert
on
\mathbb{R}
implies that

F(z)\overline{F(\overline{z})}=G(z)\overline{G(\overline{z})}.

Therefore, after looking at the zeroes of the above equation exclusively, we have

m_{F}(z)+m_{F}(\overline{z})=m_{G}(z)+m_{G}(\overline{z}).

The same argument applied to
F_{\tau}(z):=F(z+\mathrm{i}\tau)
and
G_{\tau}(z):=G(z+\mathrm{i}\tau)
yields

m_{F}(z+\mathrm{i}\tau)+m_{F}(\overline{z}+\mathrm{i}\tau)=m_{G}(z+\mathrm{i}\tau)+m_{G}(\overline{z}+\mathrm{i}\tau)

such that we can conclude that

m_{F}(z+2\mathrm{i}\tau)-m_{G}(z+2\mathrm{i}\tau)=m_{G}(\overline{z})-m_{F}(\overline{z})=m_{F}(z)-m_{G}(z).

∎

The periodicity in
m_{F}-m_{G}
directly implies that the zeroes (with multiplicities) of
F
and
G
agree everywhere if they agree on the strip
\mathbb{R}+\mathrm{i}(-\tau,\tau]
. Combining this insight with the Hadamard factorisation theorem yields that a second-order entire function is either uniquely determined (up to global phase) by its magnitude on the two parallel lines
\mathbb{R}\cup(\mathbb{R}+\mathrm{i}\tau)
or that it has at least one zero in the strip
\mathbb{R}+\mathrm{i}(-\tau,\tau]
.

Corollary 3
.

Let
\tau>0
and let
F,G\in\mathbb{C}\to\mathbb{C}
be two entire functions
of second-order
such that
\left\lvert F\right\rvert=\left\lvert G\right\rvert
on
\mathbb{R}\cup(\mathbb{R}+\mathrm{i}\tau)
. If
m_{F}-m_{G}=0
on
\mathbb{R}+\mathrm{i}(-\tau,\tau]
, then
F\sim G
.

Proof.

If
m_{F}-m_{G}=0
on
\mathbb{R}+\mathrm{i}(-\tau,\tau]
, then Lemma
2
implies
m_{F}-m_{G}=0
such that the zeroes (with multiplicity) of
F
and
G
agree. It therefore follows from Hadamard’s factorisation theorem (cf.
(
titchmarsh1939theory
, Subsection 8.24 on p. 250)
) that

F(z)=\mathrm{e}^{Q(z)}G(z),\qquad z\in\mathbb{C},

where
Q\in\mathbb{C}_{2}[z]
is a quadratic polynomial. As
\left\lvert F\right\rvert=\left\lvert G\right\rvert
on
\mathbb{R}
, we furthermore have

\left\lvert F(x)\right\rvert=\mathrm{e}^{\operatorname{Re}Q(x)}\left\lvert G(x)\right\rvert=\mathrm{e}^{\operatorname{Re}Q(x)}\left\lvert F(x)\right\rvert,\qquad x\in\mathbb{R},

which implies that
\exp(\operatorname{Re}Q)=1
holds on
\mathbb{R}\setminus Z(F)
. As
F
is entire,
Z(F)
has no accumulation point in
\mathbb{R}
and is thus of measure zero. It follows that
\operatorname{Re}Q=0
almost everywhere (and thus everywhere) on
\mathbb{R}
. We can now write

Q(z)=\mathrm{i}\left(\alpha+\lambda_{1}z+\lambda_{2}z^{2}\right),\qquad z\in\mathbb{C},

for some
\alpha,\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2}\in\mathbb{R}
. The same argument as above shows that
\left\lvert F\right\rvert=\left\lvert G\right\rvert
on
\mathbb{R}+\mathrm{i}\tau
implies
\operatorname{Re}Q=0
on
\mathbb{R}+\mathrm{i}\tau
. Therefore, we find

\operatorname{Re}Q(x+\mathrm{i}\tau)=\lambda_{1}\tau+2\lambda_{2}\tau x=0,\qquad x\in\mathbb{R},

which proves that
\lambda_{1}=\lambda_{2}=0
and thus
F=\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\alpha}G
.
∎

Remark 2
.

Corollary
3
actually holds for general entire functions of finite order
F,G\in\mathbb{C}\to\mathbb{C}
. The proof remains mostly the same: only the polynomial
Q\in\mathbb{C}[z]
is of arbitrary order instead of quadratic. We therefore have

Q(z)=\mathrm{i}\sum_{j=0}^{n}\lambda_{j}z^{j},\qquad z\in\mathbb{C},

for some
n\in\mathbb{N}
and
(\lambda_{j})_{j=0}^{n}\in\mathbb{R}
, and, as
\operatorname{Re}Q=0
on
\mathbb{R}+\mathrm{i}\tau
, we find

\sum_{j=0}^{n}\lambda_{j}\operatorname{Im}\left((x+\mathrm{i}\tau)^{j}\right)=0,\qquad x\in\mathbb{R}.

The binomial theorem, then shows

0=\sum_{j=0}^{n}\lambda_{j}\sum_{k=0}^{j}\binom{j}{k}x^{k}\tau^{j-k}\operatorname{Im}\left(\mathrm{i}^{j-k}\right)=\sum_{k=0}^{n}x^{k}\sum_{j=k}^{n}\binom{j}{k}\lambda_{j}\tau^{j-k}\operatorname{Im}\left(\mathrm{i}^{j-k}\right),

for
x\in\mathbb{R}
. Comparing coefficients yields

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=k}^{n}\binom{j}{k}\lambda_{j}\tau^{j-k}\operatorname{Im}\left(\mathrm{i}^{j-k}\right)=\sum_{j=0}^{n-k}\binom{j+k}{k}\lambda_{j+k}\tau^{j}\operatorname{Im}\left(\mathrm{i}^{j}\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=0}^{\lfloor(n-k-1)/2\rfloor}\binom{2j+k+1}{k}\lambda_{2j+k+1}\tau^{2j+1}(-1)^{j},

for
k=0,\dots,n-1
, which can be used inductively to show that
\lambda_{j}=0
, for
j=1,\dots,n
. Indeed, for
k=n-1
, we find

0=\binom{n}{n-1}\lambda_{n}\tau\implies\lambda_{n}=0.

If we assume that
\lambda_{j}=0
, for
j=k+2,\dots,n
, with
k\in\{0,\dots,n-2\}
, then

0=\sum_{j=0}^{\lfloor(n-k-1)/2\rfloor}\binom{2j+k+1}{k}\lambda_{2j+k+1}\tau^{2j+1}(-1)^{j}=\binom{k+1}{k}\lambda_{k+1}\tau\\
\implies\lambda_{k+1}=0.

We can finally turn to item 3 of our three step procedure: using bandlimitedness to show that
f=0
if
\mathcal{B}f
has infinitely many evenly spaced zeroes. The inspiration for this step actually comes from
grohs2023injectivity
;
wellershoff2021injectivity
, where a Müntz–Szász type result from
zalik1978approximation
is used in order to recover bandlimited
f
from
\left\lvert\mathcal{G}f\right\rvert
on
\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{N}
. We rely on a slight generalisation of that same Müntz–Szász type result for this paper.

Theorem 4

(Zalik’s theorem; cf. Theorem 4 in
zalik1978approximation
)
.

Let
p\in[1,\infty)
, let
a,b\in\mathbb{R}
be such that
a<b
, let
r>0
and let
(c_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\in\mathbb{C}
be a sequence of distinct complex numbers such that there exists a
\delta>0
and an
N_{0}\in\mathbb{N}
with

\left\lvert\operatorname{Re}\left[c_{n}-\tfrac{1}{2}\right]\right\rvert\geq\delta\left\lvert c_{n}-\tfrac{1}{2}\right\rvert,\qquad n\geq N_{0}.

Then,

\left\{t\mapsto\mathrm{e}^{-r^{2}(t-c_{n})^{2}}\,;\,n\in\mathbb{N}\right\}

is complete in
L^{p}([a,b])
if and only if

\sum_{n\in\mathbb{N},~c_{n}\neq 0}\left\lvert c_{n}\right\rvert^{-1}

diverges.

Proof.

The theorem follows from the original proof in
zalik1978approximation
with some small modifications.
∎

Remark 3
.

Zalik’s original result is stated for
p=2
and
real numbers

(c_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
. We do not make use of the added generality in the integrability parameter
p
here but the proof of our main result does require the sequence
(c_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
to be complex-valued.

3
The main result

We are now in a position to state and prove our main result: general bandlimited signals can be recovered from their Gabor transform magnitudes sampled at twice the Nyquist rate in two frequency bins.

Theorem 5

(Main result)
.

Let
B>0
, let
\omega_{0},\omega_{1}\in\mathbb{R}
be such that
\omega_{0}<\omega_{1}
and let
f,g\in\mathrm{PW}_{B}^{2}
. Then,
f\sim g
if (and only if)
\left\lvert\mathcal{G}f\right\rvert=\left\lvert\mathcal{G}g\right\rvert
on
\tfrac{1}{4B}\mathbb{Z}\times\{\omega_{0},\omega_{1}\}
.

Proof.

Let us suppose that
\left\lvert\mathcal{G}f\right\rvert=\left\lvert\mathcal{G}g\right\rvert
on
\tfrac{1}{4B}\mathbb{Z}\times\{\omega_{0},\omega_{1}\}
and fix
j\in\{0,1\}
. According to Lemma
1
, the functions

x\mapsto\lvert\mathcal{G}f(x,\omega_{j})\rvert^{2},\qquad x\mapsto\lvert\mathcal{G}g(x,\omega_{j})\rvert^{2}

are in
\mathrm{PW}_{2B}^{2}
. By the Nyquist–Shannon sampling theorem, we therefore find that

\left\lvert\mathcal{G}f(x,\omega_{j})\right\rvert^{2}=\left\lvert\mathcal{G}g(x,\omega_{j})\right\rvert^{2},\qquad x\in\mathbb{R}.

(2)

Next, we define the second-order entire functions
F(z):=\mathcal{B}f(z+\mathrm{i}\omega_{0})
and
G(z):=\mathcal{B}g(z+\mathrm{i}\omega_{0})
, for
z\in\mathbb{C}
, which satisfy

\left\lvert F(x)\right\rvert=\left\lvert G(x)\right\rvert,\qquad\left\lvert F(x+\mathrm{i}\tau)\right\rvert=\left\lvert G(x+\mathrm{i}\tau)\right\rvert,\qquad x\in\mathbb{R},

for
\tau:=\omega_{1}-\omega_{0}>0
, according to equation (
2
). In the rest of this proof, we will distinguish between two cases:
m_{F}-m_{G}=0
on
\mathbb{R}\times\mathrm{i}(-\tau,\tau]
and
m_{F}-m_{G}\neq 0
on
\mathbb{R}\times\mathrm{i}(-\tau,\tau]
. In the first case, i.e. when
m_{F}-m_{G}=0
on
\mathbb{R}\times\mathrm{i}(-\tau,\tau]
, Corollary
3
implies that
F\sim G
. Since the Bargmann transform is injective
2
2

2

Actually, the range of the Bargmann transform can be equipped with an inner product and thereby turned into a Hilbert space which known as the Fock space
\mathcal{F}^{2}(\mathbb{C})
. The Bargmann transform then turns out to be a unitary operator mapping
L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
onto
\mathcal{F}^{2}(\mathbb{C})

(
grochenig2001foundations
, Theorem 3.4.3 on p. 56)
.
, it follows that
f\sim g
.

In the second case, we may without loss of generality assume that there exists a complex number
z_{0}\in\mathbb{R}\times(-\tau,\tau]
such that
m_{F}(z_{0})-m_{G}(z_{0})>0
: indeed, there exists a complex number at which
m_{F}-m_{G}
is non-zero and we can exchange
F
and
G
if we have
m_{F}(z_{0})-m_{G}(z_{0})<0
. By Lemma
2
, we therefore find that

m_{F}(z_{0}+2\mathrm{i}k\tau)-m_{G}(z_{0}+2\mathrm{i}k\tau)=m_{F}(z_{0})-m_{G}(z_{0})>0,

for
k\in\mathbb{Z}
. It follows that
(z_{0}+2\mathrm{i}k\tau)_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\in\mathbb{C}
forms a sequence of zeroes of
F
, i.e.

\mathcal{B}f(z_{0}+\mathrm{i}(\omega_{0}+2k\tau))=0,\qquad k\in\mathbb{Z}.

The Bargmann transform satisfies the nice symmetry
\mathcal{B}f(-\mathrm{i}z)=\mathcal{B}\mathcal{F}f(z)
, for
z\in\mathbb{C}
(c.f.
(
bargmann1961Hilbert
, Equation (3.10a) on p. 207)
). Therefore,

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle=\mathcal{B}f(z_{0}+\mathrm{i}(\omega_{0}+2k\tau))=\mathcal{B}\mathcal{F}f(\mathrm{i}z_{0}-\omega_{0}-2k\tau)

\displaystyle=\mathrm{e}^{\tfrac{\pi}{2}(\mathrm{i}z_{0}-\omega_{0}-2k\tau)^{2}}\int_{-B}^{B}\mathcal{F}f(\xi)\mathrm{e}^{-\pi(\xi-\mathrm{i}z_{0}+\omega_{0}+2k\tau)^{2}}\,\mathrm{d}\xi

which implies that
\mathcal{F}f
is orthogonal to
\xi\mapsto\mathrm{e}^{-\pi(\xi-\mathrm{i}z_{0}+\omega_{0}+2k\tau)^{2}}
in
L^{2}([-B,B])
, for all
k\in\mathbb{Z}
. Theorem
4
now implies that
\mathcal{F}f=0
and thus
f=0
. Hence,
F=0
and as
\left\lvert F\right\rvert=\left\lvert G\right\rvert
the identity theorem of complex analysis can be used to show that
G=0
such that
g=0=f
.
∎

Our main result may alternatively be stated without reference to the Gabor transform. Indeed, it is equivalent to the following theorem.

Theorem 6
.

Let
B>0
and let
\omega_{0},\omega_{1}\in\mathbb{R}
be such that
\omega_{0}\neq\omega_{1}
. If
f,g\in\mathrm{PW}_{B}^{2}
satisfy

\left\lvert\phi\ast(\operatorname{M}_{-\omega_{j}}f)\right\rvert=\left\lvert\phi\ast(\operatorname{M}_{-\omega_{j}}g)\right\rvert,\qquad j=0,1,

then
f\sim g
.

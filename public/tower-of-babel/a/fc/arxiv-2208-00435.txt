Wall-crossing for vortex partition function and handsaw quiver varierty
Ryo Ohkawa, Yutaka Yoshida

Abstract
We investigate vortex partition functions defined from integrals over the handsaw quiver varieties of type A 1 A_{1} via wall-crossing phenomena. We consider vortex partition functions defined by two types of cohomology classes, and get functional equations for each of them. We also give explicit formula for these partition functions. This gives proofs to formula suggested by physicists. In particular, we obtain geometric interpretation of formulas for multiple hypergeometric functions including rational limit of the Kajihara transformation formula.

Introduction

We investigate vortex partition functions defined from integrals over the handsaw quiver varieties of type
A_{1}

via wall-crossing phenomena.
We get functional equations for two types of partition functions defined from different cohomology classes.
We also give explicit formula for these partition functions.
This gives proofs to formula suggested by physicists Gomis-Floch

[

These partition functions are analogue of the Nekrasov partition function.
The Nekrasov partition function

These varieties are non-compact, and hence integrations are defined by counting torus fixed points.
Furthermore descriptions of fixed points depend on stability conditions for quiver representations.
From this observation, Nakajima-Yoshioka
[
21

derived
the blow-up formula
using

[

Nekrasov’s conjecture states that these partition functions give deformations of the Seiberg-Witten prepotentials for four-dimensional (4d)
\mathcal{N}=2
supersymmetric gauge theories.
This conjecture is proven in Braverman-Etingof
[

[

\hat{\mathbb{P}}^{2}

Furthermore these arguments are extended in

This method also gives functional equations of Nekrasov functions
[

[

In physics literature, vortex analogues of instanton partition functions called
vortex partition functions
are known.
The vortex partition functions are given by the partition functions of the moduli data of vortex solutions in supersymmetric gauge theories, where Higgs branch vacua agrees with
the D-brane realization of the vortex moduli space. When the gauge group is

handsaw quiver varieties of type
A_{1}

When supersymmetic localization is applied, one can show that vortex partition functions in two (resp. three) dimensions have combinatorial descriptions
similar to Nekrasov formula of instanton partition functions in four (resp. five) dimensions.
It was shown in
[

that, when the Fayet-Iliopoulos (FI) parameter for the moduli space changes from positive to negative,
vortex partition functions in certain classes of three-dimensional (3d)

[

The authors of

\mathcal{N}=2

In two-dimensional (2d)

[

[

[

3

These relate the vortex partition functions of two different gauge theories.
However the precise relation between the formulas in

4

9
]
and wall-crossing phenomena has not been studied even in the physics literature.
In this paper, we study algebraic and geometric aspects of vortex partition functions of 2d
\mathcal{N}=(2,2)

\mathcal{N}=(2,2)
gauge theories, which establishes the precise relation between wall-crossing phenomena and

The wall-crossing formula we will derive is regarded as a rational degeneration of the wall-crossing formula in

2.1

A_{1}

For a vector space

\displaystyle W_{0}=\bigoplus_{\alpha\in J_{0}}\mathbb{C}\boldsymbol{w}_{\alpha},\quad W_{1}=\bigoplus_{\alpha\in J_{1}}\mathbb{C}\boldsymbol{w}_{\alpha}.

\displaystyle\mathbb{M}(W,V)

2.2
Partition functions

Putting

\displaystyle\psi_{\mathop{\mathrm{fund}}\nolimits}=\mathop{\mathrm{Eu}}\nolimits\left(\bigoplus_{f=1}^{r}\mathcal{V}\otimes\mathbb{C}_{q^{-1}e^{m_{f}}}\right),\quad\psi_{\mathop{\mathrm{adj}}\nolimits}=\mathop{\mathrm{Eu}}\nolimits(TM_{\pm}(\vec{r},n)\otimes\mathbb{C}_{e^{\theta}}),

\displaystyle Z^{J}_{\pm\mathop{\mathrm{fund}}\nolimits}(\varepsilon,\boldsymbol{a},\boldsymbol{m},p)

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}p^{n}\int_{M_{\pm}(\vec{r},n)}\psi_{\mathop{\mathrm{fund}}\nolimits},

\displaystyle Z^{J}_{\pm\mathop{\mathrm{adj}}\nolimits}(\varepsilon,\boldsymbol{a},\theta,p)

Remark 3.5

\displaystyle T_{\boldsymbol{k}}M_{-}(\vec{r},n)

4
Mochizuki method

Here we briefly recall methods developed in
[
16
]
, and
[
21
]
for quiver setting.
These are similar to
[
22
]
,
[
23
]
, and we will also summarize in
[
25
]
in more general setting.

4.1
\ell
-stability

For any subset
\mathfrak{I}
of
[n]=\{1,\ldots,n\}
and
\mathbb{C}^{n}=\mathbb{C}\boldsymbol{v}_{1}\oplus\cdots\oplus\mathbb{C}\boldsymbol{v}_{n}
,
we put
\mathbb{C}^{\mathfrak{I}}=\bigoplus_{i\in\mathfrak{I}}\mathbb{C}\boldsymbol{v}_{i}
.
In this section, we fix
\mathfrak{I}\subset[n]
, and often put
V=\mathbb{C}^{\mathfrak{I}}
.
We enhance data
\mathcal{A}=(B,z,w)\in\mathbb{M}(W,\mathbb{C}^{\mathfrak{I}})

with flags
F_{\bullet}=(F_{k})
of
\mathbb{C}^{\mathfrak{I}}
such
that
F_{0}=0,F_{n}=\mathbb{C}^{\mathfrak{I}}
, and
\mathop{\mathrm{dim}}\nolimits F_{k}/F_{k-1}\leq 1
for any
k
.
Hence
F_{\bullet}
may have repetitions.
For
\ell=0,1,\ldots,n
, we introduce
\ell
-stability condition for such pairs
(\mathcal{A},F_{\bullet})
.

Definition 4.6
.

A pair
(\mathcal{A},F_{\bullet})
of
\mathcal{A}\in\mathbb{M}(W,\mathbb{C}^{\mathfrak{I}})
and
a flag
F_{\bullet}
of
V=\mathbb{C}^{\mathfrak{I}}
is said to be
\ell
-stable if any sub-space
P
of
V=\mathbb{C}^{\mathfrak{I}}

with
B(P)\subset P
satisfies the following two conditions:

(1)

If
P\subset\mathop{\mathrm{ker}}\nolimits w
and
P\neq 0
, we have
P\cap F^{\ell}=0
.

(2)

If
\mathop{\mathrm{im}}\nolimits z\subset P
and
P\neq V
, we have
F^{\ell}\not\subset P
.

To construct moduli spaces
\widetilde{M}^{\ell}(\vec{r},\mathfrak{I})
of
\ell
-stable pairs,
we put
\mathfrak{I}^{\leq k}=\{i\in\mathfrak{I}\mid i\leq k\}
and

\displaystyle\widetilde{\mathbb{M}}=\widetilde{\mathbb{M}}(W,\mathbb{C}^{\mathfrak{I}})=\mathbb{M}(W,\mathbb{C}^{\mathfrak{I}})\times\prod_{k=0}^{n}\mathop{\mathrm{Hom}}\nolimits_{\mathbb{C}}(\mathbb{C}^{\mathfrak{I}^{\leq k}},\mathbb{C}^{\mathfrak{I}^{\leq k+1}}),

and
G_{\mathfrak{I}}=\operatorname{GL}(V)\times\prod_{k=1}^{n}\operatorname{GL}(\mathbb{C}^{\mathfrak{I}^{\leq k}})
.
For an element
f=(f_{k})_{k=0}^{n}\in\prod_{k=0}^{n}\mathop{\mathrm{Hom}}\nolimits_{\mathbb{C}}(\mathbb{C}^{\mathfrak{I}^{\leq k}},\mathbb{C}^{\mathfrak{I}^{\leq k+1}})
, we put

F_{k}=f_{n}\cdots f_{k}(\mathbb{C}^{\mathfrak{I}^{\leq k}})\subset\mathbb{C}^{\mathfrak{I}}
and
F_{\bullet}=(F_{k})_{k=0}^{n}
when all
f_{k}
are injective.
We write by
\widetilde{\mathbb{M}}^{\ell}(W,\mathbb{C}^{\mathfrak{I}})
a subset consisting of
(\mathcal{A},f)\in\widetilde{\mathbb{M}}(W,\mathbb{C}^{\mathfrak{I}})
such that
all
f_{k}
are injective and

(\mathcal{A},F_{\bullet}=(\mathop{\mathrm{im}}\nolimits f_{n}\cdots f_{k})_{k=0}^{n})

is
\ell
-stable.

Definition 4.7
.

For
\zeta\in\mathbb{Q}
and
\eta=(\eta_{k})_{k=1}^{n}\in(\mathbb{Q}_{>0})^{n}
, an element

(B,z,w,f)\in\widetilde{\mathbb{M}}
is said to be
(\zeta,\eta)
-semistable if
all
f_{k}
are injective and any sub-space
P
of
V=\mathbb{C}^{\mathfrak{I}}
with
B(P)\subset P
satisfies the following two conditions:

(1)

If
P\subset\mathop{\mathrm{ker}}\nolimits w
and
P\neq 0
, we have

\frac{\zeta\mathop{\mathrm{dim}}\nolimits P+\sum_{k=1}^{n}\eta_{k}\mathop{\mathrm{dim}}\nolimits P\cap F_{k}}{\mathop{\mathrm{dim}}\nolimits P}\leq\frac{\sum_{k=1}^{n}\eta_{k}\mathop{\mathrm{dim}}\nolimits F_{k}}{n+1}.

(2)

If
\mathop{\mathrm{im}}\nolimits z\subset P
and
P\neq V
, we have

\frac{\zeta\mathop{\mathrm{dim}}\nolimits V/P+\sum_{k=1}^{n}\eta_{k}\mathop{\mathrm{dim}}\nolimits(F_{k}/P\cap F_{k})}{\mathop{\mathrm{dim}}\nolimits V/P}\geq\frac{\sum_{k=1}^{n}\eta_{k}\mathop{\mathrm{dim}}\nolimits F_{k}}{n+1}.

The condition of injectivity for all
f_{k}
also follows from (1) if we remove the assumption
P\neq 0
and suitably treat the infinity.
We put
\widetilde{\mathbb{M}}^{(\zeta,\eta)}(W,\mathbb{C}^{\mathfrak{I}})=\{(B,z,w,f)\colon(\zeta,\eta)\text{-semistable}\}
.
For
\ell=1,2,\ldots,n
, we consider the following condition

\displaystyle\zeta+\sum_{k=\ell+1}^{n}\eta_{k}\cdot\mathop{\mathrm{dim}}\nolimits\mathbb{C}^{\mathfrak{I}^{\leq k}}<\frac{\sum_{k=1}^{n}\eta_{k}\cdot\mathop{\mathrm{dim}}\nolimits\mathbb{C}^{\mathfrak{I}^{\leq k}}}{n+1}<\zeta+\frac{\eta_{\ell}}{n+1}.

(21)

Remark 4.8
.

We assume (
21
) for a fixed
\ell
.
Then we have

\widetilde{\mathbb{M}}^{(\zeta,\eta)}(W,\mathbb{C}^{\mathfrak{I}})=\widetilde{\mathbb{M}}^{\ell}(\vec{r},\mathbb{C}^{\mathfrak{I}}).

Proof.

When
P\subset\mathop{\mathrm{ker}}\nolimits w
, we have

\frac{\zeta\mathop{\mathrm{dim}}\nolimits P+\sum_{k=1}^{n}\eta_{k}\mathop{\mathrm{dim}}\nolimits P\cap\mathbb{C}^{\mathfrak{I}^{\leq k}}}{\mathop{\mathrm{dim}}\nolimits P}\begin{cases}>\zeta+\frac{\eta_{\ell}}{n+1}&\text{ if }P\cap\mathbb{C}^{\mathfrak{I}^{\leq\ell}}\neq 0\\
<\zeta+\sum_{k=\ell+1}^{n}\eta_{k}\cdot\mathop{\mathrm{dim}}\nolimits\mathbb{C}^{\mathfrak{I}^{\leq k}}&\text{ if }P\cap\mathbb{C}^{\mathfrak{I}^{\leq\ell}}=0.\end{cases}

Hence in this case, the inequality in Definition
4.7
(1) is equivalent to
S_{0}\cap\tilde{V}^{\ell}=0

by (
21
).

When
P\supset\mathop{\mathrm{im}}\nolimits z
, we have

\frac{\zeta\mathop{\mathrm{dim}}\nolimits V/P+\sum_{k=1}^{n}\eta_{k}\mathop{\mathrm{dim}}\nolimits\mathbb{C}^{\mathfrak{I}^{\leq k}}/P\cap\mathbb{C}^{\mathfrak{I}^{\leq k}}}{\mathop{\mathrm{dim}}\nolimits V/P}\\
\begin{cases}>\zeta+\frac{\eta_{\ell}}{n+1}&\text{ if }P\cap V_{I^{\leq\ell}}\neq V_{I^{\leq\ell}}\\
<\zeta+\sum_{k=\ell+1}^{n}\eta_{k}\cdot\mathop{\mathrm{dim}}\nolimits\mathbb{C}^{\mathfrak{I}^{\leq k}}&\text{ if }P\cap V_{I^{\leq\ell}}=V_{I^{\leq\ell}}.\end{cases}

Hence in this case, the inequality in Definition
4.7
(2) is equivalent to
P\cap V_{I^{\leq\ell}}\neq V_{I^{\leq\ell}}

by (
21
).

In both cases, we get strict inequalities if
(B,z,w,f)
is
(\zeta,\eta)
semi-stable.
Hence we get the desired isomorphism.
∎

Thus we get moduli spaces
\widetilde{M}^{\ell}(\vec{r},\mathfrak{I})
of
\ell
-stable pairs as

Hence we also use

, but not

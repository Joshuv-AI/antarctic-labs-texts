Comparison principles for the time-fractional diffusion equations with the Robin boundary conditions. Part I: Linear equations Journal: Fract. Calc. Appl. Anal.
Yuri Luchko 1, Masahiro Yamamoto 2

Abstract
The main objective of this paper is analysis of the initial-boundary value problems for the linear time-fractional diffusion equations with a uniformly elliptic spatial differential operator of the second order and the Caputo type time-fractional derivative acting in the fractional Sobolev spaces. The boundary conditions are formulated in form of the homogeneous Neumann or Robin conditions. First we discuss the uniqueness and existence of solutions to these initial-boundary value problems. Under some suitable conditions on the problem data, we then prove positivity of the solutions. Based on these results, several comparison principles for the solutions to the initial-boundary value problems for the linear time-fractional diffusion equations are derived.

Introduction

In this paper, we deal with a linear time-fractional
diffusion equation in the form

\partial_{t}^{\alpha}(u(x,t)-a(x))=\sum_{i,j=1}^{d}\partial_{i}(a_{ij}(x)\partial_{j}u(x,t))

+\sum_{j=1}^{d}b_{j}(x,t)\partial_{j}u(x,t)+c(x,t)u(x,t)+F(x,t),\hskip 10.00002ptx\in\Omega,\,0<t<T,

(1.1)

where
\partial_{t}^{\alpha}
is the Caputo fractional derivative of order

\alpha\in(0,1)
defined on the fractional Sobolev spaces
(see Section
2
for the details) and
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{d},\ d=1,2,3
is
a bounded domain with a smooth boundary
\partial\Omega
.
All the functions under consideration are supposed to be real-valued.

In what follows, we always assume that the following conditions are satisfied:

\left\{\begin{array}[]{rl}&a_{ij}=a_{ji}\in C^{1}(\overline{\Omega}),\quad 1\leq i,j\leq d,\\
&b_{j},\,c\in C^{1}([0,T];C^{1}(\overline{\Omega}))\cap C([0,T];C^{2}(\overline{\Omega})),\quad 1\leq j\leq d,\\
&\mbox{and there exists a constant $\kappa>0$ such that}\\
&\sum_{i,j=1}^{d}a_{ij}(x)\xi_{i}\xi_{j}\geq\kappa\sum_{j=1}^{d}\xi_{j}^{2},\quad x\in\Omega,\,\xi_{1},...,\xi_{d}\in\mathbb{R}.\end{array}\right.

(1.2)

Using the notations
\partial_{j}=\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{j}}
,
j=1,2,...,d
,
we define a conormal derivative

\partial_{\nu_{A}}w
with respect to the differential operator

\sum_{i,j=1}^{d}\partial_{j}(a_{ij}\partial_{i})
by

\partial_{\nu_{A}}w(x)=\sum_{i,j=1}^{d}a_{ij}(x)\partial_{j}w(x)\nu_{i}(x),\hskip 10.00002ptx\in\partial\Omega,

(1.3)

where
\nu=\nu(x)=:(\nu_{1}(x),...,\nu_{d}(x))
is the unit outward normal
vector to
\partial\Omega
at the point
x:=(x_{1},...,x_{d})\in\partial\Omega
.

For the equation (
1.1
), we consider the initial-boundary value
problems with the homogeneous Neumann boundary condition

\partial_{\nu_{A}}u=0\hskip 10.00002pt\mbox{on $\partial\Omega\times(0,T)$}

(1.4)

or the more general homogeneous Robin boundary condition

\partial_{\nu_{A}}u+\sigma(x)u=0\hskip 10.00002pt\mbox{on $\partial\Omega\times(0,T)$},

(1.5)

where
\sigma
is a sufficiently smooth function on
\partial\Omega
that
satisfies the condition
\sigma(x)\geq 0,\ x\in\partial\Omega
.

For partial differential equations of the parabolic type
that correspond to the case
\alpha=1
in the equation (
1.1
),
several
important qualitative properties of solutions to the corresponding initial-boundary
value problems are known. In particular,
we mention a maximum principle and a comparison principle for the solutions to these problems (
PW
,
RR
).

The main purpose of this paper is the comparison principles
for the linear time-fractional
diffusion equation (
1.1
) with the Neumann or the
Robin boundary conditions.

For the equations of type (
1.1
)
with the Dirichlet boundary conditions, the maximum principles in different formulations were derived and
used in
Bor
;
Lu1
;
luchko-1
;
luchko-2
;
Lu2
;
LY1
;
LY2
;
LY3
;
Za
.
For a maximum principle for the time-fractional transport equations
we refer to
LSY
. In
Kir
, a maximum principle for the more general
space- and time-space-fractional partial differential equations has been
derived.

Because any maximum principle involves the Dirichlet boundary values, its
formulation in the case of the Neumann or Robin boundary
conditions requires more cares.
For this kind of the boundary conditions, both positivity of solutions
and the comparison
principles can be derived under some suitable restrictions on the problem
data. One typical result of this sort says
that the solution
u
to the equation (
1.1
) with the boundary
condition (
1.4
) or (
1.5
)
and an appropriately formulated initial condition is
non-negative in
\Omega\times(0,T)

if the initial value
a
and the non-homogeneous term
F
are non-negative
in
\Omega
and in
\Omega\times(0,T)
, respectively.
Such positivity properties and their applications have been intensively discussed
and used for the partial differential equations of parabolic type (
\alpha=1

in the equation (
1.1
)), see, e.g.,
E
,

Fr
,
Pao2
, or
RR
.

However, to the best knowledge of the authors, no results of this kind have been published for
the time-fractional diffusion equations in the case of the Neumann or Robin
boundary conditions. The main subject of this paper is in derivation of
a positivity property and the comparison principles for the linear equation
(
1.1
) with the boundary condition
(
1.4
) or (
1.5
) and an appropriately formulated initial
condition. In the subsequent
paper, these result will be extended to the case of the semilinear
time-fractional diffusion
equations. The arguments employed in these papers
rely on an operator theoretical approach to the fractional integrals and
derivatives in the fractional Sobolev spaces that is an extension of the
theory well-known in the case
\alpha=1
, see, e.g.,
He
,
Pa
,
Ta
. We also refer to the recent publications
Al-R
and
Lu
devoted to the comparison principles for solutions to the fractional differential inequalities with the general fractional derivatives and for solutions to the ordinary fractional differential equations, respectively.

The rest of this paper is organized as follows. In Section
2
,
some important results regarding the unique existence of solutions
to the initial-boundary value problems for the linear time-fractional
diffusion equations are presented. Section
3
is devoted to a proof
of a key lemma that is a basis for the proofs of the comparison principles for the linear and semilinear time-fractional
diffusion equations.
The lemma asserts that each solution to (
1.1
) is non-negative
in
\Omega\times(0,T)
if
a\geq 0
and
F\geq 0
, provided that

u
is assumed to satisfy some extra regularity.
In Section
4
, we prove a comparison principle that is our
main result for the problem (
1.1
) for the linear time-fractional
diffusion equation. Moreover, we establish the order-preserving properties
for other problem data (the zeroth-order coefficient
c
of the equation and the coefficient
\sigma
of the Robin condition).
Finally, a detailed proof of an important auxiliary statement is presented in an Appendix.

2
Well-posedness results

For
x\in\Omega,\thinspace 0<t<T
, we define an operator

-Av(x,t):=\sum_{i,j=1}^{d}\partial_{i}(a_{ij}(x)\partial_{j}v(x,t)+\sum_{j=1}^{d}b_{j}(x,t)\partial_{j}v(x,t)+c(x,t)v(x,t)

(2.1)

and assume that the conditions (
1.2
) for the coefficients

a_{ij},b_{j},c
are satisfied.

In this section, we deal with the following initial-boundary value problem for
the linear time-fractional diffusion equation (
1.1
) with the time-fractional derivative
of order
\alpha\in(0,1)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{rl}&\partial_{t}^{\alpha}(u(x,t)-a(x))+Au(x,t)=F(x,t),\quad x\in\Omega,\thinspace 0<t<T,\\
&\partial_{\nu_{A}}u+\sigma(x)u(x,t)=0,\quad x\in\partial\Omega,\,0<t<T,\end{array}\right.

(2.2)

along with the initial condition (
2.3
) formulated below.

To appropriately define the Caputo fractional derivative
d_{t}^{\alpha}w(t)
,
0<\alpha<1
,
we start with its definition on the space

{{}_{0}C^{1}[0,T]}:=\{u\in C^{1}[0,T];\thinspace u(0)=0\}

that reads as follows:

d_{t}^{\alpha}w(t)=\frac{1}{\Gamma(1-\alpha)}\int^{t}_{0}(t-s)^{-\alpha}\frac{dw}{ds}(s)ds,\ w\in{{}_{0}C^{1}[0,T]}.

Then we extend this operator from the domain
\mathcal{D}(d_{t}^{\alpha}):={{}_{0}C^{1}[0,T]}
to
L^{2}(0,T)
taking into account its closability
(
Yo
). As have been shown in
KRY
, there exists a unique minimum
closed
extension of
d_{t}^{\alpha}
with the domain
\mathcal{D}(d_{t}^{\alpha})={{}_{0}C^{1}[0,T]}
.
Moreover, the domain of this
extension is the closure of
{{}_{0}C^{1}[0,T]}
in the Sobolev-Slobodeckij space

H^{\alpha}(0,T)
. Let us recall that the norm
\|\cdot\|_{H^{\alpha}(0,T)}
of the Sobolev-Slobodeckij space

H^{\alpha}(0,T)
is defined as follows (
Ad
):

\|v\|_{H^{\alpha}(0,T)}:=\left(\|v\|^{2}_{L^{2}(0,T)}+\int^{T}_{0}\int^{T}_{0}\frac{|v(t)-v(s)|^{2}}{|t-s|^{1+2\alpha}}dtds\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

By setting

H_{\alpha}(0,T):=\overline{{{}_{0}C^{1}[0,T]}}^{H^{\alpha}(0,T)},

we obtain (
KRY
)

H_{\alpha}(0,T)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{rl}&H^{\alpha}(0,T),\quad 0<\alpha<\frac{1}{2},\\
&\left\{v\in H^{\frac{1}{2}}(0,T);\,\int^{T}_{0}\frac{|v(t)|^{2}}{t}dt<\infty\right\},\quad\alpha=\frac{1}{2},\\
&\{v\in H^{\alpha}(0,T);\,v(0)=0\},\quad\frac{1}{2}<\alpha<1,\end{array}\right.

and

\|v\|_{H_{\alpha}(0,T)}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{rl}&\|v\|_{H^{\alpha}(0,T)},\quad\alpha\neq\frac{1}{2},\\
&\left(\|v\|_{H^{\frac{1}{2}}(0,T)}^{2}+\int^{T}_{0}\frac{|v(t)|^{2}}{t}dt\right)^{\frac{1}{2}},\quad\alpha=\frac{1}{2}.\end{array}\right.

In what follows, we also use the Riemann-Liouville fractional
integral operator
J^{\beta}
,
\beta>0
defined by

(J^{\beta}f)(t):=\frac{1}{\Gamma(\beta)}\int^{t}_{0}(t-s)^{\beta-1}f(s)ds,\hskip 10.00002pt0<t<T.

Then, according to
GLY
and
KRY
,

H_{\alpha}(0,T)=J^{\alpha}L^{2}(0,T),\hskip 10.00002pt0<\alpha<1.

Next we define

\partial_{t}^{\alpha}=(J^{\alpha})^{-1}\hskip 10.00002pt\mbox{with $\mathcal{D}(\partial_{t}^{\alpha})=H_{\alpha}(0,T)$}.

As have been shown in
GLY
and
KRY
, there exists a constant
C>0
depending only on
\alpha
such that

C^{-1}\|v\|_{H_{\alpha}(0,T)}\leq\|\partial_{t}^{\alpha}v\|_{L^{2}(0,T)}\leq C\|v\|_{H_{\alpha}(0,T)}\hskip 10.00002pt\mbox{for all }v\in H_{\alpha}(0,T).

Now we can introduce a suitable form of initial condition for the problem
(
2.2
) as follows

u(x,\cdot)-a(x)\in H_{\alpha}(0,T)\hskip 10.00002pt\mbox{for almost all }x\in\Omega

(2.3)

and write down a complete formulation of an initial-boundary value problem
for the linear time-fractional diffusion equation (
1.1
):

\left\{\begin{array}[]{rl}&\partial_{t}^{\alpha}(u(x,t)-a(x))+Au(x,t)=F(x,t),\quad x\in\Omega,\thinspace 0<t<T,\\
&\partial_{\nu_{A}}u(x,t)+\sigma(x)u(x,t)=0,\quad x\in\partial\Omega,\,0<t<T,\\
&u(x,\cdot)-a(x)\in H_{\alpha}(0,T)\quad\mbox{for almost all }x\in\Omega.\end{array}\right.

(2.4)

It is worth mentioning that the term
\partial_{t}^{\alpha}(u(x,t)-a(x))
in the first line of (
2.4
) is
well-defined due to inclusion formulated in the third line of (
2.4
).
In particular, for
\frac{1}{2}<\alpha<1
, the Sobolev embedding leads
to the inclusions

H_{\alpha}(0,T)\subset H^{\alpha}(0,T)\subset C[0,T]
.
This means that
u\in H_{\alpha}(0,T;L^{2}(\Omega))
implies
u\in C([0,T];L^{2}(\Omega))

and thus in this case the initial condition can be formulated as

u(\cdot,0)=a
in
L^{2}
-sense. Moreover, for sufficiently smooth functions

a
and
F
, the solution to (
2.4
) can be proved to satisfy
the initial condition in a usual sense:
\lim_{t\to 0}u(\cdot,t)=a
in
L^{2}(\Omega)
(see Lemma 4 in Section 3).
Consequently, the third line of (
2.4
) can be interpreted as
a generalized initial condition.

In the following theorem, a fundamental result regarding the unique existence
of the solution to
the initial-boundary value problem (
2.4
) is presented.

Theorem 2.1

For
a\in H^{1}(\Omega)
and
F\in L^{2}(0,T;L^{2}(\Omega))
, there exists a unique
solution

u(F,a)=u(F,a)(x,t)\in L^{2}(0,T;H^{2}(\Omega))
to the initial-boundary value
problem (
2.4
) such that

u(F,a)-a\in H_{\alpha}(0,T;L^{2}(\Omega))
.

Moreover, there exists a constant
C>0
such that

\displaystyle\|u(F,a)-a\|_{H_{\alpha}(0,T;L^{2}(\Omega))}+\|u(F,a)\|_{L^{2}(0,T;H^{2}(\Omega))}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C(\|a\|_{H^{1}(\Omega)}+\|F\|_{L^{2}(0,T;L^{2}(\Omega))}).

Before starting with a proof of Theorem
2.1
, we introduce some notations and derive several helpful results needed for the proof.

For an arbitrary constant
c_{0}>0
, we define an elliptic operator
A_{0}

as follows:

\left\{\begin{array}[]{rl}&(-A_{0}v)(x):=\sum_{i,j=1}^{d}\partial_{i}(a_{ij}(x)\partial_{j}v(x))-c_{0}v(x),\quad x\in\Omega,\\
&\mathcal{D}(A_{0})=\left\{v\in H^{2}(\Omega);\,\partial_{\nu_{A}}v+\sigma v=0\quad\mbox{on }\partial\Omega\right\}.\end{array}\right.

(2.5)

We recall that in the definition (
2.5
),
\sigma
is a smooth function, the inequality
\sigma(x)\geq 0,\ x\in\partial\Omega
holds true,
and the coefficients
a_{ij}
satisfy the conditions
(
1.2
).

Henceforth, by
\|\cdot\|
and
(\cdot,\cdot)
we denote the
standard norm and
the scalar product in
L^{2}(\Omega)
, respectively.
It is well-known that the operator
A_{0}
is self-adjoint and its resolvent
is a compact operator. Moreover, for a sufficiently large constant

c_{0}>0
, by Lemma
6
in Section
5
, we can verify that
A_{0}
is
positive definite.
Therefore, by choosing the constant
c_{0}>0
large enough,
the spectrum of
A_{0}
consists entirely of discrete positive
eigenvalues
0<\lambda_{1}\leq\lambda_{2}\leq\cdots
,
which are numbered according to their multiplicities and

\lambda_{n}\to\infty
as
n\to\infty
.
Let
\varphi_{n}
be an eigenvector corresponding to the eigenvalue
\lambda_{n}
such that
A\varphi_{n}=\lambda_{n}\varphi_{n}
and

(\varphi_{n},\varphi_{m})=0
if
n\neq m
and
(\varphi_{n},\varphi_{n})=1
.
Then the system
\{\varphi_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
of the
eigenvectors forms an
orthonormal basis in
L^{2}(\Omega)
and for any
\gamma\geq 0
we can define
the fractional powers
A_{0}^{\gamma}
of the operator
A_{0}

by the following relation (see, e.g.,
Pa
):

A_{0}^{\gamma}v=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\lambda_{n}^{\gamma}(v,\varphi_{n})\varphi_{n},

where

v\in\mathcal{D}(A_{0}^{\gamma}):=\left\{v\in L^{2}(\Omega):\thinspace\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\lambda_{n}^{2\gamma}(v,\varphi_{n})^{2}<\infty\right\}

and

\|A_{0}^{\gamma}v\|=\left(\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\lambda_{n}^{2\gamma}(v,\varphi_{n})^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

We note that
\mathcal{D}(A_{0}^{\gamma})\subset H^{2\gamma}(\Omega)
.

Our proof of Theorem
2.1
is similar to the one presented in
GLY
,
KRY
for the case of the homogeneous Dirichlet
boundary condition. In particular, we
employ the operators
S(t)
and
K(t)
defined by (
GLY
,
KRY
)

S(t)a=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}E_{\alpha,1}(-\lambda_{n}t^{\alpha})(a,\varphi_{n})\varphi_{n},\hskip 10.00002pta\in L^{2}(\Omega),\thinspace t>0

(2.6)

and

K(t)a=-A_{0}^{-1}S^{\prime}(t)a=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}t^{\alpha-1}E_{\alpha,\alpha}(-\lambda_{n}t^{\alpha})(a,\varphi_{n})\varphi_{n},\hskip 10.00002pta\in L^{2}(\Omega),\thinspace t>0.

(2.7)

In the above formulas,
E_{\alpha,\beta}(z)
denotes the Mittag-Leffler function defined by
a convergent series as follows:

E_{\alpha,\beta}(z)=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{z^{k}}{\Gamma(\alpha\,k+\beta)},\ \alpha>0,\ \beta\in\mathbb{C},\ z\in\mathbb{C}.

It follows directly from the definitions given above that

A_{0}^{\gamma}K(t)a=K(t)A_{0}^{\gamma}a

and
A_{0}^{\gamma}S(t)a=S(t)A_{0}^{\gamma}a
for
a\in\mathcal{D}(A_{0}^{\gamma})
.
Moreover, the inequality (see, e.g., Theorem 1.6 (p. 35) in
Po
)

\max\{|E_{\alpha,1}(-\lambda_{n}t^{\alpha})|,\,|E_{\alpha,\alpha}(-\lambda_{n}t^{\alpha})|\}\leq\frac{C}{1+\lambda_{n}t^{\alpha}}\hskip 10.00002pt\mbox{for all $t>0$}

implicates the estimations (
GLY
)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\|A_{0}^{\gamma}S(t)a\|\leq Ct^{-\alpha\gamma}\|a\|,\\
\|A_{0}^{\gamma}K(t)a\|\leq Ct^{\alpha(1-\gamma)-1}\|a\|,\quad a\in L^{2}(\Omega),\thinspace t>0,\thinspace 0\leq\gamma\leq 1.\end{array}\right.

(2.8)

In order to shorten the notations and to focus on the dependence on the time
variable
t
, henceforth we sometimes omit the variable
x
in the functions
of two variables
x
and
t
and write, say,

u(t)
instead of
u(\cdot,t)
.

Due to the inequalities (
2.8
), the estimations provided in the formulation of Theorem
2.1
can be derived as in
the case of the fractional powers of generators of the analytic semigroups
(
He
). To do this, we first formulate and prove the following lemma:

Lemma 1

Under the conditions formulated above, the following estimates hold true for

F\in L^{2}(0,T;L^{2}(\Omega))
and
a\in L^{2}(\Omega)
:

(i)

\left\|\int^{t}_{0}A_{0}K(t-s)F(s)ds\right\|_{L^{2}(0,T;L^{2}(\Omega))}\leq C\|F\|_{L^{2}(0,T;L^{2}(\Omega))},

(ii)

\left\|\int^{t}_{0}K(t-s)F(s)ds\right\|_{H_{\alpha}(0,T;L^{2}(\Omega))}\leq C\|F\|_{L^{2}(0,T;L^{2}(\Omega))},

(iii)

\|S(t)a-a\|_{H_{\alpha}(0,T;L^{2}(\Omega))}+\|S(t)a\|_{L^{2}(0,T;H^{2}(\Omega))}\leq C\|a\|.

Proof

We start with proving the estimate (i). By (
2.7
), we have

\displaystyle\left\|\int^{t}_{0}A_{0}K(t-s)F(s)ds\right\|^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left\|\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\left(\int^{t}_{0}\lambda_{n}(t-s)^{\alpha-1}E_{\alpha,\alpha}(-\lambda_{n}(t-s)^{\alpha})(F(s),\varphi_{n})ds\right)\varphi_{n}\right\|^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\left|\int^{t}_{0}\lambda_{n}(t-s)^{\alpha-1}E_{\alpha,\alpha}(-\lambda_{n}(t-s)^{\alpha})(F(s),\varphi_{n})ds\right|^{2}.

Therefore, using the Parseval equality and the Young inequality for the
convolution, we obtain

\displaystyle\left\|\int^{t}_{0}A_{0}K(t-s)F(s)ds\right\|^{2}_{L^{2}(0,T;L^{2}(\Omega))}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\int^{T}_{0}|(\lambda_{n}s^{\alpha-1}E_{\alpha,\alpha}(-\lambda_{n}s^{\alpha})\,*\,(F(s),\varphi_{n})|^{2}ds

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\|\lambda_{n}s^{\alpha-1}E_{\alpha,\alpha}(-\lambda_{n}s^{\alpha})\,*\,(F(s),\varphi_{n})\|^{2}_{L^{2}(0,T)}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\left(\lambda_{n}\int^{t}_{0}|t^{\alpha-1}E_{\alpha,\alpha}(-\lambda_{n}t^{\alpha})|dt\right)^{2}\|(F(t),\varphi_{n})\|^{2}_{L^{2}(0,T)}.

Then we employ the representation

\frac{d}{dt}E_{\alpha,1}(-\lambda_{n}t^{\alpha})=-\lambda_{n}t^{\alpha-1}E_{\alpha,\alpha}(-\lambda_{n}t^{\alpha}),

(2.9)

and the complete monotonicity of the Mittag-Leffler function (
GKMR
)

E_{\alpha,1}(-\lambda_{n}t^{\alpha})>0,\hskip 10.00002pt\frac{d}{dt}E_{\alpha,1}(-\lambda_{n}t^{\alpha})\leq 0,\hskip 10.00002ptt\geq 0,\hskip 10.00002pt0<\alpha\leq 1

to get the inequality

\int^{T}_{0}|\lambda_{n}t^{\alpha-1}E_{\alpha,\alpha}(-\lambda_{n}t^{\alpha})|dt=-\int^{T}_{0}\frac{d}{dt}E_{\alpha,1}(-\lambda_{n}t^{\alpha})dt

(2.10)

=1-E_{\alpha,1}(-\lambda_{n}T^{\alpha})\leq 1\hskip 10.00002pt\mbox{for all $n\in\mathbb{N}$}.

Hence,

\displaystyle\left\|\int^{t}_{0}A_{0}K(t-s)F(s)ds\right\|^{2}_{L^{2}(0,T;L^{2}(\Omega))}\leq\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\|(F(t),\varphi_{n})\|^{2}_{L^{2}(0,T)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int^{T}_{0}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}|(F(t),\varphi_{n})|^{2}dt=\int^{T}_{0}\|F(\cdot,t)\|^{2}dt=\|F\|_{L^{2}(0,T;L^{2}(\Omega))}^{2}.

Now we proceed with proving the estimate (ii). For
0<t<T,\,n\in\mathbb{N}

and
f\in L^{2}(0,T)
,
we set

(L_{n}f)(t):=\int^{t}_{0}(t-s)^{\alpha-1}E_{\alpha,\alpha}(-\lambda_{n}(t-s)^{\alpha})f(s)ds.

Then

\int^{t}_{0}K(t-s)F(s)ds=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}(L_{n}f)(t)\varphi_{n}

in
L^{2}(\Omega)
for any fixed
t\in[0,T]
.

First we prove that

\left\{\begin{array}[]{rl}&L_{n}f\in H_{\alpha}(0,T),\\
&\partial_{t}^{\alpha}(L_{n}f)(t)=-\lambda_{n}L_{n}f(t)+f(t),\quad 0<t<T,\\
&\|L_{n}f\|_{H_{\alpha}(0,T)}\leq C\|f\|_{L^{2}(0,T)},\quad n\in\mathbb{N}\quad\mbox{for each }f\in L^{2}(0,T).\end{array}\right.

(2.11)

In order to prove this, we apply the Riemann-Liouville fractional
integral
J^{\alpha}
to
L_{n}f
and get the representation

\displaystyle J^{\alpha}(L_{n}f)(t)=\frac{1}{\Gamma(\alpha)}\int^{t}_{0}(t-s)^{\alpha-1}(L_{n}f)(s)ds

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\Gamma(\alpha)}\int^{t}_{0}(t-s)^{\alpha-1}\left(\int^{s}_{0}(s-\xi)^{\alpha-1}E_{\alpha,\alpha}(-\lambda_{n}(s-\xi)^{\alpha})f(\xi)d\xi\right)ds

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\Gamma(\alpha)}\int^{t}_{0}f(\xi)\left(\int^{t}_{\xi}(t-s)^{\alpha-1}(s-\xi)^{\alpha-1}E_{\alpha,\alpha}(-\lambda_{n}(s-\xi)^{\alpha})ds\right)d\xi.

By direct calculations, using (
2.9
), we obtain the formula

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\Gamma(\alpha)}\int^{t}_{\xi}(t-s)^{\alpha-1}(s-\xi)^{\alpha-1}E_{\alpha,\alpha}(-\lambda_{n}(s-\xi)^{\alpha})ds

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{\lambda_{n}}(t-\xi)^{\alpha-1}\left(E_{\alpha,\alpha}(-\lambda_{n}t^{\alpha})-\frac{1}{\Gamma(\alpha)}\right).

Therefore, we have the relation

\displaystyle J^{\alpha}(L_{n}f)(t)=-\frac{1}{\lambda_{n}}(L_{n}f)(t)+\frac{1}{\lambda_{n}}\int^{t}_{0}(t-\xi)^{\alpha-1}\frac{1}{\Gamma(\alpha)}f(\xi)d\xi

\displaystyle=

Our first result concerning the comparison

Theorem 4.1

Let the functions

and

F(x,t)\geq 0,\ (x,t)\in\Omega\times(0,T)

u(F,a)\in\mathcal{Y}_{\alpha}

2.4

non-negative, e.g., the inequality

Let us emphasize that the non-negativity of the solution

Corollary 1

Let
a_{1},a_{2}\in H^{1}(\Omega)
and
F_{1},F_{2}\in L^{2}(\Omega\times(0,T))

satisfy the inequalities
a_{1}(x)\geq a_{2}(x),\ x\in\Omega

F_{1}(x,t)\geq F_{2}(x,t),\ (x,t)\in\Omega\times(0,T)

Then the inequality

u(F_{1},a_{1})(x,t)\geq u(F_{2},a_{2})(x,t),\ (x,t)\in\Omega\times(0,T)

Proof

Setting
a:=a_{1}-a_{2}
,
F:=F_{1}-F_{2}
and
u:=u(F_{1},a_{1})-u(F_{2},a_{2})

we immediately obtain the inequalities
a(x)\geq 0,\ x\in\Omega
and
F(x,t)\geq 0,\ (x,t)\in\Omega\times(0,T)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{rl}&\partial_{t}^{\alpha}(u-a)+Au=F\geq 0\quad\mbox{in }\Omega\times(0,T),\\

Therefore, Theorem
4.1

u(x,t)\geq 0,\ (x,t)\in\Omega\times(0,T)

u(F_{1},a_{1})(x,t)\geq u(F_{2},a_{2})(x,t),\ (x,t)\in\Omega\times(0,T)
.

C^{\theta}(\overline{\Omega})
if

\sup_{x,x^{\prime}\in\Omega,\,x\neq x^{\prime}}\frac{|w(x)-w(x^{\prime})|}{|x-x^{\prime}|^{\theta}}<\infty.

For
w\in C^{\theta}(\overline{\Omega})
, we define the norm

\|w\|_{C^{\theta}(\overline{\Omega})}:=\|w\|_{C(\overline{\Omega})}+\sup_{x,x^{\prime}\in\Omega,\,x\neq x^{\prime}}\frac{|w(x)-w(x^{\prime})|}{|x-x^{\prime}|^{\theta}}

and for
w\in C^{2+\theta}(\overline{\Omega})
, the norm is given by

\|w\|_{C^{2+\theta}(\overline{\Omega})}:=\|w\|_{C^{2}(\overline{\Omega})}+\sum_{|\tau|=2}\sup_{x,x^{\prime}\in\Omega,\,x\neq x^{\prime}}\frac{|\partial_{x}^{\tau}w(x)-\partial_{x}^{\tau}w(x^{\prime})|}{|x-x^{\prime}|^{\theta}}.

In the last formula, the notations

\tau:=(\tau_{1},...,\tau_{d})\in(\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\})^{d}
,

\partial_{x}^{\tau}:=\partial_{1}^{\tau_{1}}\cdots\partial_{d}^{\tau_{d}}
, and

|\tau|:=\tau_{1}+\cdots+\tau_{d}
are employed.

For
d=1,2,3
, the Sobolev embedding theorem says that
H^{2}(\Omega)\subset C^{\theta}(\overline{\Omega})
with some
\theta\in(0,1)

(
Ad
).

Therefore, in view of (
4.14
), we obtain the inclusion

h:=A_{0}u(\cdot,t)\in C^{\theta}(\overline{\Omega})
for each
t\in[0,T]
.
Now we apply the Schauder
estimate (see, e.g.,
GT
or
LU
)
for solutions to the elliptic boundary value problem

A_{0}u(\cdot,t)=h\in C^{\theta}(\overline{\Omega})\hskip 10.00002pt\mbox{in }\Omega

with the boundary condition
\partial_{\nu_{A}}u(\cdot,t)+\sigma(\cdot)u(\cdot,t)=0

on
\partial\Omega
to reach the inclusion

u\in C([0,T];C^{2+\theta}(\overline{\Omega})).

This inclusion and (
4.11
) yield the conclusion

u\in C([0,T];C^{2}(\overline{\Omega}))
and

t^{1-\alpha}\partial_{t}u\in C([0,T];C(\overline{\Omega}))
of the lemma.

Finally we prove that
\lim_{t\to 0}\|u(t)-a\|=0
.
By (
2.8
), we have

\displaystyle\left\|\int^{t}_{0}K(t-s)h(s)ds\right\|\leq\int^{t}_{0}\|K(t-s)h(s)\|ds\leq C\int^{t}_{0}(t-s)^{\alpha-1}\|h(s)\|ds

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{Ct^{\alpha}}{\alpha}\|h\|_{L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{2}(\Omega))},

and so

\lim_{t\to 0}\int^{t}_{0}K(t-s)h(s)ds=0\hskip 10.00002pt\mbox{in $L^{2}(\Omega)$}

(4.15)

for each
h\in L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{2}(\Omega))
.
Therefore by the regularity
u\in C([0,T];C^{2}(\overline{\Omega}))
, we see that

\lim_{t\to 0}\left(\int^{t}_{0}K(t-s)F(s)ds+Ru(t)\right)=0\hskip 10.00002pt\mbox{in $L^{2}(\Omega)$},

where
R
is defined in (
2.14
).
Moreover, for justifying (
4.12
), we have already proved

\lim_{t\to 0}\|S(t)a-a\|=0
for
a\in L^{2}(\Omega)
.
Thus the proof of Lemma
4
is complete.

II. Second part of the proof.

In this part, we weaken the regularity conditions posed on the solution
u

to (
3.3
) in
Lemma
3
and prove the same
results provided that
u\in L^{2}(0,T;H^{2}(\Omega))
and

u-a\in H_{\alpha}(0,T;L^{2}(\Omega))
, under the assumption that

\min\limits_{(x,t)\in\overline{\Omega}\times[0,T]}b_{0}(x,t)>0

is sufficiently large.

Let
F\in L^{2}(0,T;L^{2}(\Omega))
and
a\in H^{1}(\Omega)
satisfy the inequalities

F(x,t)\geq 0,\ (x,t)\in\Omega\times(0,T)
and
a(x)\geq 0,\ x\in\Omega
.

Now we apply the standard mollification procedure (see, e.g.,
Ad
)
and construct the sequences

F_{n}\in C^{\infty}_{0}(\Omega\times(0,T))
and
a_{n}\in C^{\infty}_{0}(\Omega)
,

n\in\mathbb{N}
such that
F_{n}(x,t)\geq 0,\ (x,t)\in\Omega\times(0,T)
and
a_{n}(x)\geq 0,\ x\in\Omega
,
n\in\mathbb{N}
and
\lim_{n\to\infty}\|F_{n}-F\|_{L^{2}(0,T;L^{2}(\Omega))}=0
and

\lim_{n\to\infty}\|a_{n}-a\|_{H^{1}(\Omega)}=0
.
Then Lemma
4
yields the inclusion

u(F_{n},a_{n})\in C([0,T];C^{2}(\overline{\Omega})),\hskip 10.00002ptt^{1-\alpha}\partial_{t}u(F_{n},a_{n})\in C([0,T];C(\overline{\Omega})),\hskip 10.00002ptn\in\mathbb{N}

and thus Lemma
3
ensures the inequalities

u(F_{n},a_{n})(x,t)\geq 0,\ \ (x,t)\in\Omega\times(0,T),\,n\in\mathbb{N}.

(4.16)

Since Theorem
2.1
holds true for the
initial-boundary value problem (
3.3
) with
F
and
a

replaced by
F-F_{n}
and
a-a_{n}
, respectively, we have

\|u(F,a)-u(F_{n},a_{n})\|_{L^{2}(0,T;H^{2}(\Omega))}

\leq C(\|a-a_{n}\|_{H^{1}(\Omega)}+\|F-F_{n}\|_{L^{2}(0,T;L^{2}(\Omega))})\,\to\,0

as
n\to\infty
. Therefore, we can choose a subsequence
m(n)\in\mathbb{N}

such that
u(F,a)(x,t)=\lim_{m(n)\to\infty}u(F_{m(n)},a_{m(n)})(x,t)

for almost all
(x,t)\in\Omega\times(0,T)
.
Then the inequality (
4.16
) leads to the desired result, namely, to the inequality

u(F,a)(x,t)\geq 0
for almost all

(x,t)\in\Omega\times(0,T)
.

III. Third part of the proof.

Let the inequalities
a(x)\geq 0,\ x\in\Omega
and
F(x,t)\geq 0,\ (x,t)\in\Omega\times(0,T)
hold true for
a\in H^{1}(\Omega)
and
F\in L^{2}(0,T;L^{2}(\Omega))
and let
u=u(F,a)\in L^{2}(0,T;H^{2}(\Omega))
is a solution to the problem (
2.4
).
In order to complete the proof of Theorem 2, we have to demonstrate
the non-negativity of the solution without any assumptions
on the sign of the zeroth-order coefficient.

First, the zeroth-order coefficient
b_{0}(x,t)
in the definition (
3.1
) of the operator
-A_{1}
is set to a constant
b_{0}>0
that is
assumed to be sufficiently large.
In this case, the initial-boundary value problem (
2.4
)
can be rewritten as follows:

\left\{\begin{array}[]{rl}&\partial_{t}^{\alpha}(u-a)+A_{1}u=(b_{0}+c(x,t))u+F(x,t),\quad(x,t)\in\Omega\times(0,T),\\
&\partial_{\nu_{A}}u+\sigma u=0\quad\mbox{on $\partial\Omega\times(0,T)$}.\end{array}\right.

(4.17)

In what follows, we choose sufficiently large
b_{0}>0
such that

b_{0}\geq\|c\|_{C(\overline{\Omega}\times[0,T])}
.

In the previous parts of the proof, we already interpreted the solution
u
as
a unique fixed point for the equation (
4.3
).
Now let us construct an appropriate approximating sequence

u_{n}
,
n\in\mathbb{N}
for the fixed point
u
. First we set
u_{0}(x,t):=0
for
(x,t)\in\Omega\times(0,T)
and
u_{1}(x,t)=a(x)\geq 0,\ (x,t)\in\Omega\times(0,T)
. Then
we define a sequence
u_{n+1},\ n\in\mathbb{N}

of solutions to the following initial-boundary value problems with the given
u_{n}
:

\left\{\begin{array}[]{rl}&\partial_{t}^{\alpha}(u_{n+1}-a)+A_{1}u_{n+1}=(b_{0}+c(x,t))u_{n}+F(x,t)\quad\mbox{in }\Omega\times(0,T),\\
&\partial_{\nu_{A}}u_{n+1}+\sigma u_{n+1}=0\quad\mbox{on }\partial\Omega\times(0,T),\\
&u_{n+1}-a\in H_{\alpha}(0,T;L^{2}(\Omega)),\quad n\in\mathbb{N}.\end{array}\right.

(4.18)

First we show that

u_{n}(x,t)\geq 0,\hskip 10.00002pt(x,t)\in\Omega\times(0,T),\hskip 10.00002ptn\in\mathbb{N}.

(4.19)

Indeed, the inequality (
4.19
) holds for
n=1
. Now we assume that

u_{n}(x,t)\geq 0,\ (x,t)\in\Omega\times(0,T)
.
Then
(b_{0}+c(x,t))u_{n}(x,t)+F(x,t)\geq 0,\ (x,t)\in\Omega\times(0,T)
, and thus by the results
established in the second part of the proof of Theorem
4.1
,
we obtain the inequality
u_{n+1}(x,t)\geq 0,\ (x,t)\in\Omega\times(0,T)
.
By the principle of mathematical induction,
the inequality (
4.19
) holds true for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
.

Now we rewrite the problem (
4.18
) as

\partial_{t}^{\alpha}(u_{n+1}(t)-a)+A_{0}u_{n+1}(t)=(Q(t)u_{n+1}-(c(t)+b_{0})u_{n+1})+(b_{0}+c(t))u_{n}+F,

where
A_{0}
and
Q(t)
are defined by (
2.5
) and (
2.14
), respectively.
Next we estimate
w_{n+1}:=u_{n+1}-u_{n}
. By the relation (
4.18
),

w_{n+1}
is a solution to the problem

\left\{\begin{array}[]{rl}&\partial_{t}^{\alpha}w_{n+1}+A_{0}w_{n+1}=(Q(t)w_{n+1}-(c(t)+b_{0})w_{n+1})+(b_{0}+c(x,t))w_{n}\\
&\qquad\qquad\quad\mbox{in }\Omega\times(0,T),\\
&\partial_{\nu_{A}}w_{n+1}+\sigma w_{n+1}=0\quad\mbox{on }\partial\Omega\times(0,T),\\
&w_{n+1}\in H_{\alpha}(0,T;L^{2}(\Omega)),\quad n\in\mathbb{N}.\end{array}\right.

In terms of the operator
K(t)
defined by (
2.7
), acting
similarly to our analysis of the fixed point equation (
4.3
),
we obtain the integral equation

\displaystyle w_{n+1}(t)=\int^{t}_{0}K(t-s)(Qw_{n+1})(s)ds-\int^{t}_{0}K(t-s)(c(s)+b_{0})w_{n+1}(s)ds

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\int^{t}_{0}K(t-s)(b_{0}+c(s))w_{n}(s)ds,\hskip 10.00002pt0<t<T,

which leads to the inequalities

\displaystyle\|A_{0}^{\frac{1}{2}}w_{n+1}(t)\|\leq\int^{t}_{0}\|A_{0}^{\frac{1}{2}}K(t-s)\|\|Q(s)w_{n+1}(s)\|ds

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\int^{t}_{0}\|A_{0}^{\frac{1}{2}}K(t-s)\|\|(c(s)+b_{0})w_{n+1}(s)\|ds+\int^{t}_{0}\|A_{0}^{\frac{1}{2}}K(t-s)\|\|(b_{0}+c(s))w_{n}(s)\|ds

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\int^{t}_{0}(t-s)^{\frac{1}{2}\alpha-1}\|A_{0}^{\frac{1}{2}}w_{n+1}(s)\|ds+C\int^{t}_{0}(t-s)^{\frac{1}{2}\alpha-1}\|A_{0}^{\frac{1}{2}}w_{n}(s)\|ds\hskip 10.00002pt\mbox{for $0<t<T$.}

For their derivation, we used the norm estimates

\|Q(s)w_{n+1}(s)\|\leq C\|w_{n+1}(s)\|_{H^{1}(\Omega)}\leq C\|A_{0}^{\frac{1}{2}}w_{n+1}(s)\|

and

\|(c(s)+b_{0})w_{\ell}(s)\|\leq C\|w_{\ell}(s)\|_{H^{1}(\Omega)}\leq C\|A_{0}^{\frac{1}{2}}w_{\ell}(s)\|,\hskip 10.00002pt\ell=n,n+1

that hold true under the conditions (
1.2
).
Thus we arrive at the integral inequality

\displaystyle\|A_{0}^{\frac{1}{2}}w_{n+1}(t)\|\leq C\int^{t}_{0}(t-s)^{\frac{1}{2}\alpha-1}\|A_{0}^{\frac{1}{2}}w_{n+1}(s)\|ds

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle C\int^{t}_{0}(t-s)^{\frac{1}{2}\alpha-1}\|A_{0}^{\frac{1}{2}}w_{n}(s)\|ds,\hskip 10.00002pt0<t<T.

The generalized Gronwall inequality yields now the estimate

\displaystyle\|A_{0}^{\frac{1}{2}}w_{n+1}(t)\|\leq C\int^{t}_{0}(t-s)^{\frac{1}{2}\alpha-1}\|A_{0}^{\frac{1}{2}}w_{n}(s)\|ds

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle C\int^{t}_{0}(t-s)^{\frac{1}{2}\alpha-1}\left(\int^{s}_{0}(s-\xi)^{\frac{1}{2}\alpha-1}\|A_{0}^{\frac{1}{2}}w_{n}(\xi)\|d\xi\right)ds.

The second term at the right-hand side of the last inequality can be represented as follows:

\displaystyle\int^{t}_{0}(t-s)^{\frac{1}{2}\alpha-1}\left(\int^{s}_{0}(s-\xi)^{\frac{1}{2}\alpha-1}\|A_{0}^{\frac{1}{2}}w_{n}(\xi)\|d\xi\right)ds

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int^{t}_{0}\|A_{0}^{\frac{1}{2}}w_{n}(\xi)\|\left(\int^{t}_{\xi}(t-s)^{\frac{1}{2}\alpha-1}(s-\xi)^{\frac{1}{2}\alpha-1}ds\right)d\xi

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\Gamma\left(\frac{1}{2}\alpha\right)\Gamma\left(\frac{1}{2}\alpha\right)}{\Gamma(\alpha)}\int^{t}_{0}(t-\xi)^{\alpha-1}\|A_{0}^{\frac{1}{2}}w_{n}(\xi)\|d\xi

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\Gamma\left(\frac{1}{2}\alpha\right)^{2}}{\Gamma(\alpha)}T^{\frac{1}{2}\alpha}\int^{t}_{0}(t-s)^{\frac{1}{2}\alpha-1}\|A_{0}^{\frac{1}{2}}w_{n}(s)\|ds.

Thus, we can choose a constant
C>0
depending on
\alpha
and
T
,
such that

\|A_{0}^{\frac{1}{2}}w_{n+1}(t)\|\leq C\int^{t}_{0}(t-\xi)^{\frac{1}{2}\alpha-1}\|A_{0}^{\frac{1}{2}}w_{n}(s)\|ds,\hskip 10.00002pt0<t<T,\,n\in\mathbb{N}.

(4.20)

Recalling that

\int^{t}_{0}(t-s)^{\frac{1}{2}\alpha-1}\eta(s)ds=\Gamma\left(\frac{1}{2}\alpha\right)(J^{\frac{1}{2}\alpha}\eta)(t),\hskip 10.00002ptt>0,

and setting
\eta_{n}(t):=\|A_{0}^{\frac{1}{2}}w_{n}(t)\|
, we can rewrite
(
4.20
) in the form

\eta_{n+1}(t)\leq C\Gamma\left(\frac{1}{2}\alpha\right)(J^{\frac{1}{2}\alpha}\eta_{n})(t),\hskip 10.00002pt0<t<T,\,n\in\mathbb{N}.

(4.21)

Since the Riemann-Liouville integral
J^{\frac{1}{2}\alpha}
preserves the sign and the semi-group property

J^{\beta_{1}}(J^{\beta_{2}}\eta)(t)=J^{\beta_{1}+\beta_{2}}\eta(t)
is valid for
any
\beta_{1},\beta_{2}>0
, applying the inequality (
4.21
)
repeatedly, we obtain the estimates

\displaystyle\eta_{n}(t)\leq\left(C\Gamma\left(\frac{1}{2}\alpha\right)\right)^{n-1}(J^{(n-1)\frac{\alpha}{2}}\eta_{1})(t)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\left(C\Gamma\left(\frac{1}{2}\alpha\right)\right)^{n-1}}{\Gamma\left(\frac{\alpha}{2}(n-1)\right)}\left(\int^{t}_{0}(t-s)^{(n-1)\frac{1}{2}\alpha-1}ds\right)\|A_{0}^{\frac{1}{2}}a\|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\left(C\Gamma\left(\frac{1}{2}\alpha\right)\right)^{n-1}}{\Gamma\left(\frac{\alpha}{2}(n-1)\right)}\frac{t^{(n-1)\frac{1}{2}\alpha}}{(n-1)\frac{1}{2}\alpha}\|A_{0}^{\frac{1}{2}}a\|\leq C_{1}\frac{\left(C\Gamma\left(\frac{1}{2}\alpha\right)T^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}\right)^{n-1}}{\Gamma\left(\frac{\alpha}{2}(n-1)\right)}.

The known asymptotic behavior of the gamma function justifies the relation

\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{\left(C\Gamma\left(\frac{1}{2}\alpha\right)T^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}\right)^{n-1}}{\Gamma\left(\frac{\alpha}{2}(n-1)\right)}=0.

Thus we have proved that the sequence
u_{N}=w_{0}+\cdots+w_{N}
converges to
the solution

u
in
L^{\infty}(0,T;H^{1}(\Omega))
as
N\to\infty
.
Therefore, we can choose a subsequence
m(n)\in\mathbb{N}

such that
\lim_{m(n)\to\infty}u_{m(n)}(x,t)=u(x,t)
for
almost all
(x,t)\in\Omega\times(0,T)
.
This statement in combination with the inequality (
4.19
) means that

u(x,t)\geq 0
for almost all

(x,t)\in\Omega\times(0,T)
. The proof of Theorem
4.1
is completed.

Now let us fix a source function
F=F(x,t)\geq 0,\ (x,t)\in\Omega\times(0,T)
and an initial
value
a\in H^{1}(\Omega)
in the initial-boundary value problem (
2.4
)
and denote
by
u(c,\sigma)=u(c,\sigma)(x,t)
the solution
to the problem (
2.4
) with
the functions
c=c(x,t)
and
\sigma=\sigma(x)
.
Then the following comparison property regarding the coefficients

c
and
\sigma
is valid:

Theorem 4.2

Let
a\in H^{1}(\Omega)
and
F\in L^{2}(\Omega\times(0,T))
and the inequalities
a(x)\geq 0,\ x\in\Omega
and

F(x,t)\geq 0,\ (x,t)\in\Omega\times(0,T)
hold true.

(i) Let
c_{1},c_{2}\in C^{1}([0,T];C^{1}(\overline{\Omega}))\cap C([0,T];C^{2}(\overline{\Omega}))
and

c_{1}(x,t)\geq c_{2}(x,t)
for
(x,t)\in\Omega
.
Then
u(c_{1},\sigma)(x,t)\geq u(c_{2},\sigma)(x,t)
in
\Omega\times(0,T)
.

(ii) Let
c(x,t)<0,\ (x,t)\in\Omega\times(0,T)
and a constant
\sigma_{0}>0
be arbitrary and fixed.
If the smooth functions
\sigma_{1},\sigma_{2}
on
\partial\Omega
satisfy the conditions

\sigma_{2}(x)\geq\sigma_{1}(x)\geq\sigma_{0},\ x\in\partial\Omega,

then the inequality
u(c,\sigma_{1})\geq u(c,\sigma_{2}),\ x\in\Omega\times(0,T)
holds true.

Proof

We start with a proof of the statement (i). Because
a(x)\geq 0,\ x\in\Omega
and
F(x,t)\geq 0,\ (x,t)\in\Omega\times(0,T)
, Theorem
4.1

yields the inequality

u(c_{2},\sigma)(x,t)\geq 0,\ (x,t)\in\Omega\times(0,T)
.
Setting
u(x,t):=u(c_{1},\sigma)(x,t)-u(c_{2},\sigma)(x,t)
for
(x,t)\in\Omega\times(0,T)
, we obtain

\left\{\begin{array}[]{rl}&\partial_{t}^{\alpha}u-\sum_{i,j=1}^{d}\partial_{i}(a_{ij}\partial_{j}u)-\sum_{j=1}^{d}b_{j}\partial_{j}u\\
-&c_{1}(x,t)u=(c_{1}-c_{2})u(c_{2},\sigma)(x,t)\quad\mbox{in }\Omega\times(0,T),\\
&\partial_{\nu_{A}}u+\sigma u=0\quad\mbox{on }\partial\Omega,\\
&u\in H_{\alpha}(0,T;L^{2}(\Omega)).\end{array}\right.

Since
u(c_{2},\sigma)(x,t)\geq 0
and
(c_{1}-c_{2})(x,t)\geq 0
for
(x,t)\in\Omega\times(0,T)
,
Theorem
4.1
leads to the estimate
u(x,t)\geq 0
for
(x,t)\in\Omega\times(0,T)
,
which is equivalent to the inequality
u(c_{1},\sigma)(x,t)\geq u(c_{2},\sigma)(x,t)
for
(x,t)\in\Omega\times(0,T)
and the statement (i) is proved.

Now we proceed to the proof of the statement (ii). Similarly to the procedure applied for the second part of the proof of
Theorem
4.1
, we choose the
sequences
F_{n}\geq 0
,
F_{n}\in C^{\infty}_{0}(\Omega\times(0,T))
and

a_{n}\geq 0
,
a_{n}\in C^{\infty}_{0}(\Omega)
,
n\in\mathbb{N}
such that

F_{n}\to F
in
L^{2}(\Omega\times(0,T))
and

a_{n}\to a
in
H^{1}(\Omega)
. Let
u_{n}
,
v_{n}
be the
solutions to the initial-boundary value problem (
2.4
) with

F=F_{n}
,
a=a_{n}
and with the coefficients

\sigma_{1}
and
\sigma_{2}
in the boundary condition,
respectively. According to Lemma
4
, the inclusions
v_{n},u_{n}\in C(\overline{\Omega}\times[0,T])

and
t^{1-\alpha}\partial_{t}v_{n},\,t^{1-\alpha}\partial_{t}u_{n}\in C([0,T];C(\overline{\Omega}))
,
n\in\mathbb{N}
hold true
and thus Theorem
4.1
yields

v_{n}(x,t)\geq 0,\hskip 10.00002pt(x,t)\in\partial\Omega\times(0,T).

(4.22)

Moreover, the relation

\lim_{n\to\infty}\|u_{n}-u(c,\sigma_{1})\|_{L^{2}(0,T;L^{2}(\Omega))}=\lim_{n\to\infty}\|v_{n}-u(c,\sigma_{2})\|_{L^{2}(0,T;L^{2}(\Omega))}=0

follows from Theorem

.
Let us now define an auxiliary function

.
For this function, the inclusions

(4.24)

hold true. Furthermore, it is a solution to the initial-boundary value problem

\left\{\begin{array}[]{rl}&\partial_{t}^{\alpha}w_{n}+Aw_{n}=0\quad\mbox{in }\Omega\times(0,T),\\
&\partial_{\nu_{A}}w_{n}+\sigma_{1}w_{n}=(\sigma_{2}-\sigma_{1})v_{n}\quad\mbox{on }\partial\Omega\times(0,T),\\
&w_{n}(x,\cdot)\in H_{\alpha}(0,T)\quad\mbox{for almost all }x\in\Omega.\end{array}\right.

(4.25)

The inequalities (
4.22
) and
\sigma_{2}(x)\geq\sigma_{1}(x),\ x\in\partial\Omega
lead to the
estimate

\partial_{\nu_{A}}w_{n}+\sigma_{1}w_{n}\geq 0\hskip 10.00002pt\mbox{on $\partial\Omega\times(0,T)$}.

(4.26)

To finalize the proof of the theorem, a variant of Lemma
3
formulated below will be employed.

Lemma 5

Let the elliptic operator
-A
be defined by (
2.1
) and the conditions
(
1.2
) be satisfied. Moreover, let the inequality
c(x,t)<0
for
x\in\overline{\Omega}
and

0\leq t\leq T
hold true and there exist a constant
\sigma_{0}>0
such that

\sigma(x)\geq\sigma_{0}\hskip 10.00002pt\mbox{for all $x\in\partial\Omega$}.

For
a\in H^{1}(\Omega)
and
F\in L^{2}(\Omega\times(0,T))
, we further assume
that there exists a solution
u\in C([0,T];C^{2}(\overline{\Omega}))

to the initial-boundary value problem

\left\{\begin{array}[]{rl}&\partial_{t}^{\alpha}(u-a)+Au=F\quad\mbox{in $\Omega\times(0,T)$},\\
&\partial_{\nu_{A}}u+\sigma(x)u\geq 0\quad\mbox{on
$\partial\Omega\times(0,T)$},\\
&u(x,\cdot)-a\in H_{\alpha}(0,T)\quad\mbox{for almost all
$x\in\Omega$}\end{array}\right.

that satisfies the inclusion
t^{1-\alpha}\partial_{t}u\in C([0,T];C(\overline{\Omega}))
.
Then the inequalities
F(x,t)\geq 0,\ (x,t)\in\Omega\times(0,T)
and
a(x)\geq 0,\ \Omega
implicate the inequality

u(x,t)\geq 0,\ (x,t)\in\Omega\times(0,T)
.

In the formulation of this lemma, at the expense of the extra condition
\sigma(x)>0

on
\partial\Omega
, we do not assume that
\min\limits_{(x,t)\in\overline{\Omega}\times[0,T]}(-c(x,t))
is sufficiently large. This is the main
difference between the conditions supposed in Lemma
5
and in Lemma
3
.
The proof of Lemma
5
is much simpler compared to the one of Lemma
3
; it will be presented at the end of this section.

Now we complete the proof of Theorem
4.2
.
Since
c(x,t)<0
for
(x,t)\in\Omega\times(0,T)
and
\sigma_{1}(x)\geq\sigma_{0}>0

on
\partial\Omega
and taking into account the conditions (
4.24
) and (
4.26
), we can apply
Lemma
5
to the initial-boundary value problem (
4.25
) and deduce the inequality
w_{n}(x,t)\geq 0,\ (x,t)\in\Omega\times(0,T)
,
that is,

u_{n}(x,t)\geq v_{n}(x,t),\ (x,t)\in\Omega\times(0,T)
for
n\in\mathbb{N}
. Due to the relation (
4.23
),
we can choose
a suitable subsequence of
w_{n},\ n\in\mathbb{N}
and pass to the limit as
n
tends to infinity
thus arriving at the inequality

u(c,\sigma_{1})(x,t)\geq u(c,\sigma_{2})(x,t)
in
\Omega\times(0,T)
.
The proof of Theorem
4.2
is completed.

At this point, let us mention a direction for further research in connection with
the results formulated and proved in this sections. In order to remove the
negativity condition posed on the coefficient
c=c(x,t)
in Theorem
4.2

(ii),
one needs a unique existence result for solutions to the initial-boundary
value problems of type (
2.4
)
with non-zero Robin boundary condition similar to the one formulated in Theorem

2.1
.
There are several works that treat the case of the initial-boundary value
problems with non-homogeneous Dirichlet boundary conditions
(see, e.g.,
Ya18
and the references therein).
However, to the best of the authors’ knowledge, analogous results are not
available for the
initial-boundary value problems with
the non-homogeneous Neumann or Robin boundary conditions. Thus, in Theorem
4.2
(ii),
we assumed the condition

c(x,t)<0,\ (x,t)\in\Omega\times(0,T)
, although our conjecture is that this result holds true for
an arbitrary coefficient
c=c(x,t)
.

We conclude this section with
a proof of Lemma
5
that is simple because in this case we do not need the function

\psi
defined as in (
3.4
).

Proof

First we introduce an auxiliary function as follows:

\widetilde{w}(x,t):=u(x,t)+\varepsilon(1+t^{\alpha}),\hskip 10.00002ptx\in\Omega,\,0<t<T.

The inequalities
c(x,t)<0,\ (x,t)\in\overline{\Omega}\times[0,T]
and
\sigma(x)\geq\sigma_{0}>0,\ x\in\partial\Omega
and the calculations similar to the ones done in the proof of Lemma
3
implicate the inequalities

d_{t}^{\alpha}\widetilde{w}+A\widetilde{w}=F+\varepsilon\Gamma(\alpha+1)-c(x,t)\varepsilon(1+t^{\alpha})>0\hskip 10.00002pt\mbox{in $\Omega\times(0,T)$},

\partial_{\nu_{A}}\widetilde{w}+\sigma\widetilde{w}=\partial_{\nu_{A}}u+\sigma u+\sigma\varepsilon(1+t^{\alpha})\geq\sigma_{0}\varepsilon\hskip 10.00002pt\mbox{on $\partial\Omega\times(0,T)$}

and

\widetilde{w}(x,0)=a(x)+\varepsilon\geq\varepsilon\hskip 10.00002pt\mbox{in $\Omega$}.

Based on these inequalities, the same arguments that were employed after the formula (
3.10
) in the proof of
Lemma
3
readily complete the proof of Lemma
5
.

5
Appendix

In the proof of Lemma
3
that is a basis for all other derivations presented in this paper, we essentially used an auxiliary function that satisfies the conditions (
3.4
). Thus, ensuring existence of such function is an important problem worth for detailed considerations. In this Appendix, we present a solution to this problem.

For the readers’ convenience, we split our existence proof into three parts.

I. First part of the proof.

In this part, we prove the following lemma:

Lemma 6

Let the conditions (
1.2
) be satisfied and the constant

M:=\min_{(x,t)\in\overline{\Omega}\times[0,T]}b_{0}(x,t)>0

is sufficiently large.

Then there exists a constant
\kappa_{1}>0
such that

(A_{1}(t)v,\,v)\geq\kappa_{1}\|v\|_{H^{1}(\Omega)}^{2}

(5.1)

for all
v\in H^{2}(\Omega)
satisfying

\partial_{\nu_{A}}v+\sigma v=0
on
\partial\Omega
for each
t\in[0,T]
.

In particular, Lemma
6
implies that all of the eigenvalues of the operator
A_{0}

defined by (
2.5
) are positive if the constant
c_{0}>0
is sufficiently large.
Henceforth we employ the notation
b=(b_{1},...,b_{d})
.

Proof

By using the conditions (
1.2
) and the boundary condition
\partial_{\nu_{A}}v+\sigma v=0
on
\partial\Omega
,
integration by parts yields

\displaystyle(A_{1}(t)v,v)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\int_{\Omega}\sum_{i,j=1}^{d}\partial_{i}(a_{ij}(x)\partial_{j}v)vdx-\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Omega}\sum_{j=1}^{d}b_{j}(x,t)\partial_{j}(|v|^{2})dx+\int_{\Omega}b_{0}(x,t)|v|^{2}dx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\sum_{i,j=1}^{d}a_{ij}(x)(\partial_{i}v)(\partial_{j}v)dx-\int_{\partial\Omega}(\partial_{\nu_{A}}v)vdS

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Omega}(\mbox{div}\,b)|v|^{2}dx-\frac{1}{2}\int_{\partial\Omega}(b\cdot\nu)|v|^{2}dS+\int_{\Omega}b_{0}(x,t)|v|^{2}dx

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\kappa\int_{\Omega}|\nabla v|^{2}dx+\int_{\Omega}\left(\min_{(x,t)\in\overline{\Omega}\times[0,T]}b_{0}(x,t)-\frac{1}{2}|\mbox{div}\,b|\right)|v|^{2}dx

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\int_{\partial\Omega}\left(\sigma-\frac{1}{2}(b\cdot\nu)\right)|v|^{2}dS

\geq\kappa\int_{\Omega}|\nabla v|^{2}dx+\left(M-\frac{1}{2}\|\mbox{div}\,b\|_{C(\overline{\Omega}\times[0,T])}\right)\int_{\Omega}|v|^{2}dx-C\int_{\Omega}|v|^{2}dS.

(5.2)

Here and henceforth
C>0
,
C_{\varepsilon},C_{\delta}>0
, etc. denote generic
constants which are independent of the function
v
.

By the trace theorem (Theorem 9.4 (pp. 41-42) in
LM
), for

\delta\in(0,\frac{1}{2}]
, there exists
a constant
C_{\delta}>0
such that

\|v\|_{L^{2}(\partial\Omega)}\leq C_{\delta}\|v\|_{H^{\delta+\frac{1}{2}}(\Omega)}\hskip 10.00002pt\mbox{for all
$v\in H^{1}(\Omega)$.}

Now we fix
\delta\in\left(0,\,\frac{1}{2}\right)
.
The interpolation inequality for the Sobolev spaces implicates that for
any
\varepsilon>0
there exists a constant
C_{\varepsilon,\delta}>0
such that the following inequality holds true (see, e.g., Chapter IV in
Ad
or Sections 2.5 and 11 of Chapter 1 in
LM
):

\|v\|_{H^{\delta+\frac{1}{2}}(\Omega)}\leq\varepsilon\|\nabla v\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+C_{\varepsilon,\delta}\|v\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\hskip 10.00002pt\mbox{for all $v\in H^{1}(\Omega)$}.

Therefore, we obtain the estimate

\displaystyle\|v\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\partial\Omega)}\leq(\varepsilon C_{\delta}\|\nabla v\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+C_{\delta}C_{\varepsilon,\delta}\|v\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)})^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 2(\varepsilon C_{\delta})^{2}\|\nabla v\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+2(C_{\delta}C_{\varepsilon,\delta})^{2}\|v\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}

for all
v\in H^{1}(\Omega)
.
Substituting this inequality into (
5.2
), we obtain

\displaystyle(\kappa-2C(\varepsilon C_{\delta})^{2})\|\nabla v\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle(M-\frac{1}{2}\|\mbox{div}\,b\|_{C(\overline{\Omega}\times[0,T])}-2(CC_{\delta}C_{\varepsilon,\delta})^{2})\|v\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}\leq(A_{1}(t)v,v).

Choosing a sufficiently small
\varepsilon>0
such that

\kappa-2C(\varepsilon C_{\delta})^{2}>0
and a sufficiently large
M>0

such that

M>\frac{1}{2}\|\mbox{div}\,b\|_{C(\overline{\Omega}\times[0,T])}+2(CC_{\delta}C_{\varepsilon,\delta})^{2}

completes the proof of Lemma
6
.

II. Second part of the proof.

Due to the estimate (
5.1
), we can apply Theorem 3.2 (p. 137) in
LU
that implicates
existence of a constant
\theta\in(0,1)
such that for each

t\in[0,T]
, a solution
\psi(\cdot,t)\in C^{2+\theta}(\overline{\Omega})
to the problem
(
3.4
) exists uniquely, where
C^{2+\theta}(\overline{\Omega})
is the Schauder space
defined in the proof of Lemma
4
in Section
4
.

Now we introduce an auxiliary function

\eta(t):=\|\psi(\cdot,t)\|_{C^{2+\theta}(\overline{\Omega})},\hskip 10.00002pt0\leq t\leq T.

(5.3)

Because of the inclusion
\psi(\cdot,t)\in C^{2+\theta}(\overline{\Omega})
,
the value of the function
\eta(t)
is finite for each
t\in[0,T]
.

Furthermore, for an arbitrary
G\in C^{\theta}(\overline{\Omega})
,
there exists a unique solution
w=w(\cdot,t)
to the problem

\left\{\begin{array}[]{rl}&A_{1}(t)w=G\quad\mbox{in $\Omega$},\\
&\partial_{\nu_{A}}w+\sigma w=0\quad\mbox{on $\partial\Omega$}\end{array}\right.

(5.4)

for each
t\in[0,T]
.

Now we prove that
for each
t\in[0,T]
there exists a constant
C_{t}>0
such that

\|w(\cdot,t)\|_{C^{2+\theta}(\overline{\Omega})}\leq C_{t}\|G\|_{C^{\theta}(\overline{\Omega})}

(5.5)

for all solutions
w
of the problem (
5.4
). In the inequality (
5.5
), the constant
C_{t}>0
depends on the norms

\|a_{ij}\|_{C^{1}(\overline{\Omega})}
,
1\leq i,j\leq d
,

\|b_{k}\|_{C([0,T];C^{2}(\overline{\Omega}))}
,
0\leq k\leq d
of the coefficients, but not on the coefficients by themselves.

Indeed, for each
t\in[0,T]
, the inequality

\|w(\cdot,t)\|_{C^{2+\theta}(\overline{\Omega})}\leq C_{t}(\|G\|_{C^{\theta}(\overline{\Omega})}+\|w(\cdot,t)\|_{C(\overline{\Omega})})

(5.6)

holds true (see, e.g., the formula (3.7) on p. 137 in
LU
).
To obtain the desired estimate we have to eliminate the term

\|w(\cdot,t)\|_{C(\overline{\Omega})}

on the right-hand side of the last inequality.
This can be done by the standard compactness-uniqueness arguments.
More precisely,
let us assume that (
5.5
) does not hold. Then there exist the sequences
w_{n}\in C^{2+\theta}(\overline{\Omega}),\ n\in\mathbb{N}
and
G_{n}\in C^{\theta}(\overline{\Omega}),\ n\in\mathbb{N}
such that
\|w_{n}\|_{C^{2+\theta}(\overline{\Omega})}=1
and

\lim_{n\to\infty}\|G_{n}\|_{C^{\theta}(\overline{\Omega})}=0
.
By the Ascoli-Arzelà theorem, we can extract a subsequence
w_{k(n)}

from the sequence
w_{n}
such that

w_{k(n)}\longrightarrow\widetilde{w}
in
C(\overline{\Omega})
as
n\to\infty
.
Applying the estimation (
5.6
) to the equation

A_{1}(t)(w_{k(n)}-w_{k(m)})=G_{k(n)}-G_{k(m)}\hskip 10.00002pt\mbox{in $\Omega$}

equipped with the homogeneous boundary condition
\partial_{\nu_{A}}(w_{k(n)}-w_{k(m)})+\sigma(w_{k(n)}-w_{k(m)})=0
on
\partial\Omega
, we we arrive at the relation

\displaystyle\|w_{k(n)}-w_{k(m)}\|_{C^{2+\theta}(\overline{\Omega})}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C_{t}(\|G_{k(n)}-G_{k(m)}\|_{C^{\theta}(\overline{\Omega})}+\|w_{k(n)}-w_{k(m)}\|_{C(\overline{\Omega})})\,\to 0

as
n,m\to\infty
. Hence, there exists a function
w_{0}\in C^{2+\theta}(\overline{\Omega})
such that

w_{k(n)}\to w_{0}
in
C^{2+\theta}(\overline{\Omega})
. Moreover, we obtain the relations

\|w_{0}\|_{C^{2+\theta}(\overline{\Omega})}=\lim_{n\to\infty}\|w_{k(n)}\|_{C^{2+\theta}(\overline{\Omega})}=1

and
G_{k(n)}=A_{1}(t)w_{k(n)}\to A_{1}(t)w_{0}
in

C^{\theta}(\overline{\Omega})
.

Since
\lim_{n\to\infty}\|G_{k(n)}\|_{C^{\theta}(\overline{\Omega})}=0
, we obtain

A_{1}(t)w_{0}=0
in
\Omega
with
\partial_{\nu_{A}}w_{0}+\sigma w_{0}=0
on

\partial\Omega
. Then Lemma
6
yields
w_{0}(x,t)=0
in
\Omega
that
contradicts the relation
\|w_{0}\|_{C^{2+\theta}(\overline{\Omega})}=1
that we established above. The obtained contradiction implicates the desired norm estimate
(
5.5
).

III. Third part of the proof.

The last missing detail of the proof is the inclusion
\psi\in C^{1}([0,T];C^{2}(\overline{\Omega}))
for the function
\psi
constructed in the previous part of the proof.

To show this inclusion, we first verify that for an arbitrarily but fixed
t\in[0,T]
the function

d(x,s):=\psi(x,t)-\psi(x,s)
satisfies the equations

\left\{\begin{array}[]{rl}&-A_{1}(t)d(\cdot,s)=(b_{0}(t)-b_{0}(s))\psi(\cdot,s)\\
-&\sum_{j=1}^{d}(b_{j}(t)-b_{j}(s))\partial_{j}\psi(\cdot,s)\quad\mbox{in $\Omega$ for $0\leq s,t\leq T$},\\
&\partial_{\nu_{A}}d+\sigma d=0\quad\mbox{on $\partial\Omega$, $\,\,$
$0\leq s,t\leq T$}.\end{array}\right.

(5.7)

For an arbitrarily but fixed
\delta>0
we set

I_{\delta,t}:=[0,T]\cap\{s;\,|t-s|\leq\delta\}
.

Applying the relation (
5.3
) and the estimate (
5.5
) to the solution
d
of (
5.7
) yields

\displaystyle\|d(\cdot,s)\|_{C^{2+\theta}(\overline{\Omega})}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\left(\left\|\sum_{j=1}^{d}(b_{j}(t)-b_{j}(s))\partial_{j}\psi(\cdot,s)\right\|_{C^{\theta}(\overline{\Omega})}+\|(b_{0}(t)-b_{0}(s))\psi(\cdot,s)\|_{C^{\theta}(\overline{\Omega})}\right)

\leq C\sum_{j=0}^{d}\|b_{j}(s)-b_{j}(t)\|_{C^{1}(\overline{\Omega})}\eta(s)\leq C\max_{0\leq j\leq d}\|b_{j}(s)-b_{j}(t)\|_{C^{1}(\overline{\Omega})}\,\sup_{s\in I_{\delta,t}}\eta(s).

(5.8)

For the function

h(\delta):=\max_{0\leq j\leq d}\sup_{|s-t|\leq\delta}\|b_{j}(s)-b_{j}(t)\|_{C^{1}(\overline{\Omega})},

the inclusions
b_{j}\in C([0,T];C^{1}(\overline{\Omega}))
,
0\leq j\leq d
imply

\lim_{\delta\downarrow 0}h(\delta)=0
.

Now we rewrite the estimate (
5.8
) in terms of the function
\eta
defined by (
5.3
) as

|\eta(s)-\eta(t)|\leq Ch(\delta)\sup_{s\in I_{\delta,t}}\eta(s)\hskip 10.00002pt\mbox{for $s\in I_{\delta,t}$},

and thus obtain the inequality

\eta(s)\leq\eta(t)+Ch(\delta)\sup_{s\in I_{\delta,t}}\eta(s)\hskip 10.00002pt\mbox{for $s\in I_{\delta,t}$}.

Choosing
\delta:=\delta(t)>0
sufficiently small,
for a given
t\in[0,T]
, the estimate

\sup_{s\in I_{\delta(t),t}}\eta(s)\leq C_{1}\eta(t)
holds true.
Varying
t\in[0,T]
, we now choose a finite number of the intervals

I_{\delta(t),t}
that cover the whole interval
[0,T]
and thus obtain the norm estimate

\|\psi\|_{L^{\infty}(0,T;C^{2+\theta}(\overline{\Omega}))}\leq C_{2}

(5.9)

with some constant
C_{2}>0
.

For
s\in I_{\delta(t),t}
, substitution of (
5.9
) into (
5.8
) yields the estimate

\|d(\cdot,s)\|_{C^{2+\theta}(\overline{\Omega}))}=\|\psi(\cdot,t)-\psi(\cdot,s)\|_{C^{2+\theta}(\overline{\Omega}))}\leq Ch(\delta)C_{2}.

Consequently,

\lim_{s\to t}\|\psi(\cdot,s)-\psi(\cdot,t)\|_{C^{2+\theta}(\overline{\Omega})}=0
and we have shown the inclusion

\psi\in C([0,T];C^{2+\theta}(\overline{\Omega})).

(5.10)

To complete the proof, the now verify the inclusion
\psi\in C^{1}([0,T];C^{2+\theta}(\overline{\Omega}))
.
Since
-A_{1}(\xi)\psi(x,\xi)=1
in
\Omega
,
differentiating this formula with respect to
\xi

leads to the representation

\sum_{j=1}^{d}\partial_{i}(a_{ij}(x)\partial_{j}\partial_{\xi}\psi(x,\xi))+\sum_{j=1}^{d}b_{j}(\xi)\partial_{j}\partial_{\xi}\psi(x,\xi)-b_{0}(\xi)\partial_{\xi}\psi(x,\xi)

(5.11)

=-\sum_{j=1}^{d}\partial_{\xi}b_{j}(\xi)\partial_{j}\psi(x,\xi)+(\partial_{\xi}b_{0})(\xi)\psi(x,\xi)\hskip 10.00002pt\mbox{in $\Omega$}.

By subtracting the equation (
5.11
) with

\xi=s
from the one with
\xi=t
, we deduce that the function

d_{1}(x,s):=(\partial_{t}\psi)(x,t)-(\partial_{t}\psi)(x,s)
satisfies the relation

-A_{1}(t)d_{1}(x,s)

(5.12)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\biggl[-\sum_{i,j=1}^{d}(b_{j}(t)-b_{j}(s))\partial_{j}\partial_{s}\psi(x,s)+(b_{0}(t)-b_{0}(s))\partial_{s}\psi(x,s)

\displaystyle-

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{d}(\partial_{t}b_{j}(t)-\partial_{s}b_{j}(s))\partial_{j}\psi(x,s)+(\partial_{t}b_{0}(t)-\partial_{s}b_{0}(s))\psi(x,s)\biggr]

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\left[-\sum_{j=1}^{d}\partial_{t}b_{j}(t)(\partial_{j}\psi(x,t)-\partial_{j}\psi(x,s))+\partial_{t}b_{0}(t)(\psi(x,t)-\psi(x,s))\right]

\displaystyle=:

\displaystyle H_{1}(x,t,s)+H_{2}(x,t,s)\hskip 10.00002pt\mbox{in $\Omega$}

and the boundary condition
\partial_{\nu_{A}}d_{1}+\sigma d_{1}=0
on
\partial\Omega
for all

s,t\in[0,T]
.
Thus, the inclusion

\psi\in C^{1}([0,T];C^{2+\theta}(\overline{\Omega}))
will follow from the relation
\lim_{s\to t}\|d_{1}(\cdot,s)\|_{C^{2+\theta}(\overline{\Omega})}=0
that we now prove.
To this end, by applying Theorem 3.2 (p. 137) in
LU
to the equation (
5.12
),
it suffices to prove that

\lim_{s\to t}\|H_{\ell}(\cdot,t,s)\|_{C^{\theta}(\overline{\Omega})}=0,\hskip 10.00002pt\ell=1,2.

(5.13)

Applying Theorem 3.2 in
LU
to the equation (
5.11
), in view of the
regularity conditions (
1.2
) and the equation (
5.11
), we obtain
the estimates

\|\partial_{t}\psi(\cdot,t)\|_{C^{2+\theta}(\overline{\Omega})}

(5.14)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\left(\left\|\sum_{j=1}^{d}(\partial_{t}b_{j})(\cdot,t)\partial_{j}\psi(\cdot,t)\right\|_{C^{\theta}(\overline{\Omega})}+\|(\partial_{t}b_{0})(\cdot,t)\psi(\cdot,t)\|_{C^{\theta}(\overline{\Omega})}\right)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\sum_{j=0}^{d}\|b_{j}(\cdot,t)\|_{C^{1}([0,T];C^{1}(\overline{\Omega}))}\|\psi(\cdot,t)\|_{C^{2+\theta}(\overline{\Omega})}\leq C_{3}\hskip 10.00002pt\mbox{for $0\leq t\leq T$.}

The inequalities (
5.9
) and (
5.14
) lead to the norm estimate

\|H_{1}(\cdot,t,s)\|_{C^{\theta}(\overline{\Omega})}\leq C_{4}\sum_{k=0}^{1}\sum_{j=0}^{d}\|(\partial_{t}^{k}b_{j})(\cdot,t)-(\partial_{t}^{k}b_{j})(\cdot,s)\|_{C^{1}(\overline{\Omega})}.

(5.15)

By employing the arguments similar to those used above, the estimate

\|H_{2}(\cdot,t,s)\|_{C^{\theta}(\overline{\Omega})}\leq C_{4}\sum_{j=0}^{d}\|\partial_{t}b_{j}\|_{C([0,T];C^{1}(\overline{\Omega}))}\sum_{k=0}^{1}\|\nabla^{k}\psi(\cdot,t)-\nabla^{k}\psi(\cdot,s)\|_{C^{\theta}(\overline{\Omega})}

(5.16)

can be derived.
Since
\partial_{t}^{k}b_{j}\in C([0,T];C^{1}(\overline{\Omega}))
for
k=0,1
and

0\leq j\leq d
by the conditions (
1.2
) and
\nabla^{k}\psi\in C([0,T];C^{1+\theta}(\overline{\Omega}))
with
k=0,1
by the inclusion (
5.10
), the relation
\lim_{s\to t}\|H_{\ell}(\cdot,t,s)\|_{C^{\theta}(\overline{\Omega})}=0
with

\ell=1,2
immediately follows from the norm estimates (
5.15
) and (
5.16
). As mentioned above, this completes the proof
of existence of a function satisfying the conditions (
3.4
).

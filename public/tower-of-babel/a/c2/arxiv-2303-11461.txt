Unitarity of the SoV Transform for 𝐒𝐋 ⁡ ( 𝟐 , ℂ ) \boldsymbol{\mathrm{SL}(2,\mathbb{C})} Spin Chains
Alexander N. MANASHOV

Abstract
We prove the unitarity of the separation of variables transform for SL ⁡ ( 2 , ℂ ) \mathrm{SL}(2,\mathbb{C}) spin chains by a method based on the use of Gustafson integrals.

Introduction

Theory of quantum integrable models is an important part of modern theoretical physics. The solution of such models relies on the Quantum
inverse scattering method (QISM) which includes such techniques as the algebraic Bethe ansatz (ABA)
[
50

and separation of
variables (SoV)
[

{\mathbb{H}}_{\rm SoV}

Kharchev and Lebedev

2
\boldsymbol{\mathrm{SL}(2,\mathbb{C})}
spin chains

Spin chains are quantum mechanical systems whose dynamical variables are spin generators. We consider models with
spin generators belonging to the unitary continuous principal series representation,
\mathrm{T}^{(s_{k},\bar{s}_{k})}
, of the unimodular group
of complex two by two matrices. Namely, each site of the chain is equipped with two sets of generators, holomorphic (
S^{\alpha}
) and
anti-holomorphic ones
\big(\bar{S}^{\alpha}\big)
,

\displaystyle S^{-}_{k}=-\partial_{z_{k}},\qquad

\displaystyle S^{0}_{k}=z_{k}\partial_{z_{k}}+s_{k},\qquad

\displaystyle S^{+}_{k}=z_{k}^{2}\partial_{z_{k}}+2s_{k}z_{k},

\displaystyle\bar{S}^{-}_{k}=-\partial_{\bar{z}_{k}},\qquad

\displaystyle\bar{S}^{0}_{k}=\bar{z}_{k}\partial_{\bar{z}_{k}}+\bar{s}_{k},\qquad

\displaystyle\bar{S}^{+}_{k}=\bar{z}_{k}^{2}\partial_{\bar{z}_{k}}+2\bar{s}_{k}\bar{z}_{k}.

The generators
S^{\alpha}_{k}\big(\bar{S}^{\alpha}_{k}\big)
satisfy the standard
\mathfrak{sl}(2)
commutation relations, while the generators at different
sites and holomorphic and anti-holomorphic generators commute,
\big[S^{\alpha}_{k},\bar{S}^{\alpha^{\prime}}_{k}\big]=0
. The parameters
s_{k}
,
\bar{s}_{k}

specifying the representation take the form
[
21
]

\displaystyle s_{k}=\frac{1+n_{k}}{2}+{\rm i}\rho_{k},\qquad\bar{s}_{k}=\frac{1-n_{k}}{2}+{\rm i}\rho_{k},

where
n_{k}
is an integer or half-integer number and
\rho_{k}
is real, so that

\displaystyle s_{k}+\bar{s}_{k}^{*}=1\qquad\text{and}\qquad s_{k}-\bar{s}_{k}=n_{k}\in\mathbb{Z}/2.

The later condition comes from the requirement for the finite group transformations to be well defined while the former one guarantees the
unitary character of transformations and anti-hermiticity of
the generators,
\big(S_{k}^{\alpha}\big)^{\dagger}=-\bar{S}_{k}^{\alpha}
.

The Hilbert space of the model is given by the direct product of the Hilbert spaces at each node. For a chain of length
N
,
{\mathbb{H}}_{N}=\bigotimes_{k=1}^{N}\mathcal{H}_{k}
, where
\mathcal{H}_{k}=L_{2}(\mathbb{C})
.

In the QISM
[
34
,
49
,
50
,
52
]
, the dynamics of the model is determined by a family of mutually commuting
operators.
Namely, one defines the so-called
L
-operators,

\displaystyle L_{k}(u)=u+{\rm i}\begin{pmatrix}S_{k}^{0}&S_{k}^{-}\vskip 2.84526pt\\
S_{k}^{+}&-S_{0}^{-}\end{pmatrix},\qquad\bar{L}_{k}(\bar{u})=\bar{u}+{\rm i}\begin{pmatrix}\bar{S}_{k}^{0}&\bar{S}_{k}^{-}\vskip 2.84526pt\\
\bar{S}_{k}^{+}&-\bar{S}_{0}^{-}\end{pmatrix},

which are the basic building blocks in the QISM. The complex variables
u
,
\bar{u}
are called spectral parameters. The next important
object – a monodromy matrix – is given by the product of
L
operators

\displaystyle T_{N}(u)=L_{1}(u+\xi_{1})L_{2}(u+\xi_{2})\cdots L_{N}(u+\xi_{N}),

\displaystyle\bar{T}_{N}(\bar{u})=\bar{L}_{1}\big(\bar{u}+\bar{\xi}_{1}\big)\bar{L}_{2}\big(\bar{u}+\bar{\xi}_{2}\big)\cdots\bar{L}_{N}\big(\bar{u}+\bar{\xi}_{N}\big),

(2.1)

where
\xi_{k}
,
\bar{\xi}_{k}
are the so-called impurity parameters.
3
3

3

As it can already be noticed any formula in the holomorphic sector has its exact copy in the anti-holomorphic one. Therefore, from now on we write explicitly only holomorphic formulae tacitly implying its anti-holomorphic counterparts.
The entries of the monodromy matrix,

\displaystyle T_{N}(u)=\begin{pmatrix}A_{N}(u)&B_{N}(u)\\
C_{N}(u)&D_{N}(u)\end{pmatrix},

are polynomials in
u
with the operator valued coefficients, e.g.,

\displaystyle A_{N}(u)=u^{N}+u^{N-1}\big({\rm i}S^{0}+\Xi\big)+\sum_{k=2}^{N}u^{N-k}a_{k},

\displaystyle B_{N}(u)=u^{N-1}{\rm i}S^{-}+\sum_{k=2}^{N}u^{N-k}b_{k},

(2.2)

where
\Xi=\sum_{k=1}^{N}\xi_{k}
and
S^{0}
,
S^{-}
are the total generators,

\displaystyle S^{\alpha}=S^{\alpha}_{1}+\cdots+S^{\alpha}_{N}.

The entries of the monodromy matrix form commuting operator families
[
18
,
50
]

\displaystyle[A_{N}(u),A_{N}(v)]=[B_{N}(u),B_{N}(v)]=[C_{N}(u),C_{N}(v)]=[D_{N}(u),D_{N}(v)]=0.

In particular, each entry commutes with the corresponding total generator,
S^{\alpha}
,

\displaystyle\big[S^{0},A_{N}(u)\big]=\big[S^{0},D_{N}(u)\big]=0\qquad\text{and}\qquad[S^{-},B_{N}(u)]=\big[S^{+},C_{N}(u)\big]=0.

The same equations hold for the anti-holomorphic operators
\bar{A}_{N}
,
\bar{B}_{N}
,
\bar{C}_{N}
,
\bar{D}_{N}
and, of course, the holomorphic and
anti-holomorphic operators commute. Moreover it can be checked that if the impurity parameters satisfy the constraint
\bar{\xi}_{k}=\xi_{k}^{*}

for all
k
, the following relations between holomorphic and anti-holomorphic operators hold:

\displaystyle(A_{N}(u))^{\dagger}=\bar{A}_{N}(u^{*}),\qquad(B_{N}(u))^{\dagger}=\bar{B}_{N}(u^{*}),

etc. This ensures that the operators
a_{k}
and
\bar{a}_{k}
in the expansion of
A_{N}(u)
, (
2.2
), and
\bar{A}_{N}(u)
, are adjoint
to each other
a_{k}^{\dagger}=\bar{a}_{k}

(
b_{k}^{\dagger}=\bar{b}_{k}
etc.
)
.

The commutativity of the operators
A_{N}(u)
,
B_{N}(u)
,
C_{N}(u)
,
D_{N}(u)
implies that the following families of self-adjoint operators:

\displaystyle\mathfrak{A}_{N}=\big\{{\rm i}S^{0},{\rm i}\bar{S}^{0},a_{k}+\bar{a}_{k},{\rm i}(a_{k}-\bar{a}_{k}),k=2,\dots,N\big\},

\displaystyle\mathfrak{B}_{N}=\big\{{\rm i}S^{-},{\rm i}\bar{S}^{-},b_{k}+\bar{b}_{k},{\rm i}(b_{k}-\bar{b}_{k}),k=2,\dots,N\big\},

(and similarly for others) are commutative and can be diagonalized simultaneously.

4

4

the condition
{\rm i}\big(\xi_{k}-\bar{\xi}_{k}\big)=r_{k}
, where
r_{k}
are half-integers.
The corresponding eigenfunctions provide a convenient basis –
Sklyanin’s representation of Separated Variables (SoV) – for the analysis of spin chain models

The operators
B_{N}
and
C_{N}

A_{N}
and

for the homogeneous chain were constructed in

]
and later on for the operator

A_{N}

3.1
\boldsymbol{B_{N}}

Let

\displaystyle\Lambda_{n}(x|\gamma)(z_{1},\dots,z_{n}|w_{1},\dots,w_{n-1})=\prod_{k=1}^{n-1}D_{\gamma_{2k-1}-{\rm i}x}(z_{k}-w_{k})D_{\gamma_{2k}+{\rm i}x}(z_{k+1}-w_{k}),

\displaystyle\gamma_{k}=\frac{1}{2}+\frac{r_{k}}{2}+{\rm i}\sigma_{k},\qquad\bar{\gamma}_{k}=\frac{1}{2}-\frac{r_{k}}{2}+{\rm i}\sigma_{k},\qquad x=\frac{{\rm i}m}{2}+\nu,\qquad\bar{x}=-\frac{{\rm i}m}{2}+\nu.

6
Summary

In this work, we consider a generic inhomogeneous
\mathrm{SL}(2,\mathbb{C})
spin chain with impurities and construct the eigenfunctions of
the
B
and
A
entries of the monodromy matrix. We prove the unitarity of the SoV transform associated with these systems or,
equivalently, the completeness of the corresponding systems in the Hilbert space of the model. Namely, the following identities hold in the
sense of distributions:

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\int_{\mathscr{D}^{\sigma}_{N-1}}\Psi^{(N)}_{p,x}(z)\big(\Psi^{(N)}_{p,x}(z^{\prime})\big)^{\dagger}{\rm d}^{2}p{\rm d}\mu_{N}^{B}(x)=\prod_{k=1}^{N}\delta^{2}(z_{k}-z^{\prime}_{k}),

\displaystyle\int_{\mathscr{D}^{\sigma}_{N}}\Phi^{(N)}_{x}(z)\big(\Phi^{(N)}_{x}(z^{\prime})\big)^{\dagger}{\rm d}\mu_{N}^{A}(x)=\prod_{k=1}^{N}\delta^{2}(z_{k}-z^{\prime}_{k}),

and

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{C}^{N}}\Psi^{(N)}_{p,x}(z)\big(\Psi^{(N)}_{p^{\prime},x^{\prime}}(z)\big)^{\dagger}\prod_{k=1}^{N}{\rm d}^{2}z_{k}=\big(\mu^{B}_{N}(x)\big)^{-1}\delta^{2}(p-p^{\prime})\delta^{N-1}(x,x^{\prime}),

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{C}^{N}}\Phi^{(N)}_{x}(z)\big(\Phi^{(N)}_{x^{\prime}}(z)\big)^{\dagger}\prod_{k=1}^{N}{\rm d}^{2}z_{k}=\big(\mu^{A}_{N}(x)\big)^{-1}\delta^{N}(x,x^{\prime}),

where

\displaystyle\delta^{N}(x,x^{\prime})=\frac{1}{N!}\sum_{w\in S_{N}}\delta^{N}(x^{\prime}-wx),\qquad wx=(x_{w_{1}},\dots,x_{w_{N}})

and

\delta^{N}(x^{\prime}-x)=\prod_{k=1}^{N}\delta^{2}(x^{\prime}_{k}-x_{k}),\qquad\delta^{2}(x^{\prime}-x)=\delta_{nn^{\prime}}\delta(\nu-\nu^{\prime}).

The method relies heavily on the use of multidimensional Mellin–Barnes integrals which generalize integrals calculated by
R.A. Gustafson
[
26
]
. The attractive feature of our approach is that it does not depends on the details of the spin chain
such as spins and inhomogeneity parameters. We believe that this technique can also be used to prove the unitarity of the SoV transform for
the open
\mathrm{SL}(2,\mathbb{C})
spin chain.

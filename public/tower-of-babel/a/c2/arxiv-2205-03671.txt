An abstract nonlinear evolution problem: Energy decay
Paul A. Ogbiyele

Abstract
. In this paper, we consider energy decay estimates for the following nonlinear evolution problem [ P ( u t ( t ) ) ] t + A u ( t ) + B ( t , x , u t ( t ) ) = 0 , t ∈ J = ( 0 , ∞ ) , \small\begin{split}[P(u_{t}(t))]_{t}+Au(t)+B(t,x,u_{t}(t))=0,\hskip 9.24994ptt\in J=(0,\infty),\end{split} under suitable assumptions on the self-adjoint operators P P and B B and the divergence operator A A . The work extends the earlier work of Marcati, Nakao, Levin and Pucci on abstract evolution equations to the case with possibly nonlinear damping. The technique also differs from earlier techniques. Here, we make use of a modified perturbed energy technique.

1.
Introduction

Nakao
[
7
]

u^{\prime\prime}+Bu^{\prime}(t)+A(u)=f(t)

Bu^{\prime}(t)+A(u)=f(t),

[

B

The question of asymptotic stability was also studied by Pucci and Serrin in
[
15
]
, where they considered stability results for dissipative wave systems of the non-autonomous type. They extended the results in
[
14
]
by considering an abstract evolution equation of the form

[P(u^{\prime}(t))]^{\prime}+A(u(t))+Q(t,u^{\prime}(t))+F(u(t))=0,

where
A
,
F
,
P
and
Q
are nonlinear operators defined on appropriate Banach spaces. Using the technique introduced in
[
15
]
, they obtained asymptotic stability results that extend the work of
[
4
,
7
]
. The reader is referred to
[
1
,
3
,
5
,
10
,
17
]
for other results on stability/energy decay of solutions to abstract evolution equations.
In this paper, we consider abstract nonlinear evolution problem of the form

\begin{split}&[P(u_{t}(t))]_{t}+Au(t)+B(t,x,u_{t}(t))=0,\quad t>0,\;\;x\in\mathbb{R}^{n},\end{split}

(1.1)

where the operators

,

[

]

2.
Preliminaries

Let
X
and
V
be real Banach spaces with
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{X}
and
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{V}
the natural dual mapping pairings, and
X^{\prime}
,
V^{\prime}
their respective duals. In addition, we assume that there exists a Banach space
W
satisfying
X\hookrightarrow W\hookrightarrow V
continuously such that

\|u\|_{W}\leq k\|u\|_{X},

for a positive constant
k
.

We assume that
P:V\rightarrow V^{\prime}
is non-negative definite and symmetric. In addition,
P
and
B
satisfy the following conditions:

(
A_{1}
)

\mathcal{P}^{*}(v)=\langle P(v),v\rangle_{V}-\mathcal{P}(v)\geq 0\quad\text{in}\quad V
, where the function
\mathcal{P^{*}}
represents the Hamiltonian of
\mathcal{P}
in classical mechanics, and there exist constants
p>0
,
k_{0}>0
and
\ell>1
such that

(p+1)\langle P(v),v\rangle-p\mathcal{P}(v)\leq k_{0}\|P(v)\|^{\ell^{\prime}}_{V^{\prime}}\quad\text{for all}\quad v\in V.

(
A_{2}
)

|\langle P(u_{t}),\phi\rangle|\leq\|P(u_{t})\|_{W^{\prime}}\|\phi\|_{W}

and in addition there exist nonnegative locally bounded functions
\delta(t)
and
j(t)
such that for positive constants
\ell,m
satisfying
\ell\leq m
, we have

\|P(u_{t})\|_{W^{\prime}}\leq(\delta(t))^{\frac{1}{\ell}}\|P(u_{t})\|_{V^{\prime}}

and

\|P(v)\|^{\ell^{\prime}}_{V^{\prime}}\leq j(t)\langle B(t,x,u_{t}),u_{t}\rangle^{\ell/m}.

(
A_{3}
)

|\langle B(t,x,u_{t}),\phi\rangle|\leq\|B(t,x,u_{t})\|_{W^{\prime}}\|\phi\|_{W}

and
B\in C(S,W^{\prime})
where
S
is a given subset of
J\times\Omega\times V
, and there exists a non-negative locally bounded function
\eta(t)
such that

\|B(t,x,u_{t})\|_{W^{\prime}}\leq(\eta(t))^{\frac{1}{m}}\langle B(t,x,u_{t}),u_{t}\rangle^{\frac{1}{m^{\prime}}}.

To define the energy function
\mathcal{E}(u)
associated to (
1.1
), we introduce the real-valued
C^{1}
potential functions
\mathcal{A}:X\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
,
\mathcal{P}:V\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
, where the operator
A:X\rightarrow W
is the Frechét derivative of
\mathcal{A}
with respect to
u
and
\mathcal{P}
is the Frechét derivative of
P
. In addition
\mathcal{A}(0)=\mathcal{P}(0)=0
.

For the operator
A
, we have the following assumption:

(
A_{4}
)

\mathcal{A}(u)\geq 0
and there exist positive constants
c_{0}
and
q\leq\ell
such that

q\mathcal{A}(u)\leq\langle A(u),u\rangle\quad\text{and}\quad\|u\|^{q}_{X}\leq c_{0}\mathcal{A}(u)\quad\forall u\in X.

Define
K=C(J:X)\cap C^{1}(J:V)
, then by a strong solution
u\in K
of (
1.1
), we mean a function
u\in K
with
u_{0}\in X
,
v_{0}\in V
that satisfies

(a)

u\in K
and
(t,x,u_{t}(t))\in S
for a.a
\;t\in J
,

(b)

the following distribution relation

\langle P(u_{t}(s)),\phi(s)\rangle_{V}\Bigr|^{t}_{0}=\int^{t}_{0}\Bigl[\langle P(u_{t}(s)),\phi_{t}(s)\rangle_{V}-\langle A(u(s)),\phi(s)\rangle_{X}-\langle B(s,x,u_{t}(s)),\phi(s)\rangle_{W}\Bigr]ds

(2.1)

holds for all
\phi\in K
,
t\in J
with the associated total energy function

\mathcal{E}(t)=\mathcal{P}^{*}(u_{t}(t))+\mathcal{A}(u(t)),

(2.2)

satisfying the classical conservation balance

\mathcal{E}(t)=\mathcal{E}(0)-\int^{t}_{0}\langle B(s,x,u_{t}(s)),u_{t}(s)\rangle ds\quad\text{for}\quad t\in J.

Note: When
S=J\times Y
and
B:S\rightarrow Y^{\prime}
where
Y
is a Banach space with natural pairing
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{Y}
and satisfying the continuous inclusion
X\hookrightarrow W\hookrightarrow Y\hookrightarrow V
, the distribution relation (
2.1
) is clarified with

\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{W}
replaced by
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{Y}
. In addition, the embedding
W\hookrightarrow Y\hookrightarrow V
is not compact in the case of unbounded domains.

Furthermore, we have the following monotonicity and regularity condition:

(
A_{5}
)

The function
\mathcal{E}(t)
is nonincreasing and absolutely continuous on
J
for strong solution
u
of (
1.1
).

Consequently, from (
A_{2}
), we have

\|P(v)\|^{\ell^{\prime}}_{V^{\prime}}\leq j(t)\bigl[-\mathcal{E}^{\prime}(t)\bigr]^{\ell/m}

and (
A_{3}
) gives

\|B(t,x,u_{t})\|_{W^{\prime}}\leq(\eta(t))^{\frac{1}{m}}\bigl[-\mathcal{E}^{\prime}(t)\bigr]^{\frac{1}{m^{\prime}}}.

Lemma 2.1
.

Assume that the conditions (
A_{1})
-(
A_{5}
) hold. Let
u
be a strong solution of (
1.1
), then, the following is satisfied:

\mathcal{E}^{\prime}(t)\leq 0\quad\text{a.e. in J}\quad\text{and}\quad 0\leq\mathcal{E}(t)\leq\mathcal{E}(0)\quad\text{in J}.

(2.3)

Moreover,

0\leq\mathcal{A}(u(t))+\mathcal{P}(u_{t}(t))\leq\mathcal{E}(0)\quad\text{in J}\quad\text{and}\quad-\mathcal{E}^{\prime}(t)\in L^{1}(J).

The Lemma above is a partial modification of that of Pucci and Serrin
[
15
]
and the proof follows directly from (
A_{1}
), (
A_{4}
), (
2.2
) and (
2.3
).
We state without proof the existence result of (

).

Theorem 2.1
.

For initial data
u_{0}\in X
and
u_{1}\in V
, there exists a unique solution
u
of (
1.1
) such that

u\in C(\mathbb{R}_{+};X)\cap C^{1}(\mathbb{R}_{+};V).

The proof follows that of
[

,

3.
Energy Decay

For the energy decay, we define the following functions

\mathcal{H}(t)=\lambda(t)\mathcal{E}(t)+\mu\alpha(t)\mathcal{E}^{r}(t)\langle P(u_{t}),u\rangle

(3.1)

and

\mathcal{G}(t)=\mathcal{H}(t)+\nu\alpha(t)\delta^{\frac{1}{\ell}}(t)\mathcal{E}^{r+\frac{1}{q}+\frac{1}{\ell^{\prime}}}(t),

(3.2)

where the positive functions
\lambda(t)
and
\alpha(t)
are used to compensate for the compactness in the case of unbounded domains (
\lambda(t)
,
\alpha(t)
and
\delta(t)
are constants in the case of bounded domains) and
r
is a positive constant satisfying
r+\frac{1}{q}+\frac{1}{\ell^{\prime}}\geq 1
. In addition, we state the following assumptions on
\lambda(t)
,
\alpha(t)
,
\delta(t)
,
\eta(t)
and
j(t)
:

(
B_{1}
)

\lambda(t)\geq\alpha(t)\max\{\eta^{\frac{m^{\prime}}{m}}(t),\delta^{\frac{1}{\ell}}(t)\}
,

(
B_{2}
)

\bigl(\frac{\alpha(t)\delta^{\frac{1}{\ell}(t)}}{\lambda(t)}\bigr)^{\prime}\leq 0

(
B_{3}
)

\Bigl(\frac{\lambda(t)}{\alpha(t)}\Bigl|\Bigl(\frac{\alpha(t)}{\lambda(t)}\Bigr)^{\prime}\Bigr|\Bigr)\delta^{\frac{1}{\ell}}(t)\leq c_{{}_{\alpha\lambda}}

(
B_{4}
)

\frac{j^{\frac{m}{(m-\ell)}}(t)}{\eta^{\frac{m^{\prime}}{m}}(t)}\leq c_{j\eta}

where
c_{j\eta}\;and\;c_{\alpha\lambda}
are positive constants.

.

Let
u
be a solution of the problem (
1.1

A_{1}
)-(
A_{5}
) and (
B_{1}
)-(
B_{4}
) hold and
u_{0}\in X

u_{1}\in V

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}(t)\leq C\Bigl[1+\int^{t}_{0}\frac{\alpha(s)}{\lambda(s)}ds\Bigr]^{\frac{-\ell}{m-\ell}}\qquad\text{for $m>\ell$}

(3.3)

and

\mathcal{E}(t)\leq C^{*}exp\Bigl[-C^{**}\int^{t}_{0}\frac{\alpha(s)}{\lambda(s)}ds\Bigr]\qquad\text{for $m=\ell$}.

4.
Application

We give examples for which our results apply.

The canonical problem

\bigl(|u_{t}|^{\ell-2}u_{t}\bigr)_{t}-a\nabla\cdot\bigl(|\nabla u|^{q-2}\nabla u\bigr)+b|u_{t}|^{m-2}u_{t}=0,

(4.1)

with boundary conditions
u(t,x)=0\quad\text{on}\quad\partial\Omega
and initial conditions

u(0,x)=\psi(x),\quad u_{t}(0,x)=\phi(x)\quad\text{for}\quad x\in\Omega.

In the bounded domain,
W=[L^{\frac{qn}{n-q}}(\Omega)]^{n}
,
X=[W^{1,q}_{0}(\Omega)^{n}
,
V=[L^{\ell}(\Omega)]^{n}
and

Y=[L^{m}(\Omega)]^{n}
,

\alpha(t),\lambda(t),\delta^{\frac{1}{\ell}}(t)
are constants.
The
C^{1}
potential functions are given by

\begin{split}q\mathcal{A}(u)=\|\nabla u\|^{q}_{q}=\langle A(u),u\rangle,\\
\ell\mathcal{P}(v)=\|v\|^{\ell}_{\ell}=\langle P(v),v\rangle\end{split}

and it is easy to show that the conditions given are satisfied.
Generalizing (
4.1

\bigl(|u_{t}|^{\ell-2}u_{t}\bigr)_{t}-a\nabla\cdot\bigl(|\nabla u|^{q-2}\nabla u\bigr)+b(t,x)|u_{t}|^{m-2}u_{t}=0,

to an unbounded domain say, the

\mathbb{R}^{n}

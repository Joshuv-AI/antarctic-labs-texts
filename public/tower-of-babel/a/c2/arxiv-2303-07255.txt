Isogeometric multi-patch C 1 C^{1} -mortar coupling for the biharmonic equation
A. BenvenutiG. Loli, G. SangalliT. Takacs

Abstract
We propose an isogeometric mortar method for solving fourth-order elliptic problems. Specifically, our approach focuses on discretizing the biharmonic equation on C 0 C^{0} -conforming multi-patch domains, employing the mortar technique to weakly enforce C 1 C^{1} -continuity across interfaces. To guarantee discrete inf-sup stability, we introduce a carefully designed Lagrange multiplier space. In this formulation, the multipliers are splines with a degree reduced by two relative to the primal space and feature enhanced smoothness (or merged elements for splines with maximum smoothness) near the vertices. Within this framework, we establish optimal a priori error estimates and validate the theoretical results through numerical tests. Keywords: Isogeometric analysis, splines, multi-patch, mortar method, fourth-order PDEs, biharmonic equation.

A
C^{1}
mortar model problem

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
be a bounded connected domain with a piecewise polynomial boundary. We consider the following fourth-order elliptic problem with homogeneous boundary conditions, given by

\begin{cases}\begin{aligned} \Delta^{2}u&=f&&\text{in }\Omega,\\
u&=0&&\text{on }\partial\Omega,\\
\partial_{n}u&=0&&\text{on }\partial\Omega.\end{aligned}\end{cases}

(2.1)

A possible variational formulation of (
2.1
) is:
Find
u\in H^{2}_{0}(\Omega)
such that

\int_{\Omega}\nabla(\nabla u):\nabla(\nabla v)\,dx=\langle f,v\rangle_{H^{-1}\times H^{1}_{0}},\qquad\forall v\in H^{2}_{0}(\Omega),

(2.2)

where
H^{2}_{0}(\Omega)
denotes the Sobolev space of functions in
L^{2}(\Omega)
with second derivatives in
L^{2}(\Omega)
and null traces and normal derivatives on the boundary
\partial\Omega
.
In (
2.2
),
f
is a functional in
H^{-1}(\Omega)=(H^{1}_{0}(\Omega))^{\prime}
.

The mortar method applies to a decomposition of the domain
\Omega
into non-overlapping subdomains, which are commonly referred to as patches in the context of isogeometric analysis. Thus, we consider a family of open sets
\left\{\Omega_{k}\right\}_{k\in\mathcal{I}_{\Omega}}
, edges (open line segments)
\left\{\Gamma_{\ell}\right\}_{\ell\in\mathcal{I}_{\Gamma}}
, and vertices
\left\{\mathcal{V}_{i}\right\}_{i\in\mathcal{I}_{\mathcal{V}}}
, such that

\overline{\Omega}=\bigcup_{k\in\mathcal{I}_{\Omega}}{\Omega}_{k}\cup\bigcup_{\ell\in\mathcal{I}_{\Gamma}}{\Gamma}_{\ell}\cup\bigcup_{i\in\mathcal{I}_{\mathcal{V}}}{\mathcal{V}}_{i},

where
\mathcal{I}_{\Omega}
,
\mathcal{I}_{\Gamma}
, and
\mathcal{I}_{\mathcal{V}}
denote finite sets of patch, edge, and vertex indices, respectively.

Assumption 1
.

We assume that

\Omega_{k}\cap\Omega_{k^{\prime}}=\emptyset,\quad\forall\,k,k^{\prime}\in\mathcal{I}_{\Omega},\,k\neq k^{\prime},

and the patch segmentation has no hanging nodes; thus, for
k\neq k^{\prime}
, the set
\overline{\Omega}_{k}\cap\overline{\Omega}_{k^{\prime}}
is either empty, a vertex, or exactly one edge and two vertices.

Let
\mathcal{I}_{\Sigma}\subset\mathcal{I}_{\Gamma}
denote the index set of all interior edges, i.e.,
\ell\in\mathcal{I}_{\Sigma}
if and only if
{\Gamma}_{\ell}\subset\Omega
. The skeleton
\Sigma
, representing the union of all interior edges, is defined as

\Sigma:=\bigcup_{\ell\in\mathcal{I}_{\Sigma}}\Gamma_{\ell},

where

\overline{\Sigma}=\overline{\bigcup_{k\in\mathcal{I}_{\Omega}}\partial\Omega_{k}\setminus\partial\Omega}.

For each interface
\Gamma_{\ell}
, with
\ell\in\mathcal{I}_{\Sigma}
, there exists a pair of indices
k,k^{\prime}\in\mathcal{I}_{\Omega}
such that

\overline{\Gamma}_{\ell}=\partial\Omega_{k}\cap\partial\Omega_{k^{\prime}}.

Moreover, we assign arbitrarily an ordering to the patch indices with
\{k,k^{\prime}\}=\{m(\ell),s(\ell)\}
, referring to
\Omega_{m(\ell)}
as the
primary patch
of
\Gamma_{\ell}
and to
\Omega_{s(\ell)}
as the
secondary patch
.
We denote by
\textbf{n}_{\ell}
the outer normal to
\Gamma_{\ell}
from the primary side, and by
\partial_{n_{\ell}}(\cdot):=\nicefrac{{\partial(\cdot)}}{{\partial\textbf{n}_{\ell}}}
the outer normal derivative on
\Gamma_{\ell}
along
\textbf{n}_{\ell}
.

We define the space

X:=\Set{v\in H^{1}(\Omega)\left}{\ \begin{aligned} &v\lvert_{\Omega_{k}}\in H^{2}(\Omega_{k}),\forall k\in\mathcal{I}_{\Omega},\\
&v=\partial_{n}v=0,\text{ on }\partial\Omega,\\
&\llbracket\partial_{n_{\ell}}v\rrbracket_{\Gamma_{\ell}}\in H^{\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}_{00}(\Gamma_{\ell}),\forall\ell\in\mathcal{I}_{\Sigma}\end{aligned}},

where
\llbracket\cdot\rrbracket_{\Gamma_{\ell}}
denotes the jump, defined as the trace from the secondary side
s(\ell)
minus the trace from the primary side
m(\ell)
. The space
X
is equipped with the norm

\lVert v\rVert_{X}^{2}:=\sum_{k\in\mathcal{I}_{\Omega}}\lVert v\rVert_{H^{2}(\Omega_{k})}^{2}+\sum_{\ell\in\mathcal{I}_{\Sigma}}\lVert\llbracket\partial_{n_{\ell}}v\rrbracket_{\Gamma_{\ell}}\rVert_{H^{\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}_{00}(\Gamma_{\ell})}^{2}.

(2.3)

The continuous Lagrange multiplier space is defined as

M:=\prod_{\ell\in\mathcal{I}_{\Sigma}}H^{\nicefrac{{-1}}{{2}}}(\Gamma_{\ell}).

Next, we introduce the continuous bilinear form on
X\times X

a(u,v):=\sum_{k\in\mathcal{I}_{\Omega}}\int_{\Omega_{k}}\nabla(\nabla u):\nabla(\nabla v)\,dx,

and the continuous bilinear form on
X\times M

b(v,\mu):=\sum_{\ell\in\mathcal{I}_{\Sigma}}\int_{\Gamma_{\ell}}\mu\llbracket\partial_{n_{\ell}}v\rrbracket_{\Gamma_{\ell}}\,d\sigma.

We also introduce the linear continuous operator
B:X\rightarrow M^{\prime}
, defined as

\displaystyle\left\langle Bv,\mu\right\rangle_{M^{\prime}\times M}=b(v,\mu),\quad\forall v\in X,\quad\forall\mu\in M.

The following characterization holds:

H^{2}_{0}(\Omega)=\Set{v\in X:b(v,\mu)=0,\forall\mu\in M}=\ker B.

(2.4)

Lemma 1
.

For all
v\in H^{2}_{0}(\Omega)
there exists a constant
C>0
such that

\lVert v\rVert_{H^{2}(\Omega)}\leq C\lvert v\rvert_{H^{2}(\Omega)}

Proof.

For the proof we refer to
[
38
, Chapitre III, Lemme 11.1]
.
∎

Corollary 1
.

The seminorm
\lvert\cdot\rvert_{H^{2}(\Omega)}
is a norm on
H^{2}_{0}(\Omega)
.

We introduce the following saddle point problem:

Find
(u,\lambda)\in X\times M
such that

\begin{cases}\begin{aligned} a(u,v)+b(v,\lambda)&=\langle f,v\rangle_{H^{-1}\times H^{1}_{0}}&&\forall v\in X\\
b(u,\mu)&=0&&\forall\mu\in M.\end{aligned}\end{cases}

(2.5)

Theorem 1
.

The saddle point problem (
2.5
) has a unique solution.

Proof.

From (
2.4
) and Lemma
1
it follows that
a
is coercive on
\ker B
. Further, by construction we have
\mathrm{Im}B=M^{\prime}
. Hence, from
[
39
, Theorem 4.2.1]
, the existence and uniqueness for the solution of (
2.5
) follows.
∎

Proposition 1
.

If
(u,\lambda)\in X\times M
is the solution of problem (
2.5
), then
u\in H^{2}_{0}(\Omega)
and is the solution of problem (
2.2
). Furthermore if
u
is the solution of problem (
2.2
) and
u\in H^{4}(\Omega)
, then by setting

\lambda\lvert_{\Gamma_{\ell}}=-\partial_{n_{\ell}n_{\ell}}u\lvert_{\Gamma_{\ell}},

(2.6)

for all
\ell\in\mathcal{I}_{\Sigma}
,
(u,\lambda)
is the solution of problem (
2.5
).

Proof.

Let
(u,\lambda)\in X\times M
be the solution of (
2.5
). Choosing
\mu|_{\Gamma_{\ell}}=\llbracket\partial_{n_{\ell}}u\rrbracket_{\Gamma_{\ell}}
, from the second equation of (
2.5
) we obtain
u\in H^{2}_{0}(\Omega)
. For all
v\in H^{2}_{0}(\Omega)
, the first equation of (
2.5
) becomes

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\nabla(\nabla u):\nabla(\nabla v)\,dx=\langle f,v\rangle_{H^{-1}\times H^{1}_{0}},

which is (
2.2
).
To show the second part, let
u\in H^{2}_{0}(\Omega)\cap H^{4}(\Omega)
solve (
2.2
). Integrating by parts, we get

\langle f,v\rangle_{H^{-1}\times H^{1}_{0}}=\int_{\Omega}\nabla(\nabla u):\nabla(\nabla v)\,dx=\langle\Delta^{2}u,v\rangle_{H^{-1}\times H^{1}_{0}},\quad v\in H^{2}_{0}(\Omega),

implying that the functional
\Delta^{2}u-f\in H^{-1}(\Omega)
is null on the dense subspace
H^{2}_{0}(\Omega)\subset H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
. By the Hahn-Banach theorem, it is therefore the null functional on
H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
. Thus, we have

\langle\Delta^{2}u,v\rangle_{H^{-1}\times H^{1}_{0}}=\langle f,v\rangle_{H^{-1}\times H^{1}_{0}},\quad\forall v\in X.

Using Green’s formulas, it follows that

\langle\Delta^{2}u,v\rangle_{H^{-1}\times H^{1}_{0}}=\sum_{k\in\mathcal{I}_{\Omega}}\int_{\Omega_{k}}\nabla(\nabla u):\nabla(\nabla v)\,dx-\sum_{\ell\in\mathcal{I}_{\Sigma}}\int_{\Gamma_{\ell}}\partial_{n_{\ell}n_{\ell}}u\llbracket\partial_{n_{\ell}}v\rrbracket_{\Gamma_{\ell}}\,d\sigma,\quad\forall v\in X.

Finally, by setting
\lambda
as in (
2.6
), we obtain

a(u,v)+b(v,\lambda)=\langle f,v\rangle_{H^{-1}\times H^{1}_{0}},\quad\forall v\in X.

∎

3
Preliminaries on B-splines

In the following, we provide an overview of B-splines, introducing the necessary notation and fundamental concepts. For a comprehensive exposition, we refer the reader to
[
1
,
40
,
41
]
.

Given a positive integer
p
, a
p
-open knot vector
\Xi
is defined as a non-decreasing sequence of real numbers:

0=\xi_{1}=\ldots=\xi_{p+1}<\xi_{p+2}\leq\ldots\leq\xi_{n}<\xi_{n+1}=\ldots=\xi_{n+p+1}=1,

The breakpoint vector associated to
\Xi
, defined as

Z=(\zeta_{1},\ldots,\zeta_{n_{\mathrm{Z}}}),\quad\text{with }0=\zeta_{1}<\cdots<\zeta_{n_{\mathrm{Z}}}=1,

collects the knots without repetition. For any breakpoint
\zeta_{j}\in Z
, we denote by
m_{j}
its multiplicity in
\Xi
.
The B-spline basis functions of degree
p
on
\Xi
are defined recursively using the
Cox–de Boor formula
. For
1\leq i\leq n
, the basis functions are given by:

\begin{split}B_{i,0}(x)&=\begin{cases}1&\text{if }\xi_{i}\leq x<\xi_{i+1},\\
0&\text{otherwise,}\end{cases}\\
B_{i,p}(x)&=\frac{x-\xi_{i}}{\xi_{i+p}-\xi_{i}}B_{i,p-1}(x)+\frac{\xi_{i+p+1}-x}{\xi_{i+p+1}-\xi_{i+1}}B_{i+1,p-1}(x),\quad p\geq 1,\end{split}

where any division by zero is defined to yield a coefficient of zero.

These functions span the space of piecewise polynomials of degree
p
, with
p-m_{j}
continuous derivatives at the breakpoints
\zeta_{j}
:

\mathcal{S}^{p}(\Xi):=\operatorname{span}\{B_{i,p}\}_{i=1}^{n}.

Assumption 2
.

We assume that the spline space
\mathcal{S}^{p}(\Xi)
is globally at least
C^{1}
-continuous. Specifically, we require
p\geq 2
and
m_{j}\leq p-1
for
j=2,\dots,n_{\mathrm{Z}}-1
.

Given a vector of polynomial degrees
{\mathbf{p}}=(p_{1},p_{2})
and two open knot vectors
\Xi_{1}
and
\Xi_{2}
, the bi-variate B-spline basis is defined as

{\cal B}_{\mathbf{p}}(\boldsymbol{\Xi}):=\{\widehat{B}_{{\mathbf{i}},{\mathbf{p}}}(\boldsymbol{\zeta})=\widehat{B}_{i_{1},p_{1}}(\zeta_{1})\widehat{B}_{i_{2},p_{2}}(\zeta_{2}),\quad\boldsymbol{\zeta}\in(0,1)^{2}\},

where the corresponding spline space they span is given by

\mathcal{S}^{\mathbf{p}}=\mathcal{S}^{\mathbf{p}}(\Xi_{1},\Xi_{2}):=\mathcal{S}^{p_{1}}(\Xi_{1})\otimes\mathcal{S}^{p_{2}}(\Xi_{2})=\operatorname{span}({\cal B}_{\mathbf{p}}(\boldsymbol{\Xi})).

The breakpoints
(Z_{1},Z_{2})
associated with the knot vectors
(\Xi_{1},\Xi_{2})
define a mesh
\mathcal{Q}_{h}
on
\widehat{\Omega}
, which partitions the parametric domain into two-dimensional elements:

\mathcal{Q}_{h}=\mathcal{Q}_{h}(Z_{1},Z_{2}):=\{Q=(\zeta_{i}^{1},\zeta_{i+1}^{1})\times(\zeta_{j}^{2},\zeta_{j+1}^{2}):1\leq i<{n_{\mathrm{Z}}}_{1},1\leq j<{n_{\mathrm{Z}}}_{2}\}.

(3.1)

The global mesh size
h
is defined as

h:=\max_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq d\leq 2\\
1\leq i<{n_{\mathrm{Z}}}_{d}\end{subarray}}h^{d}_{i},

where
h^{d}_{i}:=\zeta^{d}_{i+1}-\zeta^{d}_{i}
and
\zeta^{d}_{i}
represents the
i
-th breakpoint in
Z_{d}
.

Assumption 3
.

We assume that the knot vectors are quasi-uniform. Specifically, there exists a constant
\alpha>0
, independent of
h
, such that

\alpha h\leq h^{d}_{i},\quad\text{for }1\leq i<{n_{\mathrm{Z}}}_{d}\text{ and }d=1,2.

4
Isogeometric discretization

We consider a standard
C^{0}
-conforming isogeometric multi-patch discretization. Each subdomain
\Omega_{k}
is defined as the image of the parametric domain
\widehat{\Omega}=(0,1)^{2}
under a spline geometry mapping
\bm{F}_{k}\colon\widehat{\Omega}\to\Omega_{k}
.

Assumption 4
.

We assume that
\bm{F}_{k}\in\mathcal{S}^{\mathbf{p}}_{k}\times\mathcal{S}^{\mathbf{p}}_{k}
, satisfies the uniform regularity condition: there exists a constant
\gamma>0
such that

\det\left(\nabla\bm{F}_{k}(\boldsymbol{\zeta})\right)\geq\gamma,\quad\forall\boldsymbol{\zeta}\in\widehat{\Omega}.

Here,
\mathcal{S}^{\mathbf{p}}_{k}=\mathcal{S}^{\mathbf{p}}(\Xi^{k}_{1},\Xi^{k}_{2})
is a spline space of fixed degree
\mathbf{p}
, with (potentially distinct) quasi-uniform knot vectors in each parametric direction.

The isogeometric discretization of the patch-local primal space on
\Omega_{k}
is defined as

X_{k,h}:=\Set{v\in H^{2}(\Omega_{k}):v\circ\bm{F}_{k}\in\mathcal{S}^{\mathbf{p}}_{k}\text{ and }v=\partial_{n}v=0\text{ on }\partial\Omega\cap\partial\Omega_{k}}.

(4.1)

To construct a global space, we assume that the spaces are
C^{0}
-conforming, as outlined below:

Assumption 5
.

We assume that, for all
\ell\in\mathcal{I}_{\Sigma}
, the trace spaces on
\Gamma_{\ell}
are equal, i.e.,
X_{m(\ell),h}|_{\Gamma_{\ell}}=X_{s(\ell),h}|_{\Gamma_{\ell}}
.

We denote by
\mathcal{I}_{\mathcal{V},k}
the set of indices corresponding to the four vertices of the patch
\Omega_{k}
. The vertices in the parametric domain
\widehat{\Omega}
are denoted by
\widehat{\mathcal{V}}_{i,k}:=\bm{F}_{k}^{-1}(\mathcal{V}_{i})
, or simply
\widehat{\mathcal{V}}_{i}=\widehat{\mathcal{V}}_{i,k}
when
k
is fixed.

On the global domain
\Omega
, we define the space of globally

-continuous isogeometric functions that are

-continuous at the vertices of the decomposition as

X_{h}:=\left\{v\in H^{1}(\Omega):v|_{\Omega_{k}}\in X_{k,h},\,\forall k\in\mathcal{I}_{\Omega},\text{ and }v\in C^{2}(\mathcal{V}_{i}),\forall i\in\mathcal{I}_{\mathcal{V}}\right\}.

(4.2)

Proposition 2
.

The inclusion
X_{h}\subset X
holds.

Proof.

Let

. By Assumption

\llbracket\partial_{n}v\rrbracket_{\Gamma_{\ell}}\in C^{0}(\Gamma_{\ell})

\llbracket\partial_{n}v\rrbracket_{\Gamma_{\ell}}

\llbracket\partial_{n}v\rrbracket_{\Gamma_{\ell}}\in H^{1/2}_{00}(\Gamma_{\ell})

.

V_{h}:=\Set{v_{h}\in X_{h}:b(v_{h},\mu_{h})=0,\forall\mu_{h}\in M_{h}}.

(4.3)

Here,

represents the isogeometric space with weak

-continuity, which depends on

.

-mortar constraints along

\begin{cases}\begin{aligned} a(u_{h},v_{h})+b(v_{h},\lambda_{h})&=\langle f,v_{h}\rangle_{H^{-1}\times H^{1}_{0}},&&\forall v_{h}\in X_{h},\\

5
Approximation properties of
X_{h}

In the following, we denote by
C
a positive, dimensionless constant, which may vary with each occurrence but remains independent of
h
. Additionally, we note that
C
does not depend on the specific function chosen within the involved spaces. To simplify the notation, we assume that the spline degree

Remark 1
.

If

, the following Sobolev compact embedding holds (see

H^{s}(\widehat{\Omega})\subset C^{2}\left(\overline{\widehat{\Omega}}\right).

Given an integer

\begin{split}\lvert v\rvert^{2}_{\mathcal{H}^{i}(\widehat{\Omega})}&:=\sum_{Q\in\mathcal{Q}_{h}}\lvert v\rvert^{2}_{H^{i}(Q)},\quad 0\leq i\leq s,\\
\lVert v\rVert^{2}_{\mathcal{H}^{s}(\widehat{\Omega})}&:=\sum_{i=0}^{s}\lvert v\rvert^{2}_{\mathcal{H}^{i}(\widehat{\Omega})}.\end{split}

Bent Sobolev spaces on patches of
\widehat{\Omega}
, on
\Omega

[
40
]

\ell\in\mathcal{I}_{\Sigma}
and any
\tau\in H^{p-\nicefrac{{3}}{{2}}}(\Gamma_{\ell})
, the space
M_{\ell}
satisfies

\inf_{\mu_{h}\in M_{\ell}}\lVert\tau-\mu_{h}\rVert_{L^{2}(\Gamma_{\ell})}\leq Ch^{p-\nicefrac{{3}}{{2}}}\lVert\tau\rVert_{H^{p-\nicefrac{{3}}{{2}}}(\Gamma_{\ell})}.

Assumption 7
.

For every
\ell\in\mathcal{I}_{\Sigma}
, the discrete trace space
W_{\ell}
and the discrete multiplier space
M_{\ell}
have the same dimension, i.e.,
\dim(W_{\ell})=\dim(M_{\ell})
. Moreover, there exists a constant
\beta_{\ell}>0
, independent of
h
, such that

\sup_{w_{h}\in W_{\ell}}\dfrac{\int_{\Gamma_{\ell}}w_{h}\,\mu_{h}\,d\sigma}{\lVert w_{h}\rVert_{L^{2}(\Gamma_{\ell})}}\geq\beta_{\ell}\lVert\mu_{h}\rVert_{L^{2}(\Gamma_{\ell})},\quad\forall\mu_{h}\in M_{\ell}.

Proposition 7
.

For each
w\in H_{00}^{\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}(\Gamma_{\ell})
, the following inequality holds:

\inf_{w_{h}\in W_{\ell}}\|w-w_{h}\|_{L^{2}(\Gamma_{\ell})}\leq C\,h^{\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}\|w\|_{H^{\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}(\Gamma_{\ell})}.

Proof.

Let
w\in H_{00}^{\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}(\Gamma_{\ell})
. Consider the solution
v
to the boundary value problem

\begin{cases}\Delta^{2}v=0&\text{in }\Omega_{s(\ell)},\\
v=0&\text{on }\partial\Omega_{s(\ell)},\\
\partial_{n}v=w&\text{on }\Gamma_{\ell},\\
\partial_{n}v=0&\text{on }\partial\Omega_{s(\ell)}\setminus\Gamma_{\ell}.\end{cases}

Here,
n
denotes the outer normal to
\partial\Omega_{s(\ell)}
. This problem has a unique solution
v\in H^{2}(\Omega_{s(\ell)})\cap H^{1}_{0}(\Omega_{s(\ell)})
, and the following estimate holds:

\|v\|_{H^{2}(\Omega_{s(\ell)})}\leq C\|w\|_{H_{00}^{\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}(\Gamma_{\ell})}.

Using Proposition
4
, we can define a projector

\displaystyle\Pi^{0}_{s(\ell),h}:

\displaystyle\left\{v_{h}\in H^{2}(\Omega_{s(\ell)})\cap H^{1}_{0}(\Omega_{s(\ell)})\mid\partial_{n}v_{h}|_{\partial\Omega_{s(\ell)}\setminus\mathring{\Gamma}_{\ell}}=0\right\}

\displaystyle\to\left\{v_{h}\in X_{s(\ell),h}\cap H^{1}_{0}(\Omega_{s(\ell)})\mid\partial_{n}v_{h}|_{\partial\Omega_{s(\ell)}\setminus\mathring{\Gamma}_{\ell}}=0\right\}.

such that

\|\nabla(v-\Pi^{0}_{s(\ell),h}v)\|_{L^{2}(\Gamma_{\ell})}\leq Ch^{\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}\|v\|_{H^{2}(\Omega_{s(\ell)})}.

Next, we define the projector
P_{h,\ell}:H_{00}^{\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}(\Gamma_{\ell})\to W_{\ell}
by

P_{h,\ell}(w):=(\partial_{n}\Pi^{0}_{s(\ell),h}v)|_{\Gamma_{\ell}}.

Finally, we estimate the error between
w
and
P_{h,\ell}w
:

\displaystyle\|w-P_{h,\ell}w\|_{L^{2}(\Gamma_{\ell})}

\displaystyle\leq\|\nabla(v-\Pi^{0}_{s(\ell),h}v)\|_{L^{2}(\Gamma_{\ell})}

\displaystyle\leq Ch^{\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}\|v\|_{H^{2}(\Omega_{s(\ell)})}

\displaystyle\leq Ch^{\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}\|w\|_{H^{\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}(\Gamma_{\ell})},

which completes the proof of the desired inequality.
∎

We define the mortar projection
\mathcal{M}_{h}
by

(\mathcal{M}_{h}v)|_{\Gamma_{\ell}}:=\mathcal{M}_{\ell}(v|_{\Gamma_{\ell}}),\quad\forall v\in L^{2}(\Sigma),

where, for each
\ell\in\mathcal{I}_{\Sigma}
, the local projection
\mathcal{M}_{\ell}\colon L^{2}(\Gamma_{\ell})\to W_{\ell}
is defined as

\int_{\Gamma_{\ell}}\mathcal{M}_{\ell}v\,\mu\,d\sigma=\int_{\Gamma_{\ell}}v\,\mu\,d\sigma,\quad\forall\mu\in M_{\ell}.

Remark 3
.

Given Assumption
7
, it follows from
[
39
, Proposition 3.4.3]
that, for every
\ell\in\mathcal{I}_{\Sigma}
, it holds

\sup_{\mu_{h}\in M_{\ell}}\dfrac{\int_{\Gamma_{\ell}}w_{h}\,\mu_{h}\,d\sigma}{\|\mu_{h}\|_{L^{2}(\Gamma_{\ell})}}\geq\beta_{\ell}\|w_{h}\|_{L^{2}(\Gamma_{\ell})},\quad\forall w_{h}\in W_{\ell},

Corollary 2
.

For every
\ell\in\mathcal{I}_{\Sigma}
, the mortar projection
\mathcal{M}_{\ell}\colon L^{2}(\Gamma_{\ell})\to W_{\ell}
is well-defined and
L^{2}
-stable:

\|\mathcal{M}_{\ell}w\|_{L^{2}(\Gamma_{\ell})}\leq C\|w\|_{L^{2}(\Gamma_{\ell})},\quad\forall w\in L^{2}(\Gamma_{\ell}).

Proof.

From Assumptions
7
and
[
39
, Proposition 3.4.3]
, the matrix associated with the scalar product
(w,\mu)_{L^{2}}
on
W_{\ell}\times M_{\ell}
is square and invertible. Therefore,
\mathcal{M}_{\ell}
is well-defined. Furthermore, from
[
39
, Theorem 4.2.3]
, it follows that
\mathcal{M}_{\ell}
is
L^{2}
-stable.
∎

Lemma 2
.

For every
\ell\in\mathcal{I}_{\Sigma}
and for every
w\in H^{\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}_{00}(\Gamma_{\ell})
, the following inequality holds:

\lVert w-\mathcal{M}_{\ell}\,w\rVert_{L^{2}(\Gamma_{\ell})}\leq C\,h^{\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}\lVert w\rVert_{H^{\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}(\Gamma_{\ell})}.

Furthermore, the mortar projection
\mathcal{M}_{\ell}
is
H^{\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}_{00}
-stable:

\|\mathcal{M}_{\ell}w\|_{H^{\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}(\Gamma_{\ell})}\leq C\|w\|_{H^{\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}(\Gamma_{\ell})},\quad\forall w\in L^{2}(\Gamma_{\ell}).

Proof.

Let
w\in H^{1}_{00}(\Gamma_{\ell})
and let
P_{h,\ell}
be the spline approximation operator defined in the proof of Proposition
7
. It follows that

\displaystyle\lVert w-\mathcal{M}_{\ell}w\rVert_{L^{2}(\Gamma_{\ell})}

\displaystyle=\lVert w-P_{h,\ell}w-\mathcal{M}_{\ell}(w-P_{h,\ell}w)\rVert_{L^{2}(\Gamma_{\ell})}

(6.2)

\displaystyle\leq C\lVert w-P_{h,\ell}w\rVert_{L^{2}(\Gamma_{\ell})}

\displaystyle\leq Ch^{1/2}\lVert w\rVert_{H^{1/2}(\Gamma_{\ell})}.

Regarding the stability in
H^{1/2}_{00}
, we apply the inverse inequality for splines and use (
6.2
) to obtain

\displaystyle\lVert\mathcal{M}_{\ell}w\rVert_{H^{1/2}(\Gamma_{\ell})}

\displaystyle\leq\lVert\mathcal{M}_{\ell}(w-P_{h,\ell}w)\rVert_{H^{1/2}(\Gamma_{\ell})}+\lVert P_{h,\ell}w\rVert_{H^{1/2}(\Gamma_{\ell})}

\displaystyle\leq Ch^{-1/2}\lVert\mathcal{M}_{\ell}(w-P_{h,\ell}w)\rVert_{L^{2}(\Gamma_{\ell})}+\lVert w\rVert_{H^{1/2}(\Gamma_{\ell})}

\displaystyle\leq C\lVert w\rVert_{H^{1/2}(\Gamma_{\ell})}.

∎

In addition to the local projector
\mathcal{M}_{\ell}
for each
\ell\in\mathcal{I}_{\Sigma}
, we define the dual operator
\mathcal{M}_{\ell}^{*}\colon L^{2}(\Gamma_{\ell})\to M_{\ell}
by the following condition:

\int_{\Gamma_{\ell}}\mathcal{M}_{\ell}^{*}\mu\,v\,d\sigma=\int_{\Gamma_{\ell}}\mu\,v\,d\sigma,\quad\forall v\in W_{\ell}.

(6.3)

Lemma 3
.

For every
\ell\in\mathcal{I}_{\Sigma}
, the dual operator
\mathcal{M}_{\ell}^{*}\colon L^{2}(\Gamma_{\ell})\to M_{\ell}
is well-defined and
L^{2}
-stable:

\|\mathcal{M}_{\ell}^{*}\mu\|_{L^{2}(\Gamma_{\ell})}\leq C\|\mu\|_{L^{2}(\Gamma_{\ell})},\quad\forall\mu\in L^{2}(\Gamma_{\ell}).

Furthermore, for all
\mu\in H^{p-\nicefrac{{3}}{{2}}}(\Gamma_{\ell})
, the following inequality holds:

\lVert\mu-\mathcal{M}_{\ell}^{*}\mu\rVert_{L^{2}(\Gamma_{\ell})}\leq C\,h^{p-\nicefrac{{3}}{{2}}}\,\lVert\mu\rVert_{H^{p-\nicefrac{{3}}{{2}}}(\Gamma_{\ell})}.

Proof.

As in the proof of Corollary
2
, Assumption
7
guarantees that
\mathcal{M}_{\ell}^{*}
is well-defined. By combining Assumption
7
with (
6.3
), we obtain the following estimate:

\begin{split}\lVert\mathcal{M}_{\ell}^{*}\mu\rVert_{L^{2}(\Gamma_{\ell})}&\leq\frac{1}{\beta_{\ell}}\sup_{w_{h}\in W_{\ell}}\frac{\int_{\Gamma_{\ell}}\mathcal{M}_{\ell}^{*}\mu\,w_{h}\,d\sigma}{\lVert w_{h}\rVert_{L^{2}(\Gamma_{\ell})}}\\
&=\frac{1}{\beta_{\ell}}\sup_{w_{h}\in W_{\ell}}\frac{\int_{\Gamma_{\ell}}\mu\,w_{h}\,d\sigma}{\lVert w_{h}\rVert_{L^{2}(\Gamma_{\ell})}}\\
&\leq\frac{1}{\beta_{\ell}}\sup_{w_{h}\in W_{\ell}}\frac{\lVert\mu\rVert_{L^{2}(\Gamma_{\ell})}\lVert w_{h}\rVert_{L^{2}(\Gamma_{\ell})}}{\lVert w_{h}\rVert_{L^{2}(\Gamma_{\ell})}}\\
&=\frac{1}{\beta_{\ell}}\lVert\mu\rVert_{L^{2}(\Gamma_{\ell})}.\end{split}

The approximation property of
\mathcal{M}_{\ell}^{*}
follows from Assumption
6
. Indeed, for
\mu\in H^{p-\nicefrac{{3}}{{2}}}(\Gamma_{\ell})
, let
\mu_{h}\in M_{\ell}
be such that

\lVert\mu-\mu_{h}\rVert_{L^{2}(\Gamma_{\ell})}\leq Ch^{p-\nicefrac{{3}}{{2}}}\lVert\mu\rVert_{H^{p-\nicefrac{{3}}{{2}}}(\Gamma_{\ell})},

then, we have the following estimate:

\begin{split}\lVert\mu-\mathcal{M}_{\ell}^{*}\mu\rVert_{L^{2}(\Gamma_{\ell})}&=\lVert\mu-\mu_{h}-\mathcal{M}_{\ell}^{*}(\mu-\mu_{h})\rVert_{L^{2}(\Gamma_{\ell})}\\
&\leq C^{\prime}\lVert\mu-\mu_{h}\rVert_{L^{2}(\Gamma_{\ell})}\\
&\leq Ch^{p-\nicefrac{{3}}{{2}}}\lVert\mu\rVert_{H^{p-\nicefrac{{3}}{{2}}}(\Gamma_{\ell})}.\end{split}

This completes the proof.
∎

We now proceed with the study of the properties of
V_{h}
.

Lemma 4
.

For every
v_{h}\in V_{h}
, the following inequality holds:

\sum_{k\in\mathcal{I}_{\Omega}}\lVert v_{h}\rVert^{2}_{H^{2}(\Omega_{k})}\leq C\sum_{k\in\mathcal{I}_{\Omega}}\lvert v_{h}\rvert^{2}_{H^{2}(\Omega_{k})}.

Proof.

The proof follows the approach outlined in
[
44
, Theorem 1]
. We first show that for all
v_{h}\in V_{h}
, it holds:

\sum_{k\in\mathcal{I}_{\Omega}}\lVert\partial_{x}v_{h}\rVert^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega_{k})}\leq C\sum_{k\in\mathcal{I}_{\Omega}}\lvert v_{h}\rvert^{2}_{H^{2}(\Omega_{k})}.

(6.4)

Let
(x,y)\in\Omega
be an arbitrary point. Consider the endpoints
(a,y)
and
(b,y)\in\partial\Omega
of a segment containing
(x,y)
, and define the set

I[a,x]:=\left\{t\in[a,x]\mid(t,y)\in\Gamma\right\},

where
N_{I}=|I[a,b]|
. Then, we have

\partial_{x}v_{h}(x,y)=\int_{a}^{x}\partial_{xx}v_{h}(t,y)\,dt+\sum_{t_{\ell}\in I[a,x]}\llbracket\partial_{x}v_{h}\rrbracket_{\Gamma_{\ell}}(t_{\ell},y).

(6.5)

The first term on the right-hand side of (
6.5
) can be bounded as follows:

\left|\int_{a}^{x}\partial_{xx}v_{h}(t,y)\,dt\right|\leq\int_{a}^{b}\left|\partial_{xx}v_{h}(t,y)\right|\,dt\leq M^{\frac{1}{2}}\left(\int_{a}^{b}\left|\partial_{xx}v_{h}(t,y)\right|^{2}\,dt\right)^{\frac{1}{2}},

where
M=\text{diam}(\Omega)
. The second term is bounded by

\left|\sum_{t_{\ell}\in I[a,x]}\llbracket\partial_{x}v_{h}\rrbracket_{\Gamma_{\ell}}(t_{\ell},y)\right|\leq\sum_{t_{\ell}\in I[a,b]}\left|\llbracket\partial_{x}v_{h}\rrbracket_{\Gamma_{\ell}}(t_{\ell},y)\right|\leq\left(N_{I}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\left(\sum_{t_{\ell}\in I[a,b]}\left|\llbracket\partial_{x}v_{h}\rrbracket(t_{\ell},y)\right|^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

Therefore, we obtain the following estimate:

\left|\partial_{x}v_{h}(x,y)\right|^{2}\leq C\int_{a}^{b}\left|\partial_{xx}v_{h}(t,y)\right|^{2}\,dt+C\sum_{t_{\ell}\in I[a,b]}\left|\llbracket\partial_{x}v_{h}\rrbracket_{\Gamma_{\ell}}(t_{\ell},y)\right|^{2}.

(6.6)

Without loss of generality, assume that
\Omega\subset[0,M]^{2}
, and extend
\left|\partial_{xx}v_{h}(t,y)\right|^{2}
to zero outside of
\Omega
. Integrating the first term on the right-hand side of (
6.6
) over
\Omega
, we get

\begin{split}\int_{\Omega}\left(\int_{a}^{b}\left|\partial_{xx}v_{h}(t,y)\right|^{2}\,dt\right)\,dx\,dy&\leq\int_{0}^{M}\int_{0}^{M}\int_{0}^{M}\left|\partial_{xx}v_{h}(t,y)\right|^{2}\,dt\,dx\,dy\\
&=M\int_{0}^{M}\int_{0}^{M}\left|\partial_{xx}v_{h}(t,y)\right|^{2}\,dt\,dy\\
&=M\int_{\Omega}\left|\partial_{xx}v_{h}(t,y)\right|^{2}\,dx\,dy.\end{split}

Similarly, integrating the second term of (
6.6
) over
\Omega
, we obtain

\int_{\Omega}\sum_{t_{\ell}\in I[a,b]}\left|\llbracket\partial_{x}v_{h}\rrbracket_{\Gamma_{\ell}}(t_{\ell},y)\right|^{2}\,dx\,dy\leq C\sum_{\ell\in\mathcal{I}_{\Sigma}}\int_{\Gamma_{\ell}}\left|\llbracket\partial_{n}v_{h}\rrbracket_{\Gamma_{\ell}}\right|^{2}\,ds.

Therefore, we have the following inequality:

\int_{\Omega}\left|\partial_{x}v_{h}(x,y)\right|^{2}\,dx\,dy\leq C\int_{\Omega}\left|\partial_{xx}v_{h}\right|^{2}\,dx\,dy+C\sum_{\ell\in\mathcal{I}_{\Sigma}}\int_{\Gamma_{\ell}}\left|\llbracket\partial_{n}v_{h}\rrbracket_{\Gamma_{\ell}}\right|^{2}\,ds.

To estimate the last integral, note that by the mortar condition (
4.3
), the average value of
\llbracket\partial_{n}v_{h}\rrbracket_{\Gamma_{\ell}}
over
\Gamma_{\ell}
is zero. Applying the standard trace theorem and the Poincaré inequality, we obtain

\int_{\Gamma_{\ell}}\left|\llbracket\partial_{n}v_{h}\rrbracket_{\Gamma_{\ell}}\right|^{2}\,ds\leq C_{m(\ell)}\lvert v_{h}\rvert^{2}_{H^{2}(\Omega_{m(\ell)})}+C_{s(\ell)}\lvert v_{h}\rvert^{2}_{H^{2}(\Omega_{s(\ell)})}.

Summing over all interfaces, we obtain (
6.4
).

Using similar arguments for the
y
-derivative, we can find a constant
C>0
such that for every
v_{h}\in V_{h}
, the following inequality holds:

\sum_{k\in\mathcal{I}_{\Omega}}\lvert v_{h}\rvert^{2}_{H^{1}(\Omega_{k})}\leq C\sum_{k\in\mathcal{I}_{\Omega}}\lvert v_{h}\rvert^{2}_{H^{2}(\Omega_{k})}.

(6.7)

Moreover, since
V_{h}\subset H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
, applying the standard Poincaré inequality yields

\sum_{k\in\mathcal{I}_{\Omega}}\lVert v_{h}\rVert^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega_{k})}\leq C\sum_{k\in\mathcal{I}_{\Omega}}\lvert v_{h}\rvert^{2}_{H^{1}(\Omega_{k})}.

(6.8)

Finally, the thesis follows by combining (
6.7
) and (
6.8
).
∎

Lemma 5
.

Let
s=\max(4,p+1)
. For all
v\in H^{s}(\Omega)
,

\inf_{v_{h}\in V_{h}}\|v-v_{h}\|_{X}\leq Ch^{p-1}\|v\|_{H^{s}(\Omega)}.

Proof.

Let us define the extension operator
\mathcal{H}_{h,\ell}:W_{\ell}\to H^{2}(\Omega_{s(\ell)})\cap H^{1}_{0}(\Omega_{s(\ell)})
, such that
\mathcal{H}_{h,\ell}w
is the unique solution to the following boundary value problem:

\begin{cases}\Delta^{2}v=0&\text{in }\Omega_{s(\ell)},\\
v=0&\text{on }\partial\Omega_{s(\ell)},\\
\partial_{n}v=-w&\text{on }\Gamma_{\ell},\\
\partial_{n}v=0&\text{on }\partial\Omega_{s(\ell)}\setminus\Gamma_{\ell}.\end{cases}

Here,
n
denotes the outer normal to
\partial\Omega_{s(\ell)}
. It follows that

\|\mathcal{H}_{h,\ell}w_{h}\|_{H^{2}(\Omega_{s(\ell)})}\leq C\|w_{h}\|_{H^{1/2}(\partial\Omega_{s(\ell)})}\leq C\|w_{h}\|_{H^{1/2}(\Gamma_{\ell})}.

We further extend
\mathcal{H}_{h,\ell}\widetilde{w}_{h}
to be zero outside
\Omega_{s(\ell)}
.

Now, let
v\in H^{p+1}(\Omega)
, and let
\Pi_{h}v\in X_{h}
be the global spline projection of
v
in
X_{h}
, as defined in Proposition
6
. We define an element
v_{h}\in V_{h}
by

v_{h}:=\Pi_{h}v-\sum_{\ell\in\mathcal{I}_{\Sigma}}\mathcal{H}_{h,\ell}\mathcal{M}_{\ell}\llbracket\partial_{n_{\ell}}\Pi_{h}v\rrbracket_{\Gamma_{\ell}}.

From the definition of
\Pi_{h}
, we have that for all
\tau_{h}\in M_{h}
,

\int_{\Gamma_{\ell}}\llbracket\partial_{n_{\ell}}v_{h}\rrbracket_{\Gamma_{\ell}}\,\tau_{h}\,d\sigma=\int_{\Gamma_{\ell}}\left(\llbracket\partial_{n_{\ell}}\Pi_{h}v\rrbracket_{\Gamma_{\ell}}-\mathcal{M}_{\ell}\llbracket\partial_{n_{\ell}}\Pi_{h}v\rrbracket\right)\tau_{h}\,d\sigma=0.

Thus,
v_{h}\in V_{h}
. Consequently, we can write

\|v-v_{h}\|_{X}\leq\|v-\Pi_{h}v\|_{X}+\left\|\sum_{\ell\in\mathcal{I}_{\Sigma}}\mathcal{H}_{h,\ell}\mathcal{M}_{\ell}\llbracket\partial_{n_{\ell}}\Pi_{h}v\rrbracket_{\Gamma_{\ell}}\right\|_{X}.

The first term is estimated in Proposition
6
.

On the other hand, using the definition of
\mathcal{H}_{h,\ell}
, we obtain the following estimate:

\begin{split}\left\|\sum_{\ell\in\mathcal{I}_{\Sigma}}\mathcal{H}_{h,\ell}\mathcal{M}_{\ell}\llbracket\partial_{n_{\ell}}\Pi_{h}v\rrbracket_{\Gamma_{\ell}}\right\|_{X}^{2}&\leq\sum_{\ell\in\mathcal{I}_{\Sigma}}\|\mathcal{H}_{h,\ell}\mathcal{M}_{\ell}\llbracket\partial_{n_{\ell}}\Pi_{h}v\rrbracket_{\Gamma_{\ell}}\|_{H^{2}\left(\Omega_{s(\ell)}\right)}^{2}+\sum_{\ell\in\mathcal{I}_{\Sigma}}\|\mathcal{M}_{\ell}\llbracket\partial_{n_{\ell}}\Pi_{h}v\rrbracket_{\Gamma_{\ell}}\|_{H^{1/2}(\Gamma_{\ell})}^{2}\\
&\leq C\sum_{\ell\in\mathcal{I}_{\Sigma}}\|\mathcal{M}_{\ell}\llbracket\partial_{n_{\ell}}\Pi_{h}v\rrbracket_{\Gamma_{\ell}}\|_{H^{1/2}(\Gamma_{\ell})}^{2}.\end{split}

The
H^{1/2}_{00}
-stability of the mortar projector
\mathcal{M}_{\ell}
, stated in Lemma
2
, and Proposition
6
yield

\begin{split}\|\mathcal{M}_{\ell}\llbracket\partial_{n_{\ell}}\Pi_{h}v\rrbracket_{\Gamma_{\ell}}\|_{H^{1/2}(\Gamma_{\ell})}^{2}&\leq\|\llbracket\partial_{n_{\ell}}\Pi_{h}v\rrbracket_{\Gamma_{\ell}}\|_{H^{1/2}(\Gamma_{\ell})}^{2}\\
&\leq Ch^{2(p-1)}\left(\|v\|_{H^{s}\left(\Omega_{m(\ell)}\right)}^{2}+\|v\|_{H^{s}\left(\Omega_{s(\ell)}\right)}^{2}\right).\end{split}

Finally, by Assumption
3
, summing over the interfaces, we obtain the desired result.
∎

7
Error analysis for the model problem

Since
V_{h}\not\subseteq H^{2}(\Omega)
(as the space
V_{h}
is only weakly
C^{1}
), we must employ Strang’s lemma to bound the numerical error in our formulation.

Lemma 6
.

Let
u
be the solution of Problem (
2.5
), and
u_{h}
the solution of the discrete Problem (
4.4
). It holds

\lVert u-u_{h}\rVert_{X}\leq C\left(\inf_{v_{h}\in V_{h}}\lVert u-v_{h}\rVert_{X}+\sup_{v_{h}\in V_{h}}\dfrac{\left\lvert\int_{\Sigma}\partial_{nn}u\llbracket\partial_{n}v_{h}\rrbracket\,d\sigma\right\rvert}{\lVert v_{h}\rVert_{X}}\right).

(7.1)

Proof.

From Lemma
4
, the bilinear form
a(\cdot,\cdot)
is continuous on
X
and coercive on
V_{h}
with respect to the broken norm
\sum_{k\in\mathcal{I}_{\Omega}}\lVert\cdot\rVert^{2}_{H^{2}(\Omega_{k})}
. Our goal is to establish coercivity on
V_{h}
with respect to
\lVert\cdot\rVert_{X}
. Using the inverse inequality, we obtain:

\lVert\llbracket\partial_{n}v_{h}\rrbracket_{\Gamma_{\ell}}\rVert_{H^{\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}(\Gamma_{\ell})}^{2}\leq Ch^{-1}\lVert\llbracket\partial_{n}v_{h}\rrbracket_{\Gamma_{\ell}}\rVert_{L^{2}(\Gamma_{\ell})}^{2}.

Following
[
45
, Lemma 3.5]
and under Assumptions
3
and
6
, if
v_{h}\in V_{h}
and
\mathcal{M}_{\ell}^{*}\colon L^{2}(\Gamma_{\ell})\to M_{\ell}
is the projector defined by equation (
6.3
), we have:

\lVert(\id-\mathcal{M}_{\ell}^{*})\llbracket\partial_{n}v_{h}\rrbracket_{\Gamma_{\ell}}\rVert_{L^{2}(\Gamma_{\ell})}^{2}=\lVert\llbracket\partial_{n}v_{h}\rrbracket_{\Gamma_{\ell}}\rVert_{L^{2}(\Gamma_{\ell})}^{2}+\lVert\llbracket\mathcal{M}_{\ell}^{*}\partial_{n}v_{h}\rrbracket_{\Gamma_{\ell}}\rVert_{L^{2}(\Gamma_{\ell})}^{2},

where this equality follows from the fact that the jump
\llbracket\partial_{n}v_{h}\rrbracket_{\Gamma_{\ell}}
is orthogonal to
M_{\ell}
for all
v_{h}\in V_{h}
and for every
\ell\in\mathcal{I}_{\Sigma}
. Using the approximation properties of
\mathcal{M}_{\ell}^{*}
stated in Lemma
3
, we derive:

\begin{split}\lVert\llbracket\partial_{n}v_{h}\rrbracket_{\Gamma_{\ell}}\rVert_{H^{\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}(\Gamma_{\ell})}^{2}&\leq Ch^{-1}\lVert\llbracket\partial_{n}v_{h}\rrbracket_{\Gamma_{\ell}}\rVert_{L^{2}(\Gamma_{\ell})}^{2}\leq Ch^{-1}\lVert(\id-\mathcal{M}_{\ell}^{*})\llbracket\partial_{n}v_{h}\rrbracket_{\Gamma_{\ell}}\rVert_{L^{2}(\Gamma_{\ell})}^{2}\\
&\leq Ch^{-1}\left(\lVert(\id-\mathcal{M}_{\ell}^{*})\partial_{n}v_{h}|_{\Omega_{m(\ell)}}\rVert_{L^{2}(\Gamma_{\ell})}^{2}+\lVert(\id-\mathcal{M}_{\ell}^{*})\partial_{n}v_{h}|_{\Omega_{s(\ell)}}\rVert_{L^{2}(\Gamma_{\ell})}^{2}\right)\\
&\leq Ch^{-1}\left(h\lVert\partial_{n}v_{h}|_{\Omega_{m(\ell)}}\rVert_{H^{\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}(\Gamma_{\ell})}^{2}+h\lVert\partial_{n}v_{h}|_{\Omega_{s(\ell)}}\rVert_{H^{\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}(\Gamma_{\ell})}^{2}\right)\\
&\leq C\left(\lVert v_{h}\rVert_{H^{2}\left(\Omega_{m(\ell)}\right)}^{2}+\lVert v_{h}\rVert_{H^{2}\left(\Omega_{s(\ell)}\right)}^{2}\right).\end{split}

Thus,
a(\cdot,\cdot)
is coercive on
V_{h}
with respect to
\lVert\cdot\rVert_{X}
, allowing us to apply the second Strang Lemma as presented in
[
46
, Theorem 4.2.2]
, which establishes (
7.1
).
∎

The first term in (
7.1
) represents the best approximation error in
V_{h}
, which was analyzed in Lemma
5
. The second term corresponds to the consistency error. The following result establishes an optimal estimate for the consistency error.

Lemma 7
.

The following inequality holds

\sup_{v_{h}\in V_{h}}\frac{\left\lvert\int_{\Sigma}\partial_{nn}u\llbracket\partial_{n}v_{h}\rrbracket\,d\sigma\right\rvert}{\lVert v_{h}\rVert_{X}}\leq C\sum_{k\in\mathcal{I}_{\Omega}}h^{p-1}\lVert u\rVert_{H^{p+1}(\Omega_{k})}.

Proof.

Let
v_{h}\in V_{h}
. By applying Lemma
3
, we have

\begin{split}\int_{\Sigma}\partial_{nn}u\llbracket\partial_{n}v_{h}\rrbracket\,d\sigma&=\int_{\Sigma}(\partial_{nn}u-\mathcal{M}_{\ell}^{*}\partial_{nn}u)\llbracket\partial_{n}v_{h}\rrbracket\,d\sigma\\
&\leq\sum_{\ell\in\mathcal{I}_{\Sigma}}\lVert\partial_{nn}u-\mathcal{M}_{\ell}^{*}\partial_{nn}u\rVert_{L^{2}(\Gamma_{\ell})}\lVert\llbracket\partial_{n}v_{h}\rrbracket_{\Gamma_{\ell}}\rVert_{L^{2}(\Gamma_{\ell})}\\
&\leq Ch^{p-\nicefrac{{3}}{{2}}}\sum_{\ell\in\mathcal{I}_{\Sigma}}\lVert\partial_{nn}u\rVert_{H^{p-\nicefrac{{3}}{{2}}}(\Gamma_{\ell})}\lVert\llbracket\partial_{n}v_{h}\rrbracket_{\Gamma_{\ell}}\rVert_{L^{2}(\Gamma_{\ell})}.\end{split}

By Lemma
2
, we further obtain

\begin{split}\lVert\llbracket\partial_{n}v_{h}\rrbracket_{\Gamma_{\ell}}\rVert_{L^{2}(\Gamma_{\ell})}&\leq\lVert(\id-\mathcal{M}_{\ell})\llbracket\partial_{n}v_{h}\rrbracket_{\Gamma_{\ell}}\rVert_{L^{2}(\Gamma_{\ell})}\\
&\leq Ch^{\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}\lVert\llbracket\partial_{n}v_{h}\rrbracket_{\Gamma_{\ell}}\rVert_{H^{\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}(\Gamma_{\ell})}\leq Ch^{\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}\lVert v_{h}\rVert_{X}.\end{split}

Using the standard trace theorem, we deduce that

\lVert\partial_{nn}u\rVert_{H^{p-\nicefrac{{3}}{{2}}}(\Gamma_{\ell})}\leq C\lVert u\rVert_{H^{p+\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}(\Gamma_{\ell})}\leq C\lVert u\rVert_{H^{p+1}(\Omega_{s(\ell)})}.

Consequently, we arrive at the following bound:

\int_{\Sigma}\partial_{n}u\llbracket\partial_{n}v_{h}\rrbracket\,d\sigma\leq Ch^{p-1}\sum_{k\in\mathcal{I}_{\Omega}}\lVert u\rVert_{H^{p+1}(\Omega_{k})}\lVert v_{h}\rVert_{X}.

This completes the proof.
∎

We combine the results from Lemmas
5
,
6
, and
7
to derive the optimal a priori error estimate.

Corollary 3
.

Let
s=\max(4,p+1)
. For
u\in H^{s}(\Omega)
, the following inequality holds:

\|u-u_{h}\|_{X}\leq C\,h^{p-1}\|u\|_{H^{s}(\Omega)}.

\widehat{\Gamma}
. On this domain, the trace space of the crossing derivative is denoted by

. The proof relies on the following technical assumption.

Proposition 8
.

For any
v\in H^{1}_{0}(\widehat{\Gamma})
, the following inequality holds:

\lVert v\rVert_{L^{2}(\widehat{\Gamma})}\leq C\lVert v^{\prime}\rVert_{H^{1}(\widehat{\Gamma})^{\prime}},

where
H^{1}(\widehat{\Gamma})^{\prime}
denotes the dual space of
H^{1}(\widehat{\Gamma})
.

Proof.

For all
w\in L^{2}(\widehat{\Gamma})
, there exists a function
\bar{w}\in H^{1}_{\int}(\widehat{\Gamma})
such that
\bar{w}^{\prime}=w
. Here, the space
H^{1}_{\int}(\widehat{\Gamma})
is defined as

H^{1}_{\int}(\widehat{\Gamma}):=\Set{\bar{w}\in H^{1}(\widehat{\Gamma}):\int_{\widehat{\Gamma}}\bar{w}\,d\sigma=0}.

Now, for any
v\in H^{1}_{0}(\widehat{\Gamma})
, we have

\begin{split}\lVert v\rVert_{L^{2}(\widehat{\Gamma})}&=\sup_{w\in L^{2}(\widehat{\Gamma})}\dfrac{\int_{\widehat{\Gamma}}v\,w\,d\sigma}{\lVert w\rVert_{L^{2}(\widehat{\Gamma})}}=\sup_{\bar{w}\in H^{1}_{\int}(\widehat{\Gamma})}\dfrac{\int_{\widehat{\Gamma}}v\bar{w}^{\prime}\,d\sigma}{\lvert\bar{w}\rvert_{H^{1}(\widehat{\Gamma})}}\\
&\leq\sqrt{2}\sup_{\bar{w}\in H^{1}_{\int}(\widehat{\Gamma})}\dfrac{\int_{\widehat{\Gamma}}\bar{w}\,v^{\prime}\,d\sigma}{\lVert\bar{w}\rVert_{H^{1}(\widehat{\Gamma})}}\leq\sqrt{2}\lVert v^{\prime}\rVert_{H^{1}(\widehat{\Gamma})^{\prime}},\end{split}

where the Poincaré inequality,
\lVert\bar{w}\rVert_{L^{2}(\widehat{\Gamma})}\leq\lvert\bar{w}\rvert_{H^{1}(\widehat{\Gamma})}
for all
\bar{w}\in H^{1}_{\int}(\widehat{\Gamma})
, is used in the last step.
∎

Proposition 9
.

For any
\phi\in\mathcal{S}_{0}^{p-1}
, the following inequality holds:

\sup_{\tau\in\mathcal{S}^{p-1}_{\textsc{m}}}\dfrac{\int_{\widehat{\Gamma}}\phi\tau\,d\sigma}{\lVert\tau\rVert_{L^{2}(\widehat{\Gamma})}}\geq C\lVert\phi\rVert_{L^{2}(\widehat{\Gamma})}.

Proof.

We will prove that, for every
\phi\in\mathcal{S}^{p-1}_{0}
, there exists a
\tau\in\mathcal{S}^{p-1}_{\textsc{m}}
such that the following conditions hold:

\displaystyle\lVert\tau\rVert_{L^{2}(\widehat{\Gamma})}

\displaystyle\leq C\lVert\phi\rVert_{L^{2}(\widehat{\Gamma})},

(8.2a)

\displaystyle\lVert\phi\rVert_{L^{2}(\widehat{\Gamma})}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq C\int_{\widehat{\Gamma}}\phi\,\tau\,d\sigma.

(8.2b)

If we can establish these conditions, the proposition will follow.

Let
\{\widehat{B}_{i}\}_{i=1}^{n}
and
\{\widetilde{B}_{i}\}_{i=1}^{n}
be the B-spline basis functions for
\mathcal{S}^{p-1}_{0}
and
\mathcal{S}^{p-1}_{\textsc{m}}
, respectively. We denote by
\widehat{I}_{i}
and
\widetilde{I}_{i}
the supports of
\widehat{B}_{i}
and
\widetilde{B}_{i}
, respectively, and by
\lvert\widehat{I}_{i}\rvert
and
\lvert\widetilde{I}_{i}\rvert
their respective lengths.
We observe that the set
\{\widetilde{B}_{0,i}\}_{i=1}^{n-2}
, obtained by removing the first and last basis functions from
\{\widetilde{B}_{i}\}_{i=1}^{n}
, forms a B-spline basis for the intersection
\mathcal{S}^{p-1}_{0}(\widehat{\Gamma})\cap\mathcal{S}^{p-1}_{\textsc{m}}(\widehat{\Gamma})
.
This set can be extended to a basis for
\mathcal{S}^{p-1}_{0}(\widehat{\Gamma})
by adding the
(1+p-r)
-th and the
(n-p+r)
-th last functions of
\{\widehat{B}_{i}\}_{i=1}^{n}
. We denote these functions by
\widehat{B}_{1+p-r}=b_{L}
and
\widehat{B}_{n-p+r}=b_{R}
, respectively, where
p
and
r
are as defined in Assumption
8
.
Next, we define the
L^{2}
-projections of
b_{L}
and
b_{R}
onto the spaces spanned by
\{\widetilde{B}_{i}\}_{i=1}^{2p-r}
and
\{\widetilde{B}_{i}\}_{i=n-2p+r+1}^{n}
, respectively. Specifically, we have the following relations:

\displaystyle\int_{\widehat{\Gamma}}\Pi_{L}\cdot\widetilde{B}_{0,i}\,d\sigma

\displaystyle=\int_{\widehat{\Gamma}}b_{L}\cdot\widetilde{B}_{0,i}\,d\sigma,\quad i=1,\ldots,2p-r,

\displaystyle\int_{\widehat{\Gamma}}\Pi_{R}\cdot\widetilde{B}_{0,i}\,d\sigma

\displaystyle=\int_{\widehat{\Gamma}}b_{R}\cdot\widetilde{B}_{0,i}\,d\sigma,\quad i=n-2p+r+1,\ldots,m.

Given
\phi\in\mathcal{S}^{p-1}_{0}(\widehat{\Gamma})
, we can express it as

\phi=\alpha_{L}b_{L}+\sum_{i=1}^{n-2}c_{i}\widetilde{B}_{0,i}+\alpha_{R}b_{R}.

We now define
\tau\in\mathcal{S}^{p-1}_{\textsc{m}}(\widehat{\Gamma})
as

\tau=\alpha_{L}\Pi_{L}+\sum_{i=1}^{n-2}c_{i}\widetilde{B}_{0,i}+\alpha_{R}\Pi_{R}.

Let
I\subset(0,1)
be an element of the mesh. The restriction of
\tau
on the element
I
can be expressed as

\tau|_{I}=\sum_{i:I\subset\widetilde{I}_{i}}c_{i}\widetilde{B}_{i},

and we have the following estimate:

\displaystyle\lVert\tau\rVert_{L^{2}(I)}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\int_{I}\left(\alpha_{L}\Pi_{L}+\sum_{i:I\subset\widetilde{I}_{i}}c_{i}\widetilde{B}_{i}+\alpha_{R}\Pi_{R}\right)^{2}d\sigma

\displaystyle\leq\int_{I}\left(\alpha_{L}^{2}+\sum_{i:I\subset\widetilde{I}_{i}}c_{i}^{2}+\alpha_{R}^{2}\right)\left(\Pi_{L}^{2}+\sum_{i:I\subset\widetilde{I}_{i}}\widetilde{B}_{i}^{2}+\Pi_{R}^{2}\right)d\sigma.

Expanding the integrand and using the fact that every function of the basis
\{\widetilde{B}_{i}\}_{i=1}^{n}
satisfies
0\leq\widetilde{B}_{i}\leq 1
, we obtain:

\lVert\tau\rVert_{L^{2}(I)}^{2}\leq\int_{I}\left(\alpha_{L}^{2}+\sum_{i:I\subset\widetilde{I}_{i}}c_{i}^{2}+\alpha_{R}^{2}\right)\left(\Pi_{L}^{2}+\sum_{i:I\subset\widetilde{I}_{i}}\widetilde{B}_{i}^{2}+\Pi_{R}^{2}\right)d\sigma.

Since each basis function
\{\widetilde{B}_{i}\}_{i=1}^{n}
satisfies
0\leq\widetilde{B}_{i}\leq 1
, we also have the inequality

\sum_{i:I\subset\widetilde{I}_{i}}\widetilde{B}_{i}^{2}\leq\sum_{i:I\subset\widetilde{I}_{i}}\widetilde{B}_{i}\leq 1.

Therefore, we obtain the following bound:

\lVert\tau\rVert_{L^{2}(I)}^{2}\leq\left(\alpha_{L}^{2}+\sum_{i:I\subset\widetilde{I}_{i}}c_{i}^{2}+\alpha_{R}^{2}\right)\left(\|\Pi_{L}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(I)}+\lvert I\rvert+\|\Pi_{R}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(I)}\right).

Using the estimates
\|\Pi_{L}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(I)}\leq\|b_{L}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\widehat{\Gamma})}\leq\lvert\supp(b_{L})\rvert
and similarly for
\Pi_{R}
, we obtain:

\lVert\tau\rVert_{L^{2}(I)}^{2}\leq\left(\alpha_{L}^{2}+\sum_{i:I\subset\widetilde{I}_{i}}c_{i}^{2}+\alpha_{R}^{2}\right)\left(\lvert\supp(b_{L})\rvert+\lvert I\rvert+\lvert\supp(b_{R})\rvert\right).

Since the mesh is quasi-uniform, there exists a constant
C>0
independent of
h
such that

\lvert\supp(b_{L})\rvert+\lvert\supp(b_{R})\rvert\leq C\lvert I\rvert.

Thus, we have the following estimate:

\lVert\tau\rVert_{L^{2}(I)}^{2}\leq C\left(\alpha_{L}^{2}+\sum_{i:I\subset\widetilde{I}_{i}}c_{i}^{2}+\alpha_{R}^{2}\right)\lvert I\rvert.

From
[

, there exists a constant
C>0
such that

\lVert\tau\rVert_{L^{2}(I)}^{2}\leq C\left(\alpha_{L}^{2}+\sum_{i:I\subset\widetilde{I}_{i}}c_{i}^{2}+\alpha_{R}^{2}\right)\lvert I\rvert\leq C\sum_{i:I\subset\widetilde{I}_{i}}\lVert\phi\rVert^{2}_{L^{2}(\widehat{I}_{i})}.

Finally, observing that
\alpha_{L}^{2}
and
\alpha_{R}^{2}
can be omitted if

or
I\nsubseteq\supp(b_{R})

8.2a

To demonstrate equation (
8.2b

n

\supp(\widetilde{B}_{2p-r})\cap\supp(\widetilde{B}_{n-2p+r+1})=\emptyset

Note that for all
2p-r+1\leq i\leq n-2p+r

\widehat{B}_{i}=\widetilde{B}_{i}

\int_{\widehat{\Gamma}}\phi\,\tau\,d\sigma=\int_{0}^{\xi_{3p-r+2}}\phi\,\tau\,d\sigma+\|\phi\|^{2}_{L^{2}\left(\xi_{3p-r+2},\xi_{n-2p+r+1}\right)}+\int_{\xi_{n-2p+r+1}}^{1}\phi\,\tau\,d\sigma.

(8.3)

We now focus on the first term of (

\int_{0}^{\xi_{3p-r+2}}\phi\,\tau\,d\sigma\geq C\,\|\phi\|^{2}_{L^{2}\left(0,\xi_{3p-r+2}\right)}.

(8.4)

Since

Remark 4

Lemma 8
.

The operator
\widehat{\mathcal{M}}^{*}_{h}\colon L^{2}(\widehat{\Gamma})\to\mathcal{S}^{p-1}_{\textsc{m}}
defined by

\int_{\widehat{\Gamma}}\widehat{\mathcal{M}}^{*}_{h}\phi\,\psi\,d\sigma=\int_{\widehat{\Gamma}}\phi\,\psi\,d\sigma,\quad\forall\psi\in\mathcal{S}^{p-1}_{0},

is well-defined and
L^{2}
-stable:

\|\widehat{\mathcal{M}}^{*}_{h}\phi\|_{L^{2}(\widehat{\Gamma})}\leq C\|\phi\|_{L^{2}(\widehat{\Gamma})},

Furthermore, when restricted to the domain
\mathcal{S}^{p-1}_{0}
, i.e., as an operator
\widehat{\mathcal{M}}^{*}_{h}\colon\mathcal{S}^{p-1}_{0}\to\mathcal{S}^{p-1}_{\textsc{m}}
, it is bijective.

Proof.

To demonstrate that the operator is well-defined, assume there exist two distinct elements
\tau_{1},\tau_{2}\in\mathcal{S}^{p-1}_{\textsc{m}}
such that

\int_{\widehat{\Gamma}}\tau_{k}\,\psi\,d\sigma=\int_{\widehat{\Gamma}}\phi\,\psi\,d\sigma,\quad\forall\psi\in\mathcal{S}^{p-1}_{0}.

By Proposition
9
, we have

\|\tau_{1}-\tau_{2}\|_{L^{2}(\widehat{\Gamma})}\leq C\sup_{\psi\in\mathcal{S}^{p-1}_{0},\,\|\psi\|_{L^{2}}=1}\int_{\widehat{\Gamma}}(\tau_{1}-\tau_{2})\,\psi\,d\sigma=0.

This implies
\tau_{1}=\tau_{2}
, confirming that the operator is well-defined.

The
L^{2}
-stability follows similarly to the proof of Lemma
3
.

To establish bijectivity as an operator between
\mathcal{S}^{p-1}_{0}
and
\mathcal{S}^{p-1}_{\textsc{m}}
, note that

\|\psi\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\widehat{\Gamma})}=\int_{\widehat{\Gamma}}\widehat{\mathcal{M}}^{*}_{h}\psi\,\psi\,d\sigma\leq\|\widehat{\mathcal{M}}^{*}_{h}\psi\|_{L^{2}(\widehat{\Gamma})}\|\psi\|_{L^{2}(\widehat{\Gamma})}.

Consequently, we obtain

\|\psi\|_{L^{2}(\widehat{\Gamma})}\leq\|\widehat{\mathcal{M}}^{*}_{h}\psi\|_{L^{2}(\widehat{\Gamma})},\quad\forall\psi\in\mathcal{S}^{p-1}_{0}.

If there exist two distinct functions
\psi_{1},\psi_{2}\in\mathcal{S}^{p-1}_{0}
such that
\widehat{\mathcal{M}}^{*}_{h}\psi_{1}=\widehat{\mathcal{M}}^{*}_{h}\psi_{2}
, it follows that
\|\psi_{1}-\psi_{2}\|_{L^{2}(\widehat{\Gamma})}=0
, implying
\psi_{1}=\psi_{2}
. Since
\dim(\mathcal{S}^{p-1}_{0})=\dim(\mathcal{S}^{p-1}_{\textsc{m}})
, surjectivity also follows.
∎

Proposition 10
.

For any
\phi\in\mathcal{S}^{p-1}_{0}
, the following inequality holds:

\sup_{\tau\in\mathcal{S}^{p-1}_{\textsc{m}}}\dfrac{\int_{\widehat{\Gamma}}\phi\tau\,d\sigma}{\lVert\tau\rVert_{H^{1}(\widehat{\Gamma})}}\geq C\lVert\phi\rVert_{H^{1}(\widehat{\Gamma})^{\prime}}.

(8.7)

Proof.

From Lemma
8
, we know that the operator
\widehat{\mathcal{M}}^{*}_{h}
is
L^{2}
-stable. We now verify that it is also
H^{1}
-stable. Let
\tau\in H^{1}(\widehat{\Gamma})
, and let
\widetilde{\Pi}_{h}:H^{1}(\widehat{\Gamma})\rightarrow\mathcal{S}^{p-1}_{\textsc{m}}
denote the quasi-interpolant operator defined in (
5.4
).
Recall that for
t=0,1
, it holds that

\lVert\tau-\widetilde{\Pi}_{h}\tau\rVert_{H^{t}(\widehat{\Gamma})}\leq Ch^{1-t}\lVert\tau\rVert_{H^{t}(\widehat{\Gamma})}.

(8.8)

Using the inverse inequality for splines, we derive:

\begin{split}\lVert\widehat{\mathcal{M}}^{*}_{h}\tau\rVert_{H^{1}(\widehat{\Gamma})}&\leq\lVert\widehat{\mathcal{M}}^{*}_{h}(\tau-\widetilde{\Pi}_{h}\tau)\rVert_{H^{1}(\widehat{\Gamma})}+\lVert\widehat{\mathcal{M}}^{*}_{h}\widetilde{\Pi}_{h}\tau\rVert_{H^{1}(\widehat{\Gamma})}\\
&\leq Ch^{-1}\lVert\tau-\widetilde{\Pi}_{h}\tau\rVert_{L^{2}(\widehat{\Gamma})}+\lVert\widetilde{\Pi}_{h}\tau\rVert_{H^{1}(\widehat{\Gamma})}.\end{split}

Applying
L^{2}
-stability and inequality (
8.8
), we have:

\begin{split}\lVert\widehat{\mathcal{M}}^{*}_{h}\tau\rVert_{H^{1}(\widehat{\Gamma})}&\leq Ch^{-1}\lVert\tau-\widetilde{\Pi}_{h}\tau\rVert_{L^{2}(\widehat{\Gamma})}+\lVert\widetilde{\Pi}_{h}\tau\rVert_{H^{1}(\widehat{\Gamma})}\\
&\leq C\lVert\tau\rVert_{H^{1}(\widehat{\Gamma})}.\end{split}

For any
\phi\in H^{1}(\widehat{\Gamma})^{\prime}
, it holds that:

\lVert\phi\rVert_{H^{1}(\widehat{\Gamma})^{\prime}}=\sup_{\eta\in H^{1}(\widehat{\Gamma})}\dfrac{\int_{\widehat{\Gamma}}\eta\phi\,d\sigma}{\lVert\eta\rVert_{H^{1}(\widehat{\Gamma})}},

(8.9)

and, in particular, this holds for any
\phi\in\mathcal{S}^{p-1}_{0}
. By Lemma
8
, for any
\psi\in\mathcal{S}^{p-1}_{\textsc{m}}
, there exists
\eta\in\mathcal{S}^{p-1}_{0}
such that
\psi=\widehat{\mathcal{M}}^{*}_{h}\eta
, which implies:

\int_{\widehat{\Gamma}}\psi\phi\,d\sigma=\int_{\widehat{\Gamma}}\eta\phi\,d\sigma,\quad\forall\phi\in\mathcal{S}^{p-1}_{0},

and
\lVert\psi\rVert_{H^{1}(\widehat{\Gamma})}\leq C^{-1}\lVert\eta\rVert_{H^{1}(\widehat{\Gamma})}
.
Thus, for all
\phi\in\mathcal{S}^{p-1}_{0}
, there exists
\psi=\widehat{\mathcal{M}}^{*}_{h}\eta\in\mathcal{S}^{p-1}_{\textsc{m}}
such that:

\dfrac{\int_{\widehat{\Gamma}}\psi\phi\,d\sigma}{\lVert\psi\rVert_{H^{1}(\widehat{\Gamma})}}\geq C\dfrac{\int_{\widehat{\Gamma}}\eta\phi\,d\sigma}{\lVert\eta\rVert_{H^{1}(\widehat{\Gamma})}}.

Taking the supremum over
\eta\in H^{1}(\widehat{\Gamma})
and applying (
8.9
), we establish (
8.7
).
∎

Proposition 11
.

For any
\tau\in\mathcal{S}^{p-1}_{\textsc{m},\int}
, the following inequality holds:

\sup_{\phi\in\mathcal{S}^{p-1}_{0,\int}}\dfrac{\int_{\widehat{\Gamma}}\phi\tau\,d\sigma}{\lVert\phi\rVert_{H^{1}(\widehat{\Gamma})^{\prime}}}\geq C\lVert\tau\rVert_{H^{1}(\widehat{\Gamma})}.

Proof.

From Proposition
10
, we know that for all
\phi\in\mathcal{S}^{p-1}_{0}
, there exists
\tau\in\mathcal{S}^{p-1}_{\textsc{m}}
such that

\int_{\widehat{\Gamma}}\phi\tau\,d\sigma\geq C\lVert\phi\rVert_{H^{1}(\widehat{\Gamma})^{\prime}}\lVert\tau\rVert_{H^{1}(\widehat{\Gamma})}.

This inequality holds in particular for all
\phi\in\mathcal{S}^{p-1}_{0,\int}
. Now, consider
\tau^{*}:=\tau-\bar{\tau}
, where
\bar{\tau}
denotes the mean value of
\tau
on
\widehat{\Gamma}
. Substituting
\tau^{*}
, we have

\int_{\widehat{\Gamma}}\phi\tau^{*}\,d\sigma=\int_{\widehat{\Gamma}}\phi\tau\,d\sigma\geq C\lVert\phi\rVert_{H^{1}(\widehat{\Gamma})^{\prime}}\lVert\tau\rVert_{H^{1}(\widehat{\Gamma})}.

It is straightforward to verify that
\lVert\tau^{*}\rVert_{H^{1}(\widehat{\Gamma})}\leq\lVert\tau\rVert_{H^{1}(\widehat{\Gamma})}
. Thus, for all
\phi\in\mathcal{S}^{p-1}_{0,\int}
, we obtain

\sup_{\tau^{*}\in\mathcal{S}^{p-1}_{\textsc{m},\int}}\dfrac{\int_{\widehat{\Gamma}}\phi\tau^{*}\,d\sigma}{\lVert\tau^{*}\rVert_{H^{1}(\widehat{\Gamma})}}\geq C\lVert\phi\rVert_{H^{1}(\widehat{\Gamma})^{\prime}}.

Finally, as discussed in Remark
3
, the interchange of the inf-sup spaces leads to the desired result.
∎

Theorem 2
.

For any
\mu\in\mathcal{S}^{p-2}_{\textsc{m}}
, the following inequality holds:

\sup_{\psi\in\mathcal{S}^{p}_{0,\partial}}\dfrac{\int_{\widehat{\Gamma}}\psi\mu\,d\sigma}{\lVert\psi\rVert_{L^{2}(\widehat{\Gamma})}}\geq C\lVert\mu\rVert_{L^{2}(\widehat{\Gamma})}.

(8.10)

Proof.

We begin by recalling Proposition
11
, which states that for any
\tau\in\mathcal{S}^{p-1}_{\textsc{m},\int}
, the following inequality holds:

\sup_{\psi\in\mathcal{S}^{p}_{0,\partial}}\frac{\int_{\widehat{\Gamma}}\psi^{\prime}\tau}{\lVert\psi^{\prime}\rVert_{H^{1}(\widehat{\Gamma})^{\prime}}}\geq C\lVert\tau\rVert_{H^{1}(\widehat{\Gamma})},

(8.11)

where we use the bijectivity between
\mathcal{S}^{p}_{0,\partial}
and
\mathcal{S}^{p-1}_{0,\int}
via the derivative operator, denoting
\psi^{\prime}=\phi
.

By integration by parts and applying Proposition
8
, the quotient in (
8.11
) can be estimated as:

\frac{\int_{\widehat{\Gamma}}\psi^{\prime}\tau}{\lVert\psi^{\prime}\rVert_{H^{1}(\widehat{\Gamma})^{\prime}}}\leq C\frac{\int_{\widehat{\Gamma}}\psi\tau^{\prime}}{\lVert\psi\rVert_{L^{2}(\widehat{\Gamma})}}=C\frac{\int_{\widehat{\Gamma}}\psi\mu}{\lVert\psi\rVert_{L^{2}(\widehat{\Gamma})}},

where we set
\mu=\tau^{\prime}
. Here,
\mu
is an arbitrary function in
\mathcal{S}^{p-2}_{\textsc{m}}
, and it satisfies

\lVert\mu\rVert_{L^{2}(\widehat{\Gamma})}=\lvert\tau\rvert_{H^{1}(\widehat{\Gamma})}\leq\lVert\tau\rVert_{H^{1}(\widehat{\Gamma})}.

Thus, inequality (
8.10
) follows directly.
∎

We now define the isogeometric multiplier space
M_{\ell}
as

M_{\ell}:=\left\{\mu\in L^{2}(\Gamma_{\ell}):\mu\circ\mathcal{F}_{s(\ell)}|_{\widehat{\Gamma}}=\widehat{\mu}\in\mathcal{S}^{p-2}_{\textsc{m}}(\widehat{\Gamma})\right\},

where
\mathcal{F}_{s(\ell)}
denotes the mapping,
\widehat{\Gamma}
is the reference domain, and
\mathcal{S}^{p-2}_{\textsc{m}}
represents the discrete multiplier space on
\widehat{\Gamma}
. Using this definition, we transfer the discrete inf-sup stability property of the pairing
\mathcal{S}^{p}_{0,\partial}/\mathcal{S}^{p-2}_{\textsc{m}}
on
\widehat{\Gamma}
to the mapped (isogeometric) pairing
W_{\ell}/M_{\ell}
on the physical domain
\Gamma_{\ell}=\partial\Omega_{m(\ell)}\cap\partial\Omega_{s(\ell)}
.

Theorem 3
.

For sufficiently small
h
, the following holds for each
\mu\in M_{\ell}
:

\sup_{\psi\in W_{\ell}}\frac{\int_{\Gamma_{\ell}}\psi\mu\,d\sigma}{\|\psi\|_{L^{2}(\Gamma_{\ell})}}\geq C\|\mu\|_{L^{2}(\Gamma_{\ell})}.

Proof.

For
\psi\in M_{\ell}
, from (
6.1
), there exists
\varphi\in\widetilde{X}_{s(\ell),h}
such that
\psi=\partial_{n}\varphi|_{\Gamma_{\ell}}
, where

\widetilde{X}_{s(\ell),h}:=\left\{\varphi\in X_{s(\ell),h}\cap H^{1}_{0}(\Omega_{s(\ell)}):\;\partial_{n}\varphi|_{\partial\Omega_{s(\ell)}\setminus\Gamma_{\ell}}=0\right\}.

Assume, without loss of generality, that
\{\textbf{F}_{s(\ell)}(0,\widehat{y}),\;\widehat{y}\in[0,1]\}=\Gamma_{\ell}
, and that
\widehat{\Gamma}=\{0\}\times[0,1]
is the preimage of
\Gamma_{\ell}
. Then, there exists
\widehat{\varphi}\in\widehat{Y}
, with
\varphi=\widehat{\varphi}\circ\textbf{F}_{s(\ell)}^{-1}
, where

\widehat{Y}:=\left\{\widehat{\varphi}\in\mathcal{S}^{\mathbf{p}}_{k}\cap H^{1}_{0}(\widehat{\Omega}):\;\partial_{\widehat{n}}\widehat{\varphi}|_{\partial\widehat{\Omega}\setminus\widehat{\Gamma}}=0\right\}.

Let
\widehat{\rho}(\widehat{y})=\lVert\partial_{\widehat{y}}\textbf{F}_{s(\ell)}(0,\widehat{y})\rVert
, and define the coefficients

\begin{split}\alpha_{s(\ell)}(\widehat{y})&:=\det\left[\partial_{\widehat{x}}\textbf{F}_{s(\ell)}(0,\widehat{y})\,\partial_{\widehat{y}}\textbf{F}_{s(\ell)}(0,\widehat{y})\right]^{-1},\\
\beta_{s(\ell)}(\widehat{y})&:=-\dfrac{\partial_{\widehat{x}}\textbf{F}_{s(\ell)}(0,\widehat{y})\cdot\partial_{\widehat{y}}\textbf{F}_{s(\ell)}(0,\widehat{y})}{{\widehat{\rho}}^{2}\det\left[\partial_{\widehat{x}}\textbf{F}_{s(\ell)}(0,\widehat{y})\,\partial_{\widehat{y}}\textbf{F}_{s(\ell)}(0,\widehat{y})\right]}.\end{split}

From
[
11
]
, specifically in the proof of Proposition 1 and 2 with
\gamma=1
, we have

\begin{split}\sup_{\psi\in W_{\ell}}\dfrac{\int_{\Gamma_{\ell}}\psi\,\mu\,d\sigma}{\lVert\psi\rVert_{L^{2}(\Gamma_{\ell})}}&=\sup_{\varphi\in\widetilde{X}_{s(\ell),h}}\dfrac{\int_{\Gamma_{\ell}}\partial_{n}\varphi\,\mu\,d\sigma}{\lVert\partial_{n}\varphi\rVert_{L^{2}(\Gamma_{\ell})}}\\
&=\sup_{\widehat{\varphi}\in\widehat{Y}}\dfrac{\int_{\widehat{\Gamma}}\left(\alpha_{s(\ell)}\partial_{\widehat{x}}\widehat{\varphi}+\beta_{s(\ell)}\partial_{\widehat{y}}\widehat{\varphi}\right)\widehat{\mu}\,\widehat{\rho}\,d\widehat{y}}{\lVert\left(\alpha_{s(\ell)}\partial_{\widehat{x}}\widehat{\varphi}+\beta_{s(\ell)}\partial_{\widehat{y}}\widehat{\varphi}\right)\sqrt{\widehat{\rho}}\rVert_{L^{2}(\widehat{\Gamma})}}.\end{split}

Since
\widehat{\eta}:=\partial_{\widehat{x}}\widehat{\varphi}|_{\widehat{\Gamma}}\in\mathcal{S}^{p}_{0,\partial}(\widehat{\Gamma})
and
\partial_{\widehat{y}}\widehat{\varphi}|_{\widehat{\Gamma}}=0
for all
\widehat{\phi}\in\widehat{Y}
, we obtain

\begin{split}\sup_{\psi\in W_{\ell}}\dfrac{\int_{\Gamma_{\ell}}\psi\,\mu\,d\sigma}{\lVert\psi\rVert_{L^{2}(\Gamma_{\ell})}}&=\sup_{\widehat{\varphi}\in\widehat{Y}}\dfrac{\int_{\widehat{\Gamma}}\alpha_{s(\ell)}\partial_{\widehat{x}}\widehat{\phi}\,\widehat{\mu}\,\widehat{\rho}\,d\widehat{y}}{\lVert\alpha_{s(\ell)}\partial_{\widehat{x}}\widehat{\phi}\,\sqrt{\widehat{\rho}}\rVert_{L^{2}(\widehat{\Gamma})}}\\
&\geq C^{-1}\sup_{\widehat{\eta}\in\mathcal{S}^{p}_{0,\partial}(\widehat{\Gamma})}\dfrac{\int_{\widehat{\Gamma}}(\widehat{\mu}\,\alpha_{s(\ell)}\widehat{\rho})\,\widehat{\eta}\,d\widehat{y}}{\lVert\widehat{\eta}\rVert_{L^{2}(\widehat{\Gamma})}},\end{split}

where we have used Assumption
4
.

Let
\widehat{\pi}^{*}\colon L^{2}(\widehat{\Gamma})\to\mathcal{S}^{p-2}_{\textsc{m}}(\widehat{\Gamma})
be a local projection with optimal approximation properties. The integration weight
\rho
is smooth except at the mesh lines, where it is only
C^{r-1}
. The following super-approximation holds:

\lVert\widehat{\mu}\alpha_{s(\ell)}\widehat{\rho}-\widehat{\pi}^{*}(\widehat{\mu}\alpha_{s(\ell)}\widehat{\rho})\rVert_{L^{2}(\widehat{\Gamma})}\leq C^{*}h\lVert\widehat{\mu}\rVert_{L^{2}(\widehat{\Gamma})},

as shown in
[
35
, proof of Theorem 13]
. We now have

\begin{split}\dfrac{\int_{\widehat{\Gamma}}(\widehat{\mu}\,\alpha_{s(\ell)}\widehat{\rho})\,\widehat{\eta}\,d\widehat{y}}{\lVert\widehat{\eta}\rVert_{L^{2}(\widehat{\Gamma})}}&=\dfrac{\int_{\widehat{\Gamma}}\widehat{\pi}^{*}(\widehat{\mu}\,\alpha_{s(\ell)}\widehat{\rho})\,\widehat{\eta}\,d\widehat{y}}{\lVert\widehat{\eta}\rVert_{L^{2}(\widehat{\Gamma})}}+\dfrac{\int_{\widehat{\Gamma}}(\widehat{\mu}\,\alpha_{s(\ell)}\widehat{\rho}-\widehat{\pi}^{*}(\widehat{\mu}\,\alpha_{s(\ell)}\widehat{\rho}))\,\widehat{\eta}\,d\widehat{y}}{\lVert\widehat{\eta}\rVert_{L^{2}(\widehat{\Gamma})}}\\
&\geq\dfrac{\int_{\widehat{\Gamma}}\widehat{\pi}^{*}(\widehat{\mu}\,\alpha_{s(\ell)}\widehat{\rho})\,\widehat{\eta}\,d\widehat{y}}{\lVert\widehat{\eta}\rVert_{L^{2}(\widehat{\Gamma})}}-\lVert\widehat{\mu}\,\alpha_{s(\ell)}\widehat{\rho}-\widehat{\pi}^{*}(\widehat{\mu}\,\alpha_{s(\ell)}\widehat{\rho})\rVert_{L^{2}(\widehat{\Gamma})},\end{split}

where the last step follows from the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality. Thus, due to Theorem
2
, we can bound

\sup_{\widehat{\eta}\in\mathcal{S}^{p}_{0,\partial}(\widehat{\Gamma})}\dfrac{\int_{\widehat{\Gamma}}(\widehat{\mu}\,\alpha_{s(\ell)}\widehat{\rho})\,\widehat{\eta}\,d\widehat{y}}{\lVert\widehat{\eta}\rVert_{L^{2}(\widehat{\Gamma})}}\geq C\lVert\widehat{\pi}^{*}(\widehat{\mu}\,\alpha_{s(\ell)}\widehat{\rho})\rVert_{L^{2}(\widehat{\Gamma})}-\lVert\widehat{\mu}\,\alpha_{s(\ell)}\widehat{\rho}-\widehat{\pi}^{*}(\widehat{\mu}\,\alpha_{s(\ell)}\widehat{\rho})\rVert_{L^{2}(\widehat{\Gamma})}

Since

\lVert\widehat{\mu}\rVert_{L^{2}(\widehat{\Gamma})}\leq\lVert\widehat{\pi}^{*}(\widehat{\mu}\alpha_{s(\ell)}\widehat{\rho})\rVert_{L^{2}(\widehat{\Gamma})}+\lVert\widehat{\pi}^{*}(\widehat{\mu}\alpha_{s(\ell)}\widehat{\rho})-\widehat{\mu}\alpha_{s(\ell)}\widehat{\rho}\rVert_{L^{2}(\widehat{\Gamma})},

for sufficiently small
h
, it holds

\begin{split}\sup_{\widehat{\eta}\in\mathcal{S}^{p}_{0,\partial}(\widehat{\Gamma})}\dfrac{\int_{\widehat{\Gamma}}(\widehat{\mu}\,\alpha_{s(\ell)}\widehat{\rho})\,\widehat{\eta}\,d\widehat{y}}{\lVert\widehat{\eta}\rVert_{L^{2}(\widehat{\Gamma})}}&\geq C\lVert\widehat{\mu}\,\alpha_{s(\ell)}\widehat{\rho}\rVert_{L^{2}(\widehat{\Gamma})}-C\lVert\widehat{\mu}\,\alpha_{s(\ell)}\widehat{\rho}-\widehat{\pi}^{*}(\widehat{\mu}\,\alpha_{s(\ell)}\widehat{\rho})\rVert_{L^{2}(\widehat{\Gamma})}\\
&\geq C\lVert\widehat{\mu}\rVert_{L^{2}(\widehat{\Gamma})}-Ch\lVert\widehat{\mu}\rVert_{L^{2}(\widehat{\Gamma})}\\
&\geq C\lVert\widehat{\mu}\rVert_{L^{2}(\widehat{\Gamma})}.\end{split}

Again, using an equivalence of norms, we can bound

\lVert\widehat{\mu}\rVert_{L^{2}(\widehat{\Gamma})}\geq C\lVert\mu\rVert_{L^{2}(\Gamma_{\ell})},

and the statement follows.
∎

Corollary 4
.

Let
h
be sufficiently small. For each
\mu\in M_{h}
, the following inequality holds:

\sup_{\psi\in W_{h}}\dfrac{\int_{\Sigma}\psi\mu d\sigma}{\lVert\psi\rVert_{L^{2}(\Sigma)}}\geq C\lVert\mu\rVert_{L^{2}(\Sigma)}.

for the pairing

, discussed in Section

. Additionally, we demonstrate that optimal convergence is achieved for pairings not covered by the theoretical framework.

All tests were conducted using Matlab R2024a and the GeoPDEs toolbox

4
). However, this assumption is not critical to the theoretical results. Numerical experiments, such as those on the quarter-circle domain, indicate that stability and accuracy are preserved on NURBS-parametrized domains.

For all tests, results obtained with higher-degree, refined meshes that are corrupted by round-off errors are excluded from the plots.

10
Conclusions

In this work, we propose an isogeometric method for solving the biharmonic equation on
C^{0}
-conforming multi-patch domains. The required
C^{1}
-continuity across patch interfaces is enforced weakly through a mortar formulation. We use a spline space of degree
p
for the primal space, and a spline space of degree
p-2
for the Lagrange multiplier space, which is built on the same underlying mesh as the primal space but with a reduced knot vector near the patch vertices. This approach ensures that the method satisfies a discrete inf-sup stability condition. As a result, we have established well-posedness for the method and proven optimal a priori error estimates in a patch-wise
H^{2}
broken norm. Numerical examples confirm these error estimates and demonstrate optimal-order convergence in
L^{2}
,
H^{1}
, and
L^{\infty}
norms.

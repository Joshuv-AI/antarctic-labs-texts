Simplices and sets of positive upper density in ℝ d \mathbb{R}^{d}
Lauren Huckaba Neil Lyall Ákos Magyar

Abstract
. We prove an extension of Bourgain’s theorem on pinned distances in measurable subset of ℝ 2 \mathbb{R}^{2} of positive upper density, namely Theorem 1 ′ 1^{\prime} in [ 1 ] , to pinned non-degenerate k k -dimensional simplices in measurable subset of ℝ d \mathbb{R}^{d} of positive upper density whenever d ≥ k + 2 d\geq k+2 and k k is any positive integer.



2000 Mathematics Subject Classification
11B30, 42B25, 42A38

1.
Introduction

Recall that the
upper density

\overline{\delta}
of a measurable set
A\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d}
is defined by

\overline{\delta}(A)=\limsup_{N\rightarrow\infty}\frac{|A\cap B_{N}|}{|B_{N}|},

where
|\cdot|
denotes Lebesgue measure on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
and

B_{N}
denotes the cube
[-N/2,N/2]^{d}
.

1.1.
Existing results

A result of Katznelson and Weiss
[
3
]
states that if
A
is a measurable subset of
\mathbb{R}^{2}
of positive upper density, then its distance set

\text{dist}(A)=\{|x-y|\,:\,x,y\in A\}

contains all large numbers. This result was later reproven using Fourier analytic techniques by Bourgain in
[
1
]
. Bourgain in fact established more, namely the following generalization and “pinned variant”.

Theorem 1.1

(Theorem 2 in
[
1
]
)
.

Let
\Delta
be a fixed non-degenerate
k
-dimensional simplex. If
A
is a measurable subset of
\mathbb{R}^{d}
of positive upper density with
d\geq k+1
, then
there exists a threshold
\lambda_{0}=\lambda_{0}(A,\Delta)

such that for all
\lambda\geq\lambda_{0}
one has

(1)

x+\lambda\cdot U(\Delta)\subseteq A

for some
x\in A
and
U\in SO(d)
.

In Theorem
1.1
, and throughout this article, we refer to a set
\Delta=\{0,v_{1},\dots,v_{k}\}
of points in
\mathbb{R}^{k}
as a non-degenerate
k
-dimensional simplex if the vectors
\{v_{1},\dots,v_{k}\}
are linearly independent.

Theorem 1.2

(Pinned distances, Theorem
1^{\prime}
in
[
1
]
)
.

If
A
is a measurable subset of
\mathbb{R}^{2}
of positive upper density, then there exist
\lambda_{0}=\lambda_{0}(A)
such that for any given
\lambda_{1}\geq\lambda_{0}
there is a fixed
x\in A
such that

(2)

A\cap(x+\lambda\cdot S^{1})\neq\emptyset

for all
\lambda_{0}\leq\lambda\leq\lambda_{1}
, where
S^{1}
denotes the unit circle centered at the origin in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
.

1.2.
New results

Throughout this article, we use
\mu
to denote the unique Haar measure on
SO(d)
.

Our first result is the following (optimal) strengthening of Theorem
1.1
above.

Theorem 1.3

(Density of Embedded Simplices)
.

Let
\Delta=\{0,v_{1},\dots,v_{k}\}\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{k}
be a fixed non-degenerate
k
-dimensional simplex and
\varepsilon>0
.

If
A
is a measurable subset of
\mathbb{R}^{d}
with
d\geq k+1
, then there exist
\lambda_{0}=\lambda_{0}(A,\Delta,\varepsilon)
such that

(3)

\int_{SO(d)}\overline{\delta}(A\cap(A+\lambda\cdot U(v_{1}))\cap\cdots\cap(A+\lambda\cdot U(v_{k})))\,d\mu(U)>\overline{\delta}(A)^{k+1}-\varepsilon

for all
\lambda\geq\lambda_{0}
.
In particular, for each
\lambda\geq\lambda_{0}
we may conclude that there exist
U\in SO(d)
such that

(4)

\overline{\delta}(A\cap(A+\lambda\cdot U(v_{1}))\cap\cdots\cap(A+\lambda\cdot U(v_{k})))>\overline{\delta}(A)^{k+1}-\varepsilon

and there exist
x\in A
such that

(5)

\mu\bigl(\bigl\{U\in SO(d)\,:\,x+\lambda\cdot U(\Delta)\subseteq A\bigr\}\bigr)>\overline{\delta}(A)^{k}-2\varepsilon.

The main result of this paper is the following (optimal) extension of Bourgain’s pinned distances theorem, Theorem
1.2
above, to non-degenerate
k
-dimensional simplices when
k\geq 2
.

Theorem 1.4

(Density of Embedded Pinned Simplices)
.

Let
\Delta=\{0,v_{1},\dots,v_{k}\}\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{k}
be a fixed non-degenerate
k
-dimensional simplex and
\varepsilon>0
.

If
A
is a measurable subset of
\mathbb{R}^{d}
with
d\geq k+2
, then there exist
\lambda_{0}=\lambda_{0}(A,\Delta,\varepsilon)
such that for any given
\lambda_{1}\geq\lambda_{0}
there is a fixed
x\in A
such that

(6)

\mu\bigl(\bigl\{U\in SO(d)\,:\,x+\lambda\cdot U(\Delta)\subseteq A\bigr\}\bigr)>\overline{\delta}(A)^{k}-\varepsilon\quad\text{for all}\quad\lambda_{0}\leq\lambda\leq\lambda_{1}.

We remark that Theorem
1.4
should hold whenever
d\geq k+1
. However, extending our result to this range appears to require an essentially non-Fourier analytic approach, specifically an adaptation of the geometric arguments in
Bourgain’s circular maximal function theorem

[
2
]
to the configuration spaces considered in this article. We plan to address this strengthening of Theorem
1.4
in a separate article.

We further remark that both Theorem
1.3
and Theorem
1.4
also hold, with the proofs essentially unchanged, if the notion of upper density is replaced with the weaker notion of upper Banach density.

1.3.
Outline of paper

We will adapt Bourgain’s approach in
[
1
]
and deduce Theorems
1.3
and
1.4
from two quantitative compact variants, namely Propositions
2.1
and
2.2
respectively. The reduction of Theorems
1.3
and
1.4

. In Section

2.2

5.1
and
5.2
, the proofs of which are presented in Section
6
.

1.4.
Further Notation

Throughout this article we use the notation
X\ll Y
to denote the fact that
X\leq C\,Y
for some absolute constant
C>0
that depends only the dimension
d
and
X\lll Y
to denote the fact that
X\leq c\,Y
for some
sufficiently small
constant
c>0
.

For any given set
A\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d}
we use
1_{A}
to denote the characteristic function of the set
A
,
while for any given integrable function
f:\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{C}
we define its
Fourier transform

\widehat{f}:\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{C}
, by

(7)

\widehat{f}(\xi)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}f(x)e^{-2\pi ix\cdot\xi}\,dx.

2.
Reducing Theorems
1.3
and
1.4
to Key Dichotomy Propositions

2.1.
Dichotomy Propositions

In Section
2.2
below we shall see that Theorems
1.3
and
1.4
are easy consequences of the following two quantitative compact variants, namely Propositions
2.1
and
2.2
.

Proposition
2.1
below (respectively Proposition
2.2
) establishes that if
A
does not contain the “expected” number of unpinned isometric copies of
\lambda\cdot\Delta
(respectively pinned isometric copies of
\lambda\cdot\Delta
with
\lambda_{0}\leq\lambda\leq\lambda_{1}
at some point
x\in A
), then this “non-random” behavior will be “detected” by the Fourier transform of the characteristic function of
A
and result in a concentration of its
L^{2}
-mass on appropriate annuli.

Proposition 2.1

(Dichotomy for Theorem
1.3
)
.

Let
\Delta=\{0,v_{1},\dots,v_{k}\}\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{k}
be a fixed non-degenerate
k
-dimensional simplex,
\varepsilon>0
,
0<\eta\lll\varepsilon^{5/2}
, and
N\geq C_{\Delta}\eta^{-4}
.

If
A\subseteq B_{N}\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d}
with
d\geq k+1
, then for any
\lambda
satisfying
1\leq\lambda\leq\eta^{4}N
one of the following statements must hold:

(i)

\int_{SO(d)}\frac{|A\cap(A+\lambda\cdot U(v_{1}))\cap\cdots\cap(A+\lambda\cdot U(v_{k}))|}{N^{d}}\,d\mu(U)>\left(\frac{|A|}{N^{d}}\right)^{k+1}-\varepsilon

(ii)

\dfrac{1}{|A|}\int_{\Omega_{\lambda}}|\widehat{1_{A}}(\xi)|^{2}\,d\xi\gg\varepsilon^{2}

where

\Omega_{\lambda}=\Omega_{\lambda}(\eta)=\{\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\,:\,\eta^{2}\,\lambda^{-1}\leq|\xi|\leq\eta^{-2}\lambda^{-1}\}.

Proposition 2.2

(Dichotomy for Theorem
1.4
)
.

Let
\Delta=\{0,v_{1},\dots,v_{k}\}\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{k}
be a fixed non-degenerate
k
-dimensional simplex,
\varepsilon>0
,
0<\eta\lll\varepsilon^{3}
, and
N\geq C_{\Delta}\eta^{-4}
.

If
A\subseteq B_{N}\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d}
with
d\geq k+2
, then for any pair
(\lambda_{0},\lambda_{1})
satisfying
1\leq\lambda_{0}\leq\lambda_{1}\leq\eta^{4}N
one of the following statements must hold:

(i)

there exist
x\in A
with the property that

\mu\bigl(\bigl\{U\in SO(d)\,:\,x+\lambda\cdot U(\Delta)\subseteq A\bigr\}\bigr)>\left(\frac{|A|}{N^{d}}\right)^{k}-\varepsilon\quad\text{for all}\quad\lambda_{0}\leq\lambda\leq\lambda_{1}

(ii)

\dfrac{1}{|A|}\int_{\Omega_{\lambda_{0},\lambda_{1}}}|\widehat{1_{A}}(\xi)|^{2}\,d\xi\gg\varepsilon^{2}

where

\Omega_{\lambda_{0},\lambda_{1}}=\Omega_{\lambda_{0},\lambda_{1}}(\eta)=\{\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\,:\,\eta^{2}\,\lambda_{1}^{-1}\leq|\xi|\leq\eta^{-2}\lambda_{0}^{-1}\}.

2.2.
Proof of Theorems
1.3
and
1.4

2.2.1.
Proof that Proposition
2.1
implies Theorem
1.3

Let
\varepsilon>0
and
0<\eta\ll\varepsilon^{5/2}
.
Suppose that
A\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d}
with
d\geq k+1
is a set for which the conclusion of Theorem
1.3
fails to hold, namely that there
exists arbitrarily large integers
\lambda
for which

\int_{SO(d)}\overline{\delta}(A\cap(A+\lambda\cdot U(v_{1}))\cap\cdots\cap(A+\lambda\cdot U(v_{k})))\,d\mu(U)\leq\overline{\delta}(A)^{k+1}-\varepsilon.

For a fixed integer
J\ggg\varepsilon^{-2}
we now choose a sequence
\{\lambda^{(j)}\}_{j=1}^{J}
of such
\lambda
’s with the additional property that

1\leq\lambda^{(j)}\leq\eta^{4}\lambda^{(j+1)}

for
1\leq j<J
.
We now choose
N
so that
\lambda^{(J)}\leq\eta^{4}N
and that we simultaneously have both that

(8)

\overline{\delta}(A)^{k+1}-\varepsilon/2\leq\left(\frac{|A\cap B_{N}|}{N^{d}}\right)^{k+1}-\varepsilon/4

and that

\int_{SO(d)}\frac{|A_{N}\cap(A_{N}+\lambda^{(j)}\cdot U(v_{1}))\cap\cdots\cap(A_{N}+\lambda^{(j)}\cdot U(v_{k}))|}{N^{d}}\,d\mu(U)\leq\overline{\delta}(A)^{k+1}-\varepsilon/2

holds for all
1\leq j\leq J
, where
A_{N}=A\cap B_{N}
. For the last inequality we exploited Fatou’s Lemma.

Abusing notation and denoting the set
A_{N}=A\cap B_{N}
by
A
, an application of Proposition
2.1
, with
\varepsilon
replaced with
\varepsilon/4
, thus allows us to conclude that for this set one must have

(9)

\sum_{j=1}^{J}\frac{1}{|A|}\int_{\Omega_{\lambda^{(j)}}}|\widehat{1_{A}}(\xi)|^{2}\,d\xi\gg J\varepsilon^{2}>1.

On the other hand it follows from the disjointness property of the sets
\Omega_{\lambda^{(j)}}
, which we guaranteed by our initial choice of sequence
\{\lambda^{(j)}\}
, and Plancherel’s Theorem that

(10)

\sum_{j=1}^{J}\frac{1}{|A|}\int_{\Omega_{\lambda^{(j)}}}|\widehat{1_{A}}(\xi)|^{2}\,d\xi\leq\frac{1}{|A|}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|\widehat{1_{A}}(\xi)|^{2}\,d\xi=1

giving a contradiction. ∎

2.2.2.
Proof that Proposition
2.2
implies Theorem
1.4

Let
\varepsilon>0
and
0<\eta\ll\varepsilon^{3}
.
Suppose that
A\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d}
with
d\geq k+2
is a set for which the conclusion of Theorem
1.4
fails to hold, namely that there
exists arbitrarily large pairs
(\lambda_{0},\lambda_{1})
of real numbers such that for all
x\in A
one has

\mu\bigl(\bigl\{U\in SO(d)\,:\,x+\lambda\cdot U(\Delta)\subseteq A\bigr\}\bigr)\leq\overline{\delta}(A)^{k}-\varepsilon

for some
\lambda_{0}\leq\lambda\leq\lambda_{1}
.

For a fixed integer
J\ggg\varepsilon^{-2}
we choose a sequence of such pairs
\{(\lambda_{0}^{(j)},\lambda_{1}^{(j)})\}_{j=1}^{J}
with the property that

1\leq\lambda_{1}^{(j)}\leq\eta^{4}\lambda_{0}^{(j+1)}

for
1\leq j<J
.
We now choose
N
so that
\lambda_{1}^{(J)}\leq\eta^{4}N
and

(11)

\overline{\delta}(A)^{k}-\varepsilon\leq\left(\frac{|A\cap B_{N}|}{N^{d}}\right)^{k}-\varepsilon/2.

Abusing notation and denoting the set
A\cap B_{N}
by
A
, an application of Proposition
2.2
thus allows us to conclude that for this set one must have

(12)

\sum_{j=1}^{J}\frac{1}{|A|}\int_{\Omega_{\lambda_{0}^{(j)},\lambda_{1}^{(j)}}}|\widehat{1_{A}}(\xi)|^{2}\,d\xi\gg J\varepsilon^{2}>1.

On the other hand it follows from the disjointness property of the sets
\Omega_{\lambda_{0}^{(j)},\lambda_{1}^{(j)}}
, which we guaranteed by our initial choice of pair sequence
\{(\lambda_{0}^{(j)},\lambda_{1}^{(j)})\}
, and Plancherel’s Theorem that

(13)

\sum_{j=1}^{J}\frac{1}{|A|}\int_{\Omega_{\lambda_{0}^{(j)},\lambda_{1}^{(j)}}}|\widehat{1_{A}}(\xi)|^{2}\,d\xi\leq\frac{1}{|A|}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|\widehat{1_{A}}(\xi)|^{2}\,d\xi=1

giving a contradiction. ∎

3.
Preliminaries

3.1.
The multi-linear operators
\mathcal{A}^{(j)}_{\lambda}

Let
\Delta=\{0,v_{1},\dots,v_{k}\}
be our fixed
k
-dimensional simplex. Without loss of generality we may assume that
|v_{1}|=1
.
For each
1\leq j\leq k
we introduce the multi-linear operator

\mathcal{A}^{(j)}_{\lambda}
, defined initially for Schwartz functions
g_{1},\dots,g_{j}
, by

(14)

\mathcal{A}^{(j)}_{\lambda}(g_{1},\dots,g_{j})(x)=\int\cdots\int g_{1}(x-\lambda y_{1})\cdots g_{j}(x-\lambda y_{j})\,d\sigma^{(d-j)}_{y_{1},\dots,y_{j-1}}(y_{j})\cdots d\sigma^{(d-1)}(y_{1})

where
\sigma^{(d-1)}
denotes the normalized measure on the sphere
S^{d-1}(0,|v_{1}|)\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d}
induced by Lebesgue measure and

\sigma^{(d-j)}_{y_{1},\dots,y_{j-1}}

denotes, for each
2\leq j\leq k
, the normalized measure on the spheres

(15)

S^{d-j}_{x_{1},\dots,x_{j-1}}=S^{d-1}(0,|v_{j}|)\cap S^{d-1}(x_{1},|v_{j}-v_{1}|)\cap\cdots\cap S^{d-1}(x_{j-1},|v_{j}-v_{j-1}|)

where
S^{d-1}(x,r)=\{x^{\prime}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\,:\,|x-x^{\prime}|=r\}
.

The multi-linear operator
\mathcal{A}^{(j)}_{\lambda}
is a natural object for us to consider in light of the observation that it could have equivalently be defined for each
1\leq j\leq k
using the formula

(16)

\mathcal{A}^{(j)}_{\lambda}(g_{1},\dots,g_{j})(x):=\int_{SO(d)}g_{1}(x-\lambda\cdot U(v_{1}))\cdots g_{j}(x-\lambda\cdot U(v_{j}))\,d\mu(U)

and hence for any bounded measurable set
A\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d}
, the quantity

(17)

\bigl\langle 1_{A},\mathcal{A}^{(k)}_{\lambda}(1_{A},\dots,1_{A})\bigr\rangle=\int_{SO(d)}|A\cap(A+\lambda\cdot U(v_{1}))\cap\cdots\cap(A+\lambda\cdot U(v_{k}))|\,d\mu(U).

A trivial, but important, observation will be the fact that

(18)

\Bigl|\mathcal{A}^{(j)}_{\lambda}(g_{1},\dots,g_{j})(x)-g_{j}(x)\,\mathcal{A}^{(j-1)}_{\lambda}(g_{1},\dots,g_{j-1})(x)\Bigr|\leq\int\bigl|g_{j}(x-\lambda y)-g_{j}(x)\bigr|\,d\sigma^{(d-j)}_{y_{1},\dots,y_{j-1}}(y)

holds for some initial choices of frame
y_{1},\dots,y_{j-1}
with
y_{i}\cdot y_{i^{\prime}}=v_{i}\cdot v_{i^{\prime}}
for
1\leq i\leq i^{\prime}\leq j-1
.

3.2.
A second averaging operator and some basic estimates

We now introduce a second averaging operator, which we also denote by
\mathcal{A}^{(j)}_{\lambda}
, defined initially for any Schwartz function
g
, by

(19)

\mathcal{A}^{(j)}_{\lambda}(g)(x)=\int\cdots\int\Bigl|\int g(x-\lambda y_{j})\,d\sigma^{(d-j)}_{y_{1},\dots,y_{j-1}}(y_{j})\Bigr|\,d\sigma^{(d-j+1)}_{y_{1},\dots,y_{j-2}}(y_{j-1})\cdots d\sigma^{(d-1)}(y_{1})

Note that if the functions
g_{1},\dots,g_{j-1}
are all bounded in absolute value by
1
, then clearly

(20)

\bigl|\mathcal{A}^{(j)}_{\lambda}(g_{1},\dots,g_{j})(x)\bigr|\leq\mathcal{A}^{(j)}_{\lambda}(g_{j})(x).

Fix
1\leq j\leq k
. It is easy to see, using Minkowski’s inequality, that for any Schwartz function
g
we have the crude estimate

(21)

\int\bigl|\mathcal{A}^{(j)}_{\lambda}(g)(x)\bigr|^{2}\,dx\leq\int|g(x)|^{2}\,dx.

However, arguing more carefully one can just as easily obtain, using Plancherel’s identity, the estimate

(22)

\int\bigl|\mathcal{A}^{(j)}_{\lambda}(g)(x)\bigr|^{2}\,dx\leq\int\cdots\int\Bigl(\int|\widehat{g}(\xi)|^{2}\bigl|\widehat{d\sigma^{(d-j)}_{y_{1},\dots,y_{j-1}}}(\lambda\,\xi)\bigr|^{2}\,d\xi\Bigr)\,d\sigma^{(d-j+1)}_{y_{1},\dots,y_{j-2}}(y_{j-1})\cdots d\sigma^{(d-1)}(y_{1}),

where as usual

(23)

\widehat{d\nu}(\xi)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}e^{-2\pi ix\cdot\xi}\,d\nu(x)

denotes the Fourier transform of any complex-valued Borel measure
d\nu
.
In light of (
22
) it will come as little surprise that is the course of our arguments we will have use for the basic estimate

(24)

\bigl|\widehat{d\sigma^{(d-j)}_{y_{1},\dots,y_{j-1}}}(\xi)\bigr|+\bigl|\nabla\widehat{d\sigma^{(d-j)}_{y_{1},\dots,y_{j-1}}}(\xi)\bigr|\leq C_{\Delta}\bigl(1+\text{dist}(\xi,\text{span}\{y_{1},\dots,y_{j-1}\})\bigr)^{-(d-j)/2},

which is a consequence of the well-known asymptotic behavior of the Fourier transform of the measure on the unit sphere
S^{d-j}\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d-j+1}
induced by Lebesgue measure, see for example
[
5
]
.

3.3.
A smooth cutoff function
\psi
and some basic properties

Let

\psi:\mathbb{R}^{d}\rightarrow(0,\infty)
be a Schwartz function that satisfies

1=\widehat{\psi}(0)\geq\widehat{\psi}(\xi)\geq 0\quad\quad\text{and}\quad\quad\widehat{\psi}(\xi)=0\ \ \text{for}\ \ |\xi|>1.

As usual, for any given
t>0
, we define

(25)

\psi_{t}(x)=t^{-d}\psi(t^{-1}x).

First we record the trivial observation that

\int\psi_{t}(x)\,dx=\int\psi(x)\,dx=\widehat{\psi}(0)=1

as well as the simple, but important, observation that
\psi
may be chosen so that

(26)

\bigl|1-\widehat{\psi}_{t}(\xi)\bigr|=\bigl|1-\widehat{\psi}(t\xi)\bigr|\ll\min\{1,t|\xi|\}.

Finally we record a formulation, appropriate to our needs, of the fact that for any given small parameter
\eta
, our cutoff function
\psi_{t}(x)
will essentially supported where
|x|\leq\eta^{-1}t
and is approximately constant on smaller scales. More precisely,

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
\eta>0
and
t>0
, then

(27)

\int_{|x|\geq\eta^{-1}t}\psi_{t}(x)\,dx\ll\eta.

and

(28)

\int\int\bigl|\psi_{t}(x-\lambda y)-\psi_{t}(x)\bigr|\,d\sigma^{(d-j)}_{y_{1},\dots,y_{j-1}}(y)\,dx\ll\eta

for any
1\leq j\leq k
and any choice of frame
y_{1},\dots,y_{j-1}
provided
t\geq\eta^{-1}\lambda
.

Proof.

Estimate (
27
) is easily verified using the fact that
\psi
is a Schwartz function on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
as

\int_{|x|\geq\eta^{-1}t}\psi_{t}(x)\,dx=\int_{|x|\geq\eta^{-1}}\psi(x)\,dx\ll\int_{|x|\geq\eta^{-1}}(1+|x|)^{-d-1}\,dx\ll\,\eta.

To verify estimate (
28
) we make use of the fact that both

\psi
and its derivative are rapidly decreasing, specifically

\displaystyle\int\int\bigl|\psi_{t}(x-\lambda y)-\psi_{t}(x)\bigr|\,d\sigma^{(d-j)}_{y_{1},\dots,y_{j-1}}(y)\,dx

\displaystyle\leq\int\int\bigl|\psi(x-\lambda y/t)-\psi(x)\bigr|\,d\sigma^{(d-j)}_{y_{1},\dots,y_{j-1}}(y)\,dx

\displaystyle\ll\frac{\lambda}{t}\int(1+|x|)^{-d-1}\,dx\ll\frac{\lambda}{t}

for any choice of frame
y_{1},\dots,y_{j-1}
.∎

4.
Proof of Proposition
2.1

Let
f=1_{A}
and
\delta=|A|/N^{d}
.
Suppose that
1\leq\lambda\leq\eta^{4}N
and that (i) does not hold, then

(29)

\langle f,\mathcal{A}^{(k)}_{\lambda}(f,\dots,f)\rangle\leq\langle f,\delta^{k}-\varepsilon\rangle=(\delta^{k}-\varepsilon)|A|.

If we let
f_{1}:=f*\psi_{\eta^{-1}\lambda}
, then by (
18
) and (
28
) it follows that for all
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
1\leq j\leq k
we have

(30)

\Bigl|\mathcal{A}^{(j)}_{\lambda}(f,\dots,f,f_{1})(x)-f_{1}(x)\,\mathcal{A}^{(j-1)}_{\lambda}(f,\dots,f)(x)\Bigr|\ll\eta

and
consequently

(31)

f_{1}(x)^{k}+\sum_{j=1}^{k}f_{1}(x)^{k-j}\mathcal{A}^{(j)}_{\lambda}(f,\dots,f,f-f_{1})(x)\ll\mathcal{A}^{(k)}_{\lambda}(f,\dots,f)(x)+\eta.

Together with (
29
) this gives

(32)

\sum_{j=1}^{k}\bigl\langle ff_{1}^{k-j},\mathcal{A}^{(j)}_{\lambda}(f,\dots,f,f-f_{1})\bigr\rangle\leq\langle f,\delta^{k}-f_{1}^{k}-\varepsilon/2\rangle

provided
\eta\lll\varepsilon
. We will now combine this with the following result, which we isolate as a lemma.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
0<\eta\ll\delta
and
f_{1}:=f*\psi_{\eta^{-1}\lambda}
, then

(33)

\langle f,\delta^{k}-f_{1}^{k}\rangle\ll\eta|A|

Combining Lemma
4.1
with (
32
) we see that if
\eta\lll\varepsilon
and (
29
) holds,
then there exist
1\leq j\leq k
such that

(34)

\Bigl|\bigl\langle ff_{1}^{k-j},\mathcal{A}^{(j)}_{\lambda}(f,\dots,f,f-f_{1})\bigr\rangle\Bigr|\gg\varepsilon|A|

and hence, using (
20
) and the fact that
0\leq f_{1}\leq 1
, that

(35)

\bigl\langle f,\mathcal{A}^{(j)}_{\lambda}(f-f_{1})\bigr\rangle\gg\varepsilon|A|.

The final ingredient in the proof of Proposition
2.1
is the following

Lemma 4.2

(Error term)
.

If
f_{2}:=f*\psi_{\eta^{2}\lambda}
,
then for any
1\leq j\leq k
we have the estimate

(36)

\bigl\langle f,\mathcal{A}^{(j)}_{\lambda}(f-f_{2})\bigr\rangle\ll\eta^{2/5}|A|.

Indeed, since

\bigl\langle f,\mathcal{A}^{(j)}_{\lambda}(f_{2}-f_{1})\bigr\rangle\geq\bigl\langle f,\mathcal{A}^{(j)}_{\lambda}(f-f_{1})\bigr\rangle-\bigl\langle f,\mathcal{A}^{(j)}_{\lambda}(f-f_{2})\bigr\rangle

we see that (
35
) together with Lemma
4.2
will imply that if
\eta\lll\varepsilon^{5/2}
and (
29
) holds, then there exist
1\leq j\leq k
such that

(37)

\bigl\langle f,\mathcal{A}^{(j)}_{\lambda}(f_{2}-f_{1})\bigr\rangle\gg\varepsilon|A|.

It then follows, via Cauchy-Schwarz and Plancherel, that

(38)

\int\bigl|\widehat{f}(\xi)\bigr|^{2}\bigl|\widehat{\psi}_{\eta^{2}\lambda}(\xi)-\widehat{\psi}_{\eta^{-1}\lambda}(\xi)\bigr|^{2}\,d\xi\gg\varepsilon^{2}\,|A|,

which is essentially the estimate that we are trying to prove and since (
26
) implies that

(39)

\bigl|\widehat{\psi}_{\eta^{2}\lambda}(\xi)-\widehat{\psi}_{\eta^{-1}\lambda}(\xi)\bigr|\ll\eta

whenever
\xi\notin\Omega_{\lambda}
, it indeed sufficies and concludes the proof of Proposition
2.1
.∎

4.1.
Proof of Lemma
4.1

It suffices to establish the result when
k=1
, specifically that

(40)

\int f(x)f_{1}(x)\,dx\geq\delta(1-C\eta)\,|A|

for some constant
C>0
, since from Hölder’s inequality we would then obtain

\delta^{k}(1-C\eta)^{k}\,|A|^{k}\leq\Bigl(\int f(x)f_{1}(x)\,dx\Bigr)^{k}\leq|A|^{k-1}\int f(x)f_{1}(x)^{k}\,dx

from which the full result immediately follows since
0<\eta\lll 1
. Towards establishing (
40
) we note that using Parserval and the fact that
0\leq\widehat{\psi}\leq 1
we have

(41)

\int f(x)f_{1}(x)\,dx=\int|\widehat{f}(\xi)|^{2}\widehat{\psi}(\eta^{-1}\lambda\,\xi)\,d\xi\geq\int|\widehat{f}(\xi)|^{2}|\widehat{\psi}(\eta^{-1}\lambda\,\xi)|^{2}\,d\xi=\int f_{1}(x)^{2}\,dx

and as such we need only show that

(42)

\int f_{1}(x)^{2}\,dx\geq\delta(1-C\eta)\,|A|

for some constant
C>0
.
Since an application of Cauchy-Schwarz gives that

(43)

\int_{B_{N}}f_{1}(x)^{2}\,dx\geq\frac{1}{|B_{N}|}\Bigl(\int_{B_{N}}f_{1}(x)\,dx\Bigr)^{2}

our task is further reduces to simply showing that

(44)

\int_{B_{N}}f_{1}(x)\,dx\geq(1-C\eta)|A|

for some constant
C>0
.
To establish (
44
) we now let
N^{\prime}=N+\eta^{-2}\lambda
and write

(45)

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}f_{1}(x)\,dx=\int_{B_{N}}f_{1}(x)\,dx+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus B_{N^{\prime}}}f_{1}(x)\,dx+\int_{B_{N^{\prime}}\setminus B_{N}}f_{1}(x)\,dx.

The fact that
\lambda\leq\eta^{4}N
ensures that

(46)

\frac{|B_{N^{\prime}}\backslash B_{N}|}{|B_{N}|}\ll\left(\frac{N^{\prime}}{N}-1\right)\ll\eta^{-2}\frac{\lambda}{N}\ll\eta^{2}

and hence, since
\eta\lll\delta
and
0\leq f_{1}\leq 1
, that

\int_{B_{N^{\prime}}\setminus B_{N}}f_{1}(x)\,dx\ll\eta^{2}|B_{N}|\leq\eta|A|,

while (
27
) ensures that

(47)

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus B_{N^{\prime}}}f_{1}(x)\,dx\leq|A|\int_{|y|\gg\eta^{-2}\lambda}\psi_{\eta^{-1}\lambda}(y)\,dy\ll\eta|A|.

Since

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}f_{1}(x)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}f(x)=|A|

estimate (
44
) follows.∎

4.2.
Proof of Lemma
4.2

It follows from an application of Cauchy-Schwarz and Plancherel that

\bigl\langle f,\mathcal{A}^{(j)}_{\lambda}(f-f_{2})\bigr\rangle^{2}\leq|A|\cdot\int|\widehat{f}(\xi)|^{2}|1-\widehat{\psi}(\eta^{2}\lambda\,\xi)|^{2}I(\lambda\,\xi)\,d\xi

where

(48)

I(\xi)=\int\cdots\int\bigl|\widehat{d\sigma^{(d-j)}_{y_{1},\dots,y_{j-1}}}(\xi)\bigr|^{2}\,d\sigma^{(d-j+1)}_{y_{1},\dots,y_{j-2}}(y_{j-1})\cdots d\sigma^{(d-1)}(y_{1}).

While from (
24
), the trivial uniform bound
I(\xi)\ll 1
, and an appropriate “conical” decomposition, depending on
\xi
, of the configuration space over which the integral
I(\xi)
is defined, we have

(49)

I(\xi)\leq C_{\Delta}(1+|\xi|)^{-(d-j)/2}.

Combining this observation with (

) we obtain the uniform bound

(50)

which, after an application of Plancherel, completes the proof.
∎

6.
Proof of Propositions
5.1
and
5.2

6.1.
Proof of Propositions
5.1

We first note that
Cauchy-Schwarz ensures

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|\mathcal{A}_{\,*}^{(j)}(g)(x)|^{2}\,dx\leq\int\cdots\int\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\sup_{\lambda_{0}\leq\lambda\leq\lambda_{1}}\Bigl|\int g(x-\lambda y_{j})\,d\sigma^{(d-j)}_{y_{1},\dots,y_{j-1}}(y_{j})\Bigr|^{2}\,dx\,d\sigma^{(d-j+1)}_{y_{1},\dots,y_{j-2}}(y_{j-1})\cdots d\sigma^{(d-1)}(y_{1}).

Now for fixed
y_{1},\dots,y_{j-1}
we can clearly identify
[y_{1},\dots,y_{j-1}]^{\perp}
with
\mathbb{R}^{d-j+1}
and
d\sigma^{(d-j)}_{y_{1},\dots,y_{j-1}}
with a constant (depending only on
d
and
\Delta
) multiple of
d\sigma^{(d-j)}
, the normalized measure on the unit sphere
S^{d-j}\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d-j+1}
induced by Lebesgue measure. Writing
\mathbb{R}^{d}=\mathbb{R}^{j-1}\times\mathbb{R}^{d-j+1}
,
g(x)=g_{x^{\prime}}(x^{\prime\prime})
, and applying
Stein’s spherical maximal function theorem
for functions in
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d-j+1})
, see Section 5.5 in
[
4
]
, which asserts that

(62)

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d-j+1}}\sup_{\lambda_{0}\leq\lambda\leq\lambda_{1}}\Bigl|\int g(x-\lambda y)\,d\sigma^{(d-j)}(y)\Bigr|^{2}\,dx\ll\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d-j+1}}|g(x)|^{2}\,dx

whenever
d\geq j+2
, gives

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\sup_{\lambda_{0}\leq\lambda\leq\lambda_{1}}\Bigl|\int g(x-\lambda y)

\displaystyle\,d\sigma^{(d-j)}_{y_{1},\dots,y_{j-1}}(y)\Bigr|^{2}\,dx

\displaystyle=C_{\Delta}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{j-1}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d-j+1}}\sup_{\lambda_{0}\leq\lambda\leq\lambda_{1}}\Bigl|\int g_{x^{\prime}}(x^{\prime\prime}-\lambda y)\,d\sigma^{(d-j)}(y)\Bigr|^{2}\,dx^{\prime\prime}\,dx^{\prime}

\displaystyle\leq C\int_{\mathbb{R}^{j-1}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d-j+1}}|g_{x^{\prime}}(x^{\prime\prime})|^{2}\,dx^{\prime\prime}\,dx^{\prime}=C\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|g(x)|^{2}\,dx

with the constant
C
independent of the initial choice of frame
y_{1},\dots,y_{j-1}
.
The result follows.
∎

6.2.
Proof of Propositions
5.2

We will deduce the validity of Proposition
5.2
from the following result for the slightly more general class of operators defined for any
L>0
by

(63)

\mathcal{M}_{L}^{(j)}(f)=\sup_{\lambda_{0}\leq\lambda\leq\lambda_{1}}\int\cdots\int\Bigl|\int f(x-\lambda y)\,d\mu^{(j)}_{L}(y)\Bigr|\,d\sigma^{(d-j+1)}_{y_{1},\dots,y_{j-2}}(y_{j-1})\cdots d\sigma^{(d-1)}(y_{1})

where

(64)

\widehat{d\mu^{(j)}_{L}}(\lambda\,\xi)=m_{L}(\xi)\,\widehat{d\sigma^{(d-j)}_{y_{1},\dots,y_{j-1}}}(\lambda\,\xi)

with the multiplier
m_{L}
now any smooth function that satisfies the estimate

(65)

|m_{L}(\xi)|\ll\min\{1,L|\xi|\}.

Recall that estimate (
26
) is precisely the statement that
|1-\widehat{\psi}(L\xi)|\ll\min\{1,L|\xi|\}.

Theorem 6.1
.

If
d\geq j+2
and
0<L\leq\lambda_{0}
, then

(66)

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|\mathcal{M}_{L}^{(j)}(f)(x)|^{2}\,dx\ll\Bigl(\frac{L}{\lambda_{0}}\Bigr)^{1/3}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|f(x)|^{2}\,dx.

Proof.

An application of Cauchy-Schwarz gives

(67)

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|\mathcal{M}_{L}^{(j)}(f)(x)|^{2}\,dx\leq\int\cdots\int\left[\,\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\sup_{\lambda_{0}\leq\lambda\leq\lambda_{1}}|M_{L,\lambda}(f)(x)|^{2}\,dx\,\right]\,d\sigma^{(d-j+1)}_{y_{1},\dots,y_{j-2}}(y_{j-1})\cdots d\sigma^{(d-1)}(y_{1}).

where
M_{L,\lambda}
is the Fourier multiplier operator defined by

(68)

\widehat{M_{L,\lambda}(f)}(\xi)=\widehat{f}(\xi)\,m_{L}(\xi)\,\widehat{d\sigma^{(d-j)}_{y_{1},\dots,y_{j-1}}}(\lambda\,\xi).

A standard application of the Fundamental Theorem of Calculus, see for example
[
4
]
, gives

(69)

\sup_{\lambda_{0}\leq\lambda\leq\lambda_{1}}|M_{L,\lambda}(f)(x)|^{2}\leq 2\int_{\lambda_{0}}^{\lambda_{1}}|M_{L,t}(f)(x)||\widetilde{M}_{L,t}(f)(x)|\,\frac{dt}{t}+|M_{L,\lambda_{0}}(f)(x)|^{2}

where
\widetilde{M}_{L,t}(f)=t\dfrac{d}{dt}M_{L,t}(f)
. We further note that
\widetilde{M}_{L,t}
is clearly also a Fourier multiplier operator, indeed

(70)

\widehat{\widetilde{M}_{L,t}(f)}(\xi)=\widehat{f}(\xi)\,m_{L}(\xi)\,\bigl(t\xi\cdot\nabla\widehat{d\sigma^{(d-j)}_{y_{1},\dots,y_{j-1}}}(t\xi)\bigr).

We now immediately see that

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|\mathcal{M}_{L}^{(j)}

\displaystyle(f)(x)|^{2}\,dx

\displaystyle\leq 2\!\!\!\!\sum_{\ell=\lfloor\log_{2}\lambda_{0}\rfloor}^{\infty}\int_{2^{\ell-1}}^{2^{\ell}}\int\cdots\int\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|M_{L,t}(f)(x)||\widetilde{M}_{L,t}(f)(x)|\,dx\,d\sigma^{(d-j+1)}_{y_{1},\dots,y_{j-2}}(y_{j-1})\cdots d\sigma^{(d-1)}(y_{1})\,\frac{dt}{t}

\displaystyle\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad+\int\cdots\int\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|M_{L,\lambda_{0}}(f)(x)|^{2}\,dx\,d\sigma^{(d-j+1)}_{y_{1},\dots,y_{j-2}}(y_{j-1})\cdots d\sigma^{(d-1)}(y_{1}).

Applying Cauchy-Schwarz to the first integral above (in the variables
x,y_{1},\dots,y_{j-1}
, and
t
together), followed by an application of Plancherel (in two resulting integrations in
x
as well as in the one that appears in the second integral above), we obtain the estimate

(71)

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|\mathcal{M}_{L}^{(j)}(f)(x)|^{2}\,dx\leq 2\!\!\!\!\sum_{\ell=\lfloor\log_{2}\lambda_{0}\rfloor}^{\infty}\bigl(\,\mathcal{I_{\ell}}\,\mathcal{\widetilde{I}_{\ell}}\,\bigr)^{1/2}+\mathcal{I}

with

(72)

\mathcal{I_{\ell}}=\int_{2^{\ell-1}}^{2^{\ell}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|\widehat{f}(\xi)|^{2}|m_{L}(\xi)|^{2}I(t\,\xi)\,d\xi\,\frac{dt}{t}

(73)

\mathcal{\widetilde{I}_{\ell}}=\int_{2^{\ell-1}}^{2^{\ell}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|\widehat{f}(\xi)|^{2}|m_{L}(\xi)|^{2}\widetilde{I}(t\,\xi)\,d\xi\,\frac{dt}{t}

and

(74)

\mathcal{I}=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|\widehat{f}(\xi)|^{2}|m_{L}(\xi)|^{2}I(\lambda_{0}\,\xi)\,d\xi

where, as in the proof of Proposition
4.2
, we have defined

(75)

I(\xi)=\int\cdots\int\bigl|\widehat{d\sigma^{(d-j)}_{y_{1},\dots,y_{j-1}}}(\xi)\bigr|^{2}\,d\sigma^{(d-j+1)}_{y_{1},\dots,y_{j-2}}(y_{j-1})\cdots d\sigma^{(d-1)}(y_{1})

and analogously now also define

(76)

\widetilde{I}(\xi)=\int\cdots\int\bigl|\xi\cdot\nabla\widehat{d\sigma^{(d-j)}_{y_{1},\dots,y_{j-1}}}(\xi)\bigr|^{2}\,d\sigma^{(d-j+1)}_{y_{1},\dots,y_{j-2}}(y_{j-1})\cdots d\sigma^{(d-1)}(y_{1}).

Combining (
65
) with (
49
), and recalling that we are assuming that
d\geq j+2
, gives

(77)

|m_{L}(\xi)|^{2}I(t\,\xi)\ll\min\{(t|\xi|)^{-1},L^{2}|\xi|^{2}\}\leq L^{2/3}t^{-2/3}

which ensures, via Plancherel, that

(78)

\mathcal{I_{\ell}}\ll\Bigl(\frac{L}{2^{\ell}}\Bigr)^{2/3}\|f\|_{2}^{2}\quad\text{and}\quad\mathcal{I}\ll\Bigl(\frac{L}{\lambda_{0}}\Bigr)^{2/3}\|f\|_{2}^{2}.

Arguing as in the proof of estimate (
49
), we can see that estimate (
24
) for
\nabla\widehat{d\sigma^{(d-j)}_{y_{1},\dots,y_{j-1}}}(\xi)
ensures that

\widetilde{I}(\xi)
is bounded whenever
d\geq j+2
, note also that it is not bounded if
d=k+1
. It follows immediately from this observation (and Plancherel) that

(79)

\mathcal{\widetilde{I}_{\ell}}\ll\|f\|_{2}^{2}.

Combining (
71
), (
78
), and (
79
), we get that

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|\mathcal{M}_{L}^{(j)}(f)(x)|^{2}\,dx

\displaystyle\ll\left(L^{1/3}\!\!\!\!\sum_{\ell=\lfloor\log_{2}\lambda_{0}\rfloor}^{\infty}2^{-\ell/3}+\Bigl(\frac{L}{\lambda_{0}}\Bigr)^{2/3}\right)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|f(x)|^{2}\,dx

\displaystyle\ll\Bigl(\frac{L}{\lambda_{0}}\Bigr)^{1/3}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|f(x)|^{2}\,dx

as required.∎

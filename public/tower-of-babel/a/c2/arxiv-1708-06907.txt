Random walks on solvable matrix groups
John J. Harrison

Abstract
. We define matrix groups FG n ⁡ ( P ) \operatorname{FG}_{n}({P}) for each natural number n n and finite set of primes P P , such that every rational-valued upper triangular matrix group is a (possibly distorted) subgroup. Brofferio and Schapira [ 3 ] , described the Poisson boundary of G ​ L n ​ ( ℚ ) GL_{n}(\mathbb{Q}) for measures of finite first moment with respect to adelic length. We show that adelic length is a word metric estimate on FG n ⁡ ( P ) \operatorname{FG}_{n}({P}) by constructing another, intermediate, word metric estimate which can be easily computed from the entries of any matrix in the group. In particular, finite first moment of a probability measure with respect to adelic length is an equivalent condition to requiring finite first moment with respect to word length in FG n ⁡ ( P ) \operatorname{FG}_{n}({P}) . We also investigate random walks in the case that P P is a length one sequence. Conditions for pointwise convergence in ℝ \mathbb{R} or ℚ p \mathbb{Q}_{{p}} are given. When these conditions are satisfied, we give path estimates from boundary points, discuss boundary triviality, show that the resulting space is a μ \mu -boundary and give cases where the μ \mu -boundary is the Poisson boundary, as conjectured by Kaimanovich in [ 12 ] .

Introduction and preliminaries

This paper is concerned with solvable matrix groups,
\operatorname{FG}_{n}({P})
, defined for each natural number
n
and each finite set of primes
P
. In Section

1
, we define these groups and prove some basic properties. Any finitely generated group of upper triangular matrices with rational entries is a subgroup of
\operatorname{FG}_{n}({P})
for a suitable choice of
n
and
P
.

In Section
2
, we give an estimate of word length on
\operatorname{FG}_{n}({P})
. Unlike the word length, the estimate can be efficiently computed from the entries of a given group element.

Brofferio and Schapira
[
3
]
described the Poisson boundary of
GL_{n}(\mathbb{Q})
for measures which have finite first moment with respect to
adelic length
.
In Section
3

\operatorname{FG}_{n}({P})
. We also discuss finite moment conditions on probability measures with respect to each notion of length.

If

is a singleton, then

is a semi-direct product,
\mathbb{Z}^{n}\ltimes\operatorname{UT}_{n}(\mathbb{Z}[\nicefrac{{1}}{{{p}}}])

is the group of upper unitriangular matrices with entries in
\mathbb{Z}[\nicefrac{{1}}{{{p}}}]

we give formulae for the multiplication of many elements and computation of inverses for semi-direct products of this form.

In the last section, we change our focus to random walks on
\operatorname{FG}_{n}({P})
when

is a singleton. We discuss conditions on
\mu
which allow almost sure pointwise convergence of the right random walk to elements of a
\mu
-boundary which is a product of copies of
\mathbb{R}
and
\mathbb{Q}_{{p}}

[
12
]
suggested might be the case. When a path converges to a point

on this

Suppose that

The

\sigma

\omega

We denote by

S(\omega_{1},\omega_{2},\omega_{3},\dots,\omega_{k},\dots)=(\omega_{1},\omega_{1}\omega_{2},\omega_{1}\omega_{2}\omega_{3},\dots,\omega_{1}\dots\omega_{k},\dots).

The measure

The group

Lemma 1.0.1
.

Let
n
be a natural number and
P=\{p_{r}\}_{r=1}^{k}
be a non-empty, finite set of primes. Let
f
be an element of
\operatorname{FG}_{n}({P})
. Then,
f
is upper triangular and there are integers

and

for natural numbers

less than or equal to
k
, such that

f_{ij}=\begin{cases}\prod_{r=1}^{k}p_{r}^{c^{(r)}_{ij}}&\textrm{if}\ i=j,\ \textrm{and}\\
r_{ij}\prod_{r=1}^{k}p_{r}^{c^{(r)}_{ij}}&\textrm{if}\ i<j\end{cases}

and

r_{ij}
is not divisible by any of the primes in
P
,
for all natural numbers

and

, such that
i\leq j\leq n
. Conversely, every matrix which may be written in this form is an element of
\operatorname{FG}_{n}({P})
.

Proof.

The forward direction follows from the well known fact that
U
generates
\operatorname{UT}_{n}(\mathbb{Z})
, see Elder, Elston and Ostheimer
[
6
]
. To prove the reverse direction, suppose that
f
is an upper triangular matrix with entries

f_{ij}=\begin{cases}\prod_{r=1}^{k}p_{r}^{c^{(r)}_{ij}}&\textrm{if}\ i=j,\ \textrm{and}\\
r_{ij}\prod_{r=1}^{k}p_{r}^{c^{(r)}_{ij}}&\textrm{if}\ i<j.\end{cases}

Let
g
be the diagonal matrix satisfying
g_{ij}=f_{ij}
whenever
i=j
.
Then,
f=gg^{-1}f
, the matrix
g^{-1}f
is an upper unitriangular and

(g^{-1}f)_{ij}=r_{ij}\prod_{r=1}^{k}p_{r}^{c^{(r)}_{ij}-c^{(r)}_{i,i+1}}

whenever

is less than
j
. With this in mind, let
M=-\min_{i\leq j}\left\{c^{(r)}_{ij}-c^{(r)}_{i,i+1}\right\}.
and let
A
be the diagonal matrix with entries

A_{jj}=\begin{cases}p^{jM}&\textrm{if}\ j<n,\ \textrm{and}\\
1&\textrm{if}\ j=n.\end{cases}

Then,
f=gA^{-1}zA

where

is in
\operatorname{UT}_{n}(\mathbb{Z})
. Since

and

are diagonal matrices which may be written as a finite product of the diagonal generators in

and we know that

\operatorname{UT}_{n}(\mathbb{Z})
, we have shown that

is a finite product of elements in
\operatorname{FG}_{n}({P})
. The matrix

is upper triangular because every generator is upper triangular.
∎

Suppose that

Let

f=gh
. Subadditivity of the word length function implies

\displaystyle\left|{f}\right|_{r+1,P}

\displaystyle=\left|{gh}\right|_{r+1,P}

\displaystyle\leq\left|{g}\right|_{r+1,P}+\left|{h}\right|_{r+1,P}

\displaystyle=\left|{g}\right|_{r,P}+\left|{h}\right|_{r+1,P}.

Hence, by the induction hypothesis

\displaystyle\left|{f}\right|_{r+1,P}

\displaystyle\leq A_{r}\left\llbracket{g}\right\rrbracket_{r,P}+\left|{h}\right|_{r+1,P}

(3)

\displaystyle=A_{r}\left\llbracket{g}\right\rrbracket_{r+1,P}+\left|{h}\right|_{r+1,P}.

The word length of
h
is bounded by
\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}\left|{\theta_{{1}{,n-j}}\left({h_{1,n-j}}\right)}\right|_{r+1,P}
, because

h=\prod_{j=0}^{n-1}\theta_{{1}{,n-j}}\left({h_{1,n-j}}\right),

where the product is taken from left to right. We will now bound the word length of each term in this product. We can write
\theta_{{1}{j}}\left({h_{11}}\right)
in terms of the generators as

(4)

\displaystyle\theta_{{1}{j}}\left({h_{11}}\right)=\prod_{r=1}^{k}\theta_{{1}{1}}\left({p_{r}}\right)^{c^{(r)}_{11}}.

Hence, the word length of
\theta_{{1}{1}}\left({h_{11}}\right)
is bounded above by
\sum_{r=1}^{k}\left|c^{(r)}_{11}\right|
.

We now bound the word length of
\theta_{{1}{j}}\left({h_{1j}}\right)
in the case that
j
is greater than or equal to
2
. If
h_{1j}
is zero, then
\theta_{{1}{j}}\left({h_{1j}}\right)
is the identity, so
|\theta_{{1}{j}}\left({h_{1j}}\right)|=0
. Suppose
h_{1j}
is not zero. We use the unique base
{b_{P}}
expansion,
p^{n_{j}}\sum_{r=0}^{t_{j}}\epsilon_{r}^{j}{{b_{P}}}^{r}
, of
h_{1j}
to construct a product equal to
\theta_{{1}{j}}\left({h_{1j}}\right)
:

(5)

\theta_{{1}{j}}\left({h_{1j}}\right)=\theta_{{1}{1}}\left({{b_{P}}}\right)^{n_{j}}\theta_{{1}{j}}\left(1\right)^{\epsilon^{0}_{j}}\left(\prod_{k=1}^{t_{j}}\theta_{{1}{1}}\left({{b_{P}}}\right)\theta_{{1}{j}}\left(1\right)^{\epsilon_{j}^{k}}\right)\theta_{{1}{1}}\left({{b_{P}}}\right)^{-n_{j}-t_{j}+1}.

Each term
\theta_{{1}{1}}\left({{b_{P}}}\right)
is equal to
\prod_{p\in P}\theta_{{1}{1}}\left({p}\right)
, which is a product of
k
generators. Hence,

\left|{\theta_{{1}{j}}\left({h_{1j}}\right)}\right|_{r+1,P}\leq(2n_{j}+2t_{j}+1)k+1+2t_{j}.

By uniqueness of the base
{b_{P}}
expansion of
h_{1j}
, we have
n_{j}=d_{-}^{{b_{P}}}(h_{1j})
and
t_{j}=d_{+}^{{b_{P}}}(h_{1j})-d_{-}^{{b_{P}}}(h_{ij})
. Hence,

\displaystyle\left|{\theta_{{1}{j}}\left({h_{1j}}\right)}\right|_{r+1,P}

\displaystyle\leq 2kd_{-}^{{b_{P}}}(h_{1j})+2(k+1)(d_{+}^{{b_{P}}}(h_{1j})-d_{-}^{{b_{P}}}(h_{ij})+1).

Since,
2(k+1)
is greater than
1
,

\displaystyle\left|{\theta_{{1}{j}}\left({h_{1j}}\right)}\right|_{r+1,P}

\displaystyle\leq 6(k+1)\left[{h_{1j}}\right]_{{b_{P}}}.

Therefore, we have the upper bound,

\displaystyle\left|{h}\right|_{r+1,P}

\displaystyle\leq 6(k+1)\left(\sum_{j=2}^{n}\left[{h_{1j}}\right]_{{b_{P}}}+\sum_{r=k}^{l}\left|c^{(r)}_{1j}\right|\right)=6(k+1)\left\llbracket{h}\right\rrbracket_{r+1,P}.

In conjunction with Equation (
3
), we have

\displaystyle\left|{f}\right|_{r+1,P}

\displaystyle\leq A_{r}\left\llbracket{g}\right\rrbracket_{r+1,P}+6(k+1)\left\llbracket{h}\right\rrbracket_{r+1,P}

\displaystyle\leq A_{r+1}\left(\left\llbracket{g}\right\rrbracket_{r+1,P}+\left\llbracket{h}\right\rrbracket_{r+1,P}\right)

\displaystyle=A_{r+1}\left\llbracket{f}\right\rrbracket_{r+1,P}.

where
A_{r+1}=6(k+1)A_{r}
. Hence,

\left|{f}\right|_{n,P}\leq 6^{n-1}(k+1)^{n-1}\left\llbracket{f}\right\rrbracket_{n,P},

which is an upper bound for
\left|{f}\right|_{n,P}
in terms of
\left\llbracket{f}\right\rrbracket_{n,P}
.

We will now find a lower bound for
\left|{f}\right|_{n,P}
. Since
f
is not the identity by assumption,
1\leq\left|{f}\right|_{n,P}
. It is clear from the choice of generating set, that

\sum_{r=1}^{k}\left|c^{(r)}_{1j}\right|\leq\left|{f}\right|_{n,P},

that

(6)

\left|d_{-}^{{b_{P}}}(f_{ij})\right|\leq\left|{f}\right|_{n,P}

and that

|f_{ij}|\leq{b_{P}}^{\left|{f}\right|_{n,P}}

hence,

(7)

|d_{+}^{{b_{P}}}(f_{ij})|\leq\log_{{b_{P}}}|f_{ij}|\leq\left|{f}\right|_{n,P},

for all upper off-diagonal entries
f_{ij}
of the matrix
f
. Equation (
2
) implies

\left[{f_{ij}}\right]_{{b_{P}}}\leq 2\left|{f}\right|_{n,P}.

As there are
\frac{n(n-1)}{2}
entries in the upper triangular part of
f
,

\sum_{r=1}^{k}\left|c^{(r)}_{1j}\right|+\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}\sum_{j=i+1}^{n}\left[{f_{ij}}\right]_{{b_{P}}}\leq n(n-1)\left|{f}\right|_{n,P},

which means that

\frac{1}{J}\left\llbracket{f}\right\rrbracket_{n,P}\leq\left|{f}\right|_{n,P}\leq J\left\llbracket{f}\right\rrbracket_{n,P},

where
J=\max\left\{n(n-1),6^{n-1}(k+1)^{n-1}\right\}
. This completes the proof.
∎

3.
Adelic length is a word metric estimate on
\operatorname{FG}_{n}({P})

Let
\mathcal{P}^{*}
be the set of all primes. Let
\mathcal{P}=\mathcal{P}^{*}\cup\{\infty\}
. For each vector
v=(v_{1},\dots,v_{n})
in
\mathbb{Q}_{p}^{n}
, let

|v|_{p}=\max_{1\leq i\leq n}|v_{i}|_{p}.

For each prime
p
and for each vector
v=(v_{1},\dots,v_{n})
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, let

|v|_{\infty}=\sqrt{\sum_{i=1}^{n}|v_{i}|^{2}}.

If
f
is an element of
GL_{n}(\mathbb{Q})
, define the operator norm

\left\|{f}\right\|_{p}=\sup_{|v|_{p}=1}|fv|_{p}.

for each
p
in
\mathcal{P}
. Note that
\left\|{\cdot}\right\|_{\infty}
is just the real spectral norm. For any
f,g
in
GL_{n}(\mathbb{Q})
, let

d_{p}^{a}(f,g)=\ln^{+}\left\|{f^{-1}g}\right\|_{p}+\ln^{+}\left\|{g^{-1}f}\right\|_{p},

for
p
prime or
p=\infty
, where
\ln^{+}
is the positive part of the natural logarithm function. The map
d_{p}
is symmetric, satisfies the triangle inequality and is left-invariant. For all
f,g
in
GL_{n}(\mathbb{Q})
, let

d^{a}(f,g)=\sum_{p\in\mathcal{P}}d_{p}^{a}(f,g).

The map
d^{a}
is a left invariant pseudometric on
GL_{n}(\mathbb{Q})
, called the
adelic pseudometric
.
The
adelic length
of an element
f
in
GL_{n}(\mathbb{Q})
is the quantity

\left\llbracket{f}\right\rrbracket_{n,a}=d^{a}(I_{n},f)=\sum_{p\in\mathcal{P}}\left(\ln^{+}\left\|{f}\right\|_{p}+\ln^{+}\left\|{f^{-1}}\right\|_{p}\right),

where
I_{n}
is the
n\times n
identity matrix.

Let
\mu
be a probability measure on
(\operatorname{GL}_{n}(\mathbb{Q}),\mu)
. For
p
and each natural number
j\leq n
, let
\lambda_{j}(p)
be the Lyapunov coefficients of
\mu
, given recursively by

\sum_{i=1}^{k}\lambda_{i}(p)=\lim_{r\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{r}\int\ln\|{\wedge}^{k}g\|_{p}\,d\mu^{\ast r}(g)

where
\wedge
is the exterior product, as defined in Section 2.2 of Winitzki
[
16
]
. Let

B_{p}=\operatorname{GL}_{n}(\mathbb{K_{p}}/{P_{p}}),

be the associated flag manifolds, where
\mathbb{K_{p}}=\mathbb{Q}_{{p}}
if
p
is prime or
\mathbb{R}
if
p=\infty
, where
P_{p}
is the normal subgroup of
\operatorname{GL}_{n}(\mathbb{K_{p}})
consisting of all matrices
f
, such that
f_{ij}
is zero whenever
\lambda_{i}(p)<\lambda_{j}(p)
.

Brofferio and Schapira described the adelic pseudometric in
[
3
]
. They showed that if a measure
\mu
on
GL_{n}(\mathbb{Q})
has finite first moment with respect to the adelic length, i.e.

\int_{GL_{n}(\mathbb{Q})}\left\llbracket{f}\right\rrbracket_{n,a}\,d\mu(f)

is finite, then there is a probability measure
\nu
supported on the product

\mathbf{B}=\prod_{p\in\mathcal{P}}B_{p},

such that
(\mathbf{B},\nu)
is the Poisson boundary of
(\operatorname{GL}_{n}(\mathbb{Q}),\mu)
.

In this section, we show that the adelic length is a word metric estimate on
\operatorname{FG}_{n}({P})
. We make use of the word metric estimate,
\left\llbracket{\cdot}\right\rrbracket_{n,P}
, from the previous section in many of the arguments. The argument is split into three subsections. The first subsection contains the technical lemmas and propositions needed to construct an upper bound of the form

\left|{f}\right|_{n,P}\leq S+T\left\llbracket{f}\right\rrbracket_{n,a},

for positive, real constants
S
and
T
. The second subsection is similar, aiming towards constructing a lower bound of the form

Q+R\left\llbracket{f}\right\rrbracket_{n,a}\leq\left|{f}\right|_{n,P},

for positive, real constants
Q
and
R
. The final section brings together the earlier sections, stating and proving that adelic length is a word metric estimate on
\operatorname{FG}_{n}({P})
, before making other concluding remarks.

3.1.
Upper bounds

Lemma 3.1.1
.

Let
p_{1}
and
p_{2}
be distinct primes. Suppose that
x
is a non-zero rational number of the form

x=p_{1}^{c_{1}}p_{2}^{c_{2}}y

for some integers
c_{1},c_{2}
and
y
, such that
p_{1}
and
p_{2}
do not divide
y
. Let
b=p_{1}p_{2}
. Then,

\displaystyle d_{-}^{b}(x)

\displaystyle=\min\left\{c_{1},c_{2}\right\}=\min\left\{-\log_{p_{1}}|x|_{p_{1}},-\log_{p_{2}}|x|_{p_{2}}\right\}.

and
x
has a finite base
b
representation.

Proof.

Suppose that
c_{1}\leq c_{2}
. Then,

x=b^{c_{1}}p_{2}^{c_{2}-c_{1}}y

and
p_{2}^{c_{2}-c_{1}}y
is an integer which is not divisible by
p_{1}
, so

c_{1}=d_{-}^{p_{1}}(p_{1}^{c_{1}})=d_{-}^{b}(x).

A similar argument shows that

c_{2}=d_{-}^{p_{2}}(p_{2}^{c_{2}})=d_{-}^{b}(x)

if
c_{2}\leq c_{1}
. It is clear that, in either case,
x
has finite base
b
representation.
∎

Corollary 3.1.2
.

Let
P=\{p_{r}\}_{r=1}^{k}
be a non-empty, finite set of primes. Let
{b_{P}}
be the product of all primes in
P
. Suppose that
x
is a non-zero rational number which is a product

x=y\prod_{r=1}^{k}p_{r}^{c_{r}}

for some integer
y
, such that no prime in
P
divides
y
. Then,

\displaystyle d_{-}^{b}(x)

\displaystyle=\min_{1\leq r\leq k}\left\{{c_{r}}\right\}=\min_{1\leq r\leq k}\left\{-\log_{p_{r}}|x|_{p_{r}}\right\}=\min_{1\leq r\leq k}\left\{d_{-}^{p_{r}}(p_{r}^{c_{r}})\right\}.

and
x
has a finite base
b
representation.

Proof.

Apply Lemma
3.1.1
recursively.
∎

Corollary 3.1.3
.

Let
P=\{p_{r}\}_{r=1}^{k}
be a non-empty, finite set of primes. Let
{b_{P}}
be the product of all primes in
P
. Suppose that
x
is a non-zero rational number which is a product

x=y\prod_{r=1}^{k}p_{r}^{c_{r}}

for some integer
y
, such that no prime in
P
divides
y
. If
d_{-}^{{b_{P}}}(x)\leq 0
, then

(8)

|d_{-}^{{b_{P}}}(x)|\leq\sum_{p\in\mathcal{P}^{*}}\ln^{+}|x|_{p}

where
\mathcal{P}^{*}
is the set of all primes. In particular, if
d_{+}^{{b_{P}}}(x)\leq 0
, then

d_{-}^{{b_{P}}}(x)\leq 0
and

|d_{+}^{{b_{P}}}(x)|\leq|d_{-}^{{b_{P}}}(x)|,

so Equation (
8
) holds with
d_{+}^{{b_{P}}}(x)
in the place of
d_{-}^{{b_{P}}}(x)
.

Proof.

By the previous corollary, there is a natural number
r\leq k
, such that

\displaystyle|d_{-}^{{b_{P}}}(x)|=-d_{-}^{{b_{P}}}(x)=\log_{p_{r}}|x|_{p_{r}}\leq\frac{\ln^{+}|x|_{p_{r}}}{\ln p_{r}}\leq\ln^{+}|x|_{p_{r}}\leq\sum_{p\in\mathcal{P}^{*}}\ln^{+}|x|_{p},

because the summand
\ln^{+}|x|_{p}
is zero whenever
p
is a prime larger than
p_{r}
.
∎

Lemma 3.1.4
.

Let
P=\{p_{r}\}_{r=1}^{k}
be a non-empty, finite set of primes. Let
{b_{P}}
be the product of all the primes in
P
. Suppose that
x
is a non-zero rational number and that
d_{+}^{{b_{P}}}(x)\geq 0
. Then,

|d_{+}^{{b_{P}}}(x)|\leq\log_{{b_{P}}}(e)\ln^{+}|x|

for every
p
in
P
. In particular, if
d_{-}^{{b_{P}}}(x)\geq 0
, then
d_{+}^{{b_{P}}}(x)\geq 0
and

|d_{-}^{{b_{P}}}(x)|\leq|d_{+}^{{b_{P}}}(x)|,

so Equation (
3.1.4
) holds with
d_{-}^{{b_{P}}}(x)
in the place of
d_{+}^{{b_{P}}}(x)
.

Proof.

By consideration of decimal representations, we have

|d_{+}^{{b_{P}}}(x)|=d_{+}^{{b_{P}}}(x)\leq\log_{{b_{P}}}|x|\leq\log^{+}_{{b_{P}}}|x|=\log_{{b_{P}}}(e)\ln^{+}|x|

The right hand equality is just the change of base formula.
∎

Proposition 3.1.5
.

Let
P=\{p_{r}\}_{r=1}^{k}
be a non-empty, finite set of primes. Let
{b_{P}}
be the product of all primes in
P
. Suppose that
f
is an element of
\operatorname{FG}_{n}({P})
. Then, there are positive, real constants
K
and
M
, such that

\left[{f_{ij}}\right]_{{b_{P}}}\leq K+M\sum_{p\in\mathcal{P}}\ln^{+}\|f\|_{p},

for every pair of natural numbers
i
and
j
, such that
i<j\leq n
, where
\mathcal{P}=\mathcal{P}^{*}\cup\{\infty\}
and
\mathcal{P}^{*}
is the set of all primes.

Proof.

First, we relate
\left[{f_{ij}}\right]_{{b_{P}}}
to
\ln^{+}\|f\|_{p}
for each
p
in
\mathcal{P}
. It follows from the max norm inequality, Equation (2.3.8) in Golub
[
10
]
, gives that

(9)

\ln^{+}|f_{ij}|\leq\ln\sqrt{n}+\ln^{+}\|f\|_{\infty}.

Let
p
be any prime. Choose the vector
w=(w_{1},\dots,w_{n})
in
\mathbb{Q}_{{p}}^{n}
, such that

w_{t}=\begin{cases}p^{-1}&\textrm{if}\ t=j,\\
0\ &\textrm{otherwise.}\end{cases}

for every natural number
t\leq n
. Then,
|w|_{p}=1
, so

\displaystyle\|f\|_{p}

\displaystyle=\sup_{|v|_{p}=1}|fv|_{p}

\displaystyle\geq\sup_{|v|_{p}=1}\max_{s}\left|\sum_{t=1}^{n}f_{st}v_{t}\right|_{p}

\displaystyle\geq\sup_{|v|_{p}=1}\left|\sum_{t=1}^{n}f_{it}v_{t}\right|_{p}

\displaystyle\geq\left|\sum_{t=1}^{n}f_{it}w_{t}\right|_{p}

\displaystyle=\ln^{+}\left|f_{ij}p^{-1}\right|_{p}

(10)

\displaystyle\geq\ln^{+}\left|f_{ij}\right|_{p}.

On the other hand, from the definition of
\left[{.}\right]_{{b_{P}}}
,

\left[{f_{ij}}\right]_{{b_{P}}}\leq 1+|d_{-}^{{b_{P}}}(f_{ij})|+|d_{+}^{{b_{P}}}(f_{ij})|.

We now seek an upper bound on the right hand side of this equation. There are four cases to consider, depending on the signs of
d_{-}^{{b_{P}}}(f_{ij})
and
d_{-}^{{b_{P}}}(f_{ij})
. If they are both non-positive, then we can use Corollary
3.1.3
. Similarly, if they are both non-negative, then we can use Lemma
3.1.4
. If
d_{+}^{{b_{P}}}(f_{ij})
is non-negative and
d_{+}^{{b_{P}}}(f_{ij})
is non-positive, then we can use the combination of those lemmas. The final case, where
d_{+}^{{b_{P}}}(f_{ij})<d_{-}^{{b_{P}}}(f_{ij})
, is impossible by definition! In any case, we have

(11)

\left[{f_{ij}}\right]_{{b_{P}}}\leq 1+\log_{{b_{P}}}(e)\sum_{p\in\mathcal{P}}\ln^{+}|f_{ij}|_{p},

Substituting equations (
9
) and (
10
) into Equation (
11
) gives

\left[{f_{ij}}\right]_{{b_{P}}}\leq K+M\sum_{p\in\mathcal{P}}\ln^{+}\|f\|_{p},

where

K=1+\log_{{b_{P}}}(e)+\log_{{b_{P}}}(e)\ln\sqrt{n}

and
M=\log_{{b_{P}}}(e)
.
∎

Lemma 3.1.6
.

Let
P=\{p_{r}\}_{r=1}^{k}
be a finite set of primes. Let
r
be a natural number less than or equal to
n
. Let
f
be an element of
\operatorname{FG}_{n}({P})
. Let
r_{ii}
and
c^{(r)}_{ii}
be integers, as in Lemma
1.0.1
, such that

f_{ii}=\prod_{r=1}^{k}p_{r}^{c^{(r)}_{ii}}

and such that each
r_{ij}
is not divisible by any of the primes in
P
. Then,

\sum_{r=1}^{k}|c^{(r)}_{ii}|\leq\frac{3\ln n}{2\ln 2}+\frac{3}{\ln 2}\sum_{p\in\mathcal{P}}\ln^{+}\|f\|_{p},

where
\mathcal{P}=\mathcal{P}^{*}\cup\{\infty\}
and
\mathcal{P}^{*}
is the set of all primes.

Proof.

We begin by observing that

\displaystyle\ln^{+}|f|

\displaystyle\geq\ln f

\displaystyle=\ln\left(\prod_{r=1}^{k}p_{r}^{c^{(r)}_{ii}}\right)

\displaystyle=-\sum_{r=1}^{k}\ln\left(p_{r}^{-c^{(r)}_{ii}}\right)

\displaystyle=-\sum_{r=1}^{k}\ln\left|f_{ii}\right|_{p_{r}}.

Since
\ln^{+}|f_{ii}|_{p}
is zero for all primes not in
P
,

\displaystyle\sum_{p\in\mathcal{P}}\ln^{+}|f_{ii}|_{p}=\ln^{+}|f|+\sum_{r=1}^{k}\ln^{+}\left|f_{ii}\right|_{p_{r}}=-\sum_{r=1}^{k}\ln^{-}\left|f_{ii}\right|_{p_{r}}\geq-\sum_{r=1}^{k}\ln\left|f_{ii}\right|_{p_{r}},

where
\ln^{-}
is the negative part of the natural logarithm function. Now,

\displaystyle-\sum_{r=1}^{k}\ln\left|f_{ii}\right|_{p_{r}}=\sum_{r=1}^{k}c^{(r)}_{ii}=\sum_{r=1}^{k}|c^{(r)}_{ii}|-2\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq r\leq k\\
\textrm{s.t.}\ c^{(r)}_{ii}<0\end{subarray}}|c^{(r)}_{ii}|

Rearranging the last expression, we have

(12)

\displaystyle\sum_{r=1}^{k}|c^{(r)}_{ii}|\leq\sum_{p\in\mathcal{P}}\ln^{+}|f_{ii}|_{p}+2\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq r\leq k\\
\textrm{s.t.}\ c^{(r)}_{ii}<0\end{subarray}}|c^{(r)}_{ii}|.

Notice that
p^{-c^{(r)}_{ii}}=|f_{ii}|_{p_{r}}
. So
-c^{(r)}_{ii}\ln(p_{r})=\ln|f_{ii}|_{p_{r}}
. Hence, if
c^{(r)}_{ii}
is negative,

\frac{\ln|f_{ii}|_{p_{r}}}{\ln 2}\geq|c^{(r)}_{ii}|=-c^{(r)}_{ii},

as
2
is the smallest prime. Returning to Equation (
12
), we have

\sum_{r=1}^{k}|c^{(r)}_{ii}|\leq\frac{3}{\ln 2}\sum_{p\in\mathcal{P}}\ln^{+}|f_{ii}|_{p}

The same argument in Proposition
3.1.5
used to arrive at equations (
9
) and (
10
) gives

\ln^{+}\left|f_{ii}\right|_{p}\leq\ln^{+}\|f\|_{p},

for every prime
p
and

\ln^{+}|f_{ii}|\leq\ln\sqrt{n}+\ln^{+}\|f\|_{\infty},

respectively. Hence,

\sum_{r=1}^{k}|c^{(r)}_{ii}|\leq\frac{3}{\ln 2}\sum_{p\in\mathcal{P}}\ln^{+}|f_{ii}|_{p}\leq\frac{3\ln n}{2\ln 2}+\frac{3}{\ln 2}\sum_{p\in\mathcal{P}}\ln^{+}\|f\|_{p},

as desired.
∎

3.2.
Lower bounds

Lemma 3.2.1
.

Let
P=\{p_{r}\}_{r=1}^{k}
be a finite set of primes. Let
f
be an element of
\operatorname{FG}_{n}({P})
. Let
r_{ij}
and
c^{(r)}_{ij}
be integers, as in Lemma
1.0.1
, such that

f_{ij}=\begin{cases}\prod_{r=1}^{k}p_{r}^{c^{(r)}_{ij}}&\textrm{if}\ i=j,\ \textrm{and}\\
r_{ij}\prod_{r=1}^{k}p_{r}^{c^{(r)}_{ij}}&\textrm{if}\ i<j.\end{cases}

and such that each
r_{ij}
is not divisible by any of the primes in
P
.
Then,

\ln^{+}\left\|{f}\right\|_{p_{r}}\leq\max_{1\leq i\leq j\leq n}\left\{|c^{(r)}_{ij}|\ln^{+}\left(p_{r}\right)\right\}

for all natural numbers
r
less than or equal to
k
.

Proof.

Let
r
be a natural number. Suppose that
v=(v_{1},\dots,v_{n})
is a vector in
\mathbb{Q}_{{p_{r}}}
with
|v|_{p_{r}}=1
. The ultrametric inequality and the definition of the
p_{r}
-adic norm imply that

\displaystyle|fv|_{p_{r}}

\displaystyle\leq\max_{1\leq i\leq j\leq n}\left|p_{r}^{c^{(r)}_{ij}}v_{j}\right|_{p_{r}}=\max_{1\leq i\leq j\leq n}\left|p_{r}^{c^{(r)}_{ij}}\right|_{p_{r}}\left|v_{j}\right|_{p_{r}}.

If
|v|_{p}=1
, then

|fv|_{p_{r}}\leq\max_{1\leq i\leq j\leq n}\left|p_{r}^{c^{(r)}_{ij}}\right|_{p_{r}}=\max_{1\leq i\leq j\leq n}p_{r}^{-c^{(r)}_{ij}}\leq\max_{1\leq i\leq j\leq n}p_{r}^{\left|c^{(r)}_{ij}\right|}.

Since
p_{r}^{\left|c^{(r)}_{ij}\right|}
is always greater than or equal to
1
, we have

\ln^{+}\left\|{f}\right\|_{p_{r}}=\ln^{+}\left(\sup_{|v|_{p}=1}|fv|_{p}\right)\leq\ln^{+}\left(\max_{1\leq i\leq j\leq n}p_{r}^{\left|c^{(r)}_{ij}\right|}\right)=\max_{1\leq i\leq j\leq n}\left\{|c^{(r)}_{ij}|\ln^{+}\left(p_{r}\right)\right\}

Which concludes the proof.

∎

Proposition 3.2.2
.

Let
f
be an element of
\operatorname{FG}_{n}({P})
for a non-empty, finite set of primes
P=\{p_{r}\}_{r=1}^{k}
. Then,

\sum_{p\in P}\left(\ln^{+}\left\|{f}\right\|_{p}+\ln^{+}\left\|{f^{-1}}\right\|_{p}\right)\leq K\left\llbracket{f}\right\rrbracket_{n,P}

where
K
is a positive, real constant.

Proof.

The claim is certainly true if
f
is the identity
I_{n}
. Let
r_{ij}
and
c^{(r)}_{ij}
be integers, as in Lemma
1.0.1
, such that

f_{ij}=\begin{cases}\prod_{r=1}^{k}p_{r}^{c^{(r)}_{ij}}&\textrm{if}\ i=j,\ \textrm{and}\\
r_{ij}\prod_{r=1}^{k}p_{r}^{c^{(r)}_{ij}}&\textrm{if}\ i<j.\end{cases}

and such that each
r_{ij}
is not divisible by any of the primes in
P
.

Since
f
is not the identity, we may apply Lemma
3.2.1
,

\displaystyle\sum_{r=1}^{k}\ln^{+}\left\|{f}\right\|_{p_{r}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{r=1}^{k}\max_{1\leq i\leq j\leq n}\left\{|c^{(r)}_{ij}|\ln^{+}\left(p_{r}\right)\right\}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{r=1}^{k}\left(\ln(p_{r})\sum_{i=1}^{n}|c^{(r)}_{ii}|\right)+\sum_{1\leq i<j\leq n}\sum_{r=1}^{k}|c^{(r)}_{ij}|\ln^{+}\left(p_{r}\right).

Let
M_{ij}=-\max_{r=1}^{k}\left\{|c^{(r)}_{ij}|\right\}
. Then, using Corollary
2.0.5
,

\displaystyle\sum_{r=1}^{k}|c^{(r)}_{ij}|\ln^{+}\left(p_{r}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{r=1}^{k}|M_{ij}|\ln^{+}\left(p_{r}\right)

\displaystyle=|M_{ij}|\sum_{r=1}^{k}\ln\left({p_{r}}\right)

(13)

\displaystyle\leq|d_{-}^{b_{P}}(f_{ij})|\sum_{r=1}^{k}\ln\left({p_{r}}\right).

Hence, we have,

\displaystyle\sum_{r=1}^{k}\ln^{+}\left\|{f}\right\|_{p_{r}}

\displaystyle\leq\left(\sum_{r=1}^{k}\ln(p_{r})\right)\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}\sum_{r=1}^{k}|c^{(r)}_{ii}|+\sum_{1\leq i<j\leq n}|d_{-}^{b_{P}}(f_{ij})|\right)

\displaystyle\leq\left(\sum_{r=1}^{k}\ln(p_{r})\right)\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}\sum_{r=1}^{k}|c^{(r)}_{ii}|+\sum_{1\leq i<j\leq n}\left[{f_{ij}}\right]_{{b_{P}}}\right),

\displaystyle=\left(\sum_{r=1}^{k}\ln(p_{r})\right)\left\llbracket{f}\right\rrbracket_{n,P}.

where
{b_{P}}
is the product of all the primes in
P
. Since
\left\llbracket{\cdot}\right\rrbracket_{n,P}
is a word metric estimate,

\frac{1}{J}\left\llbracket{f^{-1}}\right\rrbracket_{n,P}\leq\left|{f^{-1}}\right|_{n,P}=\left|{f}\right|_{n,P}\leq J\left\llbracket{f}\right\rrbracket_{n,P},

where
J
is the positive, real constant we found in Proposition
2.0.6
. This yields the desired inequality, namely,

\sum_{p\in P}\left(\ln^{+}\left\|{f}\right\|_{p}+\ln^{+}\left\|{f^{-1}}\right\|_{p}\right)\leq K\left\llbracket{f}\right\rrbracket_{n,P}

where
K=\left(\sum_{r=1}^{k}\ln(p_{r})\right)\left(1+J^{2}\right)
.
∎

Lemma 3.2.3
.

Let
P=\{p_{r}\}_{r=1}^{k}
be a finite set of primes. If
f
is a rational number equal to a product of powers of primes in
P
,

f=\prod_{r=1}^{k}p_{r}^{{c^{(r)}}},

then,

d_{+}^{b_{P}}(f)\leq 1+\sum_{r=1}^{k}|{c^{(r)}}|,

where
{b_{P}}
is the product of all the primes in
P
.

Proof.

We begin by noting that

\displaystyle d_{+}^{b_{P}}(f)

\displaystyle\leq d_{+}^{b_{P}}\left(\prod_{r=1}^{k}p_{r}^{|{c^{(r)}}|}\right)

\displaystyle\leq 1+\ln_{b_{P}}\left(\prod_{r=1}^{k}p_{r}^{|{c^{(r)}}|}\right).

Application of logarithm laws yields,

\displaystyle d_{+}^{b_{P}}(f)

\displaystyle=1+\sum_{r=1}^{k}{|{c^{(r)}}|}\ln_{b_{P}}\left(p_{r}\right).

Since
{b_{P}}
is the product of all the primes in
P
,
\ln_{b_{P}}\left(p_{r}\right)\leq 1
for each
r
in the above summation. So,

\displaystyle d_{+}^{b_{P}}(f)

\displaystyle\leq 1+\sum_{r=1}^{k}|{c^{(r)}}|.

Which concludes the proof.
∎

Proposition 3.2.4
.

Let
f
be an element of
\operatorname{FG}_{n}({P})
for a non-empty, finite set of primes
P=\{p_{r}\}_{r=1}^{k}
. Then,

\ln^{+}\left\|{f}\right\|_{\infty}+\ln^{+}\left\|{f^{-1}}\right\|_{\infty}\leq L+M\left\llbracket{f}\right\rrbracket_{n,P}

where
L
and
M
are positive, real constants.

Proof.

The max norm inequality, Equation (2.3.8) in Golub
[
10
]
, gives that

\displaystyle\ln^{+}\left\|{f}\right\|_{\infty}

\displaystyle\leq\ln\sqrt{n}+\ln\left(\max_{1\leq i\leq j\leq n}|f_{ij}|\right),

where
|\cdot|
is the ordinary absolute value. Hence,

\displaystyle\ln^{+}\left\|{f}\right\|_{\infty}

\displaystyle\leq\ln\sqrt{n}+\ln({b_{P}})\left(1+d_{+}^{b_{P}}\left(\max_{1\leq i\leq j\leq n}|f_{ij}|\right)\right)

\displaystyle\leq\ln\sqrt{n}+\ln({b_{P}})\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left(d_{+}^{b_{P}}(|f_{ii}|)\right)+\sum_{1\leq i<j\leq n}\left(d_{+}^{b_{P}}(|f_{ij}|)\right)\right).

Applying Lemma
3.2.3
, we have

\displaystyle\ln^{+}\left\|{f}\right\|_{\infty}

\displaystyle\leq\ln\sqrt{n}+\ln({b_{P}})\left(n+\sum_{i=1}^{n}\sum_{r=1}^{k}|c^{(r)}_{ii}|+\sum_{1\leq i<j\leq n}\left(d_{+}^{b_{P}}(|f_{ij}|)\right)\right)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{L}{2}+\ln({b_{P}})\left\llbracket{f}\right\rrbracket_{n,P},

where
L=\ln n+2\ln({b_{P}})
. Let
J
be as in Proposition
2.0.6
. Then, since
\left\llbracket{f}\right\rrbracket_{n,P}
is a word metric estimate,

\displaystyle\ln^{+}\left\|{f^{-1}}\right\|_{\infty}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{L}{2}+\left\llbracket{f}\right\rrbracket_{n,P}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{L}{2}+J\left|{f^{-1}}\right|_{n,P}

\displaystyle=\frac{L}{2}+J\left|{f}\right|_{n,P}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{L}{2}+J^{2}\left\llbracket{f^{-1}}\right\rrbracket_{n,P}.

Hence,

\ln^{+}\left\|{f}\right\|_{\infty}+\ln^{+}\left\|{f^{-1}}\right\|_{\infty}\leq L+M\left\llbracket{f}\right\rrbracket_{n,P}

where
M=1+J^{2}
, which concludes the proof.
∎

Lemma 3.2.5
.

Let
P=\{p_{r}\}_{r=1}^{k}
be a non-empty, finite set of primes. Let
\mu
be a probability measure on
\operatorname{FG}_{n}({P})
that has finite first moment with respect to word length. Then,

\ln^{+}\left\|{f}\right\|_{p}+\ln^{+}\left\|{f^{-1}}\right\|_{p}

is zero unless
p
is in
P
, or
p=\infty
.

Proof.

Suppose
p
is any prime not in
P
. By Lemma
1.0.1
, there are integers
r_{ij}
and
c^{(r)}_{ij}
for natural numbers
r
less than or equal to
k
, such that

(14)

\displaystyle f_{ij}=\begin{cases}\prod_{r=1}^{k}p_{r}^{c^{(r)}_{ij}}&\textrm{if}\ i=j,\ \textrm{and}\\
r_{ij}\prod_{r=1}^{k}p_{r}^{c^{(r)}_{ij}}&\textrm{if}\ i<j\end{cases}

and the integers
r_{ij}
are not divisible by any of the primes in
P
.

Suppose that
v=(v_{1},\dots,v_{n})
is a vector in
\mathbb{Q}_{{p_{r}}}
with
|v|_{p}=1
. The ultrametric inequality and the definition of the
p
-adic norm imply that

\displaystyle|fv|_{p_{r}}

\displaystyle\leq\max_{1\leq i\leq j\leq n}\left|f_{ij}v_{j}\right|_{p}

\displaystyle=\max_{1\leq i\leq j\leq n}\left|f_{ij}\right|_{p}\left|v_{j}\right|_{p}

\displaystyle\leq\max_{1\leq i\leq j\leq n}\left|f_{ij}\right|_{p}.

By considering Equation (
14
), we see that
\left|f_{ij}\right|_{p}
is less than or equal to
1
. Hence,

\left\|{f}\right\|_{p}=\sup_{|v|_{p}=1}|fv|_{p_{r}}\leq 1,

which implies that
\ln^{+}\left\|{f}\right\|_{p}
is zero. The argument to show that
\ln^{+}\left\|{f^{-1}}\right\|_{p}
is zero is similar, since
\operatorname{FG}_{n}({P})
is a group.
∎

3.3.
Conclusions

We will now prove that the adelic length is a word metric estimate.

Proposition 3.3.1
.

Let
P=\{p_{r}\}_{r=1}^{k}
be a finite set of primes. Suppose that
f
is an element of
\operatorname{FG}_{n}({P})
. Then

Q+R\left\llbracket{f}\right\rrbracket_{n,a}\leq\left|{f}\right|_{n,P}\leq S+T\left\llbracket{f}\right\rrbracket_{n,a},

for some positive, real constants
Q,R,S
and
T
.

Proof.

We will first prove the upper bound using statements in Subsection
3.2
. Proposition
2.0.6
states that there is a positive, real constant
J
, such that

\displaystyle\left|{f}\right|_{n,P}

\displaystyle\leq J\left\llbracket{f}\right\rrbracket_{n,P}

\displaystyle=J\sum_{i=1}^{n}\sum_{r=1}^{k}|c^{(r)}_{ii}|+J\sum_{1\leq i<j\leq n}\left[{f_{ij}}\right]_{{b_{P}}}.

Lemma
3.1.6
gives that

\sum_{r=1}^{k}|c^{(r)}_{ii}|\leq\frac{3\ln n}{2\ln 2}+\frac{3}{\ln 2}\sum_{p\in\mathcal{P}}\ln^{+}\|f\|_{p}.

Since there are
\frac{(n-1)(n-2)}{2}
upper off-diagonal entries in
f
, Proposition
3.1.5
gives,

\sum_{1\leq i<j\leq n}\left[{f_{ij}}\right]_{{b_{P}}}\leq\frac{(n-1)(n-2)}{2}\left(K+M\sum_{p\in\mathcal{P}}\ln^{+}\|f\|_{p}\right)

for positive, real constants
K
and
M
. Therefore, if we let

S=JK\frac{(n-1)(n-2)}{2}+J\frac{3\ln n}{2\ln 2}

and let

T=J\frac{3}{\ln 2}+JM\frac{(n-1)(n-2)}{2},

then,

\left|{f}\right|_{n,P}\leq S+T\left\llbracket{f}\right\rrbracket_{n,a},

which is the upper bound.

Now we will prove the lower bound, making use of the results in Subsection
3.1
. By definition, the adelic length of
f
is

\left\llbracket{f}\right\rrbracket_{n,a}=\sum_{p\in\mathcal{P}}\left(\ln^{+}\left\|{f}\right\|_{p}+\ln^{+}\left\|{f^{-1}}\right\|_{p}\right),

where
\mathcal{P}=\mathcal{P}^{*}\cup\{\infty\}
and
\mathcal{P}^{*}
is the set of all primes. By Lemma
3.2.5
,

\ln^{+}\left\|{f}\right\|_{p}+\ln^{+}\left\|{f^{-1}}\right\|_{p}

is zero unless
p
is in
P
or
p=\infty
. Hence,

\left\llbracket{f}\right\rrbracket_{n,a}=\sum_{p\in P\cup\{\infty\}}\left(\ln^{+}\left\|{f}\right\|_{p}+\ln^{+}\left\|{f^{-1}}\right\|_{p}\right).

By
3.2.2
, there is a positive, real constant
K
, such that

\sum_{p\in P}\left(\ln^{+}\left\|{f}\right\|_{p}+\ln^{+}\left\|{f^{-1}}\right\|_{p}\right)\leq K\left\llbracket{f}\right\rrbracket_{n,P}

and by Proposition
3.2.4
, there are positive, real constants
L
and
M
, such that

\ln^{+}\left\|{f}\right\|_{\infty}+\ln^{+}\left\|{f^{-1}}\right\|_{\infty}\leq L+M\left\llbracket{f}\right\rrbracket_{n,P}.

As before, let
J
be as in Proposition
2.0.6
. Then, taking
Q=\frac{L}{J}
and
R=\frac{K+M}{J}
,

Q+R\left\llbracket{f}\right\rrbracket_{n,a}\leq\left|{f}\right|_{n,P},

which completes the proof.
∎

Corollary 3.3.2
.

Let
P=\{p_{r}\}_{r=1}^{k}
be a non-empty, finite set of primes. Let
\mu
be a probability measure on
\operatorname{FG}_{n}({P})
. Then,
\mu
has finite first moment with respect to word length if and only if it has finite first moment with respect to adelic length.

Proof.

Suppose that
\mu
has finite first moment with respect to adelic length. The measure
\mu
has finite first moment with respect to word length if

\int_{\operatorname{FG}_{n}({P})}\left|{f}\right|_{n,P}\,d\mu(f)

is finite. By Proposition
3.3.1
, this quantity is bounded above by

S+T\int_{\operatorname{FG}_{n}({P})}\left\llbracket{f}\right\rrbracket_{n,a}\,d\mu(f)

which is finite by assumption. The converse is similar.
∎

As mentioned in Corollary
1.0.2
, any finitely generated upper triangular matrix group,
H
with rational entries is a subgroup of
\operatorname{FG}_{n}({P})
for suitable
n
and
P
. Translating the first moment condition to the word length taken with respect to the generating set on the subgroup
H
is then mostly a matter of computing the distortion of
H
in
\operatorname{FG}_{n}({P})
. See Davis and Olshanskii
[
5
]
.

Example 3.3.3

(Affine group over the dyadic rationals)
.

Denote by
\operatorname{Aff}(\mathbb{Z}[\nicefrac{{1}}{{{2}}}])
the affine group over the dyadic rationals,

\operatorname{Aff}(\mathbb{Z}[\nicefrac{{1}}{{{2}}}])=\left\{\begin{pmatrix}2^{n}&f\\
0&1\end{pmatrix}:n\in\mathbb{Z},f\in\mathbb{Z}[\nicefrac{{1}}{{{2}}}]\right\}.

The group
\operatorname{Aff}(\mathbb{Z}[\nicefrac{{1}}{{{p}}}])
is isomorphic to the solvable Baumslag-Solitar group
BS(1,p)
, see McLaury
[
15
]
. The group is a subgroup of
FG_{n}(2)
for any
n
greater than or equal to
2
and is generated by the elements
\theta_{11}(2)
and
\theta_{12}(1)
. Hence, a probability measure
\mu
on
\operatorname{Aff}(\mathbb{Z}[\nicefrac{{1}}{{{2}}}])
has finite first moment with respect to word length if and only if it has finite first moment with respect to adelic length.

Example 3.3.4
.

According to Burillo and Platón
[
4
]
, The map,
E
, from
\operatorname{UT}_{n}(\mathbb{Z})
to the natural numbers given by

E(f)=\sum_{1\leq i<j\leq n}|f_{ij}|^{\frac{1}{j-i}}

is a word metric estimate on that group. If
f
is a matrix in
\operatorname{UT}_{n}(\mathbb{Z})
, then

\displaystyle\ln E(f)

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq i<j\leq n\\
|f_{ij}|\neq 0\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{j-i}\ln\left(|f_{ij}|\right)

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq i<j\leq n\\
|f_{ij}|\neq 0\end{subarray}}\left(1+d_{+}\left(f_{ij}\right)\right)

\displaystyle=\left\llbracket{f}\right\rrbracket_{n,P}.

Since both
\left\llbracket{\cdot}\right\rrbracket_{n,P}
and
\left\llbracket{\cdot}\right\rrbracket_{n,a}
are word metric estimates, a probability measure
\mu
on
\operatorname{UT}_{n}(\mathbb{Z})
has finite first logarithmic moment with respect to word length if it has finite first moment with respect to adelic length.

4.
Formulae for products and inverses

If
P
is a singleton, then
\operatorname{FG}_{n}({P})
is a semi-direct product,
\mathbb{Z}^{n}\ltimes\operatorname{UT}_{n}(\mathbb{Z}[\nicefrac{{1}}{{{p}}}])
, where
\operatorname{UT}_{n}(\mathbb{Z}[\nicefrac{{1}}{{{p}}}])
is the group of upper unitriangular matrices with entries in
\mathbb{Z}[\nicefrac{{1}}{{{p}}}]
. In this section, we give formulae for the multiplication of many elements and computation of inverses for semi-direct products of this form.

Let
n
be a natural number and let
p
be a fixed prime. Denote by
\mathbb{F}
the field of real numbers,
p
-adic numbers
\mathbb{Q}_{{p}}
, rational numbers
\mathbb{Q}
, or the ring

\mathbb{Z}[\nicefrac{{1}}{{{p}}}]=\left\{\frac{f}{p^{m}}:f,m\in\mathbb{Z}\right\}.

Let
A_{n}(\mathbb{F})
be the group of invertible upper triangular matrices of dimension
n
over
\mathbb{F}
and let
UT_{n}(\mathbb{F})
be the subgroup of all upper unitriangular matrices in
A_{n}(\mathbb{F})
.

Unless otherwise noted, let
G
be the group
\mathbb{Z}^{n}\ltimes A_{n}(\mathbb{F})
, where the product of two elements
({x},f)
and
({y},g)
in
G
is given by

({x},f)({y},g)=\left({x}{y},f\zeta_{x}(g)\right),

where
\zeta_{x}(y)=XyX^{-1}
,

X=\begin{pmatrix}p^{x_{1}}&0&\cdots&0\\
0&p^{x_{2}}&\cdots&0\\
\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
0&0&\cdots&p^{x_{n}}\end{pmatrix}

and
{x}=(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})
.

The identity element,
e
, in
G
is
(\mathbf{0},I_{n})
, where
\mathbf{0}
is the zero vector and
I_{n}
is the
n\times n
identity matrix.

The product of
m
elements,

(15)

\displaystyle({{y}^{(m)}},{\varphi^{(m)}})

\displaystyle=\prod_{i=1}^{m}\left({{x}^{(i)}},{f^{(i)}}\right)=\left({{x}^{(1)}},{f^{(1)}}\right)\dots\left({{x}^{(m)}},{f^{(m)}}\right)

is given by the relations

{{y}^{(m)}}=\sum_{r=1}^{m}{{x}^{(m)}},\ \textrm{and}\ {\varphi^{(m)}}={\varphi^{(m-1)}}\zeta_{{{y}^{(m-1)}}}\left({f^{(m)}}\right),

where
{\varphi^{(0)}}
and
{{y}^{(0)}}
are taken to be the identity and
{{y}^{(m)}}=\left(y^{(m)}_{1},\dots,y^{(m)}_{n}\right)
. It follows from the definition of matrix multiplication that

(16)

\varphi^{(m)}_{ij}=\sum_{k=1}^{n}\varphi^{(m-1)}_{ik}f^{(m)}_{kj}p^{y^{(m-1)}_{k}-y^{(m-1)}_{j}},

whenever
m
is greater than or equal to
2
. Expanding this recurrence relation,

(17)

\varphi^{(m)}_{i,i+r}=\sum_{\{a_{k}\}\in Q(m,r)}\prod_{n=1}^{m}f^{(n)}_{i+a_{n},i+a_{n+1}}p^{y^{(n-1)}_{i+a_{n}}-y^{(n-1)}_{i+a_{n+1}}}.

where
m
is greater than or equal to
r
and
Q(l,s)
is the set of all integer sequences
\{a_{1},\dots,a_{l}\}
, of length
l
which are non-strictly monotone, start at
0
and finish at
s
. The case where
r=0
and
m
is any natural number is easily verified. It is similarly easy to show for the case where
m=1
and
r
is any appropriate natural number. The remaining cases are shown by induction. Namely, if we suppose that Equation (
17
) is satisfied for all natural numbers
r
and
m
less than a sufficiently large natural number
q
, then

\displaystyle\varphi^{(q)}_{i,i+r}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=1}^{n}\varphi^{(q-1)}_{i,k}f^{(q)}_{k,i+r}p^{y^{(q-1)}_{k}-y^{(q-1)}_{i+r}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=i}^{i+r}\varphi^{(q-1)}_{i,k}f^{(q)}_{k,i+r}p^{y^{(q-1)}_{k}-y^{(q-1)}_{i+r}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{r}\varphi^{(q-1)}_{i,i+k}f^{(q)}_{i+k,i+r}p^{y^{(q-1)}_{i+k}-y^{(q-1)}_{i+r}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{r}\left(\sum_{\{a_{k}\}\in Q(q-1,k)}\prod_{n=1}^{q-1}f^{(n)}_{i+a_{n},i+a_{n+1}}p^{y^{(n-1)}_{i+a_{n}}-y^{(n-1)}_{i+a_{n+1}}}\right)f^{(q)}_{i+k,i+r}p^{y^{(q-1)}_{i+k}-y^{(q-1)}_{i+r}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\{a_{k}\}\in Q(q,r)}\prod_{n=1}^{q}f^{(n)}_{i+a_{n},i+a_{n+1}}p^{y^{(n-1)}_{i+a_{n}}-y^{(n-1)}_{i+a_{n+1}}}.

If
G
is instead equal to
\mathbb{Z}^{n}\ltimes UT_{n}(\mathbb{F})
with the same action, where
UT_{n}(\mathbb{F})
is the group of all upper unitriangular matrices with entries in
\mathbb{F}
, then the entries on the diagonal of
{f^{(m)}}
are all equal to
1
. Equation (
17
) then gives

\displaystyle\varphi^{(m)}_{ij}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=i}^{j}\varphi^{(m-1)}_{ik}f^{(m)}_{kj}p^{y^{(m-1)}_{k}-y^{(m-1)}_{j}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=i}^{j-1}\left(\varphi^{(m-1)}_{ik}f^{(m)}_{kj}p^{y^{(m-1)}_{k}-y^{(m-1)}_{j}}\right)+\varphi^{(m-1)}_{ij}

\displaystyle=\sum_{l=1}^{m}\sum_{k=i}^{j-1}\left(\varphi^{(l-1)}_{ik}f^{(l)}_{kj}p^{y^{(l-1)}_{k}-y^{(l-1)}_{j}}\right)

(18)

\displaystyle=\sum_{l=1}^{m}\sum_{k=i}^{j-1}\left(\varphi^{(l-1)}_{ik}f^{(l)}_{kj}p^{y^{(l-1)}_{k}-y^{(l-1)}_{j}}\right).

For sufficiently large
m
, an alternate expansion of the recurrence relation in this case is

(19)

\varphi^{(m)}_{i,i+r}=\sum_{a\in\operatorname{MFI}\left({r}\right)}S_{a}^{(m)}

where
\operatorname{MFI}\left({r}\right)
is the set of all strictly monotone increasing finite integer valued sequences,
a=\{a_{n}\}
, with first term equal to
0
and last term equal to
r
indexed by the non-negative integers and

(20)

S_{a}^{(m)}=\sum_{0\leq b_{1}<\dots<b_{|a|-1}<m}\prod_{n=0}^{|a|-2}f^{(b_{n+1}+1)}_{i+a_{n},i+a_{n+1}}p^{y^{(b_{n+1})}_{i+a_{n}}-y^{(b_{n+1})}_{i+a_{n+1}}}.

The validity of this formula may be checked by induction on Equation (
17
).

Example 4.0.1
.

The set
\operatorname{MFI}\left({2}\right)
contains only the finite sequences
(0,2)
and
(0,1,2)
. Equation (
19
) gives

\displaystyle\varphi^{(m)}_{i,i+2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{a\in\operatorname{MFI}\left({2}\right)}S_{a}^{(m)}

\displaystyle=\sum_{0\leq l<m}f^{(l+1)}_{i,i+2}p^{y^{(l)}_{i}-y^{(l)}_{i+2}}+\sum_{0\leq r<l<m}f^{(r+1)}_{i,i+1}f^{(l+1)}_{i+1,i+2}p^{y^{(r)}_{i}-y^{(r)}_{i+1}}p^{y^{(l)}_{i+1}-y^{(l)}_{i+2}}

when
m
is greater than or equal to
2
.
\operatorname{MFI}\left({3}\right)
contains
(0,3),(0,1,3),(0,2,3)
and
(0,1,2,3)
. When
m
is greater than or equal to
3
, Equation (
19
) gives

\displaystyle\varphi^{(m)}_{i,i+3}=

\displaystyle\sum_{a\in\operatorname{MFI}\left({3}\right)}S_{a}^{(m)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{0\leq l<m}f^{(l+1)}_{i,i+3}p^{y^{(l)}_{i}-y^{(l)}_{i+3}}

\displaystyle+\sum_{0\leq r<l<m}f^{(r+1)}_{i,i+1}f^{(l+1)}_{i+1,i+3}p^{y^{(r)}_{i}-y^{(r)}_{i+1}}p^{y^{(l)}_{i+1}-y^{(l)}_{i+3}}

\displaystyle+\sum_{0\leq r<l<m}f^{(r+1)}_{i,i+2}f^{(l+1)}_{i+2,i+3}p^{y^{(r)}_{i}-y^{(r)}_{i+2}}p^{y^{(l)}_{i+2}-y^{(l)}_{i+3}}

\displaystyle+\sum_{0\leq s<r<l<m}f^{(s+1)}_{i,i+1}f^{(r+1)}_{i+1,i+2}f^{(l+1)}_{i+2,i+3}p^{y^{(s)}_{i}-y^{(s)}_{i+1}}p^{y^{(r)}_{i+1}-y^{(r)}_{i+2}}p^{y^{(l)}_{i+2}-y^{(l)}_{i+3}}.

The inverse of an element
({x},f)
in
G
is

({x},f)^{-1}=\left({x}^{-1},{x}^{-1}f^{-1}{x}\right).

The propositions which follow give useful explicit formulae for
f^{-1}
in the upper unitriangular and upper triangular cases.

Proposition 4.0.2
.

Suppose that
f
is an
n\times n
upper unitriangular matrix. Then, for each pair of natural numbers
i
and
s
, such that
i
is less than
n
and
s
is less than or equal to
n-i
, the inverse matrix
f^{-1}
satisfies the recurrence relations

(f_{i,i+1})^{-1}=-f_{i,i+1}\quad\textrm{and}\quad(f_{i,i+s})^{-1}=-\sum_{k=1}^{s}f_{i,i+k}(f_{i+k,i+s})^{-1}.

Hence we have

(21)

(f_{i,i+s})^{-1}=\sum_{l=1}^{s}(-1)^{l}\left(\sum_{\{h_{\zeta}\}\in H(l,s)}\left(\prod_{\xi=0}^{l-1}f_{i+h_{\xi},i+h_{\xi+1}}\right)\right),

where
H(l,s)
is the collection of finite sequences of integers
\left\{h_{0},\dots,h_{l}\right\}
, such that
h_{k}
is less than
h_{k+1}
,
h_{1}=0
and
h_{l}=s
.

Proof.

Consider the system of linear equations
ff^{-1}=I_{n}
. This system states that

0=\sum_{k=1}^{n}f_{ik}(f_{k,i+s})^{-1}=\sum_{k=i}^{i+s}f_{ik}(f_{k,i+s})^{-1}=(f_{i,i+s})^{-1}+\sum_{k=i+1}^{i+s}f_{ik}(f_{k,i+s})^{-1}

whenever
s
is a natural number. Rearranging and reindexing, we arrive at the recurrence relations in the statement of the proposition, which shows that Equation (
21
) is valid for
s=1
. Suppose that Equation (
21
) is satisfied for all natural numbers
r
less than
s
. Then,

\displaystyle(f_{i,i+s+1})^{-1}

\displaystyle=-\sum_{k=1}^{s+1}f_{{i},i+k}(f_{i+k,i+s+1})^{-1}

\displaystyle=-\sum_{k=1}^{s}f_{{i},i+k}\sum_{l=1}^{s-k+1}(-1)^{l}\left(\sum_{\{h_{\zeta}\}\in H(l,s-k+1)}\left(\prod_{\xi=0}^{l-1}f_{{i}+k+h_{\xi},i+k+h_{\xi+1}}\right)\right)-f_{{i},i+s+1}

\displaystyle=\sum_{l=1}^{s+1}(-1)^{l}\left(\sum_{\{h_{\zeta}\}\in H(l,s+1)}\left(\prod_{\xi=0}^{l-1}f_{{i}+h_{\xi},i+h_{\xi+1}}\right)\right).

Hence, Equation (
21
) is satisfied for
s+1
.

∎

Remark 4.0.3
.

A more elegant proof, pointed out to the author by George Willis, involves writing
f=I_{n}+g
for a nilpotent matrix
g
and noting that since
g
is nilpotent and all eigenvalues are zero,
(I_{n}+g)^{-1}=\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}g^{k}
.

Corollary 4.0.4
.

Suppose that
f
is an
n\times n
upper triangular matrix. Then, for each pair of natural numbers
i
and

, such that
i
is less than
n
and

is less than or equal to

5.
Random walks on
\operatorname{FG}_{n}({p})

Now we turn our attention to random walks on
\operatorname{FG}_{n}({p})
for a single prime
p
. We define a
displacement matrix
associated with every measure
\mu
on
\operatorname{FG}_{n}({p})
that has finite first moment with respect to word length. We construct a
\mu
-boundary,
(\Gamma,\nu)
, dependent on the displacement matrix, so that the right random walk converges almost surely to elements of
\Gamma
. We use this boundary to estimate paths in the right random walk with at most linear error and prove that it is a valid description of the Poisson boundary in some cases. We conclude by discussing conditions for triviality of the Poisson boundary.

Let
p
be prime and let
n
be a natural number. Then,
\operatorname{FG}_{n}({p})
is the discrete solvable group of upper triangular
n\times n
matrices whose entries are integer powers of
p
on the main diagonal and elements of
\mathbb{Z}[\nicefrac{{1}}{{{p}}}]
otherwise. It is possible to write any element
f
of
\operatorname{FG}_{n}({p})
in the form
f=g{x}
, where
{x}
is the diagonal matrix

{x}=\begin{pmatrix}p^{x_{1}}&0&\cdots&0\\
0&p^{x_{2}}&\cdots&0\\
\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
0&0&\cdots&p^{x_{n}}\end{pmatrix}.

and
g
is the upper uni-triangular matrix with entries

g_{ij}=\begin{cases}1&\textrm{if}\ i=j,\\
\frac{f_{ij}}{p^{x_{j}}}&\textrm{if}\ i<j,\textrm{and}\\
0&\textrm{if}\ i>j,\end{cases}

where
f_{ij}
and
p
are coprime. Hence,
\operatorname{FG}_{n}({p})
is an internal semi-direct product, isomorphic to
\mathbb{Z}^{n}\ltimes\operatorname{UT}_{n}(\mathbb{Z}[\nicefrac{{1}}{{{p}}}])
. We denote
\mathbb{Z}^{n}\ltimes\operatorname{UT}_{n}(\mathbb{Z}[\nicefrac{{1}}{{{p}}}])
by
G_{n}^{p}
.

Proposition 5.0.1

(Metric estimate on
G_{n}^{p}
)
.

For each element
({x},f)
in
G_{n}^{p}
, let

\left\llbracket({x},f)\right\rrbracket=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left|x_{i}\right|+\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}\sum_{j=i+1}^{n}\left[{f_{ij}}\right]_{p},

where
|z|
is the ordinary absolute value of an integer
z
. Then, there are positive, real constants
J^{\prime}
and
L^{\prime}
(dependent on
n
) such that

J^{\prime}\llbracket({x},f)\rrbracket\leq|({x},f)|\leq L^{\prime}\llbracket({x},f)\rrbracket,

i.e., the gauge function
\llbracket\cdot\rrbracket
is a metric estimate on
G_{n}^{p}
.

Proof.

See Proposition
2.0.6
. The generating set is instead the subset of
G_{n}^{p}
consisting of the elements
(\theta_{ii}(p),I_{n})
for natural numbers
i
less than
n
and
(\mathbf{0},\theta_{ij}(1))
for natural numbers
i
and
j
, such that
i
is less than
j
which is less than or equal to
n
, where
\theta_{ij}
and
\theta_{ii}
is as defined at the start of Section
1
.
∎

5.1.
The displacement matrix

Suppose that
\mu
is a measure on
G_{n}^{p}
that has finite first moment with respect to word length and that

\left({{y}^{(m)}},{\varphi^{(m)}}\right)=\prod_{i=1}^{m}\left({{x}^{(m)}},{f^{(m)}}\right)

is a path in the random walk associated with
\mu
on
G_{n}^{p}
. For every natural number
p
less than
n
, let
\pi^{{p}}
be the map from
G_{n}^{p}
to
\mathbb{Z}
given by

\pi^{{p}}({x},f)=x_{p},

where
x=(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})
. Let
\mu_{{p}}
be the pushforward measure given by

\mu_{{p}}(E)={\pi^{{p}}}_{*}{\mu}

and let
m_{1}\left({\mu_{{p}}}\right)=\int_{z\in\mathbb{Z}}z\,d\mu_{{p}}(z)
be the first moment of each measure
\mu_{{p}}
.

Similarly, for natural numbers
p
and
q
less than
n
, let
\pi^{{pq}}
be the map from
G_{n}^{p}
to
\mathbb{Z}[\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}]
given by

\pi^{{pq}}({x},f)=[f]_{pq}

and let
\mu_{{pq}}
be the pushforward measure:

\mu_{{pq}}(E)={\pi^{{pq}}}_{*}{\mu}.

Finally, let
m_{1}\left({\mu_{{pq}}}\right)=\int_{\mathbb{Z}[\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}]}z\,d\mu_{{pq}}
be the first moment of
\mu_{{pq}}
.

Lemma 5.1.1
.

Suppose that
\mu
is a measure on
G_{n}^{p}
that has finite first moment with respect to word length. Then, the first moment,
m_{1}\left({\mu_{{p}}}\right)
, of each measure
\mu_{{p}}
is finite.

Proof.

It is obvious that

\displaystyle m_{1}\left({\mu_{{p}}}\right)

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{Z}}z\,d{\pi^{{p}}}_{*}{\mu}(z)

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\mathbb{Z}}|z|\,d{\pi^{{p}}}_{*}{\mu}(z).

Because the first moment of
\mu
is finite, Theorem 3.6.1 in Bogachev
[
1
]
is applicable, stating that

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{Z}}|z|\,d{\pi^{{p}}}_{*}{\mu}(z)=\int_{G_{n}^{p}}|\pi^{{p}}(z)|\,d\mu(z)

\displaystyle\leq\int_{G_{n}^{p}}|z|\,d\mu(z),

which is finite since
|\cdot|
is equivalent to every metric estimate on
G_{n}^{p}
.
∎

Let
D
be the matrix in
\operatorname{UT}_{n}(\mathbb{R})
given by

D_{{{ij}}}=m_{1}\left({\mu_{{i}}}\right)-m_{1}\left({\mu_{{j}}}\right).

We refer to
D
as the
displacement matrix associated with
(G_{n}^{p},\mu)
. The entries of
D
are finite by Lemma
5.1.1
. Each super-diagonal entry
D_{{{ij}}}
shall be referred to as a
displacement associated with
(G_{n}^{p},\mu)
. If
i
,
j
and
k
are natural numbers such that
i\leq j\leq k\leq n
, then

D_{{{ik}}}=D_{{{ij}}}+D_{{{jk}}}.

Furthermore, if
\{a_{k}\}
is an element of
\operatorname{MFI}\left({r}\right)
, as defined for Equation (
19
), then

D_{{{i,i+r}}}=\sum_{k=0}^{|a_{k}|-1}D_{{{a_{k},a_{k+1}}}}.

In particular, by taking
\{a_{k}\}
=
\{0,1,\dots,j-i\}
in this expression, it is evident that
D
is determined exactly by its entries on the first super-diagonal.

If every super-diagonal entry of
D
is non-zero, then we say that
D
is
non-zero
. If every super-diagonal entry in a column of
D
has the same sign, then
D
is
column homogeneous
. If every super-diagonal entry in a row of
D
has the same sign, then
D
is
row homogeneous
. If all super-diagonal entries in
D
have the same sign, then we say that
D
is
homogeneous
.

Lemma 5.1.2
.

Suppose that
\mu
is a measure on
G_{n}^{p}
that has finite first moment with respect to word length. Let
D
be the displacement matrix associated with
\mu
. Let
\check{\mu}
be the reflected measure,

\check{\mu}(E):=\mu(E^{-1})

for all measurable subsets
E
of
G
. Let
\check{D}
be the displacement matrix associated with
\check{\mu}
. Then

D_{ij}=\check{D}_{ji}

for all natural numbers
i<j\leq n
. In particular,

(i)

D
is non-zero if and only if
\check{D}
is non-zero,

(ii)

D
is column consistent if and only if
\check{D}
is row consistent and

(iii)

D
is row consistent if and only if
\check{D}
is column consistent.

(iv)

D
is homogeneous if and only if
\check{D}
is homogeneous.

Proof.

If
i
is less than
j
, then

\displaystyle D_{{{ij}}}

\displaystyle=m_{1}\left({\mu_{{i}}}\right)-m_{1}\left({\mu_{{j}}}\right)

\displaystyle=\int_{z\in\mathbb{Z}}z\,d\mu_{{i}}(z)-\int_{z\in\mathbb{Z}}z\,d\mu_{{j}}(z)

\displaystyle=-\int_{z\in\mathbb{Z}}z\,d\check{\mu_{{i}}}(z)+\int_{z\in\mathbb{Z}}z\,d\check{\mu_{{i}}}(z)

\displaystyle=m_{1}\left({\check{\mu_{{j}}}}\right)-m_{1}\left({\check{\mu_{{i}}}}\right)

\displaystyle=\check{D}_{ji},

which gives the statement of the lemma.
∎

5.2.
Pointwise convergence to elements of a
\mu
-boundary

For the remainder, we suppose that
\mu
is a measure on
G_{n}^{p}
that has finite first moment with respect to word length, such that the group generated by
\operatorname{supp}\mu
is non-abelian. Let
D
be the displacement matrix associated with
\mu
. Suppose that
D
is non-zero and column consistent. Let

\left({{y}^{(m)}},{\varphi^{(m)}}\right)=\prod_{i=1}^{m}\left({{x}^{(m)}},{f^{(m)}}\right)

be a path in the random walk associated with
\mu
on
G_{n}^{p}
.

In the first part of this section we discuss convergence of entries in either
\mathbb{R}
or

, depending on the displacement matrix. In the second part we use this information to construct a
\mu
-boundary, such that the right random walk converges almost surely to elements of
\Gamma
.

Lemma 5.2.1
.

For every pair of natural numbers
i
and
j
, such that
i\leq j\leq n
,

\log\left(1+\left|f^{(m)}_{ij}\right|\right)\ \textrm{and}\ \log\left(1+\left|f^{(m)}_{ij}\right|_{p}\right)

are both in
o(m)
almost surely.

Proof.

Let
i
and
j
be natural numbers such that
i<j\leq n
. Then,

\displaystyle\left|\log\left(1+\left|f^{(m)}_{ij}\right|\right)\right|

\displaystyle\leq\left|d_{+}^{p}\left(1+\left|f^{(m)}_{ij}\right|\right)\right|

\displaystyle\leq 1+\left|d_{+}^{p}\left(f^{(m)}_{ij}\right)\right|

\displaystyle\leq 1+\max\left\{\left|d_{-}^{p}\left(f^{(m)}_{ij}\right)\right|,\left|d_{+}^{p}\left(f^{(m)}_{ij}\right)\right|\right\}

\displaystyle\leq\left[{f^{(m)}_{ij}}\right]_{p}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{K}\left|\left({{x}^{(m)}},{f^{(m)}}\right)\right|,

for some positive, real constant
K
. Similarly,

\displaystyle\left|\log\left(1+\left|f^{(m)}_{ij}\right|_{p}\right)\right|

\displaystyle\leq 1+\left|d_{-}^{p}\left(f^{(m)}_{ij}\right)\right|

\displaystyle\leq 1+\max\left\{\left|d_{-}^{p}\left(f^{(m)}_{ij}\right)\right|,\left|d_{+}^{p}\left(f^{(m)}_{ij}\right)\right|\right\}

\displaystyle\leq\left[{f^{(m)}_{ij}}\right]_{p}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{K}\left|\left({{x}^{(m)}},{f^{(m)}}\right)\right|.

Suppose that
\left|\left({{x}^{(m)}},{f^{(m)}}\right)\right|
is not in
o(m)
almost surely. The integral

\int_{G_{n}^{p}}\left|\left({x},f\right)\right|\,d\mu({x},f)

is not bounded, which is a contradiction, because we assumed
\mu
had finite first moment with respect to word length.
∎

Lemma 5.2.2
.

For all positive, real numbers
\varepsilon
, there is a natural number
N
, such that

(D_{ij}-\varepsilon)l<y^{(l)}_{i}-y^{(l)}_{j}<(D_{ij}+\varepsilon)l

whenever
l
is greater than
N
.

Proof.

This statement is just the strong law of large numbers, which states the existence of a natural number
N
, such that

\left|\frac{y^{(l)}_{i}-y^{(l)}_{j}}{l}-D_{ij}\right|<\varepsilon

whenever
l
is greater than
N
.
∎

Corollary 5.2.3
.

If
D_{ij}
is positive and
x
is a real number, then there is a natural number
N
, such that

y^{(l)}_{i}-y^{(l)}_{j}>x

whenever
l
is greater than
N
. Similarly, if
D_{ij}
is negative, then there is a natural number
N
, such that

y^{(l)}_{i}-y^{(l)}_{j}<x

whenever
l
is greater than
N
.

Proof.

Choose
\varepsilon=\frac{D_{ij}}{2}
in Lemma
5.2.2
. If
D_{ij}
is positive, then
\frac{1}{2}lD_{ij}
is greater than
x
for all sufficiently large
l
. Similarly, if
D_{ij}
is negative,
\frac{3}{2}lD_{ij}
is less than
x
for all sufficiently large
l
.
∎

Corollary 5.2.4
.

If
D_{ij}
is positive, then the sequence

\left\{\max_{1\leq r\leq l}\left(y^{(l)}_{i}-y^{(l)}_{j}\right)\right\}_{l\in\mathbb{N}}

is eventually constant.

Proof.

By Corollary
5.2.3
,
y^{(l)}_{i}-y^{(l)}_{j}
is less than
y^{(1)}_{i}-y^{(1)}_{j}
for all sufficiently large
l
.
∎

Proposition 5.2.5
.

Suppose that
\mu
is a measure on
G_{n}^{p}
that has finite first moment with respect to word length, such that the group generated by
\operatorname{supp}\mu
is non-abelian. Let
D
be the displacement matrix associated with
\mu
. Let
i
and
j
be natural numbers such that
i<j\leq n
. Suppose that
\mu
is a measure such that
D_{ij}
positive and
\operatorname{sgn}\left({D_{ij}}\right)=\operatorname{sgn}\left({D_{kj}}\right)
for all natural numbers
k
, such that
i<k<j
. Let

\left({{y}^{(m)}},{\varphi^{(m)}}\right)=\prod_{i=1}^{m}\left({{x}^{(m)}},{f^{(m)}}\right)

be a path in the random walk associated with
\mu
on
G_{n}^{p}
. The sequence
\varphi^{(m)}_{ij}
is almost surely convergent in

Proof.

Let

. Suppose that

is large, so that Equation (
19

. To show that the sequence

\displaystyle=\lim_{b_{|a|-1}\rightarrow\infty}\left|\sum_{0\leq b_{1}<\dots<b_{\left|a\right|-1}}\prod_{n=0}^{\left|a\right|-2}f^{(b_{n+1}+1)}_{i+a_{n},i+a_{n+1}}p^{y^{(b_{n+1})}_{i+a_{n}}-y^{(b_{n+1})}_{i+a_{n+1}}}\right|_{p}

Lemma 5.2.7
.

Suppose that
\mu
is a measure on
G_{n}^{p}
that has finite first moment with respect to word length, such that the group generated by
\operatorname{supp}\mu
is non-abelian. Let

be the displacement matrix associated with
\mu
. Let

and

be natural numbers such that

. Suppose that
\mu
is a measure such that

is negative. Let

. Let
a
be an element of
\operatorname{MFI}\left({r}\right)
, then there is a positive, real constant
K
, such that

U_{a}^{(m)}:=\sum_{0\leq b_{1}<\dots<b_{\left|a\right|-1}}\prod_{n=0}^{\left|a\right|-2}p^{b_{n+1}D_{i+a_{n},i+a_{n+1}}}\leq Kp^{b_{\left|a\right|-1}D_{i+a_{n},i+r}}

for each non-negative integer

less than
|a|
.

Proof.

The base case is straightforward. The series

(23)

\sum_{0\leq b_{1}<b_{2}}p^{b_{1}D_{i,i+a_{1}}}

is geometric and bounded above by a positive, real constant
K_{1}
if

is negative. If

is positive, then (

) is equal to

, which is bounded above by

Suppose that

is a natural number less than

and that there is a positive, real constant

and a natural number

less than

, such that

\sum_{0\leq b_{1}<\dots<b_{k}<b_{k+1}}\prod_{n=0}^{k}p^{b_{n+1}D_{i+a_{n},i+a_{n+1}}}\leq Kp^{b_{k+1}D_{i+a_{s},i+a_{k}}}.

If

is negative, then
D_{i+a_{n},a+a_{k}}+D_{i+a_{k},a+a_{k+1}}=D_{i+a_{n},a+a_{k+1}}
is negative by the condition given in the lemma, implying the convergence of

K_{k+1}\sum_{k-1\leq b_{k+1}<b_{k+2}}p^{b_{k+1}(D_{i+a_{s},i+a_{k}}+D_{i+a_{k},i+a_{k+1}})}

to a limit

and that

\displaystyle\sum_{0\leq b_{1}<\dots<b_{k}<b_{k+1}}\prod_{n=0}^{k}p^{b_{n+1}D_{i+a_{n},i+a_{n+1}}}

\displaystyle\leq K_{k}p^{mD_{i+a_{s},i+a_{k}}}

\displaystyle\leq K_{k}\sum_{k-1\leq b_{k+1}<b_{k+2}}p^{b_{k+1}(D_{i+a_{s},i+a_{k}}+D_{i+a_{k},i+a_{k+1}})}

\displaystyle\leq K_{k+1}=K_{k+1}p^{b_{k+2}D_{i+a_{k+1},i+a_{k+1}}}.

If

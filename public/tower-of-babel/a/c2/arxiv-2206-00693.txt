Indications for a Nonzero Lepton Asymmetry from Extremely Metal-Poor Galaxies Preprint: UCI-TR-2022-10 Preprint: YITP-SB-2022-22
Anne-Katherine Burns, Tim M.P. Tait, Mauro Valli

Abstract
The recent measurement of helium-4 from the near-infrared spectroscopy of extremely metal-poor galaxies (EMPGs) by the Subaru Survey may point to a new puzzle in the Early Universe. We exploit this new helium measurement together with the percent-level determination of primordial deuterium, to assess indications for a non-vanishing lepton asymmetry during the Big Bang Nucleosynthesis (BBN) era, paying particular attention to the role of uncertainties in the nuclear reaction network. A cutting-edge Bayesian analysis focused on the role of the newly measured EMPGs, jointly with information from the Cosmic Microwave Background, suggests the existence of a nonzero lepton asymmetry at around the 2 ​ σ 2\sigma level, providing a hint for cosmology beyond Λ \Lambda CDM. We discuss conditions for a large total lepton asymmetry to be consistently realized in the Early Universe.

Introduction

Cosmological observations from the Early Universe provide an invaluable probe of Physics Beyond the Standard Model (BSM). Observations of the

II
Primordial Lepton Asymmetries

Electric charge neutrality of the Early Universe does not allow for a large primordial asymmetry in the charged lepton sector, which is constrained to
be (at most) of the order of the baryon-to-photon ratio
\eta_{B}\equiv n_{B}/n_{\gamma}\sim\mathcal{O}(10^{-10})

Kolb and Turner 1990
;
Rubakov and Gorbunov 2017
.
Nevertheless, a large cosmic asymmetry can be hidden in the neutrino sector
Simha and Steigman 2008
:

\eta_{L}\equiv\frac{1}{n_{\gamma}}\sum_{i=e,\mu,\tau}(n_{\nu_{i}}-n_{\bar{\nu}_{i}})\simeq\frac{\pi^{2}}{33\zeta(3)}(\xi_{\nu_{e}}+\xi_{\nu_{\mu}}+\xi_{\nu_{\tau}})\ ,

(1)

where
n_{\gamma}
is the photon number density,
n_{\nu_{i}}
the flavor
i
neutrino density, and

\xi_{\nu_{i}}\equiv\mu_{\nu_{i}}/T_{\nu_{i}}
are the
degeneracy parameters
defined as
the chemical potential for each neutrino normalized to its temperature, which encode the relevant lepton asymmetries today.
Eq. (
1
) assumes
T_{\nu_{i}}/T_{\gamma}=(4/11)^{1/3}
, which is a good approximation given the modest impact of non-instantaneous neutrino decoupling
and tiny departures from the Fermi-Dirac distributions in relativistic freeze-out
Akita and Yamaguchi 2020
;
Froustey et al. 2020
;
Bennett et al. 2021
.
It is further relevant that nonzero neutrino chemical potentials play a marginal role in SM neutrino decoupling
Grohs et al. 2016
;
Escudero Abenza 2020
.

Eq. (
1
) further implements the condition
|\xi_{\nu_{i}}|<1
, as
\mathcal{O}(1)
degeneracy parameters
were probed by early-stage CMB observations about two decades ago
Kinney and Riotto 1999
;
Lesgourgues and Pastor 1999
,
and now are robustly
Oldengott and Schwarz 2017
;
Bonilla et al. 2019
;
Kumar et al. 2022
ruled out (irrespective of the lepton flavor
Abazajian et al. 2002
;
Castorina et al. 2012
).
In fact, a nonzero chemical potential for the
i
-flavored neutrino would yield a contribution to the total radiation density (relative to photons) of

\frac{\Delta\rho_{\rm rad}}{\rho_{\gamma}}\simeq\frac{15}{4\pi^{2}}\left(\frac{4}{11}\right)^{4/3}\xi_{\nu_{i}}^{2}\ ,

(2)

and would increase the expansion rate of the Universe, resulting in a positive shift of
N_{\rm eff}
that
would delay the time of matter-radiation equality which is tightly constrained by the CMB acoustic peaks.

From the Planck constraint on
N_{\rm eff}
adopting the likelihood analysis including TTTEEE and low-
\ell
measurements, as well as
baryonic acoustic oscillations (BAO) and lensing data, and assuming a flat prior on
\rm Y_{\rm P}
, one may derive a simple upper-bound on the
degeneracy parameters purely driven by the CMB.
In particular, for
N_{\rm eff}=2.97\pm 0.29
(68% probability interval)
Aghanim et al. 2020
;
Aghanim et al. 2019a
,
considering the SM prediction
N_{\rm eff}=3.044
(known better than the per-mille level)
Akita and Yamaguchi 2020
;
Froustey et al. 2020
;
Bennett et al. 2021
,
the 1
\sigma
upper-bound is:

\xi_{\nu_{e}}^{2}+\xi_{\nu_{\mu}}^{2}+\xi_{\nu_{\tau}}^{2}\lesssim 0.5\ ,

(3)

implying the conservative constraint
|\xi_{\nu_{i}}|\lesssim 0.71
, valid for each flavor individually (see also Ref.
Barenboim et al. 2017
).
Since the onset of neutrino oscillations is expected to occur around
T_{\nu}\sim 10
MeV,
flavor equilibration in the muon-tau sector is predicted to be complete by the time of neutrino decoupling (
T_{\nu}\sim 2
MeV)
Dolgov et al. 2002
;
Pastor et al. 2009
,
and the conservative CMB bound of Eq. (
3
) becomes slightly tighter for the 2
nd
and 3
rd
generation
\nu
asymmetries:

|\xi_{\nu_{\mu,\tau}}|\lesssim 0.5\ .

(4)

BBN can place stronger constraints on the electron-neutrino asymmetry (by about an order of magnitude
Simha and Steigman 2008
;
Fields et al. 2020
) largely
because an electron-neutrino asymmetry at the time of BBN affects the
\beta
equilibrium of weak interactions controlling the neutron-proton conversion
Kohri et al. 1997
.
A positive (negative) value of
\xi_{\nu_{e}}
acts through the equilibrium
reactions
n\,\nu_{e}\leftrightarrow p\,e^{-}
,
p\,\bar{\nu}_{e}\leftrightarrow n\,e^{+}
and neutron decay to reduce (enhance) the neutron-to-proton ratio:

(n_{n}/n_{p})\big|_{\rm eq.}\simeq\exp\left(-\mathcal{Q}/T_{\gamma}-\xi_{\nu_{e}}\right)\ ,

(5)

where
\mathcal{Q}\equiv m_{n}-m_{p}=1.293
MeV is the neutron-proton mass difference.
While light primordial abundances like deuterium are particularly sensitive to
\Omega_{B}
, from Eq. (
5
) it follows that helium-4 – which depends
crucially on the amount of neutrons at the time where deuterium is no longer photo-dissociated
Kolb and Turner 1990
;
Rubakov and Gorbunov 2017
–
can be regarded as a sensitive
primordial leptometer
.

In Ref.
Pitrou et al. 2018
a combined analysis of the helium and deuterium PDG
values together with a Gaussian prior on
\Omega_{B}h^{2}
from the CMB yields the precise determination:
\xi_{\nu}=0.001\pm 0.016
, consistent with zero.
By assuming full equilibration of lepton flavor asymmetries due to neutrino oscillations, this inference is more stringent than the bound outlined in Eq. (
4
).
Nevertheless, a recent state-of-the-art investigation in Ref.
Froustey and Pitrou 2022
indicates that the degree to which full flavor equilibration is realized during
the BBN era sensitively depends on the PMNS mixing angle
\theta_{13}
and on the initially generated values of the degeneracy parameters.
In the following, we revisit the determination of
\xi_{\nu}
in light of the newly measured helium-4 mass fraction from EMPGs as reported in Ref.
Matsumoto et al. 2022
.
While
\xi_{\nu}=\xi_{\nu_{e,\mu,\tau}}
may be achieved in the Early Universe,
one should bear in mind that the conservative interpretation of our main finding in Fig.
1
applies only to

\xi_{\nu}=\xi_{\nu_{e}}
, i.e. the primordial electron-neutrino asymmetry probed by BBN via
\beta
equilibrium, Eq. (
5
).

Our computation of BBN abundances via the
PRyMordial

A.-K. Burns, T. M.P. Tait, and M. Valli
code, proceeds through 3 main steps:

1)

Solving for the thermal background;

2)

Computing neutron-proton conversion;

3)

Evaluating the final primordial abundances.

For
1)
we base our computation on the approach proposed in
Escudero 2019
and further developed in
Escudero Abenza 2020
(see also
Chu et al. 2022
). It consists in solving the Boltzmann equations for the electron-photon plasma and neutrinos assuming a thermal distribution for the species, including NLO QED corrections for the plasma
Bennett et al. 2020
as well as non-instantaneous decoupling effects for the neutrino sector
Escudero Abenza 2020
. For our purposes, it suffices to describe the neutrino sector by a common temperature
T_{\nu}
, yielding the SM prediction
N_{\rm eff}=3.045
, differing from the most refined prediction in
Akita and Yamaguchi 2020
;
Froustey et al. 2020
;
Bennett et al. 2021
only at the per-mille level, well within current and future observational sensitivity
Ade et al. 2019
;
Abazajian et al. 2022
;
Aiola et al. 2022
. A nonzero chemical potential for neutrinos would influence our analysis of the thermal background via Eq. (
2
). If full neutrino equilibration is achieved, we find a-posteriori a contribution to the radiation density that would be totally negligible. Nevertheless, in our BSM analysis we also account for the possibility of a nonzero lepton asymmetry
|\xi_{\nu_{\mu,\tau}}|\gg|\xi_{\nu_{e}}|
by varying
\Delta N_{\rm eff}
(a valid interpretation of
\Delta N_{\rm eff}
in the scenario where such a shift is mainly driven by a non-vanishing muon-tau chemical potential). Note that from our bound in Eq. (
4
), a muon-tau neutrino asymmetry can induce a maximal shift
\Delta N_{\rm eff}\sim 0.1
.

Moving to
2)
, we compute
n\leftrightarrow p
matrix elements beyond the Born approximation
Sirlin 1967
, namely including isospin-breaking contributions like finite-mass
Brown and Sawyer 2001
and QED
Czarnecki et al. 2004
;
Ivanov et al. 2017
corrections, as well as finite-temperature effects
Lopez et al. 1997
, following the implementation carried out in
Pitrou et al. 2018
. Most importantly, we evaluate weak-interaction rates integrating over nucleon thermal distributions with chemical potential
\mu_{\mathcal{Q}}\equiv\mu_{n}-\mu_{p}=-\mu_{\nu_{e}}\neq 0
.

Finally, regarding
3)
, we proceed evolving the abundances according to the network of thermonuclear reactions comprising the main processes listed in
Table 1
of Ref.
Iliadis and Coc 2020
(plus
{}^{3}\textrm{He}(p,\gamma)^{4}\textrm{He}
, taken from
Serpico et al. 2004
), yielding state-of-the-art predictions for
Y_{\rm P}
and
{\rm D/H}
. In particular, for the radiative neutron capture rate we adopt the MCMC result of Ref.
Ando et al. 2006
, while in the treatment of the other 10 key reactions we distinguish two approaches:

\circ

PRIMAT driven:
Nuclear rates are implemented according to the statistical determination of Refs
Descouvemont et al. 2004
;
Iliadis et al. 2016
;
Iñesta Gómez et al. 2017
;
de Souza et al. 2019a
;
de Souza et al. 2019b
;
Moscoso et al. 2021
, i.e. following theoretical ab-initio energy modeling tuned to datasets for which an estimate of systematic errors is available
Pitrou et al. 2018
;
Pitrou et al. 2021a
;
Pitrou et al. 2021b
.

\circ

NACRE II driven:
Nuclear rates are interpolated from the updated NACRE compilation
Xu et al. 2013
, comprising charged-particle-induced reactions
2
2

2

In particular, for the key reactions
\textrm{D}(d,n)^{3}\textrm{He}
and
\textrm{D}(d,p)^{3}\textrm{H}
we either interpolate rates and corresponding uncertainties from the NACRE II numerical database or from the outcome of the Bayesian analysis of Ref.
Iliadis et al. 2016
.
; for
\textrm{D}(p,\gamma)^{3}\textrm{He}
we use the LUNA result reported in
Mossa et al. 2020
; for
{}^{7}\textrm{Be}(n,p)^{7}\textrm{Li}
we adopt the baseline of Ref.
Fields et al. 2020
.

We perform a Bayesian analysis of Early Universe data constructing the cosmological test statistic:

\textrm{TS}_{\rm cosmo}\equiv-2(\log\mathcal{L}_{\rm CMB}+\log\mathcal{L}_{\rm BBN})\ ;

(6)

the CMB likelihood explicitly reads:

\log\mathcal{L}_{\rm CMB}=-\frac{1}{2}\Delta\vec{v}^{\,T}\,\mathcal{C}^{-1}_{\rm CMB}\,\Delta\vec{v}\ ,

(7)

with
\Delta\vec{v}\equiv\vec{v}^{\,\rm th}-\vec{v}
,
\vec{v}=({\rm Y_{P}},\Omega_{B}h^{2},N_{\rm eff})^{T}\
,
using mean and standard-deviation values from the TTTEEE + low-
\ell
+ BAO + lensing Planck run varying also
{\rm Y_{P}}
,
N_{\rm eff}

Aghanim et al. 2020
;
Aghanim et al. 2019a
and also retrieving correlations in
\mathcal{C}_{\rm CMB}
from
Aghanim et al. 2019b
. The BBN likelihood of our study corresponds to:

\log\mathcal{L}_{\rm BBN}=-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{X}\left(\frac{X^{\rm th}-X}{\sigma_{X}}\right)^{2}\ ,

(8)

where
X=\{{\rm Y_{P}},{\rm D/H}
}, and we use the measurements:
{\rm Y_{P}}=0.2370(34)

Matsumoto et al. 2022
,
{\rm D/H}=0.00002547(25)

Zyla et al. 2020
.

The parameters we infer are varied according to uniform priors:
-2\leq\Delta N_{\rm eff}\leq 2
,
\ -0.2\leq\xi_{\nu}\leq 0.2
,
1\leq(\eta_{B}\times 10^{10})\leq 10
(using
\eta_{B}\times 10^{10}\simeq 273.748\,\,\Omega_{B}h^{2}
). We marginalize over the neutron lifetime and the adopted nuclear uncertainties. From the PDG analysis
Zyla et al. 2020
we assign the Gaussian prior:
\tau_{n}=(879.4\pm 0.6
) s to the neutron lifetime. For the uncertainties in the nuclear rates, we assign log-normal distributions following the method detailed in Ref.
Coc et al. 2014
, varying a total of 12 additional nuisance parameters.

We perform an MCMC analysis via the
emcee

Foreman-Mackey et al. 2013
package, using 60 walkers with 2100 steps each, discarding the first 700 steps of each walker as burn-in.
From the best-fit values minimizing
\textrm{TS}_{\rm cosmo}
we also compute for each scenario the Information Criterion
Akaike 1974
;
Gelman et al. 2013

IC\equiv-2\log\hat{\mathcal{L}}_{\rm BBN}+2(k-1)
,
k
being the number of BSM parameters and accounting for the CMB information as an extra constraint in the fit. Then, we evaluate the
IC
difference with respect to the SM prediction of the primordial light abundances within a given approach:
\Delta IC\sim\mathcal{O}(1)
(
\sim\mathcal{O}(10)
) provides positive (strong) support in favor of NP beyond
\Lambda
CDM according to the canonical scales of evidence
Kass and Raftery 1995

V
Discussion and outlook

Our study based on the addition of the newly observed EMPGs
Matsumoto et al. 2022
to the original sample of ref.
Hsyu et al. 2020
suggests that today’s total lepton asymmetry, Eq. (
1
), is large,
\eta_{L}\gg\eta_{B}
, ranging from
\sim 10^{-2}
to
\sim 1/4
, depending on the details of the neutrino sector.

There are common factors that any successful explanation of a large
\eta_{L}
must share.
At temperatures above the scale of electroweak symmetry restoration, electroweak sphalerons equilibrate

B+L
such that the final total lepton and baryon asymmetries differ by a
{\cal O}(1)
factor
Khlebnikov and Shaposhnikov 1988
;
Harvey and Turner 1990
. Thus, for a difference of orders of magnitude between
\eta_{L}
and
\eta_{B}

to persist, it must either be generated after the sphalerons become inactive (in the SM, at the electroweak phase transition around temperatures of order 100 GeV) or
the individual flavor asymmetries must be distributed such that the net
L
is much smaller than the individual asymmetries
March-Russell et al. 1999
. The latter scenario would point to
flavor-dependent NP in the lepton sector, with possible interesting implications for the smallness of
\eta_{B}
as well, see for instance
Dreiner and Ross 1993
;
March-Russell et al. 1999
;

Trend to equilibrium and hypoelliptic regularity for the relativistic Fokker-Planck equation
Anton Arnold ∗, Gayrat Toshpulatov

Abstract
We consider the relativistic, spatially inhomogeneous Fokker-Planck equation with an external confining potential. We prove the exponential time decay of solutions towards the global equilibrium in weighted L 2 L^{2} and Sobolov spaces. Our result holds for a wide class of external potentials and the estimates on the rate of convergence are explicit and constructive. Moreover, we prove that the associated semigroup of the equation has hypoelliptic regularizing properties and we obtain explicit rates on this regularization. The technique is based on the construction of suitable Lyapunov functionals.



Keywords:
Kinetic theory, Fokker-Planck equation, relativistic diffusion, confinement potential, degenerate diffusion, long-time
behavior, convergence to equilibrium, hypocoercivity, hypoellictic regularity, Lyapunov functional.

Introduction

In this paper, we study the long-time behavior of the relativistic, spatially inhomogeneous Fokker-Planck equation
[
24
,
3
]

\begin{cases}\partial_{t}f+\displaystyle\frac{p}{m\sqrt{1+\frac{|p|^{2}}{m^{2}c^{2}}}}\cdot\nabla_{x}f-q\nabla_{x}V(x)\cdot\nabla_{p}f=\text{div}_{p}(\sigma D(p)\nabla_{p}f+\nu pf),\,\,\,\,\,\,x,\,p\in\mathbb{R}^{d},\,\,t>0\\
f_{|t=0}=f_{0}\end{cases},

(1)

for any
d\in\mathbb{N}.
This kinetic model describes the time evolution of a system with a large number of particles (e.g. in a plasma) undergoing diffusion and friction. In (
1
),
x
denotes position,
p
the relativistic momentum, and
\displaystyle\frac{p}{m\sqrt{1+\frac{|p|^{2}}{m^{2}c^{2}}}}
the velocity. The unknown
f=f(t,x,p)\geq 0
represents the evolution of the phase space probability density of particles. The left hand side is the transport operator with force field
-\nabla_{x}V(x)
, while the right
hand side describes the diffusion of particles and the interaction with the environment. The (positive) physical constants denoted by
m,

c,

q,

\sigma,
and
\nu
are, respectively, the particle rest mass, the vacuum speed of light, the particle charge, diffusion, and friction coefficients.

D(p)
is the relativistic diffusion matrix given by

D=\frac{I+\frac{p\otimes p}{m^{2}c^{2}}}{\sqrt{1+\frac{|p|^{2}}{m^{2}c^{2}}}}\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times d},

(2)

where
I\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}
is the identity matrix and
\otimes
denotes the Kronecker product.
For a derivation of (
1
) and the matrix (
2
) from relativistic Langevin dynamics, we refer to
[
24
]
. Moreover, the same model is obtained in
[
3
, §2]
under the postulate that it should be Lorentz invariant in the absence of friction, i.e. for
\nu=0
(just like the non-relativistic analog is Galilean invariant for
\nu=0
):
The resulting anisotropic diffusion arises as the Laplace-Beltrami operator over the relativistic hyperboloid with respect to the Minkowski metric.
Also, this model is compatible with the finite propagation speed of particles in relativity.
Finally, we remark that there are several different “relativistic Fokker-Planck equations” in the literature, for a review see
[
23
]
.

Equation (
1
) has several properties following standard physical considerations.
Whenever
f(t,x,p)
is a (well-behaved) solution of (
1
),
one has
global conservation of mass

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2d}}f(t,x,p)dxdp=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2d}}f_{0}(x,p)dxdp,\,\,\,\,\,\,\forall t\geq 0.

(3)

Therefore, without loss of generality, we shall assume
f_{0}\geq 0
and
\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2d}}f_{0}(x,p)dxdp=1.

If
V
grows fast enough,
(
1
) has a unique normalized
steady state
or
global equilibrium

[
3
, Section 3.4]
given by

f_{\infty}(x,p)=\rho_{\infty}(x)M(p),

(4)

where

\rho_{\infty}(x)\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\displaystyle\frac{e^{-\frac{mq\nu}{\sigma}V(x)}}{\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}e^{-\frac{qm\nu}{\sigma}V(x^{\prime})}dx^{\prime}},\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\displaystyle M(p)\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\frac{e^{-\frac{mc\nu}{\sigma}\sqrt{m^{2}c^{2}+|p|^{2}}}}{\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}e^{-\frac{mc\nu}{\sigma}\sqrt{m^{2}c^{2}+|p^{\prime}|^{2}}}dp^{\prime}}.

(5)

M(p)
is called the Maxwell-Jüttner
distribution, for its normalizing integral see
[
36
]
.

(
1
) is
dissipative
in the sense that the relative entropy or free energy functional decreases: let
H
be a functional defined on the space of probability densities by

f\mapsto H[f]\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2d}}f\ln{\frac{f}{f_{\infty}}}dxdp

(
f
is not necessarily the solution). We note that
H[f_{\infty}]=0
and
\displaystyle H[f]\geq\frac{1}{2}||f-f_{\infty}||^{2}_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{2d})}
by the Csiszár-Kullback-Pinsker inequality
[
19
]
. Hence, the minimum of
H
is zero and it is attained at
f_{\infty}.
If
f=f(t,x,p)
is a smooth solution of (
1
), we have (see
[
3
, Section 3.3]
)

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}H[f(t)]\leq 0.

This decay of the functional
H
is a version of Boltzmann’s
H-
theorem stated for the Boltzmann equation
[
16
,
34
]
.

On the basis of the decay of the functional
H,
one may conjecture that
H[f(t)]
decreases to its minimum (which is zero) as
t\to\infty.
Since this minimum is obtained at
f_{\infty},
one can expect that
f(t)
converges to the equilibrium distribution
f_{\infty}
as
t\to\infty.

We shall therefore tackle the interesting problem to prove (or disprove) that solutions of (
1
) converge towards this
equilibrium as
t\to\infty
and to estimate the convergence rate with constructive bounds. Such explicit and constructive estimates are essential for applications in physics (equilibration process, numerical simulations).

Equation (
1
) was introduced in
[
24
, Eq.(47)]
and
[
3
, Eq.(8)]
as a relativistic generalization of the classical kinetic Fokker-Planck equation
[
17
,
18
,
33
]

\begin{cases}\partial_{t}f+\displaystyle\frac{p}{m}\cdot\nabla_{x}f-q\nabla_{x}V\cdot\nabla_{p}f=\text{div}_{p}(\sigma\nabla_{p}f+\nu pf),\,\,\,\,\,\,x,\,p\in\mathbb{R}^{d},\,\,t>0\\
f_{|t=0}=f_{0}\end{cases}.

(6)

This classical equation can be obtained from (
1
) by formally taking the Newtonian limit
c\to\infty.
In
[
4
]
, this formal limit was justified in the sense that solutions of (
1
) converge to solutions of (
6
) in
L^{1}
as
c\to\infty.

Equation (
6
) is inconsistent with relativistic mechanics because it has infinite speed of propagation: if the particles are initially in a compact region (i.e.
f_{0}(x,p)
has compact support with respect to
x
and
p
), then, after any short time
t>0,
we can find particles everywhere with non-zero probability (i.e.
f(t,x,p)>0
), see
[
35
, Appendix A.22]
. This property contradicts the law of special relativity that particles can not move faster than light. By contrast, Equation (
1
) is compatible with this physical law as it exhibits finite speed of propagation w.r.t. the
x
variable
[
3
, Section 3.2]
. Note, however, that the degenerate parabolicity of (
1
)
does entail infinite speed of propagation with respect to the
p
variable.

The classical equation (
6
) has been studied comprehensively: well-posedness and hypoelliptic regularity were obtained in
[
32
,
27
,
35
]
. The long-time behavior of (
6
) was studied in
[
28
]
for fast growing potentials. By using hypocoercive methods, Villani proved exponential convergence results in
[
20
,
35
]
. This result was extended in
[
12
]
for potentials with singularities. In
[
22
]
, Dolbeault, Mouhot, and Schmeiser developed a method to
obtain exponential decay in
L^{2}
for a large class of linear kinetic equations, and, as an application, an exponential decay in
L^{2}
was proven for (
6
). Their method
was also used to study the long-time behavior of (
6
) when the potential
V
is zero or grows slowly as

|x|\to\infty,
see
[
13
,
14
]
. Based on a probabilistic coupling method, Eberle, Guillin, and Zimmer
[
25
]

obtained an exponential decay result in Wasserstein distance. We also refer the recent work
[
9
]
where sharper exponential rates were obtained using a modified entropy method.

Concerning the relativistic equation (
1
), there are only few studies: global existence and uniqueness were proven in
[
3
]
.
The long-time behavior of spatially homogeneous solutions of (
1
) was studied
[
5
,
4
]
, where the authors used logarithmic Sobolev inequalities and entropy methods
[
6
,
31
]
. When (
1
) is supplemented with periodic boundary conditions (i.e.
x\in\mathbb{T}^{d}
) and
V=0,
exponential decay of solutions to the steady state was proven in
[
15
]
by using the hypocoercive method developed by Villani
[
35
]
.

In this paper,
we shall improve these previous results when there is a non-zero potential

V.
For the full system (
1
) with a non-zero potential
V
we
shall prove the exponential convergence
f(t)\to f_{\infty}
as
t\to\infty
for a wide class of
potentials
V.
Our convergence rates are explicit and constructive. We show that, although the equation is only degenerate parabolic, the equation has instantaneous regularizing
properties which is called
hypoellipticity

[
29
]
. We provide explicit rates on this regularization.
We believe our results are the first convergence and regularity results for (
1
) with a non-zero potentials
V.

The organization of this paper is as follows. In Section
2
, we define the assumptions on the
potential and state the main results. Sections
3
and
4
are devoted to prove the convergence
f(t)\to f_{\infty}
as
t\to\infty
in the weighted
L^{2}
and Sobolev spaces. We study hypoelliptic regularity properties of the equation in Section
5
and list several technical results in the Appendix.
In Section
6
we show that the decay rate for the homogeneous relativistic Fokker-Planck equation is uniform in the limit as
c\to\infty
, and we conclude in Section
7
.

2
Setting and main result

We use the notations

V_{0}(x)\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\sqrt{1+|\nabla_{x}V(x)|^{2}}\,\,\,\text{ and }\,\,\,p_{0}(p)\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\sqrt{1+|p|^{2}}.

For simplicity, we set all physical constants to unity:
m=c=q=\sigma=\nu=1.
Therefore, we shall consider the normalized equation

\begin{cases}\partial_{t}f+\displaystyle\frac{p}{p_{0}}\cdot\nabla_{x}f-\nabla_{x}V(x)\cdot\nabla_{p}f=\text{div}_{p}(D(p)\nabla_{p}f+pf),\,\,\,\,\,\,x,\,p\in\mathbb{R}^{d},\,\,t>0\\
f_{|t=0}=f_{0}\end{cases}

(7)

with
\displaystyle D(p)=\frac{I+p\otimes p}{p_{0}}.

We define the weighted spaces
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{x}^{d},\rho_{\infty}),

L^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{p}^{d},M),
and
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2d},f_{\infty})
as the Lebesgue spaces associated, respectively, to the norms

||g||_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{x}^{d},\rho_{\infty})}\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\sqrt{\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}g^{2}\rho_{\infty}dx},\,\,\,||g||_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{p}^{d},M)}\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\sqrt{\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}g^{2}Mdp},

and

||g||_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2d},f_{\infty})}\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\sqrt{\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2d}}g^{2}f_{\infty}dxdp}.

We note that
\displaystyle M(p)=\frac{e^{-\sqrt{1+|p|^{2}}}}{\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}e^{-\sqrt{1+|p^{\prime}|^{2}}}dp^{\prime}}
gives rise to the following Poincaré inequality
[
4
, Theorem 3]
: there is a positive constant
\kappa_{1}
such that

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}h^{2}Mdp-\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}hMdp\right)^{2}\leq\frac{1}{\kappa_{1}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\nabla^{T}_{p}hD\nabla_{p}hMdp

(8)

holds for all
h\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{p}^{d},M)
with
\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\nabla^{T}_{p}hD\nabla_{p}hMdp<\infty.

We shall assume that
\displaystyle\rho_{\infty}(x)=\frac{e^{-V(x)}}{\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}e^{-V(x^{\prime})}dx^{\prime}}
also gives rise to a Poincaré inequality. Also we shall assume some growth conditions on
V:

Assumption 2.1
.

i)

Let
V\in C^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
be such that
e^{-V}\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d}),
and there exists a constant
\kappa_{2}>0
such that the Poincaré inequality

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}h^{2}\rho_{\infty}dx-\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}h\rho_{\infty}dx\right)^{2}\leq\frac{1}{\kappa_{2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|\nabla_{x}h|^{2}\rho_{\infty}dx\,\,

(9)

holds for all
h\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{x}^{d},\rho_{\infty})
with
|\nabla_{x}h|\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{x}^{d},\rho_{\infty}).

ii)

There exist constants
c_{1}>0,

c_{2}\in[0,1),
and
c_{3}>0
such that

\Delta_{x}V(x)\leq c_{1}+\frac{c_{2}}{2}|\nabla_{x}V(x)|^{2},\,\,\,\,\,\,\left|\left|\frac{\partial^{2}V(x)}{\partial x^{2}}\right|\right|_{F}\leq c_{3}(1+|\nabla_{x}V(x)|),\,\,\,\,\forall\,x\in\mathbb{R}^{d},

(10)

where
\displaystyle\left|\left|\frac{\partial^{2}V(x)}{\partial x^{2}}\right|\right|_{F}\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\sqrt{\sum_{i,j=1}^{d}(\partial_{x_{i}x_{j}}V(x))^{2}}
is the Frobenius norm of
\displaystyle\frac{\partial^{2}V(x)}{\partial x^{2}}.

There are a lot of studies and sufficient conditions implying the Poincaré inequality (
9
). For example, if
V
is uniformly convex (Bakry-Emery criterion) or if

\liminf_{|x|\to\infty}\left(a|\nabla V(x)|^{2}-\Delta V(x)\right)>0

for some
a\in(0,1),
then the Poincaré inequality (
9
) holds. For more information see
[
10
]
,
[
11
, Chapter 4]
.
We note that the potentials of the form

V(x)=r|x|^{2k}+\tilde{V}(x),

where
r>0,

k>1
and
\tilde{V}\colon\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}
is a polynomial of degree
j<2k,
satisfy our assumptions.

We now state our first result:

Theorem 2.2

(
Exponential decay in
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2d},f_{\infty})
)
.

Let
\frac{f_{0}}{f_{\infty}}\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2d},f_{\infty})
and
V
satisfy Assumption
2.1
. Then there are explicitly computable constants
C_{1}>0
and
\lambda>0
(independent of
f_{0}
) such that

\left|\left|\frac{f(t)-f_{\infty}}{f_{\infty}}\right|\right|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2d},f_{\infty})}\leq C_{1}e^{-\lambda t}\left|\left|\frac{f_{0}-f_{\infty}}{f_{\infty}}\right|\right|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2d},f_{\infty})}

(11)

holds for all
t\geq 0.

Theorem
2.2
shows that the solution
\frac{f(t)-f_{\infty}}{f_{\infty}}
converges exponentially to zero in
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2d},f_{\infty})
as
t\to\infty.
Next we want to obtain this convergence in a more regular space. Hence,
we define the following weighted Sobolev space

\mathscr{H}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{2d},f_{\infty})
associated to the norm

\displaystyle||h||^{2}_{\mathscr{H}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{2d},f_{\infty})}

\displaystyle\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2d}}h^{2}f_{\infty}dxdp+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2d}}\frac{1}{V_{0}^{3}(x)p_{0}^{3}}\nabla^{T}_{x}h\left(I-\frac{p\otimes p}{p_{0}^{2}}\right)\nabla_{x}hf_{\infty}dxdp

\displaystyle\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2d}}\frac{1}{V_{0}(x)p_{0}}\nabla^{T}_{p}h(I+p\otimes p)\nabla_{p}hf_{\infty}dxdp

\displaystyle\,\,=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2d}}h^{2}f_{\infty}dxdp+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2d}}\frac{1}{V_{0}^{3}(x)p_{0}^{3}}\left(|\nabla_{x}h|^{2}-\frac{|p\cdot\nabla_{x}h|^{2}}{p_{0}^{2}}\right)f_{\infty}dxdp

\displaystyle\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2d}}\frac{1}{V_{0}(x)p_{0}}(|\nabla_{p}h|^{2}+|p\cdot\nabla_{p}h|^{2})f_{\infty}dxdp.

(12)

This norm is well-defined since the matrices
\displaystyle\frac{1}{V_{0}^{3}(x)p_{0}^{3}}\left(I-\frac{p\otimes p}{p_{0}^{2}}\right)
and
\displaystyle\frac{1}{V_{0}(x)p_{0}}(I+p\otimes p)
are positive definite for all
x,\,p\in\mathbb{R}^{d}.
Clearly,
\mathscr{H}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{2d},f_{\infty})\subset L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2d},f_{\infty}).

To motivate the above definition better, we note that
I-\frac{p\otimes p}{p_{0}^{2}}=\frac{1}{p_{0}}D(p)^{-1}
.

Our second result shows that the solution
\frac{f(t)-f_{\infty}}{f_{\infty}}
converges exponentially to zero in
\mathscr{H}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{2d},f_{\infty})
as
t\to\infty:

Theorem 2.3

(
Exponential decay in
\mathscr{H}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{2d},f_{\infty})
)
.

Let
\frac{f_{0}}{f_{\infty}}\in\mathscr{H}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{2d},f_{\infty})
and
V
satisfy Assumption
2.1
.
Then there are constants
C_{2}>0
and
\Lambda>0
(independent of
f_{0}
) such that

\left|\left|\frac{f(t)-f_{\infty}}{f_{\infty}}\right|\right|_{\mathscr{H}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{2d},f_{\infty})}\leq C_{2}e^{-\Lambda t}\left|\left|\frac{f_{0}-f_{\infty}}{f_{\infty}}\right|\right|_{\mathscr{H}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{2d},f_{\infty})}

holds for all
t\geq 0.

Our next result is about the estimates on the hypoelliptic regularization:

Theorem 2.4

(
Hypoelliptic regularity from
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2d},f_{\infty})
to
\mathscr{H}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{2d},f_{\infty})
)
.

Assume
\frac{f_{0}}{f_{\infty}}\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2d},f_{\infty})
and that there exists a constant
c_{3}>0
such that

\left|\left|\frac{\partial^{2}V(x)}{\partial x^{2}}\right|\right|_{F}\leq c_{3}(1+|\nabla_{x}V(x)|),\,\,\,\,\forall\,x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}.

Then, for any
t_{0}>0,
there are explicitly computable constants
C_{3}>0
and
C_{4}>0
(independent of
f_{0}
) such that

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2d}}\frac{1}{V_{0}^{3}(x)p_{0}^{3}}\nabla^{T}_{x}\left(\frac{f(t)}{f_{\infty}}\right)\left(I-\frac{p\otimes p}{p_{0}^{2}}\right)\nabla_{x}\left(\frac{f(t)}{f_{\infty}}\right)f_{\infty}dxdp\leq\frac{C_{3}}{t^{3}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2d}}\left(\frac{f_{0}-f_{\infty}}{f_{\infty}}\right)^{2}f_{\infty}dxdp

(13)

and

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2d}}\frac{1}{V_{0}(x)p_{0}}\nabla^{T}_{p}\left(\frac{f(t)}{f_{\infty}}\right)\left(I+p\otimes p\right)\nabla_{p}\left(\frac{f(t)}{f_{\infty}}\right)f_{\infty}dxdp\leq\frac{C_{4}}{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2d}}\left(\frac{f_{0}-f_{\infty}}{f_{\infty}}\right)^{2}f_{\infty}dxdp

(14)

hold for all
t\in(0,t_{0}].
In particular,

\left|\left|\frac{f(t)-f_{\infty}}{f_{\infty}}\right|\right|_{\mathscr{H}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{2d},f_{\infty})}\leq\frac{(C_{3}+C_{4}t_{0}^{2})^{1/2}}{t^{3/2}}\left|\left|\frac{f_{0}-f_{\infty}}{f_{\infty}}\right|\right|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2d},f_{\infty})}

(15)

holds for all
t\in(0,t_{0}].

Theorem
2.4
shows that, for any initial data
\frac{f_{0}}{f_{\infty}}\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2d},f_{\infty}),
the solution
\frac{f(t)}{f_{\infty}}\in\mathscr{H}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{2d},f_{\infty})
for any time
t>0.
Compared to Theorem
2.2
and Theorem
2.3
, we do not require the validity of a Poincaré inequality in Theorem
2.4
. Also note that the regularization rates for the
x
derivative and the
p
derivative are not the same: the regularization rate in the

derivative is faster, as it also is for the kinetic Fokker-Planck equation
[
27
,
35
,
9
]
. This difference is expected since (
7
) can be considered as a transport equation with respect to the
x
variable and as a parabolic equation with respect to the

variable.

In Theorem
2.3
we assumed that the initial data
f_{0}/f_{\infty}
is in
\mathscr{H}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{2d},f_{\infty}).
If we use the regularity estimates
from Theorem
2.4
, this condition can be relaxed:

Corollary 2.5
.

Assume
\frac{f_{0}}{f_{\infty}}\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2d},f_{\infty})
and
V
satisfies Assumption
2.1
. Then, for any
t_{0}>0,
there is an explicitly computable constant
C_{5}>0
(independent of
f_{0}
) such that

\left|\left|\frac{f(t)-f_{\infty}}{f_{\infty}}\right|\right|_{\mathscr{H}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{2d},f_{\infty})}\leq C_{5}e^{-\Lambda t}\left|\left|\frac{f_{0}-f_{\infty}}{f_{\infty}}\right|\right|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2d},f_{\infty})}

holds for all
t\geq t_{0}>0,
where
\Lambda>0
is the constant appearing in Theorem
2.3
.

Remark 2.6
.

If one considers (
7
) on a torus as done in
[
15
]
, our results also hold in this setting since the method which we use can be adapted without difficulty.

3
Exponential convergence in
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2d},f_{\infty})

3.1
The first Lyapunov functional

Let us consider the relativistic, spatially homogeneous Fokker-Planck equation

\begin{cases}\partial_{t}\varrho=\text{div}_{p}(D(p)\nabla_{p}\varrho+p\varrho),\,\,\,\,\,p\in\mathbb{R}^{d},\,\,\,\,t>0,\\
\varrho_{|t=0}=\varrho_{0},\end{cases}

(16)

with
\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\varrho dp=1.

This equation is a special case of (
7
) when the problem is independent of
x
and
V=0.
The unique normalized global equilibrium for this equation is
\displaystyle M(p)=\frac{e^{-\sqrt{1+|p|^{2}}}}{\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}e^{-\sqrt{1+|p^{\prime}|^{2}}}dp^{\prime}}.
The convergence

\varrho(t)\to M\,\,\,\text{ as }\,\,t\to\infty

can be easily proven using the Poincaré inequality (
8
): Noting that the right hand side of (
16
) equals
\displaystyle\text{div}_{p}\left(MD\nabla_{p}\left(\frac{\varrho}{M}\right)\right)
we obtain

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\left|\left|\frac{\varrho(t)-M}{M}\right|\right|^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d},M)}

\displaystyle=-2\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\nabla^{T}_{p}\left(\frac{\varrho(t)}{M}\right)D\nabla_{p}\left(\frac{\varrho(t)}{M}\right)Mdp

\displaystyle\leq-2\kappa_{1}\left|\left|\frac{\varrho(t)-M}{M}\right|\right|^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d},M)},\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\forall\,t>0.

By Grönwall’s lemma we obtain the exponential decay

\left|\left|\frac{\varrho(t)-M}{M}\right|\right|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d},M)}\leq e^{-\kappa_{1}t}\left|\left|\frac{\varrho_{0}-M}{M}\right|\right|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d},M)},\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\forall\,t>0.

(17)

On the contrary, we do not obtain easily such exponential decay for the relativistic, spatially inhomogeneous Fokker-Planck equation (
7
). As the Fokker-Planck operator on the right hand side of (
7
) acts only on the variable
p,
we only have

\frac{d}{dt}\left|\left|\frac{f(t)-f_{\infty}}{f_{\infty}}\right|\right|^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2d},f_{\infty})}=-2\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2d}}\nabla^{T}_{p}\left(\frac{f(t)}{f_{\infty}}\right)D\nabla_{p}\left(\frac{f(t)}{M}\right)f_{\infty}dxdp\leq 0.

(18)

The integral on the right hand side only gives information on the
p-
derivative and it is lacking information on the
x-
derivatives. Hence, in general, the integral on the right hand side of (
18
) is not bigger than
\displaystyle 2\lambda\left|\left|\frac{f(t)-f_{\infty}}{f_{\infty}}\right|\right|^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2d},f_{\infty})}

for some
\lambda>0.

The idea to overcome this difficulty is to construct an appropriate Lyapunov functional which is equivalent to the
L^{2}-
norm and satisfies a Grönwall type differential inequality under the evolution of the solution
f(t).

A method in Hilbert spaces was introduced by Dolbeault, Mouhot, and Schmeiser in
[
21
,
22
]
for proving exponential stability for a large class of linear kinetic models confined by an external potential. We will apply this method to (
7
) to obtain our results. In the following we explain this method for linear kinetic equations of the form

\partial_{t}f+\mathrm{T}f=\mathrm{L}f,\,\,\,t>0

(19)

in a Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}
with initial data
f_{|t=0}=f_{0}\in\mathcal{H}.
Here,
\mathrm{T}
and
\mathrm{L}
are closed linear operators such that
\mathrm{L}-\mathrm{T}
generates the strongly continuous semigroup
e^{(\mathrm{L}-\mathrm{T})t}
on
\mathcal{H}.
Let
\mathrm{I}
be the identity operator,
\Pi
be the orthogonal projection on the null space
\mathcal{N}(\mathrm{L})
of
\mathrm{L}.
The domains of
\mathrm{T}
and
\mathrm{L}
are denoted by
\mathcal{D}(\mathrm{T})
and
\mathcal{D}(\mathrm{L}),
respectively. We define the operator

\mathrm{A}f\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}(\mathrm{I}+(\mathrm{T}\Pi)^{*}\mathrm{T}\Pi)^{-1}(\mathrm{T}\Pi)^{*}f

(20)

and the functional

\mathrm{H}_{\delta}[f]\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\frac{1}{2}||f||^{2}+\delta\langle\mathrm{A}f,f\rangle,\,\,\,\,\delta>0,

(21)

where
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
denotes the scalar product in
\mathcal{H},
and
||\cdot||
denotes the norm on
\mathcal{H}
associated with the scalar product.
We assume the following conditions are satisfied:

•

(
microscopic coercivity
)
\mathrm{L}
is symmetric and there exists
\lambda_{m}>0
such that

-\langle\mathrm{L}f,f\rangle\geq\lambda_{m}||(\mathrm{I}-\Pi)f||^{2}\,\,\,\,\text{ for all }\,\,f\in\mathcal{D}(\mathrm{L}).

(22)

•

(
macroscopic coercivity
)
\mathrm{T}
is skew symmetric and there exists
\lambda_{M}>0
such that

||\mathrm{T}\Pi f||^{2}\geq\lambda_{M}||\Pi f||^{2}\,\,\,\,\text{ for all }\,\,f\in\mathcal{H}\,\,\text{ with }\,\,\Pi f\in\mathcal{D}(\mathrm{T}).

(23)

•

\Pi\mathrm{T}\Pi=0.

(24)

•

(
boundedness of auxiliary operators
) The operators
\mathrm{A}\mathrm{T}(\mathrm{I}-\Pi)
and
\mathrm{A}\mathrm{L}
are bounded, and there exists a constant
C_{M}>0
such that, for all
f\in\mathcal{H},

||\mathrm{A}\mathrm{T}(\mathrm{I}-\Pi)f||+||\mathrm{A}\mathrm{L}f||\leq C_{M}||(\mathrm{I}-\Pi)f||.

(25)

We define

\delta_{0}\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\min\left\{2,\lambda_{m},\frac{4\lambda_{m}\lambda_{M}}{4\lambda_{M}+C^{2}_{M}(1+\lambda_{M})}\right\}.

(26)

Under the validity of these conditions and for
\delta\in(0,\delta_{0}),
one can show that
\mathrm{H}_{\delta}
is a Lypunov functional for (
19
) and it decays exponentially:

Theorem 3.1

(
[
22
, Theorem 2]
)
.

Assume (
22
)-(
25
) are satisfied and
\delta\in(0,\delta_{0}).
Then,

i)

\mathrm{H}_{\delta}
and
||\cdot||^{2}
are equivalent, more precisely,

\frac{2-\delta}{4}||f||^{2}\leq\mathrm{H}_{\delta}[f]\leq\frac{2+\delta}{4}||f||^{2}\,\,\,\,\text{ for all }f\in\mathcal{H}.

ii)

There exists a positive constant
\lambda,
which is computable in terms of
\lambda_{m},

\lambda_{M},
and
C_{M},
such that, for any initial data
f_{0}\in\mathcal{H},

\frac{d}{dt}\mathrm{H}_{\delta}[e^{(\mathrm{L}-\mathrm{T})t}f_{0}]\leq-2\lambda\mathrm{H}_{\delta}[e^{(\mathrm{L}-\mathrm{T})t}f_{0}],\,\,\,\,t>0.

Consequently, we have

||e^{(\mathrm{L}-\mathrm{T})t}f_{0}||\leq\sqrt{\frac{2+\delta}{2-\delta}}e^{-\lambda t}||f_{0}||\,\,\,\,\text{ for all }t\geq 0.

(27)

This method has been successfully applied to study the long-time behavior of various linear kinetic models, see
[
22
,
13
,
26
,
2
]
. In particular, in
[
22
, Theorem 10]
, the exponential convergence
f(t)\to f_{\infty}
in
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2d},f_{\infty})
as
t\to\infty
was proven for the classical kinetic Fokker-Planck equation (
6
).

3.2
Proof of Theorem
2.2

Let
\displaystyle h\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\frac{f-f_{\infty}}{f_{\infty}}.
Then (
7
) can be written as

\begin{cases}\partial_{t}h+\displaystyle\frac{p}{p_{0}}\cdot\nabla_{x}h-\nabla_{x}V\cdot\nabla_{p}h=\frac{1}{f_{\infty}}\text{div}_{p}(D\nabla_{p}hf_{\infty})\\
\displaystyle h_{|t=0}=\frac{f_{0}-f_{\infty}}{f_{\infty}}.\end{cases}

(28)

We shall apply Theorem
3.1
to (
28
). To end we first define an appropriate Hilbert space,

\mathcal{H}\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\left\{h\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2d},f_{\infty}):\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2d}}hf_{\infty}dxdp=0\right\}

with the scalar product

\langle h,g\rangle\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2d}}{h_{1}h_{2}}{f_{\infty}}dxdp
and the norm
||h||_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2d},f_{\infty})}=\sqrt{\langle h,h\rangle}.

We note that if
h(t)
is the solution of (

), then the conservation of mass (
3
) shows
h(t)\in\mathcal{H}
for all

We can present (

) in the form of (

) with

\mathrm{T}h\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\frac{p}{p_{0}}\cdot\nabla_{x}h-\nabla_{x}V\cdot\nabla_{p}h

(29)

and

\mathrm{L}h\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\frac{1}{f_{\infty}}\text{div}_{p}(D\nabla_{p}hf_{\infty}).

(30)

\mathrm{T}
and
\mathrm{L}
can be defined in the space of smooth functions with compact support. These operators can be extended using the Friederichs extension, but we omit details concerning domain issues and extensions as we need only properties that apply to solutions of the
evolution problem (
28
).

We define

\Pi h=\Pi h(x)\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}h(x,p^{\prime})M(p^{\prime})dp^{\prime},\,\,\,\,\,\,\,h\in\mathcal{H}.

(31)

It is easy to check that
\Pi
is a symmetric operator in
\mathcal{H}
and
\Pi\circ\Pi=\Pi.

In the following proposition we show that the operators defined in (
29
)-(

) satisfy the conditions (
22
)-(
24
) in
\mathcal{H}:

Proposition 3.2
.

Assume that Assumption
2.1
holds. Then we have

i)

\mathrm{T}
and
\mathrm{L}
are, respectively, skew-symmetric and symmetric operators in
\mathcal{H}.

ii)

\Pi
is the orthogonal projection on the null space
\mathcal{N}(\mathrm{L})
of
\mathrm{L}.

Microscopic coercivity (
22
) holds with
\lambda_{m}=\kappa_{1},
where
\kappa_{1}
is the constant appearing in the Poincaré inequality (
8
).

iii)

Macroscopic coercivity (
23
) holds with
\lambda_{M}=\kappa_{2}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\frac{1}{(1+|p|^{2})^{3/2}}Mdp\right)^{-1},
where
\kappa_{2}
is the constant in the Poincaré inequality (
9
).

iv)

\Pi\mathrm{T}\Pi=0.

Proof.

i)
Let
h,\,g\in\mathcal{H}
be smooth functions with compact support. The equations

\nabla_{x}f_{\infty}=-\nabla_{x}Vf_{\infty},\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\nabla_{p}f_{\infty}=-\frac{p}{p_{0}}f_{\infty},

and integration by parts yield

\displaystyle\langle\mathrm{T}h,g\rangle

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2d}}\left(\frac{p}{p_{0}}\cdot\nabla_{x}h-\nabla_{x}V\cdot\nabla_{p}h\right)gf_{\infty}dxdp

\displaystyle=-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2d}}\left(\frac{p}{p_{0}}\cdot\nabla_{x}g-\nabla_{x}V\cdot\nabla_{p}g\right)hf_{\infty}dxdp

\displaystyle=-\langle h,\mathrm{T}g\rangle.

Then, integrating by parts we show that
\mathrm{L}
is symmetric:

\langle\mathrm{L}h,g\rangle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2d}}\text{div}_{p}(D\nabla_{p}hf_{\infty})gdxdp=-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2d}}\nabla^{T}_{p}hD\nabla_{p}gf_{\infty}dxdp=\langle h,\mathrm{L}g\rangle.

(32)

ii)

As
D=D(p)
is positive definite for all
p\in\mathbb{R}^{d},
(
32
) implies

\langle\mathrm{L}h,h\rangle=-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2d}}\nabla^{T}_{p}hD\nabla_{p}hf_{\infty}dxdp\leq 0.

This shows that
\mathrm{L}h
vanishes if
h
is constant with respect to
p,
in particular
\mathrm{L}\Pi h=0.

Moreover, the Poincaré inequality (
8
) shows

\displaystyle-\langle\mathrm{L}h,h\rangle

\displaystyle\geq\kappa_{1}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}h^{2}Mdp-\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}hMdp\right)^{2}\right)\rho_{\infty}dx

\displaystyle=\kappa_{1}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2d}}(h-\Pi h)^{2}f_{\infty}dxdp

\displaystyle=\kappa_{1}||(\mathrm{I}-\Pi)h||^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2d},f_{\infty})}.

This justifies that
\Pi
is the orthogonal projection on the null space
\mathcal{N}(\mathrm{L})
of
\mathrm{L}.

iii)
Using

\displaystyle\mathrm{T}\Pi h=\frac{p}{p_{0}}\cdot\nabla_{x}\Pi h
we compute

\displaystyle||\mathrm{T}\Pi h||^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2d},f_{\infty})}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i,j=1}^{d}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2d}}\frac{p_{i}p_{j}}{p^{2}_{0}}\partial_{x_{i}}\Pi h\,\partial_{x_{j}}\Pi hf_{\infty}dxdp

\displaystyle=-\sum_{i,j=1}^{d}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2d}}\partial_{p_{i}}{p_{0}}\partial_{x_{i}}\Pi h\,\partial_{x_{j}}\Pi h\partial_{p_{j}}f_{\infty}dxdp

\displaystyle=\sum_{i,j=1}^{d}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2d}}\partial^{2}_{p_{i}p_{j}}{p_{0}}\partial_{x_{i}}\Pi h\,\partial_{x_{j}}\Pi hf_{\infty}dxdp

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2d}}\nabla^{T}_{x}\Pi h\frac{\partial^{2}p_{0}}{\partial p^{2}}\nabla_{x}\Pi hf_{\infty}dxdp.

(33)

We have

\frac{\partial^{2}p_{0}}{\partial p^{2}}=\frac{1}{p_{0}}\left(I-\frac{p\otimes p}{p_{0}^{2}}\right)\geq\frac{1}{p_{0}^{3}}I,

(34)

where
I\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}
is the identity matrix.
(
33
) and (
34
) yield

||\mathrm{T}\Pi h||^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2d},f_{\infty})}\geq\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\frac{1}{p_{0}^{3}}Mdp\right)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|\nabla_{x}\Pi h|^{2}\rho_{\infty}dx.

Then, the Poincaré inequality (
9
) provides the claimed result.

iv)
Using
\displaystyle\nabla_{p}M=-\frac{p}{p_{0}}M
and integrating by parts with respect to
p
we obtain

\Pi\mathrm{T}\Pi h=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\frac{p}{p_{0}}\cdot\nabla_{x}\Pi hMdp=-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\nabla_{x}\Pi h\cdot\nabla_{p}Mdp=0.

∎

Next we define as in (
20
):

\mathrm{A}f\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}(\mathrm{I}+(\mathrm{T}\Pi)^{*}\mathrm{T}\Pi)^{-1}(\mathrm{T}\Pi)^{*}f,
which is a bounded operator on
\mathcal{H},
due to
[
22
, Lemma 1]
.
We now show that (

) holds.

Lemma 3.3
.

Assume Assumption
2.1
holds. Then, the operators
\mathrm{A}\mathrm{T}(\mathrm{I}-\Pi)
and
\mathrm{A}\mathrm{L}
are bounded, and there exists a constant
C_{M}>0
such that, for all

||\mathrm{A}\mathrm{T}(\mathrm{I}-\Pi)h||_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2d},f_{\infty})}+||\mathrm{A}\mathrm{L}||_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2d},f_{\infty})}\leq C_{M}||(\mathrm{I}-\Pi)h||_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2d},f_{\infty})}.

Proof.

Step 1, boundedness of
\mathrm{AT}(\mathrm{I}-\Pi)
:

The operator
\mathrm{AT}(\mathrm{I}-\Pi)
is bounded if and only if its adjoint
[\mathrm{AT}(\mathrm{I}-\Pi)]^{*}
is bounded. Since
\Pi
is self-adjoint and
\mathrm{T}
is skew-symmetric, we have

[\mathrm{AT}(\mathrm{I}-\Pi)]^{*}=-(\mathrm{I}-\Pi)\mathrm{T}^{2}\Pi[\mathrm{I}+(\mathrm{T}\Pi)^{*}(\mathrm{T}\Pi)]^{-1}.

Let
h\in\mathcal{H}
and
g\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}[\mathrm{I}+(\mathrm{T}\Pi)^{*}(\mathrm{T}\Pi)]^{-1}h,
then

[\mathrm{AT}(\mathrm{I}-\Pi)]^{*}h=-(\mathrm{I}-\Pi)\mathrm{T}^{2}\Pi g.

(35)

We compute

\mathrm{T}^{2}\Pi g=\frac{p^{T}}{p_{0}}\frac{\partial^{2}\Pi g}{\partial x^{2}}\frac{p}{p_{0}}-\nabla_{x}^{T}V\frac{\partial^{2}p_{0}}{\partial p^{2}}\nabla_{x}\Pi g=\sum_{i,j=1}^{d}\frac{p_{i}p_{j}}{p_{0}^{2}}\partial^{2}_{x_{i}x_{j}}\Pi g-\sum_{i,j=1}^{d}\partial_{x_{i}}V\partial^{2}_{p_{i}p_{j}}p_{0}\partial_{x_{j}}\Pi g.

(36)

We note that
\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\frac{p_{i}p_{j}}{p^{2}_{0}}Mdp=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\partial^{2}_{p_{i}p_{j}}p_{0}Mdp=0
if
i\neq j,
and we denote
\displaystyle a\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\frac{p^{2}_{i}}{p_{0}^{2}}Mdp=\frac{1}{d}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\frac{|p|^{2}}{1+|p|^{2}}Mdp>0.
Then, using (
36
) we compute

\Pi\mathrm{T}^{2}\Pi g=a\Delta_{x}\Pi g-a\nabla_{x}V\cdot\nabla_{x}\Pi g=\frac{a}{\rho_{\infty}}\text{div}_{x}(\nabla_{x}\Pi g\rho_{\infty}),

(37)

hence

h=g+(\mathrm{T}\Pi)^{*}\mathrm{T}\Pi g=g-\Pi\mathrm{T}^{2}\Pi g=g-\frac{a}{\rho_{\infty}}\text{div}_{x}(\nabla_{x}\Pi g\rho_{\infty}).

Applying the operator
\Pi
to this equation we get

\Pi g-\frac{a}{\rho_{\infty}}\text{div}_{x}(\nabla_{x}\Pi g\rho_{\infty})=\Pi h.

For the last equation we have the following regularity estimates (see Theorem

in Appendix

)

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|\nabla_{x}\Pi g|^{2}|\nabla_{x}V|^{2}\rho_{\infty}dx\leq C_{1}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}(\Pi h)^{2}\rho_{\infty}dx,\,\,\,\,\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left|\left|\frac{\partial^{2}\Pi g}{\partial x^{2}}\right|\right|_{F}^{2}\rho_{\infty}dx\leq C_{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}(\Pi h)^{2}\rho_{\infty}dx

(38)

for some constants
C_{1}>0
and
C_{2}>0.

Using (
35
) and (

) we estimate

\displaystyle||[\mathrm{AT}(\mathrm{I}-\Pi)]^{*}h||^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2d},f_{\infty})}

\displaystyle=||(\mathrm{I}-\Pi)\mathrm{T}^{2}\Pi g||^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2d},f_{\infty})}\leq||\mathrm{T}^{2}\Pi g||^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2d},f_{\infty})}

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2d}}\left(\frac{p^{T}}{p_{0}}\frac{\partial^{2}\Pi g}{\partial x^{2}}\frac{p}{p_{0}}-\nabla_{x}^{T}V(x)\frac{\partial^{2}p_{0}}{\partial p^{2}}\nabla_{x}\Pi g\right)^{2}f_{\infty}dxdp

\displaystyle\leq 2\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2d}}\left(\frac{p^{T}}{p_{0}}\frac{\partial^{2}\Pi g}{\partial x^{2}}\frac{p}{p_{0}}\right)^{2}f_{\infty}dxdp+2\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2d}}\left(\nabla_{x}^{T}V(x)\frac{\partial^{2}p_{0}}{\partial p^{2}}\nabla_{x}\Pi g\right)^{2}f_{\infty}dxdp.

Using the Hölder inequality and (

) we estimate the last two terms of (

where

), (

), and (

) show that

This shows that

is bounded. Moreover,

with

Step 2, boundedness of
\mathrm{AL}
:

Let

and

) we obtain

(42)

Integrating by parts we find

\displaystyle\Pi\mathrm{T}(\mathrm{L}h)

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left[\frac{p}{p_{0}}\cdot\nabla_{x}(\mathrm{L}h)-\nabla_{x}V\cdot\nabla_{p}(\mathrm{L}h)\right]Mdp

Then, for

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\frac{p_{k}}{p_{0}}\text{div}_{p}(D\nabla_{p}hf_{\infty})dp

\displaystyle=-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\nabla^{T}_{p}\left(\frac{p_{k}}{p_{0}}\right)D\nabla_{p}hf_{\infty}dp

\displaystyle=-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left[D\nabla_{p}\left(\frac{p_{k}}{p_{0}}\right)\right]\cdot\nabla_{p}hf_{\infty}dp

\displaystyle=-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\frac{1}{p_{0}^{2}}\partial_{p_{k}}hf_{\infty}dp

\displaystyle=-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left(\frac{p_{k}}{p_{0}^{3}}+\frac{2p_{k}}{p_{0}^{4}}\right)hf_{\infty}dp.

(
42
), (
43
), and (

) show that

g-\frac{a}{\rho_{\infty}}\text{div}_{x}(\nabla_{x}g\rho_{\infty})=\frac{1}{\rho_{\infty}}\text{div}_{x}\left[\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left(\frac{p}{p_{0}^{3}}+\frac{2p}{p_{0}^{4}}\right)hf_{\infty}dp\right].

(45)

We multiply this equation by

and integrate by parts

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}g^{2}\rho_{\infty}dx+a\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\nabla^{T}_{x}g\nabla_{x}g\rho_{\infty}dx

\displaystyle=-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\nabla_{x}g\cdot\left[\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left(\frac{p}{p_{0}^{3}}+\frac{2p}{p_{0}^{4}}\right)hMdp\right]\rho_{\infty}dx

\displaystyle\leq\varepsilon\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|\nabla_{x}g|^{2}\rho_{\infty}dx+\frac{1}{4\varepsilon}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left(\frac{p}{p_{0}^{3}}+\frac{2p}{p_{0}^{4}}\right)hMdp\right|^{2}\rho_{\infty}dx,

where

is small enough so that

Lemma 4.1

Conclusion and Outlook

In this paper we established exponential convergence of solutions for the relativistic Fokker-Planck equation to the unique steady state, and we included cases with non-convex external potentials (e.g. double-well potentials). Our main results are:

(a)

Exponential convergence in a weighted
L^{2}
setting, by following the
L^{2}-
hypocoercivity method of Dolbeault-Mouhot-Schmeiser
[
22
]
.

(b)

Exponential convergence in a weighted
H^{1}
setting, by establishing a new, refined entropy method that is based on a modified Fisher-type functional with a metric that depends on position and momentum.

(c)

Hypoelliptic regularization from
L^{2}
to
H^{1}
by making the Lyapunov functional from (b) depend also on time.

In the proof of (a), we used the
L^{2}-
hypocoercivity method of Dolbeault-Mouhot-Schmeiser
[
22
]
. This method was applied to the classical kinetic Fokker-Planck equation in
[
22
]
. We showed that this method can be applied to the relativistic Fokker-Planck equation which, in some sense, is more general than the classical one. As an extension of the present work one could study the long-time behavior of general degenerate non-symmetric Fokker-Planck equations with non-constant diffusion matrices. Such degenerate extensions of the kinetic Fokker-Planck equation (but with constant diffusion matrices and linear drift) have been considered in
[
7
,
1

]
.

In general, relativistic kinetic equations are mathematically more challenging than classical ones since the relativistic transport and collision terms are more intricate, thus complicating their mathematical structure. Our results show that the usual hypocoercive methods developed for the classical Fokker-Planck equation can be adapted to the relativistic case.

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}u^{2}|\nabla_{x}V|^{4}\rho_{\infty}dx

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{\kappa_{4}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|\nabla_{x}u|^{2}(1+|\nabla_{x}V(x)|^{2})\rho_{\infty}dx

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2a\kappa_{4}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}w^{2}\rho_{\infty}dx+\frac{1}{\kappa_{4}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|\nabla_{x}u|^{2}|\nabla_{x}V|^{2}\rho_{\infty}dx.

(102)

To estimate the last term in (

), we use the second condition in (
10
), (
97
), and the Poincaré inequality (

)

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}u^{2}\left|\left|\frac{\partial^{2}V}{\partial x^{2}}\right|\right|_{F}^{2}\rho_{\infty}dx

\displaystyle\leq 2c^{2}_{3}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}u^{2}(1+|\nabla_{x}V|^{2})\rho_{\infty}dx

\displaystyle\leq 2c^{2}_{3}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}w^{2}\rho_{\infty}dx+\frac{1}{\kappa_{3}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|\nabla_{x}u|^{2}\rho_{\infty}dx\right)

\displaystyle\leq 2c^{2}_{3}\left(1+\frac{1}{2a\kappa_{3}}\right)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}w^{2}\rho_{\infty}dx.

(103)

(
101
), (
102
), and (
103
) show that

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}u^{2}|\nabla_{x}V|^{2}\rho_{\infty}dx+(a-\varepsilon-\frac{\delta}{2\kappa_{4}})\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|\nabla_{x}u|^{2}|\nabla_{x}V|^{2}\rho_{\infty}dx\\
\leq\left[\frac{1}{2\delta}+\frac{\delta}{4a\kappa_{4}}+\frac{2c^{2}_{3}a^{2}}{\varepsilon}\left(1+\frac{1}{2a\kappa_{3}}\right)\right]\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}w^{2}\rho_{\infty}dx.

(104)

We choose
\delta
and
\varepsilon
such that
a-\varepsilon-\frac{\delta}{2\kappa_{4}}>0.
Then, (
104
) shows that (
90
) holds with
\displaystyle C_{1}\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\frac{1}{a-\varepsilon-\frac{\delta}{2\kappa_{4}}}\left[\frac{1}{2\delta}+\frac{\delta}{4a\kappa_{4}}+\frac{2c_{3}^{2}a^{2}}{\varepsilon}\left(1+\frac{1}{2a\kappa_{3}}\right)\right]
.

Next, we prove (
91
):
We integrate by parts twice

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left|\left|\frac{\partial^{2}u}{\partial x^{2}}\right|\right|_{F}^{2}\rho_{\infty}dx

\displaystyle=\sum_{i,j=1}^{d}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\partial^{2}_{x_{i}x_{j}}u\partial^{2}_{x_{i}x_{j}}u\rho_{\infty}dx

\displaystyle=-\sum_{i,j=1}^{d}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\partial^{3}_{x_{i}x_{j}x_{j}}u\partial_{x_{i}}u\rho_{\infty}dx+\sum_{i,j=1}^{d}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\partial^{2}_{x_{i}x_{j}}u\partial_{x_{i}}u\partial_{x_{j}}V\rho_{\infty}dx

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{d}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\partial^{2}_{x_{j}x_{j}}u\,\text{div}_{x}(\nabla_{x}u\rho_{\infty})dx+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\nabla_{x}^{T}u\frac{\partial^{2}u}{\partial x^{2}}\nabla_{x}V\rho_{\infty}dx.

(105)

We use (
89
) in the form
a\,\text{div}_{x}(\nabla_{x}u\rho_{\infty})=(u-w)\rho_{\infty}
and the Hölder inequality to estimate (
105
)

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left|\left|\frac{\partial^{2}u}{\partial x^{2}}\right|\right|_{F}^{2}\rho_{\infty}dx

\displaystyle=a^{-1}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\Delta_{x}u(u-w)\rho_{\infty}dx+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\nabla_{x}^{T}u\frac{\partial^{2}u}{\partial x^{2}}\nabla_{x}V\rho_{\infty}dx

\displaystyle\leq a^{-1}\sqrt{\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|\Delta_{x}u|^{2}\rho_{\infty}dx}\sqrt{\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}(u-w)^{2}\rho_{\infty}dx}

\displaystyle\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,+\sqrt{\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left|\left|\frac{\partial^{2}u}{\partial x^{2}}\right|\right|_{F}^{2}\rho_{\infty}dx}\sqrt{\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|\nabla_{x}u|^{2}|\nabla_{x}V|^{2}\rho_{\infty}dx}.

This inequality and
\displaystyle|\Delta_{x}u|^{2}\leq d\left|\left|\frac{\partial^{2}u}{\partial x^{2}}\right|\right|_{F}^{2}
show that

\sqrt{\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left|\left|\frac{\partial^{2}u}{\partial x^{2}}\right|\right|_{F}^{2}\rho_{\infty}dx}\leq a^{-1}\sqrt{d\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}(u-w)^{2}\rho_{\infty}dx}+\sqrt{\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|\nabla_{x}u|^{2}|\nabla_{x}V|^{2}\rho_{\infty}dx}.

(106)

Finally, (

), (

), and (

) yield (

∎

8.2
Some matrix inequalities

Lemma 8.3
.

Let
A\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}
be a symmetric, positive definite matrix. For any
u,\,v\in\mathbb{R}^{d},
we have

2u^{T}Av\leq u^{T}Au+v^{T}Av\,\,\,\,\text{ and }\,\,\,\,2u\cdot v\leq u^{T}Au+v^{T}A^{-1}v.

(107)

Proof.

Since the matrices
A\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}
and
\begin{pmatrix}1&-1\\
-1&1\end{pmatrix}\in\mathbb{R}^{2\times 2}
are positive semi-definite, their Kronecker product

\begin{pmatrix}A&-A\\
-A&A\end{pmatrix}
is also positive semi-definite, see
[
30
, Corollary 4.2.13]
. Hence,

u^{T}Au+v^{T}Av-2u^{T}Av=\begin{pmatrix}u\\
v\end{pmatrix}^{T}\begin{pmatrix}A&-A\\
-A&A\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}u\\
v\end{pmatrix}\geq 0.

The second inequality follows by replacing
v
with
A^{-1}v.

∎

We mention an alternative proof of the above lemma: If we consider
\mathbb{R}^{d}
as a Hilbert space with the inner product
\langle u,v\rangle\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}u^{T}Av,
then, in this Hilbert space, the first matrix inequality in (
107
) coincides with Young’s inequality.

Lemma 8.4
.

Let
a_{ij}\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\frac{\delta_{ij}+p_{i}p_{j}}{p_{0}},

p\in\mathbb{R}^{d}.
Then

\sum_{k,l,i,j=1}^{d}{p_{0}^{2}}\left(\delta_{kl}-\frac{p_{k}p_{l}}{p_{0}^{2}}\right)\left(\delta_{ij}-\frac{p_{i}p_{j}}{p_{0}^{2}}\right)\nabla_{p}a_{lj}\otimes\nabla_{p}a_{ki}\leq d\left(I-\frac{p\otimes p}{p_{0}^{2}}\right)

(108)

holds for all
p\in\mathbb{R}^{d}.

Proof.

We compute the element which is in the intersection of the
m^{\text{th}}
column and the
n^{\text{th}}
row:

\sum_{k,l,i,j=1}^{d}{p_{0}^{2}}\left(\delta_{kl}-\frac{p_{k}p_{l}}{p_{0}^{2}}\right)\left(\delta_{ij}-\frac{p_{i}p_{j}}{p_{0}^{2}}\right)\partial_{p_{m}}a_{ki}\partial_{p_{n}}a_{lj}\\
=\sum_{l,i=1}^{d}{p_{0}^{2}}\left(\sum_{j=1}^{d}\left(\delta_{ij}-\frac{p_{i}p_{j}}{p_{0}^{2}}\right)\partial_{p_{n}}a_{lj}\right)\left(\sum_{k=1}^{d}\left(\delta_{kl}-\frac{p_{k}p_{l}}{p_{0}^{2}}\right)\partial_{p_{m}}a_{ki}\right).

We first compute the sums in the brackets:

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{d}\left(\delta_{ij}-\frac{p_{i}p_{j}}{p_{0}^{2}}\right)\partial_{p_{n}}a_{lj}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{d}\left(\delta_{ij}-\frac{p_{i}p_{j}}{p_{0}^{2}}\right)\left(\frac{p_{l}\delta_{nj}+p_{j}\delta_{nl}}{p_{0}}-\frac{(\delta_{lj}+p_{l}p_{j})p_{n}}{p_{0}^{3}}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{p_{l}\delta_{ni}+p_{i}\delta_{nl}}{p_{0}}-\frac{(\delta_{li}+p_{l}p_{i})p_{n}}{p^{3}_{0}}-\frac{p_{i}}{p^{2}_{0}}\left(\frac{p_{l}p_{n}+|p|^{2}\delta_{nl}}{p_{0}}-\frac{(p_{l}+p_{l}|p|^{2})p_{n}}{p^{3}_{0}}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{p_{l}\delta_{ni}+p_{i}\delta_{nl}}{p_{0}}-\frac{(\delta_{li}+p_{l}p_{i})p_{n}}{p^{3}_{0}}-\frac{p_{i}p_{l}p_{n}+p_{i}|p|^{2}\delta_{nl}}{p^{3}_{0}}+\frac{p_{l}p_{i}p_{n}}{p^{3}_{0}}

\displaystyle=\frac{p_{l}\delta_{ni}+p_{i}\delta_{nl}}{p_{0}}-\frac{(\delta_{li}+p_{l}p_{i})p_{n}}{p^{3}_{0}}-\frac{p_{i}|p|^{2}\delta_{nl}}{p^{3}_{0}}

\displaystyle=\frac{p_{l}\delta_{ni}}{p_{0}}-\frac{(\delta_{li}+p_{l}p_{i})p_{n}}{p^{3}_{0}}+\frac{p_{i}\delta_{nl}}{p^{3}_{0}}

\displaystyle=\frac{p_{l}\delta_{ni}}{p_{0}}-\frac{p_{n}\delta_{li}}{p_{0}^{3}}+\frac{p_{i}\delta_{nl}}{p^{3}_{0}}-\frac{p_{l}p_{i}p_{n}}{p^{3}_{0}}.

(109)

Similarly, we can show

\sum_{k=1}^{d}\left(\delta_{kl}-\frac{p_{k}p_{l}}{p_{0}^{2}}\right)\partial_{p_{m}}a_{ki}=\frac{p_{i}\delta_{ml}}{p_{0}}-\frac{p_{m}\delta_{li}}{p_{0}^{3}}+\frac{p_{l}\delta_{mi}}{p^{3}_{0}}-\frac{p_{l}p_{i}p_{m}}{p^{3}_{0}}.

(110)

Next, we sum the product of (
109
) and (
110
) with respect to
i
and
l:

\sum_{l,i=1}^{d}\left(\frac{p_{l}\delta_{ni}}{p_{0}}-\frac{p_{n}\delta_{li}}{p_{0}^{3}}+\frac{p_{i}\delta_{nl}}{p^{3}_{0}}-\frac{p_{l}p_{i}p_{n}}{p^{3}_{0}}\right)\left(\frac{p_{i}\delta_{ml}}{p_{0}}-\frac{p_{m}\delta_{li}}{p_{0}^{3}}+\frac{p_{l}\delta_{mi}}{p^{3}_{0}}-\frac{p_{l}p_{i}p_{m}}{p^{3}_{0}}\right)

=\sum_{l=1}^{d}\left[\frac{p_{l}}{p_{0}}\left(\frac{p_{n}\delta_{ml}}{p_{0}}-\frac{p_{m}\delta_{ln}}{p_{0}^{3}}+\frac{p_{l}\delta_{mn}}{p^{3}_{0}}-\frac{p_{l}p_{n}p_{m}}{p^{3}_{0}}\right)-\frac{p_{n}}{p_{0}^{3}}\left(\frac{p_{l}\delta_{ml}}{p_{0}}-\frac{p_{m}}{p_{0}^{3}}+\frac{p_{l}\delta_{ml}}{p^{3}_{0}}-\frac{p^{2}_{l}p_{m}}{p^{3}_{0}}\right)\right.

\left.+\left(\frac{\delta_{nl}}{p^{3}_{0}}-\frac{p_{l}p_{n}}{p^{3}_{0}}\right)\left(\frac{|p|^{2}\delta_{ml}}{p_{0}}-\frac{p_{m}p_{l}}{p_{0}^{3}}+\frac{p_{l}p_{m}}{p^{3}_{0}}-\frac{p_{l}p_{m}|p|^{2}}{p^{3}_{0}}\right)\right]

=\left(\frac{p_{n}p_{m}}{p^{2}_{0}}-\frac{p_{m}p_{n}}{p_{0}^{4}}+\frac{|p|^{2}\delta_{mn}}{p^{4}_{0}}-\frac{p_{n}p_{m}|p|^{2}}{p^{4}_{0}}\right)-\left(\frac{p_{n}p_{m}}{p^{4}_{0}}-\frac{dp_{m}p_{n}}{p_{0}^{6}}+\frac{p_{m}p_{n}}{p^{6}_{0}}-\frac{p_{n}p_{m}|p|^{2}}{p^{6}_{0}}\right)

+\left(\frac{|p|^{2}\delta_{mn}}{p^{4}_{0}}-\frac{p_{n}p_{m}|p|^{2}}{p^{6}_{0}}\right)-\left(\frac{p_{n}p_{m}|p|^{2}}{p^{4}_{0}}-\frac{p_{n}p_{m}|p|^{4}}{p^{6}_{0}}\right)

=\frac{2|p|^{2}\delta_{mn}}{p_{0}^{4}}+\frac{(d-2)p_{m}p_{n}}{p_{0}^{6}}-\frac{p_{n}p_{m}|p|^{2}}{p^{4}_{0}}-\frac{p_{n}p_{m}|p|^{2}}{p^{6}_{0}}+\frac{p_{n}p_{m}|p|^{4}}{p^{6}_{0}}

=\frac{2\delta_{mn}}{p_{0}^{2}}-\frac{2\delta_{mn}}{p_{0}^{4}}+\frac{(d-2)p_{m}p_{n}}{p_{0}^{6}}-\frac{p_{n}p_{m}}{p^{2}_{0}}+\frac{p_{n}p_{m}}{p^{4}_{0}}+\frac{p_{m}p_{n}}{p_{0}^{2}}-\frac{3p_{m}p_{n}}{p_{0}^{4}}+\frac{2p_{m}p_{n}}{p_{0}^{6}}

=\frac{2\delta_{mn}}{p_{0}^{2}}-\frac{2\delta_{mn}}{p_{0}^{4}}+\frac{dp_{m}p_{n}}{p_{0}^{6}}-\frac{2p_{m}p_{n}}{p_{0}^{4}}=\frac{2}{p_{0}^{2}}(\delta_{mn}-\frac{p_{n}p_{m}}{p_{0}^{2}})-\frac{2}{p_{0}^{4}}(\delta_{mn}-\frac{p_{n}p_{m}}{p_{0}^{2}})+\frac{(d-2)p_{m}p_{n}}{p_{0}^{6}}.

This shows

\sum_{k,l,i,j=1}^{d}{p_{0}^{2}}\left(\delta_{kl}-\frac{p_{k}p_{l}}{p_{0}^{2}}\right)\left(\delta_{ij}-\frac{p_{i}p_{j}}{p_{0}^{2}}\right)\nabla_{p}a_{lj}\otimes\nabla_{p}a_{ki}=2\left(I-\frac{p\otimes p}{p_{0}^{2}}\right)-\frac{2}{p_{0}^{2}}\left(I-\frac{p\otimes p}{p_{0}^{2}}\right)+\frac{(d-2)p\otimes p}{p_{0}^{4}}.

The claimed inequality follows from

\frac{(d-2)p\otimes p}{p_{0}^{4}}<\frac{d-2}{p_{0}^{2}}I\leq(d-2)\left(I-\frac{p\otimes p}{p_{0}^{2}}\right).

∎

Lemma 8.5
.

Let
a_{ij}\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\frac{\delta_{ij}+p_{i}p_{j}}{p_{0}},

p\in\mathbb{R}^{d}.
Then

\sum_{k,l,i,j=1}^{d}{p_{0}^{2}}\left(\delta_{kl}-\frac{p_{k}p_{l}}{p_{0}^{2}}\right)\left(\delta_{ij}-\frac{p_{i}p_{j}}{p_{0}^{2}}\right)((I+p\otimes p)\nabla_{p}a_{lj})\otimes((I+p\otimes p)\nabla_{p}a_{ki})\leq d(I+p\otimes p)

(111)

holds for all
p\in\mathbb{R}^{d}.

Proof.

We compute the element which is in the intersection of the
m^{\text{th}}
column and the
n^{\text{th}}
row:

\sum_{k,l,i,j=1}^{d}{p_{0}^{2}}\left(\delta_{kl}-\frac{p_{k}p_{l}}{p_{0}^{2}}\right)\left(\delta_{ij}-\frac{p_{i}p_{j}}{p_{0}^{2}}\right)(\partial_{p_{n}}a_{lj}+p_{n}p\cdot\nabla_{p}a_{lj})(\partial_{p_{m}}a_{ki}+p_{m}p\cdot\nabla_{p}a_{ki})

=\sum_{l,i=1}^{d}p_{0}^{2}\left(\sum_{j=1}^{d}\left(\delta_{ij}-\frac{p_{i}p_{j}}{p_{0}^{2}}\right)(\partial_{p_{n}}a_{lj}+p_{n}p\cdot\nabla_{p}a_{lj})\right)\left(\sum_{k=1}^{d}\left(\delta_{kl}-\frac{p_{k}p_{l}}{p_{0}^{2}}\right)(\partial_{p_{m}}a_{ki}+p_{m}p\cdot\nabla_{p}a_{ki})\right).

We want to compute the sums in the brackets. We first compute

p\cdot\nabla_{p}a_{lj}=\sum_{r=1}^{d}p_{r}\left(\frac{p_{l}\delta_{rj}+p_{j}\delta_{rl}}{p_{0}}-\frac{(\delta_{lj}+p_{l}p_{j})p_{r}}{p_{0}^{3}}\right)=\frac{2p_{l}p_{j}}{p_{0}}-\frac{(\delta_{lj}+p_{l}p_{j})|p|^{2}}{p_{0}^{3}}

and so

\displaystyle\partial_{p_{n}}a_{lj}+p_{n}(p\cdot\nabla_{p}a_{lj})

\displaystyle=\frac{p_{l}\delta_{nj}+p_{j}\delta_{nl}}{p_{0}}-\frac{(\delta_{lj}+p_{l}p_{j})p_{n}}{p_{0}^{3}}+\frac{2p_{l}p_{j}p_{n}}{p_{0}}-\frac{(\delta_{lj}+p_{l}p_{j})p_{n}|p|^{2}}{p_{0}^{3}}

\displaystyle=\frac{p_{l}\delta_{nj}+p_{j}\delta_{nl}-\delta_{lj}p_{n}}{p_{0}}+\frac{p_{l}p_{j}p_{n}}{p_{0}}.

This helps us to compute

\sum_{j=1}^{d}\left(\delta_{ij}-\frac{p_{i}p_{j}}{p_{0}^{2}}\right)(\partial_{p_{n}}a_{lj}+p_{n}p\cdot\nabla_{p}a_{lj})=\sum_{j=1}^{d}\left(\delta_{ij}-\frac{p_{i}p_{j}}{p_{0}^{2}}\right)\left(\frac{p_{l}\delta_{nj}+p_{j}\delta_{nl}-\delta_{lj}p_{n}}{p_{0}}+\frac{p_{l}p_{j}p_{n}}{p_{0}}\right)

=\frac{p_{l}\delta_{ni}+p_{i}\delta_{nl}-\delta_{li}p_{n}}{p_{0}}+\frac{p_{l}p_{i}p_{n}}{p_{0}}-\frac{p_{i}}{p_{0}^{2}}\left(\frac{|p|^{2}\delta_{nl}}{p_{0}}+\frac{p_{l}p_{n}|p|^{2}}{p_{0}}\right)=\frac{p_{l}\delta_{ni}-\delta_{li}p_{n}}{p_{0}}+\frac{(\delta_{nl}+p_{l}p_{n})p_{i}}{p^{3}_{0}}.

Similarly, we compute

\sum_{k=1}^{d}\left(\delta_{kl}-\frac{p_{k}p_{l}}{p_{0}^{2}}\right)(\partial_{p_{m}}a_{ki}+p_{m}p\cdot\nabla_{p}a_{ki})=\frac{p_{i}\delta_{ml}-\delta_{li}p_{m}}{p_{0}}+\frac{(\delta_{mi}+p_{i}p_{m})p_{l}}{p^{3}_{0}}.

We sum the product of the last two equations with respect to
i
and
l:

\sum_{l,i=1}^{d}p_{0}^{2}\left(\sum_{j=1}^{d}\left(\delta_{ij}-\frac{p_{i}p_{j}}{p_{0}^{2}}\right)(\partial_{p_{n}}a_{lj}+p_{n}p\cdot\nabla_{p}a_{lj})\right)\left(\sum_{k=1}^{d}\left(\delta_{kl}-\frac{p_{k}p_{l}}{p_{0}^{2}}\right)(\partial_{p_{m}}a_{ki}+p_{m}p\cdot\nabla_{p}a_{ki})\right)

=\sum_{l,i=1}^{d}p_{0}^{2}\left(\frac{p_{l}\delta_{ni}-\delta_{li}p_{n}}{p_{0}}+\frac{(\delta_{nl}+p_{l}p_{n})p_{i}}{p^{3}_{0}}\right)\left(\frac{p_{i}\delta_{ml}-\delta_{li}p_{m}}{p_{0}}+\frac{(\delta_{mi}+p_{i}p_{m})p_{l}}{p^{3}_{0}}\right)

=\sum_{l=1}^{d}p_{0}^{2}\left[\frac{p_{l}}{p_{0}}\left(\frac{p_{n}\delta_{ml}-\delta_{ln}p_{m}}{p_{0}}+\frac{(\delta_{mn}+p_{n}p_{m})p_{l}}{p^{3}_{0}}\right)-\frac{p_{n}}{p_{0}}\left(\frac{p_{l}\delta_{ml}-p_{m}}{p_{0}}+\frac{(\delta_{ml}+p_{l}p_{m})p_{l}}{p^{3}_{0}}\right)\right.

\left.+\frac{(\delta_{nl}+p_{l}p_{n})}{p^{3}_{0}}\left(\frac{|p|^{2}\delta_{ml}-p_{l}p_{m}}{p_{0}}+\frac{(p_{m}+|p|^{2}p_{m})p_{l}}{p^{3}_{0}}\right)\right]

This shows

\sum_{k,l,i,j=1}^{d}{p_{0}^{2}}\left(\delta_{kl}-\frac{p_{k}p_{l}}{p_{0}^{2}}\right)\left(\delta_{ij}-\frac{p_{i}p_{j}}{p_{0}^{2}}\right)((I+p\otimes p)\nabla_{p}a_{lj})\otimes((I+p\otimes p)\nabla_{p}a_{ki})

=d(I+p\otimes p)-(d-2)I-\frac{2(I+p\otimes p)}{p^{2}_{0}}\leq d(I+p\otimes p).

∎

Lemma 8.6
.

Let
P=P(x,p)
be the matrix defined in (

). Then there are constant
\theta_{1}>0
and
\theta_{2}>0
such that

\sum_{i,j=1}^{d}\frac{1}{f_{\infty}}\partial_{p_{j}}(\partial_{p_{i}}Pa_{ij}f_{\infty})\leq\begin{pmatrix}\frac{2\theta_{1}\varepsilon^{3}}{V_{0}^{3}p_{0}^{3}}(I-\frac{p\otimes p}{p_{0}^{2}})&0\\

We have

\sum_{i,j=1}^{d}\frac{1}{f_{\infty}}\partial_{p_{j}}(\partial_{p_{i}}Pa_{ij}f_{\infty})=\sum_{i,j=1}^{d}\partial^{2}_{p_{i}p_{j}}Pa_{ij}+\sum_{i=1}^{d}\partial_{p_{i}}P\sum_{j=1}^{d}\left(\partial_{p_{j}}a_{ij}-\frac{a_{ij}p_{j}}{p_{0}}\right).

(112)

Since

we have

\sum_{j=1}^{d}\left(\partial_{p_{j}}a_{ij}-\frac{a_{ij}p_{j}}{p_{0}}\right)=\sum_{j=1}^{d}\left(\frac{p_{i}+\delta_{ij}p_{j}}{p_{0}}-\frac{\delta_{ij}+p_{i}p_{j}}{p_{0}^{3}}p_{j}-\frac{(\delta_{ij}+p_{i}p_{j})p_{j}}{p^{2}_{0}}\right)=\left(\frac{d}{p_{0}}-1\right)p_{i}.

We denote
\varepsilon_{1}=\varepsilon_{1}(x)\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\frac{\varepsilon}{V_{0}(x)}>0
which is uniformly bounded for

Then

\partial_{p_{i}}P=\begin{pmatrix}\frac{-6\varepsilon^{3}_{1}p_{i}}{p_{0}^{5}}I-\frac{2\varepsilon^{3}_{1}}{p_{0}^{5}}\partial_{p_{i}}(p\otimes p)+\frac{10\varepsilon^{3}_{1}p_{i}}{p_{0}^{7}}p\otimes p&-\frac{2\varepsilon^{2}_{1}p_{i}}{p_{0}^{4}}I\\

The last two equations show

\sum_{i=1}^{d}\partial_{p_{i}}P\sum_{j=1}^{d}\left(\partial_{p_{j}}a_{ij}-\frac{a_{ij}p_{j}}{p_{0}}\right)=\left(\frac{d}{p_{0}}-1\right)\sum_{i=1}^{d}\partial_{p_{i}}Pp_{i}\\
=\left(\frac{d}{p_{0}}-1\right)\begin{pmatrix}\frac{-6\varepsilon^{3}_{1}|p|^{2}}{p_{0}^{5}}I-\frac{4\varepsilon^{3}_{1}}{p_{0}^{5}}p\otimes p+\frac{10\varepsilon^{3}_{1}|p|^{2}}{p_{0}^{7}}p\otimes p&-\frac{2\varepsilon^{2}_{1}|p|^{2}}{p_{0}^{4}}I\\
-\frac{2\varepsilon^{2}_{1}|p|^{2}}{p_{0}^{4}}I&-\frac{2\varepsilon_{1}|p|^{2}}{p_{0}^{3}}(I+p\otimes p)+\frac{4\varepsilon_{1}}{p_{0}}p\otimes p\end{pmatrix}\\

\frac{2\varepsilon^{2}_{1}}{p_{0}^{2}}I-\frac{2\varepsilon^{2}_{1}}{p_{0}^{4}}I&-\frac{2\varepsilon_{1}}{p_{0}}(I+p\otimes p)+\frac{4\varepsilon_{1}}{p_{0}}I-\frac{2\varepsilon_{1}}{p_{0}^{3}}(I+p\otimes p)\end{pmatrix}.

(113)

One can easily check

\frac{p\otimes p}{p_{0}^{2}}<I,\,\,\,\frac{1}{p_{0}^{5}}I\leq\frac{1}{p_{0}^{3}}\left(I-\frac{p\otimes p}{p_{0}^{2}}\right),\,\,\,\frac{1}{p_{0}}I\leq\frac{1}{p_{0}}(I+p\otimes p).

(114)

These matrix inequalities help us to estimate

\left(1-\frac{d}{p_{0}}\right)\left(\frac{6\varepsilon^{3}_{1}}{p_{0}^{3}}(I-\frac{p\otimes p}{p_{0}^{2}})+\frac{10\varepsilon^{3}_{1}p\otimes p}{p_{0}^{7}}-\frac{6\varepsilon^{3}_{1}}{p_{0}^{5}}I\right)\leq\frac{22\varepsilon^{3}_{1}\left|1-\frac{d}{p_{0}}\right|}{p_{0}^{3}}\left(I-\frac{p\otimes p}{p_{0}^{2}}\right)

(115)

and

\left(1-\frac{d}{p_{0}}\right)\left(-\frac{2\varepsilon_{1}}{p_{0}}(I+p\otimes p)+\frac{4\varepsilon_{1}}{p_{0}}I-\frac{2\varepsilon_{1}}{p_{0}^{3}}(I+p\otimes p)\right)\leq\frac{8\varepsilon_{1}\left|1-\frac{d}{p_{0}}\right|}{p_{0}}\left(I+p\otimes p\right).

(116)

(
113
), (
115
), and (
116
) imply

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{d}\partial_{p_{i}}P\sum_{j=1}^{d}\left(\partial_{p_{j}}a_{ij}-\frac{a_{ij}p_{j}}{p_{0}}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\begin{pmatrix}\frac{22\varepsilon^{3}_{1}\left|1-\frac{d}{p_{0}}\right|}{p_{0}^{3}}\left(I-\frac{p\otimes p}{p_{0}^{2}}\right)&\left(1-\frac{d}{p_{0}}\right)\left(\frac{2\varepsilon^{2}_{1}}{p_{0}^{2}}-\frac{2\varepsilon^{2}_{1}}{p_{0}^{4}}\right)I\\
\left(1-\frac{d}{p_{0}}\right)\left(\frac{2\varepsilon^{2}_{1}}{p_{0}^{2}}-\frac{2\varepsilon^{2}_{1}}{p_{0}^{4}}\right)I&\frac{8\varepsilon_{1}\left|1-\frac{d}{p_{0}}\right|}{p_{0}}\left(I+p\otimes p\right)\end{pmatrix}

\displaystyle\leq\begin{pmatrix}\frac{24\varepsilon^{3}_{1}\left|1-\frac{d}{p_{0}}\right|}{p_{0}^{3}}\left(I-\frac{p\otimes p}{p_{0}^{2}}\right)&0\\
0&\frac{10\varepsilon_{1}\left|1-\frac{d}{p_{0}}\right|}{p_{0}}\left(I+p\otimes p\right)\end{pmatrix}.

(117)

In the last inequality, we used the fact that

\begin{pmatrix}-\frac{2\varepsilon^{3}_{1}\left|1-\frac{d}{p_{0}}\right|}{p_{0}^{3}}(I-\frac{p\otimes p}{p_{0}^{2}})&\left(1-\frac{d}{p_{0}}\right)\left(\frac{2\varepsilon^{2}_{1}}{p_{0}^{2}}-\frac{2\varepsilon^{2}_{1}}{p_{0}^{4}}\right)I\\
\left(1-\frac{d}{p_{0}}\right)\left(\frac{2\varepsilon^{2}_{1}}{p_{0}^{2}}-\frac{2\varepsilon^{2}_{1}}{p_{0}^{4}}\right)I&-\frac{2\varepsilon_{1}\left|1-\frac{d}{p_{0}}\right|}{p_{0}}(I+p\otimes p)\end{pmatrix}

is negative semi-definite, which can be proven by using the second inequality in (
107
).
Since
\left|1-\frac{d}{p_{0}}\right|
is uniformly bounded for
p\in\mathbb{R}^{d},
(
117
) shows that there are constants
C_{1}>0
and
C_{2}>0
such that

\sum_{i=1}^{d}\partial_{p_{i}}P\sum_{j=1}^{d}\left(\partial_{p_{j}}a_{ij}-\frac{a_{ij}p_{j}}{p_{0}}\right)\leq\begin{pmatrix}\frac{2C_{1}\varepsilon^{3}_{1}}{p_{0}^{3}}(I-\frac{p\otimes p}{p_{0}^{2}})&0\\
0&\frac{2C_{2}\varepsilon_{1}}{p_{0}}(I+p\otimes p)\end{pmatrix},\,\,\,\,\forall\,x,\,p\in\mathbb{R}^{d}.

(118)

Next, using the computation above for
\partial_{p_{i}}P
we compute

\partial^{2}_{p_{j}p_{i}}P=\begin{pmatrix}X_{ij}&-\frac{2\varepsilon^{2}_{1}\delta_{ij}}{p_{0}^{4}}I+\frac{8\varepsilon^{2}_{1}p_{i}p_{j}}{p_{0}^{6}}I\\
-\frac{2\varepsilon^{2}_{1}\delta_{ij}}{p_{0}^{4}}I+\frac{8\varepsilon^{2}_{1}p_{i}p_{j}}{p_{0}^{6}}I&Y_{ij}\end{pmatrix},

where

X_{ij}\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}-\frac{2\varepsilon^{3}_{1}[3\delta_{ij}I+\partial_{p_{i}p_{j}}^{2}(p\otimes p)]}{p_{0}^{5}}+\frac{10\varepsilon^{3}_{1}[3p_{i}p_{j}I+p_{j}\partial_{p_{i}}(p\otimes p)+p_{i}\partial_{p_{j}}(p\otimes p)+\delta_{ij}p\otimes p]}{p_{0}^{7}}-\frac{70\varepsilon^{3}_{1}p_{i}p_{j}p\otimes p}{p_{0}^{9}},

Y_{ij}\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}-2\varepsilon_{1}\left(\frac{\delta_{ij}}{p_{0}^{3}}-\frac{3p_{i}p_{j}}{p_{0}^{5}}\right)(I+p\otimes p)-\frac{2\varepsilon_{1}[p_{i}\partial_{p_{j}}(p\otimes p)+p_{j}\partial_{p_{i}}(p\otimes p)]}{p^{3}_{0}}+\frac{2\varepsilon_{1}}{p_{0}}\partial^{2}_{p_{i}p_{j}}(p\otimes p).

The identities

\sum_{i,j=1}^{d}\delta_{ij}\partial_{p_{i}p_{j}}^{2}(p\otimes p)=2I,\,\,\,\sum_{i,j=1}^{d}p_{i}p_{j}\partial_{p_{i}p_{j}}^{2}(p\otimes p)=2p\otimes p,\,\,\,\,\sum_{i=1}^{d}p_{i}\partial_{p_{i}}(p\otimes p)=2p\otimes p

will be used in the following computations:

X\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\sum_{i,j=1}^{d}X_{ij}a_{ij}=-\sum_{i,j=1}^{d}\frac{2\varepsilon^{3}_{1}[3\delta_{ij}I+\delta_{ij}\partial_{p_{i}p_{j}}^{2}(p\otimes p)+3\delta_{ij}p_{i}p_{j}I+p_{i}p_{j}\partial_{p_{i}p_{j}}^{2}(p\otimes p)]}{p_{0}^{6}}

+\sum_{i,j=1}^{d}\frac{10\varepsilon^{3}_{1}[3\delta_{ij}p_{i}p_{j}I+\delta_{ij}p_{j}\partial_{p_{i}}(p\otimes p)+\delta_{ij}p_{i}\partial_{p_{j}}(p\otimes p)+\delta_{ij}p\otimes p]}{p_{0}^{8}}

+\sum_{i,j=1}^{d}\frac{10\varepsilon^{3}_{1}p_{i}p_{j}[3p_{i}p_{j}I+p_{j}\partial_{p_{i}}(p\otimes p)+p_{i}\partial_{p_{j}}(p\otimes p)+\delta_{ij}p\otimes p]}{p_{0}^{8}}

-\sum_{i,j=1}^{d}\frac{70\varepsilon^{3}_{1}[\delta_{ij}+p_{i}p_{j}]p_{i}p_{j}p\otimes p}{p_{0}^{10}}

=-\frac{2\varepsilon^{3}_{1}[3(d-1+p_{0}^{2})I+2(I+p\otimes p)]}{p_{0}^{6}}+\frac{10\varepsilon^{3}_{1}[3|p|^{2}p_{0}^{2}I+4p_{0}^{2}p\otimes p+(d-1+p_{0}^{2})p\otimes p]}{p_{0}^{8}}-\frac{70\varepsilon^{3}_{1}|p|^{2}p\otimes p}{p_{0}^{8}}

=\frac{24\varepsilon^{3}_{1}}{p_{0}^{4}}\left(I-\frac{p\otimes p}{p_{0}^{2}}\right)-\frac{\varepsilon^{3}_{1}(28+6d)}{p_{0}^{6}}I+\frac{\varepsilon^{3}_{1}(60+10d)}{p_{0}^{8}}p\otimes p,

Y\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\sum_{i,j=1}^{d}Y_{ij}a_{ij}=-\sum_{i,j=1}^{d}2\varepsilon_{1}\left(\frac{\delta_{ij}}{p_{0}^{4}}+\frac{\delta_{ij}p_{i}p_{j}}{p_{0}^{4}}-\frac{3\delta_{ij}p_{i}p_{j}}{p_{0}^{6}}-\frac{3p^{2}_{i}p^{2}_{j}}{p_{0}^{6}}\right)(I+p\otimes p)

-\sum_{i,j=1}^{d}\frac{2\varepsilon_{1}[\delta_{ij}p_{i}\partial_{p_{j}}(p\otimes p)+\delta_{ij}p_{j}\partial_{p_{i}}(p\otimes p)]+2\varepsilon_{1}p_{i}p_{j}[p_{i}\partial_{p_{j}}(p\otimes p)+p_{j}\partial_{p_{i}}(p\otimes p)]}{p^{4}_{0}}

+\sum_{i,j=1}^{d}\frac{2\varepsilon_{1}}{p^{2}_{0}}[\delta_{ij}\partial^{2}_{p_{i}p_{j}}(p\otimes p)+p_{i}p_{j}\partial^{2}_{p_{i}p_{j}}(p\otimes p)]

=\left(\frac{4\varepsilon_{1}}{p_{0}^{2}}-\frac{2\varepsilon_{1}(d+2)}{p_{0}^{4}}\right)(I+p\otimes p)-\frac{8\varepsilon_{1}}{p_{0}^{2}}p\otimes p+\frac{4\varepsilon_{1}}{p_{0}^{2}}(I+p\otimes p)

=\frac{8\varepsilon_{1}}{p_{0}^{2}}I-\frac{2\varepsilon_{1}(d+2)}{p_{0}^{4}}(I+p\otimes p),

Z\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\sum_{i,j=1}^{d}\left(-\frac{2\varepsilon_{1}^{2}\delta_{ij}}{p_{0}^{4}}I+\frac{8\varepsilon_{1}^{2}p_{i}p_{j}}{p_{0}^{6}}I\right)a_{ij}=-\sum_{i,j=1}^{d}\left(\frac{2\varepsilon^{2}_{1}\delta_{ij}}{p_{0}^{5}}I-\frac{8\varepsilon^{2}_{1}\delta_{ij}p_{i}p_{j}}{p_{0}^{7}}I+\frac{2\varepsilon^{2}_{1}\delta_{ij}p_{i}p_{j}}{p_{0}^{5}}I-\frac{8\varepsilon^{2}_{1}p^{2}_{i}p^{2}_{j}}{p_{0}^{7}}I\right)

=\frac{6\varepsilon^{2}_{1}}{p_{0}^{3}}I-\frac{2\varepsilon^{2}_{1}(d+3)}{p_{0}^{5}}I.

According to our notations, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{i,j=1}^{d}\partial^{2}_{p_{i}p_{j}}Pa_{ij}

\displaystyle=\begin{pmatrix}X&Z\\
Z&Y\end{pmatrix}

\displaystyle=\begin{pmatrix}\frac{24\varepsilon^{3}_{1}}{p_{0}^{4}}(I-\frac{p\otimes p}{p_{0}^{2}})-\frac{\varepsilon^{3}_{1}(28+6d)}{p_{0}^{6}}I+\frac{\varepsilon^{3}_{1}(60+10d)}{p_{0}^{8}}p\otimes p&\frac{6\varepsilon^{2}_{1}}{p_{0}^{3}}I-\frac{2\varepsilon^{2}_{1}(d+3)}{p_{0}^{5}}I\\
\frac{6\varepsilon^{2}_{1}}{p_{0}^{3}}I-\frac{2\varepsilon^{2}_{1}(d+3)}{p_{0}^{5}}I&\frac{8\varepsilon_{1}}{p_{0}^{2}}I-\frac{2\varepsilon_{1}(d+2)}{p_{0}^{4}}(I+p\otimes p)\end{pmatrix}.

(119)

The matrix inequalities in (
114
) help us to estimate

\frac{24\varepsilon^{3}_{1}}{p_{0}^{4}}\left(I-\frac{p\otimes p}{p_{0}^{2}}\right)-\frac{\varepsilon^{3}_{1}(28+6d)}{p_{0}^{6}}I+\frac{\varepsilon^{3}_{1}(60+10d)}{p_{0}^{8}}p\otimes p\leq\frac{(84+10d)\varepsilon^{3}_{1}}{p_{0}^{4}}\left(I-\frac{p\otimes p}{p_{0}^{2}}\right)

and

\frac{8\varepsilon_{1}}{p_{0}^{2}}I-\frac{2\varepsilon_{1}(d+2)}{p_{0}^{4}}(I+p\otimes p)\leq\frac{8\varepsilon_{1}}{p_{0}^{2}}(I+p\otimes p).

These estimates and (
119
) show

\displaystyle\sum_{i,j=1}^{d}\partial^{2}_{p_{i}p_{j}}Pa_{ij}

\displaystyle\leq\begin{pmatrix}\frac{(84+10d)\varepsilon^{3}_{1}}{p_{0}^{4}}\left(I-\frac{p\otimes p}{p_{0}^{2}}\right)&\frac{6\varepsilon^{2}_{1}}{p_{0}^{3}}I-\frac{2\varepsilon^{2}_{1}(d+3)}{p_{0}^{5}}I\\
\frac{6\varepsilon^{2}_{1}}{p_{0}^{3}}I-\frac{2\varepsilon^{2}_{1}(d+3)}{p_{0}^{5}}I&\frac{8\varepsilon_{1}}{p_{0}^{2}}(I+p\otimes p)\end{pmatrix}

\displaystyle\leq\begin{pmatrix}\frac{\left(84+10d+\left|6-\frac{2(d+3)}{p_{0}^{2}}\right|\right)\varepsilon^{3}_{1}}{p_{0}^{4}}\left(I-\frac{p\otimes p}{p_{0}^{2}}\right)&0\\
0&&\frac{\left(8+\left|6-\frac{2(d+3)}{p_{0}^{2}}\right|\right)\varepsilon_{1}}{p_{0}^{2}}(I+p\otimes p)\end{pmatrix}.

(120)

In the last inequality, we used the fact that

\begin{pmatrix}-\frac{\left|6-\frac{2(d+3)}{p_{0}^{2}}\right|\varepsilon^{3}_{1}}{p_{0}^{4}}\left(I-\frac{p\otimes p}{p_{0}^{2}}\right)&\frac{6\varepsilon^{2}_{1}}{p_{0}^{3}}I-\frac{2\varepsilon^{2}_{1}(d+3)}{p_{0}^{5}}I\\
\frac{6\varepsilon^{2}_{1}}{p_{0}^{3}}I-\frac{2\varepsilon^{2}_{1}(d+3)}{p_{0}^{5}}I&-\frac{\left|6-\frac{2(d+3)}{p_{0}^{2}}\right|\varepsilon_{1}}{p_{0}^{2}}(I+p\otimes p)\end{pmatrix}

is negative semi-definite, which can be proven by using the second inequality in (
107
).
Since
\left|6-\frac{2(d+3)}{p_{0}^{2}}\right|
is uniformly bounded for

(
120
) shows that there are constants
C_{1}^{\prime}>0
and
C_{2}^{\prime}>0
such that

\displaystyle\sum_{i,j=1}^{d}\partial_{p_{i}p_{j}}Pa_{ij}

\displaystyle\leq\begin{pmatrix}\frac{2C_{1}^{\prime}\varepsilon^{3}_{1}}{p_{0}^{4}}(I-\frac{p\otimes p}{p_{0}^{2}})&0\\
0&\frac{2C_{2}^{\prime}\varepsilon_{1}}{p_{0}^{2}}(I+p\otimes p)\end{pmatrix}

\displaystyle\leq\begin{pmatrix}\frac{2C_{1}^{\prime}\varepsilon^{3}_{1}}{p_{0}^{3}}(I-\frac{p\otimes p}{p_{0}^{2}})&0\\
0&\frac{2C_{2}^{\prime}\varepsilon_{1}}{p_{0}}(I+p\otimes p)\end{pmatrix},\,\,\,\,\forall p\in\mathbb{R}^{d}.

Using this estimate and (

) in (

Lemma 8.7
.

Let
P=P(x,p)
be the matrix defined in (
56
). Assume there exists a constant
c_{3}>0
such that

\left|\left|\frac{\partial^{2}V(x)}{\partial x^{2}}\right|\right|_{F}\leq c_{3}(1+|\nabla_{x}V(x)|),\,\,\,\,\forall\,x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}.

(121)

Then there are constants
\theta_{3}>0
and
\theta_{4}>0
such that

\sum_{i=1}^{d}\left(\frac{p_{i}}{p_{0}}\partial_{x_{i}}P-\partial_{x_{i}}V\partial_{p_{i}}P\right)\leq\begin{pmatrix}\frac{2\theta_{3}\varepsilon^{3}}{V_{0}^{2}p_{0}^{3}}(I-\frac{p\otimes p}{p_{0}^{2}})&0\\
0&\frac{2\theta_{4}\varepsilon}{p_{0}}(I+p\otimes p)\end{pmatrix},\,\,\,\,\forall\,x,\,p\in\mathbb{R}^{d}.

(122)

Proof.

We compute

\sum_{i=1}^{d}\frac{p_{i}}{p_{0}}\partial_{x_{i}}P=\left[\sum_{i=1}^{d}\frac{\nabla_{x}V\cdot\nabla_{x}(\partial_{x_{i}}V)p_{i}}{V_{0}^{2}p_{0}}\right]\begin{pmatrix}-\frac{6\varepsilon^{3}}{V_{0}^{3}p_{0}^{3}}(I-\frac{p\otimes p}{p_{0}^{2}})&\frac{-2\varepsilon^{2}}{V_{0}^{2}p_{0}^{2}}I\\
\frac{-2\varepsilon^{2}}{V_{0}^{2}p_{0}^{2}}I&\frac{-2\varepsilon}{V_{0}p_{0}}(I+p\otimes p)\end{pmatrix}\\
=\left[\frac{1}{V_{0}^{2}p_{0}}\nabla^{T}_{x}V\frac{\partial^{2}V}{\partial x^{2}}p\right]\begin{pmatrix}-\frac{6\varepsilon^{3}}{V_{0}^{3}p_{0}^{3}}(I-\frac{p\otimes p}{p_{0}^{2}})&\frac{-2\varepsilon^{2}}{V_{0}^{2}p_{0}^{2}}I\\
\frac{-2\varepsilon^{2}}{V_{0}^{2}p_{0}^{2}}I&\frac{-2\varepsilon}{V_{0}p_{0}}(I+p\otimes p)\end{pmatrix}.

(123)

Since (
121
) implies
\left|\left|\frac{\partial^{2}V}{\partial x^{2}}\right|\right|_{F}\leq\sqrt{2}c_{3}V_{0},
we have

\left|\frac{1}{V_{0}^{2}p_{0}}\nabla^{T}_{x}V\frac{\partial^{2}V}{\partial x^{2}}p\right|\leq\frac{1}{V_{0}^{2}p_{0}}|\nabla_{x}V|\left|\left|\frac{\partial^{2}V}{\partial x^{2}}\right|\right|_{F}|p|\leq\sqrt{2}c_{3},\,\,\,\,\forall\,x,\,p\in\mathbb{R}^{d}.

This uniform bound and (
123
) show that there are constants
C_{1}>0
and
C_{2}
such that

\sum_{i=1}^{d}\frac{p_{i}}{p_{0}}\partial_{x_{i}}P\leq\begin{pmatrix}\frac{2C_{1}\varepsilon^{3}}{V_{0}^{3}p_{0}^{3}}(I-\frac{p\otimes p}{p_{0}^{2}})&0\\
0&\frac{2C_{2}\varepsilon}{V_{0}p_{0}}(I+p\otimes p)\end{pmatrix}\leq\begin{pmatrix}\frac{2C_{1}\varepsilon^{3}}{V_{0}^{2}p_{0}^{3}}(I-\frac{p\otimes p}{p_{0}^{2}})&0\\
0&\frac{2C_{2}\varepsilon}{p_{0}}(I+p\otimes p)\end{pmatrix},\,\,\,\,\forall\,x,\,p\in\mathbb{R}^{d}.

(124)

Next, we compute

-\sum_{i=1}^{d}\partial_{x_{i}}V\partial_{p_{i}}P\\
=\sum_{i=1}^{d}\begin{pmatrix}2\varepsilon^{3}\left[\frac{5\partial_{x_{i}}Vp_{i}}{V_{0}^{3}p_{0}^{5}}(I-\frac{p\otimes p}{p_{0}^{2}})-\frac{\partial_{x_{i}}V}{V^{3}_{0}p_{0}^{5}}(2p_{i}I-\partial_{p_{i}}(p\otimes p))\right]&\frac{2\varepsilon^{2}\partial_{x_{i}}Vp_{i}}{V^{2}_{0}p_{0}^{4}}I\\
\frac{2\varepsilon^{2}\partial_{x_{i}}Vp_{i}}{V_{0}^{2}p_{0}^{4}}I&\frac{2\varepsilon\partial_{x_{i}}Vp_{i}}{V_{0}p_{0}^{3}}(I+p\otimes p)-\frac{2\varepsilon\partial_{x_{i}}V}{V_{0}p_{0}}\partial_{p_{i}}(p\otimes p)\end{pmatrix}.

(125)

We denote
\tilde{p}_{i}\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\begin{pmatrix}p_{1}\\
\vdots\\
p_{i-1}\\
p_{i}-1\\
p_{i+1}\\
\vdots\\
p_{d}\end{pmatrix}=p-e_{i}
and
\bar{p}_{i}\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\begin{pmatrix}p_{1}\\
\vdots\\
p_{i-1}\\
p_{i}+1\\
p_{i+1}\\
\vdots\\
p_{d}\end{pmatrix}=p+e_{i},
where
e_{i}
denotes the
i-
th unit vector, for
i\in\{1,...,d\}.
Let
E_{i}\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}e_{i}\otimes e_{i}\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}
denote the matrix whose element in the intersection of the
i-
th column and the
i-
th row equals 1 and all other elements are zero. Using
\partial_{p_{i}}(p\otimes p)=e_{i}\otimes p+p\otimes e_{i}
one can check

\frac{1}{p_{0}^{3}}\left(I-\frac{p\otimes p}{p_{0}^{2}}\right)-\frac{1}{p_{0}^{5}}(2p_{i}I-\partial_{p_{i}}(p\otimes p))=\frac{1}{p_{0}^{5}}E_{i}+\frac{1}{p_{0}^{5}}(|\tilde{p}_{i}|^{2}I-\tilde{p}_{i}\otimes\tilde{p}_{i})\geq 0

and

\frac{1}{p_{0}^{3}}\left(I-\frac{p\otimes p}{p_{0}^{2}}\right)+\frac{1}{p_{0}^{5}}(2p_{i}I-\partial_{p_{i}}(p\otimes p))=\frac{1}{p_{0}^{5}}E_{i}+\frac{1}{p_{0}^{5}}(|\bar{p}_{i}|^{2}I-\bar{p}_{i}\otimes\bar{p}_{i})\geq 0.

From these equations we obtain

-\frac{1}{p_{0}^{3}}\left(I-\frac{p\otimes p}{p_{0}^{2}}\right)\leq\frac{1}{p_{0}^{5}}(2p_{i}I-\partial_{p_{i}}(p\otimes p))\leq\frac{1}{p_{0}^{3}}\left(I-\frac{p\otimes p}{p_{0}^{2}}\right).

Using these inequalities and the fact that
\left|\frac{\partial_{x_{i}}V}{V_{0}}\right|
and
\left|\frac{p_{i}}{p^{2}_{0}}\right|
are uniformly bounded for
x,\,p\in\mathbb{R}^{d},
we conclude that there is a constant
C^{\prime}_{1}>0
such that

2\varepsilon^{3}\left[\frac{5\partial_{x_{i}}Vp_{i}}{V_{0}^{3}p_{0}^{5}}\left(I-\frac{p\otimes p}{p_{0}^{2}}\right)-\frac{\partial_{x_{i}}V}{V^{3}_{0}p_{0}^{5}}(2p_{i}I-\partial_{p_{i}}(p\otimes p))\right]\leq\frac{2C^{\prime}_{1}\varepsilon^{3}}{V_{0}^{2}p_{0}^{3}}\left(I-\frac{p\otimes p}{p_{0}^{2}}\right),\,\,\,\,\,\forall x,p\in\mathbb{R}^{d}.

(126)

The inequalities

I+p\otimes p-\partial_{p_{i}}(p\otimes p)=\tilde{p}_{i}\otimes\tilde{p}_{i}+I-E_{i}\geq 0

and

I+p\otimes p+\partial_{p_{i}}(p\otimes p)=\bar{p}_{i}\otimes\bar{p}_{i}+I-E_{i}\geq 0

are easy to check and they imply

-(I+p\otimes p)\leq\partial_{p_{i}}(p\otimes p)\leq I+p\otimes p.

Using these inequalities and the fact that
\left|\frac{\partial_{x_{i}}V}{V_{0}}\right|
and
\left|\frac{p_{i}}{p^{2}_{0}}\right|
are uniformly bounded for
x,\,p\in\mathbb{R}^{d},
we conclude that there is a constant
C^{\prime}_{2}>0
such that

\frac{2\varepsilon\partial_{x_{i}}Vp_{i}}{V_{0}p_{0}^{3}}(I+p\otimes p)-\frac{2\varepsilon\partial_{x_{i}}V}{V_{0}p_{0}}\partial_{p_{i}}(p\otimes p)\leq\frac{2C^{\prime}_{2}\varepsilon}{p_{0}}(I+p\otimes p).

(127)

(
125
), (
126
), and (
127
) show that there are constants
C^{\prime\prime}_{1}>0
and
C^{\prime\prime}_{2}>0
such that

-\sum_{i=1}^{d}\partial_{x_{i}}V\partial_{p_{i}}P\leq\begin{pmatrix}\frac{2C^{\prime\prime}_{1}\varepsilon^{3}}{V_{0}^{2}p_{0}^{3}}(I-\frac{p\otimes p}{p_{0}^{2}})&0\\
0&\frac{2C^{\prime\prime}_{2}\varepsilon}{p_{0}}(I+p\otimes p)\end{pmatrix},\,\,\,\,\forall\,x,\,p\in\mathbb{R}^{d}.

This inequality and (
124
) yield the claimed estimate (
122
).
∎
